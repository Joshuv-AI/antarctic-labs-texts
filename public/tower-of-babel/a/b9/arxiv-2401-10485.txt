1.
Introduction

Standard sinh-Poisson equation is of the following form:

-\Delta u=\varepsilon^{2}\left(e^{u}-e^{-u}\right).

It was used to describe the vortex-type configurations for 2D turbulent Euler flow and the geometry of constant mean curvature surfaces( c.f.
[
3
,
19
,
9
,
22
,
23
,
35
]
). In the recent decades, the Dirichlet problem of the sinh-Poisson equation and its variant attracted widespread attention(see
[
17
,
26
,
12
,
34
]
and references therein).

The following problem was first considered by Spruck:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u=\varepsilon^{2}(e^{u}-e^{-u})&\mbox{in $\Omega$,}\\
u=0,&\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$.}\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

Under the condition that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a rectangle containing the origin, Spruck
[
29
]
constructed a family of positive solutions of (
1.1
) which blow up at the origin as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Later, Jost
et al.
[
20
]
studied the blow-up behaviours of the solutions of (
1.1
) as
\varepsilon\to 0
. They proved that the number of blow-up points of each solution to (
1.1
) is finite and the mass of each blow-up point of any solution to (
1.1
) is a multiple of
8\pi
. In the case that the domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is symmetric with respect to the origin and
0\in\Omega
, Grossi and Pistoia
[
18
]
constructed a sequences of sign-changing radial solutions of (
1.1
) with a multiple blow-up point at the origin. Bartsch
et al.

[
4
,
5
]
considered the relationship between the blow-up behaviours of the solutions to (
1.1
) and the critical points of the following general Kirchhoff-Routh path function

H_{\Omega}(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{N})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Gamma_{i}^{2}h(x_{i})+\sum_{i\not=j}\Gamma_{i}\Gamma_{j}G(x_{i},x_{j}),

where
h(\cdot)
and
G(\cdot,\cdot)
are the Robin function and Green function of the Dirichlet Laplacian on
\Omega
, respectively. They found some sign-changing blow-up solutions of (
1.1
), and the locations of these blow-up points constitute a critical point of
H_{\Omega}
.

The following sinh-Poisson equation with Hénon term arise from the study of Chern-Simons vortex theory(see
[
36
,
6
]
)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u=\varepsilon^{2}|x|^{2\alpha}(e^{u}-e^{-u})&\mbox{in $\Omega$,}\\
u=0,&\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$.}\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

On the smooth domain
\Omega
containing the origin, D’Aprile
[
12
]
constructed a family of sign-changing blow-up solutions of (
1.2
), whose blow-up points are uniformly away from the origin and the boundary
\partial\Omega
. Moreover, the number of blow-up points of the solutions constructed in
[
12
]
is less than 4. However, for
\Omega=B_{1}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and any integer
m>0
, Zhang and Yang
[
39
]
constructed a family of sign-changing solutions to (
1.2
) which blow up at the origin and
m
points on a circle in
B_{1}(0)
.

Only few results available considering the following Neumann problem of sinh-Poisson equation:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u=\varepsilon^{2}(e^{u}-e^{-u})&\mbox{in $\Omega$,}\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=0,&\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$.}\end{array}\right.

Given any non-negative numbers
k
and
l
satisfying
k+l\geq 1
, Wei
et al.
[
31
,
33
]
found a sequence of solutions which blow up at
k
points in
\Omega
and
l
points on
\partial\Omega
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Motivated by these work above, Wang and Zheng
[
30
]
considered the following problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+u=\varepsilon^{2}|x-q_{1}|^{2\alpha_{1}}\cdots|x-q_{s}|^{2\alpha_{s}}(e^{u}-e^{-u})&\mbox{in $\Omega$,}\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=0,&\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$,}\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

where
q_{1},q_{2},\cdots,q_{s}\in\Omega
, and
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{s}\in(0,+\infty)\char 92\relax\mathbb{N}
.
Via Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction method, they proved that given any two nonnegative integer
k
and
l
satisfying
k+l\geq 1
, there exists a solution
u_{\varepsilon}
to (
1.3
) satisfying

\varepsilon^{2}|x-q_{1}|^{2\alpha_{1}}\cdots|x-q_{s}|^{2\alpha_{s}}(e^{u_{\varepsilon}}-e^{-u_{\varepsilon}})\rightharpoonup\sum_{j=1}^{k}8\pi\delta_{\xi_{j}}+\sum_{j=1}^{l}4\pi\delta_{\xi_{k+j}}+8\pi\sum_{i=1}^{s}(1+\alpha)\delta_{q_{i}},\quad\mathrm{as}\quad\varepsilon\to 0,

where
\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{l}\in\Omega
and
\xi_{l+1},\cdots,\xi_{k+l}\in\partial\Omega
.

In the case of
s=1
, problem (
1.3
) is equivalent to the following one:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta v+v=\varepsilon^{2}|x-q|^{2\alpha}(e^{v}-e^{-v})&\mbox{in $\Omega$,}\\
\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}=0,&\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$.}\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

However, in the case of
s=1
and
\alpha_{1}=0
, problem (
1.3
) is reduced to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+u=\varepsilon^{2}(e^{u}-e^{-u})&\mbox{in $D$,}\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=0,&\mbox{on $\partial D$,}\end{array}\right.

(1.5)

which is similar to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+u=\varepsilon^{2}e^{u}&\mbox{in $\Omega$,}\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=0,&\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$.}\end{array}\right.

(1.6)

The equation (
1.6
) is used to represent steady-states of the Keller-Segal system(c.f.
[
14
,
1
,
21
]
and references therein). The blow-up behaviours of the solutions to (
1.6
) were analysed in
[
24
]
. Given any nonnegative integers
k
and
l
such that
k+l\geq 1
, del Pino and Wei
[
14
]
found a sequence of solutions to (
1.6
) which blow up at
k
points in
\Omega
and
l
points on
\partial\Omega
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. In the case of
\Omega=B_{r_{0}}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, Pistoia and Vaira
[
27
]
found a sequence of solutions to (
1.6
) which are concentrated near the whole boundary

. Later, del Pino
et al.
[
13
]
extended the results in
[
27
]
to more general domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. To explore the boundary concentration phenomena of (

[
1

considered the following problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\mathrm{div}(a(x)\nabla u)+a(x)u=\varepsilon^{2}a(x)e^{u}&\mbox{in $\Omega$,}\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=0,&\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$,}\end{array}\right.

(1.7)

where

. For any integer

, they found solutions of (

) with
m

m

) with
m

\partial\Omega

Equation (
1.7
) is called isotropic if
a(x)\equiv 1
and it is called anisotropic if the function
a(x)\not\equiv 1
( see
[
32
]
). Anisotropic problems attract much attention in recent years. Using a parabolic approach, Ciani
et al.

[
10
]
establish some elliptic estimate of the following more general singular anisotropic elliptic equations:

\sum_{i=1}^{s}\partial_{ii}u+\sum_{i=s+1}^{N}\partial_{i}(A_{i}(x,u,\nabla u))=0,\qquad in\;\;\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
1\leq s\leq N-1
. However, Clop
et al.

[
11
]
studied the minimizer of the following the following non-autonomous non-homogeneous functional

\mathcal{F}(w,\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}\left[F(x,Dw(x))-f(x)w(x)\right]dx.

Under some sub-quadratic growth condition of
F
, they proved the minimizer of the functional
\mathcal{F}(w,\Omega)
belongs to
W^{2,p}(\Omega)
, where
p\in(0,1)
. Papageorgiou
et al.

[
25
]
studied the following more general anisotropic singular Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\mathrm{div}a(z,Du)=\beta(z)u(z)^{-\eta(z)}+f(z,u(z)),\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega$},\\
u|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\qquad u>0,\end{array}\right.

(1.8)

where
a:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is measurable in
z\in\Omega
, continuous and monotone in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, positive function
\beta\in L^{+\infty}(\Omega)
and
\eta\in C(\bar{(}\Omega))
with range in
(0,1)
. From variational method, they proved (
1.8
) has at least two smooth solutions.

Eigenvalue problem is also a class of interesting problem in partial differential equations. In
[
15
]
, Du
et al.
established Li-Yau-Kröger-type estimates of the eigenvalue of the following Neumann-type eigenvalue problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta^{2}u-\tau\Delta u=\Lambda u,&\mbox{in $\Omega$},\\
(1-\sigma)\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial n^{2}}+\sigma\Delta u=0,&\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$},\\
\tau\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}-(1-\sigma)\mathrm{div}_{\partial\Omega}(D^{2}u\cdot n)_{\partial\Omega}-\frac{\partial\Delta u}{\partial n}=0,&\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$}.\end{array}\right.

In the same paper, they also give some Reilly-type estimates of the following Steklov-type eigenvalue problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta^{2}u-\tau\Delta u=0,&\mbox{in $\Omega$},\\
(1-\sigma)\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial n^{2}}+\sigma\Delta u=0,&\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$},\\
\tau\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}-(1-\sigma)\mathrm{div}_{\partial\Omega}(D^{2}u\cdot n)_{\partial\Omega}-\frac{\partial\Delta u}{\partial n}=\lambda u,&\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$}.\end{array}\right.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\mathrm{div}(a(x)\nabla u)+a(x)u=\varepsilon^{2}a(x)|x-q|^{2\alpha}e^{u}&\mbox{in $\Omega$,}\\

Here we impose a Hardy or Hénon weight on the right hand side of the first equation in (

\varepsilon\to 0

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\mathrm{div}(a(x)\nabla u)+a(x)u=\varepsilon^{2}a(x)|x-q|^{2\alpha}(e^{u}-e^{-u})&\mbox{in $\Omega$,}\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=0,&\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$,}\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

Equation (

1.4

a(x)\equiv 1

Let
b\in\{1,2\}
to be fixed and
\Omega
be a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that

\bar{\Omega}\subset\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{b}\times\mathbb{R}^{2-b}:x_{i}>0,i=1,b\}.

Denote

, where

are positive integers. Then

D:=\{(y_{1},y_{2},x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{M_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{M_{b}}\times\mathbb{R}^{2-b}:(|y_{1}|,|y_{2}|,x^{\prime})\in\Omega\}

\mathbb{R}^{N}
, where

1.4

\Gamma:=\mathcal{O}(M_{1})\times\mathcal{O}(M_{b})

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+\sum_{i=1}^{b}\frac{M_{i}-1}{x_{i}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}+u=\varepsilon^{2}(e^{u}-e^{-u}),&\mbox{in $\Omega$;}\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=0,&\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$.}\end{array}\right.

By choosing the function

1.10

In this paper, we will show that (

Since the classical sinh-Poisson Dirichlet problem (
1.1

1.10

Proof.

Let

&-2\log\frac{|x-q|^{2(1+\alpha)}}{(\varepsilon\mu_{0})^{2(1+\alpha)}+|x-q|^{2(1+\alpha)}},\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega$,}\\
\frac{\partial Y_{0}}{\partial n}=-\frac{4(1+\alpha)(\varepsilon\mu_{0})^{2(1+\alpha)}(x-q,n)}{|x-q|^{2}[(\varepsilon\mu_{0})^{2(1+\alpha)}+|x-q|^{2(1+\alpha)}],}&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$.}\end{array}\right.

It is apparent

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{4(1+\alpha)(\varepsilon\mu_{0})^{2(1+\alpha)}\nabla(\log a)\cdot(x-q)}{|x-q|^{2}[(\varepsilon\mu_{0})^{2(1+\alpha)}+|x-q|^{2(1+\alpha)}]}\right|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon\mu_{0})^{2-p}\int_{\tilde{\Omega}_{\varepsilon\mu_{0}}}\left|\frac{1}{|t|[1+|t|^{2(1+\alpha)}]}\right|^{p}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon\mu_{0})^{\min\left\{2-p,2(1+\alpha)p\right\}},

where

.
Then we get

From the same method, we also get

Now we estimate the boundary terms. In the case of

, we get

However in the case of

\displaystyle C\varepsilon\mu_{0}\int_{\tilde{\Omega}_{\varepsilon\mu_{0}}}\left|\frac{1}{1+|t|^{2(1+\alpha)}}\right|^{p}dt

with the help of
[

Then

From elliptic estimate and Sobolev embedding theorem, we get

Proof.

A direct computation yields

4.
Linear theory

It is well known that (see
[
2
]
) each bounded solution of

\Delta\rho+\frac{8}{[1+|y|^{2}]^{2}}\rho=0\quad\mathrm{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2}

is a linear combination of the following functions

Z_{0}(y)=\frac{|y|^{2}-1}{|y|^{2}+1},\qquad Z_{1}(y)=\frac{y_{1}}{1+|y|^{2}},\qquad Z_{2}(y)=\frac{y_{2}}{1+|y|^{2}}.

It is also proved in
[
16
]
that any solution of

\Delta\rho+\frac{8(1+\alpha)^{2}|y|^{2\alpha}}{[1+|y|^{2(1+\alpha)}]^{2}}\rho=0\quad\mathrm{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2}

is a multiple of

\tilde{Z}(y)=\frac{|y|^{2(1+\alpha)}-1}{|y|^{2(1+\alpha)}+1}.

Choose
R_{0}>0
to be a constant large enough. We define a cutoff function
\chi
which satisfies
\chi(r)=1
if
r\in[0,R_{0})
and
\chi(r)=0
if
r\in[R_{0}+1,\infty)
.

For
i=1,\cdots,l
, we define

\chi_{i}(y)=\chi\left(\frac{|y-\xi_{i}^{\prime}|}{\mu_{i}}\right),\qquad Z_{ij}(y)=\frac{1}{\mu_{i}}Z_{j}\left(\frac{y-\xi_{i}^{\prime}}{\mu_{i}}\right),\quad\mathrm{for}\;\;j=0,1,2.

For
i=l+1,\cdots,m
,
\xi_{i}\in\partial\Omega
, we define the rotation map
A_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
so that
A_{i}n_{\Omega}(\xi_{i})=n_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}(0)
.
Let
G_{i}(x_{1})
be a defining function defined on a neighborhood of
(0,0)
on
A_{i}(\partial\Omega-\xi_{i})
. So there exist constants
R>0
and
\delta_{2}>0
, and a smooth function
G_{i}:(-R,R)\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
G_{i}(0)=0
,
G_{i}^{\prime}(0)=0
such that

A_{i}(\Omega-\{\xi_{i}\})\cap B_{\delta_{2}}(0)=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\;:\;-R<x_{1}<R,\;x_{2}>G_{i}(x_{1})\}\cap B_{\delta_{2}}(0,0).

We define the function
F_{i}:B_{\delta_{2}}(0,0)\cap\overline{A_{i}(\Omega-\{\xi_{i}\})}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, where
F_{i}=(F_{i1},F_{i2})
and

F_{i1}(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{1}+\frac{x_{2}-G_{i}(x_{1})}{1+|G_{i}^{\prime}(x_{1})|^{2}}G_{i}^{\prime}(x_{1}),\qquad F_{i2}(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{2}-G_{i}(x_{1}).

It is obvious that
F_{i}
is a transformation which strengthen the boundary near
\xi_{i}
.
For
i=l+1,\cdots,m
, we define

F_{i}^{\varepsilon}(y)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}F_{i}(A_{i}(\varepsilon y-\xi_{i}))\;:\;B_{\frac{\delta_{2}}{\varepsilon}}(\xi_{i}^{\prime})\cap\Omega_{\varepsilon}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}

and

\chi_{i}(y)=\chi\left(\frac{|F_{i}^{\varepsilon}(y)|}{\mu_{i}}\right),\qquad Z_{ij}(y)=\frac{1}{\mu_{i}}Z_{j}\left(\frac{F_{i}^{\varepsilon}(y)}{\mu_{i}}\right),\qquad j=0,1.

As the same way, if
q\in\Omega
, we define

\chi_{0}(y)=\chi\left(\frac{|y-q^{\prime}|}{\mu_{0}}\right),\qquad Z_{00}(y)=\frac{1}{\mu_{0}}\tilde{Z}\left(\frac{y-q^{\prime}}{\mu_{0}}\right).

In the case
q\in\partial\Omega
, we define a rotation map
A_{0}
such that
A_{0}n_{\Omega}(q)=n_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}(0)
. Choose
G_{0}:(-R,R)\to\mathbb{R}

e_{ij}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}^{2}Z_{ij}^{2}dx=-\sum_{k=0,k\not=i}^{m}d_{k}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}Z_{ij}\tilde{Z}_{k0}dy-d_{i}\chi_{\{i\geq l+1\}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}Z_{i1}Z_{i0}dy,\quad i=1,2,\cdots,m

(4.14)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{m}d_{k}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}Z_{i0}\tilde{Z}_{k0}dy=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi\chi_{i}Z_{i0}dy-e_{i1}\chi_{\{i\geq l+1\}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}^{2}Z_{i1}Z_{i0}dy,\quad i=0,1,2,\cdots,m.

(4.15)

Here
\chi_{\{i\geq l+1\}}
denote a term which equals to 1 in the case of
i\geq l+1
and equals to
0
otherwise.
From the definition of
F_{i}^{\varepsilon}(y)
, we have

F_{i}^{\varepsilon}(y)=A_{i}(y-\xi_{i}^{\prime})\left(1+O(\varepsilon|y-\xi_{i}^{\prime}|)\right),\qquad\nabla F_{i}^{\varepsilon}(y)=A_{i}+O(\varepsilon|y-\xi_{i}^{\prime}|).

(4.16)

For
i\geq l+1
, we have

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}^{2}Z_{i1}Z_{i0}dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}\chi^{2}(|z|)Z_{1}(z)Z_{0}(z)\left(1+O(\varepsilon\mu_{i}|z|)\right)dz=O(\varepsilon\mu_{i}).

From the same method, we get

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}Z_{i0}Z_{i1}dy=O(\varepsilon\mu_{i}),

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}^{2}Z_{ij}^{2}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi^{2}(|z|)Z_{1}^{2}dz,&i=1,2,\cdots,l;\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}\chi^{2}(|z|)Z_{1}^{2}(z)dz+O(\varepsilon\mu_{i}),&i=l+1,\cdots,m\end{array}\right.

and

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}Z_{i0}\tilde{Z}_{i0}dy=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}Z^{2}_{i0}dy=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}\chi^{2}\tilde{Z}^{2}dy+O(\varepsilon\mu_{0})&i=0;\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi^{2}Z_{0}^{2}dy&i=1,2,\cdots,l;\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}\chi^{2}Z_{0}^{2}dy+O(\varepsilon\mu_{i})&i=l+1,\cdots,m.\end{array}\right.

For
i=1,2,\cdots,m;j=0,1,J_{i}
and
k\not=i
, we have

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}Z_{ij}\tilde{Z}_{k0}dy=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}Z_{ij}\eta_{2}^{q}\hat{Z}_{k0}dy.

If
\chi_{i}\not=0
, we get
|z_{i}^{\prime}|\leq(R_{0}+1)\mu_{i}
. It is obvious that for
\varepsilon>0
small enough, it hold that

|y-q^{\prime}|\leq|y-\xi_{i}^{\prime}|+|q^{\prime}-\xi_{i}^{\prime}|\leq 2|z_{i}|+|q^{\prime}-\xi_{i}^{\prime}|\leq 2(R_{0}+1)\mu_{i}+\frac{d}{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{2d}{\varepsilon},

and

|z_{k}|\geq\frac{1}{2}|y-\xi_{k}^{\prime}|\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(|\xi_{i}^{\prime}-\xi_{k}^{\prime}|-|y-\xi_{i}^{\prime}|\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(|\xi_{i}^{\prime}-\xi_{k}^{\prime}|-2|z_{i}|\right)\geq\frac{1}{4\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{\kappa}}.

In this case, we get

\hat{Z}_{k0}=Z_{k0}-\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}+a_{k0}^{\xi}G(\varepsilon y,\xi_{k})+(\mathring{Z}_{k0}-Z_{k0})\chi_{\{1\leq k\leq l\}}=O\left(\frac{\log|\log\varepsilon|}{\mu_{k}|\log\varepsilon|}\right).

So

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}Z_{ij}\tilde{Z}_{k0}=O\left(\frac{\mu_{i}\log|\log\varepsilon|}{\mu_{k}|\log\varepsilon|}\right).

From (
4.14
) and the estimate above, we get

|e_{ij}|\leq C\sum_{k=0}^{m}|d_{k}|\frac{\mu_{i}\log|\log\varepsilon|}{\mu_{k}|\log\varepsilon|}.

(4.17)

We also get
d_{k}
’s are uniquely determined according to (
4.15
). From its definition, we get
\tilde{\phi}
solves the following problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}L(\tilde{\phi})=h-\sum_{k=0}^{m}d_{k}L(\tilde{Z}_{k0})-\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{s=1}^{J_{k}}e_{ks}L(\chi_{k}Z_{ks}),&\mbox{in $\Omega_{\varepsilon}$,}\\
\frac{\partial\tilde{\phi}}{\partial n}=0,&\mbox{on $\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}$,}\\
\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}(y)Z_{ij}(y)\tilde{\phi}(y)dy=0,&\mbox{$i=0,1,\cdots m;$ $j=0,1,J_{i}$,}\end{array}\right.

(4.18)

From Lemma
4.2
, we get

\|\tilde{\phi}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\left[\|h\|_{*}+\sum_{k=0}^{m}|d_{k}|\|L(\tilde{Z}_{k0})\|_{*}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{s=1}^{J_{k}}|e_{ks}|\|L(\chi_{k}Z_{ks})\|_{*}\right].

(4.19)

Now we have to derive some estimate of the constants
d_{k}
’s. Multiplying the both sides of (
4.18
) by
a(\varepsilon y)\tilde{Z}_{j0}
and integrating, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m}d_{k}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}a(\varepsilon y)L(\tilde{Z}_{k0})\tilde{Z}_{j0}dy=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}a(\varepsilon y)\tilde{Z}_{j0}hdy-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}a(\varepsilon y)L(\tilde{Z}_{j0})\tilde{\phi}dy

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{l=1}^{J_{k}}e_{kl}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}a(\varepsilon y)L(\tilde{Z}_{j0})\chi_{k}Z_{kl}dy.

It is obvious that

\left|\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}a(\varepsilon y)\tilde{Z}_{j0}hdy\right|\leq\frac{C}{\mu_{j}}\|h\|_{*}.

From Lemma
A.3
, (
4.17
) and (
4.19
),

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}a(\varepsilon y)L(\tilde{Z}_{j0})\tilde{\phi}\right|\leq

\displaystyle C\|L(\tilde{Z}_{j0})\|_{*}\|\tilde{\phi}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\frac{\log|\log\varepsilon|}{\mu_{j}|\log\varepsilon|}\|\tilde{\phi}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{\log|\log\varepsilon|}{\mu_{j}|\log\varepsilon|}\left[\|h\|_{*}+\sum_{k=0}^{m}|d_{k}|\frac{\log|\log\varepsilon|}{\mu_{k}|\log\varepsilon|}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{\log|\log\varepsilon|}{\mu_{j}|\log\varepsilon|}\|h\|_{*}+C\sum_{k=0}^{m}|d_{k}|\frac{\log^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}{\mu_{j}\mu_{k}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}.

Using (
4.17
) and Lemma
A.3
, we get

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{l=1}^{J_{k}}e_{kl}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}a(\varepsilon y)L(\tilde{Z}_{j0})\chi_{k}Z_{kl}dy\right|\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{l=1}^{J_{k}}\frac{|e_{kl}|}{\mu_{k}}\|L(\tilde{Z}_{j0})\|_{*}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{l=1}^{J_{l}}\frac{\log|\log\varepsilon|}{\mu_{j}\mu_{k}|\log\varepsilon|}|e_{kl}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=0}^{m}|d_{k}|\frac{\log^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}{\mu_{j}\mu_{k}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}.

According to Lemma
A.4
and (
4.17
),

|d_{k}|\leq C\mu_{k}|\log\varepsilon|\|h\|_{*},\qquad|e_{ks}|\leq C(\mu_{k}\log|\log\varepsilon|)\|h\|_{*}.

(4.20)

From (
4.19
), (
4.20
) and Lemma
A.3
, we arrive at

\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\left[\|\tilde{\phi}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+|\log\varepsilon|\|h\|_{*}\right]\leq C|\log\varepsilon|\|h\|_{*}.

Hence this lemma follows.

∎

4.3.
Solutions to the linear problem

Now we turn around to consider problem (
4.1
). We have the following proposition.

Proposition 4.4
.

There exist positive constants
\varepsilon
and
C
such that for any
(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{m})\in\mathcal{O}_{\varepsilon}
, problem (
4.1
) has a unique solution
\phi:=T(h)
. Moreover, the solution
\phi
satisfies the following estimates

\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C|\log\varepsilon|\|h\|_{*}

(4.21)

and

\|\partial_{\xi_{kl}^{\prime}}\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\frac{|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}{\mu_{k}}\|h\|_{*}.

Proof.

We first prove the priori estimate (
4.21
). According to Lemma
4.3
, we get

\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C|\log\varepsilon|\left[\|h\|_{*}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{J_{i}}|c_{ij}|\|\chi_{i}Z_{ij}\|_{*}\right]\leq C|\log\varepsilon|\left[\|h\|_{*}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{J_{i}}\mu_{i}|c_{ij}|\right].

(4.22)

Now we estimate the constants
c_{ij}
’s. For
k=l+1,\cdots,m
, we multiply the both sides of the first equation in (
4.1
) by
a(\varepsilon y)\eta_{2}^{q}Z_{ks}
and integrate by part. Then we have

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}a(\varepsilon y)L(\eta_{2}^{q}Z_{ks})\phi dy=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}a(\varepsilon y)\eta_{2}^{q}Z_{ks}hdy+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{J_{i}}c_{ij}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}Z_{ij}\eta_{2}^{q}Z_{ks}dy.

(4.23)

It is apparent that

\displaystyle L(\eta_{2}^{q}Z_{ks})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{2}^{q}\left[\left(\frac{8\mu_{k}^{2}}{[\mu_{k}^{2}+|z_{k}|^{2}]^{2}}-W\right)Z_{ks}-\nabla(\log a(\varepsilon y))\cdot\nabla Z_{ks}+\varepsilon^{2}Z_{ks}\right]-\Delta\eta_{2}^{q}Z_{ks}-2\nabla\eta_{2}^{q}\cdot\nabla Z_{ks}

\displaystyle-\nabla(\log a(\varepsilon y))\cdot\nabla\eta_{2}^{q}Z_{ks}+O\left(\frac{\varepsilon|\eta_{2}^{q}|}{(\mu_{k}+|z_{k}|)^{2}}\right).

In the equality above, we notice

-\Delta Z_{ks}=\frac{8\mu_{k}^{2}}{[\mu_{k}^{2}+|z_{k}|^{2}]^{2}}Z_{ks}+O\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{(\mu_{k}+|z_{k}|)^{2}}\right),

(4.24)

which follows from relationship (
4.11
).

If
\frac{4d}{\varepsilon}\leq|z_{0}|\leq\frac{8d}{\varepsilon}
, we get
\frac{d}{2\varepsilon}\leq|z_{j}|\leq\frac{34d}{\varepsilon}
for
\varepsilon
small enough. There holds that

\Delta\eta_{2}^{q}Z_{ks}+2\nabla\eta_{2}^{q}\cdot\nabla Z_{ks}+\nabla(\log a(\varepsilon y))\cdot\nabla\eta_{2}^{q}Z_{ks}=O(\varepsilon^{3}).

Then

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}a(\varepsilon y)\left[\Delta\eta_{2}^{q}Z_{ks}+2\nabla\eta_{2}^{q}\cdot\nabla Z_{ks}+\nabla(\log a(\varepsilon y))\cdot\nabla\eta_{2}^{q}Z_{ks}\right]\phi dy=O\left(\varepsilon\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\right).

It is easy to get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}a(\varepsilon y)\eta_{2}^{q}\left[-\nabla(\log a(\varepsilon y))\cdot\nabla Z_{ks}+\varepsilon^{2}Z_{ks}\right]\phi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\left(\int_{\eta_{2}^{q}\not=0}\frac{\varepsilon}{(\mu_{k}+|y-\xi_{k}^{\prime}|)^{2}}dy+\int_{\eta_{2}^{q}\not=0}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{(\mu_{k}+|y-\xi_{k}^{\prime}|)}dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

From the same argument, we also get

\left|\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}a(\varepsilon y)\frac{\varepsilon\eta_{2}^{q}}{(\mu_{k}+|z_{k}|)^{2}}\phi dy\right|\leq C\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

Denote

A_{k}=\left\{y\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}\;:\;|y-\xi_{k}^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{2\kappa}}\right\},\qquad\mathrm{for}\qquad k=1,2,\cdots,m,

A_{0}=\left\{y\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}\;:\;|y-q^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{2\kappa}}\right\},

and
A_{m+1}=\Omega_{\varepsilon}\char 92\relax\cup_{j=0}^{m}A_{j}
. Then there holds

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\eta_{2}^{q}\left(\frac{8\mu_{k}^{2}}{[\mu_{k}+|z_{k}|^{2}]^{2}}-W\right)Z_{ks}\phi dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\eta_{2}^{q}\left|\frac{8\mu_{k}^{2}}{[\mu_{k}^{2}+|z_{k}|^{2}]^{2}}-W\right||Z_{ks}|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\left[\int_{A_{k}}\eta_{2}^{q}\left|\frac{8\mu_{k}^{2}}{[\mu_{k}^{2}+|z_{k}|^{2}]^{2}}-W\right||Z_{ks}|dy+\sum_{i=0,i\not=k}^{m}\int_{A_{i}}\eta_{2}^{q}\left|\frac{8\mu_{k}^{2}}{[\mu_{k}^{2}+|z_{k}|^{2}]^{2}}-W\right||Z_{ks}|dy.\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\int_{A_{m+1}}\eta_{2}^{q}\left|\frac{8\mu_{k}^{2}}{[\mu_{k}^{2}+|z_{k}|^{2}]^{2}}-W\right||Z_{ks}|dy\right]=:C\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}(I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}).

For
i\not=k
, it is apparent that

\displaystyle\int_{A_{i}}\eta_{2}^{q}\left|\frac{8\mu_{k}^{2}}{[\mu_{k}^{2}+|z_{k}|^{2}]^{2}}-W\right||Z_{ks}|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{A_{i}}\eta_{2}^{q}\left[\frac{\mu_{k}^{2}}{(\mu_{k}+|y-\xi_{k}^{\prime}|)^{4}}+\frac{\mu_{i}^{2}}{(\mu_{i}+|y-\xi_{i}^{\prime}|)^{4}}+\varepsilon^{4}|\log\varepsilon|\right]\frac{1}{\mu_{k}+|z_{k}|}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{\kappa}.

Then we get
|I_{2}|\leq C\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{\kappa}
. For
I_{3}
, we have

|I_{3}|\leq\int_{A_{m+1}}\eta_{2}^{q}\left[\frac{\mu_{k}^{2}}{(\mu_{k}+|y-\xi_{k}^{\prime}|)^{4}}+\varepsilon^{4}|\log\varepsilon|^{C}\right]\frac{1}{\mu_{k}+|z_{k}|}dy\leq C\varepsilon.

For the first term
I_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle|I_{1}|\leq

\displaystyle\int_{A_{k}}\eta_{2}^{q}\left|\frac{8\mu_{k}^{2}}{[\mu_{k}^{2}+|z_{k}|^{2}]^{2}}-W\right|\frac{1}{\mu_{k}+|z_{k}|}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{A_{k}}\eta_{2}^{q}\left|\frac{8\mu_{k}^{2}}{[\mu_{k}^{2}+|z_{k}|^{2}]^{2}}-\frac{8\mu_{k}^{2}}{[\mu_{k}^{2}+|y-\xi_{k}^{\prime}|^{2}]^{2}}\right|\frac{1}{\mu_{k}+|y-\xi_{k}^{\prime}|}dy

\displaystyle+C\int_{A_{k}}\eta_{2}^{q}\frac{8\mu_{k}^{2}}{[\mu_{k}^{2}+|y-\xi_{k}^{\prime}|^{2}]^{2}}\left|\frac{|\varepsilon y-q|^{2\alpha}}{|\xi_{k}-q|^{2\alpha}}-1\right|\frac{1}{\mu_{k}+|y-\xi_{k}^{\prime}|}dy

\displaystyle+C\int_{A_{k}}\eta_{2}^{q}\frac{8\mu_{k}^{2}}{[\mu_{k}^{2}+|y-\xi_{k}^{\prime}|^{2}]^{2}}\left[\varepsilon^{\beta}|y-\xi_{k}^{\prime}|^{\beta}+\sum_{k=0}^{m}(\varepsilon\mu_{k})^{\frac{2}{p}-1}\right]\frac{1}{\mu_{k}+|y-\xi_{k}^{\prime}|}dy

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon^{3}\int_{A_{k}}\eta_{2}^{q}\frac{1}{\mu_{k}+|y-\xi_{k}^{\prime}|}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\mu_{k}}\left[(\varepsilon\mu_{k})^{\beta}+\sum_{i=0}^{m}(\varepsilon\mu_{i})^{\frac{2}{p}-1}\right].

As a summary,

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}a(\varepsilon y)L(\eta_{2}^{q}Z_{ks})\phi dy=O\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}\left((\varepsilon\mu_{k})^{\beta}+\sum_{i=0}^{m}(\varepsilon\mu_{i})^{\frac{2}{p}-1}\right)\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\right).

(4.25)

It is easy to get

\left|\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}a(\varepsilon y)\eta_{2}^{q}Z_{ks}hdy\right|\leq\frac{C}{\mu_{k}}\|h\|_{*}

(4.26)

For
i\not=k
, we also get

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}Z_{ij}\eta_{2}^{q}Z_{ks}dy=O\left(\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|^{\kappa}\right).

It is easy to derive that for
i=1,2,\cdots,l
,

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}Z_{ij}\eta_{2}^{q}Z_{is}dy=\delta_{js}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi Z_{1}^{2}dz,

and for
i=l+1,\cdots,m
,

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}Z_{ij}\eta_{2}^{q}Z_{is}dy=\delta_{js}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}\chi Z_{1}^{2}dy+O(\varepsilon\mu_{i}).

According to (
4.23
), (
4.25
) and (
4.26
), we get

|c_{ks}|\leq C\left[\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}\|h\|_{*}+\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}\left((\varepsilon\mu_{k})^{\beta}+\sum_{i=0}^{m}(\varepsilon\mu_{i})^{\frac{2}{p}-1}\right)\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\right].

(4.27)

However, in the case of
k=1,2,\cdots,l
, (
4.27
) still holds. To prove this fact, we multiply the both sides of (
4.1
) by
a(\varepsilon y)\eta_{2}^{q}\mathring{Z}_{ks}
, where
\mathring{Z}_{ks}
is the unique solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta\mathring{Z}_{ks}-\nabla(\log a(\varepsilon y))\cdot\nabla\mathring{Z}_{ks}+\varepsilon^{2}\mathring{Z}_{ks}=-\Delta Z_{ks}-\nabla(\log a(\varepsilon y))\cdot\nabla Z_{ks}+\varepsilon^{2}Z_{ks},&\mbox{in $\Omega_{\varepsilon}$}\\
\frac{\partial\mathring{Z}_{ks}}{\partial n}=0,&\mbox{on $\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}$}\end{array}\right.

Then the estimate (
4.27
) follows from Lemma
A.1
and a similar procedure as above.

Plugging (
4.27
) into (
4.22
), we get

\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C|\log\varepsilon|\left[\|h\|_{*}+\left((\varepsilon\mu_{k})^{\beta}+\sum_{i=0}^{m}(\varepsilon\mu_{i})^{\frac{2}{p}-1}\right)\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right].

For
\varepsilon>0
small enough, we get the prior estimate (
4.21
).

Now we prove the existence and uniqueness of the solution to (
4.4
). Define the Hilbert space

H=\left\{\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\;:\;\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}Z_{ij}\phi dy=0,\;i=1,2,\cdots,m;\;j=1,J_{i}\right\}

with the norm

\|\phi\|=\left[\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\phi^{2}dy\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then (
4.1
) is equivalent to

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}a(\varepsilon y)\left[\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\varphi+\varepsilon^{2}\phi\varphi-W\phi\varphi\right]dy=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}a(\varepsilon y)h\varphi dy.

According to the prior estimate (
4.21
) and Fredholm alternative, we find a unique solution to (
4.1
).

Now we consider the dependence of
T
on
\xi_{kl}^{\prime}
. The function
\psi:=\partial_{\xi_{kl}^{\prime}}\phi
solves the following problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}L(\psi)=\left(\partial_{\xi_{kl}^{\prime}}W\right)\phi+\frac{1}{a(\varepsilon y)}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{J_{i}}\left(\partial_{\xi_{kl}^{\prime}}c_{ij}\right)\chi_{i}Z_{ij}+\frac{1}{a(\varepsilon y)}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{J_{i}}c_{ij}\partial_{\xi_{kl}^{\prime}}\left(\chi_{i}Z_{ij}\right)&\mbox{in $\Omega_{\varepsilon}$},\\
\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial n}=0&\mbox{on $\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}$},\\
\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}Z_{ij}\psi dy=-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\partial_{\xi_{kl}^{\prime}}\left(\chi_{i}Z_{ij}\right)\phi dy,\qquad i=1,\cdots,m;j=1,J_{i}.\end{array}\right.

We define
\tilde{\psi}
by

\tilde{\psi}=\psi+\sum_{t=1}^{m}\sum_{s=1}^{J_{t}}b_{ts}\eta_{t1}^{\xi}Z_{ts},

where the constants
b_{ts}
’s satisfy the following identity

\sum_{s=1}^{J_{t}}b_{is}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}Z_{ij}Z_{is}dy=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\partial_{\xi_{kl}^{\prime}}\left(\chi_{i}Z_{ij}\right)\phi dy.

(4.28)

Then the function
\tilde{\psi}
is a solution of the following problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}L(\tilde{\psi})=\left(\partial_{\xi_{kl}^{\prime}}W\right)\phi+\frac{1}{a(\varepsilon y)}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{J_{i}}\left(\partial_{\xi_{kl}^{\prime}}c_{ij}\right)\chi_{i}Z_{ij}\\
\qquad\qquad+\sum_{t=1}^{m}\sum_{s=1}^{J_{t}}b_{ts}L(\eta_{t1}^{\xi}Z_{ts})+\frac{1}{a(\varepsilon y)}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{J_{i}}c_{ij}\partial_{\xi_{kl}^{\prime}}\left(\chi_{i}Z_{ij}\right)&\mbox{in $\Omega_{\varepsilon}$},\\
\frac{\partial\tilde{\psi}}{\partial n}=0,&\mbox{on $\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}$},\\
\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}Z_{ij}\tilde{\psi}dy=0,\qquad i=1,\cdots,m;j=1,J_{i}.\end{array}\right.

We first give some estimates of the constants
b_{ts}
’s.
For
t\not=i
, we have

\left|\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}Z_{ij}Z_{ts}\right|\leq C\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\frac{\chi_{i}}{\mu_{i}+|y-\xi_{i}^{\prime}|}\frac{1}{\mu_{t}+|y-\xi_{t}^{\prime}|}dy\leq C(\varepsilon\mu_{i})|\log\varepsilon|^{\kappa}.

Whereas, for
t=i
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\chi_{i}Z_{ij}Z_{is}dy=

\displaystyle\int_{|z_{i}|\leq R+1}\chi(|z|)Z_{j}(z)Z_{s}(z)\left(1+O(\varepsilon\mu_{i}|z|)\right)dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{js}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi(|z|)Z_{1}^{2}(z)dz+O(\varepsilon\mu_{i}).

Now we estimate the right hand side of (
4.28
). Since

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{\xi_{kl}^{\prime}}(\chi_{i}Z_{ij})\right|\leq

\displaystyle C\chi\left(\frac{|z_{i}|}{\mu_{i}}\right)\left[\frac{1}{\mu_{i}^{2}}\left|\partial_{\xi_{kl}^{\prime}}\mu_{i}\right|\left|Z_{j}\left(\frac{z_{i}}{\mu_{i}}\right)\right|+\frac{C}{\mu_{i}^{3}}|z_{i}|\left|\nabla Z_{j}\left(\frac{z_{i}}{\mu_{i}}\right)\right|\left|\partial_{\xi_{kl}^{\prime}}\mu_{i}\right|\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{C}{\mu_{i}^{3}}\left|\chi^{\prime}\left(\frac{|z_{i}|}{\mu_{i}}\right)\right|\left|\partial_{\xi_{kl}^{\prime}}\mu_{i}\right||z_{i}|\left|Z_{j}\left(\frac{z_{i}}{\mu_{i}}\right)\right|+\frac{C}{\mu_{i}^{2}}\left|\chi^{\prime}\left(\frac{|z_{i}|}{\mu_{i}}\right)\right|\left|Z_{j}\left(\frac{z_{i}}{\mu_{i}}\right)\right|\left|\partial_{\xi_{kl}^{\prime}}z_{i}\right|

\displaystyle+\frac{C}{\mu_{i}^{2}}\chi\left(\frac{|z_{i}|}{\mu_{i}}\right)\left|\nabla Z_{j}\left(\frac{z_{i}}{\mu_{i}}\right)\right|\left|\partial_{\xi_{kl}^{\prime}}z_{i}\right|,

(4.29)

we get

\left|\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\partial_{\xi_{kl}^{\prime}}\left(\chi_{i}Z_{ij}\right)\phi dy\right|\leq C\left|\partial_{\xi_{kl}^{\prime}}\mu_{i}\right|\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|^{\kappa}\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})},\qquad\mathrm{for}\qquad i\not=k

and

\left|\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\partial_{\xi_{kl}^{\prime}}\left(\chi_{k}Z_{kj}\right)\phi dy\right|\leq C\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

Hence

|b_{ks}|\leq C\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})},\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad|b_{is}|\leq C\varepsilon\mu_{i}|\log\varepsilon|^{\kappa}\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\quad\mathrm{for}\quad i\not=k.

It is easy to see

\partial_{\xi_{kl}^{\prime}}W=\varepsilon^{4+2\alpha}|y-q^{\prime}|^{2\alpha}\left(e^{U_{\xi}(\varepsilon y)}-e^{-U_{\xi}(\varepsilon y)}\right)\partial_{\xi_{kl}^{\prime}}U_{\xi}(\varepsilon y).

From (
3.13
), we get

It is easy to derive

L(\eta_{t1}^{\xi}Z_{ts})=\eta_{t1}^{\xi}L(Z_{ts})-\Delta\eta_{t1}^{\xi}Z_{ts}-2\nabla\eta_{t1}^{\xi}\cdot\nabla Z_{ts}-\nabla(\log a(\varepsilon y))\cdot\nabla\eta_{t1}^{\xi}Z_{ts}.

In the case of

, we get the following estimate from (

L(Z_{ts})=\left(\frac{8\mu_{t}^{2}}{[\mu_{t}^{2}+|z_{t}|^{2}]^{2}}-W\right)Z_{ts}+O\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{(\mu_{t}+|z_{t}|)^{2}}\right)+O\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\mu_{t}+|z_{t}|}\right)

For the first term above, we get

\displaystyle\left|\left(\frac{8\mu_{t}^{2}}{[\mu_{t}^{2}+|z_{t}|^{2}]^{2}}-W\right)Z_{ts}\right|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\mu_{t}+|z_{t}|}\left(\frac{8\mu_{t}^{2}}{[\mu_{t}^{2}+|z_{t}|^{2}]^{2}}-\frac{8\mu_{t}^{2}}{[\mu_{t}^{2}+|y-\xi_{t}^{\prime}|^{2}]^{2}}\frac{|\varepsilon y-q|^{2\alpha}}{|\xi_{t}-q|^{2\alpha}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{C\mu_{t}^{2}}{(\mu_{t}+|y-\xi_{t}^{\prime}|)^{5}}\left(|\varepsilon y-\xi_{t}|^{\beta}+\sum_{k=0}^{m}(\varepsilon\mu_{k})^{\frac{2}{p}-1}\right)+\frac{C\varepsilon^{4}}{\mu_{t}+|y-\xi_{t}^{\prime}|}.

Hence there holds

Using this fact, we get

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{t=1}^{m}\sum_{s=1}^{J_{t}}b_{ts}L(\eta_{t1}^{\xi}Z_{ts})\right\|_{*}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{J_{k}}|b_{ks}|\|L(\eta_{k1}^{\xi}Z_{ks})\|_{*}+\sum_{t\not=k}\sum_{s=1}^{J_{t}}|b_{ts}|\|L(\eta_{t1}^{\xi}Z_{ts})\|_{*}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\mu_{k}}\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{\kappa}\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\mu_{k}}\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

From (
4.21
) and (
4.27
), we have
|c_{ij}|\leq\frac{C}{\mu_{i}}\|h\|_{*}
. Using this fact and (
4.3

and

Hence

\|\tilde{\psi}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq\frac{C|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}{\mu_{k}}\|h\|_{*}.

From the definition of
\tilde{\psi}

\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq\frac{C|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}{\mu_{k}}\|h\|_{*}.

This proposition follows.

\displaystyle\left|-\Delta Z_{k0}-WZ_{k0}+(1-\eta_{k1}^{\xi})W\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}-a_{k0}^{\xi}G(\varepsilon y,\xi_{k})-(\mathring{Z}_{k0}-Z_{k0})\chi_{\{1\leq k\leq l\}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{(\mu_{k}+|y-\xi_{k}^{\prime}|)^{3}}\left[(\varepsilon\mu_{k})^{\beta}+\sum_{l=0}^{m}(\varepsilon\mu_{l})^{\frac{2}{p}-1}\right]+\frac{C\varepsilon^{3}}{\mu_{k}}+(1-\eta_{k1}^{\xi})\frac{C\mu_{k}^{2}}{(\mu_{k}+|y-\xi_{k}^{\prime}|)^{4}}\frac{1+\log|\frac{y-\xi_{k}^{\prime}}{\mu_{k}}|}{\mu_{k}|\log\varepsilon|}.

Hence

\int_{A_{k}}a(\varepsilon y)(\eta_{2}^{q})^{2}\left[-\Delta Z_{k0}-WZ_{k0}+(1-\eta_{k1}^{\xi})W\left(\hat{Z}_{j0}-Z_{j0}\right)\right]\hat{Z}_{j0}=O\left(\frac{\log^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}{\mu_{j}\mu_{k}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}\right).

However, on
A_{j}
, we have

|z_{k}|\geq\frac{1}{2}|y-\xi_{k}^{\prime}|\geq\frac{1}{2}\left[|\xi_{j}^{\prime}-\xi_{i}^{\prime}|-|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|\right]\geq\frac{1}{4\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{\kappa}}

for
\varepsilon
small enough. Then we get

\displaystyle\int_{A_{j}}a(\varepsilon y)(\eta_{2}^{q})^{2}\left[-\Delta Z_{k0}+W\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}-Z_{k0}\right)-W(\mathring{Z}_{k0}-Z_{k0})\chi_{\{1\leq k\leq l\}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\left[Z_{j0}+(1-\eta_{j1}^{\xi})(\hat{Z}_{j0}-Z_{j0})\right]dy=O\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{j}\mu_{k}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}\right)

and

\int_{A_{j}}a(\varepsilon y)(\eta_{2}^{q})^{2}a_{k0}^{\xi}WG(\varepsilon y,\xi_{k})(1-\eta_{j1}^{\xi})(\hat{Z}_{j0}-Z_{j0})dy=O\left(\frac{\log|\log\varepsilon|}{\mu_{j}\mu_{k}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}\right).

For the last term, we get

\displaystyle\int_{A_{j}}a(\varepsilon y)(\eta_{2}^{q})^{2}a_{k0}WG(\varepsilon y,\xi_{k})Z_{j0}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{A_{j}}a(\varepsilon y)(\eta_{2}^{q})^{2}a_{k0}\frac{8\mu_{j}^{2}}{[\mu_{j}^{2}+|z_{j}|^{2}]^{2}}G(\varepsilon y,\xi_{k})Z_{j0}dy

\displaystyle+\int_{A_{j}}a(\varepsilon y)(\eta_{2}^{q})^{2}a_{k0}\left(W-\frac{8\mu_{j}^{2}}{[\mu_{j}^{2}+|z_{j}|^{2}]^{2}}\right)G(\varepsilon y,\xi_{k})Z_{j0}dy.

Using the estimate of
W
, we get

\int_{A_{j}}a(\varepsilon y)(\eta_{2}^{q})^{2}a_{k0}\left(W-\frac{8\mu_{j}^{2}}{[\mu_{j}^{2}+|z_{j}|^{2}]^{2}}\right)G(\varepsilon y,\xi_{k})Z_{j0}dy=O\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{j}\mu_{k}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}\right).

On
A_{j}
, we get
|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{2\kappa}}
. For
\varepsilon
small enough, we have

|z_{0}|\leq 2|y-q^{\prime}|\leq 2\left[|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|+|\xi_{j}^{\prime}-q^{\prime}|\right]\leq 2\left[\frac{1}{\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{2\kappa}}+\frac{d}{\varepsilon}\right]\leq\frac{4d}{\varepsilon}.

Hence

\displaystyle\int_{A_{j}}a(\varepsilon y)(\eta_{2}^{q})^{2}a_{k0}\frac{8\mu_{j}^{2}}{[\mu_{j}^{2}+|z_{j}|^{2}]^{2}}G(\varepsilon y,\xi_{k})Z_{j0}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a(\xi_{j})a_{k0}^{\xi}G(\xi_{j},\xi_{k})\int_{A_{j}}\frac{8\mu_{j}^{2}}{[\mu_{j}^{2}+|z_{j}|^{2}]^{2}}Z_{j0}+O\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{j}\mu_{k}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a(\xi_{j})a_{k0}^{\xi}G(\xi_{j},\xi_{k})\int_{A_{j}}-\Delta_{z_{j}}Z_{j0}dy+O\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{j}\mu_{k}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a(\xi_{j})a_{k0}^{\xi}G(\xi_{j},\xi_{k})\int_{A_{j}}-\Delta Z_{j0}dy+O\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{j}\mu_{k}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-a(\xi_{j})a_{k0}^{\xi}G(\xi_{j},\xi_{k})\int_{\partial A_{j}}\frac{\partial Z_{j0}}{\partial n}d\sigma+O\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{j}\mu_{k}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{j}\mu_{k}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}\right).

For
l\not=k,j,0
, we have the following estimates hold on
A_{l}
:

|y-\xi_{j}^{\prime}|\geq\frac{1}{2\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{\kappa}},\qquad|y-\xi_{k}^{\prime}|\geq\frac{1}{2\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{\kappa}}.

Then we also have
\hat{Z}_{j0}=O\left(\frac{\log|\log\varepsilon|}{\mu_{j}|\log\varepsilon|}\right)
.

\displaystyle-\Delta Z_{k0}+W\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}-Z_{k0}\right)-a_{k0}^{\xi}WG(\varepsilon y,\xi_{k})-W(\mathring{Z}_{k0}-Z_{k0})\chi_{\{1\leq k\leq l\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left(\left(\frac{\mu_{l}^{2}}{(\mu_{l}+|y-\xi_{l}^{\prime}|)^{4}}+\varepsilon^{2}\right)\frac{\log|\log\varepsilon|}{\mu_{k}|\log\varepsilon|}\right).

Then

\displaystyle\int_{A_{l}}a(\varepsilon y)(\eta_{2}^{q})^{2}\left[-\Delta Z_{k0}+W\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}-Z_{k0}\right)-a_{k0}^{\xi}WG(\varepsilon y,\xi_{k})-W(\mathring{Z}_{k0}-Z_{k0})\chi_{\{1\leq k\leq l\}}\right]\hat{Z}_{j0}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left(\frac{\log^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}{\mu_{j}\mu_{k}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}\right).

From the same method, we also get

\displaystyle\int_{A_{0}}a(\varepsilon y)(\eta_{2}^{q})^{2}\left[-\Delta Z_{k0}+W\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}-Z_{k0}\right)-a_{k0}^{\xi}WG(\varepsilon y,\xi_{k})-W(\mathring{Z}_{k0}-Z_{k0})\chi_{\{1\leq k\leq l\}}\right]\hat{Z}_{j0}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left(\frac{\log^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}{\mu_{j}\mu_{k}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}\right)

and

\displaystyle\int_{A_{m+1}}a(\varepsilon y)(\eta_{2}^{q})^{2}\left[-\Delta Z_{k0}+W\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}-Z_{k0}\right)-a_{k0}^{\xi}WG(\varepsilon y,\xi_{k})-W(\mathring{Z}_{k0}-Z_{k0})\chi_{\{1\leq k\leq l\}}\right]\hat{Z}_{j0}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left(\frac{\log^{2}|\log\varepsilon|}{\mu_{j}\mu_{k}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}}\right).

Hence we get (
A.23
).

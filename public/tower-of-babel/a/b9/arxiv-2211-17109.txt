Twist Positivity, L-space knots, and Concordance
Siddhi Krishna and Hugh Morton

Abstract




Abstract.

Many well studied knots can be realized as positive braid knots where the braid word contains a positive full twist; we say that such knots are
twist positive
. Some important families of knots are twist positive, including torus knots, 1-bridge braids, algebraic knots, and Lorenz knots. We prove that if a knot is twist positive, the braid index appears as the third exponent in its Alexander polynomial. We provide a few applications of this result. After observing that most known examples of L-space knots are twist positive, we prove: if
K
is a twist positive L-space knot, the braid index and bridge index of
K
agree. This allows us to provide evidence for Baker’s reinterpretation of the
slice-ribbon conjecture
: that every smooth concordance class contains at most one fibered, strongly quasipositive knot. In particular, we provide the first example of an infinite family of positive braid knots which are distinct in concordance, and where, as
g\to\infty
, the number of hyperbolic knots of genus
g
gets arbitrarily large. Finally, we collect some evidence for a few new conjectures, including the following: the braid and bridge indices agree for any L-space knot.

57K14, 57K45, 57K18 (primary); 57K10 (secondary).

Keywords:
Alexander polynomial, positive braids, braid index, concordance

1.
Introduction

By Alexander’s theorem
[
Ale23
]
, every link in
S^{3}
can be realized as the closure of some braid. Some properties of braids can be ported into important topological properties of their closures. For example, if a braid
\beta
is
homogeneous
(i.e. for every
i
, every
\sigma_{i}
appearing in
\beta
has the same sign), then the closure is a fibered link in
S^{3}

[
Sta78
]
. In a different vein, the closures of
quasipositive braids
are exactly the links arising as the transverse intersections of smooth algebraic curves
f^{-1}(0)\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
(here,
f
is a non-constant polynomial) with the unit sphere
S^{3}\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}

[
Rud83
,
BO01
,
Hay21
]
. Another property of braids with important topological consequences is
twist positivity
:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\beta
be a positive braid braid on
n
strands. If
\beta
can be factored to contain a positive full twist on
n
strands
(
i.e.
\beta=\Delta^{2}\gamma
where
\gamma
is a positive braid word
)
, then
\beta
is a
twist positive
braid on
n
strands. If
K
can be realized as the closure of a twist positive braid on
n
strands, then
K
is a
twist positive
knot on
n
strands.

Figure 1.

A full twist on three strands is seen in
Figure

1
; here,
\Delta^{2}=(\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1})^{3}
. Twist positivity is a well studied property: perhaps most notably, Morton and Franks-Williams proved that if
K
is twist positive on
n
strands, then the braid index of
K
is
n

[
Mor86
,
FW87
]
.

In this paper, we show that twist positivity unifies classical results in knot theory with modern concordance invariants. Our first theorem is about the Alexander polynomial of twist positive knots.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
K
is twist positive on
n
strands. Then the Alexander polynomial of
K
has the form
\Delta_{K}(t)=1-t+t^{n}+t^{n+1}R(t)
, where
R(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
. In particular, the braid index of
K
appears as an exponent in
\Delta_{K}(t)
.

The strength and utility of
1.2
is leveraged by way of the connection between the Alexander polynomial and knot Floer homology: not only does knot Floer homology categorify the Alexander polynomial, but it also provides a suite of powerful knot invariants. We recall: an
L-space knot
in
S^{3}
is a non-trivial knot which admits a positive Dehn surgery to an
L-space
, the closed, connected, oriented 3-manifolds with simple Heegaard Floer homology. For L-space knots, both the Alexander polynomial and knot Floer homology take on a restricted form
[
OS05
, Theorem 1.2]
. This connection leads to our first application of
1.2
.

Theorem 1.3
.

If
K
is a twist positive L-space knot, then its braid index and bridge index agree. Moreover,
K
realizes the minimal braid and bridge indices of its concordance class.

Remark 1.4
.

As we observe in
Section

3
, most explicit families of L-space knots are not only twist positive, but they also happen to be Lorenz knots.

Lorenz knots exhibit rich phenomena from the dynamical, geometric, and braid theoretic perspectives. They have been intensely studied since their introduction in 1963; see, for example,
[
BW83
,
BK09
,
CFK+11
,
Deh15
,
dPP23
]
, or
[
Bir13
]
for a survey. In
Section

3
, we observe L-space Lorenz knots include the well-studied
algebraic knots
,
1-bridge braids

[
Gab90
,
GLV18
]
, and some
twisted torus knots

[
Vaf15
]
, amongst others.

Therefore,
1.3
generalizes results of both Juhász-Miller-Zemke
[
JMZ20
]
(who prove
1.3
for torus knots) and Hom-Lidman-Park
[
HLP22
]
, who use input from bordered Floer homology to prove it for algebraic knots. Our results suggests that twist positivity is the important topological property guiding this behavior.

Birman-Williams
[
BW83
, Section 5]
noticed that Lorenz knots are always twist positive on some number of strands, and conjectured that this quantity was actually the braid index. Indeed, this conjecture follows with the subsequent announcement of the Franks-Williams result
[
FW87
]
. To the best of our knowledge, the bridge index of Lorenz knots has not been investigated.
1.3
proves that often, the bridge index and the braid index for a Lorenz knot agree, hence the Lorenz presentation cannot be minimized further to decrease the bridge index.

1.2
and
1.3
suggest that twist positivity is not only an important 3-dimensional property, but that it also provides some insights into 3.5- and 4-dimensional phenomena. Indeed, we next study concordance. Baker
[
Bak16

observed a novel relationship between concordance properties of fibered knots and Fox’s

, which asks whether every slice knot admits a ribbon presentation in

. In particular, Baker conjectured that

. Baker observes that either his conjecture is true, or the Slice-Ribbon conjecture is false

, but by

Let

Our proof of

There is an explicit infinite family of positive braid knots that are distinct in concordance, where as

While L-space knots are well-studied, a classification remains elusive; nevertheless, collecting their properties remains a central goal. L-space knots are “simple” from many perspectives: they have simplest possible knot Floer homology
[
OS05

, “simple” knot exteriors (their exteriors are realized as mapping tori)

Conjecture 1.8
.

If
K
is an L-space knot, then its braid index and bridge index agree.

This conjecture is true for the most well known families of L-space knots:

.

The Alexander polynomials of L-space knots have been of interest ever since Ozsváth-Szabó proved that the coefficients of these polynomials take values in

Conjecture 1.9

Suppose

\Delta_{K}(t)=1-t+t^{n}+\ldots+t^{2g-n-1}-t^{2g-1}+t^{2g}

If

2.
Twist Positivity and the Alexander Polynomial

This section establishes a relationship between twist positivity and the Alexander polynomial.

1.2
.

If
K
is twist positive, then
\Delta_{K}(t)=1-t+t^{n}+t^{n+1}R(t)
, where
R(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
.

The proof of
1.2
requires the
reduced Burau matrix
,
B(t)
, which is associated to a braid
\beta\in B_{n}
. We quickly recall the relevant details here, and recommend
[
Bir74
]
for a full account.

Let
\beta
be a braid on
n
strands. We write
\displaystyle\beta=\prod_{r=1}^{m}\sigma_{i_{r}}^{\epsilon_{i_{r}}}
, i.e. as the product of Artin generators.

To
\beta
, we can associate its reduced Burau matrix,
B(t)
, which is the product of
(n-1)\times(n-1)
matrices
\sigma_{i}(t)
, where
\sigma_{i}(t)
differs from the
(n-1)\times(n-1)
identity matrix
I_{n-1}
only in row
i
, as in
Figure

2
. (Note that this matrix is truncated appropriately when
i=1,n-1
.) In particular,
\displaystyle B(t)=\prod_{r=1}^{m}\sigma_{i_{r}}^{\epsilon_{i_{r}}}(t)
.

\sigma_{i}(t)=\left(\begin{tabular}[]{ccccccc}1&&&&&&\\
&$\ddots$&&&&&\\
&&1&&&&\\
&&$t$&$-t$&1&&\\
&&&&1&&\\
&&&&&$\ddots$&\\
&&&&&&1\end{tabular}\right)

Figure 2.
The template for the matrices
\sigma_{i}(t)
, the building blocks of the matrix
B(t)
.

Suppose
\widehat{\beta}=K
, a knot. The Alexander polynomial for
K
, denoted
\Delta_{K}(t)
, is derived from
B(t)
as follows:

\frac{\det(I_{n-1}-B(t))}{1-t^{n}}=\frac{\Delta_{K}(t)}{1-t}

In particular,

\Delta_{K}(t)=(1-t)\cdot(\det(I_{n-1}-B(t)))\cdot(1+t^{n}+t^{2n}+\cdots).

We collect some important properties of
B(t)
:

•

In general, the entries in a reduced Burau matrix lie in
\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}]
. However, when
\beta
is a positive braid, the entries of
B(t)
are genuine polynomial entries.

•

Recall: the full twist on
n
strands is denoted by
\Delta^{2}
, where
\Delta^{2}=(\sigma_{1}\ldots\sigma_{n-1})^{n}\approx(\sigma_{n-1}\ldots\sigma_{1})^{n}
. When
\beta=\Delta^{2}
, we have
B(t)=t^{n}I_{n-1}
. In particular, if
\beta
is a twist positive braid, then
\beta=\Delta^{2}\gamma
, so the reduced Burau matrix associated to
\beta
is
B(t)=(t^{n}I_{n-1})(C(t))=t^{n}C(t)
, where
C(t)
is the reduced Burau matrix associated with
\gamma
.

•

Suppose
\beta
is a positive braid on
n
strands. We define the polynomial
q_{B(t)}(x)
as follows:

(2.1)

\displaystyle q_{B(t)}(x):=\det(I_{n-1}-xB(t))=1+a_{1}(t)x+a_{2}(t)x^{2}+\ldots+a_{n-1}(t)x^{n-1}

We claim that the values of
a_{1}(t)
and
a_{n-1}(t)
can be quickly determined from
B(t)
: if we let
p_{B(t)}(x):=\det(xI_{n-1}-B(t))
denote the characteristic polynomial of
B(t)
, then
q_{B(t)}(x)=x^{n-1}p_{B(t)}(x^{-1})
. That is,
q_{B(t)}(x)
is obtained by
p_{B(t)}(x)
by exchanging the coefficients of
x^{i}
and
x^{(n-1)-i}
for each
i\leq\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor
. In particular, in
q_{B(t)}(t)
,
a_{n-1}(t)=(-1)^{n-1}\det(B(t))
and
a_{1}(t)=-\text{tr}(B(t))
.

We observe that substituting
x=t^{n}
into
Equation

2.1
yields:

\displaystyle\det(I_{n-1}-t^{n}B(t))

\displaystyle=1+a_{1}(t)t^{n}+a_{2}(t)t^{2n}+\ldots+t^{n^{2}-n}a_{n-1}(t)

(2.2)

\displaystyle=1+a_{1}(t)t^{n}+t^{n+1}R(t)

where
R(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
collects all the higher order terms.

With these preliminaries established, we can prove the result.

Proof of
1.2
.

Suppose
K=\widehat{\beta}
, where
\beta
is a twist positive braid on
n
strands. Then
\beta=\Delta^{2}\gamma
,
B(t)=t^{n}C(t)
, and by applying the observation of
Equation

2.2
,

\det(I_{n-1}-B(t))=\det(I_{n-1}-t^{n}C(t))=1+a_{1}(t)t^{n}+t^{n+1}R(t)

with
R(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
. In particular, this means that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Delta_{K}(t)

\displaystyle=(1-t)\cdot\left(1+a_{1}(t)t^{n}+t^{n+1}R(t)\right)\cdot(1+t^{n}+t^{2n}+\ldots)

(2.4)

\displaystyle=1-t+(1+a_{1}(t))t^{n}+t^{n+1}S(t)

where
Equation

2.4
is obtained by distributing and rearranging the terms in the righthand side of
Equation

2.3
. Here,
S(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
.

Claim 1
.

Suppose
\widehat{\beta}
is a knot
K
. Then the polynomial
a_{1}(t)
has no constant term, i.e.
a_{1}(0)=0
.

Proof of
1
.

In the paragraph following
Equation

2.1
, we argued that
a_{1}(t)=-\text{tr}(B(t))
. Therefore, to see that
a_{1}(0)=0
, we must show that
\text{tr}(B(0))=0
.

This is straightforward: first, we note that for all
i
,
\sigma_{i}(t)
is an upper triangular matrix. Since the product of upper triangular matrices is also upper triangular, the matrix
B(t)
is also an upper triangular matrix. Moreover, the diagonal entries of
B(t)
are explicitly determined by the diagonal entries of its constituent matrices; more precisely,

\displaystyle\displaystyle[B(t)]_{ii}=\prod_{r=1}^{m}\left[\sigma_{i_{r}}^{\epsilon_{i_{r}}}(t)\right]_{ii}

where
[M]_{ij}
denotes the
(i,j)
entry of the matrix
M
.

Since
\widehat{\beta}
is a knot, then for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
,
\sigma_{i}
appears in
\beta
at least once. Moreover, by the definition of the matrix
\sigma_{i}(t)
,
\left[\sigma_{i}(0)\right]_{ii}=0
. Therefore, for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
,
[B(t)]_{ii}=0
. We deduce that
\text{tr}(B(t))=0
, as desired.
∎

With this claim in hand, we can finish proving
1.2
. In particular, since
a_{1}(0)=0
, then
a_{1}(t)=e_{1}t+e_{2}t^{2}+\ldots+e_{k}t^{k}
. Plugging this into
Equation

2.4
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{K}(t)

\displaystyle=1-t+(1+a_{1}(t))t^{n}+t^{n+1}S(t)

\displaystyle=1-t+(1+(e_{1}t+e_{2}t+\ldots+e_{k}t^{k}))t^{n}+t^{n+1}S(t)

\displaystyle=1-t+t^{n}+t^{n+1}V(t)

for some
V(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
. This is exactly what we wanted to show.
∎

Corollary 2.1
.

If
K
is twist positive on
n
strands, the braid index of
K
appears as an exponent of the Alexander polynomial.

Proof.

If
K
is twist positive on
n
strands, then by
[
FW87
]
, the braid index of
K
is
n
. On the other hand,
1.2
proves that
n
appears as the exponent of the third term in
\Delta_{K}(t)
.
∎

Proof of
1.3

First, we establish that some well-studied families of knots are twist positive:

Proposition 3.1
.

Algebraic knots, 1-bridge braids, and many twisted torus knots are twist positive L-space knots. In fact, something stronger is true: these knots are all L-space Lorenz knots.

Proof.

Birman-Williams proved that every Lorenz knot is twist positive
[
BW83
]
. Moreover, Birman-Kofman
[
BK09
]
showed that Lorenz knots are in correspondence with
T-links
, which are defined to be the closures of positive braids of a particular form:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\tau=(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\ldots\sigma_{p_{1}-1})^{q_{1}}(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\ldots\sigma_{p_{2}-1})^{q_{2}}\ldots(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\ldots\sigma_{p_{s}-1})^{q_{s}}

where
2\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\ldots\leq p_{s}
,
0<q_{i}
for all
i
, and
\tau
is a braid in
B_{p_{s}}
. Thus, to prove
3.1
, it suffices to show that algebraic knots, 1-bridge braids, and twisted torus knots are
T
-links, and also confirm their L-space status.

•

Birman-Williams showed that algebraic knots are Lorenz knots
[
BW83
, Theorem 6.3]
. It is well known that algebraic knots are particular iterated cables of torus knots
[
Bra28
]
; applying Hedden’s results on cabling
[
Hed09
]
, we deduce they are L-space knots.

•

A
1-bridge braid
is a knot
K\subset S^{3}
realized as the closure of a braid
\beta
, where:

\beta=(\sigma_{b}\sigma_{b-1}\ldots\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1})(\sigma_{w-1}\sigma_{w-2}\ldots\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1})^{t}

[
GKL+23
]
proved that all 1-bridge braids admit
T
-link presentations (hence they are twist positive), and
[
GLV18
]
proved they are L-space knots.

•

In
[
Vaf15
]
, Vafaee presents twisted torus knots as the closures of positive braids on
w
strands with the following form:
\rho=(\sigma_{w-1}\sigma_{w-2}\ldots\sigma_{1})^{t}(\sigma_{w-1}\sigma_{w-2}\ldots\sigma_{w-k})^{sk}
, i.e. they are built from a torus link by inserting some full twists into
k
adjacent strands.
[
GKL+23
]
showed that all twisted torus knots admit
T
-link presentations, so they are twist positive;
[
Vaf15
]
proved that for certain parameters, they are L-space knots.

∎

3.1.
Twist positive L-space knots

We begin by proving
1.3
.

1.3
.

If
K
is a twist positive L-space knot, then its braid index and bridge index agree. Moreover,
K
realizes the minimal braid and bridge indices of its concordance class.

Proof.

If
K
is a twist positive L-space knot, then by
1.2
, we have

\Delta_{K}(t)=1-t+t^{n}+t^{n+1}R(t)

By
[
FW87
]
, we know the braid index of
K
is
n
. Moreover, we see that
n-1
appears as a gap in the exponents of
\Delta_{K}(t)
. Juhász-Miller-Zemke
[
JMZ20
]
established a relationship between knot Floer homology and the bridge index, which we now describe. We recall: the
minus version
of knot Floer homology, denoted
HFK^{-}(K)
, is a knot invariant, and has the structure of a finitely generated module over
\mathbb{F}_{2}[v]
. Moreover, there is a (non-canonical) decomposition of
HFK^{-}(K)
as follows:
HFK^{-}(K)\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}[v]\oplus HFK^{-}_{red}(K)
, where
HFK^{-}_{red}(K)
is the
\mathbb{F}_{2}[v]
-torsion submodule of
HFK^{-}(K)
. Juhász-Miller-Zemke defined the knot invariant
Ord_{v}(K)
(i.e the
torsion order
of
K
) to be the minimal integer which annihilates this torsion submodule; for more details, we refer the reader to
[
JMZ20
, Section 3]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
JMZ20
, Corollary 1.9]

If
K
is a knot in
S^{3}
,
Ord_{v}(K)\leq br(K)-1

(
where
Ord_{v}(K)
denotes the torsion order of
K
, an invariant from knot Floer homology
)
.
\Box

Let

\displaystyle n-1

\displaystyle\leq Ord_{v}(K)\leq br(K)-1\leq i(K)-1=n-1

But this means we must have equalities throughout, hence

Next, we prove that twist positive L-space knots realize the minimal braid index of their concordance classes.

(3.4)

4.1.
Preliminaries

We begin with some preliminaries.

Definition 4.1
.

Lemma 4.2

Every L-space knot of braid index three is either a torus knot or a hyperbolic knot.

Proof.

Thurston proved that every knot is either a torus knot, a satellite knot, or hyperbolic. Suppose

[
Vaf15
, Corollary 3.3]
and Lee-Vafaee
[

]

Suppose

is not a torus knot. To show that it is hyperbolic, we must show that it is not a satellite knot, i.e.

does not contain an essential torus in its exterior. Birman and Menasco
[

]
proved that if

is a link in

of braid index three, and

contains an essential, non-peripheral torus in its exterior, then

is the closure of a braid
\beta
which is conjugate to

∎

In the next lemma, we see that it suffices to study

where

Lemma 4.3

Suppose

and

\displaystyle(\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1})^{q}(\sigma_{2})^{2m}

\displaystyle\approx(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2})^{q}(\sigma_{1})^{2m}

\displaystyle=(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2})^{3r}(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2})\sigma_{1}(\sigma_{1})^{2m-1}

\displaystyle=\underline{(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2})^{3r}}\ (\underline{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}})(\sigma_{1})^{2m-1}

\displaystyle=(\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1})^{3r}(\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2})(\sigma_{1})^{2m-1}

\displaystyle=(\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1})^{3r}(\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2})\sigma_{1}(\sigma_{1})^{2m-2}

Since

Proof.

Suppose

.

(2)

k
is odd, with
K_{1}=T(3,3k+1;2)
and
K_{2}=T(3,3(k-1)+1;8)
.

We first prove the lemma for
Case (1)
. If
K_{1}
is concordant to
K_{2}
, then
K=K_{1}\#m(K_{2}^{r})
is slice. The Fox-Milnor criterion says that the Alexander polynomial of
K
would factor in a particular way: in particular,
\Delta_{K}(t)=f(t)f(t^{-1})
. To obstruct a concordance between
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
, we will show that the putative factorization of Alexander polynomial does not exist.

We recall that torus knots have simple Alexander polynomials
[
Rol76
]
: in general,

\displaystyle\Delta_{T(p,q)}(t)=\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}h|p,\ell|q\\
h,\ell\neq 1\end{subarray}}\phi_{\ell h}(t)

where
\phi_{n}(t)
denotes the
n

\Delta_{K_{1}}(t)=\Delta_{T(3,q)}(t)=\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell|q,\ \ell\neq 1\end{subarray}}\phi_{3\ell}(t)
.

In particular,
\Delta_{K_{1}}(t)
factors into a product of cyclotomic polynomials, where there are no repeated factors. Therefore, to prove that
\Delta_{K}(t)
cannot factor as described by the Fox-Milnor criterion, it suffices to prove that
\Delta_{K_{2}}(t)\neq\Delta_{K_{1}}(t)
. We will use the reduced Burau matrix, which we described in
Section

2
. In particular, when a knot is realized as the closure of some braid
B
, we have

\frac{\det(I_{n-1}-B(t))}{1-t^{n}}=\frac{\Delta_{\widehat{\beta}}(t)}{1-t}

K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are realized on
n=3
strands. So, to show that
\Delta_{K_{1}}(t)\neq\Delta_{K_{2}}(t)
, we must prove that:

(4.4)

\displaystyle\det(I_{2}-B_{1}(t))\neq\det(I_{2}-B_{2}(t))

where
B_{1}(t)
and
B_{2}(t)
denote the reduced Burau matrices of
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
, respectively, where
\widehat{\beta_{i}}\approx K_{i}
. Additionally, we notice that
\det(I_{2}-B_{i}(t))=q_{B_{i}(t)}(1)=1-\text{tr}(B_{i}(t))+\det(B_{i}(t))
, where
q_{B(t)}(x)
is the characteristic-type polynomial defined in
Equation

2.1
. Since
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
are both positive 3-braids with the same writhe,
\det(B_{1}(t))=\det(B_{2}(t))
. Therefore, to deduce that (
4.4
) holds, it suffices to prove that
\text{tr}(B_{1}(t))\neq\text{tr}(B_{2}(t))
.

Recall that

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{array}[]{cc}-t&1\\
0&1\end{array}\end{pmatrix}\hskip 56.9055pt\text{ and }\hskip 56.9055pt\sigma_{2}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\

\displaystyle B_{1}(t)=(\sigma_{2}(t)\sigma_{1}(t))^{3k+1}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{array}[]{cc}t^{3}&0\\
0&t^{3}\end{array}\end{pmatrix}^{k}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\begin{array}[]{cc}-t&1\\
-t^{2}&0\end{array}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{array}[]{cc}-t^{3k+1}&t^{3k}\\
-t^{3k+2}&0\end{array}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle B_{2}(t)=(\sigma_{2}(t)\sigma_{1}(t))^{3(k-1)+1}(\sigma_{1})^{6}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{array}[]{cc}t^{3}&0\\
0&t^{3}\end{array}\end{pmatrix}^{k-1}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\begin{array}[]{cc}-t&1\\
-t^{2}&0\end{array}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\begin{array}[]{cc}t^{6}&-t^{5}+t^{4}-t^{3}+t^{2}-t+1\\
0&1\end{array}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=t^{3(k-1)}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\begin{array}[]{cc}-t^{7}&t^{6}-t^{5}+t^{4}-t^{3}+t^{2}-t+1\\
-t^{8}&t^{7}-t^{6}+t^{5}-t^{4}+t^{3}-t^{2}\end{array}\end{pmatrix}

It is immediate that
\text{tr}(B_{1}(t))\neq\text{tr}(B_{2}(t))
. Therefore, we see that the quantities
\det(I_{2}-B_{1}(t))
and
\det(I_{2}-B_{2}(t))
do not agree, thus
\Delta_{K_{1}}(t)\neq\Delta_{K_{2}}(t)
, and
\Delta_{K}(t)
cannot factor as
f(t)f(t^{-1})
.
Therefore, in
Case (1)
, the knots
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
cannot be smoothly concordant.

An analogous argument works for
Case (2)
, which we verify below. Recall that, by
Lemma

4.3
,
T(3,3k+1;2)\approx T(3,3k+2)
. So, in this case, we have:

\displaystyle B_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=(\sigma_{2}(t)\sigma_{1}(t))^{3k+2}=t^{3k}\begin{pmatrix}\begin{array}[]{cc}-t&1\\
-t^{2}&0\end{array}\end{pmatrix}^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-t^{3k+1}\\
t^{3k+3}&-t^{3k+2}\end{array}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle B_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=(\sigma_{2}(t)\sigma_{1}(t))^{3(k-1)+1}(\sigma_{1})^{8}=t^{3(k-1)}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\begin{array}[]{cc}-t&1\\
-t^{2}&0\end{array}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\begin{array}[]{cc}t^{8}&-t^{7}+t^{6}-t^{5}+t^{4}-t^{3}+t^{2}-t+1\\
0&1\end{array}\end{pmatrix}

Once again, the quantities
\text{tr}(B_{1}(t))
and
\text{tr}(B_{2}(t))
cannot agree, so neither can the corresponding Alexander polynomials, and the knots cannot be concordant.
∎

We can now prove our last main result.

1.5

Let
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{T}\cup\mathcal{L}
, where
\mathcal{T}
is the set of all positive torus knots, and
\mathcal{L}
is the set of L-space knots of braid index three. Every concordance contains at most one knot from
\mathcal{S}
.

As we will see, the knot invariants
i(K)
,
\tau(K)
, and
\sigma(K)
, when combined, are sufficient to show that every knot in
\mathcal{S}
lies in a distinct concordance class.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the the set of knots stated above. Every knot
K\in\mathcal{S}
is a twist positive L-space knot, so by
1.3
, they each realize the minimal braid index of their concordance classes. Additionally, notice that
\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{L}=\{T(3,q)\ |\ q\geq 4\}
.

First, we notice that if the positive torus knots
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are concordant to each other, then by
1.3
, they have the same braid index. Therefore,
T_{1}=T(p,q_{1})
and
T_{2}=T(p,q_{2})
for some
p\geq 2,q_{i}\geq 3
. However, if they are concordant, then
\tau(T_{1})=\tau(T_{2})
. Since
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are fibered, strongly quasipositive knots, then by
[
Hed10
]
,
\tau(T_{1})=g(T_{1})
and
\tau(T_{2})=g(T_{2})
.
Since
g(T(r,s))=\frac{(r-1)(s-1)}{2}
, we deduce that
q_{1}=q_{2}
, hence
T_{1}\approx T_{2}
. Therefore, all the elements of
\mathcal{T}
represent distinct concordance classes.

It remains to study how the elements of
\mathcal{L}
interact with each other, and the elements of
\mathcal{T}
. If
K\in\mathcal{L}
, then by
1.3
, the only knots in
\mathcal{S}
to which
K
could be concordant must also have braid index 3, so
K
could only be concordant to another knot in
\mathcal{L}
. Thus, it suffices to show that every
K\in\mathcal{L}
represents a distinct concordance class.

We begin by sorting all knots in
\mathcal{L}
by their
\tau
invariants. Since all knots under consideration are fibered and strongly quasipositive, we can instead recast this as a sorting by Seifert genus. For all
g\geq 3
, there exists a unique torus knot
T(3,q)
of genus
g
. As we proved in
4.5
, there will be
s=\lfloor\frac{q}{3}\rfloor-1
twisted torus knots which could be potentially concordant to it. We enumerate these knots as follows:
K_{0}=T(3,q),\ldots,K_{s}\in\{T(3,4;2(3s)),T(3,4;2(3s+1))\}
, where (as in as in Lemmas
4.14
and
4.15
)
K_{s}
is determined by the congruence class of
q\mod 3
.

By combining Lemmas
4.13
,
4.14
,
4.15
, we see that all the knots potentially concordant to
K_{0}
all have pairwise distinct signatures (hence they must represent pairwise distinct concordance classes), or there is exactly one pair of knots with the same signature; however, if the latter occurs, we can apply
Lemma

4.16
, and deduce that none of the knots with the same
\tau
invariant as
K_{0}
can be concordant to it. Thus, all the knots
K_{0},K_{1},\ldots,K_{s}
must lie in distinct concordance classes.

Therefore, every knot
K\in L
lies in a unique concordance class, and these must be distinct from the classes represented by
\mathcal{T}
. Thus, every element of
\mathcal{S}
represents a distinct concordance class.
∎

1.7
.

There is an explicit infinite family of positive braid knots that are distinct in concordance, where as
g\to\infty
, the number of hyperbolic knots of genus
g
gets arbitrarily large.

Proof.

The explicit infinite family of positive braid knots is the family
\mathcal{S}
from
1.5
.
The statement follows by combining
1.5
,
Lemma

4.2
, and
4.5
.
∎

5.
Discussion

In this section, we justify some of the claims we made in the introduction. Namely, we provide evidence for
1.8
:
for any L-space knot, the braid index and bridge index agree
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
K_{n},n\geq 1
be the infinite family of hyperbolic L-space knots defined by Baker-Kegel
[
BK24
, Section 2]
. Then, for all
n
,
br(K_{n})=i(K_{n})=4
.

Proof.

Baker-Kegel define the knot
K_{n}
to be to be the closure of the 4-braid
\beta_{n}
, where

\beta_{n}=(\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{3}\sigma_{2})^{2n+1}{\sigma_{1}}^{-1}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}

In particular, for all
n\geq 1
,
i(K_{n})\leq 4
. By inspecting
[
BK24
, Proposition 3.1 (3)]
, we see that

\Delta_{K_{n}}(t)=1-t+t^{4}+t^{5}+\ldots+t^{4n+2}+\ldots-t^{8n+3}+t^{8n+2}

In particular, we see that 3 appears as a gap in the exponents of the Alexander polynomial of these knots. Thus, as in the proof of
1.3
,

3\leq Ord_{v}(K_{n})\leq br(K_{n})-1\leq i(K_{n})-1\leq 3

In particular, we must have equalities throughout, and
br(K_{n})=i(K_{n})=4
.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

If
1.8
holds for an L-space knot
K
, then it holds for all of its cables.

Proof.

Schultens
[
Sch07
]
reproves a classical result of Schubert
[
Sch54
]
: suppose
S
is a satellite knot with companion
J
and pattern
P
of wrapping number
k
(the wrapping number is the minimal geometric intersection number of the pattern with a meridional disk of the pattern torus), then
br(S)\geq k\cdot br(J)
. In particular, if
K_{p,q}
is a cable of
K
, then
br(K_{p,q})\geq p\cdot br(K)
. Williams
[
Wil92
]
proved that analogous result holds the braid index:
i(K_{p,q})=p\cdot i(K)
. Therefore, we have:

(5.1)

\displaystyle p\cdot br(K)\leq br(K_{p,q})\leq i(K_{p,q})=p\cdot i(K)

If
K
is an L-space knot with
i(K)=br(K)
, then we have equalities throughout
Equation

5.1
, proving the claim.
∎

We hope these observations provide some motivation for investigating
1.8
further.

On equivariant class formulas for t t -modules
Tiphaine Beaumont

Abstract
. We obtain an equivariant class formula for z z -deformation of t t -modules. Under mild conditions, it allows us to get an equivariant class formula for t t -modules.

2.3.

In this section, we recall some statements of

We set

We have the ring isomorphism:

comes from the evaluation at

of

.

As
\left[\widetilde{N}:U(\widetilde{E}(\widetilde{M}))\right]_{\widetilde{A}[G]}\subset\left[\Lie_{\widetilde{E}}(\widetilde{M}):\widetilde{N}\right]_{\widetilde{A}[G]}^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{G}(\widetilde{E}(\widetilde{M}))
, we have

It implies that
[N:\Lambda]_{A[G]}[\Lie_{E}(M):N]_{A[G]}\subset\mathcal{L}_{G}(E(M))
.

Thus we have

[\Lie_{E}(M):\Lambda]_{A[G]}\subset\mathcal{L}_{G}(E(M)).

Moreover, we have
[\Lie_{E}(M):U_{St}(E(M))]_{A}=\mathcal{L}(E(M)).

By Remarks
6.9
, it implies that

\displaystyle\det_{G}\left(\dfrac{[\Lie_{E}(M):\Lambda]_{A[G]}}{\mathcal{L}_{G}(E(M))}\right)=\dfrac{[\Lie_{E}(M):\Lambda]_{A}}{\mathcal{L}(E(M))}=[U_{St}(E(M)):\Lambda]_{A}.

∎

Remark 6.11
.

In particular, if

is a projective

-module.

We recall that for

Proposition 6.12
.

Let

We have the exact sequence

Corollary 6.14
.

We denote
N=\Tr_{G}(M)
. If
H(E(N))
is trivial, then
U(E(M))
and
U_{St}(E(M))
are projective
A[G]
-modules. We have

[\Lie_{E}(M):U_{St}(E(M))]_{A[G]}=\mathcal{L}_{G}(E(M)).

Furthermore,

[\Lie_{E}(M):U(E(M))]_{A[G]}|H(E(M))|_{A[G]}=\mathcal{L}_{G}(E(M)).

Proof.

We have supposed
H(E(N))
trivial. So we have
\exp_{E}(K_{\infty}^{n})+N^{n}=K^{n}_{\infty}
. First we show that
H(E(M))
is
G
-cohomologically trivial.

We have the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow M^{n}+\exp_{E}(L_{\infty}^{n})\longrightarrow L^{n}_{\infty}\longrightarrow H(E(M))\longrightarrow 0.

As

is

-cohomologically trivial, to show that

is

We have

\Tr_{G}(M^{n}+\exp_{E}(L_{\infty}^{n}))=N^{n}+\exp_{E}(K_{\infty}^{n})=K^{n}_{\infty}.

Furthermore, if we look at the

The following inclusion

So we have
\widehat{H}^{0}(G,M^{n}+\exp_{E}(L_{\infty}))=\{0\}.
By

, Thm. 6 p112]

is

is

-cohomologically trivial. By Proposition

It implies that

, we have

\dfrac{1}{[\Lie_{E}(M):U(E(M))]_{A[G]}}\mathcal{L}_{G}(E(M))\in\Fitt_{A[G]}(H(E(M)).

.

.
As the same way as before, it gives us

∎

7.
An exemple of an Artin-Schreier extension

In this section, we will see an example of a
L
-function associated to a lattice which is not contained in the ring of integers of an Artin–Schreier extension of a function field.
For this section, we take
q=p
.

First, we recall the definition of the
d
-th power residue symbol
.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
b\in A
,
P\in A
be an irreducible polynomial and
d
a divisor of
p-1
.

•

If
P\not|b
, we set
\left(\dfrac{b}{P}\right)_{d}
the unique element of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
such that

b^{\frac{p^{\deg(P)}-1}{d}}\equiv\left(\dfrac{b}{P}\right)_{d}\mod P.

•

If
P|b
, we set
\left(\dfrac{b}{P}\right)_{d}=0.

It can be extended to all non zero elements of
A
. Let
c=sgn(c)\prod_{i=1}^{s}P_{i}^{f_{i}}
be the prime decomposition of
c\in A^{*}
. Then for
b\in A
, the
d
-th power residue symbol is defined as

\left(\dfrac{b}{c}\right)_{d}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}\left(\dfrac{b}{P_{i}}\right)_{d}^{f_{i}}.

We refer the reader to
[
18
, Chapter 3]
for more details.

Let
C
be the Carlitz module : the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-morphism such that
C_{\theta}=\theta+\tau
.

We can write

C_{P}(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\deg(P)}[P,i]x^{p^{i}}

where
[P,i]\in A
,
[P,\deg(P)]=1
and
\forall i\in[\![0;\deg(P)-1|]\!],P~|~[P,i]
(see
[
18
, Chapter 12]
).
Thus
C_{P}(x)\equiv x^{p^{\deg(P)}}\mod P
.

Let
Q(x)=x^{p}-x-\dfrac{1}{\theta}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[\theta]
. We denote by
L
the decomposition field of
Q
i.e.,
L=\mathbb{F}_{p}(\alpha)
where
\alpha
is a root of
P
. We set
G=\Gal(L/k)
. We can see that it is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

By
[
8
, Theorem 2.1]
, we can show that
\mathcal{O}_{L}=A\oplus_{i=1}^{p-1}\theta A\alpha^{i}
and
\theta
is the only prime which is ramified in
L/k
. Now, we want to prove that
\theta\alpha^{p-1}
is a normal basis. For
i\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
,

\sigma_{i}(\theta\alpha^{p-1})=\theta(\alpha+i)^{p-1}=\theta\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\binom{p-1}{j}i^{j}\alpha^{p-1-j}.

If we express
\sigma_{i}(\theta\alpha^{p-1})
in function of
1
,
\theta\alpha
,
\theta\alpha^{2}
, …,
\theta\alpha^{p-1}
, we obtain the following matrix

\begin{pmatrix}0&\theta\binom{p-1}{p-1}&\theta\binom{p-1}{p-1}2^{p-1}&\ldots&\theta\binom{p-1}{p-1}(p-1)^{p-1}\\
0&\binom{p-1}{p-2}&\binom{p-1}{p-2}2^{p-2}&\ldots&\binom{p-1}{p-2}(p-1)^{p-2}\\
0&\binom{p-1}{p-3}&\binom{p-1}{p-3}2^{p-3}&\ldots&\binom{p-1}{p-3}(p-1)^{p-3}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
0&\binom{p-1}{1}&\binom{p-1}{1}2&\ldots&\binom{p-1}{1}(p-1)\\
1&1&1&\ldots&1\\
\end{pmatrix}
.
As its determinant is invertible in
k
, we have
L=\theta k[G]\alpha^{p-1}
.
We set
M=\theta A[G]\alpha^{p-1}
. From this matrix, we obtain
M=\oplus_{i=0}^{p-1}\theta A\alpha^{i}
.
We denote
\delta=\sqrt[p-1]{1+\theta^{p-1}}
and
M^{\prime}=M\delta=\theta A[G]\alpha^{p-1}\delta
.

By choice of
\delta
, we can remark that
M^{\prime}\not\subset\mathcal{O}_{L}
and
M^{\prime}
is an almost taming module for
L/k
.

First, we will look at the
\mathcal{L}
-function attached to
M^{\prime}
.

We have
(\delta\theta\alpha^{p-1})^{p}=\delta^{p}\theta^{p}(\alpha+\dfrac{1}{\theta})^{p-1}=(1+\theta^{p-1})\delta\theta(\theta\alpha+1)^{p-1}.
So
\tau(\theta\delta\alpha^{p-1})\equiv\theta\delta\mod\theta M^{\prime}.
By
[
8
, Lemma 2.3]

\Tr_{G}(\theta\alpha^{p-1})=-\theta
. Therefore,
C(M^{\prime}/\theta M^{\prime})
is annihilated by
\theta+\Tr_{G}
.

Let
P\in\Spec(A)\setminus\theta
and
\sigma_{P}
be a Frobenius associated to
P
.

We recall that
C_{P}\equiv\tau^{\deg(P)}\mod P
. Then we have

\displaystyle C_{P}(\theta\delta\alpha^{p-1})

\displaystyle\equiv\theta^{p^{\deg(P)}}\delta^{p^{\deg(P)}}(\alpha^{p-1})^{p^{\deg(P)}}\mod P

\displaystyle\equiv\theta(1+\theta^{p-1})^{\frac{p^{\deg(P)}}{p-1}}\sigma_{P}(\alpha^{p-1})\mod P

\displaystyle\equiv\theta\delta\left(\dfrac{1+\theta^{p-1}}{P}\right)_{p-1}\sigma_{P}(\alpha^{p-1})\mod P.

Thus we obtain
|C(M^{\prime}/PM^{\prime})|_{G}=P1_{G}-\left(\dfrac{1+\theta^{p-1}}{P}\right)_{p-1}\sigma_{P}
.
It implies that we get the following equivariant
\mathcal{L}
-function :

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{G}(C(M^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\dfrac{|\Lie_{C}(M^{\prime}/\theta M^{\prime})|_{G}}{|C(M^{\prime}/\theta M^{\prime})|_{G}}\prod\limits_{P\in\MSpec(A)\setminus\{\theta\}}\dfrac{|\Lie_{C}(M^{\prime}/PM^{\prime})|_{G}}{|C(M^{\prime}/PM^{\prime})|_{G}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\theta}{\theta+\Tr_{G}}\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\MSpec(A)\setminus\{\theta\}\end{subarray}}\dfrac{P}{P-\left(\tfrac{1+\theta^{p-1}}{P}\right)_{p-1}\sigma_{P}}

\displaystyle=(1-\dfrac{\Tr_{G}}{\theta})\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in A^{+},(a,\theta)=1\\
(a,1+\theta^{p-1})=1\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\left(\tfrac{1+\theta^{p-1}}{a}\right)_{p-1}\sigma_{a}}{a}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in A^{+},(a,\theta)=1\\
(a,1+\theta^{p-1})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\left(\tfrac{1+\theta^{p-1}}{a}\right)_{p-1}\sigma_{a}}{a}-\left(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in A^{+},(a,\theta)=1\\
(a,1+\theta^{p-1})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\left(\tfrac{1+\theta^{p-1}}{a}\right)_{p-1}}{a\theta}\right)\Tr_{G}.

As
k_{\infty}=A\oplus\theta^{-1}\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![\theta^{-1}]\!]
, we have
L_{\infty}=M^{\prime}\oplus\oplus_{i=0}^{p-1}\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![\dfrac{1}{\theta}]\!]\delta\alpha^{i}
and
\oplus_{i=0}^{p-1}\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![\dfrac{1}{\theta}]\!]\delta\alpha^{i}\subset\exp_{C}(L_{\infty})
thus
H(C(M^{\prime}))=\{0\}
.
It follows that
U_{St}(C(M^{\prime}))=U(C(M))
and it is
A[G]
-projective. Then

[M:U(C(M))]_{A[G]}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in A^{+},(a,\theta)=1\\
(a,1+\theta^{p-1})=1\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\left(\tfrac{1+\theta^{p-1}}{a}\right)_{p-1}\sigma_{a}}{a}-\left(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in A^{+},(a,\theta)=1\\
(a,1+\theta^{p-1})=1\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\left(\tfrac{1+\theta^{p-1}}{a}\right)_{p-1}}{a\theta}\right)\Tr_{G}.

Now, we look at the
\mathcal{L}
-function attached to
\widetilde{M}
.

We have the isomorphism of
\widetilde{A}[G]
-modules
\dfrac{\widetilde{M^{\prime}}}{P\widetilde{M^{\prime}}}\cong\dfrac{M^{\prime}}{PM^{\prime}}[z]\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[z]}\mathbb{F}_{p}(z)
.
As
\dfrac{M^{\prime}}{PM^{\prime}}[z]
is a free

\dfrac{A}{PA}[z][G]
-module, we get

|\dfrac{\widetilde{M^{\prime}}}{P\widetilde{M}}|_{G}=P1_{G}
.

By the same arguments used previously, we have
\widetilde{C}_{P}\equiv z^{\deg(P)}\tau^{\deg(P)}\mod P
.
As the same way, we can show that for
P\in\Spec(A)\setminus\theta
,
|\widetilde{C}(\widetilde{M^{\prime}}/P\widetilde{M^{\prime}})|_{G}=P1_{G}-z^{\deg(P)}\left(\dfrac{1+\theta^{p-1}}{P}\right)_{p-1}\sigma_{P}
and
|\widetilde{C}(\widetilde{M^{\prime}}/\theta\widetilde{M^{\prime}})|_{G}=\theta 1_{G}+z\Tr_{G}
.

So we obtain the
\mathcal{L}
-function :

\tiny{\mathcal{L}_{G}(\widetilde{C}(\widetilde{M^{\prime}}))=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in A^{+},(a,\theta)=1\\
(a,1+\theta^{p-1})=1\end{subarray}}\tfrac{z^{\deg(a)}\left(\tfrac{1+\theta^{p-1}}{a}\right)_{p-1}\sigma_{a}}{a}-\left(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in A^{+},(a,\theta)=1\\
(a,1+\theta^{p-1})=1\end{subarray}}\tfrac{z^{\deg(a)+1}\left(\tfrac{1+\theta^{p-1}}{a}\right)_{p-1}}{a\theta}\right)\Tr_{G}.}

To finish this exemple, we look at the
\mathcal{L}
-functions associated to
N
where
N=\Tr_{G}(M^{\prime})
. As
\Tr_{G}(\theta\alpha^{p-1})=-\theta
by
[
8
, Lemma 2.3]
,
N=\theta A\delta
.
For a prime that does not divide
1+\theta^{p-1}
,
C(N/PN)
is annihilated by
P-\left(\dfrac{1+\theta^{p-1}}{P}\right)_{p-1}
. Indeed, for
x\in N
, we can write
x=\theta a\delta
with

. Thus

\displaystyle C_{P-\left(\tfrac{1+\theta^{p-1}}{P}\right)_{p-1}}(x)

\displaystyle\equiv x^{p^{\deg(P)}}-\left(\dfrac{1+\theta^{p-1}}{P}\right)_{p-1}a\theta\delta\mod P

\displaystyle\equiv a\theta\delta^{p^{\deg(P)}}-\theta a\sqrt[p-1]{1+\theta^{p-1}}\sqrt[p-1]{1+\theta^{p-1}}^{p^{\deg(P)}-1}\mod P

\displaystyle\equiv 0\mod P.

As the same way, we can show that if
P
divides
1+\theta^{p-1}
, then
C(N/PN)
is annihilated by
P
.
We obtain the
\mathcal{L}
-function :

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(C(N))

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{P\in\MSpec(A)}\dfrac{|\Lie_{C}(N/PN)|_{A}}{|C(N/PN)|_{A}}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\MSpec(A)\\
(P,1+\theta^{p-1})=1\end{subarray}}\dfrac{P}{P-\left(\frac{1+\theta^{p-1}}{P}\right)_{p-1}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in A^{+}\\
(b,1+\theta^{p-1})=1\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\left(\frac{1+\theta^{p-1}}{b}\right)_{p-1}}{b}.

As
k_{\infty}=A\oplus\theta^{-1}\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![\theta^{-1}]\!]=N\oplus\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![\theta^{-1}]\!]b
and
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![\theta^{-1}]\!]\delta\subset\exp_{C}(k_{\infty})
, we obtain

. It means that
U(C(N))=U_{St}(N)
and

U_{St}(C(\theta\mathbb{F}_{p}[\theta]\sqrt[p-1]{1+\theta^{p-1}}))=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in A^{+}\\

As the same way, it means that for

\mathcal{L}(\widetilde{C}(\widetilde{N}))=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in A^{+}\\

U(\widetilde{C}(\theta\mathbb{F}_{q}(z)[\theta]\delta)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in A^{+}\\

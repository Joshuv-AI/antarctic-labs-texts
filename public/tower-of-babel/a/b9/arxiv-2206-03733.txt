Number of integers represented by families of binary forms
Étienne Fouvry, Michel Waldschmidt

Abstract
We extend our previous results on the number of integers which are values of some cyclotomic form of degree larger than a given value (see [ FW ] ), to more general families of binary forms with integer coefficients. Our main ingredient is an asymptotic upper bound for the cardinality of the set of values which are common to two non–isomorphic binary forms of degree greater than 3 3 . We apply our results to some typical examples of families of binary forms.

Introduction

Let
d\geq 3
be an integer. We denote by
{\mathrm{Bin}}(d,{\mathbb{Z}})

the set of binary forms
F=F(X,Y)
with integer coefficients, of degree
d
and with discriminant different from
zero. For

(1.1)

\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&a_{2}\\
a_{3}&a_{4}\end{pmatrix}\in{\mathrm{GL}}(2,{\mathbb{Q}}),

and
F\in{\mathrm{Bin}}(d,{\mathbb{Z}})
,
F\circ\gamma
is the binary form with rational coefficients, defined by

\left(F\circ\gamma\right)(X_{1},X_{2})=F(a_{1}X_{1}+a_{2}X_{2},a_{3}X_{1}+a_{4}X_{2}).

Two elements
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
in
{\mathrm{Bin}}(d,{\mathbb{Z}})
are said to be
isomorphic
is there is a
\gamma\in{\mathrm{GL}}(2,{\mathbb{Q}})
such that

F_{1}\circ\gamma=F_{2}.

To estimate the number of values simultaneously taken by the binary forms
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
, we introduce the counting function, for
N
an integer
\geq 1
,

(1.2)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}(F_{1},F_{2};N)

\displaystyle:=\sharp\left(F_{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2})\cap F_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2})\cap[-N,+N]\right)

\displaystyle=\sharp\bigl\{m:|m|\leq N,\text{ there exists }(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{4}

\displaystyle\text{ such that }m=F_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})=F_{2}(x_{3},x_{4})\bigr\}.

Our first result gives an upper bound for this function when the two forms are not isomorphic.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every
d\geq 3
, there is a constant
\vartheta_{d}<2/d
such that, for every
\varepsilon>0
, for every pair

(F_{1},F_{2})
of no–isomorphic forms of
{\mathrm{Bin}}(d,{\mathbb{Z}})
, for
N\to\infty
, one has the bound

(1.3)

{\mathcal{N}}\left(F_{1},F_{2};N\right)=O_{F_{1},F_{2},\varepsilon}\left(N^{\vartheta_{d}+\varepsilon}\right).

This theorem calls for the following comments:

Remark 1.2
.

The point in this theorem is that the constant
\vartheta_{d}
, defined in (
2.1
), satisfies the inequality
\vartheta_{d}<2/d
(see the inequalities
(
2.3
) below). In fact, it is known that for any
F\in{\mathrm{Bin}}(d,{\mathbb{Z}})
, there exists
C_{F}>0
, such that, for
N
tending to infinity, one has the equality

{\mathcal{N}}(F,F;N)=\left(C_{F}+o_{F}(1)\right)N^{2/d},

(see Theorem
A
in §
1.1
, due to Stewart and Xiao
[
SX
, Theorem 1.1]
).

Remark 1.3
.

The explicit value of
\vartheta_{d}
given in (
2.1
) leads to the inequality
\vartheta_{d}>1/d
for all
d\geq 3
(see (
2.3
)). It also shows that
\vartheta_{d}
is asymptotic
to
1/d
as
d\to\infty
. This value is asymptotically optimal as shown by the two forms

F_{1}(X,Y)=X^{d}+Y^{d}\text{ and }F_{2}(X,Y)=X^{d}+2Y^{d}.

These two forms are not isomorphic. From the equalities
F_{1}(n,0)=F_{2}(n,0)=n^{d}
, we deduce the lower bound

{\mathcal{N}}(F_{1},F_{2};N)\geq N^{1/d}\ (N\geq 1).

Remark 1.4
.

According to
[
FW
, Corollaire 3.3]
, if the two forms
F_{1}
,
F_{2}
are positive definite and at least one of them is not divisible by a linear form with rational coefficients, then the exponent
\vartheta_{d}
in the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
can be replaced by
\eta_{d}
with
\eta_{d}<\vartheta_{d}
(see the definition of
\eta_{d}
and
\vartheta_{d}
in §
2.1
).

Remark 1.5
.

We will show in §
2.4
that the exponent
\vartheta_{d}
in the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
can be replaced by the coefficient
\eta_{d}
quoted in the previous remark when the binary form
F_{1}(X,Y)F_{2}(X,Y)
has no real root.

Remark 1.6
.

Theorem
1.1
is no more valid for
d=2
. This is well known: see for instance
[
FLW
, Prop. 6.1, eq. (6.3)]
, where, choosing

F_{1}(X,Y)=X^{2}+Y^{2}
and
F_{2}(X,Y)=X^{2}+XY+Y^{2}
, one has, for
B
tending to infinity, the asymptotic formula

{\mathcal{N}}(F_{1},F_{2};B)=\left(\beta_{0}+o(1)\right)B\big(\log B)^{-3/4},

for some constant
\beta_{0}>0
.

Remark 1.7
.

Theorem
1.1
is immediately generalized to binary forms with rational coefficients, it suffices to multiply by a common denominator.

Remark 1.8
.

The following proposition shows that if
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are isomorphic, the equality (
1.3
) never holds.

Proposition 1.9
.

Let
d\geq 3
and let
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
be two isomorphic binary forms in
{\mathrm{Bin}}(d,{\mathbb{Z}})
. Then there is a positive constant
C_{F_{1},F_{2}}
, such that, for
N
tending to infinity, we have
the inequality

{\mathcal{N}}(F_{1},F_{2};N)\geq\left(C_{F_{1},F_{2}}-o_{F_{1},F_{2}}(1)\right)N^{2/d}.

Proof.

Let
\gamma
as in (
1.1
) such that
F_{1}=F_{2}\circ\gamma
. Let
D\geq 1
be an integer such that
(Da_{1},Da_{2},Da_{3},Da_{4})
belongs to
{\mathbb{Z}}^{4}
. By homogeneity, we deduce that the two forms

G_{1}(X_{1},X_{2}):=F_{1}(DX_{1},DX_{2})

and

G_{2}(X_{1},X_{2}):=F_{2}\left(Da_{1}X_{1}+Da_{2}X_{2},Da_{3}X_{1}+Da_{4}X_{2}\right)

are equal. So we have the equality of their images

G_{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2})=G_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}).

We also have the obvious inclusions

G_{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2})\subset F_{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2})\text{ and }G_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2})\subset F_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}),

which lead to the inclusion

(1.4)

G_{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2})\subset F_{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2})\cap F_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2}).

A new application of the result of Stewart and Xiao (see Theorem
A
below) gives, for some
constant
C_{G_{1}}>0
, the equality

(1.5)

{\mathcal{N}}(G_{1},G_{1};N)=\left(C_{G_{1}}+o_{G_{1}}(1)\right)N^{2/d},

as
N
tends to infinity. Gathering (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) we obtain the inequality claimed in Proposition
1.9
.
∎

Theorem
1.1
is an important tool for our generalisation of our previous study in
[
FW
]
,
where we produced an asymptotic formula for the number of values
m
, with
|m|\leq B
taken by some cyclotomic form
\Phi_{n}
of degree
\varphi(n)
greater than a fixed
d\geq 3
.
Recall that
\varphi
is the Euler function and that to the
n
–th cyclotomic polynomial
\phi_{n}(X)
, of degree
\varphi(n)
, is attached the
cyclotomic form

\Phi_{n}(X,Y):=Y^{\varphi(n)}\cdot\phi_{n}(X/Y)
.

Our purpose is to study the following general problem:

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an infinite subset of
\displaystyle{\underset{d\geq 3}{\bigcup}{\mathrm{Bin}}(d,{\mathbb{Z}})}
, satisfying natural properties. Let
A
be a fixed non negative integer. As
B
tends to infinity, estimate the counting function

(1.6)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}_{\geq d}\left(\mathcal{F},B,A\right):=\sharp\,\bigl\{m:0\leq|m|\leq B,\,\text{ there is }\,F\in\mathcal{F}\text{ with }\deg F\geq d

\displaystyle\text{ and }(x,y)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\text{ with }\max\{|x|,|y|\}\geq A,\text{ such that }F(x,y)=m\bigr\}.

The introduction of the parameter
A
may be seen as artificial. It is enacted to prevent from the following phenomenon encountered for instance in the case of the family of cyclotomic forms
\Phi_{n}
, where, for every prime
p
,
we have

(1.7)

\Phi_{p}(1,1)=p

(recall that
\Phi_{p}(X,Y)=(X^{p}-Y^{p})\big/(X-Y)
). We wish to avoid counting these values, since the set of primes, by its cardinality, completely hides the set of other values
\Phi_{n}(x,y)

when
\max\{|x|,|y|\}\geq 2
and
\varphi(n)\geq d
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a set of binary forms. We denote by
\mathcal{F}_{d}
the subset of forms of
\mathcal{F}
of degree
d
. We will study the set of values taken by forms belonging to some

(A,A_{1},d_{0},d_{1},\kappa)
–regular
families
\mathcal{F}
, that we define as follows.

Definition 1.10
.

Let
A
,
A_{1}
,
d_{0}
,
d_{1}
be integers and let
\kappa
be a real number such that

(1.8)

A\geq 1,\,A_{1}\geq 1,\,d_{1}\geq d_{0}\geq 0,\,0<\kappa<A.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a set of binary forms. We say that
\mathcal{F}
is
(A,A_{1},d_{0},d_{1},\kappa)
–regular
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

The set
\mathcal{F}
is infinite,

(ii)

We have the inclusion

\mathcal{F}\subset\bigcup_{d\geq 3}{\mathrm{Bin}}(d,{\mathbb{Z}}),

(iii)

For all
d\geq 3
, one has the inequality
\sharp\mathcal{F}_{d}\leq d^{A_{1}}
,

(iv)

Two forms of
\mathcal{F}
are isomorphic if and only if they are equal,

(v)

For any
d\geq\max\{d_{1},d_{0}+1\}
, the following holds

\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}F\in\mathcal{F}_{d},\\
(x,y)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\text{ and }F(x,y)\neq 0,\\
\max\{|x|,|y|\}\geq A,\end{array}\right\}\Rightarrow\max\{|x|,|y|\}\leq\kappa\left|F(x,y)\right|^{\frac{1}{d-d_{0}}}.

The upper bound in the right hand side of (v) is trivial for
\max\{|x|,|y|\}\leq\kappa
, this is why we request
A>\kappa
.

The family of cyclotomic forms

\boldsymbol{\Phi}:=\{\Phi_{n}:\varphi(n)\geq 4,\;n\not\equiv 2\pmod{4}\}

satisfies the assumptions (i), (ii), (iii), (iv), but
is not
(1,A_{1},d_{0},d_{1},\kappa)
–regular for any value of
A_{1}
,
d_{0}
,
d_{1}
and
\kappa
, since (
1.7
) shows that (v) is not satisfied. However
\boldsymbol{\Phi}
is
(2,2,0,4,2/\sqrt{3})
–regular, this is a consequence of
[
FW
, Théorème 4.10]
and of the classical inequality
n/(\log\log n)<\varphi(n)<n
.

1.1
Some facts on a single form

Before stating our main result concerning
\mathcal{R}_{\geq d}(\mathcal{F},B,A)
defined in (
1.6
), we recall some fundamental objects attached to a binary form
F\in{\mathrm{Bin}}(d,{\mathbb{Z}})

when
d\geq 3
:

•

The
fundamental domain
of
F
is

\mathcal{D}(F):=\left\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:|F(x,y)|\leq 1\right\},

•

The
area of the fundamental domain of
F
is the real number

(1.9)

A_{F}:=\iint_{\mathcal{D}(F)}{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}y.

We always have
0<A_{F}<\infty
.

•

The
group of automorphisms
of
F
is

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Aut}}

\displaystyle(F;{\mathbb{Q}}):=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&a_{2}\\
a_{3}&a_{4}\end{pmatrix}\in{\mathrm{GL}}(2,{\mathbb{Q}}):F(X,Y)=F(a_{1}X+a_{2}Y,a_{3}X+a_{4}Y)\right\}.

This is a finite subgroup of
{\mathrm{GL}}(2,{\mathbb{Q}})
.
We now recall the important result of Stewart and Xiao, that we already mentioned above
[
SX
, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2]
:

Theorem A
.

For every
d\geq 3
, there is a constant
\kappa_{d}<2/d
such that, for all
F\in{\mathrm{Bin}}(d,{\mathbb{Z}})
and for all
\varepsilon>0
, the following equality

\mathcal{N}(F,F;B)=A_{F}\cdot W_{F}\cdot B^{2/d}+O_{F,\varepsilon}\left(B^{\kappa_{d}+\varepsilon}\right),

holds uniformly for
B\to\infty
, where
W_{F}=W\left({\mathrm{Aut}}(F;{\mathbb{Q}})\right)
depends only on the group
{\mathrm{Aut}}(F;{\mathbb{Q}})
.

For
G
a finite subgroup of
{\mathrm{GL}}(2,{\mathbb{Q}})
which is the group of automorphisms of an element of
{\mathrm{Bin}}(d,{\mathbb{Z}})
, the constant
W(G)
is a rational number which is defined
in
[
SX
, Theorem 1.2]
. This definition is subtle since it depends on the denominators of the entries of the matrices belonging to
G
. However
for the families
\mathcal{F}
that we will meet in
this paper, we will only need the equalities

(1.10)

W\left(\{\mathrm{Id}\}\right)=1,\,W\left(\{\mathrm{Id},\,-{\mathrm{Id}}\}\right)=1/2\text{ and }W\left(\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\pm 1&0\\
0&\pm 1\end{pmatrix}\right\}\right)=1/4.

Finally, the exponent
\kappa_{d}
in Theorem
A
is defined by

(1.11)

\kappa_{d}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\frac{12}{19}}&\text{ if }d=3,\\
\displaystyle{\frac{3}{(d-2)\sqrt{d}+3}}&\text{ if }4\leq d\leq 8,\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{d-1}}&\text{ if }d\geq 9.\end{cases}

Actually the value of this exponent is improved when
F(X,Y)
does not have a linear factor over
{\mathbb{R}}[X,Y]
, see
[
SX
, formula (1.11)]
.

1.2
An asymptotic formula for
{\mathcal{R}}_{\geq d}(\mathcal{F},B,A)

Our central result is the following. The exponent
\vartheta_{d}
is defined in (
2.1
).

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
(A,A_{1},d_{0},d_{1},\kappa)
satisfying the conditions (
1.8
). Let
\mathcal{F}
be a
(A,A_{1},d_{0},d_{1},\kappa)
–regular family of binary forms. Then for every
d\geq\max\{3,d_{1}\}
and every positive
\varepsilon
,
one has the equality

\mathcal{R}_{\geq d}(\mathcal{F},B,A)=\left(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{d}}A_{F}W_{F}\right)\cdot B^{2/d}+O_{\mathcal{F},A,d,\varepsilon}\bigl(B^{\vartheta_{d}+\varepsilon}\bigr)+O_{\mathcal{F},A,d}\bigl(B^{2/d^{\dagger}}\bigr),

uniformly for
B\to\infty
.
The integer
d^{\dagger}
is defined by

d^{\dagger}:=\inf\{d^{\prime}:d^{\prime}>d\text{ such that }\mathcal{F}_{d^{\prime}}\neq\emptyset\}.

Recall that
\mathcal{F}_{d}
is not empty for infinitely many values of
d
since the set
\mathcal{F}
is infinite.

The assumption (v) in the definition
1.10
of a regular family cannot be omitted, even in the case of totally imaginary forms (homogeneous versions of polynomials without real roots), as show by the sequence of positive definite forms
(X-Y)^{2}(X-2Y)^{2}\cdots(X-dY)^{2}+dY^{2d}
, the value of which at the points
(x,y)=(n,1)
,
1\leq n\leq d
, is
d
.

The following is a direct application of (
1.10
):

Corollary 1.12
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem
1.11
and that, for every
d\geq 3
,
\mathcal{F}_{d}
satisfies one of the three following conditions:

C1
:

for all
F\in\mathcal{F}_{d}
, we have
{\mathrm{Aut}}(F,{\mathbb{Q}})=\{{\mathrm{Id}}\}
,

C2
:

for all
F\in\mathcal{F}_{d}
, we have
{\mathrm{Aut}}(F,{\mathbb{Q}})=\{\pm{\mathrm{Id}}\}
(cyclic group of order
2
),

C3
:

for all
F\in\mathcal{F}_{d}
, we have
{\mathrm{Aut}}(F,{\mathbb{Q}})=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\pm 1&0\\
0&\pm 1\end{pmatrix}\right\}
(Klein group of order
4
).

Then we have the equality

(1.12)

\mathcal{R}_{\geq d}(\mathcal{F},B,A)=C_{d}\cdot\left(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{d}}A_{F}\right)\cdot B^{2/d}+O_{\mathcal{F},A,d,\varepsilon}\bigl(B^{\vartheta_{d}+\varepsilon}\bigr)+O_{\mathcal{F},A,d}\bigl(B^{2/d^{\dagger}}\bigr),

where the coefficient
C_{d}
has respectively the values
1
,
1/2
or
1/4
according to the condition C1, C2, C3 respectively satisfied by
\mathcal{F}_{d}
.

1.3
Some applications

We now give a list of regular families
\mathcal{F}
in order to illustrate our results.

The first example of course is given by the sequence of cyclotomic binary forms
[
FLW
]
. We do not repeat it.

Our second example is given by a family of binomials
ax^{d}+by^{d}
where
d
is even while
a
,
b
have the same sign: these restrictions allow us to check easily the assumption (v) in the definition
1.10
of a regular family. Since the proof is easy, we give it right away.

The three other examples below will require more work; for them we restrict ourselves to families
\mathcal{F}
satisfying the conditions of Corollary
1.12

apply (

There are a lot of variations on these constructions.

1.3.1
Binomial forms

For each even integer
d\geq 4
, let
\mathcal{E}_{d}
be a finite subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}
. Assume
\mathcal{E}_{d}
is not empty for infinitely many
d
and has at most
d^{A_{1}}
elements for some
A_{1}>0
and all
d
. Let
\mathcal{B}_{d}
denote the family of binary forms
aX^{d}+bY^{d}
with
(a,b)\in\mathcal{E}_{d}
and let
\mathcal{B}=\cup_{d\geq 4}\mathcal{B}_{d}
.
We assume that for
(a,b)\not=(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
in
\mathcal{E}_{d}
, one at least of
a/a^{\prime}
,
b/b^{\prime}
is not a
d
-th power of a rational number, and also one at least of
a/b^{\prime}
,
b/a^{\prime}
is not a
d
-th power of a rational number.

Theorem 1.13
.

The family
\mathcal{B}
is
(2,A_{1},0,4,1)
–regular.

Further, for every
d\geq 4
and for every
\varepsilon>0
we have the equality

(1.13)

\mathcal{R}_{\geq d}(\mathcal{B},B,2)=\left(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{B}_{d}}A_{F}W_{F}\right)B^{2/d}+O_{\mathcal{B},d,\varepsilon}\left(B^{\max\{\vartheta_{d}+\varepsilon,2/d^{{\dagger}}\}}\right),

uniformly for
B\to\infty
. The integer
d^{\dagger}
is defined by

d^{\dagger}:=\inf\{d^{\prime}:d^{\prime}>d\text{ such that }\mathcal{B}_{d^{\prime}}\neq\emptyset\}.

We will check the hypothesis (iv) of the definition
1.10
of a regular family by means of the following auxiliary result.

Lemma 1.14
.

Let
d\geq 4
be even and let
a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime}
be positive integers. Then the two binary forms
aX^{d}+bY^{d}
and
a^{\prime}X^{d}+b^{\prime}Y^{d}
are isomorphic if and only if either
a/a^{\prime}
,
b/b^{\prime}
are both
d
-th power of rational numbers, or
a/b^{\prime}
,
b/a^{\prime}
are both
d
-th power of rational numbers.

Proof.

If
a/a^{\prime}=u^{d}
and
b/b^{\prime}=v^{d}
, then the two forms
aX^{d}+bY^{d}=a^{\prime}(uX)^{d}+b^{\prime}(vY)^{d}
and
a^{\prime}X^{d}+b^{\prime}Y^{d}
are isomorphic. Also
if
a/b^{\prime}=u^{d}
and
b/a^{\prime}=v^{d}
, then the two forms
aX^{d}+bY^{d}=a^{\prime}(vY)^{d}+b^{\prime}(uX)^{d}
and
a^{\prime}X^{d}+b^{\prime}Y^{d}
are isomorphic. It remains to prove the converse.

Assume that the two binary forms
aX^{d}+bY^{d}
and
a^{\prime}X^{d}+b^{\prime}Y^{d}
are isomorphic.
Let
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&a_{2}\\
a_{3}&a_{4}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{GL}(2,{\mathbb{Q}})
satisfy

a(a_{1}X+a_{2}Y)^{d}+b(a_{3}X+a_{4}Y)^{d}=a^{\prime}X^{d}+b^{\prime}Y^{d}.

We have

aa_{1}^{d}+ba_{3}^{d}=a^{\prime},\quad aa_{2}^{d}+ba_{4}^{d}=b^{\prime}

and, for
i=1,\dots,d-1
,

aa_{1}^{i}a_{2}^{d-i}+ba_{3}^{i}a_{4}^{d-i}=0.

\bullet
Assume
a_{2}=0
. From

a(a_{1}X)^{d}+b(a_{3}X+a_{4}Y)^{d}=a^{\prime}X^{d}+b^{\prime}Y^{d}

we deduce
ba_{4}^{d}=b^{\prime}
,
a_{4}\not=0
, hence
a_{3}=0
, and therefore
aa_{1}^{d}=a^{\prime}
.

\bullet
Assume
a_{1}=0
. From

a(a_{2}Y)^{d}+b(a_{3}X+a_{4}Y)^{d}=a^{\prime}X^{d}+b^{\prime}Y^{d}

we deduce
ba_{3}^{d}=a^{\prime}
,
a_{3}\not=0
, hence
a_{4}=0
, and therefore
aa_{2}^{d}=b^{\prime}
.

\bullet
Finally let us check that the case
a_{1}a_{2}\not=0
is not possible. Write

aa_{1}a_{2}^{d-1}+ba_{3}a_{4}^{d-1}=0,\quad aa_{1}^{2}a_{2}^{d-2}+ba_{3}^{2}a_{4}^{d-2}=0.

We deduce
a_{3}a_{4}\not=0
,

\frac{a_{1}}{a_{3}}=-\frac{b}{a}\left(\frac{a_{4}}{a_{2}}\right)^{d-1},\quad\left(\frac{a_{1}}{a_{3}}\right)^{2}=-\frac{b}{a}\left(\frac{a_{4}}{a_{2}}\right)^{d-2},

hence

\left(\frac{a_{4}}{a_{2}}\right)^{d}=-\frac{a}{b}

which is impossible for
a
,
b
positive and
d
even.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.13
.

The conditions (i), (ii) and (iii) in the definition

For
(a,b)\not=(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})

The second assertion of Theorem

1.11

2.2
Lemmas in diophantine approximation

Firstly we prove the following

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t]
be a polynomial of degree
d\geq 1
and with discriminant different from zero. Let
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{d}
be the complex roots of
f
.
Then there are real constants
c_{1}>0
and
c_{2}
such that

(i)

For every
t\in{\mathbb{C}}
, one has the inequality
\min_{1\leq j\leq d}|t-\xi_{j}|\leq c_{2}|f(t)|,

(ii)

For every
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the condition
|f(t)|<c_{1}
implies
the existence of a real root
\xi_{i}
such that
|t-\xi_{i}|\leq c_{2}|f(t)|
.

Proof.

This statement is trivial when
d=1
. We now suppose
d\geq 2
.

We suppose that
a_{0}
(the leading coefficient of
f
) is
\geq 1
and we factor
f
into

f(t)=a_{0}\prod_{j=1}^{d}(t-\xi_{j}).

Let
\delta:=\min_{1\leq i<j\leq d}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|
. Since the discriminant of
f
is different from zero, we have
\delta>0
.
Let
i

be an index such that

|t-\xi_{i}|=\min_{1\leq j\leq d}|t-\xi_{j}|
. The triangular inequality gives, for
j\neq i
, the lower bound

|t-\xi_{j}|\geq\frac{|t-\xi_{j}|+|t-\xi_{i}|}{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\,|\xi_{j}-\xi_{i}|\geq\frac{\delta}{2}\cdotp

We write the sequence of inequalities

|f(t)|\geq\prod_{1\leq j\leq d}|t-\xi_{j}|\geq|t-\xi_{i}|\left(\frac{\delta}{2}\right)^{d-1},

which leads to the point (i) with
c_{2}=(2/\delta)^{d-1}.

For the item (ii), we now suppose that
t
is real. We decompose the proof into three cases.

\bullet
If all the
\xi_{j}
are real, there is nothing to prove as a consequence of (i). We choose
c_{1}=1
for instance.

\bullet
If no
\xi_{j}
is real, we set

c_{1}:=\inf_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}|f(x)|

which is
>0
.

\bullet
If
f
has at least one real root and at least one non real root, we put

c_{1}=\frac{1}{c_{2}}\min\bigl\{\,|{\mathrm{Im}}(\xi_{i})|:1\leq i\leq d,\,\xi_{i}\not\in{\mathbb{R}}\,\bigr\}.

Applying the item (i) of Lemma
2.3
, we notice that for

the inequality

implies the existence of a root
\xi_{j}

such that

If
\xi_{j}
were not real, we would deduce the inequality

, which is impossible.
Hence
\xi_{j}
is real.

The following lemma provides an upper bound for the tail of the series defining the Riemann

Lemma 2.4

For all real

\displaystyle\geq(d+1)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u}{\prod_{2\leq n\leq d+1}(u^{2}+\mu_{n})^{1/d}}

\displaystyle\geq(d+1)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u}{u^{2}+\mu_{d+1}}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\geq\pi\cdot\frac{d+1}{\sqrt{\mu_{d+1}}}\cdotp

From our assumption
\mu_{d+1}\leq\lambda(d+1)
we deduce from (
4.7
) the lower bound

(4.8)

{\mathrm{Coef}}(\mathcal{Q}^{+},2d)>\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\,\sqrt{d}.

For the upper bound, we write

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Coef}}(\mathcal{Q}^{+},2d)

\displaystyle\leq(d+1)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(u^{2}+\mu_{d+1})^{1/d}}{G_{d}(u)^{1/d}}{\mathrm{d}}u

\displaystyle\leq(d+1)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u}{\prod_{1\leq n\leq d}(u^{2}+\mu_{n})^{1/d}}\cdotp

Using Hölder’s inequality we deduce

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Coef}}(\mathcal{Q}^{+},2d)

\displaystyle\leq(d+1)\prod_{n=1}^{d}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}u}{u^{2}+\mu_{n}}\right)^{1/d}

\displaystyle\leq\pi(d+1)\prod_{n=1}^{d}\mu_{n}^{-1/(2d)}\leq\pi\frac{d+1}{D}

with
D:=(d\,!)^{1/(2d)}
. Using Stirling formula (
1.21
),
we deduce

{\mathrm{Coef}}(\mathcal{Q}^{+},2d)\leq\pi\sqrt{\mathrm{e}}(\sqrt{d}+1).

Combining with (
4.8
) we complete the proof of (
1.18
). Recalling Propositions

4.2
and
4.4
, we conclude that the proof of Theorem
1.15
is now complete.

Recall that for

denotes the following binary form of degree

5.1
The family
\mathcal{Q}^{-}
is
(A,1,2,2,2\mathrm{e}\lambda)
–regular

Our goal in this subsection is to prove the following

.

For

, the family
\mathcal{Q}^{-}
is
(A,1,2,2,2\mathrm{e}\lambda)
–regular.

The proofs of items (i), (ii), (iii) and (iv) are the same as for Proposition

: one only replaces (

It remains to check the condition
(v) in Definition
1.10
of a regular family.
We start with an auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

For
m
and
d
integers satisfying
1\leq m<d
, we have

\left(\frac{d}{m}-1\right)^{d-m}\geq\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{e}^{-1}m};

further, for
n
an integer in the range

, we have

\frac{n!(d-n)!}{n^{d}}\geq\mathrm{e}^{-(1+\mathrm{e}^{-1})d}.

The numerical value for
\mathrm{e}^{1+\mathrm{e}^{-1}}
is
3.927\dots<\frac{79}{20}\cdotp

Proof of Lemma
5.2
.

Set
t=d-m
,
f_{m}(t)=\left(\frac{t}{m}\right)^{t}
,
g_{m}(t)=\log f_{m}(t)=t\log t-t\log m
. The derivative
g_{m}^{\prime}(t)=1+\log t-\log m
of

vanishes at
t=m/\mathrm{e}
, the minimum of
f_{m}(t)
on the interval
0<t\leq d-1
is reached at
t=m/\mathrm{e}
, giving the value
(t/m)^{t}=\mathrm{e}^{-t}=\mathrm{e}^{-m/\mathrm{e}}
.

The last part of Lemma
5.2
follows from the first one thanks to Stirling’s formula (
1.21
):

\frac{n!(d-n)!}{n^{d}}\geq\frac{n^{n}}{\mathrm{e}^{n}}\cdot\frac{(d-n)^{d-n}}{\mathrm{e}^{d-n}}\cdot\frac{1}{n^{d}}=\frac{(d-n)^{d-n}}{n^{d-n}\mathrm{e}^{d}}\geq\mathrm{e}^{-d}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{e}^{-1}n}\geq\mathrm{e}^{-(1+\mathrm{e}^{-1})d}.

∎

The last inequality of Lemma
5.2
implies

(5.1)

\bigl(n!(d-n)!\bigr)^{1/d}\geq\mathrm{e}^{-1-\mathrm{e}^{-1}}\max\{n,d-n\}\geq\frac{d}{2\mathrm{e}^{1+\mathrm{e}^{-1}}}\geq\frac{d}{2\mathrm{e}^{2}}\cdotp

End of the proof of Proposition
5.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
1\leq\nu\leq d+1
,
(x,y)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
. Set

. Our goal it to prove

|Q|>(2\mathrm{e}\lambda)^{-2d+2}\max\{|x|,|y|\}^{2d-2}.

We consider three cases depending on the sign of the factors

.

\bullet
If

, all factors are negative. For

When
\nu\geq 2

and obtain

For

The desired estimate (

\bullet
If

Since

This implies (

\bullet
Finally, assume that there is an

Hence

\displaystyle|Q|=

d\geq 3
, for every
p\geq d-1
, for every integers
x
and
y
such that
L_{d,p}(x,y)\neq 0
and

|x|\leq|y|
, one has the inequality

|L_{d,p}(x,y)|\geq\max\{|x|,|y|\}^{d-1}.

6.3.2
Assume
(d-2)y\leq x

Let
x
and
y
be positive integers such that
L_{d,p}(x,y)\neq 0
with
x\geq(d-2)y
, hence
x\geq(d-2)y+1
.
We
deduce from (
6.11
)

|L_{d,p}(x,y)|=x\cdot(x-y)\cdot(x-2y)\cdots(x-(d-2)y)\cdot|x-py|.

\bullet
If
y=1
, since
x\geq d-1
, we have

x-n=x\left(1-\frac{n}{x}\right)\geq x\left(1-\frac{n}{d-1}\right)=x\left(\frac{d-n-1}{d-1}\right)

for
0\leq n\leq d-2
; using the trivial lower bound
|x-p|\geq 1
together with Stirling’s formula (
1.21
), we deduce

|L_{d,p}(x,1)|\geq x\cdot(x-1)\cdot(x-2)\cdots(x-(d-2))\geq\frac{(d-1)!}{(d-1)^{d-1}}\,x^{d-1}\geq\frac{x^{d-1}}{\mathrm{e}^{d-1}}\cdotp

\bullet
We assume now
y\geq 2
. As a consequence of the hypothesis
y\leq x/(d-2)
, we have the inequality

x\cdot(x-y)\cdot(x-2y)\cdots(x-(d-3)y)\geq\frac{(d-2)!}{(d-2)^{d-2}}\,x^{d-2}.

\diamond
If
x>py
, then

x-(d-2)y\geq x\left(1-\frac{d-2}{p}\right)\geq x\left(1-\frac{d-2}{d-1}\right)=\frac{x}{d-1}

and the trivial lower bound
x-py\geq 1
suffices to deduce

L_{d,p}(x,y)\geq\frac{(d-2)!}{(d-1)(d-2)^{d-2}}\,x^{d-1}.

\diamond
If
py>x
, then from
x-(d-2)y\geq 1
and
py-x\geq 1
we deduce

(x-(d-2)y)\cdot(py-x)\geq(x-(d-2)y)+(py-x)-1\geq y(p-d+2)-1.

If
p=d-1
we use the assumption
y\geq 2
which yields

y(p-d+2)-1=y-1\geq\frac{y}{2}>\frac{x}{2p}=\frac{x}{2(d-1)},

while for
p\geq d
we use the lower bounds

y(p-d+2)-1\geq y(p-d+1)\geq py\left(1-\frac{d-1}{p}\right)>x\left(1-\frac{d-1}{d}\right)=\frac{x}{d}\cdotp

Therefore, for
(d-2)y\leq x
and
y\geq 2
, we have

|L_{d,p}(x,y)|\geq\frac{(d-2)!}{2(d-1)(d-2)^{d-2}}\,x^{d-1}\geq\frac{x^{d-1}}{2d\mathrm{e}^{d-2}}\cdotp

We deduce

Proposition 6.7
.

For
d\geq 3
,
p
prime
\geq d-1
and
(x,y)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
such that

|x|\geq(d-2)|y|
and

L_{d,p}(x,y)\neq 0

we have

\left|L_{d,p}(x,y)\right|\geq\frac{1}{d\mathrm{e}^{d}}\max\{|x|,|y|\}^{d-1}.

6.3.3
Assume
(n-1)y\leq x\leq ny
for some
n
with
2\leq n\leq d-2

We
deduce from (
6.11
)

|L_{d,p}(x,y)|=x\cdot(x-y)\cdots(x-(n-1)y)\cdot(ny-x)\cdots((d-2)y-x)\cdot(py-x).

We have

x\cdot(x-y)\cdots(x-(n-2)y)\geq(n-1)!y^{n-1}

and

\displaystyle((n+1)y-x)\cdots((d-2)y-x)\cdot(py-x)

\displaystyle\geq(d-n-2)!(p-n)y^{d-n-1}

\displaystyle\geq(d-n-1)!y^{d-n-1}.

For the product of the two terms in the middle, if
y=1
we use the trivial lower bound
(x-(n-1)y)(ny-x)\geq 1
which yields

|L_{d,p}(x,y)|\geq(n-1)!(d-n-1)!y^{d-2}\geq\frac{(n-1)!(d-n-1)!}{n^{d-2}}x^{d-2},

while for
y\geq 2
we use

(x-(n-1)y)(ny-x)\geq(x-(n-1)y)+(ny-x)-1=y-1\geq\frac{y}{2},

which yields

|L_{d,p}(x,y)|\geq\frac{1}{2}(n-1)!(d-n-1)!y^{d-1}\geq\frac{(n-1)!(d-n-1)!}{2n^{d-1}}x^{d-1}.

We now use Lemma
5.2
:

\frac{(n-1)!(d-n-1)!}{n^{d-1}}=\frac{n!(d-n)!}{n^{d}(d-n)}\geq\mathrm{e}^{-(1+\mathrm{e}^{-1})d}\frac{1}{d-n},

from which we deduce

|L_{d,p}(x,y)|\geq\mathrm{e}^{-(1+\mathrm{e}^{-1})d}\frac{1}{2(d-n)}x^{d-1}.

This proves the following result:

Proposition 6.8
.

For
d\geq 3
,
2\leq n\leq d-2
,
p
prime
\geq d-1
and
x
and
y
such that

(n-1)|y|\leq|x|\leq n|y|
and

L_{d,p}(x,y)\neq 0
,
we have

\left|L_{d,p}(x,y)\right|\geq\frac{1}{2(d-2)}\cdot\frac{\max\{|x|,|y|\}^{d-1}}{\mathrm{e}^{(1+\mathrm{e}^{-1})d}}\cdotp

For
d\geq 5
, we have

2(d-2)\cdot\mathrm{e}^{(1+\mathrm{e}^{-1})d}<9^{d-1}.

We may now gather Propositions
6.6
,
6.7
and
6.8

to deduce (
6.10
), which completes the proof of Proposition
6.5
.

6.5

\mathcal{L}
of
(x,y)
with

The difference

(6.12)

\mathcal{R}_{\geq d}(\mathcal{L},B,0)-\mathcal{R}_{\geq d}(\mathcal{L},B,10)

is bounded from above by two times the cardinality
of the set

\mathfrak{Er}_{\geq d}(B)\\
:=\{m\;:\;0<m=|L_{d^{\prime},p}(x,y)|\leq B,\;d\leq d^{\prime}\leq p<2d^{\prime},\,\max\{|x|,|y|\}\leq 9\}.

There are
19^{2}
pairs
(x,y)
with
\max\{|x|,|y|\}\leq 9
. We first count the number of
m
in
\mathfrak{Er}_{\geq d}(B)
of the form
|L_{d^{\prime},p}(x,0)|
, namely with
y=0
. For

and
y=0
we have

; for
2\leq|x|\leq 9
and
y=0
, we have
2^{d^{\prime}}\leq B

O_{d}(\log B)

.

in

6.6
Some results on
A_{F}
for
F\in\mathcal{L}

The area of the fundamental domain associated to
L_{d,p}
is,
by the definition (
1.9
), equal to

A_{L_{d,p}}=\iint_{\mathcal{D}(L_{d,p})}{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}y,

with

\mathcal{D}(L_{d,p}):=\bigl\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:\left|x(x-y)(x-2y)\cdots(x-(d-2)y)(x-py)\right|\leq 1\bigr\}.

By the change of variables
u=x
and
v=y/x
, we obtain

A_{L_{d,p}}=\iint_{\mathcal{D}^{*}(L_{d,p})}|u|\,{\mathrm{d}}u\,{\mathrm{d}}v,

with

\mathcal{D}^{*}(L_{d,p}):=\bigl\{(u,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}:|u|^{d}\cdot\left|(1-v)(1-2v)\cdots(1-(d-2)v)(1-pv)\right|\leq 1\bigr\}.

Some elementary calculations transform
A_{L_{d,p}}
into a single integral.

Lemma 6.10
.

For
d\geq 5
and
p\geq d-1
the following equalities hold

A_{L_{d,p}}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}v}{\left(\,|1-v|\cdot|1-2v|\cdots|1-(d-2)v|\cdot|1-pv|\,\right)^{2/d}}

and

A_{L_{d,p}}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}t}{\left(\,|t|\cdot|t-1|\cdot|t-2|\cdots|t-(d-2)|\cdot|t-p|\,\right)^{2/d}}\cdotp

We will only work with the second expression of
A_{L_{d,p}}
. So we introduce the function

\lambda_{d,p}(t):=t(t-1)\cdots(t-(d-2))(t-p),

which is the product of
d
linear factors in
t
.
We split the interval of integration into
d
intervals of length
1
around the singularities
0
,…,
d-2
and
p
and three remaining intervals
to write the equality:

(6.13)

A_{L_{d,p}}:=\\
\left(\int_{-\infty}^{-1/2}+\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}+\cdots+\int_{d-(5/2)}^{d-(3/2)}+\int_{d-(3/2)}^{p-(1/2)}+\int_{p-(1/2)}^{p+(1/2)}+\int_{p+(1/2)}^{\infty}\right)\frac{{\mathrm{d}}t}{|\lambda_{d,p}(t)|^{2/d}}\cdotp

We will give an upper bound and a lower bound for each of these positive integrals in order to prove

Proposition 6.11
.

Uniformly for
d\to\infty
and
d\leq p<2d
one has

\frac{\mathrm{e}^{2}-o(1)}{d}\leq A_{L_{d,p}}\leq\frac{5\,\mathrm{e}^{2}+2\mathrm{e}+o(1)}{d}\cdotp

The last part of Theorem
1.18
is obtained from this proposition after a summation over
d\leq p<2d
and an application of the Prime Number Theorem.

6.6.1
An auxiliary lemma

Lemma 6.12
.

For
d\to\infty
, we have

\left(1\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdots(2d-3)\right)^{1/d}=(2\mathrm{e}^{-1}+o(1))d.

Proof.

We write

1\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdots(2d-3)=\frac{(2d-3)!}{2^{d-2}(d-2)!}=\frac{(2d)!}{(2d-1)2^{d}d!}

and we use Stirling’s formula (
1.21
) which gives

\Bigl(\frac{2d}{\rm e}\Bigr)^{d}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{(2d-1)\cdot{\rm e}^{1/12d}}\leq 1\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdots(2d-3)\leq\Bigl(\frac{2d}{\rm e}\Bigr)^{d}\frac{\sqrt{2}\cdot{\rm e}^{1/24d}}{2d-1}\cdotp

∎

6.6.2
Study of
\int_{-\infty}^{-1/2}
and of
\int_{p+1/2}^{\infty}

Lemma 6.13
.

For
d\to\infty
and
p\geq d
, one has

0\leq\int_{-\infty}^{-1/2}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}t}{|\lambda_{d,p}(t)|^{2/d}}\leq\frac{\mathrm{e}+o(1)}{d}\cdotp

Proof.

Using Hölder inequality and Lemma
6.12
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{-1/2}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}t}{|\lambda_{d,p}(t)|^{2/d}}\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{-\infty}^{-1/2}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}t}{|t|^{2}}\right)^{1/d}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{-1/2}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}t}{|t-1|^{2}}\right)^{1/d}\cdots\left(\int_{-\infty}^{-1/2}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}t}{|t-(d-2)|^{2}}\right)^{1/d}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{-1/2}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}t}{|t-p|^{2}}\right)^{1/d}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2}{1}\cdot\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{2}{5}\cdots\frac{2}{2d-3}\cdot\frac{2}{2p+1}\right)^{1/d}\leq\left(\frac{2^{d}}{1\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdots(2d-3)\cdot(2p+1)}\right)^{1/d}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathrm{e}+o(1)}{d}\cdotp

∎

Similarly, one proves

Lemma 6.14
.

For
d\to\infty
and
p\geq d
, one has

0\leq\int_{p+(1/2)}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}t}{|\lambda_{d,p}(t)|^{2/d}}\leq\frac{\mathrm{e}+o(1)}{d}\cdotp

Proof.

For
t>p+\frac{1}{2}
, we have

|\lambda_{d,p}(t)|=\lambda_{d,p}(t)=t(t-1)\cdots(t-(d-2))(t-p).

Using Hölder inequality and Lemma
6.12
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{p+(1/2)}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}t}{|\lambda_{d,p}(t)|^{2/d}}\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{p+(1/2)}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}t}{t^{2}}\right)^{1/d}\left(\int_{p+(1/2)}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}t}{(t-1)^{2}}\right)^{1/d}\cdots\left(\int_{p+(1/2)}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}t}{(t-(d-2))^{2}}\right)^{1/d}\left(\int_{p+(1/2)}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}t}{(t-p)^{2}}\right)^{1/d}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2}{2p+1}\cdot\frac{2}{2p-1}\cdot\frac{2}{2p-3}\cdots\frac{2}{2p-2d+5}\cdot\frac{2}{1}\right)^{1/d}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2^{d}}{1\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdots(2d-3)}\right)^{1/d}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathrm{e}+o(1)}{d}\cdotp

∎

6.6.3
Study of
\int_{d-3/2}^{p-1/2}

Lemma 6.15
.

For
d\to\infty
and
d\leq p<2d
, one has

0\leq\int_{d-3/2}^{p-1/2}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}t}{|\lambda_{d,p}(t)|^{2/d}}\leq\frac{\mathrm{e}^{2}+o(1)}{d}\cdotp

Proof.

For
t
in the interval
(d-(3/2),p-(1/2))
, we have
p-t>1/2
,

|\lambda_{d,p}(t)|=t(t-1)\cdots(t-(d-2))(p-t)

and, for
0\leq n\leq d-2
,

t-n>\frac{2d-2n-3}{2},

hence

|\lambda_{d,p}(t)|\geq\frac{(2d-3)\cdot(2d-5)\cdots 3\cdot 1}{2^{d}}

and therefore

|\lambda_{d,p}(t)|^{2/d}\geq(\mathrm{e}^{-2}+o(1))d^{2}

by Lemma
6.12
.
Since
d\leq p<2d
, the interval of integration has length at most
d+1
, and so we deduce

\int_{d-3/2}^{p-1/2}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}t}{|\lambda_{d,p}(t)|^{2/d}}\leq\frac{\mathrm{e}^{2}+o(1)}{d}\cdotp

∎

6.6.4
Study of
\int_{p-1/2}^{p+1/2}

Lemma 6.16
.

For
d\geq 5
and
d\leq p<2d
, one has

0\leq\int_{p-1/2}^{p+1/2}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}t}{|\lambda_{d,p}(t)|^{2/d}}=O\left(\frac{1}{d^{2}}\right).

We introduce the polynomial

{\mathcal{M}}(t):=t(t-1)\cdots(t-(d-2))

of degree
d-1
.
It is easy to see that

\min_{|t-p|\leq 1/2}|{\mathcal{M}}(t)|=|{\mathcal{M}}(p-(1/2))|\geq{\mathcal{M}}(d-(3/2))=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{3}{2}\cdot\frac{5}{2}\cdots\frac{2d-5}{2}\cdot\frac{2d-3}{2},

hence by Lemma
6.12
, we have

\min_{|t-p|\leq 1/2}|{\mathcal{M}}(t)|^{2/d}\geq(\mathrm{e}^{-2}+o(1))d^{2}.

Since

\int_{p-1/2}^{p+1/2}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}t}{|t-p|^{2/d}}=O(1)

we conclude

\displaystyle\int_{p-1/2}^{p+1/2}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}t}{|\lambda_{d,p}(t)|^{2/d}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{p-1/2}^{p+1/2}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}t}{|t-p|^{2/d}}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\min_{|t-p|\leq 1/2}|{\mathcal{M}}(t)|}\right)^{2/d}

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{1}{d^{2}}\right).

6.6.5
Study of the remaining integrals

We are now concerned, for
\nu=0,1\dots,d-2
, with the integrals

\mathcal{I}_{\nu}=\mathcal{I}_{d,p,\nu}=\int_{\nu-1/2}^{\nu+1/2}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}t}{|\lambda_{d,p}(t)|^{2/d}},

for which we want to find an upper and a lower bound. We split the product defining
\lambda_{d,p}(t)
into four pieces

(6.14)

\lambda_{d,p}(t)=(t-\nu)\cdot(t-p)\cdot\lambda_{\nu}^{-}(t)\cdot\lambda_{d,\nu}^{+}(t),

with

\lambda_{\nu}^{-}(t):=\prod_{0\leq k<\nu}\left(t-k\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{d,\nu}^{+}(t):=\prod_{\nu<k\leq d-2}\left(t-k\right).

We have

(6.15)

\mathcal{I}_{\nu}\leq\left(\int_{\nu-1/2}^{\nu+1/2}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}t}{|t-\nu|^{2/d}}\right)\cdot\left(\min|\lambda_{\nu}^{-}(t)|\right)^{-2/d}\cdot\left(\min|\lambda_{d,\nu}^{+}(t)|\right)^{-2/d}\cdot\left(\min|t-p|\right)^{-2/d},

and

(6.16)

\mathcal{I}_{\nu}\geq\left(\int_{\nu-1/2}^{\nu+1/2}\frac{{\mathrm{d}}t}{|t-\nu|^{2/d}}\right)\cdot\left(\max|\lambda_{\nu}^{-}(t)|\right)^{-2/d}\cdot\left(\max|\lambda_{d,\nu}^{+}(t)|\right)^{-2/d}\cdot\left(\max|t-p|\right)^{-2/d}

where all the maximum and minimum are taken for
\nu-1/2\leq t\leq\nu+1/2
. Direct computations transform (
6.15
) and (
6.16
) into

\displaystyle(1-o(1))\left(\max|\lambda_{\nu}^{-}(t)|\right)^{-2/d}\cdot

\displaystyle\left(\max|\lambda_{d,\nu}^{+}(t)|\right)^{-2/d}\leq\mathcal{I}_{\nu}\leq

\displaystyle(1+o(1))\left(\min|\lambda_{\nu}^{-}(t)|\right)^{-2/d}\cdot\left(\min|\lambda_{d,\nu}^{+}(t)|\right)^{-2/d}

which is also

(6.17)

\displaystyle(1-o(1))|\lambda_{\nu}^{-}(\nu+1/2)|^{-2/d}\cdot

\displaystyle|\lambda_{d,\nu}^{+}(\nu-1/2)|^{-2/d}\leq\mathcal{I}_{\nu}\leq

\displaystyle(1+o(1))|\lambda_{\nu}^{-}(\nu-1/2)|^{-2/d}\cdot|\lambda_{d,\nu}^{+}(\nu+1/2)|^{-2/d},

uniformly for
d\to\infty
and
d\leq p<2d
.

For
1\leq\nu\leq d-2
, we have the equalities

\lambda_{\nu}^{-}(\nu+1/2)=\frac{(2\nu+1)(2\nu-1)\cdots 3}{2^{\nu}}=\frac{(2\nu+1)\,!}{2^{2\nu}\cdot\nu\,!}=\frac{(2\nu)\,!}{2^{2\nu}\cdot\nu\,!}\cdot\frac{1}{2\nu+1},

\lambda_{\nu}^{-}(\nu-1/2)=\frac{(2\nu-1)(2\nu-3)\cdots 1}{2^{\nu}}=\frac{(2\nu-1)\,!}{2^{2\nu-1}\cdot(\nu\ -1)\,!}=\frac{(2\nu)\,!}{2^{2\nu}\cdot\nu\,!},

and for
0\leq\nu\leq d-3
, we have

|\,\lambda_{d,\nu}^{+}(\nu+1/2)\,|=\frac{(2d^{*}-1)(2d^{*}-3)\cdots 3\cdot 1}{2^{d^{*}}}=\frac{(2d^{*}-1)\,!}{2^{2d^{*}-1}\cdot(d^{*}-1)\,!}=\frac{(2d^{*})\,!}{2^{2d^{*}}\cdot d^{*}\,!},

|\,\lambda_{d,\nu}^{+}(\nu-1/2)\,|=\frac{(2d^{*}+1)(2d^{*}-1)\cdots 5\cdot 3}{2^{d^{*}}}=\frac{(2d^{*}+1)\,!}{2^{2d^{*}}\cdot d^{*}\,!}=\frac{(2d^{*})\,!}{2^{2d^{*}}\cdot d^{*}\,!}\cdot(2d^{*}+1),

with the notation
d^{*}=d-2-\nu
.
Furthermore, since we have empty products in the decomposition (
6.14
) , we have

(6.18)

\lambda_{0}^{-}(1/2)=\lambda_{0}^{-}(-1/2)=\lambda_{d,d-2}^{+}(d-3/2)=\lambda_{d,d-2}^{+}(d-5/2)=1.

The following lemma shows that the inequalities (
6.17
) are sharp.

Lemma 6.17
.

Uniformly for
0\leq\nu\leq d-2
and
d\rightarrow\infty
one has

1-o(1)\leq\left(\frac{|\lambda_{\nu}^{-}(\nu-1/2)|\cdot|\lambda_{d,\nu}^{+}(\nu+1/2)|}{|\lambda_{\nu}^{-}(\nu+1/2)|\cdot|\lambda_{d,\nu}^{+}(\nu-1/2)|}\right)^{-2/d}\leq 1+o(1)

Proof.

Obvious consequence of the explicit formulas given above.
∎

For
0\leq\nu\leq d-2
, let

\Lambda=\Lambda(d,\nu):=|\lambda_{\nu}^{-}(\nu-1/2)|^{-2/d}\cdot|\lambda_{d,\nu}^{+}(\nu+1/2)|^{-2/d}

As a consequence of the explicit formulas of
\lambda_{\nu}^{-}
and
\lambda_{d,\nu}^{+}
, we have the equality

\log\Lambda=-\frac{2}{d}\Bigl\{\log((2\nu)\,!)+\log((2d^{*})\,!)\\
-\log(\nu\,!)-\log(d^{*}\,!)-2d\log 2+o(d)\Bigr\},

uniformly for
1\leq\nu\leq d-3
and
d\to\infty
. Using Stirling formula (
1.21
), we deduce

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{2}\cdot\log\Lambda

\displaystyle=\nu\log\nu+d^{*}\log d^{*}-d+o(d)

\displaystyle=\nu\log\nu+(d-\nu)\log(d-\nu)-d+o(d),

hence

(6.19)

\log\Lambda=-\frac{2}{d}\Bigl(\nu\log\nu+(d-\nu)\log(d-\nu)\Bigl)+2+o(1),

uniformly for
1\leq\nu\leq d-3
and
d\to\infty
. By a direct study of the function
f_{d}
defined by

f_{d}:t\in[1,d-1]\mapsto f_{d}(t)=t\log t+(d-t)\log(d-t),

we deduce that, for all
1\leq t\leq d-1
, the function
f_{d}
satisfies the inequality

f_{d}(d/2)=d\log(d/2)\leq f_{d}(t)\leq f_{d}(1)=f_{d}(d-1)=(d-1)\log(d-1).

Inserting this bound into (
6.19
), we obtain that

(6.20)

-2\log d+2-o(1)\leq\log\Lambda(d,\nu)\leq-2\log d+2\log 2+2+o(1),

uniformly for
1\leq\nu\leq d-3
. Actually this formula also holds for
\Lambda(d,0)
and
\Lambda(d,d-2)
thanks to the formulas (
6.18
).

Combining (
6.17
), (
6.20
) and Lemma
6.17
, we proved

Lemma 6.18
.

Uniformly for
d\to\infty
,
0\leq\nu\leq d-2
and
d\leq p<2d
, one has

\frac{{\rm e}^{2}-o(1)}{d^{2}}\leq\mathcal{I}_{d,p,\nu}\leq\frac{4\,{\rm e}^{2}+o(1)}{d^{2}}\cdotp

\bullet

\bullet

Constructions of derived equivalent hyper-Kähler fourfolds
Grzegorz Kapustka, Michal Kapustka

Abstract
. We study twisted derived equivalences of hyper-Kähler fourfolds. We describe when two hyper-Kähler fourfolds of K ​ 3 [ 2 ] K3^{[2]} -type of Picard rank 1 1 with isometric transcendental lattices are derived equivalent. Then we present new constructions of pairs of twisted derived equivalent hyper-Kähler manifolds of Picard rank ≥ 2 \geq 2 .

14J42, 14F08

1.
Introduction

The study of equivalences of derived categories of smooth projective varieties goes back to the works of Mukai
[
Mu1
,
Mu2
]
who generalised the idea of Fourier transforms to abelian varieties.
Finding Fourier-Mukai partners, i.e. non-isomorphic derived equivalent varieties, has since become a central problem in algebraic geometry, closely related to homological mirror symmetry and the study of moduli spaces of vector bundles.

By results of Mukai and Orlov
[
Mu1
]
,
[
Or
]
, two K3 surfaces are derived equivalent if and only if their transcendental lattices are Hodge isometric.
In that case, one of the surfaces is a fine moduli space of stable sheaves on the second surface and the Fourier-Mukai kernel of the equivalence is given by the universal family. In a similar way, moduli spaces of twisted sheaves give rise to twisted derived equivalences of K3 surfaces, which induce rational Hodge isometries of transcendental lattices (see
[
HS1
]
).
Conversely, if the transcendental lattices of two surfaces are Hodge isometric over
{\mathbb{Q}}
then there is a sequence of twisted derived equivalences connecting them (see
[
H
]
).
For hyper-Kähler manifolds, that are natural generalisations of K3 surfaces to higher dimension, the situation is more complicated.

In this paper, we study derived equivalence of four dimensional hyper-Kähler manifolds.
More precisely, we study this problem in the case of one of the two known families of hyper-Kähler fourfolds; the deformations of the Hilbert scheme of two points on a K3 surface called of
K3^{[2]}
-type
[
B
,
F
]
.
Recall that strong restrictions in the classification of hyper-Kähler fourfolds where found in
[
DHMV
]
.

It was proved in
[
T
]
(see
[
B
, Corollary 9.3]
) that two derived equivalent
K3^{[n]}
-type manifolds have isometric transcendental lattices. In this paper we discuss the inverse problem.

Problem 1.1
.

Is it true that if two hyper-Kähler manifolds have isometric transcendental lattices then they are derived equivalent?

Recall that, by considering equivalences of Hilbert schemes of points on
K3
surfaces induced by equivalences between those
K3
surfaces, non-isomorphic and derived equivalent
K3^{[n]}
-type manifolds with Picard group of rank
\geq 2
were found in
[
P
,
MMY
]
. Moreover, examples of twisted derived equivalent
K3^{[2]}
-type manifolds that are moduli spaces of sheaves were described in
[
S
,
ADM
]
. In those cases Problem
1.1
has a positive answer. Moreover, we shall see that
K3^{[2]}-
type manifolds with Hodge isometric transcendental lattices are always twisted derived equivalent.
On the other hand, there exist
K3^{[2]}
-type manifolds of higher Picard number which have isometric transcendental lattice, but are not derived equivalent (cf.
[
MM
, Theorem 5.13]
).

In this paper, we discuss Problem
1.1
in the case of
K3^{[2]}
-type manifolds of Picard number one.
In particular, we prove in Theorem
1.2
below, that manifolds of Picard number one with isometric transcendental lattices are derived equivalent in the cases where their polarisation has suitable degree and divisibility. The remaining cases are treated in Theorem
1.3
and lead to twisted derived equivalences. Building on the negative answer to Problem
1.1
for higher Picard number, we expect that our results are sharp in the sense that in cases treated in Theorem
1.3
, there should exist pairs of manifolds of Picard number one with polarisation of given degree and divisibility, which have isometric transcendental lattices, but are not derived equivalent.

Concretely, our main results are the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(X,L_{X})
and
(Y,L_{Y})
be two polarized hyper-Kähler fourfolds of
K3^{[2]}
-type of Picard number one and degree
2d
such that
d\equiv 1\ \mod\ 4
or
8|d
or
L_{X}
and
L_{Y}
have divisibility 2. Then their transcendental lattices
T_{X}
and
T_{Y}
are Hodge isometric if and only if
D^{b}(X)=D^{b}(Y)
. The number of Fourier-Mukai partners of
X
is then
2^{\tau(d)}
, where
\tau(d)
is the number of different prime divisors of
d
and
\tau(1)=1
.

In the case of hyper-Kähler manifolds of
K3^{[2]}
-type of Picard number one of other degrees, we deduce twisted derived equivalence, with explicit twist.
Denote by
D^{b}(X,[\frac{\delta_{X}}{2}])
the derived category of twisted sheaves with twist that is the uniquely defined Brauer class with a B-lift
\frac{\delta_{X}}{2}
such that
\delta_{X}
is a
-2
class of divisibility
2
(see Section
2.1
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(X,L_{X})
and
(Y,L_{Y})
be two polarized hyper-Kähler fourfolds of
K3^{[2]}
-type of Picard number one and degree
2d
such that
L_{X}
and
L_{Y}
are divisibilty 1,
d
is not divisible by 8 and
d\not\equiv 1\mod 4
and the transcendental lattices
T_{X}
and
T_{Y}
are Hodge isometric. Then
D^{b}(X,[\frac{\delta_{X}}{2}])=D^{b}(Y,[\frac{\delta_{Y}}{2}])
.

The theorems are proved at the end of Section
5
as Theorem
5.1
.
The idea of the proof is to proceed via deformation of Fourier-Mukai kernels starting from special examples. First, having an isometry between transcendental lattices
T_{X}
and
T_{Y}
of two hyper-Kähler fourfolds
X
,
Y
of Picard rank 1 we extend it to a rational Hodge isometry between
H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})
and
H^{2}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})
. Next, we realise this rational isometry as a rational isometry given by a Fourier-Mukai kernel on
X^{\prime}\times Y^{\prime}
of an equivalence of derived categories of Hilbert squares of K3 surfaces
X^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime}
. Then, following
[
Mar1
, §5]
(using hyperholomorphic sheaves
[
V2
]
), we deform the Fourier-Mukai kernel on
X^{\prime}\times Y^{\prime}
along a twistor path on a moduli space of Hodge isometric pairs of manifolds (see Section
2.2
) to a twisted sheaf on
X\times Y
.
Finally, we study the Brauer class of the latter twisted sheaf and use a general result on deformation of Fourier-Mukai kernels (cf. Theorem
2.3
) to prove that it induces a derived (or twisted derived) equivalence between
X
and
Y
.

The case of higher Picard rank is discussed in Section
6
. Here, new technical difficulties arise since we cannot compose a derived equivalence as in Theorem
1.2
with a twisted derived equivalence as in Theorem
1.3
, see
[
H
, Remark 1.3]
. In this case, we propose in Section
7
another construction of derived equivalence along Lagrangian fibrations.
This is a generalisation in Theorem
6.3
of
[
ADM
, Theorem A]
to the case of moduli spaces of twisted sheaves. The construction works also in higher dimension and gives rise to various examples of twisted derived equivalent
K3^{[n]}
-type manifolds.
In particular, we are able to show the derived equivalence of the following known pair of hyper-Kähler fourfolds with Picard rank
2
and isomorphic transcendental lattices.

Proposition 1.4
.

A very general double EPW quartic, i.e. a
K3^{[2]}
-type fourfold with Picard lattice
U(2)
, is derived equivalent to a special double EPW sextic, i.e. a
K3^{[2]}
-type fourfold with Picard lattice
(2)\oplus(-2)
such that the generator
(-2)
is of divisibility
1
.

The proof can be found in Section
6.4
. The technical difficulty is the computation in Proposition
6.8
of the Brauer class that is the obstruction on the double EPW sextic (isomorphic to the moduli space of twisted sheaves
M_{(2,2B+h,1)}(S,B)
) for the existence of the universal family on
S\times M_{(2,2B+h,1)}(S,B)
, where
(S,B)
is the related twisted K3 surface of degree
2
.

Finally, we describe in Section
7
a third construction of twisted derived equivalences that is a mixture of the above constructions.
We consider deformations of convolutions of two fiberwise equivalences along Lagrangian fibrations.
We infer in this way locally free Fourier-Mukai kernels that again deform as hyperholomorphic sheaves giving twisted equivalences of hyper-Kähler manifolds of Picard rank
1
. Recall that from
[
KP
]
the related dual Gushel-Mukai fourfolds (i.e. associated to dual Lagrangian spaces) are derived equivalent. Here we deduce, both from Theorem
1.2
and the latter construction, that the two corresponding dual double EPW sextics are also derived equivalent. We end the paper with a list of questions and open problems.

2.
Preliminary

In this section we introduce some technical tools that are needed in the paper.
The main method considered in this paper is the deformation a derived equivalence between two fixed hyper-Kähler manifolds to a general pair. The problem is that we infer in this way twisted derived equivalences and we have to deal with derived categories of twisted sheaves (see
[
Ca
,
Y
]
).
For this reason we start by discussing Brauer classes on hyper-Kähler manifolds adapting the approach described in
[
HS
,
vGK
,
K
]
from the case of K3 surfaces.

2.1.
Brauer classes

The cohomological Brauer group of a scheme
X
is the group

Br^{\prime}(X)=H^{2}_{et}(\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\ast})_{\operatorname{tors}}.

It is known that for projective schemes this group is isomorphic to the usual Brauer group
Br(X)
of equivalences of Azumaya algebras
[
C
,
GS
]
.
Let
X
be a hyper-Kähler manifold with
H^{3}(X,\mathbb{Z})=0
(for example of
K3^{[n]}
-type) and
\beta\in H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\ast})_{\operatorname{tors}}
be a Brauer class.
From the exponential sequence

0\to\mathbb{Z}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{\exp(2\pi i(-))}\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\ast}\to 0,

and by the fact
H^{3}(X,\mathbb{Z})=0
, we infer that there exists
\alpha\in H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}),
such that

\exp(2\pi i(\alpha))=\beta.

If the order of
\beta
is
k

then
k\alpha
is the image of some class
B_{0}\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
and we call
B=\frac{B_{0}}{k}\in\frac{1}{k}H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
a B-lift of
\beta
.
Moreover, we denote by
[\frac{B_{0}}{k}]\in H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\ast})
the Brauer class corresponding to
\frac{B_{0}}{k}
.
Note that the above class
B
is not unique and we have the ambiguity of adding a class in
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
and a class in
H^{1,1}(X,\mathbb{Z}/k)
.
The following is straightforward.

Lemma 2.1
.

The Brauer class with B-lift
\frac{\delta}{2}
such that
\delta
is a
-2
class of divisibility
2
is uniquely defined, i.e. does not depend of the choice of
\delta
.

Proof.

Observe that
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
decomposes as an orthogonal sum of a unimodular lattice and a 1-dimensional lattice generated by a -2 class of divisibility 2. It follows that any two -2 classes
\delta_{1}
,
\delta_{2}
of divisibility 2 on
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
differ by a class from
2H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
and hence
[\frac{\delta_{1}}{2}]=[\frac{\delta_{2}}{2}]
.
∎

2.2.
Deformation of a derived equivalence

Nick Addington communicated to us the following general result concerning deformations of Fourier-Mukai kernels.

Proposition 2.2
.

For a flat family of coherent sheaves on a projective family of products of manifolds
X_{t}\times Y_{t}
with
t\in B
to be an equivalence (or
fully faithful) is an open condition.

Proof.

Let
X
and
Y
be smooth projective varieties. Let
E
in
D(X\times Y)
induce a
functor
F:X\to Y
. Then the left and right adjoints
L
and
R
are induced
by
E^{\ast}
tensor
\omega_{Y}[\dim Y]
and
\omega_{X}[\dim X]
, respectively, and the
unit and counit

\operatorname{id}_{X}\implies R\circ F

F\circ L\implies\operatorname{id}_{Y}

are induced by maps of kernels (
[
CW
, Section 3.1]
).
Now
F
is an equivalence if and only if this unit and counit are
isomorphisms, which happens if and only if the cones on the corresponding maps of kernels are zero.

In the case of smooth projective families
X\to B
and
Y\to B
, we can act analogously, and we are asking about the locus in
B

over which those cones are zero, i.e. the complement of the image of their
support. But the support is closed and our families are proper so the
complement of the image of the support is open.
∎

Note that the above proposition works only for local deformations and generally for coherent sheaves.
In this paper we need global deformations and work with twisted sheaves thus we consider below a finer variant of the previous proposition.

Our method consists of deforming a given Fourier-Mukai kernel giving a derived equivalence between two fixed hyper-Kähler manifolds that are Hilbert schemes of points on a K3 surface. Then spread it to twisted projectively hyperholomorphic sheaves over elements of the considered below moduli space of pairs of hyper-Kähler manifolds. The last step is to refine Proposition
2.2
in this context and prove that it gives a twisted derived equivalence at every element in this moduli.
Let us be more precise; the following is build on
[
Mar1
]
and we start by recalling some notions introduced there.

Denote by
\Lambda_{n}
the Beauville Bogomolov Fujiki lattice of hyper-Kähler fourfolds of
K3^{[n]}
-type. For
\psi:\Lambda_{n}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}\to\Lambda_{n}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
a rational isometry,
we consider the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{\psi}
of pairs
X_{t}
and
X_{t}^{\prime}
of marked hyper-Kähler fourfolds of
K3^{[n]}
-type such that
\psi
represents via the markings a rational Hodge isometry between
X_{t}
and
X_{t}^{\prime}
(see
[
Mar1
, Section 5.2]
and
[
O2
]
).
We assume that we have a twisted vector bundle
E_{0}
on
X_{0}\times X_{0}^{\prime}
that gives a twisted derived equivalence and is compatible with this isometry
[
Mar1
, Definition 5.20]
.

Recall that a poly-stable bundle is a direct sum of stable bundles with the same slope.
It is proven that two points of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\psi}^{0}
are connected by a sequence of generic twistor lines (
[
Mar1
, Lemma 5.15]
).
In this way, if
\mathcal{E}nd(E_{0})
is poly-stable hyperholomorphic with respect to an open set of Kähler classes as in
[
Mar1
, Proposition 5.19]
then for every
t
in the connected component
{\mathcal{M}}_{\psi}^{0}
of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\psi}
there is a twisted vector bundle
E_{t}
on
X_{t}\times X^{\prime}_{t}
such that the Azumaya algebras
\mathcal{E}nd(E_{t})
and
\mathcal{E}nd(E_{0})
are deformation equivalent.
By acting step by step we consider the case of a deformation along one twistor line as in
[
Mar3
, Proposition 5.12]
). Note also that on one twistor line we obtain a twisted sheaf
\mathcal{E}
such that
\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{E})
is a hyperholomorphic sheaf on this twistor family that restricts to
\mathcal{E}nd(E_{0})
above
X_{0}\times X_{0}^{\prime}
.

Recall also that a BKR equivalence is a vector bundle on
S^{[n]}\times M^{[n]}
naturally obtained from a Fourier-Mukai kernel between two derived equivalent projective K3 surfaces
M
and
S

as in
[
Mar1
, §7]
(see also Section
3
).
Suppose
E_{0}
on
X_{0}\times X_{0}^{\prime}
is obtained as a deformation along a twistor path of a BKR equivalence.
In this case, the sheaf
\mathcal{E}nd(E_{0})
is appropriately poly-stable hyperholomorphic from
[
Mar1
, §6 and §8]
thus for every
t\in{\mathcal{M}}^{0}_{\psi}
gives rise to twisted bundles
E_{t}
on
X_{t}\times X_{t}^{\prime}
.
For an introduction to twisted sheaves and derived categories of twisted sheaves see
[
Ca
,
CS
,
HS
,
Y
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose that the sheaf
E_{0}
on
X_{0}\times X^{\prime}_{0}
is the Fourier-Mukai kernel of a BKR equivalence
D(X_{0})=D(X_{0}^{\prime})
. Then, for
t\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\psi}^{0}
, a twisted bundle
E_{t}
obtained via the above construction gives rise to a twisted derived equivalence between
D(X_{t},\beta)=D(X_{t}^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
with twists
\beta\in Br(X_{t})
,
\beta^{\prime}\in Br(X_{t}^{\prime})
given by the Azumaya algebra
\mathcal{E}nd(E_{t})
.

Proof.

For
p\in X_{0}^{\prime}
, denote by
E_{0}^{p}
the fiber of
E_{0}
above
X_{0}
. It follows from
[
Mar2
, Theorem 1.4]
that each
E_{0}^{p}
is slope-stable for each Kähler class of an open subcone of the Kähler cone of
X_{0}
. Let
E_{t}^{p}
be the twisted sheaf on
X_{t}
that is the fiber of
E_{t}
above
p
for
p\in X_{t}^{\prime}
.
Note first that for,
p,q\in X_{t}^{\prime}
, the sheaves

\mathcal{E}nd(E^{p}_{t})\ \text{and}\ \mathcal{E}nd(E^{q}_{t})

are Morita equivalent so define a
Brauer class
\beta\in Br(X_{t})
(the summand along
X_{t}
of the Brauer class given by the Azumaya algebra
\mathcal{E}nd(E_{t})
).

From
[
Ca1
, 3.2.1]
, in order to show derived equivalence it is enough to show that

Ext^{k}(E^{p}_{t},E^{q}_{t})=0\ \text{for}\ k>0\ \text{or}\ p\neq q\ \text{and}\ Hom(E^{p}_{t},E^{p}_{t})=1.

Arguing as in the proof of
[
Mar2
, Theorem 1.4]
(cf.
[
Mar2
, Theorem 1.6]
) using
[
Mar1
, Lemma 8.3]
, we can find a special deformation
X_{t_{0}}
of
X_{0}
along a twistor path, such that
X_{t_{0}}
is isomorphic to the square of a non-algebraic K3 surface and such that
E_{t_{0}}^{p}
is poly-stable with respect to every Kähler class on
X_{t_{0}}
.
It follows from
[
V1
, Corollary 8.1]
that the dimensions of cohomologies of poly-stable hyperholomorphic vector bundles are invariant along twistor lines.
We infer that

H^{k}(\mathcal{E}nd(E_{t}^{p}))=Ext^{k}(E^{p}_{t},E^{p}_{t})=H^{k}(\mathcal{E}nd(E_{0}^{p}))

for every
t\in X_{0}^{\prime}
.

To consider different
p,q\in X^{\prime}_{t}
, we study the cohomologies of the bundle
\mathcal{H}om(E^{p}_{t},E^{q}_{t})
.
As before
E^{p}_{t}
and
E^{q}_{t}
are projectively hyperholomorphic, meaning

\mathcal{E}nd(E^{p}_{t})
and
\mathcal{E}nd(E^{q}_{t})
are hyperholomorphic.
Thus they give rise to twisted vector bundles
E^{p}
and
E^{q}
on the twistor family.
Then
E^{p}\oplus E^{q}
is also a twisted vector bundle on the twistor family therefore
\mathcal{E}nd(E^{p}\oplus E^{q})
is a vector bundle on the twistor family (so a hyperholomorphic bundle).

In order to see that
\mathcal{E}nd(E^{p}_{0}\oplus E^{q}_{0})
is poly-stable with respect to Kähler classes in an open subcone of the Kähler cone of
X_{0}
it is enough to observe that
E_{0}^{p}
and
E_{0}^{q}
are poly-stable with the same slopes. Thus, by
[
Mar4
, Lemma 9.1]

we have that

\mathcal{H}om(E^{p}_{0},E^{q}_{0}),\ \mathcal{H}om(E^{q}_{0},E^{p}_{0}),\ \mathcal{E}nd(E_{0}^{p})\ \text{and}\ \mathcal{E}nd(E_{0}^{q})

split as direct sums of indecomposable slope stable bundles of the same slope.
It follows from
[
V1
, Corollary 8.1]
that
the cohomologies
H^{k}(\mathcal{E}nd(E^{p}_{t}\oplus E^{q}_{t}))
are invariant for all deformations of
X_{0}
(see again the proof of
[
Mar2
, Theorem 1.4]
). We find in this way the cohomologies of the factors of

\mathcal{E}nd(E^{p}_{t}\oplus E_{t}^{q})=\mathcal{E}nd(E^{p}_{t})\oplus\mathcal{E}nd(E_{t}^{q})\oplus\mathcal{H}om(E^{p}_{t},E^{q}_{t})\oplus\mathcal{H}om(E^{q}_{t},E^{p}_{t})

and conclude that
H^{k}(\mathcal{H}om(E^{q}_{t},E^{p}_{t}))=0
(since we know that
\mathcal{E}nd(E^{p}_{t})
and
\mathcal{E}nd(E^{q}_{t})
are hyperholomorphic and their cohomologies are invariant too).
Finally, it is well known that the Brauer class of the twisted bundle
E_{t}
is the Morita equivalence class of
\mathcal{E}nd(E_{t})
(see
[
Ca1
, Theorem 1.3.5]
).
∎

Remark 2.4
.

We can naturally generalise the previous theorem for more general manifolds if we know that
E_{0}^{p}
is poly-stable with respect to all Kähler classes.

2.3.
The Brauer class after deformation

In this section we describe the Brauer twists
\beta\in H^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X_{t}}^{\ast})_{\operatorname{tors}}
and
\beta^{\prime}\in H^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{X_{t}}^{\ast})_{\operatorname{tors}}
of the deformed Fourier-Mukai kernel described in Theorem
2.3
and study their B-lifts.

Following
[
Ca
, §4]
, let
f:X\to S
be a proper
smooth morphism of analytic spaces, and with
0
a closed point of

S
.
Let
X_{0}
be the fiber of
f
over
0
. We consider an element

\alpha\in Br(X)
, such that
\alpha|_{X_{0}}
is trivial as an element
of
Br(X_{0})
, and we assume we are given a locally free

\alpha
-twisted sheaf
\mathcal{E}
on
X
. Since
\alpha|_{X_{0}}=0
, we
can modify the transition functions of
\mathcal{E}|_{X_{0}}
in
such a way that we get an untwisted locally free sheaf
\mathcal{E}_{0}
on

X_{0}
.

Theorem 2.5
.

[
Ca
, Theorem 4.1]

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a locally free
\alpha
-twisted sheaf on
X
, and let

\mathcal{E}_{0}=\mathcal{E}|_{X_{0}}
. Assume that
S
is small enough, so that we have an identification
H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Z})\simeq H^{i}(X_{t},\mathbb{Z})
for all
i
and all
t\in S
. We assume that

\alpha|_{X_{0}}=0
, and therefore we can modify the transition
functions of
\mathcal{E}_{0}
so that it is a usual sheaf on
X_{0}
. Then we
have

\alpha=\left[-\frac{1}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{E}_{0})}c_{1}(\mathcal{E}_{0})\right].

It follows that
\alpha|_{X_{t}}
is the Brauer class of the general fiber. We find the corresponding
B
-lifts through the isomorphism
H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Z})\simeq H^{i}(X_{t},\mathbb{Z})
.

Let us explain how we shall use this result in our situation. Recall that any pair of points in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\psi}
are connected by a sequence of generic twistor lines.
In order to understand the Brauer class in an arbitrary deformation of
E_{0}
it is enough to apply the above Theorem
2.5
along a generic twistor line. Indeed, it follows from
[
Mar3
, 6.7 (3)]
that the Azumaya algebra spreads in a canonical way along one twistor line. Above each point of this twistor line, this defines uniquely the Brauer class as a Morita equivalence of Azumaya algebras.
Now the B-lift of this Brauer class at a generic point of the twistor line is computed explicitly using Theorem
2.5
and is unique up to the ambiguity described in Section
2.1
so unique in

(\frac{1}{\mathrm{rk}(E_{0})}H^{2}(X_{t},\mathbb{Z}))/H^{2}(X_{t},\mathbb{Z}).

We conclude that the class in
(\frac{1}{\mathrm{rk}(E_{0})}H^{2}(X_{t},\mathbb{Z}))/H^{2}(X_{t},\mathbb{Z})
of this B-lift is also unique at any point
t\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\psi}
and is not dependent on the choice of the twistor path.

3.
Twisted derived equivalences of r-cyclic type

In this section, let us compare the (twisted) derived categories of pairs of two hyper-Kähler fourfolds of
K3^{[2]}
-type
X
and
Y
that have Hodge isometric transcendental lattices and the Hodge isometry relating them is represented by a single reflection
\rho_{r}
with respect to a class of degree
-2r
. We will introduce and investigate special twisted Fourier-Mukai kernels relating their (twisted) derived categories that will be called of
r
-cyclic type and will be used as building blocks in our constructions of (twisted) derived equivalences.

More precisely, let us recall from
[
Mar2
]
the following result. Let
X
and
Y
be two hyper-Kähler manifolds of
K3^{[n]}
-type.
For

f\colon H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{2}(Y,{\mathbb{Q}})

a rational Hodge isometry, we say is of
r
-cyclic type, for some integer
r
, if there exists a primitive class

u\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
that satisfies
(u,u)=2r
and

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
L_{X}
,
L_{Y}
be classes of divisibility 2 and degree
2d
in
\Lambda
and
T_{X}
,
T_{Y}
their orthogonal complements in
\Lambda

\phi:T_{X}\to T_{Y}

\Lambda
to
\rho_{r}:\bar{T}_{r,s,l}\to\bar{T}^{\prime}_{r,s,l}
for some
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
\gcd(r,s)=1
, and
l
odd such that
r|l
.

For the proof we will again work with discriminants. In this case, contrary to the case of divisibility 1, for a class

of divisibility 2 we have

with

\operatorname{disc}\langle L\rangle\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{2d}\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{d}

\gamma:\operatorname{disc}T_{L}\to\operatorname{disc}\langle L\rangle
such that

\Lambda=\{(p,q)\in\langle L\rangle^{*}\oplus T_{L}^{*}\subset\Lambda_{\mathbb{Q}}|\ \gamma_{L}([q])=-[p]\}.

In fact
\gamma_{L}
is an isometry between
\operatorname{disc}T_{L}
and the subgroup of elements in
\operatorname{disc}\langle L\rangle
whose order divides
d
.

In this case, an isometry
\phi:T_{L}\to T_{L^{\prime}}
extends to an autoisometry of
\Lambda
if and only if

\gamma_{L}\circ\bar{\phi}=\gamma_{L}^{\prime}
, where
\operatorname{disc}\langle L\rangle
is identified with
\operatorname{disc}\langle L^{\prime}\rangle
by identifying

. In consequence a map of lattices

is determined up to composing on both sides with automorphisms of
\Lambda
by
\gamma_{L}(\bar{\phi}(\gamma_{L^{\prime}}^{-1}(\frac{L^{\prime}}{d})))
. Note here that
\frac{L^{\prime}}{d}
as an element of order

is in the image of
\gamma_{L^{\prime}}
.

The following gives a description of possible isometries between the discriminant groups that could arise as isometries induced by some

Lemma 4.5
.

If

,
L^{\prime}
are classes of divisibility 2 and degree

\gamma_{L}(\bar{\phi}(\gamma_{L^{\prime}}^{-1}(\frac{L^{\prime}}{d})))=a[\frac{L}{d}]

Proof.

As we have observed above, the image of
\gamma_{L}
is equal to the locus of elements of order dividing
d
in
\operatorname{disc}(\langle L\rangle)
, i.e. is generated by
[\frac{L}{d}]
.
Recall now that
\gamma_{L}
and
\gamma_{L^{\prime}}
are antiisometries with respect to the lattice structures on
\operatorname{disc}(\langle L\rangle)
which implies that

a^{2}\frac{2d}{d^{2}}=\frac{2d}{d^{2}}\in\mathbb{Q}/2\mathbb{Z}.

It follows that
\frac{2a^{2}-2}{d}\in 2\mathbb{Z}
which implies
a^{2}\equiv 1\mod d
.
∎

It remains to compare the isometries from Lemma

with isometries arising from the restrictions of maps
\rho_{r}
to

Lemma 4.6

For

Let us denote

∎

5.2.
Proof of Theorem

We can now pass to the proof of Theorem

Take

6.
Twisted derived equivalences of abelian fibrations

In order to prove twisted derived equivalences of hyper-Kähler fourfolds with Picard rank
2
we have to study Fourier-Mukai kernels of rank
0
.
They are more difficult to treat because we cannot use the results from
[
Mar1
]
and deform them as hyperholomorphic bundles.
Indeed, consider a pair of hyper-Kähler fourfolds with Picard lattices
U(2)
and
2\oplus(-2)
having the
-2
class of divisibility
2
in the transcendental lattice.
Then those manifolds have isomorphic transcendental lattices with discriminant
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3}
. The corresponding automorphism of the discriminant is given by the matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&1&0\\
0&1&1\\
0&0&1\end{array}\right).

We cannot describe this automorphism as a specialisation of a reflection so we cannot construct the derived equivalence as a specialisation of an equivalence between hyper-Kähler fourfolds of Picard rank
1
. Let us first introduce some useful tools.

6.1.

7.
Summary

The strategy described in the previous section can be applied in many cases.
Let us describe several twisted derived equivalences of hyper-Kähler manifolds of Picard rank
\geq 2
.
For a K3 surface

of degree

there are a lot of example of moduli spaces of twisted sheaves

More precisely, we choose a

that we call

If

If we use Lemma

As in the previous section we can prove the following.

The following problems arise.

Problem 7.2
.

Are all pairs of derived equivalent hyper-Kähler manifolds of
K3^{[2]}
-type related by a composition of the constructions described in this paper?

In particular are two

(cf.
[

Are the equivalences constructed in Theorem

Problem 7.4

For all currently known deformation types of hyper-Kähler manifolds a derived equivalence

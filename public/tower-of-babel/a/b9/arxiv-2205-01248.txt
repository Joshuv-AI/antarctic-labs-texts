Short time existence and smoothness of the nonlocal mean curvature flow of graphs Thanks: Keywords: Nonlocal mean curvature flow; Quasilinear evolution equations; Analytic semigroup theory; Fixed point theorem.
Anoumou Attiogbe, Mouhamed Moustapha Fall, Tobias Weth

Abstract
. We consider the geometric evolution problem of entire graphs moving by fractional mean curvature. For this, we study the associated nonlocal quasilinear evolution equation satisfied by the family of graph functions. We establish, using an analytic semigroup approach, short time existence, uniqueness and optimal Hölder regularity in time and space of classical solutions of the nonlocal equation, depending on the regularity of the initial graph. The method also yields C ∞ − C^{\infty}- smoothness estimates of the evolving graphs for positive times.



1.
Introduction

In the present paper, we study the geometric evolution problem of hypersurfaces moving by fractional mean curvature. More precisely we consider the problem of existence and uniqueness of a family of (sufficiently regular) open subsets
\{E(t)\}_{t>0}
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
satisfying

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}X_{t}\cdot\nu(X_{t}):=-H_{E(t)}^{\alpha}(X_{t}),\;\;\;\mbox{for all $X_{t}\in\partial E(t)$ and }t\in[0,T],

where,
\nu
is the unit normal vector field on
\partial E(t)
and
H_{E(t)}^{\alpha}
is the fractional mean curvature of order
\alpha\in(0,1)
at
X_{t}\in\partial E(t)
.
Recall that for a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
of class
C^{1+\beta}
, with
\beta>\alpha
, the fractional (nonlocal) mean curvature of
\partial E
is well defined and it is given at a point
x\in\partial E
by

(1.2)

\displaystyle H_{E}^{\alpha}(x):=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{E^{c}}(y)-\mathbbm{1}_{E}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+\alpha}}dy=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(x)}\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{E^{c}}(y)-\mathbbm{1}_{E}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+\alpha}}dy,

where
\mathbbm{1}_{E}
denotes the characteristic function of the set
E
and
{E^{c}}:=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus E
.
We recall that the notion of fractional (nonlocal) mean curvature of order
\alpha\in(0,1)
appeared for the first time in the work of Caffarelli and Souganidis in
[
8
]
around 2008. Since that time, the nonlocal mean curvature has been studied extensively in various settings, see e.g.
[
1
,
5
,
25
,
16
]
and the references therein.

As a nonlocal conterpart of the classical mean curvature flow (see e.g.
[
18
,
23
]
and the references therein), the nonlocal mean curvature flow has attracted much interest in recent years, see e.g
[
14
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
19
,
20
,
25
,
3
]
and the survey paper
[
13
]
.
The first paper dealing with the existence and uniqueness of solutions of this flow is
[
19
]
, where Imbert used the level set formulation of the geometric flow to prove existence and uniqueness of viscosity solutions while the recent papers
[
10
]
and
[
12
]
deal with regularity results of viscosity solution of the flow starting from Lipschitz graphs. We also mention the paper
[
25
]
where the autors proved a
comparison principle for the flow (
1.1
) and found bounds on
the maximal existence time and uniqueness of smooth solutions.

Despite the attention that the nonlocal mean curvature flow has already received, the existence and regularity of classical solutions remained an open problem until the recent work
[
20
]
. In this paper, the authors prove short time existence and uniqueness of classical solution to the fractional mean curvature flow, starting from a bounded
C^{1,1}
initial set.

In the present paper, we consider the complementary case of short time existence and uniqueness of classical solutions of problem (
1.1
) when the sets
E(t)
are given by subgraphs of functions
u(t,\cdot)\in C^{1+\beta}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
, where
\beta>\alpha
.
We recall first that if
E_{u}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times\mathbb{R}:y<u(x)\}
then, see e.g.
[
16
, Proposition 3.5]
, we have that

(1.3)

\displaystyle H(u)(x):=H^{\alpha}_{E_{u}}(x,u(x))=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{{\mathcal{G}}(p_{u}({x},{y}))}{|{x}-{y}|^{N-1+\alpha}}d{y},

where

(1.4)

p_{u}({x},{y})=\frac{u({y})-u({x})}{|{x}-{y}|},\qquad{\mathcal{G}}(p):=-\int_{-p}^{p}\frac{d\tau}{(1+\tau^{2})^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2}}}.

We point out that, due to the boundedness of the function
{\mathcal{G}}
, the expression
H(u)(x)
is well-defined if
u:\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\to\mathbb{R}
is measurable and of class
C^{1+\beta}
for some
\beta>\alpha
in a neighborhood of
x
. With this expression of the fractional mean curvature, we can derive the quasilinear evolution problem corresponding to (
1.1
) when
E_{u}(t):=\{(x(t),y(t))\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times\mathbb{R}:y(t)<u(t,x(t))\}
, with

u:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\to\mathbb{R}
. Indeed, recall that the unit interior normal vector field on
\partial E_{u}(t)
is given by
\nu(X_{t}):=\frac{(-\nabla u(t,x(t)),1)}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u(t,x(t))|^{2}}}
. Therefore for every
X_{t}\in\partial E_{u}(t)=\{(x(t),u(t,x(t))):x(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\}
, we have that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}X_{t}=\Big(\dot{x}(t),\dot{x}(t)\cdot\nabla u(t,x(t))+\partial_{t}u(t,x(t))\Big).

Hence

\displaystyle\partial_{t}X_{t}\cdot\nu(X_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{-\dot{x}(t)\cdot\nabla u(t,x(t))}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u(t,x(t))|^{2}}}+\frac{\dot{x}(t)\cdot\nabla u(t,x(t))}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u(t,x(t))|^{2}}}+\frac{\partial_{t}u(t,x(t))}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u(t,x(t))|^{2}}}

(1.5)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial_{t}u(t,x(t))}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u(t,x(t))|^{2}}}.

Therefore, from (
1
) and (
1.1
), the evolution of
u
is given by
the flow associated to the quasilinear evolution equation

(1.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=-{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}H(u),\;\;\;t\in(0,T],\;\;\;\;\;u(0)=u_{0}.

Our first main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\nu>0
,
\beta\in(\alpha,1)
,
\rho\in(0,\frac{1}{1+\alpha})
and
\gamma_{\rho}:=\beta+\rho(1+\alpha)
. Then for all
u_{0}\in C^{1+\gamma_{\rho}}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
, with
\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{C^{\gamma_{\rho}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}\leq\nu
, there exist
T,C_{0}>0
only depending on
\rho,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,N
and
\nu
such that the problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\partial_{t}u+{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}H(u)&=0&\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{in }\;[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\\
u(0)&=u_{0}&\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{in }\;\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\end{array}\right.

admits a unique solution
u\in C^{\rho}([0,T],C^{1+\beta}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}))\cap C^{1+\rho}([0,T],C^{\beta-\alpha}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}))
satisfying

(1.9)

\|u-u_{0}\|_{C^{\rho}([0,T],C^{1+\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}))\cap C^{1+\rho}([0,T],C^{\beta-\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}))}\leq C_{0}.

If, in addition,
\nabla u_{0}\in C^{1+\gamma_{\rho}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
then for all
\beta^{\prime}\in(\alpha,\beta)
there exists
C>0
only depending on
\rho,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,N,\nu,T
and
\beta^{\prime}
such that

(1.10)

\|\nabla u\|_{C^{\rho}([0,T],C^{1+\beta^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}))}\leq C\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{C^{1+\gamma_{\rho}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}.

The following result complements Theorem
1.1
by providing, under the same assumptions, smoothness estimates for positive times.

Theorem 1.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, we have
u(t,\cdot)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
for every
t\in(0,T]
.
Moreover, for every
\beta^{\prime}\in(\alpha,\beta)
,
\rho\in(0,\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}]
and for all
k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, there exists
C_{k}>0
only depending on
\rho,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,N,\nu,\beta^{\prime},T
and
k
such that

(1.11)

\|t^{k}\nabla u\|_{C^{\rho}([0,T],C^{k+\beta^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}))}\leq C_{k}.

Related to Theorem
1.2
, we also mention
[
12
]
, where the authors state that solutions to the fractional mean flow of graph, starting from a Lipschitz graph, are
C^{\infty}
-smooth for positive times provided
H(u_{0})\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
. However
[
12
]
does not provide enough details that confirm this statement. Indeed, the authors apply a priori Hölder regularity estimates from
[
27
,
26
]
to the linearization of the first equation of (
1.1
). However
the linearization of the weighted nonlocal mean curvature operator
u\mapsto{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}H(u)
does not fall in the class of nonlocal operators considered in
[
27
,
26
]
.

Our next result provides universal estimates of the Lipschitz norm and the mean curvature of the evolving graph in terms of the initial data.

Theorem 1.3
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, we have

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}\leq\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}

and

\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}\leq\|{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}}}H(u_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}.

If moreover
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
, then
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}.

As we shall see below, the existence in Theorem
1.1
is a consequence of a more general existence result concerning problem (
1.1
). Indeed, we define first the Banach space

{\mathcal{C}}^{\theta}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})=\overline{C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}^{\|\cdot\|_{C^{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}}\qquad\textrm{ for $\theta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus\mathbb{N}$},

endowed with the norm of
C^{\theta}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
. We then write (
1.1
) as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\partial_{t}u+{\mathcal{L}}_{0}u&=F(u)&\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{in }\;[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\\
u(0)&=u_{0}&\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{in }\;\mathbb{R}^{N-1},\end{array}\right.

where
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}:=D{\mathcal{H}}(u_{0}):{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\to{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\alpha}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
is the derivative of the weighted mean curvature operator

(1.14)

\displaystyle u\mapsto{\mathcal{H}}(u):={\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}H(u)

at the initial condition
u_{0}
and the nonlinear map
u\mapsto F(u)=-{\mathcal{H}}(u)+{\mathcal{L}}_{0}u
has the property that
F(u)-F(u_{0})
has superlinear growth in
u-u_{0}
. Our strategy is now to show that
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}
generates a strongly continuous analytic semigroup on
{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\alpha}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
and to apply a fixed point argument. However, we need precise estimates, as we want all the regularity estimated by the Hölder norms of
\nabla u_{0}
.

The main result from which we partly derive Theorem
1.1
is the following

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\beta\in(\alpha,1)
,
\nu>0
,
\rho\in(0,\frac{1}{1+\alpha})
and
\gamma_{\rho}:=\beta+\rho(1+\alpha)
. Then, there exists
T>0
, depending only on
\rho,\alpha,\beta,N
and
\nu
, such that for all
u_{0}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
, with

\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{C^{\gamma_{\rho}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}\leq\nu,

the initial value problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}\partial_{t}u+{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u|^{2}}}H(u)&=0&\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{in }\;[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\\
u(0)&=u_{0}&\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{in }\;\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\end{array}\right.

admits a unique solution
u\in C^{1+\rho}([0,T],{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\alpha}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}))\cap C^{\rho}([0,T],{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}))
. Moreover, there exists
C_{0}>0
, depending only on
\rho,\alpha,\beta,N
and
\nu
, such that

(1.17)

\|u-u_{0}\|_{C^{1+\rho}([0,T],C^{\beta-\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}))\cap C^{\rho}([0,T],C^{1+\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}))}\leq C_{0}.

We note that, thanks to the regularity of
E_{u}(t)
proved in Theorem
1.4
, the uniqueness of the flow
(E_{u}(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
also follows from
[
25
]
, where the authors proved that (
1.1
) admits a unique classical solution.
On the other hand, the proof of our Theorem
1.4
yields existence and uniqueness simultaneously.

Recall that, provided the linear operator
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}:=D{\mathcal{H}}(u_{0}):{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\to{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\alpha}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
generates an analytic semigroup, to get optimal
C^{\rho}([0,T],{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}))
-regularity in time for the problem (
1
), it is necessary and sufficient that
u_{0}
satisfies
F(u_{0})-{\mathcal{L}}_{0}u_{0}=-{\mathcal{H}}(u_{0})\in\mathcal{D}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{0}}(\rho,\infty)
. Here
\mathcal{D}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{0}}(\rho,\infty)
is a real interpolation space between the space
{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\alpha}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
and the domain
{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
. We refer the reader to Lunardi
[
22
]
and Sinestrari
[
28
]
on this type of optimal regularity.

We shall show here that
\mathcal{D}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{0}}(\rho,\infty)=C^{\beta+\rho(1+\alpha)-\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\alpha}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
for
\rho\not=\frac{1+\alpha-\beta}{1+\alpha}
(see Proposition
3.3
below). In addition, we will prove that
{\mathcal{H}}(u_{0})\in\mathcal{D}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{0}}(\rho,\infty)
if and only if
\nabla u_{0}\in C^{\beta+\rho(1+\alpha)}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
.

The proof of Theorem
1.4
is given in Section
5
below. Main steps in this proof consist in establishing suitable regularity properties of the fractional mean curvature operator
H
in Hölder spaces and in showing that the operator
D{\mathcal{H}}(u_{0}):{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\subset{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\alpha}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\to{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\alpha}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
is the generator of a strongly continuous analytic semigroup. We shall derive the generation of a strongly analytic semigroup by combining a result from
[
2
]
for a class of non-smooth integro-differential operators defined
{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
with Hölder estimates for integral operators with anisotropic kernels represented suitably in polar coordinates. To obtain existence and uniqueness for the non homogeneous Cauchy problem (
1
), we also need a characterization of the real interpolation space
\mathcal{D}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{0}}(\rho,\infty)
, see Section
3.2
.
Once Theorem
1.4
is established, we derive Theorem
1.1
from it by an approximation argument. The uniqueness in Theorem
1.1
and the universal estimates in Theorem
1.3
are based on a suitable version of the comparison principle, which we also prove. In fact, our comparison principle yields further interesting qualitative properties of the flow such as preservation of positivity of
u_{0}
and
H(u_{0})
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the function spaces we will use in this paper and we prove some preliminary results. Section
3
is devoted to the strongly continuous analytic semigroup generated by a general class of nonlocal operators. Section
4
deals with the regularity properties satisfied by the weighted nonlocal mean curvature operator
{\mathcal{H}}(u)
and its linearization. Finally, in Section
5
, we prove our main results.

2.
Notation and preliminary estimates

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
C_{b}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
the space of
m
-times bounded continuously differentiable functions endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C_{b}^{m}}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\|D^{k}u\|_{L^{\infty}}:=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}|D^{k}u(x)|.

For
0<\gamma<1
, we consider the space of Hölder continuous functions

(2.1)

\displaystyle C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})=\bigg\{u\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}):\,[u]_{C^{\gamma}}:=\sup_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}}{{x\neq y}}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\gamma}}<\infty\bigg\}

endowed with the norm

\displaystyle u\mapsto\|u\|_{C^{\gamma}}=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+[u]_{C^{\gamma}}.

More generally, for
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<\gamma<1
, we let

C^{m+\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})=\{u\in C_{b}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}):D^{m}u\in C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\},

endowed with the norm

u\mapsto\|u\|_{C^{m+\gamma}}=\|u\|_{C_{b}^{m}}+[D^{m}u]_{C^{\gamma}}.

For late use, we recall the interpolation inequality that, for all
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
\gamma^{\prime}\in[0,\gamma)
and for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

(2.2)

\|\nabla v\|_{C^{\gamma^{\prime}}}\leq\varepsilon\|\nabla v\|_{C^{\gamma}}+C_{\varepsilon}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\qquad\textrm{ for all $v\in C^{1,\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})$.}

Next, we define

(2.3)

{\mathcal{C}}^{m+\gamma}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}):=\overline{C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}^{\|\cdot\|_{C^{m+\gamma}}}

endowed with the
C^{m+\gamma}
-norm. We then recall the following result.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
2
, Proposition 2.1]

Let
\beta,\gamma\in(0,1)
, with
\gamma>\beta
. Let
u\in C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
be such that
\lim_{R\to\infty}\|f\|_{C^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\setminus B_{R})}=0
. Then
f\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
.

We need some more notation related to space of time dependent functions. Let
{\mathcal{X}}
be a Banach space. For
T>0
, we consider the Banach space
L^{\infty}([0,T],{\mathcal{X}})
, consisting of bounded functions
u:[0,T]\rightarrow{\mathcal{X}}.
The spaces
L^{\infty}([0,T],{\mathcal{X}})
is endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],{\mathcal{X}})}=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{X}}.

For
\mu\in(0,1)
, we define the Banach space

C^{1+\mu}([0,T],{\mathcal{X}})=\left\{u\in C^{1}([0,T],{\mathcal{X}})\,:\,[\partial_{t}u]_{C^{\mu}([0,T],{\mathcal{X}})}=\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\frac{\|\partial_{t}u(t)-\partial_{t}u(s)\|_{\mathcal{X}}}{|t-s|^{\mu}}<+\infty\right\},

endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{C^{1+\mu}([0,T],{\mathcal{X}})}=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],{\mathcal{X}})}+[\partial_{t}u]_{C^{\mu}([0,T],{\mathcal{X}})}.

2.1.
Estimates in polar coordinates

In our study of linearizations of the nonlocal mean curvature operator, we will have to consider integral operators whose associated kernels have anisotropic singularities which can be resolved partially in polar coordinates. For this, the following basic estimates will be of key importance.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\gamma\in(\alpha,1)
,
v\in C^{1+\gamma}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
be such that
\nabla v\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
. Let
\mu\in C^{\gamma-\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times[0,\infty)\times S^{N-2})
and
\nu\in C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times[0,\infty)\times S^{N-2})
with
\nu(\cdot,0,\cdot)=0
. We define

I_{e}v(x):=\int_{S^{N-2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\delta_{e}v(x,r,\theta)\mu(x,r,\theta)r^{-2-\alpha}drd\theta

and

I_{o}v(x):=\int_{S^{N-2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\delta_{o}v(x,r,\theta)\nu(x,r,\theta)r^{-2-\alpha}drd\theta

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
, where

(2.4)

\delta_{e}v(x,r,\theta):=\frac{1}{2}(2v(x)-v(x+r\theta)-v(x-r\theta))\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\delta_{o}v(x,r,\theta):=v(x)-v(x-r\theta).

Then the functions
I_{e}v
and
I_{o}v
satisfy the following estimates with constants
C
independent of
\mu,\nu
and
v
.

(i)

We have

(2.5)

\|I_{o}v\|_{C^{\gamma-\alpha}}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nu\|_{C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times[0,\infty)\times S^{N-2})}

and if
\nabla v\in C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
, then

(2.6)

\|I_{e}v\|_{C^{\gamma-\alpha}}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{C^{\gamma}}\|\mu\|_{C^{\gamma-\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times[0,\infty)\times S^{N-2})}.

(ii)

If
\nabla v\in C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
and
\nabla v
is compactly supported, then

(2.7)

\lim_{R\to\infty}\|I_{e}v\|_{C^{\gamma-\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\setminus B_{R})}=\lim_{R\to\infty}\|I_{o}v\|_{C^{\gamma-\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\setminus B_{R})}=0.

(iii)

If
v\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
, with
\beta\in(\alpha,\gamma)
, then
I_{e}v,I_{o}v\in{{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\alpha}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
.

Proof.

We assume for simplicity that
\|\nu\|_{C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times[0,\infty)\times S^{N-2})}\leq 1
.
We first observe that

(2.8)

|\nu(x,r,\theta)|\leq\min(1,r^{\gamma}),\qquad|\delta_{o}v(x,r,\theta)|=r\left|\int_{0}^{1}\nabla v(x-rt\theta)\cdot\theta dt\right|\leq r\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}

and for
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}

(2.9)

|\nu(x,r,\theta)-\nu(x^{\prime},r,\theta)|\leq\min(|x-x^{\prime}|^{\gamma},r^{\gamma}),\qquad

and

(2.10)

|\delta_{o}v(x,r,\theta)-\delta_{o}v(x^{\prime},r,\theta)|\leq 2\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}\min(|x-x^{\prime}|,r).

Then, by (
2.8
), we have

\displaystyle\|I_{o}v\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\int_{S^{N-2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\delta_{o}v(x,r,\theta)\nu(x,r,\theta)|r^{-2-\alpha}drd\theta

(2.11)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\min(1,r^{\gamma})r^{-1-\alpha}dr\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}.

We write

\displaystyle I_{o}v(x)-I_{o}v(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{N-2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-2-\alpha}\delta_{o}v(x,r,\theta)[\nu(x,r,\theta)-\nu(z,r,\theta)]\;drd\theta

\displaystyle+\int_{S^{N-2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-2-\alpha}[\delta_{o}v(x,r,\theta)-\delta_{o}v(z,r,\theta)]\nu(x,r,\theta)\;drd\theta.

From this, (
2.8
), (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), we get

\displaystyle|I_{o}v(x)-I_{o}v(z)|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-1-\alpha}\min\{r^{\gamma},|x-z|^{\gamma}\}\;dr

\displaystyle+C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\gamma-2-\alpha}\min\{|x-z|,r\}\;dr\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}|x-z|^{\gamma-\alpha}.

Therefore

(2.12)

\displaystyle[I_{o}v]_{C^{\gamma-\alpha}}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}.

The inequalities (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) imply (
2.5
).

For simplicity, we assume that

\|\mu\|_{C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times[0,\infty)\times S^{N-2})}\leq 1
.
We write

(2.13)

\delta_{e}v(x,r,\theta)=\frac{r}{2}\int_{0}^{1}[\nabla v(x-rt\theta)-\nabla v(x+rt\theta)]dt.

Hence, we have

(2.14)

|\delta_{e}v(x,r,\theta)|\leq r\min(1,r^{\gamma})\|\nabla v\|_{C^{\gamma}}

and for
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}

(2.15)

|\mu(x,r,\theta)-\mu(x^{\prime},r,\theta)|\leq\min(|x-x^{\prime}|^{\gamma},1)

and

(2.16)

|\delta_{e}v(x,r,\theta)-\delta_{e}v(x^{\prime},r,\theta)|\leq r\|\nabla v\|_{C^{\gamma}}\min(|x-x^{\prime}|^{\gamma},r^{\gamma}).

We now estimate, using (
2.14
),

\displaystyle\|I_{e}v\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\int_{S^{N-2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\delta_{e}v(x,r,\theta)\mu(x,r,\theta)|r^{-2-\alpha}drd\theta

(2.17)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{C^{\gamma}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\min(1,r^{\gamma})r^{-1-\alpha}dr\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{C^{\gamma}}.

We have

\displaystyle I_{e}v(x)-I_{e}v(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{N-2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-2-\alpha}\delta_{e}v(x,r,\theta)[\mu(x,r,\theta)-\mu(z,r,\theta)]\;drd\theta

\displaystyle+\int_{S^{N-2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-2-\alpha}[\delta_{e}v(x,r,\theta)-\delta_{e}v(z,r,\theta)]\mu(x,r,\theta)\;drd\theta.

From this, (
2.8
), (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), we get

\displaystyle|I_{e}v(x)-I_{e}v(z)|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{C^{\gamma}}\min\{1,|x-z|^{\gamma}\}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-1-\alpha}\ \min\{1,r^{\gamma}\};dr

\displaystyle+C\|\nabla v\|_{C^{\gamma}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-1-\alpha}\min\{|x-z|^{\gamma},r^{\gamma}\}\;dr\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{C^{\gamma}}|x-z|^{\gamma-\alpha}.

This and (
2.17
) give (
2.6
).
The proof of
(i)
is thus complete.

For
(ii)
, we assume that Supp
\nabla v\subset B_{R^{\prime}}
, for some
R^{\prime}>1
.
We let
R>2R^{\prime}
and we split

\displaystyle I_{e}v(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{N-2}}\int_{0}^{R}\delta_{e}v(x,r,\theta)\mu(x,r,\theta)r^{-2-\alpha}drd\theta+\int_{S^{N-2}}\int_{R}^{\infty}\delta_{e}v(x,r,\theta)\mu(x,r,\theta)r^{-2-\alpha}drd\theta

\displaystyle=:I_{e}^{1}(x)+I_{e}^{2}(x).

By (
2.13
),

(2.18)

I_{e}^{1}(x)=0\qquad\textrm{ for $|x|\geq R^{\prime}+R$.}

In addition, for
|x|>R

),

(2.19)

\displaystyle|I_{e}^{2}(x)|\leq\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{R}^{\frac{|x|+R^{\prime}}{t}}r^{-1-\alpha}dr\,dt\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(R^{-\alpha}+(|x|+R^{\prime})^{-\alpha}\right),

so that

(2.20)

\lim_{R\to\infty}\|I_{e}\|_{L^{\infty}}=0.

Now, for

,

\displaystyle|I_{e}^{2}(x+h)-I_{e}^{2}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq|h|^{\gamma-\alpha}\|\nabla v\|_{C^{\gamma-\alpha}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{R}^{\frac{|x|+|h|+R^{\prime}}{t}}r^{-1-\alpha}dr\,dt

Combining this with (
2.20
) and (

), we deduce that
\lim_{R\to\infty}\|I_{e}v\|_{C^{\gamma-\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\setminus B_{R})}=0
. By similar argument we have

, so that (

) holds.

For
(iii)
, we pick

in

∎

2.2.
A nonlocal comparison principle in the entire space

In this subsection we provide a comparison principle for a class of nonlocal evolution equations relying on kernel assumptions in polar coordinates, which will be used later on.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\beta\in(\alpha,1)
,
T>0
,
V\in C\left([0,T],C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\right)
and let
let
\mu\in C\left([0,T],C^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times[0,\infty)\times S^{N-2})\right)
with
\mu>0
and
\mu(t,x,0,\theta)=\mu(t,x,0,-\theta)

Moreover, let
\beta>\alpha
, and let
u\in L^{\infty}([0,T],C^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}))
satisfy

u(\cdot,x)\in C^{1}([0,T])\quad\text{for every $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}$,}\quad u(t,\cdot)\in C^{1+\beta}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\quad\text{for every $t\in[0,T]$}

and

(2.21)

\partial_{t}u(t,x)+P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{u(t,x)-u(t,x+y)}{|y|^{N+\alpha}}\mu(t,x,|y|,y/|y|)\,dy+V(t,x)\cdot\nabla u(t,x)\leq 0

for
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
.
Then
\displaystyle\sup_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}u=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}u(0,x).

Proof.

We consider
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{2})
with
0\leq\eta\leq 1
on
\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
and
\eta=1
on
B_{1}
. We define, for
\varepsilon,R>0
,

v(t,x)=\eta_{R}(x)u(t,x)-\varepsilon t,

where
\eta_{R}(x)=\eta(x/R)
.
We also define, for
w\in C^{1+\beta}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
,

\overline{L}(t)w(x)=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{w(x)-w(x+y)}{|y|^{N+\alpha}}\mu(t,x,|y|,y/|y|)\,dy.

We then have

\displaystyle\overline{L}(t)(\eta_{R}u(t))(x)=\eta_{R}(x)\overline{L}(t)(u(t))(x)

\displaystyle+u(t,x)P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{\eta_{R}(x)-\eta_{R}(x+y)}{|y|^{N+\alpha}}\mu(t,x,|y|,y/|y|)\,dy

\displaystyle+P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{\eta_{R}(x)-\eta_{R}(x+y)}{|y|^{N+\alpha}}(u(t,x+y)-u(t,x))\mu(t,x,|y|,y/|y|)\,dy.

From this, we get

\displaystyle\overline{L}(t)(\eta_{R}u(t))(x)-\eta_{R}(x)P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{u(t,x)-u(t,x+y)}{|y|^{N+\alpha}}\mu(t,x,|y|,y/|y|)\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{u(t,x)}{2}P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{2\eta_{R}(x)-\eta_{R}(x-y)-\eta_{R}(x+y)}{|y|^{N+\alpha}}\mu(t,x,0,y/|y|)\,dy

\displaystyle+{u(t,x)}P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{\eta_{R}(x)-\eta_{R}(x+y)}{|y|^{N+\alpha}}[\mu(t,x,|y|,y/|y|)-\mu(t,x,0,y/|y|)]\,dy

\displaystyle+P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{\eta_{R}(x)-\eta_{R}(x+y)}{|y|^{N+\alpha}}(u(t,x+y)-u(t,x))\mu(t,x,|y|,y/|y|)\,dy.

Hence, by Lemma
2.2
we get

\|\overline{L}(t)(\eta_{R}u(t))-\eta_{R}\overline{L}(t)u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}\leq C\|\nabla\eta_{R}\|_{C^{1}_{b}}\|u(t)\|_{C^{\beta}}\leq\frac{C}{R}\|u(t)\|_{C^{\beta}}

for
t\in[0,T]
. From this and (
2.21
), we then obtain

(2.22)

\partial_{t}v+\overline{L}(t)v+V\cdot\nabla v\leq-\varepsilon+F_{R}\qquad\textrm{ in $[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}$,}

with

(2.23)

\|F_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}\leq\frac{C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T),C^{\beta})}}{R}(1+\|V\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}).

We claim that

(2.24)

\max_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}v=\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}v(0,x).

Indeed, let
(t_{0},x_{0})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
be such that
v(t_{0},x_{0})=\max_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}v
. Suppose that
t_{0}>0
. The maximality property then implies that
\nabla_{x}v(t_{0},x_{0})=0
,
\partial_{t}v(t_{0},x_{0})\geq 0
and also
L(t_{0})v(x_{0})\geq 0
, since
\mu\geq 0
by assumption. By (
2.22
) we thus have

0\leq\overline{L}(t_{0})v(x_{0})\leq-\varepsilon+\|F_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}

which is not possible if
R
is large enough, thanks to (
2.23
). Therefore
t_{0}=0
and thus (
2.24
) holds as claimed.

Letting now
R\to\infty
and then
\varepsilon\to 0
in (
2.24
), we get the result.
∎

3.
Analytic semigroups, their generators, intermediate spaces and associated semilinear evolution equations

We begin by introducing some notions regarding function spaces and analytic semigroups (see e.g.
[
22
, Chapter 2]
).
For normed vector spaces
{\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}
we let
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime})
denote the space of continuous linear operators
{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}
, endowed with the usual operator norm. As usual, we also set
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}}):={\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{X}})
.

A strongly continuous analytic semigroup on a Banach space
{\mathcal{X}}
is a family of operators
\{T(t)\}_{t\geq 0}\subset{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}})
with the following properties:

(i)

T(0)=\id
,
T(t+s)=T(t)T(s)
for all
t,s\geq 0
.

(ii)

The function
(0,\infty)\to{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}})
,
t\mapsto T(t)
is analytic.

(iii)

The function
[0,\infty)\to{\mathcal{X}}
,
t\mapsto T(t)u
is continuous for every
u\in{\mathcal{X}}
.

The generator of such a semigroup is given as an (unbounded) linear operator
B_{0}:{\mathcal{Y}}\subset{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
by

B_{0}u:=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{T(t)u-u}{t},\qquad u\in{\mathcal{Y}},

where the domain
{\mathcal{Y}}\subset{\mathcal{X}}
of
B_{0}
is given as the subspace of
u\in{\mathcal{X}}
for which this limit exists.

We recall that an operator
B_{0}:{\mathcal{Y}}\subset{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
generates a strongly continuous analytic semigroup in this sense if and only if
B_{0}
is sectorial (see e.g.
[
22
, Def. 2.0.1]
for a definition) and its domain
{\mathcal{Y}}
is dense in
{\mathcal{X}}
. In such a case, the operator
B_{0}
is also closed, which means that
{\mathcal{Y}}
is a Banach space with the graph norm

u\mapsto\|u\|_{{\mathcal{X}}}+\|{\mathcal{B}}_{0}u\|_{{\mathcal{X}}}
on
{\mathcal{Y}}
. As a consequence, by the open mapping theorem, the graph norm on
{\mathcal{Y}}
is equivalent to any other given norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{Y}}}
on
{\mathcal{Y}}
for which
({\mathcal{Y}},\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{Y}}})
is a Banach space.

In the following, if
{\mathcal{B}}_{0}:{\mathcal{Y}}\subset{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
generates a strongly continuous analytic semigroup, we shall denote this semigroup by

t\mapsto e^{B_{0}t}\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{X}}),\qquad t\geq 0.

As noted in
[
22
, Proposition 2.1.1]
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
there exists
M_{k}>0
, such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|t^{k}B_{0}^{k}e^{B_{0}t}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y)}\leq M_{k},\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{for all $t\in(0,1]$.}

In order to obtain optimal regularity estimates in time, it is convenient to introduce, for
\rho\in[0,1)
, the intermediate space

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{B_{0}}(\rho,\infty)=\{f\in{\mathcal{Y}}:[f]_{\mathcal{D}_{B_{0}}(\rho,\infty)}=\sup_{0<t\leq 1}\|t^{1-\rho}B_{0}e^{B_{0}t}f\|_{\mathcal{Y}}<\infty\},

endowed with the norm
\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{B_{0}}(\rho,\infty)}=\|f\|_{\mathcal{Y}}+[f]_{\mathcal{D}_{B_{0}}(\rho,\infty)}.

We then have the following result taken from
[
22
, Theorem 4.3.1 (iii)]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
B_{0}:{\mathcal{Y}}\subset{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
be the generator of a strongly continuous analytic semigroup. Let
T>0
,
\rho\in(0,1)
,
u_{0}\in{\mathcal{X}}
, and let

f\in C^{\rho}([0,T],{\mathcal{Y}})
be such that
f(0)-B_{0}u_{0}\in{\mathcal{D}_{B_{0}}(\rho,\infty)}
. Then the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}u^{\prime}(t)+B_{0}u(t)&=&f(t),\hskip 28.45274ptt\in(0,T]\\
u(0)&=&u_{0}\end{array}\right.

admits a unique solution
u\in C^{\rho}([0,T],{\mathcal{X}})\cap C^{1+\rho}([0,T],{\mathcal{Y}})
. Moreover,
there exists
C_{T}=C(T,\rho,M_{0},M_{1},M_{2})>0
such that

\displaystyle\|B_{0}(u-u_{0})\|_{C^{\rho}([0,T],{\mathcal{Y}})}+

\displaystyle\|u-u_{0}\|_{C^{1+\rho}([0,T],{\mathcal{Y}})}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\Big(\|f-B_{0}u_{0}\|_{C^{\rho}([0,T],{\mathcal{Y}})}+\|f(0)-B_{0}u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{B_{0}}(\rho,\infty)}\Big)

and
C_{T}\leq C_{T_{0}}
for all
T\leq T_{0}
.

Proof.

The existence, uniqueness and
(
3.1
) follow from
[
22
, Theorem 4.3.1]
. Moreover, as explained in the beginning of Section 4.1 in
[
22
]
, the constant
C_{T}
is increasing in
T
. ∎

Remark 3.2
.

Let
B_{0}:{\mathcal{Y}}\subset{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
be the generator of a strongly continuous analytic semigroup and
\sigma\in(0,1)
. Then, as noted in
[
22
, Proposition 2.2.2]
, the intermediate space
\mathcal{D}_{B_{0}}(\rho,\infty)
does not depend on the operator
B_{0}
itself, as it coincides with a real interpolation space between the spaces
{\mathcal{Y}}
and
{\mathcal{X}}
(with equivalence of respective norms). We shall use this fact in the following where we consider a Hölder space setting.

3.1.
Intermediate spaces in a Hölder space setting

In our application to the nonlocal mean curvature flow, we will need to consider the special case where
{\mathcal{Y}}={\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
and
{\mathcal{X}}={\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\sigma}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
for values
\sigma\in(0,1)
,
\beta\in(\sigma,1)
. Our next result provides a characterization of the intermediate space
\mathcal{D}_{B_{0}}(\rho,\infty)
defined in (
3.2
) in this particular case. As noted in Remark
3.2
, this space does not depend on the particular choice of a generator
B_{0}:{\mathcal{Y}}\subset{\mathcal{X}}\to{\mathcal{X}}
of a strongly continuous analytic semigroup.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\sigma\in(-1,1)
,
\beta\in(\sigma,1+\sigma)
with
\beta\not\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
B_{0}:{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\subset{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\sigma}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\to{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\sigma}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
be any infinitesimal generator of a strongly continuous analytic semigroup on
{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\sigma}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
. Let
\rho\in(0,\min(1,\frac{1}{1+\sigma}))
and put
\gamma_{\rho}:=\beta+\rho(1+\sigma).

Then
{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\sigma}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\cap C^{\gamma_{\rho}-\sigma}({\mathbb{R}^{N-1}})\subset\mathcal{D}_{B_{0}}(\rho,\infty)
and there exists
c=c(\sigma,\beta,\rho)>0
such that

(3.4)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{B_{0}}(\rho,\infty)}\leq c\|f\|_{C^{\gamma_{\rho}-\sigma}}\qquad\textrm{for all $f\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\sigma}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\cap C^{\gamma_{\rho}-\sigma}({\mathbb{R}^{N-1}})$.}

If moreover
\gamma_{\rho}-\sigma\not\in\mathbb{N}
, then
{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\sigma}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\cap C^{\gamma_{\rho}-\sigma}({\mathbb{R}^{N-1}})=\mathcal{D}_{B_{0}}(\rho,\infty)
, with equivalence of their respective norms.

Proof.

By
[
2
, Corollary 2.17]
, for
\sigma\in(-1,1)
, the standard fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{\frac{1+\sigma}{2}}:{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\to{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\sigma}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
is a generator of a strongly continuous analytic semigroup, and therefore

(3.5)

\mathcal{D}_{B_{0}}(\rho,\infty)=\mathcal{D}_{(-\Delta)^{\frac{1+\sigma}{2}}}(\rho,\infty),

with equivalence of their respective norms, see Remark
3.2
, .

For simplicity, we write
\gamma=\gamma_{\rho}
.
Letting
f\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\sigma}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\cap C^{\gamma-\sigma}({\mathbb{R}^{N-1}})
, we then have

(3.6)

e^{t(-\Delta)^{\frac{1+\sigma}{2}}}f(x)=K(\cdot,t)\star f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}K(x-y,t)f(y)dy,

where
K
is the heat kernel of
(-\Delta)^{\frac{1+\sigma}{2}}
.
It is known, see e.g.
[
6
]
, that

K(x,t)=t^{-\frac{N-1}{1+\sigma}}P(t^{-\frac{1}{1+\sigma}}x),

for some radially symmetric function
P\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
, with

\qquad|D^{k}P(y)|\leq\frac{C(k,N,\sigma)}{1+|y|^{N+\sigma+k}}.

From this, we can apply
[
17
, Lemma 2.1]
to get

(3.7)

|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1+\sigma}{2}}P(z)|\leq\frac{C}{1+|z|^{N+\sigma}}.

We have, using that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1+\sigma}{2}}P(z)\,dz=0
and a change of variables,

\displaystyle H_{t}(x)

\displaystyle:=t(-\Delta)^{\frac{1+\sigma}{2}}(e^{t(-\Delta)^{\frac{1+\sigma}{2}}}f)(x)=t\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1+\sigma}{2}}K(x-y,t)f(y)\,dy

\displaystyle=t^{-\frac{N-1}{1+\sigma}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}((-\Delta)^{\frac{1+\sigma}{2}}P)(t^{-\frac{1}{1+\sigma}}(x-y))f(y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1+\sigma}{2}}P(z)f(x-t^{\frac{1}{1+\sigma}}z)\,dz=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1+\sigma}{2}}P(z)[f(x-t^{\frac{1}{1+\sigma}}z)-f(x)]\,dz.

Now using that
(-\Delta)^{\frac{1+\sigma}{2}}P
is even, we conclude that

(3.8)

H_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{1+\sigma}{2}}P(z)[f(x-t^{\frac{1}{1+\sigma}}z)+f(x+t^{\frac{1}{1+\sigma}}z)-2f(x)]\,dz.

Suppose that
\gamma-\beta\leq 1
.
We then deduce, from that

(3.9)

\displaystyle|H_{t}(x)|\leq t^{\frac{\gamma-\beta}{1+\sigma}}C[f]_{C^{\gamma-\beta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}|z|^{\gamma-\beta}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1+\sigma}{2}}P(z)|\,dz.

If also
\gamma-\beta>1
then since

\displaystyle|[f(x-t^{\frac{1}{1+\sigma}}z)

\displaystyle+f(x+t^{\frac{1}{1+\sigma}}z)-2f(x)]|\leq 2[\nabla f]_{C^{\gamma-\beta-1}}t^{\frac{1}{1+\sigma}}|z|\min(1,(t^{\frac{1}{1+\sigma}}|z|)^{\gamma-\beta-1}),

we still have (
3.9
). As a consequence

(3.10)

\displaystyle\|H_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Ct^{\rho}\|f\|_{C^{\gamma-\sigma}}\qquad\textrm{for all $\rho\in(0,\min(1,\frac{1}{1+\sigma})).$}

To proceed, we start with the case
\gamma-\sigma\leq 1
. Then
for
x\not=x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
, by (
3.7
) and the fact that
0<\gamma-\beta=\rho(1+\sigma)<1+\sigma
, we have

\displaystyle|H_{t}(x)-H_{t}(x^{\prime})|\leq C[f]_{C^{\gamma-\sigma}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1+\sigma}{2}}P(z)|\min(|x-x^{\prime}|,t^{\frac{1}{1+\sigma}}|z|)^{\gamma-\sigma}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq C[f]_{C^{\gamma-\sigma}}t^{\frac{\gamma-\sigma}{1+\sigma}}\int_{|z|\leq t^{-\frac{1}{1+\sigma}}|x-x^{\prime}|}|z|^{\gamma-\sigma}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1+\sigma}{2}}P(z)|\,dz

\displaystyle+C[f]_{C^{\gamma-\sigma}}|x-x^{\prime}|^{\gamma-\sigma}\int_{|z|\geq t^{\frac{-1}{1+\sigma}}|x-x^{\prime}|}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1+\sigma}{2}}P(z)|\,dz

\displaystyle\leq C[f]_{C^{\gamma-\sigma}}t^{\frac{\gamma-\sigma}{1+\sigma}}t^{-\frac{\beta-\sigma}{1+\sigma}}|x-x^{\prime}|^{\beta-\sigma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}|z|^{\gamma-\beta}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1+\sigma}{2}}P(z)|\,dz

\displaystyle+C[f]_{C^{\gamma-\sigma}}|x-x^{\prime}|^{\gamma-\sigma}\int_{|z|\geq t^{\frac{-1}{1+\sigma}}|x-x^{\prime}|}\frac{1}{1+|z|^{N-1+(\gamma-\beta)}}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq C[f]_{C^{\gamma-\sigma}}\left(t^{\frac{\gamma-\beta}{1+\sigma}}|x-x^{\prime}|^{\beta-\sigma}+|x-x^{\prime}|^{\gamma-\sigma}(t^{\frac{-1}{1+\alpha}}|x-x^{\prime}|)^{-(\gamma-\beta)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C[f]_{C^{\gamma-\sigma}}t^{\frac{\gamma-\beta}{1+\sigma}}|x-x^{\prime}|^{\beta-\sigma}.

This clearly implies that

\sup_{t\in(0,1)}t^{-\rho}[H_{t}]_{C^{\beta-\sigma}}\leq C[f]_{C^{\gamma-\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}.

As a consequence, using also (
3.10
), we have

(3.11)

\sup_{t\in(0,1)}t^{-\rho}\|H_{t}\|_{C^{\beta-\sigma}}\leq C\|f\|_{C^{\gamma-\sigma}}\qquad\textrm{ for $\gamma-\sigma\leq 1.$}

We now assume that
\gamma-\sigma>1
and we observe that from the upper bounds of
\rho
and
\beta
we have
\gamma-\sigma\leq 2
.
Therefore (recalling (
3.8
)) using that

\displaystyle|[f(x-t^{\frac{1}{1+\sigma}}z)

\displaystyle+f(x+t^{\frac{1}{1+\sigma}}z)-2f(x)]-[f(x^{\prime}-t^{\frac{1}{1+\sigma}}z)+f(x^{\prime}+t^{\frac{1}{1+\sigma}}z)-2f(x^{\prime})]|

\displaystyle\leq 2[\nabla f]_{C^{\gamma-\sigma-1}}t^{\frac{1}{1+\sigma}}|z|\min(|x-x^{\prime}|,(t^{\frac{1}{1+\sigma}}|z|))^{\gamma-\sigma-1}

and the same argument as above, we get

\sup_{t\in(0,1)}t^{-\rho}[H_{t}]_{C^{\beta-\sigma}}\leq C[\nabla f]_{C^{\gamma-\sigma-1}}.

From this, (
3.10
) and (
3.11
), we conclude that for all
\rho\in(0,\min(1,\frac{1}{1+\sigma}))
,

\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{(-\Delta)^{\frac{1+\sigma}{2}}}(\rho,\infty)}=\|f\|_{C^{\beta-\sigma}}+\sup_{t\in(0,1)}t^{-\rho}\|H_{t}\|_{C^{\beta-\sigma}}\leq C\|f\|_{C^{\gamma-\sigma}}.

Thanks to (
3.5
) and (
3.9
) we obtain

\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{B_{0}}({\rho},\infty)}\leq C\|f\|_{C^{\gamma-\sigma}}.

Therefore
{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\sigma}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\cap C^{\gamma_{\rho}-\sigma}({\mathbb{R}^{N-1}})\subset\mathcal{D}_{B_{0}}(\rho,\infty)
and (
3.4
) holds.

Next, by
[
22
, Proposition 2.2.2]
and
[
22
, Corollary 1.2.18]
we have that
\mathcal{D}_{B_{0}}(\rho,\infty)
is continuously embedded in
C^{\gamma_{\rho}-\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
, provided
\gamma_{\rho}-\sigma\not\in\mathbb{N}
. Since, by definition
\mathcal{D}_{B_{0}}(\rho,\infty)\subset{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\sigma}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
, we get the desired result.

∎

3.2.
A class of nonlocal operators generating strongly continous analytic semigroups

In this section, we consider a class of nonlocal operators which we prove to generate strongly continuous analytic semigroups.

For fixed
\gamma\in(\alpha,1)
, we consider linear nonlocal operator
\mathbb{L}_{K}:C^{1+\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\to C^{\gamma-\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
given by

(3.13)

\mathbb{L}_{K}u(x)=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+\alpha}}K(x,y)\,dy,

where
K:\big(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\big)\setminus\{(x,x):x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a measurable function satisfying the following assumptions.

Assumptions 3.5
.

(i)

K(x,y)=K(y,x)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
,
x\neq y
.

(ii)

\frac{1}{\kappa}\leq K(x,y)\leq\kappa
, for some
\kappa>1
.

(iii)

there exists
A_{K}\in C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times[0,\infty)\times S^{N-2})
, with
A_{K}(x,0,\theta)=A_{K}(x,0,-\theta)
such that
A_{K}(x,r,\theta)=K(x,x-r\theta)
for all
r>0
and
\|A_{K}\|_{C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times[0,\infty)\times S^{N-2})}\leq\kappa
.

In order to apply the estimates in Lemma
2.2
, it will be useful to decompose
\mathbb{L}_{K}
in two parts, writing

(3.14)

\mathbb{L}_{K}u(x)=\int_{S^{N-2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\delta_{e}u(x,r,\theta)}{r^{2+\alpha}}{A}_{K}^{e}(x,r,\theta)drd\theta+\int_{S^{N-2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\delta_{o}u(x,r,\theta)}{r^{2+\alpha}}{A}_{K}^{o}(x,r,\theta)drd\theta,

for
u\in C^{1+\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
with

{A}_{K}^{e}(x,r,\theta)=A_{K}(x,r,\theta)+A_{K}(x,r,-\theta),\qquad{A}_{K}^{o}(x,r,\theta)=A_{K}(x,r,\theta)-A_{K}(x,r,-\theta)

and
\delta_{e}u
,
\delta_{o}u
defined as in (
2.4
). We now define the set

(3.15)

\mathcal{O}_{\nu}^{\gamma}:=\{u\in C^{1+\gamma}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\,:\,\|\nabla u\|_{C^{\gamma}}\leq\nu\},

for
\nu>0
, and we state the following estimates.

Lemma 3.6
.

(i)

Let
\gamma>\alpha
. Then there exist
C,C^{\prime}>0
depending only on
N,\alpha,\gamma,\kappa
such that

(3.16)

\|\mathbb{L}_{K}u\|_{C^{\gamma-\alpha}}\leq C^{\prime}\|\nabla u\|_{C^{\gamma}}\qquad\text{for all $u\in\mathcal{O}_{\nu}^{\gamma}$}

and

(3.17)

\|\nabla u\|_{C^{\gamma}}\leq C(\|\mathbb{L}_{K}u\|_{C^{\gamma-\alpha}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}})\qquad\text{for all $u\in\mathcal{O}_{\nu}^{\gamma}$.}

(ii)

If
\beta\in(\alpha,\gamma)
, then we have
\mathbb{L}_{K}u\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\alpha}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
for all
u\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\cap\mathcal{O}_{\nu}^{\gamma}
.

Proof.

(i) It is clear from Lemma
2.2
and (
3.14
) that (
3.16
) holds.

Now we let
f(x)=\mathbb{L}_{K}u(x)
. Since
K(\cdot+x_{0},\cdot+x_{0})
satisfies also Assumptions
3.5
for all
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
, by
[
15
, Theorem 1.3
(ii)
, Theorem 1.4
(iii)
]
, we get

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{C^{\gamma}(B_{1}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla(u-u(x_{0}))\|_{C^{\gamma}(B_{1}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|f\|_{C^{\gamma-\alpha}(B_{2}(x_{0}))}+\|u-u(x_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2}(x_{0}))}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{|u(x)-u(x_{0})|}{1+|x-x_{0}|^{N+\alpha}}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|f\|_{C^{\gamma-\alpha}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\int_{|x-x_{0}|\geq 2}\frac{|u(x)-u(x_{0})|}{|x-x_{0}|^{N+\alpha}}\,dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|f\|_{C^{\gamma-\alpha}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{|x-x_{0}|\geq 2}\frac{1}{|x-x_{0}|^{N-1+\alpha}}\,dy\right).

From this, we then get

\|\nabla u\|_{C^{\gamma}(B_{1}(x_{0}))}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{C^{\gamma-\alpha}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\right),

where
C
may change value from one line to an other.
Since
x_{0}
is arbitrary, (
3.17
) follows.

(ii) By Lemma
2.2
and (
3.14
) if
u\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
, then
\mathbb{L}_{K}u\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\alpha}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
.
∎

Combining Lemma
3.6
and a result from
[
2
]
, we now exhibit a useful class of operators generating strongly continuous analytic semigroups in Hölder spaces.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\gamma\in(\beta,1)
and
b\in{{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\alpha}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
be such that

\frac{1}{\kappa}\leq|b(x)|\leq\kappa\qquad\textrm{ for all $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}$}.

Let
L:{{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\to{{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\alpha}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
be a bounded linear operator satisfying for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
c_{\varepsilon}
such that

(3.18)

\displaystyle\|Lu\|_{C^{\beta-\alpha}}\leq\varepsilon\|\mathbb{L}_{K}u\|_{C^{\beta-\alpha}}+c_{\varepsilon}\|u\|_{C^{\beta-\alpha}}\;\;\;\mbox{ for all }u\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}).

Suppose that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

(3.19)

\sup_{\theta\in S^{N-2},}\|\nabla^{k}_{\theta}A_{K}^{e}(\cdot,0,\theta)\|_{C^{\gamma}}\leq C_{k}.

Then, the operator

(3.20)

b(x)\mathbb{L}_{K}+L:{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\to{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\alpha}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}),

with domain
{{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
, generates a strongly continuous analytic semigroup on
{{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\alpha}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
.

Proof.

Recalling (
3.14
), we write

(3.21)

b(x)\mathbb{L}_{K}:=L_{1}+L_{2},

where

\displaystyle L_{1}u(x)

\displaystyle=b(x)\Bigl(P.V.\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{(u(x)-u(x+y))}{|y|^{N+\alpha}}A_{K}^{e}(x,0,y/|y|)\;dy\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{b(x)}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{(2u(x)-u(x+y)-u(x-y))}{|y|^{N+\alpha}}A_{K}^{e}(x,0,y/|y|)\;dy

and

L_{2}u(x)=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{(u(x)-u(x+y))}{|y|^{N+\alpha}}b(x)[A_{K}^{o}(x,|y|,y/|y|)+A_{K}^{e}(x,|y|,y/|y|)-A_{K}^{e}(x,0,y/|y|)]\;dy.

By Lemma
2.2
, the operator
L_{1}:{{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\to{{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\alpha}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
is bounded.

Letting
k(x,y):=b(x)A_{K}^{e}(x,0,y/|y|)|y|^{-N-\alpha}
and
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
be nonnegative, radially symmetric and satisfy
\chi=1
for
|y|\leq 2
. One can split
k
as
k=k_{1}+k_{2}
, where
k_{1}(x,y)=k(x,y)\chi(y)
and
k_{2}(x,y)=k(x,y)(1-\chi(y))
. Then, it is easy to see that
k_{1}=0
for
|y|\geq 2
and
k_{2}=0
for
|y|\leq 1
. In addition, using also (
3.19
), for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{N-1}
with
|n|\leq N
, there exists
C>0,c>0
only depending on
N,\alpha,\gamma,n
and
\kappa
such that

(3.22)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\|\partial_{y}^{n}k_{1}(\cdot,y)\|_{C^{\gamma}}\leq C|y|^{-N-\alpha-|n|},\qquad 0<|y|\leq 2,\\
&k_{1}(x,y)\leq C|y|^{-N-\alpha},\qquad\;0<|y|\leq 1,\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1},\\
&\|k_{2}(\cdot,y)\|_{C^{\gamma}}\leq C|y|^{-N-\alpha^{\prime}},\qquad 0<|y|\leq 1,\;\;\mbox{ for }\;\;0\leq\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha<1\\
&\displaystyle\int_{|y|\geq 1}\|k_{2}(\cdot,y)\|_{C^{\gamma}}\;dy<\infty,\\
&\lim\limits_{|y|\rightarrow\infty}\|k_{2}(\cdot,y)\|_{C^{\gamma}}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

In view of this, we can apply
[
2
, Corollary 2.17]
, to deduce that, for
\beta\in(\alpha,\gamma)
, the operator
L_{1}:{{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\to{{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\alpha}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
generates a strongly continuous analytic semigroup.

By Lemma
2.2
and (
2.2
), for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
C_{2}(\varepsilon)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|L_{2}u\|_{C^{\beta-\alpha}}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{C_{b}}\leq C\left(\varepsilon\|u\|_{C^{1+\beta}}+C_{2}(\varepsilon)\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\right).

Combining this information with Lemma
3.6
(i), we get

\displaystyle\|L_{2}u\|_{C^{\beta-\alpha}}\leq C\left(\varepsilon\|(L_{1}+L_{2})u\|_{C^{\beta-\alpha}}+C_{2}(\varepsilon)\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\right).

with a possibly different constant
C
not depending on
\varepsilon
.
The above two estimates give

\displaystyle\|L_{2}u\|_{C^{\beta-\alpha}}\leq C\left(\varepsilon\|L_{1}u\|_{C^{\beta-\alpha}}+C_{2}(\varepsilon)\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\right).

From this and (
3.18
), we have

\displaystyle\|(L_{2}+L)u\|_{C^{\beta-\alpha}}\leq\varepsilon\|L_{1}u\|_{C^{\beta-\alpha}}+C(\varepsilon)\|u\|_{C^{\beta-\alpha}}\;\;\;\mbox{ for all }u\in{{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}.

Now applying
[
24
, Theorem 2.1]
, we deduce that
b(x)\mathbb{L}_{K}+L=L_{1}+L_{2}+L
is a generator of a strongly continuous analytic semigroup. ∎

We shall now derive the following existence and uniqueness result from Theorem
3.1
, Proposition
3.3
and Proposition
3.7
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
0<\alpha<\beta<1
and
B_{0}:=b(x)\mathbb{L}_{K}+L:{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\to{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\alpha}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
be given by (
3.20
).
Let
T>0
and
\rho\in(0,\frac{1}{1+\alpha})
. Let
u_{0}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
and

f\in C^{\rho}([0,T],{{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\alpha}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})})
be such that
f(0)-B_{0}u_{0}\in{C^{\gamma_{\rho}-\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}
with
\gamma_{\rho}=\beta+\rho(1+\alpha)
. Then the problem

(3.23)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}u^{\prime}(t)+B_{0}u(t)&=&f(t),\hskip 28.45274ptt\in(0,T]\\
u(0)&=&u_{0}\end{array}\right.

admits a unique solution
u\in C^{\rho}([0,T],{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0})\cap C^{1+\rho}([0,T],{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\alpha}_{0})
. Moreover,
there exists
C_{T}=C(T,\rho,\kappa,N,\alpha,\gamma,\beta)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|u-u_{0}\|_{C^{\rho}([0,T],C^{1+\beta})}

\displaystyle+\|u-u_{0}\|_{C^{1+\rho}([0,T],C^{\beta-\alpha})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\Big(\|f-B_{0}u_{0}\|_{C^{\rho}([0,T],C^{\beta-\alpha})}+\|f(0)-B_{0}u_{0}\|_{C^{\gamma_{\rho}-\alpha}}\Big).

In addition,
C_{T}\leq C_{T_{0}}
for all
T\leq T_{0}
.

Proof.

From Theorem
3.1
and Proposition
3.7
, we get a unique solution
u\in C^{\rho}([0,T],{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\beta}_{0})\cap C^{1+\rho}([0,T],{\mathcal{C}}^{\beta-\alpha}_{0})
to (
3.23
). Moreover,

\displaystyle\|B_{0}(u-u_{0})\|_{C^{\rho}([0,T],C^{\beta-\alpha})}+\|u-u_{0}\|_{C^{1+\rho}([0,T],C^{\beta-\alpha})}

(3.24)

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\Big(\|f-B_{0}u_{0}\|_{C^{\rho}([0,T],C^{\beta-\alpha}))}+\|f(0)-B_{0}u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{B_{0}}(\rho,\infty)}\Big).

By Proposition
3.3
we have
\|f(0)-B_{0}u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{D}_{B_{0}}(\rho,\infty)}\leq C^{\prime}\|f(0)-B_{0}u_{0}\|_{C^{\gamma_{\rho}-\alpha}}
. On the other hand, since
B_{0}
is closed, we obtain

\|B_{0}v\|_{C^{\beta-\alpha}}+\|v\|_{C^{\beta-\alpha}}\geq C\left(\|v\|_{C^{1+\beta}}+\|v\|_{C^{\beta-\alpha}}\right),\quad\forall v\in C^{1+\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}).

From this and (
3.2
), we get the result.
∎

4.
Regularity property of the nonlocal mean curvature operator

By changes of variables and the fact that
{\mathcal{G}}(p)=-\displaystyle\int_{-p}^{p}\frac{d\tau}{(1+\tau^{2})^{\frac{N+\alpha}{2}}}
is odd, for
w\in C^{1,\gamma}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
, with
\gamma>\alpha
, we have

\displaystyle H(w)(x)

\displaystyle=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{{\mathcal{G}}(p_{w}({x},{y}))}{|{x}-{y}|^{N-1+\alpha}}d{y}

\displaystyle=\frac{P.V.}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{{\mathcal{G}}(p_{w}({x},{x-z}))+{\mathcal{G}}(p_{w}({x},{x+z}))}{|{z}|^{N-1+\alpha}}d{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{P.V.}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{{\mathcal{G}}(p_{w}({x},{x-z}))-{\mathcal{G}}(-p_{w}({x},{x+z}))}{|{z}|^{N-1+\alpha}}d{z}.

Therefore, by the fundamental theorem of calculus and the fact that
{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}(p)=-2(1+p^{2})^{\frac{-N-\alpha}{2}}
is even, we get

(4.1)

\displaystyle H(w)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{2w(x)-w(x-z)-w(x+z)}{|z|^{N+\alpha}}{\mathcal{K}}_{w}(x,z)dz,

where

(4.2)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}_{w}(x,z)=\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(1+\left(\tau p_{w}({x},{x-z})-(1-\tau)p_{w}({x},{x+z})\right)^{2}\bigg)^{-\frac{N+\alpha}{2}}d\tau.

For the remainder of this section, it will be convenient to write

(4.3)

\displaystyle H(w)(x)=\int_{S^{N-2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\delta_{e}w(x,r,\theta)}{r^{2+\alpha}}\mathcal{A}_{w}(x,r,\theta)drd\theta,

where
\delta_{e}w(x,r,\theta)=2w(x)-w(x-r\theta)-w(x+r\theta)
, for all
(x,r,\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times[0,\infty)\times S^{N-2}
and

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{rll}\mathcal{A}_{w}&:\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times[0,\infty)\times S^{N-2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\\
&(x,r,\theta)\mapsto\mathcal{A}_{w}(x,r,\theta)={\mathcal{K}}_{w}(x,r\theta),\;\;\;{\mathcal{A}}_{w}(x,0,\theta)=\bigg(1+(\nabla w(x)\cdot\theta)^{2}\bigg)^{-\frac{N+\alpha}{2}}.\end{array}\right.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<\gamma<1
. Then there exists
C=C(k,N,\alpha,\gamma)>0
such that for all
u,w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\in C^{1+\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
, we have

\|\partial^{k}_{u}\mathcal{A}_{u}[w_{1},\dots,w_{k}]\|_{C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times[0,\infty)\times S^{N-2})}\leq C\left(1+\|\nabla u\|_{C^{\gamma}}\right)^{C}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\|\nabla w_{i}\|_{C^{\gamma}}.

Proof.

This follows from the definition of
{\mathcal{A}}
.
∎

We have the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\gamma\in(\alpha,1)
.
Then the map
H:C^{1+\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\to C^{\gamma-\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
is of class
C^{\infty}
and for all
k\in\mathbb{N},

u,w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\in C^{1+\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}),

(4.6)

\|D^{k}H(u)[w_{1},\dots,w_{k}]\|_{C^{\gamma-\alpha}}\leq C\left(1+\|\nabla u\|_{C^{\gamma}}\right)^{C}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\|\nabla w_{i}\|_{C^{\gamma}}.

with
C>0
only depending on
k,N,\alpha,\gamma
. Moreover
H:{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\gamma}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})\to{{\mathcal{C}}^{\gamma-\alpha}_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
is of class
C^{\infty}
and (
4.6
) holds for all
u,w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1+\gamma}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
.

Proof.

For all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
w_{0},w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\in C^{1+\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1}),
we define

\mathcal{B}_{k}^{e}(u)[w_{0};w_{1},\dots,w_{k}](x)=\int_{S^{N-2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\delta_{e}w_{0}(x,r,\theta)\partial^{k}_{u}\mathcal{A}_{u}^{e}[w_{1},\dots,w_{k}](x,r,\theta)r^{-2-\alpha}drd\theta.

We now prove that
H
is of class
C^{\infty}
and for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

(4.7)

\displaystyle D^{k}H(u)[w_{1}\dots,w_{k}]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}^{e}_{k}(u)[u;w_{1},\dots,w_{k}]+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{B}^{e}_{k-1}(u)[w_{j};w_{1},\dots,w_{j-1},w_{j+1},\dots,w_{k}].

For this, it is enough to prove that
u\mapsto\mathcal{B}^{e}_{k}(u)[u;w_{1},\dots,w_{k}]
is differentiable. By the linearity of the map
u\mapsto\delta_{e}u
it is also enough to prove the differentiability of
u\mapsto\mathcal{B}^{e}_{k}(u)[w_{0};w_{1},\dots,w_{k}]
with

(4.8)

\displaystyle\|D_{u}\mathcal{B}^{e}_{k}(u)[w_{0};w_{1},\dots,w_{k}]\|_{C^{\gamma-\alpha}}\leq C\left(1+\|\nabla u\|_{C^{\gamma}}\right)^{C}\prod_{i=0}^{k}\|\nabla w_{i}\|_{C^{\gamma}}.

Let
v\in C^{1+\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
, with
\|\nabla v\|_{C^{\gamma}}\leq 1
. We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{e}_{k}(u+v)[w_{0};w_{1},\dots,w_{k}](x)-\mathcal{B}^{e}_{k}(u)[w_{0};w_{1},\dots,w_{k}](x)-

\displaystyle\int_{S^{N-2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\delta_{e}w_{0}(x,r,\theta)\partial^{k+1}_{u}\mathcal{A}_{u}^{e}[w_{1},\dots,w_{k},v](x,r,\theta)r^{-2-\alpha}\,drd\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{N-2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-2-\alpha}\delta_{e}w_{0}(x,r,\theta)

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{1}\left(\partial^{k+1}_{u}\mathcal{A}_{u+\rho v}^{e}[w_{1},\dots,w_{k},v](x,r,\theta)-\partial^{k+1}_{u}\mathcal{A}_{u}^{e}[w_{1},\dots,w_{k},v](x,r,\theta)\right)d\rho\,drd\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{N-2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-2-\alpha}\delta_{e}w_{0}(x,r,\theta)\int_{0}^{1}\rho\int_{0}^{1}\partial^{k+2}_{u}\mathcal{A}_{u+\rho\overline{\rho}v}^{e}[w_{1},\dots,w_{k},v,v](x,r,\theta)d\overline{\rho}d\rho\,drd\theta

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathcal{B}^{e}_{k}(u+v)[w_{0},w_{1},\dots,w_{k}]-\mathcal{B}^{e}_{k}(u)[w_{0},w_{1},\dots,w_{k}]-

\displaystyle\int_{S^{N-2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-2-\alpha}\delta_{e}w_{0}(\cdot,r,\theta)\partial^{k+1}_{u}\mathcal{A}_{u}^{e}[w_{1},\dots,w_{k},v](\cdot,r,\theta)\,drd\theta\Big\|_{C^{\gamma-\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\|\nabla u\|_{C^{\gamma-\alpha}}\right)^{C}\prod_{i=0}^{k}\|\nabla w_{i}\|_{C^{\gamma}}\|\nabla v\|_{C^{\gamma-\alpha}}^{2}.

From this, we deduce that

) holds.

Now the fact that

We compute next the explicit expression of

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

We consider the linear operator

We then have

\displaystyle H(u+w)(x)-H(u)(x)-Mw(x)

\displaystyle=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{[{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}(p_{u+\tau w}(x,y))-{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}(p_{u}(x,y))]p_{w}(x,y)}{|x-y|^{N-1+\alpha}}d\tau\,dx.

We then get

(4.10)

\displaystyle\Gamma(x):=H(u+w)(x)-H(u)(x)-Mw(x)=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\frac{(p_{w}(x,x-y))^{2}\mu(x,|y|,y/|y|)}{|y|^{N-1+\alpha}}\,dx,

where

and

Using polar coordinates and recalling (

\displaystyle\Gamma(x):=P.V.\int_{S^{N-2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-2-\alpha}(w(x)-w(x-r\theta))\int_{0}^{1}\nabla w(x-r\tau\theta)\cdot\theta\,d\tau\mu(x,r,\theta)\,drd\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{N-2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-2-\alpha}\delta_{o}w(x,r,\theta)\int_{0}^{1}(\nabla w(x-r\tau\theta)-\nabla w(x))\cdot\theta\,d\tau\mu(x,r,\theta)\,drd\theta

where we used that

{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime\prime}

we can thus apply Lemma

Recalling (

2.2
(iii)

Let

\displaystyle F(u)-F(v)=-\int_{0}^{1}(D{\mathcal{H}}(\lambda u+(1-\lambda)v)-D{\mathcal{H}}(u_{0}))[w]\,d\lambda

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\lambda\int_{0}^{1}D^{2}{\mathcal{H}}(\tau(\lambda u+(1-\lambda)v)+(1-\tau)u_{0})[\lambda(u-u_{0})+(1-\lambda)(v-u_{0}),w]\,d\tau d\lambda.

(5.3)

\|L_{\lambda,\tau}(t)-L_{\lambda,\tau}(s)\|_{C^{\beta-\alpha}}\leq CR(1+R+\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{C^{\beta}})^{C}|s-t|^{\rho}

(5.4)

\|L_{\lambda,\tau}(t)\|_{C^{\beta-\alpha}}\leq CR(1+R+\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{C^{\beta}})^{C}.

\displaystyle[F(u)-F(v)](t)-[F(u)-F(v)](s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\lambda\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl(L_{\lambda,\tau}(s)[\lambda(u(s)-u_{0})+(1-\lambda)(v(s)-u_{0}),w(s)]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\;\>-L_{\lambda,\tau}(t)[\lambda(u(t)-u_{0})+(1-\lambda)(v(t)-u_{0}),w(t)]\Bigr)d\tau d\lambda

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\;\>+L_{\lambda,\tau}(t)[\lambda(u(s)-u_{0})+(1-\lambda)(v(s)-u_{0}),w(s)-w(t)]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\;\>+L_{\lambda,\tau}(t)[\lambda(u(s)-u(t))+(1-\lambda)(v(s)-v(t)),w(t)]\Bigr)d\tau\,d\lambda.

Using (

\displaystyle\|[F(u)-F(v)](t)-[F(u)-F(v)](s)\|_{C^{\beta-\alpha}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq CR(1+R+\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{C^{\beta}})^{C}(t^{\rho}+s^{\rho})|t-s|^{\rho}\|w\|_{E_{T}}.

and taking

The result follows from (

Proof of Theorem

Let

Clearly a fixed point of the map

\displaystyle\sup_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\partial_{t}u=-\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}{\mathcal{H}}(u_{0})\qquad
and
\qquad\displaystyle\inf_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}\partial_{t}u=-\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}{\mathcal{H}}(u_{0})
.

(iii)

If moreover
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
then

\displaystyle\sup_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}u=\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}u_{0}\qquad\textrm{ and }\qquad\displaystyle\inf_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}u=\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}u_{0}

Proof.

We start with
(iii)
. If
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N-1})
then by (
1.9
) we have
u\in C^{\rho}([0,T],C^{1+\beta})\cap C^{1+\rho}([0,T],C^{\beta-\alpha})
. Now, in view of (
4.1
), we can apply Proposition
2.3
to
u
and
-u
to get
(iii)
.

Next to prove
(i)
, we define
\tau_{h}u(t,x)=\frac{u(t,x+h)-u(t,x)}{|h|}\in C([0,T],C^{1+\beta})
, for
0<|h|<1
. We then have

\partial_{t}\tau_{h}u+B[u(t,\cdot+h),u(t)]\tau_{h}u(t)=0\qquad\textrm{ in $[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}$}.

It thus follows from Corollary
4.10
that
\|\tau_{h}u\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1})}\leq\|\tau_{h}u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
. Hence, letting
h\to 0
, we get
(i)
.

Finally, by Theorem
1.2
and Lemma
4.2
we have
\partial_{t}u=-{\mathcal{H}}(u)\in C^{\rho}([\varepsilon,T],C^{k+\beta^{\prime}-\alpha})
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,T)
and
k\geq 0
. Letting
\tau\in(\varepsilon,T)
, we define
u^{\tau}(t,x)=\frac{u(t+\tau,x)-u(t,x)}{\tau}
, for
\varepsilon<t\leq T-\tau
. We thus get
u^{\tau}\in C^{\rho}([\varepsilon,T-\tau],C^{1+\beta})\cap C^{1+\rho}([\varepsilon,T-\tau],C^{\beta-\alpha})
and

\partial_{t}u^{\tau}+B[u(t+\tau),u(t)]u^{\tau}(t)=0\qquad\textrm{ on $[\varepsilon,T-\tau]\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}$}.

Applying Corollary
4.10
, we obtain

\sup_{[\varepsilon,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}u^{\tau}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}u^{\tau}(\varepsilon,x)\qquad\textrm{ and }\qquad\inf_{[\varepsilon,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}u^{\tau}=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}u^{\tau}(\varepsilon,x).

Since
\partial_{t}u=-{\mathcal{H}}(u)\in C^{\rho}([0,T],L^{\infty})
, letting first
\tau\to 0
and then
\varepsilon\to 0
in the above identities, we get
(ii)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
(completed).

It suffices to apply Lemma
5.3
.

∎

Horizontal decompositions, I
Paolo Lisca, Andrea Parma

Abstract
We show that every smooth, closed, orientable 4-manifold X X admits a special kind of handlebody decomposition that we call horizontal . We classify the closed 4 4 -manifolds with the simplest horizontal decompositions and we describe all such decompositions of ℂ ​ ℙ 2 \mathbb{CP}^{2} , showing that they give rise to infinitely many of the known embeddings of rational homology balls in the complex projective plane.

Introduction

Let
p>q\geq 0
be coprime integers and
B_{p,q}
the rational homology ball smoothing of the quotient singularity
\frac{1}{p^{2}}(pq-1,1)
used by Fintushel and Stern in the rational blow-down construction
[
7

constructed in
[

4

B_{p,q}

Let

4

\partial X=\partial_{+}X\cup-\partial_{-}X

\partial_{-}X

Definition 1.1
.

Let

3

L=\cup_{i=1}^{k}L_{i}\subset Y
a

k

is a

and real numbers

•

is a homotopically non-trivial simple closed curve on

\mathfrak{S}_{i}:=\Sigma_{g}\times\{t_{i}\}
;

•

—
\fr(L_{i})-\fr_{\mathfrak{S}_{i}}(L_{i})|=1
, where

\fr(L_{i})
is the given framing of
L_{i}
and
\fr_{\mathfrak{S}_{i}}(L_{i})

We call the difference

Definition 1.2
.

When

is a horizontal link of type
g

with
\ell=|L|
components and

we call a handlebody decomposition of an oriented cobordism

as above a

of

(g,u,\ell,h)
, or simply

(g,u,\ell,h)
-decomposition
of

We say that a smooth, closed 4-manifold

(g,u,\ell,h)

has one as a cobordism

Note that

.
Moreover, it is not difficult to check that if a link

The following establishes a basic existence result for horizontal decompositions.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X\thinspace\colon\partial_{-}X\to\partial_{+}X
be a smooth, oriented, connected,
4
-dimensional cobordism
such that
\partial_{-}X

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
X\thinspace\colon S^{3}\to S^{3}
be an oriented cobordism with a
(1,u,\ell,0)
-decomposition. Then, after possibly reversing the orientation of

X\cong\begin{cases}S^{3}\times[0,1]&\quad\text{if $\chi(X)=0$},\\
\mathbb{CP}^{2}\setminus\left(B^{4}\sqcup B^{4}\right)&\quad\text{if $\chi(X)=1$.}\end{cases}

A key fact used in the proof of Theorem
1.7
is that to a horizontal framed link
L\subset\Sigma_{g}\times[0,1]
one can naturally associate an element of the mapping class group
\Map(\Sigma_{g})
factorized as a product of Dehn twists. In fact, suppose
L=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}L_{i}
, with

L_{i}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{i}:=\Sigma_{g}\times\{t_{i}\},\qquad 0<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{k}<1.

Since

with

the Dehn twist

\tau_{i}:=\tau_{\pi(L_{i})}\in\Map(\Sigma_{g}),

where

is the projection onto the first factor. We define the

of

to be the

F_{L}=(\tau_{k}^{\delta_{k}},\tau_{k-1}^{\delta_{k-1}},\ldots,\tau_{1}^{\delta_{1}}),

where the exponent

is equal

the relative framing of

for each

.
The product

will be called the

of

Sometimes, when the horizontal link

is clear from the context, we will abuse
of language and talk about the factorization or the monodromy of the decomposition
of the associated cobordisms

or

.

Proof of Theorem
1.4

We refer the reader to
[
9
, Chapter 4]
for basic facts about handlebody
decompositions. The plan of the proof is as follows. We start from any handle decomposition of a smooth
4
-dimensional cobordism
X\thinspace\colon\partial_{-}X\to\partial_{+}X
and we modify it so that the framed link
L
consisting of the attaching curves of the
2-
handles satisfies the conditions of Definition
1.2
.

We start by recalling a few facts about the operation of addition of a
(1,2)
-cancelling pair.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be a handle decomposition of
X
without
0
- nor
4
-handles,
u

1
-handles and
h

3
-handles. Then, attaching the
1
-handles gives a cobordism
\partial_{-}X\to\partial^{u}_{-}X
.
A four-dimensional
(1,2)
-cancelling pair consists of a
1
-handle
\mathfrak{h}
and a
2
-handle

\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
such that the attaching sphere of
\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
intersects the belt sphere of
\mathfrak{h}
transversely in
a single point. The introduction a
(1,2)
-cancelling pair does not alter

X\thinspace\colon\partial_{-}X\to\partial_{+}X
but turns
\mathcal{H}
into a new handlebody decomposition
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
of

X
in such a way that the
1
-handles of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
give a subcobordism
X^{\prime}\subset X
from

\partial_{-}X
to the
3
-manifold
Y
obtained from
\partial^{u}_{-}X
by removing the
interior of two disjoint
3
-balls
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
and gluing to each other the corresponding boundary
2
-spheres via an orientation-reversing diffeomorphism.
In other words,
Y
is diffeomorphic to
\partial^{u+1}_{-}X
and
X^{\prime}\thinspace\colon\partial_{-}X\to Y
.
The cancelling
2-
handle is attached along any arbitrarily framed curve in

\partial^{u}_{-}X\setminus(B_{1}\cup B_{2})
that connects two identified points of
\partial B_{1}

and
\partial B_{2}
. Such a curve determines a component of the new framed link in
\partial^{u+1}_{-}X
.

Let now
\Sigma\subset\partial^{u}_{-}X
be a Heegaard surface of genus

g_{H}(\partial^{u}_{-}X)=u+g_{H}(\partial_{-}X)
. Since we can always add a
(1,2)
-cancelling
pair, from now on we assume
g>0
without loss of generality. The complement
of
\Sigma
in
\partial^{u}_{-}X
is a disjoint union
H_{g}\cup H^{\prime}_{g}
of two genus-
g
handlebodies. Moreover,
the handlebodies
H_{g}
and
H^{\prime}_{g}
are regular neighborhoods of graphs
G\subset H_{g}
and
G^{\prime}\subset H^{\prime}_{g}
, and

\partial^{u}_{-}X\setminus\{G\cup G^{\prime}\}
is diffeomorphic to the product of
\Sigma
with an open interval.
Up to framed isotopy we may assume
L
disjoint from
G\cup G^{\prime}
, thus contained in a closed regular
neighborhood
N
of
\Sigma
. After fixing a diffeomorphism between
N
and
\Sigma\times[0,1]
and
consequently a projection
\pi:N\rightarrow\Sigma
, we may also assume that the restriction

\pi|_{L}\thinspace\colon L\to\Sigma
is an immersion, and that each self-intersection of
\pi(L)\subset\Sigma
is a transverse
double point. Hence, we can represent
L
using its diagram
D_{L}\subset\Sigma
consisting of
\pi(L)
together
with the over-under information at each point of self-intersection. Recall that, for any framed, simple closed
curve
\gamma\subset N
contained in a horizontal copy of
\Sigma
, i.e. a surface of the form

\Sigma\times\{t\}\subset\Sigma\times[0,1]
, the relative framing of
\gamma
is the integer

\text{fr}(\gamma)-\text{fr}_{\Sigma\times\{t\}}(\gamma)
. Observe that a framed link

L=\cup_{i=1}^{k}L_{i}\subset N
is horizontal if each connected component

L_{i}\subset L\subset N
sits on a horizontal copy of
\Sigma
and:

(1)

the relative framing of
L_{i}
is
\pm 1
;

(2)

the diagram
D_{L_{i}}
of
L_{i}
has no crossings and is therefore a simple closed curve in
\Sigma
;

(3)

D_{L_{i}}
is non-separating in
\Sigma
;

moreover, for each
i<j
:

(4)

at each crossing between
D_{L_{i}}
and
D_{L_{j}}
the overpassing arc belongs to

D_{L_{j}}
.

We say that a crossing of
D_{L}
is
bad
if it involves a diagram
D_{L_{i}}
which violates
either
(2)
or
(4)
. We are going to change the handle decomposition
\mathcal{H}
into a horizontal
decomposition of
X
via a finite sequence of steps. At each step we shall either eliminate a bad
crossing or adjust the framing of some
L_{i}
, making sure that
the genus of
\Sigma
is
u+g_{H}(\partial_{-}X)
at each step, and at the last step
Conditions
(1)
through

Remark 4.3
.

Observe that if

, and since

.

Given

of

.
Moreover, let

given by

. We define the

as the set of

where the minimum

is attained:

b(S^{\delta_{2},\delta_{1}}_{n,\varepsilon}):=\left\{(x,y)\in S^{\delta_{2},\delta_{1}}_{n,\varepsilon}\ |\ |xy|=m^{\delta_{2},\delta_{1}}_{n,\varepsilon}\right\}\subset S^{\delta_{2},\delta_{1}}_{n,\varepsilon}.

Lemma 4.4
.

If
(x,y)\in S_{n,\varepsilon}^{\delta_{2},\delta_{1}}
, both mutations of

belong to

. Moreover,

(1)

there is a finite sequence of mutations which sends

into

(2)

and

.

Proof.

The proof that
(\hat{x},y)\in S_{n,\varepsilon}^{\delta_{2},\delta_{1}}

is symmetric to the proof that
(x,\hat{y})\in S_{n,\varepsilon}^{\delta_{2},\delta_{1}}
, so we
provide the argument only for
(\hat{x},y)
. One can easily check that
if
(x,y)\in S_{n,\varepsilon}^{\delta_{2},\delta_{1}}
then

(\hat{x},y)
is a solution of Equation (

).
Moreover, since

,
by Remark

we have
\gcd(x,n)=\gcd(y,n)=1
,
which implies
\gcd(\hat{x},n)=\gcd(-x-n\delta_{2}y,n)=\gcd(x,n)=1
,
so that
(\hat{x},y)\in S^{\delta_{2},\delta_{1}}_{n,\varepsilon}
as well.
This proves the first part of the statement.

Now observe that if

and
|n|\in\{1,3\}
. Hence, if

and

we must have
|x|\neq|y|
. To prove
(1)
we are going to use infinite descent to reduce to the case

. We claim that if

then
|\hat{x}|<|x|
, while if
|x|<|y|
then
|\hat{y}|<|y|
. Since the arguments in the two cases are similar, we only go through the case
|x|>|y|
. In this case it suffices to observe that if

then, using (

) we get

which is impossible because

. Therefore
|\hat{x}|<|x|
and the claim is established. Inducting on
|xy|
we can apply a sequence of mutations until we obtain a solution

with

.
If
|x_{0}y_{0}|=0

and

, and by Remark

we have
(x_{0},y_{0})\in\pm\{(1,0),(0,1)\}
and
|n|=1
, so that
m^{\delta_{2},\delta_{1}}_{n,\varepsilon}=(|n|-1)/2
.
If
|x_{0}y_{0}|=1
then we saw above that
|n|\in\{1,3\}
. Moreover, if
|n|=3
then

(again by Remark

) and

and

.
If
|n|=1
then
\hat{x}_{0}=0\Leftrightarrow n\delta_{2}y_{0}=-x_{0}\Leftrightarrow\delta_{2}=-nx_{0}y_{0}
and, similarly,
\hat{y}_{0}=0\Leftrightarrow\delta_{1}=-nx_{0}y_{0}
; however, we cannot have

because it is inconsistent with

), so that either
\hat{x}_{0}=0
or
\hat{y}_{0}=0
, which implies that
m^{\delta_{2},\delta_{1}}_{n,\varepsilon}=0=(|n|-1)/2
and that a further mutation sends
(x,y)
into

. This completes the proof.
∎

Observe that the map
(x,y)\mapsto(x,-y)
defines a bijection between

and
S_{-n,\varepsilon}^{\delta_{2},\delta_{1}}

.

.

The following hold:

(1)

4.3
Hurwitz moves and handle slides

In the last step of the proof of Theorem
1.7
, Section
4.4
, we will repeatedly
use the following Proposition
4.6
, which shows that if one applies a Hurwitz move to
the factorization of a horizontal framed link
L
, the associated horizontal decomposition changes
by a sequence of handle slides while staying horizontal. This result is essentially an adaptation
and an extension of
[

. In the following we use notation from Section

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
L=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}L_{i}\subset\Sigma_{g}\times[0,1]
be a
horizontal link with
L_{i}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{i}:=\Sigma_{g}\times\{t_{i}\}
for

Let
F_{L}=(\tau_{k}^{\delta_{k}},\ldots,\tau_{1}^{\delta_{1}})
be the factorization of
L
.
Define the horizontal link

L^{\prime}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}L^{\prime}_{i}
by setting

L^{\prime}_{j}=L_{j}
as framed knots for
j\neq i,i+1
and

\displaystyle L^{\prime}_{i}

\displaystyle:=L_{i+1}\times\{t_{i}\},

\displaystyle\fr(L^{\prime}_{i})

\displaystyle:=\fr_{\mathfrak{S}_{i}}(L^{\prime}_{i})-\delta_{i+1},

\displaystyle L^{\prime}_{i+1}

\displaystyle:=\tau_{i+1}^{\delta_{i+1}}(\pi(L_{i}))\times\{t_{i+1}\},

\displaystyle\fr(L^{\prime}_{i+1})

\displaystyle:=\fr_{\mathfrak{S}_{i+1}}(L^{\prime}_{i+1})-\delta_{i}.

by the Hurwitz move

If we set, instead,

for

and

\displaystyle L^{\prime}_{i}

\displaystyle:=\tau_{i}^{\delta_{i}}(\pi(L_{i+1}))\times\{t_{i}\},

\displaystyle\fr(L^{\prime}_{i})

\displaystyle:=\fr_{\mathfrak{S}_{i}}(L^{\prime}_{i})-\delta_{i+1},

\displaystyle L^{\prime}_{i+1}

\displaystyle:=L_{i}\times\{t_{i+1}\},

\displaystyle\fr(L^{\prime}_{i+1})

\displaystyle:=\fr_{\mathfrak{S}_{i+1}}(L^{\prime}_{i+1})-\delta_{i},

the factorization
F_{L^{\prime}}
is obtained from

and, respectively,

is obtained from the one induced on

by a sequence of handle slides.

and

are orientation-preserving diffeomorphic.

4.4
Last step

In this section we conclude the proof of Theorem

. We assume familiarity with the notation

.

and

Proof of Theorem
1.9

In this section we determine the smooth embeddings
B_{p,q}\subset\mathbb{CP}^{2}
implicit in our proof of
Theorem
1.7
. Recall that, given a
(g,u,\ell,h)
-decomposition of an oriented cobordism

X\thinspace\colon\partial_{-}X\to\partial_{+}X
, we denote by
X_{L}
the cobordism determined by attaching the
2
-handles
to the horizontal link
L\subset\partial^{u}_{-}X
. More generally, if
L^{\prime}\subset L
is a sublink of
L

we denote by
X_{L^{\prime}}
the cobordism determined by the corresponding
2
-handles.
The following lemma is key to our arguments.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\gamma_{1}\cup\gamma_{2}\subset S^{1}\times S^{2}
be a two-component link,
horizontal with respect to the standard genus-
1
Heegaard decomposition and
associated to a
(1,1,2,0)
-decomposition of
X=\mathbb{CP}^{2}\setminus(B^{4}\cup B^{4})
. Then,
setting
\hat{X}_{\gamma_{i}}:=S^{1}\times D^{3}\cup_{\partial_{-}X_{\gamma_{i}}}X_{\gamma_{i}}
, for
i=1,2
,
the disjoint union
\hat{X}_{\gamma_{1}}\sqcup\hat{X}_{\gamma_{i}}
admits a smooth orientation-preserving
embedding in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
.

Proof.

Each
2
-handle
h_{i}
,
i=1,2
, is attached along
\gamma_{i}\times\{t_{i}\}\subset\Sigma_{1}\times\{t_{i}\}\subset S^{1}\times S^{2}
,
with
0<t_{1}<t_{2}<1
. We may clearly assume that the attaching loci of the handles are disjoint.
Hence, in these cases
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
contains
S^{1}\times D^{3}\cup X_{\gamma_{1}\cup\gamma_{2}}
. Moreover,

S^{1}\times D^{3}\approx H_{1}\times[0,1]
and
\partial(H_{1}\times\{t_{i}\})=\Sigma_{1}\times\{t_{i}\}
, so

\mathbb{CP}^{2}
contains
H_{1}\times[t_{i}-\varepsilon,t_{i}+\varepsilon]\cup X_{\gamma_{i}}
for
i=1,2
and some
small
\varepsilon>0
. Therefore, the disjoint union
(S^{1}\times D^{3}\cup X_{\gamma_{1}})\cup(S^{1}\times D^{3}\cup X_{\gamma_{2}})

admits a smooth orientation-preserving embedding in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
.
∎

In view of Lemma
5.1
, to prove Theorem
1.9
we need to identify
the
4
-manifolds
\hat{X}_{\gamma_{i}}
,
i=1,2
. Note that

X_{\gamma_{i}}\thinspace\colon S^{3}\to\partial_{+}X_{\gamma_{i}}
is a cobordism having
\chi(X_{\gamma_{i}})=0
and a

(1,1,1,0)
-decomposition. Therefore, if

b_{1}(\partial_{+}X_{\gamma_{i}})=0
and the factorization is
(\tau_{2}^{\delta_{2}},\tau_{1}^{\delta_{1}})
,
it follows from Proposition
3.2
that
\hat{X}_{\gamma_{i}}
is diffeomorphic to either
B^{4}
or

\delta_{i}B_{p,q}
for some
p>q>0
. The following result clearly implies Theorem
1.9
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Suppose that a horizontal decomposition of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}\setminus(B^{4}\cup B^{4})
has type

(1,1,2,0)
, factorization
(\tau_{2}^{\delta_{2}},\tau_{1}^{\delta_{1}})
and that

b_{1}(\partial\hat{X}_{\gamma_{1}})=b_{1}(\partial\hat{X}_{\gamma_{2}})=0
.
Then, the set
\{\hat{X}_{\gamma_{1}},\hat{X}_{\gamma_{2}}\}
is one of the following:

(1)

\{B_{m+1},B_{m}\}
for some
m\geq 0
, where
B_{m}=B_{F_{2m-1},F_{2m-5}}
if
\delta_{1}=\delta_{2}=1
;

(2)

\{B^{\prime}_{m+1},B^{\prime}_{m}\}
for some
m\geq 0
, where
B^{\prime}_{m}=(-1)^{m}B_{F_{m+1},F_{m}}
if
\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}=0
;

(3)

\{B^{4}\}

if
\delta_{1}=\delta_{2}=-1
.

Moreover, in Case
(1)
we have
|\gamma_{1}\cdot\gamma_{2}|=3
,
in Cases
(2)
and
(3)
we have
|\gamma_{1}\cdot\gamma_{2}|=1
.

For the proof of Theorem
5.2
we need the following Lemma
5.3
.
Note that the involutions
S
given by
S(x,y)=(y,x)
and minus the identity
-I
generate

G
-actions on
S^{1,1}_{3,-1}
and
S^{-1,1}_{1,-1}\cup S^{1,-1}_{1,-1}
,
where
G=\langle S,-I\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/2Z\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. We denote by
H=\{I,-I\}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
the
subgroup of
G
generated by
-I
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
{
F_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset\mathbb{Z}
be the Fibonacci sequence,
given by
F_{-1}=1
,
F_{0}=0
and
F_{m+1}=F_{m}+F_{m-1}
.
Then, we have

S_{3,-1}^{1,1}=G\cdot\mathcal{S},\quad S_{1,-1}^{-1,1}=H\cdot(\mathcal{T}_{1}\cup\mathcal{T}_{2}),\quad S_{1,-1}^{1,-1}=S\cdot S_{1,-1}^{-1,1}

\text{and}\quad S^{-1,-1}_{1,-1}=\pm\{(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\},

where

\mathcal{S}=\{(-F_{2k-1},F_{2k+1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\ |\ k\geq 0\}

\mathcal{T}_{1}=\{(F_{2k+1},F_{2k})\ |\ k\geq 0\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{T}_{2}=\{(F_{2k+1},-F_{2k+2})\ |\ k\geq 0\}

Proof.

First case:

n=3
,
\delta_{1}=\delta_{2}=1
.
By Lemmas
4.4
and
4.5
,
each
(x,y)\in S_{3,-1}^{1,1}
is sent to either

(1,-1)
or
(-1,1)
by a
sequence of mutations. Then,
(x,y)
is obtained from either
(1,-1)
or
(-1,1)
applying a map of the form

\cdots UVU
or
\cdots VUV
. Since
S\thinspace\colon S_{3,-1}^{1,1}\to S_{3,-1}^{1,1}

is an involution which intertwines the actions of
U
and
V

and
(1,-1)=S(-1,1)
, if
(x,y)\in S_{3,-1}^{1,1}
then

(x,y)
belongs to the
G
-orbit of either

(VU)^{h}(-1,1)
or
U(VU)^{h}(-1,1)
for some
h\geq 0
.
Since
VU=(VS)^{2}=(-VS)^{2}
and
U=S^{2}V=S(VS)
, we can rephrase the
condition on
(x,y)
by saying that
(x,y)
belongs to the
G
-orbit of
(-VS)^{k}(-1,1)
for some
k\geq 0
.
The map
-VS
acts as multiplication by
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
1&3\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, therefore
(x,y)
belongs
to the
G
-orbit of
\{(x_{k},y_{k})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\ |\ k\geq 0\}
, where

\begin{pmatrix}x_{k}\\
y_{k}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&3\end{pmatrix}^{k}\begin{pmatrix}-1\\
1\end{pmatrix}.

The relation
(x_{k+1},y_{k+1})=(-YS)(x_{k},y_{k})
is equivalent to

x_{k+1}=-y_{k}
and
y_{k+1}=x_{k}+3y_{k}
, which imply

x_{k+2}=3x_{k+1}-x_{k}
. Since
(x_{0},y_{0})=(-1,1)
and
(x_{1},y_{1})=(-1,2)
,
we have
x_{k}=-F_{2k-1}
and
y_{k}=F_{2k+1}
for each
k\geq 0
, therefore

(x,y)
belongs to
G\cdot\mathcal{S}
. This shows
S^{1,1}_{3,-1}\subset G\cdot\mathcal{S}
.
Conversely, since
V,S
and
-I
preserve
S^{1,1}_{3,-1}
and the latter
contains
(-1,1)
, we have
G\cdot\mathcal{S}\subset S^{1,1}_{3,-1}
.

Second case:

n=1
,
\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}=0
.
By Lemmas
4.4
and
4.5
, a sequence of mutations sends
each
(x,y)\in S_{1,-1}^{-1,1}
to
(1,0)
or
(-1,0)
and each
(x,y)\in S_{1,-1}^{1,-1}

to
(0,1)
or
(0,-1)
. Since
S\thinspace\colon S_{1,-1}^{-1,1}\to S_{1,-1}^{1,-1}

defines a bijection intertwining
U
and
V
and commuting with
-I
,
it will suffice to determine the elements of
S^{-1,1}_{1,-1}
. Since

U(1,0)=(-1,0)
, if
(x,y)\in S_{1,-1}^{-1,1}

then
(x,y)
belongs to the
H
-orbit of either
(UV)^{h}(1,0)
or
V(UV)^{h}(1,0)
for some

h\geq 0
. Moreover,

UV=-\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}^{2}

and we have

(UV)^{h}=(-1)^{h}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}^{2h},

therefore
(UV)^{h}(1,0)=(-1)^{h}(x_{2h},y_{2h})
, where the sequence
\{(x_{k},y_{k})\}
is defined
by
(x_{0},y_{0})=(1,0)
and

\begin{pmatrix}x_{k+1}\\
y_{k+1}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x_{k}\\
y_{k}\end{pmatrix},\quad k\geq 0.

But this is equivalent to
y_{k}=x_{k-1}
and
x_{k+1}=x_{k}+x_{k-1}
, therefore
x_{k}=F_{k+1}
,
where
F_{k}
is the
k
-th Fibonacci number. We conclude that
(UV)^{h}(1,0)=(-1)^{h}(F_{2h+1},F_{2h})
.
Since
V(F_{2h+1},F_{2h})=(F_{2h+1},-F_{2h+1}-F_{2h})=(F_{2h+1},-F_{2h+2})\in S_{1,-1}^{-1,1}
,
the proof of this case is finished.

Third case:

n=1
,
\delta_{1}=\delta_{2}=-1
.
By Lemmas
4.4
and
4.5
, a sequence of mutations sends
each
(x,y)\in S_{1,-1}^{-1,-1}
to
(\pm 1,0)
or
(0,\pm 1)
. But it is easy to check that
acting with
U
and
V
on
\pm(1,0)
and
\pm(0,1)
the only other pairs one can get
are
\pm(1,1)
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.2
.

In view of Proposition
3.2
, identifying the possible
X_{\gamma_{1}}
and
X_{\gamma_{2}}
amounts to
identifying (up to sign) the possibile homology classes of the curves
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
. Using the notation of
Section
4
, let us fix a symplectic basis

(\mu,\lambda)
of
H_{1}(T;\mathbb{Z})
with
\mu\cdot\lambda=1
, so that
\gamma_{i}=p_{i}\mu+q_{i}\lambda

for some
p_{i},q_{i}
with
(p_{i},q_{i})=1
,
i=1,2
. Note that by Proposition
3.2

the assumption
b_{1}(\partial\hat{X}_{\gamma_{i}})=b_{1}(\partial_{+}X_{\gamma_{i}})=0

implies
p_{1}p_{2}\neq 0
.

We have already observed that the map
(x,y)\mapsto(x,-y)

defines a bijection between
S_{n,\varepsilon}^{\delta_{2},\delta_{1}}
and
S_{-n,\varepsilon}^{\delta_{2},\delta_{1}}
.
Therefore, to list the possible
(|p_{1}|,|p_{2}|)
it suffices to assume
n\in\{1,3\}
.
Moreover, by Remark
4.7
we have
\varepsilon=-\sigma(X)=-1
.
Then, by Lemma
5.3
each pair
(|p_{1}|,|p_{2}|)
is one of the following:

•

(F_{2k+1},F_{2k-1})
or
(F_{2k-1},F_{2k+1})
for some
k\geq 0
when
\delta_{1}=\delta_{2}=1
and
n=3
;

•

(F_{2k+1},F_{2k})
or
(F_{2k+1},F_{2k+2})
for some
k\geq 0
when
(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})=(1,-1)
and
n=1
;

•

(F_{2k},F_{2k+1})
or
(F_{2k+2},F_{2k+1})
for some
k\geq 0
when
(\delta_{1},\delta_{2})=(-1,1)
and
n=1
.

Recall that the Fibonacci numbers satisfy Vajda’s identity:

F_{r}F_{m+j}-F_{m}F_{r+j}=(-1)^{r+1}F_{m-r}F_{j}.

Choosing
j=4
,
m=2k-3
and
r=2k-5
we obtain the relation

(\pm F_{2k-5})F_{2k+1}-(\pm F_{2k-3})F_{2k-1}=\pm 3,

which implies

•

q_{1}\equiv\pm F_{2k-3}\bmod p_{1}
and
q_{2}\equiv\pm F_{2k-5}\bmod p_{2}
when
(|p_{1}|,|p_{2}|)=(F_{2k+1},F_{2k-1})
;

•

q_{1}\equiv\pm F_{2k-5}\bmod p_{1}
and
q_{2}\equiv\pm F_{2k-3}\bmod p_{2}
when
(|p_{1}|,|p_{2}|)=(F_{2k-1},F_{2k+1})
.

Since
B_{p,q}\cong B_{p,p-q}
, when
\delta_{1}=\delta_{2}=1
and

|n|=3
by Proposition
3.2

the 4-manifolds
\hat{X}_{\gamma_{1}}
and
\hat{X}_{\gamma_{2}}
are

B_{F_{2k+1},F_{2k-3}}=B_{k+1}\quad\text{and}\quad B_{F_{2k-1},F_{2k-5}}=B_{k},\quad k\geq 0.

Choosing
j=1
,
m=2k
and

in Vajda’s identity we obtain the relation

(\pm F_{2k-1})F_{2k+1}-(\pm F_{2k})F_{2k}=\pm 1,

which provides us with

q_{1}\equiv\pm F_{2k}\bmod p_{1}
and
q_{2}\equiv\pm F_{2k-1}\bmod p_{2}
when
(|p_{1}|,|p_{2}|)=(F_{2k+1},F_{2k})

and

when
(|p_{1}|,|p_{2}|)=(F_{2k},F_{2k+1})
.

, the corresponding rational balls are

Choosing

,
m=2k+1
and

(\pm F_{2k})F_{2k+2}-(\pm F_{2k+1})F_{2k+1}=\mp 1,

which gives

•

q_{1}\equiv\pm F_{2k}\bmod p_{1}
and
q_{2}\equiv\pm F_{2k+1}\bmod p_{2}
when
(|p_{1}|,|p_{2}|)=(F_{2k+1},F_{2k+2})

•

q_{1}\equiv\pm F_{2k+1}\bmod p_{1}
and

Finally, if

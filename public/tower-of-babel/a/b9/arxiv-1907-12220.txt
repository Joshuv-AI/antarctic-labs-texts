Rigid Analytic Vectors in Locally Analytic Representations
Aranya Lahiri

Abstract
. Let H H be a uniform pro- p p group. Associated to H H are rigid analytic affinoid groups ℍ n {\mathbb{H}}_{n} , and their “wide open” subgroups ℍ n ∘ {\mathbb{H}}_{n}^{\circ} . Denote by D la ​ ( H ) = C la ​ ( H ) b ′ D^{\rm la}(H)=C^{\rm la}(H)^{\prime}_{b} the locally analytic distribution algebra of H H and by D ⁡ ( ℍ n ∘ , H ) D({\mathbb{H}}_{n}^{\circ},H) Emerton’s ring of ℍ n ∘ {\mathbb{H}}_{n}^{\circ} -rigid analytic distributions on H H . (b_{1},\ldots,b_{r})=f_{1}.({\rm pr}_{n}^{A_{\bullet}}(c_{1}^{(1)}),\ldots,{\rm pr}^{A_{\bullet}}_{n}(c_{r}^{(1)}))+\ldots+f_{s}.({\rm pr}^{A_{\bullet}}_{n}(c_{1}^{(s)}),\ldots,{\rm pr}^{A_{\bullet}}_{n}(c_{r}^{(s)}))

It follows that

\displaystyle b_{1}\otimes x_{1}+\ldots+b_{r}\otimes x_{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{r}(b_{j}\otimes x_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\Big(\sum_{i}f_{i}.{\rm pr}^{A_{\bullet}}_{n}(c_{j}^{(i)})\Big)\otimes x_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\Big(\sum_{i}f_{i}\otimes c_{j}^{(i)}x_{j}\Big)=\sum_{i}f_{i}\otimes\Big(\sum_{j}c_{j}^{(i)}x_{j}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum f_{i}\otimes 0=0

The third equality follows from

. So,

carry a canonical
D({\mathbb{H}}_{n}^{\circ},H)
-module topology with respect to which they are
E
-vector spaces of compact type, by
4.2
(ii) and
[
6
, 5.1.1]
.

By
[
10
, 6.3]
the first functor is an anti-equivalence of categories. A quasi-inverse functor is given by
M\rightsquigarrow M^{\prime}_{b}
. We show that this functor is exact. Let

0\longrightarrow M_{1}\xrightarrow{\iota}M_{2}\xrightarrow{\pi}M_{3}\longrightarrow 0

be an exact sequence of coadmissible modules. Consider the sequence of dual spaces

(5.2)

0\longrightarrow M_{3}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\pi^{\prime}}M_{2}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\iota^{\prime}}M_{1}^{\prime}\longrightarrow 0\;.

\pi^{\prime}
is clearly injective, and
\iota^{\prime}\circ\pi^{\prime}=0
. If
\iota^{\prime}(\lambda)=0
, then
\lambda
descends to a continuous linear form
\overline{\lambda}
on
M_{2}/M_{1}
, equipped with the quotient topology. By the open mapping theorem, the continuous bijection
M_{2}/M_{1}\rightarrow M_{3}
is a homeomorphism, and
\overline{\lambda}
is a continuous linear form
\nu
on
M_{3}
, and
\pi^{\prime}(\nu)=\lambda
. Since any morphsim in
{\mathcal{C}}_{D^{\rm la}(H,E)}
is strict (proof of
[
10
, 3.6]
), so is
M_{1}\xrightarrow{\iota}M_{2}
, and any continuous linear form on
M_{1}
extends therefore to a continuous linear form on
M_{2}
(cf.
[
7
, 9.4]
). This shows that
5.2
is exact.

Since
M\rightsquigarrow M^{\prime}_{b}
is an anti-equivalence of categories, any continuous
H
-morphism between admissible locally analytic representations is strict, and the maps in
5.2
are thus strict too, i.e.,
5.2
is an exact sequence of topological vector spaces.

The functor in the middle of
5.1
is exact by
4.4
(ii). Now consider an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow M_{1}\xrightarrow{\iota}M_{2}\xrightarrow{\pi}M_{3}\longrightarrow 0

of
D({\mathbb{H}}_{n}^{\circ},H)
-modules. By
2.10
, the maps in this exact sequence are strict with closed image. It then follows from
[
9

that the sequence of dual spaces

0\longrightarrow M_{3}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\pi^{\prime}}M_{2}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\iota^{\prime}}M_{1}^{\prime}\longrightarrow 0\;.

is exact too.
∎

6.
Example: a weak Fréchet-Stein structure on
D^{\rm la}({\mathbb{Z}_{p}},E)

Let us consider the locally analytic functions
C^{\rm la}({\mathbb{Z}_{p}},E)
. This is a locally convex
E
-vector space with a compact-type topology and is naturally a
{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
-analytic representation over an
E
-vector space of
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
. The dual
D^{\rm la}({\mathbb{Z}_{p}},E)
naturally inherits the structure of a Fréchet space. Let
X
be the rigid analytic open unit disc centered at
0
over
E
, then we can show for any
z\in X({\mathbb{C}}_{p})
and
a\in{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
the function
\kappa_{z}(a):=(1+z)^{a}
defines a locally
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-analytic character of
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
. Given
\lambda\in D^{\rm la}({\mathbb{Z}_{p}},E)
we obtain a function
F_{\lambda}(z):=\lambda(\kappa_{z})
on
X({\mathbb{C}}_{p})
.
F_{\lambda}(z)
is a rigid analytic function on
X
and can thus be expanded as a power series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
where
a_{n}\in E
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}=0
for all
r<1
. If we denote the
E
-valued rigid analytic functions on
X
by
{\mathcal{O}}(X):=\Big\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}T^{n}\,\Big|a_{n}\in E\;\text{s.t}\;\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{n}r^{n}\rightarrow 0\;\forall\;r<1\Big\}
then it is known
[
1
, Thm. 1.3, Section. 2.3.4]
that

\displaystyle D^{\rm la}({\mathbb{Z}_{p}},E)

\displaystyle\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}(X)

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle\rightarrow F_{\lambda}

is an isomorphism.

Let us consider,
C^{\rm la}({\mathbb{Z}_{p}})_{p^{n}{\mathfrak{m}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{p}}-{\rm an}}
, this is the space of locally analytic functions such that around any point on
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
the function is rigid analytic on an open unit disk of radius
|p|^{n}
, i.e.,

C^{\rm la}({\mathbb{Z}_{p}},E)_{p^{n}{\mathfrak{m}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{p}}-{\rm an}}=\bigoplus_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n+1}{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathcal{O}}(a+p^{n}{\mathfrak{m}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{p}})

where
{\mathcal{O}}(a+p^{n}{\mathfrak{m}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{p}}):=\Big\{\sum_{n}c_{n}(T-a)^{n}\,\Big|\;c_{n}\in E,\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}c_{n}r^{n}=0\;\forall\;r<|p|^{n}\Big\}
. By compactness of
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
we see that

C^{\rm la}({\mathbb{Z}_{p}},E)=\varinjlim_{n\geq 0}\Big(\bigoplus_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n+1}{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathcal{O}}(a+p^{n}{\mathfrak{m}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{p}})\Big)\;,

and dually (cf.
[
7
, 16.10 (iii)]
)

D^{\rm la}({\mathbb{Z}_{p}},E)=\varprojlim_{n\geq 0}\Big(\bigoplus_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n+1}{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathcal{O}}(a+p^{n}{\mathfrak{m}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{p}})\Big)^{\prime}_{b}\;.

Our main objective is to analyze,
\bigoplus_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n+1}{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathcal{O}}\Big(a+p^{n}{\mathfrak{m}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{p}}\Big)
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}
be a representative of an element of
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n+1}{\mathbb{Z}}
. Let
a=b+p^{n+1}c
with
|c|=1
and
\frac{b-i}{p^{n+1}}\neq-c\,(\mbox{\,\rm mod\;}p)\;,0\leq i<p^{n+1}
, then

\max_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n+1}{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big|{a\choose k}\Big|=\Big|\lfloor\frac{k}{p^{n+1}}\rfloor!\Big|^{-1}\;.

Proof.

We have,

(6.1)

|a-i|=\begin{cases}|b-i|\hskip 50.0pti\neq b\,(\mbox{\,\rm mod\;}p^{n+1}{\mathbb{Z}_{p}})\\
|p^{n+1}|\hskip 50.0pti=b\,(\mbox{\,\rm mod\;}p^{n+1}{\mathbb{Z}_{p}})\end{cases}

Let
\alpha_{b,j}:=\#\{0\leq i<k\,|\;|b-i|\leq|p|^{j}\}\,,

note with our choices of
a
and
b
,

(6.2)

\alpha_{b,n+1}=\{0\leq i<k\,|\;|b-i|=|p|^{n+1}\;.\}

Using (
6.1
) and (
6.2
) we get that for a fixed
b
,

\displaystyle|a(a-1)\ldots(a-k+1)|=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=b(\,(\mbox{\,\rm mod\;}p^{n+1})\\
0\leq i<k\end{subarray}}|p|^{n+1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\neq b(\,(\mbox{\,\rm mod\;}p^{n+1})\\
0\leq i<k\end{subarray}}|a-i|

\displaystyle=|p|^{(n+1)\alpha_{b,n+1}}|p|^{n(\alpha_{b,n}-\alpha_{b,n+1})}|p|^{(n-1)(\alpha_{b,n-1}-\alpha_{b,n})}\ldots|p|^{(\alpha_{b,1}-\alpha_{b,2})}

\displaystyle=|p|^{\sum_{j=0}^{n+1}\alpha_{b,j}}

We note that
\alpha_{b,j}=\lfloor\frac{k}{p^{j}}\rfloor
or
\lfloor\frac{k}{p^{j}}\rfloor+1
and if we write
k=m.p^{n+1}+b_{0}
, then
\alpha_{b_{0},j}=\lfloor\frac{k}{p^{j}}\rfloor\;\forall\;1\leq j\leq n+1
.

So we obtain,

\max_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n+1}{\mathbb{Z}}}|a(a-1)\ldots(a-k+1)|=|p|^{\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\lfloor\frac{k}{p^{j}}\rfloor}=\Big|\frac{k!}{\lfloor\frac{k}{p^{n+1}}\rfloor!}\Big|

Or equivalently,

\max_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n+1}{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big|{a\choose k}\Big|=\Big|\Big\lfloor\frac{k}{p^{n+1}}\Big\rfloor!\Big|^{-1}

∎

Theorem 6.4
.

Let us define
r_{n}:=|p|^{\frac{1}{(p-1)p^{n}}}
and

{\mathcal{F}}_{n}:=\Big\{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}{x\choose k}\;\Big|\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}c_{k}r_{n}^{k}\epsilon^{k}\rightarrow 0\;\mbox{for all}\,\epsilon<1\Big\}

Given
f\in\bigoplus_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n+1}{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathcal{O}}(a+p^{n}{\mathfrak{m}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{p}})\,,
the map
ev_{f}:{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\rightarrow E
defined by restricting
f
to
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
is locally analytic and its Mahler series lies in
{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
. Conversely, every element of
{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
is obtained as the Mahler series of some
g\in\bigoplus_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n+1}{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathcal{O}}(a+p^{n}{\mathfrak{m}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{p}})
. The resulting map
\bigoplus_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n+1}{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathcal{O}}(a+p^{n}{\mathfrak{m}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{p}})\rightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{n}
is a topological isomorphism.

Proof.

The crux of the proof lies in estimating
p
-adic norms of
{x\choose k}
for
x\in a+p^{n}{\mathfrak{m}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{p}}
. For such an
x
we write
x=a+p^{n}y
, maximum-modulus principle allowing us to choose
|p|<|y|<1
.

If we choose representatives of
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}/p^{n+1}{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
in
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
as
a=b+p^{n+1}c
with
|c|=1
then we have,

(6.3)

|x-i|=\begin{cases}|a-i|\hskip 50.0pti\notin a+p^{n+1}{{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{p}}}\\
|p^{n}y|\hskip 54.0pti\in a+p^{n+1}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{p}}p\end{cases}

Using (
6.3
) we get,

\displaystyle|x(x-1)\ldots(x-k+1)|=|(a+p^{n}y)(a-1+p^{n}y)\ldots(a-k+1+p^{n}y)|

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a-i\in p^{n+1}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{p}}\\
0\leq i<k\end{subarray}}|p^{n}y|\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a-i\notin p^{n+1}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{p}}\\
0\leq i<k\end{subarray}}|a-i|=\prod_{0\leq i<k}|a-i|\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu_{p}(a-i)=n+1\\
0\leq i<k\end{subarray}}|\frac{y}{p}|

\displaystyle=\Big|{a\choose k}k!\Big|\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu_{p}(a-i)=n+1\\
0\leq i<k\end{subarray}}|\frac{y}{p}|

By Thm.
6.1
,

\max\Big\{\Big|{a\choose k}\Big|_{{\mathbb{B}}_{\leq p^{n+1}}(b)}\,\Big|\;b\in{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}/p^{n+1}{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\Big\}=\Big|\lfloor\frac{k}{p^{n+1}}\rfloor!\Big|^{-1}

where
{\mathbb{B}}_{\leq p^{n+1}}(b)
is the closed disk of radius
p^{n+1}
around
b
. And
\#\{\nu_{p}(a-i)=n+1\}\leq\lceil\frac{k-b}{p^{n+1}}-1\rceil
with the bound being sharp. Combining all of these together we get for
x\in a+p^{n}{\mathfrak{m}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{p}}

\displaystyle\Big|{x\choose k}\Big|

\displaystyle=|\lfloor\frac{k}{p^{n+1}}\rfloor!|^{-1}|\frac{y}{p}|^{\lceil\frac{k-b}{p^{n+1}}-1\rceil}\text{\;with\, equality\, for\, certain $a$ and $b$},

\displaystyle\leq|p|^{-\frac{k}{(p-1)p^{n+1}}-\frac{k}{p^{n+1}}}|y|^{\frac{k}{p^{n+1}}}=r_{n}^{-k}(|y|^{\frac{1}{p^{n+1}}})^{k}\text{\;(asymptotically)}

\displaystyle\leq r_{n}^{-k}\epsilon^{k}\;\;\forall\;\epsilon<1.

The second line follows from the facts
|n!|\sim|p|^{\frac{n}{p-1}}
and
\lceil\frac{k-b}{p^{n+1}}-1\rceil\sim\frac{k}{p^{n+1}}
asymptotically.

Let
f\in\bigoplus_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}/p^{n+1}{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}}{\mathcal{O}}(a+p^{n}{\mathfrak{m}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{p}})
(considered as an element of
C^{\rm la}({\mathbb{Z}_{p}},E)
) be written as
f=\sum_{k}c_{k}{x\choose k}
(Mahler series expansion of
f
). Convergence of this expression for
x\in a+p^{n}{\mathfrak{m}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{p}}
gives
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}c_{k}r_{n}^{-k}\epsilon^{k}\rightarrow 0
for every
\epsilon<1
.

That there are no
0<r<r_{n}
such that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}c_{k}r^{k}\epsilon^{k}\rightarrow 0
can be deduced from the logarithmic (in
k
) growth of

\frac{|\lfloor\frac{k}{p^{n+1}}\rfloor!|^{-1}|\frac{y}{p}|^{\lceil\frac{k-b}{p^{n+1}}-1\rceil}}{|p|^{-\frac{k}{(p-1)p^{n+1}}-\frac{k}{p^{n+1}}}|y|^{\frac{k}{p^{n+1}}}}

∎

Now we prove the following theorem

Theorem 6.6
.

Let a space of sequences
(c_{k})_{k},c_{k}\in E
be defined as

{\mathcal{F}}_{n}:=\Big\{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}{x\choose k}\,\Big|\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}c_{k}r_{n}^{-k}\epsilon^{k}\rightarrow 0\,\mbox{for all}\,\epsilon<1\Big\}\,.

Let us define for a fixed
\epsilon<1
the normed space

{\mathcal{F}}_{n,\epsilon}:=\Big\{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}{x\choose k}\;\mbox{where\;}\,\Big|\sum_{k}c_{k}{x\choose k}\Big|_{\epsilon}:=\sup\{|c_{k}|r_{n}^{-k}\epsilon^{k}\}_{k}\Big\}\,.

We have
{\mathcal{F}}_{n}=\varprojlim_{\epsilon<1}{\mathcal{F}}_{n,\epsilon}
. Then the continuous dual of this space is the space of sequence
(d_{k})_{k}
, formally written as
\sum d_{k}T^{k}
can be explicitly described as

{\mathcal{G}}_{n}:=\Big\{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}d_{k}T^{k}\,\Big|\;\exists\;R>r_{n}\;s.t.\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}d_{k}R^{k}\rightarrow 0\Big\}\,.

Proof.

Let
\phi
be a continuous linear functional from
{\mathcal{F}}_{n}\rightarrow E
. By continuity, there exists
\epsilon<1
and
C>0
such that for each
f(x):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}{x\choose k}
we have
|\phi(f(x))|\leq C|f(x)|_{\epsilon}
. Now if we write
\phi({x\choose k})=d_{k}
, then we get

\displaystyle|d_{k}|\leq Cr_{n}^{-k}\epsilon^{k}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle|d_{k}|r_{n}^{k}(\frac{1}{\epsilon})^{k}\leq C

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}|d_{k}|R^{k}=0\;\forall\;R<\frac{r_{n}}{\epsilon}

∎

As a consequence of the previous theorem we get a neat description of the dual of
\bigoplus_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n+1}{\mathbb{C}}_{p}}{\mathcal{O}}(a+p^{n}{\mathfrak{m}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{p}})

Corollary 6.7
.

(\bigoplus_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{n+1}{\mathbb{C}}_{p}}{\mathcal{O}}(a+p^{n}{\mathfrak{m}}_{{\mathbb{C}}_{p}}))^{\prime}_{b}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{r_{n}}(X)^{\dagger}
, where
{\mathcal{O}}_{r_{n}}(X)^{\dagger}
is the space of overconvergent functions on a closed disk of radius
r_{n}

Proof.

On the level of sets this immediately follows from the above theorem once we explicitly recall the description of

{\mathcal{O}}_{r_{n}}(X)^{\dagger}:=\Big\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}T^{n}\;\Big|\;\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{n}R^{n}\rightarrow 0\mbox{ for some }R>r_{n}\Big\}\;.

In fact it can be shown that the isomorphism is indeed an isomorphism of topological vector spaces.
∎

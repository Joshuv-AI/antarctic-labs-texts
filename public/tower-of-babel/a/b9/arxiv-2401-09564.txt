Well-posedness and decay for a nonlinear propagation wave model in atmospheric flows
Diego Alonso-Orán, Rafael Granero-Belinchón

Abstract
. In this note, we provide two results concerning the global well-posedness and decay of solutions to an asymptotic model describing the nonlinear wave propagation in the troposphere, namely, the morning glory phenomenon. The proof of the first result combines a pointwise estimate together with some interpolation inequalities to close the energy estimates in Sobolev spaces. The second proof relies on suitable Wiener-like functional spaces.

1.
Introduction and main result

Many fascinating events that constantly test our comprehension of the dynamic and intricate atmospheric processes on Earth characterize the field of atmospheric science. Of all these mysterious events,
the morning glory
is the one weather phenomenon that has fascinated scientists for decades. Long, horizontal cloud bands that often spread across the sky to make a recognizable and arresting pattern are what define the morning glory phenomenon. Recently in
[
3
]
, the authors derived from the general Navier-Stokes equation in rotating spherical coordinates a more tractable asymptotic nonlinear system that describe this wave propagation given by

(1a)

\displaystyle u_{t}+uu_{x}+vu_{y}

\displaystyle=\mu\Delta u+\alpha u+\beta v+F,\ \mbox{ in }\Omega,\ t>0,

(1b)

\displaystyle u_{x}+v_{y}

\displaystyle=0,\ \mbox{ in }\Omega,\ t>0.

The components of the vector velocity field are denoted by
u,v
,
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mu\in(0,\infty)
is the viscosity parameter. The force
F
represents a thermodynamic forcing term, which comprises the heat sources driving the motion. Equation (
1
b) comprises the incompressibility condition of the flow. In (
1
) the spatial domain
\Omega
is the two-dimensional channel domain

\Omega=\{(x,y)\text{ s.t. }x\in\mathbb{T},\ 0<y<1\},

and the time satisfies
t\in[0,T]
for certain
0<T\leq\infty
. Moreover, equations (
1
) are subject to the following boundary conditions

(2a)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0,\ \partial\Omega,\ t>0,

(2b)

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=0,\ \mbox{ on }\{y=0\},\ t>0.

The corresponding initial-value problem consists of the system (
1
), (
2
) along with the initial conditions

(3)

u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)

which is assumed to be smooth enough for the purposes of the work (cf. the statement of Theorem
1
for a the precise assumptions on the initial data).

As already mentioned, system (
1
) was derived by Constantin & Johnson
[
3
]
. Moreover, Constantin & Johnson provided a number of exact solutions: breeze-like flows, bore-like flows as well as oscillatory-like solutions, cf.
[
3
, §6]
. The same named authors investigated the existence of travelling-wave solutions, cf.
[
4
]
. Their analysis relies on studying a nonlinear second ordinary differential equation by means of a global analysis phase-space using Lyapunov functions. Recently, Matioc & Roberti in
[
9
]
, using an abstract quasilinear parabolic evolution framework, showed the global existence of weak solutions to (
1
) as well as the local existence of classical solutions.

In order to present the main result of this work, it is convenient to rewrite (
1
) by eliminating the variable
v
. Following the approach in
[
9
]
, we find that integrating
v
in (
1
b) from
0
to
y
, we have that

v(x,y)=\int_{0}^{y}v_{y}(x,\xi)\ d\xi=-\int_{0}^{y}u_{x}(x,\xi)\ d\xi:=-Tu(x,y),

where
Tf:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

Tf(x,y):=\int_{0}^{y}f_{x}(x,\xi)\ d\xi.

This idea of using the fundamental theorem of calculus to express
v
in terms of
u
is reminiscing of the viscous primitive equations of large scale ocean and atmosphere dynamics, cf.
[
2
]
. Therefore, using this observation system (
1
) can be rewritten as

(4a)

\displaystyle u_{t}+uu_{x}-Tuu_{y}

\displaystyle=\mu\Delta u+\alpha u-\beta Tu+F,\ \mbox{ in }\Omega,\ t>0,

(4b)

\displaystyle Tu

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{y}u_{x}(x,\xi)\ d\xi.

For convenience, we will work with a
2\pi
-periodic version of the infinite channel, namely,

\Omega=\{(x,y):x\in\mathbb{T},\ 0<y<1\},

and take
\alpha\leq 0
, and no external forcing, i.e,
F\equiv 0
. Hence, (
4
) becomes

(5a)

\displaystyle u_{t}+uu_{x}-Tuu_{y}

\displaystyle=\mu\Delta u+\alpha u-\beta Tu,\ \mbox{ in }\Omega,\ t>0,

(5b)

\displaystyle Tu

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{y}u_{x}(x,\xi)\ d\xi,

supplemented with

(6)

u=0,\ \partial\Omega,\ t>0,\ u(z,0)=u_{0}(z),\ z\in\Omega.

The main result of this work is to provide the decay of the
L^{\infty}
norm for arbitrary initial data together with a global existence of classical solutions to under a smallness assumption on the initial data. More precisely, the result reads as follows

Theorem 1
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
a zero mean function. Then, the corresponding solution satisfies

\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Furthermore, if
u_{0}
is such that
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
is sufficiently small. Then, there exists a unique global in time solution
u\in C([0,\infty),H^{1}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,\infty;H^{2}(\Omega))
.

Remark 1
.

Just after the completion of this article, the preprint
[
8
]
appeared in the arXivs. The author shows a similar result to the one provided in this article but with some key differences. Compared with
[
8
]
, our work proves the
L^{\infty}
decay of the solution. Such decay is not available in the literature before and to the best of the author’s knowledge it is new. Furthermore, our well-posedness result requires an
L^{\infty}
smallness assumption on the initial data to absorb the non-linear contribution. In
[
8
]
such nonlinear term is handled using by means of a clever and elegant extension method.

We also prove the following theorem

Theorem 2
.

Let
u_{0}\in\tilde{A^{0}}(\Omega)
a zero mean function such that
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{\tilde{A^{0}}(\Omega)}
is sufficiently small. Then, there exists a unique global in time solution
u\in C([0,\infty),\tilde{A^{0}}(\Omega))\cap L^{1}(0,\infty;\tilde{A^{2}}(\Omega))
.

Notation

We denote by
(f,g)
the
L^{2}
inner product of
f,g
. For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the natural inhomogeneous and homogeneous Sobolev norms are defined by

\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{H^{m}(\Omega)}:=\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|\partial^{m}f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{H}^{m}(\Omega)}^{2}:=\left\|\partial^{m}f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

respectively. For notational convenience, we will just write
L^{2},H^{m},\dot{H}^{m}
instead of
L^{2}(\Omega),H^{m}(\Omega),\dot{H}^{m}(\Omega).
Moreover, throughout the paper
C=C(\cdot)
will denote a positive constant that may depend on fixed parameters and
x\lesssim y
(
x\gtrsim y
) means that
x\leq Cy
(
x\geq Cy
) holds for some
C
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1

The proof follows by deriving appropriate a priori energy estimates combined with a suitable approximation procedure. To that purpose, we first provide the a priori energy estimates.
First of all, notice that mean-zero property is conserved as long as the solution exists. Denoting by
z=(x,y)\in\Omega
and integrating
(
5a
) in
\Omega
we find that

(7)

\partial_{t}\int_{\Omega}u(z,t)dz=-\int_{\Omega}\left(uu_{x}-Tuu_{y}-\mu\Delta u-\alpha u+\beta Tu\right)(z,t)\ dz.

Notice that the first and the third term on the right hand side in (
7
) are zero using the periodicity in the
x
variable and the boundary condition (
6
). Furthermore, integrating by parts in the
y
variable, recalling the definition (
5b
) and using that
u=0
in
\partial\Omega
we also have that

\int_{\Omega}Tuu_{y}\ dz=-\int_{\Omega}u_{x}u\ dz=0.

Similarly,

\beta\int_{\Omega}Tu\ dz=-\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{y}u_{x}(x,\xi)d\xi\ dz=0.

Therefore,

\partial_{t}\int_{\Omega}u(z,t)dz=\alpha\int_{\Omega}u(z,t)\ dz,

and since by assumption
u_{0}(z)
has zero mean and
\alpha\leq 0
we conclude that

(8)

\int_{\Omega}u(z,t)\ dz=0,\quad\mbox{for }t>0.

Next, let us derive a pointwise estimate. Following,
[
6
,
7
]
, we define

(9)

\displaystyle M(t)=\displaystyle\max_{z\in\Omega}u(z,t)=u(\overline{z}_{t},t),\mbox{ for }t>0,

(10)

\displaystyle m(t)=\displaystyle\min_{z\in\Omega}u(z,t)=u(\underline{z}_{t},t),\mbox{ for }t>0.

One can readily check that
M(t),m(t)
are Lipschitz functions. Moreover, one can readily check that
M(t)
satisfies

\displaystyle\left|M(t)-M(s)\right|

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}u(\overline{z}_{t},t)-u(\overline{z}_{s},s)\text{ if }M(t)>M(s)\\
u(\overline{z}_{s},s)-u(\overline{z}_{t},t)\text{ if }M(s)>M(t)\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}u(\overline{z}_{t},t)-u(\overline{z}_{t},s)\text{ if }M(t)>M(s)\\
u(\overline{z}_{s},s)-u(\overline{z}_{s},t)\text{ if }M(s)>M(t)\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}|\partial_{t}u(\overline{z}_{t},\xi)||t-s|\text{ if }M(t)>M(s)\\
|\partial_{t}u(\overline{z}_{s},\xi)||t-s|\text{ if }M(s)>M(t)\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\eta,\xi}|\partial_{t}u(\eta,\xi)||t-s|.

Similarly

\left|m(t)-m(s)\right|\leq\max_{\eta,\xi}|\partial_{t}u(\eta,\xi)||t-s|.

From Rademacher’s theorem it holds that
M(t)
and
m(t)
are differentiable in
t
almost everywhere. Furthermore

\displaystyle M^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{u(\overline{z}_{t+\delta},t+\delta)-u(\overline{z}_{t},t)}{\delta}=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{u(\overline{z}_{t+\delta},t+\delta)-u(\overline{z}_{t},t)\pm u(\overline{z}_{t+\delta},t)}{\delta}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{u(\overline{z}_{t+\delta},t+\delta)-u(\overline{z}_{t+\delta},t)}{\delta}\leq\partial_{t}u(\overline{z}_{t},t).

In a similar fashion, we obtain that

\displaystyle M^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{u(\overline{z}_{t+\delta},t+\delta)-u(\overline{z}_{t},t)}{\delta}=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{u(\overline{z}_{t+\delta},t+\delta)-u(\overline{z}_{t},t)\pm u(\overline{z}_{t},t+\delta)}{\delta}

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{u(\overline{z}_{t},t+\delta)-u(\overline{z}_{t},t)}{\delta}\geq\partial_{t}u(\overline{z}_{t},t).

As a consequence

(11)

M^{\prime}(t)=\partial_{t}u(\overline{z}_{t},t)\text{ a.e.}

Similarly

m^{\prime}(t)=\partial_{t}u(\underline{z}_{t},t)\text{ a.e.}

Therefore, using (
5a
) and noticing that
u_{x}(\overline{z}_{t},t)=u_{y}(\overline{z}_{t},t)=0
and
\Delta u(\overline{z}_{t},t)\leq 0
we find that

M^{\prime}(t)\leq 0,

which implies that

(12)

M(t)\leq M(0).

Similarly, repeating the same argument and recalling that
\Delta u(\underline{z}_{t},t)\geq 0
,

(13)

m(t)\geq m(0).

Notice that the maximum is obtained in the interior of
\Omega
, i.e.
z\in\dot{\Omega}
and moreover
M(t)>0
. Otherwise, the maximum is obtained on the boundary and hence using the boundary condition (
6
) this implies
M(t)=0
. Similarly, the minimum must be obtained in the interior of
\Omega
, otherwise
m(t)=0
but this violates (
8
). As a consequence
m(t)<0
. Hence, combining (
12
) and (
13
) we have that

(14)

\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Testing equation (
5a
) against
u
, integrating by parts and using the periodicity in
x
variable and boundary condition (
6
) we have that

(15)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(z,t)|^{2}\ dz=-\int_{\Omega}Tuu_{y}u\ dz+\alpha\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}-\beta\int_{\Omega}Tuu\ dz

Moreover, integrating by parts in the
y
variable, recalling the definition (
5b
) and using that
u=0
in
\partial\Omega
we also have that

(16)

\int_{\Omega}Tuu_{y}u\ dz=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{0}^{1}u_{x}(x,y)u^{2}(x,y)dx\ dy=0,

by periodicity in the
x
variable. Furthermore, by using Jensen’s inequality one can readily check that

\left\|Tu\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{0}^{y}u_{x}(x,\xi)\ d\xi\right)^{2}dz\right)^{1/2}\leq\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}},

which combined with Hölder’s and Young’s inequality shows that

(17)

\beta\int_{\Omega}|Tuu|\ dz\leq\beta\left\|Tu\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Hence, using (
15
), (
16
), (
17
) and recalling that
\alpha\leq 0
we have that

\frac{d}{dt}\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(z,t)|^{2}\ dz\leq\beta^{2}\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

and thus Grönwalls ineqality yields

(18)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|u(t)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\nabla u(t)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\ dt\leq\left\|u_{0}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}e^{T}.

Next we compute the evolution of the
\dot{H}^{1}
norm. To that purpose, testing (
5a
) against
-\Delta u
and integrating by parts

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\nabla u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u|^{2}\ dz

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}Tuu_{y}\Delta u\ dz-\int_{\Omega}uu_{x}\Delta u\ dz-\alpha\int_{\Omega}u\Delta u\ dz+\beta\int_{\Omega}Tu\Delta udz

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}.

By means of Hölder’s and Jensen’s inequality we find that

\displaystyle|I_{1}+I_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|Tu\right\|_{L^{4}}\left\|u_{y}\right\|_{L^{4}}\left\|\Delta u\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|u_{x}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\Delta u\right\|_{L^{2}}

(19)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|u_{x}\right\|_{L^{4}}\left\|u_{y}\right\|_{L^{4}}\left\|\Delta u\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|u_{x}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\Delta u\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Furthermore, integrating by parts in
x
and recalling the periodic boundary conditions in
x
, we have that

(20)

\left\|u_{x}\right\|^{4}_{L^{4}}\leq 3\left\|u_{x}\right\|^{2}_{L^{4}}\left\|u_{xx}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\Longleftrightarrow\left\|u_{x}\right\|^{2}_{L^{4}}\leq 3\left\|u_{xx}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Similarly, using the fact that
u=0
on
\partial\Omega
, we infer that

(21)

\left\|u_{y}\right\|^{2}_{L^{4}}\leq 3\left\|u_{yy}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Hence, using (
20
)-(
21
) into (
2
) and applying Young’s inequality we arrive to

\displaystyle|I_{1}+I_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq 3\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\Delta u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|u_{x}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\Delta u\right\|_{L^{2}}

(22)

\displaystyle\leq\left(3\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\right)\left\|\Delta u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

The remaining terms
I_{3},I_{4}
can be estimated easily, namely,

(23)

\displaystyle|I_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq|\alpha|\left\|\Delta u\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\left\|\Delta u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2\epsilon}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(24)

\displaystyle|I_{4}|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|Tu\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\Delta u\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\left\|\Delta u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{|\beta|^{2}}{2\epsilon}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Hence, collecting bounds (
22
), (
23
), (
24
), we infer that

(25)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\nabla u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u|^{2}\ dz\leq\left(3\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}+\epsilon\right)\left\|\Delta u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{|\beta|^{2}}{2\epsilon}\right)\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2\epsilon}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Invoking the
L^{\infty}
decay estimate (
14
) and taking
\epsilon
and
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
small enough we conclude that

(26)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\nabla u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u|^{2}\ dz\leq C_{\beta}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\alpha}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Integrating in time and taking supremums, we have that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\|\nabla u\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u|^{2}\ dz

\displaystyle\leq C_{\beta}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\ dt+C_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{T}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\ dt

(27)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\nabla u_{0}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+C_{\beta}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}e^{T}+C_{\alpha}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}e^{T}T

where in the second inequality we have used estimate (
18
). This concludes the
a priori
estimates. The approximation procedure can be done using Galerkin method. To obtain the uniqueness, we argue by contradiction. If
u
and
w
are two solutions emanating from the same initial data, we consider
U
their difference. Then,
U
solves

U_{t}=-uU_{x}-TUu_{y}-Uw_{x}-TwU_{y}+\mu\Delta U+\alpha U-\beta TU.

Testing against
U
and integrating by parts, we find

\frac{d}{dt}\|U\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq-\|\nabla U\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|U\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla U\|_{L^{2}}(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}})+\|U_{y}\|_{L^{2}}\|TU\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+2\|U\|_{L^{2}}\|U_{x}\|_{L^{2}}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Using the smallness hypothesis we conclude the desired bound and the result.

3.
Proof of Theorem
2

Since

\Omega=\{(x,y)\text{ s.t. }x\in\mathbb{T},\ 0<y<1\},

we consider the Fourier series representation

u(x,y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{m\geq 1}\hat{u}(n,m)e^{inx}\sin(m\pi y),

together with the Wiener-like spaces

\tilde{\mathcal{A}^{s}}=\left\{u\text{ s.t. }\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{m\geq 1}(|n|^{s}+|m|^{s})|\hat{u}(n,m)|<\infty\right\}.

These spaces have similar properties to those in
[
1
]
and the references therein. In particular, we observe that

\tilde{\mathcal{A}^{s}}\subset C^{s}

and that they form a Banach scale of Banach algebras. These spaces will allow us to achieve maximal parabolic regularity. Indeed, if we now estimate the evolution of these norms, we find that

\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)\|_{\tilde{\mathcal{A}^{0}}}\leq\|u\|_{\tilde{\mathcal{A}^{0}}}\|u\|_{\tilde{\mathcal{A}^{1}}}+\|u\|_{\tilde{\mathcal{A}^{1}}}^{2}-\mu\|u\|_{\tilde{\mathcal{A}^{2}}},

where we have used
\alpha\leq 0
and

\widehat{Tu}(n,m)=\frac{n}{m}\hat{u}(n,m).

Using interpolation, we find that

\|u\|_{\tilde{\mathcal{A}^{1}}}^{2}\leq\|u\|_{\tilde{\mathcal{A}^{0}}}\|u\|_{\tilde{\mathcal{A}^{2}}}.

As a consequence, we conclude

\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)\|_{\tilde{\mathcal{A}^{0}}}\leq 2\|u\|_{\tilde{\mathcal{A}^{0}}}\|u\|_{\tilde{\mathcal{A}^{2}}}-\mu\|u\|_{\tilde{\mathcal{A}^{2}}}.

This concludes the
a priori
estimates of the solution. The approximation procedure can be done using a standard Galerkin approximation in finite dimensional projections. The uniqueness is a consequence of the obtained regularity and a standard contradiction result, from where we conclude the desired result.

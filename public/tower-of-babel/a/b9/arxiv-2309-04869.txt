CMS results for the γ ​ γ \gamma\gamma production at the LHC: do they give a hint for a Higgs boson of the maximally CP symmetric two-Higgs-doublet model?
M. Maniatis, O. Nachtmann

Abstract
Recent measurements of the CMS experiment at the LHC show possibly, with about 3 ​ σ 3\sigma significance, a resonance in di-photon events with an invariant mass of 95.4 GeV. If this resonance can be confirmed, this could be a hint for a new elementary particle beyond the Standard Model. An additional Standard-model-like Higgs boson with this mass could be excluded by the CMS experiment. We investigate whether this resonance could fit into a two-Higgs-doublet model highly constrained by CP symmetry, the so-called maximally-CP-symmetric model (MCPM). In the strict symmetry limit of the MCPM only the fermions of the third generation obtain masses. We discuss a mechanism where the first and second generation fermions get masses through effects from interactions with very high mass particles. The latter can be integrated out at LHC energies giving effective Lagrangian terms, among them these mass terms. This procedure also gives us a Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) matrix which automatically satisfies some salient structural features observed in experiments. We call the resulting theory MCPM’. Our finding shows that indeed the enhancement measured by CMS could originate from the pseudoscalar Higgs boson h ′′ h^{\prime\prime} of this model. According to the model the boson h ′′ h^{\prime\prime} would mainly be produced in the Drell-Yan reaction by charm-anticharm-quark fusion. The main decay mode of h ′′ h^{\prime\prime} is predicted to be h ′′ → c ​ c ¯ h^{\prime\prime}\to c\bar{c} . We then consider the so called oblique parameters S S , T T , U U which give us an allowed region for the mass of the scalar Higgs boson h ​ ’ h\textquoteright versus that of the charged ones H ± H^{\pm} of the MCPM’. We calculate the effect of these charged bosons H ± H^{\pm} in the leptonic decays of the charm mesons D ± D^{\pm} and the charm-strange mesons D s ± D_{s}^{\pm} . Our results indicate that the mass m H ± m_{H^{\pm}} of the charged Higgs bosons H ± H^{\pm} of the MCPM’ should be around 300 GeV.

Introduction

The search for physics beyond the Standard Model (SM) is a central topic of the current experiments at the LHC.
One can, for instance, ask if there exist Higgs bosons in addition to the one Higgs boson of the SM, which by now is well established; see e.g.
ATLAS:2022vkf
.
One possible extension of the SM is to a model with two Higgs doublets, a 2HDM. 2HDM’s
have a long history and

An interesting feature of the Higgs sector of 2HDMs is that there one can define so called generalized CP (GCP) transformations. Due to the presence of two Higgs doublets the standard CP transformation can be augmented by a unitary mixing of the doublets. A convenient classification of GCP transformations has, for instance, been given in
Maniatis:2007vn

Maniatis:2007de
. This model is the starting point of our present study.
A short description of this model is given in
Maniatis:2010sb
.
Phenomenological consequences of the MCPM have been presented by us in
Maniatis:2009vp

Maniatis:2009by

As in all 2HDMs the MCPM contains as physical Higgs bosons three neutral ones, denoted by us as

Basics of the MCPM

The MCPM has been introduced first in
Maniatis:2007de

Maniatis:2010sb

For the convenience of the reader, we outline here the essential features of the MCPM.

The MCPM is a two-Higgs-doublet model, 2HDM. It has the two Higgs-boson doublets,

\varphi_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}\varphi_{1}^{+}\\
\varphi_{1}^{0}\end{pmatrix},\quad\varphi_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\varphi_{2}^{+}\\
\varphi_{2}^{0}\end{pmatrix}.

(2)

Both are assumed to have weak hypercharge
y=1/2
. It is convenient to use the
K

Nagel:2004sw
;
Maniatis:2006fs
;
Maniatis:2007vn
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}K_{0}=\varphi_{1}^{\dagger}\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}^{\dagger}\varphi_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\varphi_{1}^{\dagger},\varphi_{2}^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\varphi_{1}\\
\varphi_{2}\end{pmatrix},\\
\boldsymbol{K}=\begin{pmatrix}K_{1}\\
K_{2}\\
K_{3}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\varphi_{1}^{\dagger}\varphi_{2}+\varphi_{2}^{\dagger}\varphi_{1}\\
i\varphi_{2}^{\dagger}\varphi_{1}-i\varphi_{1}^{\dagger}\varphi_{2}\\
\varphi_{1}^{\dagger}\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}^{\dagger}\varphi_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\varphi_{1}^{\dagger},\varphi_{2}^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\begin{pmatrix}\varphi_{1}\\
\varphi_{2}\end{pmatrix}.\end{split}

(3)

Here
\boldsymbol{\sigma}=(\sigma^{1},\sigma^{2},\sigma^{3})^{\mathrm{T}}
and
\sigma^{a}

(a=1,2,3)
are
the Pauli matrices.
Basis changes of the Higgs fields (
2

\varphi_{i}(x)\to\varphi_{i}^{\prime}(x)=U_{ij}\varphi_{j}(x),\quad U=\left(U_{ij}\right)\in U(2),

(4)

\begin{split}&K_{0}(x)\to K_{0}^{\prime}(x)=K_{0}(x),\\
&K_{a}(x)\to K_{a}^{\prime}(x)=R_{ab}(U)K_{b}(x),\end{split}

(5)

where

U^{\dagger}\sigma^{a}U=R_{ab}(U)\sigma^{b},\quad R(U)=\left(R_{ab}(U)\right)\in SO(3).

(6)

In this formalism the most general Higgs potential reads

V=\xi_{0}K_{0}+\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\mathrm{T}}\boldsymbol{K}+\eta_{00}K_{0}^{2}+2K_{0}\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\mathrm{T}}\boldsymbol{K}+\boldsymbol{K}^{\mathrm{T}}E\boldsymbol{K}.

(7)

Here the parameters
\xi_{0}
,
\eta_{00}
, the 3-component vectors

,

Now we come to generalised CP (GCP) transformations which are generically of the form

\varphi_{i}(x)\to U_{ij}\varphi_{j}^{*}(x^{\prime}),\quad x=(x^{0},\boldsymbol{x})^{\mathrm{T}},\quad x^{\prime}=(x^{0},-\boldsymbol{x})^{\mathrm{T}},\\
U=\left(U_{ij}\right)\in U(2).

(8)

3

K

K_{0}(x)\to K_{0}(x^{\prime}),\quad\boldsymbol{K}(x)\to\tilde{R}\boldsymbol{K}(x^{\prime}),

(9)

where
\tilde{R}

\det(\tilde{R})=-1
.
For a GCP transformation we require that applying it twice gives back the unit transformation in
K

\tilde{R}^{2}=\mathbbm{1}_{3}

This leads to two types of GCPs:

\begin{split}(i)\qquad\tilde{R}=&-\mathbbm{1}_{3},\text{ point reflection in }K\text{ space,}\\
(ii)\qquad\tilde{R}=&R^{\mathrm{T}}\tilde{R}_{2}R,\text{ reflection on a plane in }K\text{ space,}\\
&\text{ where }\tilde{R}_{2}=\diag(1,-1,1),\quad R\in SO(3)\;.\end{split}

(ii)

Setting

VI
Conclusions

In this paper we have extended the maximally-CP-symmetric model, MCPM, from
Maniatis:2007de
to a realistic model which we call MCPM’. In the MCPM only the fermions of the third family have masses, the fermions of the second and first family remain massless. Clearly, this is only a first approximation to what is observed in nature. In the present work we discuss in detail the generation of masses for the first and second family and of a nontrivial CKM matrix in the MCPM’. The key idea is to assume the existence of high-mass (of order 10 TeV) fields
\chi_{i}(x)
, (
i=1,2
) and
\zeta(x)
.
The effective interactions generated by these fields for the “low energy” LHC regime give us the fermion masses for the second and first generation.
We also get an interesting method to generate EWSB; see (
119
), (
120
).
The CKM matrix which we obtain has automatically some salient features as observed in experiment (see (
180
) and (
181
)).
All this is explained in our Appendix
A
.
The Higgs sector of the MCPM’ is as in the MCPM; see
Maniatis:2007de
–
Maniatis:2009by
.

In our study, we have investigated the implications of the hypothesis that the
possible
anomaly observed by CMS
CMS:2023yay
in the
\gamma\gamma
yield at 95.4 GeV may be attributed to the production and decay of the
h^{\prime\prime}
boson of the MCPM’.
We found
from our calculation for the
product
\sigma(p+p\to h^{\prime\prime}+X)\times\text{B}(h^{\prime\prime}\to\gamma+\gamma)\approx 0.01
pb, to be
compared with the experimental result of 0.01–0.04 pb for this quantity.
We argued that our above result should be considered rather as a lower limit from theory.
How could
experimentalists explore the consequences of this finding further?

The MCPM’ gives also predictions for the decay
h^{\prime\prime}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
from the coupling shown in
Fig.
1
,

\begin{split}&\Gamma(h^{\prime\prime}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})=0.198\penalty\ \text{MeV},\\
&\text{B}(h^{\prime\prime}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-})=3.44\times 10^{-5}\;.\end{split}

(102)

Therefore, we have the prediction for
m_{h^{\prime\prime}}=95.4
GeV

\left[\sigma_{\text{DY}}(p+p\to h^{\prime\prime}+X)+\sigma_{\text{GG}}(p+p\to h^{\prime\prime}+X)\right]\\
\times\text{B}(h^{\prime\prime}\to\mu^{+}+\mu^{-})=0.62\penalty\ \text{pb}.

(103)

GeV is rather close to the

Such an experimental study is far beyond the scope of this article,
which primarily focuses on the theoretical predictions.
In this context it may be
very advantageous to consider diffractive production of the

in

where the

\mathbbm{P}

The cross section
\sigma(p(p_{1})+p(p_{2})\to\gamma\gamma+X)

Here we calculate the cross section
\sigma(p(p_{1})+p(p_{2})\to\gamma\gamma+X)
taking the finite width of the Higgs boson
h^{\prime\prime}
,
\Gamma_{h^{\prime\prime}}
, into account.
We consider the variation of the cross section for
h^{\prime\prime}
production with the actual
h^{\prime\prime}
mass, that is, with
m_{\gamma\gamma}
, and similarly the variation of
\Gamma(h^{\prime\prime}\to\gamma\gamma)
. This gives the following formula:

\sigma(p(p_{1})+p(p_{2})\to\gamma+\gamma+X)=\\
\left[B(h^{\prime\prime}\to\gamma\gamma)\times\sigma(p(p_{1})+p(p_{2})\to h^{\prime\prime}+X)\right]_{\text{eff}}=\\
\int\frac{d\sigma}{dm^{2}_{\gamma\gamma}}\big(p(p_{1})+p(p_{2})\to\gamma(k_{1})+\gamma(k_{2})+X\big)_{k^{2}=(k_{1}+k_{2})^{2}=m_{\gamma\gamma}^{2}}\\
\times 2m_{\gamma\gamma}dm_{\gamma\gamma}\;,

(189)

with

\frac{d\sigma}{dm^{2}_{\gamma\gamma}}\big(p(p_{1})+p(p_{2})\to\gamma(k_{1})+\gamma(k_{2})+X\big)_{k^{2}=m_{\gamma\gamma}^{2}}=\\
\left.B(h^{\prime\prime}(k)\to\gamma\gamma)\right|_{k^{2}=m_{\gamma\gamma}^{2}}\\
\times\left.\sigma(p(p_{1})+p(p_{2})\to h^{\prime\prime}(k)+X)\right|_{k^{2}=m_{\gamma\gamma}^{2}}\\
\times\frac{m_{\gamma\gamma}}{m_{h^{\prime\prime}}}\frac{\left.\Gamma_{h^{\prime\prime}}\right|_{k^{2}=m_{\gamma\gamma}^{2}}}{\Gamma_{h^{\prime\prime}}}\frac{m_{h^{\prime\prime}}\Gamma_{h^{\prime\prime}}/\pi}{(m^{2}_{\gamma\gamma}-m^{2}_{h^{\prime\prime}})^{2}+m^{2}_{h^{\prime\prime}}\Gamma^{2}_{h^{\prime\prime}}}\;.

\left.\Gamma_{h^{\prime\prime}}\right|_{k^{2}=m_{\gamma\gamma}^{2}}
denotes the total width with the mass of

set to the invariant mass
m_{\gamma\gamma}
of the two photons. In the integral in
(
189
)

m_{h^{\prime\prime}}-\kappa\Gamma_{h^{\prime\prime}}\leq m_{\gamma\gamma}\leq m_{h^{\prime\prime}}+\kappa\Gamma_{h^{\prime\prime}},\quad\text{with }\kappa=1/2,1,2\;.

(191)

\displaystyle\kappa=1/2:\;

\displaystyle\sigma=0.0051\penalty\ \text{pb},

\displaystyle\kappa=1:\;

\displaystyle\sigma=0.0072\penalty\ \text{pb},

(192)

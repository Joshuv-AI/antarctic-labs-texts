A hybrid framework for estimating nonlinear functions of quantum states
You Zhou, Zhenhuan Liu

Abstract
Estimating nonlinear functions of quantum states, such as the moment tr ⁡ ( ρ m ) \tr(\rho^m) , is of fundamental and practical interest in quantum science and technology. Here we show a quantum-classical hybrid framework to measure them, where the quantum part is constituted by the generalized swap test, and the classical part is realized by postprocessing the result from randomized measurements. This hybrid framework utilizes the partial coherent power of the intermediate-scale quantum processor and, at the same time, dramatically reduces the number of quantum measurements and the cost of classical postprocessing. We demonstrate the advantage of our framework in the tasks of state-moment estimation and quantum error mitigation.



Quantum measurement is one of the fundamental building blocks of quantum physics, connecting the quantum world to its classical counterpart. The linear expectation value of the quantum state
\rho
in the form
\tr(O\rho)
can be measured directly in the basis of the observable
O
by Born’s rule. However, the measurement of nonlinear functions, such as the R
\mathrm{\acute{e}}
nyi entropy and the moment
P_{m}=\tr(\rho^m)
, generally involves quantum circuits interfering among
m
copies of the state
\rho
by the generalized swap test
Ekert et al. 2002
;
Horodecki and Ekert 2002
;
Bruni 2004
;
Garcia-Escartin and Chamorro-Posada 2013
, which transform nonlinear functions to linear ones on all that copies. Although there are significant experimental advances for the
m=2
case
Islam et al. 2015
;
Kaufman et al. 2016
;
Cotler et al. 2019
;
Huang et al. 2022a
, it is still challenging to extend to a larger degree
m
with a moderate system size on current quantum platforms
Preskill 2018
.

Recently alternative approaches based on the randomized measurement (RM)
Elben et al. 2023
, such as the shadow estimation
Aaronson 2019
;
Huang et al. 2020
, are proposed. By postprocessing the results from random basis measurements of sequentially prepared states,
the shadow estimation is efficient for measuring local observables and the fidelity to some entangled states. However, when measuring nonlinear functions, such as the purity
P_{2}
, RM protocol inevitably needs an exponential number of measurements and postprocessings
Elben et al. 2020a
;
Huang et al. 2020
;
Zhou et al. 2020
, which hinders its further applications for large systems.

By trading off the swap test and the RM protocol, here we propose a hybrid framework for estimating nonlinear functions of quantum states to fill the gap between them, which inherits their advantages and reduces weaknesses. Specifically, one can conduct RM on a few jointly prepared copies of the state by utilizing the partial coherent power of the quantum processor, and then estimate many nonlinear functions in a more efficient way, i.e., less demanding on both the quantum hardware and classical post-processing.

Swap test and RM
.—First, let us take a quick review of the swap test and the RM protocol for measuring
P_{m}
.
The moment can be written as
P_{m}=\tr(S_m\rho^{\otimes m})
, with
S_{m}
the shift operation on
\mathcal{H}_{d}^{\otimes m}
satisfying
S_{m}\ket{\mb{b}_1,\mb{b}_2\cdots,\mb{b}_m}=\ket{\mb{b}_2,\cdots\mb{b}_{m},\mb{b}_{1}}
, with each
\{\ket{\mb{b}}\}
the basis of each copy.
Using the swap test, one can measure
P_{m}
efficiently with complete coherent control over multiple copies of the state, as illustrated in Fig.
1
(a).
In particular, one initializes a control qubit and prepares the
m
-copy state
\rho^{\otimes m}
, and then conducts the Controlled-shift operation

\displaystyle\mathrm{CS}_{m}=\ket{0}_{c}\bra{0}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{d^{m}}+\ket{1}_{c}\bra{1}\otimes S_{m}.

(2)

Finally one measures the control qubit to get the value of
P_{m}

Ekert et al. 2002
;
Horodecki and Ekert 2002
. The corresponding quantum circuit requires a quantum processor with a total of
N=nm+1
qubits. Although the shift operation on
m
-copy can be expressed as a product of 2-copy swap operations, this approach would significantly increase the quantum circuit depth. Furthermore, preparing
m
copies of the state in parallel imposes stringent demands on quantum memories. Therefore, the generalized swap test presents significant challenges for cases where
m\geq 3
.

The RM toolbox
Elben et al. 2023
was recently developed to ease the experimental challenge mentioned above
Struchalin et al. 2021
;
Zhang et al. 2021
;
Yu et al. 2021a
. Compared with swap test, one only needs to control a single-copy state to realize the estimation. For shadow estimation
Huang et al. 2020
, independent snapshots of the state
\{\widehat{\rho}_{(i)}\}

can be constructed using data conllected in RMs, as shown in Fig.
1
(b).
This is referred to as the shadow set and has the property that the expectation
\mathbb{E}(\widehat{\rho}_{(i)})=\rho
. Denote the random unitary evolution sampled from some ensemble as
U\in\mathcal{E}
, and the
Z
-basis measurement result as
\mathbf{b}=\{b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{n}\}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\widehat{\rho}=\mathcal{M}^{-1}\left(U^{{\dagger}}\outerproduct{\widehat{\mb{b}}}{\widehat{\mb{b}}}U\right),

(3)

where
\widehat{\mathbf{b}}
is a random variable with probability
\bra{\mb{b}}U\rho U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}}
, and the inverse (classical) postprocessing channel
\mathcal{M}^{-1}
is determined by the chosen
\mathcal{E}

Huang et al. 2020
;
Hu and You 2022
;
Hu et al. 2021
;
Ohliger et al. 2013
;
Bu et al. 2022
. For instance,
\mathcal{E}
can be the global or local Clifford ensemble, denoted as Clifford and Pauli measurements hereafter, respectively.
After constructing the shadow set, the unbiased estimator of
P_{m}
can be constructed as

\displaystyle\tr(S_m\widehat{\rho}_{(1)}\otimes\widehat{\rho}_{(2)}\otimes\cdots\widehat{\rho}_{(m)}).

(4)

In principle, any nonlinear function can be obtained with only an
n
-qubit quantum processor using sequential RMs. However, the number of measurements needed generally scales exponentially with the qubit number, and the scaling becomes worse for larger
m
values, such as
m\geq 3

Huang et al. 2020
;
Elben et al. 2020a
.

Hybrid framework for nonlinear functions
.—By trading off the quantum and classical resources, here we develop a hybrid framework for nonlinear functions. All proofs and more detailed discussions are left in Ref.
sup
.

For a nonlinear function of degree
m
such as
P_{m}
, where
m=\sum_{i=1}^{L}m_{i}
, we demonstrate that it can be estimated using a quantum processor with only
N=n(\max_{i}m_{i})+1
qubits. The core idea is to conduct RM on
\rho^{t}
, where
t=m_{i}
, by leveraging the coherent operation on
t
copies of
\rho
. Instead of directly reading the control qubit outcome to obtain
P_{t}
, as in the swap test, we perform RM on one of the prepared
t
copies of
\rho
. As the permutation symmetry holds, the RM can be performed on any copy, like the final one shown in Fig.
1
(c). By measuring the expectation value of the Pauli-
X
operator on the control qubit and performing the projective measurement on the final copy, we obtain

\displaystyle\tr[X_{c}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{d}^{\otimes(t-1)}\otimes\ket{\mb{b}}\bra{\mb{b}}\ U\ \mathrm{CS}_{t}\ (\ket{+}_{c}\bra{+}\otimes\rho^{\otimes t})\ \mathrm{CS}_{t}^{{\dagger}}\ U^{{\dagger}}]

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big\{\tr[X_{c}\ket{1}_{c}\bra{0}]\tr[\bra{\mb{b}}US_{t}\rho^{\otimes t}\ U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}}]

\displaystyle+\tr[X_{c}\ket{0}_{c}\bra{1}]\tr[\bra{\mb{b}}U\rho^{\otimes t}S_{t}^{\dagger}\ U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}}]\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bra{\mb{b}}U\rho^{t}U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}}.

Here the identity operators
\mathbb{I}_{d}^{\otimes(t-1)}
are on the first
t-1
copies, and the projective measurement
\ket{\mb{b}}\bra{\mb{b}}
and random unitary
U
is on the final
t
-th copy, as shown in Fig.
2
.The result in the last line indicates that one can effectively conduct RM on
\rho^{t}
.

Application in quantum error mitigation
.—
Recently purified-based methods are proposed
Huggins et al. 2021
;
Koczor 2021
for quantum error mitigation

;
Endo et al. 2018
;
Kandala et al. 2019
;
Endo et al. 2021
The central task there is to estimate

. The original protocol is based on the swap test to measure

, and very recently there have been ones by shadow estimation

;
Hu et al. 2022
. There is also an experimental advance for that of
m=2

, however, similarly it is still challenging to extend both approaches to
m\geq 3
. Here we show that the hybrid framework gives various advantages on estimating

.

Suppose one has access to the coherent operation on
m
-copy quantum states. By only adopting the classical postprocessing, the shadow set collected in Algorithm
1
for

;
Neven et al. 2021
;

Proof.

We take the expectation value of the estimator on the random unitary
U

\mathbf{b},b_{c}
to get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\{U,\mathbf{b},b_{c}\}}\ \widehat{\rho^{t}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\{U,\mathbf{b},b_{c}\}}\ (-1)^{\widehat{b}_{c}}\cdot\mathcal{M}^{-1}\left(U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\widehat{\mb{b}}}\bra{\widehat{\mb{b}}}U\right)

(15)

\displaystyle=\sum_{U,\mathbf{b},b_{c}}\mathrm{Pr}(U,\mathbf{b},b_{c})\ (-1)^{b_{c}}\mathcal{M}^{-1}\left(U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}}\bra{\mb{b}}U\right)

with

Given
U
and
\mathbf{b}

as

\displaystyle\sum_{b_{c}}\mathrm{Pr}(\mathbf{b},b_{c}|U)\ (-1)^{b_{c}}=

\displaystyle\tr[\bra{+}_{c}\bra{\mb{b}}U\ \mathrm{CS}_{t}\left(\ket{+}_{c}\bra{+}\otimes\rho^{\otimes t}\right)\mathrm{CS}_{t}^{{\dagger}}\ U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}}\ket{+}_{c}]

(16)

\displaystyle-\tr[\bra{-}_{c}\bra{\mb{b}}U\ \mathrm{CS}_{t}\left(\ket{+}_{c}\bra{+}\otimes\rho^{\otimes t}\right)\mathrm{CS}_{t}^{{\dagger}}\ U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}}\ket{-}_{c}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tr[X_{c}\bra{\mb{b}}U\ \mathrm{CS}_{t}\left(\ket{+}_{c}\bra{+}\otimes\rho^{\otimes t}\right)\mathrm{CS}_{t}^{{\dagger}}\ U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bra{\mb{b}}U\rho^{t}U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}}.

where in the first line we insert the probability
\mathrm{Pr}(\mathbf{b},0/1|U)
accounting for the measurement result
0/1

X

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\{U,\mathbf{b},b_{c}\}}\ \widehat{\rho^{t}}=\sum_{U}\sum_{\mathbf{b}}\mathrm{Pr}(U)\bra{\mb{b}}U\rho^{t}U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}}\mathcal{M}^{-1}\left(U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}}\bra{\mb{b}}U\right)=\rho^{t}.

The equality holds due to the following fact: by taking

in the original shadow protocol

Second, we prove the result of variance in Eq. (7).

Proof.

By definition, the variance of
\tr(O\widehat{\rho^{t}})
is

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\left[\tr(O\widehat{\rho^{t}})\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\tr(O\widehat{\rho^{t}})^{2}\right]-\tr(O\rho^{t})^{2}

(18)

with the first term being

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\tr(O\widehat{\rho^{t}})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\ \tr\left[O(-1)^{\widehat{b}_{c}}\cdot\mathcal{M}^{-1}\left(U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\widehat{\mb{b}}}\bra{\widehat{\mb{b}}}U\right)\right]^{2}

(19)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\ (-1)^{2\widehat{b}_{c}}\bra{\widehat{\mb{b}}}U\mathcal{M}^{-1}(O)U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\widehat{\mb{b}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{U}\mathrm{Pr}(U)\sum_{\mathbf{b},b_{c}}\mathrm{Pr}(\mathbf{b},b_{c}|U)\ \bra{\mb{b}}U\mathcal{M}^{-1}(O)U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}}^{2},

where we insert the definition of
\widehat{\rho^{t}}
of Eq. (6) in main text in the first line and use the self-adjoint property of
\mathcal{M}^{-1}
to equivalently act it on
O
in the second line. For simplicity, we omit the subscript
\{U,\mathbf{b},b_{c}\}
in the expectation. The interesting point is that the sign with respective to
b_{c}
disappears due to the square. By first summing the index
b_{c}
, which implies taking a partial trace on the control qubit, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{b_{c}}\mathrm{Pr}(\mathbf{b},b_{c}|U)=

\displaystyle\tr[\bra{+}_{c}\bra{\mb{b}}U\ \mathrm{CS}_{t}\left(\ket{+}_{c}\bra{+}\otimes\rho^{\otimes t}\right)\mathrm{CS}_{t}^{{\dagger}}\ U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}}\ket{+}_{c}]+\tr[\bra{-}_{c}\bra{\mb{b}}U\ \mathrm{CS}_{t}\left(\ket{+}_{c}\bra{+}\otimes\rho^{\otimes t}\right)\mathrm{CS}_{t}^{{\dagger}}\ U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}}\ket{-}_{c}]

(20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tr[\mathbb{I}_{c}\bra{\mb{b}}U\ \mathrm{CS}_{t}\left(\ket{+}_{c}\bra{+}\otimes\rho^{\otimes t}\right)\mathrm{CS}_{t}^{{\dagger}}\ U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\tr[\bra{\mb{b}}U\ \mathrm{CS}_{t}\left(\ket{0}_{c}\bra{0}\otimes\rho^{\otimes t}\right)\mathrm{CS}_{t}^{{\dagger}}\ U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}}]+\frac{1}{2}\tr[\bra{\mb{b}}U\ \mathrm{CS}_{t}\left(\ket{1}_{c}\bra{1}\otimes\rho^{\otimes t}\right)\mathrm{CS}_{t}^{{\dagger}}\ U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\tr[\bra{\mb{b}}U\ \left(\ket{0}_{c}\bra{0}\otimes\rho^{\otimes t}\right)U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}}]+\frac{1}{2}\tr[\bra{\mb{b}}U\left(\ket{1}_{c}\bra{1}\otimes S_{t}\rho^{\otimes t}S_{t}^{{\dagger}}\right)U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bra{\mb{b}}U\rho U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}},

where in the third line only two terms corresponding to the diagonal terms
\ket{0}_{c}\bra{0}
and
\ket{1}_{c}\bra{1}
from
\ket{+}_{c}\bra{+}
survive due to the partial trace, in the last line we use
S_{t}\rho^{\otimes t}S_{t}^{{\dagger}}=\rho^{\otimes t}
. See Fig.
4
for a diagram representation of the derivation.

By inserting Eq. (
20
) into the last line of Eq. (
19
), one gets

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\tr(O\widehat{\rho^{t}})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{U}\mathrm{Pr}(U)\sum_{\mathbf{b},b_{c}}\mathrm{Pr}(\mathbf{b},b_{c}|U)\ \bra{\mb{b}}U\mathcal{M}^{-1}(O)U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}}^{2}

(21)

\displaystyle=\sum_{U}\sum_{\mathbf{b}}\mathrm{Pr}(U)\bra{\mb{b}}U\rho U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}}\bra{\mb{b}}U\mathcal{M}^{-1}(O)U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{U}\sum_{\mathbf{b}}\mathrm{Pr}(U)\mathrm{Pr}(\mathbf{b}|U)_{(\rho)}\tr[O\ \mathcal{M}^{-1}(U^{{\dagger}}\ket{\mb{b}}\bra{\mb{b}}U)]^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\tr(O\widehat{\rho})^{2}\right].

The last line shows that the expectation value just equals the one in the original shadow protocol on a single-copy of the state
\rho
, no matter what value
t
takes.

As a result,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\left[\tr(O\widehat{\rho^{t}})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\tr(O\widehat{\rho})^{2}\right]-\tr(O\rho^{t})^{2}

(22)

Note that
\mathbb{E}\ \tr(O\widehat{\rho})^{2}
has already been analysed by maximizing it for all possible
\rho
, which serves as an upper bound denoted by the square of the shadow norm
\|O\|_{\mathrm{shadow}}

Huang et al. 2020
.
Consequently, by ignoring the second term
\tr(O\rho^{t})^{2}
, one has the upper bound for the variance for any
t
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\left[\tr(O\widehat{\rho^{t}})\right]\leq\|O\|^{2}_{\mathrm{shadow}}.

(23)

In addition, since shifting the operator to its traceless part
O_{0}=O-\tr(O)\mathbb{I}/d
does not change the variance for
\mathrm{Var}[\tr(O\widehat{\rho})]
, one could further bound Eq. (
22
) as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\left[\tr(O\widehat{\rho^{t}})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Var}[\tr(O\widehat{\rho})]+\tr(O\rho)^{2}-\tr(O\rho^{t})^{2}

(24)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Var}\left[\tr(O_0\widehat{\rho})\right]+\tr(O\rho)^{2}-\tr(O\rho^{t})^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\ \tr(O_0\widehat{\rho})^{2}-\tr(O_0\rho)^{2}+\tr(O\rho)^{2}-\tr(O\rho^{t})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|O_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{shadow}}+\tr(O\rho)^{2}.

∎

We remark that this reduction of variance of
\widehat{\rho^{t}}
to the
t=1
case is since one has the ability to coherently control
t
-copy of the state.
The detailed expression of
\|O\|_{\mathrm{shadow}}
actually depends on the RM primitives. We show in latter section that by using Eq. (
24
) and the results of shadow norm, one can further give upper bounds for variance of more complex estimators, which are combined from a few of
\widehat{\rho^{t}}
.

A.2
Unbiased estimator for general
M
and

In Algorithm 1 in main text, one samples
M
independent
U\in\mathcal{H}_{d}
from some unitary ensemble
\mathcal{E}
, and for a given
U
, i.e., the measurement setting, one repeats the quantum circuit in Fig. 1 (c) in main text for
K
shots to collect the measurement results
b_{c}^{(i,j)}
and
\mathbf{b}^{(i,j)}
. Here the superscript
i
labels the measurement setting, and
j
labels the shot. We list the estimator in the following proposition.

Proposition 3
.

Following the procedure in Algorithm 1 in main text, one can collect
M
independent estimators of
\rho^{t}
, i.e.,
\left\{\widehat{\rho^{t}}_{(1)},\widehat{\rho^{t}}_{(2)},\cdots\widehat{\rho^{t}}_{(M)}\right\}
with

\displaystyle\widehat{\rho^{t}}_{(i)}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{j}\widehat{\rho^{t}}^{(j)}_{(i)}.

(25)

Here each
\widehat{\rho^{t}}^{(j)}_{(i)}
is constructed according to Eq. (6) in Theorem 1 by the
j
-th measurement result
b_{c}^{(i,j)}
and
\mathbf{b}^{(i,j)}
under
i
-th measurement setting. To estimate the value of
\tr(O\rho^t)
, one can further average total
M
rounds to get the unbiased estimator

\displaystyle\tr(O \frac{\sum_i \widehat{\rho^t}_{(i)}}{M}).

(26)

Proof.

In Appendix
A.1
, we have proved the result in Theorem 1 that
\mathbb{E}_{\{U,\mathbf{b},b_{c}\}}\ \widehat{\rho^{t}}^{(j)}_{(i)}=\rho^{t}
for the single-shot case, that is, for any
i,j
. Thus the estimators here directly hold since they only involve average operations.
∎

To evaluate the variance of the estimator in Eq. (
26
). Note that
\left\{\widehat{\rho^{t}}_{(1)},\widehat{\rho^{t}}_{(2)},\cdots\widehat{\rho^{t}}_{(M)}\right\}
are
independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables. As a result,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\left[\tr(O \sum_i \widehat{\rho^t}_{(i)}/M)\right]=\frac{1}{M}\mathrm{Var}\left[\tr(O \widehat{\rho^t}_{(i_0)})\right]

(27)

for any
i_{0}
.

As a result, for the general
M
and
K=1
(which is the case considered in the original shadow estimation
Huang et al. 2020
), one can directly use the single-shot result in Eq. (7) in main text to bound the variance by dividing it by
M
. We leave the variance for general
K
to further study. Hereafter, our discussion will mainly focus on the single-shot scenario with shadow-type postprocessing.

Appendix B
Hybrid framework for general nonlinear functions

In this section, we show that our hybrid framework can work for more general nonlinear functions in the following form,

\displaystyle F_{m}(\{\rho_{i}\},\{O_{i}\})=\tr(\rho_1 O_1 \rho_2O_2 \cdots\rho_mO_m ),

(28)

for quantum states
\{\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\cdots\rho_{m}\}
with some observables
\{O_{1},O_{2}\cdots O_{m}\}
. We call it a
m
-degree function. Here for simplicity we assume that
O_{i}
is both Hermitian and unitary (e.g., Pauli operators of practical interest) in the following discussion.

B.1
Swap test and RM for
F_{m}

Like in main text, let us first briefly recast the generalized swap test and RM to get
F_{m}(\{\rho_{i}\},\{O_{i}\})
, respectively.

The quantum circuit of the swap test to measure

28
). Compared to the circuit in Fig. 1 (a), one additionally adds
\mathrm{\mathrm{CO_{i}}}
operations. The unitary
U_{c}
on the control qubit before the projective measurement determines the measurement basis. For the
X
-basis,

In the swap test, one prepares the quantum state
\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\rho_{i}
in parallel and initializes the control qubit, and further operates Controlled-
O_{i}
gate, denoted as
\mathrm{\mathrm{CO_{i}}}

\rho_{i}
after the Controlled-shift operation

in the quantum circuit, as shown in Fig.
5
. Measuring the expectation value of control qubit using Pauli-
X

\displaystyle\tr[X_{c}\ \prod_{i}\mathrm{CO_{i}}\ \mathrm{CS}_{m}\left(\ket{+}_{c}\bra{+}\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\rho_{i}\right)\mathrm{CS}_{m}^{{\dagger}}\ \prod_{i}\mathrm{CO_{i}}]

(29)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\tr[X_{c}\ket{1}_{c}\bra{0}]*\tr[\left(\bigotimes_{i}O_{i}\right)S_{m}\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\rho_{i}\right)]+\frac{1}{2}\tr[X_{c}\ket{0}_{c}\bra{1}]*\tr[\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\rho_{i}\right)S_{t}^{\dagger}\left(\bigotimes_{i}O_{i}\right)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\tr[\rho_{1}O_{1}\rho_{2}O_{2}\cdots\rho_{m}O_{m}]+\frac{1}{2}\tr[O_{m}\rho_{m}\cdots O_{2}\rho_{2}O_{1}\rho_{1}]=\frac{1}{2}(F_{m}+F_{m}^{*}),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\tr[Y_{c}\ket{1}_{c}\bra{0}]*\tr[\left(\bigotimes_{i}O_{i}\right)S_{m}\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\rho_{i}\right)]+\frac{1}{2}\tr[Y_{c}\ket{0}_{c}\bra{1}]*\tr[\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\rho_{i}\right)S_{m}^{\dagger}\left(\bigotimes_{i}O_{i}\right)]=\frac{-i}{2}(F_{m}-F_{m}^{*}).

By repeating the measurements and combining the Pauli-
X
and
Y

F_{m}
.

For the RM with shadow estimation, one can construct the following unbiased estimator,

\displaystyle\tr(\bigotimes_{i=1}^m O_iS_m\ \widehat{\rho_1}\otimes\widehat{\rho_2}\otimes\cdots\widehat{\rho_m})=\tr(\widehat{\rho_1}O_1\widehat{\rho_2}O_2\cdots\widehat{\rho_m}O_m),

(31)

where

is the snapshot for
\rho_{i}
based on the shadow protocol as shown in Eq. (3) in main text. And one can further increase the shadow size to reduce the statistical fluctuation of the estimator.

As mentioned in main text, both methods are challenging to measure
F_{m}
for a large degree

C.2
The variance of the estimators

In this subsection, we give the statistical analysis of the estimators of moments, by applying Eq. (7) in Theorem 1 , and also the properties of shadow norm. Fist, let us list the previous results on the upper bound of the shadow norm and variance, which are helpful for the later discussion.

Fact 1
.

In the shadow estimation
Huang et al. 2020
, for a single-shot estimator
\widehat{\rho}
of the quantum state
\rho\in\mathcal{H}_{d}
, and an observable
O
with its traceless part
O_{0}
, the shadow norms of the Clifford and Pauli measurement primitives are respectively upper bounded by

\displaystyle\|O_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{shadow}(c)}\leq 3\tr(O^2)\ (Proposition\ S1);

(49)

\displaystyle\|O_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{shadow}(p)}\leq 2^{\mathrm{supp}(O)}\|\tilde{O}\|_{2}^{2}\ (Proposition\ S3).

Here in the second bound for Pauli measurements we assume
O=\tilde{O}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{2^{n-k}}
with
\tilde{O}
restricted on the actively supporting
k
qubits, and thus
\mathrm{supp}(O)=k
.

For the nonlinear function estimation, suppose
\widehat{\rho}_{1}
and
\widehat{\rho}_{2}
are two distinct estimators and
O
is some operator on
\mathcal{H}_{d}^{\otimes 2}
, the variance with Clifford measurements can be upper bounded by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}[\tr(O\widehat{\rho}_1\otimes\widehat{\rho}_2)]\leq 9\tr(O^2)+\frac{6}{d}\|O\|^{2}_{\infty}\ (Lemma~S6).

(50)

For Pauli measurements with
O=S_{2}
, the variance has the upper bound (Lemma 1
Garcia et al. 2021
)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}[\tr(S_2\widehat{\rho}_1\otimes\widehat{\rho}_2)]\leq d^{3}.

(51)

We first give the proof of the variance for
\widehat{P_{3}}
in Eq. 12 in Proposition 1.

Proof.

Recall that in Eq. (11) the unbiased estimator of
P_{3}

\displaystyle\widehat{P_{3}}=\frac{1}{M_{1}M_{2}}\sum_{i\in[M_{1}],j\in[M_{2}]}\tr(S_2\widehat{\rho^{2}}_{i}\otimes\widehat{\rho}_{j}),

where
\widehat{\rho^{2}}_{i}
and
\widehat{\rho}_{j}
come from
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
independent snapshots. By definition, the variance shows

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\left(\widehat{P_{3}}\right)=\frac{1}{(M_{1}M_{2})^{2}}\sum_{i\in[M_{1}],j\in[M_{2}]}\sum_{i^{\prime}\in[M_{1}],j^{\prime}\in[M_{2}]}\mathbb{E}\left[\tr(S_2\widehat{\rho^{2}}_i\otimes\widehat{\rho}_{j})\tr(S_2\widehat{\rho^{2}}_{i'}\otimes\widehat{\rho}_{j'})\right]-P_{3}^{2}

If there is no index-coincidence, say

and

. Thus one only needs to consider the following cases with the coincidence.

One coincidence with

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\tr(S_2\widehat{\rho^{2}}_i\otimes\widehat{\rho}_{j})\tr(S_2\widehat{\rho^{2}}_{i}\otimes\widehat{\rho}_{j'})\right]-P_{3}^{2}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\tr(S_2\widehat{\rho^{2}}_i\otimes\rho)\tr(S_2\widehat{\rho^{2}}_i\otimes\rho)\right]-P_{3}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\tr(\rho\widehat{\rho^{2}}_i)^{2}\right]-P_{3}^{2}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\left[\tr(\rho\widehat{\rho^{2}})\right]\leq\|\rho-\mathbb{I}/d\|_{\mathrm{shadow}}^{2}+\tr(\rho^2)^{2}.

Here in the final line we omit the subscript
i
, and the result is just the variance of
O=\rho
on the estimator
\widehat{\rho^{2}}
for any
i
. The final inequality is due to Eq. (7) in Theorem 1 .

One coincidence

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\tr(S_2\widehat{\rho^{2}}_{i}\otimes\widehat{\rho}_{j})\tr(S_2\widehat{\rho^{2}}_{i'}\otimes\widehat{\rho}_{j})\right]-P_{3}^{2}

(54)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\tr(S_2\rho^{2}\otimes\widehat{\rho}_{j})\tr(S_2\rho^{2}\otimes\widehat{\rho}_{j})\right]-P_{3}^{2}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\left[\tr(\rho^2 \widehat{\rho})\right]\leq\|\rho^{2}-\tr(\rho^2)\mathbb{I}/d\|_{\mathrm{shadow}}^{2}.

The result is just the variance of
O=\rho^{2}
on the estimator
\widehat{\rho}
for any

Two coincidences
i=i^{\prime}
and
j=j^{\prime}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\tr(S_2\widehat{\rho^{2}}_i\otimes\widehat{\rho}_{j})\tr(S_2\widehat{\rho^{2}}_{i}\otimes\widehat{\rho}_{j})\right]-P_{3}^{2}

(55)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\left[\tr(S_2\widehat{\rho^{2}}\otimes\widehat{\rho})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tr(\rho^{2})^{2}\mathrm{Var}\left[\tr(S_2 \widehat{\sigma} \otimes\widehat{\rho})\right].

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(\widehat{P_{3}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(M_{1}M_{2})^{2}}\left\{M_{1}\binom{M_{2}}{2}\mathrm{Var}\left[\tr(\rho\widehat{\rho^{2}})\right]+M_{2}\binom{M_{1}}{2}\mathrm{Var}\left[\tr(\rho^2 \widehat{\rho})\right]+M_{1}M_{2}\mathrm{Var}\left[\tr(S_2\widehat{\rho^{2}}\otimes\widehat{\rho})\right]\right\},

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\rho-\mathbb{I}/d\|_{\mathrm{shadow}}^{2}+\tr(\rho^2)^{2}}{2M_{1}}+\frac{\|\rho^{2}-\mathbb{I}\tr(\rho^2)/d\|_{\mathrm{shadow}}^{2}}{2M_{2}}+\frac{\tr(\rho^{2})^{2}\mathrm{Var}\left[\tr(S_2 \widehat{\sigma} \otimes\widehat{\rho})\right]}{M_{1}M_{2}},

By directly applying Chebyshev’s inequality, one gets that

is sufficient to let the estimation error less than

∎

D.1

Abstract
. Let X 1 , … , X n X_{1},\ldots,X_{n} be independent identically distributed random variables. In this paper we study the behavior of concentration functions of weighted sums ∑ k = 1 n X k ​ a k \sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{k}a_{k} with respect to the arithmetic structure of coefficients a k a_{k} in the context of the Littlewood–Offord problem. In our recent papers, we discussed the relations between the inverse principles stated by Nguyen, Tao and Vu and similar principles formulated by Arak in his papers from the 1980’s. In this paper, we will derive some improved (more general and more precise) consequences of Arak’s inequalities applying our new bound in the Littlewood–Offord problem. Moreover, we also obtain an improvement of the estimates used in Rudelson and Vershynin’s least common denominator method.

Let

For

We define the size of

In fact,

is the image of an integer box

We say that

\hbox{size}(P)=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\big(2\,\lfloor L_{j}\rfloor+1\big).

For non-proper GAPs, we have, of course,

In the case

the vectors

Estimating the concentration functions
Q(F_{a},\tau)
in the Littlewood–Offord
problem, it is possible to reduce the problem to the estimation of
concentration functions of some symmetric infinitely divisible

Introduce the distribution

H

(10)

\widehat{H}(t)=\exp\Big(-\frac{\,1\,}{2}\;\sum_{k=1}^{n}\big(1-\cos\left\langle\,t,a_{k}\right\rangle\big)\Big),\quad t\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

It is clear that

Let
\widetilde{X}=X_{1}-X_{2}
be the symmetrized random variable

Lemma 2
.

For any
\varkappa,\tau>0

(12)

Q(F_{a},\tau)\ll_{d}Q(H^{p(\tau/\varkappa)},\varkappa).

Lemma

was the main tool in our recent applications of Arak’s inequalities to the
Littlewood–Offord problem (see
[

]
and
[

]

We should note that

, is a symmetric infinitely divisible
distribution with the Lévy
spectral measure
\frac{\,nb\,}{2}\;M^{*}

(13)

M^{*}=\frac{\,1\,}{2n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\big(E_{a_{k}}+E_{-a_{k}}\big).

In fact,

H^{b}=e\Big(\frac{\,nb\,}{2}\;M^{*}\Big).

Arak
[

has found connections of values of the

The proof of Lemma

is given

According to (

Corollary 2
.

For any

Lemma 3

For any

Inverse principles

In
[

A few years ago, Tao and Vu

[
28
]
formulated in the discrete case (with

\tau=0)
the so-called ”inverse principle” in the Littlewood–Offord problem, stating that

(26)

\hbox{\it A set $a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})$ with large small
ball probability}\\
\hbox{\it must have strong additive structure.}

Here
“large small ball probability” means that

Q(F_{a},0)=\max_{x}{\mathbf{P}}\{S_{a}=x\}\geq n^{-A}

with some constant

A>0
. “Strong additive structure” means that a large part of
vectors
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
belong to a GAP of bounded size.

Nguyen and Vu
[

t\in\mathbf{R}^{d}

(47)

t\,\cdot\,a=(\left\langle\,t,a_{1}\right\rangle,\ldots,\left\langle\,t,a_{n}\right\rangle)\in\mathbf{R}^{n}.

Thus,

\left\|t\cdot a\right\|^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\langle\,t,a_{k}\right\rangle^{2}=\left\langle\mathbb{A}t,t\right\rangle.

Theorem 19
.

Let
\alpha,b,D>0
,
0<\gamma<1
.
Let the distribution
H
be defined in (

Assume that

(49)

\begin{split}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\big(\left\langle\,t,a_{k}\right\rangle-m_{k}\big)^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\geq\min\big\{\gamma\left\|t\cdot a\right\|,\alpha\big\}\hbox{ for all }m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\in\mathbf{Z},\ \\
\hbox{and for all }t\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\hbox{ such that }\left\|t\right\|\leq D.\end{split}

Then

Q(H^{b},{1}/{D})\ll_{d}\Big(\frac{1}{\gamma D\sqrt{b}}\Big)^{d}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det{\mathbb{A}}}}+\exp(-4b\alpha^{2}).

It is clear that if

0<D\leq D(a)=D_{\gamma,\alpha}(a)\\
=\inf\Big\{\theta>0:\hbox{dist}(t\,\cdot\,a,\mathbf{Z}^{n})<\min\big\{\gamma\left\|t\cdot a\right\|,\alpha\big\}\\
\hbox{ for some }t\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\hbox{ such that }\|t\|=\theta\Big\},

where

Lemma 4
.

Let
\tau>0
and let

F
be a
d
-dimensional probability distribution. Then

(52)

Q(F,\tau)\ll_{d}\tau^{d}\int_{\left\|t\right\|\leq 1/\tau}|\widehat{F}(t)|\,dt.

Lemma
4
says that
Q(F,\tau)
is estimated by the mean value of
|\widehat{F}(t)|
on the ball
\{t\in\mathbf{R}^{d}:\left\|t\right\|\leq 1/\tau\}
.

Proof of Theorem
19
.
Using Lemma
4
and relation (
3
), we get

\displaystyle Q(H^{b},1/D)

\displaystyle\ll_{d}

\displaystyle D^{-d}\int_{\left\|t\right\|\leq 2\pi D}|\widehat{H}(t)|^{b}\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D^{-d}\int_{\left\|t\right\|\leq 2\pi D}\exp\Big(-\frac{\,b\,}{2}\;\sum_{k=1}^{n}\big(1-\cos\left\langle\,t,a_{k}\right\rangle\big)\Big)\,dt

.

It is easy to show
that
1-\cos x\geq 2x^{2}/\pi^{2}
, for
{|x|\leq\pi}
. For
arbitrary
x
, this implies that

1-\cos x\geq 2\,\pi^{-2}\min_{m\in\mathbf{Z}}|\,x-2\pi m|^{2}.

Substituting this
inequality into (
10
), we obtain

(53)

\displaystyle\widehat{H}(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\Big(-\cfrac{1}{\pi^{2}}\;\sum_{k=1}^{n}\min_{m_{k}\in\mathbf{Z}}\big|\left\langle\,t,a_{k}\right\rangle-2\pi m_{k}\big|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\Big(-\cfrac{1}{\pi^{2}}\;\big(\hbox{dist}(t\cdot a,2\pi\mathbf{Z}^{n})\big)^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\Big(-4\;\big(\hbox{dist}(t/2\pi\cdot a,\mathbf{Z}^{n})\big)^{2}\Big).

Hence, for
\left\|t\right\|\leq 2\pi D
, we have

(54)

\displaystyle\widehat{H}(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp\Big(-4\;\big(\min\big\{\gamma\left\|t/2\pi\cdot a\right\|,\alpha\big\}\big)^{2}\Big).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle Q(H^{b},1/D)

\displaystyle\ll_{d}

\displaystyle D^{-d}\int_{\left\|t\right\|\leq 2\pi D}\exp\Big(-4\,b\gamma^{2}\;\left\|t/2\pi\cdot a\right\|^{2}\Big)\,dt

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\qquad+\ D^{-d}\int_{\left\|t\right\|\leq 2\pi D}\exp\big(-4\,b\alpha^{2}\big)\,dt

\displaystyle\ll_{d}

\displaystyle D^{-d}\int_{\left\|t\right\|\leq 2\pi D}\exp\Big(-b\gamma^{2}\pi^{-2}\;\left\langle\mathbb{A}t,t\right\rangle\Big)\,dt

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\qquad+\ \exp\big(-4\,b\alpha^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\ll_{d}

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{\gamma D\sqrt{b}}\Big)^{d}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det{\mathbb{A}}}}+\exp(-4b\alpha^{2}),

proving Theorem
19
.
\square

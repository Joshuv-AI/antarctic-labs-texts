Left invariant Riemannian metrics with harmonic curvature are Ricci-parallel in solvable Lie groups and Lie groups of dimension ≤ 6 \leq 6
Ilyes Aberaouze, Mohamed Boucetta

Abstract
We show that any left invariant metric with harmonic curvature on a solvable Lie group is Ricci-parallel. We show the same result for any Lie group of dimension ≤ \leq 6.

Introduction

A Riemannian manifold
(M,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is said to have
harmonic curvature
if its curvature tensor
R
has a vanishing divergence, i.e., for any
X\in\Gamma(TM)
,

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla_{E_{i}}(R)(E_{i},X)=0,

where
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection and
(E_{1},\ldots,E_{n})
is a local
\langle\;,\;\rangle
-orthonormal frame field.
The study of Riemannian manifolds with harmonic curvature is old and goes to
[
10
]
and
it is known
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
8
]
that a Riemannian manifold
(M,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
has a harmonic curvature if and only if its Ricci tensor
\mathrm{Ric}
is Codazzi, i.e.,

\nabla_{X}(\mathrm{Ric})(Y,Z)=\nabla_{Y}(\mathrm{Ric})(X,Z)

(1)

for any vector fields
X,Y,Z
. Obviously, any Einstein Riemannian metric
(\mathrm{Ric}=\lambda\langle\;,\;\rangle)
and more generally any Ricci-parallel metric
(\nabla(\mathrm{Ric})=0)
satisfy (
1
). Thus the interesting case will be where
\mathrm{Ric}
satisfies (
1
) and
\nabla(\mathrm{Ric})\not=0
. Unfortunately, such metrics are difficult to find and some examples were given in
[
2
,
4
,
5
,
8
]
. To our knowledge, there is no example of a non Ricci-parallel homogeneous Riemannian manifold with harmonic curvature which supports the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1
.

Any homogeneous Riemannian manifold
M
with harmonic curvature is Ricci-parallel.

This conjecture is true in dimension four (see
[
7
]
) and when
M
is a sphere or a projective space (see
[
12
]
). It was proven in
[
1
]
for nilpotent Lie groups with left invariant metrics. Moreover, any conformally flat Riemannian manifold with constant scalar curvature satisfies (
1
) (see
[
8
, Theorem 5.1]
) and any homogeneous conformally flat Riemannian manifold has Ricci-parallel curvature (see
[
13
]
). The purpose of this paper is to show that this conjecture is true when
M
is a solvable Lie group endowed with a left invariant metric which implies that the conjecture is true for any homogeneous Riemannian manifold
G/K
where
K
is a maximal compact subgroup of
G
. We show also that the conjecture is true for any Lie group of dimension
\leq 6
endowed with a left invariant metric.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some basic properties of the Lie algebra associated to a Lie group endowed with a left invariant metric with harmonic curvature. In Section
3
, we prove Conjecture
1
for a solvable Lie group. This is based on a generalization of the main result of Lauret in
[
9
]
. In Section
4
, we prove Conjecture
1
for any Lie group of dimension
\leq 6
. The proof for the dimensions
\leq 5
is quite easy but, for dimension 6, it involves a huge computation where the use of Maple is needed.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we establish the basic tools needed for the proof of our main results.
The results of this section were first proved in
[
1
]
.

The study of left invariant Riemannian metrics on Lie groups reduces to the study of Lie algebras endowed with a Euclidean product. Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a Euclidean Lie algebra, i.e., a Lie algebra endowed with a bilinear nondegenerate form which is positive definite. The
Levi-Civita product
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is the bilinear map
\mathrm{L}:{\mathfrak{g}}\times{\mathfrak{g}}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
given by Koszul’s
formula

\displaystyle 2\langle\mathrm{L}_{u}v,w\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle[u,v],w\rangle+\langle[w,u],v\rangle+\langle[w,v],u\rangle\quad\mbox{for any}\;u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}.

(2)

For any
u,v\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,
\mathrm{L}_{u}:{\mathfrak{g}}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
is skew-symmetric and
[u,v]=\mathrm{L}_{u}v-\mathrm{L}_{v}u
.
The curvature of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is given by

K(u,v)=\mathrm{L}_{[u,v]}-[\mathrm{L}_{u},\mathrm{L}_{v}]\quad\mbox{for any}\;u,v,\in\mathfrak{g}.

The Ricci curvature
\mathrm{ric}:{\mathfrak{g}}\times{\mathfrak{g}}\longrightarrow\hbox{\bb R}
and its Ricci operator
\mathrm{Ric}:{\mathfrak{g}}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
are defined by

\langle\mathrm{Ric}(u),v\rangle=\mathrm{ric}(u,v)=\mathrm{tr}\left(w\longrightarrow K(u,w)v\right)\quad\mbox{for any}\;u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}.

The operator
\mathrm{Ric}
is symmetric and hence is diagonalizable. Thus there exists
\lambda_{1}<\ldots<\lambda_{r}
such that

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{r}

where
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
is the eigenspace of
\mathrm{Ric}
associated to the eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
.

We call
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
a Euclidean Lie algebra with harmonic curvature if
\mathrm{Ric}
is a Codazzi operator, i.e., for any
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\langle\mathrm{L}_{u}v,\mathrm{Ric}(w)\rangle+\langle\mathrm{L}_{u}w,\mathrm{Ric}(v)\rangle=\langle\mathrm{L}_{v}u,\mathrm{Ric}(w)\rangle+\langle\mathrm{L}_{v}w,\mathrm{Ric}(u)\rangle.

(3)

If we take
u\in\mathfrak{g}_{i}
,
v\in\mathfrak{g}_{j}
and
w\in\mathfrak{g}_{k}
, we get

(2\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})\langle[u,v],w\rangle=(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})(\langle[v,w],u\rangle+\langle[u,w],v\rangle).

(4)

For
i=j\not=k
, we get that
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
is a subalgebra and if we take
k=i\not=j
, we get

\langle[v,u],w\rangle+\langle[v,w],u\rangle=0\quad u,w\in\mathfrak{g}_{i},v\in\mathfrak{g}_{j},i\not=j.

For any
i<j<k
and
u_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}_{i},u_{j}\in\mathfrak{g}_{j},u_{k}\in\mathfrak{g}_{k}
, we have

\begin{cases}(2\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})\langle[u_{j},u_{k}],u_{i}\rangle-(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})(\langle[u_{k},u_{i}],u_{j}\rangle+\langle[u_{j},u_{i}],u_{k}\rangle)=0,\quad(E1)\\
(2\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{k})\langle[u_{i},u_{k}],u_{j}\rangle-(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{k})(\langle[u_{k},u_{j}],u_{i}\rangle+\langle[u_{i},u_{j}],u_{k}\rangle)=0,\quad(E2)\\
(2\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})\langle[u_{i},u_{j}],u_{k}\rangle-(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})(\langle[u_{j},u_{k}],u_{i}\rangle+\langle[u_{i},u_{k}],u_{j}\rangle)=0.\quad(E3)\end{cases}

(5)

We have

(E1)-(E2)+(E3)=0

and if we put

x=\langle[u_{k},u_{j}],u_{i}\rangle,\;y=\langle[u_{k},u_{i}],u_{j}\rangle\quad\mbox{and}\quad z=\langle[u_{i},u_{j}],u_{k}\rangle,

then the system (
5
) becomes

\begin{cases}(\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{k}-2\lambda_{i})x+(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j})y=(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j})z,\\
(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{i})x+(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{k}-2\lambda_{j})y=(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{k})z.\end{cases}

So

x=-{\frac{\left(\lambda_{{j}}-\lambda_{{k}}\right)^{2}z}{\left(\lambda_{{i}}-\lambda_{{j}}\right)^{2}}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad y={\frac{\left(\lambda_{{i}}-\lambda_{{k}}\right)^{2}z}{\left(\lambda_{{i}}-\lambda_{{j}}\right)^{2}}}.

Thus

(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}\langle[u_{j},u_{k}],u_{i}\rangle+\left(\lambda_{{j}}-\lambda_{{k}}\right)^{2}\langle[u_{j},u_{i}],u_{k}\rangle=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left(\lambda_{{i}}-\lambda_{{j}}\right)^{2}\langle[u_{i},u_{k}],u_{j}\rangle+\left(\lambda_{{i}}-\lambda_{{k}}\right)^{2}\langle[u_{i},u_{j}],u_{k}\rangle=0.

So far, we proved the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a Euclidean Lie algebra,
\lambda_{1}<\ldots<\lambda_{r}
the eigenvalues of
\;\mathrm{Ric}
and
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{r}
is the splitting of
\mathfrak{g}
on eigenspaces of
\mathrm{Ric}
. Then
\mathrm{Ric}
is a Codazzi tensor if and only if:

1.

for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
,
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
,

2.

for any
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
with
i\not=j
and for any
u,v\in\mathfrak{g}_{i}
and
w\in\mathfrak{g}_{j}
then

\langle[w,u],v\rangle+\langle[w,v],u\rangle=0,

(6)

3.

for any
i<j<k
for any
u\in\mathfrak{g}_{i},v\in\mathfrak{g}_{j},w\in\mathfrak{g}_{k}
then

\left(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\right)^{2}\langle[v,w],u\rangle+\left(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k}\right)^{2}\langle[v,u],w\rangle=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\right)^{2}\langle[u,w],v\rangle+\left(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{k}\right)^{2}\langle[u,v],w\rangle=0.

(7)

In this case,
\mathrm{Ric}
is non parallel if and only if there exists a triple
(i,j,k)
with
i<j<k
and
u_{0}\in\mathfrak{g}_{i}
,
v_{0}\in\mathfrak{g}_{j}
and
w_{0}\in\mathfrak{g}_{k}
such that

\langle[u_{0},v_{0}],w_{0}\rangle\not=0
.

Let us give an interpretation of assertion 3. in the theorem. For any
u\in\mathfrak{g}
, denote by
u_{i}
its component in
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
. Fix an index
k
and define a new product
\langle\;,\;\rangle_{k}
on
\mathfrak{g}
by

\langle u,v\rangle_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}(\delta_{jk}+(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})^{2})\langle u_{j},v_{j}\rangle,\quad u,v\in\mathfrak{g}.

Denote by
\mathfrak{g}_{k}^{\perp}
the orthogonal of
\mathfrak{g}_{k}
. It is easy to check that, for any
u_{k}\in\mathfrak{g}_{k}
and for any
u,v\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\langle[u_{k},v],w\rangle_{k}+\langle[u_{k},w],v\rangle_{k}=0

(8)

and

\rho_{k}:\mathfrak{g}_{k}\longrightarrow\mathrm{so}(\mathfrak{g}_{k}^{\perp},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{k})\quad\mbox{where}\quad\rho_{k}(u_{k})(v)=\sum_{l\not=k}[u_{k},v]_{l},

(9)

is a representation. The proof of the following proposition is given in
[
1
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a Euclidean Lie algebra whose Ricci operator is Codazzi. With the notations above, for any
i=1,\ldots,r
, let
\{V_{i,\alpha}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
. Then, for any
i=1,\ldots,r
,
u_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}_{i}
,

{\mathrm{ric}}(u_{i},u_{i})={\mathrm{ric}}_{i}(u_{i},u_{i})-\sum_{j\not=k}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\frac{(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{i})(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i})}{(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j})^{2}}\langle[V_{j,\alpha},V_{k,\beta}],u_{i}\rangle^{2},

(10)

where
{\mathrm{ric}}_{i}
is the Ricci curvature of
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
endowed with the restriction of
\langle\;,\;\rangle
. Moreover, the scalar curvature
{\mathfrak{s}}
of
\mathfrak{g}
satisfies

{\mathfrak{s}}={\mathfrak{s}}_{1}+\ldots+{\mathfrak{s}}_{r},

(11)

where
{\mathfrak{s}}_{i}
is the scalar curvature of
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
.

3
Left invariant Riemannian metrics with harmonic curvature on solvable Lie groups are Ricci-parallel

The proof of our main result is based on the following theorem which is a generalization of the famous theorem of Lauret
[
9
]
which shows that any solvable Einstein Euclidean Lie algebra is standard in the sense that the orthogonal of its derived ideal is abelian. Actually, the proof of Lauret’s theorem is still valid if we suppose that
\mathrm{Ric}_{|[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]}=c\mathrm{Id}_{[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]}
instead of
\mathrm{Ric}=c\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
. To convince the reader we copy Lauret’s proof and we give the minor modifications needed to adapt to our hypothesis.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a solvable Lie algebra such that there exists a constant
c
such that
\mathrm{Ric}_{|[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]}=c\mathrm{Id}_{[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]}
. Then
\mathfrak{g}
is standard, i.e.,
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]^{\perp}
is abelian.

Proof.

Put
{\mathfrak{n}}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
and
\mathfrak{a}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]^{\perp}
. We have

\mathrm{Ric}=R-\frac{1}{2}B-S({\mathrm{ad}}_{H})

where
S({\mathrm{ad}}_{H})=\frac{1}{2}({\mathrm{ad}}_{H}+{\mathrm{ad}}_{H}^{*})
,
\langle H,u\rangle={\mathrm{tr}}({\mathrm{ad}}_{u})
,
\langle Bu,v\rangle={\mathrm{tr}}({\mathrm{ad}}_{u}\circ{\mathrm{ad}}_{v})
and

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\langle Ru,v\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}\langle[u,e_{i}],e_{j}\rangle\langle[v,e_{i}],e_{j}\rangle+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j}\langle[e_{i},e_{j}],u\rangle\langle[e_{i},e_{j}],v\rangle,\\
\displaystyle{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathrm{Ric}+\frac{1}{2}B+S({\mathrm{ad}}_{H}))E=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j}\langle E([e_{i},e_{j}])-[E(e_{i}),e_{j}]-[e_{i},E(e_{j})],[e_{i},e_{j}]\rangle,\end{cases}

(12)

for any endomorphism
E
of
\mathfrak{g}
and where
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{g}
. Since
\mathrm{Ric}
leaves
{\mathfrak{n}}
invariant it leaves
\mathfrak{a}
invariant and
for any,
s\in\mathfrak{a}
,

\displaystyle{\mathrm{ric}}(s,s)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}\langle[s,e_{i}],e_{j}\rangle^{2}-\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{tr}}({\mathrm{ad}}_{s}^{2})=-\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{tr}}({\mathrm{ad}}_{s}\circ{\mathrm{ad}}_{s}^{*})-\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{tr}}({\mathrm{ad}}_{s}^{2})=-\frac{1}{4}{\mathrm{tr}}(({\mathrm{ad}}_{s}+{\mathrm{ad}}_{s}^{*})^{2})\leq 0

(13)

and hence
{\mathrm{ric}}(u,u)\leq 0
for any
u\in\mathfrak{g}
. If
\mathfrak{g}
is unimodular then
\mathfrak{g}
is standard according
[
6
, Theorem 1]
. We suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is not unimodular and
we choose
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{p})
and orthonormal basis of
{\mathfrak{n}}
and
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{q})
and orthonormal of
\mathfrak{a}
.

We have
{\mathrm{tr}}({\mathrm{ad}}_{H})=|H|^{2}>0
,
{\mathrm{tr}}(S({\mathrm{ad}}_{H}){\mathrm{ad}}_{H})={\mathrm{tr}}(S({\mathrm{ad}}_{H})^{2})
and since
{\mathrm{ad}}_{H}
is a derivation,
{\mathrm{tr}}(B\circ{\mathrm{ad}}_{H})=0
.
Moreover,

{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathrm{Ric}\circ{\mathrm{ad}}_{H})=\sum_{i}\langle[H,u_{i}],\mathrm{Ric}(u_{i})\rangle+\sum_{j}\langle[H,v_{j}],\mathrm{Ric}(v_{j})\rangle=c\sum_{i}\langle[H,u_{i}],u_{i}\rangle=c{\mathrm{tr}}({\mathrm{ad}}_{H}).

So, by taking
E={\mathrm{ad}}_{H}
in (
12
), we get

c=-\frac{{\mathrm{tr}}(S({\mathrm{ad}}_{H})^{2})}{{\mathrm{tr}}(S({\mathrm{ad}}_{H}))}.

(14)

We identify
{\mathfrak{n}}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
to
\hbox{\bb R}^{p}
via
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{p})
and we set
\mu=[\;,\;]_{|{\mathfrak{n}}\times{\mathfrak{n}}}
. In this way,
\mu
can be
viewed as an element of
\mathscr{N}\subset V_{p}
. If
\mu\not=0
then
\mu
lies in a unique stratum
\mathscr{S}_{\beta}
,
\beta\in\mathscr{B}
, by
[
9
, Theorem 2.10,
(i)
]
. The argument used in the proof of Lauret’s theorem to show that one can assume
\mu\in Y^{ss}_{\beta}=\mathscr{S}_{\beta}\cap W_{\beta}
can be used in our case, so we make the assumption.

We apply (
12
) to the endomorphism
E
given by
E_{|\mathfrak{a}}=0
and
E_{|{\mathfrak{n}}}=\beta+|\beta|^{2}\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{n}}
. The right hand of the second equation in
(
12
)
becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j}\langle E([u_{i},u_{j}])-[E(u_{i}),u_{j}]-[u_{i},E(u_{j})],[u_{i},u_{j}]\rangle+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{r,s}\langle E([v_{r},v_{s}]),[v_{r},v_{s}]\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,r}\langle E([u_{i},v_{r}]),[u_{i},v_{r}]\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r,i}\langle[v_{r},E(u_{i})],[v_{r},u_{i}]\rangle,

which in turn equal to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\langle\pi(\beta+|\beta|^{2}\mathrm{Id}_{{\mathfrak{n}}})\mu,\mu\rangle+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{r,s}\langle(\beta+|\beta|^{2}\mathrm{Id}_{{\mathfrak{n}}})[v_{r},v_{s}],[v_{r},v_{s}]\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,r}\langle(\beta{\mathrm{ad}}_{v_{r}}-{\mathrm{ad}}_{v_{r}}\beta)(u_{i}),{\mathrm{ad}}_{v_{r}}(u_{i})\rangle.

(15)

The first and second terms in (
15
) are
\geq 0
by
[
9
, Lemma 2.16, Lemma 2.17]
and the last one equals
\frac{1}{2}\langle[\beta,{\mathrm{ad}}_{v_{r}}],{\mathrm{ad}}_{v_{r}}\rangle
which is
\geq 0
by
[
9
, (10)]
.

Therefore, since
\mathrm{Ric}_{|{\mathfrak{n}}}=c\mathrm{Id}_{{\mathfrak{n}}}
and
E_{|\mathfrak{a}}=0
, we obtain from (
12
) and (
14
)

-\frac{{\mathrm{tr}}(S({\mathrm{ad}}_{H})^{2})}{{\mathrm{tr}}(S({\mathrm{ad}}_{H}))}{\mathrm{tr}}(E)+{\mathrm{tr}}(S({\mathrm{ad}}_{H}))E\geq 0.

(16)

But
{\mathrm{tr}}(\beta)=-1
and so

{\mathrm{tr}}(E^{2})=|\beta|^{2}(-1+p|\beta|^{2})=|\beta|^{2}{\mathrm{tr}}(E).

(17)

On the other hand, we have

{\mathrm{tr}}(S({\mathrm{ad}}_{H})E)={\mathrm{tr}}(({\mathrm{ad}}_{H})_{|{\mathfrak{n}}}(\beta+|\beta|^{2}\mathrm{Id_{\mathfrak{n}}})=|\beta|^{2}{\mathrm{tr}}(S({\mathrm{ad}}_{H})),

(18)

by
[
9
, Lemma 2.15]
. We use now (
16
), (
17
) and (
18
) to obtain

{\mathrm{tr}}(S({\mathrm{ad}}_{H})^{2}){\mathrm{tr}}(E^{2})\leq({\mathrm{tr}}(S({\mathrm{ad}}_{H})E)^{2},

(19)

a "backward" Cauchy-Schwartz inequality. This turns all inequalities mentioned after (
16
) into equalities, in particular the second term:

\frac{1}{4}\sum_{r,s}\langle(\beta+|\beta|^{2}\mathrm{Id}_{{\mathfrak{n}}})[v_{r},v_{s}],[v_{r},v_{s}]\rangle=0.

We therefore get that
[\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a}]=0
since
\beta+|\beta|^{2}\mathrm{Id}_{{\mathfrak{n}}}
is positive definite by
[
9
, Lemma 2.17]
.

It only remains to consider the case
\mu=0
. Here we argue in the same way but with
E
chosen as
E_{|{\mathfrak{n}}}=\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{n}}
and
E_{|\mathfrak{a}}=0
. It then follows from (
12
) that

\frac{1}{4}\sum_{r,s}|[v_{r},v_{s}]|^{2}=-\frac{{\mathrm{tr}}(S({\mathrm{ad}}_{H})^{2})}{{\mathrm{tr}}(S({\mathrm{ad}}_{H}))}p+{\mathrm{tr}}(S({\mathrm{ad}}_{H}))=\frac{{\mathrm{tr}}(S({\mathrm{ad}}_{H})^{2})}{{\mathrm{tr}}(S({\mathrm{ad}}_{H}))}\left(\frac{({\mathrm{tr}}(S({\mathrm{ad}}_{H})))^{2}}{{\mathrm{tr}}(S({\mathrm{ad}}_{H})^{2})}-p\right)\leq 0

and thus
[{\mathfrak{n}}^{\perp},{\mathfrak{n}}^{\perp}]=0
. This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a Euclidean Lie algebra with harmonic curvature. According to Theorem
2.1
,
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{r}
, for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
,
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
and the equations (
6
) and (
7
) hold. For any
u\in\mathfrak{g}
, we denote by
u_{i}
its component in
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
. For any
i=1,\ldots,r
, we consider

{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}=\sum_{j\not=i,k\not=i,j\not=k}[\mathfrak{g}_{j},\mathfrak{g}_{k}]_{i}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathfrak{h}}_{i}={\mathfrak{p}}_{i}^{\perp}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{i}.

(20)

Proposition 3.1
.

{\mathfrak{h}}_{i}
is a subalgebra which contains
[\mathfrak{r}_{i},\mathfrak{r}_{i}]
where
\mathfrak{r}_{i}
is the radical of
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
. Moreover,
[[\mathfrak{r}_{i},\mathfrak{r}_{i}],\mathfrak{g}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{i}
and for any
u\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{i}
and any
v\in\mathfrak{g}_{i}

{\mathrm{ric}}_{i}(u,v)=\lambda_{i}\langle u,v\rangle.

Proof.

By virtue of (
7
),
u\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{i}
if and only if for any
j\not=i
,
[u,\mathfrak{g}_{j}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{i}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{j}
. So if
u,v\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{i}
and
w\in\mathfrak{g}_{j}
with
j\not=i
then

\displaystyle[[u,v],w]

\displaystyle=[u,[v,w]]-[v,[u,w]]

\displaystyle=[u,[v,w]_{i}]+[u,[v,w]_{j}]-[v,[u,w]_{i}]-[v,[u,w]_{j}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{i}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{j},

and hence
{\mathfrak{h}}_{i}
is a subalgebra.

Let
\mathfrak{r}_{i}
be the radical of
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
and
\rho_{i}
the representation of
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
given by (
9
). Then
\rho_{i}(\mathfrak{r}_{i})
is a solvable subalgebra of
\mathrm{so}(\mathfrak{g}_{i}^{\perp})
and hence it is abelian. So
\rho_{i}([\mathfrak{r}_{i},\mathfrak{r}_{i}])=0
and hence, for any
X\in[\mathfrak{r}_{i},\mathfrak{r}_{i}]
, and any
j\not=i
,
[X,\mathfrak{g}_{j}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{i}
which shows that
[\mathfrak{r}_{i},\mathfrak{r}_{i}]\subset{\mathfrak{h}}_{i}
and
[[\mathfrak{r}_{i},\mathfrak{r}_{i}],\mathfrak{g}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{i}
. The last assertion is a consequence of (
10
).
∎

We can prove now our main theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{r}
be a Euclidean Lie algebra with harmonic curvature. If
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
is solvable for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,e\}
then
\mathfrak{g}
is Ricci-parallel.

Proof.

We will show that for any
\{i,j,k\}
which are mutually different,
\langle\mathfrak{g}_{i},[\mathfrak{g}_{j},\mathfrak{g}_{k}]\rangle=0
. This is equivalent to
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}=\{0\}
for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, where
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
is given by (
20
).

Suppose that
\lambda_{1}<\ldots<\lambda_{r}
. From (
10
), we deduce that for any
u\in\mathfrak{g}_{r}
,

\lambda_{r}|u|^{2}\leq{\mathrm{ric}}_{r}(u,u)

and hence

\begin{cases}[e_{1},e_{2}]=\mu_{1}e_{4},[e_{1},e_{3}]={\frac{\left(\lambda_{{1}}-\lambda_{{3}}\right)\mu_{{1}}}{\lambda_{{1}}-\lambda_{{2}}}}e_{5},[e_{1},e_{4}]=-{\frac{\left(\lambda_{{1}}-\lambda_{{4}}\right)^{2}\mu_{{1}}}{\left(\lambda_{{1}}-\lambda_{{2}}\right)^{2}}}e_{2},[e_{1},e_{5}]=-{\frac{\left(\lambda_{{1}}-\lambda_{{4}}\right)^{2}\mu_{{1}}}{\left(\lambda_{{1}}-\lambda_{{3}}\right)\left(\lambda_{{1}}-\lambda_{{2}}\right)}}e_{3},\\

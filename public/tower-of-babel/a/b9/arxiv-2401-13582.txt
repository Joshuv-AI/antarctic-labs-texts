1.
Introduction

1.1.
The setting and historical background

An elliptic curve is a smooth, projective, algebraic curve of genus
1
, equipped with a distinguished point at
\infty
. These curves play a central role in number theory, algebraic geometry, and cryptography. The
Mordell–Weil group
of an elliptic curve
E
, defined over a number field
K
, is the group of
K
-rational points
E(K)
, under a natural group law arising from the geometry of the curve. By the
Mordell–Weil theorem
,
E(K)
is a finitely generated abelian group, and can be expressed as

E(K)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r}\oplus T,

where:

•

r\geq 0
is an integer known as the
Mordell–Weil rank
of
E
over
K
, which measures the number of independent
K
-rational points of infinite order,

•

T
is a finite abelian group, called the
torsion subgroup
, consisting of points with finite order.

The rank
r
is a deep arithmetic invariant of the elliptic curve and is the subject of intense study. The famous Birch and Swinnerton–Dyer conjecture provides a profound connection between the rank
r
and the behavior of the Hasse–Weil
L
-function
L(E_{/K},s)
of
E
at
s=1
. Specifically, the conjecture asserts that
\operatorname{rank}E(K)
is equal to the order of vanishing of
L(E_{/K},s)
at
s=1
.

The points on
E(\overline{K})
, where
\overline{K}
is the algebraic closure of
K
, naturally come equipped with an action of the absolute Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
. This interaction allows for the arithmetic structure of
E(\overline{K})
to be analyzed through Galois theoretic methods. A key tool in this analysis is the theory of Selmer groups, which consist of global Galois cohomology classes satisfying local conditions at all places. Selmer groups also carry information about the mysterious Tate–Shafarevich group
\Sh(E/K)
, whose elements consist of homogenous spaces measuring the failure of certain local-global principles for rational points on
E
.

Understanding the behaviour of the rank of an elliptic curve over extensions of number fields is a classical problem in number theory. One aspect of this study is to recognize the variation in the rank of an elliptic curve over field extensions. In particular, given an elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
, one is interested in knowing how often is
\operatorname{rank}E(L)>\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q})
, as
L/\mathbb{Q}
varies over a family of number fields with fixed degree, ordered by absolute discriminant. In this regard, David, Fearnley and Kisilevsky
[
DFK04
,
DFK07
]
studied the phenomenon of rank jumps of elliptic curves in cyclic extensions of prime order. More specifically, given an elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
and a prime
p
, they considered all cyclic extensions
L/\mathbb{Q}
with Galois group isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
, and asked how often
\operatorname{rank}E(L)>\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbb{Q})
. Using the relationship between modular symbols and special values of cyclic twists of the Hasse-Weil
L
-function of
E
, they made predictions based on random matrix theory. In particular, it is conjectured that if
p\geq 7
, then there are only finitely many
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
-extensions in which the rank can increase. These assertions are far from being resolved.

Mazur and Rubin
[
MR10
]
study distribution questions for
2
-primary Selmer groups in quadratic twist families of elliptic curves. Their methods have applications to showing that the rank remains stable for a large number of quadratic twists. Their results imply (under additional conditions) that the rank can also become arbitrarily large after base change by a quadratic extension. For any prime
p
, the growth and stability for
p
-Selmer groups of elliptic curves in
p
-cyclic extensions of a number field has been studied by Klagsbrun, Mazur and Rubin
[
KMR14
]
. Similar results have been obtained by Smith, see
[
Smi22
,
Smi23
]
for further details. Park
[
Par24
]
has studied analogous questions over function fields. The phenomenon of rank jumps has been investigated further in other families of number field extensions. See for instance
Lemke-Oliver–Thorne
[
LOT21
]
, Schnidman–Weiss
[
SW23
]
, Beneish–Kundu–Ray
[
BKR24
]
, Berg–Ryan–Young
[
BRY24
]
. Moreover, the study of such rank stability questions has close connection to Hilbert’s tenth problem, which has been recently studied for certain families of field extensions by Garcia–Fritz and Pasten
[
GFP20
]
and Kundu–Lei–Sprung
[
KLS24
]
.

One advancement in this direction follows from the study of diophantine stability by Mazur-Rubin
[
MRL18
]
. They establish that for a simple abelian variety
A_{/K}
with
\operatorname{End}_{\overline{K}}(A)=\operatorname{End}_{K}(A)
, there is a set
S
of primes with positive density such that for all
\ell\in S
and for all integers
n>0
, there are infinitely many
\left(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z}\right)
-extensions
L/K
that have
A(L)=A(K)
. This phenomenon has been studied by Ray–Weston from a different perspective in
[
RW23
]
, where it is shown that for any prime
\ell\geq 5
, and any number field
K
, the density of elliptic curves
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
, that are diophantine stable over
K
at
\ell
is
1
.

Another question of interest in number theory is the study of Galois extensions of a fixed number field with a given Galois group. Conjectures concerning the number of such extensions, ordered by their absolute discriminant, were proposed by Malle
[
Mal02
,
Mal04
]
. These can be viewed as statistical generalizations of the inverse Galois problem for number fields. There has been considerable progress towards these predictions, especially when the Galois group is of a specific kind. For instance, Malle’s conjecture has been proved for abelian groups by Mäki
[
Mäk85
]
and Wright
[
Wri89
]
and for certain nilpotent groups by Koymans and Pagano
[
KP23
]
. Although the inverse Galois problem for finite solvable groups over number fields was resolved by Shafarevich
[
Š54
]
, Malle’s conjecture for general solvable groups is still open.

In this paper, we merge the above two themes to address the problem of counting certain solvable extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
that have the rank stability property. More specifically, given a finite abelian group
B
, an elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
with rank
E(\mathbb{Q})=0
, a rational prime
\ell
and a positive integer
n>0
, under mild hypothesis, we demonstrate the existence of infinitely many extensions
{L}/\mathbb{Q}
with
\operatorname{Gal}({L}/\mathbb{Q})\simeq B\ltimes\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z}
such that rank
E(L)=
rank
E(K)=
rank
E(\mathbb{Q})=0
. Additionally, we obtain a lower bound for the number of such metabelian extensions, ordered by their absolute discriminant. This can compared with the total number of
B\ltimes\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z}
extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
predicted by Malle’s conjectures.

1.2.
Statement of the main result

Let
p
be an odd prime number and
n
be a positive integer. Let
T
denote the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
. Let
B
be an abelian group and

\chi_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}B\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(T)\xrightarrow{\sim}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}

be a character. Let
\mathcal{G}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=B\ltimes T
with respect to
\chi_{0}
. Here, the semi-direct product is defined by the relation
xhx^{-1}=\chi_{0}(x)h
for all
x\in B
and
h\in T
. Fix a Galois extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
such that
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})
is isomorphic to
B
.

A
(\mathcal{G},K)
-extension
L/K/\mathbb{Q}
is a Galois extension
L/\mathbb{Q}
which contains
K
and there exists an isomorphism
\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{G}
which induces an isomorphism of
\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
with
T
. Thus, identifying
\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})
with
\mathcal{G}
, we find that
L^{T}=K
. Throughout the paper, we denote the absolute discriminant of
L
as
\Delta_{L}
.

Denote by
\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/L)
the
p
-Selmer group of
E
over
L
. Then, there is a natural short exact sequence

0\rightarrow E(L)/pE(L)\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/L)\rightarrow\Sh(E/L)[p]\rightarrow 0.

Thus, if
\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/L)=0
, then, in particular, the rank of
E(L)
and the
p
-primary part of the Tate-Shafarevich group
\Sh(E/L)
are zero.

With the above notation, our main theorem is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p\geq 5
be a prime number,
K/\mathbb{Q}
be an abelian number field and
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
be an elliptic curve. Assume that the following conditions are satisfied.

(1)

The
p
-Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/K)
vanishes.

(2)

The field
K
contains
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{n}})
. In particular,
B
has order divisible by
p^{n-1}(p-1)
.

(3)

The character
\chi_{0}
is nontrivial modulo
p
.

(4)

The intersection
K\cap\mathbb{Q}(E[p])
is equal to
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p})
.

(5)

The Galois representation

\rho_{E,p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
associated with the
p
-torsion of
E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
is surjective.

Then there are infinitely many
(\mathcal{G},K)
-extensions
L/K/\mathbb{Q}
such that
\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/L)=0
. In particular, the rank of
E(L)
is zero. Moreover, if
N_{\mathcal{G},K}(E;x)
denotes the total number of
(\mathcal{G},K)
-extensions such that
\operatorname{Sel}_{p}(E/L)=0
and
|\Delta_{L}|\leq x
, then we have

N_{\mathcal{G},K}(E;x)\gg_{\mathcal{G},K}\,\,x^{\frac{1}{|B|(p^{n}-1)}}\,\,(\log x)^{\left(p^{n-1}(p-1)\alpha\right)-1},

where

\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(\frac{p^{2}-p-1}{[K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p})](p-1)(p^{2}-1)}\right).

The above result is obtained as an immediate consequence of Theorem
4.8
, proved in Section 4 and Theorem
5.2
, proved in Section 5.

Given a positive real number
x>0
, set
M_{\mathcal{G},K}(x)
to denote the total number of
(\mathcal{G},K)
-extensions
L/K/\mathbb{Q}
such that
|\Delta_{L}|\leq x
. A variant of the
Malle-Bhargava principle
predicts that

(1.1)

M_{\mathcal{G},K}(x)\,\sim\,c\,x^{\frac{1}{|B|\,(p-1)\,p^{n-1}}}\,(\log x)^{b},

where
c>0
and
b\in\mathbb{Z}
are constants that depend on
\mathcal{G}
and
K
. For further details, see
[
Alb21
, p. 2519 and p.2530]
and
[
Bha07
]
. Thus, the number
N_{\mathcal{G},K}(E;x)
differs from the total number
M_{\mathcal{G},K}(x)
up to a power of
x
to the order of
O\left(\frac{1}{|B|p^{n+1}}\right)
and a factor of
\log x
.

An interesting class of examples to which Theorem
1.1
applies are when
K=\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{n}})
and
\chi_{0}(x)=x^{k}
, where
k
is not divisible by
(p-1)
. In this case,
\chi_{0}
is
k
-th power of the mod-
p^{n}
cyclotomic character. Then the conditions (
2
) and (
3
) of Theorem
1.1
are clearly satisfied. If the representation
\rho_{E,p}
is surjective, then

\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[p])/\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p}))\simeq\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}).

Since
p\geq 5
, the group
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
is simple,
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
does not have any abelian quotients, with cardinality divisible by
p
. Hence, the condition (
4
) follows from (
5
) when
K=\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{n}})
, and Theorem
1.1
is applicable. It is worth noting that the representation
\rho_{E,p}
is surjective for most elliptic curves, when ordered according to height by a theorem of Duke
[
Duk97
]
.

When
n=1
, so
K=\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p})
, we obtain a stronger result (Theorem
3.9
from Section
3
) which is also valid for
p=3
and easier to state. At the end of the Section
3
, we provide an explicit example for which our hypotheses are satisfied.

Method of proof

We now describe the ideas that go into the proofs of Theorem

(2)

\iota_{v}^{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{L_{v}}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{G}_{L}

\begin{split}&H^{i}(L,\cdot)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=H^{i}(\operatorname{G}_{L},\cdot),\\
&H^{i}(L_{v},\cdot)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=H^{i}(\operatorname{G}_{L_{v}},\cdot)\end{split}

For

-torsion subgroup of

\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{n}}(E/L)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left(H^{1}(L,E[p^{n}])\longrightarrow\prod_{v\in\Omega_{L}}H^{1}(L_{v},E)[p^{n}]\right).

. Given a

This in turn gives rise to a non-zero

-equivariant isomorphism

which is defined to be the natural composite

Here, the action of

is as follows. Given

let

of

since

is an abelian extension of

are equivalent. The association

is the inverse of

The

Theorem 3.7

(Wiles)
.

Let

\frac{\#H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\Sigma}/\mathbb{Q},M)}{\#H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}^{\vee}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\Sigma}/\mathbb{Q},M^{\vee})}=\left(\frac{\#H^{0}(\mathbb{Q},M)}{\#H^{0}(\mathbb{Q},M^{\vee})}\right)\times\prod_{\ell\in\Sigma}\left(\frac{\#\mathcal{L}_{\ell}}{\#H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},M)}\right).

Proposition 3.8

N_{\mathcal{G},K}(E;x)\geq C\sum_{V\in M(y)}r^{|V|},

where
r\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=p^{n-1}(p-1)
. Consider the function

F(s)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}V\subset\mathfrak{T}_{E}\\
V\text{ finite}\end{subarray}}\frac{r^{|V|}}{\prod\limits_{\ell\in V}\ell^{s}}=\prod_{\ell\in\mathfrak{T}_{E}}\left(1+\frac{r}{\ell^{s}}\right).

Thus, we see that

\log F(s)=r\,\sum_{\ell\in\mathfrak{T}_{E}}\frac{1}{\ell^{s}}+\theta_{1}(s),

where
\theta_{1}(s)
is holomorphic in
\operatorname{Re}(s)\geq 1
; and as a consequence,

\log F(s)=r\alpha\,\log\left(\frac{1}{s-1}\right)+\theta_{2}(s),

where
\alpha=\mathfrak{d}(\mathfrak{T}_{E})
, which is computed in Lemma
2.12
, and
\theta_{2}(s)
is holomorphic in
\operatorname{Re}(s)\geq 1
. Thus, we deduce that

F(s)=(s-1)^{-\,r\alpha}\,h(s),

where
h(s)
is a non-zero holomorphic function in
\operatorname{Re}(s)\geq 1
. By Delange’s Tauberian theorem (cf.
[
Ten15
, Theorem 7.28]
), we deduce that

N_{\mathcal{G},K}(E;x)\gg y\,(\log y)^{r\alpha-1}\gg x^{\frac{1}{|B|(p^{n}-1)}}\,(\log x)^{\left(p^{n-1}(p-1)\alpha\right)-1}.

This proves the result.
∎

The modified scattering for Dirac equations of scattering-critical nonlinearity Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 35Q41, 35Q40, 35Q55. Thanks: Keywords and phrases. Maxwell-Dirac equations, Lorenz gauge, global well-posedness, modified scattering, energy estimates, phase modification.
Yonggeun Cho, Soonsik Kwon, Kiyeon Lee, Changhun Yang

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the Maxwell-Dirac system in 3 dimension under zero magnetic field. We prove the global well-posedness and modified scattering for small solutions in the weighted Sobolev class. Imposing the Lorenz gauge condition, (and taking the Dirac projection operator), it becomes a system of Dirac equations with Hartree type nonlinearity with a long range potential as | x | − 1 |x|^{-1} . We perform the weighted energy estimates. In this procedure, we have to deal with various resonance functions that stem from the Dirac projections. We use the spacetime resonance argument of Germain-Masmoudi-Shatah ( [ 13 , 14 , 15 ] ), as well as the spinorial null-structure. On the way, we recognize a long range interaction which is responsible for a logarithmic phase correction in the modified scattering statement.

Introduction

Consider the Maxwell-Dirac equations (
MD
) in
\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
:

(MD)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} (\partial_{t}+igA_{0})&{\Psi}=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\alpha^{j}(\partial_{j}+igA_{j})\Psi-im\beta\Psi\qquad\mathrm{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}^{1+3},\\
-\square A_{0}&=g{\langle\Psi,\Psi\rangle},\\
\square A_{j}&=g{\langle\Psi,\alpha^{j}\Psi\rangle}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here the unknown
\Psi:\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}^{4}

is the spinor field,
A=(A_{0},A_{1},A_{2},A_{3})
is given real-valued
1
-form, and the
4\times 4
matrices
\alpha^{j}
’s and

\beta
are Dirac matrices as follows:

\displaystyle\alpha^{j}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\sigma^{j}\\
\sigma^{j}&\;0\end{array}\right),\qquad\beta=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}I_{2}&\;\;\;0\\
0&-I_{2}\end{array}\right),

where
\sigma^{j}
’s are Pauli matrices given by

\displaystyle\sigma^{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\;1\\
1&\;0\end{array}\right),\quad\sigma^{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\;-i\\
i&\;\;\;0\end{array}\right)\quad\sigma^{3}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\;\;\;0\\
0&\;-1\end{array}\right).

The constant
m>0
is a mass and
g>0
is a coupling constant. The
d’Alembert operator
\square
has the form
\square:=\partial_{t}^{2}-\Delta
and the inner product is defined by
{\langle\psi,\phi\rangle}=\psi^{\dagger}\phi
.

The equations (
MD
) model an electron in electromagnetic field and form a fundamental system in quantum electrodynamics.
(
MD
) is invariant under the gauge transformation:
(\Psi,A)\to(e^{i\chi}\Psi,A-d\chi)

for a real-valued function
\chi
on
\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. For concreteness of our discussion, let us choose Lorenz gauge, which is defined by the condition

\displaystyle-\partial_{t}A_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\partial_{j}A_{j}=0.

By assuming vanishing magnetic field

{\rm\text{curl}(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3})=0}

one can find a function
{\varphi}
such that
\nabla{\varphi}=(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3})
.
The gauge transformation
\psi(t,x):=e^{ig{\varphi}(t,x)}\Psi(t,x)

uncouples the equations (
MD
) to satisfy the Dirac equations (
DE
)

(DE)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \left(\partial_{t}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\alpha^{j}\partial_{j}+im\beta\right)\psi&=ic_{1}\left(|x|^{-1}*|\psi|^{2}\right)\psi\;\;\mathrm{in}\;\;\mathbb{R}^{1+3},\\
\psi(0)&=\psi_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

where
c_{1}=\frac{g^{2}}{4\pi}
and
|\psi|^{2}=\langle\psi,\psi\rangle
. We refer to
[
4
]
for rigorous derivation.
Any smooth solution to (
DE
) satisfies the
L_{x}^{2}
conservation laws:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\|\psi(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}=\|\psi_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}.

By a scaling in
L^{2}
we set
m=1
hereafter.
The purpose of this paper is to develop a modified scattering theory of global solutions to (
DE
) for small initial data.

As observed in
[
9
]
, the square of linear operator is diagonalized

(1.2)

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=1}^{3}\alpha_{j}\cdot\xi_{j}+\beta\right)^{2}=\langle\xi\rangle^{2}I_{4},

where
\langle\xi\rangle:=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}

for frequency vector
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
We introduce the Dirac projection operators
\Pi_{\pm}(D)

defined by

(1.3)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\pm}(D):=\frac{1}{2}\left(I_{4}\pm\frac{1}{\langle D\rangle}\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{3}\alpha^{j}D_{j}+\beta\Big]\right),

where
D=(D_{1},D_{2},D_{3}),D_{j}=-i\partial_{j}
, and
\mathcal{F}(\left<D\right>f)=\langle\xi\rangle\widehat{f}
. Here
\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(x)\,dx
. Then using (
1.2
),
we get

i\Big(-i\sum_{j=1}^{3}\alpha^{j}\partial_{j}+\beta\Big)=\langle D\rangle(\Pi_{+}(D)-\Pi_{-}(D)).

By definition of projections we further get

(1.4)

\Pi_{+}(D)+\Pi_{-}(D)=I_{4},\quad\Pi_{\pm}(D)\Pi_{\pm}(D)=\Pi_{\pm}(D),\quad\Pi_{\pm}(D)\Pi_{\mp}(D)=0.

By
\psi_{\pm}
we denote
\Pi_{\pm}(D)\psi
.
Then, the Dirac equation (
DE
) can be rewritten as:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} (i\partial_{t}-\langle D\rangle)\psi_{+}&=-c_{1}\Pi_{+}(D)\Big[(|x|^{-1}*|\psi|^{2})\psi\Big],\\
(i\partial_{t}+\langle D\rangle)\psi_{-}&=-c_{1}\Pi_{-}(D)\Big[(|x|^{-1}*|\psi|^{2})\psi\Big],\\
\psi_{+}(0)=\psi_{0,+}:=&\Pi_{+}(D)\psi_{0}\text{ and }\psi_{-}(0)=\psi_{0,-}:=\Pi_{-}(D)\psi_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

which is a system of half Klein-Gordon equations coupled by Hartree nonlinearity. Denoting by
e^{\mp it\langle D\rangle}\psi_{0,\pm}
the
free solutions to (
1.5

e^{\mp it\langle D\rangle}\psi_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i(x\cdot\xi\mp t\langle\xi\rangle)}\widehat{\psi_{0,\pm}}(\xi)\,d\xi,

we can express the free solution to (
DE

U(t)\psi_{0}:=e^{-it\langle D\rangle}\Pi_{+}(D)\psi_{0}+e^{it\langle D\rangle}\Pi_{-}(D)\psi_{0}.

By Duhamel’s principle, the solutions to (
1.5

(1.6)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \psi_{+}(t)&=e^{-it\langle D\rangle}\psi_{0,+}+i\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-t^{\prime})\langle D\rangle}\Pi_{+}(D)\Big[(|x|^{-1}*|\psi|^{2})\psi\Big](t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime},\\
\psi_{-}(t)&=e^{it\langle D\rangle}\psi_{0,-}+i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\langle D\rangle}\Pi_{-}(D)\Big[(|x|^{-1}*|\psi|^{2})\psi\Big](t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}.\end{aligned}\right.

The weighted energy estimates are referred as the control of quantity

The proof is based on the
bootstrap argument
. We construct a function space adapted to time decay estimates below (
2.2
). By assuming the norm is a priori small, we deduce that the solutions decay like a solution to a linear equation. To achieve this, we first establish the weighted energy estimates, which require to control

in the Fourier space by using the Taylor expansion to figure out the leading contribution where the phase logarithm correction is required. For rigorous analysis, we decompose the singular potential in the Fourier space,
\mathcal{F}(|x|^{-1})\sim|\eta|^{-2}
, with a suitable scale in

.
Then,

via the integration by parts in space variables.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we introduce a function space adapted to time decay estimates. Under a priori assumption, we establish several frequency localized multilinear estimates. In Section 3, we investigate the null structures which improve the estimates obtained in Section 2.
The improved estimates play a crucial role in the course of the weighted energy estimates.
In Section 4, we provide the proof of the main theorem by assuming the weighted energy estimates and

2.1.
Time decay of Dirac equations

For given small initial data as in (

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{\Sigma_{T}}:=\|\Pi_{+}(D)\psi\|_{\Sigma_{T}^{+}}+\|\Pi_{-}(D)\psi\|_{\Sigma_{T}^{-}}\lesssim\varepsilon_{1},

\left\|\mathcal{N}(\psi,\psi,\psi)(s)\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}&\lesssim\langle s\rangle^{-\frac{5}{2}}\epsilon_{1}^{3}.\end{aligned}

Proof.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\psi_{i}:\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{1+3}\rightarrow\mathbf{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
for

and

\displaystyle\|P_{N}\langle\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\rangle\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{\frac{3}{2}}{\langle N\rangle}^{-5}\left\|\widehat{\langle D\rangle^{10}\psi_{1}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\left\|\widehat{\langle D\rangle^{10}\psi_{2}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}},

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle N\rangle^{-2}\|\psi_{1}\|_{W^{2,\infty}}\|\psi_{2}\|_{W^{2,\infty}},

\displaystyle\|P_{N}\langle\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\rangle\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{3}\|\psi_{1}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|\psi_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}.

Proof.

By Plancherel’s theorem and Hölder inequality, we see that

\displaystyle\|P_{N}\langle\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\rangle\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\rho_{N}^{2}(\xi)\left|\mathcal{F}\langle\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\rangle(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\rho_{N}^{2}(\xi)\left|\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle\eta\rangle^{-10}\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^{-10}\Big\langle\widehat{\langle D\rangle^{10}\psi_{1}}(\eta),\widehat{\langle D\rangle^{10}\psi_{2}}(\xi-\eta)\Big\rangle d\eta\right|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\widehat{\langle D\rangle^{10}\psi_{1}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\left\|\widehat{\langle D\rangle^{10}\psi_{2}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\rho_{N}^{2}(\xi)\left|\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle\eta\rangle^{-10}\langle\xi-\eta\rangle^{-10}d\eta\right|^{2}d\xi

Next, we estimate

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|P_{N}\langle\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\rangle\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}&=\sup_{x\in\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left|\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}\rho_{N}(\xi)\mathcal{F}\langle\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\rangle(\xi)d\xi\right|\\

&\lesssim\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\rho_{N}\langle\xi\rangle^{-2})*(1-\Delta)\langle\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\rangle\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\\
&\lesssim\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\rho_{N}\langle\xi\rangle^{-2})\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\|(1-\Delta)\langle\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\rangle\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\\
&\lesssim\langle N\rangle^{-2}\|\psi_{1}\|_{W^{2,\infty}}\|\psi_{2}\|_{W^{2,\infty}}.\end{aligned}

We also have

\displaystyle\|P_{N}\langle\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\rangle\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\rho_{N}\mathcal{F}\langle\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\rangle\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\lesssim\|\rho_{N}\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\|\mathcal{F}\langle\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\rangle\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{3}\|\psi_{1}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|\psi_{2}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}.

Null-structure

To prove Theorem

, we have to use structure of the equation obtained from the Dirac projection operator (
1.3
) and the resonance function. Lemmas in this section will be used throughout the proofs of Section

From (
1.3
), the symbol of Dirac projection operators are given by

\displaystyle\Pi_{\pm}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\left(I\pm\frac{\alpha\cdot\xi+\beta}{\langle\xi\rangle}\right).

The symbol are bounded in the sense that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left|\nabla^{n}\Pi_{\pm}(\xi)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle\xi\rangle^{n-1}},

which implies the boundedness of Dirac projection operators in the Sobolev spaces: For
1<p<\infty
and
s\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{\pm}(D)\psi\|_{W^{s,p}}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{W^{s,p}}.

Furthermore, one can easily verify the null structure

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\pm}(\xi)\Pi_{\mp}(\xi)=0,

which proved very useful especially when we deal with bilinear operators in the nonlinear term (
[
9
,
10
,
1
,
2
]
).

Lemma 3.1
.

For
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfying
|\eta|\ll|\xi|
, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\Big|\nabla_{\eta}^{m}\nabla_{\xi}^{n}\left[\Pi_{\pm}(\xi)\Pi_{\mp}(\xi-\eta)\right]\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\eta|^{1-m}\max\left(\frac{1}{\langle\xi\rangle^{n+1}},\frac{1}{{\langle\xi-\eta\rangle}^{n+1}}\right).

Proof.

We only consider
\pm=+
.
We compute

\displaystyle 4\Pi_{+}(\xi)\Pi_{-}(\xi-\eta)

\displaystyle=\left(I+\frac{\alpha\cdot\xi+\beta}{\langle\xi\rangle}\right)\left(I-\frac{\alpha\cdot(\xi-\eta)+\beta}{\langle\xi-\eta\rangle}\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha\cdot\left(\frac{\xi}{\langle\xi\rangle}-\frac{\xi-\eta}{\langle\xi-\eta\rangle}\right)+\beta\left(\frac{1}{\langle\xi\rangle}-\frac{1}{\langle\xi-\eta\rangle}\right)-\frac{(\alpha\cdot\xi)(\alpha\cdot\eta)}{\langle\xi\rangle\langle\xi-\eta\rangle}

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt+\frac{(\alpha\cdot\eta)\beta}{\langle\xi\rangle\langle\xi-\eta\rangle}+\frac{(\eta-2\xi)\cdot\eta I}{\langle\xi-\eta\rangle\left(\langle\xi\rangle+\langle\xi-\eta\rangle\right)}.

For the first term, we apply the mean value theorem

\displaystyle\frac{\xi}{\langle\xi\rangle}-\frac{\xi-\eta}{\langle\xi-\eta\rangle}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\xi_{\lambda}}\left(I-\frac{\xi_{\lambda}\otimes\xi_{\lambda}}{\langle\xi_{\lambda}\rangle^{2}}\right)\eta d\lambda,

where
\xi_{\lambda}=\xi+\lambda(\xi-\eta)
. Then, a direct computation gives that

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{\xi}^{n}\left(\frac{\xi}{\langle\xi\rangle}-\frac{\xi-\eta}{\langle\xi-\eta\rangle}\right)\right|\lesssim\frac{|\eta|}{\langle\xi\rangle^{n+1}}.

The second term can be estimated similarly.
The estimates for the remaining three terms are easily dealt with thanks to (
3.1
).
∎

Using the Lemma
3.1
, we get the following bilinear estimates.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
N\in 2^{\mathbf{\mathbb{Z}}}
and
\theta\in\{+,-\}
. Assume that
\|\psi_{1}\|_{\sum_{T}^{\theta}},\|\psi_{2}\|_{\sum_{T}^{-\theta}}\lesssim\epsilon_{1}
. Then we get

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|P_{N}{\langle\Pi_{\theta}(D)\psi_{1}(t),\Pi_{-\theta}(D)\psi_{2}(t)\rangle}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim N{\langle N\rangle}^{-2}{\langle t\rangle}^{-3+\frac{{\delta}_{0}}{10}}\varepsilon_{1}^{2}.

Remark 3.3
.

As a direct consequence of (
2.8
) and the boundedness of Dirac projections (
3.1
), one has

\displaystyle\|P_{N}{\langle\Pi_{\theta}(D)\psi_{1}(t),\Pi_{-\theta}(D)\psi_{2}(t)\rangle}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim{\langle N\rangle}^{-2}{\langle t\rangle}^{-3+\frac{{\delta}_{0}}{10}}\varepsilon_{1}^{2}.

Thus, by exploiting the spinorial null structure in (
3.4
), we obtain an extra
N
factor which is useful when
N\leq 1
.

Proof.

As in (
2.6
), one can obtain

\displaystyle\|P_{N}{\langle\Pi_{\theta}(D)\psi_{1}(t),\Pi_{-\theta}(D)\psi_{2}(t)\rangle}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim A_{N}\||D|^{-\varepsilon}{\langle D\rangle}^{2}\psi_{1}(t)\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\||D|^{-\varepsilon}{\langle D\rangle}^{2}\psi_{2}(t)\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}},

where

\displaystyle A_{N}

\displaystyle=\int\!\!\!\!\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{3+3}}\left|\int\!\!\!\!\int_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{3+3}}e^{iy\cdot\xi}e^{iz\cdot\sigma}{\langle\xi+\sigma\rangle}^{-2}{\langle\sigma\rangle}^{-2}|\xi+\sigma|^{\varepsilon}|\sigma|^{\varepsilon}\Pi_{\theta}(\sigma)\Pi_{-\theta}(\xi+\sigma)d\xi d\sigma\right|dzdy,

for
\varepsilon
satisfying
0<\left(2+\frac{2}{3}{\delta}_{0}\right)\varepsilon\ll\frac{{\delta}_{0}}{10}
.
Since

\displaystyle\||D|^{-\varepsilon}\phi\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}\varepsilon}\|\phi\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}^{1-\frac{2}{3}\varepsilon}\text{ for }\phi\in L_{x}^{2}\cap L_{x}^{\infty}\text{ and }0<\varepsilon\ll 1,

we have by (
3.5
) and the a priori assumption (
2.1
) that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\||D|^{-\varepsilon}{\langle D\rangle}^{2}\psi_{j}(t)\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim{\langle t\rangle}^{-\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon+\frac{1}{3}\varepsilon{\delta}_{0}}\epsilon_{1},\text{ for }j=1,2.

Thus, we suffice to prove that

(3.6)

\displaystyle A_{N}\lesssim N\langle N\rangle^{-2}.

We apply the dyadic decomposition to obtain

A_{N}\leq\sum_{N_{1},N_{2}\in 2^{\mathbf{\mathbb{Z}}}}A_{(N,N_{1},N_{2})},

where

\displaystyle A_{(N,N_{1},N_{2})}=\iint\left|\iint e^{iy\cdot\xi}e^{iz\cdot\sigma}{K_{(N,N_{1},N_{2})}(\xi,\sigma)}d\xi d\sigma\right|dzdy,

with

(3.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &{K_{(N,N_{1},N_{2})}(\xi,\sigma)}\\
&\;\;:=\rho_{N}(\xi)\rho_{N_{1}}(\xi+\sigma)\rho_{N_{2}}(\sigma){\langle\xi+\sigma\rangle}^{-2}{\langle\sigma\rangle}^{-2}|\xi+\sigma|^{\varepsilon}|\sigma|^{\varepsilon}\Pi_{\theta}(\sigma)\Pi_{-\theta}(\xi+\sigma).\end{aligned}

A direct computation using Lemma
3.1
gives that for
N\lesssim N_{1}
,

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{\sigma}^{m}\nabla_{\xi}^{n}{K_{(N,N_{1},N_{2})}(\xi,\sigma)}\right|\lesssim\langle N_{1}\rangle^{-2}\langle N_{2}\rangle^{-2}NN_{1}^{\epsilon}N_{2}^{\epsilon}N^{-n}N_{2}^{-m}.

Integrating by parts and using the differential inequalities, we estimate

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sum_{N\lesssim N_{1}}A_{(N,N_{1},N_{2})}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{N\lesssim N_{1}}\bigg(\left\||z|^{-2}\right\|_{L^{1}(|z|\leq N_{2}^{-1})}\int\left\|\int e^{iy\cdot\xi}\Delta_{\sigma}{K_{(N,N_{1},N_{2})}(\xi,\sigma)}d\xi\right\|_{L_{\sigma}^{1}}dy\\
&\hskip 42.67912pt+\left\||z|^{-4}\right\|_{L^{1}(|z|\geq N_{2}^{-1})}\int\left\|\int e^{iy\cdot\xi}\Delta_{\sigma}^{2}{K_{(N,N_{1},N_{2})}(\xi,\sigma)}d\xi\right\|_{L_{\sigma}^{1}}dy\bigg)\\
&\lesssim\sum_{N\lesssim N_{1}}\bigg(\left\||y|^{-2}\right\|_{L^{1}(|y|\leq N^{-1})}\left\|\Delta_{\xi}(N_{2}^{-1}\Delta_{\sigma}+N_{2}\Delta_{\sigma}^{2}){K_{(N,N_{1},N_{2})}(\xi,\sigma)}\right\|_{L_{\sigma}^{1}L_{\xi}^{1}}\bigg)\\
&\hskip 42.67912pt+\left\||y|^{-4}\right\|_{L^{1}(|y|\geq N^{-1})}\left\|\Delta_{\xi}^{2}(N_{2}^{-1}\Delta_{\sigma}+N_{2}\Delta_{\sigma}^{2}){K_{(N,N_{1},N_{2})}(\xi,\sigma)}\right\|_{L_{\sigma}^{1}L_{\xi}^{1}}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{N\lesssim N_{1}}N{\langle N_{1}\rangle}^{-2}{\langle N_{2}\rangle}^{-2}N_{1}^{\varepsilon}N_{2}^{\varepsilon}\lesssim N{\langle N\rangle}^{-2}.\end{aligned}

For the remaining contribution where
N_{1}\ll N
, we can obtain the desired bound via the change of variables by the symmetry of
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
. Indeed, by defining

\widetilde{K}_{(N,N_{1},N_{2})}(\xi,\sigma):=K_{(N,N_{1},N_{2})}(\xi,-\xi+\sigma),

we have

\sum_{N_{1}\ll N}A_{(N,N_{1},N_{2})}=\sum_{N_{1}\ll N}\iint\left|\iint e^{iy^{\prime}\cdot\xi}e^{iz\cdot\sigma}\widetilde{K}_{(N,N_{1},N_{2})}(\xi,\sigma)d\xi d\sigma\right|dzdy^{\prime}.

Now, one can verify that
\widetilde{K}_{(N,N_{1},N_{2})}
satisfies the differential inequalities

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{\sigma}^{m}\nabla_{\xi}^{n}\widetilde{K}_{(N,N_{1},N_{2})}(\xi,\sigma)\right|\lesssim\langle N_{1}\rangle^{-2}\langle N_{2}\rangle^{-2}NN_{1}^{\epsilon}N_{2}^{\epsilon}N^{-n}N_{1}^{-m}.

Similar computation as in (
3.8
)
yields that
\sum_{N_{1}\ll N}A_{(N,N_{1},N_{2})}A_{N}\lesssim N{\langle N\rangle}^{-2}
.
∎

&=e^{-\theta_{0}it\langle D\rangle}\psi_{0,\theta_{0}}+i\sum_{\theta_{j}\in\{\pm\},j=1,2,3}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i\theta_{0}(t-s)}\Pi_{\theta_{0}}(D)\mathcal{N}(\psi_{\theta_{1}},\psi_{\theta_{2}},\psi_{\theta_{3}})(s)ds,\end{aligned}

where
\theta_{0}\in\{+,-\}
and
\psi_{\theta_{j}}=\Pi_{\theta_{j}}(D)\psi
for
j=1,2,3
.
In order to prove Proposition
4.1
, we prove

\displaystyle\|\psi_{\theta_{0}}\|_{\sum_{T}^{\theta_{0}}}\leq\|\psi_{\theta_{0}}\|_{\sum_{0}^{\theta_{0}}}+C\sum_{\theta_{j}\in\{\pm\},j=1,2,3}\|\psi_{\theta_{1}}\|_{\sum_{T}^{\theta_{1}}}\|\psi_{\theta_{2}}\|_{\sum_{T}^{\theta_{2}}}\|\psi_{\theta_{3}}\|_{\sum_{T}^{\theta_{3}}}.

The proof is based on the energy method in weighted energy space.

5.1.
Idea of proof

For the solution
\psi_{\theta}
to (
1.5
) with
\theta\in\{+,-\}
,
we use the interaction representation of
\psi_{\theta}(t)
so as to track the scattering states

\displaystyle f_{\theta}(t,x):=e^{\theta it\langle D\rangle}\psi_{\theta}(t,x).

We also use a method of space-time resonance
developed in the works
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
25
,
22
]
.
In addition, we exploit the spinorial null-structures intrinsic in the our main equation (
1.5
).
Then, we can rewrite (
1.6
) in terms of the Fourier transform of

f_{\theta_{0}}
. Then,

(5.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\widehat{f_{\theta_{0}}}(t,\xi)=\widehat{\psi_{0,\theta_{0}}}(\xi)+ic_{1}\sum_{\theta_{j}\in\{\pm\},j=1,2,3}\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{\Theta}}(t,\xi),\\
&\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{\Theta}}(t,\xi)=\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Pi_{\theta_{0}}(\xi)e^{is{p}_{(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})}(\xi,\eta)}|\eta|^{-2}\mathcal{F}\langle\psi_{\theta_{3}},\psi_{\theta_{2}}\rangle(s,\eta)\widehat{f_{{\theta_{1}}}}(s,\xi-\eta)d\eta ds,\end{aligned}

where
we denote 4-tuple of signs by
\mathbf{\Theta}=(\theta_{0},\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3})
and the resonance function is given by

(5.2)

\displaystyle{p}_{(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})}(\xi,\eta)={\theta_{0}}\langle\xi\rangle-{\theta_{1}}\langle\xi-\eta\rangle.

We have to deal with 4 cases of
{\mathbf{\Theta}}
.
Each
{p}_{(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})}
has their own features, so need to be dealt with separately.

Let us take a closer look on the structure
of nonlinear terms. In the course of energy estimates, we take
\nabla_{\xi}

and
\nabla_{\xi}^{2}
to
\mathcal{I}_{{\mathbf{\Theta}}}
. When the derivatives fall
on the phase
e^{isp_{({\theta_{0}},{\theta_{1}})}}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Pi_{\theta_{0}}(\xi)s\textbf{m}(\xi,\eta)e^{is{p}_{(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})}(\xi,\eta)}|\eta|^{-2}\mathcal{F}\langle\psi_{\theta_{3}},\psi_{\theta_{2}}\rangle(s,\eta)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\widehat{f_{{\theta_{1}}}}(s,\xi-\eta)d\eta ds,\end{aligned}

(5.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Pi_{\theta_{0}}(\xi)s^{2}\left[\textbf{m}(\xi,\eta)\right]^{2}e^{is{p}_{(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})}(\xi,\eta)}|\eta|^{-2}\mathcal{F}\langle\psi_{\theta_{3}},\psi_{\theta_{2}}\rangle(s,\eta)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\widehat{f_{{\theta_{1}}}}(s,\xi-\eta)d\eta ds,\end{aligned}

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\xi}{p}_{(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})}(\xi,\eta)={\theta_{0}}\frac{\xi}{\langle\xi\rangle}-{\theta_{1}}\frac{\xi-\eta}{\langle\xi-\eta\rangle},

\displaystyle\left[\textbf{m}(\xi,\eta)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=\textbf{m}(\xi,\eta)\otimes\textbf{m}(\xi,\eta).

Here, we have to bound the singularity
|\eta|^{-2}
and recover the loss of
s
. To achieve this, we use two null structures suited for given
{\mathbf{\Theta}}
.
The first one is that a smooth function
\mathbf{m}
satisfies
\mathbf{m}(\xi,0)=0
if
\theta_{0}=\theta_{1}
, more concretely,

(5.4)

|\nabla_{\xi}^{n}\nabla_{\eta}^{m}\textbf{m}(\xi,\eta)|\sim\frac{|\eta|^{1-m}}{\langle\xi\rangle^{n+1}},\qquad\text{ when }|\eta|\ll|\xi|,

Thanks to (
5.4
), we can think that the multiplier
s\textbf{m}(\xi,\eta)|\eta|^{-2}

behaves like
|\eta|^{-2}
, as far as the estimates are concerned.
The other is the spinorial null structure (
3.2
) which we can apply when
\theta_{0}\neq\theta_{1}
or
\theta_{2}\neq\theta_{3}
.
Here, (
3.3
) corresponds to (
5.4
).

On the other hand, we have to recover the loss of time growth
s^{2}
in (
5.3
). It can be compensated thanks to the null structures expect for the case when
\theta_{0}\neq\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}=\theta_{3}
where only the spinorial null structure from
\theta_{0}\neq\theta_{1}
can be applied to recover one loss of time growth
s
.
A key observation is that when
\theta_{0}=\theta_{1}
the resonance functions

{p}_{(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})}(\xi,\eta)=\theta_{0}(\langle\xi\rangle+\langle\xi-\eta\rangle)

are non-degenerate, which
enables us to use the time non-resonance via integration by parts in time variable to recover the remaining loss of
s
.
Moreover, an additional factor
|\eta|
essential to close the estimates can be obtained as a by product, which we regard as another null structure, see Lemma
3.4
.

and

6.2
) in the last inequality. Next, we approximate

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\times\widehat{f_{{\theta_{0}}}}(\xi)\left|\widehat{f_{{\theta_{2}}}}(\xi+\sigma)\right|^{2}d\eta d\sigma.

\displaystyle\lesssim M^{-2+\frac{30}{k}}{\langle N\rangle}^{-10}\sum_{({\textbf{N},\textbf{L}})\in\mathcal{A}_{3}}L^{-3}\max({N_{1},N_{3}})^{-2}\min(\langle N_{1}\rangle,\langle N_{3}\rangle)^{20}

\displaystyle\qquad\left(\left\|\nabla\widehat{f_{{\theta_{1}},N_{1}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\widehat{f_{{\theta_{3}},N_{3}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\widehat{f_{{\theta_{1}},N_{1}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla\widehat{f_{{\theta_{3}},N_{3}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)\left\|\psi_{{\theta_{2}},N_{2}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-2+\frac{30}{k}}{\langle N\rangle}^{-10}M^{{\delta}_{0}}M^{-\frac{3}{2}}M^{\frac{24}{k}}\sum_{L\geq M^{-\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{30}}}L^{-3}\sum_{L^{\prime}\geq L^{-\frac{1}{3}}M^{-\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{k}}}(L^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-\frac{7}{2}+\frac{54}{k}+{\delta}_{0}}M^{\frac{17}{8}-\frac{17}{180}}M^{\frac{1}{6}+\frac{1}{2k}}{\langle N\rangle}^{-10}\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-(1+{\delta}_{0})}{\langle N\rangle}^{-10}.

Next, from (
6.14
), one can find the pointiwse bound of
\mathbf{m}_{4}

\displaystyle|\textbf{m}_{4}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)|\lesssim\max(N_{1},N_{3})^{-2}\min(\langle N_{1}\rangle,\langle N_{3}\rangle)^{10}L^{-4}.

Using this and Hölder inequality, we estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{({\textbf{N},\textbf{L}})\in\mathcal{A}_{3}}|\mathcal{J}_{4}(s,\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim M^{-2+\frac{30}{k}}{\langle N\rangle}^{-10}\sum_{({\textbf{N},\textbf{L}})\in\mathcal{A}_{3}}\max(N_{1},N_{3})^{-2}\min(\langle N_{1}\rangle,\langle N_{3}\rangle)^{10}L^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left\|\widehat{f_{{\theta_{1}},N_{1}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\widehat{f_{{\theta_{2}},N_{2}}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\widehat{f_{{\theta_{3}},N_{3}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-2+\frac{30}{k}+{\delta}_{0}}{\langle N\rangle}^{-10}\sum_{L\geq M^{-\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{30}}}L^{-1}\sum_{L^{\prime}\geq M^{-\frac{2}{5}}}(L^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-(1+{\delta}_{0})}{\langle N\rangle}^{-10},

where we used

\displaystyle M^{-\frac{2}{5}}\lesssim L^{\prime},\qquad L^{\prime}\sim|\sigma|\lesssim\max(N_{1},N_{3}),

which easily follows from the condition on dyadic pieces (
6.11
).

Estimates for Case 4.

As in
Case 3
, we perform an integration by parts to gain a time decay. The strategy depends on combinations of sign
\mathbf{\Theta}
.

(1)

If
\theta_{1}=\theta_{3}
, we perform an integration by parts with respect to
\eta
variable using the following

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\eta}p_{\mathbf{\Theta}}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)|=\left|\theta_{1}\left(\frac{\xi+\eta}{\langle\xi+\eta\rangle}-\frac{\xi+\eta+\sigma}{\langle\xi+\eta+\sigma\rangle}\right)\right|\gtrsim L^{\prime}\langle N_{1}\rangle^{-3}.

(2)

If
\theta_{1}\neq\theta_{3}
, we perform an integration by parts with respect to
\sigma
variable using the following

(6.16)

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\sigma}p_{\mathbf{\Theta}}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)|\gtrsim\left\{\begin{aligned} &\left|\frac{\xi+\sigma}{\langle\xi+\sigma\rangle}-\frac{\xi+\eta+\sigma}{\langle\xi+\eta+\sigma\rangle}\right|\gtrsim L\langle N_{2}\rangle^{-3},&&\text{ if }\theta_{2}=\theta_{3},\\
&\left|\frac{\xi+\sigma}{\langle\xi+\sigma\rangle}+\frac{\xi+\eta+\sigma}{\langle\xi+\eta+\sigma\rangle}\right|\gtrsim N_{2}\langle N_{2}\rangle^{-1},&&\text{ if }\theta_{2}\neq\theta_{3}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Here we make an observation for (
6.16
) when
{\theta_{2}}\neq{\theta_{3}}
. If
L\sim N_{2}\sim N_{3}
,
|\nabla_{\sigma}p_{\mathbf{\Theta}}|
does not have lower bound. However, in this case, we only consider the case
L\ll N_{3}
which makes the lower bound as in (
6.16
) when
{\theta_{2}}\neq{\theta_{3}}
.

(1) Estimates when
\theta_{1}=\theta_{3}
.
We perform an integration
by parts twice in
\eta
variable to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{{\mathbf{\Theta}},\textbf{L}}^{\textbf{N}}(s,\xi)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{K}_{1}(s,\xi)+\mathcal{K}_{2}(s,\xi)+\mathcal{K}_{3}(s,\xi),

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{1}(s,\xi)

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{s^{2}}\iint_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{3+3}}e^{isp_{\mathbf{\Theta}}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}}\textbf{k}_{1}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)\nabla_{\eta}^{2}\left[\widehat{f_{{\theta_{1}},N_{1}}}(s,\xi+\eta)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.\times\Big\langle\widehat{f_{{\theta_{3}},N_{3}}}(s,\xi+\eta+\sigma),\widehat{f_{{\theta_{2}},N_{2}}}(s,\xi+\sigma)\Big\rangle\right]d\eta d\sigma,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{2}(s,\xi)

\displaystyle:=-\frac{2}{s^{2}}\iint_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{3+3}}e^{isp_{\mathbf{\Theta}}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}}\textbf{k}_{2}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)\nabla_{\eta}\left[\widehat{f_{{\theta_{1}},N_{1}}}(s,\xi+\eta)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.\times\Big\langle\widehat{f_{{\theta_{3}},N_{3}}}(s,\xi+\eta+\sigma),\widehat{f_{{\theta_{2}},N_{2}}}(s,\xi+\sigma)\Big\rangle\right]d\eta d\sigma,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{3}(s,\xi)

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{s^{2}}\iint_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{3+3}}e^{isp_{\mathbf{\Theta}}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}}\textbf{k}_{3}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)\widehat{f_{{\theta_{1}},N_{1}}}(s,\xi+\eta)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\Big\langle\widehat{f_{{\theta_{3}},N_{3}}}(s,\xi+\eta+\sigma),\widehat{f_{{\theta_{2}},N_{2}}}(s,\xi+\sigma)\Big\rangle d\eta d\sigma,

where

\displaystyle\textbf{k}_{1}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\nabla_{\eta}{{p}_{\mathbf{\Theta}}}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}}{|\nabla_{\eta}{{p}_{\mathbf{\Theta}}}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}|^{2}}\textbf{m}_{1}(\xi,\eta,\sigma),

\displaystyle\textbf{k}_{2}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\nabla_{\eta}{{p}_{\mathbf{\Theta}}}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}}{|\nabla_{\eta}{{p}_{\mathbf{\Theta}}}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}|^{2}}\textbf{m}_{2}(\xi,\eta,\sigma),

\displaystyle\textbf{k}_{3}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)

\displaystyle:=\textbf{m}_{3}(\xi,\eta,\sigma).

Here
{\bf m}_{1},{\bf m}_{2}
, and
{\bf m}_{3}
are defined in (
6.12
) and (
6.15
), respectively.
Since
\theta_{1}=\theta_{3}
, by the mean value theorem we have

|{\nabla_{\eta}p}_{{\mathbf{\Theta}}}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)|=\left|\frac{\xi+\eta}{{\langle\xi+\eta\rangle}}-\frac{\xi+\eta+\sigma}{{\langle\xi+\eta+\sigma\rangle}}\right|\gtrsim\frac{L^{\prime}}{{\langle N_{1}\rangle}^{3}},

which leads us to the differential inequalities

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left|\nabla_{\eta}^{m}\nabla_{\sigma}^{\ell}\textbf{k}_{1}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}\right|&\lesssim L^{-2}(L^{\prime})^{-2}{\langle N_{1}\rangle}^{6}{\langle N_{1}\rangle}^{2(m+\ell)}L^{-m}(L^{\prime})^{-\ell}\\
&\lesssim L^{-2}(L^{\prime})^{-2}{\langle N_{1}\rangle}^{6}M^{\frac{48}{k}}L^{-m}(L^{\prime})^{-\ell},\\
\left|\nabla_{\eta}^{m}\nabla_{\sigma}^{\ell}\textbf{k}_{2}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}\right|&\lesssim L^{-3}(L^{\prime})^{-2}{\langle N_{1}\rangle}^{6}{\langle N_{1}\rangle}^{2(m+\ell)}L^{-m}(L^{\prime})^{-\ell}\\
&\lesssim L^{-3}(L^{\prime})^{-2}{\langle N_{1}\rangle}^{6}M^{\frac{48}{k}}L^{-m}(L^{\prime})^{-\ell},\end{aligned}

for
0\leq m,\ell\leq 4
, and the pointwise bound

(6.17)

\displaystyle\left|\textbf{k}_{3}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim L^{-4}(L^{\prime})^{-2}{\langle N_{1}\rangle}^{6}.

Applying Lemma
2.7
with
\|\mathbf{k_{1}}\|_{\rm{CM}}=L^{-2}(L^{\prime})^{-2}{\langle N_{1}\rangle}^{6}M^{\frac{48}{k}}
, we estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{({\textbf{N},\textbf{L}})\in\mathcal{A}_{4}}\left|\mathcal{K}_{1}(s,\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim M^{-2+\frac{48}{k}+\frac{30}{k}}{\langle N\rangle}^{-10}\sum_{({\textbf{N},\textbf{L}})\in\mathcal{A}_{4}}L^{-2}(L^{\prime})^{-2}{\langle N_{1}\rangle}^{6}\left\|\psi_{{\theta_{2}},N_{2}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\times\left(N_{1}^{-2}\left\|x^{2}f_{{\theta_{1}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|f_{{\theta_{3}},N_{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+N_{3}^{-2}\left\|f_{{\theta_{1}},N_{1}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|x^{2}f_{{\theta_{3}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-2+\frac{78}{k}}{\langle N\rangle}^{-10}M^{-\frac{3}{2}+2{\delta}_{0}}\sum_{({\textbf{N},\textbf{L}})\in\mathcal{A}_{4}}L^{-2}(L^{\prime})^{-2}{\langle N_{2}\rangle}^{-2}N_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\langle N_{1}\rangle}^{-4}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-\frac{7}{2}+\frac{78}{k}+2{\delta}_{0}}{\langle N\rangle}^{-10}M^{1-\frac{2}{45}}M^{\frac{2}{3}+\frac{2}{k}}M^{\frac{3}{8}-\frac{1}{60}}\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-(1+{\delta}_{0})}{\langle N\rangle}^{-10},

where we used that

L^{-\frac{2}{3}}(L^{\prime})^{-2}\leq M^{\frac{2}{3}+\frac{2}{k}}\;\mbox{ and }\;M^{-\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{30}}\lesssim L\ll N_{1}.

Similarly, we estimate by using
\|\mathbf{k_{2}}\|_{\rm{CM}}=L^{-3}(L^{\prime})^{-2}{\langle N_{1}\rangle}^{6}M^{\frac{48}{k}}

\displaystyle\sum_{({\textbf{N},\textbf{L}})\in\mathcal{A}_{4}}\left|\mathcal{K}_{2}(s,\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim M^{-2+\frac{48}{k}+\frac{30}{k}}{\langle N\rangle}^{-10}\sum_{({\textbf{N},\textbf{L}})\in\mathcal{A}_{4}}L^{-3}(L^{\prime})^{-2}{\langle N_{1}\rangle}^{6}\left\|\psi_{{\theta_{2}},N_{2}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\times\left(N_{1}^{-1}\left\|xf_{{\theta_{1}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|f_{{\theta_{3}},N_{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+N_{3}^{-1}\left\|f_{{\theta_{1}},N_{1}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|xf_{{\theta_{3}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-2+\frac{78}{k}}{\langle N\rangle}^{-10}M^{\frac{7}{4}-\frac{7}{90}}M^{\frac{2}{3}+\frac{2}{k}}M^{-\frac{3}{2}}M^{{\delta}_{0}}\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-(1+{\delta}_{0})}{\langle N\rangle}^{-10},

where we used that

L^{-\frac{7}{3}}\leq M^{\frac{7}{4}-\frac{7}{90}}\;\mbox{ and }\;L^{-\frac{2}{3}}(L^{\prime})^{-2}\leq M^{\frac{2}{3}+\frac{2}{k}}.

Using Hölder inequality and (
6.17
), we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{({\textbf{N},\textbf{L}})\in\mathcal{A}_{4}}\left|\mathcal{K}_{3}(s,\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim M^{-2}\sum_{({\textbf{N},\textbf{L}})\in\mathcal{A}_{4}}L^{-1}L^{\prime}{\langle N_{1}\rangle}^{6}\left\|\widehat{f_{{\theta_{1}},N_{1}}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\widehat{f_{{\theta_{2}},N_{2}}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\widehat{f_{{\theta_{3}},N_{3}}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-2}M^{\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{30}}{\langle N\rangle}^{-10}\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-(1+{\delta}_{0})}{\langle N\rangle}^{-10}.

(2) Estimates when
\theta_{1}\neq\theta_{3}
.

The proof proceeds similarly to the previous case. We integrate by parts twice in
\sigma
variable

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{{\mathbf{\Theta}},\textbf{L}}^{\textbf{N}}(s,\xi)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\mathcal{K}_{1}}(s,\xi)+\widetilde{\mathcal{K}_{2}}(s,\xi)+\widetilde{\mathcal{K}_{3}}(s,\xi),

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{K}_{1}}(s,\xi)

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{s^{2}}\iint_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{3+3}}e^{isp_{\mathbf{\Theta}}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}}\widetilde{\textbf{k}_{1}}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)\nabla_{\sigma}^{2}\left[\widehat{f_{{\theta_{1}},N_{1}}}(s,\xi+\eta)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.\times\Big\langle\widehat{f_{{\theta_{3}},N_{3}}}(s,\xi+\eta+\sigma),\widehat{f_{{\theta_{2}},N_{2}}}(s,\xi+\sigma)\Big\rangle\right]d\eta d\sigma,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{K}_{2}}(s,\xi)

\displaystyle:=-\frac{2}{s^{2}}\iint_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{3+3}}e^{isp_{\mathbf{\Theta}}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}}\widetilde{\textbf{k}_{2}}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)\nabla_{\sigma}\left[\widehat{f_{{\theta_{1}},N_{1}}}(s,\xi+\eta)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.\times\Big\langle\widehat{f_{{\theta_{3}},N_{3}}}(s,\xi+\eta+\sigma),\widehat{f_{{\theta_{2}},N_{2}}}(s,\xi+\sigma)\Big\rangle\right]d\eta d\sigma,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{K}_{3}}(s,\xi)

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{s^{2}}\iint_{\mathbf{\mathbb{R}}^{3+3}}e^{isp_{\mathbf{\Theta}}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}}\widetilde{\textbf{k}_{3}}(\xi,\eta,\sigma)\widehat{f_{{\theta_{1}},N_{1}}}(s,\xi+\eta)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\Big\langle\widehat{f_{{\theta_{3}},N_{3}}}(s,\xi+\eta+\sigma),\widehat{f_{{\theta_{2}},N_{2}}}(s,\xi+\sigma)\Big\rangle d\eta d\sigma,

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{\textbf{k}_{1}}(\xi,\eta,\sigma):=\left[\frac{\nabla_{\sigma}{{p}_{\mathbf{\Theta}}}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}}{|\nabla_{\sigma}{{p}_{\mathbf{\Theta}}}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}|^{2}}\right]^{2}|\eta|^{-2}\rho_{L}(\eta)\rho_{L^{\prime}}(\sigma)\rho_{N_{2}}(\xi+\sigma)\rho_{N_{3}}(\xi+\eta+\sigma),

\displaystyle\widetilde{\textbf{k}_{2}}(\xi,\eta,\sigma):=\frac{\nabla_{\sigma}{{p}_{\mathbf{\Theta}}}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}}{|\nabla_{\sigma}{{p}_{\mathbf{\Theta}}}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}|^{2}}\nabla_{\sigma}\left(\frac{\nabla_{\sigma}{{p}_{\mathbf{\Theta}}}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}}{|\nabla_{\sigma}{{p}_{\mathbf{\Theta}}}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}|^{2}}\rho_{L^{\prime}}(\sigma)\right)|\eta|^{-2}

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt\times\rho_{L}(\eta)\rho_{N_{2}}(\xi+\sigma)\rho_{N_{3}}(\xi+\eta+\sigma),

\displaystyle\widetilde{\textbf{k}_{3}}(\xi,\eta,\sigma):=\nabla_{\sigma}\widetilde{\textbf{k}_{2}}(\xi,\eta,\sigma).

As observed in (
6.16
),
|\nabla_{\sigma}{{p}_{\mathbf{\Theta}}}|
has different lower bounds depending on the sign of
\theta_{2}
and
\theta_{3}
.
One can verify that, however, if
(\mathbf{N},\mathbf{L})\in\mathcal{A}_{4}
, the lower bounds of
|\nabla_{\sigma}{{p}_{\mathbf{\Theta}}}|
when
\theta_{2}\neq\theta_{3}
are always greater than those when
\theta_{2}=\theta_{3}

\displaystyle N_{2}\langle N_{2}\rangle^{-1}\gtrsim L\langle N_{2}\rangle^{-3}.

Hence, we suffice to treat the latter case, when
\theta_{2}=\theta_{3}
.
Using (
6.16
), we can show the pointwise bounds of the multipliers

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{\textbf{k}_{1}}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim L^{-4}{\langle N_{2}\rangle}^{6},

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{\textbf{k}_{2}}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim L^{-4}(L^{\prime})^{-1}M^{\frac{6}{k}}{\langle N_{2}\rangle}^{6},

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{\textbf{k}_{3}}{(\xi,\eta,\sigma)}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim L^{-4}(L^{\prime})^{-2}M^{\frac{6}{k}}{\langle N_{2}\rangle}^{6}.

Then, we estimate by the Hölder inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{({\textbf{N},\textbf{L}})\in\mathcal{A}_{4}}\left|\widetilde{\mathcal{K}_{1}}(s,\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim M^{-2}\sum_{({\textbf{N},\textbf{L}})\in\mathcal{A}_{4}}L^{-4}{\langle N_{2}\rangle}^{6}L^{3}\left\|\widehat{f_{{\theta_{1}},N_{1}}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\times\left(N_{2}^{-2}\left\|x^{2}f_{{\theta_{2}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|f_{{\theta_{3}},N_{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+N_{3}^{-2}\left\|f_{{\theta_{1}},N_{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|x^{2}f_{{\theta_{3}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-2+2{\delta}_{0}}\sum_{({\textbf{N},\textbf{L}})\in\mathcal{A}_{4}}L^{-1}(L^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\langle N_{3}\rangle}^{-14}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-2+{\delta}_{0}}M^{\frac{15}{24}-\frac{1}{36}}M^{\frac{1}{6}+\frac{1}{2k}}{\langle N\rangle}^{-10}\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-(1+{\delta}_{0})}{\langle N\rangle}^{-10},

and

\displaystyle\sum_{({\textbf{N},\textbf{L}})\in\mathcal{A}_{4}}\left|\widetilde{\mathcal{K}_{2}}(s,\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim M^{-2+\frac{6}{k}}\sum_{({\textbf{N},\textbf{L}})\in\mathcal{A}_{4}}L^{-4}(L^{\prime})^{-1}{\langle N_{2}\rangle}^{6}L^{3}\left\|\widehat{f_{{\theta_{1}},N_{1}}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\times\left(N_{2}^{-1}\left\|xf_{{\theta_{2}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|f_{{\theta_{3}},N_{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+N_{3}^{-1}\left\|f_{{\theta_{1}},N_{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|xf_{{\theta_{3}}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-2+\frac{6}{k}}M^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{90}}M^{\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{k}}M^{{\delta}_{0}}{\langle N\rangle}^{-10}\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-(1+{\delta}_{0})}{\langle N\rangle}^{-10}.

where we used that

L^{\prime}\ll N_{2}\sim N_{3},\;L^{-1}\leq M^{\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{30}},\mbox{ and }\;L^{-\frac{1}{3}}(L^{\prime})^{-1}\leq M^{\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{k}}.

In a similar way, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{({\textbf{N},\textbf{L}})\in\mathcal{A}_{4}}\left|\widetilde{\mathcal{K}_{3}}(s,\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim M^{-2+\frac{6}{k}}\sum_{({\textbf{N},\textbf{L}})\in\mathcal{A}_{4}}L^{-4}(L^{\prime})^{-2}{\langle N_{2}\rangle}^{6}L^{3}(L^{\prime})^{3}\left\|\widehat{f_{{\theta_{1}},N_{1}}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\widehat{f_{{\theta_{2}},N_{2}}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\widehat{f_{{\theta_{3}},N_{3}}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-2+\frac{6}{k}}M^{\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{30}}{\langle N\rangle}^{-10}\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}^{3}M^{-(1+{\delta}_{0})}{\langle N\rangle}^{-10}.

This concludes the proof of (
6.8
), and thus of Proposition
4.2
.

Construtions and bimodules of BiHom-alternative and BiHom-Jordan algebras
Sylvain Attan, Ismail Laraiedh

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to give some construction results and examples of BiHom-alternative and BiHom-Jordan algebras. Next, we define BiHom-alternative and BiHom-Jordan (bi)modules and we prove that from a given BiHom-alternative and BiHom-Jordan bimodules, a sequence of this kind of bimodules can be constructed. Some relations between Bihom-associative, BiHom-alternative and BiHom-Jordan bimodules are given.

17A30, 17B10, 17C50, 17D15.

BiHom-alternative algebras, BiHom-Jordan algebras, BiHom-modules, Bimodules.

Introduction

As generalization of associative algebras, alternative algebras are algebras whose associator is an alternating function. Jordan algebras is another important class of algebras and were introduced in Physics context by
Pascual Jordan in 1933 to provide a new formalism for Quantum Mechanics
and developed from the algebraic viewpoint by Jacobson in
[
15
]
.
As associative algebras are related to Jordan algebras, alternative algebras are closely related to Jordan algebras
[
10
,
16
,
26
,
29
]
.
The study of bimodule (or representation) of Jordan algebras was initiated by N. Jacobson
[
13
]
and the one of alternative algebras was considered by Schafer
[
27
]
.

Algebras where the identities defining the structure are twisted by a homomorphism are called Hom-algebras.
They started from Hom-Lie algebras introduced and discussed in

[
11
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
23
]
, motivated by quasi-deformations of Lie algebras of vector fields, in particular q-deformations of Witt and Virasoro algebras.
Hom-associative algebras were introduced in
[
25
]
while Hom-alternative and Hom-Jordan algebras are introduced in
[
22
,
32
]

as twisted generalizations of alternative and Jordan algebra respectively.
Dualizing Hom-associative algebras, one can define Hom-coassociative
coalgebras, Hom-bialgebras and Hom-Hopf algebras which were introduced in

[
24
,
23
]
, see also
[
4
,
33
,
34
,
35
]
.

(Bi)modules over an ordinary algebra has been extended to those of
Hom-algebras in many works
[
28
,
30
,
31
]
, in particular Hom-Jordan and Hom-alternative bimodules are introduced and studied in
[
2
,
3
]
.

In
[
4
]
the authors gave a new look to Hom-type algebras from a category theoretical point of view. A generalization of this approach
led the authors of
[
7
]
to introduce BiHom-algebras, which are algebras where the identities defining the structure are twisted by two homomorphisms
\alpha
and
\beta
.
This class of algebras can be viewed as an extension of the class of Hom-algebras since, when the two linear maps of a BiHom-algebra are the same, it reduces to a Hom-algebra. These
algebraic structures include BiHom-associative algebras, BiHom-Lie algebras and BiHom-bialgebras. More applications of BiHom-algebras, BiHom-Lie superalgebras, BiHom-Lie colour
algebras and BiHom-Novikov algebras can be
found in
[
1
,
12
,
8
,
9
,
5
,
6
,
20
,
21
]
.

The aim of this paper is to extend the work done in
[
2
]
in BiHom-algebras setting. More precisely, we introduce
BiHom-alternative bimodules and BiHom-Jordan bimodules and discuss about some findings. The paper is
organized as follows. Section 2 contains some necessary important basic notions and notations related to BiHom-algebras, BiHom-alternative algebras, BiHom-Jordan algebras
and modules over BiHom-associative algebras.
Section 3 presents some useful methods for constructions of BiHom-alternative algebras and BiHom-Jordan algebras. In section 4,
we give definitions and some properties of BiHom-alternative and BiHom-Jordan (bi)modules and we prove that from a given
BiHom-alternative algebra and BiHom-Jordan algebra bimodules, a sequence of this kind of bimodules can be constructed.

Throughout this paper

is an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
and
A

\mathbb{K}

Definition 2.1
.

A BiHom-module is a pair
(M,\alpha_{M},\beta_{M})
consisting of a
\mathbb{K}
-module
M
and
a linear self-maps
\alpha_{M},\beta_{M}:M\longrightarrow M
such that
\alpha_{M}\beta_{M}=\beta_{M}\alpha_{M}.
A morphism

f:(M,\alpha_{M},\beta_{M})\rightarrow(N,\alpha_{N},\beta_{N})
of BiHom-modules is a linear map

f:M\longrightarrow N
such that
f\alpha_{M}=\alpha_{N}f
and

f\beta_{M}=\beta_{N}f.

Definition 2.2
.

[
7
]

A BiHom-algebra is a quadruple
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
in which
(A,\alpha,\beta)
is a BiHom-module,
\mu:A^{\otimes 2}\rightarrow A
is a linear map.
The BiHom-algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha;\beta)
is said to be multiplicative if
\alpha\circ\mu=\mu\circ\alpha^{\otimes 2}
and
\beta\circ\mu=\mu\circ\beta^{\otimes 2}
(multiplicativity).

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
be a BiHom-algebra. Then

1.

A BiHom-subalgebra of
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
is a linear subspace
H
of
A
, which is closed for the multiplication
\mu
and invariant by
\alpha
and
\beta
, that is,
\mu(x,y)\in H,~\alpha(x)\in H
and
\beta(x)\in H
for all
x,y\in H
. If furthermore
\mu(a,b)\in H
and
\mu(b,a)\in H
for all
(a,b)\in A\times H,
then
H
is called a two-sided BiHom ideal of
A
.

2.

(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
is said to be regular if
\alpha
and
\beta
are algebra automorphisms.

3.

(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
is said to be involutive if
\alpha
and
\beta
are two involutions, that is
\alpha^{2}=\beta^{2}=id
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
and
(A^{{}^{\prime}},\mu^{{}^{\prime}},\alpha^{{}^{\prime}},\beta^{{}^{\prime}})
be two BiHom- algebras. Then
a homomorphism
f:A\longrightarrow A^{{}^{\prime}}
is said to be a BiHom-algebra morphism if the following conditions hold:

\begin{array}[]{llll}&&f\circ\mu=\mu^{{}^{\prime}}\circ(f\otimes f),\\
&&f\circ\alpha=\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}\circ f,\\
&&f\circ\beta=\beta^{{}^{\prime}}\circ f.\end{array}

Denote by
\Gamma_{f}=\{x+f(x);~~x\in A\}\subset A\oplus A^{\prime}
the graph of a linear map
f:A\longrightarrow A^{{}^{\prime}}
.

Definition 2.5
.

[
20
]
Let
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
be a BiHom-algebra and let
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
. Let
R:A\rightarrow A
be a linear map satisfying

\displaystyle\mu(R(x),R(y))=R(\mu(R(x),y)+\mu(x,R(y))+\lambda\mu(x,y)),~~~\mbox{for any $x,y\in A$}.

Then

Definition 2.6
.

[
7
]

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
be a BiHom-algebra.
A BiHom-associator of
A
is the trilinear map
as_{\alpha,\beta}:A^{\otimes 3}\longrightarrow A
defined by

as_{\alpha,\beta}=\mu\circ(\mu\otimes\beta-\alpha\otimes\mu).

(2.1)

In terms of elements, the map
as_{\alpha,\beta}
is given by

as_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y,z)=\mu(\mu(x,y),\beta(z))-\mu(\alpha(x),\mu(y,z)),\ \forall x,y,z\in A.

Note that if
\alpha=\beta=id

as(,,).

Definition 2.7
.

A BiHom-associative algebra
[
7
]
is a multiplicative Bihom-algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)

satisfying the following BiHom-associativity condition:

as_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y,z)=0,\ \text{for all}\ x,y,z\in A.\

(2.2)

Clearly, a Hom-associative algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha)
can be regarded as a BiHom-associative
algebra

.

An important class of BiHom-algebras that is considered here is the one of BiHom-alternative algebras.
These algebras have been introduced By Chtioui et al in
[

.

Definition 2.8
.

A left BiHom-alternative algebra
(
resp. right BiHom-alternative algebra
)
is a multiplicative Bihom-algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
satisfying the left BiHom-alternative identity,

as_{\alpha,\beta}(\beta(x),\alpha(y),z)+as_{\alpha,\beta}(\beta(y),\alpha(x),z)=0,

(2.3)

respectively, the right BiHom-alternative identity,

as_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\beta(y),\alpha(z))+as_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\beta(z),\alpha(y))=0,

(2.4)

for all
x,y,z\in A
.
A BiHom-alternative algebra is the one which is both a left and right BiHom-alternative algebra.

Remark 2.9

Any BiHom-associative algebra is a BiHom-alternative algebra.

As BiHom-alternative algebras, BiHom-Jordan algebras are fundamental objects of this paper. They appear as cousins
of BiHom-alternative algebras and these two BiHom-algebras are related as (Hom-)Jordan and (Hom-)alternative algebras.

Definition 2.11
.

[
5
]

A BiHom algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
is called a BiHom-Jordan algebra if:

1.

\mu\Big(\beta(x),\alpha(y)\Big)=\mu\Big(\beta(y),\alpha(x)\Big),\ \forall x,y\in A
(BiHom-commutativity condition),

2.

as_{\alpha,\beta}\Big(\mu\big(\beta^{2}(x),\alpha\beta(x)\big),\alpha^{2}\beta(y),\alpha^{3}(x)\Big)=0,\ \forall x,y\in A
(BiHom-Jordan identity).

Note that if
\alpha=\beta=id
, we obtain a Jordan algebra. Then we conclude that if
(A,\mu)
is a Jordan algebra,
(A,\mu,id,id)
can be viewed as a BiHom-Jordan algebra.

where
\circlearrowleft_{x,w,z}

3
Constructions of BiHom-alternative and BiHom-Jordan algebras

In this section, we provide
some constructions results of BiHom-alternative algebras and BiHom-Jordan algebras. Some examples are also given.

3.1
Constructions of BiHom-alternative algebras

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
be a BiHom-alternative algebra and
I
be a two-sided BiHom-ideal of
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
.
Then
(A/I,\overline{\mu},\overline{\alpha},\overline{\beta})
is a BiHom-alternative algebra where
\overline{\mu}(\overline{x},\overline{y})=\overline{\mu(x,y)}
,

\overline{\alpha}(\overline{x})=\overline{\alpha(x)}
and
\overline{\beta}(\overline{x})=\overline{\beta(x)}
, for all
\overline{x},\overline{y}\in A/I

Proof.

First, note that the multiplicativity of
\overline{\alpha}
and
\overline{\beta}
with respect to
\overline{\mu}
, follows from the one of
\alpha
and
\beta
with respect to
\mu
.
Next, pick
\overline{x},\overline{y}\in A/I
. Then the left and right BiHom-alternative identity in
(A/I,\overline{\mu},\overline{\alpha},\overline{\beta})
are proved as follows

\begin{array}[]{llllllll}as_{\overline{\alpha},\overline{\beta}}(\overline{\beta}(\overline{x}),\overline{\alpha}(\overline{x}),\overline{y})&=&\overline{\mu}(\overline{\mu}(\overline{\beta}(\overline{x}),\overline{\alpha}(\overline{x})),\overline{\beta}(\overline{y}))-\overline{\mu}(\overline{\alpha}\overline{\beta}(\overline{x}),\overline{\mu}(\overline{\alpha}(\overline{x}),\overline{y}))\\
&=&\overline{\mu(\mu(\beta(x),\alpha(x)),\beta(y))-\mu(\alpha\beta(x),\mu(\alpha(x),y))}\\
&=&\overline{as_{\alpha,\beta}(\beta(x),\alpha(x),y)}=\overline{0}~~(\textsl{by ~(\ref{is1})~ in}~A)\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{llllllll}as_{\overline{\alpha},\overline{\beta}}(\overline{x},\overline{\beta}(\overline{y}),\overline{\alpha}(\overline{y}))&=&\overline{\mu}(\overline{\mu}(\overline{x},\overline{\beta}(\overline{y})),\overline{\alpha}\overline{\beta}(\overline{y}))-\overline{\mu}(\overline{\alpha}(\overline{x}),\overline{\mu}(\overline{\beta}(\overline{y}),\overline{\alpha}(\overline{y})))\\
&=&\overline{\mu(\mu(x,\beta(y)),\alpha\beta(y))-\mu(\alpha(x),\mu(\beta(y),\alpha(y)))}\\
&=&\overline{as_{\alpha,\beta}(x,\beta(y),\alpha(y))}=\overline{0}~~(\textsl{by ~(\ref{is2})~ in}~A).\end{array}

Then
(A/I,\overline{\mu},\overline{\alpha},\overline{\beta})
is a BiHom-alternative algebra.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Given two BiHom-alternative algebras
(A,\mu_{A},\alpha_{A},\beta_{A})
and
(B,\mu_{B},\alpha_{B},\beta_{B}),
there is a BiHom-alternative algebra
(A\oplus B,\mu_{A\oplus B},\alpha_{A}+\alpha_{B},\beta_{A}+\beta_{B}),
where the bilinear map
\mu_{A\oplus B}:(A\oplus B)^{\times 2}\longrightarrow(A\oplus B)
is given by

\begin{array}[]{llll}\mu_{A\oplus B}(a_{1}+b_{1},a_{2}+b_{2})=\mu_{A}(a_{1},a_{2})+\mu_{B}(b_{1},b_{2}),\forall\ a_{1},a_{2}\in A,\ \forall\ b_{1},b_{2}\in B,\end{array}

and the linear maps
\beta=\beta_{A}+\beta_{B},~\alpha=\alpha_{A}+\alpha_{B}:(A\oplus B)\longrightarrow(A\oplus B)
are given by

\begin{array}[]{lll}(\alpha_{A}+\alpha_{B})(a+b)&=&\alpha_{A}(a)+\alpha_{B}(b),\\
(\beta_{A}+\beta_{B})(a+b)&=&\beta_{A}(a)+\beta_{B}(b),~~\forall\ (a,b)\in A\times B.\end{array}

Proof.

Since
\alpha_{A}\circ\beta_{A}=\beta_{A}\circ\alpha_{A}
and
\alpha_{B}\circ\beta_{B}=\beta_{B}\circ\alpha_{B}
, we have

(\alpha_{A}+\alpha_{B})\circ(\beta_{A}\circ\beta_{B})=(\beta_{A}\circ\beta_{B})\circ(\alpha_{A}+\alpha_{B})
.

First,
(\alpha_{A}+\alpha_{B})
is multiplicative with respect to
\mu_{A\oplus B}.
Indeed,
\forall(a_{1},b_{1}),~(a_{2},b_{2})\in A\times B

\begin{array}[]{lllllll}&&(\alpha_{A}+\alpha_{B})\circ\mu_{A\oplus B}(a_{1}+b_{1},a_{2}+b_{2})\\
&=&(\alpha_{A}+\alpha_{B})(\mu_{A}(a_{1},a_{2})+\mu_{B}(b_{1},b_{2}))\\
&=&\alpha_{A}\circ\mu_{A}(a_{1},a_{2})+\alpha_{B}\circ\mu_{B}(b_{1},b_{2})\\
&=&\mu_{A}(\alpha_{A}(a_{1}),\alpha_{A}(a_{2}))+\mu_{B}(\alpha_{B}(b_{1}),\alpha_{B}(b_{2})\\
&=&\mu_{A\oplus B}(\alpha_{A}(a_{1})+\alpha_{B}(b_{1}),\alpha_{A}(a_{2})+\alpha_{B}(b_{2}))\\
&=&\mu_{A\oplus B}((\alpha_{A}+\alpha_{B})(a_{1}+b_{1}),(\alpha_{A}+\alpha_{B})(a_{2}+b_{2})).\end{array}

Similarly, we have
(\beta_{A}+\beta_{B})\circ\mu_{A\oplus B}(a_{1}+b_{1},a_{2}+b_{2})=\mu_{A\oplus B}((\beta_{A}+\beta_{B})(a_{1}+b_{1}),(\beta_{A}+\beta_{B})(a_{2}+b_{2}))
.

Secondly, we prove the left BiHom-alternative identity for
A\oplus B
as follows

\begin{array}[]{llllll}&&as_{\alpha,\beta}((\beta_{A}+\beta_{B})(a_{1}+b_{1}),(\alpha_{A}+\alpha_{B})(a_{1}+b_{1}),a_{2}+b_{2})\\
&=&\mu_{A\oplus B}(\mu_{A\oplus B}((\beta_{A}+\beta_{B})(a_{1}+b_{1}),(\alpha_{A}+\alpha_{B})(a_{1}+b_{1})),(\beta_{A}+\beta_{B})(a_{2}+b_{2}))\\
&&-\mu_{A\oplus B}((\alpha_{A}+\alpha_{B})(\beta_{A}+\beta_{B})(a_{1}+b_{1}),\mu_{A\oplus B}((\alpha_{A}+\alpha_{B})(a_{1}+b_{1}),a_{2}+b_{2}))\\
&=&\mu_{A\oplus B}(\mu_{A\oplus B}(\beta_{A}(a_{1})+\beta_{B}(b_{1}),\alpha_{A}(a_{1})+\alpha_{B}(b_{1})),\beta_{A}(a_{2})+\beta_{B}(b_{2}))\\
&&-\mu_{A\oplus B}(\alpha_{A}\beta_{A}(a_{1})+\beta_{B}\beta_{B}(b_{1}),\mu_{A\oplus B}(\alpha_{A}(a_{1})+\alpha_{B}(b_{1}),a_{2}+b_{2}))\\
&=&\mu_{A\oplus B}(\mu_{A}(\beta_{A}(a_{1})+\alpha_{A}(a_{1}))+\mu_{B}(\beta_{B}(b_{1}),\alpha_{B}(b_{1})),(\beta_{A}(a_{2})+\beta_{B}(b_{2}))\\
&&-\mu_{A\oplus B}(\alpha_{A}\beta_{A}(a_{1})+\alpha_{B}\beta_{B}(b_{1}),\mu_{A}(\alpha_{A}(a_{1}),a_{2})+\mu_{B}(\alpha_{B}(b_{1}),b_{2}))\\
&=&\mu_{A}(\mu_{A}(\beta_{A}(a_{1}),\alpha_{A}(a_{1})),\beta_{A}(a_{2}))+\mu_{B}(\mu_{B}(\beta_{B}(b_{1}),\alpha_{B}(b_{1})),\beta_{B}(b_{2}))\\
&&-\mu_{A}(\alpha_{A}\beta_{A}(a_{1}),\mu_{A}(\alpha_{A}(a_{1}),a_{2}))-\mu_{B}(\alpha_{B}\beta_{B}(b_{1}),\mu_{B}(\alpha_{B}(b_{1}),b_{2}))\\

Similarly, we prove the right BiHom-alternative identity as follows:

&&-\mu_{A\oplus B}(\alpha_{A}(a_{1})+\alpha_{B}(b_{1}),\mu_{A}(\beta_{A}(a_{1}),\alpha_{A}(a_{2}))+\mu_{B}(\beta_{B}(b_{2}),\alpha_{B}(b_{2})))\\

Hence

.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
be a BiHom alternative algebra,
\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}:A\longrightarrow A
be two algebra homomorphisms
such that any two of the maps
\alpha,\beta,\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}

First it is easy to see that,
\forall x,y\in A

\begin{array}[]{lllll}&&(\alpha\circ\alpha^{\prime})\circ\mu_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}(x,y)=\mu_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}(\alpha\circ\alpha^{\prime}(x),\alpha\circ\alpha^{\prime}(y)),\\
&&(\beta\circ\beta^{\prime})\circ\mu_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}(x,y)=\mu_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}((\beta\circ\beta^{\prime})(x),(\beta\circ\beta^{\prime})(y)).\end{array}

\mu(a_{1}+b_{1},a_{2}+b_{2})=\mu_{A}(a_{1},a_{2})+\mu_{B}(a_{2},b_{2}),\mbox{ $\forall\ (a_{1},a_{2})\in A^{\times 2}$\ and \ $\forall\ (b_{1},b_{2})\in B^{\times 2}$}

and the linear maps
\alpha,\beta\ :A\oplus B\longrightarrow A\oplus B
are given by

\displaystyle\alpha(a+b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{A}(a)+\alpha_{B}(b),

\displaystyle\beta(a+b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta_{A}(a)+\beta_{B}(b),\mbox{ $\forall\ (a,b)\in A\times B$}.

Proof.

Note that the Multiplicativity and BiHom-commutativity in
(A\oplus B,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
follows from the those in
(A,\mu_{A},\alpha_{A},\beta_{A})
and

(B,\mu_{B},\alpha_{B},\beta_{B})
. Next, by a direct computation, we get for all
(x_{1},y_{1})\in A^{\times 2}
and
(x_{2},y_{2})\in B^{\times 2}
:

\displaystyle as_{\alpha,\beta}(\mu(\beta^{2}(x_{1}+y_{1}),\alpha\beta(x_{1}+y_{1})),\alpha^{2}\beta(x_{2}+y_{2}),\alpha^{3}(x_{1}+y_{1}))

\displaystyle=as_{\alpha_{A},\beta_{A}}(\mu_{A}(\beta_{A}^{2}(x_{1}),\alpha_{A}\beta_{A}(x_{1})),\alpha_{A}^{2}\beta_{A}(y_{1}),\alpha_{A}^{3}(x_{1}))

\displaystyle+as_{\alpha_{B},\beta_{B}}(\mu_{B}(\beta_{B}^{2}(x_{2}),\alpha_{B}\beta_{B}(x_{2})),\alpha_{A}^{2}\beta_{A}(y_{2}),\alpha_{B}^{3}(x_{2}))

Thus the BiHom-Jordan identity in
(A\oplus B,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
follows from the one in
(A,\mu_{A},\alpha_{A},\beta_{A})
and

(B,\mu_{B},\alpha_{B},\beta_{B}).
∎

Proposition 3.11
.

A linear map
\phi:(A,\mu_{A},\alpha_{A},\beta_{A})\longrightarrow(B,\mu_{B},\alpha_{B},\beta_{B})
is a morphism of BiHom-Jordan algebras if and only if its
graph of
\Gamma_{\phi}\subseteq A\oplus B
is a BiHom-subalgebra of

(A\oplus B,\mu=\mu_{A}+\mu_{B},\alpha=\alpha_{A}+\alpha_{B},\beta=\beta_{A}+\beta_{B}).

Proof.

Suppose that
\phi:(A,\mu_{A},\alpha_{A},\beta_{A})\longrightarrow(B,\mu_{B},\alpha_{B},\beta_{B})
is a morphism of BiHom-Jordan algebras. Then for all
(a,b)\in A^{\times 2}:

\displaystyle\mu(a+\phi(a),b+\phi(b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{A}(a,b)+\mu_{B}(\phi(a),\phi(b))=\mu_{A}(a,b)+\phi\mu_{A}(a,b).

Thus
\Gamma_{\phi}
is closed under the bilinear map
\mu.
Furthermore, since
\phi\alpha_{A}=\alpha_{B}\phi
and
\phi\beta_{A}=\beta_{B}\phi
we have

\displaystyle\alpha(a+\phi(a))=\alpha_{A}(a)+\alpha_{B}(\phi(a))=\alpha_{A}(a)+\phi\alpha_{A}(a)

\displaystyle\beta(a+\phi(a))=\beta_{A}(a)+\beta_{B}(\phi(a))=\beta_{A}(a)+\phi\beta_{A}(a)

which imply that
\Gamma
is closed under
\alpha
and
\beta.
It follows that
\Gamma_{\phi}
is a BiHom-subalgebra of

(A\oplus B,\mu,\alpha,\beta).

Conversely suppose that that
\Gamma_{\phi}
is a BiHom-subalgebra of

(A\oplus B,\mu,\alpha,\beta).
Then

\displaystyle\mu(a+\phi(a),b+\phi(a))=\mu_{A}(a,b)+\mu_{B}(\phi(a),\phi(b))\in\Gamma_{\phi}

which implies that
\mu_{b}(\phi(a),\phi(b))=\phi\mu_{A}(a,b).
Furthermore

\alpha(\Gamma_{\phi})\subseteq\Gamma_{\phi}
and

\beta(\Gamma_{\phi})\subseteq\Gamma_{\phi}
imply

\displaystyle\alpha(a+\phi(a))=\alpha_{A}(a)+\alpha_{B}(\phi(a))\in\Gamma_{\phi}\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle\beta(a+\phi(a))=\beta_{A}(a)+\beta_{B}(\phi(a))\in\Gamma_{\phi}

which are equivalent to conditions
\alpha_{B}\phi(a)=\alpha_{A}\phi(a)
and

\beta_{B}\phi(a)=\beta_{A}\phi(a)
that is

\alpha_{B}\phi=\alpha_{A}\phi
and

\beta_{B}\phi=\beta_{A}\phi.
Therefore,

\phi:(A,\mu_{A},\alpha_{A},\beta_{A})\longrightarrow(B,\mu_{B},\alpha_{B},\beta_{B})
is a morphism of BiHom-Jordan algebras.∎

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)
be a regular BiHom-associative algebra. Then
A^{+}=(A,\mu^{\prime},\alpha,\beta)
is a BiHom-Jordan algebra where

\mu^{\prime}(x,y)=\mu(x,y)+\mu(\alpha^{-1}\beta(y),\beta^{-1}\alpha(x)),
for all
x,y\in A.

Proof.

It is easy to see that,
\forall x,y\in A

\begin{array}[]{lllll}&&\alpha\circ\mu^{\prime}(x,y)=\mu^{\prime}(\alpha(x),\alpha(y))\\
&&\beta\circ\mu^{\prime}(x,y)=\mu^{\prime}(\beta(x),\beta(y)),\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{lllllll}\mu^{\prime}(\beta(x),\alpha(y))&=&\mu(\beta(x),\alpha(y))+\mu(\alpha^{-1}\beta(\alpha(y)),\alpha\beta^{-1}(\beta(x))\\

Now, we prove the BiHom-Jordan identity, let denote the BiHom-assoiator in

A^{+}
by

Then for all

&&-\mu^{\prime}(\mu(\alpha\beta^{2}(x),\alpha^{2}\beta(x))+\mu(\alpha\beta^{2}(x),\alpha^{2}\beta(x)),\mu(\alpha^{2}\beta(y),\alpha^{3}(x))+\mu(\alpha^{2}\beta(x)),\alpha^{3}(y)))\\

&&-2\mu^{\prime}(\mu(\alpha\beta^{2}(x),\alpha^{2}\beta(x)),\mu(\alpha^{2}\beta(y),\alpha^{3}(x))-2\mu^{\prime}(\mu(\alpha\beta^{2}(x),\alpha^{2}\beta(x)),\mu(\alpha^{2}\beta(x),\alpha^{3}(y))\\

4
BiHom-alternative algebra and BiHom-Jordan bimodules

In this section, we give the defnition of BiHom-alternative and BiHom-Jordan (bi)modules. we construct several
different bimodules of BiHom-alternative BiHom-Jordan algebras. It is also proved that a direct sum of a BiHom-Jordan algebra and a bimodule over this BiHom-algebra is a BiHom-Jordan
algebra called a split null extension of the considered BiHom-algebra. Furthermore, relations between
BiHom-associative bimodules, BiHom-alternative and BiHom-Jordan bimodules and some examples are given.

4.1
BiHom-alternative bimodules

In this subsection, we study the BiHom-alternative bimodules.
First, we start by the following definitions.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(A,\mu,\alpha,\beta)

1.

A left BiHom-alternative
A

A

\begin{array}[]{lll}as_{V_{\phi,\psi}}(v,\beta(x),\alpha(y))=-as_{V_{\phi,\psi}}(v,\beta(y),\alpha(x)),~~~~\textrm{for}~\textrm{all}~x,y\in A~\textrm{and}~v\in V.\end{array}

Proposition 4.5
.

If

(B,\alpha_{B},\beta_{B})

A
-bimodule via

\rho_{l}(a,b)=\mu_{B}(f(a),b)

\rho_{r}(b,a)=\mu_{B}(b,f(a))

Proof.

First, we have

\begin{array}[]{lllll}\alpha_{B}\rho_{l}&=&\alpha_{B}\mu_{B}(f\otimes Id_{B})\\

&=&\mu_{B}(f\circ\alpha_{A}\otimes\alpha_{B})\\
&=&\rho_{l}\circ(\alpha_{A}\otimes\alpha_{B})\end{array}

Similarly, we get that
\beta_{B}\rho_{l}=\rho_{l}\circ(\beta_{A}\otimes\beta_{B}),~\alpha_{B}\rho_{r}=\rho_{r}\circ(\alpha_{B}\otimes\alpha_{A})
and
\beta_{B}\rho_{r}=\rho_{r}\circ(\beta_{B}\otimes\beta_{A})
.
Thus
\rho_{l}
and
\rho_{r}
are morphisms of BiHom-modules.

Next, for any elements
a\in A
and
b\in B
, then there exist

such that

(i.e.

\begin{array}[]{lllll}&&as_{\alpha_{B},\beta_{B}}(\beta_{A}(a),\alpha_{A}(a),b)=as_{\alpha_{B},\beta_{B}}(\beta_{A}(a),\alpha_{A}(a),f(b^{\prime}))\\

Definition 4.6
.

An abelian extension of BiHom-alternative algebras is a short exact sequence of BiHom-alternative algebras

is a trivial BiHom-alternative algebra,

then the abelian extension is said to be split and

.

Abstract
The proximal stochastic gradient method ( 𝖯𝗋𝗈𝗑 ​ - ​ 𝖲𝖦𝖣 \mathsf{Prox}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD} ) is one of the state-of-the-art approaches for stochastic composite-type problems. In contrast to its deterministic counterpart, 𝖯𝗋𝗈𝗑 ​ - ​ 𝖲𝖦𝖣 \mathsf{Prox}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD} has been found to have difficulties with the correct identification of underlying substructures (such as supports, low rank patterns, or active constraints) and it does not possess a finite-time manifold identification property. Existing solutions rely on convexity assumptions or on the additional usage of variance reduction techniques. In this paper, we address these limitations and present a simple variant of 𝖯𝗋𝗈𝗑 ​ - ​ 𝖲𝖦𝖣 \mathsf{Prox}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD} based on Robinson’s normal map. The proposed normal map-based proximal stochastic gradient method ( 𝖭𝗈𝗋𝗆 ​ - ​ 𝖲𝖦𝖣 \mathsf{Norm}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD} ) is shown to converge globally, i.e., accumulation points of the generated iterates correspond to stationary points almost surely. In addition, we establish complexity bounds for 𝖭𝗈𝗋𝗆 ​ - ​ 𝖲𝖦𝖣 \mathsf{Norm}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD} that match the known results for 𝖯𝗋𝗈𝗑 ​ - ​ 𝖲𝖦𝖣 \mathsf{Prox}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD} and we prove that 𝖭𝗈𝗋𝗆 ​ - ​ 𝖲𝖦𝖣 \mathsf{Norm}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD} can almost surely identify active manifolds in finite-time in a general nonconvex setting. Our derivations are built on almost sure iterate convergence guarantees and utilize analysis techniques based on the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz inequality.

In this work, we propose and investigate a novel normal map-based proximal stochastic gradient method for the composite problem

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}~\psi(x):=f(x)+\varphi(x),

(1)

\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(-\infty,\infty]

\mathrm{dom\\
}(\varphi):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\varphi(x)<\infty\}

The model

Since a full evaluation of the function and gradient values

This integral mechanism, pioneered by Robbins and Monro

A normal map-based proximal stochastic gradient method (
\mathsf{Norm}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD}
)

Let
\lambda>0
and
\{\alpha_{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{++}
be given. Choose
z^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and set
x^{0}=\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(z^{0})
.

For

k=0,1,\dots

do:

z^{k+1}=z^{k}-\alpha_{k}(g^{k}+\lambda^{-1}(z^{k}-x^{k}))\quad\text{and}\quad x^{k+1}=\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(z^{k+1}).

In each iteration,
\mathsf{Norm}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD}
generates one stochastic gradient
g^{k}\approx\nabla f(x^{k})
and performs one proximal step. Thus, the computational costs of
\mathsf{Norm}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD}
and
\mathsf{Prox}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD}
are essentially identical. We now summarize some of the fundamental properties of
\mathsf{Norm}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD}
and our core contributions:

•

(Stationarity)
The design of
\mathsf{Norm}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD}
is based on Robinson’s
normal map

[
72
]

F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z):=\nabla f(x)+\lambda^{-1}(z-x)\quad\text{where}\quad x=\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(z),\quad\lambda>0.

The normal map is connected to the standard stationarity measure
\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial\psi(x))
in the sense that the condition
\|F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z)\|\leq\varepsilon
implies
\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial\psi(\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(z)))\leq\varepsilon
(cf
.

Section

1.2

\mathsf{Norm}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD}
.

•

(Complexity)
We derive complexity bounds for
\mathsf{Norm}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD}
in expectation and in terms of the normal map that align with the existing results for
\mathsf{Prox}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD}

[
22
]

•

(Global convergence)
Under
(H.1)
–

and
(S.1)

1.1
Designing
\mathsf{Norm}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD}

To motivate
\mathsf{Norm}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD}
, we first revisit the deterministic proximal gradient method and express it in a form that separates the gradient and the proximal step. Specifically, the basic update of
\mathsf{Prox}\text{-}\mathsf{GD}
can be written as

\left[\begin{aligned} z^{k+1}&=x^{k}-\lambda\nabla f(x^{k})\\
x^{k+1}&=\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(z^{k+1})\end{aligned}\right.\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\left[\begin{aligned} z^{k+1}&=z^{k}-{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\alpha}[\nabla f(x^{k})+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\lambda}^{-1}(z^{k}-x^{k})]\\
x^{k+1}&=\mathrm{prox}_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\lambda}\varphi}(z^{k+1}),\quad{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\alpha}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\lambda}.\end{aligned}\right.

(3)

This equivalent formulation naturally introduces the normal map
F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}
, which has been widely used in the field of variational inequalities and generalized equations,
[
31
]
.

Based on
eq.

3
, we can decouple the proximal parameter
\lambda
and the step size
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\alpha}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\lambda}
. In particular, we propose to replace the constant step size
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\alpha}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\lambda}
by a sequence of varying step sizes
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{k}
without adapting the proximal parameter
\lambda
. This leads to the following proximal stochastic update that defines
\mathsf{Norm}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD}
:

\boldsymbol{z}^{k+1}=\boldsymbol{z}^{k}-\alpha_{k}(\boldsymbol{g}^{k}+\lambda^{-1}(\boldsymbol{z}^{k}-\boldsymbol{x}^{k}))\quad\text{and}\quad\boldsymbol{x}^{k+1}=\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(\boldsymbol{z}^{k+1}),

(4)

where
\boldsymbol{g}^{k}
is a (unbiased) stochastic approximation of
\nabla f(\boldsymbol{x}^{k})
.
Our convergence analysis of
\mathsf{Norm}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD}
is based on and focuses on the auxiliary iterates
\{\boldsymbol{z}^{k}\}_{k}
. This is in contrast to classical studies of proximal stochastic methods
[
34
,
22
]
, which are typically limited to the main iterates
\{\boldsymbol{x}^{k}\}_{k}
. As a by-product, we can leverage the unbiasedness of the stochastic gradients
\boldsymbol{g}^{k}
, i.e., taking conditional expectation in
eq.

4
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{k}[\boldsymbol{z}^{k+1}]=\mathbb{E}_{k}[\boldsymbol{z}^{k}-\alpha_{k}(\boldsymbol{g}^{k}+\lambda^{-1}(\boldsymbol{z}^{k}-\boldsymbol{x}^{k}))]=\boldsymbol{z}^{k}-\alpha_{k}F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(\boldsymbol{z}^{k}).

This property will play a distinctive and important role in our derivations.

We now briefly discuss common stationarity measures for proximal gradient-type methods and for the problem
eq.

1
. The first-order optimality conditions associated with
eq.

1
are given by
0\in\partial\psi(x)=\nabla f(x)+\partial\varphi(x)
. Here,
\partial\psi
denotes the limiting subdifferential of
\psi
and
\partial\varphi
is the standard subdifferential for convex functions, cf.
[
73
,
5
]
. A point
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom\\
}(\varphi)
is called a stationary point of
eq.

1
if
0\in\partial\psi(\bar{x})
and we use
\mathrm{crit}(\psi)
to denote the set of all stationary points of
\psi
. We further introduce the notation
\|\partial\psi(x)\|_{-}:=\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial\psi(x)):={\min}_{v\in\partial\psi(x)}\;\|v\|
. A point
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom\\
}(\varphi)
is an
\varepsilon
-stationary point of
\psi
,
\varepsilon\geq 0
, if we have
\|\partial\psi(\bar{x})\|_{-}\leq\varepsilon
.

Natural residual.
The first-order optimality conditions for problem
eq.

1
can be equivalently expressed using the so-called
natural residual
; it holds that:

x\in\mathrm{crit}(\psi)\quad\iff\quad F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nat}}(x):=\lambda^{-1}(x-\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(x-\lambda\nabla f(x)))=0,\quad\lambda>0.

The natural residual
F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nat}}
serves as an important stationarity measure and is a basic tool in the design of algorithms for composite problems,
[
5
,
6
]
. Moreover, based on the characterization
p=\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(y)\iff p\in y-\lambda\partial\varphi(p)
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it follows that

F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nat}}(x)=\lambda^{-1}(x-x^{+})\in\nabla f(x)+\partial\varphi(x^{+}),\quad\text{where}\quad x^{+}:=\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(x-\lambda\nabla f(x)).

Normal map.
Robinson’s normal map is an alternative stationarity measure for
eq.

1
. It can be interpreted as a special subgradient of
\psi
. In particular, we have

F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(z)=\nabla f(\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(z))+\lambda^{-1}(z-\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(z))\in\partial\psi(\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(z))\quad\forall~z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(5)

Thus, if
\bar{z}
is a zero of
F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}
, then, by
eq.

5
,
\bar{x}=\mathrm{prox}_{\lambda\varphi}(\bar{z})
is a stationary point of
eq.

1
. Conversely, if
\bar{x}
satisfies
F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nat}}(\bar{x})=0
, then
\bar{z}=\bar{x}-\lambda\nabla f(\bar{x})
is a zero of the normal map, see
[

Definition 4

Proposition 5
(Approximate descent property).

Let
(H.1)
–
(H.2)
and
(S.1)
–
(S.2)

\mathsf{Norm}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD}

(12)

By

3

\displaystyle H(\boldsymbol{z}^{n})-H(\boldsymbol{z}^{m})

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\xi\tau}{4}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(\boldsymbol{z}^{m})}\|^{2}+(1-\xi)\langle\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{x}^{n}-\boldsymbol{x}^{m}\rangle+\frac{3\xi}{4\tau}\|{\boldsymbol{p}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 17.22217pt+\Big[{\Big({\frac{{\sf L}}{2}-\frac{1}{\lambda}}\Big)+\frac{\xi\lambda}{2}\Big({{\sf L}+\frac{1}{\lambda}}\Big)^{2}+\frac{\xi\lambda^{2}{\sf L}^{2}}{\tau}}\Big]\|{\boldsymbol{x}^{n}-\boldsymbol{x}^{m}}\|^{2}.

(13)

Invoking the upper bound for

11

\displaystyle H(\boldsymbol{z}^{n})-H(\boldsymbol{z}^{m})

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\xi\tau}{4}\|{F^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{nor}}(\boldsymbol{z}^{m})}\|^{2}+\frac{4-\xi}{4\tau}\|{\boldsymbol{p}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip-25.83325pt+\underbracket{\Big[\frac{{\sf L}}{2}-\frac{\xi}{\lambda}+\frac{\xi\lambda}{2}\Big({{\sf L}+\frac{1}{\lambda}}\Big)^{2}+\frac{4\xi\lambda^{2}{\sf L}^{2}-3(1-\xi)}{4\tau}\Big]}_{=:T_{1}}\|{\boldsymbol{x}^{n}-\boldsymbol{x}^{m}}\|^{2}.

In this section, we derive complexity bounds and prove global convergence of

and uses the concept of alternative time scales

(for some carefully designed subsequence

[

\tau_{t_{k},t_{k+1}}
to a given “time window” parameter

The recursive construction of

Definition 6
(Time indices).

Let
{\sf T}>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
be given. We introduce the mapping
\varpi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{\pm\infty\}

\tau_{m,n}={\sum}_{k=m}^{n-1}\alpha_{k}
. The
time indices

, associated with the
time window

{\sf T}
, are defined as

t_{0}:=\inf\{i:\alpha_{j}\leq(1-\delta){\sf T}\text{ for all $j\geq i$}\}\quad\text{and}\quad t_{k+1}:=\varpi(t_{k}).

To show convergence of
\mathsf{Norm}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD}
, we typically first analyze the behavior of the iterates and (merit) function values restricted to
\{t_{k}\}_{k}
. Subsequently, full convergence can be deduced by establishing a relationship between a generic index
i
and the subsequence
\{t_{k}\}_{k}
. To this end, we define

\kappa(i)=\max\{k:t_{k}<i\}\quad\text{for all $i>t_{0}$}.

(15)

It is evident that we have
t_{\kappa(i)}<i\leq t_{\kappa(i)+1}
for all
i>t_{0}
.

\delta{\sf T}\leq\tau_{t_{k},t_{k+1}}\leq{\sf T}

Lemma 12
(Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz-type inequality).

hold and let
\bar{z}

Conclusion

In this paper, we develop a novel normal map-based proximal stochastic gradient method,
\mathsf{Norm}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD}
, for nonconvex composite-type problems. Leveraging the unbiasedness of the stochastic normal map steps, we show that the iterates generated by
\mathsf{Norm}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD}
satisfy an approximate descent condition which allows establishing asymptotic global convergence results and non-asymptotic complexity bounds under standard assumptions.
Furthermore, we prove a
.
s
.
strong limit convergence guarantees if the objective function is definable. Based on these results,
\mathsf{Norm}\textsf{-}

\mathsf{SGD}
is shown to a
.
s
.
have a manifold identification property. This is in stark contrast to the classical proximal stochastic gradient method, which is known to not possess such a property. To our knowledge,
\mathsf{Norm}\text{-}\mathsf{SGD}
appears to be one of the first “variance reduction-free”, basic proximal stochastic algorithms for nonconvex composite optimization with this broad range of convergence properties. We believe that the normal map-based perspective and the KL-based techniques adopted in this work have general potential and can be likely applied to other families of stochastic approaches.

Abstract
. The evolution of two isothermal, incompressible, immiscible fluids in a bounded domain is governed by Cahn-Hilliard-Navier-Stokes equations (CHNS System). In this work, we study the well-posedness results for the CHNS system with nonhomogeneous boundary condition for the velocity equation. We obtain the existence of global weak solutions in the two-dimensional bounded domain. We further prove the continuous dependence of the solution on initial conditions and boundary data that will provide the uniqueness of the weak solution. The existence of strong solutions is also established in this work. Furthermore, we show that in the two-dimensional case, each global weak solution converges to a stationary solution.

Introduction

We consider the motion of an isothermal mixture of two immiscible and incompressible fluids subject to phase separation, which is described by the well-known diffuse interface model. It consists of the Navier-Stokes equations for the average velocity and a convective Cahn-Hilliard equation for the relative concentration, also known as Cahn-Hilliard-Navier-Stokes (CHNS) system or “model H”. A general model for such a system is given by:

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\varphi+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\varphi&=div(m(\varphi)\nabla\mu),\,\,\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\
\mu&=-\Delta\varphi+F^{\prime}(\varphi),\\
\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}-div(\nu(\varphi)\mathrm{D}\mathbf{u})+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}+\nabla\pi&=\mu\nabla\varphi,\,\,\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\
div~\mathbf{u}&=0,\,\,\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),\\
\end{aligned}}}\right.

(1.1)

where
\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x},t)
is the average velocity of the fluid and
\varphi(\mathbf{x},t)
is the relative concentration of the fluid. Here,
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2},
with a sufficiently smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. The density is taken as matched density, i.e., constant density, which is equal to 1. Moreover,
m
is mobility of binary mixture,
\mu
is a chemical potential,
\pi
is the pressure,
\nu
is the viscosity and
F
is a double well potential. The symmetric part of the gradient of the flow velocity vector is denoted by
\mathrm{D}\mathbf{u}
, that is,
\mathrm{D}\mathbf{u}
is the strain tensor
\frac{1}{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\nabla\mathbf{u}+(\nabla\mathbf{u})^{\top}}}\right)
. Furthermore,
\mu
is the first variation of the Helmholtz free energy functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}(\varphi):=\int_{\Omega}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\mathrm{F}(\varphi(x))}}\right)\,\/\mathrm{d}\/x,

(1.2)

where
\mathrm{F}
is a double-well potential of the regular type. A typical example of regular

is

\displaystyle\mathrm{F}(s)=(s^{2}-1)^{2},\ s\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.3)

A physically relevant but singular potential

is the Flory-Huggins potential given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{F}(\varphi)=\frac{\theta}{2}((1+\varphi)\ln(1+\varphi)+(1-\varphi)\ln(1-\varphi))-\frac{\theta_{c}}{2}\varphi^{2},\quad\varphi\in(-1,1),

where
\theta,\theta_{c}>0

Before concluding this section, we want to highlight some key aspects of the paper. The system (
1.5
) is inherently non-autonomous owing to the presence of the time-dependent boundary condition
\mathbf{h}(t)
. This introduces additional difficulties in subsequent proofs. Firstly, the conventional Galerkin approximation technique for obtaining the existence of weak solutions encounters challenges, as the appropriate test function space should incorporate discretized boundary data in the trace sense, which is not well-defined.
Moreover, the intricate nonlinear coupling prohibits direct application of fixed-point arguments. In the context of single-phase fluid flow, such as Navier-Stokes equations with non-homogeneous boundaries, the use of Dirichlet lifting has been instrumental in proving the existence of solutions, as demonstrated in
[
16
,
22
,
43
]
. Thus we introduce a suitable lifting function (cf. (
2.4
), (
6.1
) below) for the variable
\mathbf{u}
. Subsequently, using the lifting defined in (
2.4
) we rewrite the equations for the lifted variable with homogeneous boundary condition and discretize it along with the variable
\varphi
.
Then we study the existence results for the new system
for the approximate problem employing Schauder’s fixed point theorem. Finally, by deriving the requisite estimates, we proceed to the limit for the approximate problem, thereby establishing the existence of a global weak solution.
Another significant challenge is obtaining the energy inequality, which plays a pivotal role in understanding the long-term behaviour of the system. For that, we introduce a lifted energy functional for the lifted system proposed in this paper, which facilitates the derivation of uniform energy estimates for the solution.
Further, to study the long time behaviour of the system we wish to use Lojasiewicz-Simon approach (cf.
[
45
]
), which has been proven to be highly effective in studying the long-time behaviour of nonlinear evolution equations, as demonstrated in prior works such as
[
1
,
48
,
27
,
44
,
49
]
. However, our case differs from these works due to the non-autonomous nature of the system under consideration. To align with the conventional case and apply the available Lojasiewicz-Simon inequality, we convert the non homogeneous system to the homogeneous one by using an another type of appropriate lifting. Then, leveraging uniform bounds obtained for solutions and compact embeddings of Sobolev spaces, we establish the convergence of solutions for the lifted system, consequently yielding the convergence of the original system.

The structure of the paper is as follows:
In Section 2, we present the requisite functional framework to establish the well-posedness results.
Section 3 is dedicated to the establishment of the energy inequality and
proof of existence results utilizing a semi-Galerkin approximation.
Section 4 focuses on demonstrating the continuous dependence of weak solutions on both initial data and boundary terms, thereby establishing the uniqueness of the weak solution.
Moving forward to Section 5, we establish the existence of a strong solution.
In Section 6, we analyze the behaviour of the solution in the limit as time
t\to\infty
proving that the system converges to a steady state system provided the lifted system converges to its steady state.
In the end, we derive some estimates on lifted operators in Section 7.

t>0
there exists
t_{j},t_{j+1}
with
t\in[t_{j},t_{j+1}]
such that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{\hat{E}}(t)-\mathcal{\hat{E}}_{\infty}|\leq|\mathcal{\hat{E}}(t)-\mathcal{\hat{E}}(t_{j})|+|\mathcal{\hat{E}}(t_{j})-\mathcal{\hat{E}}_{\infty}|.

This yields,
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\mathcal{\hat{E}}(t)=\mathcal{\hat{E}}_{\infty}.

∎

In the next theorem we will prove the convergence of solutions to the equilibrium. We will prove the theorem along the lines of
[
12
,
27
]
.

Theorem 6.14
.

Let
(\mathbf{u}_{0},\varphi_{0})\in\mathbb{V}^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{H}^{2}
and
F
satisfies the Assumptions
2.2
. In addition,
\mathbf{h}
satisfies the decay condition (
6.8
). Then any strong solution
(\mathbf{u}(t),\varphi(t))
converges to an equilibrium
(\mathbf{u}_{\infty},\varphi_{\infty})
strongly in
\mathbb{H}^{1}\times\mathrm{L}^{2}
as
t\to+\infty.

Proof.

Firstly, from (
6.13
) we have

\|\mathbf{u}(t)-\mathbf{u}_{\infty}\|_{\mathbb{V}^{1}(\Omega)}\to 0,\text{ as }t\to+\infty.

We only need to show the convergence of
\varphi(t)\to\varphi_{\infty}
as
t\to+\infty.
Notice that, we can find an integer
j_{0}
such that for all
j\geq j_{0}
,
\|\varphi(t_{j})-\varphi_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}}<\frac{\rho}{3}
, where
\rho\in(0,1)
is a constant given in Proposition
6
that depends on
\varphi_{\infty}.
Consequently, we define

s(t_{j})=\sup\{t\geq t_{j}:\|\varphi(t)-\varphi_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}<\rho\}.

Since
\varphi\in C([0,+\infty),\mathrm{H}^{1})
, we see that

s(t_{j})>t_{j}
for any
j\geq j_{0}
.
Also from the energy estimate (
3
) and Proposition
6
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{\hat{E}}(t)-\mathcal{\hat{E}}_{\infty}\geq\min\{\nu,1\}\int_{t}^{+\infty}\mathcal{C}^{2}(t)\,dt-\int_{t}^{+\infty}r_{1}(t)\,dt,

where
\mathcal{C}(t)=\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|+\|\nabla\mu\|
and
r_{1}(t)=C_{1}(\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}_{e}\|^{2}+\|\mathbf{u}_{e}\|_{\mathbb{V}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2})+\|\mathbf{u}_{e}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{V}^{1}(\Omega)}
with constant
C_{1}
depending on
\mathcal{\hat{E}}(0)
,
\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{h}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,+\infty,\mathbb{V}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega))}
,
\|\mathbf{h}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,+\infty;\mathbb{V}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\partial\Omega))}
,

\|\mathbf{h}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,+\infty;\mathbb{V}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega))}
. Note that since
\mathbf{h}
satisfy decay conditions (
6.8
),
r_{1}(t)
also has some decay, namely,

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{+\infty}r_{1}(t)\,dt\leq C_{1}(1+t)^{-1-\gamma},\quad\forall\,t\geq 0.

Let
\theta
be defined as in Proposition
6
, we choose
\theta^{\prime}
such that
0<\theta^{\prime}\leq\theta
satisfying
0<\theta^{\prime}<\frac{\gamma}{(1+\gamma)}
. For any fix
t_{j},j\geq j_{0}
we set

K_{j}=[t_{j},s(t_{j})],\quad K_{j}^{(1)}=\{t\in K_{j}:\mathcal{C}(t)\geq(1+t)^{-(1-\theta^{\prime})(1+\gamma)}\},\quad K_{j}^{(2)}=K_{j}\backslash K_{j}^{(1)}.

Moreover, we define a functional on
K_{j}
as following

\displaystyle\Phi(t)=\mathcal{\hat{E}}(t)-\mathcal{\hat{E}}_{\infty}+\int_{t}^{s(t_{j})}r_{1}(\tau)\,d\tau,\quad\forall t\in K_{j}.

Then it follows from the convergence of energy and from the decay condition on
r_{1}(t)
that

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\Phi(t_{j})=0.

Moreover, we have from (
3
)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(|\Phi(t)|^{\theta^{\prime}}\text{sgn}\Phi(t))\leq-\theta^{\prime}\min\{\nu,1\}|\Phi(t)|^{\theta^{\prime}-1}\mathcal{C}^{2}(t)\leq 0.

(6.16)

This implies
|\Phi(t)|^{\theta^{\prime}}\text{sgn}\Phi(t)
is decreasing on
K_{j}.
Now, by using Proposition
6
we obtain that

\displaystyle|\Phi(t)|^{1-\theta^{\prime}}\leq

\displaystyle|\mathcal{\hat{E}}(t)-\mathcal{\hat{E}}_{\infty}|^{1-\theta^{\prime}}+C\big(\int_{t}^{+\infty}r_{1}(\tau)d\tau\big)^{1-\theta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|^{1-\theta^{\prime}}+\|-\Delta\varphi+F^{\prime}(\varphi)\|^{1-\theta^{\prime}}_{(\mathrm{H}^{1})^{\prime}})+C\big(\int_{t}^{+\infty}r_{1}(\tau)d\tau\big)^{1-\theta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla\overline{\mathbf{u}}(t)\|+\|\nabla\mu\|)+C(1+t)^{-(1-\theta^{\prime})(1+\gamma)}.

(6.17)

Thus, on
K_{j}^{(1)}
we have

|\Phi(t)|^{1-\theta^{\prime}}\leq C\mathcal{C}(t),

which together with (
6.16
) gives

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{dt}(|\Phi(t)|^{\theta^{\prime}}\text{sgn}\Phi(t))\geq\theta^{\prime}\min\{\gamma,1\}\mathcal{C}(t)=C\mathcal{C}(t).

Therefore, on
K_{j}^{(1)}
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{K_{j}^{(1)}}\mathcal{C}(t)\,dt\leq

\displaystyle-C\int_{K_{j}}\frac{d}{dt}(|\Phi(t)|^{\theta^{\prime}}\text{sgn}\Phi(t))\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C(|\Phi(t_{j})|^{\theta^{\prime}}+|\Phi(s(t_{j}))|^{\theta^{\prime}})<+\infty,

(6.18)

since
\Phi(s(t_{j}))=0,
when
s(t_{j})=+\infty.
On
K_{j}^{(2)}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{K_{j}^{(2)}}\mathcal{C}(t)\,dt\leq C\int_{t_{j}}^{+\infty}(1+t)^{-(1-\theta^{\prime})(1+\gamma)}=\frac{C}{-\gamma\theta^{\prime}-\theta^{\prime}+\gamma}(1+t_{j})^{\gamma\theta^{\prime}+\theta^{\prime}-\gamma},

(6.19)

due to the choice of
\theta^{\prime}
. Therefore the inequality (
6
) and (
6.19
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{K_{j}}\mathcal{C}(t)\,dt=\int_{K_{j}^{(1)}}\mathcal{C}(t)\,dt+\int_{K_{j}^{(2)}}\mathcal{C}(t)\,dt<+\infty\quad\text{for any }j.

(6.20)

From (
3.35
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{K_{j}}\|\partial_{t}\varphi(t)\|^{2}_{({\mathrm{H}^{1}})^{\prime}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{K_{j}}\mathcal{C}(t)\,dt+\int_{K_{j}}\|\mathbf{h}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{V}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\partial\Omega)}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C(|\Phi(t_{j})|^{\theta^{\prime}}+|\Phi(s(t_{j}))|^{\theta^{\prime}})+C(1+t_{j})^{\gamma\theta^{\prime}+\theta^{\prime}-\gamma}.

(6.21)

Now, we claim that there exists an integer
j_{1}\geq j_{0}
such that
s(t_{j_{1}})=+\infty
. As a consequence

\|\varphi(t)-\varphi_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}<\rho,\quad\forall t\geq t_{j_{1}}.

Indeed, if this is not true, then suppose that for any
j\geq j_{0}
, we have
s(t_{j})<+\infty
. Then by definition, we have

\|\varphi(s(t_{j}))-\varphi_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}=\rho>0.

Then, by using (
6
) and the fact that
(\mathbf{u}_{\infty},\varphi_{\infty})\in\omega(\mathbf{u}_{0},\varphi_{0})
, it follows that

\displaystyle\|\varphi(s(t_{j}))-\varphi_{\infty}\|_{(\mathrm{H}^{1})^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi(s(t_{j}))-\|\varphi(t_{j})\|_{(\mathrm{H}^{1})^{\prime}}+\|\varphi(t_{j})-\varphi_{\infty}\|_{(\mathrm{H}^{1})^{\prime}}

(6.22)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{t_{j}}^{s(t_{j})}\|\partial_{t}\varphi(t)\|^{2}_{({\mathrm{H}^{1}})^{\prime}}\,dt+\|\varphi(t_{j})-\varphi_{\infty}\|_{(\mathrm{H}^{1})^{\prime}}\to 0\text{ as }j\to+\infty.

Then, by using interpolation inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\varphi(s(t_{j}))-\varphi_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\leq\|\varphi(s(t_{j}))-\varphi_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\varphi(s(t_{j}))-\varphi_{\infty}\|_{(\mathrm{H}^{1})^{\prime}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\to 0\text{ as }j\to+\infty,

which leads to a contradiction. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 6.15
.

One can show from (
6.22
) that
\|\varphi(t)-\varphi_{\infty}\|_{(\mathrm{H}^{1})^{\prime}}\to 0
as
t\to+\infty.
Then using uniform bound on
\varphi
in
\mathrm{H}^{2}
, we can easily get that
\varphi(t)\to\varphi_{\infty}
weakly in
\mathrm{H}^{2}.

In this section, we establish some estimates on lifting functions
\mathbf{u}_{e}
and
\mathbf{u}_{p}
that we have used in previous sections.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathbb{V}^{2}(\Omega)
. Also, suppose that
\mathbf{h}
satisfy (
3.9
) and
\partial_{t}\mathbf{h}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}([0,+\infty);\mathbb{V}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega))
. Then, for any
t>0
we have the following estimates

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}_{p}(t)-\mathbf{u}_{e}(t)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{V}^{1}(\Omega)}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta(\mathbf{u}_{p}(s)-\mathbf{u}_{e}(s))\|^{2}ds\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{h}(s)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{V}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)}ds.

(7.1)

In addition, if
\partial_{t}\mathbf{h}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}([0,+\infty);\mathbb{V}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega))
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}_{p}\|=0.

(7.2)

Proof.

It follows from (
2.4
) and (
6.1
) that

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \nu_{1}\Delta(\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{u}_{e})=-\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}_{p},&\quad\text{ in }\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\
div~(\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{u}_{e})=0,&\quad\text{ in }\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\
\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{u}_{e}=0,&\quad\text{ in }\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\
\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{u}_{e}|_{t=0}=0,&\quad\text{ in }\Omega.\end{aligned}}}\right.

(7.3)

and

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}(\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{u}_{e})-\nu_{1}\Delta(\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{u}_{e})=-\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}_{e},\quad&\text{ in }\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\
div~(\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{u}_{e})=0,\quad&\text{ in }\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\
\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{u}_{e}=0,\quad&\text{ in }\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\
\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{u}_{e}|_{t=0}=0,\quad&\text{ in }\Omega.\end{aligned}}}\right.

(7.4)

Multiplying the above equation with
-\Delta(\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{u}_{e})
and integrating by parts and using Poincare inequality we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla(\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{u}_{e})\|^{2}+\nu_{1}\|\Delta(\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{u}_{e})\|^{2}\leq\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}_{e}\|^{2}.

Integrating from
0
to
t
and using the estimate (
2.8
) and Poincare inequality we have (
7.1
).

Applying now the Laplacian to the equation (
7.4
) we get,

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\Delta(\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{u}_{e})-\nu_{1}\Delta^{2}(\mathbf{u}_{p}-u_{e})=-\partial_{t}\nabla p,\quad&\text{ in }\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\
\Delta div~(\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{u}_{e})=0,\quad&\text{ in }\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\
\Delta(\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{u}_{e})=\partial_{t}\mathbf{h},\quad&\text{ in }\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\
\Delta(\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{u}_{e})|_{t=0}=0,\quad&\text{ in }\Omega.\end{aligned}}}\right.

(7.5)

Multiplying the first equation of (
7.5
) by
\Delta(\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{u}_{e})
and integrating by parts we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta(\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{u}_{e})\|^{2}+\nu_{1}\|\nabla\Delta(\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{u}_{e})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=(\partial_{t}(\nabla p),\Delta(\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{u}_{e}))+\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{n}\Delta(\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{u}_{e})\partial_{t}\mathbf{h}

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial_{n}\Delta(\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{u}_{e})\|_{\mathbb{V}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)}\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{h}\|_{\mathbb{V}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta(\mathbf{u}_{p}-\mathbf{u}_{e})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{h}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{V}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)}.

Now, by using the estimate (
7.1
) and the Lemma
6
it follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\|\Delta(\mathbf{u}_{p}(t)-\mathbf{u}_{e}(t))\|=0.

Now, from (
7.3
) and using the fact
\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}_{p}(t)\|=\|\Delta(\mathbf{u}_{p}(t)-\mathbf{u}_{e}(t))\|
, we can get (
7.2
).
This completes the proof.
∎

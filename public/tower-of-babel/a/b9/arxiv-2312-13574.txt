Bulk reconstruction in flat holography
Bin Chen, Zezhou Hu

Abstract
In this note, we discuss the bulk reconstruction of massless free fields in flat space from the highest-weight representation of boundary Carrollian conformal field theory (CCFT). We expand the bulk field as a sum of infinite descendants of a primary state defined in the boundary CCFT, and discuss the Lorentz invariant bulk-boundary propagator in detail for the BMS 3 \operatorname{BMS}_{3} / CCFT 2 \operatorname{CCFT}_{2} case. In our calculation, it is necessary to introduce a nonzero mass at the beginning and take it as vanishing at the end. The framework we proposed has the potential to probe local bulk physics from the boundary CCFT.

2.1
\operatorname{BMS}_{3}
symmetry

In Minkowski spacetime
\mathbb{R}^{1,2}
, we can introduce a new coordinate system
(r,u,\phi)
as

X^{\mu}=(0,r\phi,0)+(u+\frac{r\phi^{2}}{2})(1,0,-1)+\frac{r}{2}(1,0,1)\,,\hskip 12.91663pt\mu=0,1,2,

(2.1)

in terms of which the metric becomes

ds^{2}=\eta_{\mu\nu}dX^{\mu}dX^{\nu}=-2dudr+r^{2}d\phi^{2}\,.

(2.2)

We can obtain the null infinity, which is a null plane where a
\operatorname{CCFT}_{2}
can live, by taking
r\rightarrow\infty
. Any interval on this null plane can be null or spacelike, and this is why a massive particle with a timelike worldline can never reach the null infinity.

The Carrollian conformal transformations on the null boundary are generated by the
\operatorname{BMS}_{3}
algebra, whose generators are the vectors

M_{n}=-\phi^{n+1}\partial_{u}\,,\quad L_{m}=-\phi^{m+1}\partial_{\phi}-(m+1)\phi^{m}u\partial_{u}\,,\hskip 12.91663ptm,n\in\mathbb{Z},

(2.3)

satisfying the following commutation relations

\displaystyle[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{m+n}\,,

(2.4)

\displaystyle[L_{m},M_{n}]=(m-n)M_{m+n}\,,

\displaystyle[M_{m},M_{n}]=0.

The global part of Carrollian conformal symmetry is simply the Poincaré group
2
2

2

All the results of this work involve only Poincaré algebra, the global part of the BMS
3
algebra, but some discussions on the full BMS
3
may be useful for future study.
, generated by
M_{0}
,
M_{\pm}
,
L_{0}
,
L_{\pm}
,

\begin{aligned} &M_{-1}=-\partial_{0}+\partial_{2}\,,\\
&M_{0}=\partial_{1}\,,\\
&M_{1}=-(\partial_{0}+\partial_{2})\,,\\
\end{aligned}\begin{aligned} &\qquad L_{-1}=J_{01}+J_{12}\,,\\
&\qquad L_{0}=-J_{02}\,,\\
&\qquad L_{1}=-J_{01}+J_{12}\,,\end{aligned}

(2.5)

where we have defined that
\partial_{\mu}\equiv\partial_{X^{\mu}}
and
J_{\mu\nu}\equiv X_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}-X_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\,.

Using the coordinate transformation relations,
we have

\begin{aligned} &M_{-1}=-\partial_{u}\,,\qquad\\
&M_{0}=-\phi\partial_{u}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\phi}\,,\qquad\\
&M_{1}=-\phi^{2}\partial_{u}-2\partial_{r}+2\frac{\phi}{r}\partial_{\phi}\,,\qquad\\
\end{aligned}\begin{aligned} &L_{-1}=-\partial_{\phi}\,,\\
&L_{0}=-\phi\partial_{\phi}-u\partial_{u}+r\partial_{r}\,,\\
&L_{1}=-\phi^{2}\partial_{\phi}+2\phi(r\partial_{r}-u\partial_{u})+\frac{2u}{r}\partial_{\phi}\,.\end{aligned}

(2.6)

As we want a bulk field in correspondence with a highest-weight representation of CCFT
3
, we need to define the Hermitian conjugation of these generators to be

L_{m}^{\dagger}=L_{-m},\hskip 12.91663ptM^{\dagger}_{m}=M_{-m}\,.

(2.7)

Moreover, we need the inversion transformation
[
52
]
associated to the Hermitian conjugation relation (
2.7
) in the boundary
\operatorname{CCFT}_{2}
,

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle\rightarrow\phi^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\phi}\,,

(2.8)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\rightarrow u^{\prime}=-\frac{u}{\phi^{2}}\,,

and thereafter the transformation rule for a primary operator becomes

\mathcal{O}^{\dagger}(u,\phi)=\phi^{-2\Delta}\exp{-2\xi\frac{u}{\phi}}\mathcal{O}(-\frac{u}{\phi^{2}},\frac{1}{\phi})\,.

(2.9)

2.2
Scalar bulk reconstruction in
\operatorname{BMS}_{3}
/
\operatorname{CCFT}_{2}

To reconstruct a free scalar in the bulk, we start by considering what kind of conditions the primary operator
\mathcal{O}
should satisfy. Firstly a primary state in CCFT
2
is characterized by scaling dimension
\Delta
and boost charge
\xi
[
57
,
58
,
39
]
,

[L_{0},\mathcal{O}(0)]=\Delta\mathcal{O}(0),\hskip 12.91663pt[M_{0},\mathcal{O}(0)]=\xi\mathcal{O}(0).

(2.10)

From the
\operatorname{BMS}_{3}
algebra, we have

\displaystyle=-mL_{m}\,,

(2.11)

\displaystyle[L_{0},M_{n}]

\displaystyle=-nM_{n}\,.

Corresponding to the highest-weight representation, the generators lowering the scaling dimension should annihilate the primary state,

\displaystyle[L_{m},\mathcal{O}(0)]=0\quad\text{when}\quad m\char 62\relax 0\,,

(2.12)

\displaystyle[M_{n},\mathcal{O}(0)]=0\quad\text{when}\quad n\char 62\relax 0\,.

Secondly, the mass of the field is given by the Casimir operators
\partial^{2}
, which commute with all the generators of Poincaré group,

[\partial^{2},\mathcal{O}(0)]=m^{2}\mathcal{O}(0)\,,

(2.13)

where
m^{2}
is the square of the mass of the scalar bulk field. Since

\partial^{2}=-M_{1}M_{-1}+M_{0}^{2}\,,

(2.14)

we have

m^{2}=\xi^{2}.

(2.15)

Now we can construct the bulk field
\phi(r,u,\phi)
as

\phi(r,0,0)|0\rangle=\sum^{\infty}_{J,L=0}\lambda_{J,L}r^{-K}L_{-1}^{J}M_{-1}^{L}|\mathcal{O}\rangle\,.

(2.16)

Since the bulk field is a scalar, it must transform under Poincaré group in the following way,
[Q,\phi(X^{\mu})]=-\delta_{Q}\phi(X^{\mu})
, where
Q
can be any
conserved charge corresponding to the transformation in Poincaré group and
\delta_{Q}
is given by (
2.6
). More explicitly, we have

\displaystyle L_{0}\phi(r,0,0)|0\rangle

\displaystyle=-r\partial_{r}\phi(r,0,0)|0\rangle\,,

(2.17)

\displaystyle L_{1}\phi(r,0,0)|0\rangle

\displaystyle=0\,,

\displaystyle M_{1}\phi(r,0,0)|0\rangle

\displaystyle=2\partial_{r}\phi(r,0,0)|0\rangle\,,

\displaystyle\left(M_{0}+\frac{1}{r}L_{-1}\right)\phi(r,0,0)|0\rangle

\displaystyle=0\,.

The first equation in (
2.17
) tells

K=\Delta+J+L\,.

(2.18)

The second equation in (
2.17
) tells

2\xi(L+1)\lambda_{J,L+1}+(J+2L+2\Delta)(J+1)\lambda_{J+1,L}=0\,.\\

(2.19)

The third equation in (
2.17
) tells

2(J+1)\xi\lambda_{J+1,L}+(J+2)(J+1)\lambda_{J+2,L-1}=-2(\Delta+J+L)\lambda_{J,L}\,.

(2.20)

The fourth equation in (
2.17
) tells

(J+1)\lambda_{J+1,L-1}+\xi\lambda_{J,L}+\lambda_{J-1,L}=0\,.

(2.21)

Solving the first three equations, we can obtain a general iteration relation for
\lambda_{J,L}
as

\displaystyle\lambda_{J+1,L}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\xi}\frac{(J+2L+2\Delta-1)(J+L+\Delta)}{(J+1)(J+L+2\Delta-1)}\lambda_{J,L}\,,

(2.22)

\displaystyle\lambda_{J,L+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\xi^{2}}\frac{(J+2L+2\Delta-1)(J+2L+2\Delta)(J+L+\Delta)}{2(L+1)(J+L+2\Delta-1)}\lambda_{J,L}\,.

Setting
\lambda_{0,0}=1
, it can be expressed as

\lambda_{J,L}=\frac{2^{-L}\left(\frac{1}{\xi^{2}}\right)^{L}\left(-\frac{1}{\xi}\right)^{J}\Gamma(J+L+\Delta)\Gamma(J+2L+2\Delta-1)}{\Gamma(J+1)\Gamma(L+1)\Gamma(\Delta)\Gamma(J+L+2\Delta-1)}\,.

(2.23)

It is clear that the solution does not make sense if
\xi
is vanishing.
However, if we keep
\xi
non-vanishing, the solution does not satisfy the fourth equation. In fact, using this solution, the fourth equation in (
2.17
) becomes

\left(M_{0}+\frac{1}{r}L_{-1}\right)\phi(r,0,0)|0\rangle=\frac{1}{r}(\Delta-1)\xi\int_{r}^{\infty}dr\phi(r,0,0)|0\rangle\quad\text{for}\quad\Delta\neq 1\,,

(2.24)

and

\left(M_{0}+\frac{1}{r}L_{-1}\right)\phi(r,0,0)|0\rangle=\frac{\lambda_{0,0}\xi}{r^{\Delta}}\mathcal{O}(0,0)|0\rangle\quad\text{for}\quad\Delta=1\,.

(2.25)

In any case, the equation could be reduced to the fourth equation in (
2.17
) only if
\xi=0
. It seems that the Poincaré symmetry is broken except for the massless case. The role of a non-vanishing mass (or
\xi
) might be regarded as a regulator, and our regulation scheme somewhat breaks the Poincaré global symmetry. We will follow the prescription that we do computation with a non-vanishing
\xi
and take
\xi=0
at the end to restore the Poincaré symmetry.

So far we have reconstructed a free scalar from a highest-weight representation of
\operatorname{CCFT}_{2}
. And the bulk field at a general point
(r,u,z,\bar{z})
is obtained by

\phi(r,u,\phi)=\exp(u M_{-1}+\phi L_{-1})\phi(r,0,0)\exp(-u M_{-1}-\phi L_{-1})\,.

(2.26)

Its action on the vacuum is

\phi(r,u,\phi)|0\rangle=\sum_{J,L}\lambda_{J,L}r^{-(\Delta+J+L)}e^{\phi L_{-1}+uM_{-1}}L_{-1}^{J}M_{-1}^{L}|\mathcal{O}\rangle\,.

(2.27)

The above reconstruction of bulk scalar field can be rewritten in terms of the boundary operator,

\phi(r,0,0)=\sum^{\infty}_{J,L=0}\lambda_{J,L}r^{-K}\partial_{\phi}^{J}\partial_{u}^{L}\mathcal{O}(0,0)\,.

(2.28)

2.3
Bulk-boundary scalar propagator in
\operatorname{BMS}_{3}
/
\operatorname{CCFT}_{2}

We denote the bulk-boundary propagator for a free field as

G^{\xi}(r,u,\phi)\equiv\langle\phi(r,u,\phi)\mathcal{O}(0,0)\rangle\,,

(2.29)

where
\xi
indicates the mass of the field. When considering the massless case, the bulk-boundary propagator is determined by the differential equation

\partial^{2}G^{\xi=0}(r,u,\phi)=-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\left(r\partial_{r}G\right)-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{u}\left(r\partial_{r}G\right)+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\phi}\left(\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\phi}G\right)=0\,,

(2.30)

with the boundary condition

G^{\xi=0}(r,u,\phi)\sim\frac{1}{r^{\Delta}}\langle\mathcal{O}(u,\phi)\mathcal{O}(0,0)\rangle=\frac{1}{r^{\Delta}}\frac{1}{\phi^{2\Delta}}\,.

(2.31)

The solution is

G^{\xi=0}(r,u,\phi)=\left(\frac{1}{r\phi^{2}+2u}\right)^{\Delta}\,.

(2.32)

This method can be further used to obtain the bulk-bulk propagators.
Considering the Poincaré symmetry, the bulk-bulk propagator must be of the form

G^{\xi=0}(X_{1},X_{2})=\left(\frac{1}{\eta_{\mu\nu}(X_{1}-X_{2})^{\mu}(X_{1}-X_{2})^{\nu}}\right)^{\Delta}\,.

(2.33)

Then the equation
\partial^{2}G^{\xi=0}(X_{1},X_{2})=0
fixes the scaling dimension
\Delta=\frac{1}{2}
.

We would like to reproduce the bulk-boundary propagator (
2.32
) by using the reconstructed bulk field (
2.28
). Before we do explicit computation, we need to clarify from a different point of view why we cannot set the mass (or
\xi
) to zero at the very first beginning of the computation. Assuming the expression for
\lambda_{J,L}
has been obtained, we substitute (
2.28
) into the bulk-boundary propagator as

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{O}(u,\phi)\phi(r,0,0)\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{J,L=0}\lambda_{J,L}r^{-K}(-\partial_{\phi})^{J}(-\partial_{u})^{L}\langle\mathcal{O}(u,\phi)O(0,0)\rangle

(2.34)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{J,L=0}\lambda_{J,L}r^{-K}(-\partial_{\phi})^{J}(-\partial_{u})^{L}\left(\frac{A}{\phi^{2\Delta}}+\frac{B\delta(\phi)}{u^{2\Delta-1}}\right)\,,

then there is no way that we can get any nontrivial result: as the first term on the right-hand side does not depend on
u
while the second term is singular for
\phi
, the final result is singular, not a well-behaved function of
u
and
\phi
. On the other hand for a nonzero mass (or
\xi
), it becomes

\langle\mathcal{O}(u,\phi)\phi(r,0,0)\rangle=\sum^{\infty}_{J,L=0}\lambda_{J,L}r^{-K}(-\partial_{\phi})^{J}(-\partial_{u})^{L}\left(A\phi^{-2\Delta}\exp{-2\xi\frac{u}{\phi}}\right)\,,\\

(2.35)

which may lead to a well-behaved function. In the above discussion, we have used the fact that the two-point functions of primary operators in CCFT take the form
[
58
,
57
,
42
]

\langle\mathcal{O}(u,\phi)\mathcal{O}(0,0)\rangle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}A\phi^{-2\Delta}+B\delta(\phi)u^{-(2\Delta-1)},&\mbox{for $\xi=0$,}\\
A\phi^{-2\Delta}\exp{-2\xi\frac{u}{\phi}},&\mbox{for $\xi\neq 0$.}\end{array}\right.

(2.36)

From now on, we will derive the bulk-boundary propagator result in
\operatorname{BMS}_{3}
/
\operatorname{CCFT}_{2}
. The most direct but kind of clumsy way to calculate is just using the equation (
2.35
),

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{O}(u,\phi)\phi(r,0,0)\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{J,L=0}\lambda_{J,L}r^{-(J+L+\Delta)}(-\partial_{\phi})^{J}(-\partial_{u})^{L}\left(\phi^{-2\Delta}\exp{-2\xi\frac{u}{\phi}}\right)

(2.37)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{M=0}^{\infty}\sum_{J=0}^{M}\lambda_{J,M-J}r^{-(M+\Delta)}(-\partial_{\phi})^{J}(-\partial_{u})^{M-J}\left(\phi^{-2\Delta}\exp{-2\xi\frac{u}{\phi}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp{-2\xi\frac{u}{\phi}}\left(\frac{1}{r\phi^{2}-2u}\right)^{\Delta}\,.

Another much more sophisticated way is to consider

\displaystyle\langle 0|\mathcal{O}(u,\phi\text{)}\phi\text{(}r,0,0\text{)}|0\rangle=

\displaystyle\left\{\phi^{-2\Delta}e^{-2\xi\frac{u}{\phi}}\mathcal{O}\left(-\frac{u}{\phi^{2}},\frac{1}{\phi}\right)|0\rangle\right\}^{\dagger}\phi(r,0,0)\mid 0\rangle

(2.38)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\phi^{-2\Delta}e^{-2\xi\frac{u}{\phi}}e^{-\frac{u}{\phi^{2}}M_{-1}+\frac{1}{\phi}L_{-1}}\mathcal{O}\left(0,0\right)\mid 0\rangle\right\}^{\dagger}\phi(r,0,0)\mid 0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi^{-2\Delta}e^{-2\xi\frac{u}{\phi}}\langle\mathcal{O}|e^{-\frac{u}{\phi^{2}}M_{1}+\frac{1}{\phi}L_{1}}\phi(r,0,0)|0\rangle\,,

then using the second and the third equations in (
2.17
), it becomes

\displaystyle\phi^{-2\Delta}e^{-2\xi\frac{u}{\phi}}\langle\mathcal{O}|e^{-\frac{u}{\phi^{2}}M_{1}+\frac{1}{\phi}L_{1}}\phi(r,0,0)|0\rangle=

\displaystyle\phi^{-2\Delta}e^{-2\xi\frac{u}{\phi}}e^{-\frac{u}{\phi^{2}}2\partial_{r}}\langle\mathcal{O}|\phi(r,0,0)|0\rangle

(2.39)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi^{-2\Delta}e^{-2\xi\frac{u}{\phi}}e^{-\frac{u}{\phi^{2}}2\partial_{r}}r^{-\Delta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp{-2\xi\frac{u}{\phi}}\left(\frac{1}{r\phi^{2}-2u}\right)^{\Delta}\,.

Finally, we have derived the bulk-boundary propagator in the presence of the non-vanishing mass,

G^{\xi}(r,u,\phi)=\exp{-2\xi\frac{u}{\phi}}\left(\frac{1}{r\phi^{2}+2u}\right)^{\Delta}\,.

(2.40)

And it reduces to the massless case when simply setting the mass (or
\xi
) to zero.

2.4
Vector bulk reconstruction in
\operatorname{BMS}_{3}
/
\operatorname{CCFT}_{2}

We consider a massive vector field in the bulk, which can be described by the action

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{4}F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}m^{2}A^{\mu}A_{\mu}\,.

(2.41)

This model can be realized as a U(1) symmetry-broken gauge theory. The equations of motion for the vector field
A_{\mu}
are

\partial_{\mu}A^{\mu}=0\,,\qquad(-\partial^{2}+m^{2})A_{\mu}=0\,.

(2.42)

The vector field has two physical degrees of freedom. Without losing generality, we choose them to be
\{A^{u},A^{\phi}\}
, in terms of which the component
A^{r}
can be expressed as

A^{r}=\int^{\infty}_{r}(\partial_{u}A^{u}+\partial_{\phi}A^{\phi})dr\,.

(2.43)

There is more than one physical degree of freedom in the bulk field theory, thus there may exist more than one conformal family appearing in the reconstruction. For a specific conformal family, we make the ansatz

\displaystyle A^{u}(r,0,0)|0\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{J,L,M=0}U_{J,L}r^{-K}L_{-1}^{J}M_{-1}^{L}|\mathcal{O}^{u}\rangle\,,

(2.44)

\displaystyle A^{\phi}(r,0,0)|0\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{J,L,M=0}\Phi_{J,L}r^{-K}L_{-1}^{J}M_{-1}^{L}|\mathcal{O}^{\phi}\rangle\,.

Then the Poincaré symmetry transformations on the field,
[Q,A^{i}(X^{\mu})]=-\delta_{Q}A^{i}(X^{\mu})
, are almost the same except that we should replace the ordinary derivatives with Lie-derivatives,

\displaystyle=-r\partial_{r}A^{u}(r,0,0)-A^{u}(r,0,0)\,,

(2.45)

\displaystyle[L_{0},A^{\phi}(r,0,0)]

\displaystyle=-r\partial_{r}A^{\phi}(r,0,0)-A^{\phi}(r,0,0)\,,

\displaystyle[L_{0},A^{r}(r,0,0)]

\displaystyle=-r\partial_{r}A^{r}(r,0,0)+A^{r}(r,0,0)\,,

\displaystyle=0\,,

(2.46)

\displaystyle[L_{1},A^{\phi}(r,0,0)]

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{r}A^{u}\,,

\displaystyle[L_{1},A^{r}(r,0,0)]

\displaystyle=2rA^{\phi}(r,0,0)\,,

\displaystyle=2\partial_{r}A^{u}(r,0,0)\,,

(2.47)

\displaystyle[M_{1},A^{\phi}(r,0,0)]

\displaystyle=2\partial_{r}A^{\phi}(r,0,0)+\frac{2}{r}A^{\phi}(r,0,0)\,,

\displaystyle[M_{1},A^{r}(r,0,0)]

\displaystyle=2\partial_{r}A^{r}(r,0,0)\,,

\displaystyle=-A^{\phi}(r,0,0)\,,

(2.48)

\displaystyle[(M_{0}+\frac{1}{r}L_{-1}),A^{\phi}(r,0,0)]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{r^{2}}A^{r}(r,0,0)\,,

\displaystyle[(M_{0}+\frac{1}{r}L_{-1}),A^{r}(r,0,0)]

\displaystyle=0\,.

Substituting the ansatz into (
2.45
), we obtain

K=J+L+\Delta+1\,.

(2.49)

The difference of the expression for
K
is induced by the spin of the massless vector field.

The full computation of the bulk two-point functions could be quite complicated. But we can obtain bulk-boundary propagator directly, like in (
2.38
), and obtain

\langle A^{u}(r,u,\phi)\mathcal{O}^{u}(0,0)\rangle=\exp{-2\xi\frac{u}{\phi}}\frac{\phi^{2}}{\left(r\phi^{2}+2u\right)^{\Delta+1}}\,.

(2.50)

Here we have used the behavior of
A^{u}
in (
2.46

4
Conclusion and discussions

In this note, we presented a different point of view on bulk reconstruction in flat holography. We expressed a bulk field as a linear combination of BMS descendants of the highest weight representation, where the operator-state correspondence has already been applied. In contrast, for the induced representations, the operator-state correspondence has not been established. We found that we could not directly reconstruct a massless bulk field due to the weird behavior of the two-point functions of the primary operators of charge
\xi=0
in CCFT. Our prescription is to start with the reconstruction of a massive bulk field. Even though the reconstruction does not respect the Poincaré symmetry, which is in accordance with the fact that a massive particle can never reach the null infinity of flat spacetime, its massless limit is well-behaved. It not only respects the Poincaré symmetry but also can be used to derive nontrivial physical quantities like the bulk-boundary propagator. We managed to reproduce the bulk-boundary propagators for the scalars in both BMS
3
/CCFT
2
and BMS
4
/CCFT
3

[
46
]
.

In our study, we considered the highest-weight representation in CCFT. In particular, the ones with
\xi\neq 0
in
\operatorname{CCFT}_{3}
have not been studied carefully in the literature. It deserves to have more investigation on their properties.

In our construction, we only used the global part of the BMS symmetry. In
\operatorname{AdS}_{3}
/
\operatorname{CFT}_{2}
the Virasoro algebra, which is the asymptotic symmetry for
\operatorname{AdS}_{3}
, can completely control the behavior of the gravity because there is no local physical degree of freedom for the gravity in three dimensions. This could also be the case in
\operatorname{BMS}_{3}
/
\operatorname{CCFT}_{2}
. It is intriguing how to develop the quantum (at least semi-classical) gravity from the full BMS
3
symmetry. The situation in
\operatorname{BMS}_{4}
/CCFT
3
is more perplexing, as there do exist local physical degrees of freedom in 4d gravity. A natural question is to what extent the
\operatorname{BMS}_{4}
algebra can control the dynamics of the 4d gravity. We wish our study could shed new light on these issues.

Maximal Large Deviations and Slow Recurrences in Weakly Chaotic Systems
Leonid Bunimovich, Yaofeng Su

Abstract
We prove a maximal-type large deviation principle for dynamical systems with arbitrarily slow polynomial mixing rates. Also several applications, particularly to billiard systems, are presented.



1
Introduction

A fundamental Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem says that if
\mu
is a measure preserved by an ergodic dynamics
f:X\to X
, then almost surely for any function
\phi
with
\int\phi d\mu=0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi\circ f^{i}}{n}=0.

Equivalently, for any
\epsilon>0
,
\mu
-a.s.
x\in X
there is
N=N_{x}>0
, such that

\sup_{n\geq N}\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi\circ f^{i}(x)}{n}\Big|\leq\epsilon.

The large deviations approach provides quantitative estimates for
N
. If the dynamics is sufficiently strongly chaotic (e.g., it has fast mixing rates or, in other words, correlations decay exponentially) and
\phi
is sufficiently smooth, then there is a constant
C>0
, such that for any
n,N\geq 1

\mu\Big(\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi\circ f^{i}}{n}\Big|\geq\epsilon\Big)\approx e^{-Cn},

and

\mu\Big(\sup_{n\geq N}\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi\circ f^{i}}{n}\Big|\geq\epsilon\Big)\approx\sum_{n\geq N}e^{-Cn}\approx e^{-CN}.

If the dynamics is weakly chaotic (i.e., slow mixing rates, see e.g.
[
14
]
), then this estimate becomes weaker (see
[
14
]
for more details),

\mu\Big(\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi\circ f^{i}}{n}\Big|\geq\epsilon\Big)\precsim n^{-a}\text{ where }a>0.

Then, on contrary to the case of exponential estimate, a direct summation over
n
does not give analogous estimate

\mu\Big(\sup_{n\geq N}\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi\circ f^{i}}{n}\Big|\geq\epsilon\Big)\precsim N^{-a}\text{ where }a>0.

We call this type of estimate a
maximal large deviation
.

One of the motivations to consider maximal large deviations is due to a typical approach (called an inducing method) in ergodic theory. This approach is usually aimed on finding a reference set
Y\subsetneq X
and its first return time
R:Y\to\mathbb{N}\bigcup\{0\}
, in order to prove some nice property for the first return map
f^{R}:Y\to Y
. Then this property gets lifted to the original dynamics
f:X\to X
. This lifting technique relies on a quantitative estimate of the time
N
in
\sup_{n\geq N}\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}R\circ(f^{R})^{i}}{n}
, which is related to the maximal large deviation property considered in the present paper.

One of the main results (Theorem
3.1
) in the paper a proof of a maximal large deviation estimate for dynamical systems with arbitrarily slow polynomial mixing rates. Our estimates for maximal large deviations are optimal, in comparison to the corresponding estimates obtained in
[
12
,
11
]
(See more details in Remark
3.5
). Such optimal estimates provide somewhat better answers to various questions which arise in applications.

The main body of the present paper is devoted to applications of Theorem
3.1
. We improve/strengthen the results in in the papers
[
1
,
4
,
5
]
to the systems with arbitrarily slow polynomial mixing rates. More precisely,

1.

The paper
[
1
]
deduced the existence of a Gibbs-Markov-Young (GMY) structure, introduced in
[
22
]
, for sufficiently fast mixing rates. Our Theorems
4.5
and
4.8
show that an arbitrarily slow polynomial mixing rate is enough to guarantee the existence of a GMY structure.

2.

The paper
[
4
]
using the inducing method to study certain limit laws for one-dimensional open systems with fast mixing. Our Theorem
6.1
extends it to arbitrarily slow polynomially
mixing one-dimensional dynamics (e.g. to intermittent maps).

3.

The Theorem
5.11
derives a convergence rate to Poisson limit laws for arbitrarily slow polynomially mixing billiards systems (e.g. for stadium-type billiards), while the technique employed in
[
5
]
does not do it.

The structure of the paper is the following. The section
2
provides some necessary definitions and notations used throughout the paper. A proof of the maximal large deviations theorem is given in section
3
. The section
4
deals with Gibbs-Markov-Young structures. Applications to billiards and to one dimensional non-uniformly expanding maps are presented in the sections
5
and
6
, respectively.

Definitions and Notations

We start by introducing some notations used throughout the paper.

1.

C_{z}
denotes a constant depending on
z
.

2.

The notation “
a_{n}\precsim_{z}b_{n}
” (“
a_{n}=O_{z}(b_{n})
”) means that there is a constant
C_{z}\geq 1
such that (s.t.)
a_{n}\leq C_{z}b_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
, whereas the notation “
a_{n}\precsim b_{n}
” (or “
a_{n}=O(b_{n})
”) means that there is a constant
C\geq 1
such that
a_{n}\leq Cb_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
. Next, “
a_{n}\approx_{z}b_{n}
” and
a_{n}=C_{z}^{\pm 1}b_{n}
mean that there is a constant
C_{z}\geq 1
such that
C_{z}^{-1}b_{n}\leq a_{n}\leq C_{z}b_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
. Further, the notations “
a_{n}=C^{\pm 1}b_{n}
” and “
a_{n}\approx b_{n}
” means that there is a constant
C\geq 1
such that
C^{-1}b_{n}\leq a_{n}\leq Cb_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
. Finally, “
a_{n}=o(b_{n})
” means that
\lim_{n\to\infty}|a_{n}/b_{n}|=0
.

3.

The notation
\mathbb{P}
refers to a probability distribution on the probability space, where a random variable lives, and
\mathbb{E}
denotes the expectation of a random variable. We do not specify the probability space and the probability when we use these two notations if the context is clear.

4.

\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,3,\cdots\}
,
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{0,1,2,3,\cdots\}
.

5.

S^{1}:=[-\pi/2,\pi/2]
(endpoints are not identified).

6.

\mu_{A}
denotes a probability measure on the set
A
.

7.

\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{A})
denotes a tangent bundle of a (sub)manifold
\mathcal{A}
, while
\mathcal{T}_{x}(\mathcal{A})
denotes a tangent space at the point
x\in\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 2.1

(Transfer operators)
.

We say that
P
is a transfer operator associated to a dynamical system
(\mathcal{M},f,\mu_{\mathcal{M}})
, if it satisfies the relation
\int g\cdot P(\phi)d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}=\int g\circ f\cdot\phi d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\text{ for all }\phi\in L^{1},g\in L^{\infty}
. Suppose that the
\sigma
-algebra of
\mathcal{M}
is
\mathcal{B}
, then
[P(\phi)]\circ f=\mathbb{E}[\phi|f^{-1}\mathcal{B}]
almost surely.

3
A Theorem on Maximal Large Deviations

In this section the following main technical theorem (called a theorem on maximal large deviations) of this paper is proved. We conclude this section with a remark about the papers
[
12
,
11
]
.

Theorem 3.1

(Maximal large deviations)
.

Suppose that
\phi\in L^{\infty}
has zero mean. If there are constants
p\in\mathbb{N},C_{\phi}>0,\beta\in(0,p)
such that

||P^{n}(\phi)||^{p}_{p}\leq C_{\phi}n^{-\beta}\text{ for any }n\geq 1.

Then for any
\epsilon>0,N\geq 1

\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\Big(\sup_{n\geq N}\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi\circ f^{i}}{n}\Big|\geq\epsilon\Big)\precsim_{\epsilon,p,\beta}\max\{||\phi||^{p}_{\infty},C_{\phi}\}\cdot||\phi||^{p}_{\infty}\cdot N^{-\beta},

and

\Big|\Big|\sup_{n\geq N}\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi\circ f^{i}}{n}\Big|\Big|\Big|^{2p}_{2p}\precsim_{p,\beta}\max\{||\phi||^{p}_{\infty},C_{\phi}\}\cdot||\phi||^{p}_{\infty}\cdot N^{-\beta}.

In particular, if the function
\phi\in L^{\infty}
satisfies the inequality
||P^{n}(\phi)||_{1}\leq C_{\phi}n^{-\beta}
for some
C_{\phi}>0
and any
n\geq 1
, then for any
\epsilon>0

\displaystyle||P^{n}(\phi)||^{1+\left\lceil\beta\right\rceil}_{1+\left\lceil\beta\right\rceil}\leq C_{\phi}||\phi||^{\left\lceil\beta\right\rceil}_{\infty}n^{-\beta},

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\Big(\sup_{n\geq N}\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi\circ f^{i}}{n}\Big|\geq\epsilon\Big)\precsim_{\epsilon,\beta}\max\{||\phi||^{1+\left\lceil\beta\right\rceil}_{\infty},C_{\phi}||\phi||^{\left\lceil\beta\right\rceil}_{\infty}\}\cdot||\phi||^{1+\left\lceil\beta\right\rceil}_{\infty}\cdot N^{-\beta}.

Remark 3.2
.

Unlike
[
14
]
, we assume a certain decay rate of correlations in
L^{p}
for bounded observables, which will be used later to establish an improved estimate (a faster decay rate) for maximal large deviations for unbounded observables.

To prove Theorem
3.1
we will need two following results .

Lemma 3.3
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
hold. Then for any
N\geq 1
,

\big|\big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq N}\phi\circ f^{i}}{N}\big|\big|^{2p}_{2p}\precsim_{p,\beta}||\phi||^{p}_{\infty}C_{\phi}N^{-\beta}.

Proof.

Since
p>\beta
, we have for any
j\leq n\leq N
,

\displaystyle||\phi\cdot P^{n}(\sum_{i=j}^{n}\phi\circ f^{i})||_{p}

\displaystyle\leq||\phi||_{\infty}\cdot||P^{n}(\sum_{i=j}^{n}\phi\circ f^{i})||_{p}

\displaystyle\leq||\phi||_{\infty}\cdot\sum_{i=j}^{n}||P^{n-i}\phi||_{p}\precsim_{p,\beta}||\phi||_{\infty}\cdot C_{\phi}^{1/p}(n-1)^{1-\beta/p}.

By Rio’s inequality (see page 1738 of
[
14
]
), Definition
2.1
and because
f_{*}\mu=\mu
, we have

\displaystyle\int\Big|\sum_{i\leq N}\phi\circ f^{i}\Big|^{2p}d\mu

\displaystyle\precsim_{p}\Big[\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sup_{j\leq n}\big|\big|\phi\circ f^{n}\cdot\mathbb{E}\Big[(\sum_{i=j}^{n}\phi\circ f^{i})\Big|f^{-n}\mathcal{B}\Big]\big|\big|_{p}\Big]^{p}

\displaystyle\precsim_{p}\Big[\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sup_{j\leq n}\big|\big|\phi\cdot P^{n}(\sum_{i=j}^{n}\phi\circ f^{i})\big|\big|_{p}\Big]^{p}

\displaystyle\precsim_{p,\beta}||\phi||^{p}_{\infty}C_{\phi}\Big[\sum_{n=1}^{N}(n-1)^{1-\beta/p}\Big]^{p}\precsim_{p,\beta}||\phi||^{p}_{\infty}C_{\phi}N^{2p-\beta},

which implies that
\big|\big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq N}\phi\circ f^{i}}{N}\big|\big|^{2p}_{2p}\precsim_{p,\beta}||\phi||^{p}_{\infty}C_{\phi}N^{-\beta}
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let again the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
hold. Then for any
N\geq 1

\Big|\Big|\frac{\max_{n\leq N}|\sum_{n\leq i\leq N}\phi\circ f^{i}|}{N}\Big|\Big|^{2p}_{2p}\precsim_{p,\beta}\max\{||\phi||^{p}_{\infty},C_{\phi}\}||\phi||^{p}_{\infty}N^{-\beta}.

Proof.

The method used here is inspired by
[
19
]
. It follows from Lemma
3.3
that

\mathbb{E}|\sum_{i\leq N}\phi\circ f^{i}|^{2p}\leq g(N)^{p},\text{ where }g(N)\approx_{p,\beta}||\phi||_{\infty}C_{\phi}^{1/p}N^{2-\beta/p}.

Let
a\geq b
and
M_{a,b}:=\max_{b\leq n\leq a}|\sum_{n\leq i\leq a}\phi\circ f^{i}|
. If
n\geq N
then
|\sum_{n\leq i\leq 2N}\phi\circ f^{i}|^{2p}\leq M_{2N,N}^{2p}
. Now, if
n<N
then

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{n\leq i\leq 2N}\phi\circ f^{i}\Big|^{2p}

\displaystyle\leq\Big[M_{N,N}+\Big|\sum_{N\leq i\leq 2N}\phi\circ f^{i}\Big|\Big]^{2p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\leq 2p}C^{j}_{2p}\cdot M_{N,N}^{j}\cdot\Big|\sum_{N\leq i\leq 2N}\phi\circ f^{i}\Big|^{2p-j}

\displaystyle=M_{N,N}^{2p}+\Big|\sum_{N\leq i\leq 2N}\phi\circ f^{i}\Big|^{2p}+\sum_{0<j<2p}C^{j}_{2p}\cdot M_{N,N}^{j}\cdot\Big|\sum_{N\leq i\leq 2N}\phi\circ f^{i}\Big|^{2p-j}

\displaystyle\leq M_{N,N}^{2p}+M_{2N,N}^{2p}+\sum_{0<j<2p}C^{j}_{2p}\cdot M_{N,N}^{j}\cdot\Big|\sum_{N\leq i\leq 2N}\phi\circ f^{i}\Big|^{2p-j}.

Therefore,

M_{2N,2N}^{2p}\leq M_{2N,N}^{2p}+M_{N,N}^{2p}+\sum_{0<j<2p}C^{j}_{2p}\cdot M_{N,N}^{j}\cdot\Big|\sum_{N\leq i\leq 2N}\phi\circ f^{i}\Big|^{2p-j}.

From Hölder’s inequality, we obtain for any
j\in(0,2p)
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(M_{N,N}^{j}\cdot\Big|\sum_{N\leq i\leq 2N}\phi\circ f^{i}\Big|^{2p-j}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq(\mathbb{E}M_{N,N}^{2p})^{j/2p}\cdot\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big|\sum_{N\leq i\leq 2N}\phi\circ f^{i}\Big|^{2p}\Big)^{(2p-j)/2p}

\displaystyle\leq(\mathbb{E}M_{N,N}^{2p})^{j/2p}\cdot\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big|\sum_{i\leq N}\phi\circ f^{i}\Big|^{2p}\Big)^{(2p-j)/2p}

\displaystyle\leq(\mathbb{E}M_{N,N}^{2p})^{j/2p}\cdot g(N)^{(2p-j)/2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}M_{2N,2N}^{2p}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}M_{2N,N}^{2p}+\mathbb{E}M_{N,N}^{2p}+\sum_{0<j<2p}C^{j}_{2p}\cdot\mathbb{E}\Big(M_{N,N}^{j}\cdot\Big|\sum_{N\leq i\leq 2N}\phi\circ f^{i}\Big|^{2p-j}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}M_{N,N}^{2p}+\sum_{0<j<2p}C^{j}_{2p}\cdot(\mathbb{E}M_{N,N}^{2p})^{j/2p}g(N)^{(2p-j)/2}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}M_{2N+1,2N+1}^{2p}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}M_{2N+1,N}^{2p}+\mathbb{E}M_{N,N}^{2p}+\sum_{0<j<2p}C^{j}_{2p}\cdot(\mathbb{E}M_{N,N}^{2p})^{j/2p}g(N+1)^{(2p-j)/2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}M_{N+1,N+1}^{2p}+\mathbb{E}M_{N,N}^{2p}+\sum_{0<j<2p}C^{j}_{2p}\cdot(\mathbb{E}M_{N,N}^{2p})^{j/2p}g(N+1)^{(2p-j)/2}.

Suppose that
\mathbb{E}M_{N,N}^{2p}\leq Kg(N)^{p}
for any
N\geq 1
. We will determine now a value of
K>0
. First

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}M_{2N,2N}^{2p}

\displaystyle\leq 2Kg(N)^{p}+\sum_{0<j<2p}C_{2p}^{j}K^{j/2p}g(N)^{j/2}g(N)^{p-j/2}

\displaystyle=Kg(N)^{p}\cdot\Big(2+\sum_{0<j<2p}C_{2p}^{j}K^{\frac{j}{2p}-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=Kg(2N)^{p}\cdot\frac{2+\sum_{0<j<2p}C_{2p}^{j}K^{\frac{j}{2p}-1}}{2^{2p-\beta}}.

Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}M_{2N+1,2N+1}^{2p}

\displaystyle\leq Kg(N+1)^{p}+Kg(N)^{p}+\sum_{0<j<2p}C^{j}_{2p}\cdot K^{j/2p}g(N)^{j/2}g(N+1)^{(2p-j)/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2Kg(N+1)^{p}+\sum_{0<j<2p}C^{j}_{2p}\cdot K^{j/2p}g(N+1)^{j/2}g(N+1)^{(2p-j)/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2Kg(N+1)^{p}+\sum_{0<j<2p}C^{j}_{2p}\cdot K^{j/2p}g(N+1)^{p}

\displaystyle=Kg(2N)^{p}\cdot\Big(2+\sum_{0<j<2p}C_{2p}^{j}K^{\frac{j}{2p}-1}\Big)\cdot\frac{(N+1)^{2p-\beta}}{(2N)^{2p-\beta}}.

Note that
2p-\beta>p\geq 1
. Therefore,
(2+\sum_{0<j<2p}C_{2p}^{j}K^{\frac{j}{2p}-1})/2^{2p-\beta}\leq 1
, if
K=K_{p,\beta}
is sufficiently large. Further,
\Big(2+\sum_{0<j<2p}C_{2p}^{j}K^{\frac{j}{2p}-1}\Big)\cdot\frac{(N+1)^{2p-\beta}}{(2N)^{2p-\beta}}\leq 1
if
K=K_{p,\beta}
, and for any sufficiently large positive integer
N\geq N_{p,\beta}
. Then
\mathbb{E}M_{N,N}^{2p}\precsim_{p,\beta}g(N)^{p}
for any
N\geq N_{p,\beta}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\Big|\Big|\frac{\max_{n\leq N}|\sum_{n\leq i\leq N}\phi\circ f^{i}|}{N}\Big|\Big|^{2p}_{2p}\precsim_{p,\beta}||\phi||^{p}_{\infty}C_{\phi}N^{-\beta}

If
N\leq N_{p,\beta}
, then

\displaystyle\Big|\Big|\frac{\max_{n\leq N}|\sum_{n\leq i\leq N}\phi\circ f^{i}|}{N}\Big|\Big|^{2p}_{2p}\leq||\phi||^{2p}_{\infty}\leq||\phi||^{2p}_{\infty}N_{p,\beta}^{\beta}N^{-\beta}.

Therefore, for any
N\geq 1

\displaystyle\Big|\Big|\frac{\max_{n\leq N}|\sum_{n\leq i\leq N}\phi\circ f^{i}|}{N}\Big|\Big|^{2p}_{2p}\precsim_{p,\beta}\max\{||\phi||^{p}_{\infty},C_{\phi}\}\cdot||\phi||^{p}_{\infty}N^{-\beta},

which completes the proof.
∎

We turn now to a proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Our proof in many respects goes along the lines of
[
19
]
. Let
M_{N}:=\sup_{n\geq N}\big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi\circ f^{i}}{n}\big|
for any
N\geq 1
. If
n\geq 2N
, then clearly
\big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi\circ f^{i}}{n}\big|\leq M_{2N}
. Now, if
n\in[N,2N]
, then

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi\circ f^{i}}{n}\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq 3N}\phi\circ f^{i}}{n}-\frac{\sum_{n\leq i\leq 3N}\phi\circ f^{i}}{n}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3N}{n}\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq 3N}\phi\circ f^{i}}{3N}\Big|+\frac{3N-n}{n}\Big|\frac{\sum_{n\leq i\leq 3N}\phi\circ f^{i}}{3N-n}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq 3\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq 3N}\phi\circ f^{i}}{3N}\Big|+\frac{3N-n}{n}\frac{\sup_{N\leq n\leq 2N}|\sum_{n\leq i\leq 3N}\phi\circ f^{i}|}{N}

\displaystyle\leq 3\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq 3N}\phi\circ f^{i}}{3N}\Big|+4\frac{\sup_{N\leq n\leq 3N}|\sum_{n\leq i\leq 3N}\phi\circ f^{i}|}{2N}.

Therefore, for any
n\geq N

\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi\circ f^{i}}{n}\Big|\leq M_{2N}+3\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq 3N}\phi\circ f^{i}}{3N}\Big|+4\frac{\sup_{N\leq n\leq 3N}|\sum_{n\leq i\leq 3N}\phi\circ f^{i}|}{2N},

i.e.,

M_{N}\leq M_{2N}+3\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq 3N}\phi\circ f^{i}}{3N}\Big|+4\frac{\sup_{N\leq n\leq 3N}|\sum_{n\leq i\leq 3N}\phi\circ f^{i}|}{2N}.

Moreover,

N^{\beta}M_{N}\leq 2^{-\beta}(2N)^{\beta}M_{2N}+N^{\beta}\Big[3\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq 3N}\phi\circ f^{i}}{3N}\Big|+4\frac{\sup_{N\leq n\leq 3N}|\sum_{n\leq i\leq 3N}\phi\circ f^{i}|}{2N}\Big].

Let
C_{N}:=3\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq 3N}\phi\circ f^{i}}{3N}\Big|+4\frac{\sup_{N\leq n\leq 2N}|\sum_{n\leq i\leq 3N}\phi\circ f^{i}|}{2N},c_{N}:=N^{\beta}C_{N}
,
b_{N}:=N^{\beta}M_{N}
. Then

\displaystyle b_{N}\leq c_{N}+2^{-\beta}b_{2N}\leq c_{N}+2^{-\beta}(c_{2N}+2^{-\beta}b_{2^{2}N})=c_{N}+2^{-\beta}c_{2N}+2^{-2\beta}b_{2^{2}N}\leq\cdots.

Inductively we have for any
n,N\geq 1
that

b_{N}\leq\sum_{i\leq n-1}2^{-i\beta}c_{2^{i}N}+2^{-n\beta}b_{2^{n}N}=\sum_{i\leq n-1}2^{-i\beta}c_{2^{i}N}+N^{\beta}M_{2^{n}N}.

By Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem,
\lim_{n\to 0}M_{2^{n}N}=0
almost surely. Let now
n\to\infty
. Then for any
N\geq 1
we have

b_{N}\leq\sum_{i\geq 0}2^{-i\beta}c_{2^{i}N}\text{ almost surely, and }||b_{N}||_{2p}\leq\sum_{i\geq 0}2^{-i\beta}||c_{2^{i}N}||_{2p},

Next we will estimate
||c_{2^{i}N}||_{2p}
and show that it is uniformly bounded. By Lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle||C_{N}||_{2p}

\displaystyle\leq 3\Big|\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq 3N}\phi\circ f^{i}}{3N}\Big|\Big|_{2p}+4\Big|\Big|\frac{\sup_{N\leq n\leq 3N}|\sum_{n\leq i\leq 3N}\phi\circ f^{i}|}{2N}\Big|\Big|_{2p}

\displaystyle=3\Big|\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq 3N}\phi\circ f^{i}}{3N}\Big|\Big|_{2p}+4\Big|\Big|\frac{\sup_{n\leq 2N}|\sum_{n\leq i\leq 2N}\phi\circ f^{i}|}{2N}\Big|\Big|_{2p}

\displaystyle\precsim_{p,\beta}||\phi||^{1/2}_{\infty}C_{\phi}^{1/(2p)}N^{-\beta/(2p)}+\max\{||\phi||^{p}_{\infty},C_{\phi}\}^{1/2p}\cdot||\phi||^{1/2}_{\infty}N^{-\beta/(2p)}

\displaystyle\precsim_{p,\beta}\max\{||\phi||^{p}_{\infty},C_{\phi}\}^{1/2p}\cdot||\phi||^{1/2}_{\infty}N^{-\beta/(2p)}.

This relation implies that
||c_{N}||_{2p}\precsim_{p,\beta}\max\{||\phi||^{p}_{\infty},C_{\phi}\}^{1/2p}\cdot||\phi||^{1/2}_{\infty}N^{\beta-\beta/(2p)}
, and

\displaystyle||b_{N}||_{2p}

\displaystyle\precsim_{p,\beta}\max\{||\phi||^{p}_{\infty},C_{\phi}\}^{1/2p}\cdot||\phi||^{1/2}_{\infty}\sum_{i\geq 0}2^{-i\beta}(2^{i}N)^{\beta-\beta/(2p)}

\displaystyle\precsim_{p,\beta}\max\{||\phi||^{p}_{\infty},C_{\phi}\}^{1/2p}\cdot||\phi||^{1/2}_{\infty}N^{\beta-\beta/(2p)}.

Therefore,
||M_{N}||_{2p}=N^{-\beta}||b_{N}||_{2p}\precsim_{p,\beta}\max\{||\phi||^{p}_{\infty},C_{\phi}\}^{1/2p}\cdot||\phi||^{1/2}_{\infty}N^{-\beta/(2p)}
, and

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\{M_{N}\geq\epsilon\}\precsim_{\epsilon,p,\beta}\max\{||\phi||^{p}_{\infty},C_{\phi}\}\cdot||\phi||^{p}_{\infty}N^{-\beta},

which completes a proof of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

The result of Lemma
3.3
is obtained for
\phi\in L^{\infty}
. It will be used later to deduce an estimate of large deviations from a rate of mixing. It was proved in
[
12
,
11
]
that large deviations and maximal large deviations have similar decay rates for
L^{2}
-observables
\phi
. Unlike to
[
12
,
11
]
, one can see from the proof of Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
3.4
that the estimates for
\phi\in L^{\infty}
can be used for
\phi\in L^{2p}
, and that large deviations and maximal large deviations share exactly the same decay rates for
L^{2p}
-observables
\phi
. Instead of explicitly writing down this optimal result here, we consider in Theorem
3.1
only the case when
\phi\in L^{\infty}
, since it is technically easier to derive mixing rates for bounded observables, and such observables are a natural choice in applications, which especially plays a crucial role in billiards considered in the section
5
.

4
Gibbs-Markov-Young Structures for Some Weakly Chaotic Dynamical Systems

We start with the definition of the Gibbs-Markov-Young structures. Then we apply Theorem
3.1
to the problem of existence of Gibbs-Markov-Young structures, which was partially addressed in
[
1
]
.

Let
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
be a piecewise
C^{1+}
-endomorphism of a Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{M}
. Denote by
d
the Riemannian distance in
\mathcal{M}
, and by
\Leb
a normalised volume form on
\mathcal{M}
. By
C^{1+}
we denote the class of continuously differentiable maps with
Hölder continuous derivatives.

The definition of a Gibbs-Markov-Young structure below, can also be found in the section 1.1 of
[
22
]
or Definition 1.1 of
[
1
]
.

Definition 4.1

(Gibbs-Markov-Young (GMY) structures)
.

Suppose that there exists a ball
\Delta_{0}\subset\mathcal{M}
,
a countable partition
\mathcal{P}
(mod
0
) of
\Delta_{0}
into topological balls
U
with smooth boundaries, and a return time function
R:\Delta_{0}\to\mathbb{N}
, which is constant on each element of
\mathcal{P}
. We say that
f
admits a Gibbs–Markov-Young (GMY) structure if the following properties hold:

1.

Markov property: for each
U\in\mathcal{P}
and
R=R(U)
,
f^{R}:U\to\Delta_{0}
is a
C^{1+}
-diffeomorphism (and, in particular, it is a bijection). Thus the induced map
F:\Delta_{0}\to\Delta_{0}
,
F(x)=f^{R(x)}(x)
, is defined almost everywhere and satisfies a classical Markov property.

2.

Uniform expansion: there exists
\rho\in(0,1)
such that for almost all
x\in\Delta_{0}
we have
||DF(x)^{-1}||\leq\rho
. In particular the separation time
s(x,y)
, which is equal to the maximum integer such that
F^{i}(x)
and
F^{i}(y)
belong to the same element of the partition
\mathcal{P}
for all
i\leq s(x,y)
, is defined and is a finite for almost all
x,y\in\Delta_{0}
.

3.

Bounded distortion: there exists
C>0
such that for any two points
x,y\in\Delta_{0}
with
s(x,y)<\infty

\Big|\frac{\det DF(x)}{\det DF(y)}-1\Big|\leq C\rho^{s(F(x),F(y))},

where
\det DF
is the Radon-Nikodym derivative of
F
with respect to
\Leb
on
\Delta_{0}
.

Theorem
3.1
implies the following corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

If
f
admits a Gibbs–Markov-Young structure and
\Leb(x\in\Delta_{0}:R(x)>N)\approx N^{1+\beta}
, then there exists an open and dense set
H_{0}
in the space of Hölder functions, such that for any zero mean function
\phi\in H_{0}
, and for any
\epsilon>0
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\Big(\sup_{n\geq N}\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi\circ f^{i}}{n}\Big|\geq\epsilon\Big)}{\log N}=-\beta.

Proof.

The following lower bound estimate was proved in
[
14
]
.

\displaystyle N^{-\beta}\precsim\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\Big(\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq N}\phi\circ f^{i}}{N}\Big|\geq\epsilon\Big)\precsim\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\Big(\sup_{n\geq N}\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi\circ f^{i}}{n}\Big|\geq\epsilon\Big).

Together with the upper bound from Theorem
3.1
it concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Note that the Corollary 1 provides a stronger statement than the Theorem 1.2 in
[
14
]
.

In what follows we will consider GMY structures for an
C^{1+}
-endomorphism
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
be the space of Hölder continuous functions on
\mathcal{M}
with a Hölder constant
\alpha>0
. Recall that the Hölder norm of a function
\phi
is given by
||\phi||_{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}}:=||\phi||_{\infty}+\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|}{d(x,y)^{\alpha}}<\infty
.

Definition 4.4
.

We say that a measure
\mu_{\mathcal{M}}
on
\mathcal{M}
is expanding if
\int\log||Df^{-1}||d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\in(-\infty,0)
.

4.1
C^{1+}
-local diffeomorphism

Let
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
be a
C^{1+}
-local diffeomorphism, i.e.,
Df(x)^{-1},Df(x)
are well-defined, uniformly bounded for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
, and
\log||\big[Df(\cdot)\big]^{-1}||
is Hölder continuous.

Theorem 4.5

(Existence of GMY I)
.

Suppose that
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
is a
C^{1+}
-local diffeomorphism, and
f

admits an ergodic expanding absolutely continuous (w.r.t.
\Leb
) invariant probability measure
\mu_{\mathcal{M}}
. Further, we assume that there is
\beta>0
such that for any zero mean function
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
and for any
n\geq 1
,
\int|P^{n}(\phi)|d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\leq C_{\phi}n^{-\beta}
for some constant
C_{\phi}>0
. Then
f
admits a Gibbs-Markov-Young structure.

Remark 4.6
.

Theorem 2 improves Theorem A in
[
1
]
by allowing
\beta
to be just any positive number. This means that the dynamics has a GMY structure if the correlations decay polynomially or superpolynomially).

Proof of Theorem
4.5
.

We start with proving the first statement. Consider a zero mean Hölder function
\phi(x):=\log||Df(x)^{-1}||-\int\log||Df(x)^{-1}||d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}
. Then
\int|P^{n}(\phi)|d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\leq C_{\phi}n^{-\beta}
for some
\beta>0
and
C_{\phi}>0
. Let

\mathcal{E}:=\inf\Big\{N:\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi\circ f^{i}}{n}\leq-\frac{\int\log||Df(x)^{-1}||d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}}{2}\text{ for all }n\geq N\Big\},

\mathcal{N}:=\inf\Big\{N:\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi\circ f^{i}}{n}\Big|\leq-\frac{\int\log||Df(x)^{-1}||d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}}{2}\text{ for all }n\geq N\Big\},

Clearly
\{\mathcal{E}>N\}\subseteq\{\mathcal{N}>N\}\subseteq\Big\{\sup_{n\geq N}\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi\circ f^{i}}{n}\Big|\geq-\frac{\int\log||Df(x)^{-1}||d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}}{2}\Big\}
. Following the argument in the proof of Theorem 3.1 of
[
1
]
, we obtain that there is a ball
\Delta_{0}
such that

\Leb(\mathcal{E}>N\bigcap\Delta_{0})\precsim\mu_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{E}>N\bigcap\Delta_{0})\precsim N^{-\beta},

where the last “
\precsim
” is due to Theorem
3.1
. It follows from the proof of Theorem 3.1 in
[
1
]
that a Gibbs-Markov-Young structure with a return time
R
exists, and that
\Leb\{R>N\}\precsim N^{-\beta}
.
∎

4.2
C^{1+}
-local diffeomorphisms outside critical/singular sets

Definition 4.7
.

We say that
x
is a critical point if
Df(x)
is not invertible, and
x
is a singular point if
Df(x)
does not exist. Let
\mathcal{C}
denotes the set of critical and singular points, and
d(x,\mathcal{C})
is
the distance between a point
x\in\mathcal{M}
and the set
\mathcal{C}
. We say that the set
\mathcal{C}
of critical/singular points is nondegenerate if there are constants
B,d,\eta>0
, such that for all
\epsilon>0
the following four conditions hold.

1.

\Leb(\{x:d(x,\mathcal{C})\leq\epsilon\})\leq B\epsilon^{d}
(in particular
\Leb(\mathcal{C})=0
);

2.

B^{-1}d(x,\mathcal{C})^{\eta}\leq||Df(x)v||\leq Bd(x,\mathcal{C})^{-\eta}
for every
x\in\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathcal{C}
and
v\in\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{M}
with
||v||=1
;

3.

|\log||Df(x)^{-1}||-\log||Df(y)^{-1}|||\leq B|\log(d(y,\mathcal{C}))-\log(d(x,\mathcal{C}))|
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathcal{C}
;

4.

|\log\det|Df(x)|-\log\det|Df(y)||\leq B|\log d(y,\mathcal{C})-\log d(x,\mathcal{C})|
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathcal{C}
.

Theorem 4.8

(Existence of GMY II)
.

Suppose that
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
is a
C^{1+}
-local diffeomorphism outside a nondegenerate critical
set
\mathcal{C}
, and
f

admits an ergodic expanding absolutely continuous (w.r.t.
\Leb
) invariant probability measure
\mu_{\mathcal{M}}
with
\frac{d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}}{d\Leb}\in L^{1+\delta}
for some
\delta>0
. Let also there is such
\beta>0
that for any zero mean
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
,
\int|P^{n}(\phi)|d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\leq C_{\phi}n^{-\beta}
for some constant
C_{\phi}>0
and any
n\geq 1
. Then
f
admits a Gibbs-Markov-Young structure (see Definition
4.1
).

Remark 4.9
.

Theorem 3 proves the result of Theorem C in
[
1
]
for any positive
\beta
. Therefore the dynamics considered in this theorem has a GMY structure for all cases of polynomial or superpolynomial decay of correlations.

Proof of Theorem
4.8
.

Fix
\epsilon>0
. Define
\phi_{1}:=\log||Df^{-1}||
,
\phi_{3}(x):=-\log d_{\delta}(x,\mathcal{C})
,

\displaystyle\phi_{2}(x):=\begin{cases}-\log d(x,\mathcal{C}),&d(x,\mathcal{C})<\delta,\\
\frac{\log\delta}{\delta}(d(x,\mathcal{C})-2\delta),&\delta\leq d(x,\mathcal{C})<2\delta,\\
0,&d(x,\mathcal{C})\geq 2\delta,\\
\end{cases}\quad d_{\delta}(x,\mathcal{C}):=\begin{cases}d(x,\mathcal{C}),&d(x,\mathcal{C})<\delta,\\
1,&d(x,\mathcal{C})\geq\delta,\\
\end{cases}

where
\delta>0
is so small that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi_{3}\circ f^{i}}{n}=\int\phi_{3}d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi_{2}\circ f^{i}}{n}=\int\phi_{2}d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\leq\epsilon

(see (4.2) in
[
1
]
). Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}:=\inf\Big\{N:\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi_{1}\circ f^{i}}{n}\leq-\frac{\int\phi_{1}d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}}{2}\text{ for all }n\geq N\Big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{1}:=\inf\Big\{N:\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi_{1}\circ f^{i}}{n}-\int\phi_{1}d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\Big|\leq-\frac{\int\phi_{1}d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}}{2}\text{ for all }n\geq N\Big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{2}:=\inf\Big\{N:\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi_{3}\circ f^{i}}{n}\leq 2\epsilon\text{ for all }n\geq N\Big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{2}:=\inf\Big\{N:\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi_{3}\circ f^{i}}{n}-\int\phi_{3}d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\Big|\leq\epsilon\text{ for all }n\geq N\Big\}.

Clearly

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{E}_{1}>N\}\subseteq\{\mathcal{N}_{1}>N\}\subseteq\Big\{\sup_{n\geq N}\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi_{1}\circ f^{i}}{n}-\int\phi_{1}d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\Big|\geq-\frac{\int\phi_{1}d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}}{2}\Big\},

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{E}_{2}>N\}\subseteq\{\mathcal{N}_{2}>N\}\subseteq\Big\{\sup_{n\geq N}\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi_{3}\circ f^{i}}{n}-\int\phi_{3}d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\Big|\geq\epsilon\Big\}.

It follows from the proof of Theorem 4.2 in
[
1
]
that GMY structure with
\Leb(R>N)\precsim N^{-a}
exists, if
\mu_{\mathcal{M}}(\{\mathcal{E}_{2}>N\}\bigcup\{\mathcal{E}_{2}>N\})
is of order
O(N^{-a})
for some
a>0
. Therefore we just need to find decay rates for
\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\big\{\sup_{n\geq N}\big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi_{3}\circ f^{i}}{n}-\int\phi_{3}d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\big|\geq\epsilon\big\}
and
\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\big\{\sup_{n\geq N}\big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\phi_{1}\circ f^{i}}{n}-\int\phi_{1}d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\big|\geq-\frac{\int\phi_{1}d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}}{2}\big\}
. In order to do it we truncate the functions
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{3}
. Define

\phi_{1,k}:=\phi_{1}\mathbbm{1}_{|\phi_{1}|\leq k}+k\mathbbm{1}_{\phi_{1}>k}-k\mathbbm{1}_{\phi_{1}<-k},\quad\phi_{3,k}:=\phi_{3}\mathbbm{1}_{|\phi_{3}|\leq k}+k\mathbbm{1}_{\phi_{3}>k}-k\mathbbm{1}_{\phi_{3}<-k}.

Let
j=1,3
and
u=\frac{-\int\phi_{1}d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}}{2},\epsilon
, respectively. By making use of the maximal ergodic theorem we obtain that

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\Big(\sup_{n\geq N}\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\big({\phi_{j}}-\mathbb{E}{\phi_{j}}\big)\circ f^{i}}{n}\Big|>u\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\Big(\sup_{n\geq N}\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\big(\phi_{j,k}-\mathbb{E}\phi_{j,k}\big)\circ f^{i}}{n}\Big|>\frac{u}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\Big(\sup_{n\geq N}\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\big[\phi_{j}-\phi_{j,k}-\mathbb{E}\big(\phi_{j}-\phi_{j,k}\big)\big]\circ f^{i}}{n}\Big|>\frac{u}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\precsim_{u}\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\Big(\sup_{n\geq N}\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\big(\phi_{j,k}-\mathbb{E}\phi_{j,k}\big)\circ f^{i}}{n}\Big|>\frac{u}{2}\Big)+\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\Big(\big|\mathbb{E}(\phi_{j}-\phi_{j,k})\big|>\frac{u}{4}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\Big(\sup_{n\geq 1}\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\big(\phi_{j}-\phi_{j,k}\big)\circ f^{i}}{n}\Big|>\frac{u}{4}\Big)

\displaystyle\precsim_{u}\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\Big(\sup_{n\geq N}\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\big(\phi_{j,k}-\mathbb{E}\phi_{j,k}\big)\circ f^{i}}{n}\Big|>\frac{u}{2}\Big)+||\phi_{j}-\phi_{j,k}||_{1}

By Lemma 4.3 of
[
1
]
we have for some
\xi>0
that
||\phi_{j}-\phi_{j,k}||_{1}=\int_{t\geq k}\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\{|\phi_{j,k}|\geq t\}dt\precsim e^{-\xi k}
. Apply now the Theorem
3.1
and Lemma 5.1 of
[
1
]
. Here we choose
k
following the argument on the page 1226 of this paper. Thus

\mu_{\mathcal{M}}\Big(\sup_{n\geq N}\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\big(\phi_{j}-\mathbb{E}\phi_{j}\big)\circ f^{i}}{n}\Big|>u\Big)\precsim_{u,\delta}N^{-\beta+\delta}\text{ for a small }\delta>0.

Therefore, a GMY structure with
\Leb\{R>N\}\precsim_{\delta}N^{-\beta+\delta}
exists for sufficiently small
\delta>0
.
∎

5
Applications to Two-dimensional Billiards

In this section we study some two-dimensional billiards. First we introduce several basic notions and properties of billiards, and give a definition of Chernov-Markarian-Zhang structures (see Definition
5.4
). Then we will apply Theorem
3.1
to prove analogous statement (Theorem
5.11
) for this structure. Finally, applications to some specific classes of billiards will be considered.

Let
Q
be a bounded connected domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(sometimes called a billiard table). The boundary
\partial Q
consists of finitely many (at least)
C^{3}
-smooth curves. Each smooth component of the boundary
\partial Q
is either flat, or dispersing (convex inwards), or focusing (convex outwards of a billiard table).

We assume that each focusing boundary component is an arc of a circle, but not a full circle. Moreover, the corresponding full circle does not intersect any other boundary component. In what follows we will call this condition the simplest focusing chaos or SFC-condition. These billiards belong to the class of hyperbolic dynamical systems.

The phase space of a billiard map is
\mathcal{M}:=\partial Q\times S^{1}
. A billiard map
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
sends a configuration of unit vectors with footpoints on the boundary at reflection times to the
configuration just after the next moments of reflection off the boundary. Denote
x=(q,\phi)\in\mathcal{M}
,
\pi_{\partial Q}x=q
,
dx=(dq,d\phi)\in\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{M}
. Let
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}(q)
be the curvature of the boundary at a point
q\in\partial Q
.

We list now several basic properties and formulas (see e.g.
[
7
]
) for the class of two-dimensional billiards under consideration.

The billiard map
f
preserves a probability measure

d\mu_{\mathcal{M}}:=(2\Leb_{\partial Q}\partial Q)^{-1}\cos\phi d\phi dq,

(5.1)

which is the projection of the phase volume (for the billiard flow) to the boundary.

There exist unstable and stable cones
C^{u},C^{s}\subseteq\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{M})
defined as follows. For any
x=(q,\phi)

\displaystyle C^{u}_{x}:=\{(dq,d\phi)\in\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{M}:\mathcal{K}\leq d\phi/dq\leq\infty\}

for dispersing and flat boundary components, and

\displaystyle C^{u}_{x}:=\{(dq,d\phi)\in\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{M}:\mathcal{K}\leq d\phi/dq\leq 0\}

for focusing components, and

\displaystyle C^{s}_{x}:=\{(dq,d\phi)\in\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{M}:-\infty\leq d\phi/dq\leq-\mathcal{K}\}

for dispersing and flat boundary components and

\displaystyle C^{s}_{x}:=\{(dq,d\phi)\in\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{M}:0\leq d\phi/dq\leq-\mathcal{K}\}

for focusing components.

Let
C^{u}:=\bigcup_{x\in\mathcal{M}}C^{u}_{x}
,
C^{s}:=\bigcup_{x\in\mathcal{M}}C^{s}_{x}
in
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{M}
. Then for all
m\geq 1
,

(Df)C^{u}\subseteq C^{u},\quad(Df)^{-1}C^{s}\subseteq C^{s},

\displaystyle(Df)^{m}\mathcal{T}(\{q\}\times S^{1})\subseteq C^{u},\quad(Df)^{-m}\mathcal{T}(\{q\}\times S^{1})\subseteq C^{s}.

(5.2)

Now we fix a reference subset
X\subseteq\mathcal{M}
with
\mu_{\mathcal{M}}(X)>0
. The first return time to
X
is
R:X\to\mathbb{N}
, and the first return map is
f^{R}:X\to X
. Suppose that
X
can be partitioned into countably many connected pieces

X=\bigcup_{i}X_{i},

(5.3)

so that
R
is constant on each
X_{i}
, and

\mu_{\mathcal{M}}(\partial X_{i})=0,\quad\interior{X_{i}}\bigcap\interior{X_{j}}=\emptyset\text{ for }i\neq j.

Definition 5.1

(Singularities in
\mathcal{M}
and
X
)
.

Denote by
\mathbb{S}\subseteq X
a singularity set for
f^{R}
. We assume that
\mathbb{S}
has zero Lebesgue measure, and that
\mathbb{S}^{c}\subseteq X
consists of countably many open connected components. Let the set
\mathbb{S}_{0}
consists of the discontinuities and the points where the map
f^{R}
is not differentiable. Singularities of billiards include orbits which hit singularities of the boundary of the billiard table and orbits tangent to dispersing components of the boundary. Then
\mathbb{S}_{0}\subseteq\mathbb{S}
.

We extend the singularities in
X
to the singularities
\bigcup_{i}\bigcup_{0\leq k<R|_{X_{i}}}f^{k}(\mathbb{S}\bigcap X_{i})
in
\mathcal{M}
, and denote this set by
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{S}}
.

Definition 5.2

(Unstable and stable manifolds/curves in
X
)
.

An unstable (resp. stable) manifold in
X
is a connected component of
(\bigcup_{i\geq 0}(f^{R})^{i}\mathbb{S})^{c}
(resp.
(\bigcup_{i\geq 0}(f^{R})^{-i}\mathbb{S})^{c}
. A closed and connected part of the unstable (resp. stable) manifold will be called an unstable (resp. stable) disk. All (un)stable manifolds/disks are one-dimensional.

We denote each unstable (resp. stable) manifold/disk by
\gamma^{u}
(resp.
\gamma^{s}
), and its tangent vectors by
v^{u}
(resp.
v^{s}
).

Unstable (resp. stable) curve by
\boldsymbol{{\gamma}^{u}}
(resp.
\boldsymbol{{\gamma}^{s}}
) is a smooth curve, such that
\mathcal{T}\boldsymbol{{\gamma}^{u}}\in C^{u}
(resp.
\mathcal{T}\boldsymbol{{\gamma}^{s}}\in C^{s}
).

Denote the length of (un)stable manifolds/curves
\gamma
by
\diam\gamma
.

Remark 5.3
.

Note that (un)stable manifolds are also (un)stable curves, but not vice versa. Observe also that (un)stable cones in
[
7
]
are in
\mathcal{T}(X)
, while our
C^{u},C^{s}
are in
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{M})
. So the (un)stable curves in
[
7
]
are different from ours
\boldsymbol{\gamma^{u}}
and
\boldsymbol{\gamma^{s}}
. We will need
\boldsymbol{\gamma^{u}},C^{u}
and
\boldsymbol{\gamma^{s}},C^{s}
to address the questions considered in the present section.

Definition 5.4

(Chernov-Markarian-Zhang (CMZ) structures)
.

We say that
(X,f^{R})
is a CMZ structure of a two-dimensional mixing billiard
(\mathcal{M},f,\mu_{\mathcal{M}})
if there are constants
C>0
and
\beta\in(0,1)
, such that the following conditions hold

1.

Hyperbolicity. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
v^{u}
and
v^{s}
,

\displaystyle|D(f^{R})^{n}v^{u}|\geq C\beta^{-n}|v^{u}|,\quad|D(f^{R})^{n}v^{s}|\leq C\beta^{n}|v^{s}|,

where
|\cdot|
is the Riemannian metric induced from
\mathcal{M}
to (un)stable manifolds.

2.

SRB measures and u-SRB measures. Dynamical system
(X,f^{R},\mu_{X})
is mixing, where
\mu_{X}:=\frac{\mu_{\mathcal{M}}}{\mu_{\mathcal{M}}(X)}\big|_{X}
. The conditional distribution on each
\gamma^{u}
(say
\mu_{\gamma^{u}}
) is absolutely continuous w.r.t. Lebesgue measure
\Leb_{\gamma^{u}}
on
\gamma^{u}
.

3.

Distortion bounds. Let
d_{\gamma^{u}}(\cdot,\cdot)
be the distance measured along
\gamma^{u}
. By
\det D^{u}f^{R}
we denote the Jacobian of
Df^{R}
along the unstable manifolds. Then, if
x,y\in X
belong to a
\gamma^{u}
, and
(f^{R})^{n}
is smooth on
\gamma^{u}
, the following relation holds

\displaystyle\log\frac{\det D^{u}(f^{R})^{n}(x)}{\det D^{u}(f^{R})^{n}(y)}\leq\psi\left[d_{\gamma^{u}}\Big((f^{R})^{n}x,(f^{R})^{n}y\Big)\right],

where
\psi(\cdot)
is some function, which does not depend on
\gamma^{u}
, and
\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\psi(s)=0
.

4.

Bounded curvatures. The curvatures of all
\gamma^{u}
are uniformly
bounded by some constant
C
.

5.

Absolute continuity. Consider a holonomy map
h:\gamma^{u}_{1}\to\gamma^{u}_{2}
, which maps a point
x\in\gamma^{u}_{1}
to a point
h(x)\in\gamma^{u}_{2}
, such that both
x
and
h(x)
belong to the same
\gamma^{s}
. We assume that the holonomy map satisfies the following relation

\displaystyle\frac{\det D^{u}(f^{R})^{n}(x)}{\det D^{u}(f^{R})^{n}\big(h(x)\big)}=C^{\pm 1}\text{ for all }n\geq 1\text{ and }x\in\gamma^{u}_{1},

6.

Growth lemmas. There exist
N\in\mathbb{N}
, a sufficiently small
\delta_{0}>0
, and
constants
\kappa,\sigma>0
, which satisfy the following conditions. For any
sufficiently small
\delta>0
and for any disk on a smooth stable manifold
\gamma^{u}
with
\diam\gamma^{u}\leq\delta_{0}
, denote by
U_{\delta}\subseteq\gamma^{u}
a
\delta
-neighborhood of the subset

5.11
holds for stadium billiards.

Remark 5.31
.

Although the Assumption
5.9
is verified only for stadium-type billiards, we claim without proof that Theorem
5.11
holds for squashes, circular flowers, and for a special class of semi-dispersing billiards (see Figures
2
,
4
and
4
). It is true because these billiards share similar structures of singularities with those in stadium billiards. Moreover, the analysis for the stadium-type billiards is technically slightly more complicated
than for the other three types of billiards because a stadium has a continuous family of period two orbits, and it has focusing boundary components, rather than dispersing components in semi-dispersing billiards. Therefore for stadiums one need to consider more cases. We skip completely similar verification of the Assumption
5.9
for other classes of billiards.

Lemma 6.5

(Maximal large deviations for
R_{n}:\Delta_{n}\to\mathbb{N}
)
.

For any sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

and
\delta\in(1/2,1)
one has

\mu_{\Delta_{n}}\Big(\sup_{m\geq N}\Big|\frac{\sum_{i\leq m}R_{n}\circ F_{n}^{i}}{m}-\frac{1}{\mu_{\Delta}(\Delta_{n})}\Big|\geq\epsilon\Big)\precsim_{n,\delta,\epsilon}N^{-\beta(1-\delta)}.

Proof.

Note at first that
F_{n}^{n+1}(Z)
is one of the levels
\{(x,l)\in\Delta_{n}:l=i\}
(

) for any

. Indeed,
\mathcal{P}_{n}
is the coarsest partition, and
f
admits a GMY structure. Then
\inf_{Z\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\mu_{\Delta_{n}}\big(F^{n+1}_{n}(Z)\big)>0
, which is called a big image. Note now that the distortion estimate
\big|\frac{\det DF_{n}^{n+1}(x)}{\det DF_{n}^{n+1}(y)}-1\big|\leq C\rho^{s(F_{n}^{n+1}x,F_{n}^{n+1}y)}

holds because

(see the Definition
4.1
). Here

is the Radon-Nikodym derivative of
F_{n}^{n+1}
with respect to
\mu_{\Delta_{n}}
. The map
F_{n}^{n+1}
is a Gibbs-Markov map with big images (see the definition of big images on page 133 in
[
15
]
). Fix any

, and define

||v||_{m}:=\sup_{Z_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\frac{||v|_{Z_{i}}||_{\infty}}{R^{1/p}_{n}|_{Z_{i}}},\quad||v||_{l}:=\sup_{Z_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\sup_{x,y\in Z_{i}}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|}{\rho^{s(x,y)}\cdot R^{1/p}_{n}|_{Z_{i}}}.

Consider now a Banach space

||v||_{B}:=||v||_{l}+||v||_{m}

. By repeating the proof of Proposition 2.1 in
[

]

\displaystyle||P^{m}v||_{B}\precsim_{n,p}\rho^{m}||v||_{B}+\int|v|d\mu_{\Delta_{n}}

,
where

is a transfer operator of

(see Definition
2.1
). If

is sufficiently large, then there exists

5.14

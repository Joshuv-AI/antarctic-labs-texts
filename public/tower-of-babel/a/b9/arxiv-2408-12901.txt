Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the periodic tiling (PT) property for finite abelian groups. A finite abelian group is said to have the PT property if every non-periodic set that tiles the group by translation admits a periodic tiling complement. This notion extends the scope beyond groups with the Hajós property. We give a complete classification of cyclic groups possessing the PT property and identify certain non-cyclic groups that enjoy the PT property but fail to satisfy the Hajós property.. As a byproduct, we obtain new families of groups for which the implication “Tile ⟹ \Longrightarrow Spectral” holds. Furthermore, for elementary p p -groups with the PT property, by analyzing the structure of tiles, we prove that every tile is a complete set of representatives of the cosets of some subgroup. Keywords: periodic, translation tile, spectral set.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a locally compact abelian group, and let
\Omega\subset G
be a Borel measurable subset with
0<\mathfrak{m}(\Omega)<\infty
, where
\mathfrak{m}
denotes the Haar measure on
G
(sometimes denoted by
dx
). We say that
\Omega
is a

tile
of
G
by translation if there exists a set
T\subset G
of translates
such that

\sum_{t\in T}1_{\Omega}(x-t)=1\text{ for almost all }x\in G,

where
1_{A}
denotes the indicator function of a set
A
. Such a set
T
is called a

tiling complement
of
\Omega
and the pair
(\Omega,T)
is called a
tiling pair
. In this setting,
a tiling pair
(\Omega,T)
in
G
means that
G=\Omega+T
forms a factorization, that is each element
g\in G
can be written uniquely in the form
g=\omega+t
with
\omega\in\Omega
and
t\in T.

For a tile
\Omega
in
G
, we denote by
\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}
the set of all tiling complements of
\Omega
.

In this paper, we introduce the concept of periodic tiling property for finite abelian groups. Let
G
be a finite abelian group. A nonempty subset
A\subset G
is said to be
periodic
if there exists a non-zero element
g\in G
such that
A+g=A
.
We say that an abelian group
G
has the
periodic tiling property
(abbreviated
PT property
) if, for every tiling pair
(\Omega,T)
of
G
, either
\Omega
is periodic, or
T
can be replaced by a periodic one (that is, there exists a periodic set
T^{\prime}
such that
(\Omega,T^{\prime})
is also a tiling pair).

In 1938, G. Hajós
[
15
]
reformulated a well-known conjecture by H. Minkowski. The original conjecture stated that if a Euclidean space of any dimension is tiled by lattice-positioned hypercubes, then some pairs must meet face-to-face.
In 1941, Hajós
[
16
]
proved the following statement, which is equivalent to Minkowski’s
conjecture.

Theorem

(Hajós)
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group. If G can be written as
a direct sum of cyclic sets
A_{i}
; that is,
G=A_{1}+A_{2}+\cdots+A_{n}
, where
A_{i}
is of
the form
A_{i}=\{0,a,2a,3a,...,ka\}
with
a\in G
and
0
being the unit element
of
G
, then at least one of
A_{i}
is a subgroup of G.

This result brought attention to the factorization of finite abelian groups into factors that are not necessarily subgroups.
Following
[
41
, Page 5]
, we say that a finite abelian group
G
has the

Hajós property
if, for every tiling pair
(\Omega,T)
of
G
,
either
\Omega
or
T
is periodic.
In the literature, such groups are sometimes referred to as good groups (see, e.g.,
[
36
]
); here, we retain the term “Hajós property” to remain consistent with
[
41
]
.

In 1949, Hajós
[
17
]
posed the problem of classifying all finite abelian groups having this property. The classification was completed by Sands
[
36
]
, who provided the following complete list of finite abelian groups with the Hajós property:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}q},\ \mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}q^{2}},\ \mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}qr},\ \mathbb{Z}_{pqrs},\ \mathbb{Z}_{p^{3}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2},\ \mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3},\ \mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{4}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2},

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{4},\ \mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2},\ \mathbb{Z}_{p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{2},\ \mathbb{Z}_{9}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3},\ \mathbb{Z}_{2^{n}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2},\ \mathbb{Z}_{4}^{2},\ \mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2},

where
p,q,r,s
are distinct primes and
n
is any positive integer. The case
p=2
is admitted in all types of groups given above. All these groups and all subgroups of them have the Hajós property.

It is evident that the Hajós property implies the PT property, but the converse is not true. For instance, every tile in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{3}
admits a periodic tiling complement (see Proposition
5.8
), whereas
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{3}
(
p\geq 5
) itself does not have the Hajós property.

This naturally leads to the following question: in analogy with Hajós’ problem, can one give a complete description of all finite abelian groups having the PT property? We have obtained some partial results in this direction, including structural characterizations and several new examples of PT groups beyond the Hajós list. Nevertheless, a full classification remains out of reach. Further discussion on this problem will be given in Section
10
.

As a starting point, we consider the following question:

Question
.

If all proper subgroups of a group have the Hajós property, does the group itself have the PT property?

We show that the answer is negative, even for certain cyclic groups. In fact, we completely classify those cyclic groups that have the PT property.

Theorem 1.1
.

Finite cyclic groups with the PT property are exactly the subgroups of the groups

\mathbb{Z}_{p_{1}^{n}p_{2}p_{3}\cdots p_{k}},\quad\mathbb{Z}_{p_{1}^{2}p_{2}^{2}},

where
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},\dots,p_{k}
are distinct primes and
n\geq 1
is any positive integer.

In proving that
\mathbb{Z}_{p_{1}^{n}p_{2}p_{3}\cdots p_{k}}
has the PT property, we use the fact that all the tiling complements of a non-periodic tile in
\mathbb{Z}_{p_{1}^{n}}
share a common period
p_{1}^{n-1}
. This idea extends naturally to certain non-cyclic groups.

We need the following properties for tiles in a finite abelian group
G:

•

A tile
\Omega
is
uniformly periodic
if all its tiling complements have a common period, i.e.
T+g=T
for some
g\in G\setminus\{0\}
and for every
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}
.

•

A tile
\Omega
is
dual uniformly periodic
if there exists a periodic tile
\Omega^{\prime}
such that
(\Omega^{\prime},T)
is a tiling pair for each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}
.

We say that a finite abelian group
G
possess the
uniformly periodic tiling property (UPT property)
if every tile in
G
is either uniformly periodic or dual uniformly periodic. It is straightforward to verify that the UPT property is strictly stronger than the PT property (see Lemma
4.1
).
Groups with the PT property can be constructed from those possessing the UPT property.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group with the UPT property. Suppose
m
is a square-free positive integer such that
\gcd(|G|,m)=1
. Then the direct product group
G\times\mathbb{Z}_{m}
has UPT property, and hence the PT property.

As an application of Theorem
1.2
, by establishing that the
p
-groups

\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{5},\quad\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{3},\quad\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},\quad\mathbb{Z}_{4}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}

possess the UPT property, we obtain further families of groups with the PT property, beyond the cyclic groups listed in Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be a subgroup of one of the following groups:

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{5},\quad\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{3},\quad\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},\quad\mathbb{Z}_{4}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2},

where
p
is a prime and
n
a positive integer.
If
m
is square-free and
\gcd(|G|,m)=1
, then the group
G\times\mathbb{Z}_{m}
has the PT property.

It is worth noting that the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}q^{2}}
has the PT property but fails to have the UPT property.
The UPT property of
G
is crucial in ensuring that
G\times\mathbb{Z}_{m}
inherits the PT property (see Proposition
4.2
and Corollary
4.3
).
On the other hand, since
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}q^{2}}
(with
p
and
q
distinct primes) fail to satisfy the UPT property (see Propositions
5.2
and
5.6
), it follows that any group containing either
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}q^{2}}
as a proper subgroup typically does not possess the PT property, apart from a few exceptional cases.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group containing a proper subgroup isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}q^{2}}
, where
p,q
are distinct primes and
q
is odd.
If
G
is not isomorphic to one of the following exceptional groups:

\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2},\quad\mathbb{Z}_{2p^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}},\quad\mathbb{Z}_{9}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3},\quad\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2},\quad\mathbb{Z}_{4q^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2},

then
G
does not have the PT property.

To further explore families of groups with the PT property, we observe that the Rédei property offers a useful approach.
Following
[
40
]
, a finite abelian group is said to have the
Rédei property
if, for every tiling pair
(\Omega,T)
of
G
with
0\in\Omega\cap T
, at least one of
\Omega
or
T
is contained in a proper subgroup of
G
.
Some groups, such as
\mathbb{Z}_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{k}}
with
k\geq 6
, possess the PT property but lack the Rédei property, while others, such as
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}q^{3}}
, have the Rédei property but not the PT property.
Nevertheless, we shall see that combining the Rédei property with the UPT property of all proper subgroups guarantees that the group itself has the PT property.

We also introduce a weaker variant: a finite abelian group has the
weak Rédei property
if, for every tile
\Omega
of
G
with
0\in\Omega
and
\langle\Omega\rangle=G
, there exists a tiling complement
T
contained in a proper subgroup of
G
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group with the weak Rédei property. If every proper subgroup of
G
has the UPT property, then
G
has the PT property.

It is shown in
[
40
, Theorems 9.3.3, 9.3.4 and 9.3.10]
that the groups

\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{4},\quad\mathbb{Z}_{9}^{2},\quad\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{6}

have the Rédei property. As an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.5
, all of these groups also possess the PT property.

It is known that
\mathbb{Z}_{4}\times\mathbb{Z}_{4}
does not have the UPT property.
Nevertheless, we shall prove that
\mathbb{Z}_{8}\times\mathbb{Z}_{4}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{4}\times\mathbb{Z}_{4}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
both enjoy the PT property, relying on their Rédei property (see
[
40
, Theorem 9.3.6 and Corollary 9.3.2]
).

Theorem 1.6
.

The groups
G=\mathbb{Z}_{8}\times\mathbb{Z}_{4}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
have the PT property.

We now turn to the structure of tiles in groups with the PT property. In
[
41
]
, it was shown that every tile in a group with the Hajós property admits an
ascending chain structure
(see Section
8
for the definition). In this paper, we prove that if every tile of a group has an ascending chain structure, then the group necessarily has the PT property. Moreover, we establish the following result.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group.

(1)

If
G
and all its subgroups have the PT property, then any tile of
G
have the ascending chain structure.

(2)

If any tile of
G
has ascending chain structure, then
G
has the PT property.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.7
, we can characterize the tiles in elementary
p
-groups that have the PT property.

Theorem 1.8
.

Suppose
\Omega
is a tile of the elementary
p
-group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
.

(1)

Case
p\geq 3
: If
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
has the PT property, then
(\Omega,T)
is a tiling pair for some subgroup
T
.

(2)

Case
p=2
: If
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n-1}
have PT property, then
(\Omega,T)
is a tiling pair for some subgroup
T
.

The second motivation of this paper is to explore an application of the PT property to Fuglede’s conjecture. In harmonic and functional analysis, a fundamental question asks whether a geometric property of sets (tiling) and an analytic property (spectrality) are always two sides of the same coin. This question was initially posed by Fuglede
[
11
]
for finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces, stemmed from a question of Segal on the commutativity of certain partial differential operators.

Conjecture 1.9
.

A Borel set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
of positive and finite Lebesgue measure is a spectral set if and only if it is a tile.

The original Fuglede conjecture (Conjecture
1.9
) has been disproven in its full generality for dimensions 3 and above for both directions
[
9
,
23
,
24
,
34
,
43
]
. This means that neither implication (tiling implies spectral and vice versa) holds true in these higher dimensions. However, the connection between tiling and spectral properties remains an active area of research, particularly in lower dimensions. The conjecture is still open for the one-dimensional and two-dimensional cases (
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}^{2}
). There might be a deeper relationship to be discovered in these simpler settings (see
[
4
]
for a focused look on
\mathbb{R}
).

Despite the general counterexamples, the conjecture has been successfully proven for convex sets in higher dimensions. Iosevich, Katz, and Tao
[
18
]
initiated this progress in 2003 by demonstrating the validity of the conjecture for convex sets in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. This result was later extended to
d=3
(three dimensions) by Greenfeld and Lev
[
12
]
in 2017. Finally, Lev and Matolcsi
[
31
]
achieved a major breakthrough in 2021 by proving the conjecture for all convex sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 3
).

There has been a growing interest in extending the Fuglede conjecture beyond the realm of Euclidean spaces. Fuglede himself hinted at the possibility of exploring the conjecture in different settings. This has led to a more general version of the conjecture applicable to locally compact abelian groups.

The generalized Fuglede conjecture has been proved for different groups, particularly within the realm of finite abelian groups. These successes include groups like

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we briefly review some basic definitions, the Fourier transform, and the equivalent characterization of tiling on finite abelian groups.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group, and let
\mathbb{C}
be the set of complex numbers. A character on
G
is a group homomorphism
\chi:G\to\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
. The dual group of a finite abelian group
G
, denoted as
\widehat{G}
, is the character group of
G
. For a subset
A\subset G
, define

\chi(A):=\sum_{x\in A}\chi(x).

Any finite abelian group
G
can be written as
\mathbb{Z}_{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{n_{s}}
. For
g=(g_{1},g_{1},\dots,g_{s})\in G
, denote by
\chi_{g}
the character

\chi_{g}(x_{1},\dots,x_{s})=e^{2\pi i\sum_{j=1}^{s}\frac{x_{j}g_{j}}{n_{j}}}.

For
g,h\in G
, it is clear that

\chi_{g+h}(x)=\chi_{g}(x)\cdot\chi_{h}(x).

The dual group
\widehat{G}
is isomorphic to itself, i.e.

\widehat{G}\cong\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}_{n_{1}}\times\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}_{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}_{n_{k}}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{n_{s}}.

For two finite abelian groups
G_{1},G_{2}
, let
G
be their product
G_{1}\times G_{2}
. It is known that

\widehat{G}\cong\widehat{G_{1}}\times\widehat{G_{2}}\cong G_{1}\times G_{2}
, and each character in
\widehat{G}
can be written as

\chi_{(g_{1},g_{2})}(x_{1},x_{2})=\chi_{g_{1}}(x_{1})\chi_{g_{2}}(x_{2}),

where
g_{1}\in G_{1}
and
g_{2}\in G_{2}
.

2.1.
Fourier Transform

The Fourier transform on
G
is a linear transformation that maps a function
f:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
to a function
\widehat{f}:\widehat{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
defined as follows:

\widehat{f}(g)=\sum_{x\in G}f(x)\cdot\chi_{g}(-x)

where
\chi_{g}
is the character of
G
corresponding to
g
, and
f(x)
is the value of the function
f
at the element
x
in
G
.

For
A\subset G
, denote by

\mathcal{Z}_{A}:=\{x\in\widehat{G}:\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{A}}(x)=0\}

the set of zeros of the Fourier transform of the indicator function
\mathbbm{1}_{A}
.
The set
\mathcal{Z}_{A}
, determined by the vanishing of certain sums of roots of unity, reflects structural properties of

A
.
The following lemma, due to Rédei
[
35
]
, will be useful.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
p
be a prime and
\zeta=\zeta_{p^{n}}
be a primitive
p^{n}
-th root of unity. Suppose
c=c_{p^{n}-1}\zeta^{p^{n}-1}+c_{p^{n}-2}\zeta^{p^{n}-2}+\cdots+c_{1}\zeta+c_{0}
, where
c_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
0\leq i\leq p^{n}-1
. Then
c=0
if and only if
c_{i}=c_{j}
for any
i,j
with
i\equiv j\pmod{p^{n-1}}
.

As consequences of Lemma
2.1
, the following lemmas provide a sufficient condition for a set in
G=\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}\times H
to be periodic.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
. If
1\in\mathcal{Z}_{A}
, then
A+p^{n-1}=A
.

Proof.

Since
1\in\mathcal{Z}_{A}
, then

\sum_{x\in A}e^{2\pi i\frac{x}{p^{n}}}=0.

By Lemma
2.1
, if
x\in A
, then
x+jp^{n-1}\in A
for any
j\in\{0,1,\cdots,n-1\}.
Hence

A+p^{n-1}=A.

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
H
be a finite abelian group and let
A\subset G=\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}\times H
. If
(1,h)\in\mathcal{Z}_{A}
for each

h\in H
, then
A+(p^{n-1},0)=A
.

Proof.

Let
A_{h}=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}:(x,h)\in A\}
. Then we have

A=\bigcup_{h\in H}A_{h}\times\{h\}.

Since for each
\alpha\in H
,
(1,\alpha)\in\mathcal{Z}_{A}
, it follows that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{(x,h)\in A}e^{2\pi i\frac{x}{p^{n}}}\chi_{\alpha}(h)=\sum_{h\in H}\chi_{\alpha}(h)\sum_{x\in A_{h}}e^{2\pi i\frac{x}{p^{n}}}=0.

Let
X_{h}=\sum_{x\in A_{h}}e^{2\pi i\frac{x}{p^{n}}}
and let
\mathbf{X}=(X_{h})_{h\in H}
be the row vector with elements
X_{h}
.
Let
M_{H}=(\chi_{\alpha}(h))_{\alpha,h\in H}
be the Fourier matrix of
H
. By Equality (
2.1
), we obtain the following system of linear equations

M_{H}\cdot\mathbf{X}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

The coefficient matrix
M_{H}
is of full rank, therefore
X_{h}=0
for each
h\in H
, which implies that

A_{h}+p^{n-1}=A_{h}.

Therefore
A+(p^{n-1},0)=A
.
∎

2.2.
Equivalent characterization for tiling pairs

Assume that
(\Omega,T)
is a tiling pair of
G
. Recall that this means that

G=\Omega+T

forms a factorization, which is equivalent to

(2.2)

\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega}*\mathbbm{1}_{T}\equiv 1,

where
\mathbbm{1}_{E}
is the indicator
function of
E
. For a finite set
E
, denote by
|E|
the cardinality of
E
.
By taking Fourier transform, (
2.2
) is equivalent to

(2.3)

\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega}}\cdot\widehat{\mathbbm{1}_{T}}=|G|\cdot\delta_{0}.

We have the following equivalent conditions for a tiling pair (see
[
38
]
,
[
41
, Lemma 2.1]
).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\Omega,T
be subsets in a finite subgroup
G
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(a)

(\Omega,T)
is a tiling pair.

(b)

(T,\Omega)
is a tiling pair.

(c)

(\Omega+g,T+h)
is a tiling pair.

(d)

|\Omega|\cdot|T|=|G|
and
(\Omega-\Omega)\cap(T-T)=\{0\}
.

(e)

|\Omega|\cdot|T|=|G|
and
\mathcal{Z}_{\Omega}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{T}=G\backslash\{0\}
.

Recall that
\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}
is the set of tiling complements of
\Omega
.
For a subset
A\subset G
and integer
k
, let
kA=\{k\cdot a:a\in A\}
. The following lemma states that a tiling complement
T
can be replaced by
kT
if
k
and
|T|
are relatively prime.

Lemma 2.5
.

(
[
41
, Theorem 3.17]
)

Assume that
(\Omega,T)
is a tiling pair of a finite abelian group
G
.
If
(k,|T|)
=1, then
kT\in\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}
.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group and let
m
be an integer. If
(m,|G|)=1
, then
\Omega
tiles
G
by translation if and only if
m\Omega
tiles
G
by translation. Moreover,
\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}=\mathcal{T}_{m\Omega}.

3.
Periodic tiling in abelian groups

It is known that the subgroups of a group with the Hajós property also have the Hajós property.
This naturally leads to the following question.

Question 3.1
.

If a finite abelian group has the PT property, do all its subgroups have the PT property?

We prove that this is true under the additional condition that the groups are not
2
-group.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group that is not a
2
-group. If
G
has the PT property, then all its subgroups have the PT property.

For
2
-groups, it is unclear how the periodic tiling property of
(\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{n+1}
implies that of
(\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{n}
. However, we obtain a partial result.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
H
be a
2
-group.

(1)

If
H\times\mathbb{Z}_{2^{n}}
has the PT property, then
H
and
H\times\mathbb{Z}_{2^{n-1}}
has the PT property.

(2)

If
H\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
has the PT property, then
H
has the PT property.

Note that every subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{5}
possesses the Hajós property, and therefore also the PT property. As a consequence of Theorems
3.2
and
3.3
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group having the PT property. If the rank of
G
(the smallest cardinality of a generating set) is at most
5
, then all its subgroups have the PT property.

3.1.
Construction of tiles from the tiles in subgroups

To prove Theorems
3.2
and
3.3
, the first step is to construct non-periodic tiles based on tiles in the subgroups.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
G=H\times S
be the direct product of two finite abelian groups,
H
and
S
. Suppose
(\Omega,T)
is a tiling pair for the group
H
and identify
H
with the subset
H\times\{0\}\subset G
. For any subset
K\subset G
of the form

K=\{(h_{s},s):s\in S,h_{s}\in H\},

the pair
(\Omega+K,T)
forms a tiling pair of
G
.
Furthermore, if neither
\Omega
nor
K
is periodic, then
\Omega+K
is also non-periodic.

Proof.

Represent elements in
G
as the form
(h,s)
, with
h\in H
and
s\in S
.
Remark that if
H
is considered as a subgroup of
G
, the elements in
H
take the form
(h,0)
.

Let

\widetilde{\Omega}=\Omega+K.

Note that
H\cap(H+(h_{s},s))=\emptyset
if
s\neq 0
and
\Omega+T+(h_{s},s)=H+(0,s)
for
(h_{s},s)\in K
. Hence,
(\widetilde{\Omega},T)
forms a tiling pair of
G
.

Suppose that both
\Omega
and
K
are not periodic. We shall prove by contradiction that
\widetilde{\Omega}
is not periodic.

Assume that
\widetilde{\Omega}+(h_{0},s_{0})=\widetilde{\Omega}
for some
(h_{0},s_{0})\in G\setminus\{0\}
. It follows that

\Omega+K+(h_{0},s_{0})=\Omega+K.

If
s_{0}=0
, then
\Omega+(h_{0},0)=\Omega
, implying
(h_{0},s_{0})=(0,0)
.
Hence,
s_{0}\neq 0
and we have the following relations:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\forall s\in S,\quad\Omega+(h_{s},s)+(h_{0},s_{0})=\Omega+(h_{s+s_{0}},s+s_{0}).

As
\Omega
is non-periodic, Equality (
3.1
) implies
h_{s+s_{0}}=h_{s}+h_{0}
. Hence,

K+(h_{0},s_{0})=\{(h_{s+s_{0}},s+s_{0}):s\in S,h_{s}\in H\}=K,

which implies
K
is periodic, leading to a contradiction. Therefore,
\widetilde{\Omega}
is non-periodic.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Consider a finite abelian group
S
, and let
H=S\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
and
G=S\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n+1}}
where
n\geq 1
. Assume
(\Omega,T)
is a tiling pair of
H
, and identify
H
with the subset
\{(s,pj)\in G:s\in S,j\in\{0,1,\ldots,p^{n}-1\}\}
of
G
. For any
h\in H
, let
K\subset G
be of the form

K=\big\{(0,i)+h:i\in\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}\}.

Then
(\Omega+K,T)
forms a tiling pair of
G
. Moreover, if
\Omega
is not periodic in
H
, then
\Omega+K
is not periodic in
G
.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{\Omega}=\Omega+K.
Note that
H\cap(H+(0,i))=\emptyset
for any
1\leq i\leq p-1
and
\Omega+T+(0,i)+h=H+(0,i)
for
(0,i)+h\in K
. Hence, we have
G=\widetilde{\Omega}+T
.

Assume that
\Omega
is not periodic in
H
and
\widetilde{\Omega}+(s,i)=\widetilde{\Omega}
for some nonzero element
(s,i)\in G
. Write
i=i_{0}+pi_{1}
, where
i_{0}\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}
.

If
i_{0}=0
, then
\Omega+(s,pi_{1})=\Omega
. So, it follows that
(s,i)=(0,0)
. Hence,
i_{0}\neq 0
and

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Omega+(s,pi_{1}+i_{0})=\Omega+(0,i_{0}),

(3.3)

\displaystyle\Omega+(0,p-i_{0})+(s,pi_{1}+i_{0})=\Omega.

Equation (
3.2
) implies
i_{1}=0
and Equation (
3.3
) implies
i_{1}=-1
, which is a contradiction. Hence,
\widetilde{\Omega}
is non-periodic.
∎

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
3.2

Let
H
be a subgroup of
G
. Assume that
H
does not have the PT property.
For simplicity, we can focus on the case where
[G:H]=p
, where
p
is a prime. This is because we can use induction on the index to prove the result for any finite index subgroup. Hence, Theorem
3.2
is a consequence of the following Lemmas
3.7
,
3.10
and
3.11
.

3.2.1.
Induction from
H
to
H\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}

In this subsection, we study the behavior of the PT property under direct products.
Our main goal is to show that if a finite group
H
does not have the PT property, then the product
H\times S
also fails to have the PT property for various finite abelian groups
S
.

We first prove a general result for groups
S
with
|S|\geq 3
(Lemma
3.7
), which relies on a combinatorial fact (Lemma
3.8
).
As a direct consequence, the case
S=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with
p
an odd prime follows immediately (Corollary
3.9
).

The case
S=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is treated separately in Lemma
3.10
, since the previous combinatorial method does not apply. Here we assume that
H
is not a
2
-group, and a different argument is required to establish the result.

Together, these lemmas show that the lack of the PT property is generally preserved under direct products with cyclic groups of prime order, with special care needed for the
2
-group case.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
H
be a finite group which does not have the PT property. Then, for any finite abelian group
S
with
|S|\geq 3
, the group
G=H\times S
does not have the PT property.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
S
be a finite abelian group with
|S|\geq 3
. Then

(S\setminus\{0\})-(S\setminus\{0\})=S,

Proof.

Let
S
be a finite (additive) group with
|S|\geq 3
.
Choose two distinct elements
a,b\in S\setminus\{0\}
.
We show that every
s\in S
can be written as
s=x-y
with
x,y\in S\setminus\{0\}
.

For a given
s\in S
, consider the two elements
s+a
and
s+b
.
If
s+a\neq 0
, then set
x=s+a
and
y=a
; clearly
x,y\in S\setminus\{0\}
and
s=x-y
.
If
s+a=0
, then
s=-a
. Since
a\neq b
, we have
s+b\neq 0
. Thus in this case set
x=s+b
and
y=b
, giving
s=x-y
with
x,y\in S\setminus\{0\}
.
Therefore every
s\in S
lies in
(S\setminus\{0\})-(S\setminus\{0\})
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.7
.

Note that any element of
G
can be represented as
(h,s)
, where
h\in H
and
s\in S
.
Let
(\Omega,T)
be a tiling pair of
H
. Assume
\Omega
is not periodic and
T
cannot be replaced by a periodic set. For
0\neq h_{0}\in H
, let

K=\{(0,0)\}\cup\{(-h_{0},s):s\in S\setminus\{0\}\}.

Note that
K
is not periodic.
Let
\widetilde{\Omega}=(\Omega,0)+K.
Then, by Lemma
3.5
,
\widetilde{\Omega}
is not periodic.

Suppose
G
has the PT property. Then there exists a periodic set
\widetilde{T}
such that
G=\widetilde{\Omega}+\widetilde{T}
. For any distinct
(h_{1},s_{1}),(h_{2},s_{2})\in\widetilde{T}
, we have

\big(\widetilde{\Omega}+(h_{1},s_{1})\big)\cap\big(\widetilde{\Omega}+(h_{2},s_{2})\big)=\emptyset.

If
s_{1}=s_{2}
, then

(\Omega+h_{1})\cap(\Omega+h_{2})=\emptyset.

If
s_{1}\neq s_{2}
, then by Lemma
3.8
there exist
s_{3},s_{4}\in S\setminus\{0\}
such that

s_{1}+s_{3}=s_{2}+s_{4}.

Hence,

(\Omega-h_{0}+h_{1},s_{1}+s_{3})\cap(\Omega-h_{0}+h_{2},s_{2}+s_{4})=\emptyset,

which implies

(\Omega+h_{1})\cap(\Omega+h_{2})=\emptyset.

Define

T^{\prime}=\{\,h:(h,s)\in\widetilde{T}\ \text{for some }s\in S\,\}.

Then we obtain

\Omega+T^{\prime}=H.

Note that
(0,s)
,
s\neq 0
cannot be a period of
\widetilde{T}
since
s\in S\backslash\{0\}-S\backslash\{0\}
.
If
(h,s)
is a periodic of
\widetilde{T}
, then
(h,0)
is also a period of
T^{\prime}
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
H
be a finite group which does not have the PT property. Then, for any odd prime
p
, the group
G=H\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}
does not have the PT property.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
H
be a finite abelian group that is not a
2
-group. Assume
H
does not have the PT property. Then the group
G=H\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
does not have the PT property.

Proof.

Any element of
G
can be represented by
(h,i)
, where
h\in H
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. All elements of
H
have the form
(h,0)
.

Let
(\Omega,T)
be a tiling pair of
H
. Assume
\Omega
is not periodic and
T
can not be replaced by a periodic set. Let
h_{0}
be any element in
H
with order an odd
p
.
Let

\widetilde{\Omega}=\Omega\cup(\Omega+(-h_{0},1)).

By Lemma
3.5
, it follows that
G=\widetilde{\Omega}+T
is a factorization, and
\widetilde{\Omega}
is not periodic.

Suppose that
G
has the PT property. Then there exists a periodic subset
\widetilde{T}
such that

G=\widetilde{\Omega}+\widetilde{T}.

If
\widetilde{T}+(h^{\prime},1)=\widetilde{T}
with
\text{ord}(h^{\prime},1)=2
, then there exists

such that
\widetilde{T}=T^{\prime}+\{(0,0),(h^{\prime},1)\}
. Here

denotes the order of the element. Since

, we have

Now, let us consider the case where
(h,1)
is not a period of
\widetilde{T}
for any

with
\text{ord}(h)=2
. For any distinct

\big(\Omega+(h_{1},0)\big)\cap\big(\Omega-(h_{0},0)+(h_{2},1)+(0,1)\big)=\emptyset,

Since

3.2.2.
Induction from
S\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
and

We now turn to the study of how the PT property behaves under cyclic
p

The following lemma shows that if a group of the form

fails to have the PT property, then the same holds for the natural extension

. This result allows us to inductively extend the non-PT property along higher

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
S
be a finite abelian group, let
p
be a prime and let
H=S\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
with

. If
H
does not have the PT property, then the group
G=S\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n+1}}
does not have the PT property.

Proof.

Any element in
G
can be expressed as
(s,i)
, where
s\in S
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n+1}}
. The elements in
H
take the form
(s,pi)
.
Let
(\Omega,T)
be a tiling pair of
H
. Assume
\Omega
is not periodic and
T
cannot be replaced by a periodic set.

Let

K=\big\{(0,0),(0,1-p),\cdots,(0,p-1-p)\big\}

and

\widetilde{\Omega}=\Omega+K.

By Lemma
3.6
, by taking
h=(0,(p^{n}-1)p)
, we deduce that
\widetilde{\Omega}
is non-periodic and
G=\widetilde{\Omega}+T
is a factorization.

Suppose
G
has the PT property. Then there exists a periodic subset
\widetilde{T}
such that
G=\widetilde{\Omega}+\widetilde{T}
and
(0,0)\in\widetilde{T}
.
For any distinct
(s_{1},pi_{1}+j_{1}),(s_{2},pi_{2}+j_{2})\in\widetilde{T}

, we have

\displaystyle\big(\Omega+K+(s_{1},pi_{1}+j_{1})\big)\cap\big(\Omega+K+(s_{2},pi_{2}+j_{2})\big)=\emptyset.

Now we shall show that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\big(\Omega+(s_{1},pi_{1})\big)\cap\big(\Omega+(s_{2},pi_{2})\big)=\emptyset.

Without loss of generality, we assume that

. We distinguish two cases.

If

, then by the choice of

, Equality (

) follows automatically.
If

, then

\big(\Omega+(s_{1},pi_{1}+j_{1})\big)\cap\big(\Omega+(0,j_{1}-j_{2})+(s_{2},pi_{2}+j_{2})\big)=\emptyset,

\big(\Omega+(s_{1},pi_{1})\big)\cap\big(\Omega+(s_{2},pi_{2})\big)=\emptyset.

Let

It follows that

.

Recall that

is a periodic set. If

is a period of

, then it is also a period of

, leading to a contradiction. If

is a period of

for some

such that

, then

. Consequently,

is a period of

, once again resulting in a contradiction.

4.

Recall that a group
G

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group. If
G
has the UPT property, then
G
has the PT property.

Proof.

Let
T
be a tile of
G
. Then there exists a tiling complement
\Omega
. Therefore,
T
belongs to
\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}
.
If there does not exist a periodic
\Omega^{\prime}
such that
(T,\Omega^{\prime})
forms a tiling pair, then all elements in
\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}
are periodic. Hence, either
T
is periodic or
T
has a periodic tiling complement.
∎

We can construct more groups with the PT property based on the groups possessing the UPT property.
On the other hand, it is essential for the group
G
to possess the UPT property to guarantee that
G\times\mathbb{Z}_{m}
has the PT property.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group. If there exists a tile
\Omega
and distinct tiling complements
T_{0},T_{1},\dots,T_{n-1}\in\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}
for some integer
n\geq 2
such that the
T_{i}
’s do not share a common period, each element in
\bigcap_{i=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{T}_{T_{i}}
is not periodic, then for any integer
m
with
m\geq n
and
\gcd(m,|G|)=1
, the group
G\times\mathbb{Z}_{m}
does not have the PT property.

Proof.

Elements of
G\times\mathbb{Z}_{m}
can be represented by
(g,i)
with
g\in G
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
. Let us define:

\displaystyle B=\{(a,0):a\in\Omega\},

\displaystyle S_{i}=\{(t,i):t\in T_{i}\}\text{ for }i=0,1,\dots,n-2,

\displaystyle S_{i}=\{(t,i):t\in T_{n-1}\}\text{ for }i=n-1,\dots,m-1,

\displaystyle S=\bigcup_{i=0}^{m-1}S_{i}.

It is straightforward to verify that
B+S_{i}=G\times\{i\}
, hence
B+S=G\times\mathbb{Z}_{m}
.
We claim that
S
is not periodic and
B
cannot be replaced by a periodic set.

If
S
is a periodic set with period
(g,i)
where
g\neq 0
, then
(g,0)
is a period of
S
due to
\gcd(m,|G|)=1
. Consequently, all
S_{i}
must have the period
(g,0)
, implying that
T_{i}
for
i=0,1,\dots,n-1
share a common period
g
, contradicting our assumption. If
S
is a periodic set with period
(0,i)
, then
T_{0}=T_{i}
. By the assumption
T_{0}\neq T_{i}
if
i\neq 0
. We deduce that
i=0
. Hence
S
is not periodic.

Suppose
B
can be replaced by a periodic set
B^{\prime}
. Given that
|B^{\prime}|=|B|=|\Omega|
, and since
|\Omega|
divides
|G|
and
\gcd(m,|G|)=1
, according to Lemma
2.5
, we have
G\times\mathbb{Z}_{m}=mB^{\prime}+S
, where
mB^{\prime}
is also a periodic set. However, if we view
mB^{\prime}
as a subset of
G
, this implies
G=mB^{\prime}+T_{i}
, leading to a contradiction.
∎

The following corollary is a direct consequence of Proposition
4.2
.

Corollary 4.3
.

If a finite abelian group
G
does not have the UPT property, then for a sufficiently large integer
m
with
\gcd(m,|G|)=1
, the group
G\times\mathbb{Z}_{m}
does not have the PT property.

Now, we shall prove Theorem
1.2
]. Remark that the second statement is a consequence of Corollary
4.3
and the first statement.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
(\Omega,T)
be a tiling pair of
G\times\mathbb{Z}_{m}
with
0\in\Omega
,
0\in T
,
|\Omega|=m_{1}n_{1}
,
|T|=m_{2}n_{2}
,
m=m_{1}m_{2}
, and
|G|=n=n_{1}n_{2}
.

If
m_{2}>1
, according to Lemma

,
G\times\mathbb{Z}_{m}=m_{2}\Omega+T
. Write
G\times\mathbb{Z}_{m}=G\times\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{m_{2}}
and identify
G\times\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}
with the subset

T_{0}=T\cap(G\times\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}})
.
Then we have

G\times\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}=m_{2}\Omega+T_{0}.

Let
T^{\prime}=T_{0}\times\mathbb{Z}_{m_{2}}

G\times\mathbb{Z}_{m}=m_{2}\Omega+T^{\prime}
.
Write

\Omega=\bigcup_{j=0}^{m_{2}}\Omega_{j}\times\{j\}

with
\Omega_{j}:=\{(g,i)\in G\times\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}:(g,i,j)\in\Omega\}
.
Then,

m_{2}\Omega=\bigcup_{j=0}^{m_{2}}m_{2}\Omega_{j}\times\{0\},

and

\Omega_{j_{1}}\cap\Omega_{j_{2}}=\emptyset,\hbox{ for }j_{1}\neq j_{2}.

By Corollary

, we have

G\times\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}=(\bigcup_{j=0}^{m_{2}}\Omega_{j})+T_{0}.

Since
(h,i,j)+T^{\prime}=(h,i,0)+T^{\prime}

G\times\mathbb{Z}_{m}=\Omega+T^{\prime}
, where

is a periodic set.

Now, let’s assume

, then
|\Omega|=n_{1}m
,
|T|=n_{2}
. By Lemma

, we get

G\times\mathbb{Z}_{m}=\Omega+mT.

Each element of

, where

Consequently,

Since

5.
Uniformly periodic tiling property

It has been shown in Section

that additional groups possessing the PT property can be constructed from those exhibiting the UPT property. This section delves into the characterization of groups with the UPT property, focusing particularly on
p
-groups.

5.1.
Cyclic groups

We will show that for a non-periodic tile of a cyclic
p
-group, its tiling complements must be periodic with a common period.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
and let
\Omega
be a non-trivial tile of
G
. If
\Omega
is not periodic, then all tiling complements are periodic of period
p^{n-1}
.

Proof.

Since
G
has the Hajós property and
\Omega
is not periodic, then
T
is periodic. Hence,
T
has a period
p^{n-1}
.
∎

On the other hand, we show that the group
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}q^{2}}
does not have the UPT property.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}q^{2}}
. There exists a tile
\Omega
which has tiling complements
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}
such that
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
do not share a common period and
\mathcal{T}_{T_{1}}\cap\mathcal{T}_{T_{2}}
contains no periodic element. Consequently, the group
G=\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}q^{2}}
does not have the UPT property.

Proof.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}q^{2}}
with
\text{ord}(a)=p^{2}
and
\text{ord}(b)=q^{2}
. Let

A=\{ia:i\in\{0,\cdots,p-1\}\},\quad B=\{ib:i\in\{0,\cdots,q-1\}\},\quad\Omega=A+B.

It is clear that
\Omega
tiles
G
by translation with tiling complement

T=\{ipa+jqb:i\in\{0,\cdots,p-1\},j\in\{0,\cdots,q-1\}\}.

Let
T_{1}=M_{1}+N_{1}
with

\displaystyle M_{1}=\{ipa:i\in\{0,\cdots,p-1\}\},

\displaystyle N_{1}=\{iqb:i\in\{0,\cdots,q-2\}\}\cup\{(q-1)qb+a\},

and let
T_{2}=M_{2}+N_{2}
with

\displaystyle M_{2}=\{ipa:i\in\{0,\cdots,p-1\}\}\cup\{(p-1)pa+b\},

\displaystyle N_{2}=\{iqb:i\in\{0,\cdots,q-1\}\}.

Note that

\displaystyle\Omega+T_{1}

\displaystyle=(A+B)+(M_{1}+N_{1})

\displaystyle=\langle a\rangle+B+N_{1}

\displaystyle=\langle a\rangle+B+N_{2}

\displaystyle=G.

Similarly,
\Omega+T_{2}=G
. Hence,
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathcal{T}_{\Omega}
.

Note that
T_{1}+qb\neq T_{1}
and
T_{2}+pa\neq T_{2}
. This implies that
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
do not share a common period.
Assume
\Omega^{\prime}
is a periodic subset such that

G=\Omega^{\prime}+T_{1}=\Omega^{\prime}+T_{2}.

If
pa
were a period of
\Omega^{\prime}
, then
pa\in\Omega^{\prime}-\Omega^{\prime}
.
Note that
pa\in M_{1}-M_{1}\subseteq T_{1}-T_{1}
.
Thus
pa\in(\Omega^{\prime}-\Omega^{\prime})\cap(T_{1}-T_{1})
, which contradicts the fact that
G=\Omega^{\prime}+T_{2}
.
Hence
pa
cannot be a period of
\Omega^{\prime}
.
Similarly,
qb
cannot be a period of
\Omega^{\prime}
.

In fact, this further implies that no nontrivial linear combination
ipa+jqb
(with
i,j\neq 0
) can be a period of
\Omega
.
Otherwise,
iqpa
is a period of
\Omega
, so would
\ell iqpa
for every
\ell
coprime to
p
.
Choosing
\ell
such that
\ell iq\equiv 1\pmod{p}
, we obtain that
pa
is a period of
\Omega
, which is a contradiction.

Therefore,
\Omega^{\prime}
cannot be periodic which implies that
G
does not have the UPT property.
∎

5.2.
Rank
2

-groups

We will show that the two typical

-groups,

On the other hand, each tile in the group

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a tile of the group

with

. Then, there exists a periodic
\Omega^{\prime}
such that for any

(\Omega^{\prime},T)
forms a tiling pair of

.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
(0,0)\in\Omega
.
Let
(r,s)\in\Omega
be any nonzero element. Let

\Omega^{\prime}=\{i(r,s):i\in[0,p-1]\}.

Note that
G=\Omega+T
if and only if for any
j
with
\gcd(j,p)=1

which is equivalent to

Then, for any

with

and so

for all

Now, we shall prove that the group

We first present a useful lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

[
44
, Lemma 3.2]

Let
\Omega\subset G=\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}

(p^{i_{1}},a),(p^{i_{2}},0),\cdots,(p^{i_{s}},0)\in\mathcal{Z}_{\Omega}
for some

p^{s}||\Omega|

Proposition 5.5
.

For each prime
p
, the group

Proof.

Assume that
\Omega
is a tile of
G
. Then,
|\Omega|\mid|G|=p^{n+1}

Let

By Lemma
2.4

(p^{i},0)\in\mathcal{Z}_{T}
for

By Lemma

|I_{\Omega}|=t-1
or

When

If

On the other hand, the group
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}

Proposition 5.6

For the group

8.
Ascending chain structure of tiles

For tiles in a group with the PT property, their structures can be characterized by induction.
Let
A,B
be two subsets of a finite abelian group
G
such that
0\in A\cap B
. We define a subset
A\circ_{\phi}B
by

\{\phi(b)+b\},

where
\phi
is a certain function from
B
to
A
with
\phi(0)=0
.
We say that a set
E\subset G
has
ascending chain structure
if there exists a strictly ascending chain of subgroups
0\subset H_{1}\subset\cdots\subset H_{m}=G
and
0\in D_{j}
(
j=1,\dots,m-1
) is a complete set of coset representatives for
H_{j+1}
modulo
H_{j}
, such that

E=H_{1}+(D_{1}\circ_{\phi_{1}}(D_{2}+(\cdots\{0\})))

or

E=H_{1}\circ_{\phi_{1}}(D_{1}+(D_{2}\circ_{\phi_{2}}(\cdots\{0\})))

for some function
\phi_{i}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

We first assume that
G
has the PT property and proceed by induction. Suppose the statement holds for all proper subgroups of
G
. Let
\Omega
be a tile of
G
.

If
\Omega
is periodic, then there exists a subset
\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega
and a subgroup
H_{1}
such that
\Omega=\Omega_{1}+H_{1}
. Let
\overline{g}=g+H_{1}
and
\overline{S}=\{g+H_{1}:g\in S\}
. Then
\overline{\Omega_{1}}
tiles
G/H_{1}.
By the induction hypothesis, there exists a strictly ascending chain of subgroups

\overline{0}\subset\overline{H_{2}}\subset\cdots\subset\overline{H_{m}}=G/H_{1}

and
0\in E_{j}
(
j=2,\dots,m-1
) is a complete set of coset representatives for
\overline{H_{j+1}}
modulo
\overline{H_{j}}
, such that

\overline{\Omega_{1}}=\overline{H_{2}}+(E_{2}\circ_{\phi_{1}}(E_{3}+(\cdots\{\overline{0}\})))

or

\overline{\Omega_{1}}=\overline{H_{2}}\circ_{\phi_{1}}(E_{2}+(E_{3}\circ_{\phi_{2}}(\cdots\{\overline{0}\})))

for some function
\phi_{i}
. Lifting back to
G
, there exist subsets
D_{j}
of
G
with
\overline{D_{j}}=E_{j}
and
\overline{D_{1}}=\overline{H_{2}}
, such that

\Omega=H_{1}+\Omega_{1}=(H_{1}+D_{1})+(D_{2}\circ_{\phi_{1}}(D_{3}+(\cdots\{\overline{0}\})))

or

\Omega=H_{1}+\Omega_{1}=H_{1}+(D_{1}\circ_{\phi_{1}}(D_{2}+(D_{3}\circ_{\phi_{2}}(\cdots\{\overline{0}\})))),

where
H_{1}+D_{1}
forms a subgroup.

If
\Omega
is not periodic, then there exists a periodic set
T
such that
G=\Omega+T
. Write
T=T_{1}+H_{1}
with

T_{1}\subset T
and
H_{1}\leq G
. Then
\overline{\Omega}
tiles
G/H_{1}
. By induction, we again obtain a strictly ascending chain of subgroups

\overline{0}\subset\overline{H_{2}}\subset\cdots\subset\overline{H_{m}}=G/H_{1}

and
0\in E_{j}
(
j=2,\dots,m-1
) is a complete set of coset representatives for
\overline{H_{j+1}}
modulo
\overline{H_{j}}
, such that

\overline{\Omega}=\overline{H_{2}}+(E_{2}\circ_{\phi_{1}}(E_{3}+(\cdots\{\overline{0}\})))

or

\overline{\Omega}=\overline{H_{2}}\circ_{\phi_{1}}(E_{2}+(E_{3}\circ_{\phi_{2}}(\cdots\{\overline{0}\})))

for some function
\phi_{i}
. Lifting to
G
, there exists subsets
D_{j}
of
G
with
\overline{D_{j}}=E_{j}
and
\overline{D_{1}}=\overline{H_{2}}
, such that

\Omega=H_{1}\circ_{\phi}(D_{1}+(D_{2}\circ_{\phi_{1}}(D_{3}+(\cdots\{\overline{0}\}))))

or

\Omega=H_{1}\circ_{\phi}(D_{1}\circ_{\phi_{1}}(D_{2}+(D_{3}\circ_{\phi_{2}}(\cdots\{\overline{0}\}))))=H_{2}\circ_{\phi_{1}}(D_{2}+(D_{3}\circ_{\phi_{2}}(\cdots\{\overline{0}\}))).

Now we assume that for any factorization
G=\Omega+T
, there exists a strictly ascending chain of subgroups
0\subset H_{1}\subset\cdots\subset H_{m}=G
and
0\in D_{j}
(
j=1,\dots,m-1
) is a complete set of coset representatives for
H_{j+1}
modulo
H_{j}
, such that

\Omega=H_{1}+(D_{1}\circ_{\phi_{1}}(D_{2}+(\cdots\{0\})))

or

\Omega=H_{1}\circ_{\phi_{1}}(D_{1}+(D_{2}\circ_{\phi_{2}}(\cdots\{0\})))

for some function
\phi_{i}
.
If
\Omega
tiles
G
and
\Omega
is non-periodic, then
\Omega
has the form

\Omega=H_{1}\circ_{\phi_{1}}(D_{1}+(D_{2}\circ_{\phi_{2}}(\cdots\{0\}))).

Define

T=H_{1}+(D_{1}\circ_{\phi_{1}}(D_{2}+(\cdots\{0\}))).

It is straightforward to verify that
G=\Omega+T
, and moreover
T
is periodic. Hence
G
possesses the PT property.
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
1.7
, we can characterize the tiles in elementary
p
-groups having the PT property.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Assume
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
has the PT property. Then by Proposition
1.7
, there exists a strictly ascending chain of subgroups
0\subset H_{1}\subset\cdots\subset H_{m}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
and
0\in D_{j}
(
j=1,\dots,m-1
) is a complete set of coset representatives for
H_{j+1}
modulo
H_{j}
such that
\Omega=H_{1}\circ_{\phi_{1}}(D_{1}+(D_{2}\circ_{\phi_{2}}(\cdots\{0\})))
or
\Omega=H_{1}+(D_{1}\circ_{\phi_{1}}(D_{2}+(\cdots\{0\})))
for some function
\phi_{i}
.
Then there exist
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
such that
H_{i}=\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{s_{i}}\rangle
and
s_{m}=n
. Note that the complete set of coset representatives for
H_{j+1}
modulo
H_{j}

D_{j}=\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{s_{j}}\rangle\circ_{\phi_{j}}\langle a_{s_{j}+1},\dots,a_{s_{j+1}}\rangle
for some function
\phi_{j}
. Hence
\Omega
has the following form

\Omega=\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{s_{1}}\rangle\circ_{\phi_{1}}(\langle a_{s_{1}+1},\dots,a_{s_{2}}\rangle+((\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{s_{2}}\rangle\circ_{\phi_{2}}\langle a_{s_{2}+1},\dots,a_{s_{3}}\rangle)\circ_{\psi_{1}}(\cdots\{0\})))

or

\Omega=\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{s_{1}}\rangle+(\langle a_{s_{1}+1},\dots,a_{s_{2}}\rangle\circ_{\psi_{1}}((\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{s_{2}}\rangle\circ_{\phi_{1}}\langle a_{s_{2}+1},\dots,a_{s_{3}}\rangle)+(\cdots\{0\})))

for some functions
\phi_{i}
and
\psi_{i}
.
For the first case, define

T=\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{s_{1}}\rangle+\langle a_{s_{2}+1},\dots,a_{s_{3}}\rangle+\langle a_{s_{4}+1},\dots,a_{s_{5}}\rangle+\dots.

For the second case, define

T=\langle a_{s_{1}+1},\dots,a_{s_{2}}\rangle+\langle a_{s_{3}+1},\dots,a_{s_{4}}\rangle+\langle a_{s_{5}+1},\dots,a_{s_{6}}\rangle+\dots.

Then it is easy to check that
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}=\Omega+T
and

is a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
.
∎

9.
PT property implies “Tile
\Longrightarrow
Spectral”

In this section, we prove that ‘
T-S
’ holds in a group if the group and all its subgroups have the PT property.

Proof of Theorem
1.10
.

The proof follows an inductive approach. Assuming the statement holds for all subgroups of
G
, let
(\Omega,T)
be a tiling pair of group
G
. We distinguish between two cases: (1)
\Omega
is periodic, and (2)
\Omega
is not periodic.

(1) The tile
\Omega
is periodic.
Hence,
\Omega=\Omega+g
, for some
g\in G\setminus\{0\}
. Without loss of generality, suppose that the order
{\rm ord}(g)=p
is a prime number.
Write
G=H\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
with
g=(0,p^{n-1})
for some
n\geq 1
. Then, all elements of
G
can be represented by
(h,t)
, where
h\in H
and
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
, and all characters of
G
can be represented by
\chi\psi
, where
\chi\in\widehat{H}
and
\psi\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}}
.

Write
\Omega=\Omega^{\prime}+\{0\}\times p^{n-1}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
, for some
\Omega^{\prime}\subset H\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n-1}}\cong H\times\{0,1,\dots,p^{n-1}-1\}
. Then,

H\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n-1}}=\Omega^{\prime}+T^{\prime},

where
T^{\prime}=\{(h,t^{\prime}):(h,t)\in T,t\equiv t^{\prime}\pmod{p^{n-1}}\}
. Since
G
and all its subgroups have the PT property, then there exists
\Gamma\subset H\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n-1}}
such that
(\Omega^{\prime},\Gamma)
forms a spectral pair. Define

\Lambda=\{(h,pt+s):(h,t)\in\Gamma,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\}.

Then
|\Lambda|=p|\Gamma|
. For two distinct
(h,pt+s),(h^{\prime},pt^{\prime}+s^{\prime})\in\Lambda
, we have

\chi_{h-h^{\prime}}\psi_{pt-pt^{\prime}+s-s^{\prime}}(\Omega^{\prime}+\{0\}\times p^{n-1}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}})=\chi_{h-h^{\prime}}\psi_{pt-pt^{\prime}+s-s^{\prime}}(\Omega^{\prime})\psi_{pt-pt^{\prime}+s-s^{\prime}}(p^{n-1}\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}})=0.

which implies
(\Omega,\Lambda)
forms a spectral pair of
G
.

(2) The tile
\Omega
is not periodic.

Since
G
has the PT property, the tiling complement
T
can be chosen to be periodic. Similarly, we can write
G=H\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}
for some prime
p
and some positive integer
n
and
T=T^{\prime}+\{0\}\times\{0,p^{n-1},\cdots,(p-1)p^{n-1}\}
for some
T^{\prime}\subset H\times\{0,1,\dots,p^{n-1}-1\}.

All characters of
G
can be represented by
\chi\psi
, where
\chi\in\widehat{H}
and
\psi\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n}}}
.
Let

\Omega^{\prime}=\{(h,t):(h,t^{\prime})\in\Omega,t\equiv t^{\prime}\pmod{p^{n-1}}\}.

It follows that
|\Omega^{\prime}|=|\Omega|
and

H\times\{0,1,\dots,p^{n-1}-1\}=\Omega^{\prime}+T^{\prime}.

Hence, there exists
\Gamma\subset H\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n-1}}
such that
(\Omega^{\prime},\Gamma)
forms a spectral pair in group
H\times\mathbb{Z}_{p^{n-1}}
. For any distinct
(h,t),(h^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in\Gamma
,
\chi_{h-h^{\prime}}\psi_{pt-pt^{\prime}}(\Omega)=\chi_{h-h^{\prime}}\psi_{pt-pt^{\prime}}(\Omega^{\prime})=0
. Let
\Lambda=\{(h,pt)\in G:(h,t)\in\Gamma\}.

Then,
(\Omega,\Lambda)
forms a spectral pair in
G
.
∎

10.
Discussion and questions

In this paper, we introduce the PT property for finite abelian groups and investigate which groups possess it. We completely classify the cyclic groups with the PT property. In addition, we identify certain non-cyclic finite abelian groups that do have the PT property, as well as others that do not. An important application of the PT property is that it implies the implication “Tile
\Longrightarrow
Spectral”. Consequently, determining the full list of groups with the PT property is a problem of significant interest.

Based on Theorems
1.1
,
1.3
,
1.4
and
1.5
, it remains unresolved whether the following groups possess the PT property:

(1)

\mathbb{Z}_{p_{1}}^{l_{1}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p_{2}}^{l_{2}}\times\dots\times\mathbb{Z}_{p_{k}}^{l_{k}}
,
p_{1}=2
,
l_{1}=6
,
l_{2}\geq 1
; or
p_{1}=2
,
l_{1}\geq 7
; or
p_{1}\geq 3
,
l_{1}\geq 4
; or
l_{2}\geq 2
,

(2)

\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{s}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p_{1}}^{l_{1}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p_{2}}^{l_{2}}\times\dots\times\mathbb{Z}_{p_{k}}^{l_{k}}
,
s\leq 1
,
l_{1}\geq 2
; or
s\geq 2
,

(3)

\mathbb{Z}_{4}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q^{2}}
,

(4)

\mathbb{Z}_{q^{2}}^{2}
,

(5)

\mathbb{Z}_{2q^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q^{2}}
,

(6)

\mathbb{Z}_{9}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}
,

(7)

\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
,

where
p,p_{1},\dots,p_{k}
are distinct primes,
l_{1}\geq l_{2}\geq\dots\geq l_{k}\geq 0
,
t\geq 2
,
s\geq 0
are integers, and
q\geq 3
is a prime.

Groups possessing the UPT property are known to be useful for constructing groups with PT property. This begs the question: can we obtain a complete list of groups with UPT property? In a forthcoming paper, we will prove that
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{3}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3}
have the UPT property.

We also investigate the structure of tiles in groups with the PT property. As a byproduct, we prove that any non-trivial tile in the elementary
p
-groups
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
with the PT property admits a periodic tiling complement. For elementary
p
-groups, we show that every subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{3}
(
p\geq 3
),
\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{4}
, and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{6}
possesses the PT property. This leads us to the following question:

Question 10.1
.

Do all elementary
p
-groups
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
have the PT property?

Question
10.1
appears to be closely related to the so-called “Periodic Tiling Conjecture” on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. For a positive integer
d
, a set
E\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is said to be
periodic
if there exists a finite index subgroup
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that

E+\lambda=E
for each
\lambda\subset\Lambda
. Formally, we require more for the periodicity in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
than in finite abelian groups.
There is a large body of literature on tilings of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
by translations of finite subsets (see
[
13
,
40
]
, and references therein). In the case
d=1
, it is known that any tile of
\mathbb{Z}
by a finite set
\Omega
is periodic. However, in higher dimensions tiles need not be periodic. For
d>1
, Lagarias and Wang
[
30
]
proposed the following conjecture.

Periodic Tiling Conjecture
.

If a finite set
\Omega
tiles
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
by translation then it admits a periodic tiling.

The conjecture was established for
d=2
by Bhattacharya
[
2
]
using techniques from ergodic theory. Moreover, Greenfeld and Tao

[
13
]
established a quantitative version of the two-dimensional periodic tiling conjecture.
However, in their celebrated paper
[
14
]
, Greenfeld and Tao disproved periodic tiling conjecture for the spaces of sufficiently large dimension.

Although the definitions of periodicity in finite groups and in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

differ, both types of periodicity can be used to characterize the structure of tiles.

Vacuum polarization correction to atomic energy levels in the path integral formalism
Sreya Banerjee, Z. Harman

Abstract
Vacuum polarization corrections to the energy levels of bound electrons are calculated using a perturbative path integral formalism. We apply quantum electrodynamics in a framework which treats the strong binding nuclear field to all orders. The effective potential, derived from the Dyson-Schwinger equation for the photon propagator, is then considered pertubatively. Expressions for the vacuum polarization shift of binding energies is obtained from the poles of the spectral function up to second order. Numerical results are provided to select candidates for novel tests of strong-field quantum electrodynamics by means of precision mass spectrometry.

studies over the past decades. At the one-loop level, there are two types of contributions, the vacuum polarization (VP) and the self-energy effect, which both
scale, to leading order, with
Z^{4}

Experimental advances in the production, trapping and storing of HCI and the investigation of their properties with unprecedented accuracy

;
Schüssler et al. 2020
;
Filianin et al. 2021
;
Kromer et al. 2022
, allowing the study of QED effects in heavy HCI by
means of electron binding energy determinations through mass measurements and the mass-energy equivalence relation.

In this article, we develop a functional integral formalism for the calculation of VP corrections for bound atomic states in the Furry picture

The usual perturbative framework of quantum field theory overlooks the intricacies of the non-perturbative effects that show up in the study of bound systems. We overcome
this existing disagreement between the intrinsically nonpertubative bound states and the perturbative nature of quantum electrodynamics (QED). We use Feynman’s path

The introduction of functional integral methods to atomic systems is also motivated by the prospects of incorporating non-electromagnetic interactions

II
Schwinger-Dyson equations for the photon self-energy

We begin by summarizing the derivation of the complete expression for the VP correction to the photon propagator, also known as the photon self-energy. This is performed by defining
the Schwinger-Dyson equation for the photon propagator using path integrals in analogy to
Bufalo et al. 2011

The gauge-fixed Lagrangian of quantum electrodynamics (QED) is given as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{QED}}(x)=

\displaystyle A_{\mu}(x)\left[g^{\mu\nu}\partial^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{\xi}-1\right)\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\right]A_{\nu}(x)

(1)

\displaystyle\qquad+\bar{\psi}(x)(i\not{D}-m)\psi(x)\,,

where
A_{\mu}
is the gauge field operator for the photon field,
\psi(x)
is the Dirac field of the electron (or other bound lepton) in coordinate space,

is its bare mass,

\not{D}=\partial_{\mu}-ieA_{\mu}

The generating functional

of the photon field, and the sources
\eta

of the fermion fields

and

\displaystyle Z[\eta,\bar{\eta},J_{\mu}]

\displaystyle=\int\mathcal{D}\bar{\psi}\mathcal{D}\psi\mathcal{D}A\exp\biggl\{i\int d^{4}x[\mathcal{L}_{\text{QED}}

(2)

\displaystyle+J_{\mu}(x)A^{\mu}(x)+\bar{\psi}(x)\eta(x)+\bar{\eta}(x)\psi(x)]\biggr\}\,.

The fermion fields and their sources are anti-commuting Grassmann variables.
To arrive at the Schwinger-Dyson equation, we consider that the functional integral of a total derivative is zero,

\displaystyle\int\mathcal{D}[\phi]\functionalderivative{\phi}=0\,,

(3)

where

For the photon propagator, the derivative is taken with respect to the gauge field

\displaystyle\int\mathcal{D}[\bar{\psi}\psi A]\frac{\delta}{\delta A_{\mu}(x)}\exp\{i[S(\bar{\psi},\psi,A)

(4)

\displaystyle\Biggl[\frac{\delta S}{\delta A_{\mu}(x)}

\displaystyle\left(-i\frac{\delta}{\delta J_{\mu}},i\frac{\delta}{\delta\eta},-i\frac{\delta}{\delta\bar{\eta}}\right)

(5)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+J^{\mu}(x)\Biggr]Z[\eta,\bar{\eta},J_{\mu}]=0\,,

where we have established a correspondence between fields and their source terms through functional derivatives

\displaystyle\psi(x)\leftrightarrow-\frac{i\delta}{\delta\bar{\eta}(x)}\,,\quad\bar{\psi}(x)\leftrightarrow\frac{i\delta}{\delta{\eta}(x)}\,,\quad A^{\mu}(x)\leftrightarrow-\frac{i\delta}{\delta J_{\mu}(x)}\,.

\displaystyle\frac{\delta S}{\delta A_{\mu}(x)}

(7)

Thus, Eq. (
5

\displaystyle\biggl\{\left[g^{\mu\nu}\partial^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{\xi}-1\right)\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\right]\left(\frac{-i\delta}{\delta J^{\nu}(x)}\right)

(8)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+e\frac{-i\delta}{\delta\bar{\eta}(x)}\gamma^{\mu}\frac{i\delta}{\delta{\eta}(x)}+J^{\mu}(x)\biggr\}Z[\eta,\bar{\eta},J_{\mu}]=0\,.

In terms of the generating functional

\displaystyle\biggl\{\left[g^{\mu\nu}\partial^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{\xi}-1\right)\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\right]\left(\frac{\delta}{\delta J^{\nu}(x)}\right)

(9)

\displaystyle+ie\frac{-i\delta}{\delta\bar{\eta}(x)}\gamma^{\mu}\frac{i\delta}{\delta{\eta}(x)}\biggr\}W-ie\frac{-i\delta W}{\delta\bar{\eta}(x)}\gamma^{\mu}\frac{i\delta W}{\delta{\eta}(x)}=-J^{\mu}(x)\,.

\Gamma[\bar{\psi},\psi,A_{\mu}]=W[\eta,\bar{\eta},J_{\mu}]-\int\differential[4]{x}(\bar{\psi}\eta+\psi\bar{\eta}+A^{\mu}J_{\mu})\,.

Owing to the above transformation, we can define the field terms

\displaystyle A_{\mu}=-i\frac{\delta W[J]}{\partial J^{\mu}}\,,\quad\psi=-i\frac{\delta W[\eta]}{\delta\bar{\eta}}\,,\quad\bar{\psi}=i\frac{\delta W[\bar{\eta}]}{\delta\eta}\,.

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\Gamma[A]}{\partial A_{\mu}(x)}=i\left[g^{\mu\nu}\partial^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{\xi}-1\right)\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\right]A_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+ie\frac{-i\delta}{\delta\bar{\eta}(x)}\gamma^{\mu}\frac{i\delta}{\delta{\eta}(x)}W\,.

(11)

From the above expression, the connected two-point function – or, in this case, the complete electron propagator – in an external field

S(x,y)=i\frac{\delta^{2}W[\eta,\bar{\eta},J_{\mu}]}{\delta\eta(y)\delta\bar{\eta}(x)}|_{\psi=\bar{\psi}=0}=\left(\frac{\delta^{2}\Gamma}{\delta\psi(x)\delta\bar{\psi}(y)}\right)^{-1}\,.

(12)

\displaystyle\frac{-i\delta}{\delta\bar{\eta}(x)}\gamma^{\mu}\frac{i\delta W}{\delta{\eta}(y)}

Since our aim is to determine the photon propagator, we take the second derivative of the effective action

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{2}\Gamma[A]}{\partial A_{\mu}(x)\delta A_{\nu}(y)}=i\left[g^{\mu\nu}\partial^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{\xi}-1\right)\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\right]\delta(x-y)

Here,

\displaystyle\derivative{B^{-1}}{t}=-B^{-1}\derivative{B}{t}B^{-1}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{2}\Gamma[A]}{\partial A_{\mu}(x)\delta A_{\nu}(y)}=D_{\mu\nu}^{-1}(x-y)\,,

\displaystyle D_{\mu\nu}^{-1}(x-y)=i\left[g_{\mu\nu}\partial^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{\xi}-1\right)\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\right]\delta(x-y)

(16)

\displaystyle-ie\int\differential[4]{x_1}\differential[4]{x_2}\Tr\biggl\{\gamma_{\mu}\left(\frac{\delta^{2}\Gamma}{\delta\bar{\psi}(x)\delta{\psi}(x_{1})}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\crossproduct\left(\frac{\delta^{3}\Gamma}{\delta A_{\nu}(y)\delta\bar{\psi}(x_{1})\delta{\psi}(x_{2})}\right)\left(\frac{\delta^{2}\Gamma}{\delta\psi(x)\delta\bar{\psi}(x_{2})}\right)^{-1}\biggr\}\,.

The third derivative of

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\delta^{3}\Gamma}{\delta A_{\nu}(y)\delta\psi(x_{1})\delta\bar{\psi}(x_{2})}\right)=e\Gamma_{\nu}(y;x_{1},x_{2})\,,

\displaystyle D_{\mu\nu}^{-1}(x-y)=i\left[g_{\mu\nu}\partial^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{\xi}-1\right)\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\right]\delta(x-y)

\displaystyle\quad-ie^{2}\int\differential[4]{x_1}\differential[4]{x_2}\Tr\biggl\{S(x_{1},x)\gamma_{\mu}S(x,x_{2})\Gamma_{\nu}(x_{2},x_{1};x)\biggr\}\,.

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu\nu}(x,y)

(19)

\displaystyle\equiv-ie^{2}\int\differential[4]{x_1}\differential[4]{x_2}\Tr\biggl\{S(x_{1},x)\gamma_{\mu}S(x,x_{2})\Gamma_{\nu}(x_{2},x_{1};x)\biggr\}\,.

III
Perturbative path integrals for bound states

This Section lays the groundwork for the
perturbative approach
to the path integral formalism, following the articles
Schulman and DeWitt-Morette 1981
;
Bhagwat and Lawande 1989

We begin with the modification of the relativistic action integral, wherein the action

\displaystyle S[\mathbf{r}(t)](\mathbf{r}_{b},\mathbf{r}_{a},t_{b},t_{a})

(29)

\displaystyle=S^{(0)}[\mathbf{r}(t)](\mathbf{r}_{b},\mathbf{r}_{a},t_{b},t_{a})-\int_{t_{a}}^{t_{b}}\,\Delta V(\mathbf{r}(t),t)\,dt\,.

\mathbf{r}_{a}=\mathbf{r}(t_{a})
to the end point

\mathbf{r}_{b}=\mathbf{r}(t_{b})

of the time variable. The time interval

N

N+1

by

\displaystyle u_{j}

\displaystyle=t_{a}+jt_{j}\,,\quad\text{where,}\,\quad t_{j}=\frac{t_{b}-t_{a}}{N}=u_{j}-u_{j-1}\,,

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\sum t_{j}\,.

\displaystyle S^{(N)}[\mathbf{r}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{r}_{N-1}](\mathbf{r}_{b},\mathbf{r}_{a},t_{b},t_{a})

\displaystyle=S^{(0;N)}[\mathbf{r}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{r}_{N-1}](\mathbf{r}_{b},\mathbf{r}_{a},t_{b},t_{a})

\displaystyle e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\Delta S^{(N)}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\int_{t_{a}}^{u_{2}}du_{1}\frac{\Delta V(\mathbf{r}_{n},u_{n})}{i\hbar}\dots\frac{\Delta V(\mathbf{r}_{1},u_{1})}{i\hbar}\,.

Owing to this time-lattice divisions, the integration measure for the path integrals can be given in terms of a partial measure for each order of perturbation

\int_{\mathbf{r}_{a}}^{\mathbf{r}_{b}}\mathcal{D}^{N}\mathbf{r(u)}=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left[\int d\mathbf{r}_{k}\right]\prod_{i=0}^{n}\left[\int_{\mathbf{r}_{i}}^{\mathbf{r}_{i+1}}\mathcal{D}^{(N_{i})}\mathbf{r}(u)\right]\,.

(37)

Knowing that the Feynman path integral kernel has the form

\displaystyle K(\mathbf{r}_{b},\mathbf{r}_{a};t_{b},t_{a})

(38)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbf{r}_{a}}^{\mathbf{r}_{b}}\mathcal{D}\mathbf{r}(t)\exp\left\{\frac{i}{\hbar}S[\mathbf{r}(t)](\mathbf{r}_{b},\mathbf{r}_{a},t_{b},t_{a})\right\}\,,

with the integration measure of Eq. (
37
) and with the help of Eq. (
30
) and Eq. (
33
), the Feynman kernel can be rewritten as

\displaystyle K^{(n)}(\mathbf{r}_{b},\mathbf{r}_{a};t_{b},t_{a})=\int_{t_{a}}^{t_{b}}du_{n}\int_{t_{a}}^{u_{n}}du_{n-1}\dots\int_{t_{a}}^{u_{2}}du_{1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left[\int d\mathbf{r}_{k}\frac{\Delta V(\mathbf{r}_{k},u_{k})}{i\hbar}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\prod_{i=0}^{n}\left[K^{(0)}(\mathbf{r}_{i+1},\mathbf{r}_{i};u_{i+1},u_{i})\right]\,,

(39)

where the kernel
K^{(0)}
corresponds to the unperturbed action
S^{(N,0)}[\mathbf{r}(u)]
, defined in the limit
N\rightarrow\infty
. In case of systems
with a time-independent perturbing potential, the Feynman kernel in Eq. (
39
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle K^{(n)}(\mathbf{r}_{b},\mathbf{r}_{a};T)=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left[\int d\mathbf{r}_{k}\frac{\Delta V(\mathbf{r}_{k})}{i\hbar}\right]

(40)

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\times\prod_{i=0}^{n}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}dT_{i}\,K^{(0)}(\mathbf{r}_{i+1},\mathbf{r}_{i};T_{i})\right]\delta\left(T-\sum_{\gamma=0}^{n}T_{\gamma}\right)\,.

Correspondingly, the
n
-th order energy-dependent Green’s function is obtained by taking the Fourier transform of the propagator in the above equation with respect to time, yielding

\displaystyle G^{(n)}(\mathbf{r}_{b},\mathbf{r}_{a};E)

(41)

\displaystyle\qquad=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}d\mathbf{r}_{k}\,\Delta V(\mathbf{r}_{k})\right]\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left[G^{(0)}(\mathbf{r}_{i+1},\mathbf{r}_{i};E)\right]\,.

IV
Uehling contribution to bound-state energy shifts

The infinite summation of a perturbation expansion using path integrals developed in the last Section is now implemented to study the correction to the binding energy of an electron
due to the perturbing Uehling potential. As in the Coulomb case, we start from the Dirac equation with a given potential
V(\mathbf{r})
. The time-independent Dirac equation
is customarily expressed, in natural units (
c=1
,
\hbar=1
), as

(E-\bm{\alpha}\cdot\hat{\bm{p}}-\beta m-V(\mathbf{r}))\Psi=0\,,

(42)

where
\Psi
is the bispinor wave function of the Dirac particle,
m
is the mass,
\bm{\alpha}
and
\beta
are the usual
4\crossproduct 4
Dirac matrices, and
E
is the energy.

In order to calculate the contribution of the Uehling correction to the binding energy, we treat the Uehling potential defined in Eq. (
28
) as the perturbation.
As in Eq. (
30
), the lattice-perturbed action is given as a sum of the unperturbed action and the action due to the perturbing potential.
For our case, i.e., in the Furry picture, we consider the action due to the nuclear Coulomb potential as the unperturbed action

S^{(0)}

Inomata 1982
;
Kayed and Inomata 1984
. The unperturbed action of the bound electron is

S^{(0)}(t_{j})=\frac{m(\Delta r_{j})^{2}}{2t_{j}}-\frac{\lambda(\lambda+1)t_{j}}{2mr_{j}r_{j-1}}+\frac{Ze^{2}Et_{j}}{mr_{j}}+\frac{(E^{2}-m^{2})t_{j}}{2m}\,,

(43)

where,
\lambda\equiv|\gamma|+\frac{1}{2}(\text{sign}\,\gamma-1)
,
\gamma
being the eigenvalue of the Martin-Glauber operator
Martin and Glauber 1958
,
which is defined as
\mathcal{L}=-(\beta K+iZe^{2}\alpha_{r})
, with
K
being the Dirac operator and
\alpha_{r}=\bm{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{r}/r

Kayed and Inomata 1984
.

Coupled with the perturbing potential this yields the lattice-perturbed action. From Eq. (
30
) we obtain

\displaystyle S[\mathbf{r}(t)](\mathbf{r}_{b},\mathbf{r}_{a},t_{b},t_{a})=\frac{m(\Delta r_{j})^{2}}{2t_{j}}-\frac{\lambda(\lambda+1)t_{j}}{2mr_{j}r_{j-1}}+\frac{Ze^{2}Et_{j}}{mr_{j}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{(E^{2}-m^{2})t_{j}}{2m}-\int_{t_{a}}^{t_{b}}\,\Delta V(\mathbf{r}(t))\,dt\,,

(44)

where

\displaystyle\Delta V(\mathbf{r})=U_{\rm VP}^{\rm Ueh}

(45)

is the perturbing Uehling potential.

The corresponding Feynman kernel can be derived using Eq. (
40
) and eventually the energy-dependent Green’s function is given by Eq. (
41
). Thus,
the
n
-th order Green’s function is obtained in terms of the Dirac-Coulomb propagator, which has been determined using the path integral formalism
Kayed and Inomata 1984
, as

\displaystyle G^{(n)}(\mathbf{r}_{b},\mathbf{r}_{a};E)

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}d\mathbf{r}_{k}\,\Delta V(\mathbf{r}_{k})\right]\prod_{i=0}^{n}\left[G^{(0)}(\mathbf{r}_{i+1},\mathbf{r}_{i};E)\right]

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Biggl[\int_{0}^{\infty}d\mathbf{r}_{k}\,\Biggl\{-\frac{2\alpha Ze}{3\pi r_{k}}\Biggl[\left(1+\frac{r_{k}^{2}}{3\alpha^{2}}\right)K_{0}-\frac{r_{k}}{6\alpha}Ki_{1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\left(\frac{5}{6}+\frac{r_{k}^{2}}{3\alpha^{2}}\right)Ki_{2}\Biggr]\Biggr\}\Biggr]

\displaystyle\times\prod_{i=0}^{n}\Biggl[\sum_{j,\kappa}\frac{\Gamma(p+\lambda+1)}{2\iota kr_{1}r_{2}\Gamma(2\lambda+2)}W_{-p,\lambda+1/2}(-2\iota kr_{1})

\displaystyle\times\Biggl\{\left[m-\frac{\kappa E}{\gamma}\right]M_{-p,\lambda+1/2}(-2\iota kr_{2})\Omega_{\kappa,\kappa}^{j}(\theta_{2}\phi_{2}|\theta_{1}\phi_{1})\beta^{2}

\displaystyle-k\,\tilde{\gamma}M_{-p,\bar{\lambda}+1/2}(-2\iota kr_{2})\Omega_{\kappa,-\kappa}^{j}(\theta_{2}\phi_{2}|\theta_{1}\phi_{1})\beta^{2}\gamma_{5}\Biggr\}\Biggr]\,.

(46)

The energy-dependent Green’s function is then

\displaystyle G(\mathbf{r}_{b},\mathbf{r}_{a};E)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}G^{(n)}(\mathbf{r}_{b},\mathbf{r}_{a};E)\,.

(47)

Considering up to second order, the Green’s function in Eq. (
47
) can be written as

\displaystyle G(\mathbf{r}_{b},\mathbf{r}_{a};E)

\displaystyle=G^{(0)}(\mathbf{r}_{b},\mathbf{r}_{a};E)

(48)

\displaystyle+G^{(1)}(\mathbf{r}_{b},\mathbf{r}_{a};E)+G^{(2)}(\mathbf{r}_{b},\mathbf{r}_{a};E)\,,

where

\displaystyle G^{(1)}(\mathbf{r}_{b},\mathbf{r}_{a};E)=\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}d\mathbf{r}_{1}\,\Delta V(\mathbf{r}_{1})\right]\prod_{i=0}^{1}\left[G^{(0)}(\mathbf{r}_{i+1},\mathbf{r}_{i};E)\right]\,,

(49)

and

\displaystyle G^{(2)}(\mathbf{r}_{b},\mathbf{r}_{a};E)=\prod_{k=1}^{2}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}d\mathbf{r}_{k}\,\Delta V(\mathbf{r}_{k})\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\prod_{i=0}^{2}\left[G^{(0)}(\mathbf{r}_{i+1},\mathbf{r}_{i};E)\right]\,.

(50)

In analogy to the H-atom problem
Kayed and Inomata 1984
, the discrete energy spectrum can be obtained from the poles of the spectral function,
which is defined as

\displaystyle G(E)=\int G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r};E)\,d\mathbf{r}

(51)

with the Green’s function [e.g. in Eq.
47
] in coordinate representation. The form of the Green’s function in Eq. (
46
) does not allow
a direct evaluation, therefore, we apply the basis-state decomposition of the spectral function in terms of the perturbed states
\phi_{n}

and the corresponding eigenenergies
E_{n}
, given as

\displaystyle G(E)=\int\sum_{n}\frac{\phi_{n}(\mathbf{r})\phi^{\dagger}_{n}(\mathbf{r})}{E-E_{n}(1-i0^{+})}\,d\mathbf{r}\,.

(52)

In analogy to Refs.
Messiah 1961
;
Lepage 1977
;
Shabaev 1990
;
Shabaev 2002
, we introduce the spectral function projected to a single reference state
\ket{a}
,

G_{a}(E)=\bra{a}G\ket{a}
. This function
G(E)
possesses a pole at the eigenenergy
E_{a}
of the reference state:

\displaystyle G_{a}(E)\approx\frac{C_{a}}{E-E_{a}}\,,

(53)

where the constant
C_{a}
is the residue term. The energies are now determined using complex contour integration by considering a small contour
\Gamma
which
encircles an isolated pole at the perturbed bound-state energy
E_{a}
. From the formulas

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}dE\,E\,G_{a}(E)=E_{a}C_{a}\,,

(54)

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}dE\,G_{a}(E)=C_{a}\,.

(55)

one can extract the level energy as

\displaystyle E_{a}=\frac{\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}dE\,E\,G_{a}(E)}{\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}dE\,G_{a}(E)}\,.

(56)

It is more practical to directly adopt the expression for the perturbative energy shift

\displaystyle\Delta E_{a}=\frac{\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}dE\,\Delta E\,\Delta G_{a}(E)}{1+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}dE\,\Delta G_{a}(E)}\,,

(57)

where

\displaystyle\Delta E_{a}=\Delta E_{a}^{(1)}+\Delta E_{a}^{(2)}+\dots\,,

\displaystyle\Delta G_{a}=\Delta G_{a}^{(1)}+\Delta G_{a}^{(2)}+\dots\,,

are expansions in terms of the perturbing potential. We expand Eq. (
57
) in a geometric series and obtain, to first order,

\displaystyle\Delta E_{a}^{(1)}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}dE\,\Delta E\,\Delta G_{a}^{(1)}(E)\,.

(58)

Thus the perturbative change of the spectral function is

\displaystyle\Delta G_{a}^{(1)}(E)\sim\frac{\bra{a}V\ket{a}}{(E-E_{a})^{2}}\,,

(59)

and from Eq. (
58
), one obtains the perturbative energy shift

\displaystyle\Delta E_{a}^{(1)}=\bra{a}V\ket{a}\,,

(60)

with
\Delta E_{a}=E-E_{a}^{(0)}
. The second-order energy shift can be obtained from the next terms of the geometric expansion of Eq. (
57
) as

\displaystyle\Delta E_{a}^{(2)}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}dE\,\Delta E\,\Delta G_{a}^{(2)}(E)

(61)

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}dE\,\Delta E\,\Delta G_{a}^{(1)}(E)\right)\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}dE\,\Delta G_{a}^{(1)}(E)\right)\,.

The first term in Eq. (
61
) is the irreducible (non-degenerate) part and the second term is the reducible (degenerate) part.
For the irreducible part, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}dE\,\Delta E\,\Delta G_{a}^{(2)}(E)=

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\sum_{i\neq a}\frac{\bra{a}U_{\rm VP}^{\rm Ueh}\ket{i}\bra{i}U_{\rm VP}^{\rm Ueh}\ket{a}}{E_{a}-E_{i}}\,,

(62)

and the reducible part is given as

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}dE\,\Delta E\,\Delta G_{a}^{(1)}(E)\right)\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}dE\,\Delta G_{a}^{(1)}(E)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\Delta E_{a}^{(1)}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma}dE\,\frac{\bra{a}U_{\rm VP}^{\rm Ueh}\ket{a}}{(E-E_{a})^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\bra{a}U_{\rm VP}^{\rm Ueh}\ket{a}\bra{a}(dU_{\rm VP}^{\rm Ueh}/dE)_{E=E_{a}}\ket{a}\,.

(63)

The Uehling potential, as defined in Eq. (
45
), is independent of the bound-state energy and as such the derivative term in Eq. (
63
) vanishes,
and the second-order correction is then given by the usual Rayleigh-Schrödinger perturbative expression

\displaystyle\Delta E_{a}^{(2)}=\sum_{i\neq a}\frac{\bra{a}U_{\rm VP}^{\rm Ueh}\ket{i}\bra{i}U_{\rm VP}^{\rm Ueh}\ket{a}}{E_{a}-E_{i}}\,,

(64)

in agreement with Ref.
Yerokhin et al. 2008
.

VI
Summary

We have calculated the VP correction to energy levels of highly charged ions employing a path integral formalism.
First, the evaluation of the leptonic loop correction to the photon propagator by means of the Dyson-Schwinger equation is summarized.
The effective potential describing the correction to the nuclear potential in the lowest order in
Z\alpha
, i.e. the Uehling potential, is used in an analytical form given
by Frolov and Wardlow
Frolov and Wardlaw 2012
.
The contribution to the energy level of a hydrogenlike ion due to the perturbing Uehling potential is derived from the perturbed action using a relativistic path integral method.
We show that the VP level shifts – or any energy shift induced by a local potential – can be extracted from the poles of the Green’s function. The VP correction is given
numerically for a range of heavy ions, concluding that in sufficiently highly charged ions, the VP effect is observable with state-of-the-art mass spectrometric methods.

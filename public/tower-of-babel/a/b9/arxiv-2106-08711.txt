Operational detection of entanglement via quantum designs
Xin Yan, Ye-Chao Liu, Jiangwei Shang

Abstract
From an operational point of view, we propose several new entanglement detection criteria using quantum designs. These criteria are constructed by considering the correlations defined with quantum designs. Counter-intuitively, the criteria with more settings are exactly equivalent to the corresponding ones with the minimal number of settings, namely the symmetric informationally complete positive operator-valued measures (SIC POVMs). Fundamentally, this observation highlights the potentially unique role played by SIC POVMs in quantum information processing. Experimentally, this provides the minimal number of settings that one should choose for detecting entanglement. Furthermore, we find that nonlinear criteria are not always better than linear ones for the task of entanglement detection.



I
Introduction

Entanglement
Gühne and Tóth 2009
;
Horodecki et al. 2009
is one of the most notable characteristics of quantum theory as compared to classical theory.
Apart from its fundamental importance, quantum entanglement also plays a vital role in various tasks in quantum information processing.
Then, a basic yet crucial question to ask is how to determine whether a given quantum state is entangled or not.
Although considerable results have been obtained, a universal method for checking entanglement is still not available.

A bipartite quantum state
\rho_{AB}
is called separable if it can be written as a convex combination of product states, i.e.,

\rho_{AB}=\sum_{k}p_{k}\mbox{$|a_{k}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle a_{k}|$}\otimes\mbox{$|b_{k}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle b_{k}|$}\,,

(1)

where the coefficients
\{p_{k}\}
form a probability distribution with
{p_{k}\geqslant 0}
and
{\sum_{k}p_{k}=1}
; Otherwise,
\rho_{AB}
is entangled.
The well-known positive partial transpose (PPT) criterion
Peres 1996
;
Horodecki et al. 1996
is both necessary and sufficient for detecting entanglement for the simple
{2\times 2}
and
{2\times 3}
systems, but not for higher dimensions.
For instance, there exist the so-called bound entangled states
Horodecki et al. 1998
which are PPT and nondistillable.
An equivalent generalization of PPT, namely the entanglement witness
Gühne and Tóth 2009
;
Horodecki et al. 2009
;
Horodecki et al. 1996
, is a universal method for detecting an arbitrary entangled state
\rho
with
{\mathrm{tr}(W\rho)<0}
where
W
is a suitably-chosen (but may not be unique) Hermitian observable.

While the construction of an entanglement witness can be difficult sometimes, a number of non-universal but easily applicable methods have been proposed.
The most prominent example is the computable cross-norm or realignment (CCNR) criterion
Horodecki et al. 2006
;
Rudolph 2005
;
Albeverio et al. 2003
, which is based on the correlations of local orthogonal observables (LOOs).
Then, the local uncertainty relation (LUR)
Hofmann and Takeuchi 2003
;
Gühne et al. 2006
criterion extends the CCNR by adding extra nonlinear terms which can also be regarded as a natural nonlinear entanglement witness for CCNR.
Another class of linear correlation-based entanglement criteria is constructed using the normalized symmetric informationally complete positive operator-valued measures (SIC POVMs)
Renes et al. 2004
, dubbed as the ESIC criterion
Shang et al. 2018
; see also Refs.
Chen et al. 2015
;
Li et al. 2020
.
Very recently, a general approach for checking separability is taken by considering the linear correlations of specific operators in Refs
Sarbicki et al. 2020a
;
Sarbicki et al. 2020b
, which incidentally recovers both the CCNR and the ESIC criteria.

Apart from SIC POVMs, another extension for entanglement detection is via mutually unbiased bases (MUBs)
Wootters and Fields 1989
;
Chen and Fei 2013
;
Tao et al. 2015
;
Spengler et al. 2012
;
Bennett et al. 1999
;
Chen et al. 2014
, both of which are special cases of quantum
2
-designs.
For instance, a measurement-device-independent entanglement detection method is discussed in Ref.
Bae et al. 2019
using SIC POVMs and MUBs.
Recently, depending on the random moments calculated from quantum designs, method for characterizing multipartite entanglement is proposed
Ketterer et al. 2019
;
Ketterer et al. 2020
.
Generally speaking, however, the random moments are not directly measurable in experiments.
Therefore, here we focus on the correlations defined via quantum designs from an operational point of view.
As quantum designs correspond to a single generalized measurement, the correlations can be directly obtained in one go in experiments, rather than one by one as in other criteria like the CCNR.
Specifically, we propose several new entanglement criteria using quantum
2
-designs by extending the CCNR, ESIC, and LUR criteria.

This paper is organized as follows.
We first briefly review the concept of quantum designs in Sec.
II
, with a special emphasis on the discussion of SIC POVMs.
In Sec.
III
, upon recalling some well-known entanglement criteria including the CCNR, ESIC, and LUR criteria, we construct several new criteria using quantum designs.
Then these criteria are tested using various bipartite entangled states in Sec.
IV
,
and we conclude in Sec.
V
.

II
Quantum Designs

Design is an important mathematical concept which can be used to imitate uniform averages over certain groups, which in turn can be regarded as a pseudorandom process.
Designs are denominated either unitary or spherical, hinging on which group one chooses.
For qubit systems, the local measurement settings can be characterized over the Bloch sphere, then it is more convenient to use spherical designs than unitary designs.
A spherical
t
-design is a collection of points on the unit sphere for which the
t
th-order polynomials can be averaged over to obtain the same value as that integrating over the surface with certain measures.
Formally, a probability distribution over the set of quantum states
(p_{i},\mbox{$|\phi_{i}\rangle$})
is a quantum spherical
t
-design if

\displaystyle\sum_{i}p_{i}(\mbox{$|\phi_{i}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\phi_{i}|$})^{\otimes t}=\int_{\psi}(\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi|$})^{\otimes t}\mathrm{d}\psi\,,

(2)

where the integral over
|\psi\rangle
is taken over the Haar measure on the unit sphere
Ambainis and Emerson 2007
.

In this work, we choose
{t=2}
in particular to focus on the investigation of bipartite entanglement.
We can associate the complete set of points of a spherical
2
-design as a measurement
\{\widetilde{\Pi}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
, where
N
denotes the number of settings.
Then, a quantum state
\rho

can be reconstructed as
Słomczyński and Szymusiak 2020

\rho=(d+1)\frac{N}{d}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\widetilde{p}_{k}\widetilde{\Pi}_{k}-\openone\,,

(3)

where
d
is the dimension, and the probability of obtaining the outcome
\widetilde{\Pi}_{k}
is given by the Born rule

\widetilde{p}_{k}=\mathrm{tr}\bigl(\rho\widetilde{\Pi}_{k}\bigr)=\bigl\langle\widetilde{\Pi}_{k}\bigr\rangle\,.

(4)

The following inequality can be derived directly from Eq. (
3
), such that

\sum_{k=1}^{N}\widetilde{p}_{k}^{2}=\frac{d\bigl[1+\mathrm{tr}(\rho^{2})\bigr]}{(d+1)N}\leqslant\frac{2d}{N(d+1)}\,,

(5)

where the upper bound is saturated when
\rho
is pure.

For simplicity and later use, let’s rewrite

\Pi_{k}=\sqrt{\frac{N(d+1)}{2d}}\widetilde{\Pi}_{k}

(6)

as the normalized version of the quantum
2
-design.
Then, the constraint in Eq. (
5
) can be reformulated as

\sum_{k=1}^{N}p_{k}^{2}\leq 1\,,

(7)

where
{p_{k}=\mathrm{tr}(\rho\Pi_{k})=\langle\Pi_{k}\rangle}
for a given state
\rho
, and the equality is achieved when
\rho
is pure.

Among the typical examples of quantum
2
-designs are SIC POVMs and MUBs
Wootters and Fields 1989
;
Chen and Fei 2013
;
Tao et al. 2015
.
Both of them have been demonstrated being useful for entanglement detection
Shang et al. 2018
;
Spengler et al. 2012
;
Bennett et al. 1999
;
Chen et al. 2014
.
Here we look at SIC POVMs in specific.
A SIC POVM in dimension
d
comprises of
d^{2}
subnormalized projectors
\mbox{$|\psi_{k}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{k}|$}/d
with equal pairwise fidelity, such that

|\langle\psi_{i}|\psi_{j}\rangle|^{2}=\frac{d\delta_{ij}+1}{d+1}\,,\quad i,j=1,2,...,d^{2}\,.

(8)

One can check that the normalized version of the SIC POVM that satisfies the condition in Eq. (
7
) takes on the form

E_{k}=\sqrt{\frac{d+1}{2d}}\mbox{$|\psi_{k}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{k}|$}\,.

(9)

Although being widely believed and numerically supported, the existence of SIC POVMs in any finite dimension remains as an open problem
Zauner 2011
.
For a recent review, see Refs.
Fuchs et al. 2017
;
Ling et al. 2006
;
Medendorp et al. 2011
;
Bent et al. 2015
.

III
Correlation-based entanglement criteria

As discussed early, entanglement criteria constructed using correlations are particularly relevant for easier experimental realizations.
To be specific, in this work we first re-investigate the CCNR and ESIC criteria
Shang et al. 2018
which are linear, as well as the LUR criterion
Gühne et al. 2006
which is nonlinear.
Then, these criteria are extended straightforwardly by utilizing quantum
2
-designs, and the question whether the new criteria thus obtained are improved or not naturally follows.

III.1
Linear criteria

Consider a bipartite quantum state
\rho_{AB}
with the dimension
{d=d_{A}\times d_{B}}
, where
d_{A}
and
d_{B}
represent the local dimensions of the subsystems
{\rho_{A}=\mathrm{tr}_{B}(\rho_{AB})}
and
{\rho_{B}=\mathrm{tr}_{A}(\rho_{AB})}
respectively.
Let
\{M_{k}^{A}\}_{k=1}^{K_{A}}
and
\{M_{k}^{B}\}_{k=1}^{K_{B}}
denote the local operations acting on the two subsystems.
Then, the linear correlation matrix between these two measurements can be written as

[\mathcal{C}]_{ij}=\langle M_{i}^{A}\otimes M_{j}^{B}\rangle=\mathrm{tr}\bigl(\rho_{AB}M_{i}^{A}\otimes M_{j}^{B}\bigr),

(10)

the size of which is
K_{A}\times K_{B}
.
Then, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\{M^{A}\}
and
\{M^{B}\}
be the properly normalized local measurements acting on the bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
.
If
\rho_{AB}
is separable, then

||\mathcal{C}||_{\mathrm{tr}}\leqslant 1

(11)

has to hold; Otherwise, it is entangled.
The symbol
||\!\cdot\!||_{\mathrm{tr}}
denotes the trace norm.

The proof can be found in Refs.
Shang et al. 2018
;
Sarbicki et al. 2020a
.
Depending on the local measurements one chooses, different entanglement criteria can be derived from Proposition
1
.
For instance, if
\{M^{A}\}
and
\{M^{B}\}
are LOOs,
we get the CCNR criterion
Horodecki et al. 2006
;
Rudolph 2005
;
Albeverio et al. 2003
.
The LOOs can be found by invoking the Schmidt decomposition (assuming
d_{A}\leqslant d_{B}
), such that

\rho_{AB}=\sum_{k=1}^{d_{A}^{2}}\lambda_{k}G_{k}^{A}\otimes G_{k}^{B},

(12)

where
{\lambda_{k}=\langle G_{k}^{A}\otimes G_{k}^{B}\rangle}
are the Schmidt coefficients.
It is easy to check that the set of orthonormal bases of the Hermitian observables
\{G_{k}^{A}\}
and
\{G_{k}^{B}\}
fulfill the conditions

\mathrm{tr}(G_{k}^{A}G_{l}^{A})=\mathrm{tr}(G_{k}^{B}G_{l}^{B})=\delta_{kl}\,,

(13)

and

\sum_{k}(G_{k}^{A})^{2}=d_{A}\openone\,,\quad\sum_{k}(G_{k}^{B})^{2}=d_{B}\openone\,.

(14)

Hence, an equivalent form of the CCNR criterion is given by

\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}\leqslant 1\,,

(15)

as the Schmidt coefficients
\lambda_{k}
s happen to be the singular values of
\mathcal{C}
.

If, instead, one chooses the local measurements to be the normalized SIC POVMs as in Eq. (
9
), the ESIC criterion proposed in Ref.
Shang et al. 2018
is recovered.
As demonstrated by various examples in Ref.
Shang et al. 2018
, the ESIC criterion is more powerful as compared to CCNR.
So, here, we take a step further by asking the question whether the criterion as defined in Proposition
1
can be improved again if quantum
2
-designs besides SIC POVMs are utilized.
To distinguish the ESIC criterion, we dub the one using quantum
2
-designs as E
2
D; see below.

Proposition 2

(E
2
D)
.

Let
\{\Pi^{A}\}
and
\{\Pi^{B}\}
be the normalized
2
-designs acting on the bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
.
Then the linear correlation matrix is

[\mathcal{C}_{\Pi}]_{ij}=\bigl\langle\Pi_{i}^{A}\otimes\Pi_{j}^{B}\bigr\rangle=\mathrm{tr}\bigl(\rho_{AB}\Pi_{i}^{A}\otimes\Pi_{j}^{B}\bigr)\,.

(16)

If
\rho_{AB}
is separable, then

||\mathcal{C}_{\Pi}||_{\mathrm{tr}}\leqslant 1

(17)

has to hold; Otherwise, it is entangled.

The proof is similar to that for Proposition
1
, which can be found in Refs.
Shang et al. 2018
;
Sarbicki et al. 2020a
.

III.2
Nonlinear criteria

The LUR criterion proposed in Ref.
Gühne et al. 2006
makes use of LOOs
\{G_{k}^{A}\}
and
\{G_{k}^{B}\}
, such that for separable states,

1-\sum_{k}\langle G_{k}^{A}\otimes G_{k}^{B}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}\langle G_{k}^{A}\otimes\openone-\openone\otimes G_{k}^{B}\rangle^{2}\geqslant 0

(18)

has to hold.
One notices that LUR is strictly stronger than CCNR due to the extra nonlinear quadratic term in Eq. (
18
).
In view of this fact as well as the fact that ESIC is stronger than CCNR, we try to reformulate the LUR criterion by replacing the LOOs with the normalized SIC POVMs.
See the following proposition which we dub as the LSIC criterion.
The detailed derivation is postponed in Appendix
A
.

Proposition 3

(LSIC)
.

Let
\{E_{k}^{A}\}
and
\{E_{k}^{B}\}
be the normalized SIC POVMs acting on two subsystems.
If a bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
is separable, then

1+\sum_{k}\langle E_{k}^{A}\otimes E_{k}^{B}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}\langle E_{k}^{A}\otimes\openone+\openone\otimes E_{k}^{B}\rangle^{2}\geqslant 0

(19)

has to hold; Otherwise, it is entangled.

Notice the sign differences in Eq. (
20
) as compared to Eq. (
18
).
Similarly, here, we are interested in the question whether the criterion as defined in Proposition
3
can be further improved if quantum
2
-designs besides SIC POVMs are utilized.
To distinguish the LSIC criterion, we dub the one with quantum
2
-designs as L
2
D; see below.

Proposition 4

(L
2
D)
.

Let
\{\Pi_{k}^{A}\}
and
\{\Pi_{k}^{B}\}
be the normalized
2
-designs acting on two subsystems.
If a bipartite state
\rho_{AB}
is separable, then

1+\sum_{k}\langle\Pi_{k}^{A}\otimes\Pi_{k}^{B}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}\langle\Pi_{k}^{A}\otimes\openone+\openone\otimes\Pi_{k}^{B}\rangle^{2}\geqslant 0

(20)

has to hold; Otherwise, it is entangled.

The proof is similar to that for Proposition
3
, which can be found in Appendix.
A
.

IV
Applications

In this section, we test various entanglement criteria proposed above using simple
2\times 2
,
3\times 3
, and
2\times 3
entangled quantum states.

IV.1
2\times 2
entangled states

For the first application, we consider the noisy
2\times 2
quantum states with the form
Gühne et al. 2006

\rho_{\rm{2qb}}(p)=p\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi|$}+(1-p)\rho_{s}\,,

(21)

where the entangled state
|\psi\rangle
can be set to be one of the Bell states,

|\psi^{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigl(\mbox{$|01\rangle$}\pm\mbox{$|10\rangle$}\bigr)\,,

(22)

|\phi^{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigl(\mbox{$|00\rangle$}\pm\mbox{$|11\rangle$}\bigr)\,,

(23)

and the separable noise
\rho_{s}
is given by

\rho_{s}=\frac{2}{3}\mbox{$|00\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 00|$}+\frac{1}{3}\mbox{$|01\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 01|$}\,.

(24)

Using the PPT criterion, these four families of states can be checked to be entangled for any
{p>0}
.

The number of elements of the quantum
2
-designs that we choose for testing are
{N=4,7,9}
respectively.
Note that when
{N=4}
, it is simply the SIC POVM.
Table
1
shows the threshold values of
p
reported by various criteria.
The smaller the threshold is, the better the corresponding criterion is.
Several other features can be observed from the table.
First, ESIC is exactly equivalent to E
2
D, so is the pair of LSIC and L
2
D.
In other words, there is no difference of the detection power between the ESIC and E
2
D criteria, as well as the LSIC and L
2
D criteria.
This tells us that using quantum
2
-designs with more settings like
{N=7,9}
is not helpful for improving the detection power as compared to SIC POVM with
{N=4}
.
With this simple example, we provide additional evidence for the potentially unique role played by SIC POVMs in quantum information processing
Appleby et al. 2017
.
Second, except for the case of
|\psi^{+}\rangle
, LUR performs better than ESIC.
It is worth noting that although both criteria have been proposed previously, the comparison between them is missing until now.
This observation, to some extent, refutes our intuition that nonlinear criteria such as LUR are always better than linear ones like ESIC.
Next, both the EISC and LUR criteria are better than CCNR.
Finally, the LSIC and L
2
D criteria are completely ineffective for detecting certain entangled states.
This observation proves again that the advantage of nonlinear criteria over linear ones does not always exist.

IV.2
3\times 3
entangled states

We move on the consider the
{3\times 3}
entangled quantum states.
In dimension
{d=3}
, there exist three different families of SIC POVMs, from which we arbitrarily choose one for testing.
For each SIC POVM, the number of elements is given by
{N=9}
.
For other quantum
2
-designs that we employ for the E
2
D and L
2
D criteria, we superimpose one set of SIC POVM over another (with proper rotations) to get a measurement with
{N=18}
elements.
Note that the arbitrariness in choosing quantum
2
-designs is ensured by its rotational symmetry
Ambainis and Emerson 2007
.

Bound entangled states

We first consider the

IV.2.3
Horodecki states

The
{3\times 3}
bound entangled states introduced by Horodecki are given by
Horodecki 1997

\rho_{\rm{PH}}^{x}=\frac{1}{8x+1}\!\left(\!\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}x&0&0&0&x&0&0&0&x\\
0&x&0&0&0&0&0&0&0\\
0&0&x&0&0&0&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&x&0&0&0&0&0\\
x&0&0&0&x&0&0&0&x\\
0&0&0&0&0&x&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&0&0&0&\frac{1+x}{2}&0&\frac{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}{2}\\
0&0&0&0&0&0&0&x&0\\
x&0&0&0&x&0&\frac{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}{2}&0&\frac{1+x}{2}\\
\end{array}\!\right)\!,

(30)

with the parameter
{0<x<1}
.
Although these states cannot be detected by the PPT criterion and are not distillable, they are nevertheless all entangled.
Consider the mixture of
\rho_{\rm{PH}}^{x}
with white noise

\rho(x,p)=p\rho_{\rm{PH}}^{x}+(1-p)\frac{\openone}{9}\,,\quad 0\leqslant p\leqslant 1\,.

(31)

Moreover, the LSIC and L
2
D criteria can hardly detect any states, thus are not shown in the figure.

V
Summary

From an operational point of view, we constructed several new entanglement criteria in this work.
We first generalized the ESIC criterion
Shang et al. 2018
to the E
2
D criterion using quantum
2
-designs.
Then, based on the LUR criterion
Hofmann and Takeuchi 2003
;
Gühne et al. 2006
, we proposed the LSIC criterion using SIC POVMs, and more generally the L
2
D criterion using quantum
2
-designs.
Counter-intuitively, the E
2
D and L
2
D criteria with more settings are exactly equivalent to the corresponding ESIC and LSIC criteria respectively.
In other words, there is no difference of the detection power between the ESIC and E
2
D criteria, as well as the LSIC and L
2
D criteria.
Fundamentally, this observation highlights the potentially unique role played by SIC POVMs in quantum information processing.
Experimentally, this provides the minimal number of settings that one should choose for entanglement detection.
Moreover, we find that nonlinear criteria such as LUR are not always better than linear ones like ESIC.
This refutes the previous intuition that nonlinear criteria are always better than linear ones
Shang et al. 2018
.

As an outlook, it is interesting to re-investigate the corresponding criteria using quantum
t
-designs with
{t>2}
for multipartite entanglement detection,
for which we would also expect no improvement over that of the simplest setting of SIC POVMs.

Appendix A
Proof of Proposition
3

Here we explicitly derive the LSIC criterion as introduced in Proposition
3
.
Consider the normalized SIC POVM in a finite dimension
d
as in Eq. (
9
), i.e.,

E_{k}=\sqrt{\frac{d+1}{2d}}\mbox{$|\psi_{k}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{k}|$}\,,

(32)

and the square of it is given by

E_{k}^{2}=\frac{d+1}{2d}\mbox{$|\psi_{k}\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi_{k}|$}\,.

(33)

Because of the completeness condition, we have

\sum_{k}E_{k}^{2}=\frac{d+1}{2}\openone\,.

(34)

The corresponding probabilities satisfy the relation

\sum_{k}e_{k}^{2}\leqslant 1\,,

(35)

where

e_{k}=\mathrm{tr}(\rho E_{k})=\langle E_{k}\rangle

(36)

is the probability for getting the
k
th outcome.

Variance of the measurement operator is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\varDelta^{2}(E_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\langle E_{k}^{2}\rangle-\sum_{k}\langle E_{k}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{d+1}{2}\langle\openone\rangle-\sum_{k}e_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{d-1}{2}\,.

(37)

Next, we discuss the properties of local uncertainty relations
Hofmann and Takeuchi 2003
;
Gühne et al. 2006
.
In general, a pair of quantum systems
A
and
B
can be characterized by the operators
A_{k}
and
B_{k}
with the sum uncertainty relations giving by

\sum_{k}\varDelta^{2}(A_{k})\geqslant C_{A},\quad\sum_{k}\varDelta^{2}(B_{k})\geqslant C_{B}\,.

(38)

The positive values
C_{A}
and
C_{B}
can be computed by considering all the states.
For separable states, the measurement outcomes are uncorrelated, so the variance of
A_{k}\otimes\openone+\openone\otimes B_{k}
is equal to the sum of the local variances, i.e.,

\sum_{k}\varDelta^{2}(A_{k}\otimes\openone+\openone\otimes B_{k})\geqslant C_{A}+C_{B}\,.

(39)

Then, we have

\sum_{k}\varDelta^{2}(E_{k}^{A}\otimes\openone+\openone\otimes E_{k}^{B})\geqslant 2\times\frac{d-1}{2}=d-1\,.

(40)

Meanwhile,

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\varDelta^{2}(E_{k}^{A}\otimes\openone+\openone\otimes E_{k}^{B})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\langle E_{k}^{2}\otimes\openone+\openone\otimes E_{k}^{2}+2(E_{k}^{A}\otimes E_{k}^{B})\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{k}\langle E_{k}^{A}\otimes\openone+\openone\otimes E_{k}^{B}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d+1+2\sum_{k}\langle E_{k}^{A}\otimes E_{k}^{B}\rangle-\sum_{k}\langle E_{k}^{A}\otimes\openone+\openone\otimes E_{k}^{B}\rangle^{2}\,.

Combining the above two equations, one obtains

1+\sum_{k}\langle E_{k}^{A}\otimes E_{k}^{B}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}\langle E_{k}^{A}\otimes\openone+\openone\otimes E_{k}^{B}\rangle^{2}\geqslant 0\,,

(42)

which recovers Eq. (
20
) in the main text.

A note on Flavell’s theorem associated with Frobenius groups
Liguo He, Gang Zhu 1. Dept. of Math., Shenyang University of Technology, Shenyang, 110870, PR China2. College of Teacher Education, Harbin University, Harbin, 150086, PR China

Abstract




Abstract.

Let
G
be a Frobenius group with the Frobenius kernel
K
. Suppose that
G
contains a nontrival subgroup
D\subseteq K
such that the normalizer
N_{G}(D)\not\subseteq K
. When
D
is no
2
-group, Flavell proved, without using character theory, that
K
is a subgroup of
G
. Based on this result, we further prove that
K
is a subgroup when
D
is a
2
-group.

2000 MSC:

20Dxx

Keywords:

character, Frobenius group, finite group

1
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group. If
G
acts transitively on the finite set
\Omega=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{n}\},

n>1
, the fixed-point subgroup
C_{G}(\alpha_{i})
for each
\alpha_{i}
is nontrivial and any intersection
C_{G}(\alpha_{i})\cap C_{G}(\alpha_{j})=1
for the different
i,j
, then
G
is called a Frobenius group. Set
K=(G-\cup_{i=1}^{n}C_{G}(\alpha_{i}))\cup\{1\}
, then Frobenius group
G
obviously has the
Frobenius decomposition

G=K\cup C_{G}(\alpha_{1})\cup C_{G}(\alpha_{2})\cup\cdots\cup C_{G}(\alpha_{n})
. Usually
K
is called the Frobenius kernel and
C_{G}(\alpha_{i})
a Frobenius complement. The
K
is obviously a normal subset of
G
. Equivalently, if
G
contains a subgroup
H
such that the normalizer
N_{G}(H)=H
and any intersection
H^{x}\cap H^{y}=1
for the distinct conjugates
H^{x}
and
H^{y}
of
H
in
G
(with
x,y\in G
), then
G
is said to be a Frobenius group. The subgroup
H
is called a Frobenius complement.

Applying the character theory of finite groups, Frobenius proved that
K
is a normal subgroup of
G
and
G=KC_{G}(\alpha_{i})
with
K\cap C_{G}(\alpha_{i})=1
(for example, see
[
4
]
or
[
6
, Satz V.7.6]
), which is referred to as Frobenius’ theorem. As the action of
G
on
\Omega
is transitive, it is clear that all
C_{G}(\alpha_{i})
are conjugate each other by elements of
G
.

Two famous applications of the character theory of finite groups are Burnside’s
p^{a}q^{b}
-theorem and the above Frobenius’ theorem, which are proved at the beginning of the last century. It was then a challenge to find a proof without characters for the two theorems. The character-free proof for Burnside’s theorem was reached by Bender, Goldschmidt and Mathsuyama at the early 1970’s (see
[
1
,
5
,
8
]
). For Frobenius’ theorem, the partial results were attained by Corrádi, Hováth and Flavell in
[
2
,
3
]
.

Flavell in
[
3
]
proved the following result.

Theorem A.

Let
G
be a Frobenius group with kernel
K
, and suppose that
G
contains a non-
2
-subgroup
1\subsetneq D\subseteq K
such that
N_{G}(D)\not\subseteq K
. Then
K
is a subgroup.

Using this theorem and its proof together with Frobenius’ criterion for
p
-nilpotence (7.2.4, p170,
[
7
]
), we prove the following result.

Theorem B.

Let
G
be a Frobenius group with kernel
K
, and suppose that
G
contains a
2
-subgroup
1\subsetneq D\subseteq K
such that
N_{G}(D)\not\subseteq K
. Then
K
is a subgroup.

Combining the two theorems above, the next corollary is immediate.

Corollary C.

Let
G
be a Frobenius group with kernel
K
, and suppose that
G
contains a subgroup
1\subsetneq D\subseteq K
such that
N_{G}(D)\not\subseteq K
. Then
K
is a subgroup.

We always use
\pi
to denote a set of prime numbers and
\pi^{\prime}
the complement for
\pi
in the full set of prime numbers. For a finite group
G
, write
\pi(G)
for the set of prime divisors of the order
\lvert G\rvert
of
G
.

Unless otherwise stated, the notation and terminology is standard, as presented in the general finite group theory textbooks.

2
Preliminaries

Some useful facts are collected as follows.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a Frobenius group with the Frobenius kernel
K
and a Frobenius complement
H
, then the following statements are true.

1.

H
is a Hall
\pi
-subgroup of
G
(
\mathpzc{E}_{\pi}
-property).

2.

\lvert G\rvert=\lvert K\rvert\lvert H\rvert
.

3.

If
1\neq x\in K
, then
C_{G}(x)\subseteq K
.

Proof.

See Lemma 2.1 of
[
3
]
, for example.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a Frobenius group with kernel
K
and complement
H
, and suppose that
H
normalizes a nontrivial subgroup contained in
K
. Then
K
is a subgroup.

Proof.

See Lemma 2.2(iv) of
[
3
]
, for example.
∎

Lemma
2.1
(1) and the next result show that Frobenius complements of a Frobenius group behave just like Sylow subgroups of a finite group.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be a Frobenius group, then

1.

Every two Frobenius complements are conjugate in
G
(
\mathpzc{C}_{\pi}
-property).

2.

Any
\pi
-subgroup is contained in some Frobenius complement (
\mathpzc{D}_{\pi}
-property).

Proof.

Immediately follow from
[
3
,
Theorem E
]
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G
be a Frobenius group with kernel
K
and complement
H
, write
\pi=\pi(H)
. Suppose that
L\leq G
is neither
\pi
nor
\pi^{{}^{\prime}}
-group. Then there exists some
g\in G
such that
L\cap H^{g}\neq 1
and
L
is a Frobenius subgroup of
G
with kernel
L\cap K
and complement
L\cap H^{g}
.

Proof.

Immediately follow by
[
3
, Corollary 3.1]
and Lemma
2.3
.
∎

3
Proof of Theorem B

Let
H
be a Frobenius complement. By Lemma
2.3
(2), we may replace
D
with a suitable conjugate to suppose that
N_{G}(D)\cap H\neq 1
. Corollary 3.3 of
[
3
]
implies that
N_{G}(D)\cap K
is a normal subgroup of
N_{G}(D)
. Thus we may choose a subgroup
M\leq G
, maximal subject to

(i)

M\cap K\neq 1
and
M\cap H\neq 1
, and

(ii)

M\cap K
is a subgroup.

Let
F=M\cap K
and
\pi=\pi(F)
. Using the proof of
Theorem A
, we may assume that
F
is a
2
-group and
\pi=\{2\}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

(i)

F
is a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
G
.

(ii)

If
P\neq 1
is a characteristic subgroup of
F
, then
M=N_{G}(P)
.

(iii)

F\trianglelefteq M
and every
2
-subgroup of
M
is contained in
F
.

Proof.

Lemma
2.4
yields that
M
is a Frobenius group with kernel
F
, so
F\in Syl_{2}(M)
is normal in
M
, part(iii) follows. By the maximality of
M
, we get
M=N_{G}(F)=N_{G}(P)
, part(ii) follows. The nilpotence of
F
further implies
F\in Syl_{2}(G)
, part (i) follows.
∎

The following consequence is a complement to Lemma 4.3 in
[
3
]
, where
\pi\neq\{2\}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

All subgroups contained in
K
are
2
-nilpotent.

Proof.

Lemma
3.1
(i) shows that
F\in Syl_{2}(G)
. We shall proceed induction on the quotients of
\lvert F\rvert
by the orders of
2
-subgroups of all subgroups in
K
. Let
L\subseteq K
be a subgroup. If
L
is a
2^{\prime}
-group, there is nothing to prove. Choose
1\neq P\in Syl_{2}(L)
. Replacing
L
by a suitable conjugate, we may suppose that
1<Q\leq P\leq F
. Consider the quotient
\lvert F\rvert/\lvert Q\rvert
. If
\lvert F\rvert=\lvert Q\rvert
, then
N_{L}(Q)=N_{G}(Q)\cap L\leq M\cap K=Q
, clearly
N_{L}(Q)
is
2
-nilpotent. If
Q<F
, then
Q<R=N_{F}(Q)\leq N_{G}(Q)\cap K=N_{K}(Q)
which is a subgroup by Corollary 3.3 of
[
3
]
. As
\lvert F:R\rvert<\lvert F:Q\rvert
, the induction hypothesis implies that
N_{K}(Q)
is
2
-nilpotent, and thus
N_{L}(Q)
(
\leq N_{K}(Q)
) is also
2
-nilpotent. Frobenius’
p
-nilpotence criterion (7.2.4 in
[
7
, p170]
) implies that
L
is
2
-nilpotent.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
P
be a nontrivial subgroup of
F
. Then

(i)

N_{F}(P)
is a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
N_{G}(P)
, and

(ii)

N_{G}(P)=\mathscr{O}_{2^{\prime}}(N_{G}(P))(N_{G}(P)\cap M)
.

Proof.

We argue by induction on
\lvert F:P\rvert
. If
P=F
then
N_{G}(P)=M
and the result is trivial. Suppose now that
P<F
. Then
Q=N_{F}(P)>P
. By induction we obtain

N_{G}(Q)=\mathscr{O}_{2^{\prime}}(N_{G}(Q))(N_{G}(Q)\cap M).\quad\quad\mbox{(1)}

Since
P<Q
and
C_{G}(P)\subseteq K
(by Lemma
2.1
(2)), we further reach

\mathscr{O}_{2^{\prime}}(N_{G}(Q))\leq C_{G}(Q)\leq C_{G}(P)\leq N_{G}(P)\cap K.\quad\quad\mbox{(2)}

Using Dedekind’s lemma, it follows via (1) and (2) that

N_{G}(P)\cap N_{G}(Q)=\mathscr{O}_{2^{\prime}}(N_{G}(Q)))(N_{G}(P)\cap N_{G}(Q)\cap M).\quad\quad\mbox{(3)}

By Lemma
3.1
(iii) we see that
N_{G}(P)\cap N_{G}(Q)\cap F
is a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
N_{G}(P)\cap N_{G}(Q)\cap M
and hence of
N_{G}(P)\cap N_{G}(Q)
(by equality (3)). Since
Q=N_{F}(P)=N_{G}(P)\cap N_{G}(Q)\cap F
, we attain that
Q
is a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
N_{G}(P)\cap N_{G}(Q)
. We claim that
Q
is also a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
N_{G}(P)
. If otherwise, then there would be a
2
-subgroup
\tilde{Q}\in Syl_{2}(N_{G}(P))
such that
P<Q<\tilde{Q}\leq N_{G}(P)
, thus
\tilde{Q}\cap N_{G}(Q)/Q\leq N_{G}(P)\cap N_{G}(Q)/Q
which is a
2^{\prime}
-group, this forces
N_{\tilde{Q}}(Q)=\tilde{Q}\cap N_{G}(Q)=Q
. This contradiction shows
Q\in Syl_{2}(N_{G}(P))
, and yielding (i).

By Corollary 3.3 of
[
3
]
we have
N_{G}(P)\cap K\trianglelefteq N_{G}(P)
so (i) and the Frattini argument yield

N_{G}(P)=(N_{G}(P)\cap K)(N_{G}(P)\cap N_{G}(Q)).

Using (3) and (2) we deduce

\begin{array}[]{lll}N_{G}(P)&=&(N_{G}(P)\cap K)(N_{G}(P)\cap N_{G}(Q)\cap M)\\
&\leq&(N_{G}(P)\cap K)(N_{G}(P)\cap M)\leq N_{G}(P),\end{array}

hence
N_{G}(P)=(N_{G}(P)\cap K)(N_{G}(P)\cap M)
. Because
N_{G}(P)\cap K
is
2
-nilpotent (by Lemma
3.2
) and
Q
is a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
N_{G}(P)\cap K
, it follows
N_{G}(P)\cap K=\mathscr{O}_{2^{\prime}}(N_{G}(P)\cap K)Q
. Since
\mathscr{O}_{2^{\prime}}(N_{G}(P)\cap K)\leq\mathscr{O}_{2^{\prime}}(N_{G}(P))
and
Q\leq N_{G}(P)\cap M,
we derive that

N_{G}(P)=\mathscr{O}_{2^{\prime}}(N_{G}(P))(N_{G}(P)\cap M),

which proves (ii).
∎

The next consequence is “dual” to the above Lemma
3.3
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let the nonidentity element
f\in F
. Then

(i)

C_{F}(f)
is a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
C_{G}(f)
, and

(ii)

C_{G}(f)=\mathscr{O}_{2^{\prime}}(C_{G}(f))(C_{G}(f)\cap M)
.

Proof.

Let
P=\langle f\rangle
, then
C_{G}(f)=C_{G}(P)\trianglelefteq N_{G}(P)
. By Lemma
3.3
(i)
N_{F}(P)
is a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
N_{G}(P)
, so it is also a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
C_{G}(f)N_{F}(P)
which is contained in
N_{G}(P)
. It is seen that

\lvert C_{G}(f)N_{F}(P):N_{F}(P)\rvert=\lvert C_{G}(f):(C_{G}(f)\cap N_{F}(P))\rvert=

=\lvert C_{G}(f):(C_{G}(f)\cap N_{G}(P)\cap F)\rvert=\lvert C_{G}(f):(C_{G}(f)\cap F)\rvert=\lvert C_{G}(f):C_{F}(f)\rvert

is a
2^{\prime}
-number, thus
C_{F}(f)
is a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
C_{G}(f)
. This is proves (i).

Lemma 2.1 (iii) shows
C_{G}(f)\subseteq K
, and Lemma
3.2
further gives that
G_{G}(f)
is
2
-nilpotent and so using part (i) we attain that
C_{G}(f)=\mathscr{O}_{2^{\prime}}(C_{G}(f))C_{F}(f)
. As
C_{F}(f)=C_{M}(f)=C_{G}(f)\cap M
, we achieve

C_{G}(f)=\mathscr{O}_{2^{\prime}}(C_{G}(f))(C_{G}(f)\cap M),

which proves (ii).
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Any two elements of
F
that are conjugate in
G
are already conjugate in
M
.

Proof.

Let
f\in F
and
g\in G
be such that
f^{g}\in F
. We see
Z(F)^{g}\leq C_{G}(f^{g})
and Lemma
3.4
(i) gives that
C_{F}(f^{g})
is a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
C_{G}(f^{g})
. Then there is
c\in C_{G}(f^{g})
so that
Z(F)^{gc}\leq C_{F}(f^{g})\leq F
. Clearly
f^{g}=f^{gc}
, and thus it suffices to show that
gc\in M
. Because
N_{G}(Z(F)^{gc})=M^{gc}
and
F
is a normal Sylow
2
-group of
M
, we obtain
F^{gc}
is the unique Sylow
2
-subgroup of
N_{G}(Z(F)^{gc})
. Using Lemma
3.3
(i),
N_{F}(Z(F)^{gc})
is also a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
N_{G}(Z(F)^{gc})
. We may extract that
F^{gc}=N_{F}(Z(F)^{gc})\leq F
so
F^{gc}=F
, then
gc\in N_{G}(F)=M
, as required.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

H\leq M
.

Proof.

Let
\{f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{\alpha}\}
be a set of representatives for the conjugacy classes of nontrivial
2
-elements of
G
. Using Sylow’s theorem, we may choose
f_{i}\in F
for all
i
.

For each
i
let

T_{i}=f_{i}\mathscr{O}_{2^{\prime}}(C_{G}(f_{i})),

and we see
T_{i}\subseteq C_{G}(f_{i})\subseteq K
. Recall that each element of
G
can be expressed uniquely as a commuting product of a
2
-element and a
2^{\prime}
-element. For all
i,j
and all
g\in G
, we may deduce that

T_{i}\cap T_{j}^{g}\neq\phi\mbox{\quad if and only if \quad}i=j\mbox{ and }g\in C_{G}(f_{i}).

Then

K\supsetneq\bigcup_{g\in G}\bigcup_{i=1}^{\alpha}T_{i}^{g}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\alpha}\bigcup_{g\in G}T_{i}^{g},

Hence

\lvert K\rvert>\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha}\lvert G:C_{G}(f_{i})\rvert\lvert T_{i}\rvert.

Lemma
3.4
shows that
C_{G}(f_{i})=\mathscr{O}_{2^{\prime}}(C_{G}(f_{i}))C_{F}(f_{i})
for each
i
. Lemma 2.1(ii) gives that
\lvert G\rvert=\lvert K\rvert\lvert H\rvert
. We therefore derive that

\frac{1}{\lvert H\rvert}>\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha}\frac{1}{\lvert C_{F}(f_{i})\rvert}.

By Lemma
2.3
we allow to replace
H
by a suitable conjugate so that
M\cap H\neq 1
, then Lemma
2.4
guarantees that
M\cap H
and
F
are Frobenius complement and kernel, respectively. Lemma 2.1(ii) shows
\lvert M\rvert=\lvert H\cap M\rvert\lvert F\rvert
. Thus we obtain the following form of the above inequality.

1>\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha}\frac{\lvert H\rvert}{\lvert C_{F}(f_{i})\rvert}=\frac{\lvert H:H\cap M\rvert}{\lvert F\rvert}\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha}\frac{\lvert H\cap M\rvert\lvert F\rvert}{\lvert C_{F}(f_{i})\rvert}.

By Lemma 2.1(3) we have
C_{F}(f_{i})=C_{M}(f_{i})
for all
i
, whence

1>\frac{\lvert H:H\cap M\rvert}{\lvert F\rvert}\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha}\frac{\lvert M\rvert}{\lvert C_{M}(f_{i})\rvert}.

Lemma
3.5
implies that
\{f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{\alpha}\}
is a set of representatives for the conjugacy classes of nontrivial
2
-elements of
M
. By Lemma
3.1
the set of
2
-elements of
M
is equal to
F-\{1\}
thus

1>\frac{\lvert H:H\cap M\rvert}{\lvert F\rvert}(\lvert F\rvert-1)=\lvert H:H\cap M\rvert(1-\frac{1}{\lvert F\rvert}).

Now
\lvert F\rvert\geq 2
so
2>\lvert H:H\cap M\rvert
and hence
H\leq M
, as desired.

Finally, by Lemmas
3.6
and
2.4
we have
M=F\rtimes H
, and Lemma
2.2
implies that
K
is a normal subgroup of
G
, the whole proof is complete.
∎

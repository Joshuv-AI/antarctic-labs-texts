The harmonic H ∞ H^{\infty} -functional calculus based on the S S -spectrum
Antonino De Martino, Stefano Pinton, Peter Schlosser

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to introduce the H ∞ H^{\infty} -functional calculus for harmonic functions over the quaternions. More precisely, we give meaning to D ​ f ​ ( T ) Df(T) for unbounded sectorial operators T T and polynomially growing functions of the form D ​ f Df , where f f is a slice hyperholomorphic function and D = ∂ q 0 + e 1 ∂ q 1 + e 2 ∂ q 2 + e 3 ∂ q 3 D=\partial_{q_{0}}+e_{1}\partial_{q_{1}}+e_{2}\partial_{q_{2}}+e_{3}\partial_{q_{3}} is the Cauchy-Fueter operator. The harmonic functional calculus can be viewed as a modification of the well known S S -functional calculus f ⁡ ( T ) f(T) , with a different resolvent operator. The harmonic H ∞ H^{\infty} -functional calculus is defined in two steps: First, for functions with a certain decay property, one can make sense of the bounded operator D ​ f ​ ( T ) Df(T) directly via a Cauchy-type formula. In a second step, a regularization procedure is used to extend the functional calculus to polynomially growing functions and consequently unbounded operators D ​ f ​ ( T ) Df(T) . The harmonic functional calculus is an important functional calculus of the quaternionic fine structures on the S S -spectrum, which arise also in the Clifford setting and they encompass a variety of function spaces and the corresponding functional calculi. These function spaces emerge through all possible factorizations of the second map of the Fueter-Sce extension theorem. This field represents an emerging and expanding research area that serves as a bridge connecting operator theory, harmonic analysis, and hypercomplex analysis.

1.
Introduction

The complex Riesz-Dunford functional calculus
[
37
]
is based on the Cauchy integral formula and gives meaning to holomorphic functions of operators via the integral

(1)

f(A):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Omega}(z\mathcal{I}-A)^{-1}f(z)dz,

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
is a suitable open set containing the spectrum of the complex bounded linear operator
A
.
Extending the holomorphic functional calculus to quaternionic operators presented a formidable challenge because of the various potential concepts of quaternionic holomorphicity.
Indeed, in hypercomplex analysis, unlike in complex analysis, it is important to recognize the existence of multiple notions of analyticity, including slice hyperholomorphic functions and Cauchy-Fueter regular functions.
Moreover, in the slice hyperholomorphic setting, due to the lack of commutativity, there exist two different hyperholomorphic Cauchy kernels

(2)

S_{L}^{-1}(s,q):=(s-\overline{q})(s^{2}-2q_{0}s+|q|^{2})^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad S_{R}^{-1}(s,q):=(s^{2}-2q_{0}s+|q|^{2})^{-1}(s-\overline{q}).

For
f
in the space
\mathcal{SH}_{L}(U)
of left slice hyperholomorphic functions, the left Cauchy kernel
S_{L}^{-1}(s,q)
gives raise to the Cauchy integral formula

(3)

f(q)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\partial(U\cap\mathbb{C}_{J})}S_{L}^{-1}(s,q)ds_{J}f(s),

while for right slice hyperholomorphic functions a similar formula holds true using the right Cauchy kernel
S_{R}^{-1}(s,q)
, see for example
[
21
, Theorem 2.1.32]
. For the interpretation of the integral and the notion of slice hyperholomorphicity we refer to Definition
2.3
and Definition
2.2
. Motivated by the integral (
3
), the quaternionic extension of (
1
), called
S
-functional calculus for a quaternionic linear operator
T
, is defined by

(4)

f(T):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\partial(U\cap\mathbb{C}_{J})}S_{L}^{-1}(s,T)ds_{J}f(s),

where the left
S
-resolvent operator
S_{L}^{-1}(s,T)
is associated with (
2
) and properly defined in (
12
). In this paper we establish a functional calculus similar to (
4
), for functions in the space

\mathcal{AH}_{L}(U)=\Set{Df}{f\in\mathcal{SH}_{L}(U)},

where
D
is the Cauchy-Fueter operator

(5)

D=\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{0}}+e_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{1}}+e_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{2}}+e_{3}\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{3}}.

Due to the Fueter mapping theorem
[
38
]
, the space
\mathcal{AH}_{L}(U)
is exactly the one of axially harmonic functions. Applying the operator
D
to the formula (
3
), carrying the derivatives inside the integral and using the explicit formula

DS_{L}^{-1}(s,q)=-2(s^{2}-2q_{0}s+|q|^{2})^{-1}=:-2Q_{c,s}^{-1}(q)

of the Cauchy-kernel, we obtain the Cauchy-type formula for axially harmonic functions

Df(q)=\frac{-1}{\pi}\int_{\partial(U\cap\mathbb{C}_{J})}Q_{c,s}^{-1}(q)ds_{J}f(s).

Replacing the quaternion
q
by a suitable quaternionic operator
T
the above formula motivates the definition of the harmonic functional calculus

(6)

Df(T)=\frac{-1}{\pi}\int_{\partial(U\cap\mathbb{C}_{J})}Q_{c,s}^{-1}(T)ds_{J}f(s).

For a rigorous definition of the operator
Q_{c,s}^{-1}(T)
see (
10
) and (
11
). For bounded operators
T
, this functional calculus is already well established in
[
11
,
35
]
. In this paper we consider the class of unbounded sectorial operators (also called operators of type
\omega
). The set
U
mentioned in the boundary integration within equation (
6
) must encompass the
S
-spectrum of
T
. So for this class of operators the
S
-spectrum is unbounded and so we have to require additional decay properties on the function

to ensure the convergence of the integral.

Nonetheless, by employing a regularization procedure known as the

-functional calculus, originally introduced by A. McIntosh in the complex context
[
44
]
, we can subsequently extend the harmonic functional calculus to include functions

that exhibit polynomial growth. To explore the

-functional calculus in the complex context, one can refer to the following sources:
[

,
41
,
42
,
48
]
. Additionally, for an extension of the

-functional calculus in the monogenic Clifford setting and its applications, one can consider the books

.

We now intend to offer a comprehensive perspective on our theory by elucidating its initial motivations, detailing recent developments, and introducing a new branch known as the fine structures on the
S
-spectrum. Within this new branch, we encounter the development of the harmonic functional calculus, whose

The study of quaternionic operators is motivated by their relevance to the formulation of quantum mechanics. The foundational work by Birkhoff and von Neumann
[
8

S

The development of the spectral theory on the

In the special case of the quaternions

2.
Preliminary results on quaternionic function theory

The setting in which we will work in this paper is the one of the
quaternions

\mathbb{H}:=\Set{s_{0}+s_{1}e_{1}+s_{2}e_{2}+s_{3}e_{3}}{s_{0},s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}\in\mathbb{R}}

with the three imaginary units
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}
satisfying the relations

e_{1}^{2}=e_{2}^{2}=e_{3}^{2}=-1\qquad\text{and}\qquad\begin{array}[]{l}e_{1}e_{2}=-e_{2}e_{1}=e_{3},\\
e_{2}e_{3}=-e_{3}e_{2}=e_{1},\\
e_{3}e_{1}=-e_{1}e_{3}=e_{2}.\end{array}

For every quaternion
s\in\mathbb{H}
we set

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}(s)

\displaystyle:=s_{0},

\displaystyle(\textit{real part})

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}(s)

\displaystyle:=s_{1}e_{1}+s_{2}e_{2}+s_{3}e_{3},

\displaystyle(\textit{imaginary part})

\displaystyle\overline{s}

\displaystyle:=s_{0}-s_{1}e_{1}-s_{2}e_{2}-s_{3}e_{3},

\displaystyle(\textit{conjugate})

\displaystyle|s|

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{s_{0}^{2}+s_{1}^{2}+s_{2}^{2}+s_{3}^{2}}.

\displaystyle(\textit{modulus})

The unit sphere of purely imaginary quaternions is defined as

\mathbb{S}:=\Set{s\in\mathbb{H}}{s_{0}=0\text{ and }|s|=1},

and for every
J\in\mathbb{S}
consider the complex plane

\mathbb{C}_{J}:=\Set{u+Jv}{u,v\in\mathbb{R}},

which is an isomorphic copy of the complex numbers, since every
J\in\mathbb{S}
satisfies
J^{2}=-1
. Moreover, for every quaternion
s\in\mathbb{H}
we consider the corresponding
2
-sphere

[s]:=\Set{\operatorname{Re}(s)+J|\operatorname{Im}(s)|}{J\in\mathbb{S}}.

Next, we introduce the so called
slice hyperholomorphic functions
, which are a quaternionic analog to the complex holomorphic functions. The sets on which those functions are defined and suitable for operator theory
are the following
axially symmetric sets
.

Definition 2.1

(Axially symmetric sets)
.

A subset
U\subseteq\mathbb{H}
is called
axially symmetric
, if
[s]\subseteq U
for every
s\in U
.

Definition 2.2

(Slice hyperholomorphic functions)
.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{H}
be axially symmetric and open. A function
f:U\rightarrow\mathbb{H}
is called
left
(resp.
right
)
slice hyperholomorphic
, if there exists two continuously differentiable functions
\alpha,\beta:\mathcal{U}:=\Set{(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}{u+\mathbb{S}v\subseteq U}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}
, with

(8)

f(u+Jv)=\alpha(u,v)+J\beta(u,v),\quad\Big(\text{resp.}\;f(u+Jv)=\alpha(u,v)+\beta(u,v)J\Big),

for every
(u,v)\in\mathcal{U}
,
J\in\mathbb{S}
, and if the functions
\alpha,\beta
satisfy the symmetry conditions

\alpha(u,-v)=\alpha(u,v)\quad\text{and}\quad\beta(u,-v)=-\beta(u,v),

as well as the Cauchy-Riemann equations

(9)

\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\alpha(u,v)=\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\beta(u,v),\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\alpha(u,v)=-\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\beta(u,v).

The class of left (resp. right) slice hyperholomorphic functions on
U
is denoted by
\mathcal{SH}_{L}(U)
(resp.
\mathcal{SH}_{R}(U)
). In the special case that the functions
\alpha
and
\beta
are real valued, we call the function
f

intrinsic
and denote the space of intrinsic functions by
\mathcal{N}(U)
.

Next we introduce path integrals of slice hyperholomorphic functions. Since it is sufficient to consider paths embedded in only one complex plane
\mathbb{C}_{J}
, the idea is to reduce it to a classical complex path integral.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbb{H}
be axially symmetric,
J\in\mathbb{S}
. Then for any
f\in\mathcal{SH}_{R}(U^{\prime})
,
g\in\mathcal{SH}_{L}(U^{\prime})
for some open set
U^{\prime}\supseteq\overline{U}
, we define the integral

\int_{\partial(U\cap\mathbb{C}_{J})}f(s)ds_{J}g(s):=\int_{a}^{b}f(\gamma(t))\frac{\gamma^{\prime}(t)}{J}g(\gamma(t))dt,

where
\gamma:[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{J}
is a parametrization of the boundary
\partial(U\cap\mathbb{C}_{J})
.

From now on
V
always denotes a two-sided Banach space over the quaternions
\mathbb{H}
. In the following we will specify the class of operators with commuting components, which will be of interest in this paper.

Definition 2.4

(Operators with commuting components)
.

A right-linear closed operator
T:V\rightarrow V
with a two-sided linear domain
\operatorname{dom}(T)
is said to be an
operator with commuting components
, if there exist two-sided linear operators
T_{i}:V\rightarrow V
,
i\in\{0,\dots,3\}
, with
\operatorname{dom}(T_{i})=\operatorname{dom}(T)
, such that

T=T_{0}+e_{1}T_{1}+e_{2}T_{2}+e_{3}T_{3},

and the property that for all
i,j\in\{0,\dots,3\}

\operatorname{dom}(T^{2})\subseteq\operatorname{dom}(T_{i}T_{j})\quad\text{and}\quad T_{i}T_{j}=T_{j}T_{i}\text{ on }\operatorname{dom}(T^{2}).

We will denote the class of closed operators with commuting components by
\mathcal{KC}(V)
.

For any operator
T\in\mathcal{KC}(V)
we additionally define the
conjugate operator

\overline{T}:=T_{0}-e_{1}T_{1}-e_{2}T_{2}-e_{3}T_{3},

with
\operatorname{dom}(\overline{T}):=\operatorname{dom}(T)
. Then for every
T\in\mathcal{KC}(V)
there obviously holds the properties

\operatorname{dom}(\overline{T}T)=\operatorname{dom}(T^{2})\subseteq\operatorname{dom}(T\overline{T}),

as well as

|T|^{2}:=\overline{T}T=T\overline{T}=T_{0}^{2}+T_{1}^{2}+T_{2}^{2}+T_{3}^{2},\quad\text{on }\operatorname{dom}(T^{2}).

For any operator
T\in\mathcal{KC}(V)
we now use the operator

(10)

Q_{c,s}(T):=s^{2}\mathcal{I}-2T_{0}s+|T|^{2},\qquad\text{with }\operatorname{dom}Q_{c,s}(T)=\operatorname{dom}(T^{2}),

to define the
S
-resolvent set

(11)

\rho_{S}(T):=\Set{s\in\mathbb{H}}{Q_{c,s}(T)\text{ is bijective}}.

The complement of this set will be called the
S
-spectrum

\sigma_{S}(T):=\mathbb{H}\setminus\rho_{S}(T).

It is proven in
[
20
, Theorem 3.1.6]
that
\rho_{S}(T)
is an axially symmetric open subset of
\mathbb{H}
and that the mapping
s\mapsto Q_{c,s}^{-1}(T)
is intrinsic on
\rho_{S}(T)
. Moreover, motivated by the Cauchy kernels (
2
) we define for every
s\in\rho_{S}(T)
the
left
and the
right
S
-resolvent

(12)

S_{L}^{-1}(s,T):=(s\mathcal{I}-\overline{T})Q_{c,s}^{-1}(T)\quad\text{and}\quad S_{R}^{-1}(s,T):=sQ_{c,s}^{-1}(T)-\sum\limits_{i=0}^{3}T_{i}Q_{c,s}^{-1}(T)\overline{e_{i}}.

Note, that on
\operatorname{dom}(T)
we are allowed to interchange
T_{i}Q_{c,s}^{-1}(T)=Q_{c,s}^{-1}(T)T_{i}
, which gives the more elegant form of the right
S
-resolvent

(13)

S_{R}^{-1}(s,T)=Q_{c,s}^{-1}(T)(s\mathcal{I}-\overline{T}),\quad\text{on }\operatorname{dom}(T).

Remark 2.5
.

Note, that the original definition of the
S
-resolvent set is

(14)

\rho_{S}(T):=\Set{s\in\mathbb{H}}{Q_{s}(T)\text{ is bijective}},

using the operator

Q_{s}(T):=T^{2}-2s_{0}T+|s|^{2}.

This definition has the advantage that it is well defined for any closed operator
T:V\rightarrow V
, not only for those with commuting components. However, it is proven in
[
20
, Theorem 3.3.4]
that for operators
T\in\mathcal{KC}(V)
the two definitions (
11
) and (
14
) coincide. Moreover, also the left (resp. right)
S
-resolvent operators (
12
) are originally defined as

(15)

S_{L}^{-1}(s,T):=Q_{s}^{-1}(T)\overline{s}-TQ_{s}^{-1}(T)\quad\text{and}\quad S_{R}^{-1}(s,T):=(\overline{s}\mathcal{I}-T)Q_{s}^{-1}(T),

but turn out to be equivalent to (
12
) in the case of operators
T\in\mathcal{KC}(V)
.

In order to finally define the class of operators, for which we will introduce the harmonic
H^{\infty}
-functional calculus in this paper, we denote for every angle
\omega\in(0,\pi)
the open sector

S_{\omega}:=\Set{s\in\mathbb{H}\setminus\{0\}}{|\operatorname{Arg}(s)|<\omega},

where
\operatorname{Arg}(s)=0
if
\operatorname{Im}(s)=0
, and in the case
\operatorname{Im}(s)\neq 0
, understood as the argument of a complex number in the complex plane
s\in\mathbb{C}_{J}
, with
J=\frac{\operatorname{Im}(s)}{|\operatorname{Im}(s)|}
.

Definition 2.6

(Operators of type
\omega
)
.

Any
T\in\mathcal{KC}(V)
is called
operator of type

\omega\in(0,\pi)
, if
\sigma_{S}(T)\subseteq\overline{S_{\omega}}
and if for every
\theta\in(\omega,\pi)
there exists some
C_{\theta}\geq 0
, such that

(16)

\|S_{L}^{-1}(s,T)\|\leq\frac{C_{\theta}}{|s|}\quad\text{and}\quad\|S_{R}^{-1}(s,T)\|\leq\frac{C_{\theta}}{|s|},\quad\text{for every }s\in S_{\theta}^{c}\setminus\{0\}.

A similar estimate as in
[
18
, Lemma 3.7]
now shows, that the estimates (
16
) on the
S
-resolvent operators imply a similar estimate on the commutative
Q
-resolvent
Q_{c,s}^{-1}(T)
, which will be crucial for the existence of the integral (
19a
) of the harmonic functional calculus.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{KC}(V)
such that
T,\overline{T}
are of type
\omega
. Then for every
\theta\in(\omega,\pi)
there exists some
C_{\theta}\geq 0
, such that

(17)

\|Q_{c,s}^{-1}(T)\|\leq\frac{C_{\theta}}{|s|^{2}},\quad\text{for every }s\in S_{\theta}^{c}\setminus\{0\}.

Proof.

Using the representations (
12
) for
S_{L}^{-1}(s,T)
and (
13
) for
S_{R}^{-1}(s,T)
, gives

\displaystyle\big(S_{R}^{-1}(s,T)+S_{R}^{-1}(s,\overline{T})\big)

\displaystyle\big(S_{L}^{-1}(s,T)+S_{L}^{-1}(s,\overline{T})\big)

\displaystyle=Q_{c,s}^{-1}(T)\big((s\mathcal{I}-\overline{T})+(s\mathcal{I}-T)\big)\big((s\mathcal{I}-\overline{T})+(s\mathcal{I}-T)\big)Q_{c,s}^{-1}(T)

\displaystyle=4Q_{c,s}^{-1}(T)(s\mathcal{I}-T_{0})^{2}Q_{c,s}^{-1}(T),

as well as

\displaystyle\big(S_{R}^{-1}(s,T)-S_{R}^{-1}(s,\overline{T})\big)

\displaystyle\big(S_{L}^{-1}(s,T)-S_{L}^{-1}(s,\overline{T})\big)

\displaystyle=Q_{c,s}^{-1}(T)\big((s\mathcal{I}-\overline{T})-(s\mathcal{I}-T)\big)\big((s\mathcal{I}-\overline{T})-(s\mathcal{I}-T)\big)Q_{c,s}^{-1}(T)

\displaystyle=4Q_{c,s}^{-1}(T)(T_{0}^{2}-|T|^{2})Q_{c,s}^{-1}(T).

Note, that it is justified by
\operatorname{ran}(S_{L}^{-1}(s,T))\subseteq\operatorname{dom}(T)
to use the representation (
13
) of
S_{R}^{-1}(T)
. By subtracting these two equations we then conclude the identity

\displaystyle\big(S_{R}^{-1}(s,T)+

\displaystyle S_{R}^{-1}(s,\overline{T})\big)\big(S_{L}^{-1}(s,T)+S_{L}^{-1}(s,\overline{T})\big)

\displaystyle-\big(S_{R}^{-1}(s,T)-S_{R}^{-1}(s,\overline{T})\big)\big(S_{L}^{-1}(s,T)-S_{L}^{-1}(s,\overline{T})\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=4Q_{c,s}^{-1}(T)(s^{2}-2sT_{0}+|T|^{2})Q_{c,s}^{-1}(T)=4Q_{c,s}^{-1}(T).

Since
T
as well as
\overline{T}
are assumed to be of type
\omega
, the estimates (
16
) of the
S
-resolvents hold for
T
as well as for
\overline{T}
(let’s say for the same constant
C_{\theta}
) and we get the final estimate

\|Q_{c,s}^{-1}(T)\|\leq\frac{1}{4}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{C_{\theta}}{|s|}+\frac{C_{\theta}}{|s|}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{C_{\theta}}{|s|}+\frac{C_{\theta}}{|s|}\bigg)+\bigg(\frac{C_{\theta}}{|s|}+\frac{C_{\theta}}{|s|}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{C_{\theta}}{|s|}+\frac{C_{\theta}}{|s|}\bigg)\bigg)=\frac{2C_{\theta}^{2}}{|s|^{2}}.\qed

3.

In this section we take the first step in establishing the harmonic functional calculus
for unbounded operators of type

In the last part of this section we investigate how the harmonic functional calculus acts on intrinsic rational functions, i.e. on functions of the form

and start with a lemma, ensuring its invertibility.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
T:V\rightarrow V
be a right-linear closed operator with a two-sided linear domain and

does not admit any zeros in

Proof.

For the constant polynomial

, we have

by the assumption

. Hence the operator
q[T]=q_{0}
is bijective. Let us now consider
\deg(q)=m\geq 1
and choose some arbitrary bounded slice Cauchy domain

, containing all the zeros of

(48)

Then we will prove by induction that
\operatorname{ran}(B)\subseteq\operatorname{dom}(T^{j})
and

(49)

T^{j}B=\frac{-1}{2\pi}\int_{\partial(U\cap\mathbb{C}_{J})}S_{L}^{-1}(s,T)ds_{J}\frac{s^{j}}{q(s)},\qquad j\in\{0,\dots,m-1\}.

The induction start

is clear by definition (
48

we use the identity

(50)

S_{L}^{-1}(s,T)s=TS_{L}^{-1}(s,T)+\mathcal{I},

[

, to get

(51)

\int_{\partial(U\cap\mathbb{C}_{J})}S_{L}^{-1}(s,T)ds_{J}\frac{s^{j}}{q(s)}=\int_{\partial(U\cap\mathbb{C}_{J})}TS_{L}^{-1}(s,T)ds_{J}\frac{s^{j-1}}{q(s)}+\int_{\partial(U\cap\mathbb{C}_{J})}ds_{J}\frac{s^{j-1}}{q(s)}.

by a ball

centered at the origin and with large enough radius

(52)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial(U\cap\mathbb{C}_{J})}S_{L}^{-1}(s,T)ds_{J}\frac{s^{j}}{q(s)}

\displaystyle S_{L}^{-1}(s,T)q(s)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{2\pi}\int_{\partial(U\cap\mathbb{C}_{J})}S_{L}^{-1}(s,T)ds_{J}1=\frac{-1}{2\pi}\int_{\partial(U\cap\mathbb{C}_{J})}S_{L}^{-1}(s,T)ds_{J}\frac{q(s)}{q(s)}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{2\pi}\int_{\partial(U\cap\mathbb{C}_{J})}TS_{L}^{-1}(s,T)ds_{J}\frac{1}{q(s)}=\frac{-1}{2\pi}\int_{\partial(U\cap\mathbb{C}_{J})}(S_{L}^{-1}(s,T)s-\mathcal{I})ds_{J}\frac{1}{q(s)}

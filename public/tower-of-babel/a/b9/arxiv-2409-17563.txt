Translation-based completeness on compact intervals
Lukas Liehr

Abstract
. Given a compact interval I ⊆ ℝ I\subseteq\mathbb{R} , and a function f f that is a product of a nonzero polynomial with a Gaussian, it will be shown that the translates { f ( ⋅ − λ ) : λ ∈ Λ } \{f(\cdot-\lambda):\lambda\in\Lambda\} are complete in C ⁡ ( I ) C(I) if and only if the series of reciprocals of Λ \Lambda diverges. This extends a theorem in [R. A. Zalik, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 243, 299-308]. An additional characterization is obtained when Λ \Lambda is an arithmetic progression, and the generator f f constitutes a linear combination of translates of a function with sufficiently fast decay.

42C30, 42A65

1.
Introduction and results

Let
X
be a Banach space of functions defined on a measurable set
I\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
, such as
L^{p}(I)
or
C(I)
, equipped with the usual norms. For a given
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
and a translation set
\Lambda\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
, we define the system of
\Lambda
-translates of
f
by

\mathcal{T}(f,\Lambda)=\{f(\cdot-\lambda):\lambda\in\Lambda\}.

The
completeness problem of translates
investigates under what conditions on
X,f
, and
\Lambda
, the system
\mathcal{T}(f,\Lambda)
is complete in
X
. A result by Wiener states that if
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
(resp.
f\in{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}
) then
\mathcal{T}(f,{\mathbb{R}})
is complete in
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
(resp.
{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}
) if and only if the Fourier transform of
f
does not vanish anywhere (resp. almost everywhere)
[
25
]
. It was shown that such a characterization does not hold for
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
with
1<p<2

[
16
,
23
]
.

There has been significant interest in determining whether completeness of translates is possible when the translation set
\Lambda
is a discrete set, such as the integers or a uniformly discrete set, i.e., a set with the property that there exists a constant
c>0
such that
|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|\geq c
for all distinct
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda

[
3
,
7
,
1
,
15
,
19
]
.

In the present paper we are interested in the completeness problem of discrete translates in spaces of compactly supported functions. Precisely, we assume that
f\in C({\mathbb{R}})
is a (globally) continuous function,
I\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
is a compact set, and
\Lambda\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
is a translation set. The question is whether for every
\varepsilon>0
and every
g\in C(I)
there exists
h\in{\mathrm{span}}(\mathcal{T}(f,\Lambda))
such that

\sup_{t\in I}|g(t)-h(t)|\leq\varepsilon.

Inquiries of the above nature arise in the study of generalized periodic functions. Specifically, a function
f\in C({\mathbb{R}})
is called
mean-periodic
if the convolution equation

f*\mu(x)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x-t)\,d\mu(t)=0,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}},

admits a non-trivial solution
\mu\in\mathcal{M}
, where
\mathcal{M}
denotes the space of all compactly supported complex regular Borel measures on
{\mathbb{R}}

[
22
,
12
]
. By duality this is equivalent to the property that
\mathcal{T}(f,{\mathbb{R}})
is incomplete in
C(I)
with
I={\mathrm{supp}}(\mu)
.

Moreover, it has been recently shown that obtaining completeness results in
C(I)
with respect to a discrete translation set
\Lambda
implies solutions to the so-called
uniqueness problem in phase retrieval

[
9
,
24
]
. The phase retrieval problem refers to a non-linear inverse problem which garnered considerable attention in recent times
[
8
,
4
,
11
]
. A systematic connection between the uniqueness problem in phase retrieval and the completeness problem of translates was derived in
[
10
]
.

The completeness problem of
\mathcal{T}(f,\Lambda)
in
C(I)
for different choices of
f
and
\Lambda
was investigated by several authors:

•

Akhiezer, Borwein-Erdélyi, Min.
Let
f(t)=\frac{1}{t}
and let
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}\setminus[-1,1]
be a sequence. A theorem of Akhiezer states that
\mathcal{T}(f,\Lambda)
is complete in
C[-1,1]
, if and only if
\sum_{n}\sqrt{\lambda_{n}^{2}-1}=\infty

[
2
, p. 254, Problem 7]
. This theorem can be proved by establishing the existence of a so-called everywhere unbounded Bernstein inequality, as shown by Borwein and Erdély
[
5
,
6
]
. Generalizations of this statement to rational functions beyond
f(t)=\frac{1}{t}
are due to Min
[
18
]
.

•

Landau.
Suppose that
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\setminus\{0\}
is the restriction to
{\mathbb{R}}
of an entire function. Let
b>a>0
and let
\Lambda
be a zero-sequence of distinct numbers in
[-(b-a),b-a]
. A theorem of Landau states that
\mathcal{T}(f,\Lambda)
is complete in
C[-a,a]

[
13
]
.

•

Zalik.
Let
f(t)=e^{-ct^{2}}
with
c>0
, let
\Lambda
be a sequence of distinct real numbers, and let
I
be a compact interval. A theorem of Zalik states that
\mathcal{T}(f,\Lambda)
is complete in
L^{2}(I)
if any only if
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\frac{1}{|\lambda|}=\infty

[
26
]
. An analogous statement with
L^{2}(I)
replaced by
C(I)
was shown by Wellershoff
[
24
]
.

Motivated by Zalik’s theorem, we consider functions of the form
f=q\varphi
, where
q
is a polynomial and
\varphi
is a Gaussian. Recent studies have shown that for such products,
\mathcal{T}(f,a{\mathbb{Z}})
is complete in
C(I)
, provided a density condition is met, i.e., the parameter
a
is a sufficiently small positive constant depending on
I

[
10
]
.
The following result demonstrates that no density condition is required; instead, a Müntz-type condition is both necessary and sufficient. This leads to a characterization of completeness in the spirit of Zalik.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
q
be a nonzero polynomial, let
\varphi(t)=e^{-ct^{2}}
with
c>0
be a Gaussian, let
I\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
be a compact interval, and let
\Lambda\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
be a sequence of distinct real numbers. Further, let
X
be one of the spaces
C(I)
or
L^{p}(I)
with
p\in[1,\infty)
. Then the following two statements are equivalent.

(1)

\mathcal{T}(q\varphi,\Lambda)
is complete in
X
.

(2)

The series of reciprocals of
\Lambda
diverges,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\Lambda\\
\lambda\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|\lambda|}=\infty.

(1.1)

Theorem
1.1
asserts that in Zalik’s characterization of discrete translates, the Gaussian can be replaced by a product of a polynomial with a Gaussian. We now consider whether completeness is preserved when the generating function
f
is replaced by a linear combination of shifts of
f
. The following statement shows that this is indeed the case if the generating function has a sufficiently fast decay and
\Lambda
is an arithmetic progression, i.e.,
\Lambda=a{\mathbb{Z}}+b
for some
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
a\neq 0
.

To state this precisely, we recall that a function
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is said to have super-exponential decay if for every
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the following condition holds:

\lim\limits_{|x|\to\infty}f(x)e^{\gamma|x|}=0.

(1.2)

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
a\neq 0
, let
I\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
be a compact interval, and let
X
be one of the spaces
C(I)
or
L^{p}(I),\,p\in[1,\infty)
. If
f\in C({\mathbb{R}})
has super-exponential decay, then the following statements are equivalent.

(1)

The system
\mathcal{T}(f,a{\mathbb{Z}}+b)
is complete in
X
.

(2)

There exists
F\in{\mathrm{span}}(\mathcal{T}(f,a{\mathbb{Z}}+b))
such that
\mathcal{T}(F,a{\mathbb{Z}})
is complete in
X
.

(3)

For every
F\in{\mathrm{span}}(\mathcal{T}(f,a{\mathbb{Z}}+b))\setminus\{0\}
it holds that
\mathcal{T}(F,a{\mathbb{Z}})
is complete in
X
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
we require auxiliary results on holomorphic functions. Accordingly, we denote by
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
the space of all holomorphic functions on
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}
. Further,
\mathbb{H}_{+}\coloneqq\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:{\mathrm{Re}\,}(z)>0\}
and
\mathbb{D}\coloneqq\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|z|<1\}
denote the open right half-plane and the open unit disk, respectively. The function

\phi(z)=\frac{z-1}{z+1}

(2.1)

is a biholomorphic map from
\mathbb{H}_{+}
to
\mathbb{D}
.

We notice, that if
Z=\{z_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
are the zeros of an analytic function
F
, then
Z
is understood as a sequence. In particular, if a zero
z^{\prime}
repeats
m
times in the sequence
Z
, then
F
has a zero of order
m
at
z^{\prime}
.

The following is a classical statement on zeros of holomorphic functions on the unit disc.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D})\setminus\{0\}
be bounded with zeros
Z=\{z_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. Then the Blaschke condition is satisfied:

\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}1-|z_{n}|<\infty.

The next result of Luxemburg and Korevaar concerns Fourier transforms of smooth functions supported on a compact interval
[
17
, Theorem 5.2]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}
be a sequence of complex numbers, not containing infinitely many zeros. Further, let
I\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
be a compact interval. If the sequence
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
satisfies

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in{\mathbb{N}}\\
\gamma_{n}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|\gamma_{n}|}<\infty,

then there exists a function
g\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})\setminus\{0\}
with
{\mathrm{supp}}(g)\subseteq I
, such that

G(z)\coloneqq\int_{I}e^{-izt}g(t)\,dt

defines a non-trivial entire function with the property that
\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is contained in the zero-sequence of
G
.

In addition, we require the following basic lemma, which, in the language of generalized periodic functions, says that every non-trivial integrable and continuous function is not mean-periodic
[
20
, Proposition 6.3]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\setminus\{0\}
be continuous and let
\mu
be a compactly supported complex regular Borel measure on
{\mathbb{R}}
. If
f*\mu=0
then
\mu=0
.

We are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

In the following, we assume that the polynomial
q
is of degree
d\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and is represented by

q(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}\alpha_{j}t^{j},\quad\alpha_{j}\in{\mathbb{C}}.

(2.2)

Step 1: Sufficiency.
Suppose that
\Lambda\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
is a sequence that satisfies the condition
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}1/|\lambda|=\infty
. We show that
\mathcal{T}(q\varphi,\Lambda)
is complete in
C(I)
which also yields completeness in
L^{p}(I)
for
p\in[1,\infty)
.

Define
\Lambda_{1}=\{\lambda\in\Lambda:\lambda>0\}
and
\Lambda_{2}=\{\lambda\in\Lambda:\lambda<0\}
. Then there exists
j\in\{1,2\}
such that

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}}\frac{1}{|\lambda|}=\infty.

(2.3)

Suppose that
j=1
(the case
j=2
can be treated analogously). By Riesz representation theorem
[
21
, Theorem 6.19]
, it suffices to show that if
\mu
is a complex regular Borel measure on the interval
I
such that

\int_{I}q(t-\lambda)\varphi(t-\lambda)\,d\mu(t)=0,\quad\lambda\in\Lambda_{1},

(2.4)

then
\mu=0
. Using the factorization
\varphi(t-\lambda)=e^{-ct^{2}}e^{2ct\lambda}e^{-c\lambda^{2}}
, we observe that equation (
2.4
) is equivalent to the condition

F(\lambda)=0,\quad\lambda\in\Lambda_{1},

(2.5)

where the entire function
F\in\mathcal{O}({\mathbb{C}})
is defined by

F(z)\coloneqq\int_{I}q(t-z)e^{2ctz}\,d\tilde{\mu}(t),\quad d\tilde{\mu}(t)\coloneqq e^{-ct^{2}}d\mu(t).

The fact that
F
defines an entire function follows from the theory of holomorphic integral transforms
[
14
, Chapter XII]
.
Now let
M\coloneqq\sup I
be the supremum of
I
, and let
z=x+iy\in\mathbb{H}_{+}
. Since
x>0
, we obtain the estimate

|F(z)|\leq\|\tilde{\mu}\|e^{2cMx}\sup_{t\in I}|q(t-z)|,

where
\|\tilde{\mu}\|
denotes the total variation norm of
\tilde{\mu}
. Since
q
is of degree
d
and since
I
is compact, the map

z\mapsto\frac{\sup_{t\in I}|q(t-z)|}{|z+1|^{d+1}}

(2.6)

is bounded on
\mathbb{H}_{+}
. Suppose that
C>0
is an upper bound for the function in (
2.6
) on
\mathbb{H}_{+}
. Consider functions
G
and
H
, defined by

G(z)\coloneqq H(z)F(z),\quad H(z)\coloneqq\frac{e^{-2cMz}}{(z+1)^{d+1}}.

Then
G
and
H
are holomorphic on
\mathbb{H}_{+}
.
Moreover,
G
satisfies the upper bound

|G(z)|=\frac{e^{-2cMx}}{|z+1|^{d+1}}|F(z)|\leq C\|\tilde{\mu}\|,\quad z=x+iy\in\mathbb{H}_{+}.

(2.7)

Observe, that
H
is zero-free on
\mathbb{H}_{+}
. Hence, since
F
vanishes on
\Lambda_{1}
, so does
G
. Consequently,
G
is an analytic function on
\mathbb{H}_{+}
that vanishes on
\Lambda_{1}
and that satisfies the uniform bound (
2.7
).

With
\phi:\mathbb{H_{+}}\to\mathbb{D}
defined as in (
2.1
), it follows that
\tilde{G}\coloneqq G\circ\phi^{-1}
is a bounded holomorphic function on
\mathbb{D}
. In addition, the divergence condition in (
2.3
) implies that the zeros
Z(\tilde{G})=\{\phi(\lambda):\lambda\in\Lambda_{1}\}
satisfy

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{1}}1-|\phi(\lambda)|=\infty.

According to Lemma
2.1
,
G
must vanish identically.

It follows that
F
vanishes identically. This implies that equation (
2.4
) holds not only for
\lambda\in\Lambda_{1}
but for all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Therefore, the convolution of
\mu
with the function
t\mapsto q(-t)\varphi(-t)
vanishes identically. Lemma
2.3
implies that
\mu=0
.

Step 2: Necessity.
Let
\Lambda\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
be a sequence such that
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}1/|\lambda|<\infty
. By Riesz representation theorem it suffices to construct a non-trivial complex regular Borel measure
\mu
on
I
such that

\int_{I}q(t-\lambda)e^{-c(t-\lambda)^{2}}\,d\mu(t)=0,\quad\lambda\in\Lambda.

To do so, we start by defining a related sequence
\Gamma\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
as follows:

\Gamma=\left\{\underbrace{0,0,\dots,0}_{d+1\ \mathrm{times}},\underbrace{2ic\lambda_{1},2ic\lambda_{1},\dots,2ic\lambda_{1}}_{d+1\ \mathrm{times}},\underbrace{2ic\lambda_{2},2ic\lambda_{2},\dots,2ic\lambda_{2}}_{d+1\ \mathrm{times}},\dots\right\}.

By definition of
\Gamma
it holds that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\Gamma\\
\gamma\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|\gamma|}=\frac{d+1}{2c}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\Lambda\\
\lambda\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|\lambda|}<\infty.

According to Theorem
2.2
, there exists a function
g\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})\setminus\{0\}
with support in
I
such that the (non-trivial) entire function

G(z)=\int_{I}e^{-izt}g(t)\,dt

vanishes on
\Gamma
. By construction of
\Gamma
, it follows that every point
2ic\lambda
with
\lambda\in\Lambda
is a zero of order at least
d+1
of
G
. Notice, that if
\lambda\in\Lambda
then

G(2ic\lambda)=\int_{I}e^{2c\lambda t}g(t)\,dt.

Using the definition of the polynomial
q
as given in equation (
2.2
), we obtain that for every
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
it holds that

\begin{split}\int_{I}q(t-z)e^{2czt}g(t)\,dt&=\sum_{j=0}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{j}{k}\alpha_{j}(-1)^{j-k}z^{j-k}\int_{I}t^{j}e^{2czt}g(t)\,dt\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{d}\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{j}{k}\alpha_{j}(-1)^{j-k}z^{j-k}(-i)^{-j}\left(\frac{d^{j}}{dz^{j}}G\right)(2icz).\end{split}

(2.8)

In the second equality we used the fact that we can differentiate under the integral sign.
Since
G
has zeros of order at least
d+1
at every point
2ic\lambda
where
\lambda\in\Lambda
, it follows that the right-hand side of the previous equation vanishes if
z=\lambda\in\Lambda
. In particular, the entire function

z\mapsto e^{-cz^{2}}\int_{I}q(t-z)e^{2czt}e^{-ct^{2}}e^{ct^{2}}g(t)\,dt

vanishes on
\Lambda
. By defining the measure

d\mu(t)\coloneqq e^{ct^{2}}g(t)\,dt,

and noting that
\varphi(t-\lambda)=e^{-ct^{2}}e^{2ct\lambda}e^{-c\lambda^{2}}
, we have

\int_{I}q(t-\lambda)\varphi(t-\lambda)\,d\mu(t)=0,\quad\lambda\in\Lambda.

Since
\mu
is a non-trivial complex regular Borel measure on
I
, it follows that
\mathcal{T}(q\varphi,\Lambda)
is incomplete in
C(I)
.

In order to show the related result for the spaces
L^{p}(I),\,p\in[1,\infty)
, we observe that the map
t\mapsto e^{ct^{2}}g(t)
that determines the measure
\mu
does not vanish identically and is an element of
L^{p^{\prime}}(I)
for all
p^{\prime}\in(1,\infty]
. The statement follows from the duality
(L^{p}(I))^{*}=L^{p^{\prime}}(I)
where
p,p^{\prime}
are Hölder conjugate exponents.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

We remark that the proof of Zalik’s theorem on the completeness of Gaussian translates
[
26
, Theorem 4]
relies on the classical Weierstrass approximation theorem and Theorem
2.2
. In Zalik’s proof, the statement of Luxemburg and Korevaar is directly applicable to the sequence
\Lambda
. In comparison, Theorem
1.1
provides a characterization for arbitrary products of polynomials with Gaussians using a purely complex-analytic proof.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In the following we denote by
\mathbb{P}(\ell,n)
the space of all complex polynomials in
n
variables of degree at most
\ell
. Moreover,
T_{s}f\coloneqq f(\cdot-s)
denotes the shift of a function
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
by
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
. The proof of Theorem
1.2
requires two technical lemmas. The first one reads follows.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a function, let
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
a\neq 0
, and let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then for every
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
there exist
n
polynomials

p_{\ell,1},\dots,p_{\ell,n}

with the following properties:

(1)

For every
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
and every
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
it holds that
p_{\ell,j}\in\mathbb{P}(\ell,n)
.

(2)

For every
j\in\left\{1,\dots,n\right\}
, the largest coefficient in absolute value of
p_{\ell,j}
is bounded from above by
2^{\ell-1}
.

(3)

For every coefficient vector
c=\left(c_{1},\dots,c_{n}\right)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, it holds that the linear combination

A_{\ell}\coloneqq T_{b}f+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}p_{\ell,j}(c)T_{a(\ell+j-1)+b}f

(3.1)

is an element of
{\mathrm{span}}(\mathcal{T}(F,a{\mathbb{Z}}))
, where the function
F
is defined by

F\coloneqq T_{b}f+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}T_{aj+b}f.

Proof.

We prove the statement via induction over
\ell
. To do so, we first observe that
F\in{\mathrm{span}}(\mathcal{T}(F,a{\mathbb{Z}}))
. Hence, in the case where
\ell=1
, the statement holds true with polynomials
p_{1,1},\dots,p_{1,n}
defined according to

p_{1,j}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\coloneqq x_{j}.

Now suppose that the claim holds true for an arbitrary
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We show that it also holds for
\ell+1
.

Define

q_{j}\coloneqq p_{\ell,j}(c_{1},\dots,c_{n}),\quad j\in\{1,\dots,N\},

that is,
q_{j}
is the evaluation of the polynomial
p_{\ell,j}
at
c
. With this notation, the map
A_{\ell}
as defined in (
3.1
) is given by

A_{\ell}=T_{b}f+\left(q_{1}T_{a\ell+b}f+q_{2}T_{a(\ell+1)+b}f+\dots+q_{N}T_{a(\ell+n-1)+b}f\right).

By induction hypothesis we have
A_{\ell}\in{\mathrm{span}}(\mathcal{T}(F,a{\mathbb{Z}}))
. Observe further that

B_{\ell}\coloneqq q_{1}\cdot T_{a\ell}F\in{\mathrm{span}}(\mathcal{T}(F,a{\mathbb{Z}})).

Since
{\mathrm{span}}(\mathcal{T}(F,a{\mathbb{Z}}))
is a linear space, it holds that
A_{\ell}-B_{\ell}\in{\mathrm{span}}(\mathcal{T}(F,a{\mathbb{Z}}))
. The latter difference is given by

A_{\ell}-B_{\ell}=T_{b}f+\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(q_{j+1}-q_{1}c_{j}\right)T_{a(\ell+j)+b}f\right)-q_{1}c_{n}T_{a(\ell+n)+b}f.

(3.2)

Now define polynomials
p_{\ell+1,1},\dots,p_{\ell+1,n}
by

\begin{split}p_{\ell+1,j}(x)&\coloneqq p_{\ell,j+1}(x)-p_{\ell,1}(x)x_{j},\quad j\in\{1,\dots,n-1\},\\
p_{\ell+1,n}(x)&\coloneqq-p_{\ell,1}(x)x_{n}.\end{split}

(3.3)

Then the difference
A_{\ell}-B_{\ell}
can be written in terms of polynomial evaluations by

A_{\ell}-B_{\ell}=T_{b}f+\sum_{j=1}^{n}p_{\ell+1,j}(c)T_{a(\ell+j)+b}f.

Thus, by setting
A_{\ell+1}\coloneqq A_{\ell}-B_{\ell}
we obtain the representation claimed in equation (
3.1
).

It remains to show that the polynomials satisfy properties (
1
) and (
2
) given in the statement of the Lemma.

By induction hypothesis, the polynomials
p_{\ell,j}
are of degree
\ell
. According to the definition of the polynomials
p_{\ell+1,j}
in (
3.3
), it holds that
p_{\ell+1,j}\in\mathbb{P}(\ell+1,n)
.

To show the remaining property, let
C_{\ell}
be the largest coefficient in absolute value among all the polynomials
p_{\ell,j}
where
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. It follows from the definition of the polynomials
p_{\ell+1,j}
(as difference of at most two polynomials of the form
p_{\ell,j}
), that the largest coefficient in absolute value of
p_{\ell+1,j}
does not exceed
2C_{\ell}
.
The statement thus follows by induction.
∎

The second elementary lemma concerns super-exponentially decaying functions.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
I\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
be a compact set and suppose that
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is a measurable function with super-exponential decay. Then for every
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
a\neq 0
it holds that the function

x\mapsto\sup_{t\in I}|T_{ax+b}f(t)|

has super-exponential decay.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
, let

d\coloneqq\sup_{t\in I}|t-b|,

and let
\varepsilon>0
. Since
f
has super-exponential decay, there exists a constant
R(\varepsilon)>0
such that

|f(t)|e^{\frac{\gamma}{|a|}t}\leq\varepsilon,\quad|t|\geq R(\varepsilon).

For all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
|x|\geq\frac{R(\varepsilon)+d}{|a|}
and every
t\in I
it holds that
|t-(ax+b)|\geq R(\varepsilon)
. Accordingly, for such
x
and
t
, we obtain

\begin{split}|T_{ax+b}f(t)|e^{\gamma|x|}&=|f(t-(ax+b))|\exp\left(\tfrac{\gamma}{|a|}\left|-(t-(ax+b))+t-b\right|\right)\\
&\leq|f(t-(ax+b))|\exp\left(\tfrac{\gamma}{|a|}\left|(t-(ax+b))\right|\right)\exp\left(\tfrac{\gamma}{|a|}|t-b|\right)\\
&\leq\left(\sup_{t\in I}\exp\left(\tfrac{\gamma}{|a|}|t-b|\right)\right)\varepsilon.\end{split}

(3.4)

If
C
denote the supremum on the right-hand side of the previous inequality, then

\left(\sup_{t\in I}|T_{ax+b}f(t)|\right)e^{\gamma|x|}\leq C\varepsilon,\quad|x|\geq\frac{R(\varepsilon)+d}{|a|}.

The statement follows from that.
∎

We are prepared to prove the second main result of the present paper.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The implications
(3)\implies(2)
and
(2)\implies(1)
are clear. It remains to show that (1) implies (3).

Suppose that
\mathcal{T}(f,a{\mathbb{Z}}+b)
is complete in
X
and let
F\in{\mathrm{span}}(\mathcal{T}(f,a{\mathbb{Z}}+b))\setminus\{0\}
. We need to show that

\mathrm{cl}\left({\mathrm{span}}(\mathcal{T}(F,a{\mathbb{Z}}))\right)=X,

where
\mathrm{cl}(\cdot)
denotes the closure with respect to the norm of
X
.

By definition, there exist
m_{0},m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
m_{0}<m
such that
F
is represented by

F=\sum_{k=m_{0}}^{m}d_{k}T_{ak+b}f,

where
d_{k}\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
k\in\{m_{0},\dots,m\}
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
d_{m_{0}}\neq 0
. Set
n=m-m_{0}
and

c_{j}=\frac{d_{m_{0}+j}}{d_{m_{0}}},\quad j\in\left\{1,\dots,n\right\}.

Finally, define

F_{0}\coloneqq T_{b}f+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}T_{aj+b}f=\tfrac{1}{d_{m_{0}}}\cdot T_{-am_{0}}\,F.

Then it holds that

{\mathrm{span}}(\mathcal{T}(F,a{\mathbb{Z}}))={\mathrm{span}}(\mathcal{T}(F_{0},a{\mathbb{Z}})).

(3.5)

Step 1: Application of Lemma
3.1
.

By Lemma
3.1
and relation (
3.5
), it follows that for every
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the function

A_{\ell}=T_{b}f+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}p_{\ell,j}(c)T_{a(\ell+j-1)+b}f,

(3.6)

with polynomials
p_{\ell,j}
defined according to Lemma
3.1
, belongs to the span of
\mathcal{T}(F,a{\mathbb{Z}})
. Consequently, the maximal coefficient in absolute value among all polynomials
p_{\ell,j}
is upper bounded by
2^{\ell-1}
. Notice that a polynomial of degree
\ell
in
n
variables has at most
{n+\ell}\choose\ell
non-zero coefficients. The bound

{n\choose k}\leq\exp(k)\left(\frac{n}{k}\right)^{k}

yields the estimate

\begin{split}\left\lvert p_{\ell,j}(c)\right\rvert&\leq{{n+\ell}\choose{\ell}}2^{\ell-1}\max\{\left\lvert c_{1}\right\rvert,\dots,\left\lvert c_{N}\right\rvert\}^{\ell}\\
&\leq\exp(\ell)\left(\frac{n+\ell}{\ell}\right)^{\ell}2^{\ell-1}\max\{\left\lvert c_{1}\right\rvert,\dots,\left\lvert c_{N}\right\rvert\}^{\ell}.\end{split}

(3.7)

Since
\frac{n+\ell}{\ell}\to 1
as
\ell\to\infty
, it follows that there exists a constant
C>0
which is independent of
\ell
and
j
, such that

\left\lvert p_{\ell,j}(c)\right\rvert\leq C^{\ell},\quad\ell\in{\mathbb{N}},\quad j\in\{1,\dots,n\}.

(3.8)

Step 2: Taking the limit
\ell\to\infty
.

Let
Q_{\ell}
be the function defined by

Q_{\ell}\coloneqq\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}p_{\ell,j}(c)T_{a(\ell+j-1)+b}f.

By equation (
3.6
), we have
A_{\ell}=T_{b}f+Q_{\ell}\in{\mathrm{span}}(\mathcal{T}(F,a{\mathbb{Z}}))
for every
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
. The bound (
3.8
) implies that

\sup_{t\in I}|Q_{\ell}(t)|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}C^{\ell}\sup_{t\in I}|T_{a(\ell+j-1)+b}f(t)|.

(3.9)

Since
I
is compact and since
f
has super-exponential decay, Lemma
3.2
shows that each summand on the right-hand side of equation (
3.9
) converges to zero as
\ell\to\infty
. Consequently,
A_{\ell}\to T_{b}f
uniformly on
I
. In particular,
A_{\ell}\to T_{b}f
in the norm of
X
(the uniform norm if
X=C(I)
and the
L^{p}
-norm if
X=L^{p}(I)
). Since
A_{\ell}\in{\mathrm{span}}(\mathcal{T}(F,a{\mathbb{Z}}))
for every
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, it follows that

T_{b}f\in\mathrm{cl}\left({\mathrm{span}}(\mathcal{T}(F,a{\mathbb{Z}}))\right).

(3.10)

Step 3: Shift-invariance.

The space
Y\coloneqq\mathrm{cl}\left({\mathrm{span}}(\mathcal{T}(F,a{\mathbb{Z}}))\right)
is invariant under
a{\mathbb{Z}}
-shifts, that is, if
g\in Y
then
T_{aj}g\in Y
for all
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Together with equation (
3.10
) it follows that
T_{aj+b}f\in Y
for all
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Thus,

{\mathrm{span}}(\mathcal{T}(f,a{\mathbb{Z}}+b))\subseteq Y\subseteq X.

Since
\mathcal{T}(f,a{\mathbb{Z}}+b)
was assumed to be complete in
X
, it follows that
Y
is complete in
X
.
∎

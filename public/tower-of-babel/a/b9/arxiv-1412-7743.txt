Bosonic Fradkin-Tseytlin equations unfolded.
O.V. Shaynkman

Abstract
. We test infinite-dimensional extension of algebra 𝔰 ​ 𝔲 ​ ( k , k ) \mathfrak{su}(k,k) proposed by Fradkin and Linetsky as the candidate for conformal higher spin algebra. Adjoint and twisted-adjoint representations of 𝔰 ​ 𝔲 ​ ( k , k ) \mathfrak{su}(k,k) on the space of this algebra are carefully explored. For k = 2 k=2 corresponding unfolded system is analyzed and it is shown to encode Fradkin-Tseytlin equations for the set of all integer spins 1 , 2 , … 1,2,\ldots with infinite multiplicity.

Appendix C
\sigma_{-}
-cohomology

Let
\mathfrak{C}=\{\mathcal{C},{\partial}\}
be some co-chain complex. Here

\mathcal{C}=\oplus_{p=0}\mathcal{C}^{p}
is a graded space and

(C.1)

\displaystyle{\partial}:\quad\mathcal{C}^{p}\mapsto\mathcal{C}^{p+1}\,,\quad{\partial}^{2}=0

is a differential.
The powerful tool to calculate cohomology of
\mathfrak{C}
consists in consideration of homotopy operator

{\partial}^{*}

(C.2)

\displaystyle{\partial}^{*}:\quad\mathcal{C}^{p+1}\mapsto\mathcal{C}^{p}\,,\quad({\partial}^{*})^{2}=0\,.

According to the standard result of the cohomological algebra (see e.g.
[
16
]
)
every element of
\mathfrak{C}
-cohomology group
\mathcal{H}
has representative belonging to kernel of the
anticommutator
\Theta=\{{\partial},{\partial}^{*}\}
provided that
\Theta
is diagonalizable on
\mathcal{C}
.

Indeed,
\Theta
obviously commutes with
{\partial}
and, thus, both operators have common set of eigenvectors. Suppose
\psi\in\mathcal{C}
is

{\partial}
-closed vector from this set, such that
\Theta\psi=q\psi
,
q\neq 0
. Then acting on
\psi
by operator
\frac{1}{q}\Theta

one gets that
\psi=\frac{1}{q}{\partial}{\partial}^{*}\psi
is
{\partial}
-exact.

In paper
[
17
]
it was observed that if along with the above assumptions operator
\Theta
is
positive or negative semi-definite, representatives of
\mathcal{H}
are in one-to-one correspondence with the elements from the
kernel of
\Theta
.

Let us recall the arguments of
[
17
]
. Suppose for definiteness that
\Theta
is nonnegative. Then one can define
positive scalar product
\langle\;|\;\rangle
on
\mathcal{C}
such that operators
{\partial}
and
{\partial}^{*}
are mutually Hermitian conjugate
{\partial}^{\dagger}={\partial}^{*}

with respect to it.
One then has that:

(1)

\langle\psi|\Theta\psi\rangle=\langle\psi{\partial}|{\partial}\psi\rangle+\langle\psi{\partial}^{*}|{\partial}^{*}\psi\rangle
and, thus,
elements from the kernel of
\Theta
are necessarily
{\partial}
and
{\partial}^{*}
-closed;

(2)

those
{\partial}
-closed
\psi
that are not annulated by
\Theta
are
{\partial}
-exact due to above arguments;

(3)

oppositely, if
\psi={\partial}\chi\neq 0
is
{\partial}
-exact,
\langle\psi|\psi\rangle=\langle\chi|{\partial}^{*}\psi\rangle\neq 0
, i.e.

{\partial}^{*}\psi\neq 0
and, thus, due to item
1
,
\Theta\psi\neq 0
.

Let differential
{\partial}
and homotopy
{\partial}^{*}
are given by

(C.3)

\displaystyle{\partial}=\xi^{\alpha{\dot{\beta}}}P_{\alpha{\dot{\beta}}}\,,\qquad{\partial}^{*}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{\alpha{\dot{\beta}}}}K^{\alpha{\dot{\beta}}}\,,

where
P_{\alpha{\dot{\beta}}}
and
K^{\alpha{\dot{\beta}}}
are
\mathfrak{su}(k,k)
generators of translations and special conformal transformations
represented by operators acting in some
\mathfrak{su}(k,k)
-module
M
.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be space of differential forms (graded by the rank of the form)
taking values in
M
. In other words
\mathcal{C}=\Lambda\otimes M
, where

\Lambda
denotes
k^{2}
-dimensional external algebra generated by
\xi
.

Consider subspace
\Lambda^{p}\subset\Lambda
of the
p
-th order monomials in
\xi

\mathfrak{sl}(k)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(k)
is represented on

by operators

(C.4)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi}}{{\mathcal{L}}}_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}=\xi^{\beta{\dot{\gamma}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{\alpha{\dot{\gamma}}}}-\frac{1}{k}\delta_{\alpha}^{\beta}n_{\xi}\,,\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi}}{{\bar{\mathcal{L}}}}_{\dot{\alpha}}{}^{\dot{\beta}}=\xi^{\gamma{\dot{\beta}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi^{\gamma{\dot{\alpha}}}}-\frac{1}{k}\delta_{\dot{\alpha}}^{\dot{\beta}}n_{\xi}

with quadratic Casimir operator equal to

(C.5)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi}}{{\mathcal{C}^{2}}}_{\mathfrak{sl}(k)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(k)}=\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi}}{{\mathcal{L}}}_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi}}{{\mathcal{L}}}_{\beta}{}^{\alpha}+\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi}}{{\bar{\mathcal{L}}}}_{\dot{\alpha}}{}^{\dot{\beta}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi}}{{\bar{\mathcal{L}}}}_{\dot{\beta}}{}^{\dot{\alpha}}=2kn_{\xi}-\frac{2}{k}n_{\xi}^{2}=2kp-\frac{2}{k}p^{2}\,.

In these notation operator
\Theta
has the following form

(C.6)

\displaystyle\Theta=\{{\partial},{\partial}^{*}\}=-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\mathfrak{u}(k,k)}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\mathfrak{sl}(k)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(k)}+\frac{1}{k}(D+p)(D+p-k^{2})+\frac{1}{k}Z^{2}\,,

where

is the rank of differential form,

is quadratic
\mathfrak{u}(k,k)

)
calculated on module

and
\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\mathfrak{sl}(k)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(k)}

is quadratic

-Casimir operator (

calculated on module

, i.e.

(C.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{2}_{\mathfrak{sl}(k)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(k)}=(({\rm ad}_{\mathcal{L}}){}_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}+\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi}}{{\mathcal{L}}}_{\alpha}{}^{\beta})(({\rm ad}_{\mathcal{L}}){}_{\beta}{}^{\alpha}+\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi}}{{\mathcal{L}}}_{\beta}{}^{\alpha})+(({\rm ad}_{\bar{\mathcal{L}}}{})_{\dot{\alpha}}{}^{\dot{\beta}}+\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi}}{{\bar{\mathcal{L}}}}_{\dot{\alpha}}{}^{{\dot{\beta}}})(({\rm ad}_{\bar{\mathcal{L}}}{})_{\dot{\beta}}{}^{\dot{\alpha}}+\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi}}{{\bar{\mathcal{L}}}}_{\dot{\beta}}{}^{{\dot{\alpha}}})\,.

Note that all the ingredients of (

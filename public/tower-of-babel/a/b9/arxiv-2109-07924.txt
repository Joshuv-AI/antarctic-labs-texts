Small Promise CSPs that reduce to large CSPs
Alexandr Kazda ORCID:0000-0002-7338-037X, Peter Mayr ORCID:0000-0003-1163-313X, Dmitriy Zhuk ORCID:0000-0003-0047-5076

Abstract
. For relational structures 𝐀 , 𝐁 \mathbf{A},\mathbf{B} of the same signature, the Promise Constraint Satisfaction Problem PCSP ⁡ ( 𝐀 , 𝐁 ) \mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}) asks whether a given input structure maps homomorphically to 𝐀 \mathbf{A} or does not even map to 𝐁 \mathbf{B} . We are promised that the input satisfies exactly one of these two cases. If there exists a structure 𝐂 \mathbf{C} with homomorphisms 𝐀 → 𝐂 → 𝐁 \mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{B} , then PCSP ⁡ ( 𝐀 , 𝐁 ) \mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}) reduces naturally to CSP ⁡ ( 𝐂 ) \mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{C}) . To the best of our knowledge all known tractable PCSPs reduce to tractable CSPs in this way. However Barto [ Bar19 ] showed that some PCSPs over finite structures 𝐀 , 𝐁 \mathbf{A},\mathbf{B} require solving CSPs over infinite 𝐂 \mathbf{C} . We show that even when such a reduction to some finite 𝐂 \mathbf{C} is possible, this structure may become arbitrarily large. For every integer n > 1 n>1 and every prime p p we give 𝐀 , 𝐁 \mathbf{A},\mathbf{B} of size n n with a single relation of arity n p n^{p} such that PCSP ⁡ ( 𝐀 , 𝐁 ) \mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}) reduces via a chain of homomorphisms 𝐀 → 𝐂 → 𝐁 \mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{B} to a tractable CSP \mathrm{CSP} over some 𝐂 \mathbf{C} of size p p but not over any smaller structure. In a second family of examples, for every prime p ≥ 7 p\geq 7 we construct 𝐀 , 𝐁 \mathbf{A},\mathbf{B} of size p − 1 p-1 with a single ternary relation such that PCSP ⁡ ( 𝐀 , 𝐁 ) \mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}) reduces via 𝐀 → 𝐂 → 𝐁 \mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{B} to a tractable CSP \mathrm{CSP} over some 𝐂 \mathbf{C} of size p p but not over any smaller structure. In contrast we show that if 𝐀 , 𝐁 \mathbf{A},\mathbf{B} are graphs and PCSP ⁡ ( 𝐀 , 𝐁 ) \mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}) reduces to a tractable CSP ⁡ ( 𝐂 ) \mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{C}) for some finite digraph 𝐂 \mathbf{C} , then already 𝐀 \mathbf{A} or 𝐁 \mathbf{B} has a tractable CSP \mathrm{CSP} . This extends results and answers a question of [ DSM+21 ] .

1.
Introduction

The
Constraint Satisfaction Problem
(
\mathrm{CSP}
) for a fixed relational structure
\mathbf{A}
can be formulated as the following
homomorphism problem:

\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{A})

Input:

a relational structure
\mathbf{X}

Output:

yes, if there exists a homomorphism
\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{A}
,

no, otherwise

In
[
AHG14
,
AGH17
]
Austrin, Guruswami, and Håstad
introduced
Promise Satisfaction Problems
(
\mathrm{PCSP}
) as approximations of

\mathrm{CSP}
. For relational structures
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}
of the same finite signature with a homomorphism
\mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{B}
, let

\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})

Input:

a relational structure
\mathbf{X}

Output:

yes, if there exists a homomorphism
\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{A}
,

no, if there exists no homomorphism
\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{B}

Since we have a homomorphism
\mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{B}
by assumption, the two alternatives
(1)
\mathbf{X}
maps homomorphically to
\mathbf{A}
or (2)
\mathbf{X}
does not map homomorphically to
\mathbf{B}

are mutually exclusive for any input
\mathbf{X}
. The
promise
is that at least one
of the alternatives
holds for
\mathbf{X}
.

\mathrm{PCSP}
s are motivated by open questions about the (in)approximability of SAT and graph coloring.
The classical
approximate graph coloring problem
for
r\leq s
asks: given an
r
-colorable graph, find an
s
-coloring for
it
[
GJ76
]
.
The decision version of this is to distinguish graphs that are
r
-colorable from those that are not even
s
-colorable.
This is
\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{K}_{r},\mathbf{K}_{s})
for
\mathbf{K}_{r},\mathbf{K}_{s}
the complete graphs on
r,s
vertices, respectively.
It (and consequently the approximate graph coloring problem) has been conjectured to be
{\sf{NP}}
-hard for all
3\leq r\leq s
.
Even after more than 40 years of research this conjecture remains open in its full generality.

Let
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{C}
be relational structures of the same signature with homomorphisms
\mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{B}
. Then we say
\mathbf{C}
is

sandwiched
by
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
.
In this case
\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
has a trivial reduction to
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{C})
without changing the instance.
In general, the complexity of
the
\mathrm{PCSP}
is unknown.
Still, to the best of our knowledge, all known tractable
(i.e., solvable in polynomial time)
PCSPs reduce to tractable CSPs in this way.

As in
[
AB21
]
we call
\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})

finitely tractable
if there exists a finite
\mathbf{C}
that is sandwiched by

\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
such that
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{C})
is tractable.
Barto
[
Bar19
]
provided the first example of a PCSP
over finite structures
that is tractable but not finitely tractable.
In
[
DSM+21
]
the second author of this paper and his students gave the first example of a finitely tractable

\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
for
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}
of size
2
that does not reduce to a tractable
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{C})
for any
\mathbf{C}
of size
\leq 2
.

We extend this result in Theorem
1
as follows:
For every prime
p
and every integer
n>1
we give
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}
of size
n
such that
\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
reduces to a
tractable
\mathrm{CSP}
over some
\mathbf{C}
of size
p
but not over any smaller structure.
All these structures have a single relation with arity
n^{p}
.

In our second family of examples in Theorem
3

we observe a similar behaviour even when restricting to structures with a single ternary relation:
For every prime
p\geq 7
we construct
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}
of size
p-1
such that
\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
reduces to a
tractable
\mathrm{CSP}
over some
\mathbf{C}
of size
p
but not over any smaller structure.

In contrast we show that if finite undirected graphs
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
sandwich some finite directed graph
\mathbf{C}
with
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{C})

tractable, then already
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{A})
or
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{B})
is tractable (Corollary
6
).
This answers Problem 1 of
[
DSM+21
]
.

More generally, if a finite smooth digraph
\mathbf{A}
and a digraph
\mathbf{B}
sandwich some finite digraph
\mathbf{C}
with
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{C})

tractable, then already
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{A})
or
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{B})
is tractable (Corollary
10
).
It remains open whether every finitely tractable
\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
for finite digraphs
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}
reduces to a tractable

\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{C})
for a digraph
\mathbf{C}
of size
\leq\max(|A|,|B|)
.

2.
Preliminaries

We will only define a bare minimum of notions necessary to make sense of this article. For a more detailed introduction to promise constraint satisfaction, see
[
BBKO21
]
. For more on digraph homomorphisms, see
[
HN04
]
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, write
[n]:=\{0,\dots,n-1\}
(note that
n\not\in[n]
).

Unless indicated otherwise, arithmetical operations, like
+
and
\cdot
, are considered over the integers
even if the input numbers come, say, from the set
[p]
. By
a\bmod p
we mean the operation of taking remainder that returns a number in
[p]
. When writing formulas,
\bmod
is evaluated after addition; in particular
\sum_{i}a_{i}\bmod p
means
\left(\sum_{i}a_{i}\right)\bmod p
where
\sum_{i}a_{i}
is evaluated in
\mathbb{Z}
.

A
relation

R
of arity
n
on a set
A
is a subset of
A^{n}
. A
signature
is a (finite, in this article)
list of relation symbols, each of which is associated with an arity.
A
relational structure
of a given signature
\Gamma
,
denoted by
\mathbf{A}
, consists of a universe
A
together with a family of relations, one for each relation symbol from
\Gamma
.
If
R
is an
n
-ary relation symbol from
\Gamma

and
\mathbf{A}
is a structure over
\Gamma
with universe
A
,
then
R^{\mathbf{A}}
is an
n
-ary relation on
A
. A relational structure is finite if its universe is finite.

We call a relational structure
\mathbf{A}
with universe
A

affine
if there exists a binary operation
+
,
a unary operation
-
and a constant
0
on
A
such that
\mathbb{A}:=(A,+,-,0)
is an abelian group and every relation

R^{\mathbf{A}}
of
\mathbf{A}
is closed under
x-y+z
; equivalently, every, say,
n
-ary relation
R^{\mathbf{A}}
is a coset of a subgroup
of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
. Then
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{A})
can be solved by linear algebra and is in the complexity class
{\sf{P}}
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and
\mathbf{A}
be a relational structure. By the
n
-th power of
\mathbf{A}
,
denoted by
\mathbf{A}^{n}
, we will understand the relational structure with the universe
A^{n}
(the set-theoretic
n
-th power)
and with the same signature as
\mathbf{A}
. If
R^{\mathbf{A}}
is an
m
-ary relation of
\mathbf{A}
, then
R^{\mathbf{A}^{n}}
is the relation on

A^{n}
that contains exactly those
m
-tuples
(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m})\in(A^{n})^{m}
such that for all
i=1,2,\dots,n

the
i
-th projection of the
m
-tuple,
(\pi_{i}(u_{1}),\dots,\pi_{i}(u_{m}))
, lies in
R^{\mathbf{A}}
. Here
\pi_{i}
is the mapping

A^{n}\to A
that returns the
i
-th component of its input.

Let
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
be two relational structures of the same signature
\Gamma
. A mapping
f\colon A\to B
is a

relational structure homomorphism
(or just homomorphism for short) if for each relation
R
from
\Gamma

(denote its arity by
n
) and each
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in R^{\mathbf{A}}
we have
(f(a_{1}),\dots,f(a_{n}))\in R^{\mathbf{B}}
.
An
n
-ary polymorphism
from
\mathbf{A}
to
\mathbf{B}
is a homomorphism
\mathbf{A}^{n}\to\mathbf{B}
.

A finite relational structure
\mathbf{A}
is a
core
if any homomorphism
\mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{A}
is a bijective mapping.
It is a well known fact that any finite relational structure has a unique (up to isomorphism) core.

Let
\Gamma
be a (finite) signature and let
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
be two relational structures with signature
\Gamma
such that there exists a homomorphism from
\mathbf{A}
to
\mathbf{B}
.

The promise constraint satisfaction problem
with fixed target structures
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
, denoted by
\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
, has as its instances finite relational structures
\mathbf{X}

of signature
\Gamma
(the relations of
\mathbf{X}
are specified by listing all tuples in them). An instance is

•

a “yes” instance if there exists a homomorphism
\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{A}
, and

•

a “no” instance if there does not exist any homomorphism
\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{B}
.

It is easy to show that since
\mathbf{A}
maps homomorphically to
\mathbf{B}
no instance can be both a “Yes” and a “No” instance. However, there might exist instances that are neither “Yes,” nor “No” instances. On these inputs an algorithm solving
\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
is allowed to do anything. A
constraint satisfaction problem with target structure
\mathbf{A}
, denoted by
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{A})
, is
\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{A})
.

As a reminder,
{\sf{P}}
is the class of all problems solvable in polynomial time and
{\sf{NP}}
is the class of all problems solvable in nondeterministic polynomial time.
Informally, we will call a (promise) constraint satisfaction problem tractable if there exists a polynomial time algorithm that correctly classifies each instance as a “yes” or “no” instance and we will assume that
{\sf{P}}\neq{\sf{NP}}
since otherwise all presented complexity results are trivial.

If
\mathbf{A}
is a relational structure and
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
is the core of
\mathbf{A}
, then it is easy to verify that the sets of “yes” and
“no” instances of
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{A})
and
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})
are the same. In particular, the computational complexity of
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{A})
only
depends on the core of
\mathbf{A}
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
be relational structures on the same universe but with possibly distinct signatures. We say that
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
is

pp-definable
from
\mathbf{A}
if each relation on
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
can be defined using a first order formula which only uses relations
of
\mathbf{A}
, equality, existential quantifiers and conjunction. It is well-known and easy to see that if
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
is pp-definable
from
\mathbf{A}
, then
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})
is reducible to
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{A})
.

An important special type of relational structures are
directed graphs (digraphs)
;
these are relational structures whose signature consists of one binary relation
E
, the edge relation.
The elements of the universe of a digraph are usually called the vertices of the digraph. A symmetric (or
undirected)
graph
is a digraph whose edge relation is symmetric.
A graph is
bipartite
if its vertices can be partitioned into two sets
P
,
Q
so that each edge connects
a vertex from
P
to some vertex from
Q
.
A digraph
\mathbf{G}
is
smooth

if for each of its vertices
v
there exist vertices
u
and
w
such that
(u,v)
and
(v,w)
both lie in
E^{\mathbf{G}}
.
A
directed cycle
of length
\ell
is a digraph with vertices
[\ell]
and edge set

\{(i,(i+1)\pmod{\ell})\;:\;i\in[\ell]\}
.

In the early 1990s, Hell and Nešetřil gave the following characterization of the complexity of CSP for symmetric graphs that we will later use:

{thmC}

[
[
HN90
, Theorem 1]
]
Let
\mathbf{G}
be a finite symmetric graph. If
\mathbf{G}
is bipartite, then
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{G})
is in
{\sf{P}}
. If
\mathbf{G}
is not bipartite, then
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{G})
is
{\sf{NP}}
-complete.

Barto, Kozik and Niven later generalized the above theorem to all smooth digraphs:

{thmC}
[
[
BKN09
]
]
Let
\mathbf{G}
be a finite smooth digraph that is a core. If
\mathbf{G}
is a disjoint union of directed cycles then
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{G})
is in
{\sf{P}}
. Otherwise,
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{G})
is
{\sf{NP}}
-complete.

Our main tool for showing that some
\mathrm{PCSP}
does not reduce to a tractable
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{C})
for a structure
\mathbf{C}
of
specified size is the following result by Barto and Kozik. Recall that a function

f\colon A^{n}\to B
is
cyclic
if for any
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in A
we have

f(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=f(a_{2},a_{3},\dots,a_{n},a_{1}).

{thmC}

[
[
BK12
, Theorem 4.1]
]
Let
\mathbf{C}
be finite relational structure, and let
p
be a prime larger than the size of the universe of
\mathbf{C}
.
If there exists no cyclic
p
-ary polymorphism
\mathbf{C}^{p}\to\mathbf{C}
, then
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{C})
is
{\sf{NP}}
-complete.

If a structure
\mathbf{C}
has a cyclic
p
-ary polymorphism
t
and is sandwiched by
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
via homomorphisms

\mathbf{A}\xrightarrow{g}\mathbf{C}\xrightarrow{h}\mathbf{B}
, then the composition
f\colon A^{p}\to B
defined by

f(x_{1},\dots,x_{p}):=h(t(g(x_{1}),\dots,g(x_{p})))

is a cyclic
p
-ary polymorphism from
\mathbf{A}
to
\mathbf{B}
.
We will use the contrapositive of this statement together with Theorem
2
in the following form:
if for some prime
p
there is no cyclic
p
-ary polymorphism from
\mathbf{A}
to
\mathbf{B}
, then
\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
does not reduce
to a tractable
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{C})
for any
\mathbf{C}
of size less than
p
.

3.
Big affine sandwiches

For every
n>1
and every prime
p
we give structures
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}
of size
n
with a single
n^{p}
-ary
relation such that
\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
reduces to a tractable
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{C})
for some sandwiched
\mathbf{C}
of size
p
but
not to a tractable
\mathrm{CSP}
over any smaller sandwiched structure.
For
n=2
and
p=3
such an example was given in
[
DSM+21
]
.

Theorem 1
.

For
n,p>1
,
let
R
be a relation symbol of arity
n^{p}
, and let

\mathbf{A}=([n],R^{\mathbf{A}})
,
\mathbf{B}=([n],R^{\mathbf{B}}),\mathbf{C}=([p],R^{\mathbf{C}})
be relational structures with

\displaystyle R^{\mathbf{A}}

\displaystyle=\{f\colon[n]^{p}\to[n]\;:\;f\text{ is a projection}\},

\displaystyle R^{\mathbf{B}}

\displaystyle=\{f\colon[n]^{p}\to[n]\;:\;f\text{ is not cyclic}\},

\displaystyle R^{\mathbf{C}}

\displaystyle=\left\{f\colon[n]^{p}\to[p],\ (x_{1},\dots,x_{p})\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i}x_{i}\bmod p\;:\;a_{1},\dots,a_{p}\in[p],\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i}\bmod p=1\right\}.

Then

(1)

\mathbf{C}
is affine (hence
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{C})
is in
{\sf{P}}
)
and is sandwiched by
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
via the homomorphisms
\mathbf{A}\xrightarrow{g}\mathbf{C}\xrightarrow{h}\mathbf{B}

where
g\colon[n]\to[p],\ x\mapsto x\bmod p
, and
h\colon[p]\to[n],\ x\mapsto x\bmod n
.

(2)

If
p
is prime and
\mathbf{D}
is a structure with
|D|<p
that is sandwiched by
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
, then
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{D})
is
{\sf{NP}}
-complete.

(3)

\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
is in
{\sf{P}}
.
If
p>2
, then
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{A})
is
{\sf{NP}}
-complete; else it is in
{\sf{P}}
.
If
(n,p)\neq(2,2)
, then
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{B})
is
{\sf{NP}}
-complete; else it is in
{\sf{P}}
.

The main technical difficulty in proving Theorem
1
is to show that
h\colon\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{B}
is a homomorphism.
For this we first establish several properties of cyclic functions of a form that is slightly more general than of those
in
h(R^{\mathbf{C}})
in the next lemma.

For a rational number
q
let
\lfloor q\rfloor
denote the greatest integer less than or equal to
q
.

Lemma 2
.

For
n,p>1
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{p}\in[p]
, let

f\colon[n]^{p}\to[n],\ (x_{1},\dots,x_{p})\mapsto\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i}x_{i}\bmod p\right)\bmod n,

be cyclic. Then

(1)

a_{1},\dots,a_{p}
are all congruent modulo
n
.

(2)

If
n
does not divide
p
, then for any
x_{1},\dots,x_{p}\in[n]
we have

\left\lfloor\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i}x_{i}}{p}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{p}x_{j}}{p^{2}}\right\rfloor.

(3)

f
is symmetric, i.e., invariant under all permutations of its inputs.

(4)

\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i}\bmod p\neq 1
.

Proof 3.1
.

For (
1
) note that
f(1,0\dots,0)=\dots=f(0,\dots,0,1)
as
f
is cyclic.
Now observe that when the
1
is at the
i
-th position,
then
f(0,\dots,0,1,0,\dots,0)=a_{i}\bmod n
. Putting these two facts together gives us that all
a_{i}
’s are the same modulo
n
.

For (
2
) assume that
n
does not divide
p
.
We show that the integer part of the quotient obtained by dividing
\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i}x_{i}
by
p
depends
only on the
weight

w:=\sum_{i=1}^{p}x_{i}
of
(x_{1},\dots,x_{p})\in[n]^{p}
by induction on
w
.

The base case for
\sum_{i=1}^{p}x_{i}=0
is immediate.
Next take
x_{1},\dots,x_{p}\in[n]
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{p}x_{i}=w>0
. Without loss of generality assume

x_{1}\neq 0
.
Let
\sigma
be the cyclic permutation
\sigma:=(1,\dots,p)
, and
let
k:=\left\lfloor\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i}(w-1)}{p^{2}}\right\rfloor
. Let
y_{1}=x_{1}-1
and
y_{i}=x_{i}
for
i=2,3,\dots,p
. Since
\sum_{i=1}^{p}y_{i}=w-1
,
the induction hypothesis yields

\left\lfloor\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i}y_{i}}{p}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{p}y_{j}}{p^{2}}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i}(w-1)}{p^{2}}\right\rfloor=k.

The total weight will not change if we permute the
y_{i}
’s, so for any
s\in[p]
we also get

\left\lfloor\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i}y_{\sigma^{s}(i)}}{p}\right\rfloor=k
. In particular,
\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i}y_{\sigma^{s}(i)}-kp
lies in
[p]
for all
s
.

Going back from
y_{i}
’s to
x_{i}
’s, we get

\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i}x_{\sigma^{s}(i)}-a_{\sigma^{-s}(1)}-kp=\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i}y_{\sigma^{s}(i)}-kp\in[p].

Given that
a_{\sigma^{-s}(1)}\in[p]

.

Lemma 4
.

Let
g\colon\mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{C}
be a homomorphism where
\mathbf{A}
is symmetric and
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{C})
is in
{\sf{P}}
.
Then
g
maps
\mathbf{A}
into
\overline{\mathbf{C}}
(the symmetric part of
\mathbf{C}
) and
\mathrm{CSP}(\overline{\mathbf{C}})
is in
{\sf{P}}
.

Proof 4.1
.

Since
\mathbf{A}
is symmetric, so is its homomorphic image
g(\mathbf{A})
in
\mathbf{C}
.
Hence
g(\mathbf{A})
is a substructure of
\overline{\mathbf{C}}
.
Since
\overline{\mathbf{C}}
is pp-definable from
\mathbf{C}
,
\mathrm{CSP}(\overline{\mathbf{C}})
reduces to
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{C})
and
so is in
{\sf{P}}
.

Hence when searching for a tractable
\mathbf{C}
sandwiched by symmetric
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
, we may restrict ourselves to
searching for symmetric structures
\mathbf{C}
.

As a consequence we can answer
[
DSM+21
, Problem 1]
in the negative:
Are there some finite graphs
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}
such that
\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
is finitely tractable but only for a sandwiched digraph

\mathbf{C}
that is strictly bigger than
\max(|A|,|B|)
?

Corollary 5
.

If there exists a homomorphism from a finite (undirected)
graph
\mathbf{A}
into a finite digraph
\mathbf{C}
without loops
such that
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{C})
is in
{\sf{P}}
, then

\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{A})
is in
{\sf{P}}
.

Proof 4.2
.

Assume
{\sf{P}}\neq{\sf{NP}}
(clearly the assertion is trivial otherwise).
By Lemma
4
we may assume that
\mathbf{C}
is an undirected graph (without loops).
Since
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{C})
is in
{\sf{P}}
,
\mathbf{C}
is bipartite by Theorem
2
.
From the homomorphism
\mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{C}
we see that
\mathbf{A}
is bipartite as well.
Hence
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{A})
is in
{\sf{P}}
.

In particular finite tractability trivializes for
\mathrm{PCSP}
on graphs.

Corollary 6
.

If
\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
for finite graphs
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}
is finitely tractable, then
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{A})
or
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{B})
is tractable.

This also settles a special instance of the following question which remains open in general:

Problem 7

(Libor Barto, personal communication)
.

Are there some finite symmetric
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}
such that
\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
is finitely
tractable but only for a sandwiched
\mathbf{C}
that is strictly bigger than
\max(|A|,|B|)
?

5.
Digraphs

In this section we move from symmetric graphs to directed graphs. The situation here is more complicated.
In particular finite tractability does not trivialize for
\mathrm{PCSP}
on digraphs like it does on symmetric graphs
by Corollary
6
.
The following example was communicated to us by Jakub Bulín: Let
\mathbf{A}
be a finite tree with
{\sf{NP}}
-complete CSP,
let
\mathbf{C},\mathbf{B}
be the complete digraphs without loops on
2,3
vertices, respectively.
Clearly there exist homomorphisms
\mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{B}
and
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{C})
is tractable.
Hence
\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
is finitely tractable but
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}
have
{\sf{NP}}
-complete CSPs.

The following generalization of
[
DSM+21
, Problem 1]
is still open:

Problem 8
.

Are there some finite digraphs
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}
such that
\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
is finitely
tractable but only for a sandwiched
\mathbf{C}
that is strictly bigger than
\max(|A|,|B|)
?

Brakensiek and Guruswami showed that every
\mathrm{PCSP}
is polynomial time equivalent to
\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
for
some digraphs
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}

[
BG17
, Theorem 6.10]
. So our examples in Theorems
1

and
3
can be translated to digraphs
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{C}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{B}^{\prime}

but it is unclear whether the obtained
\mathbf{C}^{\prime}
is still the smallest sandwiched structure with tractable
\mathrm{CSP}
.

Using the classification of finite smooth digraphs with tractable
\mathrm{CSP}
in Theorem
2
,
we can get a partial answer that also generalizes Corollary
5
.

Theorem 9
.

If there exists a homomorphism from a finite smooth digraph
\mathbf{A}
into a finite digraph
\mathbf{C}
without loops
such that
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{C})
is in
{\sf{P}}
, then
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{A})
is in
{\sf{P}}
.

Proof 5.1
.

Assume
{\sf{P}}\neq{\sf{NP}}
(clearly the assertion is trivial otherwise).
Let
\mathbf{A}
be a finite smooth digraph, and let
\mathbf{C}
be a minimal digraph without loops and with tractable

\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{C})
for which there exists a homomorphism
g\colon\mathbf{A}\to\mathbf{C}
.
We proceed by a series of claims:

{clm}

The digraph
\mathbf{C}
is smooth.

Proof 5.2
.

Let
n=|C|
and let
E^{\mathbf{C}}
denote the edge relation of
\mathbf{C}
.
By the pigeonhole principle the set
V
of vertices of
\mathbf{C}
that induces the maximal smooth subgraph of
\mathbf{C}
is pp-definable as

v\in V
if and only if

\displaystyle\exists x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\;:\;(x_{1},x_{2})\in E^{\mathbf{C}}\wedge(x_{2},x_{3})\in E^{\mathbf{C}}\wedge\dots\wedge(x_{n-1},x_{n})\in E^{\mathbf{C}}

\displaystyle{}\wedge(x_{n},v)\in E^{\mathbf{C}}\wedge(v,y_{1})\in E^{\mathbf{C}}\wedge(y_{1},y_{2})\in E^{\mathbf{C}}\wedge\dots\wedge(y_{n-1},y_{n})\in E^{\mathbf{C}}.

Then the induced subgraph
\mathbf{C}^{\prime}:=(V,E^{\mathbf{C}}|_{V\times V})
is smooth and pp-definable from
\mathbf{C}
.
Further
g(\mathbf{A})
is a subgraph of
\mathbf{C}^{\prime}
because
g(\mathbf{A})
is a smooth subgraph of
\mathbf{C}
.

Since
\mathbf{C}^{\prime}
is pp-definable from
\mathbf{C}
,
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{C}^{\prime})
reduces to
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{C})
. Since the latter is tractable,
so is the former. Hence
\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{C}^{\prime}
by the minimality of
\mathbf{C}
and the claim is proved.

{clm}

The digraph
\mathbf{C}
is a core.

Proof 5.3
.

Immediate from the minimality of
\mathbf{C}
.

{clm}

\mathbf{C}
is a disjoint union of directed cycles.

Proof 5.4
.

Since
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{C})
is in
{\sf{P}}
and
\mathbf{C}
a core, this follows from Theorem
2
.

{clm}

The graph
g(\mathbf{A})
is equal to
\mathbf{C}
.

Proof 5.5
.

Since
\mathbf{A}
is smooth,
g(\mathbf{A})
is a smooth subgraph of
\mathbf{C}
. Now,
\mathbf{C}
is a disjoint union of directed cycles by Claim
5.1
. Since
g(\mathbf{A})
is a smooth subgraph of a disjoint union of directed cycles, we get that
g(\mathbf{A})

itself is a disjoint union of some cycles of
\mathbf{C}
. Claim
5.1
then follows from the minimality of
\mathbf{C}
.

{clm}

The digraph
\mathbf{C}
is the core of
\mathbf{A}
.

Proof 5.6
.

Let
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
be a substructure of
\mathbf{A}
that is isomorphic to the core of
\mathbf{A}
.
Then
g(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})=\mathbf{C}
follows as in the proof of Claim
5.1
. Hence
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\cong\mathbf{C}
.

Since
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{A})
is equivalent to the
\mathrm{CSP}
of its core
\mathbf{C}
and
\mathbf{C}
is a disjoint union of cycles (for whose
CSP there is a straightforward polynomial time algorithm), we get that
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{A})
is in
{\sf{P}}
,
concluding the proof.

As for undirected graphs we obtain the following consequence:

Corollary 10
.

If
\mathrm{PCSP}(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B})
for finite digraphs
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}
is finitely tractable and
\mathbf{A}
is smooth,
then
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{A})
or
\mathrm{CSP}(\mathbf{B})
is tractable.

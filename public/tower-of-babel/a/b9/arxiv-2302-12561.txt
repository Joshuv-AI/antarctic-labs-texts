Inducing Schemes with Finite Weighted Complexity
Jianyu Chen, Fang Wang, Hong-Kun Zhang

Abstract
In this paper, we consider a Borel measurable map of a compact metric space which admits an inducing scheme. Under the finite weighted complexity condition, we establish a thermodynamic formalism for a parameter family of potentials φ + t ​ ψ \varphi+t\psi in an interval containing t = 0 t=0 . Furthermore, if there is a generating partition compatible to the inducing scheme, we show that all ergodic invariant measures with sufficiently large pressure are liftable. Keywords: Inducing schemes, Weighted Complexity, Equilibrium measures.

Introduction

The main goal in the thermodynamic formalism is to study
the
equilibrium measures
of a dynamical system
f:X\to X

for a
potential function

\varphi:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
, i.e.,
the measures for which the supremum

\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f,X)}P_{\mu}(\varphi)

is attained, where
\mathcal{M}(f,X)
is the class of all
f
-invariant ergodic Borel probability measure on
X
, and

P_{\mu}(\varphi)
is
the
free energy
given by

P_{\mu}(\varphi):=h_{\mu}(f)+\int\varphi d\mu.

(1.1)

Here
h_{\mu}(f)
denotes the Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy of the system
(f,\mu)
.

The classical works of Sinai, Ruelle and Bowen (see e.g.
[
22
,
21
,
2
]
) demonstrated
the existence, uniqueness and ergodic properties of equilibrium measures for
uniformly hyperbolic systems.
Great efforts have later been made for systems
beyond uniform hyperbolicity,
using various
extension/inducing
techniques
(see
e.g.
[
13
,
5
,
14
,
15
,
6
,
3
,
28
,
29
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
4
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
).
Using principle results obtained by Sarig (see e.g.
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
)
on the thermodynamic formalism for the countable Markov shifts,
Pesin, Senti and Zhang
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
]
developed
a version of the
inducing scheme
method,
which is applicable to some multimodal interval maps, the
Young’s diffeomorphisms, the Hénon family and the Katok map.

In this paper, we consider a Borel measurable map
f:X\to X

of a compact metric space, possibly with discontinuities and singularities.
We assume that the map
f

admits an
inducing scheme

\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}

satisfying Conditions
(I1)-(I4)
(see Definition
1
),
which is either of hyperbolic type or of expanding type.
The inducing scheme
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}

might not have
finite complexity
(see (
2.5
)),
in particular, there may be infinitely many blocks with the same inducing time.
In such situation, we could only expect
some
finite weighted complexity
condition (see Definition
2
)
for particular weight functions.

In the context of continuous maps admitting inducing schemes,
Pesin, Senti and Zhang
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
]
have established a thermodynamic formalism
with respect to a class of
nice
potential functions,
i.e., functions satisfying the verifiable conditions
(P1)-(P4)
in Section 4 of
[
20
]

(see Definition
3
for the precise statements).
In this paper, we adapt their results to a measurable map
f:X\to X
with discontinuities and singularities,
which is assumed to have finite
variational entropy
(see (
2.1
)).
Once an inducing scheme
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}
is chosen,
we denote
Y
the forward invariant subset associated with
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}
(see (

)).
The potential functions under consideration is a one parameter family

, where

is a

and

is a

Under Condition

,

.
Applying a result by Shahidi and Zelerowicz

The precise statements of our first main result is as follows.

Theorem A
.

Let
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}
be an inducing scheme.
Assume that
\varphi
is a strongly nice potential
and
\psi
is a regular potential.
Furthermore, we assume that

(H)

there are
\underline{t}_{0}<0<\overline{t}_{0}
such that the inducing scheme
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}

has finite
\overline{\varphi+t\psi}
-complexity (see Definition
2
)
at
t=\underline{t}_{0}
and
t=\overline{t}_{0}
,
where
\overline{\varphi+t\psi}
is the induced potential of
\varphi+t\psi

(see (
2.11
)).

Then there exist
\underline{t}<0<\overline{t}
such that the following hold:

(1)

for every
t\in(\underline{t},\overline{t})
,
there exists a unique equilibrium measure
\mu_{t}

for the potential
\varphi+t\psi

in the class
\mathcal{M}_{L}(f,Y)
of all liftable measures (see (
2.8
));

(2)

if the inducing scheme
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}
satisfies the aperiodic condition (
2.3
),
then the measure
\mu_{t}
is mixing and in fact Bernoulli. Moreover,
\mu_{t}

has exponential decay of correlations and satisfies the
Central Limit Theorem (with respect to a class of functions
which contains all bounded Hölder continuous functions on
Y
).

(3)

the function
\displaystyle t\mapsto P_{L}(\varphi+t\psi)

is finite and real analytic in
(\underline{t},\overline{t})
, where
P_{L}(\cdot)
is the
variational liftable pressure given by (
2.9

We remark that
\underline{t}

\overline{t}
are chosen by the explicit formula in (

Our second result deals with the

over the inducing schemes,

is

\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{L}(f,Y)

For continuous maps admitting inducing schemes,

[

20

A crucial assumption therein is the finite complexity for non-first-return inducing times

For systems with nasty discontinuities and singularities, one could only impose

below.
Together with Condition

compatible
to the inducing scheme (see Definition

Our second main result is stated as follows.

Theorem B
.

Let
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}
be an inducing scheme.
We assume that

(L1)

the map
f:X\to X
has a generating partition
\mathcal{P}
, which is compatible to
the inducing scheme
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}
(see Definition
4
);

(L2)

the inducing scheme
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}
has finite
\overline{\varphi}
-complexity (see Definition
2
),
for some potential function
\varphi:X\to{\mathbb{R}}

Then any ergodic measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f,X)

with
\mu(W)>0
and

\mathcal{K}(\overline{\varphi})<P_{\mu}(\varphi)<\infty
is liftable,
where
\mathcal{K}(\overline{\varphi})
and

are given by
Definition
2
and (
1.1
) respectively.

An immediate corollary of
Theorem A and Theorem B is the following.

Corollary C
.

Let
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}
be an inducing scheme with inducing domain
W
.
Assume that
\varphi
is a strongly nice potential
and
\psi
is a regular potential.
If Conditions
(H)
and
(L1)
hold, then
the measure
\mu_{t}
obtained in Theorem A is in fact
the unique equilibrium measure for the potential
\varphi+t\psi

in the class

\mathcal{M}^{W}(f,X):=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f,X):\mu(W)>0\right\}.

(1.2)

Therefore,
the function

\displaystyle t\mapsto P^{W}(\varphi+t\psi)

is finite and real analytic in
(\underline{t},\overline{t})
, where

P^{W}(\cdot)
is the
variational pressure
given by (
2.10
).

2
Inducing Schemes

To work on the thermodynamics for continuous maps of compact metric spaces,
Pesin, Senti and Zhang developed an inducing scheme method in

[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
]
.
Our goal is to adapt this general framework to systems with discontinuities and singularities,
for which the finite complexity condition (see (
2.5
)) usually fails. To this end,
we first introduce the basic notations as follows.

Let
f:X\to X
be a measurable map of a compact metric space,
which is allowed to have discontinuities.
If further
X
is a smooth manifold,
there may be singularity points at which
f
is continuous but
Df
does not exist.
We assume that
f
has finite
variational entropy
, that is,

h(f):=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f,X)}h_{\mu}(f)<\infty,

(2.1)

where
\mathcal{M}(f,X)
is the set of all
f
-invariant ergodic Borel probability measures on
X

and
h_{\mu}(f)
is the Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy of the system
(f,\mu)
.

The definition of inducing schemes is given as follows.

Definition 1
.

Given a countable collection of disjoint Borel subsets
\mathcal{S}=\{J_{a}\}_{a\in S}
and
a positive integer value function
\tau:S\to{\mathbb{N}}
,
we say that
f
admits an
inducing scheme
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}
of hyperbolic type
, with
the
inducing domain

W:=\bigcup_{a\in S}J_{a}
and
the
inducing time

\tau:X\to{\mathbb{N}}
defined by

\displaystyle\tau(x):=\begin{cases}\tau(a),&x\in J_{a},\\
0,&x\not\in W,\end{cases}

provided the following conditions
(I1)-(I4)
hold:

(I1)

For any
a\in S
, we have

f^{\tau(a)}(J_{a})\subset W\ \ \text{and}\ \ \bigcup_{a\in S}f^{\tau(a)}(J_{a})=W.

(2.2)

Moreover, let
F:W\to W
be the
induced map
defined by

F|J_{a}=f^{\tau(a)}|J_{a}\ \ \text{for any}\ a\in S,

and assume that

F|J_{a}
can be extended to a homeomorphism of a neighborhood of
the closure
\overline{J_{a}}
.

(I2)

For every bi-infinite sequence
\underline{a}=(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\in S^{\mathbb{Z}}
, there exists a unique
sequence
\underline{x}=(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
with coordinates
x_{n}=x_{n}(\underline{a})
such that

(a)

\displaystyle x_{n}\in\overline{J_{a_{n}}}
and

\displaystyle f^{\tau(J_{a_{n}})}(x_{n})=x_{n+1}
;

(b)

if
\displaystyle x_{n}(\underline{a})=x_{n}(\underline{b})
for all
n\leq 0
, then
\underline{a}=\underline{b}
.

Denote by
\sigma:S^{\mathbb{Z}}\to S^{\mathbb{Z}}
the full left shift and let

\check{S}:=\left\{\underline{a}\in S^{\mathbb{Z}}:\ x_{n}(\underline{a})\in J_{a_{n}}\ \text{for all}\ n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}.

(I3)

The set
S^{\mathbb{Z}}\backslash\check{S}
supports no ergodic
\sigma
-invariant
measure which gives positive mass to any open subset.

(I4)

The induced map
F
has at least one periodic orbit in
W
.

Remark 2.1
.

We say
f
admits an
inducing scheme
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}
of expanding type

if Conditions
(I1)-(I4)
hold, with the following changes:

(1)

the second equation of (
2.2
) in
(I1)
is replaced by
f^{\tau(a)}(J_{a})=W
;

(2)

the two-sided infinite sequence in
S^{\mathbb{Z}}
is replaced by one-sided infinite sequence in
S^{{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
,
and Condition
(I2)
(b) is removed.

In this paper, we shall only state and prove results for inducing schemes of hyperbolic type,
as all the results for the expanding type can be obtained in a similar fashion,
thereby we omit the term “of hyperbolic type” when we mention inducing schemes.

We say that an inducing scheme
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}
satisfies the
aperiodicity
condition if

\displaystyle gcd\left\{\tau(a):a\in S\right\}=1.

(2.3)

In what follows, we shall fix an inducing scheme
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}
and
explain the definitions of weighted complexity,
liftable measures and regular/nice potentials,
and then we state a main theorem in
[
18
,
20
]
concerning
on the thermodynamics of the inducing scheme
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}
.

2.1
Weighted Complexity

For any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we set

S_{n}:=\{a\in S:\ \tau(a)=n\}.

(2.4)

We say that the inducing scheme
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}
has
finite complexity
if

\#S_{n}<\infty\ \text{for all}\ n\geq 1,\ \text{and}\ \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\#S_{n}<\infty.

(2.5)

Due to the presence of discontinuities and singularities,
the finite complexity condition (
2.5
) usually fails.
In fact, when
X
is a smooth manifold and
Df
blows up near the singularity points,
it is very likely that
\#S_{n}=\infty
for infinitely many
n
’s,
or every
\#S_{n}
is finite but grows super-exponentially fast.

Nevertheless, when we deal with some specific class of potentials,
it would be useful to introduce the following concept.

Definition 2
.

Given a weight function
\omega:W\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
the inducing scheme
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}
is said to have

finite
\omega
-complexity
if

(a)

\displaystyle U_{n}(\omega):=\sum_{a\in S_{n}}\sup_{x\in J_{a}}\exp{(\omega(x))}<\infty
for all
n\geq 1
;

(b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(\omega):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log U_{n}(\omega)<\infty.

We shall call
\mathcal{K}(\omega)
the
complexity function
of
\omega
.

It is clear that the finite complexity condition (
2.5
)
is a special case of the finite weighted complexity condition
with the weight function
\omega\equiv 0
.

2.2
Pressure and Equilibrium Measures

The
forward invariant hull
of the inducing domain
W
is defined by

Y:=\bigcup_{k=0}^{\infty}f^{k}(W)=\left\{f^{n}(x):\ x\in W,\ 0\leq n<\tau(x)\right\}.

(2.6)

Denote the set of
f
-invariant ergodic Borel probability measures on
X
(or
Y
) by

\mathcal{M}(f,X)
(or
\mathcal{M}(f,Y)
),
and denote the set of
F
-invariant ergodic Borel probability measures on
W
by
\mathcal{M}(F,W)
.
For any
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(F,W)
, let

Q_{\nu}:=\int_{W}\tau\ d\nu.

(2.7)

If
Q_{\nu}<\infty
, then
\nu
is
liftable
to a measure
\mu=\mathcal{L}(\nu)\in\mathcal{M}(f,Y)
given by

\mu(E):=\frac{1}{Q_{\nu}}\sum_{a\in S}\sum^{\tau(a)-1}_{k=0}\nu(f^{-k}E\cap J_{a})

for any Borel subset
E\subset Y
.
We denote the class of liftable measures by

\mathcal{M}_{L}(f,Y):=\left\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f,Y):\ \text{there is}\ \nu\in\mathcal{M}(F,W)\ \text{with}\ \mathcal{L}(\nu)=\mu\right\}.

(2.8)

The

variational liftable pressure
of a function
\varphi:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is defined by

P_{L}(\varphi):=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{L}(f,Y)}P_{\mu}(\varphi),

(2.9)

where
P_{\mu}(\varphi)
is the
pressure of
\varphi
with respect to
\mu
given by (
1.1
).
Condition
(I4)
implies that
\mathcal{M}_{L}(f,Y)\neq\emptyset

and
P_{L}(\varphi)>-\infty
. Note that it is possible that

P_{L}(\varphi)=+\infty
.
Also,
P_{L}(\varphi-q)=P_{L}(\varphi)-q
for any
q\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

A measure

\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{L}(f,Y)
is called an
equilibrium measure
of
\varphi

(in the class of
\mathcal{M}_{L}(f,Y)
of liftable measures)
if it attains the supremum of (
2.9
).
Note that the potentials
\varphi-q
and
\varphi
share the
same equilibrium measures for any
q\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

We remark that the
f
-invariant hull
Y
,
the measure class
\mathcal{M}_{L}(f,Y)
and the variational liftable pressure
P_{L}(\varphi)

depend on the choice of the inducing schemes
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}
.
A more independent quantity is the
variational pressure
given by

P^{W}(\varphi):=\sup\left\{P_{\mu}(\varphi):\ \mu\in\mathcal{M}^{W}(f,X)\ \text{with}\ \int|\varphi|d\mu<\infty\right\},

(2.10)

where the class
\mathcal{M}^{W}(f,X)
of measures is given by (
1.2
).
Notice that the only requirement on
\mathcal{M}^{W}(f,X)

is to give positive mass on the inducing domain
W
.

2.3
Regular and Nice Potentials

Condition
(I2)
allows one to define the
coding map

\pi:S^{\mathbb{Z}}\to\widehat{W}:=\bigcup_{a\in S}\overline{J_{a}}
by

\pi(\underline{a}):=x_{0}(\underline{a})=\bigcap_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}F^{-n}\overline{J_{a_{n}}}

for every bi-infinite sequence
\underline{a}=(a_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\in S^{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Note that
\pi
is a semi-conjugacy from
(S^{\mathbb{Z}},\sigma)
to
(\widehat{W},F)
,
i.e.,
\pi\circ\sigma=F\circ\pi
,
and it is one-to-one on
\check{S}
.

Let
\varphi:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a potential function.
In below, we list some verifiable conditions
that were introduced in
[
18
,
20
]
.

(P1)

the
induced potential

\overline{\varphi}:W\to{\mathbb{R}}
given by

\overline{\varphi}(x):=\sum^{\tau(x)-1}_{k=0}\varphi(f^{k}(x))

(2.11)

can be extended
by continuity to a function on
\overline{J_{a}}
for any
a\in S
;

(P2)

the function
\Phi=\overline{\varphi}\circ\pi
is a
locally Hölder continuous
function
on
S^{\mathbb{Z}}
, i.e., there are
H>0
and
0<r<1
such that for all
n\geq 1
,

V_{n}(\Phi):=\sup_{[\underline{c}]\in\mathcal{C}_{-n+1}^{n-1}}\ \sup_{\underline{a},\underline{a}^{\prime}\in[\underline{c}]}\left\{\left|\Phi(\underline{a})-\Phi(\underline{a}^{\prime})\right|\right\}\leq Hr^{n},

where

\mathcal{C}_{n}^{m}
, with integers
n\leq m
, is the collection of cylinders of the form

[\underline{c}]=[c_{n}\dots c_{m}]:=\left\{\underline{a}\in S^{\mathbb{Z}}:\ a_{k}=c_{k},\ k=n,\dots,m\right\}.

(P3)

the following summability condition holds:

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in S}\sup_{x\in J_{a}}\exp\overline{\varphi}(x)<\infty.

By Theorem 4.5 in
[
18
]
, if
\varphi
satisfies
(P1)-(P3)
,
then
P_{L}(\varphi)<\infty
.
We then define the
normalized induced potential

\varphi^{+}:W\to{\mathbb{R}}

by

\varphi^{+}:=\overline{\varphi-P_{L}(\varphi)}=\overline{\varphi}-P_{L}(\varphi)\tau.

(P4)

there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in S}\tau(a)\sup_{x\in J_{a}}\exp(\varphi^{+}(x)+\varepsilon\tau(x))<\infty.

Again by Theorem 4.5 in
[
18
]
, if
\varphi
satisfies
(P1)-(P4)
,
the normalized induced potential
\varphi^{+}
has zero topological pressure, and
there is a unique
F
-invariant equilibrium measure
\nu_{\varphi^{+}}
for
\varphi^{+}

among all the measures in
\mathcal{M}(F,W)
. Moreover,

\nu_{\varphi^{+}}
has the
Gibbs property
, i.e.,
there exists a constant
K>0
such that for any
n\geq 1
,
any cylinder
[\underline{c}]\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{n-1}
and any
x\in J_{\underline{c}}:=\pi[\underline{c}]
,
we have

K^{-1}\leq\dfrac{\nu_{\varphi^{+}}(J_{\underline{c}})}{\exp\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varphi^{+}(F^{k}(x))\right)}\leq K.

In particular, for any
a\in S
and any
x\in J_{a}
, we have

K^{-1}\leq\dfrac{\nu_{\varphi^{+}}(J_{a})}{\exp\left(\varphi^{+}(x)\right)}\leq K.

(2.12)

(P5)

We say that
\nu_{\varphi^{+}}

has
exponential tail
, if
there are
C>0
and
\theta\in(0,1)
such that
for any
n\geq 1
,

\nu_{\varphi^{+}}(\{x\in W:\ \tau(x)\geq n\})\leq C\theta^{n}.

We now introduce the following definitions for potential functions.

Definition 3
.

We say that a function
\varphi:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
is

(1)

regular
if
\varphi
satisfies Conditions
(P1)(P2)
;

(2)

nice
if
\varphi
satisfies Conditions
(P1)-(P4)
;

(3)

strongly nice
if
\varphi
satisfies Conditions
(P1)-(P5)
.

We remark that the above properties depend on the choice of the inducing scheme.
It is possible that a function is regular with respect to one inducing scheme but not regular
with respect to some others.
We also stress that the induced potential

\overline{\varphi}
may be unbounded even if
\varphi
is a regular potential.

2.4
Thermodynamics of Inducing Schemes

Let us describe some statistical properties of
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f,X)
.
We say that
\mu
has
exponential decay of correlations

with respect to a class
\mathcal{H}
of functions if there exists
\Theta\in(0,1)

such that for any
h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathcal{H}
and any
n\geq 1
,

\left|\int h_{1}\cdot h_{2}\circ f^{n}d\mu-\int h_{1}d\mu\int h_{2}d\mu\right|\leq K\Theta^{n},

for some
K=K(h_{1},h_{2})>0
.
We say that
\mu
satisfies the
Central Limit Theorem
with respect to the class
\mathcal{H}

if for any
h\in\mathcal{H}
, which is not cohomologous to a constant,
there exists
\sigma>0
such that

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(h\circ f^{k}-\int hd\mu\right)\longrightarrow N(0,\sigma^{2})\ \ \text{in law},

where
N(0,\sigma^{2})
denotes the normal distribution.

We now state some main results by Pesin, Senti and Zhang
[
18
,
20
]

on the thermodynamic formalism of inducing schemes.
We also include the result on the Bernoulli property established by Shahidi and Zelerowicz
[
26
]
.
Although we are dealing with maps with discontinuities and singularities in this paper,
the proof of the following theorem can still be carried out
by the same arguments.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\{S,\tau\}
be an inducing scheme, and
assume that the potential function
\varphi
is nice.
Then

(1)

there exists a unique equilibrium measure
\mu_{\varphi}

for
\varphi
among all liftable measures in
\mathcal{M}_{L}(f,Y)
;

(2)

if the inducing scheme
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}
satisfies the aperiodic condition (
2.3
),
then the meausre
\mu_{\varphi}
is mixing and in fact Bernoulli.
Furthermore, if

\nu_{\varphi^{+}}=\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\mu_{\varphi})
has exponential tail,
then
\mu_{\varphi}
has exponential decay of correlations and satisfies the
Central Limit Theorem (with respect to a class of functions
which contains all bounded Hölder continuous functions on
Y
).

Recall that
P_{L}(\cdot)
is the variational lifted pressure given by (
2.9
).
The following theorem was established by Shahidi and Zelerowicz
[
26
]
,
which provides a criterion for the analyticity of the variational lifted pressure.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
be two potential functions on
X
.
Assume there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that
\varphi_{1}+s\varphi_{2}
is nice
for all
|s|<\varepsilon_{0}
.

Definition 4

of

[

be the partition given by

(L1)

\mathcal{P}
is a generating partition
which is compatible to the inducing scheme
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}
.
Note that
\mathcal{P}
is
generating
means
the smallest
\sigma
-algebra containing
\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{P}_{n}

(or
\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{n}
if

is invertible)
is the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
X
, where

\mathcal{P}_{n}:=\bigvee_{k=0}^{n}f^{-k}\mathcal{P}
(or
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{n}:=\bigvee_{k=-n}^{n}f^{-k}\mathcal{P}

Let
\varphi:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
be the potential given by Condition
(L2)

the inducing scheme
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}
has finite
\overline{\varphi}
-complexity,
and thus the complexity function
\mathcal{K}(\overline{\varphi})
is finite (see Definition
2
).

Theorem B claims that under Conditions
(L1)(L2)

all ergodic measures
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f,X)
are liftable, i.e.,
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{L}(f,Y)

•

\mu
gives positive weight to the base, i.e.,
\mu(W)>0

•

\mu
has sufficiently large but finite pressure, i.e.,

\mathcal{K}(\overline{\varphi})<P_{\mu}(\varphi)<\infty

Recall that
P_{\mu}(\varphi)
is the pressure of
\varphi
with respect to
\mu
(see (

)).
As we assume that

has finite variational entropy (see (

)),
the condition that

\displaystyle\int|\varphi|d\mu<\infty
.
Hence

\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f,X)
such that
\varphi
is
\mu
-integrable.

To effectively estimate the pressure
P_{\mu}(\varphi)
from above,
we introduce the following Caratheodory-Pesin type quantity.
Given an integer
m\geq 1
,
let
\mathcal{G}_{m}
be the collection of all elements in
\cup_{n\geq m}\mathcal{P}_{n}

whose depth are marked, that is,

\mathcal{G}_{m}:=\left\{(A,n):\ n\geq m\ \text{and}\ A\in\mathcal{P}_{n}\right\}.

Given a Borel subset
Z\subset X
and
a real number
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we let

M(Z,\varphi,\alpha,m):=\inf_{\mathcal{G}}\left\{\sum_{(A,n)\in\mathcal{G}}\exp\left(-\alpha n+\sup_{x\in A\cap Z}S_{n}\varphi(x)\right)\right\},

(4.1)

where

S_{n}\varphi:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varphi\circ f^{k}
and
the infimum is taken over all sub-collection
\mathcal{G}
of
\mathcal{G}_{m}
which covers

\displaystyle Z\subset\bigcup_{(A,n)\in\mathcal{G}}A
.
Since

,
we then define

M(Z,\varphi,\alpha):=\lim_{m\to\infty}M(Z,\varphi,\alpha,m).

Moreover, we define the
pressure of
\varphi
on
Z
by

P_{Z}(\varphi):=\inf\left\{\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}:\ M(Z,\varphi,\alpha)=0\right\}=\sup\left\{\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}:\ M(Z,\varphi,\alpha)>0\right\}.

We remark that
our definition of
P_{Z}(\varphi)
is slightly different from the standard definition given in Section 11 of
[
16
]
,
as the collection
\mathcal{G}
is not taken as an open cover but a sub-collection related to the generating partition
\mathcal{P}
.
Moreover,
since the map
f
and the potential
\varphi

that we consider are not assumed to be continuous,
we may not have the
inverse variational principle
, i.e.,

\displaystyle P_{\mu}(\varphi)=\inf\{P_{Z}(\varphi):\mu(Z)>0\}
.
Nevertheless,
the following lemma is enough for our purpose,
which provides an upper bound for
P_{\mu}(\varphi)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any ergodic measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f,X)
with
\displaystyle\int|\varphi|d\mu<\infty
,
and for any Borel subset
Z\subset X
with
\mu(Z)>0
,
we have

P_{\mu}(\varphi)\leq P_{Z}(\varphi).

Proof.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, we set
\displaystyle\alpha_{\varepsilon}=P_{\mu}(\varphi)-2\varepsilon
.
Recall that
\mathcal{P}
is a generating partition, and hence
h_{\mu}(f)=h_{\mu}(f,\mathcal{P})
.

Since
\mu
is ergodic, by the Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem and the
Shannon-McMillan-Breiman theorem,
there exists
m_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
a Borel subset
X_{0}\subset X
with
\mu(X\backslash X_{0})<\frac{1}{2}\mu(Z)
,
such that for all
n\geq m_{0}
and
x\in X_{0}
,

S_{n}\varphi(x)\geq n\left(\int\varphi d\mu-\varepsilon\right)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \log\mu\left(\mathcal{P}_{n}(x)\right)\leq-n\left(h_{\mu}(f)-\varepsilon\right),

where
\mathcal{P}_{n}(x)
denotes the element of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
containing
x
.

We take
Z_{0}=Z\cap X_{0}
, then
\mu(Z_{0})>0
. Given any
m\geq m_{0}
,
let
\mathcal{G}
be a sub-collection of
\mathcal{G}_{m}
which covers
Z_{0}
.
For any
(A,n)\in\mathcal{G}
with
A\cap Z_{0}\neq\emptyset
,
we have
A=\mathcal{P}_{n}(x)
for any
x\in A\cap Z_{0}
, and hence

\begin{split}\sum_{(A,n)\in\mathcal{G}}\exp\left(-\alpha_{\varepsilon}n+\sup_{x\in A\cap Z}S_{n}\varphi(x)\right)&\geq\sum_{(A,n)\in\mathcal{G}}\exp\left(-\alpha_{\varepsilon}n+\sup_{x\in A\cap Z_{0}}S_{n}\varphi(x)\right)\\
&\geq\sum_{(A,n)\in\mathcal{G}}\exp\left(n\left(\int\varphi d\mu-\varepsilon-\alpha_{\varepsilon}\right)\right)\\
&=\sum_{(A,n)\in\mathcal{G}}\exp\left(-n\left(h_{\mu}(f)-\varepsilon\right)\right)\\
&\geq\sum_{(A,n)\in\mathcal{G}}\mu(A)\geq\mu(Z_{0}).\end{split}

Therefore,

M(Z,\varphi,\alpha_{\varepsilon})=\lim_{m\to\infty}M(Z,\varphi,\alpha_{\varepsilon},m)\geq\lim_{m\to\infty}M(Z_{0},\varphi,\alpha_{\varepsilon},m)\geq\mu(Z_{0})>0,

which implies that
P_{Z}(\varphi)\geq\alpha_{\varepsilon}=P_{\mu}(\varphi)-2\varepsilon
.
Thus
P_{Z}(\varphi)\geq P_{\mu}(\varphi)
since
\varepsilon
is arbitrarily chosen.
∎

4.2
Proof of Theorem B

Section 5 in
[
20
]
provides a necessary condition for an ergodic measure
to be non-liftable. We briefly describe it below.

Define a tower associated with the inducing scheme
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}
by

\widehat{Y}:=\left\{(x,k):x\in W,\ 0\leq k<\tau(x)\right\}\subset W\times{\mathbb{N}}

and define
\widehat{f}:\widehat{Y}\to\widehat{Y}
by

\widehat{f}(x,k):=\begin{cases}(x,k+1),&\ k<\tau(x)-1,\\
(F(x),0),&\ k=\tau(x)-1.\end{cases}

For any
N\geq 1
, we set

\widehat{E}_{N}:=\left\{(x,k)\in\widehat{Y}:\ 0\leq k\leq N\right\}.

For any
x\in W
and any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we define the
frequency of the
\widehat{f}
-orbit of the point
(x,0)
falling into
\widehat{E}_{N}

during the first
n
iterates by

A_{n}^{N}(x):=\frac{1}{n}\#\left\{0\leq j<n:\ \widehat{f}^{j}((x,0))\in\widehat{E}_{N}\right\}.

Following the ideas from Keller
[
15
]
and
Zweimüller
[
30
]
,
Pesin, Senti and Zhang obtained the following property for non-liftable measures (Lemma 5.4 in
[
20
]
).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f,X)
be such that
\mu(W)>0
.
If
\mu
is non-liftable,
then there exists an increasing sequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
of positive integers such that for any
\varepsilon>0
,
there exists
Z\subset W
satisfying that
\mu(Z)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\mu(W)
and

\lim_{k\to\infty}A_{n_{k}}^{N}(x)=0\ \ \text{uniformly on}\ Z\ \text{for all}\ N\geq 1.

In fact,
[
20
]
considered an frequency
A_{n}^{N}
falling into a larger set containing
\widehat{E}_{N}
, and thus
Lemma
4.2
here is a corollary of Lemma 5.4 in
[
20
]
.

Later in the proof of Theorem B,
we shall use the following combinatoric inequality (see e.g. Section I.5 of
[
27
]
):
for any
n\geq m\geq 0
, we have

{n\choose m}\leq\exp\left(nh\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)\right)<\exp(n),

(4.2)

where

h(t):=-t\log t-(1-t)\log(1-t),\ \text{for any}\ t\in[0,1].

(4.3)

Note that
h(t)
is increasing for
t\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]

and
\displaystyle\max_{t\in[0,1]}h(t)=\log 2<1
.

We are now ready to prove Theorem B.

Proof of Theorem B.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(f,X)
be an ergodic measure
with
\mu(W)>0
and
\mathcal{K}(\overline{\varphi})<P_{\mu}(\varphi)<\infty
.
Fix some
\mathcal{K}\in\left(\mathcal{K}(\overline{\varphi}),P_{\mu}(\varphi)\right)
.
By Definition
2
,
we can pick
D>1
and
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\begin{split}U_{n}(\overline{\varphi})\leq De^{\mathcal{K}n}\ \text{for all}\ n\geq 1,\\
U_{n}(\overline{\varphi})\leq e^{\mathcal{K}n}\ \ \text{for all}\ n\geq n_{0}.\end{split}

(4.4)

We fix a sufficiently small
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{10})
such that

\mathcal{K}_{1}:=\mathcal{K}+3\delta+h(2\delta)+2\delta\log D<P_{\mu}(\varphi),

(4.5)

where
h(\cdot)
is the function given by (
4.3
).

Now suppose that
\mu
is non-liftable.
We shall prove Theorem B by contradiction.

Fix

\displaystyle N=\lceil 1/\delta\rceil.

By Lemma
4.2
,
there exists
a Borel subset
Z\subset W
with

\mu(Z)>0

and an increasing sequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
of positive integers
(by choosing a subsequence of the original sequence in Lemma
4.2
if necessary)
such that

\sup_{x\in Z}A_{n_{k}}^{N}(x)<\delta/2,\ \ \text{for all}\ k\geq 1.

(4.6)

Without loss of generality, we may assume that for all
k\geq 1
,

\begin{split}(i)\ \ &n_{k}>\max\{n_{0},100N/\delta\},\ \text{where}\ n_{0}\ \text{is given by \eqref{est Un}};\\
(ii)\ &n^{3}\leq e^{\delta n}\ \text{for all}\ n\geq n_{k}.\end{split}

(4.7)

We would like
to estimate the quantity
M\left(Z,\varphi,\alpha,n_{k}\right)
introduced in (
4.1
).
To this end,
we construct a particular covering of
Z
as follows.

An
n_{k}
-code
via the inducing scheme
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}
is the
s
-tuple of the form

\mathbf{a}=(a_{0},\dots,a_{s-1})\in S^{s}

such that
m_{s-1}<n_{k}\leq m_{s}
,
where
m_{0}=0
and

m_{j}=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\tau(a_{i})

for
1\leq j\leq s
.
For convenience, we denote

\displaystyle n(\mathbf{a})=m_{s}

and

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(\mathbf{a})=(\tau(a_{0}),\dots,\tau(a_{s-1}))
.

Condition
(L1)
assumes that the partition
\mathcal{P}
is compatible to the inducing scheme,
which implies that for any
a\in S
, the block
J_{a}
is contained in an element of
\mathcal{P}_{\tau(a)}
.
Hence an
n_{k}
-code
\mathbf{a}
defines a Borel subset

A_{\mathbf{a}}:=J_{a_{0}}\cap f^{-m_{1}}J_{a_{1}}\cap\dots\cap f^{-m_{s-1}}J_{a_{s-1}},

which is contained in an element of
\mathcal{P}_{n(\mathbf{a})}
.

Subject to the Borel subset
Z
and the sequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
that are
obtained from Lemma
4.2
, we make the following notions:

•

let
\Gamma(Z,n_{k})
be the collection of all
n_{k}
-codes
\mathbf{a}
such that

A_{\mathbf{a}}\cap Z\neq\emptyset
.

•

we say that a string
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=(\tau_{0},\dots,\tau_{s-1})
is
(Z,n_{k})
-admissible

if there is
\mathbf{a}\in\Gamma(Z,n_{k})
such that
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(\mathbf{a})={\boldsymbol{\tau}}
.
Let
\mathcal{A}(Z,n_{k})
be the collection of
(Z,n_{k})
-admissible strings.

•

given a
(Z,n_{k})
-admissible string
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
, we
let
\Gamma(Z,{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
be the collection of all
n_{k}
-codes
\mathbf{a}\in\Gamma(Z,n_{k})
such that
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}(\mathbf{a})={\boldsymbol{\tau}}
.
It is clear that

\Gamma(Z,n_{k})=\bigcup_{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathcal{A}(Z,n_{k})}\Gamma(Z,{\boldsymbol{\tau}}).

•

given a
(Z,n_{k})
-admissible string
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=(\tau_{0},\dots,\tau_{s-1})
,
we denote
n({\boldsymbol{\tau}})=\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\tau_{i}
.
We claim that

\dfrac{\sum_{\tau_{i}\leq N}\ \tau_{i}}{n({\boldsymbol{\tau}})}<\delta,\ \text{or equivalently},\ \dfrac{\sum_{\tau_{i}>N}\ \tau_{i}}{n({\boldsymbol{\tau}})}\geq 1-\delta.

(4.8)

Indeed, let
n^{\prime}:=\sum_{0\leq i<s-1:\ \tau_{i}\leq N}\tau_{i}
, then (
4.6
) implies that

\displaystyle n^{\prime}/n_{k}<\delta/2,

and hence

\dfrac{\sum_{\tau_{i}\leq N}\ \tau_{i}}{n({\boldsymbol{\tau}})}\leq\dfrac{n^{\prime}+N}{n({\boldsymbol{\tau}})}\leq\dfrac{n^{\prime}+N}{n_{k}}\leq\delta/2+\delta/100<\delta.

It is clear that
Z\subset\bigcup_{\mathbf{a}\in\Gamma(Z,n_{k})}A_{\mathbf{a}}=\bigcup_{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathcal{A}(Z,n_{k})}\bigcup_{\mathbf{a}\in\Gamma(Z,{\boldsymbol{\tau}})}A_{\mathbf{a}}
,
where each
A_{\mathbf{a}}
lies inside an element of
\mathcal{P}_{n(\mathbf{a})}
.
Hence for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

\begin{split}M(Z,\varphi,\alpha,n_{k})&\leq\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\Gamma(Z,n_{k})}\exp\left(-\alpha n(\mathbf{a})+\sup_{x\in A_{\mathbf{a}}\cap Z}S_{n(\mathbf{a})}\varphi(x)\right)\\
&=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathcal{A}(Z,n_{k})}e^{-\alpha n({\boldsymbol{\tau}})}\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\Gamma(Z,{\boldsymbol{\tau}})}\exp\left(\sup_{x\in A_{\mathbf{a}}\cap Z}S_{n({\boldsymbol{\tau}})}\varphi(x)\right).\end{split}

Note that for any
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}=(\tau_{0},\dots,\tau_{s-1})\in\mathcal{A}(Z,n_{k})

and any

\mathbf{a}=(a_{0},\dots,a_{s-1})\in\Gamma(Z,{\boldsymbol{\tau}})
,
we set
m_{0}=0
and
m_{j}=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\tau_{i}
for
1\leq j\leq s
, then
we have

\sup_{x\in A_{\mathbf{a}}\cap Z}S_{n({\boldsymbol{\tau}})}\varphi(x)=\sup_{x\in A_{\mathbf{a}}\cap Z}\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\overline{\varphi}(f^{m_{i}}(x))\leq\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\sup_{x\in J_{a_{i}}}\overline{\varphi}(x).

Recall that
U_{n}(\cdot)
given by Definition
2
, then by (
4.4
) and
(
4.8
), we have

\begin{split}\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\Gamma(Z,{\boldsymbol{\tau}})}\exp\left(\sup_{x\in A_{\mathbf{a}}\cap Z}S_{n({\boldsymbol{\tau}})}\varphi(x)\right)&\leq\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\Gamma(Z,{\boldsymbol{\tau}})}\prod_{i=0}^{s-1}\exp\left(\sup_{x\in J_{a_{i}}}\overline{\varphi}(x)\right)\\
&\leq\prod_{i=0}^{s-1}U_{\tau_{i}}(\overline{\varphi})\\
&\leq\prod_{\tau_{i}\leq N}De^{\mathcal{K}\tau_{i}}\prod_{\tau_{i}>N}e^{\mathcal{K}\tau_{i}}\\
&\leq\exp\left(n({\boldsymbol{\tau}})\left(\mathcal{K}+\delta\log D\right)\right).\end{split}

Therefore,

\begin{split}M(Z,\varphi,\alpha,n_{k})&\leq\sum_{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathcal{A}(Z,n_{k})}\exp\left(n({\boldsymbol{\tau}})\left(\mathcal{K}-\alpha+\delta\log D\right)\right)\\
&=\sum_{n=n_{k}}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}\exp\left(n\left(\mathcal{K}-\alpha+\delta\log D\right)\right),\end{split}

where
\gamma_{n}:=\#\{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\in\mathcal{A}(Z,n_{k}):\ n({\boldsymbol{\tau}})=n\}
.
Note that
\gamma_{n}
is no more than
the number of ways to rewrite
n
as

n=\tau_{0}+\tau_{1}+\dots+\tau_{s-1}

such that each
\tau_{i}\geq 1
and
\sum_{\tau_{i}\leq N}\ \tau_{i}<\delta n
.
Let
s_{1}
be the number of
i
’s such that
\tau_{i}\leq N
,
and then
s_{2}=s-s_{1}
is the number of
i
’s such that
\tau_{i}>N
.
Then we have

\displaystyle 0\leq s_{1}<\delta n

and

\displaystyle 1\leq s_{2}\leq n/N<\delta n
.
Therefore, by the combinatoric inequality (
4.2
) and
the monotonicity of the function
h(t)
given by (
4.3
) for
t\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
,
as well as the choices of
\delta
and
n_{k}
given by (
4.5
) and (
4.7
) respectively,
we get for any
n\geq n_{k}
,

\begin{split}\gamma_{n}&\leq\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{\lfloor\delta n\rfloor}\sum_{s_{2}=1}^{\lfloor\delta n\rfloor}{n-s_{2}N-1\choose s_{1}+s_{2}-1}{s_{1}+s_{2}\choose s_{1}}\\
&\leq\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{\lfloor\delta n\rfloor}\sum_{s_{2}=1}^{\lfloor\delta n\rfloor}{n\choose s_{1}+s_{2}-1}{s_{1}+s_{2}\choose s_{1}}\\
&\leq\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{\lfloor\delta n\rfloor}\sum_{s_{2}=1}^{\lfloor\delta n\rfloor}\exp\left(nh\left(\frac{s_{1}+s_{2}-1}{n}\right)\right)\exp\left(s_{1}+s_{2}\right)\\
&\leq\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{\lfloor\delta n\rfloor}\sum_{s_{2}=1}^{\lfloor\delta n\rfloor}\exp\left(nh\left(2\delta\right)\right)\exp\left(2\delta n\right)\\
&\leq 2(\delta n)^{2}\cdot\exp\left(n\left(2\delta+h(2\delta)\right)\right)<\exp\left(n(3\delta+h(2\delta))\right).\end{split}

Finally, let
\mathcal{K}_{1}
given by (
4.5
) and take some
\alpha\in(\mathcal{K}_{1},P_{\mu}(\varphi))
, then
we obtain

\begin{split}M(Z,\varphi,\alpha,n_{k})&\leq\sum_{n=n_{k}}^{\infty}\exp\left(n\left(\mathcal{K}-\alpha+3\delta+h(2\delta)+\delta\log D\right)\right)\\
&\leq\sum_{n=n_{k}}^{\infty}\exp\left(n\left(\mathcal{K}_{1}-\alpha\right)\right)\leq\dfrac{\exp\left(n_{k}(\mathcal{K}_{1}-\alpha)\right)}{1-\exp(\mathcal{K}_{1}-\alpha)},\end{split}

which implies that
M(Z,\varphi,\alpha)=\lim_{k\to\infty}M(Z,\varphi,\alpha,n_{k})=0

and thus
P_{Z}(\varphi)\leq\alpha<P_{\mu}(\varphi)
.
However, by Lemma
4.1
, we must have
P_{\mu}(\varphi)\leq P_{Z}(\varphi)
,
which is a contradiction.
Therefore, the measure
\mu
has to be liftable.
The proof of Theorem B is complete.
∎

4.3
Proof of Corollary C

We now proceed the proof of Corollary C.

Proof of Corollary C.

Let
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}
be an inducing scheme with inducing domain
W
.
Let
\varphi
be a strongly nice potential satisfying Conditions
(P1)-(P5)

and let
\psi
be a regular potential satisfying Conditions
(P1)(P2)
.
Recall that
\mathcal{M}_{L}(f,Y)
,

\mathcal{M}^{W}(f,X)
,
P_{L}(\cdot)
and
P^{W}(\cdot)
are defined by
(
2.8
)
(
1.2
), (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) respectively.

If Condition
(H)
holds, then by Statement (1) of Theorem A, we
let
\mu_{t}
be the unique equilibrium measure for the potential
\varphi+t\psi

in the class
\mathcal{M}_{L}(f,Y)
, then

\displaystyle P_{\mu_{t}}(\varphi+t\psi)=P_{L}(\varphi+t\psi)

is finite for all
t\in(\underline{t},\overline{t})
.
It follows that
\displaystyle\int|\varphi+t\psi|d\mu_{t}<\infty
, and hence

P_{L}(\varphi+t\psi)\leq P^{W}(\varphi+t\psi)
for all
t\in(\underline{t},\overline{t})
.

If now Condition
(L1)
holds,
Lemma
3.1
, Lemma
3.5
and Lemma
3.6
imply that
the inducing scheme
\{\mathcal{S},\tau\}
has finite

\overline{\varphi+t\psi}
-complexity, and

-\infty<\mathcal{K}\left(\overline{\varphi+t\psi}\right)=\kappa(t)<q_{t}\leq p_{t}=P_{L}(\varphi+t\psi)<\infty.

For any ergodic measure
\mu_{*}\in\mathcal{M}^{W}(f,X)
such that

\begin{split}P_{\mu_{*}}(\varphi+t\psi)&=P^{W}(\varphi+t\psi)\\
&=\sup\left\{P_{\mu}(\varphi+t\psi):\ \mu\in\mathcal{M}^{W}(f,X)\ \text{and}\ \int|\varphi+t\psi|d\mu<\infty\right\},\end{split}

we have

\mathcal{K}\left(\overline{\varphi+t\psi}\right)<P_{L}(\varphi+t\psi)\leq P_{\mu_{*}}(\varphi+t\psi)\leq h(f)+\int|\varphi+t\psi|d\mu_{*}<\infty.

Then by Theorem B, the measure
\mu_{*}
is liftable and hence
\mu_{*}=\mu_{t}
.
In other words,
\mu_{t}
is in fact
the unique equilibrium measure for the potential
\varphi+t\psi

in the class
\mathcal{M}^{W}(f,X)
.
Moreover, by Statement (3) of Theorem A,
the function

\displaystyle t\mapsto P^{W}(\varphi+t\psi)=P_{L}(\varphi+t\psi)

is finite and real analytic in
(\underline{t},\overline{t})
.
∎

Resummation effects in the bottom-quark fragmentation function arXiv: 2207.10038
Fabio Maltoni, Giovanni Ridolfi, Maria Ubiali, Marco Zaro

Abstract
We compute the perturbative component of the fragmentation function of the b b quark to the best of the present theoretical knowledge. The fixed-order calculation to order α s 2 \alpha_{s}^{2} of the fragmentation function at the initial scale is matched with soft-emission logarithm resummation to next-to-next-to-leading logarithmic accuracy, so that order- α s 2 \alpha_{s}^{2} corrections are accounted for exactly, and logarithmically enhanced contributions from loops of b b quarks are included. This requires the calculation of the Mellin transform of the order- α s 2 \alpha_{s}^{2} result in the whole complex plane for the Mellin variable, which we provide for the first time for all the fragmenting partons. Evolution is performed to next-to-next-to-leading log accuracy, and mixing with the gluon fragmentation function is taken into account. The perturbative fragmentation functions are made available via LHAPDF grids.

Introduction

The production of heavy quarks (charm, bottom, top), possibly in association with other particles, is a particularly important class of processes at colliders. Not only they
provide key information for advancing our understanding of strong and electroweak interactions in the Standard Model (SM), but their very distinctive
experimental signatures typically enter as background in many SM measurements and beyond-the-SM searches. From the theoretical point of view, reliable and accurate predictions for these processes that match the current and foreseen experimental precision, are necessary. This is, however, a tall order.
Fixed-order predictions are, in general, not accurate enough and terms appear that hamper the convergence of the perturbative series and need to be included at all orders. A class of such terms involve mass logarithms, i.e. powers of
\log\frac{Q^{2}}{m^{2}}
, where
m
and
Q
are the heavy-quark mass and the typical energy scale of the process, respectively. When these terms are large, an all-order
resummation, which can be achieved by means of Dokshitzer-Gribov-Lipatov-Altarelli-Parisi (DGLAP) evolution of fragmentation functions (FFs), becomes necessary. At past colliders and at the LHC this is important
for the bottom and the charm quark. For top quarks such effects become relevant at transverse momentum above a few TeV, a regime which is marginal at the LHC but possibly of interest at future high-energy colliders. FFs make it possible to organise terms of order
\alpha_{s}^{p}\log^{q}\frac{Q^{2}}{m^{2}}
and to systematically resum them
via the DGLAP evolution, thus obtaining physical predictions at a given logarithmic accuracy. Nowadays,
the DGLAP evolution is implemented in several computer codes, such as

QCDnum

Botje:2010ay
,

ffevol

Hirai:2011si
,

APFEL

Bertone:2013vaa
,

MELA

Bertone:2015cwa
,

EKO

Candido:2022tld
,
up to next-to-next-to-leading order (NNLO).

The fragmentation function of a
b
quark is a special case: not only its dependence on the hard scale
Q
is under control in perturbation theory via DGLAP equations, but also the initial condition for evolution, typically given at a scale of the order of the
b
-quark mass, can be computed perturbatively
Mele:1990yq
;
Mele:1990cw
;
Cacciari:2001cw
;
Melnikov:2004bm
;
Mitov:2004du
. Such perturbative calculation becomes unreliable in the kinematical regime where the produced
b
quark carries most of the available energy, and therefore the recoiling radiation is soft, giving rise to a further class of large logarithms. The formalism for the resummation of soft emission logarithms is outlined in
Cacciari:2001cw
, where resummation is explicitly performed to next-to-leading logarithmic (NLL) accuracy.

We stress that having an excellent perturbative description of FFs represents only part of the solution to the problem of providing an accurate description of heavy-quark
related processes. The perturbative result has to be complemented with a
non-perturbative
part which parametrises the transition from the heavy quark to the
corresponding flavoured hadron. This contribution cannot be computed in perturbation theory, and is usually extracted by a direct comparison to data. Once the perturbative part is known, the extraction of the non-perturbative contribution to the FF is a well-defined and independent phenomenological task. Though relevant to provide physical predictions to compare with experiment, it is not addressed in this work. The interested reader can find applications to the case of heavy-quark production in leptonic collisions in
Mele:1990cw
;
Cacciari:2001cw
;
Cacciari:2005uk
;
Aglietti:2006yf
,
of the bottom quark in top decays in
Corcella:2001hz
;
Cacciari:2002re
;
Czakon:2021ohs
, and of the Higgs decay to heavy quarks in
Corcella:2004xv
.

In this work, we improve the perturbative description of the heavy-quark FF, using the framework to compute the coupled evolution of the
b
-quark fragmentation with the gluon and the other partons at NNLO accuracy described in
Ridolfi:2019bch
which builds upon
MELA

Bertone:2015cwa
.
We asses the impact of resummation of soft logarithms on the initial condition for the evolution of the
b
fragmentation
function
D_{b}(x,\mu^{2}_{0F})
by implementing the resummation formalism of
Cacciari:2001cw
up to next-to-next-to-leading logarithmic accuracy (NNLL in the following).
Resummation at NNLL of the initial condition was already performed in
Aglietti:2006yf
.
In that work the resummed result was matched to the exact perturbative calculation at next-to-leading order (NLO).
We improve on this result in three ways. First, we compute the process-dependent component of the resummed initial condition in such a way to reproduce the exact order-
\alpha_{s}^{2}
result of
Melnikov:2004bm
to NNLL accuracy, including the contributions originating from loops of heavy quarks. Note that such contributions had not been accounted for in previous works
Gardi:2005yi
;
Aglietti:2006yf
. Second, we match the NNLL resummed result to the exact NNLO initial condition provided in
Melnikov:2004bm
. This requires the calculation of the Mellin transform of the full NNLO initial condition, which was not available until now in an analitically-continued
version defined in the whole complex plane. The calculations presented here fill this gap. Finally, we include both the heavy-quark and the gluon and light quark FFs in their coupled evolution.

The work is organised as follows.
In Sect.
2
we summarise the formalism and the
relevant equations for the heavy-quark fragmentation function computation starting
from a perturbative initial condition. In particular, we focus on the dependence of the
results on the scheme used to deal with heavy flavours, and
we present our calculation of the initial bottom fragmentation function
with soft logarithms resummed at NNLL and matched with the NNLO expression.
In Sect.
3
we present selected numerical results, commenting on the impact of resummation as well as of the inclusion
of the NNLO initial conditions.
We present our conclusions in Sect.
4
. Appendix
A
contains some details about the calculation of the resummed initial condition, while in Appendix
B
we illustrate the calculation of the relevant Mellin transforms.

2
The heavy quark fragmentation function

In the fragmentation-function formalism, the cross section for the production of a heavy quark
{\mathcal{Q}}
is given by

\sigma_{\mathcal{Q}}(x,Q^{2},m^{2})=\sum_{j}\int_{x}^{1}\frac{dz}{z}\,\hat{\sigma}_{j}\left(\frac{x}{z},\alpha_{s}(\mu_{R}^{2}),\mu_{R}^{2},\mu_{F}^{2},Q^{2}\right)D_{j{\mathcal{Q}}}(z,\mu_{F}^{2},m^{2})+{\mathcal{O}}\left((m/Q)^{p}\right),

(1)

where
x
is the fraction of the available energy carried away by the produced heavy quark,
\hat{\sigma}_{j}
is a hard cross section (possibly including parton distribution functions for the initial state) and
D_{j{\mathcal{Q}}}
the fragmentation functions of partons
j
into the heavy quark
\mathcal{Q}
.

The fragmentation functions at the scale
\mu_{F}
are obtained by evolving suitable initial conditions, given at a reference scale
\mu_{0F}
, through Altarelli-Parisi equations. In the following, we will be interested in the initial condition in the case
j={\mathcal{Q}}=b
, which can be computed perturbatively at
\mu_{0F}\sim m
. We will denote by
D_{b}
the
b
fragmentation function for simplicity. We will also omit the explicit dependence of the fragmentation function on the
b
-quark mass
m
.

2.1
Fixed-order calculation of the initial condition

The initial condition for the heavy quark fragmentation function can
be computed perturbatively, as illustrated in
Mele:1990cw
;
Cacciari:2001cw
, at a
scale
\mu_{0F}
of the order of (but not necessarily equal to
Bertone:2017djs
) the heavy quark mass. The order-
\alpha_{s}
correction is given in
Mele:1990cw
, while the order-
\alpha_{s}^{2}
coefficient was computed in
Melnikov:2004bm
. The result is

D_{b}^{\rm pert}(z,\mu_{0F}^{2})=\delta(1-z)+\frac{\alpha_{s}^{(n_{f})}(\mu_{0F}^{2})}{2\pi}d_{b}^{(1)}(z,\mu_{0F}^{2})+\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}^{(n_{f})}(\mu_{0F}^{2})}{2\pi}\right)^{2}d_{b}^{(2)}(z,\mu_{0F}^{2})+{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha_{s}^{3}),

(2)

where

\displaystyle d_{b}^{(1)}(z,\mu_{0F}^{2})

\displaystyle=C_{F}\left[\frac{1+z^{2}}{1-z}\left(\log\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{m^{2}(1-z)^{2}}-1\right)\right]_{+}

(3)

\displaystyle d_{b}^{(2)}(z,\mu_{0F}^{2})

\displaystyle=C_{F}^{2}F^{(C_{F}^{2})}_{Q}(z,\mu_{0F}^{2})+C_{A}C_{F}F^{(C_{A}C_{F})}_{Q}(z,\mu_{0F}^{2})

\displaystyle+C_{F}T_{R}F^{(C_{F}T_{R})}_{Q}(z,\mu_{0F}^{2})+C_{F}T_{R}n_{\ell}F^{(C_{F}T_{R}n_{\ell})}_{Q}(z,\mu_{0F}^{2}),

(4)

and
n_{\ell}=n_{f}-1
is the number of massless flavors. The functions
F^{(C_{F}^{2})}_{Q}
,

F^{(C_{A}C_{F})}_{Q}
,
F^{(C_{F}T_{R})}_{Q}
,
F^{(C_{F}T_{R}n_{\ell})}_{Q}
are given explicitly in
Melnikov:2004bm
.
The calculation in
Melnikov:2004bm
is performed in the
\overline{\rm MS}
renormalization scheme for ultraviolet divergences, which means that both massless and massive flavors take part in the evolution of the running coupling.

For the purpose of comparison with the resummed result, it will be useful to rewrite the result of
Melnikov:2004bm
as an expansion in powers of

\alpha_{s}=\alpha_{s}^{(n_{\ell})}
. Furthermore, for greater generality it will be convenient to choose a renormalization scale
\mu_{0}
different from
\mu_{0F}
. To order
\alpha_{s}^{2}
,
we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{s}^{(n_{f})}(\mu_{0F}^{2})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})-\alpha_{s}^{2}(\mu_{0}^{2})\left\{\left[b_{0}(n_{\ell})-b_{0}(n_{f})\right]\log\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}}+b_{0}(n_{f})\log\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}}\right\}+{\cal O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})-\alpha_{s}^{2}(\mu_{0}^{2})\left\{\frac{T_{f}}{3\pi}\log\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}}+\frac{11C_{A}-4T_{f}n_{f}}{12\pi}\log\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}}\right\}+{\cal O}(\alpha_{s}^{3}),

(5)

where we have used

b_{0}(n_{\ell})=\frac{11C_{A}-4T_{f}n_{\ell}}{12\pi};\qquad n_{f}=n_{\ell}+1.

(6)

The first term in curly brackets originates from the change of renormalization scheme, while the second one arises from evolution from
\mu_{0}
to
\mu_{0F}
.
Replacing Eq. (
5
) in Eq. (
2
)
and expanding again in powers of
\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})
to order 2
we get

\displaystyle D_{b}^{\rm pert}(z,\mu_{0F}^{2})=\delta(1-z)+\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})}{2\pi}d_{b}^{(1)}(z,\mu_{0F}^{2})

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})}{2\pi}\right)^{2}\left\{d_{b}^{(2)}(z,\mu_{0F}^{2})-d_{b}^{(1)}(z,\mu_{0F}^{2})\left[\frac{2T_{f}}{3}\log\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}}+\frac{11C_{A}-4T_{f}(n_{\ell}+1)}{6}\log\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle+{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha_{s}^{3}).

(7)

The calculation of the Mellin transform of this expression,

D_{b}^{\rm pert}(N,\mu_{0F}^{2})=\int_{0}^{1}dz\,z^{N-1}D_{b}^{\rm pert}(z,\mu_{0F}^{2})

(8)

as a function of the complex variable
N
can be performed as illustrated in Appendix
B
; this is one of the main results of this paper.

2.2
Soft gluon resummation

It was observed in
Cacciari:2001cw
that logarithmic corrections to the initial condition for the heavy quark fragmentation function, arising at all orders because of soft emission, may play a relevant role, at least conceptually, in the large-
z
region. It is therefore useful to resum such contributions to all orders, to some given logarithmic accuracy, and to assess their impact on the fragmentation function itself and on related observables.
Soft gluon resummation of the
b
fragmentation function at the initial scale was performed in
Cacciari:2001cw
to NLL accuracy, and subsequently improved to
NNLL in
Aglietti:2006yf
. Here we
recompute the process-dependent component of the resummed initial
condition, by including a term that was omitted in previous
results, and improve on them by matching the resummed expression to the
order-
\alpha_{s}^{2}
result of
Melnikov:2004bm
.

Soft gluon resummation is performed in Mellin space, as illustrated in Ref.
Cacciari:2001cw
. For a generic function
g(z)
, with

0\leq z\leq 1
, we define

g(N)=\int_{0}^{1}dz\,z^{N-1}g(z).

(9)

The resummed fragmentation function at the initial scale for evolution

\mu_{0F}^{2}
takes the familiar form of an exponential,

D_{b}^{\rm res}(N,\mu_{0F}^{2})=g_{0}\left(\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}),\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}}\right)\exp\left[F\left(\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}),\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\lambda\right)\right],

(10)

where

F\left(\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}),\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\lambda\right)=\int_{0}^{1}dz\,\frac{z^{N-1}-1}{1-z}\left[\int^{\mu_{0F}^{2}}_{m^{2}(1-z)^{2}}\frac{d\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\,A(\alpha_{s}(\mu^{2}))+H(\alpha_{s}(m^{2}(1-z)^{2}))\right]

(11)

and

\lambda=b_{0}\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})\log N.

(12)

The functions
A(\alpha_{s})
and
H(\alpha_{s})
have perturbative expansions in powers of

\alpha_{s}
starting at order 1:

A(\alpha_{s})=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}A_{k}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}\right)^{k};\qquad H(\alpha_{s})=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}H_{k}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}\right)^{k}.

(13)

NNLL accuracy is achieved including
A(\alpha_{s})
up to order
\alpha_{s}^{3}
and
H(\alpha_{s})
up to order
\alpha_{s}^{2}
.

A(\alpha_{s})
is determined by the Altarelli-Parisi splitting functions, while
H(\alpha_{s})
is process dependent, and must be extracted from the fixed-order calculation. The coefficients
H_{i}
must be obtained by matching the expansion of the resummed expression Eq. (
10
) to the relevant perturbative order with the fixed-order calculation.

In Eq. (
10
) the strong coupling is computed with
n_{\ell}=4
active flavour; this is the natural choice, because the integration over
\mu^{2}
in the exponent
ranges between
m^{2}(1-z)^{2}\ll m^{2}
and
\mu_{0F}^{2}\sim m^{2}
. Its evolution from
\mu_{0F}
up to a hard scale
\mu_{F}\gg m
is however performed with
n_{f}=n_{\ell}+1
massless flavours.

It is interesting to discuss the dependence of the resummed initial condition, Eq. (
10
), on the initial factorization scale
\mu_{0F}
.
The fragmentation function evolved up to a generic hard scale
\mu_{F}
is given by

D_{b}^{\rm res}(N,\mu_{F}^{2})=E(N,\mu_{F}^{2},\mu_{0F}^{2})D_{b}^{\rm res}(N,\mu_{0F}^{2}),

(14)

where

E(N,\mu_{F}^{2},\mu_{0F}^{2})=E^{-1}(N,\mu_{0F}^{2},\mu_{F}^{2})

(15)

is the Altarelli-Parisi evolution kernel at large
N
. We expect
D_{b}^{\rm res}(N,\mu_{F}^{2})
to be independent of
the initial scale
\mu_{0F}
, which implies

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\log D_{b}^{\rm res}(N,\mu_{0F}^{2})}{\partial\log\mu_{0F}^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\log\mu_{0F}^{2}}\log E(N,\mu_{F}^{2},\mu_{0F}^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial\log\mu_{0F}^{2}}\log E(N,\mu_{0F}^{2},\mu_{F}^{2})

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\rm AP}(N,\alpha_{s}^{(n_{f})}(\mu_{0F}^{2})),

(16)

where

\gamma_{\rm AP}(N,\alpha_{s}^{(n_{f})}(\mu_{0F}^{2}))=-A(\alpha_{s}^{(n_{f})}(\mu_{0F}^{2}))\log N+{\cal O}(N^{0})

(17)

is the relevant
\overline{\rm MS}
anomalous dimension in the large-
N
limit. As already observed, the evolution between an initial scale
\mu_{0F}
, of the order or the heavy quark mass
m
, and a hard scale
\mu_{F}\gg m
, is determined by
n_{f}
massless flavours; the anomalous dimension is therefore naturally expressed as an expansion in powers of
\alpha_{s}^{(n_{f})}
, whose
coefficients
A_{k}
also depend on
n_{f}
for
k\geq 2
.
On the other hand, neglecting
{\cal O}(N^{0})
terms, the logarithmic derivative of Eq. (
10
) reads

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\log D_{b}^{\rm res}(N,\mu_{0F}^{2})}{\partial\log\mu_{0F}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}dz\,\frac{z^{N-1}-1}{1-z}\left[A(\alpha_{s}^{(n_{\ell})}(\mu_{0F}^{2}))+\frac{\partial H(\alpha_{s}^{(n_{\ell})}(m^{2}(1-z)^{2}))}{\partial\log\mu_{0F}^{2}}\right]+{\cal O}(N^{0})

\displaystyle=-\left[A(\alpha_{s}^{(n_{\ell})}(\mu_{0F}^{2}))+\frac{\partial H(\alpha_{s}^{(n_{\ell})}(m^{2}(1-z)^{2}))}{\partial\log\mu_{0F}^{2}}\right]\log N+{\cal O}(N^{0}).

(18)

Equations (
16
) and (
18
) can only be consistent with each other if the coefficients
H_{k}
for
k\geq 2
carry a dependence on
\log\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{m^{2}}
.

After performing the two integrations in Eq. (
11
), the exponent
F
is expressed as a power expansion in
\alpha_{s}
at fixed
\lambda
. To NNLL accuracy

F\left(\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}),\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\lambda\right)=\frac{1}{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})}g_{1}(\lambda)+g_{2}(\lambda)+\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})g_{3}(\lambda)+{\rm N^{3}LL}.

(19)

The functions
g_{0},g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}
are given explicitly in Appendix
A
, together with some details on their derivation, in terms of the coefficients

A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}
and
H_{1},H_{2}
.
The functions
g_{1}(\lambda)
and
g_{2}(\lambda)
were already given in
Cacciari:2001cw
, and we find full agreement with those expressions. The function

g_{3}(\lambda)
was computed in
Aglietti:2006yf
, where NNLL resummation was performed, but not explicitly provided.

The extraction of
H_{2}
from the order-
\alpha_{s}^{2}
calculation deserves some comments.
It can be obtained by expanding Eq. (
10
) to order
\alpha_{s}^{2}
and comparing the result with the fixed-order calculation in the large-
N
limit, Eq. (

H_{2}=-C_{F}\left[\frac{\pi b_{0}(n_{\ell})}{9}+C_{A}\left(\frac{9\zeta_{3}}{4}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}-\frac{11}{18}\right)\right]+\frac{C_{F}T_{f}}{54}\left[9\log^{2}\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0F}^{2}}+30\log\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0F}^{2}}+28\right],

(20)

which depends on
\log\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{m^{2}}
as expected.

We can check explicitly that, with
H_{2}
given in Eq. (
45
), the resummed initial condition
D_{b}^{\rm res}(N,\mu_{0F}^{2})
at NNLL is a solution of Eq. (
16
). Indeed, neglecting constant (i.e.
N
independent) terms,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\log D_{b}^{\rm res}(N,\mu_{0F}^{2})}{\partial\log\mu_{0F}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial\log\mu_{0F}^{2}}F\left(\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}),\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\lambda\right)

\displaystyle=-\left[\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0F}^{2})}{\pi}A_{1}+\frac{\alpha_{s}^{2}(\mu_{0F}^{2})}{\pi^{2}}A_{2}(n_{\ell})+\frac{\alpha_{s}^{2}(\mu_{0F}^{2})}{\pi^{2}}\frac{\partial H_{2}}{\partial\log\mu_{0F}^{2}}\right]\log N+{\cal O}(\alpha_{s}^{3}),

(21)

where we have taken into account that
H_{1}
is
\mu_{0F}
-independent, and we have displayed the dependence of
A_{2}
on the number of flavours
n_{\ell}
, see Eqs. (
42
,
45
). Since

\frac{\partial H_{2}}{\partial\log\mu_{0F}^{2}}=\frac{C_{F}T_{f}}{9}\left(3\log^{2}\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{m^{2}}-5\right)

(22)

we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\log D_{b}^{\rm res}(N,\mu_{0F}^{2})}{\partial\log\mu_{0F}^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\left\{\frac{A_{1}}{\pi}\left[\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0F}^{2})+\alpha_{s}^{2}(\mu_{0F}^{2})\frac{T_{f}}{3\pi}\log^{2}\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{m^{2}}\right]+\frac{\alpha_{s}^{2}(\mu_{0F}^{2})}{\pi^{2}}\left[A_{2}(n_{\ell})-\frac{5}{9}C_{F}T_{f}\right]\right\}\log N+{\cal O}(\alpha_{s}^{3}),

(23)

where we have used
A_{1}=C_{F}
.
By Eq. (
5
) with
\mu_{0}=\mu_{0F}
,

\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0F}^{2})+\alpha_{s}^{2}(\mu_{0F}^{2})\frac{T_{f}}{3\pi}\log^{2}\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{m^{2}}=\alpha_{s}^{(n_{f})}(\mu_{0F}^{2}).

(24)

Furthermore, from Eq. (
42
),

A_{2}(n_{\ell})-\frac{5}{9}C_{F}T_{f}=A_{2}(n_{f}).

(25)

Hence

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\log D_{b}^{\rm res}(N,\mu_{0F}^{2})}{\partial\log\mu_{0F}^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\left\{\frac{A_{1}}{\pi}\alpha_{s}^{(n_{f})}(\mu_{0F}^{2})+\frac{\alpha_{s}^{2}(\mu_{0F}^{2})}{\pi^{2}}A_{2}(n_{f})\right\}\log N+{\cal O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\rm AP}(N,\alpha_{s}^{(n_{f})}(\mu_{0F}^{2}))

(26)

as announced.

), differs from the value of

given in

and

) is consistent with expectations.

Gardi:2005yi
,

was extracted under the assumption that the

quarks was considered;
here, we do not make this assumption.
The value of
H_{2}
obtained in
Gardi:2005yi
was later employed in
Aglietti:2006yf
.

2.3
Matching resummed and fixed-order initial condition

Both the fixed-order initial condition for the
b
fragmentation function Eq. (
8
), accurate to NNLO, and the resummed initial condition Eq. (
10
), accurate to NNLL, are now expressed as functions of
\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})=\alpha_{s}^{(n_{\ell})}(\mu_{0}^{2})
, and of the complex variable
N
, Mellin conjugate to
z
. Their combination requires the subtraction of the resummed result expanded to order
\alpha_{s}^{2}
to avoid double counting. Our final result is therefore

\displaystyle D_{b}(N,\mu_{0F}^{2})

\displaystyle=D_{b}^{\rm pert}(N,\mu_{0F}^{2})+D_{b}^{\rm res}(N,\mu_{0F}^{2})

\displaystyle-\left[1+\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})\left.\frac{\partial D_{b}^{\rm res}(N,\mu_{0F}^{2})}{\partial\alpha_{s}}\right|_{\alpha_{s}=0}+\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{s}^{2}(\mu_{0}^{2})\left.\frac{\partial^{2}D_{b}^{\rm res}(N,\mu_{0F}^{2})}{\partial\alpha_{s}^{2}}\right|_{\alpha_{s}=0}\right].

(27)

3
Results

In this Section, we present results for the
b
-quark fragmentation function
D_{b}(z,\mu_{F}^{2})
and its Mellin transform
D_{b}(N,\mu_{F}^{2})
. Our aim is to assess the impact of the
NNLL soft-gluon resummation for the initial condition, as well as the size of
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})
terms in the latter.
We will always employ NNLL DGLAP evolution, and we will set the following values for the bottom mass and the factorisation scale:

m=4.7\,{\rm GeV},\qquad\mu_{F}=100\,{\rm GeV}.

(28)

For all results presented in this section we will set
\mu_{0F}=\mu_{0}
.

4
Conclusions

In this work, we have obtained results for the
b
-quark fragmentation function including NNLL soft-gluon resummation for the perturbative initial conditions matched with the NNLO result.

Following the approach of
Cacciari:2001cw
, we have performed the resummation of soft emission logarithms for the Mellin-transformed initial condition.
First, we have identified a term in the process-dependent component of the NNLL resummed initial condition that was omitted in previous works. Note that the inclusion of this term makes the resummed result fully consistent with the exact NNLO perturbative calculation and with the DGLAP evolution.
Moreover we have computed the Mellin transform of the full NNLO initial condition, and obtained its analytic continuation to the complex plane of the variable
N
, Mellin-conjugate to the momentum fraction
z
, which was not available in the existing literature. Finally we have considered the coupled evolution of the quark and gluon FFs.

We find that NNLL resummation improves the predictions obtained by evolving initial conditions at NNLO. Their behaviour is regular across the relevant range of the Mellin variable
N
, similarly to what happens in the NLO+NLL case. The inclusion of NNLO initial conditions provides a sizeable stabilisation of resummed predictions in
z
space, and has a moderate, yet appreciable effect on the
evolved fragmentation function. Our work makes it possible to include the NNLO+NNLL initial conditions, convolved with a suitable short-distance cross section, in a wide range of phenomenological applications related to heavy-hadron production at colliders and in particular at the LHC.
The perturbative fragmentation functions are available via LHAPDF grids and can be requested to the authors. An easy-to-use computer code implementing the NNLO+NNLL initial conditions supplemented by NNLO DGLAP evolution is in preparation.

Appendix A
Calculation of the resummed initial condition for
D_{b}

In this Appendix we describe in detail the calculation of
the resummed
b
fragmentation function at the initial scale
\mu_{0F}
, Eq. (
10
):

D_{b}^{\rm res}(N,\mu_{0F}^{2})=g_{0}\left(\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}),\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}}\right)\exp\left[F\left(\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}),\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\lambda\right)\right],

(31)

where

\lambda=b_{0}\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})\log N,

(32)

to NNLL accuracy. In this expression,
\alpha_{s}=\alpha_{s}^{(n_{\ell})}
is defined in the
n_{\ell}=n_{f}-1
renormalization scheme:

\mu^{2}\frac{d\alpha_{s}(\mu^{2})}{d\mu^{2}}=\beta(\alpha_{s})=-b_{0}\alpha_{s}^{2}-b_{1}\alpha_{s}^{3}-b_{2}\alpha_{s}^{4}+{\mathcal{O}}(\alpha_{s}^{5}),

(33)

where

\displaystyle b_{0}

\displaystyle=b_{0}(n_{\ell})=\frac{11C_{A}-4T_{f}n_{\ell}}{12\pi},

(34)

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle=b_{1}(n_{\ell})=\frac{17C_{A}^{2}-10C_{A}T_{f}n_{\ell}-6C_{F}T_{f}n_{\ell}}{24\pi^{2}},

(35)

\displaystyle b_{2}

\displaystyle=b_{2}(n_{\ell})=\frac{1}{128\pi^{3}}\left(2857-\frac{5033}{9}n_{\ell}+\frac{325}{27}n_{\ell}^{2}\right).

(36)

The renormalization scale
\mu_{0}
is taken to be
different in general from the initial factorization scale
\mu_{0F}
. Both are taken of the order of the heavy quark mass
m
.

We first consider the exponent
F
. We have

\displaystyle F\left(\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}),\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\lambda\right)=\int_{0}^{1}dz\,\frac{z^{N-1}-1}{1-z}f\left(\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}),\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\lambda_{z}\right)

(37)

\displaystyle f\left(\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}),\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\lambda_{z}\right)=\int^{\mu_{0F}^{2}}_{m^{2}(1-z)^{2}}\frac{d\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\,A(\alpha_{s}(\mu^{2}))+H(\alpha_{s}(m^{2}(1-z)^{2})),

(38)

where

\lambda_{z}=-b_{0}\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})\log(1-z).

(39)

To NNLL accuracy,

\displaystyle A(\alpha_{s})=A_{1}\frac{\alpha_{s}^{\rm NNLL}}{\pi}+A_{2}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}^{\rm NLL}}{\pi}\right)^{2}+A_{3}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}^{\rm LL}}{\pi}\right)^{3},

(40)

where

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=C_{F}

(41)

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=C_{F}\left[\frac{5}{3}\pi b_{0}+C_{A}\left(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}\right)\right]

(42)

\displaystyle A_{3}

\displaystyle=C_{F}\Bigg\{-\frac{\pi^{2}b_{0}^{2}}{3}+\pi b_{0}\left[\left(\frac{55}{16}-3\zeta_{3}\right)C_{F}+\left(\frac{253}{72}-\frac{5\pi^{2}}{18}+\frac{7\zeta_{3}}{2}\right)C_{A}\right]

\displaystyle+\left[\left(-\frac{605}{192}+\frac{11\zeta_{3}}{4}\right)C_{A}C_{F}+\left(-\frac{7}{18}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{18}-\frac{11\zeta_{3}}{4}+\frac{11\pi^{4}}{720}\right)C_{A}^{2}\right]\Bigg\},

(43)

and

H(\alpha_{s})=H_{1}\frac{\alpha_{s}^{\rm NLL}}{\pi}+H_{2}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}^{\rm LL}}{\pi}\right)^{2},

(44)

where

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=-C_{F}

(45)

\displaystyle H_{2}

\displaystyle=-C_{F}\left[\frac{\pi b_{0}}{9}+C_{A}\left(\frac{9\zeta_{3}}{4}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}-\frac{11}{18}\right)\right]+\frac{C_{F}T_{f}}{54}\left[9\log^{2}\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0F}^{2}}+30\log\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0F}^{2}}+28\right].

(46)

The
\mu^{2}
integration of the
A
term is conveniently performed in the variable

\alpha=\alpha_{s}(\mu^{2})
:

\frac{d\mu^{2}}{\mu^{2}}=\frac{d\alpha}{\beta(\alpha)}.

(47)

To NNLL accuracy,

\displaystyle f\left(\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}),\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\lambda_{z}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{1}}{\pi}\int_{\alpha^{\rm NNLL}_{-}}^{\alpha^{\rm NNLL}_{+}}\frac{d\alpha}{b_{0}\alpha+b_{1}\alpha^{2}+b_{2}\alpha^{3}}+\frac{A_{2}}{\pi^{2}}\int_{\alpha^{\rm NLL}_{-}}^{\alpha^{\rm NLL}_{+}}\frac{d\alpha}{b_{0}+b_{1}\alpha}+\frac{A_{3}}{\pi^{3}}\int_{\alpha^{\rm LL}_{-}}^{\alpha^{\rm LL}_{+}}\frac{\alpha^{2}d\alpha}{b_{0}}

\displaystyle+\frac{H_{1}}{\pi}\alpha_{s}^{\rm NLL}(m^{2}(1-z)^{2})+\frac{H_{2}}{\pi^{2}}\left(\alpha_{s}^{\rm LL}(m^{2}(1-z)^{2})\right)^{2}

(48)

where

\alpha^{\rm N^{k}LL}_{-}=\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0F}^{2});\qquad\alpha^{\rm N^{k}LL}_{+}=\alpha_{s}(m^{2}(1-z)^{2}),

(49)

and

\displaystyle\alpha_{s}^{\rm LL}(\mu^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})}{X(\mu^{2})}

\displaystyle\alpha_{s}^{\rm NLL}(\mu^{2})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{s}^{\rm LL}(\mu^{2})-\frac{b_{1}}{b_{0}}\left[\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})}{X(\mu^{2})}\right]^{2}\log X(\mu^{2})

\displaystyle\alpha_{s}^{\rm NNLL}(\mu^{2})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{s}^{\rm NLL}(\mu^{2})+\left[\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})}{X(\mu^{2})}\right]^{3}\Bigg\{\left(\frac{b_{1}}{b_{0}}\right)^{2}\left[\log^{2}X(\mu^{2})-\log X(\mu^{2})-1+X(\mu^{2})\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{b_{2}}{b_{0}}(1-X(\mu^{2}));\Bigg\}

(50)

\displaystyle X(\mu^{2})

\displaystyle=1+b_{0}\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})\log\frac{\mu^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}}.

(51)

The integrals in eq. (
48
) are easily computed, and the result is an analytic function of
\lambda_{z}
:

f\left(\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}),\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\lambda_{z}\right)=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}f_{p}\lambda_{z}^{p}=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}f_{p}(-b_{0}\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}))^{p}\log^{p}(1-z).

(52)

Hence

F\left(\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}),\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\lambda\right)=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}f_{p}(-b_{0}\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}))^{p}I_{p},

(53)

where

I_{p}=\int_{0}^{1}dz\,\frac{z^{N-1}-1}{1-z}\,\log^{p}(1-z).

(54)

The functions
I_{p}
can be computed by taking derivatives of a generating function:

I_{p}=\lim_{\eta\to 0}\frac{d^{p}}{d\eta^{p}}\,G(\eta),

(55)

where

G(\eta)=\int_{0}^{1}dz\,(z^{N-1}-1)\,(1-z)^{\eta-1}=\frac{\Gamma(N)\Gamma(\eta)}{\Gamma(N+\eta)}-\frac{1}{\eta}=\frac{1}{\eta}\left[\Gamma(1+\eta)N^{-\eta}-1\right]+{\mathcal{O}}(1/N).

(56)

In the last step we have used the Stirling approximation for the
\Gamma
function at large values of its argument, which is the limit we are interested in.
We obtain

I_{p}=\frac{1}{p+1}\sum_{k=0}^{p+1}\binom{p+1}{k}\,\Gamma^{(k)}(1)\,L^{p+1-k}+{\mathcal{O}}(1/N),

(57)

where

L=\log\frac{1}{N}.

(58)

We now observe that

\frac{1}{p+1}\,k!\,\binom{p+1}{k}\,L^{p+1-k}=-\frac{d^{k}}{dL^{k}}\int_{0}^{1-\frac{1}{N}}dz\,\frac{\log^{p}(1-z)}{1-z}.

(59)

Replacing eq. (
59
) in (
57
) we obtain

I_{p}=-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma^{(k)}(1)}{k!}\frac{d^{k}}{dL^{k}}\int_{0}^{1-\frac{1}{N}}dz\,\frac{\log^{p}(1-z)}{1-z}+{\mathcal{O}}(1/N).

(60)

Note that the sum has been extended to
\infty
because all derivatives of order
p+2
and higher vanish.
We have therefore

\displaystyle F\left(\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}),\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\lambda\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}f_{p}(-b_{0}\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}))^{p}I_{p}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}f_{p}(-b_{0}\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0})^{2})^{p}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma^{(k)}(1)}{k!}\frac{d^{k}}{dL^{k}}\int_{0}^{1-\frac{1}{N}}dz\,\frac{\log^{p}(1-z)}{1-z}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma^{(k)}(1)}{k!}\frac{d^{k}}{dL^{k}}\int_{0}^{1-\frac{1}{N}}dz\,\frac{f(\lambda_{z})}{1-z}.

(61)

The
z
integration is conveniently performed in the variable
\lambda_{z}
:

d\lambda_{z}=b_{0}\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})\frac{dz}{1-z};\qquad 0<\lambda_{z}<\lambda.

(62)

Furthermore

\frac{d^{k}}{dL^{k}}=(-b_{0}\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}))^{k}\frac{d^{k}}{d\lambda^{k}}.

(63)

We find

F\left(\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}),\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\lambda\right)=-\frac{1}{b_{0}\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma^{(k)}(1)}{k!}(-b_{0}\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}))^{k}\frac{d^{k}}{d\lambda^{k}}\int_{0}^{\lambda}d\lambda_{z}\,f(\lambda_{z}).

(64)

An even simpler expression is obtained by separating off the first term in the sum, which is the only one which requires an integration:

\displaystyle F\left(\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}),\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\lambda\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{b_{0}\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})}\int_{0}^{\lambda}d\lambda_{z}\,f(\lambda_{z})+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma^{(k)}(1)}{k!}(-b_{0}\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}))^{k-1}\frac{d^{k}}{d\lambda^{k}}\int_{0}^{\lambda}d\lambda_{z}\,f(\lambda_{z})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{b_{0}\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})}\int_{0}^{\lambda}d\lambda_{z}\,f(\lambda_{z})+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma^{(k+1)}(1)}{(k+1)!}(-b_{0}\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}))^{k}\frac{d^{k}}{d\lambda^{k}}f(\lambda).

(65)

To NNLL accuracy, only the terms
k=0,1
are relevant in the sum. We therefore obtain our final expression

F\left(\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}),\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\lambda\right)=-\frac{1}{b_{0}\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})}\int_{0}^{\lambda}d\lambda_{z}\,f(\lambda_{z})+\Gamma^{(1)}(1)f(\lambda)-\frac{\Gamma^{(2)}(1)}{2}b_{0}\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})\frac{d}{d\lambda}f(\lambda)+\rm N^{3}LL

(66)

which can be cast in the form of an expansion in powers of
\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})
at fixed
\lambda
:

F\left(\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2}),\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\lambda\right)=\frac{1}{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})}g_{1}(\lambda)+g_{2}(\lambda)+\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0}^{2})g_{3}(\lambda)+\rm N^{3}LL

(67)

(we have omitted the dependence of
g_{2},g_{3}
on
\frac{m^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}},\frac{\mu_{0F}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}}
for simplicity.)

Constant terms, i.e.
N
-independent terms, are not controlled by Sudakov resummation. Note that, because the expansion of

starts at order

, we have

\displaystyle g_{1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=g_{1}^{(2)}\lambda^{2}+g_{1}^{(3)}\lambda^{3}+{\mathcal{O}}(\lambda^{4})

Appendix B
Analitically-continued Mellin transforms for the

In this Appendix, we report on the computation of the analitically-continued Mellin transforms of
the
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})
(NNLO) initial conditions, for all fragmenting partons. These are necessary for the inversion of the expressions obtained in Mellin space back to
z

an inversion is performed using the Talbot-path method
doi:10.1002/nme.995

B.3
Terms with the Nielsen functions
\mathbf{S}_{1,2}(-z)
and
\mathbf{S}_{1,2}(z^{2})

The function
\mathbf{S}_{1,2}(z)
is defined as

\mathbf{S}_{1,2}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\log^{2}(1-t)}{t}dt\,,

(87)

from which it trivially follows that

\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{1,2}(z)=\frac{dS_{1,2}(z)}{dz}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\log^{2}(1-z)}{z}.

(88)

Given the relation between the Mellin transform of a function and of its derivative,

\mathcal{M}\left[{f(z)}\right](N)=\frac{f(1)-\mathcal{M}\left[{f^{\prime}(z)}\right](N+1)}{N}\,,

(89)

and the values

\mathbf{S}_{1,2}(1)=\zeta(3),\qquad\mathbf{S}_{1,2}(-1)=\frac{\zeta(3)}{8}\,,

(90)

we then have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\left[{\mathbf{S}_{1,2}(z)}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta(3)-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{M}\left[{\log^{2}(1-z)}\right](N)}{N}\,,

(91)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\left[{\mathbf{S}_{1,2}(-z)}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{\zeta(3)}{8}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{M}\left[{\log^{2}(1+z)}\right](N)}{N}\,.

(92)

By using

\mathcal{M}\left[{\log^{2}(1-z)}\right](N)=\frac{S_{1}^{2}(N)+S_{2}(N)}{N}\,,

(93)

the first equation reads

\mathcal{M}\left[{\mathbf{S}_{1,2}(z)}\right]=\frac{\zeta(3)}{N}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{S_{1}^{2}(N)+S_{2}(N)}{N^{2}}\,,

(94)

which agrees with Ref.
Blumlein:1998if
.
For
\mathbf{S}_{1,2}(-z)
, we have instead performed the Mellin transform of
\log^{2}(1+z)
using the
expansion shown in Ref.
Blumlein:2000hw
.

Finally, concerning the Mellin transform of
\mathbf{S}_{1,2}(z^{2})
, we have exploited the relation

\mathcal{M}\left[{f(x^{2})}\right](N)=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{M}\left[{f(x)}\right]\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)\,.

(95)

to rewrite
\mathcal{M}\left[{\mathbf{S}_{1,2}(z^{2})}\right]
in terms of
\mathcal{M}\left[{\mathbf{S}_{1,2}(z)}\right]
.

B.4
Terms involving functions of
|2z-1|

The functions

\displaystyle A_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|2z-1|,

(96)

\displaystyle A_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textrm{ArcTanh}\left(|2z-1|\right)

(97)

appear in the initial conditions of Refs.
Melnikov:2004bm
;
Mitov:2004du
, specifically in
d_{g}^{(2)}
. The Mellin transform of
A_{1}
can be easily computed:

\mathcal{M}\left[{A_{1}(z)}\right](N)=\frac{(N-1)}{N(N+1)}+2\frac{2^{-N}}{N(N+1)}.

(98)

Despite its rather simple form, a problem arises when the inverse Mellin transform is considered,

\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}dN\mathcal{M}\left[{A_{1}(z)}\right](N)\,z^{-N}=\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}dN\frac{(N-1)}{N(N+1)}\,z^{-N}+2\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}dN\frac{(2z)^{-N}}{N(N+1)}\,.

(99)

From Eq. (
99
), in particular the second term on the r.h.s., one immediately sees
that the integration contour cannot be closed on the left part of the complex plane
if
z>1/2
, rather it must be closed on the right, only for this specific term. This poses a practical problem when the inversion
is performed numerically, as in our case.
However, since the constant
c
must be larger than the real part of the left-most pole of the integrand
(in this case
c>0
), when the integration contour is closed on the right it does not encircle any pole, hence its
contribution is zero. Therefore, a practical solution is to keep the second term on the r.h.s. of
Eq. (
99
) only when
z<1/2
.

Moving on to
A_{2}
, its Mellin transform can be obtained semi-analytically.
First, while both
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
have a discontinuous derivative at
z=1/2
, their difference,

A_{\rm diff}(z)=A_{2}(z)-A_{1}(z)\,,

(100)

is smooth for
z\in(0,1)
, while at the endpoints it has logarithmic singularities. Subtracting the singularities
(and imposing that the remainder vanishes for
z=1/2
), one gets the function

A_{\rm reg}(z)=A_{\rm diff}(z)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\log(z)+\log(1-z)\right)+\log(2)\,,

(101)

which is now smooth
z\in[0,1]
, is symmetric around
z=1/2
, and it is normalised such that
A_{\rm reg}(1/2)=0
.
A_{\rm reg}(z)

can now be fitted with the functional form

A_{\rm reg}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{k_{\rm max}}c_{k}\left(z(1-z)\right)^{k},

(102)

The values of the coefficient
c_{k}
, obtained with
k_{\rm max}=7
, are reported in Tab.
2
.
In this way the Mellin transform of each term entering the sum can be expressed in terms of the Euler Beta function:

\int_{0}^{1}dzz^{N-1+p}(1-z)^{p}=\mathrm{B}(N+p,p+1)\,.

(103)

To summarise, we rewrite the function
A_{2}(z)
in the form

A_{2}(z)=A_{1}(z)-\frac{1}{2}\left(\log(z)+\log(1-z)\right)-\log(2)+\sum_{k=0}^{k_{\rm max}}c_{k}\left(z(1-z)\right)^{k}\,,

(104)

where the Mellin transform for all terms on the r.h.s. can now be easily computed.

B.5
Terms involving
{\rm Li}_{2}\left(\frac{2z-1}{z}\right)

We will consider the Mellin transforms of the following expressions:

\displaystyle F_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Li}_{2}\left(\frac{2z-1}{z}\right),

(105)

\displaystyle F_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Li}_{2}\left(\frac{2z-1}{z}\right)\log z\,,

(106)

\displaystyle F_{3}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Li}_{2}\left(\frac{2z-1}{z}\right)\log(1-z).

(107)

We will focus on the case for
F_{1}
. This function is divergent at
z\to 0
, therefore we proceed like in sec.
B.4
,
exposing the singular behaviour in this region. However, at variance with what we did there, the remainder is bounded but its derivative
is still divergent both at
z\to 0
and
z\to 1
. These terms have also to be subtracted, in order to have a regular function

{F_{1}}_{\rm reg}
to fit. We call
{F_{1}}_{\rm sing}^{0}
the singular part of
F_{1}
at
z\to 0
,
and
{F_{1}}_{d-\rm sing}^{0}
,
{F_{1}}_{d-\rm sing}^{1}
the contributions which make the derivative of

F_{1}-{F_{1}}_{\rm sing}^{0}
divergent in
z\to 0
and
z\to 1
respectively
2
2

2

Constants and regular terms may also be included in
{F_{1}}_{\rm sing}
,
{F_{1}}_{d-\rm sing}
.

. Thus we have

F_{1}(z)={F_{1}}_{\rm sing}^{0}(z)+{F_{1}}_{d-\rm sing}^{0}(z)+{F_{1}}_{d-\rm sing}^{1}(z)+{F_{1}}_{\rm reg}(z)\,,

(108)

where

\displaystyle{F_{1}}_{\rm sing}^{0}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\frac{1}{2}\log^{2}z\,,

(109)

\displaystyle{F_{1}}_{d-\rm sing}^{0}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z-2z\log z\,,

(110)

\displaystyle{F_{1}}_{d-\rm sing}^{1}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z+(1-z)\log(1-z)\,.

(111)

The remainder
{F_{1}}_{\rm reg}
, which we have chosen such that
{F_{1}}_{\rm reg}(0)=0
, can now be fit with a polinomial in
z

{F_{1}}_{\rm reg}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{k_{\rm max}}d_{k}z^{k}\,,

(112)

where we have used
k_{\rm max}=12
. The coefficients
d_{k}
are shown in tab.
2
.

For what concerns the functions
F_{2}
and
F_{3}
, the terms in Eqs.
109
-
111
are simple enough
so that the Mellin transform of their product with either
\log z
or
\log(1-z)
can be computed with
standard methods. The Mellin transform of the corresponding regular part is also trivial, since it amounts just to the
Mellin transform of
\log z
or
\log(1-z)
multiplied by a power of
z
.

B.6
Mellin transform of
{\rm Li}_{3}\left(\frac{2z-1}{z}\right)+{\rm Li}_{3}\left(\frac{2z-1}{1-z}\right)

The combination

G(z)={\rm Li}_{3}\left(\frac{2z-1}{z}\right)+{\rm Li}_{3}\left(\frac{2z-1}{1-z}\right)

(113)

appears in the gluon initial condition. Exploiting its symmetry around
z=1/2
, we can proceed
in a similar way as in Sects.
B.4
and
B.5
. The function
G
is smooth for
z\in(0,1)
, thus we proceed
by extracting the singular parts of the function, and of the derivative of the remainder, when
z\to 0
and
z\to 1

G(z)=G_{\rm sing}^{0}(z)+G_{\rm sing}^{1}(z)+G_{d-\rm sing}^{0}(z)+G_{d-\rm sing}^{1}(z)+G_{\rm reg}(z)\,,

(114)

The singular parts,
written so that the remainder satisfies s
G_{\rm reg}(1/2)=0
, read

\displaystyle G_{\rm sing}^{0}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\log(z)}{6}\left[\pi^{2}+\log^{2}(z)\right]+L_{2}\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-L_{2}+\frac{L_{2}^{2}}{6}\right)\,,

(115)

\displaystyle G_{d-\rm sing}^{0}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z\log^{2}(z)\,,

(116)

\displaystyle G_{\rm sing}^{1}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{\rm sing}^{0}(1-z)\,,

(117)

\displaystyle G_{d-\rm sing}^{1}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{d-\rm sing}^{0}(1-z)\,,

(118)

where
L_{2}=\log(2)
. Following what we did in Sect.
B.4
(see Eq. (
102
)), we
fit the regular part
G_{\rm reg}(z)
with the functional form

A_{\rm reg}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{k_{\rm max}}e_{k}\left(z(1-z)\right)^{k},

where
k_{\rm max}=7

B.7
Validation

Validation plots in

(left) and

We conclude this Appendix by presenting some validation plots for the analytically-continued Mellin transforms of the
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})

initial conditions. For all the cases presented in this Appendix,
the fitting parameters have been chosen so that the fitted function differs from the exact one by no more than few parts in
10^{5}
.
Our validation is both in
N
and in
z
space, setting
\mu_{0}\neq m
(specifically,
m=4.7\,{\rm GeV}
,
\mu_{0}=10\,{\rm GeV}
) in order to expose all terms
of the initial conditions. Starting from
N
space, we compare in the left plot of Fig.
4

results obtained by computing numerically
the Mellin transform of the expressions in Refs.
Melnikov:2004bm
;
Mitov:2004du
, by employing a Gaussian integrator,
with the analytic Mellin transforms obtained as described above.
In the upper panel of that figure, we show as lines (symbols) the absolute value of the numeric (analytic) Mellin transforms
of the initial conditions. In the lower panel, we plot the quantity

\Delta d_{X}^{\rm ana-num}=\left|\frac{d_{X}^{\rm ana}-d_{X}^{\rm num}}{d_{X}^{\rm ana}+d_{X}^{\rm num}}\right|\,,

(120)

where
X=b,\overline{b},g,q
,
i.e. the relative difference between the numeric and analytic Mellin transforms. From this panel we appreciate that
the difference between the numeric and analytic Mellin transforms never exceeds
10^{-4}
, with the exception
of
D_{\overline{b}}
at very large
N
.

The validation in
z
space, shown in the right plot of Fig.
4
, presents a very similar layout,
with the only relevant change being that the numeric (analytic) Mellin transforms of the initial conditions
are replaced with their exact expression (inverse Mellin transform) in
x
space. Again, the relative difference does not
exceed
10^{-4}
, except for those points where the initial conditions, hence the denominator in Eq. (
120
), vanish, or
in the region
z\to 1
. Here it has to be stressed that we do not plot the contribution coming from Dirac delta’s
at
z=1
or the endpoint of plus distributions, which live exactly at
z=1
, which we deem responsible for such a discrepancy. Indeed, we
have observed similar discrepancies also in the case of very simple distributions, e.g.
\left[\frac{\log(1-z)}{1-z}\right]_{+}
.

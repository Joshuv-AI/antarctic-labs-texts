Molecular Fraction in the Galactic Center: The Central Molecular and HI Zones 2022
Yoshiaki Sofue

Abstract
By mapping the molecular fraction of the Galactic Center (GC), we quantitatively address the question of how much molecular and central the CMZ (Central Molecular Zone) is. For this purpose we analyze the CO and HI-line archival data, and determine the column- (surface-) and volume-molecular fractions, f mol Σ f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma} and f mol ρ f_{\rm mol}^{\rho} , which are the ratio of column-mass density of H 2 projected on the sky to that of total gas (H 2 + HI) from the line intensities, and the ratio of volume-mass densities of H 2 to total gas from the brightness temperature, respectively. It is shown that f mol Σ f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma} is as high as ∼ 0.9 − 0.95 \sim 0.9-0.95 in the CMZ, and f mol ρ f_{\rm mol}^{\rho} is 0.93 − 0.98 0.93-0.98 in the GC Arms I and II attaining the highest value of ∼ 0.98 \sim 0.98 toward Sgr B2. The expanding molecular ring (EMR, or the parallelogram) has a slightly smaller f mol ρ f_{\rm mol}^{\rho} as ∼ 0.9 − 0.93 \sim 0.9-0.93 . We define the CMZ as the region with f mol Σ ≥ 0.8 − 0.9 f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}\geq 0.8-0.9 between the shoulders of plateau-like distribution of H 2 column density from l = − 1 ∘ ​ .1 l=-1^{\circ}.1 to + 1 ∘ ​ .8 +1^{\circ}.8 having Gaussian vertical distribution with a half thickness of ± 0 ∘ ​ .2 \pm 0^{\circ}.2 . The CMZ is embedded in the Central HI Zone (CHZ), which is defined as an HI disc between l ∼ − 2 ∘ l\sim-2^{\circ} and + 2 ∘ ​ .5 +2^{\circ}.5 , b = − 0 ∘ ​ .5 b=-0^{\circ}.5 and + 0 ∘ ​ .5 +0^{\circ}.5 . Based on the analysis, we discuss the origin of CMZ and interstellar physics such as the volume filling factors of molecular and HI gases inferred from the difference between f mol Σ f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma} and f mol ρ f_{\rm mol}^{\rho} .



1
INTRODUCTION

The Central Molecular Zone (CMZ) was introduced as the ”strong concentration of gas of about 200-pc radius” around the Galactic Center (GC)
[
Morris & Serabyn 1996
]
.
Decades later today, the exact range of the CMZ is still not well defined, referred to as ”a ring-like accumulation of molecular gas in the innermost few hundred parsecs of the Milky Way”
[
Henshaw et al. 2022
]
.
Some authors think it even smaller area
[
Tsuboi et al. 1999
,
Tokuyama et al. 2019
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to quantitatively address the question, how much molecular and central the CMZ is, and to quantitatively define the CMZ.
For such a purpose, the molecular fraction,
f_{\rm mol}
, defined by the ratio of the molecular hydrogen (H
2
gas) density to the total gas density (HI and H
2
gases) provides the most direct information along with various constraints on the physical and chemical condition of the ISM (interstellar medium)
[
Elmegreen 1993
]
.
Since it is a hybrid measure of the gas pressure (density and temperature), metallicity (dust abundance) and UV (ultra violet) radiation pressure, the fraction may be used to determine one of these quantities, given the other two are fixed.
As a first approximation to the discussion of the relative variation of molecular fraction in the GC region, we take the conservative view that the conversion factors from the CO and HI lines to the column densities of H
2
and H atoms are constant.

Analyses of large-scale distribution of the molecular fraction in nearby spiral galaxies have been extensively obtained by comparing CO and HI line mapping data, which generally indicate monotonic increase of
f_{\rm mol}
toward the galactic centers

[
Sofue et al. 1995
,
Honma et al. 1995
,
Kuno et al. 1995
,
Hidaka & Sofue 2002
,
Tanaka et al. 2014
]
.
More resolved behavior has been obtained in the Milky Way, where the molecular fraction becomes as high as
\sim 0.9
to unity inside several hundred pc of the Galactic Center
[
Sofue & Nakanishi 2016
,
Koda et al. 2016
,
Sofue 2022
]
.
Three dimensional analysis of the CO and HI discs of the Milky Way has also shown a steep vertical increase and saturation of the molecular fraction toward the galactic plane
[
Imamura & Sofue 1997
,
Sofue & Nakanishi 2016
]
.
The high molecular fraction in the central region seems ubiquitous in spiral galaxies, except for M33’s center showing values as low as
f_{\rm mol}\sim 0.2-0.3

[
Tosaki et al. 2011
]
.

In this paper, we investigate the three dimensional distribution of the molecular fraction in the central several degrees of the Milky Way.
We measure the extent and size of the high-
f_{\rm mol}
saturated region, and re-define the CMZ using the quantified parameters.
We assume that the GC distance of the Sun is 8.18 kpc
[
Gravity Collaboration et al. 2019
]
.

2.2
Fore- and background disc elimination by velocity cut (DEV)

The integrated intensity of radio line emission from the GC region around
l\sim 0^{\circ}
is significantly contaminated by the emission by the fore- and background Galactic disc rotating in the transverse direction to the line sight, where the radial velocity is degenerated to ’local velocities’ at
|V_{\rm LSR}|\leq\sim 50{\rm~km~s^{-1}~}
.
The fore- and background intensity of CO line amounts to the same order as that from the GC disc, and HI emission is stronger than the GC emission by an order of magnitude.

This makes it difficult to measure such quantities properly arising from the GC, particularly in the HI line.
The difficulty can be eased by applying the ’disc-elimination by velocity cut’ (DEV) method as used in our previous paper for a study of the three-dimensional structure of the central molecular zone (CMZ)
[
Sofue 2022
]
.
The method simply eliminates low-velocity emission from the integration, or uses only the emission at radial velocities higher than a threshold value,
||V_{\rm LSR}||>V_{\rm cut}{\rm~km~s^{-1}~}
.
An obtained map will be called ’DEV50’ map, if
V_{\rm cut}=50{\rm~km~s^{-1}~}
.

In this paper, we adopt
V_{\rm cut}=50
km s
-1
as read from observed longitude-velocity diagrams (LVD) of the CO and HI line emissions, where the fore- and background disc components are seen as numerous bright horizontal belts in the
(l,V_{\rm LSR})
plane (see the lVD in the later section).

By the DEV procedure, the emission from a fan-shaped area near the
Y
axis (Sun-GC line) between
V_{\rm LSR}=\pm 50{\rm~km~s^{-1}~}
on the radial velocity field are eliminated from the integration.
The eliminated-fan angle is
\theta\sim\pm 15^{\circ}
for
|V_{\rm LSR}|=50
and
V_{\rm rot}\sim 200{\rm~km~s^{-1}~}
, which means that about
\sim 15-20\%
of the whole galactic disc is not counted.
As this also underestimates the emission from the GC disc itself with the same proportion, the total mass of the GC disc may be obtained by multiplying a factor of
\sim 1.2
to the calculated mass from the DEV map.

Fortunately, this correction is not needed to derive the molecular fraction, because it contains only the ratio of HI and CO intensities, so that the over-elimination (underestimation) of both components cancels out.

3
Molecular Fraction

3.1
Column molecular fraction
f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}
from the integrated intensity

Because the brightness temperature of HI emission at
|V_{\rm LSR}|\geq 50\ {\rm~km~s^{-1}~}
is observed to be less than
\sim 30
K in the analyzed region (see the longitude-velocity diagrams in the later subsection), while the excitation temperature is considered to be
T_{\rm ex}\sim 140
K in the disc and still higher in the GC
[
Sofue 2018
,
Pohl et al. 2022
]
, we here assume that the HI line is optically thin.
This makes it simple to convert the intensity to the column density of HI gas.
As to the CO line, which is usually optical thick, we make use of the empirical conversion relation as described below.

The molecular fraction for the column mass density projected on the sky, hereafter column molecular fraction, is defined by

f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}{=\frac{\Sigma_{\rm H_{2}}}{\Sigma_{\rm HI}+\Sigma_{\rm H_{2}}}}=\frac{1}{1+\eta_{\Sigma}}.

(1)

Here,
\eta_{i}
is the ratio of column density of HI gas,
\Sigma_{\rm HI}
(or column number density
N_{\rm HI}
) to that of H
2
gas,
\Sigma_{\rm H_{2}}
(or
2N_{\rm H_{2}}
), which is related to the ratio of HI to
12
CO line intensities using the conversion factors as

\eta_{\Sigma}=\frac{\Sigma_{\rm HI}}{\Sigma_{\rm H_{2}}}=\frac{N_{\rm HI}}{2N_{\rm H_{2}}}=\frac{X_{\rm HI}I_{\rm HI}}{2X_{\rm CO}I_{\rm CO}}.

(2)

The conversion factor for the HI line is given by
[
Kraus 1966
]

X_{\rm HI}=1.823\times 10^{18}{\rm Hcm^{-2}[K~km~s^{-1}]^{-1}},

(3)

assuming that the HI gas is optically thin, because the observed brightness temperature (
<
several tens K) is sufficiently lower than the excitation temperature (
\sim 200
K) measured in the GC
[
Pohl et al. 2022
]
.
For the CO to H
2
conversion, we adopt the empirical relation including the galacto-centric distance
R
, which considers the metallicity gradient (increasing toward GC)
[
Arimoto et al. 1996
]
,

{\rm log}\frac{X_{\rm CO}}{X_{\rm CO,\odot}}=0.41\left(\frac{R-R_{0}}{r_{e}}\right).

(4)

Assuming a local conversion factor of

X_{\rm CO,\odot}=2.0\times 10^{20}
H
2
cm
-2
[K km s
-1
]
-1
[
Bolatto et al. 2013
,
Sofue & Kohno 2020
]
and scale radius of the Galactic disc
r_{e}=5.1
kpc for
R_{0}=8.2
kpc
[
Sofue 2022
]
,
we have

X_{\rm CO}({\rm GC})=0.51\times 10^{20}\ {\rm H_{2}\ cm^{-2}[K\ km\ s^{-1}]^{-1}},

(5)

so that

\frac{X_{\rm HI}}{2X_{\rm CO}}=1.79\times 10^{-2},

(6)

and

\eta_{\Sigma}=1.79\times 10^{-2}\frac{I_{\rm HI}}{I_{\rm CO}}.

(7)

3.3
Volume molecular fraction
f_{\rm mol}^{\rho}
from LVD

In order to examine the molecular fraction in the molecular Arms and clouds, we make use of longitude-velocity diagrams (LVDs) of HI and CO brightness temperatures
T_{\rm b}
, from which we calculate the volume-density molecular fraction (hereafter, volume molecular fraction).
LVDs were constructed using the
12
CO and HI cube data smoothed between
b=-0^{\circ}.1
and
+0^{\circ}.1
in order to cover the GC Arms I and II as well as the molecular complex around Sgr B2.
Fig.
5
shows the obtained LVDs in HI, CO, their overlay, and volume molecular fraction.

The molecular Arms and clouds are now clearly visible and resolved, which were not recognized in the integrated intensity maps on the sky.
Prominent features are the bright CO emitting region around Sgr B2 and B1 at
(l,V_{\rm LSR})\sim(0^{\circ}.5,60{\rm~km~s^{-1}~})
, the tilted LV ridge representing the molecular Arms running from
(l,V_{\rm LSR})\sim(1^{\circ}.5,120{\rm~km~s^{-1}~})
to
(-1^{\circ},-200{\rm~km~s^{-1}~})
, and the oval or parallelogram showing the expanding molecular ring or cylinder (EMR or EMC) with high non-circular motion of
\pm 150{\rm~km~s^{-1}~}
.

The volume molecular fraction is written in terms of the brightness temperature,
T_{\rm b}
, instead of the integrated intensity
I
as follows:

f_{\rm mol}^{\rho}{=\frac{\rho_{\rm H_{2}}}{\rho_{\rm HI}+\rho_{\rm H_{2}}}}=\frac{1}{1+\eta_{\rho}}.

(8)

and

\eta_{\rho}=\frac{\rho_{\rm HI}}{\rho_{\rm H_{2}}}=\frac{n_{\rm HI}}{2n_{\rm H_{2}}},

(9)

where
\rho
and
n
denote the volume mass density and number density of the gas, respectively.
Let us recall that the gas density is related to the column density and brightness temperature through

n_{i}=\frac{dN_{i}}{dx}=\frac{dN_{i}}{dv}\frac{dv}{dx}{=\frac{d(X_{i}\int T_{b,i}dv)}{dv}\frac{dv}{dx}}=X_{i}{T_{\rm b}}_{i}\frac{dv}{dx},

(10)

where
X_{i}
is constant with
i=
CO or HI.
Then, the HI to H
2
mass ratio is approximated by

\eta_{\rho}=\frac{n_{\rm HI}}{2n_{\rm H_{2}}}\simeq 1.79\times 10^{-2}\frac{{T_{\rm b}}_{\rm HI}}{{T_{\rm b}}_{\rm CO}}\frac{[dv/dx]_{\rm HI}}{[dv/dx]_{\rm CO}}.

(11)

The radial velocity of HI and CO can be written as

v_{i}(x)={V_{\rm rot}}_{i}\rm{~sin~}\theta_{i}+\delta v_{i},

(12)

where
i=
HI or CO,
{V_{\rm rot}}_{i}
is the rotation velocity,
\delta v_{i}
is the velocity dispersion, and
\theta_{i}
is the angle of the gaseous motion and line of sight.
We may safely assume that the rotation velocities of HI and CO are equal and the velocity dispersion is much smaller than
V_{\rm rot}
and almost constant along the line of sight.
Thus, we have

\frac{dv}{dx}({\rm HI})\simeq\frac{dv}{dx}({\rm CO}),

(13)

or

\eta_{\rho}\simeq 1.79\times 10^{-2}\frac{{T_{\rm b}}_{\rm HI}}{{T_{\rm b}}_{\rm CO}}.

(14)

This formula is used to calculate the distribution of
f_{\rm mol}^{\rho}
in the LVD.

4
Discussion: How central and molecular is CMZ?

4.1
Comparison with interstellar clouds and disc gas

The presently detected high
f_{\rm mol}
and low
\eta
in the CMZ may be compared with the values for interstellar clouds such as observed in
\rho
Oph with

\eta_{\Sigma}=N_{\rm HI}/(2N_{\rm H_{2}})=1/112\ (f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}=0.99)

[
Minn 1981
]
,

\eta_{\Sigma}\sim 10^{-2}\ (f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}\sim 0.99)
of a GMC
[
sato+1992
]
,
or
\eta_{\Sigma}\sim 0.1-0.15\ (f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}\sim 0.85-0.9)
of Sgr B2
[
Lang et al. 2010
]
.
However, the highest value observed toward Sgr B,
f_{\rm mol}^{\rho}=0.99
, in this study is still lower than that obtained in a local dark cloud having an extremely high value of

\eta_{\Sigma}\sim 5-27\times 10^{-4}\ (f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}\sim 0.997-0.999)

[
Goldsmith & Li 2005
]
.
On the other hand, the CMZ’s
f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}
is far greater than the values observed in HI clouds as
f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}\sim 0.2-0.5

[
Nakanishi et al. 2020
]
, and those along sight lines toward prominent radio sources through the arms (
f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}\sim 0.7-0.9
) and inter-arm regions (
\sim 0.1-0.3
)
[
Winkel et al. 2017
]
.

4.2
Definition of CMZ: Isolated island

Using the spatial distribution of the surface-mass molecular fraction in Fig.
2
and
3
, we define the CMZ as a region with a plateaued distribution of high molecular fraction between the steeply increasing gradients in both sides of the center at
f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}\sim 0.8-0.9
.
The region is well defined between the edges at
l=-1^{\circ}.1\ (-157{\rm~pc~})
and
+1^{\circ}.8\ \ (+257{\rm~pc~})
with eye-estimate errors
\pm 0^{\circ}.05
, or the scale radii to be
r_{-}=-157
and
r_{+}=257
pc (
\pm 7
pc).
It is stressed that the positions of the density shoulders exactly coincide with those of
f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}
.

Namely, the CMZ is a region with plateaued distribution of H
2
gas having shoulders both in the column-density (intensity) and molecular fraction profiles.
So, this is the answer to the question of how much central and molecular the CMZ is.
The measured extent of CMZ is thus consistent with that originally introduced
[
Morris & Serabyn 1996
]
.
Also, the plateaued nature indicates that the gas distribution inside CMZ is ring like
[
Henshaw et al. 2022
]
.
A new point to be emphasized by this analysis is that the CMZ is an isolated island in the shallow sea of low density HI that has not sufficient mass to supply the CMZ.

The latitudinal profile of
f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}
is shown in Fig.
8
, which is well represented by a Gaussian profile.
The intensity distribution also has a Gaussian profile in the latitudinal direction
[
Sofue 2022
]
.
From Fig.
8
, the latitudinal extent at a threshold level of
f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}=0.9
is measured to be
z_{\rm 0.9}=0^{\circ}.2
with eye estimate error of
\pm\sim 0^{\circ}.02
.
This height is about equal to the column-density’s scale half thickness
.
So, we define the latitudinal extent of the CMZ by this height,
h_{z}=\pm 0^{\circ}.2
(
\pm 29
pc).

Thus defined CMZ is significantly asymmetric, or lopsided, with respect to Sgr A
∗
(nucleus), being displaced to positive longitude by
\delta l\sim 0^{\circ}.35\ (50{\rm~pc~})
.
It is also offset from the Galactic plane toward the south by
\delta b\sim-0^{\circ}.05\ (7{\rm~pc~})

It is impressive that the CMZ is highly isolated from the surrounding less molecular region, particularly in the wider area plot in Fig.
3
(b) and (d), making an island of molecular gas in the central
\sim 430{\rm~pc~}
.
The CMZ composes a distinguished galactic structure (object) with the highest molecular fraction in the Milky Way.
Despite of the high
f_{\rm mol}
inside, it is surrounded by a low
f_{\rm mol}
region or a gap with
f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}\sim 0.5-0.7
at
l>\sim 2^{\circ}
and
<\sim-1^{\circ}.2
(Fig.

However, the current studies of the Milky Way using the Columbia CO survey
[
Dame et al. 2001
]
and HI survey using the Parkes 64-m telescope survey
[
HI4PI Collaboration et al. 2016
]
at lower resolutions have shown a monotonic increase of
f_{\rm mol}
toward the GC inside
R\leq\sim 2
kpc.
Studies of external spiral galaxies have also shown a monotonic increase of
f_{\rm mol}
toward the centers

[
Sofue et al. 1995
,
Honma et al. 1995
,
Kuno et al. 1995
,
Hidaka & Sofue 2002
,
Nakanishi et al. 2006
,
Tosaki et al. 2011
,
Tanaka et al. 2014
,
Sofue & Nakanishi 2016
,
Koda et al. 2016
]
.

The variable
f_{\rm mol}
outside CMZ has been detected here thanks to the high resolution (
\sim 6
pc), while the current works had effective resolutions of
\sim 100
pc or larger, so that the innermost large variation of
f_{\rm mol}
has not been detected.
It is an interesting question whether the
f_{\rm mol}
-gap around the CMZ is a general phenomenon, or it is specific in the Milky Way’s center.
This will be a subject for higher resolution observations in the CO and HI lines of spiral galaxies in the future.

4.3
Volume-filling factor and the difference of
f_{\rm mol}^{\rho}
and
f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}

The volume-mass molecular fraction was observed to be generally higher than the column-mass molecular fraction, or
f_{\rm mol}^{\rho}>f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}
, in the CMZ.
From Fig.
3
and
6
we approximate the mean values to be
f_{\rm mol}^{\rho}\sim 0.96
and
f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}\sim 0.93
.
The lower
f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}
than
f_{\rm mol}^{\rho}
is attributed to the volume filling factor
\alpha_{\rm H_{2}}
of H
2
and
\alpha_{\rm HI}
of HI gas.

We here assume that each of molecular and HI clouds has a nearly constant density and write the column densities as
N_{\rm HI}\sim\langle\rho_{\rm HI}\langle
and

2N_{\rm H_{2}}=\langle\rho_{\rm H_{z}}\rangle
, where the angle-parentheses denote the average along the line of sight.
We here introduce filling factors
\alpha_{\rm HI}
and
\alpha_{\rm H_{2}}
for HI and molecular clouds and rewrite the average in terms of the filling factors as

\langle\rho_{\rm HI}\rangle=\alpha_{\rm HI}\rho_{\rm HI}

and

\langle\rho_{\rm H_{2}}\rangle=\alpha_{\rm H_{2}}\rho_{\rm H_{2}}
.
Recalling that
\eta_{i}\ll 1
, we obtain

f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}=\frac{1}{1+\eta_{\Sigma}}\simeq 1-\frac{\langle\rho_{\rm HI}\rangle}{\langle\rho_{\rm H_{2}}\rangle}\simeq 1-\frac{\alpha_{\rm HI}\rho_{\rm HI}}{\alpha_{\rm H_{2}}\rho_{\rm H_{2}}}.

(15)

On the other hand we have

f_{\rm mol}^{\rho}\simeq 1-\frac{\rho_{\rm HI}}{\rho_{\rm H_{2}}}.

(16)

So, we obtain

\Delta f=f_{\rm mol}^{\rho}-f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}\sim\left(\frac{\alpha_{\rm H_{2}}}{\alpha_{\rm HI}}-1\right)\frac{\rho_{\rm HI}}{\rho_{\rm H_{2}}}.

(17)

The observed relation

f_{\rm mol}^{\rho}>f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}
indicates

\alpha_{\rm H_{2}}>\alpha_{\rm HI}
.
If we assume

\rho_{\rm HI}/\rho_{\rm H_{2}}\sim 0.01

(e.g.,

n_{\rm HI}\sim 20\ {\rm H\ cm^{-3}},

n_{\rm H_{2}}\sim 10^{3}\ {\rm\ H_{2}\ cm^{-3}}

) for example, the observed

\Delta f\sim 0.03

yields

\alpha_{\rm H_{2}}/\alpha_{\rm HI}\sim 2
.
The CMZ is thus a molecular gas dominant region not only by mass and density, but also by the volume filling factor.

4.4
Origin of high molecular fraction in CMZ

The molecular fraction is determined by the gas pressure
P
, metallicity
Z
, and ultra-violet (UV) radiation field
U
of the interstellar gas
[
Elmegreen 1993
]
.
It can be expressed by a function of the galacto-centric distance
R
, given a Galactic disc model for the radial variation of
P,Z
and
U
.
While
U
suppresses
f_{\rm mol}
,
P
(density) and
Z
act to increase
f_{\rm mol}
, where
Z
doubly affects to increase
f_{\rm mol}
by UV shielding and catalystic formation of H
2
on dust grain surfaces.
It has been shown that the thus calculated
f_{\rm mol}
monotonically increases toward the GC with a scale radius comparable to that of the three quantities of about
R_{e}\sim 5-7
kpc
[
Honma et al. 1995
,
Tanaka et al. 2014
,
Sofue & Nakanishi 2016
,
Koda et al. 2016
]
.

In so far as the exponential variations are assumed for
P
,
U
and
Z
, as in the current works, any model results in a monotonic increase of
f_{\rm mol}
toward the GC.
So, the observed rapid
f_{\rm mol}
variation at
R\sim 200
pc must be due to more local variation of the three quantities.
Among the three parameters, the metallicity
Z
cannot be variable at scale radius less than
\sim 1
kpc, because the time scale of variation is on the order of the Galaxy’s evolution time including the Galactic bulge.
The radiation field
U
is expected to be highest inside CMZ according to the wealth of thermal radio continuum sources indicating a number of HII regions
[
Sofue 1990
,
Sofue 2022
,
Yusef-Zadeh et al. 2022
]
.
This means that the UV field cannot be the cause for depression of
f_{\rm mol}
beyond
R\sim 200
pc.

Therefore, only the parameter that can affect the sudden change in
f_{\rm mol}
at
R\sim 100-200
pc is the pressure
P
which is proportional to
\rho T
with
\rho
and
T
being the ISM density and temperature.
Since the UV field is weaker outside CMZ, the temperature may be lower outside CMZ, which means that the pressure equilibrium between outside and inside CMZ does not hold.
So, only one possible mechanism for
P
to be increased locally inside CMZ is the dynamical confinement of ISM in the deep Galactic potential.

Such dynamical confinement can be obtained by accretion of the gas in the surrounding disc, where
f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}\sim 0.5
so that the gas is half molecular and half in the shallow HI envelope (CHZ), onto the central region by the loss of angular momentum by galactic shock waves in the bar potential
[
Sorensen et al. 1976
,
Roberts et al. 1979
,
Wada & Habe 1995
]
.
The gas between the center and bar ends are swept away to make low gas density region surrounding the accretion disc.

Once it is accumulated in the center, the gas is converted to molecules by the phase transition due to the increase in the density (
\propto P
) and maybe also due to locally high metallicity (
Z
) in the core of the Galactic bulge, unless the radiation field (
U
) is too strong such as in a starburst phase.
The accumulating dense gas composes there a ring in circular rotation as shown by the extensive numerical simulations
[
Li et al. 2015
,
Sormani et al. 2019
,
Tress et al. 2020
]
.
The ring will be sustained for a long time without collapsing by their self-gravity because of the high differential rotation on the order of
\sim 1000\ {\rm kms\ s^{-1}\ kpc^{-1}}
in the deep gravitational potential inside CMZ
[
Sofue 2013
]
.

However, there remains a question whether the observed high
f_{\rm mol}
can be maintained still in such a high shear motion even though it is anticipated by the current
f_{\rm mol}
theories that consider
P,\ Z
and
U
, but do not take into account the dynamics.
In fact, the numerical simulations as above assuming a bar potential in the GC have shown rapid inflow of gas yielding a turbulent gas ring, and accordingly indicate a very low molecular fraction of
f_{\rm mol}\sim 0.55

[
Tress et al. 2020
]
.

4.5
No HI-to-H
2
transition in the CMZ

In such a high
f_{\rm mol}^{\rho}
region as CMZ, the molecular Arms I and II, including Sgr B complex, cannot be produced by the phase change of surrounding HI gas by compression, because the HI reservoir inside CMZ contains far less mass than the arms.
Namely, the HI-to-H
2
phase transition is not the major process to form the molecular gas structures in the GC.
So, the arms can be formed by coagulation of smaller molecular clouds and diffuse H
2
by compression inside the CMZ
[
Koda et al. 2016
]
.

4.6
Central HI Zone (CHZ)

We finally mention about the HI gas distribution from the DEV50 HI maps. The horizontal HI intensity distribution in Fig.
3
shows enrichment of HI gas in GC. In contrast to the CMZ with sharp edges, the HI concentration is milder, continuous from the periphery, and shows a nearly linear increase towards the center in the semi-log plot.

Considering the absorption near Sgr A, the profile may be approximated by an exponential function with gradient of
e
folding radius of
r_{\rm HI}\sim+3^{\circ}\ (\sim 430{\rm~pc~})
on the positive longitude side, and
\sim-2^{\circ}(\sim 285{\rm~pc~})
on the negative longitude side. This ”central HI zone (CHZ)” may constitute an extended outskirt of the CMZ. The CMZ stands as an isolated island in the extended shallow sea of HI.However, the CHZ does not work as a reservoir to supply the gas to CMZ because the density and total mass is too small
[
Sofue 2022
]
.

More global HI concentration in the GC is visible in the DEV50 HI map as the peak in the central few degrees over the global minimum (Fig.
3
(a), Fig.
11
). Such HI enhancement in integrated intensity maps has been not clearly seen in the current presentations, being buried by the fore- and background disc. In the appendix, we demonstrate how efficient the DEV method is to eliminate the HI disc contamination and to quantify the density distribution of HI gas in the CHZ.

We point out that the longitudinal HI distribution is asymmetric with respect to the center in the sense that CHZ is shifted to the west (negative longitude side) by
\sim 1-2^{\circ}
. Interestingly, the HI asymmetry is in the opposite sense to that of the CMZ that is shifted to the east (positive longitudes).

5
Summary

We have obtained a detailed comparison of the CO and HI maps and LV diagrams in the GC region, and showed that the molecular fraction is as high as
f_{\rm mol}^{\rho}\sim 0.9-0.95
in the CMZ.
The highest fraction was observed toward Sgr B2 molecular complex at
f_{\rm mol}^{\rho}\sim 0.98
, and it remains at high values
0.93-0.98
in the GC Arms I and II.
The expanding molecular ring or the parallelogram shows a slightly milder fraction of
f_{\rm mol}^{\rho}\sim 0.9-0.93
, but with an order of magnitude lower gas density than CMZ.
We quantitatively re-defined the CMZ’s location and extent as the region on the sky with the column (projected) molecular fraction at
f_{\rm mol}^{\Sigma}\geq 0.8-0.9
spanning from
l=-1^{\circ}.1
to
+1^{\circ}.8
and
b=-0^{\circ}.2
to
+0^{\circ}.2
, and answered the question how much central and molecular the CMZ is.
We also defined the Central HI zone (CHZ) as an HI enhanced disc from
-2^{\circ}
to
+2^{\circ}.5
and
|b|\leq 0^{\circ}.5
, which envelopes the CMZ.

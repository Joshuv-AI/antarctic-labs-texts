Numerical Stability of Tangents and Adjoints of Implicit Functions
Uwe Naumann

Abstract
We investigate errors in tangents and adjoints of implicit functions resulting from errors in the primal solution due to approximations computed by a numerical solver. Adjoints of systems of linear equations turn out to be unconditionally numerically stable. Tangents of systems of linear equations can become instable as well as both tangents and adjoints of systems of nonlinear equations, which extends to optima of convex unconstrained objectives. Sufficient conditions for numerical stability are derived.



1
Introduction

We consider twice differentiable implicit functions

F:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}:{\bf p}\mapsto{\bf x}=F({\bf p})

(1)

defined by the roots of residuals

R:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}:({\bf x},{\bf p})\mapsto R({\bf x},{\bf p})\;.

(2)

R
is referred to as the primal residual as opposed to tangent and adjoint
residuals to be considered later.
Primal roots of the residual satisfying

R({\bf x},{\bf p})=0

(3)

are assumed to be approximated by numerical solvers

S:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}:{\bf p}\mapsto{\bf x}+\Delta{\bf x}=S({\bf p})

with an absolute error
\Delta{\bf x}
yielding a
relative error
\delta{\bf x}
of norm

\|\delta{\bf x}\|=\frac{\|\Delta{\bf x}\|}{\|{\bf x}\|}=\frac{\|S({\bf p})-F({\bf p})\|}{\|F({\bf p})\|}\;.

We investigate (relative) errors in corresponding tangents

\dot{{\bf x}}=\dot{F}({\bf x},\dot{{\bf p}})\equiv\frac{dF}{d{\bf p}}\cdot\dot{{\bf p}}

(4)

and adjoints

\bar{{\bf p}}=\bar{F}({\bf x},\bar{{\bf x}})\equiv\frac{dF}{d{\bf p}}^{T}\cdot\bar{{\bf x}}

(5)

due to
\Delta{\bf x}.

Algorithmic
tangents and adjoints result from the application of
algorithmic differentiation (AD)
[
3
,
4
]
to the
solver
S.

Symbolic
tangents and adjoints can be
derived at the solution of Equation (
3
) in terms of tangents and
adjoints of the residual
[
2
,
5
]
.
AD of the solver can thus be avoided which typically
results in a considerably lower computational complexity.

2
Prerequisites

We perform standard first-order error analysis.
For a given absolute error
\Delta{\bf p}
in the input of a function
F

the absolute error in the result is estimated as

\Delta{\bf x}\approx\frac{dF}{d{\bf p}}\cdot\Delta{\bf p}\;.

(6)

Equation (
1
) is differentiated with respect to
{\bf p}
in
the direction of the absolute error
\Delta{\bf p}.
From the Taylor series
expansion of

{\bf x}+\Delta{\bf x}={\bf x}+\frac{dF}{d{\bf p}}\cdot\Delta{\bf p}+O(\|\Delta{\bf p}\|^{2})

it follows that
negligence of the remainder within a neighborhood of
{\bf x}
containing

\Delta{\bf x}
is reasonable for
\|\Delta{\bf p}\|\rightarrow 0
and assuming
convergence of the Taylor series to the correct function value.
For linear
F
we get

\Delta{\bf x}=\frac{dF}{d{\bf p}}\cdot\Delta{\bf p}
due to the vanishing remainder.

Tangents and adjoints of Equation (
1
) can be expressed as matrix
equations over derivatives of the residual. The fundamental operations
involved are scalar multiplications and additions, outer vector
products, matrix-vector products and solutions of systems of linear equations.
In this section we recall the corresponding well-known first-order error
estimates. Norms of those estimates are considered.

2.1
Scalar Multiplication

Differentiation of

y=x_{1}\cdot x_{2}
in direction

\Delta{\bf x}=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{pmatrix}\neq 0

yields the absolute error

|\Delta y|=|\Delta x_{1}\cdot x_{2}+x_{1}\cdot\Delta x_{2}|.

The corresponding relative error is equal to

\displaystyle|\delta y|\equiv\frac{|\Delta y|}{|y|}

\displaystyle=\frac{|x_{2}\cdot\Delta x_{1}+x_{1}\cdot\Delta x_{2}|}{|x_{1}\cdot x_{2}|}=\left|\frac{x_{2}\cdot\Delta x_{1}+x_{1}\cdot\Delta x_{2}}{x_{1}\cdot x_{2}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\Delta x_{1}}{x_{1}}+\frac{\Delta x_{2}}{x_{2}}\right|=\left|\frac{\Delta x_{1}}{x_{1}}\right|+\left|\frac{\Delta x_{2}}{x_{2}}\right|=\frac{|\Delta x_{1}|}{|x_{1}|}+\frac{|\Delta x_{2}|}{|x_{2}|}=|\delta x_{1}|+|\delta x_{2}|\;.

Scalar multiplication turns out to be numerically stable. The relative error
in the result is of the order of the maximum relative error in the arguments.
A similar result holds for scalar division.
It generalizes naturally to element-wise multiplication and division of
vectors, matrices, and higher-order tensors as well as to the outer product
of two vectors.

2.2
Scalar Addition

Differentiation of

y=x_{1}+x_{2}
in direction
\Delta{\bf x}\neq 0
yields the absolute error

|\Delta y|=|\Delta x_{1}+\Delta x_{2}|.
The corresponding relative error is equal
to

|\delta y|\equiv\frac{|\Delta y|}{|y|}=\frac{|\Delta x_{1}+\Delta x_{2}|}{|x_{1}+x_{2}|}\;.

Scalar addition turns out to be numerically unstable due to

|\delta y|\rightarrow\infty
for
\Delta x_{1}\neq-\Delta x_{2}
and
x_{1}\rightarrow-x_{2}.

The relative error in the result can become unbounded for bounded relative
errors of the arguments.
A similar result holds for scalar subtraction.

Numerical instability of scalar addition prevents unconditional
numerical stability of inner vector products as well as matrix-vector/matrix
products and solutions of systems of linear equations. Sufficient conditions for
numerical stability need to be formulated.

2.3
Matrix-Vector Product

Differentiation of the matrix-vector product

{\bf x}=A\cdot{\bf b}
for

A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
{\bf x},{\bf b}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

in the direction of non-vanishing absolute errors

\Delta A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
\Delta{\bf x},\Delta{\bf b}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

yields

\displaystyle\Delta{\bf x}

\displaystyle=\Delta A\cdot{\bf b}+A\cdot\Delta{\bf b}

and hence the first-order error estimate

\displaystyle\frac{\|\Delta{\bf x}\|}{\|{\bf x}\|}

\displaystyle=\frac{\|\Delta A\cdot{\bf b}+A\cdot\Delta{\bf b}\|}{\|A\cdot{\bf b}\|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\Delta A\|\cdot\|{\bf b}\|+\|A\|\cdot\|\Delta{\bf b}\|}{\|A\cdot{\bf b}\|}=\|A^{-1}\|\cdot\frac{\|\Delta A\|\cdot\|{\bf b}\|+\|A\|\cdot\|\Delta{\bf b}\|}{\|A^{-1}\|\cdot\|A\cdot{\bf b}\|}

\displaystyle\leq\|A^{-1}\|\cdot\frac{\|\Delta A\|\cdot\|{\bf b}\|+\|A\|\cdot\|\Delta{\bf b}\|}{\|A^{-1}\cdot A\cdot{\bf b}\|}=\|A^{-1}\|\cdot\frac{\|\Delta A\|\cdot\|{\bf b}\|+\|A\|\cdot\|\Delta{\bf b}\|}{\|{\bf b}\|}

\displaystyle=\|A^{-1}\|\cdot\|A\|\cdot\left(\frac{\|\Delta A\|}{\|A\|}+\frac{\|\Delta{\bf b}\|}{\|{\bf b}\|}\right)=\kappa(A)\cdot\left(\frac{\|\Delta A\|}{\|A\|}+\frac{\|\Delta{\bf b}\|}{\|{\bf b}\|}\right)\;.

A low condition number

\kappa(A)\equiv\|A^{-1}\|\cdot\|A\|
of
A
is sufficient for numerical
stability. We write

\|\delta{\bf x}\|\approx\kappa(A)\cdot(\|\delta A\|+\|\delta{\bf b}\|)

(7)

to indicate that depending on the magnitude of
\kappa(A)

the relative error of a matrix-vector product can suffer from
a potentially dramatic amplification of the relative errors in the
arguments.

2.4
Systems of Linear Equations

Differentiation of the system of linear equations

A\cdot{\bf x}={\bf b}
for

A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
{\bf x},{\bf b}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

in the direction of non-vanishing absolute errors

\Delta A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
\Delta{\bf x},\Delta{\bf b}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

yields

\displaystyle\Delta{\bf x}=A^{-1}\cdot(\Delta{\bf b}-\Delta A\cdot{\bf x})

and hence the first-order error estimate

\displaystyle\frac{\|\Delta{\bf x}\|}{\|{\bf x}\|}

\displaystyle=\frac{\|A^{-1}\cdot(\Delta{\bf b}-\Delta A\cdot{\bf x})\|}{\|{\bf x}\|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|A^{-1}\cdot\Delta{\bf b}\|}{\|{\bf x}\|}+\frac{\|A^{-1}\cdot\Delta A\cdot{\bf x}\|}{\|{\bf x}\|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|A^{-1}\|\cdot\|\Delta{\bf b}\|}{\|{\bf x}\|}+\frac{\|A^{-1}\cdot\Delta A\cdot{\bf x}\|}{\|{\bf x}\|}=\frac{\|A\|\cdot\|A^{-1}\|\cdot\|\Delta{\bf b}\|}{\|A\|\cdot\|{\bf x}\|}+\frac{\|A^{-1}\cdot\Delta A\cdot{\bf x}\|}{\|{\bf x}\|}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa(A)\cdot\frac{\|\Delta{\bf b}\|}{\|A\cdot{\bf x}\|}+\frac{\|A^{-1}\cdot\Delta A\cdot{\bf x}\|}{\|{\bf x}\|}=\kappa(A)\cdot\frac{\|\Delta{\bf b}\|}{\|{\bf b}\|}+\frac{\|A^{-1}\cdot\Delta A\cdot{\bf x}\|}{\|{\bf x}\|}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa(A)\cdot\frac{\|\Delta{\bf b}\|}{\|{\bf b}\|}+\frac{\|A^{-1}\|\cdot\|\Delta A\|\cdot\|{\bf x}\|}{\|{\bf x}\|}=\kappa(A)\cdot\frac{\|\Delta{\bf b}\|}{\|{\bf b}\|}+\|A^{-1}\|\cdot\|\Delta A\|

\displaystyle=\kappa(A)\cdot\frac{\|\Delta{\bf b}\|}{\|{\bf b}\|}+\frac{\|A\|\cdot\|A^{-1}\|\cdot\|\Delta A\|}{\|A\|}=\kappa(A)\cdot\frac{\|\Delta{\bf b}\|}{\|{\bf b}\|}+\kappa(A)\cdot\frac{\|\Delta A\|}{\|A\|}

\displaystyle=\kappa(A)\cdot\left(\frac{\|\Delta{\bf b}\|}{\|{\bf b}\|}+\frac{\|\Delta A\|}{\|A\|}\right)\;.

As in Section
2.3
we get

\|\delta{\bf x}\|\approx\kappa(A)\cdot(\|\delta A\|+\|\delta{\bf b}\|)\;.

(8)

Again, a low condition number of
A
is sufficient for numerical
stability.

3
Errors in Tangents and Adjoints of Implicit Functions

Differentiation of Equation (
3
) with respect to
{\bf p}
yields

\frac{\partial R}{\partial{\bf x}}\cdot\frac{d{\bf x}}{d{\bf p}}+\frac{\partial R}{\partial{\bf p}}=R_{\bf x}\cdot\frac{d{\bf x}}{d{\bf p}}+R_{\bf p}=0\;,

(9)

where
\partial
denotes partial differentiation. Multiplication with
\dot{\bf p}
from the right yields the tangent residual

R_{\bf x}\cdot\frac{d{\bf x}}{d{\bf p}}\cdot\dot{\bf p}+R_{\bf p}\cdot\dot{\bf p}=R_{\bf x}\cdot\dot{\bf x}+R_{\bf p}\cdot\dot{\bf p}=0\;.

(10)

The tangent
\dot{\bf x}
can be computed as the solution of the system of
linear equations

R_{\bf x}\cdot\dot{\bf x}=-R_{\bf p}\cdot\dot{\bf p}\;.

The right-hand side is obtained by a single evaluation of the tangent residual.
Tangents in the directions of the Cartesian basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
yields
R_{\bf x}.

Potential sparsity can and should be exploited
[
1
]
.
An error
\Delta{\bf x}
in the primal solution yields a
corresponding error in the tangent for
R_{\bf x}=R_{\bf x}({\bf x})
and/or
R_{\bf p}=R_{\bf p}({\bf x}).

From Equation (
9
) it follows that for regular
R_{\bf x}

\frac{d{\bf x}}{d{\bf p}}=-R_{\bf x}^{-1}\cdot R_{\bf p}\;.

Transposition of the latter followed by multiplication with
\bar{\bf x}
from the right yields

\bar{\bf p}=\frac{d{\bf x}}{d{\bf p}}^{T}\cdot\bar{\bf x}=-R_{\bf p}^{T}\cdot R_{\bf x}^{-T}\cdot\bar{\bf x}\;.

(11)

The adjoint
\bar{\bf p}
can be computed as the solution of the system of
linear equations

R_{\bf x}^{T}\cdot{\bf z}=-\bar{\bf x}

followed by the evaluation of
the adjoint residual yielding

\bar{\bf p}=R_{\bf p}^{T}\cdot{\bf z}

. Again, an error

\Delta{\bf x}
in the primal solution yields a
corresponding error in the adjoint.

3.1
Systems of Linear Equations

The tangent of
the solution of the primal system of linear equations

A\cdot{\bf x}={\bf b}

(12)

is defined as
\dot{{\bf x}}=\dot{{\bf x}}_{A}+\dot{{\bf x}}_{\bf b},
where

A\cdot\dot{{\bf x}}_{\bf b}=\dot{{\bf b}}

(13)

and

A\cdot\dot{{\bf x}}_{A}=-\dot{A}\cdot{\bf x}

(14)

[
2
]
.
An error
\Delta{\bf x}
in the primal solution which, for example, might result from the use of
an indirect solver yields an erroneous tangent

\dot{{\bf x}}+\Delta\dot{{\bf x}}=(\dot{{\bf x}}_{A}+\Delta\dot{{\bf x}}_{A})+(\dot{{\bf x}}_{\bf b}+\Delta\dot{{\bf x}}_{\bf b})\;.

Application of Equation (
8
) to Equation (
13
) yields

\|\delta\dot{{\bf x}}_{\bf b}\|\approx\kappa(A)\cdot(\|\delta A\|+\|\delta\dot{{\bf b}}\|)\;.

Independence of
\dot{{\bf x}}_{\bf b}
from
{\bf x}
(and hence from
\Delta{\bf x}
)
implies
\delta\dot{{\bf x}}_{\bf b}=0
for error-free
A
and
\dot{{\bf b}},
that is

\Delta\dot{{\bf x}}=\Delta\dot{{\bf x}}_{A}
, respectively

\delta\dot{{\bf x}}=\delta\dot{{\bf x}}_{A}.

Let
{\bf c}=-\dot{A}\cdot{\bf x}.
With Equation (
7
) it follows that

\|\delta{\bf c}\|\approx\kappa(\dot{A})\cdot\|\delta{\bf x}\|

as
\delta\dot{A}=0

Moreover, application of Equation (
8
) to
A\cdot\dot{\bf x}_{A}={\bf c}
yields

\|\delta\dot{\bf x}_{A}\|\approx\kappa(A)\cdot\|\delta{\bf c}\|\;.

Consequently,

\|\delta\dot{\bf x}_{A}\|\approx\kappa(A)\cdot\kappa(\dot{A})\cdot\|\delta{\bf x}\|\;.

(15)

Low condition numbers of both
A
and
\dot{A}
ensure
numerical stability of tangent systems of linear equations.

The adjoint of the primal linear system in Equation (
12
)
is defined as

A^{T}\cdot\bar{{\bf b}}=\bar{{\bf x}}

(16)

and

\bar{A}=-\bar{{\bf b}}\cdot{\bf x}^{T}

(17)

[
2
]
.
Application of Equation (
8
) to Equation (
16
) yields

\delta\bar{{\bf b}}\approx\kappa(A)\cdot(\delta A+\delta\bar{{\bf x}})\;.

Independence of
\bar{\bf b}
from
{\bf x}
(and hence from
\Delta{\bf x}
)
implies
\delta\bar{{\bf b}}=0
for error-free
A
and
\bar{{\bf x}}.

The outer product

\bar{A}=-\bar{{\bf b}}\cdot{\bf x}^{T}
is numerically stable as
scalar multiplication is. Consequently, adjoint systems of linear equations
are numerically stable.

3.2
Systems of Nonlinear Equations

Differentiation of
Equation (
10
) in the direction of absolute errors

\Delta R_{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n},

\Delta\dot{{\bf x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

\Delta R_{\bf p}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
and
\Delta\dot{{\bf p}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

yields

\Delta R_{\bf x}\cdot\dot{{\bf x}}+R_{\bf x}\cdot\Delta\dot{{\bf x}}+\Delta R_{\bf p}\cdot\dot{{\bf p}}~[+\underset{=0}{\underbrace{R_{\bf p}\cdot\Delta\dot{{\bf p}}}}]=0

as
\Delta\dot{{\bf p}}=0
and hence

\displaystyle\Delta\dot{{\bf x}}

\displaystyle=R^{-1}_{\bf x}\cdot(\Delta R_{\bf x}\cdot\dot{{\bf x}}+\Delta R_{\bf p}\cdot\dot{{\bf p}})\;.

First-order estimates for

\Delta R_{\bf x}\cdot\dot{{\bf x}}=[\Delta R_{\bf x}\cdot\dot{{\bf x}}]_{i}\approx[R_{{\bf x},{\bf x}}]_{i,j,k}\cdot[\dot{{\bf x}}]_{j}\cdot[\Delta{\bf x}]_{k}\equiv\Delta\dot{R}_{\bf x}\cdot\Delta{\bf x}

and

\Delta R_{\bf p}\cdot\dot{{\bf p}}=[\Delta R_{\bf p}\cdot\dot{{\bf p}}]_{i}\approx[R_{{\bf p},{\bf x}}]_{i,j,k}\cdot[\dot{{\bf p}}]_{j}\cdot[\Delta{\bf x}]_{k}\equiv\Delta\dot{R}_{\bf p}\cdot\Delta{\bf x}

in index notation
(summation over the shared index) yield

\displaystyle\Delta\dot{{\bf x}}

\displaystyle\approx R^{-1}_{\bf x}\cdot(\Delta\dot{R}_{\bf x}+\Delta\dot{R}_{\bf p})\cdot\Delta{\bf x}

and hence, with Equation (
7
),

\|\delta\dot{{\bf x}}\|\approx\kappa(R_{\bf x})\cdot\kappa(\Delta\dot{R}_{\bf x}+\Delta\dot{R}_{\bf p})\cdot\|\delta{\bf x}\|\;.

(18)

Low condition numbers
of the respective first and second derivatives of the residual ensure
numerical stability of tangent systems of nonlinear equations.
Both
\Delta\dot{R}_{\bf x}
and
\Delta\dot{R}_{\bf p}
can be computed by
algorithmic differentiation (AD)
[
3
,
4
]
.

Application of Equation (
8
) to the system of linear equations

R_{\bf x}^{T}\cdot{\bf z}=-\bar{\bf x}

for
\Delta\bar{\bf x}=0

yields

\Delta{\bf z}=R_{\bf x}^{-T}\cdot\Delta R_{\bf x}^{T}\cdot{\bf z}

and hence

\|\delta{\bf z}\|\approx\kappa(R_{\bf x})\cdot\kappa(\Delta\bar{R}_{\bf x})\cdot\|\delta{\bf x}\|\;,

where

[\Delta R^{T}_{\bf x}\cdot{\bf z}]_{j}\approx[R_{{\bf x},{\bf x}}]_{i,j,k}\cdot[{\bf z}]_{i}\cdot[\Delta{\bf x}]_{k}\equiv\Delta\bar{R}_{\bf x}\cdot\Delta{\bf x}\;.

Differentiation of
\bar{\bf p}=R_{\bf p}^{T}\cdot{\bf z}
in the direction of
the non-vanishing absolute errors
\Delta R_{\bf p}^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and
\Delta{\bf z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

yields

\displaystyle\Delta\bar{\bf p}

\displaystyle=\Delta R_{\bf p}^{T}\cdot{\bf z}+R_{\bf p}^{T}\cdot\Delta{\bf z}=\Delta R_{\bf p}^{T}\cdot{\bf z}+R_{\bf p}^{T}\cdot R_{\bf x}^{-T}\cdot\Delta R_{\bf x}^{T}\cdot{\bf z}

and hence

\|\delta\bar{{\bf p}}\|\approx\left(\kappa(\Delta\bar{R}_{\bf p})+\kappa(R_{\bf p})\cdot\kappa(R_{\bf x})\cdot\kappa(\Delta\bar{R}_{\bf x})\right)\cdot\|\delta{\bf x}\|\;,

(19)

where

[\Delta R^{T}_{\bf p}\cdot{\bf z}]_{j}\approx[R_{{\bf p},{\bf x}}]_{i,j,k}\cdot[{\bf z}]_{i}\cdot[\Delta{\bf x}]_{k}\equiv\Delta\bar{R}_{\bf p}\cdot\Delta{\bf x}\;.

Low condition numbers
of the respective first and second derivatives of the residual ensure
numerical stability of adjoint systems of nonlinear equations.
Both
\Delta\bar{R}_{\bf x}
and
\Delta\bar{R}_{\bf p}
can be computed by AD.

3.3
Convex Unconstrained Objectives

The first-order optimality condition
for a parameterized convex unconstrained objective

f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:({\bf x},{\bf p})\mapsto y=f({\bf x},{\bf p})

yields the residual

f_{\bf x}({\bf x},{\bf p})=0.

Consequently, assuming
f
to be three times differentiable,

\|\delta\dot{{\bf x}}\|\approx\kappa(f_{{\bf x},{\bf x}})\cdot\kappa(\Delta\dot{f}_{{\bf x},{\bf x}}+\Delta\dot{f}_{{\bf x},{\bf p}})\cdot\|\delta{\bf x}\|\;,

(20)

where

\Delta f_{{\bf x},{\bf x}}\cdot\dot{{\bf x}}=[\Delta f_{{\bf x},{\bf x}}\cdot\dot{{\bf x}}]_{i}\approx[f_{{\bf x},{\bf x},{\bf x}}]_{i,j,k}\cdot[\dot{{\bf x}}]_{j}\cdot[\Delta{\bf x}]_{k}\equiv\Delta\dot{f}_{{\bf x},{\bf x}}\cdot\Delta{\bf x}

and

\Delta f_{{\bf x},{\bf p}}\cdot\dot{{\bf p}}=[\Delta f_{{\bf x},{\bf p}}\cdot\dot{{\bf p}}]_{i}\approx[f_{{\bf x},{\bf p},{\bf x}}]_{i,j,k}\cdot[\dot{{\bf p}}]_{j}\cdot[\Delta{\bf x}]_{k}\equiv\Delta\dot{f}_{{\bf x},{\bf p}}\cdot\Delta{\bf x}\;.

Similarly,

\|\delta\bar{{\bf p}}\|\approx\left(\kappa(\Delta\bar{f}_{{\bf x},{\bf p}})+\kappa(f_{{\bf x},{\bf p}})\cdot\kappa(f_{{\bf x},{\bf x}})\cdot\kappa(\Delta\bar{f}_{{\bf x},{\bf x}})\right)\cdot\|\delta{\bf x}\|\;,

(21)

where

[\Delta f_{{\bf x},{\bf x}}^{T}\cdot{\bf z}]_{j}=[\Delta f_{{\bf x},{\bf x}}\cdot{\bf z}]_{j}\approx[f_{{\bf x},{\bf x},{\bf x}}]_{i,j,k}\cdot[{\bf z}]_{i}\cdot[\Delta{\bf x}]_{k}\equiv\Delta\bar{f}_{{\bf x},{\bf x}}\cdot\Delta{\bf x}

and

[\Delta f_{{\bf x},{\bf p}}^{T}\cdot{\bf z}]_{j}\approx[f_{{\bf x},{\bf p},{\bf x}}]_{i,j,k}\cdot[{\bf z}]_{i}\cdot[\Delta{\bf x}]_{k}\equiv\Delta\bar{f}_{{\bf x},{\bf p}}\cdot\Delta{\bf x}\;.

Low
condition numbers of the respective second and third derivatives of the
objective ensure
numerical stability of tangent and adjoint optima of convex unconstrained
objectives.
Both
\Delta\dot{f}_{{\bf x},{\bf x}}
and
\Delta\dot{f}_{{\bf x},{\bf p}}
as well as

\Delta\bar{f}_{{\bf x},{\bf x}}
and
\Delta\bar{f}_{{\bf x},{\bf p}}
can be computed by AD.

4
Conclusion

Adjoint systems of linear equations are numerically stable with respect to
errors in the primal solution. However, numerical stability of tangents and
adjoints of implicit functions cannot be
guaranteed in general. Sufficient conditions in terms of derivatives of the
residual are given by Equations (
15
), (
18
),
(
19
), (
20
) and (
21
). AD
can be used to compute
these derivatives. Corresponding symbolic tangents and adjoints
should be
augmented with optional estimation of conditions of the relevant derivatives.

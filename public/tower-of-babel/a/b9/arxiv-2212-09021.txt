Landau-Bloch type theorem for elliptic and K K -quasiregular harmonic mappings
Vasudevarao Allu, Rohit Kumar

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish an improved coefficient bounds for quasiregular and elliptic harmonic mappings and using these bounds we establish Landau-Bloch type theorem for ( K , K ′ ) (K,K^{\prime}) -elliptic and K-quasiregular harmonic mappings in plane. Furthermore, we prove the coefficient estimates for K K -quasiconformal harmonic self maps defined on the unit disk 𝔻 \mathbb{D} .

1.
Introduction

A continuous complex-valued function
f=u+iv
is a complex-valued harmonic function in a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
, if both
u
and
v
are real-valued harmonic functions in
\Omega
(see
[
7
]
). The inverse function theorem and a result of Lewy
[
13
]
shows that a harmonic function
f
is locally univalent (
i.e.
, one-to-one) in
\Omega
if, and only if, the Jacobian
J_{f}
is non-zero in
\Omega
, where

J_{f}(z)=|f_{z}(z)|^{2}-|f_{\overline{z}}(z)|^{2}.

A harmonic function
f
is said to be sense-preserving if
J_{f}(z)>0
for
z
in
\Omega
.
Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the class of complex-valued harmonic functions
f
in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
, normalized by
f(0)=0=f_{z}(0)-1
. Each function
f
in
\mathcal{H}
can be expressed as
f=h+\overline{g}
, where
h
and
g
are analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
(see
[
7
]
). Here
h
and
g
are called the analytic and co-analytic parts of
f
respectively, and have power series representations

h(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\quad\mbox{and}\quad g(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}.

Let
\mathcal{S_{H}}
be the subclass of
\mathcal{H}
consisting of univalent, and sense-preserving harmonic mappings on
\mathbb{D}
. Let
\mathcal{H}^{0}=\{f\in\mathcal{H}:f_{\overline{z}}(0)=0\}
, and
\mathcal{S_{H}}^{0}=\{f\in\mathcal{S_{H}}:f_{\overline{z}}(0)=0\}
. Hence for any function
f=h+\overline{g}
in
\mathcal{H}^{0}
, its analytic and co-analytic parts can be represented by

h(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\quad\mbox{and}\quad g(z)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}

respectively. It is interesting to note that
\mathcal{S_{H}}
reduces to
\mathcal{S}
, the class of normalized analytic and univalent functions in
\mathbb{D}
, if the co-analytic part of functions in the class
\mathcal{S_{H}}
is zero. In 1984, Clunie and Sheil-Small
[
6
]
investigated the class
\mathcal{S_{H}}
, together with some geometric subclasses. Subsequently, the class
\mathcal{S_{H}}
and its subclasses have been extensively studied by several authors
(see
[
1
,
2
,
6
,
11
,
16
]
).

For a continuously differentiable function
f
, denote

\Lambda_{f}=\max_{0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi}\left|f_{z}+e^{-2i\theta}f_{\bar{z}}\right|=\left|f_{z}\right|+\left|f_{\bar{z}}\right|,

\lambda_{f}=\min_{0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi}\left|f_{z}+e^{-2i\theta}f_{\bar{z}}\right|=||f_{z}|-|f_{\bar{z}}||.

It is easy to see that
\left|J_{f}\right|=\Lambda_{f}\lambda_{f}
.

A harmonic mapping
f=h+\overline{g}
defined in the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
can be expressed as

f\left(re^{i\theta}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}r^{n}e^{in\theta}+\sum_{n=}^{\infty}\bar{b}_{n}r^{n}e^{-in\theta},\quad 0\leq r<1,

where

h(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{a}_{n}z^{n},\ g(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{b}_{n}z^{n}.

The classical Landau theorem (see
[
12
]
) asserts that if
f
is a holomorphic mapping with
f(0)=0
,
f^{\prime}(0)=1
and
|f(z)|<M
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then
f
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}_{r_{0}}
, and
f(\mathbb{D}_{r_{0}})
contains a disc
\mathbb{D}_{\sigma_{0}}
, where

r_{0}=\frac{1}{M+\sqrt{M^{2}-1}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \sigma_{0}=Mr_{0}^{2}.

The quantities
r_{0}
and
\sigma_{0}
can not be improved. The extremal function is
f_{0}(z)=Mz\left(\frac{1-Mz}{M-z}\right)
.

In 2000, Chen
et al.

[
3
]
established the following two versions of Landau-type theorems for bounded harmonic mapping on the unit disc under a suitable restriction.

Theorem A.

[
3
]

Let
f
be a harmonic mapping of the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
such that
f(0)=0
,
f_{\bar{z}}(0)=0,f_{z}(0)=1
, and
|f(z)|<M
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then,
f
is univalent on a disc
\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{0}}
with

\rho_{0}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{16mM},

and
f\left(\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{0}}\right)
contains a schlicht disc
\mathbb{D}_{R_{0}}
with

R_{0}=\rho_{0}/2=\frac{\pi^{2}}{32mM},

where
m\approx 6.85
is the minimum of the function
(3-r^{2})/(r(1-r^{2}))
for
0<r<1
.

Theorem B.

[
3
]

Let
f
be a harmonic mapping of the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
such that
f(0)=0
,
\lambda_{f}(0)=1
and
\Lambda_{f}(z)\leq\Lambda
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then,
f
is univalent on a disc
\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{0}}
with

\rho_{0}=\frac{\pi}{4(1+\Lambda)},

and
f\left(\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{0}}\right)
contains a schlicht disc
\mathbb{D}_{R_{0}}
with

R_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}=\frac{\pi}{8(1+\Lambda)}.

Theorems A and B are not sharp. In 2006, better estimates for theorem A and B were given by Grigoyan
[
9
]
. In fact, Grigoyan has proved the following lemma.

Lemma C.

[
9
]

Assume that
f=h+\overline{g}
with
h
and
g
analytic in the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
and let
h(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
g(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

(a) If
|f(z)|<M
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then

\left|a_{n}\right|,\left|b_{n}\right|\leq M,\quad n=1,2,\ldots.

(b) If
\Lambda_{f}(z)\leq\Lambda
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then

\left|a_{n}\right|+\left|b_{n}\right|\leq\frac{\Lambda}{n},\quad n=1,2,\ldots.

The following result has been proved by Grigoyan using Lemma C, which improves the estimates in Theorems A and B.

Theorem D.

[
9
]

Let
f
be harmonic mapping of the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
such that
f(0)=0,J_{f}(0)=1
and
|f(z)|<M
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then,
f
is univalent on a disc
\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{1}}
with

\rho_{1}=1-\frac{2\sqrt{2}M}{\sqrt{\pi+8M^{2}}}

and
f\left(\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{1}}\right)
contains a schlicht disc with

R_{1}=\frac{\pi}{4M}+4M-4M\sqrt{1+\frac{\pi}{8M^{2}}}.

Theorem E.

[
9
]

Let
f
be harmonic mapping of the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
such that
f(0)=0,\lambda_{f}(0)=1
and
\Lambda_{f}(z)\leq\Lambda
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then,
f
is univalent on a disc
\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{2}}
with

\rho_{2}=\rho_{2}(\Lambda)=\frac{1}{1+\Lambda}

and
f\left(\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{2}}\right)
contains a schlicht disc with

R_{2}(\Lambda)=1-\Lambda\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{\Lambda}\right).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a doamain. A mapping
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is said to be absolutely continuous on lines,
ACL
in brief, in a domain
\Omega
if for every closed rectangle
R\subset\Omega
with sides parallel to the axes
x
and
y,f
is absolutely continuous on almost every horizontal line and almost every vertical line in
R
. Such a mapping
f
has partial derivatives
f_{x}
and
f_{y}

a.e.
in
\Omega
. Moreover, we say
f\in ACL^{2}
if
f\in ACL
and its partial derivatives are locally
L^{2}
integrable in
\Omega
.

Definition 1.2
.

[
8
]

A sense-preserving and continuous mapping
f
of
\mathbb{D}
onto
\mathbb{C}
is said to be
(K,K^{\prime})
-elliptic mapping if

(1)

f
is
ACL^{2}
in
\mathbb{D}
,
J_{f}\neq 0

a.e.
in
\mathbb{D}
,

(2)

\exists\ K\geq 1
and
K^{\prime}\geq 0
such that

\left\lVert D_{f}\right\rVert^{2}\leq KJ_{f}+K^{\prime}\ {\it{a.e.}}\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{D}.

In particular, if
K^{\prime}=0
, then a
(K,K^{\prime})
-elliptic mapping becomes
K
-quasiregular mapping. For more details on elliptic mappings, we refer to (
[
5
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
14
]
).

It is easy to see that
\left\lVert D_{f}(z)\right\rVert=\Lambda(z)
. Therefore, a sense-preserving harmonic mapping
f
is said to be
K
-quasiregular harmonic
(K\geq 1)
on
\mathbb{D}
if
\Lambda_{f}^{2}\leq KJ_{f}
, or equivalently
\Lambda_{f}\leq K\lambda_{f}
holds everywhere on
\mathbb{D}
.

If
f:G\to G^{\prime}
is
K
- quasiregular harmonic homeomorphism then
f
is called
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mapping.

For
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Let

P\left(z,e^{i\theta}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{\left|1-ze^{-i\theta}\right|^{2}}

be the Poisson kernel. For a mapping
F\in L^{1}(\partial\mathbb{D})
, the Poisson integral of
F
is defined by

f(z)=P[F](z)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}P\left(z,e^{i\theta}\right)F\left(e^{i\theta}\right)d\theta.

In 2007, Partyka and Sakan
[
15
]
proved following result.

Theorem F.

[
15
]
Given
K\geq 1
, and let
f(z)=P[F](z)
be a harmonic
K
-quasiconformal mapping of
\mathbb{D}
onto itself, with the boundary function
F(t)
. If
f(0)=0
, then for a.e.
z=e^{it}\in\partial\mathbb{D}
,

\frac{2^{5\left(1-K^{2}\right)/2}}{\left(K^{2}+K-1\right)^{K}}\leq\left|F^{\prime}(t)\right|\leq K^{3K}2^{5(K-1/K)/2}.

In 2020, Chen and Ponnusamy
[
4
]
proved the following lemma which characterizes elliptic mappings.

Lemma G.

[
4
]

Let
f\in\mathrm{C}^{1}
be a sense-preserving mapping. Then
f
is a elliptic mapping if, and only if, there exist constants
k_{1}\in[0,1)
and
k_{2}\in[0,\infty)
such that

\left|f_{\bar{z}}(z)\right|\leq k_{1}\left|f_{z}(z)\right|+k_{2}

for
z\in\mathbb{D}.

In particular, if
f
is
(K,K^{\prime})
-elliptic mapping then

k_{1}=\frac{K-1}{K+1},\ \ k_{2}=\frac{\sqrt{K^{\prime}}}{1+K}.

The main aim of this paper is to establish new versions of Landau-type theorems for elliptic and
K
-quasiregular harmonic mappings and some coefficient estimates for
K
-quasiconformal harmonic mappings.

2.
Main results

We first prove coefficient estimates for elliptic harmonic mappings and
K
-quasiregular harmonic mappings.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f=h+\bar{g}
be
(K,K^{\prime})
-elliptic harmonic mapping defined on unit disc
\mathbb{D}
, where

(2.2)

h(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad g(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}.

If
\lambda_{f}(z)\leq\lambda
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then

(2.3)

\left|a_{n}\right|+\left|b_{n}\right|\leq\frac{K\lambda+\sqrt{K^{\prime}}}{n}.

We see that for the case
K=1
and
K^{\prime}=0
, we get

\left|a_{n}\right|+\left|b_{n}\right|\leq\frac{\lambda}{n}.

Using Theorem 2.1, we obtain following corollary.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
f=h+\bar{g}
be a harmonic
K
-quasiregular mapping defined on unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, where

h(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad g(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}.

If
\lambda_{f}(z)\leq\lambda
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then

\left|a_{n}\right|+\left|b_{n}\right|\leq\frac{K\lambda}{n}.

Next, we state Landau-type theorems for elliptic harmonic mappings and
K
-quasiregular mappings.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
f=h+\bar{g}
be
(K,K^{\prime})
-elliptic harmonic mapping defined on unit disc
\mathbb{D}
such that
f(0)=0
,
\lambda_{f}(0)=1
and
\lambda_{f}(z)\leq\lambda
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then
f
is univalent on a disk
\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{1}}
with

\rho_{1}=\rho_{1}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{1+K\lambda+\sqrt{K^{\prime}}}

and
f\left(\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{1}}\right)
contains a schlicht disc with radius

R_{1}=\rho_{1}(\lambda)+(K\lambda+\sqrt{K^{\prime}})(\rho_{1}(\lambda)+\ln\left(\left(K\lambda+\sqrt{K^{\prime}}\right)\rho_{1}(\lambda)\right).

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
f=h+\bar{g}
be
K
-quasiregular harmonic mapping defined on unit disk
\mathbb{D}
such that
f(0)=0
,
\lambda_{f}(0)=1
and
\lambda_{f}(z)\leq\lambda
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then
f
is univalent on a disc
\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{1}}
with

\rho_{1}=\rho_{1}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{1+K\lambda}

and
f\left(\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{1}}\right)
contains a schlicht disc with radius

R_{1}=\rho_{1}(\lambda)+K\lambda\left(\rho_{1}(\lambda)+\ln\left(K\lambda\ \rho_{1}(\lambda)\right)\right).

Using Corollary
2.4
, we proved the following theorem.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
f=h+\bar{g}
be a harmonic K-quasiregular mapping defined on unit disc
\mathbb{D}
such that
f(0)=0,J_{f}(0)=1
and
\lambda_{f}{(z)}\leq\lambda
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then
f
is univalent on a disc
\mathbb{D}_{\rho}
with

\rho(\lambda)=\frac{1}{1+\lambda K^{3/2}}

and
f\left(\mathbb{D}_{\rho}\right)
contains a schlicht disc with radius

R(\lambda)=\frac{\rho(\lambda)}{\sqrt{K}}+K\lambda\left(\rho(\lambda)+\ln(\lambda K^{3/2}\rho(\lambda)\right).

Finally, using Theorem G, we prove the coefficient bounds for
K
-quasiconformal self mappings defined on the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
f=h+\bar{g}
be a
K
-quasiconformal harmonic self mapping on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, where
h
and
g
are analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and given by

h(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad g(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}

for
z\in\mathbb{D}
.
Then,

\left|a_{n}\right|,\left|b_{n}\right|\leq\frac{K^{3K}2^{5\left(K-\frac{1}{K}\right)/2}}{n}.

3.
Proof of the main results

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let
f=h+\bar{g}
be
(K,K^{\prime})
- elliptic harmonic mapping defined on the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
, where
h
and
g
are given by (
2.2
). For fixed
r\in(0,1)
and
z=re^{i\theta}
for some
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
, we have

(3.1)

\frac{\partial h}{\partial r}(re^{i\theta})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}na_{n}r^{n-1}e^{in\theta}\ \text{ and }\ \frac{\partial g}{\partial r}(re^{i\theta})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nb_{n}r^{n-1}e^{in\theta}.

Integrating both sides of (
3.1
) with respect to
r
from
0
to
2\pi
after multiplying
e^{-in\theta}
to the left equation and multiplying
e^{in\theta}
to the right equation, we obtain

na_{n}r^{n-1}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\partial h(re^{i\theta})}{\partial r}e^{-in\theta}d\theta

and

nb_{n}r^{n-1}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\partial g(re^{i\theta})}{\partial r}e^{in\theta}d\theta.

for
n=1,2...
.
Therefore, we have

n\left|a_{n}\right|r^{n-1}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}}h(re^{i\theta})\right|d\theta

and

n\left|b_{n}\right|r^{n-1}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}}g(re^{i\theta})\right|d\theta.

Hence, we get

n\left(\left|a_{n}\right|+\left|b_{n}\right|\right)r^{n-1}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}h(re^{i\theta})\right|+\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}g(re^{i\theta})\right|\right)d\theta

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial r}h(re^{i\theta})

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial h}{\partial z}\frac{\partial z}{\partial r}+\frac{\partial h}{\partial\bar{z}}\frac{\partial\bar{z}}{\partial r}

\displaystyle=h_{z}e^{i\theta}+0\quad\quad\ \ \left(\because h_{\bar{z}}=0\right)

\displaystyle=h_{z}e^{i\theta}

and similarly,

\frac{\partial}{\partial r}g(re^{i\theta})=h_{z}e^{i\theta}.

We also see that

(3.2)

\left|f_{z}\right|=\left|h_{z}\right|\text{ and }\left|f_{\bar{z}}\right|=\left|g_{z}\right|.

In view of (
3.2
), we have

(3.3)

n\left(\left|a_{n}\right|+\left|b_{n}\right|\right)r^{n-1}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\left|h_{z}e^{i\theta}\right|+\left|g_{z}e^{i\theta}\right|\right)d\theta=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(\left|f_{z}\right|+\left|f_{\bar{z}}\right|\right)d\theta.

Since
\lambda_{f}(z)\leq\lambda
and
f
is sense preserving, we have

\left|f_{z}\right|\leq\lambda+\left|f_{\bar{z}}\right|.

Using Lemma H, we obtain

\left|f_{z}\right|\leq\lambda+k_{1}\left|f_{z}\right|+k_{2},

which implies that

\left|f_{z}\right|\leq\frac{\lambda+k_{2}}{1-k_{1}}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle|f_{z}|+|f_{\bar{z}}|

\displaystyle\leq|f_{z}|+k_{1}|f_{z}|+k_{2}

\displaystyle=\left(1+k_{1}\right)\left|f_{z}\right|+k_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+k_{1}\right)\frac{\lambda+k_{2}}{1-k_{1}}+k_{2}.

In view of Lemma H, we have

(3.4)

|f_{z}|+|f_{\bar{z}}|\leq K\lambda+\sqrt{K^{\prime}}.

By equation (
3.3
) and equation (
3.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle n\left(\left|a_{n}\right|+\left|b_{n}\right|\right)r^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(K\lambda+\sqrt{K^{\prime}}\right)d\theta

\displaystyle=K\lambda+\sqrt{K^{\prime}}.

Now by taking
r\rightarrow 1^{-}
, we obtain

\left|a_{n}\right|+\left|b_{n}\right|\leq\frac{K\lambda+\sqrt{K^{\prime}}}{n}.

This completes the theorem.
∎

2.4
.

Let
f
is
K

f
is a
(K,0)

K^{\prime}=0
in (
2.3

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

Let
f=h+\bar{g}
be
(K,K^{\prime})
- elliptic harmonic mapping defined on the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
, where
h
and
g
are given by (
2.2
). Then

f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bar{b}_{n}\bar{z}^{n}\quad\text{for}\ z\in\mathbb{D}.

Fix
r\in(0,1)
and
z_{1},z_{2}\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{r}
with
z_{1}\neq z_{2}
.
It is easy to see that

f\left(z_{2}\right)-f\left(z_{1}\right)=h\left(z_{2}\right)-h\left(z_{1}\right)+\overline{g\left(z_{2}\right)-g\left(z_{1}\right)}.

Therefore, we have

|f(z_{2})-f(z_{1})|=\left|\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}h^{\prime}(z)dz+\overline{\left(\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}g^{\prime}(z)dz\right)}\right|,

where
[z_{1},z_{2}]
is the line segment joining
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
. It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|f\left(z_{2}\right)-f\left(z_{1}\right)\right|&\geq\left|\int_{\left[z_{1},z_{2}\right]}\left(h^{\prime}(z)-h^{\prime}(0)+h^{\prime}(0)\right)dz+\overline{\int_{\left[z_{1},z_{2}\right]}\left(g^{\prime}(z)-g^{\prime}(0)+g^{\prime}(0)\right)dz}\right|\\
&\geq\left|\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}\left(h^{\prime}(z)-h^{\prime}(0)\right)dz+\int_{\left[z_{1},z_{2}\right]}h^{\prime}(0)dz+\overline{\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}g^{\prime}(0)dz}\right.\\
&\ \ \quad+\left.\overline{\int_{\left[z_{1},z_{2}\right]}\left(g^{\prime}(z)-g^{\prime}(0)\right)dz}\right|\\
&\geq\left|\int_{\left[z_{1},z_{2}\right]}h^{\prime}(0)dz+\overline{\int_{z_{1},z_{2}]}g^{\prime}(0)dz}\right|\\
&\ \ \quad-\left|\int_{\left[z_{1},z_{2}\right]}\left(h^{\prime}(z)-h^{\prime}(0)\right)dz+\overline{\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}\left(g^{\prime}(z)-g^{\prime}(0)\right)}dz\right|\end{split}

Further,we have

\displaystyle\left|f\left(z_{2}\right)-f\left(z_{1}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{f}(0)\left|z_{2}-z_{1}\right|-\left|\int_{\left[z_{1},z_{2}\right]}h^{\prime}\left(z\right)dz-\int_{\left[z_{1},z_{1}\right]}h^{\prime}(0)dz\right|

\displaystyle\ \ -\left|\int_{\left[z_{1},z_{2}\right]}g^{\prime}(z)dz-\int_{\left[z_{1},z_{2}\right]}g^{\prime}(0)dz\right|

\displaystyle=\lambda_{f}(0)\left|z_{2}-z_{1}\right|-\left|h\left(z_{2}\right)-h\left(z_{1}\right)-h^{\prime}(0)\left(z_{2}-z_{1}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\ \ \ -\left|g\left(z_{2}\right)-g\left(z_{1}\right)-g^{\prime}(0)\left(z_{2}-z_{1}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=|z_{2}-z_{1}|\left(1-\left|\frac{h\left(z_{2}\right)-h\left(z_{1}\right)}{z_{2}-z_{1}}-h^{\prime}(0)\right|-\left|\frac{g\left(z_{2}\right)-g\left(z_{1}\right)}{z_{2}-z_{1}}-g^{\prime}(0)\right|\right).

Since
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{\overline{D}}_{r}
, we obtain

\left|\frac{h\left(z_{2}\right)-h\left(z_{1}\right)}{z_{2}-z_{1}}-h^{\prime}(0)\right|\leq\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left|a_{n}\right|nr^{n-1}

and

\left|\frac{g\left(z_{2}\right)-g\left(z_{1}\right)}{z_{2}-z_{1}}-g^{\prime}(0)\right|\leq\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left|b_{n}\right|nr^{n-1}.

Hence

(3.5)

|f(z_{2})-f(z_{1})|\geq\left|z_{2}-z_{1}\right|\left(1-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(\left|a_{n}\right|+\left|b_{n}\right|\right)nr^{n-1}\right).

Using (
3.5
) and Theorem
2.1
, we obtain

\left|\frac{f(z_{2})-f(z_{1})}{z_{2}-z_{1}}\right|\geq 1-\left(K\lambda+\sqrt{K^{\prime}}\right)\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}r^{n-1}=1-\left(K\lambda+\sqrt{K^{\prime}}\right)\frac{r}{1-r}.

Since
z_{2}\neq z_{1}
, so
f\left(z_{2}\right)\neq f\left(z_{1}\right)
only if

1-\left(K\lambda+\sqrt{K^{\prime}}\right)\frac{r}{1-r}>0

which implies that

r<\frac{1}{1+K\lambda+\sqrt{K^{\prime}}}=\rho_{1}.

Hence,
f
is univalent in the disc
\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{1}}.

This completes the proof of the first part of the theorem.

To prove the second part of the theorem, let
|z|=\rho_{1}
, by Theorem
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle|f(z)|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bar{b}_{n}\bar{z}^{n}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|a_{1}z+\bar{b}_{1}\bar{z}^{\cdot}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\bar{b}_{n}\bar{z}^{n}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left|a_{1}z+\bar{b}_{1}\bar{z}\right|-\left|\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\bar{b}_{n}\bar{z}^{n}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left|\left|a_{1}\right|-\left|b_{1}\right|\right||z|-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(\left|a_{n}\right|+\left|b_{n}\right|\right)\left|z^{n}\right|

\displaystyle=\lambda_{f}(0)|z|-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(\left|a_{n}\right|+\left|b_{n}\right|\right)\left|z^{n}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\rho_{1}-(K\lambda+\sqrt{K^{\prime}})\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\rho_{1}^{n}}{n}

\displaystyle=\rho_{1}+\left(K\lambda+\sqrt{K^{\prime}}\right)\left(\rho_{1}+\ln\left(1-\rho_{1}\right)\right):=R_{1}.

Hence
f\left(\mathbb{D}_{\rho_{1}}\right)
contains a schlicht disc of radius
R_{1}
. This completes the proof.

∎

By taking
K^{\prime}=0
in Theorem
2.5
, we obtain the proof of Corollary
2.6
.

Proof of Theorem
2.7
.

Since
J_{f}(0)=1
, we have

\displaystyle 1=|f_{z}(0)|^{2}-|f_{\bar{z}}(0)|^{2}

\displaystyle=(|f_{z}(0)|+|f_{\bar{z}}(0)|)(|f_{z}(0)|-|f_{\bar{z}}(0)|)

\displaystyle\leq K\left(\lambda_{f}(0)\right)^{2}

which implies that

\lambda_{f}(0)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}.

Fix
r\in(0,1)
and
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{\overline{D}}_{r}
with
z_{1}\neq z_{2}
. Then by proof of Theorem
2.5
and Corollary
2.4
we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\frac{f(z_{2})-f(z_{1})}{z_{2}-z_{1}}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{f}(0)-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|\right)nr^{n-1}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}-K\lambda\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}r^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}-\frac{K\lambda r}{1-r}.

Since
z_{1}\neq z_{2}
, so
f\left(z_{2}\right)\neq f\left(z_{2}\right)
only if

\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}-\frac{k\lambda r}{1-r}>0,

which implies that

r<\frac{1}{1+\lambda k^{3/2}}=\rho(\lambda).

Hence,
f\left(z_{2}\right)\neq f\left(z_{1}\right)
for
r\in(0,\rho)
. This shows that
f
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}\rho
.

For
|z|=\rho
, by using the proof of the Theorem
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle|f(z)|

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{f}(0)|z|-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|\right)|z^{n}|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{K}}-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{K\lambda}{n}\rho^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{K}}+K\lambda(\rho+\ln(1-\rho))

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{K}}+K\lambda\left(\rho+\ln(\lambda K^{3/2}\rho)\right)=R(\lambda).

Hence,
f(\mathbb{D}_{\rho})
contains a schlicht disc of radius
R(\lambda).
This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.8
.

Let
f=h+\bar{g}
be a
K
-quasiconformal harmonic self mapping on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, where
h
and
g
are analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and given by

h(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad g(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}.

Fix
r\in(0,1)
, then for
z=re^{i\theta}
, we have

f(re^{i\theta})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}r^{n}e^{in\theta}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bar{b}_{n}r^{n}e^{-in\theta}.

Multiplying both the sides by
e^{-in\theta}
and integrating with respect to
\theta
from 0 to
2\pi
, we get

(3.6)

a_{n}r^{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f\left(re^{i\theta}\right)e^{-in\theta}d\theta.

Similarly,

(3.7)

\bar{b}_{n}r^{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f\left(re^{i\theta}\right)e^{in\theta}d\theta.

It is easy to see that an integration by part of (
3.6
) gives

a_{n}r^{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi in}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f_{\theta}\left(re^{i\theta}\right)e^{-in\theta}d\theta.

which implies that

(3.8)

|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi n}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|f_{\theta}(re^{i\theta})\right|d\theta.

Similarly, we see that an integration by part of (
3.7
) gives

(3.9)

|b_{n}|r^{n}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi n}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|f_{\theta}(re^{i\theta})\right|d\theta.

In 2008, Kalaj
[
10
]
proved that the radial limits of
f_{\theta}
and
f_{r}
exists almost everywhere and

\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}f_{\theta}\left(re^{i\theta}\right)=f^{\prime}(\theta).

Therefore taking
r\to 1^{-}
and by Theorem F in (
3.8
), we get

\displaystyle|a_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|f^{\prime}(\theta)\right|d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K^{3K}2^{5(K-1/K)/2}}{n}.

Similarly, by letting
r\to 1^{-}
and by Theorem F in (
3.9
), we obtain

|b_{n}|\leq\frac{K^{3K}2^{5(K-1/K)/2}}{n}.

This completes the proof.
∎

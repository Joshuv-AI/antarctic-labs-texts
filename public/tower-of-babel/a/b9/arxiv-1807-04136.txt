Shehryar Sikander

Abstract
. We give an expression for the pull back of the Hitchin connection from the moduli space of genus two curves to a ten-fold covering of a Teichmüller curve discovered by Veech. We then give an expression, in terms of iterated integrals, for the monodromy representation of this connection. As a corollary we obtain quantum representations of infinitely many pseudo-Anosov elements in the genus two mapping class group.



1.
Introduction

Let
S_{g}
be a closed connected and oriented surface of genus
g\geqslant 2
, and consider its
mapping class group

\Gamma_{g}
of orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms up to isotopy.
More precisely,

(1)

\Gamma_{g}:=\text{Diffeo}^{+}(S_{g})/\text{Diffeo}^{+}_{0}(S_{g}),

where
\text{Diffeo}^{+}(S_{g})
is the group of orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms of
S_{g}
, and
\text{Diffeo}_{0}^{+}(S_{g})
denotes the connected component of the identity.
The mapping class group is of fundamental importance in low-dimensional topology, and has been studied for more than a century. Excellent surveys on recent progress and open problems related to the group
\Gamma_{g}
can be found in
[
Ham11
,
Far06
]
, and a comprehensive treatment is given in
[
FM11
]
.
Recently, and perhaps surprisingly, ideas from quantum field theory have been applied very successfully to answer questions about the group
\Gamma_{g}
, which
resisted all traditional methods.

In the seminal paper
[
Wit89
]
, Witten laid the foundation of a three-dimensional topological quantum field theory, now called the
Witten-Reshetikhin-Turaev
TQFT. Implicit in this TQFT is a finite-dimensional projective linear representation of
\Gamma_{g}
, for any positive integer
k
, which is called the
quantum representation
at level
k
. These representations provide a powerful tool for studying the mapping class groups. Indeed, the collection of representations can distinguish any two mapping classes, except the identity and the hyper-elliptic involution in genus 2. This is the content of Turaev’s asymptotic faithfulness conjecture, which was proved by Andersen using the theory of Toeplitz operators
[
And06
,
FWW02
]
.
By applying similar technology, the quantum representations were also used to prove that
\Gamma_{g}
does not have Kazhdan’s property (T)
[
And
]
. Finally, the quantum representations have been used by Masbaum and Reid to show that any finite group occurs as a quotient of a finite-index subgroup of
\Gamma_{g}

[
MR12
]
.

There are by now (at least) three different rigorous constructions of the quantum representations of
\Gamma_{g}
. The first uses representation theory of the quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}(2))
, with
q
a fixed root of unity
[
RT90
,
RT91
,
Tur94
]
, the second gives a combinatorial construction using skein theory
[
BHMV95
]
, and the third provides a geometric description involving the parallel transport of a projectively flat connection in a vector bundle over the moduli space of smooth compact Riemann surfaces of genus
g
.
The fact that these three constructions are equivalent follows from
[
AU12
]
and
[
Las98
]
.

While quantum representations have been utilized successfully to answer questions about
\Gamma_{g}
, they are themselves far from being well understood.
Amongst all mapping classes, the quantum representations of pseudo-Anosov mapping classes
are the most mysterious. A number of problems in this direction are discussed in
[
AMU06
]
and chapter seven of
[
Oht02
]
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{g}
be the
Teichmüller space
of marked Riemann surfaces of genus
g
. It is well-known that
\mathcal{T}_{g}
has the structure of a complex manifold which is homeomorphic to an open ball in
\mathbb{C}^{3g-3}
. Moreover, it follows from Royden’s work that
\mathcal{T}_{g}
is
Kobayashi hyperbolic
.
The group
\Gamma_{g}
has a natural and properly discontinuous action on
\mathcal{T}_{g}
. In fact, for
g\geqslant 3
, Royden showed that
\Gamma_{g}
can be identified with the group of biholomorphic automorphims of
\mathcal{T}_{g}
, but in genus two, the hyperelliptic involution
\sigma
acts trivially, and the automorphisms of
\mathcal{T}_{2}
amount to
\Gamma_{2}/\langle\sigma\rangle
. The quotient
\mathcal{M}_{g}:=\mathcal{T}_{g}/\Gamma_{g}
is a complex orbifold called Riemann’s moduli space, and the orbifold singularities correspond to equivalence classes of Riemann surfaces with non-trivial automorphism group. Since
\mathcal{T}_{g}
is simply connected, the mapping class group
\Gamma_{g}
can be identified with the orbifold fundamental group
\pi_{1}^{orb}(\mathcal{M}_{g},*)
. Morevoer,
\mathcal{M}_{g}
is also Kobayashi hyperbolic since the Kobayashi metric is invariant under biholomorphic automorphisms.

Let
X
be a compact Riemann surface of genus
g\geqslant 2
and let
\mathcal{M}_{X}^{B}
be the moduli space of semi-stable vector bundles of rank 2 with trivial determinant on
X
.
Drezet and Narasimhan showed that
\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal{M}^{B}_{X})\cong\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\mathcal{L}_{X}\to\mathcal{M}_{X}^{B}
denote the ample generator of the group
\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal{M}^{B}_{X})
, and let
H^{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle($}k\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle)$}}_{X}:=H^{0}(\mathcal{M}^{B}_{X},\mathcal{L}^{k}_{X})
for any positive integer
k
. The dimension of this vector space is given by the Verlinde formula (
16
).

From
[
ADPW91
]
,
[
Hit90
]
, and
[
Fal93
]
, it follows that the vector spaces
H^{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle($}k\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle)$}}_{X}
can be glued together to form a vector bundle
\mathcal{H}^{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle($}k\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle)$}}\to\mathcal{T}_{g}
with a projectively flat connection in the associated projective bundle. We denote this projectively flat vector bundle by

(2)

(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{H}^{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle($}k\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle)$}},{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}^{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle($}k\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle)$}})\to\mathcal{T}_{g}.

The action of
\Gamma_{g}
on
\mathcal{T}_{g}
lifts to
an action on the projective bundle
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{H}^{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle($}k\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle)$}}\to\mathcal{T}_{g}
which preserves the connection
\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle($}k\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle)$}}
.
Consequently, upon passing to the quotient by this action we obtain the projectively flat vector bundle

(3)

(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{\check{H}}^{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle($}k\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle)$}},\check{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}^{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle($}k\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle)$}})\to\mathcal{M}_{g}.

Once a base point in
\mathcal{M}_{g}
is chosen, the monodromy of (
3
) gives rise to the quantum representation

(4)

\rho^{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle($}k\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle)$}}_{g}\colon\Gamma_{g}\cong\pi_{1}^{orb}(\mathcal{M}_{g},*)\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{\check{H}}^{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle($}k\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle)$}}_{\scriptscriptstyle*}).

A Teichmüller curve is a pair
(V,\phi)
where
V
is a finite area hyperbolic surface
and

\phi\colon V\to\mathcal{M}_{g}

is a generically injective holomorphic map which is an isometry for the Kobayashi metrics on the domain and the codomain. Equivalently, a Teichmüller curve is an algebraic curve in
\mathcal{M}_{g}
which is a complex geodesic for the Kobayashi (=Teichmüller) metric. A typical construction of Teichmüller curves comes from
Veech surfaces
. A Veech surface generates a complex geodesic
\mathbb{H}\to\mathcal{T}_{g}
for the Kobayashi metric, such that the image of
\mathbb{H}
under the composition with the qoutient map
\mathbb{H}\to\mathcal{T}_{g}\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
is an algebraic curve: This curve is naturally a complex geodesic for the Kobayashi metric and is thus a Teichmüller curve. Suppose a Teichmüller curve
(V,\phi)
is such that
\phi(V)
comes from a Veech surface, then
\pi_{1}^{orb}(V,*)
can be identified as the subgroup of
\pi_{1}^{orb}(\mathcal{M}_{g},\phi(*))\cong\Gamma_{g}
which preserves (setwise) the image of the Kobayashi geodesic
\mathbb{H}\to\mathcal{T}_{g}
under its action on
\mathcal{T}_{g}
. See section
3
for precise definitions and more details.

Using the holomorphic map
\phi\colon V\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
, one can pull back the projectively flat vector bundle (
3
) which we denote by

(5)

(\mathbb{P}\mathcal{H}^{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle($}k\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle)$}}_{\scriptscriptstyle V},\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle($}k\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle)$}}_{\scriptscriptstyle V})\to V.

Since this is a projectively flat vector bundle on a Riemann surface, computing its monodromy is considerably easier
than computing the monodromy of (
3
), and one can hope to obtain explicit matrices for the monodromy representation of
\pi_{1}^{orb}(V,*)
with respect to
\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle($}k\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle)$}}_{\scriptscriptstyle V}
. Assuming
\phi(V)
comes from a Veech surface, these explicit matrices will then correspond to the quantum representation of a subgroup of
\Gamma_{g}
. Moreover, since
\phi(V)
is a totally geodesic submanifold with respect to the Teichmüller metric, it follows from Bers’ characterization of pseudo-Anosov mapping classes that each geodesic on
V
corresponds to a pseudo-Anosov mapping class. Taking appropriate products of quantum representations of the generators of
\pi_{1}^{orb}(V,*)
will then give explicit matrices for the quantum representations of pseudo-Anosov elements. We refer to section
3
for further details.

In the case where

These two isomorphisms together imply that a choice of a level two theta structure on

[

]

[

]
.

Let

. The map (

[

]
, which implies that (

) is a closed irreducible subvariety of dimension

. In particular, (

) is a divisor, called the canonical theta divisor associated with

. Let

be the line bundle associated with the divisor

, and let

. For a positive integer

Let

2.1.
The Heisenberg group

Let

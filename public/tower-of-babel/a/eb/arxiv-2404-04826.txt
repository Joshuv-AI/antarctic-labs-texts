Two-photon production of f 0 f_{0} and a 0 a_{0} resonances as hadronic molecules composed of two vector mesons
Li-Ke Yang, Zheng-Li Wang, Jia-Jun Wu, Bing-Song Zou

Abstract
Ascribed as ρ ​ ρ \rho\rho and K ∗ ​ K ¯ ∗ K^{*}\bar{K}^{*} molecular states, respectively, iso-scalar f 0 ​ ( 1500 ) f_{0}(1500) and f 0 ​ ( 1710 ) f_{0}(1710) states are expected to have iso-vector partners, potentially identified as a 0 ​ ( 1450 ) a_{0}(1450) and a 0 ​ ( 1710 ) a_{0}(1710) . The predicted dominant decay modes for these two a 0 a_{0} resonances are a 0 ​ ( 1450 ) → ω ​ π ​ π a_{0}(1450)\to\omega\pi\pi and a 0 ​ ( 1710 ) → ω ​ π ​ π , ϕ ​ π ​ π a_{0}(1710)\to\omega\pi\pi,\,\phi\pi\pi . We estimate cross sections for two-photon production of these four resonances within hadronic molecular picture, and demonstrate that SuperKEKB’s luminosity is sufficient for their observation and more precise parameter measurements.



I
Introduction

Understanding the hadron spectrum is one of the most important challenges in hadron physics. There are various models interpreting the observed states, and selecting the right one is a key issue. These models can be differentiated by examining their predictions of the complete spectrum, with consideration of fundamental symmetries. For example, in the physical depiction of
K\bar{K}
molecules, both iso-scalar and iso-vector states are symmetrically generated, corresponding to the existing states
f_{0}(980)
and
a_{0}(980)

[
1
,
2
]
.

The
f_{0}(1370)
was previously considered to be an iso-scalar
\rho\rho
molecular state, dynamically generated by the
\rho\rho
interaction
[
3
,
4
]
. However, the inclusion of coupled-channels of pseudoscalar mesons suggests that the pole and partial decay widths are more closely aligned with those of the
f_{0}(1500)
listed in the Review of Particle Physics (RPP)
[
5
]
.

Similarly,
f_{0}(1710)
is interpreted as the iso-scalar
K^{*}\bar{K}^{*}
molecular state, dynamically generated by the vector meson-vector meson interaction
[
3
,
6
]
.
Including the coupled-channels of pseudoscalar mesons does not significantly shift the pole, and the mass, width, and partial widths are consistent with those of
f_{0}(1710)
reported in the RPP
[
7
]
.

However, the measurement as well as exploration of the iso-vector partners of
f_{0}(1500)
and
f_{0}(1710)
as hadronic molecules are less explicit. Given that
\rho\rho
cannot form an
a_{0}
state within the isospin formalism due to Bose-Einstein statistics, which requires
l+S+I=\text{even}
,
\rho\omega
emerges as the most promising candidate for forming the iso-vector partner of
f_{0}(1500)
. This partner is postulated to be the
a_{0}(1450)

[
8
]
. Meanwhile, the iso-vector partner of
f_{0}(1710)
is still missing, potentially identifiable as
a_{0}(1710)

[
8
]
.

Therefore, to validate the vector meson-vector meson molecule model of
f_{0}(1500)
and
f_{0}(1710)
, it is essential to confirm the nature of both
a_{0}(1450)
and
a_{0}(1710)
. However, up to now, different laboratories have not reached a consensus on the measured masses of
a_{0}(1450)
and
a_{0}(1710)
. The situation is even worse when it comes to the measurement of the width. For
a_{0}(1450)
, measurements vary across different experiments, while for
a_{0}(1710)
, only two results have been reported so far.

A notable measurement of
a_{0}(1450)
is from Belle in the study of the reaction
\gamma\gamma\to\eta\pi^{0}

[
9
]
. The partial-wave analysis of the differential cross section indicates the presence of a resonance that could be possibly attributed to
a_{0}(1450)
. However, its nominal fit mass is
1316.8\,\text{MeV}
, with no corresponding peak for
a_{0}(1450)
in the total cross section spectrum. In fact, if
a_{0}(1450)
is a hadronic molecule, its dominant decay channel is predicted to be
\omega\pi\pi

[
8
]
, so investigating
\gamma\gamma\to\omega\pi\pi
is crucial to finding
a_{0}(1450)
.

The BaBar Collaboration observed
a_{0}(1700)\to\eta\pi
in their
\gamma\gamma\to\eta_{c}\to\eta\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
analysis
[
10
]
. The BESIII Collaboration also found evidence supporting the existence of an iso-vector partner of
f_{0}(1710)

[
11
]
, reinforcing the existence of
a_{0}(1710)
with a mass close to that of
f_{0}(1710)
. However, a newly observed
a_{0}
-like state with a mass of
1.817\,\text{GeV}
is also suspected to be
a_{0}(1710)

[
12
]
.

Therefore, it is important to identify suitable reactions for the search of
a_{0}(1450)
and
a_{0}(1710)
to obtain more accurate measurements. The two-photon production reaction could be an ideal place to detect
a_{0}(1450)
,
a_{0}(1710)
, as well as
f_{0}(1500)
and
f_{0}(1710)
. In this process, one can exclude additional hadrons in the final state, except for the decay products of the target resonance, such as observing
a_{0}(1450)
in
\gamma\gamma\to\omega\pi^{+}\pi^{-},\eta\pi^{0}
. Moreover, with the full upgrade of KEKB to SuperKEKB, it is expected to provide 40 times more two-photon production events compared to KEKB
[
13
]
.

In this paper, we calculate the cross sections for the two-photon production of
a_{0}(1450)
,
a_{0}(1710)
,
f_{0}(1500)
, and
f_{0}(1710)
, followed by their subsequent decays into main decay channels, exemplified by
\gamma\gamma\to a_{0}(1450)\to\omega\pi\pi
. We also present the expected
N_{p}
s for these reactions at SuperKEKB. The paper is organized as follows: In Sec.
II
, we mainly present the Lagrangians and other needed definitions. In Sec.
III
, we show the numerical results and discussions. Finally we conclude with a summary in Sec.
IV
.

II
Formalism

II.1
The Interaction Vertices

The effective Lagrangians involved are as follows
[
14
]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{VVV}

\displaystyle=ig\langle(\partial_{\mu}V_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}V_{\mu})V^{\mu}V^{\nu}\rangle,

(1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{V\gamma}

\displaystyle=-M_{V}^{2}\frac{e}{g}A_{\mu}\langle V^{\mu}Q\rangle,

(2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{VPP}

\displaystyle=-ig\langle V^{\mu}[P,\partial_{\mu}P]\rangle,

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{V_{1}V_{2}a_{0}}

\displaystyle=g_{a_{0}V_{1}V_{2}}\phi_{a_{0}}V_{1}^{\mu}V_{2\mu},

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{V_{1}V_{2}f_{0}}

\displaystyle=g_{f_{0}V_{1}V_{2}}\phi_{f_{0}}V_{1}^{\mu}V_{2\mu}.

(5)

g=M_{V}/2f
is the hidden gauge coupling constant, where
f=93\,\text{MeV}
the pion decay constant and
M_{V}
is the mass of the vector meson
V
;
\langle...\rangle
denotes the trace in SU(3) space;
Q=diag(2,-1,-1)/3
, and
e=-|e|
is the electron charge;
\phi_{S}
is the field of the scalar resonance
S
;
V_{i}^{\mu}
, with
i=1,2
, are the fields of the vector mesons
V_{i}
. Additionally,
V_{\mu}
is the vector field matrix defined as follows:

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\rho^{0}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\omega&\rho^{+}&K^{*+}\\
\rho^{-}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\rho^{0}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\omega&K^{*0}\\
K^{*-}&\bar{K}^{*0}&\phi\end{array}\right)_{\mu},

(6)

and
P
is the pseudoscalar field matrix defined as follows:

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\pi^{0}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\eta&\pi^{+}&K^{+}\\
\pi^{-}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\pi^{0}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\eta&K^{0}\\
K^{-}&\bar{K}^{0}&-\frac{2}{\sqrt{6}}\eta\end{array}\right).

(7)

According to the Lagrangians, we can define the following tensors corresponding to different vertex structures:

\displaystyle(V_{SV_{1}V_{2}})^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=g_{SV_{1}V_{2}}g^{\mu\nu},

(8)

\displaystyle V_{SP_{1}P_{2}}

\displaystyle=g_{SP_{1}P_{2}},

(9)

\displaystyle(V_{V_{1}V_{2}V_{3}})^{\alpha\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=g_{V_{1}V_{2}V_{3}}\left\{(k_{V_{1}}^{\nu}-k_{V_{2}}^{\nu})g^{\alpha\mu}+\right.

\displaystyle\left.(k_{V_{2}}^{\alpha}-k_{V_{3}}^{\alpha})g^{\mu\nu}+(k_{V_{3}}^{\mu}-k_{V_{1}}^{\mu})g^{\nu\alpha}\right\},

(10)

\displaystyle(V_{\gamma V})^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=g_{\gamma V}g^{\mu\nu},

(11)

\displaystyle(V_{VP_{1}P_{2}})^{\mu}

\displaystyle=g_{VP_{1}P_{2}}(k^{\mu}_{P_{1}}-k^{\mu}_{P_{2}}).

(12)

Note that we assume all particles in
(V_{V_{1}V_{2}V_{3}})^{\alpha\mu\nu}
to be incident particles approaching the vertex.
In practice, note that for
(V_{\rho\pi\pi})^{\mu}
we have:

\displaystyle(V_{\rho\pi\pi})^{\mu}

\displaystyle=(V_{\rho^{0}\pi^{+}\pi^{-}})^{\mu}+(V_{\rho^{+}\pi^{0}\pi^{+}})^{\mu}+(V_{\rho^{-}\pi^{0}\pi^{-}})^{\mu}.

(13)

II.2
Propogators

The propogators for spin 0 resonance and vector meson are defined as follows:

\displaystyle G_{R}(k)=\frac{1}{k^{2}-m_{R}^{2}+im_{R}\Gamma_{R}},

(14)

\displaystyle(G_{V}(k))_{\mu\nu}=\frac{-\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}}{k^{2}-m_{V}^{2}+im_{V}\Gamma_{R}},

(15)

where k is the 4-momentum, and
m,\,\Gamma
are the mass and total width of the particle. Particularly, in the case of a vector meson in
\gamma V
vertex, where the 4-momentum satisfies
k^{2}=0
, the propagator is as follows:

\displaystyle(G^{\prime}_{V})_{\mu\nu}=\frac{g_{\mu\nu}}{m_{V}^{2}}.

(16)

Definition of
\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}
is as follows
[
15
]
:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=1}^{3}\epsilon_{\mu}(m)\epsilon^{*}_{\nu}(m)

\displaystyle=-g_{\mu\nu}+\frac{p_{\mu}p_{\nu}}{p^{2}}

\displaystyle\equiv-\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}(p),

(17)

where
\epsilon_{\mu}(m)
is the polarization vector for a massive vector meson with its 4-momentum to be
p_{\mu}
and its thrid component of spin in its rest frame to be
m
.

Next, consider the photon polarization vector
A_{\mu}
with 4-momentum
q_{\mu}
. We take into account both the Lorentz orthogonality condition
A_{\mu}q^{\mu}=0
and an additional gauge invariance condition. We assume the Coulomb gauge
A_{\mu}Q^{\mu}=0
in the c.m. frame, where
Q^{\mu}
is the summation of the momentum of two incoming photons. Let
K_{\mu}=Q_{\mu}-q_{\mu}
, the 4-momentum of the other incoming photon. The summation of polarization vectors is then given as follows
[
15
]
:

\displaystyle\sum_{m}A_{\mu}(m)A^{*}_{\nu}(m)

\displaystyle=-g_{\mu\nu}+\frac{q_{\mu}K_{\nu}+K_{\mu}q_{\nu}}{q\cdot K}

\displaystyle\equiv-g_{\mu\nu}^{\bot\bot}.

(18)

II.3
Form Factors

For each triangle loop in the Feynman diagram as shown in Fig.
2
and Fig.
5
, we introduce two types of form factors. The first type is a form factor with a smooth cutoff that serves to regularize the loop integration:

f_{\Lambda}(k)=\exp\left[{-\frac{|\vec{k}|^{2}}{(\Lambda_{1})^{2}}}\right],

(19)

where
\Lambda_{1}=1.0\,\text{GeV}

[
7
]
.
The second type is a dipole form factor for the off-shell exchanged meson
\bar{K}^{*}
:

F_{K^{*}}(k)=\frac{(\Lambda_{2})^{4}}{(\Lambda_{2})^{4}+(k^{2}-m_{K^{*}}^{2})^{2}},

(20)

where
\Lambda_{2}=1.0\,\text{GeV}
.
Note that the empirical values for the cut-off parameters in such hadronic form factors range from 0.5 to 2.0 GeV. For simplicity, we take both
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
to be
1.0\,\text{GeV}
in our calculation of the triangle hadronic loops.

For the off-shell mesons
\rho
,
\omega
, and
\phi
in the
\gamma V
vertex, we also introduce a dipole form factor:

F^{\prime}_{V}(p)=\frac{(\Lambda_{3})^{4}}{(\Lambda_{3})^{4}+(p^{2}-m_{V}^{2})^{2}},

(21)

where
\Lambda_{3}=0.63\text{--}0.83\,\text{GeV}
. The determination of
\Lambda_{3}
will be discussed in Sec.
II.6
. Besides, notice that
p^{2}=0
for the mesons coupling to the incoming photons, and we shall simply denote
F^{\prime}_{V}(p)
as
F^{\prime}_{V}
.

II.4
The Scattering Amplitudes

We can define a function
G(k_{1},k_{2},k_{5})
, which combines the propogators in the loop with the aforementioned form factors, to calculate the triangle loop
K^{*}(k_{3})-\bar{K}^{*}(k_{4})-\bar{K}^{*}(k_{5})
, where
\bar{K}^{*}(k_{5})
is the exchanged meson, and
k_{3}=k_{1}+k_{5}
,
k_{4}=k_{2}-k_{5}
:

\displaystyle G(k_{1},k_{2},k_{5})=F_{K^{*}}(k_{5})\frac{1}{k_{5}^{2}-m_{\bar{K}^{*}}^{2}+i\epsilon}

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{(k_{1}+k_{5})^{2}-m_{K^{*}}^{2}+i\epsilon}\frac{1}{(k_{2}-k_{5})^{2}-m_{\bar{K}^{*}}^{2}+i\epsilon}

\displaystyle\times\frac{(\Lambda_{2})^{4}}{(\Lambda_{2})^{4}+(k_{5}^{2}-m_{\bar{K}^{*}}^{2})^{2}}\exp\left[{-\frac{|\vec{k}_{5}|^{2}}{(\Lambda_{1})^{2}}}\right].

(22)

Next, we proceed to write down the scattering amplitude. In this section, we present one scattering amplitude as an example, while the remaining scattering amplitudes can be found in Appendix
B
.

For the Feynman diagram as shown in Fig.
2(a)
, the corresponding scattering amplitude of
\gamma(k_{1})\gamma(k_{2})\to f_{0}(1710)\to\pi(p_{1})\pi(p_{2})
is as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{2a}

\displaystyle=A_{1\mu}(m_{1},k_{1})A_{2\xi}(m_{2},k_{2})(F^{\prime}_{\rho})^{2}

\displaystyle\times(V_{\gamma(k_{1})\rho})^{\mu\nu}(V_{\gamma(k_{2})\rho})^{\xi\sigma}(G^{\prime}_{\rho})_{\nu\alpha}(G^{\prime}_{\rho})_{\sigma\kappa}

\displaystyle\times(V_{f_{0}K^{*}\bar{K}^{*}})^{\delta\eta}G_{f_{0}}(k_{1}+k_{2})V_{f_{0}P_{1}P_{2}}

\displaystyle\times\int\frac{id^{4}k_{5}}{(2\pi)^{4}}(-\tilde{g}_{\beta\delta}(k_{3}))(-\tilde{g}_{\lambda\eta}(k_{4}))(-\tilde{g}_{\gamma\theta}(k_{5}))

\displaystyle\times G(k_{1},k_{2},k_{5})\left\{(V_{\rho K^{*+}K^{*-}})^{\alpha\beta\gamma}(V_{\rho K^{*-}K^{*+}})^{\kappa\lambda\theta}\right.

\displaystyle+(V_{\rho K^{*-}K^{*+}})^{\alpha\beta\gamma}(V_{\rho K^{*+}K^{*-}})^{\kappa\lambda\theta}

\displaystyle+(V_{\rho K^{*0}\bar{K}^{*0}})^{\alpha\beta\gamma}(V_{\rho\bar{K}^{*0}K^{*0}})^{\kappa\lambda\theta}

\displaystyle+\left.(V_{\rho\bar{K}^{*0}K^{*0}})^{\alpha\beta\gamma}(V_{\rho K^{*0}\bar{K}^{*0}})^{\kappa\lambda\theta}\right\},

(23)

where
A_{1\mu},A_{2\xi}
are the polarization vectors of the two incoming photons.

II.5
Coupling Constants

The coupling constants
g_{\gamma V}
involved in the tensor
(V_{\gamma V})^{\mu\nu}
are given as follows:

\displaystyle g_{\gamma\rho}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}M_{\rho}^{2}\frac{e}{g},

(24)

\displaystyle g_{\gamma\omega}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3\sqrt{2}}M_{\omega}^{2}\frac{e}{g},

(25)

\displaystyle g_{\gamma\phi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}M_{\phi}^{2}\frac{e}{g}.

(26)

The coupling constants
g_{V_{1}V_{2}V_{3}}
involved
in the tensor
(V_{V_{1}V_{2}V_{3}})^{\mu\nu\alpha}
are given as follows:

\displaystyle g_{\rho^{0}K^{*+}K^{*-}}=g_{\rho^{0}K^{*-}K^{*+}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}ig,

(27)

\displaystyle g_{\rho^{0}K^{*0}\bar{K}^{*0}}=g_{\rho^{0}\bar{K}^{*0}K^{*0}}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}ig,

(28)

\displaystyle g_{\omega K^{*+}K^{*-}}=g_{\omega K^{*-}K^{*+}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}ig,

(29)

\displaystyle g_{\omega K^{*0}\bar{K}^{*0}}=g_{\omega\bar{K}^{*0}K^{*0}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}ig,

(30)

\displaystyle g_{\phi K^{*+}K^{*-}}=g_{\phi K^{*-}K^{*+}}=ig,

(31)

\displaystyle g_{\phi K^{*0}\bar{K}^{*0}}=g_{\phi\bar{K}^{*0}K^{*0}}=ig.

(32)

The coupling constant
g_{\rho\pi\pi}
involved in the tensor
(V_{\rho\pi\pi})^{\mu}
is given as follows:

g_{\rho\pi\pi}=-\sqrt{2}ig.

(33)

We now come to discuss the coupling constants involved in the tensors
(V_{SV_{1}V_{2}})^{\mu\nu}
and
V_{SP_{1}P_{2}}
.

For a pure molecular state composed of two constituent particles, its coupling constant with the constituent particles is given by the following formula
[
16
]
:

\frac{g_{RV_{1}V_{2}}^{2}}{4\pi}=\frac{(m_{1}+m_{2})^{\frac{5}{2}}}{(m_{1}m_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sqrt{32\epsilon},

(34)

where
m_{1},\,m_{2}
are the masses of the two component particles
V_{1},\,V_{2}
, and
\epsilon
is the binding energy. With this formula we can obtain
g_{a_{0}(1450)\rho\omega}
,
g_{a_{0}(1710)K^{*}\bar{K}^{*}}
,
g_{a_{0}(1710)\rho\phi}
,
g_{f_{0}(1500)\rho\rho}
and
g_{f_{0}(1710)K^{*}\bar{K}^{*}}
.
Note that
g_{f_{0}(1500)\rho\rho}
gives the coupling constant of
f_{0}(1500)
to both
\rho^{0}\rho^{0}
and
\rho^{+}\rho^{-}
. Specifically, we have
g_{f_{0}(1500)\rho^{0}\rho^{0}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}g_{f_{0}(1500)\rho^{+}\rho^{-}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}g_{f_{0}(1500)\rho\rho}
.

The other coupling constants are deduced from the relavent partial decay widths. For two-body decays, this determination can be performed using the following formula
[
17
]
:

\Gamma=\frac{1}{8\pi}|\mathcal{M}|^{2}\frac{|\vec{p}_{1}|}{M^{2}}.

(35)

For our calculations, the partial widths of
f_{0}(1500)
are based on the RPP recommended values or average values
[
17
]
, whereas the partial widths of
f_{0}(1710)
are derived using the unitary coupled channel approach, as predicted in
[
7
]
. Similarly, the partial widths for
a_{0}(1450)
and
a_{0}(1710)
, given by the same approach, are taken from
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
, respectively. All the necessary partial
widths and the coupling constants obtained using this method are listed in Table I and Table II.

II.6
Cutoff parameter
\Lambda_{3}

Belle has investigated
\gamma\gamma\to VV
and presented the cross section spectrum for
\gamma\gamma\to\phi\omega

[
18
]
. The phase space is expected to drop significantly near the threshold, leading to a decrease in the cross section. However, the contribution from the
0^{+}
state does not seem to decrease near
2.0\,\text{GeV}
, likely due to the contribution from
f_{0}(1710)
. Therefore, by fitting the partial wave data of
0^{+}
contribution from Belle for
\gamma\gamma\to\phi\omega

[
18
]
, we can determine the value of
\Lambda_{3}
, which represents the cutoff parameter applied in the dipole form factor for the vector mesons
\rho,\,\omega,\,\phi
in the
\gamma V
vertex. The corresponding Feynman diagram is shown in Fig.
5
.

Figure 5
:
Feynman diagram for
\gamma\gamma\to f_{0}(1710)\to\phi\omega

Note that the two vector mesons
(V(k_{1}),V(k_{2}))
represented at the
\gamma V
vertex in Fig.
5
include the following pairs:
(\rho^{0}(k_{1}),\rho^{0}(k_{2}))
,
(\phi(k_{1}),\phi(k_{2}))
,
(\omega(k_{1}),\omega(k_{2}))
,
(\phi(k_{1}),\omega(k_{2}))
,
(\omega(k_{1}),\phi(k_{2}))
. The corresponding scattering amplitude is presented in Appendix
B
. The total width of
f_{0}(1710)
is taken to be
0.150\,\text{GeV}

[
17
]
. As for the mass, we adopt a range from
1.71\,\text{GeV}

[
7
]
to
1.78\,\text{GeV}
, which is the mass threshold of
K^{*}\bar{K}^{*}
.

Through data fitting, we obtained
\Lambda_{3}=0.63\,\text{GeV}
when the mass of
f_{0}(1710)
is
1.71\,\text{GeV}
, and
\Lambda_{3}=0.83\,\text{GeV}
when the mass is
1.78\,\text{GeV}
.
Note that here
\Lambda_{3}
is especially sensitive to mass variations. This sensitivity arises because the coupling constant
g_{f_{0}K^{*}\bar{K}^{*}}
, calculated with Eq. (
34
), decreases significantly as the binding energy
\epsilon
approaches 0.

II.7
Double-Photon Luminosity Function

If the cross section
\sigma_{\gamma\gamma}
for a two-photon production reaction is known, and the integrated luminosity
\int Ldt
for
e^{+}e^{-}
scattering is provided, then we can obtain the event yield for the two-photon production as follows:

Y_{\gamma\gamma}(W)=\sigma_{\gamma\gamma}(W)L_{\gamma\gamma}(W)\int Ldt\Delta W,

(36)

where
W
is the c.m. energy of the two incident photons.
L_{\gamma\gamma}(W)
is the double-photon luminosity function.
\Delta W
is the bin width.
Y_{\gamma\gamma}(W)
represents the event yield for two-photon production in a
W
range between
W
and
W+\Delta W
.

Let
E_{0}
be the initial energy of the electron in the
e^{+}e^{-}
c.m. frame. In the same frame, assume two photons with energies
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, and four-momentum squares
-Q^{2}
and
-P^{2}
, are produced in the
e^{+}e^{-}
scattering. For
Q^{2}\to 0
and
P^{2}\to 0
, the luminosity function
L_{\gamma\gamma}
is given as follows
[

]

L_{\gamma\gamma}(W)=\int f_{\gamma/e}(y,W,Q^{2}_{\text{max}})f_{\gamma/e}(z,W,Q^{2}_{\text{max}})\frac{W}{2E_{0}^{2}}\frac{1}{2r}dr,

(37)

where
y\approx E_{1}/E_{0}
,
z=E_{2}/E_{0}
, and
r=y/z
.

The definition of
f_{\gamma/e}
is given as follows
[
9
]
:

\displaystyle f_{\gamma/e}(z,W,Q^{2}_{\text{max}})=\frac{\alpha}{\pi z}\left\{(1+(1-z)^{2})\frac{1}{2}\right.

\displaystyle\times\left.\int_{ln\left(m_{e}^{2}z^{2}/(1-z)\right)}^{lnQ^{2}_{\text{max}}}F(e^{\nu},W)d\nu-1+z\right\},

(38)

where
\alpha\approx\frac{1}{137}
is the fine-structure constant.
\nu=\ln Q^{2}
and
F(Q^{2},W)=1/(1+Q^{2}/W^{2})^{2}
[
9
]
.
Q^{2}_{\text{max}}=1\,\text{GeV}^{2}
gives a maximum virtuality of the incident photons. Details on the derivation of the expression for
L_{\gamma\gamma}
can be found in Appendix
A
.

IV
Summary

We consider
a_{0}(1450)
and
a_{0}(1710)
as hadronic molecules and calculate the cross sections for the two-photon production reactions
\gamma\gamma\to a_{0}(1450)\to\omega\pi\pi,\,\eta\pi^{0},\,K\bar{K}
,
\gamma\gamma\to a_{0}(1710)\to\phi\pi\pi,\,\omega\pi\pi,\,\eta\pi^{0},\,K\bar{K}
,
\gamma\gamma\to f_{0}(1500)\to\pi\pi,\,K\bar{K},\,4\pi
, and
\gamma\gamma\to f_{0}(1710)\to\pi\pi,\,K\bar{K},\,\eta\eta
, along with the expected event yields at SuperKEKB.

Based on our calculations, the peak cross sections for the reactions
\gamma\gamma\to f_{0}(1500)\to\pi\pi
and
\gamma\gamma\to f_{0}(1710)\to K\bar{K}
might be comparable to the experimental values reported by Belle in their
\gamma\gamma\to\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
and
\gamma\gamma\to K_{S}^{0}K_{S}^{0}
analysis
[
20
,
21
]
. Moreover, our calculations indicate that the event yield for
\gamma\gamma\to a_{0}(1450)\to\eta\pi^{0}
is not sufficient to form an observable peak in KEKB, which is consistent with the observation by Belle
[
9
]
.

Based on our predictions,
a_{0}(1450)
can be observed in the reaction
\gamma\gamma\to\omega\pi\pi
and may be possibly observed in
\gamma\gamma\to\eta\pi^{0}
.
a_{0}(1710)
could be possibly observed in
\gamma\gamma\to\phi\pi\pi,\,\omega\pi\pi,\,\eta\pi^{0},\,K\bar{K}
at SuperKEKB. Furthermore, observation of
a_{0}(1710)
in reaction
\gamma\gamma\to\phi\pi\pi
could serve as confirmation of its hidden strange quark content.

The reaction
\gamma\gamma\to\omega\pi\pi
is expected to be the most suitable for observing
a_{0}(1450)
, while
\gamma\gamma\to\phi\pi\pi
is expected to be most suitable for observing
a_{0}(1710)
.
If Belle-II successfully utilizes SuperKEKB to observe these reactions and measure the masses and decay widths of
a_{0}(1450)
and
a_{0}(1710)
more accurately, it will help us verify their hadronic molecule interpretations as
\rho\omega
and
K^{*}\bar{K}^{*}
molecular states, respectively.

The

and
f_{0}(1710)
were previously considered as glueball candidates
[
26
,
27
,
28
]
. As iso-scalar glueballs, they would have no iso-vector partners. Therefore, confirming
a_{0}(1450)
and
a_{0}(1710)
as the iso-vector partners of
f_{0}(1500)
and
f_{0}(1710)
is crucial in determining their nature.

If the above hypotheses are successfully verified, it could be confirmed that the resonances
f_{0}(980)
,
f_{0}(1500)
, and
f_{0}(1710)
in the

sector are
K\bar{K}
,
\rho\rho
, and

iso-scalar molecular states, respectively. Similarly, the resonances

,

, and

Appendix A
Supplement to Double-Photon Luminosity Function

In this appendix we detail the derivation of the double-photon luminosity function.

L_{\gamma\gamma}(W)
is the probability distribution function of a two-photon production with a total energy of
W
from a pair of beam particles, which refer to
e^{+}e^{-}
in the context. Its physical significance can be seen from the following equation
[
29
]
:

\sigma_{e^{+}e^{-}}=\int\sigma_{\gamma\gamma}(W)L_{\gamma\gamma}(W)dW.

(42)

Here,
\sigma_{e^{+}e^{-}}
denotes the cross section for the reaction
e^{+}e^{-}\to e^{+}e^{-}X
, where
X
can represent any final-state particles.

To obtain the expression for
L_{\gamma\gamma}
, we first need to derive the expression for
\sigma_{e^{+}e^{-}}
. Under the condition that the two incident photons are nearly on-shell (
Q^{2}\to 0
,
P^{2}\to 0
), Eq. (2.29) in
[
19
]
provides a simplified equation:

\sigma_{e^{+}e^{-}}=\int f_{\gamma/e}(y,W,Q^{2}_{\text{max}})f_{\gamma/e}(z,W,Q^{2}_{\text{max}})\sigma_{\gamma\gamma}(W)dydz.

(43)

Define
s=yz=W^{2}/4E_{0}^{2}
and recall that
r=y/z
. To simplify calculations, we can perform the integration variable transformation as follows:
dy\,dz\to J_{rs}\,dr\,ds
and subsequently
ds\to J_{sW}\,dW
. This introduces two Jacobian factors:

J_{rs}=\left|\frac{\partial(y,z)}{\partial(r,s)}\right|=\frac{1}{2r},

(45)

L_{\gamma\gamma}(W)=\int f_{\gamma/e}(y,W,Q^{2}_{\text{max}})f_{\gamma/e}(z,W,Q^{2}_{\text{max}})\frac{W}{2E_{0}^{2}}\frac{1}{2r}dr,

(46)

as have shown in Sec.
II
.

) is from Eq. (2.19) in
[
19
]
, and

Genuine pro- p p Iwahori–Hecke algebras, Gelfand–Graev representations, and some applications
Fan Gao, Nadya Gurevich, and Edmund Karasiewicz

Abstract
. We study the Iwahori-component of the Gelfand–Graev representation of a central cover of a split linear reductive group and utilize our results for three applications. In fact, it is advantageous to begin at the pro- p p level. Thus to begin we study the structure of a genuine pro- p p Iwahori–Hecke algebra, establishing Iwahori–Matsumoto and Bernstein presentations. With this structure theory we first describe the pro- p p part of the Gelfand–Graev representation and then the more subtle Iwahori part. For the first application we relate the Gelfand–Graev representation to the metaplectic representation of Sahi–Stokman–Venkateswaran, which conceptually realizes the Chinta–Gunnells action from the theory of Weyl group multiple Dirichlet series. For the second we compute the Whittaker dimension of the constituents of regular unramified principal series; for the third we do the same for unitary unramified principal series.

1.
Introduction

Let
F
be a
p
-adic local field with ring of integers
O_{F}
and residue field
\kappa
. Let
{\bf G}
be a smooth reductive linear algebraic group scheme over
O_{F}
. It is known that the generic fiber of
{\bf G}
is quasi-split and split over a finite unramified extension of
F
; for simplicity, we assume that it is actually split over
F
. Write
G:={\bf G}(F)
and
G_{\kappa}:={\bf G}(\kappa)
.

It is a well-known result of Rodier, Gelfand–Kazhdan and Shalika that every irreducible admissible representation
\pi\in{\rm Irr}(G)
has at most one Whittaker model, i.e.,

(1.1)

\dim{\rm Hom}_{G}(\pi,{\rm Ind}_{U^{-}}^{G}\psi)\leqslant 1,

where
U^{-}
is the unipotent radical of a Borel subgroup of
G
and
\psi
is a nondegenerate character of
U^{-}
. This is a
p
-adic analogue of the finite field case, where it was first shown by Gelfand–Graev that the finite field analogue
{\rm Ind}_{U_{\kappa}^{-}}^{G_{\kappa}}\psi_{\kappa}
of
{\rm Ind}_{U^{-}}^{G}\psi
has similar multiplicity-one property. This multiplicity-one property for
{\rm Ind}_{U_{\kappa}^{-}}^{G_{\kappa}}\psi_{\kappa}
follows from the commutativity of its endomorphism algebra
C(U_{\kappa}^{-},\psi_{\kappa}\backslash G_{\kappa}/U_{\kappa}^{-},\psi_{\kappa})
, which can be proved by exhibiting a Chevalley–Steinberg involution, see
[
Car
, §8.1]
or
[
Ste16
, Chapter 14]
. The proof for
G
essentially adapts this idea. The multiplicity one property (
1.1
) for Whittaker models is important for the theory of
L
-functions, especially the Langlands–Shahidi method and some Rankin–Selberg integrals as well.

As a refinement of (
1.1
), it was shown in
[
BuHe03
, Theorem 4.3]
that for simply generic Bernstein class
\mathfrak{s}
with respect to
\psi
, there is a canonical isomorphism

(1.2)

\mathfrak{Z}^{\mathfrak{s}}(G)\longrightarrow{\rm End}_{G}({\rm ind}_{U^{-}}^{G}\psi)^{\mathfrak{s}},

where
\mathfrak{Z}^{\mathfrak{s}}(G)
is the Bernstein center of the subcategory of
{\rm Irr}(G)
associated with
\mathfrak{s}
. Here
{\rm ind}_{U^{-}}^{G}\psi
is the compact induction, whose dual is just
{\rm Ind}_{U^{-}}^{G}\psi^{-1}
. If one concentrates further on the unramified class
\mathfrak{s}=(T,\mathbbm{1})
, then further investigation of the structure of
(({\rm ind}_{U^{-}}^{G}\psi)^{\mathfrak{s}})^{I}=({\rm ind}_{U^{-}}^{G}\psi)^{I}
was given in several works
[
BM1
,
Ree4
,
CS18
]
for example. Here
I\subset G
is the Iwahori subgroup determined by the Borel subgroup
B\subset G
. In particular, it was shown in
[
CS18
]
that if the conductor of
\psi
is
\mathfrak{p}_{F}
, then one has

(1.3)

({\rm ind}_{U^{-}}^{G}\psi)^{I}\simeq\varepsilon_{W}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{W}}\mathcal{H}_{I},

where
\mathcal{H}_{I}=C_{c}^{\infty}(I\backslash G/I)
is the Iwahori–Hecke algebra and
\varepsilon_{W}
the sign character of the finite-dimensional subalgebra
\mathcal{H}_{W}
deformed from
\mathbbm{C}[W]
. Such a result was also obtained from a more general perspective by Brubaker–Bump–Friedberg
[
BBF4
]
. For general
\mathfrak{s}=[T,\chi]
, the structure of

({\rm ind}_{U^{-}}^{G}\psi)^{\mathfrak{s}}
was determined recently by
[
MiPa21
]
.

More generally, one may consider the above problems for central covers of
G
. Assume that
F^{\times}
contains the full group
\mu_{n}
of
n
-th roots of unity. There are natural
n
-fold central covers

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G,}

of which we consider only the genuine representations, i.e.,
\mu_{n}
acts via a fixed embedding
\epsilon:\mu_{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbbm{C}^{\times}
. With the unipotent subgroup
U^{-}
splitting uniquely in
\overline{G}
, one may consider the genuine Gelfand–Graev representation

\mathcal{V}:={\rm ind}_{\mu_{n}U^{-}}^{\overline{G}}(\epsilon\otimes\psi).

In this case, for fixed
\overline{G}
we expect the right hand side of (
1.1
) to be replaced by
\left|\overline{T}/Z(\overline{T})\right|^{1/2}
, where this upper bound is achieved, see
[
GSS2
]
. In particular, multiplicity-one fails.

This multi-dimensionality of Whittaker models for genuine representation was observed decades ago. Though it gives obstacles to the classical theory of
L
-functions, it nevertheless motivates the theory of Weyl group multiple Dirichlet series (WMDS), which dates back at least to the work of Kubota. These WMDS possess meromorphic continuation and functional equation, but are not Eulerian. Conjecturally, they are equal to certain Fourier coefficients of the Borel Eisenstein series of
\overline{G}
. See
[
Bum12
]
for an exposition on this topic. There are various methods to construct such WMDS. One was given by Chinta–Gunnells
[
CG10
]
by utilizing the so-called metaplectic
W
-representation afforded by
\mathbbm{C}(Y)
, where
Y
is the cocharacter lattice of
G
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{I}=C_{\epsilon,c}^{\infty}(I\backslash\overline{G}/I)
be the Iwahori–Hecke algebra of
\overline{G}
. It was shown recently by Sahi–Stokman–Venkateswaran
[
SSV21
]
that the metaplectic
W
-representation of Chinta–Gunnells arises naturally from a certain
\mathcal{H}_{I}
-module afforded on the space
\mathbbm{C}[Y]
, which we call the SSV representation of
\mathcal{H}_{I}
. In fact, the SSV representation is afforded by the bigger space
\mathbbm{C}[P]
, where
P\supset Y
is the coweight lattice. In any case, it should be highlighted that the construction in
[
SSV21
]
is algebraic and uniform and does not involve computer-assisted checking for certain well-definedness of the Weyl group action.

In this paper we continue the study of Whittaker spaces for covering groups, focusing on the following problems:

(P1)

Describe the Iwahori-component of the Gelfand–Graev representation, that is, to determine the
\mathcal{H}_{I}
-module structure of
\mathcal{V}^{I}={\rm ind}_{\mu_{n}U^{-}}^{\overline{G}}(\epsilon\otimes\psi)^{I}
. This will be a direct generalization of
[
CS18
]
to covers.

(P2)

Give a more natural and conceptual interpretation of the SSV representation in terms of
\mathcal{V}^{I}
. Relations between these two and also the metaplectic
W
-representation have been hinted at in several works including
[
Mc2
]

[
CG10
]

[
CO
]
via the presence of the local scattering matrices, which describe the intertwining operators between the Whittaker models of principal series
I(\chi)
and
I({}^{w}\chi)
.

(P3)

Verify some speculative formulas regarding the Whittaker dimension of some Iwahori-spherical representations, especially those irreducible constituents of a regular or unitary unramified principal series, see
[

,
Ga7
,
Ga8
]
.

A solution to (P1) would provide a valuable tool to investigate (P2) and (P3). Unfortunately, naively adapting the strategy of
[
CS18
]
does not work. The fundamental obstacle is that the “supports” of
\mathcal{V}^{I}
and
\mathcal{H}_{I}
do not match. More precisely, for a linear group
G
, the Iwahori–Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{I}
is an affine Hecke algebra deformed from the extended affine Weyl group
Y\rtimes W
. In this case, the twisted Satake transform (see
[
GuKa
]
)

\mathcal{S}_{\psi}:C_{c}^{\infty}(I\backslash G/I)\longrightarrow({\rm ind}_{U^{-}}^{G}\psi)^{I}

of
\mathcal{H}_{I}
-modules is surjective, and realizes (
1.3
). However, for
\overline{G}
, the support of the genuine Iwahori–Hecke algebra only corresponds to
Y_{Q,n}\rtimes W
, where
Y_{Q,n}\subset Y
is a certain sublattice; yet the support of
\mathcal{V}^{I}
still corresponds to
Y\rtimes W
. This is the support mismatch mentioned above. Consequently,
\mathcal{S}_{\psi}
is not surjective for general covering groups.

The support mismatch disappears if
I
is replaced by its maximal pro-
p
subgroup
I_{1}
and we consider the algebra
\mathcal{H}:=C_{\epsilon,c}^{\infty}(I_{1}\backslash\overline{G}/I_{1})
. Our first goal is then the following:

(P0)

Study the larger pro-
p
Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}
instead of
\mathcal{H}_{I}
, including its presentations; explicate the structure of
\mathcal{V}^{I_{1}}
over
\mathcal{H}
.

We leverage these pro-
p

2.1.

Let
p

F

\mathbbm{Q}_{p}
with ring of integers
O=O_{F}
and maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}_{F}
. Let
\varpi\in O_{F}
be a fixed uniformizer, and thus
\mathfrak{p}=\varpi O_{F}
. Let
q
denote the size of the residue field
\kappa=O_{F}/\mathfrak{p}
.

Let
{\bf G}
be a connected reductive linear algebraic group over
O_{F}
. We assume that the generic fiber at
{\rm Spec}(F)
, which is still denoted by
{\bf G}
, is split over
F
with maximal split torus
{\bf T}
. We write

X={\rm Hom}({\bf T},{\bf G}_{m})\text{ and }Y={\rm Hom}({\bf G}_{m},{\bf T})

for the lattice of characters and cocharacters of
{\bf T}
respectively. Here
X
and
Y
form a perfect pairing

{\left\langle-,-\right\rangle}:X\times Y\rightarrow\mathbbm{Z}

given by
x\circ y(t)=t^{\langle x,y\rangle}
for
t\in{\bf G}_{m}
. Let
\Phi
and
\Phi^{\vee}
denote the roots and coroots of
({\bf G},{\bf T})
, respectively. We write
Y^{\mathrm{sc}}\subset Y
for the sublattice generated by the coroots.

Let
N({\bf T})\subset{\bf G}
be the normalizer of
{\bf T}
in
{\bf G}
. This gives the Weyl group

W=N({\bf T})/{\bf T},

which we identify with the Weyl group of the coroot system. Given
\alpha^{\vee}\in\Phi^{\vee}
, we write
w_{\alpha}
for the associated reflection of
Y\otimes\mathbbm{Q}
.

We fix a Borel subgroup
{\bf B}
containing
{\bf T}
with unipotent radical
{\bf U}
. This choice of
{\bf B}
identifies a set of positive roots
\Phi_{+}
(resp. positive coroots
\Phi^{\vee}_{+}
) and simple roots
\Delta\subset\Phi_{+}
(resp. simple coroots
\Delta^{\vee}\subset\Phi^{\vee}_{+}
). The choice of simple roots induces a length function

\ell:W\longrightarrow\mathbbm{Z}_{\geqslant 0}.

Let
{\bf B}^{-}={\bf T}{\bf U}^{-}

Consider the natural map

The function

Lemma 4.12
.

Let

(i)

\mathcal{R}

(ii)

For (i), let

4.4.
Intertwining operators

In this subsection we consider algebraic intertwining operators, following Haines–Kottwitz–Prasad. The construction of these operators mostly involves minor modifications to
[
HKP10
, §1.10]
. Thus we will be brief and focus primarily on the modifications required in the context of covering groups.

To begin, we define a completion of
\mathcal{M}
. Since
\overline{T}
is in general non-commutative, it is more convenient to define our completions using
Z(\mathcal{R})
instead of
\mathcal{R}
. Let
J
be a subset of modified coroots contained in some system of positive modified coroots, and let
\mathbbm{C}[J]
be the subalgebra of
\mathbbm{C}[Y_{Q,n}]
generated by
J
. We write
\mathbbm{C}[J]^{\wedge}
for the completion of
\mathbbm{C}[J]
with respect to the maximal ideal generated by the elements of
J
. We define the
\mathbbm{C}
-algebra

\mathcal{R}_{J}=\mathbbm{C}[J]^{\wedge}\otimes_{\mathbbm{C}[J]}\mathcal{R},

where we view
\mathcal{R}
as an
\mathbbm{C}[J]
-module via the isomorphism of Lemma
4.14
. Set

\mathcal{M}_{J}:=\mathcal{R}_{J}\otimes_{\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{M}\text{ and }\mathcal{H}_{J}:=\mathcal{R}_{J}\otimes_{R}\mathcal{H}.

The rest of
[
HKP10
, §1.10]
applies without essential change.

Since our objective is to derive the Bernstein relations, we consider only the intertwining operators associated to simple reflections. For
\alpha\in\Delta
, let

I_{\alpha}=I_{s_{\alpha}}:\mathcal{M}_{\{-\alpha_{Q,n}^{\vee}\}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\{\alpha_{Q,n}^{\vee}\}}

be the intertwining operator given by

I_{s_{\alpha}}(f)(g)=\int_{U_{\alpha}}f(\overline{w}_{\alpha}(-1)ug)du.

The Bernstein relations follow from an analogue of Haines–Kottwitz–Prasad
[
HKP10
, Lemma 1.13.1 (i)]
. We write
U^{-}(\mathfrak{p}):=U^{-}\cap I
.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
\alpha\in\Delta
and

. Then

\phi_{\alpha}({\rm SL}_{2}(F))\cap UI_{1}=U_{\alpha}(T_{\alpha}\cap I_{1})U_{-\alpha}^{1}.

The result follows from the Bruhat decomposition. Specifically,

\phi_{\alpha}({\rm SL}_{2}(F))=U_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}w_{\alpha}(1)\cup U_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}U_{-\alpha},

and
U_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}w_{\alpha}\cap UU^{-}(\mathfrak{p})\subseteq UTw_{\alpha}U^{-}\cap UTU^{-}=\emptyset
.
∎

Lemma 4.16
.

Let
\alpha\in\Delta
and

and

), (
2.6d
), (
2.6f
), and (

).
∎

Lemma 4.17
.

We have the following equality

This reduces to a rank-one calculation, which we discuss in detail. First, we determine the support of the function
I_{\alpha}(\nu_{1})(g)
. It suffices to consider representatives of the double cosets

\mu_{n}T_{1}U\backslash\overline{G}/I_{1}\cong W(1).

In fact, we can work in
G
instead of
\overline{G}
.

Suppose that
\overline{g}\in\overline{G}
and

. Let

. We may assume that

, where

and

represents an element of the Weyl group. We claim that
w
must represent
1
or
w_{\alpha}
. Since

or
w_{\alpha}

or

Now we determine

and

\bullet

If

Suppose that

4.5.
Bernstein presentation

For every

4.6.

Recall that

\mathbbm{1}_{I}=c(\mathbbm{1})=\frac{1}{\left|T_{\kappa}\right|}\sum_{h\in T_{\kappa}}\mathcal{T}_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}.

can thus be projected onto

. For
g\in\overline{G}
and
t\in\overline{T}
we write

\mathcal{T}_{g}^{I}=\mathbbm{1}_{I}*\mathcal{T}_{g}*\mathbbm{1}_{I},\quad\Theta_{t}^{I}=\mathbbm{1}_{I}*\Theta_{t}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}.

Set

\mathcal{H}_{W}:=\mathbbm{1}_{I}*\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}.

Lemma 4.20
.

The following holds:

(i)

The map

\mathcal{H}_{W}\longrightarrow C^{\infty}(I\backslash K/I)

defined by
f\mapsto f|_{s(K)}
is a
\mathbbm{C}
-algebra isomorphism.

(ii)

The element

is nonzero if and only if

. Moreover, the choice of a genuine character

induces a map

which is an isomorphism of
\mathbbm{C}
-algebras.

(iii)

The map

\mathcal{H}_{W}\otimes_{\mathbbm{C}}\mathcal{A}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{I}

given by
h\otimes a\mapsto h*a

\mathbbm{C}
-vector spaces.

(iv)

(Bernstein Relation) Let
\alpha\in\Delta
and
y\in Y_{Q,n}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{w_{\alpha}(1)}^{I}*\Theta_{w_{\alpha}(-1)\cdot\mathbf{s}_{y}}^{I}+(q-1)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbbm{Z}_{\geqslant 1-\langle y,\alpha\rangle}\\

Proof.

For (i) the proof is identical to that of Lemma
3.3

To prove (ii), one has

\mathbbm{1}_{I}*\Theta_{t}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}\neq 0
if and only if

The map
\mathcal{H}_{W}\otimes_{\mathbbm{C}}\mathcal{A}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{I}
is the restriction of the isomorphsim

from Proposition

we have
\mathbbm{1}_{I}*h=h*\mathbbm{1}_{I}

The assertion (iv) follows by multiplying equation (

) on the left by
\mathbbm{1}_{I}

5.
Gelfand–Graev Representation

In this section we study the Gelfand–Graev representation

\mathcal{V}:=\mathrm{ind}_{\mu_{n}U^{-}}^{\overline{G}}(\epsilon\otimes\psi),

where

\psi:U^{-}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{C}^{\times}

is a nondegenerate character of conductor
\mathfrak{p}
and
\overline{G}
acts by right translation on
\mathcal{V}
. Our objective is to understand
\mathcal{V}^{I}
as an
\mathcal{H}_{I}
-module, for which we begin by studying the finite Gelfand–Graev representation.

5.1.
Finite Gelfand–Graev representation

Since
\psi
has conductor
\mathfrak{p}
, it descends to a non-degenerate character (still denoted by)

\psi:U_{\kappa}^{-}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{C}^{\times}.

Set

\mathcal{V}_{\kappa}:={\rm Ind}_{U_{\kappa}^{-}}^{G_{\kappa}}(\psi)^{U_{\kappa}},

the
U_{\kappa}
-fixed vectors in the Gelfand–Graev representation for the finite group
G_{\kappa}
.

By Lemma
3.3
, for the finite Hecke algebra one has

\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}\simeq C^{\infty}_{c}(I_{1}\backslash K/I_{1})\simeq\mathbbm{C}[U_{\kappa}\backslash G_{\kappa}/U_{\kappa}],

where the measure used to define convolution in
\mathbbm{C}[U_{\kappa}\backslash G_{\kappa}/U_{\kappa}]
is
q^{-1}
times the counting measure. Thus
\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}
acts on
\mathcal{V}_{\kappa}
by convolution and so does the subalgebra of functions supported on
U_{\kappa}T_{\kappa}U_{\kappa}=U_{\kappa}T_{\kappa}=T_{\kappa}U_{\kappa}
, which is isomorphic to
\mathbbm{C}[T_{\kappa}]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

The map
\mathcal{V}_{\kappa}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{C}[T_{\kappa}]
given by
f\mapsto f|_{T_{\kappa}}
is an isomorphism of
\mathbbm{C}[T_{\kappa}]
-modules.

Proof.

The functions in
\mathcal{V}_{\kappa}
have support contained in
U_{\kappa}^{-}T_{\kappa}U_{\kappa}
. By the Bruhat decomposition the map is an isomorphism of vector spaces. It is a
\mathbbm{C}[T_{\kappa}]
-module homomorphism because
U_{\kappa}T_{\kappa}=T_{\kappa}U_{\kappa}
.
∎

Next we describe the structure of
\mathcal{V}_{\kappa}
as an
\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}
-module. For
\chi\in{\rm Hom}(T_{\kappa},\mathbbm{C}^{\times})
, recall that
c(\chi)\in\mathbbm{C}[T_{\kappa}]
is the idempotent associated with
\chi
. We also write
c(\chi)
for the element of
\mathcal{V}_{\kappa}\simeq\mathbbm{C}[T_{\kappa}]
, under the isomorphism of Lemma
5.1
. Let

\mathcal{O}\subset{\rm Hom}(T_{\kappa},\mathbbm{C}^{\times})

be a
W
-orbit with respect to the action induced from that on
T_{\kappa}
. Let

\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,\mathcal{O}}:={\rm Span}_{\mathbbm{C}}\left\{c(\chi):\chi\in\mathcal{O}\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{\kappa}.

For
\chi\in{\rm Hom}(T_{\kappa},\mathbbm{C}^{\times})
and
\alpha\in\Delta
, we set

(5.1)

\mathbf{g}_{\alpha}(\psi,\chi):=\sum_{u\in\kappa^{\times}}\psi(e_{-\alpha}(u))\cdot\chi(h_{-\alpha}(u)).

Proposition 5.2
.

The finite Gelfand–Graev representation
\mathcal{V}_{\kappa}={\rm ind}_{U_{\kappa}^{-}}^{G_{\kappa}}(\psi)^{U_{\kappa}}
decomposes as

\mathcal{V}_{\kappa}=\bigoplus_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,\mathcal{O}},

where the direct sum is taken over the
W
-orbits in
{\rm Hom}(T_{\kappa},\mathbbm{C}^{\times})
. Moreover, each
\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,\mathcal{O}}
is an irreducible
\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}
-module.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
we know that
{\rm ind}_{U_{\kappa}^{-}}^{G_{\kappa}}(\psi)^{U_{\kappa}}
decomposes as a
\mathbbm{C}[T_{\kappa}]
-module into a direct sum over all of the characters of
T_{\kappa}
. For
g\in G_{\kappa}
we write

\mathcal{T}^{\kappa}_{g}:={\rm ch}_{U_{\kappa}gU_{\kappa}}\in\mathbbm{C}[U_{\kappa}\backslash G_{\kappa}/U_{\kappa}],

the characteristic function of
U_{\kappa}gU_{\kappa}
. For
\alpha\in\Delta
, we also set

\mathcal{T}^{\kappa}_{\alpha}:=\mathcal{T}^{\kappa}_{w_{\alpha}(1)}.

For
\chi\in{\rm Hom}(T_{\kappa},\mathbbm{C}^{\times})
and
\alpha\in\Delta
we view
c(\chi)\in\mathcal{V}_{\kappa}
.
A direct computation gives

\displaystyle c(\chi)*\mathcal{T}^{\kappa}_{\alpha}(t)=

\displaystyle\int_{Uw_{\alpha}(1)U}c(\chi)(th^{-1})\mathcal{T}^{\kappa}_{w_{\alpha}(1)}(h)dh

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in\kappa^{\times}}c(\chi)(te_{\alpha}(u)w_{\alpha}(-1))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{u\in\kappa^{\times}}c(\chi)(e_{\alpha}(u)w_{\alpha}(-1))\Big){}^{w_{\alpha}}\chi(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{u\in\kappa^{\times}}\psi^{-1}(e_{-\alpha}(-u^{-1}))c(\chi)(h_{\alpha}(u))\Big){}^{w_{\alpha}}\chi(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{u\in\kappa^{\times}}\psi(e_{-\alpha}(u))\chi(h_{-\alpha}(u))\Big){}^{w_{\alpha}}\chi(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}_{\alpha}(\psi,\chi)\cdot{}^{w_{\alpha}}\chi(t)

This gives

(5.2)

c(\chi)*\mathcal{T}^{\kappa}_{\alpha}=\mathbf{g}_{\alpha}(\psi,\chi)\cdot c({}^{w_{\alpha}}\chi).

The algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}
is generated by the elements
\mathcal{T}^{\kappa}_{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\kappa}_{t}
, where
\alpha\in\Delta
and
t\in T_{\kappa}
. Thus

\mathcal{V}_{\kappa}=\bigoplus_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,\mathcal{O}},

where the direct sum is taken over
W
-orbits in
{\rm Hom}(T_{\kappa},\mathbbm{C}^{\times})
.

In fact, each
\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,\mathcal{O}}
is irreducible. Let
v\in\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,\mathcal{O}}
be a nonzero vector. By Lemma
5.1
, there is a character
\chi\in{\rm Hom}(T_{\kappa},\mathbbm{C}^{\times})
such that

v*c(\chi)=z\cdot c(\chi)

for some
z\in\mathbbm{C}^{\times}
. Finally, the equality (
5.2
) implies
v*\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}=\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,\mathcal{O}}
. Thus
\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,\mathcal{O}}
is irreducible.
∎

Now we show that each
\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,\mathcal{O}}
is an induced representation. Let
\chi\in{\rm Hom}(T_{\kappa},\mathbbm{C}^{\times})
. Then consider the algebra

\mathcal{H}_{\kappa,\chi}=c(\chi)\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}c(\chi).

A
\mathbbm{C}
-basis for
\mathcal{H}_{\kappa,\chi}
is given by

\left\{c(\chi)\mathcal{T}^{\kappa}_{w}c(\chi):w\in W_{\chi}\right\},

where
w\in G_{\kappa}
is the natural representative of an element in
W_{\chi}:={\rm Stab}_{W}(\chi)
. The action in line (
5.2
) shows that
\mathcal{H}_{\kappa,\chi}
acts on the one-dimensional subspace
\mathbbm{C}\cdot c(\chi)\subset\mathcal{V}_{\kappa}
. Henceforth, we write

\tau_{\chi}:=\mathbbm{C}\cdot c(\chi)

for this one-dimensional representation of
\mathcal{H}_{\kappa,\chi}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\chi\in{\rm Hom}(T_{\kappa},\mathbbm{C}^{\times})
and let
\mathcal{O}_{\chi}
be the
W
-orbit of
\chi
. Then the linear map

\tau_{\chi}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa,\chi}}\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}\longrightarrow\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,\mathcal{O}_{\chi}}

defined by
c(\chi)\otimes h\mapsto c(\chi)*h
is an
\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}
-module isomorphism.

Proof.

It follows from (
5.2
) that the linear map
\tau_{\chi}\longrightarrow\mathcal{V}_{\mathcal{O}}
defined by
c(\chi)\mapsto c(\chi)
is an
\mathcal{H}_{\kappa,\chi}
-module map and induces an
\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}
-module homomorphism
\tau_{\chi}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa,\chi}}\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,\mathcal{O}_{\chi}}
defined by
c(\chi)\otimes h\mapsto c(\chi)*h
. This map is surjective by (
5.2
). The map is an isomorphism because both spaces have dimension
\left|W_{\chi}\backslash W\right|
.
∎

Lemma
5.3
can be refined to an isomorphism

\tau_{\chi}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa,\chi}}c_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}c_{\mathcal{O}}\simeq\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,\mathcal{O}},

where

c_{\mathcal{O}}:=\sum_{\chi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}}c(\chi^{\prime})

is the central idempotent corresponding to the orbit
\mathcal{O}
. In particular, for the trivial orbit we have the following.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbbm{1}}
be the orbit of the trivial character
\mathbbm{1}
of
T_{\kappa}
. Then
\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbbm{1}}}=\mathbbm{C}\cdot c(\mathbbm{1})
and

\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbbm{1}}}\simeq{\rm sign}

as
C(B_{\kappa}\backslash G_{\kappa}/B_{\kappa})
-modules.

Proof.

By definition
\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbbm{1}}}=\mathbbm{C}\cdot c(\mathbbm{1})
and by a direct computation we see that

c(\mathbbm{1})*\mathcal{T}^{\kappa}_{w_{\alpha}(1)}=c(\mathbbm{1})*\mathcal{T}^{\kappa}_{\alpha}*c(\mathbbm{1})\in C(B_{\kappa}\backslash G_{\kappa}/B_{\kappa}).

The result then follows from (
5.2
).
∎

.

5.4.
Further simplification

To obtain a unique and simple description of
\mathcal{V}^{I}_{\mathcal{O}}\simeq\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,\mathcal{O}}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}}\mathcal{H}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}

\mathcal{O}
with a special “splitting” property as follows.

For use later, we introduce a slight generalization of the
W
-orbits on
Y
and
\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
.
Let

be a
W
-stable sublattice of the same rank as

with respect to the usual reflection action of
W
on
Y
. For every element

in the coweight lattice

Y
given by

w[y]_{z}:=w(y+z)-z.

It is well-defined since
z\in P
. This gives a finite dimensional permutation representation

\sigma_{[z]}^{Y/L}:W\longrightarrow{\rm Perm}(Y/L).

An orbit
\mathcal{O}\subset Y
with respect to the action

given above is called a
(W,z)
-orbit. Clearly, the
(W,0)
-orbits are just the
W
-orbits. The
w[-]_{z}

and the quotient map

is
(W,z)
-equivariant. Specializing to the special case

, we have

Definition 5.16
.

A
(W,z)
-orbit
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}=Y/Y_{Q,n}
is called splitting if there exists a section of the quotient map

which is equivariant with respect to the

-action on both
Y
and
\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
.

The orbit of

For the rest of this section, we only consider the

For

6.4
) fails, then it is possible that certain
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
is not splitting. The cover
\overline{{\rm GL}}_{2}^{(4)}
associated with
{\bf p}=1,{\bf q}=2
is such an example.

Remark 6.6
.

If
\overline{G}
is very saturated but not oasitic, then it is possible that certain
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
is not splitting. For example, consider the double cover
\overline{G}_{2}^{(2)}
of the exceptional
G_{2}
. In the Bourbaki notations, the element
\hat{\omega}_{1}\in\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
of the coweight (also a coroot)

has stabilizer subgroup

W_{\hat{\omega}_{1}}\simeq\left\{1,w_{\alpha_{2}}\right\}\times\left\{1,w_{\omega_{1}}\right\},

where the right hand is direct product. Since

is not isomorphic to a parabolic Weyl subgroup, we see that

is not splitting.

6.3.
Covers of
{\rm SL}_{2}

In this subsection we focus on covers of
{\rm SL}(2)
and determine the
\mathcal{H}_{I}
-module structure of

for any
W
-orbit
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
.

Let
\overline{{\rm SL}}_{2}
be the
n
-fold cover with
Q(\alpha^{\vee})=-1
. In this case

Y_{Q,n}=\mathbbm{Z}[\alpha_{Q,n}^{\vee}]=\mathbbm{Z}[n^{*}\alpha^{\vee}]\text{ where }n^{*}=\frac{n}{\gcd(n,2Q(\alpha^{\vee}))}.

Moreover,

\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}=\left\{j\alpha^{\vee}:j\in[-n^{*}/2,n^{*}/2]\cap\mathbbm{Z}\right\}.

Note that if
n^{*}
is even, then
n^{*}/2=-n^{*}/2\in\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
. With these representatives we can easily describe the
W
-orbits. We consider two cases according to the parity of
n^{*}
.

If

is odd, then the
W
-orbits of
\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
consist of the trivial orbit
\left\{0\right\}
and
\left\lfloor n^{*}/2\right\rfloor
-many free orbits of the form

\left\{j\alpha^{\vee},-j\alpha^{\vee}\right\},\text{ where }j\in[1,n^{*}/2]\cap\mathbbm{Z}.

In this case, by Propositions
5.19
and
5.20
we have a complete description of the
\mathcal{H}_{I}
-module structure of
\mathcal{V}^{I}_{\mathcal{O}}\simeq\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,\mathcal{O}}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}}\mathcal{H}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}
for any
W
-orbit
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
.

Now assume
n^{*}
is even. There are two trivial
W
-orbits in
\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
:

\left\{0\right\}\text{ and }\left\{n^{*}\alpha^{\vee}/2\right\},

while

\left\{j\alpha^{\vee},-j\alpha^{\vee}\right\},j\in[1,n^{*}/2-1]\cap\mathbbm{Z}

constitute the free
W
-orbits in
\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
. Again, Propositions
5.19
and
5.20
gives a complete description of the
\mathcal{H}_{I}
-module
\mathcal{V}^{I}_{\mathcal{O}}\simeq\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,\mathcal{O}}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}}\mathcal{H}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}
except for the orbit

We will describe
\mathcal{V}^{I}_{\mathcal{O}_{n^{*}/2}}\simeq\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,\mathcal{O}_{n^{*}/2}}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}}\mathcal{H}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}
directly. The result of our analysis uncovers a new phenomenon that does not follow from our general results proved above. Note that if
n^{*}
is even, then necessarily
2n^{*}=n_{\alpha}
.

Lemma 6.7
.

The element
h:=\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}^{I}\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{-n^{*}\alpha^{\vee}}}^{I}\in\mathcal{H}_{I}
satisfies
h^{2}=q
.

Proof.

Let
y=-n^{*}\alpha^{\vee}
. By Lemma
(iv)
we have

\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{y}}^{I}\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}^{I}=\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}^{I}\Theta_{w_{\alpha}(-1)\cdot\mathbf{s}_{y}}^{I}-(q-1)\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{y}\overline{h}_{\alpha}(\varpi^{2n^{*}})}^{I}.

Thus

h^{2}=(T_{\alpha}^{I})^{2}\Theta_{(w_{\alpha}(-1)\cdot\mathbf{s}_{y})\mathbf{s}_{y}}^{I}-(q-1)\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}^{I}\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{y}\overline{h}_{\alpha}(\varpi^{2n^{*}})\mathbf{s}_{y}}^{I}.

The identity
w_{\alpha}(-1)\cdot\mathbf{s}_{y}=\mathbf{s}_{y}\overline{h}_{\alpha}(\varpi^{2n^{*}})
from (
2.6g
) implies that

\displaystyle h^{2}=

\displaystyle[(\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}^{I})^{2}-(q-1)\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}^{I}]\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{y}\overline{h}_{\alpha}(\varpi^{2n^{*}})\mathbf{s}_{y}}^{I}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{y}\overline{h}_{\alpha}(\varpi^{2n^{*}})\mathbf{s}_{y}}^{I}=q,

where the last equality holds since
\mathbf{s}_{y}\overline{h}_{\alpha}(\varpi^{2n^{*}})\mathbf{s}_{y}=1\in\overline{T}
by (
2.6b
).
∎

Let
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}:=q^{-1/2}\cdot T_{\alpha}^{I}\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{-n^{*}\alpha^{\vee}}}^{I}
and consider the
\mathbbm{C}
-subalgebra

(\mathcal{H}_{I})_{0}^{\prime}:=\langle\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\rangle\subset\mathcal{H}_{I}

generated by
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. Lemma
6.7
implies
(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})^{2}=1
and thus

(\mathcal{H}_{I})_{0}^{\prime}\simeq\mathbbm{C}[\mathbbm{Z}/2\mathbbm{Z}].

Let
\sigma:(\mathcal{H}_{I})_{0}^{\prime}\to\mathbbm{C}
be the character given by

\sigma(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})=q^{-1/2}\cdot\mathbf{g}_{\alpha}(\psi,\varphi(-n^{*}\alpha^{\vee}/2))\in\left\{\pm 1\right\}.

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
y=\frac{-n^{*}}{2}\alpha^{\vee}
. The
\mathbbm{C}
-vector space map

\sigma\longrightarrow\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,\mathcal{O}_{n^{*}/2}}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}}\mathcal{H}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}

defined by
1\mapsto c(\varphi(y))\otimes\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{y}}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}
is a map of
(\mathcal{H}_{I})_{0}^{\prime}
-modules. Moreover, this map induces an isomorphism

\sigma\otimes_{(\mathcal{H}_{I})_{0}^{\prime}}\mathcal{H}_{I}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,\mathcal{O}_{n^{*}/2}}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}}\mathcal{H}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\boldsymbol{\gamma}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{V}^{I}_{\mathcal{O}_{n^{*}/2}}

of
\mathcal{H}_{I}
-modules.

Proof.

Using the Bernstein relation in (
4.6
), we have

c(\varphi(y))\otimes\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{y}}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}*\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}^{I}=\mathbf{g}_{\alpha}(\psi,\varphi(y))\cdot c(\varphi(y))\otimes\Theta_{w_{\alpha}(-1)\cdot\mathbf{s}_{y}}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}.

Thus

c(\varphi(y))\otimes\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{y}}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}*\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=\frac{\mathbf{g}_{\alpha}(\psi,\varphi(y))}{q^{1/2}}c(\varphi(y))\otimes\Theta_{(w_{\alpha}(-1)\cdot\mathbf{s}_{y})\mathbf{s}_{-n^{*}\alpha^{\vee}}}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}.

Since
w_{\alpha}(-1)\cdot\mathbf{s}_{y}=\mathbf{s}_{y}h_{\alpha}(\varpi^{n^{*}})
by (

) and
h_{\alpha}(\varpi^{n^{*}})=\mathbf{s}(\alpha^{\vee}(\varpi^{n^{*}}))
, it follows that
(w_{\alpha}(-1)\cdot\mathbf{s}_{y})\mathbf{s}(-n^{*}\alpha^{\vee})(\varpi))=\mathbf{s}_{y}
.
Hence,

c(\varphi(y))\otimes\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{y}}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}*\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=\frac{\mathbf{g}_{\alpha}(\psi,\varphi(y))}{q^{1/2}}c(\varphi(y))\otimes\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{y}}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}.

This shows that the map
\sigma\rightarrow\mathcal{V}_{\kappa,\mathcal{O}}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}}\mathcal{H}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}
defined by
1\mapsto c(\varphi(y))\otimes\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{y}}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}
is a map of
(\mathcal{H}_{I})_{0}^{\prime}
-modules. The induced map of
\mathcal{H}_{I}
-modules is an isomorphism because it takes the
\mathcal{A}
-basis
\left\{1\otimes\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{kn^{*}\alpha^{\vee}}}^{I}:k\in\mathbbm{Z}\right\}
to the
\mathcal{A}
-basis
\left\{c(\varphi(y))\otimes\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{y}}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}*\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{kn^{*}\alpha^{\vee}}}^{I}\right\}
.
∎

The orbit
\mathcal{O}_{n^{*}/2}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
does not possess a
W
-equivariant splitting. Thus, Proposition
6.8
does not follow from Theorem
5.18
. This suggests that splittings of other finite subgroups of the modified extended Weyl group
\tilde{W}_{{\rm ex}}=W\ltimes Y_{Q,n}
should also be considered, besides
W
. We make a few remarks in this direction and further elaborate on the example of Proposition
6.8
.

For any
z\in Y\otimes\mathbbm{Q}
, we set
\tilde{W}_{{\rm ex},z}:={\rm Stab}_{\tilde{W}_{\rm ex}}(z)
. In particular,
\tilde{W}_{{\rm ex},0}=W
. For any
y\in Y
we have

W_{y}=\tilde{W}_{{\rm ex},0}\cap\tilde{W}_{{\rm ex},y}.

Now we may consider

\tilde{W}_{{\rm ex},z,y}:=\tilde{W}_{{\rm ex},z}\cap\tilde{W}_{{\rm ex},y}.

Let
\eta:\tilde{W}_{{\rm ex}}=W\ltimes Y_{Q,n}\rightarrow W
be the projection map. The quotient map

Y\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}

is equivariant with respect to
\eta:\tilde{W}_{\rm ex}\twoheadrightarrow W
. For every
z\in Y\otimes\mathbbm{Q}
, the group
\tilde{W}_{{\rm ex},z}
is finite and
\eta:\tilde{W}_{{\rm ex},z}\to\eta(\tilde{W}_{{\rm ex},z})
is an isomorphism. Then an orbit
\mathcal{O}_{y}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
is said to be
\tilde{W}_{{\rm ex},z}
-splitting if

(6.5)

\eta(\tilde{W}_{{\rm ex},z,y})=\eta(\tilde{W}_{{\rm ex},z})_{\hat{y}}.

As we vary
z
, there are more possibilities for the above equality to hold.

Consider for instance the case of
n^{*}\in 2\mathbbm{Z}
and
\hat{y}=n^{*}\alpha^{\vee}/2
for
\overline{{\rm SL}}_{2}
as in Proposition
6.8
. If we take
z=0
, then the two sides of (
6.5
) are
\left\{1\right\}
and
W
respectively. On the other hand, if we take
z=-n^{*}\alpha^{\vee}/2
, then
\tilde{W}_{{\rm ex},z}\subset\tilde{W}_{{\rm ex}}
is of order 2, generated by the reflection fixing
-n^{*}\alpha^{\vee}/2
. In this case, the two sides of (
6.5
) are both equal to
\eta(\tilde{W}_{{\rm ex},z,y})
, and there is a
\tilde{W}_{{\rm ex},z}
-splitting for the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{n^{*}/2}
.

Such a
\tilde{W}_{{\rm ex},z}
-splitting is only implicitly used in the proof of Proposition
6.8
. For a general
\overline{G}
, it is not easy to identify explicitly (with concrete relations among the generators) the finite subalgebra of
\mathcal{H}_{I}
associated to
\tilde{W}_{{\rm ex},z}
. For
\overline{{\rm SL}}_{2}
, we essentially realized this by using Lemma
6.7
. We hope to return to this problem in a future work.

7.
Metaplectic representation of Sahi–Stokeman–Venkateswaran

In this section we show that the representation

\mathcal{V}^{I}\simeq\mathcal{V}_{\kappa}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}}\mathcal{H}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}

embeds naturally into the metaplectic representation constructed by Sahi–Stokeman–Venkateswaran
[
SSV21
]
for a particular choice of what they call representation parameters (see
[
SSV21
, Definition 3.5]
).

The proof of Lemma
5.15
gives that if
\left\{y_{j}\right\}_{j}\subset Y
is a set of representatives for
\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
, then

\left\{c(\varphi(y_{j}))\otimes\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{y_{j}}}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}\right\}_{j}

is an
\mathcal{A}
-basis for
\mathcal{V}_{\kappa}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}}\mathcal{H}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}
. Using the Bernstein relations for
\mathcal{H}
we can compute the action of
\mathcal{H}_{I}
on
\mathcal{V}_{\kappa}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{\kappa}}\mathcal{H}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}
in terms of the above basis
\left\{c(\varphi(y_{j}))\otimes\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{y_{j}}}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}\right\}_{j}
. We begin with a description of the action of
\mathcal{A}
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
y\in Y
and
z\in Y_{Q,n}
. Then

c(\varphi(y))\otimes\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{y}}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}*\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{z}}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}=\varepsilon^{D(y,z)}c(\phi(y))\cdot\otimes\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{y+z}}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}.

Now we describe the action of
\mathcal{H}_{W}\subset\mathcal{H}_{I}
. It suffices to consider the action of the elements
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{T}_{\overline{w}_{\alpha}(1)}
, where
\alpha\in\Delta
.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
y\in Y
and
\alpha\in\Delta
. We write
\theta_{\mathbf{s}_{y}}:=c(\varphi(y))\otimes\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{y}}
. Then

\displaystyle\theta_{\mathbf{s}_{y}}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}*\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathbf{g}_{\alpha}(\psi,\chi)\theta_{w_{\alpha}(-1)\cdot\mathbf{s}_{y}}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}+(q-1)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1-{\left\langle y,\alpha\right\rangle}\leqslant j\leqslant 0\\
j\equiv 0\mod n_{\alpha}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{jQ(\alpha^{\vee})}\theta_{\mathbf{s}_{y}h_{\alpha}(\varpi^{j})}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}&\text{ if }{\left\langle y,\alpha\right\rangle}>0,\\
\mathbf{g}_{\alpha}(\psi,\chi)\theta_{w_{\alpha}(-1)\cdot\mathbf{s}_{y}}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}&\text{ if }{\left\langle y,\alpha\right\rangle}=0,\\
\mathbf{g}_{\alpha}(\psi,\chi)\theta_{w_{\alpha}(-1)\cdot\mathbf{s}_{y}}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}+(q-1)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant j\leqslant-{\left\langle y,\alpha\right\rangle}\\
j\equiv 0\,\text{mod }n_{\alpha}\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{jQ(\alpha^{\vee})}\theta_{\mathbf{s}_{y}h_{\alpha}(\varpi^{j})}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}&\text{ if }{\left\langle y,\alpha\right\rangle}<0.\end{cases}

7.1.
The SSV representation

We briefly recall the construction of the metaplectic representation of
\mathcal{H}_{I}
in
[
SSV21
]
using notations in our paper.

For the rest of this section, we assume that
G
is semisimple and almost simple. Denote by
P
the lattice of coweights with

P\supset Y\supset Y^{sc}.

Fix
Q:Y\to\mathbbm{Z}
an integral-valued Weyl-invariant quadratic form, and extend it uniquely to
a rational-valued form

Q:P\to\mathbbm{Q}.

One has a sublattice
P_{Q,n}\subset P
given in
[
SSV21
, §2.2]
. The relations among these sublattices are as in

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{Q,n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{Q,n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y.}

In particular,

Y_{Q,n}\subseteq P_{Q,n}\cap Y,

where equality holds if
Y=Y^{sc}
, i.e., if
G
is simply-connected. Furthermore, if
Y=Y^{sc}
, we have an embedding

Y/Y_{Q,n}\hookrightarrow P/P_{Q,n}.

One has the extended affine Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(P_{Q,n})
associated with
P_{Q,n}
, and as a
\mathbbm{C}
-isomorphism we get

\mathcal{H}(P_{Q,n})\simeq\mathcal{H}_{W}\otimes_{\mathbbm{C}}\mathbbm{C}[P_{Q,n}],

where
\mathcal{H}_{W}
is the finite Hecke algebra. One has an inclusion of algebras

\mathcal{H}_{I}\subset\mathcal{H}(P_{Q,n}).

There is a natural action of
\mathcal{H}(P_{Q,n})
on
\mathbbm{C}[P_{Q,n}]
via transporting the
\mathcal{H}(P_{Q,n})
-action from the left hand side of the
\mathbbm{C}[P_{Q,n}]
-module isomorphism

\mathcal{H}(P_{Q,n})\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{W}}\mathbbm{C}\simeq\mathbbm{C}[P_{Q,n}].

It is shown in
[
SSV21
, Theorem 3.7]
that this action of
\mathcal{H}(P_{Q,n})
can be extended to the bigger space
\mathbbm{C}[P]\supset\mathbbm{C}[P_{Q,n}]
. We let
(\pi,\mathbbm{C}[P])
denote this
\mathcal{H}(P_{Q,n})
-module. In brief, it is constructed as follows:

(i)

Choose a
W
-stable subset
C\subset P
such that
0\in C
and that the quotient

P\twoheadrightarrow P/P_{Q,n}

is surjective when restricted to
C
. In
[
SSV21
]
, an explicit
C
is given.

(ii)

Consider the
\mathcal{H}(P_{Q,n})
-module

N_{C}:=\mathcal{H}(P_{Q,n})\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{W}}V_{C},

where
V_{C}
is the
\left|C\right|
-dimensional space affording the
\mathcal{H}_{W}
-action “deformed” from the Weyl action, see
[
SSV21
, §3.1]
.
Depending on certain parameters
\mathbf{c}
, there is a natural
\mathbbm{C}[P]
-module surjection

\Psi^{\mathbf{c}}_{C}:N_{C}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbbm{C}[P].

(iii)

By choosing
\mathbf{c}
properly, it is shown that
{\rm Ker}(\Psi^{\mathbf{c}}_{C})
is actually
\mathcal{H}(P_{Q,n})
-stable, and thus one has a
\mathcal{H}(P_{Q,n})
-module structure on
\mathbbm{C}[P]
by transporting that from
N_{C}
: this is the representation
\pi
.

In the notation of
[
SSV21
]
, the action of the generators of
\mathcal{H}(P_{Q,n})
on
\mathbbm{C}[P]
are given as follows:

(7.1)

\displaystyle\pi(T_{\alpha})x^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=({\bf k}_{\alpha}-{\bf k}_{\alpha}^{-1})\overline{\nabla}_{\alpha}(x^{\lambda})+{\bf p}_{\alpha}(\overline{\lambda})x^{s_{\alpha}(\lambda)},

\displaystyle\pi(x^{\nu})x^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=x^{\lambda+\nu}

for
\alpha\in\Delta,\lambda\in P
and
\nu\in P_{Q,n}
.

By restricting the action of
\mathcal{H}(P_{Q,n})
to the subalgebra
\mathcal{H}_{I}
, we get an
\mathcal{H}_{I}
-module still denoted by
(\pi,\mathbbm{C}[P])
. For every
z\in P
, it follows from (
7.1
) that the subspace

\mathbbm{C}[Y]\cdot x^{z}\subset\mathbbm{C}[P]

is stable under the
\mathcal{H}_{I}
-action and thus gives a
\mathcal{H}_{I}
-submodule
(\pi^{z},\mathbbm{C}[Y]\cdot x^{z})
. Moreover, one has a decomposition

\pi=\bigoplus_{z\in P/Y}\pi^{z}

as
\mathcal{H}_{I}
-modules.

The representation
\pi^{0}
has a further decomposition as follows. For each
W
-orbit
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
, consider the vector space

\mathbbm{C}[Y]_{\mathcal{O}}:=\bigoplus_{y\in\mathcal{O}}\mathbbm{C}[Y_{Q,n}]\cdot x^{y},

which is
\mathcal{H}_{I}
-stable by (
7.1
). We let
(\pi^{0}_{\mathcal{O}},\mathbbm{C}[Y]_{\mathcal{O}})
denote this, which gives a decomposition

\pi^{0}=\bigoplus_{\mathcal{O}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}}\pi^{0}_{\mathcal{O}}.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
\overline{G}
be an
n
-fold cover of a semisimple group
G
. Assume
\varepsilon=(-1,\varpi)_{n}=1
. Then we have an isomorphism

\mathcal{V}^{I}_{\mathcal{O}}\simeq\pi^{0}_{\mathcal{O}}

of
\mathcal{H}_{I}
-modules for every orbit
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
7.1
and Lemma
7.2
, checking the result essentially amounts to matching the notations used in our paper and those in
[
SSV21
, Theorem 3.7]
, i.e., (
7.1
) above. More precisely, the correspondence is given as follows:

•

\mathbbm{1}_{I}*\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}\longleftrightarrow\mathbf{k_{i}}T_{\alpha}
for simple root
\alpha
,

•

\mathbbm{1}_{I}*\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{y^{\prime}}}*\mathbbm{1}_{I}\longleftrightarrow x^{\nu}
where
y^{\prime}\leftrightarrow\nu\in Y_{Q,n}
,

•

c(\varphi(y))\otimes\Theta_{\mathbf{s}_{y}}\longleftrightarrow x^{\lambda}
where
y\leftrightarrow\lambda\in Y
.

Note that the assumption
\varepsilon=1
is a technical assumption, enforced by the relation that the parameters
{\bf k}_{\alpha}
(essentially the Gauss sums) need to satisfy.
∎

One of the applications of the SSV representation
\pi
is to provide a more natural proof of the Chinta–Gunnells action
[
CG10
]
. This latter work assumes that
\mu_{2n}\subset F^{\times}
, which in particular implies that
\varepsilon=1
. Using our formulas in Lemma

and

, the method of Sahi–Stokman–Venkateswaran can be easily adjusted to incorporate the slightly more general case without the assumption
\mu_{2n}\subset F^{\times}
.

Remark 7.4
.

It is possible to prove Theorem
5.18
by using Theorem

and refining several steps in the construction of
\pi
in
[

]
, as outlined in (i)–(iii) above. Indeed, if

. Examining the argument in

]
will give that

, where
\mathcal{H}_{I,y}\subset\mathcal{H}_{W}
is the subalgebra associated with the parabolic Weyl subgroup

.

.

For every

8.
Explicit Whittaker dimensions

The goal of this section and the next one is to apply Theorem
5.18
to compute explicit Whittaker dimensions of some Iwahori-spherical representations. We will eventually focus on irreducible constituents of regular unramified principal series in this section and those of a unitary unramified principal series in the next.

For every lattice
L\subset\mathscr{A}
closed under the action of
W
, one has the canonical surjection

(8.1)

\eta:L\rtimes W\twoheadrightarrow W.

Though we might use the same
\eta
for different
L
, there is no risk of confusion from the context. If
L
is a root lattice and
W
the associated Weyl group, then we write

W_{{\rm a}}=L\rtimes W

for the affine Weyl group. We also denote

\tilde{W}_{{\rm a}}=L^{\prime}\rtimes W,

where
L^{\prime}\subset L
is a modified root lattice (i.e., generated by
\left\{k_{\alpha}\alpha:\alpha\text{ simple root of }L\right\}
for some
k_{\alpha}\in\mathbbm{N}
) giving rise to the same Weyl group as
L
does. Again, the context makes it clear what
L^{\prime}
refers to.

8.1.
Some permutation representations

Recall from §
5.4
that for
z\in P
and any Weyl-invariant sublattice
L\subset Y
of the same rank as
Y
, we have the well-defined action
w[y]_{z}:=w(y+z)-z
for
y\in Y/L
. Recall that this gives a finite dimensional permutation representation

\sigma_{[z]}^{Y/L}:W\longrightarrow{\rm Perm}(Y/L).

It is easy to see that if
z-z^{\prime}\in P\cap Y
, then

\sigma_{[z]}^{Y/L}\simeq\sigma_{[z^{\prime}]}^{Y/L}.

Thus, there is a well-defined action of
P/(P\cap Y)
on
\sigma_{[0]}^{Y/L}
. For later purpose, we are interested in the orbit of
\sigma_{[0]}^{Y/L}
under this action of
P/(P\cap Y)
and also the associated stabilizer subgroup. A special case is given as follows:

Lemma 8.1
.

Assume
Y=Y^{sc}
is the root lattice of a root system
R
with simple roots
\Delta=\left\{\alpha_{i}:\ 1\leqslant i\leqslant r\right\}
. Let
S\subset Y
be a sublattice associated to a root system
R^{\prime}
with simple roots
\Delta^{\prime}=\left\{k_{i}\alpha_{i}:\alpha_{i}\in\Delta^{\prime}\right\}
for some
k_{i}\in\mathbbm{N}
. Then for the above action of
P/Y
on
\sigma_{[0]}^{Y/S}
, one has

{\rm Stab}_{P/Y}(\sigma_{[0]}^{Y/S})=\frac{P\cap(P(S)+Y)}{Y}\subseteq P/Y,

where
P(S)\subset Y\otimes\mathbbm{Q}
denotes the weight lattice of
S
.

Proof.

Write
\sigma_{[z]}
for
\sigma_{[z]}^{Y/S}
for simplicity. Consider its character
\chi_{\sigma_{[z]}}
which is integer-valued and satisfies
\chi_{\sigma_{[z]}}(w)\geqslant 0
for every
w\in W
. We observe that if
\chi_{\sigma_{[z]}}(w)\neq 0
, then

\chi_{\sigma_{[z]}}(w)=\chi_{\sigma_{[0]}}(w).

Thus, the following are equivalent:

(i)

\chi_{\sigma_{[z]}}(w)\neq 0
for every
w\in W
;

(ii)

\chi_{\sigma_{[z]}}(w)=\chi_{\sigma_{[0]}}(w)\neq 0
for every
w\in W
;

(iii)

\sigma_{[z]}\simeq\sigma_{[0]}
.

One has
z\in{\rm Stab}_{P/Y}(\sigma_{[0]})
if and only if

W\subseteq\bigcup_{y+z\in Y+z}\eta({\rm Stab}_{S\rtimes W}(y+z)),

where
\eta:S\rtimes W\twoheadrightarrow W
is the natural surjection as in (
8.1
).
However, since the right hand is a union of reflection subgroups of
W
, and the Coxeter element of
W
does not lie in any proper reflection subgroup (see
[
Stem94
, §5]
), the above inclusion holds if and only if
Y+z
has a special point of the affine Weyl group
S\rtimes W
, i.e., if and only if

z\in P(S)+Y.

This completes the proof.
∎

Specializing to
L=Y_{Q,n}
, we have a permutation representation

\sigma_{[z]}^{\mathscr{X}}:W\longrightarrow{\rm Perm}(\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}).

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
\overline{G}^{\vee}
be a very saturated cover of an almost simple simply-connected
G
with
Q(\alpha^{\vee})=1
for any short coroot
\alpha^{\vee}
. Then

\sigma_{[z]}^{\mathscr{X}}\simeq\sigma_{[0]}^{\mathscr{X}}

for every
z\in P/Y
. In fact, for every
z\in P
, there exists
y_{z}\in Y
such that the bijective map

\mathfrak{m}_{z}:\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}\longrightarrow\mathscr{X}_{Q,n},\quad y\mapsto y+y_{z}

is equivariant with respect to the
w[-]_{0}=w(-)
and
w[-]_{z}
on the domain and codomain respectively. In particular,
\mathfrak{m}_{z}
induces a bijection between the
W
-orbits and
(W,z)
-orbits in
\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
.

Proof.

For
G=F_{4},E_{8},G_{2}
one has
P/Y=\left\{0\right\}
and thus it suffices to consider
G
of other types. Since
\overline{G}
is very saturated, Lemma
6.2
gives

Y_{Q,n}=Y_{Q,n}=n\cdot Y

and also
\gcd(n,\mathscr{I}_{\Delta})=1
. Note
\mathscr{I}_{\Delta}=\left|P/Y\right|
. Now
P(Y_{Q,n}^{sc})=nP

and it follows from Lemma
8.1
that

(8.2)

{\rm Stab}_{P/Y}(\sigma_{[0]})=(P\cap(nP+Y))/Y=P/Y,

where the last equality follows from the fact that
\gcd(n,\mathscr{I}_{\Delta})=1
. This shows that

\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}_{[z]}\simeq\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}_{[0]}.

Now for the second assertion,
\mathfrak{m}_{z}
(depending on
y_{z}
) is equivariant if and only if

w[y+y_{z}]_{z}=y_{z}+w(y)\in\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}

for all
w\in W
and
y\in\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
. This equality is equivalent to

w(y_{z}+z)\equiv y_{z}+z\mod Y_{Q,n}^{sc}=nY,

i.e.,
y_{z}+z\in P(Y_{Q,n}^{sc})=nP
.
However, it follows from (
8.2
) that for every
z\in P
there always exists
y_{z}\in Y
such that
y_{z}+z\in P(Y_{Q,n}^{sc})
. This proves the equivariance of
\mathfrak{m}_{z}
, from which the last assertion is clear.
∎

8.2.
z
-persistent orbits and covers

We also recall the notion of a persistent cover as follows (see
[
Ga6
, Definition 2.3]
). In fact, we introduce a slightly more general version of
z
-persistency for any element
z\in P
in the coweight lattice. These
z
-persistent covers will give a refinement of the chain in (
6.2
) since they always contain the saturated covers, see (
8.3
) below. It is expected that
z
-persistent covers exhibit better behavour if we consider
{}^{z}\psi
-Whittaker space of Iwahori-spherical representations.

Consider the sublattices
Y_{Q,n},Y_{Q,n}^{sc}\subset Y
and

\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}^{sc}:=Y/Y_{Q,n}^{sc},\ \mathscr{X}_{Q,n}=Y/Y_{Q,n},

on which the action
w[-]_{z},z\in P/(P\cap Y)
is well-defined.
For any
y\in Y
, let
y^{\dagger}
and
\hat{y}
denote its image in
\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}^{sc}
and
\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
respectively. With respect to the action
w[-]_{z}
, we have the
(W,z)
-orbits
\mathcal{O}_{y^{\dagger}}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}^{sc}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\hat{y}}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
, which are the images of
\mathcal{O}_{y}
in the respective quotient spaces.

Definition 8.3
.

A
(W,z)
-orbit
\mathcal{O}_{y}\subset Y
is called
z
-persistent if

{\rm Stab}_{W}(y^{\dagger};\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}^{sc})={\rm Stab}_{W}(\hat{y};\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}).

A covering group
\overline{G}
is called
z
-persistent if every
(W,z)
-orbit
\mathcal{O}_{y}
is
z
-persistent.

If
\mathcal{O}_{\hat{y}}=\mathcal{O}_{\hat{x}}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
, then
\mathcal{O}_{y}\subset Y
is persistent if and only if
\mathcal{O}_{x}\subset Y
is. The proof of this is identical to
[
Ga6
, Corollary 2.5]
. Thus, checking if a covering group
\overline{G}
is
z
-persistent amounts to checking for a finite set of representative orbits of those in
\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
.

While persistency is a slightly technical condition, we note that a saturated cover is always
z
-persistent for any
z\in P
. Indeed, if
w[\hat{y}]_{z}=\hat{y}
, then

w[y]_{z}-y\in Y_{Q,n}\cap Y^{sc};

but if
G
is saturated, then
w[y]_{z}-y\in Y_{Q,n}^{sc}
, i.e.,
w
fixes
y^{\dagger}\in\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}^{sc}
. This shows
\mathcal{O}_{y}
is
z
-persistent. Thus, if
G
is almost simple and simply-connected, then the chain (
6.2
) is refined to be:

(8.3)

\left\{\text{oasitic covers}\right\}\subseteq\left\{\text{very saturated covers}\right\}\subseteq\left\{\text{saturated covers}\right\}\subseteq\left\{\text{$z$-persistent covers}\right\}.

We note that the last three inclusions actually hold for arbitrary
G
.

Example 8.4
.

Every Brylinski–Deligne cover of
{\rm GL}_{r}
is saturated and thus
z
-persistent. On the other hand, the cover
\overline{{\rm SL}}_{2}^{(n)}
associated with

Q(\alpha)=-1

is saturated if and only if
n
is odd. For
n\in 4\mathbbm{Z}+2
, the cover
\overline{{\rm SL}}_{2}^{(n)}
is
0
-persistent but not
\rho
-persistent. On the other hand, if
4|n
, then
\overline{{\rm SL}}_{2}^{(n)}
is
\rho
-persistent but not
0
-persistent.

We expect that saturated and
z
-persistent covers form subclasses of covers whose representation theory is more accessible, especially the part pertaining to Whittaker models. For instance,
the
{}^{\rho}\psi
-Whittaker dimension of a theta representation of
\overline{{\rm SL}}_{2}^{(n)}
(for
n\in 4\mathbbm{Z}+2
) depends sensitively on the choice of central characters of
Z(\overline{T})
, since it is not
(W,\rho)
-persistent. For odd-fold covers of
{\rm SL}_{2}
, such subtle dependence disappears. For simply-connected
G
, exhibiting even better properties are the very saturated covers or even the oasitic covers.

8.3.
\mathcal{O}
-Whittaker space

Recall that we have a decomposition

\mathcal{V}^{I}=\bigoplus_{\mathcal{O}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}}\mathcal{V}^{I}_{\mathcal{O}},

where for every splitting
W
-orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\hat{y}}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
one has

(8.4)

\mathcal{V}^{I}_{\mathcal{O}_{\hat{y}}}\simeq\varepsilon\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{I,\hat{y}}}\mathcal{H}_{I},

where the stabilizer subgroup
W_{\hat{y}}=W_{y}\subset W
is a parabolic subgroup for a certain representative
y
of
\hat{y}
.

For an Iwahori-spherical representation
\pi
, we define

{\rm Wh}_{\psi}(\pi):={\rm Hom}_{\overline{G}}(\mathcal{V},\check{\pi})={\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{H}_{I}}(\mathcal{V}^{I},\check{\pi}^{I})

and

{\rm Wh}_{\psi}(\pi)^{\sharp}:={\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{H}_{I}}(\pi^{I},({\rm Ind}_{U^{-}}^{\overline{G}}\psi^{-1})^{I}),

where
\check{\pi}
denotes the contragredient representation of
\pi
.
By the perfect
\overline{G}
-pairing between
{\rm ind}_{U^{-}}^{\overline{G}}\psi
and
{\rm Ind}_{U^{-}}^{\overline{G}}\psi^{-1}
, we get a canonical isomorphism

\iota_{\psi}:{\rm Wh}_{\psi}(\pi)\longrightarrow{\rm Wh}_{\psi}(\pi)^{\sharp}.

Definition 8.5
.

For every Iwahori-spherical representation
\pi
and every orbit
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
, the subspace

{{\rm Wh}_{\psi}}(\pi)_{\mathcal{O}}:={\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{H}_{I}}(\mathcal{V}^{I}_{\mathcal{O}},\check{\pi}^{I})\subset{\rm Wh}_{\psi}(\pi),

is called the
\mathcal{O}
-Whittaker subspace of
\pi
.

One has a decomposition as

{\rm Wh}_{\psi}(\pi)=\bigoplus_{\mathcal{O}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}}{{\rm Wh}_{\psi}}(\pi)_{\mathcal{O}}.

If
T\in{\rm Hom}_{\overline{G}}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})
is an intertwining operator, then it induces a well-defined
\mathbbm{C}
-homomorphism

T_{\psi,\mathcal{O}}:{\rm Wh}_{\psi}(\pi_{2})_{\mathcal{O}}\longrightarrow{\rm Wh}_{\psi}(\pi_{1})_{\mathcal{O}}.

Lemma 8.6
.

For every orbit
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
, the
\mathcal{H}_{I}
-module
\mathcal{V}^{I}_{\mathcal{O}}
is projective. Hence
{{\rm Wh}_{\psi}}(-)_{\mathcal{O}}
is an exact functor.

Proof.

Since the functor
{\rm Hom}(-,{\rm Ind}_{U^{-}}^{\overline{G}}\psi)
of taking Whittaker model is exact, we get that
\mathcal{V}^{I}
is a projective
\mathcal{H}_{I}
-module. Any of its direct summand
\mathcal{V}^{I}_{\mathcal{O}}
is thus also projective.
∎

8.4.
A concrete realization

If
\pi=I(\chi)
is an unramified genuine principal series, then we expect to have a more concrete description of
{{\rm Wh}_{\psi}}(I(\chi))_{\mathcal{O}}
which arises essentially from composing the Jacquet integral with functionals of
i(\chi)
, see
[
KP
,
GSS2
]
.

Let
{\sf Ftn}(i(\chi))
be the vector space of functions
\mathbf{c}
on
\overline{T}
satisfying

\mathbf{c}(\overline{t}\cdot\overline{z})=\mathbf{c}(\overline{t})\cdot\chi(\overline{z}),\quad\overline{t}\in\overline{T}\text{ and }\overline{z}\in\overline{A}=Z(\overline{T})\mathbf{T}(O_{F}).

The support of
\mathbf{c}\in{\sf Ftn}(i(\chi))
is a disjoint union of cosets in
\overline{T}/\overline{A}
.
For every
\gamma\in\overline{T}
, let
\mathbf{c}_{\gamma}\in{\sf Ftn}(i(\chi))
be the unique element satisfying

\text{supp}(\mathbf{c}_{\gamma})=\gamma\cdot\overline{A}\text{ and }\mathbf{c}_{\gamma}(\gamma)=1.

Clearly,
\mathbf{c}_{\gamma\cdot a}=\chi(a)^{-1}\cdot\mathbf{c}_{\gamma}
for every
a\in\overline{A}
. If
\left\{\gamma_{i}\right\}\subset\overline{T}
is a set of representatives of
\overline{T}/\overline{A}
, then
\left\{\mathbf{c}_{\gamma_{i}}\right\}
forms a basis for
{\sf Ftn}(i(\chi))
. Let
i(\chi)^{\vee}
be the vector space of functionals of
i(\chi)
, which affords the contragredient representation of
i(\chi)
.
The set
\left\{\gamma_{i}\right\}
gives rise to linear functionals
l_{\gamma_{i}}\in i(\chi)^{\vee}
such that
l_{\gamma_{i}}(\phi_{\gamma_{j}})=\delta_{ij}
, where
\phi_{\gamma_{j}}\in i(\chi)
is the unique element such that

\text{supp}(\phi_{\gamma_{j}})=\overline{A}\cdot\gamma_{j}^{-1}\text{ and }\phi_{\gamma_{j}}(\gamma_{j}^{-1})=1.

It is easy to see that for every
\gamma\in\overline{T}
and
a\in\overline{A}
, one has

\phi_{\gamma a}=\chi(a)\cdot\phi_{\gamma},\quad l_{\gamma a}=\chi(a)^{-1}\cdot l_{\gamma}.

Moreover, there is a natural isomorphism of vector spaces

{\sf Ftn}(i(\chi))\simeq i(\chi)^{\vee}

given by

\mathbf{c}\mapsto l_{\mathbf{c}}:=\sum_{\gamma_{i}\in\overline{T}/\overline{A}}\mathbf{c}(\gamma_{i})\cdot l_{\gamma_{i}}.

It can be checked easily that this isomorphism does not depend on the choice of representatives for
\overline{T}/\overline{A}
.

For any
z\in Y_{\rm ad}=P
, consider again

{}^{z}\psi(x)=\psi(z^{-1}xz).

There is an isomorphism between
i(\chi)^{\vee}
and the space
{\rm Wh}_{{}^{z}\psi}(I(\chi))^{\sharp}
of
(U^{-},(^{z}\psi)^{-1})
-Whittaker functionals on
I(\chi)
given by

l\mapsto\lambda_{l}^{\sharp}

with

\lambda_{l}^{\sharp}:I(\chi)\to\mathbbm{C},\quad f\mapsto l\circ J^{\sharp}_{\psi}(f)\text{ where }J^{\sharp}_{\psi}(f)=\int_{U^{-}}f(u)\psi(u)du\in i(\chi).

Here
f\in I(\chi)
is an
i(\chi)
-valued function on
\overline{G}
. For any
\mathbf{c}\in{\sf Ftn}(i(\chi))
, write
\lambda_{\mathbf{c}}^{\sharp}\in{\rm Wh}_{{}^{z}\psi}(I(\chi))^{\sharp}
for the
(U^{-},(^{z}\psi)^{-1})
-Whittaker functional of
I(\chi)
associated to
l_{\mathbf{c}}
. Therefore,
\mathbf{c}\mapsto\lambda_{\mathbf{c}}^{\sharp}
gives an isomorphism between
{\sf Ftn}(i(\chi))
and
\text{Wh}_{{}^{z}\psi}(I(\chi))^{\sharp}
. For
\gamma\in\overline{T}
, we will write

\lambda_{\gamma}^{\sharp}:=\lambda_{\mathbf{c}_{\gamma}}^{\sharp}.

Let
w_{G}
be the long element in the Weyl group
W
. To describe the above
{\rm Wh}_{{}^{z}\psi}(I(\chi))^{\sharp}
further, we relate it to the
(U,({}^{w_{G}z}\psi)^{-1})
-Whittaker model of
I(\chi)
which we denote by
{\rm Wh}_{{}^{w_{G}z}\psi}(I(\chi))^{\rm std}
and appeared more frequently in the literature.
More precisely, for

{}^{w_{G}}({}^{z}\psi)={}^{w_{G}zw_{G}^{-1}}({}^{w_{G}}\psi):U\longrightarrow\mathbbm{C}^{\times}

we consider the
(U,({}^{w_{G}z}\psi)^{-1})
-functional

J^{\rm std}_{{}^{w_{G}z}\psi}:I(\chi)\to i(\chi)\text{ given by }J^{\rm std}_{{}^{w_{G}z}\psi}(f)=\int_{U}f(\overline{w}_{G}^{-1}u)(^{w_{G}z}\psi)(u)du\in i(\chi).

We define
\lambda_{l}^{\rm std}:=l\circ J^{\rm std}_{{}^{w_{G}z}\psi}
and also

{\rm Wh}_{{}^{w_{G}z}\psi}(I(\chi))^{\rm std}=\left\{\lambda_{l}^{\rm std}:\ l\in i(\chi)^{\vee}\right\}.

It is clear that

(8.5)

J^{\sharp}_{{}^{z}\psi}(f)=J_{{}^{w_{G}z}\psi}^{\rm std}(R_{w_{G}}(f)),

where
R_{g}
means the right translation action of
I(\chi)
by
g\in\overline{G}
. One has a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I(\chi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I({}^{w}\chi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I(\chi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I({}^{w}\chi),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R_{\overline{w}_{G}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T(w,\chi)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R_{\overline{w}_{G}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T(w,\chi)}

which induces the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm Wh}_{{}^{z}\psi}(I(\chi))^{\sharp}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm Wh}_{{}^{z}\psi}(I({}^{w}\chi))^{\sharp}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm Wh}_{{}^{w_{G}z}\psi}(I(\chi))^{\rm std}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm Wh}_{{}^{w_{G}z}\psi}(I(^{w}\chi))^{\rm std},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T(w,\chi)_{{}^{z}\psi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R_{\overline{w}_{G}}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T(w,\chi)_{{}^{w_{G}z}\psi}^{\rm std}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R_{\overline{w}_{G}}^{*}}

Now for every
(W,w_{G}(z))
-orbit
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
, consider

{\sf Ftn}(i(\chi))_{\mathcal{O}}=\left\{\mathbf{c}\in{\sf Ftn}(i(\chi)):\ {\rm supp}(\mathbf{c})\subset\bigcup_{y\in\mathcal{O}}\mathbf{s}_{y}\cdot\overline{A}\right\}.

This gives via the above natural isomorphisms of vector spaces

{\sf Ftn}(i(\chi))\longrightarrow i(\chi)^{\vee}\longrightarrow{\rm Wh}_{{}^{z}\psi}(I(\chi))^{\sharp}

a natural subspace

{\rm Wh}_{{}^{z}\psi}(I(\chi))_{\mathcal{O}}^{\sharp}\subset{\rm Wh}_{{}^{z}\psi}(I(\chi))^{\sharp}.

Similarly, one has

{\rm Wh}_{{}^{w_{G}z}\psi}(I(\chi))^{\rm std}_{\mathcal{O}}\subset{\rm Wh}_{{}^{w_{G}z}\psi}(I(\chi))^{\rm std}

with

(8.6)

\dim{\rm Wh}_{{}^{z}\psi}(I(\chi))_{\mathcal{O}}^{\sharp}=\dim{\rm Wh}_{{}^{w_{G}z}\psi}(I(\chi))^{\rm std}_{\mathcal{O}}=\left|\mathcal{O}\right|.

As a consequence of (
8.5
), the restriction

(8.7)

R_{w_{G}}^{*}:{\rm Wh}_{{}^{w_{G}z}\psi}(I(\chi))^{\rm std}_{\mathcal{O}}\longrightarrow{\rm Wh}_{{}^{z}\psi}(I(\chi))_{\mathcal{O}}^{\sharp}

is a well-defined vector space isomorphism.

We want to show that

(8.8)

T(w,\chi)_{{}^{z}\psi,\mathcal{O}}^{\sharp}:{\rm Wh}_{{}^{z}\psi}(I({}^{w}\chi))_{\mathcal{O}}^{\sharp}\longrightarrow{\rm Wh}_{{}^{z}\psi}(I(\chi))_{\mathcal{O}}^{\sharp}

is well-defined for every
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
. In view of (
8.7
), it suffices to prove that by restricting
T(w,\chi)_{{}^{w_{G}z}\psi}^{\rm std}
the map

(8.9)

T(w,\chi)_{{}^{w_{G}z}\psi,\mathcal{O}}^{\rm std}:{\rm Wh}_{{}^{w_{G}z}\psi}(I(^{w}\chi))^{\rm std}\longrightarrow{\rm Wh}_{{}^{w_{G}z}\psi}(I(\chi))^{\rm std}

is well-defined.

For notational convenience, we write

z^{*}:=w_{G}(z)\text{ and }\quad\psi^{*}={}^{w_{G}z}\psi={}^{z^{*}}({}^{w_{G}}\psi):U\to\mathbbm{C}^{\times}.

If we choose bases
\left\{\lambda_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{{}^{w}\chi}\right\}_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\overline{T}/\overline{A}}
and
\left\{\lambda_{\gamma}^{\chi}\right\}_{\gamma\in\overline{T}/\overline{A}}
for
{\rm Wh}_{\psi^{*}}(I({}^{w}\chi))_{\mathcal{O}}^{\rm std}
and
{\rm Wh}_{\psi^{*}}(I(\chi))_{\mathcal{O}}^{\rm std}
, then
T(w,\chi)_{\psi^{*}}^{\rm std}
is naturally represented by the so-called scattering matrix

[\tau_{\psi^{*}}(w,\chi,\gamma^{\prime},\gamma)]_{\gamma^{\prime},\gamma\in\overline{T}/\overline{A}}

satisfying

(8.10)

T(w,\chi)_{\psi^{*}}^{\rm std}(\lambda_{\gamma^{\prime}}^{{}^{w}\chi})=\sum_{\gamma\in\overline{T}/\overline{A}}\tau_{\psi^{*}}(w,\chi,\gamma^{\prime},\gamma)\cdot\lambda_{\gamma}^{\chi}.

Furthermore, if one chooses a set
\mathfrak{R}\subset Y
of representatives of the set
\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
, it gives a natural basis for the domain and codomain of
T(w,\chi)_{\psi^{*}}^{\rm std}
, which is then represented by
[\tau_{\psi^{*}}(w,\chi,\mathbf{s}_{y^{\prime}},\mathbf{s}_{y})]_{y^{\prime},y\in\mathfrak{R}}
.
In any case,
\tau_{\psi^{*}}(w,\chi,\gamma^{\prime},\gamma)
satisfies the cocycle relation reflecting the decomposition of the intertwining operator
T(w,\chi)
into rank-one intertwining operators. Thus, it suffices to determine
\tau_{\psi^{*}}(w_{\alpha},\chi,\gamma^{\prime},\gamma)
for a simple reflection
w_{\alpha}
, which is given as follows:

–

\tau_{\psi^{*}}(w_{\alpha},\chi,\gamma^{\prime},\gamma)=\tau_{\psi^{*}}^{1}(w_{\alpha},\chi,\gamma^{\prime},\gamma)+\tau_{\psi^{*}}^{2}(w_{\alpha},\chi,\gamma^{\prime},\gamma)
with

\tau_{\psi^{*}}^{i}(w_{\alpha},\chi,\gamma^{\prime}\cdot a^{\prime},\gamma\cdot a)=({}^{w_{\alpha}}\chi)^{-1}(a^{\prime})\cdot\tau_{\psi^{*}}^{i}(w_{\alpha},\chi,\gamma^{\prime},\gamma)\cdot\chi(a)

for every
a,a^{\prime}\in\overline{A}
and
1\leqslant i\leqslant 2
;

–

\tau_{\psi^{*}}^{1}(w_{\alpha},\chi,\mathbf{s}_{y^{\prime}},\mathbf{s}_{y})=0
unless
y^{\prime}\equiv y\mod Y_{Q,n}
, and

\tau_{\psi^{*}}^{1}(w_{\alpha},\chi,\mathbf{s}_{y},\mathbf{s}_{y})=(1-q^{-1})\frac{\chi(\overline{h}_{\alpha}(\varpi^{n_{\alpha}}))^{k_{z,y,\alpha}}}{1-\chi(\overline{h}_{\alpha}(\varpi^{n_{\alpha}}))},

where
k_{z,y,\alpha}=\left\lceil\frac{1+{\left\langle y+z^{*},\alpha\right\rangle}}{n_{\alpha}}\right\rceil
;

–

\tau_{\psi^{*}}^{2}(w_{\alpha},\chi,\mathbf{s}_{y^{\prime}},\mathbf{s}_{y})=0
unless
y^{\prime}\equiv w_{\alpha}[y]_{z^{*}}\mod Y_{Q,n}
and

\tau_{\psi^{*}}^{2}(w_{\alpha},\chi,\mathbf{s}_{w_{\alpha}[y]_{z^{*}}},\mathbf{s}_{y})=(-1,\varpi)_{n}^{{\left\langle y+z^{*},\alpha\right\rangle}\cdot D(y,\alpha^{\vee})}\cdot\mathbf{g}_{{}^{w_{G}}\psi}({\left\langle y+z^{*},\alpha\right\rangle}Q(\alpha^{\vee})).

8.5.
Regular unramified
I(\chi)

Consider an unramified
\chi
satisfying the following:

–

\chi
is regular, that is, its stabilizer subgroup of
W
is trivial,

–

the set
\Phi(\chi):=\left\{\alpha\in\Phi:\chi(\overline{h}_{\alpha}(\cdot)^{n_{\alpha}})=\left|\cdot\right|_{F}\right\}
is a subset of
\Delta
.

Such an exceptional
\chi
(following
[
KP
]
) gives a regular unramified genuine principal series
I(\chi)
that decomposes

(8.11)

I(\chi)^{\rm ss}=\bigoplus_{S\subset\Phi(\chi)}\pi_{S},

where the left hand side denotes the semisimplification of
I(\chi)
and
\pi_{S}\in{\rm Irr}_{\rm gen}(\overline{G})
. The decomposition is multiplicity-free and the irreducible constituent
\pi_{S}
is characterized by its Jacquet module, see
[
Rod4
]
and
[
Ga6
, §3]
. For example, if
\Phi(\chi)=\Delta
, then
\pi_{\Delta}=\Theta(\chi)
is a theta representation and
\pi_{\emptyset}
is a covering analogue of the Steinberg representation.

Recall that for each
S\subset\Phi(\chi)\subset\Delta
, there is a representation
\sigma_{S}
(possibly reducible) of the Weyl group.
More precisely, one has for the Jacquet module

(\pi_{S})_{U}=\bigoplus_{w\in W_{S}}\delta_{B}^{1/2}\cdot i({}^{w^{-1}}\chi),

where
W_{S}\subset W
is a union of Kazhdan–Lusztig right cells of
W
. The representation
\sigma_{S}
is then the direct sum of the cell representations associated to
W_{S}
. In a more concrete form (see
[
Ga6
, Corollary 6.5]
), one has

(8.12)

\sigma_{S}=\sum_{S^{\prime}:\ S\subseteq S^{\prime}\subseteq\Phi(\chi)}(-1)^{\left|S^{\prime}-S\right|}\cdot{\rm Ind}_{W(S^{\prime})}^{W}\varepsilon_{W(S^{\prime})},

where
W(S^{\prime})\subset W
is the Weyl subgroup generated by
S^{\prime}
.

Also recall the permutation representation

\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}_{\mathcal{O}}:W\longrightarrow{\rm Perm}(\mathcal{O})

for each
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
, which is given by
\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}_{\mathcal{O}}(w)(y)=w(y)
.

Theorem 8.8
.

Let
I(\chi)
be a regular unramified genuine principal series such that
\Phi(\chi)\subset\Delta
. Let
S\subseteq\Phi(\chi)
. Then for every splitting orbit
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
one has

\dim{{\rm Wh}_{\psi}}(\pi_{S})_{\mathcal{O}}={\left\langle\sigma_{S},\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}_{\mathcal{O}}\right\rangle}_{W}.

Hence, for
\overline{G}
such that every orbit
\mathcal{O}
is splitting (for example, those as in Corollary
6.4
and Example
6.5
) one has

\dim{{\rm Wh}_{\psi}}(\pi_{S})={\left\langle\sigma_{S},\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}\right\rangle}_{W}.

Proof.

The idea is the same as in
[
Ga6
]
where
\mathfrak{f}(\psi)=O_{F}
. Let
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{\hat{y}}
be a splitting orbit with
W_{\hat{y}}\subset W
a parabolic subgroup.

First, we consider the case when
S=\Phi(\chi)=\Delta
, and thus
\pi_{\Delta}=\Theta(\chi)
is a theta representation of
\overline{G}
. Here
\chi
is an exceptional character and thus
\Theta(\chi)
is the Langlands quotient of
I(\chi)
, and is also the image of the intertwining operator
T(w_{G},\chi)
, where
w_{G}\in W
is the longest Weyl element. Its contragredient
\check{\pi}_{\Delta}
is the irreducible subrepresentation of
I(\chi^{-1})
, and is also the theta representation associated to the exceptional genuine character
{}^{w_{G}}(\chi^{-1})
of
Z(\overline{T})
. In any case,
\check{\pi}_{\Delta}
is the unique unramified representation of
I({}^{w_{G}}(\chi^{-1}))
, and we have that

(\check{\pi}_{\Delta})^{I}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{W}_{{\rm a}}}}

is the trivial representation. That is, assuming
\tilde{W}_{{\rm a}}
is generated by
\left\{\tilde{\alpha}_{0}^{\vee}\right\}\cup\left\{\tilde{\alpha}_{i}^{\vee}:1\leqslant i\leqslant r\right\}
, then each
T_{\tilde{\alpha}_{i}^{\vee}}\in\mathcal{H}_{I},0\leqslant i\leqslant r
acts on
(\check{\pi}_{\Delta})^{I}
by
q
. This gives

\displaystyle\dim{\rm Wh}_{\psi}(\pi_{\Delta})_{\mathcal{O}}

\displaystyle=\dim{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{H}_{I}}(\mathcal{V}^{I}_{\mathcal{O}},(\check{\pi}_{\Delta})^{I})

\displaystyle=\dim{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{H}_{I}}(\varepsilon\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{W_{\hat{y}}}}\mathcal{H}_{I},(\check{\pi}_{\Delta})^{I})\text{ by \eqref{E:gGG}}

\displaystyle=\dim{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{H}_{W_{\hat{y}}}}(\varepsilon,(\check{\pi}_{\Delta})^{I}|_{\mathcal{H}_{W_{\hat{y}}}})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }W_{\hat{y}}=\left\{1\right\},\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

This shows that

(8.13)

\dim{{\rm Wh}_{\psi}}(\pi_{\Delta})_{\mathcal{O}}={\left\langle\varepsilon_{W},\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}_{\mathcal{O}}\right\rangle}_{W}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if $\mathcal{O}$ is a free $W$-orbit},\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Second, assume in general
S\subseteq\Phi(\chi)\subseteq\Delta
. Denote by

\pi(w,\chi)\subset I(w,\chi)

the image of intertwining operator
T(w,\chi)
. We have

\pi_{S}=\sum_{S^{\prime}:\ S\subset S^{\prime}\subset\Phi(\chi)}(-1)^{\left|S^{\prime}-S\right|}\cdot\pi(w_{S^{\prime}},\chi)\in\mathscr{R}({\rm Irr}_{\rm gen}(\overline{G})),

where
w_{S^{\prime}}
means the longest element in the Weyl subgroup
W(S^{\prime})
generated by
S^{\prime}
. Since
{{\rm Wh}_{\psi}}(-)_{\mathcal{O}}
is an exact functor, we have

\dim{{\rm Wh}_{\psi}}(\pi_{S})_{\mathcal{O}}=\sum_{S^{\prime}:\ S\subset S^{\prime}\subset\Phi(\chi)}(-1)^{\left|S^{\prime}-S\right|}\cdot\dim{{\rm Wh}_{\psi}}(\pi(w_{S^{\prime}},\chi))_{\mathcal{O}}.

However, since
\pi(w_{S^{\prime}},\chi)
is equal to the full representation parabolically induced from the theta representation
\pi_{S^{\prime}}^{M_{S^{\prime}}}
of the Levi subgroup
M_{S^{\prime}}
, we have

{{\rm Wh}_{\psi}}(\pi(w_{S^{\prime}},\chi))_{\mathcal{O}}={\left\langle\varepsilon_{W(S^{\prime})},\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}_{\mathcal{O}}\right\rangle}_{W(S^{\prime})}={\left\langle{\rm Ind}_{W(S^{\prime})}^{W}\varepsilon_{W(S^{\prime})},\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}_{\mathcal{O}}\right\rangle}_{W},

where the first equality follows from (
8.13
). In view of (
8.12
), we get that

{{\rm Wh}_{\psi}}(\pi_{S})_{\mathcal{O}}={\left\langle\sigma_{S},\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}_{\mathcal{O}}\right\rangle}_{W}

for every splitting orbit
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
. This completes the proof.
∎

We can verify for
\mathfrak{f}(\psi)=\mathfrak{p}
an analogue of
[
Ga6
, Conjecture 6.9 (i)]
.

Corollary 8.9
.

Let
\Theta(\chi)=\pi_{\Delta}
be an unramified theta representation associated to
\chi
with
\Phi(\chi)=\Delta
. For every splitting orbit
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
, one has

\dim{{\rm Wh}_{\psi}}(\pi)_{\mathcal{O}}\geqslant\dim{{\rm Wh}_{\psi}}(\Theta(\chi))_{\mathcal{O}}

for every irreducible Iwahori-spherical representation
\pi
of
\overline{G}
. In particular, if every orbit
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
is splitting, then
\dim{{\rm Wh}_{\psi}}(\pi)\geqslant\dim{{\rm Wh}_{\psi}}(\Theta(\chi))
for every such
\pi
as well.

Proof.

Let
\pi
be any Iwahori-spherical representation of
\overline{G}
. From (
8.13
), we see that
\dim{{\rm Wh}_{\psi}}(\Theta(\chi))_{\mathcal{O}}=1
if and only if
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
is a free
W
-orbit. In this case,

\mathcal{V}^{I}_{\mathcal{O}}\simeq\mathcal{H}_{I}

as
\mathcal{H}_{I}
-module. It gives that

\dim{{\rm Wh}_{\psi}}(\pi)_{\mathcal{O}}=\dim{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{H}_{I}}(\mathcal{H}_{I},\check{\pi}^{I})\geqslant 1

and thus the result follows.
∎

.

Remark 8.10
.

First, retaining the notation

Henceforth, we assume that

G

for any short simple coroot
\alpha^{\vee}
. We have in this case

Y_{Q,n}=Y_{Q,n}^{sc}=n\cdot Y^{sc}.

and

\overline{G}^{\vee}\simeq G^{\vee}.

The modified affine Weyl group is

\tilde{W}_{{\rm a}}=Y_{Q,n}^{sc}\rtimes W=(nY)\rtimes W,

which acts on
Y\subset Y\otimes\mathbbm{R}
naturally. For any
y\in Y
, we have

\tilde{W}_{{\rm a},y}\subset\tilde{W}_{\rm a},

the stabilizer subgroup of
y
.

For every root
\alpha
and
k\in\mathbbm{Z}
, we have

w_{\alpha,k}=(k\alpha^{\vee},w_{\alpha})\in W_{{\rm a}}=Y\rtimes W,

where
w_{\alpha}(y)=y-{\left\langle y,\alpha\right\rangle}\alpha^{\vee}
the usual reflection. Similarly, we write

\tilde{w}_{\alpha,k}=(k\alpha_{Q,n}^{\vee},w_{\alpha})=(k\cdot n\alpha^{\vee},w_{\alpha})\in\tilde{W}_{{\rm a}}=Y_{Q,n}\rtimes W.

We have

w_{\alpha}=\tilde{w}_{\alpha,0}=w_{\alpha,0}

for every root
\alpha\in\Phi
.

Let
\alpha^{\dagger}\in\Phi^{+}
be the highest root of
\Phi
. Then
\tilde{\alpha}^{\dagger}=\alpha^{\dagger}/n\in\Phi_{Q,n}=\Phi/n
is the highest root of the modified root system.
Recall the set

S_{\rm a}=\left\{\tilde{w}_{\alpha^{\dagger},1}\right\}\cup\left\{w_{\alpha_{i}}:\ 1\leqslant i\leqslant r\right\}

of generators of
\tilde{W}_{\rm a}
. For every
y\in Y
there exists a proper subset

such that

\tilde{W}_{{\rm a},y}\simeq W(S_{{\rm a},y}),

where the right hand sides denotes the subgroup of
\tilde{W}_{\rm a}
generated by
S_{{\rm a},y}
.
The surjective map

\eta:\tilde{W}_{\rm a}\twoheadrightarrow W

restricts to give an isomorphism

\tilde{W}_{{\rm a},y}\simeq\eta(\tilde{W}_{{\rm a},y}).

If we set
\alpha_{0}:=-\alpha^{\dagger}
and define as usual

\Delta_{\rm a}=\left\{\alpha_{0}\right\}\cup\Delta,

\eta(\tilde{W}_{{\rm a},y})=\langle w_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\Delta_{{\rm a},y}\rangle\subset W

for a subset
\Delta_{{\rm a},y}\subsetneq\Delta_{\rm a}
.

below.

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
\overline{G}^{(n)}
be a very saturated cover of an almost simple simply-connected group
G
such that
Q(\alpha^{\vee})=1
for any short simple coroot
\alpha^{\vee}
. Let
y\in Y
. Assume
\alpha_{0}\in\Delta_{{\rm a},y}
. Then

\eta(\tilde{W}_{{\rm a},y})\cap R_{\chi}=\left\{1\right\}

for any unitary unramified genuine character
\chi
of
Z(\overline{T})
.

Proof.

We verify this from a case by case checking.

First, for type
A_{r}
, i.e.,
G={\rm SL}_{r+1}
, the cover
\overline{{\rm SL}}_{r+1}^{(n)}
is very saturated if and only if

\gcd(n,r+1)=1.

Identifying
W\simeq S_{r+1}
, we know that
R_{\chi}
is
W
-conjugate to
\mathbbm{Z}/d\mathbbm{Z}
generated by

w:=(1,2,...,d)(d+1,d+2,...,2d)....(kd+1,...,r+1)

for some
d
with
r+1=dk
. Note that
w
is the Coxeter element of the parabolic Weyl subgroup
\prod_{i=1}^{k}S_{d}\subset W
. Since a conjugate of
w
lies in
W(\Delta_{{\rm a},y})
, it follows that the set
\Delta_{{\rm a},y}
contains
k
-many connected components of the extended Dynkin diagram, each of which is of size at least
d-1
and one of which contains
\alpha_{0}
.
Writing
y=\sum_{i=1}^{r+1}y_{i}e_{i}
with
y_{i}\in\mathbbm{Z}
and
\sum_{i}y_{i}=0
, together with the two equalities

{\left\langle y,\tilde{\alpha}^{\dagger}\right\rangle}=(y_{1}-y_{r+1})/n=1\text{ and }\sum_{i}y_{i}=0,

this shows that there exist distinct
1\leqslant i_{j}\leqslant r+1
with
1\leqslant j\leqslant k
such that

d\cdot\left(\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant k}y_{i_{j}}\right)=n.

Since
\gcd(r+1,n)=1
and
d|(r+1)
, it follows that
d=1
and thus
w=1
.

Second, for type
B_{r}
, the cover
\overline{{\rm Spin}}_{2r+1}^{(n)}
is very saturated if and only if
n
is odd. Write

y=\sum_{i=1}^{r}y_{i}e_{i}\in Y\text{ with }y_{i}\in\mathbbm{Z}\text{ and }2|(\sum_{i}y_{i}).

Depending on the parity of
r
, we have the following two cases of
R_{\chi}
:

–

If
r
is even, then
R_{\chi}\simeq\mathbbm{Z}/2\mathbbm{Z}
is
W
-conjugate to the group generated by

w=(12)(34)...(r-1,r)=w_{\alpha_{1}}w_{\alpha_{3}}...w_{\alpha_{r-1}}.

Suppose
w\in\eta(\tilde{W}_{{\rm a},y})
with
\alpha_{0}\in\Delta_{{\rm a},y}
. Now if
\alpha_{1}\in\Delta_{{\rm a},y}
, then the two equalities

is even.

–

If

W

For such

We continue to write

By Borel’s theorem, every Iwahori-spherical representation

corresponds uniquely to a finite-dimensional irreducible

If a linear

[

]
between the ireducible

-modules and certain Kazhdan–Lusztig parameters of triples

[

]
to include the case when the center of

when

is assumed to have connected center. In recent works by Aubert–Baum–Plymen–Solleveld (for example
[
ABPS17
]

was investigated and a link to the geometric side of the parameter space in terms of the “extended quotients” was established.

Thus one has a local Langlands correspondence

\mathcal{L}:{\rm Irr}_{\epsilon}(\overline{G})^{I}\longrightarrow{\rm Irr}(\mathcal{H}_{I})\longrightarrow\left\{(\tau,u,\rho)\right\},\quad\pi\mapsto\pi^{I}\mapsto(\tau,u,\rho)

from the Iwahori-spherical representations to the set of Kazhdan–Lusztig–Reeder (KLR) parameters. Every KLR parameter that arises in the correspondence above satisfies the following:

–

\tau\in\overline{T}^{\vee}
is a semisimple element in the dual torus inside
\overline{G}^{\vee}
;

–

is a nilpotent element such that
\tau u\tau^{-1}=q^{-1}\cdot u
;

–

\rho\in{\rm Irr}_{\rm geom}(\pi_{0}(\overline{G}^{\vee}_{\tau,u}))
is a certain irreducible representation of connected component group of the mutual centralizer subgroup
\overline{G}^{\vee}_{\tau,u}\subset\overline{G}^{\vee}
that appears in the homology
H_{*}(\mathfrak{B}^{\tau,u}_{\overline{G}^{\vee}},\mathbbm{C})

Here

denotes the variety of Borel subgroups of
\overline{G}^{\vee}
containing both
\tau
and

. In particular, the irreducible constituent

has a KLR parameter

is the Satake parameter associated to

s_{\chi}(w_{1}(\tilde{\rho})-\tilde{\rho})=\xi^{-d(d-1)/2}=(-1)^{d-1}=\varepsilon_{W}(w_{1}).

\bullet

For
D_{r}
with
R_{\chi}\simeq\mathbbm{Z}/4\mathbbm{Z}
, we have necessarily
r
is odd, and a generator for
R_{\chi}
is

w_{1}=w_{\alpha_{1}}w_{\alpha_{3}}...w_{\alpha_{r-4}}\cdot w_{\alpha_{r-2}}w_{\alpha_{r-1}}w_{\alpha_{r}},

and

\chi_{\alpha_{i}}=-1\text{ for }i\in\left\{1,3,5,...,r-4,r-2\right\},\quad\chi_{\alpha_{r-1}}^{2}=-1,\quad\chi_{\alpha_{r}}=\chi_{\alpha_{r-1}}^{-1}.

A straightforward computation gives that

s_{\chi}(w_{1}(\tilde{\rho})-\tilde{\rho})=(-1)^{(r-1)/2},

which is equal to
\varepsilon_{W}(w_{1})=(-1)^{(r+3)/2}
.

\bullet

The last case is for
E_{6}
with
R_{\chi}\simeq\mathbbm{Z}/3\mathbbm{Z}
with a generator

w_{1}=w_{\alpha_{1}}w_{\alpha_{3}}\cdot w_{\alpha_{6}}w_{\alpha_{5}},

and

\chi_{\alpha_{1}}=\chi_{\alpha_{3}}=\chi_{\alpha_{5}}^{-1}=\chi_{\alpha_{6}}^{-1}=\xi,

where
\xi
is a primitive third root of unity. A direct computation gives that

s_{\chi}(w_{1}(\tilde{\rho})-\tilde{\rho})=1=\varepsilon_{W}(w_{1}),

where the first equality already follows from (
9.2
)

This completes the proof of the equality (
9.7
) and also the theorem.

\mathcal{O}

\sigma_{\mathcal{O}}^{{\rm Wh}_{\psi}}\simeq\zeta_{\tilde{\rho}}^{-1}\otimes\sigma_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathscr{X}}\simeq\zeta_{\tilde{\rho}}\otimes\sigma_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathscr{X}}.

Corollary 9.3
.

Let
\overline{G}
be an oasitic cover of a simply-connected semisimple group
G
associated with
Q(\alpha^{\vee})=1
for any short simple coroot
\alpha^{\vee}
. Then, one has

\dim{{\rm Wh}_{\psi}}(\pi_{\sigma})_{\mathcal{O}}={\left\langle\sigma\otimes\zeta_{\tilde{\rho}},\sigma_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathscr{X}}\right\rangle}_{R_{\chi}}={\left\langle\sigma\otimes\zeta_{\tilde{\rho}}^{-1},\sigma_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathscr{X}}\right\rangle}_{R_{\chi}}

for every
W
-orbit
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
and
\sigma\in{\rm Irr}(R_{\chi})
. Hence,

\dim{{\rm Wh}_{\psi}}(\pi_{\sigma})={\left\langle\sigma\otimes\zeta_{\tilde{\rho}},\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}\right\rangle}_{R_{\chi}}={\left\langle\sigma\otimes\zeta_{\tilde{\rho}}^{-1},\sigma^{\mathscr{X}}\right\rangle}_{R_{\chi}}.

9.3.
Conductor of
\psi
varied

Note that for any
z
one clearly has a decomposition

{\rm Wh}_{{}^{z}\psi}(I(\chi))^{\sharp}=\bigoplus_{\sigma\in{\rm Irr}(R_{\chi})}{\rm Wh}_{{}^{z}\psi}(\pi_{\sigma})^{\sharp}.

We want to compare
\dim{\rm Wh}_{\psi}(\pi_{\sigma})^{\sharp}
against
\dim{\rm Wh}_{{}^{\rho}\psi}(\pi_{\sigma})^{\sharp}
with
\mathfrak{f}({}^{\rho}\psi)=O_{F}
, and verify some speculations given in
[
GSS3
, Conjecture 5.7]
.

Corollary 9.4
.

Let
\overline{G}
be an oasitic cover of an almost simple simply-connected
G
with
Q(\alpha^{\vee})=1
. As in Proposition
8.2
, let
y_{-\rho}\in Y
be such that

\mathfrak{m}_{-\rho}:\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}\longrightarrow\mathscr{X}_{Q,n},y\mapsto y+y_{-\rho}

induces a bijective correspondence between
W
-orbits and
(W,-\rho)
-orbits of the domain and codomain of
\mathfrak{m}_{-\rho}
respectively. Let
I(\chi)
be a unitary unramified principal series. Assume that Conjecture
8.7
holds. Also assume
R_{\chi}\simeq\mathbbm{Z}/2\mathbbm{Z}
or
\mathbbm{Z}/2\mathbbm{Z}\times\mathbbm{Z}/2\mathbbm{Z}
. Then for every
\pi_{\sigma}\subset I(\chi)
and every
W
-orbit
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
, one has

\dim{\rm Wh}_{\psi}(\pi_{\sigma})^{\sharp}_{\mathcal{O}}=\dim{\rm Wh}_{{}^{\rho}\psi}(\pi_{\sigma\otimes\zeta^{-1}_{\tilde{\rho}}})^{\sharp}_{\mathfrak{m}_{-\rho}(\mathcal{O})};

hence,

\dim{\rm Wh}_{\psi}(\pi_{\sigma})^{\sharp}=\dim{\rm Wh}_{{}^{\rho}\psi}(\pi_{\sigma\otimes\zeta^{-1}_{\tilde{\rho}}})^{\sharp}

as well.

Proof.

Assuming Conjecture
8.7
, one has

\dim{\rm Wh}_{\psi}(\pi_{\sigma})^{\sharp}_{\mathcal{O}}=\dim{\rm Wh}_{\psi}(\pi_{\sigma})_{\mathcal{O}}={\left\langle\sigma\otimes\zeta_{\tilde{\rho}}^{-1},\sigma_{[0]}^{\mathcal{O}}\right\rangle}_{R_{\chi}},

where the second equality follows from Corollary
9.3
. (Here we use the notation
\sigma_{[0]}^{\mathcal{O}}
for
\sigma_{\mathcal{O}}^{\mathscr{X}}
to highlight the twisting parameter.)

On the other hand, from (
8.7
) we have

\dim{\rm Wh}_{{}^{\rho}\psi}(\pi_{\sigma})_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}^{\sharp}=\dim{\rm Wh}_{{}^{-\rho}(^{w_{G}}\psi)}(\pi_{\sigma})^{\rm std}_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}

for every
(W,-\rho)
-orbit
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
. Note that every
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{m}_{-\rho}(\mathcal{O})
for a unique
W
-orbit
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
. Since
\mathfrak{f}({}^{-\rho}(^{w_{G}}\psi))=O_{F}
, then for
R_{\chi}\simeq(\mathbbm{Z}/2\mathbbm{Z})^{i},i=1,2
it is shown in
[
Ga8
, Theorem 1.4]
that

(9.8)

\dim{\rm Wh}_{{}^{-\rho}(^{w_{G}}\psi)}(\pi_{\sigma\otimes\zeta_{\tilde{\rho}}^{-1}})^{\rm std}_{\mathfrak{m}_{-\rho}(\mathcal{O})}={\left\langle\sigma_{[-\rho]}^{\mathfrak{m}_{-\rho}(\mathcal{O})},\sigma\otimes\zeta_{\tilde{\rho}}^{-1}\right\rangle}_{R_{\chi}}.

However, it follows from Proposition
8.2
that for such covers
\overline{G}
, one has

\sigma_{[-\rho]}^{\mathfrak{m}_{-\rho}(\mathcal{O})}\simeq\sigma_{[0]}^{\mathcal{O}}.

The desired first equality then follows from comparing all the above. The second equality is clear from this.
∎

Para-linearity as the nonassociative counterpart of linearity Thanks: This work was supported by the NNSF of China (11771412).
Qinghai Huo, Guangbin Ren

Abstract
. In an octonionic Hilbert space H H , the octonionic linearity is taken to fail for the maps induced by the octonionic inner products, and it should be replaced with the octonionic para-linearity. However, to introduce the notion of the octonionic para-linearity we encounter an insurmountable obstacle. That is, the axiom ⟨ p ​ u , u ⟩ = p ​ ⟨ u , u ⟩ \left\langle pu,u\right\rangle=p\left\langle u,u\right\rangle for any octonion p p and element u ∈ H u\in H introduced by Goldstine and Horwitz in 1964 can not be interpreted as a property to be obeyed by the octonionic para-linear maps. In this article, we solve this critical problem by showing that this axiom is in fact non-independent from others. This enables us to initiate the study of octonionic para-linear maps. We can thus establish the octonionic Riesz representation theorem which, up to isomorphism, identifies two octonionic Hilbert spaces with one being the dual of the other. The dual space consists of continuous left para-linear functionals and it becomes a right 𝕆 \mathbb{O} -module under the multiplication defined in terms of the second associators which measures the failure of 𝕆 \mathbb{O} -linearity. This right multiplication has an alternative expression ( f ⊙ p ) ​ ( x ) = p ​ f ​ ( p − 1 ​ x ) ​ p , {(f\odot p)(x)}=pf(p^{-1}x)p, which is a generalized Moufang identity. Remarkably, the multiplication is compatible with the canonical norm, i.e., | | f ⊙ p | | = | | f | | ​ | p | . \left|\left|f\odot p\right|\right|=\left|\left|f\right|\right|\left|p\right|. Our final conclusion is that para-linearity is the nonassociative counterpart of linearity.

1.
Introduction

What is the nonassociative counterpart of linearity is
a natural question in the nonassociative realm.
This question has no answer even in octonionic functional analysis
since there still lacks the octonionic Riesz representation theorem which depends on the maps abstracted from the inner products.
In order to explore the reason for this lack, we start with the octonionic Hilbert theory.

The theory of octonionic Hilbert spaces
was initiated by Goldstine and Horwitz
[
3
]
in 1964 and
has many applications in the spectral theory
[
13
]
, operator theory
[
12
]
, and physics
[
2
,
7
,
6
,
16
]
.
But Compared with the quaternionic Hilbert space theory

[
9
,
14
,
15
,
21
,
22
]
, the development of the octonionic Hilbert space theory is lagging behind.
Nonetheless, the existing research on the octonionic Hilbert space theory has emerged some effective methods.
For example, Goldstine and Horwitz
[
3
]

attribute the study of the octonionic Hilbert space to the associative setting;
see
[
4
,
17
]
for the further development.
Ludkovsky and Sprössig
[
12
,
13
]
find the theory can get well developed if the
\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert space admits the tensor decomposition of a real Hilbert space with the algebra of octonions
\mathbb{O}
.

Now we focus on the definition of the octonionic Hilbert space.

Definition 1.1

(
[
3
]
)
.

An
\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert space
H
is a left
\mathbb{O}
-module with an
\mathbb{O}
-inner product

\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle:H\times H\rightarrow\mathbb{O}

such that
(H,\text{Re}\,\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle)
is a real Hilbert space. Here the
\mathbb{O}
-inner product satisfies the following axioms for all
u,v\in H
and
p\in\mathbb{O}
:

(a)

\left\langle u+v,w\right\rangle=\left\langle u,w\right\rangle+\left\langle v,w\right\rangle
;

(b)

\left\langle u,v\right\rangle=\overline{\left\langle v,u\right\rangle}
;

(c)

\left\langle u,u\right\rangle\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
; and
\left\langle u,u\right\rangle=0
if and only if
u=0
;

(d)

\left\langle tu,v\right\rangle=t\left\langle u,v\right\rangle
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
;

(e)

\text{Re}\,\left\langle pu,v\right\rangle=\text{Re}\,(p\left\langle u,v\right\rangle)
;

(f)

\left\langle pu,u\right\rangle=p\left\langle u,u\right\rangle
.

The functionals that need to be studied in the octonionic Hilbert space are naturally induced by the octonionic inner product

(1.1)

\displaystyle f(x)=\langle x,v\rangle

for any given element
v\in H
. However, these real linear maps in (
1.1
) are generally
not octonionic linear maps since there may hold

f(px)\neq pf(x)

for some
p\in\mathbb{O}
and
x\in H
.

It is still an unsolved problem so far
how to define an appropriate functional in octonionic functional analysis. This is because in Definition
1.1
Axiom
(f)
, as a property of
\mathbb{O}
-inner-product, cannot be abstractly interpreted into the property of a functional on a general
\mathbb{O}
-normed space.

Fortunately, we can overcome this obstacle in a roundabout way in this article.
We find that
Axiom
(f)
is actually non-independent. This motivates us to introduce a kind of maps beyond
\mathbb{O}
-linearity, called
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear maps,
defined in any
\mathbb{O}
-modules.

An
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear
map in a left
\mathbb{O}
-module
M

is defined to be a real linear map
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,\mathbb{O})
subject to the condition

(1.2)

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,[p,x,f]=0

for all
p\in\mathbb{O}
and
x\in M
, where the element

[p,x,f]:=f(px)-pf(x)

is called
the second associator
related to
f
.
Condition (
1.2
) becomes Axiom (e) in Definition
1.1

in the specific case where
f
is a map induced by the octonionic inner product as in (
1.1
).

The set of para-linear functions in a left
\mathbb{O}
-module constitutes a right
\mathbb{O}
-module under the multiplication

(1.3)

\displaystyle(f\odot p)(x):=f(x)p-[p,x,f].

Similarly,
\mathbb{K}
-para-linearity can also be defined for any real normed division algebra

\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H},\mathbb{O},

where
\mathbb{H}
is the algebra of quaternions.
Since
\mathbb{K}
-para-linearity degenerates to linearity in the associative setting, this shows para-linearity
is a nonassociative counterpart of linearity.

\mathbb{K}
-para-linearity can be used to define
\mathbb{K}
-Hilbert spaces.
In the specific case where
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{O}
, this definition is belonging to
Goldstine and Horwitz
[
3
]
except that a non-independent axiom is removed.

Definition 1.2
.

Suppose that
H
is a real Hilbert space under the real inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}

and also a left
\mathbb{K}
-module. We call
H
a

\mathbb{K}
-Hilbert space
if there exists an
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear map

\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle:H\times H\rightarrow\mathbb{K}

such that

Re\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}

and
satisfying

(
{\mathrm{i}}
)

(
\mathbb{K}
-para-linearity)

\left\langle\cdot,u\right\rangle
is left
\mathbb{K}
-para-linear for all
u\in H
.

(
{\mathrm{ii}}
)

(
\mathbb{K}
-hermiticity)

\left\langle u,v\right\rangle=\overline{\left\langle v,u\right\rangle}
for all
u,v\in H
.

(
{\mathrm{iii}}
)

(Positivity)

\left\langle u,u\right\rangle\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
; and
\left\langle u,u\right\rangle=0
if and only if
u=0
.

Now we can use
\mathbb{O}
-para-linearity to study the Riesz representation theorem in
\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert spaces
H
.
The theorem should be expressed in the following form

(1.4)

\displaystyle H^{*}\cong{H}.

Here
H^{*}
is the dual space consisting of
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear functions and
the bijection in (
1.4
) between two spaces is both an isometric isomorphism as real Banach spaces and a conjugate isomorphism as
\mathbb{O}
-modules.

Of the most difficult is to show the compatibility
between the module structure of
H^{*}
and its canonical norm, i.e.,

\left|\left|f\odot p\right|\right|=\left|\left|f\right|\right|\left|p\right|

for all
f\in H^{*}
and
p\in\mathbb{O}
, where
f\odot p
is the right multiplication defined in (
1.3
).
Overcoming this difficulty requires another expression of the right multiplication

{(f\odot p)(x)}=pf(p^{-1}x)p,

which is a generalized Moufang identity
[
18
]
.

The octonionic Riesz representation theorem in (
1.4
) can be restated as

(1.5)

\displaystyle(H^{*})^{-}\cong{H}.

Here
(H^{*})^{-}
denotes the set
H^{*}
endowed
with a left
\mathbb{O}
-module defined by

p\cdot x:=x\overline{p}.

The point is that
the bijection in (
1.5
) is an isomorphism of two left
\mathbb{O}
-modules in contrast to the conjugate isomorphism in (
1.4
).

Space
(H^{*})^{-}
can be endowed with an
\mathbb{O}
-inner product via isomorphism
(
1.5
) so that it becomes an
\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert space.
The octonionic Riesz representation theorem shows that
the two
\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert spaces in (
1.5
) coincide
up to isomorphism. This result is true if
\mathbb{O}
is replaced by any normed division algebras
\mathbb{K}
.

Theorem 1.3

(Riesz representation theorem)
.

Let
\mathbb{K}

be a normed division algebra.
Let
H
be a
\mathbb{K}
-Hilbert space and
H^{*}
its dual consisting of all continuous
\mathbb{K}
-para-linear functionals. Then we can identify the two
\mathbb{K}
-Hilbert spaces
H
and
(H^{*})^{-}

up to isomorphism.

Opposed to the associative setting, we have two distinct spaces

H^{*_{\mathbb{O}}}\subsetneq H^{*}.

Here
H^{*_{\mathbb{O}}}
consists of all continuous
\mathbb{O}
-linear functionals of
H
. Under isomorphism (
1.4
),
H^{*_{\mathbb{O}}}
is corresponding to the nucleus of
H
consisting of associative elements. Symbolically,

\displaystyle H^{*_{\mathbb{O}}}\cong\mathscr{A}(H).

This shows that the class of
\mathbb{O}
-linear functionals is too small to represent every element in an
\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert space.

We remark that there are profound relations between the associative and nonassociative theories.
For example, the algebra of octonions is closely related to the Clifford algebra of
6,7
dimensions
[
8
]
, and the category of left
\mathbb{O}
-modules is shown to be isomorphic to the category of left
C\ell_{7}
-modules
[
10
]
.
But
the octonionic theory has its intrinsic objects such as the
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear functionals as demonstrated in the octonionic Riesz representation theorem.

After para-linearity is introduced as a substitute for linearity in nonassociative framework, one can further study para-linear operators,
weak topology related to para-linear functionals,
octonionic
C^{*}
algebra, and para-linear connections.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we review some notations and basic properties about octonions
\mathbb{O}
and left
\mathbb{O}
-modules. We refer to
[
1
,
10
]

2.1.
Octonions

The set of octonions
\mathbb{O}
is a nonassociative, noncommutative, normed division algebra over
\mathbb{R}
. Let

e_{0}=1,\ e_{1},\ \dots,\ e_{7}

be a basis of
\mathbb{O}
as a real vector space subject to the multiplication rule

(2.1)

\displaystyle e_{i}e_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{7}\epsilon_{ijk}e_{k}-\delta_{ij}.

for any
i,j=1,\dots,7
. Here
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta and
\epsilon_{ijk}
is completely antisymmetric with value 1 precisely when

ijk=123,145,176,246,257,347,365.

Let
x=x_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{7}x_{i}e_{i}
be an octonion with
all
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
.
We define its conjugate

\overline{x}:=x_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{7}x_{i}e_{i},

its norm

|x|=\sqrt{x\overline{x}},

and its real part

\text{Re}\,{x}=x_{0}=\frac{1}{2}(x+\overline{x}).

2.2.
\mathbb{O}
-modules

We recall some basic notations and results on
\mathbb{O}
-modules in this subsection. We refer to

[
11
,
20
,
19
,
10
]

for octonionic modules and even for modules of alternative algebras.

A left
\mathbb{O}
-module
M
is
a real vector space
M
endowed with an
\mathbb{O}
-scalar multiplication such that the
left associator
is left alternative, i.e.,
for all
p,q\in\mathbb{O}
and
m\in M
we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle[p,q,m]=-[q,p,m].

Here the left associator is defined by

[p,q,m]:=(pq)m-p(qm).

Condition (
2.2
) is equivalent to the requirement

r(rm)=r^{2}m

for all
r\in\mathbb{O}
and all
m\in M
.

Of the most important identity involving associators in a left
\mathbb{O}
-module
M
is the five-term identity

(2.3)

[p,q,r]m+p[q,r,m]=[pq,r,m]-[p,qr,m]+[p,q,rm]

for all
p,q,r\in\mathbb{O}
and
m\in M
.
This even holds when
M
is a nonassociative algebra
[
18
]
.

In the theory of left
\mathbb{O}
-modules,
associative and conjugate associative elements play key roles
(
[
10
]
). Let
M
be a left
\mathbb{O}
-module.
An element
m\in M
is called an
associative element
if

(pq)m=p(qm)

and called
a
conjugate associative element
if

(pq)m=q(pm).

The set of all associative elements is called
the
nucleus
of
M
and denoted by
\mathscr{A}(M)
, i.e.,

\mathscr{A}(M):=\{m\in M\mid[p,q,m]=0\text{ for all }p,q\in\mathbb{O}\}.

The set of all conjugate associative elements of
M
is denoted by
\mathscr{A}^{-}(M)
, i.e.,

\mathscr{A}^{-}(M):=\{m\in M\mid(pq)m=q(pm)\text{ for all }p,q\in\mathbb{O}\}.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
M
be a left
\mathbb{O}
-module. Then

M\cong\mathbb{O}\mathscr{A}(M)\oplus{\mathbb{O}}\mathscr{A}^{-}(M).

3.
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear functions

The notion of the second associator
is used to describe the extent to which a real linear map is far away from the octonionic linear map.
This notion also allows us to define
the para-linear map.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
M
be a left
\mathbb{O}
-module and let
\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,\mathbb{O})
be the set of all real linear functions
f:M\to\mathbb{O}
.
We introduce a real trilinear map

[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]:\mathbb{O}\times M\times\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,\mathbb{O})\xlongrightarrow{}\mathbb{O},

defined by

(3.1)

[p,x,f]:=f(px)-pf(x).

We call this map
the second associator
in
M
, which measures the failure of
\mathbb{O}
-linearity.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
M
be a left
\mathbb{O}
-module.
A real linear map
f:M\xlongrightarrow{}\mathbb{O}

is called an
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear
function if it satisfies

\text{Re}\,[p,x,f]=0

for any
p\in\mathbb{O}
and
x\in M
.

We denote by
\text{Hom}_{L\mathbb{O}}(M,\mathbb{O})
the set of all
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear functions. By definition, we have

\text{Hom}_{L\mathbb{O}}(M,\mathbb{O})=\{f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,\mathbb{O}):\ \text{Re}\,[p,x,f]=0\ \ \mbox{for \ all \ $p\in\mathbb{O}$ \ and \ $x\in M$ }\}.

Example 3.3
.

The right multiplication on
\mathbb{O}
provides a typical example of
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear function, i.e.,

R_{q}\in\text{Hom}_{L\mathbb{O}}(\mathbb{O},\mathbb{O})

for any
q\in\mathbb{O}
.
Here
R_{q}
denotes the
right multiplication defined by

R_{q}(x)=xq.

In this typical example, we find that the associator and the second associator coincide, i.e.,

(3.2)

[p,x,R_{q}]=[p,x,q]

for any
p,x,q\in\mathbb{O}
.
Indeed, by definition we have

[p,x,R_{q}]=R_{q}(px)-pR_{q}(x)=(px)q-p(xq)=[p,x,q].

Identity (
3.2
) also implies

\text{Re}\,[p,x,R_{q}]=0

so that
R_{q}
is
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear.

If
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,\mathbb{O})
, then it can be written as

(3.3)

\displaystyle f(x)=f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{7}e_{i}f_{i}(x),

where

f_{\mathbb{R}}(x):=\text{Re}\,f(x)

and
f_{i}(x)\in\mathbb{R}\text{ for }i=1,\dots,7
.

Now we give a criterion for determining whether a real linear function
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,\mathbb{O})
is an
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear map.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,\mathbb{O})
with expression as in (
3.3
). Then

f
is
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear if and only if

(3.4)

\displaystyle f_{i}(x)=f_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{e_{i}}x)

for all
i=0,\dots,7
and all
x\in\mathbb{O}
.

Proof.

We assume
f

\mathbb{O}
-para-linear.
By definition, we have

\displaystyle f_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{e_{i}}x)

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\,f(\overline{e_{i}}x)

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\,(\overline{e_{i}}f(x))

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\,\Big(\overline{e_{i}}\sum_{j=0}^{7}e_{j}f_{j}(x)\Big)

\displaystyle=f_{i}(x).

Conversely, under assumptions (
3.4
), for any
i=1,\ldots,7
we claim

(3.5)

\displaystyle e_{i}f(x)=e_{i}f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)+\sum_{j,k=1}^{7}\epsilon_{ijk}e_{k}f_{j}(x)-f_{i}(x)

Indeed, by calculation we have

\displaystyle e_{i}f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{i}(f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{7}e_{j}f_{j}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{i}f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{7}e_{i}e_{j}f_{j}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{i}f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)+\sum_{j,k=1}^{7}\epsilon_{ijk}e_{k}f_{j}(x)-f_{i}(x)

and the claim holds. From the claim we have

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,[e_{i},x,f]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Re(f(e_{i}x)-e_{i}f(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{\mathbb{R}}(e_{i}x)+f_{i}(x)=0.

This finishes the proof.

∎

The second associator of a
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear map in
\text{Hom}_{L\mathbb{O}}(M,\mathbb{O})
has a close relation with the associator in
\mathbb{O}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M,\mathbb{O})
with expression as in (
3.3
). Then

f
is
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear if and only if

(3.6)

\displaystyle[p,x,f]=\sum_{i=1}^{7}e_{i}f_{\mathbb{R}}([e_{i},p,x])

for all
i=0,\dots,7
and all
p\in\mathbb{O}
and
x\in M
.

Proof.

To prove the necessity, we might as well set
p=e_{i}
with
i=1,\ldots,7
. Since
f
is
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear, Theorem
3.4
shows

(3.7)

\displaystyle f(x)=f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)-\sum_{j=1}^{7}e_{j}f_{\mathbb{R}}(e_{j}x).

Notice that

(3.8)

\displaystyle e_{j}(e_{i}x)=(e_{j}e_{i})x-[e_{j},e_{i},x]=\sum_{k=1}^{7}\epsilon_{jik}e_{k}x-\delta_{ij}x-[e_{j},e_{i},x].

Now we replace
x
by
e_{i}x
in (
3.7
) and then apply (
3.8
). Due to the real linearity of
f_{\mathbb{R}}
we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle f(e_{i}x)=f_{\mathbb{R}}(e_{i}x)-\sum_{j,k=1}^{7}\epsilon_{jik}e_{j}f_{\mathbb{R}}(e_{k}x)+e_{i}f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)+\sum_{j=1}^{7}e_{j}f_{\mathbb{R}}([e_{j},e_{i},x]).

On the other hand we have shown in (
3.5
) that

(3.10)

\displaystyle e_{i}f(x)=e_{i}f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)+\sum_{j,k=1}^{7}\epsilon_{ijk}e_{k}f_{j}(x)-f_{i}(x)

We subtract both sides in (
3.9
) and (
3.10
). Since
\epsilon_{ijk}
is completely antisymmetric, we apply the fact that

f_{i}(x)=-f_{\mathbb{R}}({e_{i}}x)

and identity (
3.4
) to conclude

\displaystyle[e_{i},x,f]=f(e_{i}x)-e_{i}f(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{7}e_{j}f_{\mathbb{R}}([e_{j},p,x]).

Conversely, if conditions (
3.6
) hold, then

\text{Re}\,[p,x,f]=0

so that
f
is
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear by definition.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Theorems
3.4
and
3.5
hold if
\mathbb{O}
is replaced by any Cayley-Dickson algebra
\mathbb{K}
(see
[

)
under the similar notion of
\mathbb{K}
-para-linear functions.

\mathbb{K}
-para-linearity becomes

As direct consequences of
Theorems
3.4
and
3.5
,
the second associator vanishes for associative element and an
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear map is uniquely determined by its real part.

Corollary 3.7
.

Assume that
f:M\to\mathbb{O}
is
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear.

(
{\mathrm{i}}
)

For each associative element
x\in\mathscr{A}(M)
, we have

[p,x,f]=0

and hence for all
p\in\mathbb{O}

f(px)=pf(x).

(
{\mathrm{ii}}
)

f_{\mathbb{R}}=0
if and only if
f=0
.

To study the
\mathbb{O}
-module structure of
\text{Hom}_{L\mathbb{O}}(M,\mathbb{O})
, we need some useful identities about the second associators.

Proposition 3.8
.

Suppose that

f\in\text{Hom}_{L\mathbb{O}}(M,\mathbb{O})
. Then for any
p,q\in\mathbb{O}
and
x\in M
we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle p[p,x,f]

\displaystyle=[p,x,f]\overline{p}=[p,\overline{p}x,f];

(3.12)

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\left(f([p,q,x])\right)

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\,\left([p,x,f]{q}\right);

(3.13)

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\left([p,x,f]q\right)

\displaystyle=-\text{Re}\,\left([q,x,f]p\right).

Proof.

As usual, we write

f(x)=f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{7}e_{i}f_{i}(x).

To prove
(
3.11
), without loss of generality we may assume that
p\in\mathit{Im}(\mathbb{O})
. Let
p=\sum_{i=1}^{7}p_{i}e_{i}
with
p_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then by Theorem
3.5
, we have

\displaystyle p[p,x,f]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{7}p_{i}p_{j}e_{i}[e_{j},x,f]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{7}p_{i}p_{j}e_{i}e_{k}f_{\mathbb{R}}([e_{k},e_{j},x]).

Similarly, we get

\displaystyle[p,x,f]\overline{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{7}p_{i}p_{j}[e_{j},x,f]\overline{e_{i}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{7}p_{i}p_{j}e_{k}e_{i}f_{\mathbb{R}}([e_{k},e_{j},x]).

Since

e_{i}e_{k}+e_{k}e_{i}=-2\delta_{ik},

it follows that

p[p,x,f]-[p,x,f]\overline{p}=-2\sum_{i,j=1}^{7}p_{i}p_{j}f_{\mathbb{R}}([e_{i},e_{j},x]).

Because of the skew symmetricity of the associator, we get

p[p,x,f]=[p,x,f]\overline{p}.

By direct calculations, we get

\displaystyle[{p},\overline{p}x,f]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\left|p\right|^{2}x)-pf({\overline{p}}x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|p\right|^{2}f(x)-pf({\overline{p}}x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p(\overline{p}f(x))-pf({\overline{p}}x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-p[\overline{p},x,f]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p[{p},x,f].

Now we come to prove (
3.12
).
without loss of generality we may assume
q=e_{i}
with
i=1,\dots,7
.
By direct calculations, we have

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\left([p,x,f]{q}\right)

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\,\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{7}(e_{j}f_{\mathbb{R}}([e_{j},p,x]))e_{i}\Big)

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\,\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{7}f_{\mathbb{R}}([e_{j},p,x])e_{j}e_{i}\Big)

\displaystyle=-f_{\mathbb{R}}([e_{i},p,x])

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\,\big(f([p,q,x])\big).

Identity (
3.13
) follows from (
3.12
) directly.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

Identity (
3.11
) generalizes the third identity of Lemma 3.9 in
[
5
]
.

Utilizing Proposition
3.8
, we can endow
\text{Hom}_{L\mathbb{O}}(M,\mathbb{O})
with a canonical right
\mathbb{O}
-module structure.
Let
r\in\mathbb{O}
,
f\in\text{Hom}_{L\mathbb{O}}(M,\mathbb{O})
, and
x\in M
. We define the right scalar multiplication

(3.14)

\displaystyle(f\odot r)(x)\coloneqq\ f(x)r-[r,x,f].

Theorem 3.10
.

If
M
be a left
\mathbb{O}
-module, then
\text{Hom}_{L\mathbb{O}}(M,\mathbb{O})
is a right
\mathbb{O}
-module.

Proof.

First we need to show that for any

f\in\text{Hom}_{L\mathbb{O}}(M,\mathbb{O})

and
r\in\mathbb{O}
we have

f\odot r\in\text{Hom}_{L\mathbb{O}}(M,\mathbb{O}).

That is to prove

(3.15)

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,[p,x,f\odot r]=0.

By direct calculations, we have

\displaystyle[p,x,f\odot r]

\displaystyle=(f\odot r)(px)-p((f\odot r)(x))

\displaystyle=f(px)r-[r,px,f]-p\left(f(x)r-[r,x,f]\right)

\displaystyle=\left(pf(x)+[p,x,f]\right)r-[r,px,f]-p\left(f(x)r-[r,x,f]\right)

(3.16)

\displaystyle=[p,f(x),r]+[p,x,f]r-[r,px,f]+p[r,x,f].

Since

\text{Re}\,[p,f(x),r]=0,\quad\text{Re}\,[r,px,f]=0,

(
3
) tells us that we have to show

\text{Re}\,([p,x,f]r)=-\text{Re}\,(p[r,x,f]).

This can be easily deduced by applying identity (
3.13
) and the fact that

\text{Re}\,(pq)=\text{Re}\,(qp)

for all
p,q\in\mathbb{O}
.

Next we come to show that
\text{Hom}_{L\mathbb{O}}(M,\mathbb{O})
is a right
\mathbb{O}
-module. It suffices to prove

(f\odot r)\odot r=f\odot(r^{2})

for any
r\in\mathbb{O}
.
Due to the uniqueness as shown in
assertion
({\mathrm{ii}})
of Corollary
3.7
, we need to prove

\text{Re}\,{\big((f\odot r)\odot r-f\odot(r^{2})\big)(x)}=0.

By definition (
3.14
) and identity (
3.11
), we have

\displaystyle{((f\odot r)\odot r)(x)}

\displaystyle={\big((f\odot r)(x)\big)}r-[r,x,f\odot r]

(3.17)

\displaystyle=f(x)r^{2}-[r,x,f]r-[r,x,f\odot r]

and

(3.18)

\displaystyle{(f\odot(r^{2}))(x)}

\displaystyle=f(x)r^{2}-[{r^{2}},x,f].

Notice that

\text{Re}\,[r,x,f\odot r]=0
,
\text{Re}\,[{r^{2}},x,f]=0
,
and

(3.19)

\displaystyle[r,x,f]=-[\overline{r},x,f].

We thus conclude from (
3.17
) and (
3.18
) that

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,{\big((f\odot r)\odot r-f\odot(r^{2})\big)(x)}

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\,([\overline{r},x,f]r)=\text{Re}\,([\overline{r},rx,f])=0.

The last but one step used (
3.11
).
This completes the proof. ∎

4.
\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert spaces

We adopt the definition of
\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert spaces
by Goldstine and Horwitz
[
3
]
except that we describe it in terms of
\mathbb{O}
-para-linearity.
In addition, we find a non-independent axiom given by Goldstine and Horwitz
[
3
]
.

4.1.
Definition of Hilbert
\mathbb{O}
-modules

We first introduce a succinct definition of
\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert spaces.
This definition also suits all normed division algebras.

Definition 4.1
.

A left
\mathbb{O}
-module
H
is called a
pre-Hilbert left
\mathbb{O}
-module
if there exists an
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear map
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle:H\times H\rightarrow\mathbb{O}
, referred to as an
\mathbb{O}
-inner product
, satisfying:

(
{\mathrm{i}}
)

(
\mathbb{O}
-para-linearity)

\left\langle\cdot,u\right\rangle
is left
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear for all
u\in H
.

(
{\mathrm{ii}}
)

(Octonion hermiticity)

\left\langle u,v\right\rangle=\overline{\left\langle v,u\right\rangle}
for all
u,v\in H
.

(
{\mathrm{iii}}
)

(Positivity)

\left\langle u,u\right\rangle\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
; and
\left\langle u,u\right\rangle=0
if and only if
u=0
.

Under axioms in Definition
4.1
, we can deduce in Lemma
4.6
below that

(4.1)

\left\langle pu,u\right\rangle=p\left\langle u,u\right\rangle

for all
p\in\mathbb{O}
and all
u\in H
. This equation is one of the axioms of Goldstine and Horwitz as part
(f)
of
[
3
, Postulate
2
]
.

We introduce a real trilinear map

[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot]:\mathbb{O}\times M\times M\xlongrightarrow{}\mathbb{O},

defined by

[p,u,v]:=\left\langle pu,v\right\rangle-p\left\langle u,v\right\rangle

for
u,v\in H
and
p\in\mathbb{O}
. We call this map
the second associator
in
H
.

Identity (
4.1
) can be restated as

[p,u,u]=0.

Remark 4.2
.

The second associator has been introduced in the space
\text{Hom}_{L\mathbb{O}}(H,\mathbb{O})
in Section
3
.
These two notions in
H
and
H^{*}
coincide if we identify
H
with
H^{*}
(see Theorem
5.6
). In fact, for any
p\in\mathbb{O}
and
u,v\in H
we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle[p,u,v]=[p,u,v^{\prime}],

where
v^{\prime}\in{\text{Hom}_{L\mathbb{O}}(H,\mathbb{O})}
is a map associated to
v
via the inner product, i.e.,

v^{\prime}=\left\langle\cdot,v\right\rangle.

Indeed, by definition we have

\displaystyle[p,u,v^{\prime}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v^{\prime}(pu)-pv^{\prime}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle pu,v\right\rangle-p\left\langle u,v\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[p,u,v].

Example 4.3
.

If
\mathbb{O}
is regarded as an
\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert space with the inner product

\left<x,y\right>:=x\overline{y}

then its second associator of
p,u,v
is given by

\displaystyle[p,u,v]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<pu,v\right>-p\left<u,v\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(pu)\overline{v}-p(u\overline{v}),

which is exactly the associator of
p,u,\overline{v}
in the algebra
\mathbb{O}
.

Second associators in an
\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert space can be studied with the aid of
the theory of
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear functionals
based on relation (
4.2
).

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
H
be a pre-Hilbert (left)
\mathbb{O}
-module,
p,q\in\mathbb{O}
and
v,u\in H
. Then we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle[p,v,u]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{7}e_{i}\text{Re}\,\left<[e_{i},p,v],u\right>;

(4.4)

\displaystyle[{pq},v,u]

\displaystyle=\left<[p,q,v],u\right>-[p,q,\left<v,u\right>]+p[q,v,u]+[p,qv,u];

(4.5)

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\left<[p,q,v],u\right>

\displaystyle=-\text{Re}\,\left(p[q,v,u]\right).

Proof.

We fix
u\in H
and take
f:=u^{\prime}\in{\text{Hom}_{L\mathbb{O}}(H,\mathbb{O})}
in
Theorem

3.5
.
Since

f(x)=u^{\prime}(x)=\langle x,u\rangle

and

f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)=\text{Re}\,\langle x,u\rangle,

we thus conclude that
identity (
4.3
) follows from
Theorem

3.5
.

Identity (
4.4
) can be proved in the manner similar to the proof of identity (
2.3
). Identity (
4.5
) follows from (
4.4
) by taking the real part on both sides.
∎

If
H
is a pre-Hilbert (left)
\mathbb{O}
-module, then
(H,\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}})
is a real Hilbert space,
where

\left\langle x,y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}:=\text{Re}\,\left\langle x,y\right\rangle.

Conversely, it is quite natural to ask if a real Hilbert space with a structure of left
\mathbb{O}
-module admits an
\mathbb{O}
-inner product.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
H
be a left
\mathbb{O}
-module equipped with a real valued inner product
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>_{0}
. Then
H
admits an
\mathbb{O}
-inner product
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>
such that

(4.6)

\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>_{\mathbb{R}}=\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>_{0}

if and only if

(4.7)

\left\langle px,y\right\rangle_{0}=\left\langle x,\overline{p}y\right\rangle_{0}

holds
for all
p\in\mathbb{O}
and all
x,y\in H
.

Proof.

Suppose the
\mathbb{O}
-inner product
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>
in
H
satisfies (
4.6
).
To prove (
4.7
), we need to show

\text{Re}\,\left<px,y\right>=\text{Re}\,\left\langle x,\overline{p}y\right\rangle

for all
p\in\mathbb{O}
and
x,y\in H
.
By direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\left<px,y\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,(p\left<x,y\right>+[p,x,y])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,(p\left<x,y\right>)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,(\overline{p\left<x,y\right>})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,({\left<y,x\right>}\overline{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,(\overline{p}{\left<y,x\right>})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,{\left<\overline{p}y,x\right>}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,\left\langle x,\overline{p}y\right\rangle.

Conversely,
suppose that (
4.7
) holds.
We claim that the following inner product satisfies all conditions:

(4.8)

\displaystyle\left\langle x,y\right\rangle_{\mathbb{O}}:=\left\langle x,y\right\rangle_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{7}\left\langle e_{i}x,y\right\rangle_{0}e_{i}.

According to the construction, equation (
4.6
) obviously holds.
It remains to show that

\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\mathbb{O}}
is an
\mathbb{O}
-inner product.

Evidently,
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\mathbb{O}}
is
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear. We come to prove the para-linearity.
Define

(4.9)

f(x):=\left<x,y\right>_{\mathbb{O}}

for any given
y\in H
. Then
f
is clearly a real linear map. We write

(4.10)

f(x)=f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{7}f_{i}(x)e_{i},

where
f_{\mathbb{R}}(x),\,f_{i}(x)\in\mathbb{R}
.
Combining (
4.8
) with (
4.9
), we obtain another expression of
f
which demonstrates that

f_{i}(x)=-\left\langle e_{i}x,y\right\rangle_{0}=-\text{Re}\,\left\langle e_{i}x,y\right\rangle=-\text{Re}\,f(e_{i}x)=-f_{\mathbb{R}}(e_{i}x)

for
i=1,\dots,7
. Then Theorem
3.4
implies that
f
is left para-linear. This proves the para-linearity of
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\mathbb{O}}
.

The octonionic hermiticity follows from condition (
4.7
). Indeed,

\displaystyle\overline{{\left<x,y\right>}_{\mathbb{O}}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle x,y\right\rangle_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{7}\left\langle e_{i}x,y\right\rangle_{0}e_{i}

\displaystyle=\left\langle y,x\right\rangle_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{7}\left\langle x,\overline{e_{i}}y\right\rangle_{0}e_{i}

\displaystyle=\left\langle y,x\right\rangle_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{7}\left\langle{e_{i}}y,x\right\rangle_{0}e_{i}

\displaystyle=\left<y,x\right>_{\mathbb{O}}.

Finally, we show the positivity of
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\mathbb{O}}
. For each
i=1,\dots,7
, we have

\left<e_{i}x,x\right>_{0}=\left<x,\overline{e_{i}}x\right>_{0}=-\left<x,e_{i}x\right>_{0}=-\left<e_{i}x,x\right>_{0}

so that
\left<e_{i}x,x\right>_{0}=0
. This means

\left<x,x\right>_{\mathbb{O}}=\left<x,x\right>_{0},

which implies the positivity. ∎

The associator
[p,q,u]
in the
\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert space
H

is
an skew-adjoint map as a function of
u
,
while the second associator
[p,u,v]
in the
\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert space
H

is an alternating real bilinear map of
u
and
v
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
H
be a pre-Hilbert left
\mathbb{O}
-module. For any
u,v\in H
and
p,q\in\mathbb{O}
, we have

(4.11)

\displaystyle\left\langle[p,q,u],v\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle u,[p,q,v]\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}};

(4.12)

\displaystyle{[p,u,v]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[p,v,u].

Proof.

Identity (
4.11
)
follows from Theorem
4.5
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\left\langle[p,q,u],v\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle(pq)u-p(qu),v\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle u,\overline{(pq)}v-\overline{q}(\overline{p}v)\big\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle u,[\overline{q},\overline{p},v]\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle u,[q,p,v]\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle u,[p,q,v]\right\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}.

Due to identities (
4.3
) and (
4.11
) we obtain

\displaystyle[p,u,v]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{7}e_{i}\left<[e_{i},p,u],v\right>_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{7}e_{i}\left<[e_{i},p,v],u\right>_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[p,v,u].

This proves (
4.12
).

∎

As a direct consequence of Lemma

4.6
, we have for any
p\in\mathbb{O}
and
u\in H

[p,u,u]=0.

The next lemma tells us how it works when factors of octonions pass through the
\mathbb{O}
-inner product.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
H
be a pre-Hilbert left
\mathbb{O}
-module. For any
u,v\in H
and
p,q\in\mathbb{O}
, the following identities hold:

(4.13)

\displaystyle\left<u,pv\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<u,v\right>\overline{p}+[p,u,v];

(4.14)

\displaystyle\left<pu,qv\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p(\left<u,v\right>\overline{q})+p[q,u,v]+[p,u,qv];

(4.15)

\displaystyle\left<pu,qv\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p\left<u,v\right>)\overline{q}+[pq,u,v]+\left<[p,q,v],u\right>.

Proof.

We first prove (
4.13
). By the octonionic hermiticity, we conclude from (
4.12
) that

\displaystyle\left<u,pv\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\left<pv,u\right>}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{p\left<v,u\right>+[p,v,u]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<u,v\right>\overline{p}-{[p,v,u]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<u,v\right>\overline{p}+[p,u,v].

(
4.14
)
can be deduced from (
4.13
) directly. Indeed,

\displaystyle\left<pu,qv\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\left<u,qv\right>+[p,u,qv]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p(\left<u,v\right>\overline{q})+p{[q,u,v]}+[p,u,qv].

It follows from identities (
4.4
) and (
4.12
) that

p[q,u,v]+[p,u,qv]=\left<[p,q,v],u\right>-[p,q,\left<v,u\right>]+[pq,u,v].

Combining this with (
4.14
), we get

\displaystyle\left<pu,qv\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p(\left<u,v\right>\overline{q})+\left<[p,q,v],u\right>-[p,q,\left<v,u\right>]+[pq,u,v]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p(\left<u,v\right>\overline{q})+[p,\left<u,v\right>,\overline{q}]+\left<[p,q,v],u\right>+[pq,u,v]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p\left<u,v\right>)\overline{q}+[pq,u,v]+\left<[p,q,v],u\right>.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

All discussions in this subsection still hold when
\mathbb{O}
is replaced by an arbitrary real normed division algebra
\mathbb{K}
.
This verifies that the Hilbert space over any normed division algebras can be defined uniformly.

4.2.
Norm of
\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert space

In this subsection, we collect some known results about the norm of an
\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert space; see
[
3
]
.
Our method is suitable for
any Hilbert space over any normed division algebras.

Definition 4.9
.

A normed left
\mathbb{O}
-module is a pair
(X,\|\cdot\|)
consisting of a left
\mathbb{O}
-module
M
and a map

\|\cdot\|:\ X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

satisfying the following axioms.

(
{\mathrm{i}}
)

\left|\left|x\right|\right|\geqslant 0
for all
x\in X
with equality if and only if
x=0
.

(
{\mathrm{ii}}
)

\left|\left|px\right|\right|=|p|\left|\left|x\right|\right|
for all
p\in\mathbb{O}
and
x\in X
.

(
{\mathrm{iii}}
)

\left|\left|x+y\right|\right|\leqslant\left|\left|x\right|\right|+\left|\left|y\right|\right|
for all
x,y\in X
.

A normed
\mathbb{O}
-module is said to be a Banach
\mathbb{O}
-module if it is complete with respect to its canonical norm.

Definition 4.10
.

An
\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert space

is a pre-Hilbert left
\mathbb{O}
-module
H
which is complete with respect to the norm

\left|\left|\cdot\right|\right|:H\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
induced by the
\mathbb{O}
-inner product, i.e.,

(4.16)

\displaystyle\left|\left|u\right|\right|=\sqrt{\left<u,u\right>}.

It is easy to check that in an
\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert space there holds the polarization identity:

(4.17)

\displaystyle\left<u,v\right>=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=0}^{7}e_{i}\left(\left|\left|e_{i}u+v\right|\right|^{2}-\left|\left|e_{i}u-v\right|\right|^{2}\right).

This means that the
\mathbb{O}
-inner-product can be recovered from the norm.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert left
\mathbb{O}
-module.
Then
H
is a Banach left
\mathbb{O}
-module with respect to the norm induced from the
\mathbb{O}
-inner product.

Proof.

Since
(H,\left|\left|\cdot\right|\right|)
is a real Banach space, it remains to show that

\left|\left|pu\right|\right|=\left|p\right|\left|\left|u\right|\right|

for any
p\in\mathbb{O}
and
x\in X
.
According to (
4.12
) and (
4.15
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left<pu,pu\right>

\displaystyle=(p\left<u,u\right>)\overline{p}+[{pp},u,u]+\left<[p,p,u],u\right>=|p|^{2}\left|\left|u\right|\right|^{2}.

This proves that
H
is a Banach left
\mathbb{O}
-module.
∎

The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality has been established in
[
3
]
. Here we give an alternative proof
based on Lemma
4.7
.

Proposition 4.12
.

For all
u,v\in H
, we have the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

(4.18)

\displaystyle\left|\left<u,v\right>\right|\leqslant\left|\left|u\right|\right|\left|\left|v\right|\right|.

Equality holds in (
4.18
) if and only if
u
and
v
are
\mathbb{O}
-linearly dependent.

Proof.

To prove the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
without loss of generality we may assume
\left|\left|v\right|\right|=1
. Denote
p=\left<u,v\right>
. It follows from (
4.13
) that

\displaystyle\left<u-pv,pv\right>_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,(\left<u-pv,v\right>\overline{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,((\left<u,v\right>-p\left<v,v\right>)\ \overline{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

This implies that

(4.19)

\displaystyle\left|\left|u\right|\right|^{2}=\left|\left|u-pv\right|\right|^{2}+\left|\left|pv\right|\right|^{2}\geqslant\left|\left|pv\right|\right|^{2}.

Since

\left|\left|pv\right|\right|=\left|p\right|\left|\left|v\right|\right|=\left|p\right|,

we can rewrite (
4.19
) as

\left|\left<u,v\right>\right|^{2}\leqslant\left|\left|u\right|\right|^{2}=\left|\left|u\right|\right|^{2}\left|\left|v\right|\right|^{2}.

Moreover, in view of (
4.19
), we conclude that
\left|\left<u,v\right>\right|=\left|\left|u\right|\right|\left|\left|v\right|\right|
if and only if

\left|\left|u-pv\right|\right|^{2}=0,

which means
u,v
are
\mathbb{O}
-linearly dependent.
∎

5.
Riesz representation theorem

Let
H
be an
\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert space. We consider

H^{*}:=\{f\in{\text{Hom}_{L\mathbb{O}}(H,\mathbb{O})}\mid f\text{ is continuous}\}.

For any
f\in H^{*}
,
we define the right scalar multiplication by

(5.1)

\displaystyle(f\odot r)(x)\coloneqq\ f(x)r-[r,x,f]

and define the norm

(5.2)

\displaystyle\left|\left|f\right|\right|=\sup_{\left|\left|x\right|\right|\leqslant 1}\left|\left|f(x)\right|\right|.

This makes
H^{*}
a Banach right
\mathbb{O}
-module.
Indeed, Theorem
3.10
shows that
H^{*}
is a right
\mathbb{O}
module.
To show it is a normed space, we need to prove

\left|\left|f\odot p\right|\right|=\left|\left|f\right|\right|\ \left|p\right|

for all
f\in H^{*}
and
p\in\mathbb{O}
.
This needs an alternative expression of the right multiplication in (
5.1
) below.

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
f\in H^{*}
and
p\in\mathbb{O}
, we have

(5.3)

{(f\odot p)(x)}=pf(p^{-1}x)p.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume
|p|=1
. This means
p^{-1}=\overline{p}
. By the definition of the second associator in (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle f(\overline{p}x)=\overline{p}f(x)+[\overline{p},x,f].

Multiplying the left and right above by
p
, we obtain

pf(p^{-1}x)p=\Big(p\big(\overline{p}f(x)+[\overline{p},x,f]\big)\Big)p.

We thus can apply the identity

[\overline{p},x,f]=-[p,x,f]

and identity (
3.11
)

p[p,x,f]=[p,x,f]\overline{p}

to conclude

(5.4)

\displaystyle pf(p^{-1}x)p=f(x)p-[p,x,f].

By the definition of the right scalar multiplication in (
3.14
), the right side above is exactly
(f\odot p)(x)
as desired.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

In our typical Example
3.3
, we have
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear function

R_{q}\in\text{Hom}_{L\mathbb{O}}(\mathbb{O},\mathbb{O})
and its
the right multiplication by an octonion
p
is given by

R_{q}\odot p=R_{qp}.

Indeed,

\displaystyle R_{q}\odot p(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{q}(x)p-[p,x,R_{q}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(xq)p-[p,x,q]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(xq)p-[x,q,p]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x(qp)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{qp}(x).

In this setting, identity (
5.3
) becomes

\displaystyle{(R_{q}\odot p)(x)}=pR_{q}(p^{-1}x)p.

That is,

(5.5)

x(qp)=p((p^{-1}x)q)p

for any
p,q,x\in\mathbb{O}
.
This is a variant of the following Moufang identity
[
18
]

(5.6)

\displaystyle(xy)(ax)=x(ya)x.

Consequently,
(
5.3
) is a generalized Moufang identity.

Theorem 5.3
.

H^{*}
is a Banach right
\mathbb{O}
-module.

Proof.

For any
f\in H^{*}
and
p\in\mathbb{O}
, it follows from identity (
5.3
) that

\displaystyle\left|\left|f\odot p\right|\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\left|\left|x\right|\right|\leqslant 1}\left|\left|pf(p^{-1}x)p\right|\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sup_{\left|\left|x\right|\right|\leqslant 1}\left|p\right|\ \left|\left|f\right|\right|\ \|p^{-1}x\|\ \left|p\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle|p|\left|\left|f\right|\right|.

Thus
f\odot p
is a continuous
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear functional, i.e.,
f\odot p\in H^{*}
. In view of Theorem
3.10
, we conclude that
H^{*}
is an
\mathbb{O}
-submodule of the right
\mathbb{O}
-module
\text{Hom}(H,\mathbb{O})
.

Since
p
and
f
are arbitrarily fixed, we can replace
p
by
p^{-1}
and
f
by
f\odot p
to get

\displaystyle\left|\left|(f\odot p)\odot p^{-1}\right|\right|\leqslant|p^{-1}|\left|\left|f\odot p\right|\right|

so that

\displaystyle\left|\left|f\right|\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left|(f\odot p)\odot p^{-1}\right|\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle|p^{-1}|\left|\left|f\odot p\right|\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle|p^{-1}|\ |p|\ \left|\left|f\right|\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left|f\right|\right|.

This implies

\left|\left|f\odot p\right|\right|=|p|\left|\left|f\right|\right|.

Hence
H^{*}
is a normed right
\mathbb{O}
-module with respect to norm (
5.2
).

Finally, we show that
H^{*}
is a Banach right
\mathbb{O}
-module. Let
H^{*_{\mathbb{R}}}
stand for the real dual space of
H
regarded as a real Hilbert space, i.e.,

H^{*_{\mathbb{R}}}=\{f:H\xlongrightarrow{}\mathbb{R}\ |\ \mbox{$f$ is a bounded real linear functional}\}.

It is well-known that
H^{*_{\mathbb{R}}}
is a real Banach space. Therefore, if
f_{n}\in H^{*}
is a Cauchy sequence, then there exists
f\in H^{*_{\mathbb{R}}}
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}=f
. It is easy to check that the maps

[p,x,\cdot]:H^{*}\to\mathbb{O}

and

\text{Re}\,:\mathbb{O}\to\mathbb{O}

are both continuous. Hence

\text{Re}\,[p,x,f]=\text{Re}\,([p,x,\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}])=\text{Re}\,(\lim_{n\to\infty}[p,x,f_{n}])=\lim_{n\to\infty}\text{Re}\,([p,x,f_{n}])=0.

This proves
f\in H^{*}
.
∎

The following lemma shows that
H^{*}
as a real Hilbert space is isometric with
H^{*_{\mathbb{R}}}
via the real part operator.

Lemma 5.4
.

The real part map

\displaystyle\text{Re}\,:H^{*}

\displaystyle\xlongrightarrow{}

\displaystyle H^{*_{\mathbb{R}}},

defined by
(Ref)(x):=f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)
,
is a norm-preserving map. That is,
for any
f\in H^{*}
we have

\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{H^{*}}=\left|\left|f_{\mathbb{R}}\right|\right|_{H^{*_{\mathbb{R}}}}.

Proof.

Let
f\in H^{*}
and
x\in H
with
\left|\left|x\right|\right|\leqslant 1
. Then there exists
p\in\mathbb{O}
with
|p|=1
such that

|f(x)|=pf(x)=\text{Re}\,(pf(x)).

Since
f
is para-linear, it follows that

|f(x)|=\text{Re}\,(pf(x))=\text{Re}\,(f(px))=f_{\mathbb{R}}(px)\leqslant\left|\left|f_{\mathbb{R}}\right|\right|_{H^{*_{\mathbb{R}}}}\left|\left|px\right|\right|\leqslant\left|\left|f_{\mathbb{R}}\right|\right|_{H^{*_{\mathbb{R}}}}.

Thus we obtain
\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{H^{*}}\leqslant\left|\left|f_{\mathbb{R}}\right|\right|_{H^{*_{\mathbb{R}}}}
.

On the other hand, it is easy to see that
\left|\left|f_{\mathbb{R}}\right|\right|_{H^{*_{\mathbb{R}}}}\leqslant\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{H^{*}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

To state the octonionic version of Riesz representation theorem, we need introduce the notion of conjugate
\mathbb{O}
-linearity.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
M_{1}
be a left
\mathbb{O}
-module and
M_{2}
a right
\mathbb{O}
-module.
A map
f\in\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(M_{1},M_{2})
is called
conjugate
\mathbb{O}
-linear
if

f(px)=f(x)\overline{p}

for all
p\in\mathbb{O}
and all
x\in M_{1}
.

Theorem 5.6

(
Riesz representation theorem
)
.

Let
H
be an
\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert space. Then there is an isometric conjugate
\mathbb{O}
-isomorphism

(5.7)

\displaystyle H^{*}\cong{H}.

Proof.

Let
f\in H^{*}
be para-linear. By Theorem
3.4
, it can be written as

f(x)=f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)-\sum_{i=1}^{7}e_{i}f_{\mathbb{R}}(e_{i}x),

where
f:H\to\mathbb{R}
is a real linear functional. Then the classical Riesz representation theorem shows there exists a unique element
z_{f}\in H
such
that

f_{\mathbb{R}}(x)=\left<x,z_{f}\right>_{\mathbb{R}}

and

\left|\left|z_{f}\right|\right|=\left|\left|f_{\mathbb{R}}\right|\right|.

Since both
f
and
\langle\cdot,z_{f}\rangle
are
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear and share the same real part, they must coincide as shown by
Corollary
3.7
,
i.e.,

f(x)=\left<x,z_{f}\right>.

By Lemma
5.4
we have

\left|\left|z_{f}\right|\right|=\left|\left|f\right|\right|.

Recall that
H^{*}
is a Banach right
\mathbb{O}
-module by Theorem
5.3
. Hence, the map

\displaystyle\sigma:H^{*}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle z_{f}

is an
isometry between
H^{*}
and
H
as real Banach spaces.

We next prove that
\sigma
is a conjugate
\mathbb{O}
-homomorphism.
For any
x\in H
and any
r\in\mathbb{O}
, by definition (
3.14
) we have

\displaystyle\left<x,z_{f\odot r}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{(f\odot r)(x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x)r-[r,x,f]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<x,z_{f}\right>\overline{\overline{r}}+[\overline{r},x,z_{f}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<x,\overline{r}z_{f}\right>.

This shows that

\sigma{(f\odot r)}=\overline{r}\sigma(f).

This implies that
\sigma
is conjugate
\mathbb{O}
-linear.

We finally show that
\sigma
is a conjugate
\mathbb{O}
-isomorphism.
We define

\displaystyle\tau:H

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle H^{*}

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle y^{\prime}:=\left<\cdot,y\right>.

We now prove
\tau
is also a conjugate
\mathbb{O}
-homomorphism.
For any
x\in H
and any
r\in\mathbb{O}
, it follows from (
3.14
) and (
4.2
) that

\displaystyle{(y^{\prime}\odot r)(x)}

\displaystyle={y^{\prime}(x)}r-[r,x,y^{\prime}]

\displaystyle=\left<x,y\right>r-[r,x,y]

\displaystyle=\left<x,y\right>\overline{\overline{r}}+[\overline{r},x,y]

\displaystyle=\left<x,\overline{r}y\right>

\displaystyle={(\overline{r}y)^{\prime}(x)}.

This shows that

\tau(y)\odot r=\tau(\overline{r}y),

i.e.,
\tau
is also a conjugate
\mathbb{O}
-homomorphism.

Moreover, it is easy to check that

\sigma\tau=id,\quad\tau\sigma=id.

Hence
\sigma
is a conjugate
\mathbb{O}
-isomorphism.
This finishes the proof.
∎

Associated to a right
\mathbb{O}
-module
M
is an induced left
\mathbb{O}
-module

M^{-}
with multiplication defined by

p\cdot x:=x\overline{p}

for all
x\in M
and all
p\in\mathbb{O}
.
We can thus identify two left modules
H
and
({H^{*}})^{-}
by the isomorphism
\sigma
in Theorem
5.6
. Moreover, this isomorphism induces on
({H^{*}})^{-}
a canonical
\mathbb{O}
-inner product

\left<f,g\right>:={\left<z_{f},z_{g}\right>}

for any
f,g\in(H^{*})^{-}
.
By definition, the octonion hermiticity and positivity can be verified directly.
It remains to show the para-linearity.
By calculation, for any
r\in\mathbb{O}
and
f,g\in H^{*}
we have

\displaystyle\left<r\cdot f,g\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<f\odot\overline{r},g\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<z_{f\odot\overline{r}},z_{g}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<rz_{f},z_{g}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r\left<z_{f},z_{g}\right>+[r,z_{f},z_{g}].

This shows that
\left<\cdot,g\right>
is para-linear as desired. Then
\sigma
is an isomorphism between two Hilbert left
\mathbb{O}
-modules.

Based on Theorem
5.6
, we can characterize the set

H^{*_{\mathbb{O}}}=\{f:H\xlongrightarrow{}\mathbb{O}\ |\ \mbox{$f$ is a bounded $\mathbb{O}$-linear functional}\}

in terms of associative elements of
H
.
To do this, we need a criterion for an element
x\in H
to be an associative element.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert left
\mathbb{O}
-module and
x\in H
. Then
x\in\mathscr{A}(H)
if and only if

(5.8)

[p,x,y]=0

for all
p\in\mathbb{O}
and
y\in H.

Proof.

If
x\in\mathscr{A}(H)
, then Corollary
3.7
shows that

[p,x,y]=[p,x,y^{\prime}]=0,

where
y^{\prime}
is defined as in the proof of Theorem
5.6
.

Conversely, if
x\in H
satisfies hypothesis (
5.8
), then identity (
4.5
) implies that

\left<[q,p,x],y\right>_{\mathbb{R}}=0

for any
p,q\in\mathbb{O}
and
y\in H
. Since
\left(H,\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>_{\mathbb{R}}\right)
is a real Hilbert space, we thus conclude that

[q,p,x]=0

for all
p,q\in\mathbb{O}
, which means that
x\in\mathscr{A}(H)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
H
be an
\mathbb{O}
-Hilbert space. Then there is an isometric isomorphism of real Banach spaces

(5.9)

\displaystyle H^{*_{\mathbb{O}}}\cong\mathscr{A}(H).

Proof.

According to Theorem
5.6
, it suffices to show that

\tau|_{\mathscr{A}(H)}:\mathscr{A}(H)\to H^{*_{\mathbb{O}}}

is surjective.

If
f\in H^{*_{\mathbb{O}}}
, then Theorem
5.6
ensures the existence of an element
z_{f}\in H
such that

f(x)=\left<x,z_{f}\right>.

Obviously,

\tau(z_{f})=f.

It remains to show that
z_{f}\in\mathscr{A}(H)
.
Since
f
is
\mathbb{O}
-linear, it follows from identity (
4.2
) that for any
p\in\mathbb{O}
,

[p,z_{f},x]=-[p,x,z_{f}]=-[p,x,f]=-(f(px)-pf(x))=0.

Then we conclude from Lemma
5.7
that
z_{f}\in\mathscr{A}(H)
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.9
.

Isomorphisms (

) and (

) indicate that the objects in octonionic functional analysis are
\mathbb{O}
-para-linear maps rather than

One-loop corrections to light neutrino masses in gauged U(1) extensions of the standard model
Sho Iwamoto, Timo J. Kärkkäinen, Zoltán Péli, Zoltán Trócsányi

Abstract
We consider gauged U(1) extensions of the standard model of particle physics with three right-handed sterile neutrinos and a singlet scalar. The neutrinos obtain mass via the type I seesaw mechanism. We compute the one loop corrections to the elements of the tree level mass matrix of the light neutrinos and show explicitly the cancellation of the gauge dependent terms. We present a general formula for the gauge independent, finite one-loop corrections for arbitrary number new U(1) groups, new complex scalars and sterile neutrinos. We estimate the size of the corrections relative to the tree level mass matrix in a particular extension, the super-weak model.

Introduction

The standard model (SM) of particle interactions is one of the most successful
physics models with unprecedented precision for predicting physical quantities, for
instance for the anomalous magnetic moment of the electron. However, it does not
contain right handed neutrinos as they are sterile under the SM gauge group.
This in turn leads to the prediction that neutrinos are massless which is in
conflict of the now well established experimental result, that at least two
neutrinos are massive
SK98
;
SNO01
, and therefore, signals that the SM
requires an extension to explain the origin of the neutrino masses. There are
lots of models attempting to explain neutrino masses. Among those perhaps
the most economical one that requires the least extension of the SM, is the
type I seesaw mechanism where neutrinos acquire masses after spontaneous
symmetry breaking (SSB) of one or more scalar fields

Fritzsch75
;
Minkowski:1977sc
;
GellMann:1980vs
;
Yanagida:1979as
;

;

;

;

Recently there has been a lot of interest in gauged U(1)-extended models in
particle physics phenomenology, motivated by the observed difference
between the measured and SM predicted values of the anomalous magnetic
moment of the muon
Abi:2021gix
and also anomalies in short-baseline
neutrino oscillations
Aguilar-Arevalo:2018gpe
. Gauged
B-L
,
B-3L_{\tau}
,

L_{e}-L_{\mu}
and

;
Ma:2001md
;

;
Bhatia:2017tgo
;

as well as a general gauged U(1) not related to flavour
Borah:2020swo

In these models, both seesaw and radiative one-loop neutrino mass generation
mechanisms have been considered.

As the effects of new physics are typically much smaller than those of the SM
interactions, computations in theories beyond the SM are often considered only at
tree level. Yet, the loop corrections may be sizable and can affect significantly
the validity region in the parameter space of the model. For instance, the
lightness of active neutrinos requires that the loop corrections to the mass
matrix of those particles must also be small in order to have a phenomenologically
viable model. Computations of such one-loop corrections have been carried out
previously in Refs.
AristizabalSierra:2011mn
;
LopezPavon:2012zg
for the
canonical seesaw case, and in the context of multi-Higgs doublet models

Grimus:2002nk
;
Grimus:2018rte
;
Dudenas:2018wlr
;
Denner:2016etu
.
In the cases of gauged U(1) models we are not aware of a computation
of the
one-loop corrections
to active neutrino mass matrix.

In this article we consider gauged U(1) extensions of the SM and derive a general
formula for the one-loop corrections of the mass matrix of the active neutrinos.
The mass matrix of the active neutrinos emerges after SSB due to the type I seesaw
mechanism. Our goal is to derive the one-loop corrections to that mass matrix and
estimate their sizes relative to the tree level for a particular example, called
the super-weak force
Trocsanyi:2018bkm
. The super-weak model contains three
additional right-handed sterile (under the SM interactions) neutrinos and one
complex scalar field in addition to the fields of the SM. The loop corrections
involve all the gauge and scalar bosons which couple to neutrinos.

In order to obtain the one-loop corrections to the elements of the light
neutrino mass matrix, we perform our computations in the
R_{\xi}

gauge and show explicitly the intricate cancellation of the gauge fixing
parameters from the corrections. In addition, we shall also demonstrate the
cancellation of the
\epsilon

integrals are regulated by dimensional regularization in
d=4-2\epsilon

dimensions. These cancellations are highly non-trivial, and therefore provide
strong checks on the correctness of the computations.

III
Neutrino mass matrix at one-loop order

We are interested in the one-loop correction
\delta\textbf{M}_{L}
to
the tree-level mass matrix of the light neutrinos.
In perturbation theory we deal with propagating states which are mass
eigenstates. Hence, we can compute loop corrections to self energies
of mass eigenstates of neutrinos. The neutrino mass matrix at one-loop
order is then obtained from Eq. (
II.35
), with diagonal mass matrix
substituted at one loop,
\textbf{M}+\delta\textbf{M}
where

\delta\textbf{M}=\operatorname{diag}(\delta m_{1},\delta m_{2},\delta m_{3},\delta m_{4},\delta m_{5},\delta m_{6})\,.

(III.1)

Hence, the correction is obtained by

\delta\textbf{M}^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}\delta\textbf{M}_{L}&\delta\textbf{M}_{D}^{T}\\
\delta\textbf{M}_{D}&\delta\textbf{M}_{N}\end{pmatrix}=\textbf{U}^{*}\delta\textbf{M}\textbf{U}^{\dagger}\,.

(III.2)

Using Eq. (
II.36
), we can compute the
3\times 3
blocks as

\delta\textbf{M}_{L}=\textbf{U}_{L}^{*}\delta\textbf{M}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger},\quad\delta\textbf{M}_{D}=\textbf{U}_{R}\delta\textbf{M}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger},\quad\delta\textbf{M}_{N}=\textbf{U}_{R}\delta\textbf{M}\textbf{U}_{R}^{T}\,.

(III.3)

In the following subsections we prove that the one-loop correction to the
mass matrix of the active neutrinos have the form

\delta\textbf{M}_{L}=\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\sum_{k=1,2}\left[3(\textbf{Z}_{\rm G})_{k1}^{2}\frac{M^{2}_{V_{k}}}{v^{2}}\textbf{F}(M^{2}_{V_{k}})+(\textbf{Z}_{\rm S})_{k1}^{2}\frac{M^{2}_{S_{k}}}{v^{2}}\textbf{F}(M^{2}_{S_{k}})\right]

(III.4)

where we introduced the finite matrix valued function

\textbf{F}_{ij}(M^{2})=\sum_{a=1}^{6}(\textbf{U}_{L}^{*})_{ia}(\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger})_{aj}\frac{m_{a}^{3}}{M^{2}}\frac{\ln\frac{m_{a}^{2}}{M^{2}}}{\frac{m_{a}^{2}}{M^{2}}-1}

(III.5)

of dimension mass and with summation running over all neutrinos.

Combining Eqs. (

(III.18)

the matrix

\displaystyle\textbf{I}^{(n)}_{ij}(M^{2})=\mathrm{i}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{6}(\textbf{U}_{L}^{*})_{ia}m_{a}^{n}(\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger})_{aj}I_{0}(M^{2},m_{a}^{2};\mu^{2},\epsilon)\,,

and using the relations (

\displaystyle\delta\textbf{M}_{L}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1,2}\left\{\left(\frac{(\textbf{Z}_{\rm G})_{k1}}{v}\right)^{2}\left(dM_{V_{k}}^{2}\textbf{I}^{(1)}(M_{V_{k}}^{2})-\textbf{I}^{(3)}(M_{V_{k}}^{2})\right)+\left(\frac{(\textbf{Z}_{S})_{k1}}{v}\right)^{2}\textbf{I}^{(3)}(M_{S_{k}}^{2})\right\}

(III.20)

with

III.6
Finiteness and scale independence of
\delta\textbf{M}_{L}

We show here that the one loop mass correction
\delta\textbf{M}_{L}
is finite
and independent of the scale
\mu
.
Evaluating the integral (
III.18
) yields

\displaystyle I_{0}(M^{2},m^{2};\mu^{2},\epsilon)

\displaystyle=I^{(\mathrm{s})}_{0}(\epsilon)+I^{(\mathrm{f})}_{0}\left(\frac{m^{2}}{M^{2}},\frac{\mu^{2}}{M^{2}}\right)+\mathrm{O}(\epsilon)

(III.21)

where ‘s’ stands for the singular and ‘f’ for the finite functions

\displaystyle I^{(\mathrm{s})}_{0}(\epsilon)=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{16\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}-\gamma_{\mathrm{E}}+\ln 4\pi+1\right)

\displaystyle,\quad I^{(\mathrm{f})}_{0}(x,x_{\mu})=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{16\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{x\ln x}{1-x}+\ln x_{\mu}\right).

(III.22)

with
\gamma_{\mathrm{E}}\simeq 0.5722
being the Euler-Mascheroni constant. It is also
convenient to split the matrix (
III.19
) in a similar fashion

\textbf{I}^{(n)}(M^{2})=\textbf{I}^{(\mathrm{s},n)}+\textbf{I}^{(\mathrm{f},n)}(M^{2})

(III.23)

such that

\textbf{I}^{(\mathrm{s},n)}=\mathrm{i}\textbf{U}_{L}^{*}\textbf{M}^{n}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}I^{(\mathrm{s},n)}_{0}\,,\quad\Big(\textbf{I}^{(\mathrm{f},n)}(M^{2})\Big)_{ij}=\mathrm{i}\sum_{a=1}^{6}(\textbf{U}_{L}^{*})_{ia}m_{a}^{n}(\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger})_{aj}I^{(\mathrm{f},n)}_{0}\left(\frac{m_{a}^{2}}{M^{2}},\frac{\mu^{2}}{M^{2}}\right).

(III.24)

Then the one-loop correction to the mass matrix of the light neutrinos can
also be decomposed as

\delta\textbf{M}_{L}=\delta\textbf{M}^{(\mathrm{s})}_{L}+\delta\textbf{M}^{(\mathrm{f})}_{L}+\mathrm{O}(\epsilon)

(III.25)

where

\displaystyle\delta\textbf{M}^{(\mathrm{s})}_{L}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1,2}\left[dM_{V_{k}}^{2}\left(\frac{(\textbf{Z}_{\rm G})_{k1}}{v}\right)^{2}\textbf{I}^{(\mathrm{s},1)}+\frac{(\textbf{Z}_{S})^{2}_{k1}-(\textbf{Z}_{\rm G})^{2}_{k1}}{v^{2}}\textbf{I}^{(\mathrm{s},3)}\right]

(III.26)

and

\displaystyle\delta\textbf{M}^{(\mathrm{f})}_{L}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=1,2}\left[\left(\frac{(\textbf{Z}_{\rm G})_{k1}}{v}\right)^{2}\left(dM_{V_{k}}^{2}\textbf{I}^{(\mathrm{f},1)}(M_{V_{k}}^{2})-\textbf{I}^{(\mathrm{f},3)}(M_{V_{k}}^{2})\right)+\left(\frac{(\textbf{Z}_{S})_{k1}}{v}\right)^{2}\textbf{I}^{(\mathrm{f},3)}(M_{S_{k}}^{2})\right].

(III.27)

In order to prove that
\delta\textbf{M}_{L}
is finite, one has to show that

\delta\textbf{M}^{(\mathrm{s})}_{L}
is free from
\epsilon
poles. We prove that
it in fact vanishes because the matrix
\textbf{I}^{(\mathrm{s},1)}
is zero matrix
due to the identity (
A.5
), while the coefficient in the second term
cancels because the matrices
Z
S
and
Z
G
are orthogonal, so

\sum_{k=1}^{2}(\textbf{Z}_{S})_{k1}^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{2}(\textbf{Z}_{G})_{k1}^{2}=0\,.

(III.28)

Hence the mass independent terms, including the divergent pieces of the light
neutrino one-loop mass correction cancel, and we can set
\epsilon=0
, which
yields
\delta\textbf{M}_{L}=\delta\textbf{M}^{(\mathrm{f})}_{L}
.
Furthermore, the terms depending on the regularization scale in

\delta\textbf{M}^{(\mathrm{f})}_{L}
cancel in an identical way as the second term does
in
\delta\textbf{M}^{(\mathrm{s})}_{L}
(using Eq. (
III.28
)).

The remaining finite terms give the final, regularization-scale independent and finite one-loop correction to the light neutrinos as given in Eq. (
III.4
).
It is the linear combination of the matrix valued function
F

given in Eq. (
III.5
) with different arguments and
coefficients corresponding to the different one-loop contributions.
The function
F
gives the mass correction corresponding to a one
loop diagram, before coupling suppression; see Fig.
4
in
Sect.
IV
for details where we shall also give a numerical estimate
for its eigenvalues
\delta m^{\nu}_{i,0}
. It is well defined for any non-negative

x
because

\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow 0}\frac{x\ln x}{1-x}=0\text{~~and~~}\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow 1}\frac{x\ln x}{1-x}=-1\,.

(III.29)

III.7

Our predictions for the one-loop correction to the light neutrino mass matrix

massive neutral gauge bosons,

neutral real scalars coupling to

V
Conclusions

In this paper, we have computed the one-loop corrections to the mass matrix of
the active neutrinos in a gauged U(1) extension of the standard model of particle
interactions. The field content of the model consists of a new complex scalar field
and three right-handed neutrinos—sterile under the standard model
interactions—in addition to the fields in the standard model. The neutrino
masses are generated by Dirac and Majorana type Yukawa terms, which after
spontaneous symmetry breaking of both scalar fields give rise to neutrino masses
in the way of the type I see-saw mass generation. We used
R_{\xi}
gauge and have
shown that the one-loop corrections are (i) independent of the gauge fixing
parameters, (ii) finite and (iii) independent of the regularization scale.
We also demonstrated how the formula for the one-loop mass corrections can be
generalized to the case of arbitrary number of new U(1) groups, complex
scalars and right-handed neutrinos.

We have provided a numerical estimate of the size of the mass corrections in
the context of the super-weak model, in which the new neutral gauge boson

Z^{\prime}
is much lighter than the
Z
boson of the standard model. We have
found that in the mass range of
M_{Z^{\prime}}\in[20,200]
MeV, motivated by
a possible explanation of the relic density of dark matter in the Universe,
the relative mass corrections to the tree-level mass matrix elements do not
exceed the per mill level. Hence the model is stable against higher-order
corrections in the neutrino sector, which motivates further studies to
explore the viable parameter space of the model regarding the mixings
between the active and sterile neutrinos
Karkkainen:2021
.

Appendix A
Some properties of neutrino mass and mixing matrices

In this appendix, we derive some useful relations among the neutrino
mass and mixing matrices. The matrix
U
that diagonalizes the
neutrino mass matrix is unitary, hence

\textbf{UU}^{\dagger}=\binom{\textbf{U}_{L}}{\textbf{U}_{R}^{*}}(\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger},\textbf{U}_{R}^{T})=\begin{pmatrix}\textbf{U}_{L}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}&\textbf{U}_{L}\textbf{U}_{R}^{T}\\
\textbf{U}_{R}^{*}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}&\textbf{U}_{R}^{*}\textbf{U}_{R}^{T}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}{\mbox{\boldmath$1$}}_{3}&\textbf{0}_{3}\\
\textbf{0}_{3}&{\mbox{\boldmath$1$}}_{3}\end{pmatrix},

(A.1)

from which we obtain the following important relations:

\textbf{U}_{L}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}={\mbox{\boldmath$1$}}_{3}\,,\quad\textbf{U}_{R}\textbf{U}_{R}^{\dagger}={\mbox{\boldmath$1$}}_{3}

(A.2)

and

\textbf{U}_{L}\textbf{U}_{R}^{T}=\textbf{U}_{R}^{*}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}=\textbf{0}_{3}

(A.3)

where
{\mbox{\boldmath$1$}}_{n}
denotes the
n\times n
unit matrix.
The second unitarity conditions gives

\textbf{U}^{\dagger}\textbf{U}=(\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger},\textbf{U}_{R}^{T})\binom{\textbf{U}_{L}}{\textbf{U}_{R}^{*}}=\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}\textbf{U}_{L}+\textbf{U}_{R}^{T}\textbf{U}_{R}^{*}={\mbox{\boldmath$1$}}_{6}\,.

(A.4)

Using Eq. (
II.35
) we derive

\displaystyle\textbf{U}_{L}^{*}\textbf{M}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\textbf{U}_{L}^{*}(\textbf{U}_{L}^{T},\textbf{U}_{R}^{\dagger})\begin{pmatrix}\textbf{0}_{3}&\textbf{M}_{D}^{T}\\
\textbf{M}_{D}&\textbf{M}_{N}\end{pmatrix}\binom{\textbf{U}_{L}}{\textbf{U}_{R}^{*}}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\textbf{U}_{L}^{*}\textbf{U}_{L}^{T}\textbf{M}_{D}\textbf{U}_{R}^{*}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}+\textbf{U}_{L}^{*}\textbf{U}_{R}^{\dagger}\textbf{M}_{D}\textbf{U}_{L}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}+\textbf{U}_{L}^{T}\textbf{U}_{R}^{\dagger}\textbf{M}_{N}\textbf{U}_{R}^{*}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}\,,

and then with relations in Eq. (
A.3
) we obtain

\textbf{U}_{L}^{*}\textbf{M}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}={\mbox{\boldmath$0$}}_{3}\,.

(A.5)

Analogous calculations yield

\textbf{U}_{R}\textbf{M}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}=\textbf{M}_{D}\,.

(A.6)

Multiplying Eq. (
A.6
) with
\textbf{U}_{R}^{\dagger}
from the
left and using Eq. (
A.4
), we find

\textbf{U}_{R}^{\dagger}\textbf{M}_{D}=\textbf{U}_{R}^{\dagger}\textbf{U}_{R}\textbf{M}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}=({\mbox{\boldmath$1$}}_{6}-\textbf{U}_{L}^{T}\textbf{U}_{L}^{*})\textbf{M}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}=\textbf{M}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}-\textbf{U}_{L}^{T}\textbf{U}_{L}^{*}\textbf{M}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}

(A.7)

where the last term vanishes by Eq. (
A.5
), so

\textbf{U}_{R}^{\dagger}\textbf{M}_{D}=\textbf{M}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}\,.

(A.8)

Finally,

\displaystyle\textbf{U}_{R}\textbf{M}\textbf{U}_{R}^{T}

\displaystyle=\textbf{U}_{R}(\textbf{U}_{L}^{T},\textbf{U}_{R}^{\dagger})\begin{pmatrix}\textbf{0}&\textbf{M}_{D}^{T}\\
\textbf{M}_{D}&\textbf{M}_{N}\end{pmatrix}\binom{\textbf{U}_{L}}{\textbf{U}_{R}^{*}}\textbf{U}_{R}^{T}

\displaystyle=\textbf{U}_{R}\Big(\textbf{U}_{L}^{T}\textbf{M}_{D}^{T}\textbf{U}_{R}^{*}+\textbf{U}_{R}^{\dagger}\textbf{M}_{D}\textbf{U}_{L}+\textbf{U}_{R}^{\dagger}\textbf{M}_{N}\textbf{U}_{R}^{*}\Big)\textbf{U}_{R}^{T}

Expanding the factors into the parenthesis, the first two terms give
vanishing contribution by Eq. (
A.4
), while utilizing
Eq. (
A.2
), the last one is simply
\textbf{M}_{N}
, so

\textbf{U}_{R}\textbf{M}\textbf{U}_{R}^{T}=\textbf{M}_{N}\,.

(A.9)

Now Eq. (
A.4
) allows us to derive

\textbf{U}_{R}^{\dagger}\textbf{M}_{N}=\textbf{U}_{R}^{\dagger}\textbf{U}_{R}\textbf{M}\textbf{U}_{R}^{T}=({\mbox{\boldmath$1$}}_{6}-\textbf{U}_{L}^{T}\textbf{U}_{L}^{*})\textbf{M}\textbf{U}_{R}^{T}=\textbf{M}\textbf{U}_{R}^{T}-\textbf{U}_{L}^{T}\textbf{U}_{L}^{*}\textbf{M}\textbf{U}_{R}^{T}\,.

(A.10)

where the second term on the right does not vanish this time.

Appendix B
Evaluation of the vector boson exchange diagram

The vector boson exchange diagrams, shown in the bottom row of Fig.
1
contribute
gauge-dependent terms to the neutrino self energy. In order to show that the
gauge-dependent terms cancel once contributions from all particles are considered,
it is useful to eliminate the loop momentum from the one loop integral
corresponding to the vector boson exchange diagram. A decomposition to achieve
this was used in Ref.
Loschner:2018idt
, and we shall derive it here as well.
In Ref.
Weinberg:1973ua
, Eq (4.4) contains the self energy:

\mathrm{i}\Sigma_{V}(p)=-\int\frac{d^{d}\ell}{(2\pi)^{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{\mu\dagger}\textbf{P}(p-\ell){\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{\nu}P_{\mu\nu}(\ell,M_{V}^{2};\xi_{V}),

(B.1)

where the
6\times 6
matrices are defined as follows. The matrix
P

is the fermion propagator, diagonal in the mass eigenstates,

\textbf{P}(p-\ell)=[(\cancel{p}-\cancel{\ell}){\mbox{\boldmath$1$}}-\textbf{M}]^{-1}\,,\text{~~while~~}{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{\mu}=-\mathrm{i}e\gamma^{\mu}\textbf{A}\,,\text{~~with~~}\textbf{A}={\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{L}P_{L}+{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{R}P_{R}.

(B.2)

In the following we shall write
P
for
\textbf{P}(p-\ell)
.
The matrix
A
is self-adjoint,
\textbf{A}^{\dagger}=\textbf{A}
, and so is

{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{L/R}
. We also introduce the abbreviation

\tilde{\textbf{A}}={\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{R}P_{L}+{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{L}P_{R}\,,

(B.3)

which will simplify our calculations.
In order to compute the loop integral easily containing the neutral vector boson
propagator in the neutrino self-energy loop, in this Appendix we perform tensor
reduction of the matrix product

\cancel{\ell}\textbf{A}^{\dagger}\textbf{P}\cancel{\ell}\textbf{A}

(B.4)

such that the numerator factor be at most linear in the loop momentum
\ell
.

When the fermion momentum
p
appears as
\cancel{p}
at the extreme
left or right of the expression, it satisfies the Dirac equation

\cancel{p}{\mbox{\boldmath$1$}}_{6}=\textbf{M}
(both Dirac and Majorana fermions do so),
thus we can replace formally
\cancel{p}
with
M
. Let us first write the identity

\displaystyle\cancel{\ell}\textbf{A}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=(\cancel{\ell}-\cancel{p}+\textbf{M})\textbf{A}^{\dagger}=\textbf{MA}^{\dagger}-(\cancel{p}-\cancel{\ell})\textbf{A}^{\dagger}.

(B.5)

The chiral coupling matrix
A
anticommutes with the Dirac matrices

\gamma_{\mu}
, hence

\displaystyle\cancel{\ell}\textbf{A}^{\dagger}=\textbf{M}\textbf{A}^{\dagger}-\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\Big((\cancel{p}-\cancel{\ell}){\mbox{\boldmath$1$}}-\textbf{M}+\textbf{M}\Big)

\displaystyle=\textbf{M}\textbf{A}^{\dagger}-\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\textbf{P}^{-1}-\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\textbf{M}\,,

(B.6)

and similarly,

\displaystyle\textbf{P}\cancel{\ell}\textbf{A}=-\textbf{A}-\textbf{P}\textbf{MA}+\textbf{P}\tilde{\textbf{A}}\textbf{M}\,.

(B.7)

Multiplying Eqs. (
B.6
) and (
B.7
),
we obtain the expression (
B.4
), and its expansion
yields

\begin{split}\cancel{\ell}\textbf{A}^{\dagger}\textbf{P}\cancel{\ell}\textbf{A}=&-\textbf{MA}^{\dagger}\textbf{A}-\textbf{MA}^{\dagger}\textbf{P}\textbf{MA}+\textbf{MA}^{\dagger}\textbf{P}\textbf{AM}+\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\textbf{P}^{-1}\textbf{A}\\
&+\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\textbf{MA}-\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\tilde{\textbf{A}}\textbf{M}+\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\textbf{MA}+\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\textbf{M}\textbf{P}\textbf{MA}-\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\textbf{M}\textbf{P}\tilde{\textbf{A}}\textbf{M}\,.\end{split}

(B.8)

Using that
\cancel{p}\textbf{A}=\tilde{\textbf{A}}\cancel{p}
,
the fourth term can rearranged as

\begin{split}\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\textbf{P}^{-1}\textbf{A}&=\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\Big((\cancel{p}-\cancel{\ell})\textbf{1}-\textbf{M}\Big)\textbf{A}=\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\cancel{p}\textbf{A}+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\cancel{p}\textbf{A}-\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\cancel{\ell}\textbf{A}-\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\textbf{MA}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\cancel{p}\textbf{A}^{\dagger}\textbf{A}+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\tilde{\textbf{A}}\cancel{p}-\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\cancel{\ell}\textbf{A}-\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\textbf{MA}\end{split}

(B.9)

The
\cancel{p}
is on extreme left and right, hence can be replaced with
M
, giving

\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\textbf{P}^{-1}\textbf{A}=\frac{1}{2}\textbf{M}\textbf{A}^{\dagger}\textbf{A}+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\tilde{\textbf{A}}\textbf{M}-\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\cancel{\ell}\textbf{A}-\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\textbf{MA}\,.

(B.10)

Substituting Eq. (
B.10
) into Eq. (
B.8
), we obtain

\cancel{\ell}\textbf{A}^{\dagger}\textbf{P}\cancel{\ell}\textbf{A}=\textbf{M}_{0}+\textbf{M}_{1}+\textbf{M}_{2}

(B.11)

where we introduced the abbreviations

\begin{split}\textbf{M}_{0}&=-\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\cancel{\ell}\textbf{A}\,,\\
\textbf{M}_{1}&=-\frac{1}{2}\textbf{M}\textbf{A}^{\dagger}\textbf{A}-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\tilde{\textbf{A}}\textbf{M}+\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\textbf{MA}\end{split}

(B.12)

and

\begin{split}\textbf{M}_{2}&=-\textbf{MA}^{\dagger}\textbf{P}\textbf{MA}+\textbf{MA}^{\dagger}\textbf{P}\tilde{\textbf{A}}\textbf{M}+\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\textbf{M}\textbf{P}\textbf{MA}-\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\textbf{M}\textbf{P}\tilde{\textbf{A}}\textbf{M}\\
&=(\textbf{MA}^{\dagger}-\tilde{\textbf{A}}^{\dagger}\textbf{M})\textbf{P}(\tilde{\textbf{A}}\textbf{M}-\textbf{MA})\,,\end{split}

(B.13)

which correspond to constant, linear and quadratic terms in the neutrino mass
matrix
M
. We now discuss the contribution from each term in
Eq. (
B.11
) separately.

The first, constant term gives vanishing contribution to the loop integral
as it is odd in the loop momentum. The other two terms can be decomposed
into left and right chiral pieces:

\textbf{M}_{i}=\textbf{M}_{i}^{L}P_{L}+\textbf{M}_{i}^{R}P_{R}\,.

(B.14)

Our goal is to compute the one-loop correction (
III.7
) to the
tree-level mass matrix of the light neutrinos. In order to obtain it, one
sandwiches the left handed pieces
\textbf{M}_{i}^{L}
between the matrices
\textbf{U}_{L}^{*}
and
\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}
. Using the properties of
the neutrino mixing matrices of Appendix
A
, we immediately see that

\textbf{U}_{L}^{*}\textbf{M}_{1}^{L}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}=0\,,

(B.15)

while lengthy computations yield

\begin{split}\textbf{U}_{L}^{*}\textbf{M}_{2}^{L}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}=&-(C^{L}_{V\nu\nu}-C^{R}_{V\nu\nu})^{2}\textbf{U}_{L}^{*}\textbf{M}\textbf{P}\textbf{MU}_{L}^{\dagger}\\
&+\text{terms that do not contribute to {\bf B}}_{L}(0)\,.\end{split}

(B.16)

Here we outline the steps needed to reach Eq. (
B.16
).

Firstly, in order to find the left-chiral part
\textbf{M}_{2}^{L}
, we substitute

A
and
\tilde{\textbf{A}}
into Eq. (
B.13
). We write
the denominator of the fermion propagator as

(\textbf{P})_{ij}=\delta_{ij}\frac{\not{p}-\not{\ell}+m_{i}}{(p-\ell)^{2}-m_{i}^{2}}

(B.17)

and use the following relations for the Dirac projectors:

P_{L/R}(\not{q}+m)P_{L/R}=mP_{L/R}\,,\quad P_{L/R}(\not{q}+m)P_{R/L}=\not{q}P_{R/L}\,,

(B.18)

valid for any momentum
q
and mass
m
.
Hence

\begin{split}P_{L/R}(\textbf{P})_{ij}P_{L/R}&=\left((\textbf{P})_{ij}-\delta_{ij}\frac{\not{p}-\not{\ell}}{(p-\ell)^{2}-m_{i}^{2}}\right)P_{L/R}\,,\\
P_{L/R}(\textbf{P})_{ij}P_{R/L}&=\delta_{ij}\frac{\not{p}-\not{\ell}}{(p-\ell)^{2}-m_{i}^{2}}P_{R/L}\,,\end{split}

(B.19)

and therefore, we obtain

\textbf{M}_{2}^{L}=-\textbf{M}{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{L\dagger}\textbf{P}\textbf{M}{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{L}+\textbf{M}{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{L\dagger}\textbf{P}{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{R}\textbf{M}+{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{R\dagger}\textbf{M}\textbf{P}\textbf{M}{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{L}-{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{R\dagger}\textbf{M}\textbf{P}{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{R}\textbf{M}+\textbf{D}

(B.20)

where the last term is proportional to
(\not{p}-\not{\ell})
:

\textbf{D}=[\textbf{M}({\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{L\dagger}-{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{R\dagger})-({\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{L\dagger}-{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{R\dagger})\textbf{M})](\not{p}-\not{\ell})[(p-l)^{2}{\mbox{\boldmath$1$}}_{6}-\textbf{M}^{2}]^{-1}({\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{R}\textbf{M}-\textbf{M}{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{L})\,.

Then using the matrix relations derived in Appendix A, we can compute
the following identities:

\displaystyle\textbf{U}_{L}^{*}\textbf{M}{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{L\dagger}

\displaystyle=-C_{V\nu\nu}^{R}\textbf{U}_{L}^{*}\textbf{M}\,,\quad\textbf{U}_{L}^{*}{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{R\,\dagger}=-C^{L}_{V\nu\nu}\textbf{U}_{L}^{*}\,,

(B.21)

\displaystyle{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{R}\textbf{MU}_{L}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=C^{R}_{V\nu\nu}\textbf{MU}_{L}^{\dagger}\,,\qquad\;{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{L}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}=C^{L}_{V\nu\nu}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}\,.

(B.22)

Finally sandwiching Eq. (
B.20
) gives us

\begin{split}\textbf{U}_{L}^{*}\textbf{M}_{2}^{L}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}&=-(C^{L}_{V\nu\nu}-C^{R}_{V\nu\nu})^{2}\textbf{U}_{L}^{*}\textbf{M}\textbf{P}\textbf{MU}_{L}^{\dagger}+\textbf{U}_{L}^{*}\textbf{D}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}\,.\end{split}

(B.23)

As mentioned, the last term is proportional to
(\not{p}-\not{\ell})
,
but only the term with
\not{\ell}
contributes to
\textbf{B}_{L}(p=0)
.
That piece, being an odd function of
\ell
, vanishes upon integration,
which completes the proof of Eq. (
B.16
).

The charged vector bosons
W^{\pm}
also contribute to the neutrino self-energy.
The corresponding Feynman rules are

{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{\mu}_{W^{-}\bar{\ell}\nu}=-\mathrm{i}e\gamma^{\mu}{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{L}_{W^{-}\bar{\ell}\nu}P_{L},\quad{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{\mu}_{W^{+}\nu\ell}=-\mathrm{i}e\gamma^{\mu}{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{L}_{W^{+}\nu\ell}P_{L}.

(B.24)

where

{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{L}_{W^{-}\bar{\ell}\nu}=C_{W\ell\nu}(\textbf{U}_{L}^{\ell}\textbf{U}_{L})_{ij}

(B.25)

with
\textbf{U}_{L}^{\ell}
being the charged lepton mixing matrix and

{\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{L}_{W^{-}\bar{\ell}\nu}=({\mbox{\boldmath$\Gamma$}}^{L}_{W^{+}\nu\ell})^{\dagger}
.
The charged vector boson contribution to (
III.7
) is proportional
to
\textbf{U}_{L}^{*}\textbf{M}\textbf{U}_{L}^{\dagger}
, which vanishes identically
as shown in Appendix
A
.

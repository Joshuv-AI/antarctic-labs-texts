From bordisms of three-manifolds to domain walls between topological orders
Yu Leon Liu, Dalton A R Sakthivadivel

Abstract
. We study a correspondence between spin three-manifolds and bosonic abelian topological orders. Let N N be a spin three-manifold. We can define a ( 2 + 1 ) (2+1) -dimensional topological order TO N \mathrm{TO}_{N} as follows: its anyons are the torsion elements in H 1 ​ ( N ) H_{1}(N) , the braiding of anyons is given by the linking form, and their topological spins are given by the quadratic refinement of the linking form obtained from the spin structure. Under this correspondence, a surgery presentation of N N gives rise to a classical Chern–Simons description of the associated topological order TO N \mathrm{TO}_{N} . We then extend the correspondence to spin bordisms between three-manifolds, and domain walls between topological orders. In particular, we construct a domain wall 𝒟 M \mathcal{D}_{M} between TO N \mathrm{TO}_{N} and TO N ′ \mathrm{TO}_{N^{\prime}} , where M M is a spin bordism from N N to N ′ N^{\prime} . This domain wall unfolds to a composition of a gapped boundary, obtained from anyon condensation, and a gapless Narain boundary CFT.

4.
Kirby diagrams and toroidal Chern–Simons theories

In this section we relate Kirby diagrams, which are the standard blueprints for constructing three-manifolds, to toroidal Chern–Simons theories, which we view as classical descriptions of abelian topological orders.

4.1.
Kirby diagrams

A well-known result of Lickorish
[
Lic62
]

[

4.2.
Toroidal Chern–Simons theory

We claim that the physical counterpart of surgery presentations of three-manifolds are toroidal Chern–Simons theories. Here we give a concise introduction to toroidal Chern–Simons theories, and refer the reader to
[

]
for more detail.

A toroidal Chern–Simons theory is given by a lattice

together with a non-degenerate even integral symmetric bilinear pairing

16

16

When

[

]
.
Recall that such a form is non-degenerate if

Here

in
\Lambda^{*}

6.1.
Anyon condensation and gapped boundaries

Let us quickly review the theory of anyon condensations.
23
23

23

We will focus on the abelian case, where the theory is significantly simpler. We refer the reader to
[

]
for the theory of anyon condensation for nonabelian topological orders.

We begin by condensing a single boson. Take a topological order
(D,q)
. Recall that an anyon

is a

if
q(a)=0
. This also implies that

. When we condense

becomes identified with the vacuum groundstate. Furthermore, the anyons which braid non-trivially with

get screened out, leaving behind only those anyons

Algebraically the condensed phase is described by the topological order

for

representing a class in the quotient

is a well-defined quadratic form when

There is a gapped domain wall
\mathcal{D}
between the original phase
(D,q)
and the condensed phase
(\mathrm{Ann}_{a}(D)/a,q^{\prime})
, whose anyon content consists of
D/a
.
24
24

24

Note that there is no braiding between anyons on the domain wall.
When we bring an anyon

to the boundary, it is sent to the equivalence class

a
can enter the bulk of the condensed phase.

More generally, we can simultaneously condense many anyons at once: let
\mathcal{A}
be a subgroup of
D
. We say that
\mathcal{A}
consists of mutual bosons if
q(a)=0
for all

. This implies anyons in

have trivial mutual braidings by (

The condensed phase is the topological order given by

. Once again

descends from

. Note that

When

is a

of

\mathcal{A}
is Lagrangian the condensed phase is trivial as

and the trivial phase is in fact a gapped boundary of

.
The twisted partition function for the boundary

We conclude this section by noting that the construction
M\mapsto\mathcal{D}_{M}
is
not
functorial. The reader can readily verify this by considering the composition of the bordism

The fundamental reason for this is because we only consider elements in

In a forthcoming paper we provide a functorial construction in the setting of twisted supersymmetric theories.

8.
Outlook

In this paper we investigated the correspondence between spin three-manifolds and bosonic abelian topological orders.
Interestingly, it seems like there is a similar correspondence between
\textrm{spin}^{c}
three-manifolds and
fermionic
topological orders. The starting point of this correspondence is the observation that a
\textrm{spin}^{c}
structure
\sigma^{c}
on a three-manifold
N
induces a quadratic function
q^{c}
on
H_{1}(N)_{\mathrm{tor}}

[
DM05
]
, generalizing the quadratic form associated to a spin structure. On the other hand, the pair
(H_{1}(N)_{\mathrm{tor}},q^{c})
defines a fermionic topological order
[
BM05
]
. We expect much of the correspondence generalizes to the
\textrm{spin}^{c}
case. For example, every Kirby diagram should give a fermionic toroidal Chern–Simons theory, and every
\textrm{spin}^{c}
bordism should give a domain wall between the fermionic topological orders.

This correspondence between spin three-manifolds and three-dimensional topological orders also fits nicely into the paradigm of 3D-3D correspondences

[
DGG14
,
GGP16
,
PY16
,
GPV17
]
. In particular, the topological orders
\mathrm{TO}_{N}
should be the compactification of the self-dual 6D theory
Z^{6d}

[
Ver95
]
on the three-manifold
N
.
This would naturally explain why bordisms give rise to domain walls, for instance.
We look forward to extending our explicit descriptions to the non-abelian case, and relating our results to the literature on 3D-3D correspondences, in particular the aspects relating more to topological order
[
CQW23
,
BSNW24
]
.

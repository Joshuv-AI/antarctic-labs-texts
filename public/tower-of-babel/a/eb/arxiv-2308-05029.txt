Dihedral long root A-packets of p p -adic G 2 G_{2} via theta correspondence
Raúl Alonso, Qiao He, Mishty Ray, Martí Roset

Abstract
. We construct local Arthur packets associated with a dihedral long root A A -parameter of a split reductive group of type G 2 G_{2} over a nonarchimedian local field of characteristic zero. The construction relies on an exceptional correspondence for the pair ( PU 3 ⋊ ℤ / 2 ​ ℤ , G 2 ) (\mathrm{PU}_{3}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},G_{2}) .

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a connected reductive linear algebraic group over a number field
F
. In
[
Art89
,
Art90
]
, J. Arthur has given a conjectural description of the constituents of the square integrable automorphic representations
\mathcal{A}_{2}(G)
of
G
. The conjecture predicts that there is a decomposition

\mathcal{A}_{2}(G)=\bigoplus_{\psi}\mathcal{A}_{2,\psi}

where each
\mathcal{A}_{2,\psi}
is (to a first approximation) a near equivalence class of representations and the sum runs over equivalence classes of discrete A-parameters
\psi
. A-parameters are admissible maps

\psi:L_{F}\times{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to\prescript{L}{}{G},

where
L_{F}
denotes the conjectural Langlands group of
F
and
\prescript{L}{}{G}=\hat{G}(\mathbb{C})\rtimes W_{F}
denotes the L-group of
G
. Here
\hat{G}(\mathbb{C})
is the complex dual group of
G
and
W_{F}
is the Weil group of
F
. We say that
\psi
is discrete if the component group
S_{\psi}
, defined as the centralizer of the image of
\psi
in
\hat{G}(\mathbb{C})
modulo the center of
\hat{G}(\mathbb{C})
, is finite. Fix such an A-parameter
\psi
. Arthur’s conjecture gives a more precise description of the constituents of
\mathcal{A}_{2,\psi}
. It first describes the local components of the representations appearing in
\mathcal{A}_{2,\psi}
, via the so-called
local A-packets
, and then determines which combinations of such local representations appear globally, using the
global A-packets
and the
multiplicity formula
.

Let
v
be a place of
F
, let
F_{v}
be the completion of
F
at
v
and denote by
L_{F_{v}}
the group
W_{F_{v}}\times\mathrm{SU}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, where
W_{F_{v}}
is the Weil group of
F_{v}
. We can pre-compose
\psi
with a fixed embedding
L_{F_{v}}\xhookrightarrow{}L_{F}
to obtain the local A-parameter
\psi_{v}:L_{F_{v}}\times{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to\prescript{L}{}{G}
. Define the local component group of
\psi_{v}
as

S_{\psi_{v}}=\pi_{0}\left(Z_{\hat{G}(\mathbb{C})}(\mathrm{Im}(\psi_{v}))Z(\hat{G}(\mathbb{C}))/Z(\hat{G}(\mathbb{C}))\right),

where
Z_{\hat{G}(\mathbb{C})}(\mathrm{Im}(\psi_{v}))
denotes the centralizer in
\hat{G}(\mathbb{C})\subset\prescript{L}{}{G}
of the image of
\psi_{v}
,
Z(\hat{G}(\mathbb{C}))
denotes the center of
\hat{G}(\mathbb{C})
and
\pi_{0}
denotes the group of connected components. Arthur predicted that to each irreducible representation
\eta_{v}
of
S_{\psi_{v}}
, we can attach a unitarizable finite length (possibly reducible, possibly zero) representation
\pi_{\eta_{v}}
of
G(F_{v})
. The collection

A_{\psi_{v}}=\left\{\pi_{\eta_{v}}\mid\eta_{v}\in\mathrm{Irr}(S_{\psi_{v}})\right\}

is the local A-packet associated to
\psi_{v}
. There are several requirements on the representations in
A_{\psi_{v}}
. One of them is that if we let
1_{v}
denote the trivial representation of
S_{\psi_{v}}
, then for all but finitely many
v
,
\pi_{1_{v}}
is the unramified representation with Satake parameter

s_{\psi_{v}}=\psi_{v}\left(\Phi_{v}\times\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}q_{v}^{-1/2}&0\\
0&q_{v}^{1/2}\end{array}\right)\right).

Here
\Phi_{v}
denotes a geometric Frobenius element at
v
and
q_{v}
is the size of the residue field at
v
. Given the local A-packets, we define the global A-packet associated to
\psi
as

A_{\psi}=\left\{\pi=\otimes_{v}^{\prime}\pi_{\eta_{v}}\mid\pi_{\eta_{v}}\in A_{\psi_{v}}\text{ for all }v\text{ and }\eta_{v}=1_{v}\text{ for all but finitely many }v\right\}.

Note that this is a set of nearly equivalent representations of
G(\mathbb{A}_{F})
, which is indexed by irreducible representations of
S_{\psi,\mathbb{A}_{F}}=\prod_{v}S_{\psi_{v}}
. For a given
\eta=\otimes_{v}\eta_{v}
, where
\eta_{v}\in\mathrm{Irr}(S_{\psi_{v}})
and
\eta_{v}=1_{v}
for all but finitely many
v
, set
\pi_{\eta}=\otimes_{v}^{\prime}\pi_{\eta_{v}}
.
Arthur constructed a quadratic character
\epsilon_{\psi}
of
S_{\psi}
and used it to determine the multiplicity of each representation
\pi_{\eta}\in A_{\psi}
appearing in
\mathcal{A}_{2,\psi}
. This yields the multiplicity formula

\mathcal{A}_{2,\psi}=\bigoplus_{\eta}m_{\eta}\pi_{\eta},\ \text{ where }\ m_{\eta}=\frac{1}{\#S_{\psi}}\left(\sum_{s\in S_{\psi}}\epsilon_{\psi}(s)\eta(s)\right).

It is worth mentioning that the conjecture also predicts when all the representations in
\mathcal{A}_{2,\psi}
are tempered, which should occur when the A-parameter
\psi
is tempered (if
\psi
restricted to
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is trivial). On the other hand, nontempered A-parameters lead to local A-packets that can contain both tempered and nontempered representations. An important feature about nontempered A-parameters is that they usually factor through subgroups
{}^{L}\!H\subset\prescript{L}{}{G}
. It is then expected that we can construct the representations in the (local and global) A-packets associated to
\psi
from representations of
H
.

From now on suppose that
G
is a split exceptional group of type
G_{2}
over
F
. Note that
\prescript{L}{}{G}
can be replaced by
G(\mathbb{C})
and that there are
4
different conjugacy classes of morphisms
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to G(\mathbb{C})
corresponding to the
4
nontrivial unipotent conjugacy classes in
G(\mathbb{C})
. They give rise to
4
families of nontempered A-parameters
\psi
for
G_{2}
: if
\psi_{|{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}
corresponds to the regular orbit, if
\psi_{|{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}
corresponds to the subregular orbit, if
\psi_{|{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}
gives the short root
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
in
G_{2}
and if
\psi_{|{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}
gives the long root
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
in
G_{2}
. For the first three mentioned families of nontempered A-parameters, Arthur’s conjecture has been verified; see
[
GGJ02
]
and
[
GG06
]
. This work is part of a larger project initiated at the 2022 Arizona Winter School which aims to verify Arthur’s conjecture for the so-called
dihedral long root A-parameters
, a type of nontempered A-parameters for
G_{2}
which belong to the fourth mentioned family. In particular, in this paper we construct the local nonarchimedean A-packets associated to dihedral long root A-parameters. We proceed to briefly introduce dihedral long root A-parameters and then we summarize how the corresponding local A-packets are constructed. For that we use a theta lift from
\mathrm{PU}_{3}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
to
G_{2}
arising from the exceptional theta correspondence for
\left(\mathrm{PU}_{3}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right)\times G_{2}
studied in
[
GS04
]
,
[
GS23
]
and especially in
[
BS
]
.

Let
K
be a quadratic extension of
F
and
c
be the nontrivial element in the Galois group
\Gal(K/F)
. Denote by
\chi
a character of
\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}/K^{\times}
which is conjugate symplectic,
i.e.
,
\chi_{|\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}}=\omega_{K/F}
, where
\omega_{K/F}
is the quadratic character attached to the extension
K/F
. Moreover, suppose that
\chi^{c}\neq\chi
. Note that we can regard
\chi
as a character of
W_{K}
. Let
\tau=\otimes_{v}^{\prime}\tau_{v}
be the representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
obtained from
\chi
by automorphic induction, namely
\tau
is the automorphic representation with
L
-parameter

\rho_{\tau}=\Ind_{W_{K}}^{W_{F}}\chi.

Note that the central character of
\tau
is
\omega_{K/F}\cdot\chi_{|\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}}=1
. Therefore, we regard
\rho_{\tau}
as a representation of
{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}
. Denote by
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2,l}
(resp.
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2,s}
) the long (resp. short) root
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
inside
G_{2}
. We define the long root A-parameter of
G
associated with
\tau
as

\psi_{\tau,l}:L_{F}\times{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\twoheadrightarrow{}W_{F}\times{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\xrightarrow{\rho_{\tau}\times\id}{\mathrm{SL}}_{2,s}(\mathbb{C})\times_{\mu_{2}}{\mathrm{SL}}_{2,l}(\mathbb{C})\subset G(\mathbb{C}).

There is a subgroup
{\mathrm{SL}}_{3,l}(\mathbb{C})\subset G(\mathbb{C})
corresponding to the root system of type
A_{2}
formed by the six long roots of
G
. Its normalizer inside
G(\mathbb{C})
is isomorphic to
{\mathrm{SL}}_{3,l}(\mathbb{C})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. This is at the same time isomorphic to the L-group of
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
, the projective unitary group in three variables associated to the extension
K/F
.

N_{G(\mathbb{C})}({\mathrm{SL}}_{3,l}(\mathbb{C}))\simeq{\mathrm{SL}}_{3,l}(\mathbb{C})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}=^{L}\!\mathrm{PU}_{3}.

In Section
4
we verify that
\psi_{\tau,l}
can be conjugated to take values in that normalizer. We can therefore define the restriction

\psi_{\chi}:L_{F}\times{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to^{L}\!\mathrm{PU}_{3}.

As discussed above, the fact that the A-parameter
\psi_{\tau,l}
factors through an A-parameter of
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
suggests that we can obtain the local A-packets for
\psi_{\tau,l}
from the local A-packets for
\psi_{\chi}
. We explain this phenomenon when
v
is a nonarchimedean place of
F
which is nonsplit in
K
, as this is the most interesting case.

Fix a place of
K
above
v
, denote by
K_{v}
the completion of
K
at that place and by
\chi_{v}
the corresponding local component of
\chi
. The local component group of
\psi_{\chi,v}
has two elements. Therefore, the local A-packet of
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
associated to
\psi_{\chi_{v}}
has the form

A_{\psi_{\chi,v}}=\left\{\sigma_{v}^{+},\sigma_{v}^{-}\right\},

where
\sigma_{v}^{+}
(resp.
\sigma_{v}^{-}
) corresponds to the trivial (resp. nontrivial) representation of the local component group. These representations can be obtained as theta lifts using the classical local theta correspondence between the unitary groups
\mathrm{U}_{1}
and
\mathrm{U}_{3}
, as we explain in Section
4
. In particular,
\sigma_{v}^{+}
is nontempered and
\sigma_{v}^{-}
is supercuspidal.

On the other hand, the local component group of
\psi_{\tau,l,v}
has either two elements if
\chi_{v}^{2}\neq 1
or one element otherwise. Therefore, we expect that the local A-packet associated to
\psi_{\tau,l}
has the form

A_{\psi_{\tau,l,v}}=\begin{cases}\{\pi_{v}^{+},\pi_{v}^{-}\}\text{ if }\chi_{v}^{2}\neq 1,\\
\{\pi_{v}^{+}\}\text{ if }\chi_{v}^{2}=1.\end{cases}

Here,
\pi_{v}^{+}
corresponds to the trivial representation of
S_{\psi_{\tau,l,v}}
and, when the local component group has two elements,
\pi_{v}^{-}
is the representation corresponding to the nontrivial representation of
S_{\psi_{\tau,l,v}}
. Denote by
\Theta_{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}
the exceptional big theta lift from
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
to
G
considered in
[
BS
]
and by
\theta_{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}
the corresponding small theta lift. Let
Q_{1}
be the non-Heisenberg parabolic subgroup of
G(F_{v})
, which has a Levi subgroup isomorphic to
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F_{v})
, and denote by
i_{Q_{1}}^{G}
the normalized parabolic induction from
Q_{1}
to
G(F_{v})
. The main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
v
be a non-archimedean place of
F
which is nonsplit in
K
. Let
A_{\psi_{\chi,v}}=\{\sigma_{v}^{+},\sigma_{v}^{-}\}
be the local A-packet associated to the A-parameter
\psi_{\chi}
of
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
, where
\sigma_{v}^{+}
is nontempered and
\sigma_{v}^{-}
is supercuspidal. Define
\pi_{v}^{\pm}=\theta_{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}(\sigma^{\pm})
. Then:

(1)

The representation
\pi_{v}^{+}
is the unique nonzero irreducible quotient of
i_{Q_{1}}^{G}(\lvert\det\rvert_{F_{v}}^{1/2}\tau_{v})
, where
\lvert\ \rvert_{F_{v}}
denotes the normalized absolute value on
F_{v}
.

(2)

If
\chi_{v}^{2}\neq 1
, the representation
\pi_{v}^{-}
is nonzero, irreducible and tempered.

(3)

If
\chi_{v}^{2}=1
, the representation
\pi_{v}^{-}
is zero.

Note that as a consequence of the main theorem, we obtain a natural construction of the elements of the local A-packet
A_{\psi_{\tau,l,v}}
as the nonzero lifts of the elements of
A_{\psi_{\chi},v}
via
\theta_{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}
. Using a similar strategy, we construct the local A-packet
A_{\psi_{\tau,l,v}}
when
v
is a non-archimedean place of
F
that is split in
K
(see Theorem
4.12
).

The first point of the main theorem is a consequence of the work of
[
BS
]
on lifts of nontempered representations. To prove the second point we compute Fourier–Jacobi periods of the minimal representation used to define
\theta_{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}
to obtain the following non-vanishing criterion: if the contragradient of a local representation of
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
, when restricted to a suitable two-variable unitary subgroup, has a quotient with trivial central character, then this representation has nonzero theta lift to
G
. We then verify that
\pi_{v}^{-}
satisfies this condition if
\chi_{v}^{2}\neq 1
using a see-saw argument. In fact, this same reasoning shows that
\pi_{v}^{+}
is nonzero in all cases, giving an alternative proof of the nonvanishing part in the first point of the theorem. The key ingredient to prove the third point of the theorem is that the twisted coinvariant spaces for
\pi_{v}^{-}
corresponding to generic characters of the unipotent of the Heisenberg group of
G(F_{v})
vanish. This is proven using that for every such character we have:

•

The explicit description of
\pi_{v}^{+}
allows to verify that the twisted coinvariant space for
\pi_{v}^{+}
is
1
-dimensional.

•

The twisted coinvariant space for
\pi_{v}^{+}\oplus\pi_{v}^{-}
can be related to a sum of toric periods for
\sigma_{v}^{+}\oplus\sigma_{v}^{-}
. Since the representations
\sigma_{v}^{\pm}
are theta lifts of characters in
\mathrm{U}_{1}
, the non-vanishing of these periods can be expressed in terms of local epsilon factors, as it is done in
[
BFG+
]
. Moreover, in [
loc. cit.
], it is proven that exactly one of these toric periods contributes to the sum with precisely dimension 1.

We expect that a similar construction should yield the local A-packets in the archimedean case, but the theta correspondence in the archimedean case has not been fully analysed yet. We refer the reader to
[
BHLHS
]
, where a definition of the local A-packets in this setting, which can be related to theta lifts in some cases, is proposed.

The
p
-adic construction presented in this article is used in [
loc. cit.
] to construct global dihedral long root A-packets and, under the hypothesis that
L(\chi,1/2)\neq 0
and certain conditions regarding the archimedean construction, verify the multiplicity formula.

We conclude the introduction with notes about the organisation of this paper. In Section
2
, we establish notation for unitary groups and recall essential results about the classical local theta correspondence between
\mathrm{U}_{1}
and
\mathrm{U}_{3}
. In Section
3
, we introduce the exceptional group of type
G_{2}
, the local exceptional theta correspondence between this group and
\mathrm{PU}_{3}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, and review results about this correspondence that we will need later. In Section
4
, we describe the construction of the local A-packets associated to
\psi_{\tau,l}
for all finite primes. In Section
5
, we prove nonvanishing of the representation
\pi_{v}^{-}
in the case
\chi_{v}^{2}\neq 1
. Finally, in Section
6
, we prove the vanishing of
\pi_{v}^{-}
in the case
\chi_{v}^{2}=1
.

2.
Theta correspondence for unitary groups

In this section, we work out the local theta correspondence for the dual pair
(\mathrm{U}_{1},\mathrm{U}_{3})
. We start by recalling some general structure theory for unitary groups in Section
2.1
, followed by a description of the theta correspondence for general unitary groups in Section
2.2
. We then apply this theory to the dual pair
(\mathrm{U}_{1},\mathrm{U}_{3})
in Section
2.3
for nonsplit places, and
(\mathrm{GL}_{1},\mathrm{GL}_{3})
in Section
2.4
for split places.

2.1.
Definitions

Let
F
be a nonarchimedian local field of characteristic zero and let
K
be a quadratic field extension with
\Gal(K/F)=\langle c\rangle
. Let
\omega_{K/F}
be the nontrivial quadratic character of
F^{\times}/N_{K/F}(K^{\times})
. A finite-dimensional Hermitian (resp. skew-Hermitian) space over
K
is a finite dimensional vector space over
K
equipped with a nondegenerate sesquilinear form
\langle\,,\,\rangle
satisfying
\langle v,w\rangle^{c}=\langle w,v\rangle
(resp.
\langle v,w\rangle^{c}=-\langle w,v\rangle
).
We adopt the convention that sesquilinear forms are linear on the first variable and conjugate-linear on the second variable.

For each positive integer
n
, there are two isomorphism classes of Hermitian spaces of dimension
n
over
K
. Given a Hermitian space of dimension
n
over
K
, its isomorphism class is determined by an invariant known as the discriminant, which we now define.

Let
V
be a Hermitian space of dimension
n
over
K
. Let
\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}\}
be a
K
-basis of
V
and let
\Phi=(\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle)
be the matrix of inner products of the basis elements. Then, the discriminant of
V
, which we denote by
\disc(V)
, is defined by

\disc(V)=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\det(\Phi)\in F^{\times}/N_{K/F}(K^{\times}).

The sign character that classifies
V
is given by

(1)

\epsilon(V)=\omega_{K/F}(\disc(V))\in\{\pm 1\}.

If
W
is a skew-Hermitian space of dimension
m
, we can define the discriminant using the same procedure, but now we get

\disc(W)\in\delta^{m}F^{\times}/N_{K/F}(K^{\times}),

where
\delta
denotes any trace-zero element in
K^{\times}
. We can again attach a sign to
W
by setting

(2)

\epsilon(W)=\omega_{K/F}(\delta^{-m}\disc(W))\in\{\pm 1\},

but note that, if
m
is odd, this definition depends on our choice of
\delta
.

For an
n
-dimensional Hermitian or skew-Hermitian space
V
, we denote by
\underline{\mathrm{U}}(V)
the corresponding unitary group. This is an algebraic group over
F
. Choose a
K
-basis of
V
and let
\Phi
be the matrix of the (skew-)Hermitian form on
V
with respect to this basis. Then, for any
F
-algebra
R
, the
R
-points of
\underline{\mathrm{U}}(V)
can be described as

\underline{\mathrm{U}}(V)(R)=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(K\otimes_{F}R)\;\colon\;g\Phi g^{\dagger}=\Phi\}.

We are primarily interested in the group of
F
-points of
\underline{\mathrm{U}}(V)
, which we denote by
\mathrm{U}(V)
.

Observe that a Hermitian form becomes skew-Hermitian after multiplication by a trace-zero element in
K^{\times}
and vice versa, without changing the associated unitary groups. Therefore, from now on we focus on Hermitian spaces.

Let
V
be an
n
-dimensional Hermitian space with Hermitian form
\langle\,,\,\rangle
. Let
a\in F^{\times}
and let
V^{a}
denote the Hermitian space with the same underlying space
V
equipped with the Hermitian form
a\langle\,,\,\rangle
. From the above description of the associated unitary groups, it is clear that
\underline{\mathrm{U}}(V^{a})=\underline{\mathrm{U}}(V)
.

If
n=2m
for a positive integer
m
, we have that
V\simeq V^{a}
from the definition of the discriminant, and it can be proved that non-isomorphic Hermitian spaces yield non-isomorphic unitary groups. When
m=1
and
\epsilon(V)=1
, we can choose a basis
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
so that
V=Ke_{1}\oplus Ke_{2}
and

\langle e_{1},e_{1}\rangle=\langle e_{2},e_{2}\rangle=0\text{ and }\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle=1.

We call this
2
-dimensional space the hyperbolic plane and denote it by
\mathbb{H}
. More generally, when
\epsilon(V)=1
, then
V\simeq\mathbb{H}^{m}
. We say that such a
V
is split and the corresponding unitary group is quasi-split. In this case, we can choose a basis for
V
for which the Hermitian form is given by

\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\cdots&0&1\\
0&0&\cdots&1&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\iddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&1&\cdots&0&0\\
1&0&\cdots&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Once we fix such a basis, the subgroup of upper-triangular matrices in
\underline{\mathrm{U}}(V)
defines a Borel subgroup, which we sometimes refer to as the standard Borel subgroup. Let
B^{\prime}
denote the
F
-points of this Borel subgroup and let
T^{\prime}\subset B^{\prime}
denote the
F
-points of the maximal torus consisting of diagonal matrices. Then
T^{\prime}
consists of the elements of the form

t(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})=\diag\left(a_{m},...,a_{1},(a_{1}^{c})^{-1},...,(a_{m}^{c})^{-1}\right),\quad\text{with }a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in K^{\times},

so we get an identification
T^{\prime}\simeq(K^{\times})^{m}
. If
\epsilon(V)=-1
, then
V
is isomorphic to the orthogonal direct sum of
m-1
hyperbolic planes and an anisotropic two-dimensional Hermitian space. In this case,
V
is nonsplit and the corresponding unitary group is not quasi-split.

If
n=2m+1
, then for an element
a\in F^{\times}
which is not a norm from
K^{\times}
, the Hermitian spaces
V
and
V^{a}
are not isomorphic. It follows that there is only one isomorphism class of unitary groups in
n
-variables, which are always quasi-split. We denote any element in this isomorphism class by
\underline{\mathrm{U}}_{n}
, and its
F
-points by
\mathrm{U}_{n}
. Then
V
is isomorphic to the orthogonal direct sum of
m
hyperbolic planes and a line. We can choose a basis of
V
for which the corresponding Hermitian form is given by

\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\cdots&0&b\\
0&0&\cdots&b&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\iddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&b&\cdots&0&0\\
b&0&\cdots&0&0\end{pmatrix},

with the class of
b
in
F^{\times}/\norm_{K/F}(K^{\times})
determined by the discriminant of
V
. Once we fix such a basis, the subgroup of upper-triangular matrices in
\underline{\mathrm{U}}(V)
defines a Borel subgroup, which we sometimes refer to as the standard Borel subgroup. Let
B^{\prime}
denote the
F
-points of this Borel subgroup and let
T^{\prime}\subset B
denote the
F
-points of the maximal torus consisting of diagonal matrices. Then
T^{\prime}
consists of the elements of the form

t(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})=\diag\left(a_{m},...,a_{1},a_{0},(a_{1}^{c})^{-1},...,(a_{m}^{c})^{-1}\right),\quad\text{with }a_{0}\in K^{1},\,a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in K^{\times},

so we get an identification
T^{\prime}\simeq(K^{\times})^{m}\times K^{1}
.

2.2.
Theta correspondence for unitary groups

Let
F
be a nonarchimedian local field of characteristic zero and let
K/F
be a quadratic field extension. Let
V
be a Hermitian space over
K
of dimension
n
and let
W
be a skew-Hermitian space over
K
of dimension
m
. We can regard
V\otimes_{K}W
as a vector space over
F
equipped with the symplectic form

\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}_{K/F}(\langle\ ,\ \rangle_{V}\otimes_{K}\langle\ ,\ \rangle_{W}).

Let
{\mathrm{Sp}}(V\otimes_{K}W)
be the symplectic group associated with this symplectic space. Then we have a natural map

\iota:\mathrm{U}(V)\times\mathrm{U}(W)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sp}(V\otimes_{K}W),

and
\mathrm{U}(V)
and
\mathrm{U}(W)
form a reductive dual pair inside
{\mathrm{Sp}}(V\otimes_{K}W)
. The aim of this section is to describe the theta correspondence for this pair.

Fix a nontrivial additive character
\psi:F\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Let
{\mathrm{Mp}}(V\otimes_{K}W)
be the metaplectic group associated with the symplectic space
V\otimes_{K}W
, which for us will be an
S^{1}
-cover of the symplectic group
{\mathrm{Sp}}(V\otimes_{K}W)
. Let
\omega_{\psi}
denote the Weil representation of
{\mathrm{Mp}}(V\otimes_{K}W)
corresponding to the character
\psi
.

After fixing two characters
\chi_{V}
,
\chi_{W}
of
K^{\times}
such that

{\chi_{V}}_{|F^{\times}}=\omega_{K/F}^{n}\text{ and }{\chi_{W}}_{|F^{\times}}=\omega_{K/F}^{m},

the work of Kudla
[
Kud94
]
provides a morphism

\tilde{\iota}_{\chi_{V},\chi_{W},\psi}:\mathrm{U}(V)\times\mathrm{U}(W)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Mp}(V\otimes_{K}W)

lifting the natural map
\iota:\mathrm{U}(V)\times\mathrm{U}(W)\to\mathrm{Sp}(V\otimes_{K}W)
. Hence, we can consider the representation of
\mathrm{U}(V)\times\mathrm{U}(W)
obtained as the pullback of
\omega_{\psi}
by
\tilde{\iota}_{\chi_{V},\chi_{W},\psi}
.
We will denote this representation by
\Omega_{V,W,\chi_{V},\chi_{W},\psi}
, or simply by
\Omega
. We use this representation to describe the theta correspondence between
\mathrm{U}(W)
and
\mathrm{U}(V)
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible smooth representation of
\mathrm{U}(W)
. The maximal
\pi
-isotypic quotient of
\Omega
is

\Omega/\bigcap_{f\in\Hom_{\mathrm{U}(W)}(\Omega,\pi)}\ker(f).

This is a representation of
\mathrm{U}(V)\times\mathrm{U}(W)
and can be written as
\Theta(\pi)\boxtimes\pi
, where
\Theta(\pi)
is a smooth representation of
\mathrm{U}(V)
. We denote by
\Theta_{V,W,\chi_{V},\chi_{W},\psi}(\pi)
, or simply by
\Theta(\pi)
, the representation of
\mathrm{U}(V)
obtained from
\pi
following this procedure. It is called the
big theta lift
of
\pi
.

The following theorem was a conjecture of Howe
[
How79
]
. For odd residue characteristic, a proof was given by Waldspurger
[
Wal90
]
. The assumption on the residue characteristic was removed by Gan–Takeda
[
GT16
]
.

Theorem 2.2

(Howe duality theorem)
.

Let
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{U}(W))
.

(1)

If
\Theta(\pi)
is nonzero, it has a unique irreducible quotient. We denote it by
\theta_{V,W,\chi_{V},\chi_{W},\psi}(\pi)
, or simply by
\theta(\pi)
. It is called the small theta lift of
\pi
.

(2)

If
\theta(\pi)\simeq\theta(\pi^{\prime})\neq 0
, then
\pi\simeq\pi^{\prime}
.

It is useful to study theta lifts by considering certain families of Hermitian spaces called Witt towers. Recall that
\mathbb{H}
denotes the hyperbolic plane. We say that two Hermitian spaces
V
and
V^{\prime}
belong to the same Witt tower if there exist integers
k,l\geq 0
such that

V\oplus\mathbb{H}^{k}\simeq V^{\prime}\oplus\mathbb{H}^{l}.

Let
V_{1}
and
V_{1}^{\prime}
denote two non-isomorphic 1-dimensional Hermitian spaces and let
V_{2}
denote a 2-dimensional anisotropic Hermitian space. There are two Witt towers of even-dimensional Hermitian spaces

\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{0}=\{\mathbb{H}^{k}\;\colon\;k\geq 0\},\quad\mathcal{W}_{0}=\{V_{2}\oplus\mathbb{H}^{k}\;\colon\;k\geq 0\},

and two Witt towers of odd-dimensional Hermitian spaces

\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{1}=\{V_{1}^{\prime}\oplus\mathbb{H}^{k}\;\colon\;k\geq 0\},\quad\mathcal{W}_{1}=\{V_{1}\oplus\mathbb{H}^{k}\;\colon\;k\geq 0\}.

We continue to denote by
W
a fixed
m
-dimensional skew-Hermitian space. Fix a character
\chi_{W}
satisfying the condition stated above, i.e., such that
{\chi_{W}}_{|F^{\times}}=\omega_{K/F}^{m}
. Since the parity of the dimension is the same for elements in a fixed Witt tower, we can choose the same splitting character
\chi_{V}
to define the corresponding theta lifts. Thus, we fix characters
\chi_{\text{odd}}
and
\chi_{\text{even}}
such that
{\chi_{\text{odd}}}_{|F^{\times}}=\omega_{K/F}
and
{\chi_{\text{even}}}_{|F^{\times}}=1
.

For an irreducible smooth admissible representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{U}(W)
, we make the following definitions:

\displaystyle n_{\mathcal{W}_{0}^{\prime}}(\pi)

\displaystyle=\min\{\dim V\;\colon\;V\in\mathcal{W}_{0}^{\prime}\text{ and }\Theta_{V,W,\chi_{\text{even}},\chi_{W},\psi}(\pi)\neq 0\};

\displaystyle n_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}(\pi)

\displaystyle=\min\{\dim V\;\colon\;V\in\mathcal{W}_{0}\text{ and }\Theta_{V,W,\chi_{\text{even}},\chi_{W},\psi}(\pi)\neq 0\};

\displaystyle n_{\mathcal{W}_{1}^{\prime}}(\pi)

\displaystyle=\min\{\dim V\;\colon\;V\in\mathcal{W}_{1}^{\prime}\text{ and }\Theta_{V,W,\chi_{\text{odd}},\chi_{W},\psi}(\pi)\neq 0\};

\displaystyle n_{\mathcal{W}_{1}}(\pi)

\displaystyle=\min\{\dim V\;\colon\;V\in\mathcal{W}_{1}\text{ and }\Theta_{V,W,\chi_{\text{odd}},\chi_{W},\psi}(\pi)\neq 0\}.

For the definition of
n_{\mathcal{W}_{0}^{\prime}}(\pi)
, we consider that, in the case
V=0
, the theta lift of
\pi
is nonzero if and only if
\pi
is the 1-dimensional representation defined by the character
\chi_{\text{even}}\circ i\circ\det_{W}
, where
i
denotes the inverse of the isomorphism
K^{\times}/F^{\times}\xrightarrow{\simeq}K^{1}
defined by
x\mapsto x/x^{c}
and
\det_{W}
denotes the natural determinant map on
\mathrm{U}(W)
.

The following result is a special case of
[
SZ15
, Thm. 1.10]
(see also the discussion preceding the statement of the theorem).

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible smooth representation of
\mathrm{U}(W)
. Then:

\displaystyle n_{\mathcal{W}_{0}^{\prime}}(\pi)+n_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}(\pi)=2m+2;

\displaystyle n_{\mathcal{W}_{1}^{\prime}}(\pi)+n_{\mathcal{W}_{1}}(\pi)=2m+2.

Moreover, for any Witt tower
\mathcal{W}
and for any
V\in\mathcal{W}
with
\dim V\geq n_{\mathcal{W}}(\pi)
, the corresponding theta lift
\Theta(\pi)
is nonzero. The same results hold if we interchange the role of Hermitian and skew-Hermitian spaces.

2.3.
Theta correspondence for
\mathrm{U}_{1}\times\mathrm{U}_{3}

We keep the definitions and the notation from the previous subsection. Let
\gamma
be a conjugate-symplectic character of
K^{\times}
, i.e., such that
\gamma_{|F^{\times}}=\omega_{K/F}
. Then, we can make the following choice of splitting characters:

\chi_{V}=\gamma^{n},\ \chi_{W}=\gamma^{m}.

We denote by
\Omega_{V,W,\gamma,\psi}
the pullback to
\mathrm{U}(V)\times\mathrm{U}(W)
of the Weil representation
\omega_{\psi}
obtained from this choice of splitting characters. For an irreducible smooth representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{U}(W)
, we denote by
\Theta_{V,W,\gamma,\psi}(\pi)
the corresponding theta lift. Due to the choice of splitting characters, the theta correspondence preserves central characters.

Assume now that
W
has dimension
m=1
and
V
has dimension
n=3
. Let
V_{1}
denote the 1-dimensional Hermitian space in the Witt tower of
V
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\mu
be a character of
\mathrm{U}(W)
. Then,

(1)

if
\Theta_{V_{1},W,\gamma,\psi}(\mu\gamma_{|K^{1}}^{-1})=0
, then
\Theta_{V,W,\gamma,\psi}(\mu)
is a nonzero irreducible supercuspidal representation of
\mathrm{U}(V)
;

(2)

if
\Theta_{V_{1},W,\gamma,\psi}(\mu\gamma_{|K^{1}}^{-1})\neq 0
, then
\Theta_{V,W,\gamma,\psi}(\mu)
is a nonzero, irreducible but not supercuspidal representation of
\mathrm{U}(V)
. Moreover,
\Theta_{V,W,\gamma,\psi}(\mu)
is a quotient of

i_{B^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{U}(V)}\left(\gamma\lvert\ \rvert_{K}^{1/2}\otimes\mu\gamma_{|K^{1}}^{-1}\right).

Proof.

This follows from
[
MVW87
, Théorème principal, p. 69]
. Indeed, any character of
\mathrm{U}(W)
is a supercuspidal representation. The appearance of the character
\gamma_{|K^{1}}^{-1}
in our statements follows from our different choice of lifting characters. With our choices, the first case above follows from statement 1.b in [
loc. cit.
]. In the second case, we have that
\Theta_{V_{1},W,\gamma,\psi}(\mu\gamma_{|K^{1}}^{-1})=\mu\gamma_{|K^{1}}^{-1}
. Therefore, in this case, it follows from statement 1.c in [
loc. cit.
] that
r_{U^{\prime}}(\Theta_{V,W,\gamma,\psi}(\mu))=\gamma\lvert\ \rvert_{K}^{1/2}\otimes\mu\gamma_{|K^{1}}^{-1}
, where
U^{\prime}
denotes the unipotent subgroup of
B^{\prime}
and
r_{U^{\prime}}
the corresponding Jacquet functor. Thus, an application of (standard) Frobenius reciprocity shows that
\Theta_{V,W,\gamma,\psi}(\mu)
is a subrepresentation of
i_{B^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{U}(V)}\left(\gamma\lvert\ \rvert_{K}^{-1/2}\otimes\mu\gamma_{|K^{1}}^{-1}\right)
, whereas an application of the Bernstein form of Frobenius reciprocity shows that it is a quotient of
i_{B^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{U}(V)}\left(\gamma\lvert\ \rvert_{K}^{1/2}\otimes\mu\gamma_{|K^{1}}^{-1}\right)
, as desired.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

Most of the previous theorem can also be deduced from
[
Gan22
, §2.12]
.

We will also need a criterion to determine whether
\Theta_{V_{1},W,\gamma,\psi}(\mu)
is nonzero. Fix an element
\delta\in K^{\times}
of trace equal to zero. We adapt the sign characters as defined in (
1
) and (
2
) to our case. In particular, we set

\epsilon(W):=\omega_{K/F}(\delta^{-1}\disc(W))\in\{\pm 1\}.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\mu
be a character of
\mathrm{U}(W)
. The theta lift
\Theta_{V_{1},W,\gamma,\psi}(\mu)
is nonzero if and only if

\epsilon(V_{1})\epsilon(W)=\epsilon_{K}\left(\frac{1}{2},\gamma\tilde{\mu}^{-1},\psi(\Tr_{K/F}(-\delta(\cdot)))\right).

where
\tilde{\mu}=\mu\circ i
and
i
denotes the inverse of the isomorphism
K^{\times}/F^{\times}\xrightarrow{\simeq}K^{1}
defined by
x\mapsto x/x^{c}
.

Proof.

This is
[
Rog92
, Proposition 3.4]
. Indeed, if
V_{1}
is the 1-dimensional Hermitian space with Hermitian form
(x,y)\mapsto 2xy^{c}
and
W
is the 1-dimensional skew-Hermitian space with skew-Hermitian form
(x,y)\mapsto\delta xy^{c}
for an element
\delta\in K^{\times}
of trace zero, then it follows from [
loc. cit.
] that the theta lift
\theta_{V_{1},W,\gamma,\psi}(\mu)
is nonzero if and only if

\gamma(2\delta)^{-1}\mu(-1)\epsilon_{K}\left(\frac{1}{2},\gamma\tilde{\mu}^{-1},\psi\circ\Tr_{K/F}\right)=1.

Now, in this particular case, we have
\epsilon(V_{1})=2
,
\epsilon(W)=1
and

\displaystyle\epsilon_{K}\left(\frac{1}{2},\gamma\tilde{\mu}^{-1},\psi(\Tr_{K/F}(-\delta(\cdot)))\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma\tilde{\mu}^{-1}(-\delta)\epsilon_{K}\left(\frac{1}{2},\gamma\tilde{\mu}^{-1},\psi\circ\Tr_{K/F}\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma(-\delta)\mu(-1)\epsilon_{K}\left(\frac{1}{2},\gamma\tilde{\mu}^{-1},\psi\circ\Tr_{K/F}\right),

so
\theta_{V_{1},W,\gamma,\psi}(\mu)
is nonzero if and only if

\epsilon_{K}\left(\frac{1}{2},\gamma\tilde{\mu}^{-1},\psi(\Tr_{K/F}(-\delta(\cdot)))\right)=\gamma(-2\delta^{2}),

and, since
-\delta^{2}\in\norm_{K/F}(K^{\times})
, the right hand side of the last equation becomes
\gamma(2)=\epsilon(V_{1})\epsilon(W)
, so we obtain the result in this case. To obtain the result for arbitrary 1-dimensional Hermitian and skew-Hermitian spaces
V_{1}
and
W
, we just need to observe that scaling the form on one of these spaces by
a\in F^{\times}
amounts to replacing the Weil representation
\Omega_{V,W,\gamma,\psi}
by
\Omega_{V,W,\gamma,\psi_{a}}
.
∎

2.4.
Theta correspondence for
\mathrm{GL}_{1}\times\mathrm{GL}_{3}

In this subsection, we briefly review the results that we will later need regarding the theta correspondence for the pair
\mathrm{GL}_{1}\times\mathrm{GL}_{3}
. This is the case that arises if, in the previous setting, we replace the quadratic field extension
K/F
by the étale quadratic
F
-algebra
F\times F
. This theta correspondence has been described by Mínguez
[
Mín08
]
.

Let
F
be a nonarchimedian local field and fix a nontrivial additive character
\psi:F\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Let
M_{n,m}(F)
denote the space of
n\times m
matrices with coefficients in
F
and let
S_{n,m}
denote the space of locally constant compactly supported
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions on
M_{n,m}(F)
. The Weil representation
\omega_{\psi}
of the metaplectic group
\mathrm{Mp}_{2nm}(F)
can be realized on the space
S_{n,m}
. The choice of a character
\gamma
of
F^{\times}
determines a lifting

\tilde{\iota}_{\gamma,\psi}:\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)\times\mathrm{GL}_{m}(F)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Mp}_{2nm}(F)

of the natural map

\iota:\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)\times\mathrm{GL}_{m}(F)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Sp}_{2nm}(F)

defined by

(\tilde{\iota}_{\gamma,\psi}(g,h)f)(x)=\gamma(\det(g))^{m}|\det(g)|_{F}^{m/2}f(g^{T}xh)\gamma(\det(h))^{n}|\det(h)|_{F}^{n/2}

for all
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
,
h\in\mathrm{GL}_{m}(F)
,
x\in M_{n,m}(F)
and
f\in S_{n,m}
. Observe that our conventions differ from those adopted in
[
Mín08
]
.

Using the lifting
\tilde{\iota}_{\gamma,\psi}
, we can define the big theta lift
\Theta(\pi)
of a smooth admissible irreducible representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{m}(F)
and, if it is nonzero, the small theta lift
\theta(\pi)
.

Specialize now to the case
m=1
and
n=3
. The main result in
[
Mín08
]
is as follows.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\mu
be a character of
\mathrm{GL}_{1}(F)
. Then
\Theta(\mu)
is nonzero and
\theta(\mu)
is the Langlands quotient
\pi(\gamma\lvert\ \rvert_{F}^{1/2},\mu\gamma^{-2},\gamma\lvert\ \rvert_{F}^{-1/2})
of
i_{B^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{GL}_{3}}(\gamma\lvert\ \rvert_{F}^{1/2}\otimes\mu\gamma^{-2}\otimes\gamma\lvert\ \rvert_{F}^{-1/2})
, where
B^{\prime}
denotes the standard Borel subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
.

3.
Theta correspondence for the pair
(\mathrm{PU}_{3}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},G_{2})

In this section, we collect general results on the theta correspondence for
(\mathrm{PU}_{3}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},G_{2})
. In Section
3.1
, we recall the basic facts for the group
G_{2}
and its parabolic subgroups that play a role in this paper. In Section
3.2
, we do the same for the group
\mathrm{PU}_{3}\rtimes\Gal(K/F)
. In Section
3.3
, we discuss the general theory of theta correspondence for
(\mathrm{PU}_{3}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},G_{2})
. This draws from theta lifting for
(\mathrm{PU}_{3},G_{2})
, which we discuss in Section
3.4
following
[
BS
]
closely. Finally, we do the same for the pair
({\mathrm{PGL}}_{3},G_{2})
in Section
3.5
, which we use later for the split places.

3.1.
The group
G_{2}

Let
F
be a nonarchimedean local field of characteristic zero, with normalized absolute value denoted by
\lvert\ \rvert
. Let
G
be a split exceptional group of type
G_{2}
. Let
B=TU
be a Borel subgroup of
G
whose Levi component
T
is a split maximal torus of
G
. Denote by

\{\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta,2\alpha+\beta,3\alpha+\beta,3\alpha+2\beta\}

the corresponding set of positive roots, where
\alpha
is the short simple root and
\beta
is the long simple root. For any root
\gamma
we will denote by
w_{\gamma}
the corresponding reflection on the Weyl group of
G
. We can identify

T\simeq F^{\times}\times F^{\times},\ t\mapsto\left((2\alpha+\beta)(t),(\alpha+\beta)(t)\right).

Hence, under this identification, if
(t_{1},t_{2})\in F^{\times}\times F^{\times}
, we have
\alpha(t_{1},t_{2})=t_{1}t_{2}^{-1}
and
\beta(t_{1},t_{2})=t_{2}^{2}t_{1}^{-1}
.

Let
Q_{1}
be the parabolic subgroup corresponding to the root
\beta
with Levi decomposition
Q_{1}=L_{1}U_{1}
. We have an isomorphism
L_{1}\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{2}
under the map determined by

(3)

t\mapsto\diag\left((\alpha+\beta)(t),\alpha(t)\right).

The group
Q_{1}
is usually called the three-step parabolic of
G_{2}
, as
U_{1}
admits a three-step filtration
U_{1}=U_{1}(1)\supset U_{1}(2)\supset U_{1}(3)\supset U_{1}(4)=1
given in
[
BS
, Section 3.1]
. Similarly, let
Q_{2}
be the parabolic corresponding to the root
\alpha
. Consider its Levi decomposition
Q_{2}=L_{2}U_{2}
. We have an isomorphism
L_{2}\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{2}
under the map determined by

(4)

t\mapsto\diag\left((2\alpha+\beta)(t),(\alpha+\beta)(t)\right).

The group
Q_{2}
is usually called Heisenberg parabolic of
G_{2}
. For
i\in\{1,2\}
, We denote by
\delta_{Q_{i}}
the modular character of
Q_{i}
.

3.2.
The group
\mathrm{PU}_{3}\rtimes\Gal(K/F)

Let
K
be a quadratic extension of
F
, and let
\mathrm{U}_{3}
be the unitary group defined in the previous section, which we identify with matrices in
\mathrm{GL}_{3}(K)
fixing the Hermitian form determined by

\Phi_{3}=e=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&-1\\
0&-1&0\\
-1&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Let
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
be the quotient of
\mathrm{U}_{3}
by its center. Equivalently, the group
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
is the group of
F
-points of the group scheme
\underline{\mathrm{U}}_{3}/\underline{\mathrm{U}}_{1}
. Denote by
B^{\prime}
the standard Borel subgroup of
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
consisting of upper triangular matrices with Levi decomposition
B^{\prime}=T^{\prime}U^{\prime}
, where
T^{\prime}
is the standard maximal torus of
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
. We have

T^{\prime}\simeq K^{\times}

via the map

\diag(a,b,c)\mapsto\frac{a}{b}.

Note that the Galois group
\Gal(K/F)
acts on
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
by acting on its coefficients. We can then consider the semidirect product
\mathrm{PU}_{3}\rtimes\Gal(K/F)
and the subgroups
B^{\prime}\rtimes\Gal(K/F)
and
T^{\prime}\rtimes\Gal(K/F)
.

3.3.
The dual pair
(G_{2},\mathrm{PU}_{3}\rtimes\Gal(K/F))
and exceptional correspondence

We closely follow
[
BS
, §1]
. Let
\mathbb{O}
be an octonion algebra over
F
and let
J=J_{3}(K)
be the set of
3\times 3
Hermitian matrices with coefficients in
K
. We can define a structure of Jordan algebra over
F
on
J
as follows. Addition on
J
is given by addition of matrices and regarding multiplication, let
e
be as above and define
x\circ y=\frac{1}{2}(xe^{\#}y+ye^{\#}x)
, where the superindex

denotes taking the adjoint, so that

. Note that from the expression of

given above, we have

. The algebra

is equipped with an anti-involution given by the action of the nontrivial element of

on the matrix entries. Let

G:=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{O}),

G_{2}
. On the other hand, let

G^{\prime}:=\mathrm{Aut}(J)\simeq\mathrm{PU}_{3}\rtimes\Gal(K/F),

where
\Gal(K/F)
acts on
\mathrm{PU}_{3}

\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{Lie}(G)

\mathfrak{h}:=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\oplus\mathbb{O}^{\circ}\oplus J^{\circ},

has a structure of a simple exceptional Lie algebra over

, where the superscript
\circ
denotes the trace zero elements. Let
H=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{h})
, which is disconnected quasi-split of absolute type
E_{6}
. By definition of

, we have an inclusion

G\times G^{\prime}\subset H

and they form a dual reductive pair. Let
\Pi
be the minimal representation of

(see
[
BS
, §1.5]
for the definition of minimal representation and
[
GS05
]
for its construction). We also use
\Pi
to denote the restriction of this representation to

, or the restriction to

. This representation induces correspondences between certain representations of
G^{\prime}
and
G
, and certain representations of
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
and
G
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\tilde{\sigma}
be a smooth irreducible representation of
G^{\prime}

\Theta(\tilde{\sigma})

G
such that

\tilde{\sigma}
-isotypic quotient of
\Pi
, viewed as a representation of

. Similarly, let
\sigma
be a smooth irreducible representation of
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
. Define
\Theta_{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}(\sigma)

\sigma
-isotypic quotient of
\Pi

.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
\sigma
be a smooth irreducible representation of
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
. Then, it can be verified that

(5)

\Theta_{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}(\sigma)=\Theta\left(\Ind_{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}^{\mathrm{PU}_{3}\rtimes\Gal(K/F)}(\sigma)\right),

where the right-hand side is interpreted as we now explain.

Let
\sigma^{c}
be the representation obtained by twisting
\sigma
by the action of the nontrivial element of
\Gal(K/F)
. We then have the following two cases:

(1)

Suppose
\sigma\not\simeq\sigma^{c}
. Then,
\Ind_{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}^{\mathrm{PU}_{3}\rtimes\Gal(K/F)}(\sigma)
is irreducible and its restriction to
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
is isomorphic to
\sigma\oplus\sigma^{c}
. In this case, the right-hand side of (
5

4.

to

in the local nonarchimedean setting and propose a construction of the corresponding A-packets. We use the results from Section

for this process.

Throughout this section

denotes a number field and

denotes the corresponding conjectural Langlands group. We denote by

the
F
-points of a split group of type

over

4.1.
Dihedral long root A-parameters

Let

\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}/K^{\times}

and

corresponding to the root system of type

formed by the six long roots of

. Let

. Let

denote the standard Weyl element of

5.
Non-vanishing of theta lifts

Let
K/F
be a quadratic extension of local fields of characteristic zero and consider the notation introduced in Section
3
. In this section, we prove the following criterion regarding non-vanishing of theta lifts from
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
to
G
: if the contragredient of a representation
\tau
of
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
(viewed as a representation of
\mathrm{U}_{3}
) has an irreducible quotient with trivial central character when restricted to a suitable two-variable unitary subgroup, then
\Theta_{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}(\tau)\neq 0
. This criterion is proven by computing Fourier–Jacobi periods: with the notation introduced in Section
3.1
, we show that, for any nontrivial character
\psi^{\prime}
of
U_{1}(2)/U_{1}(3)
,

\Hom_{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}(\Pi_{U_{1}(2),\psi^{\prime}},\tau)\neq 0.

The key to prove this result is a description of
\Pi_{U_{1}(2),\psi^{\prime}}
in terms of Weil representations attached to two-variable unitary subgroups and
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
. In Section
5.1
, we provide a suitable description of two-variable unitary subgroups; in Section
5.2
, we introduce the symplectic spaces that determine the aforementioned Weil representations, and, in Section
5.3
, we prove the criterion.

Fix a nontrivial additive character
\psi:F\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and fix also a conjugate-symplectic character
\chi:K^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Let
\sigma^{+}
and
\sigma^{-}
be the representations of
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
introduced in Definition
4.3
with respect to these data, and let
\pi^{+}
and
\pi^{-}
be their corresponding small theta lifts to the group
G
, as introduced in Definition
4.9
.
In Section
5.4
, we use a see-saw argument to verify that
\sigma^{+}
, and, under the condition that
\chi^{2}\neq 1
, also
\sigma^{-}
, satisfy the hypothesis of the non-vanishing criterion. As a consequence,
\pi^{+}\neq 0
, and, if
\chi^{2}\neq 1
, also
\pi^{-}\neq 0
, proving the non-vanishing statements in Theorem

.

5.1.
The groups

and

Let

and

is split and

-basis, the Hermitian form on

acts on the 4-dimensional

(g,A)\mapsto gAg^{\dagger}\quad\text{for }g\in\mathrm{U}(V_{2})\text{ and }A\in H_{2}(K),

and this action preserves the quadratic form defined on

by the determinant map. Moreover, it stabilizes the 3-dimensional

-subspace

to

, the

-points of the special orthogonal group associated with the 3-dimensional quadratic

-space

consists of the elements in
{\mathrm{SO}}(Q)

\norm_{K/F}(K^{\times})/(F^{\times})^{2}\subset F^{\times}/(F^{\times})^{2}
.

K

. Then we can describe

\mathrm{U}(V_{2}^{\prime})

(g,A)\mapsto gAg^{\dagger}\quad\text{for }g\in\mathrm{U}(V_{2}^{\prime})\text{ and }A\in H_{2}(K),

by the determinant map. Moreover, it stabilizes the 3-dimensional

-subspace

\mathrm{U}(V_{2}^{\prime})

{\mathrm{SO}}(Q^{\prime})

F

of
\mathrm{U}(V_{2}^{\prime})

5.2.

We choose the following description of

Let

[

, §2.1]

5.3.
Non-vanishing criterion for theta lifts

Let
\Gamma
denote a 2-dimensional symplectic space. Then
\Gamma\otimes_{F}\Delta_{0}^{\perp}
and
\Gamma\otimes_{F}\Delta_{1}^{\perp}
are 8-dimensional symplectic spaces. Let
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{1}
be the Weil representations associated with
\Gamma\otimes_{F}\Delta_{0}^{\perp}
and
\Gamma\otimes_{F}\Delta_{1}^{\perp}
, respectively, for a fixed nontrivial character
\psi^{\prime}:F\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Let
\omega_{0,Q}
,
\omega_{0,Z}
,
\omega_{1,Q^{\prime}}
and
\omega_{1,Z^{\prime}}
be the Weil representations associated with the symplectic spaces
\Gamma\otimes_{F}Q
,
\Gamma\otimes_{F}Z
,
\Gamma\otimes_{F}Q^{\prime}
and
\Gamma\otimes_{F}Z^{\prime}
, respectively, for the character
\psi^{\prime}
. Then, under the natural homomorphism

{\mathrm{Mp}}(\Gamma\otimes_{F}Q)\times{\mathrm{Mp}}(\Gamma\otimes_{F}Z)\longrightarrow{\mathrm{Mp}}(\Gamma\otimes_{F}\Delta_{0}^{\perp}),

the representation
\omega_{0}
pulls back to
\omega_{0,Q}\boxtimes\omega_{0,Z}
, and, under the homomorphism

{\mathrm{Mp}}(\Gamma\otimes_{F}Q^{\prime})\times{\mathrm{Mp}}(\Gamma\otimes_{F}Z^{\prime})\longrightarrow{\mathrm{Mp}}(\Gamma\otimes_{F}\Delta_{1}^{\perp}),

the representation
\omega_{1}
pulls back to
\omega_{1,Q^{\prime}}\boxtimes\omega_{1,Z^{\prime}}
.

Recall that, in Section
3.1
, we defined
Q_{1}
as the three-step parabolic of
G
. It has a Levi decomposition
Q_{1}=L_{1}U_{1}
, with Levi subgroup
L_{1}\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{2}
and unipotent subgroup
U_{1}
equipped with a three-step filtration
U_{1}=U_{1}(1)\supset U_{1}(2)\supset U_{1}(3)\supset U_{1}(4)=1
described in
[
BS
, §3.1]
. In particular, the quotient
U_{1}/U_{1}(3)
is isomorphic to the Heisenberg group associated with a two-dimensional symplectic space. This yields a natural action of
U_{1}/U_{1}(3)
on
\omega_{0,Z}
and
\omega_{1,Z^{\prime}}
via the Heisenberg representation. From now on, we consider the action of
U_{1}/U_{1}(3)
on
\omega_{0}=\omega_{0,Q}\boxtimes\omega_{0,Z}
and
\omega_{1}=\omega_{1,Q^{\prime}}\boxtimes\omega_{1,Z^{\prime}}
given by the trivial representation on the first factors and the Heisenberg representation on the second factors.

There is also a natural action of the metaplectic group
{\mathrm{Mp}}_{2}
on each of the representations
\omega_{0,Q}
,
\omega_{0,Z}
,
\omega_{1,Q^{\prime}}
and
\omega_{1,Z^{\prime}}
. Moreover, the resulting action of
{\mathrm{Mp}}_{2}
on
\omega_{0}=\omega_{0,Q}\boxtimes\omega_{0,Z}
and
\omega_{1}=\omega_{1,Q^{\prime}}\boxtimes\omega_{1,Z^{\prime}}
factors through
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}
. From now on, we let
{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}\subset L_{1}
act on
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{1}
via this action.

Finally, there is a natural action of
{\mathrm{SO}}(Q)
(resp.
{\mathrm{SO}}(Q^{\prime})
) on
\omega_{0,Q}
(resp.
\omega_{1,Q^{\prime}}
), and we let
\mathrm{U}(V_{2})
(resp.
\mathrm{U}(V_{2}^{\prime})
) act on
\omega_{0}=\omega_{0,Q}\boxtimes\omega_{0,Z}
(resp.
\omega_{1}=\omega_{1,Q^{\prime}}\boxtimes\omega_{1,Z^{\prime}}
) via the embedding
\mathrm{U}(V_{2})/Z(\mathrm{U}(V_{2}))\xhookrightarrow{}{\mathrm{SO}}(Q)
(resp.
\mathrm{U}(V_{2}^{\prime})/Z(\mathrm{U}(V_{2}^{\prime}))\xhookrightarrow{}{\mathrm{SO}}(Q^{\prime})

It follows from
[
BS
, §3.1.1]
that, as a representation of
({\mathrm{SL}}_{2}\ltimes U_{1}/U_{1}(3))\times\mathrm{PU}_{3}
,

\Pi_{U_{1}(2),\psi^{\prime}}=\mathrm{c}\mbox{-}{\Ind}_{\mathrm{U}(V_{2})}^{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}\omega_{0}\oplus\mathrm{c}\mbox{-}{\Ind}_{\mathrm{U}(V_{2}^{\prime})}^{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}\omega_{1}.

Now we use this description of
\Pi_{U_{1}(2),\psi^{\prime}}
to prove the following result.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\tau
be a smooth irreducible representation of
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
and let
\tau^{\vee}
be its contragredient representation. Assume that either of the following two conditions holds:

•

The restriction of
\tau^{\vee}
to
\mathrm{U}(V_{2})
has an irreducible quotient with trivial central character.

•

The restriction of
\tau^{\vee}
to
\mathrm{U}(V_{2}^{\prime})
has an irreducible quotient with trivial central character.

Then, the theta lift
\Theta_{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}(\tau)
is nonzero.

Proof.

To prove the non-vanishing of the theta lift, it suffices to prove that the space

\Hom_{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}(\Pi_{U_{1}(2),\psi^{\prime}},\tau)=\Hom_{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}(\mathrm{c}\mbox{-}{\Ind}_{\mathrm{U}(V_{2})}^{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}\omega_{0},\tau)\oplus\Hom_{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}(\mathrm{c}\mbox{-}{\Ind}_{\mathrm{U}(V_{2}^{\prime})}^{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}\omega_{0},\tau)

is nontrivial.

Assume that the restriction of
\tau^{\vee}
to
\mathrm{U}(V_{2})
has an irreducible quotient
\rho
with trivial central character. Let
\rho^{\vee}
be the contragredient of
\rho
as a smooth representation of
\mathrm{U}(V_{2})
. Note that
\rho^{\vee}
can be regarded as a representation of
\mathrm{PU}(V_{2})\simeq{\mathrm{SO}}(Q)^{+}
. Recall that
\omega_{0}=\omega_{0,Q}\boxtimes\omega_{0,Z}
, and
\mathrm{U}(V_{2})
acts on this space via the action of
{\mathrm{SO}}(Q)^{+}
on the factor
\omega_{0,Q}
. Since any irreducible representation of
{\mathrm{SO}}(Q)
(and
a fortiori
of
{\mathrm{SO}}(Q)^{+}
) appears as a quotient of the Weil representation
\omega_{0,Q}
, so does the representation
\rho^{\vee}
. Hence, we can regard
\rho^{\vee}
as a
\mathrm{U}(V_{2})
-quotient of
\omega_{0}
, and, thus, we can also regard
\mathrm{c}\mbox{-}{\Ind}_{\mathrm{U}(V_{2})}^{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}\rho^{\vee}
as a
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
-quotient of
\mathrm{c}\mbox{-}{\Ind}_{\mathrm{U}(V_{2})}^{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}\omega_{0}
. Therefore, to conclude the proof in this case we just need to show that
\tau
is a
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
-quotient of
\mathrm{c}\mbox{-}{\Ind}_{\mathrm{U}(V_{2})}^{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}\rho^{\vee}
, i.e.

\Hom_{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}(\mathrm{c}\mbox{-}{\Ind}_{\mathrm{U}(V_{2})}^{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}\rho^{\vee},\tau)\neq 0.

But, dualizing the left-hand side of the previous expression and applying Frobenius reciprocity, it becomes

\Hom_{\mathrm{U}(V_{2})}(\tau^{\vee},\rho),

which is nonzero since
\rho
is a
\mathrm{U}(V_{2})
-quotient of
\tau^{\vee}
.

The case in which the restriction of
\tau^{\vee}
to
\mathrm{U}(V_{2}^{\prime})
has an irreducible quotient with trivial central character can be dealt with in a similar way.

∎

5.4.
A see-saw argument

In this subsection, we use a see-saw argument to prove that the contragredient of the representation
\sigma^{+}
of
\mathrm{U}_{3}
defined above, when restricted to a representation of a suitable 2-variable unitary group, has a quotient with trivial central character. We also prove this for the contragredient of
\sigma^{-}
under the assumption that
\chi^{2}\neq 1
. Combined with the results in the previous subsection, this will conclude the proof of the non-vanishing of the corresponding theta lifts to the group

Recall that
\chi
is a conjugate-symplectic character of

. In general, if

and

Let

be a 3-dimensional Hermitian space over
K
. Assume that its Hermitian form, with respect to some
K

\Phi_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&-1\\

As before, let

and

be 2-dimensional Hermitian spaces such that

is split and

\displaystyle V_{3}\coloneqq V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\cong V_{1}^{\prime}\oplus V_{2}^{\prime},

where

and

are non-isomorphic 1-dimensional Hermitian spaces over
K
. Let
W
and

be 1-dimensional skew-Hermitian spaces over

such that
\Theta_{V_{1},W}(\mathbb{1}_{W})\neq 0
and
\Theta_{V_{1}^{\prime},W^{\prime}}(\mathbb{1}_{W^{\prime}})\neq 0
. Observe that, by dichotomy, the spaces
W
and
W^{\prime}
are not isomorphic. More precisely, we have that

\epsilon(V_{1})\epsilon(W)=\epsilon_{K}(1/2,\chi,\psi(\mathrm{Tr}_{K/F}(-\delta(\cdot)))=\epsilon(V_{1}^{\prime})\epsilon(W^{\prime}),

where

denotes a trace-zero element which we also use to define the signs
\epsilon(W)
and

2

We have that
\Theta_{V_{3},W}(\mathbb{1}_{W})

\displaystyle\Theta_{V_{3},W}(\mathbb{1}_{W})\text{ is non-tempered }

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\epsilon_{K}(1/2,\chi,\psi(\mathrm{Tr}_{K/F}(-\delta(\cdot)))\epsilon_{K}(1/2,\chi^{3},\psi(\mathrm{Tr}_{K/F}(-\delta(\cdot)))=1

In particular, when

, the statements above always hold.

We define

and

and

•

[

]
. Moreover, in [
loc. cit.
], it is proven that exactly one of these toric periods contributes to the sum with precisely dimension 1.

As a consequence, we obtain that the twisted coinvariant spaces for
\pi^{-}
with respect to any generic character of the unipotent radical of the Heisenberg parabolic subgroup of

are zero, from which we deduce that
\pi^{-}
does not have a nontrivial non-generic quotient. Combining this fact with the results in Section

, we conclude the vanishing of
\pi^{-}
.

6.1.
Vanishing of the generic part

In this subsection, we prove that
\pi^{-}
does not have any generic subquotient. When
\chi^{2}\neq 1
, we deduce from this that
\pi^{-}
is irreducible.
The proofs rely on results of
[
BS
, §4.3-4.4]
.

As before, let
{B}^{\prime}
be a Borel subgroup of
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
and let
U^{\prime}
be its unipotent radical. It has a filtration

, where
U^{\prime}(2)\simeq F
and
U^{\prime}/U^{\prime}(2)\simeq K
. Via the last identification, we can define a character

of

corresponding to a nontrivial additive character
\psi\circ\Tr_{K/F}
of
K
.

According to
[
BS
, Corollary 4.6]
, in order to prove the vanishing of the generic part of
\pi^{-}
, it suffices to prove the vanishing of the space of Whittaker periods

\Hom_{U^{\prime}}((\sigma^{-})^{\vee},\bar{\psi}_{U^{\prime}})\subseteq\Hom_{U^{\prime}(2)}((\sigma^{-})^{\vee},\mathbb{1}).

Note that, as explained in the proofs of Proposition
4.7
and of Proposition
4.8
, the representation

is the big theta lift of a character of
\mathrm{U}_{1}
to a representation of
\mathrm{U}_{3}
. In particular, we can use the explicit description of the coinvariant spaces of theta lifts in this setting described in
[
Gan22
, Section 2.12, Guided Exercise (ii)]
to affirm that

Since

is supercuspidal, as
(\sigma^{-})^{\vee}\simeq(\sigma^{-})^{c}
by
[
HKS96
, Lemma 2.1(ii)]
and
\sigma^{-}
is supercuspidal, the right-hand side is equal to zero, implying the vanishing of the space of Whittaker periods.

We write the result as a proposition for reference.

Proposition 6.1
.

The representation
\pi^{-}
does not have any generic subquotient.

From there, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 6.2
.

If
\chi^{2}\neq 1
, the representation
\pi^{-}
is irreducible and tempered.

Proof.

Note that
\Ind_{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}^{G^{\prime}}\sigma^{-}
is irreducible (by Proposition
4.7
) and tempered (because
\sigma^{-}
is supercuspidal). We also make the following two observations:

•

By
[
BS
, Remark 4.4]
, every irreducible quotient of
\pi^{-}
is tempered.

•

By Proposition
6.1
, every irreducible quotient of
\pi^{-}
is non-generic.

Now, Theorem

implies that there exists a nonzero irreducible quotient of
\pi^{-}
. Moreover,
[

, Proposition 4.15]
together with these two observations imply that such quotient is unique. Hence,
\pi^{-}
is irreducible and tempered as desired.
∎

6.2.

We assume from now on that

Proposition 6.3
.

Consider the same notation as above. We have:

(1)

The automorphic induction of

(11)

We start proving the first point. Note that

In view of the previous proposition, the next proposition will provide a description of

Proposition 6.4
.

The unique irreducible quotient of

is given by
i_{Q_{2}}^{G}(\mu\circ\det)
.

Proof.

The key to prove this proposition is to rewrite normalized parabolic inductions for the parabolic
Q_{1}
(resp.
Q_{2}
) in terms of the parabolic
Q_{2}
(resp.
Q_{1}
) and to use intertwining operators.

Using the isomorphism induced by (
3

(12)

i_{Q_{1}}^{G}(|\det|^{1/2}\tau)=i_{Q_{1}}^{G}i_{T(U\cap L_{1})}^{L_{1}}(\mu\lvert\ \rvert^{1/2}\otimes\mu^{-1}\lvert\ \rvert^{1/2})=i_{Q_{1}}^{G}i_{B}^{Q_{1}}(\mu\lvert\ \rvert^{1/2}(\alpha+\beta)\cdot\mu^{-1}\lvert\ \rvert^{1/2}(\alpha)),

where in the last expression we are extending

from a character of
T
to a character of

i_{Q_{1}}^{G}(|\det|^{1/2}\tau)=i_{B}^{G}\left(\mu\lvert\ \rvert^{1/2}(\alpha+\beta)\cdot\mu^{-1}\lvert\ \rvert^{1/2}(\alpha)\right).

This can be rewritten as

(13)

\begin{split}i_{B}^{G}\left(\mu\lvert\ \rvert^{1/2}(\alpha+\beta)\cdot\mu^{-1}\lvert\ \rvert^{1/2}(\alpha)\right)&=i_{B}^{G}\left(\mu^{-1}\lvert\ \rvert^{1/2}(2\alpha+\beta)\cdot\mu^{2}(\alpha+\beta)\right)\\

where in the last equality we proceeded in a similar way as in (

4

\begin{split}i_{Q_{2}}^{G}i_{T(U\cap L_{2})}^{L_{2}}\left(\mu^{-1}\lvert\ \rvert^{1/2}\otimes\mu^{2}\right)&\simeq i_{Q_{2}}^{G}i_{T(U\cap L_{2})}^{L_{2}}\left(\mu^{2}\otimes\mu^{-1}\lvert\ \rvert^{1/2}\right)\\

\begin{split}i_{Q_{1}}^{G}i_{T(U\cap L_{1})}^{L_{1}}&\left(\mu^{2}\otimes\mu\lvert\ \rvert^{1/2}\right)\simeq i_{B}^{G}\left(\mu^{2}(\alpha+\beta)\cdot\mu\lvert\ \rvert^{1/2}(\alpha)\right)\\

Proposition 6.7
.

Consider the same notation as above. Let

M_{3}(K)^{\Gal(K/F)}
is the set of matrices
H^{e}_{3}(K)\subseteq H^{e}_{3}(K)\otimes_{F}K\cong M_{3}(K)
. It follows from these remarks that there is a natural bijection between (2) and (3).
∎

We now prove that the sets appearing in the previous lemma are always non-empty.

Lemma 6.12
.

There exists an embedding
i:E\otimes_{F}K\xhookrightarrow{}M_{3}(K)
of
K
-algebras compatible with the anti-involution defined by the nontrivial element
c\in\Gal(K/F)
on each side, given on
E\otimes_{F}K
by the natural Galois action and on
M_{3}(K)
by
A\mapsto eA^{\dagger}e^{-1}
.

Proof.

For any
\lambda\in F^{\times}
, we define the following Hermitian pairing on the
K
-vector space
E\otimes_{F}K
. For
x\otimes a,y\otimes b\in E\otimes_{F}K
, we define

\langle x\otimes a,y\otimes b\rangle_{\lambda}=\lambda ab^{c}\Tr(xy),

where
\Tr:E\to F
denotes the trace form, and we extend the pairing additively. Choosing a suitable
\lambda
, the Hermitian space
(E\otimes_{F}K,\langle\,,\,\rangle_{\lambda})
is isomorphic to the Hermitian space
(V=K^{3},\Phi_{3}=e)
. This provides an embedding

E\otimes_{F}K\xhookrightarrow{}\mathrm{End}_{K}(E\otimes_{F}K)\simeq\mathrm{End}_{K}(V)=M_{3}(K).

The actions of
c\in\Gal(K/F)
on
E\otimes_{F}K
and on
M_{3}(K)
defined in the statement amount to taking the adjoint with respect to the corresponding Hermitian pairing. Therefore, the previous embedding is compatible with the action of
c
.

∎

Given a cubic separable
F
-algebra
E
, let
\underline{T}_{E,K}
denote the algebraic group over
F
such that, for an
F
-algebra
R
,

\underline{T}_{E,K}(R)=\{x\in E\otimes_{F}R\otimes_{F}K\;\colon\;\norm_{E\otimes_{F}R\otimes_{F}K/E\otimes_{F}R}(x)=1\},

and let
T_{E,K}
denote the corresponding group of
F
-points.
An embedding
i^{\prime}:E\otimes_{F}K\xhookrightarrow{}M_{3}(K)
compatible with the anti-involution defined by the nontrivial element
c\in\Gal(K/F)
on each side, as described in the previous lemma, defines an embedding of algebraic groups
i:\underline{T}_{E,K}\xhookrightarrow{}\underline{\mathrm{U}}_{3}
. For an
F
-algebra
R
, the corresponding map on
R
-points is the map

(E\otimes_{F}K\otimes_{F}R)^{1}\xhookrightarrow{}\underline{\mathrm{U}}_{3}(R)

obtained by restricting the map induced by
i^{\prime}
to norm-
1
elements. Let
i^{\prime}_{0}:E\otimes_{F}K\xhookrightarrow{}M_{3}(K)
be an embedding compatible with the action of
c\in\Gal(K/F)
and let
i_{0}
be the corresponding embedding of algebraic groups
\underline{T}_{E,K}\xhookrightarrow{}\underline{\mathrm{U}}_{3}
. Let
\tilde{X}_{E}
denote the set of embeddings of algebraic groups
\underline{T}_{E,K}\xhookrightarrow{}\underline{\mathrm{U}}_{3}
which are conjugate to
i_{0}
by an element of
\mathrm{U}_{3}(\bar{F})
.
The group
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
acts on
\tilde{X}_{E}
by conjugation.

Proposition 6.13
.

Let
E
be a cubic separable
F
-algebra. There is a bijection of
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
-sets between
X_{E}
and
\tilde{X}_{E}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.11
, we just need to show that there is a bijection of
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
-sets between
\tilde{X}_{E}
and the set of embeddings of
K
-algebras
i^{\prime}:E\otimes_{F}K\xhookrightarrow{}M_{3}(K)
which are compatible with the anti-involution defined by the nontrivial element
c\in\Gal(K/F)
on each side. Such a map can be extended to an embedding of
\bar{F}
-algebras

E\otimes_{F}K\otimes_{F}\bar{F}\xhookrightarrow{}M_{3}(K\otimes_{F}\bar{F})

compatible with the action of
c\in\Gal(K/F)
. Giving such a map is equivalent to giving an embedding of
\bar{F}
-algebras

\bar{F}\times\bar{F}\times\bar{F}\xhookrightarrow{}M_{3}(\bar{F}).

Therefore, it becomes clear that, after choosing an embedding
K\xhookrightarrow{}\bar{F}
, any two embeddings
i_{1}^{\prime},i_{2}^{\prime}:E\otimes_{F}K\xhookrightarrow{}M_{3}(K)
compatible with the action of
c\in\Gal(K/F)
are conjugate by an element of
\mathrm{U}_{3}(\bar{F})\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{3}(\bar{F})
. In particular, any embedding
i^{\prime}:E\otimes_{F}K\xhookrightarrow{}M_{3}(K)
compatible with the action of
c\in\Gal(K/F)
is conjugate to
i_{0}^{\prime}
by an element of
\mathrm{U}_{3}(\bar{F})
. Moreover, if
a\in\mathrm{U}_{3}(\bar{F})
, then
a\cdot i_{0}^{\prime}\cdot a^{-1}
defines an embedding
E\otimes_{F}K\xhookrightarrow{}M_{3}(K)
compatible with the action of
c\in\Gal(K/F)
if and only if the cocycle
\tau\mapsto a^{-1}\cdot\tau(a)
defines a cohomology class in

\ker\left(H^{1}(F,\underline{T}_{E,K})\longrightarrow H^{1}(F,\underline{\mathrm{U}}_{3})\right).

This is the same condition that
a\in\mathrm{U}_{3}(\bar{F})
must satisfy in order that
a\cdot i_{0}\cdot a^{-1}
defines an embedding of algebraic groups
\underline{T}_{E,K}\xhookrightarrow{}\underline{\mathrm{U}}_{3}
. Furthermore,
a\in\mathrm{U}_{3}(\bar{F})
fixes
i_{0}^{\prime}
if and only if
a\in i_{0}(\underline{T}_{E,K}(\bar{F}))
and thus if and only if it fixes
i_{0}
. Hence, it follows that the map defined above sending an embedding
i^{\prime}:E\otimes_{F}K\xhookrightarrow{}M_{3}(K)
compatible with the action of
c\in\Gal(K/F)
to an embedding of algebraic groups
i:\underline{T}_{E,K}\xhookrightarrow{}\underline{\mathrm{U}}_{3}
provides the desired bijection.
∎

Let
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{E,K}
denote set of
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
-orbits of
\tilde{X}_{E}
.

Corollary 6.14
.

Let
E
be a cubic separable
F
-algebra. Then,

\Pi_{U_{2},\psi_{E}}\cong\bigoplus_{i\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{E,K}}\mathrm{c}\mbox{-}{\Ind}_{i(T_{E,K})}^{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}\mathbb{1}.

Proof.

It follows from Proposition
6.10
that

\Pi_{U_{2},\psi_{E}}\cong C_{c}^{\infty}(X_{E}).

Proposition
6.13
establishes a bijection of
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
-sets between
X_{E}
and
\tilde{X}_{E}
. The stabilizer of an embedding of algebraic groups
i:\underline{T}_{E,K}\hookrightarrow\underline{\mathrm{U}}_{3}
for the conjugation action of
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
is
i(T_{E,K})
. Therefore, decomposing
X_{E}
into
\mathrm{PU}_{3}
-orbits, we obtain

C_{c}^{\infty}(X_{E})\cong\bigoplus_{i\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{E,K}}\mathrm{c}\mbox{-}{\Ind}_{i(T_{E,K})}^{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}\mathbb{1}.

∎

Corollary 6.15
.

Let
E
be a cubic separable
F
-algebra. Then,

\dim\Hom_{U_{2}}(\Theta_{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}(\sigma^{+}),\psi_{E})+\dim\Hom_{U_{2}}(\Theta_{\mathrm{PU}_{3}}(\sigma^{-}),\psi_{E})=1.

Proof.

It follows from the previous corollary and an application of Frobenius reciprocity for compact induction that the left-hand side is equal to

\sum_{{i\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{E,K}}}\left(\dim\Hom_{i(T_{E,K})}((\sigma^{+})^{\vee},\mathbb{1})+\dim\Hom_{i(T_{E,K})}((\sigma^{-})^{\vee},\mathbb{1})\right).

Since both
\sigma^{+}
and
\sigma^{-}
are unitary representations, this sum can be rewritten as

\sum_{{i\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{E,K}}}\left(\dim\Hom_{i(T_{E,K})}(\sigma^{+},\mathbb{1})+\dim\Hom_{i(T_{E,K})}(\sigma^{-},\mathbb{1})\right),

which is equal to
1
by
[
BFG+
, Corollary 4.2]
.
∎

Proposition 6.16
.

If
\chi^{2}=1
, the representation
\pi^{-}
does not have a nonzero non-generic subquotient.

Proof.

Combining Proposition
6.7
and the previous corollary, it follows that, for all character
\psi_{E}
of
U_{2}
corresponding to a cubic separable
F
-algebra
E
as above,

\Hom_{U_{2}}(\pi^{-},\psi_{E})=0.

Therefore, it follows from
[
BS
, Lemma 4.13]
that any non-generic subquotient of
\pi^{-}
is finite-dimensional. Since
\pi^{-}
is tempered by
[
BS
, Remark 4.4]
, it cannot have any finite-dimensional subquotient, which concludes the proof.
∎

Combining Proposition
6.1
and Proposition
6.16
, we finally obtain the following.

Theorem 6.17
.

If
\chi^{2}=1
, the representation
\pi^{-}
is zero.

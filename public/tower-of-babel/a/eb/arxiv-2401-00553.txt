On Cohomology group of current Lie algebras
R. García-Delgado

Abstract
. In this work we state a result that relates the cohomology groups of a Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} and a current Lie algebra 𝔤 ⊗ 𝒮 \mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S} , by means of a short exact sequence – similar to the universal coefficients theorem for modules –, where 𝒮 \mathcal{S} is a finite dimensional, commutative and associative algebra with unit over a field 𝔽 \mathbb{F} . Although this result can be applied to any Lie algebra, we determine the cohomology group of 𝔤 ⊗ 𝒮 \mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S} , where 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} is a semisimple Lie algebra.

Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra with bracket
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
and
\mathcal{S}
be an associative and commutative algebra over
\mathbb{F}
with product
(s,t)\mapsto s\,t
, for all
s,t
in
\mathcal{S}
. The skew-symmetric and bilinear map
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}}
defined on
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
, by:

[x\otimes s,y\otimes t]_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}}=[x,y]\otimes s\,t,\,\,\text{ for all }\,x,y\in\mathfrak{g},\,\text{ and }\,s,t\in\mathcal{S},

yields a Lie algebra in
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
, which is called the
current Lie algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
by
\mathcal{S}
.

Let
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
be a representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on a vector space
V
, then
V
is called a
\mathfrak{g}
-module
. The representation
\rho
can be extended to a representation
R
of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
on the vector space
V\otimes\mathcal{S}
by means of:

(1)

R(x\otimes s)(v\otimes t)=\rho(x)(v)\otimes s\,t,\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathfrak{g},\,v\in V,\,s,t\in\mathcal{S}.

Let
C(\mathfrak{g};V)=C^{0}(\mathfrak{g};V)\oplus\ldots\oplus C^{p}(\mathfrak{g};V)\oplus\ldots
be the
space of cochains from
\mathfrak{g}
into
V
, where
C^{0}(\mathfrak{g},V)
is equal to
V
and
C^{p}(\mathfrak{g};V)
is the space of the alternating
p
-multilinear maps of
\mathfrak{g}
with values in
V
. For any
\mathfrak{g}
-mdule
V
, let
\operatorname{d}:C(\mathfrak{g};V)\to C(\mathfrak{g};V)
be the differential map given by:

(2)

\begin{split}&\operatorname{d}\lambda(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p+1})=\sum_{j=1}^{p+1}(-1)^{j-1}\rho(x_{j})(\lambda(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\hat{j}},\ldots,x_{p+1}))\\
\,&+\sum_{j<k}(-1)^{j+k}\lambda([x_{j},x_{k}],x_{1},\ldots,x_{\hat{j}},\ldots,x_{\hat{k}},\ldots,x_{p+1}),\qquad p>0.\end{split}

where
\lambda
is in
C^{p}(\mathfrak{g};V)
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{p+1}
are in
\mathfrak{g}
. For
p=0
we let
\operatorname{d}(v)(x)=\rho(x)(v)
where
v
is in
V
and
x
is in
\mathfrak{g}
.

The aim of this work is to set a result that relates the cohomology groups
\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S};V\otimes\mathcal{S})
and
\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g};V)
, similar to the Universal coefficient theorems for modules (see
[
5
]
, Chapter VI, §3,
Thm. 3.3
).

To achieve our goal, in
Prop.
2.2
we introduce a map between the set of cochains of
\mathfrak{g}
and cochains of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
. Next we prove that there exists a surjective linear map
\alpha
between
\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S};V\otimes\mathcal{S})
and
\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g};V)\otimes\mathcal{S}
(see
Prop.
3.1
). In
Thm.
3.2
we determine the kernel of
\alpha
and we state a result that relates the cohomology groups
\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S};V\otimes\mathcal{S})
and
\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g};V)\otimes\mathcal{S}
by means of a short exact sequence. Finally, we determine the cohomology group
\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S};V\otimes\mathcal{S})
, when
\mathfrak{g}
is a semisimple Lie algebra and
V
is an irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module.

The results obtained here are focused at knowing the cohomology group
\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S};V\otimes\mathcal{S})
, based on the cohomology group
\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g};V)
. In
[
7
]
, is determined formulas for the first and second cohomology groups of a current Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
, with coefficients in a module
V\otimes\mathcal{A}
, where
V
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module and
\mathcal{A}
is a
\mathcal{S}
-module. In
Thm. 2.1
of
[
8
]
, is given a formula for the second cohomology group of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
, with coefficients in the trivial module and
\mathcal{S}
has no unit. In
[
6
]
is given a description of the cohomology group
\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S};\mathcal{V})
, where
\mathcal{V}
is a trivial module of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
. It seems that one of the first results with this focus appears in
[
2
]
, where it is shown that cohomology of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S}
, where
\mathcal{S}
is a local algebra, can be reduced to cohomology of
\mathfrak{g}
. On the other hand, it is unknown if there exists a criterion for recognizing whether an arbitrary Lie algebra is a current Lie algebra. A step in this direction can be found in
[
3
]
, where some examples are given in 4-dimensional current Lie algebras.

\Lambda(x_{1}\otimes t_{1},\ldots,x_{p}\otimes t_{p})=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\Lambda_{j}(x_{1}\otimes 1,\ldots,x_{p}\otimes 1)\otimes s_{j}\,\bar{t}.

We will use this remark in the proof of

In the next result we prove that

preserves the composition of maps.

2.2 Proposition
.

If

and
g:Y\to Z
are maps in
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{g})
, then
\mathcal{T}(g\circ f)=\mathcal{T}(g)\circ\mathcal{T}(f):\mathcal{T}(X)\to\mathcal{T}(Z)
is a map in
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S})
.

We shall verify that if
f:X\to Y
and
g:Y\to Z
are maps in
\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{g})

\mathcal{T}(g\circ f)
is equal to
\mathcal{T}(g)\circ\mathcal{T}(f)
. Several cases should be considered and we only will prove two of them. In the

1 Claim
.

Let
f:C^{p}(\mathfrak{g};U)\to C^{p}(\mathfrak{g};V)
and
g:C^{p}(\mathfrak{g};V)\to C^{p}(\mathfrak{g};W)
be maps, then
\mathcal{T}(g\circ f)=\mathcal{T}(g)\circ\mathcal{T}(f)
is a map between
C^{p}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S};U\otimes\mathcal{S})
and
C^{p}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S};W\otimes\mathcal{S})

Proof.

Let
\Lambda
be in
C^{p}(\mathfrak{g}\!\otimes\!\mathcal{S};U\!\otimes\!\mathcal{S})
, and
\Theta\!=\!\mathcal{T}(f)(\Lambda)
. By (
23
) we have:

(29)

\mathcal{T}(g)(\Theta)(x_{1}\otimes t_{1},\cdots,x_{p}\otimes t_{p})=\sum_{j=1}^{m}g(\mathcal{L}(\Theta_{j}))(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p})\otimes s_{j}\,\bar{t},

where
\Theta_{j}=\widehat{\omega}_{j}\circ\mathcal{T}(f)(\Lambda)
(see (
5
)). We claim that
\mathcal{L}(\Theta_{j})=f(\mathcal{L}(\Lambda_{j}))
. Indeed, using the definition of
\mathcal{L}
and applying (
23
) to
\mathcal{T}(f)(\Lambda)
, we get:

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}(\Theta_{j})(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p})=\Theta_{j}(x_{1}\otimes 1,\ldots,x_{p}\otimes 1)\\
&=\widehat{\omega}_{j}\circ\mathcal{T}(f)(\Lambda)(x_{1}\otimes 1,\ldots,x_{p}\otimes 1)\\
&=\widehat{\omega}_{j}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}f\left(\mathcal{L}(\Lambda_{k})\right)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p})\otimes s_{k}\right)=f(\mathcal{L}(\Lambda_{j}))(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p}).\end{split}

Then
\mathcal{L}(\Theta_{j})=f(\mathcal{L}(\Lambda_{j}))
, for all
j
. Substituting this in (
29
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(g)\!\circ\!\mathcal{T}(f)(\Lambda)(x_{1}\!\otimes\!t_{1},\!\ldots\!,x_{p}\!\otimes\!t_{p})

\displaystyle=\!\!\sum_{j=1}^{m}(g\circ f)(\mathcal{L}(\Lambda_{j}))(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p})\!\otimes\!s_{j}\,\bar{t},

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(g\circ f)(\Lambda)(x_{1}\!\otimes\!t_{1},\cdots,x_{p}\!\otimes\!t_{p}).

In the last step above we use (
23
). Thus
\mathcal{T}(g\circ f)\!=\!\mathcal{T}(g)\circ\mathcal{T}(f)
.
∎

2 Claim
.

Let
f:U\to C^{p}(\mathfrak{g};V)
and let
g:C^{p}(\mathfrak{g};V)\to W
be maps. Then
\mathcal{T}(g\circ f)=\mathcal{T}(g)\circ\mathcal{T}(f)
is a map between

and

Proof.

Since

is a map between

and

\mathcal{T}(g\!\circ\!f)

U\!\otimes\!\mathcal{S}
and

\mathcal{T}(g\!\circ\!f)(u\!\otimes\!s)\!=\!g(f(u))\!\otimes\!s

Let
\Lambda=\mathcal{T}(f)(u\otimes s)
which belongs to
C^{p}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S};V\otimes\mathcal{S})
. By (
24
),
\Lambda(x_{1}\otimes 1,\ldots,x_{p}\otimes 1)\!=\!f(u)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p})\otimes s
. By definition of
\mathcal{L}
we have:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(\widehat{\omega}_{j}\circ\Lambda\right)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p})=(\widehat{\omega}_{j}\circ\Lambda)(x_{1}\otimes 1,\ldots,x_{p}\otimes 1)

\displaystyle=\widehat{\omega}_{j}(\Lambda(x_{1}\otimes 1,\ldots,x_{p}\otimes 1))=\omega_{j}(s)f(u)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p}),

hence
\mathcal{L}(\widehat{\omega}_{j}\circ\Lambda)=\omega_{j}(s)f(u)
for all

. Since
\Lambda_{j}=\widehat{\omega}_{j}\circ\Lambda
and

, from (

(30)

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}(g)(\Lambda)&=g(\mathcal{L}(\widehat{\omega}_{1}\circ\Lambda))\otimes s_{1}+\ldots+g(\mathcal{L}(\widehat{\omega}_{j}\circ\Lambda))\otimes s_{m}\\
\,&=\omega_{1}(s)g(f(u))\otimes s_{1}+\ldots+\omega_{m}(s)g(f(u))\otimes s_{m}\\
\,&=g(f(u))\otimes s=\mathcal{T}(g\circ f)(u\otimes s),\end{split}

As
\Lambda=\mathcal{T}(f)(u\otimes s)
, from (

\mathcal{T}(g)\circ\mathcal{T}(f)=\mathcal{T}(g\circ f)

∎

2.3 Proposition
.

Let
f:C(\mathfrak{g};V)\to C(\mathfrak{g};W)
be a map of complexes, that is
\operatorname{d}\circ f=f\circ\operatorname{d}
and
f(C^{k}(\mathfrak{g};V))\subset C^{k}(\mathfrak{g};W)
for all

. Then
\mathcal{T}(f):C(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S};V\otimes\mathcal{S})\to C(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S};W\otimes\mathcal{S})
is a map of complexes.

Proof.

To shorten the length of the mathematical expressions, we will use the following notation:

(31)

\begin{split}&\textbf{x}\otimes\textbf{t}=(x_{1}\otimes t_{1},\ldots,x_{p+1}\otimes t_{p+1}),\\
&(\textbf{x}\otimes\textbf{t})_{i}=(x_{1}\otimes t_{1},\ldots,x_{\hat{i}}\otimes t_{\hat{i}},\ldots,x_{p+1}\otimes t_{p+1}),\\
&(\textbf{x}\otimes\textbf{t})_{i,j}=(x_{1}\otimes t_{1},\ldots,x_{\hat{i}}\otimes t_{\hat{i}},\ldots,x_{\hat{j}}\otimes t_{\hat{j}},\ldots,x_{p+1}\otimes t_{p+1}),\\
&\textbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p+1}),\qquad\textbf{x}_{i}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\hat{i}},\ldots,x_{p+1}),\\
&\textbf{x}_{i,j}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\hat{i}},\ldots,x_{\hat{j}},\ldots,x_{p+1})\end{split}

Let
\Lambda
be in
C^{p}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathcal{S};V\otimes\mathcal{S})
,

\begin{split}&\operatorname{D}\mathcal{T}(f)(\Lambda)(\textbf{x}\otimes\textbf{t})=\sum_{i=1}^{p+1}(-1)^{i-1}R(x_{i}\otimes t_{i})\left(\mathcal{T}(f)(\Lambda)\right)((\textbf{x}\otimes\textbf{t})_{i})\\

We write

and

, to denote the first and second term in (

), that is:
\operatorname{D}\mathcal{T}(f)(\Lambda)(\textbf{x}\otimes\textbf{t})\!=\!\textbf{A}\!+\!\textbf{B}
. Applying (
23
) to
\mathcal{T}(f)(\Lambda)
in

(33)

\textbf{A}=\sum_{i=1}^{p+1}\sum_{k=1}^{m}(-1)^{i-1}R(x_{i}\otimes t_{i})\left(f(\mathcal{L}(\Lambda_{k}))(\textbf{x}_{i})\otimes s_{k}\,t^{i}\right),

where

. In (

)), and we get:

(34)

\textbf{A}=\sum_{i=1}^{p+1}\sum_{k=1}^{m}(-1)^{i-1}\rho(x_{i})\left(f\left(\mathcal{L}(\Lambda_{k})\right)(\textbf{x}_{i}\right)\otimes s_{k}\,\bar{t},

for
\bar{t}\!=\!t_{i}t^{i}
. Regarding to

, we fix
i\!<\!j
; by (
23
) and (
31
) we have:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(f)(\Lambda)\left([x_{i}\otimes t_{i},x_{j}\otimes t_{j}],(\textbf{x}\otimes\textbf{t})_{i,j}\right)

\displaystyle=\!\!\mathcal{T}(f)(\Lambda)\left([x_{i},x_{j}]\otimes t_{i}t_{j},x_{1}\otimes t_{1},\ldots,x_{\hat{i}}\otimes t_{\hat{i}},\ldots,x_{\hat{j}}\otimes t_{\hat{j}},\ldots,x_{p+1}\otimes t_{p+1}\right)

for

. Hence,

(35)

\textbf{B}=\sum_{i<j}\sum_{k=1}^{m}(-1)^{i+j}f\left(\mathcal{L}(\Lambda_{k})\right)([x_{i},x_{j}],\textbf{x}_{i,j})\otimes s_{k}\,\bar{t}.

From (

By hypothesis

Let
\rho_{W}
be the representation of
\mathfrak{g}
on

and
\operatorname{D}(f(v)\otimes s)(x\otimes t)=\rho_{W}(x)(f(v))\otimes s\,t

(40)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{T}(f)\circ\operatorname{D}(v\otimes s)(x\otimes t)=\rho_{W}(x)(f(v))\otimes s\,t\\

because

\mathcal{T}(f)\circ\operatorname{D}=\operatorname{D}\circ\mathcal{T}(f)

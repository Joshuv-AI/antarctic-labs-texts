Optimization of the Implicit Constant for Upper Bounds for Moments of the Riemann Zeta Function
Tingyu Tao

Abstract
. We optimized the implicit constant for the refined upper bounds for moments of the Riemann zeta-function proved by Harper. We also computed the implicit constant for the upper bounds for moments of the Riemann zeta-function proved by Soundararajan under certain conditions.



1.
Introduction

Computing moments of the Riemann zeta function is a well-studied subject in number theory. For
k\geq 0
, the
2k
-th moment of the Riemann zeta function is defined as

I_{k}\left(T\right):=\int_{0}^{T}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}dt.

Hardy and Littlewood
[
2
]
proved that
I_{1}\left(T\right)\sim T\log T
, and Ingham
[
3
]
proved that
I_{2}\left(T\right)\sim\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}T\left(\log T\right)^{2}
, while the asymptotic formulas of the
2k
-th moment for
k>2
remain unproved. It is conjectured that for any
k\geq 0
,
I_{k}\left(T\right)\sim c_{k}T\left(\log T\right)^{k^{2}}
for some constant
c_{k}
that depends on
k
. Keating and Snaith
[
4
]
, using random matrix theory, conjectured that for any
k\geq 0
,
c_{k}=a_{k}f_{k}
, where

f_{k}=\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-k^{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\Gamma\left(j\right)\Gamma\left(j+2k\right)}{\left(\Gamma\left(j+k\right)\right)^{2}},

and

a_{k}=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{k^{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\Gamma\left(m+k\right)}{m!\Gamma\left(k\right)}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{p^{m}},

where the product if over primes, and when
k
is large enough,
c_{k}=O\left(e^{-k^{2}\log k}\right)
.

Although asymptotic results for the
2k
-th moment when
k>2
remain open, some sharp bounds for moments have been proved. For lower bounds, Radziwiłł and Soundararajan
[
5
]
proved that
I_{k}\left(T\right)\gg_{k}T\left(\log T\right)^{k^{2}}
when
k>1
. Heap and Soundararajan
[
6
]
proved that the same lower bound also holds when
0<k<1
. For upper bounds, Heap, Radziwiłł and Soundararajan
[
8
]
proved that
I_{k}\left(T\right)\ll T\left(\log T\right)^{k^{2}}
for
0\leq k\leq 2
unconditionally. Soundararajan
[
7
]
proved that
I_{k}\left(T\right)\ll_{k}T\left(\log T\right)^{k^{2}+\varepsilon}
for any
k\geq 0
and any arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, conditionally on the Riemann Hypothesis. Later, Harper
[
1
]
improved the upper bound to
T\left(\log T\right)^{k^{2}}
. More precisely, Harper proved that for a fixed
k\geq 0
, when
T
is large enough, we have

(1)

\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}dt\leq C\left(k\right)T\left(\log T\right)^{k^{2}},

where
C\left(k\right)
is a constant depending on
k
. Harper also discussed in the paper that
C\left(k\right)=e^{e^{O\left(k\right)}}
. The main focus of this paper is to build on Harper’s proof and obtain an explicit constant value of
C\left(k\right)
, by optimizing various steps in the proof. We will prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis is true, and let
k\geq 0
be fixed. For large
T
, we have

\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}dt\ll e^{e^{18.63k}}T\left(\log T\right)^{k^{2}}.

where the implicit constant is absolute.

That is, the constant
C\left(k\right)
in (
1
) satisfies
C\left(k\right)=O\left(e^{e^{18.63k}}\right)
, and the implicit constant here does not depend on
k
.

2.
Proof of the Main Theorem

Since
\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}dt=\int_{T}^{2T}e^{2k\log\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|}dt
, it is very helpful if we can obtain an approximation of
\log\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|
. Soundararajan
[
7
]
proved the following result.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis is true, and let
T
be large. For any
2\leq x\leq T^{2}
, and any
T\leq t\leq 2T
, we have

\log\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|\leq{\Re}\left(\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{1}{p^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\log x}+it}}\frac{\log\left(x/p\right)}{\log x}+\sum_{p\leq\min\left(\sqrt{x},\log T\right)}\frac{1/2}{p^{1+2it}}\right)+\frac{\log T}{\log x}+N

where
p
denotes primes, and
N
is an absolute constant.

The main idea of Harper’s proof of (
1
) is to find a partition of the interval
\left[T,2T\right]
, and compute the integral of
\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}
for each part. In order to partition the interval
\left[T,2T\right]
, we first introduce the following sequence
\left(\beta_{j}\right)
:

\beta_{0}:=0,\quad\quad{\beta_{i}}:=\frac{{c_{1}}^{i-1}}{\left(\log\log T\right)^{2}}\quad\forall i\geq 1,

where
c_{1}>0
is an absolute constant, and

\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}_{k,T}:=1+\max\Set{i:{\beta_{i}}\leq e^{-c_{2}k}},

where
c_{2}
is an absolute constant. For each
1\leq i\leq j\leq\mathcal{I}
, let

G_{\left(i,j\right)}\left(t\right)=G_{\left(i,j\right),T}\left(t\right):=\sum_{T^{\beta_{i-1}}<p\leq T^{{\beta_{i}}}}\frac{1}{p^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{{\beta_{j}}\log T}+it}}\frac{\log\left(T^{{\beta_{j}}}/p\right)}{\log\left(T^{{\beta_{j}}}\right)}.

The sets that form the partition are

\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}_{k,T}:=\Set{T\leq t\leq 2T:\left}{{\Re}\sum_{T^{\beta_{i-1}}<p\leq T^{{\beta_{i}}}}\frac{1}{p^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}\log T}+it}}\frac{\log\left(T^{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}}/p\right)}{\log\left(T^{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}}\right)}},

where
0<c_{3}<1
is an absolute constant, and for all
0\leq j\leq\mathcal{I}-1
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\left(j\right)=\mathcal{S}_{k,T}\left(j\right):=

\displaystyle\left\{T\leq t\leq 2T:\left|{\Re}G_{i,l}\left(t\right)\right|\leq{\beta_{i}}^{-c_{3}},\ \forall 1\leq i\leq j,\ \forall i\leq l\leq\mathcal{I}\right.

\displaystyle\ \left.\text{but }\left|{\Re}G_{\left(j+1,l\right)}\left(t\right)\right|>{\beta_{j+1}}^{-c_{3}}\text{ for some }j+1\leq l\leq\mathcal{I}\right\}.

Based on the above definition, we have

\left[T,2T\right]=\mathcal{T}\cup\bigcup_{j=0}^{\mathcal{I}-1}S\left(j\right),

so we have

(2)

\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}dt=\int_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}dt+\sum_{j=0}^{\mathcal{I}-1}\int_{t\in\mathcal{S}\left(j\right)}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}dt.

For the integral on each individual part, we have the following lemmas.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
k\geq 1
, and
T\geq e^{e^{\left(10000k\right)^{2}}}
. Denote
a=c_{1}^{1-c_{3}}
. If we have

\frac{ka^{2}}{a-1}e^{c_{2}k\left(1-c_{3}\right)}<\frac{1}{4},

then

\int_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\exp\left(2k{\Re}\sum_{p\leq T^{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}}}\frac{1}{p^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}\log T}+it}}\frac{\log\left(T^{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}}/p\right)}{\log\left(T^{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}}\right)}\right)dt\leq C_{1}T\left(\log T\right)^{k^{2}},

where
C_{1}
is an absolute constant.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
k\geq 1
, and
T\geq e^{e^{\left(10000k\right)^{2}}}
. Then

{\rm meas}\left(\mathcal{S}\left(0\right)\right)\leq MTe^{-2\left(\log\log T\right)^{2}/c_{1}}

for some absolute constant
M
. Assume

\log c_{1}<\frac{c_{1}}{2},\quad\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}\left(\frac{c_{1}}{4c_{3}-2}+1\right)}>2,

and
\forall 1\leq j\leq\mathcal{I}-1
, we have

\mathcal{I}-j\leq\frac{1/{\beta_{j}}}{\log c_{1}}.

Let
c_{4}=\frac{c_{1}}{4c_{3}-2}+1
, then we have

\int_{t\in\mathcal{S}\left(j\right)}\exp\left(2k{\Re}\sum_{p\leq T^{{\beta_{j}}}}\frac{1}{p^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{{\beta_{j}}\log T}+it}}\frac{\log\left(T^{{\beta_{j}}}/p\right)}{\log\left(T^{{\beta_{j}}}\right)}\right)dt\leq C_{2}e^{-\beta_{j+1}^{-1}\log\left(1/\beta_{j+1}\right)/c_{4}}T\left(\log T\right)^{k^{2}},

where
C_{2}
is an absolute constant.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
k\geq 1
and
T\geq e^{e^{\left(10000k\right)^{2}}}
. If the same assumptions in Lemma
2.2
are true, then

\int_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\exp\left(2k{\Re}\sum_{p\leq T^{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}}}\frac{1}{p^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}\log T}+it}}\frac{\log\left(T^{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}}/p\right)}{\log\left(T^{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}}\right)}+\sum_{p\leq\log T}\frac{1/2}{p^{1+2it}}\right)dt\leq D_{1}\left(k\right)T\left(\log T\right)^{k^{2}},

where
D_{1}\left(k\right)
is a constant depending on
k
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
k\geq 1
and
T\geq e^{e^{\left(10000k\right)^{2}}}
. If the same assumptions in Lemma
2.3
are true, then for
1\leq j\leq\mathcal{I}-1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{t\in\mathcal{S}\left(j\right)}\exp\left(2k{\Re}\sum_{p\leq T^{{\beta_{j}}}}\frac{1}{p^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{{\beta_{j}}\log T}+it}}\frac{\log\left(T^{{\beta_{j}}}/p\right)}{\log\left(T^{{\beta_{j}}}\right)}+\sum_{p\leq\log T}\frac{1/2}{p^{1+2it}}\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq D_{2}\left(k\right)e^{-\beta_{j+1}^{-1}\log\left(1/\beta_{j+1}\right)/c_{4}}T\left(\log T\right)^{k^{2}},

where
D_{2}\left(k\right)
is a constant depending on
k
.

Now we prove the main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
x=T^{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}}
in Proposition
2.1
, so by Lemma
2.4
we have

\int_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}dt\leq e^{2k/{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}+2kN}D_{1}\left(k\right)T\left(\log T\right)^{k^{2}}.

Let
x=T^{{\beta_{j}}}
in Proposition
2.1
, so by Lemma
2.5
we get, for all
1\leq j\leq\mathcal{I}-1
,

\int_{t\in\mathcal{S}\left(j\right)}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}dt\leq e^{2k/{\beta_{j}}+2kN}D_{2}\left(k\right)e^{-\beta_{j+1}^{-1}\log\left(1/\beta_{j+1}\right)/c_{4}}T\left(\log T\right)^{k^{2}}.

By the restriction of
c_{4}
in Lemma
2.3
, using geometric series, we have for
1\leq j\leq\mathcal{I}-1

\sum_{j=1}^{\mathcal{I}-1}e^{2k/{\beta_{j}}}e^{-\beta_{j+1}^{-1}\log\left(1/\beta_{j+1}\right)/c_{4}}\leq 1.

Then we have

\sum_{j=1}^{\mathcal{I}-1}\int_{t\in\mathcal{S}\left(j\right)}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}dt\leq\mathcal{I}e^{2kN}D_{2}\left(k\right)T\left(\log T\right)^{k^{2}}.

When
j=0
, we have

\int_{t\in\mathcal{S}\left(0\right)}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}dt\leq\sqrt{{\rm meas}\left(\mathcal{S}\left(0\right)\right)\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{4k}dt}.

By Lemma 2 in
[
7
]
, we know for any
k\geq 1
and
s>0
, there exists a constant
D_{3}\left(k,s\right)
such that

\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}dt\leq D_{3}\left(k,s\right)T\left(\log T\right)^{k^{2}+s}.

Choose
s=64k^{2}
, then by Lemma
2.3
, when
c_{1}<100
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{t\in\mathcal{S}\left(0\right)}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}dt

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{D_{3}\left(k,s\right)M}T\sqrt{\left(\log T\right)^{65k^{2}-2\log\log T/{c_{1}}}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{D_{3}\left(k,64k^{2}\right)M}T\left(\log T\right)^{k^{2}}.

Let
D_{3}\left(k\right)=D_{3}\left(k,64k^{2}\right)
, then we have

\int_{t\in\mathcal{S}\left(0\right)}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}dt\leq\sqrt{D_{3}\left(k\right)M}T\left(\log T\right)^{k^{2}}.

Putting everything together, we have

\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}dt\leq\left(e^{2k/{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}+2kN}D_{1}\left(k\right)+\mathcal{I}e^{2kN}D_{2}\left(k\right)+\sqrt{D_{3}\left(k\right)M}\right)T\left(\log T\right)^{k^{2}}.

So, if we let

C\left(k\right)=e^{2k/{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}+2kN}D_{1}\left(k\right)+\mathcal{I}e^{2kN}D_{2}\left(k\right)+\sqrt{D_{3}\left(k\right)M},

then (
1
) is proved.

Harper proved in section 6 of
[
1
]
that
D_{1}\left(k\right)
and
D_{2}\left(k\right)
are both of size
e^{O\left(k\right)}
. We have
D_{3}\left(k\right)=O\left(k\right)
by (
3
). From the definition of
{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}
we know that
{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}\leq c_{1}e^{-c_{2}k}=O\left(e^{-c_{2}k}\right)
, hence
e^{2k/{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}}=O\left(e^{e^{c_{2}k}}\right)
, which becomes the dominating term in
C\left(k\right)
. Hence we can conclude that
C\left(k\right)=O\left(e^{e^{c_{2}k}}\right)
, which means that the size of the implicit constant depends entirely on the choice of
c_{2}
. We will determine the choice of
c_{2}
in Section 4 to finish the proof.
∎

Now we prove that
D_{3}=O\left(k^{2}\right)
. We first introduce the following theorem from
[
7
]
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis is true, let
T
be large, and
V\geq 3
. Define
S\left(T,V\right):=\Set{T\leq t\leq 2T}{\log\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|\geq V}
. Denote
\log\log\log T
as
\log_{3}T
. If
10\sqrt{\log\log T}\leq V\leq\log\log T
, then there exists
b_{1}>0
such that

{\rm meas}\left(S\left(T,V\right)\right)\leq b_{1}\cdot T\frac{V}{\sqrt{\log\log T}}\exp\left(-\frac{V^{2}}{\log\log T}\left(1-\frac{4}{\log_{3}T}\right)\right).

If
\log\log T<V\leq\frac{1}{2}\log\log T\log_{3}T
, then there exists
b_{2}>0
such that

{\rm meas}\left(S\left(T,V\right)\right)\leq b_{2}\cdot T\frac{V}{\sqrt{\log\log T}}\exp\left(-\frac{V^{2}}{\log\log T}\left(1-\frac{7V}{4\log\log T\log_{3}T}\right)^{2}\right).

If
\frac{1}{2}\log\log T\log_{3}T<V
, then there exists
b_{3}>0
such that

{\rm meas}\left(S\left(T,V\right)\right)\leq b_{3}\cdot T\exp\left(-\frac{1}{33}V\log V\right).

To find
D_{3}\left(k\right)
, firstly note that

\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}dt=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{2kV}d{\rm meas}\left(S\left(T,V\right)\right)=2k\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{2kV}{\rm meas}\left(S\left(T,V\right)\right)dV.

We can separate the integral
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{2kV}{\rm meas}\left(S\left(T,V\right)\right)dV
into four parts depending on the size of
V
. For simplicity, we denote
T_{1}=10\sqrt{\log\log T}
,
T_{2}=\log\log T
,
T_{3}=\frac{1}{2}\log\log T\log_{3}T
. Since
e^{2kV}{\rm meas}\left(S\left(T,V\right)\right)
is non-negative, we have

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{2kV}{\rm meas}\left(S\left(T,V\right)\right)dV=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{T_{1}}e^{2kV}{\rm meas}\left(S\left(T,V\right)\right)dV+\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}e^{2kV}{\rm meas}\left(S\left(T,V\right)\right)dV

\displaystyle+\int_{T_{2}}^{T_{3}}e^{2kV}{\rm meas}\left(S\left(T,V\right)\right)dV+\int_{T_{3}}^{\infty}e^{2kV}{\rm meas}\left(S\left(T,V\right)\right)dV.

For the first integral, we have

\int_{-\infty}^{T_{1}}e^{2kV}{\rm meas}\left(S\left(T,V\right)\right)dV\leq\int_{-\infty}^{T_{1}}e^{2kV}TdV=\frac{T}{2T_{1}}e^{2T_{1}k}.

When
T\geq e^{e^{\left(10000k\right)^{2}}}
,
T\geq e^{e^{400}}
, so we have
10\sqrt{\log\log T}\leq\log\log T/2
, then

\frac{T}{2T_{1}}e^{2T_{1}k}\leq Te^{\log\log Tk}=T\left(\log T\right)^{k}.

For the second integral, by Theorem
2.6
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}e^{2kV}{\rm meas}\left(S\left(T,V\right)\right)dV\leq b_{1}\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}e^{2kV}T\frac{V}{\sqrt{\log\log T}}\exp\left(-\frac{V^{2}}{\log\log T}\left(1-\frac{4}{\log_{3}T}\right)\right)dV

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{1}T\sqrt{\log\log T}\log_{3}T}{2\log T\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{\log_{3}T}}\left(\log_{3}T-4\right)}

\displaystyle\left(-k\sqrt{\pi}{\rm erf}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\log\log T}\left(4+\left(k-1\right)\log_{3}T\right)}{\sqrt{\log_{3}T-4}\sqrt{\log_{3}T}}\right)\left(\log T\right)^{1+\frac{k^{2}\log_{3}T}{\log_{3}T-4}}\sqrt{\log\log T}+\right.

\displaystyle+k\sqrt{\pi}{\rm erf}\left(\frac{40+\left(k\sqrt{\log\log T}-10\right)\log_{3}T}{\sqrt{\log_{3}T-4}\sqrt{\log_{3}T}}\right)\left(\log T\right)^{1+\frac{k^{2}\log_{3}T}{\log_{3}T-4}}\sqrt{\log\log T}

\displaystyle+\left.\left(\exp\left(20k\sqrt{\log\log T}-100+\frac{400}{\log_{3}T}\right)\log T-e^{100}\left(\log T\right)^{2k+\frac{4}{\log_{3}T}}\right)\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{\log_{3}T}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq b_{1}T\sqrt{\log\log T}\left(k\sqrt{\pi}\left(\log T\right)^{\frac{k^{2}\log_{3}T}{\log_{3}T-4}}\sqrt{\log\log T}+e^{20k\sqrt{\log\log T}}\right).

In the above inequality, the function
{\rm erf}
is the error function defined by

{\rm erf}\left(x\right)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{x}e^{-t^{2}}dt.

which is bounded by
-1
and
1
.

When
T\geq e^{e^{\left(10000k\right)^{2}}}
,
\log_{3}T>5
, hence
\frac{4k^{2}}{\log_{3}T-4}<\frac{64k^{2}}{2}
,
\log\log T<\left(\log T\right)^{64k^{2}/2}
, and
20\sqrt{\log\log T}\leq\log\log T
then we have

\displaystyle\int_{T_{1}}^{T_{2}}e^{2kV}{\rm meas}\left(S\left(T,V\right)\right)dV

\displaystyle\leq b_{1}k\sqrt{\pi}T\left(\log T\right)^{65k^{2}}+b_{1}T\sqrt{\log\log T}\left(\log T\right)^{k}

\displaystyle\leq 2b_{1}k\sqrt{\pi}T\left(\log T\right)^{65k^{2}}.

For the third integral, since on
\left[T_{2},T_{3}\right]
,
V\leq\frac{1}{2}\log\log T\log_{3}T
, so
\frac{7V}{4\log\log T\log_{3}T}\leq\frac{7}{8}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{T_{2}}^{T_{3}}e^{2kV}{\rm meas}\left(S\left(T,V\right)\right)dV\leq

\displaystyle b_{2}\int_{T_{2}}^{T_{3}}e^{2kV}T\frac{V}{\sqrt{\log\log T}}\exp\left(-\frac{V^{2}}{64\log\log T}\right)dV

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 32b_{2}T\sqrt{\log\log T}\left(\left(\log T\right)^{-\frac{1}{64}+2k}-\left(\log T\right)^{k\log_{3}T-\frac{1}{256}\left(\log_{3}T\right)^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle+8k\sqrt{\pi}\left(-{\rm erf}\left(\frac{1}{8}\left(1-64k\right)\sqrt{\log\log T}\right)\right.

\displaystyle+\left.{\rm erf}\left(\frac{1}{16}\sqrt{\log\log T}\left(-128k+\log_{3}T\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\left.\left(\log T\right)^{64k^{2}}\sqrt{\log\log T}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 256b_{2}k\sqrt{\pi}T\log\log T\left(\log T\right)^{64k^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 256b_{2}k\sqrt{\pi}T\left(\log T\right)^{65k^{2}}.

For the fourth integral, by Theorem
2.6
, we have

\int_{T_{3}}^{\infty}e^{2kV}{\rm meas}\left(S\left(T,V\right)\right)dV\leq b_{3}\int_{T_{3}}^{\infty}e^{2kV}TV^{-V/33}dV.

When
V\leq e^{33\left(2k+1\right)}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{T_{3}}^{e^{33\left(2k+1\right)}}e^{2kV}TV^{-V/33}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{b_{3}}{132k}T\left(\log\log T\log_{3}T\right)^{2}\left(\log T\right)^{k\log_{3}T}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{b_{3}}{132k}T\left(\log T\right)^{11k^{2}}.

When
V\geq e^{33\left(2k+1\right)}
, we have

e^{2kV}TV^{-V/33}\leq e^{2kV}T\left(e^{33\left(2k+1\right)}\right)^{-V/33}=Te^{-V},

hence we have

\int_{e^{33\left(2k+1\right)}}^{\infty}e^{2kV}{\rm meas}\left(S\left(T,V\right)\right)dV<b_{3}T.

Combining the above four results, we have

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2k}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2k\left(T\left(\log T\right)^{k}+2b_{1}k\sqrt{\pi}T\left(\log T\right)^{65k^{2}}+256b_{2}k\sqrt{\pi}T\left(\log T\right)^{65k^{2}}+\frac{b_{3}}{132k}T\left(\log T\right)^{11k^{2}}+b_{3}T\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(2+2b_{1}\sqrt{\pi}+512b_{2}\sqrt{\pi}+3b_{3}\right)k^{2}T\left(\log T\right)^{65k^{2}}.

So, we have

(3)

D_{3}\left(k\right)\leq\left(2+b_{1}\frac{8}{\varepsilon}\sqrt{\pi}+512b_{2}\sqrt{\pi}+3b_{3}\right)k^{2}=O\left(k^{2}\right).

3.
Proofs of the Lemmas

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Denote
G_{\left(i,\mathcal{I}\right)}\left(t\right)
as
F_{i}\left(t\right)
, then by the definition of the set
\mathcal{T}
, we know that
\left|{\Re}F_{i}\left(t\right)\right|\leq{\beta_{i}}^{-c_{3}}
for all
t\in\mathcal{T}
. Then

\int_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\exp\left(2k{\Re}\sum_{p\leq T^{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}}}\frac{1}{p^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}\log T}+it}}\frac{\log\left(T^{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}}/p\right)}{\log\left(T^{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}}\right)}\right)dt=\int_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}}\exp\left(2k{\Re}F_{i}\left(t\right)\right)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}}\left(1+O\left(e^{-100k{\beta_{i}}^{-c_{3}}}\right)\right)\left(\sum_{0\leq j\leq 100k{\beta_{i}}^{-c_{3}}}\frac{k{\Re}F_{i}\left(t\right)^{j}}{j!}\right)^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+O\left(e^{-100k{\beta_{i}}^{-c_{3}}}\right)\right)\int_{T}^{2T}\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}}\left(\sum_{0\leq j\leq 100k{\beta_{i}}^{-c_{3}}}\frac{k{\Re}F_{i}\left(t\right)^{j}}{j!}\right)^{2}dt.

For all
1\leq i\leq\mathcal{I}
and
T\leq t\leq 2T
, we have

{\Re}F_{i}\left(t\right)=\sum_{T^{\beta_{i-1}}<p\leq T^{{\beta_{i}}}}\frac{\cos\left(t\log p\right)}{p^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}\log T}}}\frac{\log\left(T^{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}}/p\right)}{\log\left(T^{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}}\right)},

so

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}}\left(\sum_{0\leq j\leq 100k{\beta_{i}}^{-c_{3}}}\frac{k{\Re}F_{i}\left(t\right)^{j}}{j!}\right)^{2}dt.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\overline{j},\overline{l}}\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}}\frac{k^{j_{i}}k^{l_{i}}}{j_{i}!l_{i}!}\sum_{\overline{p},\overline{q}}C\left(\overline{p},\overline{q}\right)\int_{T}^{2T}\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r\leq j_{i},\\
1\leq s\leq l_{i}\end{subarray}}\cos\left(t\log p\left(i,r\right)\right)\cos\left(t\log q\left(i,s\right)\right)dt

where the outer sum is over vectors
\overline{j}=\left(j_{1},\cdots,j_{\mathcal{I}}\right)
and
\overline{l}=\left(l_{1},\cdots,l_{\mathcal{I}}\right)
, whose entries are between
0
and
100k{\beta_{i}}^{-\frac{3}{4}}
, and the inner sum is over vectors
\overline{p}=\left(p\left(1,1\right),p\left(1,2\right),\cdots,p\left(1,j_{1}\right),\cdots,p\left(\mathcal{I},j_{\mathcal{I}}\right)\right)
,
\overline{q}=\left(q\left(1,1\right),\cdots,q\left(\mathcal{I},l_{\mathcal{I}}\right)\right)
, whose entries are primes, such that for all
1\leq i\leq\mathcal{I}
,

T^{\beta_{i-1}}<p\left(i,1\right),\cdots,p\left(i,j_{i}\right),q\left(i,1\right),\cdots,q\left(i,l_{i}\right)\leq T^{{\beta_{i}}},

and

C\left(\overline{p},\overline{q}\right):=\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r\leq j_{i},\\
1\leq s\leq l_{i}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p\left(i,r\right)^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}\log T}}}{\log\left(T^{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}}/p\left(i,r\right)\right)}{\log\left(T^{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}}\right)}\frac{1}{q\left(i,s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}\log T}}}{\log\left(T^{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}}/q\left(i,s\right)\right)}{\log\left(T^{{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}}\right)}.

Then we have

(4)

\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r\leq j_{i},\\
1\leq s\leq l_{i}\end{subarray}}p\left(i,r\right)q\left(i,s\right)\leq\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}}T^{{\beta_{i}}\left(j_{i}+l_{i}\right)}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}}T^{2\beta_{\mathcal{I}}^{1-c_{4}}}.

In the assumption, we have

\frac{k\left(c_{1}^{1-c_{3}}\right)^{2}}{c_{1}^{1-c_{3}}-1}e^{-c_{2}k\left(1-c_{3}\right)}<\frac{1}{4}.

Then
2{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}^{1-c_{4}}<\frac{1}{2}
. Let
\delta=\frac{1}{2}-2{\beta_{\mathcal{I}}}^{1-c_{4}}
, then

\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r\leq j_{i},\\
1\leq s\leq l_{i}\end{subarray}}p\left(i,r\right)q\left(i,s\right)\leq T^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}

For
n={p_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots{p_{r}}^{\alpha_{r}}
, define
f\left(n\right)
as: if any of
\alpha_{i}
is odd, then
f\left(n\right)=0
, and otherwise

f\left(n\right):=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{1}{2^{\alpha_{i}}}\frac{\alpha_{i}!}{\left(\alpha_{i}/2\right)!^{2}}.

Note that
f
is a multiplicative, non-negative function that is supported on squares. Then we have

\int_{T}^{2T}\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r\leq j_{i},\\
1\leq s\leq l_{i}\end{subarray}}\cos\left(t\log p\left(i,r\right)\right)\cos\left(t\log q\left(i,s\right)\right)dt=Tf\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r\leq j_{i},\\
1\leq s\leq l_{i}\end{subarray}}p\left(i,r\right)q\left(i,s\right)\right)+O\left(T^{0.1}\right).

Define

D\left(\overline{p},\overline{q}\right):=\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r\leq j_{i},\\
1\leq s\leq l_{i}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{p\left(i,r\right)}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{q\left(i,s\right)}}

then
C\left(\overline{p},\overline{q}\right)\leq D\left(\overline{p},\overline{q}\right)
. Hence

(5)

\displaystyle\int_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\exp\left(2k{\Re}F_{i}\left(t\right)\right)dt\ll

\displaystyle T\sum_{\overline{j},\overline{l}}\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}}\frac{k^{j_{i}}k^{l_{i}}}{j_{i}!l_{i}!}\sum_{\overline{p},\overline{q}}D\left(\overline{p},\overline{q}\right)f\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r\leq j_{i},\\
1\leq s\leq l_{i}\end{subarray}}p\left(i,r\right)q\left(i,s\right)\right)

(6)

\displaystyle+T^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\sum_{\overline{j},\overline{l}}\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}}\frac{k^{j_{i}}k^{l_{i}}}{j_{i}!l_{i}!}\sum_{\overline{p},\overline{q}}D\left(\overline{p},\overline{q}\right)

By counting squares, we can show that

T\sum_{\overline{j},\overline{l}}\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}}\frac{k^{j_{i}}k^{l_{i}}}{j_{i}!l_{i}!}\sum_{\overline{p},\overline{q}}D\left(\overline{p},\overline{q}\right)f\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r\leq j_{i},\\
1\leq s\leq l_{i}\end{subarray}}p\left(i,r\right)q\left(i,s\right)\right)\leq T\left(\log T\right)^{k^{2}},

and

T^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\sum_{\overline{j},\overline{l}}\prod_{i=1}^{\mathcal{I}}\frac{k^{j_{i}}k^{l_{i}}}{j_{i}!l_{i}!}\sum_{\overline{p},\overline{q}}D\left(\overline{p},\overline{q}\right)\leq T^{1-2\delta}e^{2k\mathcal{I}},

which is smaller than
T\left(\log T\right)^{k^{2}}
. Lemma
2.2
is then proved.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

The idea of proving Lemma
2.3
is similar to that of Lemma
2.2
, which gives

\displaystyle\int_{t\in\mathcal{S}\left(j\right)}\exp\left(2k\Re\sum_{p\leq T^{{\beta_{j}}}}\frac{1}{p^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{{\beta_{j}}\log T}+it}}\frac{\log\left(T^{{\beta_{j}}}/p\right)}{\log\left(T^{{\beta_{j}}}\right)}\right)dt

\displaystyle\ll\left(\mathcal{I}-j\right)\exp\left(k^{2}\sum_{p\leq T^{{\beta_{j}}}}\frac{1}{p}\right)\left(\frac{\beta_{j+1}^{1/2}}{c_{1}}\sum_{T^{{\beta_{j}}}<p\leq T^{\beta_{j+1}}}\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2/c_{1}\beta_{j+1}}.

When

\mathcal{I}-j\leq\frac{\log\left(1/{\beta_{j}}\right)}{\log c_{1}},\quad\quad\sum_{T^{bj}<p\leq T^{\beta_{j+1}}}\frac{1}{p}=\log c_{1}+o\left(1\right)\leq\frac{c_{1}}{2},

4.
Optimization

By the previous discussion, among all the parameters, only
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}
affect the implicit constant, while
c_{4}
can be written as an expression in terms of
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}
. In order to simplify the calculation, we set

a={c_{1}}^{1-c_{4}},\quad b=c_{1}\left(\frac{c_{1}}{4c_{3}-2}+1\right),

and they need to satisfy the following relations:

\frac{c_{2}}{b}>2,

\frac{ka^{2}}{a-1}e^{-ak\left(1-c_{4}\right)}<\frac{1}{4}.

Since
C\left(k\right)=O\left(e^{e^{c_{2}k}}\right)
, so the objective is to make
c_{2}
as small as possible, which, by
c_{2}/b>2
, requires
b
is as small as possible too. Consider
c_{1}
and
c_{3}
as two independent variables, and then
b
is a function with respect to
c_{1}
and
c_{3}
. There is no obvious way to determine the minimum point directly, but we can find the numerical minimum by exhibiting a table of values of
b
with different choices of
c_{1}
and
c_{3}
.

By the general setting, in order to make
\left(\beta_{j}\right)
an increasing sequence,
c_{1}>1
. In order to make
c_{4}
positive,
4c_{3}-2>0
, hence
c_{3}>\frac{1}{2}
. Also from the assumption we have
c_{3}<1
. First only consider the case
c_{1}<20
. Using mathematica, the minimum of
b
in this case is reached when
c_{1}=1.38
and
c_{3}=0.56
. If
c_{1}\geq 20
, then

b=c_{1}\left(\frac{c_{1}}{4c_{3}-2}+1\right)\geq c_{1}\left(\frac{c_{1}}{2}+1\right)\geq 220.

Clearly the minimum of
b
cannot be reached when
c_{1}\geq 20
.

So, the minimum of
b
is indeed reached when
c_{1}\approx 1.38
and
c_{3}\approx 0.56
, which gives the value of
c_{2}
as
18.63
. This shows that the minimum of the implicity constant is
e^{e^{18.63k}}
. Hence Theorem
1.1
is proved.

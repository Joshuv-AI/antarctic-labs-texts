The goal of the paper is to understand further local aspect of the analysis closely related to such Schwratz

Let
F
be a local field of characteristic zero, and
G
be an
F
-split reductive group.
Take
\rho\colon G^{\vee}({\mathbb{C}})\to{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
to be any
finite dimensional representation of the complex dual group
G^{\vee}({\mathbb{C}})
.
We denote by
\Pi_{F}(G)
the set of equivalence classes of irreducible admissible smooth representations of

G(F)
, which are of Casselman-Wallach type if

is an Archimedean local field (
[
C89
]
,
[
Wal92
]
, and also
[
SZ11
]
and
[
BeK14
]

As explained in
[
JL21
, Sections 6.1 and 6.2]

{\mathfrak{R}}_{F,G}
for
G
over
F
.
For any Archimedean local fields, the local Langlands conjecture for
G
is a theorem of
Langlands, which follows from the Langlands classification theory (
[
L89
]
). At any non-Archimedean local places, for unramified representations,
their local Langlands parameters are uniquely determined by the Satake isomorphism (
[
S63
]
and also
[
C79
]
).
The only situation that needs more discussion is that
F
is non-Archimedean and the representations of
G(F)
are ramified.

Let
{\mathcal{W}}_{F}
be the Weil group attached to
F
. The set of local Langlands parameters is denoted by

\Phi_{F}(G)
, which consists of continuous, Frobenius semisimple homomorphisms

(1.2)

\displaystyle\varsigma\colon{\mathcal{W}}_{F}\times{\mathrm{SL}}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})\longrightarrow G^{\vee},

up to conjugation by
G^{\vee}
. The local Langlands conjecture asserts that there exists a reciprocity map

(1.3)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{R}}_{F,G}\colon\Pi_{F}(G)\longrightarrow\Phi_{F}(G),

which is expected to be surjective with finite fibers, and satisfy
a series of compatibility conditions. One of the key issues is to formulate and prove the uniqueness of such local Langlands reciprocity map.

When
G={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
, it is a theorem of Harris-Taylor (
[
HT01
]
), of G. Henniart (
[
H00
]
) and of
P. Scholze (
[
Sc13
]
) that the local Langlands reciprocity map exists and is unique with compatibility of
local factors, plus other conditions.
However, such a uniqueness is not known in general. When
G
is an
F
-quasisplit classical group,
such a local Langlands reciprocity map exists due to the endoscopic classification of J. Arthur
(
[
Ar13
]
).
In their recent work (
[
FS21
]
), L. Fargues and P. Scholze use the geometrization method to understand the local Langlands conjecture. In particular, they establish a local Langlands reciprocity
map for any
F
-split reductive groups considered in this paper. More precise, Theorem I.9.6 of

[

]
asserts that for any
F
-split reductive group

map

, satisfying nine compatibility conditions. In particular
when

, the reciprocity map of Fargues and Scholze coincides with the unique one for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
.
When
G
is an
F
-quasisplit classical group, the reciprocity map of Fargues and Scholze coincides with
the one by Arthur. Although it is still not known (as far as the authors know) if the reciprocity map of Fargues and Scholze is unique, it is the most promising one towards the local Langlands conjecture in
great generality.

{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\chi_{s}^{-1})={\gamma}(\frac{1}{2},\pi\times\chi_{s},\psi)\cdot\chi_{s}

and

{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\chi_{s})(x)=(k_{\pi,\psi}*\chi_{s}^{\vee})(x),

hold for
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
after meromorphic continuation. Hence for any
\pi\in\Pi_{F}(n)
, the local Langlands
{\gamma}
-functions
{\gamma}(s,\pi\times\chi,\psi)
with any
unitary characters
\chi
of
F^{\times}
are the gamma functions in the sense of Gelfand, Graev and
Piatetski-Shapiro in
[
GGPS
]
and of Weil in
[
W66
]
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Comparing with the local theory of the Braverman-Kazhdan proposal for Langlands automorphic
L
-functions
(
[
BK00
]
), the approach we take in
[
JL21
]
and this paper has the advantage that the local theory can be
completely established based on the classical work of Godement-Jacquet (
[
GJ72
]
) and Assumption
1.2
. The existence and uniqueness of the local Langlands reciprocity map is known
for many important cases. Of course, the key point is the global theory, i.e. the
corresponding Poisson summation formula. In the global theory of the Braverman-Kazhdan proposal (
[
BK00
]
) and the formulation of B. C. Ngô (
[

]
) one has to face the singularities of the
reductive monoid associated to the pair
(G,\rho)
, while in our approach, we have to face the difficulties in
analysis in order to understand the
({\sigma},\rho)
-Schwartz space
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\sigma},\rho}({\mathbb{A}}^{\times})
and the

({\sigma},\rho)
-Fourier operator
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\sigma},\rho,\psi}
on
{\mathrm{GL}}_{1}({\mathbb{A}})
, in order to establish
Conjecture
1.1
, the
({\sigma},\rho)
-Poisson summation formula on
{\mathrm{GL}}_{1}
.

2.
\pi
-Schwartz Spaces and
\pi
-Fourier Operators

Let
F
be a local field of characteristic zero. If
F
is non-Archimedean, we denote by
{\mathfrak{o}}_{F}

the ring of the integers on
F
and by
{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathfrak{p}}_{F}
the maximal ideal of
{\mathfrak{o}}={\mathfrak{o}}_{F}
.
Let
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}:={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
be the general linear group defined over
F
. Fix the following maximal (open if
F
is non-Archimedean) compact subgroup
K
of
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle K=\begin{cases}{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{F}),&F\text{ is non-Archimedean};\\
{\mathrm{O}}(n),&F={\mathbb{R}};\\
{\mathrm{U}}(n),&F={\mathbb{C}}.\end{cases}

Fix the Haar measure
\,\mathrm{d}g=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{+}g}{|\det g|_{F}^{n}}
on
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)
where
\,\mathrm{d}^{+}g
is the measure on
{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F)
, the space of all
n\times n
matrices over
F
, induced from the standard additive measure
\,\mathrm{d}^{+}x
on
F
that is in particular self-dual with respect to a given additive character
\psi=\psi_{F}
as
fixed in
[
JL21
, §2.1]
. As usual,
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)
embeds into
{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F)
in a standard way.

Let
\Pi_{F}(n)
be the set of equivalence classes of irreducible smooth representations of
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)
when
F
is non-Archimedean;
and of irreducible Casselman-Wallach representations of
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)
when
F
is Archimedean.
Set
{\mathcal{C}}(\pi)
the space of smooth matrix coefficients attached to
\pi
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F))
be the space of Schwartz-Bruhat functions on
{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F)
. In the Archimedean case, it is the space of usual Schwartz functions. The standard Fourier transform
{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}
acting on
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F))
is defined as follows,

(2.2)

{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}(f)(x)=\int_{{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F)}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(xy))f(y)\,\mathrm{d}^{+}y.

Moreover, the standard Fourier transform
{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}
extends to a unitary operator on the space
L^{2}({\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F),\,\mathrm{d}^{+}x)
and satisfies the following identity:

(2.3)

{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\circ{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi^{-1}}={\mathrm{Id}}.

For
f\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F))
, we define

(2.4)

\displaystyle\xi_{f}(g):=|\det g|_{F}^{\frac{n}{2}}\cdot f(g)

for
g\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)
. Then we define the Schwartz space on
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)
to be

(2.5)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm std}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)):=\{\xi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))\mid\quad|\det g|^{-\frac{n}{2}}\cdot\xi(g)\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F))\}.

By
[
JL21
, Prposition 2.5]
, the Schwartz space
{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm std}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
is a subspace of
L^{2}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F),\,\mathrm{d}g)
, which is the space of square-integrable functions on
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)
.

Following the reformulation of the local theory of Godement-Jacquet in
[
JL21
, Section 2.3]
, the distribution kernel is

(2.6)

\displaystyle\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}(g):=\psi({\mathrm{tr}}g)\cdot|\det g|_{F}^{\frac{n}{2}},

and the Fourier operator
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}
is

(2.7)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}(\xi)(g):=\left(\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}*\xi^{\vee}\right)(g)

for any
\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm std}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
. From
[
JL21
, Proposition 2.6]
, we obtain a relation between
the Fourier operator
{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}
and the classical Fourier transform
{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}
:

(2.8)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}(\xi)(g)=\left(\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}*\xi^{\vee}\right)(g)=|\det g|_{F}^{\frac{n}{2}}\cdot{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}(|\det g|^{-\frac{n}{2}}\xi)(g).

From the proof of
[
JL21
, Proposition 2.6]
, it is easy to obtain that

(2.9)

\displaystyle\left(\Phi_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}*\xi^{\vee}\right)(g)=|\det g|_{F}^{\frac{n}{2}}\left(\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(\cdot))*(|\det(\cdot)|_{F}^{\frac{n}{2}}\xi)^{\vee}\right)(g)

for any
\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm std}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
.

We write, for any
\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm std}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
,
\xi(g)=|\det g|_{F}^{\frac{n}{2}}\cdot f(g)
for some
f\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F))
. The local zeta integral of Godement-Jacquet can be renormalized as

(2.10)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\xi,\varphi_{\pi},\chi)=\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\xi(g)\varphi_{\pi}(g)\chi(\det g)|\det g|_{F}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}g.

It converges absolutely for
{\mathrm{Re}}(s)
sufficiently positive and
satisfies the functional equation:

(2.11)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}(1-s,{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathrm{GJ}}}(\xi),\varphi_{\pi}^{\vee},\chi^{-1})={\gamma}(s,\pi\times\chi,\psi)\cdot{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\xi,\varphi_{\pi},\chi),

which holds as meromorphic functions in
s
, where
{\gamma}(s,\pi\times\chi,\psi)
is the local
Langlands
{\gamma}
-function associated to
\pi\in\Pi_{F}(n)
and
\chi\in{\mathfrak{X}}(F^{\times})
, the set of all quasi-characters of
F^{\times}
.

2.1.
\pi
-Schwartz functions

Consider the following determinant map:

(2.12)

\displaystyle\det={\det}_{F}\colon{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)\to F^{\times}.

It is clear that the kernel
\ker(\det)={\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(F)
. For each
x\in F^{\times}
, the fiber of the determinant map

\det
is

(2.13)

\displaystyle{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)_{x}:=\{g\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)\mid\det g=x\}.

It is clear that each fiber
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)_{x}
is an
{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(F)
-torsor. Hence one has the
{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(F)
-invariant measure
\,\mathrm{d}_{x}g
that is induced from the Haar measure
\,\mathrm{d}_{1}g

on
{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(F)
. Here
\,\mathrm{d}_{1}g
is the restriction of the Haar measure
\,\mathrm{d}g
from
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)
to
{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(F)
.

Write
\xi=|\det g|^{\frac{n}{2}}\cdot f(g)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm std}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
with some
f\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F))

as defined in (
2.5
). For
\pi\in\Pi_{F}(n)
, we denote by
{\mathcal{C}}(\pi)
the space of all matrix coefficients of
\pi
. For
\varphi_{\pi}\in{\mathcal{C}}(\pi)
, as in
[
JL21
, Section 3.1]
,
we define

(2.14)

\displaystyle\phi_{\xi,\varphi_{\pi}}(x):=\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)_{x}}\xi(g)\varphi_{\pi}(g)\,\mathrm{d}_{x}g=|x|_{F}^{\frac{n}{2}}\int_{{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)_{x}}f(g)\varphi_{\pi}(g)\,\mathrm{d}_{x}g.

By
[
JL21
, Proposition 3.2]
, the function
\phi_{\xi,\varphi_{\pi}}(x)
is absolutely convergent for all
x\in F^{\times}
and is smooth over
F^{\times}
. Following
[
JL21
, Definition 3.3]
,
for any
\pi\in\Pi_{F}(n)
, the space of
\pi
-Schwartz functions is defined by

(2.15)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}(F^{\times})={\mathrm{Span}}\{\phi=\phi_{\xi,\varphi_{\pi}}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(F^{\times})\mid\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm std}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)),\varphi_{\pi}\in{\mathcal{C}}(\pi)\}.

By
[
JL21
, Corollary 3.8]
, we have

(2.16)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(F^{\times})\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}(F^{\times})\subset{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(F^{\times}).

As in
[
JL21
, Section 3.1]
, for any
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}(F^{\times})
and a quasi-character
\chi\in{\mathfrak{X}}(F^{\times})
, define a
{\mathrm{GL}}_{1}
zeta integral
{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\phi,\chi)
associated to the pair
(\phi,\chi)
to be

(2.17)

{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\phi,\chi)=\int_{F^{\times}}\phi(x)\chi(x)|x|_{F}^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}x.

When
\phi=\phi_{\xi,\varphi_{\pi}}
for some
\xi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm std}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))
and

\varphi_{\pi}\in{\mathcal{C}}(\pi)
, we have the following identity of local zeta integrals:

(2.18)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\phi,\chi)={\mathcal{Z}}(s,\xi,\varphi_{\pi},\chi),

which holds for
{\mathrm{Re}}(s)
sufficiently large and then for all
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
by meromorphic continuation.

2.2.
\pi
-Fourier operators

As in
[
JL21
, Section 3.2]
, for
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\pi}(F^{\times})
, we define the Fourier operator

\textstyle{{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm std}({\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F))\otimes{\mathcal{C}}(\pi)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{({\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathrm{GJ}}},(\cdot)^{\vee})}

More precisely, for

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}(1-s,{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\phi),\chi^{-1})={\gamma}(s,\pi\times\chi,\psi)\cdot{\mathcal{Z}}(s,\phi,\chi),

3.
\pi
-Kernel Function: non-Archimedean case

Assume that the local field
F
is non-Archimedean. We are going to introduce a
\pi
-kernel
function

k_{\pi,\psi}(x)
on
F^{\times}
for each
\pi\in\Pi_{F}(n)
, such that the local Langlands

{\gamma}
-function
{\gamma}(s,\pi,\psi)
can be represented by the Mellin transform of

k_{\pi,\psi}(x)
. Meanwhile, we show that
{\gamma}(s,\pi,\psi)
is a gamma function in the
sense of Gelfand, Graev, and Piatetski-Shapiro in
[
GGPS
]
and of Weil in
[
W66
]
.

3.1.
Some technical lemmas

For any integer
\ell\geq 1
and
{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathfrak{p}}_{F}
,
we define
K_{\ell}={\mathrm{I}}_{n}+{\mathrm{M}}_{n}({\mathfrak{p}}^{\ell})
, the principal congruence (module
{\mathfrak{p}}^{\ell}
) subgroup of the maximal open compact subgroup

K={\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{F})
of
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)
. The set
\{K_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
forms a family of open compact neighborhoods of
{\mathrm{I}}_{n}
in
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)
.
We establish some technical lemmas, which are needed for the proof of the main result of this section.
Recall that the additive character
\psi=\psi_{F}
of
F
is taken as in
[
JL21
, Section 2.1]
, which is precisely defined as follows. Let
\varpi
be a fixed uniformizer in
{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathfrak{p}}_{F}
.
Then
\psi
is an additive character of
F
that is trivial on
{\mathfrak{o}}_{F}
, but non-trivial on
\varpi^{-1}\cdot{\mathfrak{o}}_{F}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that a positive integer
n\geq 2
and the additive character
\psi=\psi_{F}
is given as above.
Let
{\mathfrak{n}}
be any open compact subset of
F^{\times}
, and
\ell_{{\mathfrak{n}}}
be a sufficiently large integer. For any
g\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)
with
\det g\in{\mathfrak{n}}
and any
\ell>m>\ell_{\mathfrak{n}}
, assume

g\in{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(\ell,m):={\mathrm{M}}_{n}({\mathfrak{p}}^{-\ell})\backslash{\mathrm{M}}_{n}({\mathfrak{p}}^{-m})
, then
the following identity holds

\int_{h\in K^{1}_{\ell_{\mathfrak{n}}}}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(gh))\,\mathrm{d}h=0

where
K^{1}_{\ell_{\mathfrak{n}}}:=K_{\ell_{\mathfrak{n}}}\cap{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(F)
is an open compact subgroup of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(F)
.

Proof.

Up to a finite union, there will be no harm to assume that
{\mathfrak{n}}=\varpi^{\alpha}\cdot{\mathfrak{o}}_{F}^{\times}
for some integer
{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, where
\varpi
is the fixed uniformizer in
{\mathfrak{o}}_{F}
.
This implies that
g
belongs to both
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)_{{\mathfrak{n}}}=\{g\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)\mid\det g\in\varpi^{\alpha}\cdot{\mathfrak{o}}^{\times}_{F}\}
and
{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(\ell,m)
.

Consider the Cartan decomposition of
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)
with respect to the maximal open compact subgroup
K={\mathrm{G}}_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{F})
, and write

g=k_{1}tk_{2}

with
k_{1},k_{2}\in K
, and
t={\rm diag}(t_{1},...,t_{n})
with
|t_{1}|_{F}\geq|t_{2}|_{F}\geq...\geq|t_{n}|_{F}
. Since the set
{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(\ell,m)={\mathrm{M}}_{n}({\mathfrak{p}}^{-\ell})\backslash{\mathrm{M}}_{n}({\mathfrak{p}}^{-m})
is stable under the left and right
translations by
K
, we obtain that
t\in{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(\ell,m)
and
\det t\in\varpi^{\alpha}\cdot{\mathfrak{o}}^{\times}_{F}
. In particular
t_{1}\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{-\ell}\backslash{\mathfrak{p}}^{-m}
and
t_{n}\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{\lfloor\frac{{\alpha}+m}{n-1}\rfloor}
. Here
\lfloor x\rfloor
is the largest integer smaller than or equal to
x
.

In the following we may fix any
\ell_{\mathfrak{n}}>n|{\alpha}|
with
\ell>m>\ell_{\mathfrak{n}}
.

Since
K^{1}_{\ell_{0}}
is a normal subgroup of
K={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{F})
, after changing variable
h\to k_{2}^{-1}hk_{2}
, we can write

(3.1)

\displaystyle\int_{h\in K^{1}_{\ell_{0}}}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(gh))\,\mathrm{d}h=\int_{h\in K^{1}_{\ell_{0}}}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(kth))\,\mathrm{d}h=\int_{h\in K^{1}_{\ell_{0}}}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(thk))\,\mathrm{d}h

with
k=k_{2}k_{1}
.
Since
k\in K
and
\det k\in{\mathfrak{o}}_{F}^{\times}
, there exists a permutation
{\sigma}
of
\{1,...,n\}
, such that the
({\sigma}(i),i)
-th entry of
k
,

k_{{\sigma}(i),i}
belongs to
{\mathfrak{o}}^{\times}_{F}
, for
i=1,2,\cdots,n
. For such a
{\sigma}
, we are going to construct a compact subgroup
\Theta_{\sigma}
of
K^{1}_{\ell_{0}}
such that
for the
t
and
k
that are determined by
g\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)_{\mathfrak{n}}\cap{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(\ell,m)
as above and
for any
h\in K^{1}_{\ell_{0}}
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\Theta_{\sigma}}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(t\theta hk))\,\mathrm{d}\theta=0

whenever
\ell>m\geq\ell_{\mathfrak{n}}
is sufficiently large. In fact, the vanishing of the integral in (
3.2
) with the reduction in (
3.1
)
is sufficient for the lemma. More precisely, we consider the following change of variable for
h
:
for any
\theta\in\Theta_{\sigma}
,

\int_{h\in K^{1}_{\ell_{0}}}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(thk))\,\mathrm{d}h=\int_{h\in K^{1}_{\ell_{0}}}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(t\theta hk))\,\mathrm{d}h.

By integrating on the variable
\theta
over the compact subgroup
\Theta_{\sigma}
on both sides, we obtain that

{\mathrm{vol}}(\Theta_{\sigma})\cdot\int_{h\in K^{1}_{\ell_{0}}}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(thk))\,\mathrm{d}h=\int_{\Theta_{\sigma}}\int_{h\in K^{1}_{\ell_{0}}}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(t\theta hk))\,\mathrm{d}h\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

Since the double integration on the right-hand side is over compact subgroups, we are able to change the order of integrations and obtain that

{\mathrm{vol}}(\Theta_{\sigma})\cdot\int_{h\in K^{1}_{\ell_{0}}}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(thk))\,\mathrm{d}h=\int_{h\in K^{1}_{\ell_{0}}}\bigg(\int_{\Theta_{\sigma}}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(thk))\,\mathrm{d}\theta\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}h.

Hence the vanishing of the integral in (
3.2
) with the reduction in (
3.1
) implies the lemma.

Now we are going to prove the vanishing of the integral in (
3.2
), for the
t
and
k
that are determined by
g\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)_{\mathfrak{n}}\cap{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(\ell,m)
as above and
for any
h\in K^{1}_{\ell_{0}}
.

First, we consider the case that
{\sigma}(1)=1
and
{\sigma}(n)=n
(with
n\geq 2
). In this case, we look at the compact subgroup
\Theta_{\sigma}
in
K^{1}_{\ell_{0}}
consisting of diagonal elements of the form:

\theta={\rm diag}(a,1,\cdots,1,a^{-1})\in K^{1}_{\ell_{0}}

with
a\in 1+{\mathfrak{p}}^{\ell_{0}}
. Since
h\in K^{1}_{\ell_{0}}
is congruence to
{\mathrm{I}}_{n}
modulo
{\mathfrak{p}}^{\ell_{0}}
, it is straightforward to see that for the
k
that is determined by
g
as above,
we still have

(hk)_{{\sigma}(i),i}\in{\mathfrak{o}}^{\times}_{F}

for every
h\in K^{1}_{\ell_{0}}
. Then the trace
{\mathrm{tr}}(t\theta hk)
can be calculated as follows:

{\mathrm{tr}}(t\theta hk)=t_{1}a(hk)_{1,1}+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}t_{j}(hk)_{j,j}+t_{n}a^{-1}(hk)_{n,n},

with
a\in 1+{\mathfrak{p}}^{\ell_{0}}
. Since
{\sigma}(n)=n
, we have that
(hk)_{n,n}\in{\mathfrak{o}}_{F}^{\times}
. Since
t_{n}\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{\lfloor\frac{{\alpha}+m}{n-1}\rfloor}
with
\ell>m>n|{\alpha}|
large, we have
\psi(t_{n}a^{-1}(hk)_{n,n})=1
.
It follows that

\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(t\theta hk))=\psi(t_{1}a(hk)_{1,1})\psi(\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}t_{j}(hk)_{j,j}).

Hence we obtain that the integral in (
3.2
) can be written as

\int_{\Theta_{\sigma}}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(t\theta hk))\,\mathrm{d}\theta=\psi(\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}t_{j}(hk)_{j,j})\int_{1+{\mathfrak{p}}^{\ell_{0}}}\psi(t_{1}a(hk)_{1,1})\,\mathrm{d}a.

Since
(hk)_{1,1}\in{\mathfrak{o}}_{F}^{\times}
and
t_{1}\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{-\ell}\backslash{\mathfrak{p}}^{-m}
with
\ell>m
sufficiently large, if we write
a=1+x
, then we have

\int_{1+{\mathfrak{p}}^{\ell_{0}}}\psi(t_{1}a(hk)_{1,1})\,\mathrm{d}a=\psi(t_{1}(hk)_{1,1})\int_{{\mathfrak{p}}^{\ell_{0}}}\psi(t_{1}x(hk)_{1,1})\,\mathrm{d}x.

The integral on the right-hand side is zero as long as
\ell>m>\ell_{0}
, due to the choice of
\psi=\psi_{F}
. Hence the vanishing of the integral in (
3.2
) is proved in this case.

Next, we consider that case where
{\sigma}(1)=n\neq 1
and
n\geq 2
. In this case, we take the following compact subgroup
\Theta_{{\sigma}}
of
K^{1}_{\ell_{0}}
that consists of elements of the form:

\theta=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&b\\
0&{\mathrm{I}}_{n-2}&0\\
c&0&d\end{pmatrix}\in K^{1}_{\ell_{0}}.

It is clear that
b,c\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{\ell_{0}}
and
d\in 1+{\mathfrak{p}}^{\ell_{0}}
with
d-bc=1
.
The trace
{\mathrm{tr}}(t\theta hk)
can be calculated as follows:

{\mathrm{tr}}(t\theta hk)=t_{1}(hk)_{1,1}+t_{1}b(hk)_{n,1}+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}t_{j}(hk)_{j,j}+t_{n}c(hk)_{1,n}+t_{n}d(hk)_{n,n}.

Since
t_{n}\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{\lfloor\frac{{\alpha}+m}{2}\rfloor}
,
c\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{\ell_{0}}
,
d\in 1+{\mathfrak{p}}^{\ell_{0}}
, and
(hk)_{1,n},(hk)_{n,n}\in{\mathfrak{o}}_{F}
, we must have that

\psi(t_{n}c(hk)_{1,n})=\psi(t_{n}d(hk)_{n,n})=1,

with
\ell>\ell_{0}
sufficiently large. The integral in (
3.2
) can be written as

\int_{\Theta_{\sigma}}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(t\theta hk))\,\mathrm{d}\theta=\psi(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}t_{j}(hk)_{j,j})\int_{\Theta_{\sigma}}\psi(t_{1}b(hk)_{n,1})\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

Since

\begin{pmatrix}1&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
c&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&b\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

Hence we obtain that

which is zero as
(hk)_{n,1}=(hk)_{{\sigma}(1),1}\in{\mathfrak{o}}_{F}^{\times}
and
t_{1}\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{\ell}\backslash{\mathfrak{p}}^{m}
with
\ell>m>\ell_{0}
, due to the choice of
\psi=\psi_{F}
. The vanishing of the integral in (
3.2
) is proved in this case. Here the
constant
\beta
is determined by the volume of the subgroup that consists of elements of type
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
c&1\end{pmatrix}
with
c\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{\ell_{0}}
.

It is clear that the same proof can be applied to the case where
{\sigma}(n)=1\neq n
and
n\geq 2
.

Finally, we consider the case where
{\sigma}(1)=j_{0}\notin\{1,n\}
and
n\geq 2
. In this case, we take a compact subgroup
\Theta_{\sigma}
of

\theta=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&b&0&0\\
0&{\mathrm{I}}_{j_{0}-2}&0&0&0\\
c&0&1&0&0\\
0&0&0&{\mathrm{I}}_{n-j_{0}-1}&0\\
0&0&0&0&d\end{pmatrix}

with

(1-bc)d=1

The trace can be written as

It is clear that
\psi(t_{n}d(hk)_{n,n})=1
. We obtain that

\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(t\theta hk))=\psi(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}t_{j}(hk)_{j,j})\cdot\psi(t_{1}b(hk)_{j_{0},1}+t_{j_{0}}c(hk)_{1,j_{0}}).

Hence the integral in (
3.2
) can be written as

\int_{\Theta_{\sigma}}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(t\theta hk))\,\mathrm{d}\theta=\psi(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}t_{j}(hk)_{j,j})\int_{\Theta_{\sigma}}\psi(t_{1}b(hk)_{j_{0},1}+t_{j_{0}}c(hk)_{1,j_{0}})\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

Here
t_{1},t_{j_{0}}\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{-\ell}\backslash{\mathfrak{p}}^{-m}
with
\ell>m
sufficiently large,
\alpha=(hk)_{j_{0},1}\in{\mathfrak{o}}_{F}^{\times}
and
\beta=(hk)_{1,j_{0}}\in{\mathfrak{o}}_{F}

As a topological space, we have

\Theta_{\sigma}\cong\{\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
c&1\end{pmatrix}\}\times\{\begin{pmatrix}1&b\\

\int_{\Theta_{\sigma}}\psi(t_{1}b(hk)_{j_{0},1}+t_{j_{0}}c(hk)_{1,j_{0}})\,\mathrm{d}\theta=\int_{\Theta_{\sigma}^{\prime}(c)}\bigg(\int_{{\mathfrak{p}}^{\ell_{0}}}\psi(t_{1}b(hk)_{j_{0},1})\,\mathrm{d}b\bigg)\psi(t_{j_{0}}c(hk)_{1,j_{0}})\,\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}(c).

Since

\ell>m>\ell_{0}

\ell>m>\ell_{0}
are sufficiently large, due to the choice of
\psi=\psi_{F}
. The vanishing of the integral in (

,

,

We are done.

\displaystyle\int_{h\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\varphi_{\widetilde{\pi}}(h^{-1})\,\mathrm{d}h\int_{\det g=x}\bigg(\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(\cdot))*(|\det(\cdot)|^{\frac{n}{2}}_{F}{\mathfrak{c}}_{\ell})^{\vee}\bigg)(g)f_{i}(gh)\,\mathrm{d}_{x}g,\quad i=1,2.

The second step is to show that the limit as

), which can be written as follows:

\displaystyle h\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)\mapsto

\displaystyle\int_{\det g=x}\bigg(\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(\cdot))*(|\det(\cdot)|^{\frac{n}{2}}_{F}{\mathfrak{c}}_{\ell})^{\vee}\bigg)(g)f_{i}(gh)\,\mathrm{d}_{x}g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\det g=x}\int_{u\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(gu))|\det u|^{\frac{n}{2}}_{F}{\mathfrak{c}}_{\ell}(u)\,\mathrm{d}uf_{i}(gh)\,\mathrm{d}_{x}g.

Since both

and

are compactly supported, one can switch the integration order, and obtain that the function in (

) is equal to the following function

(4.17)

\displaystyle h\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)\mapsto\int_{u\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}{\mathfrak{c}}_{\ell}(u)|\det u|^{\frac{n}{2}}_{F}\,\mathrm{d}u\int_{\det g=x}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(gu))f_{i}(gh)\,\mathrm{d}_{x}g.

Hence (
4.15

(4.18)

\displaystyle\int_{h\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\varphi_{\widetilde{\pi}}(h^{-1})\,\mathrm{d}h\int_{u\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}{\mathfrak{c}}_{\ell}(u)|\det u|^{\frac{n}{2}}_{F}\,\mathrm{d}u\int_{\det g=x}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(gu))f_{i}(gh)\,\mathrm{d}_{x}g.

By absolute convergence, after switching the integration order, (

) can be re-written as

\displaystyle\int_{u\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}{\mathfrak{c}}_{\ell}(u)|\det u|^{\frac{n}{2}}_{F}\,\mathrm{d}u\int_{h\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\varphi_{\widetilde{\pi}}(h^{-1})\,\mathrm{d}h\int_{\det g=x}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(gu))f_{i}(gh)\,\mathrm{d}_{x}g.

Notice that

is a delta sequence consisting of smooth compactly supported functions tending to the delta mass supported at identity. By the discussion right after (
4.1
), in order to show that as
\ell\to\infty
the limit exists, it suffices to show that the following function

u\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)\mapsto\int_{h\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\varphi_{\widetilde{\pi}}(h^{-1})\,\mathrm{d}h\int_{\det g=x}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(gu))f_{i}(gh)\,\mathrm{d}_{x}g

is a continuous function in a compact neighborhood of identity. After changing variable, the above function becomes

(4.19)

\displaystyle u\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)\mapsto\int_{h\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}\varphi_{\widetilde{\pi}}(h^{-1}u^{-1})\,\mathrm{d}h\int_{\det g=x\det u}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(g))f_{i}(gh)\,\mathrm{d}_{x}g,

which we are going to show to be absolutely convergent whenever
u
lies in a fixed compact neighborhood of identity. By taking absolute value, we consider

\int_{h\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}|\varphi_{\widetilde{\pi}}(h^{-1}u^{-1})|\,\mathrm{d}h\cdot\bigg|\int_{\det g=x\det u}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(g))f_{i}(gh)\,\mathrm{d}_{x}g\bigg|

as a function in

near the identity
{\mathrm{I}}_{n}
.
Since the matrix coefficients
\varphi_{\widetilde{\pi}}
are smooth on
{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)
, up to a constant that depends on the compact neighborhood containing
u
, it suffices to estimate the following integral

(4.20)

\int_{h\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)}|\varphi_{\widetilde{\pi}}(h^{-1})|\,\mathrm{d}h\cdot\bigg|\int_{\det g=x}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(g))f_{i}(gh)\,\mathrm{d}_{x}g\bigg|

for

lying in a compact neighborhood of
1\in{\mathrm{G}}_{1}(F)
.

We first estimate the function from the inner integration in (
4.20
), which is given by

(4.21)

h\in{\mathrm{G}}_{n}(F)\mapsto\int_{\det g=x}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(g))f_{i}(gh)\,\mathrm{d}_{x}g,\quad i=1,2.

With the chosen section

(4.22)

(4.23)

\displaystyle(y,h_{1})\in{\mathrm{G}}_{1}(F)\rtimes{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(F)\mapsto\int_{\det g=1}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(t_{1}(x)g))f_{i}(t_{1}(x)gt_{1}(y)h_{1})\,\mathrm{d}_{1}g,\quad i=1,2.

Changing variable

\displaystyle(y,h_{1})\in{\mathrm{G}}_{1}(F)\rtimes{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(F)\mapsto

\displaystyle\int_{\det g=1}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(t_{1}(x)g))f_{i}(t_{1}(xy)gh_{1})\,\mathrm{d}_{1}g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\det g=1}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(t_{1}(x)g))\widetilde{f}_{i}(xy,gh_{1})\,\mathrm{d}_{1}g,\quad i=1,2

Now we consider the matrix coefficient

\displaystyle|\varphi_{\widetilde{\pi}}(h)|\leq\begin{cases}(1+{\mathrm{tr}}({}^{t}hh)+{\mathrm{tr}}({}^{t}h^{-1}h^{-1}))^{n_{\widetilde{\pi}}},&F={\mathbb{R}},\\

-variable. It is clear that the desired convergence of the integral in (

Lemma 4.4
.

Given any positive integer
k
, a function
\widetilde{f}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{k}_{c}({\mathrm{G}}_{1}(F)\rtimes{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(F))
and
x\in{\mathfrak{n}}
with
{\mathfrak{n}}
any compact subset of
F^{\times}
near
1
.
Then for any polynomial
P
on
{\mathrm{M}}_{n}(F)
(viewed as a real vector space) of degree smaller than or equal to
k
, there exists a constant
C_{P,{\mathfrak{n}},f}
depending only on
P,{\mathfrak{n}}
and
f
, such that the following inequality holds for any
(y,h_{1})\in{\mathrm{G}}_{1}(F)\times{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(F)
,

\bigg|P(h_{1})\cdot\int_{\det g=1}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(t_{1}(x)gh_{1}))\widetilde{f}(xy,g)\,\mathrm{d}_{1}g\bigg|\leq C_{P,{\mathfrak{n}},f}.

Proof.

By elementary calculation, there exists a linear differential operator
\frac{\partial^{P,x}}{\partial g}
, which has its degree the same as that of
P
and has constant coefficients where the constants depend on
P
and linearly on
x
, such that

P(h_{1})\cdot\psi(({\mathrm{tr}}(t_{1}(x)gh_{1})))=\frac{\partial^{P,x}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(t_{1}(x)(\cdot)h_{1}))}{\partial g}(g).

Therefore the LHS of the above inequality becomes

(4.27)

\displaystyle\int_{\det g=1}\frac{\partial^{P,x}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(t_{1}(x)(\cdot)h_{1}))}{\partial g}(g)\cdot\widetilde{f}(xy,g)\,\mathrm{d}_{1}g.

According to
[
H94
, Theorem 18.1.34]
or from basic Euclidean analysis, the adjoint linear differential operator of
\frac{\partial^{P,x}}{\partial g}
exists. Note that
[
H94
, Theorem 18.1.34]
ensures the existence of the adjoint as a pseudo differential operator. But locally the adjoint is represented by a linear differential operator by basic Euclidean analysis. Thus, the adjoint is indeed a linear differential operator. Overall, by adjunction, there exists an adjoint differential operator
\frac{{}^{t}\partial^{P,x}}{\partial g}
of the same degree as
P
, with smooth coefficients linearly depending on
x\in F^{\times}
, such that the integral in (
4.27
) is equal to

\int_{\det g=1}\psi({\mathrm{tr}}(t_{1}(x)gh_{1}))\cdot\frac{{}^{t}\partial^{P,x}\widetilde{f}(xy,\cdot)}{\partial g}(g)\,\mathrm{d}_{1}g.

Using the fact that
\widetilde{f}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{k}_{c}({\mathrm{G}}_{1}(F)\rtimes{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(F))
, the result follows.
∎

Finally, by choosing the positive integer
k
in Lemma
4.4
sufficiently large comparing to the degree of
P_{\widetilde{\pi}}(h_{1}^{-1})
as a polynomial in

the integral in (

∎

Theorem 4.6

\displaystyle(k_{\pi,\psi}*\phi_{0}^{\vee})(x)=\int_{F^{\times}}k_{\pi,\psi}(y)\phi_{0}(x^{-1}y)\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}y=\int_{F^{\times}}k_{\pi,\psi}(y)\phi_{0}^{x}(y)\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}y,

We first consider the case that

\displaystyle\int_{F^{\times}}k_{\pi,\psi}(y)\sum_{{\omega}\in{\Omega}^{\wedge}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{{\mathrm{Re}}(s)=s_{0}}{\mathcal{M}}(\phi_{0}^{x})(|\cdot|_{F}^{s}{\omega}(\cdot))|y|_{F}^{-s}{\omega}(y)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}y,

which is absolutely convergent for any

as in (
4.32

4.31
) to (
4.35
), we obtain that

(5.4)

\displaystyle(k_{\pi,\psi}*\phi_{0}^{\vee})(x)=\sum_{{\omega}\in{\Omega}^{\wedge}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{{\mathrm{Re}}(s)=s_{0}}{\mathcal{M}}(\phi_{0}^{x})(|\cdot|_{F}^{s}{\omega}(\cdot)){\gamma}(s+\frac{1}{2},\pi\times{\omega},\psi)\,\mathrm{d}s

with
s_{0}<s_{\pi}
. From the definition of the Mellin transform, we obtain that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}(\phi_{0}^{x})(|\cdot|_{F}^{s}{\omega}(\cdot))=|x|^{s}_{F}{\omega}(x){\mathcal{M}}(\phi_{0})(|\cdot|^{s}_{F}{\omega}(\cdot)).

Hence we obtain that the right-hand side of (
5.4
) is equal to

(5.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{{\omega}\in{\Omega}^{\wedge}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{{\mathrm{Re}}(s)=s_{0}}{\mathcal{M}}(\phi_{0})(|\cdot|_{F}^{s}{\omega}(\cdot)){\gamma}(s+\frac{1}{2},\pi\times{\omega},\psi)|x|^{s}_{F}{\omega}(x)\,\mathrm{d}s.

By definition, we have

{\mathcal{M}}(\phi_{0})(|\cdot|_{F}^{s}{\omega}(\cdot))={\mathcal{Z}}(s+\frac{1}{2},\phi_{0},{\omega}).

Then we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}(\phi_{0})(|\cdot|_{F}^{s}{\omega}(\cdot)){\gamma}(s+\frac{1}{2},\pi\times{\omega},\psi)

\displaystyle={\gamma}(s+\frac{1}{2},\pi\times{\omega},\psi){\mathcal{Z}}(s+\frac{1}{2},\phi_{0},{\omega})

\displaystyle={\mathcal{Z}}(\frac{1}{2}-s,{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\phi_{0}),{\omega}^{-1}).

The last identity uses the functional equation in (
2.24
), which comes from
[
JL21
, Theorem 3.10]
. Similarly, we have

{\mathcal{Z}}(\frac{1}{2}-s,{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\phi_{0}),{\omega}^{-1})={\mathcal{M}}({\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\phi_{0}))(|\cdot|^{-s}{\omega}^{-1}).

When
s_{0}<s_{\pi}
, (
5.5
) is equal to

\sum_{{\omega}\in{\Omega}^{\wedge}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{{\mathrm{Re}}(s)=s_{0}}{\mathcal{M}}({\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\phi_{0}))(|\cdot|^{-s}{\omega}^{-1})|x|^{s}_{F}{\omega}(x)\,\mathrm{d}s={\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\phi_{0})(x),

according to Mellin inversion formula in
[
JL21
, Theorem 2.3]
. Therefore, we obtain

{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\phi_{0})(x)=(k_{\pi,\psi}*\phi_{0}^{\vee})(x)

as functions on
F^{\times}
. This proves the theorem for the Archimedean case.

For the non-Archimedean case, the only difference is the expression of the Mellin inversion formula for

\phi_{0}^{x}
. In the Archimedean case, it was given in (
4.31
), while the non-Archimedean case is given
by

(5.6)

\displaystyle\phi_{0}^{x}(y)=\sum_{{\omega}\in{\Omega}^{\wedge}}\bigg({\mathrm{Res}}_{s=0}({\mathcal{M}}(\phi_{0}^{x})(|\cdot|_{F}^{s}{\omega}(\cdot))|x|_{F}^{-s}q^{s})\bigg){\omega}(\mathrm{ac}(x))^{-1}.

Note that for a given function
\phi_{0}^{x}(y)
, the summation over
{\omega}\in{\Omega}^{\wedge}
only takes finitely many characters
{\omega}
. It is clear now that the proof for the Archimedean case
works in the same way for the non-Archimedean case. We omit the details here.
∎

5.2.
Homogeneous distributions on
{\mathrm{GL}}_{1}
and local
{\gamma}
-functions

Let
\chi_{s}(x)=\chi(x)\cdot|x|_{F}^{s}
be a quasi-character of
F^{\times}
with
\chi
a unitary
character of
F^{\times}
and
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
. One may regard
\chi_{s}
as a distribution (generalized function)
on
F^{\times}
by defining

(5.7)

\displaystyle(\chi_{s},\phi_{0}):=\int_{F^{\times}}\phi_{0}(x)\chi_{s}(x)\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}x

for any
\phi_{0}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(F^{\times})
. As usual, one defines the Fourier transform of the
distribution
\chi_{s}
by

(5.8)

\displaystyle({\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\chi_{s}),\phi_{0}):=(\chi_{s},{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\phi_{0}))

for any
\phi_{0}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(F^{\times})
. We may compute the distribution

{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\chi_{s}^{-1})
as follows.

\displaystyle({\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\chi_{s}^{-1}),\phi_{0})

\displaystyle=(\chi_{s}^{-1},{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\phi_{0}))

\displaystyle=\int_{F^{\times}}{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\phi_{0})(x)\chi_{s}^{-1}(x)\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}x

\displaystyle={\mathcal{Z}}(\frac{1}{2}-s,{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\phi_{0}),\chi^{-1}).

As in the proof of Theorem
5.1
, by using the functional equation in (
2.24
),
which comes from
[
JL21
, Theorem 3.10]
, we obtain that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}(\frac{1}{2}-s,{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\phi_{0}),\chi^{-1})

\displaystyle={\mathcal{Z}}(s+\frac{1}{2},\phi_{0},\chi)\cdot{\gamma}(\frac{1}{2},\pi\times\chi_{s},\psi)

\displaystyle=(\chi_{s},\phi_{0})\cdot{\gamma}(\frac{1}{2},\pi\times\chi_{s},\psi).

Hence we obtain that

({\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\chi_{s}^{-1}),\phi_{0})=(\chi_{s},\phi_{0})\cdot{\gamma}(\frac{1}{2},\pi\times\chi_{s},\psi)

for all
\phi_{0}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(F^{\times})
, which holds for all
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
by meromorphic continuation. Therefore, we deduce that
as distributions on
F^{\times}
, the following functional equation

(5.9)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\chi_{s}^{-1})={\gamma}(\frac{1}{2},\pi\times\chi_{s},\psi)\cdot\chi_{s}

holds for
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
after meromorphic continuation.

Theorem 5.2
.

For any
\pi\in\Pi_{F}(n)
, the local Langlands
{\gamma}
-functions
{\gamma}(s,\pi\times\chi,\psi)
with any
unitary characters
\chi
of
F^{\times}
are the gamma functions in the sense of Gelfand, Graev and
Piatetski-Shapiro in
[
GGPS
]
and of Weil in
[
W66
]
, i.e.

{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\chi_{s}^{-1})={\gamma}(\frac{1}{2},\pi\times\chi_{s},\psi)\cdot\chi_{s}

holds for
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
after meromorphic continuation. Moreover, the Fourier transform

{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\chi_{s})
of the homogeneous distribution
\chi_{s}
on
F^{\times}
can be expressed
as a convolution integral with
k_{\pi,\psi}(x)
as the kernel function:

{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\chi_{s})(x)=(k_{\pi,\psi}*\chi_{s}^{\vee})(x),

which holds for
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
after meromorphic continuation.

Proof.

By Theorems
3.6
and
4.6
, we have

\displaystyle(k_{\pi,\psi}*\chi_{s})(x)

\displaystyle=\int^{\mathrm{pv}}_{F^{\times}}k_{\pi,\psi}(y)\chi_{s}(y^{-1}x)\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}y=\chi_{s}(x)\int^{\mathrm{pv}}_{F^{\times}}k_{\pi,\psi}(y)\chi_{s}(y^{-1})\,\mathrm{d}^{\times}y

\displaystyle=\chi_{s}(x)\cdot(k_{\pi,\psi}*\chi_{s})(1)=\chi_{s}(x)\cdot{\gamma}(\frac{1}{2},\pi\times\chi_{s},\psi)={\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\chi_{s}^{-1})(x).

Hence we obtain the desired identity:

{\mathcal{F}}_{\pi,\psi}(\chi_{s})(x)=(k_{\pi,\psi}*\chi_{s}^{\vee})(x),

which holds for
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
after meromorphic continuation. Note that
\chi_{s}^{-1}(x)=\chi_{s}^{\vee}(x)
.
∎

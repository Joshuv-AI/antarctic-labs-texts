Full analytical solution for the magnetic field of uniformly magnetized cylinder tiles
F. Slanovc, M. Ortner, M. Moridi, C. Abert, D. Suess

Abstract
We present an analytical solution for the magnetic field of a homogeneously magnetized cylinder tile and by extension solutions for full cylinders, rings, cylinder sectors and ring segments. The derivation is done by direct integration in the magnetic surface charge picture. Results are closed-form expressions and elliptic integrals. All special cases are treated individually, which enables the field computation for all possible position arguments 𝐫 ∈ ℝ 3 {\bf r}\in\mathbb{R}^{3} . An implementation is provided in Python together with a performance analysis. The implementation is tested against numerical solutions and applied to compute the magnetic field in a discrete Halbach cylinder.

2.1
Field calculation with magnetostatic potential

In
[
56
, Sec. 5.9 C]
, the scalar magnetic potential
\Phi_{M}
is derived for volumes
V
with homogeneous magnetization
{\bf M}
in the absence of electric currents,

\displaystyle\Phi_{M}({\bf r})=\frac{1}{4\pi}\oint_{\partial V}\frac{\sigma({\bf r}^{\prime})}{\left|{\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime}\right|}\ \text{d}A^{\prime},

(2)

with the magnetic surface charge
\sigma
given by the inner product of the magnetization vector with the surface normal vector
{\bf n}
,

\displaystyle\sigma({\bf r}^{\prime})={\bf M}({\bf r}^{\prime})\cdot{\bf n}({\bf r}^{\prime}).

(3)

The magnetic field
{\bf H}
is then given by

\displaystyle{\bf H}({\bf r})=-\boldsymbol{\nabla}\Phi_{M}({\bf r})=\frac{1}{4\pi}\oint_{\partial V}\frac{\sigma({\bf r}^{\prime})({\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime})}{\left|{\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime}\right|^{3}}\ \text{d}A^{\prime}.

(4)

2.2
Magnetic surface-charge density

The tile boundary
\partial V
includes three types of surfaces. Each type is denoted by its normal vector, see Fig.
1(a)
. We calculate the magnetic surface charge density
\sigma
according to Eq. (
3
) in cylindrical coordinates for each surface type.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sigma_{r_{i}}(\varphi^{\prime})&=(-1)^{i}{\bf M}\cdot\left(\cos{\varphi^{\prime}}{\bf e}_{x}+\sin{\varphi^{\prime}}{\bf e}_{y}\right)\\
&=(-1)^{i}M\sin{\theta_{M}}\left(\cos{\varphi_{M}}\cos{\varphi^{\prime}}+\sin{\varphi_{M}}\sin{\varphi^{\prime}}\right)\\
&=(-1)^{i}M\sin{\theta_{M}}\cos(\varphi_{M}-\varphi^{\prime})\end{split}

(5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sigma_{\varphi_{j}}&=(-1)^{j}{\bf M}\cdot\left(-\sin{\varphi_{j}}{\bf e}_{x}+\cos{\varphi_{j}}{\bf e}_{y}\right)\\
&=(-1)^{j}M\sin{\theta_{M}}\left(-\cos{\varphi_{M}}\sin{\varphi_{j}}+\sin{\varphi_{M}}\cos{\varphi_{j}}\right)\\
&=(-1)^{j}M\sin{\theta_{M}}\sin(\varphi_{M}-\varphi_{j})\end{split}

(6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sigma_{z_{k}}&=(-1)^{k}{\bf M}\cdot{\bf e}_{z}\\
&=(-1)^{k}M\cos{\theta_{M}}\end{split}

(7)

It will be useful for our derivation to omit magnetization and powers of
(-1)

\displaystyle\sigma_{r_{i}}^{\prime}(\varphi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sin{\theta_{M}}\cos(\varphi_{M}-\varphi^{\prime}),

(8)

\displaystyle\sigma_{\varphi_{j}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sin{\theta_{M}}\sin(\varphi_{M}-\varphi_{j}),

(9)

\displaystyle\sigma_{z_{k}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\cos{\theta_{M}}.

2.3
Surface integrals

To express the the surface integrals (
4
) in cylindrical coordinates, we use the usual transformations,

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=r\cos{\varphi},

(11)

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=r\sin{\varphi},

(12)

\displaystyle{\bf e}_{r}

\displaystyle=\cos\varphi\ {\bf e}_{x}+\sin\varphi\ {\bf e}_{y},

(13)

\displaystyle{\bf e}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=-\sin\varphi\ {\bf e}_{x}+\cos\varphi\ {\bf e}_{y}.

(14)

To simplify the notation, we do not explicitly write the dependency of the coordinates
r,\varphi
and
z
, although almost every term depends on them. With the abbreviation

\displaystyle\xi(r^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime},z^{\prime}):=\left|{\bf r}-{\bf r}^{\prime}\right|=\sqrt{r^{2}+r^{\prime 2}-2rr^{\prime}\cos(\varphi-\varphi^{\prime})+(z-z^{\prime})^{2}},

(15)

every field component can be expressed as a sum of six terms, each originating from one tile surface, as

\displaystyle\begin{split}H_{r}=\frac{M}{4\pi}\Bigg(&\sum_{i=1}^{2}(-1)^{i}\int_{\varphi_{1}}^{\varphi_{2}}\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}\underbrace{\frac{\sigma_{r_{i}}^{\prime}(\varphi^{\prime})(r-r_{i}\cos(\varphi-\varphi^{\prime}))}{\xi(r_{i},\varphi^{\prime},z^{\prime})^{3}}r_{i}}_{=:H_{r,r_{i}}(\varphi^{\prime},z^{\prime})}\ \text{d}z^{\prime}\text{d}\varphi^{\prime}+\\
&\sum_{j=1}^{2}(-1)^{j}\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}\underbrace{\frac{\sigma_{\varphi_{j}}^{\prime}(r-r^{\prime}\cos(\varphi-\varphi_{j}))}{\xi(r^{\prime},\varphi_{j},z^{\prime})^{3}}}_{=:H_{r,\varphi_{j}}(r^{\prime},z^{\prime})}\ \text{d}z^{\prime}\text{d}r^{\prime}+\\
&\sum_{k=1}^{2}(-1)^{k}\int_{\varphi_{1}}^{\varphi_{2}}\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\underbrace{\frac{\sigma_{z_{k}}^{\prime}(r-r^{\prime}\cos(\varphi-\varphi^{\prime}))}{\xi(r^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime},z_{k})^{3}}r^{\prime}}_{=:H_{r,z_{k}}(r^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})}\ \text{d}r^{\prime}\text{d}\varphi^{\prime}\Bigg)\end{split}

(16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}H_{\varphi}=\frac{M}{4\pi}\Bigg(&\sum_{i=1}^{2}(-1)^{i}\int_{\varphi_{1}}^{\varphi_{2}}\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}\underbrace{\frac{\sigma_{r_{i}}(\varphi^{\prime})r_{i}^{2}\sin(\varphi-\varphi^{\prime})}{\xi(r_{i},\varphi^{\prime},z^{\prime})^{3}}}_{=:H_{\varphi,r_{i}}(\varphi^{\prime},z^{\prime})}\ \text{d}z^{\prime}\text{d}\varphi^{\prime}+\\
&\sum_{j=1}^{2}(-1)^{j}\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}\underbrace{\frac{\sigma_{\varphi_{j}}r^{\prime}\sin(\varphi-\varphi_{j})}{\xi(r^{\prime},\varphi_{j},z^{\prime})^{3}}}_{=:H_{\varphi,\varphi_{j}}(r^{\prime},z^{\prime})}\ \text{d}z^{\prime}\text{d}r^{\prime}+\\
&\sum_{k=1}^{2}(-1)^{k}\int_{\varphi_{1}}^{\varphi_{2}}\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\underbrace{\frac{\sigma_{z_{k}}r^{\prime 2}\sin(\varphi-\varphi^{\prime})}{\xi(r^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime},z_{k})^{3}}}_{=:H_{\varphi,z_{k}}(r^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})}\ \text{d}r^{\prime}\text{d}\varphi^{\prime}\Bigg)\end{split}

(17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}H_{z}=\frac{M}{4\pi}\Bigg(&\sum_{i=1}^{2}(-1)^{i}\int_{\varphi_{1}}^{\varphi_{2}}\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}\underbrace{\frac{\sigma_{r_{i}}(\varphi^{\prime})(z-z^{\prime})}{\xi(r_{i},\varphi^{\prime},z^{\prime})^{3}}r_{i}}_{=:H_{z,r_{i}}(\varphi^{\prime},z^{\prime})}\ \text{d}z^{\prime}\text{d}\varphi^{\prime}+\\
&\sum_{j=1}^{2}(-1)^{j}\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}\underbrace{\frac{\sigma_{\varphi_{j}}(z-z^{\prime})}{\xi(r^{\prime},\varphi_{j},z^{\prime})^{3}}}_{=:H_{z,\varphi_{j}}(r^{\prime},z^{\prime})}\ \text{d}z^{\prime}\text{d}r^{\prime}+\\
&\sum_{k=1}^{2}(-1)^{k}\int_{\varphi_{1}}^{\varphi_{2}}\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}\underbrace{\frac{\sigma_{z_{k}}(z-z_{k})}{\xi(r^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime},z_{k})^{3}}r^{\prime}}_{=:H_{z,z_{k}}(r^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})}\ \text{d}r^{\prime}\text{d}\varphi^{\prime}\Bigg)\end{split}

(18)

In the following sections, we present the analytical solution of the above nine surface integrals.
In our derivations below, we denote the solution to the above integrals using upper indices, e.g.

\displaystyle\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}H_{r,r_{i}}(\varphi^{\prime},z^{\prime})\text{d}z^{\prime}=H_{r,r_{i}}^{z_{2}}(\varphi^{\prime})-H_{r,r_{i}}^{z_{1}}(\varphi^{\prime})

(19)

which allows us to express the final solution in a very compact manner,

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}=\frac{M}{4\pi}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{2}(-1)^{i+j+k}\big(H_{\alpha,r_{i}}^{\varphi_{j},z_{k}}+H_{\alpha,\varphi_{j}}^{r_{i},z_{k}}+H_{\alpha,z_{k}}^{r_{i},\varphi_{j}}\big)

(20)

for
\alpha\in\{r,\varphi,z\}
.

Numerical verification

5.1
Implementation and performance

The analytical formulas are lengthy and tedious to implement numerically.

7
Conclusion & outlook

In this work, we have presented fully analytical expressions for the magnetic field of a homogeneously magnetized cylinder tile. The solution is implemented in Python and validated against numerical computations. The usefulness of this work is demonstrated by computing the field in a discrete Halbach cylinder.

While the provided formula set is complete from a mathematical point of view, floating point based implementations still suffer from numerical instabilities in the vicinity of special cases, indeterminate forms and at large distances. To address this problem, methods of reformulation and series expansion are planned to provide a higher level of stability.

By including the code in the open-source Python package Magpylib, the benefits of this work are also immediately accessible to the general public. Future work is dedicated to improve the Magpylib implementation work flow, stability and computation speed.

Appendix A
How to avoid integration singularities

A.1
Motivation

It is not surprising if singularities appear in the magnetic field
{\bf H}
when crossing magnetically charged surfaces. However, it is also possible that single anti-derivatives contributing to the total field Eq.
20
have singularities off-surface. As the field itself must be continuous, these singularities cancel by summation with the singularities of other anti-derivatives.

For example, if we consider Eq. (
3
) for the special case
z=z_{k},r>0
, the term has a logarithmic singularity at
\varphi=\varphi^{\prime}
if
r>r_{i}
, which disappears after the following integration step.
In contrast, for the case
r=r_{i}
in Eq. (
3
), the denominator vanishes for
\overline{\varphi}^{\prime}\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
and reveals a significant singularity that persists even after integration in the case
\overline{\varphi}_{j}\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
, making straight-forward evaluation of this term impossible.

It is crucial to understand how to eliminate the occurrence of these singularities, especially in the second integration step. Our study reveals that they are the result of poorly chosen integration constants by the computer algebra systems.
How this happens and how to avoid it will be shown in the following sections.

Appendix
A.5
).

A.2
Notation

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+2}
be a domain for the function

\displaystyle f:

\displaystyle A\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle(x,y,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\longmapsto f(x,y,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})

in two variables
x,y
and
n
additional parameters
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
. We are looking for an anti-derivative
F:A\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\partial_{x}\partial_{y}F=f=\partial_{y}\partial_{x}F
. It exists if
f
is continuous and bounded, then
F
does exist in the whole domain
A
but is not unique. Suppose we compute
F
in two steps (e.g. using a computer algebra system):

•

calculating an
x
-anti-derivative
F_{x}
of
f

•

calculating an
y
-anti-derivative
F_{x,y}
of
F_{x}

The order of integration is mathematically irrelevant, but it should be noted that it can lead to different expressions when using computer algebra systems. This is demonstrated and explained in the following sections.

A.3
Singularities in the integration constants

Since in the calculation of
F_{x}=\int f\text{ d}x
all variables except
x
are fixed, the integration constant can be an arbitrary function of
(y,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
. This constant may have any number of singularities in
A
, so
F_{x}
with integration constant may have a restricted domain
A^{\prime}\subset A
. The same problem may arise in the second integration step, the integration of
F_{x}
with integration constant by
y
. Again, another integration constant may occur depending on
(x,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
with possible singularities in the result.

When integrating, computer algebra systems attempt to choose good integration constants. This possibility, together with the ambition to write results in “simple” and “closed” form, can result in adding singular integration constants. These unwanted singularities are hidden in the resulting deceptively simple looking expressions. We illustrate this with examples in the following subsections.

A.4
Example 1

The function

\displaystyle f(x,p):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{x^{2}+p}}

on the domain
\{(x,p)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\big|p\geq 0,(x,p)\neq(0,0)\}
is continuous and bounded if one removes a small region around
(0,0)
, so the anti-derivative
F
must exist for all
p
in the domain. The integration of
f
by
x
can lead to one of the following three anti-derivatives in computer algebra systems:

\displaystyle F_{x,1}(x,p)

\displaystyle=\log\left(\sqrt{x^{2}+p}+x\right)

\displaystyle F_{x,2}(x,p)

\displaystyle=-\log\left(\sqrt{x^{2}+p}-x\right)

\displaystyle F_{x,3}(x,p)

\displaystyle=\artanh{\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^{2}+p}}\right)}

Mathematica version 10.1
[
62
]
for instance produces
F_{x,1}
with its standard integrator. The additionally loaded Rubi integrator
[
63
]
results in
F_{x,3}
.

It is easy to see that all three functions are well-defined in the case
p>0
for all
x
. However, for
p=0
we see that
F_{x,1}
is defined only for
x>0
,
F_{x,2}
is defined only for
x<0
and
F_{x,3}
is not defined for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
since the domain of
\artanh
is
(-1,1)
. Further computations show that the three possible outputs of a computer algebra system, although looking different at first glance, differ only by a
p
-dependent integration constant, which has a singularity at
p=0
, i.e.
F_{x,1}(x,p)-F_{x,2}(x,p)=\log(p)
and
F_{x,3}(x,p)-F_{x,2}(x,p)=\log(p)/2
. As described above, we observe an implicit integration constant that reduces the domain for which our anti-derivative can be used. The automatic simplification of the term to known mathematical function as
\log,\artanh
covers this problem.

A.5
Example 2

We now study an example function similar to
H_{\varphi,r_{i}}
in Eq. (
17
) and its anti-derivative in Eq. (
3
):

\displaystyle f(\varphi,z):=\frac{\sin\varphi}{\sqrt{1-\cos\varphi+z^{2}}^{3}}

on the domain

\displaystyle\{(\varphi,z)\in[0,2\pi)\times\mathbb{R}\big|(\varphi,z)\neq(0,0)\}.

(62)

For
z\neq 0
, the function term is obviously well-defined for all
\varphi
, since
(1-\cos\varphi)\geq 0
. For
z=0
, however, L’Hospital’s rule reveals significant singularities,

\displaystyle\lim_{\varphi\rightarrow 0}\frac{\sin\varphi}{\sqrt{1-\cos\varphi}^{3}}=\lim_{\varphi\rightarrow 0}\frac{\cos\varphi}{3/2\sqrt{1-\cos\varphi}\sin\varphi}=\frac{1}{0}.

An anti-derivative of
f
by
z
is given by

\displaystyle F_{z,1}(\varphi,z)=\frac{z\sin\varphi}{(1-\cos\varphi)\sqrt{1-\cos\varphi+z^{2}}},

however, the additional factor
(1-\cos\varphi)
in the denominator also generates a pole for
z\neq 0
at
\varphi=0
, which again can be seen with L’Hospital’s rule,

\displaystyle\lim_{\varphi\rightarrow 0}\frac{\sin\varphi}{1-\cos\varphi}=\lim_{\varphi\rightarrow 0}\frac{\cos\varphi}{\sin\varphi}=\frac{1}{0}.

Thus
F_{z.1}
can be used only for the case
\varphi\neq 0
. The same applies to the following anti-derivation by
\varphi

\displaystyle F_{z,\varphi,1}(\varphi,z)=-2\artanh\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{1-\cos\varphi+z^{2}}}\right),

which is again undefined in the case
\varphi=0
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}
. In order to obtain a proper anti-derivative also for the case
\varphi=0
, we first note that our goal is to calculate definite integrals of the form

\displaystyle\int_{\varphi_{1}}^{\varphi_{2}}\int_{z_{1}}^{z_{2}}f(\varphi,z)\text{ d}z\text{ d}\varphi.

It is justified to restrict our domain of interest to a rectangular subset of Eq. (
62
). That is, if
\varphi=0
lies in the interval
[\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}]
, then we can assume that
z=0
is not in
[z_{1},z_{2}]
since
(0,0)
is excluded from the domain, and moreover that
z_{1},z_{2}
are either both positive or both negative, see Fig.
5
.

Figure 5
:
The upper rectangle represents the integration area
[\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}]\times[z_{1},z_{2}]
. In this case,
z_{1}
could be moved below the horizontal axis without leaving the grayed region, i.e. the sign of
z_{1}
could also be changed. However, the lower rectangle would intersect the point
(0,0)
if the upper boundary
z_{2}
is moved to the positive part of the vertical axis, since
\varphi^{\prime}_{1}=0
.

This argument allows us to add to
F_{z,1}
a sign-dependent integration constant that also depends on
\varphi
and compensates the pole for
\varphi=0
. We define

\displaystyle F_{z,2}(\varphi,z):=\frac{\sin\varphi}{1-\cos\varphi}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{1-\cos\varphi+z^{2}}}-\sign z\right),

where the term in parentheses vanishes for
\varphi=0
and thus compensates the pole of the prefactor. Integration of this function by
\varphi
gives

\displaystyle F_{z,\varphi,2}(\varphi,z)=-2\sign z\log\left(\sqrt{1-\cos\varphi+z^{2}}+|z|\right).

which has no singularity in the case
\varphi=0,z\neq 0
any more, and can be used to compute the definite integral when
\varphi_{1}=0
or
\varphi_{2}=0
.

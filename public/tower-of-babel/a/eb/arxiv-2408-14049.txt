A general theory for the ( s , p ) (s,p) -superposition of nonlinear fractional operators
Serena Dipierro, Edoardo Proietti Lippi, Caterina Sportelli and Enrico Valdinoci

Abstract
. We consider the continuous superposition of operators of the form ∬ [ 0 , 1 ] × ( 1 , N ) ( − Δ ) p s ​ u ​ 𝑑 μ ​ ( s , p ) , \iint_{[0,1]\times(1,N)}(-\Delta)_{p}^{s}\,u\,d\mu(s,p), where μ \mu denotes a signed measure over the set [ 0 , 1 ] × ( 1 , N ) [0,1]\times(1,N) , joined to a nonlinearity satisfying a proper subcritical growth. The novelty of the paper relies in the fact that, differently from the existing literature, the superposition occurs in both s s and p p . Here we introduce a new framework which is so broad to include, for example, the scenarios of the finite sum of different (in both s s and p p ) Laplacians, or of a fractional p p -Laplacian plus a p p -Laplacian, or even combinations involving some fractional Laplacians with the “wrong” sign. The development of this new setting comes with two applications, which are related to the Weierstrass Theorem and a Mountain Pass technique. The results obtained contribute to the existing literature with several specific cases of interest which are entirely new.

In our setting, when

The exact value of the positive normalizing constant

is not important for us, except for allowing
a consistent setting for the limit cases, namely that

However, one can take

see e.g.

In this setting, we consider a bounded open set

Further comments and remarks

The study of problems (

\mbox{there exists\penalty\ $(\overline{s},\overline{p})\in(0,1]\times(1,N)$ such that }\mu^{+}\Big([\overline{s},1]\times[\overline{p},N)\Big)>0.

Also,

Due to the wide generality of our setting, things get more complicated when addressing the issue of the functional space being uniformly convex or not. To not weigh down too much this introduction, we refer the interested reader to the discussion handled in Open Problem

However, the difficulty related to the possible lack of uniform convexity (or reflexivity)

We introduce the following notation:

Let

We show instead that (

The function

Proof.

The triangle inequality and the absolute homogeneity follow from the fact

.

Moreover, let

We show that this entails that

.

and every measurable function
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
with
u=0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
,

[u]_{s,p}\leqslant C\,[u]_{S,p}.

We are now in the position to provide the desired embedding result.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
s_{\sharp}
and
p_{\sharp}
be given by (
2.2
). Then,

•

if
s_{\sharp}=1
, then there exists
C=C(p_{\sharp},N,\Omega,\mu^{+})>0

such that, for any
u\in\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
,

\|u\|_{W_{0}^{1,p_{\sharp}}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\,\|u\|_{\mu},

namely the space
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
W_{0}^{1,p_{\sharp}}(\Omega)
;

•

if
s_{\sharp}\in[\overline{s},1)
, then there exists
C=C(s_{\sharp},p_{\sharp},N,\Omega,\mu^{+})>0
such that, for any
u\in\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
,

\|u\|_{L^{(p_{\sharp})^{*}_{s_{\sharp}}}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\,\|u\|_{\mu},

namely the space
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
L^{(p_{\sharp})^{*}_{s_{\sharp}}}(\Omega)
.

In addition, if there exists
(s_{1},p_{1})\in(0,1)\times(1,N)
such that
\mu^{+}([s_{1},1)\times\{p_{1}\})>0
, then there exists
C=C(s_{1},p_{1},N,\Omega,\mu^{+})>0
such that, for any
u\in\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
,

\|u\|_{W^{s_{1},p_{1}}_{0}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\,\|u\|_{\mu},

namely the space
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
W_{0}^{s_{1},p_{1}}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

We assume first that
s_{\sharp}=1
. By (
2.2
) we see that

\mbox{there exists\penalty\ $\eta>0$ such that }\mu^{+}(\{1\}\times[p_{\sharp},N-\eta])>0.

Then, from this and (
2.1
) we deduce that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\iint_{\Sigma}[u]_{s,p}\,d\mu^{+}(s,p)

\displaystyle\geqslant\iint_{\{1\}\times[p_{\sharp},N-\eta]}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\,d\mu^{+}(s,p)

\displaystyle\geqslant\iint_{\{1\}\times[p_{\sharp},N-\eta]}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{\sharp}}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p_{\sharp}}(\Omega)}\,d\mu^{+}(s,p)

\displaystyle=\iint_{\{1\}\times[p_{\sharp},N-\eta]}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{\sharp}}}\|u\|_{W_{0}^{1,p_{\sharp}}(\Omega)}\,d\mu^{+}(s,p).

We observe that
|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{\sharp}}}
is bounded for
p\in[p_{\sharp},N-\eta]
,
namely

0<\min_{p\in[p_{\sharp},N-\eta]}\,|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{\sharp}}}\leqslant\max_{p\in[p_{\sharp},N-\eta]}\,|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{\sharp}}}.

Hence,

\|u\|_{\mu}\geqslant\min_{p\in[p_{\sharp},N-\eta]}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p_{\sharp}}}\,\mu^{+}\Big(\{1\}\times[p_{\sharp},N-\eta]\Big)\|u\|_{W_{0}^{1,p_{\sharp}}(\Omega)},

which provides the desired result when
s_{\sharp}=1
.

Let us now focus on the case
s_{\sharp}\in[\overline{s},1)
.
By (
2.2
), there exist
\delta
,
\eta>0
such that

\mu^{+}([s_{\sharp},1-\delta]\times[p_{\sharp},N-\eta])>0.

Then, since
p^{*}_{s}\geqslant(p_{\sharp})^{*}_{s_{\sharp}}
for any
s\in[s_{\sharp},1-\delta]
and
p\in[p_{\sharp},N-\eta]
, by the the Hölder inequality and
[
4
, Theorem 6.5]
we gather that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\iint_{\Sigma}[u]_{s,p}\,d\mu^{+}(s,p)

\displaystyle\geqslant\iint_{[s_{\sharp},1-\delta]\times[p_{\sharp},N-\eta]}[u]_{s,p}\,d\mu^{+}(s,p)

\displaystyle\geqslant\iint_{[s_{\sharp},1-\delta]\times[p_{\sharp},N-\eta]}C_{1}\|u\|_{L^{p^{*}_{s}}(\Omega)}\,d\mu^{+}(s,p)

\displaystyle\geqslant\iint_{[s_{\sharp},1-\delta]\times[p_{\sharp},N-\eta]}C_{2}\|u\|_{L^{(p_{\sharp})^{*}_{s_{\sharp}}}(\Omega)}\,d\mu^{+}(s,p),

being
C_{1}=C_{1}(N,s,p)
given by
[
4
, Theorem 6.5]
and

C_{2}=C_{1}(s,p,N,\Omega)\,|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p^{*}_{s}}-\frac{1}{(p_{\sharp})^{*}_{s_{\sharp}}}}.

We note that the constant
C_{2}
is bounded uniformly for
any
s\in[s_{\sharp},1-\delta]
and
p\in[p_{\sharp},N-\eta]
, namely

0<\min_{(s,p)\in[s_{\sharp},1-\delta]\times[p_{\sharp},N-\eta]}C_{2}(s,p,N,\Omega)\leqslant\max_{(s,p)\in[s_{\sharp},1-\delta]\times[p_{\sharp},N-\eta]}C_{2}(s,p,N,\Omega)<+\infty.

Thus,

\|u\|_{\mu}\geqslant\min_{(s,p)\in[s_{\sharp},1-\delta]\times[p_{\sharp},N-\eta]}C_{2}(s,p,N,\Omega)\,\mu^{+}\Big([s_{\sharp},1-\delta]\times[p_{\sharp},N-\eta]\Big)\|u\|_{L^{(p_{\sharp})^{*}_{s_{\sharp}}}(\Omega)},

which provides the desired continuous embedding.

Now, suppose that there exists
(s_{1},p_{1})\in(0,1)\times(1,N)
such that
\mu^{+}([s_{1},1)\times\{p_{1}\})>0
.
In this case,

\mbox{there exists\penalty\ $\delta>0$ such that }\mu^{+}([s_{1},1-\delta]\times\{p_{1}\})>0.

Consequently, from Theorem
2.2
, we have that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\iint_{\Sigma}[u]_{s,p}\,d\mu^{+}(s,p)

\displaystyle\geqslant\iint_{[s_{1},1-\delta]\times\{p_{1}\}}[u]_{s,p_{1}}\,d\mu^{+}(s,p)

\displaystyle\geqslant\iint_{[s_{1},1-\delta]\times\{p_{1}\}}C_{1}[u]_{s_{1},p_{1}}\,d\mu^{+}(s,p)

for some
C_{1}=C_{1}(N,\Omega,p_{1})>0
.

Thus,

\begin{split}\|u\|_{\mu}&\geqslant C_{1}\mu^{+}\Big([s_{1},1-\delta]\times\{p_{1}\}\Big)[u]_{s_{1},p_{1}}\\
&\geqslant C_{1}\mu^{+}\Big([s_{1},1-\delta]\times\{p_{1}\}\Big)\|u\|_{W^{s_{1},p_{1}}_{0}(\Omega)},\end{split}

which proves the desired embedding and concludes the proof
of Proposition
2.3
.
∎

2.3.
Uniform convexity of the functional space

A natural question in our setting is the following:

Open Problem 1
.

Is the space
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
uniformly convex? If not, is it reflexive?

As a partial positive result, here we reduce ourselves to the case in which
\mu^{+}
is a convergent series of Dirac’s delta, namely we assume that there exist a sequence
(s_{k},p_{k})\in\Sigma
and
c_{k}\geqslant 0
such that

(2.4)

\mu^{+}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}c_{k}\delta_{(s_{k},p_{k})},\quad\mbox{ with }\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}c_{k}\in(0,+\infty).

Under this hypothesis, the following result holds true.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\mu^{+}
satisfy (
2.4
).
Then,
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
is a uniformly convex space.

Proof.

From (
2.4
) we have that, for any
u\in\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
,
the definition in (
2.1
) reads as

\|u\|_{\mu}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}c_{k}[u]_{s_{k},p_{k}}.

We want to prove that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,2]
there exists
\delta>0
such that,
if
u
,
v\in\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
are such that
\|u\|_{\mu}=\|v\|_{\mu}=1
and
\|u-v\|_{\mu}\geqslant\varepsilon
, then
\|u+v\|_{\mu}\leqslant 2-\delta
.
To this end, we assume that

(2.5)

\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}c_{k}[u]_{s_{k},p_{k}}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}c_{k}[v]_{s_{k},p_{k}}=1

and

(2.6)

\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}c_{k}[u-v]_{s_{k},p_{k}}\geqslant\varepsilon.

From (
2.5
) we have that, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

c_{k}[u]_{s_{k},p_{k}}=[c_{k}u]_{s_{k},p_{k}}\leqslant 1\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad c_{k}[v]_{s_{k},p_{k}}=[c_{k}v]_{s_{k},p_{k}}\leqslant 1.

Moreover, by (
2.6
), we infer that there exists some
\bar{k}
such that

c_{\bar{k}}[u-v]_{s_{\bar{k}},p_{\bar{k}}}=[c_{\bar{k}}u-c_{\bar{k}}v]_{s_{\bar{k}},p_{\bar{k}}}>0.

Notice that we can define

\varepsilon_{\bar{k}}:=c_{\bar{k}}[u-v]_{s_{\bar{k}},p_{\bar{k}}},

so that
\varepsilon_{\bar{k}}\leqslant 2
.

Now, for this
\bar{k}
we have that
c_{\bar{k}}u
,
c_{\bar{k}}v\in W^{s_{\bar{k}},p_{\bar{k}}}_{0}(\Omega)
, which is a uniformly convex space with the norm
[\,\cdot\,]_{s_{\bar{k}},p_{\bar{k}}}
as
p_{\bar{k}}>1
.
Thus, there exists some
\delta_{\bar{k}}>0
such that

[c_{\bar{k}}u+c_{\bar{k}}v]_{s_{\bar{k}},p_{\bar{k}}}\leqslant[c_{\bar{k}}u]_{s_{\bar{k}},p_{\bar{k}}}+[c_{\bar{k}}v]_{s_{\bar{k}},p_{\bar{k}}}-\delta_{k}.

From this and the triangle inequality, and recalling (
2.5
), we deduce that

\|u+v\|_{\mu}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}c_{k}[u+v]_{s_{k},p_{k}}\leqslant\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\Big(c_{k}[u]_{s_{k},p_{k}}+c_{k}[v]_{s_{k},p_{k}}\Big)-\delta_{\bar{k}}=2-\delta_{\bar{k}}.

The desired result follows by setting
\delta:=\delta_{\bar{k}}
.
∎

2.4.
Reabsorbing properties

We now show that the negative components of the signed measure
\mu
(if any) can be conveniently “reabsorbed” into the positive ones.
Indeed, the following result holds:

Theorem 2.7
.

Let (
1.2
), (
1.7
), (
1.12
) and (
1.13
) be satisfied.

Then, there exists a constant
c_{0}=c_{0}(N,\Omega)>0
such that, for any
u\in\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
,

\iint_{[0,\overline{s})\times(1,N)}[u]^{p}_{s,p}\,d\mu^{-}(s,p)\leqslant c_{0}\gamma\iint_{[\overline{s},1]\times(1,N)}[u]^{p}_{s,p}\,d\mu(s,p).

Proof.

We notice that, by (
1.7
) and (
1.13
),

(2.7)

\iint_{[0,\overline{s})\times(1,N)}[u]^{p}_{s,p}\,d\mu^{-}(s,p)=\int_{[1+\delta,N-\eta]}\left(\,\int_{[0,\overline{s})}[u]^{p}_{s,p}\,d\mu_{s}^{-}(s)\right)\,d\mu_{p}(p).

Moreover, by Theorem
2.2
, it follows that

\begin{split}\int_{[1+\delta,N-\eta]}\left(\,\int_{[0,\overline{s})}[u]^{p}_{s,p}\,d\mu_{s}^{-}(s)\right)\,d\mu_{p}(p)&\leqslant\int_{[1+\delta,N-\eta]}\left(\,\int_{[0,\overline{s})}C^{p}(N,\Omega,p)[u]^{p}_{\overline{s},p}\,d\mu_{s}^{-}(s)\right)\,d\mu_{p}(p)\\
&=\int_{[1+\delta,N-\eta]}C^{p}(N,\Omega,p)[u]^{p}_{\overline{s},p}\,\mu_{s}^{-}\big([0,\overline{s})\big)\,d\mu_{p}(p).\end{split}

Also, since Theorem
2.2
holds true for
every
p\in(1,N)
, we have that

(2.8)

C_{1}(N,\Omega):=\max_{p\in[1+\delta,N-\eta]}C^{p}(N,\Omega,p)<+\infty.

Therefore, by (
1.12
) and Theorem
2.2
,

(2.9)

\begin{split}\int_{[1+\delta,N-\eta]}\left(\,\int_{[0,\overline{s})}[u]^{p}_{s,p}\,d\mu_{s}^{-}(s)\right)\,d\mu_{p}(p)&\leqslant C_{1}(N,\Omega)\int_{[1+\delta,N-\eta]}[u]^{p}_{\overline{s},p}\,\mu_{s}^{-}\big([0,\overline{s})\big)\,d\mu_{p}(p)\\
&\leqslant\gamma C_{1}(N,\Omega)\int_{[1+\delta,N-\eta]}[u]^{p}_{\overline{s},p}\,\mu_{s}^{+}\big([\overline{s},1]\big)\,d\mu_{p}(p)\\
&=\gamma C_{1}(N,\Omega)\int_{[1+\delta,N-\eta]}\left(\,\int_{[\overline{s},1]}[u]^{p}_{\overline{s},p}\,d\mu_{s}^{+}(s)\right)\,d\mu_{p}(p)\\
&\leqslant\gamma C_{1}^{2}(N,\Omega)\int_{[1+\delta,N-\eta]}\left(\,\int_{[\overline{s},1]}[u]^{p}_{s,p}\,d\mu_{s}^{+}(s)\right)\,d\mu_{p}(p).\end{split}

Accordingly, by using (
2.9
) and (
2.7
),
we conclude that

\displaystyle\iint_{[0,\overline{s})\times(1,N)}[u]^{p}_{s,p}\,d\mu^{-}(s,p)

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma C_{1}^{2}(N,\Omega)\int_{[1+\delta,N-\eta]}\left(\,\int_{[\overline{s},1]}[u]^{p}_{s,p}\,d\mu_{s}^{+}(s)\right)\,d\mu_{p}(p)

\displaystyle=c_{0}\gamma\iint_{[\overline{s},1]\times(1,N)}[u]^{p}_{s,p}\,d\mu^{+}(s,p),

where
c_{0}:=C_{1}^{2}(N,\Omega)
.

Finally, by (
1.2
), we see that

\iint_{[\overline{s},1]\times(1,N)}[u]^{p}_{s,p}\,d\mu^{+}(s,p)=\iint_{[\overline{s},1]\times(1,N)}[u]^{p}_{s,p}\,d\mu(s,p),

which concludes the proof.
∎

From Theorem
2.7
we deduce the following result:

Corollary 2.8
.

Let (
1.2
), (
1.7
), (
1.12
) and (
1.13
) be satisfied.

Then, there exists
c_{1}=c_{1}(N,\Omega)>0
such that, for any

,

\iint_{[0,\overline{s})\times(1,N)}\frac{1}{p}[u]^{p}_{s,p}\,d\mu^{-}(s,p)\leqslant c_{1}\gamma\iint_{[\overline{s},1]\times(1,N)}\frac{1}{p}[u]^{p}_{s,p}\,d\mu(s,p).

Explicitely, one can take

being

as in (

).

We notice that, since

, we can employ Theorem

. Thus, there exists a constant

such that

\begin{split}\iint_{[0,\overline{s})\times(1,N)}\frac{1}{p}[u]^{p}_{s,p}\,d\mu^{-}(s,p)&\leqslant\iint_{[0,\overline{s})\times(1,N)}[u]^{p}_{s,p}\,d\mu^{-}(s,p)\\

.
∎

3.1.
Preliminary observations

We prove here a useful integration by parts formula.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
u
,

v\,(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u\in L^{1}(\Omega\times\Sigma,dx\,d\mu(s,p))

Then,

(3.1)

\begin{split}&\iint_{\Sigma}\frac{c_{N,s,p}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu(s,p)\\
&\qquad=\iint_{\Sigma}\int_{\Omega}v(x)(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u(x)\,dx\,d\mu(s,p).\end{split}

Proof.

We point out that the integrability assumptions in Proposition
3.1
guarantee that
both sides of (
3.1
) are finite.

We aim to show that, for any

,

\frac{c_{N,s,p}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dx\,dy=\int_{\Omega}v(x)(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u(x)\,dx.

From this, the identity in (
3.1
) will plainly follow.

Now, we have that

\displaystyle c_{N,s,p}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{N,s,p}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}v(x)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\qquad-c_{N,s,p}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}v(y)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2c_{N,s,p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{\Omega}v(x)\,(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u(x)\,dx,

as desired.
∎

Remark 3.3

We stress that expressions such as

.
Let
J:\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
be the functional defined as

(3.5)

\begin{split}J(u):=\sum_{{i=1,\dots,n}\atop{j=1,\dots,m}}[u]^{p_{j}}_{s_{i},p_{j}}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{[0,\overline{s})}\frac{1}{2p_{j}}[u]_{s,p_{j}}^{p_{j}}\,d\mu_{s}^{-}(s)-\int_{\Omega}g(x)u\,dx.\end{split}

Then, we have the following properties of
J
:

Lemma 3.4
.

For every
u\in\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
, we have that

|J(u)|<+\infty.

Proof.

Let
u\in\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
.
Recalling (
3.3
), we gave that

\sum_{{i=1,\dots,n}\atop{j=1,\dots,m}}[u]_{s_{i},p_{j}}=\|u\|_{\mu}<+\infty.

This entails that

\sum_{{i=1,\dots,n}\atop{j=1,\dots,m}}\frac{1}{2p_{j}}[u]^{p_{j}}_{s_{i},p_{j}}<+\infty.

Moreover, by (
3.2
) and Corollary
2.8
, we infer that

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{[0,\overline{s})}\frac{1}{2p_{j}}[u]_{s,p_{j}}^{p_{j}}\,d\mu_{s}^{-}(s)\leqslant c_{1}\gamma\sum_{{i=1,\dots,n}\atop{j=1,\dots,m}}\frac{1}{2p_{j}}[u]^{p_{j}}_{s_{i},p_{j}}<+\infty.

Also, by the Hölder inequality (with exponents
\widehat{p}

and
\widehat{p}^{\prime}
)
and Proposition
2.3
(recall that
p_{\sharp}=\widehat{p}
in this setting), we have that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}g(x)u(x)\,dx\right|\leqslant\left(\int_{\Omega}|g(x)|^{\widehat{p}^{\prime}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\widehat{p}^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{\widehat{p}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\widehat{p}}}<+\infty.

Gathering these pieces of information, we obtain that
|J(u)|<+\infty
, as desired.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

We have that, for all
u
,
v\in\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
,

(3.6)

\begin{split}\langle J^{\prime}(u),v\rangle&=\sum_{{i=1,\dots,n}\atop{j=1,\dots,m}}\frac{c_{N,s_{i},p_{j}}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p_{j}-2}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+s_{i}p_{j}}}\,dx\,dy\\
&-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{[0,\overline{s})}\frac{c_{N,s,p_{j}}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p_{j}-2}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp_{j}}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu_{s}^{-}(s)\\
&-\int_{\Omega}g(x)v(x)\,dx.\end{split}

Furthermore, critical points of
J
are weak solutions of the problem (
1.9
).

Proof.

By (
3.5
), we obtain that

\begin{split}&J(u+\varepsilon v)=J(u)+\varepsilon\sum_{{i=1,\dots,n}\atop{j=1,\dots,m}}\frac{c_{N,s_{i},p_{j}}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p_{j}-2}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+s_{i}p_{j}}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\quad-\varepsilon\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{[0,\overline{s})}\frac{c_{N,s,p_{j}}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p_{j}-2}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp_{j}}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu_{s}^{-}(s)\\
&\quad+o(\varepsilon)\left(\sum_{{i=1,\dots,n}\atop{j=1,\dots,m}}\frac{1}{2p_{j}}[u]^{p_{j}}_{s_{i},p_{j}}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{[0,\overline{s})}\frac{1}{2p_{j}}[u]_{s,p_{j}}^{p_{j}}\,d\mu_{s}^{-}(s)\right)+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}g(x)v(x)\,dx.\end{split}

From this, we infer that

\begin{split}&\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{J(u+\varepsilon v)-J(u)}{\varepsilon}=\sum_{{i=1,\dots,n}\atop{j=1,\dots,m}}\frac{c_{N,s_{i},p_{j}}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p_{j}-2}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+s_{i}p_{j}}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\qquad-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{[0,\overline{s})}\frac{c_{N,s,p_{j}}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p_{j}-2}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp_{j}}}\,dx\,dy\,d\mu_{s}^{-}(s)\\
&\qquad-\int_{\Omega}g(x)v(x)\,dx,\end{split}

namely (
3.6
) holds true.

From (
3.6
), we see that if
u
is a critical point of
J
, then
u
is a weak solution of (
1.9
), thanks to Definition
3.2
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

The functional
J
is weakly lower semicontinuous.

Proof.

We let
u_{k}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
which converges
weakly to some
u\in\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
as
k\to+\infty

and we claim that

(3.7)

\liminf_{k\to+\infty}J(u_{k})\geqslant J(u).

To check this, we observe that, by Fatou’s Lemma,

(3.8)

\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\sum_{{i=1,\dots,n}\atop{j=1,\dots,m}}\frac{1}{2p_{j}}[u_{k}]^{p_{j}}_{s_{i},p_{j}}\geqslant\sum_{{i=1,\dots,n}\atop{j=1,\dots,m}}\frac{1}{2p_{j}}[u]^{p_{j}}_{s_{i},p_{j}}.

Moreover, by Corollary
2.4
we infer that
u_{k}

converges strongly to
u
in
L^{\widehat{p}}(\Omega)
, and thus,
by the Hölder inequality,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\left|\int_{\Omega}g(x)\big(u_{k}(x)-u(x)\big)\,dx\right|\leqslant\lim_{k\to+\infty}\|g\|_{L^{\widehat{p}^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\|u_{k}-u\|_{L^{\widehat{p}}(\Omega)}=0,

which implies that

(3.9)

\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}g(x)u_{k}(x)\,dx=\int_{\Omega}g(x)u(x)\,dx.

Now, we want to show that

(3.10)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{[0,\overline{s})}\frac{1}{2p_{j}}[u_{k}]_{s,p_{j}}^{p_{j}}\,d\mu_{s}^{-}(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{[0,\overline{s})}\frac{1}{2p_{j}}[u]_{s,p_{j}}^{p_{j}}\,d\mu_{s}^{-}(s).

Since
u_{k}
converges weakly in
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
, it is
bounded in
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
.
Thus, for any
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
,
u_{k}
is bounded in
W^{s_{i},p_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Up to a subsequence, we can assume that
u_{k}
converges weakly to
u

in
W^{s_{i},p_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for any
i
and
j
.
Then, from Corollary
2.4
we have that

(3.11)

u_{k}\to u\ \mbox{as\penalty\ $k\to+\infty$ in $L^{p_{j}}(\Omega)$, for any $j$}.

Moreover, for any
s\in[0,\overline{s})
, we can exploit
[
2
, Theorem 1]
and obtain that, for any
\theta\in(0,1)
, there exists a positive constant
C=C(N,\theta,p_{j},\overline{s})
such that

\|u_{k}-u\|_{W^{s,p_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leqslant C\|u_{k}-u\|_{L^{p_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\theta}\|u_{k}-u\|_{W^{\overline{s},p_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{1-\theta}.

This and (
3.11
) entail that, for all
j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{[0,\overline{s})}[u_{k}-u]_{s,p_{j}}^{p_{j}}\,d\mu_{s}^{-}(s)\leqslant\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{[0,\overline{s})}\|u_{k}-u\|_{W^{s,p_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{p_{j}}\,d\mu_{s}^{-}(s)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leqslant C\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{[0,\overline{s})}\|u_{k}-u\|_{L^{p_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\theta p_{j}}\|u_{k}-u\|_{W^{\overline{s},p_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{(1-\theta)p_{j}}\,d\mu_{s}^{-}(s)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leqslant C\mu_{s}^{-}([0,\overline{s}))\lim_{k\to+\infty}\|u_{k}-u\|_{L^{p_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\theta p_{j}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=0.

This gives (
3.10
).

Combining (
3.8
), (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) we get the claim in (
3.7
).
∎

We recall that, for every
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
,

(3.12)

|x_{1}+\cdots+x_{m}|^{p}\leqslant m^{p-1}(|x_{1}|^{p}+\cdots+|x_{m}|^{p})\quad\mbox{for any }p>1.

We also point out that so far we have not used the smallness
of the parameter
\gamma
appearing in the assumption (
1.12
). The next statement instead will
require the additional assumption that
\gamma
is sufficiently small in order
to reabsorb the contribution coming from the negative part of the measure.

Lemma 3.7
.

There exists
\gamma_{0}>0
such that, if
\gamma\in[0,\gamma_{0}]
,
the functional
J
is coercive, namely

(3.13)

\lim_{\|u\|_{\mu}\to+\infty}\frac{J(u)}{\|u\|_{\mu}}=+\infty.

Proof.

From (
2.12
), (
3.5
)
and the Hölder inequality we have that

J(u)\geqslant c\sum_{{i=1,\dots,n}\atop{j=1,\dots,m}}\frac{1}{2p_{j}}[u]^{p_{j}}_{s_{i},p_{j}}-\|g\|_{L^{\widehat{p}^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{L^{\widehat{p}}(\Omega)},

provided that
\gamma
is taken sufficiently small.

This and Proposition
2.3
give that

(3.14)

J(u)\geqslant c\sum_{{i=1,\dots,n}\atop{j=1,\dots,m}}\frac{1}{2p_{j}}[u]^{p_{j}}_{s_{i},p_{j}}-C\|u\|_{\mu},

for some
C>0
.

Now we notice that if
\|u\|_{\mu}\geqslant nm
,
then the set

K:=\Big\{(i,j)\in\{1,\cdots,n\}\times\{1,\cdots,m\}\mbox{ s.t. }[u]_{s_{i},p_{j}}\geqslant 1\Big\}

is non empty.

Hence, if
\|u\|_{\mu}
goes to infinity,
then, for at least one couple
(i,j)\in K
, we have that
[u]_{s_{i},p_{j}}
goes to infinity. Accordingly,

(3.15)

\lim_{\|u\|_{\mu}\to+\infty}\sum_{(i,j)\in K}[u]_{s_{i},p_{j}}=+\infty.

We denote by
|K|
the cardinality of the set
K
and we define

p_{min}:=\min_{(i,j)\in K}\{p_{j}\}.

As a result, by using (
3.12
),

(3.16)

\sum_{{i=1,\dots,n}\atop{j=1,\dots,m}}[u]_{s_{i},p_{j}}^{p_{j}}\geqslant\sum_{(i,j)\in K}[u]_{s_{i},p_{j}}^{p_{j}}\geqslant\sum_{(i,j)\in K}[u]_{s_{i},p_{j}}^{p_{min}}\geqslant\frac{1}{|K|^{p_{min}-1}}\left(\sum_{(i,j)\in K}[u]_{s_{i},p_{j}}\right)^{p_{min}}.

We also have that

\|u\|_{\mu}=\sum_{{i=1,\dots,n}\atop{j=1,\dots,m}}[u]_{s_{i},p_{j}}\sum_{(i,j)\in K}[u]_{s_{i},p_{j}}+\sum_{(i,j)\in\{1,\cdots,n\}\times\{1,\cdots,m\}\setminus K}[u]_{s_{i},p_{j}}\leqslant\sum_{(i,j)\in K}[u]_{s_{i},p_{j}}+nm-|K|.

Therefore, combining this, (
3.14
), (
3.15
)
and (
3.16
),

\displaystyle\lim_{\|u\|_{\mu}\to+\infty}\frac{J(u)}{\|u\|_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\geqslant\lim_{\|u\|_{\mu}\to+\infty}\frac{c}{2\widehat{p}\,\|u\|_{\mu}}\sum_{{i=1,\dots,n}\atop{j=1,\dots,m}}[u]_{s_{i},p_{j}}^{p_{j}}-C

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{c}{2\widehat{p}\,|K|^{p_{min}-1}}\lim_{\|u\|_{\mu}\to+\infty}\dfrac{\displaystyle\left(\sum_{(i,j)\in K}[u]_{s_{i},p_{j}}\right)^{p_{min}}}{\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in K}[u]_{s_{i},p_{j}}+nm-|K|}-C

\displaystyle=+\infty,

which proves the claim in (
3.13
).
∎

We now proceed with the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

In light of Proposition
3.5
, we aim at finding
critical points of the functional
J
in (
3.5
).

Since
J
is weakly lower semicontinuous (thanks to Lemma
3.6
)
and
coercive (thanks to Lemma
3.7
)
and the space
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
is reflexive (see Proposition
2.6
), by the Weierstrass theorem, we infer the existence of a minimizer for the functional
J
,
which is a weak solution of problem (
1.9
).
This establishes the first claim in Theorem
1.1
.

In addition, if
\mu^{-}\equiv 0
, then the functional
J
reduces to

J(u)=\sum_{{i=1,\dots,n}\atop{j=1,\dots,m}}\frac{1}{2p_{j}}[u]^{p_{j}}_{s_{i},p_{j}}-\int_{\Omega}g(x)u(x)\,dx.

Since
J
is strictly convex, we see that
the global minimum is the only critical point of
J
, concluding the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

3.3.
Proof of the Theorem
1.5

We now focus on giving the proof of Theorem
1.5
.
We recall that, in this setting,

(3.17)

\mu^{+}(s,p)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\delta_{(s_{k},p_{k})},

namely
\mu^{+}
is a particular case of the measure defined in (
2.4
). With this choice, the definition in (
2.1
) boils down to

(3.18)

\|u\|_{\mu}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}[u]_{s_{k},p_{k}}

and the operator defined in (
1.4
) becomes

(3.19)

L_{\mu}\,u:=\sum_{k=1}^{m}(-\Delta)^{s_{k}}_{p_{k}}u.

Our goal is to show the existence of a Mountain Pass solution for the problem (
1.14
), under
the assumptions that
f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Carthéodory function satisfying (
1.15
), (
1.16
), (
1.18
) and (
1.19
).

With this aim, we provide the definition of weak solutions for the problem (
1.14
).

Definition 3.8
.

We say that
u\in\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
is a weak solution of (
1.14
) if, for any
v\in\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
,

\begin{split}&\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{c_{N,s_{k},p_{k}}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p_{k}-2}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+s_{k}p_{k}}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad=\int_{\Omega}f(x,u(x))v(x)\,dx.\end{split}

Also, let
F
be defined as in (
1.17
) and
I:\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
be the functional defined as

(3.20)

\begin{split}I(u):=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{2p_{k}}[u]^{p_{k}}_{s_{k},p_{k}}-\int_{\Omega}F(x,u(x))\,dx.\end{split}

Bu arguing as in the proof of Proposition
3.5
, we
obtain the following statement:

Proposition 3.9
.

We have that, for all
u
,
v\in\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
,

(3.21)

\begin{split}\langle I^{\prime}(u),v\rangle=&\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{c_{N,s_{k},p_{k}}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p_{k}-2}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+s_{k}p_{k}}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\qquad\qquad-\int_{\Omega}f(x,u(x))v(x)\,dx.\end{split}

Furthermore, critical points of
I
are weak solutions of the problem (
1.14
).

We also recall the following result stated in
[
10
, Lemma 3]
:

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
f
satisfy (
1.15
) and (
1.16
). Then, for any
\varepsilon>0

there exists
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that,
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
and for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(3.22)

\begin{split}&|f(x,t)|\leqslant\widehat{p}\varepsilon|t|^{\widehat{p}-1}+q\delta(\varepsilon)|t|^{q-1}\\
{\mbox{and }}\qquad&|F(x,t)|\leqslant\varepsilon|t|^{\widehat{p}}+\delta(\varepsilon)|t|^{q},\end{split}

where
F
is defined in (
1.17
).

We now prove that the geometry of the Mountain Pass Theorem is satisfied.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
f
satisfy (
1.15
) and (
1.16
).

Then, there exist
\rho
,
\beta>0
such that, for any
u\in\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
with
\|u\|_{\mu}=\rho
, we have that
I(u)\geqslant\beta
.

Proof.

By (
3.17
) and Remark
2.5
, we have that
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded in
L^{r}(\Omega)
for any
r\in[1,(p_{\sharp})^{*}_{s_{\sharp}})
, and therefore it is compactly embedded in
L^{\widehat{p}}(\Omega)

and in
L^{q}(\Omega)

(recall that
q\in(\widehat{p},(p_{\sharp})^{*}_{s_{\sharp}})
, in light
of the assumption in (
1.15
)).

Hence, by means of (
3.20
), (
3.22
) and (
3.18
), we infer that, for any
\varepsilon>0
,
there exists
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that, for all
u\in\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle I(u)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{2p_{k}}[u]^{p_{k}}_{s_{k},p_{k}}-\varepsilon\|u\|^{\widehat{p}}_{L^{\widehat{p}}(\Omega)}-\delta(\varepsilon)\|u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\widehat{p}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}[u]^{p_{k}}_{s_{k},p_{k}}-C\varepsilon\|u\|^{\widehat{p}}_{\mu}-C\delta(\varepsilon)\|u\|^{q}_{\mu},

for some
C>0
.

From this, and using also (
3.12
),
we see that, if
\|u\|_{\mu}\leqslant 1
,

\begin{split}I(u)&\geqslant\frac{1}{2\widehat{p}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}[u]^{\widehat{p}}_{s_{k},p_{k}}-C\varepsilon\|u\|^{\widehat{p}}_{\mu}-C\delta(\varepsilon)\|u\|^{q}_{\mu}\\
&\geqslant\frac{1}{2\widehat{p}\,m^{\widehat{p}-1}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}[u]_{s_{k},p_{k}}\right)^{\widehat{p}}-C\varepsilon\|u\|_{\mu}^{\widehat{p}}-C\delta(\varepsilon)\|u\|^{q}_{\mu}\\
&=\|u\|^{\widehat{p}}_{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{2\widehat{p}\,m^{\widehat{p}-1}}-C\varepsilon-C\delta(\varepsilon)\|u\|^{q-\widehat{p}}_{\mu}\right).\end{split}

Thus, choosing

\varepsilon:=\frac{1}{4C\widehat{p}\,m^{\widehat{p}-1}},

we obtain that

I(u)\geqslant\|u\|^{\widehat{p}}_{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{4\widehat{p}\,m^{\widehat{p}-1}}-C\delta\|u\|^{q-\widehat{p}}_{\mu}\right),

where now
\delta
depends on the structural constants of the problem.

Now we take

\rho:=\min\left\{\frac{1}{8C\delta\widehat{p}\,m^{\widehat{p}-1}},1\right\}.

In this way, if
\|u\|_{\mu}=\rho
,

I(u)\geqslant\rho^{\widehat{p}}\frac{1}{8\widehat{p}\,m^{\widehat{p}-1}}=:\beta>0,

as desired.
∎

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
f
satisfy (
1.15
), (
1.16
) and (
1.19
).

Then, there exists
e\in\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
such that
e\geqslant 0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\|e\|_{\mu}>\rho
and
I(e)<\beta
, where
\rho
and
\beta
are given in Proposition
3.11
.

Proof.

Let
t\geqslant 1
and let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\varphi\geqslant 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\varphi=1
in some ball
B\Subset\Omega
.

By (
3.20
), (
3.18
) and (
1.19
), we infer that

\begin{split}I(t\varphi)&=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{t^{p_{k}}}{2p_{k}}[\varphi]^{p_{k}}_{s_{k},p_{k}}-\int_{\Omega}F(x,t\varphi(x))\,dx\\
&\leqslant\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{t^{p_{k}}}{2p_{k}}[\varphi]^{p_{k}}_{s_{k},p_{k}}-a_{3}t^{\widetilde{\vartheta}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\widetilde{\vartheta}}(\Omega)}^{\widetilde{\vartheta}}+\int_{\Omega}a_{4}(x)\,dx\\
&\leqslant\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{t^{\widehat{p}}}{2p_{k}}[\varphi]^{p_{k}}_{s_{k},p_{k}}-a_{3}t^{\widetilde{\vartheta}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\widetilde{\vartheta}}(\Omega)}^{\widetilde{\vartheta}}+\int_{\Omega}a_{4}(x)\,dx.\end{split}

Let also

\bar{t}:=\max\left\{\rho,\left(\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{k}}[\varphi]^{p_{k}}_{s_{k},p_{k}}}{a_{3}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\widetilde{\vartheta}}(\Omega)}^{\widetilde{\vartheta}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\widetilde{\vartheta}-\widehat{p}}},\left(\frac{\int_{\Omega}a_{4}(x)\,dx}{a_{3}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\widetilde{\vartheta}}(\Omega)}^{\widetilde{\vartheta}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\widetilde{\vartheta}}}\right\}+1.

In this way, since
\widetilde{\vartheta}>\widehat{p}
,
taking
e:=\frac{\bar{t}\varphi}{\|\varphi\|_{\mu}}
, we see that

\|e\|_{\mu}>\rho\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad I(e)<\beta,

as desired.∎

Now, we want to prove that the Palais-Smale condition holds at any level. We start by proving that every Palais-Smale sequence is bounded.

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
f
satisfy (
1.15
), (
1.16
) and (
1.18
). Let
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
u_{n}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
such that

(3.23)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}I(u_{n})=c

and

(3.24)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)\\
\|v\|_{\mu}=1\end{subarray}}|\langle I^{\prime}(u_{n}),v\rangle|=0.

Then,
u_{n}
is bounded in
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

By (
3.23
) and (
3.24
), there exists
M>0
such that, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

|I(u_{n})|\leqslant M\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\left|\left\langle I^{\prime}(u_{n}),\frac{u_{n}}{\|u_{n}\|_{\mu}}\right\rangle\right|\leqslant M.

Thus, if
\vartheta
is as in (
1.18
), we have that

(3.25)

\vartheta I(u_{n})-\langle I^{\prime}(u_{n}),u_{n}\rangle\leqslant M(\vartheta+\|u_{n}\|_{\mu}).

Moreover, we take
r
as in assumption (
1.18
) and notice that there are two possible cases. On the one hand, if
r=0
, then

(3.26)

\int_{\Omega}\Big(f(x,u_{n}(x))u_{n}(x)-\vartheta F(x,u_{n}(x))\Big)\,dx>0.

On the other hand, if
r>0
, then, by using Lemma
3.10
with
\varepsilon:=1
, we find that

(3.27)

\begin{split}&\left|\;\int_{\Omega\cap\{|u_{n}|\leqslant r\}}\Big(f(x,u_{n}(x))u_{n}(x)-\vartheta F(x,u_{n}(x))\Big)\,dx\right|\\
&\qquad\qquad\leqslant\Big(\widehat{p}r^{\widehat{p}}+q\delta(1)r^{q}+\vartheta r^{\widehat{p}}+\vartheta\delta(1)r^{q}\Big)|\Omega|=:C_{r}.\end{split}

Now, by (
3.20
), (
3.21
) and
either (
3.26
) if
r=0
or (
3.27
) if

, we see that

\begin{split}&\vartheta I(u_{n})-\langle I^{\prime}(u_{n}),u_{n}\rangle\\
=\;&\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\vartheta}{p_{k}}-1\right)[u_{n}]_{s_{k},p_{k}}^{p_{k}}+\int_{\Omega}\Big(f(x,u_{n}(x))u_{n}(x)-\vartheta F(x,u_{n}(x))\Big)\,dx\\
\geqslant\;&\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\vartheta}{p_{k}}-1\right)[u_{n}]_{s_{k},p_{k}}^{p_{k}}+\int_{\Omega\cap\{|u_{n}|\leqslant r\}}\Big(f(x,u_{n}(x))u_{n}(x)-\vartheta F(x,u_{n}(x))\Big)\,dx\\
\geqslant\;&\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\vartheta}{p_{k}}-1\right)[u_{n}]_{s_{k},p_{k}}^{p_{k}}-C_{r}\\
\geqslant\;&\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\vartheta}{\widehat{p}}-1\right)\sum_{k=1}^{m}[u_{n}]_{s_{k},p_{k}}^{p_{k}}-C_{r}.\end{split}

Combining this with (
3.25
), we obtain that

(3.28)

\sum_{k=1}^{m}[u_{n}]_{s_{k},p_{k}}^{p_{k}}\leqslant\frac{2\widehat{p}M(\vartheta+\|u_{n}\|_{\mu})+2\widehat{p}C_{r}}{(\vartheta-\widehat{p})}.

We claim that (

) implies that
u_{n}
is bounded
in
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
.
In order to check this, assume by contradiction that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\|u_{n}\|_{\mu}=+\infty.

Then, defining the set

s.t. }\;[u_{n}]_{s_{k},p_{k}}\geqslant 1\mbox{ for every }n\geqslant n_{0}\Big\},

we have that

(3.29)

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sum_{k\in K}[u_{n}]_{s_{k},p_{k}}=+\infty.

Moreover, using (

) and denoting by

the cardinality of the set

we see that

(3.30)

\sum_{k=1}^{m}[u_{n}]_{s_{k},p_{k}}^{p_{k}}\geqslant\sum_{k\in K}[u_{n}]_{s_{k},p_{k}}^{p_{k}}\geqslant\sum_{k\in K}[u_{n}]_{s_{k},p_{k}}^{p_{min}}\geqslant\frac{1}{|K|^{p_{min}-1}}\left(\sum_{k\in K}[u_{n}]_{s_{k},p_{k}}\right)^{p_{min}}.

Also,

\|u_{n}\|_{\mu}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}[u_{n}]_{s_{k},p_{k}}\leqslant\sum_{k\in K}[u_{n}]_{s_{k},p_{k}}+m-|K|.

As a consequence, combining (
3.29
),
(
3.30
) and (

),

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{\|u_{n}\|_{\mu}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}[u_{n}]_{s_{k},p_{k}}^{p_{k}}\geqslant\frac{1}{|K|^{p_{min}-1}}\lim_{n\to+\infty}\dfrac{\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k\in K}[u_{n}]_{s_{k},p_{k}}\right)^{p_{min}}}{\displaystyle\sum_{k\in K}[u_{n}]_{s_{k},p_{k}}+m-|K|}=+\infty,

which is in contradiction with (
3.28
).

.

Let

satisfy (

), we have that

namely

(3.34)

\begin{split}&\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Bigg|\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{c_{N,s_{k},p_{k}}}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{(u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y))^{p_{k}-2}(u_{n}(x)-u_{n}(y))((u_{n}-u)(x)-(u_{n}-u)(y))}{|x-y|^{N+s_{k}p_{k}}}\,dx\,dy\\

we know that

converges strongly to

in
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}(\Omega)
,

Appendix A
Discussion on the well-posedness of the energy functional

In order to deal with problem (
1.6
), we restrict ourselves to
the case of a measure of the form (
1.8
).
The reason for this is that
one can find measures for which the
the functional
associated with problem (
1.6
) is not well defined, even though
the associated norm is finite on the functional space.

More precisely, our aim is to
find measures
\mu
and functions
u\in\mathcal{X}_{\mu}
such that

\|u\|_{\mu}<+\infty\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad\iint_{\Sigma}\frac{1}{2p}[u]_{s,p}^{p}\,d\mu^{+}(s,p)=+\infty,

or, in other words, that the associated functional is not well
defined on the space
\mathcal{X}_{\mu}
.
On this matter, we provide two examples.

First, we deal with the case

\mu^{+}:=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}c_{k}\delta_{(s_{k},p_{k})}.

Let
\Omega=B_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, with
N\geqslant 2
, and let

s_{k}:=1,\,p_{k}:=N-\frac{1}{k}\,\mbox{ and }c_{k}:=k^{-1-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{p_{k}}}\mbox{ for every }k\in\mathbb{N}\mbox{ and }\varepsilon\in(0,1).

Then,

\|u\|_{\mu}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}c_{k}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p_{k}}(B_{1})}.

We want to show that there exists some
u
with
\|u\|_{\mu}<+\infty

and

\iint_{\Sigma}\frac{1}{2p}[u]_{s,p}^{p}\,d\mu^{+}(s,p)=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{c_{k}}{2p_{k}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p_{k}}(B_{1})}^{p_{k}}=+\infty.

To do this, consider the function
u(x):=\log|x|
, so that
\nabla u(x)=x/|x|^{2}
. Clearly,

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p_{k}}(B_{1})}=\left(\;\int_{B_{1}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{p_{k}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}=\omega_{N-1}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{N-p_{k}-1}\,d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\frac{\omega_{N-1}^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}}{(N-p_{k})^{\frac{1}{p_{k}}}}=\omega_{N-1}^{\frac{1}{N-1/k}}k^{\frac{1}{N-1/k}}

Thus,

\|u\|_{\mu}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}c_{k}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p_{k}}(B_{1})}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}k^{-1-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{N-1/k}}\omega_{N-1}^{\frac{1}{N-1/k}}k^{\frac{1}{N-1/k}}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\omega_{N-1}^{\frac{1}{N-1/k}}k^{-1-\frac{\varepsilon}{N-1/k}}<+\infty.

On the other hand,

\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{c_{k}}{2p_{k}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p_{k}}(B_{1})}^{p_{k}}\geqslant\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}k^{-1-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{N-1/k}}\omega_{N-1}k=\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{2N}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}k^{-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{N-1/k}}=+\infty,

as desired.

For the second example, we consider

\mu^{+}=\delta_{1}\times\nu_{p}.

Here,
\nu_{p}
is a measure defined on
(1,N)
such that
d\nu_{p}=h(p)\,dp

for some measurable function
h:(1,N)\to\mathbb{R}
.

Let
\Omega=B_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, with
N\geqslant 2
, and let
h(p):=2p
.
Then,

\|u\|_{\mu}=\int_{1}^{N}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\,dp.

In this case, we want to show that there exists some
u
with
\|u\|_{\mu}<+\infty
and

\iint_{\Sigma}\frac{1}{2p}[u]_{s,p}^{p}\,d\mu^{+}(s,p)=\int_{1}^{N}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}^{p}\,dp=+\infty.

In order to do this, we argue as in the first example
and consider the function
u(x):=\log|x|
.
Now,

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}=\frac{\omega_{N-1}^{\frac{1}{p}}}{(N-p)^{\frac{1}{p}}}.

Thus,

\|u\|_{\mu}=\int_{1}^{N}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\,dp=\int_{1}^{N}\frac{\omega_{N-1}^{\frac{1}{p}}}{(N-p)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\,dp.

Clearly,

(A.1)

\int_{1}^{N-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\omega_{N-1}^{\frac{1}{p}}}{(N-p)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\,dp<+\infty.

Moreover, if
p\in(N-\frac{1}{2},N)
we have that

(N-p)^{\frac{1}{N-\frac{1}{2}}}\leqslant(N-p)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant(N-p)^{\frac{1}{N}},

and so

\int_{N-\frac{1}{2}}^{N}\frac{\omega_{N-1}^{\frac{1}{p}}}{(N-p)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\,dp\leqslant\int_{N-\frac{1}{2}}^{N}\frac{\omega_{N-1}^{\frac{1}{p}}}{(N-p)^{\frac{1}{N-\frac{1}{2}}}}\,dp<+\infty.

From this and (
A.1
) we infer that
\|u\|_{\mu}<+\infty
.

In addition,

\int_{1}^{N}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}^{p}\,dp=\int_{1}^{N}\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{(N-p)}\,dp=+\infty,

as desired.

Trace formula and Levinson’s theorem as an index pairing in the presence of resonances
Angus Alexander

Abstract
We realise the number of bound states of a Schrödinger operator on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} as an index pairing in all dimensions. Expanding on ideas of Guillopé and others, we use high-energy corrections to find representatives of the K K -theory class of the scattering operator. These representatives allow us to compute the number of bound states using an integral formula involving heat kernel coefficients.



1
Introduction

In this paper we use the known form of the wave operator to construct an index pairing and prove Levinson’s theorem for Schrödinger operators on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in each dimension via the index of the wave operator.

In dimension
n\leq 4
there are correction terms required due to resonances and the low-energy behaviour of the scattering operator, whilst in dimensions
n\geq 2
there are corrections required to account for the high-energy behaviour of the scattering operator. These correction terms are constructed explicitly using a pseudodifferential expansion of the resolvent and used to compute the index of the wave operator as a winding number, from which Levinson’s theorem follows.

As a consequence, we obtain the value of the spectral shift function at zero in all dimensions in the presence of any resonant behaviour. To the best of our knowledge, no such statement regarding the behaviour of the spectral shift function at zero in dimension
n=4
has appeared in the literature.

Levinson’s original theorem
[
51
]
states that in dimension
n=1
the number of eigenvalues (counted with multiplicity) of the Schrödinger operator
H=H_{0}+V
(with
H_{0}=-\Delta
) for a potential
V
decaying sufficiently fast at infinity satisfies

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}(\delta(0)-\delta(\infty))+\frac{1}{2}\nu,

where
\delta
is the scattering phase and
\nu\in\{0,1\}
depends on the existence of a resonance (a distributional solution to
H\psi=0
with
\psi\notin L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
). In higher dimensions, such a relation holds in each angular momentum mode as shown by Newton
[
54
]
in dimension
n=3
(see also
[
22
]
for higher dimensions). In addition, the work of Jensen
[
38
,
39
,
41
]
, Klaus and Simon
[
48
]
and Bollé et al.
[
12
,
14
]
shows that resonant behaviour can occur in dimension
n\leq 4
only. The resonant contributions in dimension
n=2,4
to Levinson type results differ from those in dimension
n=1,3
in that resonances contribute integer values rather than half-integers. The differing behaviour in dimension
n=2,4
is due to the logarithmic singularity near
z=0
of the resolvent
(H-z)^{-1}
as first observed by Klaus and Simon
[
48
]
and Jensen
[
41
]
(see also
[
12
,
14
,
42
]
for additional detail).

In Section
4
we follow techniques of Colin de Verdière
[
20
]
and Guillopé
[
31
,
32
]
to construct a function

given by

\displaystyle P_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=2\pi i\beta_{n}(V)+\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor}C_{j}(n,V)\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-j},

where the
C_{j}(n,V),\beta_{n}(V)
are heat-kernel type coefficients. In addition we show that the spectral shift function
\xi=\xi(\cdot,H,H_{0})
associated to the pair
(H,H_{0})
satisfies

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\left(-2\pi i\xi(\lambda)-P_{n}(\lambda)\right)

\displaystyle=0.

(1.1)

Equation (
1.1
) is proved by computing a resolvent expansion and using the limiting absorption principle to analyse the high-energy behaviour of the spectral shift function. As a result of Equation (

) we can construct a different representative of the
K
-theory class
[S]
of the scattering operator which converges to the identity at infinity in the trace norm.

Taking into account the relation
\textup{Tr}(S(\lambda)^{*}S^{\prime}(\lambda))=-2\pi i\xi^{\prime}(\lambda)

(counted with multiplicity) is obtained by computing the index of the wave operator
W_{-}
as a winding number and reads

\displaystyle-N

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\textup{Tr}(S(\lambda)^{*}S^{\prime}(\lambda))-P_{n}^{\prime}(\lambda)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda-\frac{1}{2\pi i}P_{n}(0)+N_{res},

(1.2)

where
N_{res}
is a contribution related to the existence of resonances in dimension
n\leq 4
. The term

is either
0
or
\frac{1}{2}
in dimension
n=1,3
, either
0
,
1
or
2
in dimension
n=2
and either
0
or
1
in dimension
n=4
. Levinson’s theorem in the form of Equation (

) was first proved in dimension

by Bollé and Osborn
[
11
]
and Newton
[
55
]
and in dimension
n=2
by Bollé et al. in
[

,
14
]
. In higher odd dimensions, Equation (

[
31
,
32
]
and for all other dimensions to Jia, Nicoleau and Wang
[
43
]
.

The computation of the index as a winding number relies on the wave operator

having a particular form. In
[

, Theorem 5]
in dimension

,
[

\displaystyle W_{-}

\displaystyle=\textup{Id}+\varphi(D_{n})(S-\textup{Id})+K,

where

denotes the scattering matrix,

denotes the generator of the dilation group on

\displaystyle\varphi(D_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\textup{Id}+\tanh{(\pi D_{n})}-iA\cosh{(\pi D_{n})}^{-1}\right),

In
[
4

it was shown that if

and the wave operator

is of the form of Equation (

), then there is a pairing between the
K
-theory class
[S]\in K_{1}(C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\otimes\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})))
and the
K
-homology class
[D_{+}]\in K^{1}(C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\otimes\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})))
, where
D_{+}
denotes the generator of the dilation group on

. In addition,
[

shows that this index pairing is computed via

\displaystyle\langle[S],[D_{+}]\rangle

\displaystyle=-\textup{Index}(W_{-}).

The proof of
[
4
, Theorem 4.7]
generalises to a class of ‘admissable’ unitary operators
U
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
commuting with
H_{0}
which, when considered in the spectral representation of
H_{0}
, satisfy
\displaystyle U(0)=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}U(\lambda)=\textup{Id}
. In this case we show in Section
3
that we can construct the operator

\displaystyle W_{U}

\displaystyle=\textup{Id}+\varphi(D_{n})(U-\textup{Id})

and as a consequence we have the pairing

\displaystyle\langle[U],[D_{+}]\rangle

\displaystyle=-\textup{Index}(W_{U}).

In dimension
n=1,3
it is not always the case that
S(0)=\textup{Id}
and thus we introduce an additional unitary
\sigma
so that
S\sigma^{*}
defines an admissable unitary.

Even for an admissable unitary, the limit
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}U(\lambda)=\textup{Id}
holds only in operator norm and not necessarily in trace norm. The pairing gives us the flexibility to choose different representatives of the class
[S]
with better trace norm behaviour at infinity. The remainder of the paper uses this flexibility to find a representative of
[S]
that converges to the identity at infinity in trace norm.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
we fix notation and recall a number of results from scattering theory. In particular the definition of resonances, the high and low energy behaviour of the scattering operator, the spectral shift function and the form of the wave operator. In Section
3
we define a class of Fredholm operators
(W_{U})\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
parametrised by particular admissable unitaries
U\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and show that the pairing between the class
[U]\in K_{1}(C_{c}0(\mathbb{R}^{+})\otimes\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})))
and
[D_{+}]
is computed by the Fredholm index of
W_{U}
. Generically,
S
is an admissable unitary and the class
[S]
can be paired with
[D_{+}]
to compute the Fredholm index of
W_{-}
. When
S(0)\neq\textup{Id}
, an additional unitary is required to account for this low-energy behaviour.

In Section
4
we use the limiting absorption principle to compute the high-energy behaviour of the spectral shift function in terms of particular traces of resolvents. The high-energy behaviour of the spectral shift function is given by a polynomial in the energy, whose coefficients we show are related to the coefficients of a heat-type expansion. Finally, in Section
5
we construct explicit low-energy corrections to the scattering operator in dimension
n=1,3
and high-energy corrections to the scattering operator in dimensions
n\geq 2
, and thus prove Levinson’s theorem in all dimensions by computing the Fredholm index of
W_{-}
as a winding number. As a consequence, we determine the behaviour of the spectral shift function at zero in each dimension, accounting for the presence of resonances.

Preliminaries on scattering theory

Throughout this document we will consider the scattering theory on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
associated to the operators

H_{0}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}=-\Delta\quad\mbox{and}\quad H=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}+V

where the (multiplication operator by the)
potential
V
is real-valued and satisfies

\displaystyle|V(x)|

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|x|)^{-\rho}

(2.1)

for various values of
\rho
depending on the particular result and the dimension
n
.
We denote the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and its dual
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and recall the weighted Sobolev spaces

H^{s,t}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{H^{s,t}}:=\left|\left|(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{t}{2}}(\textup{Id}-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f\right|\right|<\infty\Big\}

with index
s\in\mathbb{R}
indicating derivatives and
t\in\mathbb{R}
associated to decay at infinity
[
6
, Section 4.1]
. With
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the Euclidean inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote the Fourier transform by

\mathcal{F}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\qquad[\mathcal{F}f](\xi)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-i\langle x,\xi\rangle}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Note that the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}
is an isomorphism from
H^{s,t}
to
H^{t,s}
for any
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
. We will frequently drop the reference to the space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for simplicity of notation.

We denote by
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
and
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
the bounded and compact operators from
\mathcal{H}_{1}
to
\mathcal{H}_{2}
and for
p\in\mathbb{N}
we write
\mathcal{L}^{p}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
for the
p
-th Schatten classes. In particular we have the trace class operators
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
and the Hilbert-Schmidt operators
\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2})
. For
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
T\in\mathcal{L}^{p}(\mathcal{H}_{1})
we write
\textup{Det}_{p}(\textup{Id}+T)
for the
p
-th regularised determinant of
\textup{Id}+T
(see
[
64
]
for more details). For
p=1
we obtain the Fredholm determinant.
For
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
, we let

R_{0}(z)=(H_{0}-z)^{-1},\qquad R(z)=(H-z)^{-1}

and the boundary values of the resolvent are defined as

\displaystyle R_{0}(\lambda\pm i0)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{R_{0}(\lambda\pm i\varepsilon)}\quad\mbox{and}\quad R(\lambda\pm i0)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{R(\lambda\pm i\varepsilon)}.

(2.2)

The limiting absorption principle
[
1
, Theorems 4.1 and 4.2]
tells us that these boundary values exist in
\mathcal{B}(H^{0,t},H^{2,-t})
for any
t>\frac{1}{2}
and
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
. The operator
H_{0}
has purely absolutely continuous spectrum, and in particular no kernel. The operator
H
can have eigenvalues and for
V
satisfying Assumption (
2.1
) with
\rho>1
we have that these eigenvalues are negative, or zero
[
66
, Theorem 6.1.1]
.

The one-parameter unitary group of dilations on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
given on
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

\displaystyle[U_{n}(t)f](x)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{nt}{2}}f(\mathrm{e}^{t}x),\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.3)

We denote the self-adjoint generator of
U_{n}
by
D_{n}
.
The generator of the group
(U_{+}(t))
of dilations on the half-line
\mathbb{R}^{+}
is denoted
D_{+}

(which is
D_{1}
restricted to the positive half-line). The generators of the dilation groups are given by

\displaystyle D_{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{y}{i}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}+\frac{1}{2i}{\rm Id},\qquad D_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{x_{j}}{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{n}{2i}{\rm Id}.

(2.4)

Since each of
D_{+},D_{n}
generate one-parameter groups, we can recognise functions of these operators. For
D_{n}
and
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
a bounded function whose Fourier transform has rapid decay and
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\displaystyle[\varphi(D_{n})g](\rho)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{[\mathcal{F}^{*}\varphi](t)\mathrm{e}^{\frac{nt}{2}}g(\mathrm{e}^{t}\rho)\,\mathrm{d}t},

with a similar formula for
D_{+}
.

Several Hilbert spaces recur, and we adopt the notation (following
[
40
, Section 2]
for the relations between the spaces and operators we introduce here)

\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\quad\mathcal{P}=L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}),\quad\mathcal{H}_{spec}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{P})\cong L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\otimes\mathcal{P}.

Here
\mathcal{H}_{spec}
provides the Hilbert space on which we can diagonalise the free Hamiltonian
H_{0}
.

Since
V
is bounded,
H=H_{0}+V
is self-adjoint with
\Dom(H)=\Dom(H_{0})
. The wave operators

W_{\pm}=\mathop{\textup{s-lim}}_{t\to\pm\infty}e^{itH}e^{-itH_{0}}

exist and are asymptotically complete if
\rho>1

[
66
, Theorem 1.6.2]
.
The wave operators are partial isometries satisfying
W_{\pm}^{*}W_{\pm}=\textup{Id}
and
W_{\pm}W_{\pm}^{*}=P_{ac}
, the projection onto the absolutely continuous subspace for
H
. In addition, the wave operators are Fredholm operators and their index can be determined directly in terms of the eigenvalues of
H
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose that
V
satisfies (

) for some

. Let

be the total number of eigenvalues of

, counted with multiplicity. Then

\displaystyle\textup{Index}(W_{\pm})

\displaystyle=-N.

Proof.

This follows from the relations

and
W_{\pm}W_{\pm}^{*}=\textup{Id}-P_{p}
, where

.
∎

For

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=W_{+}^{*}W_{-},

which commutes strongly with the free Hamiltonian

.
For our analysis of the scattering operator, we introduce the unitary which diagonalise the free Hamiltonian.

.

For

,

(
[

)
.

The operator

In dimension

Lemma 2.12

Suppose that

We now recall the spectral shift function

for the pair

Theorem 2.13
.

Suppose that
V
satisfies Assumption
2.4
and let
S
be the corresponding scattering operator. Then there exists a unique (up to an additive constant) real-valued function
\xi(\cdot,H,H_{0}):\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\textup{Tr}(f(H)-f(H_{0}))

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\xi(\lambda,H,H_{0})f^{\prime}(\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,

(2.8)

at least for all
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. We specify
\xi(\cdot,H,H_{0})
uniquely by the convention
\xi(\lambda,H,H_{0})=0
for
\lambda
sufficiently negative and thus
\xi(\cdot,H,H_{0})
satisfies for
\lambda<0
the relation

\displaystyle\xi(\lambda,H,H_{0})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{K}M(\lambda_{k})\chi_{[\lambda_{k},\infty)}(\lambda),

where we we have indexed the distinct eigenvalues of
H
as
\lambda_{1}<\cdots<\lambda_{K}\leq 0
and each
\lambda_{j}
has multiplicity
M(\lambda_{j})
. Furthermore, we have
\xi(\cdot,H,H_{0})|_{(0,\infty)}\in C^{1}(0,\infty)
and for
\lambda>0
the relations

\displaystyle\textup{Det}(S(\lambda))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi i\xi(\lambda)}\quad\textup{ and }\quad\textup{Tr}\left(S(\lambda)^{*}S^{\prime}(\lambda)\right)=-2\pi i\xi^{\prime}(\lambda)

hold.

We call
\xi(\cdot,H,H_{0})
the spectral shift function for the pair
(H,H_{0})
and will often just write
\xi=\xi(\cdot,H,H_{0})
. Using integration by parts we can rewrite the definining property (
2.8
), obtaining the Birman-Kreĭn trace formula.

Lemma 2.14
.

Suppose that
V
satisfies Assumption
2.4
and let
S,\xi
be the corresponding scattering operator and spectral shift function. Then for all
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
we have

\displaystyle\textup{Tr}(f(H)-f(H_{0}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(\lambda)\textup{Tr}\left(S(\lambda)^{*}S^{\prime}(\lambda)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda+\sum_{k=1}^{K}f(\lambda_{k})M(\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle\quad+f(0)\left(\xi(0-)-\xi(0+)-M(0)\right),

where we have defined
\displaystyle\xi(0\pm)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\xi(\pm\varepsilon)
.

In fact by Theorem
2.13
we have, with
N
the total number of eigenvalues of
H
counted with multiplicity, the relation
\xi(0-)=-N+M(0)
. We can then rewrite the Birman-Kreĭn trace formula as

\displaystyle\textup{Tr}(f(H)-f(H_{0}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(\lambda)\textup{Tr}\left(S(\lambda)^{*}S^{\prime}(\lambda)\right)\mathrm{d}\lambda+\sum_{k=1}^{K}f(\lambda_{k})M(\lambda_{k})+f(0)\left(-N-\xi(0+)\right).

Combining the results of Lemmas
2.10
and
2.12

2.13
we can determine the high-energy behaviour of the spectral shift function.

Lemma 2.15
.

Suppose that
\rho
satisfies Assumption
2.4
. If

, let

, if

let

and if
n\geq 4
let

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\left(-2\pi i\xi(\lambda)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{p-1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell}\textup{Tr}\left((q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda+i0)q_{2})^{\ell}-(q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda-i0)q_{2})^{\ell}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=2\pi m

for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}

Proof.

Using Lemma
2.10
and Theorem
2.13
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\textup{Det}_{p}\left(\textup{Id}+q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda-i0)q_{2}\right)}{\textup{Det}_{p}\left(\textup{Id}+q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda+i0)q_{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=\textup{Det}(S(\lambda))\exp\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{p-1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell}\textup{Tr}\left((q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda+i0)q_{2})^{\ell}-(q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda-i0)q_{2})^{\ell}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-2\pi i\xi(\lambda)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{p-1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell}\textup{Tr}\left((q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda+i0)q_{2})^{\ell}-(q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda-i0)q_{2})^{\ell}\right)\right).

from which the result follows.
∎

2.2
The form of the wave operator

Define the operator

\displaystyle\varphi(D_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\textup{Id}+\tanh{(\pi D_{n})}-iA\cosh{(\pi D_{n})}^{-1}\right),

(2.9)

where
A=\textup{Id}
if
n\geq 2
and
A
is a self-adjoint involution which commutes with
D_{1}
if
n=1
.

The following result is the culmination of a number of works,
[
46
, Theorem 5]
in dimension
n=1
,
[
62
, Theorem 1.3]
and
[
3
, Lemma 4.3]
in dimension
n=2
,
[
59
, Theorem 1.1]
in dimension
n=3
,
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
and
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
in dimension
n=4
and
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
in dimension
n\geq 5
.

Theorem 2.17
.

Suppose that
V
satisfies Assumption
2.4
. Then the wave operator
W_{-}
satisfies

\displaystyle W_{-}

\displaystyle=\left(\textup{Id}+\varphi(D_{n})(S-\textup{Id})\right)W_{res}+K,

(2.10)

where
K
is a compact operator,
W_{res}=\textup{Id}
for
n\neq 2,4
and
W_{res}
is a Fredholm operator depending on the existence of
s
-resonances in dimension
n=4
and on the existence of
p
-resonances in dimension
n=2
. In particular, for
n=4
we have
\textup{Index}(W_{res})
is equal to the number of
s
-resonances and for
n=2
we have
\textup{Index}(W_{res})
is equal to the number of
p
-resonances.

The explicit form of
W_{res}
is not important for the analysis we present, and has been discussed thoroughly in dimension
n=2
in
[
3
]
and in dimension
n=4
in
[
5
]
.

3
Generalised wave operators and index pairings

Motivated by the form of the wave operator in Theorem
2.17
we define a class of Fredholm operators, parametrised by particular unitaries, which allow us to reinterpret Levinson’s theorem as an index pairing, as well as accounting for the contribution of resonances in dimensions
n=1,3
. Let
D_{n}
be as in Equation (
2.4
) and let
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a self-adjoint involution which commutes with
D_{n}
. Define the operator
\varphi(D_{n})
by Equation (
2.9
).

If
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
commutes with
H_{0}
, there exists a family
\{T(\lambda)\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}
of operators in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{P})
such that
[F_{0}TF_{0}^{*}f](\lambda,\omega)=T(\lambda)f(\lambda,\omega)
for all
f\in\mathcal{H}_{spec}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. We call
T(\cdot)
the matrix of
T
.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that a unitary
U\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is admissable if
[U,H_{0}]=0
, the matrix of
U
is norm continuous in
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
U(\lambda)-\textup{Id}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{P})
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. We say that
U
is properly admissable if in addition we have
\displaystyle U(0)=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}U(\lambda)=\textup{Id}
, where the limit is taken in the norm of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{P})
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
U\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a properly admissable unitary. Then
U
defines a
K
-theory class
[U]\in K_{1}\left(\left(C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\otimes\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{P})\right)^{\sim}\right)
.

Proof.

Since
U-\textup{Id}\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\otimes\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{P})
,
U\in\left(C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\otimes\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{P})\right)^{\sim}
and thus defines a
K
-theory class
[U]\in K_{1}\left(\left(C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\otimes\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{P})\right)^{\sim}\right)
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
U\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a properly admissable unitary. Then the operator
W_{U}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
defined by

\displaystyle W_{U}

\displaystyle=\textup{Id}+\varphi(D_{n})(U-\textup{Id})

(3.1)

is a Fredholm operator.

Proof.

We show that
W_{U}^{*}
is an inverse for
W_{U}
up to compacts. We compute that

\displaystyle W_{U}W_{U}^{*}

\displaystyle=\left(\textup{Id}+\varphi(D_{n})(U-\textup{Id})\right)\left(\textup{Id}+\left(U^{*}-\textup{Id}\right)\varphi(D_{n})^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\textup{Id}+\varphi(D_{n})(U-\textup{Id})+(U^{*}-\textup{Id})\varphi(D_{n})^{*}+\varphi(D_{n})(U-\textup{Id})(U^{*}-\textup{Id})\varphi(D_{n})^{*}.

By
[
6
, Theorem 4.1.10]
the commutators
[U^{*},\varphi(D_{n})^{*}]
and
[U+U^{*},\varphi(D_{n})^{*}]
are compact and thus we can write

\displaystyle W_{U}W_{U}^{*}

\displaystyle=\textup{Id}+\varphi(D_{n})(U-\textup{Id})+\varphi(D_{n})^{*}(U^{*}-\textup{Id})+\varphi(D_{n})\varphi(D_{n})^{*}(U-\textup{Id})(U^{*}-\textup{Id})+K_{1},

where
K_{1}
is compact. By
[
4
, Lemma 3.3]
the operator
(\varphi(D_{n})^{*}-\varphi(D_{n}))(U^{*}-\textup{Id})
is compact and so we have

\displaystyle W_{U}W_{U}^{*}

\displaystyle=\textup{Id}+\varphi(D_{n})(U-\textup{Id})+\varphi(D_{n})(U^{*}-\textup{Id})+\varphi(D_{n})\varphi(D_{n})^{*}(U-\textup{Id})(U^{*}-\textup{Id})+K_{2},

where
K_{2}
is compact. Using the identity
\cosh{(\cdot)}^{-2}+\tanh{(\cdot)}^{2}=1
and the fact that
A
is a self-adjoint involution commuting with all functions of
D_{n}
we find

\displaystyle\varphi(D_{n})\varphi(D_{n})^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\textup{Id}+\tanh{(\pi D_{n})}\right).

A further application of
[
4
, Lemma 3.3]
then gives that
\left(\varphi(D_{n})\varphi(D_{n})^{*}-\varphi(D_{n})\right)(U-\textup{Id})
is a compact operator and so we have

\displaystyle W_{U}W_{U}^{*}

\displaystyle=\textup{Id}+\varphi(D_{n})(U-\textup{Id})+\varphi(D_{n})(U^{*}-\textup{Id})+\varphi(D_{n})(U-\textup{Id})(U^{*}-\textup{Id})+K_{3}

\displaystyle=\textup{Id}+K_{3},

where
K_{3}
is a compact operator. A similar calculation shows that
W_{U}^{*}
is a right inverse for
W_{U}
up to compacts and so
W_{U}
defines a Fredholm operator.
∎

Since the composition of properly admissable unitaries is itself a properly admissable unitary, we have a natural product rule for operators of the form
W_{U}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
U_{1},U_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be properly admissable unitaries. For
j=1,2
, define operators
W_{U_{j}}
by Equation (
3.1
). Then we have
W_{U_{1}}W_{U_{2}}=W_{U_{1}U_{2}}
, up to a compact operator.

Proof.

We compute the product

\displaystyle W_{U_{1}}W_{U_{2}}

\displaystyle=\textup{Id}+\varphi(D_{n})(U_{1}+U_{2}-2\textup{Id})+\varphi(D_{n})(U_{1}-\textup{Id})\varphi(D_{n})(U_{2}-\textup{Id}).

Noting by
[
6
, Theorem 4.1.10]
that the commutator
[U_{1},\varphi(D_{n})]
is compact we have

\displaystyle W_{U_{1}}W_{U_{2}}

\displaystyle=\textup{Id}+\varphi(D_{n})(U_{1}+U_{2}-2\textup{Id})+\varphi(D_{n})^{2}(U_{1}-\textup{Id})(U_{2}-\textup{Id})+K_{1}

for a compact operator
K_{1}
. Using, as in the proof of Lemma
3.3
, that the operator given by
\left(\varphi(D_{n})\varphi(D_{n})^{*}-\varphi(D_{n})\right)(U_{1}-\textup{Id})
is compact, we find

\displaystyle W_{U_{1}}W_{U_{2}}

\displaystyle=\textup{Id}+\varphi(D_{n})(U_{1}+U_{2}-2\textup{Id})+\varphi(D_{n})(U_{1}-\textup{Id})(U_{2}-\textup{Id})+K_{2}

\displaystyle=\textup{Id}+\varphi(D_{n})\left(U_{1}U_{2}-\textup{Id}\right)+K_{2}

\displaystyle=W_{U_{1}U_{2}}+K_{2},

for a compact operator
K_{2}
.
∎

In fact Lemma
3.4
can be extended to show that we have a partially defined product rule.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
U_{1},U_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be admissable unitary operators with
U_{1}(0)=U_{2}(0)
and
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}U_{1}(\lambda)=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}U_{2}(\lambda)
, where the limits are taken in the norm of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{P})
. For
j=1,2
, define operators
W_{U_{j}}
by Equation (
3.1
). Then we have
W_{U_{1}}=W_{U_{1}U_{2}^{*}}W_{U_{2}}
, up to a compact operator.

Proof.

Note that
U_{1}U_{2}^{*}
is a properly admissable unitary by construction. Hence we can apply (the proof of) Lemma
3.4
to see that

\displaystyle W_{U_{1}U_{2}^{*}}W_{U_{2}}

\displaystyle=W_{U_{1}}+K

for some compact operator
K
.
∎

The following useful property shows that even if neither of the unitaries
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are properly admissable, we may still deduce the Fredholm property of
W_{U_{2}}
given that of
W_{U_{1}}
and that both are close enough in some sense.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
U_{1},U_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be admissable unitary operators with
U_{1}(0)=U_{2}(0)
and
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}U_{1}(\lambda)=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}U_{2}(\lambda)
, where the limits are taken in the norm of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{P})
. For
j=1,2
, define operators
W_{U_{j}}
by Equation (
3.1
). Then if one of the operators
W_{U_{j}}
is Fredholm, so is the other.

Proof.

Suppose, without loss of generality, that
W_{U_{2}}
is Fredholm. Then since the unitary
U_{1}U_{2}^{*}
is properly admissable, we have
W_{U_{1}U_{2}^{*}}
is Fredholm by Lemma
3.3
. Since the composition of Fredholm operators is also Fredholm, Lemma
3.5
shows that
W_{U_{1}}
is Fredholm also.
∎

3.1
The index pairing

We now show how the form of the wave operator implies, generically, that the number of bound states can be computed as an index pairing between
K
-theory and
K
-homology, see
[
16
,
33
]
.

We begin by recalling the definition of a spectral triple.

Definition 3.7
.

An odd spectral triple
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{D})
is given by a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, a dense

*
-subalgebra
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
acting on
\mathcal{H}
, and a densely defined unbounded self-adjoint operator
\mathcal{D}
such that

1.

a\cdot\textup{Dom}(\mathcal{D})\subset\textup{Dom}(\mathcal{D})
for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
, so that
\mathrm{d}a:=[\mathcal{D},a]
is densely defined. Moreover
\mathrm{d}a
extends to a bounded operator for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
;

2.

a(\textup{Id}+\mathcal{D}^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
.

The following is
[
4
, Corollary 4.4]
, providing the spectral triple of interest to us for scattering purposes.

Lemma 3.8
.

The data
\left(C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\otimes\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{P}),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\otimes\mathcal{P},D_{+}\otimes\textup{Id}\right)
defines an odd spectral triple, and so a class
[D_{+}]
in odd
K
-homology.

Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.8
tell us that for a properly admissable unitary
U
we can pair the classes
[D_{+}]
and
[U]
to obtain an integer (see
[
33
, Section 8.7]
for details). The following can be proved in an identical manner to
[
4
, Theorem 4.7]
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
U\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a properly admissable unitary and define
W_{U}
as in Equation (
3.1
). Let
[D_{+}]
be defined by Lemma
3.8
. Then

\displaystyle\langle[U],[D_{+}]\rangle

\displaystyle=-\textup{Index}(W_{U}).

Generically, we have that
S(0)=\textup{Id}
and thus we obtain, as in
[
4
, Theorem 4.7]
the following pairing for scattering operators.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
H=H_{0}+V
be such that the wave operators exist, are complete and are of the form of Equation (
3.1
). Let
S
be the corresponding scattering operator. If
S(0)=\textup{Id}
then we have the pairing

\displaystyle\langle[S],[D_{+}]\rangle

\displaystyle=-\textup{Index}(W_{-})=N,

the number of bound states for
H
.

We note that the
S(0)\neq\textup{Id}
case cannot immediately be handled by the simple pairing desribed in Lemma
3.10
and we address this in Section
5
. In the presence of
p
-resonances in dimension
n=2
and
s
-resonances in dimension
n=4
the wave operator is not of the form of Equation (
3.1
) and thus requires some modification, resulting in the following.

Lemma 3.11
.

Suppose that
V
satisfies Assumption
2.4
,
n=2,4
and let
S
be the corresponding scattering operator. Let
W_{res}
be as in Theorem
2.17
. Then we have the pairing

\displaystyle\langle[D_{+}],[S]\rangle

\displaystyle=-\textup{Index}(W_{-}W_{res}^{*})=N+\textup{Index}(W_{res}),

where
\textup{Index}(W_{res})
is the number of
p
-resonances if
n=2
and the number of
s
-resonances if
n=4
.

4
Trace class properties of the scattering matrix

In this section we describe the high-energy behaviour of the scattering operator in the trace norm using the expression for
\textup{Det}(S(\lambda))
of Lemma
2.10
. As a result we determine, using pseudodifferential expansions of the resolvent and the limiting absorption principle, the leading order high-energy asymptotics of the spectral shift function
\xi
and its derivative. These leading order asymptotics are related to the heat kernel expansion as
t\to 0^{+}
of the trace of the difference
\mathrm{e}^{-tH}-\mathrm{e}^{-tH_{0}}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
g_{1},g_{2}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
are compactly supported with
g=g_{1}g_{2}
. For
\lambda>0
define the operator
B(\lambda)\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
by
B(\lambda)=g_{1}\left(R_{0}(\lambda+i0)-R_{0}(\lambda-i0)\right)g_{2}
. Then
B(\lambda)\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{H})
and

\displaystyle\textup{Tr}\left(B(\lambda)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi i)\lambda^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\textup{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}{2(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Furthermore, for
t>\frac{1}{2}
and
\lambda>0
we have the equality
R_{0}(\lambda+i0)-R_{0}(\lambda-i0)=2\pi i\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)^{*}\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)
as operators in
\mathcal{B}(H^{0,t},H^{0,-t})
.

Proof.

Fix
t>\frac{1}{2}
and
\lambda>0
. As an operator in
\mathcal{B}(H^{0,t},H^{0,-t})
we have (see
[
1
, Equation 4.3]
and
[
7
, Equation 15]
) that
A(\lambda)=R_{0}(\lambda+i0)-R_{0}(\lambda-i0)
is an integral operator with integral kernel

\displaystyle A(\lambda,x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi i)}{2(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\mathrm{e}^{i\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\omega,x-y\rangle}\,\mathrm{d}\omega,

for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Direct computation shows that the integral kernel of
(2\pi i)\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)^{*}\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)
is the same as that of
A(\lambda)
.

That
B(\lambda)
is trace class is proved in
[
31
, Lemma III.2]
(see also the comments on the bottom of page
32
), however we provide an alternative proof. For any
t>\frac{1}{2}
direct computation shows that
g_{1}\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)^{*}\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{H})
and
\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)g_{2}\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{P})
and thus
B(\lambda)\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{H})
.

We can then compute the trace of
B(\lambda)
by integrating along the diagonal, which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The assumption of compact support on the potential
V
is stronger than necessary to guarantee that the operator
B(\lambda)
is trace class. It is sufficient that the multiplication operators corresponding to
g_{1},g_{2}
map
\mathcal{H}
into
H^{0,t}
for some
t>\frac{1}{2}
. The computation of the trace of
B(\lambda)
was first done by Buslaev
[
15
]
(see also Newton
[
55
]
and Bollé and Osborn
[
11
]
).

In fact we can differentiate
B(\lambda)
arbitrarily in
\lambda
in the trace norm.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose that
g_{1},g_{2}
are compactly supported and
g=g_{1}g_{2}
. For
\lambda>0
define the operator
B(\lambda)\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{H})
by
B(\lambda)=g_{1}\left(R_{0}(\lambda+i0)-R_{0}(\lambda-i0)\right)g_{2}
. Then
B(\lambda)
is differentiable in
\lambda
in the norm of
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{H})
and for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{\ell-1}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\ell-1}}B(\lambda)

\displaystyle=(\ell-1)!g_{1}\left(R_{0}(\lambda+i0)^{\ell}-R_{0}(\lambda-i0)^{\ell}\right)g_{2}.

(4.1)

In particular for
\lambda>0
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
we have
that
g_{1}\left(R_{0}(\lambda+i0)^{\ell}-R_{0}(\lambda-i0)^{\ell}\right)g_{2}
has for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the integral kernel

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi i)\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-\ell}}{2(\ell-1)!\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+1-\ell\right)(2\pi)^{n}}g_{1}(x)g_{2}(y)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\mathrm{e}^{-i\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\omega,y-x\rangle}\,\mathrm{d}\omega+\tilde{A}(\lambda,x,y)

where
\tilde{A}(\lambda,\cdot,\cdot)
vanishes on the diagonal and thus

\displaystyle\textup{Tr}\left(g_{1}\left(R_{0}(\lambda+i0)^{\ell}-R_{0}(\lambda-i0)^{\ell}\right)g_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi i)\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-\ell}\textup{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}{2(\ell-1)!\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+1-\ell\right)(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(4.2)

Proof.

That
B(\lambda)
is differentiable is proved in
[
66
, Lemma 8.1.8]
and so we obtain Equation (
4.1
). Differentiating
(\ell-1)
times the integral kernel for
B(\lambda)
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{\ell-1}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\ell-1}}B(\lambda,x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi i)}{2(2\pi)^{n}}g_{1}(x)g_{2}(y)\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\binom{\ell-1}{j}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}^{j}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda^{j}}\lambda^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}^{\ell-1-j}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda^{\ell-1-j}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\mathrm{e}^{i\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\omega,x-y\rangle}\,\mathrm{d}\omega.

By factoring as the product of two Hilbert-Schmidt operators as in Lemma
4.1
, each term in the sum is individually trace class. All terms except the
j=\ell-1
term vanish on the diagonal since they contain an
\langle\omega,x-y\rangle^{j}
term, so integrating over the diagonal gives Equation (
4.2
).
∎

Remark 4.4
.

The differentiability of
B(\lambda)
and Equation (
4.1
) can be checked directly at the level of kernels. Doing so demonstrates that again the assumption of compact support is stronger than necessary, it is enough that the multiplication operators
(1+|\cdot|)^{\ell}g_{1},(1+|\cdot|)^{\ell}g_{2}
map
\mathcal{H}
into
H^{0,t}
for some
t>\frac{1}{2}
.

To evaluate some further traces, we need to be able to integrate polynomials over
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. We use the following result
[
26
]
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\alpha
be a multi-index of length
n
and let
P_{\alpha}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be given by
P_{\alpha}(x)=x^{\alpha}=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}P_{\alpha}(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0,\quad&\textup{ if some }\alpha_{j}\textup{ is odd,}\\
\frac{2\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+1}{2}\right)\cdots\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{n}+1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+|\alpha|}{2}\right)},\quad&\textup{ if all }\alpha_{j}\textup{ are even.}\end{cases}

Lemma 4.6
.

Suppose that
\alpha
is a multi-index of length
n
,
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and let
X=g\partial^{\alpha}
, a differential operator of order
|\alpha|
. Then for
t>0
we have

\displaystyle\textup{Tr}\left(X\mathrm{e}^{-tH_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-i)^{|\alpha|}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+1}{2}\right)\cdots\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{n}+1}{2}\right)t^{-\frac{n+|\alpha|}{2}}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)

if all
\alpha_{j}
are even and
\textup{Tr}\left(X\mathrm{e}^{-tH_{0}}\right)=0
otherwise.

Proof.

We compute the trace as

\displaystyle\textup{Tr}\left(X\mathrm{e}^{-tH_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-i)^{|\alpha|}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}y^{\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-t|y|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right).

If any of the components
\alpha_{j}
is odd, then by changing to polar coordinates we find that
\textup{Tr}\left(X\mathrm{e}^{-tH_{0}}\right)=0
by Lemma
4.5
. If all of the
\alpha_{j}
are even we obtain

\displaystyle\textup{Tr}\left(X\mathrm{e}^{-tH_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2(-i)^{|\alpha|}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+1}{2}\right)\cdots\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{n}+1}{2}\right)}{(2\pi)^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{n+|\alpha|}{2}\right)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{|\alpha|+n-1}\mathrm{e}^{-tr^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}r\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-i)^{|\alpha|}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+1}{2}\right)\cdots\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{n}+1}{2}\right)t^{-\frac{n+|\alpha|}{2}}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right),

where we have used polar coordinates and Lemma
4.5
to compute the spherical integral.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Suppose that
\alpha
is a multi-index of length
n
,
g_{1},g_{2}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and let
g=g_{1}g_{2}
. Let
X=g_{2}\partial^{\alpha}
be a differential operator of order
|\alpha|
. Fix
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
with
\ell\leq\frac{n+|\alpha|}{2}
if
n
is even. Then for
\lambda>0
we have

\displaystyle\textup{Tr}\left(X\left(R_{0}(\lambda+i0)^{\ell}-R_{0}(\lambda-i0)^{\ell}\right)g_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-i)^{|\alpha|}(2\pi i)\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+1}{2}\right)\cdots\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{n}+1}{2}\right)\lambda^{\frac{n+|\alpha|}{2}-\ell}}{(\ell-1)!\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+1-\ell+\frac{|\alpha|}{2}\right)(2\pi)^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)

if all
\alpha_{j}
are even and
\textup{Tr}\left(X\left(R_{0}(\lambda+i0)^{\ell}-R_{0}(\lambda-i0)^{\ell}\right)g_{1}\right)=0
otherwise. If
n
is even and
\ell>\frac{n+|\alpha|}{2}
then
\textup{Tr}\left(X\left(R_{0}(\lambda+i0)^{\ell}-R_{0}(\lambda-i0)^{\ell}\right)g_{1}\right)=0
also.

Proof.

Fix
\lambda>0
. The integral kernel of
B(\lambda)=\left(R_{0}(\lambda+i0)-R_{0}(\lambda-i0)\right)g_{1}
is given by

\displaystyle B(\lambda,x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi i)\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}{2(2\pi)^{n}}g_{1}(y)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\mathrm{e}^{-i\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\omega,y-x\rangle}\,\mathrm{d}\omega.

Letting
A(\lambda)=X\left(R_{0}(\lambda+i0)-R_{0}(\lambda-i0)\right)g_{1}
we see by Lemma
4.1
that
A(\lambda)
has integral kernel

\displaystyle A(\lambda,x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-i)^{|\alpha|}(2\pi i)\lambda^{\frac{n+|\alpha|}{2}-1}}{2(2\pi)^{n}}g_{2}(x)g_{1}(y)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\omega_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\omega_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-i\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\omega,y-x\rangle}\,\mathrm{d}\omega.

Applying now the techniques of Lemma
4.3
we differentiate
(\ell-1)
times to find that the operator
A_{\ell}(\lambda)=X(R_{0}(\lambda+i0)^{\ell}-R_{0}(\lambda-i0)^{\ell})
has integral kernel

\displaystyle A_{\ell}(\lambda,x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-i)^{|\alpha|}(2\pi i)\Gamma\left(\frac{n+|\alpha|}{2}\right)\lambda^{\frac{n+|\alpha|}{2}-1}}{2\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+1-\ell+\frac{|\alpha|}{2}\right)(2\pi)^{n}}g_{2}(x)g_{1}(y)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\omega_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\omega_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-i\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\omega,y-x\rangle}\,\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle\quad+\tilde{A}(\lambda,x,y),

where
\tilde{A}(\lambda,\cdot,\cdot)
has vanishing trace.
Integrating over the diagonal gives that

\displaystyle\textup{Tr}\left(X\left(R_{0}(\lambda+i0)^{\ell}-R_{0}(\lambda-i0)^{\ell}\right)g_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-i)^{|\alpha|}(2\pi i)\Gamma\left(\frac{n+|\alpha|}{2}\right)\lambda^{\frac{n+|\alpha|}{2}-\ell}}{2(\ell-1)!\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+1-\ell+\frac{|\alpha|}{2}\right)(2\pi)^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\omega_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\omega_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\right).

If some
\alpha_{j}
is odd we find
\textup{Tr}\left(X\left(R_{0}(\lambda+i0)^{\ell}-R_{0}(\lambda-i0)^{\ell}\right)g_{1}\right)=0
by Lemma
4.5
. If all
\alpha_{j}
are even we have

\displaystyle\textup{Tr}\left(X\left(R_{0}(\lambda+i0)^{\ell}-R_{0}(\lambda-i0)^{\ell}\right)g_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-i)^{|\alpha|}(2\pi i)\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}+1}{2}\right)\cdots\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha_{n}+1}{2}\right)\lambda^{\frac{n+|\alpha|}{2}-\ell}}{(\ell-1)!\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+1-\ell+\frac{|\alpha|}{2}\right)(2\pi)^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right),

again by Lemma
4.5
.
∎

For the next statement, we introduce for
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the notation
f^{(m)}=[H_{0},[H_{0},[\cdots,[H_{0},f]\cdots]]]
(where the expression has
m
commutators). Note that
f^{(m)}
is a differential operator of order
m
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Suppose that
q_{1},q_{2}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
V=q_{1}q_{2}
. Then for all
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
K\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
we have

\displaystyle(q_{1}R_{0}(z)q_{2})^{\ell}

\displaystyle=q_{1}\left(\sum_{|k|=0}^{K}(-1)^{|k|+1}C_{\ell-1}(k)V^{(k_{1})}\cdots V^{(k_{\ell-1})}R_{0}(z)^{\ell+|k|}\right)q_{2}+q_{1}P_{K,\ell}(z)q_{2},

(4.3)

where
P_{K,\ell}(z)
is of order at most
-2\ell-K-1
,
k
is a multi-index of length
(\ell-1)
and

\displaystyle C_{\ell}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{(|k|+\ell)!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{\ell}!(k_{1}+1)(k_{1}+k_{2}+2)\cdots(|k|+\ell)}.

When
\ell=1
we have no remainder term.
For all
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
,
M\in\mathbb{N}
and
K\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
we have

\displaystyle R(z)-R_{0}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left(\sum_{|k|=0}^{K}(-1)^{m+|k|}C_{m}(k)V^{(k_{1})}\cdots V^{(k_{m})}R_{0}(z)^{m+|k|+1}+P_{K,m}(z)\right)

(4.4)

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{M+1}(R_{0}(z)V)^{M+1}R(z),

where
P_{K,m}(z)
has order (at most)
-2m-K-3
and
k
is a multi-index of length
m
.

Proof.

Equation (
4.3
) follows from the pseudodifferential calculus of
[
18
, Lemma 6.11]
. For Equation (
4.4
), we write

\displaystyle R(z)-R_{0}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{M}(-1)^{m}(R_{0}(z)V)^{m}R_{0}(z)+(-1)^{M+1}(R_{0}(z)V)^{M+1}R(z).

Applying again
[
18
, Lemma 6.11]
we have

\displaystyle R(z)-R_{0}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left(\sum_{|k|=0}^{K}(-1)^{m+|k|}C_{m}(k)V^{(k_{1})}\cdots V^{(k_{m})}R_{0}(z)^{m+|k|+1}+P_{K,m}(z)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{M+1}(R_{0}(z)V)^{M+1}R(z),

where
P_{K,m}(z)
has order (at most)
-2m-K-3
.
∎

Since
V\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
k
a multi-index of length
\ell
we have
V^{(k_{1})}\cdots V^{(k_{\ell})}
is a differential operator of order
|k|
with smooth compactly supported coefficients, we can write

\displaystyle V^{(k_{1})}\cdots V^{(k_{\ell})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|r|=0}^{|k|}g_{k,r}\partial^{r},

(4.5)

with
r
a multi-index with
n
components and
g_{r,k}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. With this notation, we obtain the following heat kernel expansion due to Colin de Verdière
[
20
]
in odd dimensions (see also
[
25
, Theorem 3.64]
for a general proof and
[
8
]
for explicit computations of some coefficients). We provide a detailed proof using the pseudodifferential expansion of Lemma
4.8
and the trace formulae of Lemma
4.6
. As a result we obtain an expression for the coefficients which differs from
[
25
, Theorem 3.64]
or
[
8
]
, although is equivalent. This new expression is necessary for a comparison with the high-energy behaviour of the scattering operator, as we show in Theorem
4.15
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Suppose that
V\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then for any
J\in\mathbb{N}
we have the expansion

\displaystyle\textup{Tr}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-tH}-\mathrm{e}^{-tH_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{J}a_{j}(n,V)t^{k-\frac{n}{2}}+E_{J}(t),

where
E_{J}(t)=O\left(t^{J+1-\frac{n}{2}}\right)
as
t\to 0^{+}
. For
j\in\mathbb{N}
we define the set

\displaystyle Q_{M,K}(j)

\displaystyle=\bigg\{(m,k,r)\in\{0,1,\dots,M\}\times\{0,1,\dots,K\}^{m}\times\{0,1,\dots,K\}^{n}:|r|\leq|k|,

\displaystyle\quad\textup{all }r_{j}\textup{ are even and }m+|k|+1-\frac{|r|}{2}=j\bigg\}.

The constants
a_{j}(n,V)
are given by

\displaystyle a_{j}(n,V)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(m,k,r)\in Q_{M,K}(j)}\frac{(-i)^{|r|}C_{m}(k)(-1)^{m+|k|+1}\Gamma\left(\frac{r_{1}+1}{2}\right)\cdots\Gamma\left(\frac{r_{n}+1}{2}\right)}{(2\pi)^{n}(m+1)(m+|k|)!}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V(x)g_{k,r}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right),

where we have used the notation of Equation (
4.5
).

Proof.

Fix
t>0
. By
[
25
, Theorem 3.64]
we have
\mathrm{e}^{-tH}-\mathrm{e}^{-tH_{0}}\in\mathcal{L^{1}}(\mathcal{H})
. Choose
a>0
such that
a>|\lambda|
for all
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(H)
and define the vertical line
\gamma_{t}=\{-a-\frac{1}{t}+iv:v\in\mathbb{R}\}
. We write

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-tH}-\mathrm{e}^{-tH_{0}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\mathrm{e}^{-t(H_{0}+sV)}\,\mathrm{d}s=-t\int_{0}^{1}V\mathrm{e}^{-t(H_{0}+sV)}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Cauchy’s integral theorem tells us that for all
s\in[0,1]
we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-t(H_{0}+sV)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma_{t}}\mathrm{e}^{-tz}(z-H_{0}-sV)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}z=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma_{t}}\mathrm{e}^{-tz}R_{s}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z,

where we have introduced the notation
R_{s}(z)=(H_{0}+sV-z)^{-1}
. An application of Lemma
4.8
gives us for all
M,K\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
the expansion

\displaystyle R_{s}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{M}\left(\sum_{|k|=0}^{K}(-1)^{m+|k|}s^{m}C_{m}(k)V^{(k_{1})}\cdots V^{(k_{m})}R_{0}(z)^{m+|k|+1}+s^{m}P_{K,m}(z)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{M+1}s^{M+1}(R_{0}(z)V)^{M+1}R_{s}(z).

Thus we write

\displaystyle\textup{Tr}\left(V\mathrm{e}^{-t(H_{0}+sV)}\right)=-\textup{Tr}\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma_{t}}\mathrm{e}^{-tz}R_{s}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\bigg)

\displaystyle=-\textup{Tr}\Bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma_{t}}\mathrm{e}^{-tz}\sum_{m=0}^{M}\sum_{|k|=0}^{K}(-1)^{m+|k|}s^{m}C_{m}(k)VV^{(k_{1})}\cdots V^{(k_{m})}R_{0}(z)^{m+|k|+1}\,\mathrm{d}z\Bigg)

\displaystyle-\textup{Tr}\Bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma_{t}}\mathrm{e}^{-tz}\sum_{m=0}^{M}s^{m}VP_{K,m}(z)\mathrm{d}z-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma_{t}}\mathrm{e}^{-tz}(-1)^{M+1}s^{M+1}V(R_{0}(z)V)^{M+1}R_{s}(z)\mathrm{d}z\Bigg)

\displaystyle:=-\textup{Tr}\Bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma_{t}}\mathrm{e}^{-tz}\sum_{m=0}^{M}\sum_{|k|=0}^{K}(-1)^{m+|k|}s^{m}C_{m}(k)VV^{(k_{1})}\cdots V^{(k_{m})}R_{0}(z)^{m+|k|+1}\,\mathrm{d}z\Bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+e_{M,K}(t,s).

We now apply Cauchy’s integral formula again to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma_{t}}\mathrm{e}^{-tz}R_{0}(z)^{m+|k|+1}\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m+|k|+1}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma_{t}}\mathrm{e}^{-tz}(z-H_{0})^{-m-|k|-1}\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{m+|k|+1}}{(m+|k|)!}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{m+|k|}}{dz^{m+|k|}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-tz}\right)|_{z=H_{0}}=-\frac{t^{m+|k|}}{(m+|k|)!}\mathrm{e}^{-tH_{0}}.

Hence we find

\displaystyle\textup{Tr}\left(V\mathrm{e}^{-t(H_{0}+sV)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{M}\sum_{|k|=0}^{K}(-1)^{m+|k|}s^{m}\frac{C_{m}(k)}{(m+|k|)!}t^{m+|k|}\textup{Tr}\left(VV^{(k_{1})}\cdots V^{(k_{m})}\mathrm{e}^{-tH_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+e_{M,K}(t,s).

Integrating out the
s
variable we have

\displaystyle\textup{Tr}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-tH}-\mathrm{e}^{-tH_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=-t\int_{0}^{1}\textup{Tr}\left(V\mathrm{e}^{-t(H_{0}+sV)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{M}\sum_{|k|=0}^{K}\frac{(-1)^{m+|k|+1}C_{m}(k)}{(m+1)(m+|k|)!}t^{m+|k|+1}\textup{Tr}\left(VV^{(k_{1})}\cdots V^{(k_{m})}\mathrm{e}^{-tH_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-t\int_{0}^{1}e_{M,K}(t,s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

We now use Lemma
4.6
to write

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{M}\sum_{|k|=0}^{K}\frac{(-1)^{m+|k|+1}C_{m}(k)}{(m+1)(m+|k|)!}t^{m+|k|+1}\textup{Tr}\left(VV^{(k_{1})}\cdots V^{(k_{m})}\mathrm{e}^{-tH_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{M}\sum_{|k|=0}^{K}\mathop{\sum_{|r|=0}^{|k|}}_{r\textup{ even}}\frac{(-i)^{|r|}(-1)^{\ell+m+|k|}C_{m}(k)\Gamma\left(\frac{r_{1}+1}{2}\right)\cdots\Gamma\left(\frac{r_{n}+1}{2}\right)t^{m+|k|+1-\frac{n+|r|}{2}}}{(m+1)(m+|k|)!(2\pi)^{n}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V(x)g_{k,r}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right),

where the sum is over all multi-indices
r
of length
n
such that all
r_{j}
are even. We now collect together powers of
t
. For
j\in\mathbb{N}
we define the set

\displaystyle Q_{M,K}(j)

\displaystyle=\bigg\{(m,k,r)\in\{0,1,\dots,M\}\times\{0,1,\dots,K\}^{m}\times\{0,1,\dots,K\}^{n}:|r|\leq|k|,

\displaystyle\quad\textup{all }r_{j}\textup{ are even and }m+|k|+1-\frac{|r|}{2}=j\bigg\}

and the coefficients

\displaystyle a_{j}(n,V)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(m,k,r)\in Q_{M,K}(j)}\frac{(-i)^{|r|}(-1)^{m+|k|+1}C_{m}(k)\Gamma\left(\frac{r_{1}+1}{2}\right)\cdots\Gamma\left(\frac{r_{n}+1}{2}\right)}{(m+1)(m+|k|)!(2\pi)^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V(x)g_{k,r}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right).

The
a_{j}(n,V)
allow us to write

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{M}\sum_{|k|=0}^{K}\frac{(-1)^{m+|k|+1}C_{m}(k)}{(m+1)(m+|k|)!}t^{m+|k|+1}\textup{Tr}\left(VV^{\{k_{1}\}}\cdots V^{\{k_{m}\}}\mathrm{e}^{-tH_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{M+1}a_{j}(n,V)t^{-\frac{n}{2}+j}+G(t),

where
G(t)=O(t^{M+2-\frac{n}{2}})
as
t\to 0^{+}
. Thus we have the expansion

\displaystyle\textup{Tr}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-tH}-\mathrm{e}^{-tH_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{M+1}a_{j}(n,V)t^{-\frac{n}{2}+j}+G(t)-t\int_{0}^{1}e_{M,K}(t,s).

It remains to check the behaviour of the final remainder term, which consists of two types of terms. Recall that the operator
P_{K,m}(z)
has order (at most)
-2m-K-3
. By
[
18
, Lemma 6.12]
there exists a constant
C
(depending on
V
,
m
and
K
but not
z
) such that

\displaystyle\left|\left|R_{0}(z)^{-m-\frac{K}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}q_{2}P_{K,m}(z)\right|\right|

\displaystyle\leq C.

Choose
K=n-m
, large enough so that
q_{1}R_{0}(z)^{m+\frac{K}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}
defines a trace-class operator. So we use Hölder’s inequality for Schatten classes to find

\displaystyle\left|\left|VP_{K,m}(z)\right|\right|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\left|\left|q_{1}R_{0}(z)^{m+\frac{K}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}\right|\right|_{1}.

We can now estimate the remainder term via

\displaystyle\left|\left|\int_{0}^{1}s^{m}\int_{\gamma_{t}}\mathrm{e}^{-tz}P_{K,m}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}s\right|\right|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{m+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{at+1}\left|\left|q_{1}R_{0}(z)^{m+\frac{K}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}\right|\right|_{1}\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\mathrm{e}^{at+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\left(a+\frac{1}{t}\right)^{2}+v^{2}\right)^{-\frac{m}{2}-\frac{K}{4}-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{n}{4}}\,\mathrm{d}v.

Make the substitution
v=\left(a+\frac{1}{t}\right)w
to find

\displaystyle\left|\left|\int_{0}^{1}s^{m}\int_{\gamma_{t}}\mathrm{e}^{-tz}P_{K,m}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}s\right|\right|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\mathrm{e}^{at+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\left(a+\frac{1}{t}\right)^{2}+v^{2}\right)^{-\frac{m}{2}-\frac{K}{4}-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{n}{4}}\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\tilde{C}\mathrm{e}^{at}\left(a+\frac{1}{t}\right)^{-m-\frac{K}{2}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+w^{2})^{-\frac{m}{2}-\frac{K}{4}-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{n}{4}}\,\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\leq C\mathrm{e}^{at}t^{m+\frac{K}{2}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+w^{2})^{-\frac{m}{2}-\frac{K}{4}-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{n}{4}}\,\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle=O\left(t^{m+\frac{K+1}{2}-\frac{n}{2}}\right)

as
t\to 0+
. A similar estimate shows that

\displaystyle\left|\left|\int_{0}^{1}s^{M+1}\int_{\gamma_{t}}V(R_{0}(z)V)^{M+1}R_{s}(z)\mathrm{e}^{-tz}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}s\right|\right|_{1}

\displaystyle=O\left(t^{M+2-\frac{n}{2}}\right)

as
t\to 0^{+}
. Taking
J=M+1
completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.15
.

Suppose that

with

. Then for all
\lambda>0
and

we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{J}\left(\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell}\textup{Tr}\left((q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda+i0)q_{2})^{\ell}-(q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda-i0))^{\ell}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=1}^{J}C_{j}(n,V)\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-j}+E_{J}(\lambda),

(4.8)

where

as

and

is differentiable. If
n
is even we have
C_{j}(n,V)=0
for all

. If
n
is even and
j\leq\frac{n}{2}
or
n
is odd the coefficients

are given by

\displaystyle C_{j}(n,V)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi i)a_{j}(n,V)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}-j+1\right)},

(4.9)

with the

the heat kernel coefficients of Proposition
4.9
. Note also that for
n
even we have
C_{\frac{n}{2}}(n,V)=2\pi i\beta_{n}(V)

Proof.

For
\lambda>0
, we have

\displaystyle\textup{Tr}\left(q_{1}\left(R_{0}(\lambda+i0)\!-\!R_{0}(\lambda-i0)\right)q_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi i)\lambda^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\textup{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}{2(2\pi)^{n}}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=-\frac{(2\pi i)a_{1}(n,V)\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-1}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}.

by Lemma
4.1
, with no need for any kind of expansion. We now consider the
\ell\geq 2
terms in the sum.
For

we use Lemma
4.8
to obtain the expansion

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=2}^{L}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell}(q_{1}R_{0}(z)q_{2})^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=2}^{L}\left(\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell}q_{1}\left(\sum_{|k|=0}^{K}(-1)^{|k|+1}C_{\ell-1}(k)V^{(k_{1})}\cdots V^{(k_{\ell-1})}R_{0}(z)^{\ell+|k|}\right)q_{2}+\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell}q_{1}P_{K,\ell}(z)q_{2}\right),

. By the limiting absorption principle, this equality extends to

for

and thus we find

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=2}^{L}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell}\textup{Tr}\left((q_{1}(R_{0}(\lambda+i0)q_{2})^{\ell}-(q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda-i0))q_{2})^{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=2}^{L}\textup{Tr}\Bigg(q_{1}\!\left(\sum_{|k|=0}^{K}\frac{(-1)^{\ell+|k|+1}}{\ell}C_{\ell-1}(k)V^{(k_{1})}\cdots V^{(k_{\ell-1})}\left(R_{0}(\lambda+i0)^{\ell+|k|}\!-\!R_{0}(\lambda-i0)^{\ell+|k|}\right)\right)\!q_{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell}q_{1}\left(P_{K,\ell}(\lambda+i0)-P_{L,\ell}(\lambda-i0)\right)q_{2}\Bigg).

We now use the expansion of Equation (

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=2}^{L}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell}\textup{Tr}\left((q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda+i0)-R_{0}(\lambda-i0))q_{2})^{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=2}^{L}\textup{Tr}\Bigg(q_{1}\left(\sum_{|k|=0}^{K}\sum_{|r|=0}^{|k|}\frac{(-1)^{\ell+|k|+1}}{\ell}C_{\ell-1}(k)q_{1}g_{k,r}\partial^{r}\left(R_{0}(\lambda+i0)^{\ell+|k|}-R_{0}(\lambda-i0)^{\ell+|k|}\right)\right)q_{2}

each term of which is trace-class by Lemma

\displaystyle\left|\left|q_{1}\left(\prod_{m=1}^{M}A_{m}R_{0}(\lambda+i0)^{\alpha_{m}}-\prod_{m=1}^{M}A_{m}R_{0}(\lambda-i0)^{\alpha_{m}}\right)q_{2}\right|\right|_{1}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left|\left|\tilde{A}_{p+1}g_{p+1}\left(\prod_{p<m\leq M}R_{0}(\lambda+i0)^{\alpha_{m}}f_{m+1}\right)q_{2}\right|\right|,

where we use the convention

.
By
[
1

(see also
[

and differential operator

\displaystyle\left|\left|q_{1}AR_{0}(\lambda\pm i0)^{\alpha}q_{2}\right|\right|

\displaystyle=O\left(\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}\alpha+\frac{a}{4}}\right)

as
\lambda\to\infty
.
By Lemma
4.7
we have the estimate

\displaystyle\left|\left|q_{1}A\left(R_{0}(\lambda+i0)^{\alpha}-R_{0}(\lambda-i0)^{\alpha}\right)q_{2}\right|\right|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-\alpha+\frac{a}{2}}.

Combining these we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\left|q_{1}\left(\prod_{m=1}^{M}q_{1}A_{m}q_{2}R_{0}(\lambda+i0)^{\alpha_{m}}-\prod_{m=1}^{M}q_{1}A_{m}q_{2}R_{0}(\lambda-i0)^{\alpha_{m}}\right)q_{2}\right|\right|_{1}

\displaystyle=C\sum_{p=1}^{M}\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-\alpha_{p}+\frac{a_{p}}{2}}\left(\prod_{m\neq p}O\left(\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{m}+\frac{a_{m}}{4}}\right)\right)\leq O\left(\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-\ell-\frac{L+1}{2}}\right)

as
\lambda\to\infty
.
By varying
K
we obtain Equation (
4.8
). That
E_{L}
is differentiable follows from the observation that the left-hand side of Equation (
4.8
) is differentiable, as is each power of
\lambda
on the right-hand side.
∎

Remark 4.16
.

Choosing

in Theorem

gives

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{J}\left(\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell}\textup{Tr}\left((q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda+i0)q_{2})^{\ell}-(q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda-i0))^{\ell}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=-P_{n}(\lambda)-E(\lambda),

with

5
Low and high energy behaviour of the scattering matrix and Levinson’s theorem

Due to the generic failure of the equality

in dimensions

some adjustments are required to obtain an analogue of Theorem
3.9
in these cases via the use of an additional unitary operator. In higher dimensions, an additional unitary can be used to obtain a representative of the class
[S]
with better behaviour at high-energy in the trace norm, from which we are able to deduce Levinson’s theorem in each dimension.

To account for the low-energy behaviour of the scattering matrix, we introduce the following definition following
[
17
]
.

Definition 5.1
.

Suppose that
V
satisfies Assumption
2.4
and let
S
be the corresponding scattering operator. Let
\sigma
be a once-continuously differentiable admissable unitary. Then
\textup{Det}(\sigma(\lambda))\in\mathbb{T}
exists for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and we say that
\sigma

if
\sigma(0)=S(0)
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\sigma(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\textup{Id}

when taken in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,

is Fredholm and
\textup{Index}(W_{\sigma})=0
.

We note that low-energy corrections are only required to account for the behaviour of the scattering matrix at zero in dimensions
n=1,3
, since for all other dimensions we always have

by Theorem
2.8
. We will construct explicit low-energy corrections in these dimensions as needed.

To account for the high-energy behaviour of the scattering matrix in the trace norm, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 5.2
.

Suppose that
V
satisfies Assumption
2.4
and let
S
be the corresponding scattering operator. Let
\beta
be a once-continuously differentiable properly admissable unitary. Then

and we say that
\beta
is a high-energy correction for

if

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\textup{Det}(S(\lambda)\beta(\lambda))

\displaystyle=1,

and
\textup{Index}(W_{\beta})=0
.

We note that high-energy corrections are not required in dimension

, since in this case
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}S(\lambda)=\textup{Id}
in
\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{0}))=M_{2}(\mathbb{C})=\mathcal{L}^{1}(L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{0}))
. We will construct explicit high-energy corrections as needed in all higher dimensions.

We note the following key result, which follows immediately from
[
46
, Section 4.1]
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
U\in\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

|\textup{Tr}(U(\cdot)^{*}U^{\prime}(\cdot))|\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+})

\displaystyle\textup{Index}(W_{U})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\textup{Tr}\left(U(\lambda)^{*}U^{\prime}(\lambda)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

(5.1)

5.1
Low energy corrections in dimension

and

As a direct result of Theorem

we have the following.

Theorem 5.4
.

Suppose

,
V
satisfies Assumption
2.4
and let
S
be the corresponding scattering operator. Then for any low-energy correction

we have the pairing

\displaystyle\langle[D_{+}],[S\sigma^{*}]\rangle

\displaystyle=-\textup{Index}(W_{S\sigma^{*}})=\textup{Index}(W_{-})=N,

is the total number of bound states of

.

Proof.

Let
\sigma
be a low-energy correction for

\displaystyle\langle[D_{+}],[S\sigma^{*}]\rangle

\displaystyle=-\textup{Index}(W_{S\sigma^{*}}).

∎

In dimension

we require two types of low-energy corrections.

Definition 5.5
.

We say a map
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to M_{2}(\mathbb{C})

\theta:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
and

\theta

|f(\lambda)|^{2}+|g(\lambda)|^{2}=1

\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}

\theta(\infty)\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
,
\theta(0)\in(2\mathbb{Z}+1)\pi

\theta(\infty)-\theta(0)=\pi
,
-g(0)=f(\infty)=1
and
f(0)=g(\infty)=0
such that

\displaystyle\sigma(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}f(\lambda)&g(\lambda)\\
-\mathrm{e}^{i\theta(\lambda)}\overline{g(\lambda)}&\mathrm{e}^{i\theta(\lambda)}\overline{f(\lambda)}\end{pmatrix}.

(5.2)

We say a map
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is a resonant correction if there exist differentiable functions
\theta:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
and
f,g:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\theta
is increasing,
|f(\lambda)|^{2}+|g(\lambda)|^{2}=1
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
\theta(\infty),\theta(0)\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
and
\theta(\infty)-\theta(0)=0
with
f(0)=2c_{+}c_{-}
,
g(0)=c_{+}^{2}-c_{-}^{2}
,
f(\infty)=1
and
g(\infty)=0
for some
c_{+},c_{-}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
with
c_{+}^{2}+c_{-}^{2}=1
such that Equation (

) holds.

We note that the existence of low-energy corrections in dimension

is guaranteed by explicit choices of the functions
\theta,f,g
. For example in the generic case we can choose

\displaystyle\theta(\lambda)

\displaystyle=2\tan^{-1}(\lambda),\quad g(\lambda)=-\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda},\quad\textup{ and }\quad f(\lambda)=\sqrt{1-\mathrm{e}^{-2\lambda}}.

Lemma 5.6
.

Suppose
n=1
,
V
satisfies Assumption
2.4
and let
S
be the corresponding scattering operator. If
H
has no resonances, let
\sigma
be a generic correction and if
H
has a resonance let
\sigma
be a resonant correction with
c_{+},c_{-}
determined by Theorem
2.6
. Then
\sigma
defines a low-energy correction for
S
.

Proof.

We first make the observation that

\displaystyle\sigma(0)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad&\textup{ if }\sigma\textup{ is a generic correction},\\
\begin{pmatrix}2c_{+}c_{-}&c_{+}^{2}-c_{-}^{2}\\
c_{-}^{2}-c_{+}^{2}&2c_{+}c_{-}\end{pmatrix},\quad&\textup{ if }\sigma\textup{ is a resonant correction}.\end{cases}

Thus in both the generic and resonant cases we have that
\sigma(0)=S(0)
. By construction we also have that

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\sigma(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\textup{Id}.

Since
\sigma(0)=S(0)
and
W_{S}
is Fredholm, we find that
W_{\sigma}
is Fredholm also by Lemma
3.5
. It remains to check that
\textup{Index}(W_{\sigma})=0
. To do so, we use Gohberg-Kreĭn index theory in the form of
[
46
, Section 4.1]
. In particular, we have that

\displaystyle\textup{Index}(W_{\sigma})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\textup{Det}(\sigma(\lambda))}{\textup{Det}(\sigma(\lambda))}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\textup{Det}(\textup{Id}+\varphi(s)(\sigma(0)-\textup{Id}))}{\textup{Det}(\textup{Id}+\varphi(s)(\sigma(0)-\textup{Id}))}\,\mathrm{d}s.

The first term is easily evaluated as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\textup{Det}(\sigma(\lambda))}{\textup{Det}(\sigma(\lambda))}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\theta^{\prime}(\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2},\quad&\textup{ if }\sigma\textup{ is a generic correction},\\
0,\quad&\textup{ if }\sigma\textup{ is a resonant correction}.\end{cases}

The second integral has already been evaluated in
[
46
, Proposition 9]
(using a different choice of basis) as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\textup{Det}(\textup{Id}+\varphi(s)(\sigma(0)-\textup{Id}))}{\textup{Det}(\textup{Id}+\varphi(s)(\sigma(0)-\textup{Id}))}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}

and thus
\textup{Index}(W_{\sigma})=0
.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
n=3
and suppose that
V
satisfies Assumption
2.4
with
S
the corresponding scattering operator. Let
P_{s}
be the projection onto the spherical harmonics of order zero in
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
if there exists a resonance for
H=H_{0}+V
and
P_{s}=0
otherwise. Let
\theta:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
be an increasing differentiable function such that
\theta(0)=0
and
\theta(\infty)=\pi
. Then the unitary operator
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, defined for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
by

\displaystyle\sigma(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\textup{Id}-\left(1+\mathrm{e}^{i\theta(\lambda)}\right)P_{s},

defines a low-energy correction for
S
.

Proof.

If
P_{s}=0
then
\sigma(\lambda)=\textup{Id}
and the result is clear, so we suppose that there does exist a resonance. Since
P_{s}
is a finite rank projection, we have that
\sigma(\lambda)-\textup{Id}
is trace class for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. By construction we have
\sigma(0)=\textup{Id}-2P_{s}=S(0)
and
\sigma(\infty)=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}S(\lambda)=\textup{Id}
(with the limit taken in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{P})
). Since
W_{-}=W_{S}
(up to compacts) by Theorem
2.17
, we have that
W_{S}
is Fredholm and thus
W_{\sigma}
is Fredholm by Corollary
3.6
. We can compute using Gohberg-Kreĭn theory in the form of
[
47
, Section 6]
(see also
[
28
]
and
[
61
]
) that

\displaystyle\textup{Index}(W_{\sigma})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\textup{Det}(\sigma(\lambda))}{\textup{Det}(\sigma(\lambda))}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\textup{Det}\left(\textup{Id}+\varphi\left(-2s\right)(S(0)-\textup{Id})\right)}{\textup{Det}\left(\textup{Id}+\varphi\left(-2s\right)(S(0)-\textup{Id})\right)}\,\mathrm{d}s.

The first integral can be evaluated as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\textup{Det}(\sigma(\lambda))}{\textup{Det}(\sigma(\lambda))}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{i\theta^{\prime}(\lambda)\mathrm{e}^{i\theta(\lambda)}}{\mathrm{e}^{i\theta(\lambda)}}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda=\frac{1}{2}.

For the second integral, since
S(0)-\textup{Id}=-2P_{s}
and
P_{s}
is a rank-one projection, we find that the operator
\varphi\left(-2s\right)(S(0)-\textup{Id})
has a single eigenvalue given by
-2\varphi\left(-2s\right)
. Thus we find

\displaystyle\textup{Det}\left(\textup{Id}+\varphi\left(-2s\right)(S(0)-\textup{Id})\right)

\displaystyle=1-2\varphi\left(-2s\right).

Hence we can evaluate the
s
-integral as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\textup{Det}\left(\textup{Id}+\varphi\left(-2s\right)(S(0)-\textup{Id})\right)}{\textup{Det}\left(\textup{Id}+\varphi\left(-2s\right)(S(0)-\textup{Id})\right)}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\left(1-2\varphi\left(-2s\right)\right)}{1-2\varphi\left(-2s\right)}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\left(\tanh\left(2\pi s\right)+i\cosh\left(2\pi s\right)^{-1}\right)}{\tanh\left(2\pi s\right)+i\cosh\left(2\pi s\right)^{-1}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{2\pi s}}{\mathrm{e}^{4\pi s}+1}\,\mathrm{d}s=-\frac{1}{2}.

Combining these we find
\textup{Index}(W_{\sigma})=0
so that
\sigma
defines a low-energy correction for
S
.
∎

5.2
High energy corrections in dimension

We now construct high-energy corrections for dimension
n\geq 2
. We will use explicitly the trace relations of Section
4
and thus we impose the stronger condition that
V\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In dimension
n=1
, no corrections are necessary as
\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}S(\lambda)=\textup{Id}
in trace norm.

Lemma 5.8
.

Suppose that
n\geq 2
and
q_{1},q_{2}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
q_{1}q_{2}=V
and corresponding scattering operator
S
and fix

with
\tilde{\chi}(\lambda)=0
for

\chi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}

\displaystyle\chi(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{0}^{\lambda}\tilde{\chi}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u}{\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{\chi}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u}.

If
n=2,3
let
p=2
and if
n\geq 4
let
p\geq n
. For
\lambda>0

\tilde{A}(\lambda)\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{P})

\displaystyle\tilde{A}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=-2\pi\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)q_{2}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{p-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell}\left(q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda+i0)q_{2}\right)^{j}\left(q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda-i0)q_{2}\right)^{\ell-j-1}\right)q_{1}\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)^{*},

For

let
A(\lambda)=\chi(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}})\tilde{A}(\lambda)

\beta(\lambda)=\mathrm{e}^{iA(\lambda)}
defines a high-energy correction for
S
.

Proof.

By construction we have that
A(\lambda)
is self-adjoint for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
We have
A(0)=0
and the norm limit

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}A(\lambda)

\displaystyle=0

by an application of the estimate (
2.6
) and
[
53
, Theorem 1]
. By Lemma
4.1
we have that
A(\lambda)
is a trace-class operator and by cyclicity of the trace we have

\displaystyle\textup{Tr}(A(\lambda))

\displaystyle=i\chi(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}})\sum_{\ell=1}^{p-1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell}\textup{Tr}\left(\left(q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda+i0)q_{2}\right)^{\ell}-\left(q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda-i0)q_{2}\right)^{\ell}\right).

As a consequence of Hölder’s inequality for Schatten ideals,
\beta(\lambda)-\textup{Id}\in\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{P})
and so
\beta
defines a properly admissable unitary. Thus
\textup{Det}(\beta(\lambda))=\mathrm{e}^{i\textup{Tr}(A(\lambda))}
and so by Lemma
2.10
we find that

\displaystyle\textup{Det}(S(\lambda))\textup{Det}(\beta(\lambda))\frac{\textup{Det}_{p}(\textup{Id}+q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda+i0)q_{2})}{\textup{Det}_{p}(\textup{Id}+q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda-i0)q_{2})}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{p-1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell}\textup{Tr}\left((q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda+i0)q_{2})^{\ell}-(q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda-i0)q_{2})^{\ell}\right)+i\textup{Tr}(A(\lambda))\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left((1-\chi(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}))\sum_{\ell=1}^{p-1}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell}\textup{Tr}\left((q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda+i0)q_{2})^{\ell}-(q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda-i0)q_{2})^{\ell}\right)\right).

An application of Theorem
4.15
and Lemma
2.12
gives that

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\textup{Det}(S(\lambda)\beta(\lambda))

\displaystyle=1.

To see that
\textup{Index}(W_{\beta})=0
, we consider for
t\in[0,1]
the homotopy

\displaystyle A_{t}(\lambda)=2\pi\chi((1-t)\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}})\tilde{A}(\lambda).

The path
A_{t}(\lambda)
defines a norm-continuous path in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{P})
from
A_{0}(\lambda)=A(\lambda)
to
A_{1}(\lambda)=0
. Defining the path
A_{t}=A_{t}(L)\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
we obtain a norm continuous path in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
from
A
to
0
. To see this, fix
t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,1)
and define
d=(\min\{(1-t_{1})^{-1},(1-t_{2})^{-1}\})^{2}\geq 1
. Then for
g\in\mathcal{H}_{spec}
we find

\displaystyle\left|\left|(A_{t_{2}}(\cdot)-A_{t_{1}}(\cdot))g\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}_{spec}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|[(A_{t_{2}}(\lambda)-A_{t_{1}}(\lambda))g(\lambda,\cdot)](\omega)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\int_{d}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|[(A_{t_{2}}(\lambda)-A_{t_{1}}(\lambda))g(\lambda,\cdot)](\omega)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\int_{d}^{\infty}|\chi(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-t_{2}))-\chi(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-t_{1}))|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|[\tilde{A}(\lambda)g(\lambda,\cdot)](\omega)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\leq C|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{2}\int_{d}^{\infty}\lambda\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|[\tilde{A}(\lambda)g(\lambda,\cdot)](\omega)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

It remains to estimate
|[\tilde{A}(\lambda)g(\lambda,\cdot)](\omega)|
. We recall from
[
53
, Theorem 1]
that for sufficiently large
\lambda
we have

\displaystyle\left|\left|q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda\pm i0)^{j}q_{2}\right|\right|

\displaystyle=O(\lambda^{-\frac{j}{2}})

and so we may define

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{2\pi\sum_{\ell=1}^{p-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\frac{1}{\ell}\left|\left|(q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda+i0)q_{2})^{j}(q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda-i0)q_{2})^{\ell-j-1}\right|\right|:\lambda\in[d,\infty)\right\}.

Using twice the estimate (
2.6
) we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|[\tilde{A}(\lambda)g(\lambda,\cdot)](\omega)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega=\left|\left|A(\lambda)g(\lambda,\cdot)\right|\right|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}

\displaystyle\leq 2\pi\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{p-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\frac{1}{\ell}\left|\left|(q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda+i0)q_{2})^{j}(q_{1}R_{0}(\lambda-i0)q_{2})^{\ell-j-1}q_{1}\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)^{*}g(\lambda,\cdot)\right|\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq K\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left|\left|q_{1}\Gamma_{0}(\lambda)^{*}g(\lambda,\cdot)\right|\right|^{2}\leq K\lambda^{-1}\left|\left|g(\lambda,\cdot)\right|\right|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}.

Combining these we find

\displaystyle\left|\left|(A_{t_{2}}(\cdot)-A_{t_{1}}(\cdot))g\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}_{spec}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{2}\int_{d}^{\infty}\lambda\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|[\tilde{A}(\lambda)g(\lambda,\cdot)](\omega)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\leq CK|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{2}\int_{d}^{\infty}\left|\left|g(\lambda,\cdot)\right|\right|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\leq CK|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{2}\left|\left|g\right|\right|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{spec}}.

The case when either or both of
t_{1},t_{2}
is one follows from a similar calculation.

As a result, the path
\beta_{t}=\mathrm{e}^{iA_{t}}
defines a norm continuous path of unitary operators in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
from
\beta
to
Id
. Hence the path
W_{\beta_{t}}
defines a norm continuous path in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
from
W_{\beta}
to
Id
, along which the Fredholm index is constant and equal to zero.
∎

5.3
Levinson’s theorem and the spectral shift function at zero

We now use the high-energy behaviour of the spectral shift function of Section

Proposition 5.9
.

Suppose that

with corresponding scattering operator

\displaystyle\textup{Index}(W_{S})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\textup{Tr}\left(S(\lambda)^{*}S^{\prime}(\lambda)\right)\,-p_{n}(\lambda)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda-\beta_{n}(V)+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\textup{Tr}(\sigma(\lambda)^{*}\sigma^{\prime}(\lambda))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda,

(5.3)

where
\sigma
is a low-energy correction in dimension
n=1,3
and otherwise
\sigma=\textup{Id}
. Here

\displaystyle p_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor}c_{j}(n,V)\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-j-1}

is as in Definition
4.11
and
\beta_{n}(V)
is as defined in Equation (

).

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\chi},\chi
be as in Lemma
5.8
and let
\beta
be the corresponding high-energy correction for
S
and
\sigma
the corresponding low-energy correction. Then we have

\displaystyle\textup{Index}(W_{S})=\textup{Index}(W_{S\beta\sigma^{*}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\textup{Det}(S(\lambda)\beta(\lambda)\sigma(\lambda))}{\textup{Det}(S(\lambda)\beta(\lambda)\sigma(\lambda))}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\textup{Tr}\left(S(\lambda)^{*}S^{\prime}(\lambda)\right)+i\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\chi(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}})\textup{Tr}(\tilde{A}(\lambda))\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\textup{Tr}(\sigma(\lambda)^{*}\sigma^{\prime}(\lambda))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

By adding zero we account for the high-energy behaviour of
\textup{Tr}\left(S(\cdot)^{*}S^{\prime}(\cdot)\right)
using Lemma
4.12
and Definition
4.11
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\textup{Tr}\left(S(\lambda)^{*}S^{\prime}(\lambda)\right)+i\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\chi(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}})\textup{Tr}(\tilde{A}(\lambda))\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\textup{Tr}\left(S(\lambda)^{*}S^{\prime}(\lambda)\right)-p_{n}(\lambda)\right)+\left(p_{n}(\lambda)+i\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\chi(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}})\textup{Tr}(\tilde{A}(\lambda))\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\textup{Tr}\left(S(\lambda)^{*}S^{\prime}(\lambda)\right)-p_{n}(\lambda)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(p_{n}(\lambda)+i\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\left(\chi(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}})\textup{Tr}(\tilde{A}(\lambda))\right)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda.

Using Theorem
4.15
(see also Remark
4.16
) we write

\displaystyle i\textup{Tr}(\tilde{A}(\lambda))

\displaystyle=-P_{n}(\lambda)-E(\lambda)

where
E
is differentiable,
E(\lambda)=O(\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}})
as
\lambda\to\infty
,

\displaystyle P_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}C_{j}(n,V)\lambda^{\frac{n}{2}-j},\quad\textup{ and }\quad P_{n}^{\prime}(\lambda)=p_{n}(\lambda).

Thus we find

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\textup{Tr}\left(S(\lambda)^{*}S^{\prime}(\lambda)\right)+i\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\chi(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}})\textup{Tr}(\tilde{A}(\lambda))\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\textup{Tr}\left(S(\lambda)^{*}S^{\prime}(\lambda)\right)-p_{n}(\lambda)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(p_{n}(\lambda)-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\left(\chi(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}})(P_{n}(\lambda)+E(\lambda))\right)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\textup{Tr}\left(S(\lambda)^{*}S^{\prime}(\lambda)\right)-p_{n}(\lambda)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}\left(P_{n}(\lambda)\left(1-\chi(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}})\right)-\chi(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}})E(\lambda)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\textup{Tr}\left(S(\lambda)^{*}S^{\prime}(\lambda)\right)-p_{n}(\lambda)\right)-\frac{1}{2\pi i}P_{n}(0),

where we have used the properties of
\chi
as defined in Lemma
5.8
.
Observing that
P_{n}(0)=2\pi i\beta_{n}(V)
completes the proof.
∎

We can now prove Levinson’s theorem in all dimensions.

Theorem 5.10
.

Suppose that
V\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with corresponding scattering operator
S
. Then the number of bound states
N
of
H=H_{0}+V
is given by

\displaystyle-N

\displaystyle=\textup{Index}(W_{-})=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\textup{Tr}\left(S(\lambda)^{*}S^{\prime}(\lambda)\right)-p_{n}(\lambda)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda-\beta_{n}(V)+N_{res},

(5.4)

where
p_{n}
is as in Definition
4.11
,
\beta_{n}(V)
is as defined in Equation (
4.7
) and

\displaystyle N_{res}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2},&\quad\textup{ if }n=1\textup{ and there are no resonances,}\\
\textup{Index}(W_{res}),&\quad\textup{ if }n=2,4,\\
\frac{1}{2},&\quad\textup{ if }n=3\textup{ and there exists a resonance},\\
0,&\quad\textup{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

For
n\geq 5
this is the statement of Proposition
5.9
combined with the observation that
\textup{Index}(W_{-})=\textup{Index}(W_{S})=-N
of Proposition
2.1
and Theorem
2.17
. For
n=1,3
we use the additional observation (see Lemmas
5.6
and
5.7
) that for a low energy correction
\sigma
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\textup{Tr}(\sigma(\lambda)^{*}\sigma^{\prime}(\lambda))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2},&\quad\textup{ if }n=1\textup{ and there are no resonances,}\\
\frac{1}{2},&\quad\textup{ if }n=3\textup{ and there exists a resonance},\\
0,&\quad\textup{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

For
n=2,4
we observe that
\textup{Index}(W_{-})=\textup{Index}(W_{S})+\textup{Index}(W_{res})
by Theorem
2.17
and the result follows from Lemma
5.8
.
∎

In particular, we have the following low-dimensional statements of Levinson’s theorem. In dimension
n=1
, we recover Levinson’s original result
[
51
]
that

\displaystyle-N

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\textup{Tr}(S(\lambda)^{*}S^{\prime}(\lambda))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda+\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2},\quad&\textup{ if there are no resonances},\\
0,\quad&\textup{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

In dimension
n=2
we obtain the result of Bollé et al.
[
14
, Theorem 6.3]
,

\displaystyle-N

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\textup{Tr}(S(\lambda)^{*}S^{\prime}(\lambda))\,\mathrm{d}\lambda+\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V(x)\,\mathrm{d}x+N_{res}.

In dimension
n=3
we recover the result of Bollé and Osborn
[
11
]
,

\displaystyle-N

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\textup{Tr}(S(\lambda)^{*}S^{\prime}(\lambda))+\frac{i\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}V(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda+\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2},\quad&\textup{ if there is a resonance},\\
0,\quad&\textup{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

In dimension
n=4
we obtain the result of Jia, Nicoleau and Wang
[
43
, Theorem 5.3]
,

\displaystyle-N

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\left(\textup{Tr}(S(\lambda)^{*}S^{\prime}(\lambda))\!+\!\frac{(2\pi i)\textup{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}{2(2\pi)^{4}}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}V(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)\!\mathrm{d}\lambda-\frac{\textup{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}{4(2\pi)^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}V(x)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+N_{res}

Finally, as a result of Levinson’s theorem and the discussion in Remark
4.13
we deduce the low-energy behaviour of the spectral shift function.

Corollary 5.11
.

Suppose that
V\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with corresponding scattering operator
S
. Then for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\displaystyle\xi(\lambda)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{K}M(\lambda_{k})\chi_{[\lambda_{k},\infty)}(\lambda)-N_{res}\chi_{[0,\infty)}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\quad-\chi_{[0,\infty)}(\lambda)\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{\lambda}\left(\textup{Tr}\left(S(\mu)^{*}S^{\prime}(\mu)\right)-p_{n}(\mu)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mu,

where the distinct eigenvalues of
H
are listed in numerical order
\lambda_{1}<\cdots<\lambda_{K}\leq 0
with each eigenvalue
\lambda_{j}
having multiplicity
M(\lambda_{j})
. In particular we have
\xi(0+)=-N-N_{res}
.

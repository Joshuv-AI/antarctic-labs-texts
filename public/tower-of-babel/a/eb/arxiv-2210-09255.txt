A Unified Algorithm for Stochastic Path Problems
Christoph Dann, Chen-Yu Wei, Julian Zimmert

Abstract
We study reinforcement learning in stochastic path (SP) problems. The goal in these problems is to maximize the expected sum of rewards until the agent reaches a terminal state. We provide the first regret guarantees in this general problem by analyzing a simple optimistic algorithm. Our regret bound matches the best known results for the well-studied special case of stochastic shortest path (SSP) with all non-positive rewards. For SSP, we present an adaptation procedure for the case when the scale of rewards B ⋆ B_{\star} is unknown. We show that there is no price for adaptation, and our regret bound matches that with a known B ⋆ B_{\star} . We also provide a scale adaptation procedure for the special case of stochastic longest paths (SLP) where all rewards are non-negative. However, unlike in SSP, we show through a lower bound that there is an unavoidable price for adaptation.

Introduction

Imagine a web application with recurring user visits (epochs).
During each visit, the app can choose from different content to present to the user (actions), which might lead to a desired interaction such as a purchase or click on an ad (reward).
The user’s behaviour depends on their internal state, which is influenced by the content provided to them.
Inevitably, at some point the user will abandon the session.

It is natural to model this as an episodic reinforcement-learning problem.
However, the length of each episode is random and depends on the agent’s actions.
To deal with the random episode length, and hence potentially unbounded cumulative reward in a single episode, one could either consider a fixed horizon problem by clipping the length of each episode, or consider discounted rewards.
Both approaches introduce biases to the actual objective of the agent and we consider a third option: the
stochastic longest path
(SLP) setting, which is analogous to the
stochastic shortest path
(SSP) problem
(
Rosenberg et al. 2020
;
Cohen et al. 2021
;
Tarbouriech et al. 2021b
;
Chen et al. 2021a
, e.g.,)
but with positive rewards instead of costs.

In more generality, we can assume a setting in which there are both negative rewards (cost), as well as positive rewards.
For example, users without subscription using the free part of an application might induce an overall negative reward due to the cost of infrastructure.
However, the hope is that the user is convinced by the
free service to upgrade to a subscription, which provides revenue.
We call this setting the
stochastic path
(SP) problem.
We make the following contributions:

1.

We formalize the general SP problem and provide a simple unified algorithm for it with SSP and SLP as special cases.

2.

We present the first regret upper-bound for the SP and SLP problem and show through lower-bounds that they are minimax-optimal up to log-factors and lower-order terms. Technically, our analysis gives the first near-optimal near-horizon-free regret bound for episodic MDPs when the reward could be positive or negative. In comparison, previous analysis in
Zhang et al. 2021
;
Tarbouriech et al. 2021b
;
Chen et al. 2021a
can only get near-horizon-free bounds for all-non-negative or all-non-positive reward (see
Section
3
for more discussions).

3.

For SSP, when the scale of the sum of rewards
B_{\star}
is unknown, we derive an improved procedure to adapt to
B_{\star}
. Unlike prior work
(
Tarbouriech et al. 2021b
;
Chen et al. 2021a
)
, our adaptation procedure allows us to recover the regret bound achieved with a known
B_{\star}
.

4.

For SLP, we also derive an algorithm that adapts to unknown
B_{\star}
. This adaptation is qualitatively different than in the SSP case. In fact, we show through a lower bound that adaptivity to unkonwn
B_{\star}
comes at an unavoidable price in SLP.

The contributions 3 and 4 above jointly formalize a distinction between SSP and SLP when the scale of cumulative rewards is unknown. An overview of the main regret bounds derived in this work and a comparison to existing results is available in

Preliminaries

We consider a stochastic path (SP) problem with a finite state space
\mathcal{S}

\mathcal{A}

S=|\mathcal{S}|

s_{1}=s_{\text{init}}
1
1

1

on which every action leads to zero reward and next state distribution

. At the

, takes an action

, which leads to a reward value

A history-dependent deterministic policy
\pi=(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\ldots)
is a mapping from state-action histories to actions, i.e.,
\pi_{i}:(\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A})^{i-1}\times\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
; we use
\Pi^{\text{{HD}}}
to denote the set of all history-dependent deterministic policies. A stationary deterministic policy
\pi
is a mapping from states to actions, i.e.,
\pi:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
; we use
\Pi^{\text{{SD}}}
to denote the set of all stationary deterministic policies. The state value function of a policy
\pi\in\Pi^{\text{{HD}}}
on state

are defined as

\displaystyle V^{\pi}(s)

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbb{E}^{\pi}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}r(s_{i},a_{i})~\Bigg|~s_{1}=s\right],

where
\tau=\min\{i:~s_{i+1}=g\}
, i.e., the timestep right before reaching the terminal state
g
(or
\infty
if
g
is never reached), and
\mathbb{E}^{\pi}
denotes expectation under policy
\pi
. Naturally,
V^{\pi}(g)\triangleq 0
.

A policy
\pi
is called

if
g
is reached with probability
1
under policy
\pi
starting from any state.
In this paper, we make the following assumption:

Assumption 1
.

All policies in
\Pi^{\text{{HD}}}
are proper.

Assumption
1
is stronger than those in previous works on SSP
(
Rosenberg et al. 2020
;
Cohen et al. 2021
;
Tarbouriech et al. 2021b
;
Chen et al. 2021a
)
, which only require the

of a proper policy. We note that the algorithmic trick they developed (adding a small amount of cost to every step) can also help us weaken

1

By Theorem 7.1.9 of

Assumption
1
implies that there is a stationary and deterministic optimal policy
\pi^{\star}\in\Pi^{\text{{SD}}}
such that
V^{\pi^{\star}}(s)\geq V^{\pi}(s)
for any
\pi\in\Pi^{\text{{HD}}}
and any
s
. We let
V^{\star}(\cdot)\triangleq V^{\pi^{\star}}(\cdot)
and define

\displaystyle V_{\star}\triangleq\left|V^{\star}(s_{\text{init}})\right|,\quad B_{\star}\triangleq\max_{s}\left|V^{\star}(s)\right|.

To establish our result, we also need the following definitions:

Definition 1
.

Define

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle\triangleq\sup_{\pi\in\Pi^{\text{{HD}}}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}^{\pi}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}r(s_{i},a_{i})\right)^{2}~\Bigg|~s_{1}=s_{\text{init}}\right]},

\displaystyle R_{\max}

\displaystyle\triangleq\max_{s}\sup_{\pi\in\Pi^{\text{{HD}}}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}^{\pi}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}r(s_{i},a_{i})\right)^{2}~\Bigg|~s_{1}=s\right]},

\displaystyle T_{\max}

\displaystyle\triangleq\max_{s}\sup_{\pi\in\Pi^{\text{{HD}}}}\mathbb{E}^{\pi}\big[\tau~|~s_{1}=s\big],

where
\tau=\min\{i:~s_{i+1}=g\}

.

In words,

Under

2.1
Learning Protocol

The learning procedure considered in this paper is the same as previous works on SSP. We let the learner interact with the SP for
K
episodes, each started from
s_{\text{init}}
. We define the regret as the difference between

(the expected total reward obtained by the optimal policy) and the total reward of the learner. We keep a time index

to track the number of steps executed by the learner, and let

denote the state the learner sees at time
t

k

t_{k}
, and thus
s_{t_{k}}=s_{\text{init}}
. At time
t
, the learner takes an action

, and transitions to

. If

, we let

; otherwise, we let

to be the first step of episode

1

\displaystyle\text{\rm Reg}_{K}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(V^{\star}(s_{\text{init}})-\sum_{t=t_{k}}^{e_{k}}r(s_{t},a_{t})\right),

with
e_{k}=t_{k+1}-1
.
We let
T
to be the total number of steps during
K
episodes. That is,
T=e_{K}
.

3.1
Lower bound

In this subsection, we show that the upper bound established in
Theorem
2
is nearly tight. The proofs for this subsection can be found in
Appendix
D
.

Theorem 3
.

For any
u\geq 2
, and
K\geq\Omega(SA)
, we can construct a set of SP instances such that
R\leq u
for all instances, and there exists a distribution over these instances such that the expected regret of any algorithm is at least
\Omega(u\sqrt{SAK})
.

Specially, in the lower bound construction of
Theorem
3
,
V_{\star}
and
B_{\star}
are of order
O(1)
for all
u\leq\sqrt{K/(SA)}
, showing that
V_{\star}
and
B_{\star}
are insufficient to characterize the regret bound for general SP problems. As we will see in the following sections, this contrasts with the special cases SLP and SSP, where except for logarithmic terms, the coefficients in the regret bound can be completely characterized by
V_{\star}
and
B_{\star}
.

The quantity
R
in the regret upper and lower bounds (
Theorem
2
and
Theorem
3
) is undesirable because its definition involves a
supremum
over all policies, which might be very large. Is it possible to refine the upper bound so that it only depends on quantities that correspond to the
optimal policy
? Specifically, we define

\displaystyle R_{\star}

\displaystyle~\triangleq~\max_{s}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}^{\pi^{\star}}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}r(s_{i},a_{i})\right)^{2}~\Bigg|~s_{1}=s\right]},

and ask: can the regret bound only depends on
R_{\star}
? Notice that
Theorem
3
is uninformative for this question because
R\approx R_{\star}
in its construction. The next theorem gives a negative answer
when the learner is agnostic of the value of
R_{\star}
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
u\geq 2
be arbitrarily chosen, and let
K\geq\Omega(SA)
. For any algorithm that obtains a expected regret bound of
\tilde{O}(u\sqrt{SAK})
for all problem instances with
R_{\star}=R_{\max}\leq u
, there exists a problem instance with
R_{\star}=O(1)
and
R_{\max}\leq u
but the expected regret is at least
\tilde{\Omega}(u\sqrt{SAK})
.

Given
Theorem
4
, a left open question is whether
\tilde{O}(R_{\star}\sqrt{SAK})
is achievable when the learner has information about
R_{\star}
. Note that our algorithm
Algorithm
1
only requires knowledge of
B_{\star}
, which, in general, does not provide information about
R_{\star}
(e.g., in the construction of
Theorem
4
,
B_{\star}=O(1)
for all
u\leq\sqrt{K/(SA)}
). Therefore, an algorithm with such a refined guarantee would be quite different from our algorithm.

4.2

B_{\star}

If the magnitude of

See

is fundamentally harder than knowing an order optimal bound on

without dominating lower order terms, and one in which we allow a constant cost for adapting to an unknown

Corollary 10

Any algorithm with an asymptotic upper bound of

\displaystyle\tilde{O}\left(B_{\star}^{\alpha}V_{\star}^{1-\alpha}\sqrt{SAK}\right)+o\left(B_{\star}^{2}\right)\,,

satisfies at least
\alpha\geq 1
and

\displaystyle O\left(\sqrt{V_{\star}B_{\star}SAK}+\left(\frac{B_{\star}}{V_{\star}}\right)^{2}\text{poly}(V_{\star},S,A)\right)

\tilde{\Omega}\left(\frac{B_{\star}^{2}SA}{V_{\star}}\right)

Proof.

For the first part, note that for any
\alpha<1

Theorem
9

reads

Similarly, in the second case, we can make

SSP has been studied extensively recently. The works by

6
Conclusions and Open Problems

In this work, we formulate the SP problem and give the first near-optimal regret bound for it. For special cases SLP and SSP, we further investigate the situation when the scale of the total reward
B_{\star}
is unknown. By improving previous adaptation results for SSP, and giving new lower bounds for SLP, we formally show a distinction between these two cases when
B_{\star}
is unknown.

In the general case, although our algorithm achieves near-worst-case-optimal bounds in terms of
R
, there is still possibility of improving the bound using more refined quantities. We have ruled out possibility of
V_{\star},B_{\star}
, and the possibility of
R_{\star}
when its value is unknown, but perhaps there are other candidate quantities.
Further, there is a discrepancy in the analysis between the general SP / SLP setting and the SSP setting, i.e., while our result for the general case recovers that for the SLP case (up to logarithmic factors), it does not imply that for the SSP case. This also hints that
R
does not always capture the true difficulty of every instance.

In general SP when
B_{\star}
is unknown, can we achieve the bound of order
\tilde{O}(R_{\max}\sqrt{\text{poly}(S,A)K})
, without any constant term that scales super-linearly with
R_{\max}
? This would be analogous to the

Lemma 14
.

Define

\displaystyle f(P,V,n,\iota)=PV+\max\left\{c_{1}\sqrt{\frac{\mathbb{V}(P,V)\iota}{n}},\frac{c_{2}B\iota}{n}\right\}

If
2c_{1}^{2}\leq c_{2}
and
-B\leq V(\cdot)\leq B
, then
f(P,V,n,\iota)
is increasing in
V
.

Proof.

We compute the derivative of
f(P,V,n,\iota)
over
V(s^{\star})
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f(P,V,n,\iota)}{\partial V(s^{\star})}

\displaystyle=P(s^{\star})+\mathbf{1}\left\{c_{1}\sqrt{\frac{\mathbb{V}(P,V)\iota}{n}}>\frac{c_{2}B\iota}{n}\right\}\times\frac{c_{1}P(s^{\star})(V(s^{\star})-PV)\iota}{\sqrt{n\mathbb{V}(P,V)\iota}}

\displaystyle\geq P(s^{\star})+\mathbf{1}\left\{c_{1}\sqrt{\frac{\mathbb{V}(P,V)\iota}{n}}>\frac{c_{2}B\iota}{n}\right\}\times\frac{c_{1}^{2}P(s^{\star})(V(s^{\star})-PV)}{c_{2}B}

\displaystyle\geq P(s^{\star})-\frac{2c_{1}^{2}}{c_{2}}P(s^{\star})

\displaystyle\geq 0.

∎

Lemma 15
.

If

, then with probability at least
1-O(\delta)
,

for all
(s,a)
and

Proof.

We use induction to prove this. When

,

for all

. Suppose that

for all for all

(which implies

for all

and

only differ in the entry
(s_{t},a_{t})
, we only need to check
Q_{t+1}(s_{t},a_{t})\geq Q^{\star}(s_{t},a_{t})
. This can be seen from the calculation below:

\displaystyle Q_{t+1}(s_{t},a_{t})

\displaystyle=r(s_{t},a_{t})+\bar{P}_{t}V_{t}+b_{t}

\displaystyle=r(s_{t},a_{t})+\bar{P}_{t}V_{t}+\max\left\{c_{1}\sqrt{\frac{\mathbb{V}(\bar{P}_{t},V_{t})\iota_{t}}{n_{t}}},\frac{c_{2}B\iota_{t}}{n_{t}}\right\}

\displaystyle=r(s_{t},a_{t})+\bar{P}_{t}V^{\star}+\max\left\{c_{1}\sqrt{\frac{\mathbb{V}(\bar{P}_{t},V^{\star})\iota_{t}}{n_{t}}},\frac{c_{2}B\iota_{t}}{n_{t}}\right\}

(By
Lemma

\displaystyle=Q^{\star}(s_{t},a_{t}).

Note that we can apply

∎

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{s_{t}}(V_{t}-V^{\star})+\sum_{t=2}^{T}\sum_{s}(V_{t-1}(s)-V_{t}(s))+O(B)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{s_{t}}(V_{t}-V^{\star})+O(BS).

(8)

We have

A.3
Bounding the regret

Theorem
2
.

We use the following notations:

\displaystyle Y_{k}\triangleq\sum_{t=t_{k}}^{e_{k}}\mathbb{V}(P_{t},V^{\star}),\quad Z_{k}\triangleq\sum_{t=t_{k}}^{e_{k}}(V^{\star}(s_{t})-Q^{\star}(s_{t},a_{t})).

\displaystyle\text{\rm Reg}_{K}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(V^{\star}(s_{t_{k}})-\sum_{t=t_{k}}^{e_{k}}r(s_{t},a_{t})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}(V^{\star}(s_{t})-V^{\star}(s_{t}^{\prime})-r(s_{t},a_{t}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(V^{\star}(s_{t})-Q^{\star}(s_{t},a_{t})\right)+\sum_{t=1}^{T}(P_{t}V^{\star}-V^{\star}(s_{t}^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(V^{\star}(s_{t})-Q^{\star}(s_{t},a_{t})\right)+O\left(\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{V}(P_{t},V^{\star})\tilde{\iota}_{T,B,\delta}}+B_{\star}\tilde{\iota}_{T,B,\delta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq O\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K}Z_{k}+\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K}Y_{k}\tilde{\iota}_{T,B,\delta}}+B_{\star}\tilde{\iota}_{T,B,\delta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq O\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K}Z_{k}+\sqrt{\left(R_{\max}\ln_{+}\left(\frac{R_{\max}}{R}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{K}Z_{k}+R^{2}K+R_{\max}^{2}\ln\frac{R_{\max}K}{R\delta}\ln\frac{\ln(R_{\max}K)}{\delta}\right)\tilde{\iota}_{T,B,\delta}}+B_{\star}\tilde{\iota}_{T,B,\delta}\right)

(
Lemma
19
)

\displaystyle\leq O\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K}Z_{k}+R\sqrt{K\tilde{\iota}_{T,B,\delta}}+R_{\max}\ln\left(\frac{R_{\max}K}{R\delta}\right)\tilde{\iota}_{T,B,\delta}\right).

(AM-GM)

(23)

It remains to bound
\sum_{k=1}^{K}Z_{k}
.
By
Lemma
16
, we have with probability at least
1-O(\delta)
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}Z_{k}\leq O\left(\sqrt{SA\sum_{k=1}^{K}Y_{k}\tilde{\iota}_{T,B,\delta}}+BS^{2}A\tilde{\iota}_{T,B,\delta}\right).

(24)

Further using
Lemma
19
on the right-hand side,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}Z_{k}\leq

\displaystyle O\left(\sqrt{SA\left(R_{\max}\ln_{+}\left(\frac{R_{\max}}{R}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{K}Z_{k}+R^{2}K+R_{\max}^{2}\ln\frac{R_{\max}K}{R\delta}\ln\frac{\ln(R_{\max}K)}{\delta}\right)\tilde{\iota}_{T,B,\delta}}+BS^{2}A\tilde{\iota}_{T,B,\delta}\right).

Solving for

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}Z_{k}\leq O\left(R\sqrt{SAK\tilde{\iota}_{T,B,\delta}}+R_{\max}SA\ln\left(\frac{R_{\max}K}{R\delta}\right)\tilde{\iota}_{T,B,\delta}+BS^{2}A\tilde{\iota}_{T,B,\delta}\right).

Plugging this to

(AM-GM)

Theorem

.

As mentioned in
Footnote
1
, we can convert between the cases of “deterministic initial state” and “random initial state” by just introducing one additional state. In our lower bound proof, our construction is based on random initial states, but converting it to deterministic initial state is straightforward. In this proof, we use
P(\cdot|s,a)=P_{s,a}(\cdot)
to denote the transition probability.

We first consider a MDP with two non-terminal states
x,y
and a terminal state
g
. The number of actions is
A
. Let the initial state distribution be
\text{uniform}\{x,y\}
. For all action
a
, let
r(x,a)=1
and
r(y,a)=-1
. Let
\epsilon,\Delta\in(0,\frac{1}{4}]
be quantities to be determined later. For all action
a
, let

\displaystyle P(\cdot~|~y,a)

\displaystyle=(1-\epsilon)\text{uniform}\{x,y\}+\epsilon\mathbf{1}_{g}.

For all but one single
good
action
a^{\star}
, let

\displaystyle P(\cdot~|~x,a)=(1-\epsilon)\text{uniform}\{x,y\}+\epsilon\mathbf{1}_{g}.

For the good action
a^{\star}
, let

\displaystyle P(\cdot~|~x,a^{\star})=\frac{1-\epsilon+\Delta}{2}\mathbf{1}_{x}+\frac{1-\epsilon-\Delta}{2}\mathbf{1}_{y}+\epsilon\mathbf{1}_{g}.

First, we calculate the optimal value function of this MDP.

Claim 1

V^{\star}(x)=1+\frac{\Delta}{\epsilon}\frac{1+\epsilon}{2-\Delta}
, and
Q^{\star}(x,a)=1+\frac{\Delta}{\epsilon}\frac{1-\epsilon}{2-\Delta}
for
a\neq a^{\star}
.

Claim 1.

We first show that the optimal policy is to always choose
a^{\star}
on state
x
. We only need to compare two deterministic policies: always choose
a^{\star}
, or always choose some other action
a\neq a^{\star}
. For the policy with
\pi(x)=a^{\star}
, we have

\displaystyle V^{\pi}(x)

\displaystyle=r(x,a^{\star})+P(x|x,a^{\star})V^{\pi}(x)+P(y|x,a^{\star})V^{\pi}(y)=1+\frac{1-\epsilon+\Delta}{2}V^{\pi}(x)+\frac{1-\epsilon-\Delta}{2}V^{\pi}(y),

\displaystyle V^{\pi}(y)

\displaystyle=r(y,\cdot)+P(x|y,\cdot)V^{\pi}(x)+P(y|y,\cdot)V^{\pi}(y)=-1+\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}V^{\pi}(x)+\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}V^{\pi}(y).

Solving the equations, we get
V^{\pi}(x)=1+\frac{\Delta}{\epsilon}\frac{1+\epsilon}{2-\Delta}
. On the other hand, if
\pi(x)\neq a
, then by similar calculation, we get
V^{\pi}(x)=1
, which is smaller. This shows that the optimal policy is to always choose
a^{\star}
on
x
. Thus,
V^{\star}(x)=Q^{\star}(x,a^{\star})=1+\frac{\Delta}{\epsilon}\frac{1+\epsilon}{2-\Delta}
. Plugging this in another bellman equation

\displaystyle V^{\star}(y)=-1+\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}V^{\star}(x)+\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}V^{\star}(y)

we get
V^{\star}(y)=-1+\frac{\Delta}{\epsilon}\frac{1-\epsilon}{2-\Delta}
, and thus for
a\neq a^{\star}
,

\displaystyle Q^{\star}(x,a)=1+\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}\left(V^{\star}(x)+V^{\star}(y)\right)=1+\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}\left(\frac{\Delta}{\epsilon}\frac{2}{2-\Delta}\right)=1+\frac{\Delta}{\epsilon}\frac{1-\epsilon}{2-\Delta}.

∎

Next, we follow the proof idea in
Rosenberg et al. 2020
and consider a
truncated process
: First, we view the
K
episodes as a continuous process in which once the learner reaches
g
, a new state is drawn from the initial distribution and the learner restarts from there. Then we cap the process to make it contain at most
\frac{K}{\epsilon}
step: if the learner has not finished all
K
episodes after
\frac{K}{\epsilon}
steps, then we stop the learner before it finishes all
K
episodes.

In the original process, we let
Reg
to be the regret,
T_{x}
and
T_{y}
be the number of steps the learner visits
x
and
y
, respectively,
T_{x,a}
be the number of steps the learner visits
x
and chooses action
a
, and let
T=T_{x}+T_{y}
. We define
T_{x}^{-},T_{y}^{-},T_{x,a}^{-},T^{-}
to be the corresponding quantities in the truncated process. We first show the following claim:

Claim 2

\mathbb{E}[\text{\rm Reg}]\geq\frac{2\Delta}{2-\Delta}\mathbb{E}[T_{x}^{-}-T_{x,a^{\star}}^{-}]
.

Claim 2.

We first focus on episode
k
in the original process. Let the episode starts from
t=t_{k}
, and the last step in the episode before reaching
g
is
t=e_{k}
. Then the expected regret is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[V^{\star}(s_{t_{k}})-\sum_{t=t_{k}}^{e_{k}}r(s_{t},a_{t})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[V^{\star}(s_{t_{k}})-\sum_{t=t_{k}}^{e_{k}}(Q^{\star}(s_{t},a_{t})-P_{s_{t},a_{t}}V^{\star})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[V^{\star}(s_{t_{k}})-\sum_{t=t_{k}}^{e_{k}}(Q^{\star}(s_{t},a_{t})-V^{\star}(s_{t+1}))\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=t_{k}}^{e_{k}}(V^{\star}(s_{t})-Q^{\star}(s_{t},a_{t}))\right].

Notice that
V^{\star}(s_{t})-Q^{\star}(s_{t},a_{t})=0
when
s_{t}=y
or
(s_{t},a_{t})=(x,a^{\star})
. When
s_{t}=x
and
a_{t}\neq a^{\star}
, we have
V^{\star}(s_{t})-Q^{\star}(s_{t},a_{t})=\frac{\Delta}{\epsilon}\frac{2\epsilon}{2-\Delta}=\frac{2\Delta}{2-\Delta}
according to Claim 1. Thus, the expected regret in episode

is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\text{\rm Reg}_{k}]=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{2\Delta}{2-\Delta}\sum_{t=t_{k}}^{e_{k}}\mathbf{1}[s_{t}=x,a_{t}\neq a^{\star}]\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{2\Delta}{2-\Delta}\sum_{t=t_{k}}^{e_{k}}(\mathbf{1}[s_{t}=x]-\mathbf{1}[s_{t}=x,a_{t}=a^{\star}])\right].

Summing this over episodes, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\text{\rm Reg}]=\frac{2\Delta}{2-\Delta}\mathbb{E}[T_{x}-T_{x,a^{\star}}].

Using the simple fact that
T_{x}-T_{x,a^{\star}}\geq T_{x}^{-}-T_{x,a^{\star}}^{-}
finishes the proof.
∎

Claim 3

\mathbb{E}[T_{x}^{-}]\geq\frac{K}{6\epsilon}

Claim 3.

Since on every state-action pair

The following proof follows the standard lower bound proof for multi-armed bandits

Claim 4

With standard arguments from

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{a}[T_{x,a}^{-}]-\mathbb{E}_{\textrm{unif}}[T_{x,a}^{-}]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K}{\epsilon}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\text{KL}(\mathbb{P}_{a},\mathbb{P}_{\textrm{unif}})}

\displaystyle=\frac{K}{\epsilon}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{K/\epsilon-1}\mathbb{P}_{\textrm{unif}}[(s_{t},a_{t})=(x,a)]\text{KL}\left(\text{Multi}\left(\frac{1-\epsilon}{2},\frac{1-\epsilon}{2},\epsilon\right),\text{Multi}\left(\frac{1-\epsilon+\Delta}{2},\frac{1-\epsilon-\Delta}{2},\epsilon\right)\right)}

, then
(2B+b)^{2}2^{-\frac{M}{2}}\leq c^{2}
, and the last expression can be further upper bounded by

\displaystyle O\left((B+b)\ln_{+}\left(\frac{B+b}{c}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}X_{t}\right]+c^{2}\sum_{m=M+1}^{\infty}2^{-\frac{m}{2}+1}m^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=O\left((B+b)\ln_{+}\left(\frac{B+b}{c}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}X_{t}\right]+c^{2}\right),

which proves (b).
(c) is an immediate result of (b) by picking
c=B+b
and noticing that
\mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=1}^{\tau}X_{t}]\leq B
.
∎

Lemma 25

(Exercise 5.15 in
Lattimore and Szepesvári 2020
)
.

Let
X_{t}
be a real valued random variable in the filtration of
\mathcal{F}_{t}=(X_{1},\dots X_{t-1})
such that
\mathbb{E}[X_{t}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{t}]=0
and assume
\eta>0
,
\mathbb{E}[X_{t}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{t}]<\eta^{-1}
a.s. Then with probability at least
1-\delta
for all
0<t\leq T
,

\sum_{s=1}^{t}X_{s}\leq\eta\sum_{s=1}^{t}\mathbb{E}[X_{s}^{2}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{s}]+\eta^{-1}\ln(\delta^{-1})\,.

Proof.

The claim is stated for a fixed horizon
T
in
Lattimore and Szepesvári 2020
. However, we can define the surrogate random variable

\displaystyle X^{\prime}_{i}\triangleq X_{i}\cdot\left(1-\mathbb{I}\left\{\exists 0\leq j<i:\,\sum_{s=1}^{j}X_{s}>\eta\sum_{s=1}^{j}\mathbb{E}[X_{s}^{2}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{s}]+\eta^{-1}\ln(\delta^{-1})\right\}\right).

X^{\prime}_{i}
is adapted to the filtration and it holds with probability
1-\delta
,

\sum_{s=1}^{T}X^{\prime}_{s}\leq\eta\sum_{s=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}[X^{\prime 2}_{s}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{s}]+\eta^{-1}\ln(\delta^{-1})\,,

which is equivalent to the anytime result for
X_{i}
.
∎

Lemma 26
.

Let
X_{t}
be a real valued random variable in the filtration of
\mathcal{F}_{t}=(X_{1},\dots X_{t-1})
such that
\mathbb{E}[X_{t}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{t}]=0
and assume
\mathbb{E}[|X_{t}|\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{t}]<\infty
a.s. Then with probability at least
1-\delta
uniformly over all
T>0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}X_{t}\leq 4\sqrt{V_{T}\left(\ln(\delta^{-1})+2\ln\ln(V_{T}))\right)}+eU_{T}\ln\left(\frac{2\ln^{2}(eU_{T})}{\delta}\right)\,,

where
V_{T}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}[X_{t}^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]
,
U_{T}=\max\{1,\max_{t\in[T]}X_{t}\}
.

Proof.

Define
Z_{t}^{(i)}=X_{t}\cdot\mathbb{I}\left\{U_{t}\leq\exp(i)\right\}
, then
Z_{t}^{(i)}\exp(-i)\leq 1
almost surely.
By Exercise 5.15 of
Lattimore and Szepesvári 2020
, with probability at least
1-\delta/(2i^{2})
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}Z_{t}^{(i)}\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}(Z_{t}^{(i)}-\mathbb{E}[Z_{t}^{(i)}~|~\mathcal{F}_{t}])\leq\exp(-i)\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(Z_{t}^{(i)}\right)^{2}~\Big|~\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]+\exp(i)\ln\left(\frac{2i^{2}}{\delta}\right)\,.

By a union bound, this holds with probability
1-\delta
uniformly over all
i\geq 1
. Note that
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\big[\big(Z_{t}^{(i)}\big)^{2}~|~\mathcal{F}_{t}\big]\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}[X_{t}^{2}~|~\mathcal{F}_{t}]=V_{T}
and for any
i
such that
\exp(i)\geq U_{T}
, we have
\sum_{t=1}^{T}Z_{t}^{(i)}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}X_{t}
. Hence with probability
1-\delta
,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}X_{t}

\displaystyle\leq\min_{i:\exp(i)\geq U_{T}}\exp(-i)V_{T}+\exp(i)\ln\left(\frac{2i^{2}}{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq eU_{T}\ln\left(\frac{2\ln(eU_{T})^{2}}{\delta}\right)+\min_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp(-i)V_{T}+\exp(i)\ln\left(\frac{2i^{2}}{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq eU_{T}\ln\left(\frac{2\ln(eU_{T})^{2}}{\delta}\right)+2\min_{\gamma>0}\gamma^{-1}V_{T}+\gamma\ln\left(\frac{2\ln(\gamma)^{2}}{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq eU_{T}\ln\left(\frac{2\ln(eU_{T})^{2}}{\delta}\right)+4\sqrt{V_{T}\ln\left(\frac{\ln^{2}(V_{T})}{\delta}\right)}\,.

(choosing
\gamma=\sqrt{\frac{V_{T}}{\ln(2\delta^{-1}\ln^{2}(V_{T})}}
)

The reasoning for the second inequality is the following. We are computing
\min_{i\geq i_{0}}f(i)+g(i)
, where
f(i)
is monotonically decreasing in
i
and
g(i)
is monotonically increasing. Let
i^{*}=\argmin_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}f(i)+g(i)
.
If
i^{*}\geq i_{0}
, the equation is obviously true. Otherwise the optimal solution is
\min_{i\geq i_{0}}f(i)+g(i)=f(i_{0})+g(i_{0})\leq f(i^{*})+g(i_{0})
by the monotonicity.

∎

Lemma 27
.

Let
X_{1},\dots\in\mathbb{R}^{S}
be a sequence of i.i.d. random vectors with mean
\mu
, variance
\Sigma
such that
\left\|X_{i}\right\|_{1}<c
almost surely. Then with probability at least
1-2S\delta
, it holds for all
w\in\mathbb{R}^{S}
such that
\left\|w\right\|_{\infty}<C
and
T>0
simultaneously:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\langle X_{t},w\rangle\leq 4\sqrt{STw^{\top}\Sigma w\left(\ln(\delta^{-1})+2\ln\ln(Tc^{2})\right)}+eScC\ln\left(\frac{2\ln^{2}(ecC)}{\delta}\right)\,.

Proof.

The proof is extends
Lemma
26
.
Let
v_{1},\dots,v_{S}
be the eigenvectors of
\Sigma
, then we have with probability
1-2S\delta
for all
v_{i}
simultaneously

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\langle X_{t},v_{i}\rangle\right|\leq 4\sqrt{Tv_{i}^{\top}\Sigma v_{i}\left(\ln(\delta^{-1})+2\ln\ln(Tc^{2})\right)}+ec\ln\left(\frac{2\ln^{2}(ec)}{\delta}\right)\,.

Let
w=\sum_{i=1}^{S}a_{i}v_{i}
, where we know that
\left\|w\right\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{S}C
and
\sum_{i=1}^{S}|a_{i}|\leq SC
.
This implies

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\langle X_{t},w\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{S}4\sqrt{Ta_{i}^{2}v_{i}^{\top}\Sigma v_{i}\left(\ln(\delta^{-1})+2\ln\ln(Tc^{2})\right)}+|a_{i}|ec\ln\left(\frac{2\ln^{2}(ec)}{\delta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 4\sqrt{STw^{\top}\Sigma w\left(\ln(\delta^{-1})+2\ln\ln(Tc^{2})\right)}+ecCS\ln\left(\frac{2\ln^{2}(ec)}{\delta}\right)\,.

∎

Lemma 28
.

Let
X_{1},\dots\in[0,B]
be a sequence of i.i.d. random variables with mean
\mu
and variance
\sigma^{2}
. Then with probability at least
1-\delta
, it holds for all
T\geq 8\ln(2\delta^{-1})
simultaneously:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}X_{t}-T\mu\leq\sqrt{8\sum_{i=1}^{T}\left(X_{i}-\frac{1}{T}\sum_{j=1}^{T}X_{j}\right)^{2}(\ln(2\delta^{-1})+4\ln\ln(T))}+12B(\ln(2\delta^{-1})+4\ln\ln(T)).

Proof.

By
Lemma
26
, we have that with probability
1-\delta/2
, for all
T>0
simultaneously,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}X_{t}-T\mu\leq\sqrt{T\sigma^{2}(\ln(2\delta^{-1})+4\ln\ln(T))}+B(\ln(2\delta^{-1})+4\ln\ln(T))\,.

Applying the same Lemma to the sequence
Z_{i}=-(X_{i}-\mu)^{2}+\sigma^{2}
, we have with probability
1-\delta/2
for all
T>0
simultaneously

\displaystyle T\sigma^{2}-\sum_{t=1}^{T}(X_{t}-\mu)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_{t-1}[((X_{t}-\mu)^{2}-\sigma^{2})^{2}](\ln(2\delta^{-1})+4\ln\ln(T))}+\sigma^{2}(\ln(2\delta^{-1})+4\ln\ln(T))

\displaystyle\leq B\sqrt{T\sigma^{2}(\ln(2\delta^{-1})+4\ln\ln(T))}+\sigma^{2}(\ln(2\delta^{-1})+4\ln\ln(T))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{T}{2}\sigma^{2}+5B^{2}(\ln(2\delta^{-1})+4\ln\ln(T))\,.

Finally we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}(X_{t}-\mu)^{2}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(X_{t}-\frac{1}{T}\sum_{j=1}^{T}X_{j}\right)^{2}+T\left(\mu-\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}X_{t}\right)^{2}\,.

Combining everything and taking a union bounds, leads to with probability
1-\delta

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}X_{t}-T\mu

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(X_{t}-\frac{1}{T}\sum_{j=1}^{T}X_{j}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{T}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}X_{t}-T\mu\right)^{2}+10B^{2}\right)(\ln(2\delta^{-1})+4\ln\ln(T))}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+B(\ln(2\delta^{-1})+4\ln\ln(T))

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T}X_{t}-T\mu\right|\sqrt{\frac{2\ln(2\delta^{-1})+4\ln\ln(T)}{T}}+\sqrt{2\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(X_{t}-\frac{1}{T}\sum_{j=1}^{T}X_{j}\right)^{2}}+6B(\ln(2\delta^{-1})+4\ln\ln(T)).

For
T\geq 8\ln(2\delta^{-1})
, we have
\frac{2\ln(2\delta^{-1})+4\ln\ln(T)}{T}\leq\frac{1}{4}
and rearranging finishes the proof.
∎

Appendix G
Weakening the assumption on proper policies

Assumption
1
can be weakened to the following:

Assumption 2
.

There exists a policy
\pi^{\star}\in\Pi^{\text{{SD}}}
such that

•

V^{\pi^{\star}}(s)\geq V^{\pi}(s)
for all
s\in\mathcal{S}
and
\pi\in\Pi^{\text{{HD}}}
.

•

T_{\star}\triangleq\max_{s}\mathbb{E}^{\pi^{\star}}[\tau~|~s_{1}=s]<\infty
, where
\tau
is the time index right before reaching
g
.

In words,
Assumption
2
assumes that there exists an optimal policy that is stationary and proper.
If such an optimal policy is not unique, we can take
T_{\star}
to be the minimum over all such policies. Notice that the first part of
Assumption
2
is sufficient for all our algorithms to work, though the regret bound has a
\ln T
factor, which could be unbounded. That is why in the main text we introduced the stronger
Assumption
1
and upper bound
T
by the order of
KT_{\max}
. Below we show that with the additional second part of
Assumption
2
and the algorithmic trick introduced in
Tarbouriech et al. 2021b
, the
T_{\max}
dependency can be replaced by
T_{\star}
.

Assuming that an order optimal bound of
T_{\star}
is known, the agent modifies the MDP by modifying all rewards
\tilde{r}(s,a)=r(s,a)-\frac{1}{KT_{\star}}
.
The value of the optimal policy in the modified MDP is smaller than that in the original MDP by at most
\frac{1}{K}
. It is then sufficient to bound the regret for the modified MDP, since

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(V^{\star}(s_{\text{init}})-\sum_{t=t_{k}}^{e_{k}}r(s_{t},a_{t})\right)\leq 1+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left({\tilde{V}}^{\star}(s_{\text{init}})-\sum_{t=t_{k}}^{e_{k}}\tilde{r}(s_{t},a_{t})\right)\,.

For the modified MDP, we can show that the total time horizon
T
is bounded. By a trivial bound of
\mathbb{V}(P_{t},V^{\star})\leq B_{\star}^{2}
, combined with
Lemma
16
, we get that with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left({\tilde{V}}^{\star}(s_{\text{init}})-\sum_{t=t_{k}}^{e_{k}}\tilde{r}(s_{t},a_{t})\right)\leq O\left(\sqrt{SAB_{\star}^{2}T\tilde{\iota}_{T,B,\delta}}+BS^{2}A\tilde{\iota}_{T,B,\delta}\right)\,.

Notice that
K\tilde{V}^{\star}(s_{\text{init}})\geq KV^{\star}(s_{\text{init}})-1
and
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\tilde{r}(s_{t},a_{t})=-\frac{T}{KT_{\star}}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}r(s_{t},a_{t})\leq-\frac{T}{KT_{\star}}+KV^{\star}(s_{\text{init}})+R\sqrt{K\ln(KR/\delta)}+R_{\max}\ln(KR_{\max}/\delta)
with probability
\geq 1-O(\delta)
by
Lemma
26
(notice that
\mathbb{E}\big[\sum_{t=1}^{T}r(s_{t},a_{t})\big]\leq KV^{\star}(s_{\text{init}})
).
Rearranging by
T
leads to with probability at least
1-O(\delta)
,

\displaystyle T\leq O\left(\text{poly}(S,A,B,B_{\star},R,R_{\max},K,T_{\star},\delta^{-1})\right)\,.

This upper bound on
T
helps us to replace the
\ln T
dependency in the regret by a log term that only involves algorithm-independent quantities.

If an order optimal bound of
T_{\star}
is not known, we can follow the arguments in
Tarbouriech et al. 2021b
that sets
\tilde{r}(s,a)=r(s,a)-\frac{1}{K^{n}}
for some
n\gg 1
. By this, we can also remove the dependency on
T_{\max}
, with the price of an additional
\frac{T_{\star}}{K^{n-1}}
regret.

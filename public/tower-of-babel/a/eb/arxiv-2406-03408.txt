1.
Introduction

The main results of classical harmonic analysis are related to homogeneous spaces
(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mu)
;
see, for example, monograph
[
10
]
.
By definition, this means that

\mu
has the standard doubling property
\mu(2Q)\leq C\mu(Q)

for an arbitrary cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
The principal motivation of the present work is to consider the Calderón–Zygmund operators in a non-classical setting,
that is, on non-homogeneous spaces.
Indeed, the main object in this work is the functional space
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mu)

introduced by X. Tolsa
[
11
]
for an
n
-dimensional measure
\mu
.
Thus, the measure
\mu
may not have the standard doubling property.
Other generalizations of the space
\mathrm{BMO}
were also considered
for such measures;
see, for example,
[
7
,
8
]
.
However,
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mu)
has various genuine properties of the classical space
\mathrm{BMO}
,
for example, the John–Nirenberg inequality holds for
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mu)
.

Let
T
be a Calderón–Zygmund operator
such that its kernel satisfies the appropriate cancellation property
(see (
1.4
)).
In
[
2
]
, the authors obtained a condition on the function
T1
, which
guarantees that the operator
T
is bounded on

\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)=\mathrm{RBMO}(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mu)
.
In this work, we prove that the condition from
[
2
]
,
after a slight modification, is not only sufficient, but also necessary for the boundedness of
T
.
In other words, in the present paper,
we obtain a so-called
T1
theorem for the operator
T
on
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
,
that is, we give a characterization of the bounded Calderón–Zygmund operators

T:\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)\to\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)

in terms of the function
T1
.

We introduce certain definitions and facts from
[
11
]
and
[
2
]
.

1.1.
Cubes and
n
-dimensional measures

Unless otherwise stated, everywhere below
\mu
is a finite positive measure defined on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
By definition, cube is a closed cube in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, its sides are parallel to the coordinate axes
and its center belongs to the support of
\mu
.
The side length of
Q
is denoted by
\ell=\ell(Q)
.

As in
[
11
]
, we always assume that
\mu
is an
n
-dimensional measure on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, where

0<n\leq m
.
By definition, it means that

(1.1)

\mu(Q)\leq C\ell^{n}(Q)\ \textrm{for every cube\ }Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{m},\ \ell(Q)>0,

with a constant
C>0
.

1.2.
Calderón–Zygmund operators.

A Calderón–Zygmund kernel associated with an
n
-dimensional measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}

is a measurable function

\mathcal{K}(x,y)
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{(x,x):x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\}

and satisfying the following conditions:

(1.2)

|\mathcal{K}(x,y)|\leq Cd^{-n}(x,y),

(1.3)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{K}(x_{1},y)-\mathcal{K}(x_{2},y)|

\displaystyle+|\mathcal{K}(y,x_{1})-\mathcal{K}(y,x_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{d^{\delta}(x_{1},x_{2})}{d^{n+\delta}(x_{1},y)},\quad\textrm{whenever}\ \ 2d(x_{1},x_{2})\leq d(x_{1},y),

where
C>0
is a universal constant and

\delta
,
0<\delta\leq 1
, is a regularity constant.
Restrictions (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) are standard by now.
Also, remark that property (
1.3
)
is used in the celebrated
T1
theorem of David and Journé
[
1
]
.

Also, in the main theorem of the present paper, we use the following more specific
cancellation property:

(1.4)

\left|\int_{Q(x,R)\setminus Q(x,r)}\mathcal{K}(x,y)\,d\mu(y)\right|\leq C,\quad 0<r<R,

where
C>0
is a universal constant.
This type of restriction is sufficiently common
(see, for example,
[
10
, Ch. 7, Exercise. 5.17]
in the classical setting,
see also
[
3
]
in the case of nonhomogeneous spaces).

The Calderón–Zygmund operator associated to the kernel
\mathcal{K}(x,y)
and the measure

\mu
is defined as

Tf(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\mathcal{K}(x,y)f(y)\,d\mu(y)

for
x\notin\textrm{supp}(f\mu)
.
In the general situation, one introduces the truncated operators

T_{\varepsilon}
,
\varepsilon>0
:

T_{\varepsilon}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus Q(x,\varepsilon)}\mathcal{K}(x,y)f(y)\,d\mu(y).

We say that
T
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mu)

provided that the operators
T_{\varepsilon}
are bounded on
L^{p}(\mu)
uniformly in
\varepsilon>0
.

1.3.
Regular BMO space

To define
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
, we need coefficients
K(Q,R)

and doubling cubes.

1.3.1.
Coefficients
K(Q,R)

For embedded cubes
Q\subset R
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, put

K(Q,R)=1+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{Q,R}}\frac{\mu(2^{j}Q)}{\ell^{n}(2^{j}Q)},

where
N_{Q,R}
is the minimal
integer
k
such that
\ell(2^{k}Q)\geq\ell(R)
.
Clearly,
K(Q,R)\geq 1
.
Also, remark that
K(Q,R)
is estimated by

C\log(\ell(R)/\ell(Q))
, since
\mu
is
n
-dimensional.

1.3.2.
Doubling cubes

Let
\alpha>1
,
\beta>\alpha^{n}
. A cube
Q
is called
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling if

\mu(\alpha Q)<\beta\mu(Q).

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure defined on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\alpha>1
.
As indicated in
[
11
]
, it is known that, for a sufficiently large parameter

\beta=\beta(\alpha,n)
, for
\mu
-almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

there exists a sequence of
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cubes
\{Q_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
centered at
x

and such that

\ell(Q_{k})\to 0
as
k\to\infty
.
For definiteness, let
\beta_{n}
denote the double infimum of the corresponding constants

\beta(10,n)
.

Definition 1
.

A cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}

is called
doubling
(we write
Q\in\mathcal{D}
)
if
Q
is
(10,\beta_{n})
-doubling.

Remark 1
.

The analogous definition in

[
2
]
is based on

(4,\beta_{n})
-doubling cubes in the place of
(10,\beta_{n})
-doubling ones.
Also, remark that Tolsa
[
11
]
uses
(2,\beta)
-doubling cubes in his original definition.
However, further work showed that
in Tolsa theory in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
one can use
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cubes with
\alpha>1

(see, for example,
[
5
,
6
]
).

1.3.3.
Definition of the space
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)

The definition of the space
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
given below is used as the main one in
[
2
]
.
Remark that Tolsa
[
11
]
used a different equivalent definition as his principal one.

Definition 2
.

The space
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
consists of
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mu)
such that
there exists a constant
C_{\mathfrak{E}}>0

and a collection of constants
\{f_{Q}\}

(one constant for every doubling cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
)
such that

(1.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}|f-f_{Q}|\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathfrak{E}},

(1.6)

\displaystyle|f_{Q}-f_{R}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathfrak{E}}K(Q,R)

for all cubes
Q,R\in\mathcal{D}
,
Q\subset R
.
Let
\|f\|_{\mathfrak{E}}
denote the infimum of the
corresponding constants
C_{\mathfrak{E}}>0
.

It is not difficult to verify that

\|f\|_{\mathfrak{E}}
is a norm on the space
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)

modulo constants.
Next,
\mu
is a finite
n
-dimensional measure, hence,

\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)\subset L^{1}(\mu)
by standard arguments.
Direct inspection shows that
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)

is a Banach space with respect to the following norm:

\|f\|_{*}:=\|f\|_{\mathfrak{E}}+\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}.

1.4.
Main theorem

Recall that
\mu(\mathbb{R}^{m})<\infty
by assumption.
For a cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, put

K(Q)=K(Q,2^{k}Q),

where
k
is the minimal positive integer such that
\mu(2^{k}Q)>\frac{1}{2}\mu(\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mu
be a finite positive
n
-dimensional measure defined on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
0<n\leq m
.
Let
T
be a Calderón–Zygmund operator such that the cancellation property (
1.4
) holds.
Then the following properties are equivalent:

(i)

The operator
T
is bounded on the Banach space
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
.

(ii)

For every cube
Q\in\mathcal{D}
, there exists a constant
b_{Q}
such that

(1.7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}|T1-b_{Q}|\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{K(Q)}\quad\textrm{for all cubes}\ Q\in\mathcal{D},

(1.8)

\displaystyle|b_{Q}-b_{R}|

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{K(Q,R)}{K(Q)}\quad\textrm{for all cubes}\ Q,R\in\mathcal{D},\ Q\subset R,

where the constant
C>0
does not depend on
Q
and
R
.

Remark 2
.

By definition,
T
is bounded on

RBMO(\mu)

provided that the truncated operators
T_{\varepsilon}
,
\varepsilon>0
,
are uniformly bounded on the space
RBMO(\mu)
.
Analogously, by definition,
inequalities (
1.7
) and (
1.8
)
mean that these estimates hold for the operators
T_{\varepsilon}

uniformly in
\varepsilon>0
.

Remark 3
.

Firstly, under hypothesis of Theorem
1
, one implicitly assumes that
T1\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
.
Indeed, this property follows from (
1.4
) by
[
2
, Lemma 2.6]
.
Secondly, by Lemma 2.8 from
[
2
]
condition (
1.4
)
implies that
T
is a bounded operator on
L^{2}(\mu)
.

2.
Auxiliary results

2.1.
An equivalent norm on
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)

Definition 3
.

Let
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mu)
. Fix a constant
\rho>1
.
Let
\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},\rho}
denote the infimum of the constants

C_{\mathfrak{A}}=C_{\mathfrak{A},\rho}>0

with the following properties:
for every cube
Q
, there exists a constant
f_{Q}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{\mu(\rho Q)}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathfrak{A}},

(2.2)

\displaystyle|f_{Q}-f_{R}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathfrak{A}}K(Q,R)\quad\text{for all cubes\ }Q\subset R.

Direct inspection shows that

\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{A}}
is a seminorm on
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
.

Remark 4
.

The original seminorm introduced by Tolsa
[
11
]
on
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)

differs from the seminorms given in Definitions
2
and
3
.
However, it is known that all these seminorms are equivalent (see
[
11
]
and
[
2
, Section 2]
).

2.2.
Decomposition of a function from
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
into three terms

Let
T
denote a Carderón–Zygmund operator bounded on
L^{2}(\mu)
.
Assume that
f\in\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
and
\rho=2
.
Applying Definition
3
, for every cube
2Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
we select a constant
f_{2Q}

such that properties (
2.1
)
and (
2.2
)
hold for the constant
C_{\mathfrak{A},\rho}=2\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},\rho}

and for
2Q
in the place of
Q
, that is,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{2Q}\frac{1}{\mu(2\rho Q)}\int_{2Q}

\displaystyle|f(x)-f_{2Q}|\,d\mu(x)\leq 2\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},\rho},

(2.4)

\displaystyle|f_{2Q}-f_{2R}|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},\rho}K(2Q,2R)\quad\text{for all cubes\ }Q\subset R.

Introduce the following functions:

\displaystyle f_{1}=f_{1,Q}

\displaystyle=f_{2Q},

\displaystyle f_{2}=f_{2,Q}

\displaystyle=(f-f_{2Q})\chi_{2Q},

\displaystyle f_{3}=f_{3,Q}

\displaystyle=(f-f_{2Q})\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 2Q}.

Observe that

f=f_{1}+f_{2}+f_{3}.

Similar decompositions are well known (see, for example,
[
4
]
).

Put
b_{2,Q}=0
and

b_{3,Q}=\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}Tf_{3,Q}(y)\,d\mu(y).

We will need two lemmas from
[
2
]
about properties of the above functions and constants.

Lemma 1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}|Tf_{k}-b_{k,Q}|\,d\mu\leq C\|f\|,\quad k=2,3,

for any cube
Q\in\mathcal{D}
.

Lemma 2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

|b_{k,Q}-b_{k,R}|\leq C\|f\|K(Q,R),\quad k=2,3.

for any cubes
Q\subset R
.

3.
Test functions

In this section, we construct test functions used to prove the implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii).
We introduce the following notation:

|x|=\max\{|x_{j}|:j=1,2,\dots,m\},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Put

(3.1)

\varphi(y)=\varphi(|y|)=1+\int_{|y|<|t|}\frac{d\mu(t)}{|t|^{n}},\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{0\}.

The measure
\mu
is
n
-dimensional and
\mu(\mathbb{R}^{m})<\infty
, thus, the above integral converges.
The function
\varphi
is continuous and
\ell^{\infty}
-radial on its domain.

Let
Q(r)
,
r>0
, denote the
cube centered at the origin and with side length
2r
.

Remark 5
.

Using the dyadic decomposition for the integral defining
\varphi(y)
, we conclude that

\varphi(y)\approx K(Q(|y|))

for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
. See
[
11
]
.

The following result is motivated by Lemma 1.1
from
[
9
]
, where

\mu
is Lebesgue measure.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\varphi
be defined by (
3.1
),
\alpha\geq 5
and
\beta>\alpha^{n}
.
Then
\varphi\in\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
.
There exist constants
C
and
C(\alpha,\beta)>0
such that

(3.2)

|\langle\varphi_{S}\rangle|\geq CK(S)-C(\alpha,\beta)

for every
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cube
S
centered at the origin.

Remark 6
.

We apply Lemma
3
with a sufficiently large
K(S)
, therefore,

CK(S)-C(\alpha,\beta)

in estimate (
3.2
)
becomes positive in this case.

Proof of Lemma
3
.

The argument is based on the following plan: on the first two steps, for an arbitrary cube
Q
,
we verify conditions (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) from Definition
3
with
\rho=5
.
The final third step is devoted to proving estimate (
3.2
)
for an
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cube
S
centered at the origin.

Step I.

On this step, we verify the oscillation condition (
2.1
) from Definition
3
with
\rho=5
.
To do this, fix a cube
Q
with center
x_{Q}
and with side length
\ell_{Q}
.
Let
\widetilde{Q}
denote the smallest cube with center at the origin and containing the cube
Q
.
Choose
y_{Q}\in\partial Q
such that
|y_{Q}|=\ell_{Q}/2+|x_{Q}|
. Put
\varphi_{Q}=\varphi(y_{Q})
. We have

\begin{split}I&=\int_{Q}|\varphi(z)-\varphi(y_{Q})|d\mu(z)=\int_{Q}\left|\int_{|t|>|z|}\frac{d\mu(t)}{|t|^{n}}-\int_{|t|>|y_{Q}|}\frac{d\mu(t)}{|t|^{n}}\right|d\mu(z)\\
&=\int_{Q}d\mu(z)\int_{|z|<|t|<|y_{Q}|}\frac{d\mu(t)}{|t|^{n}}\\
&\leq\int_{\widetilde{Q}}d\mu(z)\int_{|z|<|t|<|y_{Q}|}\frac{d\mu(t)}{|t|^{n}}.\end{split}

Now, consider two cases depending on the fulfillment of the inequality

\ell_{Q}>|x_{Q}|.

If
\ell_{Q}>|x_{Q}|
, then
the inequalities
|y_{Q}|=|x_{Q}|+\ell_{Q}/2<3\ell_{Q}/2
and
|y_{Q}|+|x_{Q}|<5\ell_{Q}/2

imply that
\widetilde{Q}=Q(|y_{Q}|)
.
Therefore,

Q\subset\widetilde{Q}\subset 5Q
. Hence,

I\leq\int_{\widetilde{Q}}d\mu(z)\int_{|z|<|t|<|y_{Q}|}\frac{d\mu(t)}{|t|^{n}}=\int_{|z|<|y_{Q}|}d\mu(z)\int_{|z|<|t|<|y_{Q}|}\frac{d\mu(t)}{|t|^{n}}.

Changing the order of integration, we continue as

I\leq\int_{|t|<|y_{Q}|}\frac{d\mu(t)}{|t|^{n}}\int_{|z|<|t|}d\mu(z)\leq\int_{|t|<|y_{Q}|}\frac{\mu(Q(|t|))}{|t|^{n}}d\mu(t).

Since
\mu
is
n
-dimensional, we have

\mu(Q(|t|))\leq C|t|^{n}
, therefore,

I\lesssim\int_{|t|>|y_{Q}|}d\mu(t)=\mu(\widetilde{Q})\leq\mu(5Q),

as required. The analysis of the first case is completed.

If
\ell_{Q}\leq|x_{Q}|
, then
|z|\geq|x_{Q}|/2
for
z\in Q
. Also, we have
|y_{Q}|\leq 3|x_{Q}|/2
.
Hence, for the inner integral, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{|z|<|t|<|y_{Q}|}\frac{d\mu(t)}{|t|^{n}}&\leq\int_{|x_{Q}|/2<|t|<3|x_{Q}|/2}\frac{d\mu(t)}{|t|^{n}}\\
&\leq\frac{2^{n}}{|x_{Q}|^{n}}\int_{|t|<3|x_{Q}|/2}d\mu(t)\\
&=\frac{2^{n}}{|x_{Q}|^{n}}\mu(Q(3|x_{Q}|/2)\leq\frac{3^{n}}{|\ell_{Q}|^{n}}|x_{Q}|^{n}\leq C,\end{split}

since
\mu
is
n
-dimensional.

Thus, for the entire integral
I
, we have

I\lesssim\int_{Q}d\mu(z)=\mu(Q),

which completes the analysis of the second case.
Therefore, the oscillation condition from Definition
3

is verified with parameter
\rho=5
.

Step II.

On this step, we verify
K
-condition from
Definition
3
with
\rho=5
.
Recall that
\varphi_{Q}=\varphi(y_{Q})
for
y_{Q}\in\partial Q
such that
|y_{Q}|=|x_{Q}|+\ell_{Q}/2
.

Consider cubes
Q\subset R
. Let
k
be the minimal integer such that

Q_{0}:=2^{k}Q\supset R
.
Let
\widetilde{Q}
,

\widetilde{Q}_{0}
and
\widetilde{R}
denote the smallest cubes centered at zero
and containing
Q
,

Q_{0}
and
R
, respectively.

We have

|\varphi_{Q}-\varphi_{R}|\leq|\varphi_{Q}-\varphi_{Q_{0}}|+|\varphi_{Q_{0}}-\varphi_{R}|=A+B.

The cubes
Q_{0}
and
Q
are concentric, thus,
|y_{Q_{0}}|=|x_{Q}|+2^{k-1}\ell_{Q}
.
Hence,

A=\left|\int_{|t|>|y_{Q}|}\frac{d\mu(t)}{|t|}-\int_{|t|>|y_{Q_{0}}|}\frac{d\mu(t)}{|t|^{n}}\right|=\int_{|y_{Q}|<|t|<|y_{Q_{0}}|}\frac{d\mu(t)}{|t|^{n}}\approx K(\widetilde{Q},\widetilde{Q}_{0})

by the dyadic decomposition of the corresponding integral (cf.
[
11
]
).

Now, consider two cases depending on the fulfillment of the condition

2|y_{Q}|\geq|y_{Q_{0}}|.

If
2|y_{Q}|\geq|y_{Q_{0}}|
, then the cubes
\widetilde{Q}
and
\widetilde{Q}_{0}

are comparable. Therefore,

K(\widetilde{Q},\widetilde{Q}_{0})\leq C.

If
2|y_{Q}|<|y_{Q_{0}}|
,
then
|x_{Q}|<\ell_{Q_{0}}/2
. Hence,

0\in Q_{0}
. Therefore,
Q_{0}\subset\widetilde{Q}_{0}\subset 3Q_{0}
and we have

\begin{split}K(\widetilde{Q},\widetilde{Q}_{0})&\leq K(\widetilde{Q},3Q_{0})\\
&\leq K(Q,3Q_{0})=K(Q,Q_{0})+K(Q_{0},3Q_{0})\leq K(Q,Q_{0})+C\approx K(Q,R),\end{split}

since the cubes
Q_{0}
and
R
are comparable.

Next, we have

B=|\varphi_{Q_{0}}-\varphi_{R}|=\left|\int_{|t|>|y_{Q_{0}}|}\frac{d\mu(t)}{|t|^{n}}-\int_{|t|>|y_{R}|}\frac{d\mu(t)}{|t|^{n}}\right|\approx K(\widetilde{R},\widetilde{Q}_{0}).

Consider two cases depending on the fulfillment of the condition

2|y_{R}|\geq|y_{Q_{0}}|.

If
2|y_{R}|\geq|y_{Q_{0}}|
, then the cubes
\widetilde{R}
and
\widetilde{Q}_{0}
are comparable.
Hence,

K(\widetilde{R},\widetilde{Q}_{0})\leq C.

Suppose that
2|y_{R}|<|y_{Q_{0}}|
.
Since
R\supset Q
and the cubes
Q
and
Q_{0}
are concentric, we have

|x_{Q}|<|y_{R}|<|y_{Q_{0}}|/2=1/2(|x_{Q}|+\ell_{Q_{0}}/2).

Therefore,
|x_{Q}|<\ell_{Q_{0}}/2
and
0\in Q_{0}
.
Thus,
Q_{0}\subset\widetilde{Q}_{0}\subset 3Q_{0}
and we obtain

B\leq K(\widetilde{R},\widetilde{Q}_{0})\leq K(R,3Q_{0})=K(R,Q_{0})+K(Q_{0},3Q_{0})\leq C,

since the cubes
Q_{0}
and
R
are comparable.

Step III.

On this step, we assume that

S
is a centered at the origin

-doubling cube,

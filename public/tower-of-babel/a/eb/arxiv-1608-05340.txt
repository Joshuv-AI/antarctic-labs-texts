Relativistic Kinematics of Two-Parametric Riemann Surface in Genus Two


Abstract
It is considered a model of compact Riemann surface in genus two, represented geometrically by two-parametric hyperbolic octagon with an order four automorphism and described algebraically by the corresponding Fuchsian group. Introducing the Fenchel–Nielsen variables, we compute the Weil–Petersson (WP) symplectic two-form for parameter space and analyze the closed isoperimetric orbits of octagons. WP-Area in parameter space and the canonical action–angle variables for the orbits are found. Exploiting the ideas from the loop quantum gravity, we generate relativistic kinematics by the Lorentz boost and quantize WP-area. We treat the evolution in terms of global variables within the “big bounce” concept. Keywords: Riemann surfaces in genus two; geometrodynamics; area quantization

2
Model Octagon and Riemann Surface

The Poincaré model of two-dimensional hyperbolic space is given by open disk

\mathbb{D}=\left\{z=x+\mathrm{i}y||z|<1\right\}
and the metric
\mathrm{d}s^{2}=4|\mathrm{d}z|^{2}/(1-|z|^{2})^{2}
.
The geodesic in
(\mathbb{D},\mathrm{d}s^{2})
is an circle arc inside
\mathbb{D}
with radius
R
and
center at the point
z_{0}=\sqrt{1+R^{2}}\exp{(\mathrm{i}\phi)}
lying beyond the unit disk.
In particular case, the geodesics emanating from the origin are the Euclidean straight
lines (diameters). All geodesics intersect the boundary
\partial\mathbb{D}
orthogonally.

The group of all orientation-preserving isometries
\gamma
of
(\mathbb{D},\mathrm{d}s^{2})
,
denoted by
{\rm Isom^{+}(\mathbb{D})}
, acts via the Möbius transformation:

z\mapsto\gamma[z]=\frac{uz+v}{\overline{v}z+\overline{u}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

(1)

where
u
and
v
satisfy relation
|u|^{2}-|v|^{2}=1
;
\bar{u}
,
\bar{v}
are the complex
conjugates. Thus, it is convenient to identify a generator
\gamma
with an element of group

{\rm SU(1,1)}=\left\{\left.\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u&v\\
\overline{v}&\overline{u}\end{array}\right)\right||u|^{2}-|v|^{2}=1\right\}.

(2)

The Riemann surface
S
is understood here as a compact two-dimensional orientable manifold
with the metric of constant negative curvature. Such a surface is obtained from hyperbolic
simply connected octagon
{\cal F}
in
\mathbb{D}
via gluing opposite sides formed by
eight geodesic arcs, whose intersections serve as vertices.

Since the opposite sides of

g_{k}

g_{0}g^{-1}_{1}g_{2}g^{-1}_{3}g^{-1}_{0}g_{1}g^{-1}_{2}g_{3}={\rm id},

(6)

and we then define surface

as a quotient
\mathbb{D}/\Gamma

Our calculations give the dependence of
g_{0}
and
g_{1}

(a,\alpha)

\displaystyle g_{0}=N(a,\tilde{\alpha})\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a(1-\tan{\tilde{\alpha}})&(a^{2}-\tan{\tilde{\alpha}})+\mathrm{i}(1-a^{2})\\
(a^{2}-\tan{\tilde{\alpha}})-\mathrm{i}(1-a^{2})&a(1-\tan{\tilde{\alpha}})\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle g_{1}=N(a,\tilde{\alpha})\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a(1+\tan{\tilde{\alpha}})&(1-a^{2})+\mathrm{i}(a^{2}+\tan{\tilde{\alpha}})\\

The remaining generators are simply obtained by rotations:

g_{2,3}=R_{\frac{\pi}{2}}g_{0,1}R^{-1}_{\frac{\pi}{2}},\quad g^{-1}_{k}=R_{\pi}g_{k}R^{-1}_{\pi};

R_{\varphi}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\exp{\left(\mathrm{i}{\varphi}/{2}\right)}&0\\

The set of all marking equivalence classes of the closed and compact Riemann surfaces

{\cal T}_{g}

In our case, the Riemann surfaces result in the subset of total
{\cal T}_{2}

3
Structure of Parameter Space
{\cal A}

3.1
Symplectic Two-Form

A hyperbolic Riemann surface of genus
g
without boundary always contains a system of

3g-3
simple closed geodesics that are neither homotopic to each other nor homotopically
trivial. The cut along these geodesics always decomposes surface into
2g-2
pairs of pants
(three-holed spheres), playing a role of natural building blocks for Riemann surface
[
15
]
.

In the case at hand, surface
S
is two-holed torus which can be decomposed into two pairs
of pants by a system of three closed geodesics. This surgery results in computing the
Fenchel–Nielsen (FN) parameters: lengths
\ell_{k}
of these geodesics and the corresponding
twists
\tau_{k}
(see
[
9
,
15
]
).

For one of possible pants decompositions, we have found these quantities
[
16
]
:

\displaystyle\ell_{1,2}=2~{\rm arccosh}~{\frac{a^{2}}{1-a^{2}}},

\displaystyle\ell_{3}=2\ln{\frac{1+a}{1-a}},

(14)

\displaystyle\tau_{1,2}={\rm arccosh}\left[\frac{2a^{2}-1}{a^{2}(1-b^{2})}-1\right],

\displaystyle\tau_{3}=\ln{\frac{1+a}{1-a}}.

(15)

Since the Teichmüller space
{\cal T}_{2}
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{6}
, one can
identify the FN variables with global coordinates on it. Moreover, the Teichmüller space
carries additional structure, namely, the Weil–Petersson (WP) symplectic two-form. Due to
a theorem by Wolpert
[
9
,
17
]
, WP-form for compact closed Riemann surfaces of genus
g

takes on a particularly simple form in terms of FN variables,

\omega_{\rm WP}=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{3g-3}\mathrm{d}\ell_{k}\wedge\mathrm{d}\tau_{k},

(16)

which is invariant with respect to any pants decomposition. Introducing
\theta_{k}=2\pi\tau_{k}/\ell_{k}
,
the simple Dehn twist
\theta_{k}\to\theta_{k}+2\pi
gives us isometrically the same surface.

Substituting the expressions for
\ell_{k}
and
\tau_{k}
into (
16
), WP-form becomes

\omega_{\rm WP}=\frac{8a}{(1-a^{2})(2a^{2}\cos^{2}{\tilde{\alpha}}-1)}\mathrm{d}a\wedge\mathrm{d}\tilde{\alpha}.

(17)

To verify the uniqueness of last formula, we can consider another pants decomposition.
In terms of our parameters, a new decomposition simply leads to replacement,

a\leftrightarrow b,\qquad\tilde{\alpha}\leftrightarrow-\tilde{\alpha},

(18)

in length and twist functions of previous decomposition. Although the set of new functions
is obtained, the resulting two-form
\omega_{\rm WP}
remains the same.

Thus, domain
{\cal A}
of admissible parameters
(a,\alpha)
is symplectic manifold

({\cal A},\omega_{\rm WP})
. We may also treat two-form (
17
) as an area
element of
{\cal A}
.

3.2
Isoperimetric Orbits

Since the hyperbolic area of admissible octagons is always equal to
4\pi
, a simplest
way to control the surface changes globally consists in consideration of octagon perimeter:

P=8~{\rm arccosh}~\frac{1-a^{2}b^{2}+\sqrt{(1-a^{2})^{2}+(1-b^{2})^{2}}}{(1-a^{2})(1-b^{2})}.

(19)

Although perimeter
P
is obviously invariant under automorphism and pants decomposition, it can also
take on the same value for different pairs
(a,\alpha)
. We are aiming to describe the corresponding orbits.
On the contrary to our case, two parameters of
flat
octagon with the same automorphism and
the homothety property are exactly defined by fixing its area and perimeter.

For further calculations it is useful to introduce two auxiliary quantities:

T\equiv\tanh{(P/16)},\qquad\varepsilon=\pm 1,

(20)

where the latter one reflects an existence of two sheets in
{\cal A}
labeled by

{\rm sign}~\tilde{\alpha}

For a given

\tilde{\alpha}=0

(21)

Solution to equation

3.3
Global Canonical Variables

Finding the canonical variables for isoperimetric orbits, let us define

Q(x,T)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\frac{1-T^{2}}{\sqrt{T^{2}-x_{-}}}\left[\frac{F(u,k)}{1-T^{2}}-\frac{\Pi(u,\nu_{1},k)}{1-x_{-}}+\frac{\Pi(u,\nu_{2},k)}{T^{2}-2x_{-}+1}\right],

(26)

where
F
and
\Pi
are elliptic integrals of the first and third kind, respectively.

Amplitude
u
, module
k
, and parameters
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
are given by

\displaystyle u=\sqrt{\frac{x-x_{-}}{x_{+}-x_{-}}},\qquad k=\sqrt{\frac{x_{+}-x_{-}}{T^{2}-x_{-}}},

\displaystyle\nu_{1}=\frac{x_{+}-x_{-}}{1-x_{-}},\qquad\nu_{2}=2\frac{x_{+}-x_{-}}{T^{2}-2x_{-}+1}.

(27)

Then, symplectic WP-form becomes

\omega_{\rm WP}=\varepsilon\mathrm{d}Q(x,T)\wedge\mathrm{d}P.

(28)

Integrating over
x
, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}A_{\rm WP}}{\mathrm{d}P}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\oint_{P={\rm const}}\varepsilon\mathrm{d}Q(x,T)=\int_{x_{-}}^{x_{+}}\mathrm{d}Q(x,T)-\int_{x_{+}}^{x_{-}}\mathrm{d}Q(x,T)

(29)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2Q(x_{+},T),

where amplitude
u=1
results in the complete elliptic integrals.

Defining an action variable (or “angular momentum”) as

J(P)=\frac{1}{4\pi}A_{\rm WP}(P),

(30)

we find the angle variable
\Phi
from equation
\omega_{\rm WP}=2\mathrm{d}\Phi\wedge\mathrm{d}J
;
the Poisson bracket is then
\{J,\Phi\}_{\rm WP}=1
. One first has that

\mathrm{d}\Phi=\frac{\pi\varepsilon}{Q(x_{+},T)}\mathrm{d}Q(x,T),\qquad\oint_{P={\rm const}}\mathrm{d}\Phi=2\pi,

(31)

where the rule of calculation of integrals containing
\varepsilon
(see (
29
)) is applied.

At fixed
P
, we come to expression for the angle variable:

\Phi=\pi\varepsilon\frac{Q(x,T)}{Q(x_{+},T)},\quad\Phi\in[-\pi,\pi].

(32)

Now it seems trivially to quantize the system in terms of
J
and
\Phi
what leads
immediately to estimation (in the Planck units) for relatively large
n

A_{\rm WP}\sim 4\pi n,\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(33)

We specify this formula below due to consideration of the relativity theory.

4
Relativistic Kinematics

At this stage, geometrodynamics of Riemann surface within the considered model is
ambiguous because of purely gauge nature. There are only the geometric constraints
defining the “physical sector” of parameters variety and no definitions of time and
the Hamiltonian function having the physical meaning and generating an evolution of

J
and
\Phi
. However, we appeal here to (2+1)-dimensional gravity
where
{\rm SO}(2,1)\sim{\rm SU}(1,1)
plays a role of the Lorentz group. It allows
us to construct a dynamical model with the same symmetry as follows.

Combining
J
and
\Phi
, we extend the set of observables up to

J_{0}=J,\quad J_{\pm}=\sqrt{J^{2}-C}\exp{(\mp\mathrm{i}\Phi)},

(34)

where
C
is a constant such that
J^{2}\geqslant C\geqslant 0
.

The Poisson algebra of new variables is
\mathfrak{su}(1,1)
Lie algebra:

\{J_{+},J_{-}\}_{\rm WP}=2\mathrm{i}J_{0},\qquad\{J_{\pm},J_{0}\}_{\rm WP}=\pm\mathrm{i}J_{\pm}.

(35)

More generally, generators
J_{0,\pm}
may be replaced by an infinite
number of quantities
L_{n}=J\exp{(\mathrm{i}n\Phi)}
,
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
generating the Witt algebra.

Since the evolution of conservative system is usually described by canonical transformations,
we find that indeed SU(1,1) transformations are canonical transformations of a given system.
Let us introduce the matrix

{\cal M}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}J_{0}&J_{+}\\
J_{-}&J_{0}\end{array}\right),

(36)

whose determinant
C\equiv J^{2}_{0}-J_{+}J_{-}
is the Casimir of
\mathfrak{su}(1,1)
algebra.

Action of matrix
U\in{\rm SU}(1,1)
looks like
{\cal M}\mapsto\tilde{\cal M}=U{\cal M}U^{\dagger}

and preserves the Casimir,
\det\tilde{\cal M}=C
.

5
Conclusions

Here, we pay great attention to the structure of admissible parameters space
{\cal A}

determining the geometry of the Riemann surface in genus two with an order four automorphism.
First, using the Weil–Petersson (WP) geometry, two-dimensional space
{\cal A}
is equipped
with the fundamental symplectic two-form. Further, we perform the dense covering of
{\cal A}

by the orbits generated by the isoperimetric constraint which is imposed on fundamental domain
of the surface. It is argued that an existence of these orbits is due to the Riemann surface
definition. The canonically conjugate action–angle variables for isoperimetric orbits are
found by identifying WP-area of domain bounded by the orbit with the action variable.
As the result, we take on a possibility to construct relativistic model in terms of special
invariants without knowing the Riemannian metric of
{\cal A}
.

To build the physically meaningful model, we extend the set of global canonical variables up
to generators of
\mathfrak{su}(1,1)
algebra. This trick leads to appearance of the
Casimir playing a role of additional parameter. We can only assume that its value should be
minimal and non-zero. However, it leads after quantization to non-vanishing discrete spectrum
of WP-area in a contrast with initial theory where WP-area becomes zero for the Riemann
surface associated with the regular hyperbolic octagon. In any case, we should remember that
WP-area determines the surface geometry up to diffeomorphism in classical theory.
We may need to use the infinite Witt and Virasoro algebras instead of
\mathfrak{su}(1,1)

in order to describe the system spectrum.

We also consider relativistic kinematics or free geometrodynamics of the Riemann surface,
generated by the Lorentz boost which acts on the constructed generators of
\mathfrak{su}(1,1)

algebra. The time dependence of global variables leads to “big bounce” scenario and is
similar for quantities of different origin. However, solving equations with respect to
the surface parameters, we have obtained the trajectory (independent on time definition) in
space
{\cal A}
. In this picture, the system does not tend to reach the regular octagon
configuration corresponding to the maximal information entropy

and preserves some
kind of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry related to admissibility range of angle variable during whole

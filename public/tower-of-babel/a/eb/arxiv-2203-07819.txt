The generalized X-join of Cayley graphs
Allen Herman, Javad Bagherian, Hanieh Memarzadeh

Abstract
As a main result of this paper we give conditions under which the generalized X X -join of Cayley graphs is a Cayley graph. In particular, we show that X X -join of isomorphic Cayley graphs is a Cayley grpah. To do this, new properties for a generalized wreath product of permutation groups are given in the case where the base group acts regularly. These are used to give conditions for the generalized wreath product to contain a regular subgroup, which are then applied to generalized X X -joins of Cayley graphs. Along the way, it is shown that the generalized X X -join of isomorphic Cayley graphs will always be a vertex transitive graph.



Key words:

X
-join; Lexicographic product; Cayley graphs; Generalized wreath product.

Primary: 05C25; Secondary: 05C50.

Introduction

In this article we investigate properties of the generalized
X
-join of graphs introduced in
[
3
]
. This graph product generalizes the
X
-join of graphs originally introduced by Sabidussi and Hemminger in
[
16
]
and
[
10
]
. Given a graph
G
with vertex set
\{1,\dots,n\}
, and
n
graphs
H_{1},\dots,H_{n}
, the
G
-join

G[H_{1},\dots,H_{n}]
is the graph that extends the disjoint union of the graphs
H_{1},\dots,H_{n}
by adding edges between all vertices of
H_{i}
and
H_{j}
when
(i,j)
is an edge in
G
for
1\leq i,j\leq n
. (This construction does not require the graphs to be ordinary, nor finite, and applies equally well to looped graphs or digraphs.) This was treated for ordinary graphs under the name generalized composition by Schwenk
[
17
]
, where he showed that if the
H_{i}
are regular graphs, then the collection
\{H_{1},\dots,H_{n}\}
gives an equitable partition of
G[H_{1},\dots,H_{n}].
One recovers the lexicographic product
G\circ H
in the case where all of the graphs
H_{i}
are copies of the same graph
H
. Recently some articles have appeared establishing new properties for generalized compositions of graphs (see, for instance,
[
4
]
,
[
13
]
) and studying their applications in hierarchical and computer networks (see
[
1
]
and
[
8
]
).

The definition of the generalized
X
-join
G\circ_{\lambda}\{H_{1},\dots,H_{n}\}
we investigate here is a modification of the one for generalized composition, the latter corresponding to the case where the elements of the defining partition of
G
into
n
disjoint subsets are all singletons (see
[
1
, Definition 2]
). The definition is quite flexible compared to the lexicographic product or the
X
-join, as it applies to many other graphs. In
[
3
]
, the last two authors considered the question of how to recognize a graph as a generalized
X
-join of graphs. They showed that the automorphism group of
G\circ_{\lambda}\{H_{1},\dots,H_{n}\}
contains the generalized wreath product
\aut(G)\circ_{\lambda}\{\aut(H_{1}),\dots,\aut(H_{n})\}
as a subgroup, and gave conditions for these to be equal. (For the definition of the generalized wreath product of groups, see
[
2
]
,
[
7
]
.) Their result generalizes several earlier results for the automorphism group of lexicographic products
[
6
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
, and the
X
-join
[
10
]
.

Graphs that are the direct, lexicographic, wreathed, or generalized wreathed product of Cayley graphs have been shown to be a Cayley graph (see
[
5
]
,
[
9
]
, and
[
11
]
), but we do not know if this will hold for the generalized
X
-join of Cayley graphs.
Note that it is also unknown for
X
-join of Cayley graphs.
The main result of this article gives conditions for the generalized
X
-join of Cayley graphs to be a Cayley graph.
In particular, it shows that the
X
-join of isomorphic Cayley graphs is a Cayley graph.

In Section 2 we review the definitions of the generalized
X
-join of graphs and generalized wreath product of groups from
[
3
]
and explore conditions where a generalized wreath product of groups contains a regular subgroup in a natural way. In Section 3 we study properties of the generalized
X
-join of Cayley graphs. When
W
is the generalized
X
-join of Cayley graphs defined over the groups
A
and
C_{X}
for
X\in\Sigma
, we find a natural condition under which the generalized wreath product
A\circ_{\lambda}\{C_{X}\}_{X\in\Sigma}
will be a subgroup of
\aut(W)
that acts transitively on vertices. We then give conditions for the generalized
X
-join of Cayley graphs to be a Cayley graph, and conclude with two examples.

We remark that, at least in spirit, the generalized
X
-join construction discussed here is already being applied in the “lifting Hamilton cycles” attacks on Lovaczs’ conjecture that every finite Cayley graph should have a Hamilton cycle (see
[
12
, pg. 5493]
). Indeed, it is relatively easy to see that a graph which is a generalized
X
-join of graphs that have Hamilton cycles will have a Hamilton cycle itself.

Preliminaries

Throughout this paper,
G=(V,E)
will denote an ordinary graph; i.e. a finite undirected graph with no multiple edges whose vertex set is
V=V(G)
and edge set is
E=E(G)
. If all pairs of vertices of a subgraph
H
of
G

that are adjacent in
G
are also adjacent in
H
, then
H
is an induced subgraph. For

we write
G[X]
for the subgraph of

induced by
X
.

For two graphs
G=(V,E)
and
H=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
, a graph homomorphism

is a mapping

such that
(f(u),f(v))\in E^{\prime}
whenever
(u,v)\in E
. If
f:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
is a bijection and

is also a graph homomorphism, then

is called a graph isomorphism, and we say that
G
and
H
are isomorphic.

For a finite set
V
, the group of all permutations of
V
is denoted by
\sym(V)
. The automorphism group of a graph
G=(V,E)
is the subgroup
\aut(G)
of
\sym(V)
consisting of the permutations of

that induce automorphisms of the graph
G
. Every subgroup of
\sym(V)
is called a permutation group on
V
. For

and
X\subseteq V
, the setwise stabilizer of
X
in
F
is
\{f\in F:X^{f}=X\}
and the pointwise stabilizer of
X
in
F
is
F_{(X)}=\{f\in F:x^{f}=x,\,\forall x\in X\}.
By a system of blocks
\Sigma
for a permutation group
F\leq\sym(\Omega)
we mean

(1)

\Sigma
is a partition of
\Omega
; and

(2)

for every
X\in\Sigma
and every
f\in F
,
X^{f}\cap X=\varnothing
or
X^{f}=X.

If
\Sigma
is a system of blocks for
F
and
X\in\Sigma
, we write
F^{X}
for the setwise stabilizer of
X
in
F
, viewed as a permutation group on
X
. For other terminology and notation not defined here, see
[
3
]
.

2.2 Generalized wreath products with a regular base group

Here we review the generalized wreath product construction from
[
2
]
in the special case where the base group
F
acts regularly. Up to permutation isomorphism we can assume
F
acts regularly on itself by right multiplication. If
\Sigma
is a system of blocks for this action, then for a fixed
X\in\Sigma
and
x_{0}\in X
, we will have that
H=\{f\in F:x_{0}f\in X\}
is a subgroup of
F
, and the map
x_{0}f\mapsto f
for
f\in H
gives a bijection from
X
onto
H
. For all
X^{\prime}\in\Sigma
, we can choose an
f_{X^{\prime}}\in F
with
X^{\prime}=Xf_{X^{\prime}}
, and set
f_{X}=1_{F}
for convenience. Let
\mathcal{S}=\{f_{X^{\prime}}:X^{\prime}\in\Sigma\}
be this fixed set of right coset representatives for
H
in
F
. With these choices, the setwise stabilizer
F^{X^{\prime}}=\{f\in F:X^{\prime}f=X^{\prime}\}
can be identified with the conjugate subgroup
H^{f_{X^{\prime}}}
for all
X^{\prime}\in\Sigma
, and so conjugation by
f_{X^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}
defines an isomorphism
\mu_{XX^{\prime}}:F^{X}\rightarrow F^{X^{\prime}}
. Also, for each
X^{\prime}\in\Sigma
, right multiplication by
f_{X^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}
defines a bijection
\varepsilon_{XX^{\prime}}:X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
. Given any two elements
X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}\in\Sigma
, we let
\mu_{X^{\prime}X^{\prime\prime}}:F^{X^{\prime}}\rightarrow F^{X^{\prime\prime}}
be conjugation by
f_{X^{\prime}}^{-1}f_{X^{\prime\prime}}
and
\varepsilon_{X^{\prime}X^{\prime\prime}}:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X^{\prime\prime}
to be equal to right multiplication by
f_{X^{\prime}}^{-1}f_{X^{\prime\prime}}
.

Suppose
\{M_{X^{\prime}}:X^{\prime}\in\Sigma\}
is a set of pairwise isomorphic groups indexed by
\Sigma
. For our fixed
X\in\Sigma
, let
\theta_{X}:M_{X}\rightarrow H
be a fixed group epimorphism with kernel
K_{X}
. Since
\theta_{X}:M_{X}\rightarrow H
is an epimorphism, for all
h\in H
we can choose
t_{h}\in M_{X}
such that
\theta_{X}(t_{h})=h
, and set
t_{1_{F}}=1_{M_{X}}
. The set
T_{H}=\{t_{h}:h\in H\}
will be a set of right coset representatives for
K_{X}
in
M_{X}
. For each
X^{\prime}\in\Sigma
, we fix a group isomorphism
\psi_{XX^{\prime}}:M_{X}\rightarrow M_{X^{\prime}}
. Then we can define a group epimorphism
\theta_{X^{\prime}}:M_{X^{\prime}}\rightarrow F^{X^{\prime}}=H^{f_{X^{\prime}}}
by setting
\theta_{X^{\prime}}(m^{\prime})=\mu_{XX^{\prime}}(\theta_{X}(\psi_{XX^{\prime}}^{-1}(m^{\prime})))
for all
m^{\prime}\in M_{X^{\prime}}
. Then these maps have the property that for all
m\in M_{X}
,

\theta_{X^{\prime}}(\psi_{XX^{\prime}}(m))=\mu_{XX^{\prime}}(\theta_{X}(m)).

Given any pair
X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}\in\Sigma
, the map
\psi_{X^{\prime}X^{\prime\prime}}=\psi_{XX^{\prime\prime}}\circ\psi_{XX^{\prime}}^{-1}
will satisfy

\theta_{X^{\prime\prime}}(\psi_{X^{\prime}X^{\prime\prime}}(m^{\prime}))=\mu_{X^{\prime}X^{\prime\prime}}(\theta_{X^{\prime}}(m^{\prime})),\quad\mbox{ for all }m^{\prime}\in M_{X^{\prime}}.

For each
X^{\prime}\in\Sigma
, let
K_{X^{\prime}}
be the kernel of
\theta_{X^{\prime}}
, so
M_{X^{\prime}}/K_{X^{\prime}}\simeq F^{X^{\prime}}
via the canonical isomorphism.

Let
Y_{X}=M_{X}
as sets, and let
M_{X}
act on
Y_{X}
by right multiplication. Let
\lambda_{X}:Y_{X}\rightarrow X
be defined by
\lambda_{X}(y)=\theta_{X}(y)
for all
y\in Y_{X}
. This map has the property that for all
y\in Y_{X}
and
m\in M_{X}
,
\lambda_{X}(ym)=\lambda_{X}(y)\theta_{X}(m)
. For each
X^{\prime}=Hf_{X^{\prime}}
, let
Y_{X^{\prime}}
be a copy of
Y_{X}
. Let
\phi_{XX^{\prime}}:Y_{X}\rightarrow Y_{X^{\prime}}
be a fixed bijection. Then there is a unique surjection
\lambda_{X^{\prime}}:Y_{X^{\prime}}\rightarrow X^{\prime}=Hf_{X^{\prime}}
satisfying

\lambda_{X^{\prime}}(\phi_{XX^{\prime}}(y))=\varepsilon_{XX^{\prime}}(\lambda_{X}(y)),\mbox{ for all }y\in Y_{X}.

We can define an action of
M_{X^{\prime}}
on
Y_{X^{\prime}}
such that for all
y\in Y_{X}
and
m\in M_{X}
,

\phi_{XX^{\prime}}(y)\psi_{XX^{\prime}}(m)=\phi_{XX^{\prime}}(ym).

For every pair
X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}\in\Sigma
, we set
\phi_{X^{\prime}X^{\prime\prime}}:Y_{X^{\prime}}\rightarrow Y_{X^{\prime\prime}}
to be the bijection
\phi_{X^{\prime}X^{\prime\prime}}=\phi_{XX^{\prime\prime}}\circ\phi_{XX^{\prime}}^{-1}
. These bijections satisfy the compatibility relation
\lambda_{X^{\prime\prime}}(\phi_{X^{\prime}X^{\prime\prime}}(y^{\prime}))=\varepsilon_{X^{\prime}X^{\prime\prime}}(\lambda_{X^{\prime}}(y^{\prime}))
for all
y^{\prime}\in Y_{X^{\prime}}
, and furthermore, for all
y^{\prime}\in Y_{X^{\prime}}
and
m^{\prime}\in M_{X^{\prime}}
,
\phi_{X^{\prime}X^{\prime\prime}}(y^{\prime})\psi_{X^{\prime}X^{\prime\prime}}(m^{\prime})=\phi_{X^{\prime}X^{\prime\prime}}(y^{\prime}m^{\prime}).

Now, let
Y=\displaystyle{\bigcup_{X^{\prime}\in\Sigma}}Y_{X^{\prime}}
, and let
K=\prod_{X^{\prime}\in\Sigma}K_{X^{\prime}}
. We view
K
as a subgroup of
Sym(Y)
in the following way: if
k=\prod_{X^{\prime}}k_{X^{\prime}}
for some independent choices
k_{X^{\prime}}\in K_{X^{\prime}}
for
X^{\prime}\in\Sigma
, then
y\cdot k=yk_{X^{\prime}}
whenever
y\in Y
belongs to
Y_{X^{\prime}}
. As a subgroup of
Sym(Y)
,
K
contains the natural
diagonal
subgroup
J=\{\prod_{X^{\prime}\in\Sigma}\psi_{XX^{\prime}}(\ell):\ell\in K_{X}\}
. For
\ell\in K_{X}
, we let
\hat{\ell}=\prod_{X^{\prime}}\psi_{XX^{\prime}}(\ell)\in J
.

For each
f\in F
, we associate
f
to a permutation
\overline{f}\in Sym(Y)
that lifts right multiplication by
f
as a map from
F
to
F
. Given
y\in Y_{X^{\prime}}\subset Y
, either
X^{\prime}f=X^{\prime}
or
X^{\prime}f=X^{\prime\prime}\neq X^{\prime}
. If
X^{\prime}f=X^{\prime}
, then
f_{X^{\prime}}f=hf_{X^{\prime}}
for some
h\in H
. In this case

is defined to be
y\overline{f}=y\psi_{XX^{\prime}}(t_{h})
. If
X^{\prime}f=X^{\prime\prime}\neq X^{\prime}
, then
f_{X^{\prime}}f=hf_{X^{\prime\prime}}
for some
h\in H
, so
f=h^{f_{X^{\prime}}}f_{X^{\prime}}^{-1}f_{X^{\prime\prime}}
. So in this case we define
y\overline{f}=\phi_{X^{\prime}X^{\prime\prime}}(y\psi_{XX^{\prime}}(t_{h}))
.
(This definition is equivalent to that of
[
2
]
in the case where the base group
F
has a regular action.) We remark that for each
f\in F
, the definition of
\bar{f}
is dependent upon the choice of coset representatives for the elements of
T_{H}
, so in fact we have a precise definition of
\bar{f}
only up to composition by an element of
K
. Intuitively, think of
Y_{X^{\prime}}
as
Y_{X}\overline{f_{X^{\prime}}}
, and think of right multiplication by
\overline{f_{X^{\prime}}}
for
f_{X^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}
as the map
\phi_{XX^{\prime}}
, since this lifts right multiplication
H\rightarrow Hf_{X^{\prime}}
to a map
Y_{X}\rightarrow Y_{X^{\prime}}
. When
f_{X^{\prime}}f=hf_{X^{\prime\prime}}
for
X^{\prime\prime}\in\Sigma
, then the restriction of
\overline{f}
to
Y_{X^{\prime}}
will take the same image on
Y_{X^{\prime}}
as
\overline{f_{X^{\prime}}}^{-1}\overline{h}\,\overline{f_{X^{\prime\prime}}}
. We can also intuitively think of
M_{X^{\prime}}
as the conjugate
(M_{X})^{\overline{f_{X^{\prime}}}}
, so that the identity
\phi_{XX^{\prime}}(y)\psi_{XX^{\prime}}(m)=\phi_{XX^{\prime}}(ym)
for

and

becomes
(y\overline{f_{X^{\prime}}})(m^{\overline{f_{X^{\prime}}}})=(ym)\overline{f_{X^{\prime}}}
.

Let
\overline{F}=\{\overline{f}:f\in F\}
.
\overline{F}
is a subset of
Sym(Y)
, which is a subgroup only under a strict condition.

Lemma 2.3
.

The following are equivalent:

(i)

The map
f\mapsto\overline{f}
, for all
f\in F
, is a group isomorphism
F\simeq\overline{F}
.

(ii)

T_{H}
is a group.

(iii)

M_{X}=K_{X}\rtimes T_{H}\simeq K_{X}\rtimes H
.

Proof.

For
f_{1},f_{2}\in F
, if
y^{\prime}\in Y_{X^{\prime}}\subset Y
is equal to
y\overline{f_{X^{\prime}}}
for some
y\in Y_{X}
, then there exist
h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime}\in H
and
f_{X^{\prime\prime}},f_{X^{\prime\prime\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}
such that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}y^{\prime}\cdot\overline{f_{1}}\cdot\overline{f_{2}}&=&(yt_{h^{\prime}})\overline{f_{X^{\prime\prime}}}\cdot\overline{f_{2}}\mbox{ (if $f_{X^{\prime}}f_{1}=h^{\prime}f_{X^{\prime\prime}}$)}\\
&=&(yt_{h^{\prime}}t_{h^{\prime\prime}})\overline{f_{X^{\prime\prime\prime}}}\mbox{ ( if $f_{X^{\prime\prime}}f_{2}=h^{\prime\prime}f_{X^{\prime\prime\prime}}$).}\end{array}

On the other hand,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}y^{\prime}\cdot\overline{f_{1}f_{2}}=yt_{h^{\prime}h^{\prime\prime}}\overline{f_{X^{\prime\prime\prime}}},\mbox{ since $f_{X^{\prime}}f_{1}f_{2}=h^{\prime}h^{\prime\prime}f_{X^{\prime\prime\prime}}$.}\end{array}

From this one can see that
\overline{f_{1}}\,\overline{f_{2}}=\overline{f_{1}f_{2}}
for all
f_{1},f_{2}\in F
if and only if
t_{h^{\prime}}t_{h"}t_{h^{\prime}h"}^{-1}=1
for all
h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime}\in H
. The latter is equivalent to
T_{H}
being a group isomorphic to
H
, and this is equivalent to
M_{X}
being a split extension of
K_{X}
by
T_{H}
.

In the notation of the above proof, we can see that the obstruction to

being a group in general is:

Proposition 2.4
.

(i)

For all
f\in F
, for all

, there exists an

such that

.

(ii)

F\circ_{\lambda}\{M_{X^{\prime}}\}_{X^{\prime}\in\Sigma}
acts transitively on

.

Proof.

Let
f\in F
. For all
y^{\prime}\in Y_{X^{\prime}}
, if
y^{\prime}=\phi_{XX^{\prime}}(y)
then

, where

is the unique element of

, we have

, so
Y_{X^{\prime}}\bar{f}=Y_{X}\overline{f_{X^{\prime\prime}}}=\phi_{XX^{\prime\prime}}(Y_{X})=Y_{X^{\prime\prime}}

Let

. It suffices to show that for all

, there is a

.
∎

We are interested in cases where the elements used for

acts regularly on
Y
. In order to do so, it must be as small as possible. The next result gives a sufficient condition for
|\langle J,\overline{F}\rangle|=|K_{X}||F|=|Y|
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
J,\overline{F}\subset F\circ_{\lambda}\{M_{X^{\prime}}\}_{X\in\Sigma}\leq Sym(Y)
be as above.
If
F=HC_{F}(H)
and
M_{X}=K_{X}C_{M_{X}}(K_{X})
, and either
F=H\times F_{1}
for some subgroup
F_{1}
of
F
or
M_{X}\simeq K_{X}\times H
then
J\overline{F}=\overline{F}J
is a subgroup of
Sym(Y)
of order
|J||F|
that acts regularly on
Y
.

Proof.

Assume suitable choices have been made defining the elements of
\bar{F}
. From our observation preceding the statement of the theorem, it suffices to show
(i)
that
(\overline{F})^{2}\subseteq J\overline{F}
, and
(ii)
that the set
\overline{F}
normalizes
J
, as this will show
J\overline{F}=\overline{F}J
will be a group of the correct order.

(i)

First suppose
M_{X}\simeq K_{X}\times H
. Since this implies the elements of
T_{H}
can be chosen to form a group, it follows that the elements of
\overline{F}
can be chosen to form a group, and so (i) will hold. So assume
F=H\times F_{1}
for some subgroup
F_{1}\subseteq C_{F}(H)
,
M_{X}=K_{X}C_{M_{X}}(K_{X})
, and that
M_{X}
is not isomorphic to
K_{X}\times H
. In this case
\overline{F}
may not be a group, so recall the calculation of the obstruction. Given
f_{1},f_{2}\in F
, then for
y^{\prime}=y\overline{f_{X^{\prime}}}\in Y_{X^{\prime}}\subset Y
with
y\in Y_{X}
,

y^{\prime}\overline{f_{1}}\,\overline{f_{2}}\,(\overline{f_{1}f_{2}})^{-1}=y^{\prime}(\overline{f_{X^{\prime}}})^{-1}\,\overline{h^{\prime}}\,\overline{h^{\prime\prime}}\,(\overline{h^{\prime}h^{\prime\prime}})^{-1}\overline{f_{X^{\prime}}},

where
f_{X^{\prime}}f_{1}=h^{\prime}f_{X^{\prime\prime}}
and
f_{X^{\prime\prime}}f_{2}=h^{\prime\prime}f_{X^{\prime\prime\prime}}
for
h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime}\in H
and
f_{X^{\prime\prime}},f_{X^{\prime\prime\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}
.
In order for the map on the right to lie in
J
, it must be equal to an
\hat{\ell}
for some
\ell\in K_{X}
, and this means it cannot depend on the choice of
X^{\prime}
. However, the choice of
h^{\prime}
and subsequently
h^{\prime\prime}
will depend on the choice of
X^{\prime}
, unless we are in the case where the choices of
h^{\prime}
and
h^{\prime\prime}
are not affected by
f_{X^{\prime}}
. This requires
\mathcal{S}
to be a subgroup of
F
that centralizes
H
, since then

f_{X^{\prime}}f_{1}f_{2}=h^{\prime}f_{X^{\prime\prime}}f_{2}=h^{\prime}h^{\prime\prime}f_{X^{\prime\prime\prime}}
and the elements
h^{\prime}
and
h^{\prime\prime}
that appear do not depend on
f_{X^{\prime}}
. So in the case
M_{X}=K_{X}C_{M_{X}}(K_{X})
and
F=H\times F_{1}
for some subgroup
F_{1}\subseteq C_{F}(H)
, (i) will hold when we choose
\mathcal{S}=F_{1}
.

(ii)

When
\mathcal{S}
centralizes
H
, then for all
h\in H
and all
y^{\prime}\in Y_{X^{\prime}}
with
y^{\prime}=y\overline{f_{X^{\prime}}}
for some
y\in Y_{X}
, we have
f_{X^{\prime}}h=hf_{X^{\prime}}
, so

y^{\prime}\cdot\overline{h}=y\overline{f_{X^{\prime}}}\,\overline{h}=y\overline{h}\,\overline{f_{X^{\prime}}}

=(yt_{h})\overline{f_{X^{\prime}}}=y\overline{f_{X^{\prime}}}\psi_{XX^{\prime}}(t_{h})=y^{\prime}\psi_{XX^{\prime}}(t_{h}).

(When
f_{X^{\prime}}
and
h
do not commute,
y^{\prime}\overline{h}
will not be equal to
y^{\prime}\psi_{XX^{\prime}}(t_{h})
.)

Similarly, the assumption
M_{X}=K_{X}C_{M_{X}}(K_{X})
implies that we can choose the set of coset representatives
T_{H}
for
K_{X}
in
M_{X}
with
t_{h}\in C_{M_{X}}(K_{X})
for all
h\in H
.

Let
k\in K_{X}
, and let
\hat{k}=\prod_{X^{\prime}}\psi_{XX^{\prime}}(k)
be the corresponding element of
J
. Let
f\in F
, with
f=hf_{X^{\prime\prime}}
for
h\in H
and
f_{X^{\prime\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}
. Let
y^{\prime}\in Y
and assume
y^{\prime}=\phi_{XX^{\prime}}(y)\in Y_{X^{\prime}}
for some
y\in Y_{X}
. Let
h^{\prime}\in H
and
f_{X^{\prime\prime\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}
be such that
f_{X^{\prime}}f=f_{X^{\prime}}hf_{X^{\prime\prime}}=hh^{\prime}f_{X^{\prime\prime\prime}}
. Then

\begin{array}[]{rcl}y^{\prime}\cdot\hat{k}\cdot\overline{f}&=&\phi_{XX^{\prime}}(y)\hat{k}\cdot\overline{f}\\
&=&\phi_{XX^{\prime}}(y)\psi_{XX^{\prime}}(k)\overline{f}\\
&=&\phi_{XX^{\prime}}(yk)\overline{f}\\
&=&(yk)\overline{f_{X^{\prime}}}\,\overline{f}\\
&=&(yk)\overline{hh^{\prime}}\,\overline{f_{X^{\prime\prime\prime}}}\quad\mbox{ (since $f_{X^{\prime}}f=hh^{\prime}f_{X^{\prime\prime\prime}}$)}\\
&=&(ykt_{hh^{\prime}})\overline{f_{X^{\prime\prime\prime}}}\\
&=&\phi_{XX^{\prime\prime\prime}}(ykt_{hh^{\prime}})\\
&=&\phi_{XX^{\prime\prime\prime}}(yt_{hh^{\prime}}k)\quad\mbox{ (since $t_{hh^{\prime}}$ commutes with $k$)}\\
&=&\phi_{XX^{\prime\prime\prime}}(yt_{hh^{\prime}})\psi_{XX^{\prime\prime\prime}}(k)\\
&=&(yt_{hh^{\prime}})\overline{f_{X^{\prime\prime\prime}}}\hat{k}\\
&=&y\overline{hh^{\prime}}\,\overline{f_{X^{\prime\prime\prime}}}\hat{k}\\
&=&y^{\prime}\cdot\overline{f}\hat{k}.\end{array}

Therefore, when
\mathcal{S}\subseteq C_{F}(H)
and
T_{H}\subseteq C_{M_{X}}(K_{X})
,
\overline{F}
normalizes
J
. This implies
J\overline{F}=\overline{F}J
as subsets of
Sym(Y)
, so (ii) holds. The proof of the theorem is complete.

∎

3
The generalized
X
-join of Cayley graphs

Recall the definition of a Cayley graph of a finite group with respect to a symmetric set.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
A
be a finite group and let
S
be a subset of
A
that is closed under inverses and does not contain the identity. Then the Cayley graph
Cay(A,S)
is the graph with vertex set
A
and edge set
E(Cay(A,S))=\{(a,b):ab^{-1}\in S\}.

Let
G=Cay(A,S_{A})
be a Cayley graph, with vertices identified with elements of
A
and
(a,b)\in E(G)\iff ab^{-1}\in S_{A}
. Then
A
acts regularly on
G
, with right multiplications by elements of
A
correspond to graph automorphisms of
G
. It is well known that a Cayley graph is vertex-transitive, but the converse need not be true. A graph
G
is isomorphic to a Cayley graph
Cay(A,S)
if and only if
\aut(G)
contains a subgroup
A_{0}
that is regular as a permutation group on
V(G).
In this case
A=A_{0}
. See
[
9
, section 10]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
G=Cay(A,S_{A})
be a Cayley graph and suppose
\Sigma
is a system of blocks for the action of
A
by right multiplication on itself. For a fixed
X_{1}\in\Sigma
, let
H=\{h\in A:X_{1}h=X_{1}\}
and let
\{a_{X}:X\in\Sigma\}
be a set of right coset representatives for
H
in
A
.
Suppose that
\{B_{X}=Cay(C_{X},S_{X})\}_{X\in\Sigma}
is a set of pairwise isomorphic Cayley graphs, and assume
\theta_{X_{1}}:C_{X_{1}}\rightarrow H
is a group epimorphism whose values agree with those of a graph epimorphism
\lambda_{X_{1}}:B_{X_{1}}\rightarrow G[X_{1}]
. Let
Y=\bigcup_{X\in\Sigma}Y_{X}
, where
Y_{X}=Y_{X_{1}}\overline{a_{X}}
is a copy of
B_{X_{1}}
for which
(y\mapsto y\overline{a_{X}})
,
y\in Y_{X_{1}}
, is a graph isomorphism. For all
X\in\Sigma
, let
\lambda_{X}:Y_{X}\rightarrow Ha_{X}
be the graph epimorphism given by
\lambda_{X}(y\overline{a_{X}})=\lambda_{X_{1}}(y)a_{X}
, for all
y\in Y_{X_{1}}
. Let
\lambda=\{\lambda_{X}\}_{X\in\Sigma}
be this family of graph epimorphisms.
Then the generalized wreath product
A\circ_{\lambda}\{C_{X}\}_{X\in\Sigma}
is a subgroup of the automorphism group of the generalized
X
-join graph
W=G\circ_{\lambda}\{B_{X}\}_{X\in\Sigma}
.

Proof.

By the above argument we can consider
A\circ_{\lambda}\{C_{X}\}_{X\in\Sigma}=\langle K,\overline{A}\rangle
where
K=\prod_{X^{\prime}\in\Sigma}{K_{X^{\prime}}}
. We let
{k_{X^{\prime}}}\in K_{X^{\prime}}
for all
X^{\prime}\in\Sigma.

Suppose
(u^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime})\in E(W)
, with
u^{\prime}\in Y_{X^{\prime}}
and
v^{\prime\prime}\in Y_{X^{\prime\prime}}
for
X^{\prime},X^{\prime\prime}\in\Sigma
. Let
u,v\in Y_{X_{1}}
with
u^{\prime}=u\overline{a_{X^{\prime}}}
and
v^{\prime\prime}=v\overline{a_{X^{\prime\prime}}}
. Then

(u^{\prime}k,v^{\prime\prime}k)=(u^{\prime}k_{X^{\prime}},v^{\prime\prime}k_{X^{\prime\prime}})
. If
X^{\prime}\neq X^{\prime\prime}
, then

\begin{array}[]{rcl}(u^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime})\in E(W)&\iff&(\lambda_{X^{\prime}}(u^{\prime}),\lambda_{X^{\prime\prime}}(v^{\prime\prime}))\in E(G).\\
\end{array}

Since
K_{X^{\prime}}=\ker\theta_{X^{\prime}}
for all
X^{\prime}\in\Sigma
,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}(\lambda_{X^{\prime}}(u^{\prime}k),\lambda_{X^{\prime\prime}}(v^{\prime\prime}k))&=&(\lambda_{X^{\prime}}(u^{\prime}k_{X^{\prime}}),\lambda_{X^{\prime\prime}}(v^{\prime\prime}k_{X^{\prime\prime}}))\\
&=&(\lambda_{X^{\prime}}(u^{\prime})\theta_{X^{\prime}}(k_{X^{\prime}}),\lambda_{X^{\prime\prime}}(v^{\prime\prime})\theta_{X^{\prime\prime}}(k_{X^{\prime\prime}}))\\
&=&(\lambda_{X^{\prime}}(u^{\prime}),\lambda_{X^{\prime\prime}}(v^{\prime\prime})),\end{array}

so
(u^{\prime}k,v^{\prime\prime}k)\in E(W)
. If
X^{\prime}=X^{\prime\prime}
, then
(u^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime})=(u\overline{a_{X^{\prime}}},v\overline{a_{X^{\prime}}})=(\phi_{{X_{1}}X^{\prime}}(u),\phi_{{X_{1}}X^{\prime}}(v))
. So

\begin{array}[]{rcl}(u^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime})\in E(W)&\iff&(u^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime})\in E(B_{X^{\prime}})\\
&\iff&(\phi_{{X_{1}}X^{\prime}}(u),\phi_{{X_{1}}X^{\prime}}(v))\in E(B_{X^{\prime}})\\
&\iff&(u,v)\in E(B_{X_{1}})\quad\mbox{ (since $\phi_{{X_{1}}X^{\prime}}$ is a graph isomorphism).}\\
\end{array}

Now, we have that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}(u^{\prime}k,v^{\prime\prime}k)&=&(\phi_{{X_{1}}X^{\prime}}(u)k_{X^{\prime}},\phi_{{X_{1}}X^{\prime}}(v)k_{X^{\prime}}).\end{array}

Since
\phi_{{X_{1}}X^{\prime}}
and right multiplication by
k_{X^{\prime}}\in C_{X^{\prime}}
are graph isomorphisms,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}(u^{\prime}k,v^{\prime\prime}k)\in E(W)&\iff&(\phi_{{X_{1}}X^{\prime}}(u)k_{X^{\prime}},\phi_{{X_{1}}X^{\prime}}(v)k_{X^{\prime}})\in E(B_{X^{\prime}})\\
&\iff&(\phi_{{X_{1}}X^{\prime}}(u),\phi_{{X_{1}}X^{\prime}}(v))\in E(B_{X^{\prime}})\\
&\iff&(u,v)\in E(B_{X_{1}}),\\
\end{array}

thus
(u^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime})\in E(W)\iff(u^{\prime}k,v^{\prime\prime}k)\in E(W)
.

Next, let
\overline{a}\in\overline{A}
. Suppose
(u^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime})\in E(W)
, with
u^{\prime}\in Y_{X^{\prime}}
and
v^{\prime\prime}\in Y_{X^{\prime\prime}}
. Let
u,v\in Y_{X_{1}}
be such that
u^{\prime}=\phi_{{X_{1}}X^{\prime}}(u)
and
v^{\prime\prime}=\phi_{{X_{1}}X^{\prime\prime}}(v)
. If
X^{\prime}\neq X^{\prime\prime}
, then

\begin{array}[]{rcl}(u^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime})\in E(W)&\iff&(\lambda_{X^{\prime}}(u^{\prime}),\lambda_{X^{\prime\prime}}(v^{\prime\prime}))\in E(G)\\
&\iff&(\lambda_{X^{\prime}}(\phi_{{X_{1}}X^{\prime}}(u)),\lambda_{X^{\prime\prime}}(\phi_{{X_{1}}X^{\prime\prime}}(v))\in E(G)\\
&\iff&(\lambda_{{X_{1}}}(u)a_{X^{\prime}},\lambda_{{X_{1}}}(v)a_{X^{\prime\prime}})\in E(G).\\
\end{array}

Suppose
a_{X^{\prime}}a=h_{2}a_{X_{2}}
and
a_{X^{\prime\prime}}a=h_{3}a_{X_{3}}
for
h_{2},h_{3}\in H
and
a_{X_{2}},a_{X_{3}}\in\mathcal{S}
. Then

\begin{array}[]{rcl}(u^{\prime}\overline{a},v^{\prime\prime}\overline{a})&=&(u\overline{h_{2}}\overline{a_{X_{2}}},v\overline{h_{3}}\overline{a_{X_{3}}})\\
&=&(\phi_{{X_{1}}{X_{2}}}(ut_{h_{2}}),\phi_{{X_{1}}{X_{3}}}(vt_{h_{3}})).\end{array}

As Proposition
2.4
(i) implies
\bar{a}
will map distinct elements of
\{Y_{X}:X\in\Sigma\}
to distinct elements, we must have
X_{2}\neq X_{3}
, and so

\begin{array}[]{rcl}(u^{\prime}\overline{a},v^{\prime\prime}\overline{a})\in E(W)&\iff&(\phi_{{X_{1}}{X_{2}}}(ut_{h_{2}}),\phi_{{X_{1}}{X_{3}}}(vt_{h_{3}}))\in E(W)\\
&\iff&(\lambda_{X_{2}}(\phi_{{X_{1}}{X_{2}}}(ut_{h_{2}})),\lambda_{X_{3}}(\phi_{{X_{1}}{X_{3}}}(vt_{h_{3}})))\in E(G)\\
&\iff&(\lambda_{{X_{1}}}(ut_{h_{2}})a_{X_{2}},\lambda_{{X_{1}}}(vt_{h_{3}})a_{X_{3}})\in E(G)\\
&\iff&(\lambda_{{X_{1}}}(u)h_{2}a_{X_{2}},\lambda_{{X_{1}}}(v)h_{3}a_{X_{3}})\in E(G)\\
&\iff&(\lambda_{{X_{1}}}(u)a_{X^{\prime}}a,\lambda_{{X_{1}}}(v)a_{X^{\prime\prime}}a)\in E(G)\\
&\iff&(\lambda_{{X_{1}}}(u)a_{X^{\prime}},\lambda_{{X_{1}}}(v)a_{X^{\prime\prime}})\in E(G)\\
&\iff&(\lambda_{X^{\prime}}(u\overline{a_{X^{\prime}}}),\lambda_{X^{\prime\prime}}(v\overline{a_{X^{\prime\prime}}}))\in E(G)\\
&\iff&(\lambda_{X^{\prime}}(u^{\prime}),\lambda_{X^{\prime\prime}}(v^{\prime\prime}))\in E(G)\\
&\iff&(u^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime})\in E(W).\\
\end{array}

Therefore,
(u^{\prime}\overline{a},v^{\prime\prime}\overline{a})\in E(W)
when
X^{\prime}\neq X^{\prime\prime}
.

If
X^{\prime}=X^{\prime\prime}
, then continuing with the above notation,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}(u^{\prime}\overline{a},v^{\prime\prime}\overline{a})&=&(u\overline{h}\overline{a_{X_{2}}},v\overline{h}\overline{a_{X_{2}}})\\
&=&(\phi_{{X_{1}}{X_{2}}}(ut_{h}),\phi_{{X_{1}}{X_{2}}}(vt_{h})),\end{array}

then the fact that
\phi_{{X_{1}}{X_{2}}}
is a graph isomorphism means this is an edge in
W
if and only if
(ut_{h},vt_{h})\in E(B_{X_{1}})
. When
(u^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime})\in E(W)
, we have
(\phi_{{X_{1}}X^{\prime}}(u),\phi_{{X_{1}}X^{\prime}}(v))\in E(B_{X^{\prime}})\iff(u,v)\in E(B_{X_{1}})\iff(ut_{h},vt_{h})\in E(B_{X_{1}}).
So
(u^{\prime}\overline{a},v^{\prime\prime}\overline{a})\in E(W)
.

From the above we can conclude that
K
and
\overline{A}
are subsets of
\aut(W)
, so they generate a subgroup of
\aut(W)
. This proves the theorem.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Suppose that
G=Cay(A,S_{A})
is a Cayley graph,
\Sigma
is a system of blocks for the natural action of
A
on
G
, and
\{B_{X}=Cay(C_{X},S_{X})\}_{X\in\Sigma}
is a set of pairwise isomorphic Cayley graphs. Let
\theta_{X_{1}}:C_{X_{1}}\rightarrow A^{X_{1}}
be a group epimorphism agreeing with a graph epimorphism from
B_{X_{1}}
to
G[X_{1}]
that induces graph epimorphisms
\lambda_{X}:B_{X}\rightarrow X
for all
X\in\Sigma
.
Then the graph
G\circ_{\lambda}\{B_{X}\}_{X\in\Sigma}
is a vertex transitive graph.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.2
,
A\circ_{\lambda}\{C_{X}\}_{X\in\Sigma}
is a subgroup of
\aut W
.
As we can interpret the natural action of
A
on
V(G)
as right multiplication of
A
on itself, it follows from Proposition
2.4
(ii) that
A\circ_{\lambda}\{C_{X}\}_{X\in\Sigma}
is a subgroup of
\aut W
that acts transitively on
V(W)
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
W=G\circ_{\lambda}\{B_{X}\}_{X\in\Sigma}
be a generalized
X
-join of a Cayley graph
G=Cay(A,S_{A})

with a set of pairwise isomorphic Cayley graphs
\{B_{X}=Cay(C_{X},S_{X})\}_{X\in\Sigma}
.
Let
H
be the stabilizer in
A
of a fixed
X_{1}\in\Sigma
, and let
K_{X_{1}}
be the kernel of the group epimorphism
C_{X_{1}}\rightarrow H
.
Suppose
A=HC_{A}(H)
,
C_{X_{1}}=K_{X_{1}}C_{C_{X_{1}}}(K_{X_{1}})
, and either
A=H\times A_{1}
for some subgroup
A_{1}
of
A
, or
C_{X_{1}}\simeq K_{X_{1}}\rtimes H
. Then
W
is a Cayley graph.

Proof.

Let
J=\{\hat{k}:k\in K_{X}\}
and
\overline{A}
be as defined in Section 2. The assumptions here allow us to apply Theorem
2.5
, so we can conclude that
R=J\overline{A}
is a subgroup of
\aut W
that acts regularly on
V(W)
. Therefore,
W
is a Cayley graph on
R
.
∎

We now proceed to determine the connection set for the Cayley graph in Theorem
3.4
. Let
e
be the vertex in
Y_{X_{1}}
labelled by the identity element of
C_{X_{1}}
. Since
R
acts regularly on
W
, the set
Y=V(W)
is equal to
\{e\cdot r:r\in R\}
. For each
X\in\Sigma
, we have set bijections between
Y_{X}
,
C_{X}
, and
J\overline{H}\,\overline{a_{X}}
. So each vertex in
Y
is labelled by an element of
C_{X}
and as
e\cdot r
for some
r\in J\overline{H}\,\overline{a_{X}}
. For subsets
S
of
C_{X}
,
X\in\Sigma
, we denote by
{S}^{*}
the set of elements of
R
which correspond to elements of
S
under these bijections.

With the labelling by elements of
R
the connection set for this Cayley graph is determined by the elements
r\in R
for which
(e\cdot 1,e\cdot r)
is an edge in
W
. If
e\cdot r
is the label of a vertex in
Y_{X_{1}}
, then
r=\hat{\ell}\overline{h}
for
\ell\in K_{X}
and
h\in H
. If
(e\cdot 1,e\cdot r)\in E(B_{X_{1}})
, then
r=\hat{\ell}\overline{h}
corresponds in
C_{X_{1}}
to
\ell t_{h}\in S_{X_{1}}
. Therefore,
r\in[{S_{X_{1}}}]^{*}
.

If
r
represents the same vertex as an element of
Y_{X}
, for
X\neq X_{1}
, then
r=\hat{\ell}\overline{h}\,\overline{a_{X}}
for some
\ell\in K_{X}
and
h\in H
. The vertex of
Y_{X}
represented by
e\cdot r
for this

will be
\phi_{X_{1}X}(\ell t_{h})
. It follows that the image of this vertex under
\lambda_{X}
will be equal to
\lambda_{X_{1}}(\ell t_{h})a_{X}=ha_{X}
, and so

\begin{array}[]{rcl}(e\cdot 1,e\cdot r)\in E(W)&\iff&(\lambda_{X_{1}}(1_{C_{X_{1}}}),\lambda_{X}(\phi_{X_{1}X}(\ell t_{h})))\in E(G)\\
&\iff&(1_{A},ha_{X})\in E(G)\\
&\iff&ha_{X}\in S_{A}\cap Ha_{X}\\
&\iff&\phi_{X_{1}X}(\ell t_{h})\in\lambda_{X}^{-1}(S_{A}\cap Ha_{X}).\end{array}

Therefore,
r\in[{\lambda_{X}^{-1}(S_{A}\cap Ha_{X})}]^{*}
, and so we can conclude that
W=Cay(R,S_{R})
, where
R=J\overline{A}
and

S_{R}=[{S_{X_{1}}}]^{*}\cup\big(\bigcup_{X\neq X_{1}}[{\lambda_{X}^{-1}(S_{A}\cap Ha_{X})}]^{*}\big).

Example 3.6
.

Let
A=C_{2}\times C_{2}\times C_{2}=\langle a\rangle\times\langle b\rangle\times\langle c\rangle

G

H=\langle a\rangle\times\langle b\rangle
, and let
\Sigma=\{X=H,X^{\prime}=Hc\}
. Then
G[X]=Cay(H,\{a,b\})
is graph isomorphic to

via the map
\varepsilon_{XX^{\prime}}
that is defined by right multiplication by
c
.

Let

\theta_{X}:C_{X}\rightarrow H
given by
\theta_{X}(i)=a

. Define

to be an isomorphic copy of

, and let

. Then we have a generalized
X
-join of Cayley graphs

.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
G=Cay(A,S_{A})
be a Cayley graph and
\{B_{x}=Cay(C_{x},S_{x})\}_{x\in A}
be a set of pairwise isomorphic Cayley graphs. Then the

G
-join
G[B_{x}]_{x\in A}
is a Cayley graph.

Proof.

With the notation of Theorem
3.4
if
X_{1}=\{e\}
, then
H=\{e\}
and
K_{X_{1}}=C_{X_{1}}
.
Clearly,
A=HC_{A}(H)
,
C_{X_{1}}=K_{X_{1}}C_{C_{X_{1}}}(K_{X_{1}})
, and
A=H\times A
. So it follows from Theorem
3.4

that the
G
-join
G[B_{x}]_{x\in A}
is a Cayley graph.

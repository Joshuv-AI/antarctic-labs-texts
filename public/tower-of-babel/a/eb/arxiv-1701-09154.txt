Nested Artin Strong Approximation Property.
Zunaira Kosar and Dorin Popescu

Abstract
. We study the Artin Approximation property with constraints in a different frame. As a consequence we give a nested Artin Strong Approximation property for algebraic power series rings over a field. Key words: Henselian rings, Etale neighborhood, Algebraic power series rings, Nested Artin approximation property, Artin approximation with constraints. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary 13B40, Secondary : 13J05, 14B12.

For any

Also M. Artin proved before (see

of

such that
y^{\prime}(x)\equiv y(x)

y^{\prime}_{j}(x)\in k\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{s_{j}}\}\ \ \forall j\in[p].

Shortly after, A. Gabrielov

1.
Properties on polynomial rings similar to the Artin approximation with constraints.

Question
2
together with
[

Proposition 6
.

Let
M\subset K[x]^{p}
be a finitely generated
K[x]
-submodule and
K\rightarrow K^{\prime}
a field extension. Let
J_{i}
,
i\in[p]
be subsets of
[n]
,
x_{J_{i}}=(x_{k})_{k\in J_{i}}
and
A_{i}=K[x_{J_{i}}]
,
A^{\prime}_{i}=K^{\prime}[x_{J_{i}}]
,
i\in[p]
. Set

\mathcal{N}=M\cap(A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{p})
and

\mathcal{N^{\prime}}=(K^{\prime}[x]M)\cap(A^{\prime}_{1}\times\cdots\times A^{\prime}_{p}).

If
\hat{u}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}w_{j}\hat{v}_{j}\in\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
for some
w_{j}\in M
and
\hat{v}_{j}\in K^{\prime}[x]
then there exist
v_{j}\in K[x]
such that

u=\sum_{j=1}^{r}w_{j}v_{j}\in\mathcal{N}
and
\operatorname{min}_{i\in[p]}\operatorname{ord}u_{i}=\operatorname{min}_{i\in[p]}\operatorname{ord}\hat{u}_{i}
.

Proof.

Let
w_{1},\ldots,w_{r}
be the generators of
M
and
{\hat{u}}\in\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
. We have

(1)

\hat{u}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}w_{j}\hat{v}_{j}

has this form for some polynomials
\hat{v}_{j}\in K^{\prime}[x]
.
Let
\hat{u}_{i}=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}^{n},|l|\leq\alpha}\hat{u}_{il}x^{l}
,
w_{ij}=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{N}^{n},|t|\leq\beta}w_{i,j,t}x^{t}
,
i\in[p]
and
\hat{v}_{j}=\sum_{q\in\mathbb{N}^{n},|q|\leq\gamma}\hat{v}_{j,q}x^{q}
, where
\alpha=\beta+\gamma
.
The components of equation (
1
) give a system of
(p(\sum_{1\leq s\leq\beta}{s+n-1\choose s}))
-linear equations as

T\tilde{v}=\tilde{u},

where
T
is a
(p(\sum_{1\leq s\leq\beta}{s+n-1\choose s}))\times(r(\sum_{1\leq s^{\prime}\leq\gamma}{s^{\prime}+n-1\choose s^{\prime}}))
matrix of entries of coefficients of
w_{j}
from
K
,
\tilde{v}
is a vector of entries from the coefficients of
\hat{v}_{j}
from
K^{\prime}
and
\tilde{u}
is a vector of entries from the coefficients of
\hat{u}_{i}
from
K^{\prime}
. Now this is a system
L
of linear equations with coefficients in
K
and has a solution
(\hat{v}_{jq}),(\hat{u}_{il})
in
K^{\prime}
. We may consider in
L
only variables corresponding to those
(\hat{v}_{jq}),(\hat{u}_{il})
which are not zero. Since
K\rightarrow K^{\prime}
is a flat morphism there exists a solution of
L
in
K
say
(v_{jq}),(u_{il})
. Thus we have
u=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}^{n},|l|\leq\alpha}{u}_{l}x^{l}\in K[x]^{p}
, and
{v}_{j}=\sum_{q\in\mathbb{N}^{n},|q|\leq\gamma}{v}_{jq}x^{q}\in K[x]
such that
u=\sum_{j=1}^{r}w_{j}v_{j}
.

Assume that
\operatorname{min}_{i\in[p]}\operatorname{ord}\hat{u}_{i}=\operatorname{ord}\hat{u}_{i_{0}}=c
for some
i_{0}\in[p]
. Then there exists
\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sum_{i}\nu_{i}=c
and
\hat{u}_{i_{0}\nu}\neq 0
. If we have
u_{i_{0}\nu}=0
for all solutions of
L
from
K
then we get a contradiction because
(\hat{u}_{il})
is generated by the solutions of
L
from
K
. Hence

u_{i_{0}\nu}\not=0
. Since the coefficients of
u_{i}
are zero when the corresponding coefficients of
\hat{u}_{i}
are zero we see that
u\in\mathcal{N}
and it has no coefficients corresponding to
{\hat{u}}_{il}
when
|l|<c
. Hence we have
\operatorname{min}_{i}\operatorname{ord}u_{i}=\operatorname{ord}u_{i_{0}}=c
.

∎

Proposition 7
.

Let
K\rightarrow K^{\prime}
be an algebraically pure morphism of fields and
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
. Let
J_{i}
,
i\in[p]
be subsets of
[n]
,
x_{J_{i}}=(x_{k})_{k\in J_{i}}
and
A_{i}=K[x_{J_{i}}]_{(x_{J_{i}})}
,
A^{\prime}_{i}=K^{\prime}[x_{J_{i}}]_{(x_{J_{i}})}
,
i\in[p]
. Set

\mathcal{N}=A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{p}
and

\mathcal{N^{\prime}}=A^{\prime}_{1}\times\cdots\times A^{\prime}_{p}
. Let
f
be a system of polynomials from
K[x]_{(x)}[Y]
,
Y=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{p})
, and
{\hat{y}}\in\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
, such that
f({\hat{y}})=0
.
Then there exists
y\in\mathcal{N}
such that
f(y)=0
and
\operatorname{ord}y_{i}=\operatorname{ord}\hat{y}_{i}
for
i\in[p]
.

Proof.

Let
f\in K[x]_{(x)}[Y]
be of the form

f(Y)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{n},|j|<\alpha}w_{j}Y^{j},

where
w_{j}
are the coefficients of
f
in
Y
and belong to
K[x]_{(x)}
. Since
{\hat{y}}\in\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
is a solution of
f
, we get

(2)

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{n},|j|<\alpha}w_{j}\hat{y}^{j}=0

Let
\hat{y}=\frac{\hat{P}(x)}{\hat{Q}(x)}\in\mathcal{N^{\prime}}
, where
\hat{P}(x)=(\hat{P}_{1}(x),\ldots,\hat{P}_{p}(x))
,
\hat{Q}(x)=(\hat{Q}_{1}(x),\ldots,\hat{Q}_{p}(x))
,
\hat{P}_{i}(x),\hat{Q}_{i}(x)\in K^{\prime}[x_{J_{i}}]
, and
\hat{Q}_{i}(x)\notin(x_{J_{i}})
for all
i\in[p]
. Let
w_{j}=(w_{1j},\ldots,w_{pj})
,
w_{ij}=\frac{P^{\prime}_{ij}(x)}{Q^{\prime}_{ij}(x)}\in K[x]_{(x)}
, where
j\in\mathbb{N}^{n},|j|<\alpha
,
Q^{\prime}_{ij}(x)\in K[x]
and
Q^{\prime}_{ij}(x)\notin(x)
.
Let
\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}(x)
be the least common multiple of
((Q^{\prime}_{ij})(x))
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}^{n},|j|<\alpha
and
\mathbf{\hat{Q}}(x)=\Pi_{i\in[p]}(\hat{Q}_{i}(x))
. If we multiply the equation (2) by
\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}(x)\mathbf{\hat{Q}}(x)^{\alpha}
we get

(3)

\mathbf{Q}^{\prime}(x)\mathbf{\hat{Q}}(x)^{\alpha}(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{n},|j|<\alpha}w_{j}\hat{y}^{j})=0.

From equation (
3
) we see that
f
will be transformed in a different system of equations in
\hat{P}_{i},\hat{Q}_{i}
, that is we change
f
by another system in more
\hat{y}
(in fact
2p
will be the new
p
) but this time they are from
K^{\prime}[x_{J_{1}}]\times\ldots\times K^{\prime}[x_{J_{p}}]
for some bigger
p
and we look for a solution from
K[x_{J_{1}}]\times\ldots\times K[x_{J_{p}}]
. This new system will give a system of equations
F
in the nonzero coefficients
(\hat{y}_{iq})
of
\hat{y}_{i}
in
x
.
Moreover we add for each
i,q
with
\hat{y}_{iq}\not=0
a new equation
G_{iq}=Y_{iq}Y_{iq}^{{}^{\prime}-1}-1
which has a solution in
K^{\prime}
given by
\hat{y}_{iq},\hat{y}_{iq}^{-1}
.
Thus
F,G=(G_{iq})
must have a solution
(y_{iq}),(y^{\prime}_{iq})
also in
K
. Then

\{q\in\mathbb{N}^{n}:y_{iq}\not=0\}=\{q\in\mathbb{N}^{n}:{\hat{y}}_{iq}\not=0\}

for any
i\in[p]
.
It follows that the new
y
given by
y_{i}=\sum_{q}y_{iq}x^{q}
satisfies
y\in K[x_{J_{1}}]\times\ldots\times K[x_{J_{p}}]\subset\mathcal{N}
,

f(y)=0
and
\operatorname{ord}y_{i}=\operatorname{ord}\ \hat{y}_{i}
for all
i\in[p]
.

∎

is an ultrafilter if and only if
\mathbb{N}\setminus s\in D
for each subset
s\subset\mathbb{N}
such that
s\notin D
.
It follows that
s\cup t=\mathbb{N}
implies
s\in D
or
t\in D
because
D
is an ultrafilter.
An ultrafilter is called nonprincipal if there exist no
r
such that
D=\{s|r\in s\subset\mathbb{N}\}
. More precisely, an ultrafilter is nonprincipal if and only if it contains the filter of all cofinite sets of
\mathbb{N}
.

Let
(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a family of rings and
\mathcal{I}=\{(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}A_{i}|\{i|x_{i}=0\}\in D\}
an ideal in
\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}A_{i}
. We call the factor ring
\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}A_{i}/\mathcal{I}
the
ultraproduct
of the family
(A_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with respect to the ultrafilter
D
. If
A_{i}=A
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
, then denote
A^{*}=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}A_{i}/\mathcal{I}
and call
A^{*}
the
ultrapower
of
A
with repect to the ultrafilter
D
. An element
a\in A^{*}
has the form
[(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}]
,
a_{i}\in A
, where “[ ]” means the class modulo
\mathcal{I}
. We recall below some properties of the ultrapower given in
[
4
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
.

If
A
is a local ring with
{\mathfrak{m}}
its maximal ideal, then
{\mathfrak{m}}^{*}=\{[(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}]|\{i_{i}\in\mathbb{N}|x_{i}\in{\mathfrak{m}}\}\in D\}
is a maximal ideal in
A^{*}
and the unique one. If
K
is the residue field of
A
then the residue field of
A^{*}
is the ultrapower
K^{*}
of
K
with respect to the ultrafilter
D
.

Theorem 11
.

Let
K
be a field,
A=K\langle x\rangle
,
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
,
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})\in K\langle x\rangle[Y]^{r}
,
Y=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{p})
and
0\leq s_{1}\leq\ldots\leq s_{p}\leq n
be some non-negative integers.
Then there exists a map
\nu:\mathbb{N}^{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that if
y^{\prime}=(y^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,y^{\prime}_{p})
,
y^{\prime}_{i}\in K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s_{i}}]
,
i\in[p]
satisfies
f(y^{\prime})\equiv 0
modulo
(x)^{\nu(c)}
for some
c=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
and
\operatorname{ord}y^{\prime}_{i}=c_{i}
,
i\in[p]
then there exists
y_{i}\in K\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{s_{i}}\rangle
for all
i\in[p]
such that
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{p})
is a solution of
f
in
A
and
\operatorname{ord}y_{i}=c_{i}
for all
i\in[p]
.

Proof.

We will show that given
c=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
there exists an integer
k_{c}\in\mathbb{N}
such that if
y^{\prime}=(y^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,y^{\prime}_{p})
,
y^{\prime}_{i}\in K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s_{i}}]
satisfies
f(y^{\prime})\equiv 0
modulo
(x)^{k_{c}}
and
\operatorname{ord}y^{\prime}_{i}=c_{i}
,
i\in[p]
then there exists
y_{i}\in K\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{s_{i}}\rangle
for all
i\in[p]
such that
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{p})
is a solution of
f
in
A
and
\operatorname{ord}y_{i}=c_{i}
for all
i\in[p]
.

Assume that this is false. Thus there exists
c
such that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
y^{\prime(k)}=(y^{\prime(k)}_{1}\ldots,y^{\prime(k)}_{p})
,
y^{\prime(k)}_{i}\in K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s_{i}}]
with
f(y^{\prime(k)})\equiv 0
modulo
(x)^{k}
and
\operatorname{ord}y^{\prime(k)}_{i}=c_{i}
,
i\in[p]
,
but there exists no
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{p})
,
y_{i}\in K\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{s_{i}}\rangle
for all
i\in[p]
which is a solution of
f
in
A
and
\operatorname{ord}y_{i}=c_{i}
for all
i\in[p]
.

Let
D
be a nonprincipal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
and
(K\langle x\rangle)^{*}
be the ultrapower of
K\langle x\rangle
with respect to
D
.
Take
{y^{\prime}}_{i}^{*}=[({y^{\prime}}_{i}^{(k)})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}]\in(K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s_{i}}])^{*}\subset(K\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{s_{i}}\rangle)^{*}\subset(K\langle x\rangle)^{*}
. Consider the canonical surjection

\eta_{i}:(K\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{s_{i}}\rangle)^{*}\rightarrow(K\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{s_{i}}\rangle)^{*}/\bigcap_{l\in\mathbb{N}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s_{i}})^{l},

and set
\hat{{y^{\prime}}_{i}^{*}}=\eta_{i}({y^{\prime}}_{i}^{*})
. We have the following commutative diagram:

\textstyle{(K\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{s_{i}}\rangle)^{*}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\eta_{i}}
\textstyle{(K\langle x\rangle)^{*}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(K\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{s_{i}}\rangle)^{*}/\bigcap_{l\in\mathbb{N}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s_{i}})^{l}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(K\langle x\rangle)^{*}/\bigcap_{l\in\mathbb{N}}(x)^{l}}

By
[
16
, Proposition 2.3]
(see also
[
17
]
,
[
18
, Theorem 2.5]
,
[
20
, Lemma 3.3.2]
) we know that

(K\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{s_{i}}\rangle)^{*}/\bigcap_{l\in\mathbb{N}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s_{i}})^{l}\cong K^{*}[[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s_{i}}]]

and similarly

(K\langle x\rangle)^{*}/\bigcap_{l\in\mathbb{N}}(x)^{l}\cong K^{*}[[x]].

Thus we may consider
\hat{{y^{\prime}}^{*}_{i}}\in K^{*}[[x_{1},\ldots,x_{s_{i}}]]\subset K^{*}[[x]]
and note that

\hat{{y^{\prime}}^{*}}=(\hat{{y^{\prime}}_{1}^{*}},\ldots,\hat{{y^{\prime}}_{p}^{*}})
is a solution of
f
in
K^{*}[[x]]
. Indeed,
we have
f(y^{\prime(k)})\equiv 0
modulo
(x)^{k}
for all
k
. Fix
t\in\mathbb{N}
. we have
f(y^{\prime(k)})\in(x)^{t}
for all
k\geq t
and so
\{k\in\mathbb{N}:f(y^{\prime(k)})\in(x)^{t}\}\in D
because
D
contains cofinite sets, being a nonprincipal ultrafilter. It follows that
f({y^{\prime}}^{*})\in(x)^{t}(K\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle)^{*}
for all

. So we have
f({y^{\prime}}^{*})\in\bigcap_{t\in\mathbb{N}}(x)^{t}(K\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle)^{*}
and hence
\hat{{y^{\prime}}^{*}}=(\hat{{y^{\prime}}_{1}^{*}},\ldots,\hat{{y^{\prime}}_{p}^{*}})
gives a solution of
f
in
K^{*}[[x]]
.

We claim that
\operatorname{ord}\hat{{y^{\prime}}^{*}}_{i}=c_{i}
. Indeed, set

s_{ij}=\{k\in\mathbb{N}:{y^{\prime}}^{(k)}_{ij}\not=0\}
.
By assumption
\operatorname{ord}{y^{\prime}}^{(k)}_{i}=c_{i}
,

and so
\cup_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{n},|j|=c_{i}}s_{ij}=\mathbb{N}
which implies

s_{ij_{i}}\in D
for some
j_{i}
because

is an ultrafilter. It follows that
{y^{\prime}}^{*}_{ij_{i}}\not=0
and so
\hat{{y^{\prime}}^{*}}_{ij_{i}}\not=0
, which shows our claim.

By Nested Artin Approximation Property
[
17
, Theorem 3.7]
(see also
[
18
, Corollary 3.7]
,
[
20
, Theorem 5.2.1]
) this implies that for all
u\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a solution
\tilde{y}=(\tilde{y}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{y}_{p})\in K^{*}\langle x\rangle
of
f
with
\tilde{y}_{i}\in K^{*}\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{s_{i}}\rangle
and
\tilde{y}_{i}\equiv\hat{{y^{\prime}}_{i}^{*}}
mod

for all

. Take

for all

. This implies that ord
\tilde{y}_{i}
= ord
\hat{{y^{\prime}}_{i}^{*}}
,

. Since

is algebraically pure we get by Theorem

a

in

such that

with ord

and

, a contradiction.

∎

Nonlocal-to-local convergence of the Cahn-Hilliard equation with degenerate mobility and the Flory-Huggins potential.
Charles Elbar, Jakub Skrzeczkowski

Abstract
The Cahn-Hilliard equation is a fundamental model for phase separation phenomena. Its rigorous derivation from the nonlocal aggregation equation, motivated by the desire to link interacting particle systems and continuous descriptions, has received much attention in recent years. In the recent article, we showed how to treat the case of degenerate mobility for the first time. Here, we discuss how to adapt the exploited tools to the case of the mobility m ⁡ ( u ) = u ⁡ ( 1 − u ) m(u)=u\,(1-u) as in the original works of Giacomin-Lebowitz and Elliott-Garcke. The main additional information is the boundedness of u u , implied by the form of mobility, which allows handling the nonlinear terms. We also discuss the case of (mildly) singular kernels and a model of cell-cell adhesion with the same mobility.

We consider the nonlocal Cahn-Hilliard equation with degenerate mobility

\begin{split}\partial_{t}u=\DIV(m(u)\nabla\mu)\quad\text{in}\quad&(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{d},\\
\mu=B[u]+F^{\prime}(u)\quad\text{in}\quad&(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{d},\end{split}

(1.1)

equipped with an initial datum
u^{0}\geq 0
. Here, the mobility is
m(u)=u(1-u)
,
\mathbb{T}^{d}
is the
d
-dimensional flat torus, which is particularly useful when treating nonlocal operators,
B
is the nonlocal operator
B=B_{\varepsilon}
defined with

B_{\varepsilon}[u](x)=(J_{\varepsilon}\ast 1)u(x)-J_{\varepsilon}\ast u(x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}J_{\varepsilon}(x-y)(u(x)-u(y))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

(1.2)

The convolution kernel
J_{\varepsilon}
is of the form

J_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{\omega_{\varepsilon}(x)}{\varepsilon^{2-\alpha}|x|^{\alpha}}

(1.3)

for
0\leq\alpha<d-1
, where
\{\omega_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
is the family of standard mollifiers:

\omega_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\omega\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)

(1.4)

with
\omega
being a nonnegative smooth radially symmetric kernel which is compactly supported in the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\frac{\omega(y)y_{i}y_{j}}{|y|^{\alpha}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=\frac{W}{d}\delta_{i,j},

(1.5)

for some
W>0
.

Remark 1.1
.

The range
\alpha\in[0,d-1)
ensures that the kernel is in
W^{1,1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and therefore we can apply the existing theory of the existence of weak solutions for the nonlocal equation. This type of kernel has for instance been considered in
[
19
]
.

The potential
F
is the usual double-well logarithmic potential

F(s)=s\log(s)+(1-s)\log(1-s)-\theta\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+k,

(1.6)

where
\theta>1
and
k
is a constant such that
F(s)\geq 0
for all
s\in(0,1)
. Note in particular the identities

F^{\prime}(s)=\log\left(\frac{s}{1-s}\right)+\theta(1-2s),\quad F^{\prime\prime}(s)=\frac{1}{s(1-s)}-2\theta,\quad m(s)F^{\prime\prime}(s)=1-2\theta s(1-s).

(1.7)

We impose the constraint on the parameter
\theta

2\,\theta<\inf_{\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}}(J_{\varepsilon}\ast 1)

(1.8)

for some
\varepsilon_{0}
. This is always satisfied for
\varepsilon_{0}
small enough since

J_{\varepsilon}\ast 1=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\frac{\omega(y)}{|y|^{\alpha}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

As we intend to send
\varepsilon\to 0
, we can assume that this condition is always satisfied. The condition (
1.8
) ensures that the coefficient
(J_{\varepsilon}\ast 1)+F^{\prime\prime}(s)
(standing next to
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
in (
1.1
)) is strictly positive and the existence as well as uniqueness of the weak solution follows.

Our target is to prove that as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the solutions of

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}=\DIV(m(u_{\varepsilon})\nabla\mu_{\varepsilon})\quad\text{in}\quad

\displaystyle(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{d},

(1.9)

\displaystyle\mu_{\varepsilon}=B_{\varepsilon}[u_{\varepsilon}]+F^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\quad\text{in}\quad

\displaystyle(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{d},

(1.10)

tend to the weak solution of the degenerate Cahn-Hilliard equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=\DIV(m(u)\nabla\mu)\quad\text{in}\quad

\displaystyle(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{d},

(1.11)

\displaystyle\mu=-\frac{W}{2}\Delta u+F^{\prime}(u)\quad\text{in}\quad

\displaystyle(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}.

(1.12)

(Convergence of nonlocal to local Cahn-Hilliard equation on the torus)

Let

where

is a weak solution of the degenerate Cahn-Hilliard equation (
1.11

If

Lemma 4.3

(compactness)
.

There exists

4.2
Nonlocal gradient
S_{\varepsilon}
and its convergence properties

The limit equation that we obtain is a fourth-order equation. The number of derivatives can be reduced by the integration by parts in a weak formulation. Therefore, to obtain this weak formulation we need to mimic the same integration by parts at the nonlocal level (i.e. with
B_{\varepsilon}[u]
instead of
-\Delta u
). This can be done if we introduce a nonlocal approximation of
\nabla
. Thus we define the operator (see e.g.
[
25
]
)

S_{\varepsilon}[\varphi](x,y):=\frac{\sqrt{\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)}}{\sqrt{2}\,\varepsilon^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}|y|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}(\varphi(x-y)-\varphi(x)),

(4.1)

which has the following algebraic properties:

Lemma 4.4
.

The operator
S_{\varepsilon}
satisfies:

(S1)

S_{\varepsilon}
is a linear operator that commutes with derivatives with respect to
x
,

(S2)

for all functions
f,g:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
we have the Leibniz rule up to an error:

\displaystyle S_{\varepsilon}[fg](x,y)-S_{\varepsilon}[f](x,y)g(x)

\displaystyle-S_{\varepsilon}[g](x,y)f(x)=

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)}}{\sqrt{2}\,\varepsilon^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}|y|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}(f(x-y)-f(x))\,(g(x-y)-g(x)).

(S3)

for all
u,\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})

\langle B_{\varepsilon}[u](\cdot),\varphi(\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}=\langle S_{\varepsilon}[u](\cdot,\cdot),S_{\varepsilon}[\varphi](\cdot,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}.

For the simple proof, we refer to
[
25
, Lemma 3.4]

, when proving the convergence

, we will need to deal with the operator

in two directions: allowing to consider singular potentials and including the case of general mobility

Lemma 4.5

Let

The nonlocal-to-local limit for the cell-cell adhesion model

Here, we explain, using similar ideas, how to treat a recently introduced model in the theory of cell-cell adhesion from
[
13
, eq. (15)]
which also involves the mobility
m(u)=u\,(1-u)
. More precisely, we consider the PDE posed on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}

\partial_{t}u=\DIV(u\nabla u)-a\,\DIV(u\,(1-u)\,K[u]),

(6.1)

where the parameter
a
measures the relative cell-cell adhesion strength while
K
is the nonlocal kernel
K
defined as

K[u]=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{S^{d-1}}u(x+r\,\eta)\,V(r\,\eta)\,r^{d-1}\,\eta\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}S(\eta)\,\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r.

Here,
V
is a smooth, radially symmetric and compactly supported kernel with
\supp V\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
and
S^{d-1}
is a unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. It is useful to observe that, with the assumptions on
V
,
K
can be written as a convolution operator

K[u](x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u(x-y)\,V(y)\,\frac{y}{|y|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=u\ast\left(V(\cdot)\,\left(\frac{\cdot}{|\cdot|}\right)\right)

We now rescale the kernel by taking
V(x):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\,\omega_{\varepsilon}(x)
in (
6.1
) where
\omega_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d}}\omega\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)
and
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth, radially symmetric and compactly supported kernel. The scaling factor
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
is chosen to obtain a meaningful PDE in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
as explained below. Hence, we consider solutions to the PDE

\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}=\DIV(u_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})-a\,\DIV(u_{\varepsilon}\,(1-u_{\varepsilon})\,K_{\varepsilon}[u_{\varepsilon}])

(6.2)

with

K_{\varepsilon}[u]=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u(x-y)\,\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)\,\frac{y}{|y|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

(6.3)

and we want to understand the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
. To guess what is the expected PDE, let
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
. Then, by Taylor’s expansion,

\begin{split}K_{\varepsilon}[\psi]&=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\psi(x-y)-\psi(x))\,\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)\,\frac{y}{|y|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=\\
&=\nabla\psi(x)\,\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)\,|y|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y+R_{\varepsilon}=\nabla\psi(x)\,\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\omega(y)\,|y|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)+R_{\varepsilon},\end{split}

(6.4)

where
R_{\varepsilon}\leq C(\omega)\,\|D^{2}\psi\|_{\infty}\,\varepsilon\to 0
when
\varepsilon\to 0
. Hence, we expect that the limiting PDE reads

\partial_{t}u=\DIV(u\nabla u)-C_{\omega}\,a\,\DIV(u\,(1-u)\,\nabla u),

(6.5)

where
C_{\omega}:=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\omega(y)\,|y|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y
.

To have sufficient uniform a priori estimates with respect to
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we have to assume that the parameter
a
satisfies

|a|\,\sqrt{C}<1,

(6.6)

where
C
is a constant in the inequality

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\omega_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\leq C\,\|f\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2},

(6.7)

which holds true by Lemma
C.2
. The inequality (
6.7
) implies also the uniform bound on
K_{\varepsilon}[u]
: using that
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)\frac{y}{|y|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=0
we have

K_{\varepsilon}[u]=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u(x-y)\,\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)\,\frac{y}{|y|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(u(x-y)-u(x))\,\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)\,\frac{y}{|y|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

so that by Jensen’s inequality

\|K_{\varepsilon}[u]\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\frac{(u(x-y)-u(x))^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,\omega_{\varepsilon}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\leq C\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}.

(6.8)

For the nonlocal-to-local limit, the main idea is again the same as in the case of the Cahn-Hilliard system: the mobility
m(u)=u\,(1-u)
ensures that the solution remains bounded so that we can easily handle the nonlinear terms. We also want to comment that in
[
13
]
Authors derive also systems of PDEs of the form (
6.2
) but these are not treatable mathematically because, in full generality, there is currently no mathematical theory of such systems. Here we focus on a single equation.

6.1
Existence of weak solutions to (
6.2
)

Definition 6.1
.

We say that a function
u_{\varepsilon}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\to[0,1]
is a weak solution to (
6.2
) with initial condition
u^{0}
if
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
and for all
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
we have

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u^{0}\,\psi(0,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}\,\partial_{t}\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(u_{\varepsilon}\,\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-a\,(1-u_{\varepsilon})\,u_{\varepsilon}\,K_{\varepsilon}[u_{\varepsilon}])\cdot\nabla\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
0\leq u^{0}\leq 1
and let the parameter
a
satisfy (
6.6
). Then, there exists a weak solution to (
6.2
) with the initial condition
u^{0}
. Moreover, the following sequences are bounded uniformly with respect to
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
:

(E1)

\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
in
L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
with
0\leq u_{\varepsilon}\leq 1
,

(E2)

\{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\}
in
L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
,

(E3)

\{\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}\}
in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
.

Proof.

Step 1: An auxiliary problem.
For fixed
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
we consider an auxiliary problem

\partial_{t}u_{\delta}=\DIV(u_{\delta}\nabla u_{\delta})-a\,\DIV((1-u_{\delta})\,(u_{\delta}\,K_{\varepsilon}[u_{\delta}])\ast\varphi_{\delta})

(6.9)

where
\{\varphi_{\delta}\}_{\delta\in(0,1)}
is a standard mollifying sequence. A solution to (
6.9
) with regularity
u_{\delta}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
can be obtained by standard methods, for example by the Schauder’s fixed point theorem. We want to pass with
\delta\to 0
in (
6.9
).

Step 2: Uniform bounds with respect to
\delta
and
\varepsilon
.
We claim that the following sequences are uniformly bounded with respect to
\delta,\varepsilon\in(0,1)
:

(F1)

\{u_{\delta}\}
in
L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
with
0\leq u_{\delta}\leq 1
,

(F2)

\{\nabla u_{\delta}\}
in
L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
,

(F3)

\{\partial_{t}u_{\delta}\}
in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
.

Let
p
be a function such that
p(s)=0
for
s<1
and
p^{\prime}(s)\geq 0
for
s\geq 1
. Let
P(s)=\int_{1}^{s}p(r)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r
. Integrating in space we deduce

\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}P(u_{\delta})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u_{\delta}\,p^{\prime}(u_{\delta})\,|\nabla u_{\delta}|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-a\,\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}p(u_{\delta})\,\DIV((1-u_{\delta})\,(u_{\delta}\,K_{\varepsilon}[u_{\delta}])\ast\varphi_{\delta})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

Since
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u_{\delta}\,p^{\prime}(u_{\delta})\,|\nabla u_{\delta}|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\geq 0
and
u_{\delta}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
, we can approximate function
\mbox{sign}_{+}(u-1)=\mathds{1}_{u>1}
with
p
and deduce

\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|u_{\delta}-1|_{+}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\leq-a\,\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mbox{sign}_{+}(u_{\delta}-1)\,\DIV((1-u_{\delta})\,(u_{\delta}\,K_{\varepsilon}[u_{\delta}])\ast\varphi_{\delta})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

where
|u-1|_{+}=(u-1)\,\mathds{1}_{u>1}
. The integrand on the (RHS) can be written again in the divergence form as
-\DIV((1-u_{\delta})_{+}\,(u_{\delta}\,K_{\varepsilon}[u_{\delta}])\ast\varphi_{\delta})
so the respective integral vanish and we conclude that
u_{\delta}\leq 1
. In a similar way we prove that
u_{\delta}\geq 0
.

Next, we multiply by
\log u_{\delta}
and integrate by parts to get

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u_{\delta}\log u_{\delta}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla u_{\delta}|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\leq|a|\,\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla u_{\delta}|\,|u_{\delta}\,K_{\varepsilon}[u_{\delta}]|\ast\varphi_{\delta}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\leq\\
\phantom{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}&\leq|a|\,\|\nabla u_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\,\|u_{\delta}\,K_{\varepsilon}[u_{\delta}]\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq|a|\,\|\nabla u_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\,\|K_{\varepsilon}[u_{\delta}]\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}.\end{split}

(6.10)

Using (
6.8
), we obtain

\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u_{\delta}\log u_{\delta}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla u_{\delta}|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\leq|a|\,\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla u_{\delta}|\,|u_{\delta}\,K_{\varepsilon}[u_{\delta}]|\ast\varphi_{\delta}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\leq|a|\,\sqrt{C}\,\|\nabla u_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}.

Hence, since
|a|\,\sqrt{C}<1
, we obtain
(F2)
.

Finally, multiplying (
6.9
) by
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
and using that
0\leq u_{\delta}\leq 1
as well as (
6.8
) we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u_{\delta}\,\partial_{t}\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u_{\delta}\,\nabla u_{\delta}\,\nabla\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right|+\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(1-u_{\delta})\,(u_{\delta}\,K_{\varepsilon}[u_{\delta}])\ast\varphi_{\delta}\,\nabla\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\|\nabla u_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}+\|K_{\varepsilon}[u_{\delta}]\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}\right)\,\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}\phantom{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\sqrt{C})\,\|\nabla u_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})}\,\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})},\phantom{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}

so that
(F3)
follows by
(F2)
.

Step 3: The limit
\delta\to 0
and the estimates
(E1)
–
(E3)
.
By the Aubin-Lions lemma, the Banach-Alaoglu theorem and interpolation in
L^{p}
spaces, there exists a subsequence such that

\begin{split}&u_{\delta}\to u\mbox{ strongly in }L^{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})\mbox{ for }p\in[1,\infty),\\
&\nabla u_{\delta}\rightharpoonup u\mbox{ weakly in }L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}),\\
&\partial_{t}u_{\delta}\rightharpoonup\partial_{t}u\mbox{ weakly in }L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})).\end{split}

(6.11)

This is sufficient to pass to the limit
\delta\to 0
in the expression

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u^{0}\,\psi(0,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u_{\delta}\,\partial_{t}\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(u_{\delta}\,\nabla u_{\delta}-a\,(1-u_{\delta})\,(u_{\delta}\,K_{\varepsilon}[u_{\delta}])\ast\varphi_{\delta})\cdot\nabla\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

(keep in mind that
\varepsilon
is fixed!) for
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,T);\mathbb{T}^{d})
so that
u
satisfies Definition
6.1
. Finally, since bounds
(F1)
–
(F3)
are preserved along the limit (
6.11
),
(E1)
–
(E3)
follow.

∎

6.2
Passing to the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
in (
6.2
).

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
0\leq u^{0}\leq 1
and let the parameter
a
satisfy (
6.6
). Let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
be a sequence of solutions to (

satisfying uniform bounds
(E1)
–
(E3)
. Then, there exists a subsequence (not relabelled) such that

&\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup u\mbox{ weakly in }L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}),\end{split}

(6.12)

where

satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u^{0}\,\psi(0,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u\,\partial_{t}\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(u\,\nabla u-C_{\omega}\,a\,(1-u)\,u\,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,

for all

, i.e.

is a distributional solution of (

).

Proof.

As in the proof of Lemma
6.2
, by the Aubin-Lions lemma, the Banach-Alaoglu theorem, interpolation in
L^{p}
spaces and the bounds
(E1)
–
(E3)
, there exists a subsequence as in (
6.12
).

We want to pass to the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
in Definition
6.1
. The only difficulty is to justify the limit in the term

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(1-u_{\varepsilon})\,u_{\varepsilon}\,K_{\varepsilon}[u_{\varepsilon}]\cdot\nabla\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

due to the singularity

in the nonlocal operator

. By the bound (

), there exists

such that

To identify
\xi
, let

. Since
K_{\varepsilon}
is a convolution operator with an antisymmetric kernel (see

)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}K_{\varepsilon}[u_{\varepsilon}]\,\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}\,K_{\varepsilon}[\psi]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

so that passing to the limit in (

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\xi\,\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=-C_{\omega}\,\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u\,\nabla\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

and the proof is concluded.
∎

Appendix A
Results from classical analysis

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
be a sequence strongly compact in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))
. Then,

\frac{u_{\varepsilon}(t,x-\varepsilon y)-u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)}{\varepsilon|y|}\to-\nabla u(t,x)\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}\mbox{ strongly in }L^{\infty}_{y}(\mathbb{T}^{d};L^{2}_{(t,x)}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})).

Proof.

We write

\displaystyle\frac{u_{\varepsilon}(t,x-\varepsilon y)-u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)}{\varepsilon|y|}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(t,x-\varepsilon sy)\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=-\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\frac{y}{|y|}-\int_{0}^{1}[\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(t,x-\varepsilon sy)-\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)]\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Therefore

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{u_{\varepsilon}(t,x-\varepsilon y)-u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)}{\varepsilon|y|}+\nabla u(t,x)\frac{y}{|y|}\right\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|(\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u)\cdot\frac{y}{|y|}\right\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(t,x-\varepsilon sy)-\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

With the assumptions of the lemma, we deduce that the first term converges to 0 uniformly with respect to
y
. Concerning the second term, we use the Fréchet-Kolmogorov theorem to deduce that the translation converge to 0 uniformly: there exists a modulus of continuity
\gamma:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that
\lim_{r\to 0}\gamma(r)=0
and

\int_{(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(t,x-\varepsilon sy)-\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq\gamma(\varepsilon\,s\,|y|)\leq\gamma(\varepsilon).

This proves the result.
∎

We consider a sequence of radial functions
\{\rho_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
such that
\rho_{\varepsilon}\geq 0
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{\varepsilon}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=1

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{|x|>\delta}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=0\mbox{ for all }\delta>0.

For the formulation of the compactness result, we use another sequence
\{\varphi_{\delta}\}_{\delta\in(0,1)}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of standard mollifiers with mass 1 such that
\varphi_{\delta}(x)=\frac{1}{\delta^{d}}\varphi(\frac{x}{\delta})
with
\varphi
of mass 1 and compactly supported.

.

Let

. Let
\{f_{\varepsilon}\}

\{\rho_{\varepsilon}\}

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\frac{|f_{\varepsilon}(t,x)-f_{\varepsilon}(t,y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{2}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(|x-y|)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq C

(B.1)

for some constant
C
. Then,
\{f_{\varepsilon}\}
is compact in space in

, i.e.

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|f_{\varepsilon}\ast\varphi_{\delta}(t,x)-f_{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=0.

(B.2)

Remark B.2
.

Let
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function as in (
1.4
)-(

). Consider

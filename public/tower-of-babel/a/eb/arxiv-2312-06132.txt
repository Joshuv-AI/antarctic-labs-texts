MacMahon’s sums-of-divisors and their connection to multiple Eisenstein series
Henrik Bachmann

Abstract
. We give explicit expressions for MacMahon’s generalized sums-of-divisors q q -series A r A_{r} and C r C_{r} by relating them to (odd) multiple Eisenstein series. Recently, these sums-of-divisors have been studied in the context of quasimodular forms, vertex algebras, N = 4 N=4 S ​ U ​ ( N ) SU(N) Super-Yang-Mills theory, and the study of congruences of partitions. We relate them to a broader mathematical framework and give explicit expressions for both q q -series in terms of Eisenstein series and their odd variants.

Quasi-shuffle products

In this section, we recall some basic facts on quasi-shuffle products as, for example, presented in
[
HI
]
. Let
L
be a countable set, called
alphabet
, whose elements we will refer to as
letters
. A monic monomial in the non-commutative polynomial ring
\mathbb{Q}\langle L\rangle
will be called a
word
, and we denote the empty word by
{\bf 1}
.
Suppose that we have a commutative and associative product
\diamond
on the vector space
\mathbb{Q}L
. Then the
quasi-shuffle product

\ast_{\diamond}
on
\mathbb{Q}\langle L\rangle
is defined as the
\mathbb{Q}
-bilinear product, which satisfies
{\bf 1}\ast_{\diamond}w=w\ast_{\diamond}{\bf 1}=w
for any word
w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle L\rangle
and

\displaystyle aw\ast_{\diamond}bv=a(w\ast_{\diamond}bv)+b(aw\ast_{\diamond}v)+(a\diamond b)(w\ast_{\diamond}v)

for any letters
a,b\in L
and words
w,v\in\mathbb{Q}\langle L\rangle
. This gives a commutative
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
(\mathbb{Q}\langle L\rangle,\ast_{\diamond})
, which is called quasi-shuffle algebra.

In the context of multiple zeta values and multiple Eisenstein series, we are interested in the alphabet
L_{z}=\{z_{k}\mid k\geq 1\}
together with the product
z_{k_{1}}\diamond z_{k_{2}}=z_{k_{1}+k_{2}}
for
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 1
. The corresponding quasi-shuffle product
\ast=\ast_{\diamond}
is called the
harmonic product
.

The space
\mathfrak{H}^{1}:=\mathbb{Q}\langle L_{z}\rangle
equipped with the harmonic product gives a commutative
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{\ast}
. For an index
{\bf k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{r}
we define the word
z_{\bf k}=z_{k_{1}}\cdots z_{k_{r}}
. Notice that, as a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space,
\mathfrak{H}^{1}
is spanned by
z_{\bf k}
for arbitrary indices
{\bf k}
. We define the subspace
\mathfrak{H}^{0}
as the space spanned by
z_{\bf k}
for admissible (meaning
k_{1}\geq 2
) indices
{\bf k}
. We also consider the following subspace of
\mathfrak{H}^{0}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}^{2}=\mathbb{Q}+\langle k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\mid r\geq 1,k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq 2\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}\,,

which is spanned by
z_{\bf k}
such that the multiple Eisenstein series
\mathbb{G}_{{\bf k}}
is defined. Notice that both
\mathfrak{H}^{0}
and
\mathfrak{H}^{2}
are closed under
\ast
. We obtain the following inclusion of
\mathbb{Q}
-algebras

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}^{2}_{\ast}\subset\mathfrak{H}^{0}_{\ast}

\displaystyle\subset\mathfrak{H}^{1}_{\ast}\,.

We can view the multiple zeta values as a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map defined for
w\in\mathfrak{H}^{0}
by

\displaystyle\zeta:\mathfrak{H}^{0}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{Z}\,,

\displaystyle w=z_{\bf k}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\zeta(w)=\zeta({\bf k})\,.

By the definition of multiple zeta values as an iterated sum, it is easy to see that this map is an algebra homomorphism
\mathfrak{H}^{0}_{\ast}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}
. Similarly, one can show that the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear maps defined by

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}:\mathfrak{H}^{2}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\,,

\displaystyle z_{\bf k}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\mathbb{G}_{\bf k}\,.

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}^{{\bf o}}:\mathfrak{H}^{2}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{H})\,,

\displaystyle z_{\bf k}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\mathbb{G}^{{\bf o}}_{\bf k}\,.

are also algebra homomorphism.

For Theorem
1.1
we need the following fact for quasi-shuffle algebras: Assume that we have an algebra homomorphism
\varphi:(\mathbb{Q}\langle L\rangle,\ast_{\diamond})\rightarrow R
in some
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra

. Then for any

the following identity holds (see
[
B3
, (2.13)]
or
[

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varphi(a^{n})T^{n}=\exp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-1}\varphi(a^{\diamond n})\frac{T^{n}}{n}\right).

Here

and
a^{n}=a\cdots a
. In particular, by choosing

and the harmonic product, we get for any

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{G}_{\underbrace{k,\dots,k}_{n}}(\tau)T^{n}=\exp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-1}\mathbb{G}_{nk}(\tau)\frac{T^{n}}{n}\right).

(3.1)

This shows that for any

the multiple Eisenstein series
\mathbb{G}_{2k,\dots,2k}(\tau)
is a (quasi)modular form of homogeneous weight

and we see that

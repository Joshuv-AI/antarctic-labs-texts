Nijenhuis deformations of Poisson algebras and F F -manifold algebras
Anusuiya Baishya, Apurba Das

Abstract
. The notion of pre-Poisson algebras was introduced by Aguiar in his study of zinbiel algebras and pre-Lie algebras. In this paper, we first introduce NS-Poisson algebras as a generalization of both Poisson algebras and pre-Poisson algebras. An NS-Poisson algebra has an associated sub-adjacent Poisson algebra. We show that a Nijenhuis operator and a twisted Rota-Baxter operator on a Poisson algebra deforms the structure into an NS-Poisson algebra. The semi-classical limit of an NS-algebra deformation and a suitable filtration of an NS-algebra produce NS-Poisson algebras. On the other hand, F F -manifold algebras were introduced by Dotsenko as the underlying algebraic structure of F F -manifolds. We also introduce NS- F F -manifold algebras as a simultaneous generalization of NS-Poisson algebras, F F -manifold algebras and pre- F F -manifold algebras. In the end, we show that Nijenhuis deformations of F F -manifold algebras and the semi-classical limits of NS-pre-Lie algebra deformations have NS- F F -manifold algebra structures.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Poisson algebras and
F
-manifold algebras

Poisson manifolds appear naturally in Hamiltonian mechanics and are also central objects in the study of deformation quantization
[
14
]
. Poisson algebras are the underlying algebraic structure of Poisson manifolds. Explicitly, a
Poisson algebra
is a commutative associative algebra
(A,\cdot)
equipped with a Lie bracket
\{~,~\}:A\times A\rightarrow A
that satisfies the Leibniz rule:

(1)

\displaystyle\{x,y\cdot z\}=\{x,y\}\cdot z+y\cdot\{x,z\},\text{ for }x,y,z\in A.

The algebra of observables in symplectic geometry, the space of smooth functions on the dual of a Lie algebra, vertex operator algebras and the semi-classical limit of any associative formal deformations of commutative associative algebras are examples of Poisson algebras. Representations of Poisson algebras are also extensively studied in the literature
[
5
]
,
[
22
]
. Recall that a
representation
of a Poisson algebra
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
is a triple
(V,\mu,\rho)
in which
(V,\mu)
is a representation of the commutative associative algebra
(A,\cdot)
and
(V,\rho)
is a representation of the Lie algebra
(A,\{~,~\})
satisfying additionally

(2)

\displaystyle\rho_{x}\circ\mu_{y}-\mu_{y}\circ\rho_{x}-\mu_{\{x,y\}}=0,

(3)

\displaystyle\mu_{x}\circ\rho_{y}+\mu_{y}\circ\rho_{x}-\rho_{x\cdot y}=0,

for all
x,y\in A
.
See also
[
15
]
for more details about Poisson algebras.

On the other hand, the notion of an
F
-manifold
(also called
weak Frobenius manifold
) was introduced by Hertling and Manin
[
12
]
as a generalization of Frobenius manifold that appeared in
2
-dimensional topological field theories. An
F
-manifold
is a pair
(M,\circ)
consisting of a smooth supermanifold
M
and a smooth bilinear commutative associative product
\circ
on the tangent sheaf
TM
such that

\displaystyle P_{X_{1}\circ X_{2}}(X_{3},X_{4})=X_{1}\circ P_{X_{2}}(X_{3},X_{4})+(-1)^{|X_{1}||X_{2}|}X_{2}\circ P_{X_{1}}(X_{3},X_{4}),

where
P_{X_{1}}(X_{2},X_{3})=[X_{1},X_{2}\circ X_{3}]-[X_{1},X_{2}]\circ X_{3}-(-1)^{|X_{1}||X_{2}|}X_{2}\circ[X_{1},X_{3}]
measures to what extent the product
\circ
and the Lie bracket
[~,~]
of vector fields fail to satisfy the Leibniz rule of a Poisson algebra (see also
[
19
]
). In
[
8
]
, Dotsenko first considered the notion of an
F
-manifold algebra
as the underlying algebraic structure of the
F
-manifolds. He further showed that the graded object of the filtration of the operad
PreLie
is the operad
FMan
encoding
F
-manifold algebras. It has been observed by Liu, Sheng and Bai
[
18
]
that
F
-manifold algebras are the semi-classical limits of pre-Lie formal deformations of commutative pre-Lie algebras. Thus, it follows that
F
-manifold algebras are related to pre-Lie algebras in the same way Poisson algebras are related to associative algebras.

1.2.
Rota-Baxter deformations and pre-algebras

The notion of
Rota-Baxter operators
originated in the probability study of Baxter
[
4
]
, promoted by the combinatorial study of Rota
[
21
]
, and found important applications in mathematics and mathematical physics. In
[
1
]
, Aguiar first observed that a Rota-Baxter operator on an associative algebra deforms the structure into a
dendriform algebra
, a structure that splits the usual associativity. More generally, a Rota-Baxter operator on a class of binary quadratic nonsymmetric operads gives the black square product of dendriform algebra with these operads
[
9
,
24
]
. For arbitrary binary quadratic operads, the above result was generalized in
[
2
]
, hence setting the general operadic definition for the notion of splitting of algebraic structures. More generally, given a binary quadratic operad
\mathcal{P}
, they consider an operad
\mathrm{BSu}(\mathcal{P})
the bisuccessor of
\mathcal{P}
. It has been observed that the operad
\mathrm{BSu}(\mathcal{P})
is equivalent to taking the Manin black product of the operad
PreLie
and the operad
\mathcal{P}
. The algebras over the operad
\mathrm{BSu}(\mathcal{P})
are called pre-
\mathcal{P}
-algebras. When
\mathcal{P}=Ass,Com,Lie
, the corresponding pre-
\mathcal{P}
-algebras are respectively dendriform algebras, zinbiel algebras and pre-Lie algebras. When
\mathcal{P}=PreLie
, the corresponding pre-
\mathcal{P}
-algebra is called an
L-dendriform algebra
introduced in
[
3
]
. In
[
1
]
, Aguiar introduced the notion of a
pre-Poisson algebra
by combining a zinbiel algebra and a pre-Lie algebra structure on the same vector space. Pre-Poisson algebras are nothing but pre-
\mathcal{P}
-algebras for
\mathcal{P}=Poiss
, the operad for Poisson algebras. He also showed that the semi-classical limit of dendriform deformation of a zinbiel algebra inherits a pre-Poisson algebra structure.

1.3.
Nijenhuis deformations, twisted Rota-Baxter deformations and NS-algebras

The notion of
NS-algebras
(also called NS-associative algebras) was introduced by Leroux
[
17
]
in the study of Nijenhuis operators on associative algebras. Roughly, an NS-algebra is a vector space equipped with three bilinear operations
\prec,\succ
and
\curlyvee
satisfying certain axioms that imply that the new operation
\ast=\prec+\succ+\curlyvee
is associative. NS-algebras generalize dendriform algebras and split the associativity. In
[
23
]
, Uchino also considered NS-algebras and showed that NS-algebras can be obtained from twisted Rota-Baxter operators (in particular, Reynolds operators) on associative algebras. Recently, the author in
[
6
]
introduced the notion of an
NS-Lie algebra
while studying Nijenhuis operators and twisted Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras. NS-Lie algebras simultaneously generalize Lie algebras and pre-Lie algebras. Some more details about NS-algebras, NS-Lie algebras and other generalizations can be found in
[
6
,
7
,
10
,
20
]
. The above discussions lead to the question about the effect of Nijenhuis operators and twisted Rota-Baxter operators on a binary quadratic operad
\mathcal{P}
. This is equivalent to understanding NS-
\mathcal{P}
-algebras for any binary quadratic operad
\mathcal{P}
. However, unlike pre-
\mathcal{P}
-algebras, there has been no well-known description of NS-
\mathcal{P}
-algebras in terms of the operad
\mathcal{P}
. Thus, to understand NS-
\mathcal{P}
-algebras for some given
\mathcal{P}
, one needs to explicitly write down the effect of Nijenhuis operators on
\mathcal{P}
-algebras. For instance, the authors in
[
11
]
considered
NS-pre-Lie algebras
in their study of Nijenhuis operators and twisted Rota-Baxter operators on pre-Lie algebras.

1.4.
NS-Poisson algebras and NS-
F
-manifold algebras

Our aim in this paper is to study the effect of Nijenhuis operators on Poisson algebras and
F
-manifold algebras. We first introduce the notion of an
NS-Poisson algebra
by combining NS-commutative algebra and NS-Lie algebra on the same vector space (cf. Definition
3.1
). Our notion of NS-Poisson algebras generalizes both Poisson algebras and pre-Poisson algebras. We show that an NS-Poisson algebra gives rise to a Poisson algebra structure on the underlying vector space, called the
subadjacent
Poisson algebra (cf. Theorem
3.3
). Thus, an NS-Poisson algebra can be realized as a splitting of a Poisson algebra. On the other hand, any Nijenhuis operator on a Poisson algebra deforms the structure into an NS-Poisson algebra (cf. Proposition
3.4
). It follows that an NS-Poisson algebra is the underlying structure of Nijenhuis deformations of a Poisson algebra. Next, we consider twisted Rota-Baxter operators on Poisson algebras and show that they also induce NS-Poisson algebras (cf. Theorem
3.11
). Conversely, any NS-Poisson algebra arise from a twisted Rota-Baxter operator (cf. Proposition
3.12
). Since a Reynolds operator on a Poisson algebra can be regarded as a twisted Rota-Baxter operator, it also induces an NS-Poisson algebra structure (cf. Proposition
3.14
). Next, we consider NS-algebra deformations of an NS-commutative algebra and show that NS-Poisson algebras are the corresponding semi-classical limit (cf. Theorem
4.1
). Finally, we show that a suitable filtration of an NS-algebra induces an NS-Poisson algebra structure on the associated graded vector space (cf. Theorem
4.2
).

By viewing
F
-manifold algebras as a generalization of Poisson algebras, one could also study the effect of Nijenhuis operators on an
F
-manifold algebra. In another part of the paper, we introduce the notion of an
NS-
F
-manifold algebra
as a simultaneous generalization of
F
-manifold algebras, pre-
F
-manifold algebras
[

]
and NS-Poisson algebras. Any NS-

-manifold algebra naturally gives rise to an

5.4
). We show that NS-

5.5
and

In

, Aguiar also introduced the notion of a dual pre-Poisson algebra. It has been observed that an averaging operator on a Poisson algebra gives rise to a dual pre-Poisson algebra. Generalizing his results, the authors of

2.
Pre-algebras and NS-algebras

In this section, we recall various pre-algebras (e.g. zinbiel algebras, pre-Lie algebras and pre-Poisson algebras), NS-algebras and NS-Lie algebras.

2.1 Definition
.

A
(left) zinbiel algebra
is a pair
(A,\ast)
of a vector space
A
with a bilinear operation
\ast:A\times A\rightarrow A
satisfying

(4)

x\ast(y\ast z)=(x\ast y+y\ast x)\ast z,\text{ for }x,y,z\in A.

2.2 Definition
.

A
(left) pre-Lie algebra
is a pair
(A,\diamond)
consisting of a vector space
A
equipped with a bilinear operation
\diamond:A\times A\rightarrow A
satisfying the following identity:

(5)

x\diamond(y\diamond z)-(x\diamond y)\diamond z=y\diamond(x\diamond z)-(y\diamond x)\diamond z,\text{ for }x,y,z\in A.

2.3 Definition
.

A
(left) pre-Poisson algebra
is a triple
(A,\ast,\diamond)
in which
(A,\ast)
is a zinbiel algebra and
(A,\diamond)
is a pre-Lie algebra satisfying the following compatibilities: for any
x,y,z\in A
,

(6)

\displaystyle(x\diamond y-y\diamond x)\ast z=~

\displaystyle x\diamond(y\ast z)-y\ast(x\diamond z),

(7)

\displaystyle(x\ast y+y\ast x)\diamond z=~

\displaystyle x\ast(y\diamond z)+y\ast(x\diamond z).

Let
(A,\ast,\diamond)
be a pre-Poisson algebra. Then it has been shown in
[
1
]
that
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
becomes a Poisson algebra, where

\displaystyle x\cdot y:=x\ast y+y\ast x\quad\text{ and }\quad\{x,y\}:=x\diamond y-y\diamond x,\text{ for }x,y\in A.

This is called the sub-adjacent Poisson algebra of the pre-Poisson algebra
(A,\ast,\diamond)
.

See
[
1
]
for some examples of pre-Poisson algebras. In particular, a Rota-Baxter operator on a Poisson algebra gives rise to a pre-Poisson algebra. Next, we consider NS-algebras introduced by Leroux
[
17
]
and NS-Lie algebras introduced in
[
6
]
.

2.4 Definition
.

An
NS-associative algebra
(or simply an
NS-algebra
) is a quadruple
(A,\prec,\succ,\curlyvee)
consisting of a vector space
A
with three bilinear operations
\prec,\succ,\curlyvee:A\times A\rightarrow A
subject to satisfy the following identities:

(8)

\displaystyle(x\prec y)\prec z=

\displaystyle~x\prec(y\odot z),

(9)

\displaystyle(x\succ y)\prec z=

\displaystyle~x\succ(y\prec z),

(10)

\displaystyle(x\odot y)\succ z=

\displaystyle~x\succ(y\succ z),

(11)

\displaystyle(x\curlyvee y)\prec z+(x\odot y)\curlyvee z=

\displaystyle~x\succ(y\curlyvee z)+x\curlyvee(y\odot z),

for
x,y,z\in A
, where we used the notation
x\odot y=x\prec y+x\succ y+x\curlyvee y
.

2.5 Remark
.

Let
(A,\prec,\succ,\curlyvee)
be an NS-algebra. Then it follows from the identities (
8
)-(
11
) that the bilinear operation
\odot
is associative.

For an NS-algebra
(A,\prec,\succ,\curlyvee)
, if the symmetries
x\prec y=y\succ x
and
x\curlyvee y=y\curlyvee x
hold for all
x,y\in A
, then we call it as an NS-commutative algebra. Thus, an
NS-commutative algebra
can be described by a triple
(A,\ast,\curlyvee)
, where
\ast,\curlyvee:A\times A\rightarrow A
are bilinear operations in which
\curlyvee
is commutative (i.e.
x\curlyvee y=y\curlyvee x
, for all
x,y\in A
) satisfying the following identities:

(12)

\displaystyle x\ast(y\ast z)=~

\displaystyle(x\odot y)\ast z,

(13)

\displaystyle x\ast(y\curlyvee z)+x\curlyvee(y\odot z)=~

\displaystyle y\ast(x\curlyvee z)+y\curlyvee(x\odot z),

for all
x,y,z\in A
. Here
x\odot y=x\ast y+y\ast x+x\curlyvee y
.

Any NS-commutative algebra
(A,\ast,\curlyvee)
with trivial
\curlyvee
is simply a zinbiel algebra. Thus, NS-commutative algebras are a generalization of zinbiel algebras.

2.6 Remark
.

Let
(A,\ast,\curlyvee)
be an NS-commutative algebra. Then
(A,\odot)
is a commutative associative algebra, called the subadjacent algebra. Moreover, the map
\mu:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(A),x\mapsto\mu_{x}
given by

(14)

\displaystyle\mu_{x}(y):=x\ast y,\text{~~~for~}x,y\in A,

defines a representation of the commutative associative algebra
(A,\odot)
on the vector space
A
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a commutative associative algebra. A linear map
N:A\rightarrow A
is said to be a
Nijenhuis operator
on
A
if it satisfies

(15)

\displaystyle N(x)\cdot N(y)=N(N(x)\cdot y+x\cdot N(y)-N(x\cdot y)),\text{ for all }x,y\in A.

2.7 Proposition
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a commutative associative algebra and
N:A\rightarrow A
be a Nijenhuis operator on it. We define bilinear operations
\ast,\curlyvee:A\times A\rightarrow A
by

\displaystyle x\ast y=N(x)\cdot y\text{~~and~~}x\curlyvee y=-N(x\cdot y),\text{ for }x,y\in A.

Then
(A,\ast,\curlyvee)
is an NS-commutative algebra. Moreover, the corresponding subadjacent commutative associative algebra is given by
(A,\cdot_{N})
, where
x\cdot_{N}y:=N(x)\cdot y+x\cdot N(y)-N(x\cdot y)
, for
x,y\in A
. Additionally, we have
N(x\cdot_{N}y)=N(x)\cdot N(y),
for
x,y\in A
.

2.8 Definition
.

An
NS-Lie algebra
is a triple
(A,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
consisting of a vector space
A
with two bilinear operations
\diamond,\smallblackdiamond:A\times A\rightarrow A
in which the operation
\smallblackdiamond
is skew-symmetric and the following identities hold:

(16)

\displaystyle x\diamond(y\diamond z)-(x\diamond y)\diamond z-y\diamond(x\diamond z)+(y\diamond x)\diamond z=(x\smallblackdiamond y)\diamond z,

(17)

\displaystyle x\smallblackdiamond\{\!\!\{y,z\}\!\!\}+y\smallblackdiamond\{\!\!\{z,x\}\!\!\}+z\smallblackdiamond\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}+x\diamond(y\smallblackdiamond z)+y\diamond(z\smallblackdiamond x)+z\diamond(x\smallblackdiamond y)=0,

for
x,y,z\in A
. Here we used the notation
\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}=x\diamond y-y\diamond x+x\smallblackdiamond y
.

Any NS-Lie algebra
(A,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
with trivial
\smallblackdiamond
is simply a pre-Lie algebra. On the other hand, an NS-Lie algebra
(A,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
with trivial
\diamond
is nothing but a Lie algebra. Thus, NS-Lie algebras are a generalization of both pre-Lie algebras and Lie algebras.

2.9 Remark
.

Let
(A,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
be an NS-Lie algebra. Then it has been observed in
[
6
]
that

(A,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
is a Lie algebra. This is called the subadjacent Lie algebra of
(A,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
and it is denoted by
A^{c}
.
Moreover, the map
\rho:A^{c}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(A),x\mapsto\rho_{x}
given by

(18)

\rho_{x}(y)=x\diamond y,\text{ ~for~}x,y\in A,

defines a representation of the subadjacent Lie algebra
A^{c}
on the vector space
A
.

Let
(A,\{~,~\})
be a Lie algebra. A linear map
N:A\rightarrow A
is called a
Nijenhuis operator
on the Lie algebra
(A,\{~,~\})
if it satisfies

(19)

\displaystyle\{N(x),N(y)\}=N(\{N(x),y\}+\{x,N(y)\}-N\{x,y\}),\text{ for all }x,y\in A.

The following result has been proved in
[
6
]
.

2.10 Proposition
.

Let
(A,\{~,~\})
be a Lie algebra and
N:A\rightarrow A
be a Nijenhuis operator on it. We define bilinear operations
\diamond,\smallblackdiamond:A\times A\rightarrow A
by

\displaystyle x\diamond y=\{N(x),y\}\text{~~and~~}x\smallblackdiamond y=-N\{x,y\},\text{ for }x,y\in A.

Then
(A,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
is an NS-Lie algebra. Moreover, the corresponding subadjacent Lie algebra is given by
(A,\{~,~\}_{N})
, where
\{x,y\}_{N}:=\{N(x),y\}+\{x,N(y)\}-N\{x,y\}
, for
x,y\in A
. Additionally, we have
N(\{x,y\}_{N})=\{N(x),N(y)\}
, for
x,y\in A
.

3.
NS-Poisson algebras

In this section, we introduce NS-Poisson algebras as a generalization of both pre-Poisson algebras and Poisson algebras. We show that an NS-Poisson algebra naturally induces a Poisson algebra structure. Further, NS-Poisson algebras can be seen as the underlying structure of Nijenhuis operators and twisted Rota-Baxter operators on Poisson algebras. In fact, we observe that any NS-Poisson algebra arises from a twisted Rota-Baxter operator. We end this section by considering NS-Gerstenhaber algebras as the graded version of NS-Poisson algebras.

3.1 Definition
.

An
NS-Poisson algebra
is a quintuple
(A,\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
consisting of a vector space
A
with four bilinear operations
\ast
,
\curlyvee
,
\diamond
,
\smallblackdiamond

:A\times A\rightarrow A
such that

(A,\ast,\curlyvee)
is an NS-commutative algebra,
(A,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
is an NS-Lie algebra and the following compatibilities hold:

(NSP1)

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}\ast z=

\displaystyle~x\diamond(y\ast z)-y\ast(x\diamond z),

(NSP2)

\displaystyle(x\odot y)\diamond z=

\displaystyle~x\ast(y\diamond z)+y\ast(x\diamond z),

(NSP3)

\displaystyle x\smallblackdiamond(y\odot z)+x\diamond(y\curlyvee z)=

\displaystyle~\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}\curlyvee z+z\ast(x\smallblackdiamond y)+y\curlyvee\{\!\!\{x,z\}\!\!\}+y\ast(x\smallblackdiamond z),

for
x,y,z\in A
. Here
x\odot y=x\ast y+y\ast x+x\curlyvee y
and
\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}=x\diamond y-y\diamond x+x\smallblackdiamond y
.

3.2 Remark
.

Let
(A,\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
be an NS-Poisson algebra in which
\curlyvee
and
\smallblackdiamond
are trivial. Then
(A,\ast,\diamond)
turns out to be a pre-Poisson algebra. On the other hand, if
(A,\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
is an NS-Poisson algebra in which
\ast
and
\diamond
are trivial then
(A,\curlyvee,\smallblackdiamond=\{~,~\})
becomes a Poisson algebra. Thus, NS-Poisson algebras can be regarded as a common generalization of both pre-Poisson algebras and Poisson algebras.

3.3 Theorem
.

Let
(A,\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
be an NS-Poisson algebra.

(i)

Then
(A,\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
is a Poisson algebra, where
\odot
and
\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\}
are defined in Definition
3.1
. (This is called the sub-adjacent Poisson algebra of
(A,\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
and is denoted by
A^{c}
.)

(ii)

Moreover, the triple
(A,\mu,\rho)
is a representation of the sub-adjacent Poisson algebra
A^{c}
, where
\mu
and
\rho
are given by (
14
) and (
18
), respectively.

Proof.

(i) Since
(A,\ast,\curlyvee)
is an NS-commutative algebra, it follows that
(A,\odot)
is a commutative associative algebra. On the other hand,

(A,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
is an NS-Lie algebra implies that
(A,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
is a Lie algebra. To show that
(A,\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
is a Poisson algebra, we need to verify the Leibniz rule. For any
x,y,z\in A
, we have

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}\odot z+\{\!\!\{x,z\}\!\!\}\odot y

\displaystyle=\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}\ast z+z\ast\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}+\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}\curlyvee z+\{\!\!\{x,z\}\!\!\}\ast y+y\ast\{\!\!\{x,z\}\!\!\}+\{\!\!\{x,z\}\!\!\}\curlyvee y

\displaystyle=\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}\ast z+z\ast(x\diamond y)-z\ast(y\diamond x)+z\ast(x\smallblackdiamond y)+\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}\curlyvee z+\{\!\!\{x,z\}\!\!\}\ast y

\displaystyle\quad+y\ast(x\diamond z)-y\ast(z\diamond x)+y\ast(x\smallblackdiamond z)+\{\!\!\{x,z\}\!\!\}\curlyvee y

\displaystyle=\big(\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}\ast z+y\ast(x\diamond z)\big)+\big(z\ast(x\diamond y)+\{\!\!\{x,z\}\!\!\}\ast y\big)-\big(z\ast(y\diamond x)+y\ast(z\diamond x)\big)

\displaystyle\quad+\big(z\ast(x\smallblackdiamond y)+\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}\curlyvee z+y\ast(x\smallblackdiamond z)+\{\!\!\{x,z\}\!\!\}\curlyvee y\big)

\displaystyle=x\diamond(y\ast z)+x\diamond(z\ast y)-(y\odot z)\diamond x+x\smallblackdiamond(y\odot z)+x\diamond(y\curlyvee z)

\displaystyle=x\diamond(y\odot z)-(y\odot z)\diamond x+x\smallblackdiamond(y\odot z)

\displaystyle=\{\!\!\{x,y\odot z\}\!\!\}

which verifies the Leibniz rule. Hence
(A,\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
is a Poisson algebra.

(ii) We have already seen in Remark
2.6
that
(A,\mu)
defines a representation of
(A,\odot)
. Similarly, in Remark
2.9
we have seen that
(A,\rho)
defines a representation of the Lie algebra
(A,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
. Thus, to show that
(A,\mu,\rho)
is a representation of the sub-adjacent Poisson algebra
A^{c}=(A,\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
, we need to verify the identities (
2
) and (
3
).

Since
(A,\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
is an NS-Poisson algebra, it follows from (
NSP1
) that

\displaystyle\mu_{\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}}(z)=(\rho_{x}\circ\mu_{y}-\mu_{y}\circ\rho_{x})(z),\text{ for }x,y,z\in A.

Hence the identity (
2
) follows. On the other hand, it follows from (
NSP2
) that

\displaystyle\rho_{x\odot y}(z)=(\mu_{x}\circ\rho_{y}+\mu_{y}\circ\rho_{x})(z),\text{ for }x,y,z\in A.

This verifies the identity (
3
). Hence complete the proof.
∎

The above construction of the sub-adjacent Poisson algebra of an NS-Poisson algebra generalizes the similar construction for pre-Poisson algebras.

In the following, we consider Nijenhuis operators on a Poisson algebra and show that a Nijenhuis operator induces an NS-Poisson algebra structure.
Let
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra. Recall that a
Nijenhuis operator
on the Poisson algebra
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
is a linear map
N:A\rightarrow A
that is a Nijenhuis operator on both the commutative associative algebra
(A,\cdot)
and the Lie algebra
(A,\{~,~\})
. Thus,
N
is a Nijenhuis operator if both the identities (
15
) and (
19
) hold.

3.4 Proposition
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra and
N:A\rightarrow A
be a Nijenhuis operator on it. Define four bilinear operations
\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond:A\times A\rightarrow A
by

x\ast y=N(x)\cdot y,\quad x\curlyvee y=-N(x\cdot y),\quad x\diamond y=\{N(x),y\}\quad\text{ and }\quad x\smallblackdiamond y=-N\{x,y\},\text{ for }x,y\in A.

Then
(A,\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
is an NS-Poisson algebra.

Proof.

Since
N:A\rightarrow A
is a Nijenhuis operator on the commutative associative algebra
(A,\cdot)
, it follows from Proposition
2.7
that
(A,\ast,\curlyvee)
is an NS-commutative algebra. On the other hand, since
N
is also a Nijenhuis operator on the Lie algebra
(A,\{~,~\})
, it follows from Proposition
2.10
that
(A,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
is an NS-Lie algebra. Thus, to show that
(A,\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
is an NS-Poisson algebra, we need to verify the identities (
NSP1
), (
NSP2
) and (
NSP3
). For any
x,y,z\in A
, we first observe that

\displaystyle x\diamond(y\ast z)-y\ast(x\diamond z)

\displaystyle=\{N(x),N(y)\cdot z\}-N(y)\cdot\{N(x),z\}

\displaystyle=\{N(x),N(y)\}\cdot z

\displaystyle=N(\{x,y\}_{N})\cdot z

\displaystyle=\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}\ast z.

This verifies the identity (
NSP1
). Similarly, we have

\displaystyle x\ast(y\diamond z)+y\ast(x\diamond z)

\displaystyle=N(x)\cdot\{N(y),z\}+N(y)\cdot\{N(x),z\}

\displaystyle=\{N(x)\cdot N(y),z\}

\displaystyle=\{N(x\cdot_{N}y),z\}

\displaystyle=(x\odot y)\diamond z,

which verifies (
NSP2
). Finally, we have

\displaystyle x\smallblackdiamond(y\odot z)+x\diamond(y\curlyvee z)-\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}\curlyvee z-z\ast(x\smallblackdiamond y)-y\curlyvee\{\!\!\{x,z\}\!\!\}-y\ast(x\smallblackdiamond z)

\displaystyle=-N\{x,y\cdot_{N}z\}-\{N(x),N(y\cdot z)\}+N(\{x,y\}_{N}\cdot z)+N(z)\cdot N\{x,y\}+N(y\cdot\{x,z\}_{N})

\displaystyle\quad+N(y)\cdot N\{x,z\}

\displaystyle=N\big(-\{x,y\cdot_{N}z\}-\{x,N(y\cdot z)\}-\{N(x),y\cdot z\}+N\{x,y\cdot z\}+\{x,y\}_{N}\cdot z+z\cdot N\{x,y\}

\displaystyle\quad+N(z)\cdot\{x,y\}-N(z\cdot\{x,y\})+y\cdot\{x,z\}_{N}+y\cdot N\{x,z\}+N(y)\cdot\{x,z\}-N(y\cdot\{x,z\})\big)

\displaystyle=N\Big(\big(-\{x,y\cdot_{N}z\}-\{x,N(y\cdot z)\}\big)-\{N(x),y\cdot z\}+N\big({\{x,y\cdot z\}}-{z\cdot\{x,y\}}-{y\cdot\{x,z\}}\big)

\displaystyle\quad+\big(\{x,y\}_{N}\cdot z+z\cdot N\{x,y\}\big)+N(z)\cdot\{x,y\}+\big(y\cdot\{x,z\}_{N}+y\cdot N\{x,z\}\big)+N(y)\cdot\{x,z\}\Big)

\displaystyle=N\big(-\{x,N(y)\cdot z\}-\{x,y\cdot N(z)\}-\{N(x),y\cdot z\}+\{N(x),y\}\cdot z+\{x,N(y)\}\cdot z+N(z)\cdot\{x,y\}

\displaystyle\quad+y\cdot\{N(x),z\}+y\cdot\{x,N(z)\}+N(y)\cdot\{x,z\}\big)

\displaystyle=0.

Hence the identity (
NSP3
) also holds. This completes the proof.
∎

Let
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra and
N:A\rightarrow A
be a Nijenhuis operator on it. Then it follows from the above proposition that
(A,\cdot_{N},\{~,~\}_{N})
is a Poisson algebra, where

\displaystyle x\cdot_{N}y=N(x)\cdot y+x\cdot N(y)-N(x\cdot y)~~~\text{ and }~~~\{x,y\}_{N}=\{N(x),y\}+\{x,N(y)\}-N\{x,y\},

for
x,y\in A
. The Poisson algebra
(A,\cdot_{N},\{~,~\}_{N})
is said to be the
Nijenhuis deformation
of
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
by the Nijenhuis operator
N
.

It is well-known
[
16
]
that a Nijenhuis operator on a commutative associative algebra (resp. Lie algebra) gives rise to a hierarchy of new commutative associative algebra structures (resp. Lie algebra structures) on the underlying vector space. In the following, we generalize this result in the context of Poisson algebras using the help of NS-Poisson algebras. We start with the following notion.

3.5 Definition
.

Let
(A,\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
and
(A,\ast^{\prime},\curlyvee^{\prime},\diamond^{\prime},\smallblackdiamond^{\prime})
be two NS-Poisson algebra structures on a vector space
A
. They are said to be
compatible
if
(A,\ast+\ast^{\prime},\curlyvee+\curlyvee^{\prime},\diamond+\diamond^{\prime},\smallblackdiamond+\smallblackdiamond^{\prime})
is also an NS-Poisson algebra.

With the above definition, we have the following simple result.

3.6 Proposition
.

Let
(A,\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
and
(A,\ast^{\prime},\curlyvee^{\prime},\diamond^{\prime},\smallblackdiamond^{\prime})
be compatible NS-Poisson algebra structures on
A
. Then the corresponding sub-adjacent Poisson algebras
(A,\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
and
(A,\odot^{\prime},\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\}^{\prime})
are also compatible in the sense that
(A,\odot+\odot^{\prime},\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\}+\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\}^{\prime})
is a Poisson algebra.

3.7 Proposition
.

Let
(A,\cdot.\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra and
N:A\rightarrow A
be a Nijenhuis operator on it. Then we have the following.

(i)

For any
k\geq 0
, the map
N^{k}:A\rightarrow A
is also a Nijenhuis operator on the Poisson algebra
A
. Hence one obtains the induced NS-Poisson algebra
(A,\ast_{N^{k}},\curlyvee_{N^{k}},\diamond_{N^{k}},\smallblackdiamond_{N^{k}})
and the corresponding sub-adjacent Poisson algebra
(A,\cdot_{N^{k}},\{~,~\}_{N^{k}})
.

(ii)

For any
k,l\geq 0
, the NS-Poisson algebras
(A,\ast_{N^{k}},\curlyvee_{N^{k}},\diamond_{N^{k}},\smallblackdiamond_{N^{k}})
and
(A,\ast_{N^{l}},\curlyvee_{N^{l}},\diamond_{N^{l}},\smallblackdiamond_{N^{l}})
are compatible. As a result, the corresponding sub-adjacent Poisson algebras
(A,\cdot_{N^{k}},\{~,~\}_{N^{k}})
and
(A,\cdot_{N^{l}},\{~,~\}_{N^{l}})
are compatible.

Proof.

(i) Since
N:A\rightarrow A
is a Nijenhuis operator on the commutative associative algebra
(A,\cdot)

(
resp. on the Lie algebra
(A,\{~,~\})
)
, it follows that
N^{k}
is also a Nijenhuis operator on
(A,\cdot)

(
resp. on
(A,\{~,~\})
)

[
16
]
. Hence the result follows.

(ii) Since
N
is a Nijenhuis operator on both the commutative associative algebra
(A,\cdot)
and on the Lie algebra
(A,\{~,~\})
, the Nijenhuis operators
N^{k}
and
N^{l}
are compatible Nijenhuis operators on them
[
16
]
. In other words, the sum
N^{k}+N^{l}
is also a Nijenhuis operator on both
(A,\cdot)
and
(A,\{~,~\})
. The NS-Poisson algebra structure induced by
N^{k}+N^{l}
is given by

\displaystyle(A,\ast_{N^{k}}+\ast_{N^{l}},\curlyvee_{N^{k}}+\curlyvee_{N^{l}},\diamond_{N^{k}}+\diamond_{N^{l}},\smallblackdiamond_{N^{k}}+\smallblackdiamond_{N^{l}}).

This shows that the NS-Poisson algebras
(A,\ast_{N^{k}},\curlyvee_{N^{k}},\diamond_{N^{k}},\smallblackdiamond_{N^{k}})
and
(A,\ast_{N^{l}},\curlyvee_{N^{l}},\diamond_{N^{l}},\smallblackdiamond_{N^{l}})
are compatible. The last part follows from Proposition
3.6
.
∎

3.8 Example
.

Let
A
be a
4
-dimensional vector space with basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
. Define a commutative associative multiplication and a Lie bracket on
A
by

\displaystyle e_{1}\cdot e_{1}=e_{2},\quad e_{1}\cdot e_{2}=e_{2}\cdot e_{1}=e_{3},

\displaystyle\{e_{1},e_{4}\}=ae_{3}+be_{4}=-\{e_{4},e_{1}\},

for some constants
a,b\in{\bf k}
. Then
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
is a Poisson algebra. Moreover, the map
N:A\rightarrow A
given by

\displaystyle N(e_{1})=re_{1},\quad N(e_{2})=re_{2}+se_{3}+te_{4},\quad N(e_{3})=re_{3},\quad N(e_{4})=re_{4}

is a Nijenhuis operator on the Poisson algebra
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
. Thus, by Proposition
3.4
, we have that
(A,\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
is an NS-Poisson algebra, where

\displaystyle e_{1}\ast e_{1}=re_{2},\quad e_{1}\ast e_{2}=e_{2}\ast e_{1}=re_{3},

\displaystyle e_{1}\curlyvee e_{1}=-re_{2}-se_{3}-te_{4},\quad e_{1}\curlyvee e_{2}=e_{2}\curlyvee e_{1}=-re_{3},

\displaystyle e_{1}\diamond e_{4}=rae_{3}+rbe_{4}=-e_{4}\diamond e_{1},\quad e_{2}\diamond e_{1}=-tae_{3}-tbe_{4},

\displaystyle e_{1}\smallblackdiamond e_{4}=-rae_{3}-rbe_{4}=-e_{4}\smallblackdiamond e_{1}.

In
[
23
]
, Uchino considered twisted Rota-Baxter operators on associative algebras and showed that they induce NS-algebras. Since Reynolds operators can be realized as twisted Rota-Baxter operators, they also induce NS-algebras.
These results were generalized to the context of Lie algebras in
[
6
]
. Here we will consider twisted Rota-Baxter operators and Reynolds operators on Poisson algebras and show that they induce NS-Poisson algebras.
First, recall that the cohomology of a Poisson algebra with coefficients in a representation was given by
[
13
]
. To define twisted Rota-Baxter operators on Poisson algebras, we only require the Poisson
2
-cocycles. Let
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra and
V=(V,\mu,\rho)
be a representation. A
Poisson
2
-cocycle
of
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
with coefficients in the representation
V
is a pair
(h,H)
, where

(i)
h\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes 2},V)
is a
2
-cocycle on the Harrison complex of the commutative associative algebra
(A,\cdot)
with coefficients in the representation
(V,\mu)
, i.e.

(20)

\displaystyle h(x,y)=h(y,x)\quad\text{ and }\quad\mu_{x}h(y,z)+h(x,y\cdot z)=\mu_{y}h(x,z)+h(y,x\cdot z),\text{ for }x,y,z\in A,

(ii)
H\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}A,V)
is a
2
-cocycle on the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of the Lie algebra
(A,\{~,~\})
with coefficients in the representation
(V,\rho)
, i.e.

(21)

\displaystyle\rho_{x}H(y,z)+\rho_{y}H(z,x)+\rho_{z}H(x,y)+H(x,\{y,z\})+H(y,\{z,z\})+H(z,\{x,y\})=0,\text{ for }x,y,z\in A,

that satisfy the following compatibility:

(22)

\displaystyle H(x,y\cdot z)+\rho_{x}h(y,z)=h(\{x,y\},z)+\mu_{z}H(x,y)+h(y,\{x,z\}+\mu_{y}H(x,z),\text{ for all }x,y,z\in A.

3.9 Remark
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra. Then its adjoint representation (also called the regular representation) is given by
(A,\mu,\rho)
, where
\mu_{x}y=x\cdot y
and
\rho_{x}y=\{x,y\}
, for
x,y\in A
. It is easy to observe that the pair
(h,H)=(\cdot,\{~,~\})
is a Poisson
2
-cocycle of
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
with coefficients in the adjoint representation.

3.10 Definition
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra and
(V,\mu,\rho)
be a representation of it. Suppose
(h,H)
is a Poisson
2
-cocycle of
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
with coefficients in the representation
V
. A linear map
R:V\rightarrow A
is said to be a
(h,H)
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator
on
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
with respect to the representation
V
if it satisfies

(23)

\displaystyle R(u)\cdot R(v)=~

\displaystyle R\big(\mu_{R(u)}v+\mu_{R(v)}u+h(R(u),R(v))\big),

(24)

\displaystyle\{R(u),R(v)\}=~

\displaystyle R\big(\rho_{R(u)}v-\rho_{R(v)}u+H(R(u),R(v))\big),

for all
u,v\in V
.

3.11 Theorem
.

Let
R:V\rightarrow A
be a
(h,H)
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator on
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
with respect to the representation
V
. Then the quintuple
(V,\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
is an NS-Poisson algebra, where

\displaystyle u\ast v:=~

\displaystyle\mu_{R(u)}v,\qquad u\curlyvee v:=h(R(u),R(v)),\qquad u\diamond v:=\rho_{R(u)}v

\displaystyle\text{ and }~~~~u\smallblackdiamond v:=H(R(u),R(v)),\text{ for }u,v\in V.

Proof.

For any
u,v,w\in V
, we have

\displaystyle u\ast(v\ast w)=\mu_{R(u)}\mu_{R(v)}w=\mu_{R(u)\cdot R(v)}w\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{first-tw})}}{{=}}\mu_{R(u\odot v)}w=(u\odot v)\ast v

and

\displaystyle u\ast(v\curlyvee w)+u\curlyvee(v\odot w)-v\ast(u\curlyvee w)-v\curlyvee(u\odot w)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{first-tw})}}{{=}}\mu_{R(u)}h(R(v),R(w))+h(R(u),R(v)\cdot R(w))-\mu_{R(v)}h(R(u),R(w))-h(R(v),R(u)\cdot R(w))=0.

This shows that
(V,\ast,\curlyvee)
is an NS-commutative algebra. On the other hand,
R
satisfies (
24
) implies that
(V,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
is an NS-Lie algebra (see
[
6
, Proposition 6.7]
). Thus, we are only left to verify the identities (
NSP1
), (
NSP2
) and (
NSP3
).

For any
u,v,w\in V
, we observe that

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{u,v\}\!\!\}\ast w-u\diamond(v\ast w)-v\ast(u\diamond w)

\displaystyle=\mu_{R\{\!\!\{u,v\}\!\!\}}w-\rho_{R(u)}\rho_{R(v)}w-\mu_{R(v)}\rho_{R(u)}w

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{second-tw})}}{{=}}\mu_{\{R(u),R(v)\}}w-\rho_{R(u)}\rho_{R(v)}w-\mu_{R(v)}\rho_{R(u)}w\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{rep-pois1})}}{{=}}0

and

\displaystyle(u\odot v)\diamond w-u\ast(v\diamond w)-v\ast(u\diamond w)

\displaystyle=\rho_{R(u\odot v)}w-\mu_{R(u)}\mu_{R(v)}w-\mu_{R(v)}\mu_{R(u)}w

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{first-tw})}}{{=}}\rho_{R(u)\cdot R(v)}w-\mu_{R(u)}\mu_{R(v)}w-\mu_{R(v)}\mu_{R(u)}w\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{rep-pois2})}}{{=}}0

which proves (
NSP1
) and (
NSP2
). Finally, we have

\displaystyle u\smallblackdiamond(v\odot w)+u\diamond(v\curlyvee w)-\{\!\!\{u,v\}\!\!\}\curlyvee w-w\ast(u\smallblackdiamond v)-v\curlyvee\{\!\!\{u,w\}\!\!\}-v\ast(u\smallblackdiamond w)

\displaystyle=H(R(u),R(v)\cdot R(w))+\rho_{R(u)}h(R(v),R(w))-h(\{R(u),R(v)\},R(w))-\mu_{R(w)}H(R(u),R(v))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-h(R(v),\{R(u),R(w)\})-\mu_{R(v)}H(R(u),R(w))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{comp-2co})}}{{=}}0

which verifies (
NSP3
). Hence the result follows.
∎

In the above theorem, we showed that a twisted Rota-Baxter operator on a Poisson algebra with respect to a representation induces an NS-Poisson algebra structure on the representation space. In the following, we prove the converse. Namely, we show that any NS-Poisson algebra is always induced by a twisted Rota-Baxter operator. Let
(A,\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
be any NS-Poisson algebra. Consider the subadjacent Poisson algebra
(A,\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
given in Theorem
3.3
. We have also seen that the maps
\mu,\rho:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(A)
given by

\displaystyle\mu_{x}y=x\ast y~~~~\text{ and }~~~~\rho_{x}y=x\diamond y,\text{ for }x,y\in A,

makes the triple
(A,\mu,\rho)
into a representation of the subadjacent Poisson algebra
(A,\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
. We now define maps
h\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes 2},A)
and
H\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}A,A)
by

\displaystyle h(x,y)=x\curlyvee y~~~~\text{ and }~~~~H(x,y)=x\smallblackdiamond y,\text{ for }x,y\in A.

3.12 Proposition
.

With the above notations, the pair
(h,H)
is a Poisson
2
-cocycle of
(A,\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
with coefficients in the representation
(A,\mu,\rho)
.

Moreover, the identity map
\mathrm{Id}:A\rightarrow A
is a
(h,H)
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator on
(A,\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
with respect to the representation
(A,\mu,\rho)
. Further, the induced NS-Poisson algebra structure on
A
coincides with the given one.

Proof.

Since
(A,\ast,\curlyvee)
is an NS-commutative algebra, it follows that the map
h
satisfies (
20
). Similarly,
(A,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
is an NS-Lie algebra implies that the map
H
satisfies (
21
). Next, for any
x,y,z\in A
,

\displaystyle H(x,y\odot z)+\rho_{x}h(y,z)

\displaystyle=x\smallblackdiamond(y\odot z)+x\diamond(y\curlyvee z)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\text{\ref{nsp3})}}}{{=}}\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}\curlyvee z+z\ast(x\smallblackdiamond y)+y\curlyvee\{\!\!\{x,z\}\!\!\}+y\ast(x\smallblackdiamond z)

\displaystyle=h(\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\},z)+\mu_{z}H(x,y)+h(y,\{\!\!\{x,z\}\!\!\})+\mu_{y}H(x,z)

which verifies the condition (
22
). Hence
(h,H)
is a Poisson
2
-cocycle of
(A,\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
with coefficients in the representation
(A,\mu,\rho)
.

For the second part, we observe that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Id}(x)\odot\mathrm{Id}(y)=x\odot y=~

\displaystyle x\ast y+y\ast x+x\curlyvee y

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\mathrm{Id}\big(\mu_{\mathrm{Id}(x)}y+\mu_{\mathrm{Id}(y)}x+h(\mathrm{Id}(x),\mathrm{Id}(y))\big)

and similarly,
\{\!\!\{\mathrm{Id}(x),\mathrm{Id}(y)\}\!\!\}=\mathrm{Id}\big(\rho_{\mathrm{Id}(x)}y-\rho_{\mathrm{Id}(y)}x+H(\mathrm{Id}(x),\mathrm{Id}(y))\big)
, for
x,y\in A
. Hence the identity map
\mathrm{Id}:A\rightarrow A
is a
(h,H)
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator.

Finally, if
(A,\ast^{\prime},\curlyvee^{\prime},\diamond^{\prime},\smallblackdiamond^{\prime})
is the induced NS-Poisson algebra structure on
A
, then we have

\displaystyle x\ast^{\prime}y=~

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathrm{Id}(x)}y=x\ast y,\qquad\quad x\curlyvee^{\prime}y=h(\mathrm{Id}(x),\mathrm{Id}(y))=x\curlyvee y,

\displaystyle x\diamond^{\prime}y=~

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{Id}(x)}y=x\diamond y~~~\text{ and }~~~x\smallblackdiamond^{\prime}y=H(\mathrm{Id}(x),\mathrm{Id}(y))=x\smallblackdiamond y,

for
x,y\in A
. This concludes the last part.
∎

A Reynolds operator on a Poisson algebra
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
is a
(h,H)
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator, where
(h,H)=-(\cdot,\{~,~\})
is the Poisson
2
-cocycle of
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
with coefficients in the adjoint representation (see Remark
3.9
).

3.13 Definition
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
be a Poisson algebra. A linear map
R:A\rightarrow A
is said to be a
Reynolds operator
on
A
if for any

\displaystyle R(x)\cdot R(y)

\displaystyle=R(R(x)\cdot y+x\cdot R(y)-R(x)\cdot R(y)),

(26)

\displaystyle\{R(x),R(y)\}

\displaystyle=R(\{R(x),y\}+\{x,R(y)\}-\{R(x),R(y)\}).

3.14 Proposition

Let

3.15 Example

Let

be a

}. Define a bilinear multiplication and a bracket on

by

\displaystyle e_{1}\cdot e_{1}=e_{3},\quad e_{1}\cdot e_{2}=e_{2}\cdot e_{1}=e_{4},

\displaystyle\{e_{1},e_{4}\}=-\{e_{4},e_{1}\}=e_{4},\quad\{e_{2},e_{3}\}=-\{e_{3},e_{2}\}=-2e_{4}.

Then (

) is a Poisson algebra. We define a linear map

by

\displaystyle R(e_{1})=ae_{1},\quad R(e_{3})=\frac{a}{2-a}e_{3},\quad R(e_{2})=R(e_{4})=0,

where

\displaystyle e_{1}\ast e_{1}=ae_{3},\quad e_{1}\ast e_{2}=ae_{4},

\displaystyle e_{1}\curlyvee e_{1}=-a^{2}e_{3},

\displaystyle e_{1}\diamond e_{4}=ae_{4},\quad e_{3}\diamond e_{2}=\frac{2a}{2-a}e_{4}.

Since Gerstenhaber algebras
[

are the graded analog of Poisson algebras, one could generalize many results of Poisson algebras to Gerstenhaber algebras. In

3.16 Definition
.

A
Gerstenhaber algebra
is a triple
(\mathcal{A},\cdot,\{~,~\})
consisting of a graded vector space
\mathcal{A}=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{A}^{i}
with a degree
0
graded bilinear product
\cdot:\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
that makes
(\mathcal{A},\cdot)
into a graded commutative associative algebra and a degree
-1
graded bilinear bracket
\{~,~\}:\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
satisfying

(27)

\displaystyle\{x,y\}=-(-1)^{(|x|-1)(|y|-1)}\{y,x\},

(28)

\displaystyle(-1)^{(|x|-1)(|z|-1)}\{\{x,y\},z\}+(-1)^{(|y|-1)(|x|-1)}\{\{y,z\},x\}+(-1)^{(|z|-1)(|y|-1)}\{\{z,x\},y\}=0

and the following graded Leibniz identity holds:

\{x,y\cdot z\}=\{x,y\}\cdot z+(-1)^{(|x|-1)|y|}y\cdot\{x,z\},

for all homogeneous elements
x\in\mathcal{A}^{|x|},y\in\mathcal{A}^{|y|},z\in\mathcal{A}^{|z|}.

In a Gerstenhaber algebra
(\mathcal{A},\cdot,\{~,~\})
, the identities (
27
) and (
28
) are equivalent to mean that the shifted graded vector space
\mathcal{A}[+1]=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{A}^{i+1}
with the bracket
\{~,~\}
is a graded Lie algebra.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\cdot,\{~,~\})
be a Gerstenhaber algebra. A
Nijenhuis operator
on
\mathcal{A}
is a degree
0
graded linear map
N:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
that satisfies

\displaystyle N(x)\cdot N(y)

\displaystyle=N(N(x)\cdot y+x\cdot N(y)-N(x\cdot y)),

\displaystyle\{N(x),N(y)\}

\displaystyle=N(\{N(x),y\}+\{x,N(y)\}-N\{x,y\}),\text{~for all~}x,y\in\mathcal{A}.

Before defining NS-Gerstenhaber algebras, we first consider the following notions.

.

A

(30)

\displaystyle x\ast(y\curlyvee z)+x\curlyvee(y\odot z)=~

4.
NS-Poisson algebras arising from deformations and filtrations of NS-algebras

In this section, we first define the semi-classical limit of an NS-algebra deformation of a given NS-commutative algebra. We show that the semi-classical limit carries an NS-Poisson algebra structure. This generalizes the well-known semi-classical limit of an associative algebra deformation of a commutative associative algebra. Next, we consider filtrations of an NS-algebra and show that some particular classes of filtrations produce NS-Poisson algebra structures.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be a commutative associative algebra. An associative formal deformation of
A
is given by a
{\bf k}[\![t]\!]
-bilinear multiplication
\cdot_{t}:A[\![t]\!]\times A[\![t]\!]\rightarrow A[\![t]\!]
of the form

\displaystyle x\cdot_{t}y=x\cdot_{0}y+(x\cdot_{1}y)t+(x\cdot_{2}y)t^{2}+(x\cdot_{3}y)t^{3}+\cdots~~~(\text{with }x\cdot_{0}y=x\cdot y,\text{ for }x,y\in A)

that makes
(A[\![t]\!],\cdot_{t})
into an associative algebra over the ring
{\bf k}[\![t]\!]
.
Then we can define a bilinear skewsymmetric bracket
\{~,~\}:A\times A\rightarrow A
by

\{x,y\}:=\frac{x\cdot_{t}y-y\cdot_{t}x}{t}\big|_{t=0}=x\cdot_{1}y-y\cdot_{1}x,\text{ for }x,y\in A.

Then it is well-known that
\{~,~\}
is a Lie bracket on
A
and satisfies the Leibniz rule in the sense of (
1
). In other words,
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
is a Poisson algebra, called the
semi-classical limit
of
(A[\![t]\!],\cdot_{t})
.

In
[
1
]
, Aguiar generalized the above construction in the context of pre-Poisson algebras. Explicitly, he first considered zinbiel algebras (viewed them as commutative dendriform algebras) and their dendriform deformations. Then, using a similar construction as above, he showed that the underlying space inherits a pre-Poisson algebra structure.

In the following, we generalize both the above constructions by considering NS-commutative algebras and their NS-algebra deformations. For more details about deformations of NS-algebras and their relations with cohomology, we refer
[
7
]
.

Let
(A,\ast,\curlyvee_{0})
be an NS-commutative algebra. We realize it as an NS-algebra
(A,\prec_{0},\succ_{0},\curlyvee_{0})
by setting
x\succ_{0}y=y\prec_{0}x=x\ast y
, for all
x,y\in A
. An NS-algebra deformation of
(A,\ast,\curlyvee_{0})
is a deformation of
(A,\prec_{0},\succ_{0},\curlyvee_{0})
as an NS-algebra. Thus, it consist of
{\bf k}[\![t]\!]
-bilinear maps
\prec_{t},\succ_{t},\curlyvee_{t}:A[\![t]\!]\times A[\![t]\!]\rightarrow A[\![t]\!]
of the form

\displaystyle x\prec_{t}y=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(x\prec_{i}y)t^{i},\quad x\succ_{t}y=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(x\succ_{i}y)t^{i}~~~\text{ and }~~~x\curlyvee_{t}y=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(x\curlyvee_{i}y)t^{i}

that makes
(A[\![t]\!],\prec_{t},\succ_{t},\curlyvee_{t})
into an NS-algebra over the ring
{\bf k}[\![t]\!].
Then we can define bilinear maps
\diamond,\smallblackdiamond:A\times A\rightarrow A
by

\displaystyle x\diamond y:=~

\displaystyle\frac{x\succ_{t}y-y\prec_{t}x}{t}\Big|_{t=0}=x\succ_{1}y-y\prec_{1}x,

\displaystyle x\smallblackdiamond y:=~

\displaystyle\frac{x\curlyvee_{t}y-y\curlyvee_{t}x}{t}\Big|_{t=0}=x\curlyvee_{1}y-y\curlyvee_{1}x.

\displaystyle\quad=y\curlyvee_{1}(x\ast z+z\ast x+x\curlyvee_{0}z)+y\succ_{1}(x\curlyvee_{0}z)-(y\ast x+x\ast y+y\curlyvee_{0}x)\curlyvee_{1}z-(y\curlyvee_{0}x)\prec_{1}z.

By adding these identities, we obtain

\displaystyle~x\curlyvee_{1}(y\ast z+z\ast y+y\curlyvee_{0}z)-(y\ast z+z\ast y+y\curlyvee_{0}z)\curlyvee_{1}x+x\succ_{1}(y\curlyvee_{0}z)-(y\curlyvee_{0}z)\prec_{1}x

\displaystyle~~+z\ast(y\curlyvee_{1}x)~{-z\ast(x\curlyvee_{1}y)}-y\ast(x\curlyvee_{1}z)+y\ast(z\curlyvee_{1}x)-(x\succ_{1}y+x\prec_{1}y+x\curlyvee_{1}y)\curlyvee_{0}z

\displaystyle~~+(y\succ_{1}x+y\prec_{1}x+y\curlyvee_{1}x)\curlyvee_{0}z-y\curlyvee_{0}(x\succ_{1}z+x\prec_{1}z+x\curlyvee_{1}z)+y\curlyvee_{0}(z\succ_{1}x+z\prec_{1}x+z\curlyvee_{1}x)

\displaystyle\qquad=0,

i.e.
x\smallblackdiamond(y\odot z)+x\diamond(y\curlyvee_{0}z)-z\ast(x\smallblackdiamond y)-y\ast(x\smallblackdiamond z)-\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}\curlyvee_{0}z-y\curlyvee_{0}\{\!\!\{x,z\}\!\!\}=0.
In other words, the identity
(
NSP3
) also holds in

. This completes the proof.
∎

The NS-Poisson algebra
(A,\ast,\curlyvee_{0},\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
constructed in the above theorem is called the
semi-classical limit
of the NS-algebra deformation
(A[\![t]\!],\prec_{t},\succ_{t},\curlyvee_{t})
of the NS-commutative algebra
(A,\ast,\curlyvee_{0})
.

In the following, we consider filtrations of an NS-algebra and show that some particular classes of filtrations produce NS-Poisson algebras. Let
(A,\prec,\succ,\curlyvee)
be an NS-algebra. A
filtration
of the NS-algebra
A
is an increasing sequence of subspaces
A_{0}\subseteq A_{1}\subseteq A_{2}\subseteq\cdots~
such that

A=\bigcup\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}\quad\text{ and }\quad(A_{n}\succ A_{m}+A_{n}\prec A_{m})\subseteq A_{n+m},\quad(A_{n}\curlyvee A_{m})\subseteq A_{n+m}.

A filtration
A_{0}\subseteq A_{1}\subseteq A_{2}\subseteq\cdots~
is said to be an
NS-Lie filtration
if it additionally satisfies

x\succ y-y\prec x\in A_{n+m+1}~\text{ and }~x\curlyvee y\in A_{n+m+1},\text{ for }x\in A_{n+1},y\in A_{m+1}.

4.2 Theorem
.

Let
(A,\prec,\succ,\curlyvee)
be an NS-algebra and
A_{0}\subseteq A_{1}\subseteq A_{2}\subseteq\cdots~
be an NS-Lie filtration of it. Then the graded space

Gr(A):=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{A_{n+1}}{A_{n}}

inherits an NS-Poisson algebra structure with the operations

\displaystyle(x+A_{n})

\displaystyle\ast(y+A_{m})=x\succ y+A_{n+m+1},

\displaystyle(x+A_{n})

\displaystyle\curlyvee(y+A_{m})=x\curlyvee y+A_{n+m+1},

\displaystyle(x+A_{n})

\displaystyle\diamond(y+A_{m})=x\succ y-y\prec x+A_{n+m},

\displaystyle(x+A_{n})

\displaystyle\smallblackdiamond(y+A_{m})=x\curlyvee y-y\curlyvee x+A_{n+m},

for
x\in A_{n+1}
and
y\in A_{m+1}
.

Proof.

Let
x+A_{n},~y+A_{m},~z+A_{l}\in Gr(A).
According to our assumption, we have
x\succ y-y\prec x\in A_{n+m+1}
. In other words,
x\succ y+A_{n+m+1}=y\prec x+A_{n+m+1}
which implies
(x+A_{n})\succ(y+A_{m})=(y+A_{m})\prec(x+A_{n})
. Moreover,
x\curlyvee y-y\curlyvee x\in A_{n+m+1}
implies that
(x+A_{n})\curlyvee(y+A_{m})=(y+A_{m})\curlyvee(x+A_{n})
. Thus, the associated graded NS-algebra happens to be commutative.

From the definition, it is clear that
\smallblackdiamond
is a skewsymmetric operation on
Gr(A)
. Thus, with the operations
\diamond
and
\smallblackdiamond
, the space
Gr(A)
is an NS-Lie algebra
[
6
, Proposition 6.5]
.

We now prove the compatibility conditions of NS-Poisson algebra. First, observe that

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{x+A_{n},y+A_{m}\}\!\!\}\ast(z+A_{l})-(x+A_{n})\diamond\big((y+A_{m})\ast(z+A_{l})\big)+(y+A_{m})\ast\big((x+A_{n})\diamond(z+A_{l})\big)

\displaystyle=(x\succ y-y\prec x-y\succ x+x\prec y+x\curlyvee y_{y}\curlyvee x)\succ z-x\succ(y\succ z)+(y\succ z)\prec x+y\succ(x\succ z)

\displaystyle\quad-y\succ(z\prec x)+A_{n+m+l+1}

\displaystyle=\big((x\odot y)\succ z-x\succ(y\succ z)\big)-\big((y\odot x)\succ z-y\succ(x\succ z)\big)+\big((y\succ z)\prec x-y\succ(z\prec x)\big)+A_{n+m+l+1},

where
x\odot y
is defined as in Definition
2.4
. Since
(A,\prec,\succ,\curlyvee)
is an NS-algebra, the identity (
NSP1
) holds in
Gr(A)
.
Next, we see that

\displaystyle\big((x+A_{n})\odot(y+A_{m})\big)\diamond(z+A_{l})-(x+A_{n})\ast\big((y+A_{m})\diamond(z+A_{l})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-(y+A_{m})\ast\big((x+A_{n})\diamond(z+A_{l})\big)

\displaystyle=(x\succ y+x\prec y+x\curlyvee y)\succ z-z\prec(x\succ y+x\prec y+x\curlyvee y)-x\succ(y\succ z)+x\succ(z\prec y)-(x\succ z)\prec y

\displaystyle\quad+(z\prec x)\prec y+A_{n+m+l+1}

\displaystyle=\big((x\odot y)\succ z-x\succ(y\succ z)\big)-\big(z\prec(x\odot y)+(z\prec x)\prec y\big)+\big(x\succ(z\prec y)-(x\succ z)\prec y\big)+A_{n+m+l+1}

\displaystyle=A_{n+m+l+1}.

This proves the identity (
NSP2
). Finally, we have

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{x+A_{n},y+A_{m}\}\!\!\}\curlyvee(z+A_{l})+(z+A_{l})\ast\big((x+A_{n})\smallblackdiamond(y+A_{m})\big)+(y+A_{m})\curlyvee\{\!\!\{x+A_{n},z+A_{l}\}\!\!\}

\displaystyle~~+(y+A_{m})\ast\big((x+A_{n})\smallblackdiamond(z+A_{l})\big)-(x+A_{n})\smallblackdiamond\big((y+A_{m})\odot(z+A_{l})\big)

\displaystyle~~-(x+A_{n})\diamond\big((y+A_{m})\curlyvee(z+A_{l})\big)

\displaystyle=(x\succ y-y\prec x-y\succ x+x\prec y+x\curlyvee y-y\curlyvee x)\curlyvee z+(x\curlyvee y-y\curlyvee x)\prec z

\displaystyle~~+y\curlyvee(x\succ z-z\prec x-z\succ x+x\prec z+x\curlyvee z-z\curlyvee x)+y\succ(x\curlyvee z-z\curlyvee x)

\displaystyle~~-x\curlyvee(y\succ z+y\prec z+y\curlyvee z)+(y\succ z+y\prec z+y\curlyvee z)\curlyvee x-x\succ(y\curlyvee z)+(y\curlyvee z)\prec x+A_{n+m+l+1}

\displaystyle=\big((x\odot y)\curlyvee z+(x\curlyvee y)\prec z-x\curlyvee(y\odot z)-x\succ(y\curlyvee z)\big)-\big((y\odot x)\curlyvee z+(y\curlyvee x)\prec z-y\curlyvee(x\odot z)

\displaystyle~~-y\succ(x\curlyvee z)\big)+\big((y\odot z)\curlyvee x+(y\curlyvee z)\prec x-y\curlyvee(z\odot x)-y\succ(z\curlyvee x)\big)+A_{n+m+l+1}

\displaystyle=A_{n+m+l+1}.

Therefore, (
NSP3
) also holds in

. This proves that
(Gr(A),\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
is an NS-Poisson algebra.
∎

5.
NS-
F
-manifold algebras

In this section, we introduce NS-
F
-manifold algebras as a generalization of NS-Poisson algebras,
F
-manifold algebras and pre-
F
-manifold algebras. Any NS-
F
-manifold algebra has a sub-adjacent
F
-manifold algebra. We show that Nijenhuis operators and Reynolds operators on
F
-manifold algebras give rise to NS-
F
-manifold algebras. Finally, we consider the NS-pre-Lie algebra deformation of an NS-commutative algebra and observe that the corresponding semi-classical limit inherits an NS-
F
-manifold algebra structure.

5.1 Definition
.

An
F
-manifold algebra
is a triple
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
in which
(A,\cdot)
is a commutative associative algebra,
(A,\{~,~\})
is a Lie algebra and the following Hertling-Manin relation holds:

(41)

\displaystyle P_{x\cdot y}(z,w)=x\cdot P_{y}(z,w)+y\cdot P_{x}(z,w),

for all
x,y,z,w\in A
. Here
P_{x}(y,z)
is defined by

\displaystyle P_{x}(y,z)=\{x,y\cdot z\}-\{x,y\}\cdot z-y\cdot\{x,z\},\text{ for }x,y,z\in A.

It follows that an
F
-manifold algebra for which
P_{x}(y,z)=0
(for all
x,y,z\in A
) is a Poisson algebra. The notion of
F
-manifold algebra was introduced by Dotsenko
[
8
]
as the underlying algebraic structure of
F
-manifolds. It has been shown by Liu, Sheng and Bai
[
18
]
that
F
-manifold algebras are the semi-classical limits of pre-Lie formal deformations of commutative pre-Lie algebras.

Let
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
be an
F
-manifold algebra. Recall that
[
18
]
a representation of
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
is a triple
(V,\mu,\rho)
in which
(V,\mu)
is a representation of the commutative associative algebra
(A,\cdot)
and
(V,\rho)
is a representation of the Lie algebra
(A,\{~,~\})
satisfying additionally

\displaystyle R_{\mu,\rho}(x\cdot y,z)=~

\displaystyle\mu_{x}\circ R_{\mu,\rho}(y,z)+\mu_{y}\circ R_{\mu,\rho}(x,z),

\displaystyle\mu_{P_{x}(y,z)}=~

\displaystyle S_{\mu,\rho}(y,z)\circ\mu_{x}-\mu_{x}\circ S_{\mu,\rho}(y,z),

for all
x,y,z\in A
, where
R_{\mu,\rho},S_{\mu,\rho}:A\otimes A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V)
are defined by

\displaystyle R_{\mu,\rho}(x,y):=\rho_{x}\circ\mu_{y}-\mu_{y}\circ\rho_{x}-\mu_{\{x,y\}}~~~\text{ and }~~~S_{\mu,\rho}(x,y):=\mu_{x}\circ\rho_{y}+\mu_{y}\circ\rho_{x}-\rho_{x\cdot y}.

In
[
18
]
the authors also introduced the notion of pre-
F
-manifold algebras as a generalization of pre-Poisson algebras. Moreover, they showed that Rota-Baxter operators on
F
-manifold algebras induce pre-
F
-manifold algebras. In the following, we will introduce NS-
F
-manifold algebras as a simultaneous generalization of NS-Poisson algebras,
F
-manifold algebras and pre-
F
-manifold algebras.

5.2 Definition
.

An
NS-
F
-manifold algebra
is a quintuple
(A,\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond),
where
(A,\ast,\curlyvee)
is an NS-commutative algebra and
(A,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
is an NS-Lie algebra such that the following compatibility conditions hold:

(NSF1)

\displaystyle F_{1}(x\odot y,z,w)=~

\displaystyle x\ast F_{1}(y,z,w)+y\ast F_{1}(x,z,w),

(NSF2)

\displaystyle(F_{1}(x,y,z)+F_{1}(x,z,y)+F_{2}(y,z,x)+F_{3}(x,y,z))\ast w=~

\displaystyle F_{2}(y,z,x\ast w)-x\ast F_{2}(y,z,w),

(NSF3)

\displaystyle F_{3}(x\odot y,z,w)+F_{2}(z,w,y\curlyvee x)=~

\displaystyle x\ast F_{3}(y,z,w)+y\ast F_{3}(x,z,w)

\displaystyle+x\curlyvee(F_{1}(y,z,w)~+~

\displaystyle F_{1}(y,w,z)+F_{2}(z,w,y)+F_{3}(y,z,w))

\displaystyle+y\curlyvee(F_{1}(x,z,w)~+~

\displaystyle F_{1}(x,w,z)+F_{2}(z,w,x)+F_{3}(x,z,w)),

for all
x,y,z,w\in A.
Here
F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}:\otimes^{3}A\rightarrow A
are the maps defined by

\displaystyle F_{1}(x,y,z)=x\diamond(y\ast z)-y\ast(x\diamond z)-\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}\ast z,

\displaystyle F_{2}(x,y,z)=x\ast(y\diamond z)+y\ast(x\diamond z)-(x\odot y)\diamond z,

\displaystyle F_{3}(x,y,z)=x\smallblackdiamond(y\odot z)+x\diamond(y\curlyvee z)-z\ast(x\smallblackdiamond y)-y\ast(x\smallblackdiamond z)-y\curlyvee\{\!\!\{x,z\}\!\!\}-\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}\curlyvee z,

where
x\odot y=x\ast y+y\ast x+x\curlyvee y
and
\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}=x\diamond y-y\diamond x+x\smallblackdiamond y.

5.3 Remark
.

Let
(A,\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
be an NS-
F
-manifold algebra.

(i) If
F_{1}=F_{2}=F_{3}=0
then it is simply an NS-Poisson algebra introduced in Definition
3.1
. Thus,
F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}
measures to what extent
(A,\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
become an NS-Poisson algebra.

(ii) If
\ast
and
\diamond
are trivial then it follows that
F_{!}=F_{2}=0
and

\displaystyle F_{3}(x,y,z)=x\smallblackdiamond(y\curlyvee z)-y\curlyvee(x\smallblackdiamond z)-(x\smallblackdiamond y)\curlyvee z,\text{ for }x,y,z\in A.

In this case, the identities (
NSF1
), (
NSF2
) hold trivially and the identity (
NSF3
) implies that

\displaystyle F_{3}(x\curlyvee y,z,w)=x\curlyvee F_{3}(y,z,w)+y\curlyvee F_{3}(x,z,w),\text{ for }x,y,z,w\in A.

Thus,
(A,\curlyvee,\smallblackdiamond=\{~,~\})
is an
F
-manifold algebra.

(iii) If
\curlyvee
and
\smallblackdiamond
are trivial then it follows that
F_{3}=0
and hence the identity (
NSF3
) holds trivially. In this case, the triple
(A,\ast,\diamond)
becomes a pre-
F
-manifold algebra in the sense of
[
18
]
.

5.4 Theorem
.

Let
(A,\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
be an NS-
F
-manifold algebra.

(i)

Then
(A,\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
is an
F
-manifold algebra, called the sub-adjacent
F
-manifold algebra of
(A,\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
and it is denoted by
A^{c}
. Here the operations
\odot
and
\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\}
are defined in the Definition
5.2
.

(ii)

Moreover, the triple
(A,\mu,\rho)
is a representation of the sub-adjacent
F
-manifold algebra
A^{c}
, where
\mu
and
\rho
are defined by (
14
) and (
18
), respectively.

Proof.

(i) Since
(A,\ast,\curlyvee)
is an NS-commutative algebra, it follows that
(A,\odot)
is a commutative associative algebra. On the other hand,
(A,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
is an NS-Lie algebra implies that
(A,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
is a Lie algebra. Thus it remains to verify the Hertling-Manin relation for
(A,\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
. For any
x,y,z\in A
, we first observe that

\displaystyle P_{x}(y,z)=

\displaystyle~\{\!\!\{x,y\odot z\}\!\!\}-\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}\odot z-y\odot\{\!\!\{x,z\}\!\!\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~x\diamond(y\ast z+z\ast y+y\curlyvee z)-(y\odot z)\diamond x+x\smallblackdiamond(y\odot z)

\displaystyle-\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}\ast z-z\ast(x\diamond y-y\diamond x+x\smallblackdiamond y)-\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}\curlyvee z

\displaystyle-y\ast(x\diamond z-z\diamond x+x\smallblackdiamond z)-\{\!\!\{x,z\}\!\!\}\ast y-y\curlyvee\{\!\!\{x,z\}\!\!\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\big(x\diamond(y\ast z)-y\ast(x\diamond z)-\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}\ast z\big)+\big(x\diamond(z\ast y)-z\ast(x\diamond y)-\{\!\!\{x,z\}\!\!\}\ast y\big)

\displaystyle+\big(y\ast(z\diamond x)+z\ast(y\diamond x)-(y\odot z)\diamond x\big)+\big(x\smallblackdiamond(y\odot z)+x\diamond(y\curlyvee z)-z\ast(x\smallblackdiamond y)

\displaystyle-y\ast(x\smallblackdiamond z)-y\curlyvee\{\!\!\{x,z\}\!\!\}-\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}\curlyvee z\big).

Thus, we have

(42)

\displaystyle P_{x}(y,z)=F_{1}(x,y,z)+F_{1}(x,z,y)+F_{2}(y,z,x)+F_{3}(x,y,z).

Hence

\displaystyle P_{x\odot y}(z,w)-x\odot P_{y}(z,w)-y\odot P_{x}(z,w)

\displaystyle=F_{1}(x\odot y,z,w)+F_{1}(x\odot y,w,z)+F_{2}(z,w,x\ast y)+F_{2}(z,w,y\ast x)+F_{2}(z,w,y\curlyvee x)+F_{3}(x\odot y,z,w)

\displaystyle\quad-x\ast\big(F_{1}(y,z,w)+F_{1}(y,w,z)+F_{2}(z,w,y)+F_{3}(y,z,w)\big)-P_{y}(z,w)\ast x-x\curlyvee P_{y}(z,w)

\displaystyle\quad-y\ast\big(F_{1}(x,z,w)+F_{1}(x,w,z)+F_{2}(z,w,x)+F_{3}(x,z,w)\big)-P_{x}(z,w)\ast y-y\curlyvee P_{x}(z,w)

\displaystyle=\big(F_{1}(x\odot y,z,w)-x\ast F_{1}(y,z,w)-y\ast F_{1}(x,z,w)\big)+\big(F_{1}(x\odot y,w,z)-x\ast F_{1}(y,w,z)

\displaystyle\quad-y\ast F_{1}(x,w,z)\big)-\big(P_{y}(z,w)\ast x-F_{2}(z,w,y\ast x)+y\ast F_{2}(z,w,x)\big)-\big(P_{x}(z,w)\ast y

\displaystyle\quad-F_{2}(z,w,x\ast y)+x\ast F_{2}(z,w,y)\big)+\big(F_{3}(x\odot y,z,w)+F_{2}(z,w,y\curlyvee x)-x\ast F_{3}(y,z,w)

\displaystyle\quad-y\ast F_{3}(x,z,w)-x\curlyvee P_{y}(z,w)-y\curlyvee P_{x}(z,w)\big),

which vanishes by using (
42
) along with the compatibility conditions of the NS-
F
-manifold algebra. Therefore,
(A,\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
is an
F
-manifold algebra.

(ii) We have already seen that
(A,\mu)
is a representation of the commutative associative algebra
(A,\odot)
, and
(A,\rho)
is a representation of the Lie algebra
(A,\{\!\!\{~,~\}\!\!\})
. Next, observe that

\displaystyle F_{1}(x,y,z)=~

\displaystyle(\rho_{x}\circ\mu_{y}-\mu_{y}\circ\rho_{x}-\mu_{\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}})(z)=R_{\mu,\rho}(x,y)(z),

\displaystyle F_{2}(x,y,z)=~

\displaystyle(\mu_{x}\circ\rho_{y}+\mu_{y}\circ\rho_{x}-\rho_{x\odot y})(z)=S_{\mu,\rho}(x,y)(z),

for

\displaystyle R_{\mu,\rho}(x\odot y,z)(w)=F_{1}(x\odot y,z,w)

\displaystyle=x\ast F_{1}(y,z,w)+y\ast F_{1}(x,z,w)

\displaystyle=(\mu_{x}\circ R_{\mu,\rho}(y,z)+\mu_{y}\circ R_{\mu,\rho}(x,z))(w)

and by using (

) and (

\displaystyle\mu_{P_{x}(y,z)}(w)=P_{x}(y,z)\ast w

\displaystyle=F_{2}(y,z,x\ast w)-x\ast F_{2}(y,z,w)

\displaystyle=(S_{\mu,\rho}(y,z)\circ\mu_{x}-\mu_{x}\circ S_{\mu,\rho}(y,z))(w),

for

. This proves that
(A,\mu,\rho)
is a representation of the sub-adjacent
F
-manifold algebra
A^{c}
.
∎

In the following, we consider Nijenhuis operators and Reynolds operators on an
F
-manifold algebra and show that they induce NS-
F
-manifold algebra structures. Let
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
be an
F
-manifold algebra. A
Nijenhuis operator
on
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
is a linear map
N:A\rightarrow A
that is a Nijenhuis operator on both the underlying commutative associative algebra and on the Lie algebra. Similarly, a Reynolds operator on
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
is a linear map
R:A\rightarrow A
that is a Reynolds operator on both the underlying commutative associative algebra and on the Lie algebra.

5.5 Proposition
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\{~,~\})
be an
F
-manifold algebra and
N:A\rightarrow A
be a Nijenhuis operator on it. Then
(A,\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
is an NS-
F
-manifold algebra, where

x\ast y=N(x)\cdot y,~~~~x\curlyvee y=-N(x\cdot y),~~~~x\diamond y=\{N(x),y\}~~~\text{ and }~~~x\smallblackdiamond y=-N\{x,y\},~\text{ for }x,y\in A.

Proof.

Since
N:A\rightarrow A
is a Nijenhuis operator on the commutative associative algebra
(A,\cdot)
, it follows that
(A,\ast,\curlyvee)
is an NS-commutative algebra. Similarly,
N
is a Nijenhuis operator on the Lie algebra
(A,\{~,~\})
implies that the triple
(A,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
is an NS-Lie algebra. Next, we observe that

\displaystyle F_{1}(x,y,z)=~

\displaystyle\{N(x),N(y)\cdot z\}-N(y)\cdot\{N(x),z\}-N\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}\cdot z

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\{N(x),N(y)\cdot z\}-N(y)\cdot\{N(x),z\}-\{N(x),N(y)\}\cdot z

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle P_{N(x)}(N(y),z)=P_{N(x)}(z,N(y)),

\displaystyle F_{2}(x,y,z)=~

\displaystyle N(x)\cdot\{N(y),z\}+N(y)\cdot\{N(x),z\}-\{N(x)\cdot N(y),z\}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle P_{z}(N(x),N(y))=P_{z}(N(y),N(x)),

\displaystyle F_{3}(x,y,z)=~

\displaystyle-N\{x,y\odot z\}-\{N(x),N(y\cdot z)\}+N(z)\cdot N\{x,y\}+N(y)\cdot N\{x,z\}

\displaystyle+N(y\cdot\{\!\!\{x,z\}\!\!\})+N(\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}\cdot z)

\displaystyle=~

A
together with bilinear maps
\rhd,\lhd,\circ:A\times A\rightarrow A
satisfying the following set of identities:

(47)

\displaystyle(x\odot y)\rhd z-x\rhd(y\rhd z)=~

\displaystyle(y\odot x)\rhd z-y\rhd(x\rhd z),

(48)

\displaystyle x\rhd(y\lhd z)-(x\rhd y)\lhd z=~

\displaystyle y\lhd(x\odot z)-(y\lhd x)\lhd z,

(49)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(x\odot y)\circ z-x\circ(y\odot z)+~&(x\circ y)\lhd z-x\rhd(y\circ z)\\
=~&(y\odot x)\circ z-y\circ(x\odot z)+(y\circ x)\lhd z-y\rhd(x\circ z),\end{split}

for all
x,y,z\in A
. Here
x\odot y=x\rhd y+x\lhd y+x\circ y
.

5.13 Remark
.

Any NS-algebra
(A,\prec,\succ,\curlyvee)
can be regarded as an NS-pre-Lie algebra
(A,\rhd,\lhd,\circ)
by setting
x\rhd y=x\succ y,x\lhd y=x\prec y
and
x\circ y=x\curlyvee y
. Thus NS-pre-Lie algebras generalize NS-algebras. Taking the product
\circ
as trivial in any NS-pre-Lie algebra gives us an L-dendriform algebra
[
3
]
. So, NS-pre-Lie algebras are also a generalization of L-dendriform algebras.

It is well-known that a pre-Lie algebra gives rise to a Lie algebra by skew-symmetrization. The following result is a generalization for NS-pre-Lie algebras.

5.14 Proposition
.

Let
(A,\rhd,\lhd,\circ)
be an NS-pre-Lie algebra. Then
(A,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
is an NS-Lie algebra, where

\displaystyle x\diamond y:=x\rhd y-y\lhd x~~~\text{ and }~~~x\smallblackdiamond y:=x\circ y-y\circ x,\text{ for }x,y\in A.

Proof.

For any
x,y,z\in A
, we have

\displaystyle x\diamond(y\diamond z)

\displaystyle=x\rhd(y\rhd z)-x\rhd(z\lhd y)-(y\rhd z)\lhd x+(z\lhd y)\lhd x,

\displaystyle(x\diamond y)\diamond z

\displaystyle=(x\rhd y)\rhd z-(y\lhd x)\rhd z-z\lhd(x\rhd y)+z\lhd(y\lhd x),

\displaystyle(x\smallblackdiamond y)\diamond z

\displaystyle=(x\circ y)\rhd z-(y\circ x)\rhd z-z\lhd(x\circ y)+z\lhd(y\circ x).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle x\diamond(y\diamond z)-(x\diamond y)\diamond z-y\diamond(x\diamond z)+(y\diamond x)\diamond z-(x\smallblackdiamond y)\diamond z

\displaystyle=x\rhd(y\rhd z)-x\rhd(z\lhd y)-(y\rhd z)\lhd x+(z\lhd y)\lhd x-(x\rhd y)\rhd z+(y\lhd x)\rhd z+z\lhd(x\rhd y)

\displaystyle\quad-z\lhd(y\lhd x)-y\rhd(x\rhd z)+y\rhd(z\lhd x)+(x\rhd z)\lhd y-(z\lhd x)\lhd y+(y\rhd x)\rhd z-(x\lhd y)\rhd z

\displaystyle\quad-z\lhd(y\rhd x)+z\lhd(x\lhd x)-(x\circ y)\rhd z+(y\circ x)\rhd z+z\lhd(x\circ y)-z\lhd(y\circ x)

\displaystyle=\big(x\rhd(y\rhd z)-y\rhd(x\rhd z)-(x\rhd y+x\lhd y+x\circ y)\rhd z+(y\rhd x+y\lhd x+y\circ x)\rhd z\big)

\displaystyle\quad+\big(z\lhd(x\rhd y+x\lhd y+x\circ y)-(z\lhd x)\lhd y+(x\rhd z)\lhd y-x\rhd(z\lhd y)\big)

\displaystyle\quad+\big((z\lhd y)\lhd x+y\rhd(z\lhd x)-(y\rhd z)\lhd x-z\lhd(y\rhd x+y\lhd x+y\circ x)\big)

\displaystyle=0.

This proves the first identity of an NS-Lie algebra. Next, observe that

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}=x\diamond y-y\diamond x+x\smallblackdiamond y=x\odot y-y\odot x,

where
\odot
is defined as in Definition
5.12
. Note that the identity (
49
) can be written as

(50)

\displaystyle-(x\odot y)\circ z~+~

\displaystyle x\circ(y\odot z)-(x\circ y)\lhd z+x\rhd(y\circ z)+(y\odot x)\circ z-y\circ(x\odot z)

\displaystyle+(y\circ x)\lhd z-y\rhd(x\circ z)=0,

By considering the two more identities similar to (
50
) taking the variables
x,y,z
in the cyclic order, and then adding all three identities, we get

\displaystyle\big(x\circ(y\odot z-z\odot y)-(y\odot z-z\odot y)\circ x\big)+\big(y\circ(z\odot x-x\odot z)-(z\odot x-x\odot z)\circ y\big)

\displaystyle+\big(z\circ(x\odot y-y\odot x)-(x\odot y-y\odot x)\circ z\big)+\big(x\rhd(y\circ z-z\circ y)-(y\circ z-z\circ y)\lhd x\big)

\displaystyle+\big(y\rhd(z\circ x-x\circ z)-(z\circ x-x\circ z)\lhd y\big)+\big(z\rhd(x\circ y-y\circ x)-(x\circ y-y\circ x)\lhd z\big)=0,

equivalently,
x\smallblackdiamond\{\!\!\{y,z\}\!\!\}+y\smallblackdiamond\{\!\!\{z,x\}\!\!\}+z\smallblackdiamond\{\!\!\{x,y\}\!\!\}+x\diamond(y\smallblackdiamond z)+y\diamond(z\smallblackdiamond x)+z\diamond(x\smallblackdiamond y)=0.

Hence, the second identity of an NS-Lie algebra also holds. In other words,
(A,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
is an NS-Lie algebra.
∎

Let
(A,\ast,\curlyvee)
be an NS-commutative algebra. Then it can be realized as an NS-algebra and hence an NS-pre-Lie algebra
(A,\rhd_{0},\lhd_{0},\circ_{0})
, where
x\rhd_{0}y=y\lhd_{0}x=x\ast y
and
x\circ_{0}y=x\curlyvee y
, for all
x,y\in A.

5.15 Theorem
.

Let
(A,\ast,\curlyvee)
be an NS-commutative algebra with the corresponding NS-pre-Lie algebra
(A,\rhd_{0},\lhd_{0},\circ_{0})
. Let
(A[\![t]\!],\rhd_{t},\lhd_{t},\circ_{t})
be an NS-pre-Lie algebra deformation of
(A,\rhd_{0},\lhd_{0},\circ_{0})
.
Define two bilinear operations
\diamond,\smallblackdiamond:A\times A\rightarrow A
by

\displaystyle x\diamond y:=~

\displaystyle\frac{x\rhd_{t}y-y\lhd_{t}x}{t}\Big|_{t=0}=x\rhd_{1}y-y\lhd_{1}x,

\displaystyle x\smallblackdiamond y:=~

\displaystyle\frac{x\circ_{t}y-y\circ_{t}x}{t}\Big|_{t=0}=x\circ_{1}y-y\circ_{1}x,

for
x,y\in A
. Then
(A,\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
is an NS-
F
-manifold algebra.

Proof.

Since
(A[\![t]\!],\rhd_{t},\lhd_{t},\circ_{t})
is an NS-pre-Lie algebra over the ring
{\bf k}[\![t]\!]
, it follows from Proposition
5.14
that
(A[\![t]\!],\diamond_{t},\smallblackdiamond_{t})
is an NS-Lie algebra over
{\bf k}[\![t]\!]
, where

\displaystyle x\diamond_{t}y:=x\rhd_{t}y-y\lhd_{t}x~~~~\text{ and }~~~~x\smallblackdiamond_{t}y:=x\circ_{t}y-y\circ_{t}x,\text{ for }x,y\in A.

In the NS-Lie algebra identities of
(A[\![t]\!],\diamond_{t},\smallblackdiamond_{t})
, if we compare the coefficient of
t^{2}
in both sides of the identities, one obtains the NS-Lie algebra identities for
(A,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
(similar to Theorem
4.1
). In other words,
(A,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
becomes an NS-Lie algebra.

On the other hand,
(A[\![t]\!],\rhd_{t},\lhd_{t},\circ_{t})
is an NS-pre-Lie algebra implies that the identities (
47
), (
48
) and (
49
) hold if we replace
\rhd,\lhd,\circ
by
\rhd_{t},\lhd_{t}
and
\circ_{t}
, respectively. In all these identities, we get a set of equations if we compare various powers of
t
. In particular, if we compare coefficients of
t
, we get three equations (each one comes from (
47
), (
48
), (
49
), respectively). Then, by a direct calculation (similar to the one given in Theorem
4.1
), one can show that
(A,\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
is an NS-
F
-manifold algebra.
∎

The NS-
F
-manifold algebra
(A,\ast,\curlyvee,\diamond,\smallblackdiamond)
constructed in the above theorem is called the
semi-classical limit
of the NS-pre-Lie algebra deformation
(A[\![t]\!],\rhd_{t},\lhd_{t},\circ_{t})
of the NS-commutative algebra
(A,\ast,\curlyvee).
By taking
\curlyvee=0
in Theorem
5.15
, we get the following observation which is also a new result.

5.16 Remark
.

Let
(A,\ast)
be a zinbiel algebra. Then it can be realized as an L-dendriform algebra
(A,\rhd_{0},\lhd_{0})
by setting
x\rhd_{0}y=y\lhd_{0}x=x\ast y
, for all
x,y\in A
. If
(A[\![t]\!],\rhd_{t},\lhd_{t})
is an L-dendriform algebra deformation of
(A,\rhd_{0},\lhd_{0})
then
(A,\ast,\diamond)
is a pre-
F
-manifold algebra, where

\displaystyle x\diamond y:=~

\displaystyle\frac{x\rhd_{t}y-y\lhd_{t}x}{t}\Big|_{t=0}=x\rhd_{1}y-y\lhd_{1}x,\text{ for }x,y\in A.

The pre-
F
-manifold algebra
(A,\ast,\diamond)
is called the
semi-classical limit
of the L-dendriform algebra deformation
(A[\![t]\!],\rhd_{t},\lhd_{t})
of the zinbiel algebra
(A,\ast)
.

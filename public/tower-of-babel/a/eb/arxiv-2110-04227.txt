Universal Joint Approximation of Manifolds and Densities by Simple Injective Flows
Michael Puthawala, Matti Lassas, Ivan Dokmanić, Maarten de Hoop

Abstract
We study approximation of probability measures supported on n n -dimensional manifolds embedded in ℝ m \mathbb{R}^{m} by injective flows —neural networks composed of invertible flows and injective layers. We show that in general, injective flows between ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} and ℝ m \mathbb{R}^{m} universally approximate measures supported on images of extendable embeddings, which are a subset of standard embeddings: when the embedding dimension m m is small, topological obstructions may preclude certain manifolds as admissible targets. When the embedding dimension is sufficiently large, m ≥ 3 ​ n + 1 m\geq 3n+1 , we use an argument from algebraic topology known as the clean trick to prove that the topological obstructions vanish and injective flows universally approximate any differentiable embedding. Along the way we show that the studied injective flows admit efficient projections on the range, and that their optimality can be established ”in reverse,” resolving a conjecture made in ( Brehmer and Cranmer 2020 )

Introduction

Invertible flow networks emerged as powerful deep learning models to learn maps between distributions
(
Durkan et al. 2019a
;
Grathwohl et al. 2018
;
Huang et al. 2018
;
Jaini et al. 2019
;
Kingma et al. 2016
;
Kingma and Dhariwal 2018
;
Kobyzev et al. 2020
;
Kruse et al. 2019
;
Papamakarios et al. 2019
)
. They generate high-quality samples
(
Kingma and Dhariwal 2018
)
and facilitate solving scientific inference problems
(
Brehmer and Cranmer 2020
;
Kruse et al. 2021
)
.

By design, however, invertible flows are bijective and may not be a natural choice when the target distribution has low-dimensional support. This problem can be overcome by combining bijective flows with expansive, injective layers, which map to higher dimensions
(
Brehmer and Cranmer 2020
;
Cunningham et al. 2020
;
Kothari et al. 2021
)
. Despite their empirical success, the theoretical aspects of such globally injective architectures are not well understood.

In this work, we address approximation-theoretic properties of injective flows. We prove that under mild conditions these networks universally approximate probability measures supported on low-dimensional manifolds and describe how their design enables applications to inference and inverse problems.

1.2
Our Contribution

We derive new approximation results for neural networks composed of bijective flows and injective expansive layers, including those introduced by
(
Brehmer and Cranmer 2020
)
and
(
Kothari et al. 2021
)
. We show that these networks universally jointly approximate a large class of manifolds and densities supported on them.

We build on the results of
Teshima et al. 2020
and develop a new theoretical device which we refer to as the
embedding gap
. This gap is a measure of how nearly a mapping from
\mathbb{R}^{o}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
embeds an
n
-dimensional manifold in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, where
n\leq o
. We find a natural relationship between the embedding gap and the problem of approximating probability measures with low-dimensional support.

We then relate the embedding gap to a relaxation of universality we call the
manifold embedding property
. We show that this property captures the essential geometric aspects of universality and uncover important topological restrictions on the approximation power of these networks, to our knowledge, heretofore unknown in the literature. We give an example of an absolutely continuous measure
\mu
and embedding
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
{f}{}_{\#}{\mu}
can not be approximated with combinations of flow layers and linear expansive layers. This may be surprising since it was previously conjectured that networks such as those of
Brehmer and Cranmer 2020
can approximate any “nice” density supported on a “nice” manifold. We establish universality for manifolds with suitable topology, described in terms of
extendable embeddings
. We find that the set of extendable embeddings is a proper subset of all embeddings, but when
m\geq 3n+1
, via an application of the
clean trick
from algebraic topology, we show that all diffeomorphisms are extendable and thus injective flows approximate distributions on arbitrary manifolds. Our universality proof also implies that optimality of the approximating network can be established in reverse: optimality of a given layer can be established without optimality of preceding layers. This settles a (generalization of a) conjecture posed for a three-part network (composed of two flow networks and zero padding) in
(
Brehmer and Cranmer 2020
)
.
Finally, we show that these universal architectures are also practical and admit exact layer-wise projections, as well as other properties discussed in Section
3.5
.

2.1
Expansive Layers

The expansive elements transform an
n

embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{n_{\ell-1}}

(Expansive Element)

A family of functions
\mathcal{R}\subset C(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{m})
is called an family of expansive elements if
m>n
, and each
R\in\mathcal{R}

Examples of expansive elements include

(R1)

Zero padding:

(R2)

Multiplication by an arbitrary full-rank matrix, or one-by-one convolution:

\displaystyle R(x)=Wx,\quad\text{or}\quad R(x)=w\star x

(2)

\rank(W)=n

(
Cunningham et al. 2020
)
, and

(
Kingma and Dhariwal 2018
)
.

(R3)

Injective

R{(x)}=\operatorname{ReLU}(Wx)

)

(R4)

Injective

(
Puthawala et al. 2020
, Theorem 5)
. These are functions

R(x)=W_{L+1}\operatorname{ReLU}(\dots\operatorname{ReLU}(W_{1}x+b_{1})\dots)+b_{L}

.

Continuous piecewise-differentiable functions with bounded gradients are always Lipschitz. Thus, the Lipschitzness assumption is automatically satisfied by feed-forward networks with piecewise-differentiable activation functions with bounded gradients. This includes compositions of
\operatorname{ReLU}
and sigmoid layers.

3.1
Embedding Gap

We call a function
f
an embedding and denote it by

if

is continuous, injective, and
f^{-1}\colon f(X)\to X
is continuous
3
3

3

Note that if
X

is automatic, and need not be assumed
(
Sutherland 2009

|x|\to\infty

(
Mukherjee 2015

the map

. Also

We now introduce the

Definition 3

(Embedding Gap)
.

Let

,

be compact and non-empty,

\displaystyle B_{K,W}(f,g)=\inf_{r\in\emb(f(K),g(W))}\left\lVert I-r\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(f(K))}

where

\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X)}=\esssup_{x\in X}\left\lVert h{(x)}\right\rVert_{2}

is some

Remark 1
.

As

contains

such that

. Thus, the map

6

Before giving properties of

the set of probability measures over

. If the embedding gap between

and

nearly embeds the range of

into

.

\displaystyle\inf_{\mu_{o}\in\mathcal{P}(W)}\ \operatorname{W}_{2}\left({f}{}_{\#}{\mu_{n}},{g}{}_{\#}{\mu_{o}}\right)\leq B_{K,W}(f,g)

. This is proven in Lemma
5

then upper bounds the 2-Wasserstein distance between

3.2
Manifold Embedding Property

We now introduce a central concept, the manifold embedding property (MEP). A family of networks has the MEP if it can, as measured by the embedding gap, nearly embed a large class of manifolds of certain dimension and regularity. The MEP is a property of a family of functions
\mathcal{E}\subset\emb({W},\mathbb{R}^{m})
where
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{o}
. In this manuscript,
\mathcal{E}
will always be formed by taking
\mathcal{E}\coloneqq\mathcal{T}\circ\mathcal{R}
, where
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{T}
are the expansive layers and bijective flow networks described in sections
2.1
and
2.2
respectively.

We note here that
\mathcal{E}
having the MEP is closely related to the question of whether or not a given
n
-dimensional manifold
\mathcal{M}=f(K)
for
f\in\emb(K,\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, can be approximated by an
E\in\mathcal{E}
. This choice of first applying (possibly non-universal) expansive layers, and then universal layers puts some topological restrictions on the expressivity, which we discuss in great detail in Section
3.3
.

In anticipation of these topological difficulties, when we refer to the MEP, we consider it with respect to a class of functions
\mathcal{F}\subset\emb(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{m})
. The MEP can be interpreted as a density statement, saying that our networks
\mathcal{E}
are dense in some set
\mathcal{F}\subset\emb(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{m})
in the topology induced by the ‘
B_{K,W}
distance.’ Two examples of
\mathcal{F}
that we are particularly interested in are the following. When
\mathcal{F}=\emb(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{m})
, and also when each
f\in\mathcal{F}
can be written as
f=D\circ L
where
L:\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is a linear map of rank
n
and
D:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a
C^{k}
diffeomorphism with
k\geq 1
.

Definition 4

(Manifold Embedding Property)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}\subset\emb(\mathbb{R}^{o},\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
\mathcal{F}\subset\emb(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{m})
be two families of functions. We say that
\mathcal{E}
has the
m,n,o
Manifold Embedding Property (MEP) w.r.t.
\mathcal{F}
if for every compact non-empty set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
f\in\mathcal{F}
, and
\epsilon>0
, there is an
E\in\mathcal{E}
and a compact set
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{o}

such that the restriction of
f
to
K

E

\displaystyle B_{K,W}(f,E)<\epsilon.

(7)

When it is clear from the context, we abbreviate the

Definition 5

(Uniform Universal Approximator)
.

For a non-empty subset

.

Let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
. If for
i=1,\dots,n
,
\left[y\right]_{i}\neq\left[y\right]_{i+n}
then

\displaystyle R^{\dagger}(y)\coloneqq\left(M_{y}W\right)^{-1}M_{y}y=\operatornamewithlimits{argmin}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\lVert y-R(x)\right\rVert_{2}.

(17)

Further, if there is a
i\in\left\{1,\dots,n\right\}
such that
\left[y\right]_{i}=\left[y\right]_{i+n}
, then there are multiple minimizers of
\left\lVert y-R(x)\right\rVert_{2}
, one of which is
R^{\dagger}(y)
.

The proof of Theorem
3
is given in Appendix
C.5.1
.

Remark 2
.

We note that Theorem
3
is different from many of the existing work on inverting expansive layers, e.g.
(
Aberdam et al. 2020
;
Bora et al. 2017
;
Lei et al. 2019
)
, our result gives a direct inversion algorithm that is provably the least-squares minimizer. Further, if each expansive layer is any combination of (R1), (R2), or (R3) then the entire network can be inverted end-to-end by using either the above result or solving the normal equations directly.

4
Conclusion

Bijective flow networks are a powerful tool for learning push-forward mappings in a space of fixed dimension. Increasingly, these flow networks have been used in combination with networks that increase dimension in order to produce networks which are purportedly universal.

In this work, we have studied the theory underpinning these flow and expansive networks by introducing two new notions, the embedding gap and the manifold embedding property. We show that these notions are both necessary and sufficient for proving universality, but require important topological and geometrical considerations which are, heretofore, under-explored in the literature. We also find that optimality of the studied networks can be established ‘in reverse,’ by minimizing the embedding gap, which we expect opens the door to convergence of layer-wise training schemes. Without compromising universality, we can also use specific expansive layers with a new layerwise projection result.

C.1.1
Embedding Gap

To aid all of our subsequent proofs, we first present the following lemma which present inequalities and identities for the embedding gap.

Lemma 5
.

For all of the following results,
f\in\emb(K,\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
g\in\emb(W,\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
n\leq o\leq m
.

1.

\displaystyle B_{K,W}(f,g)

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{x_{n}\in K}\inf_{x_{o}\in W}\left\lVert g(x_{o})-f(x_{n})\right\rVert_{2}.

(19)

2.

Let
X,Y\subset W
, let
g
be Lipschitz on
W
, and
r\in\emb(X,Y)
.
Then, there is a
r^{\prime}\in\emb(g(X),g(Y))
such that
g\circ r=r^{\prime}\circ g
and
\left\lVert I-r^{\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(g(X))}\leq\left\lVert I-r\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X)}\operatornamewithlimits{Lip}(g)
.

3.

\displaystyle\left\lVert I-r\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(K)}=\left\lVert I-r^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(r(K))}

(20)

4.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{o}
be compact sets. Also, let
f\in\emb(K,W)
and
h\in\emb(X,W)
, and let
g\in\emb(W,\mathbb{R}^{m})
be
a Lipschitz map. Then

\displaystyle B_{K,X}(g\circ f,g\circ h)

\displaystyle\leq\operatornamewithlimits{Lip}(g)B_{K,X}(f,h).

(21)

5.

B_{K,W}(f,g)\leq\sup_{x\in K}\left\lVert g\circ h(x)-f(x)\right\rVert_{2}

where
h\in\emb(K,\mathbb{R}^{o})
is a map satisfying
h(K)\subset W
.

6.

For any
X
that is the closure of an open set , if
h\in\emb(X,W)
then

\displaystyle B_{K,W}(f,g)\leq B_{K,X}(f,g\circ h)

(22)

7.

For any
r\in\emb(f(K),\mathbb{R}^{m})
,

\displaystyle B_{K,W}(f,g)\leq\left\lVert I-r\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(f(K))}+B_{K,W}(r\circ f,g).

(23)

8.

For any
r\in\emb(f(K),g(W))
and
h\in\emb(X,W)
where
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
is the closure of a set
U
which is open in the subspace topology of some vector space of dimension
p
, where
n\leq p\leq o
we have that

\displaystyle B_{K,X}(f,g\circ h)\leq\left\lVert I-r\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(f(K))}+\operatornamewithlimits{Lip}(g)B_{K,X}(g^{-1}\circ r\circ f,h)

(24)

where
\operatornamewithlimits{Lip}(g)
denotes the Lipschitz constant of
g
.

9.

For any
\mu_{n}\in\mathcal{P}(K)
there is a
\mu_{o}\in\mathcal{P}(W)
such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{W}_{2}\left({f}{}_{\#}{\mu_{n}},{g}{}_{\#}{\mu_{o}}\right)\leq B_{K,W}(f,g)

(25)

Proof.

1.

Let
r\in C(f(K),g(W))
, then

\displaystyle\left\lVert I-r\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(f(K))}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x_{n}\in K}\left\lVert(I-r)f(x_{n})\right\rVert_{2}=\sup_{x_{n}\in K}\left\lVert f(x_{n})-r\circ f(x_{n})\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x_{n}\in K}\left\lVert f(x_{n})-g(x_{o})\right\rVert_{2}\text{ where }x_{o}=g^{-1}\circ r\circ f(x_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{x_{n}\in K}\inf_{x_{o}\in W}\left\lVert f(x_{n})-g(x_{o})\right\rVert_{2}.

2.

g
is injective on
X
, hence we can define
r^{\prime}
such that
r^{\prime}=g\circ r\circ g^{-1}:g(X)\to g(r(X))\subset g(Y)
such that
r^{\prime}\in\emb(g(X),g(Y))
, and thus

,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(I-r^{\prime}\right)\circ g(x)\right\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert g(x)-g\circ r(x)\right\rVert_{2}\leq\operatornamewithlimits{Lip}(g)\left\lVert I-r\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X)}

(26)

where we have used
\left\lVert r(x)-x\right\rVert_{2}\leq\left\lVert I-r\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X)}
.

3.

For every
x\in r(K)
, we have a
y\in K
such that

, thus
\forall x\in r(K)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left(I-r^{-1}\right)(x)\right\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert\left(r-I\right)(y)\right\rVert_{2}.

(27)

But

is clearly surjective onto it’s range, hence taking the supremum over all

yields

\displaystyle\left\lVert I-r^{-1}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(r(K))}=\left\lVert I-r\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(K)}

(28)

4.

As
g\in\emb(W,\mathbb{R}^{m})
, the map
g:W\to g(W)
is a homeomorphism
and there is
g^{-1}\in\emb(g(W),W)
.
For a map

. Also, the opposite is valid as

\displaystyle B_{K,X}(g\circ f,g\circ h)

\displaystyle=\inf_{r\in\emb(g\circ f(K),g\circ h(X))}\|I-r\|_{L^{\infty}(g\circ f(K))}

\displaystyle=\inf_{r=g\circ\hat{r}\circ g^{-1}\in\emb(g\circ f(K),g\circ h(X))}\|I-g\circ\hat{r}\circ g^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(g\circ f(K))}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\hat{r}\in\emb(f(K),h(X))}\|g\circ(I-\hat{r})\circ g^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(g\circ f(K))}

\displaystyle\leq\operatornamewithlimits{Lip}(g)\inf_{\hat{r}\in\emb(f(K),h(X))}\|(I-\hat{r})\circ g^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(g\circ f(K))}

\displaystyle\leq\operatornamewithlimits{Lip}(g)\inf_{\hat{r}\in\emb(f(K),h(X))}\|I-\hat{r}\|_{L^{\infty}(f(K))}

\displaystyle\leq\operatornamewithlimits{Lip}(g)\,B_{K,X}(f,h)

5.

If we let

\displaystyle B_{K,W}(f,g)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\left\lVert\left(I-r\right)\circ f(x)\right\rVert_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(K)}

(29)

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left\lVert f(x)-g\circ h\circ f^{-1}\circ f(x)\right\rVert_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(K)}\leq\sup_{x\in K}\left\lVert f(x)-g\circ h(x)\right\rVert_{2}.

(30)

6.

Given that

, we have that

, thus the infimum in Eqn.

7.

Note that for any

,

, and so we have

\displaystyle B_{K,W}(f,g)\leq\left\lVert I-r^{\prime}\circ r\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(f(K))}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert I-r\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(f(K))}+\left\lVert r-r^{\prime}\circ r\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(f(K))}

(31)

\displaystyle=\left\lVert I-r\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(f(K))}+\left\lVert I-r^{\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(r\circ f(K))}

(32)

where we have used that

is injective for the final equality. This holds for all possible

, hence we have the result.

8.

Recall that
f\in\emb(K,W)
,

,

\displaystyle r\circ f=g\circ g^{-1}\circ r\circ f.

Thus Lemma

\displaystyle B_{K,X}(f,g\circ h)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert I-r\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(f(K))}+B_{K,X}(r\circ f,g\circ h)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert I-r\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(f(K))}+B_{K,X}(g\circ g^{-1}\circ r\circ f,g\circ h)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert I-r\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(f(K))}+\operatornamewithlimits{Lip}(g)\,B_{K,X}(g^{-1}\circ r\circ f,h),

which proves the claim.

9.

Let
r_{\epsilon}\in\emb(f(K),g(W))
be such that
\left\lVert I-r_{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}\left(\range(f)\right)}\leq B_{K,W}(f,g)+\epsilon

, there exists

such that

. From injectivity of

, we have that

g^{-1}\circ r_{\epsilon}\circ f\in\emb(K,W)

\mu^{\prime}_{\epsilon}\coloneqq{(g^{-1}\circ r_{\epsilon}\circ f)}{}_{\#}{\mu}

{g}{}_{\#}{\mu^{\prime}_{\epsilon}}={r_{\epsilon}\circ f}{}_{\#}{\mu}

\displaystyle\operatorname{W}_{2}\left({f}{}_{\#}{\mu},{g}{}_{\#}{\mu^{\prime}_{\epsilon}}\right)=\operatorname{W}_{2}\left({f}{}_{\#}{\mu},{r_{\epsilon}\circ f}{}_{\#}{\mu}\right)

(33)

and so

\displaystyle\operatorname{W}_{2}\left({f}{}_{\#}{\mu},{g}{}_{\#}{\mu^{\prime}_{\epsilon}}\right)\leq\left(\int_{K}\left\lVert I-r_{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}d{f}{}_{\#}{\mu}\right)^{1/2}\leq B_{K,W}(f,g)+\epsilon.

(34)

As the set

is compact, by Prokhoros’s theorem, see

and thus

in Wasserstein-2 metric and we see that

\displaystyle\operatorname{W}_{2}\left({f}{}_{\#}{\mu},{g}{}_{\#}{\mu_{o}}\right)\leq B_{K,W}(f,g).

(35)

C.3

C.3.1

can not be Mapped Extendably to the Trefoil Knot

We first show that there are no maps
E\coloneqq T\circ R
where
R\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
T
is a homeomorphism and
E(S^{1})
is a trefoil knot. We use the fact that the trivial knot
S^{1}
and the trefoil knot
\mathcal{M}=f(S^{1})
are not equivalent, that is, there are no homeomorphisms in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
that map
S^{1}
to
\mathcal{M}
. Indeed, by
(
Murasugi 2008
, Section 3.2)
, the trefoil knot
\mathcal{M}
and its mirror image are not equivalent, whereas the trivial knot

The input space

First, identify at least one point within each affine polyhedra
\left\{\mathcal{H}_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{n_{j}}
. Then identify the boundaries between polyhedra. The boundaries between sections are always one affine ‘piece’ of piecewise hyperplanes
\left\{\mathcal{H}_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{n_{j}}

corresponds to a
\operatorname{ReLU}

The above algorithm recovers all of the weights exactly provided that the LRA is satisfied. The LRA is satisfied if for every distinct

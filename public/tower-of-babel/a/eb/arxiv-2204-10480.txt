Restricting Representations from a complex reductive group to a real form
Lucas Mason-Brown

Abstract
. Let G G be a complex connected reductive algebraic group and let G ℝ G_{\mathbb{R}} be a real form of G G . We construct a sequence of functors L i ​ ℛ L_{i}\mathcal{R} from admissible (resp. finite-length) representations of G G to admissible (resp. finite-length) representations of G ℝ G_{\mathbb{R}} . We establish many basic properties of these functors, including their behavior with respect to infinitesimal character, associated variety, and restriction to a maximal compact subgroup. We deduce that each L i ​ ℛ L_{i}\mathcal{R} takes unipotent representations of G G to unipotent representations of G ℝ G_{\mathbb{R}} . Taking the alternating sum of L i ​ ℛ L_{i}\mathcal{R} , we get a well-defined homomorphism on the level of characters. We compute this homomorphism in the case when G ℝ G_{\mathbb{R}} is split.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a complex connected reductive algebraic group and let
G_{\mathbb{R}}
be a real form of
G
. Write
M_{fl}(G)
(resp.
M_{fl}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
) for the category of finite-length admissible representations of
G
(resp.
G_{\mathbb{R}}
). There are many difficult questions about admissible representations of real reductive groups which have (relatively) easy answers in the case of complex groups. One example is the classification of unipotent representations. Thus, it is natural to look for a relationship between the categories
M_{fl}(G)
and
M_{fl}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
. One such relationship is
base change
(see
[
AC89
, Chapter 1.7]
). This is a map

\mathrm{BC}:KM_{fl}(G_{\mathbb{R}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}KM_{fl}(G)

from the Grothendieck group of
M_{fl}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
to the Grothendieck group of
M_{fl}(G)
. In Section
3
, we will define a homomorphism in the
opposite
direction. In fact, we will define a sequence of functors

(1.0.1)

L_{i}\mathcal{R}:M_{fl}(G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}M_{fl}(G_{\mathbb{R}}),\qquad i\geq 0.

These functors have nice behavior with respect to several important invariants, including infinitesimal character and associated variety (see Lemma
3.0.1
and Proposition
3.0.2
).

Taking the alternating sum of
L_{i}R
, we get a group homomorphism

(1.0.2)

\mathcal{R}:KM_{fl}(G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\to}}KM_{fl}(G_{\mathbb{R}}),\qquad\mathcal{R}[X]=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}[L_{i}\mathcal{R}(X)]

This homomorphism has even nicer behavior than the individual functors. In particular, the restriction of
\mathcal{R}[X]
to a maximal compact subgroup is given by a relatively simple formula (see Corollary
3.0.5
).

In Section
4
, we will compute the homomorphism (
1.0.2
), in the case when
G_{\mathbb{R}}
is split, in terms of the basis of standard representations.

In Section
5
, we will show that the functors
L_{i}\mathcal{R}
take unipotent representations of
G
to unipotent representations of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
. The classification and construction of unipotent representations of
G
is relatively well-understood, see
[
LMM21
]
,
[
MM
]
. So the functors
L_{i}\mathcal{R}
should provide new and useful constructions of unipotent representations of real reductive groups.

Restriction of representations

We continue with the notation of Section

. Consider the categories

\displaystyle M(G_{\mathbb{R}})

\displaystyle=\text{category of }(\mathfrak{g},K)\text{-modules}

\displaystyle M(G)

\displaystyle=\text{category of }(\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g},G_{\Delta})\text{-modules}

(resp.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}(X_{1}\wedge...\wedge\widehat{X}_{i}\wedge...\wedge X_{n}\otimes X_{i}v)

\displaystyle\sum_{j<k}(-1)^{j+k}([X_{j},X_{k}]\wedge X_{1}\wedge...\wedge\widehat{X}_{j}\wedge...\wedge\widehat{X}_{k}\wedge...\wedge X_{n}\otimes v)

.

\widehat{H}\simeq\widehat{T}\times\widehat{A}\simeq X^{*}(H)\times\mathfrak{h}^{*},\qquad\widehat{H}_{\mathbb{R}}\simeq\widehat{T}_{\mathbb{R}}\times\widehat{A}_{\mathbb{R}}\simeq X^{*}(H)/2X^{*}(H)\times\mathfrak{h}^{*}

\mathbb{C}(\lambda,\nu)
(resp.
\mathbb{C}(\overline{\lambda},\nu)

H
(resp.
H_{\mathbb{R}}

\mathbb{C}(\lambda,\nu)

H

\frac{1}{2}(\nu+\lambda,\nu-\lambda)\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}\times\mathfrak{h}^{*}

\nu\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}

(resp.

\Lambda_{\mathbb{C}}\simeq\{(\epsilon,\delta)\in X^{*}(H)\times\mathfrak{h}^{*}\mid\frac{1}{2}(\delta\pm\epsilon)\in\Lambda\},\qquad\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}\simeq\{(\overline{\epsilon},\epsilon)\in X^{*}(H)/2X^{*}(H)\times\mathfrak{h}^{*}\mid\epsilon\in\Lambda\}.

For

of

is an irreducible finite-dimensional representation of

. As a representation of

, it is isomorphic to
F(\frac{1}{2}(\delta+\epsilon))\otimes F(\frac{1}{2}(\delta-\epsilon))

is the irreducible algebraic representation of

with extremal weight
\epsilon
. For
(\overline{\epsilon},\epsilon)\in\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}

Recall that the classes

.

For each

\mathcal{R}[I_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda,\nu)]=[I_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{\lambda},\frac{1}{2}(\lambda+\nu))].

The proof will come after several lemmas. The first step is to prove Theorem

Lemma 4.0.2
.

The following are true:

(i)

\gr[I_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{0},0)]=[\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}_{\theta}}]
in
K^{K}(\mathcal{N}_{\theta})
.

(ii)

\gr[I_{\mathbb{C}}(0,0)]=[\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}_{-\Delta}}]
in
K^{G_{\Delta}}(\mathcal{N}_{-\Delta})
.

Proof.

Kostant shows in
[
Kos69
]
that
[I_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{0},0)]=_{K}\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{N}_{\theta}]
as representations of
K
. Since

is reductive,
\gr[I_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{0},0)]=_{K}[I_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{0},0)]
. Hence,
\gr[I_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{0},0)]=_{K}\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{N}_{\theta}]

\gr[I_{\mathbb{C}}(0,0)]=[\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{N}_{-\Delta}}]
in
K^{G_{\Delta}}(\mathcal{N}_{-\Delta})

∎

Lemma 4.0.3

There is an identity in

By Corollary
3.0.4

(4.0.2)

\gr\mathcal{R}[I_{\mathbb{C}}(0,0)]=R\gr[I_{\mathbb{C}}(0,0)]

By Lemma

Thus,

\gr\mathcal{R}[I_{\mathbb{C}}(0,0)]=\gr[I_{\mathbb{R}}(0,0)]

for the Grothendieck group of finite-length

Lemma 4.0.4

For

Define

Note that

[I_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda+\gamma,\nu+\gamma^{\prime})]\otimes[F_{\mathbb{C}}(\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime})]=\sum_{(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\in\Delta(F_{\mathbb{C}}(\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime})}[I_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda+\gamma+\mu,\nu+\gamma^{\prime}+\mu^{\prime})]

In particular (setting

and

(4.0.4)

[I_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda+\gamma,\nu+\gamma)]\otimes[F_{\mathbb{C}}(\epsilon,\epsilon)]=\sum_{\mu\in\Delta(\epsilon)}[I_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda+\gamma+\mu,\nu+\gamma+\mu)]

to both sides of (

\mathcal{R}[I_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda+\gamma,\nu+\gamma)]\otimes[F_{\mathbb{R}}(\overline{\epsilon},\epsilon)]=\sum_{\mu\in F(\epsilon)}\mathcal{R}[I_{\mathbb{C}}(\lambda+\gamma+\mu,\nu+\gamma+\mu)]

Proof of Theorem
4.0.1
.

By Lemma

(applied in the case when

is a coherent family. And by Lemma

\Theta_{(0,0)}(\overline{0},0)=[I_{\mathbb{R}}(0,0)]

Taking

and applying Corollary

, we deduce

5.
Unipotent representations

A
complex nilpotent cover
for
G_{\mathbb{R}}
is a finite connected
G
-equivariant cover
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}
of a nilpotent co-adjoint
G
-orbit
\mathbb{O}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Write
\mathsf{Cov}_{n}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
for the set of isomorphism classes of complex nilpotent covers. In
[
LMM21
]
, we associate a primitive ideal
I(\widetilde{\mathbb{O}})\subset U(\mathfrak{g})
to each nilpotent cover
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}\in\mathsf{Cov}_{n}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
. We call
I(\widetilde{\mathbb{O}})
the
unipotent ideal
attached to
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}
. Below, we record some basic properties of unipotent ideals. Recall that the
associated variety
of a two-sided ideal
I\subset U(\mathfrak{g})
is the
G
-invariant subset
V(I)\subset\mathfrak{g}^{*}
defined by the ideal
\gr(I)\subset S(\mathfrak{g})\simeq\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]
.

Proposition 5.0.1

(Proposition 6.1.2,
[
LMM21
]
and Theorem 5.0.1,
[
MM
]
)
.

For each
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}\in\mathsf{Cov}_{n}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
, the ideal
I(\widetilde{\mathbb{O}})\subset U(\mathfrak{g})
has the following properties:

(i)

I(\widetilde{\mathbb{O}})
is completely prime.

(ii)

I(\widetilde{\mathbb{O}})
is maximal (among two-sided ideals in
U(\mathfrak{g})
).

(iii)

V(I(\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}))=\overline{\mathbb{O}}
.

Definition 5.0.2
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}\in\mathsf{Cov}_{n}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
. Write
M_{\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
for the full subcategory of
M_{fl}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
consisting of
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-modules
X
annihilated by the ideal
I(\widetilde{\mathbb{O}})
. A
unipotent representation
of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
attached to
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}
is an irreducible object in
M_{\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
.

Remark 5.0.3
.

If
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
is a nilpotent adjoint orbit for the Langlands dual group
G^{\vee}
, there is a notion of a
special unipotent representation
attached to
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}
, defined by Barbasch and Vogan in
[
BV85
]
. In
[
LMM21
]
, we attach to
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}
a complex nilpotent cover
d(\mathbb{O}^{\vee})\in\mathsf{Cov}_{n}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
such that the special unipotent representations attached to
\mathbb{O}^{\vee}
coincide with the unipotent representations attached to
d(\mathbb{O}^{\vee})
. Thus, Definition
5.0.2
generalizes the notion of ‘special unipotent’.

If
X\in M_{fl}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
, the associated variety
\mathcal{V}(X)
of
X
is the
G
-saturation of the associated variety
V(\mathrm{Ann}(X))
of its annihilator.

Lemma 5.0.4
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}\in\mathsf{Cov}_{n}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
and let
X\in M_{fl}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
. Then
X\in M_{\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
if and only if:

(i)

X
has infinitesimal character
\gamma(\widetilde{\mathbb{O}})
, and

(ii)

\mathcal{V}(X)\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{O}}
.

Proof.

The ‘only if’ direction is trivial. We proceed to the ‘if’ direction. Suppose
X
satisfies conditions (i) and (ii). Let
Y
be an irreducible composition factor of
X
and let
I\subset U(\mathfrak{g})
be the annihilator of
Y
. It suffices to show that
I=I(\widetilde{\mathbb{O}})
.

Property (i) implies that
I
is a primitive ideal with infinitesimal character
\gamma(\widetilde{\mathbb{O}})
. Property (ii) implies that
V(I)=G\mathcal{V}(Y)\subset G\mathcal{V}(X)\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{O}}
.

By an old result of Duflo (
[
Duf77
]
), there is a unique maximal ideal of each infinitesimal character, and
I(\widetilde{\mathbb{O}})
is maximal by Proposition
5.0.1
(ii). So there must be an inclusion
I\subseteq I(\widetilde{\mathbb{O}})
. If this inclusion is proper, then
V(I)\supsetneq V(I(\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}))=\overline{\mathbb{O}}
(see
[
BK76
, Corollary 3.6]
). This is a contradiction. So indeed,
I=I(\widetilde{\mathbb{O}})
, as asserted.
∎

Note that
\mathsf{Cov}_{n}(G)\simeq\mathsf{Cov}_{n}(G_{\mathbb{R}})\times\mathsf{Cov}_{n}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
. If
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{L}\times\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{R}\in\mathsf{Cov}_{n}(G)
, then

\mathbb{O}_{L}\cap\mathbb{O}_{R}\cap(\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta})^{*}\neq\emptyset\implies\mathbb{O}_{L}=\mathbb{O}_{R}

So
M_{\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{L}\times\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{R}}(G)=0
unless
\mathbb{O}_{L}=\mathbb{O}_{R}
.

Corollary 5.0.5
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{L}\times\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{R}\in\mathsf{Cov}_{n}(G)
. Then for each
0\leq i\leq d
, the functor
L_{i}\mathcal{R}
takes
M_{\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{L}\times\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{R}}(G)
to
M_{\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{L}}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
.

Proof.

Suppose
X\in M_{\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{L}\times\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{R}}(G)
. Then by Lemma
5.0.4
,
(\gamma_{L},\gamma_{R})=(\gamma(\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{L}),\gamma(\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{R}))
and
\mathcal{V}(X)\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{O}}_{L}\times\overline{\mathbb{O}}_{L}
. By Proposition
3.0.2
(v), there is an inclusion

\mathcal{V}(L_{i}\mathcal{R}(X))\subseteq\mathrm{Supp}(L_{i}R(\gr(X)))

and by Lemma
2.0.1
(iv), there is an inclusion

\mathrm{Supp}(L_{i}R(\gr(X)))\subseteq\mathcal{V}(X)\cap\mathfrak{g}_{L}^{*}\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{O}}_{L}.

Hence

\mathcal{V}(L_{i}\mathcal{R}(X))\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{O}}_{L}.

On the other hand,
L_{i}\mathcal{R}(X)
has infinitesimal character
\gamma(\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{L})
by Lemma
3.0.1
(iii). Hence,
L_{i}\mathcal{R}(X)\in M_{\widetilde{\mathbb{O}}_{L}}(G_{\mathbb{R}})
by a second application of Lemma
5.0.4
.
∎

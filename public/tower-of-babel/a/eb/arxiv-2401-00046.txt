Fluctuation Theorem on a Riemannian Manifold
Yifan Cai, Tao Wang, Liu Zhao

Abstract
Based on the covariant underdamped and overdamped Langevin equations with Stratonovich coupling to multiplicative noises and the associated Fokker-Planck equations on Riemannian manifold, we present the first law of stochastic thermodynamics on the trajectory level. The corresponding fluctuation theorems are also established, with the total entropy production of the Brownian particle and the heat reservoir playing the role of dissipation function.



I
Introduction

Since the early 1990s, fluctuation theorems (FTs)
evans1994equilibrium
;
gallavotti1995dynamical
;
crooks1999entropy
have played an
indispensable role in understanding the origin of
macroscopic irreversibility. Such theorems, often realized in the
form of unequal probabilities for the forward and reversed processes, greatly helped in
resolving the long lasting puzzles and debates regarding Boltzmann’s H-theorem,
known as Loschmidt paradox. Based on Sekimoto’s work
sekimoto1998langevin

on stochastic energetics, Seifert
seifert2005entropy
was able to establish a
version of FT associated to the stochastic trajectories
described by overdamped Langevin equation (OLE), and subsequent works

imparato2006fluctuation
;
chernyak2006path
;
ohkuma2007fluctuation
extended
the construction to the cases of various generalized forms of Langevin equation (LE).

Can we establish FTs on the trajectory level
on curved Riemannian manifold?
This is the question we wish to address in this work.
In recent years, stochastic thermodynamics has gained increasing importance
in understanding phenomena at the mesoscopic scale
randel2004molecular
;
muthukumar2006simulation
;
seifert2012stochastic
;
qian2016entropy
;
golokolenov2023thermodynamics
. There are certain realistic scenarios,
such as the diffusion of individual protein molecule on a biological membrane,
which calls for a construction of LE and FT
on Riemannian manifolds. Another motivation for the quest of stochastic thermodynamics
and FT on Riemannian manifolds is to take it as a midway step towards
general relativistic description of these fields, which is important because,
in essence, every physics system must abide by the principles of relativity,
whereas the spacetime symmetries in general relativity
impose much stronger restrictions, making
it harder to break the time reversal symmetry.

Historically, there have been some
repeated attempts in the construction of LE on Riemannian manifolds
in the mathematical
Elworthy1998
;
hsu2002stochastic
;
10.1007/BFb0088730
;
emery2012stochastic
;
armstrong2016coordinate
;
armstrong2018intrinsic

and physical
robert1977covariant
;
robert1985covariant

literature. However, the first law and other thermodynamic
relations were not considered in these works. Xing
et al.

ding2020covariant
;
ding2022covariant
explored an Ito-type nonlinear LE and established
a FT on Riemannian manifold. Most of these works
have interpreted the word “covariance”
in the sense of second order (or
jet bundle
) geometry, which is
different from the usual coordinate covariance in
standard Riemannian geometry. Ref.
robert1977covariant
seems to be an exception.
However, the difference between our work and
robert1977covariant
is acute:
the LE in
robert1977covariant
is presented only in the configuration space,
something like the OLE to be described in Section
II.2
.
However, as will be explained near the end of Section
II.2
,
the covariance of LE in
robert1977covariant

actually holds only in flat manifolds and only with respect to
linear coordinate transformations, while our work covers both the configuration space (OLE)
and phase space (ULE) descriptions, and our formalism is genuinely covariant under general
coordinate transformation in the sense of first order geometry.

II
Langevin and Fokker-Planck equations on Riemannian manifold

II.1
Underdamped case

In Ref.
cai2023relativistic
, the relativistic
covariant underdamped Langevin equation (ULE) on
pseudo-Riemannian spacetime

is established. The same procedure can be used for constructing the LE
for a point particle of mass
m>0
moving
on
d
-dimensional
Riemannian space

M
with positive definite
metric
g_{\mu\nu}(x)
, so we directly present the result,

\displaystyle{\rm d}\tilde{x}_{t}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{p}_{t}^{\mu}}{m}{\rm d}t,

(1)

\displaystyle{\rm d}\tilde{p}_{t}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\left[R^{\mu}{}_{a}\circ_{S}{\rm d}\tilde{w}^{a}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}R^{\mu}{}_{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\nu}}R^{\nu}{}_{a}{\rm d}t\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{m}K^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\tilde{p}_{t}^{\nu}{\rm d}t+f^{\mu}_{\text{ex}}{\rm d}t-\frac{1}{m}\Gamma^{\mu}{}_{\alpha\beta}\tilde{p}_{t}^{\alpha}\tilde{p}_{t}^{\beta}{\rm d}t,

(2)

where
R^{\mu}{}_{a}
represent the stochastic amplitudes
which may depend on
\tilde{x}^{\mu}
and
\tilde{p}^{\mu}
,
\Gamma^{\mu}{}_{\alpha\beta}
is the
Christoffel connection associated with
g_{\mu\nu}(x)
,
K^{\mu}{}_{\nu}
is the
tensorial damping coefficient (referred to as damping tensor henceforth),
and
{\rm d}\tilde{w}^{a}_{t}
are Gaussian noises with probability distribution functions (PDFs)

\displaystyle\Pr[{\rm d}\tilde{w}_{t}^{a}={\rm d}w^{a}]=\frac{1}{(2\pi{\rm d}t)^{d/2}}\exp\left[-\frac{\delta_{ab}{\rm d}w^{a}{\rm d}w^{b}}{2{\rm d}t}\right].

(3)

The symbol
\circ_{S}
represents the Stratonovich coupling which ensures the chain rule
in stochastic calculus.
Greek indices
\mu,\nu,\cdots

label spatial directions and latin indices
a,b,\cdots

label independent noises,
which are all running from 1 to
d
.
Tilded variables like
\tilde{x}^{\mu},\tilde{p}^{\mu}

represent random variables, and the un-tilded symbols like
x^{\mu},p^{\mu}

their realizations. In particular,
(x^{\mu},p^{\mu})
denotes the coordinate of
the Brownian particle on
TM
in a concrete realization.

Some remarks are in due here.

(i) The LE described above is a system of stochastic differential equations
on the tangent bundle
TM

regarded as the space of micro states of the Brownian particle. Normally, the
space of micro states for a particle is taken to be the cotangent bundle
T^{\ast}M
.
However, due to the non-degeneracy of the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
, the tangent and cotangent
spaces are dual to each other, and the tangent and cotangent bundles can be
used interchangeably (see
sarbach2013rel
;
sarbach2014geo
;
Acuna-Cardenas
for
explicit use of both approaches).
Recall that
TM
is naturally equipped with the Sasaki metric

sarbach2013rel
;
sarbach2014geo
;
sasaki1958differential

\displaystyle\hat{g}:=g_{\mu\nu}{\rm d}x^{\mu}\otimes{\rm d}x^{\nu}+g_{\mu\nu}\theta^{\mu}\otimes\theta^{\nu},

where
\theta^{\mu}={\rm d}p^{\mu}+\Gamma^{\mu}{}_{\alpha\beta}p^{\alpha}{\rm d}x^{\beta}
,
together with the invariant volume element [here
g(x):=\det g_{\mu\nu}(x)
]

\displaystyle{\rm d}^{2d}X

\displaystyle=g(x)~{\rm d}x^{1}\wedge{\rm d}x^{2}\wedge...\wedge{\rm d}x^{d}\wedge{\rm d}p^{1}\wedge...\wedge{\rm d}p^{d}.

As explained in
sarbach2013rel
;
sarbach2014geo
,
the Sasaki metric is closely connected to the symplectic structure on
TM
.

Notice that the metric
g_{\mu\nu}(x)
on
M
plays an indispensable role while
obtaining the above Sasaki metric and also while describing the last term of
eq. (
2
).

(ii) The choice for the stochastic amplitude
R^{\mu}{}_{a}
is non-unique.
Different choices correspond to different Langevin systems, and the result of this
work should hold for all choices such that

R^{\mu}{}_{a}
is an invertible matrix function which is differentiable in
(x^{\mu},p^{\mu})

and transforms as a vector for each fixed
a
.

(iii) Our approach to LE is the
traditional
one, as opposed to the more abstract
nonlinear approach used in
ding2020covariant
;
ding2022covariant
.
Eq. (
2
) can be viewed as
the geodesic equation supplemented by additional force terms, including a
stochastic force

\displaystyle\xi^{\mu}:=R^{\mu}{}_{a}\circ_{S}{\rm d}\tilde{w}^{a}_{t}/{\rm d}t+\frac{1}{2}R^{\mu}{}_{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\nu}}R^{\nu}{}_{a},

a damping force
f^{\mu}_{\text{dp}}:=-K^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\tilde{p}_{t}^{\nu}/m
and an
external force
f^{\mu}_{\text{ex}}
. The second term in the stochastic force
is known as
additional stochastic force

klimontovich1994nonlinear
;
cai2023relativistic2
, which is required in order for the Brownian particle to be able to
reach thermal equilibrium with the heat reservoir.
In one-dimensional case,
the stochastic force can also be expressed in the form of post-point rule

\xi=R\circ_{p}{\rm d}\tilde{w}_{t}/{\rm d}t
, hence some authors argued that the post-point rule
is better suited for LE with multiplicative noises. However, in higher-dimensional
cases, the post-point rule leads to a different result,

R^{\mu}{}_{a}\circ_{p}{\rm d}\tilde{w}^{a}_{t}/{\rm d}t=R^{\mu}{}_{a}\circ_{S}{\rm d}\tilde{w}^{a}_{t}/{\rm d}t+\frac{1}{2}R^{\nu}{}_{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\nu}}R^{\mu}{}_{a}.

(iv) The Stratonovich coupling maintains the chain rule, which
ensures the covariance in the usual sense in Riemannian geometry.
This makes an important difference from the previous works

10.1007/BFb0088730
;
emery2012stochastic
;
armstrong2016coordinate
;
armstrong2018intrinsic
;
ding2020covariant
;
ding2022covariant
.

(v) Although eqs. (
1
)-(
2
) look the same as their
relativistic counterparts
cai2023relativistic
, there are some essential
differences. First, the time
t

used here is absolute, meaning that eqs. (
1
)-(
2
) are

non-relativistic
; Second, the heat reservoir hiding behind the stochastic
and damping force terms is also non-relativistic, there is no need to worry about the
relativistic effects such as the Tolman-Ehrenfest red shift;
Last, the momentum space is flat, as opposed to the relativistic case.

The external force term depends on the position
x
of the Brownian particle
and an external control parameter
\lambda
, and can be separated into
conservative and non-conservative parts,

\displaystyle f^{\mu}_{\text{ex}}=f^{\mu}_{\text{con}}+f^{\mu}_{\text{noc}}=-\nabla^{\mu}U(x,\lambda)+f^{\mu}_{\text{noc}}.

There is some ambiguity in this decomposition,
e.g.

\displaystyle f^{\mu}_{\text{ex}}=(f^{\mu}_{\text{con}}+\hat{f}^{\mu})+(f^{\mu}_{\text{noc}}-\hat{f}^{\mu})=\hat{f}^{\mu}_{\text{con}}+\hat{f}^{\mu}_{\text{noc}},

where
\hat{f}^{\mu}
is an arbitrary conservative force.
This also leads to an ambiguity in the energy

\displaystyle E(x,p,\lambda)=\frac{1}{2m}g_{\mu\nu}(x)p^{\mu}p^{\nu}+U(x,\lambda)

of the Brownian particle. In the extremal case with
\hat{f}^{\mu}=-f^{\mu}_{\text{con}}
,
The energy will be consisted purely of the kinematic energy.

Since the Stratonovich coupling preserves the chain rule, we have

\displaystyle{\rm d}\tilde{E}_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial E}{\partial p^{\mu}}{\rm d}\tilde{p}^{\mu}_{t}+\frac{\partial E}{\partial x^{\mu}}{\rm d}\tilde{x}_{t}^{\mu}+{\rm d}_{\lambda}U

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}(\tilde{p}_{t})_{\mu}(\xi^{\mu}+f^{\mu}_{\text{dp}}){\rm d}t+\frac{1}{m}(\tilde{p}_{t})_{\mu}f^{\mu}_{\text{noc}}{\rm d}t+{\rm d}_{\lambda}U,

(4)

where
\tilde{E}_{t}
is the energy considered as a random variable and
E
its realization.
The part of the increase of energy caused by the heat reservoir is purely a thermal effect
and thus comprehended as
trajectory heat

{\rm d}\tilde{Q}_{t}
,
the rest part is purely mechanical and should be comprehended
as
trajectory work

{\rm d}\tilde{W}_{t}
,

\displaystyle{\rm d}\tilde{Q}_{t}=\frac{(\tilde{p}_{t})_{\mu}}{m}\left(\xi^{\mu}+f^{\mu}_{\text{dp}}\right){\rm d}t,

\displaystyle{\rm d}\tilde{W}_{t}=\frac{(\tilde{p}_{t})_{\mu}}{m}f^{\mu}_{\text{noc}}{\rm d}t+{\rm d}_{\lambda}U.

(5)

Thus eq. (
4
) becomes the first law of stochastic thermodynamics
on the trajectory level,
i.e.

{\rm d}\tilde{E}_{t}={\rm d}\tilde{Q}_{t}+{\rm d}\tilde{W}_{t}.

The ambiguity in the decomposition of the external force
also leads to an ambiguity in the trajectory work. However,
in any case, the trajectory heat

{\rm d}\tilde{Q}_{t}={\rm d}\tilde{E}_{t}-{\rm d}\tilde{W}_{t}
is always unambiguously defined.
Please be reminded that, unlike the usual heat and work in standard thermodynamics
which are inexact differentials defined on the space of macro states,
the
trajectory heat and work
are only defined
on a stochastic trajectory of the Brownian particle.

The Fokker-Planck equation (FPE) associated to
eqs. (
1
)-(
2
) can also be established on
TM
.
The PDF for the Brownian particle under the
measure
{\rm d}^{2d}X
is denoted as
\Phi_{t}(x,p):=\Pr[\tilde{x}_{t}=x,\tilde{p}_{t}=p]
,
and is clearly coordinate-independent. By use of the diffusion operator method

bakry2014analysis
, one can get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Phi_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\mu}}\left[\frac{1}{2}D^{\mu\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\nu}}\Phi_{t}+\frac{1}{m}K^{\mu}{}_{\nu}p^{\nu}\Phi_{t}-f^{\mu}_{\text{ex}}\Phi_{t}\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{m}\mathcal{L}(\Phi_{t}),

(6)

where
D^{\mu\nu}:=R^{\mu}{}_{a}R^{\nu}{}_{a}
is the diffusion tensor and

\mathcal{L}=p^{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}-\Gamma^{\mu}{}_{\alpha\beta}p^{\alpha}p^{\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\mu}}

is the Liouville vector field on
TM

sarbach2013rel
;
sarbach2014geo
.

Let the non-conservative force be temporarily turned off and the external control
parameter be fixed. Then, after sufficiently long period of time,
the Brownian particle will reach a thermal equilibrium with the
heat reservoir, yielding the equilibrium PDF

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}(x,p)=\frac{1}{Z}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{T}\left(\frac{g_{\mu\nu}p^{\mu}p^{\nu}}{2m}+U(x,\lambda)\right)\right].

(7)

Putting this PDF into the FPE (
6
), one gets
the Einstein relation

\displaystyle D^{\mu\nu}=2TK^{\mu\nu},

(8)

which implies that the damping tensor
K^{\mu\nu}
are not independent of
the stochastic amplitudes
R^{\mu}{}_{a}
and that
K^{\mu\nu}
is invertible as a matrix.
As long as only the FPE is concerned,
there is an additional freedom in the sign choice of
R^{\mu}{}_{a}
, because
D^{\mu\nu}

appear as a quadratic form in
R^{\mu}{}_{a}
.

To facilitate the discussion about FT, we introduce
the time-reversal transform (TRT) for the process ranging from
t_{I}
to
t_{F}
:

\displaystyle I:\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}x^{\mu}(t)\mapsto x^{\mu}(t_{F}+t_{I}-t)\cr p^{\mu}(t)\mapsto-p^{\mu}(t_{F}+t_{I}-t)\end{array}\right.,

which is often briefly described as
I:(x,p)\mapsto(x,-p)
for short. Notice that
the infinitesimal time increment
{\rm d}t
is not affected by such transformation
and remains to be positive.

It is obvious that TRT preserves the metric,

i.e.
I^{*}\hat{g}=\hat{g}
.
The damping force
f^{\mu}_{\text{dp}}|_{X}=-K^{\mu}{}_{\nu}p^{\nu}/m
reverses
sign under TRT, thus the damping tensor must be invariant under TRT,

\displaystyle K^{\mu\nu}|_{I(X)}=K^{\mu\nu}|_{X},\quad X=(x,p),\quad I(X)=(x,-p).

Eq. (
8
) implies that the diffusion tensor
is also invariant under TRT. There is some freedom in choosing
the stochastic amplitudes, and hence also in determining their behaviors under TRT.
Here we assume the simplest transformation rule,

\displaystyle R^{\mu}{}_{a}|_{I(X)}=R^{\mu}{}_{a}|_{X}.

An immediate consequence is that the additional stochastic force should
reverse sign under TRT. The coefficients of
{\rm d}t
in eq. (
2
)
can be classified into even and odd parts under TRT,
i.e.

\displaystyle F^{\mu}=f^{\mu}_{\text{ex}}-\frac{1}{m}\Gamma^{\mu}{}_{\alpha\beta}p^{\alpha}p^{\beta}\text{\, with \,}F^{\mu}|_{I(X)}=F^{\mu}|_{X},

and

\displaystyle\bar{F}^{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}R^{\mu}{}_{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\nu}}R^{\nu}{}_{a}-\frac{1}{m}K^{\mu}{}_{\nu}p^{\nu}\text{\, with \,}\bar{F}^{\mu}|_{I(X)}=-\bar{F}^{\mu}|_{X}.

Then eq. (
2
) can be recast in a simpler form,

\displaystyle{\rm d}\tilde{p}^{\mu}_{t}=R^{\mu}{}_{a}\circ_{S}{\rm d}\tilde{w}^{\mu}_{t}+F^{\mu}{\rm d}t+\bar{F}^{\mu}{\rm d}t.

(9)

The last equation is the starting point for introducing the discretized version of the
ULE in Appendix
B.2
.

II.2
Overdamped case

The stochastic mechanics characterized by LE is a branch of
physics with multiple time scales, of which the smallest one is the time scale

{\rm d}t
which allows for a sufficient number of collisions between the Brownian particle
and the heat reservoir particles which cause little changes in the state of
the Brownian particle
sekimoto2010stochastic
. If the temporal resolution

\Delta t
greatly exceeds
{\rm d}t
but is still smaller than the relaxation time,
the ULE (
1
)-(
2
) emerges.
If
\Delta t
is further increased so that it is
much larger than the relaxation time, the overdamped limit emerges.

The relaxation process of the Brownian particle
can be viewed either as the process by which the damping force attains
a state of mechanical equilibrium with other forces (mechanical
relaxation process), or as the process by
which the Brownian particle achieves local thermal equilibrium with the
heat reservoir (thermodynamic relaxation process).
The characteristic timescales associated with both processes are

m/\kappa
, where
\kappa
is the eigenvalue of
K^{\mu\nu}
.
Consequently, the OLE should arise when
\Delta t
greatly exceeds
m/\kappa
.

The two kinds of relaxation process correspond to two approaches
for taking the overdamped limit. From the perspective of mechanical relaxation process,
the OLE can be described as the condition for mechanical
equilibrium

\displaystyle 0=f^{\mu}_{\text{dp}}+f^{\mu}_{\text{ex}}+\xi^{\mu}.

(10)

This result is achieved in flat space under the condition that
the damping tensor is position- and momentum-independent

bodrova2016underdamped
;
huang2021quantitative
,
and is often viewed as a stochastic differential equation in
configuration space. However, if the stochastic amplitudes
are momentum-dependent, so is the additional stochastic force. Thus the
mechanical equilibrium condition cannot be understood
as a stochastic differential equation in
configuration space. To avoid the above difficulty, let us consider the
simpler situation in which the stochastic amplitudes are momentum-independent.
Using the thermodynamic relaxation approach
durang2015overdamped
,
it will be shown that, even in this simpler situation,
a nontrivial additional stochastic force term still arises in the corresponding OLE.

The overdamped condition implies that the momentum space PDF already
reaches the equilibrium form, while the configuration space PDF does not,
so that the full PDF
\Phi_{t}(x,p)
can be factorized,

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}(x,p)=\rho_{t}(x)P^{s}(x,p),

where

\displaystyle P^{s}(x,p):=\frac{1}{(2\pi mT)^{-d/2}}\exp\left[-\frac{g_{\mu\nu}(x)p^{\mu}p^{\nu}}{2mT}\right]

(11)

is the (Maxwell) equilibrium PDF in momentum space,
and
\displaystyle\rho_{t}(x):=\int g^{{1}/{2}}{\rm d}^{d}p~\Phi_{t}(x,p)=\Pr[\tilde{x}_{t}=x]
is the PDF in configuration space. By adding the first order
corrections from the near equilibrium states, the overdamped FPE is found to be
(see Appendix.
A
),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{t}=\nabla_{\mu}\left[\frac{1}{2}\hat{D}^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\nu}\rho_{t}-\hat{K}^{\mu}{}_{\nu}f^{\nu}_{\text{ex}}\rho_{t}\right],

(12)

where
\hat{K}^{\mu\nu}=(K^{-1})^{\mu\nu}
and
\hat{D}^{\mu\nu}=4T^{2}(D^{-1})^{\mu\nu}
.
Two important properties of eq. (
12
) are worth of notice:
1) The Einstein relation still holds for the rescaled damping and diffusion tensors

\displaystyle\hat{D}^{\mu\nu}=2T\hat{K}^{\mu\nu};

and 2) The Boltzmann distribution

\displaystyle\rho_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{Z_{x}}{\rm e}^{-U(x,\lambda)/T}

is a solution of eq. (
12
) provided the non-conservative force is turned off
and the external parameter is fixed, wherein
Z_{x}
represents the configuration space
normalization factor, which should not be confused with the normalization
factor
Z
appeared in eq. (
7
).

Using the diffusion operator method,
it can be checked that the LE associated with
eq. (
12
) takes the same form as eq. (
10
),
but with a momentum-independent stochastic force term

\displaystyle\xi^{\mu}=R^{\mu}{}_{a}\circ_{S}{\rm d}\tilde{w}^{a}_{t}/{\rm d}t+\frac{1}{2}R^{\mu}{}_{a}\nabla_{\nu}(\hat{K}^{\nu}{}_{\alpha}R^{\alpha}{}_{a}).

(13)

Denoting

\displaystyle\hat{R}^{\mu}{}_{a}:=\hat{K}^{\mu}{}_{\nu}R^{\nu}{}_{a},\,\hat{F}^{\mu}=\hat{K}^{\mu}{}_{\nu}f^{\nu}_{\text{ex}}+\frac{1}{2}\hat{R}^{\mu}{}_{a}\nabla_{\nu}\hat{R}^{\nu}{}_{a},

(14)

the OLE can be written in a simpler form

\displaystyle{\rm d}\tilde{x}_{t}^{\mu}=\hat{R}^{\mu}{}_{a}\circ_{S}{\rm d}\tilde{w}^{a}_{t}+\hat{F}^{\mu}{\rm d}t.

(15)

This equation is similar in form to the LE presented in
robert1985covariant
.
However, unlike eq. (
14
), the additional stochastic force
presented in
robert1985covariant
contains only
an ordinary coordinate derivative rather than covariant derivative.
Consequently, the claimed general covariance of the LE of
robert1977covariant

is questionable: it actually holds only for
flat manifolds and only with respects to linear coordinate transformations.

Since the inertial effect can be ignored in the overdamped case, the energy of
the Brownian particle contains only the potential energy,
i.e.
E(x,\lambda)=U(x,\lambda)
.
Using eq. (
10
) and the chain rule, we have

\displaystyle{\rm d}\tilde{E}_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial U}{\partial x^{\mu}}{\rm d}\tilde{x}_{t}^{\mu}+{\rm d}_{\lambda}U=-(f_{\text{con}})_{\mu}{\rm d}\tilde{x}_{t}^{\mu}+{\rm d}_{\lambda}U

\displaystyle=[\xi_{\mu}+(f_{\text{dp}})_{\mu}]{\rm d}\tilde{x}_{t}^{\mu}+(f_{\text{noc}})_{\mu}{\rm d}\tilde{x}^{\mu}_{t}+{\rm d}_{\lambda}U.

Similar to the underdamped case, the energy absorbed from the heat reservoir
is understood as the trajectory heat, and the
rest part of the energy increase as trajectory work. Thus we have,
thanks to eq. (
10
),

\displaystyle{\rm d}\tilde{Q}_{t}=[\xi_{\mu}+(f_{\text{dp}})_{\mu}]{\rm d}\tilde{x}_{t}^{\mu}=-(f_{\text{ex}})_{\mu}{\rm d}\tilde{x}^{\mu}_{t},

(16)

\displaystyle{\rm d}\tilde{W}_{t}=(f_{\text{noc}})_{\mu}{\rm d}\tilde{x}^{\mu}_{t}+{\rm d}_{\lambda}U.

III
Fluctuation theorem

Now we come to the stage for describing FT on Riemannian manifold
based on the description of stochastic trajectories.
Since the trajectory probability in continuous time is hard to deal with,
we adopt the following strategy: first we take a discrete equidistant
set of time nodes
t_{I}=t_{0}<t_{1}<...<t_{n}=t_{F}
to rewrite the LE and
factorize the corresponding trajectory probabilities,
and then take the continuum limit at the end of the calculation.

The stochastic process in discrete time can be viewed as a sequence of
random variables,
i.e.
\tilde{X}_{[t]}=(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{X}_{1},...,\tilde{X}_{n})

with
\tilde{X}_{i}=\tilde{X}_{t_{i}}
for the underdamped case and

\tilde{x}_{[t]}=(\tilde{x}_{0},\tilde{x}_{1},...,\tilde{x}_{n})
,
with
\tilde{x}_{i}=\tilde{x}_{t_{i}}
for the overdamped case.

Before proceeding, it is necessary to clarify the
concepts of
ensemble
and
trajectory
entropy productions.
At the time
t
, the ensemble entropy of the Brownian particle reads

S_{t}=-\int{\rm d}^{2d}X\Phi_{t}\ln\Phi_{t},

and the ensemble entropy production in the process is

\Delta S=S_{t_{F}}-S_{t_{I}}
.
Notice that (throughout this paper, an overline denotes ensemble average,
while
\langle~~\rangle
denotes trajectory average)

-\overline{\ln\rho_{t}}:=-\int g^{{1}/{2}}{\rm d}^{d}x~\rho_{t}\ln\rho_{t}

is
not
the entropy of the overdamped Brownian particle, however, the ensemble
entropy production of the Brownian particle can be represented as the difference
of
-\overline{\ln\rho_{t}}
, because the ensemble entropy for the overdamped Brownian particle
can be evaluated to be

\displaystyle S_{t}=-\overline{\ln\rho_{t}}+\frac{d}{2}+\frac{d}{2}\ln(2\pi mT),

where the last two terms arise from the momentum
space integration of the term involving the distribution
P^{s}(x,p)
given in
eq. (
11
).
Subtracting the initial value from the final value leaves only the difference of

-\overline{\ln\rho_{t}}
,

\displaystyle\Delta S=\overline{\ln\rho_{t_{I}}}-\overline{\ln\rho_{t_{F}}}.

In contrast, the trajectory entropy production is defined simply
to be the difference between the logarithms
of the PDF at the initial and final times,
i.e.

\Delta S_{X_{[t]}}=\ln\Phi_{t_{I}}(X_{0})-\ln\Phi_{t_{F}}(X_{n})

for the underdamped and

\Delta S_{x_{[t]}}=\ln\rho_{t_{I}}(X_{0})-\ln\rho_{t_{F}}(X_{n})

for the overdamped cases.

Now let us consider the underdamped case. It is important to distinguish the
terms
process
and
trajectory
: the latter is a realization of the former.
The forward process
\tilde{X}_{[t]}
refers to a stochastic process governed by the
ULE (
1
)-(
2
), wherein
the external control parameter
\lambda_{[t]}=(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n})

varies over time. Correspondingly, the reversed process
\tilde{X}^{-}_{[t]}
also
refers to a stochastic process governed by the same LE,
but its initial state should be identified with the time-reversal of
the final state of the forward process,
i.e.
\tilde{X}^{-}_{0}=I(\tilde{X}_{n})
,
and the corresponding external control parameter should satisfy

\lambda^{-}_{i}=\lambda_{n-i}
. The reversed trajectory
X_{[t]}=(X_{0},X_{1},...,X_{n})

is defined such that
X_{i}^{-}:=I(X_{n-i})
.

We will prove that the total entropy production,
i.e.
the sum of the
trajectory entropy production with the change of the entropy of the
heat reservoir, should be

\displaystyle\Sigma_{X_{[t]}}=\ln\frac{\Pr[\tilde{X}_{[t]}=X_{[t]}]}{\Pr[\tilde{X}^{-}_{[t]}=X^{-}_{[t]}]}.

(17)

Since the Brownian motion is a Markov process, the trajectory probability
can be decomposed into product of transition probabilities,

\displaystyle\Pr[\tilde{X}_{[t]}=X_{[t]}]

\displaystyle\quad={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Big(}\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\Pr[\tilde{X}_{i+1}=X_{i+1}|\tilde{X}_{i}=X_{i}]{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Big)}\Pr[\tilde{X}_{0}=X_{0}].

A similar decomposition can be made for
\Pr[\tilde{X}^{-}_{[t]}=X^{-}_{[t]}]
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\Sigma_{X_{[t]}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\ln\frac{\Pr[\tilde{X}_{i+1}=X_{i+1}|\tilde{X}_{i}=X_{i}]}{\Pr[\tilde{X}_{i+1}^{-}=X_{i+1}^{-}|\tilde{X}_{i}^{-}=X_{i}^{-}]}+\ln\frac{\Pr[\tilde{X}_{0}=X_{0}]}{\Pr[\tilde{X}^{-}_{0}=X^{-}_{0}]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\ln\frac{\Pr[\tilde{X}_{i+1}=X_{i+1}|\tilde{X}_{i}=X_{i}]}{\Pr[\tilde{X}_{n-i}^{-}=I(X_{i})|\tilde{X}_{n-i-1}^{-}=I(X_{i+1})]}+\ln\frac{\Pr[\tilde{X}_{0}=X_{0}]}{\Pr[\tilde{X}_{n}=X_{n}]},

(18)

where, in the last step, the definitions of the reversed process and
reversed trajectory have been used and rearrange of terms in the summation
has been adopted.
The last term in eq. (
III
) is simply the trajectory
entropy production, because

\Phi_{t_{I}}(X_{0})=\Pr[\tilde{X}_{0}=X_{0}],\quad\Phi_{t_{F}}(X_{n})=\Pr[\tilde{X}_{n}=X_{n}].

On the other hand, the continuum limit of the first term
reads (see Appendix.
B.2
)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\ln\frac{\Pr[\tilde{X}_{i+1}=X_{i+1}|\tilde{X}_{i}=X_{i}]}{\Pr[\tilde{X}_{n-i}^{-}=I(X_{i})|\tilde{X}_{n-i-1}^{-}=I(X_{i+1})]}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{T}\int_{t_{I}}^{t_{F}}{\rm d}t\frac{p^{\mu}}{m}\nabla_{\mu}\left(\mathcal{T}+U\right)+\frac{1}{T}\int_{t_{I}}^{t_{F}}{\rm d}t\frac{p_{\mu}}{m}f^{\mu}_{\text{noc}},

(19)

where
\mathcal{T}:=p^{\mu}p_{\mu}/2m
is the kinematic energy.
According to eq. (
II.1
), the trajectory work should be

\displaystyle\Delta W_{X_{[t]}}=\int_{t_{I}}^{t_{F}}{\rm d}t\left(\frac{p_{\mu}}{m}f^{\mu}_{\text{noc}}+\frac{\partial U}{\partial\lambda}\frac{{\rm d}\lambda}{{\rm d}t}\right),

and the change of energy is

\displaystyle\Delta E_{X_{[t]}}

\displaystyle=\Delta\mathcal{T}_{X_{[t]}}+\Delta U_{X_{[t]}}

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{I}}^{t_{F}}{\rm d}t\frac{p^{\mu}}{m}\nabla_{\mu}\mathcal{T}+\int_{t_{I}}^{t_{F}}{\rm d}t\left(\frac{p^{\mu}}{m}\nabla_{\mu}U+\frac{\partial U}{\partial\lambda}\frac{{\rm d}\lambda}{{\rm d}t}\right).

Since the trajectory heat is
\Delta Q_{X_{[t]}}=\Delta E_{X_{[t]}}-\Delta W_{X_{[t]}}
,
eq. (
III
) can also be rewritten as

\displaystyle\Sigma_{X_{[t]}}=\Delta S_{X_{[t]}}-\frac{1}{T}\Delta Q_{X_{[t]}}=\Delta S_{X_{[t]}}+\Delta S_{\rm Res},

(20)

where, since the heat reservoir maintains in equilibrium, the Clausius equality holds,
the change of the entropy of the heat reservoir reads

\Delta S_{\rm Res}=\Delta Q_{\rm Res}/T=-\Delta Q_{X_{[t]}}/T.

Thus
\Sigma_{X_{[t]}}
is indeed the total entropy production.
Inserting eq. (
20
) back into eq. (
17
),
we get the desired FT

\displaystyle\frac{\Pr[\tilde{X}_{[t]}=X_{[t]}]}{\Pr[\tilde{X}^{-}_{[t]}=X^{-}_{[t]}]}={\rm e}^{\Sigma_{X_{[t]}}}={\rm e}^{\Delta S_{X_{[t]}}-\Delta Q_{X_{[t]}}/T},

(21)

which tells that the process with positive total entropy production
is probabilistically more preferred.

Taking the trajectory average of eq. (
21
),
we get, by use of the Jensen inequality, the following result,

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{-\big\langle\Sigma_{\tilde{X}_{[t]}}\big\rangle}

\displaystyle\leq\big\langle{\rm e}^{-\Sigma_{\tilde{X}_{[t]}}}\big\rangle=\int\mathcal{D}[X_{[t]}]\Pr[\tilde{X}^{-}_{[t]}=X^{-}_{[t]}]

\displaystyle=\int\mathcal{D}[X^{-}_{[t]}]\Pr[\tilde{X}^{-}_{[t]}=X^{-}_{[t]}]=1,

(22)

where
\mathcal{D}[X_{[t]}]={\rm d}X_{0}\wedge{\rm d}X_{1}\wedge...\wedge{\rm d}X_{n}
is
the measure on the trajectory space. It is easy to prove that
the map
X_{[t]}\mapsto X^{-}_{[t]}
preserves the measure,
i.e.
\mathcal{D}[X_{[t]}]=\mathcal{D}[X^{-}_{[t]}]
.
Eq.(
III
) is the so-called integral
FT, which tells that the entropy production is
non-negative in any macroscopic process,
i.e.
\big\langle\Sigma_{\tilde{X}_{[t]}}\big\rangle\geq 0
.

The FT in the overdamped case can be constructed
following a similar fashion, however the processes must be described solely
in configuration space. The definitions of the reversed process
\tilde{x}^{-}_{[t]}
and
the reversed trajectory
x^{-}_{[t]}
are similar to the underdamped case,
with the replacement
X_{i}\to x_{i}
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{x_{[t]}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}:=}\ln\frac{\Pr[\tilde{x}_{[t]}=x_{[t]}]}{\Pr[\tilde{x}^{-}_{[t]}=x^{-}_{[t]}]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\ln\frac{\Pr[\tilde{x}_{i+1}=x_{i+1}|\tilde{x}_{i}=x_{i}]}{\Pr[\tilde{x}_{n-i}^{-}=x_{i}|\tilde{x}_{n-i-1}^{-}=x_{i+1}]}+\ln\frac{\Pr[\tilde{x}_{0}=x_{0}]}{\Pr[\tilde{x}_{n}=x_{n}]}.

In the continuum limit, we have (see Appendix.
B.1
)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\ln\frac{\Pr[\tilde{x}_{i+1}=x_{i+1}|\tilde{x}_{i}=x_{i}]}{\Pr[\tilde{x}_{n-i}^{-}=x_{i}|\tilde{x}_{n-i-1}^{-}=x_{i+1}]}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{T}\int_{t_{I}}^{t_{F}}v_{\mu}f^{\mu}_{\text{ex}}{\rm d}t,

(23)

where
v^{\mu}
is the velocity of the Brownian particle.
According to eq. (
16
),
the trajectory heat is

\displaystyle\Delta Q_{x_{[t]}}=-\int_{t_{I}}^{t_{F}}v_{\mu}f^{\mu}_{\text{ex}}{\rm d}t,

and the trajectory entropy production is

\displaystyle\Delta S_{x_{[t]}}=\ln\rho_{t_{I}}(x_{0})-\ln\rho_{t_{F}}(x_{n})=\ln\frac{\Pr[\tilde{x}_{0}=x_{0}]}{\Pr[\tilde{x}_{n}=x_{n}]}.

Finally, we arrive at the desired FT

\displaystyle\frac{\Pr[\tilde{x}_{[t]}=x_{[t]}]}{\Pr[\tilde{x}^{-}_{[t]}=x^{-}_{[t]}]}={\rm e}^{\Sigma_{x_{[t]}}}={\rm e}^{\Delta S_{x_{[t]}}-\Delta Q_{x_{[t]}}/T}.

The integral FT in the overdamped case can be obtained in complete analogy
to the underdamped case, therefore, there is no need to repeat the construction.

IV
Concluding remarks

The covariant LE and FPE on a Riemannian manifold are constructed
in both underdamped and overdamped cases. The concepts of trajectory heat and
trajectory work are clarified, and the first law on the trajectory
level is established. The Stratonovich coupling plays an important role in
establishing the appropriate form of the first law. In either cases, the corresponding FTs
are proved in both differential and integral forms. These results allow for a
complete extension of the existing stochastic thermodynamics to arbitrary
Riemannian manifolds, which in turn may be helpful in understanding the origin of
irreversibility in certain biological scenarios.

During the construction, we also clarified the link between the PDF with the ensemble and trajectory entropy productions, which may also
shed some light in the parallel constructions in flat spaces. Moreover,
the different forms of the additional stochastic forces in the
underdamped and overdamped cases are also worth of notice. Last, we hope the results
presented here could be inspiring for an ultimate resolution for the fully
general relativistic construction for the FTs.

Appendix A
Overdamped Fokker-Planck equation

Here we outline the procedure for taking the overdamped limit following the line of
Ref.
durang2015overdamped
.
Using the diffusion operator

A=\frac{\delta^{ab}}{2}L_{a}L_{b}+L_{0}\hskip 8.50012pt\text{with }L_{0}=\frac{1}{m}\mathcal{L}-\frac{1}{m}K^{\mu}{}_{\nu}p^{\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\mu}},\hskip 8.50012ptL_{a}=R^{\mu}{}_{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\mu}},

the underdamped FPE can be written as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Phi_{t}=A^{\dagger}\Phi_{t},

(24)

where
A^{\dagger}
is the adjoint of A. The FPE can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bar{\Phi}_{t}=\bar{A}^{\dagger}\bar{\Phi}_{t},

where
\bar{\Phi}_{t}:=[P^{s}]^{-{1}/{2}}\Phi_{t}
and

\bar{A}^{\dagger}:=[P^{s}]^{-{1}/{2}}A^{\dagger}[P^{s}]^{{1}/{2}}
,
and
P^{s}
is given in eq. (
11
).

\bar{A}^{\dagger}
can be decomposed in terms of the
creation and annihilation operators

\displaystyle a_{\mu}=\zeta\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\mu}}+\frac{1}{2\zeta}p_{\mu},\hskip 17.00024pta^{\dagger}_{\mu}=-\zeta\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\mu}}+\frac{1}{2\zeta}p_{\mu}

which obey the commutation relation
[a_{\mu},a_{\nu}^{\dagger}]=g_{\mu\nu}
,
wherein
\zeta=\sqrt{mT}
.

Since
K^{\mu\nu}
is a symmetric tensor, its eigenvectors

e^{\mu}{}_{\hat{\nu}}
constitute an orthonormal basis.
The components of a tensor under the orthonormal basis are
denoted by adding a hat on its index,
e.g.
W_{\hat{\mu}}=e^{\nu}{}_{\hat{\mu}}W_{\nu}
,

V_{\hat{\mu}}=e_{\nu\hat{\mu}}V^{\nu}
,
where
e_{\nu\hat{\mu}}
is the dual basis. For convenience, only lower indices are used
under the orthonormal basis. The commutator between the creation and the
annihilation operators can be rewritten as

[a_{\hat{\mu}},a^{\dagger}_{\hat{\nu}}]=\delta_{\hat{\mu}\hat{\nu}}
.
Moreover,
p_{\mu}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\mu}}
can be decomposed as

\displaystyle p_{\mu}=\zeta e_{\mu\hat{\nu}}(a_{\hat{\nu}}+a^{\dagger}_{\hat{\nu}}),\hskip 17.00024pt\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\mu}}=\frac{1}{2\zeta}e_{\mu\hat{\nu}}(a_{\hat{\nu}}-a^{\dagger}_{\hat{\nu}}).

Using the above notations, one has

\displaystyle\bar{A}^{\dagger}=-\frac{1}{m}\kappa^{\hat{\mu}}N_{\hat{\mu}}+\frac{1}{\zeta}f^{\hat{\mu}}_{\text{ex}}a^{\dagger}_{\hat{\mu}}-\frac{1}{m}\mathcal{L},

where
\kappa^{\hat{\mu}}
represent the eigenvalues of
K^{\mu\nu}
, and

N_{\hat{\mu}}:=a^{\dagger}_{\hat{\mu}}a_{\hat{\mu}}
. Let

\psi_{0}:=[P^{s}]^{{1}/{2}}
,
\psi_{\hat{\mu}}:=a^{\dagger}_{\hat{\mu}}\psi_{0}
.
Clearly, we have
a_{\hat{\mu}}\psi_{0}=0
,
i.e.
the ground state
\psi_{0}

of the Fock space generated by
a^{\dagger}_{\hat{\mu}}

corresponds to the equilibrium distribution
P^{s}
, and
the excited states to non-equilibrium modifications.

In principle,
\bar{\Phi}_{t}
can be expanded as a linear superposition of
the eigenstates of
N_{\hat{\mu}}
. However, in the overdamped limit,
the time resolution
\Delta t
is considered to be much larger than the
relaxation time, so, in the first-order approximation near
equilibrium state, we have

\displaystyle\bar{\Phi}_{t}(x,p)\approx C^{0}(x,t)\psi_{0}(x,p)+C^{\hat{\mu}}(x,t)\psi_{\hat{\mu}}(x,p).

(25)

Some important commutation relations are listed below:

\displaystyle[p^{\alpha},a^{\dagger}_{\hat{\mu}}]=\zeta e^{\alpha}{}_{\hat{\mu}},\hskip 8.50012pt[\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\alpha}},a^{\dagger}_{\hat{\mu}}]=\frac{1}{2\zeta}e_{\alpha\hat{\mu}},

\displaystyle[\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\alpha}},a^{\dagger}_{\hat{\mu}}]=\frac{1}{2\zeta}\partial_{\alpha}g_{\nu\beta}p^{\beta}e^{\nu}{}_{\hat{\mu}}+a^{\dagger}_{\nu}\partial_{\alpha}e^{\nu}{}_{\hat{\mu}},

\displaystyle[\mathcal{L},a^{\dagger}_{\hat{\mu}}]=\zeta e^{\sigma}{}_{\hat{\mu}}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\sigma}}-\Gamma^{\nu}{}_{\alpha\sigma}p^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\nu}}\right]+\zeta e_{\nu\hat{\alpha}}\nabla_{\hat{\beta}}e^{\nu}{}_{\hat{\mu}}(a_{\hat{\beta}}+a^{\dagger}_{\hat{\beta}})a^{\dagger}_{\hat{\alpha}}.

(26)

Using eq. (
26
) one gets

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\bar{\Phi}_{t})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}(C^{0})\psi_{0}+\mathcal{L}(C^{\hat{\mu}})\psi_{\hat{\mu}}+C^{\hat{\mu}}\mathcal{L}(\psi_{\hat{\mu}})=\mathcal{L}(C^{0})\psi_{0}+\mathcal{L}(C^{\hat{\mu}})\psi_{\hat{\mu}}+C^{\hat{\mu}}[\mathcal{L},a^{\dagger}_{\hat{\mu}}]\psi_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}(C^{0})\psi_{0}+\mathcal{L}(C^{\hat{\mu}})\psi_{\hat{\mu}}+\zeta\nabla_{\nu}e^{\nu}{}_{\hat{\mu}}C^{\hat{\mu}}\psi_{0}+\zeta e_{\nu\hat{\alpha}}(\nabla_{\hat{\beta}}e^{\nu}{}_{\hat{\mu}})C^{\hat{\mu}}(a^{\dagger}_{\hat{\beta}}a^{\dagger}_{\hat{\alpha}}\psi_{0}),

where the property
\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\sigma}}-\Gamma^{\nu}{}_{\alpha\sigma}p^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial p^{\nu}}\right)\psi^{0}=0
has been used.
Since
\mathcal{L}(C^{0})
and
\mathcal{L}(C^{\hat{\mu}})
still contain momentum,
these expressions can be further expanded,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(C^{0})\psi_{0}=\partial^{\hat{\mu}}C^{0}p_{\hat{\mu}}\psi_{0}=\zeta\nabla^{\hat{\mu}}C^{0}\psi_{\hat{\mu}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(C^{\hat{\nu}})\psi_{\hat{\nu}}=\zeta\nabla_{\hat{\mu}}C^{\hat{\mu}}\psi_{0}+\zeta\nabla_{\hat{\alpha}}C^{\hat{\beta}}(a^{\dagger}_{\hat{\alpha}}a^{\dagger}_{\hat{\beta}}\psi_{0}).

Defining an operator
D_{\hat{\mu}}:=(\zeta/T)(f^{\hat{\mu}}_{\text{ex}}-T\partial_{\hat{\mu}})
and substituting eq. (
25
) into
eq. (
24
), the evolution equations of
C^{0}
and
C^{\hat{\mu}}

follow,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}C^{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{T}{\zeta}\left[\nabla_{\hat{\mu}}C^{\hat{\mu}}+(\nabla_{\nu}e^{\nu}{}_{\hat{\mu}})C^{\hat{\mu}}\right],

(27)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}C^{\hat{\mu}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa^{\hat{\mu}}}{m}C^{\hat{\mu}}+\frac{1}{m}D_{\hat{\mu}}C^{0}.

(28)

The overdamped limit means that
{\kappa}^{\hat{\mu}}
is large. In such a limit,

C^{0}
and
C^{\hat{\mu}}
are respectively of orders
O(({\kappa}^{\hat{\mu}})^{0})
and

O(({\kappa}^{\hat{\mu}})^{-1})
. Therefore, up to order
O(({\kappa}^{\hat{\mu}})^{0})
,
we can safely ignore the left hand side of eq. (
28
), yielding

C^{\hat{\mu}}\approx\frac{1}{\kappa^{\hat{\mu}}}D_{\hat{\mu}}C^{0}.

Inserting this result into eq. (
27
), we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}C^{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{T}{\zeta}\left[\nabla_{\hat{\mu}}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa^{\hat{\mu}}}D_{\hat{\mu}}C^{0}\right)+(\nabla_{\nu}e^{\nu}{}_{\hat{\mu}})\frac{1}{\kappa^{\hat{\mu}}}D_{\hat{\mu}}C^{0}\right]=-\frac{T}{\zeta}\nabla_{\nu}\left(e^{\nu}{}_{\hat{\mu}}\frac{1}{\kappa^{\hat{\mu}}}e^{\alpha}{}_{\hat{\mu}}D_{\alpha}C^{0}\right)

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{\nu}\hat{K}^{\nu\alpha}\left[g_{\alpha\mu}f^{\mu}_{\text{ex}}C^{0}-T\nabla_{\alpha}C^{0}\right]

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\mu}\left[\frac{1}{2}\hat{D}^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\nu}C^{0}-\hat{K}^{\mu}{}_{\nu}f^{\nu}_{\text{ex}}C^{0}\right],

(29)

where
\hat{K}^{\mu\nu}=e^{\mu}{}_{\hat{\alpha}}e^{\nu}{}_{\hat{\alpha}}(\kappa^{\hat{\alpha}})^{-1}
is inverse of the damping tensor
K^{\mu\nu}
,
and
\hat{D}^{\mu\nu}:=2T\hat{K}^{\mu\nu}=4T^{2}(D^{-1})^{\mu\nu}
is the
rescaled diffusion tensor. Since

\displaystyle\rho_{t}(x)=\int g^{{1}/{2}}{\rm d}^{d}p~\Phi_{t}(x,p)=\int g^{{1}/{2}}{\rm d}^{d}p~\psi_{0}(x,p)\bar{\Phi}_{t}(x,p)=C^{0}(x,t),

the overdamped FPE (
12
) follows from eq. (
29
).

Appendix B
Continuum limit

Let us first introduce some mathematical tricks.
Let
A
be a full-rank square matrix and
B
be a matrix of the same size,
the determinant of
A+B{\rm d}t
can be expanded as

\displaystyle\det[A+B{\rm d}t+O({\rm d}t^{2})]

\displaystyle=\det[A]\det[I+A^{-1}B{\rm d}t+O({\rm d}t^{2})]=\det[A]+\det[A]\Tr[A^{-1}B]{\rm d}t+O({\rm d}t^{2}).

(30)

Let
f(t)
be a continuous function on
[t_{I},t_{F}]
. Then
there is a continuum limit

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}[1+f(t_{i}){\rm d}t+O({\rm d}t^{2})]=\exp\left[\int_{t_{I}}^{t_{F}}{\rm d}tf(t)\right],

(31)

where
{\rm d}t=(t_{F}-t_{I})/n
and
t_{i}\in[i{\rm d}t,(i+1){\rm d}t]
.
Combining eqs. (
30
)-(
31
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{\det[A(t_{i})+B(t_{i}){\rm d}t+O({\rm d}t^{2})]}{\det[A(t_{i})+C(t_{i}){\rm d}t+O({\rm d}t^{2})]}=\exp\left[\int_{t_{I}}^{t_{F}}{\rm d}t\Tr[A^{-1}(B-C)]\right],

(32)

where
A(t),B(t),C(t)
are continuous matrix functions on
[t_{I},t_{F}]
.

B.1
Overdamped case

The discrete time version of the OLE
(
15
) reads

\displaystyle\tilde{x}^{\mu}_{i+1}-\tilde{x}^{\mu}_{i}=\hat{F}^{\mu}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})|_{(\tilde{x}_{i+1}+\tilde{x}_{i})/2}{\rm d}t+\hat{R}^{\mu}{}_{a}|_{(\tilde{x}_{i+1}+\tilde{x}_{i})/2}{\rm d}\tilde{w}^{a}_{i},

where
\bar{\lambda}_{i}:=(\lambda_{i+1}+\lambda_{i})/2
.
The choice of discretization rule for
\hat{F}^{\mu}
does not affect the continuum limit,
but we still take the middle-point rule for consistency of notations.
We introduce a function

\displaystyle{\rm d}w^{a}(x,y,\lambda):=(\hat{R}^{-1})^{a}{}_{\nu}|_{\bar{x}}\left[x^{\nu}-y^{\nu}+\hat{F}^{\nu}(\lambda)|_{\bar{x}}{\rm d}t\right],

where
\bar{x}:=(x+y)/2
. This function is connected with
the Gaussian noise
{\rm d}\tilde{w}^{a}_{i}
via the relation

\displaystyle{\rm d}\tilde{w}_{i}^{a}={\rm d}w^{a}(\tilde{x}_{i+1},\tilde{x}_{i},\bar{\lambda}_{i}).

(33)

The reversed process is governed by same LE with the external
control parameter time-reversed, so that

\displaystyle{\rm d}\tilde{w}^{a}_{i}={\rm d}w^{a}(\tilde{x}^{-}_{i+1},\tilde{x}^{-}_{i},\bar{\lambda}_{i}^{-})={\rm d}w^{a}(\tilde{x}^{-}_{i+1},\tilde{x}^{-}_{i},\bar{\lambda}_{n-i-1}).

The last equation can also be rewritten as

\displaystyle{\rm d}\tilde{w}^{a}_{n-i-1}={\rm d}w^{a}(\tilde{x}^{-}_{n-i},\tilde{x}^{-}_{n-i-1},\bar{\lambda}_{i}).

(34)

Using eqs. (
33
) and (
34
), the transition probabilities of
the forward and reversed processes can be expressed as

\displaystyle\Pr[\tilde{x}_{i+1}=x_{i+1}|\tilde{x}_{i}=x_{i}]=g^{-{1}/{2}}(x_{i+1})\det\left[\frac{\partial{\rm d}w^{a}}{\partial x^{\mu}}\right](x_{i+1},x_{i},\bar{\lambda}_{i})\Pr[{\rm d}\tilde{w}^{a}_{i}={\rm d}w^{a}(x_{i+1},x_{i},\bar{\lambda}_{i})]

(35)

\displaystyle\Pr[\tilde{x}_{n-i}^{-}=x_{i}|\tilde{x}_{n-i-1}^{-}=x_{i+1}]=g^{-{1}/{2}}(x_{i})\det\left[\frac{\partial{\rm d}w^{a}}{\partial x^{\mu}}\right](x_{i},x_{i+1},\bar{\lambda}_{i})\Pr[{\rm d}\tilde{w}^{a}_{n-i-1}={\rm d}w^{a}(x_{i},x_{i+1},\bar{\lambda}_{i})].

(36)

It is easy to calculate the Jacobi matrix of
{\rm d}w^{a}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\rm d}w^{a}}{\partial x^{\mu}}(x_{i+1},x_{i},\bar{\lambda}_{i})

\displaystyle=(\hat{R}^{-1})^{a}{}_{\mu}|_{\bar{x}_{i}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\{\partial_{\mu}(\hat{R}^{-1})^{a}{}_{\nu}v^{\nu}_{i}-\partial_{\mu}[(\hat{R}^{-1})^{a}{}_{\nu}\hat{F}^{\nu}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})]\right\}_{\bar{x}_{i}}{\rm d}t,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\rm d}w^{a}}{\partial x^{\mu}}(x_{i},x_{i+1},\bar{\lambda}_{i})

\displaystyle=(\hat{R}^{-1})^{a}{}_{\mu}|_{\bar{x}_{i}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\{-\partial_{\mu}(\hat{R}^{-1})^{a}{}_{\nu}v^{\nu}_{i}-\partial_{\mu}[(\hat{R}^{-1})^{a}{}_{\nu}\hat{F}^{\nu}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})]\right\}_{\bar{x}_{i}}{\rm d}t,

where
v_{i}:=(x_{i+1}-x_{i})/{\rm d}t
is the velocity of the Brownian particle.
Using eq. (
32
), we can get

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{g^{-1/2}(x_{i+1})\det\left[\frac{\partial{\rm d}w^{a}}{\partial x^{\mu}}\right](x_{i+1},x_{i},\bar{\lambda}_{i})}{g^{-1/2}(x_{i})\det\left[\frac{\partial{\rm d}w^{a}}{\partial x^{\mu}}\right](x_{i},x_{i+1},\bar{\lambda}_{i})}=\exp\left[-\int_{t_{I}}^{t_{F}}v^{\nu}(\hat{R}^{-1})^{a}{}_{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\hat{R}^{\mu}{}_{a}{\rm d}t\right].

(37)

Therefore, using eq. (
3
), we arrive at the following continuum limit,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{\Pr[{\rm d}\tilde{w}^{a}_{i}={\rm d}w^{a}(x_{i+1},x_{i},\bar{\lambda}_{i})]}{\Pr[{\rm d}\tilde{w}^{a}_{n-i-1}={\rm d}w^{a}(x_{i},x_{i+1},\bar{\lambda}_{i})]}=\exp\left[\frac{1}{T}\int_{t_{I}}^{t_{F}}v_{\mu}f^{\mu}_{\text{ex}}{\rm d}t\right]\exp\left[\int_{t_{I}}^{t_{F}}v^{\nu}(\hat{R}^{-1})^{a}{}_{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\hat{R}^{\mu}{}_{a}{\rm d}t\right].

(38)

Finally, using eqs. (
35
)-(
36
) and (
38
), we get

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{\Pr[\tilde{x}_{i+1}=x_{i+1}|\tilde{x}_{i}=x_{i}]}{\Pr[\tilde{x}_{n-i}^{-}=x_{i}|\tilde{x}_{n-i-1}^{-}=x_{i+1}]}\ =\exp\left[\frac{1}{T}\int_{t_{I}}^{t_{F}}v_{\mu}f^{\mu}_{\text{ex}}{\rm d}t\right],

which is essentially identical to eq. (
23
).

B.2
Underdamped case

Similarly, we write down the discrete time version of
eqs. (
1
) and (
9
),

\displaystyle\tilde{x}^{\mu}_{i+1}-\tilde{x}^{\mu}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{p}^{\mu}_{i+1}+\tilde{p}^{\mu}_{i}}{2m}{\rm d}t,

\displaystyle\tilde{p}^{\mu}_{i+1}-\tilde{p}^{\mu}_{i}

\displaystyle=F^{\mu}(\bar{\lambda}_{i})|_{(\tilde{X}_{i+1}+\tilde{X}_{i})/2}{\rm d}t+\bar{F}^{\mu}|_{(\tilde{X}_{i+1}+\tilde{X}_{i})/2}{\rm d}t+R^{\mu}{}_{a}|_{(\tilde{X}_{i+1}+\tilde{X}_{i})/2}{\rm d}\tilde{w}^{a}_{i}.

Now
\tilde{x}_{i+1}
should be viewed as a function in
\tilde{p}_{i+1}
and

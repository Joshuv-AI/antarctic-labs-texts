A substitute for Kazhdan’s property (T) for universal non-lattices
Narutaka Ozawa

Abstract
. The well-known theorem of Shalom–Vaserstein and Ershov–Jaikin-Zapirain states that the group EL n ​ ( ℛ ) \mathrm{EL}_{n}(\mathcal{R}) , generated by elementary matrices over a finitely generated commutative ring ℛ \mathcal{R} , has Kazhdan’s property (T) as soon as n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 . This is no longer true if the ring ℛ \mathcal{R} is replaced by a commutative rng (a ring but without the identity) due to nilpotent quotients EL n ​ ( ℛ / ℛ k ) \mathrm{EL}_{n}(\mathcal{R}/\mathcal{R}^{k}) . In this paper, we prove that even in such a case the group EL n ​ ( ℛ ) \mathrm{EL}_{n}(\mathcal{R}) satisfies a certain property that can substitute property (T), provided that n n is large enough.

2.
Preliminary

Let
\Gamma=\langle S\rangle
be a group together with a
finite symmetric generating subset
S
.
We denote by
\mathbb{R}[\Gamma]
the real group algebra
with the involution
\ast
which is the linear
extension of
x^{*}:=x^{-1}
on
\Gamma
.
The identity element of
\Gamma
as well as
\mathbb{R}[\Gamma]
is simply denoted by
1
.
Recall the positive cone of
sums of (hermitian) squares
is given by

\Sigma^{2}\mathbb{R}[\Gamma]:=\{\sum_{i}\xi_{i}^{*}\xi_{i}:\xi_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[\Gamma]\}\subset\mathbb{R}[\Gamma]^{\mathrm{her}}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}[\Gamma]:\xi=\xi^{*}\}.

The elements in
\Sigma^{2}\mathbb{R}[\Gamma]
are considered positive.
For
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}[\Gamma]^{\mathrm{her}}
, we write
\xi\preceq\eta

if
\eta-\xi\in\Sigma^{2}\mathbb{R}[\Gamma]
.
It is obvious that
\xi\succeq 0
implies
\xi\geq 0
in the full group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}[\Gamma]
,
that is to say,
\pi(\xi)
is positive selfadjoint for every orthogonal (or unitary)
representation
\pi
of
\Gamma
on a real (or complex) Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
The converse is true up to the
archimedean closure
:

\overline{\Sigma^{2}\mathbb{R}[\Gamma]}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}[\Gamma]:\forall\varepsilon>0\ \xi+\varepsilon\cdot 1\succeq 0\}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}[\Gamma]:\xi\geq 0\mbox{ in }\mathrm{C}^{*}[\Gamma]\}.

See, e.g.,
[
Ci
,
Oz1
,
Sc
]
for this.
On this occasion, we remind the basic fact that

0\preceq\xi\preceq\eta
(or
0\leq\xi\leq\eta
)
need not imply
0\leq\xi^{2}\leq\eta^{2}
.
Note that since any orthogonal representation of
\Gamma
dilates
to an orthogonal representation of any supergroup
\Gamma_{1}\geq\Gamma

by induction (i.e.,
\mathrm{C}^{*}[\Gamma]\subset\mathrm{C}^{*}[\Gamma_{1}]
in short),
whether
\xi\geq 0
or not does not depend on the ambient group.
The same holds true for
\xi\succeq 0
, by the coset decomposition.
The
combinatorial Laplacian
, w.r.t. the (symmetric) generating subset
S
,

\Delta:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s\in S}(1-s)^{*}(1-s)=|S|-\sum_{s\in S}s

satisfies, for every orthogonal representation
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
and a vector
v\in\mathcal{H}
,

\mathopen{\langle}\pi(\Delta)v,v\mathclose{\rangle}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s\in S}\|v-\pi(s)v\|^{2}.

3.
Proof of Main Theorem, Prelude

For any rng
\mathcal{R}
, we denote by
\EL_{n}(\mathcal{R})\subset\SL_{n}(\mathcal{R}^{1})

the group generated by the elementary matrices over the rng
\mathcal{R}
.
The elementary matrices are those
e_{i,j}(r)\in\SL_{n}(\mathcal{R}^{1})

with
1
’s on the diagonal,
r\in\mathcal{R}
in the
(i,j)
-th entry (
i\neq j
),
and zeros everywhere else.
They satisfy the Steinberg relations:

•

e_{i,j}(r)e_{i,j}(s)=e_{i,j}(r+s)
;

•

[e_{i,j}(r),e_{j,k}(s)]=e_{i,k}(rs)
if
i\neq k
;

•

[e_{i,j}(r),e_{k,l}(s)]=1
if
i\neq l
and
j\neq k
.

We note that every rng homomorphism
\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{S}

induces by entrywise operation a group homomorphism

\EL_{n}(\mathcal{R})\to\EL_{n}(\mathcal{S})
and that
\EL_{n}(\mathcal{R}/\mathcal{R}^{k})

is nilpotent for every
k
, where
\mathcal{R}^{k}:=\lh\{r_{1}\cdots r_{k}:r_{i}\in\mathcal{R}\}
.
To ease notation, we will write

E_{i,j}(r):=(1-e_{i,j}(r))^{*}(1-e_{i,j}(r))=2-e_{i,j}(r)-e_{i,j}(r)^{*}\in\mathbb{R}[\EL_{n}(\mathcal{R})].

We now consider the case
\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{Z}\langle t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}\rangle
and
start proving Main Theorem.
Recall that the combinatorial Laplacians w.r.t. the
generating subset
\{e_{i,j}(\pm t_{r})\}
are given by

\Delta_{n}:=\sum_{i\neq j}\sum_{r=1}^{d}E_{i,j}(t_{r})\ \mbox{ and }\ \Delta_{n}^{(2)}:=\sum_{i\neq j}\sum_{r,s=1}^{d}E_{i,j}(t_{r}t_{s}).

We follow the idea of
[
KKN
]
about the stability w.r.t.
n

of the relation like
\Delta^{(2)}_{n}\ll\Delta^{2}_{n}
.
Here
\xi\ll\eta
means that
\xi\leq R\eta
for some
R>0
in the full group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra.
For each
n
, put
\mathrm{E}_{n}:=\{\{i,j\}:1\leq i,j\leq n,\ i\neq j\}

and, for
\mathrm{e},\mathrm{f}\in\mathrm{E}_{n}
, write
\mathrm{e}\sim\mathrm{f}
if
|\mathrm{e}\cap\mathrm{f}|=1
and

\mathrm{e}\perp\mathrm{f}
if
\mathrm{e}\cap\mathrm{f}=\emptyset
.
One has

\Delta_{n}=\sum_{\mathrm{e}\in\mathrm{E}_{n}}\Delta_{\mathrm{e}},

where
\Delta_{\{i,j\}}:=\sum_{r=1}^{d}E_{i,j}(t_{r})+E_{j,i}(t_{r})
.
Thus

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathrm{e}}\Delta_{\mathrm{e}}^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{e}\sim\mathrm{f}}\Delta_{\mathrm{e}}\Delta_{\mathrm{f}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{e}\perp\mathrm{f}}\Delta_{\mathrm{e}}\Delta_{\mathrm{f}}

\displaystyle=:\Sq_{n}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Adj_{n}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Op_{n}.

The elements
\Sq_{n}
and
\Op_{n}
are positive,
while
\Adj_{n}
is not and this causes trouble.

For
m<n
, we view
\EL_{m}(\mathcal{R})
as a subgroup
of
\EL_{n}(\mathcal{R})
sitting at the left upper corner.
The symmetric group
\Sym(n)
acts on
\EL_{n}(\mathcal{R})
by permutation of the indices.
We note that
|\mathrm{E}_{m}|=\frac{1}{2}m(m-1)
,

|\{(\mathrm{e},\mathrm{f})\in\mathrm{E}_{m}^{2}:\mathrm{e}\sim\mathrm{f}\}|=m(m-1)(m-2)
,
and
|\{(\mathrm{e},\mathrm{f})\in\mathrm{E}_{m}^{2}:\mathrm{e}\perp\mathrm{f}\}|=\frac{1}{4}m(m-1)(m-2)(m-3)
.
Hence, as it is proved in
[
KKN
]
, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\Sym(n)}\sigma(\Delta^{(2)}_{m})

\displaystyle=m(m-1)\cdot(n-2)!\cdot\Delta^{(2)}_{n},

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\Sym(n)}\sigma(\Adj_{m})

\displaystyle=m(m-1)(m-2)\cdot(n-3)!\cdot\Adj_{n},

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\Sym(n)}\sigma(\Op_{m})

\displaystyle=m(m-1)(m-2)(m-3)\cdot(n-4)!\cdot\Op_{n}.

Thus if we know there are
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
R>0
, and
\varepsilon>0
such that

\Adj_{m}+R\Op_{m}\geq\varepsilon\Delta^{(2)}_{m}

holds true in
\mathrm{C}^{*}[\EL_{m}(\mathcal{R})]
, then it follows

\frac{n-2}{m-2}\varepsilon\Delta^{(2)}_{n}\leq\Adj_{n}+\frac{m-3}{n-3}R\Op_{n}\leq\Delta_{n}^{2}

for all
n
such that
\frac{m-3}{n-3}R\leq 1
and Main Theorem is proved.
This is Proposition 4.1 in
[
KKN
]
.
To apply this machinery, we further expand
\Adj_{m}
:

\displaystyle\Adj_{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r,s}\sum_{i,j,k;\ \mbox{\scriptsize distinct}}(E_{i,j}(t_{r})+E_{j,i}(t_{r}))(E_{j,k}(t_{s})+E_{k,j}(t_{s}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{r,s}\sum_{i,j,k;\ \mbox{\scriptsize distinct}}\bigl(E_{i,j}(t_{r})E_{j,k}(t_{s})+E_{j,k}(t_{s})E_{i,j}(t_{r})

\displaystyle\hskip 100.0pt+E_{i,j}(t_{r})E_{i,k}(t_{s})+E_{j,k}(t_{s})E_{i,k}(t_{r})\bigr).

Therefore, if there are
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
R>0
,
\varepsilon>0
,
and distinct indices
i,j,k,l
such that

(
\spadesuit
)

\displaystyle E_{i,j}(t_{r})E_{j,k}(t_{s})+E_{j,k}(t_{s})E_{i,j}(t_{r})

\displaystyle\quad+E_{i,j}(t_{r})E_{i,l}(t_{s})+E_{j,k}(t_{s})E_{l,k}(t_{r})+R\Op_{m}\geq\varepsilon E_{i,k}(t_{r}t_{s})

holds true, then we obtain
(\heartsuit)
(for different
R>0
and
\varepsilon>0
)
by summing up this over the
\Sym(m)
-orbit and over
r,s
.
This is what we will prove in the next section.

Lemma 1

For any dense subset

\bigoplus_{\theta\in I}\pi_{\theta}

\mathrm{C}^{*}[\mathbf{H}]

Proof.

For the readers’ convenience, we sketch the proof.

\tau_{\theta}

\mathrm{C}^{*}[\mathbf{H}]

\pi_{\theta}

That is to say, if

\displaystyle+4+2(1-\tan\pi\theta)\mathopen{\langle}\frac{\lambda}{2}\pi_{\theta}(x+x^{*})\xi,\pi_{\theta}(y+y^{*})\xi\mathclose{\rangle}

\displaystyle-\sum_{m}|\xi_{m-1}-\xi_{m+1}-\lambda s_{m}\xi_{m}|^{2}.

Proof.

Because the statements are formulated in a different way in

we replicate here the proof from
[
BZ
]
:

\displaystyle\|H_{\lambda,\theta}\xi\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}|\lambda c_{m}\xi_{m}+\xi_{m-1}+\xi_{m+1}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}+4+\sum_{m}\bigl(-\lambda^{2}s_{m}^{2}\xi_{m}^{2}-|\xi_{m-1}-\xi_{m+1}|^{2}+2\lambda c_{m}\xi_{m}(\xi_{m-1}+\xi_{m+1})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}+4-\sum_{m}|\xi_{m-1}-\xi_{m+1}-\lambda s_{m}\xi_{m}|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-2\lambda\sum_{m}s_{m}(\xi_{m-1}-\xi_{m+1})\xi_{m}+2\lambda\sum_{m}c_{m}\xi_{m}(\xi_{m-1}+\xi_{m+1}).

We continue computation:

\displaystyle\sum_{m}c_{m}\xi_{m}(\xi_{m-1}+\xi_{m+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}(c_{m-1}+c_{m})\xi_{m-1}\xi_{m}

\displaystyle=2\cos\pi\theta\sum_{m}\xi_{m-1}\xi_{m}\cos(2m-1)\pi\theta

and similarly

\displaystyle-\sum_{m}s_{m}(\xi_{m-1}-\xi_{m+1})\xi_{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}(s_{m-1}-s_{m})\xi_{m-1}\xi_{m}

\displaystyle=-2\sin\pi\theta\sum_{m}\xi_{m-1}\xi_{m}\cos(2m-1)\pi\theta

\displaystyle=-\tan\theta\sum_{m}c_{m}\xi_{m}(\xi_{m-1}+\xi_{m+1}).

Thus one obtains the purported formula for
\|H_{\lambda,\theta}\xi\|^{2}
.
We also observe that

\displaystyle\|H_{\lambda,\theta}\xi\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{2}+4+4\lambda(\cos\pi\theta-\sin\pi\theta)\sum_{m}\xi_{m-1}\xi_{m}\cos(2m-1)\pi\theta

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{2}+4+4\lambda(1-\sin\pi\theta).

This yields the purported estimate for
\|H_{\theta,\lambda}\|
.
∎

Corollary 3
.

In the full group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}[\mathbf{H}]
, one has

X+Y\geq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{Z}.

Proof.

By Lemma
1
, it suffices to show the assertion in
\mathcal{A}_{\theta}
for each
\theta\in[0,1/2]
.
This follows from Theorem
2
with
\lambda=2
that

X_{\theta}+Y_{\theta}=4-H_{\theta,2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{Z_{\theta}}
.
∎

Since
Z
is central,
X+Y\geq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{Z}
is equivalent
to
4(X+Y)^{2}\geq Z
in
\mathrm{C}^{*}[\mathbf{H}]
.
However, there is no
R>0
such that
R(X+Y)^{2}\succeq Z
in
\mathbb{R}[\mathbf{H}]
.
We will elaborate this in Section

.

Corollary 4
.

Let
R\geq 1
,
0<\kappa<1
,
and
\theta_{0}:=\min\{\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{\pi}\arcsin(\kappa\sqrt{\frac{1-\kappa}{R}})\}
.
Then for any
\theta\in[0,\theta_{0}]
, one has

RX_{\theta}+Y_{\theta}\geq\frac{\sqrt{(1-\kappa)R}}{2}\sqrt{Z_{\theta}}.

Proof.

We write
s_{0}:=\sin\pi\theta_{0}
,
c:=\diag_{m}c_{m}=\pi_{\theta}(\frac{x+x^{*}}{2})=1-\frac{1}{2}X_{\theta}
,
and
C=\sqrt{(1-\kappa)R}
.
Let
\theta\in[0,\theta_{0}]
and a real unit vector
\xi\in\ell_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})
be given.
We need to prove
\mathopen{\langle}(RX_{\theta}+Y_{\theta})\xi,\xi\mathclose{\rangle}\geq Cs
.
For this, we may assume that
\mathopen{\langle}\pi_{\theta}(x+x^{*})\xi,\pi_{\theta}(y+y^{*})\xi\mathclose{\rangle}>0

because otherwise
\mathopen{\langle}(X_{\theta}+Y_{\theta})\xi,\xi\mathclose{\rangle}\geq 4-\|\pi_{\theta}(x+x^{*}+y+y^{*})\xi\|\geq 4-2\sqrt{2}
.
Put
\varepsilon:=1-\|c\xi\|
.
If
\varepsilon\geq\frac{Cs}{2R}
, then

\mathopen{\langle}RX_{\theta}\xi,\xi\mathclose{\rangle}\geq 2R\varepsilon\geq Cs
and we are done.
From now on, we assume that
\varepsilon<\frac{Cs}{2R}
.
By Theorem
2
for
\lambda:=2R/C
,
one has

\displaystyle\|H_{\lambda,\theta}\xi\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{2}+4+2\lambda(1-s)\mathopen{\langle}c\xi,(H_{\theta,\lambda}-\lambda c)\xi\mathclose{\rangle}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{2}+4+2\lambda(1-s)(1-\varepsilon)\|H_{\theta,\lambda}\xi\|-2\lambda^{2}(1-s)(1-\varepsilon)^{2}

and hence

\displaystyle\bigl(\|H_{\lambda,\theta}\xi\|-\lambda(1-s)(1-\varepsilon)\bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4+\lambda^{2}(1-2(1-s)(1-\varepsilon)^{2}+(1-s)^{2}(1-\varepsilon)^{2})

\displaystyle=4+\lambda^{2}(1-(1-s^{2})(1-\varepsilon)^{2})

\displaystyle\leq 4+\lambda^{2}(s^{2}+2\varepsilon).

Thus

\|H_{\lambda,\theta}\xi\|\leq 2+\lambda s(\frac{1}{4}\lambda s_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda\frac{\varepsilon}{s})+\lambda(1-s).

By our choices,
\lambda s_{0}=\frac{2R}{C}\cdot\frac{\kappa\sqrt{(1-\kappa)}}{\sqrt{R}}=2\kappa
and
\lambda\varepsilon/s\leq 1
.
Therefore,

\|H_{\lambda,\theta}\xi\|\leq\lambda+2-(1-\frac{1}{4}\cdot 2\kappa-\frac{1}{2})\cdot 2\sqrt{\frac{R}{1-\kappa}}s=\lambda+2-Cs.

Since
\lambda+2-H_{\lambda,\theta}=\frac{\lambda}{2}X_{\theta}+Y_{\theta}\leq RX_{\theta}+Y_{\theta}
, we are done.
∎

Proposition 5
.

In the full group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}[\mathbf{H}]
, one has

(X+Y)\sqrt{Z}+\frac{1}{2}(XY+YX)\geq 0.

Proof.

By Lemma
1
, it suffices to show the same for
X_{\theta}
’s.
We write
b_{m}:=1-c_{m}=1-\cos 2m\pi\theta=2\sin^{2}m\pi\theta
.
We observe that

X_{\theta}=\left[\begin{matrix}\ddots&&&\\
&2b_{m-1}&&\\
&&2b_{m}&\\
&&&\ddots\end{matrix}\right],\quad Y_{\theta}=\left[\begin{matrix}\ddots&&&\\
&2&-1&\\
&-1&2&\\
&&&\ddots\end{matrix}\right],

\frac{1}{2}(X_{\theta}Y_{\theta}+Y_{\theta}X_{\theta})=\left[\begin{matrix}\ddots&&&\\
&4b_{m-1}&-(b_{m-1}+b_{m})&\\
&-(b_{m-1}+b_{m})&4b_{m}&\\
&&&\ddots\end{matrix}\right].

These are the sums of the following 2-by-2 matrices
sitting at
(m-1)
-to-
m
-th corners:

X_{\theta,m}=\left[\begin{matrix}b_{m-1}&\\
&b_{m}\end{matrix}\right],\quad Y_{\theta,m}=\left[\begin{matrix}1&-1\\
-1&1\end{matrix}\right],

\frac{1}{2}(XY+YX)_{\theta,m}:=\left[\begin{matrix}2b_{m-1}&-(b_{m-1}+b_{m})\\
-(b_{m-1}+b_{m})&2b_{m}\end{matrix}\right].

Thus, it suffices to show

\displaystyle T_{\theta,m}

\displaystyle:=2s(X_{\theta,m}+Y_{\theta,m})+\frac{1}{2}(XY+YX)_{\theta,m}

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{matrix}2(s+1)b_{m-1}+2s&-(2s+b_{m-1}+b_{m})\\
-(2s+b_{m-1}+b_{m})&2(s+1)b_{m}+2s\end{matrix}\right]

is positive in
\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
for every
m
.
We only need to calculate the determinant:

\displaystyle\mathrm{det}(T_{\theta,m})

\displaystyle\geq 4b_{m-1}b_{m}+4s(s+1)(b_{m-1}+b_{m})+4s^{2}-(2s+b_{m-1}+b_{m})^{2}

\displaystyle=4s^{2}(b_{m-1}+b_{m})-(b_{m-1}-b_{m})^{2}

\displaystyle=8s^{2}(\sin^{2}(m-1)\pi\theta+\sin^{2}m\pi\theta)-4s^{2}\sin^{2}(2m-1)\pi\theta

\displaystyle\geq 0.

Here, we have used the formulas
b_{m}=2\sin^{2}m\pi\theta
,

b_{m-1}-b_{m}=-2s\theta\sin(2m-1)\pi\theta
,
and
|\sin(2m-1)\pi\theta|\leq|\sin(m-1)\pi\theta|+|\sin m\pi\theta|
.
∎

A similar calculation shows
Z+\frac{1}{2}(XY+YX)\geq 0
in
\mathrm{C}^{*}[\mathbf{H}]
.
In fact, it is a sum of squares:

Z+\frac{1}{2}(XY+YX)=\frac{1}{4}(X+Y)Z+\frac{1}{8}\sum(1-b)^{\delta}(1-a)^{\epsilon}(1-a)^{\bar{\epsilon}}(1-b)^{\bar{\delta}},

where
\sum
is over the 8 terms
(a,b)\in\{(x,y),(y,x)\}
and
(\epsilon,\bar{\epsilon}),(\delta,\bar{\delta})\in\{(\ast,\,\cdot\,),(\,\cdot\,,\ast)\}
.

Now, we consider the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\theta}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{\theta}

on
\ell_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})\otimes\ell_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})
.
We continue to view
Z_{\theta}
as a scalar in
\mathcal{A}_{\theta}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{\theta}
.
We want to find an inequality that leads to
(\spadesuit)
.
The following does the job for small
\theta>0
. We note that it fails at
\theta_{0}=1/2
.

Lemma 6
.

There are
\theta_{0}>0
,
R>1
, and
\varepsilon>0

such that for every
\theta\in[0,\theta_{0}]

R(X_{\theta}\otimes Y_{\theta}+Y_{\theta}\otimes X_{\theta})+X_{\theta}\otimes X_{\theta}+Y_{\theta}\otimes Y_{\theta}+(X_{\theta}Y_{\theta}+Y_{\theta}X_{\theta})\otimes 1\geq\varepsilon Z_{\theta}.

By Corollary
4
, there are
\theta_{0}>0
and

Lemma 7
.

For every
\theta\in[0,1/2]
, one has

\|\pi_{\theta}((1-x)(1-y))\|\leq 4\cos(\pi\theta/2).

Proof.

The expansion of
(1-y)^{*}(1-x)^{*}(1-x)(1-y)
has 16 terms (counting multiplicity)
and among them are

-(1+z)x
,
-(1+z)^{*}x^{*}
and
x(zy^{*}+y)+x^{*}(z^{*}y^{*}+y)
.
One has
|1+z|=2\cos\pi\theta
and

\displaystyle\|x(zy^{*}+

\displaystyle y)+x^{*}(z^{*}y^{*}+y)\|\leq\|\left[\begin{smallmatrix}xy^{*}&x^{*}y^{*}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\|\|\left[\begin{smallmatrix}z+y^{2}\\
z^{*}+y^{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\|(z+y^{2})^{*}(z+y^{2})+(z^{*}+y^{2})^{*}(z^{*}+y^{2})\|^{1/2}=4\cos\pi\theta.

Hence

\|\pi_{\theta}((1-y)^{*}(1-x)^{*}(1-x)(1-y))\|\leq 8+8\cos\pi\theta=16\cos^{2}(\pi\theta/2)
.
∎

For a positive operator
A
, we denote by
\mathbb{P}_{A\leq\delta}

(resp.
\mathbb{P}_{A>\delta}=1-\mathbb{P}_{A\leq\delta}
) the spectral projection
of
A
corresponding to the spectrum
[0,\delta]
(resp.
(\delta,\infty)
).
We also denote by
\mathbb{P}_{A\leq\delta\,\wedge\,B\leq\delta}
etc. for
the orthogonal projection onto
\ran\mathbb{P}_{A\leq\delta}\cap\ran\mathbb{P}_{B\leq\delta}
etc.
Note that if
A
and
B
commute, then so are their spectral projections and

\mathbb{P}_{A\leq\delta\,\wedge\,B\leq\delta}=\mathbb{P}_{A\leq\delta}\mathbb{P}_{B\leq\delta}
.

Lemma 8
.

For every
\theta\in(0,1/2]
and
0<\delta<2(1-\cos\pi\theta)
, one has

\mathbb{P}_{X_{\theta}\leq\delta}Y_{\theta}\mathbb{P}_{X_{\theta}\leq\delta}=2\mathbb{P}_{X_{\theta}\leq\delta},

the same with
X_{\theta}
and
Y_{\theta}
interchanged, and

\|\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\theta}\leq\delta}\mathbb{P}_{X_{\theta}\leq\delta}\|\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{4-\delta}}.

In particular,
\ell_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})
is decomposed into a direct sum

\ell_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})=\ran\mathbb{P}_{X_{\theta}\leq\delta}+\ran\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\theta}\leq\delta}+\ran\mathbb{P}_{X_{\theta}>\delta\,\wedge\,Y_{\theta}>\delta}

and the corresponding (not necessarily orthogonal) projections
have norm at most
\sqrt{\frac{4-\delta}{2-\delta}}
.

Proof.

We observe that
\mathbb{P}_{X_{\theta}\leq\delta}
is the projection
onto
\ell_{2}(E)
with

E:=\{m:2(1-\cos 2m\pi\theta)\leq\delta\}\subset\{m:m\theta\in(-\theta/2,\theta/2)+\mathbb{Z}\}.

The set
E
does not contain consecutive numbers and the first assertion follows.
The second follows from the unitary equivalence of the pairs

(X_{\theta},Y_{\theta})
and
(Y_{\theta},X_{\theta})
.
Since

Y_{\theta}\leq\delta\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\theta}\leq\delta}+4(1-\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\theta}\leq\delta})=4-(4-\delta)\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\theta}\leq\delta}
,
one has

2\mathbb{P}_{X_{\theta}\leq\delta}\leq 4\mathbb{P}_{X_{\theta}\leq\delta}-(4-\delta)\mathbb{P}_{X_{\theta}\leq\delta}\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\theta}\leq\delta}\mathbb{P}_{X_{\theta}\leq\delta}

and
\|\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\theta}\leq\delta}\mathbb{P}_{X_{\theta}\leq\delta}\|^{2}=\|\mathbb{P}_{X_{\theta}\leq\delta}\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\theta}\leq\delta}\mathbb{P}_{X_{\theta}\leq\delta}\|\leq 2/(4-\delta)
.
This gives the desired estimate
for
\|\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\theta}\leq\delta}\mathbb{P}_{X_{\theta}\leq\delta}\|
.
We remark that this estimate can be improved to
\approx 1/\sqrt{3}

if
\theta
is away from
1/2
and
\delta>0
is small enough.
Indeed, the gaps of
E
will have length at least two and hence
any unit vectors
\xi\in\ran\mathbb{P}_{X_{\theta}\leq\delta}
and

\eta\in\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\theta}\leq\delta}
satisfy

|\mathopen{\langle}\xi,\eta\mathclose{\rangle}|\approx|\mathopen{\langle}\xi,\frac{1}{3}\pi_{\theta}(1+y+y^{*})\eta\mathclose{\rangle}|=|\mathopen{\langle}\frac{1}{3}\pi_{\theta}(1+y+y^{*})\xi,\eta\mathclose{\rangle}|\leq 1/\sqrt{3}.

The projection onto the third subspace is orthogonal.
On the other hand,
any
\xi+\eta\in\ran\mathbb{P}_{X_{\theta}\leq\delta}+\ran\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\theta}\leq\delta}

satisfies

\|\xi+\eta\|^{2}\geq\|\xi\|^{2}+\|\eta\|^{2}-2\|\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\theta}\leq\delta}\mathbb{P}_{X_{\theta}\leq\delta}\|\|\xi\|\|\eta\|\geq(1-\|\mathbb{P}_{Y_{\theta}\leq\delta}\mathbb{P}_{X_{\theta}\leq\delta}\|^{2})\|\xi\|^{2}.

This gives the desired norm estimate.
∎

Now, we consider this time the cubic tensor product

\mathbf{H}_{3}:=\{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&\ast&\ast&\ast&\ast\\
&1&0&0&\ast\\
&&1&0&\ast\\

in

Note that

Theorem 9
.

There are
R>0
and
\varepsilon>0
such that

R(X_{1}Y_{2}+Y_{1}X_{2}+X_{1}Y_{3}+Y_{1}X_{3})+X_{1}X_{2}+Y_{1}Y_{2}+X_{1}Y_{1}+Y_{1}X_{1}\geq\varepsilon Z

\mathrm{C}^{*}[\mathbf{H}_{3}]

By Lemma
1
(adapted to this case),
it suffices to prove the assertion
in
\mathcal{A}_{\theta}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{\theta}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{\theta}
for each
\theta\in[0,1/2]
.
We write
X_{i,\theta}
for

in the

\displaystyle\mathopen{\langle}\bigl(R(X_{1,\theta}Y_{2,\theta}+Y_{1,\theta}X_{2,\theta}+X_{1,\theta}Y_{3,\theta}+Y_{1,\theta}X_{3,\theta})

6

8
,

Since

\mathbb{P}_{Y_{2,\theta}>\delta}\eta+\mathbb{P}_{Y_{2,\theta}>\delta}\gamma\approx_{\delta}\mathbb{P}_{X_{1,\theta}\leq\delta\,\wedge\,Y_{2,\theta}>\delta}(\xi+\eta+\gamma)-\mathbb{P}_{Y_{2,\theta}>\delta}\xi=\mathbb{P}_{X_{1,\theta}\leq\delta\,\wedge\,Y_{2,\theta}>\delta}\eta.

\ran\mathbb{P}_{X_{1,\theta}\leq\delta}

\ran\mathbb{P}_{Y_{1,\theta}\leq\delta}

\mathbb{P}_{Y_{2,\theta}>\delta}\eta\approx_{\delta}\mathbb{P}_{X_{1,\theta}\leq\delta\,\wedge\,Y_{2,\theta}>\delta}\eta\ \mbox{ and }\ \mathbb{P}_{Y_{2,\theta}>\delta}\gamma\approx_{\delta}0.

\mathbb{P}_{Y_{1,\theta}\leq\delta}\mathbb{P}_{X_{1,\theta}>\delta}\mathbb{P}_{Y_{1,\theta}\leq\delta}\geq\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{P}_{Y_{1,\theta}\leq\delta}

\eta\approx_{2\delta}\mathbb{P}_{Y_{2,\theta}\leq\delta}\eta.

The same consideration on

\mathbb{P}_{X_{2,\theta}>\delta}\gamma\approx_{\delta}0\ \mbox{ and }\ \xi\approx_{2\delta}\mathbb{P}_{X_{2,\theta}\leq\delta}\xi.

Further, the same for

and

\xi\approx_{2\delta}\mathbb{P}_{X_{3,\theta}\leq\delta}\xi\ \mbox{ and }\ \eta\approx_{2\delta}\mathbb{P}_{Y_{3,\theta}\leq\delta}\eta.

Now a routine but tedious calculation with Lemma

\mathopen{\langle}X_{1,\theta}X_{2,\theta}\zeta,\zeta\mathclose{\rangle}\approx_{C\delta}\mathopen{\langle}X_{1,\theta}X_{2,\theta}\mathbb{P}_{Y_{1,\theta}\leq\delta\,\wedge\,Y_{2,\theta}\leq\delta}\eta,\mathbb{P}_{Y_{1,\theta}\leq\delta\,\wedge\,Y_{2,\theta}\leq\delta}\eta\mathclose{\rangle}\approx_{16\delta}4\|\eta\|^{2}

\mathopen{\langle}Y_{1,\theta}Y_{2,\theta}\zeta,\zeta\mathclose{\rangle}\approx_{C\delta}4\|\xi\|^{2}.

7
and
8

\displaystyle|\mathopen{\langle}(X_{1,\theta}Y_{1,\theta}+Y_{1,\theta}X_{1,\theta})\zeta,\zeta\mathclose{\rangle}|

\displaystyle\qquad\approx_{C\delta}2|\mathopen{\langle}X_{1,\theta}Y_{1,\theta}\mathbb{P}_{X_{1,\theta}\leq\delta\,\wedge\,X_{2,\theta}\leq\delta\,\wedge\,X_{3,\theta}\leq\delta}\xi,\mathbb{P}_{Y_{1,\theta}\leq\delta\,\wedge\,Y_{2,\theta}\leq\delta\,\wedge\,Y_{3,\theta}\leq\delta}\eta\mathclose{\rangle}|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2\|\mathbb{P}_{Y_{1,\theta}\leq\delta}\pi_{\theta}(1-x_{1}^{*})\|\|\pi_{\theta}((1-x_{1})(1-y_{1}))\|\|\pi_{\theta}(1-y_{1}^{*})\mathbb{P}_{X_{1,\theta}\leq\delta}\|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\|\mathbb{P}_{X_{2,\theta}\leq\delta}\mathbb{P}_{Y_{2,\theta}\leq\delta}\|\|\mathbb{P}_{X_{3,\theta}\leq\delta}\mathbb{P}_{Y_{3,\theta}\leq\delta}\|\|\xi\|\|\eta\|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 16(\cos\frac{\pi\theta}{2})\cdot\frac{2}{4-\delta}\|\xi\|\eta\|.

If we have chosen
\delta>0
small enough,
then

\varepsilon:=8-16(\cos\frac{\pi\theta_{0}}{2})\cdot\frac{2}{4-\delta}>4C\delta.

Observe that
\delta>0
and
\varepsilon>0
depends on
the absolute constants
\theta_{0}>0
and

,
but not on
\theta\in[\theta_{0},1/2]
.
In the end,

\displaystyle|\mathopen{\langle}(X_{1,\theta}Y_{1,\theta}+X_{1,\theta}Y_{1,\theta})\zeta,\zeta\mathclose{\rangle}|

\displaystyle\leq(8-\varepsilon)\|\xi\|\eta\|+C\delta

In this section, we continue the study of

In addition to the notations from Section
2
,
we denote by

I[\Gamma]:=\lh\{1-x:x\in\Gamma\}\subset\mathbb{R}[\Gamma]

the
augmentation ideal
.
We observe that
\Sigma^{2}I[\Gamma]=I[\Gamma]\cap\Sigma^{2}\mathbb{R}[\Gamma]
and
hence there is no ambiguity about the order
\preceq
on
I[\Gamma]
.
In
[
Oz2
]
, it was observed that the combinatorial Laplacian

\Delta\in\Sigma^{2}I[\Gamma]

is an
order unit
for
I[\Gamma]
(more precisely for
I[\Gamma]^{\mathrm{her}}
, but this abuse
of terminology should not cause any problem). That is to say,
for every
\xi\in I[\Gamma]^{\mathrm{her}}
, there is
R>0

such that
\xi\preceq R\Delta
. We will indicate this by the notation
\xi\mathrel{\scalebox{.8}[1]{$\prec$}\mkern-4.0mu\scalebox{.4}[1]{$\prec$}\mkern-5.5mu\scalebox{.4}[1]{$\prec$}}\Delta
.

We review the relation between positive linear functionals
on
I[\Gamma]
and
1
-cocycles
(with unitary coefficients).
A linear functional
\varphi
on
I[\Gamma]
is said to be
positive
if
it is selfadjoint and
\varphi(\Sigma^{2}I[\Gamma])\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.
One has
\varphi(\Delta)=0
if and only if
\varphi=0
.
Every positive linear functional
\varphi
gives rise to a semi-inner product

\mathopen{\langle}\xi,\eta\mathclose{\rangle}:=\varphi(\xi^{*}\eta)
and the corresponding semi-norm

\|\xi\|:=\varphi(\xi^{*}\xi)^{1/2}
on
I[\Gamma]
, with respect to which
the left multiplication by an element of
\Gamma
is orthogonal.
This is the Gelfand–Naimark construction.
The map
b\colon\Gamma\ni t\mapsto 1-t\in I[\Gamma]
is a
1
-cocycle, i.e., it satisfies

b(st)=b(s)+sb(t)
for every
s,t\in\Gamma
.
We note that
\varphi(1-t)=\frac{1}{2}\varphi((1-t)^{*}(1-t))=\frac{1}{2}\|b(t)\|^{2}
and

\varphi(\Delta)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{s\in S}\|b(s)\|^{2}
.
In fact, every
1
-cocycle arises in this way.
See, e.g., Appendix C in
[
BHV
]
and Appendix D in
[
BO
]
for a comprehensive treatment.

It is proved in
[
Oz2
]
that
\overline{\Sigma^{2}I[\Gamma]}=I[\Gamma]^{\mathrm{her}}\cap\overline{\Sigma^{2}\mathbb{R}[\Gamma]}
. That is to say,

\displaystyle\overline{\Sigma^{2}I[\Gamma]}

\displaystyle:=\{\xi\in I[\Gamma]^{\mathrm{her}}:\forall\varepsilon>0\ \xi+\varepsilon\Delta\succeq 0\}

\displaystyle=\{\xi\in I[\Gamma]^{\mathrm{her}}:\varphi(\xi)\geq 0\mbox{ for every positive linear functional $\varphi$ on $I[\Gamma]$}\}

\displaystyle=\{\xi\in I[\Gamma]^{\mathrm{her}}:\xi\geq 0\mbox{ in }\mathrm{C}^{*}[\Gamma]\}

We also record an easy consequence of the Hahn–Banach
separation theorem (a.k.a. Eidelheit–Kakutani separation theorem in this context).
For
\xi,\eta\in I[\Gamma]^{\mathrm{her}}
(or in any real ordered vector
space with an order unit
\Delta
), the following are equivalent.

(1)

\varphi(\xi)=0\Rightarrow\varphi(\eta)\leq 0
for every positive linear functional
\varphi
on
I[\Gamma]
.

(2)

-\eta\in\overline{\Sigma^{2}I[\Gamma]-\mathbb{R}\xi}
.

(3)

\forall\varepsilon>0

\exists R\in\mathbb{R}
such that
R\xi-\eta+\varepsilon\Delta\succeq 0
.

We observe that since

\varphi(\Delta^{2})=\mathopen{\langle}\Delta,\Delta\mathclose{\rangle}=\|\sum_{s\in S}b(s)\|^{2}.

one has
\varphi(\Delta^{2})=0
if and only if the corresponding

1
-cocycle
b
is
harmonic
in the sense
\sum_{s\in S}b(s)=0
.
This observation recovers Shalom’s theorem (
[
Sh1
]
)
that every finitely generated group without property (T)
has a non-zero harmonic
1
-cocycle. An essentially same proof
was given in
[
Ni
]
.

We record the following well-known fact.

•

If a
1
-cocycle

vanishes on
a normal subgroup
N\triangleleft\Gamma
,
then
N
acts trivially on
\lh b(\Gamma)
and
hence
b
factors through the quotient
\Gamma/N

•

If
b
is a harmonic
1
-cocycle on
\Gamma

then the center
\mathcal{Z}(\Gamma)
acts trivially on

\lh b(\Gamma)
and
\Gamma
acts trivially on
\lh b(\mathcal{Z}(\Gamma))

•

Every harmonic
1
-cocycle on an abelian group is an additive homomorphism.

The first assertion is not difficult to show. The second follows from the identity

(1-x)b(z)=(1-z)b(x)
for
x\in\Gamma
and
z\in\mathcal{Z}(\Gamma)

If
b
is harmonic, then
(|S|-\sum_{s\in S}s)b(z)=0

by strict convexity of a Hilbert space,
b(z)=sb(z)
for
s\in S

An additive character

can be viewed as
a harmonic
1
-cocycle. The corresponding positive linear functional

This should not be confused with the linear extension

which is not even selfadjoint.
The positive linear functional

factors through the abelianization

We denote the

by

I^{k}[\Gamma]:=\lh(I[\Gamma]^{k})\subset\mathbb{R}[\Gamma].

It is well-known and easy to see from the formula

is generated as a rng by

On the other hand, every additive homomorphism

\chi

\Gamma

We will see that

The element

We next show that

\{\Gamma_{i}\}

(\tau)
.

[

has property (T) if

[

\Delta_{k}:=\sum_{i\neq j}\sum_{r=1}^{d}(1-e_{i,j}(t_{r}^{k}))^{*}(1-e_{i,j}(t_{r}^{k}))\in\mathbb{R}[\EL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}\langle t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}\rangle)]

(so

We may also assume that

satisfies

.

Let
\pi,\mathcal{H}
and
v
be given for
\EL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}\langle t_{1},\ldots,t_{d}\rangle)

(but we will omit writing

to ease notation) and put

We assume
\delta<(1/2)^{10}
and put
\rho:=\delta^{1/10}
.
Recall the notation
that
\mathbb{P}_{\Delta\leq(\delta/\rho)^{2}}
stands for the spectral projection
of
\Delta
for the interval
[0,(\delta/\rho)^{2}]
.
For
v_{0}:=\mathbb{P}_{\Delta\leq(\delta/\rho)^{2}}v
, one has
\|v-v_{0}\|\leq\rho
and

\mathopen{\langle}(\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2})v_{0},v_{0}\mathclose{\rangle}\leq\delta^{2}+\varepsilon^{-1}(\delta/\rho)^{4}=:\delta_{0}^{2}.

Now,

satisfies

\|v_{0}-v_{1}\|\leq\rho^{2}

\mathopen{\langle}(\Delta_{2}+\Delta_{3})v_{1},v_{1}\mathclose{\rangle}\leq\varepsilon^{-1}(\delta_{0}/\rho^{2})^{4}=:\delta_{1}^{2}.

We continue this and obtain

such that

\mathopen{\langle}(\Delta_{2^{k}}+\Delta_{2^{k}+1})v_{k},v_{k}\mathclose{\rangle}\leq\varepsilon^{-1}(\delta_{k-1}/\rho^{k+1})^{4}=:\delta_{k}^{2}.

Then the vector
w:=\lim_{k}v_{k}
satisfies
\|v_{k}-w\|\leq\rho^{k+1}
(as
\rho<1/2
).
Moreover,

\displaystyle 2^{-k}|\log\delta_{k}|

\displaystyle=2^{-(k-1)}|\log\delta_{k-1}|-2^{-(k-1)}(k+1)|\log\rho|+2^{-(k+1)}\log\varepsilon

\displaystyle=|\log\delta_{0}|-\bigl(\sum_{m=1}^{k}2^{-(m-1)}(m+1)\bigr)|\log\rho|+\frac{1}{2}(1-2^{-k})\log\varepsilon

\displaystyle>|\log\delta|/10

\delta>0
is small enough compared to
\varepsilon>0
.

\delta_{k}\to 0
at a double exponential rate.

\lim_{l}\max_{i,j,r}\|w-e_{i,j}(t_{r}^{l})w\|=0

We first observe that

\|w-e_{i,j}(t_{r}^{2^{k}})w\|\leq 2\|v_{k}-w\|+\delta_{k}\leq\rho^{k}+\delta_{k}

l
be given. Take

such that

with

.
Then for

\|e_{i,j}(t_{r}^{l})w-e_{i,j}(t_{r}^{2^{k}+b})w\|\leq\sum_{m=\lfloor k/2\rfloor}^{k-1}a(m)(\rho^{m}+\delta_{m}),

which tends to

as

.
Observe that the recurrence relation
p_{0}:=2^{k-\lfloor k/2\rfloor}

and
p_{m+1}:=2p_{m}+a(\lfloor k/2\rfloor-1-m)
gives

p_{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}=2^{k}+b

Now by arguing as in the previous paragraph,
but starting at
v_{k-\lfloor k/2\rfloor}

(\Delta_{p_{m}}+\Delta_{p_{m}+1})^{2}\geq\varepsilon(\Delta_{p_{m+1}}+\Delta_{p_{m+1}+1})

one obtains

\|v_{k-\lfloor k/2\rfloor}-e_{i,j}(t_{r}^{2^{k}+b})v_{k-\lfloor k/2\rfloor}\|\leq\rho^{k-\lfloor k/2\rfloor}+\delta_{k}\to 0.

Since
\|v_{k-\lfloor k/2\rfloor}-w\|\to 0
as
l\to\infty
, this completes the proof.
∎

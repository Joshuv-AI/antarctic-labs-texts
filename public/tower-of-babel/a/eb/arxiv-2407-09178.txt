Shafarevich–Tate groups of holomorphic Lagrangian fibrations II
Anna Abasheva

Abstract
Let X X be a compact hyperkähler manifold with a Lagrangian fibration π : X → B \pi\colon X\to B . A Shafarevich–Tate twist of X X is a holomorphic symplectic manifold with a Lagrangian fibration π φ : X φ → B \pi^{\varphi}\colon X^{\varphi}\to B which is isomorphic to π \pi locally over the base. In particular, π φ \pi^{\varphi} has the same fibers as π \pi . A twist X φ X^{\varphi} corresponds to an element φ \varphi in the Shafarevich–Tate group Ш \Sha of X X . We show that X φ X^{\varphi} is Kähler when a multiple of φ \varphi lies in the connected component of unity of Ш \Sha and give a necessary condition for X φ X^{\varphi} to be bimeromorphic to a Kähler manifold.

1.2
Statement of the results

Theorem A

(Theorem
5.0.1
, Theorem
3.2.3
)
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to B
be a Lagrangian fibration on an irreducible hyperkähler manifold

1.

The twist

is Kähler.

2.

Assume

with respect to

is projective if and only if

is torsion.

We proved a version of this theorem in
[
2
, Theorem 1.3]
for a general hyperkähler manifold assuming

. The new proof does not require these assumptions. Another proof of Theorem
A
assuming
\varphi\in\Sha^{0}
was given by Soldatenkov–Verbitsky in
[
35
]
, however, our arguments are different.

One of the steps of the proof of Theorem
A
(1) in the previous version of the paper, namely Lemma 4.6.1, crucially relied on the argument used by Perego in
[
31
, Proposition 2.6]
to show that certain limits of hyperkähler manifolds are Fujiki class
\mathcal{C}
. Unfortunately, both our and Perego’s arguments turned out to be faulty, as was pointed out in
[
35
]
. Instead of trying to fix this step of the proof, we came up with a substantially different proof of Theorem

that doesn’t rely at all on Perego’s results. The new proof still uses some arguments from the previous version of the paper: Subsections
5.1
-
5.3
(= Subsections 4.3 - 4.5 in the previous version) remain essentially the same. However, the new proof includes one more ingredient, which we find interesting in its own right.

Theorem B

(
4.0.17
)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{D}
be a family of irreducible hyperkähler manifolds. Suppose that the restriction
\mathcal{X}_{U}
of
\mathcal{X}
to an open subset
U\subset\mathbb{D}
with
0\in\overline{U}
admits a family of Lagrangian fibrations
\Pi_{U}\colon\mathcal{X}_{U}\to\mathcal{B}_{U}
. Assume that the central fiber

is non-projective. Then the family of fibrations

extends to a family of Lagrangian fibrations

.

Note that the theorem may not hold if the central fiber is projective because in this case the isotropic line bundle

may not remain nef. As was shown in
[

]
, the theorem still holds if we assume

A Shafarevich–Tate twist

of a holomorphic symplectic manifold is holomorphic symplectic, and the fibration

(

)

Let

1.2.2.

In the next theorem we compute the second Betti number of Shafarevich–Tate twists. Note that the differential

on the second page of the Leray spectral sequence of

for the map

maps

Theorem D

(
6.3.11
)
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to B
be a Lagrangian fibration on an irreducible hyperkähler manifold
X
and
\varphi\in\Sha
. Then exactly one of the following two cases occurs.

1.

If the image
\overline{\varphi}
of
\varphi
in
H^{2}(R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})
lies in the image of
d_{2}
, then
b_{2}(X^{\varphi})=b_{2}(X)
. Moreover, there is a cohomology class

which restricts to an ample class on a smooth fiber.

If

Definition 1.2.3
.

A complex manifold is said to be of

if it is bimeromorphic to a Kähler manifold.

We will derive the following criterion for non-Kählerness of Shafarevich–Tate twists as an easy corollary of Theorem

Theorem E

(
6.3.12
)
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to B
be a Lagrangian fibration on a hyperkähler manifold

. Pick

2.1.1. Beauville-Bogomolov-Fujiki form.

One of the key cohomological features of hyperkähler manifold is the existence of a quadratic form on their second cohomology called
Beauville-Bogomolov-Fujiki form (BBF form)
.

Theorem 2.1.2

(
[
12
, Part III, Corollary 23.11 & Proposition 23.14]
)
.

Let
X
be an irreducible hyperkähler manifold of dimension

. Then there exists an integral symmetric non-degenerate form

on

such that
\forall\alpha\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})

c_{X}q(\alpha)^{n}=\int_{X}\alpha^{2n}.

The constant

is positive and depends only on the deformation type of
X
.

Remark 2.1.3
.

The integral form

from Theorem
2.1.2
is uniquely defined if we require it to be non-divisible.

Definition 2.1.4
.

The form

from Theorem

is called the
Beauville–Bogomolov–Fujiki form
or BBF form.

2.1.5. Fibers of Lagrangian fibrations are abelian varieties.

Consider a Lagrangian fibration
\pi\colon X\to B
(Definition

) on an irreducible holomorphic symplectic manifold

. A general fiber of

is a complex torus and even an abelian variety

Theorem 2.1.6

(
[
37
,
26
]
)

Let
\pi\colon X\to B
be a Lagrangian fibration on a hyperkähler manifold and

its smooth fiber. Then the restriction map

H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{2}(F,\mathbb{Q})

has rank one.

Thanks to Theorem

The image in

2.1.8. Vertical vector fields.

The holomorphic symplectic form

\Omega_{X}\xrightarrow{\iota_{\sigma}}T_{X}

denote the subset

\pi^{*}\Omega_{B^{reg}}\hookrightarrow\Omega_{X^{\prime}}\xrightarrow[\sim]{\iota_{\sigma}}T_{X^{\prime}}\to\pi^{*}T_{B^{reg}}.

It is easy to see that it vanishes on

. Indeed, for every form

is tangent to smooth fibers of

Since

\pi_{*}\pi^{*}\Omega_{B^{reg}}\simeq\Omega_{B^{reg}}
, and we get a map:

\Omega_{B^{reg}}\hookrightarrow\pi_{*}T_{X^{\prime}/B^{reg}}.

(2)

The sheaf

is the kernel of the map

(Definition

is reflexive. The pushforward of a reflexive sheaf along an equidimensional morphism is reflexive

(3)

Here

. Equivalently,

, where

is the embedding of the smooth locus of

into

Corollary 2.1.14
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to B
be a Lagrangian fibration on an irreducible hyperkähler manifold, not necessarily projective. Then Theorem

holds for any Shafarevich–Tate twist

of

Proof:
.

By
[

any non-trivial family of deformations of an irreducible hyperkähler manifolds contains a projective deformation. Therefore there exists a projective Shafarevich–Tate deformation

of the Lagrangian fibration

The sheaf of groups

Theorem 2.1.16
.

Let
B
be the base of a Lagrangian fibration on an irreducible hyperkähler manifold
X
. Then the cohomology groups
H^{j}(B,\Omega^{[i]}_{B})
are the same as for
B=\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

Proof:
.

Step 1.
By Corollary
2.1.14
,
H^{j}(\Omega^{[i]}_{B})\simeq H^{j}(R^{i}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})
. It follows from a result by Kollár
[
21
, p.172]
that

R\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}\simeq\bigoplus R^{i}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}[-i].

Therefore the Leray spectral sequence for
\mathcal{O}_{X}
degenerates on
E^{2}
and

h^{0,k}(X)=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k}h^{k-i}(R^{i}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}).

When
k
is odd
h^{0,k}=0
and when
k
is even
h^{0,k}=1
. We see immediately that
H^{j}(R^{i}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
when
i+j
is odd. When
k
is even, there is exactly one
i\leq k
such that
H^{k-i}(R^{i}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})
is non-zero.

Step 2.
We will show that
H^{i}(R^{i}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})
does not vanish. This will complete the proof. Consider the filtration
F^{i}H^{0,k}(X)
on
H^{0,k}(X)
induced by the Leray spectral sequence. First, consider the case
k=2
. The cohomology group
H^{0,2}(X)
is generated by
\overline{\sigma}
. The restriction of
\overline{\sigma}
to a smooth fiber is zero, hence the image of
\overline{\sigma}
in
H^{0}(B,R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})
vanishes. The form
\overline{\sigma}
is non-degenerate, hence not the pullback of a
(0,2)
-form on the base even locally. Therefore
F^{0}H^{0,2}(X)=0
and
F^{1}H^{0,2}(X)=F^{2}H^{0,2}(X)=H^{0,2}(X)
.

It follows that
\overline{\sigma}^{i}\in F^{i}H^{0,2i}(X)
for all
i
. Suppose that we know that
\overline{\sigma}^{i}\not\in F^{i-1}H^{0,2i}(X)
. Then
H^{i}(R^{i}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})=F^{i}H^{0,2i}(X)/F^{i-1}H^{0,2i}(X)
is non-zero, and we are done. If
\overline{\sigma}^{i}
happens to be contained in
F^{i-1}H^{0,2i}(X)
, then
\overline{\sigma}^{n}
is contained in
F^{n-1}H^{0,2n}(X)
. However,
F^{n-1}H^{0,2n}(X)
vanishes for dimension reasons. Indeed,
H^{n+k}(R^{n-k}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
for
k>0
. Hence
\overline{\sigma}^{n}=0
, contradiction.
∎

2.2

2.2.1. Structure of the Shafarevich–Tate group.

Recall that the sheaf of groups
Aut^{0}_{X/B}
is defined as the image of the exponential map
\pi_{*}T_{X/B}\to Aut_{X/B}
(Definition
1.1.4
). Define
\Gamma
to be the kernel of this map. The short exact sequence

0\to\Gamma\to\pi_{*}T_{X/B}\to Aut^{0}_{X/B}\to 0

induces the long exact sequence of cohomology groups:

H^{1}(\Gamma)\to H^{1}(\pi_{*}T_{X/B})\to\Sha\to H^{2}(\Gamma).

(4)

We will call the image of
H^{1}(\pi_{*}T_{X/B})
in
\Sha
the
connected component of unity
of
\Sha
and will denote it by
\Sha^{0}
. The quotient
\Sha/\Sha^{0}
is the
discrete part
of
\Sha
.

The sequence (
4
) is exact on the right. Indeed, the cohomology group
H^{2}(\pi_{*}T_{X/B})
is isomorphic to
H^{2}(\Omega^{[1]}_{B})
by Theorem
2.1.11
. By Theorem
2.1.16
this cohomology group vanishes. Similarly, the vector space

is isomorphic to

and is one-dimensional.

There is a useful differential geometric point of view on Shafarevich–Tate deformations

Definition 2.2.3
.

Denote by

of

It is not hard to see that the fibration

Degenerate twistor deformations form a family

(

We will see in Theorem

Theorem 2.2.7

Proof:
.

By
[
2

, the Leray spectral sequence induces the following isomorphisms:

H^{1}(B,R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})\simeq H^{0,2}(X),\>\>\>\>\>\text{and}\>\>\>\>\>H^{1}(B,R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z})=W_{\mathbb{Z}}/\eta.

It follows from (

is isogenous to

For every ring

3.1

The goal of this chapter is to prove the second part of Theorem

Theorem 3.1.1

Let
\pi\colon X\to B
be a Lagrangian fibration on an irreducible holomorphic symplectic manifold, and

•

The proof of Theorem

L
on
X
and cover
B
by open disks

. Let
L_{i}
denote the restriction of

Lemma 3.1.2

Let
\varphi
be an
r

. Cover

by small open subsets

and represent

3.2
Criterion of projectivity

In this Subsection we will prove Theorem
A
(2). We will begin with a useful general criterion of projectivity of Lagrangian fibrations, or more generally, of irreducible hyperkähler manifold with an integral isotropic class.

Lemma 3.2.1
.

Let
X
be an irreducible hyperkähler manifold with an isotropic class
\eta\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. Then
X
is projective if and only if
NS(X)\not\subset\eta^{\perp}
. In particular, if
X
is not projective, then all curves on
X
are orthogonal to
\eta
.

Proof:
.

If
X
is projective and
\alpha
is an ample class on
X
, then
q(\alpha,\eta)\neq 0
. Indeed, the BBF form is negative definite on
\alpha^{\perp}\in H^{1,1}(X)

[
12
, Corollary 23.11]
, hence
\alpha
can’t be orthogonal to an isotropic class. Conversely, let
\alpha\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
be a class such that
q(\alpha,\eta)\neq 0
. By possibly changing the sign of
\alpha
, we may assume that
q(\alpha,\eta)>0
. Then the class
\alpha^{\prime}:=\alpha+t\eta
has positive square for a large enough
t\in\mathbb{Z}
. Indeed,

q(\alpha+t\eta)=q(\alpha)+2tq(\alpha,\eta).

By
[
17
, Thm. 3.11]
, an irreducible hyperkähler manifold with a positive integral class
\alpha^{\prime}\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
is projective.

∎

Denote by
p\colon H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})\to H^{0,2}(X)
the Hodge projection. Let
\sigma
be the holomorphic symplectic form such that
q(\sigma,\overline{\sigma})=1
. In the following lemma we will give a characterization of torsion in
\Sha^{0}
in terms of the BBF form.

Lemma 3.2.2
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to B
be a Lagrangian fibration. Consider a class
t\overline{\sigma}\in H^{0,2}(X)
,
t\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
[t\overline{\sigma}]
denote its image in
\Sha^{0}
. Then
[t\overline{\sigma}]
is torsion if and only if there exists a rational class
l\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
such that
q(l,\sigma)=t
.

Proof:
.

By Theorem
2.2.7
, the group
\Sha^{0}
is isogenous to
H^{0,2}(X)/p(H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. Hence, the class
[t\overline{\sigma}]
is torsion if and only if
t\overline{\sigma}
lies in the image of the group
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
. The Hodge projection
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})\to H^{0,2}(X)
is given by the formula

v\mapsto q(v,\sigma)\overline{\sigma}.

Hence,
t\overline{\sigma}
lies in the image of
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
under the Hodge projection if and only if there exists a class
l\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
such that
q(l,\sigma)=t
.
∎

Instead of proving Theorem
A
(2) directly, we will show a more general statement.

Theorem 3.2.3
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to B
be a Lagrangian fibration on a projective hyperkähler manifold, and

there is a class

.

(1)

By Theorem

, there is a relatively ample class on

. Hence

An ample class

We can find a torsion element

such that

is arbitrarily close to

if and only if the restriction of

the manifold

∎

Corollary 3.2.4
.

Let

4
Limits of Lagrangian fibrations

4.0.1. Douady spaces.

Theorem
B
relies on careful examination of Douady spaces of hyperkähler manifolds, which we will introduce below. The main reference for this subsection are lecture notes
[
24
]
.

Douady showed that the set of complex subspaces of a complex space
X
admits a structure of a complex space
[
24
, Theorem 6.0.2]
.

Definition 4.0.2
.

The complex space parametrizing purely
k
-dimensional complex subspaces of a complex space
X
is called the
Douady space
of
X
and will be denoted by
\mathfrak{D}_{k}(X)
.

Similarly to the Hilbert scheme of a projective variety, the Douady space of
X
represents the functor from complex spaces to sets sending
S
to the set of flat families of
k
-dimensional subspaces of
X
parametrized by
S
. A flat family of subspaces of
X
over
S
is a subspace
Y\subset S\times X
having pure relative dimension
k
over
S
, flat over
S
, and proper over
X
. In particular, the Douady space admits a
universal family

Y\subset\mathfrak{D}_{k}(X)\times X
of subspaces of
X
.

We collect here a few useful facts about Douady spaces. We will formulate them for Kähler spaces, which are potentially singular generalizations of Kähler manifolds
[
24
, Definition 4.2.3]
.

Theorem 4.0.3

(
[
24
, Corollary 11.3.2]
)
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler space. Then every connected component of
\mathfrak{D}_{k}(X)
is compact.

Theorem 4.0.4
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler space. Then every connected component of
\mathfrak{D}_{k}(X)
is of Fujiki class
\mathcal{C}
, i.e. bimeromorphic to a Kähler manifold.

Remark 4.0.5
.

The Douady space
\mathfrak{D}_{k}(X)
admits a proper birational map to the Barlet space
\mathfrak{B}_{k}(X)
of
X
parametrizing
k
-dimensional cycles on
X

[
24
, Theorem 11.3.1]
. As was shown by Varouchas, every connected component of the Barlet space of a compact Kähler space is a Kähler space
[
24
, Theorem 11.1.6]
. It is an open question whether the Douady space of a compact Kähler space is Kähler.

The construction of a Douady space works in families, and Theorem
4.0.3
holds in a relative setting.

Theorem 4.0.6

(
[
11
, Proposition 2.6]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{D}^{n}
be a smooth family of compact complex manifolds over a polydisk. Suppose that the central fiber
X_{0}
is Kähler. Then every connected component of the relative Douady space
\mathfrak{D}_{k}(\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{D}^{n})\to\mathbb{D}^{n}
is proper over
\mathbb{D}^{n}
after possibly shrinking
\mathbb{D}^{n}
.

Remark 4.0.7
.

The theorem above is formulated in
[
11
]
for Barlet spaces, but by Remark
4.0.5
, the same result holds for Douady spaces.

In the following example the Douady space has a particularly easy description. The details of the proof are technical when the base is not assumed to be smooth.

Lemma 4.0.8
.

Let

be a flat morphism of compact Kähler spaces. Assume that

is reduced and irreducible and

for any fiber

of

is isomorphic to

Proof:
.

Step 1.
Let

be the universal family over the connected component

of the Douady space. By the universal property of the Douady space, there is a morphism

such that the morphism

via the morphism

. The morphism

is injective on points. We claim that it induces an isomorphism on tangent spaces. Indeed, the tangent space at a point of

. If

is smooth, this is enough to conclude that

is an isomorphism. Indeed, by the holomorphic inverse function theorem and injectivity of

must be an open embedding. Since

is an isomorphism. If

Step 2.
By semi-continuity, there exists an open subset

are connected. Denote the preimage of

Step 3.
We claim that every subvariety

is set-theoretically contained in a fiber of

. Pick a Kähler form

on

on

. Denote by

. Then

because the cohomology classes

and

coincide. The integral of

of

is not contracted to a point by

. Therefore

, contradiction. We conclude that every irreducible component of

is contained in a fiber of

. Since

is connected, it is contained in a single fiber.

Step 4.
We claim that there exists a retraction
r\colon T^{\prime}\to S
making the following diagram commutative.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}

Indeed, by Step 2, the map

6.0.1. Higher pushforwards of
\mathbb{Q}_{X}
do not depend on a twist.

Let
\pi\colon X\to B
be a Lagrangian fibration and
\pi^{\varphi}\colon X^{\varphi}\to B
its Shafarevich–Tate twist. Then the sheaves
R^{k}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z}
and
R^{k}\pi^{\varphi}_{*}\mathbb{Z}
are canonically identified. Indeed, represent
\varphi
as a Čech cocycle
(\varphi_{ij})
, where
\varphi_{ij}\in Aut^{0}_{X/B}(U_{ij})
. The automorphisms
\varphi_{ij}
are flows of vector fields, hence they act trivially on
H^{k}(X_{ij})
.

In particular the vector spaces
H^{0}(R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})
and
H^{0}(R^{2}\pi^{\varphi}_{*}\mathbb{Q})
are canonically identified. However, the differentals

d_{2}\colon H^{0}(R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})\to H^{2}(R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})\>\>\>\>\>\text{and}\>\>\>\>\>d_{2}^{\varphi}\colon H^{0}(R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})\to H^{2}(R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})

from the Leray spectral sequence of
X
and
X^{\varphi}
respectively may be different.

6.0.2. The restriction map
H^{2}(X)\to H^{2}(F)
has rank at most one.

Suppose that
X
is hyperkähler, and let
F
be a smooth fiber of
\pi\colon X\to B
. By Theorem
2.1.6
, the restriction map
H^{2}(X)\to H^{2}(F)
has a one-dimensional image generated by an ample class. Global invariant cycle theorem implies that

\operatorname{im}\left(H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{2}(F,\mathbb{Q})\right)=H^{2}(F,\mathbb{Q})^{\pi_{1}(B^{\circ})}=H^{0}(B^{\circ},R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{B^{\circ}}}\right.}).

Here
H^{2}(F,\mathbb{Q})^{\pi_{1}(B^{\circ})}
denotes the subspace of
H^{2}(F)
invariant under the monodromy action of
\pi_{1}(B^{\circ})
. It follows that
H^{2}(F,\mathbb{Q})^{\pi_{1}(B^{\circ})}
is one-dimensional and generated by an ample class.

Let
X^{\varphi}
be a Shafarevich–Tate twist of
X
, not necessarily Kähler. Then the image of the map
H^{2}(X^{\varphi},\mathbb{Q})\to H^{2}(F,\mathbb{Q})
still lies in
H^{2}(F,\mathbb{Q})^{\pi_{1}(B^{\circ})}
. The latter space is isomorphic to
H^{0}(B^{\circ},R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{B^{\circ}}}\right.})
, hence does not depend on a twist. We obtain the following statement.

Proposition 6.0.3
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to B
be a Lagrangian fibration on an irreducible hyperkähler manifold
X
and
X^{\varphi}
be its Shafarevich–Tate twist. Then the restriction map

is either trivial or has a one-dimensional image generated by an ample class of
F
.

Lemma 6.1.1

to
Y^{\circ}
is the pullback of a form from
S^{\circ}
. By Lemma
2.1.10
, the form
\alpha
must be the pullback of a reflexive form from
S
.
∎

6.2.4.

Let
\pi\colon X\to B
be a Lagrangian fibration. Consider the subsheaf
(\pi_{*}\Omega^{2}_{X})^{\prime}
of
\pi_{*}\Omega^{2}_{X}
consisting of holomorphic 2-forms
\xi
with trivial restriction to all smooth fibers. Thanks to Lemma
6.2.3
, there is a natural map

(\pi_{*}\Omega^{2}_{X})^{\prime}\to\mathcal{Hom}(\pi_{*}T_{X/B},\Omega^{[1]}_{B}).

(22)

The holomorphic symplectic form
\sigma
on
X
induces an isomorphism
\pi_{*}T_{X/B}\simeq\Omega^{[1]}_{B}
(Theorem
2.1.11
). Composing the map (
22
) with this isomorphism, we obtain a map of sheaves

\rho\colon(\pi_{*}\Omega^{2}_{X})^{\prime}\to\mathcal{End}(\Omega^{[1]}_{B}).

Lemma 6.2.5
.

Define the sheaf
\mathcal{End}^{\prime}_{X}(\Omega^{[1]}_{B})
as the image of
\rho
. Then for any Shafarevich–Tate twist
X^{\varphi}
the sheaf
\mathcal{End}^{\prime}_{X^{\varphi}}(\Omega^{[1]}_{B})
coincides with
\mathcal{End}^{\prime}_{X}(\Omega^{[1]}_{B})
.

Proof:
.

The statement is local on
B
. For every sufficiently small open disk
U\subset B
, the manifolds
\pi^{-1}(U)
and
(\pi^{\varphi})^{-1}(U)
are isomorphic as Lagrangian fibrations, hence the claim.
∎

Lemma 6.2.6
.

The sequence of sheaves on
B

0\to\Omega^{[2]}_{B}\to(\pi_{*}\Omega^{2}_{X})^{\prime}\to\mathcal{End}^{\prime}_{X}(\Omega^{[1]}_{B})\to 0

is exact.

Proof:
.

Note that the map
(\pi_{*}\Omega^{2}_{X})^{\prime}\to\mathcal{End}^{\prime}_{X}(\Omega^{[1]}_{B})
is surjective by the definition of
\mathcal{End}^{\prime}_{X}(\Omega^{[1]}_{B})
. The first map
\Omega^{[2]}_{B}\to(\pi_{*}\Omega^{2}_{X})^{\prime}
is clearly injective.

The composite map
\Omega^{[2]}_{B}\to\mathcal{End}^{\prime}_{X}(\Omega^{[1]}_{B})
vanishes. Indeed, let
\alpha
be a local section of
\Omega^{[2]}_{B}
. Then for any vertical vector field
v
, the form
\iota_{v}\pi^{*}\alpha
vanishes on
X^{\circ}
, hence vanishes everywhere. Therefore,
\rho(\alpha)=0
.

It remains to prove exactness in the middle term. Let
U\subset B
be an open subset and
\xi
a holomorphic
2
-form on
\pi^{-1}(U)
such that
\rho(\xi)=0
. Consider the restriction of
\xi
to
X^{\circ}
. Since
\iota_{v}\xi=0
for every vertical vector field
v
, the form
\xi
is contained in
\pi^{*}\Omega^{2}_{B}(\pi^{-1}(U\cap B^{\circ}))
. The projection formula together with the fact that
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\circ}}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{B^{\circ}}
implies that

\pi^{*}\Omega^{2}_{B}(\pi^{-1}(U\cap B^{\circ}))=\pi_{*}\pi^{*}\Omega^{2}_{B}(U\cap B^{\circ})=\Omega^{2}_{B}(U\cap B^{\circ}).

Hence there exists a holomorphic
2
-form
\alpha^{\circ}
on
U\cap B^{\circ}
such that
\xi{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{\pi^{-1}(U\cap B^{\circ})}}\right.}=\pi^{*}\alpha^{\circ}
. By Lemma
2.1.10
,
\xi=\pi^{*}\alpha
for some reflexive holomorphic
2
-form
\alpha
on
U
.
∎

We are finally ready to show that all holomorphic
2
-forms on
X^{\varphi}
are multiples of
\sigma_{\varphi}
.

Theorem 6.2.7
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to B
be a Lagrangian fibration on an irreducible hyperkähler manifold
X
. Then
H^{0}(\Omega^{2}_{X^{\varphi}})
is generated by the holomorphic symplectic form
\sigma_{\varphi}
for all
\varphi\in\Sha
.

Proof:
.

By Lemma
6.2.2
, every holomorphic
2
-form
\xi
on
X^{\varphi}
restricts trivially to every smooth fiber. Therefore,

H^{0}(B,(\pi_{*}\Omega^{2}_{X^{\varphi}})^{\prime})=H^{0}(X^{\varphi},\Omega^{2}_{X^{\varphi}}).

Lemma
6.2.6
shows that the sequence

0\to\Omega^{[2]}_{B}\to(\pi_{*}\Omega^{2}_{X^{\varphi}})^{\prime}\to\mathcal{End}^{\prime}_{X^{\varphi}}(\Omega^{[1]}_{B})\to 0

is exact. Consider its long exact sequence of cohomology

0\to H^{0}(\Omega^{[2]}_{B})\to H^{2,0}(X^{\varphi})\to H^{0}(\mathcal{End}^{\prime}_{X^{\varphi}}(\Omega^{[1]}_{B}))\to H^{1}(\Omega^{[2]}_{B})

The cohomology groups
H^{i}(\Omega^{[2]}_{B})
vanish for
i=0,1
(Theorem
2.1.16
). Therefore,

H^{2,0}(X^{\varphi})\simeq H^{0}(\mathcal{End}^{\prime}_{X^{\varphi}}(\Omega^{[1]}_{B})).

The sheaf
\mathcal{End}_{X^{\varphi}}^{\prime}(\Omega^{[1]}_{B})
does not depend on a twist by Lemma
6.2.5
, therefore
H^{2,0}(X^{\varphi})
does not depend on a twist.
∎

Remark 6.2.8
.

When
B=\mathbb{P}^{n}
, the proof of Theorem
6.2.7
can be simplified because
\operatorname{End}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}})\simeq\mathbb{C}
. By Lemma
6.2.2
, every holomorphic
2
-form
\xi
on
X^{\varphi}
restricts trivially to smooth fibers. Hence
\xi
induces an endomorphism
\rho(\xi)
of
\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
(Lemma
6.2.3
). Since
\operatorname{End}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}})=\mathbb{C}
, there exists a number
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\rho(\xi-\lambda\sigma)=0
. The contraction of every vertical vector field on
X
with
\xi-\lambda\sigma
vanishes, hence

(\xi-\lambda\sigma){\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{X^{\circ}}}\right.}\in\pi^{*}\Omega^{2}_{(\mathbb{P}^{n})^{\circ}}.

Since
\pi_{*}\pi^{*}\Omega^{2}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}=\Omega^{2}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}
, we have
(\xi-\lambda\sigma){\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{X^{\circ}}}\right.}=\pi^{*}\alpha^{\circ}
for some holomorphic
2
-form
\alpha^{\circ}
on
(\mathbb{P}^{n})^{\circ}
. By Lemma
2.1.10
,
\alpha^{\circ}
extends to a holomorphic form
\alpha
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
\xi-\lambda\sigma=\pi^{*}\alpha
. There are no non-trivial holomorphic forms on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, hence
\xi=\lambda\sigma
.

We were unable to show that
\operatorname{End}(\Omega^{[1]}_{B})\simeq\mathbb{C}
for any base of a Lagrangian fibration, although we expect it to be true.

6.2.9. Proof of Theorem
C
.

The statement immediately follows from Proposition
6.1.2
and Theorem
6.2.7
.
∎

6.3
Second cohomology of a twist

Our goal now is to prove Theorems
D
and Theorem
E
.

Lemma 6.3.1
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to B
be a Lagrangian fibration on an irreducible hyperkähler manifold
X
. Define the sheaf
\mathcal{NS}
on
B
as the image of the Chern class map
R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times}\to R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z}
. Then

H^{0}(B,\mathcal{NS})=H^{0}(R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z}).

In other words, for every section
\xi
of
R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z}
and a sufficiently fine open cover
B=\bigcup U_{i}
, there are line bundles
L_{i}
on
X_{i}
such that
\xi{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{U_{i}}}\right.}=c_{1}(L_{i})
. In particular, every section
\xi
of
R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z}
is locally the class of a closed
(1,1)
-form.

Proof:
.

Consider the exponential exact sequence

0\to\mathbb{Z}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times}\to 0.

It induces a long exact sequence of pushforward sheaves:

R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times}\to R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z}\to R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}.

The sheaf
R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z}/\operatorname{im}(R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times})=R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z}/\mathcal{NS}
is a subsheaf of
R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}\simeq\Omega^{[2]}_{B}
. Since
H^{0}(\Omega^{2}_{B})=0
(Theorem
2.1.16
), the sheaf
R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z}/\mathcal{NS}
has no global sections. Hence the natural inclusion
H^{0}(B,\mathcal{NS})\to H^{0}(R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z})
is an isomorphism.
∎

6.3.2. Isomorphisms between
T_{X/B}
and
R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
.

Let
\xi
be a global section of
H^{0}(R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})
. It defines a map
f_{\xi}\colon\pi_{*}T_{X/B}\to R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
in a similar way that a class
\omega\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Q})
defines a map
f_{\omega}
in
2.1.12
. Namely, by Lemma
6.3.1
we can represent
\xi{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{U_{i}}}\right.}
by a closed
(1,1)
-form
\xi_{i}
on
X_{i}
. Consider the map

f_{\xi_{i}}\colon\pi_{*}T_{X_{i}/U_{i}}\to R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{U_{i}}}\right.}

sending
v
to the class of
[\iota_{v}\xi_{i}]
under the
\bar{\partial}
-differential. Since the sheaf
R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
is torsion-free (Theorem
2.1.13
), the map
f_{\xi_{i}}
is determined uniquely by its restriction to
B^{\circ}\cap U_{i}
. The restrictions of both sheaves
\pi_{*}T_{X_{i}/U_{i}}
and
R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{U_{i}}}\right.}
to
B^{\circ}\cap U_{i}
are vector bundles. For every point
b\in B^{\circ}\cap U_{i}
, the map
f_{\xi_{i}}
over
b
is the map

H^{0}(F,T_{F})=H^{1,0}(F)^{\vee}\to H^{0,1}(F)

given by the contraction with
[\xi{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{F}}\right.}]\in H^{1,1}(F)
. Here
F
denotes
\pi^{-1}(b)
. Therefore, the map
f_{\xi_{i}}
depends only on the class
[\xi{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{F}}\right.}]
of the restriction of
\xi
to a smooth fiber
F
. In particular, maps
f_{\xi_{i}}
do not depend on the choice of the forms
\xi_{i}
representing
\xi\in H^{0}(R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})
and glue into a well-defined map

f_{\xi}\colon\pi_{*}T_{X/B}\to R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}.

6.3.3.

The argument above also shows that the map
H^{0}(B,R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{C})\to\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_{*}T_{X/B},R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X})
sending
\xi
to
f_{\xi}
factors through the restriction to a smooth fiber
F
:

H^{0}(B,R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{C})\to H^{0}(B^{\circ},R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{C})=H^{2}(F)^{\pi_{1}(B^{\circ})}\to\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_{*}T_{X/B},R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}).

The vector space
H^{2}(F)^{\pi_{1}(B^{\circ})}
is one-dimensional by
6.0.2
. Fix an element
\xi_{0}\in H^{0}(B,R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z})
which restricts non-trivially to
F
and let
f_{0}:=f_{\xi_{0}}
be the induced isomorphism
\pi_{*}T_{X/B}\to R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
. It follows that for every
\xi\in H^{0}(B,R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{C})
there exists a unique number
\lambda_{\xi}
such that

f_{\xi}=\lambda_{\xi}f_{0}.

(23)

Recall that the isomorphism
f_{0}\colon\pi_{*}T_{X/B}\to R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}

sends
\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}=\operatorname{ker}(\pi_{*}T_{X/B}\to Aut^{0}_{X/B})\otimes\mathbb{Q}\subset\pi_{*}T_{X/B}
isomorphically onto
R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}\subset R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
(
2.2.5
). We identify the group
H^{2}(\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}})=(\Sha/\Sha^{0})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
with
H^{2}(R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})
using the isomorphism
f_{0}{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}}}\right.}\colon\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}\to R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}
.

6.3.4. Boundary map
\Sha\to H^{2}(\Gamma)
.

The boundary map
\Sha=H^{1}(Aut^{0}(X/B))\to H^{2}(\Gamma)
coming from the short exact sequence

0\to\Gamma\to\pi_{*}T_{X/B}\to Aut^{0}_{X/B}\to 0

can be described in terms of Čech cocycles as follows. Pick
\varphi\in\Sha
and represent it by a
1
-cocycle
\varphi_{ij}\in Aut^{0}_{X/B}(U_{ij})
. We can find a vertical vector field
v_{ij}
on
X_{ij}
such that
\exp(v_{ij})=\varphi_{ij}
. The vector field
v_{ij}+v_{jk}+v_{ki}
on
X_{ijk}
lies in
\Gamma
thanks to the cocycle condition on
\varphi_{ij}
. It represents the class
\overline{\varphi}\in H^{2}(\Gamma)
, where
\overline{\varphi}
denotes the image of
\varphi
under the boundary map
\Sha\to H^{2}(\Gamma)
.

6.3.5. Boundary map
H^{0}(R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})\to H^{2}(R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})
.

We will describe the boundary map

d_{2}\colon H^{0}(R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})\to H^{2}(R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})\simeq H^{2}(\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}})

from the Leray spectral sequence of
\pi
in terms of Čech cocycles. Let
\xi
be a section of
H^{0}(R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})
. Represent it locally by
(1,1)
-forms
\xi_{i}
on
X_{i}
. The difference
\xi_{j}-\xi_{i}
is an exact form, hence

\xi_{j}-\xi_{i}=d\rho_{ij}

for some
1
-form
\rho_{ij}
on
X_{ij}
. The form
\rho_{ij}+\rho_{jk}+\rho_{ki}
is closed on
X_{ijk}
, hence defines a cocycle with coefficients in
R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}
. By
2.2.5
, there exists a unique vertical vector field
w_{ijk}\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}(U_{ijk})
on
X_{ijk}
such that
f_{0}(w_{ijk})
is equal to the class of the (0,1)-form
\rho^{0,1}_{ij}+\rho^{0,1}_{jk}+\rho^{0,1}_{ki}
under the
\bar{\partial}
-differential. The class of the cocycle
\{w_{ijk}\}
in
H^{2}(\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}})
is the image of
\xi
under the boundary map.

Proposition 6.3.6
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to B
be a Lagrangian fibration. Pick a class
\varphi\in\Sha
. Let

d_{2}^{\varphi}\colon H^{0}(R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})\to H^{2}(R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})\simeq H^{2}(\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}})

be the differential in the Leray spectral sequence for
\pi^{\varphi}\colon X^{\varphi}\to B
. Then for any
\xi\in H^{0}(R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})

d_{2}^{\varphi}(\xi)=d_{2}(\xi)+\lambda_{\xi}\overline{\varphi},

where
\overline{\varphi}
is the image of
\varphi\in\Sha
under the map
\Sha\to H^{2}(\Gamma)
and
\lambda_{\xi}
is as in (
23
).

Proof:
.

Represent
\xi\in H^{0}(R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})
by a collection of closed
(1,1)
-forms
\xi_{i}
on
X_{i}
. When we view
X_{i}
as an open subset of
X^{\varphi}
, we will denote the same forms by
\xi_{i}^{\varphi}
. The difference
\xi_{j}^{\varphi}-\xi_{i}^{\varphi}
is not the same as
\xi_{j}-\xi_{i}
because there is a twist by
\varphi_{ij}\in Aut^{0}_{X/B}(U_{ij})
involved. Namely

\xi_{j}^{\varphi}-\xi_{i}^{\varphi}=\varphi_{ij}^{*}\xi_{j}-\xi_{i}=(\varphi_{ij}^{*}\xi_{j}-\xi_{j})+(\xi_{j}-\xi_{i}).

As in
6.3.5
, write
\xi_{j}-\xi_{i}=d\rho_{ij}
. Find a vector field
v_{ij}
such that
\varphi_{ij}=\exp(v_{ij})
. Then

\varphi_{ij}^{*}\xi_{j}-\xi_{j}=\int\limits_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\exp(tv_{ij})^{*}\xi_{j}\right)dt=\int\limits_{0}^{1}\exp(tv_{ij})^{*}(\mathrm{L}_{v_{ij}}\xi_{j})dt=\\
=\int\limits_{0}^{1}\exp(tv_{ij})^{*}(d\iota_{v_{ij}}+\iota_{v_{ij}}d)\xi_{j}dt=d\int\limits_{0}^{1}\exp(tv_{ij})^{*}\iota_{v_{ij}}\xi_{j}dt.

Here
\mathrm{L}
denotes the Lie derivative. The second equality follows from the definition of the Lie derivative:

\mathrm{L}_{v}\xi=\left.\frac{d}{dt}(\exp(tv)^{*}\xi)\right|_{t=0}.

The third equality is the Cartan formula

\mathrm{L}=d\iota_{v}+\iota_{v}d,

and the last equality holds because
\xi_{j}
is closed. Set
\gamma_{ij}:=\int\limits_{0}^{1}\exp(tv_{ij})^{*}\iota_{v_{ij}}\xi_{j}dt
. Then

\xi_{j}^{\varphi}-\xi_{i}^{\varphi}=d(\rho_{ij}+\gamma_{ij}).

(24)

The form
\gamma_{ij}
is of type
(0,1)
and
\bar{\partial}
-closed. Its class under the
\bar{\partial}
-differential is

\int\limits_{0}^{1}[\exp(tv_{ij})^{*}\iota_{v_{ij}}\xi_{j}]dt=\int\limits_{0}^{1}[\iota_{v_{ij}}\xi_{j}]dt=[\iota_{v_{ij}}\xi_{j}]=f_{\xi}(v_{ij})=\lambda_{\xi}f_{0}(v_{ij}).

The third equality follows from the definition of
f_{\xi}
in
6.3.2
and the last equality from the definition of
\lambda_{\xi}
in (
23
). As in
6.3.5
, let
w_{ijk}
be the unique vector field in
\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}(U_{ijk})
such that
f_{0}(w_{ijk})=[\rho^{0,1}_{ij}+\rho^{0,1}_{jk}+\rho^{0,1}_{ki}]
. It follows from (
24
) that the class
d_{2}^{\varphi}(\xi)
can be represented by the cocycle

w_{ijk}+\lambda_{\xi}(v_{ij}+v_{jk}+v_{ki}).

Indeed,

f_{0}(w_{ijk}+\lambda_{\xi}(v_{ij}+v_{jk}+v_{ki}))=[\rho^{0,1}_{ij}+\gamma_{ij}+\rho^{0,1}_{jk}+\gamma_{jk}+\rho^{0,1}_{ki}+\gamma_{ki}].

By
6.3.5
, the class of the cocycle
w_{ijk}
in
H^{2}(\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}})
is
d_{2}(\xi)
and by
6.3.4
, the class of the cocycle
v_{ij}+v_{jk}+v_{ki}
in
H^{2}(\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}})
is
\overline{\varphi}
. The claim follows.

∎

Corollary 6.3.7
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to B
be a Lagrangian fibration. Then for any
\varphi\in\Sha^{\prime}
we have
d_{2}^{\varphi}=d_{2}
.

Proof:
.

For any
\varphi\in\Sha^{\prime}
, the class
\overline{\varphi}\in H^{2}(\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}})
vanishes by the definition of
\Sha^{\prime}
. Proposition
6.3.6
implies that
d_{2}^{\varphi}=d_{2}
.
∎

Corollary 6.3.8
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to B
be a Lagrangian fibration. Pick a class
\varphi\in\Sha
such that
\overline{\varphi}\in H^{2}(\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}})
does not vanish. Consider the restriction maps

r\colon H^{2}(X)\to H^{0}(R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}),\>\>\>\>\>r^{\varphi}\colon H^{2}(X^{\varphi})\to H^{0}(R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}).

Let
H^{2}(X)^{0}
(resp.
H^{2}(X^{\varphi})^{0})
denote the subspace of classes in
H^{2}(X)
(resp.
H^{2}(X^{\varphi}))
that restrict trivially to a smooth fiber. Then

\operatorname{im}r\cap\operatorname{im}r^{\varphi}=r(H^{2}(X)^{0})=r^{\varphi}(H^{2}(X^{\varphi})^{0}).

Proof:
.

The image of the restriction map
r
(resp.
r^{\varphi}
) coincides with the kernel of
d_{2}
(resp.
d_{2}^{\varphi}
). By Proposition
6.3.6
, a class
\xi
lies in the kernel of both
d_{2}
and
d_{2}^{\varphi}
if and only if
\lambda_{\xi}=0
, i.e., the restriction of
\xi
to a smooth fiber is trivial. The claim follows.
∎

Proposition 6.3.9
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to B
be a Lagrangian fibration on an irreducible hyperkähler manifold and
X^{\varphi}
its Shafarevich–Tate twist. Then either
b_{2}(X^{\varphi})=b_{2}(X)
or
b_{2}(X^{\varphi})=b_{2}(X)-1
. The first case occurs if and only if there is a class
h\in H^{2}(X)
whose restriction to a smooth fiber is non-trivial.

Proof:
.

As will be explained below, it follows from the Leray spectral sequence for
\mathbb{Q}_{X^{\varphi}}
that

b_{2}(X^{\varphi})=b_{2}(B)+\dim H^{1}(R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})+\operatorname{rk}r^{\varphi}.

(25)

Indeed,

h^{2}(X^{\varphi})=E_{\infty}^{2,0}+E_{\infty}^{1,1}+E_{\infty}^{0,2}.

The vector space
H^{0}(R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})
vanishes by Proposition
6.1.2
, hence the map
H^{2}(B)\to H^{2}(X^{\varphi})
is injective and
E_{\infty}^{2,0}=E_{2}^{2,0}=H^{2}(B,\mathbb{Q})
. Moreover, the second differential
d_{2}\colon H^{1}(R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})\to H^{3}(B,\mathbb{Q})
is zero because
H^{3}(B,\mathbb{Q})=0

[
34
]
. Thus
E_{\infty}^{1,1}=E_{2}^{1,1}=H^{1}(R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})
. Finally
E^{0,2}_{\infty}=\operatorname{im}r_{\varphi}
. The formula (
25
) follows. Applying this formula to
X^{\varphi}
and
X
, we obtain that

b_{2}(X^{\varphi})-b_{2}(X)=\operatorname{rk}r^{\varphi}-\operatorname{rk}r.

Since
X
is hyperkähler, the subspace
H^{2}(X)^{0}
has codimension
1
in
H^{2}(X)
, hence

\operatorname{rk}r=\dim r(H^{2}(X)^{0})+1=\dim r^{\varphi}(H^{2}(X^{\varphi})^{0})+1,

where the last equality holds by Corollary
6.3.7
if
\overline{\varphi}=0
and by Corollary
6.3.8
if
\overline{\varphi}\neq 0
. If there is a class in
H^{2}(X^{\varphi})
restricting non-trivially to a smooth fiber, then
H^{2}(X^{\varphi})^{0}
is a hyperplane in
H^{2}(X^{\varphi})
(Proposition
6.0.3
) and

\operatorname{rk}r^{\varphi}=\dim r^{\varphi}(H^{2}(X^{\varphi})^{0})+1=\operatorname{rk}r.

In this case,
b_{2}(X)=b_{2}(X^{\varphi})
. Otherwise,
H^{2}(X^{\varphi})^{0}=H^{2}(X^{\varphi})
, hence

\operatorname{rk}r^{\varphi}=\dim r^{\varphi}(H^{2}(X^{\varphi})^{0})=\operatorname{rk}r-1,

and

b_{2}(X)=b_{2}(X^{\varphi})+1.

∎

Proposition 6.3.10
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to B
be a Lagrangian fibration on an irreducible hyperkähler manifold and
X^{\varphi}
its Shafarevich–Tate twist. Then there is a class
h\in H^{2}(X^{\varphi})
that restricts non-trivially to a smooth fiber if and only if
\overline{\varphi}\in H^{2}(\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}})
is in the image of the boundary map
d_{2}\colon H^{0}(R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})\to H^{2}(R^{1}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})\simeq H^{2}(\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Proof:
.

Suppose that there is a class
h\in H^{2}(X^{\varphi})
that restricts non-trivially to a smooth fiber. Let
\overline{h}
be its image in
H^{0}(B,R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})
. By Proposition
6.3.6
,

0=d_{2}^{\varphi}(\overline{h})=d_{2}(\overline{h})+\lambda_{h}\overline{\varphi}.

Therefore,
\overline{\varphi}=-d_{2}(\overline{h})/\lambda_{h}
is in the image of
d_{2}
.

Conversely, suppose that
\overline{\varphi}
is in the image of
d_{2}
, i.e., there is a class
\xi\in H^{0}(R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})
such that
d_{2}\xi=\overline{\varphi}
. Let
h_{0}
be a Kähler class on
X
with
\lambda_{h_{0}}=1
and
\overline{h_{0}}
its image in
H^{0}(R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q})
. Consider the class

\xi^{\prime}:=(1+\lambda_{\xi})\overline{h_{0}}-\xi.

Then
\lambda_{\xi^{\prime}}=1
and

d_{2}^{\varphi}(\xi^{\prime})=d_{2}(\xi^{\prime})+\overline{\varphi}=(1+\lambda_{\xi})d_{2}(\overline{h_{0}})-d_{2}(\xi)+\overline{\varphi}=-d_{2}(\xi)+\overline{\varphi}=0.

Here the first equality holds by Proposition
6.3.6
. Therefore,
\xi^{\prime}
lifts to a class in
H^{2}(X^{\varphi})
which restricts non-trivially to smooth fibers.
∎

6.3.11. Proof of Theorem
D
.

Immediately follows from Propositions
6.3.9
and
6.3.10
.
∎

6.3.12. Proof of Theorem
E
.

Suppose
X^{\varphi}
is of Fujiki class
\mathcal{C}
, i.e., there is a rational map
f\colon X^{\varphi}\dashrightarrow Y
to a Kähler manifold
Y
. Let
h\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
be the pullback of a Kähler form on
Y
. The restriction of
f
to a general fiber
F
of
\pi^{\varphi}
is birational onto its image, hence
h{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{F}}\right.}
is non-trivial. By Theorem
D
, the twist satisfies
\overline{\varphi}\in\operatorname{im}d_{2}
.
∎

A family of weighted Erdős-Mordell inequality and applications
Tran Quang Hung

Abstract
. We establish some new generalizations of Erdős-Mordell inequality by adding weights to its terms. Using these generalizations, we derived strengthened versions of the original Erdős-Mordell inequality. We also found two other variations of Erdős-Mordell inequality to be generalizable in the same way.

1.
Introduction

Throughout this article we denote the distance between two points
X
and
Y

XY
. Let

be an interior point of the triangle

PA+PB+PC\geq 2(d_{a}+d_{b}+d_{c}),

where

, and

are the distances from

This is the well-known and elegant Erdős–Mordell inequality. It was named after Erdős, who conjectured it in 1935

In this article, following the ideas of generalizing Erdős–Mordell inequality using weights from Dar and Gueron
[
3
]

[

, we developed a new generalization using a different type of weight. As a result, we discovered new weighted generalizations together with strengthened versions of the original Erdős–Mordell inequality. In addition, the new weighting method is able to generalize two other variations of the original Erdős–Mordell inequality. The first one is Barrow’s inequality

PA+PB+PC\geq 2(l_{a}+l_{b}+l_{c}),

where
l_{a}
,
l_{b}
, and

are the lengths of the bisectors of
\angle BPC
,
\angle CPA
, and
\angle APB
, respectively, and equality holds if and only if triangle

is equilateral and

is its center. The second variation is from Dao, Nguyen, and Pham
[

R_{A}+R_{B}+R_{C}\geq 2(d_{a}+d_{b}+d_{c}),

where

,

, and

We present the new weighted generalizations with proofs in Section 2 and the resulting some applications and strengthened inequalities will be given in Section 3.

2.
Main theorems and proofs

We begin with a new weighted version of Erdős-Mordell inequality:

Theorem 1

(A new weighted version of Erdős-Mordell inequality)
.

Let
x
,
y
,
z
,
u
,
v
, and
w
be positive real numbers such that
xyz=uvw=1
. Let
P
be an interior point of the triangle
ABC
. Denote by
d_{a}
,
d_{b}
, and
d_{c}
the distances of
P
from the sides
AB
,
BC
, and
CA
, respectively. Then

(1)

x(PA+u^{3}d_{a})+y(PB+v^{3}d_{b})+z(PC+w^{3}d_{c})\geq 3(ud_{a}+vd_{b}+wd_{c}).

Equality holds if and only if
x=y=z=u=v=w=1
, triangle
ABC
is equilateral and
P
is its center.

Proof.

With standard notations
BC=a
,
CA=b
, and
AB=c
, we recall a result in
[
3
]
:

(2)

PA\geq\frac{b\cdot d_{c}+c\cdot d_{b}}{a}.

Equality in (
2
) holds if and only if
P
lies on the line connecting
A
to the circumcenter of
ABC
.
Since
x
is a positive real number,

(3)

x(PA+u^{3}d_{a})\geq x\left(\frac{b\cdot d_{c}+c\cdot d_{b}}{a}+u^{3}d_{a}\right).

Similarly, with positive real numbers
y
and
z
, we get

(4)

y(PB+v^{3}d_{b})\geq y\left(\frac{c\cdot d_{a}+a\cdot d_{c}}{b}+v^{3}d_{b}\right)

and

(5)

z(PC+w^{3}d_{c})\geq z\left(\frac{a\cdot d_{b}+b\cdot d_{a}}{c}+w^{3}d_{c}\right)

with analogous conditions for equality. Now summing the inequalities (
3
), (
4
), and (
5
), we obtain

(6)

x(PA+u^{3}d_{a})+y(PB+v^{3}d_{b})+z(PC+w^{3}d_{c})\geq\left(y\cdot\frac{c}{b}+z\cdot\frac{b}{c}+xu^{3}\right)d_{a}+\\
+\left(z\cdot\frac{b}{a}+x\cdot\frac{c}{a}+yv^{3}\right)d_{b}+\left(x\cdot\frac{a}{b}+y\cdot\frac{a}{b}+zw^{3}\right)d_{c}.

and equality holds if and only if
P
is circumcenter of
\triangle ABC
.

Using the arithmetic mean-geometric mean inequality for three positive numbers, with notice that
xyz=1
, we have

(7)

y\cdot\frac{c}{b}+z\cdot\frac{b}{c}+xu^{3}\geq 3\sqrt[3]{y\cdot\frac{c}{b}\cdot z\cdot\frac{b}{c}\cdot xu^{3}}=3u.

with equality holds if and only if

(8)

y\cdot\frac{c}{b}=z\cdot\frac{b}{c}=xu^{3},

which is equivalent to

(9)

u^{3}\cdot\frac{x}{y}=\frac{c}{b}\quad\text{and}\quad u^{3}\cdot\frac{x}{z}=\frac{b}{c}.

Similarly,

(10)

z\cdot\frac{b}{a}+x\cdot\frac{c}{a}+yv^{3}\geq 3v,

and

(11)

x\cdot\frac{a}{b}+y\cdot\frac{a}{b}+zw^{3}\geq 3w

Since
uvw=1
, the equalities hold if and only if

(12)

x=y=z=u=v=w=1\quad\text{and}\quad a=b=c.

Finally, combining (
6
), (
7
), (
10
), and (
11
), we get

(13)

x(PA+u^{3}d_{a})+y(PB+v^{3}d_{b})+z(PC+w^{3}d_{c})\geq 3(ud_{a}+vd_{b}+wd_{c})

with equality holds if and only if

(14)

x=y=z=u=v=w=1\quad\text{and}\quad a=b=c.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1
.
∎

Remark
.

In Theorem
1
, where
u=v=w=1
, we obtain

(15)

x(PA+d_{a})+y(PB+d_{b})+z(PC+d_{c})\geq 3(d_{a}+d_{b}+d_{c}).

In Theorem
1
, where
x=y=z=1
, we obtain

(16)

PA+PB+PC\geq(3u-u^{3})d_{a}+(3v-v^{3})d_{b}+(3w-w^{3})d_{c}.

Theses inequalities can be considered as other weighted versions of Erdős-Mordell inequality.

Similarly, we get a new weighted Dao-Nguyen-Pham’s inequality in

Theorem 2

(A new weighted version of Dao-Nguyen-Pham’s inequality)
.

Let
x
,
y
,
z
,
u
,
v
, and
w
be positive real numbers such that
xyz=uvw=1
. Let
P
be an interior point of the triangle
ABC
with circumcircle
(\omega)
. Denote by
R_{A}
,
R_{B}
, and
R_{C}
the distances of
P
from the tangents to
(\omega)
at
A
,
B
,
C
respectively,
d_{a}
,
d_{b}
, and
d_{c}
the distances of
P
from the sides
AB
,
BC
, and
CA
, respectively. Then

(17)

x(R_{A}+u^{3}d_{a})+y(R_{B}+v^{3}d_{b})+z(R_{C}+w^{3}d_{c})\geq 3(ud_{a}+vd_{b}+wd_{c}).

Equality holds if and only if
x=y=z=u=v=w=1
, triangle
ABC
is equilateral and
P
is its center.

Proof.

With standard notations
BC=a
,
CA=b
, and
AB=c
, we recall a result of the second proof in
[
17
]
:

(18)

R_{A}=\frac{b\cdot d_{c}+c\cdot d_{b}}{a}.

Since
x
is a positive real number,

(19)

x(R_{A}+u^{3}d_{a})=x\left(\frac{b\cdot d_{c}+c\cdot d_{b}}{a}+u^{3}d_{a}\right).

Similarly, with positive real numbers
y
and
z
, we get

(20)

y(R_{B}+v^{3}d_{b})=y\left(\frac{c\cdot d_{a}+a\cdot d_{c}}{b}+v^{3}d_{b}\right)

and

(21)

z(R_{C}+w^{3}d_{c})=z\left(\frac{a\cdot d_{b}+b\cdot d_{a}}{c}+w^{3}d_{c}\right)

Now summing the inequalities (
19
), (
20
), and (
21
), we obtain the identity

(22)

x(R_{A}+u^{3}d_{a})+y(R_{B}+v^{3}d_{b})+z(R_{C}+w^{3}d_{c})=\left(y\cdot\frac{c}{b}+z\cdot\frac{b}{c}+xu^{3}\right)d_{a}+\\
+\left(z\cdot\frac{b}{a}+x\cdot\frac{c}{a}+yv^{3}\right)d_{b}+\left(x\cdot\frac{a}{b}+y\cdot\frac{a}{b}+zw^{3}\right)d_{c}.

Using the arithmetic mean-geometric mean inequality for three positive numbers, with notice that
xyz=1
, we have

(23)

y\cdot\frac{c}{b}+z\cdot\frac{b}{c}+xu^{3}\geq 3\sqrt[3]{y\cdot\frac{c}{b}\cdot z\cdot\frac{b}{c}\cdot xu^{3}}=3u.

with equality holds if and only if

(24)

y\cdot\frac{c}{b}=z\cdot\frac{b}{c}=xu^{3},

which is equivalent to

(25)

u^{3}\cdot\frac{x}{y}=\frac{c}{b}\quad\text{and}\quad u^{3}\cdot\frac{x}{z}=\frac{b}{c}.

Similarly,

(26)

z\cdot\frac{b}{a}+x\cdot\frac{c}{a}+yv^{3}\geq 3v,

and

(27)

x\cdot\frac{a}{b}+y\cdot\frac{a}{b}+zw^{3}\geq 3w

Since
uvw=1
, the equalities hold if and only if

(28)

x=y=z=u=v=w=1\quad\text{and}\quad a=b=c.

Finally, combining (
22
), (
23
), (
26
), and (
27
), we get

(29)

x(R_{A}+u^{3}d_{a})+y(R_{B}+v^{3}d_{b})+z(R_{C}+w^{3}d_{c})\geq 3(ud_{a}+vd_{b}+wd_{c})

with equality holds if and only if

(30)

x=y=z=u=v=w=1\quad\text{and}\quad a=b=c.

This completes the proof of Theorem
2
.
∎

Remark
.

Since
R_{A}\leq PA
,
R_{b}\leq PB
, and
R_{c}\leq PC
, Theorem
2
can be considered as a strengthened version of Theorem
1
. In Theorem
2
, let
u=v=w=1
or
x=y=z=1
, we obtain two weighted inequalities

(31)

x(R_{A}+d_{a})+y(R_{B}+d_{b})+z(R_{C}+d_{c})\geq 3(d_{a}+d_{b}+d_{c}),

and

(32)

R_{A}+R_{B}+R_{C}\geq(3u-u^{3})d_{a}+(3v-v^{3})d_{b}+(3w-w^{3})d_{c}.

They are considered as other weighted versions of Dao-Nguyen-Pham’s inequality.

By the same way, we have a generalization for Barrow’s inequality. Similar to Barow’s proof, we will use Wolstenholme’s inequality
[
4
]
to prove this generalization.

Theorem 3

(A new weighted version of Barrow’s inequality)
.

Let
x
,
y
,
z
,
u
,
v
, and
w
be positive real numbers such that
xyz=uvw=1
. Let
P
be an interior point of the triangle
ABC
. Denote by
l_{a}
,
l_{b}
, and
l_{c}
the lengths of the bisectors of
\angle BPC
,
\angle CPA
, and
\angle APB
, respectively. Then

(33)

x(PA+u^{3}l_{a})+y(PB+v^{3}l_{b})+z(PC+w^{3}l_{c})\geq(3ul_{a}+vl_{b}+wl_{c}).

Equality holds if and only if
x=y=z=u=v=w=1
, triangle
ABC
is equilateral and
P
is its center.

Proof.

Let
\angle BPC=\alpha
,
\angle CPA=\beta
, angle
APB=\gamma
, using formula of bisector, we have

(34)

l_{a}=\frac{PB\cdot PC\cos\frac{\alpha}{2}}{PB+PC},

this is equivalent to

(35)

2\sqrt{PB\cdot PC}\cos\frac{\alpha}{2}=\left(\sqrt{\frac{PB}{PC}}+\sqrt{\frac{PC}{PB}}\right)l_{a}.

Multiplying both sides of (
35
) with
\sqrt{yz}
, we have

(36)

2\sqrt{yPB\cdot zPC}\cos\frac{\alpha}{2}=\sqrt{yz}\left(\sqrt{\frac{PB}{PC}}+\sqrt{\frac{PC}{PB}}\right)l_{a},

and similarly,

(37)

2\sqrt{zPC\cdot xPA}\cos\frac{\beta}{2}=\sqrt{zx}\left(\sqrt{\frac{PC}{PA}}+\sqrt{\frac{PA}{PC}}\right)l_{b},

(38)

2\sqrt{xPA\cdot yPB}\cos\frac{\gamma}{2}=\sqrt{xy}\left(\sqrt{\frac{PA}{PB}}+\sqrt{\frac{PB}{PA}}\right)l_{c}.

Since
\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}=\pi
, using Wolstenholme’s inequality
[
4
]
, we get

(39)

2\sqrt{PB\cdot PC}\cos\frac{\alpha}{2}+2\sqrt{zPC\cdot xPA}\cos\frac{\beta}{2}+\\
+2\sqrt{xPA\cdot yPB}\cos\frac{\gamma}{2}\leq xPA+yPB+zPC.

From (
36
), (
37
), (
38
), and (
39
), we obtain

(40)

xPA+yPB+zPC\geq\sqrt{yz}\left(\sqrt{\frac{PB}{PC}}+\sqrt{\frac{PC}{PB}}\right)l_{a}+\\
+\sqrt{zx}\left(\sqrt{\frac{PC}{PA}}+\sqrt{\frac{PA}{PC}}\right)l_{b}+\sqrt{xy}\left(\sqrt{\frac{PA}{PB}}+\sqrt{\frac{PB}{PA}}\right)l_{c}.

Adding two sides of (
40
) with
xu^{3}l_{a}+yv^{3}l_{b}+zw^{3}l_{c}
, we get

(41)

x(PA+u^{3}l_{a})+y(PB+v^{3}l_{b})+z(PC+w^{3}l_{c})\geq\\
\left(\sqrt{yz}\sqrt{\frac{PB}{PC}}+\sqrt{yz}\sqrt{\frac{PC}{PB}}+xu^{3}\right)l_{a}+\left(\sqrt{zx}\sqrt{\frac{PC}{PA}}+\sqrt{zx}\sqrt{\frac{PA}{PC}}+yv^{3}\right)l_{b}+\\
+\left(\sqrt{xy}\sqrt{\frac{PA}{PB}}+\sqrt{xy}\sqrt{\frac{PB}{PA}}+zw^{3}\right)l_{c}.

Since
xyz=1
, using arithmetic mean-geometric mean inequality for three positive numbers, we have

(42)

\sqrt{yz}\sqrt{\frac{PB}{PC}}+\sqrt{yz}\sqrt{\frac{PC}{PB}}+xu^{3}\geq 3\sqrt[3]{\sqrt{yz}\sqrt{\frac{PB}{PC}}\cdot\sqrt{yz}\sqrt{\frac{PC}{PB}}\cdot xu^{3}}=3u,

and similarly,

(43)

\sqrt{zx}\sqrt{\frac{PC}{PA}}+\sqrt{zx}\sqrt{\frac{PA}{PC}}+y\geq 3v,

(44)

\sqrt{xy}\sqrt{\frac{PA}{PB}}+\sqrt{xy}\sqrt{\frac{PB}{PA}}+z\geq 3w.

Combining (
41
), (
42
), (
43
), and (
44
), we have

(45)

x(PA+u^{3}l_{a})+y(PB+v^{3}l_{b})+z(PC+w^{3}l_{c})\geq 3(ul_{a}+vl_{b}+wl_{c}),

with equality holds if and only if

(46)

x=y=z=u=v=w=1,

triangle
ABC
is equilateral and
P
is its center.
This completes the proof of Theorem
3
.
∎

Remark
.

Similarly, we have two particular cases as new weighted versions of Barrow’s inequality. In Theorem
3
, let
u=v=w=1
then

(47)

x(PA+l_{a})+y(PB+l_{b})+z(PC+l_{c})\geq 3(l_{a}+l_{b}+l_{c}),

let
x=y=z=1
then

(48)

PA+PB+PC\geq(3u-u^{3})l_{a}+(3v-v^{3})l_{b}+(3w-w^{3})l_{c}.

Theorem 4
.

Let
u
,
v
, and
w
be positive real numbers such that
uvw=1
. Let
P
be an interior point of the triangle
ABC
. Denote by

,

, and
d_{c}
the distances of

,

, and

u=v=w=1

, let

(50)

It is easily seen that

(PA+u^{3}d_{a})(PB+v^{3}d_{b})(PC+w^{3}d_{c})\geq(ud_{a}+vd_{b}+wd_{c})^{3}.

Equality holds if and only if
u=v=w=1

Theorem 8

(Another strengthened version of Barrow’s inequality)
.

Let
P
be an interior point of the triangle
ABC
. Denote by
l_{a}
,
l_{b}
, and
l_{c}
the lengths of the bisectors of
\angle BPC
,
\angle CPA
, and
\angle APB
, respectively. Then

(64)

\displaystyle\frac{PA+PB+PC}{2}

(65)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{PA^{2}(PB+PC)^{2}+PB^{2}(PC+PA)^{2}+PC^{2}(PA+PB)^{2}}{(PB+PC)(PC+PA)(PA+PB)}

(66)

\displaystyle\geq l_{a}+l_{b}+l_{c}.

Equality at (
65
) holds if and only if
PA=PB=PC
; equality at (
66
) holds if and only if triangle
ABC
is equilateral and
P
is its center.

Proof.

Let
PA=p
,
PB=q
, and
PC=r
then the first inequality comes from the following algebraic identity and inequality

(67)

(p+q+r)(p+q)(q+r)(r+p)-2p^{2}(q+r)^{2}-2q^{2}(r+p)^{2}-\\
-2r^{2}(p+q)^{2}=pq(p-q)^{2}+qr(q-r)^{2}+rp(r-p)^{2}\geq 0.

Equality at (
67
) holds if and only if
p=q=r
or
PA=PB=PC
.

For the second inequality, from Theorem
7

Factorization for J / ψ J/\psi leptoproduction at small transverse momentum
Miguel G. Echevarria, Samuel F. Romera, and Pieter Taels

Abstract
Nonrelativistic Quantum Chromodynamics (NRQCD) breaks down in the region of low transverse momentum, where the transverse momentum of the produced quarkonium state is sensitive to multiple scattering with the incoming hadron and to soft gluon radiation. In this kinematic regime, the transverse-momentum-dependent (TMD) factorization framework is required, promoting the long-distance matrix elements (LDMEs) of NRQCD to the so-called TMD shape functions (TMDShFs), which encode both the soft gluon radiation and the formation of the heavy-quark bound state. In this work, we apply an effective-field theory approach (combining NRQCD and SCET) to the photon-gluon fusion process in inclusive J / ψ J/\psi leptoproduction. We derive a factorization theorem for the cross section in terms of TMDShFs, compute these quantities at next-to-leading order, establish their evolution, and study their matching onto the corresponding LDMEs in the high-transverse-momentum region. Our results are particularly relevant to the Electron-Ion Collider, where J / ψ J/\psi leptoproduction can be used to probe gluon transverse-momentum-dependent parton distribution functions (gluon TMDPDFs).

In this work, we limit ourselves to a study of TMD factorization at leading power.
In the SIDIS process under consideration, this also implies that only the photon-gluon fusion channel plays a role, where the heavy-quark pair is produced in a color-octet configuration.
Any other channel, be it with an incoming quark instead of a gluon, or with the heavy quarks being in a color-singlet state, would require an extra hard emission in the final state to carry away the remaining color charge.
However, any such emission not only adds an additional power in the strong coupling, but also in the power counting parameter
\lambda\sim P_{\psi T}/Q_{H}

3.3
Matching onto LDMEs

In the limit of large transverse momentum, TMDShFs can be matched onto LDMEs, similar to how TMDPDFs can be matched onto collinear PDFs. This is done by performing operator product expansion (OPE) and then extracting the transverse dependence in terms of the Wilson coefficients:

\displaystyle S_{[n]\to J/\psi}(b_{T};\mu,\zeta_{B})=\sum_{[m]}C^{[n]}_{[m]}(b_{T};\mu,\zeta_{B})\times\frac{\left<\mathcal{O}^{[m]}\right>}{N^{(J)}_{pol}}+\mathcal{O}(b_{T}\Lambda_{\text{QCD}})\;,

[m]=\,^{2S+1}L_{J}^{[col.]}

.
Note that

.
We simplify the notation by naming the

-state TMDShF as
[n]=S
and the

-states TMDShF as

Doing this, we have, e.g., that

TMDShF onto

Following this notation and getting back to the LDMEs in eq. (

\displaystyle C^{S}_{{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}}(b_{T};\mu,\zeta_{B})

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{A}}{2\pi}L_{T}\left(1-\hbox{ln}\,\zeta_{B}\right)\;,

(73)

\displaystyle C^{S}_{{}^{1}P_{1}^{[1]}}(b_{T};\mu)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\frac{8\,C_{F}}{3m_{c}^{2}}L_{T}\;,

3.4
Cross-check

We would like to conclude this section by discussing two points regarding the results obtained above.
Firstly, we match the QCD cross section onto the SCET one at NLO.
As mentioned in
section 2
, the total cross section is the sum of all the cross sections for each channel, resulting in four hard functions, one for each channel.
Since the hard function originates from the virtual contribution, we can refer to the result for photoproduction in:
[
84
]

\displaystyle\sigma^{(b)}_{[n]}

\displaystyle=\delta(1-x)(\sigma_{[n]}^{0(b)}+\sigma_{[n]}^{V(b)})\;,

(75)

\displaystyle\sigma_{[n]}^{V(b)}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{[n]}^{0(b)}\frac{\alpha^{(b)}_{s}}{2\pi}f_{\varepsilon}(Q_{H}^{2})

\displaystyle\times\left[\frac{\beta_{0}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\rm{UV}}}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\rm{IR}}}\right)+\left(C_{F}-\frac{C_{A}}{2}\right)\frac{\pi^{2}}{\text{v}}-C_{A}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\rm{IR}}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\rm{IR}}}\right)+\mathcal{D}_{[n]}\right]\;,

with
Q_{H}
the hard scale where
Q_{H}=M_{\psi}
for photoproduction and
Q_{H}=f(Q,M_{\psi})
for leptoproduction.
In the above equation, the superscript
(b)
explicitly indicates that this result should be renormalized,
\sigma^{0(b)}_{[n]}
is the Born cross section for the channel
[n]
,
\mathcal{D}_{[n]}
are finite terms for each channel, and

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}(Q_{H}^{2})\left.\right|_{\overline{\text{MS}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\mu^{2}e^{\gamma_{E}}}{Q_{H}^{2}}\right)^{\varepsilon}\Gamma(1+\varepsilon)=1+\varepsilon\,\hbox{ln}\frac{\mu^{2}}{Q_{H}^{2}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{12}\left(\pi^{2}+\hbox{ln}^{2}\frac{\mu^{2}}{Q_{H}^{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3})\;.

(76)

Since the Born cross section depends on
\alpha^{(b)}_{s}(\mu)
, we renormalize the cross section by using the renormalization of the QCD strong coupling.
By doing this, we remove the UV divergence.
Then we insert the expanded
f_{\varepsilon}(Q_{H}^{2})
in
\sigma^{V}_{[n]}
, obtaining the following renormalized virtual contribution to the cross section for the channel
[n]

\displaystyle\sigma^{V}_{[n]}

\displaystyle=\sigma^{0}_{[n]}\left[1+\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\left\{\left[\left(C_{F}-C_{A}/2\right)\frac{\pi^{2}}{\text{v}}-\frac{C_{A}}{\varepsilon_{\rm{IR}}}\right]-\left[\frac{C_{A}}{\varepsilon_{\rm{IR}}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\rm{IR}}}\left(\frac{\beta_{0}}{2}+C_{A}\,\hbox{ln}\frac{\mu^{2}}{Q_{H}^{2}}\right)\right]\right.\right.

(77)

\displaystyle\left.\left.-C_{A}\left(\frac{\beta_{0}}{2C_{A}}\,\hbox{ln}\frac{\mu^{2}}{Q_{H}^{2}}+\hbox{ln}\frac{\mu^{2}}{Q_{H}^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\,\hbox{ln}^{2}\frac{\mu^{2}}{Q_{H}^{2}}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}\right)+\mathcal{D}_{[n]}\right\}\right]\;.

Here

Although this cross section is not specifically for leptoproduction, its UV and IR behavior is the same, allowing us to use the first line of eq. (

In fact, the first square of the first line corresponds to the virtual contribution of the TMDShF, as described in eq. (

The remainder of the result will be determined by the hard function; however, the NLO calculation of the cross section for leptoproduction has not yet been completed.
We will present the results in the future, accompanied by a phenomenological analysis.

In addition to the previous discussion, we provide an analysis on the anomalous dimensions.
We calculate the anomalous dimension of the Hard function,
\gamma_{H}

\displaystyle\gamma_{H}\equiv\frac{1}{H}\frac{d\,H}{d\,\hbox{ln}\mu}=-\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)C_{A}}{\pi}\left(1+\hbox{ln}\frac{\mu^{2}}{Q_{H}^{2}}\right)\;.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma}\frac{d\,\sigma}{d\,\hbox{ln}\mu}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{0}}\frac{d\,\sigma_{0}}{d\,\hbox{ln}\mu}

4
Conclusions

In this work, we have studied
J/\psi
production at small transverse momentum
P_{\psi\perp}\!\ll\!Q_{H}
in SIDIS. To do so, we have used an effective field theory approach which combines vNRQCD with SCET. At leading power in
\lambda\equiv P_{\psi\perp}/Q_{H}
, the
J/\psi
is produced in gluon-photon fusion through the intermediate color-octet states
{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}
,
{}^{3}S_{1}^{[8]}
,
{}^{3}P_{J\!=\!0,1,2}^{[8]}
. We have provided a precise definition of the relevant vNRQCD+SCET operators and matched the corresponding coefficients onto the tree-level QCD calculation. At the leading power in
\lambda
and to all orders in
\alpha_{s}
, the
{}^{3}S_{1}^{[8]}
contributions are observed to vanish, as was earlier observed in refs.
[
5
,
7
]
.

The main result of our paper is the first leading-power factorization theorem for this process in terms of the gluon TMDPDF as well as different TMDShFs. Every color-octet state has a corresponding TMDShF which parameterizes the hadronization of the bound state into the outgoing
J/\psi
at low transverse momentum. We have calculated the TMDShFs and their collinear counterparts, the LDMEs, at next-to-leading order in perturbation theory. Moreover, we have obtained the Wilson coefficients to match the TMDShFs onto the LDMEs in the limit of large transverse momentum. Finally, we have established the evolution equations for the TMDShFs from a renormalization-group analysis. Intermediate results of our study, namely the LDMEs at NLO, agree with earlier results in the literature, see
[
84
]
.

At the scales under consideration, we expect the color-singlet contribution to be relevant as well. Indeed, although suppressed in powers of
\alpha_{s}
and
\lambda
, the color singlet is enhanced in the NRQCD parameter
v
with respect to the color-octet states. We plan to address this channel in future work.

Appendix A
vNRQCD

In this section we establish the formalism we have used in the LDME and TMDShF calculations.
In vNRQCD
[
87
,
64
]
there are two hard scales: the heavy quark mass,
m
, and the hard scale of the process,
Q_{H}
. In the low energy theory there are two scales: the heavy quark momentum,
m\text{v}
, and the kinetic energy of the heavy quark,
m\text{v}^{2}
, inside quarkonium. At the matching scale
m
we integrate out the hard modes with momentum
p^{\mu}\sim m
and the off-shell ones including gluons with energy
p^{0}\sim m\text{v}
and momentum
\mathbf{p}\sim m\text{v}^{2}
.

By using the time-like vector
v^{\mu}
such that
v^{2}=1
, we can split the four-momentum of the heavy quark
Q
into

\displaystyle p^{\mu}_{Q}=mv^{\mu}+k^{\mu}\;,

(83)

where
k^{0}
is the kinetic energy and
\mathbf{k}
is the three-momenta of the heavy quark. We consider the heavy quark is on-shell, so
p_{Q}^{2}=m^{2}
.
In the nonrelativistic limit, the three-momentum
\mathbf{k}
is small compared to the mass, so
\mathbf{k}\sim m\text{v}
and the only solution for the on-shell equation is for
k^{0}
to be the kinetic energy,
k^{0}\sim m\text{v}^{2}
, so we set the
k
-scaling from the on-shell condition:

m^{2}+2mk^{0}+(k^{0})^{2}-\mathbf{k}^{2}=m^{2}\quad\mathrm{such\;that}\quad k\sim m(\text{v}^{2},\text{v},\text{v},\text{v})\;.

(84)

To distinguish the soft and ultrasoft modes in the
k^{\mu}
definition we use the label-momentum formalism, in which we split the
k^{\mu}
momentum into a large label (soft) part,
\ell^{\mu}
, and a small residual (ultrasoft) part,
r^{\mu}
:

k^{\mu}=\ell^{\mu}+r^{\mu}\quad\mathrm{with}\quad\ell^{\mu}\sim m(0,\text{v},\text{v},\text{v})\quad\mathrm{and}\quad r^{\mu}\sim m(\text{v}^{2},\text{v}^{2},\text{v}^{2},\text{v}^{2})\;.

(85)

Therefore, denoting the soft gluons and massless quark fields at the scale
m\text{v}
as
A_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}^{\mu}
and
\psi_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
, and denoting the ultrasoft gluons and massless quark fields at the scale
m\text{v}^{2}
as
A_{us}^{\mu}
and
\psi_{us}
, the vNRQCD Lagrangian is constructed:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{vNRQCD}}=\mathcal{L}_{p}+\mathcal{L}_{s}+\mathcal{L}_{us}\;.

(86)

The potential vNRQCD Lagrangian,
\mathcal{L}_{p}
, describes the interaction between heavy (anti) quark fields
\psi_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
resp.
\chi_{-\boldsymbol{\ell}}
and ultrasoft gluon fields
A_{us}^{\mu}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\psi^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\left\{iD^{0}-\frac{(\mathbf{P}-i\mathbf{D})^{2}}{2m}\right\}\psi_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}+(\psi\rightarrow\chi,\,T\to\bar{T})

(87)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\boldsymbol{\ell},\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}}\frac{4\pi\alpha_{s}}{(\boldsymbol{\ell}-\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime})^{2}}\,\psi_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}^{\dagger}T^{a}\psi_{\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}}\,\chi_{-\boldsymbol{\ell}}^{\dagger}\bar{T}^{a}\chi_{-\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}}+\mathcal{O}(\text{v}^{6})\;.

In the above equation, the covariant derivative is defined as
iD^{\mu}=i\partial^{\mu}-gA_{us}^{\mu}
.
14
14

14

Note that this definition is not consistent with the one provided in section 2. In the current section, we use the definition of the covariant derivative so that the vNRQCD framework is in accordance with the previous works.
The full derivative operator has been decomposed as follows:
i\partial_{\mu}\rightarrow\mathbf{P}+i\partial_{\mu}
with the label operator
\mathbf{P}
acting on the label momentum as
\mathbf{P}\,\psi_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}=\boldsymbol{\ell}\,\psi_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
. The potential heavy-quark fields scale as
\psi\sim\chi\sim\text{v}^{3/2}
and the covariant derivative as
D^{\mu}\sim\text{v}^{2}
. Thus the potential vNRQCD Lagrangian scales as
\mathcal{L}_{p}\sim\text{v}^{5}+\alpha_{s}\text{v}^{4}+\mathcal{O}(\text{v}^{6})
.

The soft vNRQCD Lagrangian,
\mathcal{L}_{s}
, involves a pure soft fields part and interaction terms involving both potential heavy quarks,
\psi_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
, and soft fields. At leading power in v, the only term in the interaction Lagrangian is the following:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\text{int}}_{s}=-4\pi\alpha_{s}\sum_{q,q^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\ell},\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{q^{0}}\,\psi^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\left[A^{0}_{q},A^{0}_{q^{\prime}}\right]\psi_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}+(\psi\rightarrow\chi,\,T\to\bar{T})+\ldots\;.

(88)

As we can see, at this order there is not interaction between heavy (anti)quarks and a single soft gluon. Moreover, the double soft gluon emission described by eq. (
88
) is not relevant for the calculation at NLO, so in the NLO calculation of the LDME and TMDShF in
appendix C
, we will only have interactions between the quarks and the usoft gluons.

The ultrasoft piece of the vNRQCD Lagrangian,
\mathcal{L}_{us}
, involves only pure ultrasoft field terms.
Note that the ultrasoft field strength scales as
G^{\mu\nu}_{us}\sim\text{v}^{4}
. The leading dimension term would be
\psi^{\dagger}_{us}\,iD\!\!\!\!/\penalty\,\psi_{us}\sim\text{v}^{5}
and the next-to-leading contribution
G^{\mu\nu}_{us}G_{us,\mu\nu}\sim\text{v}^{8}
.

The first term in the potential vNRQCD Lagrangian, see (
87
) and denoted as
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{(1)}
for the following, is the only one in which the ultrasoft gluon couples to the heavy quarks at leading order in
\alpha_{s}
. However, the ultrasoft gluon can be decoupled from the heavy quarks using a BPS field redefinition:

\psi_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}(x)\rightarrow\,Y_{u}(x)\psi_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}(x)\quad\mathrm{with}\quad Y_{u}(x)=P\exp\left(-ig\int_{-\infty}^{0}dtA_{us}^{0}(x+t,\mathbf{x})\right)\;.

(89)

The term
(\mathbf{P}-i\mathbf{D})^{2}
in
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{(1)}
can be expanded into
\mathbf{P}^{2}-\mathbf{D}^{2}-i2\mathbf{D}\cdot\mathbf{P}\sim\text{v}^{2}-\text{v}^{4}-\text{v}^{3}
, so
\mathbf{D}^{2}
is suppressed. The operator
iD^{0}-\mathbf{P}^{2}/2m
is the kinetic term of the heavy-quark fields. As we will see later, the operator
\mathbf{D}\cdot\mathbf{P}
, which must be treated as a perturbation, will turn out to play an important role since it is responsible for the mixing of the LDMEs at NLO. After the BPS field redefinition, the next-to-leading contribution
\mathbf{D}\cdot\mathbf{P}
becomes:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{p}^{(1)}=-\sum_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\psi^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}(x)\frac{-i\mathbf{D}\cdot\mathbf{P}}{m}\psi_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}(x)\rightarrow-g\sum_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\psi^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}(x)\frac{\mathbf{B}_{us}\cdot\mathbf{P}}{m}\psi_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}(x)\;,

(90)

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{us}(x)=-\frac{1}{g}Y_{u}^{\dagger}(x)\,i\mathbf{D}\,Y_{u}(x)\;,

where
\mathbf{B}_{us}
is called the ultrasoft gluon building block.

Finally, the leading and next-to-leading contributions of the vNRQCD interaction Lagrangian are given by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\text{int.}}_{\text{vNRQCD}}

\displaystyle=-g\sum_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\psi_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}^{\dagger}\left(\frac{\mathbf{B}_{us}\cdot\mathbf{P}}{m}\right)\psi_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}(x)+(\psi\rightarrow\chi,\,T\to\bar{T})

(91)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\boldsymbol{\ell},\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}}\frac{4\pi\alpha_{s}}{(\boldsymbol{\ell}-\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime})^{2}}\,\psi_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}^{\dagger}T^{a}\psi_{\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}}\,\chi_{-\boldsymbol{\ell}}^{\dagger}\bar{T}^{a}\chi_{-\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle-4\pi\alpha_{s}\sum_{q,q^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\ell},\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{q^{0}}\,\psi^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\left[A^{0}_{q},A^{0}_{q^{\prime}}\right]\psi_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}+(\psi\rightarrow\chi,\,T\to\bar{T})+\mathcal{O}(\text{v}^{6})\;,

which scales as
\mathcal{L}_{\text{vNRQCD}}\sim-\text{v}^{5}+\alpha_{s}\text{v}^{4}-\alpha_{s}\text{v}^{4}+\mathcal{O}(\text{v}^{6})
.

Appendix B
Matching onto QCD

In this section, we compute the QCD amplitude for the partonic process:

\gamma^{*}(k_{1})+g(k_{2})\to c(p)\bar{c}(\bar{p})\;.

(92)

Notice we have changed the notation in this section for the virtual photon (
q\to k_{1}
) and the incoming gluon (
p_{g}\to k_{2}
).
After the QCD calculation, we compute the same amplitude using the effective
[n]
-operators, and compare both results to obtain the matching tensors
\Gamma_{[n]}
.

The heavy-quark fields in the
[n]
-operators are described by the vNRQCD framework (see
appendix A
), and their momenta,
p
and
\bar{p}
, are defined in the rest frame of the
J/\psi
.
In this frame, in which the three-momenta of the heavy quarks satisfy
\mathbf{p}=-\bar{\mathbf{p}}
, their relative momentum
\mathbf{q}
, which is defined as
\mathbf{q}\equiv(\mathbf{p}-\bar{\mathbf{p}})/2
, is small w.r.t. the heavy-quark mass
m_{c}
.
We perform the QCD calculation in this scenario, which is the so-called nonrelativistic limit of QCD. As shown in this section, one needs to Taylor expand the amplitude of QCD around
\mathbf{q}
, and each order of the expansion gives the contribution of an particular angular momentum configuration denoted by the quantum number
L
. Therefore, we perform the matching onto the effective amplitude term by term, i.e., the S-state matching tensor will be given by the matching at order
\mathbf{q}^{0}
, while the P-state one by the matching at order
\mathbf{q}^{1}
.

Moreover, the QCD calculation in the nonrelativistic limit is easier in the frame in which the total three-momentum of the quark and antiquark is equal to the three-momentum
\mathbf{P}
.
As usual, the heavy-quark momenta are defined as follows:
p=P/2+\tilde{q}
and
\bar{p}=P/2-\tilde{q}
, where
P
is the total momentum of the heavy-quark pair defined as
P=p+\bar{p}
, and
\tilde{q}=(p-\bar{p})/2
is the boosted relative momentum
\mathbf{q}
from the
J/\psi
rest frame to this frame:
\tilde{q}^{\mu}\equiv(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\cdot\mathbf{q})^{\mu}
. The boost matrix,
\Lambda_{i}^{\mu}
, is defined as follows
[
68
]
:

\Lambda^{0}_{i}=\frac{P_{i}}{2E_{q}}\quad\text{and}\quad\Lambda^{j}_{i}=\delta_{i}^{j}+\left(\frac{P^{0}}{2E_{q}}-1\right)\frac{P_{i}P^{j}}{\mathbf{P}^{2}}\;,

(93)

where
\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{p}+\bar{\mathbf{p}}
,
E_{q}=\sqrt{m_{c}^{2}+\mathbf{q}^{2}}
and
P^{2}=4E_{q}^{2}
.
As we will see later, an important consequence of how the boost matrix is defined is that:

\left(\Lambda\cdot P\right)_{i}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\left(\Lambda\cdot k_{1}\right)_{i}=-\left(\Lambda\cdot k_{2}\right)_{i}\;.

(94)

Figure 2:
Feynman diagram for the QCD calculation (left) of the process
\gamma^{*}g\to c\bar{c}
and for the amplitude calculation (right) by using the
[n]
-effective operators.

We now are ready to start with the calculation of the QCD amplitude. The process we are interested is shown in the left picture of the
figure 2

The amplitude is given by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\gamma^{*}g\to c\bar{c})

\displaystyle=\frac{ige}{2}\epsilon_{\mu}(k_{1})\epsilon_{\nu}(k_{2})

(95)

\displaystyle\times\bar{u}(p)T^{a}\left[\frac{\gamma^{\mu}({p\!\!\!/\penalty}-{k\!\!\!/\penalty}_{1}+m_{c})\gamma^{\nu}}{k_{1}^{2}-2p\cdot k_{1}}+\frac{\gamma^{\nu}(-{\bar{p}\!\!\!/\penalty}+{k\!\!\!/\penalty}_{1}+m_{c})\gamma^{\mu}}{k_{1}^{2}-2\bar{p}\cdot k_{1}}\right]v(\bar{p})

\displaystyle=\frac{ige}{2}\epsilon_{\mu}(k_{1})\epsilon_{\nu}(k_{2})\left(\tilde{s}-\frac{4(\tilde{q}\cdot k_{1})^{2}}{\tilde{s}}\right)^{-1}\bar{u}(p)T^{a}\left[D^{\mu\nu}_{+}-\frac{2\,\tilde{q}\cdot k_{1}}{\tilde{s}}\,D^{\mu\nu}_{-}\right]v(\bar{p})\;,

by momentum conservation, and we define

as follows:
\tilde{s}\equiv(P^{2}-k_{1}^{2})/2

stands for the charge of the electron such that

and

(96)

\displaystyle=-({p\!\!\!/\penalty}-m_{c})\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\pm\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}({\bar{p}\!\!\!/\penalty}+m_{c})+2(p\mp\bar{p})^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}-(\gamma^{\mu}{k\!\!\!/\penalty}_{1}\gamma^{\nu}\mp\gamma^{\nu}{k\!\!\!/\penalty}_{1}\gamma^{\mu})\;.

We now use the Dirac equation, getting the following formula for the amplitude:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\gamma^{*}g\to c\bar{c})

\displaystyle=\frac{ige}{2}\epsilon_{\mu}(k_{1})\epsilon_{\nu}(k_{2})\left(\tilde{s}-\frac{4(\tilde{q}\cdot k_{1})^{2}}{\tilde{s}}\right)^{-1}\left[\mathcal{D}_{+}^{\mu\nu,a}-\frac{2\,\tilde{q}\cdot k_{1}}{\tilde{s}}\,\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\mu\nu,a}\right]\;,

(97)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{+}^{\mu\nu,a}\equiv\bar{u}(p)T^{a}D_{+}^{\mu\nu}v(\bar{p})

(98)

\displaystyle=i2\,ge\left\{g_{\perp}^{\nu\sigma}\left[g^{\mu\rho}\frac{M^{2}-Q^{2}}{M^{2}+Q^{2}}+\frac{x_{S}^{2}P_{N}^{+2}}{4(M^{2}+Q^{2})}\left(1-\frac{2\,x_{S}Q^{2}}{\gamma_{q}(M^{2}+Q^{2})}\right)n^{\mu}n^{\rho}+\frac{\gamma_{q}}{2}\,\bar{n}^{\mu}n^{\rho}\right]\right.

\displaystyle+g_{\perp}^{\nu\rho}\left[\left.g^{\mu\sigma}+\frac{x_{S}^{2}P_{N}^{+2}}{2(M^{2}+Q^{2})^{2}}\left(M^{2}+Q^{2}\frac{1-\gamma_{q}}{\gamma_{q}}\right)n^{\mu}n^{\sigma}-\frac{\gamma_{q}}{2}\bar{n}^{\mu}n^{\sigma}\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.-g^{\nu\mu}_{\perp}\frac{x_{S}^{2}P_{N}^{+2}M^{2}}{(M^{2}+Q^{2})^{2}}\,n^{\sigma}n^{\rho}\right\}\times\left(\epsilon_{\perp\nu}-\frac{k_{2\perp\nu}}{\bar{n}\cdot k_{2}}\bar{n}\cdot\epsilon\right)\times\left(\frac{(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\cdot\mathbf{q})_{\sigma}}{M}\,\xi^{\dagger}T^{a}\left(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right)_{\rho}\eta\right)\;.

We now use that the product
q^{i}\sigma^{j}
can be decomposed into the following three tensors:

q^{i}\sigma^{j}=\frac{\delta^{ij}}{3}\mathbf{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}+\frac{\epsilon^{ijk}}{2}(\mathbf{q}\times\boldsymbol{\sigma})^{k}+q^{(i}\sigma^{j)}\;,

(110)

with
q^{(i}\sigma^{j)}=(q^{i}\sigma^{j}+q^{j}\sigma^{i})/2-\mathbf{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}\delta^{ij}/3
.
We obtain the tesnorial structures for
J=0,1,2
from this decomposition.
In fact, the scalar term is describing the state
J=0
, the vector one is describing the state
J=1
and the symmetric-traceless one is describing the state
J=2
.
Thus we apply (
110
) to the P-wave contribution of the QCD amplitude (
109
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\mu}_{{}^{3}P_{0}^{[8]}}

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{\mu\nu\sigma\rho}\left(-g_{\sigma\rho}+\frac{P_{\sigma}P_{\rho}}{P^{2}}\right)\,\epsilon_{\perp\nu}\times\frac{1}{3}\left(\xi^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma})\,T^{a}\eta_{-\mathbf{q}}\right)\;,

(111)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\mu}_{{}^{3}P_{1}^{[8]}}

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{\mu\nu\sigma\rho}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\sigma\rho}\Lambda^{\alpha}_{k}\frac{P^{\beta}}{M}\,\epsilon_{\perp\nu}\times\frac{1}{2}\left(\xi^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{q}\times\boldsymbol{\sigma})^{k}\,T^{a}\eta_{-\mathbf{q}}\right)\;,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\mu}_{{}^{3}P_{2}^{[8]}}

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{\mu\nu\sigma\rho}\Lambda^{i}_{\sigma}\Lambda^{j}_{\rho}\,\epsilon_{\perp\nu}\times\left(\xi^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{q}}\,(q_{(i}\sigma_{j)})\,T^{a}\eta_{-\mathbf{q}}\right)\;,

where we have used that
\Lambda^{\alpha}_{i}\Lambda^{\beta}_{i}=-g^{\alpha\beta}+P^{\alpha}P^{\beta}/P^{2}
and
\epsilon^{ijk}\Lambda_{j}^{\alpha}\Lambda_{k}^{\beta}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\Lambda_{\mu}^{i}P_{\nu}/\sqrt{P^{2}}
and the tensor
\Gamma^{\mu\nu\sigma\rho}
is defined as:

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mu\nu\sigma\rho}

\displaystyle=\frac{i2ge}{M}\left\{g_{\perp}^{\nu\sigma}\left[g^{\mu\rho}\frac{M^{2}-Q^{2}}{M^{2}+Q^{2}}+\frac{M^{2}+Q^{2}}{4Q^{4}}x_{B}^{2}P_{N}^{+2}\left(1-\frac{2x_{B}}{\gamma_{q}}\right)n^{\mu}n^{\rho}+\frac{\gamma_{q}}{2}\bar{n}^{\mu}n^{\rho}\right]\right.

(112)

\displaystyle+g_{\perp}^{\nu\rho}

\displaystyle\left.\left[g^{\mu\sigma}+\frac{x_{B}^{2}P_{N}^{+2}}{2Q^{4}}\left(M^{2}+Q^{2}\frac{(1-\gamma_{q})}{\gamma_{q}}\right)n^{\mu}n^{\sigma}-\frac{\gamma_{q}}{2}\bar{n}^{\mu}n^{\sigma}\right]-g_{\perp}^{\nu\mu}\frac{x_{B}^{2}P_{N}^{+2}M^{2}}{Q^{4}}n^{\sigma}n^{\rho}\right\}\;.

where we use that
x_{S}=x_{B}(M^{2}+Q^{2})/Q^{2}
.

So far, we have calculated the amplitude for the S and P -states in the nonrelativistic limit of QCD.
To obtain the matching tensors, we need to deal with the calculation of the amplitude by using the
[n]
-operators defined in eq. (
23
).
Note that the expansion of the gluon building block of SCET can be written in terms of gluon fields as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{n\perp}^{\mu}=A_{n\perp}^{\mu}-\frac{k_{2\perp}^{\mu}}{\bar{n}\cdot q}\bar{n}\cdot A_{n,k_{2}}+\ldots\;,

(113)

where the dots denote terms with more collinear gluon fields, i.e., higher orders in the
\lambda
-expansion.
Since the components of the collinear gluon field scales in the same way as the components of the collinear momentum, the transverse component of a collinear gluon field
A_{n\perp}
scales as
\lambda
.
In addition, we can see from the vNRQCD Lagrangian that the heavy-quark fields scale as
\lambda^{0}
.
Thus the effective
[n]
-operators scale as
\lambda
.
Therefore, since we have expanded the QCD amplitude up to order
\lambda
, we are able to do the matching between both sides.
The effective amplitude is given by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\mu,\text{eff}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}^{\mu,\text{eff}}_{{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}}+\mathcal{A}^{\mu,\text{eff}}_{{}^{3}P_{0}^{[8]}}+\mathcal{A}^{\mu,\text{eff}}_{{}^{3}P_{1}^{[8]}}\;+\mathcal{A}^{\mu,\text{eff}}_{{}^{3}P_{2}^{[8]}}\;,

(114)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\mu,\text{eff}}_{{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}}

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{\mu\nu}_{{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}}\left<c\bar{c}\right|\psi^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}\left(A_{n\perp\nu}-\frac{k_{2\perp\nu}}{\bar{n}\cdot k_{2}}\bar{n}\cdot A_{n,k_{2}}\right)\chi_{\bar{\mathbf{p}}}\left|g\right>

(115)

\displaystyle=M\,\Gamma^{\mu\nu}_{{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}}\,\epsilon_{\perp\nu}\times\left(\xi_{\mathbf{p}}^{\dagger}T^{a}\eta_{\bar{\mathbf{p}}}\right)\;,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\mu,\text{eff}}_{{}^{3}P_{0}^{[8]}}

\displaystyle=M\,\Gamma_{{}^{3}P_{0}^{[8]}}^{\mu\nu}\,\epsilon_{\perp\nu}\times\left(\frac{1}{M}\,\xi^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}\,\left(\mathbf{q}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right)\,T^{a}\eta_{\bar{\mathbf{p}}}\right)\;,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\mu,\text{eff}}_{{}^{3}P_{1}^{[8]}}

\displaystyle=M\,\Gamma_{{}^{3}P_{1}^{[8]}}^{\mu\nu,k}\,\epsilon_{\perp\nu}\times\left(\frac{1}{M}\,\xi^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}\,\left(\mathbf{q}\times\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right)^{k}\,T^{a}\eta_{\bar{\mathbf{p}}}\right)\;,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\mu,\text{eff}}_{{}^{3}P_{2}^{[8]}}

\displaystyle=M\,\Gamma_{{}^{3}P_{2}^{[8]}}^{\mu\nu,ij}\,\epsilon_{\perp\nu}\times\left(\frac{1}{M}\,\xi^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}\,\left(q^{(i}\sigma^{j)}\right)\,T^{a}\eta_{\bar{\mathbf{p}}}\right)\;.

where we have used that
k_{2\perp}^{\nu}=0
,
\left<c\bar{c}\right|\psi^{\dagger}\chi\left|0\right>=M\,\xi^{\dagger}\eta
in perturbative NRQCD. Equating the QCD amplitude (
107
) with the effective amplitude (
114

{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}
and
{}^{3}P_{J=0,1,2}^{[8]}
matching tensors:

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mu\nu}_{{}^{3}P_{0}^{[8]}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{i2\,ge}{3M}\left(\frac{M^{2}(\gamma_{q}^{2}+2)+Q^{2}\gamma_{q}^{2}}{M^{2}+Q^{2}}\right)g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}\;,

(116)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mu\nu,k}_{{}^{3}P_{1}^{[8]}}

\displaystyle=\frac{i\,ge}{M}\left(\frac{x_{B}^{2}P_{N}^{+2}\left(M^{2}\gamma_{q}+Q^{2}\left(\gamma_{q}-1\right)\right)(n\cdot\Lambda)_{k}-\gamma_{q}^{2}Q^{4}(\bar{n}\cdot\Lambda)_{k}}{\gamma_{q}x_{B}P_{N}^{+}M\left(M^{2}+Q^{2}\right)}\right)\epsilon_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}\;,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mu\nu,ij}_{{}^{3}P_{2}^{[8]}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{i2\,ge}{M}\left(\frac{x_{B}^{2}P_{N}^{+2}M^{2}}{Q^{4}}\right)(n\cdot\Lambda)_{i}(n\cdot\Lambda)_{j}\,g_{\perp}^{\mu\nu}\;,

with

\displaystyle(n\cdot\Lambda)^{i}=\delta^{3i}+P^{i}\left(\frac{1}{2M}+\frac{1}{P^{3}}\left(\frac{P^{0}}{M}-1\right)\right)\;,

(117)

\displaystyle(\bar{n}\cdot\Lambda)^{i}=-\delta^{3i}+P^{i}\left(\frac{1}{2M}-\frac{1}{P^{3}}\left(\frac{P^{0}}{M}-1\right)\right)\;.

Considering that
n^{\mu}=(1,0,0,1)
and
\bar{n}^{\mu}=(1,0,0,-1)
, we can write
P^{0}
and

\displaystyle P^{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{B}P_{N}^{+}}{2}\left(\frac{M^{2}+Q^{2}}{Q^{2}}-\frac{1}{\gamma_{q}}\right)+\frac{\gamma_{q}Q^{2}}{2x_{B}P_{N}^{+}}\;,

(118)

\displaystyle P^{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{B}P_{N}^{+}}{2}\left(\frac{M^{2}+Q^{2}}{Q^{2}}-\frac{1}{\gamma_{q}}\right)-\frac{\gamma_{q}Q^{2}}{2x_{B}P_{N}^{+}}\;.

C.1
NLO calculation of the LDME

Diagrams showing the LO and the Coulomb interaction. Hermitian conjugate of right diagram is not shown.

Figure 4:
Diagrams showing the ultrasoft gluon exchanges between heavy quarks and (anti)quarks contributing to NLO. Hermitian conjugates of diagrams a and c are not shown. The soft and ultrasoft Wilson lines are not shown.

From
[
52
]
, we know that the operator for state
{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}
, in terms of the heavy-quark annihilation field
\psi
and the heavy-antiquark creation field
\chi
, is as follows

\mathcal{O}_{8}\left({}^{1}S_{0}\right)=\chi^{\dagger}T^{a}\psi\,\mathcal{N}_{\psi}\,\psi^{\dagger}T^{a}\chi\;,

(119)

where
\mathcal{N}_{\psi}\equiv a_{\psi}^{\dagger}a_{\psi}
is defined above as the number operator for the state
J/\psi
.
Consequently, the
{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}
LDME will be the vacuum expectation value of that operator.
From the figures presented in this appendix, it can be seen how the LDME can be illustrated as a crossed circle and two quark lines. The crossed circle represents the state in which the heavy-quark pair is produced or decays through the bilinear formed by the fields and the color and angular momentum structure, e.g., for the state we are concerned with, the left crossed circle will be
\psi^{\dagger}T^{a}\chi
.
In particular, in the
figure 3
and
figure 4
the Feynman diagrams for the LDME NLO contribution are shown.

At LO, the
{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}
LDME looks like this

\left<0|\mathcal{O}_{8}(^{1}S_{0})|0\right>^{\text{LO}}\equiv\left<{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}\right>^{\text{LO}}=M^{2}\,\eta^{\dagger}T^{a}\xi\times\xi^{\dagger}T^{a}\eta\;.

(120)

Here
\eta
and
\xi
are the Pauli 2-component spinors. The Dirac 4-component spinors
u(p)
and
v(\bar{p})
are defined in terms of the Pauli spinors in the standard nonrelativistic form (see eq. (
99
), where we explicitly show the relation between both). Expanding the Dirac spinors in the velocity in perturbative NRQCD, one finds
\left<c\bar{c}\right|\psi^{\dagger}\chi\left|0\right>=M\xi^{\dagger}\eta
, with
M
the mass of the heavy-quark state, and where we have implicitly summed over the helicity of the bound state.

The diagrams which contribute to the NLO are called Coulomb interaction and chromo-electric dipole transition.
The Coulomb interaction contribution arises from the term of the second line term of vNRQCD interaction Lagrangian in eq. (
91
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{vNRQCD}}^{\text{Coul.}}=\sum_{\mathbf{p},\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\frac{4\pi\alpha_{s}}{(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime})^{2}}\,\psi_{\mathbf{p}}^{\dagger}T^{a}\psi_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\,\chi_{-\mathbf{p}}^{\dagger}\bar{T}^{a}\chi_{-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\;.

(121)

The leading order Lagrangian gives the intermediate fermion propagator for the below equation
p^{0}+k^{0}-(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{k})^{2}/2m
. Additionally, considering that
\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}=\mathbf{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left<{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}\right>^{\text{Coul.}}

\displaystyle=\left<{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}\right>^{\text{LO}}(-i4\pi\alpha_{s})\left(C_{F}-C_{A}/2\right)\int\frac{d^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{k}^{2}}\frac{1}{[p^{0}+k^{0}-(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{k})^{2}/2m+i\epsilon]}

(122)

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{[p^{0}-k^{0}-(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{k})^{2}/2m+i\epsilon]}+h.c.\;.

Here we compute the integral on
k^{0}
by contour integration, i.e., closing the contour in the lower half of the complex plane and picking up the pole
k^{0}=-p^{0}+(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{k})^{2}/2m-i\epsilon
. We find that the contribution reduces to the following integral on the three-momentum:

\displaystyle\left<{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}\right>^{\text{Coul.}}

\displaystyle=\left<{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}\right>^{\text{LO}}(4\pi\alpha_{s}m_{c})\left(C_{F}-C_{A}/2\right)\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{k}^{2}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{k}^{2}+2\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{k}-i\epsilon}+h.c.

(123)

\displaystyle=\left<{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}\right>^{\text{LO}}\left(C_{F}-C_{A}/2\right)\frac{\pi\alpha_{s}}{2\text{v}}\;,

where we have used that
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{p}/m_{c}
and
\text{v}=|\mathbf{v}|
.

The chromo-electric effect contribution arises from the term of the first line of the vNRQCD interaction Lagrangian:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{vNRQCD}}^{\text{Chro.}}=-g\sum_{\mathbf{p}}\psi_{\mathbf{p}}^{\dagger}\left(\frac{\mathbf{B}_{us}\cdot\mathbf{P}}{m_{c}}\right)\psi_{\mathbf{p}}(x)+(\psi\rightarrow\chi,\,T\to\bar{T})\;.

(124)

Let us look at this term in more detail. As mentioned before, after BPS redefinition in eq. (
89
), the usoft Wilson lines arise. In the rest of the paper, we assume they are implicit in the soft Wilson lines but here we need write explicitly (at NLO):

\displaystyle Y_{v}(x)

\displaystyle=1-ig\int_{-\infty}^{0}dt^{\prime}A_{us}^{0}(t+t^{\prime},\mathbf{x})+\mathcal{O}(g^{2})

(125)

\displaystyle=1+\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}e^{-ik\cdot x}\left(\frac{g\,T^{a}}{k^{0}}\right)A_{us}^{0,a}(k)+\mathcal{O}(g^{2})\;,

where we use that
v=(1,\mathbf{0})
in the rest frame of the
J/\psi
.
Inserting the expanded usoft Wilson line (
125
) in eq. (
124
) through the product
\mathbf{B}_{us}\cdot\mathbf{P}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{us}\cdot\mathbf{P}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{-1}{g}Y_{u}^{\dagger}(iD^{i})Y_{u}\right)P^{i}

(126)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}e^{-ik\cdot x}\left(\mathbf{A}_{us}\cdot\mathbf{P}-\frac{A_{us}^{0}}{k^{0}}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{P}\right)\;,

and consequently, the chromo-electric term in the Lagrangian is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{vNRQCD}}^{\text{Chro.}}

\displaystyle=\frac{-g}{m_{c}}\sum_{\mathbf{p}}\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}e^{-ik\cdot x}\psi^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}\left(\mathbf{A}_{us}\cdot\mathbf{p}-\frac{A_{us}^{0}}{k^{0}}\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{p}\right)\psi_{\mathbf{p}}+(\psi\rightarrow\chi,\,T\to\bar{T})\;.

(127)

Here we act with the label momentum operator on the heavy-(anti)quark fields as follows
\mathbf{P}\,\psi_{\mathbf{p}}=\mathbf{p}\,\psi_{\mathbf{p}}
.
From this form of the interaction term, the loop integral reads (e.g., here we show the contribution of the
figure 4
b)

\displaystyle\left<{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}\right>^{\ref{fig:TMDShF-ChroElectric}\text{b}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi\alpha_{s}}{m_{c}^{2}}\,I^{\ref{fig:TMDShF-ChroElectric}\text{b}}\eta^{\dagger}T^{a}T^{b}\xi\times\xi^{\dagger}T^{b}T^{a}\eta\;,

(128)

with

\displaystyle I^{\ref{fig:TMDShF-ChroElectric}\text{b}}

\displaystyle=i\mu^{2\varepsilon}\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}-(\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{k})^{2}/\mathbf{k}^{2}}{(k^{0})^{2}-\mathbf{k}^{2}+i\epsilon}\,\frac{1}{[p^{0}-k^{0}-(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{k})^{2}/2m]^{2}}

(129)

\displaystyle=\mu^{2\varepsilon}\int\frac{d^{3-2\varepsilon}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3-2\varepsilon}}\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}-(\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{k})^{2}/\mathbf{k}^{2}}{2|\mathbf{k}|}\frac{1}{-|\mathbf{k}|}\,\frac{1}{-|\mathbf{k}|}

\displaystyle=\mu^{2\varepsilon}\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d^{3-2\varepsilon}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3-2\varepsilon}}\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}-(\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{k})^{2}/\mathbf{k}^{2}}{|\mathbf{k}|^{3}}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}}{6\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\rm{UV}}}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\rm{IR}}}\right)\;.

In the second equality we have computed the contour-integral over
k^{0}
around the pole
k^{0}=|\mathbf{k}|-i\epsilon
and in the third equality we have expanded the integrand in powers of
\mathbf{p}/m_{c}
and
\mathbf{k}/m_{c}
. To do so we need the following integral:

\displaystyle\mu^{2\varepsilon}\int d^{3-2\varepsilon}\mathbf{k}\,\frac{1}{|\mathbf{k}|^{3}}

\displaystyle=2\pi\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\rm{UV}}}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\rm{IR}}}\right)\;.

(130)

Diagrams
figure 4
c,d give the same contribution as (
128

\displaystyle\left<{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}\right>^{\ref{fig:TMDShF-ChroElectric}\text{c}}

\displaystyle=\left<{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}\right>^{\ref{fig:TMDShF-ChroElectric}\text{b}}\left(T^{a}T^{b}\otimes T^{b}T^{a}\rightarrow T^{a}T^{b}\otimes T^{a}T^{b}\right)\;,

(131)

\displaystyle\left<{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}\right>^{\ref{fig:TMDShF-ChroElectric}\text{c}^{*}}

\displaystyle=\left<{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}\right>^{\ref{fig:TMDShF-ChroElectric}\text{b}}\left(T^{a}T^{b}\otimes T^{b}T^{a}\rightarrow T^{b}T^{a}\otimes T^{b}T^{a}\right)\;,

\displaystyle T^{a}T^{b}\otimes T^{b}T^{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{F}}{2N_{c}}\,1\otimes 1+\frac{N_{c}^{2}-2}{2N_{c}}\,T^{a}\otimes T^{a}\;,

\displaystyle\left<{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}\right>^{\ref{fig:TMDShF-ChroElectric}\text{b,c,d}}

\displaystyle\left<{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}\right>^{\ref{fig:TMDShF-ChroElectric}}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\left(C_{F}-C_{A}/2\right)\frac{\pi\alpha_{s}}{2\text{v}}\right)\left<{}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]}\right>^{\text{LO}}

Results for P-states

As previously mentioned, the computation of the

Upon inspection of the diagrams and the vNRQCD Lagrangian interaction, it becomes evident that the methodology for computing the P-waves mirrors that of the S-wave. At LO, the LDME primarily encompasses the spin factor denoting the configuration of angular momentum, spin, and color of the heavy quarks in the final state. Analogous to our explanation in eq. (

\displaystyle\left<{}^{3}P_{0}^{[8]}\right>^{\text{LO}}

(152)

\displaystyle\left<{}^{3}P_{1}^{[8]}\right>^{\text{LO}}

Non-finitely generated monoids corresponding to finitely generated homogeneous subalgebras
Akihiro Higashitani, Koichiro Tani

Abstract
. The goal of this paper is to study the possible monoids appearing as the associated monoids of the initial algebra of a finitely generated homogeneous 𝕜 \Bbbk -subalgebra of a polynomial ring 𝕜 ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] \Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}] . Clearly, any affine monoid can be realized since the initial algebra of the affine monoid 𝕜 \Bbbk -algebra is itself. On the other hand, the initial algebra of a finitely generated homogeneous 𝕜 \Bbbk -algebra is not necessarily finitely generated. In this paper, we provide a new family of non-finitely generated monoids which can be realized as the initial algebras of finitely generated homogeneous 𝕜 \Bbbk -algebras. Moreover, we also provide an example of a non-finitely generated monoid which cannot be realized as the initial algebra of any finitely generated homogeneous 𝕜 \Bbbk -algebra.

Introduction

Let
\Bbbk

\Bbbk

,
we define

\deg_{\preceq}{f}\coloneqq\max_{\preceq}(\supp{f})

be a finitely generated
\Bbbk
-subalgebra of

\deg_{\preceq}{R}\coloneqq\{\deg_{\preceq}{f}\mid f\in R\backslash\{0\}\}

of

A subset

.
The word ”SAGBI” is introduced by Robbiano and Sweedler
[
5

and stands for ”Subalgebra Analog to Gröbner Bases for Ideal”.
Remark that

is not necessarily a finitely generated
\Bbbk

may have no finite SAGBI basis with respect to any monomial orders.
The sufficient condition for

In
[
4
]
, it is claimed that it will be helpful for this problem to study the mechanism of non-finite generation of the initial algebras.
The goal of this paper is to study non-finitely generated monoids appearing as non-finitely generated homogeneous monoid
\Bbbk
-algebras.

Let
M
be a monoid, a set with an operation
M\times M\rightarrow M
that is associative and has the identity.
For a monoid
M\subset(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}
and a field
\Bbbk

\Bbbk[M]
is a
\Bbbk
-vector space with the base
\{x^{\bf u}\mid{\bf u}\in M\}
.
Since both
\Bbbk[M]
and
\ini_{\preceq}{R}
are
\Bbbk
-subalgebras of

generated by monomials, there exists a monoid
M
such that

\ini_{\preceq}{R}=\Bbbk[M]

for any initial algebras
\ini_{\preceq}{R}
. Concretely, such a monoid

is
\deg_{\preceq}{R}
.
Therefore, to check whether
\ini_{\preceq}{R}

M(=\deg_{\preceq}{R})
is so.
In that sense, the following question naturally arises.

Question 1.1
.

Can we characterize non-finitely generated monoids arising from some finitely generated
\Bbbk
-subalgebras?

Towards the solution of Question
1.1
, in this paper, we concentrate on our discussion in the case of homogeneous subalgebras of
\Bbbk[x,y]
.
In particular, we mainly study subalgebras generated by one homogeneous binomial
x^{{\bf v}_{1}}+x^{{\bf v}_{2}}
and
finitely many monomials
x^{{\bf u}_{1}},x^{{\bf u}_{2}},\ldots,x^{{\bf u}_{t}}
.

There are two main results in this paper.
The first main result is to provide a class of non-finitely generated monoids that correspond to some finitely generated subalgebras.

Theorem
3.4
.

Let
{\bf v}_{1},{\bf v}_{2}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{2}
be linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
.
Let
\preceq
be a monomial order with
{\bf v}_{1}\succeq{\bf v}_{2}
and let

be the cone generated by
{\bf v}_{1},{\bf v}_{2}
.
We take

from

denotes the interior of

.
Let

, and let

We define

The following second main result is to show that

do not necessarily correspond to some initial algebras of finitely generated homogeneous subalgebras.

Theorem

Let

Then, for any subalgebra

This paper is organized as follows. In Section

We found these examples through computational experiments by using the package

First, we discuss the case

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
{\bf v}_{1},{\bf v}_{2}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{2}
be linearly independent.
For any

, the
\Bbbk

Proof.

We may assume
{\bf v}_{1}\succeq{\bf v}_{2}
without loss of generality.

Let
{\bf v}_{1},{\bf u}
be linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
. We consider a linear relation

a_{1}{\bf v}_{1}+a_{2}{\bf u}=b_{1}{\bf v}_{1}+b_{2}{\bf u},

{\bf v}_{1},{\bf u}
are linearly independent, we have
a_{1}=b_{1}
and

.
Thus, any cancellation of initial terms in
R
cannot occur and we obtain that

is a SAGBI basis of

.

Let
m{\bf v}_{1}=\ell{\bf u}
with some positive integers

. Then, we can obtain a polynomial in

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{\binom{m}{1}}((x^{{\bf v}_{1}}+x^{{\bf v}_{2}})^{m}-(x^{\bf u})^{\ell})

\displaystyle=x^{(m-1){\bf v}_{1}+{\bf v}_{2}}+\frac{\binom{m}{2}}{\binom{m}{1}}x^{(m-2){\bf v}_{1}+2{\bf v}_{2}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{\binom{m}{1}}x^{m{\bf v}_{2}}.

Now, we prove
\{f,x^{\bf u}\}
is a SAGBI basis of
R
. Similarly to the previous case, we consider the equality

a_{1}((m-1){\bf v}_{1}+{\bf v}_{2})+a_{2}{\bf u}=b_{1}((m-1){\bf v}_{1}+{\bf v}_{2})+b_{2}{\bf u}.

Since
{\bf v}_{1}
and
{\bf u}
are linearly dependent while
{\bf v}_{1}
and
{\bf v}_{2}
are linearly independent, we obtain
a_{1}=b_{1}
.
Therefore we obtain

. Thus, there is no relation between
(m-1){\bf v}_{1}+{\bf v}_{2}

. Hence,

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
{\bf v}_{1},{\bf v}_{2}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{2}
be linearly independent and let
\preceq
be a monomial order with
{\bf v}_{1}\succeq{\bf v}_{2}
.
Let

be a cone generated by
{\bf v}_{1},{\bf v}_{2}
.
Fix
{\bf u}_{1},{\bf u}_{2},\ldots,{\bf u}_{s}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{2}\cap C^{\circ}

,

Proof.

Let
{\bf w}_{1},{\bf w}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that

\displaystyle\langle{\bf w}_{1},{\bf v}_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=0\text{ and }\langle{\bf w}_{1},{\bf x}\rangle\geq 0\quad(\forall{\bf x}\in C);

\displaystyle\langle{\bf w}_{2},{\bf v}_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=0\text{ and }\langle{\bf w}_{2},{\bf x}\rangle\geq 0\quad(\forall{\bf x}\in C).

are chosen as they define the facets

Let, say,

is an infinite subset of

On the contrary, suppose that

Then

can be written as follows:

of (

This section is devoted to proving the second main theorem, which provides an example of a monoid

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
M
be a submonoid of
(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{2}
generated by infinitely many irreducible elements
\{(1,n^{2})\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}

For any subalgebra
R
generated by finitely many homogeneous polynomials in
\Bbbk[x,y]
and any monomial order
\preceq
in
(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{2}
,

\ini_{\preceq}{R}
cannot be equal to
\Bbbk[M]
.

Proof.

Suppose the existence of a subalgebra
R
and a monomial order
\preceq
such that
\ini_{\preceq}{R}=\Bbbk[M]
.
Let
g_{n}
be a polynomial with
\ini_{\preceq}{g_{n}}=xy^{n^{2}}
for each
n
and let
G=\{g_{0},g_{1},\ldots\}
be the reduced SAGBI basis of
R
.
Since we assume that
R
is generated by homogeneous polynomials of
\Bbbk[x,y]
, the reduced SAGBI basis
G
can be chosen as a set of homogeneous polynomials.
Since
R
is finitely generated and
R=\Bbbk[g_{0},g_{1},\ldots]
,
there exists
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
R=k[g_{0},g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}]
. Then
\deg_{\preceq}{R}
is a submonoid of
the monoid generated by
\bigcup_{i=0}^{m}{\supp{g_{i}}}
because all monomials appearing in polynomials of
R

can be written as a product of finitely many monomials appearing in
g_{0},g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}
.

(The first step)
:
First, we prove that there exists a positive integer
a
with
(0,a)\in\bigcup_{i=0}^{m}{\supp{g_{i}}}
.
On the contrary, suppose that
(0,a)\not\in\bigcup_{i=0}^{m}{\supp{g_{i}}}
for any
a\geq 1
.
Let
C
be the cone generated by
\bigcup_{i=0}^{m}{\supp{g_{i}}}
.
Then
C
does not include
\mathbb{R}_{>0}(0,1)
. Since
C
is finitely generated, we can take a point
(b_{1},b_{2})\in C
satisfying
b_{1}>0
and

\displaystyle y\leq\frac{b_{2}}{b_{1}}x
for any
(x,y)\in C
.
However, for sufficiently large
\ell
, we get
\displaystyle\ell^{2}\geq\frac{b_{2}}{b_{1}}
, so
(1,\ell^{2})
is out of
C
, i.e., out of
\deg_{\preceq}{R}
, a contradiction.

Hence, we have
(0,a)\in\bigcup_{i=0}^{m}{\supp{g_{i}}}
for some
a\geq 1
.

(The second step)
: Next, we prove that each
g_{i}
can be written like
g_{i}=y^{i^{2}}(x+ay)
for some
a\in\Bbbk\setminus\{0\}
.
Note that
a
is independent of
i
.

Since each

is homogeneous, we can write

\displaystyle g_{0}

\displaystyle=x+a_{0}y

\displaystyle g_{1}

\displaystyle=xy+\cdots+a_{1}y^{2}+\cdots

\displaystyle g_{m}

\displaystyle=xy^{m^{2}}+\cdots+a_{m}y^{m^{2}+1}+\cdots

with
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{m},\ldots\in\Bbbk
and one of
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
is not
0
.
Since
\ini_{\preceq}{g_{i}}=xy^{i^{2}}
, we get
xy^{i^{2}}\succeq y^{i^{2}+1}
. Therefore, we have
x\succeq y
.
For
b\geq 2
, monomials

do not appear in

because

.
Thus we can rewrite
g_{i}
’s by

We prove
a_{0}=a_{1}=\ldots=a_{m}=\cdots
by induction on
m
. In the case of
m=2
, we consider

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\coloneqq g_{0}^{3}g_{2}-g_{1}^{4}-(3a_{0}+a_{2}-4a_{1})g_{0}g_{1}g_{2}

\displaystyle\;=(a_{0}a_{1}-a_{0}a_{2}-2a_{1}^{2}+3a_{1}a_{2}-a_{2}^{2})x^{2}y^{6}

\displaystyle\quad+(a_{0}^{3}-3a_{0}^{2}a_{1}+4a_{0}a_{1}^{2}-a_{0}a_{2}^{2}-4a_{1}^{3}+4a_{1}^{2}a_{2}-a_{1}a_{2}^{2})xy^{7}

.
Thus, all coefficients of

should be

. Let

\displaystyle h_{1}

\displaystyle=X_{0}X_{1}-X_{0}X_{2}-2X_{1}^{2}+3X_{1}X_{2}-X_{2}^{2};

\displaystyle h_{2}

\displaystyle=X_{0}^{3}-3X_{0}^{2}X_{1}+4X_{0}X_{1}^{2}-X_{0}X_{2}^{2}-4X_{1}^{3}+4X_{1}^{2}X_{2}-X_{1}X_{2}^{2};

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{1}

\displaystyle=X_{1}^{4}-4X_{1}^{3}X_{2}+6X_{1}^{2}X_{2}^{2}-4X_{1}X_{2}^{3}+X_{2}^{4}=(X_{1}-X_{2})^{4};

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{2}

Finally, we introduce the most complicated example through our experiments.

Example 5.5
.

Let
R=\Bbbk[x^{2}-y^{2},x^{3}-y^{3},x^{4}-y^{4}]
and
\preceq
a monomial order with
x\succeq y
. Computing a SAGBI basis of
R
,
we observe that the following monomials appear as initial terms:

\displaystyle x^{2},x^{3},x^{2}y^{2},x^{3}y^{3},x^{5}y^{7},x^{6}y^{8},x^{6}y^{10},x^{7}y^{11},x^{7}y^{13},x^{8}y^{14},x^{8}y^{16},x^{9}y^{17},x^{9}y^{19},\ldots.

Thus, generators of
M
which satisfy
\ini_{\preceq}{R}=\Bbbk[M]
are

(2,0),(3,0),(2,2),(3,3),(5,7),(6,8),(6,10),(7,11),(7,13),(8,14),(8,16),(9,17),(9,19),\ldots.

All points following
(3,3)
are contained in

\{(5,7)+m(1,3)\mid m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}\cup\{(6,8)+m(1,3)\mid m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}.

However, first four points
(2,0),(3,0),(2,2),(3,3)
are not contained there,
and
(2,2)+(1,3)=(3,5)
and
(3,3)+(1,3)=(4,6)
are not contained in
M
.
Moreover, the monomial
xy^{3}
does not appear in generators of
R
.

In contrast, let us change the signs of generators of
R
, i.e. let
R=\Bbbk[x^{2}+y^{2},x^{3}+y^{3},x^{4}+y^{4}]
.
Then
R
has a finite SAGBI basis with respect to a monomial order with
x\succeq y
, which is

\{x^{2}+y^{2},x^{3}+y^{3},x^{2}y^{2},x^{3}y^{3}\}.

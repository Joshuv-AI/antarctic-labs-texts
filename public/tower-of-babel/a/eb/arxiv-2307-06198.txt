Introduction

It is well-known in
[
21

(see the Fourier transform of

r^{\lambda}
with the formula

r^{\lambda}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{m}\frac{\lambda^{n}}{n!}(\ln r)^{n}+o(\lambda^{m})\quad{\rm as}\ \,\lambda\to 0^{+}.

(1.1)

Our aim in this article is to find a sequence of the integro-differential operators whose Fourier transforms having the form
(\ln r)^{n}
with
n\in\mathbb{N}
, which gives the related series expansion for the Riesz convolutions and the fractional Laplacians, their Fourier symbols
|\zeta|^{\pm 2s}
respectively, as the order tends to zero, i.e.
s\to 0

Let the integer
N\geq 1
,
s\in(0,\frac{N}{2})
and

\Phi_{s}(x)=\kappa_{N,s}|x|^{2s-N}\quad{\rm for}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\},

where
\kappa_{N,s}
is a normalized constant with the form

\kappa_{N,s}=2^{-2s}\pi^{-\frac{N}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N-2s}{2})}{\Gamma(1+s)}s.

(1.3)

Here
\Gamma

\mathcal{F}

denote the Fourier transform.
With this constant, there holds in the distributional sense

\mathcal{F}(\Phi_{s}\ast u)(\zeta)=|\zeta|^{-2s}\hat{u}\quad{\rm for\ suitable}\ \,u.

s

With this normalized constant, there holds that for

\mathcal{F}((-\Delta)^{s}u)(\xi)=|\xi|^{2s}\hat{u}(\xi)\quad{\rm for\ all}\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(1.5)

Theorem 1.1

Let
\Phi_{s}
be the normalized Riesz kernel with formula (

) and

\zeta\in C^{\alpha}_{\rm uloc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

For

, we denote

\mathcal{L}_{m}\zeta(x)=\sum^{m}_{j=0}\alpha_{j}\mathcal{K}_{j}\zeta(x),

(1.16)

where

\alpha_{0}=(-1)^{m}\kappa_{2}^{(m)}(0),\qquad\alpha_{j}=m(-1)^{m+j}\binom{m-1}{j-1}\kappa_{1}^{(m-j)}(0),\ \ j=1,2,\cdots,m.

Then we have that

\frac{d^{m}}{ds^{m}}\Big|_{s=0^{+}}\big(\Phi_{s}\ast u\big)(x)=(-1)^{m}\mathcal{L}_{m}u(x),

\mathcal{F}\big(\mathcal{L}_{m}u\big)(\zeta)=\big(2\ln|\zeta|\big)^{m}\,\hat{u}(\zeta)

(1.17)

and
for

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{m!}{s^{m}}\Big(\Phi_{s}\ast u-u-\sum^{m-1}_{j=1}\frac{(-1)^{j}s^{j}}{j!}\mathcal{L}_{j}u\Big)=(-)^{m}\mathcal{L}_{m}u\quad{\rm in}\ L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Here the binomial coefficient

Remark 1.1

(ii)

(iii)

Thanks to the fact that

for any

(1.20)

Thanks to the singularity of the kernels, the high order logarithmic Laplacians have special nonlocal properties and very weak differentiation property. So there are many new properties for this type of operators related to the order
m
and our next interest is to study the fundamental properties: the Dirichlet eigenvalues, basic regularity estimates.

We consider the bounds of the Dirichlet eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}_{m}

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathcal{L}_{m}u=\lambda u&{\rm in}\ \,\Omega,\\[4.2679pt]
\phantom{\mathcal{L}_{m}}u=0&{\rm in}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.21)

where
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

In order to find these eigenvalues, we need involve the related Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{m,0}(\Omega)
.
Denote by
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
the set of functions in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with compact support in
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
and by
\mathcal{H}_{m,0}(\Omega)
the closure of functions in
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
under the norm

\|u\|_{m}=\sqrt{\int_{\Omega}u^{2}(x)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}\mathbf{k}_{m}(x-y)dxdy}.

Note that
\mathcal{H}_{m,0}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space with the inner product

(u,v)_{\mathcal{H}_{m,0}}=(u,v)_{0}+(u,v)_{m},

where

\displaystyle(u,v)_{0}=\int_{\Omega}uvdx,\quad\ (u,v)_{j}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))\mathbf{k}_{j}(x-y)\,dxdy\quad{\rm for}\ \,u,v\in\mathcal{H}_{m,0}(\Omega).

Denote the energy functional of
\mathcal{L}_{m}

\mathcal{I}_{m}:\mathcal{H}_{m,0}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{H}_{m,0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R},\qquad\mathcal{I}_{m}(u,v)=\mathcal{E}_{m}(u,v)-\mathcal{A}_{m}(u,v),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{m}(u,v)=\sum^{m}_{j=0}\alpha_{j}(u,v)_{j},\qquad\mathcal{A}_{m}(u,v)=\sum^{m}_{n=1}\alpha_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(x)v(y)\,{\mathbf{j}}_{n}(x-y)dxdy\quad{\rm for}\ \,u,v\in\mathcal{H}_{m,0}(\Omega).

Note that the quadratic form
\mathcal{I}_{m}
is bilinear, closed, symmetric and semi-bounded in
\mathcal{H}_{m,0}(\Omega)
.

A function
u\in\mathcal{H}_{m,0}(\Omega)
is called the eigenfunction of (
1.21
) corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda
if

\mathcal{I}_{m}(u,\phi)=\lambda\int_{\Omega}u\phi dx,\quad\forall\,\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{m,0}(\Omega).

(1.22)

We have the following characterization of Dirichlet eigenvalues and corresponding eigenfunctions for the operator
\mathcal{L}_{m}
.

Theorem 1.3

Assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain.

(i)

Problem (
1.21
) admits an eigenvalue
\lambda_{m,1}(\Omega)>0

\lambda_{m,1}(\Omega)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\mathcal{H}_{m,0}(\Omega)\\

(1.23)

with

\mathcal{P}_{m,1}(\Omega):=\{u\in\mathcal{H}_{m,0}(\Omega):\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1\},

and there exists a function
\phi_{m,1}\in\mathcal{P}_{m,1}(\Omega)
achieving the minimum of
\mathcal{I}_{m}
, i.e.

\lambda_{m,1}(\Omega)=\mathcal{I}_{m}(\phi_{m,1},\phi_{m,1}).

(ii)

If

is even, then

.

(iii)

Problem (

) admits a sequence of eigenvalues

with

with corresponding eigenfunctions
\phi_{m,k}
,

Moreover, for any

can be characterized as

(v)

Assume more that

Then

Remark 1.2

It is worth noting that we don’t obtain the one-fold of the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}_{m}
and the positivity of the corresponding eigenfunction in a general bounded domain when

. The difficulty arises from

with
\alpha_{m}>0

\{\alpha_{j}\}
for

To this end, we only consider the eigenvalues for small domain

, where

such that the the sum of the kernel
\sum^{m}_{j=0}\alpha_{j}{\bf q}_{j}>0
in

.

We also appendix the Dirichlet eigenvalues of the dominated part
\mathcal{K}_{m}
of
\mathcal{L}_{m}
. In this case, we can show that first Dirichlet eigenvalue is simple and the corresponding eigenfunction is positive.

Finally, we study the Faber-Krahn inequality of the first eigenvalue for the

Let

Lemma 4.3

Assume that

(a)

\Psi:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
is a positive convex, monotone increasing function such that
\Psi(0)=0

\Psi^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
and
t\Psi^{\prime}(t)
convex;

(b)

K:(0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
is monotone decreasing;

(c)

\rho:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
is monotone increasing.

Let
u^{*}
be the equi-measurable rearrangement of
u
.

Then for
f,g
nonnegative functions,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Psi\Big[\frac{f(x)-g(y))}{\rho(|x-y|)}\Big]K(|x-y|)dxdy\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Psi\Big[\frac{f*(x)-g^{*}(y))}{\rho(|x-y|)}\Big]K(|x-y|)dxdy.

Moreover, if
K
is strictly decreasing and
\Psi
is strictly convex,
the above equality holds only when
f=f^{*}
and
g=g^{*}

Quantum backflow of a Dirac fermion on a ring
Valentin Daniel Paccoia, Orlando Panella, Pinaki Roy

Abstract
We study the quantum backflow problem of a relativistic charged Dirac fermion constrained to move on a ring of radius R R . Using the relativistic current operator we compute the probability flux through a generic time interval to show emergence of quantum backflow. We also discuss the limiting case when the particle moves along a line.



I
Introduction

Quantum backflow (QB) is a phenomenon in which the probability density of a quantum mechanical particle may flow in a direction opposite to the momentum
Allcock 1969
;
Bracken and Melloy 1994
. One of the most interesting results in this context is that there is a limit on the maximum amount of backflow. It essentially means that although backflow may increase with time, the increase is bounded by a dimensionless number
c_{bm}\approx 0.04

Bracken and Melloy 1994
. Later, improved estimates of the number
c_{bm}
were found
Eveson et al. 2005
;
Penz et al. 2006
. QB has been studied in the context of various nonrelativistic systems e.g., optical systems
Berry 2010
;
Eliezer et al. 2020
, elementary quantum systems
Yearsley et al. 2012
, particle in a constant magnetic field
Strange 2012
, correlated quantum states
Goussev 2020
, many-particle systems
Barbier 2020
, systems with a scattering potential
Bostelmann et al. 2017
;
Cadamuro 2017
, noncommutative plane
Paccoia et al. 2020
, open quantum systems
Mousavi and Miret-Artés 2020
, dissipative systems
Mousavi and Miret-Artés 2020
. Interestingly, quantum backflow has also been found to be related to nonclassicality
Albarelli et al. 2016
, superoscillations
Muga and Leavens 2000
, decay of quasi stable systems
van Dijk and Toyama 2019
and classically forbidden probability flow
Goussev 2019
, transmitting defects
de Vasconcelos Jr 2020
, negative flow of probability
Villanueva 2020
etc. It is thus natural to inquire whether or not the concept of QB can be extended to relativistic systems which are by nature more complicated than the nonrelativistic ones. The answer to this question is in the affirmative and it was shown that, despite the localization problem of Dirac particles, the QB effect does take place for a Dirac particle described by the one particle Dirac equation
Melloy and Bracken 1998
;
Su and Chen 2018a
;
Ashfaque et al. 2019
;
Das 2021
. It has also been shown that, for a free Dirac particle, the probability that flows in a direction opposite to the momentum in a time interval of length
T
depends on a dimensionless parameter
\epsilon
which is a function of mass
m
, velocity of light
c
, Planck’s constant and
T

Melloy and Bracken 1998
. Later, backflow eigenfunctions were determined numerically over a range of values of
\epsilon
and an explicit expression for relativistic backflow was found
Ashfaque et al. 2019
. Also, backflow of a free Dirac particle in a superposition consisting of various energies and helicities
Su and Chen 2018b
, in electron wave packets
Das 2021
and for spin-orbit coupled particles
Mardonov et al. 2014
have been studied. Interestingly QB has also been studied recently within the context of Dirac equation, Maxwell equations and linearized gravity
Bialynicki-Birula et al. 2022
.

Although there have been formulations of quantum backflow more amenable to experimental verification
Palmero et al. 2013
;
Miller et al. 2021
;
Barbier and Goussev 2021
;
Barbier et al. 2023
, this effect, whether in the nonrelativistic domain or in the relativistic domain, is an as yet unobserved phenomenon. The main reason for the non observation of QB is that the net backflow through a point over a finite time interval is a very small quantity
Melloy and Bracken 1998
. However, it has been shown that, when a nonrelativistic particle is constrained to move on a ring
Goussev 2021
, the chances of observing quantum backflow increase manyfold as compared to when the particle moves on a line. In the present paper our aim is to
examine whether or not this strategy works
for a relativistic quantum mechanical system. More precisely, we shall consider a Dirac fermion on a ring of radius
R
in the presence as well as in the absence of a constant magnetic field
B
and for this system we shall compute the quantum backflow as a function of the aforementioned parameter
\epsilon
and
B
. Finally, we shall also compare our results in the
c\rightarrow\infty
limit with those of the nonrelativistic backflow problem.

The rest of the paper is organised as follows: in Sec.
II
we set up the problem of the relativistic fermion on a circular ring of radius
R
;
Sec.
III
discusses the quantum backflow of the relativistic fermion;
Sec.
III.2
discusses the full numerical analysis of the corresponding homogeneous Fredholm integral equation to which the quantum backflow problem on the ring is reduced. Finally Sec.
V
presents the discussion and conclusions.

II
Problem setup

The Hamiltonian of a charged Dirac fermion constrained on a plane (a two-dimensional system) reads:

H=c\,\bm{\sigma}\cdot{\bm{P}}+mc^{2}\sigma^{3},

(1)

where
\bm{\sigma}=(\sigma^{1},\sigma^{2})
, and
\sigma^{i}
, (
i=1,2,3
) are the Pauli matrices and the coupling to the electromagnetic field is introduced via the minimal prescription:

\bm{P}=\bm{p}-\frac{q}{c}\bm{A}\,,

(2)

where
\bm{p}=-i\hbar\,(\partial_{x},\partial_{y})
and
\bm{A}
is the vector potential.

In this work we will consider the dynamics of a charged fermion in a constant magnetic field directed along the
z
-axis,
\bm{B}=B\bm{e}_{z}
, perpendicular to the
(x,y)
plane, which we describe in the so-called symmetric gauge with the following potential:

\bm{A}=\left(-\frac{By}{2},\frac{Bx}{2}\right)\,.

(3)

The Hamiltonian in Eq. (
1
) becomes:

H=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}mc^{2}&cP_{-}\\
cP_{+}&-mc^{2}\end{array}\right),

(4)

where we have defined:

P_{\pm}=P_{x}\pm iP_{y}\,.

(5)

The operators
P_{\pm}
are conveniently written in polar coordinates:

P_{\pm}=-i\,e^{\pm i\varphi}\,\left[\left(\hbar\,\partial_{r}\pm i\,\frac{\hbar}{r}\,\partial_{\varphi}\right)\pm\frac{qBr}{2c}\right].

(6)

In the following we will be interested specifically in the quantum backflow of the Dirac fermion on the plane when it is constrained to move on a circle of radius
R
. The circle is assumed to be centered at the origin
O=(0,0)
of the
(x,y)
coordinate system. Therefore, imposing the constraint
r=R
, the operators
P_{\pm}
in Eq. (
5
) are given by:

c\,P_{\pm}=-i\,\frac{\hbar c}{R}e^{\pm i\varphi}\,\left(\pm i\,\partial_{\varphi}\pm\frac{qBR^{2}}{2\hbar c}\right)\,.

(7)

We now introduce the dimensionless quantity

\beta=\frac{qBR^{2}}{2\hbar c}.

(8)

This gives the flux
\Phi
of the magnetic field across the circle of radius
R
,
\Phi=\pi BR^{2}
, in units of the flux-quantum
\Phi_{0}=hc/e
(assuming the charge
q
to be measured in units of
e
). The
P_{\pm}
are then written as:

c\,P_{\pm}=\pm\,\frac{\hbar c}{R}e^{\pm i\varphi}\,\left(\partial_{\varphi}-i\beta\right)\,,

(9)

so that the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
1
) becomes:

H=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}mc^{2}&-\frac{\hbar c}{R}e^{-i\varphi}\left(\partial_{\varphi}-i\beta\right)\\
\frac{\hbar c}{R}e^{+i\varphi}\left(\partial_{\varphi}-i\beta\right)&-mc^{2}\end{array}\right).

(10)

The time dependent Dirac equation with the 2-dimensional Hamiltonian in Eq. (
1
):

i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}=H\psi,

(11)

admits a continuity equation which,
for a generic two-component spinor state
\psi=(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})^{T}
, can be written as:

\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{J}=0,

(12)

where the probability density is given by:

\rho=\psi^{\dagger}\,\psi=\chi_{1}^{*}\chi_{1}^{\phantom{*}}+\chi_{2}^{*}\chi_{2}^{\phantom{*}},

(13)

and the current
\bm{J}
is

\bm{J}=c\,\psi^{\dagger}\bm{\sigma}\,\psi\,.

(14)

The explicit expression of the current in Eq. (
14
) is the usual
U(1)
current of the Dirac equation minimally coupled to the electromagnetic field. The two-dimensional time dependent Dirac equation in Eq. (
11
) is written in manifestly covariant form by introducing a set of two-dimensional gamma matrices
\gamma^{\mu}
as
\gamma^{0}=\sigma^{3}
,
\gamma^{i}=\sigma^{3}\sigma^{i}
, (
i=1,2
) which satisfy the Clifford algebra. Then, a straightforward application of Noether’s theorem to the global phase symmetry provides the well known
U(1)
current
j^{\mu}=\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi
with
\bar{\psi}=\psi^{\dagger}\gamma^{0}
which is conserved
\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}=0
. This last condition, when written out explicitly
in terms of its time and space components, reproduces exactly Eqs. (
12
,
13
,
14
). We have also checked that the same result is obtained by combining the time dependent equation with its adjoint using the explicit form of the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
10
).

We now proceed to compute the explicit form of the
current in terms of its azimuthal (
J_{\varphi}
) and radial (
J_{r}
) components
\bm{J}=J_{\varphi}\bm{e}_{\varphi}+J_{r}\bm{e}_{r}
, with
\bm{e}_{\varphi}
and
\bm{e}_{r}
being respectively the unit vectors of the azimuthal and radial directions:
\bm{e}_{\varphi}=-\sin\varphi\,\bm{e}_{x}+\cos\varphi\,\bm{e}_{y}
, and
\bm{e}_{r}=\cos\varphi\,\bm{e}_{x}+\sin\varphi\,\bm{e}_{y}
. We also compute the divergence of the current
\nabla\cdot\bm{J}
for the Dirac
fermion constrained to move on the ring of radius
R
for which system the gradient operator is simply
\nabla=(\bm{e}_{\varphi}/R)\partial_{\varphi}
. A straightforward calculation shows that:

\displaystyle{\bm{\nabla}}\cdot\bm{J}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{R}J_{r}+\frac{1}{R}\frac{\partial J_{\varphi}}{\partial\varphi},

(15a)

\displaystyle J_{r}

\displaystyle=c(e^{-i\varphi}\chi_{1}^{*}\chi_{2}^{\phantom{*}}+c.c.),

(15b)

\displaystyle J_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=c(-ie^{-i\varphi}\chi_{1}^{*}\chi_{2}^{\phantom{*}}+c.c.).

(15c)

The spectrum and corresponding eigenfunctions can be easily obtained and are given by (positive energy branch):

\displaystyle E_{\ell}

\displaystyle={\sqrt{m^{2}c^{4}+\left(\frac{\hbar c}{R}\right)^{2}(\ell-\beta)(\ell-\beta+1)}}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{\ell}\,mc^{2}\qquad\qquad\ell=0,\pm 1,\pm 2\cdots

(16a)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{\lambdabar_{C}}{R}\right)^{2}(\ell-\beta)(\ell-\beta+1)}

(16b)

with
\lambdabar_{C}=\hbar/(mc)
the reduced Compton wavelength of the particle of mass
m
.
The eigenstates of the Hamiltonian are:

\psi_{\ell}={\mathcal{N}}_{\ell}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}e^{i\ell\varphi}\\
\frac{\hbar c/R}{E_{\ell}+mc^{2}}\,i\left(\ell-\beta\right)\,e^{i(\ell+1)\varphi}\end{array}\right)

(17)

with
{\mathcal{N}}_{\ell}
a normalization constant which, without loss of generality, can be taken to be real. It can be easily verified that the eigenstates are orthogonal and the normalisation constant
{\mathcal{N}}_{\ell}
can be obtained from the standard condition

\oint_{{\mathcal{C}}_{R}}dz~\psi_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\psi_{\ell}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}R~d\varphi\,\psi_{\ell^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(\varphi)\psi_{\ell}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}(\varphi)=\delta_{\ell^{\prime}\ell}\,,

(18)

{\mathcal{C}}_{R}
being the ring of radius
R
. The result for the normalisation constant
{\mathcal{N}}_{\ell}
can be cast as:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{R}}\,A_{\ell}\,;

(19a)

\displaystyle A_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left[1+{\left(\frac{\hbar c}{R}\right)^{2}\frac{(\ell-\beta)^{2}}{(E_{\ell}+mc^{2})^{2}}}\right]^{-1/2}\,.

(19b)

It may be pointed out that the eigenstates (
17
) are also eigenstates of
J_{z}=L_{z}+\frac{\hbar}{2}\sigma_{z}
with eigenvalues
\hbar(\ell+\frac{1}{2})
. Note that in the nonrelativistic limit (
c\to\infty
)
we have
E_{\ell}\to mc^{2}
and
\lambdabar_{\text{C}}\to 0
and
A_{\ell}\to 1/\sqrt{2\pi}
.
On the other end in the ultra-relativistic limit (
E_{\ell}\gg mc^{2})
we obtain:

A_{\ell}\longrightarrow\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(1+\frac{\ell-\beta}{\ell-\beta+1}\right)^{-1/2}\,.

(20)

The eigenstate
\psi_{\ell}
in Eq. (
17
) has a constant azimuthal current:

J_{\varphi}^{(\ell)}=\frac{A_{\ell}^{2}}{E_{\ell}+mc^{2}}\,\frac{2\hbar c^{2}}{R^{2}}\,{\left(\ell-\beta\right)}\,.

(21)

Note that, denoting by
\lceil x\rceil
the Ceiling function of
x
, if
\ell\geq\lceil\beta\rceil
then the azimuthal current is positive semi-definite,
J_{\varphi}^{(\ell)}\geq 0
. The nonrelativistic limit of the azimuthal current in Eq. (
21
) reduces to

J_{\varphi}^{(\ell)}\to\frac{\hbar}{2\pi mR^{2}}\,{\left(\ell-\beta\right)}

(22)

which coincides exactly with the nonrelativistic current discussed in
Goussev 2021
. On the other end it is worthwhile pointing out that for the eigenstate
\psi_{\ell}
the corresponding radial current
J_{r}^{(\ell)}
vanishes identically.

III
Relativistic Quantum Backflow on a Ring

In this section we discuss the quantum backflow of the relativistic fermion constrained on the ring of radius
R
. We follow closely the approach discussed in
Goussev 2021
for the nonrelativistic particle highlighting differences and similarities.

We consider a generic physical state given by a linear combination of the energy eigenstates
\psi_{\ell}(\varphi,t)
with
\ell\geq\lceil\beta\rceil
:

\Psi(\varphi,t)=\sum_{\ell=\lceil\beta\rceil}^{+\infty}\,c_{\ell}\,\psi_{\ell}(\varphi,t)

(23)

which we assume to be normalized, and thus the coefficients
c_{\ell}
satisfy:

\sum_{\ell=\lceil\beta\rceil}^{+\infty}\,|c_{\ell}|^{2}\,=1\,.

(24)

Before proceeding further, let us mention that as the particle is constrained to move on the circumference of a circle of fixed radius
R
, we shall consider only the azimuthal current
J_{\varphi}
and we will investigate the azimuthal backflow in line with what was done in ref.
Strange 2012
. In this context it may also be noted that, although the radial current does not vanish in general, the total radial flux across the ring can be shown to always be zero. This is easily understood since the radial current turns out to be a periodic function of
\varphi
, see the Appendix for details.

The azimuthal probability current
J_{\varphi}^{(\Psi)}(\varphi,t)
corresponding to the state
\Psi
in Eq. (
23
) through Eq. (
15c
) reads:

\displaystyle J^{(\Psi)}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar c^{2}}{R^{2}}\!\!\!\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}=\lceil\beta\rceil}^{\infty}c_{\ell}^{*}A^{\phantom{*}}_{\ell}\left[\frac{{{\ell-\beta}}}{E_{\ell}+mc^{2}}+\frac{{\ell^{\prime}-\beta}}{E_{\ell^{\prime}}+mc^{2}}\right]A_{\ell^{\prime}}c_{\ell^{\prime}}

(25)

\displaystyle\times e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}(E_{\ell}-E_{\ell^{\prime}})t}e^{-i(\ell-\ell^{\prime})\varphi}\,.

For the state
\Psi
the integrated probability flowing through a fixed point (say
\varphi=0
) in the azimuthal direction (
P_{\Psi})
in a time window
[-T/2,T/2]
is :

P_{\Psi}=\int_{-\frac{T}{2}}^{+\frac{T}{2}}\,dt\,J_{\varphi}^{(\Psi)}(0,t)\,,

(26)

and
for the state in Eq. (
23
) given the time dependence of the current in Eq. (
25
) we just need the time integral:

\displaystyle\int_{-\frac{T}{2}}^{+\frac{T}{2}}e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}\left(E_{\ell}-E_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)t}dt

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}T&\ell=\ell^{\prime}\\
\hbar\left.\displaystyle\frac{e^{{\frac{i}{\hbar}}\left(E_{\ell}-E_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)t}}{i\left(E_{\ell}-E_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)}\right|_{-\frac{T}{2}}^{+\frac{T}{2}}&\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle=T\,\text{sinc}\left[\frac{T\,\left(E_{\ell}-E_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)}{2\hbar}\right]

so that we finally obtain
P_{\Psi}
in the form:

P_{\Psi}=\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}=\lceil\beta\rceil}^{+\infty}c_{\ell}^{*}\,K_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\,c_{\ell^{\prime}}

(28)

with

\displaystyle K_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar c^{2}T}{R^{2}}\,A_{\ell}\,\left(\frac{{{\ell-\beta}}}{E_{\ell}+mc^{2}}+\frac{{{\ell^{\prime}-\beta}}}{E_{\ell^{\prime}}+mc^{2}}\right)A_{\ell^{\prime}}\times

(29)

\displaystyle\phantom{xxxx}\text{sinc}\left[\frac{T(E_{\ell}-E_{\ell^{\prime}})}{2\hbar}\right]\,.

Defining the dimensionless quantity:

\alpha=\frac{\hbar T}{4mR^{2}}

(30)

the kernel
K_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}
can be rewritten:

\displaystyle K_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)\,4\pi\,A_{\ell}\,\left(\frac{\ell-\beta}{1+\varepsilon_{\ell}}+\frac{\ell^{\prime}-\beta}{1+\varepsilon_{\ell^{\prime}}}\right)A_{\ell^{\prime}}\times

(31)

\displaystyle\phantom{xxxx}\text{sinc}\left[2\alpha\left(\frac{R}{\lambdabar_{\text{C}}}\right)^{2}(\varepsilon_{\ell}-\varepsilon_{\ell^{\prime}})\right]\,.

We begin the study of the relativistic backflow on the ring by considering a quantum state with only two eigenstates,
\psi_{\ell_{1}}
and
\psi_{\ell_{2}}
with
\lceil\beta\rceil\leq\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}
. Thus in Eq. (
23
) we parametrize the coefficients
c_{\ell}
as:

c_{\ell}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\cos\frac{\delta}{2}&\quad\ell=\ell_{1}\geq\lceil\beta\rceil\\
e^{i\gamma}\sin\frac{\delta}{2}&\quad\ell=\ell_{2}>\ell_{1}\\
0&\quad\ell\neq\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\end{array}\right.

(32)

with
0\leq\delta\leq\pi
and
0\leq\gamma<2\pi
. This choice of parameters
will ensure that the normalization condition in Eq. (
24
) is satisfied. Inserting Eq. (
32
) into Eq. (
28
) using either Eq. (
29
) or Eq. (
31
) we obtain:

P_{\Psi}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}=\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\Big[A-B\cos\delta+C\,\text{sinc}(D)\cos\gamma\sin\delta\Big]

(33)

where the quantities
A,B,C
and
D
are given by:

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle A=4\pi\left(A_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\,\frac{\ell_{2}-\beta}{1+\varepsilon_{\ell_{2}}}+A_{\ell_{1}}^{2}\frac{\ell_{1}-\beta}{1+\varepsilon_{\ell_{1}}}\,\right)\\
\displaystyle B=4\pi\left(A_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\,\frac{\ell_{2}-\beta}{1+\varepsilon_{\ell_{2}}}-A_{\ell_{1}}^{2}\frac{\ell_{1}-\beta}{1+\varepsilon_{\ell_{1}}}\,\right)\\
\displaystyle C=4\pi A_{\ell_{2}}A_{\ell_{1}}\left(\frac{\ell_{2}-\beta}{1+\varepsilon_{\ell_{2}}}+\frac{\ell_{1}-\beta}{1+\varepsilon_{\ell_{1}}}\right)\\
\displaystyle D=2\alpha\left(\frac{R}{\lambdabar_{\text{C}}}\right)^{2}\left(\varepsilon_{\ell_{2}}-\varepsilon_{\ell_{1}}\right)\,.\end{array}

(34)

We then perform a minimisation procedure over the parameters
\delta,\gamma
:

{\mathcal{P}}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}(\alpha,\beta)=\min\nolimits_{\left\{\delta,\gamma\right\}}P^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}_{\Psi}(\delta,\gamma)

(35)

and, similarly to what is found in
Goussev 2021
, the result is:

{\mathcal{P}}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\left(A-\sqrt{B^{2}+C^{2}\,\text{sinc}^{2}(D)}\right)\,.

(36)

Note however that the coefficients
A,B,C
and
D
have a different definitions with respect to that of the nonrelativistic problem.

III.1
General Formulation

Here we shall consider a more general superposition of eigenstates, and study the corresponding problem of maximizing the integrated probability flux through
\varphi=0
, given in Eq. (
28
),
i.e. maximize the quantum backflow considering a generic state as in Eq. (
23
) subject to the normalization constraint Eq. (
24
).
A comment is in order here about a symmetry of our system. Indeed it can easily be checked that the eigenvalues, cf. Eq. (
16
), satisfy the following relation:

E_{\ell}^{(\beta)}=E_{\ell-1}^{(\beta-1)}

(37)

and the same is true for the normalization constants, Eq. (
19b
). This in turn implies that the quantity
{\mathcal{P}}_{\psi}
as given in Eq. (
28
) is invariant under the transformation
\beta\to\beta-1
and
c_{\ell}\to c_{\ell+1}
(or equivalently
\beta\to\beta+1
and
c_{\ell}\to c_{\ell-1}
). Therefore, it will be enough to consider
\beta
in the interval:

\beta\in(-1,0]\qquad\text{with}\qquad\lceil\beta\rceil=0\,.

(38)

Hereafter we shall therefore assume
\lceil\beta\rceil=0
.

As also discussed in
Goussev 2021
the maximal backflow can be found defining a real-valued functional of the set of coefficients
\left\{c_{\ell}\right\}
by:

I\left[c_{\ell}\right]=\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}c_{\ell}^{*}K_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}c_{\ell^{\prime}}-\lambda\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}c_{\ell}^{*}c_{\ell}

(39)

and performing an unconstrained minimization
where
\lambda
plays the role of a Lagrange multiplier. The variation of the functional
I[\left\{c_{\ell}\right\}]
with respect to independent variations
\delta c_{\ell}
and
\delta c_{\ell}^{*}
produces the corresponding eigenvalue problem (Euler-Lagrange equations):

\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}K_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}c_{\ell^{\prime}}=\lambda\,c_{\ell}

(40)

so that the maximal amount of quantum backflow is attained when the value of
P_{\Psi}
, Eq. (
28
), reaches its infimum and this coincides with the lowest eigenvalue of the spectrum {
\lambda
} of the unconstrained variational problem:

\mathcal{P}(\alpha,\beta,\frac{\lambdabar_{\text{C}}}{R})\equiv\inf_{\Psi}P_{\Psi}=\inf\{\lambda\}\,.

(41)

We note that the minimisation procedure leading to Eq. (
40
) and Eq. (
41
), i.e. that the maximal amount of backflow is related to the lowest (negative) eigenvalue of the kernel in Eq. (
31
) is a simple application of the min-max (Rayleigh quotient) theorem
Gelfand and Fomin 2012
.

We conclude this subsection with a comment regarding the fact that the presence of a nonvanishing radial current does not affect at all the analysis of the azimuthal backflow presented here. First of all, we note that since the kernel
K_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}
is real and symmetric the eigenvectors of the eigenvalue problem in Eq. (
40
) are real. This means that the state
\Psi
maximising the azimuthal backflow is given by Eq. (
23
) with all coefficients
c_{\ell}
real.
For a state
\Psi
built with real coefficients the integral of the radial current at
\varphi=0
,
J_{r}(0,t)
, over a symmetric time interval always vanishes, see Eq. (
58
), whereas the corresponding time integrated azimuthal current (cf.
P_{\Psi}
in Eq. (
23
)) reaches its infimum value (its absolute value is maximised). Therefore any worry that a nonzero radial component of the current might affect the azimuthal backflow analysis presented here should be discarded at once.
At last, we also note that
\varphi=0
is just one point on the ring and therefore given the axial symmetry of the problem at hand, the above conclusion will hold as well at any other point.

In the next subsection we will discuss the numerical solution of the eigenvalue equation in Eq. (
40
), then in the next section we will address the line limit of backflow on a ring which is obviously reached when
R\to\infty
.

III.2
Numerical analysis

Following the procedure described in
Goussev 2021
, we truncate the sum in Eq. (
40

IV
Line limit

We discuss here the line limit of the quantum backflow on the ring. Let us consider the limit
R\to\infty
of the eigenvalue equation, Eq. (
40
), with
K_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}
given in Eq. (
31
). We note that:

\varepsilon_{\ell}=\sqrt{1+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{m^{2}c^{2}R^{2}}\frac{1}{\alpha}\,\sqrt{\alpha}(\ell-\beta)\sqrt{\alpha}(\ell-\beta+1)}\,.

(47)

The line limit is reached when
\alpha\to 0
with
\sqrt{\alpha}\beta\to 0
so that using
\alpha=\hbar T/(4mR^{2})
we have:

\varepsilon_{\ell}\to\sqrt{1+\frac{4\hbar T^{-1}}{mc^{2}}\,(\sqrt{\alpha}\ell)^{2}}

(48)

and with the definitions:

\epsilon=\sqrt{\frac{4\hbar T^{-1}}{mc^{2}}}\,,\qquad z=\sqrt{\alpha}\,\ell

(49)

the line limit of the quantity
\varepsilon_{\ell}
is:

\varepsilon_{\ell}\to\sqrt{1+\epsilon^{2}z^{2}}\equiv\gamma(z)\,.

(50)

A little algebra shows, with the same reasoning, that the coefficients
A_{\ell}
in eq. (
19b
) reduce to:

A_{\ell}\to\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sqrt{\frac{\gamma(z)+1}{2\gamma(z)}}\,.

(51)

With this in mind,
the eigenvalue equation, Eq. (
40
), can be first manipulated by distributing
\sqrt{\alpha}
in the numerators of the two terms in the round brackets in Eq. (
31
). We thus find:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\,\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}\,4\pi\,A_{\ell}\,\left[\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}(\ell-\beta)}{1+\varepsilon_{\ell}}+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}(\ell^{\prime}-\beta)}{1+\varepsilon_{\ell^{\prime}}}\right]A_{\ell^{\prime}}\times

\displaystyle\phantom{xxxxxxxxxxx}\text{Sinc}\left[\frac{2}{\epsilon^{2}}(\varepsilon_{\ell}-\varepsilon_{\ell^{\prime}})\right]c_{\ell^{\prime}}=\lambda\frac{c_{\ell}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}

(52)

so that taking the limit
\alpha\to 0
by defining the variables
z=\sqrt{\alpha}\,\ell
,
z^{\prime}=\sqrt{\alpha}\,\ell^{\prime}
, the function
f(z)=c_{\ell}/\sqrt{\alpha}
, using the results in Eqs. (
49
,
50
,
51
) and replacing the discrete sum over
\ell^{\prime}
by an integral over the variable
z^{\prime}
one finds:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\!\!\!dz^{\prime}\,\sqrt{\frac{\gamma(z)+1}{\gamma(z)}}\,\left[\frac{z}{1+\gamma(z)}+\frac{z^{\prime}}{1+\gamma(z^{\prime})}\right]\sqrt{\frac{\gamma(z^{\prime})+1}{\gamma(z^{\prime})}}

\displaystyle\phantom{xxx}\times\text{sinc}\left[\frac{2}{\epsilon^{2}}(\gamma(z)-\gamma(z^{\prime}))\right]\,f(z^{\prime})=\lambda\,f(z)

(53)

that can be straightforwardly rearranged into

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\!\!\!dz^{\prime}\,\frac{z(\gamma(z^{\prime})+1)+z^{\prime}(\gamma(z)+1)}{\sqrt{\gamma(z)(\gamma(z)+1)\,\gamma(z^{\prime})(\gamma(z^{\prime})+1)}}\phantom{xxxxxxxx}

\displaystyle\phantom{xxxxx}\times\text{sinc}\left[\frac{2}{\epsilon^{2}}(\gamma(z)-\gamma(z^{\prime}))\right]\,f(z^{\prime})=\lambda\,f(z)

(54)

which coincides exactly with the integral eigenvalue equation for a relativistic spin-half fermion on the line discussed in
Melloy and Bracken 1998
;
Ashfaque et al. 2019
(cf. see their equation 14).

V
Discussion and Conclusions

In this paper we have presented a study of the quantum backflow of a charged relativistic massive fermion in a plane within a perpendicular, constant magnetic field and constrained to move on a ring of radius
R
. The same problem for a charged nonrelativistic spinless particle has also been studied
Goussev 2021
. We use the same approach to the problem presented in
Goussev 2021
highlighting differences and similarities with the results of the nonrelativistic analysis. In this context it may be noted that an experimental realization of the setup could be possible in a ring of a two dimensional material like gapped graphene where the quasiparticles are massive
Pereira et al. 2008
;
Belouad et al. 2018
.

Before recalling our main results we note that constraining the particle in 2+1 dimensions into a ring of radius
R
reduces the system to one which is effectively 1+1 dimensional. If we were to start from a 3+1 dimensional Dirac equation and fix the radial coordinate at
r=R
we would make the system effectively 2+1 dimensional (a relativistic particle moving on a sphere of radius
R
). This would somehow parallel previous studies,
Strange 2012
;
Paccoia et al. 2020
, (where a non-relativistic particle moves on a plane) where some issues were discussed such as that of a proper definition of the QB probability
Paccoia et al. 2020
. The treatment of the relativistic Dirac particle on a fixed radius sphere (again a two-dimensional system) could perhaps be addressed as well but it certainly goes beyond the scope of the present work.

The main finding reported here is that the maximal amount of backflow for the relativistic Dirac fermion on the ring is obtained when the radius of the ring is of the order of the reduced Compton wavelength of the particle,
\lambdabar_{\text{C}}/R=0.730
(with
\beta=0
and
\alpha/\pi\simeq 0.36252
) and it amounts to
c_{\text{Dring}}=0.092
. We have then discussed the line limit showing that the eigenvalue problem reduces to the integral equation for the quantum backflow of a relativistic particle on a line previously studied in the literature
Ashfaque et al. 2019
.

We have also computed the probability current flowing through the point
\varphi=0
as a function of time for the state that maximises the net flow in the time interval [
-T/2,+T/2
], finding, as expected, that it is always negative in this interval.

It is also interesting to compare the result in Eq. (
42
) with the corresponding result obtained in
Goussev 2021
for the nonrelativistic problem on the ring
c_{\text{ring non-rel}}=0.116816
. We note that the amount of backflow for the Dirac fermion on a ring is about 20% smaller than that of a nonrelativistic particle but still larger than the backflow value on the line
c_{\text{line}}=0.0389
.

It is interesting to find out how our results look in the nonrelativistic limit. To this end we refer the reader to Eqns.(
43
)-(
44
) which were used to compute the backflow. The results of this computation differ from those of
Goussev 2021
and this difference is due to the fact that the nonrelativistic limit of a constrained spin
1/2
Dirac system does not necessarily reduce to the Schrödinger equation for a spin
0
particle.

Finally, we provide preliminary estimates of the amount of backflow in the limit
\lambdabar_{\text{C}}/R\to\infty
which corresponds to the massless fermion case (
m\to 0)
. While we intend to study the massless case comprehensively in a separate work, these preliminary results in our opinion show that there is definitely quantum backflow for a massless Dirac fermion on a ring.

Radial current

The radial probability current
J_{r}^{(\Psi)}
as in Eq. (
15b
) corresponding to the general state
\Psi
in Eq. (
23
), is

J_{r}^{(\Psi)}(\varphi,t)=\frac{\hbar c^{2}}{R^{2}}\!\!\!\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}=\lceil\beta\rceil}^{\infty}\operatorname{Re}\,i\,c_{\ell^{\prime}}^{*}A_{\ell^{\prime}}\left[\frac{{{\ell-\beta}}}{E_{\ell}+mc^{2}}-\frac{{\ell^{\prime}-\beta}}{E_{\ell^{\prime}}+mc^{2}}\right]A_{\ell}c_{\ell}\,\,e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}(E_{\ell}-E_{\ell^{\prime}})t}\,\,e^{i(\ell-\ell^{\prime})\varphi}\,.

(55)

The probability flux over the ring
{\mathcal{C}}_{R}
along the radial direction is given by:

\oint_{{\mathcal{C}}_{R}}dz~\bm{J}^{(\Psi)}\cdot\bm{e}_{r}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}R~d\varphi\,J_{r}^{(\Psi)}(\varphi,t)\,.

(56)

Integrating
\varphi
over the interval
\left[0,2\pi\right]
results in

\oint_{{\mathcal{C}}_{R}}dz~\bm{J}^{(\Psi)}\cdot\bm{e}_{r}=2\pi\frac{\hbar c^{2}}{R}\!\!\!\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}=\lceil\beta\rceil}^{\infty}\!\!\operatorname{Re}\,i\,c_{\ell^{\prime}}^{*}A_{\ell^{\prime}}\left[\frac{{{\ell-\beta}}}{E_{\ell}+mc^{2}}-\frac{{\ell^{\prime}-\beta}}{E_{\ell^{\prime}}+mc^{2}}\right]A_{\ell}\,c_{\ell}\,\,e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}(E_{\ell}-E_{\ell^{\prime}})t}\,e^{i(\ell-\ell^{\prime})\pi}\,\text{Sinc}\left[\left(\ell-\ell^{\prime}\right)\pi\right]\,.

(57)

In the expansion in Eq. (
57
) each term is equal to
0
both for
\ell=\ell^{\prime}
and
\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}
, which means that the total radial probability flux over the ring always vanishes for the most general state
\Psi
given by Eq. (
23
).
It is interesting to note that in a non-relativistic setting results similar to ours can be observed. For example, it can be shown that in the framework of ref
Strange 2012
the radial current for an individual eigenstate vanishes while it is non vanishing for a general superposition state and the total radial flux is zero.
It can also be easily shown that if the state
\Psi
given by Eq. (
23
) is chosen with general complex coefficients
c_{\ell}=|c_{\ell}|e^{i\gamma_{\ell}}
than the integral of
J^{\Psi}_{r}(0,t)
over a symmetric time integral can be cast as:

\int_{-T/2}^{+T/2}dtJ^{\Psi}_{r}(0,t)=-\frac{\hbar c^{2}}{R^{2}}T\!\!\!\sum_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}=\lceil\beta\rceil}^{\infty}|c_{\ell^{\prime}}|A_{\ell^{\prime}}\left[\frac{{{\ell-\beta}}}{E_{\ell}+mc^{2}}-\frac{{\ell^{\prime}-\beta}}{E_{\ell^{\prime}}+mc^{2}}\right]A_{\ell}|c_{\ell}|\,\text{Sin}\left(\gamma_{\ell}-\gamma_{\ell^{\prime}}\right)\text{Sinc}\left[\frac{(E_{\ell}-E_{\ell}^{\prime})T}{2\hbar}\right]\,\,

(58)

and vanishes identically when all the coefficients
c_{\ell}
are real i.e.
\gamma_{\ell}=0
or
\pi
. The fact that with real coefficients the integral of
J_{r}(0,t)
, over a symmetric time interval, vanishes is to be compared with what happens for the corresponding azimuthal component of the current which defines
P_{\Psi}
, defined in Eq. (
26
), whose absolute value is instead maximised in the quantum backflow analysis by a state characterised by real coefficients. We notice that in
Barbier et al. 2023
the authors discuss the azimuthal quantum backflow of a non relativistic particle confined in a disk of radius
R
punctured in the center by a magnetic flux line perpendicular to the disk, i.e. the particle moves in a Aharonov-Bohm like potential.
We point out that the azimuthal backflow analysis in this truly two-dimensional system is carried out notwithstanding the presence of a radial current
Barbier et al. 2023
. This radial current, while vanishing for the eigenstates, is
non vanishing
for a general superposition with complex coefficients similarly to what happens in the analysis of ref.
Strange 2012
and in the present work as well.

Accelerating Universe in Terms of Hankel Function Index
K. Suratgar, M. Mohsenzadeh, E. Yusofi, F. Taghizadeh-Farahmand

Abstract
In this paper, F ⁡ ( ν ) F(\nu) cosmology is proposed for the accelerating universe with asymptotic de Sitter expansion in terms of Hankel function index ν \nu . To some extent, both the initial expansion during early inflation and the current accelerated expansion can be studied with a vacuum cosmic fluid i.e. Λ \Lambda in the pure de Sitter phase. Observational data further support the notion of a quasi-vacuum fluid, rather than a pure vacuum, contributing to the quasi-de Sitter acceleration in both the early and late universe. By examining the asymptotic expansion of the Henkel function as an approximate solution of the Mukhanov-Sasaki equation, we seek a more detailed study of quasi-de Sitter solutions in cosmology containing vacuum-like fluid. I
Introduction and Motivation

Inflation is the first period of rapid cosmic expansion in the very early universe. Its exponential expansion is thought to be responsible for the present large-scale homogeneity and isotropy
Guth 1987
;
Liddle 1999
. The important achievement of this scenario is the prediction of the almost constant power spectrum of the primary quantum fluctuations. This theoretical prediction is supported by what we have measured today in cosmic fluid perturbations. With the limited information that we can extract from the analysis of these spectra, we will be faced with a wide range of acceptable inflation scenarios, but unfortunately, we have not reached a unified model of it up to now
Guth 2003
;
Linde 2018
.

Another accelerated phase of the expansion of the universe is related to the post-matter-dominant phase during the current universe
Nojiri and Odintsov 2004
;
Nojiri et al. 2017
. ”Dark energy” is an unknown component of an energy source that causes this late cosmic acceleration. Observational data from Type Ia supernovae (SNe), large-scale structure (LSS), baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO), and cosmic microwave background radiation (CMBR) have confirmed the existence of large-scale accelerative expansion with very good accuracy
Amendola and Tsujikawa 2010
.

The recent CMB results from Planck satellite
Ade et al. 2014
;
Ade et al. 2016
;
Akrami et al. 2020
, and data from the
Wilkinson Microwave Anisotropy Probe (WMAP), impose an important
constraint on the value of scalar spectral index. The most recent constrains on
n_{s}
and tensor-to-scalar ratio
r
, are as follows
Akrami et al. 2020
,

n_{s}={0.9647}\pm{0.0044}\quad,\qquad r<0.16

. In

Suratgar et al. 2022
, we have considered this constraint to show that the index
of Hankel function, lies in the range of

1.5076\leq\nu\leq 1.5098

.

The standard Bunch-Davies (BD) mode
Baumann 2011
is used just for the
pure de Sitter(dS) space-time i.e., it is obtained by exactly
setting
\nu={1.5000}
, thus the above mentioned range of index
\nu
,
motivate us to consider quasi-de Sitter modes with
\nu\neq{1.5000}
. In our previous works
Mohsenzadeh et al. 2014
;
Mohsenzadeh et al. 2015
;
Yusofi and Mohsenzadeh 2014a
and
Yusofi and Mohsenzadeh 2014b
;
Yusofi and Mohsenzadeh 2015
;
Yusofi 2018
;
Yusofi and Mohsenzadeh 2018
, we utilized an approximate method as well as the asymptotic expansion of Hankel functions to obtain these alternative modes.

Hankel functions
H_{\nu}^{(1)}(x)
and
H_{\nu}^{(2)}(x)
, are defined by

H_{\nu}^{(1)}(x)=B_{\nu}(x)+iN_{\nu}(x)

H_{\nu}^{(2)}(x)=B_{\nu}(x)-iN_{\nu}(x),

(1)

where
i=\sqrt{-1}
. These two linear combinations are also known as

Bessel functions of the third kind
.
B_{\nu}(x)
and
N_{\nu}(x)
are the Bessel functions of the
first and second kind, respectively
Arfken et al. 1985
;
Abramowitz and Stegun 1974
. Depending on the positive or negative sign of the frequency, the physical application of the Hankel functions is to express outward and inward propagating cylindrical or spherical solutions of the wave equation. Also, they have been used to express the general solutions of the Mukhanov-Sasaki equation during cosmic inflation in theoretical cosmology
Baumann 2011
;
Baumann 2018
;
Mukhanov 2013
.

By considering the asymptotic expansion of the Hankel function at the far past time limit, we can derive alternative solutions for early inflation. For asymptotic de Sitter modes with
\nu\neq{1.5000}
, we have expanded the range of possibilities beyond just the pure de Sitter space-time of the standard Bunch-Davies (BD) mode. Our research focuses on these alternative modes of the early universe, and we have derived the cosmic expansion, equation of state, and power spectrum in terms of the index
\nu
. The method adopted in this study may provide new insights into the dynamics of the early universe and have important implications for cosmology from a geometrical point of view.

So in section

II
Initial Asymptotic de Sitter Modes

In terms of gauge invariant potentials
\Phi
and
\Psi
, the diagonal metric in the perturbed form of Newtonian gauge for the early universe can be written as follows
Baumann 2018
;
Linde 2008
;
Guth 2003
,

\displaystyle ds^{2}=a^{2}(\tau)(-(1+2\Phi){d\tau}^{2}+(1-2\Psi){d\textbf{x}}^{2}).

(2)

The following action shows the dynamics of quantum fluctuations of the scalar inflationary field
Baumann 2018

S=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{3}xd\tau\left((u^{\prime})^{2}-(\nabla u)^{2}+\frac{z^{\prime\prime}}{z}u^{2}\right).

(3)

Considering variables
u
and
z\equiv\frac{a\dot{\phi}}{H}
in the Fourier space the equation of motion for inflaton perturbations can be written as the following
Mukhanov-Sasaki
equation
Stewart and Lyth 1993
;
Baumann 2011

{u}_{k}^{\prime\prime}+\left(k^{2}-\frac{z^{\prime\prime}}{z}\right)u_{k}=0.

(4)

u_{k}
is the Fourier mode of quantum field and the prime symbol represents the derivative with respect to conformal time
\tau
is in the range
(-\infty,\tau_{0}]
. Also we can write
\frac{z^{\prime\prime}}{z}
in terms of index
\nu
as
Stewart and Lyth 1993
;
Yusofi and Mohsenzadeh 2014b
,

\frac{z^{\prime\prime}}{z}\propto\frac{a^{\prime\prime}}{a}=\frac{\nu^{2}-1/4}{\tau^{2}}.

(5)

The general solution of the equation (
4
) can be written as a linear combination of the Henkel functions of the first and second kind
H_{\nu}^{(1)}
and
H_{\nu}^{(2)}
as follows,
Baumann 2018
;
Linde 2008
;
Guth 2003
:

u_{k}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi\tau}}{2}\Big(A_{k}H_{\nu}^{(1)}(|k\tau|)+B_{k}H_{\nu}^{(2)}(|k\tau|)\Big).

(6)

Using the asymptotic expansion of the Hankel function, we can obtain an alternative solution for the equation (

-i\frac{(4\nu^{2}-1)(4\nu^{2}-9)(4\nu^{2}-25)}{3!(8|k\tau|)^{3}}-...].

(7)

II.2
Introducing of F(
\nu

For asymptotic solutions (

F(\nu,\tau)=\frac{1}{2}.\frac{2\nu+1}{(2\nu-1)}+(2\nu+1)^{2}\frac{(4\nu^{2}-9)^{2}}{64k^{2}\tau^{2}}+....

(9)

As can be seen from relation (
9

F(\nu)=\frac{1}{2}.\frac{2\nu+1}{(2\nu-1)}.

(10)

II.3
Correspondence between background geometry and Hankel index
\nu

We claim that the parameter
\nu

\nu
in generalized solutions (
7

Heydarzadeh et al. 2021

Yusofi and Mohsenzadeh 2015

u_{k}^{\pm 1/2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2k}}e^{-ik\tau},

(11)

u_{k}^{\pm 3/2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2k}}(1-\frac{i}{k\tau})e^{-ik\tau},

(12)

u_{k}^{\pm 5/2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2k}}(1-\frac{3i}{k\tau}-\frac{3}{k^{2}\tau^{2}})e^{-ik\tau},

(13)

u_{k}^{\pm 7/2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2k}}(1-\frac{6i}{k\tau}-\frac{15}{k^{2}\tau^{2}}-\frac{15i}{k^{3}\tau^{3}})e^{-ik\tau}.

It is clearly seen that the wave function

represents the flat space-time mode function and

IV
Modified Form of Particle Creation in F(
\nu
) Cosmology

Using the Bogoliubov coefficients
Farley and D’Eath 2005
, the standard formula (
{\rm sta}
) for the initially created particles in terms of the vacuum modes
u_{k}
has been given by
Mijic 1998
;
Pereira et al. 2010
;
Rodriguez Roman 2020
,

\langle N\rangle_{\rm sta}=\frac{1}{4\omega_{k}(\tau)}|{u}^{\prime}_{k}(\tau)|^{2}+\frac{\omega_{k}(\tau)}{4}|{u}_{k}(\tau)|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}.

(31)

Usually, particles are created due to changes in the gravitational field caused by a curved or expanding background. In the standard view, flat space-time is considered to be the background that has the lowest energy and the minimum number of particles. Therefore, if we put the flat space-time state (
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2k}}e^{-ik\tau}
) instead of the state
u_{k}(\tau)
in the relation (
31
), the result
\langle N\rangle_{\rm sta}
vanishes. This means that in the standard method, the value
\frac{1}{2}
in the formula is due to choosing a flat background.

\langle N\rangle_{\rm flat}=\frac{1}{4\omega_{k}(\tau)}|{u_{k}^{\pm 1/2}}^{\prime}(\tau)|^{2}+\frac{\omega_{k}(\tau)}{4}|{u_{k}^{\pm 1/2}}(\tau)|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}

(32)

As a result of above discussion, we can write
Ziyaee et al. 2020
:

\langle N\rangle_{\rm sta}=\langle N\rangle_{\rm phy}-\langle N\rangle_{\rm bac}=\langle N\rangle_{\rm ads}-\langle N\rangle_{\rm flat}

(33)

So in terms of physical (phy), asymptotic de Sitter (ads) and background (bac) vacuum modes
u_{k}(\tau)
, we can write the relation (
31
) as follows

\langle N\rangle_{\rm sta}=\frac{1}{4\omega_{k}(\tau)}|{u^{\rm ads}}^{\prime}_{k}(\tau)|^{2}+\frac{\omega_{k}(\tau)}{4}|{u^{\rm ads}}_{k}(\tau)|^{2}-\frac{1}{4\omega_{k}(\tau)}|{u_{k}^{\rm flat}}^{\prime}(\tau)|^{2}+\frac{\omega_{k}(\tau)}{4}|{u_{k}^{\rm flat}}(\tau)|^{2}.

(34)

Choosing flat background in (
33
) has two following problems;

(1) Covariance Problem: The formula for calculating of the number of particles in (
33
) does not have covariance under curved space-time symmetry
Ziyaee et al. 2020
;
Ziyaee et al. 2021
.

(2) Negative Number Problem: According to relation (
34
), for some values of
\nu
that related some curved space-times, we reach a negative value for the number of particles i.e.
\langle N\rangle_{\rm sta}<0
. See this issue more clearly in
Yusofi and Mohsenzadeh 2018
;
Ziyaee et al. 2020
.

Our suggestion to modify of relation (
34
) has two steps. First, both vacuum modes must be selected from curved (cur), and the covariant (cov) form of the formula changes as,

\langle N\rangle_{\rm cov}=\langle N\rangle_{\rm phy}-\langle N\rangle_{\rm cur},

(35)

The second step in our efforts is to solve the issue of negative number particles. It is crucial for us to consider the background space-time that is related to the one with the minimum number of particles, as highlighted in the
Ziyaee et al. 2021
. Based on the analysis of this paper, we have developed a final modified (mod) formula for particle creation during asymptotic de Sitter inflation. The result formula is as follows:

\langle N\rangle_{\rm mod}=\langle N\rangle_{\rm ads}-\langle N\rangle_{\rm min}

(36)

or in terms of vacuum modes
Ziyaee et al. 2021
,

\langle N\rangle_{\rm mod}=\frac{1}{4\omega_{k}(\tau)}|{u^{\rm ads}}^{\prime}_{k}(\tau)|^{2}+\frac{\omega_{k}(\tau)}{4}|{u^{\rm ads}}_{k}(\tau)|^{2}-\frac{1}{4\omega_{k}(\tau)}|{u^{\rm min}}^{\prime}_{k}(\tau)|^{2}+\frac{\omega_{k}(\tau)}{4}|{u^{\rm min}}_{k}(\tau)|^{2}.

(37)

VI
Discussions and Conclusions

VI.1
Quasi-de Sitter Space-time, Primordal Gravitational Wave and Particle Creation

For several reasons, in recent years, the attention of physicists, especially cosmologists, has been drawn to de Sitter space-time. Among these reasons, apart from the maximum symmetry, is the very simple geometry of this space-time, which makes it a great experimental model for studying the physics of the universe. Also, in terms of observations, cosmic inflation is a quasi-exponential expansion that results from solving de Sitter of Friedmann’s equations.
As mentioned in the introduction, the observations show that in the inflationary period, space-time is not pure de Sitter, but in the approximation of the first order, it is pure de Sitter. So with this view and according to the background field method, The quasi (asymptotic) de Sitter solutions (
13
) and (
14
) can be obtained from the effect of gravitational wave fluctuations
\hat{h}_{\mu\nu}
in the primordial unperturbed background
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
as two following methods
Mohsenzadeh et al. 2014
;
Mohsenzadeh et al. 2009
;
Ziyaee et al. 2020
;
Ziyaee et al. 2021
,

\hat{g}_{\rm\mu\nu}=\bar{g}^{\rm flat}_{\rm\mu\nu}+\hat{h}^{\rm(2)}_{\rm\mu\nu}

(43)

and

\hat{g}_{\rm\mu\nu}=\bar{g}^{\rm dS}_{\rm\mu\nu}+\hat{h}^{\rm(1)}_{\rm\mu\nu}

(44)

where
\hat{h}^{\rm(1)}_{\rm\mu\nu}
is a first order gravitational wave acts as a linear perturbation on the pure de Sitter background
\bar{g}^{\rm dS}_{\rm\mu\nu}
, while
\hat{h}^{\rm(2)}_{\rm\mu\nu}
acts as a second order (non-linear) perturbation on the pure flat background
\bar{g}^{\rm flat}_{\rm\mu\nu}
. In the (
44
), the total geometry is usually taken to be quasi-de Sitter, and the background geometry is pure de Sitter. Given that the geometry of the total universe is not pure de Sitter, but in the first approximation is pure de Sitter, therefore if we do the calculations using the standard method, in fact, we have just reached the first approximation of the geometry of the universe and not the actual geometry of the total universe. If we use the method (
44
), although we may not have reached the true geometry of the total universe, the result is closer to the physical world than the method (
43
).

Calculations with asymptotic solutions have shown that the effect of our proposed method (
44
) appears only when both physical and background vacuum sets are chosen from different curved space-times. Instead of the standard way to calculate the spectra, we have proposed that the vacuum states at the initial time are selected from the curved space-time, which is asymptotically very close to de Sitter space-time at the very early time period. The calculations carried out in the framework of asymptotic dS space-time have shown that the minimum of created particles are not related to flat space-time, but in asymptotic dS space-time the number of particles created by it is less than the flat one. In the special case of c = 2.34 (please see Fig. 3 in
Ziyaee et al. 2020
), we have the minimum number of particle creation. As an alternative proposal, we have chosen the background vacuum based on the minimum number of created particles in that space-time rather than flat space-time and we have modified the formula for the number of particle creation.

VI.2
Quasi-Vacuum Fluid, Cosmic Voids, Dark Matter and Dark Energy

It has been observed from present study that the equation of state parameter
w

w=-1

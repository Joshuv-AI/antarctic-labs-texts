Log K3 surfaces with irreducible boundary
János Kollár

Abstract
. We determine the automorphism group of an open log K3 surface with irreducible boundary.



August 24, 2026

A
log K3
surface over a field
k
is a pair
(S,N)
, where

S
is a projective surface over
k
such that
S_{\bar{k}}
is rational, and
N\in|-K_{S}|
is a curve with only nodes as singularities. These are frequently called
anticanonical pairs
or
log Calabi-Yau
surfaces.
We allow
S_{\bar{k}}
to have Du Val singularities away from
N
.
The field
k
can be arbitrary, but some minor issues are left to the reader in characteristic 2.

Our aim is to understand whether
the open surface
S\setminus N
determines the pair

(S,N)
.
As a first counterexample,
let
s\in N
be a node that is a smooth point of
S
, and
\pi:S^{\prime}\to S
its blow up with exceptional curve
E
. Then
(S^{\prime},N^{\prime}+E)
is another log K3 surface, and

S\setminus N\cong S^{\prime}\setminus(N^{\prime}+E)
.
Less obvious examples are given in (
10
.1–4).
These suggest that the best one can hope for is the following.

Theorem 1
.

Let
k
be a field and
(S_{i},N_{i})
smooth, log K3 surfaces over
k
with geometrically irreducible boundaries.
Then

[formula]

S_{1}\setminus N_{1}\cong S_{2}\setminus N_{2}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad(S_{1},N_{1})\cong(S_{2},N_{2}).

The natural approach to proving (
1
.1) would be to show that
every isomorphism

\phi^{\circ}:S_{1}\setminus N_{1}\cong S_{2}\setminus N_{2}

extends to an isomorphism

\phi:S_{1}\cong S_{2}
. This turns out to hold if
(N_{i}^{2})\leq 2
, but not otherwise.

The failure of this isomorphism-extension property is measured by the
difference between
\operatorname{Aut}(S\setminus N)
and
\operatorname{Aut}(S,N)
.
We show that there is a natural normal subgroup

\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(S\setminus N)\subset\operatorname{Aut}(S\setminus N)
such that

[formula]

\operatorname{Aut}(S\setminus N)\cong\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(S\setminus N)\rtimes\operatorname{Aut}(S,N).

It is easy to check that
\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(S\setminus N)
is trivial if either

N
is smooth or
(N^{2})\leq 0
; see (
18
) and (
19
).
We are thus left with the cases when
S
is a Del Pezo surface (
11
) and

N
is a nodal curve. We determine
\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(S\setminus N)
for all of them.

Theorem 2
.

Let
k
be a field,

S
a Del Pezzo surface over
k
and
N\in|-K_{S}|
a geometrically irreducible curve with a node
s\in N
, such that
S
is smooth at
s
.
Then
\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(S\setminus N)
is

(2.1)

trivial if
\deg S=1,2
,

(2.2)

{\mathbb{Z}}/2
if
\deg S=3
,

(2.3)

trivial if
4\leq\deg S\leq 9
and the node is unsplit (
12
), and

(2.4)

the infinite dihedral group
D_{\infty}
(
13
) if
4\leq\deg S\leq 9
and the node is split.

Previous results.

[
Yos85
, 4.2.2]
describes an infinite order element of
\operatorname{Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{P}}^{2}\setminus(\mbox{nodal cubic})\bigr)
. The same construction is
used in
[
Ore02
, Sec.6]
to obtain highly cuspidal rational curves in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. It turns out that a variant
gives the whole
\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}({\mathbb{P}}^{2}\setminus N)
; most likely both authors suspected this. Versions for other Del Pezzo surfaces are considered in
[
MS24
, Sec.2.1]
.
A more detailed discussion of the relationship with the maps considered in

[
Yos85
,
Ore02
,
MS24
]
is given in
(
25
).

Remarks 3
.

If
S
is a Del Pezzo surface, then
\operatorname{Aut}(S,N)
is always finite, and trivial in most cases; see (
26
) for some examples.

By contrast,
\operatorname{Aut}(S,N)
can be infinite if
(N^{2})\leq 0
.
A comprehensive study is given in

[
Li22
]
, which builds on the works of

[
GHK15b
,
GHK15a
,
Fri16
]
.

I do not know how to write down a priori the quotient map

\operatorname{Aut}(S\setminus N)\to\operatorname{Aut}(S,N)
.
Thus we determine the structure of
\operatorname{Aut}(S\setminus N)
in all cases, and then observe that they have a semidirect product structure as claimed in
(
1
.2).

4
Geiser-type involutions.

Let

S
be a Del Pezzo surface of degree
\geq 4
, and
N\in|-K_{S}|
a geometrically irreducible curve with a split node
s\in N
, such that
S
is smooth at
s
.
In (
23
) we construct two Geiser-type involutions

[formula]

\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-}\in\operatorname{Aut}(S\setminus N),

indexed by the local branches
B_{\pm}
of
N
at
s
.
There is no natural way to distinguish the branches from each other, so
it is best to think of
\{\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-}\}
as a pair of involutions.
Then we define

[formula]

\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(S\setminus N):=\langle\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-}\rangle.

We check in Paragraph
6
that

\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(S\setminus N)\cong D_{\infty}
, and we see in
(
27
) that its normalizer contains
\operatorname{Aut}(S,N)
.

It takes longer to show that the Geiser involutions and
\operatorname{Aut}(S,N)
generate

\operatorname{Aut}(S\setminus N)
.
The approach is modeled on the Noether-Fano method, as implemented by Segre and Manin for cubic surfaces; see
[
Seg42
,
Man72
]
or
[
KSC04
, Sec.2.1]
.

The first step is a computation of how the basic numerical invariants of a cuspidal curve transform under
\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-}
. The following is equivalent to
[
MS24
, 2.2.6]
; in the first version the second case in (
5
.2) was overlooked.
The formulas are simplest using the normalized degree

[formula]

\operatorname{ndeg}(C):=\tfrac{1}{\deg S}(-K_{S}\cdot C)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\operatorname{mult}(C):=\operatorname{mult}_{s}(C).

Lemma 5
.

Let

S
be a Del Pezzo surface of degree
d\geq 4
, and
N\in|-K_{S}|
an irreducible curve with a split node
s\in N
, such that
S
is smooth at
s
.
Let
C\subset S
be an irreducible curve with only single, cuspidal point on
N
. Then, for a suitable choice of the local branch
B_{+}
of
N
,

\begin{array}[]{ccl}(\ref{cusp.transf.cor}.1)\qquad\operatorname{ndeg}\bigl(\sigma_{+}(C)\bigr)&=&(d{-}1)\operatorname{ndeg}(C)-\operatorname{mult}(C),\quad\mbox{and}\\[4.30554pt]
(\ref{cusp.transf.cor}.2)\qquad\operatorname{ndeg}\bigl(\sigma_{-}(C)\bigr)&=&\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\operatorname{mult}(C)-\operatorname{ndeg}(C)\quad\mbox{if $\tfrac{d}{d{-}1}\geq\tfrac{\operatorname{mult}C}{\operatorname{ndeg}C}$, and}\\[4.30554pt]
(d{-}1)\operatorname{ndeg}(C)-(d{-}2)\operatorname{mult}(C)\quad\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.\end{array}

The proof is given in (
32
).

6
Bounds on the multiplicity.

Note first that
2\cdot\operatorname{mult}(C)\leq(N\cdot C)=d\cdot\operatorname{ndeg}(C)
, thus, if

d\geq 4
then

[formula]

\operatorname{ndeg}\bigl(\sigma_{+}(C)\bigr)>\operatorname{ndeg}(C),

except when
d=4
and
\operatorname{mult}(C)=2\cdot\operatorname{ndeg}(C)
.
Thus we can increase the degree by one of

\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-}
, hence they generate an infinite dihedral subgroup of

\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(S\setminus N)
.

By explicit computation we also see that

[formula]

\operatorname{ndeg}\bigl(\sigma_{-}(C)\bigr)<\operatorname{ndeg}(C)

provided

[formula]

\operatorname{ndeg}(C)<\operatorname{mult}(C)<2\cdot\operatorname{ndeg}(C).

The upper bound
\operatorname{mult}(C)<2\cdot\operatorname{ndeg}(C)
holds for almost all curves, see
Corollary
34
.
The exceptional cases are when
d\in\{4,5\}
and
\operatorname{mult}(C)=2=2\cdot\operatorname{ndeg}(C)
.

Therefore, using Lemma
5
we can lower the degree of
C
, and get the following.

Corollary 7
.

Let
(S,N)
and
C\subset S
be as in
Lemma
5
.
Then there is a
\phi\in\langle\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-}\rangle
such that

(7.1)

either
\operatorname{mult}\bigl(\phi(C)\bigr)\leq\operatorname{ndeg}\bigl(\phi(C)\bigr)
,

(7.2)

or
d\in\{4,5\}
,
C
is rational, and
\phi(C)\in|-K_{S}|
has a
(2,3)
cusp at
s
.

Moreover, for (
7
.1),

(7.3)

either
\phi
is unique and the inequality in (
7
.1) is strict,

(7.4)

or there are two such
\phi
and equality holds in (
7
.1) for both.

The inequality (
7
.1) is exactly the one needed for
the Noether-Fano method.
(The Noether-Fano method usually works with the base points of birational transforms of very ample linear systems. In the current situation, studying the cusps of curves works better.)

To complete the proof of Theorem
2

we start with an elliptic curve
E\subset S
that meets
N
at a single smooth point, see (
38
).
Given
\psi\in\operatorname{Aut}(S\setminus N)
, we apply Corollary
7
to

\psi(E)
.
Since
E
is not rational, we are in case (
7
.1).
Thus we have a
\phi\in\langle\sigma_{+},\sigma_{-}\rangle

such that

\operatorname{mult}\bigl(\phi\circ\psi(E)\bigr)\leq\operatorname{ndeg}\bigl(\phi\circ\psi(E)\bigr).

The Noether-Fano method now says that
\phi\circ\psi
is an
automorphism of
(S,N)
, as needed; see Section
8
for details.∎

is the study of

These are called

Examples 10
.

These examples show that the assumptions in Theorem
1
are mostly necessary.

(
10
.1)
A boundary
N=\cup_{i}N_{i}
could be called minimal if none of its irreducible components are
(-1)
-curves. If
(N_{i}^{2})\leq-2
for every
i
then it is easy to see that

S\setminus N
determines
(S,N)
.
However,
Theorem
1
does not extend to all minimal boundaries.
For example,
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\times{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
can be compactified to

\bigl({\mathbb{P}}^{2},(xyz=0)\bigr)
and to

\bigl({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{x}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{y},(x_{0}x_{1}y_{0}y_{1}=0)\bigr)
, neither boundary contains a curve with
-1
self-intersection.

(
10
.2)
A more sophisticated version is the following.
Let
S
be a nontrivial Severi-Brauer surface and

N\subset S
the union of 3 conjugate lines.
Note that
N
is irreducible, but not geometrically irreducible.

Blowing up the 3 nodes of
N
and then contracting the birational transform of
N
gives the dual Severi-Brauer surface
(S^{\prime},N^{\prime})
.
Thus
S\setminus N\cong S^{\prime}\setminus N^{\prime}
, but
S\not\cong S^{\prime}
.

(
10
.3) Let
(S,N)
be a log K3 surface such that
d:=(N^{2})\leq 2
.
Assume that
S
is smooth at the node
s\in N
.
Blowing up
s
, the birational transform
\tilde{N}
of
N
is a smooth rational curve with self-intersection
d-4
. Contracting it we get a
log K3 surface
(S^{\prime},N^{\prime})
such that

S\setminus N\cong S^{\prime}\setminus N^{\prime}
but
S\not\cong S^{\prime}
.
Here
S^{\prime}
is singular at the node
s^{\prime}\in N^{\prime}
.
If
d=2
then
S^{\prime}
is also a Del Pezzo surface with an
A_{1}
singularity at
the node
s^{\prime}
.

(
10
.4) If
(N^{2})<0
then
N
is (analytically) contractible

(S,N)\to(S^{*},s^{*})
. Then
S^{*}
is the one-point compactification of

S\setminus N
, and
S
is the minimal resolution of the singularity at
s^{*}
.
Thus the isomorphism-extension property holds.
The same holds if
N
is reducible with a negative definite intersection matrix.

2.
Notation and definitions

11
Del Pezzo surfaces.

Let
k
be a field.
I follow the terminology of
[
Dol12
, 8.1.12]
, according to which a

Del Pezzo surface

is a projective
k
-surface
S

such that
-K_{S}
is ample, and
S_{\bar{k}}
is irreducible with at worst
Du Val singularites.

Du Val singularites are also called rational double points or ADE singularites.
In our discussions only the
A_{n}
singularities
(xy=z^{n+1})
will appear.

The
degree
of
S
is the self-intersection number of
-K_{S}
.

A
weak Del Pezzo surface
is a projective surface
S
such that
(-K_{S}\cdot C)\geq 0
for every curve, the self-intersection number of
-K_{S}
is positive, and
S_{\bar{k}}
has at worst Du Val singularites.

For such an
S
there is a unique
birational morphism
m_{S}:S\to S^{*}
to a Del Pezzo surface
S^{*}
such that
K_{S}=m_{S}^{*}K_{S^{*}}
.

S
and
S^{*}
have the same degrees, and

m_{S}
contracts all the curves
C\subset S
for which

(-K_{S}\cdot C)=0
. If
S
is smooth, these are the smooth rational curves with self-intersection
-2
.

S^{*}
is singular at the images of these curves.

Note on terminology.
Many authors require (sometimes implicitly) that a Del Pezzo surface be smooth. For us the more encompassing definition is more convenient. In the construction of the Geiser involutions, the
most important surface is a singular Del Pezzo surface; see Example
24
.

A normal, projective surface
S
with
-K_{S}
ample
is called a
log Del Pezzo surface.
Some authors assume that the singularities are quotient singularities, some allow a boundary, and some drop the modifier ‘log.’
So
caveat lector.

Definition 12

(Split nodes)
.

Let
C
be a curve over a field
k
and

c\in C(k)
a point.
C
has a
node
at
c
if, after completion, it is isomorphic to
\operatorname{Spec}k[[x,y]]/(q)
where
q
is a homogeneous degree 2 polynomial with 2 simple roots.

The node is
split
if
q
has 2 roots in
k
. Then we can use the standard form
\operatorname{Spec}k[[x,y]]/(xy)
.
The node is
unsplit
if the roots of
q
are conjugate over
k
.
If
\operatorname{char}k\neq 2
then we can use the normal form

\operatorname{Spec}k[[x,y]]/(x^{2}-ay^{2})
, where
a
is not a square in
k
.

Definition 13
.

Let
D_{\infty}
denote the
infinite dihedral group.

It can be given as
\langle a,b:a^{2}=1,aba=b^{-1}\rangle
, as

\langle a,c:a^{2}=c^{2}=1\rangle
(setting
c=ab
), as
{\mathbb{Z}}/2\ast{\mathbb{Z}}/2
, or as

{\mathbb{Z}}\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}/2
with the
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-action
r\to-r
on
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

14
Moduli of log K3 pairs.

Let
S
be a Del Pezzo surface and
N\in|-K_{S}|
a geometrically irreducible, nodal curve.

If
S={\mathbb{P}}^{2}
and
N
has a split node, then, up to isomorphism
\bigl({\mathbb{P}}^{2},(xyz=x^{3}+ay^{3})\bigr)
are the only ones over any field
k
. If
a
is a cube in
k
then we get
\bigl({\mathbb{P}}^{2},(xyz=x^{3}+y^{3})\bigr)
.

For
d<9
the nodal pairs
(S,N_{S})
with
\deg S=(N_{S}^{2})=d
form a

(9-d)
-dimensional family, see
[
GHK15b
]
for a detailed treatment of their moduli theory.

A naive argument is the following.
If
S
is smooth then we can write
\pi:S\to{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
as the blow-up of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
at
c
points. Each
\pi
-exceptional curve meets
N_{S}
with multiplicity 1, so

(S,N_{S})
is the blow-up of
({\mathbb{P}}^{2},N)
at
c
smooth points on
N
.
Thus the moduli space is roughly the same as the space of
c
smooth points in
N
.

Note that if
c=1
or
c=2
, then
\pi:S\to{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
is unique, but not
for
c\geq 3
. We have
finitely many choices for
\pi
for
c\leq 8
, and at most countably many for
c\geq 9
.

Note that the Torelli theorem of
[
GHK15b
, §5]

suggests that the ‘best’ choice may be repeatedly blowing up the
point
(1{:}{-}1{:}0)\in(xyz=x^{3}+y^{3})\bigr)
.
This gives the unique pair where the mixed Hodge structure on
H_{2}(S\setminus N_{S})
is split. However, this is only a weak Del Pezzo surface for
8\geq c\geq 2
.

For general introductions see

[

or
[

However, we need only 3 simple facts, which are easy to state

15
Discrepancies for surface pairs.

Let
S
be a smooth surface and
\pi:T\to S
a proper, birational morphism.
We are interested in the local behavior of
\pi
over a point
s\in S
,
so assume that
\pi
is an isomorphism over
S\setminus\{s\}
, and
T
is normal.

We can write
K_{T}\sim\pi^{*}K_{S}+E
where
E
is effective and
\pi
-exceptional.

Next let
C
be a (possibly reducible) curve on
S
and
C_{T}
its birational transform on
T
. Then
C_{T}\sim\pi^{*}C-D
where
D
is effective and
\pi
-exceptional. For any (say rational)
c>0
we can formally write

[formula]

K_{T}+cC_{T}\sim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\pi^{*}(K_{S}+cC)+E-cD.

The coefficients in
E-cD
are called the
discrepancies
of

(S,cC)
.

The claims below can be established by checking them for 1 blow-up and using induction.
For precise references see
[
Kol13
, 2.29]

for (
15
.2–3) and
[
KM98
, 2.31]
or
[
Kol13
, 2.7]
for
(
15
.4).

(
15
.2)
E-cD
is effective for every
\pi
iff

c\cdot\operatorname{mult}_{s}C\leq 1
. (Such pairs
(S,cC)
are called
canonical.
)

(
15
.3)
E-cD
is effective and
\operatorname{Supp}(E-cD)=\operatorname{Ex}(\pi)
for every
\pi
iff

c\cdot\operatorname{mult}_{s}C<1
. (Such pairs
(S,cC)
are called
terminal.
)

(
15
.4) If
C
has a node at
s
and
c\leq 1
then
all coefficients in
E-cD
are
\geq-1
.
(The latter is the
log canonical
property.)

Preliminary remarks for the proof of Theorem

.

Let
(S,N)
be a log K3 surface with irreducible boundary.
Then

\operatorname{Cl}(S\setminus N)\cong\operatorname{Cl}(S)/{\mathbb{Z}}[-K_{S}]
.
In particular, if
k

then the class group of

(16.1)

{\mathbb{Z}}/3
if

,

(16.2)

{\mathbb{Z}}+{\mathbb{Z}}/2
if
S\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, and

(16.3)

{\mathbb{Z}}^{9-d}
in all other cases, where

4.
Geiser involutions

Geiser involutions are constructed using a natural automorphism of
degree 2 Del Pezzo surfaces. We start by recalling their properties; see

[
Dol12
, Chap.8]
for details.
We assume for simplicity that the characteristic is
\neq 2
.

21
Degree 2 Del Pezzo surfaces.

Let
T
be a degree 2 Del Pezzo surface.
The linear system
|-K_{T}|
gives a morphism
p:T\to{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, which
is a double cover, branched along a
quartic curve
R\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.
We let
\tau
or
\tau_{T}
denote the covering involution.

The anticanonical class is the pull-back of the line class
L
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.

T
is smooth iff
R
is smooth.
The singularities of
T
correspond to the singular points of
R
, and they have the same name in the ADE classification.

A curve
E\subset T
is called a
line
if
(-K_{T}\cdot E)=1
.
Since
-K_{T}=p^{*}L
, we see that
E
is a line iff

p(E)\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
is a line and
p^{-1}(p(E))=E+E^{\prime}
is a sum of 2 lines.
This happens over the bitangents of
R
. If
R
is smooth, there are 28 bitangents, giving 56 lines on
T
.
(If
p(E)\subset R
then
p^{-1}(p(E))=2E

If

are 2 lines and
p(E)\neq p(E^{\prime})
, then
E\cap E^{\prime}
is at most 1 point.
Thus, if
E\cap E^{\prime}
consists of 2 points, then
p(E)=p(E^{\prime})
, hence

\tau(E)=E^{\prime}
.

Let
D\subset T
be a curve. Then
D+\tau_{T}(D)=p^{-1}(p(D))
, hence

[formula]

D+\tau_{T}(D)\in|-rK_{T}|\quad\mbox{where}\quad r=(-K_{T}\cdot D).

Let
T
be a smooth, weak Del Pezzo surface of degree 2
and
m_{T}:T\to T^{*}
the corresponding Del Pezzo surface of degree 2.
Then the
covering involution
\tau^{*}
on
T^{*}
lifts to an involution
\tau
on
T
.

21
.2 Note on characteristic 2.

In the cases that we consider later,

is always separable, so
the above descriptions work.

Geiser involutions.

These were discovered in
[

; see
[

Start with

For the current purposes, the representation

Next we look at a special case where the blow-up centers of

are in a rather degenerate position.

Geiser involutions and Yoshihara twists.

A version of the Geiser involution (
23

{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
was considered in

[

]
, rediscovered in
[
Ore02
]
and then

[

.

The constructions in these papers
start with the same

then contract the curves

N_{T},E_{1},\dots,E_{r-1}
, starting with
N_{T}
.

The end result is then a birational map
\phi:(S,N)\dasharrow(S^{\prime},N^{\prime})

and that

is also a nodal cubic.

For

There are 6 distinct

The maps in

Ore02

.

For

over

[

Automorphisms of log K3 surfaces.

The automorphism group of
(S,N)
acts on
N
, and
\operatorname{Aut}(N)\cong{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}/2
. The action also fixes
{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(K_{S})|_{N}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{d}(N)
where
d=\deg S
.
Note that
\lambda\in{\mathbb{G}}_{m}=\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(N)
acts on
\operatorname{Pic}^{d}(N)
by multiplication by
\lambda^{d}
. Thus, for
d\neq 0
, the subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(N)
that leaves

{\mathcal{O}}_{S}(K_{S})|_{N}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{d}(N)
invariant is the dihedral group

D_{2d}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/d\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}/2
.
Thus we get a homomorphism

\operatorname{Aut}(S,N)\to D_{2d}
.
If
d\geq 1
then the kernel is cyclic.

If
k
contains a 3rd root of unity
\epsilon\neq 1
, then
\operatorname{Aut}\bigl({\mathbb{P}}^{2},(xyz=x^{3}+y^{3})\bigr)\cong S_{3}
, generated by the involution
\rho_{2}:(x:y:z)\mapsto(y:x:z)
and
the order 3 element
\rho_{3}:(x:y:z)\mapsto(\epsilon x:\epsilon^{2}y:z)
.
As another example, if
\operatorname{char}k\neq 2
then

is the dihedral group
D_{8}
.
For
d\leq 8
the general pair
(S,N)
has no automorphisms.

Let
T_{\pm}\to S
be the blow-ups involved in the construction of the Geiser involutions. If
\rho\in\operatorname{Aut}(S,N)

interchanges the branches

, then
\rho

lifts to an isomorphism
\bar{\rho}:T_{+}\cong T_{+}

interchanges the branches

, then

,

Definition 28

(Cusp)
.

Let
C
be
a geometrically reduced curve over a field
k

\pi:\bar{C}\to C
. For a point

\bar{c}:=\operatorname{red}\pi^{-1}(c)
.
We say that

or is

k(\bar{c})=k(c)=k
.

Let
S
be a smooth surface germ and fix local (analytic or formal)
coordinates
(x,y)
at
s\in S
. Then a cusp
s\in C\subset S

can be parametrized as

[formula]

t\mapsto\bigl(x(t):=t^{p}+(\mbox{higher terms}),y(t):=t^{q}+(\mbox{higher terms})\bigr),

(after scaling
x,y
if necessary).
The values
p,q
are the
contact orders
of
C
with the axes:

(C\cdot(\mbox{$x$-axis}))_{s}=q
and

(C\cdot(\mbox{$y$-axis}))_{s}=p
.

The multiplicity of
C
is
\min\{p,q\}
, independent of the choice of the coordinates. If
q\nmid p
and
p\nmid q
then both
p,q
are invariants of
C
; we then call
C
a
(p,q)
-cusp.

Note that if
p=rq
then the coordinate change
x^{\prime}:=x-y^{r}

gives
\operatorname{mult}x^{\prime}(t)>\operatorname{mult}x(t)
.
Newton proved that if
C
is singular, then after finitely many steps we reach a

t\mapsto\bigl(x(t):=t^{p^{\prime}}+(\mbox{higher terms}),y(t):=t^{q}+(\mbox{higher terms})\bigr),

such that
q\nmid p^{\prime}
;
see
[
BK86
, Sec.8.3]
or
[
Kol07
, Sec.1.1]
.
Then
C
is a
(p^{\prime},q)
-cusp.

After blowing up the origin, the parametrization (
28
.1) changes to

[formula]

\begin{array}[]{lcll}(x/y,y)&=&\bigl(t^{p-q}+(\mbox{higher}),t^{q}+(\mbox{higher})\bigr)&\mbox{if $p>q$, and}\\[4.30554pt]
(x,y/x)&=&\bigl(t^{p}+(\mbox{higher}),t^{q-p}+(\mbox{higher})\bigr)&\mbox{if $p<q$.}\end{array}

33
Max Noether’s genus formula.

(See
[
BK86
, Sec.9.2]
or
[
Kol07
, p.38]
.)
The
genus
of a planar curve singularity is
\delta(0\in C):=\sum\frac{m_{i}(m_{i}-1)}{2}
, where
m_{i}
are the multiplicities of all points that appear after some blow-ups (called infinitely near points).
If
C
is an irreducible, projective curve on a smooth surface
S
with normalization
\bar{C}
, then Noether’s formula is

[formula]

g(\bar{C})=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(C\cdot(C+K_{S})\bigr)+1-\textstyle{\sum}_{c\in C}\delta(c\in C).

Let
S
be a smooth, weak Del Pezzo surface of degree
d
and

C\subset S
an irreducible curve. Write

C\sim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}-mK_{S}+F
where
(K_{S}\cdot F)=0
.
Then

\bigl(C\cdot(C+K_{S})\bigr)=dm(m-1)+(F^{2}).

Thus we get that

[formula]

2\cdot\textstyle{\sum}_{c\in C}\delta(c\in C)=dm(m-1)+2-2g(\bar{C})+(F^{2}).

Using (
28
.2) and induction we see that

[formula]

2\cdot\delta\bigl(\mbox{$(p,q)$-cusp}\bigr)\geq(p-1)(q-1),

and equality holds iff
(p,q)=1
. Putting these together gives the following.

Claim
33
.4.
Let
S
be a smooth, weak Del Pezzo surface of degree
d
and

C\subset S
an irreducible curve such that

C\sim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}-mK_{S}+F
where
(K_{S}\cdot F)=0
. If
C
has a
(p,q)
-cusp then

(p-1)(q-1)\leq dm(m-1)+2,

with equality holding iff
(p,q)=1
,
C
is rational with at most 1 singularity, and
C\sim_{{\mathbb{Q}}}-mK_{S}
for some
m
. ∎

Corollary 34
.

Let

Example 48
.

Let
S
be a smooth Del Pezzo surface and
C\subset S
a smooth, rational curve with
(C^{2})\geq 0
. Set
L:={\mathcal{O}}_{S}(C)
.

Let
N\in|-K_{S}|
be a nodal curve. Consider the sequence

0\to\omega_{S}(L)\to L\to L|_{N}\to 0.

By the adjunction formula,
\deg L|_{N}=2+(C^{2})
. Thus there are

2+(C^{2})
sections
s_{i}\in H^{0}(N,L|_{N})
such that
(s_{i}=0)
is a single smooth point
c_{i}\in N
.
Since
H^{1}\bigl(S,\omega_{S}(L)\bigr)=0
, the
s_{i}
lift back to sections
of
L
. Thus we get divisors

such that

.

Example 49
.

Let
N\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
be a nodal cubic
and
x\in N
a smooth point.
Blow up
x
to get
S:=B_{x}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
and
N\subset|-K_{S}|
.
Let
E\subset S
be the
(-1)
-curve and
F\subset S
a fiber of the projection
\pi:S\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
from
x
.

For any
r\geq 1
, the general members of
|E+rF|
are smooth rational curves of self-intersection
2r-1
. Thus by (
48
) there are
2r+1
different points

c_{i,r}\in N

C_{i,r}\in|E+rF|
such that
C_{i,r}\cap N=\{c_{i,r}\}
.

If
C_{i}
is reducible, then it is of the form
F_{i,r}+C^{\prime}_{i,r}
where

F_{i,r}
is the fiber through
c_{i,r}

2[c_{i,r}]\sim F|_{N}

[c_{i,r}]\sim[E|_{N}]=[E\cap N]

So
E\cap N

\pi|_{N}
.
This means that
x\in N
is a flex point.
Then
C_{i,r}=E+rF_{i,r}

Thus we conclude the following.

For each
r\geq 1
, the curves
C_{i,r}\in|E+rF|
give

(49.1)

2r+1
different
\operatorname{Aut}(S\setminus N)
-orbits of affine lines on
S\setminus N
if
x\in N
is not a flex point, but only

(49.2)

2r
different
\operatorname{Aut}(S\setminus N)
-orbits of affine lines if
x\in N
is a flex point.

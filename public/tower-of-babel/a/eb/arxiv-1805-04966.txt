On the k k -partition dimension of graphs
Alejandro Estrada-Moreno

Abstract
As a generalization of the concept of the partition dimension of a graph, this article introduces the notion of the k k -partition dimension. Given a nontrivial connected graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) , a partition Π \Pi of V V is said to be a k k -partition generator of G G if any pair of different vertices u , v ∈ V u,v\in V is distinguished by at least k k vertex sets of Π \Pi , i.e ., there exist at least k k vertex sets S 1 , … , S k ∈ Π S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}\in\Pi such that d ⁡ ( u , S i ) ≠ d ⁡ ( v , S i ) d(u,S_{i})\neq d(v,S_{i}) for every i ∈ { 1 , … , k } i\in\{1,\ldots,k\} . A k k -partition generator of G G with minimum cardinality among all their k k -partition generators is called a k k -partition basis of G G and its cardinality the k k -partition dimension of G G . A nontrivial connected graph G G is k k -partition dimensional if k k is the largest integer such that G G has a k k -partition basis. We give a necessary and sufficient condition for a graph to be r r -partition dimensional and we obtain several results on the k k -partition dimension for k ∈ { 1 , … , r } k\in\{1,\ldots,r\} .

k
-partition dimension;
k
-metric dimension; partition dimension; metric dimension.

From now on we consider simple and nontrivial connected graphs
G=(V,E)
. It is said that a vertex
v\in V

d_{G}(v,x)\neq d_{G}(v,y)

\displaystyle\min_{v\in S}\{d_{G}(a,v)\}
. A partition

d_{G}(u,S_{i})\neq d_{G}(v,S_{i}),\;\text{for every}\;i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}.

(1)

A

-partition generator of the minimum cardinality of G is called a

-partition basis
and its cardinality the

-partition dimension
of

, which will be denoted by

It can be noted that if

-partition generator

of

-partition generator. Moreover,

-partition generators and

-partition generators are resolving partitions and fault-tolerance resolving partitions defined in
[

]

]
, respectively. It can be observed that the property of a given partition

-partition generator of

, reduces to checking Condition (

. Hence it follows that if

, then

are distinguished by at least

and

. However, if

) for every pair of different vertices of the graph. In general, for any pair of vertices

such that

,

and

Throughout the paper, we will use the notation

3
On
k
-partition dimensional graphs

In this section we discuss a natural problem in the study of the
k
-partition dimension of a graph
G
which consists of finding the largest integer
k
such that there exists a
k
-partition generator of
G
. We say that a connected graph
G
is
k
-partition dimensional
if
k
is the largest integer such that there exists a
k
-partition basis of
G
.

Given a graph
G
and two different vertices
x,y\in V(G)
, we denote by
\mathcal{D}_{G}(x,y)
the set of vertices that distinguish the pair
x,y
,
i.e.
,

\mathcal{D}_{G}(x,y)=\{z\in V:d(z,x)\neq d(z,y)\}.

It can also be noted that for any two different vertices
x,y\in V
we have that
x,y\in\mathcal{D}_{G}(x,y)
and vertices
x,y
belong to the same twin equivalence class of
G
if and only if
\mathcal{D}_{G}(x,y)=\{x,y\}
. With this definition in mind, we show the following proposition which allows us to prove Theorem
3
, which is the main result in this section.

Proposition 1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a nontrivial connected graph and let
\Pi
be a partition of
V
. For any vertex set
S\in\Pi
that distinguishes a pair of different vertices
x,y\in V
, there exists a vertex
z\in S
that distinguishes
x,y
.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a
z\in S
such
d(x,z)=d(x,S)
and
d(y,z)=d(y,S)
, then since
S
distinguishes
x,y
it follows that
d(x,z)=d(x,S)\neq d(y,S)=d(y,z)
, which leads
z
distinguishes
x,y
. Otherwise, we take a vertex
x^{\prime}\in S
such that
d(x,x^{\prime})=d(x,S)
. If
x^{\prime}
distinguishes
x,y
, we are done. Suppose that for any
x^{\prime}\in S
such that
d(x,x^{\prime})=d(x,S)
we have that
x^{\prime}
does not distinguish
x,y
,
i.e.
,
d(x,x^{\prime})=d(y,x^{\prime})
. Thus, for any
y^{\prime}\in S
such that
d(y,y^{\prime})=d(y,S)
it follows that
d(x,y^{\prime})\geq d(x,S)=d(x,x^{\prime})=d(y,x^{\prime})\geq d(y,S)=d(y,y^{\prime})
. Since
S
distinguishes
x,y
, we deduce that
d(x,S)>d(y,S)
, and as a consequence,
y^{\prime}
distinguishes
x,y
.
∎

Corollary 2
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a nontrivial connected graph and let
\Pi
be a
k
-partition generator of
G
. For any pair of different vertices
x,y\in V
and all vertex sets
S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{r}\in\Pi
that distinguish it, we have that
r\geq k
and
\mathcal{D}_{G}(x,y)\cap S_{i}\neq\emptyset
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
.

We now define the following parameter
\mathfrak{d}(G)=\displaystyle\min_{x,y\in V,x\neq y}\{|\mathcal{D}_{G}(x,y)|\}
.

Theorem 3
.

Any graph
G
of order
n\geq 2
is
\mathfrak{d}(G)
-partition dimensional and the time complexity of computing
\mathfrak{d}(G)
is
O(n^{3})
.

Proof.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a
k
-partition dimensional graph and let
\Pi
be a
k
-partition basis of
G
. Let
x,y\in V
be two different vertices such that
\mathfrak{d}(G)=|\mathcal{D}_{G}(x,y)|
. By Corollary
2
, for the
r
vertex sets of
\Pi
that distinguish
x,y
, we deduce that
k\leq r\leq\mathfrak{d}(G)
.
On the other hand, if we take the partition of singletons
\Pi^{\prime}=\left\{{\left\{{v}\right\}:v\in V}\right\}
on
V
, then any pair of different vertices
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in V
is distinguished by at least
\mathfrak{d}(G)
elements of
\Pi^{\prime}
. Thus
\Pi^{\prime}
is a
\mathfrak{d}(G)
-partition generator of
G
, and as a consequence,
k\geq\mathfrak{d}(G)
.
Therefore,
k=\mathfrak{d}(G).

Finally, it was shown in
[
45
]
that the time complexity of computing
\mathfrak{d}(G)
is
O(n^{3})
.
∎

As Theorem
3
shows, in general, the problem of computing

\mathfrak{d}(G)
is very easy to solve. Even so, it would be desirable to obtain some general results on this subject.

Since for every pair of different vertices

we have that
|\mathcal{D}(x,y)|\geq 2
, it follows that the partition of singletons
\Pi=\left\{{\left\{{v}\right\}:v\in V}\right\}

Theorem 6

4
On the
k
-partition dimension of a graph

In this section we study the problem of computing or bounding the
k
-partition dimension of a graph. Since for any connected graph
G=(V,E)
of order
n\geq 2
and any
k
-partition basis
\Pi
of
G
, we have all pairs of different vertices of
V
are distinguished by at least
k
vertex set of
\Pi
, it follows that
\operatorname{pd}_{k}(G)\geq k
for any
k>1
. Particularly,
2\leq\operatorname{pd}_{1}(G)\leq n
. On the other hand, by definition we know that every
k
-partition basis of
G
, for
k>1
, is a
(k-1)
-partition generator of
G
. Therefore, the next result follows.

Theorem 14
.

Let G be a nontrivial connected graph and let
k_{1},k_{2}
be two integers. If
1<k_{1}<k_{2}\leq\mathfrak{d}(G)
, then
k_{1}\leq\operatorname{pd}_{k_{1}}(G)\leq\operatorname{pd}_{k_{2}}(G)\leq n
. Moreover,
2\leq\operatorname{pd}_{1}(G)\leq n
.

It was shown in
[
11
]
that
\operatorname{pd}_{1}(G)=2
if and only if
G\cong P_{n}
, and
\operatorname{pd}_{1}(G)=n
if and only if
G\cong K_{n}
. We consider the limit case of the trivial bound
\operatorname{pd}_{k}(G)\geq k
for
k\geq 2
.

Theorem 15
.

If
G
is a nontrivial connected graph, then
\operatorname{pd}_{k}(G)=k
if and only if
k=2
and
G\cong K_{2}
.

Proof.

It is readily seen that if
k=2
and
G\cong K_{2}
, then the partition of singletons on vertex set of
G
is a
2
-partition basis of
G
with cardinality
2
.

Conversely, assume that
\operatorname{pd}_{k}(G)=k
. By Thereom
14
, we have that
k\geq 2
. Let
\Pi
be a
k
-partition basis of
G=(V,E)
. If there exists one vertex set
S\in\Pi
such that
|S|\geq 2
, then for any pair of different vertices
x,y\in S
we need at least other
k
vertex set in
\Pi
, which contradicts the fact that
|\Pi|=k
. Thus,
\Pi
is the partition of singletons on vertex set of
G
, which leads to
\operatorname{pd}_{n}(G)=n
. By Remark
4
, we conclude that
k=2
and
G\cong K_{2}
.
∎

The existing relationship between
k
-metric dimension and
k
-partition dimension of graph for
k=1
was shown in
[
11
]
.

Theorem 16
.

[
11
]

If
G
is a nontrivial connected graph, then

\operatorname{pd}_{1}(G)\leq\dim_{1}(G)+1

This upper bound, although in general is true, is never achieved for
k\geq 2

Theorem 17
.

If

is a connected graph of order

, then for any

\operatorname{pd}_{k}(G)\leq\dim_{k}(G)+1.

Moreover, if
\dim_{\mathfrak{d}(G)}(G)<n
, then
\operatorname{pd}_{\mathfrak{d}(G)}(G)\leq\dim_{\mathfrak{d}(G)}(G)+1
, otherwise,
\operatorname{pd}_{\mathfrak{d}(G)}(G)\leq\dim_{\mathfrak{d}(G)}(G)

Proof.

Let

and let

be a

-metric basis of

. It was shown in
[
16

that for

we have that
\dim_{k}(G)<n
. Firstly, we assume that either

or

and
\dim_{\mathfrak{d}(G)}(G)<n
, and as a consequence,

. We consider the partition
\Pi=\{S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{s+1}\}
of

and

. Since

is a

-metric basis of

all pairs of different vertices are distinguished by at least

is a
k
-partition generator of

\operatorname{pd}_{k}(G)\leq|\Pi|=\dim_{k}(G)+1
.

Suppose that

and

. In this case we take the partition of singletons

\operatorname{pd}_{\mathfrak{d}(G)}(G)\leq|\Pi^{\prime}|=\dim_{\mathfrak{d}(G)}(G)

Proposition 19

B=\bigcup_{j=3}^{\tau}\bigcup_{l=1}^{\max_{1\leq i\leq\kappa}\left\{|A_{ij}|\right\}}\{B_{j}^{l}\},

B_{j}^{l}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\kappa}A_{ij}^{l},

and finally,

C=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}V-\bigcup_{i=1}^{\kappa}\left(\bigcup_{l=1}^{\min\left\{l(w_{i}),\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor\right\}}A_{i1}^{l}\cup\bigcup_{l=1}^{\max\left\{k-l(w_{i}),\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil\right\}}A_{i2}^{l}\right)&\text{if }\tau=2,\\
&\\
V-\bigcup_{i=1}^{\kappa}\left(\bigcup_{l=1}^{\min\left\{l(w_{i}),\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor\right\}}A_{i1}^{l}\cup\bigcup_{l=1}^{\max\left\{k-l(w_{i}),\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil\right\}}A_{i2}^{l}\right)-&\text{otherwise.}\\
-\bigcup_{j=3}^{\tau}\bigcup_{l=1}^{\max_{1\leq i\leq\kappa}\left\{|A_{ij}|\right\}}B_{j}^{l}&\end{array}\right.

It can be noted that
|A|=k\kappa
, and if
\tau\geq 3
, then
|B|=\mathcal{I}_{k}(T)
and otherwise
\mathcal{I}_{k}(T)=0
. Thus,
|\Pi|=k\kappa+\mathcal{I}_{k}(T)+1
. We consider two different vertices
x,y\in V
.

Case 1:

x\in A_{ij}^{l}
and
y\in A_{ij^{\prime}}^{l^{\prime}}
. If
w_{i}
distinguishes
x,y
, then at least
k
of the
k+1
vertex sets in
A_{i1}\cup A_{i2}\cup C
distinguish
x,y
. Suppose that
w_{i}
does not distinguish
x,y
. In this case
j\neq j^{\prime}
. If
j,j^{\prime}\geq 3
, then
x,y
are distinguished by all vertex sets in
\displaystyle\bigcup_{l=1}^{|A_{ij}|}\{B_{j}^{l}\}\cup\bigcup_{l=1}^{|A_{ij^{\prime}}|}\{B_{j^{\prime}}^{l}\}
. If
j<3
and
j^{\prime}\geq 3
, then all vertex sets in
\displaystyle A_{ij}\cup\bigcup_{l=1}^{|A_{ij^{\prime}}|}\{B_{j^{\prime}}^{l}\}
distinguish
x,y
. By last, if
j,j^{\prime}<3
, then
x,y
are distinguished by all vertex sets in
A_{i1}\cup A_{i2}
. In any case,
x,y
are distinguished by at least
k
vertex sets of
\Pi
.

Case 2:

x\in A_{ij}^{l}
,
y\in A_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}^{l^{\prime}}
and
i\neq i^{\prime}
. In this case
w_{i}
or
w_{i^{\prime}}
distinguish
x,y
, say
w_{i}
. Thus,
x,y
are distinguished by the
k
vertex sets in
A_{i1}\cup A_{i2}
.

Case 3:

x\in A_{ij}^{l}
and
y\in C
. If
|C|=1
, then
i=1
,
\{y\}=\{w_{1}\}=\mathcal{M}(T)
and since
T
is not a path,
y
has at least three terminal vertex,
i.e.
,
\tau=\operatorname{ter}(y)\geq 3
. In this case
x,y
are distinguished by all vertex sets in
\displaystyle\bigcup_{r=1,r\neq j}^{\tau}A_{1r}\subseteq\Pi
. Suppose that

.

Case 4:

x,y\in C

w_{i}\in\mathcal{M}(T)
such that

. Hence
x,y
is distinguished by the
k

.

Therefore,

\operatorname{pd}_{k}(T)\leq|\Pi|=k\kappa+\mathcal{I}_{k}(T)+1
.

Floquet-Weyl semimetals generated by an optically resonant interband-transition Preprint: APS/123-QED
Runnan Zhang, Ken-ichi Hino, Nobuya Maeshima

Abstract
Floquet-Weyl semimetals (FWSMs) generated by irradiation of a continuous-wave laser with left-hand circular polarization (rotating in counterclockwise sense with time) on the group II-V narrow gap semiconductor Zn 3 As 2 are theoretically investigated, where the frequency of the laser is set nearly resonant with a band gap of the crystal. It is found that the excitation of the crystal by such a laser induce two types of FWSM phases that differ absolutely in characters. To be specific, the associated two pairs of Weyl points are stably formed by band touching between Floquet sidebands ascribable to a valence band labeled as J z = ± 3 / 2 J_{z}=\pm 3/2 and a conduction band labeled as J z = ± 1 / 2 J_{z}=\pm 1/2 , where J z J_{z} represents the z z -component of total angular momentum quantum number of Γ \Gamma -point and a double sign corresponds. Here, one FWSM state composed of the up-spin Floquet sidebands relevant to J z = 3 / 2 J_{z}=3/2 and 1 / 2 1/2 shows almost quadratic band-touching in the vicinity of the associated pair of Weyl points, while the other FWSM state composed of the down-spin Floquet sidebands relevant to J z = − 3 / 2 J_{z}=-3/2 and − 1 / 2 -1/2 shows linear band-touching. Further, it is revealed that both up-spin and down-spin sidebands host nontrivial two-dimensional surface states that are pinned to the respective pairs of the Weyl points. Both surface states also show different energy dispersions and physical properties. More detailed discussion is made in the text on the origin of the above findings, chirality of the FWSM phases, alteration of topological order, laser-induced magnetic properties, and so on.

II.3
Symmetries

It is evident that
both T- and I-symmetries are conserved in
\mathcal{H}(\bm{k})
,
that is,

\Theta^{-1}\>\mathcal{H}(-\bm{k})\Theta=\mathcal{H}(\bm{k})
, and

\Pi^{-1}\>\mathcal{H}(-\bm{k})\Pi=\mathcal{H}(\bm{k})
,
where
\Theta
and
\Pi
represent the T- and I-operators, defined by

\Theta=-iI_{2}\otimes\sigma_{y}K
and
\Pi=\tau_{z}\otimes I_{2}
, respectively, where

K
means an operation of taking complex conjugate.
Further,
the T-symmetry is still respected in
H(\bm{k},t)
for a linearly polarized light,
that is,

\Theta^{-1}H(-\bm{k},-t)\Theta=H(\bm{k},t)
,
and thus, a pair of up-spin and down-spin Floquet bands forms Kramers degeneracy.
On the other hand,
the T-symmetry is broken for a circularly polarized light,
that is,

\Theta^{-1}H(-\bm{k},-t)\Theta\not=H(\bm{k},t)
.

As regards the I-symmetry, this is broken,
that is,

\Pi^{-1}H(-\bm{k},t)\Pi\not=H(\bm{k},t)
,
because

D_{j}(-\bm{k},t)\not=-D_{j}(\bm{k},t)
for
j=
3 and 5,
D_{4}(-\bm{k},t)\not=D_{4}(\bm{k},t)
,
and

\Pi^{-1}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(t)\Pi\not=\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(t)
.
In fact, it is shown that in terms of an operator defined as

\tilde{\Pi}=\Pi\mathcal{T}_{1/2}
, the symmetry

\tilde{\Pi}^{-1}H(-\bm{k},t+T/2)\tilde{\Pi}=H(\bm{k},t)

is retrieved,
where
\mathcal{T}_{1/2}
represents the operation of putting
t
ahead by a half period
T/2
,
namely, the replacement of
t\rightarrow t+T/2
.
Zhang2021
;
Morimoto2017

This is the time-glide
I
-operator mentioned in Sec.
I
.
Therefore, despite the breaking of the I-symmetry,
a Floquet band disperses in a symmetric manner with respect to
\bm{k}
, namely,
E_{\alpha}(\bm{k})=E_{\alpha}(-\bm{k})
.
For a linearly polarized light,
it is still probable that a four-fold band crossing occurs at the high-symmetry points,
namely, the time-reversal invariant momenta.

III.4
Physical Properties

First, discussion is made on the chirality of the FWSM phases and the related topological phase transitions.
It is considered that based on the qualitative discussion in Sec.
III.1
, the conditions of generating the Weyl nodes for the up-spin and down-spin states are approximately evaluated as Eqs. (
42
) and (
38
),
respectively.
According to these, when
\omega
is made greater from
\omega_{1}^{W(\pm)}
and eventually identical to
\omega_{2}^{W(\pm)}
,
the Weyl nodes at
\bm{k}^{W(\pm)}
move along
k_{z}
axis from the
\Gamma
point
k_{z}=0
toward the boundary of the BZ
at
k_{z}=\pi/d_{z}
to annihilate with the other pair of the Weyl nodes at
-\bm{k}^{W(\pm)}
that move in the opposite direction toward the boundary
at
k_{z}=-\pi/d_{z}
; a double sign corresponds.
This implies that the Weyl nodes at
\bm{k}^{W(\pm)}
possess
opposite handedness from that at the other Weyl nodes at
-\bm{k}^{W(\pm)}
.
In other words, there should be the relations that

h_{+}^{(+)}h_{-}^{(+)}=-1,\;h_{+}^{(-)}h_{-}^{(-)}=-1,

(51)

where

h_{\pm}^{(+)}
and
h_{\pm}^{(-)}
, which are either 1 or -1, represent helicities of the Weyl cones at

\pm\bm{k}^{W(+)}
for the up-spin and
\pm\bm{k}^{W(-)}
for the down-spin, respectively.
Further, it is noted that the handedness of the Weyl node for the up-spin state
at
\bm{k}^{W(+)}(-\bm{k}^{W(+)})
is opposite from that for the down-spin
state at
\bm{k}^{W(-)}(-\bm{k}^{W(-)})
, because
a pair of Weyl nodes for the up-spin and down-spin states at
\bm{k}^{W(+)}
and
\bm{k}^{W(-)}
, respectively, are generated by splitting of the Dirac node at
\bm{k}^{D}(-\bm{k}^{D})
due to the breaking of the T-symmetry.
That is, there should be the relations that

h_{+}^{(+)}h_{+}^{(-)}=-1,\;h_{-}^{(+)}h_{-}^{(-)}=-1.

(52)

Actually, the above relations of Eqs. (
51
) and (
52
) are confirmed
by defining these helicities as Eqs. (
160
) and (
171
), followed by
mathematical evaluation, as developed in Appendix
B.4
.
Here, these expressions of helicities are extracted from effective Fourier-Weyl Hamiltonians
of Eqs. (
154
) and (
165
), which are
reduced from the original Floquet Hamiltonian of Eq. (
15
).
Here, one mentions that recently, dynamical characterization of Floquet-Weyl nodes is discussed in Ref.
Umer2021b
.

In passing, when
\omega
exceeds
\omega_{2}^{W(\pm)}
, the topological order is changed from the FWSM phase to a phase of Floquet topological insulator
due to the gap opening.
Further, the reduction of
\omega

\omega_{1}^{W(\pm)}
in the other direction
brings the FWSM phase just back to a trivial insulator phase.

Second, discussion is made on a magnetic property induced by the irradiation of the
intense laser with a left-hand circular polarization.
As far as the nearly resonant optical transition is concerned,
down-spin electrons that are situated in a valence band before the irradiation are selectively excited to a conduction band, and some fractions of the excited
electrons are deexcited back to the valence band due to
the Rabi oscillation,
whereas up-spin electrons remain almost in the valence band;
consult Sec.
II.2
.
In terms of the Floquet picture,
these excitation and deexcitation processes in a series of the non-equilibrium dynamics
are interpreted as couplings between
one pair of down-spin bands

and
e(0)

another pair of down-spin bands
hh(0)
and
e(-1)

and
hh(1)

which are further coupled by the two-photon interaction mentioned in

III.2
to form the FWSM phase through the ac-Stark splitting.
On the contrary, it is considered that the up-spin bands of
e(-1)
and
hh(1)
are almost unoccupied.
Therefore, the down-spin electrons are exclusively distributed over the surface,
while these
coexist with the up-spin electrons in the bulk though both electronic states are
energetically separated by the amount of
\mathcal{E}_{g}^{(-)}
.

This implies that the system of concern exhibits transient surface
magnetization with down spins that survives for
as long as the associated population relaxation time,
besides bulk magnetization that is expected to be induced as well.
In addition with such an effect of spin magnetization, it is likely that
the circularly polarized laser induces the inverse Faraday effect, which is
a sort of a generation mechanism of orbital magnetization.

Pershan1966
;
Kimel2005
;
Hertel2006
;
Zhang2009
;
Battiato2014

This effect is expected to contribute the above surface magnetization to a certain extent.
Indeed, the surface magnetization seems faint and transient, but the magnitude of it can
be somewhat enhanced by increasing the strength of the circularly polarized laser.
Moreover, the measurement of such an intriguing phenomenon would be feasible
by means of the longitudinal magneto-optic Kerr effect that can detect the degree of strength of magnetization manifested just in the surface.

Kerr1877
;
Weinberg2008
;
Haider2017

To do this, a pump-probe measurement is expected to be effective, in which
a linear polarized laser causing the magneto-optic Kerr effect is

on the results described in Secs

smeared with homogeneous broadening due to an electron correlation effect

the up-spin bands of

and

the continuum (bulk) of the FNLSM phase of the up-spin band; consult Figs.
4
and
5
.
When a spin flip interaction attributed to the spin-orbit coupling is tuned on, the surface states become somewhat unstable
due to the effect of Fano resonance, namely, the collapse of the discrete levels of the surface states into the continuum states which is caused by the interaction between both of these states.

The spin flip interaction becomes effective when either

or

has a non-negligible contribution to

).

has a bulk rotational symmetry around the

-axis,

)
and (

).

IV
Conclusions

It is found that the narrow gap semiconductor Zn
3
As
2
is driven by
a left-hand circularly-polarized continuous-wave laser with
frequency nearly resonant with the band gap
E_{g}

to produce the two types of FWSM phases simultaneously in the crystal,
which are sharply distinguished by their spins.
The bulk rotational symmetry around the
z
-axis protects a pair of Weyl nodes
with opposite chirality along the

k_{z}
-axis in the respective FWSM phases
under the condition of either Eq. (
38
) or Eq. (
42
).
In the down-spin FWSM phase, the Floquet bands of
e(-1)
and
hh(1)
touch
in a linear manner
in the vicinity of the Weyl nodes situated
at
\pm\bm{k}^{W(-)}
, hosting the
nontrivial surface states pinned to both nodes.
Since the above-mentioned laser makes electrons excited exclusively
in the down-spin Floquet bands, it is considered that
the surface states are selectively occupied by
such spin-polarized electrons, showing transient magnetization with
partial modification by the inverse Faraday effect.
This surface magnetization would be measured by virtue of the magneto-optic Kerr effect.
On the other hand, in the up-spin FWSM phase,
the Floquet bands of
e(-1)
and
hh(1)
touch in the vicinity of the Weyl nodes situated
at
\pm\bm{k}^{W(+)}

almost in a quadratic manner in the
k_{x}
- and
k_{y}
-directions and in a linear manner
in the
k_{z}
-direction.
Because of the negligibly small band gap, this up-spin FWSM phase is rather
considered as the FNLSM phase.
To detect this phase somehow or other,
it would be necessary to make excited electrons occupied in the up-spin bands in advance
prior to the irradiation of the circularly polarized laser.
The exploration of the transient non-equilibrium dynamics of the concerned system
is inevitable in addition with Floquet band structures
to deepen the understandings of the underlying physics of the FWSMs.

B.1

The eigenvalue problem of the following Floquet Hamiltonian for up- and down-spins

L^{(\pm)}(\bm{k},t)=H^{(\pm)}(\bm{k},t)+V^{(\pm)}(t)-iI_{2}{\partial\over\partial t}

(77)

is solved approximately to obtain analytic expressions of eigenvalue
E(\bm{k})
,
where

H^{(\pm)}(\bm{k},t)
and
V^{(\pm)}(t)
are given in
Eqs. (
21
) and (
19
), respectively.
The associated Fourier-Floquet matrix

is represented as

\tilde{L}^{(\pm)}(\bm{k},\omega)=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{\,}cccc@{\,}}\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\reflectbox{$\ddots$}\\
\ldots&\mathcal{L}^{(\pm)}_{n,n}&\mathcal{X}^{(\pm)}_{n,n-1}&\ldots\\
\ldots&\mathcal{X}^{(\pm)}_{n-1,n}&\mathcal{L}^{(\pm)}_{n-1,n-1}&\ldots\\
\reflectbox{$\ddots$}&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{array}\right)

(78)

where

\mathcal{L}^{(\pm)}_{n,n}
and
\mathcal{X}^{(\pm)}_{n,n-1}

2\times 2
-block-matrices defined by

\mathcal{L}^{(\pm)}_{n,n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{\,}cccc@{\,}}\tilde{L}^{(\pm)}_{1(n)1(n)}&\tilde{L}^{(\pm)}_{1(n)2(n+1)}\\
\tilde{L}^{(\pm)}_{2(n+1)1(n)}&\tilde{L}^{(\pm)}_{2(n+1)2(n+1)}\end{array}\right)

(79)

and

\mathcal{X}^{(\pm)}_{n,n-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{\,}cccc@{\,}}\tilde{L}^{(\pm)}_{1(n)1(n-1)}&\tilde{L}^{(\pm)}_{1(n)2(n)}\\
\tilde{L}^{(\pm)}_{2(n+1)1(n-1)}&\tilde{L}^{(\pm)}_{2(n+1)2(n)}\end{array}\right)

(80)

with

\mathcal{X}^{(\pm)}_{n-1,n}=[\mathcal{X}^{(\pm)}_{n,n-1}]^{\dagger},

respectively.
Here,
\tilde{L}^{(\pm)}_{b(n)b^{\prime}(n^{\prime})}

the
(b,b^{\prime})
-matrix element of
L^{(\pm)}(\bm{k},t)

(81)

where it is understood that the index

Now, an approximation is made that

is replaced by
a sequence of
4\times 4
-block matrices
\{L^{(\pm)}_{n}\}

\tilde{L}^{(\pm)}(\bm{k},\omega)\approx\left(\begin{array}[]{@{\,}ccccc@{\,}}\ddots&&&&\\
&L^{(\pm)}_{n+1}&&O&\\
&&L^{(\pm)}_{n}&&\\
&{}^{t}O&&L^{(\pm)}_{n-1}&\\
&&&&\ddots\end{array}\right),

where

O
and its transpose

In Eq. (

\tilde{L}^{(\pm)}_{2(n+1)2(n+1)}

\tilde{L}^{(\pm)}_{1(n)2(n+1)}

\tilde{L}^{(\pm)}_{2(n+1)1(n)}

Setting a set of eigenvalues and the associated eigenvectors of

as
\mathcal{E}^{(\pm)}_{n,\alpha}
and
u^{(\pm)}_{n,\alpha}
with
\alpha=1,2

\mathcal{L}^{(\pm)}_{n,n}u^{(\pm)}_{n,\alpha}=\mathcal{E}^{(\pm)}_{n,\alpha}u^{(\pm)}_{n,\alpha},

(84)

2\times 2
-unitary matrix given by

U^{(\pm)}_{n}=\left(u^{(\pm)}_{n,1}u^{(\pm)}_{n,2}\right),

(85)

\bar{L}^{(\pm)}_{n}
defined as

\bar{L}^{(\pm)}_{n}=U^{(\pm)\dagger}_{n}L^{(\pm)}_{n}U^{(\pm)}_{n}

(86)

becomes of the form

\displaystyle\bar{L}^{(\pm)}_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{@{\,}cc@{\,}}\mathcal{E}^{(\pm)}_{n}&\bar{\mathcal{X}}^{(\pm)}_{n,n-1}\\
\bar{\mathcal{X}}^{(\pm)}_{n-1,n}&\mathcal{E}^{(\pm)}_{n-1}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{@{\,}c|cc|c@{\,}}\mathcal{E}^{(\pm)}_{n,1}&0&\times&\times\\
\hline\cr 0&\mathcal{E}^{(\pm)}_{n,2}&\mathcal{V}^{(\pm)}&\times\\
\times&\mathcal{V}^{(\pm)*}&\mathcal{E}^{(\pm)}_{n-1,1}&0\\
\hline\cr\times&\times&0&\mathcal{E}^{(\pm)}_{n-1,2}\end{array}\right),

where

\mathcal{E}^{(\pm)}_{n}=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{\,}cc@{\,}}\mathcal{E}^{(\pm)}_{n,1}&0\\
0&\mathcal{E}^{(\pm)}_{n,2}\end{array}\right)

(95)

and

\bar{\mathcal{X}}^{(\pm)}_{n.n-1}=U^{(\pm)\dagger}_{n}\mathcal{X}^{(\pm)}_{n,n-1}U^{(\pm)}_{n-1}\equiv\left(\begin{array}[]{@{\,}cc@{\,}}\times&\times\\

B.4
Floquet-Weyl Hamiltonian and Chirality

Here, effective Floquet-Weyl Hamiltonians the eigenvalues of which are given by
Eqs. (
145
) and (
146
) are derived from Eq. (
97
) in the vicinity of
the Weyl points
\bm{k}^{W(+)}
and
\bm{k}^{W(-)}
.
The Hamiltonian for the up-spin band, represented as

L_{n}^{W(+)}\equiv\bar{L}_{D,n}^{(+)}
, is cast into

\displaystyle L_{n}^{W(+)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\omega+\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\eta_{z}^{(1)}(\Delta k_{z}d_{z})&\sum_{l=x,y}\nu^{(+)}_{l}(k_{l}d_{l})\\
\sum_{l=x,y}\nu_{l}^{(+)*}(k_{l}d_{l})&\eta_{z}^{(1)}(\Delta k_{z}d_{z})\end{array}\right)

(154)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\omega+h_{+}^{(+)}\bm{p}^{(+)}\cdot\bm{\sigma},

where Eqs. (
99
), (
132
), (
133
), and (
134
) are used.
Further, the effective momentum
\bm{p}^{(+)}
, defined in the right-handed system,
is given by

\displaystyle p_{l}^{(+)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{+}^{(+)}\sum_{l^{\prime}=x,y,z}v_{ll^{\prime}}^{(+)}(k_{l^{\prime}}-k_{l^{\prime}}^{W(+)})

(155)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle h_{+}^{(+)}\bm{v}_{l}^{(+)}\cdot(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{W(+)}),

where

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\bm{v}_{x}^{(+)}=(\nu^{(+)}_{x}d_{x},0,0)\\
\bm{v}_{y}^{(+)}=(0,\nu^{(+)}_{x}d_{y},0)\\
\bm{v}_{z}^{(+)}=(0,0,-\eta_{z}^{(1)}d_{z})\end{array}\right.

and

h_{+}^{(+)}
is a helicity of particle at
the Weyl point
\bm{k}^{W(+)}
,
defined by

h_{+}^{(+)}=\text{sgn}(\bm{v}_{x}^{(+)}\times\bm{v}_{y}^{(+)}\cdot\bm{v}_{z}^{(+)}),

(160)

which is either +1 or -1.
Thus, the positive eigenvalue of Eq. (
154
) for
n=0
is given by

\displaystyle E^{W(+)}(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|(\eta_{z}^{(1)})^{2}(\Delta k_{z}d_{z})^{2}+\nu_{x}^{(+)2}\sum_{l=x,y}(k_{l}d_{l})^{2}\right|^{1/2}

(161)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\left|\eta_{z}^{(1)}(\Delta k_{z}d_{z})\right|,

where in the second equality the fact that
\nu^{(+)}_{x}\approx 0
is considered.
This is identical to Eq. (
145
) within the first order with respect to

k_{x},k_{y},
and
\Delta k_{z}
.

Similarly,
the Hamiltonian for the down-spin band, represented as

L_{n}^{W(-)}\equiv\bar{L}_{D,n}^{(-)}

\displaystyle L_{n}^{W(-)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\omega+\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-{\eta_{0}\eta_{z}^{(1)}\over(\omega/2)}(\Delta k_{z}d_{z})&\sum_{l=x,y}\nu^{(-)}_{l}(k_{l}d_{l})\\

\displaystyle n\omega+h_{+}^{(-)}\bm{p}^{(-)}\cdot\bm{\sigma}.

\bm{p}^{(-)}

\displaystyle p_{l}^{(-)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{+}^{(-)}\sum_{l^{\prime}=x,y,z}v_{ll^{\prime}}^{(-)}(k_{l^{\prime}}-k_{l^{\prime}}^{W(-)})

(166)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle h_{+}^{(-)}\bm{v}_{l}^{(-)}\cdot(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{W(-)}),

where

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\bm{v}_{x}^{(-)}=(\nu^{(-)}_{x}d_{x},0,0)\\
\bm{v}_{y}^{(-)}=(0,-\nu^{(-)}_{x}d_{y},0)\\
\bm{v}_{z}^{(-)}=(0,0,-{\eta_{0}\eta_{z}^{(1)}\over(\omega/2)}d_{z})\end{array}\right.

and

h_{+}^{(-)}
is a helicity of particle at
the Weyl point
\bm{k}^{W(-)}
, defined by

h_{+}^{(-)}=\text{sgn}(\bm{v}_{x}^{(-)}\times\bm{v}_{y}^{(-)}\cdot\bm{v}_{z}^{(-)}),

(171)

which is either +1 or -1.
It is obvious that the positive eigenvalue of Eq. (
165

n=0

E^{W(-)}(\bm{k})
,
is identical to Eq. (
146

Finally, the chiralities of FWSMs for both up- and down-spins are examined.
Let the helicities of the Weyl cones at
-\bm{k}^{W(+)}
and
-\bm{k}^{W(-)}

be represented as
h_{-}^{(+)}
and
h_{-}^{(-)}
, respectively.
It is evident that
h_{+}^{(+)}h_{-}^{(+)}=-1
and
h_{+}^{(-)}h_{-}^{(-)}=-1
,
since according to Eqs (
135
) and (
140

the nodal position
at
\bm{k}^{W(\pm)}

-\bm{k}^{W(\pm)}
still keeps

\nu_{x}^{(\pm)}

\eta_{z}^{(1)}

\eta_{z}^{(1)}

the sign of
\nu_{\pm}^{(+)}

\nu_{\pm}^{(-)}

Therefore, it is verified that each of four pairs of the Weyl cones at
\pm\bm{k}^{W(+)}

\pm\bm{k}^{W(-)}
,
\bm{k}^{W(\pm)}
, and
-\bm{k}^{W(\pm)}

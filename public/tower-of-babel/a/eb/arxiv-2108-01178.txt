Higman-Thompson groups from self-similar groupoid actions
Valentin Deaconu

Abstract
. Given a self-similar groupoid action ( G , E ) (G,E) on a finite directed graph, we prove some properties of the corresponding ample groupoid of germs 𝒢 ⁡ ( G , E ) \mathcal{G}(G,E) . We study the analogue of the Higman-Thompson group associated to ( G , E ) (G,E) using G G -tables and relate it to the topological full group of 𝒢 ⁡ ( G , E ) \mathcal{G}(G,E) , which is isomorphic to a subgroup of unitaries in the algebra C ∗ ​ ( G , E ) C^{*}(G,E) . After recalling some concepts in groupoid homology, we discuss the Matui’s AH-conjecture for 𝒢 ⁡ ( G , E ) \mathcal{G}(G,E) in some particular cases.

1.
introduction

Self-similar group actions are defined using subgroups of the automorphism group of a rooted tree which is viewed as the path space of a graph with one vertex and
n
edges. Given a finite directed graph
E
with no sources, the corresponding path space gives rise to a union of trees (or forest)
T_{E}
, and it is natural to consider self-similar actions of subgroupoids
G
of PIso
(T_{E})
, the set of partial isomorphsims of
T_{E}
.

Given such a self-similar action
(G,E)
, we study the properties of the generalized Exel-Pardo ample groupoid
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
. Since the source map of the graph is in general not
G
-equivariant, this determines certain modifications in the proofs of similar results from
[
6
]
, where
G
is a group acting on
E
by graph automorphisms. We study the topological full group
\ldbrack\mathcal{G}(G,E)\rdbrack
and we relate it to the Higman-Thompson type group defined by
G
-tables for the action
(G,E)
.
After recalling some results about groupoid homology, we discuss the Matui’s AH-conjecture for
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
and related issues.

We begin by recalling the definition of a self-similar groupoid action
(G,E)
from
[
8
]
and give several examples, illustrating some differences with the case when
G
is a group acting by automorphisms of the graph
E
. We recall some facts about the structure of the associated
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(G,E)
defined using a
C^{*}
-correspondence over
C^{*}(G)
and show that it contains a copy of the graph algebra
C^{*}(E)
. We review the definition of the ample groupoid of germs
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
, inspired from the Exel-Pardo groupoid from
[
6
]
and describe when this groupoid is minimal and effective.

We then define the Higman-Thompson group
V_{E}(G)
associated to
(G,E)
using
G
-tables and describe a faithful unitary representation of
V_{E}(G)
in the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(G,E)
. Topological full groups associated to dynamical systems and to étale groupoids are complete invariants for continuous orbit equivalence and groupoid isomorphism. They provide means of constructing new countable groups with interesting properties.

After recalling the definion of homology of étale groupoids introduced by Crainic and Moerdijk in
[
2
]
and Matui’s AH-conjecture, we specialize to the groupoid
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
associated to a self-similar action
(G,E)
and its topological full group
\ldbrack\mathcal{G}(G,E)\rdbrack
which is isomorphic to
V_{E}(G)
. The kernel
\mathcal{H}(G,E)
of the groupoid cocycle
\rho:\mathcal{G}(G,E)\to\mathbb{Z}
plays an important rôle in the computation of the groupoid homology
H_{i}(\mathcal{G}(G,E))
, particularly when
G
is transitive. We prove that in some cases, the index map
I_{\mathcal{H}}:\ldbrack\mathcal{H}(G,E)\rdbrack\to H_{1}(\mathcal{H}(G,E))
is surjective. We also show that in a particular example, the kernel of the index map
\ldbrack\mathcal{G}(G,E)\rdbrack\to H_{1}(\mathcal{G}(G,E))
is not generated by transpositions.

Self-similar actions of groupoids on graphs

Let

We are interested in self-similar actions of groupoids on the path space of directed graphs

A groupoid
G
is a small category with inverses. We will use
d
and
t
for the domain and target maps
d,t:G\to G^{(0)}
to distinguish them from the source and range maps
s,r:E^{1}\to E^{0}
on directed graphs. For

, we write

G_{u}=\{g\in G:d(g)=u\},\;\;G^{v}=\{g\in G:t(g)=v\},\;\;G_{u}^{v}=G_{u}\cap G^{v}.

The set of composable pairs is denoted
G^{(2)}
.

An étale groupoid is a topological groupoid where the target map
t
(and necessarily the
domain map
d
) is a local homeomorphism (as a map from
G
to
G
). The unit space
G^{(0)}
of an étale groupoid is always an open subset of
G
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G
be an étale groupoid. A bisection is an open subset
U\subseteq G
such that
d
and
t
are both injective when restricted to
U
.

Two units

belong to the same
G
-orbit if there exists
g\in G
such that
d(g)=x
and
t(g)=y
. We denote by

the
G
-orbit of
x
. When every
G
-orbit is dense in
G^{(0)}
, the groupoid

is called minimal.

The isotropy group of a unit

is the group

G_{x}^{x}:=\{g\in G\;|\;d(g)=t(g)=x\},

and the isotropy bundle is

G^{\prime}:=\{g\in G\;|\;d(g)=t(g)\}=\bigcup_{x\in G^{(0)}}G_{x}^{x}.

A groupoid
G
is said to be principal if all isotropy groups are trivial, or equivalently,
G^{\prime}=G^{(0)}
. We say that
G
is effective if the interior of
G^{\prime}
equals
G^{(0)}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
E
be a finite directed graph with no sources, and let

be a discrete groupoid with unit space

. A

(G,E)

g\cdot(e\mu)=(g\cdot e)(h\cdot\mu)\;\;\text{for all}\;\;\mu\in s(e)E^{*}.

We have

The faithfulness condition ensures that for each

Definition 2.5
.

A self-similar action

W:G\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{M}),\;\;W_{g}(i_{e}\otimes a)=\begin{cases}i_{g\cdot e}\otimes i_{g|_{e}}a\;\;\text{if}\;d(g)=r(e)\\
0\;\;\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where

and
i_{g}\in C_{c}(G)
are point masses for

and

. The inner product of

is given by

\langle\xi\otimes a,\eta\otimes b\rangle=\langle\langle\eta,\xi\rangle\;a,b\rangle=a^{*}\langle\xi,\eta\rangle\;b

for
\xi,\eta\in C(E^{1})
and

Remark 3.2
.

The elements

for

form a Parseval frame for

and every

In particular, if

denotes the dual

. The isomorphism is given by

i_{e_{j}}\otimes i_{g}\otimes i_{e_{k}}^{*}\mapsto e_{jk}\otimes i_{g}

for

and for matrix units

\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{X}(E))\to\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{M})
given by

We recall the following result, see Propositions 4.4 and 4.7 in

]

Theorem 3.3
.

If

and

There is a gauge action

Remark 3.4
.

The graph

by considering elements

For each

\phi_{k}(i_{\mu}\otimes i_{g}\otimes i_{\nu}^{*})=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{x\in d(g)E^{1}}i_{\mu y}\otimes i_{g|_{x}}\otimes i_{\nu x}^{*},\;\text{if}\;g\in G_{s(\nu)}^{s(\mu)}\;\text{and}\;g\cdot x=y\\
0,\;\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Definition 3.5
.

A self-similar groupoid action
(G,E)
is called pseudo free if for every
g\in G
and every

, the condition
g\cdot e=e
and

implies that

.

For example, the self-similar action in Example

is pseudo free.

Remark 3.6
.

If
(G,E)
is pseudo free, then
g_{1}\cdot\alpha=g_{2}\cdot\alpha
and
g_{1}|_{\alpha}=g_{2}|_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in E^{*}
implies
g_{1}=g_{2}
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Given a self-similar groupoid action
(G,E)
, there is an inverse semigroup

\mathcal{S}(G,E)=\{(\alpha,g,\beta):\alpha,\beta\in E^{*},g\in G_{s(\beta)}^{s(\alpha)}\}\cup\{0\}

with operations

(\alpha,g,\beta)(\gamma,h,\zeta)=\begin{cases}(\alpha,g(h|_{h^{-1}\cdot\mu}),\zeta(h^{-1}\cdot\mu))&\;\text{if}\;\beta=\gamma\mu\\
(\alpha(g\cdot\mu),g|_{\mu}h,\zeta)&\;\text{if}\;\gamma=\beta\mu\\
0&\;\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

and
(\alpha,g,\beta)^{*}=(\beta,g^{-1},\alpha)
.

Remark 3.8
.

The inverse semigroup
\mathcal{S}(G,E)
acts on the infinite path space
E^{\infty}
by partial homeomorphisms. The action of
(\alpha,g,\beta)\in\mathcal{S}(G,E)
on
\xi=\beta\mu\in\beta E^{\infty}
is given by

(\alpha,g,\beta)\cdot\beta\mu=\alpha(g\cdot\mu)\in\alpha E^{\infty}.

Note that

r(g\cdot\mu)=g\cdot r(\mu)=g\cdot s(\beta)=s(\alpha),

so the action is well defined.

Theorem 3.9
.

If the self-similar action
(G,E)
is pseudo free, then there is a locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
such that

C^{*}(G,E)\cong C^{*}(\mathcal{G}(G,E)).

If
G
is amenable, then
C^{*}(G,E)
is nuclear and
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
is also amenable.

Proof.

Consider the groupoid of germs associated with
(\mathcal{S}(G,E),E^{\infty})
:

\mathcal{G}(G,E)=\{[\alpha,g,\beta;\xi]:\alpha,\beta\in E^{*},\;g\in G^{s(\alpha)}_{s(\beta)},\;\xi\in\beta E^{\infty}\}.

Two germs
[\alpha,g,\beta;\xi],[\alpha^{\prime},g^{\prime},\beta^{\prime};\xi^{\prime}]
in
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
are equal if and only if
\xi=\xi^{\prime}
and there is an idempotent
z\in\mathcal{S}(G,E)\setminus\{0\}
such that
z\cdot\xi=\xi
and
(\alpha,g,\beta)z=(\alpha^{\prime},g^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})z
.
Using the form of idempotents, we obtain that
\xi=\beta\gamma\eta
for
\gamma\in E^{*}
and
\eta\in E^{\infty}
, with
r(\gamma)=s(\beta)
and
r(\eta)=s(\gamma)
. Moreover,

\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha(g\cdot\gamma),\beta^{\prime}=\beta\gamma,\;\mbox{and}\;g^{\prime}=g|_{\gamma},

so

[\alpha,g,\beta;\beta\gamma\eta]=[\alpha(g\cdot\gamma),g|_{\gamma},\beta\gamma;\beta\gamma\eta].

The unit space of
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
is

\mathcal{G}(G,E)^{(0)}=\{[\alpha,s(\alpha),\alpha;\xi]:\xi\in\alpha E^{\infty}\},

identified with
E^{\infty}
by the map
[\alpha,s(\alpha),\alpha;\xi]\mapsto\xi
.

The target and domain maps of the groupoid
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
are given by

t([\alpha,g,\beta;\beta\mu])=\alpha(g\cdot\mu),\;\;d([\alpha,g,\beta;\beta\mu])=\beta\mu.

If two elements
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathcal{G}(G,E)
are composable, then

\gamma_{1}=[\alpha_{1},g_{1},\alpha_{2};\alpha_{2}(g_{2}\cdot\xi)],\;\;\gamma_{2}=[\alpha_{2},g_{2},\beta;\beta\xi]

for some
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta\in E^{*},\xi\in E^{\infty},g_{1},g_{2}\in G
and in this case

\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}=[\alpha_{1},g_{1}g_{2},\beta;\beta\xi].

In particular,
[\alpha,g,\beta;\beta\mu]^{-1}=[\beta,g^{-1},\alpha;\alpha(g\cdot\mu)]
.

The topology on
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
is generated by the compact open bisections of the form

Z(\alpha,g,\beta;U)=\{[\alpha,g,\beta;\xi]\in\mathcal{G}(G,E):\xi\in U\},

where
U
is an open compact subset of
Z(\beta)=\beta E^{\infty}
. Since any open set
U\subseteq Z(\beta)
is a disjoint union
\displaystyle U=\bigsqcup_{i}Z(\beta\gamma_{i})
where
\gamma_{i}\in E^{*}
with
r(\gamma_{i})=s(\beta)
, we get

Z(\alpha,g,\beta;U)=\bigsqcup_{i}Z(\alpha,g,\beta;Z(\beta\gamma_{i}))=\bigsqcup_{i}Z(\alpha(g\cdot\gamma_{i}),g|_{\gamma_{i}},\beta\gamma_{i};Z(\beta\gamma_{i})).

It follows that the sets
Z(\alpha,g,\beta)=Z(\alpha,g,\beta;Z(\beta))
form a basis for the topology of
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
. In particular,

d(Z(\alpha,g,\beta))=Z(\beta)\;\;\text{and}\;\;t(Z(\alpha,g,\beta))=Z(\alpha).

Since
(G,E)
is pseudo free, it follows that
[\alpha,g,\beta;\xi]=[\alpha,g^{\prime},\beta;\xi]
if and only if
g=g^{\prime}
. Moreover, the groupoid
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
is Hausdorff, see Proposition 12.1 in
[
6
]
. Using the properties given in Theorem
3.3
and the groupoid multiplication, the isomorphism
\phi:C^{*}(G,E)\to C^{*}(\mathcal{G}(G,E))
is given by

\phi(P_{v})=\chi_{Z(v,v,v)},

\phi(T_{e})=\chi_{Z(e,s(e),s(e))},

\phi(U_{g})=\chi_{Z(t(g),g,d(g))}

for
v\in E^{0},e\in E^{1}
and
g\in G
. Here
\chi_{Z}
is the indicator function of
Z
.

Since
G
is amenable, it follows that
C^{*}(G)
is nuclear and hence the Cuntz-Pimsner algebra
C^{*}(G,E)
is nuclear. The same kind of argument as in
[
6
]
shows that
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
is also amenable.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

Note that the graph groupoid
\mathcal{G}_{E}
is isomorphic to an open subgroupoid of

with the same unit space

\mathcal{G}_{E}=\{(\alpha\xi,|\alpha|-|\beta|,\beta\xi)\in E^{\infty}\times\mathbb{Z}\times E^{\infty}:\alpha,\beta\in E^{*},\;s(\alpha)=s(\beta)\}\cong

\cong\{[\alpha,s(\alpha),\beta;\beta\xi]\in\mathcal{G}(G,E):\alpha,\beta\in E^{*},\;s(\alpha)=s(\beta),\;\xi\in E^{\infty}\}.

Definition 3.11
.

Given a self-similar action
(G,E)
, we say that

is
G
-transitive if given two vertices

, there are vertices
u_{0}=u,u_{1},...,u_{2n}=v
such that we can connect each

to

by a path in

and there are

with

and

for all

Using the same method as in Theorem 13.6 and Corollary 13.7 in
[

Theorem 3.12
.

Given a self-similar action
(G,E)
, the groupoid
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
is minimal iff

is
G
-transitive. In particular,
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
is minimal if

is transitive or if the adjacency matrix

of

is irreducible. The groupoid
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
is also minimal if the action of

is level transitive, see Definition
2.5
.

Recall that a circuit (also called a cycle or a loop) in
E
is a path
\alpha\in E^{*}
with
|\alpha|\geq 1
such that
s(\alpha)=r(\alpha)
. A
G
-circuit in

is a pair
(g,\alpha)
with
g\in G
and
\alpha\in d(g)E^{*}
with

such that

.

Note that a circuit

in

may be concatenated to produce an infinite path
\xi=\alpha\alpha\alpha\cdots
in

. If

, then

since
r(\alpha)=s(\alpha)
.

To create fixed points from
G
-circuits
(g,\alpha)
,
define a sequence
\{\alpha^{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
of finite paths and a sequence
\{g_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
of groupoid elements recursively by
\alpha^{1}=\alpha,g_{1}=g
and
\alpha^{n+1}=g_{n}\cdot\alpha^{n}
where

for

. Then

r(\alpha^{2})=r(g\cdot\alpha)=g\cdot r(\alpha)=s(\alpha)

and assuming

for some

r(\alpha^{k+1})=r(g_{k}\cdot\alpha^{k})=g_{k}\cdot r(\alpha^{k})=g_{k}\cdot s(\alpha^{k-1})=

=g_{k-1}|_{\alpha^{k-1}}\cdot s(\alpha^{k-1})=s(g_{k-1}\cdot\alpha^{k-1})=s(\alpha^{k}).

The concatenation

is an infinite path such that
g\cdot\xi=\alpha^{2}\alpha^{3}\alpha^{4}\cdots
. For any path
\beta\in E^{*}
with

, the infinite path
\beta\xi
is fixed by

because

(\beta\alpha,g,\beta)\cdot\beta\xi=\beta\alpha(g\cdot\xi)=\beta\xi.

Remark 3.13
.

Given
z=(\alpha,g,\beta)\in\mathcal{S}(G,E)
with
|\alpha|>|\beta|
, then
z
admits at most one fixed point in
E^{\infty}
and assuming
z\cdot\xi=\xi
, then there is a

-circuit
(g,\gamma)
such that
\alpha=\beta\gamma
and
\xi=\beta\zeta
with
\zeta
constructed from
(g,\gamma)
.

Indeed, if
z\cdot\xi=\xi
, then
\xi\in Z(\beta)
so
\xi=\beta\zeta
. Then

\beta\zeta=\xi=(\alpha,g,\beta)(\beta\zeta)=\alpha(g\cdot\zeta)

and since

, we must have
\alpha=\beta\gamma
such that
g\cdot r(\gamma)=g\cdot s(\beta)=s(\alpha)=s(\gamma)
, i.e.
(g,\gamma)
is a G-circuit. Moreover,
\beta\zeta=\beta\gamma(g\cdot\zeta)
, so
\zeta=\gamma(g\cdot\zeta)
. If we write
\zeta=\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}\cdots
with

for

, then

\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}\cdots=\gamma(g\cdot\zeta)=\gamma(g\cdot\gamma^{1})(g|_{\gamma^{1}}\cdot\gamma^{2})(g|_{\gamma^{1}}|_{\gamma^{2}}\cdot\gamma^{3})\cdots.

It follows that
\gamma^{1}=\gamma
and
\gamma^{n+1}=g_{n}\cdot\gamma^{n}
where
g_{1}=g
and

Using the methods of Theorem 14.10 and Corollary 14.13 in
[

to prove that the interior of the isotropy of

is

, we obtain

Theorem 3.14
.

Suppose that we have a pseudo free self-similar action
(G,E)
. Then the groupoid
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
is effective if and only if

(a) every
G
-circuit has an entry (which is the same as saying that every circuit has an entry, since

is finite);

(b) for every

there is
\zeta\in Z(d(g))
such that

Corollary 3.15
.

If
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
is Hausdorff, effective and minimal, then
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
is purely infinite.

Proof.

This follows since the open ample subgroupoid
\mathcal{G}_{E}
of
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
is purely infinite.
∎

For example, the groupoid
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
obtained from Example

-algebra is simple and purely infinite.

4.
Tables and the Higman-Thompson groups

Consider a finite directed graph
E
with no sources such that
E^{\infty}
is a Cantor set. We recall first some facts about the Higman-Thompson group of
E
.

Definition 4.1
.

A table over the graph
E
is a matrix of the form

\tau=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\alpha_{1}&\alpha_{2}&\cdots&\alpha_{m}\\
\beta_{1}&\beta_{2}&\cdots&\beta_{m}\end{array}\right),

where
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\in E^{*}
are such that
s(\alpha_{i})=s(\beta_{i})
and
E^{\infty}
is decomposed into disjoint unions

E^{\infty}=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{m}Z(\alpha_{i})=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{m}Z(\beta_{j}),

i.e. for every infinite path
\mu\in E^{\infty}
exactly one
\alpha_{i}
and exactly one
\beta_{j}
is a prefix of
\mu
.

It follows that the finite paths in each row of a table are incomparable (none of them is a begining of the other) and the corresponding cylinder sets
Z(\alpha_{i})
and
Z(\beta_{j})
are indeed disjoint. Every table
\tau
defines a homeomorphism
\bar{\tau}
of
E^{\infty}
by the rule

\bar{\tau}(\beta_{i}\mu)=\alpha_{i}\mu,\;\text{ for }\;i=1,...,m.

This homeomorphism does not change if we permute the columns of
\tau
. Its inverse is given by

\tau^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\beta_{1}&\beta_{2}&\cdots&\beta_{m}\\
\alpha_{1}&\alpha_{2}&\cdots&\alpha_{m}\end{array}\right).

A split of a table is a matrix obtained from the original one by several replacements of a column
\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\alpha_{i}\\
\beta_{i}\end{array}\right)
by a matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\alpha_{i}e_{1}&\alpha_{i}e_{2}&\cdots&\alpha_{i}e_{d}\\
\beta_{i}e_{1}&\beta_{i}e_{2}&\cdots&\beta_{i}e_{d}\end{array}\right),

where
s(\alpha_{i})E^{1}=\{e_{1},e_{2},...,e_{d}\}=s(\beta_{i})E^{1}
. It is easy to check that a split of a table is also a table, and two tables
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
define the same homeomorphisms of
E^{\infty}
if they have splits which agree up to a permutation of the columns.

The set of all homeomorphisms of
E^{\infty}
defined by such tables is a subgroup of Homeo
(E^{\infty})
, called the Higman-Thompson group of
E
and denoted by
V_{E}
. In
[
9
]
, the authors define tables associated to a one-sided topological Markov shift
(X_{A},\sigma_{A})
for an irreducible
N\times N
matrix
A
with entries in
\{0,1\}
. They studied the corresponding Higman-Thompson group and proved that it is isomorphic to the topological full group of the groupoid associated to
(X_{A},\sigma_{A})
. Their results were generalized for graph groupoids in
[
18
]
.

Remark 4.2
.

The correspondence

\tau=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\alpha_{1}&\alpha_{2}&\cdots&\alpha_{m}\\
\beta_{1}&\beta_{2}&\cdots&\beta_{m}\end{array}\right)\mapsto T=S_{\alpha_{1}}S_{\beta_{1}}^{*}+S_{\alpha_{2}}S_{\beta_{2}}^{*}+\cdots+S_{\alpha_{m}}S_{\beta_{m}}^{*}

defines a faithful unitary representation of the group
V_{E}
in the
C^{*}
-algebra of the graph
E
. Moreover,

T\in N(C(E^{\infty}),C^{*}(E))=\{u\in U(C^{*}(E)):uC(E^{\infty})u^{*}=C(E^{\infty})\}

and
f\circ\bar{\tau}^{-1}=TfT^{*}
for
f\in C(E^{\infty})
.

Proof.

Indeed, as in
[
9
]
,
T=S_{\alpha_{1}}S_{\beta_{1}}^{*}+S_{\alpha_{2}}S_{\beta_{2}}^{*}+\cdots+S_{\alpha_{m}}S_{\beta_{m}}^{*}
is a unitary in
C^{*}(E)
since
\displaystyle E^{\infty}=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{m}Z(\alpha_{i})=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{m}Z(\beta_{i})
and therefore

T^{*}T=(\sum_{i=1}^{m}S_{\beta_{i}}S_{\alpha_{i}}^{*})(\sum_{j=1}^{m}S_{\alpha_{j}}S_{\beta_{j}}^{*})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}S_{\beta_{i}}S_{\beta_{i}}^{*}=\sum_{v\in E^{0}}P_{v}=I,

TT^{*}=(\sum_{i=1}^{m}S_{\alpha_{i}}S_{\beta_{i}}^{*})(\sum_{j=1}^{m}S_{\beta_{j}}S_{\alpha_{j}}^{*})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}S_{\alpha_{i}}S_{\alpha_{i}}^{*}=\sum_{v\in E^{0}}P_{v}=I.

For
f=\chi_{Z(\eta)}=S_{\eta}S_{\eta}^{*}\in C(E^{\infty})
a computation shows that
T\chi_{Z(\eta)}T^{*}=\chi_{Z(\eta)}\circ\bar{\tau}^{-1}
.
∎

Example 4.3
.

For the graph

in Example

, a table is

u&e_{5}&e_{4}&v\end{array}\right),

Definition 4.4
.

A

g\\

5.
Topological full groups and the AH conjecture

The study of full groups in the setting of topological dynamics was initiated by T. Giordano, I. F. Putnam and C. F. Skau in
[
7
]
. For a minimal action
\varphi:\mathbb{Z}\curvearrowright X
on a Cantor set, they defined several types of full groups and showed that these groups completely determine the orbit equivalence class, the strong orbit equivalence class and the flip conjugacy class of
\varphi
, respectively. Topological full groups associated to dynamical systems and to étale groupoids are complete invariants for continuous orbit equivalence and groupoid isomorphism.
They also provide means of constructing new groups with interesting properties, most notably by providing the first examples of finitely generated infinite simple groups that are amenable.

Example 5.1
.

For the
AF
-equivalence relation associated to a Bratteli diagram
(V,E)
with levels
(V_{m},E_{m})
, the topological full group is an increasing union of groups of the form
\displaystyle\bigoplus_{v\in V_{m}}S_{n(v)}
, where
n(v)
denotes the number of paths from the top vertex
v_{0}
to
v\in V_{m}
and
S_{n}
is the symmetric group, see Proposition 3.3 in
[
10
]
.

Definition 5.2
.

A second countable groupoid
G
is ample if it is étale and
G^{(0)}
is zero-dimensional; equivalently,
G
is ample if it has a basis of compact open bisections. An ample groupoid
G
is elementary if it is compact and principal.
G
is an
AF
groupoid if there exists an ascending chain of open elementary subgroupoids
G_{1}\subseteq G_{2}\subseteq...\subseteq G
such that
G=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}G_{i}
.

We recall now the definion of homology of étale groupoids which was introduced by Crainic and Moerdijk in
[
2
]
. Let
A
be an abelian group and
let
\pi:X\to Y
be a local homeomorphism between two locally compact Hausdorff spaces. Given any
f\in C_{c}(X,A)
we define

\pi_{*}(f)(y):=\sum_{\pi(x)=y}f(x).

It follows that
\pi_{*}(f)\in C_{c}(Y,A)
.
Given an étale groupoid
G
, let
G^{(1)}=G
and for
n\geq 2
let
G^{(n)}
be the space of composable strings
of
n
elements in
G
with the product topology. For
n\geq 2
and
i=0,...,n
, we let
\partial_{i}:G^{(n)}\to G^{(n-1)}
be the face maps defined by

\partial_{i}(g_{1},g_{2},...,g_{n})=\begin{cases}(g_{2},g_{3},...,g_{n})&\;\text{if}\;i=0,\\
(g_{1},...,g_{i}g_{i+1},...,g_{n})&\;\text{if}\;1\leq i\leq n-1,\\
(g_{1},g_{2},...,g_{n-1})&\;\text{if}\;i=n.\end{cases},

which are local homeomorphisms.
We define the homomorphisms
\delta_{n}:C_{c}(G^{(n)},A)\to C_{c}(G^{(n-1)},A)
given by

\delta_{1}=d_{*}-t_{*},\;\;\delta_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\partial_{i*}\;\text{for}\;n\geq 2.

Recall that
d,t:G\to G^{(0)}
are the domain and target maps.
It can be verified that
\delta_{n}\circ\delta_{n+1}=0
for all
n\geq 1
.

The homology groups
H_{n}(G,A)
are by definition the homology groups of the chain complex
C_{c}(G^{(*)},A)
given by

0\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta_{0}}}{{\longleftarrow}}C_{c}(G^{(0)},A)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta_{1}}}{{\longleftarrow}}C_{c}(G^{(1)},A)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta_{2}}}{{\longleftarrow}}C_{c}(G^{(2)},A)\longleftarrow\cdots,

i.e.
H_{n}(G,A)=\ker\delta_{n}/\text{im }\delta_{n+1}
, where
\delta_{0}=0
.
If
A=\mathbb{Z}
, we write
H_{n}(G)
for
H_{n}(G,\mathbb{Z})
.

Example 5.3
.

For the transformation groupoid
\Gamma\ltimes X
associated to a countable discrete group action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
on a Cantor set, it follows that

H_{n}(\Gamma\ltimes X)\cong H_{n}(\Gamma,C(X,\mathbb{Z})),

the group homology of
\Gamma
with coefficients in
C(X,\mathbb{Z})
.

If
U\subseteq G
is a bisection of an étale groupoid
G
, then
t|_{U}:U\to t(U)
is a homeomorphism, and similarly for
d
. Thus we get a homeomorphism
\pi_{U}:=t|_{U}\circ(d|_{U})^{-1}
from
d(U)
to
t(U)
which maps
d(g)
to
t(g)
for each
g\in U
. We say that the bisection
U
is full if
t(U)=d(U)=G^{(0)}
, and in this case
\pi_{U}
is a homeomorphism of
G^{(0)}
. For a homeomorphism
\alpha:X\to X
of a topological space
X
we define the support of
\alpha
to be the set

\text{supp}(\alpha):=\overline{\{x\in X\mid\alpha(x)\neq x\}}.

Definition 5.4
.

The topological full group of an effective étale groupoid
G
is

\ldbrack G\rdbrack:=\{\pi_{U}\mid U\subseteq G\;\text{full bisection}\},

which is a subgroup of the homeomorphism group of
G^{(0)}
such that
\pi_{U}\circ\pi_{V}=\pi_{UV}
. The commutator subgroup of
\ldbrack G\rdbrack
is denoted by
D(\ldbrack G\rdbrack)
and the abelianization of
\ldbrack G\rdbrack
is
\ldbrack G\rdbrack_{ab}=\ldbrack G\rdbrack/D(\ldbrack G\rdbrack)
.

We observe that when
G
is effective and Hausdorff, then
\text{supp}(\pi_{U})
is also open for any full bisection
U
.

Remark 5.5
.

For a second countable effective ample groupoid
G
, the group
\ldbrack G\rdbrack
is at most countable and there is a natural short exact sequence

1\to U(C(G^{(0)}))\to N(C(G^{(0)}),C^{*}_{r}(G))\to\ldbrack G\rdbrack\to 1,

where
N(C(G^{(0)}),C^{*}_{r}(G))
denotes the group of unitary normalizers of
C(G^{(0)})
in
C^{*}_{r}(G)
(Proposition 5.6 in
[
11
]
).

Given a bisection
U\subseteq G
with
t(U)\cap d(U)=\emptyset
, construct

\hat{U}:=U\sqcup U^{-1}\sqcup(G^{(0)}\setminus(t(U)\cup d(U))),

which is a full bisection. The associated homeomorphism
\pi_{\hat{U}}\in\ldbrack G\rdbrack
satisfies

\pi_{\hat{U}}(d(U))=t(U),\;\pi_{\hat{U}}(t(U))=d(U),

\text{supp}(\pi_{\hat{U}})=t(U)\cup d(U),\;\pi_{\hat{U}}\circ\pi_{\hat{U}}=id_{G^{(0)}}.

An element
\pi\in\ldbrack G\rdbrack
that satisfies
\pi^{2}=id_{G^{(0)}}
is called a transposition.

For an effective minimal second countable Hausdorff ample groupoid
G
with
G^{(0)}
a Cantor set, the AH-conjecture of Matui claims that the following sequence is exact

H_{0}(G)\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j}}{{\longrightarrow}}\ldbrack G\rdbrack_{ab}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle I_{ab}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H_{1}(G)\to 0.

In particular, if the group
H_{0}(G)
is
2
-divisible, then this implies that
\ldbrack G\rdbrack_{ab}\cong H_{1}(G)
.
The map
I_{ab}
is induced by the index map
I:\ldbrack G\rdbrack\to H_{1}(G)
given by
\pi_{U}\mapsto[\chi_{U}]
, where
U
is a full bisection of
G
and
\chi_{U}
is the indicator function of
U
, which belongs to
\ker\delta_{1}=\ker(d_{*}-t_{*})
. The map
j
takes
[\chi_{d(U)}]\otimes\bar{1}
into
[\pi_{\hat{U}}]
, where
U\subseteq G
is a bisection with
d(U)\cap t(U)=\emptyset
and
\pi_{\hat{U}}\in\ldbrack G\rdbrack
is the transposition defined above.

Remark 5.6
.

The AH-conjecture has been verified for several cases of groupoids, but so far no counter-example has been found. It is shown by H. Matui in
[
12
]
that if the groupoid
G
is purely infinite, then the index map
I:\ldbrack G\rdbrack\to H_{1}(G)
is surjective. Also, in this case,
\ldbrack G\rdbrack
contains a copy of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\ast\mathbb{Z}_{3}
, so
\ldbrack G\rdbrack
is not amenable.

Denote by
\mathcal{T}(G)
the subgroup of
\ldbrack G\rdbrack
generated by all transpositions. The subgroup
\mathcal{T}(G)
is the analog of the symmetric group. One has
\mathcal{T}(G)\subseteq\ker(I)
. Indeed, using Lemma 7.3 in
[
11
]
we have

I(\pi_{\hat{U}})=[\chi_{\hat{U}}]=[\chi_{U\sqcup U^{-1}\sqcup(G^{(0)}\setminus\text{supp}(\pi_{\hat{U}})}]=[\chi_{U}+\chi_{U^{-1}}]=0\in H_{1}(G).

We say that
G
has property TR if
\mathcal{T}(G)=\ker(I)
. In order to verify the AH conjecture for
G
, it suffices to establish property TR. In general, property TR implies the inclusion
\ker(I_{ab})\subseteq\text{im}(j)
. The converse holds if the commutator subgroup
D(\ldbrack G\rdbrack)
is simple, since in this case
D(\ldbrack G\rdbrack)=\mathcal{A}(G)
, the analog of the alternating subgroup of
\mathcal{T}(G)
defined in
[
17
]
. The group
D(\ldbrack G\rdbrack)
is known to be simple for minimal groupoids which are either almost finite or purely infinite, see
[
12
]
.

We now specialize to the groupoid
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
associated to a self-similar action
(G,E)
such that
E^{\infty}
is a Cantor set. Recall that
E
is a finite graph with no sources and
G^{(0)}=E^{0}
.

Theorem 5.7
.

For a self-similar action
(G,E)
such that
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
is effective, we have
V_{E}(G)\cong\ldbrack\mathcal{G}(G,E)\rdbrack
. In particular,
\ldbrack\mathcal{G}_{E}\rdbrack\subseteq\ldbrack\mathcal{G}(G,E)\rdbrack
.

Proof.

Indeed, given a unitary element
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}S_{\alpha_{i}}U_{g_{i}}S_{\beta_{i}}^{*}
of

corresponding to the
G
-table
\displaystyle\tau=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\alpha_{1}&\alpha_{2}&\cdots&\alpha_{m}\\
g_{1}&g_{2}&\cdots&g_{m}\\
\beta_{1}&\beta_{2}&\cdots&\beta_{m}\end{array}\right)
, this determines a full bisection

of

and an element
\pi_{U}=\bar{\tau}
of
\ldbrack\mathcal{G}(G,E)\rdbrack
, so we get a map

. It is easy to see that this map is a group homomorphism. It is injective since if

determines the identity of

, then

and

To show surjectivity, given

, since
\mathcal{G}(G,E)
is effective, there is a unique full bisection

with

which determines an element

.
∎

We have

G_{u}^{u}\cong\mathbb{Z}
. In particular,
\tau
is not a product of transpositions.

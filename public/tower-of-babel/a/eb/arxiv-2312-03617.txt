Gordana Matić, Ferit Öztürk, Javier Reyes, András I. Stipsicz, Giancarlo Urzúa

Abstract
. We show an example of an embedded copy of 5 ​ ℝ ​ ℙ 2 5{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}} in the four-sphere S 4 S^{4} which is topologically standard but smoothly knotted, i.e. smoothly not isotopic to the standard embedding.



1.
Introduction

The real projective plane
{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
can be embedded in the four-sphere

S^{4}
by simply capping off a Möbius band in
S^{3}
having unknotted
boundary, with a slice disk of its boundary unknot. Indeed, in this
way we get two different embeddings, as there are two Möbius bands
with the property above. The two
{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
’s will have normal Euler
numbers
-2
and
2
. Another way to present these embeddings is to
take the quotient of the complex projective plane
{\mathbb{C}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
(or
{\overline{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}}
)
with the involution given by complex conjugation in homogeneous
coordinates
[
16
]
. The quotient is

S^{4}
, and the fixed point set is the real projective
plane
{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
. Connect summing the two embeddings above in

S^{4}
we get embeddings of
n{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
into
S^{4}
with Euler numbers
-2n,-2n+4,\ldots,2n-4,2n
. These embeddings are the
standard
embeddings
with the appropriate Euler numbers. We note that the
complement of the standardly embedded
n{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
has fundamental
group isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

In
[
5
,
6
]
S. Finashin and M. Kreck showed that the 10-fold connected sum

10{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
admits (indeed, infinitely many) embeddings with Euler number

\pm 16
into
S^{4}
which are topologically isotopic,
but smoothly not. The argument utilized the discovery of exotic
smooth structures on the four-manifold
{\mathbb{C}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}\#9{\overline{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}}
by
S. Donaldson
[
3
]
. This construction was improved by S. Finashin
in
[
4
]
to exotic embeddings of
6{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
with Euler number
\pm 8

— the basis of the argument was similar to the one above: in this
case, the existence of exotic smooth structures on
{\mathbb{C}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}\#5{\overline{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}}

that were found in
[
20
]
. Further exotic embeddings of

n{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
with
n=6,7,8,9
and other Euler numbers were found by
A. Havens
[
10
]
.

The common theme of the proofs of the results mentioned above was that
the exotic smooth manifolds can be presented as double branched covers
of
S^{4}
branched along a connected sum of some number of copies of

{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
; the fact that the branched covers are not diffeomorphic then
shows the non-existence of smooth isotopies (or even diffeomorphisms
of pairs) between the branching loci. This method constructed
infinitely many pairwise smoothly non-isotopic embeddings. An
adaptation of the method of M. Kreck
[
12
]
then verified that
there are at most finitely many topological (locally flat) isotopy
classes, providing infinitely many topologically isotopic, smoothly
distinct smooth embeddings.

In this paper we show

Theorem 1.1
.

There is an embedding
\iota\colon 5{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}\hookrightarrow S^{4}
with
Euler number
6
which is
smoothly non-isotopic to the standard embedding, such that
the complement
S^{4}\setminus\iota(5{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}})
has fundamental group
isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.

We combine this theorem with a recent result of
A. Conway, P. Orson and M. Powell
[
2
, Theorem A]
,
stating that a locally flat embedding of
n{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}

into
S^{4}
with the fundamental group of the complement
being
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and the Euler number being less than
2n
in absolute
value is topologically (locally flat) isotopic to the standard embedding.
As a result, we get

Corollary 1.2
.

There is an embedding of
5{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
into
S^{4}
with
normal Euler number
6
which is topologically
isotopic to the standard embedding, but smoothly non-isotopic to it.

Proof.

According to
[
2
, Theorem A]
an embedding of

5{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
with Euler number
6
and complement with fundamental
group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is topologically isotopic to the standard embedding
with the same Euler number.
The corollary then follows from Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Remark 1.3
.

Theorem A of
[
2
]
indeed implies that all the embeddings found in

[
4
,
5
,
6
]
are exotic, that is, topologically isotopic
to the standard embedding,
but smoothly not isotopic to it.

Recently a very interesting preprint
[
17
]
of J. Miyazawa
appeared on the arXiv, claiming a much stronger result: the existence
of infinitely many exotic embeddings of a single
{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
in
S^{4}
. The
techniques of the proof, and indeed the embeddings found in

[
17
]
are, however, very different from the one we discuss
in this paper: Miyazawa’s exotic examples are connected sums of the
standard
{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
with knotted spheres, hence the double branched covers
all contain spheres generating the second homology, implying that the
Seiberg-Witten invariants of the branched covers are trivial. In fact,
the proof of the fact that the embeddings are smoothly distinct relies
on a novel invariant (called Real Seiberg-Witten invariant in

[
17
]
). Indeed, it was shown that the double branched
covers along these smoothly knotted
{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
’s are all standard
(diffeomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
with one of its orientations)
[
11
]
,
and the involutions generated by the branched covers are exotic. The
following question is still open:

Question 1.4
.

Is there a smooth exotic
{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}\subset S^{4}
which is not the connected
sum of the standard
{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
with a knotted sphere?

The main ingredient of the proof of Theorem
1.1

is the construction of an
exotic smooth structure on

{\mathbb{C}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}\#4{\overline{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}}
via the rational blow-down method
in the fashion discussed in
[
22
,
23
]
.
By choosing the appropriate construction, the implementation of
Finashin’s methods from
[
4
]
leads to the proof of
Theorem
1.1
. The crucial observation in this construction
is that in the pencil defining the elliptic fibration on
{\mathbb{C}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}\#9{\overline{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}}

the generating curves and all the points to be blown up in the further
blow-ups are
real, so that one can perform each blow-up equivariantly. Moreover,
as in
[
4
]
, one can perform the rational blow-downs equivariantly,
thus the resulting four-manifold has a natural involution, with the
fixed point set
\widehat{F}\cong\#5{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
and quotient
S^{4}
.

Remark 1.5
.

Notice that Theorem
1.1
provides a single exotic
embedding of
5{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
, while
[
4
]
gives infinitely many distinct
embeddings for
6{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
. This difference is due to the fact that at
the moment we have only one example of an exotic
{\mathbb{C}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}\#4{\overline{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}}

with an appropriate involution, while there are infinitely many
exotic
{\mathbb{C}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}\#5{\overline{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}}
with this extra structure, constructed by
knot surgeries which we were unable to perform in an equivariant way
on our example.

The paper is organized as follows. We describe the construction of the
exotic smooth structure on
{\mathbb{C}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}\#4{\overline{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}}
and verify the existence of
an embedding of
5{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}\subset S^{4}
with Euler number equal to 6 in
Section
2
. The proof of
Theorem
1.1
is then concluded by calculating the
fundamental group of the complement in Section
3
and
proving exoticness of the smooth structure on

{\mathbb{C}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}\#4{\overline{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}}
in the subsequent sections. In
Section

we give a proof using the Seiberg-Witten

contains a proof with a more

), provides an embedded surface
\Sigma_{p^{2},pq-1}\subset S^{3}
with boundary the two-bridge knot
K_{p^{2},pq-1}

\Sigma_{p^{2},pq-1}

,

as the double branched cover of

along this pushed in

15

.

The embedding of

3.
Fundamental group calculation

In order to complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we need
to determine the fundamental group of the complement of

\widehat{F}=\iota(5{\mathbb{R}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}})\subset S^{4}
. This computation will rely
on the Seifert-Van Kampen theorem. Recall, that if
X=X_{1}\cup X_{2}

and
X_{1}\cap X_{2}
is path connected, then (after choosing the base
point in
X_{1}\cap X_{2}
) the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(X)
can be
presented as the amalgamated product
\pi_{1}(X_{1})*_{\pi_{1}(X_{1}\cap X_{2})}\pi_{1}(X_{2})
, with maps
i_{j}\colon\pi_{1}(X_{1}\cap X_{2})\to\pi_{1}(X_{j})
(
j=1,2
) induced by embeddings. In our argument we
will use the following principle:

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that for the amalgamated product
A*_{Q}B
of the groups
A,B
over

Q
with homomorphisms
\varphi_{A}\colon Q\to A
and

\varphi_{B}\colon Q\to B
we have

•

\varphi_{B}
is onto, and

•

\ker\varphi_{B}\leq\ker\varphi_{A}
.

Then
A*_{Q}B
is isomorphic to
A
.

Proof.

Since
\ker\varphi_{B}\leqslant\ker\varphi_{A}
and
\varphi_{B}
is surjective, there is a
map
r\colon B\to A
such that
\varphi_{A}=r\circ\varphi_{B}
. Together with the identity
A\to A
, they induce a
map
F\colon A\ast_{Q}B\to A
which, when precomposed with the
natural map
G_{A}\colon A\to A\ast_{Q}B
, gives the identity on

A
. Therefore
G_{A}
is injective, hence to show that
G_{A}
is an
isomorphism, we need to show that it is surjective. Let
G_{B}\colon B\to A\ast_{Q}B
be the other natural map, and let

In this section, using Seiberg-Witten theory, we verify

[

Let us first recall some definitions from algebraic geometry to be
applied to our particular situation. A Wahl singularity is a
2-dimensional cyclic quotient singularity of type

\frac{1}{p^{2}}(1,pq-1)
, where gcd
(p,q)=1
.
For a complex surface

W^{\prime}
with only Wahl singularities, we consider its minimal resolution

\sigma\colon W\to W^{\prime}
, where
W
is a nonsingular complex
projective surface and
\sigma
is a birational morphism. The
pre-image of each Wahl singularity is a Wahl chain
\mathcal{C}_{j}=\cup_{i}C^{j}_{i}
, where
the
C_{i}^{j}

d(C_{i}^{j})
of
C_{i}^{j}
is a
rational number such that

K_{W}\equiv\sigma^{*}(K_{W^{\prime}})+\sum_{j}\sum_{i}d(C^{j}_{i})\,C^{j}_{i},

(see e.g.
[
13
, Definition 2.22]

K_{W}
,
K_{W^{\prime}}
are
canonical divisors, the symbol

is numerical equivalence of
divisors, and the sum runs through all the Wahl chains
\mathcal{C}_{j}

over Wahl singularities with
C_{i}^{j}
the
i^{{\rm{th}}}
sphere in
the Wahl chain
\mathcal{C}_{j}
. One can show that

-1<d(C^{j}_{i})<0
. To compute these numbers one uses the adjunction
formula and solves the linear system

-(C_{k}^{j}\cdot C_{k}^{j})-2=\sum_{i}d(C_{i}^{j})\,(C_{i}^{j}\cdot C_{k}^{j}),\ \ \text{for
all}\ k,

for each Wahl chain. This system has a unique solution
since the intersection matrix is negative definite
[
18
]
. The
canonical divisor
K_{W^{\prime}}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, and so there
is
u\in\mathbb{N}
such that
uK_{W^{\prime}}
is a Cartier divisor. This integer

be taken as the least common multiple of the indices
p
of the singularities

\frac{1}{p^{2}}(1,pq-1)
in
W^{\prime}
. The canonical class
K_{W^{\prime}}
is
ample

if there is an integer

so that the sections of the line bundle
associated to
uvK_{W^{\prime}}
define an embedding of
W^{\prime}
into some
projective space.

[
23
]
to prove exoticness.

Theorem 5.1
.

(Theorem 2.3 and Corollary 2.5 of
[
23
]

Let

be a complex projective surface with only Wahl singularities such that

is ample. Then, the rational blow-down
\widehat{W}
of the Wahl chains
\cup_{j}\mathcal{C}_{j}
in the minimal resolution
W
of
W^{\prime}
is a (smoothly) minimal symplectic 4-manifold. Moreover, if
\widehat{W}
is simply-connected and it has an odd intersection form, then
\widehat{W}
is an exotic

(2p_{g}(W)+1){\mathbb{C}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}\;\#\;(10p_{g}(W)+9-K_{W^{\prime}}^{2}){\overline{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}}

with
10p_{g}(W)+9-K_{W^{\prime}}^{2}>0
. Thus if
W
is rational (e.g. it is some blow-up of
{\mathbb{C}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}
), then
\widehat{W}
is a minimal exotic
{\mathbb{C}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}\#(9-K_{W^{\prime}}^{2}){\overline{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}}
.

Informally, singular surfaces in the
Kollár–Shepherd-Barron–Alexeev compactification of the moduli
space of complex surfaces of general type (the analogue to the
Deligne-Mumford compactification of the moduli space of compact
Riemann surfaces of genus
g\geq 2
) with only Wahl singularities
produce minimal exotic 4-manifolds.

The idea of the proof is to give a symplectic structure on

\widehat{W}
via the embedding of
W
into a projective space defined
by a very ample line bundle of the form
\sigma^{*}(NK_{W^{\prime}})-D
where

N\gg 0
and
D
is an effective sum of curves in the Wahl chains
contracted by
\sigma
. The symplectic form will be then in the class
of
NK_{W^{\prime}}
. Thus the ampleness of
K_{W^{\prime}}
rules out the existence
of
(-1)
2-spheres in
\widehat{W}
. See the proof of
[
23
, Theorem
2.3]
for details.

We now return to our particular construction. Recall that we
considered the following pair of chains
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}

of equivariant curves in
W={\mathbb{C}}{{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}\#22{\overline{{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}}}
. We include the
intersection numbers and also the discrepancies to be used in the proof of
Theorem
2.1
:

\begin{array}[]{*{19}c}\mathcal{C}_{1}&\colon&B&-&C&-&A&-&E_{6}&-&F_{1}&-&E_{9}&-&L&-&E_{19}&-&E_{20}\\
\text{self-int}&\colon&-4&&-3&&-3&&-2&&-6&&-3&&-3&&-2&&-2\\
\text{discr}&\colon&-\frac{47}{65}&&-\frac{58}{65}&&-\frac{62}{65}&&-\frac{63}{65}&&-\frac{64}{65}&&-\frac{61}{65}&&-\frac{54}{65}&&-\frac{36}{65}&&-\frac{18}{65},\end{array}

and

\begin{array}[]{*{19}c}\mathcal{C}_{2}&\colon&Y&-&E_{5}&-&Z&-&H&-&E_{1}&-&X&-&E_{3}&-&E_{4}&-&E_{13}\\
\text{self-int}&\colon&-3&&-3&&-4&&-5&&-3&&-2&&-3&&-3&&-2\\
\text{discr}&\colon&-\frac{49}{79}&&-\frac{68}{79}&&-\frac{76}{79}&&-\frac{78}{79}&&-\frac{77}{79}&&-\frac{74}{79}&&-\frac{71}{79}&&-\frac{60}{79}&&-\frac{30}{79}.\end{array}

We will prove here the following:

Theorem 5.2
.

The symplectic 4-manifold
\widehat{W}
obtained by rationally blowing
down the two disjoint Wahl chains
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}

is minimal.

Proof.

We have shown already in Section
2
that
\widehat{W}
is simply connected. Here we will apply Theorem
5.1
, and so we only need to check that
K_{W^{\prime}}
is ample. To verify ampleness, by the
Nakai-Moishezon criterion (see e.g.
[
13
, Theorem 1.37]
),
we need to prove that
K_{W^{\prime}}\cdot\Gamma>0
for every
irreducible curve
\Gamma
in
W^{\prime}
. (We already know that

K_{W^{\prime}}^{2}=5>0
.) We remark that
W^{\prime}
is a projective surface with
two Wahl singularities
\frac{1}{65^{2}}(1,65\cdot 18-1)
for

\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\frac{1}{79^{2}}(1,79\cdot 30-1)
for

\mathcal{C}_{2}
. Let
\sigma\colon W\to W^{\prime}
be the contraction
as above. In what follows, we will use numerical
equivalence
\equiv
for divisors, as we only need to check intersection numbers.

Let
S\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
be the elliptic surface found in Section
2
to construct
W
. First, we know that
-K_{S}
is linearly equivalent to a fiber, and so we have

K_{S}\equiv-\frac{1}{3}(X+E_{1}+Y+E_{5}+Z+E_{3})-\frac{1}{3}(A+B+C)-\frac{1}{3}F_{1}.

Using the pull-back formula
for the canonical class after a blow-up, we obtain (using the previous
equation) that

\displaystyle K_{W}

\displaystyle\equiv-\frac{1}{3}(X+E_{1}+Y+E_{5}+Z+E_{3}+A+B+C+F_{1})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2}{3}(E_{16}+E_{15}+E_{17}+E_{12})+\frac{1}{3}(E_{11}+E_{10}+E_{18}+E_{22})

\displaystyle\quad+E_{19}+2E_{20}+3E_{21}+\frac{2}{3}E_{13}+\frac{4}{3}E_{14}.

On the other hand, by the definition of discrepancy of exceptional divisors, we have the equality

\displaystyle\sigma^{*}K_{W^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\equiv K_{W}+\frac{47}{65}B+\frac{58}{65}C+\frac{62}{65}A+\frac{63}{65}E_{6}+\frac{64}{65}F_{1}+\frac{61}{65}E_{9}+\frac{54}{65}L

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{36}{65}E_{19}+\frac{18}{65}E_{20}+\frac{49}{79}Y+\frac{68}{79}E_{5}+\frac{76}{79}Z+\frac{78}{79}H+\frac{77}{79}E_{1}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{74}{79}X+\frac{71}{79}E_{3}+\frac{60}{79}E_{4}+\frac{30}{79}E_{13}.

Therefore, by plugging in
K_{W}
in the last formula, we obtain that

\sigma^{*}K_{W^{\prime}}
can be written as a sum of curves with positive rational coefficients (i.e. it is a
\mathbb{Q}
-effective divisor). We fix this representative of
\sigma^{*}K_{W^{\prime}}
, and we refer to these curves as the support of
\sigma^{*}K_{W^{\prime}}
.
To check that

K_{W^{\prime}}
is ample, suppose
\Gamma\subseteq W^{\prime}
is a curve such that

K_{W^{\prime}}\cdot\Gamma\leq 0
. Let
\tilde{\Gamma}
be its strict transform
in
W
. Then

K_{W^{\prime}}\cdot\Gamma=\sigma^{*}K_{W^{\prime}}\cdot\sigma^{*}\Gamma=\sigma^{*}K_{W^{\prime}}\cdot\tilde{\Gamma}.

Since
\sigma^{*}K_{W^{\prime}}
can be written as a
\mathbb{Q}
-effective divisor, any curve not included in its support must intersect
\sigma^{*}K_{W^{\prime}}
non-negatively. Moreover,
\tilde{\Gamma}
is a strict transform of a curve in
W^{\prime}
, and so it is not contracted by
\sigma
. Thus
\tilde{\Gamma}
is either one of the non-contractible curves in the support of
\sigma^{*}K_{W^{\prime}}
, or it does not
intersect this support at all.

In the first case,
\tilde{\Gamma}
can be one of the following curves:

E_{11}
,
E_{16}
,
E_{15}
,
E_{10}
,
E_{17}
,
E_{12}
,
E_{18}
,

E_{22}
,
E_{21}
or
E_{14}
. Since all of them are
(-1)
-curves in

W
, their intersection with
K_{W}
is
-1
, and so we obtain

\displaystyle\sigma^{*}K_{W^{\prime}}\cdot E_{11}

\displaystyle=-1+\frac{76}{79}+\frac{71}{79}>0,

\displaystyle\sigma^{*}K_{W^{\prime}}\cdot E_{16}

\displaystyle=-1+\frac{76}{79}+\frac{61}{65}>0,

\displaystyle\sigma^{*}K_{W^{\prime}}\cdot E_{15}

\displaystyle=-1+\frac{68}{79}+\frac{63}{65}>0,

\displaystyle\sigma^{*}K_{W^{\prime}}\cdot E_{10}

\displaystyle=-1+\frac{49}{79}+\frac{77}{79}>0,

\displaystyle\sigma^{*}K_{W^{\prime}}\cdot E_{17}

\displaystyle=-1+\frac{58}{65}+\frac{61}{65}>0,

\displaystyle\sigma^{*}K_{W^{\prime}}\cdot E_{12}

\displaystyle=-1+\frac{47}{65}+\frac{60}{79}>0,

\displaystyle\sigma^{*}K_{W^{\prime}}\cdot E_{18}

\displaystyle=-1+\frac{47}{65}+\frac{62}{65}>0,

\displaystyle\sigma^{*}K_{W^{\prime}}\cdot E_{22}

\displaystyle=-1+\frac{64}{65}+\frac{64}{65}>0,

\displaystyle\sigma^{*}K_{W^{\prime}}\cdot E_{21}

\displaystyle=-1+\frac{18}{65}+\frac{78}{79}>0,

\displaystyle\sigma^{*}K_{W^{\prime}}\cdot E_{14}

\displaystyle=-1+\frac{30}{79}+\frac{64}{65}>0.

This leaves with the case that
\tilde{\Gamma}
does not intersect the
support of
\sigma^{*}K_{W^{\prime}}
. But this is impossible as this support
contains
X
,
E_{1}
,
E_{10}
,
Y
,
E_{5}
,
E_{15}
,
Z
,
E_{16}
,

E_{11}
,
E_{3}
, and these curves form a fiber of the induced elliptic
fibration
W\to\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
. But then
\tilde{\Gamma}
must
be a component of some other singular fiber, and the only possibility
would be
Q
(see Figure
2
), whose intersection with

\sigma^{*}K_{W^{\prime}}
is not zero. Therefore such
\tilde{\Gamma}
does not
exist, and so
K_{W^{\prime}}
is ample.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

As
K_{W^{\prime}}
is ample, the surface
W^{\prime}
lives in the
Kollár–Shepherd-Barron–Alexeev moduli space of surfaces with

K^{2}=5
and
p_{g}=q=0
. The local-to-global obstruction for complex
deformations of
W^{\prime}
is in
H^{2}(W^{\prime},T_{W^{\prime}})
, and by
[
23
, §4]

one can compute that
H^{2}(W^{\prime},T_{W^{\prime}})=\mathbb{C}^{2}
(almost the same
proof as for
[
23
, Proposition 5.4]
). Therefore it is not trivial
to decide whether there is a complex (
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein)
smoothing of
W^{\prime}
or not. If so, then this smoothing would produce
simply connected surfaces of general type with
K^{2}=5
and

p_{g}=0
. There are no such examples in the literature, this is an open problem. We also have

H^{1}(W^{\prime},T_{W^{\prime}})=0
by
[
23
, Theorem 4.1]
, and so
W^{\prime}
is
equisingularly rigid.

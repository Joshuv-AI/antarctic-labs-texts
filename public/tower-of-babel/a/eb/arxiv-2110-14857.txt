Cohomologies and crossed modules for pre-Lie Rinehart algebras Thanks: ∗ the corresponding author
Liangyun Chen, Meijun Liu, Jiefeng Liu ∗

Abstract
. A pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra is an algebraic generalization of the notion of a left-symmetric algebroid. We construct pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras from r r -matrices through Lie algebra actions. We study cohomologies of pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras and show that abelian extensions of pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras are classified by the second cohomology groups. We introduce the notion of crossed modules for pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras and show that they are classified by the third cohomology groups of pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras. At last, we use (pre-)Lie-Rinehart 2 2 -algebras to characterize the crossed modules for (pre-)Lie Rinehart algebras.

1.
Introduction

The notion of a Lie-Rinehart algebra is an algebraic generalization of Lie algebroids. It was first introduced by Rinehart in his seminal paper
[
23
]
, in which he used this notion to develop a formalism of differential forms for general commutative algebras. In
[
13
]
, Huebschmann studied Lie-Rinehart algebras systematically and emphasized their important applications in Poisson geometry. See
[
7
,
9
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
for more details and applications on Lie-Rinehart algebras.

Pre-Lie algebras (or left-symmetric algebras) arose from the study of convex homogeneous cones (
[
26
]
), affine manifolds and affine structures on Lie groups (
[
18
]
), deformation and cohomology theory of associative algebras (
[
11
]
) and then appear in many fields in mathematics and mathematical physics. See the survey article
[
5
]
and the references therein. The notion of a pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra was introduced by Fl
{\o}
ystad, Manchon and Munthe-Kaas in
[
10
]
. They organized colored aromatic trees into a pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra endowed with a natural trace map, which yields
the algebraic foundations of aromatic B-series. The importance of a pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra is that the commutator gives rise to a Lie-Rinehart algebra and the left multiplication gives rise to a representation of the commutator Lie-Rinehart algebra. A pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra is also an algebraic generalization of the notion of a left-symmetric algebroid (
[
20
]
). See
[
3
,
21
,
22
,
27
]
for more details on left-symmetric algebroids. Recently, the authors in
[
2
]
studies the cohomology and deformation of the pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra (they called it a left-symmetric Rinehart algebra).

The notion of crossed modules was introduced by Whitehead in
[
28
]
to study relative homotopy groups. After that, crossed modules have been one of the fundamental concepts in homotopy theory, algebraic
K
-theory, combinatorial group theory and homological algebras. Crossed modules for Lie algebras were studied in
[
12
,
17
]
and it was shown that they are classified by the third cohomology groups of Lie algebras. Lie algebras can be categorified to Lie
2
-algebras. It is well-known that the category of strict Lie
2
-algebras is isomorphic to the category of crossed modules for Lie algebras (
[
1
]
). The author in
[
24
]
introduced the notion of crossed modules for pre-Lie algebras and gave the equivalence between the category of strict Lie
2
-algebras and the category of crossed modules for pre-Lie
2
-algebras, in which the pre-Lie
2
-algebra is a categorification of the pre-Lie algebra. As a natural generalization of crossed modules for Lie algebras, the authors in
[
6
]
introduced the notion of crossed modules for Lie-Rinehart algebras and proved that they are classified by the third cohomology groups of Lie-Rinehart algebras.

In this paper, we introduce the notion of crossed modules for pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras and classify crossed modules via the third cohomology groups of pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras. Then we give the notion of (pre-)Lie-Rinehart
2
-algebras, which is the algebraic generalization of (pre-)Lie
2
-algebroids given in
[
19
,
25
]
. Furthermore, we establish a one-to-one correspondence between strict (pre-)Lie-Rinehart
2
-algebras and crossed modules for (pre-)Lie Rinehart algebras.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, first we review the representations and cohomologies of Lie-Rinehart algebras. Then we recall the notion of a pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra and illustrate it by some examples. In Section
3
, we use
r
-matrices to construct pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras. In Section
4
, we study the cohomologies of pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras and show that abelian extensions of pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras can be classified by the second cohomology groups. In Section
5
, we introduce the notion of crossed modules for pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras and show that they are classified by the third cohomology groups of pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras. In section
6
, we introduce the notion of (pre-)Lie-Rinehart
2
-algebras and then use strict (pre-)Lie-Rinehart
2
-algebras to characterize the crossed modules for (pre-)Lie Rinehart algebras.

2.
Lie-Rinehart algebras and pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras

In this section, we first recall the representations and cohomologies of Lie-Rinehart algebras. Then we introduce the notion of a pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra and give some constructions of pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras.

2.1.
Lie-Rinehart algebras

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field and
A
be a unitary commutative algebra over
\mathbb{K}
. A derivation of
A
is a
\mathbb{K}
-linear map
D:A\longrightarrow A
satisfying the Leibniz rule
D(ab)=aD(b)+D(a)b
for all
a,b\in A
. The
A
-module
\mathrm{Der}(A)
of all derivations of
A
is a Lie
\mathbb{K}
-algebra under the commutator
[D_{1},D_{2}]^{c}:=D_{1}\circ D_{2}-D_{2}\circ D_{1}
for
D_{1},D_{2}\in\mathrm{Der}(A)
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
Lie-Rinehart algebra
consists of the following data:

\bullet

a unitary commutative
\mathbb{K}
-algebra
A
;

\bullet

an
A
-module
E
;

\bullet

an
A
-module map
\theta_{E}:E\longrightarrow\mathrm{Der}(A)
, called the anchor;

\bullet

a
\mathbb{K}
-linear Lie bracket
[-,-]_{E}:E\otimes E\to E
.

These data satisfy the following additional conditions:

(1)

\displaystyle[X,aY]_{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a[X,Y]_{E}+\theta_{E}(X)(a)Y,

(2)

\displaystyle\theta_{E}([X,Y]_{E})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\theta_{E}(X),\theta_{E}(Y)]^{c},\quad\forall~X,Y\in E,a\in A.

We denote a Lie-Rinehart algebra by
(E,[-,-]_{E},\theta_{E})
if
A
is fixed.

Example 2.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{L},[-,-]_{\mathcal{L}},\theta_{\mathcal{L}})
be a Lie algebroid on a manifold
M
. Then
(E=\Gamma(\mathcal{L}),[-,-]_{\mathcal{L}},\theta_{\mathcal{L}})
is a Lie-Rinehart algebra with
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
and
A=C^{\infty}(M)
the algebra of smooth functions on a manifold
M
.

Recall that an
action
of a Lie
\mathbb{K}
-algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
on a unitary commutative
\mathbb{K}
-algebra
A
is a Lie algebra homomorphism for
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathrm{Der}(A)
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Lie
\mathbb{K}
-algebra and
\lambda:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Der}(A)
an action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
A
. Then the
transformation Lie-Rinehart algebra
is
E=A\otimes\mathfrak{g}
with Lie bracket

[a\otimes x,b\otimes y]_{\rho}=ab\otimes[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+a\rho(x)(b)\otimes y-b\rho(y)(a)\otimes x

and the anchor
\theta_{\rho}(a\otimes x)(b)=a\rho(x)(b)
, where
a,b\in A,x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an
A
-module. A
derivation
of
\mathcal{E}
is a pair
(D,\sigma_{D})
, where
D:\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}
is a
\mathbb{K}
-linear map and
\sigma_{D}\in\mathrm{Der}(A)
satisfying the following compatibility condition:

D(au)=aD(u)+\sigma_{D}(a)u,\quad\forall a\in A,u\in\mathcal{E}.

Derivations of
\mathcal{E}
form an
A
-module denoted by
\mathfrak{D}(\mathcal{E})
.

Example 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an
A
-module. Then
\mathfrak{D}(\mathcal{E})
is a Lie-Rinehart algebra with respect to the commutator bracket and the anchor
\theta_{\mathfrak{D}}
is given by
\theta_{\mathfrak{D}}(D,\sigma)=\sigma
, which we call a
gauge Lie-Rinehart algebra
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(E,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E},\theta_{E})
and
(F,[\cdot,\cdot]_{F},\theta_{F})
be two Lie-Rinehart algebras over
A
. A
homomorphism

from
E
to
F
is an
A
-module morphism
\varphi:E\longrightarrow F
such
that

\displaystyle\varphi[X,Y]_{E}=[\varphi(X),\varphi(Y)]_{F},\quad\theta_{F}\circ\varphi=\theta_{E},\quad\forall X,Y\in E.

Definition 2.6
.

A
representation
of a Lie-Rinehart algebra
(E,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E},\theta_{E})
on an
A
-module
\mathcal{E}
is a homomorphism
\rho
from
E
to the gauge Lie-Rinehart algebra
\mathfrak{D}(\mathcal{E})
.

An
A
-module
\mathcal{E}
equipped with a representation of
E
is said to be an
E
-module.

For every Lie-Rinehart algebra
(E,[\cdot,\cdot]_{E},\theta_{E})
,
A
is a
E
-module with
\rho=\theta_{E}
.

Consider the
A
-module
E
with representation
(\mathcal{E};\rho)
, one defines for each
k\geq 0
the module of
k
-cochains on
E
with coefficients in
A
to be

\Omega^{k}(E,\mathcal{E}):=\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\wedge^{k}E,\mathcal{E}).

The corresponding coboundary operator

{\mathrm{d}}:\Omega^{k}(E,\mathcal{E})\longrightarrow\Omega^{k+1}(E,\mathcal{E})
is given by

\displaystyle{\mathrm{d}}\varpi(X_{1},\cdots,X_{k+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k+1}(-1)^{i+1}\rho(X_{i})\varpi(X_{1}\cdots,\widehat{X_{i}},\cdots,X_{k+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k+1}(-1)^{i+j}\varpi([X_{i},X_{j}]_{E},X_{1}\cdots,\widehat{X_{i}},\cdots,\widehat{X_{j}},\cdots,X_{k+1}).

We denote the

-th cohomology group of

.

2.2.
Pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras

Definition 2.7
.

A
pre-Lie
\mathbb{K}
-algebra
is a pair
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
, where
\mathfrak{g}
is a vector space over
\mathbb{K}
, and
\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a
\mathbb{K}
-bilinear operation
such that for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
, the associator

(3)

(x,y,z)\triangleq x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z)-(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z

is symmetric in
x,y
,
i.e.

(x,y,z)=(y,x,z),\;\;{\rm or}\;\;{\rm equivalently,}\;\;x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z)-(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z=y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}(x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z)-(y\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}x)\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}z.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a pre-Lie
\mathbb{K}
-algebra. The commutator
[x,y]^{c}=x\cdot y-y\cdot x
defines a Lie
\mathbb{K}
-algebra structure
on
\mathfrak{g}
, which is called the
sub-adjacent Lie algebra
of

(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
and denoted by
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
. Furthermore,

L:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
with
x\rightarrow L_{x}
, where

L_{x}y=x\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}}y
, for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, gives a representation of
the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
on
\mathfrak{g}
. See
[
5
]
for more details.

Definition 2.8
.

(
[
10
]
)

A
pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra
consists of the following data:

\bullet

a unitary commutative
\mathbb{K}
-algebra
A
;

\bullet

an
A
-module
E
;

\bullet

an
A
-module map
\theta_{E}:E\longrightarrow\mathrm{Der}(A)
, called the anchor;

\bullet

a
\mathbb{K}
-linear pre-Lie operation
\cdot_{E}:E\otimes E\to E
.

These data satisfy the following additional conditions:

(4)

\displaystyle X\cdot_{E}(aY)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a(X\cdot_{E}Y)+\theta_{E}(X)(a)Y,

(5)

\displaystyle(aX)\cdot_{E}Y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a(X\cdot_{E}Y),

\displaystyle\theta_{E}(X\cdot_{E}Y-Y\cdot_{E}X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\theta_{E}(X),\theta_{E}(Y)]^{c},\quad\forall~X,Y\in E,a\in A.

We denote a pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra by
(E,\cdot_{E},\theta_{E})
if
A

For any

, we define
L_{X}:E\longrightarrow E
and

Similar to the proof Theorem 3.2 in

.

-bilinear bracket operation

Then

.

3.
Form
r
-matrices to pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras

Let
A
be a commutative
\mathbb{K}
-algebra and
\mathcal{M}
be an
A
-module. A
\mathbb{K}
-linear map
D:A\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}
is a called a
derivation
if it satisfies the Leibniz rule:

D(ab)=D(a)b+aD(b),\quad\forall~a,b\in A.

\mathcal{M}
-valued derivations form an
A
-module denoted by
\mathrm{Der}(A,\mathcal{M})
.

The functor
\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Der}(A,\mathcal{M})
is (co)representable: there exists an
A
-module
\Omega^{1}
, unique up to a unique isomorphism, together with a natural isomorphism of
A
-modules

\mathrm{Der}(A,\mathcal{M})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\Omega^{1},\mathcal{M}).

In particular, putting
\mathcal{M}=\Omega^{1}
, the identity map on the right hand side corresponds to the universal derivation

d:A\longrightarrow\Omega^{1},

in which
\Omega^{1}
can be written by a formal finite sums of terms of the form
adb
for
a,b\in A
obeying the Leibniz relation

d(ab)=d(a)b+ad(b),\quad\forall~a,b\in A.

The module
\mathfrak{X}^{1}:=\mathrm{Der}(A)
forms a Lie algebra under the commutator bracket. By the universal property of
\Omega^{1}
one has

\mathfrak{X}^{1}\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\Omega^{1},A).

Recall that a
Poisson algebra
is a triple
(A,\cdot,\{-,-\})
, where
(A,\cdot)
is a commutative associative algebra and
(A,\{-,-\})
is a Lie algebra, such that the Leibniz rule
\{a,b\cdot c\}=\{a,b\}\cdot c+b\cdot\{a,c\}
holds for all
a,b,c\in A
. For
a,b,u,v\in A
, define
\pi:\Omega^{1}\otimes\Omega^{1}\longrightarrow A
by

(8)

\pi(adu\otimes bdv)=ab\{u,v\}.

It is not hard to see that
\pi
is
A
-linear and skew-symmetric.

Define
\pi^{\sharp}:\Omega^{1}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\Omega^{1},A)\cong\mathrm{Der}(A)
by

(9)

\pi^{\sharp}(adu)(v)=a\{u,v\}.

Define
[-,-]_{\Omega^{1}}:\Omega^{1}\times\Omega^{1}\longrightarrow\Omega^{1}
by

(10)

[adu,bdv]_{\Omega^{1}}=a\{u,b\}dv+b\{a,v\}du+abd\{u,v\}.

Then Huebschmann in

is a Lie-Rinehart algebra and
\pi^{\sharp}
is a morphism of Lie-Rinehart algebras between

Let

\displaystyle\{a,\{b,c\}\}+\{c,\{a,b\}\}+\{b,\{c,a\}\}=\frac{1}{2}\lambda(\left\llbracket r,r\right\rrbracket)(a,b,c),\quad\forall~a,b,c\in A.

For

Define

Proposition 3.1
.

With the above notations,

Proof.

It is easy to see that the operation

Thus we only need to show that

holds for

Remark 3.2
.

In
[

]
, Boucetta found that by the action of a Lie algebra on a manifold, an

Example 3.3
.

Consider the standard base of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{K})

Define

The Poisson algebra on

and the corresponding Poisson structure is given by

with the condition

Note that

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(r_{1}x_{1}-r_{3}x_{2})\mathrm{d}x_{1}+r_{2}x_{2}\mathrm{d}x_{2},\quad\mathrm{d}x_{2}\cdot_{\Omega^{1}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}=-r_{1}x_{1}\mathrm{d}x_{1}+(-r_{2}x_{2}+r_{3}x_{1})\mathrm{d}x_{2},

4.
Cohomologies and abelian extensions of pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras

In this section, we study cohomologies and abelian extensions of pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras.

4.1.
Cohomologies of pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras

Definition 4.1
.

A
representation
of a pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra
(E,\cdot_{E},\theta_{E})
on an
A
-module
\mathcal{E}
consists of a pair

(\rho,\mu)
, where
(\rho;\mathcal{E})
is a representation
of the sub-adjacent Lie-Rinehart algebra

on
\mathcal{E}
and

(16)

\displaystyle\rho(X)\mu(Y)-\mu(Y)\rho(X)=\mu(X\cdot_{E}Y)-\mu(Y)\mu(X).

Denote a representation by
(\mathcal{E};\rho,\mu)
.

Let
(E,\cdot_{E},\theta_{E})
be a pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra. It is obvious that if
(\mathcal{E};\rho)
is a representation of the sub-adjacent Lie-Rinehart algebra
E^{c}
, then
(\mathcal{E};\rho,0)
is a representation of pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra
(E,\cdot_{E},\theta_{E})
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(E,\cdot_{E},\theta_{E})
be a pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra and
(\mathcal{E};\rho,\mu)
its representation. Then
(F=E\oplus\mathcal{E},\ast_{F},\theta_{F})
is a pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra, where

\ast_{F}
and
\theta_{F}
are given by

\displaystyle(X+u)\ast_{F}(Y+v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X\cdot_{E}Y+\rho(X)v+\mu(Y)u,

\displaystyle\theta_{F}(X+u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta_{E}(X),

for all
X,Y\in E,u,v\in\mathcal{E}
. We denote this pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra by
E\ltimes_{\rho,\mu}\mathcal{E}
and call it the
semidirect product
of
E
and
\mathcal{E}
.

Now we define the cohomology complex for a pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra
(E,\cdot_{E},\theta_{E})
with a representation
(\mathcal{E};\rho,\mu)
. Denote the set of
(n+1)
-cochains by

C^{n+1}(E,\mathcal{E})=\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(\wedge^{n}E\otimes E,\mathcal{E}),\ n\geq 0.

For all

, define

is given by

\displaystyle\delta\varphi(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}\rho(X_{i})\omega(X_{1},\cdots,\hat{X_{i}},\cdots,X_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}\mu(X_{n+1})\omega(X_{1},\cdots,\hat{X_{i}},\cdots,X_{n},X_{i})

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+1}\varphi(X_{1},\cdots,\hat{X_{i}},\cdots,X_{n},X_{i}\cdot_{E}X_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(-1)^{i+j}\varphi([X_{i},X_{j}]_{E},X_{1},\cdots,\hat{X_{i}},\cdots,\hat{X_{j}},\cdots,X_{n+1})

for all
X_{i}\in E,i=1,\cdots,n+1
.

Proposition 4.3
.

With the above notations,
\delta
is a
\mathbb{K}
-linear map and
\delta\varphi\in C^{n+1}(E,\mathcal{E})
for all

. Furthermore,

. Thus, we have a well-defined cochain complex
(\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}C^{n+1}(E,\mathcal{E}),\delta)
and denote the

-th cohomology group of
(\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}C^{n+1}(E,\mathcal{E}),\delta)
by

.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of Proposition 5.7 in
[
20
]
.

In the following, we study the relations between the cohomology groups of pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras and Lie-Rinehart algebras.

Given a representation
(\mathcal{E};\rho,\mu)
on the pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra
(E,\cdot_{E},\theta_{E})
, for
X\in E
, define
\varrho(X):C^{1}(E,\mathcal{E})\longrightarrow C^{1}(E,\mathcal{E})
by

(17)

\displaystyle\varrho(X)(\psi)(Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(X)\psi(Y)+\mu(Y)\psi(X)-\psi(X\cdot_{E}Y)

for
\psi\in C^{1}(E,\mathcal{E})
and
Y\in E
.

Lemma 4.4
.

With the above notations,
\varrho
gives a representation of the sub-adjacent Lie-Rinehart algebra
E^{c}
on
C^{1}(E,\mathcal{E})
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\varrho(aX)=a\varrho(X),\quad\varrho(X)(a\psi)=a\varrho(X)(\psi)+\theta_{E}(X)(a\psi).

Next we show that
\varrho([X,Y]_{E})=[\varrho(X),\sigma(Y)]^{c}
. In fact, since
(\mathcal{E};\rho,\mu)
a representation on the pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra
(E,\cdot_{E},\theta_{E})
, we have

\displaystyle\varrho([X,Y]_{E})(\psi)(Z)-[\varrho(X),\sigma(Y)]^{c}(\psi)(Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\rho([X,Y]_{E}-[\rho(X),\rho(Y)]^{c}\big)(\psi(Z))-\psi\big([X,Y]_{E}\cdot_{E}Z+Y\cdot_{E}(X\cdot_{E}Z)

\displaystyle-X\cdot_{E}(Y\cdot_{E}Z)\big)-\big(\rho(X)\mu(Z)+\mu(Z)\rho(X)-\mu(X\cdot_{E}Z)+\mu(Z)\mu(X)\big)(\psi(Y))

\displaystyle+\big(\rho(Y)\mu(Z)+\mu(Z)\rho(Y)-\mu(Y\cdot_{E}Z)+\mu(Z)\mu(Y)\big)(\psi(X))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Thus
\varrho
is a representation of the sub-adjacent Lie-Rinehart algebra
E^{c}
on
C^{1}(E,\mathcal{E})
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
(E,\cdot_{E},\theta_{E})
be a pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra and
(\mathcal{E};\rho,\mu)
a representation. Define
H:\Omega^{n}(E,C^{1}(E,\mathcal{E}))\longrightarrow C^{n+1}(E,\mathcal{E})
by

H(\psi)(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n+1})=\psi(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})(X_{n+1}),\quad\psi\in\Omega^{n}(E,C^{1}(E,\mathcal{E})),X_{i}\in E,

which induces an isomorphism of cochain complexes
\Omega^{\ast}(E,C^{1}(E,\mathcal{E}))
associated to the representation
\varrho
of
E^{c}
on
C^{1}(E,\mathcal{E})
and
C^{\ast}(E,\mathcal{E})
associated to the representations
(\rho,\mu)
of
E
on
\mathcal{E}
. In particular,

H_{\mathrm{PRin}}^{n+1}(E,\mathcal{E})\cong H_{\mathrm{Rin}}^{n}(E^{c},C^{1}(E,\mathcal{E})),\quad n>0.

Proof.

First, we need to prove that for any
n\geq 0
the following diagram is commutative

\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\Omega^{n}(E,C^{1}(E,\mathcal{E}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{d}}
\scriptstyle{F}
\textstyle{\Omega^{n+1}(E,C^{1}(E,\mathcal{E}))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces C^{n+1}(E,\mathcal{E})}
\scriptstyle{\delta}
\textstyle{C^{n+2}(E,\mathcal{E}).}

For
\psi\in\Omega^{n}(E,C^{1}(E,\mathcal{E}))
, we have

\displaystyle H(\mathrm{d}\psi)(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n+2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{d}\psi(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n+1}))(X_{n+2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{i+1}\big(\varrho(X_{i})\psi(X_{1}\cdots,\widehat{X_{i}},\cdots,X_{n+1})\big)(X_{n+2})

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{i+j}\big(\psi([X_{i},X_{j}]_{E},X_{1}\cdots,\widehat{X_{i}},\cdots,\widehat{X_{j}},\cdots,X_{n+1})\big)(X_{n+2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{i+1}\rho(X_{i})H(\psi)(X_{1}\cdots,\widehat{X_{i}},\cdots,X_{n+1},X_{n+2})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{i+1}\mu(X_{n+2})H(\psi)(X_{1}\cdots,\widehat{X_{i}},\cdots,X_{n+1},X_{i})

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{i+1}H(\psi)(X_{1}\cdots,\widehat{X_{i}},\cdots,X_{n+1},X_{i}\cdot_{E}X_{n+2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta H(\psi)(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n+2}),

which implies that
H\circ\mathrm{d}\psi=\delta\circ H(\psi).
The second conclusion follows immediately.

4.2.
Abelian extensions of pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras

Definition 4.6
.

(1)

Let
E^{\prime}
,
E
,
E^{\prime\prime}
be pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras over
A
. Assume that the anchor map of pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra
E^{\prime}
is trivial. An
abelian extension
of pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras is a short exact sequence of pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras:

0\longrightarrow E^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\imath}}{{\longrightarrow}}E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}E^{\prime\prime}\longrightarrow 0.

We say that
E
is an abelian extension of
E^{\prime\prime}
by
E^{\prime}
.

(2)

The abelian extension of pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras is called
split
if there exists an
A
-linear map
\sigma:E^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow E
such that
p\circ\sigma=\rm{id}
.

(3)

Two abelian extensions of
E^{\prime\prime}
by
E^{\prime}
,
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, are said to be
equivalent
if there exists a pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra morphism
\tau:E_{2}\longrightarrow E_{1}
such that we have the following commutative diagram:

(18)

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}0&\longrightarrow&E^{\prime}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\imath_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&E_{2}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&E^{\prime\prime}&\longrightarrow&0\\
&&\Big\|&&\tau\Big\downarrow&&\Big\|&&\\
0&\longrightarrow&E^{\prime}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\imath_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&E_{1}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&E^{\prime\prime}&\longrightarrow&0.\end{array}

Let
E
be an abelian extension of
E^{\prime\prime}
by
E^{\prime}
, and
\sigma:E^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow E
a split. Define
\omega:E^{\prime\prime}\times E^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow E^{\prime}
,

\rho:E^{\prime\prime}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(E^{\prime},E^{\prime})
and
\mu:E^{\prime\prime}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(E^{\prime},E^{\prime})
respectively by

(19)

\displaystyle\omega(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma(X)\cdot_{E}\sigma(Y)-\sigma(X\cdot_{E^{\prime\prime}}Y),\quad\forall X,Y\in E^{\prime\prime},

(20)

\displaystyle\rho(X)(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma(X)\cdot_{E}u,\quad\forall X\in E,~u\in E^{\prime},

(21)

\displaystyle\mu(X)(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u\cdot_{E}\sigma(X),\quad\forall X\in E,~u\in E^{\prime}.

It is easy to check that
\omega\in\mathrm{Hom}(E^{\prime\prime}\otimes_{A}E^{\prime\prime},E^{\prime})
,
\rho
and
\mu
are
A
-linear maps, and
\mu(X)\in\mathrm{Hom}_{A}(E^{\prime},E^{\prime})
.

Given a split
\sigma
, we have

as

(22)

\displaystyle(X+u)\ast_{\sigma}(Y+v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X\cdot_{E^{\prime\prime}}Y+\omega(X,Y)+\rho(X)(v)+\mu(Y)(u)+u\cdot_{E^{\prime}}v,

(23)

\displaystyle\theta_{\sigma}(X+u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta_{E^{\prime\prime}}(X).

Proposition 4.7
.

With the above notations,
(E^{\prime\prime}\oplus E^{\prime},\ast_{\sigma},\theta_{\sigma})
is a pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra if and only if
\rho,\mu
and
\omega
satisfy the following equalities:

(24)

\displaystyle{[\rho(X),\rho(Y)]^{c}}-\rho([X,Y]_{E^{\prime\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L^{\prime}_{\omega(X,Y)-\omega(Y,X)},

(25)

\displaystyle\rho(X)\mu(Y)-\mu(Y)\rho(X)-\mu(X\cdot_{E^{\prime\prime}}Y)+\mu(Y)\mu(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{\prime}_{\omega(X,Y)},

(26)

\displaystyle(\rho-\mu)(X)(u)\cdot_{E^{\prime}}v+u\cdot_{E^{\prime}}\rho(X)(v)-\rho(X)(u\cdot_{E^{\prime}}v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(27)

\displaystyle u\cdot_{E^{\prime}}\mu(X)(v)-v\cdot_{E^{\prime}}\mu(X)(u)-\mu(X)([u,v]_{E^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(28)

\displaystyle\omega(Y,X\cdot_{E^{\prime\prime}}Z)-\omega(X,Y\cdot_{E^{\prime\prime}}Z)+\omega([X,Y]_{E^{\prime\prime}},Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(X)\omega(Y,Z)-\rho(Y)\omega(X,Z)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\mu(Z)\omega(X,Y)+\mu(Z)\omega(Y,X).

Proof.

It follows by a direct calculation.

Now we assume that
E^{\prime}
is abelian. Then (
24
) means that
(\rho,\mu)
is a representation of
E^{\prime\prime}
on
E^{\prime}
and (

) means that
\omega
is a

-cocycle of the pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra

associated to the representation
(E^{\prime};\rho,\mu)
. Choosing the other split
\sigma^{\prime}:E^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow E
of

-module, let

\omega^{\prime}(X,Y)=\sigma^{\prime}(X)\cdot_{E}\sigma^{\prime}(Y)-\sigma^{\prime}(X\cdot_{E^{\prime\prime}}Y).

Since

\displaystyle\rho(X)(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma(X)\cdot_{E}u=\sigma^{\prime}(X)\cdot_{E}u,

\displaystyle\mu(X)(u)

\displaystyle=

Then we obtain that

\displaystyle(\omega-\omega^{\prime})(X,Y)=\rho(X)\varphi(Y)+\mu(Y)\varphi(X)-\varphi(X\cdot_{E^{\prime\prime}}Y)=\delta\varphi(X,Y).

5.

In this section, we firs construct a certain transformation pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra, called a free pre-Lie-Rinehart algebra, and show that for

5.1.
Free pre-Lie-Rinehart algebras

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\cdot_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a pre-Lie
\mathbb{K}
-algebra and
\mathfrak{g}^{c}
its sub-adjacent Lie algebra. Let

The following lemma gives a description of free pre-Lie algebra generated by a vector space
V
instead of rooted trees.

Lemma 5.1
.

(
[
8
]
)

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a free pre-Lie
\mathbb{K}
-algebra generated by a vector space

\sigma:V\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}

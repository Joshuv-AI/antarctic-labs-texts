Abstract
A graph G G is called F F -saturated if G G does not contain F F as a subgraph (not necessarily induced) but the addition of any missing edge to G G creates a copy of F F . The saturation number of F F , denoted by s ​ a ​ t ​ ( n , F ) sat(n,F) , is the minimum number of edges in an n n -vertex F F -saturated graph. Determining the saturation number of complete bipartite graphs is one of the most important problems in the study of the saturation number. The value of s ​ a ​ t ​ ( n , K 2 , 2 ) sat(n,K_{2,2}) was shown to be ⌊ 3 ​ n − 5 2 ⌋ \lfloor\frac{3n-5}{2}\rfloor by Ollmann, and a shorter proof was later given by Tuza. For K 2 , 3 K_{2,3} , there has been a series of study aiming to determine s ​ a ​ t ​ ( n , K 2 , 3 ) sat(n,K_{2,3}) over the years. This was finally achieved by Chen who confirmed a conjecture of Bohman, Fonoberova, and Pikhurko that s ​ a ​ t ​ ( n , K 2 , 3 ) = 2 ​ n − 3 sat(n,K_{2,3})=2n-3 for all n ≥ 5 n\geq 5 . Pikhurko and Schmitt conjectured that s ​ a ​ t ​ ( n , K 3 , 3 ) = ( 3 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) ​ n sat(n,K_{3,3})=(3+o(1))n . In this paper, for n ≥ 9 n\geq 9 , we give an upper bound of 3 ​ n − 9 3n-9 for s ​ a ​ t ​ ( n , K 3 , 3 ) sat(n,K_{3,3}) , and prove that 3 ​ n − 9 3n-9 is also a lower bound when the minimum degree of the K 3 , 3 K_{3,3} -saturated graphs is 2 2 or 5 5 , where it is trivial when the minimum degree is greater than 5 5 . Keywords: saturation number; complete bipartite graph; minimum degree

4
Conclusion

Based on above results, we make the following conjecture, which gives an exact value for
sat(n,K_{3,3})
.

Conjecture 4.1

For
n\geq 9
,
sat(n,K_{3,3})=3n-9
.

By Theorem
1.2
,
sat(n,K_{3,3})\leq 3n-9
for
n\geq 9
. To confirm Conjecture
4.1
, it suffices to prove
sat(n,K_{3,3})\geq 3n-9
for
n\geq 9
.
Let
G
be a
K_{3,3}
-saturated graph with
n
vertices and
n\geq 9
. Proposition
3.1
(i) implies
\delta(G)\geq 2
. If
\delta(G)\geq 6
, then
e(G)\geq 3n\geq 3n-9
.
Thus we only need to consider
2\leq\delta(G)\leq 5
. We have proved
sat_{\delta}(n,K_{3,3})\geq 3n-9
when
\delta\in\{2,5\}
.
Actually, for
\delta\in\{3,4\}
, we can also apply the method in this paper, but it is more complex and there are quite a few cases to consider.

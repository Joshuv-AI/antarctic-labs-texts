Space-time fluctuations in a quasi-static limit
Cédric Bernardin, Patricia Gonçalves, Stefano Olla

Abstract
We consider the macroscopic limit for the space-time density fluctuations in the open symmetric simple exclusion in the quasi-static scaling limit. We prove that the distribution of these fluctuations converge to a gaussian space-time field that is delta correlated in time but with long-range correlations in space.

Introduction

Consider a stochastic dynamics of interacting particles where the only conserved quantity
is given by the number of particles, in equilibrium with a reversible stationary (equilibrium) Gibbs
measure
\mu_{\rho}
,
\rho
indicates here the average density of particles.
The fluctuation field of the density of particles at a given time
t
is defined by averaging in space
on a macroscopic scale
N
.
After a
diffusive
rescaling of space and time the fluctuation field
in space of the density of particles converges
to a distribution
{{\mathcalboondox Y}}_{t}(u)
expected to evolve in time
following the infinite dimensional Langevin equation (
Spohn
, section II.2.9)

\partial_{t}{{\mathcalboondox Y}}_{t}(u)=\mathcal{D}_{\rho}\partial_{u}^{2}{{\mathcalboondox Y}}_{t}(u)-\sqrt{2\chi_{\rho}\mathcal{D}_{\rho}}\partial_{u}w(t,u),

(1)

where
\mathcal{D}_{\rho}
is the diffusivity at density
\rho
,
\chi_{\rho}
is the static compressibility
and
w(t,u)
is a standard space-time white noise. The equation (
1
) shall be
considered in a weak sense and we wrote it in one dimension for simplicity.
The only stationary distribution for (
1
) is given by the centered Gaussian
distribution on with covariance

\mathbb{E}\left({{\mathcalboondox Y}}_{t}(u){{\mathcalboondox Y}}_{t}(v)\right)=\chi_{\rho}\delta(u-v),

(2)

that correspond to the static fluctuation field of the stationary Gibbs measure,
that typically have exponential decay of space correlations on the microscopic scale.
The time correlations of the solution of (
1
) have exponential decay.

In this article we consider the
space-time
fluctuations of the density of a open system
on the space-time box:
\{1,\ldots,N-1\}\times[0,N^{2+\alpha}T]
. When
\alpha>0
as
N\to\infty

this correspond to
a
quasi-static
scaling limit, where the time scale is larger than the typical scale where the
dynamics relax to equilibrium (i.e. the diffusive time scale with
\alpha=0

DO-qs
).

We expect that the limit space-time field is a centered gaussian field
\mathbb{Y}(t,u)
with covariance

\mathbb{E}\left({\mathbb{Y}}(t,u){\mathbb{Y}}(s,v)\right)=\chi_{\rho}\delta(t-s)(-\partial_{u}^{2})^{-1}(u,v),

(3)

where
(-\partial_{u}^{2})^{-1}(u,v)
is the Green’s function of the laplacian on
[0,1]

with
Dirichlet
boundary conditions.
While the delta-correlation in time is a natural consequence of the quasi-static time scale,
at first sight it may look surprising the long range correlation in space appearing in (
3
).

In the present article we prove such macroscopic behaviour for the open
symmetric simple exclusion on the discrete interval
\{1,\dots,N-1\}

with boundary reservoirs, but we go beyond the equilibrium case (reservoirs at the same density
\rho
)
and we consider reservoirs at different densities (
\rho_{-}(t)
on the left and
\rho_{+}(t)
on the right)
that change in time on the macroscopic quasi-static scale, in the same situation as considered in

DO-qs
. When
\rho_{\pm}
do not change in time, a stationary state exists that has a density profile

\rho(u)=(\rho_{+}-\rho_{-})u+\rho_{-}
and has long range
correlations.
The static fluctuation field in this stationary state in the limit
N\to\infty

has covariance
Spohn_2
;
lmo

\mathbb{E}\left({{\mathcalboondox Y}}(u){{\mathcalboondox Y}}(v)\right)=\chi_{\rho(u)}\delta(u-v)-\left(\rho_{+}-\rho_{-}\right)^{2}(-\partial_{u}^{2})^{-1}(u,v).

(4)

where
\chi_{\rho}=\rho(1-\rho)
.
A similar limit is found for the density fluctation at macroscopic time
t
in
DO-qs
when
\rho_{\pm}(t)

are time dependent. Then we prove in this situation that the space-time fluctuation
field converges to a centered gaussian field with covariance defined by (
15
) (
16
).

Even though long-range space correlations related to the Green function of the Dirichlet Laplacian
appears in (
3
) (and its generalization (
15
)) as well as in (
4
),
there is not a direct connection between the two results. Notice that in the covariance of the space-time
fluctuations (
3
) the long range correlations appear also in equilibrium (i.e.
\rho_{-}=\rho_{+}
),
while in the space-fluctuations in the stationary state they appears only if
\rho_{-}\neq\rho_{+}
.

Furthermore in (
3
) there is no term with a delta correlation in space and the long range
terms appear with a positive sign (while they are negative in (
4
)).

In Section
2
we present the model under investigation, we recall from
DO-qs
the hydrodynamic limit but also estimates on correlation functions (which are crucial to achieve our results) and we state our main theorem.
We carry out the complete proof of our main theorem for the open SSEP in Section 3, while in Section 4 we sketch the extension to other
symmetric
stochastic dynamics like the zero-range model.

We believe the results of the present article can be easily extended to multidimensional cases,
for example on the open square of side length
N
. In the equilibrium case, with reservoirs at the boundaries with density
\rho
we expect that the macroscopic space-time fluctuations are distributed
like the gaussian field with covariance

\mathbb{E}\left({\mathbb{Y}}(t,u){\mathbb{Y}}(s,v)\right)=\chi_{\rho}\delta(t-s)(-\Delta)^{-1}(u,v),

(5)

where
(-\delta^{2})^{-1}(u,v)
is the Green’s function of the laplacian on
[0,1]^{d}

with Dirichlet boundary conditions.
For time changing boundaries densities and in non-equilibrium, under reasonable conditions we expect the corresponding generalization of (
15
) (
16
) should hold.

Another interesting generalization would be the investigazion of the space-time fluctuation for the
asymmetric dynamics, like the ASEP where there exists results for the quasi-static limit
DMLO
.

2
SSEP with boundary reservoirs

2.1
The model

Let
\alpha>0
. We consider the symmetric simple exclusion process
\{\eta_{t}\;;\;t\geq 0\}
on the interval
\{1,\dots,N-1\}
, with boundary creation/destruction of particles with rates
\rho_{-}(t)
at
x=1
and
\rho_{+}(t)
at
x=N-1
. The generator of the time inhomogeneous pure jumps Markov process
\{\eta_{tN^{2+\alpha}}\;;\;t\geq 0\}
with state space
\Omega^{N}=\{0,1\}^{\{1,\dots,N-1\}}
is given by

{{\mathcalboondox L}}_{N,t}=N^{2+\alpha}\left({{\mathcalboondox L}}_{N}^{0}+{\mathcalboondox L}_{N,t}^{\pm}\right)

where the generators above are acting on functions
f:\Omega^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
according to

({\mathcalboondox L}_{N}^{0}f)(\eta)=\sum_{x=1}^{N-2}\ \left[f(\eta^{x,x+1})-f(\eta)\right]

and

({\mathcalboondox L}_{N,t}^{-}f)(\eta)=\left[1-\rho_{-}(t)\right]\eta(1)\ \left[f(\eta^{1})-f(\eta)\right]+\rho_{-}(t)(1-\eta(1))\ \left[f(\eta^{1})-f(\eta)\right]

and

({\mathcalboondox L}_{N,t}^{+}f)(\eta)=\left[1-\rho_{+}(t)\right]\eta(N-1)\ \left[f(\eta^{N-1})-f(\eta)\right]+\rho_{+}(t)(1-\eta(N-1))\ \left[f(\eta^{N-1})-f(\eta)\right].

Above,
\eta^{x,y}
is the configuration obtained from
\eta
by exchanging the occupation variables at sites
x
and
y
, while
\eta^{x}
is the configuration obtained from
\eta
by flipping the occupation variable at site
x
, that is:

\eta^{x,y}(z)=\begin{cases}\eta(z)&\text{ if }z\notin\{x,y\},\\
\eta(y)&\text{ if }z=x,\\
\eta(x)&\text{ if }z=y,\end{cases}

and

\eta^{x}(y)=\begin{cases}\eta(y)&\text{ if }y\neq x,\\
1-\eta(x)&\text{ if }y=x.\end{cases}

2.2
Hydrodynamic limit

It is proved in
DO-qs
the following Law of Large Numbers:
if
\rho_{0}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
is a measurable profile and
at initial time the (deterministic or random) initial probability measure
\mu_{N}
on
\Omega^{N}
is such that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{N}\sum_{x=1}^{N-1}G\left(\cfrac{x}{N}\right)\eta_{0}(x)\ \mathop{\longrightarrow}^{\rm{prob.}}_{N\to\infty}\ \int_{0}^{1}G(u)\rho_{0}(u)\;du,\end{split}

(6)

for any continuous function
G:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
, then at any time
t\geq 0
it holds

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{N}\sum_{x=1}^{N-1}G\left(\cfrac{x}{N}\right)\eta_{tN^{2+\alpha}}(x)\ \mathop{\longrightarrow}^{\rm{prob.}}_{N\to\infty}\ \int_{0}^{1}G(u){\rho}_{t}(u)\;du,\\
&\text{with\quad}\rho_{t}(u)=\left(\rho_{+}(t)-\rho_{-}(t)\right)u+\rho_{-}(t).\end{split}

(7)

Let
\mu_{N}
be a probability measure on
\Omega^{N}
and, for
t\geq 0
,
we denote

\rho^{N}_{t}\left(x\right):=\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{N}}\left[\eta_{tN^{2+\alpha}}(x)\right],\quad x\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\},

(8)

with
\rho^{N}_{t}\left(0\right):=\rho_{-}(t)
, and
\rho^{N}_{t}\left(N\right):=\rho_{+}(t)
. Define for any
u,v\in[0,1]
and
s,t\geq 0

{\varphi}^{N}(u,v;s,t):=\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{N}}\left[\left(\eta_{sN^{2+\alpha}}([Nu])-\rho^{N}_{s}([Nu])\right)\left(\eta_{tN^{2+\alpha}}([Nv])-\rho^{N}_{t}([Nv])\right)\right].

(9)

We assume that

\begin{split}\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{u,v\in[0,1],u\neq v}N|{\varphi}^{N}(u,v;0,0)|\lesssim 1\end{split}

(10)

where here and in the following,
\lesssim
denotes an inequality that is correct up to a multiplicative constant independent of
N
.

It is proved in Theorem 2.2 of
DO-qs
that, for
u\neq v
and
u,v\not\in\{0,1\}
,

\begin{split}&\lim_{N\to\infty}N{\varphi}^{N}(u,v;t,t)=-\left(\rho_{+}(t)-\rho_{-}(t)\right)u(1-v)\end{split}

(11)

and for
t\neq s

\lim_{N\to\infty}N{\varphi}^{N}(u,v;s,t)=0.

(12)

We also note that from Lemma 4.3 in
lmo
and from (
10
) we get that

\sup_{N\geq 1}\max_{x,y\in\{1,\cdots,N-1\},x\neq y}\,N|{\varphi}^{N}\big(\tfrac{x}{N},\tfrac{y}{N};t,t\big)|\lesssim 1.

(13)

Above we used the proof of
lmo
that can be adapted to our case, since our correlation function
{\varphi}^{N}(u,v;t,t)
(see (
9
) with
s=t
) also satisfies an equation similar to (4.5) in
lmo
. By following exactly the same steps as in the proof of Lemma 4.3 of
lmo
we conclude (
13
). We leave these details to the interested reader.

2.3
Main result

For a fixed
T>0
, we denote the open set
\Omega_{T}=(0,T)\times(0,1)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
We define the (centered) space-time distribution valued random field

\mathbb{Y}^{N}
acting on

f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\left(\Omega_{T},\mathbb{C}\right)
as

\mathbb{Y}^{N}(f)=\int_{0}^{T}\cfrac{1}{\sqrt{N^{1-\alpha}}}\sum_{x=1}^{N-1}{f}(t,\tfrac{x}{N})\left(\eta_{tN^{2+\alpha}}(x)-{\rho^{N}_{t}(x)}\right)dt.

(14)

Let
\mathbb{Y}
be the space-time distribution valued centered
Gaussian field such that for any function
f

the variance
\mathbb{V}(f)
of
\mathbb{Y}(f)
is given by

\mathbb{V}(f)=2\int_{0}^{T}ds\int_{0}^{1}du\ \rho_{s}(u)(1-\rho_{s}(u))\left|\partial_{u}F(s,u)\right|^{2}

(15)

where
F
is the solution of

\begin{cases}&\left(\partial_{u}^{2}F\right)(t,u)=f(t,u),\quad u\in[0,1],\\
&F(t,0)=F(t,1)=0,\end{cases}

(16)

for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1
.

The sequence
\{\mathbb{Y}^{N}\;;\;N\geq 1\}
converges in law, as
N\to+\infty
, to
\mathbb{Y}
.

Remark 1
.

We have defined
\mathbb{Y}^{N}
as an element of the dual of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\left(\Omega_{T},\mathbb{C}\right)
. In the theory of distributions is usually denoted
\mathcal{E}\left(\Omega_{T},\mathbb{C}\right)=\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\left(\Omega_{T},\mathbb{C}\right)
and
\mathcal{D}\left(\Omega_{T},\mathbb{C}\right)=\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}\left(\Omega_{T},\mathbb{C}\right)
. The restriction from
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\left(\Omega_{T},\mathbb{C}\right)
to
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\left(\Omega_{T},\mathbb{C}\right)
is injective (cf.
DK
, Chapter 8). In Subsection
3.2
we prove that
\{\mathbb{Y}^{N}\;;\;N\geq 1\}
is tight in a Sobolev space
{\mathcalboondox H}_{-m,-k}\subset\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\left(\Omega_{T},\mathbb{C}\right)
.

3
Proof of Theorem
1

The proof of Theorem
1
is a consequence of two ingredients: convergence of finite dimensional distributions and tightness.
Therefore, in Proposition
1
we establish
the convergence in law of the sequence
\{\mathbb{Y}^{N}(f)\;;\;N\geq 1\}
to
\mathbb{Y}(f)

for any test function
f
and in Lemma

we prove that the sequence
\{\mathbb{Y}^{N}\;;\;N\geq 1\}

is tight in a suitable Sobolev space of distributions.

3.1
Convergence of finite-dimensional distributions

Proposition 1
.

For any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and for any
f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega_{T},\mathbb{C})
it holds that the sequence
\{\mathbb{Y}^{N}(f)\;;\;N\geq 1\}
converges in law to the centred complex Gaussian random variable with variance
\mathbb{V}(f)
where
\mathbb{V}(f)
is defined in (
15
).

Proof.

We claim first that it is sufficient to prove that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(\exp\left\{i\theta{\mathbb{Y}}^{N}(f)\right\}\right)=\exp\left\{-\tfrac{\theta^{2}}{2}\mathbb{V}(f)\right\}

(17)

for real-valued functions.

Indeed, if this is the case, then let
f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega_{T},\mathbb{C})
be decomposed as
f=g+ih
where
g,h\in C^{\infty}(\Omega_{T},\mathbb{R})
. Therefore, for any real numbers
\lambda,\mu
, (
17
) is valid for
\lambda g+\mu h
, i.e. the sequence of real random variables

\left\{\mathbb{Y}^{N}(\lambda g+\mu h)=\lambda\mathbb{Y}^{N}(g)+\mu\mathbb{Y}^{N}(h)\;;\;N\geq 1\right\}

converges to a Gaussian random variable with variance
\mathbb{V}(\lambda g+\mu h)
. Let us define

\mathbb{V}(g,h)=2\int_{0}^{T}ds\int_{0}^{1}du\ \rho_{s}(u)(1-\rho_{s}(u))\partial_{u}G(s,u)\ \partial_{u}H(s,u)

(18)

where
G
and
H
are the solutions, respectively, of

\begin{cases}&\left(\partial_{u}^{2}G\right)(t,u)=g(t,u),\quad u\in[0,1],\\
&G(t,0)=G(t,1)=0,\end{cases}

(19)

for all
t\in[0,T]
,
and

\begin{cases}&\left(\partial_{u}^{2}H\right)(t,u)=h(t,u),\quad u\in[0,1],\\
&H(t,0)=H(t,1)=0,\end{cases}

(20)

for all
t\in[0,T]
. It is not difficult to check from the definition of
\mathbb{V}
given in (
15

\mathbb{V}(\lambda g+\mu h)=\lambda^{2}\mathbb{V}(g)+\mu^{2}\mathbb{V}(h)+2\lambda\mu\mathbb{V}(g,h)
. This implies that the sequence of random vectors

\left\{(\mathbb{Y}^{N}(g),\mathbb{Y}^{N}(h))\;;\;N\geq 1\right\}

converges in law to a centred Gaussian random vector with covariance matrix

\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{V}(g)&\mathbb{V}(g,h)\\
\mathbb{V}(g,h)&\mathbb{V}(h)\end{pmatrix}\ .

Hence the sequence
\{\mathbb{Y}^{N}(f)=\mathbb{Y}^{N}(g)+i\mathbb{Y}^{N}(h)\;;\;N\geq 1\}
converges in law to a complex centered Gaussian variable with variance (observe that the solution of (
16
) is
F=G+iH
)

\mathbb{V}(g)+\mathbb{V}(h)=\mathbb{V}(f)

and this proves the claim.

Note that from (

&\lesssim N^{-(1+\alpha/2)}\left\{\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{T}_{N}}}\left[f\left(s,\tfrac{x}{N}\right)\right]^{2}ds+N^{-2\alpha}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{T}_{N}}}\left[\partial_{s}F^{N}\left(s,\tfrac{x}{N}\right)\right]^{2}ds\right\}.\end{split}

(49)

Hence the square of the
L^{2}
-norm of the third term on the right-hand side of (
47
) is bounded above by
N^{\alpha/2-2}
. It goes to
0
if, and only if,
\alpha<4
. This ends the proof.

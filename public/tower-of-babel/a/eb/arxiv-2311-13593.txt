Partial Resolutions of Affine Symplectic Singularities
Alberto San Miguel Malaney

Abstract
. We explore the relationship between the Poisson deformation theory, birational geometry, and Springer theory of partial resolutions of affine symplectic singularities. Let ρ : X ′ → X \rho:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X be a crepant partial resolution of a conical affine symplectic singularity X X . We show that the Poisson deformation functor of X ′ X^{\prime} is prorepresentable and unobstructed. Additionally, we define a version of the Namikawa Weyl group for these crepant partial resolutions. In particular, the Namikawa Weyl group of X ′ X^{\prime} is a parabolic subgroup of the Namikawa Weyl group of X X that is determined by the birational geometry of X ′ X^{\prime} . If π : Y → X \pi:Y\rightarrow X is a ℚ \mathbb{Q} -factorial terminalization of X X that covers X ′ X^{\prime} , we show there is a natural functor from Poisson deformations of Y Y to those of X ′ X^{\prime} . Building on work of Namikawa in [ 23 ] , we show that this morphism is a Galois covering and the Galois group is the Namikawa Weyl group of X ′ X^{\prime} . Finally, we put these partial resolutions and their universal deformations into the context of recent work of McGerty and Nevins in [ 17 ] , obtaining some preliminary results concerning their Springer theory. In particular, if the universal deformation of X ′ X^{\prime} is rationally smooth, we compute the cohomology of the fibers of ρ \rho in terms of the cohomology of the fibers of π \pi and the Namikawa Weyl group of X ′ X^{\prime} .



1.
Introduction

In this paper we study the deformation theory, birational geometry, and Springer theory of partial resolutions of conical affine symplectic singularities.

1.1.
Motivation

The Springer resolution
\pi:\tilde{\mathcal{N}}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}
of a semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a central object in geometric representation theory. In particular, it is the unique symplectic resolution of the nilpotent cone
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathfrak{g}
. More generally, for every conjugacy class
\mathcal{P}
of parabolic subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
, we obtain the following partial resolution of
\mathcal{N}
:

\tilde{\mathcal{N}}^{\mathcal{P}}:=\{(x,\mathfrak{p})|x\in\mathfrak{p}\cap\mathcal{N},\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}\}.

If we let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of Borel subalgebras we recover the Springer resolution. Furthermore, each Borel subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}\subset\mathfrak{g}
is contained in a unique element of each conjugacy class
\mathcal{P}
of parabolic subalgebras, so we obtain birational and projective maps

\tilde{\mathcal{N}}\xrightarrow{\eta}\tilde{\mathcal{N}}^{\mathcal{P}}\xrightarrow{\xi}\mathcal{N}.

Let
W
be the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then we can take the Weyl group of the Levi subalgebra of any
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}
to obtain a parabolic subgroup
W_{\mathcal{P}}\subset W
of the Weyl group. This gives us a bijection between conjugacy classes of parabolic subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
and parabolic subgroups of
W
, both of which can also be classified by subsets of the simple roots of
\mathfrak{g}
.

The relationship between the geometry of these partial resolutions and the combinatorics of the Weyl group was studied extensively in

1.2.
Outline of Results

In this paper we introduce partial resolutions into the above story, treating them as the appropriate analogue of parabolic subalgebras in the symplectic setting. We introduce a natural definition of a symplectic partial resolution, which we go on to show is equivalent to being crepant and to being covered by a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization. The main construction of the paper is a generalization of the Namikawa Weyl group to crepant partial resolutions
\rho:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
of conical affine symplectic singularities, such that the Namikawa Weyl group
W_{X^{\prime}}
of a partial resolution is a parabolic subgroup of
W_{X}
.

In order to proceed we prove that the Poisson deformation functor
\operatorname{PD}_{X^{\prime}}
of
X^{\prime}
is also prorepresentable and unobstructed and that
X^{\prime}
has a universal conic deformation over
\operatorname{PD}_{X^{\prime}}(\mathbb{C}[\epsilon])

\pi:Y\rightarrow X

\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization of
X

\operatorname{PD}_{Y}\xrightarrow{\pi^{\prime}_{*}}\operatorname{PD}_{X^{\prime}}\xrightarrow{\rho_{*}}\operatorname{PD}_{X}.

One key result and motivation for this paper is Namikawa’s result in
[
25

that a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
of a conical affine symplectic singularity is also a relative Mori Dream Space. We explain that this implies that crepant partial resolutions of
X
are classified by faces of the Mori fan of
\overline{{\operatorname{Mov}(Y/X)}}
. This provides a combinatorial description of the crepant partial resolutions of
X
, and it is also the setting in which we first define the Namikawa Weyl group of a partial resolution.

In particular, to every simple generator
s
of the Namikawa Weyl group
W_{X}
, we assign a hyperplane
H_{s}\subset H^{2}(Y^{\text{reg}},\mathbb{C})
, which bounds
\overline{{\operatorname{Mov}(Y/X)}}
. Given a face
F
of
\overline{{\operatorname{Mov}(Y/X)}}
, we define the Namikawa Weyl group of the corresponding crepant partial resolution to be the parabolic subgroup of
W_{X}
generated by all the simple generators
s
such that
F\subset H_{s}
.

The nilpotent cone also has the special property that it has a unique
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization. Whenever this is the case, each face of the Mori fan will be given by intersecting
\overline{{\operatorname{Mov}(Y/X)}}
with a unique subset of the
H_{s}
above. Thus we can view this as a way of expressing the bijection between conjugacy classes of parabolic subalgebras, parabolic subgroups of the Weyl group, and subsets of the simple roots. It also shows that these are in natural bijection with crepant partial resolutions.

These bijections generalize to a bijection
[\rho:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X]\mapsto W_{X^{\prime}}
between crepant partial resolutions and parabolic subgroups of
W_{X}
anytime
X
has a unique
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization. As proved in
4.17
, this map is not a bijection for every conical affine symplectic singularity, but it is in general surjective. This is because if there is more than one
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization, there will be faces in the interior of
\overline{{\operatorname{Mov}(Y/X)}}
that correspond to small contractions that allow us to flop between
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalizations. As a corollary of this theorem we also prove in
4.18
that every crepant partial resolution of a nilpotent cone
\mathcal{N}
is of the form
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}^{\mathcal{P}}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}
described at the beginning of the introduction.

In the final section of the paper we begin to study Springer theory of crepant partial resolutions as described above. We briefly highlight two instances of interest. The first is a generalization of equation (
1
). There are simple examples of conical affine symplectic singularities with trivial Namikawa Weyl group but nontrivial crepant partial resolutions, which shows the analogous equation could not be true in general. However, we show in
8.7
that the analogous statement
H^{*}(\rho^{-1}(x),\mathbb{C})\cong H^{*}(\pi^{-1}(x),\mathbb{C})^{W_{X^{\prime}}}
is true as long as the universal conic deformation
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
of
X
is rationally smooth.

Secondly, we introduce the algebra
\operatorname{End}(\mathsf{Spr}^{\prime})\cong H^{\text{BM}}_{top}(Z^{\prime})
where
\mathsf{Spr}^{\prime}:=\pi^{\prime}_{*}(\mathbb{C}_{Y})
and
Z^{\prime}:=Y\times_{X^{\prime}}Y
. As explained in diagram (
52
), this algebra has a
W_{X^{\prime}}
-action that is compatible with the natural map
\rho_{*}:\operatorname{End}(\mathsf{Spr}^{\prime})\rightarrow\operatorname{End}(\mathsf{Spr})
, which we show is an injection, and with the
W_{X}
-action on
\mathsf{Spr}
. In particular, this algebra provides a coarser invariant than
W_{X^{\prime}}
that we can attach to a crepant partial resolution.

1.3.
Summary

In
Section

2
we review group actions on simply-laced Dynkin diagrams and fix a formalism for the folded Dynkin diagrams we obtain by taking a quotient. We note that unlike in some formalisms for folded Dynkin diagrams, we allow the group action to permute nodes that have edges connecting them. For example we will have
A_{4}/({\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}})\cong B_{2}
. In
Section

3
we review some basics about symplectic singularities. We recall some facts about
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalizations as well as the definition of the Namikawa Weyl group.

In
Section

4
we begin to study crepant partial resolutions of conical affine symplectic singularities. In particular, we define them and prove a few equivalent characterizations of them. We review the main theorem of
[
25
]
, which roughly states that if
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization of a conical affine symplectic singularity, then it is also a relative Mori Dream space. Using this we obtain a combinatorial description of crepant partial resolutions by faces of the Mori fan of
\overline{{\operatorname{Mov}(Y/X)}}
, and we use this description to define the Namikawa Weyl group of a crepant partial resolution. In
4.17
we prove the key result that the map from crepant partial resolutions to parabolic subgroups of
W_{X}
is surjective, and bijective if and only if
X
has a unique
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization.

In
Section

5
we begin to study the deformation theory of crepant partial resolutions. We use many of the techniques in
[
22
]
and
[
24
]
to show that
\operatorname{PD}_{X^{\prime}}
is prorepresentable and unobstructed for any crepant partial resolution
\rho:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
. Additionally, we define the pushforward functors
\operatorname{PD}_{Y}\rightarrow\operatorname{PD}_{X^{\prime}}\rightarrow\operatorname{PD}_{X}
on deformations, where
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization that covers
\rho
. In
Section

6
we continue by studying the deformation theory of crepant partial resolutions of Kleinian singularities. The main strategy is that these partial resolutions have finitely many singular points, with singularities which are also Kleinian and correspond to a subset of the Dynkin diagram corresponding to the original Kleinian singularity. Using this we are able to prove the main theorem for partial resolutions of Kleinian singularities.

In
Section

7
we adapt the argument in
[
23
, 1.1]
to prove the main theorem. Given
Y\rightarrow X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
as above, we proceed by looking at the partial resolution and
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization locally over a small neighborhood of a point in each codimension two symplectic leaf of
X
. Thus we obtain full and partial resolutions of the Kleinian singularity given by taking a transverse slice to the symplectic leaf. We are able to use the results from the previous section to describe the pushforward maps
\operatorname{PD}_{Y}\rightarrow\operatorname{PD}_{X^{\prime}}\rightarrow\operatorname{PD}_{X}
in the general case. Thus we obtain an explicit description of the universal conic deformation of
\operatorname{PD}_{X^{\prime}}
, proving the main theorem,
7.3
.

Finally, in
Section

8
we use the main theorem to study the symplectic Springer theory of crepant partial resolutions, using many of the techniques from
[
17
]
. We explain how many of the results in that paper that establish the viability of symplectic Springer theory carry over for crepant partial resolutions. Thus we are able to define the partial symplectic Springer and Harish-Chandra sheaves, the former of which is also constructible with respect to the stratification by symplectic leaves. We end with a generalization of
[
4
, 2.8]
, showing that if the universal conic deformation
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
is rationally smooth, then
H^{*}(\rho^{-1}(x),\mathbb{C})\cong H^{*}(\pi^{-1}(x),\mathbb{C})^{W_{X^{\prime}}}
.

1.4.
Notation and Conventions

All varieties and schemes will be assumed to be over
\mathbb{C}
. The results of this paper primarily involve algebraic varieties, but if we use the word “neighborhood” it will usually mean an open set in the complex analytic topology. Germs will also be taken in the complex analytic topology. Finally,
\epsilon
will be assumed to square to zero, so that
\mathbb{C}[\epsilon]
will always denote the dual numbers.

Throughout the paper
X
will always be a conical affine symplectic singularity,
\rho:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
will always be a crepant partial resolution and
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
will always be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization of
X
, except in
Section

8
, where
\pi
will always be a symplectic resolution. If
\pi
covers
\rho
then
\pi^{\prime}:Y\rightarrow X^{\prime}
will be the map making
Y\rightarrow X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
commute. Adding a prime symbol will denote the “partial” version. For example, the partial Springer and Harish-Chandra sheaves will be denoted
\mathsf{Spr}^{\prime}
and
\mathsf{HC}^{\prime}
.

Whenever there is a group action on a Dynkin diagram, we will denote the unfolded Dynkin diagram and all of its data (such as the Cartan and Weyl group) with a hat symbol. The folded Dynkin diagram and its data will have no hat. In
Section

6
there are no group actions so we will drop the hats entirely. Additionally, hats are also used to denote formal completions, but the notation
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
will only appear to denote the Cartan of a simply laced Dynkin diagram.

Finally, we call a map of normal varieties
f:Y\rightarrow X
a Galois cover if it is finite, surjective, and the degree of the map is
|\textrm{Aut}(Y/X)|
. This last condition is equivalent to the extension
[\mathbb{C}(Y),\mathbb{C}(X)]
of function fields being Galois. In the literature this is often called “generically Galois.” We will also refer to
\textrm{Aut}(Y/X)
as the Galois group. Given a Galois cover
f:Y\rightarrow X
and an open set
U\subset X
in the complex analytic topology, we will also refer to the map
f^{-1}(U)\rightarrow U
as a Galois cover.

, 1.1-1.6]
. Clearly as a word in
W
,
l(ww^{0}_{j_{n}})=l(w)-1
where
l
is the word length function. Thus by
[
31
, 1.8]
,
ww^{0}_{j_{n}}
makes one less positive root of
\Phi
negative than
w
.

Since
w\in\hat{W}^{\prime}
,
[
31
, 1.15]
tells us that as an element of
\hat{W}
it permutes all the positive roots of
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
whose support is not contained in
I^{\prime}
. In particular it sends
\alpha_{i}
to a positive root for each
i\in j_{n}
. Thus since
\beta_{j_{n}}
is a linear combination of these
\alpha_{i}
, it must map
\beta_{j_{n}}
to the positive cone of
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
. Since the positive cone of
\mathfrak{h}
is contained in the positive cone of
\hat{\mathfrak{h}}
and
\beta_{j_{n}}\in\Phi
,
w
must send
\beta_{j_{n}}
to a positive root of
\Phi
. By
[
31
, 1.7]
,
w^{0}_{j_{n}}
permutes all the positive roots of
\Phi
except for
\beta_{j_{n}}
, which it negates. Thus if
\{r_{1},...,r_{k}\}\subset\Phi
is the set of positive roots that
w
sends to negative roots, the analogous set for
ww^{0}_{j_{n}}
is
\{w^{0}_{j_{n}}(r_{1}),...,w^{0}_{j_{n}}(r_{k}),\beta_{j_{n}}\}
. However this contradicts the previous paragraph, so
\hat{W}^{\prime}\cap W\subset W^{\prime}
and we are done.

∎

We will now briefly recall the relationship between Dynkin diagrams and subregular Springer fibers. Given a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with Springer resolution
\pi:\tilde{\mathcal{N}}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}\subset\mathfrak{g}
, the subregular orbit
\mathbb{O}_{\text{subreg}}\subset\mathcal{N}
is the unique nilpotent orbit which is open in the complement of the regular nilpotent orbit. A number of equivalent conditions to being subregular are stated in
[
32
, 3.10, Theorem 2]
. These conditions also imply that the subregular nilpotent orbit can be characterized as the Richardson orbit of any minimal parabolic.

Let
D
be the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{g}
and let
\hat{D}
be the unique simply laced Dynkin diagram such that
D\cong\hat{D}/\Gamma
, where
\Gamma\ \rotatebox[origin]{-90.0}{$\circlearrowright$}\ \hat{D}
is a faithful group action which does not permute nodes that are connected by an edge. Additionally, let
G
be a simple Lie algebra of adjoint type with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
x\in\mathcal{N}
be a subregular nilpotent element and let
\beta
be a simple root of
\mathfrak{g}
. Furthermore, let
\mathcal{P}_{\beta}
be the corresponding conjugacy class of minimal parabolic subalgebras, which is in bijection with
G/P
, for any parabolic subgroup
P
of type
\beta
. By
[
32
, 3.10, Theorem 2]
,
x
is in the nilradical of
|\beta|^{2}/|\beta_{\text{min}}|^{2}
parabolic subalgebras
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{\beta}
. Note that
|\beta|^{2}/|\beta_{\text{min}}|^{2}
is the same as the number of nodes in
\hat{D}
that map to the node in
D
corresponding to
\beta
. Each of the these parabolic subalgebras
\mathfrak{p}
produces a projective line in
\pi^{-1}(x)
given by the set of Borel subalgebras contained in
\mathfrak{p}
, which is isomorphic to
P/B\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Thus,
\pi^{-1}(x)
consists of a union of projective lines that are in bijection with the nodes of
\hat{D}
. By
[
32
, 3.10, Theorem 2]
or
[
29
, Section 6.3]
, two of these projective lines intersect at a point if the corresponding nodes of
\hat{D}
are connected and do not intersect otherwise. Furthermore, by
[
29
, 7.5, Proposition]
, when
D
is not simply laced the action of
G
on
\hat{D}
is isomorphic to the action of
Z_{G}(x)/Z_{G}(x)^{\circ}
, the component group of the centralizer of
x
, on the projective lines comprising
\pi^{-1}(x)
. Note that if
D
is simply laced then this action is trivial since the projective lines correspond to minimal parabolic subalgebras in different conjugacy classes. Finally, two projective lines are permuted by
Z_{G}(x)/Z_{G}(x)^{\circ}
if and only if they are permuted by the monodromy action of
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{O}_{\text{subreg}},x)
. The results described in this paragraph produce the following result:

Corollary 2.6
.

Given any
x\in\mathbb{O}_{\text{subreg}}
, there is a canonical bijection between the simple roots of
\mathfrak{g}
and the
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{O}_{\text{subreg}},x)
-orbits of the irreducible components of
\pi^{-1}(x)
. This bijection is given by sending each simple root
\beta
to the set of projective lines given by
\{(x,\mathfrak{b})\ |\ \mathfrak{b}\subset\mathfrak{p}\}
, for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{\beta}
with
x
in the nilradical of
\mathfrak{p}
.

3.
Preliminaries and Background

Definition 3.1
.

A symplectic singularity (or symplectic variety) is a normal algebraic variety
X
with a symplectic form
\omega
on
X_{\text{reg}}
such that for any (equivalently, some) resolution of singularities
\pi:\tilde{X}\rightarrow X
,
\pi^{*}(\omega)
extends to a regular 2-form on
\tilde{X}
. If it extends to a symplectic form then
\tilde{X}
is called a symplectic resolution.

The symplectic form induces a Poisson bracket on
\mathcal{O}(X_{\text{reg}})
, and since
X
is normal, this bracket extends to all of
X
, making
X
a Poisson scheme. Throughout the paper we shall study the universal Poisson deformations of affine symplectic singularities and their (partial) symplectic resolutions. In order to ensure that universal formal deformations algebraicize to deformations over affine spaces, we need the following condition as well.

Definition 3.2
.

A symplectic singularity
X
is conical if it has an algebraic
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action such that
\mathcal{O}(X)
has non-negative weights,
\mathcal{O}(X)^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}=\mathbb{C}
, and
\omega_{X}
is positively weighted with respect to
\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

Remark 3.3
.

The conditions that the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action has non-negative weights and
\mathcal{O}(X)^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}=\mathbb{C}
are equivalent to the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action contracting
X
to a unique cone point.

The following theorem is one of the most important facts about symplectic singularities:

Theorem 3.4

(
[
12
, 2.3]
)
.

Every symplectic singularity has a finite stratification by symplectic leaves.

Throughout this paper we will be particularly interested in the codimension two symplectic leaves. Thus we recall (see
[
19
, Cor. 1]
) that a symplectic singularity has terminal singularities if and only if it has no codimension two symplectic leaves.

In general not every symplectic singularity has a symplectic resolution, but they always have a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization, which is defined below:

Definition 3.5
.

Given a symplectic singularity
X
, a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization (or simply
\mathbb{Q}
-terminalization)
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
is a crepant birational projective morphism such that
Y
is a normal
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial variety with terminal singularities.

We recall some essential facts about
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalizations below:

Theorem 3.6

([
[
15
, 2.1]
)
.

Let
X
be a symplectic singularity:

(1)

X
has a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization,
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
.

(2)

Y
is a symplectic singularity and
\pi
is a Poisson map.

(3)

There is a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
Y
such that
\pi
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant.

Remark 3.7
.

The proof of part (1) is based primarily on
[
3
, 1.4.3]
. Additionally, part (3) was first proven in
[
22
, A.7]
.

From now on, let
X
be a conical affine symplectic singularity, and
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization. Let
U\subset X
be the complement of the symplectic leaves of codimension four or greater. This inherits the structure of a Poisson scheme from
X
, and since
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalizations do not have any codimension two leaves,
\tilde{U}:=\pi^{-1}(U)
is a symplectic resolution of
U
.

Given a codimension two symplectic leaf
\mathcal{L}_{i}\subset U
and a point
x_{i}\in\mathcal{L}_{i}
, we can take a transverse slice
S_{i}
to
x_{i}
, which will be a Kleinian singularity. Let
\hat{D}_{i}
be the Dynkin diagram corresponding to this singularity, with Cartan
\hat{\mathfrak{h}_{i}}
and Weyl group
\hat{W_{i}}
. Thus
\pi^{-1}(x_{i})
is a union of projective lines in the shape of
\hat{D}_{i}
, and we obtain a monodromy action of
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{i},x_{i})
on
\hat{D}_{i}
by graph automorphism. This induces an action of
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{i},x_{i})
on
\hat{\mathfrak{h}_{i}}
, and we define

\mathfrak{h}_{i}:=\hat{\mathfrak{h}}_{i}^{\pi_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{i},x_{i})}\hskip 20.0pt\text{and}\hskip 20.0ptW_{i}=\{w\in\hat{W_{i}}\ |\ w(\mathfrak{h}_{i})=\mathfrak{h}_{i}\}.

In particular, these are the Cartan and Weyl group of
D_{i}:=\hat{D}_{i}/{\pi_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{i},x_{i})}
, as defined in
Section

2
.

Remark 3.8
.

Although it is not always possible to take an algebraic transverse slice to each codimension two symplectic leaf, we can always take either a formal slice or a transverse slice in the complex analytic category.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
\{\mathcal{L}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be the set of codimension two symplectic leaves of
X
. Then the Namikawa Weyl group of
X
is

W_{X}:=\prod_{i\in I}W_{i}

This group appears naturally when studying the Poisson deformations of
X
and
Y
, as explained in
Section

1
.

4.
Partial Symplectic Resolutions and Birational Geometry

Definition 4.1
.

A map
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
of normal varieties is a partial resolution if
\pi
is projective and birational and the restriction of
\pi
to
\pi^{-1}(X_{\textrm{reg}})
is an isomorphism. Furthermore we say
\pi
is a symplectic partial resolution if the symplectic form
\omega_{X_{\textrm{reg}}}
extends to a symplectic form on
Y_{\textrm{reg}}
.

Remark 4.2
.

Traditionally a (partial) resolution of singularities is only required to be proper, rather than projective. However, for our purposes we will only be interested in projective (partial) resolutions.

Before proceeding we recall that Zariski’s main theorem (see
[
9
, III.11.4]
) implies that for any partial resolution
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
, we have
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
, i.e.
\pi
is a contraction.

Lemma 4.3
.

Any symplectic partial resolution
Y
of a symplectic singularity
X
is itself a symplectic singularity.

Proof.

By definition
Y
is normal and has a symplectic form
\omega_{Y_{\textrm{reg}}}
on its smooth locus that extends
\omega_{X_{\textrm{reg}}}
. All that is left to check is that given a resolution
\tilde{\pi}:Z\rightarrow Y
,
\tilde{\pi}^{*}(\omega_{Y_{\textrm{reg}}})
extends to a regular 2-form on
Z
. Since
\pi\circ\tilde{\pi}
is a resolution of
X
, and
X
is a symplectic singularity,
\omega_{X_{\textrm{reg}}}
extends to a regular 2-form
\omega_{Z}
on
Z
. Note that since
Z
is smooth, the sheaf of 2-forms is a vector bundle, and thus restriction to a Zariski open set is injective. Since we can treat
X_{\textrm{reg}}
and
Y_{\textrm{reg}}
as Zariski open sets of
Z
, this means that the extensions
\omega_{Y_{\textrm{reg}}}
and
\omega_{Z}
of
\omega_{X_{\textrm{reg}}}
are unique. In particular,
\omega_{Z}
must extend
\omega_{Y_{\textrm{reg}}}
.

∎

The following argument is identical for full resolutions (see
[
8
, Prop. 1.1]
) but we include it for completeness.

Theorem 4.4
.

A partial resolution is symplectic if and only if it is crepant.

Proof.

Let
X
be a symplectic singularity with
\dim X=2n
and let
\omega_{X}
be the symplectic form on
X_{\textrm{reg}}
. Then
\wedge^{n}\omega_{X}
provides a trivialization of
K_{X}
over
X_{\text{reg}}
which extends to a trivialization of
K_{X}
by normality. Thus every symplectic singularity has trivial canonical sheaf. In particular, given a partial symplectic resolution
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
, we have
\pi^{*}(K_{X})=\pi^{*}0=0=K_{Y}
, so the map is crepant.

Conversely, say
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
is crepant. Since
X
has symplectic singularities,
\pi^{*}\omega_{X}
extends to a regular 2-form
\omega_{Y}
on
Y_{\textrm{reg}}
. Additionally,
\wedge^{n}\omega_{X}
extends to a non-vanishing global section
f
of
K_{X}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Thus,
f
pulls back to a non-vanishing section of
K_{Y}\cong\mathcal{O}_{Y}
that extends
\pi^{*}(\wedge^{n}\omega_{X})=\wedge^{n}\pi^{*}\omega_{X}
and thus
\wedge^{n}\omega_{Y}
. Therefore,
\wedge^{n}\omega_{Y}
is a non-vanishing section of
K_{Y}|_{Y_{\text{reg}}}\cong\Omega^{n}_{Y_{\text{reg}}}
, which implies that
\omega_{Y}
is non-degenerate. Finally, since
\pi^{*}
commutes with differentials,
\operatorname{d}(\omega_{Y})
extends
\pi^{*}\operatorname{d}(\omega_{X})=0
on
\pi^{-1}(X_{\text{reg}})
, which extends uniquely to 0 on
Y_{\text{reg}}
. Thus
\omega_{Y}
is closed and we are done.

∎

The above theorem implies that if
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization of a symplectic singularity
X
, then
Y
is a symplectic partial resolution of
X
. In fact,
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalizations can be described as the maximal elements of the poset of symplectic partial resolutions, as shown below.

Lemma 4.5
.

A partial resolution
\rho:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
of a symplectic singularity
X
is symplectic if and only if it is covered by a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization of
X
.

Proof.

If
\rho:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
is symplectic then by
Lemma

4.3
it has symplectic singularities and thus by
3.6
there is some
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization
\pi^{\prime}:Y\rightarrow X^{\prime}
. Thus,
\pi:=\rho\circ\pi^{\prime}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization of
X
that covers
\rho
.

Conversely, let
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization of
X
that covers
\rho
, with covering map
\pi^{\prime}:Y\rightarrow X^{\prime}
. Then the symplectic form
\omega_{X_{\textrm{reg}}}
on
X_{\text{reg}}
extends to a symplectic form on
Y_{\text{reg}}
. In particular,
\pi=\rho\circ\pi^{\prime}
implies that
X^{\prime}_{\text{reg}}\subset Y_{\text{reg}}
, so
\omega_{X_{\textrm{reg}}}
extends to a symplectic form on
X^{\prime}_{\text{reg}}
. Thus
\rho
is symplectic.

∎

From now on we will only be interested in symplectic partial resolutions, so we will simply call them partial resolutions and they will be assumed to be symplectic.

Additionally, for the remainder of the paper let
X
be a conical affine symplectic singularity, and let
\rho:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
be a partial resolution which is covered by a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
. Since
X
is affine we have
H^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0,\forall i>0
. Furthermore, since
X
,
X^{\prime}
, and
Y
all have rational singularities, we have quasi-isomorphisms
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
and
\rho_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
in the derived category, which implies
H^{i}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})=H^{i}(X^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}})=0,\forall i>0
.

The following theorem will allow us pass back and forth between the settings of birational geometry and Poisson deformation theory, as will become clearer in the following section.

Theorem 4.6

(
[
14
, 4.4.6]
)
.

The first Chern class map provides an isomorphism

c_{1}:\operatorname{Pic}(Y^{\text{reg}})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathbb{Q}\rightarrow H^{2}(Y^{\text{reg}},\mathbb{Q}).

Remark 4.7
.

The analogue of the above theorem for the analytic Picard group can be deduced via a standard computation using the exponential short exact sequence. However, the above statement applies to the algebraic Picard group, which requires an argument using the GAGA principle.

Traditionally, the cones described in
4.8
below would be subsets of

\operatorname{Pic}(Y/X)\otimes\mathbb{R}:=(\operatorname{Pic}(Y)/\pi^{*}(\operatorname{Pic}(X)))\otimes\mathbb{R}.

However by
[
14
, 7.1.1]
, the Picard group of every normal affine conical scheme is trivial, and thus
\operatorname{Pic}(X)=0
. Additionally, by
[
9
, 6.5]
,
\operatorname{Cl}(Y)\cong\operatorname{Cl}(Y^{\text{reg}})
where
\operatorname{Cl}
denotes the divisor class group, since
Y-Y^{\text{reg}}
has codimension at least four. Finally since
Y
and
Y^{\text{reg}}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial, this implies
\operatorname{Pic}(Y)\otimes\mathbb{Q}=\operatorname{Pic}(Y^{\text{reg}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
. So indeed we have

\operatorname{Pic}(Y/X)\otimes\mathbb{R}\cong H^{2}(Y^{\text{reg}},\mathbb{R}).

As detailed in
[
23
]
,
W_{X}
acts on
H^{2}(Y^{\text{reg}},\mathbb{C})
as a finite reflection group, and the action is generically free. The following celebrated theorem of Namikawa will allow us to classify partial resolutions of conical affine symplectic singularities.

Theorem 4.8

(
[
25
, Main Theorem, Remark 10]
)
.

If
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization, then there are finitely many hyperplanes
\{H_{i}\}\subset H^{2}(Y^{\text{reg}},\mathbb{R})
such that

(1)

The union
\mathcal{D}
of the
H_{i}
is invariant under the action of
W_{X}
.

(2)

\mathcal{D}
is the locus over which the universal deformation map
\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
is not a fiberwise isomorphism, as described in diagram (
2
).

(3)

For every
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial termination
\pi^{\prime}:Y^{\prime}\rightarrow X
,
\overline{{\operatorname{Amp}(\pi^{\prime})}}=\operatorname{Nef}(\pi^{\prime})
is a unique chamber cut out by the hyperplanes.

(4)

The union of
\overline{{\operatorname{Amp}(\pi^{\prime})}}
over every
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial termination
\pi^{\prime}:Y^{\prime}\rightarrow X
is
\overline{{\operatorname{Mov}(Y/X)}}
.

In particular,
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
is a relative Mori Dream Space in the sense of
[
27
]
.

Remark 4.9
.

As emphasized by Namikawa in
[
25
, p.2]
, a crepant resolution
Y\rightarrow X
is a relative Mori Dream space whenever
X
has rational Gorenstein singularities, but in the case of a conical affine symplectic singularity and a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization we have the added feature that the Mori fan is given by full hyperplanes.

Corollary 4.10
.

Partial resolutions of
X
are in bijection with faces of the Mori fan of
\overline{{\operatorname{Mov}(Y/X)}}
.

Proof.

As explained in
[
27
, 4.4]
, there is a bijection between faces of
\overline{{\operatorname{Mov}(Y/X)}}
and birational contractions
Y\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
, where
X^{\prime}
is projective over
X
. Each of these birational contractions is a contraction of some
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization of
X
, and thus by
Lemma

4.5
it is a partial resolution of
X
. Conversely, given a partial resolution
\rho:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
of
X
, it is covered by some
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization of
X
via a map
\pi^{\prime}:Y^{\prime}\rightarrow X^{\prime}
. This map is a contraction, so there is a face of
\operatorname{Nef}(Y/X)
which corresponds to
\rho:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
.

∎

Because
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
is a relative Mori Dream Space,
\operatorname{Nef}(Y/X)
is a closed polyhedral cone generated by semiample divisors. The cone of effective curves
\operatorname{NE}_{1}(Y/X)
is dual to
\operatorname{Nef}(Y/X)
, and thus it is a polyhedral cone generated by finitely many curve classes
[C_{k}]
. Thus every codimension one face
F
of
\operatorname{Nef}(Y/X)
is of the form
\operatorname{Nef}(Y/X)\cap[C_{k}]^{\perp}
for a unique extremal ray,
\langle[C_{k}]\rangle\subset\operatorname{NE}_{1}(Y/X)
. We can recover this extremal ray as the kernel of
\pi^{\prime}_{*}:\mathcal{N}_{1}(Y/X)\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{1}(X^{\prime}/X)
, where
\pi^{\prime}:Y\rightarrow X^{\prime}
is the contraction corresponding to
F
.

We follow
[
1
, Def 3.4]
with the following definition

Definition 4.11
.

Given a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization
Y\rightarrow X
, the cone of essential curves,
\operatorname{Ess}(Y/X)
is the cone generated by curve classes
[C_{i}]\in\mathcal{N}_{1}(Y/X)
of contracted curves

whose image intersects a codimension two symplectic leaf of

.

As explained in
Section

3
, given any codimension two leaf
\mathcal{L}_{i}\subset X
and
x_{i}\in\mathcal{L}_{i}
,
\pi^{-1}(x_{i})
is given by a union of projective lines
C_{k}
in the shape on the Dynkin diagram
\hat{D}_{i}
corresponding to the transverse slice to
\mathcal{L}_{i}
. Since these curve classes are all contracted by
\pi
, they also represent rays of
\operatorname{NE}_{1}(Y/X)
, and in particular they generate
\operatorname{Ess}(Y/X)\subset\operatorname{NE}_{1}(Y/X)
.

Given a simple generator
s_{k}
of
W_{X}
corresponding to a node of

, we can take a projective line
C_{k}
that maps to

and that is in the orbit of the monodromy action of
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{i},x_{i})
that corresponds to the same node of

as

. It follows from
[

, 4.4]
that two of the above projective lines
C_{k}
and
C_{l}
are in the same curve class if they map to the same symplectic leaf and they are in the same orbit of the

-action. Thus
s_{k}\mapsto[C_{k}]
is a well-defined map

\Phi:\{\text{Simple generators of }W_{X}\}\rightarrow\{\text{Generators of }\operatorname{Ess}(Y/X)\}.

This map is surjective by construction since for every projective line
C_{k}
that maps to a codimension two leaf
\mathcal{L}_{i}
, we can take the node of
D_{i}
corresponding to its orbit under the monodromy action of
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{i},x_{i})
, and the corresponding simple generator

will map to
[C_{k}]
. The fact that it is a bijection will follow from the work below.

Given a simple generator
s_{k}
of the Namikawa Weyl group
W_{X}
, let
H_{s_{k}}\subset H^{2}(Y^{\text{reg}},\mathbb{R})\cong\operatorname{Pic}(Y/X)\otimes\mathbb{R}
be the hyperplane
\Phi(s_{k})^{\perp}
.

Lemma 4.12
.

The hyperplanes
H_{s_{i}}
are in general position in
H^{2}(Y^{\text{reg}},\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

This follows from
[
5
, Prop. 2.17]
. Although in that paper it is assumed that
Y
is a symplectic resolution, the same argument applies, which we shall briefly explain below.

We will show the natural restriction map

(4)

H^{2}(Y^{\text{reg}},\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i\in I}H^{2}(\pi^{-1}(x_{i}),\mathbb{R})^{\pi_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{i},x_{i})}

is surjective. It follows from
[
24
, p. 31]
that the restriction map

is an isomorphism, where

is the union of the open and codimension two symplectic leaves of

. To show that the restriction map

H^{2}(\pi^{-1}(U),\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i\in I}H^{2}(\pi^{-1}(x_{i}),\mathbb{R})^{\pi_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{i},x_{i})}

is surjective we can apply
[

, Section 4]
, which explains that the irreducible components of the exceptional divisor of

are in bijection with the nodes of the

and that two projective lines in a fiber

Let

Remark 4.15

Lemma 4.16

The above construction does not depend on a choice of

-factorial terminalization.

Theorem 4.17

The map

5.
Deformation Theory and Prorepresentability

In this section we study the Poisson deformation theory of partial resolutions of conical affine symplectic singularities. Let
\textrm{Art}_{\mathbb{C}}
be the category of Artinian local rings with residue field
\mathbb{C}
. Given a Poisson scheme (or complex analytic space)
X
, we define the Poisson deformation 2-functor
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{D}_{X}:\textrm{Art}_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\text{Groupoids}
, where
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{D}_{X}(A)
is the groupoid where objects are flat Poisson schemes
X_{A}
over
\operatorname{Spec}(A)
such that
X_{A}\times_{\operatorname{Spec}(A)}\operatorname{Spec}(k)\cong X
and morphisms are given by isomorphisms that restrict to the identity on
X
over
\operatorname{Spec}(k)
. Finally, functors are given by base change.

If we take the underlying functor to Sets,
PD_{X}:\textrm{Art}_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\textrm{Sets}
given by
PD_{X}(A)=\textrm{Ob}(\mathcal{P}\mathcal{D}_{X}(A))/\cong
, we get the usual Poisson deformation functor, studied extensively by Namikawa in
[
22
,
24
,
23
,
25
]
. In particular Namikawa proves in these papers that for a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminalization
\pi:Y\rightarrow X
of a conical affine symplectic singularity
X
,
\operatorname{PD}_{X}
and
\operatorname{PD}_{Y}
are both prorepresentable and unobstructed. Throughout this section we shall adapt many of the techniques and arguments in those papers to partial resolutions.

5.1.
Prorepresentability

The proof of
5.2
below is very similar to that of other deformations of algebraic objects, and we assume it is known to experts. However, we include it for the benefit of the reader, since as far as we are aware it is absent from other papers on symplectic singularities. Before the proof we recall the following key lemma:

Lemma 5.1

(
[
30
,
Tag 0D2I
]
)
.

Assume we have the diagram of rings

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{B^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ {B}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A}}

with
A^{\prime}\rightarrow A
surjective and
B^{\prime}=B\times_{A}A^{\prime}
. Additionally, for a ring

, let
\textrm{Flat}_{R}
be the full subcategory of

consisting of flat modules. Then there is an equivalence of categories

\textrm{Flat}_{B^{\prime}}\rightarrow\textrm{Flat}_{B}\times_{\textrm{Flat}_{A}}\textrm{Flat}_{A^{\prime}}

given by
M\mapsto(M\otimes_{B^{\prime}}B)\times_{(M\otimes_{B^{\prime}}A)}(M\otimes_{B^{\prime}}A^{\prime})
.

Theorem 5.2
.

\mathcal{P}\mathcal{D}_{X}
satisfies the Rim-Schlessinger conditions, as defined in
[
30
,
Definition 06J2
]
.

Proof.

By
[
30
,
Lemma 06J5
]
, we have to show that given a diagram in
\textrm{Art}_{\mathbb{C}}
as in
Lemma

5.1

\mathcal{P}\mathcal{D}_{X}(B^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathcal{D}_{X}(B)\times_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{D}_{X}(A)}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{D}_{X}(A^{\prime})
is an equivalence of categories. Note that since any

is a thickening of

is a thickening of

and thus has the same underlying topological space. In particular, we can think of

as a sheaf of Poisson

Since all the data commutes with localization, this indeed forms a sheaf of Poisson

(in fact, the pushout

To show that the functor is fully faithful, let

\operatorname{Hom}(X_{1}|_{B}(U),X_{2}|_{B}(U))\times_{\operatorname{Hom}(X_{1}|_{A}(U),X_{2}|_{A}(U))}\operatorname{Hom}(X_{1}|_{A^{\prime}}(U),X_{2}|_{A^{\prime}}(U))

Lemma 5.5

Let

The profinite completion map is left adjoint to the forgetful map from profinite groups to groups and thus it is a right exact functor. In particular the induced map

6.1.

Let

\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow H_{2}(\tilde{S},\mathbb{C}),\hskip 10.0pt\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\mapsto[C_{i}]

\mathfrak{h}^{*}\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{2}(\tilde{S},\mathbb{C})\cong(H_{2}(\tilde{S},\mathbb{C}))^{*}

which have non-negative pairing with each simple coroot. We note that this map is obtained from the map in

Additionally, via the natural isomorphism

Lemma 6.4
.

The map
\pi_{*,lt}^{\prime}(\mathbb{C}[\epsilon])
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

For each
x_{i}\in\Sigma
, let
U_{i}
be a sufficiently small neighborhood of
x_{i}
in
S^{\prime}
and
\tilde{U}_{i}:=\pi^{\prime-1}(U_{i})
.

An element
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{PD}_{lt,S^{\prime}}(\mathbb{C}[\epsilon])
can be given by the data of locally trivial Poisson deformations
\mathcal{U}_{i}\in\operatorname{PD}_{lt,U_{i}}(\mathbb{C}[\epsilon])
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{0}\in\operatorname{PD}_{lt,S^{\prime}-\Sigma}(\mathbb{C}[\epsilon])\cong\operatorname{PD}_{S^{\prime}-\Sigma}(\mathbb{C}[\epsilon])
along with an identification
\Phi_{i}
of each
\mathcal{U}_{i}|_{U_{i}-x_{i}}
with
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{0}|_{U_{i}-x_{i}}
. However, the identifications
\Phi_{i}
are only defined up to automorphism of
\mathcal{U}_{i}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{0}
. In particular by normality, every automorphism of
\mathcal{U}_{i}|_{U_{i}-x_{i}}
extends to an automorphism of
\mathcal{U}_{i}
. This means that
\Phi_{i}
is the trivial gluing datum.

Since every locally trivial deformation of
U_{i}
is trivial, this is means that
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is determined by
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{0}
, and in particular an element of
\operatorname{PD}_{lt,S^{\prime}}(\mathbb{C}[\epsilon])
is given by the data of an element of
\operatorname{PD}_{S^{\prime}-\Sigma}(\mathbb{C}[\epsilon])
that restricts to the trivial deformation on each
U_{i}-x_{i}
.

Similarly, and element
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}\in\operatorname{PD}_{\tilde{S}}^{\pi^{\prime}}(\mathbb{C}[\epsilon])
is given by elements
\tilde{\mathcal{U}_{i}}\in\operatorname{PD}_{\tilde{U}_{i}}^{\pi^{\prime}}(\mathbb{C}[\epsilon])
and
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\in\operatorname{PD}_{\tilde{S}-\pi^{\prime-1}(\Sigma)}^{\pi^{\prime}}(\mathbb{C}[\epsilon])\cong\operatorname{PD}_{\tilde{S}-\pi^{\prime-1}(\Sigma)}(\mathbb{C}[\epsilon])
along with an identification
\tilde{\Phi}_{i}
of each
\tilde{\mathcal{U}}_{i}|_{\tilde{U}_{i}-\pi^{\prime-1}(x_{i})}
with
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{0}|_{\tilde{U_{i}}-\pi^{\prime-1}(x_{i})}
. Replacing each
U_{i}
with a smaller set if necessary, each
\tilde{\mathcal{U}_{i}}
must be trivial by the definition of
\operatorname{PD}_{\tilde{S}}^{\pi^{\prime}}(\mathbb{C}[\epsilon])
. Thus an element
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}\in\operatorname{PD}_{\tilde{S}}^{\pi^{\prime}}(\mathbb{C}[\epsilon])
is equivalent to the data of a deformation
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\in\operatorname{PD}_{\tilde{S}-\pi^{\prime-1}(\Sigma)}(\mathbb{C}[\epsilon])
along with a trivialization
\tilde{\Phi}_{i}
over each
\tilde{U}_{i}^{0}:=\tilde{U}_{i}-\pi^{\prime-1}(x_{i})
. As above, we wish to show that each
\tilde{\Phi}_{i}
extends to
\tilde{U}_{i}
.

Let
T:=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[\epsilon])
. By
5.4
, recall that the sheaf of Poisson automorphisms of
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}
is given by the the subsheaf of the first cohomology sheaf

of the Poisson-Lichnerowicz complex
(\wedge^{\geq 1}\Theta_{\tilde{\mathcal{S}}/T},\delta)
that vanishes over the central fiber. Since
\tilde{S}
is smooth,

. Thus we can apply the short exact sequence (

). Taking long exact sequences in cohomology associated to sections over
\tilde{U}_{i}
and

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{0}(\tilde{U}_{i},\mathbb{C}[\epsilon])}}

Lemma 6.5

We cover the singular locus
\Sigma
of

for

An element of
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{PD}_{U^{\prime}}(\mathbb{C}[\epsilon])
is given by the data of a first order Poisson deformation

of each

\Phi_{jk}
be the identification between
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}_{j}
and
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}_{k}
on

for
j<k\in J
. These identifications must be compatible on triple intersections, and furthermore they are only defined up to global automorphism of the

, for

. In particular, the gluing data is best described as the middle Čech cohomology group of the following chain complex:

(27)

\bigoplus_{j\in J}H^{0}(U^{\prime}_{j},PG_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}/T})\xrightarrow{r_{1}}\hskip-5.0pt\bigoplus_{j<k\in J}\hskip-5.0ptH^{0}(U^{\prime}_{j}\cap U^{\prime}_{k},PG_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}/T})\xrightarrow{r_{2}}\hskip-10.0pt\bigoplus_{j<k<l\in J}\hskip-5.0ptH^{0}(U^{\prime}_{j}\cap U^{\prime}_{k}\cap U^{\prime}_{l},PG_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}/T})

We shall briefly explain why every element of

extends uniquely to an element of
H^{0}(U^{\prime}_{k},PG_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}/T})
, for
k\in\overline{{J}}
. Every element of

extends uniquely to an element of
H^{0}(U^{\prime}_{k},PG_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}/T})
by normality, where

is the smooth locus of

. Then since

and

are both smooth we can apply the argument from
Lemma

6.4
using (
26
) to show that every automorphism of
U^{\prime}_{0}\cap U^{\prime}_{k}
extends uniquely to
U^{\prime\text{reg}}_{k}
. Similarly, every element of
H^{0}(U^{\prime}_{0}\cap U^{\prime}_{k}\cap U^{\prime}_{l},PG_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}/T})
extends uniquely to an element of
H^{0}(U^{\prime}_{k}\cap U^{\prime}_{l},PG_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}/T})
, for
k<l\in\overline{{J}}
. This last claim implies that any element

in (

) must be trivial in every component where

. In particular,

\ker(r_{2})\subset\bigoplus_{k\in\overline{{J}}}H^{0}(U^{\prime}_{0}\cap U^{\prime}_{k},PG_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}/T}).

Additionally, since every element of
H^{0}(U^{\prime}_{0}\cap U^{\prime}_{k},PG_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}/T})
extends uniquely to an element of
H^{0}(U^{\prime}_{k},PG_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}/T})
, for

Thus we have

and the gluing data is always trivial.

Now if

then by definition each

must be trivial for
j\in\overline{{J}}
. Thus an element of
\operatorname{PD}_{U^{\prime}}^{\rho}(\mathbb{C}[\epsilon])
is equivalent to the data of a Poisson deformation
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}_{0}
of
U^{\prime}_{0}
which is trivializable on each of the

, for
j\in\overline{{J}}
. In particular, since

maps
U^{\prime}_{0}
homeomorphically onto
U_{0}
this tells us that the map

is indeed an injection. Clearly the same argument applies to

, so we obtain a series of injections

\operatorname{PD}_{\tilde{U}}^{\pi}(\mathbb{C}[\epsilon])\xrightarrow{\pi^{\prime}_{*}}\operatorname{PD}_{U^{\prime}}^{\rho}(\mathbb{C}[\epsilon])\xrightarrow{\rho_{*}}\operatorname{PD}_{lt,U}(\mathbb{C}[\epsilon])

in

we have

so the above injections must both be isomorphisms.

Remark 8.8
.

In
[
4
]
, Borho and MacPherson study partial resolutions of the nilpotent cone. In that case the universal deformations
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
of the partial resolutions are all given by the smooth varieties
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{P}\cong G\times_{P}\mathfrak{p}
, for different parabolic subgroups
P\subset G
of a reductive lie group
G
.

The above result is false in general because there are many examples of symplectic singularities with nontrivial symplectic resolutions that have trivial Namikawa Weyl group. For example the symplectic resolution
T^{*}\mathbb{P}^{2}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{O}_{\text{subreg}}}\subset\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{sl}_{3}}
has trivial Weyl group, but the fiber at zero is the zero section. However, in the cases where the corollary applies, it shows a strong relationship between the geometry and the combinatorics of a given partial resolution.

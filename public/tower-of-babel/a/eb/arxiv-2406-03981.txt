Quadrature error estimates on non–matching grids in a fictitious domain framework for fluid–structure interaction problems
Daniele Boffi, Fabio Credali, Lucia Gastaldi

Abstract
. We consider a fictitious domain formulation for fluid-structure interaction problems based on a distributed Lagrange multiplier to couple the fluid and solid behaviors. How to deal with the coupling term is crucial since the construction of the associated finite element matrix requires the integration of functions defined over non-matching grids: the exact computation can be performed by intersecting the involved meshes, whereas an approximate coupling matrix can be evaluated on the original meshes by introducing a quadrature error. The purpose of this paper is twofold: we prove that the discrete problem is well-posed also when the coupling term is constructed in approximate way and we discuss quadrature error estimates over non-matching grids. Keywords: Fluid-structure interactions, Fictitious domain, Non-matching grids, Quadrature error estimates.

1.
Introduction

A wide range of physical phenomena generating interest within the scientific community can be classified as fluid-structure interaction problems. Several applications can be found, for instance, in biology and medicine, as well as in structural engineering. Due to the complexity of the equations governing this kind of systems, which usually involve nonlinear terms and complex geometries, several mathematical models have been developed during the last decades.

Mathematical approaches to fluid-structure interaction problems are
usually divided into two categories. In
boundary fitted approaches
,
fluid and solid are discretized by two meshes sharing the interface. The solid
evolution is usually described in Lagrangian framework, whereas the fluid grid
evolves and deforms around the solid one during the simulation. The Arbitrary
Lagrangian Eulerian (ALE)
formulation
[
29
,
22
,
30
]

belongs to this category. The family of
boundary unfitted approaches
,
on the other hand, considers meshes which are not fitting with the interface.
Several approaches fall in this family: we mention, for instance, the level set formulation
[
20
]
, the Nitsche–XFEM method
[
1
,
19
]
, the fictitious domain approach introduced in
[
26
,
27
]
, the CutFEM method
[
18
]
, the shifted boundary method
[
5
]
and the immersed boundary-conformal isogeometric method
[
38
]
.
In our case, the fluid and solid are discretized by two completely independent
meshes and then the solid discretization is in some way superimposed to the
fluid one. We will be more precise in the description of our model.
In case of
fitted
approaches, the kinematic constraints combining fluid dynamics and solid evolution are automatically satisfied thanks to the construction of the method itself, but mesh distortion may cause stability issues. On the other hand,
unfitted
approaches overcome the presence of distorted meshes, but they require additional effort to impose kinematic constraints. This can be done, for instance, by introducing Lagrange multipliers or stabilization terms on the mesh elements close to the interface.

In this paper, we focus on the approach introduced in
[
11
]
.
This method is an evolution of Peskin’s immersed boundary
method
[
35
,
36
]
where, first, finite element are
used for the approximation of the partial differential
equations
[
14
]
and, then, a
Lagrange multiplier formulation is considered in the spirit of the
fictitious
domain method.
Fluid and solid domain are discretized by two independent meshes. More
precisely, evolution and deformation of the immersed solid body are studied by
Lagrangian description: the equations are defined on a fixed reference domain,
which is mapped, at each time step, into the actual position of the solid. The
fluid dynamics is described in Eulerian coordinates on a fixed mesh, which is
extended to the region occupied by the structure.

A distributed Lagrange multiplier and a suitable bilinear form are
used to impose the kinematic constraint at variational level: this condition relates the solid material velocity, defined on the fictitiously extended fluid domain, with the Lagrangian map, defined on the solid reference domain. The additional term appearing in the variational formulation is also known as
coupling term
. In the finite element framework, the associated coupling (or interface) matrix is obtained by integrating the product of solid and fluid basis functions over the solid elements.
In particular, the fluid basis functions are composed with the solid
mapping at the previous time step. This poses the issue of integrating functions defined on two different non-matching grids: the accuracy of this procedure not only depends on the precision of the considered quadrature rule, but also on the algorithm we employ to compute the reciprocal position between the two meshes.
Several works have been focused on similar aspects: among others, we quote, for example, the interpolation technique between unfitted grids discussed in
[
25
]
, the efficient three dimensional CutFEM approach presented in
[
34
]
, the variational transfer method discussed and implemented in
[
31
]
and, finally, in the case of codimension one interface problems, the mortar element approach introduced in
[
33
]
.

In
[
13
]
, we presented and discussed two possible integration techniques that can be adopted to assemble the finite element coupling matrix. The coupling term can be assembled
exactly
if we compute the intersection between the fluid mesh and the mapped solid one and use a quadrature formula which is exact for the polynomials involved. With this procedure, we construct a finer mesh for the solid domain, which is used just to implement a composite quadrature rule able to take fully into account the interaction between fluid and solid elements. Since this algorithm may be computationally demanding (the coupling term is assembled at each time step), we discuss in this paper an
inexact
approach by integrating directly over the solid elements.
In this case, the fluid elements interacting with the solid are determined by the position of the mapped quadrature nodes, so that an additional source of error is introduced. The purpose of this paper is twofold: we prove the well-posedness of the discrete problem when the coupling term is constructed in approximate way and we provide estimates for the quadrature error. Our theoretical results are confirmed also at numerical level.

The paper is organized as follows. After recalling the functional analysis notation in Section
2
, we discuss the problem setting in Section
3
. We then summarize in Section
4
the existing theoretical results concerning the well-posedness of both the continuous and the discrete problem. In Section
5
, we discuss the assembly techniques for the coupling term and, in Section
6
, we prove a general theorem showing the effect of numerical integration. Quadrature error estimates for the coupling term are then presented and proved in Section
7
, while in Section
8
we discuss the well-posedness of the discrete problem in case of approximate coupling. In the last Section
9
, we report some numerical tests confirming our theoretical results.

), respectively)
, then the following error estimate holds true

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\Omega}+\left\lVert p-p_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\mathcal{B}}+\left\lVert\bm{\lambda}-\bm{\lambda}_{h}\right\rVert_{{\bm{\Lambda}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\inf_{\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}}\left\lVert\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\Omega}+\inf_{q_{h}\in Q_{h}}\left\lVert p-q_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\inf_{\mathbf{Y}_{h}\in\mathbf{S}_{h}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{Y}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\mathcal{B}}+\inf_{{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\in{\bm{\Lambda}}_{h}}\left\lVert\bm{\lambda}-{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\right\rVert_{{\bm{\Lambda}}}\big).

In contrast with popular unfitted methods, which can achieve higher
order convergence when the solution is piecewise smooth, our method
converges with a rate depending on the global regularity of the
solution.
In the wide literature of unfitted finite elements, each method has
advantages and disadvantages. We refer the interested reader to
[
9
]

for a comparison of our method and other popular unfitted schemes, such
as the CutFEM method, in the case of interface problems.

5.
Computational aspects

In this section, we briefly describe some computational aspects regarding the discrete problem in (
17
), with particular focus to the coupling term. We now restrict our discussion to
d=2
with triangular meshes for both solid and fluid domain.

First, let us write the problem in matrix form

(21)

\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}ccc|c@{}}\mathsf{A}_{f}&0&\mathsf{C}_{f}^{\top}&\mathsf{B}^{\top}\\
0&\mathsf{A}_{s}&-\mathsf{C}_{s}^{\top}&0\\
\mathsf{C}_{f}&-\mathsf{C}_{s}&0&0\\
\hline\cr\mathsf{B}&0&0&0\\
\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{u}_{h}\\
\mathbf{X}_{h}\\
\bm{\lambda}_{h}\\
\hline\cr p_{h}\\
\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{f}\\
\mathbf{g}\\
\mathbf{d}\\
\hline\cr\mathbf{0}\end{array}\right].

Notice that
\mathsf{A}_{f}
,
\mathsf{B}
act only on
\mathbf{u}_{h}
and
p_{h}
, which
are defined on the fluid mesh
\mathcal{T}^{\Omega}_{h}
, while
\mathsf{A}_{s}
and
\mathsf{C}_{s}
act only on the solid variables
\mathbf{X}_{h}
and
\bm{\lambda}_{h}
defined on
\mathcal{T}^{\mathcal{B}}_{h}
. All these matrices can be assembled in exact way provided that a sufficiently precise quadrature rule is employed. This fact is not anymore true if we look at
\mathsf{C}_{f}
, which aims at coupling the behavior of fluid and solid in the fictitious part of the fluid domain.
Possible techniques that can be adopted for this procedure have been
presented and discussed with several numerical tests
in
[
13
]
. In this section we only recall the main features
of this computational procedure and, in the next sections, we investigate the
quadrature error committed when the construction is not exact.

The interface matrix originates from the form

\mathbf{c}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))
, where we have to compute integrals on
\mathcal{B}
involving
{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\in{\bm{\Lambda}}_{h}
and
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
, that are discrete functions defined on two different meshes. Notice that the velocity-like function
\mathbf{v}_{h}
is composed with the map
\overline{\mathbf{X}}
to take into account the actual position of the solid body. To fix the ideas, we report in Figure
2
a simple example of a solid triangle immersed into the fluid mesh through
\overline{\mathbf{X}}
: the mismatch between the supports of fluid and mapped solid basis functions is evident.

(a)

(b)

Figure 2.
On the left, mapping of a solid element into the fluid mesh. On the right, the yellow support of the fluid basis function associated with the starred node only partially matches the mapped solid triangle.

In the following, we denote by
\{(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0},\omega_{k}^{0})\}_{k=1}^{K_{0}}
nodes and weights for a generic quadrature rule for
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{B})
inner product of functions, while, for the product of gradients, we use a rule with nodes and weights denoted by
\{(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{1},\omega_{k}^{1})\}_{k=1}^{K_{1}}
. Both formulas are defined on a generic triangle
\mathrm{T}\in\mathcal{T}^{\mathcal{B}}_{h}
.

The discrete counterparts of the two possible choices for the coupling term are the following:

(22a)

\mathbf{c}_{0}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))=\sum_{\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{s}}\in\mathcal{T}^{\mathcal{B}}_{h}}\int_{\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{s}}}{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s},

(22b)

\mathbf{c}_{1}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))=\sum_{\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{s}}\in\mathcal{T}^{\mathcal{B}}_{h}}\int_{\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{s}}}\left({\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})+\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}:\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}.

The exact computation of these terms can be carried out by making use
of a composite quadrature rule, taking into account that
\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})
is a piecewise polynomial in each
\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{s}}\in\mathcal{T}^{\mathcal{B}}_{h}
. To this aim, one should compute the intersection between the fluid and the mapped solid mesh.

Otherwise, we can proceed in an approximate way directly using a quadrature formula, without paying attention to the fact that
\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})
is a piecewise polynomial in each
\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{s}}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathcal{B}}
. We have for (
22
)

(23a)

\mathbf{c}_{0,h}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))=\sum_{\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{s}}\in\mathcal{T}^{\mathcal{B}}_{h}}\left\lvert\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{s}}\right\rvert\sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}\omega_{k}^{0}\,{\bm{\mu}}_{h}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0})\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0})),

(23b)

\displaystyle\mathbf{c}_{1,h}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{s}}\in\mathcal{T}^{\mathcal{B}}_{h}}\left\lvert\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{s}}\right\rvert\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}\omega_{k}^{0}\,{\bm{\mu}}_{h}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0})\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0}))+\sum_{k=1}^{K_{1}}\omega_{k}^{1}\,\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{1}):\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{1}))\right).

In the following, the notation
\mathbf{c}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is used for both cases, as in the continuous setting. It is clear that the approach in (
23
) produces a quadrature error, which is the subject of our study.

In this section, following
[

{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\in{\bm{\Lambda}}_{h}

Problem 3
.

Let
\overline{\mathbf{X}}\in\mathbf{W}^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{B})

\mathbf{f}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
,
\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{B})

and
\bm{\lambda}_{h}^{\star}\in{\bm{\Lambda}}_{h}

(24a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}_{f}(\mathbf{u}_{h}^{\star},\mathbf{v}_{h})-(\div\mathbf{v}_{h},p_{h}^{\star})_{\Omega}+\mathbf{c}_{h}(\bm{\lambda}_{h}^{\star},\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))=(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}_{h})_{h,\Omega}

\displaystyle\forall\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}

(24b)

\displaystyle(\div\mathbf{u}_{h}^{\star},q_{h})_{\Omega}=0

\displaystyle\forall q_{h}\in Q_{h}

(24c)

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}_{s}(\mathbf{X}_{h}^{\star},\mathbf{Y}_{h})-\mathbf{c}(\bm{\lambda}_{h}^{\star},\mathbf{Y}_{h})=(\mathbf{g},\mathbf{Y}_{h})_{h,\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle\forall\mathbf{Y}_{h}\in\mathbf{S}_{h}

Similarly to the continuous and discrete case, we now introduce a new bilinear form
{\mathbb{A}_{h}:\mathbb{V}_{h}\times\mathbb{V}_{h}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}}

(25)

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{h}(\mathcal{U}_{h},\mathcal{V}_{h})=\mathbf{a}_{f}(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h})+\mathbf{a}_{s}(\mathbf{X}_{h},\mathbf{Y}_{h})

\displaystyle+\mathbf{c}_{h}(\bm{\lambda}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))-\mathbf{c}(\bm{\lambda}_{h},\mathbf{Y}_{h})-\mathbf{c}_{h}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))+\mathbf{c}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{X}_{h})

for all
\mathcal{U}_{h},\,\mathcal{V}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
. We do not need to introduce
\mathbb{B}_{h}
since
\mathbb{B}
can be computed exactly.

Replacing
\mathbb{A}
with
\mathbb{A}_{h}

(26)

\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}ccc|c@{}}\mathsf{A}_{f}&0&\mathsf{C}_{f,h}^{\top}&\mathsf{B}^{\top}\\
0&\mathsf{A}_{s}&-\mathsf{C}_{s}^{\top}&0\\
\mathsf{C}_{f,h}&-\mathsf{C}_{s}&0&0\\
\hline\cr\mathsf{B}&0&0&0\\
\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{u}_{h}^{\star}\\
\mathbf{X}_{h}^{\star}\\
\bm{\lambda}_{h}^{\star}\\
\hline\cr p_{h}^{\star}\\
\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{f}_{h}\\
\mathbf{g}_{h}\\
\mathbf{d}_{h}\\
\hline\cr\mathbf{0}\end{array}\right],

where
\mathsf{C}_{f,h}
,
\mathbf{f}_{h}
,
\mathbf{g}_{h}

Moreover, we define the operator
\mathcal{L}_{h}^{\star}:\mathbb{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}\rightarrow\mathbb{V}_{h}^{\prime}\times Q_{h}^{\prime}

(27)

\langle\mathcal{L}_{h}^{\star}(\mathcal{U}_{h},p_{h}),(\mathcal{V}_{h},q_{h})\rangle=\mathbb{A}_{h}(\mathcal{U}_{h},\mathcal{V}_{h})+\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{V}_{h},p_{h})+\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{U}_{h},q_{h})\quad\forall\mathcal{V}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h},\forall q_{h}\in Q_{h}

in such a way that
(\mathcal{U}_{h}^{\star},p_{h}^{\star})

3

{\mathcal{L}_{h}^{\star}(\mathcal{U}_{h}^{\star},p_{h}^{\star})=(\mathbf{f}_{h},\mathbf{g}_{h},\mathbf{d}_{h},\mathbf{0})}

Our goal is to measure the distance between the approximated solution
(\mathbf{u}_{h}^{\star},p_{h}^{\star},\mathbf{X}_{h}^{\star},\bm{\lambda}_{h}^{\star})
and the continuous one
(\mathbf{u},p,\mathbf{X},\bm{\lambda})

The error analysis relies again on inf-sup conditions similar to (
19
). Since the bilinear form
\mathbb{B}
is computed exactly, we assume that the discrete form
\mathbb{A}_{h}
satisfied the inf-sup condition; the proof is postponed to Section

Assumption 1
.

\mathbb{A}_{h}
satisfies the inf-sup condition, that is there exists a positive constant
\theta^{\star}
independent of h such that

(28)

\inf_{\mathcal{U}_{h}\in\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{B},h}}\sup_{\mathcal{V}_{h}\in\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{B},h}}\frac{\mathbb{A}_{h}(\mathcal{U}_{h},\mathcal{V}_{h})}{\left\vvvert\mathcal{U}_{h}\right\vvvert\left\vvvert\mathcal{V}_{h}\right\vvvert}\geq\theta^{\star},

where
\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{B},h}
was defined in (
20
).

Thanks to the results in
[
39
]
, Assumption
1
and the first inf-sup condition in (
19
)
[
17
]
imply the following inf-sup condition
[

(29)

(\left\vvvert\mathcal{W}_{h}\right\vvvert^{2}+\left\lVert r_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}^{2})^{1/2}\leq M\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{V}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}\\
q_{h}\in Q_{h}\end{subarray}}\frac{\langle\mathcal{L}_{h}^{\star}(\mathcal{W}_{h},r_{h}),(\mathcal{V}_{h},q_{h})\rangle}{(\left\vvvert\mathcal{V}_{h}\right\vvvert^{2}+\left\lVert q_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega})^{1/2}}

for
\mathcal{W}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
and

, with the constant

\tilde{\eta}
and

and
\mathbb{V}_{h}^{\prime}\times Q_{h}^{\prime}
.

Exploiting that

is solution to the discrete Problem
2

(30)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}_{h}^{\star}(\mathcal{U}_{h}-\mathcal{U}_{h}^{\star},

\displaystyle p_{h}-p_{h}^{\star}),(\mathcal{V}_{h},q_{h})\rangle=\langle(\mathcal{L}_{h}^{\star}-\mathcal{L}_{h})(\mathcal{U}_{h},p_{h}),(\mathcal{V}_{h},q_{h})\rangle

\displaystyle+[(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}_{h})_{\Omega}-(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}_{h})_{h,\Omega}]+[(\mathbf{g},\mathbf{Y}_{h})_{\mathcal{B}}-(\mathbf{g},\mathbf{Y}_{h})_{h,\mathcal{B}}]+[\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{d},{\bm{\mu}}_{h})-\mathbf{c}_{h}(\mathbf{d},{\bm{\mu}}_{h})].

The definitions of

and

\displaystyle\langle(\mathcal{L}_{h}^{\star}-\mathcal{L}_{h})(\mathcal{U}_{h},p_{h}),(\mathcal{V}_{h},q_{h})\rangle

Therefore, we obtain

(32)

\displaystyle\left(\left\vvvert\mathcal{U}_{h}-\mathcal{U}_{h}^{\star}\right\vvvert^{2}+\left\lVert p_{h}-p_{h}^{\star}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq M\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{V}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}\\
q_{h}\in Q_{h}\end{subarray}}\biggl(\frac{|\mathbf{c}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))-\mathbf{c}_{h}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})|+|\mathbf{c}(\bm{\lambda}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))-\mathbf{c}_{h}(\bm{\lambda}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))|}{(\left\vvvert\mathcal{V}_{h}\right\vvvert^{2}+\left\lVert q_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{0,\Omega})^{1/2}}

Before presenting the final estimate, we introduce the following assumption which will be then proved in the next section.

Assumption 2
.

There exist positive functions
\rho_{\ell}(h)
,
\ell=0,1
, tending to zero as
h
goes to zero, such that for all
{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\in{\bm{\Lambda}}_{h}
and
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
it holds

|\mathbf{c}_{\ell}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))-\mathbf{c}_{\ell,h}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})|\leq C\rho_{\ell}(h)\,\|{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\|_{\ell,\mathcal{B}}\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}

with
\rho_{\ell}(h)
depending on the definition of
\mathbf{c}
.

It turns out that if
\mathbf{f}
,
\mathbf{g}
, and
\mathbf{d}
are sufficiently regular, then the second term on the right hand side of (
32
) can be estimated by the classical theory provided that the quadrature rule is appropriately chosen (see, for instance,
[
21
]
).

Lemma 1
.

Let
s>d/2
be an integer number. Let
\mathbf{f}\in\mathbf{H}^{s}(\Omega)
,

\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{H}^{s}(\mathcal{B})
and consider a quadrature rule exact for polynomials
of degree
2s-2
. Then it holds

(33)

\displaystyle\left\lvert(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}_{h})_{\Omega}-(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{v}_{h})_{h,\Omega}\right\rvert\leq Ch_{\Omega}^{s}\,|\mathbf{f}|_{s,\Omega}\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}

\displaystyle\forall\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}

\displaystyle\left\lvert(\mathbf{g},\mathbf{Y}_{h})_{\mathcal{B}}-(\mathbf{g},\mathbf{Y}_{h})_{h,\mathcal{B}}\right\rvert\leq Ch_{\mathcal{B}}^{s}\,|\mathbf{g}|_{s,\mathcal{B}}\|\mathbf{Y}_{h}\|_{1,\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle\forall\mathbf{Y}_{h}\in\mathbf{S}_{h}.

Moreover, given
\mathbf{d}\in\mathbf{H}^{r}(\mathcal{B})
with
r>d/2+\ell
,

{\bm{\mu}}_{h}
piecewise linear function in
\mathcal{B}
, and a quadrature rule exact for
polynomials of degree
2
, the following bound holds

(34)

|\mathbf{c}_{\ell}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{d})-\mathbf{c}_{\ell,h}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{d})|\leq Ch_{\mathcal{B}}^{r-\ell}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}|\mathbf{d}|_{r,\mathcal{B}}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\|_{\ell,\mathcal{B}}\qquad\forall{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\in{\bm{\Lambda}}_{h}.

Proof.

The estimates (
33
) are standard results, see
[
21
, Chap. 4, Sect. 4.1]
. Concerning (
34
), we distinguish the cases
\ell=0,1
.

If
\ell=0
,
\mathbf{c}_{0}(\cdot,\cdot)=(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathcal{B}}
and
\mathbf{c}_{0,h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the corresponding approximation with a quadrature rule exact for polynomials of degree
2
. For all
{\bm{\mu}}_{h}
, we have

\mathbf{c}_{0}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{d})-\mathbf{c}_{0,h}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{d})=\sum_{\mathrm{T}\in\mathcal{T}^{\mathcal{B}}_{h}}\left(\int_{\mathrm{T}}{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{d}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}-|\mathrm{T}|\sum_{k=1}^{K}\omega_{k}{\bm{\mu}}_{h}(\mathbf{p}_{k})\cdot\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{p}_{k})\right).

We estimate the contribution of each element
\mathrm{T}
as follows. Let
\mathbf{d}_{I}
be the interpolant of
\mathbf{d}
; taking into account the degree of exactness of the quadrature rule, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{T}}{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{d}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}-|\mathrm{T}|\sum_{k=1}^{K}\omega_{k}{\bm{\mu}}_{h}(\mathbf{p}_{k})\cdot\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{p}_{k})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{T}}{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot(\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{d}_{I})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}+|\mathrm{T}|\sum_{k=1}^{K}\omega_{k}{\bm{\mu}}_{h}(\mathbf{p}_{k})\cdot(\mathbf{d}_{I}(\mathbf{p}_{k})-\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{p}_{k}))

\displaystyle\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\displaystyle\leq Ch_{\mathcal{B}}^{r}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\|_{0,\mathrm{T}}|\mathbf{d}|_{r,\mathrm{T}}+\left(|\mathrm{T}|\sum_{k=1}^{K}\omega_{k}|{\bm{\mu}}_{h}(\mathbf{p}_{k})|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(|\mathrm{T}|\sum_{k=1}^{K}\omega_{k}|\mathbf{d}_{I}(\mathbf{p}_{k})-\mathbf{d}(\mathbf{p}_{k})|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\displaystyle\leq Ch_{\mathcal{B}}^{r}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\|_{0,\mathrm{T}}|\mathbf{d}|_{r,\mathrm{T}}+|\mathrm{T}|^{1/2}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\|_{0,\mathrm{T}}\|\mathbf{d}_{I}-\mathbf{d}\|_{\infty,\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\displaystyle\leq Ch_{\mathcal{B}}^{r}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\|_{0,\mathrm{T}}|\mathbf{d}|_{r,\mathrm{T}}+C|\mathrm{T}|^{1/2}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\|_{0,\mathrm{T}}|\mathrm{T}|^{-1/2}h_{\mathcal{B}}^{r}|\mathbf{d}|_{r,\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\displaystyle\leq Ch_{\mathcal{B}}^{r}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\|_{0,\mathrm{T}}|\mathbf{d}|_{r,\mathrm{T}}.

The last inequality has been obtained using standard interpolation estimates,
the discrete Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, and the precision of the
quadrature rule. This implies (
34
) for
\ell=0
.

For
\ell=1
,
\mathbf{c}_{1}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{d})=({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{d})_{\mathcal{B}}+(\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h},\grads\mathbf{d})_{\mathcal{B}}
and
\mathbf{c}_{1,h}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{d})
is its approximation by means of the same quadrature rule. Hence by using the same argument as before, we arrive at (
34
) for
\ell=1
.
∎

Hence, the final result reads as follows.

Theorem 2
.

Under Assumption
1
and
Assumption
2
, if
(\mathbf{u},p,\mathbf{X},\bm{\lambda})
is the solution to
Problem
1
,
(\mathbf{u}_{h},p_{h},\mathbf{X}_{h},\bm{\lambda}_{h})
is the solution to
Problem
2
,
and

(\mathbf{u}_{h}^{\star},p_{h}^{\star},\mathbf{X}_{h}^{\star},\bm{\lambda}_{h}^{\star})
is the solution to
Problem

, then the following error estimate
holds for
\ell=0,1

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}^{\star}\right\rVert_{1,\Omega}+\left\lVert p-p_{h}^{\star}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{h}^{\star}\right\rVert_{1,\mathcal{B}}+\left\lVert\bm{\lambda}-\bm{\lambda}_{h}^{\star}\right\rVert_{{\bm{\Lambda}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\big(\inf_{\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}}\left\lVert\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\Omega}+\inf_{q_{h}\in Q_{h}}\left\lVert p-q_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\inf_{\mathbf{Y}_{h}\in\mathbf{S}_{h}}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{Y}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\mathcal{B}}+\inf_{{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\in{\bm{\Lambda}}_{h}}\left\lVert\bm{\lambda}-{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\right\rVert_{{\bm{\Lambda}}}

\displaystyle+(h_{\mathcal{B}}^{min})^{\ell-1}\rho_{\ell}(h)\|\mathbf{u}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}+\rho_{\ell}(h)\,\|\bm{\lambda}_{h}\|_{\ell,\mathcal{B}}+h_{\Omega}^{s}\,|\mathbf{f}|_{s,\Omega}+h_{\mathcal{B}}^{s}\,|\mathbf{g}|_{s,\mathcal{B}}+\,h_{\mathcal{B}}^{r-\ell}\|\mathbf{d}\|_{r,\mathcal{B}}\big).

7.
Quadrature error for the coupling term

The goal of this section is to estimate the quadrature error occurring
between the exact bilinear form
\mathbf{c}
and its numerical counterpart
\mathbf{c}_{h}

computed with approximate integration. More precisely, we want to check
Assumption
2
and determine
\rho_{\ell}(h)
in terms of
h_{\Omega}
and
h_{\mathcal{B}}
.

From now on, we consider the case
d=2
with triangular
meshes and we use the Bercovier–Pironneau element for the Stokes part of the
system and continuous piecewise linear elements for both the solid unknown and the Lagrange multiplier (see (
14
)).

We introduce the following notion of quadrature error functional over a
generic element
\mathrm{T}

[
21
]
.

Definition 1
.

Given a generic function
f
and a quadrature rule with nodes and weights
\{(\mathbf{p}_{k},\omega_{k})\}_{k=1}^{K}
, the quadrature error functional
E_{\mathrm{T}}
over
\mathrm{T}
is defined as the difference between the exact integral and the numerical one, i.e.

(35)

E_{\mathrm{T}}(f)=\int_{\mathrm{T}}f(\mathbf{x})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}-\left\lvert\mathrm{T}\right\rvert\sum_{k=1}^{K}\omega_{k}f(\mathbf{p}_{k}).

The analysis on the quadrature error we are going to perform will strongly depend on the function
\overline{\mathbf{X}}
. Therefore, we introduce the following assumption, which is reasonable for the FSI application.

Assumption 3
.

We assume that
\overline{\mathbf{X}}\in\mathbf{S}_{h}
. This implies that it is linear in
\mathrm{T}
for all
\mathrm{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{B}
. Consequently, the composed function

\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})
is continuous and piecewise linear on

\mathrm{T}
.

The next lemma states a fundamental inequality for continuous and piecewise linear functions
\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})
that will be used later on.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\mathrm{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathcal{B}}
be such that
\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\mathrm{T})
is not included in an element of

\mathcal{T}_{h/2}^{\Omega}
and let Assumption
3
hold true. Then
\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})\in\mathbf{H}^{1+s}(\mathrm{T})
for
0\leq s<1/2
and

\|\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})\|_{s,\mathrm{T}}\leq\frac{C}{1-2s}\|\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})\|_{0,\mathrm{T}}.

Proof.

Let
{\varphi}
be one of the components of
\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}
. Since
\mathrm{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathcal{B}}
is such
that
\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\mathrm{T})
is not included in an element of
\mathcal{T}_{h/2}^{\Omega}
, we can
subdivide
\mathrm{T}
in polygons
P_{j}
with
j=1,\dots,J
so that

T=\bigcup_{j=1}^{J}P_{j}
and
{\varphi}
is constant on each
P_{j}
. We set
{\varphi}_{j}={\varphi}|_{P_{j}}
. Since,

{H^{1}(P_{j})\subset H^{s}(P_{j})}
, for
0\leq s<1/2
, we have that the extension by
zero
\tilde{\varphi}_{j}
of
{\varphi}_{j}\in H^{s}(P_{j})
belongs to
H^{s}(\mathrm{T})
with the
following bound, see
[
32
, Chap. 1, Th. 11.4]
and
[
24
, Sect. 3.1]

\|\tilde{\varphi}_{j}\|_{s,\mathrm{T}}\leq\frac{C}{1-2s}\|{\varphi}_{j}\|_{s,P_{j}}.

Then
{\varphi}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\tilde{\varphi}_{j}
belongs to
H^{s}(\mathrm{T})
and since

{\varphi}_{j}
is constant for
j=1,\dots,J
, we have

\|{\varphi}\|_{s,\mathrm{T}}\leq\frac{C}{1-2s}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|{\varphi}_{j}\|^{2}_{s,P_{j}}\right)^{1/2}=\frac{C}{1-2s}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J}\|{\varphi}_{j}\|^{2}_{0,P_{j}}\right)^{1/2}.

Hence we have proved that
\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}^{s}(\mathrm{T})
and

\|\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{s,\mathrm{T}}\leq\frac{C}{1-2s}\|\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{0,\mathrm{T}}.

∎

If
\widehat{T}
is a reference element corresponding to
T\in\mathcal{T}
, we
denote by
\widehat{\bullet}
quantities defined on
\widehat{T}
associated with

\bullet
.

We have now all the tools we need to prove the error estimates for the two choices of
\mathbf{c}
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\mathbf{V}_{h}
and
{\bm{\Lambda}}_{h}
be given by (
14
). Let us assume that
\overline{\mathbf{X}}
satisfies Assumption
3
.
Given a quadrature rule
\{(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0},\omega_{k}^{0})\}_{k=1}^{K_{0}}
exact for quadratic polynomials, i.e. such that

(36)

\widehat{E}(\widehat{f})=0\quad\forall\widehat{f}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\widehat{\mathrm{T}}),

the following estimate holds true

(37)

\left\lvert E_{\mathcal{B}}({\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))\right\rvert\leq Ch_{\mathcal{B}}^{3/2}|\log h_{\mathcal{B}}^{min}|\left\lVert{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\mathcal{B}}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left\lvert\mathbf{v}_{h}\right\rvert_{1,\Omega}\quad\forall{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\in{\bm{\Lambda}}_{h},\forall\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h},

where
E_{\mathcal{B}}
is the sum of
E_{\mathrm{T}}({\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))
for all
\mathrm{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathcal{B}}
and
h_{\mathcal{B}}^{min}=\min_{\mathrm{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathcal{B}}}h_{\mathrm{T}}
.

Proof.

We start proving a local estimate in a single element
\mathrm{T}
of the solid mesh

\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathcal{B}}
: we notice that, thanks to (
36
), if

\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\mathrm{T})
is included in
an element of the velocity mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h/2}^{\Omega}
, then the error is zero. For this reason, we consider the
following two subsets of
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathcal{B}}

(38)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{h,1}^{\mathcal{B}}=\{\mathrm{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathcal{B}}:\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\mathrm{T})\text{ is included in an element of }\mathcal{T}_{h/2}^{\Omega}\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{h,2}^{\mathcal{B}}=\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathcal{B}}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{h,1}^{\mathcal{B}}.

Hence,
\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})
is a piecewise linear
polynomial on
\mathrm{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h,2}^{\mathcal{B}}
.
Our aim is to find a bound for

(39)

E_{\mathrm{T}}({\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))=\int_{\mathrm{T}}{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}-\left\lvert\mathrm{T}\right\rvert\sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}\omega_{k}^{0}{\bm{\mu}}_{h}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0})\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0}))

where
{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\in[\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathrm{T})]^{2}
and
\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})
is a continuous piecewise linear
polynomial on
\mathrm{T}
.

Let us now introduce the linear interpolant
\mathbf{v}_{I}\in[\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathrm{T})]^{2}
of

\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})
, so that with simple manipulations we can write

(40)

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{T}}({\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{T}}{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot\big(\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})-\mathbf{v}_{I}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}+\int_{\mathrm{T}}{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{I}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}-\left\lvert\mathrm{T}\right\rvert\sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}\omega_{k}^{0}{\bm{\mu}}_{h}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0})\cdot\mathbf{v}_{I}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0})

\displaystyle\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\displaystyle-\left\lvert\mathrm{T}\right\rvert\sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}\omega_{k}^{0}{\bm{\mu}}_{h}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0})\cdot\big(\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0}))-\mathbf{v}_{I}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0})\big)

and we can study each term separately.

The first term can be handled applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,
a classical interpolation result, and Lemma
2
. Therefore, for
0\leq s<1/2
, we have

(41)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{\mathrm{T}}{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot\big(\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})-\mathbf{v}_{I}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\mathrm{T}}\left\lVert\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})-\mathbf{v}_{I}\right\rVert_{0,\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle\leq h_{\mathrm{T}}^{1+s}\left\lVert{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\mathrm{T}}|\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})|_{1+s,\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{h_{\mathrm{T}}^{1+s}}{1-2s}\left\lVert{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\mathrm{T}}|\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})|_{1,\mathrm{T}}.

The second term is the quadrature error of the product
{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{I}
which
can be estimated as

(42)

E_{\mathrm{T}}({\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{I})=\int_{\mathrm{T}}{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{I}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}-\left\lvert\mathrm{T}\right\rvert\sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}\omega_{k}^{0}{\bm{\mu}}_{h}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0})\cdot\mathbf{v}_{I}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0})=0

from the construction of
\mathbf{v}_{I}
and the choice of quadrature rule.

Now, in order to estimate the last term, we use Cauchy–Schwarz
inequality and the exactness of the quadrature rule

(43)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\lvert\mathrm{T}\right\rvert\sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}\omega_{k}^{0}{\bm{\mu}}_{h}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0})\cdot\big(\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0}))-\mathbf{v}_{I}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0})\big)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\left\lvert\mathrm{T}\right\rvert\sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}\omega_{k}^{0}\,{\bm{\mu}}_{h}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0})^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\,\bigg(\left\lvert\mathrm{T}\right\rvert\sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}\omega_{k}^{0}\,\big(\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0}))-\mathbf{v}_{I}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0})\big)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int_{\mathrm{T}}\left\lvert{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\right\rvert^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\,\bigg(\left\lvert\mathrm{T}\right\rvert\sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}\omega_{k}^{0}\,\big(\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0}))-\mathbf{v}_{I}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0})\big)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}\left\lvert\mathrm{T}\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\mathrm{T}}\left\lVert\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})-\mathbf{v}_{I}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathrm{T}}

The estimate for
\left\lVert\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})-\mathbf{v}_{I}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathrm{T}}
is not trivial.
Since
\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})
is piecewise linear in
\mathrm{T}
, we have from
Lemma
2
, that

\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})\in\mathbf{H}^{1+s}(\mathrm{T})
with
0\leq s<1/2
.

As mentioned before, using standard arguments for finite elements (see,
[
21
]
),

\mathrm{T}
is affine equivalent to a reference element
\widehat{\mathrm{T}}
and thanks to the
inclusion
H^{1+s}(\widehat{\mathrm{T}})\subset L^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathrm{T}})
, we can
write

(44)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})-\mathbf{v}_{I}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\widehat{\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})}-\widehat{\mathbf{v}_{I}}\right\rVert_{\infty,\widehat{\mathrm{T}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{I}-\widehat{\Pi}\right\rVert_{\mathscr{L}(H^{1+s}(\widehat{\mathrm{T}}),L^{\infty}(\widehat{\mathrm{T}}))}\,\inf_{\widehat{\mathbf{q}}\in[\mathcal{P}_{1}(\widehat{\mathrm{T}})]^{2}}\left\lVert\widehat{\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})}+\widehat{\mathbf{q}}\right\rVert_{1+s,\widehat{\mathrm{T}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lvert\widehat{\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})}\right\rvert_{1+s,\widehat{\mathrm{T}}}

where
\widehat{\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})}
is defined via the affine mapping
F_{\mathrm{T}}
as
\widehat{\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\widehat{\mathbf{s}}))}=\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}(F_{\mathrm{T}}(\widehat{\mathbf{s}})))
,
\mathcal{I}
is the identity operator and
\widehat{\Pi}
the interpolation operator.

Now, following
[
23
]
and using the definition of fractional Sobolev seminorm, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\widehat{\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})}\right\rvert_{1+s,\widehat{\mathrm{T}}}^{2}=\left\lvert\grads\widehat{\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})}\right\rvert_{s,\widehat{\mathrm{T}}}^{2}=\int_{\widehat{\mathrm{T}}}\int_{\widehat{\mathrm{T}}}\frac{\left\lvert\grads\widehat{\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\widehat{\mathbf{s}}_{1}))}-\grads\widehat{\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\widehat{\mathbf{s}}_{2}))}\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert\widehat{\mathbf{s}}_{1}-\widehat{\mathbf{s}}_{2}\right\rvert^{2(s+1)}}\,d\widehat{\mathbf{s}}_{1}d\widehat{\mathbf{s}}_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\det B_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rvert^{-2}\int_{\mathrm{T}}\int_{\mathrm{T}}\frac{\left\lvert B_{\mathrm{T}}\big(\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{s}_{1}))-\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{s}_{2}))\big)\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert\mathbf{s}_{1}-\mathbf{s}_{2}\right\rvert^{2(s+1)}}\bigg(\frac{\left\lvert\mathbf{s}_{1}-\mathbf{s}_{2}\right\rvert}{\left\lvert B_{\mathrm{T}}^{-1}(\mathbf{s}_{1}-\mathbf{s}_{2})\right\rvert}\bigg)^{2(s+1)}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}_{1}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\lVert B_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rVert^{4+2s}}{\left\lvert\det B_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rvert^{2}}\left\lvert\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})\right\rvert_{s,\mathrm{T}}^{2}

so that

(45)

\left\lvert\widehat{\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})}\right\rvert_{1+s,\widehat{\mathrm{T}}}^{2}\leq C\,h_{\mathrm{T}}^{2s}\left\lVert\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})\right\rVert_{s,\mathrm{T}}^{2}.

By applying again Lemma
2
, putting together
(
43
), (
44
), and (
45
)
and taking into account that
\left\lvert\mathrm{T}\right\rvert\leq h_{\mathrm{T}}^{2}
, we get

(46)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\lvert\mathrm{T}\right\rvert\sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}\omega_{k}{\bm{\mu}}_{h}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0})\cdot\big(\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0}))-\mathbf{v}_{I}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0})\big)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{1-2s}\left\lvert\mathrm{T}\right\rvert^{1/2}h_{\mathrm{T}}^{s}\left\lVert{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\mathrm{T}}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left\lVert\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})\right\rVert_{0,\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{h_{\mathrm{T}}^{1+s}}{1-2s}\left\lVert{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\mathrm{T}}\left\lVert\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})\right\rVert_{0,\mathrm{T}}.

Finally, for
0\leq s<1/2
, the local estimate reads:

(47)

\left\lvert E_{\mathrm{T}}({\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))\right\rvert\leq C\frac{h_{\mathrm{T}}^{1+s}}{1-2s}\left\lVert{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\mathrm{T}}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left\lVert\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})\right\rVert_{0,\mathrm{T}}.

Taking
s=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\log h_{\mathrm{T}}}
in the above inequality, we obtain with
simple computations the following local estimate:

(48)

\left\lvert E_{\mathrm{T}}({\bm{\mu}}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))\right\rvert\leq Ch_{\mathrm{T}}^{3/2}|\log h_{\mathrm{T}}|\left\lVert{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\mathrm{T}}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left\lVert\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})\right\rVert_{0,\mathrm{T}}.

Summing on all the triangles in
\mathcal{T}_{h,2}^{\mathcal{B}}
, we get the global
estimate (
37
).∎

We now study the following proposition for the estimate of the error for the
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{B})
scalar product of gradients.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\mathbf{V}_{h}
and
{\bm{\Lambda}}_{h}
given by (
14
). Let us assume that
\overline{\mathbf{X}}
satisfies Assumption
3
.
Given a quadrature rule
\{(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{1},\omega_{k}^{1})\}_{k=1}^{K_{1}}
exact for constants, i.e. such that

(49)

\widehat{E}(\widehat{f})=0\quad\forall\widehat{f}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(\widehat{\mathrm{T}}),

and a quasi-uniform mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\Omega}
, the following estimate holds

(50)

\left\lvert E_{\mathcal{B}}(\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}:\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))\right\rvert\leq C\bigg(h_{\mathcal{B}}^{1/2}|\log h_{\mathcal{B}}^{min}|+\frac{h_{\mathcal{B}}}{h_{\Omega}}\bigg)\left\lVert\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\mathcal{B}}\left\lVert\Grad\mathbf{v}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}

for all
{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\in{\bm{\Lambda}}_{h}
and
\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
. Here
E_{\mathcal{B}}
is the sum of
E_{\mathrm{T}}(\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}:\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))
for all
\mathrm{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathcal{B}}
and
h_{\mathcal{B}}^{min}=\min_{\mathrm{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathcal{B}}}h_{\mathrm{T}}
.

Proof.

Using the same technique as in the proof of Proposition
3
,
we partition the solid mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathcal{B}}
as in
(
38
) and we work locally in an element

\mathrm{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h,2}^{\mathcal{B}}
: this means that we have to bound the difference

(51)

E_{\mathrm{T}}(\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}:\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))=\int_{\mathrm{T}}\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}:\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}-\left\lvert\mathrm{T}\right\rvert\sum_{k=1}^{K_{1}}\omega_{k}^{1}\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{1}):\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{1})).

We recall that
\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\in[\mathcal{P}_{0}(\mathrm{T})]^{2}
, while
\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})
is
a discontinuous piecewise constant function in
\mathrm{T}
.
Working again with the interpolant
\mathbf{v}_{I}\in[\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathrm{T}))]^{2}
of
\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})
,
we can write

(52)

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{T}}(\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}:\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{T}}\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}:\big(\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})-\grads\mathbf{v}_{I}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathrm{T}}\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}:\grads\mathbf{v}_{I}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}-\left\lvert\mathrm{T}\right\rvert\sum_{k=1}^{K_{1}}\omega_{k}^{1}\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{1}):\grads\mathbf{v}_{I}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{1})

\displaystyle\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\displaystyle-\left\lvert\mathrm{T}\right\rvert\sum_{k=1}^{K_{1}}\omega_{k}^{1}\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{1}):\big(\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{1}))-\grads\mathbf{v}_{I}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{1})\big)

so that we can deal separately with each term.

For the first term, we use the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality

(53)

\left\lvert\int_{\mathrm{T}}\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}:\big(\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})-\grads\mathbf{v}_{I}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\mathrm{T}}\left\lVert\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})-\grads\mathbf{v}_{I}\right\rVert_{0,\mathrm{T}},

then using Lemma
2
we get

\left\lVert\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})-\grads\mathbf{v}_{I}\right\rVert_{0,\mathrm{T}}\leq h_{\mathrm{T}}^{s}\left\lVert\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})\right\rVert_{s,\mathrm{T}}\leq\frac{h_{\mathrm{T}}^{s}}{1-2s}\left\lVert\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})\right\rVert_{0,\mathrm{T}}

so that, taking
s=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\log h_{\mathrm{T}}}
, we find

(54)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{\mathrm{T}}\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}:\big(\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})-\grads\mathbf{v}_{I}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq Ch_{\mathrm{T}}^{1/2}\left\lvert\log h_{\mathrm{T}}\right\rvert\left\lVert\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\mathrm{T}}\left\lVert\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})\right\rVert_{0,\mathrm{T}}.

The second term is zero by construction of
\mathbf{v}_{I}
and the choice of quadrature rule

(55)

E_{\mathrm{T}}(\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}:\grads\mathbf{v}_{I})=\int_{\mathrm{T}}\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}:\grads\mathbf{v}_{I}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}-\left\lvert\mathrm{T}\right\rvert\sum_{k=1}^{K_{1}}\omega_{k}^{1}\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{1}):\grads\mathbf{v}_{I}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{1})=0.

For the last term, we proceed as in (
43
) so that we have

(56)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\lvert\mathrm{T}\right\rvert\sum_{k=1}^{K_{1}}\omega_{k}^{1}\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{1}):\big(\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{1}))-\grads\mathbf{v}_{I}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{1})\big)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq K_{1}\left\lvert\mathrm{T}\right\rvert^{1/2}\left\lVert\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\mathrm{T}}\left\lVert\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})-\grads\mathbf{v}_{I}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathrm{T}}

In order to estimate
\left\lVert\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})-\grads\mathbf{v}_{I}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathrm{T}}
,
we take into account the discontinuity of
\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})
in
\mathrm{T}
:
hence we consider again the tessellation of
\mathrm{T}
into disjoint polygons

P_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,J
, with
\mathrm{T}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{J}P_{j}

and
\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})
constant in each
P_{j}
.
Using that
\left\lVert\grads\mathbf{v}_{I}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathrm{T}}\leq\left\lVert\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathrm{T}}
,
we can write

(57)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})-\grads\mathbf{v}_{I}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle=\max_{j=1,\dots,J}\left\lVert\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})-\grads\mathbf{v}_{I}\right\rVert_{\infty,P_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\max_{j=1,\dots,J}\left\lVert\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})\right\rVert_{\infty,P_{j}}.

We notice that each polygon
P_{j}
is the inverse image
through
\overline{\mathbf{X}}
of
\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\mathrm{T})\cap\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{f},j}
with
\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{f},j}\in\mathcal{T}_{h/2}^{\Omega}
.
Moreover, we denote by
\omega_{\mathrm{T}}
the macroelement
consisting of all fluid triangles intersecting
\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\mathrm{T})
,
i.e.
{\omega_{\mathrm{T}}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{J}\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{f},j}}
.
Therefore, by applying the inverse inequality

\|\mathbf{w}_{h}\|_{\infty,\tau}\leq C_{I}/h_{\tau}\|\mathbf{w}_{h}\|_{0,\tau}
for
\mathbf{w}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}

and
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{h/2}^{\Omega}
, we get

(58)

\displaystyle\max_{j=1,\dots,J}\left\lVert\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})\right\rVert_{\infty,P_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C_{\overline{\mathbf{X}}}\max_{j=1,\dots,J}\left\lVert\Grad\mathbf{v}_{h}\right\rVert_{\infty,\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{f},j}}

\displaystyle\leq\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C_{\overline{\mathbf{X}}}\max_{j=1,\dots,J}\,\frac{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C_{\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{f},j}}}{h_{\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{f},j}}}\left\lVert\Grad\mathbf{v}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\mathrm{T}_{\mathrm{f},j}}\leq\frac{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C}{h_{\Omega}}\left\lVert\Grad\mathbf{v}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\omega_{\mathrm{T}}},

where

depends on

and
, in the last
inequality, we exploited that
\mathcal{T}_{h/2}^{\Omega}
is quasi-uniform. Putting
together (

) and
(

(59)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\lvert\mathrm{T}\right\rvert\sum_{k=1}^{K_{1}}\omega_{k}^{1}\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{1}):\big(\grads\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{1}))-\grads\mathbf{v}_{I}(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{1})\big)\right\rvert

Proposition 7
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
be a mesh of the domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and let

V_{h}
be a finite element space of degree
m
. Then, there exists a constant
C
such that for all discrete function
v_{h}\in V_{h}
it holds

\sum_{\mathrm{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{\mathrm{T}}^{2}\|v_{h}\|_{0,\mathrm{T}}^{2}\leq C\|v_{h}\|_{-1,D}^{2},

where
C
depends on the degree of
V_{h}
and on the shape regularity constant of the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

Proof.

It follows from a result by Schatz and Wahlbin
[
37
, Lemma 1.1]
that

\sum_{\mathrm{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|v\|_{-1,\mathrm{T}}^{2}\leq\|v\|_{-1,D}^{2},\qquad\forall v\in H^{-1}(D).

If we prove that

(63)

\|v_{h}\|_{0,\mathrm{T}}\leq Ch_{\mathrm{T}}^{-1}\|v_{h}\|_{-1,\mathrm{T}}\qquad\forall v_{h}\in\mathcal{P}_{m}(\mathrm{T}),

with
C
depending only on
m
and the aspect ratio of
\mathrm{T}
, then the statement follows by

\sum_{\mathrm{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{\mathrm{T}}^{2}\|v_{h}\|_{0,\mathrm{T}}^{2}\leq C\sum_{\mathrm{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|v_{h}\|_{-1,\mathrm{T}}^{2}\leq C\|v_{h}\|_{-1,D}^{2}.

It remains to prove (
63
), which follows by standard scaling arguments. Let
\widehat{\mathrm{T}}
be a reference triangle, and
F_{\mathrm{T}}
a linear function mapping
\widehat{\mathrm{T}}
onto
\mathrm{T}
, and define
\widehat{v}_{h}
by
\widehat{v}_{h}(\widehat{\mathbf{x}})=v_{h}({\mathbf{x}})
with
{\mathbf{x}}=F_{\mathrm{T}}(\widehat{\mathbf{x}})
. Then

(64)

\|v_{h}\|_{0,\mathrm{T}}\leq Ch_{\mathrm{T}}\|\widehat{v}_{h}\|_{0,\widehat{\mathrm{T}}},

and by equivalence of norms in
\mathcal{P}_{m}(\widehat{\mathrm{T}})
we have

(65)

\|\widehat{v}_{h}\|_{0,\widehat{\mathrm{T}}}\leq C\|\widehat{v}_{h}\|_{-1,\widehat{\mathrm{T}}}.

By definition

\|\widehat{v}_{h}\|_{-1,\widehat{\mathrm{T}}}=\sup_{\widehat{w}\in H_{0}^{1}(\widehat{\mathrm{T}})}\frac{\left(\widehat{v}_{h},\widehat{w}\right)_{\widehat{\mathrm{T}}}}{\|\widehat{w}\|_{1,\widehat{\mathrm{T}}}}.

But, since
\widehat{w}(\widehat{\mathbf{x}})=w({\mathbf{x}})
,

\left(\widehat{v}_{h},\widehat{w}\right)_{\widehat{\mathrm{T}}}\leq Ch_{\mathrm{T}}^{-2}\left(v_{h},w\right),

and

\|\widehat{w}\|_{1,\widehat{\mathrm{T}}}\geq Ch_{\mathrm{T}}^{-1}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{1}h_{\mathrm{T}}^{2i}|w|^{2}_{i,\mathrm{T}}\right)^{1/2}\geq C\|w\|_{1,\mathrm{T}}.

Therefore

(66)

\|\widehat{v}_{h}\|_{-1,\widehat{\mathrm{T}}}\leq Ch_{\mathrm{T}}^{-2}\|v_{h}\|_{-1,\mathrm{T}}.

Then, from (
64
)–(
66
) we
obtain (
63
).

Remark 1
.

When dealing with fluid-structure interaction problems, the solution of the
fictitious domain approach is singular across the interface between fluid and
solid.
Typically, for instance,
\mathbf{u}
belongs to

\mathbf{H}^{3/2-\varepsilon}(\Omega)
but not in
\mathbf{H}^{3/2}(\Omega)

because of the jump of the normal stresses. It is then natural to consider

optimal
an error estimate like the one of Corollary
1
which
gives a rate of convergence of
O(h^{1/2})
up to the logarithmic factor.

It could be interesting however to see if, in case of smoother solutions, a
higher rate of convergence could be obtained which fully exploits the
polynomial order used for the approximation. It turns out that this is the
case as we are going to show now.

The following proposition refines the estimate of Corollary
1
in the
case of smooth solutions.

Proposition 8
.

Let us assume that
(\mathbf{u},p,\mathbf{X},\bm{\lambda})
belongs to
\mathbf{H}^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{H}^{2}(\mathcal{B})\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{B})
and that

(67)

\|\mathbf{u}\|_{2,\Omega}+\|p\|_{1,\Omega}+\|\mathbf{X}\|_{2,\mathcal{B}}+\|\bm{\lambda}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}\leq C(\|\mathbf{f}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\mathbf{g}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}+\|\mathbf{d}\|_{2,\mathcal{B}}).

Moreover, let us assume for simplicity that the right hand sides in
Problem
3
are computed exactly and that the
mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathcal{B}}
is quasiuniform. Then under the same hypotheses as in
Corollary
1
we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}^{\star}\right\rVert_{1,\Omega}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert p-p_{h}^{\star}\right\rVert_{0,\Omega}+\left\lVert\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{h}^{\star}\right\rVert_{1,\mathcal{B}}+\left\lVert\bm{\lambda}-\bm{\lambda}_{h}^{\star}\right\rVert_{{\bm{\Lambda}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\max(h_{\Omega},h_{\mathcal{B}})\big(|\mathbf{u}|_{2,\Omega}+|p|_{1,\Omega}+|\mathbf{X}|_{2,\mathcal{B}}+\|\bm{\lambda}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}\big).

Proof.

As for the proof of Corollary
1
, we need to estimate the various
consistency terms appearing in Equation (
32
). Here we comment
on how to improve the bound of the following two terms under our regularity
assumptions:

(68)

\displaystyle\sup_{{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\in{\bm{\Lambda}}_{h}}\frac{|\mathbf{c}_{0}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))-\mathbf{c}_{0,h}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))|}{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\|_{{\bm{\Lambda}}}},

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}}\frac{|\mathbf{c}_{0}(\bm{\lambda}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))-\mathbf{c}_{0,h}(\bm{\lambda}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))|}{\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}},

here
\bm{\lambda}_{h}
and
\mathbf{u}_{h}
are components of the solution to
Problem
2
.

We estimate the first term in (
68
).
We observe that (
48
) holds true even when the function

\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})
is replaced by
\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})-\mathbf{w}_{h}
where
\mathbf{w}_{h}
is any function
of
\mathbf{S}_{h}
.
Hence, we introduce the piecewise linear interpolant
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{B}}\mathbf{u}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})\in\mathbf{S}_{h}

of
\mathbf{u}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})
on the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{B}}
and proceed as follows.

\displaystyle\mathbf{c}_{0}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))-\mathbf{c}_{0,h}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))

\displaystyle=\mathbf{c}_{0}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})-\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{B}}\mathbf{u}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))-\mathbf{c}_{0,h}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})-\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{B}}\mathbf{u}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{\mathrm{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{B}}}h^{3/2}_{\mathrm{T}}{|\log h_{\mathrm{T}}|}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\|_{0,\mathrm{T}}\|{\grads(\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})-\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{B}}\mathbf{u}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))}\|_{0,\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{\mathrm{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{B}}}h^{3/2}_{\mathrm{T}}{|\log h_{\mathrm{T}}|}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\|_{0,\mathrm{T}}(\|{\grads(\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})-\mathbf{u}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))}\|_{0,\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle+\|{\grads(\mathbf{u}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})-\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{B}}\mathbf{u}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))}\|_{0,\mathrm{T}})

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{3/2}_{\mathcal{B}}{|\log h_{\mathcal{B}}|}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}\|\mathbf{u}_{h}-\mathbf{u}\|_{1,\Omega}+C\sum_{\mathrm{T}\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{B}}}h^{3/2}_{\mathrm{T}}{|\log h_{\mathrm{T}}|}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\|_{0,\mathrm{T}}h_{\mathrm{T}}|\mathbf{u}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})|_{2,\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{1/2}_{\mathcal{B}}{|\log h_{\mathcal{B}}|}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\|_{\bm{\Lambda}}\|\mathbf{u}_{h}-\mathbf{u}\|_{1,\Omega}+Ch^{3/2}_{\mathcal{B}}{|\log h_{\mathcal{B}}|}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\|_{\bm{\Lambda}}|\mathbf{u}|_{2,\Omega},

where in the last inequality we used the inverse estimate
in (
61
).

The regularity assumption (
67
) and Theorem
1
imply

(69)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\Omega}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\big(h_{\Omega}(\|\mathbf{u}\|_{2,\Omega}+\|p\|_{1,\Omega})+h_{\mathcal{B}}(\|\mathbf{X}\|_{2,\mathcal{B}}+\|\bm{\lambda}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}})\big).

We thus have

(70)

\displaystyle\sup_{{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\in{\bm{\Lambda}}_{h}}\frac{|\mathbf{c}_{0}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))-\mathbf{c}_{0,h}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))|}{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\|_{{\bm{\Lambda}}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,h_{\mathcal{B}}^{1/2}|\log h_{\mathcal{B}}|\max(h_{\Omega},h_{\mathcal{B}})\bigg(\|\mathbf{u}\|_{2,\Omega}+\|p\|_{1,\Omega}+\|\mathbf{X}\|_{2,\mathcal{B}}+\|\bm{\lambda}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}\bigg)

\displaystyle+C\,h_{\mathcal{B}}(\|\mathbf{u}\|_{2,\Omega}+\|\mathbf{X}\|_{2,\mathcal{B}}).

The second term in (
68
) can be bounded using Proposition
5
as
follows:

\sup_{\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}}\frac{|\mathbf{c}_{0}(\bm{\lambda}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))-\mathbf{c}_{0,h}(\bm{\lambda}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))|}{\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}}\leq Ch_{\mathcal{B}}^{3/2}|\log h_{\mathcal{B}}|\|\bm{\lambda}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}.

We now estimate
\|\bm{\lambda}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}
exploiting the regularity of

\bm{\lambda}
. We consider the
L^{2}
projection
\Pi^{0}:\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{B})\rightarrow{\bm{\Lambda}}_{h}
, that is for any
{\bm{\eta}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{B})}
,
(\bm{\eta}-\Pi^{0}\bm{\eta},{\bm{\mu}}_{h})=0
for all
{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\in{\bm{\Lambda}}_{h}
. The projection has the following properties thanks to the fact that
{\bm{\Lambda}}_{h}=\mathbf{S}_{h}
:

(71)

\|\Pi^{0}\bm{\eta}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}\leq\|\bm{\eta}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}},\qquad\qquad\|\bm{\eta}-\Pi^{0}\bm{\eta}\|_{\bm{\Lambda}}\leq C\,h_{\mathcal{B}}\|\bm{\eta}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}.

In order to estimate
\|\bm{\lambda}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}
, we bound

\|\bm{\lambda}_{h}-\Pi^{0}\bm{\lambda}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}
taking into account that
\mathbf{S}_{h}={\bm{\Lambda}}_{h}

(72)

\displaystyle\|\Pi^{0}\bm{\lambda}-\bm{\lambda}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\Pi^{0}\bm{\lambda}-\bm{\lambda}_{h},\Pi^{0}\bm{\lambda}-\bm{\lambda}_{h})_{\mathcal{B}}}{\|\Pi^{0}\bm{\lambda}-\bm{\lambda}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}}\leq\sup_{\mathbf{Y}_{h}\in\mathbf{S}_{h}}\frac{\mathbf{c}_{0}(\Pi^{0}\bm{\lambda}-\bm{\lambda}_{h},\mathbf{Y}_{h})}{\|\mathbf{Y}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\mathbf{Y}_{h}\in\mathbf{S}_{h}}\frac{\mathbf{c}_{0}(\Pi^{0}\bm{\lambda}-\bm{\lambda},\mathbf{Y}_{h})+\mathbf{c}_{0}(\bm{\lambda}-\bm{\lambda}_{h},\mathbf{Y}_{h})}{\|\mathbf{Y}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}}.

Since

is the

projection of
\bm{\lambda}
in
{\bm{\Lambda}}_{h}=\mathbf{S}_{h}
, the
term
\mathbf{c}_{0}(\Pi^{0}\bm{\lambda}-\bm{\lambda}_{h},\mathbf{Y}_{h})
vanishes.
Moreover, the difference of the solid equations in
Problems

and
2

gives

\mathbf{c}_{0}(\bm{\lambda}-\bm{\lambda}_{h},\mathbf{Y}_{h})=\mathbf{a}_{s}(\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{h},\mathbf{Y}_{h})\quad\forall\mathbf{Y}_{h}\in\mathbf{S}_{h}.

Putting together the last two relations, a standard inverse inequality yields

(73)

\displaystyle\|\Pi^{0}\bm{\lambda}-\bm{\lambda}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\mathbf{Y}_{h}\in\mathbf{S}_{h}}\frac{\mathbf{a}_{s}(\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{h},\mathbf{Y}_{h})}{\|\mathbf{Y}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\sup_{\mathbf{Y}_{h}\in\mathbf{S}_{h}}\frac{\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{h}\|_{1,\mathcal{B}}\|\mathbf{Y}_{h}\|_{1,\mathcal{B}}}{\|\mathbf{Y}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,h_{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_{h}\|_{1,\mathcal{B}}.

The bound (
69
) implies that

(74)

\displaystyle\|\bm{\lambda}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Pi^{0}\bm{\lambda}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}+\|\Pi^{0}\bm{\lambda}-\bm{\lambda}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\bm{\lambda}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}+C\,\big(h_{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}h_{\Omega}(\|\mathbf{u}\|_{2,\Omega}+\|p\|_{1,\Omega})+\|\mathbf{X}\|_{2,\mathcal{B}}+\|\bm{\lambda}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}\big)

so that
\bm{\lambda}_{h}
is bounded in
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{B})
.
Hence, we have the following estimate for the second term in (

(75)

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}}

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathbf{c}_{0}(\bm{\lambda}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))-\mathbf{c}_{0,h}(\bm{\lambda}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))|}{\|\mathbf{v}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}}\leq Ch_{\mathcal{B}}^{3/2}|\log h_{\mathcal{B}}|\|\bm{\lambda}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch_{\mathcal{B}}^{3/2}|\log h_{\mathcal{B}}|\|\bm{\lambda}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle+Ch_{\mathcal{B}}^{3/2}|\log h_{\mathcal{B}}|\Big(h_{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}h_{\Omega}(\|\mathbf{u}\|_{2,\Omega}+\|p\|_{1,\Omega})+\|\mathbf{X}\|_{2,\mathcal{B}}+\|\bm{\lambda}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}\Big).

Finally, by combining the estimates in (

)
and (

) with (

8.
Stability proof with inexact integration

This section is devoted to check Assumption
1
, which
corresponds to the discrete inf-sup condition in the case of inexact integration of the coupling term. We adopt the approach already used in
[
15
]
and based on
[
39
]
. Exploiting the saddle point structure of
\mathbb{A}_{h}
in operator form

\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}cc:c@{}}\mathsf{A}_{f}&0\hfil\lx@intercol\vrule\lx@add@cssclass&\mathsf{C}_{f,h}^{\top}\\
0&\mathsf{A}_{s}\hfil\lx@intercol\vrule\lx@add@cssclass&-\mathsf{C}_{s}^{\top}\\
\hline\cr\mathsf{C}_{f,h}&-\mathsf{C}_{s}\hfil\lx@intercol\vrule\lx@add@cssclass&0\end{array}\right],

the proof splits into two steps: first, we prove the inf-sup condition for

\mathbb{C}_{h}=[\mathsf{C}_{f,h},\,-\mathsf{C}_{s}]

and then we show that

\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}cc@{}}\mathsf{A}_{f}&0\\
0&\mathsf{A}_{s}\end{array}\right]

is elliptic in the kernel of
\mathbb{C}_{h}
.

Proposition 9
.

Let us assume that the
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{B})
term of
\mathbf{c}_{h}
is computed with a quadrature rule
\{(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0},\omega_{k}^{0})\}_{k=1}^{K_{0}}
which is exact for quadratic polynomials, while the
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{B})
scalar product of gradients is approximated with a quadrature rule
\{(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{1},\omega_{k}^{1})\}_{k=1}^{K_{1}}
which is exact for constants. Moreover, we assume that the
L^{2}
projection
\Pi^{0}
from
\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathcal{B})
to
\mathbf{S}_{h}
is
H^{1}
stable, that is
\|\Pi^{0}\mathbf{Y}\|_{1,\mathcal{B}}\leq C_{0}\|\mathbf{Y}\|_{1,\mathcal{B}}
for all
\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathcal{B})
. Then, there exists a constant
\beta_{0}
such that the following condition holds true

(76)

\sup_{(\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{Y}_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{0,h}\times\mathbf{S}_{h}}\frac{\mathbf{c}_{h}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})-\mathbf{Y}_{h})}{(\left\lVert\mathbf{v}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{1,\Omega}+\left\lVert\mathbf{Y}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{1,\mathcal{B}})^{1/2}}\geq\beta_{0}\left\lVert{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\right\rVert_{{\bm{\Lambda}}}\qquad\forall{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\in{\bm{\Lambda}}_{h},

where

\mathbf{V}_{0,h}=\{\mathbf{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}:(\div\mathbf{v}_{h},q_{h})_{\Omega}=0\quad\forall q_{h}\in Q_{h}\}.

Proof.

Case 1.
Let us consider
\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{c}_{0}
and
\mathbf{c}_{h}=\mathbf{c}_{0,h}
given by (
22a
). Using the definition of norm in the dual space
{\bm{\Lambda}}=\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathcal{B})^{\prime}
, there exists
\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathcal{B})
realizing the supremum in the continuous case, so that

(77)

\left\lVert{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\right\rVert_{{\bm{\Lambda}}}=\sup_{\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathcal{B})}\frac{({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{Y})_{\mathcal{B}}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{1,\mathcal{B}}}=\sup_{\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathcal{B})}\frac{\mathbf{c}_{0}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{Y})}{\left\lVert\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert_{1,\mathcal{B}}}=\frac{\mathbf{c}_{0}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\tilde{\mathbf{Y}})}{\|\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}\|_{1,\mathcal{B}}}.

By exploiting the projection
\Pi^{0}
, we have

(78)

\frac{\mathbf{c}_{0}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\tilde{\mathbf{Y}})}{\|\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}\|_{1,\mathcal{B}}}=\frac{\mathbf{c}_{0}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\Pi^{0}\tilde{\mathbf{Y}})}{\|\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}\|_{1,\mathcal{B}}}\leq C_{0}\frac{\mathbf{c}_{0}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\Pi^{0}\tilde{\mathbf{Y}})}{\|\Pi^{0}\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}\|_{1,\mathcal{B}}}\leq\sup_{\mathbf{Y}_{h}\in\mathbf{S}_{h}}\frac{\mathbf{c}_{0}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{Y}_{h})}{\left\lVert\mathbf{Y}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\mathcal{B}}}.

Thanks to the exactness of the chosen quadrature rule, we can notice that
\mathbf{c}
and
\mathbf{c}_{h}
coincide in the solid domain, therefore

(79)

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\right\rVert_{{\bm{\Lambda}}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\mathbf{Y}_{h}\in\mathbf{S}_{h}}\frac{\mathbf{c}_{0}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{Y}_{h})}{\left\lVert\mathbf{Y}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\mathcal{B}}}=\sup_{\mathbf{Y}_{h}\in\mathbf{S}_{h}}\frac{\mathbf{c}_{0,h}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{Y}_{h})}{\left\lVert\mathbf{Y}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\mathcal{B}}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{(\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{Y}_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{0,h}\times\mathbf{S}_{h}}\frac{\mathbf{c}_{0,h}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbf{Y}_{h}-\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))}{(\left\lVert\mathbf{v}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{1,\Omega}+\left\lVert\mathbf{Y}_{h}\right\rVert^{2}_{1,\mathcal{B}})^{1/2}}.

Case 2.
Now, let us take
\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{c}_{1}
and
\mathbf{c}_{h}=\mathbf{c}_{1,h}
as defined in (
5
) and (
22b
), respectively. Given
{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\in{\bm{\Lambda}}_{h}
, we can take
\mathbf{Y}_{h}={\bm{\mu}}_{h}
so that

(80)

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\right\rVert_{{\bm{\Lambda}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{Y}_{h})_{\mathcal{B}}+(\grads{\bm{\mu}}_{h},\grads\mathbf{Y}_{h})_{\mathcal{B}}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{Y}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\mathcal{B}}}=\frac{\mathbf{c}_{1}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{Y}_{h})}{\left\lVert\mathbf{Y}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\mathcal{B}}}\leq\sup_{\mathbf{Y}_{h}\in\mathbf{S}_{h}}\frac{\mathbf{c}_{1}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{Y}_{h})}{\left\lVert\mathbf{Y}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\mathcal{B}}}.

Therefore, working as in (
79
), the result is proved.
∎

We now prove the ellipticity of
\mathbb{A}_{h}
in the kernel of
\mathbb{C}_{h}
.

Proposition 10
.

Let us assume that the
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{B})
term of
\mathbf{c}_{h}
is computed with a quadrature rule
\{(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{0},\omega_{k}^{0})\}_{k=1}^{K_{0}}
which is exact for quadratic polynomials, while the
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{B})
scalar product of gradients is approximated with a quadrature rule
\{(\mathbf{p}_{k}^{1},\omega_{k}^{1})\}_{k=1}^{K_{1}}
which is exact for constants.
Then, there exists
\theta^{\star}>0
independent on the mesh sizes such that

(81)

\mathbf{a}_{f}(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h})+\mathbf{a}_{s}(\mathbf{X}_{h},\mathbf{X}_{h})\geq\theta^{\star}(\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\Omega}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{X}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\mathcal{B}}^{2})

for all pairs
(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{X}_{h})
in the kernel of
\mathbb{C}_{h}
defined as

(82)

\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{C}_{h}}=\{(\mathbf{v}_{h},\mathbf{Y}_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{0,h}\times\mathbf{S}_{h}:\mathbf{c}_{h}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{v}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))-\mathbf{c}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{Y}_{h})=0\quad\forall{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\in{\bm{\Lambda}}_{h}\}.

Proof.

Recalling the definition of the bilinear forms (
6
), if
\beta>0
, we simply have that

(83)

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}_{f}(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h})+\mathbf{a}_{s}(\mathbf{X}_{h},\mathbf{X}_{h})

\displaystyle\geq C\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\Omega}^{2}+\beta\left\lVert\mathbf{X}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\mathcal{B}}^{2}+\kappa\left\lVert\grads\mathbf{X}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\mathcal{B}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq C\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\Omega}^{2}+\min\{\beta,\kappa\}\left\lVert\mathbf{X}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\mathcal{B}}^{2}.

and the property is satisfied. Otherwise, if
\beta=0
, we have that

(84)

\mathbf{a}_{f}(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h})+\mathbf{a}_{s}(\mathbf{X}_{h},\mathbf{X}_{h})\geq C\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\Omega}^{2}+\kappa\left\lVert\grads\mathbf{X}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\mathcal{B}}^{2};

hence, in this situation, we have to control the missing term

\left\lVert\mathbf{X}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\mathcal{B}}
. We follow the sketch used in
[
16
]
. In this way,
we can prove this proposition at once for both choices of
\mathbf{c}_{h}
.

We introduce the mean of
\mathbf{X}_{h}
as

(85)

\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h}=\left\lvert\mathcal{B}\right\rvert^{-1}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\mathbf{X}_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}

so that, using the Poincaré inequality, we can write

(86)

\|\mathbf{X}_{h}-\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}\leq C\left\lVert\grads\mathbf{X}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\mathcal{B}}

and therefore

(87)

\left\lVert\mathbf{X}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\mathcal{B}}\leq\|\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}+\|\mathbf{X}_{h}-\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}\leq\|\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}+C\left\lVert\grads\mathbf{X}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\mathcal{B}}.

Exploiting that
(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{X}_{h})\in\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{C}_{h}}
, we have that

(88)

\mathbf{c}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h})=\mathbf{c}_{h}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))-\mathbf{c}({\bm{\mu}}_{h},\mathbf{X}_{h}-\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h})\quad\forall{\bm{\mu}}_{h}\in{\bm{\Lambda}}_{h}

hence, taking
{\bm{\mu}}_{h}=\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h}
, which is constant, we have

(89)

\|\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}^{2}=\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h},\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h})=\mathbf{c}_{0,h}(\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))-\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h},\mathbf{X}_{h}-\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h});

therefore, using the definition of
\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h}
in (
85
), with some computations we can see that
\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h},\mathbf{X}_{h}-\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h})=0
, indeed

(90)

\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h},\mathbf{X}_{h}-\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h})=\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h}\bigg(\int_{\mathcal{B}}\mathbf{X}_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{s}-\left\lvert\mathcal{B}\right\rvert\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h}\bigg)=0

At this point, we need to find a bound for
\mathbf{c}_{h}(\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))
. Adding and subtracting the same quantity, we can write

(91)

\mathbf{c}_{0,h}(\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))=\mathbf{c}_{0,h}(\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))-\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))+\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}));

in particular, for continuity we have

(92)

\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))\leq\|\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}\|\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}})\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}

while, applying the result of Proposition
5
,

(93)

\mathbf{c}_{0,h}(\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))-\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{X}}))\leq Ch_{\mathcal{B}}^{3/2}|\log h_{\mathcal{B}}^{min}|\|\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}\|\mathbf{u}_{h}\|_{1,\Omega}

so that, in combination with (
90
), we get

(94)

\|\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{m}}_{h}\|_{0,\mathcal{B}}\leq C(1+h_{\mathcal{B}}^{3/2}|\log h_{\mathcal{B}}^{min}|)\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\Omega}.

Consequently, putting together (
94
) and (
87
), we have

(95)

\left\lVert\mathbf{X}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\mathcal{B}}\leq C(1+h_{\mathcal{B}}^{3/2}|\log h_{\mathcal{B}}^{min}|)\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\Omega}+C\left\lVert\grads\mathbf{X}_{h}\right\rVert_{0,\mathcal{B}}

and therefore, we can conclude the proof by saying that we can find a constant
\theta^{\star}
such that

(96)

\mathbf{a}_{f}(\mathbf{u}_{h},\mathbf{u}_{h})+\mathbf{a}_{s}(\mathbf{X}_{h},\mathbf{X}_{h})\geq\theta^{\star}(\left\lVert\mathbf{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\Omega}^{2}+\left\lVert\mathbf{X}_{h}\right\rVert_{1,\mathcal{B}}^{2}).

∎

Putting together the results of the previous propositions, we find that the inf-sup condition for
\mathbb{A}_{h}
holds true.

Proposition 11
.

There exists a positive constant
\theta^{\star}
independent of h such that the following inf-sup condition holds true

(97)

\inf_{\mathcal{U}\in\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{B}_{h}}}\sup_{\mathcal{V}\in\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{B}_{h}}}\frac{\mathbb{A}_{h}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})}{\left\vvvert\mathcal{U}\right\vvvert\left\vvvert\mathcal{V}\right\vvvert}\geq\theta^{\star}.

In

10.
Conclusions

We discussed how to deal with the coupling term in the finite element discretization of fluid-structure interaction problems modeled by the formulation with distributed Lagrange multiplier introduced in
[
11
]
: this is used to enforce the kinematic condition in a fictitious domain framework.

The construction of the coupling term consists in integrating on the solid domain both fluid and solid variables, which are defined on two independent non-matching grids. Two approaches can be considered: the
exact approach
requires the computation of the intersection between the two meshes, so that a composite quadrature rule is implemented, whereas the
approximate approach
consists in inexact integration over the solid domain. We focused on the quadrature error due to the effect of inexact integration.

We showed that the discrete problem is stable, i.e. it satisfies the
inf-sup conditions, also when the coupling term is computed inexactly.
Moreover, we proved quadrature error estimates: the coupling form
\mathbf{c}_{0}

behaves well provided that the solid mesh size
h_{\mathcal{B}}\rightarrow 0
, whereas for
\mathbf{c}_{1}
also the condition
h_{\mathcal{B}}/h_{\Omega}\rightarrow 0
is required. Our theoretical results are validated by two numerical tests and are also consistent with the experimental results we obtained in our previous studies
[
13
,
12
]
.

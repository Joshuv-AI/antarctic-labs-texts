Abstract
. We study the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -zero-divisor cup-length, denoted by zcl ℤ 2 ⁡ ( G ~ n , 3 ) \operatorname{zcl}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3}) , of the Grassmann manifolds G ~ n , 3 \widetilde{G}_{n,3} of oriented 3 3 -dimensional vector subspaces in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . Some lower and upper bounds for this invariant are obtained for all integers n ≥ 6 n\geq 6 . For infinitely many of them the exact value of zcl ℤ 2 ⁡ ( G ~ n , 3 ) \operatorname{zcl}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3}) is computed, and in the rest of the cases these bounds differ by 1. We thus establish lower bounds for the topological complexity of Grassmannians G ~ n , 3 \widetilde{G}_{n,3} .

1.
Introduction

The topological complexity of a path connected space
X
, which we denote by
\mathrm{TC}(X)
, was defined in
[
4
]
as the Schwarz genus of the fibration
\pi:X^{I}\rightarrow X\times X
(
X^{I}
is the space of free paths in
X
) given by
\pi(\omega)=(\omega(0),\omega(1))
, i.e., as the minimal integer
m
such that there exists an open cover
\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{m}\}
of
X\times X
with the property that
\pi
has a section
s_{i}:U_{i}\rightarrow X^{I}
over each
U_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq m
.
Computing the exact value of topological complexity is in general very difficult problem.
Already for real projective spaces computing topological complexity is equivalent to finding
immersion dimension (see
[
5
]
), which is a long-standing open
problem in topology.

In
[
4
]
a lower bound (in terms of cohomology) for
\mathrm{TC}(X)
was detected – the zero-divisor cup-length of
X
.
For a commutative ring
R
and a graded
R
-algebra
A
, let
A\otimes A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle.}}{{\longrightarrow}}A
be the multiplication map. The
zero-divisor cup-length of
A
, denoted by
\operatorname{zcl}(A)
, is defined as the supremum of the set of all integers
d
such that there exist elements of positive degree
z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{d}\in\ker\big(A\otimes A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle.}}{{\longrightarrow}}A\big)
with the property that the product
z_{1}z_{2}\cdots z_{d}
is nontrivial in
A\otimes A
. For a space
X
we define the
R
-zero-divisor cup-length of
X
,
\operatorname{zcl}_{R}(X):=\operatorname{zcl}\big(H^{*}(X;R)\big)
. The statement of
[
4
, Theorem 7]
is that for any field
R
one has

\mathrm{TC}(X)\geq 1+\operatorname{zcl}_{R}(X).

For integers
n
and
k
such that
n\geq 2k\geq 2
, let
G_{n,k}
be the Grassmann manifold of
k
-dimensional subspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\widetilde{G}_{n,k}
the Grassmann manifold of oriented
k
-dimensional subspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In the recent works of Pavešić
[
9
]
and Radovanović
[
10
]
the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-zero-divisor cup-length of
G_{n,k}
was studied for some values of
n
and
k
. When it comes to ”oriented” Grassmannians
\widetilde{G}_{n,k}
, in
[
11
]
Ramani calculated
\operatorname{zcl}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\widetilde{G}_{n,k})
for all
n
and
k
. She also computes
\operatorname{zcl}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
for
6\leq n\leq 11
, and notes that in these cases (and most likely in all others)
\operatorname{zcl}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
is a better lower bound for
\mathrm{TC}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
than
\operatorname{zcl}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
.

In this paper we obtain lower bounds for
\operatorname{zcl}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
for all

n\geq 6
. We prove that if either
2^{t}-1\leq n<2^{t}+2^{t-1}/3+1
or
2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}+1\leq n\leq 2^{t+1}-2
(for some integer
t\geq 4
), then this lower bound is actually the exact value of
\operatorname{zcl}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
; while if
2^{t}+2^{t-1}/3+1<n\leq 2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}
, then
\operatorname{zcl}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
is either equal to this lower bound or is greater than it by
1
. These results then provide lower bounds for
\mathrm{TC}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
as listed in Table
4
.

Let
W_{n}
be the image of the map
p^{*}:H^{*}(G_{n,3};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\rightarrow H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
induced by the well-known two-fold covering
p:\widetilde{G}_{n,3}\rightarrow G_{n,3}
(which forgets the orientation of a subspace). The main tool for establishing our results on
\operatorname{zcl}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
is the Gröbner basis
F_{n}
(obtained in
[
3
]
)
for the ideal
I_{n}
that determines
W_{n}
. Indeed, the additive basis
\mathcal{B}_{n}
for
W_{n}
(induced by this Gröbner basis
F_{n}
) and some
identities in
W_{n}
obtained from certain elements of
F_{n}
, proved
to be essential for our calculations.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
we establish a framework for the subsequent calculations and proofs. We first recall some basic facts concerning the cohomology of
\widetilde{G}_{n,3}
, and list a few results from
[
3
]
. These include some identities involving the polynomials that generate the ideals
I_{n}
, Gröbner bases for these ideals etc. We also present some new identities in this regard. In this section some general results concerning the zero-divisor cup-length are given as well. The main part of the paper is Section
3
, in which we compute the exact value of
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})
(see Theorem
3.2
). In Section
4
we make a comparison between
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})
and
\operatorname{zcl}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
, obtaining our results on
\operatorname{zcl}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
and thus the lower bounds for the topological complexity of
\widetilde{G}_{n,3}
.

In the rest of the paper the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
coefficients for cohomology will be understood, and so we will abbreviate
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
to
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
, and
\operatorname{zcl}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
to
\operatorname{zcl}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
.

Background on cohomology algebra

n\geq 6

\widetilde{G}_{n,3}

\mathbb{R}^{n}

H^{*}(G_{n,3})
of the corresponding ”unoriented” Grassmannian
G_{n,3}
is generated by the Stiefel–Whitney classes of the canonical vector bundle over
G_{n,3}
, which pulls back via
p:\widetilde{G}_{n,3}\rightarrow G_{n,3}
to the canonical vector bundle over
\widetilde{G}_{n,3}
, and since
\widetilde{G}_{n,3}
is simply connected, the subalgebra
W_{n}=\operatorname{im}p^{*}
of
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
is generated by the Stiefel–Whitney classes
\widetilde{w}_{2}
and
\widetilde{w}_{3}
of this canonical bundle. It is well known (see e.g.
[
6

(2.1)

W_{n}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3}]/I_{n},

where
I_{n}
is the homogeneous ideal in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3}]
generated by certain (homogeneous) polynomials
g_{n-2}
,
g_{n-1}
and
g_{n}
(the subscripts for both variables and polynomials indicate their degrees in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3}]
).

The polynomials
g_{r}

(2.2)

(in the ring of power series
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[[w_{2},w_{3}]]
), which leads to the recurrence formula:

(2.3)

g_{r+3}=w_{2}g_{r+1}+w_{3}g_{r}\quad\mbox{for all }r\geq 0.

This formula shows that the sequence of ideals
\{I_{n}\}_{n\geq 2}

and more generally, that

g_{r}\in I_{n}\quad\mbox{ for all }r\geq n-2.

(2.5)

f(w_{2},w_{3})\in I_{n}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f(\widetilde{w}_{2},\widetilde{w}_{3})=0\mbox{ in }W_{n}.

, which involve the polynomials

,

.

Let

be an integer. Then:

(a)

g_{2^{t}-3}=0
;

(b)

g_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}-3}=w_{3}^{2^{t-1}-1}
;

(c)

g_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}-3}=w_{2}^{2^{t-2}}w_{3}^{2^{t-2}-1}

(d)

g_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}-3}=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}}w_{3}^{2^{t-2}-1}

).

We also state an important lemma from
[
3
, Lemma 4.3]
, which establishes a nice property of the ideals
w_{3}I_{n}=\{w_{3}p\mid p\in I_{n}\}
,
n\geq 2
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3}]
and
n\geq 2
. If
f\in w_{3}I_{n}
, then
f^{2}\in w_{3}I_{2n+1}
. In particular, the following implication holds:

f\in w_{3}I_{n}\,\Longrightarrow\,f^{2}\in w_{3}I_{2n}.

Note also that

(2.6)

w_{3}I_{n}=(w_{3}g_{n-2},w_{3}g_{n-1},w_{3}g_{n})\subseteq I_{n+1},

since
w_{3}g_{n-2}=w_{2}g_{n-1}+g_{n+1}\in I_{n+1}
(by (
2.3
)).

We now state and prove some additional identities in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3}]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For all nonnegative integers

and

we have

g_{2^{i}(r+3)-3}=w_{3}^{2^{i}-1}g_{r}^{2^{i}}.

Proof.

Our proof is by induction on
i\geq 0
. The case
i=0
is trivial,
while the case
i=1
is in fact

[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
.

So, we assume that the identity holds for some
i\geq 1
and prove it for
i+1
.
Then, using the base case and inductional hypothesis we get

g_{2^{i+1}(r+3)-3}=w_{3}g_{2^{i}(r+3)-3}^{2}=w_{3}\left(w_{3}^{2^{i}-1}g_{r}^{2^{i}}\right)^{2}=w_{3}^{2^{i+1}-1}g_{r}^{2^{i+1}},

and we are done.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

For every
n\geq 1
one has:

g_{2n}=g_{n}^{2}+w_{2}g_{n-1}^{2}.

Proof.

Our proof is by induction on
n
. By looking at Table
1

holds for
n\in\{1,2,3\}
. So, suppose that it is true for all
m\in\{1,\ldots,n\}

and let us prove it for

. By (

) and the inductional hypothesis
we have

\displaystyle g_{2n+2}

\displaystyle=w_{2}g_{2n}+w_{3}g_{2n-1}=w_{2}(w_{2}g_{2n-2}+w_{3}g_{2n-3})+w_{3}(w_{2}g_{2n-3}+w_{3}g_{2n-4})

\displaystyle=w_{2}^{2}g_{2n-2}+w_{3}^{2}g_{2n-4}=w_{2}^{2}(g_{n-1}^{2}+w_{2}g_{n-2}^{2})+w_{3}^{2}(g_{n-2}^{2}+w_{2}g_{n-3}^{2})

\displaystyle=(w_{2}g_{n-1}+w_{3}g_{n-2})^{2}+w_{2}(w_{2}g_{n-2}+w_{3}g_{n-3})^{2}=g_{n+1}^{2}+w_{2}g_{n}^{2},

which completes our proof.
∎

The subalgebra
W_{n}
is strictly smaller than
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
. In particular, it does not contain the nontrivial cohomology class in the top dimension (see e.g.
[

). Put in other words, since the dimension of the manifold
\widetilde{G}_{n,3}
is
3n-9
, the following implication holds:

(2.7)

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}\neq 0\mbox{ in }H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2b+3c<3n-9.

2.2.
Gröbner basis for the ideal
I_{n}

In
[
3
]
Gröbner bases for the ideals
I_{n}
,
n\geq 7
, were obtained. These bases are with respect to the lexicographic monomial ordering in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3}]
in which
w_{2}
is greater than
w_{3}
. So, the exponents of
w_{2}
are first compared, and if they are equal, then one compares the exponents of
w_{3}
.

For an integer
n\geq 7
this Gröbner basis for the ideal
I_{n}=(g_{n-2},g_{n-1},g_{n})
is given as follows. First,
n
is placed between two adjacent powers of two, more precisely:
2^{t}-1\leq n<2^{t+1}-1
for some integer
t\geq 3
. Then one takes binary digits
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{t-1}
of the number
n-2^{t}+1
:

n-2^{t}+1=\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\alpha_{j}2^{j};

and for
-1\leq i\leq t-1
defines
s_{i}:=\sum_{j=0}^{i}\alpha_{j}2^{j}
(so
s_{-1}=0
). The Gröbner basis is now specified in the following theorem
[
3
, Theorem 3.14]
.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
3
]
)
.

The set
F_{n}=\{f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{t-1}\}
is a Gröbner basis for
I_{n}
(with respect to the specified monomial ordering), where

f_{i}=w_{3}^{\alpha_{i}s_{i-1}}g_{n-2+2^{i}-s_{i}},\quad 0\leq i\leq t-1.

The leading monomials of the polynomials from
F_{n}
are calculated in
[
3
, Proposition 3.9]
:

\mathrm{LM}(f_{i})=w_{2}^{\frac{n+1-s_{i}}{2}-2^{i}}w_{3}^{\alpha_{i}s_{i-1}+2^{i}-1},\quad 0\leq i\leq t-1.

Also, it is not hard to check (or to find in the proof of
[
3
, Proposition 3.9]
) that
(n+1-s_{i})/2-2^{i}
is an integer divisible by
2^{i}
; more precisely,

(2.8)

\mathrm{LM}(f_{i})=w_{2}^{2^{i}l_{i}}w_{3}^{\alpha_{i}s_{i-1}+2^{i}-1},\quad 0\leq i\leq t-1,

where

l_{i}=2^{t-1-i}+\sum_{j=i+1}^{t-1}\alpha_{j}2^{j-i-1}-1.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{n}
be the set of all cohomology classes of the form
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}\in W_{n}\subset H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
such that the corresponding monomial
w_{2}^{b}w_{3}^{c}
is not divisible by any of the leading monomials
\mathrm{LM}(f_{i})
,
0\leq i\leq t-1
. Then a well-known fact from the theory of Gröbner bases (together with the isomorphism (
2.1
) and Theorem
2.5
) ensures that
\mathcal{B}_{n}
is an additive basis of
W_{n}
.

The main purpose of Gröbner bases is deciding whether a polynomial belongs to the given ideal or not, i.e., whether its coset in the quotient ring is zero or not. This is done by reducing the polynomial using the elements of a Gröbner basis. In the following lemma (which will be used frequently in the next section) we describe the reduction of a monomial
w_{2}^{b}w_{3}^{c}
by the polynomial
f_{i}\in F_{n}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
0\leq i\leq t-1
. If
b
and
c
are nonnegative integers such that
\mathrm{LM}(f_{i})\mid w_{2}^{b}w_{3}^{c}
, i.e., if the monomial
w_{2}^{b}w_{3}^{c}
can be reduced by
f_{i}
, then in
W_{n}
the following equality holds:

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2d+3e=2l_{i}\\
e>0\end{subarray}}\binom{d+e}{e}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b-2^{i}(l_{i}-d)}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c+2^{i}e},

where the sum is taken over all pairs of integers
(d,e)
such that
d\geq 0
,
e>0
and
2d+3e=2l_{i}
.

Proof.

By (
2.5
) it is enough to prove

(2.9)

w_{2}^{b}w_{3}^{c}\equiv\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2d+3e=2l_{i}\\
e>0\end{subarray}}\binom{d+e}{e}w_{2}^{b-2^{i}(l_{i}-d)}w_{3}^{c+2^{i}e}\pmod{I_{n}}.

In order to do so, let us note first that

(2.10)

f_{i}=w_{3}^{\alpha_{i}s_{i-1}}g_{2^{i}(2l_{i}+3)-3}.

This follows from the definition of
f_{i}
(given in Theorem
2.5
) and the equality
n-2+2^{i}-s_{i}=2^{i}(2l_{i}+3)-3
, which can be easily verified (see also the proof of
[
3
, Proposition 3.9]
).

By (
2.8
), the assumption
\mathrm{LM}(f_{i})\mid w_{2}^{b}w_{3}^{c}
actually means that
b\geq 2^{i}l_{i}
and
c\geq\alpha_{i}s_{i-1}+2^{i}-1
. Now we use (
2.10
), Lemma
2.3
and formula (
2.4
) to calculate:

\displaystyle w_{2}^{b}w_{3}^{c}

\displaystyle\equiv w_{2}^{b-2^{i}l_{i}}w_{3}^{c-\left(\alpha_{i}s_{i-1}+2^{i}-1\right)}\left(f_{i}+w_{2}^{2^{i}l_{i}}w_{3}^{\alpha_{i}s_{i-1}+2^{i}-1}\right)

\displaystyle=w_{2}^{b-2^{i}l_{i}}w_{3}^{c-\left(\alpha_{i}s_{i-1}+2^{i}-1\right)}\left(w_{3}^{\alpha_{i}s_{i-1}}g_{2^{i}(2l_{i}+3)-3}+w_{2}^{2^{i}l_{i}}w_{3}^{\alpha_{i}s_{i-1}+2^{i}-1}\right)

\displaystyle=w_{2}^{b-2^{i}l_{i}}w_{3}^{c}\left(g_{2l_{i}}^{2^{i}}+w_{2}^{2^{i}l_{i}}\right)=w_{2}^{b-2^{i}l_{i}}w_{3}^{c}\left(g_{2l_{i}}+w_{2}^{l_{i}}\right)^{2^{i}}

\displaystyle=w_{2}^{b-2^{i}l_{i}}w_{3}^{c}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2d+3e=2l_{i}\\
e>0\end{subarray}}\binom{d+e}{e}w_{2}^{2^{i}d}w_{3}^{2^{i}e}\pmod{I_{n}},

and (
2.9
) follows.
∎

2.3.
Background on zero-divisor cup-length

Let
A
be a graded commutative
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-algebra with identity. The elements of the kernel of the multiplication map
A\otimes A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle.}}{{\longrightarrow}}A
are called zero-divisors. If
a\in A
is an arbitrary element, then an obvious zero-divisor is

z(a):=a\otimes 1+1\otimes a.

It is straightforward that for
a,b\in A
one has

(2.11)

z(a+b)=z(a)+z(b),

and note that the following rule holds:

(2.12)

z(ab)=z(a)z(b)+(1\otimes b)z(a)+(1\otimes a)z(b).

Namely,

\displaystyle z(a)z(b)

\displaystyle=(a\otimes 1+1\otimes a)(b\otimes 1+1\otimes b)=ab\otimes 1+1\otimes ab+a\otimes b+b\otimes a

\displaystyle=z(ab)+(1\otimes b)(a\otimes 1+1\otimes a)+(1\otimes a)(b\otimes 1+1\otimes b)

\displaystyle=z(ab)+(1\otimes b)z(a)+(1\otimes a)z(b).

When
a=b
, (
2.12
) simplifies to
z(a^{2})=z(a)^{2}
, and this routinely generalizes to

(2.13)

z\big(a^{2^{l}}\big)=z(a)^{2^{l}}\qquad\mbox{for all }l\geq 0.

The zero-divisor cup-length of
A
,
\operatorname{zcl}(A)
, is the maximal number of zero-divisors of positive degree with nonzero product in
A\otimes A
. Since the ideal
\ker\big(A\otimes A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle.}}{{\longrightarrow}}A\big)
is generated by the elements
z(a)
,
a\in A

[
2
, Lemma 5.2]
,
\operatorname{zcl}(A)
is reached by a product of the form
z(a_{1})z(a_{2})\cdots z(a_{m})
, where
a_{i}\in A
,
1\leq i\leq m
, are elements of positive degree.

An element of positive degree in
A
is indecomposable if it cannot be expressed as a polynomial in elements of smaller degree. Now, using (
2.11
) and (
2.12
), we see that
\operatorname{zcl}(A)
is in fact reached by a product
z(a_{1})z(a_{2})\cdots z(a_{m})
, where
a_{i}\in A
,
1\leq i\leq m
, are indecomposable elements.

Recall that the height of an algebra element
x
is the maximal integer
d\geq 1
such that
x^{d}\neq 0
. We denote the height of
x
by
0pt(x)
. By
[
9
, Lemma 4.3]
for an element
a\in A
the following implication holds:

(2.14)

2^{t}\leq 0pt(a)<2^{t+1}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 0pt(z(a))=2^{t+1}-1.

The heights of the Stiefel-Whitney
classes
\widetilde{w}_{2}
and
\widetilde{w}_{3}
(in
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
) are obtained in
[
3
, Theorems 1.2 and 1.3]
(see also
[
3
, Table 1]
). This
result, combined with (
2.14
), will be very important in our calculations.

Theorem 2.7

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
n\geq 7
and
t\geq 3
be integers such that
2^{t}-1\leq n<2^{t+1}-1

\displaystyle 0pt(\widetilde{w}_{2})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2^{t}-4,&2^{t}-1\leq n\leq 2^{t}+2^{t-1}\\
2^{t+1}-3\cdot 2^{s}-1,&2^{t+1}-2^{s+1}+1\leq n\leq 2^{t+1}-2^{s}\quad(1\leq s\leq t-2)\\
\end{cases},

\displaystyle 0pt(\widetilde{w}_{3})

\displaystyle=\max\{2^{t-1}-2,n-2^{t}-1\}.

3.
The zero-divisor cup-length of
W_{n}

In this section we prove our results on
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})
. By definition of
W_{n}
, the only indecomposable elements in this algebra are
\widetilde{w}_{2}
and
\widetilde{w}_{3}
, so
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})
is realized by a product of the form
z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}
. This fact will be used throughout the paper.

We begin by showing that
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})
increases with
n
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For all integers
n\geq 6
one has

\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})\leq\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n+1}).

Proof.

Let
m=\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n+1})
. It suffices to prove that
z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}=0
in
W_{n}\otimes W_{n}
, whenever
\beta+\gamma>m
.

Since
I_{n+1}\subseteq I_{n}
, the identity map on
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3}]
induces the algebra morphism

\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3}]/I_{n+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3}]/I_{n},

and using the isomorphism (
2.1
) we get a map
\phi:W_{n+1}\rightarrow W_{n}
mapping
\widetilde{w}_{i}\in W_{n+1}
to
\widetilde{w}_{i}\in W_{n}
,
i=2,3
. Note that then the algebra morphism

\phi\otimes\phi:W_{n+1}\otimes W_{n+1}\rightarrow W_{n}\otimes W_{n}

maps
z(\widetilde{w}_{i})\in W_{n+1}\otimes W_{n+1}
to
z(\phi(\widetilde{w}_{i}))=z(\widetilde{w}_{i})\in W_{n}\otimes W_{n}
,
i=2,3
. However, if
\beta+\gamma>m
, then
z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}=0
in
W_{n+1}\otimes W_{n+1}
(since
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n+1})=m
), and so

z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}=(\phi\otimes\phi)\big(z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}\big)=(\phi\otimes\phi)(0)=0\quad\mbox{ in }W_{n}\otimes W_{n},

completing the proof.
∎

The rest of this section is devoted to proving Theorem
3.2
. Throughout the proof we will use Lemma
3.1
. For example, by this lemma, to prove
that
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})=2^{t}+2^{t-1}-4
for
2^{t}-1\leq n\leq 2^{t}+2^{t-2}
, it is enough to
prove that
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{2^{t}-1})\geq 2^{t}+2^{t-1}-4
and

\operatorname{zcl}(W_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}})\leq 2^{t}+2^{t-1}-4
. The results of the first type will be referred
to as ”the lower bound” and the results of the second type as ”the upper bound”.

The lower bounds will be established by detecting a nonzero monomial of the form
z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}\in W_{n}\otimes W_{n}
. In proving that such a monomial is nonzero we will rely on the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\beta
,
\gamma
and
r
be nonnegative integers such that
r\leq 2\beta+3\gamma
. Observe the element

(3.1)

\sum_{2b+3c=r}\binom{\beta}{b}\binom{\gamma}{c}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{\beta-b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{\gamma-c}

in
W_{n}\otimes W_{n}\subset H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})\otimes H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
, where the sum is taken over all pairs of integers
(b,c)
such that
0\leq b\leq\beta
,
0\leq c\leq\gamma
and
2b+3c=r
. If (
3.1
) is nonzero, then
z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}\neq 0
, and so

\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})\geq\beta+\gamma.

Proof.

Since

z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}\in\big(H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})\otimes H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})\big)_{2\beta+3\gamma}=\bigoplus_{r=0}^{2\beta+3\gamma}H^{r}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})\otimes H^{2\beta+3\gamma-r}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3}),

it suffices to find
r\in\{0,1,\ldots,2\beta+3\gamma\}
such that the summand of
z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}
in
H^{r}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})\otimes H^{2\beta+3\gamma-r}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
is nonzero. It remains to see that this summand is exactly (
3.1
), which is obvious from the following calculation:

\displaystyle z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}\otimes 1+1\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}\big)^{\beta}\big(\widetilde{w}_{3}\otimes 1+1\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}\big)^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b=0}^{\beta}\binom{\beta}{b}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{\beta-b}\sum_{c=0}^{\gamma}\binom{\gamma}{c}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{\gamma-c}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b=0}^{\beta}\sum_{c=0}^{\gamma}\binom{\beta}{b}\binom{\gamma}{c}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{\beta-b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{\gamma-c}.

∎

Also, in the upcoming computations we will repeatedly use the following fact (which holds because we are working over a field): for
\sigma,\tau\in H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
we have that
\sigma\otimes\tau\neq 0
in
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})\otimes H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
if and only if
\sigma\neq 0
and
\tau\neq 0
in
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
.

We break the proof of Theorem
3.2
in seven cases, as listed in the theorem, but the
strategy in each of them is quite similar.

3.1.
The case
\mathbf{2^{t}-1\leq n\leq 2^{t}+2^{t-2}}

In order to establish the lower bound in this case, we start off by identifying the elements of the Gröbner basis
F_{2^{t}-1}
(from Theorem
2.5
) and their leading monomials.

Lemma 3.5
.

For
n=2^{t}-1
, where
t\geq 3
, we have:

f_{i}=g_{2^{t}+2^{i}-3}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathrm{LM}(f_{i})=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2^{i}}w_{3}^{2^{i}-1},\quad\mbox{for }0\leq i\leq t-1.

Proof.

We have
n-2^{t}+1=0
, and hence
\alpha_{i}=0
and
s_{i}=0
for all

i
. So, the lemma follows from the definition of the
polynomials
f_{i}
and (
2.8
).
∎

Now we consider the monomials of the form
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}
in
H^{2^{t+1}-11}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
and deduce which of them are nonzero.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
n=2^{t}-1
,
t\geq 3
. The only nonzero monomials of the form
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}
in
H^{2^{t+1}-11}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
are
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{k}-3}
for
2\leq k\leq t-1
. Furthermore, all of them are equal, i.e., for all
k\in\{2,\ldots,t-1\}
we have the equality

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{k}-3}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-3},

and the monomial
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-3}
is in the additive basis
\mathcal{B}_{n}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.5
, none of
\mathrm{LM}(f_{i})
, for
0\leq i\leq t-1
,
divides
w_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}w_{3}^{2^{t-1}-3}
, so
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-3}\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
. In particular,
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-3}\neq 0
. Also, since
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{3})=2^{t-1}-2
(by Theorem
2.7
),
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=0
if
c>2^{t-1}-2
.

By using backward induction on
k
, where
2\leq k\leq t-1
, we prove that

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}
, with
2b+3c=2^{t+1}-11
and
2^{k}-3\leq c<2^{k+1}-3
, is
nonzero if and only if
c=2^{k}-3
, and that
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{k}-3}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-3}
. Note that
2b+3c=2^{t+1}-11

implies
c
is odd, and we will use this throughout the proof.

The induction base (
k=t-1
) follows from the first paragraph of the proof (and the fact that
c
is odd).

Now, let
2\leq k\leq t-2
and
2^{k}-3\leq c\leq 2^{k+1}-5
.

Suppose first that
c>2^{k}-3
. We are going to reduce the monomial
w_{2}^{b}w_{3}^{c}
by the polynomial
f_{k}
. This can be done, because
\mathrm{LM}(f_{k})=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2^{k}}w_{3}^{2^{k}-1}
(Lemma
3.5
),

b=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\left(2^{t+1}-11-3c\right)\geq 2^{t}-3\cdot 2^{k}+2>2^{t-1}-2^{k}\quad\mbox{and}\quad c\geq 2^{k}-1.

According to Lemma
2.6
we have (
l_{k}=2^{t-1-k}-1
since
\alpha_{j}=0
for all
j
):

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2d+3e=2^{t-k}-2\\
e>0\end{subarray}}\binom{d+e}{e}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b-2^{k}(2^{t-1-k}-1-d)}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c+2^{k}e}.

Notice that in each summand from this sum
e
is even, and therefore
e\geq 2
, which leads to

c+2^{k}e\geq c+2^{k+1}>2^{k+1}-3
. So we can apply inductive hypothesis to
conclude that if the term
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b-2^{k}(2^{t-1-k}-1-d)}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c+2^{k}e}
is nonzero, then

c+2^{k}e=2^{m}-3
for some
m>k+1
. It follows that
c\equiv-3\pmod{2^{k+1}}
, which is
impossible since
2^{k}-1\leq c\leq 2^{k+1}-5
. Therefore, all summands in the last
sum are zero, and hence
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=0
.

Suppose now
c=2^{k}-3
. In this case we reduce
w_{2}^{b}w_{3}^{c}
by
f_{k-1}
. Again, this is possible since
\mathrm{LM}(f_{k-1})=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2^{k-1}}w_{3}^{2^{k-1}-1}
(Lemma

3.5
),

b=2^{t}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1>2^{t-1}-2^{k-1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad c=2^{k}-3\geq 2^{k-1}-1.

Using again Lemma
2.6
we get:

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2d+3e=2^{t-k+1}-2\\
e>0\end{subarray}}\binom{d+e}{e}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b-2^{k-1}(2^{t-k}-1-d)}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c+2^{k-1}e}.

Similarly as in the first case, we have that

c+2^{k-1}e\geq c+2^{k}=2^{k+1}-3
. So, for a nonzero summand
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b-2^{k-1}(2^{t-k}-1-d)}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c+2^{k-1}e}
one has
c+2^{k-1}e=2^{m}-3
, for some
m\geq k+1
. Then
e=2^{m-k+1}-2
and

d+e=2^{t-k}-2^{m-k}
. If
m\geq k+2
, then
e\equiv 2\pmod{4}
and
d+e\equiv 0\pmod{4}
,
and hence, by Lucas’ theorem,
\binom{d+e}{e}
is zero. Therefore,
only the term with
m=k+1
is possibly nonzero. Then
e=2
and
d+e=2^{t-k}-2
, and
hence
\binom{d+e}{e}
is nonzero by Lucas’ theorem, so

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{k}-3}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b-2^{k-1}(2^{t-k}-1-d)}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c+2^{k-1}e}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-3\cdot 2^{k}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{k+1}-3},

which is equal to
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-3}
by the induction hypothesis.
∎

Next, we prove a lemma (and its consequence) that will be used in subsequent cases as well.

Lemma 3.7
.

For
t\geq 4
we have

g_{3\cdot 2^{t-1}}+w_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{t-2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{t-3}w_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{k-1}}w_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2^{k}}\in w_{3}I_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+2^{t-4}}.

Proof.

Our proof is by induction on
t\geq 4
. By looking at Table
1
, we see that for
t=4
the claim is

w_{3}I_{21}\ni g_{24}+w_{2}^{12}+w_{2}^{3}w_{3}^{6}=w_{2}^{9}w_{3}^{2}+w_{2}^{3}w_{3}^{6}+w_{3}^{8}=w_{3}(w_{2}g_{19}+g_{21}),

which is true (since
g_{19},g_{21}\in I_{21}
). So, suppose that it is true for some

t\geq 4
and let us prove it for
t+1
. By Lemma
2.2
, then we have

\displaystyle w_{3}I_{2^{t+1}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}}

\displaystyle\ni g_{3\cdot 2^{t-1}}^{2}+w_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{t-1}}+\sum_{k=1}^{t-3}w_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{k}}w_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{k+1}}

\displaystyle=g_{3\cdot 2^{t-1}}^{2}+w_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{t-1}}+\sum_{k=2}^{t-2}w_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{k-1}}w_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{k}},

and hence it is enough to prove

g_{3\cdot 2^{t}}+g_{3\cdot 2^{t-1}}^{2}+w_{2}^{3}w_{3}^{2^{t}-2}\in w_{3}I_{2^{t+1}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}}.

By Lemma
2.4
, (
2.3
), Lemma
2.1
(b), and Lemma

2.3
, we have

\displaystyle g_{3\cdot 2^{t}}+g_{3\cdot 2^{t-1}}^{2}+w_{2}^{3}w_{3}^{2^{t}-2}

\displaystyle=w_{2}g_{3\cdot 2^{t-1}-1}^{2}+w_{2}^{3}w_{3}^{2^{t}-2}

\displaystyle=w_{2}(w_{2}^{2}g_{3\cdot 2^{t-1}-3}^{2}+w_{3}^{2}g_{3\cdot 2^{t-1}-4}^{2})+w_{2}^{3}w_{3}^{2^{t}-2}

\displaystyle=w_{2}w_{3}^{2}g_{3\cdot 2^{t-1}-4}^{2}=w_{2}w_{3}g_{3\cdot 2^{t}-5}.

Since
3\cdot 2^{t}-5\geq 2^{t+1}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}-2
, we have

g_{3\cdot 2^{t}-5}\in I_{2^{t+1}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}}
, which completes our proof.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

For
t\geq 3
one has

w_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{t-2}}\equiv\sum_{k=1}^{t-3}w_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{k-1}}w_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2^{k}}\pmod{I_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+2}}.

(Note that the left-hand side of this congruence corresponds to the (nonexisting) summand for
k=t-1
in the sum on the right-hand side.)

Proof.

For
t=3
it is understood that the sum on the right-hand side is zero, and we actually need to check that
w_{2}^{6}\in I_{12}
. But
w_{2}^{6}=w_{2}g_{10}\in I_{12}
(see Table
1
).

For
t\geq 4
one has
2^{t}+2^{t-2}+2^{t-4}\geq 2^{t}+2^{t-2}+1
, and so
w_{3}I_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+2^{t-4}}\subseteq w_{3}I_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+1}\subseteq I_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+2}
(by (
2.6
)). Now, Lemma
3.7
gives us

w_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{t-2}}\equiv g_{3\cdot 2^{t-1}}+\sum_{k=1}^{t-3}w_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{k-1}}w_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2^{k}}\pmod{I_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+2}}.

Moreover,
g_{3\cdot 2^{t-1}}\in I_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+2}
(since
3\cdot 2^{t-1}>2^{t}+2^{t-2}
), and we are done.
∎

Now we are ready to establish the lower bound for
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})
in this case.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
2^{t}-1\leq n\leq 2^{t}+2^{t-2}
, where
t\geq 4
. Then

\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})\geq 2^{t}+2^{t-1}-4.

Proof.

Note that by Lemma
3.1
, it is enough to prove the inequality for

n=2^{t}-1
. We apply Lemma
3.4
for
\beta=2^{t}-1
,
\gamma=2^{t-1}-3
and
r=2^{t+1}-11
. We thus need to prove that

\sum_{2b+3c=2^{t+1}-11}\binom{2^{t}-1}{b}\binom{2^{t-1}-3}{c}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-1-b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-3-c}\neq 0

in
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})\otimes H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
. By Lemma
3.6
, we only need to consider the summands with

(b,c)=(2^{t}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1,2^{k}-3)
, for
2\leq k\leq t-1
, and for each of them

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-3}
. So, by Lucas’ theorem, equivalence (
2.5
), the fact
I_{n}=I_{2^{t}-1}\supseteq I_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+2}
and Corollary

3.8
, the last sum becomes

\displaystyle\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-3}\otimes

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{t-1}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{k-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2^{k}}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-3}\otimes\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{t-3}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}+\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2}\big).

We know that the first coordinate of the last tensor is nonzero, and since we are working over a field, it is enough to prove that the second coordinate is nonzero too.
By Lemmas
3.5
and
2.1
(c), we have

f_{t-2}=g_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}-3}=w_{2}^{2^{t-2}}w_{3}^{2^{t-2}-1}
,
and hence
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{t-3}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}=0
, while

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2}\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
, since, by Lemma
3.5
,
none of
\mathrm{LM}(f_{i})
, for
0\leq i\leq t-1
, divides
w_{2}^{3}w_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2}
(here we use the assumption
t\geq 4
). This completes our proof.
∎

As the first step towards establishing the upper bound in this case, we display the elements of the Gröbner basis
F_{n}
for
n=2^{t}+2^{t-2}
. Then, in Lemma
3.11
we prove a technical result, which is crucial for obtaining the upper bound. Also, since the corresponding proof in the next case goes along the same lines as this one, here we present them together by including the case
n=2^{t}+2^{t-2}+1
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
n=2^{t}+2^{t-2}+\varepsilon
, where
t\geq 4
and
\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}
.
Then for the elements of the Gröbner basis
F_{n}
(from Theorem
2.5
) one has:

•

f_{0}=g_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+2\varepsilon-2}
,
\mathrm{LM}(f_{0})=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}+\varepsilon-1}
;

•

f_{i}=w_{3}^{2^{i}-1}\left(g_{2^{t-i}+2^{t-i-2}-2}\right)^{2^{i}}
,
\mathrm{LM}(f_{i})=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}-2^{i}}w_{3}^{2^{i}-1}
, for
1\leq i\leq t-3
;

•

f_{t-3}=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}}w_{3}^{2^{t-3}-1}+w_{2}^{2^{t-3}}w_{3}^{2^{t-2}+2^{t-3}-1}
;

•

f_{t-2}=w_{2}^{2^{t-2}}w_{3}^{2^{t-2}+\varepsilon}
,
f_{t-1}=w_{3}^{2^{t-1}-1}
.

Proof.

We have
n-2^{t}+1=2^{t-2}+1+\varepsilon
, and hence
\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{3}=\dots=\alpha_{t-3}=0
,

\alpha_{t-2}=1
,
\alpha_{t-1}=0
, and
s_{1}=s_{2}=\dots=s_{t-3}=1+\varepsilon
,

s_{t-2}=s_{t-1}=2^{t-2}+1+\varepsilon
. Furthermore,
\alpha_{0}=s_{0}=1-\varepsilon
and
\alpha_{1}=\varepsilon
.

It is now straightforward from the definition of
f_{i}
(given in Theorem
2.5
) that
f_{0}=g_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+2\varepsilon-2}
and

f_{i}=g_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+2^{i}-3}=w_{3}^{2^{i}-1}\left(g_{2^{t-i}+2^{t-i-2}-2}\right)^{2^{i}}\qquad\mbox{for }1\leq i\leq t-3,

by Lemma
2.3
. In particular, for
i=t-3
we get

f_{t-3}=w_{3}^{2^{t-3}-1}g_{8}^{2^{t-3}}=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}}w_{3}^{2^{t-3}-1}+w_{2}^{2^{t-3}}w_{3}^{2^{t-2}+2^{t-3}-1}

(see Table
1
). Also,

f_{t-2}=w_{3}^{1+\varepsilon}g_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}-3}=w_{2}^{2^{t-2}}w_{3}^{2^{t-2}+\varepsilon}\mbox{ and }f_{t-1}=g_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}-3}=w_{3}^{2^{t-1}-1},

by Lemma
2.1
(parts (c) and (b) respectively).

The statements about leading monomials follow from the fact
\mathrm{LM}(g_{2l})=w_{2}^{l}
(which is easily seen from (
2.4
)).
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
t\geq 4
and
n=2^{t}+2^{t-2}+\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}
.
Then in
W_{n}\otimes W_{n}
one has:

(a)

if
\varepsilon=0
, then
z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t}-1}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-1}-2}=0
and

z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t}-2}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-1}-1}=0
;

(b)

if
\varepsilon=1
, then
z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t}-1}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-1}-1}=0
.

Proof.

Let us consider
z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t}-\varepsilon^{\prime}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-1}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}
, where

(\varepsilon^{\prime},\varepsilon^{\prime\prime})\in\{(1,2),(2,1)\}
in part (a), while

\varepsilon^{\prime}=\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}=1
in part (b). We want to prove that this element is zero in
W_{n}\otimes W_{n}
.

First, let us show that

(3.2)

z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t}-\varepsilon^{\prime}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-1}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-3}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}\cdot A,

where

A=\sum_{k=1}^{t-3}\Big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{k-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2^{k}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{3\cdot 2^{t-3}}+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{3\cdot 2^{t-3}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{k-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2^{k}}\Big).

We will use the relation

(3.3)

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}-1}=0,

which holds in
W_{n}
. Namely, according to Lemma
3.10
,
f_{t-1}=w_{3}^{2^{t-1}-1}
and
f_{t-3}=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}}w_{3}^{2^{t-3}-1}+w_{2}^{2^{t-3}}w_{3}^{2^{t-2}+2^{t-3}-1}
, so
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}-1}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-3}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-1}=0
.

By (
2.13
) we have

\displaystyle z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t}-\varepsilon^{\prime}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-1}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}\!\!

\displaystyle=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t-1}-\varepsilon^{\prime}}z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t-1}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-2}}

\displaystyle=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t-1}-\varepsilon^{\prime}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}z\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\big)z\big(\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}\big)

\displaystyle=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t-1}-\varepsilon^{\prime}}\!z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}\!\!\Big(\!\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}\!\!+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\!\Big),

since
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}=0
(by (
3.3
)).
If we denote our element
z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t}-\varepsilon^{\prime}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-1}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}
by
x
, we thus have

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}z\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}}\big)\Big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\Big)

\displaystyle=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}\Big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}\Big).

The latter equality is due to the fact

y:=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}\Big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}+\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\Big)=0.

Namely, by Lemma
3.10
,
f_{t-2}=w_{2}^{2^{t-2}}w_{3}^{2^{t-2}+\varepsilon}
, so for

\varepsilon=0
we clearly have
y=0
. If
\varepsilon=1
(in that case
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}=1
), then the fact
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}+1}=0
and the expansion
z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-2}-1}=\sum_{i+j=2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{i}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{j}
lead to

y=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t-2}-1}\Big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}-1}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}+\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}w_{3}^{2^{t-2}-1}\Big)=0,

by (
3.3
). Therefore,

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-3}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}})\Big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}\Big)

\displaystyle=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-3}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}\Big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{t-2}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}+2^{t-3}}+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}+2^{t-3}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{t-2}}\Big),

since
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}+2^{t-3}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}+2^{t-3}}=0
(by using the Gröbner basis elements
f_{t-3}
and
f_{t-2}
). Finally, (
3.2
) now follows from Corollary
3.8
(and the fact
I_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+2}\subseteq I_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+\varepsilon}
).

Now, by (
3.2
) we have
x=x_{1}+x_{2}
, where

x_{1}:=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-3}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}\sum_{k=1}^{t-3}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{k-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2^{k}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{3\cdot 2^{t-3}}

and

x_{2}:=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-3}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}\sum_{k=1}^{t-3}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{3\cdot 2^{t-3}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{k-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2^{k}}.

We want to prove that
x_{1}=x_{2}
. If we expand
x_{1}
by binomial formula, we obtain summands of the form

\binom{2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime}}{i}\binom{2^{t-3}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}{j}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{k-1}+i}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2^{k}+j}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime}-i}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}-j},

where
1\leq k\leq t-3
,
0\leq i\leq 2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime}
and
0\leq j\leq 2^{t-3}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}
.
Let us first observe

\sigma(k,i,j):=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{k-1}+i}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2^{k}+j}.

Note that
2^{t-1}-2^{k}+j\geq 2^{t-1}-2^{t-3}>2^{t-2}+\varepsilon
, so, if

3\cdot 2^{k-1}+i\geq 2^{t-2}
, then
\sigma(k,i,j)=0
(since,
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}+\varepsilon}=0
). Therefore, we may assume
i\leq 2^{t-2}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1
. Similarly,
if
2^{t-1}-2^{k}+j\geq 2^{t-1}-1
, then
\sigma(k,i,j)=0
(since
\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-1}=0
), so we can shrink the interval for
j
as well:
0\leq j\leq 2^{k}-2
. Moreover, for the same reason, in order for

\tau(k,i,j):=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime}-i}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}-j}

to be nonzero, one must have
2^{t-1}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}-j\leq 2^{t-1}-2
, i.e.,
j\geq 2-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}
. Finally, we conclude that

x_{1}=\sum_{k=1}^{t-3}\,\,\sum_{i=1-\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{2^{t-2}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1}\sum_{j=2-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}^{2^{k}-2}\binom{2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime}}{i}\binom{2^{t-3}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}{j}\sigma(k,i,j)\otimes\tau(k,i,j).

For technical reasons (which will be clear soon) we set the lower boundary for
i
to be
1-\varepsilon^{\prime}
, and it is understood that
\binom{2^{t-2}-2}{-1}=0
(if
\varepsilon^{\prime}=2
).

By the same token, the corresponding expansion of
x_{2}
is the following:

x_{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{t-3}\,\,\sum_{\overline{i}=1-\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{2^{t-2}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1}\sum_{\overline{j}=2-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}^{2^{k}-2}\binom{2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime}}{\overline{i}}\binom{2^{t-3}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}{\overline{j}}\tau(k,\overline{i},\overline{j})\otimes\sigma(k,\overline{i},\overline{j}).

The idea now is to note that the change of variables
\overline{i}:=2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-i
and
\overline{j}:=2^{k}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}-j
transforms the sum
x_{1}
to the sum
x_{2}
, leading to the conclusion
x_{1}=x_{2}
. Obviously,
1-\varepsilon^{\prime}\leq i\leq 2^{t-2}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1
is equivalent to
1-\varepsilon^{\prime}\leq\overline{i}\leq 2^{t-2}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1
, and likewise,
2-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}\leq j\leq 2^{k}-2
is equivalent to
2-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}\leq\overline{j}\leq 2^{k}-2
. Also it is routine to check that
\sigma(k,i,j)=\tau(k,\overline{i},\overline{j})
and
\tau(k,i,j)=\sigma(k,\overline{i},\overline{j})
, so it remains to establish the congruence

\binom{2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime}}{i}\binom{2^{t-3}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}{j}\equiv\binom{2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime}}{\overline{i}}\binom{2^{t-3}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}}{\overline{j}}\pmod{2}.

Since for all
l\in\{0,1,\ldots,2^{m}-1\}
,
\binom{2^{m}-1}{l}\equiv 1\pmod{2}
, and
\binom{2^{m}-2}{l}\equiv 1\pmod{2}
if and only if
l
is even, this amounts to showing that if
\varepsilon^{\prime}=2
, then
i
and
\overline{i}
are of the same parity, and if
\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}=2
, then
j
and
\overline{j}
are of the same parity. The latter implication is clear from
\overline{j}=2^{k}-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}-j=2^{k}-2-j
. For the former one, from
\overline{i}=2^{t-2}-\varepsilon^{\prime}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-i=2^{t-2}-2-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-i
we see that the only problem is the case
k=1
(and
\varepsilon^{\prime}=2
). However, this case is impossible (more precisely, then the summand for
k=1
is zero in both sums), since
\varepsilon^{\prime}=2
implies
\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}=1
(see the very beginning of the proof), and we would have
1=2-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}\leq j\leq 2^{k}-2=0
.
∎

We are finally able to conclude the proof of Theorem
3.2
in this case, by verifying the inequality
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})\leq 2^{t}+2^{t-1}-4
(the opposite inequality is proved in Proposition
3.9
).

Proposition 3.12
.

For
t\geq 4
and
2^{t}-1\leq n\leq 2^{t}+2^{t-2}
one has

\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})\leq 2^{t}+2^{t-1}-4.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
it is enough to prove

\operatorname{zcl}(W_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}})\leq 2^{t}+2^{t-1}-4
. Suppose to the contrary that for

n=2^{t}+2^{t-2}
there are integers
\beta,\gamma\geq 0
such that

z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}\neq 0\quad\mbox{in }W_{n}\otimes W_{n}\quad\quad\mbox{and}\quad\beta+\gamma=2^{t}+2^{t-1}-3.

According to Theorem
2.7
,
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{2})=2^{t}-4
and
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{3})=2^{t-1}-2
.
So, by (
2.14
),

0pt(z(\widetilde{w}_{2}))=2^{t}-1
and

0pt(z(\widetilde{w}_{3}))=2^{t-1}-1
, which implies
\beta\leq 2^{t}-1
and
\gamma\leq 2^{t-1}-1
. Hence

(\beta,\gamma)\in\{(2^{t}-1,2^{t-1}-2),(2^{t}-2,2^{t-1}-1)\}
, which contradicts
Lemma
3.11
(a).
∎

3.2.
The case
\mathbf{n=2^{t}+2^{t-2}+1}

As we have already mentioned, the strategy of the proof is more or less the same in every case. So, in order to establish the lower bound, we use Lemma
3.4
. We are going to pick the cohomology dimension
r
in which we will be able to suitably sort all nonzero monomials (as we did in Lemma
3.6
in the previous case). This will be done in Lemma
3.14
. However, similarly as in some points in the previous case, the corresponding claim for the next case is proved in literally the same way, so we include the case
n=2^{t}+2^{t-2}+2
in Lemma
3.14
. For that, we will first need to identify the members of the Gröbner basis
F_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+2}
(for
F_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+1}
this was done in Lemma
3.10
).

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
n=2^{t}+2^{t-2}+2
, where
t\geq 4
.
Then for the elements of the Gröbner basis
F_{n}
(from Theorem
2.5
) one has:

•

f_{0}=g_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}}
,
\mathrm{LM}(f_{0})=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}}
;

•

f_{1}=w_{3}^{2}\left(g_{2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}-2}\right)^{2}
,
\mathrm{LM}(f_{1})=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}-2}w_{3}^{2}
;

•

f_{i}=w_{3}^{2^{i}-1}\left(g_{2^{t-i}+2^{t-i-2}-2}\right)^{2^{i}}
,
\mathrm{LM}(f_{i})=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}-2^{i}}w_{3}^{2^{i}-1}
, for
2\leq i\leq t-3
;

•

f_{t-3}=\begin{cases}w_{2}^{8}w_{3}^{2}+w_{2}^{2}w_{3}^{6},&\,t=4\\
w_{2}^{2^{t-1}}w_{3}^{2^{t-3}-1}+w_{2}^{2^{t-3}}w_{3}^{2^{t-2}+2^{t-3}-1},&\,t\geq 5\end{cases}
;

•

f_{t-2}=w_{2}^{2^{t-2}}w_{3}^{2^{t-2}+2}
,
f_{t-1}=w_{3}^{2^{t-1}-1}
.

Proof.

We have
n-2^{t}+1=2^{t-2}+2+1
, and hence
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{1}=1
,
\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{3}=\dots=\alpha_{t-3}=0
,

\alpha_{t-2}=1
,
\alpha_{t-1}=0
, and
s_{0}=1
,
s_{1}=s_{2}=\dots=s_{t-3}=3
,

s_{t-2}=s_{t-1}=2^{t-2}+3
. Therefore, the polynomials
f_{i}
, for
2\leq i\leq t-1
, can be obtained as in the proof of Lemma
3.10
by putting
\varepsilon=2
. The exceptions are
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
, which we calculate by definition (see Theorem
2.5
) and Lemma
2.3
:

f_{0}=g_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}},\qquad f_{1}=w_{3}g_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1}=w_{3}^{2}\left(g_{2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}-2}\right)^{2}.

The leading monomials are again obtained from the fact
\mathrm{LM}(g_{2l})=w_{2}^{l}
.
∎

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
n=2^{t}+2^{t-2}+\varepsilon
, where
t\geq 5
and
\varepsilon\in\{1,2\}
.
Then the only nonzero monomials of the form
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}
in

H^{2^{t+1}-8}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
are

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{k}-2}
for
1\leq k\leq t-1
. Furthermore, they are all equal, i.e.,

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{k}-2}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2}\quad\mbox{for }1\leq k\leq t-1,

and
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2}\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
.

Proof.

It is obvious by Lemmas
3.10
and
3.13
that
w_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}w_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2}
is not divisible by any of
\mathrm{LM}(f_{i})
for
0\leq i\leq t-1
, so
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2}\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
, and hence
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2}\neq 0
.

According to Theorem
2.7
,
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{3})=2^{t-1}-2
, which means that
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=0
whenever
c>2^{t-1}-2
.

By using backward induction on
k
, where
1\leq k\leq t-1
, we now prove that
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}
, with
2b+3c=2^{t+1}-8
and
2^{k}-2\leq c\leq 2^{k+1}-3
, is nonzero if and only if
c=2^{k}-2
, and that

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{k}-2}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2}
.
Note that
2b+3c=2^{t+1}-8
implies that
c
must be even.

We have already established this claim for
k=t-1
.

Now we deal with the case
k=t-2
,
i.e.,
2^{t-2}-2\leq c\leq 2^{t-1}-4
. Firstly, suppose
c\geq 2^{t-2}+2
. Then

2b=2^{t+1}-8-3c
implies
b\geq 2^{t-2}+2
, and hence

w_{2}^{b}w_{3}^{c}
is divisible by
f_{t-2}=w_{2}^{2^{t-2}}w_{3}^{2^{t-2}+\varepsilon}
implying
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=0
.

Secondly, let
c=2^{t-2}-2+2\delta
, where

\delta\in\{0,1\}
. Then
b=2^{t}-3\cdot 2^{t-3}-1-3\delta
, and note that
b\geq 2^{t-1}
because
t\geq 5
. Since
f_{t-3}\in I_{n}
,
by Lemmas
3.10
and
3.13
we have
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}-1}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-3}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}+2^{t-3}-1}
, and hence:

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b-2^{t-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c-2^{t-3}+1}\cdot\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-3}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}+2^{t-3}-1}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1-3\delta}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2+2\delta}.

For
\delta=1
we get zero because
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{3})=2^{t-1}-2
, and for
\delta=0
we get the desired nonzero class. This finishes the case
k=t-2
.

Now let
t-3\geq k\geq 1
, and take
c
such that
2^{k}-2\leq c\leq 2^{k+1}-4
. We have two cases.
Suppose first that
c\geq 2^{k}
(then
k\geq 2
). We will reduce

w_{2}^{b}w_{3}^{c}
by
f_{k}
. By Lemmas
3.10
and
3.13
we know that
\mathrm{LM}(f_{k})=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}-2^{k}}w_{3}^{2^{k}-1}
, and since
2b=2^{t+1}-8-3c\geq 2^{t+1}-8-3\cdot(2^{k+1}-4)
, we
have

b>2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}-2^{k}\quad\mbox{and}\quad c\geq 2^{k},

which means that we can indeed reduce
w_{2}^{b}w_{3}^{c}
by
f_{k}
, i.e., apply Lemma
2.6
for
i=k
. It is obvious from (
2.8
) and the fact
\mathrm{LM}(f_{k})=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}-2^{k}}w_{3}^{2^{k}-1}
that
l_{k}=2^{t-1-k}+2^{t-3-k}-1
, and so (by Lemma
2.6
):

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2d+3e=2^{t-k}+2^{t-2-k}-2\\
e>0\end{subarray}}\binom{d+e}{e}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b-2^{k}\left(2^{t-1-k}+2^{t-3-k}-1-d\right)}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c+2^{k}e}.

Obviously,
e
must be even in every summand, and so
e\geq 2
, which leads to

c+2^{k}e\geq c+2^{k+1}>2^{k+1}-2
. Hence, we can apply inductive hypothesis to
conclude that if the term
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b-2^{t-1}-2^{t-3}+2^{k}(d+1)}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c+2^{k}e}

is nonzero, we have
c+2^{k}e=2^{m}-2
for some
m>k+1
.
This implies
c\equiv-2\pmod{2^{k+1}}
, which is false, because
2^{k}\leq c\leq 2^{k+1}-4
. Therefore, every summand in the last sum is zero, and hence

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=0
.

Suppose now that
c=2^{k}-2
(for
k\geq 1
).
We have
b=2^{t}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1>2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}
,
\mathrm{LM}(f_{0})=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}}
(see Lemmas
3.10
and
3.13
), so we can reduce
w_{2}^{b}w_{3}^{c}=w_{2}^{2^{t}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1}w_{3}^{2^{k}-2}
by
f_{0}
, and by Lemma
2.6
we get:

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{k}-2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2d+3e=2^{t}+2^{t-2}\\
e>0\end{subarray}}\binom{d+e}{e}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1-\left(2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}-d\right)}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{k}-2+e},

since
l_{0}=2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}
. For every summand
2^{k}-2+e\geq 2^{k}
, so we can apply inductive hypothesis to conclude
that if the term
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{t-3}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1+d}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{k}-2+e}
is nonzero, it must be
2^{k}-2+e=2^{m}-2
, i.e.,
e=2^{m}-2^{k}
, and consequently
d=2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}-3\cdot 2^{m-1}+3\cdot 2^{k-1}
, for some
m
such that
k+1\leq m\leq t-1
.
Thus if we single out only possibly nonzero summands in the above sum, we obtain

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{k}-2}=\sum_{m=k+1}^{t-1}\binom{2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}-2^{m-1}+2^{k-1}}{2^{m}-2^{k}}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-3\cdot 2^{m-1}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{m}-2}.

If
k=t-3
, then we have two summands in this sum (for
m=t-2
and
m=t-1
), and the corresponding binomial coefficients are
\binom{2^{t-1}+2^{t-4}}{2^{t-3}}\equiv 0\pmod{2}
and
\binom{2^{t-2}+2^{t-3}+2^{t-4}}{2^{t-2}+2^{t-3}}\equiv 1\pmod{2}
. Therefore,
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{k}-2}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2}
.

If
k\leq t-4
, let us prove that the only nonzero summand in the above sum is the one for
m=k+1
. Namely, for
m\geq k+2
we have
2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}-2^{m-1}+2^{k-1}\equiv 2^{k-1}\pmod{2^{k+1}}
, while
2^{m}-2^{k}\equiv 2^{k}\pmod{2^{k+1}}
, and by Lucas’ theorem the corresponding binomial coefficient vanishes. For
m=k+1
we obtain
\binom{2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}-2^{k-1}}{2^{k}}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-3\cdot 2^{k}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{k+1}-2}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-3\cdot 2^{k}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{k+1}-2}
(again by Lucas’ theorem). Finally, by the induction hypothesis, this monomial is equal to
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2}
. This concludes the induction step and the proof of the lemma.
∎

We now prove the lower bound in this case.

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
t\geq 4
. Then
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+1})\geq 2^{t}+2^{t-1}-3
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.4
(for
\beta=2^{t}-1
,
\gamma=2^{t-1}-2
and
r=2^{t+1}-8

(3.4)

\sum_{2b+3c=2^{t+1}-8}\binom{2^{t}-1}{b}\binom{2^{t-1}-2}{c}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-1-b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2-c}

is nonzero in
W_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+1}\otimes W_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+1}
.

If
t\geq 5
, then by Lemma
3.14
, we only need to consider the summands with

(b,c)=(2^{t}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1,2^{k}-2)
, for
1\leq k\leq t-1
, and for each of them, we know that

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2}
. So, by Lucas’ theorem and Corollary
3.8
(along with the fact
I_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+2}\subseteq I_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+1}
and equivalence (
2.5
)), (

) becomes

\displaystyle\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2}\otimes\sum_{k=1}^{t-1}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{k-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2^{k}}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}+2^{t-3}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}.

This last simple tensor is nonzero, because we already know that its first coordinate is in the additive basis

\mathcal{B}_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+1}

3.10
) that the second one belongs to
\mathcal{B}_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+1}

If
t=4
, then (
3.4
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\sum_{2b+3c=24}\binom{15}{b}\binom{6}{c}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{15-b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{6-c}=

\displaystyle\,\,\widetilde{w}_{2}^{12}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{6}+\widetilde{w}_{2}^{9}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{6}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{4}

\displaystyle+\widetilde{w}_{2}^{6}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{4}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{9}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2}+\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{6}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{12}.

From Lemma
3.10
we see that the Gröbner basis
F_{21}
consists of polynomials
f_{0}=g_{20}=w_{2}^{10}+w_{2}w_{3}^{6}
(see Table
1
),
f_{1}=w_{2}^{8}w_{3}+w_{2}^{2}w_{3}^{5}
,
f_{2}=w_{2}^{4}w_{3}^{5}
and
f_{3}=w_{3}^{7}
. It is now routine to verify that
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{6}
and
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{6}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{4}
are two distinct elements of the additive basis
\mathcal{B}_{21}
, and that
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{12}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{9}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{6}

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{6}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{6}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{4}+\widetilde{w}_{2}^{6}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{4}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{6},

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{6}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{6}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{4}

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{6}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{4}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{6}

\{e\otimes f\mid e,f\in\mathcal{B}_{21}\}
of

.
∎

We are left to prove the upper bound in this case. Fortunately, we have everything prepared for this in the previous subsection (Lemma
3.11
(b)).

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
t\geq 4
. Then
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+1})\leq 2^{t}+2^{t-1}-3
.

Proof.

Let

, where
\beta+\gamma=2^{t}+2^{t-1}-2

Since
0pt(z(\widetilde{w}_{2}))=2^{t}-1
and
0pt(z(\widetilde{w}_{3}))=2^{t-1}-1
(by (
2.14
) and Theorem

), if

3.11
(b).
∎

Propositions
3.15
and
3.16
prove Theorem
3.2
in the case

.

3.3.
The case
\mathbf{2^{t}+2^{t-2}+2\leq n\leq 2^{t}+2^{t-1}}

In this case, for the upper bound the roughest estimate will do. Namely,
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{2})=2^{t}-4
and
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{3})\in\{2^{t-1}-2,2^{t-1}-1\}

Proposition 3.17
.

Let
2^{t}+2^{t-2}+2\leq n\leq 2^{t}+2^{t-1}

\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})\geq 2^{t}+2^{t-1}-2.

Proof.

3.1

3.4
for
\beta=2^{t}-1
,
\gamma=2^{t-1}-1
(by the discussion preceding the proposition,
\beta
and

have to be exactly these ones) and

(3.5)

\sum_{2b+3c=2^{t+1}-8}\binom{2^{t}-1}{b}\binom{2^{t-1}-1}{c}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-1-b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-1-c}

is nonzero in

In the case

3.14

). They are the ones with

\displaystyle\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2}\otimes\sum_{k=1}^{t-1}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{k-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2^{k}+1}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}+2^{t-3}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}+1},

In the case

\displaystyle\sum_{2b+3c=24}\binom{15}{b}\binom{7}{c}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{15-b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{7-c}=

\displaystyle+\widetilde{w}_{2}^{6}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{4}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{9}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{3}+\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{6}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{12}\widetilde{w}_{3}.

gives us the Gröbner basis

,

3.4.
The case
\mathbf{n=2^{t}+2^{t-1}+1}

In a similar fashion as before, in order to obtain the lower bound in this case, we start off with the Gröbner basis
F_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+1}
, and then choose a cohomological dimension

to apply Lemma
3.4
.

Lemma 3.18
.

Let
n=2^{t}+2^{t-1}+1
, where
t\geq 4
. Then for the elements
f_{i}
(
0\leq i\leq t-1
) of the Gröbner basis
F_{n}
one has:

•

f_{0}=g_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}}
,
\mathrm{LM}(f_{0})=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}
;

•

f_{i}=w_{3}^{2^{i}-1}(g_{2^{t-i}+2^{t-1-i}-2})^{2^{i}}
,

\mathrm{LM}(f_{i})=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}-2^{i}}w_{3}^{2^{i}-1}
for
1\leq i\leq t-2
;

•

f_{t-2}=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}}w_{3}^{2^{t-2}-1}
,
f_{t-1}=w_{3}^{2^{t-1}+1}
.

Proof.

We have
n-2^{t}+1=2^{t-1}+2
, and hence
\alpha_{0}=0
,
\alpha_{1}=1
,
\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{3}=\dots=\alpha_{t-2}=0
,
\alpha_{t-1}=1
, and

s_{0}=0
,
s_{1}=s_{2}=\dots=s_{t-2}=2
, and

s_{t-1}=2^{t-1}+2
. Since
f_{i}=w_{3}^{\alpha_{i}s_{i-1}}g_{n-2+2^{i}-s_{i}}
, this implies

f_{0}=g_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}}
,

f_{i}=g_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}-3+2^{i}}=w_{3}^{2^{i}-1}(g_{2^{t-i}+2^{t-1-i}-2})^{2^{i}},\mbox{ for }1\leq i\leq t-2,

by Lemma

. In particular,

(see Table

).
The claims about the leading monomials are obvious consequences of
\mathrm{LM}(g_{2l})=w_{2}^{l}
(

).
Finally,
f_{t-1}=w_{3}^{2}g_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}-3}=w_{3}^{2^{t-1}+1}
by Lemma
2.1
(b).
∎

The following lemma and its corollary will be used in this, as well as in the following two cases.

Lemma 3.19
.

For
t\geq 4
we have

g_{2^{t+1}-6}+w_{2}^{2^{t}-3}+w_{2}^{2^{t-2}-3}w_{3}^{2^{t-1}}\in w_{3}I_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}+2^{t-4}}.

Our proof is by induction on
t\geq 4
. For
t=4
the claim is

g_{26}+w_{2}^{13}+w_{2}w_{3}^{8}\in w_{3}I_{27},

which is clearly true, since
g_{26}+w_{2}^{13}+w_{2}w_{3}^{8}=0
(see Table
1
).

So, suppose that the claim is true for some

t\geq 4
and let us prove it for
t+1
. By Lemma
2.2
:

g_{2^{t+1}-6}^{2}+w_{2}^{2^{t+1}-6}+w_{2}^{2^{t-1}-6}w_{3}^{2^{t}}\in w_{3}I_{2^{t+1}+2^{t}+2^{t-2}+2^{t-3}},

and hence

w_{2}^{3}g_{2^{t+1}-6}^{2}+w_{2}^{2^{t+1}-3}+w_{2}^{2^{t-1}-3}w_{3}^{2^{t}}\in w_{3}I_{2^{t+1}+2^{t}+2^{t-2}+2^{t-3}}.

So, it is enough to prove

g_{2^{t+2}-6}+w_{2}^{3}g_{2^{t+1}-6}^{2}\in w_{3}I_{2^{t+1}+2^{t}+2^{t-2}+2^{t-3}}.

By Lemma
2.4
, Lemma
2.1
.(a), (
2.3
) and Lemma

2.3
, we have

\displaystyle g_{2^{t+2}-6}+w_{2}^{3}g_{2^{t+1}-6}^{2}

\displaystyle=g_{2^{t+1}-3}^{2}+w_{2}g_{2^{t+1}-4}^{2}+w_{2}^{3}g_{2^{t+1}-6}^{2}

\displaystyle=w_{2}(w_{2}g_{2^{t+1}-6}+w_{3}g_{2^{t+1}-7})^{2}+w_{2}^{3}g_{2^{t+1}-6}^{2}

\displaystyle=w_{2}w_{3}^{2}g_{2^{t+1}-7}^{2}=w_{2}w_{3}g_{2^{t+2}-11}.

Since
2^{t+2}-11>2^{t+1}+2^{t}+2^{t-2}+2^{t-3}-2
for

, we have

g_{2^{t+2}-11}\in I_{2^{t+1}+2^{t}+2^{t-2}+2^{t-3}}
, which completes our proof.
∎

Corollary 3.20
.

For
t\geq 4
one has

w_{2}^{2^{t}-3}\equiv w_{2}^{2^{t-2}-3}w_{3}^{2^{t-1}}\pmod{I_{13\cdot 2^{t-3}+1}}.

Proof.

For
t\geq 4
one clearly has
2^{t+1}-6\geq 2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}+2^{t-4}-2
and hence
g_{2^{t+1}-6}\in I_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}+2^{t-4}}
. Since

, by Lemma
3.19
we have

w_{2}^{2^{t}-3}+w_{2}^{2^{t-2}-3}w_{3}^{2^{t-1}}\in I_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}+2^{t-4}}\subseteq I_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}+1}=I_{13\cdot 2^{t-3}+1},

∎

We are now ready for the proof of the lower bound.

Proposition 3.21
.

Let
t\geq 4
. Then
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+1})\geq 2^{t+1}+2^{t-3}-3
.

Proof.

We apply Lemma
3.4
for
\beta=2^{t+1}-1
,
\gamma=2^{t-3}-2
and
r=2^{t+1}+2^{t-1}-2
. We need to show that

\sum_{2b+3c=2^{t+1}+2^{t-1}-2}\binom{2^{t+1}-1}{b}\binom{2^{t-3}-2}{c}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t+1}-1-b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}-2-c}\neq 0.

Let us prove that all summands in this sum, except the one with
(b,c)=(2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1,0)
, are zero.

By Theorem
2.7
,
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{2})=2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1
, and so
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=0
if
b>2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1
.

If
b<2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1
, then
c>0
; but also,
c\leq 2^{t-3}-2
implies
2b=2^{t+1}+2^{t-1}-2-3c\geq 2^{t+1}+2^{t-3}+4
, i.e.,
b\geq 2^{t}+2^{t-4}+2>2^{t}-3
, and by Corollary
3.20
(using (
2.5
) and the fact
I_{13\cdot 2^{t-3}+1}\subseteq I_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+1}
) we have

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b-2^{t}+3}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-3}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b-2^{t}+3}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-3}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}+c}=0,

because
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{3})=2^{t-1}
(see Theorem
2.7
).

Therefore, by Lucas’ theorem, the last sum becomes

(3.6)

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}-2}.

The first coordinate of this simple tensor is nonzero because
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{2})=2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1
,
so it suffices to prove
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}-2}\neq 0
.

If
t=4
, then the simple tensor (
3.6
) is
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{19}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{12}
, and since its first coordinate is nonzero, so is the second.

If
t\geq 5
, then by Lemma
3.18
and Table
1
we have

f_{t-4}=w_{3}^{2^{t-4}-1}g_{22}^{2^{t-4}}=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}+2^{t-4}}w_{3}^{2^{t-4}-1}+w_{2}^{2^{t-1}}w_{3}^{2^{t-3}+2^{t-4}-1},

and hence
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}-2}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-4}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}-2}
. It is routine to check that

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-4}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}-2}\in\mathcal{B}_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+1}
(by Lemma

3.18
), which completes our proof.
∎

For the proof of the upper bound, in order to avoid unnecessary repeating of the same arguments, it will be convenient to include the next case as well. For that reason, we now establish upper bounds jointly for this case and the next one.

Proposition 3.22
.

If
t\geq 4
and
2^{t}+2^{t-1}+1\leq n\leq 2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}
, then

\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})\leq 2^{t+1}+2^{t-3}-2.

Moreover,

\operatorname{zcl}(W_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+1})\leq 2^{t+1}+2^{t-3}-3.

Proof.

Let us prove the first inequality. Assume to the contrary that

x:=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}\neq 0\quad\mbox{in }W_{n}\otimes W_{n},

for some integers
\beta,\gamma\geq 0
such that
\beta+\gamma=2^{t+1}+2^{t-3}-1
.

Since
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{2})=2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1
and
2^{t-1}\leq 0pt(\widetilde{w}_{3})\leq 2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}-1
(see Theorem
2.7
), by (
2.14
) we have

0pt(z(\widetilde{w}_{2}))=2^{t+1}-1
and
0pt(z(\widetilde{w}_{3}))=2^{t}-1
. Hence,

\beta\leq 2^{t+1}-1
and
\gamma\leq 2^{t}-1
, and so
\gamma\geq 2^{t-3}
and
\beta\geq 2^{t}+2^{t-3}>2^{t}
.
Further,
g_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}-2},g_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}}\in I_{n}
, and hence

w_{3}g_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}-3}=w_{2}g_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}-2}+g_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}}\in I_{n}.

By Lemma
2.1
(e), this implies
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}}=0

in
W_{n}
. So, by (
2.13
)

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta-2^{t}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma-2^{t-3}}z\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\big)z\big(\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}}\big)

\displaystyle=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta-2^{t}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma-2^{t-3}}\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\otimes 1+1\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\big)\big(\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}}\otimes 1+1\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}}\big)

\displaystyle=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta-2^{t}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma-2^{t-3}}\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}}+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\big)

\displaystyle=z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma-2^{t-3}}\sum_{i+j=\beta-2^{t}}\binom{\beta-2^{t}}{i}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{i}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{j}\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}}+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\big).

Since
x\neq 0
there exists a pair of nonnegative integers
(i,j)
(with
i+j=\beta-2^{t}
) such that the term
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{i}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{j}(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}}+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}})=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}+i}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{j}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}}+\widetilde{w}_{2}^{i}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}+j}
is nonzero. But then

\min\{i,j\}\leq 2^{t-2}-1
(since
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{2})=2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1
) and
\max\{i,j\}\leq 2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}-1
(since
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}}=0
). We conclude that

\beta-2^{t}=i+j=\min\{i,j\}+\max\{i,j\}\leq 2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}+2^{t-3}-2,

and then
\beta+\gamma=2^{t+1}+2^{t-3}-1
implies that actually we must have
\gamma\geq 2^{t-2}+1>2^{t-2}
. Now, as above we
get

(3.7)

x=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta-2^{t}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma-2^{t-2}}\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\big).

We have
2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}-3\geq n-2
, and so
g_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}-3}\in I_{n}
, which by Lemma
2.1
(d) (and (
2.5
)) means that

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}-1}=0
in
W_{n}
. Along with the fact
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{2})=2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1
this gives us that

z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t-1}}\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}\!+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\big)\!=\!\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\otimes 1+1\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\big)\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}\!+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\big)

is equal to zero. In the light of (
3.7
) and the assumption
x\neq 0
, this means that
\beta-2^{t}\leq 2^{t-1}-1
, which in turn implies
\gamma\geq 2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}
(because
\beta+\gamma=2^{t+1}+2^{t-3}-1
). Finally, we have

x=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta-2^{t}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma-2^{t-1}-2^{t-3}}\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}}+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\big)\big(\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}}\otimes 1+1\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}}\big)=0,

since
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}}=0
and
\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}}=0
(
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{3})\leq 2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}-1
). This contradicts the assumption
x\neq 0
, and thus concludes the proof of the first inequality.

Let us now prove the second inequality. Suppose
to the contrary that there are integers
\beta,\gamma\geq 0
such that

y:=z(w_{2})^{\beta}z(w_{3})^{\gamma}\neq 0\quad\mbox{in }W_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+1}\otimes W_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+1},

and
\beta+\gamma=2^{t+1}+2^{t-3}-2
.

The same analysis as above now leads to
\gamma\geq 2^{t-3}-1
and
\beta\geq 2^{t}+2^{t-3}-1>2^{t}
. If additionally
\gamma\geq 2^{t-3}
and
\beta\neq 2^{t}+2^{t-1}-1
, then it is routine to check that we can use the same proof
as above to obtain a contradiction. So we are left with the cases
(\beta,\gamma)=(2^{t+1}-1,2^{t-3}-1)
and
(\beta,\gamma)=(2^{t}+2^{t-1}-1,2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}-1)
.

Note that
g_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}-3}\in I_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+1}
, so, by Lemma
2.1
(e),
we have

(3.8)

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}-1}=0\quad\mbox{in }W_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+1}.

Also, we know that
13\cdot 2^{t-3}+1>12\cdot 2^{t-3}+1=2^{t}+2^{t-1}+1
, and consequently
I_{13\cdot 2^{t-3}+1}\subseteq I_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+1}
, so Corollary
3.20
establishes the equality

(3.9)

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-3}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-3}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}}\quad\mbox{in }W_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+1}.

Let us consider the case
(\beta,\gamma)=(2^{t+1}-1,2^{t-3}-1)
. Since
\binom{2^{l}-1}{i}\equiv 1\pmod{2}
for all
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,2^{l}-1\}
, the binomial formula leads to

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{2^{t+1}-1}\,\,\sum_{j=0}^{2^{t-3}-1}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{i}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{j}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t+1}-1-i}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}-1-j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}^{2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1}\,\,\sum_{j=0}^{2^{t-3}-1}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{i}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{j}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t+1}-1-i}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}-1-j},

since
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{2})=2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1
. For
i\geq 2^{t}-3
the only possibly nonzero summand is the one for
j=0
. Namely, due to (
3.9
) and the fact
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{3})=2^{t-1}
(see Theorem
2.7
) we have
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{i}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{j}=0
if
j>0
. Similarly, if
i\leq 2^{t}+2
, then
2^{t+1}-1-i\geq 2^{t}-3
, so
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t+1}-1-i}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}-1-j}=0
unless
j=2^{t-3}-1
. This means that for
i\in\{2^{t}-3,\ldots,2^{t}+2\}
there are no nonzero summands, and that

y=\sum_{i=2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}^{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{i}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}-1}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t+1}-1-i}+\sum_{i=2^{t}+3}^{2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{i}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t+1}-1-i}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}-1}.

But both of these sums are zero due to (
3.8
), contradicting the assumption
y\neq 0
.

Finally, we consider the case
(\beta,\gamma)=(2^{t}+2^{t-1}-1,2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}-1)
. Then

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t-1}-1}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-3}-1}\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}}+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\big)

\displaystyle=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t-1}-1}\sum_{i=0}^{2^{t-3}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{i}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}-1-i}\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}}+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\big)

\displaystyle=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t-1}-1}\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}-1}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}}+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}-1}\big).

The first equality holds because
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}}=0
(by (
3.8
)), and the third because
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{3})=2^{t-1}
. However, the expression in the brackets is zero (again by (
3.8
)), and so
y=0
. This contradiction concludes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Propositions
3.21
and
3.22
prove Theorem
3.2
in the case
n=2^{t}+2^{t-1}+1
.

3.5.
The case
\mathbf{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2\leq n\leq 13\cdot 2^{t-3}}

We have already established the upper bound for this case in Proposition
3.22
:

\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})\leq 2^{t+1}+2^{t-3}-2.

Therefore, the following proposition completes the proof of Theorem
3.2
in this case.

Let us first state a result from
[
3
, p. 282]
, which will be used in this case, as well as in the next one: if
t
,
s
and
n
are integers such that
t\geq 3
,
1\leq s\leq t-2
and
2^{t+1}-2^{s+1}+1\leq n\leq 2^{t+1}-2^{s}
, then

(3.10)

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t+1}-3\cdot 2^{s}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{n-2^{t+1}+2^{s+1}-1}\neq 0\quad\mbox{in }W_{n}\subset H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3}).

In particular, for
s=t-2
and
n=2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2
we get

(3.11)

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}\neq 0\quad\mbox{in }W_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2}.

Proposition 3.23
.

Let
2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2\leq n\leq 2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}
, where
t\geq 4
. Then:

\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})\geq 2^{t+1}+2^{t-3}-2.

Proof.

We are going to prove
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2})\geq 2^{t+1}+2^{t-3}-2
, and then the proposition follows from Lemma
3.1
. By applying Lemma
3.4
for
\beta=2^{t+1}-1
,
\gamma=2^{t-3}-1
and
r=2^{t+1}+2^{t-1}+1
we see that it suffices to verify that

\sum_{2b+3c=2^{t+1}+2^{t-1}+1}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t+1}-1-b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}-1-c}\neq 0\quad\mbox{in }W_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2}\otimes W_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2}.

Similarly as in the proof of Proposition
3.21
, we show that, excluding the summand with
(b,c)=(2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1,1)
, all others are zero.

If
b>2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1
, then
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{2})=2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1
(Theorem
2.7
) implies
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=0
.

If
b<2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1
, then
c>1
. On the other hand,
c\leq 2^{t-3}-1
implies
2b=2^{t+1}+2^{t-1}+1-3c\geq 2^{t+1}+2^{t-3}+4
, i.e.,
b\geq 2^{t}+2^{t-4}+2>2^{t}-3
, and then Corollary
3.20
(along with (
2.5
) and the fact
I_{13\cdot 2^{t-3}+1}\subseteq I_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2}
) gives us

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b-2^{t}+3}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-3}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b-2^{t}+3}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-3}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}+c}=0,

since
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{3})=2^{t-1}+1
(see Theorem
2.7
).

So, the above sum is equal to

\displaystyle\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}-2}.

According to (
3.11
), the first coordinate of this simple tensor is nonzero, and so we are left to prove
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}-2}\neq 0
in
W_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2}
. However, in the proof of Proposition
3.21
we established that the corresponding class is nonzero in
W_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+1}
, which means (by (
2.5
)) that
w_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}w_{3}^{2^{t-3}-2}\notin I_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+1}
. On the other hand,
I_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2}\subseteq I_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+1}
, and so
w_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}w_{3}^{2^{t-3}-2}\notin I_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2}
, i.e,
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}-2}\neq 0
in
W_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2}
as well.
∎

3.6.
The case
\mathbf{13\cdot 2^{t-3}+1\leq n\leq 2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}

For the lower bound in this case we will use (
3.10
) for
s=t-2
and
n=2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}+1=13\cdot 2^{t-3}+1
:

(3.12)

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}}\neq 0\quad\mbox{in }W_{13\cdot 2^{t-3}+1}.

Proposition 3.24
.

Let
13\cdot 2^{t-3}+1\leq n\leq 2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}
, where
t\geq 4
. Then

\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})\geq 2^{t+1}+2^{t-2}-2.

Proof.

We know that
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})
increases with
n
(Lemma
3.1
), which means that it suffices to prove
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{13\cdot 2^{t-3}+1})\geq 2^{t+1}+2^{t-2}-2
.
The proof relies on Lemma
3.4
. We apply that lemma for
\beta=2^{t+1}-1
,
\gamma=2^{t-2}-1
and
r=23\cdot 2^{t-3}-2
, and so we need to show that

\sum_{2b+3c=23\cdot 2^{t-3}-2}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t+1}-1-b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}-1-c}\neq 0\quad\mbox{in }W_{13\cdot 2^{t-3}+1}\otimes W_{13\cdot 2^{t-3}+1}.

We are going to prove that the only nonzero summand in this sum is the one for
(b,c)=(2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1,2^{t-3})
. First of all, that summand is really nonzero, because it is
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-3}-1}
, its first coordinate is nonzero by (
3.12
), and the second divides the first, so it is nonzero too. So, we are left to prove that all summands with
b\neq 2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1
are zero.

If
b>2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1
, then
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=0
since
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{2})=2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1
(Theorem
2.7
).

If
2^{t}-3\leq b<2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1
, then
c>2^{t-3}
and by Corollary
3.20
one has

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b-2^{t}+3}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-3}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b-2^{t}+3}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-3}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}+c}=0,

since
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{3})=2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}
(see Theorem
2.7
).

Finally, there are no summands in the above sum with
b\leq 2^{t}-4
, since then
c\leq 2^{t-2}-1
would imply
2b+3c\leq 2\cdot(2^{t}-4)+3\cdot(2^{t-2}-1)=22\cdot 2^{t-3}-11<23\cdot 2^{t-3}-2
.
∎

We are concluding the proof of Theorem
3.2
in this case by verifying the opposite inequality (the upper bound).

Proposition 3.25
.

Let
t\geq 4
and
13\cdot 2^{t-3}+1\leq n\leq 2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}
. Then

\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})\leq 2^{t+1}+2^{t-2}-2.

Proof.

As usual, we suppose to the contrary that there exist integers
\beta,\gamma\geq 0
such that

x:=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}\neq 0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\beta+\gamma=2^{t+1}+2^{t-2}-1.

Theorem
2.7
gives us
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{2})=2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1
and
2^{t-1}+2^{t-3}\leq 0pt(\widetilde{w}_{3})\leq 2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}-1
, and then (
2.14
) implies
0pt(z(\widetilde{w}_{2}))=2^{t+1}-1
and
0pt(z(\widetilde{w}_{3}))=2^{t}-1
. This means that
\beta\leq 2^{t+1}-1
and
\gamma\leq 2^{t}-1
, and together with
\beta+\gamma=2^{t+1}+2^{t-2}-1
these then imply
\gamma\geq 2^{t-2}
and
\beta\geq 2^{t}+2^{t-2}
.

Note that the inequality
n-2\leq 2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}-2
implies

w_{3}g_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}-3}=w_{2}g_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}-2}+g_{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}\in I_{n},

which, according to Lemma
2.1
(d), leads to

(3.13)

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}=0\quad\mbox{in }W_{n}.

This is used for the second of the following equalities:

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta-2^{t}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma-2^{t-2}}\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\otimes 1+1\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\big)\big(\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}\otimes 1+1\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}\big)

\displaystyle=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta-2^{t}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma-2^{t-2}}\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\big)

\displaystyle=z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma-2^{t-2}}\sum_{i+j=\beta-2^{t}}\binom{\beta-2^{t}}{i}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{i}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{j}\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\big)

\displaystyle=z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma-2^{t-2}}\sum_{i+j=\beta-2^{t}}\binom{\beta-2^{t}}{i}\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}+i}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{j}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}+\widetilde{w}_{2}^{i}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}+j}\big).

Since
x\neq 0
there must exist nonnegative integers
i
and
j
with
i+j=\beta-2^{t}
and
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}+i}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{j}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}+\widetilde{w}_{2}^{i}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}+j}\neq 0
. Then
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{2})=2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1
implies
\min\{i,j\}\leq 2^{t-2}-1
, and from (
3.13
) we then conclude
\max\{i,j\}\leq 2^{t-1}-1
. This leads to
\beta-2^{t}=i+j\leq 2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}-2
, i.e.,
\gamma\geq 2^{t-1}+1
(since
\beta+\gamma=2^{t+1}+2^{t-2}-1
).

Similarly as above, using (
3.13
) we now obtain that

x=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta-2^{t}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma-2^{t-1}}\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}}+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\big).

Again by (
3.13
) and the fact
\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}=0
(since
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{3})\leq 2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}-1
) we have that

z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-2}}\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}}\!+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\big)\!=\!\big(\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}\otimes 1+1\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}\big)\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}}\!+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\big)

is equal to zero, which means that we must have
\gamma-2^{t-1}\leq 2^{t-2}-1
(since
x\neq 0
). However, this implies
\beta=2^{t+1}+2^{t-2}-1-\gamma\geq 2^{t}+2^{t-1}
, and so

x=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta-2^{t}-2^{t-1}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma-2^{t-1}}\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}}+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\big)\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\otimes 1+1\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\big)=0,

once again by (
3.13
) and the fact
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{2})=2^{t}+2^{t-2}-1
. This contradicts the assumption
x\neq 0
, and thus we are done.
∎

3.7.
The case
\mathbf{2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}+1\leq n\leq 2^{t+1}-2}

Let
s\in\{1,2,\ldots,t-3\}
be the (unique) integer such that
2^{t+1}-2^{s+1}+1\leq n\leq 2^{t+1}-2^{s}
. We want to prove that
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})=3\cdot 2^{t}-2^{s+1}-2
, which will finish the proof of Theorem
3.2
.

For the lower bound we will need the Gröbner basis
F_{2^{t+1}-2^{s+1}+1}
.

Lemma 3.26
.

If
n=2^{t+1}-2^{s+1}+1
, then for
F_{n}=\{f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{t-1}\}
(the Gröbner basis from Theorem
2.5
) we have:

•

f_{0}=g_{2^{t+1}-2^{s+1}}
,
\mathrm{LM}(f_{0})=w_{2}^{2^{t}-2^{s}}
;

•

f_{i}=w_{3}^{2^{i}-1}(g_{2^{t+1-i}-2^{s+1-i}-2})^{2^{i}}
,
\mathrm{LM}(f_{i})=w_{2}^{2^{t}-2^{s}-2^{i}}w_{3}^{2^{i}-1}
,
1\leq i\leq s
;

•

f_{i}=w_{3}^{2^{i+1}-2^{s+1}+1}(g_{2^{t+1-i}-4})^{2^{i}}
,
\mathrm{LM}(f_{i})=w_{2}^{2^{t}-2^{i+1}}w_{3}^{2^{i+1}-2^{s+1}+1}
,

s+1\leq i\leq t-1
.

•

f_{t-2}=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}}w_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2^{s+1}+1}
,
f_{t-1}=w_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}+1}
.

Proof.

We have
n-2^{t}+1=2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}+\dots+2^{s+1}+2
, and hence

\alpha_{0}=0
,
\alpha_{1}=1
,
\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{3}=\dots=\alpha_{s}=0
,
\alpha_{s+1}=\alpha_{s+2}=\dots=\alpha_{t-1}=1
,
s_{0}=0
,

s_{i}=2
for
1\leq i\leq s
, and
s_{i}=2^{i}+2^{i-1}+\dots+2^{s+1}+2=2^{i+1}-2^{s+1}+2
for

s+1\leq i\leq t-1
. By definition,
f_{i}=w_{3}^{\alpha_{i}s_{i-1}}g_{n-2+2^{i}-s_{i}}
, and so
f_{0}=g_{2^{t+1}-2^{s+1}}
,

\displaystyle f_{i}

\displaystyle=g_{2^{t+1}-2^{s+1}+2^{i}-3}=w_{3}^{2^{i}-1}(g_{2^{t+1-i}-2^{s+1-i}-2})^{2^{i}}\quad(\mbox{for }1\leq i\leq s),

\displaystyle f_{i}

\displaystyle=w_{3}^{2^{i}-2^{s+1}+2}g_{2^{t+1}-2^{i}-3}=w_{3}^{2^{i+1}-2^{s+1}+1}(g_{2^{t+1-i}-4})^{2^{i}}\quad(\mbox{for }s+1\leq i\leq t-1),

by Lemma
2.3
. In particular, by looking at Table
1
we see that

\displaystyle f_{t-2}

\displaystyle=w_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2^{s+1}+1}g_{4}^{2^{t-2}}=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}}w_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2^{s+1}+1},

\displaystyle f_{t-1}

\displaystyle=w_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}+1}g_{0}^{2^{t-1}}=w_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}+1}.

The statements about leading monomials follow from
\mathrm{LM}(g_{2l})=w_{2}^{l}
(
l\geq 0
).
∎

In order to apply Lemma
3.4
for cohomological dimension
r=2^{t+2}-3\cdot 2^{s+1}-5
, we now detect all nonzero monomials of the form
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}
, where
2b+3c=2^{t+2}-3\cdot 2^{s+1}-5
.

Lemma 3.27
.

Let
n=2^{t+1}-2^{s+1}+1
(
1\leq s\leq t-3
). Then in
H^{2^{t+2}-3\cdot 2^{s+1}-5}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
the
only nonzero monomials of the form
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}
are

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t+1}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{k}-2^{s+1}-1}
for
s+2\leq k\leq t
.
Furthermore, they are all equal, i.e.,

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t+1}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{k}-2^{s+1}-1}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}-1}

for all
k\in\{s+2,s+3,\ldots,t\}
, and
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}-1}\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
.

Proof.

It is obvious from Lemma
3.26
that
w_{2}^{2^{t-1}-1}w_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}-1}
is not divisible by any of the leading monomials
\mathrm{LM}(f_{i})
,
0\leq i\leq t-1
. So,

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}-1}\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
, and particularly,
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}-1}\neq 0
.

Note also that Theorem
2.7
gives us that
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{3})=2^{t}-2^{s+1}
, which means that
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=0
whenever
c>2^{t}-2^{s+1}
.

Now we use backward induction on
k
, where
s+2\leq k\leq t
, to prove that
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}
,
with
2b+3c=2^{t+2}-3\cdot 2^{s+1}-5
and

2^{k}-2^{s+1}-1\leq c<2^{k+1}-2^{s+1}-1
, is nonzero if
and only if
c=2^{k}-2^{s+1}-1
, and that
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t+1}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{k}-2^{s+1}-1}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}-1}
. Throughout the proof we will use the fact that
c
must be odd (this follows from

2b+3c=2^{t+2}-3\cdot 2^{s+1}-5
).

We have already discussed the cases
c=2^{t}-2^{s+1}-1
and
c>2^{t}-2^{s+1}
. Since
c
must be odd, the induction base (
k=t
) is verified.

Proceeding to the induction step, let
s+2\leq k\leq t-1
(and
2^{k}-2^{s+1}-1\leq c<2^{k+1}-2^{s+1}-1
). We will distinguish two cases.
Suppose first that
c\geq 2^{k}-2^{s+1}+1
. Note that
\mathrm{LM}(f_{k-1})\mid w_{2}^{b}w_{3}^{c}
in this case. Namely,
\mathrm{LM}(f_{k-1})=w_{2}^{2^{t}-2^{k}}w_{3}^{2^{k}-2^{s+1}+1}
(Lemma
3.26
), while

c\geq 2^{k}-2^{s+1}+1\mbox{ and }b\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(2^{t+2}-3\cdot 2^{s+1}-5-3\cdot(2^{k+1}-2^{s+1}-3)\right)>2^{t}-2^{k}.

So, we can reduce
w_{2}^{b}w_{3}^{c}
by
f_{k-1}
, i.e., apply Lemma
2.6
for
i=k-1
, and obtain

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2d+3e=2^{t+2-k}-4\\
e>0\end{subarray}}\binom{d+e}{e}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b-2^{k-1}\left(2^{t+1-k}-2-d\right)}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c+2^{k-1}e}.

Obviously, in each summand of this sum
e
must be even, and so
e>0
implies
e\geq 2
. Therefore,
c+2^{k-1}e\geq 2^{k}-2^{s+1}+1+2^{k}>2^{k+1}-2^{s+1}-1
, which means that we can apply inductive hypothesis to conclude that if

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b-2^{t}+2^{k}+2^{k-1}d}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c+2^{k-1}e}\neq 0
, then we must have

c+2^{k-1}e=2^{m}-2^{s+1}-1
for some
m>k
. This implies
c\equiv-2^{s+1}-1\pmod{2^{k}}
,
which is impossible since

2^{k}-2^{s+1}+1\leq c\leq 2^{k+1}-2^{s+1}-3
(and
s+1\leq k-1
). So, each summand from the last sum is zero,
and hence
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=0
.

Suppose now
c=2^{k}-2^{s+1}-1
. Then
b=2^{t+1}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1>2^{t}-2^{s+1}
(because
k\leq t-1
) and
c>2^{s}-1
(because
k\geq s+2
). Since
\mathrm{LM}(f_{s})=w_{2}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}}w_{3}^{2^{s}-1}
(Lemma
3.26
) we can reduce
w_{2}^{b}w_{3}^{c}
by
f_{s}
, and Lemma
2.6
leads to

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2d+3e=2^{t+1-s}-4\\
e>0\end{subarray}}\binom{d+e}{e}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b-2^{s}\left(2^{t-s}-2-d\right)}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c+2^{s}e}.

Similarly as above,
e
must be even, and so
e\geq 2
. This means that
c+2^{s}e\geq 2^{k}-2^{s+1}-1+2^{s+1}=2^{k}-1>2^{k}-2^{s+1}+1
, and hence, by induction hypothesis and the first part of the induction step, if
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b-2^{t}+2^{s+1}+2^{s}d}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c+2^{s}e}\neq 0
, then
c+2^{s}e=2^{m}-2^{s+1}-1
for some
m\in\{k+1,k+2,\ldots,t\}
. This implies
e=2^{m-s}-2^{k-s}
and
d=(2^{t+1-s}-4-3\cdot 2^{m-s}+3\cdot 2^{k-s})/2=2^{t-s}-2-3\cdot 2^{m-s-1}+3\cdot 2^{k-s-1}
. Therefore,

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=\sum_{m=k+1}^{t}\binom{2^{t-s}-2-2^{m-s-1}+2^{k-s-1}}{2^{m-s}-2^{k-s}}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t+1}-3\cdot 2^{m-1}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{m}-2^{s+1}-1}.

For
m=k+1
the binomial coefficient is
\binom{2^{t-s}-2-2^{k-s-1}}{2^{k-s}}\equiv 1\pmod{2}
by Lucas’ theorem, since
2^{t-s}-2-2^{k-s-1}\equiv 2^{k-s}+2^{k-s-1}-2\pmod{2^{k-s+1}}
. On the other hand, for
k+2\leq m\leq t
the binomial coefficient vanishes, since
2^{t-s}-2-2^{m-s-1}+2^{k-s-1}\equiv 2^{k-s-1}-2\pmod{2^{k-s+1}}
, while
2^{m-s}-2^{k-s}\equiv 2^{k-s}\pmod{2^{k-s+1}}
. So the only nonzero summand in the above sum is the one for
m=k+1
. Finally, we conclude that

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t+1}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}\!-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{k}-2^{s+1}\!-1}\!\!=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t+1}-3\cdot 2^{k}\!-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{k+1}-2^{s+1}\!-1}\!\!=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}\!-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}\!-1},

by induction hypothesis.

We are left to prove that
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=0
if
c<2^{s+2}-2^{s+1}-1=2^{s+1}-1
(and
2b+3c=2^{t+2}-3\cdot 2^{s+1}-5
). Actually, we are going to continue the above backward induction on
k
, and prove that for
1\leq k\leq s
,
2^{k}-1\leq c<2^{k+1}-1
(and
2b+3c=2^{t+2}-3\cdot 2^{s+1}-5
) one has
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=0
.

So, let
2^{k}-1\leq c<2^{k+1}-1
for some
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,s\}
. Then

b>(2^{t+2}-3\cdot 2^{s+1}-5-3\cdot 2^{k+1}+3)/2=2^{t+1}-3\cdot 2^{s}-3\cdot 2^{k}-1>2^{t}-2^{s}-2^{k}
(since
k\leq s\leq t-3
). By Lemma
3.26
we know that
\mathrm{LM}(f_{k})=w_{2}^{2^{t}-2^{s}-2^{k}}w_{3}^{2^{k}-1}
, and therefore we can
reduce
w_{2}^{b}w_{3}^{c}
by
f_{k}
. Lemma
2.6
then gives us

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2d+3e=2^{t+1-k}-2^{s+1-k}-2\\
e>0\end{subarray}}\binom{d+e}{e}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b-2^{k}\left(2^{t-k}-2^{s-k}-1-d\right)}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c+2^{k}e}.

Again,
e
must be even, which leads to
e\geq 2
, and consequently
c+2^{k}e>2^{k}e\geq 2^{k+1}
. By inductive hypothesis, in order for
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b-2^{t}+2^{s}+2^{k}+2^{k}d}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c+2^{k}e}
to be nonzero, we must have
c+2^{k}e=2^{m}-2^{s+1}-1
for some
m\in\{s+2,s+3,\ldots,t\}
. However, this would imply
c\equiv-1\pmod{2^{k+1}}
, which contradicts the fact
2^{k}-1\leq c<2^{k+1}-1
. Finally, we conclude
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=0
, and the proof of the lemma is now complete.
∎

We are now ready to establish the lower bound.

Proposition 3.28
.

Let
2^{t+1}-2^{s+1}+1\leq n\leq 2^{t+1}-2^{s}
(where
1\leq s\leq t-3
). Then:

\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})\geq 3\cdot 2^{t}-2^{s+1}-2.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
, it is enough to prove the inequality for

n=2^{t+1}-2^{s+1}+1
. In order to do so, we apply Lemma
3.4
for
\beta=2^{t+1}-1
,
\gamma=2^{t}-2^{s+1}-1
and
r=2^{t+2}-3\cdot 2^{s+1}-5
. By that lemma, it suffices to show that

\sum_{2b+3c=2^{t+2}-3\cdot 2^{s+1}-5}\!\binom{2^{t+1}-1}{b}\binom{2^{t}-2^{s+1}-1}{c}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t+1}-1-b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}-1-c}\!\neq 0

in
W_{n}\otimes W_{n}
. By Lemma
3.27
, we only need to consider the summands with

(b,c)=(2^{t+1}-3\cdot 2^{k-1}-1,2^{k}-2^{s+1}-1)
, for
s+2\leq k\leq t
, and for each
of them we know that
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}-1}
. So, by Lucas’ theorem,
the last sum simplifies to

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}-1}\otimes\sum_{k=s+2}^{t}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{k-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{k}}.

We know that
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}-1}\neq 0
(by Lemma
3.27
), so it remains to establish that
\sum_{k=s+2}^{t}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{k-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{k}}\neq 0
in
W_{n}
. However, we are going to prove that this sum multiplied by
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-3\cdot 2^{s}-1}
is nonzero, so the sum itself must be nonzero. Namely:

\displaystyle\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-3\cdot 2^{s}-1}\sum_{k=s+2}^{t}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{k-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=s+2}^{t}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-3\cdot 2^{s}-1+3\cdot 2^{k-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{k}}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t+1}-3\cdot 2^{s}-1}\neq 0.

The second equality holds because for
s+2\leq k<t
one has
2^{t-1}-3\cdot 2^{s}-1+3\cdot 2^{k-1}>2^{t-1}
and
2^{t}-2^{k}>2^{t-1}-2^{s+1}+1
, and then
f_{t-2}=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}}w_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2^{s+1}+1}
(Lemma
3.26
) implies that
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-3\cdot 2^{s}-1+3\cdot 2^{k-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{k}}=0
in this case. So, only the summand for
k=t
remains, and
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t+1}-3\cdot 2^{s}-1}\neq 0
because
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{2})
is exactly
2^{t+1}-3\cdot 2^{s}-1
(see Theorem
2.7
).
∎

In the final part of the section we obtain the upper bound for
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})
in the case

2^{t+1}-2^{s+1}+1\leq n\leq 2^{t+1}-2^{s}
(where
t\geq 4
and
1\leq s\leq t-3
).
For this proof we will need the following two lemmas.

Lemma 3.29
.

In
W_{2^{t+1}-2^{s}}
the following relations hold:

(a)

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}-2^{s}}=\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}-2^{s}}
;

(b)

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2^{s}}=0
;

(c)

\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s}}=0
.

Proof.

We will use the members of the Gröbner basis
F_{2^{t+1}-2^{s}}
, defined in Theorem
2.5
.
If
n=2^{t+1}-2^{s}
, then
n-2^{t}+1=2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}+\dots+2^{s}+1
, and since
s\leq t-3
, we have
\alpha_{t-3}=\alpha_{t-2}=\alpha_{t-1}=1
and
s_{i}=2^{i+1}-2^{s}+1
for
t-4\leq i\leq t-1
.
Therefore, for
t-3\leq i\leq t-1
, by Lemma
2.3
we have

f_{i}=w_{3}^{\alpha_{i}s_{i-1}}g_{n-2+2^{i}-s_{i}}=w_{3}^{2^{i}-2^{s}+1}g_{2^{t+1}-2^{i}-3}=w_{3}^{2^{i+1}-2^{s}}(g_{2^{t-i+1}-4})^{2^{i}}.

Now Table
1
leads to

\displaystyle f_{t-3}

\displaystyle=w_{3}^{2^{t-2}-2^{s}}g_{12}^{2^{t-3}}=w_{3}^{2^{t-2}-2^{s}}(w_{2}^{6}+w_{3}^{4})^{2^{t-3}}\!=w_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{t-2}}w_{3}^{2^{t-2}-2^{s}}+w_{3}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}-2^{s}};

\displaystyle f_{t-2}

\displaystyle=w_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2^{s}}g_{4}^{2^{t-2}}=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}}w_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2^{s}};

\displaystyle f_{t-1}

\displaystyle=w_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s}}g_{0}^{2^{t-1}}=w_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s}}.

Of course, all these polynomials belong to
I_{2^{t+1}-2^{s}}
, and so (
2.5
) concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.30
.

Let
n=2^{t+1}-2^{s}
(where
t\geq 4
and
1\leq s\leq t-3

Then in
W_{n}\otimes W_{n}
one has:

z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t+1}-2^{s+1}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t}-2^{s}}=0\quad\mbox{ and }\quad z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t+1}-2^{s}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}}=0.

Proof.

Let
x:=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t+1}-2^{s+1}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}}
. We need to prove that
x\cdot z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{s}}=0
and
x\cdot z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{s}}=0
. Let us first establish that

x=A^{2^{s}}\cdot(\widetilde{w}_{3}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}},

where

According to (
2.13
) and using the fact
s+1\leq t-2
, we have

x=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t-1}-2^{s+1}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-2}-2^{s+1}}z\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}}\big)z\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\big)z\big(\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}}\big)z\big(\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}\big).

By Lemma
3.29
(b),
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}}=0
, and hence

x=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t-1}-2^{s+1}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-2}-2^{s+1}}z\big(\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}\big)\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}+2^{t-1}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}}+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}+2^{t-1}}\big).

Again by Lemma
3.29
(part (a) multiplied by
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{s}}
and part (b) multiplied by
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}+2^{s}}
),
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}+2^{t-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}=0
, and so

x=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t-1}-2^{s+1}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-2}-2^{s+1}}\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}+2^{t-1}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}+2^{t-1}}\big).

As in some instances before, we will expand the first two factors by binomial formula, while the expression in the brackets will be expanded using Corollary
3.8
. By that corollary (applied for
t+1

),
w_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{t-1}}\equiv\sum_{k=1}^{t-2}w_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{k-1}}w_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{k}}\pmod{I_{2^{t+1}+2^{t-1}+2}}
, and since
I_{n}=I_{2^{t+1}-2^{s}}\supseteq I_{2^{t+1}+2^{t-1}+2}

2.5
) we get

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}+2^{t-1}}=\sum_{k=1}^{t-2}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{k-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{k}}\quad\mbox{in }W_{n}.

Every summand from the expansion of
z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{t-1}-2^{s+1}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t-2}-2^{s+1}}
is of the

\binom{2^{t-1}-2^{s+1}}{l}\binom{2^{t-2}-2^{s+1}}{m}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{l}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{m}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2^{s+1}-l}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-2}-2^{s+1}-m},

and
0\leq m\leq 2^{t-2}-2^{s+1}

By Lucas’ theorem,
\binom{2^{t-1}-2^{s+1}}{l}
(resp.
\binom{2^{t-2}-2^{s+1}}{m}

if and only if

for some
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,2^{t-s-2}-1\}
(resp.
m=2^{s+1}j
for some

). We conclude that

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{2^{t-s-2}-1}\sum_{j=0}^{2^{t-s-3}-1}\sum_{k=1}^{t-2}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{k-1}+2^{s+1}i}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{k}+2^{s+1}j}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2^{s+1}(i+1)}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}(j+1)}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=0}^{2^{t-s-2}-1}\sum_{j=0}^{2^{t-s-3}-1}\sum_{k=1}^{t-2}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2^{s+1}(i+1)}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}(j+1)}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{k-1}+2^{s+1}i}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{k}+2^{s+1}j}.

Also, Lemma

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=s+2}^{t-2}\sum_{i=0}^{2^{t-s-2}-1}\,\sum_{j=0}^{2^{t-s-3}-1}\!\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2^{s+1}(i+1)}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}(j+1)}\!\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{k-1}+2^{s+1}i}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{k}+2^{s+1}j}

\displaystyle+y\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{2^{t-s-2}-2}\bigg(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{s}+2^{s+1}i}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2^{s+1}(i+1)}+\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2^{s+1}(i+1)}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{s}+2^{s+1}i}\bigg),

where
y=\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}}=(\widetilde{w}_{3}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}}
. It is obvious that the last sum is
A^{2^{s}}
(see (
3.14
)), and if we denote the two triple sums by
x_{1}
and

respectively, we will prove (
3.14
) as soon as we show that

(i.e., that these two triple sums cancel out).

Let us introduce the following notation:

\sigma(k,i,j)\!:=\!\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3\cdot 2^{k-1}+2^{s+1}i}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{k}+2^{s+1}j}\mbox{ and }\tau(k,i,j)\!:=\!\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2^{s+1}(i+1)}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}(j+1)}.

We know that
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2^{s}}=0
(Lemma
3.29
(b)), and since
2^{t}-2^{k}+2^{s+1}j\geq 2^{t}-2^{t-2}>2^{t-1}-2^{s}
, we conclude that we must have
3\cdot 2^{k-1}+2^{s+1}i<2^{t-1}
, i.e.,
i\leq 2^{t-s-2}-3\cdot 2^{k-s-2}-1
, in order for
\sigma(k,i,j)
to be nonzero. Similarly, since
\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s}}=0
(Lemma
3.29
(c)), one more necessary condition for
\sigma(k,i,j)\neq 0
is
2^{t}-2^{k}+2^{s+1}j<2^{t}-2^{s}
, and this amounts to
2^{t}-2^{k}+2^{s+1}j\leq 2^{t}-2^{s+1}
because
2^{t}-2^{k}+2^{s+1}j
is divisible by
2^{s+1}
. So,
\sigma(k,i,j)\neq 0
implies

. We conclude that

\displaystyle x_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=s+2}^{t-2}\,\sum_{i=0}^{2^{t-s-2}-3\cdot 2^{k-s-2}-1}\,\,\sum_{j=0}^{2^{k-s-1}-1}\sigma(k,i,j)\otimes\tau(k,i,j)\quad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle x_{2}

so the change of variables

\displaystyle(\widetilde{w}_{3}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{s}}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}}+\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s}}=0,

and then (

.

\displaystyle(\widetilde{w}_{2}\otimes 1+1\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2})\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{2^{t-s-2}-2}\bigg(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3+2i}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-s-1}-2-2i}+\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-s-1}-2-2i}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{3+2i}\bigg)

The change of variable

transforms the second sum to the third, so these two cancel out. For the first and the fourth, if we apply the change of variable
i\mapsto 2^{t-s-2}-3-i

\displaystyle z(\widetilde{w}_{2})\cdot A

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{2^{t-s-2}-2}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{4+2i}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-s-1}-2-2i}+\sum_{i=-1}^{2^{t-s-2}-3}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{4+2i}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-s-1}-2-2i}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-s-1}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2}+\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-s-1}}.

Now, by (
3.14
) we have

\displaystyle z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{2^{s}}\cdot x

\displaystyle=\big(z(\widetilde{w}_{2})\cdot A\big)^{2^{s}}(\widetilde{w}_{3}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3})^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}}

\displaystyle=\big(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{s+1}}+\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{s+1}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\big)\big(\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}}\big)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{s+1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}}+\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{s+1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}}\otimes\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t}-2^{s+1}}

\displaystyle=0,

by Lemma
3.29
(b), and the proof is complete.
∎

We can now easily prove the upper bound in this case.

Proposition 3.31
.

Let
1\leq s\leq t-3
and

2^{t+1}-2^{s+1}+1\leq n\leq 2^{t+1}-2^{s}
. Then

\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})\leq 3\cdot 2^{t}-2^{s+1}-2.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
,
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})\leq\operatorname{zcl}(W_{2^{t+1}-2^{s}})
, so it is enough to prove that
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{2^{t+1}-2^{s}})\leq 3\cdot 2^{t}-2^{s+1}-2
.
Assume to the contrary that there are integers
\beta,\gamma\geq 0
such that
\beta+\gamma=2^{t+1}+2^{t}-2^{s+1}-1
and

z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}\neq 0\quad\mbox{in }W_{2^{t+1}-2^{s}}\otimes W_{2^{t+1}-2^{s}}.

We know that
0pt(\widetilde{w}_{2})=2^{t+1}-3\cdot 2^{s}-1
and

0pt(\widetilde{w}_{3})=2^{t}-2^{s}-1
(by Theorem
2.7
), and then (
2.14
)
gives us
0pt(z(\widetilde{w}_{2}))=2^{t+1}-1

and
0pt(z(\widetilde{w}_{3}))=2^{t}-1
. This means that we must have
\beta\leq 2^{t+1}-1
and
\gamma\leq 2^{t}-1
, and consequently
\beta=2^{t+1}+2^{t}-2^{s+1}-1-\gamma\geq 2^{t+1}-2^{s+1}
and
\gamma\geq 2^{t}-2^{s+1}
. Now for
\beta^{\prime}:=\beta-2^{t+1}+2^{s+1}
and

\gamma^{\prime}:=\gamma-2^{t}+2^{s+1}
one has
\beta^{\prime}+\gamma^{\prime}=2^{s+1}-1
, and hence either
\beta^{\prime}\geq 2^{s}
or
\gamma^{\prime}\geq 2^{s}
, i.e., either
\beta\geq 2^{t+1}-2^{s}
or
\gamma\geq 2^{t}-2^{s}
. However, this contradicts Lemma
3.30
.
∎

The proof of Theorem
3.2
is now completed.

4.
Comparison between
\operatorname{zcl}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
and
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})

In this section we compare
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})
with
\operatorname{zcl}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
and thus establish some lower bounds for
\mathrm{TC}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
. Since we are working over a field,
W_{n}\subseteq H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
implies
W_{n}\otimes W_{n}\subseteq H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})\otimes H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
, and so we certainly have
\operatorname{zcl}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})\geq\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})
. However, we can prove more.

Proposition 4.1
.

For every integer
n\geq 6
one has

\operatorname{zcl}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})\geq 1+\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n}).

Proof.

Let
z=z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}
be a monomial which realizes
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})
(so
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})=\beta+\gamma
), and let
m=2\beta+3\gamma
. Since

z\neq 0\quad\mbox{ in }\big(H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})\otimes H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})\big)_{m}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{m}H^{i}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})\otimes H^{m-i}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3}),

if we write
z
in the form
\sum_{i=0}^{m}u_{i}\otimes v_{m-i}
, where
u_{i}\otimes v_{m-i}\in H^{i}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})\otimes H^{m-i}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
, then there exists
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,m\}
with the property
u_{k}\otimes v_{m-k}\neq 0
. Now,
u_{k}\in H^{k}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
is a nonzero polynomial in
\widetilde{w}_{2}
and
\widetilde{w}_{3}
, and so
k<3n-9
(by (
2.7
)). Poincaré duality applies to give us a class
a\in H^{3n-9-k}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
such that
au_{k}\neq 0
.

Let us show that
z(a)z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}\neq 0
in
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})\otimes H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
, which will prove the proposition. The degree of this element in
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})\otimes H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
is
3n-9-k+m
, and we have

z(a)z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}=(a\otimes 1+1\otimes a)\sum_{i=0}^{m}u_{i}\otimes v_{m-i}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}au_{i}\otimes v_{m-i}+\sum_{i=0}^{m}u_{i}\otimes av_{m-i}.

Since
u_{3n-9}=0
(by (
2.7
)), we see that the summand in
H^{3n-9}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})\otimes H^{m-k}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
is
au_{k}\otimes v_{m-k}\neq 0
, which completes the proof.
∎

So the difference between
\operatorname{zcl}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
and
\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})
is at least
1
, but, as we show in the next proposition, not more than
2
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For every integer
n\geq 15
one has

\operatorname{zcl}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})\leq 2+\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n}).

Proof.

Let
t\geq 4
be the integer with the property
2^{t}-1\leq n\leq 2^{t+1}-2
. According to
[
1
, Theorem A]
, there are indecomposable classes in

outside
W_{n}
, but at most two of them. Let us denote these classes by
a
and
b
, where
|a|<|b|
(in the cases in which there is only one indecomposable class, it is denoted by
a
). Then
|a|=\min\{3n-2^{t+1}-1,2^{t+1}-4\}
, and if
b
exists,
then
|b|=\max\{3n-2^{t+1}-1,2^{t+1}-4\}
. Since the dimension of the manifold is
3n-9
, it follows that
0pt(a)\leq 3
and
0pt(b)=1
, and so

and
0pt(z(b))=1
(see (
2.14

Suppose to the contrary that
\operatorname{zcl}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})\geq 3+\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})
. Then
\operatorname{zcl}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
is realized by a monomial of the form

, where
p+q\geq 3
. By the previous discussion we also have
p+q\leq 0pt(z(a))+0pt(z(b))\leq 4
. Now,
p+q+\beta+\gamma=\operatorname{zcl}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})\geq 3+\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})
, and we conclude

(4.1)

\beta+\gamma\geq 3+\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})-p-q\geq\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})-1,

i.e.,

(4.2)

The proof of Lemma 2.3 from
[

works equally well for the oriented Grassmannians, and so
p|a|+q|b|+2\beta+3\gamma=|z(a)^{p}z(b)^{q}z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}|\leq 6(n-3)-1

Conjecture 4.3
.

For all
n\geq 6
,
\operatorname{zcl}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})=1+\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})
.

In the following proposition we prove the conjecture for approximately
5/12
of the integers in the range
[2^{t}-1,2^{t+1}-2]
(more precisely, in the first sixth and the last quarter of this range).

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
t\geq 4
. If either
2^{t}-1\leq n<2^{t}+2^{t-1}/3+1
or
2^{t}+2^{t-1}+2^{t-2}+1\leq n\leq 2^{t+1}-2
, then

\operatorname{zcl}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})=1+\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n}).

Proof.

Assume to the contrary that
\operatorname{zcl}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})=2+\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})
. This means that
\operatorname{zcl}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
is reached by a monomial of the form
z(a)^{p}z(b)^{q}z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}
with
p+q\geq 2
, where
a
and
b
(if
b
exists) are the two indecomposable classes outside
W_{n}
, as in the proof of Proposition
4.2
. In that proof we showed that
p+q
cannot exceed
2
, so
p+q=2
. Since
0pt(z(b))=1
(if
b
exists),
\operatorname{zcl}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
is reached by either
z(a)^{2}z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}
or
z(a)z(b)z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}
, where
\beta+\gamma=\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})
.

Suppose that
\operatorname{zcl}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
is reached by
z(a)^{2}z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}
. Note that

z(a)^{2}z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}=z(a^{2})z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}=(a^{2}\otimes 1+1\otimes a^{2})z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}

is a sum of simple tensors of the form
a^{2}u\otimes v
and
u\otimes a^{2}v
, where
u,v\in W_{n}
are such that
|u|+|v|=2\beta+3\gamma
. Since
z(a)^{2}z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}\neq 0
, at least one of these simple tensors is nonzero. If
a^{2}u\otimes v
is a nonzero simple tensor, then
a^{2}u\neq 0
in
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
and
v\neq 0
in
W_{n}
(and similarly if
u\otimes a^{2}v\neq 0
). This means that

(4.5)

2|a|+|u|\leq 3n-9\quad\mbox{ and }\quad|v|\leq 3n-9-|a|.

Namely, the dimension of the manifold
\widetilde{G}_{n,3}
is
3n-9
, and since
v\in W_{n}
is nonzero, Poincaré duality gives us a class
x\in H^{3n-9-|v|}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
with the property
vx\neq 0
. But if
|v|
were greater than
3n-9-|a|
, then we would have
|x|<|a|
, which would mean that
x\in W_{n}
(since
a
is the class of smallest degree in
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
which is not in
W_{n}
), and so
vx\in W_{n}
. This would contradict (
2.7
).

Similarly as in the proof of Proposition
4.2
, we also have

\gamma=\beta+\gamma-\beta=\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})-\beta\geq\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})-0pt(z(\widetilde{w}_{2})).

Using this and summing up the inequalities form (
4.5
) we get

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})

\displaystyle\leq 2(\beta+\gamma)+\gamma-\big(\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})-0pt(z(\widetilde{w}_{2}))\big)

\displaystyle=2\beta+3\gamma-\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})+0pt(z(\widetilde{w}_{2}))=|u|+|v|-\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})+0pt(z(\widetilde{w}_{2}))

\displaystyle\leq 6n-18-3|a|-\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})+0pt(z(\widetilde{w}_{2})),

that is

6n+0pt(z(\widetilde{w}_{2}))\geq 3\big(|a|+\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})\big)+18.

However, this contradicts (
4.4
). So
\operatorname{zcl}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
is not realized by
z(a)^{2}z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}
.

This proves the proposition for
n\in\{2^{t}-1,2^{t},2^{t+1}-3,2^{t+1}-2\}
, since in these cases
a
is the only (up to addition of an element from
W_{n}
) indecomposable class outside
W_{n}
(i.e.,
b
does not exist in these cases).

Now suppose that
n\notin\{2^{t}-1,2^{t},2^{t+1}-3,2^{t+1}-2\}
and that
\operatorname{zcl}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
is reached by
z(a)z(b)z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}
,
\beta+\gamma=\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})
. Let us note that, as in the proof of Proposition
4.2
,
|a|+|b|+2\beta+3\gamma=|z(a)z(b)z(\widetilde{w}_{2})^{\beta}z(\widetilde{w}_{3})^{\gamma}|\leq 6n-19
. On the other hand, according to
[

,

, and similarly as before we have
\gamma\geq\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})-0pt(z(\widetilde{w}_{2}))
, so

\displaystyle 3n-5+2\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})

\displaystyle\leq|a|+|b|+2(\beta+\gamma)+\gamma-\big(\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})-0pt(z(\widetilde{w}_{2}))\big)

\displaystyle=|a|+|b|+2\beta+3\gamma-\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})+0pt(z(\widetilde{w}_{2}))

\displaystyle\leq 6n-19-\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})+0pt(z(\widetilde{w}_{2})).

We conclude that
3n+0pt(z(\widetilde{w}_{2}))\geq 3\operatorname{zcl}(W_{n})+14

However, if

3

while

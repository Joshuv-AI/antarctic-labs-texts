Abhishek Ghosh, Naijia Liu, Jan Rozendaal, Liang Song

Abstract
. We use the Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators to obtain bounds for spherical maximal functions in L p ​ ( ℝ n ) L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) , n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , where the radii of the spheres are restricted to a compact subset of ( 0 , ∞ ) (0,\infty) . These bounds extend to general hypersurfaces with non-vanishing Gaussian curvature, to the complex spherical means, and to geodesic spheres on compact manifolds. We also obtain improved maximal function bounds and pointwise convergence statements for wave equations, both on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} and on compact manifolds. The maximal function bounds are essentially sharp for all p ∈ [ 1 , 2 ] ∪ [ 2 ​ ( n + 1 ) n − 1 , ∞ ] p\in[1,2]\cup[\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1},\infty] , for each such hypersurface, every complex spherical mean, and on every manifold.

1.
Introduction

For
n\geq 2
,
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, set

(1.1)

\mathcal{M}_{[1,2]}f(x):=\sup_{1\leq t\leq 2}\Big|\int_{S^{n-1}}f(x-ty)\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\Big|,

where
\mathrm{d}\sigma
is the induced Lebesgue measure on the unit sphere
S^{n-1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In this article we will obtain
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
bounds for
\mathcal{M}_{[1,2]}f
, and for related maximal functions, if
f
is an element of a suitable Hardy space for Fourier integral operators.

1.1.
Main results

For
p\in[1,\infty]
, set

(1.2)

s(p):=\frac{n-1}{2}\Big|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\Big|

and

(1.3)

d(p):=\begin{cases}s(p)-\frac{1}{p}&\text{if }\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}\leq p\leq\infty,\\
0&\text{if }2\leq p<\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1},\\
s(p)&\text{if }1\leq p\leq 2.\end{cases}

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\langle D\rangle^{-s}\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a Sobolev space over the Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for Fourier integral operators from Definition
2.1
. Then our main result for
\mathcal{M}_{[1,2]}
is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s>d(p)+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{n-1}{2}
. Then there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

(1.4)

\|\mathcal{M}_{[1,2]}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

In fact, (
1.4
) extends from
S^{n-1}
to arbitrary compact hypersurfaces with non-vanishing Gaussian curvature, cf. Theorem
3.6
, and a variable-coefficient version of Theorem
1.1
also holds; see Theorem
3.9
. A consequence of the latter is a version of (
1.4
) for geodesic spheres on compact manifolds, Proposition
3.15
. These results, and in particular Theorem
1.1
, are essentially sharp for all
p\in[1,2]\cup[\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1},\infty]
, as is shown in Theorems
4.8
and
4.9
and Proposition
4.12
.

Another extension of Theorem
1.1
arises by considering maximal functions associated with the complex spherical means from
[
35
]
. In Proposition
3.11
we show that (
1.4
) holds for
s>d(p)+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{n-1}{2}-\Real(\alpha)
if one replaces the average in (
1.1
) by the dilated complex spherical mean of order
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, with Theorem
1.1
corresponding to the case where
\alpha=0
. These bounds are essentially sharp for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
and
p\in[1,2]\cup[\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1},\infty]
, as follows from Proposition
4.11
.

We also obtain the following maximal function bounds and pointwise convergence statements for the Euclidean wave equation.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
s>d(p)+\frac{1}{p}
. Then there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

(1.5)

\big\|\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Hence
e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f(x)\to f(x)
as
t\to 0
, for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem
1.2
is a special case of Corollary
3.2
, and (
1.5
) is essentially sharp for all
p\in[1,2]\cup[\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1},\infty)
, cf. Theorems
4.2
and
4.5
. A version of Theorem
1.2
also holds on compact Riemannian manifolds
(M,g)
; see Proposition
3.14
. In the latter case one replaces the Euclidean Laplacian
\Delta
by the Laplace–Beltrami operator
\Delta_{g}
, and
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by the space
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
from Definition
2.5
. On every such manifold, the resulting maximal function bounds are sharp for
p\in[1,2]\cup[\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1},\infty)
, as is shown in Proposition
4.13
.

1.2.
Previous work

Bounds for
\mathcal{M}_{[1,2]}
arise naturally in the study of the
L^{p}
boundedness of the full spherical maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{(0,\infty)}
, given by

(1.6)

\mathcal{M}_{(0,\infty)}f(x):=\sup_{0<t<\infty}\Big|\int_{S^{n-1}}f(x-ty)\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\Big|

for
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In
[
35
]
,
Stein proved the inequality

(1.7)

\|\mathcal{M}_{(0,\infty)}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for
n\geq 3
and
p>n/(n-1)
. It was later shown by Bourgain in
[
4
]

that (
1.7
) also holds for
n=2
and
p>2
. Since (
1.7
) does not hold if
n=1
and
p<\infty
, nor if
n\geq 2
and
p\leq n/(n-1)
, this completely settled the question of the
L^{p}
boundedness of
\mathcal{M}_{(0,\infty)}
. Indeed, apart from the fact that the condition
p>n/(n-1)
is sharp, for such
p
one cannot improve (
1.7
) by considering a larger space than
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
on the right-hand side of (
1.7
). This follows from the fact that the spherical averages converge pointwise almost everywhere to a multiple of
f
as
t\to 0
.

On the other hand, one can work with a larger space on the right-hand side of (
1.7
) if one replaces
\mathcal{M}_{(0,\infty)}f
by
\mathcal{M}_{[1,2]}f
. For example, inequalities of the form

(1.8)

\|\mathcal{M}_{[1,2]}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

for
n\geq 3
,
p>n/(n-1)
and suitable
s<0
, follow from the work of Sogge and Stein in
[
34
]
. In fact, they obtained a version of (
1.7
) for more general hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, by combining bounds as in (
1.8
) with a scaling argument. A similar approach was used in the work
[
23
]
of Mockenhoupt, Seeger and Sogge, who relied on improved bounds for
\|\mathcal{M}_{[1,2]}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
to give a simplied proof of (
1.7
) for
n=2
and
p>2
(see also
[
32
,
24
]
for more general hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
). Estimates as in (
1.8
) are also strongly related to the frequently studied question of the
L^{p}
boundedness of maximal functions associated with the complex spherical means
(see e.g.
[
23
,
21
,
22
]
). Finally, in
[
28
]
Schlag obtained sharp
L^{p}\mapsto L^{q}
bounds for
\mathcal{M}_{[1,2]}
, with
q>p
, which are related to (
1.8
) due to Sobolev embeddings (see also
[
29
,
18
]
). However,
for general
p
it does not appear to be known what the optimal condition on
s
is for (
1.8
) to hold.

Bounds for spherical maximal functions are intimately connected to maximal function estimates for wave propagators.
This connection arises due to the fact that the Fourier transform of the measure
\mathrm{d}\sigma
in (
3.12
) can be expressed in terms of the Euclidean half-wave propagators. However, maximal function estimates for solutions to partial differential equations, and the associated pointwise convergence statements, are of independent interest. Such statements are particularly topical for the Schrödinger equation, due to the recent (almost) resolution of Carleson’s problem on the pointwise convergence of solutions to their initial data in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(see
[
16
,
5
,
8
,
9
]
). In the case of the wave equation, bounds of the form

(1.9)

\big\|\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

are known to hold for
p\geq 2(n+1)/(n-1)
and
s>2s(p)
, as a corollary of

[
6
]
(see
[
1
,
2
]
for the analogous statement on compact manifolds). For
n=2
one can obtain (
1.9
) for
p>2n/(n-1)
and
s>2s(p)
by relying on
[
12
]
, and similarly for compact Riemannian surfaces, using
[
11
]
. These bounds are all essentially sharp in terms of the exponent
s
.
Moreover, in
[
15
]
the endpoint case of (
1.9
) was obtained for
n\geq 4
,
p>2+\frac{4}{n-3}>2\frac{n+1}{n-1}
and
s=2s(p)
, with an analogous statement on compact manifolds being contained in
[
19
]
. Nonetheless, for general
p
it does not appear to be known what the optimal condition on
s
is for (
1.9
) to hold.

1.3.
Discussion of the results

In this article we obtain versions of (
1.8
) and (
1.9
) where
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is replaced by a Hardy space for Fourier integral operators. In doing so we improve, for certain values of
p
,
the known results regarding
(
1.8
) and (
1.9
),
even in cases where the essentially optimal Sobolev exponent
s
(in the
W^{s,p}
scale) has been established. In other cases we complement the existing results.

The Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for Fourier integral operators was introduced for
p=1
by Smith in
[
31
]
, and his definition was extended to all
1\leq p\leq\infty
by Hassell, Portal and the third author in
[
13
]
. These spaces are invariant under suitable Fourier integral operators, and they satisfy the Sobolev embeddings

(1.10)

W^{s(p),p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq W^{-s(p),p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

for
1<p<\infty
, with the natural modifications involving the local Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
p=1
, and
\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
p=\infty
.

In particular, for
1<p<\infty
, Theorem
1.1
yields (
1.8
) with
s>d(p)+s(p)+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{n-1}{2}
, and Theorem
1.2
yields (
1.9
) with
s>d(p)+s(p)+\frac{1}{p}
. One has
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, so for
p=2
all our results apply to
W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. However, for
p\neq 2
the corollaries of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
for
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are strictly weaker than the estimates in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
themselves, since

W^{s(p),p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a proper subspace of
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In fact, the Sobolev exponents in (
1.10
) are sharp, and therefore the bounds in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are significantly stronger than their corollaries regarding (
1.8
) and (
1.9
), at least for certain initial data. This is particularly relevant for
p\geq 2(n+1)/(n-1)
, where one has
d(p)+s(p)+\frac{1}{p}=2s(p)
and the resulting condition on
s
in (
1.8
) and (
1.9
) is essentially sharp, due to the mapping properties of the individual spherical means and half-wave propagators. Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
show that (
1.8
) and (
1.9
) can nonetheless be improved, by working with a different space of initial data. We also note that, for suitable Fourier integral operators
T
, one can replace
f
by
Tf
on the left-hand sides of (
1.4
) and (
1.5
), whereas this is not possible when working directly with (
1.8
) and (
1.9
).

For
n=2
and
p>2n/(n-1)
, one can use
[
12
]
to obtain (
1.8
) and (
1.9
) for
s>2s(p)-\frac{n-1}{2}
and
s>2s(p)
, respectively.
Due to the sharpness of the Sobolev exponents in (
1.10
), for
2n/(n-1)<p<2(n+1)/(n-1)
these bounds do not follow from Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
,
nor does the converse implication hold.
Also, the endpoint version of (
1.9
) in
[
15
]

neither follows from Theorem
1.2

, nor the other way around. A similar comparison holds for the analogues of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
on compact manifolds, Propositions
3.15
and
3.14
, and the results in
[
11
,
19
]
.

1.4.
Ideas behind the proofs

Just as in
[
34
,
23
,
29
]
and in more recent work on the
L^{p}
boundedness of complex spherical maximal functions and related operators (see e.g.
[
22
,
21
,
3
]
), our spherical maximal function bounds follow from estimates for wave propagators.
More precisely, to derive Theorem
1.1
, we make use of both fixed-time and local smoothing estimates for Fourier integral operators, as can be found in
[
31
,
13
,
27
,
20
]
. This approach, which relies on the same ideas as in
[
34
]
(see also
[
33
,
2
]
), leads to a version of Theorem
1.2
for general Fourier integral operators, which in turn implies both Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
as well as their various extensions.

A significant part of the article is dedicated to proving that our results are essentially sharp. In the case of Theorem
1.2
, sharpness follows for
p\geq 2(n+1)/(n-1)
by combining (
1.10
) with the
L^{p}
mapping properties of the individual operators, but in Theorem
4.2
we prove a more general lower bound for
s
in (
1.5
) which does not hold in the
L^{p}
scale (see Remark
4.4
). To prove sharpness for
1\leq p\leq 2
one has to produce additional focusing of order
1/p
, which we do in Theorem
4.5
using a Knapp example that cannot be used to prove sharpness of the corresponding estimate in the
L^{p}
scale (see Remark
4.7
). In fact, Theorems
4.2
and
4.5
both concern more general Fourier integral operators, and the proofs rely on an improvement of a lemma from
[
27
,
20
]
which relates such operators to associated flow maps, Lemma
4.1
. We do not know whether our results are sharp for
2<p<2(n+1)/(n-1)
(see also Remark
4.6
).

Although the spherical averages can be expressed as a linear combination of terms involving the half-wave propagators, the contributions of these terms might, a priori, cancel each other out. To nonetheless derive sharpness of Theorem
1.1
from sharpness of maximal function bounds for half-wave propagators, in the proof of Theorem
4.8
we microlocalize to regions of phase space where there is only a single such term. Moreover, due to the fact that our maximal function bounds for Fourier integral operators are sharp under very mild conditions, we can use FIO calculus to show that various extensions of Theorem
1.1
are also essentially sharp.

Finally, given that the assumptions for the sharpness statements in Theorems
4.2
and
4.5
are of a local nature, it suffices to work in local coordinates when deriving sharpness of our results on manifolds. There the required statements follows from the sharpness of our other results.

1.6.
Notation and terminology

The natural numbers are
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\ldots\}
, and
\mathbb{Z}_{+}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Throughout, we fix an
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq 2
.

For
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we write
\langle\xi\rangle=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{1/2}
, and
\hat{\xi}=\xi/|\xi|
if
\xi\neq 0
. We use multi-index notation, where
\partial_{\xi}=(\partial_{\xi_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{\xi_{n}})
and
\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}=\partial^{\alpha_{1}}_{\xi_{1}}\ldots\partial^{\alpha_{n}}_{\xi_{n}}

for
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
. Moreover,
\partial_{x\eta}^{2}\Phi
denotes the mixed Hessian of a function
\Phi
of the variables
x
and
\eta
.

The Fourier transform of a tempered distribution
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is denoted by
\mathcal{F}f
or
\widehat{f}
, and the Fourier multiplier with symbol
\varphi
is
\varphi(D)
. The space of bounded linear operators
between Banach spaces
X
and
Y
is
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
, and
\mathcal{L}(X):=\mathcal{L}(X,X)
.

We write
f(s)\lesssim g(s)
to indicate that
f(s)\leq Cg(s)
for all
s
and a constant
C>0
independent of
s
, and similarly for
f(s)\gtrsim g(s)
and
g(s)\eqsim f(s)
.

2.
Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators

In this section we first define the Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We then collect some of their basic properties, together with bounds for suitable Fourier integral operators. In the final subsection we introduce the Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators on compact manifolds.

2.1.
Definitions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

For simplicity of notation, we write
\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
p\in(1,\infty)
. Moreover,
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the local real Hardy space, with norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\|q(D)f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|(1-q)(D)f\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for
f\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Here and throughout,
q\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is such that
q(\xi)=1
for
|\xi|\leq 2
, and
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the classical real Hardy space. Finally,
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the dual of
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and we write
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\langle D\rangle^{-s}\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Fix a collection
(\varphi_{\omega})_{\omega\in S^{n-1}}\subseteq C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with the following properties:

(1)

For all
\omega\in S^{n-1}
and
\xi\neq 0
, one has
\varphi_{\omega}(\xi)=0
if
|\xi|<\frac{1}{4}
or
|\hat{\xi}-\omega|>|\xi|^{-1/2}
.

(2)

For all
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, there exists a
C_{\alpha,\beta}\geq 0
such that

|(\omega\cdot\partial_{\xi})^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}\varphi_{\omega}(\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}|\xi|^{\frac{n-1}{4}-\frac{|\alpha|}{2}-\beta}

for all
\omega\in S^{n-1}
and
\xi\neq 0
.

(3)

The map
(\xi,\omega)\mapsto\varphi_{\omega}(\xi)
is measurable on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times S^{n-1}
, and

\int_{S^{n-1}}\varphi_{\omega}(\xi)^{2}\mathrm{d}\omega=1

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|\xi|\geq 1
.

For the construction of such a family, see
[
26
,
14
]
.

We can now define the Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators.

Definition 2.1
.

For
p\in[1,\infty)
, the space
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
consists of all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
q(D)f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\varphi_{\omega}(D)f\in\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for almost all
\omega\in S^{n-1}
, and
(\int_{S^{n-1}}\|\varphi_{\omega}(D)f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}\omega)^{1/p}<\infty
, endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\|q(D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\Big(\int_{S^{n-1}}\|\varphi_{\omega}(D)f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}\omega\Big)^{1/p}.

Moreover,
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}
. For
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
consists of all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\langle D\rangle^{s}f\in\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\|\langle D\rangle^{s}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Originally, in
[
31
,
13
]
, the Hardy spaces for FIOs were defined differently. It follows from
[
26
,
10
]
that the original definition is equivalent to the one given here.

For
m\in\mathbb{R}
, we will work with the Kohn–Nirenberg symbol class
S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
, consisting of those
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

(2.1)

\sup\{\langle\eta\rangle^{|\beta|-m}|\partial_{z}^{\alpha}\partial_{\eta}^{\beta}a(z,\eta)|\mid z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}<\infty

for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n+1}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
. We write
S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for the subspace of symbols which do not depend on the
z
variable.

2.2.
Results on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

In this subsection we first collect a few basic properties of the Hardy spaces for Fourier integral operators, and then we state two results about the boundedness of suitable Fourier integral operators.

Firstly, the following extension of the Sobolev embeddings from (
1.10
) holds:

(2.2)

\mathcal{H}^{s+s(p),p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{s-s(p),p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

for all
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, as follows from
[
13
, Theorem 7.4]
. Here
s(p)
is as in (
1.2
). If
p<\infty
, then the Schwartz functions lie dense in
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(see
[
13
, Proposition 6.6]
). Although we will not make explicit use of the following properties in the present article, we also note that the Hardy spaces for FIOs form a complex interpolation scale, cf.
[
13
, Proposition 6.7]
, and that
(\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))^{*}=\mathcal{H}^{-s,p^{\prime}}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, cf.
[
13
, Proposition 6.8]
. By
[
31
,
13
,
20
]
,
\mathcal{H}^{s,1}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
has suitable molecular and atomic decompositions.

The following proposition is a slight generalization of
[
27
, Corollary 3.6 and Theorem 4.4]
, to the case where the phase function is not defined globally and the symbol is time dependent. Recall that a subset
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
is
conic
if
\lambda\eta\in V
for all
\eta\in V
and
\lambda>0
, and recall the definition of the exponent
d(p)
from (
1.3
).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
be an open conic subset, and let
\phi\in C^{\infty}(V)
be real-valued and positively homogeneous of degree
1
. Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
\mathrm{supp}(a)\subseteq V^{\prime}\cup\{0\}
, for some conic
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
which is closed in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. Then, for all
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and for each compact interval
I\subseteq(0,\infty)
, the following statements hold.

(1)

One has
e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD):\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
t>0
, and

(2.3)

\sup_{t\in I}\|e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}<\infty.

(2)

Suppose, additionally, that
\mathrm{rank}\,\partial_{\eta\eta}^{2}\phi(\eta)=n-1
for all
\eta\in V
. If
p\in(2,\infty)
and
s>d(p)+m
,
then there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

(2.4)

\Big(\int_{I}\|e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{1/p}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Note that
e^{it\phi(D)}a(D)
is well-defined if we extend
\phi
to all of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The operator then extends by adjoint action to
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. A similar remark applies to (
2.5
) below.

Proof.

Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
be real-valued and positively homogeneous of degree zero, and such that
\mathrm{supp}(\chi)\subseteq V
and
\chi\equiv 1
on
V^{\prime}
. Then
\phi^{\prime}:=\phi\chi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
is real-valued and positively homogeneous of degree
1
. Moreover,
\mathrm{supp}(a(t\cdot))\subseteq V^{\prime}\cup\{0\}
for all
t>0
, since
V^{\prime}
is conic, and
\{a(t\cdot)\mid t\in I\}
is a uniformly bounded collection in
S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Hence (
1
) follows by applying
[
27
, Lemma 3.5]
to
e^{it\phi^{\prime}(D)}a(tD)\langle D\rangle^{-m}=e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)\langle D\rangle^{-m}
, for
t\in I
.

On the other hand, the proof of (
2
) is analogous to that of
[
27
, Theorem 4.4]
, relying in particular on the
\ell^{p}
decoupling inequality from
[
7
]
, for hypersurfaces with non-vanishing Gaussian curvature. Note that the condition that
\mathrm{rank}\,\partial_{\eta\eta}^{2}\phi(\eta)=n-1
for all
\eta\in V
is equivalent to
\{\eta\in V\mid\phi(\eta)=1\}
having non-vanishing Gaussian curvature.
∎

The following proposition is a variable-coefficient version of Proposition
2.2
, involving operators with symbols that have compact spatial support. A subset
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
is
conic
if
(z,\lambda\eta)\in V
whenever
(z,\eta)\in V
and
\lambda>0
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
be an open conic subset, and let
\Phi\in C^{\infty}(V)
be real-valued and positively homogenous of degree
1
in the fiber variable. Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
(z,\eta)\in V
whenever
(z,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}(a)
and
\eta\neq 0
. Suppose that
\mathrm{rank}\,\partial_{x\eta}^{2}\Phi(z_{0},\eta_{0})=n
for all
(z_{0},\eta_{0})\in\mathrm{supp}(a)
with
\eta_{0}\neq 0
, where we write
z=(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
, and that there exists a compact set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
such that
a(z,\eta)=0
for all
(z,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
z\notin K
. Set

(2.5)

Tf(z):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\Phi(z,\eta)}a(z,\eta)\widehat{f}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta

for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, and
T_{t}f(x):=Tf(x,t)
. Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

One has
T_{t}:\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and

(2.6)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|T_{t}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{s+m,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}<\infty.

(2)

Suppose, additionally, that for all
(z_{0},\eta_{0})\in\mathrm{supp}(a)
with
\eta_{0}\neq 0
, one has

(2.7)

\mathrm{rank}\,\partial_{\eta\eta}^{2}(\partial_{z}\Phi(z_{0},\eta)\cdot G(z_{0},\eta_{0}))|_{\eta=\eta_{0}}=n-1,

where
G(z_{0},\eta_{0})
is the generalized Gauss map. Then, for all
p\in(2,\infty)
and
s>d(p)+m
, one has

T:\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}).

Proof.

The result is contained in
[
20
]
. More precisely, (
1
) is
[
20
, Corollary 2.4 and Remark 2.6]
, and (
2
) can be deduced from
[
20
, Corollary 5.4]
in the same way as in the proof of
[
20
, Theorem 5.7]
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Recall that, for
(z_{0},\eta_{0})\in\mathrm{supp}(a)
with
\eta_{0}\neq 0
, the generalized Gauss map is
G(z_{0},\eta_{0})=G_{0}(z_{0},\eta_{0})/|G_{0}(z_{0},\eta_{0})|
, where
G_{0}(z_{0},\eta_{0}):=\wedge_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{\eta_{i}}\partial_{z}\Phi(z_{0},\eta_{0})
. In other words, (
2.7
) says that the unit normal vectors
\pm\theta
to the conical hypersurface
\{\partial_{z}\Phi(z_{0},\lambda\eta)\mid(z_{0},\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}(a),\lambda>0\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
at
\partial_{z}\Phi(z_{0},\eta_{0})
satisfy

\mathrm{rank}\,\partial^{2}_{\eta\eta}(\partial_{z}\Phi(z_{0},\eta)\cdot\theta)|_{\eta=\eta_{0}}=n-1.

This is in turn equivalent to
\{\partial_{z}\Phi(z_{0},\lambda\eta)\mid(z_{0},\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}(a),\lambda>0\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
having
n-1
non-vanishing principal curvatures, which, in the terminology of
[
32
]
, means that the canonical relation of
T
satisfies the
cinematic curvature condition
.

Note also that, if (
2.7
) holds, then one cannot have
\partial_{t}\Phi(x_{0},t,\eta_{0})|_{t=t_{0}}\neq 0
for all
(z_{0},\eta_{0})
in an open subset of
\mathrm{supp}(a)
, where we write
z_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})
. Indeed, otherwise an open subset of the conical hypersurface
\{\partial_{z}\Phi(z_{0},\lambda\eta)\mid(z_{0},\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}(a),\lambda>0\}
would be contained in the plane where the last coordinate vanishes, which would prohibit it from having
n-1
nonvanishing principal curvatures.

We will also use frequently that
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is invariant under changes of coordinates, for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, as follows from
[
13
, Theorem 6.10]
(see also
[
27
, Proposition 3.3]
).

2.3.
Spaces on manifolds

Let
(M,g)
be a (smooth)
n
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold without boundary, and
K
a finite collection of coordinate charts,
\kappa:U_{\kappa}\to\kappa(U_{k})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, such that
\cup_{\kappa\in K}U_{\kappa}=M
. Let
(\psi_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in K}\subseteq C^{\infty}(M)
be such that

(2.8)

\sum_{\kappa\in K}\psi_{\kappa}^{2}(x)=1

for all
x\in M
, and such that
\mathrm{supp}(\psi_{\kappa})\subseteq U_{\kappa}
for each
\kappa\in K
.

We denote the collection of smooth half densities on
M
by
\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
, and its dual, the space of distributional half densities, by
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
. We recall that each
f\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
is of the form
f=\tilde{f}\sqrt{dV_{g}}
for some
\tilde{f}\in C^{\infty}(M)
, and each
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
is of the form
f=\tilde{f}(dV_{g})^{-1/2}
for some linear functional
\tilde{f}
on
C^{\infty}(M)
. Here
dV_{g}
is the Riemannian density on
M
.

Finally, for
\kappa\in K
, we write
\kappa^{*}
for pullback by
\kappa
, acting on either smooth or distributional half densities, and
\kappa_{*}:=(\kappa^{*})^{-1}
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
consists of those
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
such that
\kappa_{*}(\psi_{k}f)\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
\kappa\in K
, endowed with the norm
\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)}:=\|(\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}f))_{\kappa\in K}\|_{\ell^{p}(K;\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}
.

More explicitly,

(2.9)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)}:=\Big(\sum_{\kappa\in K}\|\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}f)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

for
p<\infty
, and

(2.10)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(M)}:=\max_{\kappa\in K}\|\kappa_{*}(\psi_{\kappa}f)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Remark 2.6
.

Up to norm equivalence, the definition of
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
is independent of the choice of
K
and
(\psi_{\kappa})_{\kappa\in K}
as bove. This follows from a simple calculation, using (
2.8
) and the invariance of
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
under coordinate changes and multiplication by smooth cutoffs (see also
[
20
, Proposition 4.6]
).

Remark 2.7
.

Since
K
is finite, one could equally well replace the
\ell^{p}
norms in (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) by an
\ell^{q}
norm for any
1\leq q\leq\infty
. On the other hand, one can define
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
on general Riemannian manifolds with bounded geometry, a class which includes both compact manifolds and
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and for this larger class the
\ell^{p}
norm in (
2.9
) is natural, as is already evident on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see
[
20
, Theorem 3.6]
).

In
[
20
]
,
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
was defined in terms of collections of geodesic normal coordinate charts satisfying a uniformity condition on the overlap of their domains. For finite
K
, the latter condition is automatically satisfied. Moreover, although one could choose
K
to consist of geodesic normal coordinate charts, this assumption is not needed on compact manifolds, cf. Remark
2.6
.

The basic properties of
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
carry over to
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
, for all
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. For example,
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
is invariant under Fourier integral operators of order zero associated with a local canonical graph, cf.
[
20
, Theorem 4.16]
, and the Sobolev embeddings

(2.11)

\mathcal{H}^{s+s(p),p}(M)\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{s-s(p),p}(M)

hold, by
[
20
, Theorem 4.15]
. Here
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(M)
is defined in the same way as
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
, in local coordinates (in particular, (
2.11
) then follows directly from (
2.2
)). We also note that
\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
lies dense in
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
if
p<\infty
.

3.
Maximal theorems

In this section we prove various results about maximal functions, including in particular Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. We first prove two results concerning maximal functions for Fourier integral operators, from which we then derive statements about maximal functions associated with hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, complex spherical means and geodesic spheres on manifolds.

3.1.
Fourier integral operators

We first prove a theorem about maximal functions for the Fourier integral operators from Proposition
2.2
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
be an open conic subset, and let
\phi\in C^{\infty}(V)
be real-valued, positively homogeneous of degree
1
, and such that
\mathrm{rank}\,\partial_{\eta\eta}^{2}\phi(\eta)=n-1
for all
\eta\in V
. Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
\mathrm{supp}(a)\subseteq V^{\prime}\cup\{0\}
, for some conic
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
which is closed in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. Then, for all
p\in[1,\infty)
and
s>d(p)+\frac{1}{p}+m
, and for each compact interval
I\subseteq(0,\infty)
, there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

(3.1)

\big\|\sup_{t\in I}|e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

By Theorems
4.2
and
4.5
, (
3.1
) is essentially sharp for all
p\in[1,2]\cup[\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1},\infty)
if there exist a non-empty conic subset
V^{\prime\prime}\subseteq V^{\prime}
and
c^{\prime},C^{\prime}>0
such that
|a(\eta)|\geq c^{\prime}|\eta|^{m}
for all
\eta\in V^{\prime\prime}
with
|\eta|\geq C^{\prime}
.

Proof.

First consider
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then

(x,t)\mapsto e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f(x)
is a continuous function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times I
. Hence
\sup_{t\in I}|e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f(x)|
is a lower semi-continuous function of
x
, and as such measurable. This implies that the left-hand side of (
3.1
) is well-defined.

Now, to prove (
3.1
), we first deal with the low-frequency component of
a
. Let
r\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and note that
e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)r(D)
has kernel
K_{t}
given by

K_{t}(x,y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i((x-y)\cdot\eta+t\phi(\eta))}a(t\eta)r(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta,

for
t\in I
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. So integration by parts yields, for each
N\geq 0
, a
C_{N}\geq 0
such that
|x-y|^{N}|K_{t}(x,y)|\leq C_{N}
. Let
r^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
r(D)=r(D)r^{\prime}(D)
. Since
r^{\prime}(D)
is smoothing, Young’s inequality and (
2.2
) yield

\displaystyle\big\|\sup_{t\in I}|e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)r(D)f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup_{t\in I}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{t}(x,y)(r^{\prime}(D)f)(y)\mathrm{d}y\Big|^{p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|x-y|)^{-N}|(r^{\prime}(D)f)(y)|\mathrm{d}y\Big)^{p}\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\|r^{\prime}(D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}

for
N\geq 0
large enough. In the remainder, it thus suffices to show that

\big\|\sup_{t\in I}|e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

under the additional assumption that
\widehat{f}(\xi)=0
if
|\xi|\leq 1/2
.

Next, note that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in I}\|e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t\in I}\|e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s(p)+m,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Here we used the embeddings
\mathcal{H}^{s(p),p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for the first inequality, and (
2.3
) for the second one. Moreover, for each
\varepsilon>0
, one has

(3.3)

\Big(\int_{I}\|e^{ir\phi(D)}a(rD)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}r\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d(p)+m+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

by (
2.3
) and (
2.4
). The same arguments yield

(3.4)

\Big(\int_{I}\|\partial_{r}(e^{ir\phi(D)}a(rD)f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}r\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d(p)+m+1+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

since
\widehat{f}(\xi)=0
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|\xi|\leq 1/2
.

Now, if
(\psi_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a standard Littlewood–Paley decomposition, then the collection
(\psi_{k}(D))_{k=0}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
is uniformly bounded, given that the convolution operators
\psi_{k}(D)
have kernels that are uniformly in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Hence, by standard arguments and because
\widehat{f}(\xi)=0
if
|\xi|\leq 1/2
, it suffices to show that, for all
\varepsilon>0
and
k\geq 0
, one has

\big\|\sup_{t\in I}|e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim 2^{k(d(p)+\frac{1}{p}+m+\varepsilon)}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

if
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f}\,)\subseteq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid 2^{k-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{k+1}\}
.

Fix such
\varepsilon
and
k
, and let
t_{0}
be the left endpoint of
I
. Then one can use the fundamental theorem of calculus and Hölder’s inequality to write

\displaystyle|e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f(x)|^{p}=|e^{it_{0}\phi(D)}a(t_{0}D)f(x)|^{p}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\partial_{r}|e^{ir\phi(D)}a(rD)f(x)|^{p}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq|e^{it_{0}\phi(D)}a(t_{0}D)f(x)|^{p}

\displaystyle+p\Big(\int_{I}|e^{ir\phi(D)}a(rD)f(x)|^{p}\mathrm{d}r\Big)^{1/p^{\prime}}\Big(\int_{I}|\partial_{r}(e^{ir\phi(D)}a(rD)f)(x)|^{p}\mathrm{d}r\Big)^{1/p}

for all
t\in I
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Hence, after integrating in
x
and applying Hölder’s inequality once again, one can use (
3.2
), (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) to write

\displaystyle\big\|\sup_{t\in I}|e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{it_{0}\phi(D)}a(t_{0}D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}

\displaystyle+p\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{I}|e^{ir\phi(D)}a(rD)f(x)|^{p}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}x\!\Big)^{1/p^{\prime}}\!\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{I}|\partial_{r}(e^{ir\phi(D)}a(rD)f)(x)|^{p}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}x\!\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s(p)+m,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}+\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d(p)+m+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p/p^{\prime}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{d(p)+m+1+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\eqsim\big(2^{kp(s(p)+m)}+2^{k(d(p)+m+\varepsilon)\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}2^{k(d(p)+m+1+\varepsilon)}\big)\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}

\displaystyle\eqsim 2^{kp(d(p)+\frac{1}{p}+m+\varepsilon)}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p},

as required.

It remains to extend (
3.1
) to general
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Here it is relevant to note that, a priori, it is not even clear why the left-hand side of (
3.1
) is well defined for such
f
. Indeed, initially
e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f
is defined as a tempered distribution for each
t\in I
, but Proposition
2.2
shows that it can be identified with an element of
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We have to prove that there exists a set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
of measure zero such that
x\mapsto\sup_{t\in I}|e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f(x)|\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus U}(x)
is measurable and satisfies (
3.1
).

There exists a sequence
(f_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
converging to
f
in
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Set
F_{k}(x,t):=e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f_{k}(x)
for
k\geq 0
and
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times I
. By (
3.1
),
(F_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n};C(I))
, so it has a limit
F\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n};C(I))
satisfying

(3.5)

\big\|\sup_{t\in I}|F(\cdot,t)|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

We may also suppose that there exists a set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
of measure zero such that, for all
x\notin U
, one has
F_{k}(x,t)\to F(x,t)
, uniformly in
t\in I
.

On the other hand, by Proposition
2.2
(
1
), for each
t\in I
one has
F_{k}(\cdot,t)\to e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f(\cdot)
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as
k\to\infty
. So there exists a set
U_{t}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
of measure zero, and a subsequence
(F_{m_{k,t}})_{k=0}^{\infty}
of
(F_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}
, such that
F_{m_{k,t}}(x,t)\to e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f(x)
for
x\notin U_{t}
. Hence
e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f(x)=F(x,t)
for
x\notin U\cup U_{t}
. Since
e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f
is only determined up to sets of measure zero, we can choose a representative such that
e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f(x)\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus U}(x)=F(x,t)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Now apply (
3.5
).
∎

The following corollary contains Theorem
1.2
as a special case.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
,
\phi\in C^{\infty}(V)
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be as in Theorem
3.1
, and let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
s>d(p)+\frac{1}{p}+m
. Then, for all
t_{0}>0
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, one has

(3.6)

e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f(x)\to e^{it_{0}\phi(D)}a(t_{0}D)f(x)

as
t\to t_{0}
. Moreover, if
V=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and
a\equiv 1
, then (
3.1
) holds for any bounded interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, and (
3.6
) holds for all
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

First note that (
3.6
) holds for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
if
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since the Schwartz functions lie dense in
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if
p<\infty
, and because
d(p)+\frac{1}{p}\geq s(p)
, one can apply (
3.1
) in a standard manner to obtain (
3.6
).

Next, suppose that
V=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and that
a\equiv 1
, and let
I=[t_{1},t_{2}]\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a bounded interval. For
t_{3}<t_{1}
, one can use (
3.1
) and Proposition
2.2
(
1
) to write

\displaystyle\big\|\sup_{t\in I}|e^{it\phi(D)}f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\big\|\sup_{t_{1}-t_{3}\leq t\leq t_{2}-t_{3}}|e^{it\phi(D)}(e^{it_{3}\phi(D)}f)|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|e^{it_{3}\phi(D)}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Now the extension of (
3.6
) to
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
follows as before.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Consider the case where
p=\infty
in Theorem
3.1
. For all
s>d(\infty)+m
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, Proposition
2.2
yields
e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f\in\mathcal{H}^{s-d(\infty)-m,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subseteq L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for each
t>0
, with

(3.7)

\sup_{t\in I}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f(x)|\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for any compact interval
I\subseteq(0,\infty)
. Moreover, if
f\in\overline{\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{s,\infty}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
t\mapsto e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f
is continuous as an
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-valued map, and (
3.6
) holds as well. Finally, by Theorems
4.2
and
4.5
, (
3.7
) is essentially sharp under the same assumptions as mentioned below Theorem
3.1
.

The case where
p=\infty
in Theorem
3.4
and Propositions
3.11
and
3.14
below is straightforward for similar reasons.

Next, we consider a variable-coefficient version of Theorem
3.1
. However, unlike in the latter result, the symbols of the operators under consideration here have compact spatial support.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
T
be an operator as in Proposition
2.3
, satisfying (
2.7
). Then, for all
p\in[1,\infty)
and
s>d(p)+\frac{1}{p}+m
, there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

(3.8)

\big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|T_{t}f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Hence, for all
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, one has

(3.9)

T_{t}f(x)\to T_{t_{0}}f(x)

as
t\to t_{0}
.

Although (
3.8
), unlike (
3.1
), involves the supremum over all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, it should be noted that
T_{t}=0
for all
t
outside of a compact interval, by assumption. Similarly, (
3.9
) holds for all
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
because
T_{t}
is an FIO of order
m
for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

By Theorems
4.2
and
4.5
and Remark
2.4
, (
3.8
) is essentially sharp for all
p\in[1,2]\cup[\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1},\infty)
under mild assumptions on
a
.

Proof.

The proof is almost identical to that of Theorem
3.1
. Firstly, (
3.9
) follows from (
3.8
), and it suffices to prove (
3.8
) for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, just as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
. For such
f
the left-hand side of (
3.8
) is well-defined.

Now, to prove (
3.8
), the low-frequency component
T_{t}r(D)
of each
T_{t}
is a smoothing operator for
r\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. One can therefore apply Young’s inequality and (
2.2
) to this term. On the other hand, the arguments from the proof of Theorem
3.1
and the bounds in Proposition
2.3
can be combined to deal with the high frequencies.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

If, in Theorem
3.1
, one does not require that
\mathrm{rank}\,\partial_{\eta\eta}^{2}\phi(\eta)=n-1
for all
\eta\in V
, then one still has

(3.10)

\big\|\sup_{t\in I}|e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
p\in[1,\infty)
,
s>s(p)+\frac{1}{p}+m
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. This follows from the same arguments, except that one cannot rely on (
2.4
) to bound the left-hand sides of (
3.3
) and (
3.4
). Instead, for all
\varepsilon>0
and
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\widehat{f}(\xi)=0
for
|\xi|\leq 1/2
, (
2.3
) and (
2.2
) yield

\Big(\int_{I}\|e^{ir\phi(D)}a(rD)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}r\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s(p)+m+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

and

\Big(\int_{I}\|\partial_{r}(e^{ir\phi(D)}a(rD)f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\mathrm{d}r\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s(p)+m+1+\varepsilon,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

after which the proof proceeds as before. The appropriate extension of (
3.6
) holds as well. Note that, for
1\leq p\leq 2
, (
3.10
) coincides with (
3.1
). In this range (
3.10
) is essentially sharp under mild assumptions on
a
, as long as
\phi
is not identically zero on the support of

, by Theorem
4.5
.

A similar remark applies to Theorem
3.4
, in the case where (
2.7
) does not hold.

3.2.
Hypersurfaces

In this subsection we use Theorems
3.1
and
3.4
to prove results about maximal functions associated with hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

For
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
a (smooth) hypersurface,
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Sigma)
,
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, set

(3.11)

\mathcal{A}_{t}f(x):=\int_{\Sigma}f(x-ty)\psi(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)

for
t>0
, and

(3.12)

\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma,\psi}f(x):=\sup_{1\leq t\leq 2}|\mathcal{A}_{t}f(x)|.

Here and throughout,
\mathrm{d}\sigma
is the induced Lebesgue measure on
\Sigma
. The following result contains Theorem
1.1
as a special case. By Theorem
4.8
, it is essentially sharp for all
p\in[1,2]\cup[\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1},\infty]
whenever
\psi
is not identically zero.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a hypersurface with non-vanishing Gaussian curvature, and let
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Sigma)
. Then, for all
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s>d(p)+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{n-1}{2}
, there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

(3.13)

\|\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma,\psi}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

In (
3.12
), one could replace the supremum over
[1,2]
by the supremum over an arbitrary compact interval
I\subseteq(0,\infty)
, as follows from the proof.

Proof.

Fix
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then the left-hand side of (
3.13
) is well defined. Moreover,
\mathcal{A}_{t}f=b(tD)f
, where
b:=\widehat{\psi\mathrm{d}\sigma}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
b(tD)f
is initially defined as a tempered distribution. In fact, after decomposing the support of
\psi
into finitely many pieces and changing coordinates, we may suppose that

b(\xi)=a(\xi)e^{i\phi(\xi)}+e(\xi)

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see e.g.
[
36
, Section VIII.5.7]
or
[
33
, Theorem 1.2.1]
). Here
\phi\in C^{\infty}(V)
is real-valued and positively homogeneous of degree
1
, defined on an open conic subset
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, and
\mathrm{rank}\,\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi(\xi)=n-1
for all
\xi\in V
. Also,
a\in S^{-(n-1)/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies
\mathrm{supp}(a)\subseteq V^{\prime}\cup\{0\}
for some conic
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
which is closed in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, and
e\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
m\in\mathbb{R}
. It follows that

\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma,\psi}f(x)\leq\sup_{1\leq t\leq 2}|e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f(x)|+|e(tD)f(x)|

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Now, for each
s>d(p)+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{n-1}{2}
, Theorem
3.1
and Remark
3.3
yield

\big\|\sup_{1\leq t\leq 2}|e^{it\phi(D)}a(tD)f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

On the other hand, by (
3.10
) and Proposition
2.2
(both with
\phi\equiv 0
and
m=d(p)-s(p)-\frac{n-1}{2}
), one has

\big\|\sup_{1\leq t\leq 2}|e(tD)f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

as well, thereby concluding the proof.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

It is natural to ask whether one can extend (
3.13
) to more general
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Here one approach is to recall that, for
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, one has
\mathcal{A}_{t}f=b(tD)f
for
b=\widehat{\psi\mathrm{d}\sigma}
. Since
b(tD):\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
continuously,
\mathcal{A}_{t}f
can be extended in a distributional sense to general
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover,
b(tD)f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, as follows from Proposition
2.2
and the proof of Theorem
3.6
, so that this extension of (
3.12
) also makes sense pointwise almost everywhere. Of course, there is a subtlety here regarding the choice of a pointwise representative of
b(tD)f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for different values of
t\in[1,2]
, but this can be dealt with as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
. By approximation, (
3.13
) then extends to all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if
p<\infty
.

On the other hand, one might wonder whether this extension of
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma,\psi}f
can again be expressed using integrals as in (
3.11
). By the reasoning in
[
36
, Section XI.3.5]
, this is indeed the case if, for example,
p<\infty
and
f\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. On the other hand, if
p>\frac{2}{n-1}
and
s\in(d(p)+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{n-1}{2},s(p))
, then there exist
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
which are not contained in
L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For such
f
it is less clear how to make sense of the integral in (
3.11
).

A similar remark applies to Theorem
3.9
and Proposition
3.15
below.

Next, we consider a version of Theorem
3.6
where the integral in (
3.11
) is taken over a hypersurface
\Sigma_{x,t}
which depends smoothly on
x
and
t
. More precisely, for
\Psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, set

(3.14)

\Sigma_{x,t}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\Psi_{t}(x,y)=0\}

where
\Psi_{t}(x,y):=\Psi(x,t,y)
. We will assume that the following two conditions hold:

(1)

For all
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
y\in\Sigma_{x,t}
, one has
\partial_{x}\Psi_{t}(x,y)\neq 0
,
\partial_{y}\Psi_{t}(x,y)\neq 0
, and

(3.15)

\det\begin{pmatrix}0&\partial_{y}\Psi_{t}(x,y)\\
\partial_{x}\Psi_{t}(x,y)&\partial_{xy}^{2}\Psi_{t}(x,y)\end{pmatrix}\neq 0.

Here we interpret
\partial_{y}\Psi_{t}
as a row vector and
\partial_{x}\Psi_{t}
as a column vector.

(2)

For all
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, the conical hypersurface

(3.16)

\{(\theta\partial_{x}\Psi_{t}(x,y),\theta\partial_{t}\Psi_{t}(x,y))\mid y\in\Sigma_{x,t},\theta\neq 0\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}

has
n-1
non-vanishing principal curvatures.

Remark 3.8
.

We note that (
1
) is the
rotational curvature
condition from
[
25
]
. It implies that
\Sigma_{x,t}
is a hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and (
3.15
) is equivalent to the statement that the Jacobian of
(y,\theta)\mapsto(\theta\partial_{x}\Psi_{t}(x,y),\Psi_{t}(x,y))
is invertible where
\theta=1
. On the other hand, (
2
) says that the conormal bundle

\{(x,t,\theta\partial_{x}\Psi_{t}(x,y),\theta\partial_{t}\Psi_{t}(x,y),y,-\theta\partial_{y}\Psi_{t}(x,y))\mid x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},t\in\mathbb{R},y\in\Sigma_{x,t},\theta\neq 0\}

of
\{(x,t,y)\mid(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},y\in\Sigma_{x,t}\}
satisfies the cinematic curvature condition from
[
32
]
(see also
[
33
, Section 8.1]
and Remark
2.4
).

Let
\mathrm{d}\sigma_{x,t}
be Lebesgue measure restricted to
\Sigma_{x,t}
, and let
\rho\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, set

(3.17)

T_{t}f(x):=\int_{\Sigma_{x,t}}f(y)\rho(x,t,y)\mathrm{d}\sigma_{x,t}(y).

We then obtain a version of Theorem
3.6
for varying hypersurfaces. By Theorem
4.9
(see also Remark
4.10
), this result is essentially sharp for all
p\in[1,2]\cup[\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1},\infty]
whenever
\rho
is not identically zero.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\Psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that (
1
) and (
2
) hold, and let
\rho\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, let
T_{t}
be as in (
3.17
). Then, for all
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s>d(p)+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{n-1}{2}
, there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

(3.18)

\big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|T_{t}f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

We will reduce to the setting of Theorem
3.4
by decomposing into finitely many operators and applying changes of coordinates. However, some care has to be taken to deal with the fact that (
3.18
) is not invariant under general changes of coordinates in the
(x,t)
variable in (
3.17
).

Note that

(3.19)

T_{t}f(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\theta\Psi_{t}(x,y)}\rho_{0}(x,t,y)f(y)\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}y

for all
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\rho_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R})
satisfies
\rho_{0}(x,t,y)=\rho(x,t,y)|\partial_{y}\Psi_{t}(x,y)|
for
y\in\Sigma_{x,t}
(see e.g.
[
36
, Section XI.3.1.2]
). Hence the kernel of
T_{t}
is a Lagrangian distribution of order
-(n-1)/2
, and
T_{t}
is a Fourier integral operator of order
-(n-1)/2
with canonical relation

\mathcal{C}_{t}:=\{(x,\theta\partial_{x}\Psi_{t}(x,y),y,-\theta\partial_{y}\Psi_{t}(x,y))\mid x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},y\in\Sigma_{x,t},\theta\neq 0\}.

Condition (
1
) implies that
\mathcal{C}_{t}
is a local canonical graph (see e.g.
[
33
, p. 171]
).

Next, set
Tf(x,t):=T_{t}f(x,t)
for
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Then the canonical relation of
T
is

\{(x,t,\theta\partial_{x}\Psi_{t}(x,y),\theta\partial_{t}\Psi_{t}(x,y),y,-\theta\partial_{y}\Psi_{t}(x,y))\mid(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},y\in\Sigma_{x,t},\theta\neq 0\}.

As noted in Remark
3.8
, it satisfies the cinematic curvature condition, due to (
2
).

Now, by e.g.
[
20
, Proposition A.2]
, there exists a collection
(T_{j})_{j=1}^{l}
of operators as in Proposition
2.3
, with
m=-(n-1)/2
, changes of coordinates
(S_{j})_{j=1}^{l}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and an operator
R
with a kernel which is a Schwartz function, such that
T=\sum_{j=1}^{l}T_{j}S_{j}+R
(that no changes of coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
are required is noted in
[
20
, Remark A.3]
). The smoothing operator
R
satisfies the required maximal function estimate, since it maps
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
continuously. Moreover,
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is invariant under the changes of coordinates
S_{j}
, and each
T_{j}
satisfies the required maximal function estimate, by Theorem
3.4
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

Clearly,
\Psi
does not need to be defined on all of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It suffices for conditions (
1
) and (
2
) to hold for all
(x,t,y)
in a neighborhood of
\mathrm{supp}(\rho)
.

Moreover, if
\Psi
satisfies (
1
) but not (
2
), then one still has

\big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|T_{t}f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
p\in[1,\infty]
,
s>s(p)+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{n-1}{2}
and
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. This follows as before, if one relies on Remark
3.5
instead of Theorem
3.4
.

Similarly, suppose that
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a hypersurface of the form
\Sigma=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\phi(y)=1\}
, for some real-valued
\phi\in C^{\infty}(V)
, positively homogeneous of degree
1
and defined on an open subset
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. Then, even if one does not assume that
\mathrm{rank}\,\partial_{\xi\xi}^{2}\phi(\xi)=n-1
for all
\xi\in V
, for each
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
one still has

\|M_{\Sigma,\psi}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
p\in[1,\infty]
,
s>s(p)+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{n-1}{2}
and
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. This follows as in the proof of Theorem
3.6
, but relying on (
3.10
) instead of Theorem
3.1
.

3.3.
Spherical means of complex order

In this subsection we derive from Theorem
3.1
maximal bounds for the complex spherical means from
[
35
]
.

For
\alpha,t>0
,
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, set

(3.20)

\mathcal{M}^{\alpha}_{t}f(x):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{B_{1}(0)}(1-|y|^{2})^{\alpha-1}f(x-ty)\mathrm{d}y.

Here
B_{1}(0)
is the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Note that
\mathcal{M}^{\alpha}_{t}f=K_{\alpha,t}\ast f
, where
K_{\alpha,t}(x):=t^{-n}K_{\alpha}(t^{-1}x)
and
K_{\alpha}(x):=\Gamma(\alpha)^{-1}\max((1-|x|^{2})^{\alpha-1},0)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. One has

(3.21)

m_{\alpha}(\xi):=\pi^{-\alpha+1}|\xi|^{-n/2-\alpha+1}J_{n/2+\alpha-1}(2\pi|\xi|)=\widehat{K_{\alpha}}(\xi)

for all
\xi\neq 0
, where
J_{\beta}
is the Bessel function of the first kind, of order
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
(see
[
37
, Theorem IV.4.15]
). For a general
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
one can then also define
m_{\alpha}
as in (
3.21
). In turn, this then leads to the definition of the complex spherical mean
\mathcal{M}^{\alpha}_{t}
, by setting

(3.22)

\mathcal{M}^{\alpha}_{t}f:=m_{\alpha}(tD)f

for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

We now obtain a maximal function bound for the complex spherical means. This result is essentially sharp for all
p\in[1,2]\cup[\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1},\infty)
, cf. Proposition
4.11
.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
. Then, for all
p\in[1,\infty)
and
s>d(p)+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{n-1}{2}-\Real(\alpha)
, and for each compact interval
I\subseteq(0,\infty)
, there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

\big\|\sup_{t\in I}|\mathcal{M}^{\alpha}_{t}f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

Note that, as follows from asymptotics of Bessel functions (see
[
38
, Chapters 3 and 7]
), there exist
a^{0}_{\alpha}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
a^{+}_{\alpha},a^{-}_{\alpha}\in S^{-\frac{n-1}{2}-\Real(\alpha)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

(3.23)

m_{\alpha}(\xi)=a_{\alpha}^{0}(\xi)+e^{i|\xi|}a^{+}_{\alpha}(\xi)+e^{-i|\xi|}a^{-}_{\alpha}(\xi)

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In particular,
m_{\alpha}(tD):\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
continuously for each
t>0
, and therefore
m_{\alpha}(tD):\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
continuously as well. Hence
\mathcal{M}^{\alpha}_{t}f
is a priori well defined, as a tempered distribution, for every
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Now, as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
one has

\big\|\sup_{t\in I}|a^{0}_{\alpha}(tD)f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Moreover, Theorem
3.1
yields the required bounds for the remaining terms in (
3.23
).
∎

Remark 3.12
.

The expression for
\mathcal{M}^{\alpha}_{t}f
in (
3.20
), for
\alpha>0
and
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, extends to
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Real(\alpha)>0
. Hence, if
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then Proposition
3.11
yields maximal function bounds for these integrals. As in Remark
3.7
, these estimates can in turn be extended to
f\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, the bounds in this subsection extend to
p=\infty
, as in Remark
3.3
.

Remark 3.13
.

For
\alpha=0
, one recovers the spherical maximal function from (
1.1
). On the other hand, for
\alpha=1
, Proposition
3.11
and the previous remark yield

(3.24)

\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup_{t\in I}\Big|\fint_{B_{t}(x)}f(y)\mathrm{d}y\Big|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
1\leq p<\infty
,
s>d(p)+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{n+1}{2}
and
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. If, additionally,
f
is non-negative, then the left-hand side of (
3.24
) is a version of the (centered) Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator where the radii of balls are restricted to a compact interval in
(0,\infty)
.

3.4.
Maximal functions on manifolds

In this subsection we derive versions of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
on compact manifolds.

Throughout, fix an
n
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
without boundary, and let
\Delta_{g}
be the associated (non-positive) Laplace–Beltrami operator. Then
\Delta_{g}
acts on half densities on
M
, as do the operators
e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}}
, defined for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
through the spectral theorem on
L^{2}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
.

We first consider an analogue of Theorem
1.2
on
M
. Due to the fact that
L^{p}(M)
is a proper subspace of
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
, this result improves upon
[
2
, Theorem 46]
.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
s>d(p)+\frac{1}{p}
. Then, for each bounded interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

(3.25)

\big\|\sup_{t\in I}|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}}f|\big\|_{L^{p}(M)}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)}

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
. Hence, for all
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and for almost all
x\in M
, one has

(3.26)

e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}}f(x)\to e^{it_{0}\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}}f(x)

as
t\to t_{0}
.

Proof.

Firstly, (
3.26
) follows from (
3.25
) as before, by approximating a given
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by elements of
\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
, for which (
3.26
) holds for every
x\in M
.

Now, for
f\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in M
, set
Tf(x,t):=e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}}f(x)
. By e.g.
[
17
, Theorem 29.1.1 and Proposition 29.1.9]
,
T
is an FIO of order
-1/4
from
M
to
M\times\mathbb{R}
, with canonical relation

(3.27)

\mathcal{C}=\{(x,t,\xi,\tau,y,\eta)\mid(x,\xi)=\chi_{t}(y,\eta),\tau=|\xi|_{g}\}.

Here
(\chi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is the geodesic flow on
T^{*}M
. In particular, each
e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}}
is a Fourier integral operator of order
0
, the canonical relation of which is the graph of
\chi_{t}
. This in turn means that
e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}}
acts continuously on
\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
, and
e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}}f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
is a priori well defined for each
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
. Moreover, if
f\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
then the left-hand side of (
3.25
) is well defined.

Finally,
\mathcal{C}
satisfies the cinematic curvature condition (see e.g.
[
33
, Section 8.1]
). Thus, by working in local coordinates, one can argue as in the proof of Theorem
3.9
to derive the required statement from Theorem
3.4
.
∎

Next, let
\mathrm{inj}(M)>0
be the injectivity radius of
M
. For
x\in M
and
t\in(0,\mathrm{inj}(M))
, let
B_{t}(x)\subseteq M
be the (open) geodesic ball around
x
of radius
t
, and let
S_{t}(x)
be its boundary, the geodesic sphere of radius
t
around
x
. Set

(3.28)

\mathcal{A}_{t,g}f(x):=\int_{S_{t}(x)}f(y)\sqrt{dV_{g}}

for
t\in(0,\mathrm{inj}(M))
,
f\in C(M,\Omega_{1/2})
and
x\in M
.

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s>d(p)+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{n-1}{2}
. Then, for each compact interval
I\subseteq(0,\mathrm{inj}(M))
, there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

\big\|\sup_{t\in I}|\mathcal{A}_{t,g}f|\big\|_{L^{p}(M)}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)}

for all
f\in C(M,\Omega_{1/2})\cap\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
.

Proof.

If one sets
Tf(x,t):=\mathcal{A}_{t,g}f(x)
for
f\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
and
(x,t)\in M\times I
, then the canonical relation of
T
is the union of the relation in (
3.27
) and the corresponding one with the
\tau
coordinate replaced by
-\tau
. Since each
\mathcal{A}_{t,g}
is an FIO of order
-(n-1)/2
, one can now argue as in the proof of Proposition
3.14
.

Alternatively, one can reason as follows. It suffices to work in a sufficiently small geodesic normal coordinate neighborhood of a given point
x_{0}\in M
. There each
\mathcal{A}_{t,g}
is as in (
3.17
), with
\Sigma_{x_{0},t}=tS^{n-1}
and
\Psi_{t}(x_{0},y)=|x_{0}-y|-t
. Then
\Psi_{t}(x,y)
is a small perturbation of
\tilde{\Psi}_{t}(x,y):=|x-y|-t
for
x
near
x_{0}
. Since
\tilde{\Psi}_{t}
satisfies conditions (
1
) and (
2
), and because these conditions are stable under small perturbations, the required statement follows from Theorem
3.9
.
∎

4.
Sharpness

In this section we show that the results from the previous section are essentially sharp for suitable values of
p\in[1,\infty]
. We first consider the maximal function bounds for Fourier integral operators. We then prove sharpness of the maximal function estimates for hypersurfaces, and for complex spherical averages, by reducing to the corresponding statements for Fourier integral operators. Finally, we obtain sharpness of the maximal function bounds on manifolds by working in local coordinates.

4.1.
Fourier integral operators

Throughout this subsection, we consider operators as in (
2.5
). More precisely, let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
be an open conic subset, and let
\Phi\in C^{\infty}(V)
be real-valued and positively homogenous of degree
1
in the fiber variable. Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
a\in S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
(z,\eta)\in V
whenever
(z,\eta)\in\mathrm{supp}(a)
and
\eta\neq 0
. Suppose that
\mathrm{rank}\,\partial_{x\eta}^{2}\Phi(z_{0},\eta_{0})=n
for all
(z_{0},\eta_{0})\in\mathrm{supp}(a)
with
\eta_{0}\neq 0
, where we write
z=(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
. Set

(4.1)

Tf(z):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\Phi(z,\eta)}a(z,\eta)\widehat{f}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta

for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, and
T_{t}f(x):=Tf(x,t)
. Then, cf.
[
17
]
,
T
is a Fourier integral operator of order
m-1/4
, and each
T_{t}
is a Fourier integral operator of order
m
associated with a local canonical graph.

To prove sharpness of the results in Section
3.1
, we will rely on the following lemma, an improvement of
[
20
, Lemma 7.1]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
m=0
and that there exists a non-empty conic
V^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
such that
a(0,\eta)=1
and
\Phi(0,\eta)=0

for all
\eta\in V^{\prime}
. Then there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

|Th(z)-(2\pi)^{n}h(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu))|\leq C\|\widehat{h}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|z|(1+\gamma(h,\nu))

for all
h\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{h})\subseteq V^{\prime}
compact, and all
\nu\in V^{\prime}\cap S^{n-1}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Here

\gamma(h,\nu):=\sup\{|\hat{\eta}-\nu|^{2}|\eta|\mid\eta\in\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{h})\}.

Proof.

The proof follows that of
[
20
, Lemma 7.1]
, but ends with an additional step.

Fix
\nu\in S^{n-1}\cap V^{\prime}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, and write

\displaystyle Th(z)-(2\pi)^{n}h(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu))=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(e^{i\Phi(z,\eta)}a(z,\eta)-e^{i\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu)\cdot\eta}\big)\widehat{h}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\Phi(z,\eta)}(a(z,\eta)-1)\widehat{h}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(e^{i\Phi(z,\eta)}-e^{i\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu)\cdot\eta}\big)\widehat{h}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta.

We will bound each of these terms separately. For the first term, simply note that

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\Phi(z,\eta)}(a(z,\eta)-1)\widehat{h}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta\Big|\lesssim\|\widehat{h}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|z|.

Here we used Taylor approximation and the assumptions that
m=0
and that
a(0,\eta)=1
for all
\eta\in\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{h})
.

On the other hand, for the second term we use the homogeneity of
\Phi
to write:

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(e^{i\Phi(z,\eta)}-e^{i\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu)\cdot\eta}\big)\widehat{h}(\eta)\mathrm{d}\eta\Big|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|e^{i(\Phi(z,\eta)-\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu)\cdot\eta)}-1\big|\,|\widehat{h}(\eta)|\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|e^{i(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\hat{\eta})-\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu))\cdot\eta}-1\big|\,|\widehat{h}(\eta)|\mathrm{d}\eta\leq\sqrt{2}\|\widehat{h}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\delta(h,\nu,z),

where

\delta(h,\nu,z):=\sup\{|\eta\cdot(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu)-\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\hat{\eta}))|\mid\eta\in\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{h})\}.

Here we also used that
|e^{ix}-1|=|\cos(x)-1+i\sin(x)|\leq\sqrt{2}x
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

It thus remains to show that
\delta(h,\nu,z)\lesssim|z|\gamma(h,\nu)
. To this end, for
\eta\in\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{h})
, we apply another Taylor expansion and write
\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu)-\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\hat{\eta})
as

\partial_{\eta\eta}^{2}\Phi(z,\hat{\eta})(\nu-\hat{\eta})+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},|\alpha|=2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)\partial_{\eta}^{\alpha}\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,(1-r)\nu+r\hat{\eta})\mathrm{d}r\cdot(\nu-\hat{\eta})^{\alpha}.

Moreover,
\partial_{\eta_{i}}\Phi(z,\eta)
is positively homogeneous of order zero in
\eta
for every
1\leq i\leq n
, because
\Phi(z,\eta)
is positively homogeneous of order one in
\eta
. In particular, the radial derivative
(\eta\cdot\partial_{\eta})\partial_{\eta_{i}}\Phi(z,\hat{\eta})
vanishes for every
1\leq i\leq n
. Hence

\eta\cdot\big(\partial_{\eta\eta}^{2}\Phi(z,\hat{\eta})(\nu-\hat{\eta})\big)=\big(\eta^{t}\partial_{\eta\eta}^{2}\Phi(z,\hat{\eta})\big)\cdot(\nu-\hat{\eta})=\big((\eta\cdot\partial_{\eta})\partial_{\eta_{i}}\Phi(z,\hat{\eta})\big)_{i=1}^{n}\cdot(\nu-\hat{\eta})=0.

We thus obtain that

|\eta\cdot(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu)-\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\hat{\eta}))|\leq|\eta|\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},|\alpha|=2}\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{\eta}^{\alpha}\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,(1-r)\nu+r\hat{\eta})|\mathrm{d}r|\nu-\hat{\eta}|^{2}.

Finally, note that
\partial_{\eta}^{\alpha}\partial_{\eta}^{2}\Phi(0,(1-r)\nu+r\hat{\eta})=0
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
and
0\leq r\leq 1
, since
\Phi(0,\eta^{\prime})=0
for all
\eta^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
. Hence for each
r
we can use another Taylor approximation, this time with respect to
z
, to obtain

|\eta\cdot(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu)-\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\hat{\eta}))|\lesssim|z|\,|\hat{\eta}-\nu|^{2}|\eta|.\qed

Now, one clearly has

\|T_{t_{0}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|T_{t}f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for any
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
d(p)+\frac{1}{p}=s(p)=\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})
for
p\geq\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}
. Hence the following result shows that, under mild conditions on the symbol of the operator, the maximal function bounds in Theorems
3.1
and
3.4
, and in Corollary
3.2
and Remark
3.3
, are essentially sharp for such
p
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Suppose that there exist a
z_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, a non-empty open conic set
V^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and
c^{\prime},C^{\prime}>0
such that
|a(z_{0},\eta)|\geq c^{\prime}|\eta|^{m}
for all
\eta\in V^{\prime}
with
|\eta|\geq C^{\prime}
. If there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

(4.2)

\|T_{t_{0}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
s\geq\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})+m
.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to that of
[
20
, Theorem 7.3]
. However, given that the setting under consideration presently is slightly different, and because we will use some of the ideas later on, we provide most of the details.

We first make several reduction steps. By translating and precomposing with
\langle D\rangle^{-m}
, we may suppose that
z_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})=0
and
m=0
. Then, by adding a harmless smoothing term which modifies the low frequencies of
a
, we may suppose that
|a(0,\eta)|\geq c^{\prime}
for all
\eta\in V^{\prime}
.

Next, by shrinking
V^{\prime}
, we may suppose that the closure of
V^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
is a strict subset of a closed cone
V_{0}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
such that
|a(0,\eta)|\geq c^{\prime}
for all
\eta\in V_{0}
. Let
\rho\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\subseteq V_{0}
and
\rho(\eta)=1
for all
\eta\in V^{\prime}
, and set

\theta(\eta):=e^{-i\Phi(0,\eta)}\frac{\rho(\eta)}{a(0,\eta)}

for
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By Proposition
2.2
,
\theta(D)
is bounded on
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and (
4.2
) implies that
\|T_{0}\theta(D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover,
T\theta(D)
is an operator as in (
4.1
), with phase function
\widetilde{\Phi}
and symbol
\widetilde{a}
satisfying
\widetilde{\Phi}(0,\eta)=0
and
\widetilde{a}(0,\eta)=1
for
\eta\in V^{\prime}
. By replacing
T
by
T\theta(D)
, we may thus suppose in the remainder that
\Phi(0,\eta)=0
and
a(0,\eta)=1
for all
\eta\in V^{\prime}
.

After these reduction steps, we get to the heart of the proof. For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, let
\Theta_{k}\subseteq S^{n-1}
be a maximal collection of unit vectors such that
|\nu-\nu^{\prime}|\geq 2^{-k/2}
for all
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in\Theta_{k}
with
\nu\neq\nu^{\prime}
. Let
V^{\prime\prime}\subseteq V^{\prime}
be a non-empty cone which is closed in
V^{\prime}
, and fix a
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that
V_{k}:=\Theta_{k}\cap V^{\prime\prime}
has approximately
2^{k(n-1)/2}
elements. Note that this is true for all large
k
, since
V^{\prime\prime}
has a nonzero aperture. Next, let
(\chi_{\nu})_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\subseteq C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
be an associated partition of unity. In particular, each
\chi_{\nu}
is positively homogeneous of degree
0
and satisfies
0\leq\chi_{\nu}\leq 1
,

\mathrm{supp}(\chi_{\nu})\subseteq\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\mid|\hat{\eta}-\nu|\leq 2^{-k/2+1}\},

and
\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\chi_{\nu}(\eta)=1
for all
\eta\neq 0
. We may also suppose that
\chi_{\nu}(\eta)=1
whenever
|\hat{\eta}-\nu|\leq 2^{-k/2-1}
.
Finally, fix a
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\widehat{\psi}
is non-negative and not identically zero, and such that
\widehat{\psi}(\eta)=0
if
|\eta|>c
, for some small constant
c>0
.

Now, for
\nu\in V_{k}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, set

(4.3)

f_{\nu}(x):=e^{i2^{k}\nu\cdot x}\psi(2^{k}(\nu\cdot x)\nu+2^{k/2}\Pi_{\nu}^{\perp}x),

where
\Pi_{\nu}^{\perp}
is the orthogonal projection onto the complement of the span of
\nu
. Since
V^{\prime\prime}
is closed in
V^{\prime}
, by choosing
c
sufficiently small, we may suppose that

\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f_{\nu}})\subseteq V^{\prime}\cap\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid 2^{k-1}\leq|\eta|\leq 2^{k+1},|\hat{\eta}-\nu|\leq 2^{-k/2-1}\}

for each
\nu
. In particular,
\chi_{\nu}(D)f_{\nu}=f_{\nu}
. Note also that
\|\widehat{f_{\nu}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim 1
and
\|f_{\nu}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim 2^{-k\frac{n+1}{2p}}
, for implicit constants independent of
\nu
and
k
. Set
f:=\sum_{\nu\in V_{k}}f_{\nu}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then
\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f}\,)\subseteq V^{\prime}\cap\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid 2^{k-1}\leq|\eta|\leq 2^{k+1}\}
and

(4.4)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim 2^{k(s+\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}))}\Big(\sum_{\nu\in\Theta_{k}}\|f_{\nu}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\eqsim 2^{k(s+\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{1}{p})},

with the obvious modification for
p=\infty
, as follows from
[
27
, Proposition 4.1]
.

Next, note that
\Phi
and all of its derivatives are bounded on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
. Hence
z\mapsto\partial_{\eta}\Phi(z,\nu)
is a locally Lipschitz flow for each
\nu\in V_{k}
, with Lipschitz constants independent of
\nu
. Moreover, by assumption,
\partial_{\eta}\Phi(0,\nu)=0
and
f_{\nu}(0)=\|\widehat{\psi}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}>0
for each
\nu
. It thus follows that
\Real(f_{\nu}(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(x,0,\nu)))\gtrsim 1
whenever
|x|\lesssim 2^{-k}
for some implicit constant independent of
k
and
\nu
. On the other hand,
|\hat{\eta}-\nu|^{2}|\eta|\lesssim 1
for all
\eta\in\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f_{\nu}}\,)
. Hence Lemma
4.1
yields

|T_{0}f_{\nu}(x)-(2\pi)^{n}f_{\nu}(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(x,0,\nu))|\lesssim|x|

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We thus see that

\Real T_{0}f_{\nu}(x)\geq(2\pi)^{n}\Real f_{\nu}(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(x,0,\nu))-|T_{0}f_{\nu}(x)-(2\pi)^{n}f_{\nu}(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(x,0,\nu))|\gtrsim 1

if
|x|\lesssim 2^{-k}
. Finally, we can combine (
4.2
) and (
4.4
):

\displaystyle 2^{-k\frac{n}{p}}2^{k\frac{n-1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big|\sum_{\nu\in V_{k}}T_{0}f_{\nu}(x)\Big|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{1/p}=\|T_{0}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\eqsim 2^{k(s+\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{1}{p})},

with the obvious modification for
p=\infty
. This implies the required statement.
∎

Note that Theorem
3.1
is in fact a statement about a single Fourier integral operator acting on function spaces over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and the implicit time parameter plays no role in the proof. Combined with the Sobolev embeddings in (
2.2
), the theorem shows that the bounds in Proposition
2.2
(
1
) and Proposition
2.3
(
1
) are sharp under very mild assumptions, for
p\in[2,\infty)
. It was already shown in
[
27
,
20
]
that Proposition (
2.2
) (
2
) and Proposition
2.3
(
2
) are sharp under the same assumptions.

Remark 4.4
.

The conclusion of Theorem
4.2
essentially follows from
[
30
]
(see also
[
33
, pp. 185-186]
) if the projection of the canonical relation

\{(x,\partial_{x}\Phi(x,t_{0},\eta)),(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(x,t_{0},\eta),\eta)\mid(x,\eta)\in V\}\subseteq T^{*}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times T^{*}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

of
T_{t_{0}}
onto the base space
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
has full rank at
x_{0}
, and in that case we may replace (
4.2
) by the weaker assumption that

(4.5)

T_{t_{0}}:\mathcal{H}^{s+s(p),p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

is bounded. This assumption on the rank of the projection is satisfied in the setting of Theorem
1.2
and, combined with some of the arguments later on in this section, also in the results in Sections
3.2
,
3.3
and
3.4
. On the other hand, (
4.5
) definitely does not imply that
s\geq s(p)
for general
\Phi
as in Theorem
4.2
, since the latter result also applies when, for example,
T_{t_{0}}
is the identity operator.

Next, we consider sharpness of the results in Section
3.1
for
p\in[1,2]
. Here one cannot expect to rely on an approach as general as in Theorem
4.2
. Indeed, let
T_{t_{0}}
be a Fourier integral operator of order
m
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, associated with a local canonical graph and with a principal symbol which is not identically zero. Then one may set
Tf(x,t):=\chi(t)T_{t_{0}}f(x)
for
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, where
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
is not identically zero. Now
T
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
4.2
, and

\big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|T_{t}f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim\|T_{t_{0}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

However, an inequality as in (
4.2
) merely implies that
s\geq s(p)
, whereas
s(p)<d(p)+\frac{1}{p}
for all
p\in[1,2]
. Instead, one has to take the dependence of the phase on the variable
t
into account.

We will now show that nontrivial dependence of the phase on
t
is in fact the only additional assumption required to prove the desired sharpness bound. The following result implies in particular that, under mild conditions on the symbol of the operator, the maximal function bounds in Theorems
3.1
and
3.4
, as well as in Corollary
3.2
and Remark
3.3
, are essentially sharp for
1\leq p\leq 2
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Suppose that there exist a
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, a non-empty open conic set
V^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and
c^{\prime},C^{\prime}>0
such that
|a(z_{0},\eta)|\geq c^{\prime}|\eta|^{m}
and

\partial_{t}\Phi(x_{0},t,\eta)|_{t=t_{0}}\neq 0
for all
\eta\in V^{\prime}
with
|\eta|\geq C^{\prime}
. If there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

(4.6)

\big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|T_{t}f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
s\geq\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})+\frac{1}{p}+m
.

Proof.

In the same way as in the proof of Theorem
4.2
, one may reduce to the case where
z_{0}=0
,
m=0
, and
\Phi(0,\eta)=0
and
a(0,\eta)=1
for all
\eta\in V^{\prime}
.
Fix a
\nu\in V^{\prime}
. Then, by precomposing with the change of coordinates
y\mapsto\partial_{\eta x}^{2}\Phi(0,\nu)^{-1}y
, which is a diffeomorphism on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by the assumptions on
\Phi
and therefore induces an automorphism of
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we may suppose additionally that
\partial_{x\eta}^{2}\Phi(0,\nu)
is the identity matrix. Finally, after rotating, we may assume that
\nu=e_{1}
, which will be of minor notational convenience.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
\widehat{\psi}
is non-negative and not identically zero, and such that
\widehat{\psi}(\eta)=0
if
|\eta|>\theta
, for some small parameter
\theta>0
to be fixed later. Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, and let
f
be as in (
4.3
):

f(x):=e^{i2^{k}x_{1}}\psi(2^{k}x_{1},2^{k/2}x^{\prime})

for
x=(x_{1},x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By choosing
\theta
sufficiently small, we may suppose that

\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f}\,)\subseteq V^{\prime}\cap\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid 2^{k-1}\leq|\eta|\leq 2^{k+1},|\hat{\eta}-e_{1}|\leq 2^{-k/2}\}.

Then
\|\widehat{f}\,\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim 1
and
\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim 2^{-k\frac{n+1}{2p}}
, and in particular

(4.7)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim 2^{k(s+\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}))}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim 2^{k(s+\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{n+1}{2p})},

by
[
27
, Proposition 2.2]
. Here the implicit constants depend on
\theta
, but this does not matter since we will only apply (
4.7
) once we have already fixed
\theta
. Finally, there exist
c_{0},\delta>0
such that

(4.8)

\Real f(x)\geq c_{0}

whenever
|x_{1}|\leq\delta 2^{-k}
and
|x^{\prime}|\leq\delta 2^{-k/2}
.

Next, set

E_{0}:=\{y=(y_{1},y^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid y_{1}=0,|y^{\prime}|\leq\theta 2^{-k/2}\}.

Then, for every
y\in E_{0}
, the curve
\gamma_{y}
defined by

\partial_{\eta}\Phi(\gamma_{y}(t),t,e_{1})=\partial_{\eta}\Phi(y,0,e_{1})

is well defined and smooth in a neighborhood of zero, by the implicit function theorem. Moreover, by homogeneity,

-e_{1}\cdot\gamma_{0}^{\prime}(0)=e_{1}\cdot\partial_{t}\partial_{\eta}\Phi(0,0,e_{1})=\partial_{t}(e_{1}\cdot\partial_{\eta}\Phi(0,0,e_{1}))=\partial_{t}\Phi(0,0,e_{1})\neq 0.

So in a small neighborhood of zero in
E_{0}
, at
t=0
the curves
\gamma_{y}
all point out of the plane where
x_{1}=0
, and in particular out of
E_{0}
. Hence, by shrinking
\theta
even more, we may suppose that the curves
\gamma_{y}
are mutually disjoint as
y
ranges over
E_{0}
. Set

E:=\cup_{y\in E_{0}}\gamma_{y}([-\theta,\theta]).

Then, since the
(n-1)
-dimensional measure of
E_{0}
is approximately
2^{-k\frac{n-1}{2}}
, the
n
-dimensional measure of
E
is approximately
2^{-k\frac{n-1}{2}}
as well. Again, here the implicit constants depend on
\theta
, but this will not matter once
\theta
has been fixed.

By definition, for each
x\in E
there exist
y\in E_{0}
and
t\in[-\theta,\theta]
such that
\partial_{\eta}\Phi(x,t,e_{1})=\partial_{\eta}\Phi(y,0,e_{1})
. Taylor approximation, combined with the assumptions on
\Phi
, show that the latter equals

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta}\Phi(0,0,e_{1})+\partial_{x\eta}^{2}\Phi(0,0,e_{1})y+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},|\alpha|=2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\eta}\Phi(ry,0,e_{1})\mathrm{d}r\cdot y^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=y+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},|\alpha|=2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\eta}\Phi(ry,0,e_{1})\mathrm{d}r\cdot y^{\alpha}.

Since
y_{1}=0
and
|y^{\prime}|\leq\theta 2^{-k/2}
, we obtain from this that the first coordinate of
\partial_{\eta}\Phi(x,t,e_{1})
is bounded in absolute value by

|y_{1}|+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},|\alpha|=2}\Big|\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\eta}\Phi(ry,0,e_{1})\mathrm{d}r\Big||y|^{\alpha}\lesssim\theta 2^{-k}.

On the other hand, the remaining coordinates are bounded by

|y^{\prime}|+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n},|\alpha|=2}\Big|\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\eta}\Phi(ry,0,e_{1})\mathrm{d}r\Big||y|^{\alpha}\lesssim\theta 2^{-k/2}.

Thus, by (
4.8
), we can choose
\theta
small enough such that

(4.9)

\Real f(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(x,t,e_{1}))\geq c_{0}

for every
x\in E
and a suitable
t\in[-\theta,\theta]
.

We can now derive the crucial estimate. Indeed, Lemma
4.1
shows that

|T_{t}f(x)-(2\pi)^{n}f(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(x,t,e_{1}))|\lesssim\|\widehat{f}\,\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|(x,t)|\lesssim\theta

for all
x\in E
and
t\in[-\theta,\theta]
. So (
4.9
) implies that

\Real T_{t}f(x)\geq(2\pi)^{n}\Real f(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(x,t,e_{1}))-|T_{t}f(x)-(2\pi)^{n}f(\partial_{\eta}\Phi(x,0,e_{1}))|\gtrsim 1

for the appropriate
t\in[-\theta,\theta]
, where we possibly had to shrink
\theta
again. Fix the resulting
\theta
.

Then the previous bound, combined with the estimate for
|E|
as well as (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), allows us to conclude the proof. Indeed, one has

\displaystyle 2^{-k\frac{n-1}{2p}}

\displaystyle\eqsim|E|^{1/p}\lesssim\Big(\int_{E}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|T_{t}f(x)|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\eqsim 2^{k(s+\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{n+1}{2p})}.

After rearranging and letting
k\to\infty
, this concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

Combined, Theorem
4.2
and
4.5
show that, under the assumptions of Theorem
4.5
, for general
p\in[1,\infty]
one has

s\geq\begin{cases}s(p)+\frac{1}{p}+m&\text{if }1\leq p\leq 2,\\
-s(p)+\frac{1}{p}+m&\text{if }2\leq p\leq\frac{2n}{n-1},\\
s(p)+m&\text{if }\frac{2n}{n-1}\leq p\leq\infty.\end{cases}

The same holds for the other sharpness statements in this section. We do not know whether the corresponding results in Section
3
can be improved for
2<p<\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}
.

Remark 4.7
.

Recall that, under the assumptions of Theorems
3.1
and
3.4
, the Sobolev embeddings in (
2.2
) yield an inequality of the form

(4.10)

\big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|T_{t}f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
p\in[1,2]
,
s>2s(p)+\frac{1}{p}+m
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. On the other hand, the arguments in the proof of Theorem
4.5
merely show that an inequality such as (
4.10
) implies that
s>\frac{1}{p}+m
, due to the discrepancy between the
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm and the
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm of the function
f
in (
4.7
), in particular for

.

4.2.
Sharpness for hypersurfaces

In this subsection we prove that the maximal function bounds in Sections
3.2
and
3.3
are essentially sharp for suitable
p
.

We first prove sharpness in the case of Theorem
3.6
. Recall the definitions of
\mathcal{A}_{t}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma,\psi}
from (
3.11
) and (
3.12
), for
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
a hypersurface,
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Sigma)
and
t>0
. The following result shows that the maximal function estimates in Theorem
3.6
are essentially sharp for
p\in[1,2]\cup[\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1},\infty]
in all nontrivial cases, and in particular in the case of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a hypersurface with non-vanishing Gaussian curvature, and let
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Sigma)
not be identically zero. If there exist
t_{0},C_{t_{0}}>0
such that

(4.11)

\|\mathcal{A}_{t_{0}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{t_{0}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
s\geq\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{n-1}{2}
. Moreover, if there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

(4.12)

\|\mathcal{M}_{\Sigma,\psi}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
s\geq\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{n-1}{2}
.

Proof.

We would like to apply Theorems
4.2
and
4.5
. This is complicated by the fact that, although one may express
\mathcal{A}_{t}
in terms of a sum of operators as in (
4.1
), the contributions from the summands may, a priori, cancel each other out. To deal with this issue we will microlocalize in regions of phase space where there is only a single summand. To this end we will reason in a manner which can also be used for other results in this section. Throughout, we freely make use of the theory of Fourier integral operators as can be found in
[
17
, Chapter XXV]
(see also
[
33
, Chapter 6]
).

We first do some preliminary work. Note that we can represent
\mathcal{A}_{1}
as in (
3.19
):

\mathcal{A}_{1}f(x)=\int_{x-\Sigma}f(y)\psi(x-y)\mathrm{d}\sigma(x-y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\theta\Psi_{1}(x,y)}\rho_{0}(x,1,y)f(y)\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}y

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Here
\Psi_{1}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
is such that
x-\Sigma=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\Psi_{1}(x,y)=0\}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\rho_{0}(x,1,y)=(2\pi)^{-1}\psi(x-y)|\partial_{y}\Psi_{1}(x,y)|
for
y\in x-\Sigma
. By dilation, one then has

(4.13)

\mathcal{A}_{t}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\theta\Psi_{t}(x,y)}\rho_{0}(x,t,y)f(y)\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}y

for all
t>0
,
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\Psi_{t}(x,y)=\Psi_{1}(x/t,y/t)
and
\rho_{0}(x,t,y)=t^{-n}\rho_{0}(x/t,1,y/t)
. Moreover, the assumption that
\Sigma
has non-vanishing Gaussian curvature is equivalent to (
3.15
) (see e.g.
[
36
, Section XI.3.1]
), and
\rho_{0}(\cdot,t,\cdot)
is not identically zero. It follows that each
\mathcal{A}_{t}
is a Fourier integral operator of order
-(n-1)/2
with canonical relation

\mathcal{C}_{t}=\{(x,\theta\partial_{x}\Psi_{t}(x,y),y,-\theta\partial_{y}\Psi_{t}(x,y))\mid\Psi_{t}(x,y)=0,\theta\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\}.

Also, (
3.15
) just says that the Jacobian of

(y,\theta)\mapsto\big(\partial_{x}(\theta\Psi_{t}(x,y)),\partial_{\theta}(\theta\Psi_{t}(x,y))\big)

does not vanish where
\Psi_{t}(x,y)=0
and
\theta=1
. Hence one can solve
\Psi_{t}(x,y)=0
and
\theta\partial_{x}\Psi_{t}(x,y)=\xi
with respect to
(y,\theta)
and use
(x,\xi)
as local coordinates on
\mathcal{C}_{t}
. This in turn implies that the projections from
\mathcal{C}_{t}
onto the
x
coordinate and onto the last two coordinates are submersions.
Finally, since
\rho_{0}(\cdot,t,\cdot)
is not identically zero, the principal symbol of
\mathcal{A}_{t}
does not vanish at some point
c_{t}=(x_{t},\xi_{t},y_{t},\eta_{t})\in\mathcal{C}_{t}
.

Now suppose that (
4.11
) holds. Let
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
be pseudodifferential operators of order zero, with principal symbols that are nonzero at
(x_{t_{0}},\xi_{t_{0}})
and
(y_{t_{0}},\eta_{t_{0}})
, respectively, and with microsupport contained in small conic neighborhoods of these points. Since both
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are invariant under pseudodifferential operators of order zero, one has

\|S_{1}\mathcal{A}_{t_{0}}S_{2}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, the canonical relation of
S_{1}\mathcal{A}_{t_{0}}S_{2}
is contained in a small conic neighborhood of
c_{t_{0}}
, and its principal symbol does not vanish at
c_{t_{0}}
. In fact, after multiplying by a smooth cutoff, we may additionally suppose that
S_{1}\mathcal{A}_{t_{0}}S_{2}f=0
if
f
is supported outside a small neighborhood of
y_{t_{0}}
.

It then follows (see
[
20
, Lemma A.2 and Remark A.4]
) that one can write
S_{1}\mathcal{A}_{t_{0}}S_{2}=T_{t_{0}}S_{t_{0}}+R_{t_{0}}
, where
R_{t_{0}}
is a smoothing operator,
S_{t_{0}}
is a change of coordinates and
T_{t_{0}}
is an operator as in (
4.1
), with
m=-(n-1)/2
, albeit with the
(n+1)
-dimensional
z
variable replaced by the
n
-dimensional
x
variable. Moreover,
T_{t_{0}}
satisfies the
n
-dimensional version of the conditions in Theorem
4.2
. Here we have used the projection conditions on
\mathcal{C}_{t_{0}}
.

Since
\|S_{t_{0}}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
supported in a suitably small neighborhood of
y_{t_{0}}
, and because
R_{t_{0}}:\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\|T_{t_{0}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Now Theorem
4.2
and Remark
4.3
imply that
s\geq\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{n-1}{2}
.

Next, suppose that (
4.12
) holds. We will reduce to Theorem
4.5
in a similar manner as above, with the minor caveat that the mixed norm implicit on the left-hand side of (
4.12
) is not invariant under applications of general pseudodifferential operators, and we need to be more careful when microlocalizing. More importantly, we have to work with a phase function satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
4.5
.

Set
\mathcal{A}f(x,t):=\chi(t)\mathcal{A}_{t}f(x)
for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies
\mathrm{supp}(\chi)\subseteq[1,2]
and does not vanish at some point
t_{0}\in(1,2)
. Then
\mathcal{A}
is a Fourier integral operator of order
-\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{1}{4}
with canonical relation

(4.14)

\mathcal{C}:=\{(x,t,\theta\partial_{x}\Psi_{t}(x,y),\theta\partial_{t}\Psi_{t}(x,y),y,\theta\partial_{x}\Psi_{t}(x,y))\mid y\in x-\Sigma,\theta\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\}.

As before, the projections onto the
(x,t)
coordinate and onto the last two coordinates are submersions. This in turn implies that
\mathcal{C}
can be locally parametrized using the
(x,t)
coordinate and the last coordinate. Moreover, the function
\chi(t_{0})\rho_{0}(\cdot,t_{0},\cdot)
is not identically zero. Hence the principal symbol of
\mathcal{A}
does not vanish at some point
c_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0},\xi_{0},\tau_{0},y_{0},\eta_{0})\in\mathcal{C}
, and we may suppose that
x_{0}\neq y_{0}
.

Let
S_{2}
be a pseudodifferential operator of order zero with a principal symbol which is nonzero at
(y_{0},\eta_{0})
, and with microsupport contained in a small conic neighborhood of this point. Let
\chi_{1}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be nonzero at
(x_{0},t_{0})
and supported in a small neighborhood of this point. Then

\big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|\chi_{1}(\cdot,t)\mathcal{A}S_{2}f(\cdot,t)|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\big\|\sup_{1\leq t\leq 2}|\chi(t)\mathcal{A}_{t}S_{2}f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Here we used (
4.12
) and that
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is invariant under
S_{2}
. Moreover, since
\mathcal{C}
can be parametrized using the
(x,t)
coordinate and its last coordinate, it suffices to localize near
(x_{0},t_{0})
and microlocalize near
(y_{0},\eta_{0})
, as we have done, to guarantee that the canonical relation of
\chi_{1}\mathcal{A}S_{2}
is contained in a small conical neighborhood of
c_{0}
. Finally, after multiplying by a smooth cutoff, we may suppose that
\chi_{1}\mathcal{A}S_{2}f=0
if
f
is supported outside a small neighborhood of
y_{0}
.

It then follows (see
[
20
, Lemma A.2 and Remark A.4]
) that
\chi_{1}\mathcal{A}S_{2}=TS+R
, where
R
is a smoothing operator,
S
is a change of coordinates and
T
is an operator as in (
4.1
), with
m=-(n-1)/2
, satisfying the conditions of Theorem
4.2
. One again has
\|Sf\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\eqsim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
for
f
supported in a suitably small neighborhood of
y_{0}
. Moreover, as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, one can show that
\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|Rf(\cdot,t)|\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Hence

(4.15)

\big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|T_{t}f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. It thus remains to show that the phase
\Phi
of satisfies the nontriviality condition in Theorem
3.4
.

To this end first note that, by the expression for
T
in (
4.1
), the canonical relation of
T
is contained in

(4.16)

\mathcal{C}_{1}:=\{(x,t,\partial_{x}\Phi(z,\eta),\partial_{t}\Phi(z,\eta),\partial_{\eta}\Phi(x,t,\eta),\eta)\mid(x,t,\eta)\in V\}.

On the other hand, since
R
is smoothing, the canonical relation of
TS
coincides with that of
\chi_{1}\mathcal{A}S_{2}
, which in turn is contained in a small neighborhood of
c_{0}
in
\mathcal{C}
. Moreover, since
S
is a change of coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, this means that the canonical relation of
T
is contained in

(4.17)

\mathcal{C}_{2}:=\{(x,t,\xi,\tau,\kappa(y),(\partial_{\kappa}(y))_{*}\eta)\mid(x,t,\xi,\tau,y,\eta)\in\mathcal{C}\}.

Here
\kappa
is a diffeomorphism on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
(\partial_{\kappa}(y))_{*}
is the induced map on the cotangent space
T^{*}_{y}\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
V^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
be as in Theorem
4.5
, and let
\eta^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
. Then, by comparing (
4.14
), (
4.16
) and (
4.17
), we see that there exist
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
y\in x-\Sigma
and
\theta\neq 0
such that
\partial_{t}\Phi(z_{0},\eta^{\prime})=\theta\partial_{t}\Psi_{t}(x,y)
. Since
x_{0}\neq y_{0}
, we may also assume that
x\neq y
. On the other hand, since
\mathcal{A}_{1}
is translation invariant, one has
\Psi_{t}(x,y)=\Psi(\frac{x-y}{t})
for some
\Psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. So

\partial_{t}\Phi(z_{0},\eta^{\prime})=-\tfrac{1}{t^{2}}\theta(\nabla\Psi)(\tfrac{x-y}{t})\cdot(x-y),

and the latter is nonzero by the assumption that
\Sigma
has non-vanishing Gaussian curvature. Now Theorem
4.5
and (
4.15
) conclude the proof.
∎

Next, we consider Theorem
3.9
, which we show to be essentially sharp for all
p\in[1,2]\cup[\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1},\infty]
whenever the function
\rho
is not identically under natural nontriviality assumptions. We use notation as in Theorem
3.9
.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\Psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that (
1
) holds, and let
\rho\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
y_{0}\in\Sigma_{x_{0},t_{0}}
be such that
\rho(x_{0},t_{0},y_{0})\neq 0
. If there exists a
C_{t_{0}}\geq 0
such that

\|T_{t_{0}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{t_{0}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
s\geq\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{n-1}{2}
. Moreover, if
\partial_{t}\Psi_{t}(x_{0},y_{0})|_{t=t_{0}}\neq 0
and if there exists a
C\geq 0
such that

\big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|T_{t}f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
s\geq\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{n-1}{2}
.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to that of Theorem
4.8
, but it requires less work. Indeed, each
T_{t}
can be expressed as in (
4.13
), and (
1
) is satisfied by assumption. Then the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem
4.8
yield the first statement.

The proof of the second statement is also analogous to that of Theorem
4.8
, but it requires slightly less work because
\partial_{t}\Psi_{t}(x_{0},y_{0})|_{t=t_{0}}\neq 0
holds by assumption.
∎

Remark 4.10
.

As in Theorem
3.9
(see Remark
3.10
), one does not need
\Psi
to satisfy (
1
) on all of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, only in a neighborhood of the support of
\rho
.

Also, one may suppose that
\partial_{t}\Psi_{t}(x_{0},y_{0})|_{t=t_{0}}\neq 0
in Theorem
4.9
whenever condition (
2
) in Theorem
3.9
is satisfied, by the same argument as in Remark
2.4
.

We also obtain sharpness for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
and
p\in[1,2]\cup[\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1},\infty]
in Proposition
3.11
, as a consequence of the following result. Recall the definition of
\mathcal{M}_{t}^{\alpha}
from (
3.22
), for
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
and
t>0
.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
. If there exist
t_{0},C_{t_{0}}>0
such that

(4.18)

\|\mathcal{M}_{t_{0}}^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{t_{0}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
s\geq\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{n-1}{2}-\Real(\alpha)
. Moreover, if there exist a non-empty open interval
I\subseteq(0,\infty)
and a
C\geq 0
such that

(4.19)

\big\|\sup_{t\in I}|\mathcal{M}_{t}^{\alpha}f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
s\geq\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{n-1}{2}-\Real(\alpha)
.

Proof.

The scheme of the proof is similar to that of Theorem
4.8
, although some care is required to show that the principal symbol of each
\mathcal{M}_{t}^{\alpha}
is nonzero somewhere.

Recall from (
3.23
) that

m_{\alpha}(\xi)=a_{\alpha}^{0}(\xi)+e^{i|\xi|}a_{\alpha}^{+}(\xi)+e^{-i|\xi|}a^{-}_{\alpha}(\xi)

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
a_{\alpha}^{0}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
a_{\alpha}^{+},a_{\alpha}^{-}\in S^{-\frac{n-1}{2}-\Real(\alpha)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In fact, it follows from asymptotics of Bessel functions (see
[
38
, Section 7.21]
) that
a^{+}_{\alpha}
and
a^{-}_{\alpha}
are elliptic. For
t>0
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, set

T_{t}^{+}(\xi):=e^{it|\xi|}a_{\alpha}^{+}(t\xi)\ \text{ and }\ T_{t}^{-}(\xi):=e^{-it|\xi|}a_{\alpha}^{-}(t\xi)

Note that

\mathcal{C}^{+}_{t}:=\{(x,\eta,x+t\hat{\eta},\eta)\mid x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\}

is the canonical relation of
T_{t}^{+}(D)
, and

\mathcal{C}^{-}_{t}:=\{(x,\eta,x-t\hat{\eta},\eta)\mid x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\}

is the canonical relation of
T^{-}_{t}(D)
.

Now, let
t_{0}>0
be as in (
4.18
). Fix
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
be pseudodifferential operators of order zero, with principal symbols that are nonzero at
(x_{0},e_{1})
and
(x_{0}+t_{0}e_{1},e_{1})
, respectively, and with microsupport contained in small neighborhoods of these points. Here
e_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the first basis vector. Then, by construction, the principal symbol of
S_{1}T_{t_{0}}^{+}(D)S_{2}
is nonzero at
(x_{0},e_{1},x_{0}+t_{0}e_{1},e_{1})
, and the canonical relation of
S_{1}T_{t_{0}}^{+}(D)S_{2}
is contained in a small conic neighborhood in
\mathcal{C}^{+}_{t}
of this point. Moreover, the canonical relation of
S_{1}T_{t_{0}}^{-}(D)S_{2}
is empty, so
S_{1}T_{t_{0}}^{-}(D)S_{2}
is a smoothing operator.

By (
4.18
) and because
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are invariant under pseudodifferential operators of order zero, one has
\|S_{1}\mathcal{M}_{t_{0}}^{\alpha}S_{2}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In turn, since
a_{\alpha}^{0}(t_{0}D)
and
S_{1}T_{t_{0}}^{-}(D)S_{2}
are smoothing operators, this implies that

\|S_{1}T_{t_{0}}^{+}(D)S_{2}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, one can again write
S_{1}T_{t_{0}}^{+}(D)S_{2}
as the sum of an operator as in Theorem
4.2
precomposed with a change of coordinates, and a harmless smoothing operator. So Theorem
4.2
shows that
s\geq s(p)-\frac{n-1}{2}-\Real(\alpha)
if
p\geq 2
.

Next, let
I\subseteq(0,\infty)
be a non-empty open interval such that (
4.19
) holds, and fix
t_{0}\in I
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
S_{2}
be as before, and let
\chi_{1}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
be a smooth cutoff which localizes to a small neighborhood of
(x_{0},t_{0})
. Then (
4.19
) implies that

\big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|\chi_{1}(\cdot,t)\mathcal{M}_{t}^{\alpha}S_{2}f(\cdot)|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Set
T^{+}f(x,t):=T_{t}^{+}(D)f(x)
and
T^{-}f(x,t):=T^{-}_{t}(D)f(x)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t>0
. Then
S_{1}T^{-}S_{2}
is a smoothing operator, as is the term corresponding to
a_{\alpha}^{0}
. It then follows that

\big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|\chi_{1}(\cdot,t)T^{+}S_{2}f(\cdot,t)|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since the the canonical relation of
\chi_{1}T^{+}S_{2}
is contained in

\{(x,t,\eta,-|\eta|,x-t\hat{\eta},\eta)\mid(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\}

and its principal symbol does not vanish at
(x_{0},t_{0},\eta,-|\eta|,x_{0}-t_{0}e_{1},\eta)
, a similar argument as in the second half of the proof of Theorem
4.8
, relying on Theorem
4.5
, shows that
s\geq s(p)+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{n-1}{2}-\Real(\alpha)
if
p\leq 2
, as required.
∎

4.3.
Sharpness on manifolds

Finally, we show that the results in Section
3.4
are essentially sharp for suitable values of
p\in[1,\infty]
. Fix an
n
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
without boundary, and let
\Delta_{g}
be the associated Laplace–Beltrami operator.

We first observe that the maximal function estimate in Proposition
3.14
(see also Remark
3.3
) is essentially sharp for all
p\in[1,2]\cup[\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1},\infty]
.

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. If there exist
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
C_{t_{0}}\geq 0
such that

\|e^{it_{0}\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}}f\|_{L^{p}(M)}\leq C_{t_{0}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)}

for all
f\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
, then
s\geq\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})
. Moreover, if there exists a non-empty open interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and a
C\geq 0
such that

(4.20)

\big\|\sup_{t\in I}|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}}f|\big\|_{L^{p}(M)}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)}

for all
f\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
, then
s\geq\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})+\frac{1}{p}
.

Proof.

For the first statement one can reduce to Theorem
4.2
by working in local coordinates. Alternatively, one can reason as follows. By replacing
f
by
e^{-it_{0}\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}}f
and using the invariance of
\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)
under
e^{-it_{0}\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}}
, we may suppose that
t_{0}=0
. But then the first statement follows from the fact that the Sobolev embeddings in (
2.11
) are sharp (which in turn is a consequence of, e.g., Theorem
4.2
).

For the second statement we do work explicitly in local coordinates. More precisely, let
K
be a collection of coordinate charts as in Definition
2.5
. Fix a
\kappa\in K
,
\kappa:U_{\kappa}\to\kappa(U_{\kappa})\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and the associated
\psi_{\kappa}
from (
2.8
). We may suppose that
\psi_{\kappa}\equiv 1
on an open subset
U\subseteq U_{\kappa}
. Now let
0\neq\psi\in C^{\infty}(M)
be such that
\mathrm{supp}(\psi)\subseteq U
. Set
\tilde{\psi}:=\psi\circ\kappa^{-1}
and

T_{t}f(x):=\kappa_{*}\big(\psi_{\kappa}e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}}\kappa^{*}(\tilde{\psi}f)\big)(x),

for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy
\mathrm{supp}(\chi)\subseteq I
while not being identically zero, and set
Tf(x,t):=\chi(t)T_{t}f(x)
. Then, by (
4.20
) and Definition
2.5
, we have

(4.21)

\big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|Tf(\cdot,t)|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|\tilde{\psi}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Next, one can argue as in the proof of Theorem
3.6
to express
T
microlocally in terms of an operator as in (
4.1
), with
m=0
, after which one can combine (
4.21
) with Theorems
4.2
and
4.5
. This is possible because
T
is a Fourier integral operator of order
-1/4
, with a principal symbol which does not vanish identically, and with a canonical relation of the form

(4.22)

\mathcal{C}:=\{(x,t,\xi,-p(x,\xi),y,\eta)\mid(x,\xi)=\chi_{t}(y,\eta)\}.

Here
\chi_{t}
is the bicharacteristic flow at time
t
associated with
\sqrt{g}
in local coordinates, and
p(x,\xi)>0
. These statements about
T
in turn follow by expressing the canonical relation of
(e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
(see e.g.
[
17
, Theorem 29.11]
) in local coordinates. In particular, as in the proof of Theorem
4.8
, the fact that
p
is nonzero implies that the resulting operator satisfies the conditions of Theorem
4.5
.
∎

Finally, the maximal function estimate in Proposition
3.15
is also essentially sharp for all
p\in[1,2]\cup[\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1},\infty]
. We use notation as in (
3.28
).

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. If if there exist
t_{0}\in(0,\mathrm{inj}(M))
and
C_{t_{0}}\geq 0
such that

\|\mathcal{A}_{t_{0},g}f\|_{L^{p}(M)}\leq C_{t_{0}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)}

for all
f\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
, then
s\geq\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{n-1}{2}
. Moreover, if if there exists a non-empty open interval
I\subseteq(0,\mathrm{inj}(M))
and a
C\geq 0
such that

\big\|\sup_{t\in I}|\mathcal{A}_{t,g}f|\big\|_{L^{p}(M)}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,p}_{FIO}(M)}

for all
f\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
, then
s\geq\frac{n-1}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{n-1}{2}
.

Proof.

Here one can argue in two ways.

Firstly, by working in local coordinates as in the proof of Proposition
4.12
, one can reduce to an application of Theorems
4.2
and
4.5
. This is possible for the first statement because
\mathcal{A}_{t_{0},g}
is a Fourier integral operator of order
-(n-1)/2
, associated with a local canonical graph and with a non-vanishing principal symbol. For the second statement one can also use that the canonical relation of the operator
T
, given by
Tf(x,t):=\chi(t)\mathcal{A}_{t,g}f(x)
for
f\in\mathcal{D}(M,\Omega_{1/2})
,
x\in M
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
0\neq\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\mathrm{supp}(\chi)\subseteq I
, is contained in the union of the canonical relation in (
4.22
) and the corresponding one with
-p(x,\xi)
replaced by
p(x,\xi)
.

Alternatively, as in the proof of Proposition
3.15
, one can observe that, in geodesic normal coordinate charts, each
\mathcal{A}_{t,g}
can be expressed as in (
3.17
) for some
\rho\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
which is not identically zero, and a defining function
\Psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying the conditions (
1
) and (
2
) introduced there. Then Theorem
4.9
(see also Remark
4.10
) concludes the proof.
∎

Curvature functionals on convex bodies Keywords: Steiner formula, curvature measures, L p L_{p} Brunn Minkowski theory; 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 52A39 (Primary), 28A75, 52A20, 53A07 (Secondary)
Kateryna Tatarko, Elisabeth M. Werner

Abstract
We investigate the weighted L p L_{p} affine surface areas which appear in the recently established L p L_{p} Steiner formula of the L p L_{p} Brunn Minkowski theory. We show that they are valuations on the set of convex bodies and prove isoperimetric inequalities for them. We show that they are related to f f divergences of the cone measures of the convex body and its polar, namely the Kullback-Leibler divergence and the Rényi-divergence.

1.1
Main Results

In this paper, we look at the

We investigate in detail those weighted

We show that they are homogeneous of a certain degree and are invariant under rotations and reflections.
We show that they are valuations on the set of convex bodies. Valuations have become a vitally import subject of study in convexity and affine and
differential geometry, e.g.,
[

,
8
,
15
,
16
,
22
,
33
]
.
The weighted

affine surface areas satisfy isoperimetric inequalities which generalize the
L_{p}
affine isoperimetric inequalities
of
[

,
39
]
. This is shown in Theorem
3.2
.

Theorem 3.2

Let
s\neq-n,r\neq-n,t\neq-n
be
real numbers. Let
K
be a
C^{2}_{+}
convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
centroid at the origin.

(i) If
\frac{(n+r)(t-s)}{(n+t)(r-s)}>1
, then

\mu_{\vec{i}}-as_{r}(K)\leq\big(\mu_{\vec{i}}-as_{t}(K)\big)^{\frac{(r-s)(n+t)}{(t-s)(n+r)}}\big(\mu_{\vec{i}}-as_{s}(K)\big)^{\frac{(t-r)(n+s)}{(t-s)(n+r)}}.

(ii) If
\frac{(n+r)t}{(n+t)r}>1
, then

\frac{\mu_{\vec{i}}-as_{r}(K)}{\mu_{\vec{i}}-{\rm vol}_{n}(K)}\leq n^{\frac{n(t-r)}{t(n+r)}}\bigg(\frac{\mu_{\vec{i}}-as_{t}(K)}{\mu_{\vec{i}}-{\rm vol}_{n}(K)}\bigg)^{\frac{r(n+t)}{{t(n+r)}}}.

Equality holds in the above inequalities, if and only if
K
is an ellipsoid.

We show in Section
3.3
that the weighted
L_{p}
affine surface areas have natural geometric interpretations in terms of certain convex bodies
associated with the given convex body
K
.

We prove a monotonicity behavior in the parameter
p
for the weighted
L_{p}
affine surface areas which allows to establish
asymptotics for the weighted
L_{p}
affine surface areas. These asymptotics connects them to entropy powers, namely to the Kullbak-Leibler divergence
D_{KL}
of the cone measures
of
K
and its polar
K^{\circ}
,
Q_{K}
and
P_{K}
. We quote the relevant Theorem
3.8
and refer to Section
3.4
for the details.
We put

\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K)=\int\limits_{\partial K}\frac{H_{n-1}(x)^{\frac{p}{n+p}}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{\frac{n(p-1)}{n+p}}}\,\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{m-k}\,\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\left\{{n-1\choose j}^{i_{j}}H_{j}^{i_{j}}(x)\right\}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x),

and then the following theorem holds.

Theorem 3.8

Let
K
be a
C^{2}_{+}
convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
centroid at the origin. Then

(i)

\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K)}{\omega^{\infty}_{m,k,\vec{i}}(K)}\right)^{n+p}=\textup{exp}\left(-\frac{n\,D_{KL}(P_{K}||Q_{K})}{\mu_{\vec{i}}-{\rm vol}_{n}(K^{\circ})}\right).

(ii)

\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow 0}\left(\frac{\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K^{\circ})}{\omega^{0}_{m,k,\vec{i}}(K^{\circ})}\right)^{\frac{n(n+p)}{p}}=\textup{exp}\left(-\frac{n\,D_{KL}(P_{K^{\circ}}||Q_{K^{\circ}})}{\mu_{\vec{i}}-{\rm vol}_{n}(K^{\circ})}\right).

This leads naturally to consider more general
f
-divergences than just the Kullbak-Leibler divergence. We treat that in Section
4

L_{p}
affine surface areas themselves are special
f
-divergences.

Throughout the paper we assume that the convex bodies
K
are
C^{2}_{+}
, i.e.,
K
has twice continuously differentiable boundary with strictly positive Gauss curvature everywhere and such that

K

.

2
Weighted
L_{p}
-affine surface areas

2.1
Background from differential geometry

For more information and the details in this section we refer to e.g.,
[
13
,
30
]
.

Let
K
be a convex body of class
C^{2}.
For a point
x
on the boundary
\partial K
of
K
we denote by
N(x)
the unique outward unit normal vector of
K
at
x.
The map
N_{K}:\partial K\to S^{n-1}
is called the spherical image map or Gauss map of
K
and is of class
C^{1}.
Its differential is called the Weingarten map. The eigenvalues of the Weingarten map are the principal curvatures
k_{i}(x)
of
K
at
x.

The
j
-th normalized elementary symmetric functions of the principal curvatures are denoted by
H_{j}
. They are defined as follows

H_{j}={n-1\choose j}^{-1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{j}\leq n-1}k_{i_{1}}\cdots k_{i_{j}}

(3)

for
j=1,\dots,n-1
and
H_{0}=1.
Note that

H_{1}=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{1\leq i\leq n-1}k_{i}

is the mean curvature, that is the average of principal curvatures, and

H_{n-1}=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}k_{i}

is the Gauss curvature.

We say that
K
is of class
C^{2}_{+}
if
K
is of class
C^{2}
and the Gauss map
\nu
is a diffeomorphism. This means in particular that
N_{K}
has a smooth inverse. This assumption is stronger than just
C^{2},
and is equivalent to the assumption that all principal curvatures are strictly positive, or that the Gauss curvature
H_{n-1}\neq 0.

It also means that the differential of
N_{K}
, i.e., the Weingarten map, is of maximal rank everywhere.

Let
K
be of class
C^{2}_{+}
. For
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, let
\xi_{K}(u)
be the unique point on the boundary of
K
at which
u
is an outward normal vector.
The map
\xi_{K}
is defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. Its restriction to the sphere
S^{n-1},
the map
\bar{\xi}_{K}:S^{n-1}\to\partial K
, is called the reverse spherical image map, or reverse Gauss map. The differential of
\bar{\xi}_{K}
is called the reverse Weingarten map. The eigenvalues of the reverse Weingarten map are called the principal radii of curvature
r_{1},\dots,r_{n-1}
of
K
at
u\in S^{n-1}.

The
j
-th normalized elementary symmetric functions of the principal radii of curvature are denoted by
s_{j}
. In particular,
s_{0}=1
, and for
1\leq j\leq n-1
they are defined by

s_{j}={n-1\choose j}^{-1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{j}\leq n-1}r_{i_{1}}\cdots r_{i_{j}}.

(4)

Note that the principal curvatures are functions on the boundary of
K
and the principal radii of curvature are functions on the sphere.

Now we describe the connection between
H_{j}
and
s_{j}.
For a body
K
of class
C^{2}_{+}
, we have for
u\in S^{n-1}
that
\bar{\xi}_{K}(u)=N_{K}^{-1}(u)
.
In particular, the principal radii of curvature are reciprocals of the principal curvatures, that is

r_{i}(u)=\frac{1}{k_{i}(\bar{\xi}_{K}(u))}.

This implies that for
x\in\partial K
with
\nu(x)=u
,

s_{j}={n-1\choose j}^{-1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{j}\leq n-1}\frac{1}{k_{i_{1}}(\bar{\xi}_{K}(u))\cdots k_{i_{j}}(\bar{\xi}_{K}(u))}=\frac{H_{n-1-j}}{H_{n-1}}\Big(\bar{\xi}_{K}(u)\Big)

and

H_{j}=\frac{s_{n-1-j}}{s_{n-1}}\Big(\nu(x)\Big),

for
j=1,\dots,n-1.

2.2
Definitions

For fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and fixed sequence
\vec{i}=\{i_{j}\}_{j=0}^{n-1}
such that
i_{1}+2i_{2}+\cdots(n-1)i_{n-1}=m
and all
p\in\mathbb{R}
,
p\neq-n
,
we define

\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K)=\int\limits_{\partial K}\frac{H_{n-1}(x)^{\frac{p}{n+p}}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{\frac{n(p-1)}{n+p}}}\,\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{m-k}\,\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\left\{{n-1\choose j}^{i_{j}}H_{j}^{i_{j}}(x)\right\}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)

(5)

and

u_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K)=\int\limits_{S^{n-1}}\frac{f_{K}(u)^{\frac{n}{n+p}}}{h_{K}(u)^{\frac{n(p-1)}{n+p}}}\,h_{K}(u)^{m-k}\,\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}{n-1\choose j}^{i_{j}}s_{n-1-j}^{i_{j}}(u)}{s_{n-1}^{\sum\limits_{j}i_{j}+\frac{p}{n+p}}(u)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(u).

(6)

Remark 2.1

Note that the above quantities are vanishing for polytopes. Therefore, we will treat only
C^{2}_{+}
convex bodies throughout the text and then

\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K)=u_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K).

(7)

Denote

c(n)=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}{n-1\choose j}^{i_{j}}.

Let
\mu_{m,k,\vec{i}}
be the measure on
\partial K
with density

d\mu_{m,k,\vec{i}}(x)=c(n)\,\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{m-k}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}H_{j}^{i_{j}}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)

with respect to the surface measure
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
on
\partial K
and let
\nu_{m,k,\vec{i}}
be the measure on
S^{n-1}
with density

d\nu_{m,k,\vec{i}}(u)=c(n)\,h^{m-k}_{K}(u)\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}s_{n-1-j}^{i_{j}}(u)}{s_{n-1}^{\sum\limits_{j}i_{j}+\frac{p}{n+p}}(u)}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(u).

with respect to the surface measure
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
on
S^{n-1}
.
To keep notations simple we mostly write
\mu_{\vec{i}}
and
\nu_{\vec{i}}
instead of
\mu_{m,k,\vec{i}}
and
\nu_{m,k,\vec{i}}
.

We then define the
weighted
L_{p}
-affine surface areas
by

\mu_{\vec{i}}-as_{p}(K)=\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K)=\int\limits_{\partial K}\frac{H_{n-1}(x)^{\frac{p}{n+p}}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{\frac{n(p-1)}{n+p}}}d\mu_{\vec{i}}(x).

(8)

Definition (
2
) explains that those can be considered as
L_{p}
-affine surface area weighted by the measure
\mu_{\vec{i}}
.
In particular,

\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{0}(K)=\int\limits_{\partial K}\langle x,N(x)\rangle d\mu_{\vec{i}}(x)=n(\mu_{\vec{i}}-{\rm vol}_{n}(K))

is a weighted volume of
K
, weighted by the measure
\mu_{\vec{i}}

and

\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{\infty}(K)=\int\limits_{\partial K}\frac{H_{n-1}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{n}}d\mu_{\vec{i}}(x)=n(\mu_{\vec{i}}-{\rm vol}_{n}(K^{\circ}))

is a weighted volume of
K^{\circ}
, weighted by the measure
\mu_{\vec{i}}
, where

K^{\circ}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle y,x\rangle\leq 1,\text{ for all }x\in K\}

is the polar body of
K
.

We can take another point of view for the measure
d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
on
S^{n-1}
via the density
f_{p}(K,u)=\left(\frac{f_{K}(u)}{h_{K}^{p-1}(u)}\right)^{\frac{n}{n+p}}
. This density was introduced by Lutwak
[
23
]
. Then

u_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K)=c(n)\int\limits_{S^{n-1}}f_{p}(K,u)h_{K}(u)^{m-k}\,\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}s_{n-1-j}^{i_{j}}(u)}{s_{n-1}^{\sum\limits_{j}i_{j}+\frac{p}{n+p}}(u)}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(u).

2.3
Special cases

Note that

\omega_{0,0,0}^{p}(K)=u_{0,0,0}^{p}(K)=as_{p}(K),

where we denote the sequence
\vec{i}=\{0,\dots,0\}
as
0
.

1. When
k=m
, we get

\omega_{m,m,\vec{i}}^{p}(K)=\int\limits_{\partial K}\frac{H_{n-1}(x)^{\frac{p}{n+p}}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{\frac{n(p-1)}{n+p}}}\,\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\left\{{n-1\choose j}^{i_{j}}H_{j}^{i_{j}}(x)\right\}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).

2.
m=0
implies that
\vec{i}=0
and (
5
) simplifies to

\omega_{0,k,0}^{p}(K)=\int\limits_{\partial K}\frac{H_{n-1}(x)^{\frac{p}{n+p}}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{\frac{n(p-1)}{n+p}+k}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)=as_{p+\frac{k}{n}(n+p),\,-k}(K)

(see
[
34
, Eq. (29)]
).

3. For the Euclidean unit ball
B^{n}_{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(B^{n}_{2})={\rm vol}_{n-1}(B^{n}_{2})\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}{n-1\choose j}^{i_{j}}=c(n)\,{\rm vol}_{n-1}(\partial B^{n}_{2})

(9)

which does not depend on
k
. Note that if
i_{0}\neq 0
and
i_{1}=\dots=i_{n-1}=0
, that is
\vec{i}=\{i_{0},0,\dots,0\}
, then
\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(B^{n}_{2})={\rm vol}_{n-1}(\partial B^{n}_{2})
.

4. If
p=0
, we have

\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{0}(K)=c(n)\int\limits_{\partial K}\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{m-k+1}\,\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}H_{j}^{i_{j}}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).

When in addition
k=m
, we get

\omega_{m,m,\vec{i}}^{0}(K)=c(n)\int\limits_{\partial K}\langle x,N(x)\rangle\,\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}H_{j}^{i_{j}}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).

5. If
p=\infty
, we get that

\omega_{m,m,\vec{i}}^{\infty}(K)=c(n)\int\limits_{\partial K}\frac{H_{n-1}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{n}}\,\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}H_{j}^{i_{j}}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).

3
Properties of the weighted
L_{p}
-affine surface areas

3.1
Valuation, Invariance and Homogeneity

Proposition 3.1

Let
K
be a
C^{2}_{+}
convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
centroid at the origin. Let
p\in\mathbb{R}
,
p\neq-n
.
Then
\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K)
is a
n\frac{n-p}{n+p}-k
-homogeneous valuation that is invariant under rotations and reflections.

Proof

The proof follows immediately from results in
[
35
]
. We present an outline of the proof for completeness.

1.
Valuation.
As was shown in
[
35
, Theorem 5.9]
, for all
1\leq i_{1},\dots,i_{n-1}\leq n-1
,
1\leq j\leq n-1
, and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{j}\geq 0
,

\int\limits_{\partial K}\frac{H_{n-1}(x)^{\frac{p}{n+p}}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{\frac{n(p-1)}{n+p}}}\,\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{k-m}\,\prod\limits_{i=1}^{j}k_{i_{j}}^{\alpha_{j}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)

is a valuation. It immediately follows that
\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K)
are valuations as the linear combination of valuations is again a valuation.

2.
Homogeneity.
Similarly to
[
35
, Theorem 5.1]
we can show that
\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K)
are homogeneous of order
n\frac{n-p}{n+p}-k
. Applying
[
35
, Proposition 5.4]
with
T=a\,Id
, we get that
\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K)=a^{k-n\frac{n-p}{n+p}}\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(aK)
.

3.
Invariance.
If
T
is a rotation or a reflection, then
|\det T|=1
,
\|T^{-1t}(N_{K}(T^{-1}(y)))\|=\|N_{K}(T^{-1}(y))\|=1
and
for all
1\leq j\leq n-1
,

\{H_{j}(y):y\in\partial T(K)\}=\{H_{j}\left(x\right):\,x\in\partial K\}.

Thus, using these observations and
[
35
, Proposition 5.4]
, we get

\displaystyle\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K)=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\partial T(K)}\langle y,N_{T(K)}(y)\rangle^{m-k+\frac{n(1-p)}{n+p}}H_{n-1}^{\frac{p}{n+p}}(y)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}{n-1\choose j}^{i_{j}}H_{j}^{i_{j}}\left(y\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)=\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(T(K)).

3.2
Inequalities

Theorem 3.2

Let
s\neq-n,r\neq-n,t\neq-n
be
real numbers. Let
K
be a
C^{2}_{+}
convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
centroid at the origin.

(i) If
\frac{(n+r)(t-s)}{(n+t)(r-s)}>1
, then

\mu_{\vec{i}}-as_{r}(K)\leq\big(\mu_{\vec{i}}-as_{t}(K)\big)^{\frac{(r-s)(n+t)}{(t-s)(n+r)}}\big(\mu_{\vec{i}}-as_{s}(K)\big)^{\frac{(t-r)(n+s)}{(t-s)(n+r)}}.

(ii) If
\frac{(n+r)t}{(n+t)r}>1
, then

\frac{\mu_{\vec{i}}-as_{r}(K)}{\mu_{\vec{i}}-{\rm vol}_{n}(K)}\leq n^{\frac{n(t-r)}{t(n+r)}}\bigg(\frac{\mu_{\vec{i}}-as_{t}(K)}{\mu_{\vec{i}}-{\rm vol}_{n}(K)}\bigg)^{\frac{r(n+t)}{{t(n+r)}}}.

Equality holds in the above inequalities, if and only if
K
is an ellipsoid.

Proof

(i) By
Hölder’s inequality - which enforces the condition
\frac{(n+r)(s-t)}{(n+t)(s-r)}>1
, we then get

\displaystyle\mu_{\vec{i}}-as_{r}(K)=\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{r}(K)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\partial K}\frac{H_{n-1}(x)^{\frac{r}{n+r}}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{\frac{n(r-1)}{n+r}}}\,\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{m-k}\,\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\left\{{n-1\choose j}^{i_{j}}H_{j}^{i_{j}}(x)\right\}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K}\left(\frac{H_{n-1}(x)^{\frac{t}{n+t}}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{\frac{n(t-1)}{n+t}}}\right)^{\frac{(r-s)(n+t)}{(t-s)(n+r)}}\left(\frac{H_{n-1}(x)^{\frac{s}{n+s}}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{\frac{n(s-1)}{n+s}}}\right)^{\frac{(t-r)(n+s)}{(t-s)(n+r)}}\,d\mu_{\vec{i}}(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big(\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{t}(K)\big)^{\frac{(r-s)(n+t)}{(t-s)(n+r)}}\big(\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{s}(K)\big)^{\frac{(t-r)(n+s)}{(t-s)(n+r)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\mu_{\vec{i}}-as_{t}(K)\big)^{\frac{(r-s)(n+t)}{(t-s)(n+r)}}\big(\mu_{\vec{i}}-as_{s}(K)\big)^{\frac{(t-r)(n+s)}{(t-s)(n+r)}}.

(ii) Similarly, again using Hölder’s inequality -which now
enforces the condition
~\frac{(n+r)t}{(n+t)r}>1,~

\displaystyle\mu_{\vec{i}}-as_{r}(K)=\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{r}(K)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\partial K}\frac{H_{n-1}(x)^{\frac{r}{n+r}}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{\frac{n(r-1)}{n+r}}}\,d\mu_{\vec{i}}(x)=\int\limits_{\partial K}\left(\frac{H_{n-1}(x)^{\frac{t}{n+t}}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{\frac{n(t-1)}{n+t}}}\right)^{\frac{r(n+t)}{t(n+r)}}\frac{d\mu_{\vec{i}}(x)}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{\frac{(r-t)n}{(n+r)t}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big(\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{t}(K)\big)^{\frac{r(n+t)}{t(n+r)}}\big(\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{0}(K)\big)^{\frac{(t-r)n}{(n+r)t}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(\mu_{\vec{i}}-as_{t}(K)\bigg)^{\frac{r(n+t)}{{t(n+r)}}}\bigg(n(\mu_{\vec{i}}-{\rm vol}_{n}(K))\bigg)^{\frac{n(t-r)}{{t(n+r)}}}.

The equality characterizations follow from the equality characterization of Hölder’s inequality.

Equality holds in
(i)
and
(ii)
if and only if equality holds in Hölder’s inequality which happens if and only if

\frac{H_{n-1}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{n+1}}=\textup{constant}

\mu_{\vec{i}}
-almost everywhere on
\partial K
. As
\partial K
is
C^{2}_{+}
,
\{x\in\partial K:\mu_{\vec{i}}(x)=0\}=\emptyset

and therefore

\frac{H_{n-1}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{n+1}}=\textup{constant}

holds for all

.
Thus we can use the following theorem by Petty

]
, which then finishes the proof.

3.3
Geometric interpretations

We recall several constructions of convex bodies associated with a given convex body

1.
Weighted floating bodies

[

Let

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

\lambda

\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Let

The weighted floating body

[

[

4

5

H^{+}

It was shown in
[

2\bigg(\frac{\mbox{vol}_{n-1}(B^{n-1}_{2})}{n+1}\bigg)^{\frac{2}{n+1}}\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{\mbox{vol}_{n}(K)-\mbox{vol}_{n}(F(K,f,s))}{s^{\frac{2}{n+1}}}=\int_{\partial K}\frac{H_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}}{f^{\frac{2}{n+1}}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Surface bodies

[

Let

\int_{\partial K}f\,\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=1
. The probability

\mathbb{P}_{f}

\partial K
with density
f
.
Let
s\geq 0
. The surface body

[

as the intersection of all the closed half-spaces

H^{+}

whose defining hyperplanes

\mathbb{P}_{f}

It was shown in

2\bigg(\mbox{vol}_{n-1}(B^{n-1}_{2})\bigg)^{\frac{2}{n-1}}\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{\mbox{\rm vol}_{n}(K)-\mbox{\rm vol}_{n}(S(K,f,s))}{s^{\frac{2}{n-1}}}=\int_{\partial K}\frac{H_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}}{f^{\frac{2}{n-1}}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

3.
Random polytopes

[

A random polytope is the

In
[
31
]
, random polytopes are considered where the points are

\partial K

with respect to

f:\partial K\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

Then the expected volume of such a random polytope is

\mathbb{E}(f,N)=\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{P}_{f},N)=\int_{\partial K}\cdots\int_{\partial K}\mbox{vol}_{n}([x_{1},\dots,x_{N}])d\mathbb{P}_{f}(x_{1})\dots d\mathbb{P}_{f}(x_{N}),

where
[x_{1},\dots,x_{N}]
is the convex hull of the points
x_{1},\dots,x_{N}
.
It was shown in
[
31
]
that under mild smoothness assumptions on

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\mbox{\rm vol}_{n}(K)-\mathbb{E}(f,N)}{\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)^{\frac{2}{n-1}}}=c_{n}\int_{\partial K}\frac{H_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}}{f^{\frac{2}{n-1}}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

c_{n}=\frac{(n-1)^{\frac{n+1}{n-1}}\Gamma\left(n+1+\tfrac{2}{n-1}\right)}{2(n+1)!(\mbox{\rm vol}_{n-2}(\partial B_{2}^{n-1}))^{\frac{2}{n-1}}}

4.

[
19
]

Given a Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the

associated to

was introduced by Huang and Slomka
[
18

\mathcal{M}(\mu)=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq f\leq 1,\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}fd{\mu}=1\end{subarray}}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}yf\left(y\right)d{\mu}\left(y\right)\right\},

where the union is taken over all functions

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}fd{\mu=1}
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}yf\left(y\right)d{\mu}\left(y\right)

exists. The metronoid
\mathcal{M}_{\delta}(K)

the uniform measure on

.
Namely, let

In

\lim_{\delta\searrow 0}\frac{\mbox{vol}_{n}\left(K\right)-\mbox{vol}_{n}\left(\mathcal{M}_{\delta}(K,f)\right)}{\delta^{\frac{2}{n+1}}}=2\frac{n+1}{n+3}\left(\frac{{\rm vol}_{n-1}(B_{2}^{n-1})}{n+1}\right)^{\frac{2}{n+1}}\int_{\partial K}\frac{H_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}}{f^{\frac{2}{n+1}}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

This leads to geometric interpretations of the weighted

-affine surface areas in terms of these associated bodies.
That is, if we let

in the case of surface bodies and random polytopes, respectively, and denote by
K_{f,s}
the corresponding associated body, i.e.,

K_{f,s}=F(K,f,s),\hskip 5.69054pt\text{or}\hskip 5.69054ptK_{f,s}=S(K,f,s),\hskip 5.69054pt\text{or}\hskip 5.69054ptK_{f,s}=\mathcal{M}_{s}(K,f),\hskip 5.69054pt\text{or}\hskip 5.69054pt{\rm vol}_{n}(K_{f,s})=\mathbb{E}(f,N),

c_{n}\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{\mbox{\rm vol}_{n}(K)-\mbox{\rm vol}_{n}(K_{f,s})}{s^{\frac{2}{n-1}}}=\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K).

3.4
Asymptotics for the weighted
L_{p}
-affine surface areas

Let
(X,\mu)
be a measure space and
dP=pd\mu
and
Q=qd\mu
be measures in
X
that are absolutely continuous with respect to the measure
\mu
. The
Kullback-Leibler divergence
or
relative entropy
from
P
to
Q
is defined as in
[
11
]

D_{KL}(P||Q)=\int\limits_{X}p\log{\frac{p}{q}}d\mu.

(10)

The
information inequality
(also called
Gibb’s inequality
)
[
11
]
holds for the Kullback-Leibler divergence. Let
P
and
Q
be as above, then

D_{KL}(P||Q)\geq 0.

(11)

with equality if and only if
P=Q
.

We will apply this when
(X,\mu)=(\partial K,\mu_{\vec{i}})
, where
K
is
C^{2}_{+}
and densities
p
and
q
with respect to
\mu_{\vec{i}}
given by

p_{K}(x)=\frac{H_{n-1}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{n}},\qquad q_{K}(x)=\langle x,N(x)\rangle.

(12)

We let

P_{K}=\frac{H_{n-1}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{n}}\mu_{\vec{i}},\qquad Q_{K}=\langle x,N(x)\rangle\mu_{\vec{i}}.

(13)

Recall that classical cone measure
cm_{K}
on
\partial K
is defined as follows: for every measurable set
A\subseteq\partial K

cm_{K}(A)={\rm vol}_{n}(\{ta:\ a\in A,\,t\in[0,1]\}).

It is well-known (see, e.g,
[
26
]
for a proof) that the measures
\frac{H_{n-1}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{n}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
and
\langle x,N(x)\rangle\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
are the cone measures of
K
and
K^{\circ}
,

cm_{K}(A)=\frac{1}{n}\int\limits_{A}\langle x,N(x)\rangle\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x),\qquad cm_{K^{\circ}}(A)=\frac{1}{n}\int\limits_{A}\frac{H_{n-1}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{n}}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x).

The interpretation of
cm_{K^{\circ}}
as the “cone measure of
K^{\circ}
” is via the Gauss map on
K^{\circ}
,
N_{K^{\circ}}:\partial K^{\circ}\rightarrow S^{n-1},\ x\longmapsto N_{K^{\circ}}(x)
and the inverse of the Gauss map on
K

N_{K}:\partial K\rightarrow S^{n-1},\ x\longmapsto N_{K}(x)
,

N_{K}^{-1}N_{K^{\circ}}cm_{K^{\circ}}

where we use
N_{K}(x)
to emphasize that it is the normal vector of a body

K
at
x\in\partial K
.

We now define the
weighted cone measures

\mu_{\vec{i}}-cm_{K}
of
K
and
\mu_{\vec{i}}-cm_{K^{\circ}}
of
K^{\circ}
by

\mu_{\vec{i}}-cm_{K}=\frac{1}{n}\int\limits_{A}\langle x,N(x)\rangle\mu_{\vec{i}}(x),

(14)

\mu_{\vec{i}}-cm_{K^{\circ}}(A)=\frac{1}{n}\int\limits_{A}\frac{H_{n-1}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{n}}\mu_{\vec{i}}(x).

(15)

We show that the limits of the weighted
L_{p}
affine surface areas are entropy powers.

Theorem 3.8

Let
K
be a
C^{2}_{+}
convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
centroid at the origin.

(i)

\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K)}{\omega^{\infty}_{m,k,\vec{i}}(K)}\right)^{n+p}=\textup{exp}\left(-\frac{n\,D_{KL}(P_{K}||Q_{K})}{\mu_{\vec{i}}-{\rm vol}_{n}(K^{\circ})}\right).

(ii)

\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow 0}\left(\frac{\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K^{\circ})}{\omega^{0}_{m,k,\vec{i}}(K^{\circ})}\right)^{\frac{n(n+p)}{p}}=\textup{exp}\left(-\frac{n\,D_{KL}(P_{K^{\circ}}||Q_{K^{\circ}})}{\mu_{\vec{i}}-{\rm vol}_{n}(K^{\circ})}\right).

Proof.

(i) We use l’Hospitals rule

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow\infty}\ln\left(\left(\frac{\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K)}{\omega^{\infty}_{m,k,\vec{i}}(K)}\right)^{n+p}\right)=\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln\left(\frac{\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K)}{\omega^{\infty}_{m,k,\vec{i}}(K)}\right)}{(n+p)^{-1}}=-\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow\infty}(n+p)^{2}\frac{\frac{d}{dp}\left(\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K)\right)}{\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K)}

\displaystyle=-\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(n+p)^{2}}{\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K)}\int\limits_{\partial K}\frac{d}{dp}\left(\textup{exp}\left(\ln\left(\frac{H_{n-1}(x)^{\frac{p}{n+p}}}{\langle x,N_{K}(x)\rangle^{\frac{n(p-1)}{n+p}}}\right)\right)\right)d\mu_{\vec{i}}(x)

\displaystyle=-\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n}{\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K)}\int\limits_{\partial K}\frac{H_{n-1}(x)^{\frac{p}{n+p}}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{\frac{n(p-1)}{n+p}}}\ln{\left(\frac{H_{n-1}(x)}{\langle x,N_{K}(x)\rangle^{n+1}}\right)}d\mu_{\vec{i}}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{n}{\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{\infty}(K)}\int\limits_{\partial K}\frac{H_{n-1}(x)}{\langle x,N_{K}(x)\rangle^{n}}\ln{\left(\frac{H_{n-1}(x)}{\langle x,N_{K}(x)\rangle^{n+1}}\right)}d\mu_{\vec{i}}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{n}{\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{\infty}(K)}D_{KL}(P_{K}||Q_{K}).

(ii) We use l’Hospitals rule

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow 0}\ln\left(\left(\frac{\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K^{\circ})}{\omega^{0}_{m,k,\vec{i}}(K^{\circ})}\right)^{\frac{n(n+p)}{p}}\right)=\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow 0}n\frac{\ln\left(\frac{\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K^{\circ})}{\omega^{0}_{m,k,\vec{i}}(K^{\circ})}\right)}{p(n+p)^{-1}}=\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow 0}(n+p)^{2}\frac{\frac{d}{dp}\omega_{m,k,\vec{i}}^{p}(K^{\circ})}{\omega^{p}_{m,k,\vec{i}}(K^{\circ})}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow 0}\frac{(n+p)^{2}}{\omega^{p}_{m,k,\vec{i}}(K^{\circ})}\int\limits_{\partial K^{\circ}}\frac{d}{dp}\left(\textup{exp}\left(\ln\left(\frac{H_{n-1}(x)^{\frac{p}{n+p}}}{\langle x,N_{K^{\circ}}(x)\rangle^{\frac{n(p-1)}{n+p}}}\right)\right)\right)d\mu_{\vec{i}}(x)

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow 0}\frac{n}{\omega^{p}_{m,k,\vec{i}}(K^{\circ})}\int\limits_{\partial K^{\circ}}\frac{H_{n-1}(x)^{\frac{p}{n+p}}}{\langle x,N_{K^{\circ}}(x)\rangle^{\frac{n(p-1)}{n+p}}}\ln{\left(\frac{H_{n-1}(x)}{\langle x,N_{K^{\circ}}(x)\rangle^{n+1}}\right)}d\mu_{\vec{i}}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{\omega^{0}_{m,k,\vec{i}}(K^{\circ})}\int\limits_{\partial K^{\circ}}\langle x,N_{K^{\circ}}(x)\rangle\ln{\left(\frac{H_{n-1}(x)}{\langle x,N_{K^{\circ}}(x)\rangle^{n+1}}\right)}d\mu_{\vec{i}}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{n}{\omega^{0}_{m,k,\vec{i}}(K^{\circ})}D_{KL}(Q_{K^{\circ}}||P_{K^{\circ}}).

4
f
-divergence

The results in the previous section lead naturally to consider more general
f
-divergences than just the Kullback-Leibler divergence.

4.1
Background on
f
-divergence.

In information theory, probability theory and statistics, an

f
-divergence is a function that measures the difference between two (probability)
distributions. This notion was introduced by
Csiszár
[
12
]
, and independently Morimoto
[
25
]
and Ali & Silvery
[
2
]
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a measure space and let
P=p\mu
and
Q=q\mu
be (probability) measures on
X
that are absolutely continuous with respect to the measure
\mu
.
Let
f:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a convex or a concave function.
The
*
-adjoint function
f^{*}:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of
f
is defined by

f^{*}(t)=tf(1/t),\ \ t\in(0,\infty).

(16)

It is obvious that
(f^{*})^{*}=f
and that
f^{*}
is again convex if
f
is convex, respectively concave if
f
is concave.
Then the
f
-divergence
D_{f}(P,Q)
of the measures
P
and
Q
is defined by

\displaystyle D_{f}(P,Q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{pq>0\}}f\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)qd\mu+f(0)\ Q\left(\{x\in X:p(x)=0\}\right)

(17)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle f^{*}(0)\ P\left(\{x\in X:q(x)=0\}\right),

provided the expressions exist. Here

f(0)=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}f(t)\ \ \text{ and}\ \ f^{*}(0)=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}f^{*}(t).

(18)

We make the convention that
0\cdot\infty=0
.

Please note that

D_{f}(P,Q)=D_{f^{*}}(Q,P).

(19)

With (
18
) and as

f^{*}(0)\ P\left(\{x\in X:q(x)=0\}\right)=\int_{\{q=0\}}f^{*}\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)pd\mu=\int_{\{q=0\}}f\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)qd\mu,

we can write in short

D_{f}(P,Q)=\int_{X}f\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)qd\mu.

(20)

Examples of
f
-divergences are as follows.

1. For
f(t)=t\ln t
(with
*
-adjoint function
f^{*}(t)=-\ln t
), the
f
-divergence is
Kullback-Leibler divergence
or
relative entropy
from
P
to
Q
(see
[
11
]
)

D_{KL}(P\|Q)=\int_{X}p\ln\frac{p}{q}d\mu.

(21)

2. For the convex or concave functions
f(t)=t^{\alpha}
we obtain the
Hellinger integrals
(e.g.
[
20
]
)

H_{\alpha}(P,Q)=\int_{X}p^{\alpha}q^{1-\alpha}d\mu.

(22)

Those are related to the
Rényi divergence of order
\alpha
,
\alpha\neq 1
, introduced by Rényi
[
28
]
(for
\alpha>0
) as

D_{\alpha}(P\|Q)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\ln\left(\int_{X}p^{\alpha}q^{1-\alpha}d\mu\right)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\ln\left(H_{\alpha}(P,Q)\right).

(23)

The case
\alpha=1
is the relative entropy
D_{KL}(P\|Q)
.

More on
f
-divergence can be found in e.g.
[
7
,
9
,
10
,
20
,
37
,
38
,
40
]
.

4.2
f
-divergence for the
\mu_{\vec{i}}
-measure

In
[
38
]
,
f
-divergence with respect to the surface area measure
\mu_{K}
was introduced for a convex body
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
0
in its interior.
We now introduce similarly
f
-divergence with respect to the measure
\mu_{\vec{i}}
.

Definition 4.1

Let
f:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a convex or concave function. Let
p_{K}
and
q_{K}
be as in (
12
).
Then the
f
-divergence
D_{f}\left(P_{K},Q_{K}\right)
of a convex body
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to the
\mu_{\vec{i}}
-measure is

\displaystyle D_{f}\left(P_{K},Q_{K}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K}f\left(\frac{p_{K}}{q_{K}}\right)q_{K}d\mu_{\vec{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K}f\left(\frac{H_{n-1}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{n+1}}\right)\langle x,N(x)\rangle d\mu_{\vec{i}}

In particular, for

we recover the above Kullback-Leibler divergences of Section

and for

, we obtain
the weighted

-affine surface areas.

Proposition 4.2

Let
f:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a concave function.
Then

\displaystyle D_{f}\left(P_{K},Q_{K}\right)\leq f\left(\frac{\mu_{\vec{i}}-{\rm vol}_{n}(K^{\circ})}{\mu_{\vec{i}}-{\rm vol}_{n}(K)}\right)\,\mu_{\vec{i}}-{\rm vol}_{n}(K).

If
f
is convex, the inequality is reversed.
If
f
is linear, equality holds in (
4.2
).
If
f
is not linear, equality holds
iff
K
is an ellipsoid.

Proof

Let
f:(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a concave function. By Jensen’s inequality

\displaystyle D_{f}\left(P_{K},Q_{K}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\partial K}f\left(\frac{H_{n-1}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{n+1}}\right)\langle x,N(x)\rangle\,\frac{d\mu_{\vec{i}}}{\mu_{\vec{i}}-{\rm vol}_{n}(K)}\right)\,\mu_{\vec{i}}-{\rm vol}_{n}(K)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f\left(\int_{\partial K}\frac{H_{n-1}}{\langle x,N(x)\rangle^{n+1}}\langle x,N(x)\rangle\frac{d\mu_{\vec{i}}}{\mu_{\vec{i}}-{\rm vol}_{n}(K)}\right)\,\mu_{\vec{i}}-{\rm vol}_{n}(K)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{\mu_{\vec{i}}-{\rm vol}_{n}(K^{\circ}}{\mu_{\vec{i}}-{\rm vol}_{n}(K)}\right)\,\mu_{\vec{i}}-{\rm vol}_{n}(K).

Equality holds in Jensen’s inequality iff either
f
is linear or
\frac{p_{K}}{q_{K}}
is constant.
Indeed, if
f(t)=at+b
, then

\displaystyle D_{f}(P_{K},Q_{K})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K}\left(a\frac{p_{K}}{q_{K}}+b\right)q_{K}\,d\mu_{\vec{i}}=a\int_{\partial K}p_{K}\,d\mu_{\vec{i}}+b\int_{\partial K}q_{K}\,d\mu_{\vec{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\,\mu_{\vec{i}}-{\rm vol}_{n}(K^{\circ})+b\,\mu_{\vec{i}}-{\rm vol}_{n}(K).

If
f
is not linear, equality holds iff
\frac{p_{K}}{q_{K}}=c
,
c
is a constant.
By the above quoted Theorem
3.3
of Petty, this holds iff
K
is an ellipsoid.
Note that the constant
c
is different from
0
, as we assume that
K
is
C^{2}_{+}
.

Proposition 4.3

Let
K
be a convex body in

with the origin in its interior and let

Proof.

To prove (
27
), we proceed as in

. For completeness, we include the argument. We decompose

\partial(K\cup L)=(\partial K\cap\partial L)\cup(\partial K\cap L^{c})\cup(K^{c}\cap\partial L),

\partial(K\cap L)=(\partial K\cap\partial L)\cup(\partial K\cap\text{int}L)\cup(\text{int}K\cap\partial L),

\partial K=(\partial K\cap\partial L)\cup(\partial K\cap L^{c})\cup(\partial K\cap\text{int}L),

\partial L=(\partial K\cap\partial L)\cup(\partial K^{c}\cap\partial L)\cup(\text{int}K\cap\partial L),

where all unions on the right hand side are disjoint. Note that for

\kappa_{K\cup L}(x)=\min\{\kappa_{K}(x),\kappa_{L}(x)\},\ \ \kappa_{K\cap L}(x)=\max\{\kappa_{K}(x),\kappa_{L}(x)\}.

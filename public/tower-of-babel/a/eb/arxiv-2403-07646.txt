introduction

In this paper, we only consider the undirected finite simple graphs
G=(V,E)
.
The distance between two vertices of
x,y\in V(G)
,
\mathrm{d}(x,y)=\mathrm{d}_{G}(x,y)
, is the length of the shortest path between them. The diameter of
G
,
\mathrm{diam}(G)

G
.

2
-distance graph of a graph
G
,
D_{2}(G)

V(G)
and two vertices are adjacent if and only their distance is
2
in the graph
G
. For any

, we set
\mathrm{d_{2}}(u,v)=\mathrm{d}_{D_{2}(G)}(u,v)
.

The idea of 2-distance graphs is a particular case of a general notion of k-distance
graphs, which was first studied by Harary et al.
[
6
]
. They investigated the connectedness of 2-distance graph of some graphs. In the book by Prisner
[
10
]
, the dynamics of the k-distance operator was explored. Furthermore, Boland et al.
[
3
]
extended the k-distance operator to a graph invariant they call distance-n domination number.
To solve one problem posed by Prisner
[
10
]
, Zelinka

[
12
]
constructed a graph that is r-periodic under the 2-distance operator for each
positive even integer r. Prisner’s problem was completely solved by Leonor in her
master’s thesis
[
9
]
, wherein she worked with a graph construction different from
that of Zelinka’s.
Recently, Azimi and Farrokhi
[
1
]
studied all graphs whose 2-distance graphs
have maximum degree 2. They also solved the problem of finding all graphs whose
2-distance graphs are paths or cycles. More recently, the same authors
[
2
]
determined
all finite simple graphs with no square, diamond, or triangles with a common
vertex that are self 2-distance graphs (that is, graphs
G
satisfying the equation

D^{2}(G)=G
. They further showed the nonexistence of cubic self 2-distance
graphs.
In
[
5
]
, Ching gives some characterizations of 2-distance graphs and finds all graphs X such that

or

, where

is an integer,
P_{2}
is the path of order
2

is the complete graph of order

.
In
[
8
]
Khormali gives some conditions for connectivity

. For

is disconnected.
In
[
7

is connected if and only if
G
has no spanning complete bipartite subgraphs.

In this paper, for graphs
G
with diameter 2, we show that
\mathrm{diam}(D_{2}(G))
can be any integer

. For graphs

\mathrm{diam}(G)\geqslant 3

and this inequality is sharp. Also, for

\mathrm{diam}(D_{2}(G))\leqslant 5
and this inequality is sharp.

3
Conclusions

In this paper, we focused on the diameter of the connected
2
-distance graph of graphs. We found a sharp lower bound for the diameter of
2
-distance graphs relative to the diameter of their initial graphs. Also, we showed that
\mathrm{diam}(D_{2}(G))
can be any integer
t\geqslant 2
when
\mathrm{diam}(G)=2
. But for
\mathrm{diam}(G)=3
, we obtained a sharp upper bound for
\mathrm{diam}(D_{2}(G))
. The above achievements raise the following questions.

Conjecture 3.1
.

Let
\mathrm{diam}(G)=n\geqslant 3
. Then
\mathrm{diam}(D_{2}(G))\leqslant n+2
.

Problem 3.2.
Charactrize all graphs
G
which
\mathrm{diam}(G)=2
and
\mathrm{diam}(D_{2}(G))=2\text{ or }3
.

In Theorem
2.5
, we proved
\mathrm{diam}(D_{2}(G))\geqslant\left\lceil\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{diam}(G)\right\rceil
and in Example
2.7
we show this inequality is sharp.

Problem 3.3.
Are there some graphs with
\mathrm{diam}(G)=n
and
\mathrm{diam}(D_{2}(G))=\left\lceil\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{diam}(G)\right\rceil+1
.

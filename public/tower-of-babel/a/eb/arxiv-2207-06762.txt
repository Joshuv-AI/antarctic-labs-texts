A Short Note on the Pell-Lucas-Eisenstein Series
Mine Uysal, Ilker Inam, Engin Özkan

Abstract
In this work, we define a new type of Eisenstein-like series by using Pell-Lucas numbers and call them the Pell-Lucas-Eisenstein Series. Firstly, we show that the Pell-Lucas-Eisenstein series are convergent on their domain. Afterwards we prove that they satisfy some certain functional equations. Proofs follows from some on calculations on Pell-Lucas numbers. MSC (2010): Primary: 11P37, secondary: 11P81, 11P83 Keywords: Eisenstein series, Pell-Lucas numbers, modular forms, integer partitions.



1
Introduction and Statement of the Results

Eisenstein series play a fundamental theory role in the theory of modular forms which brings different branches of mathematics together. More precisely, they serve as important examples with coefficients that are easy to compute even with a very high number of coefficients is requested. For example, in
[
4
]
, Eisenstein series are effectively used for obtaining Hecke eigenforms of half-integral weight by Rankin-Cohen brackets. For more details on modular forms see
[
3
]
and
[
8
]
.

In a recent work in
[
6
]
, Matthew Just and Robert Schneider define an Eisenstein-like series summed over integer partitions, and use it to construct families of "semi-modular forms". As a continuation work, in
[
2
]
, replacing integer partitions by Fibonacci numbers, Agbolade P. Akande and Robert Schneider construct a family of Eisenstein-like series to produce semi-modular forms as well as Lucas sequences.

Naturally, our inspiration for this work is coming from looking for further examples on partition Eisenstein series. In a recent paper
[
5
]
, we define the Pell-Eisenstein Series and give their properties. In this short note, as a follow up work, we define the Pell-Lucas-Eisenstein series.

Definition 1.1
.

The
n
th-Pell-Lucas number is denoted by
Q_{n}
and defined as
Q_{0}=2
and
Q_{1}=2
and for
n\geq 3

Q_{n}=2Q_{n-1}+Q_{n-2}.

The reader is referred to
[
7
]
for details about Pell-Lucas numbers. A recent work on the topic can be seen at
[
3
]
.

Definition 1.2
.

The Pell-Lucas-Eisenstein Series of weight
m
is denoted by
Q_{m}(z)
defined as

Q_{m}(z):=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}(Q_{j}z+Q_{j-1})^{-m}

First of all, we should check whether
Q_{m}(z)
is well-defined.

Theorem 1.3
.

For
m>1
,
Q_{m}(z)
Pell-Lucas-Eisenstein series are convergent on its domain.

Proof.

By Weierstrass criterion, it is easy to see that we can bound
Q_{m}(z)
by
\zeta(m)
and the theorem follows.
∎

After a short calculation, one can get the following well-known fact.

Theorem 1.4
.

[
5
]

Let
Q_{n}
be the
n
th-Pell-Lucas number. Then

Q_{-n}=(-1)^{n}Q_{n}.

Now we are ready to state the main contribution of the paper:

Theorem 1.5
.

The Pell-Lucas-Eisenstein series are satisfying the following functional equations:

(i)

Q_{2k}(\frac{-1}{z})=z^{2k}Q_{2k}(z)

(ii)

Q_{2k}(2-z)=Q_{2k}(z)

(iii)

Q_{2k}(z+2)=z^{-2k}Q_{2k}(\frac{1}{z})

(iv)

Q_{2k}(-z)=z^{-2k}Q_{2k}(\frac{1}{z})

2
Proof of the Main Result

In this section, we will give proof of the main result, namely Theorem
1.5
. It will be based on direct calculation.

Proof.

(i)

Let
Q_{2k}
be the Pell-Lucas-Eisenstein series of weight
2k
. Then, it is clear that

Q_{2k}\left(\frac{-1}{z}\right)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(Q_{j}\left(\frac{-1}{z}\right)+Q_{j-1}\right)^{-2k}.

By expanding the series, we have

\displaystyle Q_{2k}\left(\frac{-1}{z}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dots+\left(\frac{14}{z}+34\right)^{-2k}+\left(\frac{-6}{z}-14\right)^{-2k}+\left(\frac{2}{z}+6\right)^{-2k}+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{-2}{z}-2\right)^{-2k}+\left(\frac{-2}{z}+2\right)^{-2k}+\left(-\frac{6}{z}+2\right)^{-2k}+\left(-\frac{14}{z}+6\right)^{-2k}+\dots

By equating the denominator and taking common brackets, we get

\displaystyle Q_{2k}\left(\frac{-1}{z}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z^{2k}[\dots+(34z+14)^{-2k}+(-14z-6)^{-2k}+

\displaystyle(6z+2)^{-2k}+(-2z-2)^{-2k}+(2z-2)^{-2k}

\displaystyle+(2z-6)^{-2k}+(6z-14)^{-2k}+\dots]=z^{2k}Q_{2k}(z)

as desired.

(ii)

By the definition of the Pell-Eisenstein series, it is clear that

Q_{2k}(2-z)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}(Q_{j}(2-z)+Q_{j-1})^{-2k}=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}(Q_{j}(z)+Q_{j-1})^{-2k}=Q_{2k}(z).

(iii)

Let us define

Q_{m}(z)=Q_{m}^{-}(z)+Q_{m}^{+}(z)

where
Q_{m}^{-}(z):=\sum_{-\infty<n\leq 0}(Q_{n}z+Q_{n-1})^{-m}
and
Q_{m}^{+}(z):=\sum_{1\leq n\leq\infty}(Q_{n}z+Q_{n-1})^{-m}
.

It is clear that

\displaystyle Q_{2k}^{+}(z+2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}(Q_{n}(z+2)+Q_{n-1})^{-2k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}(Q_{n}z+2Q_{n}+Q_{n-1})^{-2k}=\sum_{n\geq 1}(Q_{n}z+Q_{n+1})^{-2k}.

By expanding the series and multiplying by
z^{2k}
both sides, we have

\sum_{n\geq 1}(Q_{n}z+Q_{n+1})^{-2k}=\sum_{n\geq 1}(Q_{n}(\frac{1}{z})+Q_{n-1})^{-2k},

hence we get

Q_{2k}^{+}(z+2)=z^{-2k}Q_{2k}^{+}(\frac{1}{z})-(\frac{2}{z}+2)^{-2k}.

Similarly, we have
Q_{2k}^{-}(z+2)=z^{-2k}Q_{2k}^{-}(\frac{1}{z})+(\frac{2}{z}+2)^{-2k}
.

Finally, by combining two results above, we find

Q_{2k}(z+2)=z^{-2k}Q_{2k}(\frac{1}{z}),

as desired.

(iv)

Assume the setting above and let us consider

Q_{2k}^{-}(-z)=\sum_{n\leq 0}(Q_{n}(-z)+Q_{n-1})^{-2k}

If we expand the series and afterwards multiplying by
z^{2k}
then we can easily conclude that

Q_{2k}^{-}(-z)=z^{-2k}Q_{2k}^{+}(\frac{1}{z}).

Exactly the same arguments are valid for the positive part and we get

Q_{2k}^{+}(-z)=z^{-2k}Q_{2k}^{-}(\frac{1}{z})

The last step will be

Q_{2k}^{-}(-z)+Q_{2k}^{+}(-z)=z^{-2k}(Q_{2k}^{+}(1/z)+Q_{2k}^{-}(1/z))

and the result follows.

∎

3
Conclusion Remarks and Future Work

In this short note, we introduce the Pell-Lucas-Eisenstein series and study their basic properties. This work gives a positive answer to the theoretical question in
[
1
]
about the existence of classes of special functions in another direction. Other arithmetic properties of the Pell-Lucas-Eisenstein series is an open problem for the moment.

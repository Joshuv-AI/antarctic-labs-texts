The Weak Galerkin Finite Element Method For The Steklov Eigenvalue Problem
Shusheng Li, Hehu Xie, Qilong Zhai

Abstract
This paper introduces a weak Galerkin (WG) finite element method to solve the Steklov eigenvalue problems, focusing on obtaining lower bounds of the eigenvalues. Compared with the existing work, our method can provide asymptotic lower bound approximations of eigenvalues with arbitrary high order convergence and no additional conditions are required. Moreover, through adjustments in stabilizer coefficients, we obtain guaranteed lower bound analysis for the weak Galerkin finite element method with arbitrary high degree. Numerical results demonstrate the accuracy and lower bound property of the numerical scheme.



keywords

weak Galerkin finite element method, Steklov eigenvalue problem, high order, asymptotic lower bound, guaranteed lower bound.

Introduction

The Steklov eigenvalue problems with eigenvalue parameter appearing at the boundary have received extensive attention and have been applied to the fields of mathematics and physics, for example, stability of mechanical oscillators immersed in a viscous fluid
[
9
]
, surface waves
[
4
]
, the vibration modes of a structure in contact with an incompressible fluid
[
5
]
, vibrations of a pendulum
[
1
]
, the antiplane shearing on a system of collinear faults under slip-dependent friction law
[
7
]
, eigenoscillations of mechanical systems with boundary
conditions containing frequency
[
10
]
, among many others.

Several research papers have explored various numerical techniques for approximating the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions associated with the Steklov eigenvalue problems. These studies can be summarized as follows. Bramble and Osborn
[
6
]
delved into the use of the conforming finite element methods to solve the Steklov eigenvalue problems. Andreev and Todorov
[
2
]
focused their researches on finite element methods tailored to approximating the Steklov eigenvalue problems for second-order, self-adjoint, elliptic differential operators. Furthermore, Yang and Bi
[
27
]
made contributions by introducing local
a

priori
and
a

posteriori
error estimates for the conforming finite element approximations of the Steklov eigenvalue problems. These estimates served as valuable tools for assessing the accuracy of numerical solutions. In
[
19
]
, Wang et al. analyzed the use of the conforming virtual element methods for non-self-adjoint Steklov eigenvalue problems. They also provided an
a

priori
error estimate for discrete eigenvalues and eigenfunctions.

The reference to the minimum-maximum principle highlights a limitation of the conforming finite element methods, as they can only provide upper bounds of the eigenvalues. This limitation underscores the importance of obtaining lower bounds of the eigenvalues. Yang et al.
[
28
]
studied the nonconforming finite element methods for the Steklov eigenvalue problems on convex domains. They specifically looked into methods for obtaining lower bounds of eigenvalues using different finite element types, such as the Crouzeix–Raviart element, the
Q_{1}^{rot}
element and the
EQ_{1}^{rot}
element. Their findings highlighted that the
EQ_{1}^{rot}
element can get asymptotic lower bounds of all exact eigenvalues, while the Crouzeix–Raviart element and the
Q_{1}^{rot}
element can only get lower bounds of the large eigenvalues, e.g., the upper bounds of the smallest eigenvalue can be obtained by these two methods. Furthermore, Li et al.
[
11
]
studied the nonconforming finite element methods for the Steklov eigenvalue problems on the convex and concave domains. They introduced a novel element, the ECR element, and conducted rigorous analysis of asymptotic lower bound approximations for the Steklov eigenvalues problems on the general domains. To provide rigorous lower eigenvalue bounds for the Steklov eigenvalue problems, You et al.
[
29
]
introduced an enhanced version of an eigenvalue estimate algorithm designed to handle positive semidefinite bilinear forms. This algorithm extended the applicability of existing methods tailored for positive definite bilinear forms. In addition, Zhang et al.
[
34
]
introduced a corrective approach to the Crouzeix-Raviart finite element eigenvalue approximations. They managed to obtain asymptotic lower bounds of eigenvalues for the Steklov eigenvalue problems featuring variable coefficients. Crucially, their work eliminated the need for specific conditions, such as singular eigenfunctions and large eigenvalues, thus enhancing the versatility. Unfortunately, the above work only considered lower bounds of eigenvalues with lower order convergence.

The main goal of this paper is to use the weak Galerkin (WG) finite element method to solve the Steklov eigenvalue problems and obtain lower bounds of eigenvalues. The WG method, originally proposed in
[
21
]
, has been successfully applied to second order elliptic problems
[
14
,
20
,
22
]
, Stokes equations
[
16
,
23
,
25
,
26
,
31
]
, Helmholtz equations
[
13
,
24
]
, biharmonic equations
[
33
]
, Maxwell equations
[
15
]
, Navier-Stokes equations
[
32
]
and Stokes-Darcy problems
[
17
]
. The WG method uses the special weak differential operators instead of the traditional differential operators and uses discontinuous piecewise polynomials as basis functions on polygonal or polyhedral finite element partitions. Therefore, the WG method can flexibly solve partial differential equations.

In
[
30
]
, Zhai et al. proposed the WG method for elliptic eigenvalue problems. A general framework was proposed and applied to elliptic eigenvalue problems. As long as (A1)-(A7) in
[
30
]
are proved, the eigenvalues obtained by the WG method are asymptotic lower bounds of the exact eigenvalues. Subsequently, Carstensen et al.
[
8
]
proposed a skeletal finite element method that can compute lower eigenvalue bounds. They rigorously demonstrated the existence of guaranteed lower eigenvalue bounds (GLB) for the Laplacian eigenvalue problems and conducted an analysis of the lowest-order case. The main contributions of this paper can be summarized as follows. Firstly, we use the WG method to solve the Steklov eigenvalue problems for the first time and extend the general framework in
[
30
]
and the GLB in
[
8
]
to the Steklov eigenvalue problems. Secondly, our method can obtain asymptotic lower bounds of the exact eigenvalues with arbitrary high order convergence, by adjusting the coefficients of stabilizer, we obtain the guaranteed lower bound analysis for the WG method with arbitrary high degree.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce the WG scheme for the Steklov eigenvalue problems. In Section 3, we get error estimates of eigenvalues and eigenfunctions, then the asymptotic lower bounds with arbitrary high order convergence are obtained. The error estimates in
H^{1}
and
L^{2}
norms for the source problems corresponding to the Steklov eigenvalue problems are given in Section 4. In Section 5, we prove the GLB theorem which gives the conditions for guaranteed lower eigenvalue bounds. Numerical experiments are presented to demonstrate the accuracy and lower bound property of the WG scheme in Section 6.

2
The weak Galerkin finite element method

In this section, we propose the WG scheme for the Steklov eigenvalue problems and give the general notations.

2.1
The Steklov eigenvalue problem

In this paper, for simplicity, we consider the following equation:

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta{u}+u&=0,&&\quad\text{in }\Omega,&\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\mathbf{n}}&=\lambda{u},&&\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,&\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Omega
is a bounded polygonal or polyhedral domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=2,3
),
\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}}
is the outward normal derivative of
\partial\Omega
.

The corresponding variational form of the equation (
2.1
) is: Find
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
such that
\|u\|_{b}=1
and

(2.2)

a(u,v)=\lambda b(u,v),~~~\forall v\in H^{1}(\Omega),

where

\displaystyle a(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u\nabla v+uv)dx,

\displaystyle b(u,v)=\int_{\partial\Omega}uvd\sigma,~~\|u\|_{b}=b(u,u)^{1/2},

where
\sigma
is the measure on
\partial\Omega
.

From
[
4
,
6
]
, the Steklov eigenvalue problem (
2.1
) has an eigenvalue sequence
\{\lambda_{i}\}
:

0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{i}\leq\cdots,~~\lim\limits_{i\to\infty}\lambda_{i}=\infty,

with the corresponding eigenfunction sequence

u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{i},\cdots,

where
b(u_{i},u_{j})=\delta_{ij}.

In this paper, we adopt the standard Sobolev space notation. Consider any open bounded domain
D
with a Lipschitz continuous boundary in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=2,3
). We denote the seminorm on this domain as
|\cdot|_{D}
. The norm and inner product are represented as
\|\cdot\|_{D}
and

(\cdot,\cdot)_{D}
, respectively. When
D=\Omega
, we will omit the subscript
D
.

Consider
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
as a partition of the domain
\Omega
, where the cells in
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
are either polygons in two dimensions or polyhedra in three dimensions satisfying the regular assumptions specified in
[
22
]
. We denote the set of all edges or faces of
T
as
\mathcal{E}(T)
. The set of all edges or faces in
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
is represented by
\mathcal{E}_{h}
, and
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{0}
stands for the set of all interior edges or faces.
For each cell
T
in
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
,
h_{T}
represents its diameter, and
h
is the maximum diameter among all cells in
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
. Let
C
be a positive constant which is independent of the mesh size. We use the notation
a\lesssim b
to denote
a\leq Cb
.

2.2
The weak Galerkin scheme

First we introduce the WG space for the Steklov eigenvalue problem (
2.1
).
For any integer
k\geq 1
, we define

V_{h}=\{v=\{v_{0},v_{b}\}:v_{0}|_{T}\in P_{k}(T),v_{b}|_{e}\in P_{k}(e),\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\forall e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\},

where
P_{k}(T)
denotes the space of ploynomials whose degree is not more than
k
on
T
,
P_{k}(e)
denotes the space of polynomials whose degree is not more than
k
on
e
.

For each cell
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we introduce the
L^{2}
projection operator
Q_{0}
, which projects from
L^{2}(T)
onto
P_{k}(T)
. Similarly, we denote
Q_{b}
as the
L^{2}
projection operator, but this one projects from
L^{2}(e)
onto
P_{k}(e)
for each edge/face
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
. Additionally, for each cell
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we define
\mathbb{Q}_{h}
as the
L^{2}
projection operator from
[L^{2}(T)]^{d}
onto
[P_{k-1}(T)]^{d}
. We define
Q_{h}=\{Q_{0},Q_{b}\}
to denote the projection operator from
H^{1}(\Omega)
onto
V_{h}
.

Definition 1
.

For each
v_{h}\in V_{h}
, its weak gradient is defined as the following polynomial
\nabla_{w}v_{h}|_{T}\in[P_{k-1}(T)]^{d}
satisfying

(2.3)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{w}v_{h},{\mathbf{w}})_{T}=-(v_{0},\nabla\cdot{\mathbf{w}})_{T}+\langle v_{b},{\mathbf{w}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\forall{\mathbf{w}}\in[P_{k-1}(T)]^{d},

where
{\mathbf{n}}
denotes the outward unit normal vector.

For any
v_{h},w_{h}\in V_{h}
, we define three bilinear forms on
V_{h}

\displaystyle s(v_{h},w_{h})=\gamma(h)\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\langle v_{0}-v_{b},w_{0}-w_{b}\rangle_{\partial T},

\displaystyle a_{w}(v_{h},w_{h})=(\nabla_{w}v_{h},\nabla_{w}w_{h})+(v_{0},w_{0})+s(v_{h},w_{h}),

\displaystyle b_{w}(v_{h},w_{h})=\langle v_{b},w_{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega},

where
\gamma(h)
satisfies
\gamma(h)\leq 1
and
\gamma(h)\rightarrow 0
as
h\rightarrow 0
. The selection of
\gamma(h)
is not unique, such as
\gamma(h)=h^{\varepsilon}
or
-\frac{1}{\log(h)}
, where
\varepsilon
is a positive constant satisfying
0<\varepsilon<1/4
.

Next we give the WG algorithm of (
2.1
).

Weak Galerkin Algorithm 1
.

Find
(\lambda_{h},u_{h})\in\mathbb{R}\times V_{h}
such that
\|u_{h}\|_{b}=1
and

(2.4)

\displaystyle a_{w}(u_{h},v_{h})=\lambda_{h}b_{w}(u_{h},v_{h}),\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.

3
Asymptotic lower bound

In this section, we give some technical tools that are used later and the error estimates for the Steklov eigenvalue problem (
2.2
). Finally, we obtain asymptotic lower bound approximations of the exact eigenvalues with arbitrary high order convergence.

3.1
Technical tools

Next we introduce several inequalities used in this paper, and the proofs can be found in
[
22
]
.

Lemma 2
.

(Trace inequality). For each cell
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and each edge
e\subset\partial T
, we have

\|g\|_{e}^{2}\leq C(h_{T}^{-1}\|g\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}\|\nabla g\|_{T}^{2}),\quad\forall g\in H^{1}(T).

Lemma 3
.

(Inverse inequality). Let
\varphi
be a piecewise polynomial on cell
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, then we have

\|\nabla\varphi\|_{T}\leq Ch_{T}^{-1}\|\varphi\|_{T}.

Lemma 4
.

(Projection inequality). For any
\phi\in H^{k+1}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\phi-Q_{0}\phi\|_{T}^{2}+\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{2}\|\nabla(\phi-Q_{0}\phi)\|_{T}^{2}\leq Ch^{2(k+1)}\|\phi\|_{k+1}^{2},

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla\phi-\mathbb{Q}_{h}(\nabla\phi)\|_{T}^{2}+\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{2}\|\nabla(\nabla\phi-\mathbb{Q}_{h}(\nabla\phi))\|_{T}^{2}\leq Ch^{2k}\|\phi\|_{k+1}^{2},

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\phi-Q_{b}\phi\|^{2}_{\partial T}\leq Ch^{2k+1}\|\phi\|_{k+1}^{2}.

Proof.

The proofs of the first two inequalities can be found in
[
22
]
, we only give the proof of the third one. From the trace inequality and the first projection inequality, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\phi-Q_{b}\phi\|^{2}_{\partial T}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\phi-Q_{0}\phi\|^{2}_{\partial T}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}C(h_{T}^{-1}\|\phi-Q_{0}\phi\|^{2}_{T}+h_{T}\|\nabla(\phi-Q_{0}\phi)\|^{2}_{T})

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2k+1}\|\phi\|_{k+1}^{2},

which completes the proof.
∎

The following commutativity property plays a crucial role in the theoretical analysis, and the proof can be found in
[
14
, Lemma 5.1]
.

Lemma 5
.

For any cell
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we have

\nabla_{w}(Q_{h}\phi)=\mathbb{Q}_{h}(\nabla\phi),\quad\forall\phi\in H^{1}(T).

3.2
Error estimate

Let
V_{c}=H^{1}(\Omega)
, and
V=V_{c}+V_{h}
.
For any
u\in V
, we define the following norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{V}^{2}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Big(\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{T}^{2}+\|u_{0}\|_{T}^{2}+h_{T}^{-1}\|u_{0}-u_{b}\|^{2}_{\partial T}\Big).

Here
u_{0}
represents the value of
u
on the interior of each cell
T
in the partition
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, while
u_{b}
represents the value of
u
on each edge or face
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
.

Next we verify the (A1)-(A7) in Section 2 of
[
30
]
to prove lower bound property of the eigenvalues.

First we verify (A1):

a(\cdot,\cdot)
and
a_{w}(\cdot,\cdot)
are symmetric, and

\displaystyle a(u,u)\geq\gamma_{c}\|u\|_{V}^{2},\quad\forall u\in V_{c},

\displaystyle a_{w}(u_{h},u_{h})\geq\gamma(h)\|u_{h}\|_{V},\quad\forall u_{h}\in V_{h},

where
\gamma_{c}
and
\gamma(h)
are two positive constants for given
h
.

It is easy to verify that
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is symmetric, coercive and continuous, and
a_{w}(\cdot,\cdot)
is symmetric and continuous. Therefore, we only need to prove that
a_{w}(\cdot,\cdot)
is coercive.

Lemma 6
.

For any
u_{h}\in V_{h}
, we have

a_{w}(u_{h},u_{h})\gtrsim\gamma(h)\|u_{h}\|_{V}^{2}.

Proof.

Similar to
[
30
, Lemma 3.2]
, we can obtain

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{T}^{2}\leq C\gamma(h)^{-1}a_{w}(u_{h},u_{h}),

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|u_{0}-u_{b}\|_{\partial T}^{2}\leq C\gamma(h)^{-1}a_{w}(u_{h},u_{h}),

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|u_{0}\|_{T}^{2}\leq\gamma(h)^{-1}a_{w}(u_{h},u_{h}).

Combining the above three inequalities, we have

a_{w}(u_{h},u_{h})\gtrsim\gamma(h)\|u_{h}\|_{V}^{2},

which completes the proof.
∎

We define two operators
K,K_{h}
as follows

\displaystyle K:~V_{c}\rightarrow V_{c},~~a(Kf,v)=b(f,v),~~~\forall v\in V_{c},

\displaystyle K_{h}:~V_{h}\rightarrow V_{h},~~a_{w}(K_{h}f_{h},v_{h})=b_{w}(f_{h},v_{h}),~~~\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.

It is easy to prove that
K
and
K_{h}
are well-defined by the Lax-Milgram theorem.

Next we verify (A2):
K,K_{h}
are compact.

Lemma 7
.

K,K_{h}
are compact operators.

Proof.

From
[
5
, Proposition 4.4]
and
[
6
, (4.10)]
, we have
\|Kf\|_{1+r}\leq C\|f\|_{\frac{1}{2},\partial\Omega}
, where
1/2<r\leq 1
. It follows from the trace inequality that
\|f\|_{\frac{1}{2},\partial\Omega}\leq C\|f\|_{1}
. And
H^{1+r}(\Omega)
is compact embedded into
H^{1}(\Omega)
, so
K
is a compact operator. For the operator
K_{h}
, since it is a finite rank operator, it is only necessary to prove that it is a bounded linear operator. From Lemma
6
, we have

\gamma(h)\|K_{h}f_{h}\|_{V}^{2}\lesssim a_{w}(K_{h}f_{h},K_{h}f_{h})=b_{w}(f_{h},K_{h}f_{h}).

It follows from
[
18
, Lemma 3.11]
that

\displaystyle b_{w}(K_{h}f_{h},f_{h})\leq\|K_{h}f_{h}\|_{b}\|f_{h}\|_{b}\leq C|\!|\!|K_{h}f_{h}|\!|\!|_{1}|\!|\!|f_{h}|\!|\!|_{1},

where

|\!|\!|v|\!|\!|_{1}=\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla_{w}v\|^{2}_{T}+\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|v_{0}\|^{2}_{T}+\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|v_{0}-v_{b}\|^{2}_{\partial T},~\forall v\in V_{h}.

According to
[
14
, Lemma 5.3]
, there exist two positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that the following inequality holds

\displaystyle C_{1}\|v\|_{V}\leq|\!|\!|v|\!|\!|_{1}\leq C_{2}\|v\|_{V},~~~\forall v\in V_{h}.

Thus, we have
\|K_{h}f_{h}\|_{V}\leq C\|f_{h}\|_{V}
, so
K_{h}
is a bounded linear operator. Then we complete the proof.
∎

Next we verify (A3): there exists a bounded linear operator
Q_{h}:V\rightarrow V_{h}
satisfying

\displaystyle Q_{h}v_{h}=v_{h},\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h},

\displaystyle b(Q_{h}w,v_{h})=b(w,v_{h}),\quad\forall w\in V,v_{h}\in V_{h}.

Obviously,
Q_{h}v_{h}=v_{h},\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}
, and

b(w,v_{h})=\langle w_{b},v_{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega}=\langle Q_{b}w_{b},v_{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega}=b(Q_{h}w,v_{h}).

Then (A3) is verified.

Let
X
be the
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
space. Denote

\displaystyle\delta_{h,\mu}=\sup\limits_{v\in R(E_{\mu}(K)),\atop\|v\|_{V}=1}\|v-Q_{h}v\|_{V},

\displaystyle\delta_{h,\mu}^{\prime}=\sup\limits_{v\in R(E_{\mu}(K)),\atop\|v\|_{V}=1}\|v-Q_{h}v\|_{X}.

The definition of
R(E_{\mu}(K))
can be found in Section 2 of
[
30
]
. It is the eigenspace corresponding to the eigenvalue
\mu
.

Denote

\displaystyle e_{h,\mu}=\|(K-K_{h}Q_{h})|_{R(E_{\mu}(K)}\|_{V},

\displaystyle e_{h,\mu}^{\prime}=\|(\Pi_{0}K-\Pi_{0}K_{h}Q_{h})|_{R(E_{\mu}(K))}\|_{X},

where
\Pi_{0}
is the projection operator from
V
onto
X
under the inner product
b(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Next we verify (A4) and (A5):

\displaystyle(A4):e_{h,\mu}\rightarrow 0~{\rm as}~h\rightarrow 0,{\rm and}~\delta_{h,\mu}\gamma(h)^{-1}\rightarrow 0~{\rm as}~h\rightarrow 0,

\displaystyle(A5):e_{h,\mu}^{\prime}\rightarrow 0~{\rm as}~h\rightarrow 0,{\rm and}~\delta_{h,\mu}^{\prime}\gamma(h)^{-1}\rightarrow 0~{\rm as}~h\rightarrow 0.

Lemma 8
.

Assume
R(E_{\mu}(K))\subset H^{k+1}(\Omega)
, we can establish the following estimates

\displaystyle\delta_{h,\mu}\lesssim h^{k},

\displaystyle\delta_{h,\mu}^{\prime}\lesssim h^{k+\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

Assume
u\in R(E_{\mu}(K))\subset H^{k+1}(\Omega)
. From the trace inequality and the projection inequality, we can obtain

\displaystyle\|u-Q_{h}u\|_{V}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla(u-Q_{0}u)\|_{T}^{2}+\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|(u-Q_{0}u)\|_{T}^{2}+\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|Q_{0}u-Q_{b}u\|_{\partial T}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla(u-Q_{0}u)\|_{T}^{2}+\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|(u-Q_{0}u)\|_{T}^{2}+\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}\|Q_{0}u-u\|_{\partial T}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla(u-Q_{0}u)\|_{T}^{2}+\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|(u-Q_{0}u)\|_{T}^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}C(h_{T}^{-2}\|Q_{0}u-u\|_{T}^{2}+\|\nabla(Q_{0}u-u)\|_{T}^{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle h^{2k},

and

\|u-Q_{h}u\|_{X}^{2}\leq\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|(u-Q_{b}u)\|_{\partial T}^{2}\lesssim h^{2k+1}.

Then we complete the proof.
∎

Consider the source problem corresponding to the Steklov eigenvalue problem (
2.1
):

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta{u}+u&=0,&&\quad\text{in }\Omega,&\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\mathbf{n}}&=f,&&\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,&\\
\end{aligned}\right.

where
f\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
. The corresponding WG scheme is: Find
u_{h}\in V_{h}
such that

(3.2)

And we have the following two equations:

(3.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} a(Kf,v)=b(f,v),\quad\forall v\in V_{c},\\
a(u,v)=b(f,v),\quad\forall v\in V_{c},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} a_{w}(K_{h}Q_{h}f,v_{h})=b(f,v_{h}),\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h},\\
a_{w}(u_{h},v_{h})=b(f,v_{h}),\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.\end{aligned}\right.

From (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we can get
Kf=u
and

K_{h}Q_{h}f=u_{h}
, respectively. The
H^{1}
and
L^{2}
error estimates of (
3.2
) are given in the next section, and the details can be seen in Theorems
18
and
19
. Hence we have the following lemma.

Lemma 9
.

Assume
R(E_{\mu}(K))\subset H^{k+1}(\Omega)
, the following error estimates hold

\displaystyle e_{h,\mu}\lesssim\gamma(h)^{-1}h^{k},

\displaystyle e_{h,\mu}^{\prime}\lesssim\gamma(h)^{-1}h^{k+\frac{r}{2}},

where
1/2<r\leq 1
.

According to
[
30
, Theorem 2.1, Theorem 2.2]
, we have the following theorem. The details can be seen in
[
3
, Theorem 7.1]
.

Theorem 10
.

Let
\lambda
be an eigenvalue of
(\ref{2.2})
with multiplicity

,
\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}
represent a basis of the corresponding m-dimensional eigenspace
R(E_{\mu}(K))\subset H^{k+1}(\Omega)
. Similarly, let

\{u_{j,h}\}_{j=1}^{m}
be a basis of the corresponding eigenspace

4
The source problem error estimate

In this section, we derive the
H^{1}
and
L^{2}
error estimates of the WG scheme (
3.2
). First, we recall the source problem and the corresponding numerical scheme:

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta{u}+u&=0,&&\quad\text{in }\Omega,&\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\mathbf{n}}&=f,&&\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,&\\
\end{aligned}\right.

where
f\in L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
. The corresponding WG scheme is: Find
u_{h}\in V_{h}
such that

(3.2)

a_{w}(u_{h},v_{h})=b(f,v_{h}),\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.

4.1
Error equation

Let
u
represent the solution of (
3.1
), and
u_{h}
denote the solution of (
3.2
). Denote

e_{h}=Q_{h}u-u_{h}=\{e_{0},e_{b}\}=\{Q_{0}u-u_{0},Q_{b}u-u_{b}\},

then
e_{h}
satisfies the following lemma.

Lemma 16
.

For any
v_{h}\in V_{h}
, we have

a_{w}(e_{h},v_{h})=\ell(u,v_{h})+s(Q_{h}u,v_{h}),

where

\ell(u,v_{h})=\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\langle(\nabla u-\mathbb{Q}_{h}\nabla u)\cdot\mathbf{n},v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}.

Proof.

It follows from the definition of the weak gradient (
2.3
) and Lemma
5
that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}Q_{h}u,\nabla_{w}v_{h})_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\mathbb{Q}_{h}\nabla u,\nabla_{w}v_{h})_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\nabla\cdot\mathbb{Q}_{h}\nabla u,v_{0})_{T}+\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\mathbb{Q}_{h}(\nabla u)\cdot\mathbf{n},v_{b})_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\mathbb{Q}_{h}\nabla u,\nabla v_{0})_{T}-\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\langle\mathbb{Q}_{h}(\nabla u)\cdot\mathbf{n},v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}

(4.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\nabla u,\nabla v_{0})_{T}-\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\langle\mathbb{Q}_{h}(\nabla u)\cdot\mathbf{n},v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}.

For the source problem (
3.1
), we use
v_{0}
and
v_{b}
as test functions, respectively. Then we have

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\Delta u,v_{0})_{T}+\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(u,v_{0})_{T}

(4.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\nabla u,\nabla v_{0})_{T}+\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(u,v_{0})_{T}-\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\langle\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n},v_{0}\rangle_{\partial T}=0,

and

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\langle\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n},v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}=\langle\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n},v_{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega}=\langle f,v_{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega}.

From
(\ref{3.2})
and
(\ref{3.3})
, we arrive at

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\nabla u,\nabla v_{0})_{T}+\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(u,v_{0})_{T}-\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\langle\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n},v_{0}-v_{b}\rangle_{\partial T}=\langle f,v_{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega}.

Combining
(\ref{3.1})
with
(\ref{3.4})
, we can obtain

\displaystyle a_{w}(Q_{h}u,v_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(Q_{0}u-u,v_{0})_{T}+\langle f,v_{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega}+s(Q_{h}u,v_{h})+\ell(u,v_{h}).

Notice that
u_{h}
is the solution that satisfies the following equation

a_{w}(u_{h},v_{h})=\langle f,v_{h}\rangle_{\partial\Omega}=\langle f,v_{b}\rangle_{\partial\Omega},

and
u
satisfies the following equation

\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(Q_{0}u-u,v_{0})_{T}=0.

Thus, we have

a_{w}(e_{h},v_{h})=\ell(u,v_{h})+s(Q_{h}u,v_{h}),\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h},

which completes the proof.
∎

5
Guaranteed lower bounds

In this section, we give a new numerical scheme and prove the GLB theorem.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
be a regular triangulation of the domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}~(d=2,3)
into simplices,
\alpha
be a global parameter that satisfies
\alpha>0
.
Next we give the another WG algorithm of (
2.1
).

Weak Galerkin Algorithm 2
.

Find
(\lambda_{h},u_{h})\in\mathbb{R}\times V_{h}
such that
\|u_{h}\|_{b}=1
and

(5.1)

\displaystyle a_{w}(u_{h},v_{h})=\lambda_{h}b_{w}(u_{h},v_{h}),\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h},

where

\displaystyle a_{w}(v_{h},w_{h})=(\nabla_{w}v_{h},\nabla_{w}w_{h})+(v_{0},w_{0})+s(v_{h},w_{h}),

\displaystyle b_{w}(v_{h},w_{h})=\langle v_{b},w_{b}\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

\displaystyle s(v_{h},w_{h})=\frac{\alpha}{d+1}\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sum\limits_{e\in\mathcal{E}(T)}h_{T}^{-2}|e|^{-1}|T|\langle v_{0}-v_{b},w_{0}-w_{b}\rangle_{L^{2}(e)}.

Positive constants
\delta
and
\Lambda
are related to the maximal diameter
h_{\max}
in the shape-regular triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, then we have the following two fundamental estimates

(A)

\displaystyle\|(I-Q_{b})f\|^{2}_{X}\leq\delta\|(I-\mathbb{Q}_{h})\nabla f\|^{2},\quad\forall f\in H^{1}(\Omega),

(B)

\displaystyle s(Q_{h}f,Q_{h}f)\leq\alpha\Lambda\|(I-\mathbb{Q}_{h})\nabla f\|^{2},\quad\forall f\in H^{1}(\Omega).

Proof.

From
[
8
, Proposition 4.3]
, we have

\displaystyle\|(I-Q_{b})f\|_{X}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|(I-Q_{0})f\|_{X}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{1}\|\nabla(I-Q_{0})f\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{st}^{2}\delta_{1}\|(I-\mathbb{Q}_{h})\nabla f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\delta\|(I-\mathbb{Q}_{h})\nabla f\|^{2}.

It follows from
[
8
, Theorem 4.1]
that

s(Q_{h}f,Q_{h}f)\leq\alpha\Lambda\|(I-\mathbb{Q}_{h})\nabla f\|^{2}.

Then we complete the proof.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

In the above proof,
\Lambda,\,C_{st},\,\delta_{1}
are positive constants, and when
k=1
,
\Lambda,\,\delta_{1}
have explicit expressions. The details can be seen in
[
8
, Theorem 3.1, Proposition 4.3]
.

Let
\lambda
be the
j
-th exact eigenvalue satisfying (
2.2
),
\lambda_{h}
be the
j
-th discrete eigenvalue satisfying (
5.1
).

Theorem 20
.

(GLB). If
\delta
,
\Lambda
and
\alpha>0
satisfy either
(1)

\delta\lambda+\alpha\Lambda\leq 1
or
(2)

\delta\lambda_{h}+\alpha\Lambda\leq 1
, then
\lambda_{h}\leq\lambda.

Proof.

The proof of this theorem is similar to that of
[
8
, Theorem 5.1]
.

Step one.
If
\delta\lambda\geq 1
, the first condition (1) is impossible. So when the second condition (2) holds, we can obtain
\lambda_{h}\leq\lambda
. Therefore, for the remaining steps of this proof, we only consider case
\delta\lambda<1
.

Step two.
Let
\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{j}
be the first
j\in\mathbb{N}
exact eigenfunctions of the Steklov eigenvalue problem (
2.2
). Let
\lambda=\lambda_{j}
be the
j
-th exact eigenvalue. Next, we prove that
Q_{b}\phi_{1},\cdots,Q_{b}\phi_{j}
are linearly independent in
P_{k}(\mathcal{E}_{h})
. We assume that they are linearly dependent, then suppose that
\psi\in{\rm{span}}\{\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{j}\}
satisfies
\|\psi\|_{X}=1
and
Q_{b}\psi=0
on
\partial\Omega
.
It follows from (
A
) that

\displaystyle 1=\|\psi\|_{X}^{2}=\|(I-Q_{b})\psi\|_{X}^{2}\leq\delta\|(I-\mathbb{Q}_{h})\nabla\psi\|^{2}.

According to the orthogonality of the projection operator, we have

(5.2)

\|{\psi}\|_{1}^{2}=\|\psi\|^{2}+\|\nabla\psi\|^{2}=\|\psi\|^{2}+\|\mathbb{Q}_{h}\nabla\psi\|^{2}+\|(I-\mathbb{Q}_{h})\nabla\psi\|^{2}.

Thus, we can obtain

1\leq\delta\|{\psi}\|_{1}^{2}.

On the other hand, it follows from the minimum-maximum principle on the exact eigenvalues that

\|{\psi}\|_{1}^{2}\leq\lambda\|\psi\|_{X}^{2}=\lambda.

From the above two inequalities, we have
1\leq\delta\lambda
.
This contradicts
\delta\lambda<1
.

Step three.
Step two proves that
Q_{b}\phi_{1},\cdots,Q_{b}\phi_{j}
are linearly independent in
P_{k}(\mathcal{E}_{h})
. So
U_{h}
=span
\{Q_{h}\phi_{1},\cdots,Q_{h}\phi_{j}\}
is a linear subspace of
V_{h}
with dimension
j
. Let
\mathcal{U}(j)
denote the set of
all subspaces of
V_{h}
with dimension
j
. So the minimum-maximum principle characterizes the
j
-th discrete eigenvalue
\lambda_{h}
as

(5.3)

\lambda_{h}=\mathop{\min}\limits_{U\in\mathcal{U}(j)}\mathop{\max}\limits_{v_{h}\in U\setminus\{0\}}\frac{a_{w}(v_{h},v_{h})}{b_{w}(v_{h},v_{h})}.

Therefore,
\lambda_{h}
is a lower bound for the maximal quotient
a_{h}(v_{h},v_{h})/b_{h}(v_{h},v_{h})
among all nonzero
v_{h}\in U_{h}
. In the finite dimensional space, the maximum can be obtained, so there exists
\phi\in
span
\{\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{j}\}
that satisfies
\|\phi\|_{X}=1
and

(5.4)

\lambda_{h}b_{w}(Q_{h}\phi,Q_{h}\phi)\leq a_{w}(Q_{h}\phi,Q_{h}\phi).

In addition, it follows from the minimum-maximum principle on the exact eigenvalues that
\|\phi\|_{1}^{2}\leq\lambda
.

Step four.
We estimate the lower bound of
b_{w}(Q_{h}\phi,Q_{h}\phi)
. Recall that
\|\phi\|_{X}=1
, we have

b_{w}(Q_{h}\phi,Q_{h}\phi)=b_{w}(Q_{b}\phi,Q_{b}\phi)=\|Q_{b}\phi\|_{X}^{2}=1-\|(I-Q_{b})\phi\|_{X}^{2}.

Combining the above equation with (
A
), we arrive at

(5.5)

1-\delta\|(I-\mathbb{Q}_{h})\nabla\phi\|^{2}\leq b_{w}(Q_{h}\phi,Q_{h}\phi).

Step five.
We estimate the upper bound of
a_{w}(Q_{h}\phi,Q_{h}\phi)
.
It follows from Lemma
5
that
\mathbb{Q}_{h}\nabla\phi=\nabla_{w}Q_{h}\phi
. This leads to

(5.6)

a_{w}(Q_{h}\phi,Q_{h}\phi)=\|\mathbb{Q}_{h}\nabla\phi\|^{2}+\|Q_{0}\phi\|^{2}+s(Q_{h}\phi,Q_{h}\phi).

It follows from (
5.6
), (
B
), (
5.2
) and
\|\phi\|_{1}^{2}\leq 1
that

(5.7)

a_{w}(Q_{h}\phi,Q_{h}\phi)+\|(I-\mathbb{Q}_{h})\nabla\phi\|^{2}\leq\lambda+\alpha\Lambda\|(I-\mathbb{Q}_{h})\nabla\phi\|^{2}.

Step six.
Combining (
5.4
) with (
5.5
) and (
5.7
), we have

(5.8)

(1-\alpha\Lambda-\delta\lambda_{h})\|(I-\mathbb{Q}_{h})\nabla\phi\|^{2}\leq\lambda-\lambda_{h}.

In case (2), the left-hand side of (
5.8
) is nonnegative. This shows that
\lambda-\lambda_{h}\geq 0
. In case (1), from (
5.8
), we can obtain

(5.9)

\delta(\lambda-\lambda_{h})\|(I-\mathbb{Q}_{h})\nabla\phi\|^{2}\leq\lambda-\lambda_{h}.

Combining (
5.2
) with
\|\phi\|_{1}^{2}\leq\lambda
, we have
\delta\|(I-\mathbb{Q}_{h})\nabla\phi\|^{2}\leq\delta\lambda<1
. This shows that
\lambda-\lambda_{h}
must be nonnegative.
Then we complete the proof.
∎

Abstract
The Weighted ℱ \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\mathcal{F} - Vertex Deletion for a class ℱ \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\mathcal{F} of graphs asks, weighted graph G \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G , for a minimum weight vertex set S \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}S such that G − S ∈ ℱ . \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G-S\in\mathcal{F}. The case when ℱ \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\mathcal{F} is minor-closed and excludes some graph as a minor has received particular attention but a constant-factor approximation remained elusive for Weighted ℱ \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\mathcal{F} - Vertex Deletion . Only three cases of minor-closed ℱ \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\mathcal{F} are known to admit constant-factor approximations, namely Vertex Cover , Feedback Vertex Set and Diamond Hitting Set . We study the problem for the class ℱ \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\mathcal{F} of θ c \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\theta_{c} -minor-free graphs, under the equivalent setting of the Weighted c \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c -Bond Cover problem, and present a constant-factor approximation algorithm using the primal-dual method. For this, we leverage a structure theorem implicit in [ Joret, Paul, Sau, Saurabh, and Thomassé, SIDMA’14 ] which states the following: any graph G \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G containing a θ c \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\theta_{c} -minor-model either contains a large two-terminal protrusion , or contains a constant-size θ c \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\theta_{c} -minor-model, or a collection of pairwise disjoint constant-sized connected sets that can be contracted simultaneously to yield a dense graph. In the first case, we tame the graph by replacing the protrusion with a special-purpose weighted gadget. For the second and third case, we provide a weighting scheme which guarantees a local approximation ratio. Besides making an important step in the quest of (dis)proving a constant-factor approximation for Weighted ℱ \color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\mathcal{F} - Vertex Deletion , our result may be useful as a template for algorithms for other minor-closed families.

In both cases, we convert the current weighted graph

Basic definitions and preliminary results

We use
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\mathbb{N}
for the set of non-negative integers and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
for the set of non-negative reals.
Given a set
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X
and two functions
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}w_{1},w_{2}:X\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},
we denote by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}w_{1}\pm w_{2}:X\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
the function where for each
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}x\in X,

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}(w_{1}\pm w_{2})(x)=w_{1}(x)\pm w_{2}(x).

Given some
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}r\in\mathbb{N},
we define
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}[r]=\{1,\ldots,r\}.

Given some collection
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\cal A}
of objects on which the union operation can be defined,
we define
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\bigcup}}{\cal A}=\bigcup_{A\in{\cal A}}A.

All graphs we consider are multigraphs without loops.
We denote a graph by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G=(V,E)
where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}V
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}E
are its vertex and edge set respectively. When we deal with vertex-weighted graphs, we denote them by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G=(V,E,w)
where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}w:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and we say that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
is a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}w
-weighted graph.
When considering edge contractions we sum up edge multiplicities of multiple edges that are created during the contraction. However, when a loop appears after a contraction, then we suppress it.
We use
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}V(G)
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}E(G)
for the vertex set and the edge multiset of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G.
We also refer to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}|V(G)|
as the
size
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G.

If
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X\subseteq V(G),
we denote by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G[X]
the subgraph of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
induced by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X
and by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G-X
the graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G[V(G)\setminus X].

We say that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X
is
connected in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G[X]
is connected.
A graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
is a
minor
of a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
can be obtained from a subgraph of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
after contracting edges.
Given a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H,
we say that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
is
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
-minor free
if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
does not contain
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
as a minor.

Given an edge
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}e
of a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G,
we denote by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}m(e)
its multipliciy and we define
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}μ(G)=\max\{m(e)\mid e\in E(G)\}.

Given a vertex
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}v\in V(G),
we define the edge-degree of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}v
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G,
denoted by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\sf edeg}_{G}(v),
as the number of edges of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
that are incident to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}v.
We denote by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}N_{G}(v)
the set of all neighbors of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}v
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
and
we call
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}|N_{G}(v)|

vertex-degree
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}v
.
We denote by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}δ(G)
the minimum edge-degree of a vertex in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G.

Bonds and pumpkins.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
be a graph.
Given two disjoint subsets
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X,

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}Y
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}V(G),

the
edges crossing

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}Y
is the set of edges with one endpoint in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X
and the other in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}Y.
The graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}θ_{c}
is the graph on two vertices joined by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
parallel edges (
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}θ_{c}
is also known as the
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-pumpkin
).
Notice that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}θ_{c}
is a minor of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
iff

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
contains two disjoint connected sets
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}Y

crossed by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
edges of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G.
We call the union
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}M:=X\cup Y

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}θ_{c}
-model
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G.

Given a bipartition
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\{V_{1},V_{2}\}
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}V(G),
the set of edges crossing
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}V_{1}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}V_{2}
is called the
cut
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\{V_{1},V_{2}\}
and an edge set
is a
cut
if it is a cut of some vertex bipartition. For disjoint vertex subsets
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}S,T\subseteq V,
an
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}(S,T)
-cut
is
a cut of a bipartition
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\{V_{1},V_{2}\}
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}S\subseteq V_{1}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}T\subseteq V_{2}.
Note that, for a connected graph, cuts are precisely the edge sets whose deletion strictly increases the number of connected components.
A
block
of a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
is either an isolated vertex (a vertex of vertex-degree 0) or a bridge or a biconnected component of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G.

A minimal non-empty cut is known as a
bond
in the literature. We remark that the bonds of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
are precisely the circuits of the cographic matroid of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G.

Given a positive integer
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c,
a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-bond
of a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
is any minimal cut of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
of size at least
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c.

Notice that if a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}(X,Y)
-cut of a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
is a bond and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X\cup Y=V(G),
then both
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}Y
are connected sets of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G.

The problem of finding the maximum
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
for which a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
contains a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-bond has been examined both from the approximation
[
28
,
18
]
and the parameterized point of view
[
20
]
.
We next see how a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-bond is related to a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\theta_{c}
-model.

Observation 3
.

For every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c\in\mathbb{N}
, a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
contains
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\theta_{c}
as a minor iff it has a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-bond.

Proof.

Consider a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\theta_{c}
-model
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}M=X\cup Y
where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}Y
are two disjoint connected vertex sets of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G,
with at least
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
edges crossing
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}Y.

For every vertex
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}v
in the same connected component
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}C
as
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X\cup Y,
add
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}v
to either of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X
or
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}Y
in which
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}v
has a neighbor.
Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X^{\prime}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}Y^{\prime}
be the resulting sets with
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X\subseteq X^{\prime}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}Y\subseteq Y^{\prime},
and let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}F
be the edge set crossing
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X^{\prime}
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}Y^{\prime}.

Clearly
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}F
is the cut of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}(X^{\prime},V\setminus X^{\prime}).

To show that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}F
is a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-bond, suppose that the cut
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}F^{\prime}
of a bipartition
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}(A,B)
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}V
is a subset of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}F.

If
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\emptyset\neq A\cap X^{\prime}\cap C\subsetneq X^{\prime}\cap C,

then the cut of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}(A,B)
contains all the edges crossing
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X^{\prime}\cap C\cap A
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}(X^{\prime}\cap C)\setminus A.

Since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X^{\prime}\cap C
is connected, there is at least one edge
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}e
crossing
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X^{\prime}\cap C\cap A
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}(X^{\prime}\cap C)\setminus A.
Because
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}e
is an edge whose both endpoints belong to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X^{\prime}\cap C,

it follows
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}e\in F^{\prime}\setminus F,
a contradiction. Therefore, either
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}A\cap X^{\prime}\cap C=\emptyset
or
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}A\cap X^{\prime}\cap C=X^{\prime}\cap C
holds, and likewise either
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}B\cap Y^{\prime}\cap C=\emptyset
or

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}B\cap Y^{\prime}\cap C=Y^{\prime}\cap C
holds. It follows that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}F^{\prime}
is either an empty set or equals
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}F,
thus proving that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}F
is a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-bond. The backward direction is straightforward
from that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}F
takes all its edges from a single connected component of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G.

Treewidth.

A
tree decomposition
of a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
is a pair
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}(T,\chi),
where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}T
is a tree
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\chi:V(T)\rightarrow 2^{V(G)}

such that:

1.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\bigcup_{q\in V(T)}\chi(q)=V(G),

2.

for every edge
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\{u,v\}\in E,
there is a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}q\in V(T)
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\{u,v\}\subseteq\chi(q),
and

3.

for every vertex
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}v\in V(G),
the set
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\{t\in V(T)\mid v\in\chi(t)\}
is connected in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}T.

The
width
of a tree decomposition
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}(T,\chi)
is
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\max\{|\chi(q)|\mid{q\in V(T)}\}-1.
The treewidth of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
is the minimum width over all tree decompositions of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G.

We need the following two observations. Observations (i) and (ii) follow from the combinatorial results of
[
38
]
and
[
16
]
respectively.

Proposition 4
.

For every positive integer
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
and every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}θ_{c}
-minor free multigraph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G,
it holds that (i)
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}|E(G)|\leq 2c\cdot|V(G)|
and (ii)
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G

has treewidth less than
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}2c.

Cluster collections.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
be a graph.
A
cluster collection
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G

is a non-empty collection
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\cal C}=\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{r}\}
of pairwise disjoint non-empty connected subsets of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}V(G).

In case
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\bigcup}}{\cal C}=V(G)
we say that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\cal C}
is a
cluster partition
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G.

The
capacity
of a cluster collection
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\cal C}
is the maximum number of vertices of a cluster in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\cal C}.

We use the notation
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G/{\cal C}
for the multigraph obtained from
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G[\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\bigcup}}{\cal C}]
by contracting all edges in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G[C_{i}]
for each
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}.

Given a cluster

.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}Z

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}μ(Z)<c
and let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\{A,B\}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}V(Z)

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}Z

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}k+1

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}k/c
in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G/{\cal C}.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}a\in A
and let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}b\in N_{G}(a)\cap B.
Observe that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
has less than
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}a

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}b

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}A\setminus\{a\}.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}B_{a}
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}N_{G}(a)\cap B

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\{a\}\cup B_{a}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}A\setminus a.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}B_{a}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}a\in A

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}k/c

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}a\cup B_{a}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\bigcup}}{\cal C}\setminus(\{a\}\cup B_{a}),

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\cal C}:=\{a\cup B_{a}\mid a\in A\}.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}B_{a}=\emptyset

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}a\in A.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}a\in A

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}k/c

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}a\cup B_{a}

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\bigcup}}{\cal C}\setminus(\{a\}\cup B_{a}).

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}p

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}a

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{\bigcup}}{\cal C}\setminus(\{a\}\cup B_{a}).

The weighting scheme

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
be a graph and let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\cal C}
be a cluster partition of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
of capacity at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}r.
Let also
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
be an instance of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-Bond Cover
for some positive integer
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c.

We define the vertex weighting function

so that if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}v\in C\in\mathcal{C},
then

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\displaystyle w_{\cal C}(v)

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\displaystyle=

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\displaystyle\frac{|{\sf ext}(C)|}{|C|}.

(1)

When

is clear from the context, we simply write
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}w
instead
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}w_{\cal C}.

The main result of this section is that, with respect to the weight function
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}w
in

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c

Lemma 10
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
be a non negative integer,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
be a graph,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}r
be a positive integer,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\cal C}
be a connected partition of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
of capacity at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}r
and such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\delta(G/{\cal C})\geq 8c,
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}w:V(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a vertex weighting function as in
Equation 1
. Then
for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-bond covering
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G,
it holds that

\frac{1}{2r}\cdot|E(G/\mathcal{C})|\leq\sum_{v\in X}w(v)\leq 2\cdot|E(G/\mathcal{C})|.

Proof.

For the upper bound, note that

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\sum_{v\in X}w(v)=\sum_{v\in X}\frac{|{\sf ext}(C)|}{|C|}\leq\sum_{C\in{\cal C}}\sum_{v\in C}\frac{|{\sf ext}(C)|}{|C|}=\sum_{C\in{\cal C}}|{\sf ext}(C)|=\sum_{x\in V(G/\mathcal{C})}{\sf edeg}_{G/\mathcal{C}}(x)=2\cdot|E(G/\mathcal{C})|.

For the lower bound, let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X
be a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-bond covering of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
and let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}F=V(G)\setminus X,

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\cal C}_{X}=\{C\in{\cal C}\mid C\cap X\neq\emptyset\},
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\cal C}_{F}={\cal C}\setminus{\cal C}_{X}.

Since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\delta(G/\mathcal{C})\geq 8c,
we obtain that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}|E(G/\mathcal{C})|/2\geq 2c\cdot|V(G/\mathcal{C})|=2c\cdot|{\cal C}|.

We claim that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{X}}|{\sf ext}(C)|\geq|E(G/\mathcal{C})|/2.
Indeed, if this is not the case then, by the fact that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}|E(G/\mathcal{C})|\leq|E(G[F]/\mathcal{C}_{F})|+\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{X}}|{\sf ext}(C)|,

we have that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}|E(G[F]/\mathcal{C}_{F})|>|E(G/\mathcal{C})|/2\geq 2c\cdot|\mathcal{C}|\geq 2c\cdot|\mathcal{C}_{F}|
and this last inequality, from
Section 2
.i, gives that

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}θ_{c}
is a minor of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G/\mathcal{C}_{F}
which is a minor of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G[F],
a contradiction.
Therefore, since each set in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\cal C}_{X}
contains at least one vertex of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X,

\sum_{v\in X}w(v)\geq\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{X}}\frac{|\mathsf{ext}(C)|}{|C|}\geq\frac{1}{r}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}_{X}}|\mathsf{ext}(C)|\geq\frac{|E(G/\mathcal{C})|}{2r},

which proves the lower bound.
∎

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}(S\setminus V(K))\cup\{x_{p}\}
is a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-bond cover of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G^{\prime}.

Lastly, observe that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}K^{(u,v)}_{p}
is a minor of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G[X\cup Y\cup V(K)],
and
because
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}S\cap V(K)
is a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-bond cover of the latter, it is also a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-bond cover of the former.
Therefore, we have
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}w(S\cap V(K))\geq w_{p},
from which we have
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\mathsf{opt}(G)=w(S)\geq w(S\setminus V(K))+w_{p}=w((S\setminus V(K))\cup\{x_{p}\})\geq\mathsf{opt}(G^{\prime}).

This finishes the proof.
∎

6
The primal-dual approach

We begin the section by formalizing the notions of replacer and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\alpha
-thin layer.
A
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-outgrowth replacer
(hereinafter
replacer
) is a polynomial-time algorithm which, given a weighted graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G=(V,E,w)

and a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-outgrowth
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\bf K}=(K,u,v)
of size at least
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c,
outputs a weighted graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime},w^{\prime})
with the following property.

1.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\bf K}
is replaced by another
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-outgrowth
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\bf K^{\prime}}=(K^{\prime},u,v)
of size at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c-1.

2.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\mathsf{opt}(G)=\mathsf{opt}({G^{\prime}}).

3.

Given a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-bond cover
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}S^{\prime}\subseteq V(G^{\prime}),
one can construct in polynomial time a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-bond cover
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}S\subseteq V(G)
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}w(S)\leq w(S^{\prime}).

An
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\alpha
-thin layer
of a weighted graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G=(V,E,w)
is a weighted graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H=(V,E,w^{o})
such that the following holds.

•

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}w^{o}(v)\leq w(v)
for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}v\in V,

•

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}w^{o}(v)=w(v)
for some
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}v\in V,
and

•

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}w^{o}(S)\geq(1/\alpha)\cdot w^{o}(V)
for any
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-bond cover
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}S\subseteq V
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H.

We are now ready to prove our main approximation result.

Theorem
1
.

There is a uniformly polynomial-time algorithm which, given a positive integer
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
and a weighted graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G=(V,E,w),

computes a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-bond cover of weight at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\alpha\cdot\mathsf{opt}(G)
for some
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\alpha=\alpha(c).

7
Discussion

In this paper we construct a polynomial constant-factor approximation algorithm for the
Weighted

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\mathcal{F}
-
Vertex Deletion
problem in the case

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\cal F}
is the class of graphs not containing a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-bond or, alternatively, the
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}θ_{c}
-minor free graphs. The constant-factor of our approximation algorithm is a (constructible) function of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
and the running time is uniformly polynomial, that is it runs in time
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}O_{c}(n^{{\cal O}(1)}).
Our results, in case
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c=2,
yield a constant-factor approximation for the
Vertex Weighted Feedback Set
. Also, a
constant-factor approximation for
Diamond Hitting Set
can easily be derived for the case
where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c=3.
For this we apply our results on simple graphs and observe that each time a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}θ_{3}
-minor-model appears, this model, under the absence of multiple edges, should contain
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}4
vertices and therefore is a minor-model of the diamond
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}K_{4}^{-}
(that is
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}K_{4}
without an edge).

Certainly the general open question is whether
Weighted

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\mathcal{F}
-
Vertex Deletion
admits a constant-factor approximation for more general instantiations of the minor-closed class
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\cal F}.
In this direction, the challenge is to
use our approach when the graphs in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\mathcal{F}
have bounded treewidth (or, equivalently, if the minor obstruction of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\cal F}
contains some planar graph). For this, one needs to extend the structural
result of
Section 1
and, based on this to build a replacer as in
Section 5
.

Given an
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}r\in\mathbb{N},
an
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}r
-protrustion
of a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
is a set
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X\subseteq V(G)
such that

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G[X]
has treewidth at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}t
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}|\partial_{G}(X)|\leq t,
were
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\partial_{G}(X)
is the set of vertices of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X
that are incident to edges not in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G[X].
We conjecture that a possible extension of
Section 1

might be the following.

Conjecture 15
.

There are functions
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}f_{3}:\mathbb{N}^{2}\to\mathbb{N}
and

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}f_{4}:\mathbb{N}^{3}\to\mathbb{N}

such that, for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}h
-vertex planar graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
and every two positive integers
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}t,p,
there is a uniformly polynomial time algorithm that, given as input a graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G,
outputs one of the following:

1.

an
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\hyperref@@ii[label_cristianesmo]{f_{\ref*{label_cristianesmo}}}(h,t)
-protrusion
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X
of size at least
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}p,

or

2.

a minor-model of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
of size at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\hyperref@@ii[label_inconsistent]{f_{\ref*{label_inconsistent}}}(h,t,p),
or

3.

a cluster collection
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\cal C}
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
of capacity at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\hyperref@@ii[label_inconsistent]{f_{\ref*{label_inconsistent}}}(h,t,p)
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}δ(G/{\cal C})\geq t,

Given a proof of some suitable version of
Section 7
at hand, cases 1,2, and 3 above can be treated using the method proposed in this paper. In the first case, we need to find a
weighted prorusion replacer
that can replace, in the weighed graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G=(V,E,w),
the subgraph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G[X]
by another one (glued on the same boundary) and create a new weighted graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime},w^{\prime})
so that an optimal solution has the same weight in both instances. In our case, the role of a protrusion is played by the
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-outgrowth, where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X
is the vertex set of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}K^{(u,v)}
that has treewidth at most
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}2c
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}|\partial_{G}(X)|\leq 2,
i.e.,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}V(K^{(u,v)})
is a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}2c
-protrusion of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G.
In the case of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}θ_{c},
the replacer is given in
Section 5
. The existence of such a replacer in the general case
is wide open, first because the boundary
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\partial_{G}(X)
has bigger size (depending on
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}h
but perhaps also on
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}t
) and second, and most important, because we now must deal with
weights
which does not permit us to use any protrusion replacement machinery such as the one used in
[
23
,
22
]

unweighted version of the problem (based on the, so called,
FII
-property
[
15
]
for more details).

We believe that a possible way to prove
Section 7
is to use as departure the proof of the main combinatorial result in
[
48
]
.
However, in our opinion, the most challenging
step is to design a weighted protrusion replacer (or, on the negative side, to provide instantiations of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
where such a replacer does not exist). As such a replacer needs to work on the presence of weights, we suggest that its design might use techniques related to mimicking networks technology
(see
[
27
,
36
]
).

Finally, since our algorithm is based on the primal-dual framework and proceeds by constructing suitable weights for the second and third case where every feasible solution is
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\cal O}(1)
-approximate,
one can ask whether it is possible to
bypass
the need for a replacer and construct suitable weights for the first case.
Indeed, the previous approximation algorithms for
Weighted Feedback Vertex Set

[
7
,
13
,
19
]
designed suitable weights even for the case 1
where every
minimal
solution is
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\cal O}(1)
-approximate.
(And used the additional “reverse delete” step at the end to ensure that the final solution remains minimal, for every weighted graph constructed.)
In Appendix
B
, we show that such weights
cannot exist
for a simple planar graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H,
which suggests that replacers are inherently needed for this class of algorithms for
Weighted

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\mathcal{F}
-
Vertex Deletion
.

Appendix A
The case of big minimum edge-degree

It is known that if
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\cal F}
is a non-trivial minor-closed graph class, then there is some
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}d_{\cal F}
such that for every (multi) graph in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\cal F}
it holds that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}|E(G)|\leq μ(G)\cdot d_{\cal F}\cdot|V(G)|
(see e.g.
[
47
]
).

Lemma 16
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
be an instance of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\mathcal{F}
-
Vertex Deletion
where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}μ(G)\leq m
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\delta(G)\geq 3m\cdot d_{\cal F}.
Then for every solution
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\mathcal{F}
-
Vertex Deletion
on
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G,
it holds that

|E(G)|\leq 2\sum_{v\in X}{\sf edeg}_{G}(v)\leq 4|E(G)|.

Proof.

We prove the first inequality as the second one is obvious.
Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}F:=G-X
,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}d:=d_{\cal F},
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}b:=\delta(G)\geq 3md.
Observe that

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\displaystyle|E(X,F)|

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in F}{\sf edeg}_{G}(v)-\sum_{v\in F}{\sf edeg}_{F}(v)

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\displaystyle\geq b\cdot|V(F)|-2|E(F)|

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\displaystyle\geq b\cdot|V(F)|-2md\cdot|V(F)|

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\displaystyle\because|E(F)|\leq md\cdot|V(F)|

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\displaystyle\geq md\cdot|V(F)|

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\displaystyle\because b\geq 3md

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\displaystyle\geq|E(F)|

Therefore, we deduce

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\displaystyle\sum_{v\in X}{\sf edeg}_{G}(v)

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\displaystyle=2|E(X)|+|E(X,F)|

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|E(X)|+\frac{1}{2}|E(X,F)|+\frac{1}{2}|E(X,F)|

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\big(|E(X)|+|E(X,F)|+|E(F)|\big)

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\cdot|E(G)|

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\cdot\sum_{v\in V(G)}{\sf edeg}_{G}(v)

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|E(G)|

as claimed.
∎

In the special case where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\cal F}
is the class of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}θ_{c}
-free graphs, we know, from
Section 2
.i, that every simple
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}θ_{c}
-minor free graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
satisfies
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}E(G)\leq 2c\cdot|V(G)|.
As for such graphs
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}μ(G)<c,
we can apply the above lemma for
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}d_{\cal F}=2.

Therefore, given an instance
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
of (unweighted)
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-Bond Cover
where
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\delta(G)\geq 6c,

every feasible solution
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X
is
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}4
-approximation.
This implies that if we set
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}w(v):={\sf edeg}_{G}(v)
for every
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}v\in V,
every feasible solution to
Weighted
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}c
-Bond Cover
is a 4-approximate solution.

Appendix B
Necessity of a replacer

The previous approximation algorithms for
Weighted Feedback Vertex Set
, at least the ones stated in
[
7
,
13
,
19
]
, did not use the notion of a
replacer
that we introduce here.
Instead, these algorithms use the primal-dual framework by
always
finding suitable weightings
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}w:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
of the current graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
such that
any minimal
feedback vertex set (i.e., cycle cover) for
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
is a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}2
-approximate solution with respect to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}w.
(In this section, minimality is defined with respect to set inclusion.)
The existence of such a weighting
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}w
relies on the fact that, for any induced path
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}P
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G,
any minimal feedback vertex set has at most one internal vertex from
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}P.

We prove the following lemma revealing that such weights cannot exist for
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
-minor cover for some planar graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H.
(In fact, every graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
that satisfies the mild technical conditions (1) and (2) in the proof.)

Lemma 17
.

There exists a fixed planar graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
such that the following holds.
For infinitely many values of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}n,
there exists an
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}n
-vertex graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
such that
for any weighting
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}w:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},
there exists a minimal
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
-minor cover
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}S\subseteq V(G)
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}w(S)=\Omega(n)\cdot\mathsf{opt}(G).

Proof.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
be any 2-connected planar graph with treewidth at least five (for this, one may just take the
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}(5\times 5)
-grid).
Choose
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}s,t\in V(H)
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\{s,t\}\in E(H).

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}k
be any parameter bigger than five. The graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
is constructed as follows.

•

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}R=\{0,1,2,3\}\times[k]
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}V(G)=V(H)\cup R.
So
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}n=4k+|V(H)|.

•

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}E(G)
consists of the following edges (
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\oplus
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\ominus
denotes the addition and subtraction modulo 4 respectively).

–

the edges in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}E(H)\setminus\{\{s,t\}\}.

–

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}s
is adjacent to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}(i,1)
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}t
is adjacent to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}(i,k)
for each
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}i\in\{0,1,2,3\}.

–

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G[R]
is the
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}(4\times k)
-grid. Formally, for
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}1\leq j\leq k
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}0\leq i<3,

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}E(G)
contains the edge
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\{(i,j),(i\oplus 1,j)\}
. Moreover, for
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}1\leq j<k
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}0\leq i<3,

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}E(G)
contains the edges
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\{(i,j),(i,j+1)\}
.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}Z:=G[\{s,t\}\cup R].
We need the following two properties of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
(we remark that the lemma is true for any
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
that satisfies them):

(1)

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
is
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}2
-vertex connected and

(2)

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
is not a minor of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}Z.

For the above, it is easy to verify that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}Z=G[\{s,t\}\cup R]

has treewidth at most four, therefore it cannot contain as a minor the graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
that has treewidth at least five.

For
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}1\leq j\leq k,
let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}Y_{j}=\{(0,j),(1,j),(2,j),(3,j)\}
and notice that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}Y_{i}
is a
minimal
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}s
-
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}t
separator
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}R
(that is
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}s
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}t
are in different connected components of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}R-S
and none of its subsets has this property). In the proof of the following claim, by

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
-
minor-model
we mean a cluster collection
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\cal M}=\{M_{v}\mid v\in V(H)\}

of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G/{\cal M}
is equal to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
with possibly some additional edges.

Claim 18
.

If
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}S
is a minimal
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}s
-
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}t
separator of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}Z
, then

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}S
is a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
-minor cover of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G.

Proof of Claim.

Once
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}S
is removed from
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G,
there is no
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}s
-
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}t
path in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}Z.

Let
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}A\subseteq R
be the vertices connected to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}s
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}B\subseteq R
be the vertices connected to
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}t
(so
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}(A,B,S)
is a partition of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}R
).
Assume towards contradiction that there exists a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
-minor-model
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}{\cal M}=\{M_{v}\mid v\in V(H)\}
of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G^{\prime}=G-S.

We consider the following cases.

–

If there exists some
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}v\in V(H)
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}M_{v}\subseteq A,
the 2-vertex-connectivity of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
ensures that there is no
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}w\in V(H)
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}M_{w}\subseteq V(G-S)\setminus(A\cup\{s\}),

since
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}s
is a singleton vertex cut separating
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}A
and
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}V(G-S)\setminus(A\cup\{s\}).
By letting
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}M_{v}\leftarrow M_{v}\cap(A\cup\{s\})
for each
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}v\in V(H),
this implies that there exists a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
-model contained in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}A\cup\{s\},
which contradicts the fact
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
is not a minor of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G[\{s\}\cup R].
Similarly, there is no
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}v\in V(H)
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}M_{v}\subseteq B.

–

Then there is no
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}v\in V(H)
such that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}M_{v}\subseteq A
or
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}M_{v}\subseteq B.
In that case, letting
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}M_{v}\leftarrow M_{v}\setminus(A\cup B)
for each
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}v\in V(H)
gives a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
-model of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G-S
entirely contained in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}V(G-S)\setminus(A\cup B).
But note that

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G-(S\cup A\cup B)=G-R
is exactly
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
minus one edge, so contradiction.

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\blacklozenge

On the other hand, we define, for
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}0\leq i<3,
the sets

X_{i}=(\{i\ominus 1\}\times\{2,\dots,k-1\})\cup(\{i\oplus 1\}\times\{2,\dots,k-1\})\cup\{(i,k),(i\oplus 2,1)\}.

By construction, in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}Z,
each
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X_{i}
is a minimal
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}s
-
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}t
separator.
Therefore,
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X_{i}
is a minimal
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
-minor-cover of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G.

For any
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}w:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},
note that
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\sum_{j=1}^{k}w(Y_{j})\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{3}w(X_{i}).
Indeed, for each
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}(i,j)\in R,

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}(i,j)
appears at least twice in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\{X_{0},X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}\}
and exactly once in
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\{Y_{j}:1\leq j\leq k\}.

Since each
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}Y_{j}
is a
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
-minor cover, it holds that

k\cdot\mathsf{opt}^{(w)}(G)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}w(Y_{j})\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in\{0,1,2,3\}}w(X_{i}).

(Here we denote by
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\mathsf{opt}^{(w)}(G)
the optimum value attained with respect to the weighting
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}w.
)
Therefore, for some
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}i,

\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}X_{i}
is a minimal
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
-cover whose weight is at least
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}\frac{k}{2}\cdot\mathsf{opt}^{(w)}(G).
As
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}|V(G)|=25+4k,
the
lemma follows.
∎

We stress that in the above proof, the treewidth of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
is always equal to five (that is the treewidth of
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}H
). This means that one may not expect a better behavior than the one testified by
Appendix B
, even when restricted to graphs of fixed treewidth. Notice also that the graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G
is not planar (while the graph
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}Z
is). Any construction of a planar
\color[rgb]{0.1,0.1,0.28}G,
as in
Appendix B
, seems to be an interesting challenge.

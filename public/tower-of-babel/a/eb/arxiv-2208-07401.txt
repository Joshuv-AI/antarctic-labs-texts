Algebraic Hyperbolicity of Complements of Generic Hypersurfaces in Projective Spaces
Xi Chen, Eric Riedl, Wern Yeong

Abstract
. We study the algebraic hyperbolicity of the complement of very general degree 2 ​ n 2n hypersurfaces in ℙ n {{\mathbb{P}}}^{n} . We prove the Algebraic Green-Griffiths-Lang Conjecture for these complements, and in the case of the complement of a quartic plane curve, we completely characterize the exceptional locus as the union of the flex and bitangent lines.

1.
Introduction

The relationship between the canonical bundle of a smooth variety
X
and the holomorphic maps from
\mathbb{C}
to
X
is a fundamental driving theme in the study of these holomorphic maps. A major conjecture in this area is the Green-Griffiths-Lang Conjecture, which we state in the logarithmic form that is the focus of this paper.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and
D
a simple normal crossings divisor such that
K_{X}+D
is big. Then there is some proper subvariety
S\subsetneq X
containing the images of all nonconstant holomorphic maps
\mathbb{C}\to X\setminus D
, i.e.
X\setminus D
is pseudo Brody hyperbolic.

As it stands, a full proof of Conjecture
1.1
appears far out of reach. In
[
Che04
]
, the author introduced the following algebraic version of hyperbolicity for log varieties, which is a natural extension of Demailly’s definition for projective varieties
[
Dem97
]
. Given a smooth projective variety
X
and a simple normal crossings divisor
D
, we say that the log pair
(X,D)
is
algebraically hyperbolic
if there exists an
\epsilon>0
such that every map
f:C\to X
from a smooth projective curve
C
that is birational onto its image and
f(C)\not\subset D
satisfies
2g(C)-2+|f^{-1}(D)|>\epsilon\deg f^{*}L
, where
L
is an ample line bundle on
X
and
g(C)
is the geometric genus of
C
.
The following weaker version of Conjecture
1.1
with Brody hyperbolicity replaced by algebraic hyperbolicity has turned out to be more tractable.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and
D
be a simple normal crossings divisor such that
K_{X}+D
is big. Then there exists an
\epsilon>0
and a subvariety
S\subsetneq X
such that every map
f:C\to X
from a smooth projective curve
C
that is birational onto its image and
f(C)\not\subset D\cup S
satisfies

2g(C)-2+|f^{-1}(D)|>\epsilon\deg f^{*}L

where
L
is an ample line bundle on
X
and
g(C)
is the geometric genus of
C
.

This conjecture is closely related to some conjectures of Vojta
[
Voj99
]
, and is sometimes called the algebraic Lang-Vojta Conjecture for function fields
[
CZ08
]
, although neither Lang nor Vojta put their conjectures in this form. See
[
Jav20
]
for a survey on various notions of hyperbolicity. We prove Conjecture
1.2
for very general hypersurfaces in
{{\mathbb{P}}}^{n}
of degree
d=2n
and give a complete description of the exceptional locus
S
in the case
n=2
,
d=4
.

Theorem 1.3
.

For a very general hypersurface
D\subseteq{{\mathbb{P}}}^{n}
of degree
d=2n
, there is some proper exceptional locus
S\subsetneq{{\mathbb{P}}}^{n}
such that for every map
f:C\to{{\mathbb{P}}}^{n}
from a smooth projective curve
C
that is birational onto its image and
f(C)\not\subset D\cup S
, we have:

•

for
n\geq 3

(1.1)

2g(C)-2+|f^{-1}(D)|\geq\deg f^{*}L

•

for
n=2
,

(1.2)

2g(C)-2+|f^{-1}(D)|\geq\frac{1}{2}\deg f^{*}L

where

is the hyperplane bundle of
{{\mathbb{P}}}^{n}
.
In the case
n=2
,

is exactly the union of the bitangent and flex lines to

.

Zaidenberg
[

]
shows that for

, the set

cannot be empty, as there exist lines meeting

Theorem

proves a well-known conjecture of Lang about semi-abelian varieties in this case (Conjecture 2.7 in
[

).

Corollary 1.4
.

Any nonconstant map from a semiabelian variety to the complement of a very general quartic in
{{\mathbb{P}}}^{2}
must have image in the locus of flex and bitangent lines.

In fact, the following stronger statement follows from Corollary

combined with Lemmas 3.15 and 3.16 of

Corollary 1.5

Any nonconstant map from a connected algebraic group over
{{\mathbb{C}}}
to the complement of a very general quartic

{{\mathbb{P}}}^{2}
must have image in the locus
\Delta
of flex and bitangent lines, i.e.
{{\mathbb{P}}}^{2}\setminus D
is groupless modulo

2.
Notation and conventions

2.1.
Lines with fixed modulus

A key consideration in this paper is the modulus of intersection of a line with a given hypersurface. Recall that
\overline{M}_{0,d}
is the moduli space of stable rational curves with
d
marked points. It is a projective variety, and forms a fine moduli space for families of stable rational curves. After taking a quotient by the natural
S_{d}
action, we obtain the space of stable rational curves with
d
unordered marked points, which we call
\overline{M}_{0,d}^{\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits}
. Similarly, we have the space
\overline{M}_{0,d+1}
and can take its quotient by the
S_{d}
action on the first
d
points and get a space
\overline{U}_{0,d}^{\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits}
. The space
\overline{M}_{0,d}^{\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits}
is a coarse moduli space for families of stable rational curves with
d
marked, unordered points, and
\overline{U}_{0,d}^{\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits}
is the coarse moduli space for families of stable rational curves with one special marked point together with
d
other, unordered marked points.

Let
V\subset{{\mathbb{P}}}^{n}\times\mathbb{G}(1,n)
be the universal line in
{{\mathbb{P}}}^{n}
, and let
\pi
be the projection to
{{\mathbb{P}}}^{n}
and
\eta
the projection to
\mathbb{G}(1,n)
. Given a hypersurface

, we have a rational map
\theta:V\dashrightarrow\overline{U}_{0,d}^{\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits}
given by sending a pair
(p,\ell)
to the element
(D\cap\ell,p)
, where the
d
unordered points are given by
D\cap\ell
and the
(d+1)
st point is given by
p
.

This map will be particularly important in the case
d=4
,
n=2
, where we study the fibers of
\theta
in detail. In this case the map
\theta
is defined everywhere except for pairs
(p,\ell)
where
p\cup\ell\cap D
has a multiplicity three point along the line
\ell
, where it isn’t clear which stable curve corresponds to
\ell\cap D
. In particular, this means that
\theta
is undefined along the flex locus, or along tangent lines to
D
when the point
p
coincides with the point of tangency.

2.3.
Dominant families of curves

Our last result shows that dominant families of curves satisfy a strong form of algebraic hyperbolicity. Thus, the challenge in proving Conjecture
1.2
lies in showing that all curves violating the inequality are contained in some subvariety, not some countable union of subvarieties.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth variety and
D
be a smooth divisor in
X
and
f:C\to X
be the general element of a family of curves with fixed genus
g
and number of intersection points
i
with
D
, such that the family of curves dominates
X
. Then

2g-2+i\geq(K_{X}+D)\cdot f(C).

Proof.

Because
C
is a general element of a dominant families of curves in
X
, the log normal sheaf
N_{f/X}(\log D)
is generically globally generated, and hence has non-negative degree. The degree of the log normal sheaf is
2g-2+i-(K_{X}+D)\cdot f(C)
. The result follows.
∎

3.
A variational argument

In this section we study the curves that may violate algebraic hyperbolicity. We show that to every point in such a curve, there is an associated line
\ell
, and that such lines either intersect
D
with fixed modulus or intersect
D
in few points.
More precisely, we show the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
D\subset{{\mathbb{P}}}^{n}
be a very general hypersurface of degree
d=2n

C
in
{{\mathbb{P}}}^{n}
not contained in
D

(1)

\displaystyle 2g(C)-2+i(C,D)\geq\deg C\text{ and $n\geq 3$}

\displaystyle 2g(C)-2+i(C,D)\geq\frac{1}{2}\deg C\text{ and $n=2$}

(2)

\theta:V\dashrightarrow\overline{U}_{0,d}^{\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits}
.

3.3.

W
to the space of quartic plane curves is generically finite. To see this, note that given a fixed
(C,D)
in
W
with
D
smooth, the deformations of
C
that still meet
D
to order 6 at one point will be parameterized by
H^{0}(N_{C/{{\mathbb{P}}}^{2}}(\log D))
. A simple degree calculation shows that the degree of
N_{C/{{\mathbb{P}}}^{2}}(\log D)
is
-1
, and since
C
and

are both smooth, we see that the normal bundle will be a vector bundle. It follows that
H^{0}(N_{C/{{\mathbb{P}}}^{2}}(\log D))=0
, which completes the proof.

∎

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

We follow Demailly’s argument in the projective case.
Suppose that there is a nonconstant map
\alpha:A\to{{\mathbb{P}}}^{2}\setminus D
from a semiabelian variety
A
to
{{\mathbb{P}}}^{2}\setminus D
whose image does not lie in
S
. Let
[n]:A\to A
be the map sending
a\to na
by the group law and let
f:C\hookrightarrow A
be a curve in
A
cut out by
\dim A-1
generic very ample divisors.
Clearly,
h_{n}=\alpha\circ[n]\circ f:C\to{{\mathbb{P}}}^{2}\setminus D
is nonconstant for all
n
. Let
C\hookrightarrow\overline{C}
be the completion of
C
to a smooth projective curve and
\overline{h}_{n}:\overline{C}\to{{\mathbb{P}}}^{2}
be the completion of
h_{n}
. Since
\overline{h}_{n}^{-1}(D)\subset\overline{C}\setminus C
,

2g(\overline{C})-2+|\overline{C}\setminus C|\geq 2g(\overline{C})-2+|\overline{h}_{n}^{-1}(D)|

Since
\overline{h}_{n}(C)
does not lie in
S
, we have

2g(\overline{C})-2+|\overline{C}\setminus C|\geq 2g(\overline{C})-2+|\overline{h}_{n}^{-1}(D)|\geq\frac{1}{2}\deg\overline{h}_{n}^{*}L

for

1.3
. This is impossible since

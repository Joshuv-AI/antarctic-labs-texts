Conjecture

In this section, we shall introduce

A

the Jacobian conjecture.
We start by recalling several fundamental results
on Jacobian pairs.
Let
\deg(f)

Proposition 2.1
.

[

If
(f,g)
is a Jacobian pair with

\deg(g)>1

The following result is a consequence of

[

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
f,g\in\mathbb{C}[x,y]
and suppose that
the endomorphism

\pi:\mathcal{R}\longrightarrow\mathcal{R}
given by

\pi(x)=f

\pi(y)=g

Then either
\deg(f)|\deg(g)
or

\deg(g)|\deg(f)
.

The following result is due to Abhyanker
[
18
, Theorem 10.2.23 (3)
\leftrightarrow
(4)]

Theorem 2.3
.

The following statements are equivalent.

•

For any Jacobian pair
(f,g)
,
one has that either
\deg(f)|\deg(g)
or
\deg(g)|\deg(f)
.

•

For any Jacobian pair
(f,g)
, the Newton polygons

N^{0}(f)
and
N^{0}(g)
are triangles (where a line segment is considered
to be a triangle).

•

The Jacobian conjecture.

We now present a conjecture equivalent to the Jacobian conjecture.

Conjecture A
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
be relatively prime with
2\leq a<b
.
Suppose that
F,G\in\mathcal{R}
satisfy the following:

(1)

[F,G]\in\mathbb{C}
;

(2)

\{(0,0),(0,1),(1,0)\}\subseteq N^{0}(F)
, and
N^{0}(F)
is similar to
N^{0}(G)
with the origin as the center of similarity and with the ratio
\deg(F):\deg(G)=a:b
.

Then
[F,G]=0
.

Throughout the paper, we often use uppercase letters
F,G
to denote
polynomials satisfying conditions (1) and/or (2) of
Conjecture

, and
lowercase letters
f,g
to denote arbitrary polynomials.

Proposition 2.4
.

Conjecture

A
holds if and only if the Jacobian conjecture
holds.

Proof.

For “
\Rightarrow
”, assume that
Conjecture

A
is true and the Jacobian conjecture is false.
By Theorem
2.3
,
there exists a Jacobian pair
(f,g)
such that
\deg(f)\nmid\deg(g)
and
\deg(g)\nmid\deg(f)
.
In particular,
\deg(f)>1
,
\deg(g)>1
.
By
Proposition

2.1
,

N^{0}(f)
is similar to
N^{0}(g)
with the origin as the center of similarity
and a similarity ratio of
\deg(f):\deg(g)
.
If
\deg(f)/\deg(g)
or
\deg(g)/\deg(f)
is an integer,
then the first statement of
Theorem

2.3
holds.
Otherwise, without loss of generality we assume
\deg(f)/\deg(g)=a/b

for relatively prime positive integers

such that

. Since the degrees of both
f
and
g
are
greater than

[
18

implies that the Newton polygons of both

. So the first two conditions of

hold, and hence

Thus, we have shown that if
(f,g)
is a Jacobian pair,
the first statement of
Theorem

2.3
holds. Therefore,
by
Theorem

2.3
, the Jacobian conjecture holds.

For “
\Leftarrow
”, suppose that the Jacobian conjecture holds.
Suppose that
F,G
satisfy conditions (1), (2) of
Conjecture

A
.
If
[F,G]\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
, then
by
Theorem

2.3
, either
\deg(F)|\deg(G)
or
\deg(G)|\deg(F)
.
This contradicts condition (2) of
Conjecture

A
. So
[F,G]=0
.
∎

6.1.

Recall that for a polynomial

in one variable,
\deg f
is the highest of the degrees of
f

{\rm lowdeg\,}f
to be the lowest of the degrees of
f
’s monomials with non-zero coefficients. For example,

\deg(2x^{3}+4x^{5})=5
and
{\rm lowdeg\,}(2x^{3}+4x^{5})=3
.

Recall the definition of the (generalized) binomial coefficients:
for
c\in\mathbb{R}
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, denote the binomial coefficient

\binom{c}{i}=\frac{c(c-1)(c-2)\cdots(c-i+1)}{i!}
.
Let
A,B\in\mathbb{C}[s]
such that the constant term of
A
is nonzero. Then
A^{-1}
is well-defined in
\mathbb{C}[[s]]
.
For
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
c\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
ac\in\mathbb{Z}
, define the rational power expansion

(A^{a}+B)^{c}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}{c\choose i}A^{a(c-i)}B^{i}
.
(See §
10
for the definition of multinomial coefficients and a similar expansion.)

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{C}[s]
. Suppose that the constant term of
A
is nonzero,
\deg B<a({\rm lowdeg\,}B)
, and
\deg A<{\rm lowdeg\,}B
.
For each

x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathcal{R}
, we have the following expansion in the ring

(1+x_{1}t+\cdots+x_{n}t^{n})^{A}=\sum_{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}{A\choose v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}}x_{1}^{v_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{v_{n}}t^{v_{1}+2v_{2}+\cdots+nv_{n}}.

In general, for
x_{0},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathcal{R}
and for

a\in\mathbb{Z}
,

we have the following identity in the ring

(10.2)

(x_{0}+x_{1}t+\cdots+x_{n}t^{n})^{A}=\sum_{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}{A\choose v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}}x_{0}^{A-\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}}x_{1}^{v_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{v_{n}}t^{v_{1}+\cdots+nv_{n}}.

Lemma 10.5
.

Let
w=(u,v)\in W
.
Let
R
be any polynomial ring over
K
,
f\in R
be a
w
-homogeneous polynomial of degree
d_{f}>0
, and
g
be a nonzero
w
-homogeneous function of degree
d_{g}\in\mathbb{Z}
in the fractional field of
R
such that the Jacobian
[g,f]=0
.
Define
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
to be the largest integer such that
h=f^{1/r}
is a polynomial.
Then there exists a unique

so that
g=c\cdot h^{s}
, where
s=rd_{g}/d_{f}

Lemma 10.6
.

Suppose
F,
is a nonzero polynomial and
d=\deg(F_{+})
and
e=\deg(G_{+})
.
Write the homogeneous decompositions
F=\sum_{i=0}^{d}F_{i}
, and define

\widetilde{F}=F_{d}+F_{d-1}t+\cdots+F_{0}t^{d}
.
Then
[\widetilde{F},\widetilde{F}^{\frac{e-\gamma}{d}}]=0

whenever
\frac{e-\gamma}{d}=\ell\cdot\frac{1}{r}
for
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

There exists
h=x^{m/r}y^{n/r}+h_{1}(x,y)t+h_{2}(x,y)t^{2}+\cdots\in\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm}1,y^{\pm}1][[t]]
such that
\widetilde{F}=h^{r}
,
\widetilde{F}^{\frac{e-\gamma}{d}}=h^{\ell}
. Then

[\widetilde{F},\widetilde{F}^{\frac{e-\gamma}{d}}]=[h^{r},h^{\ell}]=\begin{vmatrix}rh^{r-1}h_{x}&rh^{r-1}h_{y}\\
\ell h^{\ell-1}h_{x}&\ell h^{\ell-1}h_{y}\end{vmatrix}=r\ell h^{r+\ell-2}h_{x}h_{y}-r\ell h^{r+\ell-2}h_{x}h_{y}=0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
10.4
.

Let
\mu_{0}=d+e-1
.
Define constants

\lambda_{\ell}=\begin{cases}&[F,G],\text{ for $\ell=\mu_{0}$,}\\
&0,\text{ for $\ell\neq\mu_{0}$}.\end{cases}

Then

(10.6)

\lambda_{\ell}=\big[[\widetilde{F},\widetilde{G}]\big]_{t^{\ell}}=\big[[\sum_{i\geq 0}F_{d-i}t^{i},\sum_{j\geq 0}G_{e-j}t^{j}]\big]_{t^{\ell}}=\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i,j\geq 0}}{{i+j=\ell}}}[F_{d-i},G_{e-j}],

so

(10.7)

[G_{e-\ell},F_{d}]=-[F_{d},G_{e-\ell}]=-\lambda_{\ell}+\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i>0,j\geq 0}}{{i+j=\ell}}}[F_{d-i},G_{e-j}].

We will prove the theorem by induction on
\mu
.
The base case (
\mu=0
) follows from
[F_{d},G_{e}]=0
and
Lemma

10.5
.
For the inductive step, assume that
\mu>0
,
that
c_{0},\dots,c_{\mu-1}
have already been determined,
and that (
10.4
) holds for
\mu^{\prime}<\mu
.
Note that in the right-hand side of
(
10.4
),
the only coefficients

c_{\gamma}
that contribute to the coefficient of
t^{\mu-\gamma}
are those with

\gamma\leq\mu
, so we show that
requiring
(
10.4
) to hold uniquely determines
c_{\mu}

from
G_{e-\mu}
together with
c_{0},\dots,c_{\mu-1}
.
Define

(10.8)

H_{e-\mu}:=G_{e-\mu}-\sum_{\gamma=0}^{\mu-1}c_{\gamma}[\widetilde{F}^{\frac{e-\gamma}{d}}]_{t^{\mu-\gamma}}.

We claim that:

\text{ For }\mu\leq\mu_{0},\quad[H_{e-\mu},F_{d}]=-\lambda_{\mu}.

Indeed,

\displaystyle[H_{e-\mu},F_{d}]

\displaystyle=[G_{e-\mu},F_{d}]-[\sum_{\gamma=0}^{\mu-1}c_{\gamma}[\widetilde{F}^{\frac{e-\gamma}{d}}]_{t^{\mu-\gamma}},F_{d}]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}-\lambda_{\mu}+\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i>0,j\geq 0}}{{i+j=\mu}}}[F_{d-i},G_{e-j}]-\sum_{\gamma=0}^{\mu-1}[c_{\gamma}[\widetilde{F}^{\frac{e-\gamma}{d}}]_{t^{\mu-\gamma}},F_{d}]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{=}}-\lambda_{\mu}+\sum_{j=0}^{\mu-1}[F_{d-\mu+j},\sum_{\gamma=0}^{j}c_{\gamma}[\widetilde{F}^{\frac{e-\gamma}{d}}]_{t^{j-\gamma}}]-\sum_{\gamma=0}^{\mu-1}[c_{\gamma}[\widetilde{F}^{\frac{e-\gamma}{d}}]_{t^{\mu-\gamma}},F_{d}]

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{\mu}+\sum_{\gamma=0}^{\mu-1}c_{\gamma}\Big(\sum_{j=\gamma}^{\mu-1}\big[F_{d-\mu+j},[\widetilde{F}^{\frac{e-\gamma}{d}}]_{t^{j-\gamma}}\big]-\big[[\widetilde{F}^{\frac{e-\gamma}{d}}]_{t^{\mu-\gamma}},F_{d}\big]\Big)

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{\mu}+\sum_{\gamma=0}^{\mu-1}c_{\gamma}\Big(\sum_{j=\gamma}^{\mu}\big[F_{d-\mu+j},[\widetilde{F}^{\frac{e-\gamma}{d}}]_{t^{j-\gamma}}\big]\Big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{=}}-\lambda_{\mu}+\sum_{\gamma=0}^{\mu-1}c_{\gamma}\Big(\sum_{i\leq\mu-\gamma}\big[[\widetilde{F}]_{t^{i}},[\widetilde{F}^{\frac{e-\gamma}{d}}]_{t^{\mu-\gamma-i}}\big]\Big)

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{\mu}+\sum_{\gamma=0}^{\mu-1}c_{\gamma}\Big(\big[[\widetilde{F},\widetilde{F}^{\frac{e-\gamma}{d}}]\big]_{t^{\mu-\gamma}}\Big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(d)}}{{=}}-\lambda_{\mu}

where (a) uses (
10.7
), (b) uses the inductive hypothesis, (c) is obtained by substitution
i=\mu-j
and the fact that
[\widetilde{F}]_{t^{i}}=0
for
i<0
, and (d) uses
Lemma

10.6
.

First, consider the case
\mu<\mu_{0}
.
The above claim asserts that
[H_{e-\mu},F_{d}]=-\lambda_{\mu}=0
.
If
H_{e-\mu}=0
,
then
c_{\mu}=0
is the unique constant satisfying (
10.4
).
Now suppose
H_{e-\mu}\neq 0
.
By the inductive hypothesis and

10.7
,

H_{e-\mu}
is a homogeneous Laurent polynomial in

By

10.5
(with

Next, consider the case

∎

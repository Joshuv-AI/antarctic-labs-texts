The Frobenius transform of a symmetric function
Mitchell Lee

Abstract
. We define an abelian group homomorphism ℱ \mathscr{F} , which we call the Frobenius transform , from the ring of symmetric functions to the ring of the symmetric power series. The matrix entries of ℱ \mathscr{F} in the Schur basis are the restriction coefficients r λ μ = dim Hom 𝔖 n ⁡ ( V μ , 𝕊 λ ​ ℂ n ) r_{\lambda}^{\mu}=\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(V_{\mu},\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n}) , which are known to be nonnegative integers but have no known combinatorial interpretation. The Frobenius transform satisfies the identity ℱ ​ { f ​ g } = ℱ ​ { f } ∗ ℱ ​ { g } \mathscr{F}\{fg\}=\mathscr{F}\{f\}\ast\mathscr{F}\{g\} , where ∗ \ast is the Kronecker product. We prove for all symmetric functions f f that ℱ ⁡ { f } = ℱ Sur ​ { f } ⋅ ( 1 + h 1 + h 2 + ⋯ ) \mathscr{F}\{f\}=\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}\left\{f\right\}\cdot(1+h_{1}+h_{2}+\cdots) , where ℱ Sur ​ { f } \mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}\left\{f\right\} is a symmetric function with the same degree and leading term as f f . Then, we compute the matrix entries of ℱ Sur \mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}} in the complete homogeneous, elementary, and power sum bases and of ℱ Sur − 1 \mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}^{-1} in the complete homogeneous and elementary bases, giving combinatorial interpretations of the coefficients where possible. 1.
Introduction

Let
n\geq 0
and let
\lambda
be a partition with at most
n
parts. There is a corresponding irreducible
GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-module: the Schur module
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Because the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
embeds in
GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
by permutation matrices, one may ask: how does the restriction of
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n}
to
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
decompose into irreducible
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-modules?

In other words, let
\lambda
and
\mu
be partitions and let
n=|\mu|
. What is the value of the
restriction coefficient

r_{\lambda}^{\mu}=\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(V_{\mu},\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n}),

where
V_{\mu}
is the Specht module corresponding to the partition
\mu
? This problem, called the
restriction problem
, has held considerable recent interest
[
2
,
15
,
14
,
18
,
20
]
. However, there remains no known combinatorial interpretation for
r_{\lambda}^{\mu}
.

Let
\Lambda
be the ring of symmetric functions in the variables
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots
and let
\overline{\Lambda}
be the ring of symmetric power series in
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots
. In this paper, we will consider the abelian group homomorphism
\mathscr{F}\colon\Lambda\to\overline{\Lambda}
defined on the basis
\{s_{\lambda}\}
of Schur functions by

\mathscr{F}\{s_{\lambda}\}=\sum_{\mu}r_{\lambda}^{\mu}s_{\mu}.

Equivalently,
\mathscr{F}\{s_{\lambda}\}
is the result of applying the Frobenius character map to the representation
\bigoplus_{n}\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n}
of
\bigoplus_{n}\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
. For this reason, we call
\mathscr{F}
the
Frobenius transform
. It encodes all information about all the restriction coefficients.

Section

3
will cover the basic properties of the Frobenius transform, many of which are implicit in the work of Orellana and Zabrocki. For example, for any symmetric functions
f,g
, we have
\mathscr{F}\{fg\}=\mathscr{F}\{f\}\ast\mathscr{F}\{g\}
, where
\ast
is the Kronecker product of symmetric functions. Moreover, for any
f\in\Lambda
, there exists
\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}\left\{f\right\}\in\Lambda
with the same degree and leading term as
f
such that
\mathscr{F}\{f\}=\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}\left\{f\right\}\cdot(1+h_{1}+h_{2}+\cdots)
. We refer to the map
\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda
as the
surjective Frobenius transform
. Because the surjective Frobenius transform preserves degree and leading term, it has an inverse, which we denote by
\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}^{-1}
.

The
induced trivial character basis

\{\tilde{h}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
and the
irreducible character basis

\{\tilde{s}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
, which were introduced by Orellana and Zabrocki in 2021
[
18
]
, can be defined in terms of the inverse surjective Frobenius transform. Namely,
\tilde{h}_{\lambda}=\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}^{-1}\left\{h_{\lambda}\right\}
and
\tilde{s}_{\lambda}=\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}^{-1}\left\{(1+h_{1}+h_{2}+\cdots)^{\perp}s_{\lambda}\right\}
, where
f^{\perp}\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda
denotes the operator adjoint under the Hall inner product to multiplication by
f
.

In
Section

4
, we will use the Frobenius transform to study
stable restriction coefficients
; that is, the limits

a_{\lambda}^{\mu}=\lim_{n\to\infty}r_{\lambda}^{(n-|\mu|,\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{\ell(\mu)})},

which exist for all
\lambda,\mu
by a classical result of Littlewood
[
8
]
. In 2019, Assaf and Speyer found a formula for
a_{\lambda}^{\mu}
and for the entries
b_{\lambda}^{\mu}
of the inverse matrix
[b_{\lambda}^{\mu}]=[a_{\lambda}^{\mu}]^{-1}

[
2
]
. We will provide an alternative proof of these formulas using the Frobenius transform. In
Theorem

7
, we will broadly summarize the known relationships between the five kinds of restriction coefficients considered in this paper.

In
Section

5

\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda
as a sum of operators of the form

.

Theorem 1
.

Let

be a symmetric function. Then

\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}\left\{f\right\}=\sum_{\lambda}s_{\lambda}(s_{\lambda}[h_{2}+h_{3}+h_{4}+\cdots])^{\perp}f,

where the sum is over all partitions

Since

and stable restriction coefficients

vanish.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\lambda,\mu
be partitions. If

|\lambda\cap\hat{\mu}|\geq 2|\hat{\mu}|-|\lambda|
, where
\hat{\mu}=(\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{\ell(\mu)})
is the partition formed by removing the first part of

.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\lambda,\mu
be partitions. If
a_{\lambda}^{\mu}>0
, then
|\lambda\cap\mu|\geq 2|\mu|-|\lambda|
.

In
Section

6

\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}\left\{f\right\}
when

is a complete homogeneous, elementary, or power sum symmetric function. We have
\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}\left\{e_{n}\right\}=e_{n}
and
\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}\left\{p_{n}\right\}=\sum_{d\mid n}p_{d}
for

. More generally, with
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}

Theorem 4
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition and let

be its length.

(a)

Then

\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}\left\{h_{\lambda}\right\}=\sum_{M}\prod_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}}h_{M(j)},

where the sum is over all functions

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}}j_{i}M(j)=\lambda_{i}

(b)

Then

\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}\left\{e_{\lambda}\right\}=\sum_{M}\prod_{j\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}}\begin{cases}h_{M(j)}&\mbox{if $j_{1}+\cdots+j_{\ell}$ is even;}\\
e_{M(j)}&\mbox{if $j_{1}+\cdots+j_{\ell}$ is odd,}\end{cases}

where the sum is over all functions

such that
M(0,\ldots,0)=0
and
\sum_{j\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}}j_{i}M(j)=\lambda_{i}

Then

\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}\left\{p_{\lambda}\right\}=\sum_{\pi}\prod_{U\in\pi}\left(\sum_{d\mid\gcd\{\lambda_{i}\colon i\in U\}}d^{|U|-1}p_{d}\right),

A statement equivalent to parts (a) and (b) of this theorem has appeared previously in the work of Orellana and Zabrocki
[

Theorem

\mu

the size of the Durfee square of

Theorem 5
.

Let

(A)

There exists a partition

(B)

.

In particular,

Finally, in

Apart from the definition of the restriction coefficients

Let

denote the ring of symmetric functions over

in the variables

. Let
\overline{\Lambda}

, where each

For any partition

and the

. Let

\lambda

. Let
\lambda^{T}
denote the dual (i.e. transpose) of
\lambda
. Let

, which is an irreducible

-module. Let

denote the character of

. Let

denote the corresponding

, which is an endofunctor of the category of vector spaces over

For any partitions
\lambda,\mu

by

\lambda
and

. Let us say that

Let

\omega(p_{k})=(-1)^{k-1}p_{k}

\omega
is an involution and that for all partitions
\lambda

\omega(h_{\lambda})=e_{\lambda}

\omega(s_{\lambda})=s_{\lambda^{T}}

The
Lyndon symmetric function

L_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)p_{d}^{n/d}

for

, where
\mu\colon\{1,2,3,\ldots\}\to\{-1,0,1\}

For all

, the
skewing operator

is the adjoint to multiplication by
f
under the Hall inner product:

\langle g,f^{\perp}h\rangle=\langle fg,h\rangle.

We say that a Schur function

appears
in

if
\langle s_{\lambda},f\rangle\neq 0
. We say that

if
\langle s_{\lambda},f\rangle\geq 0
for all partitions
\lambda
.

Let
C_{n}
denote the space of all
\mathbb{C}
-valued class functions on
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. Let
R_{n}
denote the additive group of all virtual characters on
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. In other words,

is the subgroup of

generated by the irreducible characters
\chi_{\lambda}
. The
n

is the map

defined by

\ch_{n}(\chi)=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\chi(w)p_{c(w)},

where

denotes the cycle type of

. It is well-known that
\ch_{n}
is an isomorphism and that
\ch_{n}(\chi_{\lambda})=s_{\lambda}
for all
\lambda
with
|\lambda|=n
.

The

is the unique bilinear operator
\ast\colon\Lambda\times\Lambda\to\Lambda
satisfying
p_{\lambda}\ast p_{\mu}=\delta_{\lambda\mu}z_{\lambda}p_{\lambda}
for all
\lambda,\mu
. It extends to a bilinear operator

by continuity. The Frobenius character map and the Kronecker product are related in the following way: for any
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in R_{n}
, we have
\ch(\chi_{1}\chi_{2})=\ch(\chi_{1})\ast\ch(\chi_{2})
.

For any
f,g\in\overline{\Lambda}
, let

denote the plethysm of
f
by
g
. This is well-defined as long as

or

Proposition 1

(Plethystic Addition Formula,
[
9

)
.

Let

. Then

where the sum is over all partitions

.

For a variable

, let

\displaystyle H(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}h_{n}t^{n}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\geq 1}\frac{1}{1-x_{i}t}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{p_{k}}{k}t^{k}\right)

\displaystyle\in\Lambda\llbracket t\rrbracket

\displaystyle E(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}e_{n}t^{n}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\geq 1}(1+x_{i}t)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{p_{k}}{k}(-1)^{k-1}t^{k}\right)

It is clear that
E(t)=\frac{1}{H(-t)}
. Let
H=H(1)=1+h_{1}+h_{2}+\cdots
and
H_{+}=h_{1}+h_{2}+h_{3}+\cdots=H-1

For any partitions
\lambda,\mu,\nu
, the

Let
\lambda,\mu
be partitions and let

3.
The Frobenius transform: definition and basic properties

Recall from the introduction that it is a long-standing open problem to find a combinatorial interpretation of
r_{\lambda}^{\mu}
. As a potential way to approach this problem, we now define the Frobenius transform, which is the primary object of study in this paper.

Definition 2
.

The
Frobenius transform
is the abelian group homomorphism
\mathscr{F}\colon\Lambda\to\overline{\Lambda}
defined on the basis
\{s_{\lambda}\}
by

\mathscr{F}\{s_{\lambda}\}=\sum_{\mu}r_{\lambda}^{\mu}s_{\mu},

where the sum is over all partitions
\mu
.

Remark 1
.

By a classical result of Littlewood
[
8
]
, we have

r_{\lambda}^{\mu}=\langle s_{\lambda},s_{\mu}[H]\rangle.

Hence,
\mathscr{F}
is adjoint to plethysm by
H
under the Hall inner product.

Here is the reason for calling
\mathscr{F}
the Frobenius transform. Let
n\geq 0
and let
\lambda
be any partition. Then
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n}
, considered as an
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-module, can be expressed as a direct sum of Specht modules:

\bigoplus_{|\mu|=n}r_{\lambda}^{\mu}V_{\mu}=\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Taking the character of both sides and applying the Frobenius character map, we obtain

(1)

\sum_{|\mu|=n}r_{\lambda}^{\mu}s_{\mu}=\ch_{n}(\chi_{\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n}}).

In other words, the degree
n
part of
\mathscr{F}\{s_{\lambda}\}
is equal to the Frobenius character of
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Example 1
.

Let
r\geq 0
. We will compute
\mathscr{F}\{e_{r}\}
. First,
e_{r}=s_{\lambda}
, where
\lambda=(1^{r})
. Hence, for any
n
, the degree
n
part of
\mathscr{F}\{e_{r}\}
is the Frobenius character of

\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n}=\wedge^{r}\mathbb{C}^{n}=\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n-r}}^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(V_{(1^{r})}\otimes V_{(n-r)}),

considered as an
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-module. Thus, it is equal to
e_{r}h_{n-r}

[
21
, Proposition 7.18.2]
. Taking the sum over all
n
yields
\mathscr{F}\{e_{r}\}=e_{r}\cdot H
.

3.1.
The Frobenius transform and representations of combinatorial categories

The purpose of this subsection is to provide an alternate perspective on the Frobenius transform. This subsection is not essential to the proofs of our main results, so the reader may skip it. For a more complete introduction to the representation theory of categories, see Wiltshire-Gordon’s 2016 PhD thesis

In what follows, let
\text{Vect}_{\mathbb{C}}
be the category whose objects are vector spaces over
\mathbb{C}
and whose morphisms are linear transformations. (The ground field

can be replaced by any algebraically closed field of characteristic

.)

Definition 3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category. A

-module
(over the ground field

We say that a

-module

is finite-dimensional for all
U\in\operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal{C})
.

If

and

are

-modules, we may form the
direct sum

by
(M\oplus N)[U]=M[U]\oplus N[U]
.

Let

be the category whose objects are finite sets and whose morphisms are bijections, and let

be a
\mathrm{Bij}
-module. (Previous authors have referred to

-modules as

or

[

Additionally, if

Not every symmetric power series can be written in the form

\mathrm{Bij}
-module. (Every symmetric power series can be written as the Frobenius character of a

-module

Proof.

Remark 2

We are now ready to show that

Proposition 3

Let

\mathscr{F}\{\ch(M)\}=\ch(\iota^{*}\iota_{!}M)

Proof.

For any partition

On the other hand, by

By Schur-Weyl duality, this is isomorphic as a

It follows that

3.2.
The Frobenius transform and evaluation at roots of unity

Let us now describe the expansion of
\mathscr{F}\{s_{\lambda}\}
in the power sum basis. In order to simplify the description, we will do it one degree at a time. In this subsection, we will write
f_{n}
to mean the degree
n
part of
f
.

By (
1
), we have

(3)

(\mathscr{F}\{s_{\lambda}\})_{n}=\ch_{n}(\chi_{\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n}})=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\chi_{\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n}}(w)p_{c(w)}.

It is well-known
[
5
, Section 8.3]
that the character of
\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n}
as a
GL_{n}
-module is the Schur function
s_{\lambda}
itself. In other words, if
g\in GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
has eigenvalues
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
, then
\chi_{\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n}}(g)
is equal to the evaluation
s_{\lambda}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
.

For
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, we have
\chi_{\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n}}(w)=s_{\lambda}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, where
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
are the eigenvalues of the permutation matrix
P_{w}
. Let
\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{\ell})
be the cycle type of
w
. Then the eigenvalues of
P_{w}
are the roots of unity

\displaystyle 1,\exp\left(\frac{2\pi i}{\mu_{1}}\right),\exp\left(\frac{4\pi i}{\mu_{1}}\right),\ldots,\exp\left(\frac{2(\mu_{1}-1)\pi i}{\mu_{1}}\right),

\displaystyle 1,\exp\left(\frac{2\pi i}{\mu_{\ell}}\right),\exp\left(\frac{4\pi i}{\mu_{\ell}}\right),\ldots,\exp\left(\frac{2(\mu_{\ell}-1)\pi i}{\mu_{\ell}}\right).

Following Orellana and Zabrocki
[
17
,
18
]
, let
\Xi_{\mu}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
denote this sequence. We have that
\chi_{\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n}}(w)
is the result of evaluating
s_{\lambda}
at these roots of unity:

\chi_{\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n}}(w)=s_{\lambda}\left(\Xi_{\mu}\right).

Now, let us group the terms on the right-hand side of (
3
) according to the cycle type
\mu=c(w)
. The number of permutations
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
with cycle type
\mu
is
\frac{n!}{z_{\mu}}
, so

\displaystyle(\mathscr{F}\{s_{\lambda}\})_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\chi_{\mathbb{S}^{\lambda}\mathbb{C}^{n}}(w)p_{c(w)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{|\mu|=n}\frac{n!}{z_{\mu}}s_{\lambda}(\Xi_{\mu})p_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\mu|=n}s_{\lambda}(\Xi_{\mu})\frac{p_{\mu}}{z_{\mu}}.

Taking the sum over all
n
, we obtain

\mathscr{F}\{s_{\lambda}\}=\sum_{\mu}s_{\lambda}(\Xi_{\mu})\frac{p_{\mu}}{z_{\mu}}.

Finally, by linearity, we may extend this result to any
f\in\Lambda
. We have proved the following.

Proposition 5
.

Let
f\in\Lambda
. Then

\mathscr{F}\{f\}=\sum_{\mu}f(\Xi_{\mu})\frac{p_{\mu}}{z_{\mu}}.

In the notation of Orellana and Zabrocki
[
17
]
, this proposition can be written as

\mathscr{F}\{f\}=\phi_{0}(f)+\phi_{1}(f)+\phi_{2}(f)+\cdots.

3.3.
The Frobenius transform and the Kronecker product

The Frobenius transform relates the ordinary product of symmetric functions to the Kronecker product in the following way.

Proposition 6

(cf.
[
17
, Section 2.3]
)
.

Let

. Then
\mathscr{F}\{fg\}=\mathscr{F}\{f\}\ast\mathscr{F}\{g\}
.

We provide two different proofs of
6
: a category-theoretic proof using
3
and a direct computational proof using
5
.

First proof.

Because both sides of the desired equation are bilinear, we may assume that there exist
\mathrm{Bij}
-modules
M,N
such that
\ch(M)=f
and
\ch(N)=g
. By
4
, we have
\ch(M\cdot N)=\ch(M)\ch(N)=fg
, where

is the product as defined in
6
.

By
[
7
, Equation 2.1(ii)]
, we have
\iota^{*}\iota_{!}(M\cdot N)=(\iota^{*}\iota_{!}M)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}(\iota^{*}\iota_{!}N)
, where

denotes the object-wise tensor product of
\mathrm{Bij}
-modules. Applying
\ch
to both sides, we obtain
\mathscr{F}\{fg\}=\mathscr{F}\{f\}\ast\mathscr{F}\{g\}
, as desired.
∎

Second proof.

By
5
, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}\{f\}\ast\mathscr{F}\{g\}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\mu}f(\Xi_{\mu})\frac{p_{\mu}}{z_{\mu}}\right)\ast\left(\sum_{\mu}g(\Xi_{\mu})\frac{p_{\mu}}{z_{\mu}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu}f(\Xi_{\mu})g(\Xi_{\mu})\frac{p_{\mu}}{z_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{F}\{fg\}

as desired.
∎

Corollary 1
.

Let
\lambda,\mu,\nu
be partitions. Then

\sum_{\nu^{\prime}}r^{\nu}_{\nu^{\prime}}c^{\nu^{\prime}}_{\lambda\mu}=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}}r^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{\lambda}r^{\mu^{\prime}}_{\mu}g_{\lambda^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}\nu}.

Proof.

In

and

. Then take the Hall inner product of both sides with

.
∎

3.4.
The surjective Frobenius transform

Proposition 7
.

Let
f\in\Lambda
. Then there exists a symmetric function
\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}\left\{f\right\}\in\Lambda
such that
\mathscr{F}\{f\}=\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}\left\{f\right\}\cdot H
. Moreover,
\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}\left\{f\right\}
has the same degree and leading term as

.

For example, in
1
we showed that
\mathscr{F}\{e_{r}\}=e_{r}\cdot H
. Thus,
\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}\left\{e_{r}\right\}=e_{r}
. (Note, however, that in general,
\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}
does not preserve the property of being homogeneous.) Again, we provide two separate proofs of this proposition: a category-theoretic proof and a direct computational proof.

First proof.

Because both sides of the desired equation are linear in
f
, we may assume that there exists a
\mathrm{Bij}
-module
M
such that
\ch(M)=f
.

Let
\mathrm{Sur}
be the category whose objects are finite sets and whose morphisms are surjections, and let
\kappa\colon\mathrm{Bij}\to\mathrm{Sur}
be the inclusion functor. Let
\kappa_{!}\colon\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{Bij}}\to\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{Fun}}
be the left Kan extension functor along
\kappa
, which exists by
[
22
, Proposition 2.6.7]
. We claim that the choice
\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}\left\{f\right\}=\ch(\kappa^{*}\kappa_{!}M)
satisfies the desired properties. For this, we will show that there is a natural isomorphism

(4)

\iota^{*}\iota_{!}M=\kappa^{*}\kappa_{!}M\cdot\mathbb{C}E,

where
\iota
and
\cdot
are defined as in
Section

3.1
and
\mathbb{C}E
is the
\mathrm{Bij}
-module given by
(\mathbb{C}E)[U]=\mathbb{C}
for all finite sets
U
.

By
2
, we have

(5)

(\iota^{*}\iota_{!}M)[U]=\bigoplus_{n}\mathbb{C}U^{n}\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}M[n].

Think of
U^{n}
as the set of all functions

. By grouping those functions by their image

, we may write
U^{n}
as a disjoint union:

U^{n}=\sum_{V\subseteq U}\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}([n],V).

Substituting into (

\displaystyle(\iota^{*}\iota_{!}M)[U]

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{n}\mathbb{C}\left(\sum_{V\subseteq U}\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}([n],V)\right)\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}M[n]

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{n}\left(\bigoplus_{V\subseteq U}\mathbb{C}\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}([n],V)\right)\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}M[n]

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{V\subseteq U}\bigoplus_{n}\mathbb{C}\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}([n],V)\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}M[n]

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{V\subseteq U}(\kappa^{*}\kappa_{!}M)[V]

where the last step follows from
[
22
, Proposition 2.6.7]
. Recognizing the latter as
(\kappa^{*}\kappa_{!}M\cdot\mathbb{C}E)[U]
, we have shown (
4
). Applying
\ch
to both sides and using
3
and
4
yields
\mathscr{F}\{f\}=\ch(\kappa^{*}\kappa_{!}M)\cdot H
.

It remains to show that
\ch(\kappa^{*}\kappa_{!}M)
has the same degree and leading term as
f
. For this, we will prove that
(\kappa^{*}\kappa_{!}M)[m]
and
M[m]
are isomorphic as
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
-modules for all
m\geq\deg f
. Consider the natural isomorphism

(\kappa^{*}\kappa_{!}M)[U]=\bigoplus_{n}\mathbb{C}\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}([n],U)\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}M[n]

with
U=[m]
. In each summand, the factor
\mathbb{C}\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}([n],[m])
vanishes when
n<m
and the factor
M[n]
vanishes when
n>\deg f
. If

, this means that every term vanishes except possibly the term

. Hence,

\displaystyle(\kappa^{*}\kappa_{!}M)[m]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}([m],[m])\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}_{m}}M[m]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{S}_{m}\otimes_{\mathfrak{S}_{m}}M[m]

\displaystyle=M[m]

as desired.
∎

Second proof.

We claim that the choice

\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}\left\{f\right\}=\sum_{\mu}\langle f,s_{\mu}[H_{+}]\rangle s_{\mu}

satisfies the desired properties. In other words, we may take
\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}
to be the adjoint to plethysm by
H_{+}
under the Hall inner product.

By
1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}\{f\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda}\langle f,s_{\lambda}[H]\rangle s_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda}\langle f,s_{\lambda}[H_{+}+1]\rangle s_{\lambda}.

By the plethystic addition formula (
1
), we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}\{f\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda,\mu}\langle f,s_{\mu}[H_{+}]s_{\lambda/\mu}[1]\rangle s_{\lambda}.

We have

s_{\lambda/\mu}[1]=s_{\lambda/\mu}(1,0,0,\ldots)=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $\lambda/\mu$ is a horizontal strip;}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise,}\end{cases}

so

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}\{f\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda,\mu\\
\text{$\lambda/\mu$ h. strip}\end{subarray}}\langle f,s_{\mu}[H_{+}]\rangle s_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu}\langle f,s_{\mu}[H_{+}]\rangle\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\\
\text{$\lambda/\mu$ h. strip}\end{subarray}}s_{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu}\langle f,s_{\mu}[H_{+}]\rangle s_{\mu}\cdot H,

where the last equality follows from the Pieri rule.

Now, the only thing left to show is that
f
has the same degree and leading term as

\sum_{\mu}\langle f,s_{\mu}[H_{+}]\rangle s_{\mu}.

This follows directly from the observation that if
|\mu|\geq\deg f
, then
\langle f,s_{\mu}[H_{+}]\rangle=\langle f,s_{\mu}\rangle
.
∎

We refer to
\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda
as the
surjective Frobenius transform
. Clearly, it is invertible:

Corollary 2
.

There exists a two-sided inverse
\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}^{-1}\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda
of
\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}
.

Proof.

Define
\mathcal{M}\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda
by
\mathcal{M}\{f\}=f-\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}\left\{f\right\}
. By
7
, we have

\deg(\mathcal{M}\{f\})<\deg f

for any
f\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}
. Hence,
\mathcal{M}^{k}\{f\}=0
for any
k>\deg f
.

Define
\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}^{-1}\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda
by

\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}^{-1}\left\{f\right\}=f+\mathcal{M}\{f\}+\mathcal{M}^{2}\{f\}+\cdots.

This is well-defined by the above, and it is easy to check that it is the two-sided inverse of
\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}
.
∎

Like the ordinary Frobenius transform, the surjective Frobenius transform can be described in terms of its matrix entries in the Schur basis.

]

We wish to show that

(20)

(\overline{\omega}\circ\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}^{-1}\circ\overline{\omega})\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}H(t_{i})\right\}=\prod_{w\in\Lyndon([\ell])}H(t^{w}).

To do so, let
f\in\Lambda
be arbitrary. It is sufficient to show that each side of (
20
) has the same Hall inner product with
f
. By
Theorem

7
(c),
\overline{\omega}\circ\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}^{-1}\circ\overline{\omega}
is adjoint to plethysm by
L
, so

\displaystyle\left\langle f,(\overline{\omega}\circ\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}^{-1}\circ\overline{\omega})\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}H(t_{i})\right\}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle f[L],\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}H(t_{i})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=(f[L])[t_{1}+\cdots+t_{\ell}]

\displaystyle=f[L[t_{1}+\cdots+t_{\ell}]]

\displaystyle=f[L(t_{1},\ldots,t_{\ell})]

\displaystyle=f\left[\sum_{w\in\Lyndon([\ell])}t^{w}\right]

\displaystyle=\left\langle f,\prod_{w\in\Lyndon([\ell])}H(t^{w})\right\rangle,

where we used
Lemma

7
,
Lemma

5
,
Lemma

6
, and
Theorem

9
. This completes the proof of (
20
) and of part (a) of the theorem.

(b) Again, let
f\in\Lambda
be arbitrary. It is sufficient to show that each side of the equation has the same Hall inner product with
f
. By
Theorem

7
(c),
\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}^{-1}
is adjoint to plethysm by
\tilde{L}
, so

\displaystyle\left\langle f,\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}^{-1}\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}H(t_{i})\right\}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle f[\tilde{L}],\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}H(t_{i})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=(f[\tilde{L}])[t_{1}+\cdots+t_{\ell}]

(21)

\displaystyle=f[\tilde{L}[t_{1}+\cdots+t_{\ell}]]

Now, let us describe the monomials that appear in the plethysm
\tilde{L}[t_{1}+\cdots+t_{\ell}]
. By the definition of Lyndon symmetric functions, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{L}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}(-1)^{n-1}\omega(L_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n}\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)\omega(p_{d}^{n/d})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n}\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)(-1)^{(d-1)n/d}p_{d}^{n/d}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)(-1)^{n/d-1}p_{d}^{n/d}.

Now, the term
(-1)^{n/d-1}
is equal to
-1
if
d
divides
\frac{n}{2}
and
1
otherwise. Hence,

\tilde{L}=\sum_{n}\frac{1}{n}\left(\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)p_{d}^{n/d}-2\sum_{d\mid\frac{n}{2}}\mu(d)p_{d}^{n/d}\right)

where the sum over
d\mid\frac{n}{2}
is understood to be empty if
n
is odd. Splitting this into two sums and then performing the change of variables
\frac{n}{2}\to n
in the second, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{L}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)p_{d}^{n/d}-\sum_{n}\frac{2}{n}\sum_{d\mid\frac{n}{2}}\mu(d)p_{d}^{n/d}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)p_{d}^{n/d}-\sum_{n}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)p_{d}^{2n/d}

\displaystyle=L-L[p_{1}^{2}].

By
Lemma

5
and
Theorem

9
,

\displaystyle\tilde{L}[t_{1}+\cdots+t_{\ell}]

\displaystyle=L[t_{1}+\cdots+t_{\ell}]-(L[p_{1}^{2}])[t_{1}+\cdots+t_{\ell}]

\displaystyle=L[t_{1}+\cdots+t_{\ell}]-L[p_{1}^{2}[t_{1}+\cdots+t_{\ell}]]

\displaystyle=L[t_{1}+\cdots+t_{\ell}]-L[(t_{1}+\cdots+t_{\ell})^{2}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in\Lyndon([\ell])}t^{w}-\sum_{w\in\Lyndon([\ell]^{2})}t^{w}.

Substituting into (
21
) and using
Lemma

7
one last time, we finally obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle f,\mathscr{F}_{\mathchoice{\operatorname{Sur}}{\operatorname{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}{\mathrm{Sur}}}^{-1}\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}H(t_{i})\right\}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=f\left[\sum_{w\in\Lyndon([\ell])}t^{w}-\sum_{w\in\Lyndon([\ell]^{2})}t^{w}\right]

\displaystyle=\left\langle f,\frac{\prod_{w\in\Lyndon([\ell])}H(t^{w})}{\prod_{w\in\Lyndon([\ell]^{2})}H(t^{w})}\right\rangle,

completing the proof.
∎

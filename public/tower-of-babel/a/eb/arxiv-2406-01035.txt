Explicit inverse of symmetric, tridiagonal near Toeplitz matrices with strictly diagonally dominant Toeplitz part
Bakytzhan Kurmanbek, Yogi Erlangga, Yerlan Amanbek

Abstract
This study investigates tridiagonal near-Toeplitz matrices in which the Toeplitz part is strictly diagonally dominant. The focus is on determining the exact inverse of these matrices and establishing upper bounds for the infinite norms of the inverse matrices. For cases with b > 2 b>2 and b < − 2 b<-2 , we derive the compact form of the entries of the exact inverse. These results remain valid even when the matrices’ corners are not diagonally dominant, specifically when | b ~ | < 1 |\widetilde{b}|<1 . Furthermore, we calculate the traces and row sums of the inverse matrices. Afterwards, we present upper bound theorems for the infinite norms of the inverse matrices. To demonstrate the effectiveness of the bounds and their application, we provide numerical results for solving Fisher’s problem. Our findings reveal that the converging rates of fixed-point iterations closely align with the expected rates, and there is minimal disparity between the upper bounds and infinite norm of the inverse matrix. Specifically, this observation holds true when b > 2 b>2 with b ~ ≤ 1 \widetilde{b}\leq 1 and b < − 2 b<-2 with b ~ ≥ − 1 \widetilde{b}\geq-1 . For other cases, there is potential for further improvement in the obtained upper bounds. This study contributes to the field of numerical analysis of fixed-point iterations by improving the convergence rate of iterations and reducing the computing time of the inverse matrices.

In solving differential equations, for instance, a finite difference discretization of the differential equation results in a system of equations which involved a banded near-Toeplitz matrix, which is perturbed slightly from the Toeplitz structure due to the inclusion of the boundary conditions. In nonlinear differential equations, a fixed-point iteration can be employed to solve the nonlinear system. In this case, one often times is interested in the study of the convergence of the method, which involves the inverse of the underlying near-Toeplitz matrix.

As an illustration, consider the differential equation

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}u}{dx^{2}}=f(u),\quad x\in\Omega=(0,L).

with some boundary conditions at
x\in\{0,L\}
and some nonlinear function
u
. Examples are the so-called Fisher equation, with
f(u)=ku(1-u)
, and the Gelfand-Bratu equation, with
f(u)=ke^{u}
. Consider an approximation to the second derivative using the central difference scheme

\frac{d^{2}u}{dx^{2}}(x_{i})\approx\frac{1}{h^{2}}\left(u_{i-1}-2u_{i}+u_{i+1}\right),

(1.1)

where
x_{i}=ih\in[0,L]
, with
h
be a constant step size and
i=1,\dots,n=L/h
, and
u_{i}\equiv u(x_{i})
. This approximation process results in a system of nonlinear equations, which can be written as

\widehat{T}_{n}\bm{u}=h^{2}f(\bm{u})

(1.2)

where
\widehat{T}_{n}
is an
n
-by-
n
tridiagonal near Toeplitz matrix and
\bm{u}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Starting from an initial guess of the solution
\bm{u}^{0}
, an approximate solution of the differential equation can be computed via the fixed-point recurrence

\bm{u}^{k+1}=h^{2}\widehat{T}^{-1}_{n}f(\bm{u}^{k}),\quad k=1,2,\dots,

(1.3)

provided that the recurrence converges. Assuming that
f
is a Lipschitz continuous function in the domain
\Omega
, the sequence of solutions generated by the fixed-point iteration satisfies the inequality, for
p\in\{1,2,\infty\}
,

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}^{k+1}-\bm{u}^{k}\|_{p}=\|h^{2}\widehat{T}^{-1}_{n}\left(f(\bm{u}^{k})-f(\bm{u}^{k-1})\right)\|_{p}\leq h^{2}\|\widehat{T}^{-1}_{n}\|_{p}L_{c}\|\bm{u}^{k}-\bm{u}^{k-1}\|_{p},

where
L_{c}
is the Lipschitz constant, which depends on the nature of
f(\bm{u})
. To ensure the convergence of the fixed point iteration method, we require
h^{2}L_{c}\|\widehat{T}^{-1}_{n}\|_{p}<1
. Here, an upper bound of
\|\widehat{T}^{-1}_{n}\|_{p}
is useful in the convergence analysis.

In this paper, we present inverse properties of
n\times n
symmetric tridiagonal near-Toeplitz matrix

\widetilde{T}_{n}=\begin{bmatrix}\overline{b}&\hat{c}&&&\\
\hat{c}&\hat{b}&\ddots&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&\ddots&\hat{b}&\hat{c}\\
&&&\hat{c}&\overline{b}\end{bmatrix}=-\hat{c}\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\widetilde{b}&-1&&&\\
-1&b&\ddots&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&\ddots&b&-1\\
&&&-1&\widetilde{b}\end{bmatrix}}_{\widetilde{T}_{n}}=:-\hat{c}\,\widetilde{T}_{n},

(1.4)

with
n\geq 3
, such as the one arising in (
1.2
). Here,
b=-\hat{b}/\hat{c}
, and
\widetilde{b}=-\overline{b}/\hat{c}
. Since
\widehat{T}_{n}
is a factor of
\widetilde{T}_{n}
, we will primarily focus on the latter.

The near-Toeplitz matrix
\widetilde{T}_{n}
in (
1.4
) can be expressed as a rank-2 decomposition

\widetilde{T}_{n}=T_{n}+(\widetilde{b}-b)UU^{T},

(1.5)

where
T_{n}=\text{tridiag}(-1,b,-1)
is the Toeplitz part of
\widetilde{T}_{n}
and the matrix
U^{T}
is given by

U^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&\dots&0&1\end{bmatrix}.

(1.6)

The inverse of the Toeplitz part
T_{n}
, if it exists, can be characterized and explicitly expressed by the roots of the polynomial
p(r)=-r^{2}+br-1
(see
[
8
]
). If
T_{n}
is diagonally dominant, meaning
|b|\geq 2
, then
p(r)
has either two distinct real roots or equal roots, with the latter occurring when
b=2
. Otherwise,
p(r)
has two complex roots.

Suppose the inverse of
\widetilde{T}_{n}
exists. Applying the Sherman-Morrison formula, the inverse of
\widetilde{T}_{n}
is given by

\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}=T^{-1}_{n}-(\widetilde{b}-b)T^{-1}_{n}UM^{-1}U^{T}T^{-1}_{n},

(1.7)

where
M=I_{2}+(\widetilde{b}-b)U^{T}T^{-1}_{n}U\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
.
Let
\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}=:[\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}]
,
T^{-1}_{n}=:[t^{-1}_{i,j}]
, and
\beta=\widetilde{b}-b
. Then, for
M=:[m_{i,j}]_{i,j=1,2}
, we have

\displaystyle m_{11}

\displaystyle=m_{22}=1+(\widetilde{b}-b)t^{-1}_{1,1}=1+\beta t^{-1}_{n,n},

\displaystyle m_{12}

\displaystyle=m_{21}=(\widetilde{b}-b)t^{-1}_{1,n}=\beta t^{-1}_{n,1},

due to the symmetry and centrosymmetry of
T_{n}
. Furthermore,

M^{-1}=\frac{1}{\Delta}\begin{bmatrix}m_{11}&-m_{12}\\
-m_{12}&m_{11}\end{bmatrix},

(1.8)

where
\Delta=m_{11}^{2}-m_{12}^{2}=(1+\beta t^{-1}_{n,n})^{2}-\beta^{2}(t^{-1}_{n,1})^{2}
. With this, the
(i,j)
-entry (with
i\geq j
) of the inverse of
\widetilde{T}_{n}
is given explicitly by the formula

\widetilde{t}_{i,j}^{-1}=t^{-1}_{i,j}-\frac{\beta}{\Delta}\left[t_{i,1}^{-1}(m_{11}t^{-1}_{1,j}-m_{12}t^{-1}_{n,j})+t^{-1}_{i,n}(-m_{12}t^{-1}_{1,j}+m_{11}t^{-1}_{n,j})\right].

(1.9)

The trace of
\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}
can be calculated using the formula

\text{Tr}(\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n}):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{i,i}-\frac{\beta}{\Delta}\left\{m_{11}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left((t^{-1}_{i,1})^{2}+(t^{-1}_{n,i})^{2}\right)-2m_{12}\sum_{i=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{i,1}t^{-1}_{n,i}\right\},

(1.10)

due to the symmetry of
T_{n}
. The sum of the elements in the
i
-th row of the inverse is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{i,j}-\frac{\beta}{\Delta}\left[t_{i,1}^{-1}(m_{11}\sum_{j=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{1,j}-m_{12}\sum_{j=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{n,j})+t^{-1}_{i,n}(-m_{12}\sum_{j=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{1,j}+m_{11}\sum_{j=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{n,j})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{i,j}-\frac{\beta}{\Delta}\left[t_{i,1}^{-1}(m_{11}-m_{12})\sum_{j=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{n,j}+t^{-1}_{i,n}(-m_{12}+m_{11})\sum_{j=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{n,j}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{i,j}-\frac{\beta}{m_{11}+m_{12}}(t^{-1}_{i,1}+t^{-1}_{i,n})\sum_{j=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{n,j},

(1.11)

after making use of the centrosymmetry of
T_{n}
and
T^{-1}_{n}
, with

m_{11}+m_{12}=1+\beta(t^{-1}_{n,1}+t^{-1}_{n,n}).

2
Preliminary results

The inverse of the Toeplitz matrix
T_{n}=\text{tridiag}(-1,b,-1)
, with
|b|>2
, in (
1.5
) can be characterized by the two roots of the polynomial
p(r)=-r^{2}+br-1
:
r_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(b+\sqrt{b^{2}-4})
and
r_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(b-\sqrt{b^{2}-4})
; see
[
8
]
. Let
\gamma_{k}=r_{1}^{k}-r_{2}^{k}
with
\gamma_{0}=0
and
\gamma_{1}=\sqrt{b^{2}-4}
. Its inverse is given by

\displaystyle t^{-1}_{i,j}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{j}\gamma_{n+1-i}}{\sqrt{b^{2}-4}\gamma_{n+1}},&i\geq j,\\
t^{-1}_{j,i},&i<j.\end{cases}

(2.1)

Now, we list a couple of intermediate results, pertaining to
r_{1},\gamma_{k}
, and
b
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
b>2
. For any integer
k\geq 2
, the following holds true:

(i)

\gamma_{k}+\gamma_{k-2}=b\gamma_{k-1}
;

(ii)

\frac{b}{2}<r_{1}\leq\frac{\gamma_{k+1}}{\gamma_{k}}\leq b
;

(iii)

\gamma_{n+1-i}\gamma_{i+1}-\gamma_{i}\gamma_{n-i}=\gamma_{n+1}\gamma_{1}
.

Proof.

Using
\gamma_{k}=r_{1}^{k}-r_{2}^{k}
,

\gamma_{k}+\gamma_{k-2}=r_{1}^{k-1}(r_{1}+r_{1}^{-1})-r_{2}^{k-1}(r_{2}+r_{2}^{-1}),

from which Part (i) is derived via a direct calculation. From Part (i),
\gamma_{k+1}=b\gamma_{k}-\gamma_{k-1}\leq b\gamma_{k}
. Also,

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{k+1}}{\gamma_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{1}^{k+1}-r_{2}^{k+1}}{r_{1}^{k}-r_{2}^{k}}=r_{1}+r_{2}-\frac{r_{1}^{k}r_{2}-r_{2}^{k}r_{1}}{r_{1}^{k}-r_{2}^{k}}=b-\frac{r_{1}^{k-1}-r_{2}^{k-1}}{r_{1}^{k}-r_{2}^{k}}=b-\frac{r_{2}-r_{1}(r_{2}/r_{1})^{k}}{1-(r_{2}/r_{1})^{k}},

after using the fact that
r_{1}r_{2}=1
. Since
r_{1}>r_{2}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{k+1}}{\gamma_{k}}\geq b-\frac{r_{2}(1-(r_{2}/r_{1})^{k})}{1-(r_{2}/r_{1})^{k}}=b-r_{2}=r_{1}>\frac{b}{2},

completing Part (ii). For Part (iii), using
r_{1}r_{2}=1
,

\displaystyle\gamma_{n+1-i}\gamma_{i+1}-\gamma_{i}\gamma_{n-i}

\displaystyle=r^{n+2}_{1}-r^{n-2i}_{1}-r^{n-2i}_{2}+r^{n+2}_{2}-r^{n}_{1}+r^{n-2i}_{1}+r^{n-2i}_{2}-r^{n}_{2}

\displaystyle=r^{n+1}_{1}r^{1}_{1}-r^{n+1}_{1}r^{1}_{2}-r^{n+1}_{2}r^{1}_{1}+r^{n+1}_{2}r^{1}_{2}=\gamma_{n+1}\gamma_{1}.

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
|b|>2
. For any integer
p\geq 1
, the following relations hold:

(i)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{p}\gamma_{k}=\frac{1}{b-2}(\gamma_{p+1}-\gamma_{p}-\gamma_{1})
;

(ii)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{p}k\gamma_{k}=\frac{1}{b-2}(p\gamma_{p+1}-(p+1)\gamma_{p})
.

Proof.

For Part (i),

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{p}\gamma_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{p}(r_{1}^{k}-r_{2}^{k})=\sum_{k=0}^{p}r_{1}^{k}-1-\sum_{k=0}^{p}r_{2}^{k}+1=\frac{r_{1}^{p+1}-1}{r_{1}-1}-\frac{r_{2}^{p+1}-1}{r_{2}-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{1}^{p+1}-r_{2}^{p+1}-(r_{1}^{p}-r_{2}^{p})-(r_{1}-r_{2})}{b-2}=\frac{1}{b-2}(\gamma_{p+1}-\gamma_{p}-\gamma_{1}).

For Part (ii),

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{p}k\gamma_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{p}k(r_{1}^{k}-r_{2}^{k})=r_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{p}kr_{1}^{k-1}-r_{2}\sum_{k=1}^{p}kr_{2}^{k-1}

\displaystyle=r_{1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{p}r_{1}^{k}\right)^{\prime}-r_{2}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{p}r_{2}^{k}\right)^{\prime}=r_{1}\left(\frac{r_{1}^{p+1}-1}{r_{1}-1}\right)^{\prime}-r_{2}\left(\frac{r_{2}^{p+1}-1}{r_{2}-1}\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{pr_{1}^{p+2}-(p+1)r_{1}^{p+1}+r_{1}}{(r_{1}-1)^{2}}-\frac{pr_{2}^{p+2}-(p+1)r_{2}^{p+1}+r_{2}}{(r_{2}-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{pr_{1}^{p+1}-(p+1)r_{1}^{p}+1}{b-2}-\frac{pr_{2}^{p+1}-(p+1)r_{2}^{p}+1}{b-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b-2}(p\gamma_{p+1}-(p+1)\gamma_{p}).

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

For
b>2
and
n\geq 3
,

b-1<b-\frac{2}{b}\leq\frac{\gamma_{n+1}}{\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{1}}<r_{1}<b.

The detailed proof of Lemma
2.3
is provided in Appendix
A

Lemma 3.1

3.1
Trace of the inverse

The general formula for the trace of the inverse matrix
\widetilde{T}_{n}^{-1}
is given by (

t^{-1}_{n,n}=\gamma_{n}/\gamma_{n+1}

\displaystyle\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,i}

\displaystyle=t^{-1}_{i,i}-\frac{\widetilde{b}-b}{\Delta}\left[\frac{\gamma_{n+1}+(\widetilde{b}-b)\gamma_{n}}{\gamma_{n+1}}\left(\left(\frac{\gamma_{n+1-i}}{\gamma_{n+1}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\gamma_{i}}{\gamma_{n+1}}\right)^{2}\right)-2(\widetilde{b}-b)\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{n+1}}\frac{\gamma_{i}\gamma_{n+1-i}}{\gamma_{n+1}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[1+\frac{2(\widetilde{b}-b)^{2}(b^{2}-4)}{\Delta\gamma_{n+1}^{2}}\right]t^{-1}_{i,i}-\frac{(\widetilde{b}-b)(\gamma_{n+1}+(\widetilde{b}-b)\gamma_{n})}{\Delta\gamma^{3}_{n+1}}(\gamma_{i}^{2}+\gamma_{n+1-i}^{2}).

(3.1)

The trace formula (

\displaystyle\text{Tr}(\widetilde{T}^{-1}_{n})=\left[1+\frac{2(\widetilde{b}-b)^{2}(b^{2}-4)}{\Delta\gamma_{n+1}^{2}}\right]\sum_{i=1}^{n}t^{-1}_{i,i}-\frac{(\widetilde{b}-b)(\gamma_{n+1}+(\widetilde{b}-b)\gamma_{n})}{\Delta\gamma^{3}_{n+1}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\{\gamma_{i}^{2}+\gamma_{n+1-i}^{2}\right\}.

(3.2)

Lemma 3.3
.

For

\displaystyle\text{Tr}(T^{-1}_{n})=\frac{n+1}{\sqrt{b^{2}-4}}\left(1+\frac{2r_{2}^{n+1}}{\gamma_{n+1}}\right)-\frac{b}{b^{2}-4}.

Lemma 3.6

(Positivity)
.

Let

\widetilde{b}\geq 1

Proof.

For

Theorem 3.10

6
Conclusion

The objective of this work is to present the explicit or the exact formula of bounds for the norm of inverse of some tridiagonal near-Toeplitz matrices. In addition, we provide the value of the traces and row sums of the inverse matrices. We show bounds for
|b|>2
.
Theoretical results were validated by the numerical experiments. The findings suggest that these bounds could be useful for the fixed-point iterations in the convergence analysis.
Future research outcomes should explore scenarios where the Toeplitz part of tridiagonal near-Toeplitz matrices is weakly dominant, specifically when
|b|=2
.

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma
2.3

Using Lemma
2.1
,

\frac{\gamma_{n+1}-\gamma_{n}-\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{1}}=\frac{\gamma_{n+1}}{\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{1}}-1=b-1-\frac{\gamma_{n-1}+b\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{1}}.

Since
\gamma_{n}<b\gamma_{n-1}
(Lemma
2.1
),

\frac{\gamma_{n+1}-\gamma_{n}-\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{1}}<b-1-\frac{\gamma_{n-1}+b\gamma_{1}}{b\gamma_{n-1}+\gamma_{1}}=b-1-\frac{1}{b}\frac{\gamma_{n-1}+b\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{n-1}+\frac{1}{b}\gamma_{1}}<b-1-\frac{1}{b}<b-1,

for
b>2
.
Now, let’s have a look at

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{n-1}+b\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{1}}=\frac{\gamma_{n-1}+\gamma_{2}}{\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{n-1}_{1}-r^{n-1}_{2}+r^{2}_{1}-r^{2}_{2}}{r^{n}_{1}-r^{n}_{2}+r_{1}-r_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(r^{\frac{n+1}{2}}_{1}-r^{\frac{n+1}{2}}_{2}\right)\left(r^{\frac{n-3}{2}}_{1}+r^{\frac{n-3}{2}}_{2}\right)}{\left(r^{\frac{n+1}{2}}_{1}-r^{\frac{n+1}{2}}_{2}\right)\left(r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}_{1}+r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}_{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{\frac{n-3}{2}}_{1}+r^{\frac{n-3}{2}}_{2}}{r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}_{1}+r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}_{2}}.

Let’s look at the difference

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{n-1}+b\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{1}}-\frac{\gamma_{n}+b\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{n+1}+\gamma_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{\frac{n-3}{2}}_{1}+r^{\frac{n-3}{2}}_{2}}{r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}_{1}+r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}_{2}}-\frac{r^{\frac{n-2}{2}}_{1}+r^{\frac{n-2}{2}}_{2}}{r^{\frac{n}{2}}_{1}+r^{\frac{n}{2}}_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{\frac{3}{2}}_{1}+r^{\frac{3}{2}}_{2}-r^{\frac{1}{2}}_{1}-r^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2}}{\left(r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}_{1}+r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}_{2}\right)\left(r^{\frac{n}{2}}_{1}+r^{\frac{n}{2}}_{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}_{1}+r_{2}-r_{1}-1}{r^{\frac{1}{2}}_{1}\left(r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}_{1}+r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}_{2}\right)\left(r^{\frac{n}{2}}_{1}+r^{\frac{n}{2}}_{2}\right)}.

And note that

\displaystyle r^{2}_{1}+r_{2}-r_{1}-1

\displaystyle=\frac{b^{2}-2+b\sqrt{b^{2}-4}}{2}-\sqrt{b^{2}-4}-1

\displaystyle=\frac{b^{2}-4}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{b^{2}-4}}{2}(b-2)>0.

Therefore,

\frac{\gamma_{n-1}+b\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{1}}>\frac{\gamma_{n}+b\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{n+1}+\gamma_{1}},

which means

\displaystyle\max_{n\geq 3}{\frac{\gamma_{n-1}+b\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{1}}}=\frac{2\gamma_{2}}{\gamma_{3}+\gamma_{1}}=\frac{2\gamma_{2}}{b\gamma_{2}}=\frac{2}{b}.

Also, we note that

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{n-1}+b\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{1}}=\frac{r^{\frac{n-3}{2}}_{1}+r^{\frac{n-3}{2}}_{2}}{r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}_{1}+r^{\frac{n-1}{2}}_{2}}>\frac{r^{\frac{n-3}{2}}_{1}+r^{\frac{n-3}{2}}_{2}}{r_{1}\left(r^{\frac{n-3}{2}}_{1}+r^{\frac{n-3}{2}}_{2}\right)}=\frac{1}{r_{1}}=r_{2}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{n+1}-\gamma_{n}-\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{1}}=b-1-\frac{\gamma_{n-1}+b\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{1}}\geq b-1-\frac{2}{b}

and

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{n+1}-\gamma_{n}-\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{1}}<b-1-r_{2}=r_{1}-1.

Putting all together, we have

b-2<b-1-\frac{2}{b}\leq\frac{\gamma_{n+1}-\gamma_{n}-\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{1}}<r_{1}-1<b-1,

which can be simplified to get the lemma.

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{j}\gamma_{n+1-i}}{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{n+1}}-\beta\frac{\gamma_{n+1-i}(\gamma_{n+1-j}+\beta\gamma_{n-j})+\gamma_{i}(\gamma_{j}+\beta\gamma_{j-1})}{(\gamma_{n+1}+\beta\gamma_{n})^{2}-(\beta\gamma_{1})^{2}}.

(B.2)

Now, let us consider the following parts of the
\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}
, so we can simply further

\displaystyle P_{i,j}

\displaystyle:=\gamma_{j}((\gamma_{n+1}+\beta\gamma_{n})^{2}-(\beta\gamma_{1})^{2})-\beta\gamma_{1}\gamma_{n+1}(\gamma_{n+1-j}+\beta\gamma_{n-j})\quad\text{[using $\gamma^{2}_{n}-\gamma^{2}_{1}=\gamma_{n+1}\gamma_{n-1}$]}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{j}\gamma^{2}_{n+1}+\beta(2\gamma_{j}\gamma_{n}\gamma_{n+1}-\gamma_{1}\gamma_{n+1}\gamma_{n+1-j})+\beta^{2}(\gamma_{j}\gamma_{n+1}\gamma_{n-1}-\gamma_{1}\gamma_{n+1}\gamma_{n-j})

\displaystyle=\gamma_{n+1}\left[\gamma_{j}\gamma_{n+1}+\beta(2\gamma_{j}\gamma_{n}-\gamma_{1}\gamma_{n+1-j})+\beta^{2}(\gamma_{j}\gamma_{n-1}-\gamma_{1}\gamma_{n-j})\right]

\displaystyle=\gamma_{n+1}\left[\gamma_{j}\gamma_{n+1}+\beta(\gamma_{j}\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{n+1}\gamma_{j-1})+\beta^{2}\gamma_{n}\gamma_{j-1}\right].

The last equality is due to the previous shown identity
\gamma_{n}\gamma_{n+1-j}-\gamma_{1}\gamma_{j}=\gamma_{n+1}\gamma_{n-j}
with
j\rightarrow n+1-j
, so we get
\gamma_{j}\gamma_{n}-\gamma_{1}\gamma_{n+1-j}=\gamma_{n+1}\gamma_{j-1}
and substituting

\displaystyle Q_{i,j}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\gamma_{n+1-i}\cdot P_{i,j}}{\gamma_{n+1}}-\beta\gamma_{1}\gamma_{i}(\gamma_{j}+\beta\gamma_{j-1})

\displaystyle=\gamma_{n+1-i}\left[\gamma_{j}\gamma_{n+1}+\beta(\gamma_{j}\gamma_{n}+\gamma_{n+1}\gamma_{j-1})+\beta^{2}\gamma_{n}\gamma_{j-1}\right]-\beta\gamma_{1}\gamma_{i}(\gamma_{j}+\beta\gamma_{j-1})

\displaystyle=\gamma_{n+1-i}\gamma_{n+1}(\gamma_{j}+\beta\gamma_{j-1})+\beta\gamma_{j}(\gamma_{n+1-i}\gamma_{n}-\gamma_{1}\gamma_{i})+\beta^{2}\gamma_{j-1}(\gamma_{n+1-i}\gamma_{n}-\gamma_{1}\gamma_{i})

\displaystyle=\gamma_{n+1-i}\gamma_{n+1}(\gamma_{j}+\beta\gamma_{j-1})+\beta\gamma_{j}\gamma_{n+1}\gamma_{n-i}+\beta^{2}\gamma_{j-1}\gamma_{n+1}\gamma_{n-i}\quad\text{[by identity]}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{n+1-i}\gamma_{n+1}(\gamma_{j}+\beta\gamma_{j-1})+\beta\gamma_{n+1}\gamma_{n-i}(\gamma_{j}+\beta\gamma_{j-1})

\displaystyle=\gamma_{n+1}(\gamma_{n+1-i}+\beta\gamma_{n-i})(\gamma_{j}+\beta\gamma_{j-1}).

Therefore for

,

\displaystyle\widetilde{t}^{-1}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{n+1-i}}{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{n+1}((\gamma_{n+1}+\beta\gamma_{n})^{2}-(\beta\gamma_{1})^{2})}P_{i,j}-\frac{\beta\gamma_{i}(\gamma_{j}+\beta\gamma_{j-1})}{(\gamma_{n+1}+\beta\gamma_{n})^{2}-(\beta\gamma_{1})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{Q_{i,j}}{\gamma_{1}((\gamma_{n+1}+\beta\gamma_{n})^{2}-(\beta\gamma_{1})^{2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{n+1}}{\gamma_{1}((\gamma_{n+1}+\beta\gamma_{n})^{2}-(\beta\gamma_{1})^{2})}[\gamma_{n+1-i}+\beta\gamma_{n-i}][\gamma_{j}+\beta\gamma_{j-1}]

\displaystyle=C(\gamma_{n+1-i}+\beta\gamma_{n-i})(\gamma_{j}+\beta\gamma_{j-1})

(B.3)

where

.

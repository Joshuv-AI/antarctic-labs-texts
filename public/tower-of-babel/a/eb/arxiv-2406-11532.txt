Global well-posedness, scattering and blow-up for the energy-critical, Schrödinger equation with general nonlinearity in the radial case
Jun Wang, Zhaoyang Yin

Abstract
In this paper, we study the well-posedness theory and the scattering asymptotics for the energy-critical, Schrödinger equation with general nonlinearity { i ∂ t u + Δ u + f ( u ) = 0 , ( x , t ) ∈ ℝ N × ℝ , u | t = 0 = u 0 ∈ H 1 ​ ( ℝ N ) , \left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+f(u)=0,\ (x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R},\\ \left.u\right|_{t=0}=u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\end{array}\right. where f : ℂ → ℂ f:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C} satisfies Sobolev critical growth condition. Using contraction mapping method and concentration compactness argument, we obtain the well-posedness theory in proper function spaces and scattering asymptotics. This paper generalizes the conclusions in [ 17 ] (Invent. Math).

Introduction and main results

This paper studies the well-posedness theory and the scattering asymptotics for the energy-critical, focusing, Schrödinger equation with general nonlinearity

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u+f(u)=0,\ (x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R},\\
\left.u\right|_{t=0}=u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
f:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfies Sobolev critical growth condition.

In
[
17
]
, Kenig and Merle studied the
\dot{H}^{1}
critical non-linear Schrödinger equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u\pm|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R},\\
\left.u\right|_{t=0}=u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).\end{array}\right.

Here the
-
sign corresponds to the defocusing problem, while the
+
sign corresponds to the focusing problem. They obtained the global well-posedness, scattering and blow-up results in the radial case and
3\leq N\leq 5
. Recently, Oh and Wang in
[
21

considered global well-posedness for one-dimensional cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation by introducing a new function space. For
N=2
,
[
6

established global well-posedness results in the defocusing case, posed on the two-dimensional unit ball. For high dimensions case, Tao et.al in
[
22
]
established global well-posedness and scattering for solutions to the defocusing mass-critical nonlinear Schrödinger equation. For more well-posedness results, please refer to

Unlike other articles on well-posedness and scattering, this paper presents for the first time results on general critical nonlinear terms. This idea mainly comes from the variational method. In recent decades, many researchers have been interested in semi-linear Schrödinger problems with general nonlinear terms. From a mathematical perspective, it is very meaningful. In order to obtain the solution of the equation, it is necessary to impose appropriate conditions on the nonlinear term. The most classic condition is the Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz(AR) condition:

\text{There exists }\mu>2\text{ such that }G(t)\geq\mu g(t)>0\text{ for all }t>0\text{, }

where
G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(s)ds
. As is well known, (AR) condition plays a crucial role in getting a bounded (PS)-sequence, so we can use the Nehari manifold method to obtain the solution of the equation. As we can see, the (AR) condition ensures that nonlinearity has good properties. However, we use another weaker Berestycki-Lions(BL) condition than (AR) in this paper, namely

\text{There exists }\xi>0\text{ such that }G(\xi)>0.

As mentioned in
[
2
]
, under the (BL) condition, we can use Pohozaev manifold to obtain the existence of standing wave solutions(even the existence of infinite solutions), which is crucial for obtaining variational estimates in Section 6.

Now, we give the most general assumption of growth, i.e., assume that
f:\ \mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is
C^{1}
and satisfies:

(F_{1})

\lim\limits_{|z|\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{|f(z)\overline{z}|}{|z|^{2}}=0
.

(F_{2})

\limsup\limits_{|z|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|f(z)\overline{z}|}{|z|^{2^{*}}}<\infty
, where
2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
.

(F_{3})

\lim\limits_{|\nabla z|\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{|\nabla(f(z))|}{|\nabla z|}=0
.

(F_{4})

\limsup\limits_{|\nabla z|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|\nabla(f(z))|}{|z|^{2^{*}-2}||\nabla z|}<\infty
.

Remark 1.1
.

By
(F_{1})
,
(F_{2})
,
(F_{3})
,
(F_{4})
, there exist
\varepsilon,\ C>0
such that

\frac{|f(z)\overline{z}|}{|z|^{2}}<\varepsilon,\ \frac{|\nabla(f(z))|}{|\nabla z|}<\varepsilon

and

\frac{|f(z)\overline{z}|}{|z|^{2^{*}}}<C,\ \frac{|\nabla(f(z))|}{|z|^{2^{*}-2}||\nabla z|}<C.

Hence, it follows from

that there exist
C_{1},\ C_{2},\ C_{3},\ C_{4}>0
such that

|f(z)|\leq C_{1}|z|+C_{2}|z|^{2^{*}-1},\ |\nabla(f(z))|\leq C_{3}|\nabla z|+C_{4}|z|^{2^{*}-2}|\nabla z|.

Remark 1.2
.

Let
f(u)=|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u+|u|^{\frac{2}{N-2}}u
, then clearly

|f(u)|\leq|u|^{\frac{N+2}{N-2}}+|u|^{\frac{N}{N-2}},\left|\partial_{z}f(u)\right|\leq C|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}+|u|^{\frac{2}{N-2}},\left|\partial_{\bar{z}}f(u)\right|\leq C|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}+|u|^{\frac{2}{N-2}}.

Moreover, for
3\leq N\leq 4
,

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\left|\partial_{z}f(u)-\partial_{z}f(v)\right|\\
\left|\partial_{\bar{z}}f(u)-\partial_{\bar{z}}f(v)\right|\end{array}\right\}\leq C|u-v|\cdot\left\{|u|^{\frac{6-N}{N-2}}+|v|^{\frac{6-N}{N-2}}+|u|^{\frac{4-N}{N-2}}+|v|^{\frac{4-N}{N-2}}\right\}.

Also,

|f(u)-f(v)|\leq|u-v|\left\{|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}+|v|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}+|u|^{\frac{2}{N-2}}+|v|^{\frac{2}{N-2}}\right\}.

In addition,

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}(f(u(x)))-\nabla_{x}(f(v(x)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\nabla f)(u(x))\nabla u-(\nabla f)(v(x))\nabla v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\nabla f)(u(x))\nabla u-(\nabla f)(u(x))\nabla v+\{\nabla f(u(x)))-\nabla f(v(x))\}\nabla v,

so

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{x}f(u(x))-\nabla_{x}f(v(x))\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}|\nabla u-\nabla v|+C|\nabla v|\left\{|u|^{\frac{6-N}{N-2}}+|v|^{\frac{6-N}{N-2}}\right\}|u-v|

\displaystyle+C|u|^{\frac{2}{N-2}}|\nabla u-\nabla v|+C|\nabla v|\left\{|u|^{\frac{6-N}{N-2}}+|v|^{\frac{4-N}{N-2}}\right\}|u-v|.

First, we get the existence and uniqueness of solution to (

Lemma 3.1

and

4
Blow up solutions

In this section, we will investigate the blow up solutions of (
1.1
). Let
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be radial and satisfy

\psi(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{2}r^{2},&\text{ for }r\leqslant 1\\
0,&\text{ for }r\geqslant 2\end{array}\quad\text{ and }\quad\psi^{\prime\prime}(r)\leqslant 1\quad\text{ for }r=|x|\geqslant 0.\right.

For a fixed
R>0
, we define the rescaled function
\psi_{R}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by setting

\psi_{R}(r):=R^{2}\psi\left(\frac{r}{R}\right).

(4.1)

Next we will show that

1-\psi_{R}^{\prime\prime}(r)\geqslant 0,\quad 1-\frac{\psi_{R}^{\prime}(r)}{r}\geqslant 0,\quad N-\Delta\psi_{R}(r)\geq 0\quad\text{ for all }r\geqslant 0.

(4.2)

Indeed, this first inequality follows from
\psi_{R}^{\prime\prime}(r)=\psi^{\prime\prime}(\frac{r}{R})\leq 1
. We obtain the second inequality by integrating the first inequality on
[0,r]
and using that
\psi_{R}^{\prime}(0)=0
. Finally, we see that last inequality follows from

N-\Delta\psi_{R}(r)=1-\psi_{R}^{\prime\prime}(r)+(N-1)\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\psi_{R}^{\prime}(r)\right)\geqslant 0.

Besides (
4.2
),
\psi_{R}
admits the following properties, which can be easily checked.
We define

\mathcal{M}_{\psi_{R}}[u(t)]:=2\mathrm{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\overline{u}(t)\nabla\psi_{R}\cdot\nabla u(t)dx=2\mathrm{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\overline{u}(t)\partial_{j}\psi_{R}\partial_{j}u(t)dx.

Define the self-adjoint differential operator

\Gamma_{\psi_{R}}:=i\left(\nabla\cdot\nabla\psi_{R}+\nabla\psi_{R}\cdot\nabla\right),

which acts on functions according to

\Gamma_{\psi_{R}}f=i\left(\nabla\cdot\left(\left(\nabla\psi_{R}\right)f\right)+\left(\nabla\psi_{R}\right)\cdot(\nabla f)\right).

It’s easy to check that

\mathcal{M}_{\psi_{R}}[u(t)]=\left\langle u(t),\Gamma_{\psi_{R}}u(t)\right\rangle.

Next, we show the following useful lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
N\geq 3
, and
u\in H_{\text{rad }}^{1}
is a solution of (
1.1
). Let
\psi_{R}
be as in (
4.1
),
T^{*}
be the maximal existence time of solution
u(t)
in
H_{\text{rad }}^{1}
. Then for sufficiently large
R
, it holds

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{M}_{\psi_{R}}[u(t)]\leq 8E(u(t)),\quad t\in\left[0,T^{*}\right).

Proof.

By taking the derivative of
\mathcal{M}_{\psi_{R}}[u(t)]
with respect to time
t
and using the equation of
u(t)
, for any
t\in[0,T)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{M}_{\psi_{R}}[u(t)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle u(t),\left[-\Delta,i\Gamma_{\psi_{R}}\right]u(t)\right\rangle+\left\langle-f(u),i\Gamma_{\psi_{R}}u(t)\right\rangle+\left\langle u(t),i\Gamma_{\psi_{R}}f(u)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3},

where
[X,Y]\equiv XY-YX
denotes the commutator of operators
X
and
Y
.
According to the localized radial virial estimate in
[
5
]
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle u(t),\left[-\Delta,i\Gamma_{\psi_{R}}\right]u(t)\right\rangle\leq 8\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+CR^{-2},

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle-f(u),i\Gamma_{\psi_{R}}u(t)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle f(u),\left(\nabla\cdot\left(\left(\nabla\psi_{R}\right)u\right)+\left(\nabla\psi_{R}\right)\cdot(\nabla u)\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi_{R}\cdot\nabla\left(F(u)\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\Delta\psi_{R}\right)F(u)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2(N+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u)dx-2\int_{|x|\geq R}(\Delta\psi_{R}-N)F(u)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-2(N+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u)dx+2\int_{|x|\geq R}(N-\Delta\psi_{R})(\varepsilon|u|^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}|u|^{2^{*}})dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-2(N+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u)dx+2CR^{-2}\|N-\Delta\psi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(|x|\geq R)}^{2},

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle u(t),i\Gamma_{\psi_{R}}f(u)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle u(t),\left(\nabla\cdot\left(\left(\nabla\psi_{R}\right)f(u)\right)+\left(\nabla\psi_{R}\right)\cdot(\nabla f(u))\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi_{R}\cdot\nabla\left(F(u)\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\Delta\psi_{R}\right)F(u)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2(N+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u)dx-2\int_{|x|\geq R}(\Delta\psi_{R}-N)F(u)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-2(N+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u)dx+2C\int_{|x|\geq R}(N-\Delta\psi_{R})(|u|^{2}+|u|^{2^{*}})dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-2(N+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u)dx+2CR^{-2}\|N-\Delta\psi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(|x|\geq R)}^{2},

Therefore,

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{M}_{\psi_{R}}[u(t)]\leq 8\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+CR^{-2}-4(N+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u)dx+4CR^{-2}\|N-\Delta\psi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(|x|\geq R)}^{2},

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
R
. When
R>1
is sufficiently large, then

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{M}_{\psi_{R}}[u(t)]\leq 8E(u(t))=8E(u_{0}).

∎

1.2
.

The next lemma is a localized virial identity about general nonlinear equation. The proof idea comes from Merle

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),t\in\left[0,T_{+}\left(u_{0}\right)\right)
. Then:

i)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\psi dx=2\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bar{u}\nabla u\nabla\psi dx

ii)

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\psi dx=4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla|u|^{2}\nabla(\Delta\psi)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi[F(u)-f(u)\bar{u}]dx.

Proof.

By (
1.1
) and direct calculation, we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\operatorname{Re}\psi\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\bar{u}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\operatorname{Re}\left[i\Delta u+if(u)\right]\bar{u}\psi dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta u\bar{u}\psi dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla u\bar{u}\nabla\psi dx

because of
\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla u\overline{\nabla u}\psi dx=0
and part (i) follows.

Using (i), we know that

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left[\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\bar{u}\nabla\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}dx+\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\frac{\overline{\partial u}}{\partial t}\nabla udx\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left[2\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\frac{\overline{\partial u}}{\partial t}\nabla udx-\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi\bar{u}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}dx\right].

On the one hand,

\displaystyle-\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi\bar{u}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi\bar{u}\left(\Delta u+f(u)\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi f(u)\bar{u}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla|u|^{2}\nabla(\Delta\psi)dx.

On the other hand, note that

\displaystyle-2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\Delta u\overline{\nabla u}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi\nabla u\overline{\nabla u}dx+2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\overline{\Delta u}\nabla udx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi\nabla u\overline{\nabla u}dx+2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\overline{\Delta u\overline{\nabla u}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi\nabla u\overline{\nabla u}dx+2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\Delta u\overline{\nabla u}dx,

so

-2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\Delta u\overline{\nabla u}dx=\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi\nabla u\overline{\nabla u}dx.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\frac{\overline{\partial u}}{\partial t}\nabla udx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\overline{\nabla u}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\Delta u\overline{\nabla u}dx-2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi f(u)\overline{\nabla u}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi\nabla u\overline{\nabla u}dx-2\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi f(u)\overline{\nabla u}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi F(u)dx.

Combining the above two estimates, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\psi(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left[2\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla\psi\frac{\overline{\partial u}}{\partial t}\nabla udx-\operatorname{Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi\bar{u}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}dx\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi F(u)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi f(u)\bar{u}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi|\nabla u|^{2}dx\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla|u|^{2}\nabla(\Delta\psi)dx\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla|u|^{2}\nabla(\Delta\psi)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\psi[F(u)-f(u)\bar{u}]dx,

which implies that the conclusion is valid.
∎

Lindblad evolutions, Born rule, Heralding and cloning
A. Auerbach, J. Avron, D. Gershoni, N. Tur

Abstract
We discuss an apparent difficulty in computing the radiation emitted by a system undergoing Lindblad evolution. The difficulty is resolved by viewing the problem as Born rule for conserved currents defined by the appropriate terms in the adjoint Lindbladian. In Heralding Alice prepares Bob’s system in a state that mirrors her test. We show that heralding is consistent with no-cloning. This follows from the observation that heralding is not a completely positive map.

The problem and its resolution

It is a common practice to model simple radiating systems, such as atoms or a quantum dots, by a finite dimensional system, whose quantum state
\rho
evolves according to a suitable Lindblad operator
[
1
,
5
,
8
]
. The radiation field (and possibly other degrees of freedom) are modeled by suitable “jump operators” in the Lindbladian.
In the special case of a 2-level system the photo-emission is found to be proportional to

Tr\Big(\rho\left|1\right\rangle\!\left\langle 1\right|\Big)

(1.1)

where
\left|1\right\rangle\!\left\langle 1\right|
projects on the excited, i.e. radiating, energy level.

There are several difficulties with this formula. First,
\rho
is a Markovian approximation to the partial trace of the joint system and radiation:

\rho=Tr_{r}\,\rho_{sr},\quad\rho\in\mathbf{S},\ \rho_{sr}\in\mathbf{S}\otimes\mathbf{R}

(1.2)

As the partial trace erases the information regarding the radiation degrees of freedom, it is surprising that one can extract information on the radiation from knowledge of the reduced density matrix of the system.

Second, Eq.
1.1
has the form of Born rule for the detecting the excited state. This is puzzling: By the rules of quantum mechanics, a measurement prepares the system
1
1

1

A single shot does not reveal the quantum state prior to the measurement, which requires tomography, and hence many copies.
. Since the detection of the radiation prepared the system in the ground state the Born rule should have been
Tr\,\big(\rho\left|0\right\rangle\!\left\langle 0\right|\big)
rather than Eq.
1.1
. This, however, leads to the patently absurd result that the system in the ground state radiates.

These difficulties suggest that the observable
\left|1\right\rangle\!\left\langle 1\right|
in Eq.
1.1
has an alternate interpretation that agrees with the Born rule. This interpretation should also explains how come measurements performed on the radiation can be determined from the reduced density matrix of the system.

The protection of information comes from conservation laws. Indeed, the reduced density matrix
\rho
carries no information on the radiation, however, there is such information in
\dot{\rho}
. The conserved quantity is the ‘‘total excitation number” which is exact constant of motion in the rotation wave approximation. It says that the sum of the excitation and photon number is conserved. It is, of course, related to energy conservation
2
2

2

It is equivalent to energy conservation if one neglects the energy associated with the coupling of the systems. This is a good approximation in the long time limit, when the systems separate.
. In general, one may have to consider also other conservation laws, such as conservation of angular momentum. We discuss this in section
2.1
.

Due to the conservation law of total excitation, the (outgoing) radiation current and the (incoming) excitation current, balance the population rate of the corresponding energy level. In section
2
, we construct the
system observable
corresponding to the contributions of radiation to the population rate. In the case of a two-level system it is given by either:

\mu D^{*}(A,\left|0\right\rangle\!\left\langle 0\right|)=-\mu D^{*}(A,\left|1\right\rangle\!\left\langle 1\right|)

(1.3)

\mu D^{*}
is the radiative term in the adjoint Lindbladian
L^{*}
, see Eq.
2.8
below.
A
is the radiation jump operator, see Eq.
2.4
, and
\mu
the decay rate. The two sides of the equation represent the population rate of the ground state and minus the population rate of the excited state. The formula is independent of the choice of basis for representing the jump operator
A
and the projection
\left|0\right\rangle\!\left\langle 0\right|
.

The situation in a two level system is particularly simple. Inserting the jump operator, Eq.
2.4
, in Eq.
1.3
gives

\mu D^{*}(A,\left|0\right\rangle\!\left\langle 0\right|)=\mu\left|1\right\rangle\!\left\langle 1\right|

(1.4)

The radiation current happens to be proportional to the projection on the radiating level. Eq.
1.1
is therefore
not
the Born rule for the detection of the excited state, but the Born rule for the contribution of radiation to the population rate of the ground state (or its minus for the excited state).

It may well be that neither Eq.
1.3
nor its interpretation are new. But, we did not find either in standard textbooks

Heralding is consistent with no cloning

The ability to prepare a quantum system remotely is the discovery of EPR
[
3
]
. It is the standard way of preparing a radiating system, also known as heralding
[
6
]
. Heralding is an important resource of quantum technologies.

In the simplest heralding scheme, Alice and Bob start with a shared maximally entangle (Bell) state. By testing her qubit, Alice prepares her qubit in the state
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}
, and prepares Bob’s qubit in a ”mirror” state
\left|\bar{\psi}\right\rangle
(see Eq.
3.5
below). Since this creates two ”mirror” images of the a-priori arbitrary state
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}
, one wonders if heralding violates no-cloning
[
9
]
.

\rho_{B}

The basic primitive for transmission of quantum information is a quantum channel
[

,

]
:

\rho_{B}=\Phi(\rho_{A}).

(3.1)

\Phi
is, by definition, a completely positive trace preserving linear map (CPTP)
[

.
Herlading is not a completely positive map and hence can not be interpreted as a quantum channel. We shall use this fact to reconcile heralding with no-cloning.

Consider a Bell state shared by Alice and Bob

\sqrt{2}\left|\beta\right\rangle_{AB}=\left|00\right\rangle_{AB}+\left|11\right\rangle_{AB}.

(3.2)

Alice makes a successful test of

\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}=\psi_{0}\left|0\right\rangle_{A}+\psi_{1}\left|1\right\rangle_{A}.

(3.3)

This heralds Bob the “mirror” state

\sqrt{2}\langle\psi|\beta\rangle_{B}=\bar{\psi}_{0}\left|0\right\rangle_{B}+\bar{\psi}_{1}\left|1\right\rangle_{B}=\left|\bar{\psi}\right\rangle_{B},

(3.4)

where bar denotes complex conjugation. The mapping of the Alice’s vector to Bob’s vector

\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}\mapsto\left|\bar{\psi}\right\rangle_{B}

(3.5)

translates to mapping of Alice’s state to Bob’s state:

\Big(\left|\psi\right\rangle\!\left\langle\psi\right|\Big)_{A}=\begin{pmatrix}|\psi_{0}|^{2}&\psi_{0}\bar{\psi}_{1}\\
\psi_{1}\bar{\psi}_{0}&|\psi_{1}|^{2}\end{pmatrix}_{A}\mapsto\Big(\left|\bar{\psi}\right\rangle\!\left\langle\bar{\psi}\right|\Big)_{B}=\begin{pmatrix}|\psi_{0}|^{2}&\bar{\psi}_{0}\psi_{1}\\
\bar{\psi}_{1}\psi_{0}&|\psi_{1}|^{2}\end{pmatrix}_{B}\ .

(3.6)

The map
\Phi
is transposition

\rho_{B}=\Phi(\rho_{A})=\rho^{T}_{A}\ .

(3.7)

Transposition acts on the Bloch ball as reflection
Y\mapsto-Y
. It is the canonical example of positive map that is not completely positive
[
5
,
7
]
.

There is no unitary, independent of
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}
, that Bob can apply to his state to recover Alice’s state. This can be seen by observing that reflection does not preserve the Pauli operators commutation relations. Therefore, it can’t be implemented as a unitary
4
4

4

It can be implemented by an anti-unitary.
. Bob can not make a precise copy of
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}
without additional information.
Of course, if Alice gives Bob classical information about her test
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}
, for example, the protocol for making
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}
, Bob could (probabilistically) reproduce
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}
without using the available entanglement resource.

The inability of Bob to recover Alice’s state is a health certificate. For, if Bob could use heralding to create a copy of
\left|\psi\right\rangle_{A}
, this would be a trap-door to cloning
[
9

Lindblad evolution of a two level system

The Lindbladian in Eq.

admits an elementary solution of the equations of motion. Write
\rho\geq 0
as

\rho=\frac{\mathds{1}+xX+yY+zZ}{2},\quad x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}\leq 1

with

the three Pauli matrices. The Lindblad equation of motion takes the from

\displaystyle\dot{x}

\displaystyle=-\beta\,x-\epsilon y,

\displaystyle\dot{y}

\displaystyle=\epsilon x-\beta y,

(A.2)

\displaystyle\dot{z}

\displaystyle=-\lambda+\mu-(\lambda+\mu)\,z

with

\beta=\frac{2\delta+\lambda+\mu}{2}\geq 0

The motion along the z axis describes exponential relaxation to the stationary value

The equation of motion in the x-y plane can be combined to

\dot{\zeta}=(i\epsilon-\beta)\,\zeta,\quad\zeta=x+iy

whose solution describes an exponential spiral towards the axis

\zeta(t)=\zeta_{0}e^{(i\epsilon-\beta))t}\ .

(A.6)

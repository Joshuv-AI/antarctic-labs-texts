Strong anomalous proximity effect from spin-singlet superconductors Preprint: APS/123-QED
Satoshi Ikegaya 1,2 , Jaechul Lee 3 , Andreas P. Schnyder 1 , and Yasuhiro Asano 3

Abstract
The proximity effect from a spin-triplet p x p_{x} -wave superconductor to a dirty normal-metal has been shown to result in various unusual electromagnetic properties, reflecting a cooperative relation between topologically protected zero-energy quasiparticles and odd-frequency Cooper pairs. However, because of a lack of candidate materials for spin-triplet p x p_{x} -wave superconductors, observing this effect has been difficult. In this paper, we demonstrate that the anomalous proximity effect, which is essentially equivalent to that of a spin-triplet p x p_{x} -wave superconductor, can occur in a semiconductor/high- T c T_{c} cuprate superconductor hybrid device in which two potentials coexist: a spin-singlet d d -wave pair potential and a spin–orbit coupling potential sustaining the persistent spin-helix state. As a result, we propose an alternative and promising route to observe the anomalous proximity effect related to the profound nature of topologically protected quasiparticles and odd-frequency Cooper pairs.



I
Introduction

When a superconductor (SC) is attached to a normal-metal, Cooper pairs (CPs) penetrate into the attached normal segment and modify the electromagnetic properties there.
This phenomenon is known as the proximity effect and has been a central research subject in the field of superconductivity.
When we consider a conventional spin-singlet
s
-wave SC, the attached normal-metal exhibits superconducting-like electromagnetic properties.
However, the proximity effect from a spin-triplet
p_{x}
-wave SC to a dirty normal-metal (DN) has been shown to result in various counter-intuitive transport properties
such as the quantization of zero-bias conductance (ZBC) in DN/SC junctions

;
tanaka_05(1)
;
asano_07
;
ikegaya_15
;
ikegaya_16(1)

ikegaya_16(2)

Moreover, although the spin-triplet
p_{x}
-wave SC shows a diamagnetic response, the magnetic response in the attached DN is reversed to paramagnetic
asano_11

Such a drastic proximity effect of a spin-triplet
p_{x}
-wave SC has been referred to as an anomalous proximity effect (APE)

The APE of spin-triplet
p_{x}
-wave SCs has attracted intensive attention because its mechanism is related to two particles clad in novel concepts:
a topologically-protected zero-energy quasiparticle and an odd-frequency CP.

The unusual transport properties are a direct consequence of the resonant tunneling of quasiparticles via such topologically protected ZESs in the DN.
Moreover, it has been shown that the ZESs penetrated from a spin-triplet

The APE is a remarkable phenomenon related to the intrinsic natures of topologically protected zero-energy quasiparticles and odd-frequency CPs.
Thus, experimental observations of this effect are an important topic in the physics of superconductivity.

The main difficulty in observing the APE is a serious lack of candidate materials for spin-triplet

Thus far, several theoretical models for effective

To resolve this stalemate, in this Letter, we explore an alternative route to observing the APE.

II
Model

We describe the present system using a 2D tight-binding model.
A lattice site is indicated by a vector
\bm{r}=j\bm{x}+m\bm{y}
, where
\bm{x}
(
\bm{y}
) is a unit vector in the
x
(
y
) direction.
The system comprises three segments: a semi-infinite lead wire (ballistic semiconductor segment) for
-\infty\leq j<1
,
a DN segment (dirty semiconductor segment) for
1\leq j\leq L
,
and a semi-infinite SC segment (ballistic semiconductor segment with a proximity-induced pair potential) for
L<j<\infty
.
In the
y
direction, the number of lattice sites is given by
W
and a periodic boundary condition is applied.
The Bogoliubov–de Gennes (BdG) Hamiltonian reads
H=H_{\mathrm{kin}}+H_{\mathrm{PSH}}+H_{\Delta}+H_{\mathrm{v}}
.
The kinetic energy is given by

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{kin}}=

\displaystyle-t\sum_{\langle\bm{r},\bm{r}^{\prime}\rangle}\sum_{\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow}(c_{\bm{r},\sigma}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{r}^{\prime},\sigma}+c_{\bm{r}^{\prime},\sigma}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{r},\sigma})

\displaystyle+(4t-\mu)\sum_{\bm{r},\sigma}c_{\bm{r},\sigma}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{r},\sigma},

(1)

where
c_{\bm{r},\sigma}^{\dagger}
(
c_{\bm{r},\sigma}
) is the creation (annihilation) operator for an electron at
\bm{r}
with spin
\sigma
;

t
and
\mu
denote the nearest-neighbor hopping integral and chemical potential, respectively.
The PSH is characterized by a unidirectional SOC potential given by

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{PSH}}=\frac{i\lambda}{2}\sum_{\bm{r},\sigma}s_{\sigma}(c_{\bm{r}+\bm{y},\sigma}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{r},\sigma}-c_{\bm{r},\sigma}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{r}+\bm{y},\sigma}),

(2)

where
s_{\uparrow(\downarrow)}=+1(-1)
.
The SOC potential in Eq. (
2
) describes a Dresselhaus[110] SOC potential realized in
zinc-blende III–V semiconductor quantum wells grown along the
[110]
direction
bernevig_06
;
chang_06
;
salis_14
.
The equivalent SOC potentials can also be obtained in quantum wells in which
Rashba and Dresselhaus[100] SOC potentials have equal amplitudes
bernevig_06
;
chang_06
;
awschalom_09
;
salis_12
;
kohda_12

and in ferroelectric thin-film materials
tsymbal_18
;
ishii_19
;
ishii_19(2)
;
picozzi_19
;
zhao_19
;
jin_20
;
nardelli_20
;
chang_20
.
The proximity-induced spin-singlet
d_{xy}
-wave pair potential is given by

\displaystyle H_{\Delta}=

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta}{4}\sum_{j=L+1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{W}(c_{\bm{r}+\bm{x}+\bm{y},\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{r},\downarrow}^{\dagger}+c_{\bm{r},\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{r}+\bm{x}+\bm{y},\downarrow}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle-c_{\bm{r}+\bm{x}-\bm{y},\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{r},\downarrow}^{\dagger}-c_{\bm{r},\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{r}+\bm{x}-\bm{y},\downarrow}^{\dagger})+\mathrm{H.c.},

(3)

where
\Delta
denotes the amplitude of the pair potential.
The disorder potential in the DN segment is described by

\displaystyle H_{v}=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\sum_{m=1}^{W}\sum_{\sigma}v(\bm{r})c_{\bm{r},\sigma}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{r},\sigma},

(4)

where
v(\bm{r})
is given randomly in the range
-X\leq v(\bm{r})\leq X
.

In the following numerical calculations, we fix the parameters as
\mu=t
,
\lambda=0.5t
,
\Delta=0.1t
,
L=50
, and
W=50
.
For simplicity, we assume that
t
,
\mu
, and
\lambda
are uniform in the entire system.
For the ensemble average, 500 samples are used.
To observe the APE experimentally, the thermal coherence length
\xi_{T}=\sqrt{\hbar D/2\pi k_{B}T}
must be longer than the length of the DN segment,
where
T
and
D
represent the temperature and the diffusion constant in the DN segment, respectively.
For simplicity, we assume zero temperature in the following calculations.

III.1
Anomalous proximity effect

We first focus on the differential conductance in the present device.
Within the Blonder–Tinkham–Klapwijk formalism

(5)

where

III.2
Index theorem

We here discuss an Atiyah–Singer index theorem that characterizes the APE in the present junction.
To evaluate the topological property of the SC segment, we remove the DN segment from the system and apply a periodic boundary in the
x
direction.
Moreover, for simplicity, we describe the present SC in continuous space.
Consequently, the BdG Hamiltonian in momentum space is given by

\displaystyle H(\bm{k})=\xi(\bm{k})\sigma_{0}\tau_{z}+\lambda k_{y}\hat{\sigma}_{z}\tau_{0}-\Delta(\bm{k})\sigma_{y}\tau_{y},

(10)

where
\xi(\bm{k})=(\hbar^{2}k^{2}/2m)-\mu
with
m
representing the effective mass of an electron,

\Delta(\bm{k})=\Delta(k_{x}k_{y}/k^{2}_{F})
with
k_{F}=\sqrt{2m\mu}/\hbar
representing the Fermi wavenumber,

\sigma_{\alpha}
(
\tau_{\alpha}
) for
\alpha=x,y,z
are the Pauli matrices acting on spin (Nambu) space, and
\sigma_{0}
(
\tau_{0}
) is the unit matrix in spin (Nambu) space.
The BdG Hamiltonian
H(\bm{k})
intrinsically preserves particle–hole symmetry as
CH(\bm{k})C^{-1}=-H(-\bm{k})
,
where
C=\tau_{x}{\cal K}
with
{\cal K}
representing the complex conjugation operator.
We also find time-reversal symmetry as
T_{-}H(\bm{k})T_{-}^{-1}=H(-\bm{k})
with
T_{-}=i\sigma_{y}\tau_{0}{\cal K}
.
Because
C^{2}=+1
and
T_{-}^{2}=-1
, the BdG Hamiltonian belongs to the DIII symmetry class
schnyder_08
.
Importantly, because of the nature of the PSH
bernevig_06
,
the BdG Hamiltonian preserves spin-rotation symmetry along the
z
axis even in the presence of the SOC potential:

R_{z}H(\bm{k})R_{z}^{-1}=H(\bm{k})
with
R_{z}=\sigma_{z}\tau_{z}
.
By combining
T_{-}
and
R_{z}
, we obtain
T_{+}H(\bm{k})T_{+}^{-1}=H(-\bm{k})
,
where
T_{+}=R_{z}T_{-}
represents an additional time-reversal symmetry obeying
T_{+}^{2}=+1
.
Because
C^{2}=+1
and
T_{+}^{2}=+1
, we find that
H(\bm{k})
can be simultaneously classified into the BDI symmetry class
schnyder_08
.
The energy spectrum of
H(\bm{k})
is given by

E_{s_{\sigma}}(\bm{k})=\pm\sqrt{\left\{\xi(\bm{k})+s_{\sigma}\lambda k_{y}\right\}^{2}+\Delta^{2}(\bm{k})}
.
The branch of
E_{s_{\sigma}}(\bm{k})
exhibits four superconducting gap nodes at

(k_{x},k_{y})=(\pm k_{F},0)
and
(0,\pm k_{\lambda}-s_{\sigma}\bar{\lambda})
, where

\displaystyle k_{\lambda}=\sqrt{k_{F}^{2}+\bar{\lambda}^{2}},\quad\bar{\lambda}=m\lambda/\hbar^{2}.

(11)

On the basis of the Atiyah–Singer index theorem
ikegaya_15
;
ikegaya_16(1)
;
ikegaya_18
;
ikegaya_17
,
a topological index that characterizes the number of zero-energy states at a
dirty
surface of a nodal SC is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}={\sum_{k_{y}}}^{\prime}w_{\mathrm{BDI}}(k_{y}),

(12)

\displaystyle w_{\mathrm{BDI}}(k_{y})=\frac{i}{4\pi}\int dk_{x}\mathrm{Tr}[S_{+}\left\{H(\bm{k})\right\}^{-1}\partial_{k_{x}}H(\bm{k})],

(13)

where
S_{+}=iT_{+}C=-\sigma_{x}\tau_{y}
represents the chiral symmetry operator with respect to the BDI symmetry class
and
\sum_{k_{y}}^{\prime}
denotes a summation over
k_{y}
excluding the nodal points.
Using Eq. (
13
), we obtain

\displaystyle w_{\mathrm{BDI}}(k_{y})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1&\text{for}\quad k_{\lambda}-\bar{\lambda}<|k_{y}|<k_{\lambda}+\bar{\lambda}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.,

and therefore
\mathbb{Z}=\sum_{k_{\lambda}-\bar{\lambda}<|k_{y}|<k_{\lambda}+\bar{\lambda}}
.
When the momentum
k_{y}
is assumed to be a continuous variable, the discrete summation of
k_{y}
is replaced with the integration as

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{y}}\rightarrow\frac{W}{2\pi}\int dk_{y}.

(16)

Using Eq. (
16
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}=\left[(\lambda k_{F}/2\mu)N_{c}\right]_{\mathrm{G}},

(17)

where
[\cdots]_{\mathrm{G}}
is the Gauss symbol giving the integer part of the argument, and
N_{c}=2Wk_{F}/\pi
,
where
[N_{c}]_{\mathrm{G}}
represents the number of propagating channels.
The index
\mathbb{Z}
becomes finite only in the presence of the PSH (
\lambda\neq 0
).
According to the Atiyah–Singer index theorem
ikegaya_15
;
ikegaya_16(1)
;
ikegaya_18
;
ikegaya_17
,
the
|\mathbb{Z}|
ZESs can robustly remain at zero-energy even in the presence of potential disorders;
they can therefore penetrate into the attached DN while retaining their
|\mathbb{Z}|
-fold degeneracy
ikegaya_15
;
ikegaya_16(1)
.
In the presence of the chiral symmetry of
S_{+}
, each ZES can form a perfect Andreev reflection channel at zero-energy
ikegaya_16(1)
;
fulga_11
,
which explains the ZBC quantization in Eq. (
6
).

We here note that the number of stable ZESs in typical
fully gapped
topological SCs is limited to a few.
However, the present hybrid system can host the ZESs with a high degree of degeneracy at zero-energy.
For instance, when we assume that
\mu=2\mathrm{meV}
,
\lambda=10\mathrm{meV}\mathrm{nm}
, and
m=0.07m_{e}
,
where
m_{e}
is the electron rest mass, we obtain
\mathbb{Z}\approx[0.128N_{c}]_{\mathrm{G}}
,
which means that approximately
10\%
of the propagating channels contribute to the resonant transmission.
We can therefore reasonably expect a drastic signature of the APE, which should be easily detectable in experiments.

IV
Discussion

We briefly discuss an effect of a perturbative Rashba SOC potential described by
H_{R}(\bm{k})=\lambda_{R}(k_{y}\sigma_{x}\tau_{0}-k_{x}\sigma_{y}\tau_{z})
.
Because the Rashba SOC breaks the chiral symmetry of
S_{+}
, the index
\mathbb{Z}
in Eq. (
12
) can no longer be defined.
Therefore, in the presence of Rashba SOC, the ZESs cannot retain their high degree of degeneracy at zero-energy.
Fig.
4
shows the ZBC as a function of the strength of the Rashba SOC
\lambda_{R}
.
The ZBC is substantially increased in the vicinity of the PSH state (i.e.,
\lambda_{R}=0
).
In principle, the amplitude of Rashba SOC potentials can be tuned experimentally by applying gate voltages or pressures.
Therefore, in experiments, a sudden enhancement in the ZBC as the strength of the Rashba SOC is varied is a possible observable signature of the APE.

In summary, we demonstrate that a spin-singlet
d_{xy}
-wave SC in the presence of PSH exhibits the APE.
The proposed experimental setup can be fabricated by interfacing existing materials.
Our proposal therefore represents a promising approach to observing the APE.

In this Supplemental Material, we analyze pairing correlations in the
d_{xy}
-wave superconductor (SC) in the presence of the persistent spin helix (PSH).
The Bogoliubov-de Gennes (BdG) Hamiltonian in momentum space reads

\displaystyle\check{H}(\bm{k})=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\hat{h}(\bm{k})&\hat{\Delta}(\bm{k})\\
-\hat{\Delta}(-\bm{k})&-\hat{h}(-\bm{k})\end{array}\right],

\displaystyle\hat{h}(\bm{k})=\xi(\bm{k})\hat{\sigma}_{0}+\lambda k_{y}\hat{\sigma}_{z},\quad\hat{\Delta}(\bm{k})=\frac{\Delta k_{x}k_{y}}{k_{F}^{2}}\hat{\sigma}_{y},

(20)

which is equivalent to the BdG Hamiltonian in Eq. (10) of the main text.
This Hamiltonian can be divided into two
2\times 2
block components as

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{s}(\bm{k})=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\xi(\bm{k})+s\lambda k_{y}&s\Delta(k_{x}k_{y}/k^{2}_{F})\\
s\Delta(k_{x}k_{y}/k^{2}_{F})&-\xi(\bm{k})-s\lambda k_{y}\end{array}\right],

where
s=\pm
.
The Matsubara Green’s function in momentum space is obtained by

\displaystyle\hat{{\cal G}}_{s}(\bm{k},\omega)

\displaystyle=\left[i\omega-\hat{H}_{s}(\bm{k})\right]^{-1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}G_{s}(\bm{k},\omega)&F_{s}(\bm{k},\omega)\\
\underline{F}_{s}(\bm{k},\omega)&\underline{G}_{s}(\bm{k},\omega)\\
\end{array}\right],

where

\displaystyle G_{s}(\bm{k},\omega)=-\frac{i\omega+\xi(\bm{k})+s\lambda k_{y}}{A_{s}(\bm{k})},\quad\underline{G}_{s}(\bm{k},\omega)=-\frac{i\omega-\xi(\bm{k})-s\lambda k_{y}}{A_{s}(\bm{k})},

(25)

\displaystyle F_{s}(\bm{k},\omega)=\underline{F}_{s}(\bm{k},\omega)=\frac{s\Delta(k_{x}k_{y}/k^{2}_{F})}{A_{s}(\bm{k})},

(26)

\displaystyle A_{s}(\bm{k})=\omega^{2}+\left\{\xi(\bm{k})+s\lambda k_{y}\right\}^{2}+\left\{\Delta(k_{x}k_{y}/k^{2}_{F})\right\}^{2}.

(27)

The anomalous part of the Green’s function
F_{s}(\bm{k},\omega)
is deformed as

\displaystyle F_{s}(\bm{k},\omega)=sF_{d_{xy}}(\bm{k},\omega)+F_{p_{x}}(\bm{k},\omega),

(28)

\displaystyle F_{d_{xy}}(\bm{k},\omega)=\frac{\Delta B(\bm{k})}{B(\bm{k})^{2}-4\lambda^{2}\xi^{2}(\bm{k})k^{2}_{y}}\frac{k_{x}k_{y}}{k^{2}_{F}},\quad F_{p_{x}}(\bm{k},\omega)=-\frac{2\Delta\lambda\xi(\bm{k})k_{y}}{B(\bm{k})^{2}-4\lambda^{2}\xi^{2}(\bm{k})k^{2}_{y}}\frac{k_{x}k_{y}}{k^{2}_{F}},

(29)

\displaystyle B(\bm{k})=\omega^{2}+\xi^{2}(\bm{k})+\lambda^{2}k^{2}_{y}+\left\{\Delta(k_{x}k_{y}/k^{2}_{F})\right\}^{2}.

(30)

The first term in Eq. (
28
) describes the
d_{xy}
-wave pairing correlation satisfying

\displaystyle F_{d_{xy}}(-k_{x},k_{y},\omega)=F_{d_{xy}}(k_{x},-k_{y},\omega)=-F_{d_{xy}}(k_{x},k_{y},\omega),\quad F_{d_{xy}}(-k_{x},-k_{y},\omega)=F_{d_{xy}}(k_{x},k_{y},\omega).

(31)

On the other hand, the second term in Eq. (
28
) describes the induced
p_{x}
-wave pairing correlation satisfying

\displaystyle F_{p_{x}}(-k_{x},k_{y},\omega)=-F_{p_{x}}(k_{x},k_{y},\omega),\quad F_{p_{x}}(k_{x},-k_{y},\omega)=F_{p_{x}}(k_{x},k_{y},\omega),

(32)

where we note that
F_{p_{x}}(\bm{k},\omega)=0
in the absence of the PSH (i.e.,
\lambda=0
).
In the original basis, the anomalous part of the Green’s function is given by

\displaystyle\hat{F}(\bm{k},\omega)=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}F_{\uparrow,\uparrow}(\bm{k},\omega)&F_{\uparrow,\downarrow}(\bm{k},\omega)\\
F_{\downarrow,\uparrow}(\bm{k},\omega)&F_{\downarrow,\downarrow}(\bm{k},\omega)\\
\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&F_{d_{xy}}(\bm{k},\omega)+F_{p_{x}}(\bm{k},\omega)\\
-F_{d_{xy}}(\bm{k},\omega)+F_{p_{x}}(\bm{k},\omega)&0\\
\end{array}\right].

As also discussed in the main text, the induced spin-triplet
p_{x}
-wave pairing correlation
F_{p_{x}}(\bm{k},\omega)
can be the source of
the odd-frequency spin-triplet
s
-wave pairing correlation in a dirty normal metal attached to the SC.

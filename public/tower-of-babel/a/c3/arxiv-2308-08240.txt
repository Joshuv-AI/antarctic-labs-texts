1.
Introduction

In this paper, all graphs considered are simple and undirected. Given a non-empty set
\Upsilon
and a collection
\mathbf{D}
of subsets of
\Upsilon
, the
intersection graph
of
\mathbf{D}
is defined as the graph with vertex set
\mathbf{D}
such that two elements of
\mathbf{D}
are adjacent if and only if their intersection is non-empty. An
\ell
-box is the Cartesian product
[x_{1},\ y_{1}]\times\ldots\times[x_{\ell},\ y_{\ell}]
of
\ell
closed and bounded intervals of the real line. The
boxicity
of a graph
G=\left(V(G),E(G)\right)
, denoted by
box(G)
, is the least positive integer
\ell
such that
G
is isomorphic to the intersection graph of a family of
\ell
-boxes in Euclidean
\ell
-space.
From a computational point of view, the boxicity of a graph is an NP-hard parameter, which was proved by Yannakakis
[
29
]
. However, various bounds have been found in terms of the maximum degree, acylic chromatic number, weak
r
-coloring number, genus, etc. See for instance
[
1
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
14
]
-
[
18
]
.
Boxiity is used as a measure of the complexity of ecological
[
25
]
and social
[
19
]
networks, and has applications in fleet maintenance
[
23
]
.
In
[
12
]
, S. Chandran et al., proved that
box(G)\leq 2\Delta^{2}(G)
for any graph
G
, where
\Delta(G)
is the maximum degree of
G
. This bound was improved by L. Esperet in
[
17
]
to
box(G)\leq\frac{\left\lfloor\Delta(G)^{2}\right\rfloor}{2}+2
.

For a given finite class of graphs
H_{i}=(V,E_{i})
,
i=1,2,\ldots,k
with same vertex set
V
,
the
intersection

H_{1}\cap H_{2}\cap\ldots\cap H_{k}
of the graphs
H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{k}
is the graph with vertex set
V
and edge set
E_{1}\cap E_{2}\cap\ldots\cap E_{k}
. A graph

is said to be an
interval graph
if there is a function
f
from the vertex set of

into a set of closed and bounded intervals in the real line such that

if and only if
f(u)\cap f(v)\neq\emptyset
. Such a function

is called an
interval representation
of
G
. Note that
box(G)=1
if and only if
G
is an interval graph.

Let us recall that a result proved by Roberts in
[
24
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
24
]
)
.

Let

be a graph. Then

if and only if there exist

\ell

with vertex set

such that
E(G)=E(I_{1})\cap\ldots\cap E(I_{\ell})
holds.

For a given

, the
subgraph of
G

by

is denoted by
\langle S\rangle
. A

in

Let

The

of a graph

Let

is a special class of circulant graphs which play an important role in the study of circular chromatic number. This can be seen in
[

In
[
9
]
, Bhowmick et al., proved that

holds for any graph

with no asteroidal triples. This result is not true in general. For example, the graph

(see

So it is interesting to characterize graphs

In this direction, Akira Kamibeppu

3.

In this section we first obtain a bounds for boxicity of

Theorem 3.1
.

Let

and

be

-pairwise disjoint graphs such that at least one of

M\leq box(G[H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{n}])\leq\mathop{\sum}\limits_{i=1}^{n}box(H_{i})
, where

M=\max\Big\{\mathop{\sum}\limits_{j=1}^{k}box(H_{i_{j}})\ |\{u_{i_{1}},\ldots,u_{i_{k}}\}
is a clique in
G
and each
H_{i_{j}}
is not a complete graph
\Big\}
.

Proof.

It is easy to note that

(i)

if
G
and
H
are not complete graphs, then
box(G\vee H)\geq box(G)+box(H)
;

(ii)

if
G^{\prime}
is an induced subgraph of

, then
box(G)\geq box(G^{\prime})
.

If
\langle\{u_{i_{1}},\ldots,u_{i_{k}}\}\rangle
is a clique in
G
,
H_{i_{j}}
is not a complete graph, for

is an induced subgraph of
G[H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{n}]
. Thus by (i) and (ii),

box(G[H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{n}])\geq box(G[H_{i_{1}},H_{i_{2}},\ldots,H_{i_{k}}])\geq\mathop{\sum}\limits_{j=1}^{k}box(H_{i_{j}}),

by induction on
k
and hence the lower bound follows.

Next, we prove the upper bound.
For
1\leq i\leq n
, let
box(H_{i})=k_{i}
. Then, by Theorem
1.1
, there exist interval graphs

with vertex set

such that

. We now define an interval graph
J_{i}^{j}
such that

is a spanning subgraph of

, for all
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq j\leq k_{i}
.

For
1\leq i\leq n
, denote by

the set
of all vertices

of
G
which are adjacent to
u_{i}
in

.
Let

be the graph with vertex set

and edge set

Then

is a spanning subgraph of every

. For every

, let

be the complete graph with vertex set

. Define

and

. Clearly

.

Claim 1.

is an interval graph, for

and

.

Proof of Claim 1.

Let

be an interval representation of

. Then there exist real numbers

Let

We extend the function

. Let

.

•

If

•

•

If

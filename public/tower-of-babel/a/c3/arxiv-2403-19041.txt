1.
Introduction

Let
S\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
be linear operator or relation in a Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}

which has lower bound
m(S)=\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
, i.e.,
\gamma

is the supremum of all
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
for which

(1.1)

(\varphi^{\prime},\varphi)\geq c(\varphi,\varphi),\quad\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S.

In what follows the notation
\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
stands for the class of all linear relations
from
{\mathfrak{H}}
to the Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{K}}
; this is abbreviated to
\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
when
{\mathfrak{K}}={\mathfrak{H}}
.
It is clear from (
1.1
) that a semibounded relation is automatically symmetric.
Among all semibounded selfadjoint extensions
H\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
of
S
there exists a largest
semibounded selfadjoint extension
S_{\rm F}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
, the Friedrichs extension,
with the same lower bound as
S
,
such that
H\leq S_{\rm F}
.
Likewise, for any choice of
c\leq\gamma
, there exists
among all semibounded selfadjoint extensions
H\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
of
S

a smallest semibounded selfadjoint extension
S_{{\rm K},c}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})

the so-called Kreĭn type extension, with lower bound
c
,
such that for any selfadjoint extension
H\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
of
S
with
H\geq c

one has
H\geq S_{{\rm K},c}
.
Thus, for any semibounded selfadjoint extension

H
of
S
one has the equivalence

(1.2)

c\leq H\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad S_{{\rm K},c}\leq H\leq S_{\rm F}.

For the ordering of semibounded selfadjoint operators
and relations as in (
1.2
), see
[
7
]
.
For each
c\leq\gamma
the Friedrichs extension
S_{\rm F}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})

and the Kreĭn type extension
S_{{\rm K},c}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
are connected
as linear relations by the identity

(1.3)

S_{{\rm K},c}=c+(((S-c)^{-1})_{\rm F})^{-1}.

In addition, there are also weak versions of the Friedrichs
and Kreĭn type extensions, which are connected as in (
1.3
),
and they play an important role in applications.
Note that for
\gamma\geq 0
and
c=0
these results (in the context of
densely defined linear operators) go back to
J. von Neumann
[
25
]
and M.G. Kreĭn
[
23
,
24
]
; and then
one usually speaks of the Kreĭn-von Neumann extension
instead of the Kreĭn type extension.
For a recent treatment of all the above extensions
in the context of linear relations, see
[
7
]
,
but one may also consult
[
14
]
for various other approaches.

The purpose of the present paper is to view the construction
of the above semibounded selfadjoint extensions
systematically from the point of view of semibounded sesquilinear forms.
In what follows
\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
stands for the set of all sesquilinear forms
whose domains belong to the Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}
.
Let
{\mathfrak{t}}\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
be any semibounded form with lower bound
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
For each
c\leq\gamma
there is a linear operator
Q_{c}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
,
where
{\mathfrak{K}}
is a Hilbert space, the so-called representing map, such that
the nonnegative form
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
can be written as

({\mathfrak{t}}-c)[\varphi,\psi]=(Q_{c}\varphi,Q_{c}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}(S)={\rm dom\,}Q_{c}.

The specific choice of
Q_{c}
does not play a central role in studying the form
{\mathfrak{t}}
.
The properties of representing maps for general nonnegative forms have been
studied in detail in
[
20
,
21
]
; see also
[
35
]
.
Now return to the semibounded linear relation

S\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
and assume that it has lower bound
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then there is a natural well-defined
sesquilinear form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
,
defined by the left-hand side of (
1.1
), with
{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}(S)={\rm dom\,}S

and such that
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)
has the same lower bound as
S
. In general,
this form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)
is not closed; see, for instance, in
[
8
]
,
[
10
]
.
By using the specific properties of the representing map for
the form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)-c
,
c\leq\gamma
, it follows that there exists
another semibounded form
{\mathfrak{s}}(S)\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
which is closed with
lower bound
c
, so that

{\mathfrak{t}}(S)\subset{\mathfrak{s}}(S).

In particular, this shows that the original form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)
is closable.
The present paper gives an overview of the construction of
the semibounded selfadjoint extensions of the semibounded relation
S\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})

by means of appropriate semibounded forms.
The Friedrichs extension is constructed via the form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)
,
the Kreĭn type extensions via the form
{\mathfrak{s}}(S)
,
and the closely related extremal extensions via intermediate forms.
All these constructions are based on the representing map of
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)-c
.
The arguments are much influenced by the work of Sebestyén and Stochel;
see
[
26
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
]
. It should be stressed that the treatment
in this paper is general in the sense that the possibly multivalued operator
S

need not be closed or densely defined.

The contents of the paper are now described.
The forms
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)
and
{\mathfrak{s}}(S)
, induced by the semibounded relation
S\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
,
are constructed in Section
2
, where also their relevant properties are discussed.
In Section
3
there is a further discussion of the forms
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)
and
{\mathfrak{s}}(S)
;
in particular, some attention will be paid to the (non)uniqueness of their representing maps.
Here also the connection with the construction of Sebestyén and Stochel
is discussed; see Remark
3.4
.
The Friedrichs extension
S_{\rm F}
of
S
and its weak version are treated
in Section
4
.
A similar reasoning involving the corresponding inverse
(S-c)^{-1}

with
c\leq\gamma
is given Section
5
.
This leads to analogous extensions of
(S-c)^{-1}
in terms of
appropriate representing maps and companion relations.
The Kreĭn type extension
S_{{\rm K},c}
and its weak version
are introduced in Section
6
. The identity in (
1.3
)
is a straightforward consequence of the approach
via the representing maps for the forms associated with
S
and
(S-c)^{-1}
;
cf. Section
5
.
As an application of the above constructions,
also the notion of extremal extensions is treated in this context,
see Section
7
. By way of illustration Section
8

is devoted to symmetric relations whose domain and range are
orthogonal; see
[
13
,
27
]
.
In Section
9
attention is paid to the representation in terms of a
closable representing map of a symmetric relation generated by a
semibounded form which itself is not necessarily closed.

2.
Semibounded relations and representing maps

In this section representing maps are introduced for semibounded forms
which are generated by semibounded relations.
Also a couple of their key properties are derived for later purposes.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
S\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a semibounded relation
with lower bound
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then the semibounded sesquilinear form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})

is defined by

(2.1)

{\mathfrak{t}}(S)[\varphi,\psi]=(\varphi^{\prime},\psi)=(\varphi,\psi^{\prime}),\quad\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\},\,\{\psi,\psi^{\prime}\}\in S,

so that
{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}(S)={\rm dom\,}S
.

Since
{\rm mul\,}S\subset{\rm mul\,}S^{*}=({\rm dom\,}S)^{\perp}

the form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)
is well-defined and, in fact,
it is semibounded with lower bound
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

The new ingredient in the present treatment is the notion of the representing map.
Let
c\leq\gamma
, then for the form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)-c

there exists a so-called
representing map

Q_{c}
such that

(2.2)

{\mathfrak{t}}(S)[\varphi,\psi]=c(\varphi,\psi)+(Q_{c}\varphi,Q_{c}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}(S)={\rm dom\,}S={\rm dom\,}Q_{c},

where
Q_{c}
is a linear operator in
\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{c})
with a Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{K}}_{c}
;
see
[
19
, Lemma 4.1]
.
Due to the definition of
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)
in (
2.1
) the
representing map
Q_{c}
has a so-called

companion relation

J_{c}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{K}}_{c},{\mathfrak{H}})
, which is introduced by

(2.3)

J_{c}=\big\{\,\{Q_{c}\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi\}:\,\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S\,\big\}.

The operator
Q_{c}
and the relation
J_{c}
form a dual pair in the sense that

(2.4)

Q_{c}\subset J_{c}^{\ast}\quad\mbox{or, equivalently,}\quad J_{c}\subset Q_{c}^{*},

as follows from a combination of the identities (
2.1
) and (
2.2
):

(2.5)

(\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi,\psi)=(\varphi,\psi^{\prime}-c\psi)=(Q_{c}\varphi,Q_{c}\psi),\quad\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\},\{\psi,\psi^{\prime}\}\in S.

The identity (
2.3
) shows that the relation
c+J_{c}Q_{c}
is an extension of
S
:

(2.6)

S\subset c+J_{c}Q_{c}.

Taking into account (
2.4
) one gets immediately two nonnegative extensions for
S-c
:

S-c\subset Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}\quad\text{and}\quad S-c\subset J_{c}J_{c}^{*}.

The adjoint
J_{c}^{*}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{c})
of the companion relation
J_{c}

is a closed relation by definition.
Let
\pi
be the orthogonal projection from
{\mathfrak{K}}_{c}
onto

(2.7)

{\rm mul\,}J_{c}^{*}=({\rm dom\,}J_{c})^{\perp}=({\rm ran\,}Q_{c})^{\perp}.

Then it follows from (
2.4
) and (
2.7
) that,
with the orthogonal operator part
(J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg}
,
one also has

(2.8)

Q_{c}\subset(J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg},\quad\mbox{where}\quad(J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg}=(I-\pi)\,J_{c}^{*}.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
S\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a semibounded relation
with lower bound
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
and let
c\leq\gamma
.
Let
Q_{c}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{c})
be a representing map for
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)-c

with companion relation
J_{c}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{K}}_{c},{\mathfrak{H}})
.
Then the sesquilinear form
{\mathfrak{s}}(S)\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
is defined by

(2.9)

{\mathfrak{s}}(S)[\varphi,\psi]=c(\varphi,\psi)+((J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg}\varphi,(J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{s}}(S)={\rm dom\,}J_{c}^{*}.

Consequently, the form
{\mathfrak{s}}(S)
is semibounded and closed.

By definition
(J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg}
is a closed operator
and it is clear from (
2.8
) that

(Q_{c})^{**}\subset(J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg}.

Hence, it follows from (
2.2
) and (
2.9
) that
the closed form
{\mathfrak{s}}(S)
extends the form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)
.
Therefore the form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)
is closable; the representation
of the closure is immediate from (
2.2
);
cf.
[
21
]
.
Observe, that the form
{\mathfrak{s}}(S)
is bounded below by
c
.
In fact,
{\mathfrak{s}}(S)
has lower bound
c
:
For
c<\gamma
this follows from

{{\rm ker\,}\,}(J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg}={{\rm ker\,}\,}J_{c}^{*}=({\rm ran\,}J_{c})^{\perp}=({\rm ran\,}(S-c))^{\perp}={{\rm ker\,}\,}(S^{*}-c)\neq\{0\},

while for
c=\gamma
this is holds by the inclusion
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)\subset{\mathfrak{s}}(S)
.
These observations are summarized in the next proposition.

Proposition 2.3
.

The closed form
{\mathfrak{s}}(S)\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
defined in (
2.9
)
extends the form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
in (
2.1
)
and its lower bound is
c
.
Consequently, the form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)
is closable and

{\mathfrak{t}}(S)\subset\bar{{\mathfrak{t}}}(S)\subset{\mathfrak{s}}(S).

Furthermore, the closure
\bar{{\mathfrak{t}}}(s)\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
is given by

(2.10)

{\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}(S)[\varphi,\psi]=c(\varphi,\psi)+(Q_{c}^{**}\varphi,Q_{c}^{**}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}(S)={\rm dom\,}Q_{c}^{**}.

As a corollary of Proposition
2.3
it is shown that the formula (
2.1
)
in Definition
2.1
holds under more general circumstances.

Corollary 2.4
.

Assume that
\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S^{*}

and that
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}\bar{{\mathfrak{t}}}(S)
. Then

(2.11)

\bar{{\mathfrak{t}}}(S)[\varphi,\psi]=(\varphi^{\prime},\psi)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\psi\in{\rm dom\,}\bar{{\mathfrak{t}}}(S).

Proof.

Since
Q_{c}
is closable with
{\rm dom\,}\bar{\mathfrak{t}}(S)={\rm dom\,}Q_{c}^{**}
and

Q_{c}^{**}\subset J_{c}^{*}
, one has

(2.12)

(\varphi,\psi^{\prime}-c\psi)=(Q_{c}^{**}\varphi,Q_{c}\psi),\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}Q_{c}^{**},\,\,\{\psi,\psi^{\prime}\}\in S.

Now assume that in (
2.12
) one also has
\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S^{*}
.
Then for all
\{\psi,\psi^{\prime}\}\in S
one sees by definition

(\varphi^{\prime},\psi)=(\varphi,\psi^{\prime})
. Thus (
2.12
), with
{\rm dom\,}S={\rm dom\,}Q_{c}
, reads as

(2.13)

(\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi,\psi)=(Q_{c}^{**}\varphi,Q_{c}\psi),\quad\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S^{*},\,\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}Q_{c}^{**},\,\,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}Q_{c}.

Taking limits
\{\psi_{n},Q_{c}\psi_{n}\}\to\{\psi,Q_{c}^{**}\psi\}
in (
2.13
) one obtains

(\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi,\psi)=(Q_{c}^{**}\varphi,Q_{c}^{**}\psi),\quad\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S^{*},\;\varphi,\,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}Q_{c}^{**},

which leads to (
2.11
).
∎

The next result concerns the description of the (form) domain of
\bar{{\mathfrak{t}}}(S)

which is easily derived from the associated representing map
Q_{c}
.
This type of description goes back to Freudenthal
[
11
]
.
There is also a useful description of
{\rm ran\,}Q_{c}^{*}
by means of
S

which determines the form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)
; cf.
[
12
,
17
,
29
]
.
The contents of this theorem will be interpreted in terms
of Friedrichs extensions later in Section
4
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
S\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
be semibounded with lower bound
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
let
c\leq\gamma
, and let
Q_{c}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}^{\prime})
be a representing map for
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)-c
.
Then
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}(S)

if and only if there is a sequence
\{\varphi_{n},\varphi_{n}^{\prime}\}\in S
such that

\varphi_{n}\to\varphi\quad\text{and}\quad(\varphi_{n}^{\prime}-\varphi_{m}^{\prime},\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{m})\to 0.

In addition,
\varphi\in{{\rm ker\,}\,}({\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}(S)-c)

if and only if there exists

\{\varphi_{n},\varphi_{n}^{\prime}\}\in S
such that

\varphi_{n}\to\varphi\quad\text{and}\quad(\varphi_{n}^{\prime}-c\varphi_{n},\varphi_{n})\to 0.

Furthermore,
\varphi\in{\rm ran\,}Q_{c}^{*}
if and only if there exists
C_{\varphi}<\infty
such that

(2.14)

|(\psi,\varphi)|^{2}\leq C_{\varphi}(\psi^{\prime},\psi)\quad\text{ for all }\;\{\psi,\psi^{\prime}\}\in S-c.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.3
one sees that
{\rm dom\,}{\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}(S)={\rm dom\,}Q_{c}^{**}
and hence
the condition that
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}(S)
is equivalent to

\varphi_{n}\to\varphi\quad\text{and}\quad\|Q_{c}(\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{m})\|^{2}\to 0.

It remains to apply (
2.5
) to get the description of
{\rm dom\,}{\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}(S)

in the first statement.

As to the second statement, again by Proposition
2.3

one has similarly that
{{\rm ker\,}\,}({\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}(S)-c)={{\rm ker\,}\,}Q_{c}^{**}
.
Hence,
\varphi\in{{\rm ker\,}\,}Q_{c}^{**}
if and only if the exists a sequence

\{\varphi_{n},\varphi_{n}^{\prime}\}\in S
such that
\varphi_{n}\to\varphi
and
Q_{c}\varphi_{n}\to 0
.
Using (
2.5
) this second condition can be rewritten as

(\varphi_{n}^{\prime}-c\varphi_{n},\varphi_{n})=\|Q_{c}\varphi_{n}\|^{2}\to 0.

Finally, to prove the last statement recall a general description of
{\rm ran\,}T^{*}
of
T\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
,
see e.g.
[
12
,
17
,
29
]
:
\varphi\in{\rm ran\,}Q_{c}^{*}
if and only if for some
c_{\varphi}<\infty
,

(2.15)

|(f,\varphi)|\leq c_{\varphi}\|Q_{c}f\|\;\text{ for all }f\in{\rm dom\,}Q_{c}.

Now using (
2.5
) the estimate in (
2.15
) can be rewritten as

(2.16)

|(f,\varphi)|^{2}\leq c_{\varphi}^{2}\,\|Q_{c}f\|^{2}=c_{\varphi}^{2}\,(f^{\prime}-cf,f)\quad\text{ for all }\;\{f,f^{\prime}\}\in S.

Take
C_{\varphi}=c_{\varphi}^{2}
and observe that
\{f,f^{\prime}\}\in S

is equivalent to
\{f,f^{\prime}-cf\}\in S-c
.
It remains to replace
f
by
\psi
and
f^{\prime}-cf
by
\psi^{\prime}

to conclude that (
2.16
) is equivalent to (
2.14
).
This completes the proof.
∎

The interest in the closure
\bar{\mathfrak{t}}(S)
of the form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)
lies in the possibility
to uniquely associate a semibounded selfadjoint relation which represents
\bar{\mathfrak{t}}(S)

and extends the semibounded relation
S
.
To be more specific, recall that for every closed semibounded
form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
there exists a unique semibounded selfadjoint
relation
H\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
, such that

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]=(\varphi^{\prime},\psi),\quad\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in H,\quad\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}},

see
[
7
,
21
]
.
Moreover, any semibounded selfadjoint relation
H\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
is obtained in this way;
cf.
[
21
, Lemma 6.3]
.
Hence, there is a one-to-one correspondence between the semibounded closed forms
{\mathfrak{t}}\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})

and the semibounded selfadjoint relations
H\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
which is indicated by

(2.17)

{\mathfrak{t}}={\mathfrak{t}}_{H}\quad\mbox{or}\quad H=H_{\mathfrak{t}},

depending on the point of view.
Furthermore, it should be remembered that for any
semibounded selfadjoint relation
H\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})

one may define the form
{\mathfrak{t}}(H)\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
as in (
2.1
) by

{\mathfrak{t}}(H)=(\varphi^{\prime},\psi),\quad\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\},\{\psi,\psi^{\prime}\}\in H.

Consequently, the form
{\mathfrak{t}}(H)
is semibounded and closable by Proposition
2.3
,
so that its closure
\bar{\mathfrak{t}}(H)
is a closed semibounded form.
Since the forms
\bar{{\mathfrak{t}}}(H)
and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{H}
coincide on
{\rm dom\,}H

it follows that for any semibounded selfadjoint relation
H\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
one has

(2.18)

{\mathfrak{t}}(H)\subset\bar{\mathfrak{t}}(H)={\mathfrak{t}}_{H},

see (
2.17
) and
[
7
]
.
As a consequence it is clear that for semibounded selfadjoint relations
H,K\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})

one has the equivalent statements

(2.19)

{\mathfrak{t}}(H)\leq{\mathfrak{t}}(K)\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad{\mathfrak{t}}_{H}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}_{K}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad H\leq K,

see
[
7
]
. These results will be tacitly used in the rest of this paper.
Note that for a semibounded relation
S\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})

the form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
in (
2.1
) was denoted by
{\mathfrak{t}}_{S}
in
[
7
]
;
however, this last notation will not be used in this paper (unless
S
is selfadjoint) as it may be confusing
with the above conventions (
2.17
) and (
2.18
).
At this stage it should be mentioned that for general semibounded forms one can also introduce
uniquely determined symmetric relations, see
[
21
, Theorem 6.1]
and, for special cases
in the present context, Section
4
and
Section
6
.

Now return to the semibounded form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
from (
2.1
).
The closure of the form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)
in (
2.10
) induces a unique
semibounded selfadjoint relation denoted by
S_{\rm F}

and the closed form
{\mathfrak{s}}(S)
in Definition
2.2
induces another unique
semibounded selfadjoint extension denoted by
S_{{\rm K},c}
.
It follows from (
2.6
) that
S_{\rm F}
and
S_{{\rm K},c}
are extensions of
S
.
They are usually called the Friedrichs extension and the Kreĭn type extension of
S
,
respectively. In the notation as discussed in (
2.17
) one may write

{\mathfrak{t}}_{S_{\rm F}}={\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}(S)\subset{\mathfrak{s}}(S)={\mathfrak{t}}_{S_{{\rm K},c}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathfrak{t}}_{S_{{\rm K},c}}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}_{S_{\rm F}}.

Since dealing with relations allows taking inverses,
it is easy to show that
J_{c}^{-1}
is a representing map for the nonnegative form
{\mathfrak{t}}((S-c)^{-1})

and that
Q_{c}^{-1}
is its companion relation. This observation leads immediately to the connecting formula
(
1.3
). The following sections will be devoted to the details of these observations.

3.
Semibounded forms induced by semibounded relations

First consider a general semibounded sesquilinear form with lower bound
\gamma

and let
c\leq\gamma
as discussed in
[
21
]
.
If
Q_{c}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{c})
and
Q_{c}^{\prime}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{c^{\prime}})

are representing maps for
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
, then clearly there exists
a partial isometry
V\in\mathbf{B}({\mathfrak{K}}_{c},{\mathfrak{K}}_{c}^{\prime})

with initial space
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q_{c}
and final space
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q_{c}^{\prime}
, such that

(3.1)

Q_{c}^{\prime}=VQ_{c}.

Here the notation
\mathbf{B}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
stands for the class of all bounded
everywhere defined operators from the Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}
to the Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{K}}
.
Since
V\in\mathbf{B}({\mathfrak{K}}_{c},{\mathfrak{K}}_{c}^{\prime})
, it follows
that the corresponding adjoints are connected via

(Q_{c}^{\prime})^{*}=(Q_{c})^{*}V^{*};

see e.g.
[
7
, Proposition 1.3.9]
.
Therefore the representation of the form
{\mathfrak{t}}

in terms of semibounded symmetric
or selfadjoint relations involving a representing map
Q_{c}

is not affected by the nonuniqueness:

(3.2)

(Q_{c}^{\prime})^{*}Q_{c}^{\prime}=Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c},\quad(Q_{c}^{\prime})^{*}(Q_{c}^{\prime})^{**}=Q_{c}^{*}(Q_{c})^{**},

for these results see
[
21
]
.

In the particular case that
{\rm ran\,}Q_{c}
and
{\rm ran\,}Q_{c}^{\prime}
are dense subspaces,
the isometry
V
in (
3.1
) becomes a unitary operator in
\mathbf{B}({\mathfrak{K}}_{c},{\mathfrak{K}}_{c}^{\prime})
.
This
minimality of the representing map
in
[
21
, Definition 2.3]

can be obtained by replacing the spaces
{\mathfrak{K}}
and
{\mathfrak{K}}^{\prime}
by
the closed subspaces
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q_{c}
and
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q_{c}^{\prime}
, respectively.
However, in the present work this assumption is not used and
the constructions are carried out without any minimality assumption.
This simplifies the treatment when a representing map is to be constructed
for a new form from forms whose representing maps are already given:
for instance, to define a representing map for a restriction of a given form,
see e.g. Section
7
(proof of Lemma
7.2
),
or when constructing representing maps involving the functional calculus of given forms;
cf.
[
21
, Sections 4, 5]
(sum decompositions of forms, mutual singularity of pairs of forms, etc.).

Now let
S\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
be semibounded with lower bound
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}

and let
c\leq\gamma
. The associated semibounded form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)
in (
2.1
)
leads to a representing map
Q_{c}
via (
2.5
) and its companion relation
J_{c}
in (
2.3
)
whose basic properties are now established.
The following identities are straightforward consequences from the definition:

(3.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm dom\,}J_{c}={\rm ran\,}Q_{c},&{{\rm ker\,}\,}J_{c}=\{0\},\\
{\rm ran\,}J_{c}={\rm ran\,}(S-c),&{\rm mul\,}J_{c}=\{\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi:\,\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S,\,Q_{c}\varphi=0\}.\end{array}\right.

In the next result the identity involving
{\rm mul\,}J_{c}
will be expressed differently,
independent of the representing map
Q_{c}
. In particular, it follows that

J_{c}
is an operator when
S
is densely defined, in which case
S
is an operator.
The contents of the next theorem will be interpreted for
the Kreĭn type extensions later in Section
6
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
S\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
be semibounded with lower bound
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}

and let
c\leq\gamma
. Then the multivalued part of
J_{c}
in (
2.3
) is given by

(3.4)

{\rm mul\,}J_{c}={\rm ran\,}(S-c)\cap{\rm mul\,}S^{*}.

The element
\psi^{\prime}\in{\rm mul\,}J_{c}^{**}
if and only if there exists

\{\varphi_{n},\varphi_{n}^{\prime}\}\in S
such that

(3.5)

\varphi_{n}^{\prime}-c\varphi_{n}\to\psi^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad(\varphi_{n}^{\prime}-c\varphi_{n},\varphi_{n})\to 0.

Furthermore,
\psi^{\prime}\in{\rm dom\,}J_{c}^{*}
if and only if there exists
C_{\psi^{\prime}}<\infty
such that

(3.6)

|(\varphi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})|^{2}\leq C_{\psi^{\prime}}(\varphi^{\prime},\varphi)\quad\text{ for all }\;\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S-c.

Proof.

The following equivalence is a consequence of (
2.5
):

(\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi,\varphi)=0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad Q_{c}\varphi=0

for all
\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S
. In other words,

(3.7)

{\rm mul\,}J_{c}=\big\{\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi\in{\mathfrak{H}}:\,(\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi,\varphi)=0\,\,\mbox{for some}\,\,\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S\big\}.

Now (
3.4
) can be easily proved; cf.
[
15
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Assume that
\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi\in{\rm ran\,}(S-c)\cap{\rm mul\,}S^{*}

for some
\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S
.
This implies that
(\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi,\varphi)=0

and therefore, by (
3.7
),
\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi\in{\rm mul\,}J_{c}
.

Conversely, assume that
\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi\in{\rm mul\,}J_{c}
.
Therefore, by (
3.7
), one sees that

\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S

and
(\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi,\varphi)=0
.
Clearly with
\{\psi,\psi^{\prime}\}\in S
the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
for the nonnegative form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)-c
gives

|(\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi,\psi)|^{2}\leq(\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi,\varphi)(\psi^{\prime}-c\psi,\psi).

Hence
(\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi,\psi)=0
for all
\psi\in{\rm dom\,}S
, so that

\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi\in({\rm dom\,}S)^{\perp}={\rm mul\,}S^{*}
.
Therefore, it follows that
\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi\in{\rm ran\,}(S-c)\cap{\rm mul\,}S^{*}
.

To prove (
3.5
) let
\psi^{\prime}\in{\rm mul\,}J_{c}^{**}
. Using (
2.3
) this means that
there exists a sequence
\{\varphi_{n},\varphi_{n}^{\prime}\}\in S
such that
Q_{c}\varphi_{n}\to 0
and

\varphi_{n}^{\prime}-c\varphi_{n}\to\psi^{\prime}
. Now apply (
2.5
) to see that

(\varphi_{n}^{\prime}-c\varphi_{n},\varphi_{n})=\|Q_{c}\varphi_{n}\|^{2}\to 0.

The converse statement is shown similarly.

To prove (
3.6
) observe that by (
3.3
)
J_{c}^{-1}
is an operator and that

{\rm dom\,}J_{c}^{*}={\rm ran\,}(J_{c}^{-1})^{*}
.
Now proceeding as in the proof of Theorem
2.5
one concludes that
\psi^{\prime}\in{\rm dom\,}J_{c}^{*}

if and only if there exists
c_{\psi^{\prime}}<\infty
such that

|(f^{\prime}-cf,\psi^{\prime})|\leq c_{\psi^{\prime}}\|J_{c}^{-1}(f^{\prime}-cf)\|=c_{\psi^{\prime}}\|Q_{c}f\|\;\text{ for all }\{f,f^{\prime}\}\in S.

Again squaring this, using (
2.5
), and then replacing
f
by
\varphi
and
f^{\prime}-cf
by
\varphi^{\prime}
yields

|(\varphi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})|^{2}\leq c_{\psi^{\prime}}^{2}\,(\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})\quad\text{ for all }\;\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S-c.

Thus with
C_{\psi^{\prime}}=c_{\psi^{\prime}}^{2}
one gets (
3.6
).
This completes the proof.
∎

Since the representing map
Q_{c}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{c})
in (
2.2
)
is only determined up to a partial isometry,
its choice will influence the companion relation
J_{c}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{K}}_{c},{\mathfrak{H}})
in (
2.3
).
Thus it will have to be checked whether the choice of
Q_{c}
influences
the relation
c+J_{c}Q_{c}
in (
2.6
) and the form
{\mathfrak{s}}(S)
in (
2.9
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
S\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be semibounded with lower bound
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}

and let
c\leq\gamma
. Assume that
Q_{c}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{c})

and
Q_{c}^{\prime}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{c^{\prime}})
are representing
maps for
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)-c
which are connected via
Q_{c}^{\prime}=VQ_{c}

by a partial isometry
V\in\mathbf{B}({\mathfrak{K}}_{c},{\mathfrak{K}}_{c}^{\prime})
as in (
3.1
).
Let
J_{c}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{K}}_{c},{\mathfrak{H}})
and
J_{c}^{\prime}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{K}}_{c}^{\prime},{\mathfrak{H}})

be the companion relations of
Q_{c}
and

Q_{c}^{\prime}
, respectively. Then
J_{c}
and
J_{c}^{\prime}
are connected by

(3.8)

J_{c}^{\prime}\,(V{\upharpoonright\,}{\rm ran\,}Q_{c})=J_{c}.

In particular, one has

(3.9)

S\subset c+J_{c}^{\prime}Q_{c}^{\prime}=c+J_{c}Q_{c};

here the inclusion is an equality if and only if
{\rm ran\,}(S-c)\cap{\rm mul\,}S^{*}={\rm mul\,}S
.
Moreover, the adjoints are connected via

(3.10)

((J_{c}^{\prime})^{\ast})_{\rm reg}=V\,(J_{c}^{\ast})_{\rm reg}.

Proof.

Let
V
be the indicated partial isometry.
Then the identity (
3.8
) is obtained from (
3.1
)
by means of the formula (
2.3
).

The identity in (
3.9
) is a straightforward consequence of (
3.1
) and (
3.8
).
The inclusion in (
3.9
) was already stated in (
2.6
).
Next observe that
{\rm dom\,}J_{c}Q_{c}={\rm dom\,}S
.
Hence, the inclusion in (
3.9
) is an equality if and only if

{\rm mul\,}S={\rm mul\,}J_{c}Q_{c}={\rm mul\,}J_{c}
.
Now the assertion follows from (
3.4
) in Theorem
3.1
.

In order to prove the identity (
3.10
) note that

(3.11)

({\rm mul\,}(J_{c}^{\prime})^{*})^{\perp}={\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q_{c}^{\prime}=V\,{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q_{c}=V\,({\rm mul\,}J_{c}^{*})^{\perp}.

Next assume that
\{f,f^{\prime}\}\in V\,(J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg}
.
Then
\{f,h\}\in(J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg}
and
f^{\prime}=Vh
.
Since
J_{c}^{*}
is closed
\{f,h\}\in(J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg}
implies that

\{f,h\}\in J_{c}^{*}
and
h\in{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q_{c}
. Hence,
f^{\prime}\in{\rm\overline{ran}\,}Q_{c}^{\prime}
,
and using (
2.3
) for all
\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S
one gets

\begin{split}0&=(h,Q_{c}\varphi)-(f,\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi)\\
&=(Vh,VQ_{c}\varphi)-(f,\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi)\\
&=(f^{\prime},Q_{c}^{\prime}\varphi)-(f,\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi),\end{split}

which gives
\{f,f^{\prime}\}\in(J_{c}^{\prime})^{*}
. Hence
the inclusion
V\,(J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg}\subset((J_{c}^{\prime})^{*})_{\rm reg}

has been shown; cf. (
3.11
).
For the reverse inclusion, let
\{f,f^{\prime}\}\in((J_{c}^{\prime})^{*})_{\rm reg}
.
In other words,
\{f,f^{\prime}\}\in(J_{c}^{\prime})^{*}
and
f^{\prime}=Vh

for some
h\in({\rm mul\,}(J_{c}^{*})^{\perp}
by (
3.11
).
Therefore, retracing the above steps shows
\{f,h\}\in(J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg}
,
and hence
\{f,f^{\prime}\}\in V(J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg}
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Under the circumstances of Lemma
3.2
one has

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}J_{c}^{\prime}(J_{c}^{\prime})^{*}=J_{c}^{\prime}((J_{c}^{\prime})^{*})_{\rm reg}=J_{c}(J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg}=J_{c}J_{c}^{*},\\
(J_{c}^{\prime})^{\ast\ast}(J_{c}^{\prime})^{\ast}=(((J_{c}^{\prime})^{\ast})_{\rm reg})^{*}((J_{c}^{\prime})^{\ast})_{\rm reg}=((J_{c}^{\ast})_{\rm reg})^{*}(J_{c}^{\ast})_{\rm reg}=J_{c}^{\ast\ast}J_{c}^{\ast}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Recall that for any linear relation
T\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
one has

(3.12)

TT^{*}=T(T^{*})_{\rm reg}\quad\mbox{and}\quad T^{**}T^{*}=((T^{*})_{\rm reg})^{*}(T^{*})_{\rm reg},

see
[
19
]
for similar results.
Therefore, due to the identies in (
3.12
) it suffices to verify that

J_{c}^{\prime}((J_{c}^{\prime})^{*})_{\rm reg}=J_{c}(J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg}\quad\mbox{and}\quad(((J_{c}^{\prime})^{\ast})_{\rm reg})^{*}((J_{c}^{\prime})^{\ast})_{\rm reg}=((J_{c}^{\ast})_{\rm reg})^{*}(J_{c}^{\ast})_{\rm reg}.

However, these identities follow immediately from (
3.8
) and (
3.10
).
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Let
S\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a semibounded relation in a Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}

with lower bound
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
c\leq\gamma
.
Then
S-c\geq 0
and this implies that

(3.13)

(\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi,\psi^{\prime}-c\psi)_{S-c}:=(\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi,\psi)=(\varphi,\psi^{\prime}-c\psi),\quad\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\},\,\{\psi,\psi^{\prime}\}\in S,

defines a semi-inner product on the linear subspace
{\rm ran\,}(S-c)
.
It is immediately clear that the linear subspace of null elements
of
{\rm ran\,}(S-c)
, provided with this semi-inner product, is given by

{\rm ran\,}(S-c)\cap{\rm mul\,}S^{*}
; see (
3.7
) and Theorem
3.1
.
The Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}_{S-c}
is defined as the completion of

{\rm ran\,}(S-c)/({\rm ran\,}(S-c)\cap{\rm mul\,}S^{*})

with the inner product

([\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi],[\psi^{\prime}-c\psi])_{S-c}:=(\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi,\psi^{\prime}-c\psi)_{S-c},\quad\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\},\,\{\psi,\psi^{\prime}\}\in S,

where
[\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi]
and
[\psi^{\prime}-c\psi]

denote the relevant equivalence classes.
Then, clearly, the operator
q_{c}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{H}}_{S-c})
given by

q_{c}\varphi=[\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi],\quad\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S,

is well defined and, automatically, one has that
{\rm ran\,}q_{c}

is dense in
{\mathfrak{H}}_{S-c}
.
It follows from the above identity (
3.13
) that

(q_{c}\varphi,q_{c}\psi)_{S-c}=({\mathfrak{t}}(S)-c)[\varphi,\psi],\quad\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\},\,\{\psi,\psi^{\prime}\}\in S.

Thus the operator
q_{c}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{H}}_{S-c})

acts as a minimal representing map for the form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)-c
.
This construction has been used for nonnegative relations
in
[
15
]
, see also
[
16
,
28
]
for the sectorial case.
In the case when
S
is densely defined one has
{\rm mul\,}S^{*}=\{0\}
,
i.e.,
S^{*}
and, therefore, also
J_{c}
is an operator.
When
S
is a closed densely defined nonnegative operator, this construction
reduces to that of Sebestyén and Stochel appearing in
[
30
]
: in other words,
their construction produced a minimal representing map for the closable form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)\geq 0
.

4.
A characterization of the Friedrichs extensions

Let
S\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a semibounded relation
with lower bound
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)
be the corresponding closable form
with representing map
Q_{c}
in (
2.2
)
and
{\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}(S)
be its closure with representing map
Q_{c}^{**}
in (
2.10
).
Then the semibounded selfadjoint relation
S_{\rm F}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
which is
uniquely determined by the semibounded closed form
{\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}(S)\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
, is given by

(4.1)

S_{\rm F}=c+Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}^{**},

while the symmetric semibounded relation
S_{\rm f}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
which is uniquely
determined by the closable form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
, is given by

(4.2)

S_{\rm f}=c+Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c},

see
[
21
, Theorem 6.1 and Corollary 6.4]
for a characterization and details.
Moreover, the relations
S_{\rm F}
and
S_{\rm f}

do not depend on the particular choice of the representing map
Q_{c}
;
see (
3.2
).
It is a direct consequence of (
2.6
)
(using
J_{c}\subset Q_{c}^{*}
) and Lemma
3.2

that
S_{\rm f}
and
S_{\rm F}
are extensions of
S
, such that

(4.3)

S\subset S_{\rm f}=c+Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}\subset S_{\rm F}=c+Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}^{**}.

The multivalued parts
{\rm mul\,}S_{\rm f}={\rm mul\,}S_{\rm F}={\rm mul\,}Q_{c}^{*}=({\rm dom\,}Q_{c})^{\perp}

satisfy

(4.4)

{\rm mul\,}S_{\rm f}={\rm mul\,}S_{\rm F}={\rm mul\,}S^{*}.

Moreover, it follows from
[
21
, Corollary 6.4]

that the lower bounds of
S_{\rm f}
and
S_{\rm F}

are given by

m(S_{\rm f})=m(S_{\rm F})=\gamma.

The semibounded selfadjoint extension
S_{\rm F}
in (
4.1
)
is the well-known
Friedrichs extension
,
while the semibounded symmetric extension
S_{\rm f}
in (
4.2
)
will be called the
weak Friedrichs extension
.
It is clear that for any linear extension
T
of
S
with
S\subset T\subset S_{\rm F}

it follows that also the inclusions
S\subset\overline{T}\subset S_{\rm F}
hold,
where
\overline{T}
stands for the closure of
T
. Hence it follows that

{\mathfrak{t}}(S)\subset{\mathfrak{t}}(\overline{T})\subset{\mathfrak{t}}(S_{\rm F})\subset{\mathfrak{t}}_{S_{\rm F}}={\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}(S)
,
which gives

(4.5)

{\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}(\overline{T})={\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}(S)\quad\mbox{and}\quad(\overline{T})_{\rm F}=S_{\rm F},

so that the Friedrichs extension of such a
semibounded extension
T
of
S
remains the same.
In particular, one has
(\overline{S})_{\rm F}=S_{\rm F}
.
From the definition the following translation invariance property
of
S_{\rm F}
is immediate

(S-c)_{\rm F}=S_{\rm F}-c.

The extensions
S_{\rm f}
and
S_{\rm F}
are intrinsically
characterized in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
S\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a semibounded linear relation with lower bound

\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
and let
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)
be the corresponding semibounded form in (
2.1
).
Then the following statements hold:

(a)

The Friedrichs extension
S_{\rm F}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
of
S
in (
4.1
)
is given by

(4.6)

S_{\rm F}=\big\{\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S^{*}:\,\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}\bar{{\mathfrak{t}}}(S)\,\big\}.

If
H\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
is a selfadjoint extension of
S
with

(4.7)

{\rm dom\,}H\subset{\rm dom\,}\bar{\mathfrak{t}}(S),

then
H=S_{\rm F}
.

(b)

The weak Friedrichs extension

S_{\rm f}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
of
S
in (
4.2
)
is given by

(4.8)

S_{\rm f}=S\hplus(\{0\}\times{\rm mul\,}S^{*}).

If
H\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
is a symmetric extension of
S
with

(4.9)

{\rm dom\,}H\subset{\rm dom\,}S,

then
H\subset S_{\rm f}
.

(c)

The equality
S_{\rm f}=S_{\rm F}
holds if and only if

(4.10)

{\rm dom\,}S={\rm\overline{dom}\,}S\cap{\rm dom\,}S^{*}.

In particular, (
4.10
) holds if
{\rm dom\,}S
is closed.

Proof.

(a) It is clear from
S\subset S_{\rm F}
that
S_{\rm F}\subset S^{*}
.
Moreover, it follows from
(
4.1
) that
{\rm dom\,}S_{\rm F}\subset{\rm dom\,}Q_{c}^{**}={\rm dom\,}\bar{{\mathfrak{t}}}(S)
.
Thus
S_{\rm F}
belongs to the right-hand side of (
4.6
).
However, observe that

(\varphi^{\prime},\varphi)=\bar{{\mathfrak{t}}}(S)[\varphi,\varphi]\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S^{*},\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}\bar{{\mathfrak{t}}}(S),

cf. Corollary
2.4
, so that the right-hand side of (
4.6
)
is a symmetric relation. Therefore, (
4.6
) has been shown.

Let
H\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a selfadjoint extension of
S
with (
4.7
). Then it follows from
(
4.6
) that
H\subset S_{\rm F}
. Since both relations are selfadjoint, one
concludes that
H=S_{\rm F}
.

(b) It is clear from
S\subset S_{\rm f}
that
S_{\rm f}\subset S^{*}
.
Moreover, it follows from (
4.2
)
that
{\rm dom\,}S_{\rm f}\subset{\rm dom\,}Q_{c}={\rm dom\,}S
.
Hence one obtains

(4.11)

S_{\rm f}\subset\big\{\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S^{*}:\,\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}S\,\big\}.

Now observe that

\big\{\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S^{*}:\,\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}S\,\big\}=S\hplus(\{0\}\times{\rm mul\,}S^{*})\subset S_{\rm f},

so that (
4.11
) gives (
4.8
).

Let
H\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a symmetric extension of
S
with (
4.9
).
Then it follows from (
4.11
) that
H\subset S_{\rm f}
.

(c) Thanks to (
4.3
), it is clear that
S_{\rm f}=S_{\rm F}

if and only if
S_{\rm f}
is selfadjoint,
which is equivalent to
{\rm dom\,}S={\rm\overline{dom}\,}S\cap{\rm dom\,}S^{*}
; cf.
[
7
, Lemma 1.5.7]
.
∎

The linear subspace
{\rm ran\,}(S_{\rm F}-c)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}
can be characterized
in terms of the representing map
Q_{c}
for
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
.
By (
4.1
) one has
S_{\rm F}-c=Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}^{**}
and therefore

{\rm ran\,}(S_{\rm F}-c)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}={\rm ran\,}Q_{c}^{*}.

Thus the following result is clear from Theorem
2.5
; cf.
[
12
,
17
,
26
,
31
]
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
S\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be as in Theorem
4.1
and let
S_{\rm F}
be its Friedrichs extension.
Then
\varphi\in{\rm ran\,}(S_{\rm F}-c)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}
if and only if there exists
C_{\varphi}<\infty
such that

|(\psi,\varphi)|^{2}\leq C_{\varphi}(\psi^{\prime},\psi)\text{ for all }\{\psi,\psi^{\prime}\}\in S-c.

Similarly, one has in terms of the representing map
Q_{c}

{\rm dom\,}(S_{\rm F}-c)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}={\rm dom\,}{\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}(S)={\rm dom\,}Q_{c}^{**},

and this domain was described already in Theorem
2.5
;
cf.
[
14
, Remark 4.6]
,
[
12
, eq. (7)]
,
[
7
, Corollary 5.3.4]
.
Additionaly, one has

{{\rm ker\,}\,}(S_{\rm F}-c)={{\rm ker\,}\,}({\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}(S)-c)={{\rm ker\,}\,}Q_{c}^{**},

which was also described in Theorem
2.5
.
In particular, if
S
is nonnegative, i.e.
\gamma\geq 0
,
then one can take
c=0
; cf.
[
14
, Proposition 4.10]
.

The Friedrichs extension
S_{\rm F}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
guarantees the existence of at least
one semibounded selfadjoint extension of a semibounded relation
S\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
and, in fact,

S_{\rm F}
has the same lower bound as
S
.
It is a consequence of (
4.4
) that
S_{\rm F}
is an operator if and only if

S
is densely defined. In this case
S
is automatically an operator and all
selfadjoint extensions of
S
are also operators.
In the general nondensely defined case,
S_{\rm F}

is the largest semibounded selfadjoint extension of
S
.
In particular, one should note that if
S\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
is semibounded and selfadjoint
then
S_{\rm F}=S
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
S\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a semibounded linear relation
with lower bound
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
H\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
be any semibounded selfadjoint extension of
S
, then

(4.12)

H\leq S_{\rm F}.

Proof.

First recall that for any semibounded selfadjoint extension
H
the corresponding
form
{\mathfrak{t}}(H)
is semibounded and that
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)\subset{\mathfrak{t}}(H)
. Since
H
is selfadjoint

{\mathfrak{t}}(H)
is closable and its closure is denoted by
{\mathfrak{t}}_{H}
; see (
2.17
).
Thus it is clear that

{\mathfrak{t}}_{S_{\rm F}}={\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}(S)\subset{\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}(H)={\mathfrak{t}}_{H}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathfrak{t}}_{H}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}_{S_{\rm F}},

which gives the inequality (
4.12
); see
[
7
]
.
∎

5.
Forms and representing maps induced by inverses of semibounded relations

In Section
4
the explicit formula (
4.1
) for the Friedrichs extension
of a semibounded relations
S
and its extremality property in Corollary
4.3

was established. This was done by means of the representing map
Q_{c}
of the form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)-c

using the fact that
{\mathfrak{t}}_{S_{\rm F}}
is the closure of the form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)
and that

{\mathfrak{t}}_{S_{\rm F}}\subset{\mathfrak{t}}_{H}
for every semibounded selfadjoint extension
H
of
S
.
Among all semibounded selfadjoint extensions
H
of
S
the lower bound
m(H)

can be any number below
\gamma
.
After fixing a real number
c\leq\gamma
a natural question is whether there is
a minimal semibounded selfadjoint extension among all semibounded selfadjoint extensions
of
S
satisfying
m(H)\geq c
. The most interesting case here is the case where
c=\gamma
.
This problem has a long history in the case of nonnegative operators
S
and an affirmative answer
to the problem was given by M.G. Kreĭn in his famous papers
[
23
,
24
]
,
where all nonnegative selfadjoint extensions
H\geq 0
in the densely defined case
were characterized via the operator interval

S_{\rm K}\leq H\leq S_{\rm F}.

The minimal solution
S_{\rm K}
is nowadays called the Kreĭn extension or the
Kreĭn-von Neumann extension of
S
.
These results were extended to the case of nonnegative linear relations in
[
9
]

using the key connection

(5.1)

S_{\rm K}=((S^{-1})_{\rm F})^{-1}.

The general semibounded situation has been treated in
[
7
]
using the form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)

and its closure to define
S_{\rm F}
and the analog (
1.3
) of the formula (
5.1
)
for the nonnegative relation
S-c
; see
[
7
, Sections 5.3 & 5.4]
.
The new ingredient in the present approach is to use representing maps
for the nonnegative forms induced by
S-c
and its inverse
(S-c)^{-1}

with a fixed
c\leq\gamma
.
For the form
{\mathfrak{t}}((S-c)^{-1})
this leads to an explicit formula for the corresponding minimal extension

S_{{\rm K},c}
of
S
in (
1.2
) as will be seen in Section
6
.

Now let
S\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a semibounded linear relation with lower bound

\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
and let
c\leq\gamma
be fixed.
The relation
S-c
is nonnegative and so is its inverse

(5.2)

(S-c)^{-1}=\big\{\{\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi,\varphi\}:\,\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S\big\},\quad c\leq\gamma.

The inverse
(S-c)^{-1}
induces a nonnegative sesquilinear form

{\mathfrak{t}}((S-c)^{-1})
, whose domain is given by

{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}((S-c)^{-1})={\rm ran\,}(S-c),

and according to (
2.1
), (
2.5
), and (
5.2
) one has for
all
\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\},\{\psi,\psi^{\prime}\}\in S
,

(5.3)

{\mathfrak{t}}((S-c)^{-1})[\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi,\psi^{\prime}-c\psi]=(\varphi,\psi^{\prime}-c\psi)=({\mathfrak{t}}(S)-c)[\varphi,\psi]=(Q_{c}\varphi,Q_{c}\psi).

The next proposition identifies a representing map and
a corresponding companion relation for the nonnegative form
{\mathfrak{t}}((S-c)^{-1})
which,
in fact, can be expressed in terms of the corresponding data for the semibounded form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)

introduced already in Section
2
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
S\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a semibounded relation
with lower bound
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
and let
c\leq\gamma
.
Let
Q_{c}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{c})
be a representing map for
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)-c

with the companion relation
J_{c}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{K}}_{c},{\mathfrak{H}})
in (
2.3
).
Then the inverse
J_{c}^{-1}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{c})
of
J_{c}
is an operator and
it is a representing map for the form
{\mathfrak{t}}((S-c)^{-1})
,

(5.4)

{\mathfrak{t}}((S-c)^{-1})[\eta,\zeta]=(J_{c}^{-1}\eta,J_{c}^{-1}\zeta),\quad\eta,\zeta\in{\rm ran\,}(S-c).

The companion relation of the representing map
J_{c}^{-1}
is given by
Q_{c}^{-1}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{K}}_{c},{\mathfrak{H}})
.
Moreover, with orthogonal operator part of the adjoint of
Q_{c}^{-1}
,

(5.5)

((Q_{c}^{-1})^{*})_{\rm reg}=(I-\pi)(Q_{c}^{-1})^{*},

where
\pi
is the orthogonal projection from
{\mathfrak{K}}_{c}
onto
{\rm mul\,}J_{c}^{*}
as in (
2.7
),
the sesquilinear form
{\mathfrak{s}}((S-c)^{-1})\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})

(
cf. Definition
2.8
)

is given by

(5.6)

{\mathfrak{s}}((S-c)^{-1})[\eta,\zeta]=((Q_{c}^{-1})^{*})_{\rm reg}\,\eta,((Q_{c}^{-1})^{*})_{\rm reg}\,\zeta),\quad\eta,\zeta\in{\rm dom\,}(Q_{c}^{-1})^{*}.

It is well-defined, nonnegative, and closed.

Proof.

According to (
2.3
) the inverse
(J_{c})^{-1}
has the form

(5.7)

(J_{c})^{-1}=\big\{\,\{\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi,Q_{c}\varphi\}:\,\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S\,\big\}

and as noted in (
3.3
)
J_{c}^{-1}
is an operator.
The formula (
5.4
) is now clear from (
5.3
).

It follows from (
5.7
) that

Q_{c}^{-1}=\big\{\{J_{c}^{-1}(\psi^{\prime}-c\psi),\psi\}:\,\{\psi,\psi^{\prime}\}\in S\big\},

so that
Q_{c}^{-1}
is the companion relation (with
0
as base point)
of the representing map
J_{c}^{-1}
.
The adjoint
(Q_{c}^{-1})^{*}
of the companion relation
Q_{c}^{-1}
is closed by definition
and due to the identity

{\rm mul\,}(Q_{c}^{-1})^{*}=({\rm ran\,}Q_{c})^{\perp}={\rm mul\,}J_{c}^{*},

its orthogonal operator part is given by (
5.5
).
Therefore, Definition
2.2
can be applied to the form
{\mathfrak{s}}((S-c)^{-1})
and this yields
a well-defined nonnegative closed form defined on the linear space
{\rm dom\,}(Q_{c}^{-1})^{*}

given by the formula (
5.6
).
∎

It follows from Proposition
5.1
that the operator
J_{c}^{-1}

and the relation
Q_{c}^{-1}
form a dual pair

(5.8)

J_{c}^{-1}\subset(Q_{c}^{-1})^{*}\quad\mbox{or, equivalently,}\ \quad Q_{c}^{-1}\subset(J_{c}^{-1})^{*};

cf. (
2.4
).
Moreover, the relation
Q_{c}^{-1}J_{c}^{-1}
is an extension of
(S-c)^{-1}
:

(5.9)

(S-c)^{-1}\subset Q_{c}^{-1}J_{c}^{-1},

which is symmetric as follows from (
5.8
); cf. (
2.6
).
The analog of the pairing stated in (
2.8
), when applied
to the operator
J_{c}^{-1}
and the relation
((Q_{c})^{-1})^{*}

in (
5.8
), reads as

J_{c}^{-1}\subset((Q_{c}^{-1})^{*})_{\rm reg}.

Thus the form
{\mathfrak{t}}((S-c)^{-1})
, as a restriction of the closed form
{\mathfrak{s}}((S-c)^{-1})
,
is closable and its closure is given by

(5.10)

{\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}((S-c)^{-1})[\eta,\zeta]=((J_{c}^{-1})^{**}\eta,(J_{c}^{-1})^{**}\zeta),

for all
\eta,\zeta\in{\rm dom\,}{\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}((S-c)^{-1})={\rm dom\,}(J_{c}^{-1})^{**}
.

In the terminology of Section
4
, applied to the closable form
{\mathfrak{t}}((S-c)^{-1})
,
it follows from (
5.4
) that the weak Friedrichs extension

((S-c)^{-1})_{\rm f}
of
(S-c)^{-1}
is given by

(5.11)

((S-c)^{-1})_{\rm f}=(J_{c}^{-1})^{*}(J_{c}^{-1}),

while, similarly, it follows from (
5.10
)
that the Friedrichs extension
((S-c)^{-1})_{\rm F}

of
(S-c)^{-1}
is given by

(5.12)

((S-c)^{-1})_{\rm F}=(J_{c}^{-1})^{*}(J_{c}^{-1})^{**}.

According to (
5.9
) these two nonnegative relations are extensions
of
(S-c)^{-1}
, they satisfy

(S-c)^{-1}\subset((S-c)^{-1})_{\rm f}\subset((S-c)^{-1})_{\rm F},

and they all have the same lower bound.

6.
A characterization of the Kreĭn type extensions

Let
S\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a semibounded relation with lower bound
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}

and let
{\mathfrak{s}}(S)\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
be the corresponding closed form with representing map

(J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg}
as in (
2.9
). Then the semibounded selfadjoint relation

S_{{\rm K},c}
, determined by the closed form
{{\mathfrak{s}}}(S)
, is clearly given by

(6.1)

S_{{\rm K},c}=c+((J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg})^{*}(J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg}=c+J_{c}^{**}J_{c}^{*},

where the last identity follows from Corollary
3.3
.
By Corollary
3.3
the relation
S_{{\rm K},c}

does not depend on the particular
choice of representing map
Q_{c}
; cf.
[
21
]
.
It is a direct consequence of (
2.6
)
(using
Q_{c}\subset J_{c}^{*}
in (
2.6
)) that
S_{{\rm K},c}

is an extension of
S
, i.e.,

S\subset S_{{\rm K},c}.

The multivalued part
{\rm mul\,}S_{{\rm K},c}
satisfies

{\rm mul\,}S_{{\rm K},c}={\rm mul\,}J_{c}^{**},

and the formula (
3.5
) in Theorem
3.1
offers a description for this.
By Proposition
2.3
the lower bound of
{\mathfrak{s}}(S)
is
c
and consequently
c

is also the lower bound of
S_{{\rm K},c}
; cf.
[
21
]
.
The semibounded selfadjoint extension
S_{{\rm K},c}
is the so-called

Kreĭn type extension
relative to the point
c\leq\gamma
.
By Corollary
4.3
one sees immediately that
S_{{\rm K},c}\leq S_{\rm F}
.
From the right-hand side of (
6.1
) it follows that

S_{{\rm K},c}-c=J_{c}^{**}J_{c}^{*}=\left((J_{c}^{-1})^{*}(J_{c}^{-1})^{**}\right)^{-1}=\left(((S-c)^{-1})_{\rm F}\right)^{-1},

where for the last identity (
5.12
) was used.
Therefore, one concludes that the Kreĭn type extension

S_{{\rm K},c}
can be expressed in terms of the
Friedrichs extension induced by the closable form
{\mathfrak{t}}((S-c)^{-1})
:

(6.2)

S_{{\rm K},c}=c+(((S-c)^{-1})_{\rm F})^{-1}.

Let
T
be any linear extension
T
of
S
with
S\subset T\subset S_{{\rm K},c}
.
In conjunction with (
4.5
)
it becomes clear that

{\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}((\overline{T}-c)^{-1})={\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}((S-c)^{-1})\quad\mbox{and}\quad(\overline{T})_{{\rm K},c}=S_{{\rm K},c},

since
{\rm clos\,}(T-c)^{-1}=(\overline{T}-c)^{-1}
.
In particular, one has
(\overline{S})_{{\rm K},c}=S_{{\rm K},c}
.
There is also a weak version of the Kreĭn type extension,
but it has to be approached in an indirect way (since the form
{\mathfrak{s}}(S)
is closed).
Inspired by the definition in (
6.1
)
the semibounded symmetric relation
S_{{\rm k},c}
will be introduced as follows

(6.3)

S_{{\rm k},c}:=c+J_{c}(J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg}=c+J_{c}J_{c}^{*},

where the last identity follows from Corollary
3.3
.
By Corollary
3.3
the relation
S_{{\rm k},c}
does not depend on the particular
choice of representing map
Q_{c}
.
It is a direct consequence of (
2.6
) or, equivalently, of (
5.9
)
(using
Q_{c}\subset J_{c}^{*}
) that
S_{{\rm k},c}

is an extension of
S
and that, in fact,

S\subset S_{{\rm k},c}=c+J_{c}J_{c}^{*}\subset S_{{\rm K},c}=c+J_{c}^{**}J_{c}^{*}.

Moreover, (
3.3
) together with Theorem
3.1
implies that the multivalued parts satisfy

{\rm mul\,}S_{{\rm k},c}={\rm ran\,}(S-c)\cap{\rm mul\,}S^{*}\subset{\rm mul\,}S_{{\rm K},c}={\rm mul\,}J_{c}^{**}.

Since
m(S_{{\rm K},c})=c
, it follows from (
6.1
) and (
6.3
)
that
m(S_{{\rm k},c})=c
.
The semibounded selfadjoint extension
S_{{\rm k},c}
is called the

weak Kreĭn type extension
relative to the point
c\leq\gamma
.
From the right-hand side of (
6.3
) it follows that

S_{{\rm k},c}-c=J_{c}J_{c}^{*}=\left((J_{c}^{-1})^{*}J_{c}^{-1}\right)^{-1}=\left(((S-c)^{-1})_{\rm f}\right)^{-1},

where for the last identity (
5.11
) was used. Therefore one concludes

(6.4)

S_{{\rm k},c}=c+(((S-c)^{-1})_{\rm f})^{-1}.

Although
S_{{\rm k},c}
has been defined directly in (
6.3
),
it is now clear from

(S_{{\rm k},c}-c)^{-1}=((S-c)^{-1})_{\rm f},

that
(S_{{\rm k},c}-c)^{-1}
can actually be expressed
in terms of the weak Friedrichs extension induced
by the closable form
{\mathfrak{t}}((S-c)^{-1})
.

With (
6.1
) and (
6.3
) in mind, it is now possible
to translate Theorem
4.1
to the present context.
Thus the extensions
S_{{\rm k},c}
and
S_{{\rm K},c}
are intrinsically
characterized in the following theorem. Remember for (a) and (b) that

{\rm ran\,}(S-c)={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}((S-c)^{-1})\subset{\rm dom\,}{\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}((S-c)^{-1})

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
S\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a semibounded linear relation
with lower bound
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}

and let
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)
be the corresponding semibounded form in (
2.1
).
Let
c\leq\gamma
.
Then the following statements hold:

(a)

The Kreĭn type extension
S_{{\rm K},c}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})

of
S
in (
6.1
) is given by

(6.5)

S_{{\rm K},c}=\big\{\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S^{*}:\,\varphi^{\prime}-c\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}\bar{\mathfrak{t}}((S-c)^{-1})\big\}.

If
H\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
is a selfadjoint extension of
S
with

{\rm ran\,}(H-c)\subset{\rm dom\,}\bar{\mathfrak{t}}((S-c)^{-1}),

then
H=S_{{\rm K},c}
.

(b)

The weak Kreĭn type extension

S_{{\rm k},c}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})

of
S
in (
6.3
) is given by

(6.6)

S_{{\rm k},c}=S\hplus{{\widehat{\mathfrak{N}}}}_{c}(S^{*}).

If
H\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
is a symmetric extension of
S
with

{\rm ran\,}(H-c)\subset{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}((S-c)^{-1})={\rm ran\,}(S-c),

then
H\subset S_{{\rm k},c}
.

(c)

For all
c<\gamma

S_{{\rm K},c}=\overline{S}\hplus{{\widehat{\mathfrak{N}}}}_{c}(S^{*}),

and the identity
S_{{\rm k},\gamma}=S_{{\rm K},\gamma}
holds
if and only if

(6.7)

{\rm ran\,}(S-\gamma)={\rm\overline{ran}\,}(S-\gamma)\cap{\rm ran\,}(S^{*}-\gamma).

In particular the identity
S_{{\rm k},\gamma}=S_{{\rm K},\gamma}

holds if
{\rm ran\,}(S-\gamma)
is closed.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.1
one sees that

((S-c)^{-1})_{\rm F}=\big\{\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in((S-c)^{-1})^{*}:\,\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}\bar{{\mathfrak{t}}}(S-c)^{-1})\,\big\}

and, likewise,

((S-c)^{-1})_{\rm f}=(S-c)^{-1}\hplus\left(\{0\}\times{{\rm ker\,}\,}(S^{*}-c)\right).

It follows from (
6.4
) and (
6.2
) that

(S_{\rm k,c}-c)^{-1}=((S-c)^{-1})_{\rm f},\quad\mbox{and}\quad(S_{\rm K,c}-c)^{-1}=((S-c)^{-1})_{\rm F},

and then, by Theorem
4.1
,
(
6.6
) and (
6.5
) follow.
Also by Theorem
4.1
one has for
c\leq\gamma
that

S_{{\rm k},c}=S_{{\rm K},c}
if and only if (
6.7
) holds.

{\rm ran\,}(S-c)={\rm\overline{ran}\,}(S-c)\cap{\rm ran\,}(S^{*}-c)

holds. For every
c\leq\gamma
the inclusion

\overline{S}\hplus{{\widehat{\mathfrak{N}}}}_{c}(S^{*})\subset S_{{\rm K},c}

is clear, and for
c<\gamma
there is actually equality
as both sides are selfadjoint; cf.
[
7
, Lemma 5.4.1]
.
∎

The linear subspace
{\rm dom\,}(S_{{\rm K},c}-c)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}

can be characterized in terms of the representing map

(J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg}
for
{\mathfrak{s}}(S)\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
.
By (
6.1
) one has
S_{{\rm K},c}-c=J_{c}^{**}J_{c}^{*}

and therefore

{\rm dom\,}(S_{{\rm K},c}-c)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}={\rm dom\,}J_{c}^{*}.

Thus the following result is clear from Theorem
3.1
,
cf.
[
1
,
5
,
12
,
14
,
17
]
.

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
S\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be as in Theorem
6.1

and let
S_{{\rm K},c}
be its Kreĭn type extension.
Then
\psi^{\prime}\in{\rm dom\,}(S_{{\rm K},c}-c)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}
if and only if
there exists
C_{\psi^{\prime}}<\infty
such that

(6.8)

|(\varphi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})|^{2}\leq C_{\psi^{\prime}}(\varphi^{\prime},\varphi)\quad\text{ for all }\;\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S-c.

The description of
{\rm dom\,}(S_{{\rm K},c}-c)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}
can also be obtained from Corollary
4.2

by means of the identity (
6.2
). Moreover, it follows from (
6.2
) that

{\rm ran\,}(S_{{\rm K},c}-c)^{{\frac{1}{2}}}={\rm dom\,}(((S-c)^{-1})_{\rm F})^{{\frac{1}{2}}}={\rm dom\,}{\bar{\mathfrak{t}}}((S-c)^{-1}),

where the right-hand side can be described by means of Theorem
2.5
.

Let
S\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be semibounded with lower bound
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}

and let
c\leq\gamma
.
The Kreĭn type extension
S_{{\rm K},c}
of
S
has lower bound
c

and, in fact, it is the smallest semibounded selfadjoint
extension of
S
which is bounded below by
c
.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
S\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a semibounded linear relation with lower bound

\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
c\leq\gamma
.
Let
H\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a semibounded selfadjoint extension of
S
,
then there is the equivalence

c\leq H\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad{\mathfrak{t}}_{S_{{{\rm K},c}}}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}_{H}.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
) Assume that
c\leq H
. Since
S\subset H
,
it follows that
(S-c)^{-1}\subset(H-c)^{-1}
.
Thus also
(H-c)^{-1}\leq((S-c)^{-1})_{\rm F}
(see (
4.12
)) and,
by antitonicity, one obtains

(((S-c)^{-1})_{\rm F})^{-1}\leq((H-c)^{-1})^{-1}=H-c,

see
[
7
]
, or by (
6.2
)
S_{{\rm K},c}\leq H
.
Consequently,
{\mathfrak{t}}_{S_{{\rm K},c}}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}_{H}
.

(
\Leftarrow
) This implication is straightforward.
∎

For a semibounded relation
S\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})

it is clear that
S_{\rm F}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
is an operator if and only if
S
is densely defined;
and in this case all selfadjoint extensions of
S
are operators.
The question when
S_{{\rm K},c}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
is an operator will be addressed now;
note that then
S
is automatically an operator.
First observe, that if
S
is a closed (or closable) operator, then by Theorem
6.1

{\rm dom\,}S_{{\rm K},c}={\rm dom\,}\overline{S}+{{\rm ker\,}\,}(S^{*}-c),\quad c<\gamma,

is dense in
{\mathfrak{H}}
, and the selfadjoint relation
S_{{\rm K},c}
with
c<\gamma

is an operator.
The next proposition deals with the case that
S
is an operator
which is not necessarily closed or closable;
see also
[
14
, Proposition 4.9]
,
[
7
, Corollary 5.4.8]
,

[
32
, Theorem 4.1]
, and
[
33
, Theorem 2.1]
.
The limit criterion (
6.9
) below (with
c=0
) was introduced in

[
1
]
for a closed nonnegative operator
S
, which was then called

positively closable
.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
S\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a semibounded operator with lower bound
\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}

and let
c\leq\gamma
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

the Krein type extension
S_{{\rm K},c}
is an operator or,
equivalently, the form
{\mathfrak{s}}(S)
is densely defined;

(ii)

Let

\begin{split}({\mathfrak{t}}(H)-c)[f-h,f-h]&-\|(J_{c}^{*})_{\rm op}f-Q_{c}h\|^{2}\\

(a) Since

(7.10)

8.
Symmetric relations whose domain and range are orthogonal

Let
S\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a linear relation.
Then the
numerical range

{\mathcal{W}}(S)
of
S
is defined by

{\mathcal{W}}(S)=\big\{(\varphi^{\prime},\varphi):\,\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S:\,\|\varphi\|=1\,\big\}\subset{\mathbb{C}},

when
{\rm dom\,}S\neq\{0\}
, and by
\{0\}\subset{\mathbb{C}}

if
{\rm dom\,}S=\{0\}
, i.e., if
S
is purely multivalued.
For linear relations
S\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
the numerical range

{\mathcal{W}}(S)
is a convex set; see
[
22
, Proposition 2.18]
.
Moreover, it is clear that all eigenvalues in
{\mathbb{C}}
of
S
belong
to
{\mathcal{W}}(S)
.
Clearly, the numerical range of the inverse of
S
is given by

{\mathcal{W}}(S^{-1})=\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}:\overline{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{W}}(S)\,\}.

The interest in this section is in linear relations
S\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})

for which
{\mathcal{W}}(S)=\{0\}
; this class was discussed earlier in
[
8
]
.
Here is a characterization of it; see
[
13
]
and also
[
27
]
.

Lemma 8.1
.

For every linear relation
S\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

{\mathcal{W}}(S)=\{0\}
;

(ii)

{\rm dom\,}S\perp{\rm ran\,}S
.

Now assume that
{\mathcal{W}}(S)=\{0\}
. Then the linear relation
S
is nonnegative
and the form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
, determined by
S
in (
2.1
),
is trivial by Lemma
8.1
:

{\mathfrak{t}}(S)[\varphi,\psi]=(\varphi^{\prime},\psi)=0,\quad\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\},\,\{\psi,\psi^{\prime}\}\in S.

Thus
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
is the null form defined on
{\rm dom\,}S
:

{\mathfrak{t}}(S)=0,\quad{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}(S)={\rm dom\,}S.

This shows that
\gamma=0
and, therefore, in (
2.2
)
one may choose
c=0
and a representing map
Q\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
by

(8.1)

Q={\rm dom\,}S\times\{0\}.

Clearly, the corresponding companion relation
J\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
, given by (
2.3
), reduces to

J=\big\{\,\{0,\varphi^{\prime}\}:\,\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in S\,\big\}=\{0\}\times{\rm ran\,}S,

so that

J^{*}=({\rm ran\,}S)^{\perp}\times{\mathfrak{H}}={{\rm ker\,}\,}S^{*}\times{\mathfrak{H}}.

Since
{\rm mul\,}J^{*}={\mathfrak{H}}
, it follows that

(J^{*})_{\rm reg}={{\rm ker\,}\,}S^{*}\times\{0\}.

Hence the form
{\mathfrak{s}}(S)
defined in (
2.9
) is given by

{\mathfrak{s}}(S)[\varphi,\psi]=0,\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{s}}(S)={{\rm ker\,}\,}S^{*}.

Note that (
2.4
) implies
{\rm dom\,}S\subset{{\rm ker\,}\,}S^{*}
and
{\rm ran\,}S\subset{\rm mul\,}S^{*}
,
which can be seen immediately from the assumption
{\mathcal{W}}(S)=\{0\}
.
It is clear from these observations that
{\mathfrak{t}}(S)\subset{\mathfrak{s}}(S)
.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
S\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a linear relation such that
{\mathcal{W}}(S)=\{0\}
.
Then the Friedrichs extension of
S
is given by

(8.2)

S_{\rm F}={\rm\overline{dom}\,}S\times{\rm mul\,}S^{*}={\rm\overline{ker}\,}S\times{\rm mul\,}S^{*},

and the Kreĭn type extension
S_{\rm K}=S_{{\rm K},0}
is given by

(8.3)

S_{\rm K}={{\rm ker\,}\,}S^{*}\times{\rm\overline{ran}\,}S={{\rm ker\,}\,}S^{*}\times{\rm\overline{mul}\,}S.

Proof.

It follows from the representation (
8.1
) that

Q^{**}={\rm\overline{dom}\,}S\times\{0\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad Q^{*}={\mathfrak{H}}\times{\rm mul\,}S^{*}.

By (
4.1
) one has
S_{\rm F}=Q^{*}Q^{**}
, which gives (
8.2
).
Likewise, one has

((J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg})^{*}={\mathfrak{H}}\times{\rm\overline{ran}\,}S.

By (
6.1
) one has
S_{\rm K}=((J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg})^{*}(J_{c}^{*})_{\rm reg}
,
which gives (
8.3
).
∎

Thus one sees that both nonnegative selfadjoint extensions
S_{\rm F}
and
S_{\rm K}

are singular relations.
It follows from Lemma
8.2
that
S_{\rm F}=S_{\rm K}
if and only if

{{\rm ker\,}\,}S^{*}={\rm\overline{dom}\,}S\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\rm mul\,}S^{*}={\rm\overline{ran}\,}S.

Furthermore,

S_{\rm F}\cap S_{\rm K}={\rm\overline{ker}\,}S\times{\rm\overline{ran}\,}S,

which implies that
S_{\rm F}\cap S_{\rm K}=\overline{S}
.
In addition, observe that the description in Corollary
4.2
, when applied
to
S
with
{\mathcal{W}}(S)=\{0\}
, shows that

{\rm ran\,}S_{\rm F}^{{\frac{1}{2}}}=({\rm dom\,}S)^{\perp}={\rm mul\,}S^{*}={\rm mul\,}S_{\rm F},

which agrees with (

). Similarly Corollary
6.2
yields

and this agrees with (

).

Notice that if
{\mathcal{W}}(S)=\{0\}
, then by Lemma
8.2
it follows that

{\mathcal{W}}(S_{\rm F})=\{0\}
and, likewise,
{\mathcal{W}}(S_{\rm K})=\{0\}
.
This observation gives a connection to the nonnegative extremal extensions of
S

(i.e., extremal with respect to
0
).

Proposition 8.3
.

Let
S\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
be nonnegative relation such that
{\mathcal{W}}(S)=\{0\}
.
Let
H\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a selfadjoint extension of
S
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

{\mathcal{W}}(H)=\{0\}
;

(ii)

H
is a nonnegative extremal extension of
S
.

Proof.

The assumption
{\mathcal{W}}(S)=\{0\}
implies that
{\mathcal{W}}(S_{\rm K})=\{0\}
.
In other words,
{\rm dom\,}S_{\rm K}\perp{\rm ran\,}S_{\rm K}
. Hence the form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S_{\rm K})

corresponding to
S_{\rm K}
is the zero form on
{\rm dom\,}S_{\rm K}
.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) Assume that
{\mathcal{W}}(H)=\{0\}
. Then clearly
{\mathfrak{t}}(H)
is the zero form
and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{H}=0
. Since the assumption
{\mathcal{W}}(H)=\{0\}
implies that
{\mathcal{W}}(S)=\{0\}
,
it follows from Lemma
8.2
that
{\mathcal{W}}(S_{\rm F})=0
and
{\mathcal{W}}(S_{\rm K})=0
.
Thanks to
H
being a nonnegative selfadjoint extension of
S
, Corollary
6.3

implies that
{\mathfrak{t}}_{S_{{{\rm K}}}}\leq{\mathfrak{t}}_{H}
,
which gives
{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{H}\subset{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}_{S_{{{\rm K}}}}
.
Recall that
{\mathfrak{t}}_{S_{\rm F}}\subset{\mathfrak{t}}_{H}
.
All these forms are null forms and one obtains

{\mathfrak{t}}_{S_{\rm F}}\subset{\mathfrak{t}}_{H}\subset{\mathfrak{t}}_{S_{{{\rm K}}}}
.
Now Theorem
7.4
shows that
H
is extremal.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) Let
H
be an extremal extension of
S
.
Then by Theorem
7.4
one has
{\mathfrak{t}}(H)\subset{\mathfrak{t}}(S_{\rm K})
.
Hence
{\mathfrak{t}}(H)
is the zero form on
{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}(H)
. In particular, it follows that
{\mathcal{W}}(H)={0}
.
∎

9.
Symmetric relations defined by semibounded quadratic forms

In this section attention is paid to the representation of a general semibounded form

{\mathfrak{t}}\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
as given in
[
21
]
. It is known that such a form induces
a semibounded relation
S_{\mathfrak{t}}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
and a semibounded
selfadjoint relation
{{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
which extends
S_{\mathfrak{t}}
.
The relation
{{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}}
is the Friedrichs extension of
an, in general, nontrivial extension of
S_{\mathfrak{t}}
.
The semibounded relation
S_{\mathfrak{t}}
itself gives rise to a semibounded form

{\mathfrak{t}}(S_{\mathfrak{t}})
, which induces a Friedrichs extension of
S_{\mathfrak{t}}
.

The general semibounded form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
is given by

(9.1)

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]=c(\varphi,\psi)+(Q\varphi,Q\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}Q,

for some
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
and a representing map
Q\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
.
Note that the form
{\mathfrak{t}}
is closable (closed) or singular if and only if the
operator
Q
is closable (closed) or singular, respectively.
As mentioned above, the form
{\mathfrak{t}}
induces
a semibounded selfadjoint relation
{{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})

and a semibounded relation
S_{\mathfrak{t}}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
, and they are given by

(9.2)

{{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}}=c+Q^{*}Q^{**}\quad\mbox{and}\quad S_{\mathfrak{t}}=c+Q^{*}Q,

which are characterized in
[
21
, Theorem 6.1]
, see also
[
2
, Theorem 3.2]
.
It follows from (
9.2
) that

S_{\mathfrak{t}}\subset{{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}}.

Denote the regular
part of
Q
by
Q_{\rm reg}
and the regular.
part of
{\mathfrak{t}}
by
{\mathfrak{r}}
, so that

(9.3)

{\mathfrak{r}}[\varphi,\psi]=c(\varphi,\psi)+(Q_{\rm reg}\varphi,Q_{\rm reg}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}Q,

is a regular (closable) form in
\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
; its closure is given by

\bar{\mathfrak{r}}[\varphi,\psi]=c(\varphi,\psi)+(Q_{\rm reg}^{**}\varphi,Q_{\rm reg}^{**}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}Q^{**}.

Then, likewise, there are
a semibounded selfadjoint relation
{{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{r}}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})

and a semibounded relation
S_{\mathfrak{r}}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
, given by

(9.4)

S_{{\mathfrak{r}}}=c+(Q_{c,\rm reg})^{*}Q_{c,\rm reg}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{{\widetilde{A}}}_{{\mathfrak{r}}}=c+(Q_{c,\rm reg})^{*}(Q_{c,\rm reg})^{**},

and they satisfy

S_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\subset S_{{\mathfrak{r}}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{{\widetilde{A}}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}={{\widetilde{A}}}_{{\mathfrak{r}}}=A_{\bar{\mathfrak{r}}};

where
A_{\bar{\mathfrak{r}}}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
is the semibounded selfadjoint relation
corresponding to the
closed form
\bar{\mathfrak{r}}\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
; see
[
21
, Theorem 6.5]
.
It is clear from (
9.4
) that
{{\widetilde{A}}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}}={{\widetilde{A}}}_{{\mathfrak{r}}}=A_{\bar{\mathfrak{r}}}

is the Friedrichs extension
of the semibounded relation
S_{{\mathfrak{r}}}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}})
; see Section
4
.

Next consider the connection of the above facts with the constructions
in the present paper.
Let the semibounded form
{\mathfrak{t}}\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})

be given by (
9.1
) and let the
corresponding semibounded relation
S_{\mathfrak{t}}
be as in (
9.2
).
Then this relation
S_{\mathfrak{t}}
induces a semibounded form

{\mathfrak{t}}(S_{\mathfrak{t}})\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
as in Definition
2.1
.
First observe that the domain of
S_{\mathfrak{t}}
in (
9.2
)
satisfies
{\rm dom\,}S_{\mathfrak{t}}={\mathcal{R}}
,
when the linear subspace
{\mathcal{R}}\subset{\rm dom\,}Q
is defined by

{\mathcal{R}}=\big\{\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}Q:\,Q\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}Q^{*}\big\},

so that in general one has

\{0\}\subset{\mathcal{R}}\subset{\rm dom\,}Q.

Define the operator
\dot{Q}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})

as the restriction of
Q
to the linear subspace
{\mathcal{R}}\subset{\rm dom\,}Q
:

\dot{Q}=Q{\upharpoonright\,}{\mathcal{R}},\quad\mbox{where}\quad{\rm dom\,}\dot{Q}={\mathcal{R}}.

Then it follows directly from the definition that with
\varphi\in{\mathcal{R}}
one has

Q\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}Q^{*}\subset({\rm mul\,}Q^{**})^{\perp},

which shows
Q\varphi=(I-P)Q\varphi
,
when
P
is the orthogonal projection onto
{\rm mul\,}Q^{**}
.
Hence, one sees the inclusion

(9.5)

\dot{Q}\subset Q_{\rm reg},

which shows that
\dot{Q}\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
is closable.
Observe that
\dot{Q}=Q_{\rm reg}
if and only if
{\mathcal{R}}={\rm dom\,}Q
.
Now let
\{\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\},\{\psi,\psi^{\prime}\}\in S_{\mathfrak{t}}
,
so that in particular
\{Q\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\}\in Q^{*}
and
\{\psi,Q\psi\}\in Q
.
As a consequence, it follows by Definition
2.2

that the form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S_{\mathfrak{t}})\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})

is given by

(9.6)

{\mathfrak{t}}(S_{\mathfrak{t}})[\varphi,\psi]=c(\varphi,\psi)+(\dot{Q}\varphi,\dot{Q}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}\dot{Q}={\rm dom\,}S_{\mathfrak{t}}={\mathcal{R}},

which confirms that
{\mathfrak{t}}(S_{\mathfrak{t}})
is closable.
It follows from the inclusion (
9.5
) that

{\mathfrak{t}}(S_{\mathfrak{t}})\subset{\mathfrak{r}},

and, since both forms are closable, one obtains.

\bar{\mathfrak{t}}(S_{\mathfrak{t}})\subset\bar{\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{r}}}
.
This last inclusion implies the inequality
\bar{\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathfrak{r}}}\leq\bar{\mathfrak{t}}(S_{\mathfrak{t}})

or, equivalently,

c+Q_{\rm reg}^{*}Q_{\rm reg}^{**}=(S_{\mathfrak{r}})_{\rm F}\leq(S_{\mathfrak{t}})_{\rm F}=c+\dot{Q}^{*}\dot{Q}^{**},

see
[
21
]
. This shows the ordering between the Friedrichs extension of
S_{\mathfrak{r}}

and the Friedrichs extension of
S_{\mathfrak{t}}
.

The above discussion will be illustrated by the following extreme example
involving a nonnegative and singular form.

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{t}}\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a nonnegative singular form
and let
Q\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
be a singular representing map for
{\mathfrak{t}}
, so that

{\mathfrak{t}}[\varphi,\psi]=(Q\varphi,Q\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}={\rm dom\,}Q,

in which case
Q_{\rm reg}=0
and
{\mathfrak{r}}={\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}=0
.
Then the following relations, corresponding
to the forms
{\mathfrak{t}}
and
{\mathfrak{r}}
in (
9.1
) and (
9.3
), are singular:

(9.7)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}S_{{\mathfrak{r}}}=(Q_{\rm reg})^{*}Q_{\rm reg}={\rm dom\,}Q\times({\rm dom\,}Q)^{\perp},\\
{{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}}=(Q_{\rm reg})^{*}(Q_{\rm reg})^{**}={{\widetilde{A}}}_{{\mathfrak{r}}}={\rm\overline{dom}\,}Q\times({\rm dom\,}Q)^{\perp},\end{array}\right.

and
S_{{\mathfrak{r}}}
is essentially selfadjoint. Furthermore, the following relations
corresponding to the form
{\mathfrak{t}}(S_{\mathfrak{t}})
in (
9.6
), are singular,

(9.8)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}S_{\mathfrak{t}}=Q^{*}Q={{\rm ker\,}\,}Q\times({\rm dom\,}Q)^{\perp},\\
(S_{\mathfrak{t}})_{\rm F}=Q^{*}Q^{**}={\rm\overline{ker}\,}Q\times({{\rm ker\,}\,}Q)^{\perp},\end{array}\right.

and
S_{\mathfrak{t}}
is essentially selfadjoint if and only if
{\rm\overline{dom}\,}Q={\rm\overline{ker}\,}Q
.

Proof.

Since
{\mathfrak{t}}
is singular one has
{\mathfrak{r}}={\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}=0
.
Thus, equivalently,
Q=Q_{\rm sing}
or
Q_{\rm reg}=0
. Therefore
it is clear that

Q_{\rm reg}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}\times\{0\},\quad(Q_{\rm reg})^{*}={\mathfrak{K}}\times({\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}})^{\perp},\quad(Q_{\rm reg})^{**}={\rm\overline{dom}\,}{\mathfrak{t}}\times\{0\},

from which the assertions in (
9.7
) follow.

Since
Q
is singular, recall that
{\rm dom\,}Q^{*}={{\rm ker\,}\,}Q^{*}
.
Let
\{f,f^{\prime}\}\in Q^{*}Q
, so that
\{f,\varphi\}\in Q
and
\{\varphi,f^{\prime}\}\in Q^{*}

for some
\varphi\in{\mathfrak{K}}
. In fact,
\varphi\in{\rm ran\,}Q\cap{{\rm ker\,}\,}Q^{*}=\{0\}
.
Thus
f\in{{\rm ker\,}\,}Q
and
f^{\prime}\in{\rm mul\,}Q^{*}
, which gives
\{f,f^{\prime}\}\in{{\rm ker\,}\,}Q\times{\rm mul\,}Q^{*}
.
Therefore
Q^{*}Q\subset{{\rm ker\,}\,}Q\times{\rm mul\,}Q^{*}
. Since the reverse inclusion is obvious,
one obtains the identity
Q^{*}Q={{\rm ker\,}\,}Q\times{\rm mul\,}Q^{*}
. Thus (
9.8
) follows.
∎

As an application a Lebesgue decomposition of a form
involving nonzero regular and singular parts is given in the following example.

Example 9.2
.

Let
{\mathfrak{t}}\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
be the singular form from Proposition
9.1

and assume without loss of generality that the singular representing map

Q\in\mathbf{L}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
for
{\mathfrak{t}}
is minimal; see
[
21
]
).
Since
{\mathfrak{t}}
is singular its lower bound satisfies
\gamma=0
, see
[
21
]
.
Observe that
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}
has representing map
Q_{\rm reg}={\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}\times\{0\}
,
while
{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm sing}
has
Q
as a representing map.
Now consider the shifted form
{\mathfrak{t}}-c\in\mathbf{F}({\mathfrak{H}})
with
c<\gamma=0
.
Observe that

({\mathfrak{t}}-c)_{\rm reg}={\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm reg}-c=-c\quad\mbox{and}\quad({\mathfrak{t}}-c)_{\rm sing}={\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm sing}={\mathfrak{t}},

see
[
34
, Corollary 2.3]
or
[
21
]
. This leads to
the Lebesgue decomposition of the form
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
:

(9.9)

{\mathfrak{t}}-c=({\mathfrak{t}}-c)_{\rm reg}+({\mathfrak{t}}-c)_{\rm sing}=-c+{\mathfrak{t}}_{\rm sing}=-c+{\mathfrak{t}},

see
[
18
,
20
,
21
]
.
In order to determine a representing map for
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
,
first define for
c<\gamma

(9.10)

q_{c}:{\mathfrak{H}}\to{\mathfrak{H}}={\rm\overline{dom}\,}{\mathfrak{t}}\oplus({\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}})^{\perp},\quad\varphi\to\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{|c|}\varphi\\
0\end{pmatrix},\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}},

so that
q_{c}
is a representing map for the nonnegative form

-c(\varphi,\psi)
,
\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
.
It is clear that the column operator
Q_{c}
defined by

(9.11)

Q_{c}=\begin{pmatrix}q_{c}\\
Q\end{pmatrix}:{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}\to{\mathfrak{H}}\times{\mathfrak{K}},

is a representing map for
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
, which in general is not minimal.
Consider the Lebesgue decomposition of
Q
:
Q=Q_{\rm reg}+Q_{\rm sing}
.
Since
Q
is singular and
q_{c}
is a bounded operator on
{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}

one sees that

{\rm mul\,}Q_{c}^{**}=\{0\}\times{\rm mul\,}Q^{**}=\{0\}\times{\mathfrak{K}}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}\times{\mathfrak{K}},

where the second identity holds by minimality of
Q
.
Hence, with
P_{\mathfrak{K}}
the orthogonal projection from

{\mathfrak{H}}\times{\mathfrak{K}}
onto
{\mathfrak{K}}
, it follows that

(9.12)

Q_{c,{\rm reg}}=(I_{{\mathfrak{H}}\times{\mathfrak{K}}}-P_{\mathfrak{K}})Q_{c}=q_{c}\quad\mbox{and}\quad Q_{c,{\rm sing}}=P_{\mathfrak{K}}Q_{c}=Q

are representing maps for
({\mathfrak{t}}-c)_{\rm reg}
and
({\mathfrak{t}}-c)_{\rm sing}
.

Next the interest is in the semibounded relations generated
by the form
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
, given in terms of the
corresponding representing map
Q_{c}
.
It follows from the definition in (
9.10
) that

(9.13)

(Q_{c,{\rm reg}})^{*}=q_{c}^{*}=\big\{\{\psi,\psi^{\prime}\}\in{\mathfrak{H}}\times{\mathfrak{H}}:\,\psi^{\prime}-\sqrt{|c|}\,\psi\in({\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}})^{\perp}\big\}.

Therefore it is clear from (
9.13
) that

(9.14)

(Q_{c,{\rm reg}})^{*}Q_{c,{\rm reg}}=q_{c}^{*}q_{c}=\big\{\{\varphi,-c\varphi+\chi\}:\,\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}},\,\,\chi\in({\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}})^{\perp}\big\},

and

(9.15)

(Q_{c,{\rm reg}})^{*}(Q_{c,{\rm reg}})^{**}=q_{c}^{*}q_{c}^{**}=\big\{\{\varphi,-c\varphi+\chi\}:\,\varphi\in{\rm\overline{dom}\,}{\mathfrak{t}},\,\,\chi\in({\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}})^{\perp}\big\}.

On the other hand, using (
9.11
) and Proposition
9.1
one concludes that

Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}=Q^{*}Q+q_{c}^{*}q_{c}=\big\{\{\varphi,-c\varphi+\chi\}:\,\varphi\in{{\rm ker\,}\,}{\mathfrak{t}},\,\,\chi\in({\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}})^{\perp}\big\},

and

Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}^{**}=Q^{*}Q^{**}+q_{c}^{*}q_{c}^{**}=\big\{\{\varphi,-c\varphi+\chi\}:\,\varphi\in{\rm\overline{ker}\,}{\mathfrak{t}},\,\,\chi\in({\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}})^{\perp}\big\}.

Thus one has

Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}=Q^{*}Q-c\quad\mbox{and}\quad Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}^{**}=Q^{*}Q^{**}-c

Therefore the relations
S_{{\mathfrak{t}}-c}
and
{{\widetilde{A}}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}-c}

associated with the shifted form
{\mathfrak{t}}-c
in (
9.2
) are given by

S_{{\mathfrak{t}}-c}=Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}=Q^{*}Q-c,\quad{{\widetilde{A}}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}-c}=Q_{c}^{*}Q_{c}^{**}=Q^{*}Q^{**}-c,

with the explicit formulas for
S_{\mathfrak{t}}=Q^{*}Q
and
{{\widetilde{A}}}_{\mathfrak{t}}=Q^{*}Q^{**}
as given in Proposition
9.1
.
In particular,
(S_{{\mathfrak{t}}-c})^{**}\subset{{\widetilde{A}}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}-c}
,
but
(S_{{\mathfrak{t}}-c})^{**}\neq{{\widetilde{A}}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}-c}
whenever the form
{\mathfrak{t}}
is not a zero form on
{\rm dom\,}{\mathfrak{t}}
.
Finally, from (
9.7
), (
9.14
) and (
9.15
), it is directly seen that

(Q_{c,{\rm reg}})^{*}(Q_{c,{\rm reg}})^{**}=q_{c}^{*}q_{c}^{**}=Q^{*}Q^{**}-c={{\widetilde{A}}}_{{\mathfrak{t}}-c}=(S_{{\mathfrak{r}}-c})^{**}=(S_{\mathfrak{r}})^{**}-c.

The connection between Lebesgue decompositions of linear relations in (
9.12
) and
Lebesgue decompositions of semibounded forms in (
9.9
)
is a general fact, which relies on the existence of representing maps for forms;
for details see
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
.

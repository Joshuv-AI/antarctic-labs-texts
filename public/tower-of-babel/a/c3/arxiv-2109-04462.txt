Markov limits of steady states of the KPZ equation on an interval
Włodek Bryc, Alexey Kuznetsov

Abstract
. This paper builds upon the research of Corwin and Knizel [ CK21 ] who proved the existence of stationary measures for the KPZ equation on an interval and characterized them through a Laplace transform formula. Ref. [ BKWW21 ] found a probabilistic description of the stationary measures in terms of a Doob transform of some Markov kernels; essentially at the same time, another description connecting the stationary measures to the exponential functionals of the Brownian motion appeared in [ BL22 ] . Our first main result clarifies and proves the equivalence of the two probabilistic description of these stationary measures. We then use the Markovian description to give rigorous proofs of some of the results claimed in [ BL22 ] . We analyze how the stationary measures of the KPZ equation on [ 0 , τ ] [0,\tau] behave at large scale, as τ \tau goes to infinity. We also analyze the behaviour of the stationary measures of the KPZ equation on [ 0 , τ ] [0,\tau] without rescaling, when τ \tau goes to infinity. Finally, we analyze the measures on [ 0 , ∞ ) [0,\infty) at large scale, which according to [ BL22 ] should correspond to stationary measures of a hypothetical KPZ fixed point on [ 0 , ∞ ) [0,\infty) .

1.
Notation and background

The Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ)
equation was proposed in
[
KPZ86
]
as a model for the evolution of the profile of a growing interface driven by the space-time white noise
\zeta
. The interface profile is
described by a height function
H(t,s)
, where
t\geq 0
is a time variable and
s
is a spatial variable, which in one spatial dimension
formally satisfies

(1.1)

\partial_{t}H(t,s)=\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{s}^{2}H(t,s)+\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{s}H(t,s)\right)^{2}+\zeta(t,s).

We refer the reader to the reviews
[
Cor12
,
Qua11
]
about the KPZ equation and its universality class.

We are interested in (
1.1
) on a
finite interval,
s\in[0,1]
, with Neumann boundary conditions

(1.2)

\partial_{s}H(t,0)=\mathsf{c}/2,\;\partial_{s}H(t,1)=-\mathsf{a}/2.

Since the KPZ equation is ill-posed, the standard notion of the solution is in the Cole-Hopf sense, i. e. that
they are defined as
H(t,s)=\log Z(t,s)
, where
Z(t,s)

solves the stochastic heat equation

(1.3)

\partial_{t}Z(t,s)=\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{s}^{2}Z(t,s)+Z(t,s)\zeta(t,s),\;\;t\geq 0,\;s\in[0,1]

with boundary conditions (
1.2
)

(1.4)

\partial_{s}Z(t,s)|_{s=0}=(\mathsf{c}-1)Z(t,0)/2,\quad\partial_{s}Z(t,s)|_{s=1}=(1-\mathsf{a})Z(t,1)/2.

The solutions of (
1.3
) are not differentiable, so the meaning of
(
1.4
) is not obvious. A proper definition was given in
[
CS18
, Definition 2.5]
, see also
[

,
GPS20
]

A stationary measure for the open KPZ equation is the law of a random function
(\widetilde{H}_{s})_{s\in[0,1]}

with
\widetilde{H}_{0}=0
, defined by the property that if

is a Cole-Hopf solution of equation (

) and with the initial condition

for

, then the law of

does not depend on

Description of limits in

(ii)

It is conjectured in

We will need small index asymptotics for the modified Bessel K function.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that
\nu\neq 0
is an arbitrary complex number. If
x>0
then

(4.2)

\lim_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}K_{\nu/T}(e^{-xT})=\sinh(\nu x)/\nu.

If
x\leq 0
then

(4.3)

\lim_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}K_{\nu/T}(e^{-xT})=0.

Proof.

We know (
[
EMOT54
, 6.8 (26)]
) that the Mellin transform of Bessel
K
-function is

(4.4)

\int_{0}^{\infty}K_{\nu}(x)x^{s-1}{\textnormal{d}}x=2^{s-2}\Gamma((s+\nu)/2)\Gamma((s-\nu)/2),\;\;\;\textnormal{Re}(s)>|\textnormal{Re}(\nu)|.

Thus with
x>0
we can write

K_{\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{8\pi{\textnormal{i}}}\int_{c+{\textnormal{i}}{\mathbb{R}}}\Gamma((s+\nu)/2)\Gamma((s-\nu)/2)(x/2)^{-s}{\textnormal{d}}s,

where
c
is any positive number greater than
|\textnormal{Re}(\nu)|
.
We shift the contour of integration
c+{\textnormal{i}}{\mathbb{R}}\mapsto-1/2+{\textnormal{i}}{\mathbb{R}}
. We pick up two residues at poles
s=\pm\nu
, coming from Gamma functions, compute the residues at these poles and obtain

(4.5)

K_{\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\Gamma(\nu)(x/2)^{-\nu}+\Gamma(-\nu)(x/2)^{\nu}\Big)+\frac{1}{8\pi{\textnormal{i}}}\int_{-1/2+{\textnormal{i}}{\mathbb{R}}}\Gamma((s+\nu)/2)\Gamma((s-\nu)/2)(x/2)^{-s}{\textnormal{d}}s.

In the strip
-3/8<\textnormal{Re}(z)<-1/8
we have a uniform bound
|\Gamma(z)|<Ce^{-\frac{\pi}{4}|\textnormal{Im}(z)|}
for some absolute constant
C
(this is true for any strip of finite width which does not contain the poles of the Gamma function and it follows from Stirling’s asymptotic approximation to the Gamma function), thus we can estimate the integral in the right-hand side of (
4.5
) as follows

\bigg|\int_{-1/2+{\textnormal{i}}{\mathbb{R}}}\Gamma((s+\nu)/2)\Gamma((s-\nu)/2)(x/2)^{-s}{\textnormal{d}}s\bigg|\leq Cx^{1/2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{4}|t|}{\textnormal{d}}t=x^{1/2}\frac{8C}{\pi}

and thus we obtain the following result:

\bigg|K_{\nu}(x)-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\Gamma(\nu)(x/2)^{-\nu}+\Gamma(-\nu)(x/2)^{\nu}\Big)\bigg|<\tilde{C}x^{1/2},

which is valid for all
x>0
,
\nu\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
with
|\nu|<1/4
and some absolute constant
\tilde{C}
.

Since
\Gamma(z)\sim 1/z
as
|z|\to 0
, this implies that for
x>0
, and complex
\nu\neq 0

\lim_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T}K_{\nu/T}(e^{-xT})=\lim_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{2\nu}\Big(e^{\nu x}\frac{T}{\nu}\Gamma(\nu/T)2^{\nu/T}+e^{-\nu x}\frac{T}{\nu}\Gamma(-\nu/T)2^{-\nu/T}\Big)=\sinh(\nu x)/\nu.

On the other hand,
for
x\leq 0
, complex
\nu\neq 0
and large enough
T
, we have

|K_{\nu/T}(e^{-xT})|\leq K_{|\nu/T|}(e^{-xT})\leq K_{1/2}(e^{-xT})=\sqrt{\pi/2}\frac{\exp(-e^{-XT})}{e^{-xT/2}},

which gives (
4.3
).

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{k\geq 0\;:\;\mathsf{a}+2k<0}4e^{\tau(\mathsf{a}+2k)^{2}/4}K_{\mathsf{a}+2k}(e^{-x})\frac{1}{\Gamma(-\mathsf{a}-2k)}.

\mathsf{c}_{\varepsilon}\to-\mathsf{a}

From (

\varepsilon\to 0

+\frac{\Gamma(\frac{(1+s)\varepsilon}{2},\mathsf{a})}{2\mathsf{c}\Gamma(\mathsf{a})}\sum_{\{k\geq 0:\;-\mathsf{a}+2k<0\}}e^{\tau(\mathsf{c}+2k)^{2}/4}(\mathsf{c}+2k)\frac{(\mathsf{c},\frac{\varepsilon}{2})_{k}}{k!(1-\mathsf{a})_{k}}.

Since the integral converges and
\Gamma(x)\sim\tfrac{1}{x}-\gamma
as
x\to 0

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\exp\left(-s\varepsilon Y_{0}^{(\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon},\mathsf{c}_{\varepsilon})}\right)\right]=\frac{C_{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon},\mathsf{c}_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon s}^{(\tau)}}{C_{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon},\mathsf{c}_{\varepsilon}}^{(\tau)}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{\Gamma((1+s)\varepsilon/2)}{\Gamma(\varepsilon/2)}\to\frac{1}{1+s},

which is the Laplace transform of the exponential

4.7.2.
Proof of asymptotic normality (
2.12
)

We note that as indicated in (
1.9
), the law of
Y_{0}^{(\mathsf{a},\mathsf{c})}
depends on
\tau
though the normalizing constant and though
H_{0}
.

Proof of (
2.12
).

We use (
4.25
) to determine the Laplace transform.

L_{\tau}(s):={\mathbb{E}}\left[e^{-s(Y_{0}^{(\mathsf{a},\mathsf{c})}+\tau\mathsf{c}/2)/\sqrt{\tau}}\right]=e^{-s\mathsf{c}\sqrt{\tau}/2}\frac{C_{\mathsf{a},\mathsf{c}+s/\sqrt{\tau}}^{(\tau)}}{C_{\mathsf{a},\mathsf{c}}^{(\tau)}}.

It is enough to determine how the numerator depends on
s
for
s<0
, discarding all multiplicative constants that will cancel out with the denominator.
From (
4.1
) we know that
C_{\mathsf{a},\mathsf{c}+\varepsilon s/\sqrt{\tau}}^{(\tau)}=I_{\tau}(s)+D_{\tau}(s)
is the sum of the integral

\displaystyle I_{\tau}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\tau u^{2}/4}\frac{|\Gamma(\tfrac{\mathsf{a}+{\textnormal{i}}u}{2},\tfrac{\mathsf{c}+s/\sqrt{\tau}+{\textnormal{i}}u}{2})|^{2}}{|\Gamma({\textnormal{i}}u)|^{2}}{\textnormal{d}}u

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u^{2}}\frac{|\Gamma(\tfrac{\mathsf{a}+{\textnormal{i}}u}{2},\tfrac{\mathsf{c}+s/\sqrt{\tau}+{\textnormal{i}}u}{2})|^{2}}{|\Gamma({\textnormal{i}}u)|^{2}}{\textnormal{d}}u\to\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u^{2}}\frac{|\Gamma(\tfrac{\mathsf{a}+{\textnormal{i}}u}{2},\tfrac{\mathsf{c}+{\textnormal{i}}u}{2})|^{2}}{|\Gamma({\textnormal{i}}u)|^{2}}{\textnormal{d}}u,

which is bounded in
\tau
, and the finite sum

\displaystyle D_{\tau}(s)

\displaystyle=2^{\mathsf{a}+\mathsf{c}+s/\sqrt{\tau}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\mathsf{a}+\mathsf{c}+s/\sqrt{\tau}}{2},\frac{\mathsf{a}-\mathsf{c}-s/\sqrt{\tau}}{2})}{2(\mathsf{c}+s/\sqrt{\tau})\Gamma(-\mathsf{c}-s/\sqrt{\tau})}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\{k\geq 0:\;\mathsf{c}+s/\sqrt{\tau}+2k<0\}}e^{\tau(\mathsf{c}+s/\sqrt{\tau}+2k)^{2}/4}(\mathsf{c}+s/\sqrt{\tau}+2k)\tfrac{\left(\mathsf{c}+s/\sqrt{\tau},\tfrac{\mathsf{a}+\mathsf{c}+s/\sqrt{\tau}}{2}\right)_{k}}{k!\left(1+\tfrac{\mathsf{c}+s/\sqrt{\tau}-\mathsf{a}}{2}\right)_{k}}

\displaystyle=2^{\mathsf{a}+\mathsf{c}+s/\sqrt{\tau}-1}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\mathsf{a}+\mathsf{c}+s/\sqrt{\tau}}{2},\frac{\mathsf{a}-\mathsf{c}-s/\sqrt{\tau}}{2})}{\Gamma(-\mathsf{c}-s/\sqrt{\tau})}

\displaystyle\times\left(e^{\tau(\mathsf{c}+s/\sqrt{\tau})^{2}/4}+\frac{(\mathsf{c}+s/\sqrt{\tau}+2)(\mathsf{a}+\mathsf{c}+s/\sqrt{\tau})}{2+\mathsf{c}+s/\sqrt{\tau}-\mathsf{a}}e^{\tau(\mathsf{c}+s/\sqrt{\tau}+2)^{2}/4}+\dots\right).

With
s<0
, we see that
e^{-s\mathsf{c}\sqrt{\tau}}I_{\tau}\to 0
and that the leading term in the asymptotics comes from the first term in the sum
D_{\tau}
. We get

\lim_{\tau\to\infty}L_{\tau}(s)=\lim_{\tau\to\infty}e^{-s\mathsf{c}\sqrt{\tau}/2}\frac{D_{\tau}(s)}{D_{\tau}(0)}=\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\frac{e^{\tau(\mathsf{c}+s/\sqrt{\tau})^{2}/4-s\mathsf{c}\sqrt{\tau}/2}}{e^{\tau\mathsf{c}^{2}/4}}=e^{s^{2}/4}.

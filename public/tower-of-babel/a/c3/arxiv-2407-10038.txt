Abstract
. In this note, we define and study Asai gamma factors over finite fields. We also prove some results about local Asai L-functions over p-adic fields for level zero representations.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
22E50,20C33

1.
Introduction

In representation theory of connected linear algebraic group over local and finite fields, it is very useful and important to attach local invariants to irreducible representations, which encode various information about the representations in question. For example, this includes the work of Jacquet,Piatetski-Shapiro and Shalika (
[
JPSS
]
) and Shahidi (
[
Sh
]
).

Over finite fields, in her master thesis
[
R
]
, Roditty-Gershon defined a finite field analog of the tensor gamma factor of Jacquet-Piatetski-Shapiro-Shlika. These analogs were then used by Nien to prove a finite field analogue of Jacquet’s conjecture in
[
N
]
. Later, Rongqing Ye in
[
Ye
]
showed that these tensor gamma factors are related to their local field counterpart through level zero supercuspidal representations.

This paper is inspired by the work of Ye
[
Ye
]
, and we consider the Asai gamma factors here. To be more precise, let
F=F_{q}
be a finite field with
q
elements, and
E/F
is a quadratic extension so that
E=F_{q^{2}}
. Let
\pi
be an irreducible cuspidal representation of
GL_{n}(E)
with Whittaker model
{\mathcal{W}}(\pi,\psi)
. Choose a function
\varphi
on
F^{n}
, define the sum

Z(W,\varphi;\psi)=\sum_{g\in N_{n}(F)\backslash GL_{n}(F)}W(g)\varphi(e_{n}g)

where
e_{n}=(0,...,0,1)\in F^{n}
. This sum
Z(W,\varphi;\psi)
admits a functional equation, which defines the Asai gamma factor
\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
\pi
is irreducible cuspidal and not distinguished with respect to
GL_{n}(F)
. Then for any
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}(F^{n})
, and any
W\in{\mathcal{W}}(\pi,\psi_{E})
, we have

\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)Z(W,\varphi;\psi)=Z(\widetilde{W},{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\varphi;\psi^{-1}).

We next compute the absolute value of this
\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible cuspidal representation of
GL_{n}(E)
.

(1). If
\pi
is not distinguished with respect to
GL_{n}(F)
, then

|\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)|=q^{\frac{n}{2}}.

(2). If
\pi
is distinguished with respect to
GL_{n}(F)
, then

\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)=-1.

We also relate
\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)
to that of level zero representations, see Proposition
4.3
for a precise statement.

2.
Notations and Preliminaries

Let
L/K
be a unramified quadratic extensions of p-adic fields. We denote by
\theta
the nontrivial Galois element of this field extension. We choose
L/K
so that the residue field of
K
is a finite field
F=F_{q}
with
q
elements, and then the residue field of
L
is
E=F_{q^{2}}
, which is a quadratic extension of
F
. Let
{\mathfrak{o}}_{L},{\mathfrak{o}}_{K}
be ring of integers of
L,K
, with maximal ideals
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L},{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}
, respectively. Choose a uniformizer
\varpi
for both
{\mathfrak{o}}_{L}
and
{\mathfrak{o}}_{K}
, as
L/K
is unramified. Use
|\cdot|_{L}
and
|\cdot|_{K}
to denote their respective absolute values on
L
and
K
. They are normalized so that
|\varpi|_{L}=q^{-2}
and
|\varpi|_{K}=q^{-1}
. We choose a nontrivial additive character
\psi_{K}
of
K
, which has conductor precisely
{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}
. This character
\psi_{K}
descends to a nontrivial character of
F={\mathfrak{o}}_{K}/{\mathfrak{p}}_{K}
. We will use
\psi
to denote both these two characters when there is no confusion. Choose some
z\in{\mathfrak{o}}_{L}^{\times}\backslash{\mathfrak{o}}_{K}^{\times}
so that
z-\theta(z)\in{\mathfrak{o}}_{L}^{\times}
, and set

\psi_{L}(x)=\psi(\frac{x-\theta(x)}{z-\theta(z)}).

One then can check that
\psi_{L}
has conductor precisely
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
, and it descends to a nontrivial character
\psi_{E}
of
E={\mathfrak{o}}_{L}/{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
. It then follows that
\psi_{L}
is trivial on
K
and
\psi_{E}
is trivial on
F
.

If
R
is a commutative ring, we use
N_{n}(R)
to denote the standard
n\times n
unipotent matrices with coefficients in
R
. If
\psi_{R}:R\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
is a nontrivial additive character of
R
, we extend it to
N_{n}(R)
by

\psi_{R}(u)=\psi_{R}(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}u_{i,i+1}).

for
u=(u_{ij})\in N_{n}(R)
, and still denote it by
\psi_{R}
.

Set
G=GL_{n}(E),H=GL_{n}(F)
. Let
\pi
be an irreducible representation
\pi
of
G
, we say
\pi
is generic if
{\mathrm{Hom}}_{G}(\pi,Ind_{N_{n}(E)}^{G}\psi_{E})\neq 0
. It is well known that the dimension of this
{\mathrm{Hom}}
space is at most one, and when
\pi
is generic, it is one. Then by Frobenius reciprocity law,
\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{N_{n}(E)}(\pi|_{N_{n}(E)},\psi_{E})=1
. Let
l\in{\mathrm{Hom}}_{N_{n}(E)}(\pi|_{N_{n}(E)},\psi_{E})
be a nonzero Whittaker functional of
\pi
. Then for
v\in V_{\pi}
, define
W_{v}(g):=l(\pi(g)v)
, which is called the Whittaker function attached to the vector
v
. The space generated by all Whittaker functions
W_{v}(g)
is called the Whittaker model
{\mathcal{W}}(\pi,\psi_{E})
of
\pi
.

For a Whittaker function
W_{v}\in{\mathcal{W}}(\pi,\psi_{E})
, define a function
\widetilde{W}_{v}
on
G
by

\widetilde{W}_{v}(g)=W_{v}(\omega_{n}({{}^{t}}g^{-1}))

where
\omega_{n}
is the longest Weyl element of
G
, with 1’s on the anti-diagonal and zero elsewhere. Then the function
\widetilde{W}_{v}(g)\in{\mathcal{W}}(\widetilde{\pi},\psi_{E}^{-1})
, where
\widetilde{\pi}
denotes the contragredient representation of
\pi
.

An irreducible representation
\pi
of
G
is called cuspidal if it has no
N_{n}(E)
-fixed vectors for any unipotent radical
N(E)
of proper standard parabolic subgroups.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible generic representation of
G
. We call a function
{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}
a Bessel function with respect to
(\pi,\psi_{E})
, if
{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}
is a Whittaker function and satisfies

{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(u_{1}gu_{2})=\psi_{E}(u_{1}u_{2}){\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(g)

for all
g\in G,u_{1},u_{2}\in N_{n}(E)
. We will normalize
{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}
so that it take value 1 at the identity.

The existence and uniqueness of Bessel functions are guaranteed by Proposition 4.2 and 4.3 in
[
Gel
]
. It also has the following explicit expression. Let
\chi_{\pi}
be the character of
\pi
, then for all
g\in G
,

{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(g)=|N_{n}(E)|^{-1}\sum_{u\in N_{n}(E)}\psi_{E}^{-1}(u)\chi_{\pi}(gu).

By Proposition 2.15 and 3.5 in
[
N
]
, Bessel function satisfies the following identity

{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(g^{-1})=\overline{{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(g)}={\mathcal{B}}_{\widetilde{\pi},\psi_{E}^{-1}}(g).

Now we turn to p-adic field and recall the local Asai gamma factor defined by A.Kable in
[
K
]
. We shall identify
K^{n}
with the space of row vectors of length
n
. Consider a Schwartz function
\varphi
on
K^{n}
, we define its Fourier transform as

{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\varphi(y)=\int_{K^{n}}\varphi(x)\psi(x{{}^{t}}y)dx.

Let
\sigma
be a smooth irreducible generic representation of
GL_{n}(L)
with Whittaker model
{\mathcal{W}}(\sigma,\psi_{L})
with respect to the character
\psi_{L}
. For
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
\varphi
a Schwartz function on
K^{n}
, and
W\in{\mathcal{W}}(\sigma,\psi_{L})
, we define the integral

Z(W,\varphi,s)=\int_{N_{n}(K)\backslash GL_{n}(K)}W(g)\varphi(e_{n}g)|\det g|_{K}^{s}dg

where
e_{n}=(0,...,0,1)
.

Theorem 2.2
.

(A.Kable)
(1). The integral
Z(W,\varphi,s)
converges absolutely when
{\mathrm{Re}}(s)
is sufficiently large. The subspace of
{\mathbb{C}}(q^{-s})
spanned by the local integrals
Z(W,\varphi,s)
is a
{\mathbb{C}}[q^{s},q^{-s}]
-fractional ideal and contains
1
.

(2). There exists a function
\gamma(s,\sigma,\psi,As)\in{\mathbb{C}}(q^{-s})
such that

\gamma(s,\sigma,\psi,As)Z(W,\varphi,s)=Z(\widetilde{W},{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\varphi,1-s)

for all Schwartz functions
\varphi
and all Whittaker functions
W\in{\mathcal{W}}(\sigma,\psi_{L})
.

We can use this theorem to define local Asai L-factors and epsilon factors. Note that
{\mathbb{C}}[q^{-s},q^{s}]
is a PID and hence the fractional ideal is principal. Since this ideal contains
1
, we may choose a unique generator of the form
1/P_{\sigma}(q^{-s})
, where
P_{\sigma}(T)\in{\mathbb{C}}[T]
and
P(0)=1
. Then we define

L(s,\sigma,As)=\frac{1}{P_{\sigma}(q^{-s})}.

This is the local Asai L-factor, and the local epsilon factor is defined in the usual way as

\varepsilon(s,\sigma,\psi,As)=\gamma(s,\sigma,\psi,As)\frac{L(s,\sigma,As)}{L(1-s,\widetilde{\sigma},As)}.

3.
Asai gamma factors

Assume
\pi
is generic with Whittaker model
{\mathcal{W}}(\pi,\psi_{E})
. Let
{\mathcal{S}}(F^{n})
be the space of functions
\varphi:F^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}
. For a function
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}(F^{n})
, we define its Fourier transform
{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\varphi:F^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}
by the formula

{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\varphi(y)=\sum_{x\in F^{n}}\varphi(x)\psi(<x,y>),

where if
x=(x_{1},...,x_{n})\in F^{n}
and
y=(y_{1},...,y_{n})\in F^{n}
, then
<x,y>
is the standard pairing

<x,y>=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}.

Take a Whittaker function
W\in{\mathcal{W}}(\pi,\psi)
, and a function
\varphi
on
F^{n}
. Consider the following sum

Z(W,\varphi;\psi)=\sum_{g\in N_{n}(F)\backslash H}W(g)\varphi(e_{n}g).

The following theorem establishes the functional equation for these sums and defines Asai gamma factor for cuspidal representations. Its proof is imitated from Theorem 2.3 in
[
Ye
]
with necessary modifications.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
\pi
is an irreducible cuspidal representation of
G
, then there exists a nonzero constant
\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)
, such that for any
W\in{\mathcal{W}}(\pi,\psi_{E})
, and any
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}(F^{n})
with
\varphi(0)=0
, we have

\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)Z(W,\varphi;\psi)=Z(\widetilde{W},{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\varphi;\psi^{-1}).

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(F^{n})
be the set of functions
\varphi
on
F^{n}
such that
\varphi(0)=0
. The group
H
acts on
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(F^{n})
by right multiplication, i.e.,

(R(g)\varphi)(x)=\varphi(xg)

for
g\in H
and
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(F^{n})
. If we set

L_{1}(W,\varphi)=\sum_{g\in N_{n}(F)\backslash H}W(g)\varphi(e_{n}g)

and

L_{2}(W,\varphi)=\sum_{g\in N_{n}(F)\backslash H}\widetilde{W}(g){\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\varphi(e_{n}g),

we can check that both
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
belong to the space
{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H}(\pi\otimes{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(F^{n}),1)
. We are going to show that this space has dimension one. Set
P_{n}
to be the mirabolic subgroup consisting of matrices with last row being
(0,...,0,1)
.

Claim
:
\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H}(\pi\otimes{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(F^{n}),1)=\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{P_{n}(F)}(\pi,1)
.

We first prove this Claim. Let
(v,\varphi)
be an
H
-invariant bilinear form on
\pi\otimes{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(F^{n})
. Let
\phi_{n}
be the indicator function of
e_{n}
, and consider a linear form

l(v)=(v,\phi_{n})

on
\pi
. Since
\phi_{n}
is fixed by the
P_{n}(F)
and
l(v)
is a
P_{n}(F)
-invariant linear form on
\pi
,
l\in{\mathrm{Hom}}_{P_{n}(F)}(\pi,1)
. This implies that
\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H}(\pi\otimes{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(F^{n}),1)\leqslant\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{P_{n}(F)}(\pi,1)
. Conversely, for any
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(F^{n})
, since
H
acts transitively on
F^{n}\backslash\{0\}
, we can write

\varphi=\sum_{g\in H/P_{n}(F)}c_{g}R(g)\phi_{n}

for some
c_{g}\in{\mathbb{C}}
. We can then define
(v,\varphi)
on
\pi\otimes{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(F^{n})
as

(v,\varphi)=\sum_{g\in H/P_{n}(F)}c_{g}l(g^{-1}v).

It can be checked that
(v,\varphi)
is
H
-invariant. Hence
\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H}(\pi\otimes{\mathcal{S}}_{0}(F^{n}),1)\geqslant\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{P_{n}(F)}(\pi,1)
, and the Claim is proved.

Therefore, it suffices to prove that
\dim{\mathrm{Hom}}_{P_{n}(F)}(\pi,1)=1
. Since
\pi
is a cuspidal representation of
G
, we have
\pi|_{P_{n}(E)}={\mathrm{Ind}}_{N_{n}(E)}^{P_{n}(E)}\psi_{E}
is irreducible, for example, by Theorem 2.3 in
[
Gel
]
. Then by Proposition 4.3 in
[
AM
]
, we have

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{P_{n}(F)}({\mathrm{Ind}}_{N_{n}(E)}^{P_{n}(E)}\psi_{E},1)\cong{\mathbb{C}}

which finishes the proof.
∎

Similar to the Rankin-Selberg case, we can express
\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)
in terms of Bessel functions.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
\pi
is irreducible cuspidal, then

\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)=\sum_{g\in N_{n}(F)\backslash H}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(g)\psi(<e_{n}g^{-1},e_{1}>),

where
e_{1}=(1,0,...,0)\in F^{n}
.

Proof.

We choose
\varphi
to be
\phi_{n}
, the indicator function of
e_{n}
. Then one computes the Fourier transform

{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\phi_{n}(y)=\sum_{x\in F^{n}}\phi_{n}(x)\psi(<y,x>)=\psi(<y,e_{n}>)=\psi(y_{n})

if we write
y=(y_{1},...,y_{n})
.

In the functional equation

\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)Z(W,\varphi;\psi)=Z(\widetilde{W},{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\varphi;\psi^{-1}),

we have

Z({\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}},\varphi;\psi)=\sum_{g\in N_{n}(F)\backslash H}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(g)\phi_{n}(g)=\sum_{g\in N_{n}(F)\backslash P_{n}(F)}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(g).

By Proposition 4.9 and its corollary in
[
Gel
]
, when restricted to
P_{n}(F)
,
{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(g)=0
unless
g\in N_{n}(F)
, and thus this sum reduces to
1
.

For the right hand side, we have

Z(\widetilde{{\mathcal{B}}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}},{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\varphi;\psi^{-1})=\sum_{g\in N_{n}(F)\backslash H}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(g){\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\phi_{n}(e_{1}{{}^{t}}g^{-1})=\sum_{g\in N_{n}(F)\backslash H}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(g)\psi(g_{n1})

if we write
g^{-1}=(g_{ij})
.

Direct computation shows that
\psi(<e_{n}g^{-1},e_{1}>)=\psi(g_{n1})
and the proposition follows.
∎

We note that

\displaystyle\overline{\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in N_{n}(F)\backslash H}\overline{{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(g)}\cdot\overline{\psi(<e_{n}g^{-1},e_{1}>)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in N_{n}(F)\backslash H}{\mathcal{B}}_{\widetilde{\pi},\psi_{E}^{-1}}(g)\psi^{-1}(<e_{n}g^{-1},e_{1}>)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma(\widetilde{\pi},\psi^{-1},As).

We say
\pi
is distinguished with respect to
H
if
{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H}(\pi,1)\neq 0
. The next result extends the above functional equation to general functions
\varphi
when
\pi
is not distinguished with respect to
H
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose that
\pi
is irreducible cuspidal and not distinguished with respect to
H
. Then for any
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}(F^{n})
, and any
W\in{\mathcal{W}}(\pi,\psi_{E})
, we have

\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)Z(W,\varphi;\psi)=Z(\widetilde{W},{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\varphi;\psi^{-1}).

Proof.

Set
\delta_{0}
to be the indicator function of
0\in F^{n}
. First we note that

Z(W,\delta_{0};\psi)=\sum_{g\in N_{n}(F)\backslash H}W(g)\delta_{0}(e_{n}g)=0.

Secondly, use
1
to denote the constant function, then

Z(W,1;\psi)=\sum_{g\in N_{n}(F)\backslash H}W(g)=0

since this is an
H
-invariant linear form on
\pi
and
\pi
is not distinguished with respect to
H
.

Now for general
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}(F^{n})
, write
\varphi=\varphi_{0}+\varphi_{1}
, where
\varphi_{0}=\varphi-\varphi(0)
and
\varphi_{1}=\varphi(0)
. Then
\varphi_{0}(0)=0
and one computes
{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\varphi_{1}=q^{n}\varphi(0)\delta_{0}
. Apply the functional equation in Theorem
3.1
to
\varphi_{0}
, and note that both
Z
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}
are linear in
\varphi
, we find

\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)(Z(W,\varphi;\psi)-Z(W,\varphi_{1};\psi))=Z(\widetilde{W},{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\varphi;\psi^{-1})-Z(\widetilde{W},{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\varphi_{1};\psi^{-1}).

By what we discussed above we find

\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)Z(W,\varphi;\psi)=Z(\widetilde{W},{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\varphi;\psi^{-1}).

∎

As a corollary, we find the absolute value of
\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\pi
be irreducible cuspidal and it is not distinguished with respect to
H
. Then

\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)\gamma(\widetilde{\pi},\psi^{-1},As)=q^{n}

and therefore

|\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)|=q^{\frac{n}{2}}.

Proof.

By the functional equation in Proposition
3.3
,

\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)Z(W,\varphi;\psi)=Z(\widetilde{W},{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\varphi;\psi^{-1}).

Apply this to
\widetilde{\pi}
again, we have

\gamma(\widetilde{\pi},\psi^{-1},As)Z(\widetilde{W},{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\varphi;\psi^{-1})=Z(\widetilde{\widetilde{W}},{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi^{-1}}{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\varphi,\psi).

Now the corollary follows since
{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi^{-1}}{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\varphi=q^{n}\varphi
.
∎

For the case when
\pi
is distinguished, we will use method in
[
SZ
]
to compute
\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)
. In the next section, another method similar to that in
[
Ye
]
will be present. Before that, we first need the following lemma which characterizes the distinction of
\pi
in terms of Bessel function
{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}
. First note that the Galois involution
\theta
induces an involution of
G
by componentwise, still denoted as
\theta
. Define
\tau:G\to G
by

\tau(g)={{}^{t}}(\theta(g))^{-1}.

We also note that by the definition,

\psi_{E}(\theta(u))=\psi_{E}(u^{-1})

for all
u\in N_{n}(E)
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible cuspidal representation of
G
with Bessel function
{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}
. Then
\pi
is distinguished with respect to
H
if and only if
{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(\theta(g)^{-1})={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(g)
. In particular, if
\pi
is distinguished, then for all
g\in H
, we have
{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(g^{-1})={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(g)
.

Proof.

Assume
\pi
is distinguished, then by Theorem 3.6 in
[
G
]
,
\chi_{\pi}(\tau(g))=\chi_{\pi}(g)
. Then we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(\theta(g)^{-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|N_{n}(E)|}\sum_{u\in N_{n}(E)}\psi_{E}(u^{-1})\chi_{\pi}(\theta(g)^{-1}u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|N_{n}(E)|}\sum_{u\in N_{n}(E)}\psi_{E}(u^{-1})\chi_{\pi}({{}^{t}}g\tau(u))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|N_{n}(E)|}\sum_{u\in N_{n}(E)}\psi_{E}(u^{-1})\chi_{\pi}(\theta(u)^{-1}g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|N_{n}(E)|}\sum_{u\in N_{n}(E)}\psi_{E}(u^{-1})\chi_{\pi}(g\theta(u)^{-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|N_{n}(E)|}\sum_{u\in N_{n}(E)}\psi_{E}(\theta(u))\chi_{\pi}(gu)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|N_{n}(E)|}\sum_{u\in N_{n}(E)}\psi_{E}(u^{-1})\chi_{\pi}(gu)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(g).

Conversely, if
{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(\theta(g)^{-1})={\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(g)
for all
g\in G
, by Proposition 5.4 in
[
Gel
]
, we have

\displaystyle\chi_{\pi}(\tau(g))

\displaystyle=\chi_{\pi}(\theta(g)^{-1})=\frac{1}{|N_{n-1}(E)|}\sum_{p\in P_{n}(E)}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(p\theta(g)^{-1}p^{-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|N_{n-1}(E)|}\sum_{p\in P_{n}(E)}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(\theta(p)^{-1}g\theta(p))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|N_{n-1}(E)|}\sum_{p\in P_{n}(E)}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(pgp^{-1})

\displaystyle=\chi_{\pi}(g)

and hence
\pi
is distinguished by Theorem 3.6 in
[
G
]
again.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Suppose
\pi
is an irreducible cuspidal distinguished representation of
G
. Then

\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)=-1.

Proof.

By Proposition 4.3 in
[
AM
]
, since
\pi
is cuspidal, we have

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{P_{n}(F)}(\pi,1)\cong{\mathbb{C}}.

On the other hand, by Theorem 3.6 in
[
G
]
,

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H}(\pi,1)\cong{\mathbb{C}},

So the containment
{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H}(\pi,1)\subseteq{\mathrm{Hom}}_{P_{n}(F)}(\pi,1)
is in fact an equality.

Consider the linear form, for
v\in V_{\pi}
,

l(v)=\sum_{p\in N_{n}(F)\backslash P_{n}(F)}W_{v}(p).

This is nonzero and
l\in{\mathrm{Hom}}_{P_{n}(F)}(\pi,1)
, which then implies that
l\in{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H}(\pi,1)
. This means that
l(\pi(g)v)=l(v)
for any
v
and any
g\in H
. Apply this to
{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}
, and by Proposition 4.9 and its corollary in
[
Gel
]
, we find

\sum_{p\in N_{n}(F)\backslash P_{n}(F)}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(pg)=\sum_{p\in N_{n}(F)\backslash P_{n}(F)}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(p)=1.

Now by Proposition
3.2
and Lemma
3.5
, we get

\displaystyle\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in N_{n}(F)\backslash H}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(g)\psi(<e_{n}g^{-1},e_{1}>)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|N_{n}(F)|}\sum_{g\in H}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(g)\psi(<e_{n}g^{-1},e_{1}>)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|N_{n}(F)|}\sum_{g\in H}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(g^{-1})\psi(<e_{n}g^{-1},e_{1}>)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|N_{n}(F)|}\sum_{g\in H}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(g)\psi(<e_{n}g,e_{1}>)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in N_{n}(F)\backslash H}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(g)\psi(<e_{n}g,e_{1}>)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in P_{n}(F)\backslash H}\sum_{p\in N_{n}(F)\backslash P_{n}(F)}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(pg)\psi(<e_{n}g,e_{1}>)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in P_{n}(F)\backslash H}\psi(<e_{n}g,e_{1}>).

Now the conclusion follows from the computation in the proof of Lemma A.1 in
[
SZ
]
.
∎

In the above proof, we find that, if
\pi
is cuspidal distinguished, then

\sum_{g\in N_{n}(F)\backslash H}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi_{E}}(g)=\sum_{P_{n}(F)\backslash H}1=\frac{|H|}{|P_{n}(F)|}=q^{n}-1.

This will be used in the next section. We remark that there is a formula in
[
AM
]
, see also
[
Ya
]
, of such sum for general generic representations.

4.
Level zero representations

A representation
\sigma
of
GL_{n}(L)
is of level zero if it is of the form

\sigma\cong ind_{L^{\times}GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})}^{GL_{n}(L)}\Lambda

where
\Lambda
is a representation of
L^{\times}GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})
such that
\Lambda|_{GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})}
is an inflation of
\pi
via
GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})\xrightarrow{mod{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}GL_{n}(E)
. Here
\pi
is an irreducible cuspidal representation of
GL_{n}(E)
, and
ind
is the smooth compact induction.
Note that
L^{\times}\bigcap GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})\cong{\mathfrak{o}}_{L}^{\times}
, so
\Lambda
is determined by
\pi
and the nonzero complex number
\lambda=\omega_{\Lambda}(\varpi)
, where
\omega_{\Lambda}
is the central character of
\Lambda
. Note that
\omega_{\sigma}=\omega_{\Lambda}
in this case. By Theorem 8.4.1 in
[
BK
]
, we then get a parametrization of level zero representations with the pair
(\lambda,\pi)
. We will just say a level zero representation
\sigma
is from
(\lambda,\pi)
.

Recall that we have a unramified quadratic extension of p-adic fields
L/K
, with quadratic extension of residue fields
E/F
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\sigma
be a level zero representation of
GL_{n}(L)
coming from
(\lambda,\pi)
, where
\pi
is a distinguished representation of
GL_{n}(E)
with respect to
GL_{n}(F)
. Then

L(s,\sigma,As)=(1-\lambda q^{-ns})^{-1}.

Proof.

By Proposition 3.6 in
[
M
]
,

L(s,\sigma,As)=\prod(1-q^{s_{0}-s})^{-1}

where the product is taken over the
q^{s_{0}}
’s such that
\sigma\otimes|\cdot|_{L}^{s_{0}/2}
is distinguished. Now if
s_{0}
is a such pole, by Lemma 2.1 in
[
Ye
]
,
\sigma\otimes|\cdot|_{L}^{s_{0}/2}
is a level zero representation from
(\lambda q^{-ns_{0}},\pi)
. As this representation is distinguished, the restriction of its central character to
K^{\times}
is trivial. Since the extension
L/K
is unramified,
\varpi\in{\mathfrak{o}}_{K}
and thus
\lambda q^{-ns_{0}}=1
.

Conversely, if
s_{0}
is a complex number satisfying
\lambda q^{-ns_{0}}=1
. Consider the pair
(1,\pi)
. It determines a level zero representation
\sigma^{\prime}\cong ind_{L^{\times}GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})}^{GL_{n}(L)}\Lambda^{\prime}
. Set
G^{\prime}=GL_{n}(L),H^{\prime}=GL_{n}(K)
, by standard Mackey’s theory as in section 5 of
[
HK
]
for example, we have a decomposition

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H^{\prime}}(\sigma^{\prime},1)=\bigoplus_{g\in L^{\times}GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})\backslash G^{\prime}/H^{\prime}}{\mathrm{Hom}}_{L^{\times}GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})\cap gH^{\prime}g^{-1}}(\Lambda^{\prime},1).

Consider the direct summand corresponding to the identity double coset in the right hand side of the above equality, we have
L^{\times}GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})\cap H^{\prime}=K^{\times}GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{K})
and

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{K^{\times}GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{K})}(\Lambda^{\prime},1)\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H}(\pi,1)\cong{\mathbb{C}}.

Since the pair
(G^{\prime},H^{\prime})
is a Gelfand pair, it follows that other direct summands in the above decomposition are all zero and

{\mathrm{Hom}}_{H^{\prime}}(\sigma^{\prime},1)\cong{\mathrm{Hom}}_{K^{\times}GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{K})}(\Lambda^{\prime},1)\cong{\mathbb{C}},

which then implies that
\sigma^{\prime}
is distinguished, and such
s_{0}
is a pole of
L(s,\sigma,As)
.

Hence
s_{0}
is a pole of
L(s,\sigma,As)
if and only if
\lambda q^{-ns_{0}}=1
. There are
n
such poles, and if we fix one pole
s_{0}
, these
n
poles are

s_{0},s_{0}e^{\frac{2\pi i}{n\ln q}},...,s_{0}e^{\frac{2(n-1)\pi i}{n\ln q}}.

Therefore,

L(s,\sigma,As)=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-q^{s_{0}-s}e^{\frac{2\pi ik}{n}})^{-1}=(1-q^{ns_{0}-ns})^{-1}=(1-\lambda q^{-ns})^{-1},

which finishes the proof.
∎

Suppose we are given a level zero representation
\sigma=ind_{L^{\times}GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})}^{GL_{n}(L)}\Lambda
of
GL_{n}(L)
corresponding to the pair
(\lambda,\pi)
. Denote
\bar{k}
the image of
k\in GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})
under the natural map
GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})\xrightarrow{mod{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}}GL_{n}(E)
. By Theorem 5.8 in
[
PS
]
, there exists a Whittaker function
W_{\sigma}
of
\sigma
with respect to
\psi_{L}
with the following properties:

(1).
SuppW_{\sigma}\subseteq N_{n}(L)L^{\times}GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})
and

W_{\sigma}(ug)=\psi_{L}(u){\mathcal{J}}_{\sigma}(g)

for any
y\in N_{n}(L),g\in L^{\times}GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})
,
where for
g=ak\in L^{\times}GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})
, with
a\in L^{\times},k\in GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})
,

{\mathcal{J}}_{\sigma}(g)=\omega_{\sigma}(a)|N_{n}(E)|^{-1}\sum_{h\in N_{n}(E)}\psi_{E}(h^{-1})\chi_{\pi}(\bar{k}h).

(2).
SuppW_{\sigma}\bigcap P_{n}(L)=N_{n}(L)(H_{n}^{1}(L)\cap P_{n}(L))
and for
u\in N_{n}(L)
and
h\in H_{n}^{1}(L)\cap P_{n}(L)
,

W_{\sigma}(uh)=\psi_{L}(u).

Here
H_{n}^{1}(L)
is the subgroup of
GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})
consisting of matrices which will reduce to identity modulo
{\mathfrak{p}}_{L}
.

Let us recall results in section 4 and 5 in
[
PS
]
to say more on the function
{\mathcal{J}}_{\sigma}
. Define a map
\Psi_{L}
on
(N_{n}(L)\cap GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L}))H_{n}^{1}(L)
by

\Psi_{L}(uh)=\psi_{L}(u)

for any
u\in N_{n}(L)\cap GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L}),h\in H_{n}^{1}(L)
. This is a character of the group
(N_{n}(L)\cap GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L}))H_{n}^{1}(L)
since
H_{n}^{1}(L)
is normal in
GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})
. Now by Theorem 4.4 in
[
PS
]
, we know that the restriction
\Lambda|_{(P_{n}(L)\cap GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L}))H_{n}^{1}(L)}
is irreducible and in fact we have

\Lambda|_{{\mathcal{M}}}\cong Ind_{{\mathcal{U}}}^{{\mathcal{M}}}\Psi_{L}.

where
{\mathcal{M}}=(P_{n}(L)\cap GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L}))H_{n}^{1}(L),{\mathcal{U}}=(N_{n}(L)\cap GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L}))H_{n}^{1}(L)
.
To get the function
{\mathcal{J}}_{\sigma}
, take the group
{\mathcal{N}}=(N_{n}(L)\cap GL_{n}({\mathfrak{p}}_{L}))H_{n}^{1}(L)
in the kernel of
\Psi_{L}
, then, for
g\in GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})
,

{\mathcal{J}}_{\sigma}(g)=({\mathcal{U}}:{\mathcal{N}})^{-1}\sum_{h\in{\mathcal{U}}/{\mathcal{N}}}\Psi_{L}(h^{-1})\chi_{\Lambda}(gh)=|N_{n}(E)|^{-1}\sum_{h\in N_{n}(E)}\psi_{E}(h^{-1})\chi_{\pi}(\bar{g}h).

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume
\sigma
is of level zero, and let
W_{\sigma}
be the Whittaker function of
\sigma
defined as above. Set
\varphi
to be the indicator function of
e_{n}H_{n}^{1}(K)
. Then, when restricted to
GL_{n}(K)
, the function

W_{\sigma}(g)\varphi(e_{n}g)

is the indicator function on the set
N_{n}(K)H_{n}^{1}(K)
, and

Z(W,\varphi,s)=vol(N_{n}(K)\backslash N_{n}(K)H_{n}^{1}(K)).

Proof.

We first note that
SuppW_{\sigma}\subseteq N_{n}(L)L^{\times}GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})
and
Supp\varphi(e_{n}g)=P_{n}(L)H_{n}^{1}(L)
. Thus the support of
W_{\sigma}(g)\varphi(e_{n}g)
is contained in

N_{n}(L)L^{\times}GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})\bigcap P_{n}(L)H_{n}^{1}(L)=N_{n}(L)(GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})\bigcap P_{n}(L))H_{n}^{1}(L).

Then, for any
u\in N_{n}(L),g\in(GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})\bigcap P_{n}(L))H_{n}^{1}(L)
,

W_{\sigma}(ug)\varphi(e_{n}ug)=\psi_{L}(u){\mathcal{J}}_{\sigma}(g).

Now by Proposition 5.3 in
[
PS
]
,
{\mathcal{J}}_{\sigma}(g)=\Psi_{L}(g)
if
g\in(GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{L})\bigcap N_{n}(L))H_{n}^{1}(L)
, and
{\mathcal{J}}_{\sigma}(g)=0
elsewhere. Now restrict to
GL_{n}(K)
, both
\psi_{L}
and
\Psi_{L}
are trivial, so
W_{\sigma}(g)\varphi(e_{n}g)
is the indicator function on
N_{n}(K)H_{n}^{1}(K)
.

Since
H_{n}^{1}(K)
is compact and
N_{n}(K)
is unipotent, we get
|\det g|_{F}=1
for all
g\in N_{n}(K)H_{n}^{1}(K)
, and
Z(W,\varphi,s)=vol(N_{n}(K)\backslash N_{n}(K)H_{n}^{1}(K))
.
∎

We are now ready to prove the following result which relates Asai gamma factors over finite fields and level zero representations. The proof is similar to that of Theorem 4.1 in
[
Ye
]
, and we only sketch it.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\sigma
be a level zero representation of
GL_{n}(L)
coming from a pair
(\lambda,\pi)
, and if
\pi
is not distinguished. Then

\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)=vol(N_{n}(K)\backslash N_{n}(K)H_{n}^{1}(K))\gamma(s,\sigma,\psi,As).

Proof.

Take
W_{\sigma}
to be Paskunas-Stevens’Whittaker function
W_{\sigma}(g)
, and let
\varphi
be the indicator function on
e_{n}H_{n}^{1}(K)
. Consider the functional equation defining
\gamma(s,\sigma,\psi,As)
as in Theorem
2.2
. By Lemma
4.2
, the left hand side of the functional equation is simply

\gamma(s,\sigma,\psi,As)vol(N_{n}(K)\backslash N_{n}(K)H_{n}^{1}(K)).

Use change of variable
g\to\omega_{n}{{}^{t}}g^{-1}
, we get

\displaystyle RHS

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{N_{n}(K)\backslash GL_{n}(K)}W_{\sigma}(g){\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\varphi(e_{1}{{}^{t}}g^{-1})|\det g|_{K}^{s-1}dg

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}}q^{-nl(s-1)}\omega_{\sigma}(\varpi)^{l}\int_{N_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{K})\backslash GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{K})}{\mathcal{J}}_{\sigma}(g){\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\varphi(\varpi^{-l}e_{1}{{}^{t}}g^{-1})dg

since
SuppW_{\sigma}\bigcap GL_{n}(K)\subseteq\coprod_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\varpi^{l}N_{n}(K)GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{K})
.
{\mathcal{F}}_{\psi}\varphi(\varpi^{-l}e_{1}{{}^{t}}g^{-1})
is calculated in the middle of page 9 in
[
Ye
]
, plug that formula in and we get

(4.1)

\displaystyle RHS

\displaystyle=(\sum_{l<0}q^{-nl(s-1)}\omega_{\sigma}(\varpi)^{l})\int_{N_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{K})\backslash GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{K})}{\mathcal{J}}_{\sigma}(g)dg

\displaystyle+\int_{N_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{K})\backslash GL_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{K})}{\mathcal{J}}_{\sigma}(g)\psi(e_{1}{{}^{t}}g^{-1}e_{n})dg

\displaystyle=(\sum_{l<0}q^{-nl(s-1)}\omega_{\sigma}(\varpi)^{l})\sum_{N_{n}(F)\backslash GL_{n}(F)}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(g)+\sum_{N_{n}(F)\backslash GL_{n}(F)}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(g)\psi(e_{1}{{}^{t}}g^{-1}e_{n})

The sum
\sum_{N_{n}(F)\backslash GL_{n}(F)}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(g)
is zero since
\pi
is not distinguished. Then the result follows from Proposition
3.2
.
∎

As an application, following the method in
[
Ye
]
, we give another way to compute the value of Asai gamma factor over finite fields when the representation is distinguished.

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose
\pi
is an irreducible cuspidal distinguished representation of
G
. Then

\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)=-1,

Proof.

Let
\sigma
be the level zero representation of
GL_{n}(L)
for the pair
(\lambda,\pi)
, where
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
. Note that
\omega_{\sigma}(\varpi)=\lambda
, and the contragrdient
\widetilde{\pi}
is from
(\lambda^{-1},\widetilde{\sigma})
by Lemma 2.1 in
[
Ye
]
. From equation
4.1
, we get

vol(N_{n}(K)\backslash N_{n}(K)H_{n}^{1}(K))\gamma(s,\sigma,\psi,As)=\frac{q^{n(s-1)}}{\lambda-q^{n(s-1)}}\sum_{N_{n}(F)\backslash GL_{n}(F)}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(g)+\gamma(\pi,\psi,As).

Now from Lemma
4.1
,

L(s,\sigma,As)=(1-\lambda q^{-ns})^{-1}

and

L(s,\sigma,As)=(1-\lambda^{-1}q^{-ns})^{-1}

Therefore

(4.2)

\displaystyle\epsilon(s,\sigma,\psi,As)

\displaystyle=\gamma(s,\sigma,\psi,As)\frac{L(s,\sigma,As)}{L(1-s,\widetilde{\sigma},As)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{vol(N_{n}(K)\backslash N_{n}(K)H_{n}^{1}(K))}\frac{\lambda^{-1}q^{-n}[c_{1}-\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)]q^{ns}+\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)}{q^{ns}-\lambda}\cdot q^{ns},

where
c_{1}=\sum_{N_{n}(F)\backslash GL_{n}(F)}{\mathcal{B}}_{\pi,\psi}(g)=q^{n}-1
. By Theorem 3 in
[
K
]
,

\epsilon(s,\sigma,\psi,As)=c_{2}^{\prime}q^{c_{3}s}

where
c_{2}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times},c_{3}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
depending on
\lambda
.

Then equality
4.2
becomes

(4.3)

\displaystyle q^{2ns}q^{-n}(c_{1}-\gamma(\pi,\psi,As))+\lambda\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)q^{ns}=c_{2}\lambda q^{(c_{3}+n)s}-c_{2}\lambda^{2}q^{c_{3}s}.

where
c_{2}=vol(N_{n}(K)\backslash N_{n}(K)H_{n}^{1}(K))\cdot c_{2}^{\prime}
. We note that
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}
and
\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)
are independent of
s
. By comparing the powers of
q^{s}
and the corresponding coefficients, we get

\displaystyle 2n

\displaystyle=c_{3}+n

\displaystyle q^{-n}(c_{1}-\gamma(\pi,\psi,As))

\displaystyle=c_{2}\lambda

\displaystyle\lambda\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)

\displaystyle=-c_{2}\lambda^{2}.

We then find

\displaystyle c_{3}

\displaystyle=n

\displaystyle\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{1}}{1-q^{n}}=-1

\displaystyle\gamma(\pi,\psi,As)

\displaystyle=-c_{2}\lambda.

Now the conclusion follows.
∎

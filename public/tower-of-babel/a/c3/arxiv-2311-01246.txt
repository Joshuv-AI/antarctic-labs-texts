Abstract
. We study supersolutions and superharmonic functions related to problems involving nonlocal operators with Orlicz growth, which are crucial tools for the development of nonlocal nonlinear potential theory. We provide several fine properties of supersolutions and superharmonic functions, and reveal the relation between them. Along the way we prove some results for nonlocal obstacle problems such as the well-posedness and (both interior and boundary) regularity estimates, which are of independent interest.

1.
Introduction

Nonlocal nonlinear potential theory (NNPT) is the study of harmonic functions associated with nonlocal nonlinear operators. It was initiated by Kuusi–Mingione–Sire
[
28
]
and its basis has been established by Korvenpää–Kuusi–Palatucci
[
27
]
, Lindgren–Lindqvist
[
31
]
, and Korvenpää–Kuusi–Lindgren
[
25
]
. As in the classical nonlinear potential theory (NPT), it turns out that it is crucial to study supersolutions and superharmonic functions in NNPT. Indeed, the Perron method was developed in
[
27
,
31
]
by using superharmonic functions. Moreover, the Wiener criterion was recently obtained by Kim–Lee–Lee
[
23
]
based on these results.

The nonlinearity governing the nonlocal problems in the aforementioned papers is given by a polynomial of degree
p>1
. However, as in the classical NPT, various nonlinearities can come into play in NNPT. For instance, one can consider nonlocal problems with Orlicz growth, variable exponent, double phase structure, and so on. In this work, we focus on the nonlinearity described by an Orlicz function. To the best of our knowledge, NNPT with Orlicz growth has not been developed yet. The aim of this paper is to present a comprehensive study on supersolutions and superharmonic functions associated with nonlocal operators with Orlicz growth as a first step toward the development of NNPT with Orlicz growth.

The nonlocal operator we are interested in is defined by

\mathcal{L}u(x)=2\,\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}y,

where
g
is given by the derivative of a Young function
G
(see
Section

2
for the definition),

D^{s}u=D^{s}u(x,y)=\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}

denotes a
fractional gradient
of
u
of order
s\in(0,1)
, and
k:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty]
is a measurable function satisfying
\Lambda^{-1}\leq k(x,y)=k(y,x)\leq\Lambda
for a.e.
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some ellipticity constant
\Lambda\geq 1
. Note that if
G
has the standard growth, i.e.,
G(t)=t^{p}
for some
p>1
, then our study falls into the rather standard case of fractional
p
-Laplacian-type operators. Let us first provide the definition of a supersolution with respect to
\mathcal{L}
. We refer the reader to
Section

2
for the definitions of function spaces.

Definition 1.1
.

A function
u\in W_{\text{loc}}^{s,G}(\Omega)
with
u_{-}\in L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a
(weak) supersolution
of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
if

(1.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}D^{s}\varphi\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\geq 0

for all nonnegative functions
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. A function
u
is a
(weak) subsolution
if
-u
is a supersolution, and
u
is a
(weak) solution
if it is both a subsolution and a supersolution.

Let us next define a superharmonic function with respect to
\mathcal{L}
. While a supersolution is defined by using the energy corresponding to the operator
\mathcal{L}
, a superharmonic function is defined by means of the comparison principle.

Definition 1.2
.

A measurable function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[-\infty,\infty]
is a (
\mathcal{L}
-)
superharmonic function
in
\Omega
if it satisfies the following:

(i)

u<\infty
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u>-\infty
everywhere in
\Omega
,

(ii)

u
is lower semicontinuous in
\Omega
,

(iii)

if
D\Subset\Omega
is an open set and
v\in C(\overline{D})
is a solution of
\mathcal{L}v=0
in
D
with
v_{+}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
u\geq v
on
\partial D
and almost everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus D
, then
u\geq v
in
D
,

(iv)

u_{-}\in L_{s}^{g}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Growth function

A function
G:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is called a
Young function
if it has the form

G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau

for some non-decreasing, left-continuous function
g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
which is not identically equal to 0. Clearly, a Young function is convex. Throughout the paper, we assume that
G
is a differentiable Young function, with a strictly increasing function
g=G^{\prime}
, and there exist
1<p\leq q
such that

(2.1)

pG(t)\leq tg(t)\leq qG(t).

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
G(1)=1
.

It is sometimes more convenient to use a function

(2.2)

\bar{g}(t)=G(t)/t

instead of
g
because
\bar{g}
satisfies a growth condition

(2.3)

(p-1)\bar{g}(t)\leq t\bar{g}^{\prime}(t)\leq(q-1)\bar{g}(t).

We emphasize that the condition (
2.3
) with
\bar{g}
replaced by
g
does not follow from the assumption (
2.1
). Note that
\bar{g}
satisfies

(2.4)

p\bar{g}(t)\leq g(t)\leq q\bar{g}(t)\quad\text{and}\quad p^{1/(q-1)}g^{-1}(t)\leq\bar{g}^{-1}(t)\leq q^{1/(p-1)}g^{-1}(t).

Moreover, it is easy to see that
G
,
g
and
\bar{g}
satisfy the following properties; see Chaker–Kim–Weidner
[
7
,
8
]
for instance.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
t,t^{\prime}\geq 0
and
c=p^{1/(q-1)}/q^{1/(p-1)}
.

(i)

For all
\lambda\geq 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\lambda^{p}G(t)\leq G(\lambda t)\leq\lambda^{q}G(t),

\displaystyle\lambda^{1/q}G^{-1}(t)\leq G^{-1}(\lambda t)\leq\lambda^{1/p}G^{-1}(t),

\displaystyle\lambda^{p-1}\bar{g}(t)\leq\bar{g}(\lambda t)\leq\lambda^{q-1}\bar{g}(t),

\displaystyle\lambda^{1/(q-1)}\bar{g}^{-1}(t)\leq\bar{g}^{-1}(\lambda t)\leq\lambda^{1/(p-1)}\bar{g}^{-1}(t),

\displaystyle\frac{p}{q}\lambda^{p-1}g(t)\leq g(\lambda t)\leq\frac{q}{p}\lambda^{q-1}g(t),

\displaystyle c\lambda^{1/(q-1)}g^{-1}(t)\leq g^{-1}(\lambda t)\leq c^{-1}\lambda^{1/(p-1)}g^{-1}(t).

(ii)

For all
\lambda\leq 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\lambda^{q}G(t)\leq G(\lambda t)\leq\lambda^{p}G(t),

\displaystyle\lambda^{1/p}G^{-1}(t)\leq G^{-1}(\lambda t)\leq\lambda^{1/q}G^{-1}(t),

\displaystyle\lambda^{q-1}\bar{g}(t)\leq\bar{g}(\lambda t)\leq\lambda^{p-1}\bar{g}(t),

\displaystyle\lambda^{1/(p-1)}\bar{g}^{-1}(t)\leq\bar{g}^{-1}(\lambda t)\leq\lambda^{1/(q-1)}\bar{g}^{-1}(t),

\displaystyle\frac{p}{q}\lambda^{q-1}g(t)\leq g(\lambda t)\leq\frac{q}{p}\lambda^{p-1}g(t),

\displaystyle c\lambda^{1/(p-1)}g^{-1}(t)\leq g^{-1}(\lambda t)\leq c^{-1}\lambda^{1/(q-1)}g^{-1}(t).

(iii)

It holds that

\displaystyle G(t+t^{\prime})\leq 2^{q}(G(t)+G(t^{\prime})),

\displaystyle G^{-1}(t+t^{\prime})\leq 2^{1/p}(G^{-1}(t)+G^{-1}(t^{\prime})),

\displaystyle\bar{g}(t+t^{\prime})\leq 2^{q-1}(\bar{g}(t)+\bar{g}(t^{\prime})),

\displaystyle\bar{g}^{-1}(t+t^{\prime})\leq 2^{1/(p-1)}(\bar{g}^{-1}(t)+\bar{g}^{-1}(t^{\prime})),

\displaystyle g(t+t^{\prime})\leq\frac{q}{p}2^{q-1}(g(t)+g(t^{\prime})),

\displaystyle g^{-1}(t+t^{\prime})\leq c^{-1}2^{1/(p-1)}(g^{-1}(t)+g^{-1}(t^{\prime})).

Another useful property of the growth functions is the following: for any
a,b\geq 0
and
\varepsilon>0
,

(2.5)

g(a)b\leq\varepsilon g(a)a+g(b/\varepsilon)b

Notice that (
2.5
) follows by splitting the cases
b\leq\varepsilon a
and
b>\varepsilon a
.

The
conjugate function
of a Young function
H
is defined by
H^{\ast}(\tau)=\sup_{t\geq 0}(t\tau-H(t))
for all
\tau\geq 0
. We obtain by definition

(2.6)

t\tau\leq H(t)+H^{\ast}(\tau)\quad\text{for all}~t,\tau\geq 0,

which is called
Young’s inequality
. The following lemma, together with Young’s inequality, is frequently used in the sequel.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
H(t)=G(t^{1/a})
for
a\in(0,p]
. Then
H^{\ast}(G(t)/t^{a})\sim G(t)
. In particular,
G^{\ast}(g(t))\sim G(t)
.

Proof.

Note that we have
H^{-1}(t)=G^{-1}(t)^{a}
. It follows from Theorem 2.4 in Harjulehto–Hästö
[
19
]
that
H^{-1}(t)(H^{\ast})^{-1}(t)\sim t
, and hence
(H^{\ast})^{-1}(t)\sim t/G^{-1}(t)^{a}
. This concludes
H^{\ast}(G(t)/t^{a})\sim G(t)
.
∎

We refer the reader to Diening–Harjulehto–Hästö–Růžička
[
14
]
and Harjulehto–Hästö
[
19
]
for an exhaustive description on Orlicz functions.

2.2.
Function spaces

Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
Orlicz
and
(fractional) Orlicz–Sobolev spaces
are defined by

\displaystyle L^{G}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\left\{u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}~\text{measurable}:\varrho_{L^{G}(\Omega)}(u)<\infty\right\},

\displaystyle W^{s,G}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in L^{G}(\Omega):\varrho_{W^{s,G}(\Omega)}(u)<\infty\right\},

\displaystyle V^{s,G}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\left\{u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}~\text{measurable}:u|_{\Omega}\in L^{G}(\Omega),\varrho_{V^{s,G}(\Omega)}(u)<\infty\right\},

where

\displaystyle\varrho_{L^{G}(\Omega)}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}G(|u|)\,\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\varrho_{W^{s,G}(\Omega)}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}G(|D^{s}u|)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}},

\displaystyle\varrho_{V^{s,G}(\Omega)}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(|D^{s}u|)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}.

These spaces are Banach spaces with the norms

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\varrho_{L^{G}(\Omega)}(u/\lambda)\leq 1\right\},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}+\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\varrho_{W^{s,G}(\Omega)}(u/\lambda)\leq 1\right\},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{V^{s,G}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}+\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\varrho_{V^{s,G}(\Omega)}(u/\lambda)\leq 1\right\},

respectively. By
W^{s,G}_{\text{loc}}(\Omega)
we denote the space of functions that belong to
W^{s,G}(D)
for each open set
D\Subset\Omega
. We also define the space
V^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)=\overline{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{V^{s,G}(\Omega)}
. We refer the reader to
[
23
, Remark 2.2]
for remarks on the spaces
V^{s,G}(\Omega)
and
V^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
. The following theorem will be useful in the sequel. See Proposition 3.2 in Salort
[
36
]
for the proof of
Theorem

2.3
.

Theorem 2.3

(Fractional Poincaré inequality)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and bounded. There exists a constant
C=C(n,p,q,s)>0
such that

\int_{\Omega}G(|u|)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(\mathrm{diam}^{s}(\Omega)|D^{s}u|)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

for all
u\in W_{0}^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
.

Since we work with nonlocal equations, we need a function space that captures integrability of functions in the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We define the
tail space

L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\left\{u\in L^{g}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\left(\frac{|u(x)|}{(1+|x|)^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{(1+|x|)^{n+s}}<\infty\right\},

or equivalently,

L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\left\{u\in L^{g}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u;x_{0},R)<\infty~\text{for some $x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$ and $R>0$}\right\},

where
\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u;x_{0},R)
is a
(nonlocal) tail
defined by

\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u;x_{0},R)=R^{s}g^{-1}\left(R^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}g\left(\frac{|u(x)|}{|x-x_{0}|^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+s}}\right).

Note that
\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u;x_{0},R)
is finite for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
R>0
if
u\in L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since we often investigate the integral in the tail, we introduce

T(u;x_{0},R):=g\left(\frac{\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u;x_{0},R)}{R^{s}}\right)=R^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}g\left(\frac{|u(x)|}{|x-x_{0}|^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+s}}

for notational convenience.

Having the spaces
W^{s,G}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
and
L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
at hand, we can define supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
as in
Definition

1.1
. Note that
W^{s,G}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
is consistent with the local counterpart
W^{1,G}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
and serves as a natural function space for the interior regularity theory. However, a delicate issue arises when we choose an appropriate function space for the Dirichlet problem and the boundary regularity theory. Indeed, there have been several approaches to find suitable spaces for functions regular across the boundary of the domain. For instance, Byun–Kim–Ok
[
3
]
and Kim–Lee–Lee
[
23
]
used
V^{s,G}(\Omega)
and Korvenpää–Kuusi–Palatucci
[
26
]
used
W^{1,G}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega^{\prime})
. However, these spaces are not appropriate for our purpose due to the following reasons:

(i)

Functions in
V^{s,G}(\Omega)
belong to
L^{G}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which is smaller than
L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Supersolutions and superharmonic functions are not necessarily in
L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(ii)

W^{s,G}(\Omega^{\prime})
converges to
W^{1,G}(\Omega^{\prime})
as
s\nearrow 1
, which relies on the particular choice of
\Omega^{\prime}(\Supset\Omega)
.

To complement aforementioned problems, we introduce a new space. Let
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}
be open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\Omega\Subset\Omega^{\prime}
. We define

W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})=\left\{u:\Omega^{\prime}\to\mathbb{R}~\text{measurable}:u|_{\Omega}\in L^{G}(\Omega),\varrho_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}(u)<\infty\right\},

where

\varrho_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}G(|D^{s}u|)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}.

It is a Banach space with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}=\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}+\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\varrho_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}(u/\lambda)\leq 1\right\}.

Obviously, we have
V^{s,G}(\Omega)\subset W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})\subset W^{s,G}(\Omega)\subset L^{G}(\Omega)
. We also define a space
W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})=\overline{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}
. A couple of remarks are in order. First, functions in
W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
do not belong to
L^{G}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in general unless
\Omega^{\prime}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Second,
W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
converges to
W^{1,G}(\Omega)
as
s\nearrow 1
, whereas
W^{s,G}(\Omega^{\prime})
converges to
W^{1,G}(\Omega^{\prime})
, see Fernández Bonder–Salort
[
15
]
.

2.3.
Supersolutions and superharmonic functions

In the introduction, we defined supersolutions and superharmonic functions. We provide several properties and make some remarks on these functions. Let us begin with supersolutions. For measurable functions
u,v:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, we consider a quantity

\mathcal{E}(u,v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}D^{s}v\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.

A sufficient condition for
\mathcal{E}
to be well defined is given in the next lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
u\in W^{s,G}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and for
v\in W^{s,G}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
with compact support in
\Omega
,
\mathcal{E}(u,v)
is finite.

Proof.

We first prove

(2.7)

\int_{\Omega}\int_{E}g(|D^{s}u|)|D^{s}v|\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}\leq C\left([u]^{q-1}\land[u]^{p-1}\right)[v],

where
E
is either
\Omega
,
\Omega^{\prime}
, or
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
[\cdot]
is the corresponding norm, namely,
[\cdot]=[\cdot]_{W^{s,G}(\Omega)}
,
[\cdot]=[\cdot]_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}
, or
[\cdot]=[\cdot]_{V^{s,G}(\Omega)}
, respectively. Indeed, we may assume that
[u]\neq 0
and
[v]\neq 0
, then by (
2.6
),
Lemma

2.1
,
Lemma

2.2
, and the unit ball property, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{E}\frac{q}{p}\frac{g(|D^{s}u|)}{[u]^{q-1}\land[u]^{p-1}}\frac{|D^{s}v|}{[v]}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\int_{E}\left(G^{\ast}\left(\frac{p}{q}\frac{g(|D^{s}u|)}{[u]^{p-1}\land[u]^{q-1}}\right)+G\left(\frac{|D^{s}v|}{[v]}\right)\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\int_{E}\left(G^{\ast}\left(g\left(\frac{|D^{s}u|}{[u]}\right)\right)+G\left(\frac{|D^{s}v|}{[v]}\right)\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq C+1,

as desired. Note that the same argument also shows that

(2.8)

\int_{\Omega}g(|u|)|v|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\left(\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}^{q-1}\land\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}^{p-1}\right)\|v\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}.

Let
D=\mathrm{supp}\,v
. Then for
D\Subset D^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
, we have by using (
2.7
) and (
2.8
)

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\mathcal{E}(u,v)|&\leq 2\Lambda\int_{D}\int_{D^{\prime}}g(|D^{s}u|)|D^{s}v|\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}\\
&\quad+2\Lambda\int_{D}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus D^{\prime}}g\left(\frac{|u(x)|+|u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{|v(x)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}\\
&\leq C[u]_{W^{s,G}(D,D^{\prime})}^{p-1}[v]_{W^{s,G}(D,D^{\prime})}+C\|u\|_{L^{G}(D)}^{p-1}\|v\|_{L^{G}(D)}+CT(u;x_{0},d)\|v\|_{L^{1}(D)}\\

3.

In this section, we study the local boundedness of subsolutions and the weak Harnack inequality for supersolutions. Such results for local operators with Orlicz growth are very well known, see Lieberman

, Moscariello–Nania
[

Some results similar to

were proved in Byun–Kim–Ok
[

]
and Chaker–Kim–Weidner
[

]
by using De Giorgi’s iteration technique. These results are obtained under some additional regularity assumption on

(

)), but the proofs indeed do not use this assumption. Thus,

3.1
can be seen as a slight modification of the results in
[
3
,
8
]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in(0,1)
. Let
\delta\in(0,\frac{n}{n-sp})

By Jensen’s inequality and the fractional Poincaré inequality, we arrive at

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq\left(\fint_{B_{r}(z_{0})}|\log(\bar{u}(x)+d)-(\log(\bar{u}+d))_{B_{r}(z_{0})}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}

4.
The obstacle problem

The nonlocal obstacle problem for the case
G(t)=t^{p}
was first introduced in Korvenpää–Kuusi–Palatucci
[
26
]
as a tool in the development of nonlocal nonlinear potential theory. In this section, we generalize the nonlocal obstacle problem in two aspects: on the one hand, we study the nonlocal obstacle problem with Orlicz growth. On the other hand, we improve the classical nonlocal obstacle problem with the standard growth by considering a larger admissible set. See
Remark

4.1
. We also refer the reader to Lieberman
[
30
]
for local obstacle problems with Orlicz growth and to Chlebicka–Karppinen
[
9
]
and Karppinen–Lee
[
21
]
for local obstacle problems with nonstandard growth.

Throughout this section, we assume that
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\prime}
are bounded subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\Omega\Subset\Omega^{\prime}
. Let
\psi:\Omega\to[-\infty,\infty]
and
\vartheta\in W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})\cap L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We define a class of functions

(4.1)

\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})=\{u\in W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}):u\geq\psi~\text{a.e.\ in}~\Omega,~u-\vartheta\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})\}.

The function
\psi
is called an
obstacle
. Let us make some remarks on the class
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
.

Remark 4.1
.

Our definition of the admissible set (
4.1
) is a bit different from the one

(4.2)

\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})=\{u\in W^{s,p}(\Omega^{\prime}):u\geq\psi~\text{a.e.~in}~\Omega,~u=\vartheta~\text{a.e.\ on}~\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\}

considered in
[
26
]
even when
G(t)=t^{p}
. First of all, recall that, in the local case, the admissible class consists of functions in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
(see Chapter 3 in Heinonen–Kilpeläinen–Martio
[
20
]
for instance). Thus, it is more natural to have
W^{s,p}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
than
W^{s,p}(\Omega^{\prime})
since

(1-s)\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\to C_{n,p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

as
s\nearrow 1
whereas
(1-s)[u]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega^{\prime})}^{p}\to C_{n,p}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime})}^{p}
as
s\nearrow 1
, see Foghem Gounoue–Kassmann–Voigt
[
18
]
. An advantage of using (
4.1
) instead of (
4.2
) is that some results for another obstacle problem with an obstacle
\vartheta\in V^{s,G}(\Omega)
and an admissible set

(4.3)

\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega)=\{u\in V^{s,G}(\Omega):u\geq\psi~\text{a.e.~in}~\Omega,~u-\vartheta\in V^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)\}

can be deduced from the results for our obstacle problem. See
Section

4.4
below. We note however that one cannot use the space
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
in the definition of admissible set because we need functions that are regular across the boundary of
\Omega
as we will see later.

Another difference between (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) is that functions in (
4.1
) achieve the boundary data
\vartheta
in the weak sense. Notice that this coincides with the requirement
u=\vartheta~\mathrm{a.e.}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
when the boundary of
\Omega
is sufficiently regular. However, these two requirements are different in general, see Remark 2.2 (ii) in Kim–Lee–Lee
[
23
]
.

Let us provide the definition of solution to the nonlocal obstacle problem with Orlicz growth.

Definition 4.2
.

We say that
u\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
is a
solution to the (nonlocal) obstacle problem in
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
if

\mathcal{E}(u,v-u)\geq 0

for all
v\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
.

Note that
\mathcal{E}(u,w)
is well defined for
u\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
and
w\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
by
Lemma

2.4
.

4.1.
Solvability of the obstacle problem

We discuss the solvability of the obstacle problem in
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
. We need the following assumption:

(4.4)

t\mapsto g(t)/t^{q-1}\quad\text{is non-increasing}.

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume (
4.4
). If
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
is non-empty, then there exists a unique solution to the obstacle problem in
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
. Moreover, the solution to the obstacle problem is a supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
.

We prove the existence result in
Theorem

4.3
by using a general result in the theory of monotone operators. We define a mapping
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})\to(W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}))^{\prime}
by

\langle\mathcal{A}u,w\rangle=\mathcal{E}(u,w).

Notice that a computation similar to (
2.9
) shows that

|\mathcal{E}(u,w)|\leq C\left(\|u\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}^{p-1}+T(u;x_{0},d)\right)\|w\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})},

where
x_{0}
is any point in
\Omega
and
d=\mathrm{dist}(\Omega,\partial\Omega^{\prime})
. Thus, the functional
\mathcal{A}u
belongs to
(W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}))^{\prime}
. In order to solve the obstacle problem, it is enough to prove the following lemma, see, for instance, Corollary III 1.8 in Kinderlehrer–Stampacchia
[
24
]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume (
4.4
). Suppose that
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
is non-empty. The mapping
\mathcal{A}
is monotone, weakly continuous, and coercive on the set
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
.

Proof.

We first observe that the fundamental theorem of calculus yields

(4.5)

\left(g(|a|)\frac{a}{|a|}-g(|b|)\frac{b}{|b|}\right)(a-b)\geq 0\quad\text{for all}~a,b\in\mathbb{R}.

This proves the monotonicity of
\mathcal{A}
, i.e.,
\langle\mathcal{A}u-\mathcal{A}v,u-v\rangle\geq 0
holds for every
u,v\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
.

Let us next prove the weak continuity of
\mathcal{A}
. Suppose that a sequence
\{u_{j}\}\subset\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
converges to
u\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
in
W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
. We claim that
\langle\mathcal{A}u_{j}-\mathcal{A}u,w\rangle\to 0
as
j\to\infty
for any
w\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
. Indeed, by applying
Lemma

A.5
we have for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle|\langle\mathcal{A}u_{j}-\mathcal{A}u,w\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq 2\Lambda\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|g(|D^{s}u_{j}|)\frac{D^{s}u_{j}}{|D^{s}u_{j}|}-g(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}\right||D^{s}w|\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(|D^{s}(u_{j}-u)|)|D^{s}w|\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\quad+2\Lambda\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(g(|D^{s}u_{j}|)+g(|D^{s}u|))|D^{s}w|\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}.

Following the computation in (
2.9
) and noticing that
u_{j}-u\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\langle\mathcal{A}u_{j}-\mathcal{A}u,w\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{j}-u\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}^{p-1}\|w\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\quad+C\varepsilon\left(\|u_{j}\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}^{p-1}+T(u_{j};x_{0},d)\right)\|w\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\quad+C\varepsilon\left(\|u\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}^{p-1}+T(u;x_{0},d)\right)\|w\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})},

where
x_{0}
is any point in
\Omega
and
d=\mathrm{dist}(\Omega,\partial\Omega^{\prime})
. Since
u_{j}\to u
in
W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
, we arrive at

\limsup_{j\to\infty}|\langle\mathcal{A}u_{j}-\mathcal{A}u,w\rangle|\leq C\varepsilon\left(\|u\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}^{p-1}+T(u;x_{0},d)\right)\|w\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
implies the weak continuity of
\mathcal{A}
.

Finally, we prove the coercivity of
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
, i.e., we show that there exists a function
v\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
such that

\frac{\langle\mathcal{A}u_{j}-\mathcal{A}v,u_{j}-v\rangle}{\|u_{j}-v\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}}\to\infty

whenever
u_{j}
is a sequence in
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
with
\|u_{j}\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}\to\infty
. Since
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
is non-empty, we can take a function
v\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
. Then we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{A}u_{j}-\mathcal{A}v,u_{j}-v\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{p}{\Lambda}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(G(|D^{s}u_{j}|)+G(|D^{s}v|))\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\quad-2\Lambda\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(g(|D^{s}u_{j}|)|D^{s}v|+g(|D^{s}v|)|D^{s}u_{j}|)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}=I_{1}+I_{2}.

By using
Lemma

2.1
and
Theorem

2.3
, we obtain

I_{1}\geq C\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}G(|D^{s}(u_{j}-v)|)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}\geq C\left(\|u_{j}-v\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}^{p}\land\|u_{j}-v\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}^{q}\right).

For
I_{2}
, we utilize the techniques used above to estimate

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\geq-C\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(g(|D^{s}(u_{j}-v)|)+g(|D^{s}v|))|D^{s}v|\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\quad-C\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(|D^{s}v|)(|D^{s}(u_{j}-v)|+|D^{s}v|)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\geq-C\|u_{j}-v\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}^{p-1}\|v\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}-C\left(\|v\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}^{p}\land\|v\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}^{q}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-C\left(\|v\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}^{p-1}+T(\vartheta;x_{0},d)\right)\|u_{j}-v\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}.

Since
\|u_{j}-v\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}\to\infty
as
\|u_{j}\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})}\to\infty
, we conclude the coercivity of
\mathcal{A}
.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
u
be the solution to the obstacle problem in
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
. If
B_{R}=B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
is such that

\essinf_{B_{R}}(u-\psi)>0,

then
u
is a solution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
B_{R}
. In particular, if
u
is lower semicontinuous and
\psi
is upper semicontinuous in
\Omega
, then
u
is a solution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\{x\in\Omega:u(x)>\psi(x)\}
.

For the proofs of
Theorem

4.3
and
Corollary

4.5
, we refer the reader to the proofs of Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 in Korvenpää–Kuusi–Palatucci
[
26
]
.

4.2.
Local boundedness

Solutions to the obstacle problems enjoy some regularity properties. This section is devoted to the the local boundedness of solutions up to the boundary. Notice that we interpret
\esssup_{A}=-\infty
and
\essinf_{A}=\infty
when
A=\emptyset
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
u
be the solution to the obstacle problem in
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
and let
B_{R}=B_{R}(x_{0})
satisfy
B_{R}\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset
. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
p_{0}>0
.

(i)

If

(4.6)

M:=\max\left\{\esssup_{B_{R}\setminus\Omega}\vartheta,\esssup_{B_{R}\cap\Omega}\psi\right\}<\infty,

then

(4.7)

\esssup_{B_{R/2}}\,(u-k)_{+}\leq\varepsilon\,\mathrm{Tail}_{g}((u-k)_{+};x_{0},R/2)+C\left(\fint_{B_{R}}(u-k)_{+}^{p_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p_{0}}

for all
k\geq M
, where
C=C(n,p,p_{0},q,s,\Lambda,\varepsilon)>0
.

(ii)

If

(4.8)

m:=\essinf_{B_{R}\setminus\Omega}\vartheta>-\infty,

then

\esssup_{B_{R/2}}\,(u-k)_{-}\leq\varepsilon\,\mathrm{Tail}_{g}((u-k)_{-};x_{0},R/2)+C\left(\fint_{B_{R}}(u-k)_{-}^{p_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p_{0}}

for all
k\leq m
, where
C=C(n,p,p_{0},q,s,\Lambda,\varepsilon)>0
.

Proof.

Let us first assume (
4.6
). Let
B_{\rho}(y)\subset B_{R}
. Then for any
k\geq M
and for any functions
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{\rho}(y))
satisfying
\eta\in[0,1]
, we have
v=u-(u-k)_{+}\eta^{q}\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
, and hence
\mathcal{E}(u,(u-k)_{+}\eta^{q})\leq 0
. Thus,

satisfies a Caccioppoli-type estimate (see Equation (3.10) in Chaker–Kim–Weidner
[
7
]
) for all

, which is enough to deduce

(4.9)

\esssup_{B_{\rho/2}(y)}\,(u-k)_{+}\leq\varepsilon_{0}\mathrm{Tail}_{g}((u-k)_{+};y,\rho/2)+C\rho^{s}G^{-1}\left(\fint_{B_{\rho}(y)}G\left(\frac{(u-k)_{+}}{\rho^{s}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\right),

for all
k\geq M
and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
p
,
q
,
s
,
\Lambda
, and
\varepsilon_{0}
. See the proof of Theorem 3.1 in Chaker–Kim–Weidner
[
8
]
. One can now follow the argument in the proof of
Theorem

3.3
and use

G^{-1}\left(\fint_{B_{\rho}(y)}G\left(\frac{(u-k)_{+}}{\rho^{s}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\leq C\left(\fint_{B_{\rho}(y)}\left(\frac{(u-k)_{+}}{\rho^{s}}\right)^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/\alpha}

for large
\alpha>1
(by modifying
Lemma

3.10
) instead of (
3.29
) to deduce (
4.7
) from (
4.9
).

If (
4.8
) holds, then we repeat the same argument with
v=u+(u-k)_{-}\eta^{q}\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
for
k\leq m
.
∎

4.3.
Hölder regularity

We prove Hölder continuity of solutions to the obstacle problem. Let us begin with the interior estimates. Here, for an obstacle
\psi
, we define

\displaystyle\omega_{\psi}(\rho)\equiv\omega_{\psi}(\rho,x_{0}):=\osc_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega}\psi\quad\text{for $\rho>0$}.

Theorem 4.7
.

Suppose that
\psi
is locally Hölder continuous in
\Omega
or
\psi\equiv-\infty
. Then the solution
u
to the obstacle problem in
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
has a representative which is locally Hölder continuous in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Let us fix
x_{0}\in\Omega
. If

\essinf_{B_{R}}(u-\psi)>0

for some
B_{R}=B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
, then
u
is a solution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
B_{R}
by
Corollary

4.5
, and so is
u-\psi(x_{0})
. Thus, by Theorem 1.2 in Chaker–Kim–Weidner
[
7
]

\essosc_{B_{r}}u\leq C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\alpha}\left(\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u-\psi(x_{0});x_{0},R/2)+\|u-\psi(x_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R/2})}\right)

for all
r\in(0,R/8]
.

Suppose now that

(4.10)

\essinf_{B_{R}}(u-\psi)=0

for every
B_{R}=B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
. We claim that that there exist constants
\tau\in(0,1)
and
C>0
, depending only on
n
,
p
,
q
,
s
, and
\Lambda
, such that

(4.11)

\essosc_{B_{\tau R}}u+\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u-\psi(x_{0});x_{0},\tau R)\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\essosc_{B_{R}}u+\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u-\psi(x_{0});x_{0},R)\right)+C\omega_{\psi}(R)

whenever
B_{R}\subset\Omega
. To prove the claim, we consider a function
u_{d}:=u-d
, where
d=\psi(x_{0})-\omega_{\psi}(R)
, which is nonnegative in
B_{R}
by (
4.10
). Let
r\in(0,R/4]
. Since
u_{d}
is a supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u_{d}=0
in
B_{4r}\subset B_{R}
,
Theorem

3.2
provides

\displaystyle(4r)^{s}g^{-1}\left(\fint_{B_{2r}}g\left(\frac{u_{d}}{(4r)^{s}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\essinf_{B_{2r}}u_{d}+C\,\mathrm{Tail}_{g}((u_{d})_{-};x_{0},4r)

\displaystyle\leq C\omega_{\psi}(R)+C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\frac{s}{q-1}}\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{d};x_{0},R).

On the other hand,
Theorem

4.6
with
k=d+2\omega_{\psi}(R)
shows that

(4.12)

\esssup_{B_{r}}\,u_{d}\leq 2\omega_{\psi}(R)+\varepsilon\,\mathrm{Tail}_{g}((u_{d})_{+};x_{0},r)+C(\varepsilon)\left(\fint_{B_{2r}}u_{d}^{p_{0}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p_{0}}

for any
p_{0}>0
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. By taking sufficiently small
p_{0}
(and using
Lemma

3.10
), we can combine two displays above. Then we obtain

\essosc_{B_{r}}u\leq\esssup_{B_{r}}\,u_{d}\leq C\omega_{\psi}(R)+\varepsilon\,\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{d};x_{0},r)+C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\frac{sq}{q-1}}\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{d};x_{0},R).

Since

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{d};x_{0},r)

\displaystyle\leq C\esssup_{B_{R}}u_{d}+C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\frac{sq}{q-1}}\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{d};x_{0},R)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\essosc_{B_{R}}u+2\omega_{\psi}(R)\right)+C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\frac{sq}{q-1}}\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{d};x_{0},R),

we deduce

\essosc_{B_{r}}u\leq C\varepsilon\essosc_{B_{R}}u+C\omega_{\psi}(R)+C(\varepsilon)\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\frac{sq}{q-1}}\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{d};x_{0},R).

For any
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in(0,1)
, we choose
\varepsilon
and then
\tilde{\tau}\in(0,1/4)
accordingly so that
C\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}
and
C(\varepsilon)\tilde{\tau}^{\frac{sq}{q-1}}\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}
. Then for
r=\tilde{\tau}R
we have

(4.13)

\essosc_{B_{\tilde{\tau}R}}u\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}\left(\essosc_{B_{R}}u+\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u-\psi(x_{0});x_{0},r)\right)+C\omega_{\psi}(r).

Now, for any
\tau\in(0,\tilde{\tau})
we obtain from (
4.10
) and (
4.13
) that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u-\psi(x_{0});x_{0},\tau R)

\displaystyle\leq C\essosc_{B_{\tilde{\tau}R}}u+C\omega_{\psi}(R)+C\left(\frac{\tau}{\tilde{\tau}}\right)^{\frac{sq}{q-1}}\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u-\psi(x_{0});x_{0},\tilde{\tau}R)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\prime}\left(\essosc_{B_{R}}u+\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u-\psi(x_{0});x_{0},R)\right)+C\omega_{\psi}(R)

\displaystyle\quad+C\left(\frac{\tau}{\tilde{\tau}}\right)^{\frac{sq}{q-1}}\left(\essosc_{B_{R}}u+\omega_{\psi}(R)+\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u-\psi(x_{0});x_{0},R)\right).

Therefore, we arrive at the claim (
4.11
) by taking
\tau
and
\varepsilon^{\prime}
so small that
(\tau/\tilde{\tau})^{sq/(q-1)}\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}
and
2C\varepsilon^{\prime}\leq 1/2
.

The desired result follows by iterating (
4.11
), using (
4.12
), and recalling that
\psi
is locally Hölder continuous.
∎

We move our attention to the Hölder continuity of solutions up to the boundary. To this end, we need to impose some regularity assumption on
\Omega
. We say that a measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies a
measure density condition
if there exist
R_{0}>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
such that

\inf_{R\in(0,R_{0})}\frac{|E\cap B_{R}(x_{0})|}{|B_{R}(x_{0})|}\geq\delta

for every
x_{0}\in\partial E
. Note that if
D
and
\Omega
are open sets such that
D\Subset\Omega
, there always exists an open set
U
such that
D\Subset U\Subset\Omega
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus U
satisfying the measure density condition.

Theorem 4.8
.

Assume that
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
satisfies the measure density condition with
R_{0}>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
. Let
B_{R}=B_{R}(x_{0})
satisfy
B_{R}\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset
and
R\leq R_{0}
. If
\vartheta\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
is Hölder continuous in
B_{R}\setminus\Omega
and if
\psi
is Hölder continuous in
B_{R}\cap\Omega
or
h\equiv-\infty
, then the solution
u
to the obstacle problem in
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
has a representative which is Hölder continuous in
B_{R}
.

Proof.

If
\essinf_{B_{r}}(u-\psi)=0
for every
r\in(0,R)
, then one can follow the lines of proof of
Theorem

4.7
and use
Theorem

3.4
instead of
Theorem

3.2
to conclude theorem. Thus, the remaining part of the proof focuses on the case

\essinf_{B_{r_{0}}}(u-\psi)>0\quad\text{for some}~r_{0}\in(0,R).

Note that in this case
u
is a solution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
B_{r_{0}}\cap\Omega
by
Corollary

4.5
.

We claim that there exist constants
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\lambda>0
, a non-increasing sequence
\{M_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}
, and a non-decreasing sequence
\{m_{j}\}_{j\geq 0}
such that
m_{j}\leq\essinf_{B^{j}}u\leq\esssup_{B^{j}}u\leq M_{j}
and
M_{j}-m_{j}=\lambda r_{j}^{\alpha}
, where
r_{j}=2^{-j}r_{0}
and
B^{j}=B_{r_{j}}
for
j\geq 0
. We argue by induction on
j
. For
j=0
, we choose
M_{0}=\esssup_{B^{0}}u
,
m_{0}=M_{0}-\lambda r_{0}^{\alpha}
, and

(4.14)

\lambda\geq 2r_{0}^{-\alpha}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{0})}

so that
\essinf_{B^{0}}u\geq m_{0}
.

Assume now that we have constructed sequences
\{M_{j}\}
and
\{m_{j}\}
up to the index
j
. Since
v:=M_{j}-u
is a solution of
\mathcal{L}v=0
in
B_{r_{0}}\cap\Omega
such that
v\geq 0
in
B^{j}
,
Theorem

3.4
shows that

r_{j}^{s}g^{-1}\left(\fint_{B^{j+1}}g\left(r_{j}^{-s}w\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\leq C\essinf_{B^{j+1}}w+C\,\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(w_{-};x_{0},r_{j}),

where
w=v_{M_{j}-\widetilde{M}_{j}}^{-}
and
\widetilde{M}_{j}=\sup_{B^{j}\setminus\Omega}\vartheta
. On the one hand, we have from
w=M_{j}-\widetilde{M}_{j}
in
B^{j+1}\setminus\Omega
that

r_{j}^{s}g^{-1}\left(\fint_{B^{j+1}}g\left(r_{j}^{-s}w\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\geq r_{j}^{s}g^{-1}\left(\frac{|B^{j+1}\setminus\Omega|}{|B^{j+1}|}g\left(\frac{M_{j}-\widetilde{M}_{j}}{r_{j}^{s}}\right)\right)\geq c\left(M_{j}-\widetilde{M}_{j}\right)

for some
c=c(p,q,\delta)>0
, where we used the measure density condition and
Lemma

2.1
. On the other hand, we have

\essinf_{B^{j+1}}w\leq M_{j}-M_{j+1},

and hence

(4.15)

M_{j}-\widetilde{M}_{j}\leq C(M_{j}-M_{j+1})+C\,\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(w_{-};x_{0},r_{j}).

In order to estimate the tail term on the right-hand side of (
4.15
), we use the inductive hypothesis; for
0\leq k\leq j-1
, we have

\displaystyle w_{-}\leq M_{k}-M_{j}\leq M_{k}-m_{k}-(M_{j}-m_{j})\leq\lambda(r_{k}^{\alpha}-r_{j}^{\alpha})\quad\text{in}~B^{k}\setminus B^{k+1}~\text{and}

\displaystyle w_{-}\leq(M_{j}-u)_{-}\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{0})}+|u|\quad\text{outside}~B^{0}.

Thus, we obtain
\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(w_{-};x_{0},r_{j})\leq C(I_{1}+I_{2})
, where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=r_{j}^{s}g^{-1}\left(r_{j}^{s}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\int_{B^{k}\setminus B^{k+1}}g\left(\lambda\frac{r_{k}^{\alpha}-r_{j}^{\alpha}}{|x-x_{0}|^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+s}}\right)\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=r_{j}^{s}g^{-1}\left(r_{j}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B^{0}}g\left(\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{0})}+|u|}{|x-x_{0}|^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+s}}\right).

We observe that we have

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq Cr_{j}^{s}g^{-1}\left(r_{j}^{s}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}g\left(\lambda\frac{r_{k}^{\alpha}-r_{j}^{\alpha}}{r_{k+1}^{s}}\right)\frac{1}{r_{k+1}^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq Cr_{j}^{s}g^{-1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}g\left(\lambda r_{j}^{\alpha-s}\frac{2^{\alpha(j-k)}-1}{2^{s(j-k)}}\right)\frac{1}{2^{s(j-k)}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq Cr_{j}^{s}g^{-1}\left(g(\lambda r_{j}^{\alpha-s})\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\frac{(2^{\alpha(j-k)}-1)^{p-1}}{2^{sp(j-k)}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq CS(\alpha)\lambda r_{j}^{\alpha}

by using
Lemma

2.1
, where

S(\alpha)=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2^{\alpha k}-1)^{p-1}}{2^{spk}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\lor\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2^{\alpha k}-1)^{p-1}}{2^{spk}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}.

Note that
S(\alpha)\to 0
as
\alpha\to 0
. Moreover, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq r_{j}^{s}g^{-1}\left(r_{j}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B^{0}}g\left(\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{0})}}{|x-x_{0}|^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+s}}+\left(\frac{r_{j}}{r_{0}}\right)^{s}T(u;x_{0},r_{0})\right)

\displaystyle\leq Cr_{j}^{s}g^{-1}\left(\left(\frac{r_{j}}{r_{0}}\right)^{s}g\left(\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{0})}}{r_{0}^{s}}\right)+\left(\frac{r_{j}}{r_{0}}\right)^{s}T(u;x_{0},r_{0})\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(r_{j}/r_{0})^{\frac{sq}{q-1}}\left(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{0})}+\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},r_{0})\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{0})}+\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u;x_{0},r_{0})}{r_{0}^{\alpha}}r_{j}^{\alpha}

by using
Lemma

2.1
and assuming
\alpha<sq/(q-1)
. Therefore, we arrive at

M_{j}-\widetilde{M}_{j}\leq C(M_{j}-M_{j+1})+C\left(S(\alpha)\lambda+\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{0})}+\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u;x_{0},r_{0})}{r_{0}^{\alpha}}\right)r_{j}^{\alpha}.

Similarly, by using
u-m_{j}
we obtain

\widetilde{m}_{j}-m_{j}\leq C(m_{j+1}-m_{j})+C\left(S(\alpha)\lambda+\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{0})}+\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u;x_{0},r_{0})}{r_{0}^{\alpha}}\right)r_{j}^{\alpha},

where
\widetilde{m}_{j}=\inf_{B^{j}\setminus\Omega}\vartheta
. By addition we get

\displaystyle\essosc_{B^{j+1}}u

\displaystyle\leq(1-\gamma)\lambda r_{j}^{\alpha}+\osc_{B^{j}\setminus\Omega}\vartheta+C\left(S(\alpha)\lambda+\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{0})}+\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u;x_{0},r_{0})}{r_{0}^{\alpha}}\right)r_{j}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\alpha}(1-\gamma+CS(\alpha))\lambda r_{j+1}^{\alpha}+[\vartheta]_{C^{\beta}(\overline{B^{0}})}(2r_{j})^{\beta}+C\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{0})}+\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u;x_{0},r_{0})}{r_{0}^{\alpha}}r_{j+1}^{\alpha}

for some
\gamma\in(0,1)
, where
\beta
is the Hölder exponent of
\vartheta
. Assuming
\alpha<\beta
, we have
(2r_{j})^{\beta}\leq 2^{\alpha+\beta}r_{0}^{\beta-\alpha}r_{j+1}^{\alpha}
. We now choose
\alpha\in(0,\min\{\frac{sq}{q-1},\beta\})
sufficiently small so that
2^{\alpha}(1-\gamma+CS(\alpha))\leq 1-\gamma/2
and then we set

\lambda=\frac{2}{\gamma}\left(2^{\alpha+\beta}r_{0}^{\beta}[\vartheta]_{C^{\beta}(\overline{B^{0}})}+C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B^{0})}+C\,\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u;x_{0},r_{0})\right)r_{0}^{\alpha},

which is in accordance with (
4.14
). Then we have

\essosc_{B^{j+1}}u\leq\lambda r_{j+1}^{\alpha}.

We may choose
M_{j+1}
and
m_{j+1}
so that

m_{j}\leq m_{j+1}\leq\essinf_{B^{j+1}}u\leq\esssup_{B^{j+1}}u\leq M_{j+1}\leq M_{j},\quad M_{j+1}-m_{j+1}=\lambda r_{j+1}^{\alpha},

which completes the induction.
∎

4.4.
Other obstacle problems

As mentioned in
Remark

4.1
, the results obtained in this section can be translated into those for other types of obstacle problems. For instance, we say that
u\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega)
, where
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega)
is defined as in (
4.3
), is a solution to the obstacle problem in
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega)
if
\mathcal{E}(u,v-u)\geq 0
for all

. Then we have the following results.

Theorem 4.9
.

If
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega)
is non-empty, then there exists a unique solution
u
to the obstacle problem in
\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega)
. Moreover,

is a supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
.

We point out that the condition (
4.4
) is not required in
Theorem

4.9
.

Proof of
Theorem

4.9
.

Recall that the assumption (
4.4
) is used only when we check the weak continuity of the mapping
\mathcal{A}
in
Lemma

4.4
. Since the monotonicity and coercivity of
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega)\to(V_{0}^{s,G}(\Omega))^{\prime}
can be proved in the same way, let us provide the proof of the weak continuity. Suppose that a sequence
\{u_{j}\}\subset\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega)
converges to
u\in\mathcal{K}_{\psi,\vartheta}(\Omega)
in
V^{s,G}(\Omega)
. We choose a subsequence
\{u_{j_{i}}\}
such that
u_{j_{i}}\to u
a.e. in
\Omega
. Since
L^{G^{\ast}}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n};|x-y|^{-n}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x)
-norms of

are uniformly bounded,
g(|D^{s}u_{j_{i}}|)\frac{D^{s}u_{j_{i}}}{|D^{s}u_{j_{i}}|}
weakly converges to

in

. Since the weak limit is independent of the choice of the subsequence,
g(|D^{s}u_{j}|)\frac{D^{s}u_{j}}{|D^{s}u_{j}|}
weakly converges to

in

. Therefore, we have
\langle\mathcal{A}u_{j}-\mathcal{A}u,w\rangle\to 0
as

for all

5.
Properties of supersolutions

In this section, we provide some properties of supersolutions, which are crucial in the development of nonlocal nonlinear potential theory.

5.1.
Lower semicontinuity

In Byun–Kim–Ok
[
3
]
and Chaker–Kim–Weidner
[
7
]
, it has been shown that solutions of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
have a representative which is Hölder continuous in
\Omega
. More generally, we proved in the previous section that the solution to the nonlocal obstacle problem with Orlicz growth has a representative which is (Hölder) continuous in
\Omega
when the obstacle is (Hölder) continuous. For a supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
, one can find a representative which is lower semicontinuous in
\Omega
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
u
be a supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
. Then

u(x)=\essliminf_{y\to x}u(y)\quad\text{for a.e.~}x\in\Omega.

In particular,
u
has a representative which is lower semicontinuous in
\Omega
.

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as in Theorem 9 in Korvenpää–Kuusi–Palatucci
[
27
]
. One can utilize
Theorem

3.1
instead of
[
27
, Theorem 4]
in the proof.
∎

5.2.
Comparison principle

Supersolutions and subsolutions satisfy the following form of the comparison principle.

Lemma 5.2

(Comparison principle)
.

Let
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}
be open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\Omega\Subset\Omega^{\prime}
. Let
u\in W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
be a supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
and
v\in W^{s,G}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
be a subsolution of
\mathcal{L}v=0
in
\Omega
such that
(u-v)_{-}\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
. Then
u\geq v
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

By using
\varphi:=(u-v)_{-}
as a test function, we obtain

0\leq\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)-\mathcal{E}(v,\varphi)=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3},

where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\{u<v\}}\int_{\{u<v\}}\left(g(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}-g(|D^{s}v|)\frac{D^{s}v}{|D^{s}v|}\right)D^{s}\varphi\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\{u<v\}}\int_{\{u\geq v\}}\left(g(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}-g(|D^{s}v|)\frac{D^{s}v}{|D^{s}v|}\right)\frac{\varphi(x)}{|x-y|^{s}}\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{\{u\geq v\}}\int_{\{u<v\}}\left(g(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}-g(|D^{s}v|)\frac{D^{s}v}{|D^{s}v|}\right)\frac{-\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
D^{s}\varphi=-(D^{s}u-D^{s}v)
on
\{u<v\}\times\{u<v\}
, (
4.5
) yields
I_{1}\leq 0
. If
u(x)<v(x)
and
u(y)\geq v(y)
, then
u(x)-u(y)<v(x)-v(y)
and hence the integrand of
I_{2}
is non-positive. This shows that
I_{2}\leq 0
, and similarly
I_{3}\leq 0
. Therefore, each integral term must vanish and, in particular,
I_{1}=0
. In other words, we have
|\{u<v\}|=0
and so
u\geq v
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
∎

5.3.
Convergence results

This section is devoted to convergence results of supersolutions. We provide some sufficient conditions for the pointwise limit of a sequence of supersolutions to be a supersolution again.

Before we state the convergence results, let us establish the following energy estimate.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
M>0
and
h\in L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfy
h\leq M
a.e. in
\Omega
. Let
u
be a supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
such that
u\geq h
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u\leq M
a.e. in
\Omega
. For each open set
D\Subset\Omega
, there exists a constant
C=C(n,p,q,s,\Lambda,M,h,\Omega,D)
such that

\int_{D}\int_{D}G(|D^{s}u|)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}\leq C.

To prove
Lemma

5.3
, it is enough to prove the following two lemmas. We refer the reader to Lemma 5 in Korvenpää–Kuusi–Palatucci
[
27
]
for the proof of
Lemma

5.3
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
u
be a supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
and let
h\in L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Assume that
u\geq h
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u\leq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
. For each open set
D\Subset\Omega
, there exists a constant
C=C(n,p,q,s,\Lambda,h,\Omega,D)>0
such that

(5.1)

\essinf_{D}u\geq-C.

Proof.

We claim that

(5.2)

\esssup_{B_{R}(x_{0})}u_{-}\leq C_{0}

whenever
B_{2R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
, where
C_{0}
is a constant depending only on
n
,
p
,
q
,
s
,
\Lambda
, and
h
. Once we prove the claim (
5.2
), the desired estimate (
5.1
) follows by a covering argument. Indeed, if we cover
D
by finitely many balls
B_{R_{i}}(x_{i})
,
i=1,\dots,N
, with
B_{2R_{i}}(x_{i})\subset\Omega
, then

\essinf_{D}u\geq-\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\esssup_{B_{R_{i}}(x_{i})}u_{-}\geq-NC_{0},

where we used the assumption that
u\leq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
.

Let us prove the claim (
5.2
). Since
u_{-}
is a nonnegative subsolution in
\Omega
,
Theorem

3.1
with
\varepsilon=1
shows that

\esssup_{B_{R}(x_{0})}u_{-}\leq\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{-};x_{0},R)+Cg^{-1}\left(\fint_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}g(u_{-})\,\mathrm{d}x\right).

Since
u\geq h
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the right-hand side of the display above is bounded by

\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(h_{-};x_{0},R)+Cg^{-1}\left(\fint_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}g(h_{-})\,\mathrm{d}x\right),

which is finite by the fact that
h_{-}\in L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
u
be a supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
B_{2R}=B_{2R}(x_{0})
which is essentially bounded from above in
B_{3R/2}
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(n,p,q,s,\Lambda)>0
such that

\fint_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}G(|D^{s}u|)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}\leq CG\left(\frac{H}{R^{s}}\right),

where

H:=\sup_{B_{3R/2}}u_{+}+R^{s}G^{-1}\left(\fint_{B_{3R/2}}G\left(\frac{u_{-}}{R^{s}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)+\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{-};x_{0},3R/2).

Proof.

Let
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{5R/4})
be a cut-off function such that
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
\eta=1
in
B_{R}
, and
|D\eta|\leq C/R
. Let us define a function
w:=2H-u
, which is nonnegative in
B_{3R/2}
. By using
\varphi:=w\eta^{q}
as a test function, we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}D^{s}\varphi\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{B_{3R/2}}\int_{B_{3R/2}}g(|D^{s}w|)\frac{D^{s}w}{|D^{s}w|}D^{s}\varphi\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{B_{3R/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{3R/2}}g(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}\frac{(2H-u(x))\eta^{q}(x)}{|x-y|^{s}}\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

To estimate the term
I_{1}
, we apply
Lemma

A.6
to have

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq-c\int_{B_{3R/2}}\int_{B_{3R/2}}G(|D^{s}u|)(\eta(x)\land\eta(y))^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\int_{B_{3R/2}}G\left(\frac{w(x)}{R^{s}}\right)\int_{B_{3R/2}}((R^{s}|D^{s}\eta|)^{p}+(R^{s}|D^{s}\eta|)^{q})\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq-c\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}G(|D^{s}u|)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}+C\int_{B_{3R/2}}G\left(\frac{w(x)}{R^{s}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
w\leq 2H+u_{-}
, we arrive at

I_{1}\leq-c\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}G(|D^{s}u|)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}+CR^{n}G\left(\frac{H}{R^{s}}\right).

Let us next estimate the term
I_{2}
as follows:

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\Lambda\int_{B_{5R/4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{3R/2}}g\left(\frac{(u(x)-u(y))_{+}}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{2H-u(x)}{|x-y|^{s}}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{B_{5R/4}}(2H+u_{-}(x))\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{3R/2}}g\left(\frac{H+u_{-}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+s}}.

By Jensen’s inequality, we have

\fint_{B_{5R/4}}u_{-}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq CR^{s}G^{-1}\left(\fint_{B_{3R/2}}G\left(\frac{u_{-}(x)}{R^{s}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\leq CH,

Moreover, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{3R/2}}g\left(\frac{H+u_{-}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+s}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R^{s}}g\left(\frac{H}{R^{s}}\right)+\frac{C}{R^{s}}g\left(\frac{\mathrm{Tail}_{g}(u_{-};x_{0},3R/2)}{R^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R^{s}}g\left(\frac{H}{R^{s}}\right),

and hence

I_{2}\leq CR^{n}G\left(\frac{H}{R^{s}}\right).

Combining the estimates for
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
finishes the proof.
∎

We now move our attention to the convergence results for supersolutions. Note that we need the assumption (
4.4
) in the following theorem.

Theorem 5.6
.

Assume (
4.4
). Let
h_{1},h_{2}\in L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that
h_{1}\leq h_{2}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\{u_{j}\}
be an increasing sequence of supersolutions of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
such that
h_{1}\leq u_{j}\leq h_{2}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and that
\{u_{j}\}
is uniformly locally essentially bounded from above in
\Omega
. If
u_{j}
converges to a function
u
pointwise almost everywhere in
\Omega
, then
u
is a supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

We first observe that
u\in W^{s,G}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\cap L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Indeed, for each open sets
D_{2}\Subset D_{1}\Subset\Omega
the function
u_{j}
is uniformly essentially bounded from above in
D_{1}
. We may assume that
u_{j}\leq 0
in
D_{1}
for all
j
. Note that

\int_{D_{2}}G(|u_{j}|)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{D_{2}}G(|u_{1}|)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\quad\text{uniformly in}~j.

Moreover, by
Lemma

5.3

(5.3)

\int_{D_{2}}\int_{D_{2}}G(|D^{s}u_{j}|)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}\leq C\quad\text{uniformly in}~j.

Thus, Fatou’s lemma shows that
u\in W^{s,G}(D_{2})
. Moreover, it follows from
u_{j}\to u
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that
h_{1}\leq u\leq h_{2}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, meaning that
u\in L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Since
\mathcal{E}(u_{j},\varphi)\geq 0
for all nonnegative functions
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
, it is enough to prove that
\mathcal{E}(u_{j},\varphi)\to\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)
as
j\to\infty
. We may assume that
\mathrm{supp}\,\varphi=D_{3}\Subset D_{2}
. For
\theta\in(0,1)
we define a set
A_{\theta}:=\{x\in D_{2}:|u_{j}(x)-u(x)|<\theta\}
. We write

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u_{j},\varphi)-\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int_{A_{\theta}}\int_{A_{\theta}}\left(g(|D^{s}u_{j}|)\frac{D^{s}u_{j}}{|D^{s}u_{j}|}-g(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}\right)D^{s}\varphi\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\iint_{D_{2}^{2}\setminus A_{\theta}^{2}}\left(g(|D^{s}u_{j}|)\frac{D^{s}u_{j}}{|D^{s}u_{j}|}-g(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}\right)D^{s}\varphi\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{D_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus D_{2}}\left(g(|D^{s}u_{j}|)\frac{D^{s}u_{j}}{|D^{s}u_{j}|}-g(|D^{s}u|)\frac{D^{s}u}{|D^{s}u|}\right)D^{s}\varphi\frac{k(x,y)}{|x-y|^{n}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

Let
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{q-1}]
and apply
Lemma

A.5
with
a=D^{s}u_{j}
and
b=D^{s}u
, then we have

|I_{1}|\leq\frac{C}{\delta}g^{\delta}(\theta)\int_{A_{\theta}}\int_{A_{\theta}}\left(g^{1-\delta}(|D^{s}u_{j}|)+g^{1-\delta}(|D^{s}u|)\right)\frac{|D^{s}\varphi|}{|x-y|^{(q-1)\delta s}}\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}.

We define a Young function
H(t)=G^{\ast}(t^{1/(1-\delta)})
. By applying Young’s inequality
Lemma

2.2
, we obtain

|I_{1}|\leq Cg^{\delta}(\theta)\int_{D_{2}}\int_{D_{2}}\left(G(|D^{s}u_{j}|)+G(|D^{s}u|)+H^{\ast}\left(\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|}{|x-y|^{s+(q-1)\delta s}}\right)\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}.

By recalling (
5.3
) and taking
\delta
sufficiently small so that
s+(q-1)\delta s<1
, we obtain
|I_{1}|\leq Cg^{\delta}(\theta)
.

For
I_{2}
, we have by means of Young’s inequality

|I_{2}|\leq\varepsilon\iint_{D_{2}^{2}\setminus A_{\theta}^{2}}(G(|D^{s}u_{j}|)+G(|D^{s}u|))\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}+C(\varepsilon)\iint_{D_{2}^{2}\setminus A_{\theta}^{2}}G(|D^{s}\varphi|)\frac{\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x}{|x-y|^{n}}

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\lim_{j\to\infty}|D_{2}^{2}\setminus A_{\theta}^{2}|=0
due to the pointwise convergence of
u_{j}
, we have
\limsup_{j\to\infty}|I_{2}|\leq C\varepsilon
. Therefore, by sending
\varepsilon\to 0
and then
\theta\to 0
, we conclude

(5.4)

\limsup_{j\to\infty}(|I_{1}|+|I_{2}|)=0.

Let us next investigate
I_{3}
. We observe that the integrand in
I_{3}
is bounded by

C\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left(g\left(\frac{|h_{i}(x)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)+g\left(\frac{|h_{i}(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\right)\frac{\varphi(x)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}.

Note also that

\int_{D_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus D_{2}}g\left(\frac{|h_{i}(x)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{\varphi(x)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\int_{D_{3}}g(|h_{i}|)\,\mathrm{d}x

for some
C=C(n,p,q,s,\mathrm{dist}(D_{3},\partial D_{2}))>0
and

\displaystyle\int_{D_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus D_{2}}g\left(\frac{|h_{i}(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{\varphi(x)}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\left(\frac{|h_{i}(y)|}{(1+|y|)^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{(1+|y|)^{n+s}}

for some
C=C(n,p,q,s,\mathrm{dist}(D_{3},\partial D_{2}),\mathrm{diam}(D_{3}))>0
. Since
h_{i}\in L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we can apply the dominated convergence theorem to conclude that

(5.5)

\lim_{j\to\infty}|I_{3}|=0.

It follows from (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) that
\mathcal{E}(u_{j},\varphi)\to\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)
as
j\to\infty
.
∎

The boundedness assumption in
Theorem

5.6
can be removed if we assume that the sequence is increasing.

Corollary 5.7
.

Assume (
4.4
). Let
\{u_{j}\}
be an increasing sequence of supersolutions of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
such that
u_{j}
converges a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to a function
u\in W^{s,G}_{\mathrm{loc}}\cap L_{s}^{g}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as
j\to\infty
. Then
u
is a supersolution in
\Omega
as well.

Proof.

For each
M>0
, we define
u_{k}:=u\land k
and
u_{j,k}:=u_{j}\land k
. Then
u_{j,k}
is a supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u_{j,k}=0
in
\Omega
. To prove this, one can follow the lines of proof of Lemma 7 in Korvenpää–Kuusi–Palatucci
[
27
]
. We omit the proof because it is essentially the same. Moreover, since
u_{1,k}\leq u_{j,k}\leq k
for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
u_{j,k}\to u_{k}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as
j\to\infty
,
Theorem

5.6
shows that
u_{k}
is a supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u_{k}=0
in
\Omega
, namely
\mathcal{E}(u_{k},\varphi)\geq 0
for any nonnegative function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
. Since

\left|g(|D^{s}u_{k}|)\frac{D^{s}u_{k}}{|D^{s}u_{k}|}D^{s}\varphi\right|\leq g(|D^{s}u|)|D^{s}\varphi|\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2n};|x-y|^{-n})

for all

, we employ the dominated convergence theorem to arrive at the conclusion.
∎

6.
Properties of superharmonic functions

As explained in the introduction, superharmonic functions play a fundamental role in nonlocal nonlinear potential theory. We investigate the relation between supersolutions and superharmoinc functions, and study some properties of superharmonic functions.

6.1.
Relation between supersolutions and superharmonic functions

Supersolutions and superharmonic functions are closely related to each other. We prove that under certain circumstances supersolutions are superharmonic functions and vice versa.

Theorem 6.1
.

(i)

Let
u
be a supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
that is lower semicontinuous in
\Omega
and satisfies

(6.1)

u(x)=\essliminf_{y\to x}u(y)\quad\text{for every}~x\in\Omega.

Then
u
is superharmonic in
\Omega
.

(ii)

Let
u
be a superharmonic function in
\Omega
. If
u
is locally bounded from above in
\Omega
or
W^{s,G}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, then
u
is a supersolution of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
.

Proof of
Theorem

6.1
(i).

It is enough to check that
Definition

1.2
(iii) holds. Let
D\Subset\Omega
be an open set and
v\in C(\overline{D})
a solution of
\mathcal{L}v=0
in
D
with
v_{+}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
u\geq v
on
\partial D
and a.e. on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus D
. For
\varepsilon>0
, we consider a compact set
K_{\varepsilon}:=\{v-\varepsilon\geq u\}\cap\overline{D}
, which satisfies
K_{\varepsilon}\cap\partial D=\emptyset
. Let
D_{1}
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with Lipschitz boundary such that
K_{\varepsilon}\subset D_{1}\Subset D
. Since
u\geq v-\varepsilon
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus D_{1}
and

is Lipschitz, we have
(u-v+\varepsilon)_{-}\in W^{s,G}_{0}(D_{1},\Omega)
, see Remark 2.2 (ii) in Kim–Lee–Lee
[
23
]
. Thus,
Lemma

5.2
yields
u\geq v-\varepsilon
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The desired result

in
D
follows from the assumption (
6.1
) and continuity of

. Indeed, for
x\in D
there exists

such that

and

u(x)\geq\essinf_{B_{r}(x)}u-\varepsilon\geq\inf_{B_{r}(x)}v-2\varepsilon\geq v(x)-3\varepsilon.

Since

and
x\in D
were arbitrary, we conclude
u\geq v
in
D
.
∎

The following lemma shows that one can approximate a superharmonic function by continuous supersolutions.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
u
be a superharmonic function in
\Omega

be an open set. There exists a sequence

of increasing supersolutions of
\mathcal{L}u_{j}=0

2.7
(ii). We define

It follows from the smoothness of
\psi_{j}
and the fact that

in

.An application of

is an increasing sequence. Indeed, for

. Moreover, we have

The following lemma shows the second part of

Lemma 6.3
.

6.3.

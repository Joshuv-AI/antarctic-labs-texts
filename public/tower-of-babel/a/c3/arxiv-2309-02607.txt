Big tiles in hyperbolic groups
Azer Akhmedov

Abstract
. We prove that if Γ \Gamma is a word hyperbolic group and K K is a finite subset of Γ \Gamma , then Γ \Gamma admits a tile containing K K .



A
tile
in a countable group
\Gamma
is any finite subset
F
of
\Gamma
of cardinality at least two such that with non-overlapping left shifts of
F
one can cover the whole group
\Gamma
. If
F
is a tile of
\Gamma
, then
\Gamma
admits a partitioning
\Gamma=\displaystyle\mathop{\sqcup}_{g\in C}gF
for some subset
C\subset\Gamma
called the
center set
of the
tiling

\tau(\Gamma,F)=\displaystyle\mathop{\sqcup}_{g\in C}gF
. A tile may have more than one (in fact, uncountably many) tiling.

The following property of groups is studied in
[
3
]
and
[
13
]
:

(P)
: Suppose that
\Gamma
is a countable group. We say that
\Gamma
has property
(P)
if and only if for all finite
subsets
K
of
\Gamma
, there is a tile of
\Gamma
containing
K
.

Example 1.
A 2-element subset
\{x,y\}
is a tile of a countable group
\Gamma
if and only if the element
x^{-1}y
is either non-torsion or has even order.

Example 2.
The set
\{0,1,3\}
is not a tile of
\mathbb{Z}
. More generally, the set
\{0,1,x\}

is a tile if and only if
x\equiv 2(mod\ 3)
. On the other hand, any finite arithmetic progression in
\mathbb{Z}
is a tile of
\mathbb{Z}
.

Example 3.
Let
p
be a prime number,
A=\{a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{p-1}\}\subset\mathbb{Z}
be a
finite subset where
a_{0}<a_{1}<\dots<a_{p-1}
and
A(z)=\displaystyle\mathop{\sum}_{i=0}^{p-1}z^{b_{i}}

where

for all

is a tile if and only if there
exists

such that the cyclotomic polynomial

divides

, see
[
12

. Newman

has
determined all tiles

of
\mathbb{Z}
where

is a prime power. In general, however, the
problem remains open. A positive solution to the following conjecture would be a nice characterization of tiles in

Conjecture of Coven-Meyerowitz,

In

3
]
the following facts are observed and proved:

(i) If

is a normal subgroup of

(ii) If

(iii) Abelian groups have property

.

Since a set of representatives of all cosets with respect to a given finite index subgroup form a tile, we can add the following statement:

(iv) Residually finite groups have property
(P)
.

As a corollary of (i) and (iii), one can state that (as it is done in
[
3
]
) solvable groups have property
(P)
. Let us recall that not all (finitely generated) solvable groups are residually finite. As an immediate corollary of (i), (ii) and (iii), it is also observed in
[

]

(P)
. Properties (i)-(iii) also allow us to observe that the property

is preserved under taking direct sums, direct products, wreath products, etc. Let us also observe that it is preserved under taking the free product (this is shown also in
[
3
]
by a different argument):

(v) If

have property
(P)
, then so does their free product

.

Indeed, we have a short exact sequence

where

is the normal subgroup generated by the set

. But the subgroup
[G,H]
is free (cf.
[

]
, Prop.4, page 6) and free groups have property
(P)
. (It suffices to verify property
(P)
for finitely generated subgroups and finitely generated free groups satisfy

since, with respect to the standard generating set, any connected set is a tile
[
1
]
or alternatively, any ball is a tile; see Example 5.)

The following question, asked in
[

]
and
[

]
, is still open:
Does every countable group have property

?

The purpose of this paper is to answer this question positively for word hyperbolic groups. Let us remind a reader that a finitely generated group is word hyperbolic if its Cayley graph with respect to some (consequently, with respect to any) finite generating set is a hyperbolic space in the sense of Gromov (see
[
5
]
,
[
9
]
or
[
8
]
for this and other basic notions of the theory of hyperbolic groups and hyperbolic spaces in general). Let us recall that for a geodesic metric space
X
, we say
X
is
\delta
-
hyperbolic
for some non-negative
\delta\geq 0
if all triangles are
\delta
-thin. This means that for any three points
p_{i}\in X,i\in\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
and geodesics
\alpha_{i}
(viewed as subsets of
X
) connecting
p_{i+1}
and
p_{i-1}
, for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
, any point of
\alpha_{i}
has distance at most
\delta
from the union
\alpha_{i-1}\cup\alpha_{i+1}
. The metric space
X
is called
hyperbolic
if it is
\delta
-hyperbolic for some
\delta\geq 0
.

We will also use another well-known description of thinness of triangles. For this purpose, for positive real numbers
p,q,r
, let
T_{p,q,r}
be a tree with one vertex of degree 3 (called the
central vertex
) and three vertices of degree 1 (called
leaves
) endowed with the tree metric where the edges have length
p,q
and
r
. If the triangle
\Delta=\alpha_{1}\cup\alpha_{2}\cup\alpha_{3}
(as in the previous paragraph), consisting of geodesics
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}
is
\delta
-thin, then there exist points
m_{i}\in\alpha_{i},i\in\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
such that

\max\{d(m_{i},m_{i+1}):i\in\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}\}\leq 4\delta,d(p_{i},m_{i+1})=d(p_{i},m_{i-1}):=l_{i},

and there exists a continuous map
\phi:\Delta\to T_{l_{1},l_{2},l_{3}}
where
T_{l_{1},l_{2},l_{3}}
is consisting of geodesics
\eta_{0},\eta_{1},\eta_{2}
of length
l_{0},l_{1},l_{2}
respectively connecting the central point
a
with the leaves
v_{0},v_{1},v_{2}
respectively such that the restriction of
\phi
to each side of
\Delta
is an isometry; in addition,

\phi^{-1}(a)=\{m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}\}\ \mathrm{and}\ \phi^{-1}(v_{i})=\{p_{i}\},i\in\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}

and for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z},x\in\eta_{i}\backslash\{a,v_{i}\}
, we have
\phi^{-1}(x)=\{y,z\}
where the point
y
belongs to the part of geodesic
\alpha_{i+1}
connecting
p_{i}
and
m_{i+1}
, the point
z
belongs to the part of geodesic
\alpha_{i-1}
connecting
p_{i}
and
m_{i-1}
, and
d(y,z)\leq 4\delta.
A geodesic triangle with vertices at
m_{0},m_{1},m_{2}
will be called a
core
of the triangle
\Delta
. Such a core will lie in a ball of radius
4\delta
.

We now state our key result.

Proposition 1.
Let
\Gamma
be a non-elementary word hyperbolic group without a non-trivial finite normal subgroup. Then for any finite subset
K\subset\Gamma
there exists
\xi\in\Gamma
such that
K\cup\{\xi\}
is a tile of
\Gamma
.

For the proof of this proposition, we need to recall some facts about word hyperbolic groups. Let
\Gamma
be a word hyperbolic group with a fixed finite generating set
S
where
S=S^{-1}
and
1\notin S
. Then the Cayley graph with respect to this generating set is
\delta
-hyperbolic for some
\delta>0
. For any
g\in\Gamma
, we will write
|g|
for the shortest length of a path representing
g
, and
d(g_{1},g_{2})=|g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}|
for the corresponding left-invariant Cayley metric.

\Gamma
acts on
\Gamma
by left translations which are isometries. In addition,
\Gamma
admits a boundary
\partial\Gamma
which is a compact metric space upon which
\Gamma
acts by homeomorphisms.
\Gamma\cup\partial\Gamma
is also compact and can be viewed as a compactification of
\Gamma
.

Non-identity isometries of a hyperbolic space
X
can be classified as
hyperbolic
(elements with two fixed points on the boundary
\partial X
one of which is attractive and another one repelling),
parabolic
(elements with one fixed point on the boundary
\partial X
where attractive and repelling points coincide) and
elliptic
(all other non-identity isometries; equivalently, an elliptic isometry is a non-identity isometry such that the orbit of any point of
X
under the iterations of it is bounded). In the restricted context when
X
is the Cayley graph of
\Gamma
, a non-torsion element acts as a hyperbolic element and torsion elements are elliptic (so we have no parabolic elements). A non-torsion element
\gamma\in\Gamma
has two fixed points
P_{a}(\gamma)
and
P_{r}(\gamma)
on
\partial\Gamma
;
P_{a}(\gamma)
(
P_{r}(\gamma)
) is an attractive (repelling) point, i.e. for all open neighborhoods
U,V
of
P_{a}(\gamma)
and
P_{r}(\gamma)
respectively, there exists
N\geq 1
such that for all
n>N
,
\gamma^{n}(\partial\Gamma\backslash V)\subseteq U
(
\gamma^{-n}(\partial\Gamma\backslash U)\subseteq V
). A torsion element may have no fixed point or many (finitely many or infinitely many) fixed points; in particular, an elliptic element may fix all points of
\partial\Gamma
. We will collect several well known facts in the following

Proposition 2.
Let
\Gamma
be a non-elementary word hyperbolic group. Then

a)
\partial\Gamma
is infinite;

b) the sets
\{P_{a}(\gamma):\gamma\ \mathrm{is\ a\ non}
-
\mathrm{torsion\ element}\}
and
\{P_{r}(\gamma):\gamma\ \mathrm{is\ a\ non}
-
\mathrm{torsion\ element}\}
are dense in
\partial\Gamma
;

c) the set
\{(P_{a}(\gamma),P_{r}(\gamma)):\gamma\ \mathrm{is\ a\ non}
-
\mathrm{torsion\ element}\}
is dense in
\partial\Gamma\times\partial\Gamma
;

d) The set
\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\gamma\ \mathrm{fixes}\ \partial\Gamma\ \mathrm{pointwise}\}
forms a finite normal subgroup.
\square

Using the above proposition, we will prove the following

Lemma 1.
Let
\Gamma
be a non-elementary word hyperbolic group with a fixed generating set and without a non-trivial finite normal subgroup, and
B
be a finite subset of
\Gamma\backslash\{1\}
. Then for all
N\geq 1
there exists a non-torsion element
\xi\in\Gamma
such that
|\xi^{mi}b\xi^{mj}|>N
for all
m\geq 1,i,j\in\{-1,1\}
and
b\in B
.

Proof.
Let
A=B\cup B^{-1}\cup\{x\in\Gamma:0<|x|\leq N\}
and
A=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\}
. Since
\Gamma
has no finite normal subgroup, for all

, there exists
p_{s}\in\partial\Gamma
such that
a_{s}(p_{s})\neq p_{s}
.

Let
P=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\}
. By Proposition 2, there exists a hyperbolic
\eta\in\Gamma\backslash\{1\}
such that
\{a(P):a\in A\cup\{1\}\}\cap Fix(\eta)=\emptyset
. Since
\eta
is non-torsion, we have
|\eta^{n}|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. If the claim of the lemma does not hold for any power
\xi=\eta^{l}
of
\eta
, then we have
|\eta^{mi}b\eta^{mj}|\leq N
for some

and for infinitely many

. Then, necessarily, for all

, there exist

and

such that

. Then, since

is finite, there exist
b\in A,i,j,k\in\{-1,1\}
such that for all

there exist

such that
\eta^{ip}b^{k}\eta^{jq}=b\ (\dagger)
.

Let

for some

. By our choice,
\{p_{r},b(p_{r}),b^{-1}(p_{r})\}\cap Fix(\eta)=\emptyset
. But for any open set

, if

and

are sufficiently big, then
\eta^{ip}b^{k}\eta^{jq}\in U
thus using

and disjoint from

.
\square

on
\gamma
such that

\max\{d(p,y),d(p,z)\}<0.2|\xi|\ \mathrm{and}\ \min\{d(p,y\xi^{i}),d(p,z\xi^{j})\}>0.8|\xi|\ (7)

Indeed, let
y_{1}=y\xi^{i},z_{1}=z\xi^{j}
. Then

hence by the inequality
|\xi^{-i}b\xi^{j}|>1.9|\xi|

d(y_{1},z_{1})>1.9|\xi|
. Then, defining the triangle
\Delta
and the points
g,g_{1},g_{2}
as before, we can write

1.9|\xi|<d(y_{1},z_{1})\leq d(y_{1},g_{1})+d(g_{1},g_{2})+d(g_{2},z_{1})\leq d(y_{1},g_{1})+4\delta+d(g_{2},z_{1})=

(d(y_{1},x)-d(x,g_{1}))+4\delta+(d(z_{1},x)-d(x,g_{2})=d(y_{1},x)+d(z_{1},x)+4\delta-2d(x,g_{1})\leq

d(g,y_{1})>0.8|\xi|\ (11)
. Similarly, we establish that
d(g,z_{1})>0.8|\xi|\ (12)

Now, by letting

, from the inequalities (9), (10), (11) and (12), we obtain the inequalities (7).

Again, without loss of generality we may assume that
p
is at most
\delta
apart from
\alpha
and using triangle inequalities as in (6) again, we have
d(1,y\xi^{i})\geq d(1,p)+d(p,y\xi^{i})-2\delta

|y|+r\geq|y\xi^{i}|=d(1,y\xi^{i})\geq d(1,p)+d(p,y\xi^{i})-2\delta\geq(|y|-0.2|\xi|)+d(p,y\xi^{i})-2\delta

r+2\delta\geq 0.6|\xi|
. Contradiction; thus we established the
4r
-separatedness of
S_{\xi,r}
.

Now, let
C_{\xi,r}=\{x\in S_{\xi,r}:|x\xi^{-1}|<|x|+r\}
. We first observe that the element
v^{-1}\xi
is not a torsion. Indeed, assuming the opposite, let
(\xi^{-1}v)^{q}=1
for some
q\geq 1
and
S
be the set consisting of elements
1,\xi^{-1},\xi^{-1}v,\xi^{-1}v\xi^{-1},\dots,(\xi^{-1}v)^{q-1},(\xi^{-1}v)^{q-1}\xi^{-1}
.

Let
\theta\in S
such that
|\theta|=\displaystyle\mathop{\max}_{x\in S}|x|
. Then either
\theta=(\xi^{-1}v)^{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq q-1
or

\theta=(\xi^{-1}v)^{i}\xi^{-1}
for some
0\leq i\leq q-2
. In the former case, we have
|\theta\xi^{-1}|\leq|\theta|<|\theta|+r
and
|\theta v^{-1}\xi|\leq|\theta|<|\theta|+r
thus
\theta,\theta v^{-1}\in S_{\xi,r}
, but this contradicts the fact that
S_{\xi,r}
is
r
-separated. In the latter case, we have
|\theta\xi|\leq|\theta|<|\theta|+r
and
|\theta v\xi^{-1}|\leq|\theta|<|\theta|+r
thus
\theta,\theta v\in S_{\xi,r}
which again contradicts
r
-separatedness of
S_{\xi,r}
.

Thus we established that
v^{-1}\xi
is not a torsion. Then
C_{\xi,r}\subseteq S_{\xi,r}
and for all
s\in C_{\xi,r}
we have
sv^{-1}\xi\in C_{\xi,r},\{s,sv^{-1}\xi\}\subset sv^{-1}(K\cup\{\xi\})
, moreover,
C_{\xi,r}\cap sv^{-1}(K\cup\{\xi\})=\{s,sv^{-1}\xi\}
. (To see why

, notice that we have
v\neq 1
and

. On the other hand,
s\in C_{\xi,r}\subset S_{\xi,r}
. But

is
4r
-separated. So

|sv^{-1}\xi|\geq|sv^{-1}|+r.

Then

|(sv^{-1}\xi)\xi^{-1}|=|sv^{-1}|\leq|sv^{-1}\xi|-r<|sv^{-1}\xi|+r.

Hence

.)

Then we have a subset (and enumeration)
\{s_{1},s_{2},\dots\}\subset C_{\xi,r}
and a partition
C_{\xi,r}=\displaystyle\mathop{\sqcup}_{n\geq 1}\{s_{n},s_{n}v^{-1}\xi\}
such that
|s_{i}|\leq|s_{j}|
for all

.

Let

. By the definition of

, for any two distinct

, we have
s_{i}v^{-1}(K\cup\{\xi\})\cap s_{j}v^{-1}(K\cup\{\xi\})=\emptyset
. We also have

.

Let
x_{1}^{(1)},x_{2}^{(1)},\dots
be all elements of the set

such that
|x_{i}^{(1)}|\leq|x_{j}^{(1)}|
if
i<j
. We start an infinite process of covering the set

by the non-overlapping shifts of

, namely:

Let
B_{1}=B,B_{2}=B\backslash x_{1}^{(1)}\xi^{-1}(K\cup\{\xi\})
. We denote the elements of
B_{2}
by

x_{1}^{(2)},x_{2}^{(2)},\dots
so that
|x_{i}^{(2)}|\leq|x_{j}^{(2)}|
if

, and let

B_{3}=B_{2}\backslash(x_{1}^{(1)}\xi^{-1}(K\cup\{\xi\})\cup x_{1}^{(2)}\xi^{-1}(K\cup\{\xi\})),

and continue the process to cover all elements of the set
B
, i.e. if the set

and the elements

Proposition 3.
If
\Gamma
is a non-elementary word hyperbolic group, then
\Gamma
satisfies property
(P)
.

Proof.
Indeed, let
H
be a finite normal subgroup of
\Gamma
(if no such subgroup exists, then we are done by Proposition 1). Then, since
\Gamma
does not contain an infinite direct limit of finite groups (let us recall a well known fact that any amenable subgroup of a word hyperbolic group is virtually cyclic), there exists a finite subgroup
H_{1}\triangleleft\Gamma
such that
\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma/H_{1}
is a non-elementary word hyperbolic group with no non-trivial finite normal subgroup. By Proposition 1,
\Gamma_{1}
satisfies property
(P)
. Since
H_{1}
is finite, it also satisfies
(P)
. Then, by the observation (i) in
[
3
]
as mentioned above, the group
\Gamma
, as an extension of
\Gamma_{1}
by
H_{1}
, satisfies
(P)
.
\square

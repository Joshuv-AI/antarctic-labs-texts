Abstract
. We review some results and proofs on eigenvalue bounds for random Schrödinger operators with complex-valued potentials. We also include new Schatten norm estimates for the resolvent and use them to obtain bounds for sums of eigenvalues.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Eigenvalue estimates for Schrödinger operators with complex potentials

Schrödinger operators with complex-valued potentials arise in the analysis of scattering resonances, open or damped quantum systems, etc. For decaying potentials
V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
that are not too singular,
-\Delta-V
can be realized as an m-sectorial operator in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with essential spectrum
[0,\infty)
; see, e.g.,
[
13
, Proposition B.2]
1
1

1

In the present situation, all definitions of essential spectrum coincide.
.
We are interested in the discrete eigenvalues of finite algebraic multiplicity,
\sigma_{\rm disc}(-\Delta-V)
, which can only accumulate at
[0,\infty)
. Natural quantities of interest are, e.g., the number and distribution of eigenvalues of
-\Delta-V
. For complex-valued
V
, the analysis of these quantities yields results that are surprisingly different from those in the case of real-valued
V
. We refer to
[
14
, Section 5.13]
and the references therein for a survey.

In the first part of this note, we review estimates for single eigenvalues of
-\Delta-V
which only depend on
\|V\|_{q}
.
In the second part, we prove new estimates for sums of eigenvalues.
As in previous studies on this subject, the decisive feature of
V
is its decay.
Hence, for the rest of the paper, we will always assume that
V
is bounded.

To motivate the sought-after estimates, let us consider the situation of real-valued
V
for a moment. In this case, the Keller–Lieb–Thirring inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda_{j}\in\sigma_{\rm disc}(-\Delta-V)}|\lambda_{j}|^{q-d/2}\lesssim_{d,q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x)_{+}^{q}\,dx,\quad\begin{cases}q\in[1,\infty),&\ d=1\\
q\in(1,\infty),&\ d=2\\
q\in[d/2,\infty),&\ d\geq 3\end{cases}

(1)

quantifies the eigenvalue accumulation at zero when
q>d/2
and the number of eigenvalues when
q=d/2
and
d\geq 3
. Here and below, we write
A\lesssim B
for two non-negative quantities
A,B\geq 0
to indicate that there is a constant
C>0
such that
A\leq CB
. The dependence of the constant on fixed parameters like
d
and
q
is usually omitted.
The notation
A\sim B
means
A\lesssim B\lesssim A
.
Inequality (
1
) for single eigenvalues was first proved by Keller
[
17
]
and later generalized to eigenvalue sums by Lieb and Thirring
[
21
]
, who used this inequality to give a simple proof of stability of quantum matter.
Inequality (
1
) is interesting because it is invariant under spatial rescalings and recovers Weyl’s law in the strong-coupling limit, i.e., when one replaces
V
with
\kappa V
, and considers the limit
\kappa\to\infty
.

A natural question to ask is how much of (
1
) survives when
V
is complex-valued. A first answer in the context of single eigenvalues was given by Abramov, Aslanyan, and Davies
[
1
]
. For
d=1
, they showed

\displaystyle|z|^{1/2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|V|\,dx,\quad z\in\sigma_{\rm disc}(-\Delta-V).

(2)

Shortly afterwards, Frank, Laptev, Lieb, and Seiringer
[
15
]
considered the higher-dimensional case and
V\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
q\geq d/2
, and showed

\displaystyle\sum_{z_{j}\in\sigma_{\rm disc}(-\Delta-V),\,|\operatorname{Im}z_{j}|\geq\kappa\operatorname{Re}z_{j}}|z_{j}|^{q-\frac{d}{2}}\lesssim\left(1+\kappa^{-1}\right)^{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|V|^{q}.

(3)

While this inequality shows that (
1
) continues to hold for eigenvalues of
-\Delta-V
outside any fixed sector in the complex plane, (
3
) deteriorates for eigenvalues close to
[0,\infty)
.
For individual eigenvalues, Laptev and Safronov
[
19
, p. 31]
made the following

Conjecture 1
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
q\in(d/2,d]
. Then, any eigenvalue
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
of
-\Delta-V
satisfies

\displaystyle|z|^{q-\frac{d}{2}}\lesssim_{d,q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|V|^{q}.

(4)

Laptev and Safronov were careful to state this conjecture only for
q\leq d
because they were aware of the real-valued Wigner–Neumann potential
[
23
, p. 223]
. This is a radial potential which oscillates and decays like
|x|^{-1}
, i.e., it almost belongs to
L^{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and generates an eigenvalue embedded at
1
.
Bögli
[
2
]
proved that the range of
q
would be best possible for radial potentials. Frank and Simon
[
16
]
proved Conjecture
1
for radial potentials when
q<d
. Disregarding the endpoint
q=d
, these two results provide a complete answer to the Laptev–Safronov conjecture for radial potentials.
For general potentials, Frank
[
12
]
proved

\displaystyle|z|^{q-d/2}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|V(x)|^{q}\,dx,\quad z\in\sigma_{\rm disc}(-\Delta-V),\ q\leq\frac{d+1}{2}.

(5)

The starting point to prove (
5
) is the Birman–Schwinger principle, i.e., the statement that

\displaystyle z\in\sigma_{\rm disc}(-\Delta-V)\Leftrightarrow 1\in\sigma_{\rm disc}(BS(z)),\quad BS(z):=|V|^{1/2}(-\Delta-z)^{-1}V^{1/2},

(6)

with
V^{1/2}=V/|V|^{1/2}
.
In particular, if
z\in\sigma_{\rm disc}(-\Delta-V)
, then the spectral radius
{\rm spr}(BS(z))\geq 1
. Thus, to get an eigenvalue estimate, it suffices to bound
{\rm spr}(BS(z))
from above by a negative power of
|z|
. A simple estimate is
{\rm spr}(BS(z))\leq\|BS(z)\|
.
By Hölder’s inequality and the homogeneity of
-\Delta
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}1\leq{\rm spr}(BS(z))\leq\|BS(z)\|&\leq\|V\|_{q}\|(-\Delta-z)^{-1}\|_{p\to p^{\prime}}\\
&=|z|^{d/(2q)-1}\|V\|_{q}\|(-\Delta-z/|z|)^{-1}\|_{p\to p^{\prime}},\end{split}

(7)

where
1/q=1/p-1/p^{\prime}
and
p^{\prime}=(1-1/p)^{-1}
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
Kenig, Ruiz, and Sogge
[
18
]
showed
\|(-\Delta-z/|z|)^{-1}\|_{p\to p^{\prime}}\lesssim_{p,d}1
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
if and only if
q\in[d/2,(d+1)/2]
.
Thus, (
5
) follows.
Let us comment on the necessity of
q\in[d/2,(d+1)/2]
. The lower bound is a consequence of Sobolev’s inequality. We explain the necessity of
q\leq(d+1)/2
. By Stone’s formula and the spectral theorem or Plancherel’s theorem,
\operatorname{Im}(-\Delta-1+i0)^{-1}
is proportional to the operator
\mathcal{E}\mathcal{E}^{*}
, i.e., the composition of the Fourier restriction operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ni f\mapsto(\mathcal{E}^{*}f)(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dx\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}f(x)\Big|_{\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})

(8)

and the Fourier extension operator

\displaystyle L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})\ni g\mapsto(\mathcal{E}g)(x):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}d\sigma(\xi)\mathrm{e}^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}g(\xi)\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(9)

with the Leray surface measure
\sigma
on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.
In the following, we will also write
\hat{f}(\xi):=(\mathcal{F}f)(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}f(x)\,dx
for the Fourier transform of
f
and
(f)^{\vee}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}f(\xi)\,d\xi
for the inverse Fourier transform.
Thus, to investigate necessary conditions for the
L^{p}\to L^{p^{\prime}}
-boundedness of
(-\Delta-z)^{-1}
, it suffices to investigate necessary conditions for
p
on the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
-boundedness of
\mathcal{E}^{*}
. On the one hand, by the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma,
\|\mathcal{E}^{*}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}\lesssim 1
. On the other hand, since the Fourier transform maps
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
unitarily into itself, a Fourier restriction of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-functions is meaningless, since
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-functions belong to an equivalence class of functions within which its members are allowed to differ from each other on Lebesgue measure zero sets, i.e., in particular on hypersurfaces like
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Considering the Fourier transform of an indicator function on a rectangular box
[0,\varepsilon^{-1}]^{d-1}\times[0,\varepsilon^{-2}]
and exploiting the quadratic curvature of the sphere, one observes
p\leq 2(d+1)/(d+3)
is a necessary condition for
\mathcal{E}^{*}
to be
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
-bounded—this is known as Knapp’s example
[
27
,
24
]
. In particular, Knapp’s example shows the necessity of
q\leq(d+1)/2
in the resolvent bound of Kenig, Ruiz, and Sogge, and led to the belief that Frank’s bound (
5
) is optimal. Inspired by Knapp’s construction, Bögli and the first author
[
3
]
succeeded to construct potentials
V\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
q>(d+1)/2
for which (
4
) is violated.

1.2.
Eigenvalue estimates for Schrödinger operators with random complex potentials

While the results
[
2
,
16
,
12
,
3
]
provide a complete answer to the Laptev–Safronov conjecture
1
, it is interesting to investigate if the range of exponents in (
5
) can be increased for “generic”
V
.
Here, we investigate how strong one must perturb or destroy the counterexample of
[
2
,
3
]
to recover a Keller–Lieb–Thirring inequality.
A rather strong way to do so is to take this counterexample, or, more generally, any given deterministic potential, decomposing its support into boxes of side length
h>0
, and multiplying it on every
h
-cube with a mean-zero random variable. More precisely, for independently and identically distributed, mean-zero Gaussian or symmetric Bernoulli random variables
(\omega_{j})_{j\in h\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
, we consider the Anderson randomization of
V
, given by

\displaystyle V_{\omega}(x)=\sum_{j\in h\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\omega_{j}V(x)\mathbf{1}_{Q}((x-j)/h),\quad Q=[0,1)^{d}.

(10)

We denote the product measure associated to the
\omega_{j}
by
\mathbf{P}
and the expectation by
\mathbf{E}
.

Our results in
[
9
]
, Theorems
1
–
2
below, say that Frank’s eigenvalue estimate (
5
) continues to hold for random potentials that are allowed to decay almost twice as slowly as their deterministic counterparts. We state this result in two forms. The first form involves compactly supported potentials with
\operatorname{supp}V\subseteq B(R)
.
We write
\langle x\rangle:=2+|x|
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
B(x,R):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,|x-y|<R\}
, and
B(R)
for a ball with radius
R
and unspecified center.

Theorem 1

(
[
9
, Theorem 1]
)
.

There exist constants
M_{0},c>0
such that the following holds. For any
R,\lambda>0
,
0<h<R
,
|\varepsilon|\ll\lambda
,
q\leq d+1
, for any
V\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\operatorname{supp}V\subseteq B(R)
, and for any
M\geq M_{0}
, each eigenvalue
z=(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon)^{2}
of
-\Delta-V_{\omega}
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{2-\frac{d}{q}}}{\langle\lambda h\rangle^{d/2}(\ln\langle\lambda R\rangle)^{7/2}}\leq M\|V\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(11)

except for
\omega
in a set of measure at most
\exp(-cM^{2})
.

While compactly supported potentials belong to any
L^{q}
-space, the point of (
11
) is the weak (logarithmic) dependence on
R
. In contrast, the deterministic estimate (
5
) and Hölder’s inequality yield a power law, i.e.,

\displaystyle\lambda^{\frac{2}{d+1}}\lesssim R^{d(\frac{2}{d+1}-\frac{1}{q})}\|V\|_{L^{q}},\quad q\geq\frac{d+1}{2}.

(12)

In the next theorem, we consider
V\in L^{q}
, which are not assumed to be compactly supported anymore.

Theorem 2

(
[
9
, Theorem 3]
)
.

For any
q<d+1
, there exist constants
M_{0},c>0
such that the following holds. For any
h,\lambda>0
,
|\varepsilon|\ll\lambda
, for any
V\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and for any
M\geq M_{0}
, each eigenvalue
z=(\lambda+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon)^{2}
of
-\Delta-V_{\omega}
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{2-\frac{d}{q}}}{\langle\lambda h\rangle^{d/2}(\ln\langle\lambda h\rangle)^{2}}\leq M\|V\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(13)

except for
\omega
in a set of measure at most
\exp(-cM^{2})
.

In the next Subsection, we explain the strategy of the proof of Theorems
1
–
2
.
In Section
2
, we prove new Schatten norm estimates (Theorem
5
) and apply them to conclude bounds for eigenvalue sums of
-\Delta-V_{\omega}
(Theorem
7
).

1.3.
Ideas of the proof of Theorems
1
–
2

By scaling, it suffices to consider eigenvalues of the form
z=(1+i\varepsilon)^{2}
. Moreover, by (
3
), it suffices to consider
|\varepsilon|\ll 1
.
Again, the first step is to use the Birman–Schwinger principle.
For technical reasons, we consider the spectral radius, using Gelfand’s formula

\displaystyle{\rm spr}(BS(z))=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|BS(z)^{n}\|^{1/n},

(14)

instead of
\|BS(z)\|
.
By the decay of
V
and the uncertainty principle, the resolvents in

\displaystyle|V|^{1/2}R(z)VR(z)V...

(15)

are expected to be smoothed out in Fourier space. Indeed, by the convolution theorem, we get, for any two balls
B(x_{1},R_{1})
and
B(x_{2},R_{2})
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{B(x_{1},R_{1})}R(z)\mathbf{1}_{B(x_{2},R_{2})}=\mathbf{1}_{B(x_{1},R_{1})}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}-z}\ast\gamma_{\delta}\right)\mathcal{F}\mathbf{1}_{B(x_{2},R_{2})},

(16)

where
\delta^{-1}\geq R_{1}+R_{2}+|x_{1}-x_{2}|
and
\gamma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Schwartz function with
\check{\gamma}\in C_{c}^{\infty}
.
To simplify the following notation, we denote, for
\delta>0
, by
C^{(\delta)}(D)
any Fourier multiplier whose symbol obeys

\displaystyle|C^{(\delta)}(\xi)|\leq(||\xi|^{2}-|z||+\delta)^{-1/2},\quad z=(1+i\varepsilon)^{2},\ |\varepsilon|\ll 1.

(17)

We now explain some details of the proofs of Theorems
1
and
2
.

1.3.1.
On the proof of Theorem
1

In the situation of Theorem
1
, all resolvents appearing in Gelfand’s formula for
{\rm spr}(BS(z))
can be replaced with
C^{(\delta)}(D)^{2}
with
\delta^{-1}\geq 2R
. Thus, to bound
{\rm spr}(BS(z))
, it suffices to estimate the operator norm of the “elementary operators”
C^{(\delta)}(D)V_{\omega}C^{(\delta^{\prime})}(D)
for suitable
\delta,\delta^{\prime}>0
. By elliptic estimates, it suffices to consider those frequencies
|\xi|
for which
|\xi|\sim|z|\sim 1
. By the spectral theorem or Plancherel’s theorem, we have, for any
F:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}
,

\displaystyle F(|D|)=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\lambda\,F(\lambda)\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{*},

(18)

where we denoted
M_{\lambda}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,|\xi|=\lambda\}
, the Fourier extension and restriction operators

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}:L^{2}(M_{\lambda},d\sigma_{\lambda})\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}g)(x)=\int_{M_{\lambda}}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}g(\xi)\,d\sigma_{\lambda}(\xi),

and the Leray surface measure
\sigma_{\lambda}
on
M_{\lambda}
. Thus, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|C^{(\delta)}(D)\mathbf{1}_{|D|\in[1/2,2]}V_{\omega}C^{(\delta^{\prime})}(D)\mathbf{1}_{|D|\in[1/2,2]}\|\\
&\quad\lesssim\ln^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{\delta}\ln^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}\sup_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in[1/2,2]}\|\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{*}V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}(M_{\lambda^{\prime}})\to L^{2}(M_{\lambda})},\end{split}

(19)

see also
[
9
, Lemma 18]
. Thus, it suffices to estimate
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{*}V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda^{\prime}}
. The main technical result in
[
9
]
is the following

Theorem 3

(
[
9
, Lemma 15]
)
.

Let
q<d+1
,
R\geq h
, and
V_{\omega}(x)
be defined as in (
10
) with
\operatorname{supp}V_{\omega}\subseteq B(R)
. Then,

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in[1/2,2]}\mathbf{E}\|\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{*}V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}(M_{\lambda^{\prime}})\to L^{2}(M_{\lambda})}\lesssim\langle h\rangle^{\frac{d}{2}}(\ln\langle R\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\ln\langle h\rangle+\ln\langle R\rangle\right)^{2}\|V\|_{q}.

(20)

In particular, there are constants
M_{0},c>0
such that for any
M\geq M_{0}
, the estimate

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in[1/2,2]}\|\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{*}V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}(M_{\lambda^{\prime}})\to L^{2}(M_{\lambda})}\lesssim M\langle h\rangle^{\frac{d}{2}}(\ln\langle R\rangle)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\ln\langle h\rangle+\ln\langle R\rangle\right)^{2}\|V\|_{q}

(21)

holds for all
\omega
outside a set of measure at most
\mathrm{e}^{-cM^{2}}
.

Estimate (
21
) follows from (
20
) and the Gaussian tail bound

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(\|X\|>t)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{ct^{2}}{(\mathbf{E}\|X\|)^{2}}\right)

(22)

for Banach space valued random variables
X
with symmetric Bernoulli or Gaussian distribution
2
2

2

We are not aware of tail bounds for Banach space valued random variables with more general sub-Gaussian distributions. This is the reason for our assumption on the distribution of the
\omega_{j}
in Theorems
1
–
3
, see, e.g.,
[
9
, Lemma 12]
and the reference
[
20
]
therein.
Plugging (
21
) into (
19
) and combining it with the previous strategy allows to conclude the proof of Theorem
1
.

1.3.2.
On the proof of Theorem
3

The proof of Theorem
3
uses techniques of Bourgain
[
4
,
5
]
in the context of scattering theory of random lattice Schrödinger operators with long-range potentials. The basic ingredient to prove Theorem
3
is the square root cancellation in the estimate

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\sup_{j\leq N}\sum_{i=1}^{j}|X_{i}|\lesssim\sqrt{\log N}\sup_{j\leq N}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{j}\|X_{i}\|_{\psi_{2}}^{2}}

(23)

for a sequence of symmetric Bernoulli or Gaussian random variables
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.
This is stated in
[
9
, Section 4]
, where we refer to
[
28
]
for a reference of the definition of the
\psi_{2}
-norm and the statement and proof of (
23
).
Our goal is to apply (
23
) to estimate the right-hand side of

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\|\mathcal{E}^{*}V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}\|=\mathbf{E}\sup_{g,g^{\prime}\in L^{2}(M),\|g\|,\|g^{\prime}\|\leq 1}|\langle\mathcal{E}g,V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}g^{\prime}\rangle|.

(24)

Since
L^{2}(M)
has infinite cardinality, (
23
) cannot be applied directly.
However, some reductions are possible. Recall that we consider a problem where both the frequencies and the positions are localized; indeed
|\xi|\sim 1
and
|x|\leq R
. By the uncertainty principle, we expect locally constant properties in position and frequency on the respective reciprocal scales, i.e., on the unit scale in position space, and on the scale
R^{-1}
in frequency space.
In particular, we expect that we can discretize the problem in position space on the unit scale and in frequency space on the scale
R^{-1}
, and consider

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbf{E}\sup_{g,g^{\prime}\in\ell^{2}(M\cap\Lambda_{R}^{*}),\|g\|,\|g^{\prime}\|\leq 1}|\langle\mathcal{E}_{\rm d}g,v_{\omega}\mathcal{E}_{\rm d}g^{\prime}\rangle|\end{split}

(25)

instead of (
24
), for
v_{\omega}=v_{\omega}(n)=\omega_{n}v_{n}
,
n\in h\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, discrete versions
\mathcal{E}_{\rm d}^{*}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\rm d}
of the Fourier restriction and extension operators, and a
1/R
-net
\Lambda_{R}^{*}\cap M
of points on
M
.
Following Bourgain, we replace the supremum over the set
\{\mathcal{E}_{\rm d}g:\,\|g\|_{\ell^{2}(M\cap\Lambda_{R}^{*})}\leq 1\}
on the right-hand side of (
25
) by a supremum over a finite set up to an “entropy” error. Importantly, we can prove suitable bounds for the logarithm of the cardinality of this finite set, called “entropy”
[
28
, p. 170]
.
The idea to achieve this replacement is as follows.
Consider the set
\{\mathcal{E}_{\rm d}g:\,\|g\|_{\ell^{2}(M\cap\Lambda_{R}^{*})}\leq 1\}
and cover it with disjoint dyadic boxes of side length
2^{-k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, in the
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap B(R))
-metric. We collect the centers of these boxes in the set
Z_{k}:=\{\xi_{k}(j)\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. Then, to approximate any given
\mathcal{E}_{\rm d}g\in\ell^{\infty}
with
\|g\|_{\ell^{2}(M\cap\Lambda_{R}^{*})}\leq 1
, we start from the origin, walk to the center
\xi_{0}(j_{0})
of a box with side length
2^{0}
, which is closest to
\mathcal{E}_{\rm d}g
, then walk to the center
\xi_{1}(j_{1})
of a box with side length
2^{-1}
, which is closest to
\mathcal{E}_{\rm d}g
, etc. In this way, we can, for any
g\in\ell^{2}(M\cap\Lambda_{R}^{*})
with
\|g\|_{2}\leq 1
, construct a chain of centers
\{\xi_{k}\}
of boxes of side length
2^{-k}
converging to
\mathcal{E}_{\rm d}g
. Formally, this is just the telescoping series

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{\rm d}g&=\mathcal{E}_{\rm d}g-\xi_{0}(j_{0})+\xi_{0}(j_{0})=\mathcal{E}_{\rm d}g-\xi_{0}(j_{0})+\xi_{0}(j_{0})-\xi_{1}(j_{1})+\xi_{1}(j_{1})=...\\
&=:\mathcal{E}_{\rm d}g-\xi_{0}(j_{0})+\sum_{k\geq 0}\xi^{(k)},\quad\xi^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}_{k}\subseteq Z_{k}-Z_{k+1},\end{split}

(26)

with the property that
\|\xi^{(k)}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\lesssim 2^{-k}
,
\xi^{(k)}\in\mathcal{F}_{k}
. To apply (
23
), we thus need to compute the cardinality of the subset
\mathcal{F}_{k}
of all difference vectors between boxes with centers in
Z_{k}
and
Z_{k+1}
which are as close to each other as possible. To that end, we use the “dual Sudakov inequality”, first proved by Pajor and Tomczak–Jaegermann
[
22
]
and later, in a simpler fashion, by Bourgain, Milman, and Lindenstrauss
[
6
, Proposition 4.2]
. It says that
\ln|Z_{k}|\lesssim\ln(\langle R\rangle)4^{k}
. Thus,

1.3.3.
On the proof of Theorem

Even if

To quantify the decay of

[

\displaystyle V=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}V_{i},\quad V_{i}=V\mathbf{1}_{H_{i}\geq|V|\geq H_{i+1}},\quad H_{i}=\inf\{t>0:\,|\{|V|>t\}|\leq 2^{i-1}\}.

The widths of the supports of

\|H_{i}2^{i/q}\|_{\ell^{r}_{i}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})}\sim\|V\|_{L^{q,r}}

depends on the diameter of the
\Omega_{j}
and their relative distance. While the sizes of the
\operatorname{supp}(V_{i})
are known, their diameter and relative distance is not accessible yet. To control these properties, we apply a sparse decomposition to each
\operatorname{supp}(V_{i})
. We say that for
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\gamma,R>0
, a family

are

-separated.
For fixed

be the number of sparse families used to cover
\operatorname{supp}(V_{i})
, we let

be the number of balls inside any sparse family, and we let

Then, according to Tao

(see also

\displaystyle V_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{K_{i}}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{i}}V_{ijk},

Plugging the decomposition (

2.
Eigenvalue sums

Being able to locate a region in the complex plane for which we can say where all eigenvalues of
-\Delta-V_{\omega}
are located, it is natural to consider the accumulation of these eigenvalues.
This will lead us to estimate the singular values of

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{M_{\lambda}}^{(\omega)}:=\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{*}V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda},\quad\lambda>0.

(36)

That is, we will estimate
\|\mathcal{V}_{M}^{(\omega)}\|_{\mathcal{S}^{p}}
for some
p\geq 1
, with the

\|T\|_{\mathcal{S}^{p}(\mathcal{H})}^{p}:=\sum_{n\geq 1}s_{n}(T)^{p}
and the singular values
\{s_{n}(T)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of some compact operator

on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. We also write
\|T\|_{\mathcal{S}^{p,\infty}(\mathcal{H})}:=\sup_{m}s_{m}(T)m^{\frac{1}{p}}
for the weak
p
-th Schatten norm.
For the sake of simplicity, and, because our methods are not likely to yield optimal results for
L^{q}
-potentials,
we will only consider compactly supported or pointwise decaying potentials in this section.

Our approach to bound
s_{k}(\mathcal{V}_{M_{\lambda}}^{(\omega)})
consists of two steps. First, we adapt the estimate (
20
) for
\mathbf{E}\|\mathcal{V}_{M}^{(\omega)}\|
. Then, using an observation recorded, e.g., in
[
11
, (3.4.13)]
, we obtain bounds for all other singular values of
\mathcal{V}_{M}^{(\omega)}
by essentially comparing them to the singular values of powers of the Laplace–Beltrami operator
-\Delta_{M}
; see Theorem
5
for the final result. As an application, we prove estimates for sums of eigenvalues of
-\Delta-V_{\omega}
(Theorem
7
).

Proof.

Theorem 5
.

Let

and

(53)

Consequently, for all
\varepsilon>0
,
R_{0}\geq 1
and

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\|\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{*}V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}\|_{\mathcal{S}^{(d-1)/\nu,\infty}}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}R_{0}^{\varepsilon}\langle\lambda h\rangle^{d/2}\lambda^{1/2+\nu+\varepsilon-d}\|\langle x\rangle^{1/2+\varepsilon+\nu}V\|_{\infty}.

(54)

Proof.

Formula (
54
) follows from (
53
) by a dyadic decomposition and using that
\ln(\langle\lambda R\rangle)^{5/2}\lesssim(\lambda R)^{\varepsilon}\,R_{0}^{\varepsilon}
since

without loss of generality. To prove (

\displaystyle\begin{split}s_{k}(\mathcal{V}_{M}^{(\omega)})&\leq s_{k}(\langle-\Delta_{M}\rangle^{-\nu/4})^{2}\cdot\|\langle-\Delta_{M}\rangle^{\nu/4}\mathcal{E}^{*}V_{\omega}\mathcal{E}\langle-\Delta_{M}\rangle^{\nu/4}\|\\

To bound the operator norm on the right-hand side, we proceed as in Section

. By an integration by parts,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\langle-\Delta_{M}\rangle^{\nu/4}=:\mathcal{E}_{a},

(56)

where

\displaystyle(\mathcal{E}_{a}g)(x)=\int_{M}d\sigma(\xi)e(x\cdot\xi)a(x,\xi)g(\xi),

(57)

4

\displaystyle\|\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda_{R}^{*}}b(\mu)\cdot a(x,\mu)\cdot e(\mu\cdot x)\|_{L^{2}(|x|<R)}\lesssim R^{d/2+\nu/2}\|b(\mu)\|_{\ell_{\mu}^{2}(\Lambda_{R}^{*})}.

(68)

where

(71)

are those points for which

fails to be invertible.

6
with

\displaystyle p=\frac{d-1}{\varepsilon}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma=\frac{3}{4}-\frac{3\varepsilon}{2}>0,

(72)

and the bound (

) for

yields the following

V\in\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}-3\varepsilon}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Let
\{z_{j}\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
denote the set of discrete eigenvalues of finite algebraic multiplicities of
-\Delta-V_{\omega}
, repeated according to their algebraic multiplicity. Then, there are constants
M_{0},c>0
such that for all
R_{0}\geq 1
, and
M\geq M_{0}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{R_{0}^{-1/2}\leq|z_{j}|\leq h^{-1/2}}\delta(z_{j})|z_{j}|^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\frac{3(d-1)}{2\varepsilon}-3d+2+\varepsilon)_{+}}\\
&\quad\lesssim_{d,\nu,\varepsilon}(R_{0}^{\varepsilon}\|\langle x\rangle^{1/2+3\varepsilon}V\|_{\infty})^{\frac{1}{3/2-3\varepsilon}(1+(\frac{3(d-1)}{2\varepsilon}-3d+2+\varepsilon)_{+})}\end{split}

(73)

holds for all
\omega
outside a set of measure at most
\mathrm{e}^{-cM^{2}}
.
In particular, for these
\omega
, there are
c_{1}=c_{1}(R_{0},\varepsilon,d)>0
and
c_{2}=c_{2}(\varepsilon,d)>1
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{R_{0}^{-1/2}\leq|z_{j}|\leq h^{-1/2}}\delta(z_{j})\leq c_{1}\|\langle x\rangle^{1/2+3\varepsilon}V\|_{\infty}^{c_{2}}.

(74)

Note that there is
\varepsilon_{d}>0
such that
\frac{3(d-1)}{2\varepsilon}-3d+2+\varepsilon>0
if and only if
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{d}
.

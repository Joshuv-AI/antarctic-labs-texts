Introduction

Let
\displaystyle(A,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a Noetherian local ring of dimension
\displaystyle d

with an infinite residue field.
An
\displaystyle F_{1}
-good filtration,
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}

\displaystyle A=F_{0}\supseteq F_{1}\supseteq\cdots

\displaystyle i,j\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle F_{n}=A

\displaystyle n\leq 0

\displaystyle F_{n+1}=F_{1}F_{n}

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}

[

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}.

2.
Gorensteinness of
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
in small reduction numbers

In this section, we discuss the Gorenstein property of
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
of a Hilbert filtration
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
in the case of
\displaystyle r(\mathcal{F})\leq 2
.

In
[
6
]
Heinzer, Kim and Ulrich gave criteria for

\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
to be Gorenstein for an Artinian Gorenstein local ring.
In the following theorem, we extend this result for any

\displaystyle d
-dimensional Gorenstein local ring.
This plays a very important role in determining the Gorenstein
property of the associated graded ring in terms of
the Hilbert coefficients of
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\displaystyle(A,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a
\displaystyle d
-dimensional Gorenstein local ring,
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
a Hilbert filtration.
Suppose
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is Cohen-Macaulay.
Let

HS_{G(\mathcal{F})}(z)=\frac{h_{0}+h_{1}t+\cdots+h_{s}t^{s}}{(1-t)^{d}}\;\;(h_{s}\neq 0)

be the Hilbert series of
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
.
Then
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is Gorenstein if and only if
\displaystyle h_{s-i}=h_{i}
for all
\displaystyle i=0,1,\ldots,s
.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle Q
be a minimal reduction of
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
.
By
[
8
, Theorem 8.6.3]
there exists a generating set

\displaystyle x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
of
\displaystyle Q
such that
\displaystyle x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
is a superficial sequence.
Since
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is Cohen-Macaulay,

\displaystyle x_{1}^{*},\ldots x_{d}^{*}
is a
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
-regular sequence
by
[
7
, Lemma 2.1]
where
\displaystyle x_{i}^{*}=x_{i}+F_{2}

for
\displaystyle i=1,2,\ldots,d
.
Therefore
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})/(x_{1}^{*},\ldots,x_{d}^{*})\cong G(\mathcal{F}/Q)
.
Since
\displaystyle x_{1}^{*},\ldots,x_{d}^{*}
is a regular sequence in
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
,

HS_{G(\mathcal{F})/(x_{1}^{*},\ldots,x_{d}^{*})}(t)=h_{0}+h_{1}t+\cdots+h_{s}t^{s}.

Hence

HS_{G(\mathcal{F}/Q)}(t)=h_{0}+h_{1}t+\cdots+h_{s}t^{s}.

Therefore by
[
6
, Theorem 4.2]

\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F}/Q)
is Gorenstein
if and only if
\displaystyle h_{s-i}=h_{i}~\textrm{ for }i=0,1,\ldots,s
.
Now the result follows because
\displaystyle x_{1}^{*},\ldots,x_{d}^{*}
is
a regular sequence in
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
and

\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})/(x_{1}^{*},\ldots,x_{d}^{*})\cong G(\mathcal{F}/Q)
.
∎

In general for an arbitrary filtration
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
, we have
\displaystyle\nr_{Q}(\mathcal{F})\leq r_{Q}(\mathcal{F})
,
and hence
\displaystyle\nr(\mathcal{F})\leq r(\mathcal{F})
. In
[

the authors gave an example for which the inequality is
strict for the filtration
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{\overline{I^{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
In the following proposition we show that if
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is Cohen-Macaulay,
then
\displaystyle\nr(\mathcal{F})=r(\mathcal{F})
for any Hilbert filtration
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\displaystyle(A,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
\displaystyle d
.
Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a Hilbert filtration.
If
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is Cohen-Macaulay,
then
\displaystyle\nr_{Q}(\mathcal{F})=r_{Q}(\mathcal{F})
for any minimal reduction
\displaystyle Q
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
.
In particular,
\displaystyle\nr(\mathcal{F})=r(\mathcal{F}).

Proof.

We always have
\displaystyle\nr_{Q}(\mathcal{F})\leq r_{Q}(\mathcal{F})
.
Let
\displaystyle Q
be a minimal reduction of
\displaystyle F_{1}
such that
\displaystyle\mu(Q)=d
.
Since
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is Cohen-Macaulay, by
[
7
, Proposition 3.5]

\displaystyle Q\cap F_{n}=QF_{n-1}
for all
\displaystyle n\geq 1
.
Let
\displaystyle m=\nr_{Q}(\mathcal{F}).
Then
\displaystyle F_{m+1}=QF_{m}
.
Hence
\displaystyle F_{m+2}\subseteq F_{m+1}\subseteq Q
.
Therefore

F_{m+2}=F_{m+2}\cap Q=QF_{m+1}.

Continuing this successively we get
\displaystyle{F_{k+1}}={QF_{k}}
for all
\displaystyle k\geq m
.
This implies that
\displaystyle r_{Q}(\mathcal{F})\leq m=\nr_{Q}(\mathcal{F})
.
Thus
\displaystyle r_{Q}(\mathcal{F})=\nr_{Q}(\mathcal{F})
.
∎

Our goal is to use Theorem
2.1

to obtain criteria for
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
to be Gorenstein in terms of the Hilbert coefficients. For this, first of all we should know whether
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is Cohen-Macaulay. There is a huge research in the literature on obtaining criteria for
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})

to be Cohen-Macaulay in terms of numerical invariants such as Hilbert coefficients and reduction number. Below we present a well-known criteria for
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
to be Cohen-Macaulay in terms of reduction number. We remark that the condition in
\displaystyle(b)
is not restrictive. For instance, the filtrations
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muI^{n}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
satisfy this criterion.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\displaystyle(A,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a
\displaystyle d
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring and
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
a Hilbert filtration. Then
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is Cohen-Macaulay in each of the following cases:

(a)

\displaystyle r(\mathcal{F})=1;

(b)

there exists a minimal reduction
\displaystyle Q
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
such that
\displaystyle r_{Q}(\mathcal{F})=2
and
\displaystyle F_{2}\cap Q=QF_{1}
.

Proof.

The proof follows by
[
7
, Proposition 3.5]
.
∎

Now in order to provide conditions for
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
to be Gorenstein, we need to compute the Hilbert series of
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
.
The computation of the Hilbert series of
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is not an easy task in general.
In the following proposition we compute the Hilbert series of
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})

in the case
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
satisfies the conditions
\displaystyle(a),

\displaystyle(b)
of
Lemma
2.3
, and under certain additional assumptions
when
\displaystyle r(\mathcal{F})\geq 3.

For this, we recall the following definition:
For a Hilbert filtration
\displaystyle\mathcal{F},
the
postulation number

of
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
is given by

n(\mathcal{F}):=\sup\{n\in\mathbb{Z}|H_{\mathcal{F}}(n)\neq P_{\mathcal{F}}(n)\}.

If
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muI^{n}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},
then
\displaystyle n(\mathcal{F})
is denoted by
\displaystyle\overline{n}(I)
.
We also recall the result
[
15
, Corollary 3.8]
that will be used frequently in the remainder of the paper.
Let
\displaystyle(A,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a
\displaystyle d
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring with infinite residue field and
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{F_{n}\}_{n\mathbb{Z}}
a Hilbert filtration of ideals where
\displaystyle F_{1}
is an
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary ideal.
Suppose
\displaystyle\depth G(\mathcal{F})\geq d-1
. Then

(2.1)

r({\mathcal{F}})=n(\mathcal{F})+d.

We can use the following lemma in order to reduce several problems to the case of dimension one.
Recall that if
\displaystyle f:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{N}
is a numerical function,
then
\displaystyle\Delta(f):\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}
is a numerical function defined by
\displaystyle\Delta(f)(n):=f(n)-f(n-1)
.

Lemma 2.4

(cf.
[
15
, Lemma 3.14]
)
.

Let
\displaystyle(A,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
\displaystyle d\geq 1

and
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
a Hilbert filtration.
Let
\displaystyle k
be an integer with
\displaystyle 1\leq k\leq d
.
Take a sequence
\displaystyle x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}\in F_{1}
such that

(i)

\displaystyle Q=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})
is a minimal reduction of
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
.

(ii)

\displaystyle\underline{x}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
is a superficial sequence in
\displaystyle F_{1}
.

If
\displaystyle\depth G(\mathcal{F})\geq k
, then

(a)

\displaystyle x_{1}^{*},\ldots,x_{k}^{*}
forms a
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
-regular sequence.

(b)

If we put
\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{F}}:=\mathcal{F}/(\underline{x}):=\{F_{n}+(\underline{x})/(\underline{x})\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, then

P_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}(n)=\Delta^{k}(P_{\mathcal{F}}(n))=\sum_{i=0}^{d-k}e_{i}(\mathcal{F})(-1)^{i}{n+d-k-1-i\choose d-k-i}.

In particular,
\displaystyle e_{i}(\overline{\mathcal{F}})=e_{i}(\mathcal{F})
for each
\displaystyle i=0,1,\ldots,d-k
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\displaystyle(A,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring
of dimension
\displaystyle d\geq 1
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
a Hilbert filtration.
Put
\displaystyle\lambda=\ell(A/F_{1})
,
\displaystyle e_{i}=e_{i}(\mathcal{F})
for
\displaystyle i\geq 0
.

(a)

If
\displaystyle r(\mathcal{F})=1
, then
\displaystyle e_{1}=e_{0}-\lambda
,
\displaystyle e_{i}\geq 0
for all
\displaystyle i\geq 2
and

HS_{G(\mathcal{F})}(t)=\frac{\lambda+(e_{0}-\lambda)t}{(1-t)^{d}}=\frac{(e_{0}-e_{1})+e_{1}t}{(1-t)^{d}}.

(b)

Suppose there exists a minimal reduction
\displaystyle Q
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
such that
\displaystyle r_{Q}(\mathcal{F})=2
and
\displaystyle F_{2}\cap Q=QF_{1}
.
Then
\displaystyle e_{i}=0
for
\displaystyle i\geq 3
and

HS_{G(\mathcal{F})}(t)=\frac{\lambda+(2e_{0}-e_{1}-2\lambda)t+(\lambda-e_{0}+e_{1})t^{2}}{(1-t)^{d}}.

Also, if
\displaystyle d\geq 2
, then
\displaystyle e_{2}=\lambda-e_{0}+e_{1}
and

HS_{G(\mathcal{F})}(t)=\frac{(e_{0}-e_{1}+e_{2})+(e_{1}-2e_{2})t+e_{2}t^{2}}{(1-t)^{d}}.

Proof.

First suppose
\displaystyle d=1
.
(a) By Lemma
2.3

\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is Cohen-Macaulay.
Hence using Equation (
2.1
) and Proposition
2.2

we get
\displaystyle n(\mathcal{F})=1-1=0
. Hence for all
\displaystyle n\geq 1
,
\displaystyle H_{\mathcal{F}}(n)=P_{\mathcal{F}}(n)=e_{0}n-e_{1}
.
Taking
\displaystyle n=1
, we obtain
\displaystyle\lambda=\ell(A/F_{1})=e_{0}-e_{1}
.
Also,

\displaystyle\ell(F_{n}/F_{n+1})=\ell(A/F_{n+1})-\ell(A/F_{n})=P_{\mathcal{F}}(n+1)-P_{\mathcal{F}}(n)=e_{0}

for all
\displaystyle n\geq 1
.
Therefore

HS_{G(\mathcal{F})}(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\ell(F_{n}/F_{n+1})t^{n}=\lambda+\sum_{n\geq 1}e_{0}t^{n}=\frac{\lambda+(e_{0}-\lambda)t}{(1-t)}.

(b) By Lemma
2.3

\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is Cohen-Macaulay.
Hence using Equation (
2.1
)
\displaystyle n(\mathcal{F})=2-1=1
.
In particular,
\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{F}}(n)=H_{\mathcal{F}}(n)
for all
\displaystyle n\geq 2
.
Taking
\displaystyle n=2
, we obtain
\displaystyle\ell(A/F_{2})=2e_{0}-e_{1}
.
Also,
\displaystyle\ell(F_{n}/F_{n+1})=P_{\mathcal{F}}(n+1)-P_{\mathcal{F}}(n)=e_{0}
for all
\displaystyle n\geq 2
.
Thus the Hilbert series of
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is given by

\begin{split}HS_{G(\mathcal{F})}(t)&=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\ell(F_{n}/F_{n+1})t^{n}\\
&=\ell(A/F_{1})+(\ell(A/F_{2})-\ell(A/F_{1}))t+\sum_{n\geq 2}\ell(F_{n}/F_{n+1})t^{n}\\
&=\lambda+(2e_{0}-e_{1}-\lambda)t+\sum_{n\geq 2}e_{0}t^{n}\\
&=\frac{\lambda+(2e_{0}-e_{1}-2\lambda)t+(\lambda-e_{0}+e_{1})t^{2}}{1-t}.\end{split}

Next suppose that
\displaystyle d\geq 2
.
By Lemma
2.3

\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is Cohen-Macaulay.
Then one can take a sequence
\displaystyle x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}
which satisfies
the condition of Lemma
2.4
for
\displaystyle k=d-1
.
If we put
\displaystyle\overline{A}=A/(\underline{x})
, then
\displaystyle\overline{A}
is a one-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring and
\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{F}}
is
a Hilbert filtration.
Also,
\displaystyle\ell(\overline{A}/\overline{F_{1}})=\lambda
,

\displaystyle e_{i}(\overline{\mathcal{F}})=e_{i}
for
\displaystyle i=0,1
, and
\displaystyle r(\overline{\mathcal{F}})=r(\mathcal{F})
.
Indeed, the last assertion follows from the following fact:

(2.2)

\dfrac{\overline{F_{n+1}}}{x_{d}\overline{F_{n}}}=\dfrac{F_{n+1}+\underline{x}A}{QF_{n}+\underline{x}A}\cong\dfrac{F_{n+1}}{(QF_{n}+\underline{x}A)\cap F_{n+1}}=\dfrac{F_{n+1}}{QF_{n}+\underline{x}F_{n}}=\dfrac{F_{n+1}}{QF_{n}}.

Therefore one can reduce the proof to the case of dimension one.

Moreover, if
\displaystyle d\geq 2
, then since
\displaystyle n(\mathcal{F})=2-d\leq 0
,
we have
\displaystyle H_{\mathcal{F}}(1)=P_{\mathcal{F}}(1)
and thus
\displaystyle\lambda=e_{0}-e_{1}+e_{2}
.
Also,
\displaystyle e_{i}(\mathcal{F})=0
for
\displaystyle i\geq 3
.
∎

Using Proposition
2.5
we provide criteria for
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
to be Gorenstein in terms of the Hilbert coefficients.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\displaystyle(A,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a Gorenstein local ring of dimension
\displaystyle d\geq 1
. Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a Hilbert filtration.

(a)

Let
\displaystyle r(\mathcal{F})=1
. Then
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is Gorenstein if and only if
\displaystyle e_{0}(\mathcal{F})=2e_{1}(\mathcal{F})
.

(b)

Suppose there exists a minimal reduction
\displaystyle Q
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
such that
\displaystyle r_{Q}(\mathcal{F})=2
and
\displaystyle F_{2}\cap Q=QF_{1}
.
Then
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is Gorenstein if and only if
\displaystyle e_{0}(\mathcal{F})=e_{1}(\mathcal{F})
.

Proof.

Put
\displaystyle\lambda=\ell(A/F_{1})
and
\displaystyle e_{i}=e_{i}(\mathcal{F})
for all
\displaystyle i\geq 0
.
In both the cases, by Lemma
2.3

\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is Cohen-Macaulay.

(a)
By Proposition
2.5
, we have

HS_{G(\mathcal{F})}(t)=\frac{(e_{0}-e_{1})+e_{1}t}{(1-t)^{d}}.

Note that
\displaystyle e_{1}\neq 0
. Using Theorem
2.1
, we obtain
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is Gorenstein if and only if
\displaystyle e_{0}=2e_{1}
.

(b)
By Proposition
2.5
, we have

HS_{G(\mathcal{F})}(t)=\frac{\lambda+(2e_{0}-e_{1}-2\lambda)t+(\lambda-e_{0}+e_{1})t^{2}}{(1-t)^{d}}.

By Theorem
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is Gorenstein if and only if
\displaystyle e_{0}=e_{1}
.
∎

, Theorem 2.1]
. Moreover, we extend it for any
\displaystyle d
-dimensional analytically unramified Gorenstein local ring.

.

3.
A Bound on relative reduction number

In this section we obtain an upper bound for
the relative reduction number

\displaystyle\nr(\mathcal{F})
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
when the
\displaystyle\depth
of
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is almost maximal.

In general, the reduction number
\displaystyle r_{Q}(\mathcal{F})
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}

and the relative reduction number
\displaystyle\nr_{Q}(\mathcal{F})
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
are
dependent on the minimal reduction
\displaystyle Q
.
It is well-known that if
\displaystyle\depth{G(\mathcal{F})}\geq d-1
,
then
\displaystyle r_{Q}(\mathcal{F})
is independent of the minimal reduction
\displaystyle Q
.
In the following proposition we show that
\displaystyle\nr_{Q}(\mathcal{F})
is also
independent of
\displaystyle Q
if
\displaystyle\depth{G(\mathcal{F})}\geq d-1
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\displaystyle(A,m)
be a
\displaystyle d
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring,
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
a Hilbert filtration.
If
\displaystyle\depth{G(\mathcal{F})}\geq d-1
, then
\displaystyle\nr_{Q}(\mathcal{F})
is independent
of any minimal reduction
\displaystyle Q
.

Proof.

Using
[
15
, Theorem 3.6]
for all
\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{Z},
we have

\Delta^{d}(P_{\mathcal{F}}(n)-H_{\mathcal{F}}(n))=\ell(F_{n+d}/QF_{n+d-1}).

Since the expression on the left-hand side is independent of
\displaystyle Q
, we get
\displaystyle\ell(F_{n+d}/QF_{n+d-1})
is independent of
\displaystyle Q
. Hence it is easy to verify that
\displaystyle\nr_{Q}(\mathcal{F})
is independent of
\displaystyle Q.

∎

If
\displaystyle(A,{\mathfrak{m}})
is a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension two and
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{\overline{I^{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},
then
\displaystyle\depth G(\mathcal{F})\geq 1
by
[
15
, Proposition 3.25]
. Hence by Proposition
3.1

\displaystyle{\nr}_{Q}(I)
is independent of
\displaystyle Q
and thus
\displaystyle{\nr}(I)={\nr}_{Q}(I).
This is a reason that the authors use the notation
\displaystyle{nr}(I)
(without suffix) for the relative normal reduction number in
[
16
]
.

In the next theorem we extend a result by Okuma, Rossi, Watanabe, and Yoshida. In
[
16
, Theorem 2.7]
they showed that if
\displaystyle(A,{\mathfrak{m}})
is an excellent two-dimensional normal local domain containing an algebraically closed field
\displaystyle A/{\mathfrak{m}},
then for an
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary ideal
\displaystyle I

\nr(I)\leq{\overline{e}_{1}}(I)-\overline{e}_{0}(I)+\ell(A/\overline{I})+1.

They also provided a criterion for the equality to hold.
In the following theorem we prove an analogue bound for
any Hilbert filtration in a
\displaystyle d
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay
local ring, provided
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
has almost maximal depth.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\displaystyle(A,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a
\displaystyle d
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring,
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
a Hilbert filtration.
Suppose
\displaystyle\depth{G(\mathcal{F})}\geq d-1
. Then

(a)

\displaystyle\nr(\mathcal{F})\leq{e_{1}}(\mathcal{F})-e_{0}(\mathcal{F})+\ell(A/F_{1})+1
.

(b)

The equality holds in (1)
if and only if
\displaystyle\nr(\mathcal{F})=r(\mathcal{F})
and

\displaystyle\ell(F_{n}/QF_{n-1})=1

for all
\displaystyle 2\leq n\leq\nr(\mathcal{F})
for any minimal reduction
\displaystyle Q
of

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
.

When this is the case,
\displaystyle e_{i}(\mathcal{F})=\binom{\nr(\mathcal{F})}{i}
for
\displaystyle 2\leq i\leq d
.

A Hilbert filtration
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
is said to have
maximal relative reduction number
(or,
\displaystyle\nr(\mathcal{F})
is
maximal
) if
\displaystyle\nr(\mathcal{F})=e_{1}(\mathcal{F})-e_{0}(\mathcal{F})+\ell(A/F_{1})+1
.

Proof.

(a) Using
[
7
, Proposition 4.6]
, for any minimal reduction
\displaystyle Q
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
we have,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle e_{1}({\mathcal{F}})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}^{r(\mathcal{F})}\ell(F_{n}/QF_{n-1})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ell(F_{1}/Q)+\sum_{n\geq 2}^{r(\mathcal{F})}\ell(F_{n}/QF_{n-1})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle e_{0}(\mathcal{F})-\ell(A/F_{1})+\sum_{n\geq 2}^{r(\mathcal{F})}\ell(F_{n}/QF_{n-1}){\mbox{\quad(as $\displaystyle e_{0}(\mathcal{F})=\ell(A/Q))$}}

We observe that
\displaystyle\ell(F_{n}/QF_{n-1})\geq 1
for
\displaystyle 2\leq n\leq\nr(\mathcal{F})
.
Therefore by (
3.1
)

(3.2)

\nr(\mathcal{F})-1\leq\sum_{n\geq 2}^{r(\mathcal{F})}\ell(F_{n}/QF_{n-1})=e_{1}(\mathcal{F})-e_{0}(\mathcal{F})+\ell(A/F_{1}),

and hence
\displaystyle\nr(\mathcal{F})\leq e_{1}(\mathcal{F})-e_{0}(\mathcal{F})+\ell(A/F_{1})+1
.

(b) Suppose
\displaystyle\nr(\mathcal{F})=e_{1}(\mathcal{F})-e_{0}(\mathcal{F})+\ell(A/F_{1})+1
.
Then by (
3.2
)
\displaystyle\ell(F_{n}/QF_{n-1})=0
for

\displaystyle n\geq\nr_{Q}(\mathcal{F})+1
and
\displaystyle\ell(F_{n}/QF_{n-1})=1

for
\displaystyle 2\leq n\leq\nr_{Q}(\mathcal{F})
.
Therefore
\displaystyle\nr_{Q}(\mathcal{F})=r_{Q}(\mathcal{F})
,
which yields
\displaystyle\nr(\mathcal{F})=r(\mathcal{F})
.
The converse is also true.

Again using
[
7
, Proposition 4.6]
we have, for
\displaystyle i\geq 2
,

e_{i}(\mathcal{F})=\sum_{n\geq i}^{\nr(\mathcal{F})}\ell(F_{n}/QF_{n-1})\binom{n-1}{i-1}=\sum_{n\geq i}^{\nr(\mathcal{F})}\binom{n-1}{i-1}=\binom{\nr(\mathcal{F})}{i}.

∎

Using Theorem
3.2
for the filtration
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muI^{n}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
in dimension two, we recover and extend the result
[
16
, Theorem 2.7]
by Okuma, Rossi, Watanabe and Yoshida.

Corollary 3.3
.

[
16
, Theorem 2.7]

Let
\displaystyle(A,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a two-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay analytically unramified local ring, and
\displaystyle I
an
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary ideal.
Let
\displaystyle Q
a minimal reduction of
\displaystyle I
.
Then

{\nr}(I)\leq\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mue\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\ell(A/\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muI\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)+1.

Moreover, the equality holds if and only if

\displaystyle\ell(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muI^{n}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu/Q\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muI^{n-1}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)=1
for all
\displaystyle 2\leq n\leq{\nr}(I)
.
In this case,
\displaystyle{\nr}(I)=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mur\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(I)
.
Also,
\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mue\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{2}(I)=\binom{{\nr}(I)}{2}
.

Proof.

Since
\displaystyle\depth{\overline{G}(I)}\geq 1

by
[
15
, Proposition 3.25]
,
the result follows from Theorem
3.2
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

If
\displaystyle r(\mathcal{F})=1
, then
\displaystyle\nr(\mathcal{F})=r(\mathcal{F})
is maximal.

Suppose
\displaystyle d\geq 2
and there exists a minimal reduction

\displaystyle Q
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
such that
\displaystyle r_{Q}(\mathcal{F})=2
and
\displaystyle F_{2}\cap Q=QF_{1}
. Then

\displaystyle\nr(\mathcal{F})
is maximal if and only if
\displaystyle e_{2}(\mathcal{F})=1
.

Now we compute the Hilbert series of
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
when
\displaystyle\nr(\mathcal{F})
is maximal.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\displaystyle(A,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
\displaystyle d\geq 1
, and
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{{F_{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
a Hilbert filtration.
Suppose
\displaystyle\depth G(\mathcal{F})\geq d-1
, and
\displaystyle\nr(\mathcal{F})=r\geq 2
is maximal.
Put
\displaystyle\lambda=\ell(A/F_{1})
and
\displaystyle e_{0}=e_{0}(\mathcal{F})
.
Then

HS_{{G}(\mathcal{F})}(t)=\frac{\lambda+(e_{0}-\lambda-1)t+t^{r}}{(1-t)^{d}}.

Proof.

First suppose
\displaystyle d=1
.
Let
\displaystyle Q=(x)
a minimal reduction of
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}
.
By Theorem
3.2
we have
\displaystyle{r}(\mathcal{F})=\nr(\mathcal{F})
and
\displaystyle\ell({F_{n}}/Q{F_{n-1}})=1
for all
\displaystyle 2\leq n\leq{r}(\mathcal{F})
.
Observe that for
\displaystyle 1\leq n\leq r-1
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ell\left({F_{n}}/{F_{n+1}}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ell(A/F_{n+1})-\ell(A/F_{n})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ell\left(A/x{F_{n}}\right)-\ell\left({F_{n+1}}/x{F_{n}}\right)-\ell\left(A/{F_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ell\left(A/xA\right)+\ell\left(A/{F_{n}}\right)-\ell\left({F_{n+1}}/x{F_{n}}\right)-\ell\left(A/{F_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle e_{0}-1.

Therefore

\displaystyle\displaystyle HS_{{G}(\mathcal{F})}(t)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}^{\infty}\ell({F_{n}}/{F_{n+1}})t^{n}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\lambda+\sum_{n=1}^{r-1}(e_{0}-1)t^{n}+\sum_{n\geq r}e_{0}t^{n}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\lambda+(e_{0}-1)\dfrac{t(1-t^{r-1})}{1-t}+\dfrac{e_{0}t^{r}}{1-t}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\dfrac{\lambda+(e_{0}-\lambda-1)t+t^{r}}{1-t}.

Next suppose
\displaystyle d\geq 2
.
We take a sequence
\displaystyle x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}
in
\displaystyle F_{1}
which satisfies
the condition of Lemma
2.4
.
Put
\displaystyle Q=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})A
,
\displaystyle\underline{x}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-1}
,

\displaystyle\overline{A}=A/(\underline{x})A
, and
\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{F}}=\mathcal{F}/(\underline{x})
.
Then
\displaystyle\nr(\overline{\mathcal{F}})
is also a maximal reduction number.
Hence it follows from the argument as above that

HS_{G(\overline{F})}(t)=\dfrac{\lambda+(e_{0}-\lambda-1)t+t^{r}}{1-t}

and thus

HS_{G(\mathcal{F})}(t)=\dfrac{HS_{G(\overline{F})}(t)}{(1-t)^{d-1}}=\dfrac{\lambda+(e_{0}-\lambda-1)t+t^{r}}{(1-t)^{d}}.

∎

As a consequence, we provide criteria for
\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
to be Gorenstein
in terms of Hilbert coefficients in the case where
\displaystyle\nr(\mathcal{F})
is maximal.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\displaystyle(A,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a Gorenstein local ring of dimension
\displaystyle d\geq 1
,
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\{{F_{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
a Hilbert filtration.
Suppose that
\displaystyle\nr(\mathcal{F})=r\geq 2
is maximal and

\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is Cohen-Macaulay.
Then

(a)

When
\displaystyle r=2
,

\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is Gorenstein if and only if
\displaystyle F_{1}={\mathfrak{m}}
.

(b)

When
\displaystyle r\geq 3
,

\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is Gorenstein if and only if
\displaystyle F_{1}={\mathfrak{m}}
and
\displaystyle e_{0}({\mathfrak{m}})=2
.

Proof.

(a) By Theorem
3.5
,
we have

HS_{G(\mathcal{F})}(t)=\dfrac{\lambda+(e_{0}-\lambda-1)t+t^{2}}{(1-t)^{d}}.

Thus, by Theorem
2.1
,

\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is Gorenstein if and only if
\displaystyle\lambda=1
, that is,

\displaystyle F_{1}={\mathfrak{m}}
.

(b) By Theorem
3.5
,
we have

HS_{G(\mathcal{F})}(t)=\dfrac{\lambda+(e_{0}-\lambda-1)t+t^{r}}{(1-t)^{d}}.

Thus, by Theorem
2.1
,

\displaystyle G(\mathcal{F})
is Gorenstein if and only if
\displaystyle\lambda=1
and
\displaystyle e_{0}-\lambda-1=0
, that is,

\displaystyle F_{1}={\mathfrak{m}}
and
\displaystyle e_{0}({\mathfrak{m}})=2
.
∎

for the filtration

Definition 4.1

5.
Gorensteinness of
\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muG\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu({\mathfrak{m}})
in Zariski hypersurface rings

Consider the following setup in the rest of the paper.
Let
\displaystyle K
be an algebraically closed field, and
let
\displaystyle m\geq 1
,
\displaystyle 2\leq a\leq b
be integers,
and put
\displaystyle S=K[[x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]]
be a formal power series ring
with
\displaystyle(m+1)
-variables over
\displaystyle K
.
Then
\displaystyle S
is a complete regular local ring with
the unique maximal ideal
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}_{S}=(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})
.
Put
\displaystyle\underline{y}=y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}
.
Let
\displaystyle g
,
\displaystyle f
be polynomials such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle g(\underline{y})\in(\underline{y})^{b}\setminus(\underline{y})^{b+1},

\displaystyle\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle f(x,\underline{y})=x^{a}-g(\underline{y}).

Then a hypersurface
\displaystyle A=S/(f)

is called “
Zariski-type hypersurface
” if
\displaystyle a\neq 0
in
\displaystyle K
.
Let
\displaystyle Q=(\underline{y})A
.
Note that
\displaystyle Q
is a minimal reduction of
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}={\mathfrak{m}}_{S}A
.
Indeed,
\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}^{a}=Q{\mathfrak{m}}^{a-1}
.
In this section, we compute
\displaystyle\br({\mathfrak{m}})
and prove the Cohen-Macaulayness of
\displaystyle\overline{G}({\mathfrak{m}})
.
In order to do that, we generalize several results for Brieskorn hypersurfaces in
[
19
, Section 3]
to that of Zariski-type hypersurfaces.
We fix the following notation for the rest of the paper:

d=\gcd(a,b),\quad a^{\prime}=\frac{a}{d},\quad b^{\prime}=\frac{b}{d}\quad\text{and}\quad n_{k}=\lfloor\frac{kb}{a}\rfloor\quad\text{for $\displaystyle k=1,2,\ldots,a-1$.}

For all
\displaystyle n\geq 1
, we define the following two ideals:

\displaystyle\displaystyle J_{n}

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\displaystyle Q^{n}+xQ^{n-n_{1}}+x^{2}Q^{n-n_{2}}+\cdots+x^{a-1}Q^{n-n_{a-1}},

\displaystyle\displaystyle I_{n}

\displaystyle\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(x^{k}y_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots y_{m}^{i_{m}}\,\big|\,kb^{\prime}+(i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m})a^{\prime}\geq n,k,i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\right)A.

Proposition 5.1
.

\displaystyle J_{n}=I_{na^{\prime}}
for every
\displaystyle n\geq 1
.

Proof.

First we prove
\displaystyle J_{n}\subset I_{na^{\prime}}
.
It is enough to show
\displaystyle x^{k}Q^{n-n_{k}}\subset I_{na^{\prime}}
for every
\displaystyle k=1,2,\ldots,a-1
.
For each
\displaystyle k
with
\displaystyle 1\leq k\leq a-1
, if
\displaystyle i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m}\geq n-n_{k}
,
then

kb^{\prime}+(i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m})a^{\prime}\geq kb^{\prime}+(n-n_{k})a^{\prime}\geq kb^{\prime}+\big(n-\frac{kb^{\prime}}{a^{\prime}}\big)a^{\prime}=na^{\prime},

as required. Next we prove the converse.
If
\displaystyle x^{k}y_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots y_{m}^{i_{m}}\in I_{na^{\prime}}
, then

\displaystyle kb^{\prime}+(i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m})a^{\prime}\geq na^{\prime}
.
Thus

n_{k}=\lfloor\frac{kb^{\prime}}{a^{\prime}}\rfloor\geq n-(i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m}),\;\text{that is,}\;i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m}\geq n-n_{k}.

Hence
\displaystyle x^{k}y_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots y_{m}^{i_{m}}\in x^{k}Q^{n-n_{k}}
.
This yields
\displaystyle I_{na^{\prime}}\subset J_{n}
.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Under the notation above,

(a)

For every
\displaystyle k,n\geq 1
,
\displaystyle x^{k}\in\overline{Q^{n}}

(b)

\displaystyle n\geq 2
is any integer.

Proof.

Note that
\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mur\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu({\mathfrak{m}})=\lfloor\frac{(a-1)b}{a}\rfloor\geq a-1
by Theorem
5.8
.

(a)
\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mur\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu({\mathfrak{m}})=1
implies
\displaystyle a=2
by the above remark.
Thus one can easily see that
\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mur\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu({\mathfrak{m}})=1
if
\displaystyle(a,b)=(2,2)
,
\displaystyle(2,3)
.

(b)
\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mur\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu({\mathfrak{m}})=2

\displaystyle a=2,3
by the above remark.
When
\displaystyle a=2

\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mur\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu({\mathfrak{m}})=\lfloor\frac{b}{2}\rfloor=2

\displaystyle b=4,5
.
When
\displaystyle a=3

\displaystyle\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mur\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu({\mathfrak{m}})=\lfloor\frac{2b}{3}\rfloor=2

\displaystyle b=3,4
.

(c) Suppose
\displaystyle r=\nr(m)
is maximal. We may assume
\displaystyle r\geq 2
.
By Theorem
3.2
,
we have
\displaystyle\ell(\overline{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}}/Q\overline{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n-1}})=1

for all
\displaystyle n=2,\ldots,r
.
On the other hand, using Theorem
5.8
, we can
show that
\displaystyle\ell(\overline{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}}/Q\overline{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n-1}}))=\max\{a-\lceil\frac{an}{b}\rceil,0\}

for all

Thus

This means
\displaystyle\lceil\frac{2a}{b}\rceil=a-1

It is not so difficult to chech that the converse is also true.

6.
Normal Tangent cones of maximal embedding dimension

Let
\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{\overline{G}}
denote the unique graded maximal ideal of

\displaystyle\overline{G}=\overline{G}({\mathfrak{m}})
.
Then
\displaystyle\emb(\overline{G})
, the
embedding dimension
of
\displaystyle\overline{G}
,
is the number of minimal system of generators of
\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{\overline{G}}
.
Since
\displaystyle\overline{G}
is Cohen-Macaulay,

(6.1)

\emb(\overline{G})\leq e_{0}(\overline{G})+\dim\overline{G}-1=a+m-1

If equality holds in Eq.(
6.1
), then
\displaystyle\overline{G}
is said to
have
maximal embedding dimension
(in the sense of Sally).
Note that
\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{\overline{G}}
is generated by

\displaystyle\{x^{k}t^{n_{k}}\}_{k=1,2,\ldots,a-1}
, and
\displaystyle y_{1}t,y_{2}t,\ldots,y_{m}t
.

We can characterize those
\displaystyle\overline{G}
in terms of
\displaystyle a
and
\displaystyle b
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\displaystyle A=S/(x^{a}-g(\underline{y}))
be a Zariski hypersurface with

\displaystyle b=\ord_{(\underline{y})}(g)\geq a
.
Then
\displaystyle\overline{G}({\mathfrak{m}})
has maximal embedding dimension
if and only if
one of the following cases occurs
\displaystyle:

(a)

\displaystyle a=2
.

(b)

\displaystyle a\geq 3
, and
\displaystyle b\equiv a-1\pmod{a}
.

When this is the case,

\displaystyle\overline{G}({\mathfrak{m}})
is Gorenstein if and only if
\displaystyle a=2
.

Proof.

In the case of
\displaystyle a=2
, since
\displaystyle\overline{G}
is a hypersurface of
\displaystyle e_{0}(\overline{G})=2
, it is a Gorenstein ring having maximal embedding dimension. So we may assume that
\displaystyle a\geq 3
.

\displaystyle\rm{(b)}\Longrightarrow\rm{(a)}:

We may compute
\displaystyle n_{k}
as follows:

n_{k}=\lfloor\frac{k(n_{1}a+a-1)}{a}\rfloor=kn_{1}+k+\lfloor\frac{-k}{a}\rfloor=kn_{1}+k-\lceil\frac{k}{a}\rceil=kn_{1}+k-1

for every
\displaystyle k=1,2,\ldots,a-1
.
Then a sequence

xt^{n_{1}},\,x^{2}t^{2n_{1}+1},\,x^{3}t^{3n_{1}+2},\,\ldots,\,x^{a-1}t^{(a-1)n_{1}+a-2},\;y_{1}t,\ldots,y_{m}t

forms a minimal system of generators of
\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{\overline{G}}
, we have

\emb(\overline{G})=a+m-1=e_{0}(\overline{G})+\dim\overline{G}-1.

\displaystyle\rm{(a)}\Longrightarrow\rm{(b)}:

In order to complete the proof, we prove the following two lemmata.

Lemma 6.2
.

If
\displaystyle d>1
and
\displaystyle a\geq 3
, then
\displaystyle\overline{G}
does not have maximal embedding dimension.

Proof.

We note that
\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{\overline{G}}=(\{x^{k}t^{n_{k}}\}_{k=1,2,\ldots,a-1},y_{1}t,\ldots,y_{m}t)
.
For every
\displaystyle s

\displaystyle(1\leq s\leq d)
,
\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle(1\leq\delta\leq a^{\prime}-1)
,
we have

n_{sa^{\prime}+\delta}=\lfloor\frac{(sa^{\prime}+\delta)b}{a}\rfloor=\lfloor\frac{(sa^{\prime}+\delta)b^{\prime}}{a^{\prime}}\rfloor=sb^{\prime}+\lfloor\frac{\delta b^{\prime}}{a^{\prime}}\rfloor=sn_{a^{\prime}}+n_{\delta},

and hence

x^{sa^{\prime}+\delta}t^{n_{sa^{\prime}+\delta}}=x^{sa^{\prime}+\delta}t^{sn_{a^{\prime}}+n_{\delta}}=(x^{a^{\prime}}t^{n_{a^{\prime}}})^{s}\cdot(x^{\delta}t^{n_{\delta}})\in\mathfrak{M}_{\overline{G}}^{2}.

Moreover, if
\displaystyle s\geq 2
, then

x^{sa^{\prime}}t^{n_{sa^{\prime}}}=x^{sa^{\prime}}t^{sn_{a^{\prime}}}=(x^{a^{\prime}}t^{n_{a^{\prime}}})^{s}\in\mathfrak{M}_{\overline{G}}^{2}.

Hence
\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{\overline{G}}=(\{x^{k}t^{n_{k}}\}_{k=1,2,\ldots,a^{\prime}},y_{1}t,\ldots,y_{m}t)
.
Since
\displaystyle a\geq 3
and
\displaystyle d\geq 2
, we obtain that

\emb(\overline{G})\leq a^{\prime}+m\leq\frac{a}{2}+m<(a-1)+m=a+m-1,

as required.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

If
\displaystyle d=1
and
\displaystyle 1\leq\delta\leq a-2
, then
\displaystyle\overline{G}
does not have maximal embedding dimension.

Proof.

For each
\displaystyle k

\displaystyle(1\leq k\leq a-1)
, if we put
\displaystyle m_{k}=n_{k}-kn_{1}
, we have

m_{k}=n_{k}-kn_{1}=\lfloor\frac{kb}{a}\rfloor-kn_{1}=\lfloor\frac{k(n_{1}a+\delta)}{a}\rfloor-kn_{1}=\lfloor\frac{k\delta}{a}\rfloor.

Since
\displaystyle 1\leq\delta\leq a-2
and
\displaystyle a\geq 3
, we get

m_{a-1}=\lfloor\frac{(a-1)\delta}{a}\rfloor\leq\lfloor\frac{(a-1)(a-2)}{a}\rfloor=a-3+\lfloor\frac{2}{a}\rfloor=a-3.

Then since
\displaystyle 0\leq m_{1}\leq m_{2}\leq\cdots\leq m_{a-1}\leq a-3
,
there exists an integer
\displaystyle i
with
\displaystyle 2\leq i\leq a-1
such that

\displaystyle m_{i-1}=m_{i}
by Pigeonhole principle.
Since
\displaystyle n_{1}+n_{i-1}=n_{1}+\left\{(i-1)n_{1}+m_{i-1}\right\}=in_{1}+m_{i}=n_{i}
,
we get

x^{i}t^{n_{i}}=x^{i}t^{n_{1}+n_{i-1}}=(xt^{n_{1}})(x^{i-1}t^{n_{i-1}})\in\mathfrak{M}_{\overline{G}}^{2}.

This implies
\displaystyle\emb(\overline{G})\leq a+m-2<e_{0}(\overline{G})+\dim\overline{G}-1
,
that is,
\displaystyle\overline{G}
does not have maximal embedding dimension.
∎

By the lemmata as above, we complete the proof of Proposition
6.1
.
∎

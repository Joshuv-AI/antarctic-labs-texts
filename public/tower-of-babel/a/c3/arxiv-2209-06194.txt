Nonreciprocal devices based on voltage-tunable junctions
Catherine Leroux, Adrian Parra-Rodriguez, Ross Shillito, Agustin Di Paolo, William D. Oliver, Charles M. Marcus, Morten Kjaergaard, András Gyenis, Alexandre Blais

Abstract
We propose to couple the flux degree of freedom of one mode with the charge degree of freedom of a second mode in a hybrid superconducting-semiconducting architecture. Nonreciprocity can arise in this architecture in the presence of external static magnetic fields alone. We leverage this property to engineer a passive on-chip gyrator, the fundamental two-port nonreciprocal device which can be used to build other nonreciprocal devices such as circulators. We analytically and numerically investigate how the nonlinearity of the interaction, circuit disorder and parasitic couplings affect the scattering response of the gyrator.

III
Gyrator design

The simplest and most fundamental nonreciprocal device based on the flux-charge coupling of
Eq.

4
is the gyrator. An ideal gyrator is characterized by the scattering matrix

\bm{\mathcal{S}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},

(6)

which relates the amplitude of the incoming (
\bm{a}
) and outgoing (
\bm{b}
) fields, at each port of the device via
\bm{b}=\bm{S}\cdot\bm{a}
.
The circuit Lagrangian of an ideal gyrator takes the form of
Parra-Rodriguez et al. 2019
;
Rymarz et al. 2021

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gyr}}=\frac{G}{2}\left(\dot{\Phi}_{2}\Phi_{1}-\dot{\Phi}_{1}\Phi_{2}\right),

(7)

where
G
is the conductance of the gyrator,
\Phi_{1(2)}
is the branch flux and
\dot{\Phi}_{1(2)}
is the voltage at port 1(2).

To realize
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gyr}}
using the FENNEC interaction, we consider the lumped-element circuit of
Fig.

2
comprising two identical internal modes
1
(blue) and
2
(green). Each mode contains a symmetric SQUID loop of semiconducting junctions biased at half quantum flux. The FENNEC interaction is realized by capacitively coupling each mode to the voltage port of the other mode’s voltage-tunable junction. The presence of semiconducting junctions in half-quantum-flux-biased SQUIDs results only in the flux-charge interaction without any additional nonlinearity in the inductance of the internal modes of the gyrator. Both modes are also shunted by LC circuits with resonance frequencies setting the central frequency of the device. The flux bias between the LC circuit and the SQUID loop is set to one quarter quantum flux to render the FENNEC interaction quadratic as needed for gyration in
Eq.

7
. Finally, each internal gyrator mode is coupled to an external port via an inductance. Stray capacitive coupling between the two modes will be mostly present in a realistic implementation and will be briefly analyzed later on when we discuss circuit disorder. We stress that the device, which involves only two modes, is both compact and passive.

Scattering matrix.
Starting from the equations of motion of the mean-field Lagrangian, we find that the linear scattering response can be expressed as

\bm{S}(\omega)=\left(\bm{1}-\frac{\bm{Z_{\omega}}^{-1}}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}^{-1}}\right)^{-1}\cdot\left(\bm{1}+\frac{\bm{Z_{\omega}}^{-1}}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}^{-1}}\right),

(11)

(see
Section

B.5
) where
Z_{\mathrm{TL}}
is the characteristic impedance of the input-output transmission lines, and
\bm{Z_{\omega}}=i\omega\bm{L_{c}}+[i\omega\bm{C}+(i\omega\bm{L})^{-1}+iG\bm{\sigma_{y}}]^{-1}
encodes the total impedance of the gyrator modes. Here,
\bm{C}
and
\bm{L}
are the
2\times 2
capacitance and inductance matrices of the gyrator modes respectively,
\bm{\sigma_{y}}
is the Pauli matrix, and
\bm{L_{c}}
is the coupling inductance matrix between the transmission lines and the gyrator modes. In the ideal case where
\bm{L_{c}}=L_{c}\bm{1}
,
\bm{C}=C_{0}\bm{1}
and
\bm{L}=L_{0}\bm{1}
, the scattering matrix reduces to the simple form

\bm{S}(\omega)=\cos(2\theta_{\omega})\bm{1}+i\sin(2\theta_{\omega})\bm{\sigma_{y}},

(12)

where

\tan(2\theta_{\omega})=\frac{2G\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}(\omega)}{1-\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}^{2}(\omega)/\overline{Z}_{0}^{2}(\omega)-G^{2}\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}^{2}(\omega)},

(13)

and

\displaystyle\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}(\omega)=\frac{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}{\left[1+Z_{c}(\omega)/Z_{0}(\omega)\right]^{2}+G^{2}Z_{c}^{2}(\omega)},

(14)

\displaystyle\overline{Z}_{0}(\omega)=\frac{Z_{0}(\omega)}{1+[Z_{c}(\omega)/Z_{0}(\omega)]\left[1+G^{2}Z_{0}^{2}(\omega)\right]},

(15)

are the frequency-dependent characteristic impedance of the lines and renormalized load impedance due to the coupling inductance, respectively. Here
Z_{0}(\omega)=[i\omega C_{0}+(i\omega L_{0})^{-1}]^{-1}
is the impedance of the load whereas
Z_{c}(\omega)=i\omega L_{c}
is the impedance of the coupling inductance.
\bm{S}
approaches the ideal scattering matrix of a gyrator
Eq.

6
for
|\tan(2\theta_{\omega})|\to\infty
or, equivalently, when the circuit is perfectly impedance-matched such that transmission is maximal.

Central frequency.
The central frequency of the device corresponds to the frequency for which the denominator in
Eq.

13
vanishes with the smallest
G
possible. As discussed in further details in
Section

B.5
, the central frequency is close to the resonance frequency of the internal gyrator modes
\omega_{0}=1/\sqrt{L_{0}C_{0}}
.

Impedance-matched conductance.
The conductance for which the scattering matrix approaches that of an ideal gyrator at
\omega=\omega_{0}
is approximately

G_{0}=\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}(\omega_{0})^{-1}=\frac{\sqrt{1+2\left(\sqrt{2}L_{c}\omega_{0}/Z_{\mathrm{TL}}\right)^{2}}-1}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}\left(\sqrt{2}L_{c}\omega_{0}/Z_{\mathrm{TL}}\right)^{2}},

(16)

which becomes
G_{0}=Z_{\mathrm{TL}}^{-1}
as

. To maximize transmission, we set
G_{\mathrm{max}}
in
Eq.

9
equal to
G_{0}
in
Eq.

16
. We also note that
G_{0}
decreases with increasing
L_{c}
. As will be shown below, we ideally want
L_{c}=0
such as to maximize the frequency bandwidth of the device leaving us with the constraint
G_{\mathrm{max}}=Z_{\mathrm{TL}}^{-1}
. In cases where the transmission lines have a characteristic impedance
Z_{\mathrm{TL}}\ll G_{\mathrm{max}}^{-1}
, which is most likely for typical circuit parameters, we can nonetheless use a matching circuit between the lines and the gyrator

;
Naaman and Aumentado 2022

Frequency bandwidth.
We also introduce the frequency bandwidth
\delta=\omega_{+}-\omega_{-}
for gyration with
\omega_{\pm}
the cut-off frequencies for which reflection equals transmission, where
|\tan(2\theta_{\omega})|=1
. At large
L_{c}
, where
G_{0}\approx(L_{c}\omega_{0})^{-1}
, we find (see
Section

B.5
)

\delta\approx\frac{Z_{0}Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}{L_{c}^{2}\omega_{0}}.

(17)

The same expression for zero
L_{c}
is instead
\delta=2\omega_{0}\sqrt{1+\beta\left(Z_{0}/Z_{\mathrm{TL}}\right)^{2}}
where
4\beta=G^{2}Z_{\mathrm{TL}}+2|G|Z_{\mathrm{TL}}-1
.

Compression point.
As discussed above, frequency mixing can lead to reduced transmission and here we define the compression level as the maximum average photon number
N
for which the scattering-matrix components deviate by 1 dB from the expected values in the zero-photon linear limit. Near the central frequency
\omega_{0}
we find that
|\tan(2\theta_{\omega_{0}})|\approx 2(1-x)/[1-(1-x)^{2}]
, where
x=\pi Z_{0}N/2R_{Q}
using the mean-field expression for the conductance in
Eq.

10
, see
Section

B.5
. From this expression, we find a maximum average photon number

N_{\mathrm{max}}\approx\frac{R_{Q}}{\pi Z_{0}}

(18)

by setting
|\tan(2\theta_{\omega_{0}})|\approx 1.31
and with
\theta_{\omega_{0}}
the angle at which transmission drops by 1 dB in
Eq.

12
at the central frequency
\omega_{0}
with nonzero average photon number
N
. That the maximal photon number

decreases with increasing
Z_{0}
is a signature that the system dynamics is more affected by the junctions nonlinearity for large zero-point fluctuations of the internal gyrator modes.
Assuming a typical mode impedance
Z_{0}=50\,\Omega

18

Noise sensitivity
. The gyrator interaction in

7
is akin to a Jaynes-Cummings interaction between two resonant LC oscillators that are the internal gyrator modes. This quadratic model, with energy splitting
2G
, is insensitive to both charge and flux noise. Nevertheless, for the design of

2

is sensitive to both charge noise
\dot{\Phi}_{1(2)}\to\dot{\Phi}_{1(2)}+\delta\dot{\Phi}_{1(2)}
and flux noise
\Phi_{1(2)}\to\Phi_{1(2)}+\delta\Phi_{1(2)}
. To leading order in the noise, we find that
G\to G+(\partial G_{\mathrm{max}}/\partial V_{0})[\delta\dot{\Phi}_{1}+\delta\dot{\Phi}_{2}]/2
is insensitive to flux noise but sensitive to charge noise.
\partial G_{\mathrm{max}}/\partial V_{0}\propto E_{J}^{\prime\prime}(V_{0})
is however orders of magnitude smaller than
G_{\mathrm{max}}
and consequently charge noise is negligible. Derivations and full analysis for both flux and charge noise can be found in
Section

.

Circuit disorder.
Gyration is fragile to frequency mismatches and stray couplings, both unavoidable in realistic circuit implementations and resulting in
\bm{\sigma_{z}}
and
\bm{\sigma}_{x}
components in the scattering matrix

12

\bm{L_{c}}=L_{c}\bm{1}+dL_{c}\bm{\sigma_{z}}
,
\bm{C}=C_{0}\bm{1}+dC_{0}\bm{\sigma_{z}}-C_{12}\bm{\sigma_{x}}
and
\bm{L}=L_{0}\bm{1}+dL_{0}\bm{\sigma_{z}}-L_{12}\bm{\sigma_{x}}
with
dL_{c}
,
dC_{0}
,
dL_{0}
the disorder in
L_{c}
,
C_{0}
,
L_{0}

C_{12}
,
L_{12}
the parasitic capacitive and inductive couplings between active nodes and loops, respectively. As shown in
Appendix

D

\bm{\sigma_{z}}
and
\bm{\sigma_{x}}

,

dL_{0}\ll L_{0}^{2}\omega_{0}Z_{\mathrm{TL}}G_{0}^{2}

C_{12}\ll Z_{\mathrm{TL}}G_{0}^{2}/\omega_{0}
and
L_{12}\ll L_{0}^{2}\omega_{0}Z_{\mathrm{TL}}G_{0}^{2}
. These constraints are all realizable in superconducting circuits. We note that a larger optimal conductance
G_{0}
[i.e. a smaller
L_{c}
in

16

Eq.

17
. Further discussions regarding circuit disorder can be found in
Appendix

.

Optimal circuit parameters.
Important circuit parameters are the coupling inductance
L_{c}
, the conductance
G_{\mathrm{max}}
in
Eq.

9
which must be set to the optimal conductance value
G_{0}
, and the characteristic impedance
Z_{0}
of the shunting LC resonators. For typical semiconducting junctions,
G_{\mathrm{max}}\ll Z_{\mathrm{TL}}^{-1}
which forces

to be large such that the condition that
G_{\mathrm{max}}=G_{0}
can be satisfied accordingly to
Eq.

16
unless we use a matching circuit between the transmission lines and the gyrator. A larger

(i.e. smaller

Eq.

17

to maximize

18
which equally contributes in reducing the frequency bandwidth. Overall the larger

Beyond mean-field theory.
So far we have used the mean-field approach to capture the leading order effects of the circuit nonlinearity in the scattering matrix. However, this approach does not take into account the impact of quantum fluctuations. The time evolution of the full circuit under a dissipative master equation is analyzed in
Appendix

C
, where we show that quantum fluctuations are indeed negligible when comparing reflection against mean-field theory for different input powers and load impedances. In
Appendix

E
, we also follow the circuit-quantization procedure
Vool and Devoret 2017
on a generic circuit with the FENNEC interaction which is nonlinear in both the phase and charge quadratures due to the higher derivatives of
E_{J}
. To this end, we introduce a perturbative expansion for the canonical charges with respect to the voltages
\dot{\Phi}_{1}
and
\dot{\Phi}_{2}
to take into account the higher derivatives of
E_{J}
. The perturbative expansion yields nonlinear corrections to the quantized circuit Hamiltonian. The leading order effect resulting from the second derivative of
E_{J}
is a nonlinear capacitive energy that depends on the phase of the other mode.

V
Conclusion

We proposed a flux-charge interaction that breaks time-reversal symmetry in the presence of static external magnetic fields and which can be used as a building block for passive nonreciprocal devices such as gyrators and circulators. We analytically and numerically investigated the scattering matrix of a gyrator based on the this interaction. The strength of the FENNEC interaction, which we wish to maximize, will determine both the frequency bandwidth of the device and the sensitivity to circuit disorder. The nonlinearity of the junctions will also result in compression similarly to other proposals for circulators
Koch et al. 2010
;
Müller et al. 2018
;
Richman and Taylor 2021
. Despite its narrow bandwidth, the advantages of our gyrator are both its compactness and passiveness.

Beyond applications to nonreciprocal devices, the FENNEC interaction yields either quadratic or nonlinear two-body interactions opening up new possibilities for engineering two-qubit gates and next-generation superconducting qubits
Gyenis et al. 2021
. Indeed, based on the recent by proposal by
Rymarz et al. 2021
, it can be shown that GKP states
Gottesman et al. 2001
can be stabilized with this interaction
et al.
.

Appendix A

A.1
Time-reversal symmetry

Voltages and currents are typically considered even and odd variables with respect to time inversion, i.e.
V(-t)=V(t)
and
I(-t)=-I(t)
. Given that the fluxes and charges are their time integrals respectively, i.e.
\Phi(t)=\int_{0}^{t}V(\tau)d\tau
and
Q(t)=\int_{0}^{t}I(\tau)d\tau
, we would then define
\Phi\rightarrow-\Phi
and
\dot{\Phi}\rightarrow\dot{\Phi}
under time inversion.

A.2
FENNEC Lagrangian

We consider a generic circuit Lagrangian of the form
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{0}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{i}nt}

(20)

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{i}nt}&=-\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{1},\bm{T_{1}},V_{1}+\dot{\Phi}_{2},\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{ex}},\varphi_{1})=-\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\dot{\Phi}_{2}^{n}}{n!}\frac{\Phi_{1}^{m}}{m!}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\right)^{m}\frac{\partial^{n+m}\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{1},\bm{T_{1}},V_{1},\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{ex}},0)}{\partial V^{n}\partial\varphi^{m}}\end{split}

(21)

\bm{\Phi}=\left(\Phi_{1},\ \Phi_{2}\right)

\Phi_{1}
(
\Phi_{2}

\bm{\varphi}=2\pi\bm{\Phi}/\Phi_{0}

\bm{C_{0}}

\bm{V}

\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta,\bm{T},V,\varphi^{\mathrm{ex}},\varphi)=-\Delta\sum_{i}\sqrt{1-[\bm{T}(V)]_{i}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\varphi-\varphi^{\mathrm{e}x}}{2}\right)},

(22)

k

[\bm{T_{k}}]_{i}

k

(27)

the shift in the capacitance

\displaystyle c_{2}=-\frac{\partial^{2}\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{1},\bm{T_{1}},V_{1},\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{ex}},0)}{\partial V^{2}}=-\sum_{i}\frac{\Delta_{1}\sin^{2}\left(\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{e}x}/2\right)/2}{\sqrt{1-[\bm{T_{1}}(V_{1})]_{i}\sin^{2}\left(\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{e}x}/2\right)}}\left[[\bm{T_{1}}(V_{1})]_{i}^{\prime\prime}-\frac{([\bm{T_{1}}(V_{1})]_{i})^{2}\sin^{2}\left(\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{e}x}/2\right)/2}{1-[\bm{T_{1}}(V_{1})]_{i}\sin^{2}\left(\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{e}x}/2\right)}\right],

\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta,\bm{T},V,\varphi^{\mathrm{ex}}\pm\pi,0)=-\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta,\bm{T},V,\varphi^{\mathrm{ex}},0)
in the weak transmission limit
[\bm{T}(V_{1})]_{i}\ll 1
, as will be clear below. Here
\bm{\Phi}=\left(\Phi_{1},\ \Phi_{2}\right)
is a vector comprising the branch flux
\Phi_{1}
(
\Phi_{2}
) of the first (second) mode,
\bm{\varphi}=2\pi\bm{\Phi}/\Phi_{0}
are the associated branch phases,
\bm{C_{0}}
and
\bm{C_{c}}
are capacitance matrices due to the shunt capacitors and the coupling capacitors respectively,
\bm{C_{J}}
is the capacitance matrix associated with the coupling to the control voltage lines
\bm{V}
,
\bm{L_{0}}
is an inductance matrix,

\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta,\bm{T},V,\varphi^{\mathrm{ex}},\varphi)=-\Delta\sum_{i}\sqrt{1-[\bm{T}(V)]_{i}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\varphi-\varphi^{\mathrm{e}x}}{2}\right)},

(50)

is the form of the Andreev bound-state energy of any semiconducting junction in the circuit,
\Delta_{k}
is the gap energy of the
k
th junction with transmission probability
[\bm{T_{k}}(V_{k})]_{i}
,
\Phi_{k}^{\mathrm{e}x}
is an external flux threading the
k
th loop. In this work we focus on the leading order contribution of the interaction Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{target}}=G_{+}(t)\left(\dot{\Phi}_{2}\Phi_{1}+\dot{\Phi}_{1}\Phi_{2}\right)/2+G_{-}(t)\left(\dot{\Phi}_{2}\Phi_{1}-\dot{\Phi}_{1}\Phi_{2}\right)/2,

(51)

where we defined the amplitudes

G_{\pm}=-\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\frac{\partial^{2}\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{1},\bm{T_{1}},V_{1},\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{ex}},0)}{\partial V\partial\varphi}\mp\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\frac{\partial^{2}\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{2},\bm{T_{2}},V_{2},\varphi_{2}^{\mathrm{ex}},0)}{\partial V\partial\varphi}.

(52)

Here

\frac{\partial^{2}\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta,\bm{T},V,\varphi^{\mathrm{ex}},0)}{\partial V\partial\varphi}=-\frac{\Delta}{4}\sum_{i}\frac{\partial[\bm{T}(V)]_{i}}{\partial V}\sin\left(\varphi^{\mathrm{e}x}\right)\frac{1-[\bm{T}(V)]_{i}\sin^{2}\left(\varphi^{\mathrm{e}x}/2\right)/2}{\sqrt{1-[\bm{T}(V)]_{i}\sin^{2}\left(\varphi^{\mathrm{e}x}/2\right)}^{3}}.

(53)

Moreover
G_{-}(t)=1/(2R)
, where
R
is the resistance of a gyrator. Overall we truncate the interaction Lagrangian to

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{int}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{c}or}\approx\sum_{k=1}^{2}\left[\frac{c_{k}}{2}\dot{\Phi}_{k}^{2}+\frac{\Phi_{k}^{2}}{2\ell_{k}}+\alpha_{k}\dot{\Phi}_{k}+\beta_{k}\Phi_{k}\right]+G_{+}(t)\left(\dot{\Phi}_{2}\Phi_{1}+\dot{\Phi}_{1}\Phi_{2}\right)/2+G_{-}(t)\left(\dot{\Phi}_{2}\Phi_{1}-\dot{\Phi}_{1}\Phi_{2}\right)/2

(54)

where we defined

\displaystyle c_{k}=-\frac{\partial^{2}\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{\ell},\bm{T_{\ell}},V_{\ell},\varphi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{ex}},0)}{\partial V^{2}},

(55)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ell_{k}}=-\left(\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\right)^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{k},\bm{T_{k}},V_{k},\varphi_{k}^{\mathrm{ex}},0)}{\partial\varphi^{2}}-\left(\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\right)^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{k},\bm{T_{k}},V_{k},\varphi_{k}^{\mathrm{ex}}+(-1)^{k}\pi,0)}{\partial\varphi^{2}},

(56)

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}=-\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{\ell},\bm{T_{\ell}},V_{\ell},\varphi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{ex}},0)}{\partial V},

(57)

\displaystyle\beta_{k}=-\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{k},\bm{T_{k}},V_{k},\varphi_{k}^{\mathrm{ex}},0)}{\partial\varphi}-\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{k},\bm{T_{k}},V_{k},\varphi_{k}^{\mathrm{ex}}+(-1)^{k}\pi,0)}{\partial\varphi}.

(58)

For optimal gyration we wish for
G_{-}(t)
(
G_{+}(t)
) to be maximized (minimized).
G_{-}(t)
leads to a resonant Jaynes-Cummings-type interaction with a
\pi/2
relative phase (
i\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{b}+\mathrm{h.c}
) whereas
G_{+}(t)
leads to a off-resonant two-mode-squeezing-type interaction (
i\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{b}^{\dagger}+\mathrm{h.c}
).

B.2
Weak transmission limit

In the weak transmission (
[\bm{T_{k}}(V_{k})]_{i}\ll 1
) limit we find that

\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta,\bm{T},V,\varphi^{\mathrm{ex}},\varphi)\approx\Delta+\frac{\Delta\sum_{i}[\bm{T}(V)]_{i}}{4}-\frac{\Delta\sum_{i}[\bm{T}(V)]_{i}}{4}\cos\left(\varphi-\varphi^{\mathrm{e}x}\right)=\Delta+E_{J}(\Delta,\bm{T},V)-E_{J}(\Delta,\bm{T},V)\cos\left(\varphi-\varphi^{\mathrm{e}x}\right).

(59)

Notice that

\displaystyle c_{k}=-\left(1-\cos\left(\varphi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{ex}}\right)\right)\frac{\partial^{2}E_{J}(\Delta_{\ell},\bm{T_{\ell}},V_{\ell})}{\partial V^{2}},

(60)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ell_{k}}=0,

(61)

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}=-\left(1-\cos\left(\varphi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{ex}}\right)\right)\frac{\partial E_{J}(\Delta_{\ell},\bm{T_{\ell}},V_{\ell})}{\partial V},

(62)

\displaystyle\beta_{k}=0.

(63)

From now on we will drop
c_{k}
and
1/\ell_{k}
in the assumption that they are negligible contributions to the capacitance and inductance of the modes.

Gyration.

If the FENNEC interaction can prove useful for two-qubit gates the main application is the realization of nonreciprocal devices. It follows that

G_{-}(t)\approx\frac{\pi}{R_{Q}}\sin\left(\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{e}x}\right)\frac{1}{2e}\frac{\partial E_{J}(\Delta_{1},\bm{T_{1}},V_{1})}{\partial V}-\frac{\pi}{R_{Q}}\sin\left(\varphi_{2}^{\mathrm{e}x}\right)\frac{1}{2e}\frac{\partial E_{J}(\Delta_{2},\bm{T_{2}},V_{2})}{\partial V}

(64)

where we defined
R_{Q}=\Phi_{0}/(2e)=h/(2e)^{2}\simeq 6.5
k
\Omega
the resistance quantum. Importantly, this implies that the resistance of the gyrator is

R=\frac{R_{Q}}{2\pi}\left(\sin\left(\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{e}x}\right)\frac{1}{2e}\frac{\partial E_{J}(\Delta_{1},\bm{T_{1}},V_{1})}{\partial V}-\sin\left(\varphi_{2}^{\mathrm{e}x}\right)\frac{1}{2e}\frac{\partial E_{J}(\Delta_{2},\bm{T_{2}},V_{2})}{\partial V}\right)^{-1}.

(65)

Typically
|\partial E_{J}(\Delta_{k},\bm{T_{k}},V_{k})/\partial V|\ll 1
and it is therefore clear that the resistance of the gyrator
R
is mostly likely larger than the resistance quantum
R_{Q}
.

B.3
Noise sensitivity

In this section we analyze the noise sensitivity of the device. Notice that given the term
\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{1},\bm{T_{1}},V_{1}+\dot{\Phi}_{2},\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{ex}},\varphi_{1})
, charge noise in the second mode, such that
\dot{\Phi}_{2}\to\dot{\Phi}_{2}+\delta\dot{\Phi}_{2}
, is equivalent to
V_{1}\to V_{1}+\delta\dot{\Phi}_{2}
. Similarly, flux noise in the first mode, such that
\varphi_{1}\to\varphi_{1}+\delta\varphi_{1}
, is equivalent to
\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{ex}}\to\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{ex}}-\delta\varphi_{1}
.

Charge noise.
In presence of charge noise, which amounts to
V_{1}\to V_{1}+\delta\dot{\Phi}_{2}
and
V_{2}\to V_{2}+\delta\dot{\Phi}_{1}
in the FENNEC interaction strength
G_{-}(t)
, we find that
G_{-}(t)\to G_{-}(t)+\delta G_{-}(t)
where

\delta G_{-}(t)\approx\frac{\pi}{R_{Q}}\sin\left(\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{e}x}\right)\frac{1}{2e}\frac{\partial^{2}E_{J}(\Delta_{1},\bm{T_{1}},V_{1})}{\partial V^{2}}\delta\dot{\Phi}_{2}-\frac{\pi}{R_{Q}}\sin\left(\varphi_{2}^{\mathrm{e}x}\right)\frac{1}{2e}\frac{\partial^{2}E_{J}(\Delta_{2},\bm{T_{2}},V_{2})}{\partial V^{2}}\delta\dot{\Phi}_{1}

(66)

to leading order in the noise.

The frequencies of the normal modes of gyrator become
\omega_{\pm}=\omega_{0}\pm G_{-}(t)\pm\delta G_{-}(t)
. We observe that the dispersion is linear in charge noise and determined by the second derivative of
E_{J}
.

Flux noise.
In presence of flux noise, which can be implemented with
\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{ex}}\to\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{ex}}-\delta\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}^{\mathrm{ex}}\to\varphi_{2}^{\mathrm{ex}}-\delta\varphi_{2}
in the FENNEC interaction strength
G_{-}(t)
, we find that
G_{-}(t)\to G_{-}(t)+\delta G_{-}(t)
where

\delta G_{-}(t)\approx-\frac{\pi}{R_{Q}}\cos\left(\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{e}x}\right)\frac{1}{2e}\frac{\partial E_{J}(\Delta_{1},\bm{T_{1}},V_{1})}{\partial V}\delta\varphi_{1}+\frac{\pi}{R_{Q}}\cos\left(\varphi_{2}^{\mathrm{e}x}\right)\frac{1}{2e}\frac{\partial E_{J}(\Delta_{2},\bm{T_{2}},V_{2})}{\partial V}\delta\varphi_{2}.

(67)

We observe that the system is insensitive to flux noise to leading order at the optimal gyration point
|\varphi_{k}^{\mathrm{ex}}|=\pi/2
.

B.4
Mean-field theory

In this section we linearized the FENNEC interaction within a mean-field theory approximation:

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{i}nt}^{\mathrm{mf}}&=-\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{1},\bm{T_{1}},V_{1}+\dot{\Phi}_{2},\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{ex}},\varphi_{1})-\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{2},\bm{T_{2}},V_{2}+\dot{\Phi}_{1},\varphi_{2}^{\mathrm{ex}},\varphi_{2})\\
&=-\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\delta\dot{\Phi}_{2}^{n}}{n!}\frac{\delta\Phi_{1}^{m}}{m!}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\right)^{m}\frac{\partial^{n+m}\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{1},\bm{T_{1}},V_{1}+\langle\dot{\Phi}_{2}\rangle,\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{ex}}-\langle\varphi_{1}\rangle,0)}{\partial V^{n}\partial\varphi^{m}}\\
&-\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\delta\dot{\Phi}_{1}^{n}}{n!}\frac{\delta\Phi_{2}^{m}}{m!}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\right)^{m}\frac{\partial^{n+m}\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{2},\bm{T_{2}},V_{2}+\langle\dot{\Phi}_{1}\rangle,\varphi_{2}^{\mathrm{ex}}-\langle\varphi_{2}\rangle,0)}{\partial V^{n}\partial\varphi^{m}},\end{split}

(68)

where
\delta\Phi_{k}=\Phi_{k}-\langle\Phi_{k}\rangle
. Similarly,

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{c}or}^{\mathrm{mf}}&=-\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{1},\bm{T_{1}},V_{1}+\dot{\Phi}_{2},\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{ex}}-\pi,\varphi_{1})-\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{2},\bm{T_{2}},V_{2}+\dot{\Phi}_{1},\varphi_{2}^{\mathrm{ex}}+\pi,\varphi_{2})\\
&=-\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\delta\Phi_{1}^{m}}{m!}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\right)^{m}\frac{\partial^{m}\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{1},\bm{T_{1}},V_{1},\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{ex}}-\pi-\langle\varphi_{1}\rangle,0)}{\partial\varphi^{m}}\\
&-\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\delta\Phi_{2}^{m}}{m!}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\right)^{m}\frac{\partial^{m}\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{2},\bm{T_{2}},V_{2},\varphi_{2}^{\mathrm{ex}}+\pi-\langle\varphi_{2}\rangle,0)}{\partial\varphi^{m}}.\end{split}

(69)

To second order we therefore arrive at the effective interaction Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{int}}^{\mathrm{mf}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cor}}^{\mathrm{mf}}\approx\sum_{k=1}^{2}\left[\alpha_{k}(t)\dot{\Phi}_{k}+\beta_{k}(t)\Phi_{k}\right]+G_{+}(t)\left(\dot{\Phi}_{2}\Phi_{1}+\dot{\Phi}_{1}\Phi_{2}\right)/2+G_{-}(t)\left(\dot{\Phi}_{2}\Phi_{1}-\dot{\Phi}_{1}\Phi_{2}\right)/2,

(70)

where we defined

\displaystyle G_{\pm}(t)=-\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\frac{\partial^{2}\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{1},\bm{T_{1}},V_{1}+\langle\dot{\Phi}_{2}\rangle,\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{ex}}-\langle\varphi_{1}\rangle,0)}{\partial V\partial\varphi}\mp\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\frac{\partial^{2}\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{2},\bm{T_{2}},V_{2}+\langle\dot{\Phi}_{1}\rangle,\varphi_{2}^{\mathrm{ex}}-\langle\varphi_{2}\rangle,0)}{\partial V\partial\varphi},

(71)

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}(t)=-\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{\ell},\bm{T_{\ell}},V_{\ell}+\langle\dot{\Phi}_{k}\rangle,\varphi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{ex}}-\langle\varphi_{\ell}\rangle,0)}{\partial V}-\langle\Phi_{\ell}\rangle\frac{G_{+}(t)+(-1)^{\ell}G_{-}(t)}{4},

(72)

\displaystyle\beta_{k}(t)=-\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{k},\bm{T_{k}},V_{k}+\langle\dot{\Phi}_{\ell}\rangle,\varphi_{k}^{\mathrm{ex}}-\langle\varphi_{k}\rangle,0)}{\partial\varphi}-\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\frac{\partial\varepsilon_{J}(\Delta_{k},\bm{T_{k}},V_{k},\varphi_{k}^{\mathrm{ex}}+(-1)^{k}\pi-\langle\varphi_{k}\rangle,0)}{\partial\varphi}

\displaystyle\phantom{\beta_{k}(t)=}-\langle\dot{\Phi}_{\ell}\rangle\frac{G_{+}(t)+(-1)^{\ell}G_{-}(t)}{4},

(73)

where
\ell\neq k
. The field averages have to be solved self-consistently. To quartic order in the flux while neglecting higher order derivatives in either
V
or
\varphi
, we find that

\displaystyle G_{\pm}(t)=\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\frac{\partial E_{J}(\Delta_{1},\bm{T_{1}},V_{1})}{\partial V}\sin\left(\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{ex}}\right)\left(1-\frac{\langle\varphi_{1}\rangle^{2}}{2}\right)\pm\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\frac{\partial E_{J}(\Delta_{2},\bm{T_{2}},V_{2})}{\partial V}\sin\left(\varphi_{2}^{\mathrm{ex}}\right)\left(1-\frac{\langle\varphi_{2}\rangle^{2}}{2}\right)

(74)

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}(t)=-\left(1-\cos(\varphi_{\ell}^{\mathrm{ex}})\right)\frac{\partial E_{J}(\Delta_{\ell},\bm{T_{\ell}},V_{\ell})}{\partial V},

(75)

and
\beta_{k}(t)=0
.

B.5
Scattering matrix of the linearized system

In this section, we focus on the linear mean-field Lagrangian
Hazard et al. 2019
:

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{mf}}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{0}dx\left[\frac{c}{2}\left(\partial_{t}\tilde{\Phi}_{i}(x,t)\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2\ell}\left(\partial_{x}\tilde{\Phi}_{i}(x,t)\right)^{2}\right]-\left(\bm{\tilde{\Phi}}(0,t)-\bm{\Phi}\right)^{T}\cdot\frac{\bm{L_{c}}^{-1}}{2}\cdot\left(\bm{\tilde{\Phi}}(0,t)-\bm{\Phi}\right)+\bm{\alpha}^{T}\cdot\dot{\bm{\Phi}}\\
+\dot{\bm{\Phi}}^{T}\cdot\frac{\bm{C}}{2}\cdot\dot{\bm{\Phi}}-\bm{\Phi}^{T}\cdot\frac{\bm{L}^{-1}}{2}\cdot\bm{\Phi}+\dot{\bm{\Phi}}^{T}\cdot\frac{G_{+}(t)\bm{\sigma_{x}}-iG_{-}(t)\bm{\sigma_{y}}}{4}\cdot\bm{\Phi}+\bm{\Phi}^{T}\cdot\frac{G_{+}(t)\bm{\sigma_{x}}+iG_{-}(t)\bm{\sigma_{y}}}{4}\cdot\dot{\bm{\Phi}},\end{split}

(76)

where
\bm{L_{c}}
is assumed to be diagonal. Here
\bm{\sigma_{x}}
and
\bm{\sigma_{y}}
are the Pauli matrices.

Equations of motion

The equations of motion for the effectively linearized Lagrangian are given by

0=\partial_{t}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{mf}}}{\partial(\partial_{t}\tilde{\Phi}_{i})}+\partial_{x}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{mf}}}{\partial(\partial_{x}\tilde{\Phi}_{i})}-\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{mf}}}{\partial\tilde{\Phi}_{i}}=\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{mf}}}{\partial\dot{\Phi}_{i}}-\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{mf}}}{\partial\Phi_{i}},\quad i=1,2,

(77)

which explicitly take the form

\displaystyle c\partial_{t}^{2}\bm{\tilde{\Phi}}(x,t)=\frac{1}{\ell}\partial_{x}^{2}\bm{\tilde{\Phi}}(x,t),

(78)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ell}\partial_{x}\bm{\tilde{\Phi}}(0,t)=\bm{L_{c}}^{-1}\cdot\left(\bm{\tilde{\Phi}}(0,t)-\bm{\Phi}\right),

(79)

\displaystyle 0=\bm{C}\cdot\ddot{\bm{\Phi}}+\bm{L}^{-1}\cdot\bm{\Phi}-iG_{-}(t)\bm{\sigma_{y}}\cdot\dot{\bm{\Phi}}-\frac{1}{\ell}\partial_{x}\bm{\tilde{\Phi}}(0,t).

(80)

Given the wave-equation
Eq.

78
we find that the quantized field in the transmission line
j
has the form

\displaystyle\bm{\tilde{\Phi}}(x,t)=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{4\pi c}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\sqrt{\omega}}\left(e^{i\omega t+ik_{\omega}x}\bm{a_{\omega}}^{\dagger}+e^{i\omega t-ik_{\omega}x}\bm{b}_{\omega}^{\dagger}+\mathrm{h.c.}\right),

(81)

with the dispersion relation
k_{\omega}=\omega\sqrt{c\hskip 1.00006pt\ell}=\omega/\omega_{\mathrm{TL}}
and commutation relations
\left[\hat{a}_{i,\omega},\hat{a}_{j,\omega^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\right]=\delta_{ij}\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime})
and
\left[\hat{b}_{i,\omega},\hat{b}_{j,\omega^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\right]=\delta_{ij}\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime})
. Here
\bm{a_{\omega}}
(
\bm{b_{\omega}}
) are the annihilation operators associated with the ingoing (outgoing) fields at frequency
\omega
.

Fourier transform

We apply a Fourier transform on
Eqs.

79
and
80
(with the definition
\hat{y}(\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\ y(t)e^{i\omega t}/\sqrt{2\pi}
and property
y(-\omega)=[y(\omega)]^{*}
):

\displaystyle\bm{\hat{\Phi}}(\omega)=\bm{\hat{\tilde{\Phi}}}(0,\omega)-\frac{\bm{L_{c}}}{\ell}\cdot\partial_{x}\bm{\hat{\tilde{\Phi}}}(0,\omega),

(82)

\displaystyle 0=\left(-\omega^{2}\bm{C}+\bm{L}^{-1}\right)\cdot\bm{\hat{\Phi}}(\omega)-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega^{\prime}\ \omega^{\prime}\hat{G}_{-}(\omega-\omega^{\prime})\bm{\sigma_{y}}\cdot\bm{\hat{\Phi}}(\omega^{\prime})-\frac{1}{\ell}\partial_{x}\bm{\hat{\tilde{\Phi}}}(0,\omega).

(83)

Expansion in the amplitude of the flux fields

G_{-}(t)
depends on the average of the flux fields which we assume to have small amplitude. We write
G_{-}(t)=\overline{G}_{-}+\lambda dG_{-}(t)

\overline{G}_{-}=\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\frac{\partial E_{J}(\Delta_{1},\bm{T}1,V_{1})}{\partial V}\sin\left(\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{ex}}\right)-\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\frac{\partial E_{J}(\Delta_{2},\bm{T_{2}},V_{2})}{\partial V}\sin\left(\varphi_{2}^{\mathrm{ex}}\right)

(84)

\begin{split}\lambda dG_{-}(t)&=\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\frac{\partial E_{J}(\Delta_{2},\bm{T_{2}},V_{2})}{\partial V}\sin\left(\varphi_{2}^{\mathrm{ex}}\right)\frac{\langle\varphi_{2}(t)\rangle^{2}}{2}-\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\frac{\partial E_{J}(\Delta_{1},\bm{T_{1}},V_{1})}{\partial V}\sin\left(\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{ex}}\right)\frac{\langle\varphi_{1}(t)\rangle^{2}}{2}\end{split},

(85)

depends on the flux fields following the mean-field approximation. We do a perturbative expansion in
\lambda
, i.e.
\hat{\bm{\tilde{\Phi}}}(0,\omega)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{k}\hat{\bm{\tilde{\Phi}}}^{(k)}(0,\omega)
and
\lambda dG_{-}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{k+1}dG_{-}^{(k)}(t)

Order 0:

\displaystyle 0=\left(-\omega^{2}\bm{C}+\bm{L}^{-1}-\omega\overline{G}_{-}\bm{\sigma_{y}}\right)\cdot\left(\bm{\hat{\tilde{\Phi}}}^{(0)}(0,\omega)-\frac{\bm{L_{c}}}{\ell}\cdot\partial_{x}\bm{\hat{\tilde{\Phi}}}^{(0)}(0,\omega)\right)-\frac{1}{\ell}\partial_{x}\bm{\hat{\tilde{\Phi}}}^{(0)}(0,\omega).

(86)

Order 1:

\displaystyle\begin{split}0=\left(-\omega^{2}\bm{C}+\bm{L}^{-1}-\omega\overline{G}_{-}\bm{\sigma_{y}}\right)\cdot\left(\bm{\hat{\tilde{\Phi}}}^{(1)}(0,\omega)-\frac{\bm{L_{c}}}{\ell}\cdot\partial_{x}\bm{\hat{\tilde{\Phi}}}^{(1)}(0,\omega)\right)-\frac{1}{\ell}\partial_{x}\bm{\hat{\tilde{\Phi}}}^{(1)}(0,\omega)\\

(87)

Consistently with the perturbative expansion we have that

\begin{split}\lambda dG_{-}^{0}(t)&=\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\frac{\partial E_{J}(\Delta_{2},\bm{T_{2}},V_{2})}{\partial V}\sin\left(\varphi_{2}^{\mathrm{ex}}\right)\frac{\langle\varphi_{2}^{(0)}(t)\rangle^{2}}{2}-\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\frac{\partial E_{J}(\Delta_{1},\bm{T_{1}},V_{1})}{\partial V}\sin\left(\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{ex}}\right)\frac{\langle\varphi_{1}^{(0)}(t)\rangle^{2}}{2}\end{split}.

(88)

Input/output equations

Accordingly to

\bm{\hat{\tilde{\Phi}}}^{(k)}(x,\omega>0)=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{4\pi c\omega}}\left(e^{-ik_{\omega}x}\bm{a_{\omega}}\delta_{k,0}+e^{ik_{\omega}x}\bm{b_{\omega}}^{(k)}\right),

At zeroth order the scattering matrix is then

Similarly we observe that

reduces to

\displaystyle\bm{b_{\omega}}^{(1)}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega^{\prime}\ \frac{\omega^{\prime}}{\omega}Z_{\mathrm{TL}}\hat{dG}_{-}^{(0)}(\omega-\omega^{\prime})\left(\frac{\bm{Z}(\omega^{\prime})}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}-1\right)^{-1}\cdot\frac{\bm{Z_{0}}(\omega^{\prime})}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}\cdot i2\bm{\sigma_{y}}\cdot\left(\frac{\bm{Z}(\omega^{\prime})}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}-1\right)^{-1}\cdot\frac{\bm{Z_{0}}(\omega^{\prime})}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}\cdot\bm{a_{\omega^{\prime}}},

(93)

using the identities

\displaystyle 1+\bm{S}^{(0)}(\omega)=2\left(\frac{\bm{Z}(\omega)}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}-1\right)^{-1}\cdot\frac{\bm{Z}(\omega)}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}\quad\textrm{and}\quad 1-\bm{S}^{(0)}(\omega)=-2\left(\frac{\bm{Z}(\omega)}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}-1\right)^{-1}.

(94)

Next we must solve for
dG_{-}^{(0)}(t)
.

yields, for

\bm{\hat{\Phi}}^{(0)}(\omega)=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{4\pi c\omega}}\left(\bm{a_{\omega}}+\bm{b_{\omega}}^{(0)}+\frac{i\omega\bm{L_{c}}}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}\cdot\left(\bm{a_{\omega}}-\bm{b_{\omega}}^{(0)}\right)\right)=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{\pi c\omega}}\left(\frac{\bm{Z}(\omega)}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}-1\right)^{-1}\frac{\bm{Z_{0}}(\omega)}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}\cdot\bm{a_{\omega}},

(95)

Monotonic incoming field

In what follows we assume that the incoming field is monotonic with frequency
\omega_{0}
. As a result we find that

\begin{split}\langle\bm{\varphi}(t)\rangle^{2}&=\chi\left(\frac{\bm{Z}^{\dagger}(\omega_{0})}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}-1\right)^{-1}\frac{\bm{Z_{0}}^{\dagger}(\omega_{0})}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}\cdot|\langle\bm{a_{\omega_{0}}}\rangle|^{2}\cdot\frac{\bm{Z_{0}}(\omega_{0})}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}\cdot\left(\frac{\bm{Z}(\omega_{0})}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}-1\right)^{-1}\\
&+\frac{\chi}{2}\left(\frac{\bm{Z}^{T}(\omega_{0})}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}-1\right)^{-1}\frac{\bm{Z_{0}}^{T}(\omega_{0})}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}\cdot\langle\bm{a_{\omega_{0}}}\rangle^{2}\cdot\frac{\bm{Z_{0}}(\omega_{0})}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}\cdot\left(\frac{\bm{Z}(\omega_{0})}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}-1\right)^{-1}e^{-i2\omega_{0}t}+\mathrm{h.c.},\end{split}

(96)

where we defined the quantity

\chi=\left(\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\right)^{2}\frac{2\hbar}{\pi c\omega}=\frac{4}{R_{Q}c\omega}

(97)

Now we observe that

\displaystyle\hat{dG}_{-}^{(0)}(\omega)=\hat{dG}_{-}^{(0)}(0)\delta(\omega)+\hat{dG}_{-}^{(0)}(2\omega_{0})\delta(\omega-\omega_{0})+\hat{dG}_{-}^{(0)}(-2\omega_{0})\delta(\omega+\omega_{0}),

(98)

in other words,
\hat{dG}_{-}^{(0)}(\omega)
is sharply peaked at three frequencies. The
\omega=0
component leads to compression, and under conservation of total exctations,
\omega=\pm 2\omega_{0}
lead to frequency mixing. Indeed, the outgoing fields are no longer monotonic as they oscillate at both
\omega_{0}
and
3\omega_{0}
, the latter having much smaller amplitude. Importantly we see that the scattering matrix is rectangular:

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\bm{b_{-3\omega_{0}}}\\
\bm{b_{-\omega_{0}}}\\
\bm{b_{+\omega_{0}}}\\
\bm{b_{+3\omega_{0}}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{M}(-3\omega_{0};-\omega_{0})&\bm{0}\\
\bm{S}^{(0)}(-\omega_{0})&\bm{M}(-\omega_{0};+\omega_{0})\\
\bm{M}(+\omega_{0};-\omega_{0})&\bm{S}^{(0)}(+\omega_{0})\\
\bm{0}&\bm{M}(+3\omega_{0};+\omega_{0})\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\bm{a_{-\omega_{0}}}\\
\bm{a_{+\omega_{0}}}\end{pmatrix}

(99)

where we defined the frequency-mixing matrices

\displaystyle\bm{M}(\omega;\omega^{\prime})=\frac{\omega^{\prime}}{\omega}Z_{\mathrm{TL}}\hat{dG}_{-}^{(0)}(\omega-\omega^{\prime})\left(\frac{\bm{Z}(\omega^{\prime})}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}-1\right)^{-1}\cdot\frac{\bm{Z_{0}}(\omega^{\prime})}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}\cdot i2\bm{\sigma_{y}}\cdot\left(\frac{\bm{Z}(\omega^{\prime})}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}-1\right)^{-1}\cdot\frac{\bm{Z_{0}}(\omega^{\prime})}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}},

(100)

which are ultimately proportional to
\chi
.

Effective linear response theory

The leading order effect of the nonlinearity is compression: frequency mixing can be thought as its direct consequence. Compression is associated with the static component of
dG_{-}(t)
only. We therefore propose an effective linear response theory that captures compression:

\bm{b_{\omega}}\simeq\bm{S}(\omega)\cdot\bm{a_{\omega}}=\left(\frac{\bm{Z}(\omega)}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}-1\right)^{-1}\cdot\left(\frac{\bm{Z}(\omega)}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}+1\right)\cdot\bm{a_{\omega}},

(101)

where

\displaystyle\bm{Z}(\omega)=i\omega\bm{L_{c}}+\left(i\omega\bm{C}+(i\omega\bm{L})^{-1}+iG\bm{\sigma_{y}}\right)^{-1},

(102)

\displaystyle G=\overline{G}_{-}+\lambda\hat{dG}_{-}^{0}(0)=\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\frac{\partial E_{J}(\Delta_{1},\bm{T_{1}},V_{1})}{\partial V}\sin\left(\varphi_{1}^{\mathrm{ex}}\right)\left(1-\frac{\pi Z_{0}N_{1}}{2R_{Q}}\right)-\frac{2\pi}{\Phi_{0}}\frac{\partial E_{J}(\Delta_{2},\bm{T_{2}},V_{2})}{\partial V}\sin\left(\varphi_{2}^{\mathrm{ex}}\right)\left(1-\frac{\pi Z_{0}N_{2}}{2R_{Q}}\right),

(103)

with
N_{1(2)}
the average photon number in the internal gyrator mode
1(2)
with characteristic impedance
Z_{0}
. It can be verified that Taylor expanding
Eq.

101
to leading order in
\lambda\hat{dG}_{-}^{0}(0)
returns the linear part of
Eq.

93
.

Ideal case.

We consider the limiting case
\bm{L_{c}}=L_{c}\bm{1}
,
\bm{C}=C_{0}\bm{1}
and
\bm{L}=L_{0}\bm{1}
. In this case we find that

\frac{\bm{Z}(\omega)}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}=\frac{Z_{c}(\omega)}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}\bm{1}+\frac{\left(Z_{0}^{-1}(\omega)\bm{1}+iG\bm{\sigma_{y}}\right)^{-1}}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}=\frac{Z_{c}(\omega)}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}\bm{1}+\frac{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}/Z_{0}(\omega)}{(Z_{\mathrm{TL}}/Z_{0}(\omega))^{2}+G^{2}Z_{\mathrm{TL}}^{2}}\bm{1}-\frac{iGZ_{\mathrm{TL}}}{(Z_{\mathrm{TL}}/Z_{0}(\omega))^{2}+G^{2}Z_{\mathrm{TL}}^{2}}\bm{\sigma_{y}},

(104)

where
Z_{0}(\omega)=\left(i\omega C_{0}+(i\omega L_{0})^{-1}\right)^{-1}
is the load impedance and
Z_{c}(\omega)=i\omega L_{c}
is the impedance associated with the coupling inductance. Our goal will be to redefine
Z_{0}(\omega)
and
Z_{\mathrm{TL}}
such as to include
L_{c}
. To this end consider

\frac{\bm{Z}(\omega)}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}=\frac{\left(\overline{Z}_{0}^{-1}(\omega)\bm{1}+iG\bm{\sigma_{y}}\right)^{-1}}{\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}(\omega)}=\frac{\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}(\omega)/\overline{Z}_{0}(\omega)}{(\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}(\omega)/\overline{Z}_{0}(\omega))^{2}+G^{2}\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}^{2}(\omega)}\bm{1}-\frac{iG\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}(\omega)}{(\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}(\omega)/\overline{Z}_{0}(\omega))^{2}+G^{2}\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}^{2}(\omega)}\bm{\sigma_{y}}.

(105)

By putting the last two equations equal we find the effective load impedance due to the coupling inductance

\overline{Z}_{0}(\omega)=\frac{Z_{0}(\omega)}{1+(Z_{c}(\omega)/Z_{0}(\omega))(1+G^{2}Z_{0}^{2}(\omega))},

(106)

and the effective frequency-dependent characteristic impedance of the lines

\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}(\omega)=\frac{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}{\left(1+Z_{c}(\omega)/Z_{0}(\omega)\right)^{2}+G^{2}Z_{c}^{2}(\omega)}.

(107)

With these definitions we find that

\left(\frac{\bm{Z}(\omega)}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}-1\right)^{-1}\cdot\left(\frac{\bm{Z}(\omega)}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}+1\right)=\frac{((a-1)\bm{1}-ib\bm{\sigma_{y}})\cdot((a+1)\bm{1}+ib\bm{\sigma_{y}})}{(a-1)^{2}+b^{2}}=\frac{(a^{2}+b^{2}-1)\bm{1}-i2b\bm{\sigma_{y}}}{(a-1)^{2}+b^{2}},

(108)

where
\bm{Z}(\omega)=a\bm{1}+ib\bm{\sigma_{y}}
with

\displaystyle a=\frac{\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}(\omega)/\overline{Z}_{0}(\omega)}{(\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}(\omega)/\overline{Z}_{0}(\omega))^{2}+G^{2}\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}^{2}(\omega)},

(109)

\displaystyle b=-\frac{G\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}(\omega)}{(\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}(\omega)/\overline{Z}_{0}(\omega))^{2}+G^{2}\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}^{2}(\omega)}.

(110)

We therefore find that the scattering matrix reduces to

\bm{S}(\omega)=\cos(2\theta_{\omega})\bm{1}+i\sin(2\theta_{\omega})\bm{\sigma_{y}}

(111)

where we defined the angle
\theta_{\omega}
via

\tan(2\theta_{\omega})=\frac{2G\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}(\omega)}{1-\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}^{2}(\omega)/\overline{Z}_{0}^{2}(\omega)-G^{2}\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}^{2}(\omega)}.

(112)

Central frequency.

The central frequency
\omega_{0}^{\prime}
of the device corresponds to the frequency for which the denominator in
Eq.

112
vanishes, i.e.

G^{2}=\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}^{-2}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})-\overline{Z}_{0}^{-2}(\omega_{0}^{\prime}).

(113)

Moreover we wish for the optimal conductance
G
to be minimized, i.e.
\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}^{2}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})/\overline{Z}_{0}^{2}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})\to 0
and
G=1/\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})
. Naturally we wish for both
\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})
and
\overline{Z}_{0}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})
to be large.
\omega_{0}^{\prime}
must therefore be the frequency at which
\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})
peaks. Using
G=1/\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})
we find that
Eq.

107
becomes

0=Z_{c}^{2}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}^{-2}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})-Z_{\mathrm{TL}}\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}^{-1}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})+\left(1+Z_{c}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})/Z_{0}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})\right)^{2},

(114)

which reduces to

\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}^{-1}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})=\frac{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}{2Z_{c}^{2}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})}\left(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{4Z_{c}^{2}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}^{2}}\left(1+\frac{Z_{c}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})}{Z_{0}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})}\right)^{2}}\right),

(115)

where
Z_{c}^{2}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})<0
.

We would like to minimize
|\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}^{-1}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})|
such as to maximize
|\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})|
. We find a root at
\omega_{0}^{\prime}/\omega_{0}=\sqrt{1+Z_{0}/L_{c}\omega_{0}}
. However this is not sufficient to define the central frequency. Indeed, we notice that for
L_{c}\to 0
,
|\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}^{-1}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})|=Z_{\mathrm{TL}}^{-1}
is flat meaning that it cannot be minimized. This leads us to also consider the second condition that
\overline{Z}_{0}^{-1}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})=0
to satisfy
Eq.

113
:

0=Z_{0}^{-1}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})+Z_{c}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})Z_{0}^{-2}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})+Z_{c}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}^{-2}(\omega_{0}^{\prime}).

(116)

We quickly observe that for
L_{c}=0
, thus
Z_{c}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})=0
, we exactly find
\omega_{0}^{\prime}=\omega_{0}
. For large
L_{c}
, such that
\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}^{-1}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})\approx-iZ_{0}^{-1}(\omega_{0}^{\prime})
, we still find
\omega_{0}^{\prime}\approx\omega_{0}
.

In what follows we therefore approximate
\omega_{0}^{\prime}\approx\omega_{0}
to compute optimal parameters for gyration such as the conductance
G\approx 1/\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}(\omega_{0})
. Because of this approximation we emphasize that in the end the central frequency will be found by numerically solving
Eq.

113
.

However for an arbitrary
G
, solving
\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}^{-2}(\omega)-\overline{Z}_{0}^{-2}(\omega)-G^{2}=0
reveals that

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{c}}{\omega_{0}}-1\approx\frac{\overline{Z}_{\mathrm{TL}}^{-2}(\omega_{0})-G^{2}+G^{4}L_{c}^{2}\omega_{0}^{2}}{2G^{2}(2L_{c}\omega_{0}/Z_{0}-G^{2}L_{c}^{2}\omega_{0}^{2})+4Z_{\mathrm{TL}}^{-2}(1-G^{2}L_{c}^{2}\omega_{0}^{2})(2L_{c}\omega_{0}/Z_{0}+G^{2}L_{c}^{2}\omega_{0}^{2})}.

(117)

Appendix C
System Hamiltonian and Effective Lindblad Master equation

C.1
Canonical quantization

We consider the gyrator Lagrangian in
Eq.

46
and add transmission lines interacting with internal gyrator modes via a coupling inductance as in
Eq.

76
. We stop at the first derivative of the junctions’ transmission coefficients.

The canonical charge fields are

\displaystyle\tilde{q}_{1}(x,t)=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{t}\tilde{\Phi}_{1}(x,t))}=c\partial_{t}\Phi_{1}(x,t),\quad\tilde{q}_{2}(x,t)=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{t}\tilde{\Phi}_{1}(x,t))}=c\partial_{t}\Phi_{2}(x,t),

(132)

while the canonical charges are

\displaystyle q_{1}=C_{0}\dot{\Phi}_{1}-\frac{\Delta}{4}\frac{\partial T}{\partial V}\Big|_{V_{0}}\sin(\varphi_{2}),\quad q_{2}=C_{0}\dot{\Phi}_{2}+\frac{\Delta}{4}\frac{\partial T}{\partial V}\Big|_{V_{0}}\sin(\varphi_{1}).

(133)

The full system Hamiltonian is
\mathcal{H}=\sum_{i}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\tilde{q}_{i}(x,t)\partial_{t}\tilde{\Phi}_{i}(x,t)+q_{i}\dot{\Phi}_{i}-\mathcal{L}
,

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}&=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{0}dx\left[\frac{(\tilde{q}_{i}(x,t))^{2}}{2c}+\frac{1}{2\ell}\left(\partial_{x}\tilde{\Phi}_{i}(x,t)\right)^{2}\right]+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{\left(\tilde{\Phi}_{i}(0,t)-\Phi_{i}\right)^{2}}{2L}\\
&+\frac{1}{2C_{0}}\left(q_{1}+\frac{C_{0}g}{2e}\sin(\varphi_{2})\right)^{2}+\frac{\Phi_{1}^{2}}{2L_{0}}+\frac{1}{2C_{0}}\left(q_{2}-\frac{C_{0}g}{2e}\sin(\varphi_{1})\right)^{2}+\frac{\Phi_{2}^{2}}{2L_{0}}.\end{split}

(134)

The quantized fields in the transmission line
j
have the form

\displaystyle\hat{\Phi}_{j}(x,t)=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{4\pi c}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\sqrt{\omega}}\left(e^{ik_{\omega}x}\hat{a}_{j,\omega}^{+}+e^{-ik_{\omega}x}\hat{a}_{j,\omega}^{-}+\mathrm{h.c.}\right),

(135)

\displaystyle\hat{q}_{j}(x,t)=-i\sqrt{\frac{\hbar c}{4\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\sqrt{\omega}\left(e^{ik_{\omega}x}\hat{a}_{j,\omega}^{+}+e^{-ik_{\omega}x}\hat{a}_{j,\omega}^{-}-\mathrm{h.c.}\right)

(136)

with the dispersion relation
k_{\omega}=\omega\sqrt{c\hskip 1.00006pt\ell}
. Here
\left[\hat{a}_{j,\omega},\hat{a}_{j^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\right]=\delta_{ij}\delta(\omega-\omega^{\prime})
.
The quantized Hamiltonian then takes the form
\hat{H}\approx\hat{H}_{g}+\hat{H}_{t}+\hat{H}_{gt}
, where

\begin{split}\hat{H}_{g}&=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{1}{2C_{0}}\left(\hat{q}_{i}-\frac{(-1)^{i}C_{0}g}{2e}\sin(\hat{\varphi}_{j})\right)^{2}+\frac{\hat{\Phi}_{0}^{2}\hat{\varphi}_{i}^{2}}{8\pi^{2}L_{0}}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{j\neq i}4E_{C}\left(\hat{n}_{i}-\frac{(-1)^{i}g}{8E_{C}}\sin\hat{\varphi}_{j}\right)^{2}+\frac{E_{L}\hat{\varphi}_{i}^{2}}{2},\\
\hat{H}_{t}&=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\hbar\omega\hat{a}_{i,\omega}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{i,\omega},\\
\hat{H}_{gt}&=-\frac{\Phi_{0}}{2\pi L}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{4\pi c}}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\sqrt{\omega}}\left(\hat{a}_{i,\omega}^{+}+\hat{a}_{i,\omega}^{-}+\mathrm{h.c.}\right)\hat{\varphi}_{i},\end{split}

(137)

where
E_{C}=e^{2}/2C_{0}
and
E_{L}=(\Phi_{0}/2\pi)^{2}/L_{0}
. Here we assume small zero-point fluctuations
\sqrt[4]{2E_{C}/E_{L}}\ll 1
and small gyrator strength
g\ll\sqrt{8E_{C}E_{L}}
. Thus, we can expand the gyrator modes in the Fock basis:

\hat{n}_{i}=\frac{1}{2i\eta}(b_{i}-b_{i}^{\dagger}),\ \hat{\varphi}_{i}=\eta(b_{i}+b_{i}^{\dagger}),\textrm{with}\ \eta=\sqrt[4]{2E_{C}(E_{L}+(g^{2}/16E_{C}))^{-1}},

(138)

and truncate
\textrm{sin}(\hat{\varphi_{i}})
to
5
-th order in
\hat{\varphi_{i}}
.

C.2
Time evolution

Following a similar process to that in
Müller et al. 2018
, we can derive a master equation for the gyrator. Assuming a single-mode, coherent-field input in the transmission lines, we find

\dot{\hat{\rho}}=\mathcal{L}(t)\hat{\rho}=\-i[\hat{H}^{\prime}(t),\hat{\rho}]/\hbar+\sum_{j}\kappa\left[\hat{b}_{j}\hat{\rho}\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger}-\left\{\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{j},\hat{\rho}\right\}/2\right],

(139)

where we defined the reduced system Hamiltonian

\hat{H}^{\prime}(t)/\hbar=\hat{H}_{g}/\hbar-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{i\sqrt{\kappa}}{2}\left(\beta_{i}e^{-i\omega_{s}t}\hat{b}_{i}^{\dagger}-\mathrm{h.c.}\right),

(140)

where we included an incoming photon fluxes with coherent amplitude
\beta_{i}
and frequency
\omega_{s}
in each of the lines, with decay rate
\kappa=\hbar/(2cL^{2}\omega_{0})
.

The Master equation is used to compute the average outgoing fields in the steady limit,

\alpha_{j}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\textrm{Tr}\left\{e^{i\omega_{s}t}\hat{b}_{j}\hat{\rho}(t)\right\},

(141)

where
\hat{b}_{j}
is the annihilation operator of the
j
th gyrator mode, and reserving contributions from
\sin(\hat{\varphi}_{i})
to
5
-th order. The components of the scattering matrix are then given by
\mathcal{S}_{ij}=\alpha_{i}/\beta_{j}-\delta_{ij}
.

The scattering matrix is extracted from the time-ordered integral of the evolution operator over a period of the drive
T=2\pi/\omega_{s}
,

\mathcal{V}(T)=\mathcal{T}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{L}(t)dt\right),

(142)

which is calculated using an exponential integrator
Shillito et al. 2021
. The evolution is performed in the diagonalized basis of
\hat{H}_{g}
and truncated to the first
21
states, allowing for a total of
5
excitations in the composite system. The steady state is subsequently found by renormalizing the right eigenvector of
\mathcal{V}
with eigenvalue norm
1
. These results were corroborated by numerical integration of
Eq.

139
using the
mesolve
function of QuTiP
Johansson et al. 2013
.

Circuit nonidealities

We start from the effective linear response theory proposed in
Section

:

\bm{b_{\omega}}\simeq\bm{S}(\omega)\cdot\bm{a_{\omega}}=\left(\frac{\bm{Z}(\omega)}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}-1\right)^{-1}\cdot\left(\frac{\bm{Z}(\omega)}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}+1\right)\cdot\bm{a_{\omega}},

(143)

where

\displaystyle\bm{Z}(\omega)=i\omega\bm{L_{c}}+\left(i\omega\bm{C}+(i\omega\bm{L})^{-1}+iG\bm{\sigma_{y}}\right)^{-1}.

(144)

We now consider the general case
\bm{L_{c}}=L_{c}\bm{1}+dL_{c}\bm{\sigma_{z}}
,
\bm{C}=C_{0}\bm{1}+dC_{0}\bm{\sigma_{z}}-C_{12}\bm{\sigma_{x}}
and
\bm{L}=L_{0}\bm{1}+dL_{0}\bm{\sigma_{z}}-L_{12}\bm{\sigma_{x}}
. Here
C_{12}
and
L_{12}
are the stray capacitive and inductive couplings respectively between the two internal gyrator modes.
dL_{c}
,
dC_{0}
and
dL_{0}
are due to asymmetries in the circuit. We highlight that
G
is the average of the FENNEC interaction strength on both sides of the gyrator and is therefore insensitive to asymmetries – we only care about impedance matching with the characteristic impedance of the transmission lines.

We assume that
dL_{c}
,
dC_{0}
,
dL_{0}
,
C_{12}
and
L_{12}
are much smaller than
G
and Taylor expand the perturbed impedance and scattering matrix
\bm{Z}^{\prime}(\omega)
and
\bm{S}^{\prime}(\omega)
to leading order in those quantities, i.e.
\bm{Z}^{\prime}(\omega)=\bm{Z}(\omega)+d\bm{Z}(\omega)
and
\bm{S}^{\prime}(\omega)=\bm{S}(\omega)+d\bm{S}(\omega)
where

d\bm{Z}(\omega)=i\omega dL_{c}\bm{\sigma_{z}}-\frac{i\omega dC_{0}-dL_{0}/(iL_{0}^{2}\omega)}{Z_{0}^{-2}(\omega)+G^{2}}\bm{\sigma_{z}}+\frac{i\omega C_{12}-L_{12}/(iL_{0}^{2}\omega)}{Z_{0}^{-2}(\omega)+G^{2}}\bm{\sigma_{x}},

(145)

and therefore

d\bm{S}(\omega)=-\left(\bm{1}-\bm{S}(\omega)\right)\cdot\frac{d\bm{Z}(\omega)}{2Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}\cdot\left(\bm{1}-\bm{S}(\omega)\right).

(146)

Recall that

\bm{S}(\omega)=\cos(2\theta_{\omega})\bm{1}+i\sin(2\theta_{\omega})\bm{\sigma_{y}},

(147)

and therefore we find that

\begin{split}d\bm{S}(\omega)=\frac{1-\cos(2\theta_{\omega})}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}\left(i\omega dL_{c}\bm{\sigma_{z}}-\frac{i\omega dC_{0}-dL_{0}/(iL_{0}^{2}\omega)}{Z_{0}^{-2}(\omega)+G^{2}}\bm{\sigma_{z}}+\frac{i\omega C_{12}-L_{12}/(iL_{0}^{2}\omega)}{Z_{0}^{-2}(\omega)+G^{2}}\bm{\sigma_{x}}\right).\end{split}

(148)

At central frequency
\omega_{0}=1/\sqrt{L_{0}C_{0}}
, where

, and at perfect impedance matching
\theta_{\omega}=\pi/4
we observe that

\begin{split}d\bm{S}(\omega)=\frac{1}{Z_{\mathrm{TL}}}\left(i\omega dL_{c}\bm{\sigma_{z}}-\frac{i\omega_{0}dC_{0}-dL_{0}/(iL_{0}^{2}\omega_{0})}{G^{2}}\bm{\sigma_{z}}+\frac{i\omega_{0}C_{12}-L_{12}/(iL_{0}^{2}\omega_{0})}{G^{2}}\bm{\sigma_{x}}\right).\end{split}

(149)

Frequency mismatches, due to disorder in the circuit design, yield a

error in

, which mostly affects reflection, whereas stray couplings result in a
\bm{\sigma_{x}}
error, which instead mostly impacts transmission. We get the constraints

\displaystyle|\omega_{0}dL_{c}/Z_{\mathrm{TL}}|,\quad|\omega_{0}dC_{0}/Z_{\mathrm{TL}}G^{2}|,\quad|dL_{0}/(Z_{\mathrm{TL}}L_{0}^{2}\omega_{0}G^{2})|,\quad|\omega_{0}C_{12}/Z_{\mathrm{TL}}G^{2}|,\quad|L_{12}/(Z_{\mathrm{TL}}L_{0}^{2}\omega_{0}G^{2})|\ll 1.

Generic Lagrangian

We consider a generic circuit with total Lagrangian of the circuit is
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{0}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{c}p}

is the Lagrangian all the capacitive and inductive contributions commonly found in superconducting circuits and

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{i}nt}=-\varepsilon_{J,1}(\dot{\Phi}_{2},\varphi_{1})-\varepsilon_{J,2}(\dot{\Phi}_{1},\varphi_{2}),\quad\varepsilon_{J,k}(\dot{\Phi}_{\ell},\varphi_{k})=-\Delta_{k}\sqrt{1-T_{k}(V_{J,k},\dot{\Phi}_{\ell})\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\varphi_{k}-\varphi_{\mathrm{e}x,k}}{2}\right)},\end{split}

(152)

results from the FENNEC interaction alone. Here
\bm{\Phi}=\left(\Phi_{1},\ \Phi_{2}\right)
is a vector comprising the branch flux
\Phi_{1}
(
\Phi_{2}
) of the first (second) mode,
\bm{\varphi}=2\pi\bm{\Phi}/\Phi_{0}
are the associated branch phases,
\bm{C_{0}}
and
\bm{C_{c}}
are capacitance matrices due to the shunt capacitors and the coupling capacitors respectively,
\bm{C_{J}}
is the capacitance matrix associated with the coupling to the control voltage lines
\bm{V_{J}}
,

is the potential energy of the two modes defined by the shaded regions in the circuit,
\Delta_{1}
(
\Delta_{2}
) is the ABS energy of the small junction of mode 1 (mode 2),

(

) is the transmission probability of the small junction of mode 1 (mode 2),

(

Taylor-expanded form

We will now simplify the interaction Lagrangian. First we do a Taylor expansion in

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{i}nt}=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\dot{\Phi}_{2}^{n}}{n!}\frac{\partial^{n}\varepsilon_{J,1}(\dot{\Phi}_{2},\varphi_{1})}{\partial\dot{\Phi}_{2}^{n}}\Big|_{\dot{\Phi}_{2}=0}-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\dot{\Phi}_{1}^{n}}{n!}\frac{\partial^{n}\varepsilon_{J,2}(\dot{\Phi}_{1},\varphi_{2})}{\partial\dot{\Phi}_{1}^{n}}\Big|_{\dot{\Phi}_{1}=0}\end{split}

Second we also do a Taylor expansion in

(158)

In virtue of Hamilton’s principle, the canonical charges,

(161)

Let’s consider

(165)

(166)

\displaystyle\bm{X_{1}}=-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\bm{C}^{-1}(\bm{\varphi})\cdot\bm{g_{n+1}}(\bm{\varphi})\cdot\frac{\bm{X_{0}}^{\circ n}}{n!},

(167)

\displaystyle\bm{X_{2}}=-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\bm{C}^{-1}(\bm{\varphi})\cdot\bm{g_{n+1}}(\bm{\varphi})\cdot\frac{\bm{X_{0}}^{\circ(n-1)}}{(n-1)!}\circ\bm{X_{1}}

(168)

\displaystyle\bm{X_{3}}=-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\bm{C}^{-1}(\bm{\varphi})\cdot\bm{g_{n+1}}(\bm{\varphi})\cdot\left(\frac{\bm{X_{0}}^{\circ(n-1)}}{(n-1)!}\circ\bm{X_{2}}+\frac{\bm{X_{0}}^{\circ(n-2)}}{(n-2)!}\circ\frac{\bm{X_{1}}^{\circ 2}}{2!}\right)

(169)

\displaystyle\cdots

(170)

We emphasize that

A note on Haag duality
Alan Garbarz, Gabriel Palau

Abstract
Haag duality is a remarkable property in QFT stating that the commutant of the algebra of observables localized in some region of spacetime is exactly the algebra associated to the causally disconnected region. It is a strong condition on the local structure and has direct consequences on entanglement measures. It was first shown to hold for a free scalar field and causal diamonds by Araki in 1964 and later by many authors in different ways. In particular, Eckmann and Osterwalder (EO) used Tomita-Takesaki modular theory to give a direct proof. However, it is not straightforward to relate this proof to the works of Araki, since they rely on two forms of the canonical commutation relations (CCR), called Segal and Weyl formulations, while EO work as starting point assumes that duality holds in the so-called “first quantization” in the Weyl formulation. It is our purpose to first introduce the works of Araki in a more easy-to-read but still rigorous and self-contained fashion, and show how Haag duality is stated in the Segal and Weyl formulations and in both first and second quantizations (and their immediate combination). This permits to understand the setting of the EO proof of Haag duality. There is nothing essentially new in this manuscript, with the exception of what we consider a simplification of EO proof that uses the adjoint S ∗ S^{*} of the Tomita operator S S instead of introducing several auxiliary operators. We hope this note will be useful for those seeking to understand where Haag duality comes from in a free scalar QFT. E-mails: alan@df.uba.ar, gpalau@df.uba.ar

Appendix C
More on the initial conditions of the Klein-Gordon equation

Given the real function
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\subset\mathfrak{F}_{\pi}
and
g\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\subset\mathfrak{F}_{\varphi}
, in what follows we are going to seek
h\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{4},\mathbb{R})
such that
F_{E(h)}
is a smooth function that solves the following system

\text{KG initial-value problem:}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\square+m^{2})F_{E(h)}(x)=0\\
F_{E(h)}(0,\vec{x})=f(\vec{x})\\
\frac{\partial F_{E(h)}}{\partial x^{0}}(0,\vec{x})=g(\vec{x})\end{array}\right.

(77)

We follow
[
16
]
, where in Corollary 1.1 is stated the relation between initial conditions and the solution. Suspecting that
E
in that reference can only differ in a global sign with the operator
\Delta
we have been using, we believe
h(x)
should be
-\rho^{\prime}_{0}(f)(x)+\rho^{\prime}_{1}(g)(x)
. Before showing this is the solution, let us briefly describe the maps
\rho_{0}
and
\rho_{1}
and their corresponding pull-backs
\rho^{\prime}_{0}
and
\rho^{\prime}_{1}
.

Let
\rho_{0}:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{4})\to\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be the restriction to
x^{0}=0
and
\rho_{1}:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{4})\to\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
the derivative with respect to
x^{0}
evaluated on the
x^{0}=0
hypersurface. The pull-backs of these maps act by precomposition in the space of tempered distributions
\rho^{\prime}_{0}:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{\prime}\to\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{4})^{\prime}
, namely
\rho^{\prime}_{0}(v)=v\circ\rho_{0}
for any
v\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{\prime}
. Similarly for
\rho_{1}^{\prime}
. We can view the initial conditions as elements of the dual space
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{\prime}
, since
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\subset\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{\prime}
.

Given,
h\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
and the initial condition
g
in (
77
),

(\rho_{0}^{\prime}(g))(h)=(g\circ\rho_{0})(h)=g(h|_{x^{0}=0})=\int g(\vec{x})h(0,\vec{x})d^{3}x=\int h(x)\delta(x^{0})g(\vec{x})d^{4}x,

from where we read
\rho_{0}^{\prime}(g)(x)=\delta(x^{0})g(\vec{x})
. Similarly, with the other initial condition and
\rho_{1}^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle(\rho_{1}^{\prime}(f))(h)=(f\circ\rho_{1})(h)=f\left(\frac{\partial h}{\partial x^{0}}(0,\vec{x})\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(\vec{x})\frac{\partial h}{\partial x^{0}}(0,\vec{x})d^{3}x,

(78)

so we see that
\rho_{1}^{\prime}(f)=-\delta^{\prime}(x^{0})f(\vec{x})
, where
′
means weak derivative. Now we define the distribution

p(x)=-\delta(x^{0})g(\vec{x})-\delta^{\prime}(x^{0})f(\vec{x}),

(79)

and follow the steps of section
5.4
, constructing
p_{s}
as in equation (
73
) and defining an smooth function

h(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi(s)p_{s}(x)ds,

(80)

as in equation (
74
) in order to obtain our solution
F_{E(h)}(x)
, with
h
a smooth function.

We can now show that
F_{E(h)}
is the solution to (
77
) given by (
2
) with
h
as in (
80
). That it is a solution is immediate since we are propagating with the causal Klein-Gordon propagator
\Delta
. Let us then evaluate
F_{E(h)}
at
x^{0}=0
,

\displaystyle F_{E(h)}(0,\vec{x})=\frac{-i}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{i\vec{p}.\vec{x}}\delta(p^{2}-m^{2})\sgn(p^{0})\mathscr{F}(h)(p)d^{4}p

\displaystyle=\frac{-i}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{i\vec{p}.\vec{x}}\delta(p^{2}-m^{2})\theta(p^{0})\mathscr{F}(h)(p)d^{4}p+\frac{i}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{i\vec{p}.\vec{x}}\delta(p^{2}-m^{2})\theta(-p^{0})\mathscr{F}(h)(p)d^{4}p,

\displaystyle=\frac{-i}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i\vec{p}.\vec{x}}\mathscr{F}(h)(\omega_{p},\vec{p})\frac{d^{3}p}{2\omega_{p}}+\frac{i}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i\vec{p}.\vec{x}}\mathscr{F}(h)(-\omega_{p},\vec{p})\frac{d^{3}p}{2\omega_{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{-i}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i\vec{p}.\vec{x}}\frac{\mathscr{F}(h)(\omega_{p},\vec{p})-\mathscr{F}(h)^{\ast}(\omega_{p},-\vec{p})}{2}\frac{d^{3}p}{\omega_{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i\vec{p}.\vec{x}}\frac{1}{i\omega_{p}}\mathscr{F}(h_{-})(\omega_{p},\vec{p})d^{3}p=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i\vec{p}.\vec{x}}\mathscr{F}(\delta_{1}h_{-})(\vec{p})d^{3}p

\displaystyle=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\mathscr{F}(\delta_{1}h_{-})(\vec{x})=\delta_{1}h_{-}(\vec{x}).

And at the same time this should be equal to the initial condition

\displaystyle F_{E(h)}(0,\vec{x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{-1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sin(\omega_{p}(x^{0}-y^0)-\vec{p}.(\vec{x}-\vec{y}))\frac{d^{3}p}{\omega_{p}}h(y)d^{4}y\bigg|_{x^{0}=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sin(\omega_{p}y^{0})\cos(\vec{p}(\vec{x}-\vec{y}))\frac{h(y)}{\omega_{p}}d^{3}pd^{4}y

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sin(\omega_{p}y^{0})\cos(\vec{p}(\vec{x}-\vec{y}))\frac{-\delta(y^{0})g(\vec{y})-\delta^{\prime}(y^{0})f(\vec{y})}{\omega_{p}}d^{3}pd^{4}y

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sin(\omega_{p}y^{0})\cos(\vec{p}(\vec{x}-\vec{y}))\frac{\delta^{\prime}(y^{0})f(\vec{y})}{\omega_{p}}d^{3}pd^{4}y

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\cancel{\omega_{p}}\cos(\omega_{p}y^{0})\cos(\vec{p}(\vec{x}-\vec{y}))\frac{\delta(y^{0})f(\vec{y})}{\cancel{\omega_{p}}}d^{3}pd^{4}y

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\cos(\vec{p}(\vec{x}-\vec{y}))f(\vec{y})d^{3}pd^{3}y

\displaystyle=\real\left\{\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i\vec{p}(\vec{x}-\vec{y})}f(\vec{y})d^{3}pd^{3}y\right\}

\displaystyle=\real\left\{\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left[\mathscr{F}(f)\right](\vec{x})\right\}=\real\left[f(\vec{x})\right]=f(\vec{x})

.

Appendix D
Generic position of subspaces

The aim of this Appendix is to show that the subspaces of
\mathfrak{F}_{\varphi}
defined in the first quantization map (
34
) are in generic position, namely that,

\displaystyle\mathsf{S}_{R}(B)\cap\mathsf{S}_{I}(B)

\displaystyle=\{0\}

\displaystyle\mathsf{S}_{R}(B)\cap\mathsf{S}_{I}(B)^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\{0\}

\displaystyle\mathsf{S}_{R}(B)^{\perp}\cap\mathsf{S}_{I}(B)

\displaystyle=\{0\}

\displaystyle\mathsf{S}_{R}(B)^{\perp}\cap\mathsf{S}_{I}(B)^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\{0\}.

In order to show the second and third lines, it is enough to prove

\mathsf{S}_{R}(B)^{\perp}\vee\mathsf{S}_{I}(B)=\mathfrak{F}_{\varphi}
and
\mathsf{S}_{R}(B)\vee\mathsf{S}_{I}(B)^{\perp}=\mathfrak{F}_{\varphi}
(where
A\vee B=\overline{A+B}
). The former is deduced by first noticing that
\mathsf{S}_{R}(B)^{\perp}\vee\mathsf{S}_{I}(B)=\mathsf{S}_{I}(B^{c})\vee\mathsf{S}_{I}(B)
thanks to Theorem
4.1
, and then by definition of
\mathsf{S}_{I}
one concludes
\mathsf{S}_{R}(B)^{\perp}\vee\mathsf{S}_{I}(B)=\bar{\beta}(j_{1}^{-1}(L^{2}(B^{c}))\vee j_{1}^{-1}(L^{2}(B)))=\bar{\beta}j_{1}^{-1}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))=\bar{\beta}\mathfrak{F}_{\pi}=\mathfrak{F}_{\varphi}
. As for
\mathsf{S}_{R}(B)\vee\mathsf{S}_{I}(B)^{\perp}=\mathfrak{F}_{\varphi}
, it can be shown analogously by using the other relation in Theorem
4.1
.
Regarding the remaining two intersections above, we need the following result.

Lemma D.1

(Lemma 5 in
[
4
]
)
.

If
f\in\mathsf{S}_{R}(B)
in not zero, then
\bar{\beta}f
never vanishes in the entire neighborhood of any point in
\left(supp(f)\right)^{c}
.

In order to prove this, we consider the function
\displaystyle{F(z)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i(\vec{p}\cdot\vec{z}-\omega_{p}z^{0})}\mathscr{F}(f)(\vec{p})\omega^{-1}_{p}d^{3}p}
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
it is a solution of Klein-Gordon equation
(\square_{x}+m^{2})F(x)=0
with initial conditions
F(0,\vec{x})=(\bar{\beta}f)(\vec{x})
and
\displaystyle{\frac{\partial F}{\partial x^{0}}}(0,\vec{x})=-if(\vec{x})
. Moreover,
F(z)
is analytic for
\imaginary z
in the past light cone, since the integrand is analytic and the integral converges. Now, let us consider a point
\vec{y}\in supp(f)^{c}
and assume that
\bar{\beta}f
vanishes in any real three-dimensional neighborhood of
\vec{y}
. Then, since
f
also vanishes there, the solution to the Klein-Gordon equation
F(x)
vanishes on the real four-dimensional nieghborhood of
(0,\vec{y})
. Then, by the identity theorem,
F(z)
vanishes identically, implying
f(x)=0
.

We can now show that
\mathsf{S}_{R}(B)\cap\mathsf{S}_{I}(B)=\{0\}
. This is equivalent to
\bar{\beta}\mathsf{S}_{R}(B)\cap\bar{\beta}\mathsf{S}_{I}(B)=\{0\}
, since
\bar{\beta}
is unitary. Let us consider then some
g\in\bar{\beta}\mathsf{S}_{R}(B)\cap\bar{\beta}\mathsf{S}_{I}(B)
. As
g\in\bar{\beta}\mathsf{S}_{I}(B)=j_{1}^{-1}L_{2}(B)
,
g
must vanish in all
B^{c}
, but at the same time, by the previous lemma and the fact that
g\in\bar{\beta}\mathsf{S}_{R}(B)
,
g
cannot be zero in an entire neighborhood of any point in
B^{c}
unless it vanishes identically. Therefore
\bar{\beta}\mathsf{S}_{R}(B)\cap\bar{\beta}\mathsf{S}_{I}(B)=\{0\}
. As for the remaining equality
\mathsf{S}_{R}(B)^{\perp}\cap\mathsf{S}_{I}(B)^{\perp}=\{0\}
, we can apply Theorem
4.1
in order to write it as
\mathsf{S}_{I}(B^{c})\cap\mathsf{S}_{R}(B^{c})=\{0\}
which is equivalent to the first one, which we just proved, exchanging
B
with
B^{c}
.

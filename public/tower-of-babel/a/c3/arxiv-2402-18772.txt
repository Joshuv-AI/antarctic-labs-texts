Sparsity of stable primes for dynamical sequences
Joachim König

Abstract
. We show that a dynamical sequence ( f n ) n ∈ ℕ (f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}} of polynomials over a number field whose set of stable primes is of positive density must necessarily have a very restricted, and in particular virtually pro-solvable dynamical Galois group. Together with existing heuristics, our results suggest moreover that a polynomial f f all of whose iterates are irreducible modulo a positive density subset of the primes must necessarily be a composition of linear functions, monomials and Dickson polynomials.

4.
A conditional result

Finally, we will address Question

.
The following question is asked in a much broader context in

]

Question 4.1
.

Let
\kappa
be a number field and

be a polynomial of degree
\geq 2
. Let

(with

is finite whenever none of the following hold?

1)

a
lies in the forward orbit of some ramification point of
f
.

2)

a
is fixed by some nonidentity map
\psi:\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\kappa}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\kappa}

.

Note that Case 2) above includes the case of

Theorem 4.2
.

Assuming that Question
4.1
has a positive answer, it follows that for any polynomial

which is not a composition of monomials, Dickson polynomials and linear polynomials, and for all
a\in\kappa
, the set of stable primes of

.

Proof.

Note first that a point
a
lying in the forward orbit of some ramification point of
f
leads to

being inseparable for some, and hence all sufficiently large
n
, and hence there are no stable primes at all for
(f,a)
in this case. Regarding Condition 2) in Question
4.1
, we divide the investigation into the case
\deg(\psi)=1
and
\deg(\psi)>1

[
22
]
; see also Theorem 1.2 and the following discussion in
[
20
]
) that a polynomial (here,

) commuting with some rational function
\psi

>1
must either be linearly related to a monomial or Dickson polynomial, or must have a common iterate with
\psi
.
Since we have excluded the former, it suffices to consider the case were

for suitable

. But then Condition 2) implies that

Lastly, assume we are in Condition 2), but with

of

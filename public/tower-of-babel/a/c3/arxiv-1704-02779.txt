Line-soliton and rational solutions to (2+1)-dimensional Boussinesq equation by Dbar-problem
Junyi Zhu

Abstract
We present a generalized (2+1)-dimensional Boussinesq equation, including two cases which are called the plus Boussinesq equation and the minus one. To investigate these equations, we apply the ∂ ¯ \bar{\partial} approach to a coupled (2+1)-dimensional nonlinear equation, which reduces to the Boussinesq equation. For the plus equation, we give the line-solitons and rational solutions, for the minus one, we give some freak solutions. Keywords : Boussinesq equation, Dbar-problem, line-soliton,rational solution



1
Introduction

The method based on the
\bar{\partial}
(Dbar)-problem
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
is a powerful tool to investigate the integrability of nonlinear PDEs ,
especially higher dimensional equations, and to find their explicit solutions, including solitons and rational solutions.
The departure from analyticity of a complex function can be measured by the Dbr derivative, a special case of this departure
is the jump condition for sectionally analytic function in the Riemann-Hilbert problem. However, if the associated eigenfunctions is analytic nowhere,
the Riemann-Hilbert approach fails, but the Dbar approach still works
[
5
]
.

We consider a (2+1)-dimensional coupled nonlinear equation
[
6
]

\displaystyle u_{t}-2u_{y}+\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon u_{xx}-3\varepsilon v_{x}=0,

(1)

\displaystyle v_{t}-2v_{y}-u_{y}+\varepsilon u_{xxx}-uu_{x}-\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon v_{xx}=0,

where
\varepsilon^{2}=\pm 1
. The method we used in this paper is based on
the Dbar-problem in complex
k
plane

\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{k}}\chi(k,\bar{k})=\iint\chi(\lambda,\bar{\lambda})R(\lambda,\bar{\lambda};k,\bar{k}){\rm d}\lambda\wedge{\rm d}\bar{\lambda},

(2)

with canonical normalization
\chi\to 1
at
k\to\infty
. Here the domain of the integration is the complex
k
plane, and will be omitted in the paper.

System (
1
) after elimination of
v(x,y,t)
reduces to a generalized (2+1)-dimensional Boussinesq equation

u_{tt}-4u_{yt}+4u_{yy}-3\varepsilon u_{xy}+\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon^{2}u_{xxxx}-\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon(u^{2})_{xx}=0,

(3)

which can be reduces to the classical Boussinesq equation (see, e.g.
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
).
The Boussinesq equation is integrable by the inverse problem method (see,
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
10
,
15
]
), the Lax pair for this equation
was constructed in
[
16
]
.
We now consider the following form of solution
[
17
,
18
]

u(x,y,t)=\kappa{\rm e}^{ikx-\varepsilon ik^{3}y-\varepsilon(2ik^{3}+\frac{3}{2}k^{2})t},\quad\kappa\ll 1,x,y,k\in{\mathbb{R}},t>0.

(4)

For
\varepsilon=1
, (
4
) is a small amplitude solution, which make the nonlinear term of (
3
) negligible.
In this case, the time frequency of (
4
) is complex, but we get the exponential growth at a rate of about
{\rm e}^{-\frac{3}{2}k^{2}t}
.
while, for
\varepsilon=i
, the amplitude of solution (
4
) is
\kappa{\rm e}^{-3k^{3}t}
, which is ill-posed for
k<0
.
In the following, we call the Boussinesq equation (
3
) with
\varepsilon=1
the ”plus” type, and
\varepsilon=i
be ”minus” type.

We note that Bogdanov and Zakharov investigated the continuous spectrum and soliton solutions for the two type Boussinesq equations
(named ”plus” and ”minus” Boussinesq equations) by using the Dbar-dressing method
[
17
]
.
In that paper, the Boussinesq equation is a dimensional reduction in the framework of the KP hierarchy.
So the associated covariant derivative is related to that of KP equation.
In this paper, the generalized (2+1)-dimensional Boussinesq equation (
3
) is derived from different covariant derivative.

Although the two type equations have different properties, we present a useful method to discuss them at same time.
For the plus Boussinesq equation, we obtain line-soliton and rational solutions by choosing special degenerate kernel of the Dbar problem.
We note that the line-soliton solutions are complexiton solutions
[
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
, which show some periodic motions.
In addition, the rational solution can not be reduced to the lump solution for the plus Boussinesq equation.
For minus equation, we give some explicit solutions which show some strange appearance. Here we call them freak solutions.
It is remarked that the rational solutions for the plus Boussinesq equation also show some strange properties.

In
[
6
]
, the author only studied the case of
\varepsilon=1
of system (
1
)
by Darboux transformation and gave some periodic solutions. In the last part of this paper, we also extend the Darboux transformation
to both cases of the Boussinesq equation, and obtain some new rational solution and freak solution, respectively.
The present equation is different from the known (2+1)-dimensional Boussinesq equations studied in
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, the Dbar dressing method is presented to derive the Lax pair of a coupled (2+1)-dimensional
nonlinear equation system, which can be reduced to the generalized (2+1)-dimensional Boussinesq equation.
In section 3, the explicit solutions, including line-solitons and rational solutions, are given by virtue of different choice of the kernel of the
Dbar problem. We give some discussions in the last section, and present some novel solutions by extending the method used in
[
6
]
.

2
Dbar dressing Method

Since the time frequency of (
4
) is complex, we consider the wave number is
k
, in stead of
ik
, for convenience.
So, we introduce a set of operators
D_{j}
as

D_{1}=\partial_{x}+k,\quad D_{2}=\partial_{y}+\varepsilon k^{3},\quad D_{3}=\partial_{t}+\varepsilon(2k^{3}+\frac{3}{2}k^{2}),

(5)

and the general kernel of the Dbar-problem is

R(\lambda,\bar{\lambda};k,\bar{k})={\rm e}^{\theta(\lambda)}R_{0}(\lambda,\bar{\lambda};k,\bar{k}){\rm e}^{-\theta(k)}.

(6)

Here
R_{0}
is an arbitrary function and

\theta(k)=kx+\varepsilon k^{3}y+\varepsilon(2k^{3}+\frac{3}{2}k^{2})t.

(7)

Suppose that the analytic function
\chi(x,y,t;k)
has the following expansion

\chi(x,y,t;k)=1+\frac{1}{k}\chi^{(-1)}(x,y,t)+\frac{1}{k^{2}}\chi^{(-2)}(x,y,t)+\cdots,\quad k\to\infty.

(8)

It is readily verified that the following two expressions have no singularity at
k\to\infty

\displaystyle D_{2}\chi-\varepsilon D_{1}^{3}\chi+uD_{1}\chi+v\chi=O(\frac{1}{k}),

(9)

\displaystyle D_{3}\chi-2\varepsilon D_{1}^{3}\chi-\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon D_{1}^{2}\chi+2uD_{1}\chi+(u+2v)\chi=O(\frac{1}{k}),

where

u=3\varepsilon\partial_{x}\chi^{(-1)},\quad v=\varepsilon\left(3\partial_{x}^{2}\chi^{(-1)}-\frac{3}{2}\partial_{x}(\chi^{(-1)})^{2}+3\partial_{x}\chi^{(-2)}\right).

(10)

Thus, according to the Liouville’s theorem, we have two linear equations

L_{1}\chi:=D_{2}\chi-\varepsilon D_{1}^{3}\chi+uD_{1}\chi+v\chi=0,

(11)

and

L_{2}\chi:=D_{3}\chi-2\varepsilon D_{1}^{3}\chi-\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon D_{1}^{2}\chi+2uD_{1}\chi+(u+2v)\chi=0.

(12)

It is noted that the second linear equation takes another form

\tilde{L}_{2}\chi:=D_{3}\chi-D_{2}\chi-\varepsilon D_{1}^{3}\chi-\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon D_{1}^{2}\chi+uD_{1}\chi+(u+v)\chi=0.

(13)

Now, if one introduces new function
\psi(x,y,t;k)
by the following transformation

\psi(x,y,t;k)=\chi(x,y,t;k){\rm e}^{\theta(x,y,t;k)},

(14)

then the system (
11
) and (
12
) give rise to

\displaystyle\psi_{y}-\varepsilon\psi_{xxx}+u\psi_{x}+v\psi=0,

(15)

\displaystyle\psi_{t}-2\varepsilon\psi_{xxx}-\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon\psi_{xx}+2u\psi_{x}+(u+2v)\psi=0.

The compatibility condition
\psi_{ty}=\psi_{yt}
of the system (
15
) imply the (2+1)-dimensional nonlinear equation (
1
).

3
Solutions

It is noted that the Dbar-problem (
2
) with the canonical normalization is equivalent to a integral equation

\chi(k,\bar{k})=1+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\iint\left\{\iint\chi(\mu,\bar{\mu})R(\mu,\bar{\mu};\lambda,\bar{\lambda}){\rm d}\mu\wedge{\rm}\bar{\mu}\right\}\frac{{\rm d}\lambda\wedge{\rm d}\bar{\lambda}}{\lambda-k}.

(16)

In the case of the degenerate kernel

R(\mu,\bar{\mu};\lambda,\bar{\lambda})=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}f_{j}(\mu,\bar{\mu})g_{j}(\lambda,\bar{\lambda}),

(17)

the linear integral equation (
16
) is reduced to the linear algebraic system.
Suppose that the solution of problem (
16
) has the asymptotic behaviors (
8
), then

\chi^{(-1)}=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum\limits_{j,l=1}^{N}\xi_{l}(A^{-1})_{lj}\eta_{j}^{0},\quad\chi^{(-2)}=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum\limits_{j,l=1}^{N}\xi_{l}(A^{-1})_{lj}\eta_{j}^{1}

(18)

where

\xi_{l}=\iint f_{l}(\lambda,\bar{\lambda}){\rm d}\lambda\wedge{\rm d}\bar{\lambda},\quad\eta_{j}^{m}=\iint\lambda^{m}g_{j}(\lambda,\bar{\lambda}){\rm d}\lambda\wedge{\rm d}\bar{\lambda},\quad(m=0,1),

(19)

and

A_{lj}=\delta_{lj}+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\iint{\rm d}\mu\wedge{\rm}\bar{\mu}\iint\frac{{\rm d}\lambda\wedge{\rm d}\bar{\lambda}}{\lambda-\mu}g_{l}(\mu,\bar{\mu})f_{j}(\lambda,\bar{\lambda}).

(20)

3.1
Line Solitons

Existing the soliton solution is an important property for the integrable system. To derive the line solitons,
we consider functions
f_{l}
and
g_{j}
in the kernel (
17
) as

f_{l}(\mu,\bar{\mu})=f_{l}{\rm e}^{\theta(\mu)}\delta(\mu-k_{l}),\quad g_{j}(\lambda,\bar{\lambda})=g_{j}{\rm e}^{-\theta(\lambda)}\delta(\lambda-\tilde{k}_{j}),

(21)

where
f_{l},g_{j}
and
k_{l},\tilde{k}_{j}
are constants. Here the function
\theta
is defined by (
7

In this case, from (
19
) and (
20

\xi_{l}=-2if_{l}{\rm e}^{\theta(k_{l})},\quad\eta_{j}^{m}=-2ig_{j}\tilde{k}_{j}^{m}{\rm e}^{-\theta(\tilde{k}_{j})},\quad A_{lj}=\delta_{lj}+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{\eta_{l}^{0}\xi_{j}}{k_{j}-\tilde{k}_{l}}.

(22)

Now, the representations in (
18
) can be rewritten as the following form

\chi^{(-1)}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{\det A^{(a)}}{\det A},\quad\chi^{(-2)}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{\det A^{(b)}}{\det A},

(23)

the block matrix
A^{(a)}
and
A^{(b)}
are defined by

A^{(a)}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&\hat{\xi}\\
\hat{\eta^{0}}^{T}&A\end{matrix}\right),\quad A^{(b)}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&\hat{\xi}\\
\hat{\eta^{1}}^{T}&A\end{matrix}\right),

(24)

where the row vectors
\hat{\xi}
and
\hat{\eta^{m}},(m=0,1)
are defined as

\displaystyle\hat{\xi}

\displaystyle=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\cdots,\xi_{N}),\quad

\displaystyle\hat{\eta^{m}}=(\eta_{1}^{m},\eta_{2}^{m},\cdots,\eta_{N}^{m}).

(25)

Substitution (
23
) into (
10
), we have the explicit solution of the system (
1
).
We note that the representation
u=3\varepsilon\partial_{x}(\chi^{-1})
will give the explicit solution of the generalized (2+1)-dimensional
Boussinesq equation (
3
). In the following, we will not to put stress on it.

Particularly, for
N=1

\chi^{(-1)}=-\frac{\exp(\theta(k_{1})-\theta(\tilde{k}_{1})+a)}{1+\frac{\exp(\theta(k_{1})-\theta(\tilde{k}_{1})+a)}{k1-\tilde{k}_{1}}},\quad\chi^{(-2)}=-\frac{\tilde{k}_{1}\exp(\theta(k_{1})-\theta(\tilde{k}_{1})+a)}{1+\frac{\exp(\theta(k_{1})-\theta(\tilde{k}_{1})+a)}{k1-\tilde{k}_{1}}},

3.2
Rational solutions

To obtain the rational solutions of the equations in (
1
), we choose the kernel as

R_{0}(\mu,\bar{\mu};\lambda,\bar{\lambda})=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2N}f_{j}(\mu)\delta(\mu-k_{j})g_{j}(\lambda)\delta(\lambda-k_{j}),

(31)

where
f_{j}(\mu)
and
g_{j}(\lambda)
are smooth functions. Substituting
R={\rm e}^{\theta(\mu)}R_{0}{\rm e}^{-\theta(\lambda)}
into (
16
), we have
a representation of
\chi(k,\bar{k})

\chi(k,\bar{k})=1+\frac{2i}{\pi}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2N}\frac{\chi_{j}f_{j}g_{j}}{k_{j}-k},

(32)

where
\chi_{j}=\chi(k_{j},\bar{k}_{j}),f_{j}=f_{j}(k_{j}),g_{j}=g_{j}(k_{j})
. For convenience, we choose
f_{i}=\pi/(2i)
and
g_{j}=1
,
then (
32
) reduces to

\chi(k,\bar{k})=1+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2N}\frac{\chi_{j}}{k_{j}-k}.

(33)

We note that
\chi_{j}
can be obtained from (
16
) and (
31
) by letting
k=k_{l}
, that is

\chi_{l}=1+\sum\limits_{j\neq l}^{2N}\frac{\chi_{j}}{k_{j}-k_{l}}-\theta^{\prime}(k_{l})\chi_{l},\quad l=1,2,\cdots,2N.

If one introduce the following matrices

\displaystyle M=\left(\begin{matrix}1+\theta^{\prime}(k_{1})&\frac{1}{k_{1}-k_{2}}&\cdots&\frac{1}{k_{1}-k_{2N}}\\
\frac{1}{k_{2}-k_{1}}&1+\theta^{\prime}(k_{2})&\cdots&\frac{1}{k_{2}-k_{2N}}\\
\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\\
\frac{1}{k_{2N}-k_{1}}&\frac{1}{k_{2N}-k_{2}}&\cdots&1+\theta^{\prime}(k_{2N}),\end{matrix}\right)

(34)

\displaystyle\check{\chi}=(\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\cdots,\chi_{2N}),\quad\check{E}=(1,1,\cdots,1)_{1\times 2N},

\displaystyle\theta^{\prime}(k_{j})=x+3\varepsilon k_{j}^{2}y+\varepsilon(6k_{j}^{2}+3k_{j})t.

then

\check{\chi}=\check{E}M^{-1}.

From (
32
), one find that
\chi(k,\bar{k})
has the following asymptotic behavior

\chi(k,\bar{k})=1+\frac{1}{k}\chi^{(-1)}+\frac{1}{k^{2}}\chi^{(-2)}+\cdots,\quad,k\to\infty,

where

\chi^{(-1)}=-\check{E}M^{-1}\check{E}^{T},\quad\chi^{(-2)}=-\check{k}M^{-1}\check{E}^{T},\quad\check{k}=(k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{2N}).

(35)

We note that the representations of
\chi_{-1}
and
\chi_{-2}
take another forms

\chi^{(-1)}=\frac{\det M^{(a)}}{\det M},\quad\chi^{(-2)}=\frac{\det M^{(b)}}{\det M},

(36)

where
M^{(a)}
and
M^{(b)}
are
(2N+1)\times(2N+1)
matrices

M^{(a)}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&\check{E}\\
\check{E}^{T}&M\end{matrix}\right),\quad M^{(b)}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&\check{k}\\
\check{E}^{T}&M\end{matrix}\right).

(37)

Thus, we obtain the rational solution of the equation (
1
)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=3\partial_{x}\left(\frac{\det M^{(a)}}{\det M}\right),

(38)

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=3\partial_{x}^{2}\left(\frac{\det M^{(a)}}{\det M}\right)-\frac{3}{2}\partial_{x}\left(\frac{\det M^{(a)}}{\det M}\right)^{2}+3\partial_{x}\left(\frac{\det M^{(b)}}{\det M}\right).

For
N=1
, we find

\displaystyle\det M

\displaystyle=[1+\theta^{\prime}(k_{1})][1+\theta^{\prime}(k_{2})]+\frac{1}{(k_{1}-k_{2})^{2}},

\displaystyle\det{M^{(a)}}

\displaystyle=-[2+\theta^{\prime}(k_{1})+\theta^{\prime}(k_{2})],

\displaystyle\det{M^{(b)}}

\displaystyle=1-[k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{2}\theta^{\prime}(k_{1})+k_{1}\theta^{\prime}(k_{2})],

where
\theta^{\prime}(k_{j})
is defined in (
34
).

Particularly, if take

,

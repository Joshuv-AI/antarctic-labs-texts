Equivalent generating pairs of an ideal of a commutative ring
Luc Guyot

Abstract
. Let R R be a commutative ring with identity and let I I be a two-generated ideal of R R . We denote by SL 2 ​ ( R ) \textnormal{SL}_{2}(R) the group of 2 × 2 2\times 2 matrices over R R with determinant 1 1 . We study the action of SL 2 ​ ( R ) \textnormal{SL}_{2}(R) by matrix right-multiplication on V 2 ⁡ ( I ) \operatorname{V}_{2}(I) , the set of generating pairs of I I . Let Fitt 1 ⁡ ( I ) \operatorname{Fitt}_{1}(I) be the second Fitting ideal of I I . Our main result asserts that V 2 ⁡ ( I ) / SL 2 ​ ( R ) \operatorname{V}_{2}(I)/\textnormal{SL}_{2}(R) identifies with a group of units of R / Fitt 1 ⁡ ( I ) R/\operatorname{Fitt}_{1}(I) via a natural generalization of the determinant if I I can be generated by two regular elements. This result is illustrated in several Bass rings for which we also show that SL n ​ ( R ) \textnormal{SL}_{n}(R) acts transitively on V n ⁡ ( I ) \operatorname{V}_{n}(I) for every n > 2 n>2 . As an application, we derive a formula for the number of cusps of a modular group over a quadratic order.

Introduction

Rings are supposed unital and commutative.
The unit group of a ring

R^{\times}
. Let

-module.
We denote by

For

Let

Two generating vectors

Apart from Section
3.1
where

In some applications, we also address the case

For

To introduce further results, let us define the

of a finitely generated

-module

as the exterior product

The module

The

An

Theorem A

(Theorem
3.5
)
.

Let

be a two-generated ideal of a ring

such that
\operatorname{Fitt}_{1}(I)=II^{-1}
, e.g.,

Then the determinant map induces an injection from

into
\operatorname{V}_{1}(\det(I))\simeq(R/\operatorname{Fitt}_{1}(R))^{\times}

We investigate in Section

conditions under which the determinant map induces a bijection. The main outcome is

Corollary B

(Corollary
4.4
)
.

Let

be a ring whose quotients by regular ideals have stable rank

. Let

be an ideal of

Then the determinant map induces a bijection from
\operatorname{V}_{2}(I)/\textnormal{SL}_{2}(R)
onto

.

In the remainder of this introduction, we shall illustrate

in several Bass rings (see Section

In such rings, every ideal can be generated by two elements.

Our first application generalizes a theorem of Maass
[
vdG88
, Proposition I.1.1]
asserting
that the number of cusps of the Hilbert modular group
\text{PSL}_{2}(\mathfrak{O})
, with
\mathfrak{O}
the ring of integers of a
totally real algebraic number field, is equal to the class number of

\mathfrak{O}
. To introduce this result, further definitions are required.

The
ring of mutlipliers

\varrho(I)
of a factional ideal
I
of
R
is the overring
\{x\in K(R)\,|\,xI\subseteq I\}
.
The
ideal class

[I]
of a fractional ideal
I
of
R
is
the equivalence class of
I
for the following relation on fractional ideals:

I\sim J
if there are two regular elements
r,s\in R
such that
rI=sJ
.
We denote by
\operatorname{Cl}(R)
the monoid of the ideal classes of
R
.
We define the
projective line

\mathbb{P}^{1}(K(R))
over
K(R)
as the quotient of
K(R)\times K(R)\setminus\{(0,0)\}

by the equivalence relation defined through:
\mathbf{v}\sim\mathbf{w}
if there are two regular elements
r,s\in R
such that
r\mathbf{v}=s\mathbf{w}
.
There is a natural action of
\textnormal{SL}_{2}(R)
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(K(R))
from the right.
The set of
cusps of
R
is the orbit set
\mathbb{P}^{1}(K(R))/\textnormal{SL}_{2}(R)
.

Corollary C

(Propositions
5.1
and
5.4
)
.

Let
R
be an order in a number field. Then the number of cusps of
R
is

\sum_{[I]\in\operatorname{Cl}(R),\,\mu(I)\leq 2}[(R/\operatorname{Fitt}_{1}(I))^{\times}:\epsilon(I)].

where
\epsilon(I)
is the natural image of
\varrho(I)^{\times}
in
(R/\operatorname{Fitt}_{1}(I))^{\times}
, see Definition
5.3
.

The finiteness of
\operatorname{Cl}(R)
for
R
an order in a number field is a direct consequence of
[
BS66
, Theorem 2.6.3]
.
As a result, such an order has finitely many cusps, which is a special case of
[
Shi63
, Theorem 5]
.
We use Corollary
C
to effectively compute the number of cusps of a quadratic order. To state this result, we shall denote by
\operatorname{Pic}(R)
,
the
Picard group
of
R
, that is, the group of the invertible elements of
\operatorname{Cl}(R)
.

Theorem D
.

Let
\mathfrak{O}
be the ring of integers of a quadratic number field.
Let
f>0
be a rational integer and let
\mathfrak{O}_{f}
be the unique order of index
f
in
\mathfrak{O}
.
Then the number of cusps of
\mathfrak{O}_{f}
is

\sum_{f^{\prime}|f}\frac{\varphi(f/f^{\prime})}{2^{\epsilon(f,f^{\prime})}}|\operatorname{Pic}(\mathfrak{O}_{f^{\prime}})|

where
\varphi
is the Euler totient function and

\epsilon(f,f^{\prime})=1
if
\mathfrak{O}_{f^{\prime}}
has a unit of norm
-1
and
f/f^{\prime}>2
, else
\epsilon(f,f^{\prime})=0
.

By
[
Neu99
, Theorem 12.12]
, we have

|\operatorname{Pic}(\mathfrak{O}_{f^{\prime}})|=\frac{|\operatorname{Pic}(\mathfrak{O})|}{|\mathfrak{O}^{\times}/\mathfrak{O}_{f^{\prime}}^{\times}|}\frac{|(\mathfrak{O}/\mathfrak{O}f^{\prime})^{\times}|}{|(\mathfrak{O}_{f^{\prime}}/\mathfrak{O}f^{\prime})^{\times}|}

and moreover
|\operatorname{Pic}(\mathfrak{O})|
divides
|\operatorname{Pic}(\mathfrak{O}_{f^{\prime}})|
.
Thus
|\operatorname{Pic}(\mathfrak{O})|
, which is the number of cusps of
\mathfrak{O}
, divides the number of cusps of
\mathfrak{O}_{f}
.
Note that the computation of
\epsilon(f,f^{\prime})
is a well-studied and solved problem, see e.g.,
[
Fur59
, Theorem 2]
,
[
Jen62
]
and the seminal work of Rédei
[
R5́3
]
.

Theorem

relies on the computation of
\operatorname{Fitt}_{1}(I)
for an arbitrary ideal

of

.
This computation allows us moreover to describe

\operatorname{V}_{2}(I)/\textnormal{SL}_{2}(R),\textnormal{Aut}_{R}(I)\backslash\operatorname{V}_{2}(I)/\textnormal{SL}_{2}(R)\text{ and }\operatorname{V}_{2}(I)/\textnormal{GL}_{2}(R)

and to show that

acts transitively on

The results of Section

enable us to solve in

a problem in combinatorial group theory,
namely the description of the Nielsen equivalence classes of generating vectors in semi-direct products of the form

We present now an analysis similar to the one of Section

for another Bass domain: the coordinate ring

It is well-known and easy to show that

For this example, the computation of

The following definition is required to state this last result. For

, a ring containing

of

Proposition E

(Lemma

and Proposition

)
.

Let

be a field and let

Let

be an ideal of

Then the following hold.

(i)

\varrho(I)=K[x^{2}]+K[x]x^{f-1}

(ii)

\operatorname{Fitt}_{1}(I)=(R:\varrho(I))=K[x^{2}]x^{n-f}+K[x]x^{n-1}

(iii)

(iv)

The group
\textnormal{SL}_{k}(R)

\operatorname{V}_{k}(I)

Thanks to Corollary

.
iii

with the group
(K[x^{2}]/K[x^{2}]x^{n-f})^{\times}

Final remarks

Within the course of the proofs of Theorem
D
and Proposition
E
, we established a common fact that we couldn’t generalize to arbitrary Bass domains:

\operatorname{Fitt}_{1}(I)=(R:\varrho(I))
for

\operatorname{Fitt}_{1}(I)

lies in a finite set of conductors as

has only finitely many overrings contained in its normalization
[

, Proposition 2.2.

]
.
We also noticed for each Bass ring example in this article the following properties:

•

\operatorname{Fitt}_{1}(\operatorname{Fitt}_{1}(I))=\operatorname{Fitt}_{1}(I)
,

•

\varrho(\operatorname{Fitt}_{1}(I))=\rho(I)
.

Question 1.1
.

To which extent can these observations be generalized?

Notation and background

We assume throughout that
R
is a commutative unital ring and

is a finitely generated
R
-module.
If
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime ideal of

, we denote by
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
the localization of
R
at
\mathfrak{p}
.
Similarly, we denote by
M_{\mathfrak{p}}
its localization at
\mathfrak{p}
, which we identify with
M\otimes_{R}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Given an element
m
in
M
, we abuse notation in denoting also by
m
its image

. If

. If
Rm
and

are two cyclic submodules of

(Rm:Rm^{\prime})
.

R
and

Given two

\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})\in M^{n}

, we denote by
\mathbf{m}^{\top}

\mathbf{m}\mathbf{r}^{\top}=\mathbf{r}\mathbf{m}^{\top}\Doteq\sum_{i}r_{i}m_{i}

and

{1_{n}}
the

.

2.1.
Fitting ideals and the determinant module

The determinant map is the invariant on which our study of
\operatorname{V}_{2}(M)/\textnormal{SL}_{2}(R)
hinges.
This map can be effectively computed by means of the second Fitting ideal.

Fitting ideals

Let

F\xrightarrow{\varphi}G\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0

be an exact sequence such that
F
and
G
are free modules over
R
and
G
is finitely generated. Let
I_{i}(\varphi)
the ideal of

R
generated by the
i\times i
minors of the matrix of
\varphi:F\rightarrow G
, agreeing that
I_{0}(\varphi)=R
.
Then the
(i+1)
-th Fitting ideal of
M
is defined as
\operatorname{Fitt}_{i}(M)\Doteq I_{\mu(G)-i}(\varphi)
.
The Fitting ideals are independent of
\varphi
by Fitting’s lemma
[
Eis95
, Corollary 20.4]
.
They commute with
base change
, i.e,
\operatorname{Fitt}_{i}(M\otimes_{R}S)=\operatorname{Fitt}_{i}(M)\otimes_{R}S
for any
R
-algebra
S

[
Eis95
, Corollary 20.5]
, and hence with localization.

The determinant of a finitely generated module

Let
\mu=\mu(M)
.
Recall that the determinant
\det(M)
of
M
is the exterior product
\bigwedge^{\mu}M
. This is a cyclic
R
-module whose annihilator is
\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mu-1}(M)

[
Eis95
, Exercise 20.9.i]
.
Given
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{\mu})\in\operatorname{V}_{\mu}(M)
, we denote by
\phi_{\mathbf{m}}:\det(M)\rightarrow R/\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mu-1}(M)
the
R
-isomorphism induced by the map
m_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge m_{\mu}\rightarrow 1+\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mu-1}(M)
. Given another generating pair
\mathbf{m}^{\prime}=(m_{1}^{\prime},\dots,m_{\mu}^{\prime})\in M^{\mu}
, we define

\text{det}_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{m}^{\prime})\Doteq\phi_{\mathbf{m}}(m_{1}^{\prime}\wedge\dots\wedge m_{\mu}^{\prime}).

It is easily checked that the image of
\operatorname{V}_{\mu}(M)
under
\det_{\mathbf{m}}
is a subgroup of
(R/\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mu-1}(M))^{\times}
which does not depend on the choice of
\mathbf{m}
.

The following lemma is straightforward.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m}^{\prime},\mathbf{m}^{\prime\prime}\in\operatorname{V}_{\mu}(M)
.
Then the following assertions hold.

(i)

\det_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{m}^{\prime}A)=\det(A)\det_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{m}^{\prime})
, for every
\mu\times\mu
matrix
A
over
R
.

(ii)

\det_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{m}^{\prime\prime})=\det_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{m}^{\prime})\det_{\mathbf{m}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{m}^{\prime\prime})
.

(iii)

\det_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{m}^{\prime})=1+\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mu-1}(M)
, if and only if,
\det_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{m}^{\prime})=1+\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mu-1}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})

for every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
, where
\mathbf{m}
and
\mathbf{m}^{\prime}
denote (abusively) their natural images in
\operatorname{V}_{\mu-1}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})
in the left-hand side of the identity.

\square

When there is no risk of ambiguity, we simply denote by
1
the identity element of
R/\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mu-1}(M)
.
The ideal
\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mu-1}(M)
has a convenient description which makes the computation of
\det_{\mathbf{m}}

effective when a workable presentation of
M
is given. Given
\mathbf{m}\in\operatorname{V}_{\mu}(M)
, we say that an element
r
is
involved in a relation of
\mathbf{m}

if there is
(r_{1},\dots,r_{\mu})\in R^{\mu}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{\mu}r_{i}m_{i}=0
and
r=r_{i}
for some
i
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathbf{m}\in\operatorname{V}_{\mu}(M)
. Then
\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mu-1}(M)
is the set of the elements of
R
involved in a relation of
\mathbf{m}
.

\square

Given
\mathbf{m}\in\operatorname{V}_{\mu}(M)
, let
\overline{\mathbf{m}}=\pi(\mathbf{m})
be the image of
\mathbf{m}\in V_{\mu}(M)
by the natural map
\pi:M\rightarrow M/\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mu-1}(M)M

and let
\mathbf{e}
be the canonical basis of
(R/\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mu-1}(M))^{\mu}
. Then the map
\overline{\mathbf{m}}\mapsto\mathbf{e}
induces an isomorphism
\varphi_{\mathbf{m}}
from
M/\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mu-1}(M)M
onto
(R/\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mu-1}(M))^{\mu}
.
The following lemma shows how the map
\pi
can be used to compute
\det_{\mathbf{m}}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{V}_{\mu}(M)
. Then
\det_{\mathbf{m}}(\mathbf{m}^{\prime})
is the determinant of the unique
\mu\times\mu
matrix
\overline{A}
over
R/\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mu-1}(M)

satisfying
\pi(\mathbf{m}^{\prime})=\pi(\mathbf{m})\overline{A}
, that is the matrix of
\varphi_{\mathbf{m}}\circ\pi(\mathbf{m}^{\prime})
with respect to
\mathbf{e}
.

\square

2.2.
Ranks

Following
[
MR87
, Section 6.7.2]
, we define the Bass stable rank of a finitely generated
R
-module
M
.
An integer
n>0
lies in the
stable range of

M
if for every
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n+1})\in\operatorname{V}_{n+1}(M)
, there is
r\in R
such that
(m_{1}+rm_{n+1},\dots,m_{n}+rm_{n+1})
belongs to
\operatorname{V}_{n}(M)
.
If
n
lies in the stable range of
M
, then so does
k
for every
k>n

[
MR87
, Lemma 11.3.3]
.
The
stable rank
\textnormal{sr}(M)
of
M
is the least integer in the stable range of
M
.
Extending naturally the linear rank introduced in
[
MR87
, Section 11.3.3]
to finitely generated modules, we define the
linear rank
\textnormal{glr}(M)
of
M

as the least integer
n\geq\mu(M)
such that
\textnormal{GL}_{k}(R)
, acts transitively on
\operatorname{V}_{k}(M)
for every
k>n
.
Note that the analogous definition based on
\textnormal{SL}_{k}(R)
yields also the rank
\textnormal{glr}(M)
.
It is easily checked that
\textnormal{SL}_{2}(R)
acts transitively on
\operatorname{V}_{2}(R)
. Hence
\textnormal{glr}(R)=2
is equivalent to

\textnormal{glr}(R)=1

Lemma 3.6

Let

Lemma 3.7

Proof.

Proof of Theorem

Let

Proposition 6.8
.

Let

be a non-zero ideal of

and let

be the positive rational integer such that

.

(ii)

\operatorname{V}_{2}(I)/\textnormal{GL}_{2}(R)

onto the quotient of
(R/\operatorname{Fitt}_{1}(I))^{\times}
by the image of
R^{\times}

through the natural map

doesn’t act transitively on

Proof.

(ii)
.

Consider now

6.2.
Ideals in the coordinate ring of the curve
x^{2}=y^{n}

This section is dedicated to the proof of Proposition
E
which illustrates
Theorem
A
in the coordinate ring
R
of the curve
x^{2}=y^{n}
over
K
with
n\geq 3
an odd integer and
K
an arbitrary field.
The coordinate ring of an algebraic curve is a Bass domain if and only if the only singularities of the curve are double points
[
Gre82
, Theorem 2.1]
.
This is well-known to hold for
R
.
We identify
R
with
K[x^{2},x^{n}]
, a notation which stays in effect throughout this section.
The integral closure of
R
in its field of fractions is
K[x]
and we have

R=K[x^{2}]+K[x]x^{n-1}=K[x^{2}]+K[x^{2}]x^{n}
and
(R:K[x])=K[x]x^{n-1}
, i.e., the conductor of
R
in
K[x]
is
K[x]x^{n-1}
.

We compute first
\operatorname{Fitt}_{1}(I)
for
I
a non-zero ideal of
R
. This is achieved by means of the next two lemmas.

Lemma 6.9
.

Let
I
be a non-zero ideal of
R
and let
d(x)\in K[x]
be a generator of
K[x]I
. Then there is an even integer
\nu
satisfying
0\leq\nu\leq n-1

and two coprime polynomials
p(x)=\sum_{i}p_{i}x^{i},\,q(x)=\sum_{i}q_{i}x^{i}
of
K[x]
such that the following hold:

(i)

Rp(x)+Rq(x)=\frac{1}{d(x)}I
,

(ii)

p_{0}q_{\nu+1}-p_{1}q_{\nu}\neq 0
if
\nu<n-1
,

(iii)

p_{0}\neq 0
and
q_{i}=0
for every
i<\nu
.

The
x
-valuation
of a polynomial
p(x)\in K[x]
is the greatest integer
v\geq 0
such that
x^{v}
divides
p(x)
.

Proof.

Let
p(x),p^{\prime}(x)
be the quotients of two generators of
I
by
d(x)
. Then
p(x)
and
p^{\prime}(x)
are coprime in
K[x]
. Swapping them if need be, we can suppose that
p(0)\neq 0
.
Let
q(x)\in p(x)+Rp^{\prime}(x)
be a polynomial with maximal
x
-valuation. If
q(x)=0
, the result is obvious. Thus we can assume that
q(x)\neq 0
.
Let
m
be the greatest even integer such that
q_{m}
or
q_{m+1}
is non-zero. Then we have
p_{0}q_{m+1}-p_{1}q_{m}\neq 0
since otherwise

q(x)-\lambda x^{m}p(x)
would have an
x
-valuation greater than the
x
-valuation of
q(x)
for some
\lambda\in K
.
Setting
\nu=\min(m,n-3)
, we easily check that
\nu,p(x)
and
q(x)
satisfy
the conditions
(i)
,
(ii)
and
(iii)
.
∎

Lemma 6.10
.

Let
I
be an ideal of
R
and let
p(x),q(x)
and
\nu
be as in Lemma

.
Then

\operatorname{Fitt}_{1}(I)=K[x^{2}]x^{n-\nu-1}+K[x]x^{n-1}.

\nu

.

Proof.

As

and

are coprime elements of

, it easily follows from Lemma

that
\operatorname{Fitt}_{1}(I)
is
generated by the elements of the form

and
h(x)q(x)
with

such that

(1)

h(x)p(x)\in R,h(x)q(x)\in R.

Hence
\operatorname{Fitt}_{1}(I)
contains the conductor
K[x]x^{n-1}
of

in
K[x]
. Since

R/K[x]x^{n-1}\simeq K[x^{2}]/K[x^{2}]x^{n-1}

is a local principal ideal ring
whose maximal ideal is generated by the image of

, we infer that
\operatorname{Fitt}_{1}(I)=K[x^{2}]x^{\nu^{\prime}}+K[x]x^{n-1}
for some even integer
\nu^{\prime}\geq 0
.
We claim that every polynomial
h(x)
satisfying
(\ref{EqH})
has an
x
-valuation at least

and there is such an

with an
x
-valuation equal to
n-\nu-1
.
It immediately follows from the claim that
\nu^{\prime}=n-\nu-1
, hence the result.

In order to prove the claim, we consider a polynomial
h(x)=\sum_{i}h_{i}x^{i}\in K[x]
.
and let
\mathbf{h}=(h_{0},h_{1},\dots,h_{n-2})

p_{3}&p_{2}&p_{1}&p_{0}&&&\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&&\\
p_{n-2}&p_{n-3}&p_{n-4}&p_{n-5}&\cdots&p_{1}&p_{0}\\
\end{pmatrix}

to

If

which satisfies

Lemma 6.11
.

Let

be an ideal of

and let

be as in Lemma

Then we have

Let us prove first that every proper

\nu^{\prime}>0

Clearly, there are exactly
\frac{n-1}{2}

\frac{n-1}{2}

This follows from the isomorphisms

To complete the proof, we show that

Let

We shall consider an

•

•

be the matrix whose columns are the component vectors of the images in

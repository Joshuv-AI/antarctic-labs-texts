Pointed semibiproducts of monoids
Nelson Martins-Ferreira

Abstract
. Semibiproducts of monoids are introduced here as a common generalization to biproducts (of abelian groups) and to semidirect products (of groups) for exploring a wide class of monoid extensions. More generally, abstract semibiproducts exist in any concrete category over sets in which map addition is meaningful thus reinterpreting Mac Lane’s relative biproducts. In the pointed case they give rise to a special class of extensions called semibiproduct extensions. Not all monoid extensions are semibiproduct extensions but all group extensions are. A categorical equivalence is established between the category of pointed semibiproducts of monoids and the category of pointed monoid action systems, a new category of actions that emerges from the equivalence. The main difference to classical extension theory is that semibiproduct extensions are treated in the same way as split extensions, even though the section map may fail to be a homomorphism. A list with all the 14 semibiproduct extensions of 2-element monoids is provided.

3.
\mathbf{Mag}
-categories and semibiproducts

Let
\mathbf{Mag}

is required to be a natural inclusion. As expected the category

is a
\mathbf{Mag}
-category with

the magma of all maps from

to

. If

is a magma homomorphism from

to

is nothing but

considered as a map between the underlying sets of
A
and
B
. In the same way the categories of groups, abelian groups, monoids and commutative monoids are
\mathbf{Mag}
-categories. However, there is a significant distinction between the Ab-category of abelian groups, the linear category of commutative monoids and the Mag-categories of groups or monoids. If
A
is an object in an Ab-category then

is a ring. If
A
is an object in a linear category then
\hom(A,A)
is a semiring. In contrast, if

is a group (or a monoid) then

is a subset of the near-ring

, whereas

There is an obvious abuse of notation in the previous conditions. This is justified because we will be mostly concerned with the case in which

4.
Stability properties of pointed semibiproducts

From now on the category
\mathbf{C}
is assumed to be either the category of groups or the category of monoids (occasionally we will refer to the category of unitary magmas) and
\mathrm{map}(A,B)
is the magma of zero-preserving maps with component-wise addition.
In each case the category
\mathbf{C}
is pointed and the composition of maps is well defined. It will be convenient to consider
pointed semibiproducts
by requiring two further conditions, namely

\displaystyle pk=0_{X,B},\quad qs=0_{B,X}.

(6)

However, as it is well known, in the case of groups this distinction is irrelevant.

Proposition 1
.

Every semibiproduct of groups is pointed.

Proof.

We have
pk=p1_{A}k=p(kq+sp)k=pkqk+pspk=pk+pk
. And

. Hence we may conclude

and
kqs=0
. Since

is a monomorphism
qs=0
.
∎

The previous proof also shows that a semibiproduct of monoids is pointed as soon as the monoid

admits right cancellation. Clearly, this is not a general fact.

Proof.

Condition

∎

7.
The equivalence

In order to establish a categorical equivalence between
\mathbf{Act}
and
\mathbf{Psb}
we need a procedure to associate a pointed monoid action system to every pointed semibiproduct of monoids in a functorial manner.

Theorem 3
.

Let
(X,A,B,p,k,q,s)
be an object in
\mathbf{Psb}
. The system
(X,B,\rho,\varphi,\gamma)
with

\displaystyle\rho(x,b)=q(k(x)+s(b))

(47)

\displaystyle\varphi(b,x)=q(s(b)+k(x))

(48)

\displaystyle\gamma(b,b^{\prime})=q(s(b)+s(b^{\prime}))

(49)

is an object in
\mathbf{Act}
.
Moreover, if
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})
is a morphism in
\mathbf{Psb}
then
(f_{1},f_{3})
is a morphism in
\mathbf{Act}
.

Proof.

To see that the system
(X,B,\rho,\varphi,\gamma)
is a well defined object in
\mathbf{Act}
we recall that
q
and
s
are zero-preserving maps and hence conditions
(\ref{eq:act01})
–
(\ref{eq:act03})
are satisfied. Conditions
(\ref{eq:act04})
–
(\ref{eq:act06})
are obtained by applying the map
q
to both sides of equations

\displaystyle k(x)+s(b)=kq(k(x)+s(b))+s(b)

\displaystyle s(b)+k(x)=kq(s(b)+k(x))+s(b)

\displaystyle s(b)+s(b^{\prime})=kq(s(b)+s(b^{\prime}))+s(b+b^{\prime})

which hold because
(X,A,B,p,k,q,s)
is a pointed semibiproduct of monoids. Condition
(\ref{eq:act07})
follows from Theorem
1
with
a=k(x)+s(b)
,
a^{\prime}=k(x^{\prime})+s(b^{\prime})+k(x^{\prime\prime})+s(b^{\prime\prime})
on the one hand whereas on the other hand
a=k(x)+s(b)+k(x^{\prime})+s(b^{\prime})
,
a^{\prime}=k(x^{\prime\prime})+s(b^{\prime\prime})
. Moreover, the pair
(f_{1},f_{3})
is a morphism of actions as soon as the triple
(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})
is a morphism of semibiproducts, indeed we have

\displaystyle f_{1}(\rho(x,b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}q(k(x)+s(b))=q^{\prime}f_{2}(k(x)+s(b))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{\prime}(k^{\prime}f_{1}(x)+s^{\prime}f_{3}(b)))=\rho^{\prime}(f_{1}(x),f_{3}(b))

and similarly for
\varphi
and
\gamma
thus proving conditions
(\ref{eq:act08})
–
(\ref{eq:act10})
.
∎

P\colon{\mathbf{Psb}\to\mathbf{Act}}

9.
Conclusion

A new tool has been introduced for the study of monoid extensions from which a new notion of action has emerged in order to establish the categorical equivalence of Theorem
4
. A clear drawback to this approach is the necessity of handling morphisms and maps at the same level.
We have solved the problem by extending the hom-functor through an appropriate profunctor (Definition
1
). Other possible solutions would consider maps as an extra structure in higher dimensions
[
8
,
18
,
19
]
or as imaginary morphisms
[
4
,
23
]
.
Developing further a categorical framework in which to study semibiproducts seems desirable due to several important cases occurring in different settings. For example, semibiproduct extensions can be studied in the context of preordered monoids
[
21
]
and preordered groups
[
9
]
, where the maps
q
and
s
are required to be monotone maps rather than zero-preserving maps. The context of topological monoids
[
12
]
should also be worthwhile studying with
q
and
s
required to be continuous maps.

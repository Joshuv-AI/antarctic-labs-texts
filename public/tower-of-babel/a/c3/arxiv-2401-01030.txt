Spectral conditions for factor-criticality of graphs
Jin Cai, Bo Zhou

Abstract
A graph G G is k k -factor-critical if G − S G-S has a perfect matching for any k k -subset S S of the vertex set of G G . In this paper, we investigate the factor-criticality of graphs with fixed minimum degree and provide sufficient conditions for such graphs to be k k -factor-critical in terms of spectral radius and signless Laplacian spectral radius. Keywords: factor-critical graph, minimum degree, spectral radius, signless Laplacian spectral radius

For any

For two vertex disjoint graphs

denotes the disjoint union of

and
G_{2}

and

by adding all possible edges between any vertex of
G_{1}
and any vertex of
G_{2}
. For positive integer

and a graph

kG
denotes the graphs consisting of
k
vertex disjoint copies of

.
Denote by

the complete graph of order

A perfect matching in a graph is a set

of

are adjacent and every vertex is incident to an edge that belongs to
M
. A graph

-factor-critical
[
5
,
11
]

if

has a perfect matching for any
k
-subset

of

. In particular, a
0
-factor-critical graph is a graph with a perfect matching,
a
1
-factor-critical graph is known as a factor-critical graph, and a

-factor-critical graph is known as a factor-bicritical graph, see
[

,
6
,
8
]
.
By definition, if a graph of order

is

-factor-critical, then

.

It has been a subject of interest to find sufficient conditions for a

-factor-critical graph, see, e.g.
[

,
4
]
. Recently, Zhou et al.
[

]
gave several spectral radius conditions for the existence of

-connected

For positive integers

and

, let

Let

In this paper, we provide sufficient conditions for graphs in

Theorem 1.1

Let

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=x^{3}-(n+k-\delta-3)x^{2}-(n+\delta^{2}-k\delta+k-2)x

\displaystyle\quad-2\delta^{3}+(n+3k-4)\delta^{2}+(n+3k-nk-k^{2}-2)\delta.

Let

\displaystyle g(x)

\displaystyle=x^{3}-(3n-\delta+2k-6)x^{2}

We need some lemmas in the proofs.

By Perron-Frobenius theorem, we have the following lemma.

\displaystyle\quad X^{T}(\mathbf{A}(H^{\prime})-\mathbf{A}(H^{\prime}_{s}))X

Lemma 3.3

\displaystyle x^{3}-(n+k-s-3)x^{2}-(n+s^{2}-ks+k-2)x-2s^{3}

\displaystyle\frac{f_{s}(x)-f_{\delta}(x)}{s-\delta}=h(x)

\displaystyle\quad+(n+3k-4)(s+\delta)+n-k^{2}-nk+3k-2.

\rho(G)\leq\rho(K_{s}\vee((s-k+1)K_{\delta+1-s}\cup K_{n-s-(s-k+1)(\delta+1-s)}))

with equality if and only if
G\cong K_{s}\vee((s-k+1)K_{\delta+1-s}\cup K_{n-s-(s-k+1)(\delta+1-s)})
.
By Lemma
3.1
,

\rho(K_{s}\vee((s-k+1)K_{\delta+1-s}\cup K_{n-s-(s-k+1)(\delta+1-s)}))<\rho(H(n,\delta,k)),

which is a contradiction.

Case 2.

s\geq\delta
.

By Lemmas
2.4
and
2.5
, we have

\rho(G^{\prime})\leq\rho(H_{s})

with equality if and only if
G^{\prime}\cong H_{s}
. Thus

\rho(G)\leq\rho(H_{s})

with equality if and only if
G\cong H_{s}
.
By the assumption,
\rho(G)\geq\rho(H(n,\delta,k))
. Thus

\rho(H(n,\delta,k))\leq\rho(G)\leq\rho(H_{s}).

If
s\geq\delta+1
, then we have by Lemma
3.3
that

\rho(H(n,\delta,k))\leq\rho(H_{s})<\rho(H(n,\delta,k))
, which is a contradiction.
Thus
s=\delta
and
\rho(G)=\rho(H(n,\delta,k))
, so
G\cong H(n,\delta,k)
, which is also a contradiction.

The result follows by Lemma
3.2
.
∎

If
k\geq 1
, then
\delta\geq 2
in Theorem
1.1
, and it is easy to check that the condition on
n

may be weakened to
n\geq 4\delta+1
.

3.2
Signless Laplacian spectral radius

Lemma 3.4
.

For positive integers
\delta,s,k,n
with
n\geq 9\delta-2k+12
,
\delta+1\leq s\leq\frac{n+k-2}{2}
,

q(K_{s}\vee((s-k+1)K_{1}\cup K_{n-2s+k-1}))<q(H(n,\delta,k)).

Proof.

Let
H_{s}=K_{s}\vee((s-k+1)K_{1}\cup K_{n-2s+k-1})
.
Partition
V(H_{s})
into
S\cup V_{1}\cup V_{2}
, where
V_{1}=V((s-k+1)K_{1})
and
V_{2}=V(K_{n-2s+k-1})
.
It is easy to see that this partition is an equitable partition. The corresponding quotient matrix is

\mathbf{B}=\begin{pmatrix}n+s-2&s-k+1&n-2s+k-1\\
s&s&0\\
s&0&2n-3s+2k-4\end{pmatrix}.

By a simple calculation, the characteristic polynomial of
\mathbf{B}
is

\displaystyle f_{s}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{3}-(3n+2k-s-6)x^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+(2n^{2}+sn+2kn-8n-4s^{2}+4(k-1)s-4k+8)x

\displaystyle\quad-2s^{3}+(4n+4k-10)s^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-(2n^{2}+4kn-10n+2k^{2}-10k+12)s.

By Lemma
2.2
,
q(H_{s})
is equal to the largest root of the equation
f_{s}(x)=0
.
Similarly,

q(H(n,\delta,k))
is equal to the largest root of
f_{\delta}(x)=0
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{f_{s}(x)-f_{\delta}(x)}{s-\delta}=h(x)

\displaystyle:=x^{2}+(n-4s-4\delta+4k-4)x

\displaystyle\quad-2(\delta^{2}+\delta s+s^{2})+(4n+4k-10)(s+\delta)

\displaystyle\quad-2n^{2}-4kn+10n-2k^{2}+10k-12.

The symmetry axis of
h(x)
is
x=x_{0}:=-\frac{1}{2}n+2s+2\delta-2k+2<-\frac{1}{2}n+4s-2k+2
.
Since
n\geq 2s-k+2
, we have
2s\leq n+k-2<n+\delta-2
. Thus

\displaystyle x_{0}

\displaystyle<\frac{3}{2}n+2\delta-2k-2

\displaystyle\leq 2n-\frac{1}{2}(9\delta-2k+12)+2\delta-2k-2

\displaystyle<2n-2\delta+2k-4.

It then follows that
h(x)
is strictly increasing for
x\in[2n-2\delta+2k-4,+\infty)
.
As
\delta+1\leq s\leq\frac{n+k-2}{2}
and
n\geq 9\delta-2k+12
, we have

\displaystyle h(x)

\displaystyle\geq h(2n-2\delta+2k-4)

\displaystyle=-2s^{2}-(4n-6\delta+4k-6)s+4n^{2}-(14\delta-14k+18)n+10\delta^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-(20k-30)\delta+10k^{2}-30k+20

\displaystyle\geq-2\left(\frac{n+k-2}{2}\right)^{2}-(4n-6\delta+4k-6)\frac{n+k-2}{2}

\displaystyle\quad+4n^{2}-(14\delta-14k+18)n

\displaystyle\quad+10\delta^{2}-(20k-30)\delta+10k^{2}-30k+20

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}n^{2}+(12k-14\delta-9)n+\frac{25}{2}\delta^{2}-(22k-29)\delta+10k^{2}-26k+12

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3}{2}(9\delta-2k+12)^{2}+(12k-14\delta-9)(9\delta-2k+12)+\frac{25}{2}\delta^{2}-(22k-29)\delta

\displaystyle\quad+10k^{2}-26k+12

\displaystyle=8\delta^{2}+60\delta k+104\delta-8k^{2}+64k+120

\displaystyle>0.

Thus
f_{s}(x)>f_{\delta}(x)
for
x\in[2n-2\delta+2k-4,+\infty)
. By Lemma
2.1
, we have

q(H(n,\delta,k))>q(K_{n-\delta+k-1})=2n-2\delta+2k-4
,
so

f_{s}(q(H(n,\delta,k)))>f_{\delta}(q(H(n,\delta,k)))
,
implying that
q(H_{s})<q(H(n,\delta,k))
.
∎

Now, we prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Suppose contradiction that
G
is not
k
-factor-critical. By Lemma
2.3
, there exists a vertex subset
S\subset V(G)
such that
o(G-S)>|S|-k
, where
|S|\geq k
. Let
|S|=s
. Since
k\equiv n\equiv s+o(G-S)\pmod{2}
, one gets
o(G-S)\equiv k-s\pmod{2}
. Thus
o(G-S)\geq s-k+2
. Since
n\geq s+o(G-S)
, we have
n\geq 2s-k+2
.

Let
t=s-k+2
.
Let
n_{1}\leq\dots\leq n_{t-1}
be the orders
the odd components of
G-S
with the first
t-1
smallest orders, and let
n_{t}=n-s-n_{1}-\dots-n_{t-1}
.
Then
G
is a spanning subgraph of the graph
G^{\prime}:=K_{s}\vee(K_{n_{1}}\cup\dots\cup K_{n_{t}})
.
By Lemma
2.1
,

q(G)\leq q(G^{\prime})

with equality if and only if
G\cong G^{\prime}
. Let

H_{s}=K_{s}\vee((s-k+1)K_{1}\cup K_{n-2s+k-1})
.

Suppose first that
s\leq\delta-1
.
If
s=0
, then
k=0
and
t=2
, so we have
q(G)\leq q(G^{\prime})\leq q(K_{n-\delta-1})<q(H(n,\delta,k))
by Lemma
2.1
, a contradiction. Suppose that
s\geq 1
.
Then
n_{1}-1+s\geq\delta
, i.e.,
n_{1}\geq\delta+1-s
.
By Lemmas
2.4
and
2.5
, we have

q(G)\leq q(G^{\prime})\leq q(K_{s}\vee((s-k+1)K_{\delta+1-s}\cup K_{n-s-(s-k+1)(\delta+1-s)}))

with equality if and only if
G\cong K_{s}\vee((s-k+1)K_{\delta+1-s}\cup K_{n-s-(s-k+1)(\delta+1-s)})
.
By Lemma
3.1
,

q(K_{s}\vee((s-k+1)K_{\delta+1-s}\cup K_{n-s-(s-k+1)(\delta+1-s)}))<q(H(n,\delta,k)),

So
q(G)<q(H(n,\delta,k))
,
which is a contradiction.

Suppose next that
s\geq\delta
. By Lemmas
2.4
and
2.5
, we have

q(G)\leq q(G^{\prime})\leq q(H_{s})
with equality if and only if
G\cong H_{s}
.
By the assumption,
q(H(n,\delta,k))\leq q(G)
. By Lemma
3.4
,
s=\delta
, so
G=H(n,\delta,k)
, which is a contradiction.

The result follows by Lemma
3.2
.
∎

If
k\geq 1
, then
\delta\geq 2
in Theorem
1.2
, and it is easy to check that the condition on
n

may be weakened to

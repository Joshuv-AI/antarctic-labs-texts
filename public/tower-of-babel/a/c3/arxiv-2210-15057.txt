Schrödinger–Newton equation with spontaneous wave function collapse
Lajos Diósi

Abstract
Based on the assumption that the standard Schrödinger equation becomes gravitationally modified for massive macroscopic objects, two independent proposals has survived from the nineteen-eighties. The Schrödinger–Newton equation (1984) provides well-localized solitons for free macro-objects but lacks the mechanism how extended wave functions collapse on solitons. The gravity-related stochastic Schrödinger equation (1989) provides the spontaneous collapse but the resulting solitons undergo a tiny diffusion leading to an inconvenient steady increase of the kinetic energy. We propose the stochastic Schrödinger–Newton equation which contains the above two gravity-related modifications together. Then the wave functions of free macroscopic bodies will gradually and stochastically collapse to solitons which perform inertial motion without the momentum diffusion: conservation of momentum and energy is restored.



I
Introduction

The conjectured validity of quantum theory in the macroscopic world
is famously problematic. This is shown usually on the
elementary example: free motion of an isolated macroscopic mass
M
.
Its center of mass (c.o.m) wave packet is widening eternally
while we would expect a certain stationary localization.
The most spectacular paradox concerns the Schrödinger cat states which are
superpositions of two distant wave packets, legitimate in
the microworld but problematic for a macroscopic body.

A Newtonian semiclassical (mean-field) modification of the Schrödinger equation to ensure
stationary localization was proposed first
Diósi 1984
.
This Schrödinger–Newton equation (SNE) yields well-localized soliton
wave packets for the mass
M
. However, the Schrödinger cat states remain
legitimate solutions and an independent mechanism is required to destroy them —
as stated in ref.
Diósi 1984
.
To this end, another Newton-gravity-related (G-related) mechanism was proposed
in two steps.
The G-related master equation (G-ME) decoheres the cat states
Diósi 1987
.
Its unraveling,
the G-related stochastic Schrödinger equation (G-SSE)
Diósi 1989
collapses them
spontaneously to one
or the other wave packet, and drives this component into a soliton — with one
annoying effect though. The c.o.m. motion of the soliton
never becomes stationary, it remains subject of tiny stochastic fluctuations
generating kinetic energy at constant rate.

Here we propose the combination of the SNE and the
G-SSE, obtaining the new stochastic SNE (SSNE).
We show that the momentum diffusion of solitons of
massive isolated objects get canceled by the interplay between the stochasticity
of the collapse mechanism and the semiclassical Newtonian self-interaction.
The remainder of the stochasticity is merely an extreme small universal
coordinate diffusion proportional to
\hbar/M
.
Our SSNE is partially realizing the concept of ref.
Diósi 2019

where the diffusion effects of a stereotypical collapse equation
[our eq. (
11
) below]
were completely eliminated by imposing frame drag. Also a related vision of
induced gravity was put forward.

Secs.
II
and
III
recapitulate the basics of the SNE and the
G-SSE, invoking the results of
Refs.
Diósi 1984
;
Diósi 1987
;
Diósi 1989
.
The combination of the two equations is introduced in sec.
IV
.
Then sec.
V

proves that the soliton momenta of SSNE
are constant, their diffusion is canceled.
Sec.
VI
contains final remarks.

II
Semiclassical Schrödinger–Newton Equation

Consider the standard Schrödinger equation

d\left|\Psi\right>/dt=-(i/\hbar){\hat{H}}\left|\Psi\right>

of a massive non-relativistic many-body system, with Hamiltonian
{\hat{H}}

not including the Newtonian pair potential. Let
{\hat{\varrho}}(\mathbf{r})
denote the
operator of mass density at location
\mathbf{r}
,
normalized to the total mass
M
. Instead of the Newton pair potential,
one postulates that the classical gravitational is sourced by the
mean-field
\left<{\hat{\varrho}}(\mathbf{s})\right>=\left<\Psi\right|{\hat{\varrho}}(\mathbf{r})\left|\Psi\right>
.
The semiclassical SNE reads

\displaystyle\frac{d\left|\Psi\right>}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{\hbar}{\hat{H}}\left|\Psi\right>+\frac{i}{\hbar}G\int\int\frac{{\hat{\varrho}}(\mathbf{r})\left<{\hat{\varrho}}(\mathbf{s})\right>d\mathbf{r}d\mathbf{s}}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{s}|}\left|\Psi\right>

(1)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{\hbar}({\hat{H}}+{\hat{V}}_{\Psi})\left|\Psi\right>,

where
{\hat{V}}_{\Psi}
is the
\Psi
dependent Newtonian
semiclassical (mean-field) interaction.

The SNE possesses soliton solutions for the c.o.m. wave function of isolated bodies.
Consider the motion of a single spherical symmetric rigid mass
M
,
the canonical position and momentum operators are
\hat{\mathbf{x}},\hat{\mathbf{p}}
respectively.
The mass density operator can be written as

{\hat{\varrho}}(\mathbf{r})=Mf(\mathbf{r}-\hat{\mathbf{x}})

(2)

where
f
is a normalized non-negative spherical symmetric function.
Let us introduce the frequency parameter
\omega_{G}

as defined in ref.
Diósi 2007
:

\omega_{G}^{2}=\frac{4\pi}{3}GM\int f^{2}(\mathbf{r})d\mathbf{r}.

(3)

This sets an effective strength of Newtonian self-attraction
(of the G-related spontaneous collapse, too, cf.
secs.
III
-
V
)
when the position uncertainty
\Delta\mathrm{x}
in state
\left|\Psi\right>
is
much smaller than the characteristic length scale(s) of
f(\mathbf{r})

not
.
Then, ignoring higher (than 2nd) order terms in
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{c}=\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\left<\hat{\mathbf{x}}\right>
, we can write
the SNE (
1
) into the simple form (cf. Appendix
A.
):

\frac{d\left|\Psi\right>}{dt}=-{\frac{i}{\hbar}}{\hat{H}}\left|\Psi\right>-\frac{i}{2\hbar}M\omega_{G}^{2}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{c}^{2}\left|\Psi\right>.

(4)

If
{\hat{H}}=\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{2}/2M
and
\left<\hat{\mathbf{x}}\right>=0

then the ground state coincides with the ground state of a central
harmonic oscillator of frequency
\omega_{G}
:

\Psi_{0}(\mathbf{x})=\mathcal{N}\exp\left(-\frac{M\omega_{G}\mathbf{x}^{2}}{2\hbar}\right).

(5)

This is a soliton standing at the origin.
The soliton and its inertially traveling versions are suitably
representing the stationary localization of free macro objects.

However, there is no mechanism that drives larger solitons and large macroscopically
extended wave functions toward the basic soliton states of shape (
5
).
Consider, e.g., a Schrödinger cat state which is superposition of two solitons
at a large distance
\ell
from each other:

\left|\mbox{Cat}\right>=\frac{\left|\mbox{left}\right>+\left|\mbox{right}\right>}{\sqrt{2}}.

(6)

They attract each other via
{\hat{V}}_{\Psi}
,
can even
form a Kepler system, but cannot collapse to a single soliton.
Ref.
Diósi 1984
emphasizes that collapse needs an additional
mechanism, not present in the SNE.

III
Gravity-Related Wavefunction Collapse

Alternatively to the SNE (
1
),
the G-SSE
considers the following stochastic modification of the standard Schrödinger equation:

\frac{d\left|\Psi\right>}{dt}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}{\hat{H}}\left|\Psi\right>-\frac{G}{2\hbar}\int\int\frac{{\hat{\varrho}}_{c}(\mathbf{r}){\hat{\varrho}}_{c}(\mathbf{s})d\mathbf{r}d\mathbf{s}}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{s}|}\left|\Psi\right>+\frac{1}{\hbar}\int{\hat{\varrho}}_{c}(\mathbf{r})\Phi(\mathbf{r})d\mathbf{r}\left|\Psi\right>,

(7)

where
{\hat{\varrho}}_{c}(\mathbf{r})={\hat{\varrho}}(\mathbf{r})-\left<{\hat{\varrho}}(\mathbf{r})\right>

and
\Phi(\mathbf{r},t)
is a white-noise field of spatial correlation

\mathbf{M}\Phi(\mathbf{r},t)\Phi(\mathbf{s},\tau)=\frac{G\hbar}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{s}|}\delta(t-\tau),

(8)

\mathbf{M}
stands for the stochastic mean.
The white-noise is to be taken in terms of the Ito differential calculus.
This G-SSE
yields the spontaneous collapse of massive macroscopic spatial superpositions,
of the Schrödinger cat states in particular.
The average state, i.e., the density matrix
{\hat{\rho}}=\mathbf{M}\left|\Psi\right>\left<\Psi\right|

satisfies the G-ME:

\displaystyle\frac{d{\hat{\rho}}}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{\hbar}[{\hat{H}},{\hat{\rho}}]-\frac{G}{2\hbar}\int\int\frac{[{\hat{\varrho}}(\mathbf{r}),[{\hat{\varrho}}(\mathbf{s}),{\hat{\rho}}]]d\mathbf{r}d\mathbf{s}}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{s}|}

(9)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{\hbar}[{\hat{H}},{\hat{\rho}}]+\mathcal{D}{\hat{\rho}}.

This suppresses the coherence of macroscopically
distinct superpositions of the mass density, yielding their statistical
mixture without the collapse.
The G-SSE (
7
) adds the collapse as well to the decoherence.
The two distant wavepackets of the Schrödinger cat state (
6
)
collapse onto one of them randomly at the rate

\frac{\Delta E_{G}}{\hbar},

(10)

where
\Delta E_{G}
denotes how much the collapse reduces the gravitational
energy of the cat
Penrose 1996
;
Penrose 1998
;
Penrose 2014
.

The G-SSE (
7
) becomes simple for a single
mass in the regime of small
\Delta\mathrm{x}
(cf. Appendices
B
,
C
):

\frac{d\left|\Psi\right>}{dt}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}{\hat{H}}\left|\Psi\right>-\frac{1}{2\hbar}M\omega_{G}^{2}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{c}^{2}\left|\Psi\right>+\sqrt{\frac{M}{\hbar}}\omega_{G}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{c}\mathbf{w}\left|\Psi\right>,

(11)

where
\mathbf{w}
is the vector of three independent standard white-noises.
(Correspondence with notations in ref.
Diósi 1989
is

\Gamma=2M\omega_{G}^{2}/\hbar
and
d\xi=\sqrt{M/\hbar}\omega_{G}\mathbf{w}
.)
Note, the Hermitian self-attracting potential in the SNE (
4
)
becomes anti-Hermitian here.
For free bodies (
{\hat{H}}=\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{2}/2M
), the solutions converge to
solitons of the steady shape

\Psi_{0}(\mathbf{x})=\mathcal{N}\exp\left(-(1-i)\frac{M\omega_{G}\mathbf{x}^{2}}{2\hbar}\right).

(12)

This is slightly different from (
5
), by the new complex factor

1-i
which leaves the spread
\Delta\mathrm{x}=\sqrt{(\hbar/2M\omega_{G}})
unchanged
just creating the correlation
\hbar/2
between
\hat{\mathbf{x}}
and
\hat{\mathbf{p}}
.
But the major difference is in the c.o.m. motion which is plagued by a
certain correlated diffusion of both the position and the momentum:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left<\hat{\mathbf{x}}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left<\hat{\mathbf{p}}\right>}{M}+\sqrt{\hbar/M}\mathbf{w},

(13)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left<\hat{\mathbf{p}}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\hbar M}\omega_{G}\mathbf{w}.

(14)

The momentum diffusion (
14
) is increasing the kinetic energy at rate
\frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega_{G}^{2}

which is probably an unphysical artifact of the collapse model.
We get rid of it below..

IV
Schrödinger–Newton Equation with Wavefunction Collapse

We slightly alter the original 1989-version (
7
) of the G-SSE.
We insert a factor
\exp(-i\pi/4)=(1-i)/\sqrt{2}
in front of the anti-Hermitian
stochastic potential:

\frac{d\left|\Psi\right>}{dt}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}{\hat{H}}\left|\Psi\right>-\frac{G}{2\hbar}\int\int\frac{{\hat{\varrho}}_{c}(\mathbf{r}){\hat{\varrho}}_{c}(\mathbf{s})d\mathbf{r}d\mathbf{s}}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{s}|}\left|\Psi\right>+\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-i\pi/4}}{\hbar}\int{\hat{\varrho}}_{c}(\mathbf{r})\Phi(\mathbf{r})d\mathbf{r}\left|\Psi\right>.

(15)

This yields the same G-ME (
9
) for the density matrix, and also encodes
the collapse of macroscopic superpositions. The novel feature is the appearance
of the Hermitian stochastic potential
\Phi(\mathbf{r})/\sqrt{2}
which will cancel
the momentum diffusion (
14
) of the solitons —
provided we include the gravitational self-attraction contained
in
{\hat{V}}_{\Psi}
of the SNE.

Our new proposal is the following combination of the
SNE (
1
) and the modified G-SSE (
15
):

\frac{d\left|\Psi\right>}{dt}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}({\hat{H}}\!+\!{\hat{V}}_{\Psi})\!\left|\Psi\right>-\frac{G}{2\hbar}\int\int\frac{{\hat{\varrho}}_{c}(\mathbf{r}){\hat{\varrho}}_{c}(\mathbf{s})d\mathbf{r}d\mathbf{s}}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{s}|}\left|\Psi\right>+\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-i\pi/4}}{\hbar}\!\!\int\!\!{\hat{\varrho}}_{c}(\mathbf{r})\Phi(\mathbf{r})d\mathbf{r}\!\left|\Psi\right>\!.

(16)

The ME of the average state reads:

\frac{d{\hat{\rho}}}{dt}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}[{\hat{H}}+{\hat{V}}_{\Psi},{\hat{\rho}}]+\mathcal{D}{\hat{\rho}}.

(17)

This is not a closed equation for
{\hat{\rho}}
since
{\hat{V}}_{\Psi}
depends on the
pure state
\left|\Psi\right>
. The lack of a closed linear ME is the signature
of anomalies
Gisin 1989
already troubling the SNE
and inherited by our SSNE (
16
). There is, however, a difference.
The spontaneous collapse might shadow the anomalies.
One of them, the fake action-at-a-distance is based on the attraction
caused by
{\hat{V}}_{\Psi}
between the two halves

\left|\mbox{left}\right>,\left|\mbox{right}\right>
of the Schrödinger cat
Diósi 2016
.
Unlike in case of the SNE, the cat now has the finite lifetime
\hbar/\Delta E_{G}

which may be
too short to reach detectable shifts of the left or the right wavepackets

Großardt 2022
.

V
Solitons with Energy Conservation

We consider the soliton solutions of the new
spontaneous collapse dynamics SSNE
in the small-
\Delta\mathrm{x}
approximation.
The single body special case of the SSNE (
16
)
takes this form:

\frac{d\left|\Psi\right>}{dt}=-{\frac{i}{\hbar}}{\hat{H}}\left|\Psi\right>-\frac{1+i}{2\hbar}M\omega_{G}^{2}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{c}^{2}\left|\Psi\right>+(1-i)\sqrt{\frac{M}{2\hbar}}\omega_{G}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{c}\mathbf{w}\left|\Psi\right>.

(18)

Note the important complex factors
(1+i)
and
(1-i)
compared
to the small-
\Delta\mathrm{x}
approximation (
11
) of the G-SSE model.
With these two factors, as we show below,
the soliton’s momentum diffusion (
14
) cancels.

For the time-dependent wave function of the soliton we
take the following Ansatz:

\displaystyle\Psi_{t}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\exp\left(-(1-i)\frac{|\mathbf{x}-\left<\hat{\mathbf{x}}\right>_{t}|^{2}}{4\Delta\mathrm{x}^{2}}+\frac{i}{\hbar}\left<\hat{\mathbf{p}}\right>\hat{\mathbf{x}}\right),

\displaystyle\Delta\mathrm{x}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2}M\omega_{G}},

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left<\hat{\mathbf{x}}\right>_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{p}}{M}+\sqrt{\hbar/M}\mathbf{w}_{t}.

(19)

These solutions correspond to inertial c.o.m. motion at constant momentum
\left<\hat{\mathbf{p}}\right>
,
apart from a minuscule diffusion of the coordinate.

We still owe to show that the wavefunciton
\Psi_{t}(\mathbf{x})

satisfies the eq. (
18
). It is sufficient if we prove it for the
soliton initially at rest at the origin, i.e., for
\left<\hat{\mathbf{x}}\right>_{0}=0
and
\left<\hat{\mathbf{p}}\right>=0
.
First, we apply the eq. (
18
) to
\Psi_{0}(\mathbf{x})
:

\frac{d\Psi_{0}(\mathbf{x})}{dt}=\left(\!i\hbar\frac{\nabla^{2}}{2M}\!-\!\frac{1+i}{2\hbar}M\omega_{G}^{2}\mathbf{x}^{2}\!+\!(1\!-\!i)\sqrt{\frac{M}{2\hbar}}\omega_{G}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{w}\!\right)\!\!\Psi_{0}(\mathbf{x}).

Second, we derive
d\Psi_{0}(\mathbf{x})/dt
from the Ansatz (
V
):

\frac{d\Psi_{0}(\mathbf{x})}{dt}=\left(-\sqrt{\hbar/M}\mathbf{w}\nabla+\frac{\hbar}{2M}\nabla^{2}\right)\Psi_{0}(\mathbf{x}),

(20)

where the second term is comes from the Ito-correction

(\mathbf{x}\mathbf{w}dt\nabla)^{2}=\mathbf{x}^{2}\nabla^{2}dt
.
We substitute the expression

\nabla\Psi_{0}=-(1-i)\frac{\mathbf{x}}{2\Delta\mathrm{x}^{2}}\Psi_{0}=-(1-i)\frac{\mathbf{x}}{2}\frac{\sqrt{2}M\omega_{G}}{\hbar}\Psi_{0}

and then observe that the stochastic term coincides with that of (
V
).
We also substitute the expression

\nabla^{2}\Psi_{0}=\left(-i\frac{\mathbf{x}^{2}}{2\Delta\mathrm{x}^{4}}-(1-i)\frac{1}{2\Delta\mathrm{x}^{2}}\right)\Psi_{0}

in both eq. (
V
) and eq. (
20
). Then, after elementary algebraic
steps, their deterministic parts will also coincide.

VI
Final remarks

Penrose also proposed the spontaneous
collapse rate (
10
) of the Schrödinger cat as well as the SNE (
1
) to generate the
stationary states after the collapse
Penrose 1996
;
Penrose 1998
;
Penrose 2014
.
He is treating the G-related spontaneous collapse and the SNE
together. However, he has been in a holding position
regarding any concrete dynamics (like the G-SSE or others)
of the collapse.

With the goal of reaching a closed linear ME to
avoid the anomalies of the SNE, ref.
Nimmrichter and Hornberger 2015

proposed a formal completion of the SNE by stochastic terms.
Ref.
Tilloy and Diósi 2016
showed that this goal can be achieved
by combining the SNE with the G-SSE, of course differently
from the present proposal SSNE which, contrary to
refs.
Nimmrichter and Hornberger 2015
;
Tilloy and Diósi 2016
,
sacrifices the closed ME in favor of
energy-momentum conservation — at least in the free c.o.m.
motion of the macroscopic body.

The advantage of the newly proposed SSNE (
16
) compared to the
G-SSE (
7
) is this:
when the state of a free massive body is collapsing towards the
localized soliton, the spontaneous gain of kinetic energy is
gradually disappearing and
in the soliton states the energy-momentum conservation becomes restored.
The non-conservations by the G-SSE (and by
other collapse models) are probably warnings of infancy of the models.
Yet, their related predictions are the only testable effects currently
Bassi et al. 2013
,
since massive Schrödinger cat states are not available in the laboratory to date.
To test the G-SSE, ref.
Helou et al. 2017
searched for the predicted c.o.m.
momentum diffusion
in the super-precise data of Lisa Pathfinder experiment.
Work
Donadi et al. 2021
was hunting the spontaneous radiation
predicted by the momentum diffusion of the nuclei inside the detector
in the super-low-background Gran Sasso laboratory. Both
works put an upper bound on the strength of the G-related
spontaneous collapse. If we adopt the new SSNE,
a different interpretation of the Lisa Pathfinder data is possible.
But the interpretation of the Gran Sasso data can be retained
because the new model SSNE has not removed the momentum diffusion
of the microscopic constituents but of the c.o.m. of the macro-object.

Our proposal is the first dynamical model of spontaneous collapse with
partial restoration of energy-momentum conservation at the price
of typical anomalies of semiclassical theories which might become
partially masked by the collapse mechanism. Future works should
aim at more complete restoration of energy-momentum
conservation and at better understanding whether the said anomalies
would become completely neutralized.

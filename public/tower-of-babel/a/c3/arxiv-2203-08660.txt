A Liouville type result and quantization effects on the system − Δ ​ u = u ​ J ′ ​ ( 1 − | u | 2 ) -\Delta u=uJ^{\prime}(1-|u|^{2}) for a potential convex near zero
U. De Maio, R. Hadiji, and C. Lefter, C. Perugia

Abstract
We consider a Ginzburg-Landau type equation in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} of the form − Δ ​ u = u ​ J ′ ​ ( 1 − | u | 2 ) -\Delta u=uJ^{\prime}(1-|u|^{2}) with a potential function J J satisfying weak conditions allowing for example a zero of infinite order in the origin. We extend in this context the results concerning quantization of finite potential solutions of H.Brezis, F.Merle, T.Rivière from [ 10 ] who treat the case when J J behaves polinomially near 0, as well as a result of Th. Cazenave, found in the same reference, and concerning the form of finite energy solutions. Keywords: Ginzburg-Landau type functional, variational problem, quantization of energy, finite energy solutions. 2010 AMS subject classifications: 35Q56, 35J50, 35B25, 35J55, 35Q40.

\psi_{R}=\frac{1}{2\pi R}\int_{S_{R}}\psi{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d\sigma}.

Multiplying
\left(\ref{DHP11}\right)
by
\psi-\psi_{R}
and integrating over
A_{R}=B_{R}\setminus B_{R_{0}}
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{A_{R}}\rho^{2}\left(\dfrac{d}{r}V+\nabla\psi\right)\nabla\psi{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}=\int_{S_{R}}\rho^{2}\left(\dfrac{d}{r}V\cdot\nu+\dfrac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}\right)\left(\psi-\psi_{R}\right){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d\sigma}\\
\\
\displaystyle-\int_{S_{R_{0}}}\rho^{2}\left(\dfrac{d}{r}V\cdot\nu+\dfrac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}\right)\left(\psi-\psi_{R}\right){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d\sigma}.\\
\end{array}

(4.31)

As
V\cdot\nu=0
, by
\left(\ref{DHP12*}\right)
we get

\int_{S_{R_{0}}}\rho^{2}\left(\dfrac{d}{r}V\cdot\nu+\dfrac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}\right)\left(\psi-\psi_{R}\right){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d\sigma}=\int_{S_{R_{0}}}\rho^{2}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}\psi{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d\sigma}=C

(4.32)

where
C
is a constant independent of
R
. We also observe that

\int_{A_{R}}\dfrac{d}{r}V\cdot\nabla\psi{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}=\int_{A_{R}}\frac{d}{r}\dfrac{\partial\psi}{\partial\tau}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}=0.

(4.33)

By
\left(\ref{DHP16}\right)
,
\left(\ref{DHP17}\right)
and
\left(\ref{DHP18}\right)
we have

\int_{A_{R}}\rho^{2}\left|\nabla\psi\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}\leq\int_{S_{R}}\left|\dfrac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}\right|\left|\psi-\psi_{R}\right|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d\sigma}+\int_{A_{R}}\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)\dfrac{d}{r}\left|\nabla\psi\right|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d\sigma}+C.

(4.34)

Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies

\left|\int_{S_{R}}\dfrac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}\left(\psi-\psi_{R}\right){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d\sigma}\right|\leq\left(\int_{S_{R}}\left|\dfrac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d\sigma}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{S_{R}}\left|\psi-\psi_{R}\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d\sigma}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We recall the following Poincaré inequality

\int_{S_{R}}\left|\psi-\psi_{R}\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d\sigma}\leq R^{2}\int_{S_{R}}\left|\nabla_{\tau}\psi\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d\sigma}.

(4.35)

Therefore by
\left(\ref{DHP20}\right)
and Young inequality we have

\left|\int_{S_{R}}\dfrac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}\left(\psi-\psi_{R}\right){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d\sigma}\right|\leq\dfrac{R}{2}\int_{S_{R}}\left|\dfrac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d\sigma}+\dfrac{R}{2}\int_{S_{R}}\left|\nabla_{\tau}\psi\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d\sigma}=\dfrac{R}{2}\int_{S_{R}}\left|\nabla\psi\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d\sigma}.

(4.36)

By
\left(\ref{DHP21}\right)
and
\left(\ref{DHP19}\right)
we obtain

\int_{A_{R}}\rho^{2}\left|\nabla\psi\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}\leq\dfrac{R}{2}\int_{S_{R}}\left|\nabla\psi\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d\sigma}+\int_{A_{R}}\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)\dfrac{d}{r}\left|\nabla\psi\right|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}+C.

(4.37)

Remember that
A_{R}=B_{R}\setminus B_{R_{0}}
. Given
0<\epsilon<\frac{1}{4}
we choose
R_{0}
big enough such that

1-\epsilon\leq\rho^{2}(x)\leq 1,\,\mbox{ for }|x|\geq R_{0}.

Denote by

f(R)=\int_{A_{R}}\left|\nabla\psi\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}.

(4.38)

whose derivative is

f^{\prime}(R)=\int_{S_{R}}\left|\nabla\psi\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d\sigma}

and by (
4.8
) and (
4.25
) satisfies the estimate

f(R)\leq CR.

(4.39)

Thus (
4.37
) becomes:

(1-\epsilon)f(R)\leq\frac{R}{2}f^{\prime}(R)+\int_{A_{R}}\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)\dfrac{d}{r}\left|\nabla\psi\right|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}+C.

(4.40)

We estimate the second term in (
4.40
). By Young inequality we obtain

\int_{A_{R}}\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)\dfrac{d}{r}\left|\nabla\psi\right|{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}\leq\frac{d^{2}}{\epsilon}\int_{A_{R}}\dfrac{\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)^{2}}{r^{2}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}+\epsilon\int_{A_{R}}\left|\nabla\psi\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}=\frac{d^{2}}{\epsilon}\int_{A_{R}}\dfrac{\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)^{2}}{r^{2}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}+\epsilon f(R).

(4.41)

and thus (
4.40
) implies

(1-2\epsilon)f(R)\leq\frac{R}{2}f^{\prime}(R)+\frac{d^{2}}{\epsilon}\int_{A_{R}}\dfrac{\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)^{2}}{r^{2}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}+C.

(4.42)

Observe, by l’Hospital rule that

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log R}\int_{A_{R}}\dfrac{\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)^{2}}{r^{2}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}=\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{R}\int_{S_{R}}(1-\rho^{2})^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d\sigma}=0,

(4.43)

because
\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}\rho(x)=1
. Consequently we may rewrite (
4.42
) in the form

f(R)\leq\frac{R}{\beta}f^{\prime}(R)+F(R),

(4.44)

where
\beta=2(1-2\epsilon)>1
and
F(R)=\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{\epsilon(1-2\epsilon)}\int_{A_{R}}\frac{\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)^{2}}{r^{2}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}+\dfrac{C}{1-2\epsilon}
which, by (
4.43
) satisfies

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\frac{F(R)}{\log R}=0.

(4.45)

Denoted by

g(R)=\frac{f(R)}{\log R},

we want to prove

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}g(R)=0.

We plug the expression for
g
in (
4.44
) and obtain:

g(R)\leq g^{\prime}(R)\frac{R\log R}{\beta\log R-1}+\frac{\beta F(R)}{\beta\log R-1}=\frac{g^{\prime}(R)}{\alpha(R)}+H(R),

(4.46)

where we denoted by
\displaystyle\alpha(R)=\frac{\beta\log R-1}{R\log R}
and by
\displaystyle H(R)=\frac{\beta F(R)}{\beta\log R-1}
.
Denote by
\displaystyle\tilde{\beta}=\frac{\beta+1}{2}>1
and observe that

\frac{\tilde{\beta}}{R}\leq\alpha(R)\leq\frac{\beta}{R},

(4.47)

for
R>\tilde{R}
big enough.

Observe that, by (
4.45
),

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}H(R)=0.

Hence, given
\mu>0
there exists
R(\mu)>\tilde{R}
such that
H(R)<\mu
for all
R>R(\mu)
. Consequently, by (
4.46
) we have
for
R>R(\mu)

\alpha(R)(g(R)-\mu)\leq g^{\prime}(R)=(g(R)-\mu)^{\prime}.

(4.48)

Given
R>\theta>R(\mu)
we multiply (
4.48
) by
\displaystyle\exp\left(-\int_{\theta}^{R}\alpha(\tau)d\tau\right)
and find that the function

R\rightarrow(g(R)-\mu)\exp\left(-\int_{\theta}^{R}\alpha(\tau)d\tau\right)

is increasing and thus, using also the linear growth of
f

i.e
(
4.39
), we find

(g(\theta)-\mu)\leq(g(R)-\mu)\exp\left(-\int_{\theta}^{R}\alpha(\tau)d\tau\right)\leq\left(\frac{CR}{\log R}-\mu\right)\exp\left(-\int_{\theta}^{R}\alpha(\tau)d\tau\right).

(4.49)

By (
4.47
) we have

\exp\left(-\int_{\theta}^{R}\alpha(\tau)d\tau\right)\leq\left(\frac{\theta}{R}\right)^{\tilde{\beta}},

which plugged into (
4.49
) gives

(g(\theta)-\mu)\leq\left(\frac{CR}{\log R}+\mu\right)\left(\frac{\theta}{R}\right)^{\tilde{\beta}}\rightarrow 0\;\text{ for }R\rightarrow+\infty.

(4.50)

We found that for
\theta>R(\mu)
one has
0\leq g(\theta)\leq\mu
. As
\mu>0
may be chosen arbitrarily small,
we obtain that

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}g(R)=0,

that is (
4.20
).

Now we want to prove
\left(\ref{DHP7}\right)
. To this aim let us consider the function
\eta
as in the proof of Theorem
2
and set
\eta_{R}(x)=\eta\left(\dfrac{x}{R}\right)
. Multiplying (
4.23
) by

(1-\varrho)\eta_{R}
and integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{R_{0}}
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\left|\nabla\rho\right|^{2}\eta_{R}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\left(1-\rho\right)\nabla\rho\nabla\eta_{R}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\rho\left(1-\rho\right)\left|\nabla\varphi\right|^{2}\eta_{R}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}\\
\\
-\displaystyle\int_{S_{R_{0}}}\dfrac{\partial\rho}{\partial\nu}\left(1-\rho\right)\eta_{R}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}d\sigma}-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\rho\left(1-\rho\right)j\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)\eta_{R}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}.\\
\end{array}

(4.51)

Now we estimate each term in (
4.51
). By definition of
\eta_{R}
and as
\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)\leq 1
, we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\left(1-\rho\right)\nabla\rho\nabla\eta_{R}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}=-\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\nabla\left(1-\rho\right)^{2}\nabla\eta_{R}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}=\dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\left(1-\rho\right)^{2}\Delta\eta_{R}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}\\
\\
=\dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\left(1-\rho\right)^{2}\Delta\eta_{R}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}\leq\frac{C}{R^{2}}\displaystyle\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\left(1-\rho\right)^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}\leq C.\\
\end{array}

(4.52)

Let us observe that by
\left(\ref{DHP10}\right)
we can write

\left|\nabla\varphi\right|^{2}\leq 2\left(\dfrac{d^{2}}{r^{2}}+\left|\nabla\psi\right|^{2}\right).

(4.53)

Then by definition of
\eta_{R}
,
\left(\ref{DHP8}\right)
and
\left(\ref{DHP36}\right)
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\rho\left(1-\rho\right)\left|\nabla\varphi\right|^{2}\eta_{R}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}\leq 2\displaystyle\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\rho\left(1-\rho\right)\left(\dfrac{d^{2}}{r^{2}}+\left|\nabla\psi\right|^{2}\right)\eta_{R}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}\\
\\
\leq 2d^{2}\displaystyle\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\dfrac{\rho\left(1-\rho\right)}{r^{2}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}+2\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\rho\left(1-\rho\right)\left|\nabla\psi\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}\\
\\
\leq 2d^{2}\displaystyle\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\dfrac{\rho\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)}{\left(1+\rho\right)r^{2}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}+2\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\left|\nabla\psi\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}\\
\\
\leq C_{1}\displaystyle\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\dfrac{\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)}{r^{2}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}+C_{2}\displaystyle\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\left|\nabla\psi\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx},\\
\end{array}

(4.54)

C_{1}
and
C_{2}
being constants independent of
R
.

By (
4.43
) we have that

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log R}\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\dfrac{\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)}{r^{2}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}=0,

(4.55)

which combined with (
4.20
) gives that

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\rho\left(1-\rho\right)\left|\nabla\varphi\right|^{2}\eta_{R}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}=0.

(4.56)

Finally, by
(\mathit{H2})
, we can assert that

-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\rho\left(1-\rho\right)j\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)\eta_{R}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}\leq 0.

(4.57)

By putting
\left(\ref{DHP35}\right)
,
\left(\ref{ologR}\right)
and
\left(\ref{DHP38}\right)
in
\left(\ref{DHP34}\right)
and as
\eta_{R}(x)=1
in
B_{R}
, we find

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log R}\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\left|\nabla\rho\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}\leq\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\left|\nabla\rho\right|^{2}\eta_{R}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}=0,

(4.58)

which is (
4.21
).
∎

4.1
Quantization of the energy

The final step is to prove

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}J\left(1-\rho^{2}\right){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}=\pi d^{2}.

(4.59)

Let us consider

\begin{array}[]{l}E=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}J\left(1-\rho^{2}\right){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx},\\
\\
E(r)=\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}J\left(1-\rho^{2}\right){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}.\end{array}

We have that

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}E(r)=E,\\
\\
\displaystyle\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\dfrac{1}{\log R}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}\dfrac{E(r)}{r}dr=E.\end{array}

(4.60)

By integrating
\left(\ref{DHP40}\right)
for
r\in(0,R)
we get

\int_{B_{R}}\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}+2\int_{0}^{R}\dfrac{E(r)}{r}dr=\int_{B_{R}}\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\tau}\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}+E(R).

By dividing previous equality by
\log R
, we obtain

\dfrac{1}{\log R}\int_{B_{R}}\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}+\dfrac{2}{\log R}\int_{0}^{R}\dfrac{E(r)}{r}dr=\dfrac{1}{\log R}\int_{B_{R}}\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\tau}\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}+\dfrac{1}{\log R}E(R).

(4.61)

We observe that for
r>R_{0}

\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}=\left|\dfrac{\partial\rho}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}+\rho^{2}\left|\dfrac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}\leq\left|\nabla\rho\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla\psi\right|^{2}

(4.62)

and by (
4.25
)

\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\tau}\right|^{2}=\left|\dfrac{\partial\rho}{\partial\tau}\right|^{2}+\rho^{2}\left|\dfrac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\tau}\right|^{2}=\left|\dfrac{\partial\rho}{\partial\tau}\right|^{2}+\rho^{2}\left(\dfrac{d}{r}+\dfrac{\partial\psi}{\partial\tau}\right)^{2}.

Hence

\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\tau}\right|^{2}-\dfrac{d^{2}}{r^{2}}=\left|\dfrac{\partial\rho}{\partial\tau}\right|^{2}-(1-\rho^{2})\dfrac{d^{2}}{r^{2}}+\left|\dfrac{\partial\psi}{\partial\tau}\right|^{2}-2(1-\rho^{2})\dfrac{d}{r}\dfrac{\partial\psi}{\partial\tau}+2\dfrac{d}{r}\dfrac{\partial\psi}{\partial\tau}.

(4.63)

By integrating on
B_{R}\setminus B_{R_{0}}
and using (
4.33
) we easily get

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\left(\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\tau}\right|^{2}-\dfrac{d^{2}}{r^{2}}\right){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}=\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\left(\left|\dfrac{\partial\rho}{\partial\tau}\right|^{2}+\left|\dfrac{\partial\psi}{\partial\tau}\right|^{2}\right){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}-d^{2}\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}\setminus R_{0}}\dfrac{1-\rho^{2}}{r^{2}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}\\
\\
-2d\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}\setminus R_{0}}\dfrac{1-\rho^{2}}{r}\dfrac{\partial\psi}{\partial\tau}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}.\end{array}

(4.64)

By Proposition
2.1
and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
4.64
) becomes

\left|\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\left(\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\tau}\right|^{2}-\dfrac{d^{2}}{r^{2}}\right){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}\right|\leq

\leq\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\left(\left|\nabla\rho\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla\psi\right|^{2}\right){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}+d^{2}\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}\setminus R_{0}}\dfrac{1-\rho^{2}}{r^{2}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}+2d\sqrt{\log\dfrac{R}{R_{0}}}\left(\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}\setminus R_{0}}\left|\nabla\psi\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.65)

Combining
\left(\ref{DHP7*}\right)
,
\left(\ref{DHP7}\right)
, (
4.55
),
and
\left(\ref{DHP45}\right)
, we have

\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\left|\dfrac{1}{\log R}\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{R_{0}}}\left(\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\tau}\right|^{2}-\dfrac{d^{2}}{r^{2}}\right){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}\right|=0,

and so

\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\dfrac{1}{\log R}\int_{B_{R}}\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\tau}\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}=2\pi d^{2}.

(4.66)

Moreover, by
\left(\ref{DHP7*}\right)
,
\left(\ref{DHP7}\right)
and
\left(\ref{DHP43}\right)
it results

\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\dfrac{1}{\log R}\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{R_{o}}}\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}dx}=0.

(4.67)

Finally, from
\left(\ref{DHP41}\right)
,
\left(\ref{DHP46}\right)
and

\left(\ref{DHP47}\right)
, by passing to the limit as
R\rightarrow+\infty
in
\left(\ref{DHP42}\right)
, by (
4.1
) we obtain

E=\pi d^{2},

(4.68)

which is
\left(\ref{DHP39}\right)
.

Hence Theorem
2
is completely proved.

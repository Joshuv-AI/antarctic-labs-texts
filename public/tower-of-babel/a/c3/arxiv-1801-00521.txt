Asymptotic Gap Probability Distributions of the Gaussian Unitary Ensembles and Jacobi Unitary Ensembles
Shulin Lyu, Yang Chen, Engui Fan

Abstract
In this paper, we address a class of problems in unitary ensembles. Specifically, we study the probability that a gap symmetric about 0, i.e. ( − a , a ) (-a,a) is found in the Gaussian unitary ensembles (GUE) and the Jacobi unitary ensembles (JUE) (where in the JUE, we take the parameters α = β \alpha=\beta ). By exploiting the even parity of the weight, a doubling of the interval to ( a 2 , ∞ ) (a^{2},\infty) for the GUE, and ( a 2 , 1 ) (a^{2},1) , for the (symmetric) JUE, shows that the gap probabilities maybe determined as the product of the smallest eigenvalue distributions of the LUE with parameter α = − 1 / 2 , \alpha=-1/2, and α = 1 / 2 \alpha=1/2 and the (shifted) JUE with weights x 1 / 2 ​ ( 1 − x ) β x^{1/2}(1-x)^{\beta} and x − 1 / 2 ( 1 − x ) β x^{-1/2}(1-x)^{\beta} The σ \sigma function, namely, the derivative of the log of the smallest eigenvalue distributions of the finite- n n LUE or the JUE, satisfies the Jimbo-Miwa-Okamoto σ \sigma form of P V P_{V} and P V ​ I P_{VI} , although in the shift Jacobi case, with the weight x α ​ ( 1 − x ) β , x^{\alpha}(1-x)^{\beta}, the β \beta parameter does not show up in the equation. We also obtain the asymptotic expansions for the smallest eigenvalue distributions of the Laguerre unitary and Jacobi unitary ensembles after appropriate double scalings, and obtained the constants in the asymptotic expansion of the gap probablities, expressed in term of the Barnes G − G- function valuated at special point.

\displaystyle D_{n}(t,\alpha)=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}h_{j}(t,\alpha).

We list here a number of facts about orthogonal polynomials.

From orthogonality, there follows the three-term recurrence relation

\displaystyle zP_{n}(z;t,\alpha)={P_{n+1}(z;t,\alpha)}+\alpha_{n}(t,\alpha)P_{n}(z;t,\alpha)+\beta_{n}(t,\alpha)P_{n-1}(z;t,\alpha),\qquad n\geq 0,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{D_{n}(t,\alpha)}\frac{1}{n!}\int_{(t,\infty)^{n}}\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{j}-x_{i})^{2}\prod\limits^{n}_{k=1}(z-x_{k})w(x_{k},\alpha)dx_{k}

\displaystyle\frac{\det\left(\int_{t}^{\infty}x^{i+j}(z-x)x^{\alpha}{\rm e}^{-x}dx\right)_{i,j=0}^{n-1}}{\det\left(\int_{t}^{\infty}x^{i+j}x^{\alpha}{\rm e}^{-x}dx\right)_{i,j=0}^{n-1}}.

\displaystyle P_{n}(0;0,\alpha)=(-1)^{n}\frac{\Gamma(n+1+\alpha)}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}.

Theorem 4.1

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(a,n)=\frac{D_{n}(a)}{D_{n}(0)},

\displaystyle D_{n}(a)=\begin{cases}\widetilde{D}_{k+1}\,\widehat{D}_{k},&n=2k+1,\\

\displaystyle\widehat{D}_{m}(a):=

\displaystyle\det\left(\int_{a^{2}}^{\infty}t^{i+j}t^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm e}^{-t}dt\right)_{i,j=0}^{m-1}.

From this, we find

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(b):=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(a,n\right)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{D_{n}\left(a\right)}{D_{n}(0)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\widetilde{D}_{k}\left(a\right)}{\widetilde{D}_{k}(0)}\cdot\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\widehat{D}_{k}\left(a\right)}{\widehat{D}_{k}(0)},

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(b)=

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(a^{2},-\tfrac{1}{2},k)\cdot\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(a^{2},\tfrac{1}{2},k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(b^{2},-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\cdot\mathbb{P}\left(b^{2},\tfrac{1}{2}\right).

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\sqrt{\pi}2^{-2n-\alpha-\beta}\frac{n^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)},

(5.17)

so that, from (
5.6
),

\displaystyle P_{n}(1;0,\alpha,\beta)=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}P_{n}(0;0,\beta,\alpha)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\sqrt{\pi}2^{-2n-\alpha-\beta}\frac{n^{\beta+\frac{1}{2}}}{\Gamma(\beta+1)}.

(5.18)

Combining (
5.17
) with (
5.15
), and (
5.18
) with (
5.16
), we find

\displaystyle\frac{P_{n}(0;\frac{s}{4n^{2}},\alpha,\beta)}{P_{n}(0;0,\alpha,\beta)}\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}{\rm e}^{\sqrt{s}}s^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\exp\left[\log\left(\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\right)+\sqrt{s}-\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)\log s\right],

(5.19)

and, assuming
\beta>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{P_{n}(1;\frac{s}{4n^{2}},\alpha,\beta)}{P_{n}(1;0,\alpha,\beta)}\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(\beta+1)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}{\rm e}^{\beta}\beta^{-\beta-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left[\log\left(\frac{\Gamma(\beta+1)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\right)+\beta-(\beta+\tfrac{1}{2})\log\beta\right].

(5.20)

Comparing (
5.19
) with (
5.13
), and (
5.20
) with (
5.14
), we arrive at

\displaystyle c_{2}(\alpha+1,\beta)-c_{2}(\alpha,\beta)=\log\left(\tfrac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\right),

(5.21)

and

\displaystyle c_{2}(\alpha,\beta+1)-c_{2}(\alpha,\beta)=\log\left(\frac{\Gamma(\beta+1)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\right)+\beta-(\beta+\tfrac{1}{2})\log\beta,

(5.22)

respectively. Hence, it follows from (
5.21
)

\displaystyle c_{2}(\alpha,\beta)=\log\frac{G(\alpha+1)}{(2\pi)^{\alpha/2}}+f(\beta),

and from (
5.22
) we find a difference equation satisfied by
f(\beta)
:

\displaystyle f(\beta+1)-f(\beta)=\log\left(\frac{\Gamma(\beta+1)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\right)+\beta-(\beta+\tfrac{1}{2})\log\beta,

and consequently,

\displaystyle f(\beta)=\log\frac{G^{2}(\beta+1)}{(2\pi)^{\beta/2}}+\frac{\beta(\beta-1)}{2}-(\beta+\tfrac{1}{2})\log\Gamma(\beta).

Therefore, we finally obtain

\displaystyle c_{2}(\alpha,\beta)=\log\left[\frac{G(\alpha+1)G^{2}(\beta+1)}{(2\pi)^{(\alpha+\beta)/2}}\right]+\frac{\beta(\beta-1)}{2}-(\beta+\tfrac{1}{2})\log\Gamma(\beta).

∎

The Asymptotics of the Gap Probability of the Jacobi Unitary Ensembles

The probability that the interval
(-a,a)
has no eigenvalues in the (symmetric) Jacobi unitary ensembles with the weight

\displaystyle w_{0}(x,\beta)=(1-x^{2})^{\beta},\qquad x\in[-1,1],\quad\beta>0,

is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(a,\beta,n)=\frac{\det\left(\int_{-1}^{1}x^{i+j}w(x,a,\beta)dx\right)_{i,j=0}^{n-1}}{\det\left(\int_{-1}^{1}x^{i+j}w_{0}(x,\beta)dx\right)_{i,j=0}^{n-1}}=:\frac{D_{n}(a,\beta)}{D_{n}(0,\beta)}.

(6.1)

Here
w(x,a,\beta)
is the discontinuous Jacobi weight with two jumps

\displaystyle w(x,a,\beta)=

\displaystyle(1-x^{2})^{\beta}\theta(x^{2}-a^{2}),\qquad x\in[-1,1],\quad\beta>0,

where
\theta(x)
is 1 if
x\geq 0
and 0 otherwise.
We shall be concerned with the behavior of
\mathbb{P}(a,\beta,n)
under double scaling.

We begin with the normalization relation of the monic polynomials orthogonal with respect to
w(x,a,\beta)
over
[-1,1]
:

\displaystyle h_{j}(a,\beta):=

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}P_{j}^{2}(x;a,\beta)(1-x^{2})^{\beta}\theta(x^{2}-a^{2})dx{}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{1}P_{j}^{2}(x;a,\beta)(1-x^{2})^{\beta}\theta(x^{2}-a^{2})dx.

(6.2)

Note that
\{P_{j}\}
can be normalized as
[
17
]

\displaystyle P_{2n}(x;a,\beta)=x^{2n}+p(2n,a,\beta)x^{2n-2}+\cdots+P_{2n}(0;a,\beta),

and

\displaystyle P_{2n+1}(x;a,\beta)=

\displaystyle x^{2n+1}+p(2n+1,a,\beta)x^{2n-1}+\cdots+{\rm const.}\,x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\left(x^{2n}+p(2n+1,a,\beta)x^{2n-2}+\cdots+{\rm const.}\right).

By introducing into (
6.2
) the change of variable
x^{2}=t
, we find

\displaystyle h_{2n}(a,\beta)=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{1}P_{2n}^{2}(x;a,\beta)(1-x^{2})^{\beta}\theta(x^{2}-a^{2})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{1}P_{2n}^{2}(\sqrt{t};a,\beta)(1-t)^{\beta}\theta(t-a^{2})\frac{dt}{2\sqrt{t}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{a^{2}}^{1}\widetilde{P}_{n}^{2}(t;a,\beta)t^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)^{\beta}dt=:\widetilde{h}_{n}(a,\beta),

and

\displaystyle h_{2n+1}(a,\beta)=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{1}P_{2n+1}^{2}(x;a,\beta)(1-x^{2})^{\beta}\theta(x^{2}-a^{2})dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{1}\left\{\sqrt{t}\,\widehat{P}_{n}(t;a,\beta)\right\}^{2}(1-t)^{\beta}\theta(t-a^{2})\frac{dt}{2\sqrt{t}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{a^{2}}^{1}\widehat{P}_{n}^{2}(t;a,\beta)t^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)^{\beta}dt=:\widehat{h}_{n}(a,\beta).

Here
\widetilde{P}_{n}(t;a,\beta)
and
\widehat{P}_{n}(t;a,\beta)
are monic polynomials of degree
n
in the variable
t
, orthogonal with respect to
t^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)^{\beta}
and
t^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)^{\beta}
over
[a^{2},1]
respectively.

If we define the Hankel determinants generated by
t^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)^{\beta}
and
t^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)^{\beta},t\in[a^{2},1]
, by

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{m}(a,\beta):=

\displaystyle\det\left(\int_{a^{2}}^{1}t^{i+j}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)^{\beta}dt\right)_{i,j=0}^{m-1}=\prod_{l=0}^{m-1}\widetilde{h}_{l}(a,\beta),

\displaystyle\widehat{D}_{m}(a,\beta):=

\displaystyle\det\left(\int_{a^{2}}^{1}t^{i+j}t^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)^{\beta}\right)_{i,j=0}^{m-1}=\prod_{l=0}^{m-1}\widehat{h}_{l}(a,\beta),

then it follows that

\displaystyle D_{n}(a,\beta):=

\displaystyle\det\left(\int_{-1}^{1}x^{i+j}w(x,a,\beta)dx\right)_{i,j=0}^{n-1}=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}h_{j}(a,\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\widetilde{D}_{k+1}\,\widehat{D}_{k},&n=2k+1,\\
\widetilde{D}_{k}\,\widehat{D}_{k},&n=2k.\end{cases}

(6.3)

Based on (
5.12
), we establish in the next theorem the asymptotic expression for

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(b,\beta):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(a,\beta,n\right),\qquad b:=na,

under the assumption that
a\rightarrow 0
and
n\rightarrow\infty
such that
b
is fixed.
Note the double scaling is unlike the GUE case, where
\tau=2{\sqrt{2n}}\>a

Theorem 6.1
.

The probability that the interval
(-\tfrac{b}{n},\tfrac{b}{n}),n\rightarrow\infty
, is free of eigenvalues in the Jacobi unitary ensembles with weight
(1-x^{2})^{\beta},x\in[-1,1],\;\beta>0
is approximated by

\displaystyle\log\mathbb{P}(b,\beta)=

\displaystyle-\frac{b^{2}}{2}-\frac{\log b}{4}+\log\left[G(\tfrac{1}{2})^{2}\sqrt{\pi}\right]+\log\left[\frac{G^{4}(\beta+1)}{(2\pi)^{\beta}}\right]

\displaystyle+\beta(\beta-1)-(2\beta+1)\log\Gamma(\beta)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{32\,b^{2}}+\frac{5}{128\,b^{4}}+\frac{131}{768\,b^{6}}+O\left(b^{-8}\right),\qquad b\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

From (
6.1
) and (
6.3
), we find

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(b,\beta)=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(a,\beta,n\right)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{D_{n}\left(a,\beta\right)}{D_{n}(0,\beta)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\widetilde{D}_{k}\left(a,\beta\right)}{\widetilde{D}_{k}(0,\beta)}\cdot\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\widehat{D}_{k}\left(a,\beta\right)}{\widehat{D}_{k}(0,\beta)},

so that, in view of (
5.1
) and noting that
b^{2}=n^{2}a^{2}\sim 4k^{2}a^{2}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(b,\beta)=

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(a^{2},-\tfrac{1}{2},\beta,k)\cdot\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(a^{2},\tfrac{1}{2},\beta,k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(b^{2},-\tfrac{1}{2},\beta)\cdot\mathbb{P}(b^{2},\tfrac{1}{2},\beta),

Here
\mathbb{P}(a^{2},\alpha,\beta,k)
is the probability that all the eigenvalues of
k\times k
Hermitian matrices with weight
x^{\alpha}(1-x)^{\beta}
are greater than
a^{2}
and
\mathbb{P}\left(b^{2},\alpha,\beta\right)
defined by (
5.9
) is the scaled limiting probability of
\mathbb{P}(a^{2},\alpha,\beta,k)
, i.e.
\mathbb{P}\left(b^{2},\alpha,\beta\right)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{b^{2}}{4k^{2}},\alpha,\beta,k\right)
.

Therefore, according to (
5.12
), we establish

\displaystyle\log\mathbb{P}(b,\beta)=

\displaystyle\log\mathbb{P}(b^{2},-\tfrac{1}{2},\beta)+\log\mathbb{P}(b^{2},\tfrac{1}{2},\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{2}\left(-\tfrac{1}{2},\beta\right)+c_{2}(\tfrac{1}{2},\beta)-\frac{b^{2}}{2}-\alpha^{2}\log b+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{8}b^{-2}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\alpha^{4}}{16}+\frac{9\alpha^{2}}{64}\right)b^{-4}+\left(\frac{\alpha^{6}}{24}+\frac{15\alpha^{4}}{32}+\frac{9\alpha^{2}}{16}\right)b^{-6}

\displaystyle+O\left(b^{-8}\right),\qquad b\rightarrow\infty.

Here the constant term reads

\displaystyle c_{2}\left(-\tfrac{1}{2},\beta\right)

\displaystyle+c_{2}(\tfrac{1}{2},\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\left[G(\tfrac{1}{2})^{2}\sqrt{\pi}\right]+\log\left[\frac{G^{4}(\beta+1)}{(2\pi)^{\beta}}\right]+\beta(\beta-1)-(2\beta+1)\log\Gamma(\beta),

where the properties
G(\tfrac{3}{2})=G(\tfrac{1}{2})\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2})
and
\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})=\sqrt{\pi}
are used.
The proof is completed.
∎

Conclusion

We obtained in this paper the constant term in the asymptotic expansion of the large gap probability of the Gaussian unitary ensembles;
reproducing the Widom-Dyson constant. This is done through the study of the smallest eigenvalue distribution of the Laguerre
unitary ensembles, and specializing
\alpha=\pm 1/2,
in the constant obtained. Finally, we derive the asymptotic expansion of the smallest eigenvalue
distribution of the Jacobi unitary ensembles
(x^{\alpha}(1-x)^{\beta}
,
x\in(0,1),\;\;\alpha>-1,\;\beta>0)
. In this situation, although the double
scaled
\sigma
equation is identical with the LUE
\sigma-
equation, however the constant in the
JUB
problem depends on
\beta
.

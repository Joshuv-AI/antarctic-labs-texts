On the geometric P=W conjecture
Mirko Mauri, Enrica Mazzon, Matthew Stevenson

Abstract
. We formulate the geometric P=W conjecture for singular character varieties. We establish it for compact Riemann surfaces of genus one, and obtain partial results in arbitrary genus. To this end, we employ non-Archimedean, birational and degeneration techniques to study the topology of the dual boundary complex of character varieties. We also clarify the relation between the geometric and the cohomological P=W conjectures.



1.
Introduction

Given a smooth quasi-projective variety
X
, choose a compactification
\overline{X}=X\cup\partial X
such that the boundary
\partial X
has simple normal crossings (snc). The
dual boundary complex

\mathcal{D}(\partial X)
is a cell complex encoding information about the irreducible components of
\partial X
and their intersections. Roughly, the combinatorics of
\mathcal{D}(\partial X)
tells us about the geometry of
X
at infinity.

The purpose of this paper is to study the topology of dual boundary complexes of certain character varieties in relation to the geometric P=W conjecture.

1.1.
The P=W conjectures.

Let
C
be a Riemann surface of genus
g
, and let
G
be a complex reductive algebraic group.
The Dolbeault and Betti moduli spaces
M_{\mathrm{Dol}}(C,G)
and
M_{\mathrm{B}}(C,G)
are central objects in non-abelian Hodge theory. The former parametrises principal
G
-Higgs bundles with vanishing Chern classes, while the latter, also called
G
-character variety, parametrises representations of the fundamental group of
C
with values in
G
; see
Section

4.2
. Although the algebraic moduli problems which they solve are very
different, there exists a canonical real analytic isomorphism

\Psi\colon M_{\mathrm{Dol}}(C,G)\to M_{\mathrm{B}}(C,G),

called
non-abelian Hodge correspondence
. Note that
\Psi
is not a biholomorphism: the Dolbeault moduli space carries a projective Lagrangian fibration onto an affine space

\chi:M_{\mathrm{Dol}}(C,G)\to\mathbb{C}^{\frac{1}{2}\dim M_{\mathrm{Dol}}(C,G)},

called
Hitchin fibration
, whose regular fibres are holomorphic Lagrangian tori; instead the Betti moduli space does not contain any proper subvariety of positive dimension, since it is affine.

The geometric P=W conjecture aims to a better understanding of the asymptotic behaviour at infinity of the transcendental map
\Psi
.
Formulated by Katzarkov, Noll, Pandit, and Simpson in
[
Kat+15
, Conjecture 1.1]
, it says in brief that

Geometric P=W conjecture
(abridged version)
.

The dual boundary complex
\mathcal{D}(\partial M_{\mathrm{B}}(C,G))
is homotopy equivalent to a sphere.

The dual boundary complex should furthermore be naturally identified with the sphere at infinity on the base of the Hitchin fibration.
The full statement actually involves the commutativity of a square diagram, but we postpone the precise formulation of the conjecture to
Section

4
. There we propose a tweaked version of the original statement in
[
Kat+15
]
necessary to deal with the singularities of the Betti and Dolbeault moduli spaces.

The geometric P=W conjecture takes its name after a conjecture formulated by de Cataldo, Hausel and Migliorini in
[
dCHM12
]
, called cohomological P=W conjecture; see
Section

6.1
. The different geometry of
M_{\mathrm{Dol}}(C,G)
and
M_{\mathrm{B}}(C,G)
is encoded in two filtrations on their isomorphic singular cohomology, named respectively P and W, which stand for perverse and weight filtrations.
Despite the different origin of the filtrations, the conjecture asserts that

Cohomological P=W conjecture.

The non-abelian Hodge correspondence
\Psi
exchanges the perverse and weight filtrations.

In
Section

6.2
, we clarify the relation between the cohomological and geometric conjectures, thus answering the final question of
[
Mig17
]
; see also
[
Har19
, §1]

1.2.
New evidence for the geometric P=W conjecture.

Up to date, the evidence for the geometric P=W conjecture in the literature concerns only the case of punctured spheres, and the commutativity of the square diagram is known only in few low-dimensional examples; see

In this paper, following a suggestion of Simpson in

1.3.
The PL-homeomorphism type of the dual boundary complex of character varieties

In order to address the geometric P=W conjecture, one must make sense of
\mathcal{D}(\partial M_{\mathrm{B}}(C,G))
.
It is not a priori clear how one can do so, since the affine variety
M_{\mathrm{B}}(C,G)
can be singular, hence it may not admit an snc compactification. Thus, the task is to find mildly singular compactifications to which a dual complex is still attached.

Our solution is to consider
divisorial log terminal (dlt) compactifications
; see (
2.0.6
) for a definition. Indeed, such compactifications have well-defined dual complex, whose homotopy type is independent of
the choice of a specific dlt compactification by
[
dFKX17
]
. In
4.3.2
we show that dlt compactifications do exist for Betti moduli spaces of compact Riemann surfaces of any genus and structural group either
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
or
\mathrm{SL}_{n}
.

Among all possible dlt compactifications of
M_{\mathrm{B}}(C,G)
, it is convenient to restrict to special ones, more precisely to
dlt log Calabi–Yau (logCY) compactifications
(if they exist!). This is an algebraic condition which rigidifies the configuration of divisors at infinity:
the dual complex of any dlt logCY compactification identifies a distinguished PL-homeomorphism class
in the homotopy equivalence class
of the dual complex of any dlt compactification. Not all quasi-projective varieties admit logCY compactifications, but a folk conjecture
[
Sim16
]
says that character varieties should do,
and this is indeed proved for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
-local systems on punctured surfaces
in
[
Wha20
]
. If these compactifications were also dlt, then a positive answer to
[
KX16
, Question 4]
would essentially imply that
\mathcal{D}(\partial M_{\mathrm{B}}(C,G))
is homeomorphic to a sphere.

These observations suggest the following partial strengthening of the geometric P=W conjecture.

Conjecture 1.3.1.

M_{\mathrm{B}}(C,G)
admits a dlt logCY compactification
(\overline{M}_{\mathrm{B}}(C,G),\partial M_{\mathrm{B}}(C,G))
, and the associated dual boundary complex
\mathcal{D}(\partial M_{\mathrm{B}}(C,G))
is
\PL
-homeomorphic to a sphere

(m+1)

.

In
Section

we prove this stronger conjecture for a compact Riemann surface
\mathrm{E}

Theorem D

(Theorem
5.1.3
)
.

M_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathrm{E},\mathrm{GL}_{n})
admits a dlt logCY compactification, and the dual boundary complex
\mathcal{D}(\partial M_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathrm{E},\mathrm{GL}_{n}))
of such compactification is
\PL
-homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}
.

Theorem E

(Theorem
5.1.5
)
.

M_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathrm{E},\mathrm{SL}_{n})
admits a dlt logCY compactification, and the dual boundary complex
\mathcal{D}(\partial M_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathrm{E},\mathrm{SL}_{n}))

\PL
-homeomorphic to

It is an open question whether

1.4.
Non-Archimedean approach

In Theorems
D
and
E
we show that dlt logCY compactifications of
M_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathrm{E},G)
do exist via the minimal model program: this is a non-constructive existence result, and the authors are not aware of more explicit dlt logCY compactifications. However, we can determine the PL-homeomorphism type of the dual boundary complex of such compactifications by recasting the problem in terms of non-Archimedean geometry.
In this approach, boundary divisors are thought as suitable divisorial valuations, in the sense of (
2.0.3
).
This viewpoint permits to reinterpret the dual complex
\mathcal{D}(\partial M_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathrm{E},G))
as the level set of a suitable function inside a space of valuations, namely as the minimality locus of a log discrepancy function, called
skeleton
; see
Definition

3.0.1
and
Lemma

3.0.2
. This idea is compatible with the expectation of a correspondence between valuations on the Betti moduli space and directions in the Hitchin base, conjectured in
[
Kat+15
, §1.1]
.

In particular, the valuative interpretation of dual complexes allows us to prove the following fact about their topology, which is used to prove Theorems
D
and
E
, and is of independent interest.

Theorem F

(Theorem
3.0.7
)
.

Let
(X,\Delta_{X})
be a log canonical pair. Let
\Gamma
be a finite subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(X,\Delta_{X})
, i.e. the group of automorphisms
\gamma\colon X\to X
such that
\gamma^{*}\Delta_{X}=\Delta_{X}
.
Suppose also that the quotient map
q\colon X\to X/\Gamma
is quasi-étale, i.e. the action of
\Gamma
is free in codimension
1
. Denote
\mathcal{DMR}(X,\Delta_{X})
the dual complex of a dlt modification of
(X,\Delta_{X})
,
3
3

3

The notation
\mathcal{DMR}
stands for “Dual complex of a Minimal divisorial log terminal partial Resolution”.
and
\Delta_{X/\Gamma}\coloneqq q_{*}\Delta_{X}
. Then, there exists a
\PL
-homeomorphism

\mathcal{DMR}(X/\Gamma,\Delta_{X/\Gamma})\simeq_{\mathrm{PL}}\mathcal{DMR}(X,\Delta_{X})/\Gamma.

It is worth remarking that one could determine the homotopy type of
\mathcal{D}(\partial M_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathrm{E},G))
by using the classical notion of skeleton for toroidal pairs as in
[
Thu07
]
. However, the definition of skeleton in
Section

3
adapts well to the more singular case of dlt compactifications, which appear in the formulation of the geometric P=W conjecture. Further, in the logCY case, it allows to establish the actual
\PL
-homeomorphism class of the dual boundary complex.

1.5.
Hyper-Kähler degenerations on the Betti side

2.
Preliminaries: Berkovich spaces, log discrepancy, and dual complexes

(2.0.1)

Let
X
be a normal proper algebraic variety over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic zero. A
pair

(X,\Delta)
is the datum of
X
together with a Weil
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta
with coefficients in
(0,1]
, called boundary, such that
K_{X}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier.
Write
\Delta^{=1}=\sum_{i\in I}\Delta_{i}
for the union of all irreducible components of
\Delta
whose coefficient equals
1
.
The irreducible components of the intersection
\Delta_{i_{1}}\cap\ldots\cap\Delta_{i_{r}}
, with
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}\}\subseteq I
, are called
strata
of
\Delta^{=1}
.

The pair
(X,\Delta)
is
log Calabi–Yau
(logCY) if
K_{X}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly equivalent to zero, written

K_{X}+\Delta\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
.

(2.0.2)

We denote by
X^{\mathrm{bir}}
the locus of birational points of the
Berkovich analytification
of
X
. As a set,
X^{\mathrm{bir}}
is the space of real valuations of the fraction field
k(X)
of
X
that extend the trivial valuation
v_{0}
of
k
, i.e. the set of functions
v\colon k(X)^{*}\to\mathbb{R}

with the properties that:

(1)

v(f\cdot g)=v(f)+v(g)
for all
f,g\in k(X)
;

(2)

v(f+g)\geqslant\min\{v(f),v(g)\}
for all
f,g\in k(X)
;

(3)

v(h)=0
for all
h\in k^{*}
.

We endow
X^{\mathrm{bir}}
with the coarsest topology which makes
the maps
X^{\mathrm{bir}}\to\mathbb{R}

v\mapsto v(f)
, continuous for any
f\in k(X)^{*}
.
Note that a dominant morphism of algebraic varieties

induces a surjective continuous map

g^{\mathrm{bir}}\colon X^{\mathrm{bir}}\to Y^{\mathrm{bir}}
by precomposition
v(\cdot)\mapsto v(\cdot\circ g)
.

By the valuative criterion of properness, any valuation
v
admits a
center
on
X
, i.e. there exists a unique schematic point
c_{X}(v)\in X
such that
v(f)\geqslant 0
for any
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X,c_{X}(v)}
and
v(f)>0
if and only if
f\in\mathfrak{m}_{c_{X}(v)}
.

(2.0.3)

Given a non-negative real number
c

h\colon Y\to X

[

(2.0.4)

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a pair, and

The

(2.0.6)

Given an lc pair
(X,\Delta)
, an

with

The

(2.0.7)

There are several advantages to working with dlt pairs over snc pairs.
Most notably, we
use the fact that any lc pair
(X,\Delta)
is crepant birational to a (non-unique) dlt pair

This fact is a consequence of the existence of

(1)

Denote by

3.
Dual complex of quotient of lc pairs

Let
(X,\Delta_{X})

5.2.
A degeneration of Hilbert schemes on K3 to
M_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathrm{E},\mathrm{GL}_{n})

(5.2.1)

The proofs of

5.1
are inspired by the result in
[
BM19
, Proposition 6.2.4]
. There, Brown and the second author show that the dual complex of a degeneration of the Hilbert scheme of
n

.

In this section, we exhibit a direct connection between the two results:
we show how

5.1.3
and
5.1.5
can be deduced from
[
BM19
, Proposition 6.2.4]
.
This alternative proof relies on
the construction of explicit degenerations of hyper-Kähler varieties (see
5.2.9
and
5.3.2
), and
a global-to-local argument (

5.2.5
) that relates the dual complex of a degeneration to that of an lc logCY pair.
While the proofs of
Theorem

5.1.3
and
5.1.5
presented in
Section

5.1
are technically more elementary,
we expect both strategies to prove useful for future calculations of dual complexes, as explained in
Section

1.5
.

Definition 5.2.2.

[
Kol13
, §4.18]

Let
(X,\Delta_{X})
be a dlt pair with
\Delta_{X}^{=1}=\sum_{i}\Delta_{i}
. For any stratum
W
of
(X,\Delta_{X})
, there exists a unique
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\operatorname{Diff}^{*}_{W}(\Delta_{X})
, called
different
, with the following property: for
m\in\mathbb{N}
divisible enough, the Poincaré residue map
\omega^{[m]}_{X}(m\Delta_{X})|_{W}\simeq\omega^{[m]}_{W}
on the snc locus extends to the isomorphism
\omega^{[m]}_{X}(m\Delta_{X})|_{W}\simeq\omega^{[m]}_{W}(\operatorname{Diff}^{*}_{W}(\Delta_{X}))
on the locus where
\omega^{[m]}_{X}(m\Delta_{X})|_{W}
and
\omega^{[m]}_{W}
are locally free and hence everywhere, being reflexive sheaves.

In particular, this yields

(K_{X}+\Delta_{X})|_{W}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}K_{W}+\operatorname{Diff}^{*}_{W}(\Delta_{X}),

and by adjunction we have that

(5.2.3)

\operatorname{Diff}^{*}_{W}(\Delta_{X})=\operatorname{Diff}^{*}_{W}(\Delta_{X})^{=1}+\operatorname{Diff}^{*}_{W}(\Delta_{X})^{<1}=\sum_{i\colon W\nsubseteq\Delta_{i}}\Delta_{i}|_{W}+\operatorname{Diff}^{*}_{W}(\Delta_{X})^{<1}.

In particular, any stratum
W
of a dlt (logCY) pair has an induced structure of dlt (logCY) pair
(W,\operatorname{Diff}^{*}_{W}(\Delta_{X}))
such that

(5.2.4)

\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Diff}^{*}_{W}(\Delta_{X})^{=1})\simeq\mathcal{D}\bigg(\sum_{i\colon W\nsubseteq\Delta_{i}}\Delta_{i}|_{W}\bigg).

Lemma 5.2.5 (Global-to-local argument).

Let
(X,\Delta_{X})
be a dlt pair such that the dual complex of
\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{X})
is a
\PL
-manifold. For any stratum
W
of
\Delta_{X}^{=1}
,
\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Diff}^{*}_{W}(\Delta_{X}))
is
\PL
-homeomorphic to a sphere.

Proof.

If
\sigma_{W}
is a cell of
\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{X})
corresponding to the stratum
W
we define:

•

the (open) star of
\sigma_{W}
, denoted
\mathrm{St}(\sigma_{W})
, as the union of the interiors of the
cells whose closure intersects the cell
\sigma_{W}
;

•

the closed star of
\sigma_{W}
, denoted
\mathrm{St}(\sigma_{W})
, is the closure of
\mathrm{St}(\sigma_{W})
;

•

the link of
\sigma_{W}
, denoted

, is the difference
\overline{\mathrm{St}}(\sigma_{W})\setminus St(\sigma_{W})
in the first
barycentrical subdivision of
\mathcal{D}(\Delta_{X})
.

By (

), the vertices of

are in correspondence with the strata of
\operatorname{Diff}^{*}_{W}(\Delta_{X})^{=1}
, and
\mathrm{Link}(\sigma_{W})
is PL-homeomorphic to
\mathcal{D}(\operatorname{Diff}^{*}_{W}(\Delta_{X}))
. Since

is a
\PL
-manifold,
\mathrm{Link}(\sigma_{W})
is
\PL
-homeomorphic to a sphere.
∎

By

Step 1.

The goal is to choose the map

6.1.
The cohomological P=W conjecture

(6.1.1)

Lemma 7.0.1.

Let

be a compact Riemann surface, and

Remark 7.0.6 (Relation with other work III).

It is reasonable to formulate the geometric P=W conjecture also for moduli spaces

of
G
-local systems on punctured curves

, such that conjugacy classes

of the monodromy around the punctures

are fixed.
Actually, the first evidence of the geometric P=W conjecture have been provided in this setting: when

is the
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
-character variety of local systems on a punctured sphere
(with fixed trace at the punctures),

in
[
Sim16
, Theorem 1.1]
that the dual boundary complex

has the homotopy type of a sphere; see also
[
Kom15
, Theorem 1.4]
.
For punctured spheres, there is no known proof of the commutativity of the diagram (
4.2.8
), except for some partial results in the case of five punctures due to Szabó

[
Sza21a
]
. The full geometric P=W conjecture has also been proved in the Painlevé cases in
[
Sza21
,
Sza19
,
NS20
]
. Therefore, the results contained in this paper are the first evidence of the geometric P=W conjecture for compact curves and high rank.

Remark 7.0.7 (Relation with other work IV).

Let

be the character variety defined in

. For generic choice of the monodromy of the local systems around the punctures,

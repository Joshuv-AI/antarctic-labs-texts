ACC of plc threshold
Sung Rak Choi, Sungwook Jang

Abstract
. In this paper, we define the potential log canonical threshold and prove the ascending chain condition (ACC) of the set of these thresholds satisfies. We also consider collections of Fano type varieties and study their basic properties including boundedness.



1.
Introduction

Recently, various thresholds in birational geometry have attracted much attention as their interesting behaviours (e.g., ascending chain condition, ACC) turned out to have important implications especially in the minimal model program. In this paper, we are interested in the so called
potential thresholds
for the pair
(X,\Delta)
such that
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
is pseudoeffective. It is a generalization of the well-known log canonical thresholds.
The notion of potential pairs was first introduced and studied in
[
CP
]
. It was defined as a means to bound the singularities of the outcome of the
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
-minimal model program (MMP).
We believe that the deeper understanding of this notion will be useful in establishing the
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP.

For a pair
(X,\Delta)
with an effective divisor
\Delta
such that
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
is pseudoeffective, consider a birational morphism
f:Y\to X
and the divisorial Zariski decomposition
-f^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)=P+N
. If
F
is a prime divisor on
Y
,

denotes the log discrepancy of
(X,\Delta)
at
F
. We define the
potential log discrepancy
of
(X,\Delta)
at
F
as
\bar{a}(F;X,\Delta):=a(F;X,\Delta)-\operatorname{mult}_{F}N
.
The given pair
(X,\Delta)
is said to be potentially klt (resp.
\epsilon
-potentially lc for
\epsilon\geq 0
) if
\inf_{f,F}\bar{a}(F;X,\Delta)>0
(resp.
\geq\epsilon
) holds. We write pklt for potentially klt and
\epsilon
-plc for
\epsilon
-potentially lc. See Definition
2.3
and
[

]
for more details.

By construction, the notions of pklt,
\epsilon
-plc capture the singularities of the pair
(X,\Delta)
as well as the positivity of
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
. However, if
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
is nef, then the notions of pklt,
\epsilon
-plc coincide with the usual klt,
\epsilon
-lc. One of the most important properties of these notions is their invariant property under
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP (Proposition
2.7
).

Recall that the log canonical threshold
\operatorname{lct}(X,\Delta;D)
of a klt pair
(X,\Delta)
with respect to a given divisor

is defined as the supremum of the real numbers

for which
(X,\Delta+tD)
is lc. The

of a plc pair

with respect to

is defined similarly and by definition it is at most the effective threshold
t_{\eff}(X,\Delta;D):=\sup\{t\geq 0|-(K_{X}+\Delta+tD)\text{ is pseudoeffective}\}
. See Definition
2.3

the good Zariski decomposition. Then we can write
-P-N+E_{Y}=g^{\ast}(K_{X^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime})
. Since
D
is nef,
N\leq N(t_{\eff})
. By
[
KKL
, Lemma 4.1]
,
N(t_{\eff})
is
g
-exceptional and so is
N
. Moreover, by definition,
E_{Y}
is
g
-exceptional. Thus, by the negativity lemma,
E_{Y}\leq N
.

By assumption (i), we know that
(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
is
\epsilon
-lc. Thus the pair
(F^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime}|_{F^{\prime}})
is also
\epsilon
-lc. Now consider the restriction

-(K_{X^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}+t_{\eff}D^{\prime})|_{F^{\prime}}=-(K_{F^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}|_{F^{\prime}}+t_{\eff}D^{\prime}|_{F^{\prime}})\equiv 0.

Since

\delta D^{\prime}|_{F^{\prime}}\equiv-(K_{F^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}|_{F^{\prime}}+(t_{\eff}-\delta)D^{\prime}|_{F^{\prime}})=-(K_{X^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}+(t_{\eff}-\delta)D^{\prime})|_{F^{\prime}}

and the right-hand side is ample,
D^{\prime}|_{F^{\prime}}
is ample. This implies that
-(K_{F^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}|_{F^{\prime}})
is ample. By
[
Bir2
, Theorem 1.1]
,
F^{\prime}
belongs to a bounded family
\mathcal{F}
.

Finally by arguing as in the proof of
[
HL
, Theorem 1.3]
, we finish the proof as follows. Note first that we can write for some very ample Cartier divisor
M_{F^{\prime}}
on
F^{\prime}

t_{\eff}=\frac{1}{d}\left(-K_{F^{\prime}}\cdot M_{F^{\prime}}^{\dim F^{\prime}-1}-\Delta^{\prime}|_{F^{\prime}}\cdot M_{F^{\prime}}^{\dim F^{\prime}-1}\right)

where
d=D^{\prime}|_{F^{\prime}}\cdot M_{F^{\prime}}^{\dim F^{\prime}-1}
.
By the boundedness of
F^{\prime}
, we may assume that
-K_{F^{\prime}}\cdot M_{F^{\prime}}^{\dim F^{\prime}-1}\leq r
for some fixed number
r>0
. It is easy to see that
d=D^{\prime}|_{F^{\prime}}\cdot M_{F^{\prime}}^{\dim F^{\prime}-1}
belongs to a DCC set (e.g.,
I^{\prime}:=\{a\cdot m|a\in I,m\in\mathbb{N}\}
). Note also that
\Delta^{\prime}|_{F^{\prime}}\cdot M_{F^{\prime}}^{\dim F^{\prime}-1}
belongs to the DCC set
I^{\prime}
. Therefore, it is immediate that
t_{\eff}
belongs to some ACC set.
∎

Due to the following example, the
\epsilon
-plc condition on
(X,\Delta)
with
\epsilon>0
in Theorem
1.1
cannot be strengthened to plc condition (i.e.,
\epsilon=0
).

Example 3.1
.

Let
X_{n}=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(n))
be a Hirzebruch surface,
M
the negative section, and
F
a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-fiber. Then
-K_{X_{n}}=((1+\frac{2}{n})M+(n+2)F)+(1-\frac{2}{n})M
is the Zariski decomposition with the negative part
N=(1-\frac{2}{n})M
. By Theorem
2.6
, we know that
X_{n}
is pklt. If we let
D_{n}=M+(n+3)F
, then one can see that
t_{\eff}(X_{n},0,D_{n})=\frac{n+2}{n+3}
. Clearly,
\left\{\frac{n+2}{n+3}\left|n\in\mathbb{N}\right.\right\}
does not satisfy ACC. This shows that the condition being
\epsilon
-plc in Theorem
1.1
is necessary. Note that
\bar{a}(M;X_{n},0)=\frac{2}{n}
.

The following result is used in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
HMX
, Theorem 1.5]
)
.

Fix a positive integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
and a DCC set
I\subseteq[0,1]
. Then there is a finite subset
I_{0}\subseteq I
such that if

(1)

X
is a projective variety of dimension
n
,

(2)

(X,\Delta)
is log canonical,

(3)

\Delta=\sum\delta_{i}\Delta_{i}
, where
\delta_{i}\in I
,

(4)

K_{X}+\Delta\equiv 0
,

then
\delta_{i}\in I_{0}
.

Now we prove our main result Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Note that by definition, we have
\operatorname{plct}(X,\Delta;D)\leq t_{\eff}(X,\Delta;D)
. We will prove the ACC of the
\operatorname{plct}(X,\Delta;D)
for the following two cases separately;
\Sigma=\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2}
where

\begin{array}[]{l}\Sigma_{1}:=\{\lambda_{1}|\lambda_{1}=\operatorname{plct}(X,\Delta;D)<t_{\eff}(X,\Delta;D)\},\\
\Sigma_{2}:=\{\lambda_{2}|\lambda_{2}=\operatorname{plct}(X,\Delta;D)=t_{\eff}(X,\Delta;D)\}.\end{array}

Suppose first that
\lambda_{1}\in\Sigma_{1}
, that is,
\lambda_{1}=\operatorname{plct}(X,\Delta;D)<t_{\eff}(X,\Delta;D)=t_{\eff}
. Since
X
is of Fano type by Theorem
2.9
, the ring
R(X,-(K_{X}+\Delta),-(K_{X}+\Delta+\lambda_{1}D))
is finitely generated. Take a resolution
f:Y\to X
as in Theorem
2.12
(4). Let
-f^{\ast}(K_{X}+\Delta+\lambda D)=P(\lambda)+N(\lambda)
be the good Zariski decomposition for
0\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{1}
. There exists a semiample fibration
g:Y\to X^{\prime}
associated to all
P(\lambda_{1}-\delta)
with sufficiently small
\delta>0
. By
[
KKL
, Theorem 4.2]
, the induced rational map
\varphi:X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
is a semiample model for
-(K_{X}+\Delta+\lambda D)
for any
\lambda\in[\lambda_{1}-\delta,\lambda_{1}]
. Let
K_{X^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}+\lambda D^{\prime}=\varphi_{\ast}(K_{X}+\Delta+\lambda D)
. Then
K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}+\lambda f^{\ast}D+N(\lambda)=g^{\ast}(K_{X^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}+\lambda D^{\prime})
. By Remark
,
(X,\Delta+\lambda D)
is pklt if and only if the coefficients of
\Delta_{Y}+\lambda f^{\ast}D+N(\lambda)
are less than 1. Therefore we obtain that
\operatorname{plct}(X,\Delta;D)=\operatorname{lct}(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime};D^{\prime})
. Hence by Theorem
2.11
, the set
\Sigma_{1}=\{\lambda_{1}\penalty\ |\penalty\ \lambda_{1}=\operatorname{plct}(X,\Delta;D)<t_{\eff}(X,\Delta;D)\}
satisfies ACC.

Now, consider the set
\Sigma_{2}=\{\lambda_{2}\penalty\ |\penalty\ \lambda_{2}=\operatorname{plct}(X,\Delta;D)=t_{\eff}(X,\Delta;D)\}
. We will show that
\Sigma_{2}
also satisfies ACC. For
\lambda_{2}\in\Sigma_{2}
, take
X,X^{\prime},\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},D
and
D^{\prime}
as above. Then,
\lambda_{2}=\operatorname{plct}(X,\Delta;D)<\operatorname{lct}(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime};D^{\prime})
, because
(X,\Delta+\lambda_{2}D)
is pklt. Therefore we cannot apply Theorem
2.11
. Instead, we proceed using the following argument.

Let
t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq\cdots
be a nondecreasing sequence of
\Sigma_{2}
. Then for each
i
, there are a variety
X^{\prime}_{i}
and divisors
\Delta^{\prime}_{i},D^{\prime}_{i}
associated to
t_{i}
as we constructed before. Let
h_{i}:X^{\prime}_{i}\to Z^{\prime}_{i}
be the semiample fibration associated to
-(K_{X^{\prime}_{i}}+\Delta^{\prime}_{i}+t_{i}D^{\prime}_{i})
and
F^{\prime}_{i}
a general fiber of

. Then
(F^{\prime}_{i},\Delta^{\prime}_{i}|_{F^{\prime}_{i}}+t_{i}D^{\prime}_{i}|_{F^{\prime}_{i}})
is klt and

K_{F^{\prime}_{i}}+\Delta^{\prime}_{i}|_{F^{\prime}_{i}}+t_{i}D^{\prime}_{i}|_{F^{\prime}_{i}}\equiv 0.

By passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
\dim F^{\prime}_{i}
is constant. Since
\{t_{i}\}
is nondecreasing, the coefficients of
\Delta^{\prime}_{i}|_{F^{\prime}_{i}}+t_{i}D^{\prime}_{i}|_{F^{\prime}_{i}}
belong to a DCC set. By Theorem
3.2
, the coefficients of
\Delta^{\prime}_{i}|_{F^{\prime}_{i}}+t_{i}D^{\prime}_{i}|_{F^{\prime}_{i}}
, indeed, belong to a finite set. It follows that a sequence
\{t_{i}\}
stabilizes.

Since
\Sigma=\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2}
and union of two ACC sets again satisfies ACC, as we desired,
\Sigma
satisfies ACC.
∎

Lastly, we state the following relation to the threshold concerning the
\mathbb{R}
-complement of Shokurov.
We recall from
[
Sho
]
that a pair
(X,\Delta)
is said to have an
\mathbb{R}
-complement
if there exists an effective divisor
\Delta^{\prime}
such that
\Delta^{\prime}\geq\Delta
,
(X,\Delta^{\prime})
is lc, and

.

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose that
(X,\Delta)
is a pklt pair such that
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
is big.
Then

\operatorname{plct}(X,\Delta;D)=\sup\{t\geq 0\;|\;(X,\Delta+tD)\;\text{ has an $\mathbb{R}$-complement}\}.

The threshold on the righthand side is called the
\mathbb{R}
-complement threshold
in
[
Sho
]
and
\mathbb{R}
-complementary threshold
in
[
HLS
]
.

Proof.

Note that by Theorem
2.9
the variety
X
in the given pair
(X,\Delta)
is a Fano type variety.
We claim that if
(X,\Delta)
is an lc pair with a Fano type variety
X
,
(X,\Delta)
has an
\mathbb{R}
-complement if and only if
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
is pseudoeffective and
(X,\Delta)
is plc.

Let
\Delta^{\prime}
be an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on

such that
\Delta^{\prime}\geq\Delta
,
(X,\Delta^{\prime})
is lc, and
K_{X}+\Delta^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{R}}0
. Then, clearly,
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
is pseudo-effective. Furthermore, since
-(K_{X}+\Delta^{\prime})
is
\mathbb{R}
-trivial,
(X,\Delta^{\prime})
is plc and hence
(X,\Delta)
is also plc. Conversely, assume that
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
is pseudoeffective and
(X,\Delta)
is plc. Since
X
is of Fano type, we can find a resolution
f:Y\to X
such that
-f^{\ast}(K_{X}+\Delta)
admits the good Zariski decomposition
-f^{\ast}(K_{X}+\Delta)=P+N
with the semiample positive part

\mathbb{R}
-complement of

\operatorname{plct}(X,\Delta;D)=\sup\{t\geq 0\;|\;(X,\Delta+tD)\;\text{ has an $\mathbb{R}$-complement}\}.

∎

4.
Boundedness of
\epsilon
-plc pairs

It is known that the ACC of log canonical thresholds (Theorem
2.11
) is implied by the following Birkar’s result on the boundedness of
\epsilon
-lc Fano varieties (
[
HMX
]
).

Theorem 4.1

(
[
Bir2
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
d
be a natural number and
\epsilon
a positive real number. Then the projective varieties
X
such that

\bullet

(X,\Delta)
is
\epsilon
-lc of dimension
d
for some boundary
\Delta
, and

\bullet

-(K_{X}+\Delta)
is nef and big,

form a bounded family.

Let
d
be a positive integer and
\epsilon
a nonnegative real number. Let us consider the following collections of Fano type varieties:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\FT(d,\epsilon)&:=\left\{X\left|\begin{array}[]{l}\text{$X$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial normal projective variety of dimension $d$,}\\
\text{$(X,\Delta)$ is $\epsilon$-lc and $-(K_{X}+\Delta)$ is nef and big for some boundary $\Delta$}\end{array}\right.\right\},\\
&\\
\pFT(d,\epsilon)&:=\left\{X\left|\begin{array}[]{l}\text{$X$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial normal projective variety of dimension $d$,}\\
\text{$(X,\Delta)$ is $\epsilon$-plc and $-(K_{X}+\Delta)$ is big for some boundary $\Delta$}\end{array}\right.\right\}.\end{array}

We will write
\FT(d,0)=\FT(d)
and
\pFT(d,0)=\pFT(d)
.

By definition,
\FT(d,\epsilon)\subseteq\pFT(d,\epsilon)
for any
\epsilon\geq 0
and the following holds:

\FT(d)=\bigcup_{\epsilon>0}\FT(d,\epsilon)=\bigcup_{\epsilon>0}\pFT(d,\epsilon).

Theorem
4.1
proves that
\FT(d,\epsilon)
is bounded for any
\epsilon>0
.
It is well known that the Fano type varieties are Mori dream spaces by
[
BCHM
]
.
Thus the varieties
X\in\FT(d,\epsilon)
can be understood as the images of some
X^{\prime}\in\pFT(d,\epsilon)
that are obtained by running the Minimal model program on some
\epsilon
-lc pair
(X^{\prime},\Delta)
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
d
be a positive integer and
\epsilon
a positive real number. Then
\pFT(d,\epsilon)
is a bounded family.

Proof.

Let
X\in\pFT(d,\epsilon)
. Then
X
is of Fano type and we can run
-K_{X}
-MMP. Let
\phi:X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
be a
-K_{X}
-MMP which terminates with nef
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
-K_{X^{\prime}}
. Then
X^{\prime}
is
\epsilon
-lc and
-K_{X^{\prime}}
is nef and big. Thus, by
[
Bir2
, Theorem 1.1]
,
X^{\prime}
belongs to a bounded family. This implies that there is a positive integer
n
such that
-nK_{X^{\prime}}
is Cartier. Now by the effective base point free theorem, we can also assume that
|-nK_{X^{\prime}}|
is base point free. Let
H^{\prime}\in|-nK_{X^{\prime}}|
be a general element and let
B^{\prime}=\frac{1}{n}H^{\prime}
. Then
K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}
is a klt
n
-complement of
K_{X^{\prime}}
and it gives a klt
n
-complement
K_{X}+B
of
K_{X}
(cf.
[
Bir1
, 6.1.(3)]
). Finally, by
[
HX
, Theorem 1.3]
,
X
belongs to a bounded family.
∎

We also have the following result.

Proposition 4.3
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that
\FT(d,\epsilon)\subsetneq\pFT(d,\delta)
.

Proof.

We know that for a fixed
\epsilon>0
,
\FT(d,\epsilon)\subseteq\pFT(d,\epsilon)\subseteq\pFT(d,\epsilon^{\prime})
for any
\epsilon^{\prime}<\epsilon
. Suppose that
\FT(d,\epsilon)=\pFT(d,\epsilon^{\prime})
for any
0<\epsilon^{\prime}<\epsilon
.
Note that

\FT(d)=\bigcup_{r>0}\pFT(d,r)=\bigcup_{\epsilon>r>0}\pFT(d,r)=\FT(d,\epsilon).

However, it is clear that
\FT(d)\neq\FT(d,\epsilon)
.
∎

Proposition
4.3
implies that
\pFT(d,\epsilon)
is not necessarily of the form
\FT(d,\epsilon)
. Thus Theorem
4.1
cannot be applied directly to obtain the boundedness of
\pFT(d,\epsilon)
.
The following proposition gives an alternative proof for the boundedness of
\pFT(d,\epsilon)
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\epsilon
be a positive real number and
d
a positive integer. Then
\pFT(d,\epsilon)\subseteq\FT(d,\epsilon^{\prime})
for any
0<\epsilon^{\prime}<\epsilon
.

Proof.

Suppose that
X\in\pFT(d,\epsilon)
. Then there exists a boundary divisor
\Delta
on
X
such that
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
is big and
(X,\Delta)
is
\epsilon
-plc. If
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
is nef, then obviously,
X\in\FT(d,\epsilon)
. Thus we assume that
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
is not nef. Since
X
is of Fano type, the section ring
R(-(K_{X}+\Delta))
is finitely generated. Let
f:Y\to X
be a log resolution of
(X,\Delta)
and let
-f^{\ast}(K_{X}+\Delta)=P+N
be the good Zariski decomposition. Write
K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}=f^{\ast}(K_{X}+\Delta)
. Take a general element
P_{0}\in|P|_{\mathbb{Q}}
and let
D:=f_{\ast}(P_{0}+N)
. Then
K_{X}+\Delta+D\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
and
f^{\ast}(K_{X}+\Delta+D)=K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}+N+P_{0}
. Since
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
is big, there are ample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
A
and an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta^{\prime}
such that
-(K_{X}+\Delta)\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}A+\Delta^{\prime}
. Let
\delta
be a positive real number. Consider the pair
(X,\Delta+\delta\Delta^{\prime}+(1-\delta)D)
. By construction,

f^{\ast}(K_{X}+\Delta+\delta\Delta^{\prime}+(1-\delta)D)=K_{Y}+\Delta_{Y}+N+\delta(f^{\ast}\Delta^{\prime}-N)+P_{0}.

If the coefficients of
f^{\ast}\Delta^{\prime}
are bounded, then we can show that the assertion is true by taking sufficiently small
\delta
. Since
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
is not nef, by the cone theorem (cf.
[
KM
, Theorem 3.7]
), there exists a rational curve
C
on
X
such that
0<-C\cdot(K_{X}+\Delta)\leq 2d
. Let
C^{\prime}
be a curve on
Y
such that
f(C^{\prime})=C
. Then we have

C^{\prime}\cdot f^{\ast}\Delta^{\prime}<C^{\prime}\cdot f^{\ast}(A+\Delta^{\prime})\leq 2d.

Therefore, for any
y\in Y
,

\operatorname{mult}_{y}C^{\prime}\cdot\operatorname{mult}_{y}f^{\ast}\Delta^{\prime}<2d.

Hence the coefficients of
f^{\ast}\Delta^{\prime}
is bounded.
∎

Since
\FT(d,\epsilon^{\prime})
is bounded by Theorem
4.1
, so is
\pFT(d,\epsilon)
.

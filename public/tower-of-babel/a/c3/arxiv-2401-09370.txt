Universality of the Brownian net
Rongfeng Sun, Jan M. Swart, Jinjiong Yu

Abstract
The Brownian web is a collection of one-dimensional coalescing Brownian motions starting from every point in space and time, while the Brownian net is an extension that also allows branching. We show here that the Brownian net is the universal scaling limit of one-dimensional branching-coalescing random walks with weak binary branching and arbitrary increment distributions that have finite ( 3 + ε ) (3+\varepsilon) -th moment. This gives the first example in the domain of attraction of the Brownian net where paths can cross without coalescing.

Keywords.
Branching-coalescing random walks, Brownian web, Brownian net, invariance principle.

1
Introduction and main result

The Brownian web
{\cal W}
is a collection of coalescing Brownian motions on
{\mathbb{R}}
, starting from every point
in the space-time plane
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. It originated in Arratia’s Ph.D. thesis
[
A79
]
, where he constructed
coalescing Brownian motions starting from every point in
{\mathbb{R}}
at time
0
. In a subsequent unpublished manuscript

[
A81
]
, his attempt to construct coalescing Brownian motions starting from every point in the space-time plane
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
was
incomplete due to the presence of special points in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
where multiple Brownian paths can emanate. The construction
was completed by Tóth and Werner in
[
TW98
]
, where a variant of Arratia’s coalescing Brownian motions played a crucial
role in the study of the so-called true self-repelling motion. They established detailed properties for Arratia’s
coalescing Brownian motions, including a complete classification of its special points. Subsequently, Fontes, Isopi, Newman and
Ravishankar
[
FINR02
,
FINR04
]
came across the same object in the study of aging in the zero-temperature dynamics of Ising and Potts
models on
{\mathbb{Z}}
. They introduced a topology for Arratia’s coalescing Brownian motions so that it becomes a random variable taking
values in a Polish space, and they named it the
Brownian web

{\cal W}
. They also provided a characterisation for the Brownian web,
which they used to prove the weak convergence of systems of coalescing simple random walks to the Brownian web.

In
[
SS08
]
, Sun and Swart made an important generalisation of the Brownian web
{\cal W}
by allowing paths to branch, which they
call the
Brownian net

{\cal N}
. To counter the instantaneous coalescence between paths, the branching rate is effectively “infinite”.
Independently, a different construction of the Brownian net was given by Newman, Ravishankar, and Schertzer in
[
NRS10
]
.

Since their inception, the Brownian web and the Brownian net have found connections to many other topics of research, including population
genetic models
[
AS11
,
GSW16
,
EFS17
]
, drainage network type models
[
CDF09
,
CV14
,
RSS16
,
PPR22
,
SVZ22
,
RSS23
,
VZ23
]
, Poisson webs

[
FFW05
,
FVV15
]
, the directed spanning forest
[
CSST21
]
, oriented percolation
[
SS11
,
BGS19
,
SS19
]
, planar aggregation models
[
NT12
,
NT15
]
, random walks in i.i.d. space-time random environments
[
SSS14
]
, interface growth models
[
Y16
,
CH23
,
VV23
,
DDP23
]
, traffic models
[
A17
]
, etc.
We refer to the survey article
[
SSS17
]
for a more comprehensive list of earlier results.

The Brownian web
{\cal W}
and the Brownian net
{\cal N}
are expected to be the universal scaling limits of one dimensional coalescing
systems, and branching-coalescing systems with weak branching, respectively. The universality of the Brownian web
{\cal W}
has been studied
extensively. Models which have been shown to converge to the Brownian web fall under two classes: either paths must coalesce whenever they attempt to cross each other, called the
non-crossing
case; or paths can cross each other without coalescing, called the
crossing case
. For the non-crossing case, convergence criteria were formulated in
[
FINR04
]
and has been verified for many models, including coalescing simple random walks
[
FINR04
]
, extremal paths in supercritical oriented percolation
[
SS11
,
SS19
]
, paths in drainage network models
[
CDF09
,
RSS16
]
, Poisson webs
[
FFW05
,
FVV15
]
, the directed spanning forest
[
CSST21
]
, etc. The crossing case is much more challenging due to the lack of ordering among paths. The first example
in the crossing case that was shown to converge to the Brownian web is the collection of coalescing random walks on
{\mathbb{Z}}
with general increment distributions, provided the increments have finite
(3+\eta)
-th moment for some
\eta>0

[
NRS05
,
BMSV06
]
. In
[
NRS05
]
, the authors formulated a set of convergence criteria tailored for the crossing case, which has also been verified for some drainage network models in
[
CV14
,
VZ23
]
.

The universality of the Brownian net
{\cal N}
has been much less studied. For branching-coalescing simple symmetric random walks on
{\mathbb{Z}}
with weak branching, i.e., the branching probability is proportional to the space-time diffusive scaling parameter
\varepsilon
, convergence to the Brownian net
{\cal N}
was established in
[
SS08
]
, which relies heavily on the
non-crossing
property that paths must meet before crossing.
In particular, the set of leftmost (resp. rightmost) paths starting from each point in the space-time lattice forms a non-crossing system of coalescing random walk paths. The only other model in the non-crossing case that has been shown to converge to the Brownian net is the set of genealogical paths of a spatial
\Lambda
-Fleming-Viot process considered in
[
EFS17
]
. There has also been partial progress for a drainage network model with weak branching
[
SVZ22
]
, which also belongs to the non-crossing case.

For the crossing case, the analogue of the convergence result in
[
NRS05
,
BMSV06
]
for the Brownian web
{\cal W}
is long believed to hold also for the Brownian net
{\cal N}
. The fact that such a result is only proved now is due to fundamental difficulties that arise from branching together with the lack of ordering
among paths. The main contribution of this paper is to overcome these difficulties and provide the first example in the crossing case that is shown to converge to the Brownian net
{\cal N}
. More precisely, we consider the system of
branching-coalescing random walks
on
{\mathbb{Z}}
with weak binary branching and increments that have finite
(3+\eta)
-th moment for some
\eta>0
. The ideas we develop here should also be useful for proving convergence to the Brownian net for other models in the crossing case.

1.1
Model and Main Result

We now introduce the collection of branching-coalescing random walk paths on
{\mathbb{Z}}
, which are discrete analogues of the Brownian net, and hence called
discrete nets
.

Let
a(\cdot)
be a probability kernel on
{\mathbb{Z}}
, which specifies the increment distribution for a random walk on
{\mathbb{Z}}
.
We assume that

\begin{array}[]{rl}{\rm(i)}&a(\cdot)\mbox{ defines an irreducible and aperiodic random walk on }{\mathbb{Z}},\\[5.0pt]
{\rm(ii)}&\displaystyle\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}}xa(x)=0,\\[5.0pt]
{\rm(iii)}&\displaystyle\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|x|^{3+\eta}a(x)<\infty\mbox{ for some }\eta>0.\end{array}

(1.1)

We denote
\sigma^{2}:=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}}x^{2}a(x)
throughout the rest of the paper.

Before introducing branching, we first give a graphical construction of coalescing random walks starting from every point of the
space-time lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}=\{(x,t):x,t\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
, which we call a
discrete web
. Let
\omega=(\omega(x,t))_{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}
be i.i.d. random variables with common distribution
a(\cdot)
. From each
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, we then draw an arrow from
(x,t)
to
(x+\omega(x,t),t+1)
. Note that for each

(x,t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, there is a unique path
\pi
starting from
(x,t)
by following the arrows, and two paths coalesce when they meet at the space-time
lattice point. We will denote by
\sigma_{\pi}:=t
the starting time of the path
\pi
, and identify
\pi:[\sigma_{\pi},\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}
with a continuous function by linearly interpolating between consecutive integer times. Given
\omega
, let
\pi_{(x,t)}
denote the random walk path starting from
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
. We then call the random set of paths

W:=\big\{\pi_{(x,t)}:(x,t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\big\}

(1.2)

a
discrete web with jump distribution
a(\cdot)
.

To allow for binary branching, we need to allow the possibility of two arrows coming out of each
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
. Let

(\omega^{1}(x,t),\omega^{2}(x,t))_{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}
be an i.i.d. family of
{\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}
-valued random variables with common law
a^{(2)}(\cdot,\cdot)
, to be specified in (
1.3
). Note that each of the two families
\omega^{1}=(\omega^{1}(x,t))_{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}
and
\omega^{2}=(\omega^{2}(x,t))_{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}
induces a set of arrows and a corresponding discrete web, which we denote by
W^{1}
and
W^{2}
respectively.
The paths between
W^{1}
and
W^{2}
undergo sticky interaction, where stickiness comes from
(x,t)
with
\omega^{1}(x,t)=\omega^{2}(x,t)
. If we consider all paths that can follow both types of arrows, induced by either
\omega^{1}
or
\omega^{2}
, then paths still coalesce when they meet at the same space-time in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, but a path can also branch into two at
(x,t)
with
\omega^{1}(x,t)\neq\omega^{2}(x,t)
. We call the set of all paths obtained by following arrows induced by either
\omega^{1}
or
\omega^{2}
a
discrete net
.

From now on, we assume that the law
a^{(2)}
is of the form

a^{(2)}(x_{1},x_{2}):=(1-\varepsilon)1_{\{x_{1}=x_{2}\}}a(x_{1})+\varepsilon a(x_{1})a(x_{2}),

(1.3)

which means that with probability
1-\varepsilon
, a single arrow comes out of
(x,t)
with law
a(\cdot)
; and with probability
\varepsilon
, two independent arrows with common law
a(\cdot)
are drawn from
(x,t)
, which coalesce at time
t+1
if they coincide. Let us denote the resulting discrete net by
N_{\varepsilon}
, which we call a
discrete net with (binary) branching probability
\varepsilon
and jump distribution
a(\cdot)
. The branching is weak in the sense that we will consider a sequence of such models with
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
.

Our main result is to show that under diffusive scaling of space-time (recall
\sigma
from (
1.1
))

S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}:(x,t)\to(\varepsilon x,\sigma^{2}\varepsilon^{2}t)

(1.4)

with the same
\varepsilon
as the branching probability in
N_{\varepsilon}
, the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
converges to a Brownian net
{\cal N}
.
With a slight overload of notation, for each path
\pi:[\sigma_{\pi},\infty)\to{\mathbb{R}}
, we will use
S_{\varepsilon}\pi
to denote the path whose graph is the image of the graph of
\pi
under
S_{\varepsilon}
, and for each set of paths
K
, we will use
S_{\varepsilon}K
to denote the set of paths obtained by applying
S_{\varepsilon}
to each path in
K
. In Section
2.1
, we will recall the topology introduced in
[
FINR04
]
for the Brownian web
{\cal W}
. In particular,
N_{\varepsilon}

and
{\cal N}
are both random subsets of the path space
(\Pi,d)
. Furthermore,
{\cal N}
and
\overline{N}_{\varepsilon}
, the closure of
N_{\varepsilon}
in
(\Pi,d)

(see Remark
2.1
), are both compact so that they are random variables taking values in the complete separable metric space
({\cal H},d_{\cal H})
of compact subsets of
(\Pi,d)
. To keep the notation simple, we will often write
N_{\varepsilon}
instead of
\overline{N}_{\varepsilon}
. Our main convergence result can then be formulated as follows.

Theorem 1.1 (Convergence to the Brownian net)

For
\varepsilon>0
, let
N_{\varepsilon}
be the discrete net with (binary) branching probability
\varepsilon
and jump distribution
a(\cdot)

satisfying (
1.1
). Then

S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}N_{\varepsilon}\underset{{\varepsilon}\to 0}{\Longrightarrow}{\cal N}\,,

(1.5)

where
{\cal N}
is the standard Brownian net, and
\Rightarrow
denotes weak convergence of
{\cal H}
-valued random variables.

Remark 1.2

In Theorem
1.1
, the finite
(3+\eta)
-th moment assumption on
a(\cdot)
in (
1.1
) is almost optimal.
It was shown in
[
BMSV06
, Theorem 1.1
(
ii
)
]
that if
\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\frac{|x|^{3}}{\log^{\beta}(|x|+2)}a(x)=\infty
for some
\beta>1
, then
the laws of the rescaled discrete web
(S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W)_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
fails to be tight due to the presence of arbitrarily large jumps
originating from any finite space-time domain. Therefore the laws of
(S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}N_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
also fail to be tight for such
a(\cdot)
.

Remark 1.3

In Theorem
1.1
, if we used the scaling map
\widetilde{S}_{\varepsilon,\sigma}:(x,t)\to(\varepsilon x/\sigma,\varepsilon^{2}t)
instead of
S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}

defined in (
1.4
), then the limit in (
1.5
) will be the image of
{\cal N}
under the map
(x,t)\to(x/\sigma,t/\sigma^{2})
, which is a Brownian net
{\cal N}_{\sigma}
with branching parameter
\sigma
, see Theorem
2.7
.

1.2
Proof strategy

We first recall from
[
SS08
]
the proof strategy for branching-coalescing simple random walks on
{\mathbb{Z}}
, which is a non-crossing system where paths must
meet before crossing (see also the survey article
[
SSS17
]
). We will then explain the fundamental difficulties posed by crossing systems and how our proof strategy overcomes these difficulties for the system of branching-coalescing random walks we consider.

Non-crossing Case.

In
[
SS08
]
, the authors considered the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
on the even space-time lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\rm even}^{2}:=\{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}:x+t\mbox{ is even}\}
, where the jump distribution is given by
a(\pm 1)=1/2
and each
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}_{\rm even}
is a branching point with probability
\varepsilon
.
The key observation behind the construction of the Brownian net
{\cal N}
in
[
SS08
]
is that, if we consider the discrete webs
W_{\varepsilon}^{\rm l},W_{\varepsilon}^{\rm r}\subset N_{\varepsilon}
, which consist of the leftmost (reps. rightmost) paths in
N_{\varepsilon}
starting from every
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}_{\rm even}
,
then under the diffusive scaling map
S_{\varepsilon}:=S_{\varepsilon,1}
(with
\sigma=1
),
S_{\varepsilon}W_{\varepsilon}^{\rm l}
(resp.
S_{\varepsilon}W_{\varepsilon}^{\rm r}
) converges in distribution to a Brownian web
{\cal W}^{\rm l}
(resp.
{\cal W}^{\rm r}
) consisting of coalescing Brownian motions with drift
-1
(resp.
+1
). Furthermore,

(S_{\varepsilon}W_{\varepsilon}^{\rm l},S_{\varepsilon}W_{\varepsilon}^{\rm r})
converges jointly to a pair of coupled Brownian webs
({\cal W}^{\rm l},{\cal W}^{\rm r})
, called the
left-right web
, where paths in
{\cal W}^{\rm l}
and
{\cal W}^{\rm r}
undergo sticky interaction. The fact that paths in
N_{\varepsilon}
cannot cross without meeting ensures that there is a well-defined leftmost (resp. rightmost) path in
N_{\varepsilon}
starting from each
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}_{\rm even}
.

Note that paths in the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
can always be constructed by concatenating paths in the pair of discrete webs
(W_{\varepsilon}^{\rm l},W_{\varepsilon}^{\rm r})
.
This motivated the construction of the Brownian net
{\cal N}
in
[
SS08
]
by concatenating paths in the left-right web
({\cal W}^{\rm l},{\cal W}^{\rm r})
and
then taking closure in the path space
(\Pi,d)
(concatenation between two paths is only allowed at a time of coincidence between the two paths that is
strictly larger than their starting times). This is called the
hopping construction
of the
standard Brownian net

{\cal N}
from the left-right web
({\cal W}^{\rm l},{\cal W}^{\rm r})
, where standard refers to the fact that the Brownian paths in
{\cal W}^{\rm l}
and
{\cal W}^{\rm r}
have drift
-1
and
+1
respectively.

In
[
SS08
]
, the proof of convergence of the rescaled discrete nets
(S_{\varepsilon}N_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
to the Brownian net
{\cal N}
consists of
three steps: a lower bound via hopping, an upper bound via wedges, and tightness via leftmost and rightmost paths.

To show the lower bound that any subsequential weak limit of
(S_{\varepsilon}N_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
contains a copy of
{\cal N}
, thanks to the hopping construction of the Brownian net, it suffices to show that the
concatenation between any two paths in
({\cal W}^{\rm l},{\cal W}^{\rm r})
can be approximated by the concatenation of paths in the rescaled discrete webs

(S_{\varepsilon}W^{\rm l}_{\varepsilon},S_{\varepsilon}W^{\rm r}_{\varepsilon})
. This is an easy consequence of the convergence
(S_{\varepsilon}W_{\varepsilon}^{\rm l},S_{\varepsilon}W_{\varepsilon}^{\rm r})\Rightarrow({\cal W}^{\rm l},{\cal W}^{\rm r})
.

To show the upper bound that any subsequential weak limit of
(S_{\varepsilon}N_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
contains no additional paths besides
{\cal N}
, the proof
in
[
SS08
]
(also in
[
EFS17
]
) relied on the so-called
wedge characterization
of the Brownian net given in
[
SS08
]
(see also the survey
[
SSS17
]
), which uses the dual of the left-right web
({\cal W}^{\rm l},{\cal W}^{\rm r})
to identify forbidden regions (called wedges) that cannot be entered by any path in
{\cal N}
. But the definition of wedges also requires
N_{\varepsilon}
to have well-defined leftmost and rightmost paths.

Proving tightness for the family of rescaled discrete nets
(S_{\varepsilon}N_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
amounts to controlling the uniform modulus of continuity
of paths in
S_{\varepsilon}N_{\varepsilon}
, see
[
FINR04
, Prop. B.1]
or Prop.
6.1
below. Since all paths in
N_{\varepsilon}
starting from each
(x,t)
are bounded between the unique path in
W^{\rm l}_{\varepsilon}
, resp.
W^{\rm r}_{\varepsilon}
, starting from
(x,t)
, the uniform modulus of continuity of paths in
N_{\varepsilon}
is controlled by the uniform modulus of continuity of paths in
W_{\varepsilon}^{\rm l}\cup W_{\varepsilon}^{\rm r}
. The tightness of
(S_{\varepsilon}N_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
then follows directly from the tightness of the rescaled discrete webs
(S_{\varepsilon}W_{\varepsilon}^{\rm l})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
and
(S_{\varepsilon}W_{\varepsilon}^{\rm r})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
, which follows from the convergence of
S_{\varepsilon}W_{\varepsilon}^{\rm l}\Rightarrow{\cal W}^{\rm l}
and
S_{\varepsilon}W_{\varepsilon}^{\rm r}\Rightarrow{\cal W}^{\rm r}
.

Of the three steps above, the upper bound and tightness proof depend crucially on the non-crossing property, which implies that every path in

N_{\varepsilon}
is bounded between the leftmost and rightmost path in
N_{\varepsilon}
starting from the same point. These arguments have to be replaced by completely new ideas in the crossing case. Only the idea of giving a lower bound via hopping has a natural extension in the crossing case.

We now explain our proof strategy for the branching-coalescing systems we consider, which belongs to the crossing case.

Lower Bound.
Since there is no longer a discrete left-right web
(W^{\rm l}_{\varepsilon},W^{\rm r}_{\varepsilon})
, we will sample
two discrete webs
(W^{1},W^{2})
as defined after (
1.2
), so that the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
can still be constructed by concatenating paths
in
W^{1}
and
W^{2}
. We will show in Theorem
3.1
that
(S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W^{1},S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W^{2})
converges in distribution to a pair of sticky Brownian webs
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
, whose construction and characterisation will be recalled in Section
2.3
. Similar to the hopping construction of the Brownian net
{\cal N}
from the left right web
({\cal W}^{\rm l},{\cal W}^{\rm r})
, we can also construct a Brownian net by concatenating paths in
{\cal W}^{1}\cup{\cal W}^{2}
and then taking closure. The lower bound is then obtained by showing that every subsequential weak limit of
(S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}N_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
contains a copy of
{\cal N}
constructed from
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
via the hopping construction. The key step in the proof is the convergence
(S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W^{1},S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W^{2})\Rightarrow({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
, which requires finding a suitable harmonic function for a random walk with sticky interaction at the origin (see Prop.
3.4
).

Upper Bound.
To show that every subsequential weak limit of
(S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}N_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
contains no additional paths besides
the Brownian net
{\cal N}
constructed in the lower bound, we use a different density argument than in
[
SS08
]
. The key observation is that we can dominate the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
by an auxiliary branching-coalescing system which we call the
Bernoulli net

\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}
, see Section
4
. In particular, the Bernoulli net allows branching with arbitrary number of offsprings. What is
special about the Bernoulli net
\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}
is that we can identify explicitly a product invariant law for the branching-coalescing random walks in
\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}
,
which converges under diffusive scaling to a Poisson point process with intensity
2
on
{\mathbb{R}}
and is precisely the invariant law for the branching-coalescing Brownian motions in the Brownian net
{\cal N}
. This convergence can then be used to rule out the existence of additional paths in the limit besides paths in
{\cal N}
. A key ingredient in our proof is Theorem
4.6
, which shows that for the set of paths in the Bernoulli net
\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}
started from every point in
{\mathbb{Z}}
at time
0
, their density at large time
t>0
still decays at the rate of
1/\sqrt{t}
, which is comparable to coalescing random walks without branching.

Tightness.
In contrast to the non-crossing case, where tightness comes for free as a result of tightness of the rescaled left right webs
(S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W^{\rm l}_{\varepsilon},S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W^{\rm r}_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
, in our case, tightness is the most technical part of the proof. The overall strategy follows the multiscale argument developed in
[
BMSV06
]
, which proves tightness for the rescaled discrete webs (coalescing random walks)
(S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W)_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
under the same assumption on
a(\cdot)
as in (
1.1
). We go through an exponentially increasing sequence of space-time scales. From one scale to the next, we exploit the coalescence to reduce the density of walks as the scale increases, and at the same time, we control the probability that at least one of the walks makes a large excursion that leads to a poor modulus of continuity. Although this overall strategy is not new, the technical difficulties of incorporating branching are serious and we find it the most difficult part of the proof and took us the longest to complete.

For branching-coalescing random walks with weak branching as we consider, coalescence dominates on small scales. So the density reduction argument can still be applied. However, the difficulty is that even though the branching is weak, a single random walk could still have an arbitrarily large number of
descendants on large time scales. Controlling how far a single random walk and any of its descendants can travel within a short time becomes the key challenge in the crossing case, while in the
non-crossing case, it suffices to consider the large deviation of the leftmost and the rightmost random walk among all the descendants. We overcome this difficulty by controlling the number of
relevant branching points
in the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
(see Theorem
6.9
), which are space-time points where a descendant of
the original random walk (starting at some initial time
S
) branches into two random walks, and each random walk has a descendant such that the two descendants do not meet before some terminal time
T
. The relevant branching points are the branching points that lead to new random walk positions at time
T
. To control the large deviation of all descendants of a single random walk, it suffices to group the descendants according to the choice made at each relevant branching point and then control the large deviation of a random walk sampled from each group.

Remark 1.4

The universality of the Brownian net
{\cal N}
should hold beyond the family of branching-coalescing random walks considered in Theorem
1.1
. For example, we could consider branching-coalescing random walks in continuous time that arise naturally as the dual of biased voter models
[
SSY19
,
SSY21
]
. In particular, consider the setting where each random walk jumps at rate
1
with kernel
a(\cdot)
satisfying the assumptions in (
1.1
), and at rate
\varepsilon>0
, each random walk gives birth to a new particle which immediately makes a jump with kernel
a(\cdot)
, while particles coalesce when they meet. We believe our proof can be extended without much difficulty to this continuous time model. One element becomes even simpler, namely that by a generator calculation, it can be shown that this branching-coalescing random walk admits a product invariant measure. Therefore we no longer need the Bernoulli net introduced in Section
4
. We could also consider other systems such as branching-coalescing random walks with non-binary branching, although these lie beyond our current techniques because they do not admit explicit invariant measures and cannot be embedded in a Bernoulli net. Nevertheless, we hope that our methods can be extended in the future to cover such systems as well.

1.3
Outline

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some necessary background on the Brownian web, the Brownian net, and sticky Brownian webs. Assuming tightness of the family of rescaled discrete nets
(S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}\overline{N}_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
, we show in Section
3
that every subsequential weak limit of
S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}\overline{N}_{\varepsilon}
contains a copy of the standard Brownian net
{\cal N}
. In Section
4
, we introduce an auxiliary Bernoulli net which admits an explicit invariant measure and dominates the discrete net. In Section
5
, we use the domination by the Bernoulli net to show that every subsequential weak
limit of
S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}\overline{N}_{\varepsilon}
contains no additional paths besides the Brownian net
{\cal N}
. In Section
6
, we prove the tightness for the family of rescaled discrete nets
(S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}\overline{N}_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
. Lastly, in Appendix
A
, we sketch
the proof of the equivalence between two alternative constructions of a pair of sticky Brownian webs, and the construction of the Brownian net from sticky Brownian webs.

Background

We first recall in Subsection
2.1
the space of compact sets of paths in which the Brownian web
{\cal W}
and the Brownian net
{\cal N}
take their values. Then in Subsections
2.2
,
2.3
and
2.4
, we introduce necessary results on the Brownian web, sticky Brownian webs, and the Brownian net, respectively. Proposition
2.6
below on the equivalence of two alternative constructions of a pair of sticky Brownian webs and

2.1
Space of compact sets of paths

We recall here the Polish space
{\cal H}
of compact sets of paths, where the Brownian web and the Brownian net take their values. This space was first introduced in
[
FINR04
]
, and we follow the variant defined in
[
SS08
, Appendix]
.

Let
R^{2}_{\rm c}:={\mathbb{R}}^{2}\cup\{(\pm\infty,t):t\in{\mathbb{R}}\}\cup\{(\ast,\pm\infty)\}
be the compactification of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
obtained by equipping the set

R^{2}_{\rm c}
with a
topology such that
(x_{n},t_{n})\to(\pm\infty,t)
if
x_{n}\to\pm\infty
and

t_{n}\to t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
(x_{n},t_{n})\to(\ast,\pm\infty)
if
t_{n}\to\pm\infty

(regardless of the behavior of
x_{n}
). Such a compactification can be achieved
by taking the completion of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with respect to the metric

\rho((x_{1},t_{1}),(x_{2},t_{2}))=|\Theta_{1}(x_{1},t_{1})-\Theta_{1}(x_{2},t_{2})|\vee|\Theta_{2}(t_{1})-\Theta_{2}(t_{2})|,

(2.1)

where the map
\Theta=(\Theta_{1},\Theta_{2})
is defined by

\Theta(x,t)=\big(\Theta_{1}(x,t),\Theta_{2}(t)\big):=\Big(\frac{\tanh(x)}{1+|t|},\tanh(t)\Big).

(2.2)

We can think of
R^{2}_{\rm c}
as the image of
[-\infty,\infty]^{2}
under the
map
\Theta
.

A
path
in
R^{2}_{\rm c}
is any continuous function

\pi:[\sigma_{\pi},\infty]\to[-\infty,\infty]\cup\{\ast\}
, with

\sigma_{\pi}\in[-\infty,\infty]
, such that

\pi:[\sigma_{\pi},\infty]\cap{\mathbb{R}}\to[-\infty,\infty]
is continuous, and

\pi(\pm\infty)=\ast
whenever
\pm\infty\in[\sigma_{\pi},\infty]
. Equivalently,
if we identify
R^{2}_{\rm c}
with the image of
[-\infty,\infty]^{2}
under the map

\Theta
, then
\pi
can be identified with its graph, which is a compact subset of
\Theta([-\infty,\infty]^{2})
with the property that
for each
t\in[\Theta_{2}(\sigma_{\pi}),\Theta_{2}(\infty)]
, there is exactly one
x
with
(x,t)
on the graph of
\pi
. We will often identify
\pi
with
its graph in the rest of the paper.

Let
\Pi
denote the space of all paths in
R^{2}_{\rm c}
, equipped with the metric

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}\displaystyle d(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})&:=&\displaystyle|\Theta_{2}(\sigma_{\pi_{1}})-\Theta_{2}(\sigma_{\pi_{2}})|\\[5.0pt]
&&\displaystyle\quad\vee\sup_{t\geq\sigma_{\pi_{1}}\wedge\sigma_{\pi_{2}}}\big|\Theta_{1}\big(\pi_{1}(t\vee\sigma_{\pi_{1}}),t)-\Theta_{1}(\pi_{2}(t\vee\sigma_{\pi_{2}}),t\big)\big|.\end{array}

(2.3)

The space
(\Pi,d)
is complete and separable. Note that paths
converge in
(\Pi,d)
if and only if their starting times converge and
the functions converge locally uniformly on
{\mathbb{R}}
.

Let
{\cal H}
be the space of compact subsets of
(\Pi,d)
,
equipped with the Hausdorff metric
d_{\cal H}
. Namely, for
{\cal X}_{1},{\cal X}_{2}\in{\cal H}
,

d_{{\cal H}}({\cal X}_{1},{\cal X}_{2})=\sup_{x_{1}\in{\cal X}_{1}}\inf_{x_{2}\in{\cal X}_{2}}d(x_{1},x_{2})\vee\sup_{x_{2}\in{\cal X}_{2}}\inf_{x_{1}\in{\cal X}_{1}}d(x_{1},x_{2}).

(2.4)

The metric space
({\cal H},d_{\cal H})
is also complete and separable. For more properties,
see e.g.
[
SSS14
, Appendix B]
. Let
(\widehat{\cal H},d_{\widehat{\cal H}})
be defined similarly to
({\cal H},d_{\cal H})
,
except that all paths run backwards in time.

Remark 2.1

To view the discrete nets
N_{\varepsilon}
as random variables taking values in
{\cal H}
, we need to
take the closure of
N_{\varepsilon}
in
(\Pi,d)
and show that
\overline{N}_{\varepsilon}
is compact.
This is easily verified if the jump distribution
a(\cdot)
in (
1.1
) has a finite
first moment, see e.g.
[
NRS05
, Lemma 1.1]
. It is then easily seen
that
\overline{N}_{\varepsilon}\backslash N_{\varepsilon}
only contains the trivial paths
\pi
with

\sigma_{\pi}\in\{\pm\infty\}\cup{\mathbb{Z}}
, and either
\pi(t)\equiv-\infty
or
\pi(t)\equiv\infty
for all
t\in[\sigma_{\pi},\infty)
.
To keep the notation simple, we will write
N_{\varepsilon}
instead of
\overline{N}_{\varepsilon}
in the rest of the paper.

We will adopt the following notational convention:

•

For
{\cal X}\subset{\cal H}
and
A\subset R^{2}_{\rm c}
, let
{\cal X}(A)
denote the subset of paths in
{\cal X}

with starting points in
A
. Sometimes we will also write
{\cal X}(A)
as
{\cal X}^{A}
.

•

For
A=\{z\}
, we simply write
{\cal X}(z)
instead of
{\cal X}(\{z\})
.

2.2
The Brownian web

We recall from
[
FINR04
]
the characterization
of the Brownian web
{\cal W}
as a random variable taking values in the complete separable metric space
({\cal H},d_{\cal H})
.

Theorem 2.2 (Characterization of the Brownian web)

There exists an
{\cal H}
-valued random variable
{\cal W}
, called the standard Brownian web, whose
distribution is uniquely determined by the following properties:

(i)

For each deterministic
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, almost surely there is a
unique path
\pi_{z}\in{\cal W}(z)
.

(ii)

For any finite deterministic set of points

z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, the paths
(\pi_{z_{1}},\ldots,\pi_{z_{k}})
are
distributed as a collection of coalescing standard Brownian motions.

(iii)

For any deterministic countable dense subset
{\cal D}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, almost surely,
{\cal W}
is the closure of

\{\pi_{z}:z\in{\cal D}\}
in
(\Pi,d)
.

The Brownian web
{\cal W}
can be coupled to a dual Brownian web
\widehat{\cal W}\in\widehat{\cal H}
, which consists of coalescing Brownian motions running backwards in time and has the same distribution as
{\cal W}
if space-time is rotated by
180^{\circ}
around the origin. Almost surely,
{\cal W}
and
\widehat{\cal W}
determine each other by the condition that
paths in
{\cal W}
cannot cross paths in
\widehat{\cal W}
, i.e., if
\pi\in{\cal W}
and
\hat{\pi}\in\widehat{\cal W}
, then we cannot find
s<t
with
(\pi(s)-\hat{\pi}(s))(\pi(t)-\hat{\pi}(t))<0
. Furthermore, paths in
{\cal W}
and
\widehat{\cal W}
interact through Skorohod reflection. For further properties and alternative characterizations of
({\cal W},\widehat{\cal W})
, see the survey article
[
SSS17
]
and the references therein.

The following analogue of Theorem
1.1
for the Brownian web
{\cal W}
is due to
[
NRS05
]
, which assumed finite
5
-th moment for the random walk
increments in the proof of tightness, and it was then improved in
[
BMSV06
]
to the nearly optimal assumption of a finite
(3+\eta)
-th moment.

Theorem 2.3 (Convergence to the Brownian web)

Let
W
be the discrete web defined in (
1.2
) with jump distribution
a(\cdot)

satisfying (
1.1
). Then

S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W\underset{{\varepsilon}\to 0}{\Longrightarrow}{\cal W},

(2.5)

where
{\cal W}
is the standard Brownian web, and
\Rightarrow
denotes weak convergence of
{\cal H}
-valued random variables.

2.3
Sticky Brownian webs

The Brownian net
{\cal N}
was first constructed in
[
SS08
]
(see
[
NRS10
]
for a later alternative construction), which generalises the Brownian web
{\cal W}
by allowing paths to branch. An intermediate object in the construction of the Brownian net
{\cal N}
in
[
SS08
]
is a pair of sticky Brownian webs

({\cal W}^{\rm l},{\cal W}^{\rm r})
with drift
-1
and
+1
respectively. For our purpose, we will give an alternative construction using a pair
of
sticky Brownian webs

({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
with the same drift
0
.

We first motivate the sticky Brownian webs (with drift
0
) through discrete approximation.
Recall the definition of the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
in Section
1.1
, which is constructed from an i.i.d. family of
random pairs
(\omega^{1}(x,t),\omega^{2}(x,t))_{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}
with common law
a^{(2)}
that satisfies (
1.3
). Each
family
(\omega^{i}(x,t))_{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}
,
i=1,2
, determines a discrete web
W^{i}
, and by Theorem
2.3
,
S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W^{i}
converges in distribution to a Brownian web
{\cal W}^{i}
. In the special case when
\omega^{i}(x,t)\in\{\pm 1\}
so that the paths make nearest neighbour jumps, and when we only consider paths starting from the even lattice sites
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}_{\rm even}:=\{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}:x+t\mbox{ is even}\}
to avoid parity issues, results from
[
SSS14
]
imply that the pair

(S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W^{1},S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W^{2})
converges to a pair of coupled Brownian webs
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
, called
sticky Brownian webs
because paths between
{\cal W}^{1}
and
{\cal W}^{2}
undergo sticky interaction.

Instead of the discrete webs
(W^{1},W^{2})
defined above,
[
SS08
]
considered the discrete webs
W^{\rm l}_{\varepsilon}
and
W^{\rm r}_{\varepsilon}
, induced respectively by

\omega^{\rm l}(x,t)=\omega^{1}(x,t)\wedge\omega^{2}(x,t),\qquad\omega^{\rm r}(x,t)=\omega^{1}(x,t)\vee\omega^{2}(x,t),

(2.6)

so that whenever two (nearest neighbour) arrows originate from
(x,t)
, paths in
W^{\rm l}_{\varepsilon}
follow the arrow to the left and paths in
W^{\rm r}_{\varepsilon}
follow the arrow to the right. It was shown in
[
SS08
]
that
(S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W^{\rm l}_{\varepsilon},S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W^{\rm r}_{\varepsilon})
converges to a pair of sticky Brownian webs
({\cal W}^{\rm l},{\cal W}^{\rm r})
with drift
-1
and
+1
respectively, called the
left-right Brownian web
. The key observation of
[
SS08
]
is that, since the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
can be constructed by concatenating paths in the two discrete webs
W^{\rm l}_{\varepsilon}
and
W^{\rm r}_{\varepsilon}
(i.e., switching back and forth between arrows induced by either
\omega^{\rm l}
and
\omega^{\rm r}
), the limit of
S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}N_{\varepsilon}
can also be recovered by concatenating paths in the pair of sticky Brownian webs
({\cal W}^{\rm l},{\cal W}^{\rm r})
. This was called the
hopping construction
of the Brownian net
{\cal N}
. Since the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
can also be constructed by concatenating paths in the two discrete webs
W^{1}
and
W^{2}
(i.e., switching back and forth between arrows induced by either
\omega^{1}
and
\omega^{2}
), we can also recover
{\cal N}
by concatenating paths in the pair of sticky Brownian webs
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
with drift
0
. This is the content of Theorem
2.7
below.

We will mostly work with the sticky Brownian webs
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
with drift
0
, for which we will need two equivalent characterizations. The first was introduced in
[
HW09b
]
, which we call the
martingale characterization
. We will use it to prove
(S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W^{1},S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W^{2})\Rightarrow({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
for general jump distribution
a(\cdot)
satisfying (
1.1
). The second was introduced in
[
SSS14
]
, which we call the
marking construction
. It constructs the pair of sticky Brownian webs
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
and the Brownian net
{\cal N}
simultaneously through a Poisson marking procedure that was first developed in
[
NRS10
]
. This allows us to recover the Brownian net
{\cal N}
from
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
through the
hopping construction
sketched above, stated in Theorem
2.7
below. We will show that these two characterizations of the sticky Brownian webs give the same object, so that we can choose the most convenient characterization depending on the context.

Martingale Characterization.
Following
[
HW09a
]
,
(Y^{1},Y^{2})=(Y^{1}_{t},Y^{2}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is called a pair
of
sticky Brownian motions with parameter

\theta\geq 0
if
Y^{1}
and
Y^{2}
are standard Brownian motions w.r.t. some common filtration
({\cal F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, and the following processes are martingales
w.r.t. this filtration

\begin{array}[]{rl}{\rm(i)}&\displaystyle Y^{1}_{t}Y^{2}_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{Y^{1}_{s}=Y^{2}_{s}\}}\mathrm{d}s,\\[5.0pt]
{\rm(ii)}&\displaystyle|Y^{1}_{t}-Y^{2}_{t}|-2\theta\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{Y^{1}_{s}=Y^{2}_{s}\}}\mathrm{d}s.\end{array}

(2.7)

By
[
HW09b
, Prop. 3]
, we have the following fact.

Proposition 2.4 (Characterization of sticky Brownian motions)

For each
\theta\geq 0
and
(y_{1},y_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, there exists a pair
(Y^{1},Y^{2})

of sticky Brownian motions with parameter
\theta\geq 0
and initial
state
(Y^{1}_{0},Y^{2}_{0})=(y_{1},y_{2})
, and such a pair is unique in law.

We can readily extend the definition of sticky Brownian motions to the case where
Y^{1}

and
Y^{2}
start at different times. Note that the smaller the value of
\theta
is, the stronger
is the stickiness. When
\theta=0
,
(Y^{1},Y^{2})
is a pair of coalescing Brownian motions.
The case where
Y^{1},Y^{2}
have nonzero drifts is are treated in
[
HW09b
, Prop. 14]
. In particular,
when
Y^{1}
has a smaller drift than
Y^{2}
and the pair
(Y^{1},Y^{2})
stays ordered once they meet,

(Y^{1},Y^{2})
is equal in distribution to the unique solution of the
left-right SDE
in
[
SS08
, Sec. 2]
.

Note that each Brownian web
{\cal W}^{i}
defines in a natural way a filtration
{\cal F}^{i}_{t}
, which is the

\sigma
-field generated by the restriction of all paths
in
{\cal W}^{i}
to times
\leq t
. Let
{\cal F}_{t}:={\cal F}^{1}_{t}\vee{\cal F}^{2}_{t}:=\sigma({\cal F}^{1}\cup{\cal F}^{2})
be the smallest

\sigma
-field containing
{\cal F}^{1}_{t}
and
{\cal F}^{2}_{t}
. Then
{\cal F}_{t}
also forms a filtration. We can now state the
martingale characterization of a pair of sticky Brownian webs
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
, which is a variant of

[
HW09b
, Thm. 4]
and
[
SS19
, Thm. 1]
.

Theorem 2.5 (Martingale characterization of sticky Brownian webs)

There exists an
{\cal H}\times{\cal H}
-valued random variable
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
, called a pair of sticky Brownian webs
with parameter
\theta\geq 0
, whose distribution is uniquely determined by the following properties:

(i)

Both
{\cal W}^{1}
and
{\cal W}^{2}
are distributed as the standard Brownian web;

(ii)

For each deterministic pair
z=(x,t),z^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, the pair of paths

(X^{1}_{z},X^{2}_{z^{\prime}})
, with
X^{1}_{z}\in{\cal W}^{1}(z)
and
X^{2}_{z^{\prime}}\in{\cal W}^{2}(z^{\prime})
, is distributed
as a pair of sticky Brownian motions with parameter
\theta
relative to the filtration
({\cal F}_{u})_{u\in{\mathbb{R}}}
;

(iii)

(
Co-adaptedness
)
For each deterministic pair
z=(x,t),z^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, if
X^{i}_{z}\in{\cal W}^{i}(z)

and
X^{j}_{z^{\prime}}\in{\cal W}^{j}(z^{\prime})
for
i,j=1,2
, then the pair
(X^{i}_{z},X^{j}_{z^{\prime}})
is a Markov process relative to the filtration

({\cal F}_{u})_{u\in{\mathbb{R}}}
.

This formulation follows that of
[
SS19
, Thm. 1]
for the
left-right Brownian web
, cf. (
2.6
). The
formulation for sticky Brownian webs was discussed in the proof of
[
SS19
, Thm. 1]
.
Here, co-adaptedness is formulated differently than in
[
HW09b
, Thm. 4]
in order to make it more clear how
[
H07
, Thm. 76]
can be applied to show that the law of
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
is uniquely determined by conditions
(i)-(ii) plus co-adaptedness (see the proof of
[
SS19
, Thm. 1]
for more details).

Marking Construction.
We now sketch the marking construction of the sticky Brownian webs
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
from

[
SSS14
, Section 3]
, which is an extension of the marking construction of the Brownian net in
[
NRS10
]
. We refer the reader to these
references for details.

Given a Brownian web
{\cal W}
and its dual
\widehat{\cal W}
, each pair
(\pi,\hat{\pi})\in({\cal W},\widehat{\cal W})

interacts through Skorohod reflection. In particular, there is a well-defined
intersection local time measure

\ell
on the intersection between the graphs of
\pi
and
\hat{\pi}
, for
\pi\in{\cal W}
and
\hat{\pi}\in\widehat{\cal W}
(see
[
SSS14
, Prop. 3.4]
). Each point of intersection
(x,t)
between a path
\pi\in{\cal W}
with
\sigma_{\pi}<t
, and
\hat{\pi}\in\widehat{\cal W}
with
\sigma_{\hat{\pi}}>t
, is called a
special point of
{\cal W}
of type
(1,2)
because
\pi
is the unique
path in
{\cal W}
passing through
(x,t)
, and besides
\pi
, there is a second path
\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal W}
that starts from
(x,t)
.
We then define a Poisson point set
S
on all such
(1,2)
points with intensity measure
\theta\ell
. Given
{\cal W}
and

S
, we then construct a second set of paths
{\cal W}^{\prime}
such that paths in
{\cal W}^{\prime}
follow paths in
{\cal W}
, except when a path
p\in{\cal W}^{\prime}
enters a point
(x,t)\in S
, instead of continuing along
\pi\in{\cal W}
that passes through
(x,t)
,
p
switches to the
second path
\pi^{\prime}\in{\cal W}
that starts from
(x,t)
. It was shown in
[
SSS14
, Theorem 3.5]
that
{\cal W}^{\prime}
is well-defined, and the pair
({\cal W},{\cal W}^{\prime})
is called a
pair of sticky Brownian webs with coupling parameter
\theta
. If we include both the path that continues along
\pi
and the path
that continues along
\pi^{\prime}
, then we obtain a Brownian net, see
[
SSS14
, Theorem 3.5 and 6.15]
.

We will show in Appendix
A
that the two definitions of sticky Brownian webs above are equivalent.

Proposition 2.6 (Equivalence)

Let
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
be a pair of sticky Brownian webs with parameter
\theta\geq 0
defined in Theorem
2.5
, and let
({\cal W},{\cal W}^{\prime})
a pair of sticky Brownian webs with coupling parameter
\theta
defined via the marking construction above. Then
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
and
({\cal W},{\cal W}^{\prime})
have the same distribution.

Both the martingale characterization and the marking construction of sticky Brownian webs can be extended to the case with non-zero drifts and parameter
\theta\geq 0
. For simplicity, we have restricted to the case of zero drift. When
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
is a
left-right Brownian web
, where
{\cal W}^{1}
is a Brownian web with a smaller drift than
{\cal W}_{2}
and
any pair
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})\in({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
stay ordered once they meet, it was shown in
[
SSS14
, Lemma 6.18]
that the marking construction of the left-right web is equivalent to a characterization similar to Theorem
2.5
.

2.4
The Brownian net

The Brownian net
{\cal N}
was first defined in
[
SS08
]
as an extension of the Brownian web
{\cal W}
by allowing branching.
To counter the instantaneous coalescence between paths, the
branching rate
is effectively infinite. The actual construction, called
hopping construction
in
[
SS08
, Thm. 1.3]
, was motivated by the discrete analogue discussed after (
2.6
), where
{\cal N}
was constructed by
concatenating paths in the left-right web
({\cal W}^{\rm l},{\cal W}^{\rm r})
.

The left-right web
({\cal W}^{\rm l},{\cal W}^{\rm r})
is characterized by the properties that
{\cal W}^{\rm l}
, resp.
{\cal W}^{\rm r}
, is a Brownian web with drift
-1
, resp.
+1
, and any pair of paths
(l,r)\in({\cal W}^{\rm l},{\cal W}^{\rm r})
with
deterministic starting points evolve as a pair of sticky Brownian motions with drifts
(-1,1)
and stays ordered once they meet. More generally, the left-right web
({\cal W}^{\rm l},{\cal W}^{\rm r})
could have arbitrary drifts
(a,b)
with
a<b
. We refer the reader to
[
SS08
]
for the precise characterization of
({\cal W}^{\rm l},{\cal W}^{\rm r})
. Our goal here is to show that
{\cal N}
can also be constructed by concatenation (hopping) between paths in the pair of sticky Brownian webs
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
.

First, we give the precise definition of hopping between paths in
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
, which also applies to
({\cal W}^{\rm l},{\cal W}^{\rm r})
. Given two paths
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi
,
we call
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
an
intersection time
if
\sigma_{\pi},\sigma_{\pi^{\prime}}<t<\infty
and
\pi(t)=\pi^{\prime}(t)
. At such an intersection time

t
, we can then define a new path
\pi^{\prime\prime}
by concatenating the piece of
\pi
before
t
with the piece of
\pi^{\prime}
after

t
, i.e., by setting
\pi^{\prime\prime}(s):=\pi(s)
for
s\in[\sigma_{\pi},t]
and
\pi^{\prime\prime}(s)=\pi^{\prime}(s)
for
s\in[t,\infty)
. For
any collection of paths
{\cal A}\subset\Pi
, we let
{\cal H}_{\rm int}({\cal A})
denote the
smallest set of paths containing
{\cal A}
that is closed under such ‘hopping’
from one path onto another at intersection times, i.e.,
{\cal H}_{\rm int}({\cal A})

is the set of all paths
\pi\in\Pi
of the form

\pi(s):=\pi_{k}(s)\mbox{ for }s\in[t_{k-1},t_{k}],\quad 1\leq k\leq m,

(2.8)

where
\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{m}\in{\cal A}
,
\sigma_{\pi_{1}}=t_{0}<\cdots<t_{m}=\infty
,
and
t_{k}
is an intersection time of
\pi_{k}
and

In

was constructed by setting it to be the closure

{\cal N}:=\overline{{\cal H}_{\rm int}({\cal W}^{\rm l}\cup{\cal W}^{\rm r})},

(2.9)

. It is

is the image of

under the diffusive scaling map

.

We have the following alternative hopping construction of the Brownian net, whose proof is deferred to Appendix

.

Theorem 2.7 (Hopping construction of a Brownian net)

Let
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
be a pair of sticky Brownian webs with parameter
\theta
.
Then

{\cal N}_{\theta}:=\overline{{\cal H}_{\rm int}({\cal W}^{1}\cup{\cal W}^{2})}

(2.10)

is distributed as a Brownian net with branching parameter
\theta
.

If
K

(see definition above (
2.1
)), then an analogue of (

) holds, i.e.,

{\cal N}_{\theta}(K)=\overline{{\cal H}_{\rm int}({\cal W}^{1}\cup{\cal W}^{2})(K)}.

(2.11)

As mentioned before,
[
NRS10
]
gave an
alternative
marking construction
of
{\cal N}
. There are also alternative
characterizations of the Brownian net, see
[
SS08
]
and the
review article
[
SSS17
]
. For more details on the coupling between

and

through Poisson marking of

We will need later the fact that the Brownian net is a maximal collection of paths in the sense that it contains every path in its graph (or image set).
Let
\Pi_{t}:=\{\pi\in\Pi:\sigma_{\pi}=t\}
denote the space of all paths starting at time

, and for any

denote the subset of paths in

starting at time

. Then the following result says that every path

(which is identified with its graph as a subset of

must also be a path in
{\cal N}_{t}
, see

Proposition 2.8 (Image set property)

Let
{\cal N}
be the standard Brownian net. Then almost surely for all

{\cal N}_{t}=\Big\{\pi\in\Pi_{t}:\pi\subset\bigcup_{p\in{\cal N}_{t}}p\Big\}.

(2.13)

3
The lower bound

The main result of this section is Theorem
3.5
, which shows that every subsequential weak limit of the rescaled discrete net

S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}N_{\varepsilon}
contains a copy of the Brownian net
{\cal N}
constructed as in Theorem
2.7
by hopping between paths in
a pair of sticky Brownian webs
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
. Our strategy is to first show that the pair of discrete sticky webs
(W^{1}_{\varepsilon},W^{2}_{\varepsilon})
defined as in (
1.3
) converges to a pair of sticky Brownian webs
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
as characterised in Theorem
2.5
.
We then show that the hopping between paths in
{\cal W}^{1}\cup{\cal W}^{2}
can always be approximated by their discrete counterparts.

3.1
Convergence of discrete sticky webs

The main result of this subsection is the convergence of a pair of discrete sticky webs and the intersection times between paths in
the two discrete webs.

Theorem 3.1 (Convergence of discrete sticky webs)

Let
\varepsilon>0
, and let
(W^{1}_{\varepsilon},W^{2}_{\varepsilon})
be the pair of discrete sticky webs defined as in (
1.3
)
with branching probability
\varepsilon>0
and jump distribution

satisfying (

). Then,

(S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W^{1}_{\varepsilon},S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W^{2}_{\varepsilon})\underset{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}{\Longrightarrow}({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2}),

(3.1)

where
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
is a pair of sticky Brownian webs with parameter
1
as in Theorem

.

Proof
The convergence of the marginals
S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W^{1}_{\varepsilon}\Rightarrow{\cal W}^{1}
and
S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W^{2}_{\varepsilon}\Rightarrow{\cal W}^{2}
follows
from Theorem
2.3
. To prove their joint convergence, it suffices to show that every subsequential weak limit of
(S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W^{1}_{\varepsilon},S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W^{2}_{\varepsilon})
satisfies conditions (i)-(iii) in Theorem
2.5
. Condition (i) follows
from the convergence of
S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W^{1}_{\varepsilon}
and
S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W^{2}_{\varepsilon}
.

To verify condition (ii), it suffices to show that a pair of paths
(X^{1},X^{2})\in(W^{1}_{\varepsilon},W^{2}_{\varepsilon})
, whose starting points
converge under diffusive scaling, must converge to a pair of sticky Brownian motions in the sense of Proposition
2.4
. By
waiting for the earlier path to reach the starting time of the later path, and by translation invariance, it suffices to consider
paths starting at the same time
0
. A stronger form of such a convergence is established in Theorem
3.2
below.

The verification of condition (iii) follows the same argument as in the proof of Theorem 2 in
[
SS19
, Section 4]
. Recall that the filtration
({\cal F}_{u})_{u\in{\mathbb{R}}}
is generated by paths in
{\cal W}^{1}
and
{\cal W}^{2}
, where we may further restrict to
{\cal W}^{1}({\cal D})
and
{\cal W}^{2}({\cal D})
for a deterministic countable dense set
{\cal D}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. For any deterministic
z=(x,t),z^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, to prove that
(X^{i}_{z},X^{j}_{z^{\prime}})
with
X^{i}_{z}\in{\cal W}^{i}(z)
and
X^{j}_{z^{\prime}}\in{\cal W}^{j}(z^{\prime})
is a Markov process w.r.t.
({\cal F}_{u})_{u\in{\mathbb{R}}}
, it suffices to show that for any
s\geq\max\{t,t^{\prime}\}
and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\in{\cal D}
with time coordinates less than
s
, conditioned on
(X^{i}_{z}(s),X^{j}_{z^{\prime}}(s))
for some
s\geq\max\{t,t^{\prime}\}
, the law of
(X^{i}_{z}(u),X^{j}_{z^{\prime}}(u))_{u\geq s}
in the future does not depend on the past realisation
(X^{i}_{z}(u),X^{j}_{z^{\prime}}(u),(X^{1}_{z_{k}}(u),X^{2}_{z_{k}}(u))_{1\leq k\leq m})_{u<s}
.
This property follows readily from approximation by paths in the discrete sticky webs, for which an analogous property holds. This verifies condition (iii).

We now state a strong form of convergence of a pair of sticky random walks to sticky Brownian motions, which also includes the convergence
of their intersection times. For technical convenience, the random walk paths are defined on
[0,\infty)
by piecewise constant extension
to non-integer times and regarded as random variables taking values in the Skorohod space
{\cal D}([0,\infty),{\mathbb{R}})

Theorem 3.2 (Pair convergence including intersection times)

Let
X^{1}
and
X^{2}
be the unique paths in
W^{1}_{\varepsilon}
and
W^{2}_{\varepsilon}
starting at time zero at positions
x^{1}_{\varepsilon}
and

x^{2}_{\varepsilon}
, respectively, such that
(\varepsilon x^{1}_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon x^{2}_{\varepsilon})\to(y^{1},y^{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
. Set

Y_{\varepsilon}^{i}(t):=\varepsilon X^{i}\big(\lfloor t/\sigma^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\rfloor\big)\quad(i=1,2)\quad\mbox{and}\quad Z_{\varepsilon}(t):=\sigma^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{s=0}^{\lfloor t/\sigma^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\rfloor}1_{\{Y_{\varepsilon}^{1}(s)=Y_{\varepsilon}^{2}(s)\}}.

(3.2)

Then as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, the process
(Y_{\varepsilon}^{1},Y_{\varepsilon}^{2},Z_{\varepsilon})
converges in distribution on the Skorohod space

{\cal D}([0,\infty),{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
to a limiting process
(Y^{1},Y^{2},Z)
, where
(Y^{1},Y^{2})

is a pair of sticky Brownian motions with parameter
1
and
starting point
(y^{1},y^{2})
characterised in Proposition
2.4
, and

Z(t)=\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{Y^{1}(s)=Y^{2}(s)\}}\mathrm{d}s.

(3.3)

Thanks to Theorem
3.2
, we can strengthen Theorem
3.1
to include the convergence of intersection times
between paths in
W^{1}_{\varepsilon}
and
W^{2}_{\varepsilon}
.

Corollary 3.3

Let
{\cal D}
be a countable dense subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. For each
z=(x,t)\in{\cal D}
and
i=1,2
, let
X^{i}_{\varepsilon,z}
be the unique path in

W^{i}_{\varepsilon}
starting from position
\varepsilon\lfloor x\varepsilon^{-1}\rfloor
at time
\sigma^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\lfloor t/\sigma^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\rfloor
. For
z,z^{\prime}\in{\cal D}
,
let
Z_{\varepsilon;z,z^{\prime}}
be the rescaled intersection time between
X^{1}_{\varepsilon,z}
and
X^{2}_{\varepsilon,z^{\prime}}
defined as in (
3.2
). Then
we have

(S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W^{1}_{\varepsilon},S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W^{2}_{\varepsilon},(Z_{\varepsilon;z,z^{\prime}})_{z,z^{\prime}\in{\cal D}})\underset{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}{\Longrightarrow}({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2},(Z_{z,z^{\prime}})_{z,z^{\prime}\in{\cal D}}),

(3.4)

where
Z_{z,z^{\prime}}(u)=\int_{\max\{t,t^{\prime}\}}^{u}1_{\{Y^{1}_{z}(s)=Y^{2}_{z^{\prime}}(s)\}}\mathrm{d}s
is the intersection time between the unique path
Y^{1}_{z}\in{\cal W}^{1}(z)

and
Y^{2}_{z^{\prime}}\in{\cal W}^{2}(z^{\prime})
.

This corollary follows readily from Theorem
3.1
and
3.2
and the fact that for

,

is determined by
Y^{1}_{z}\in{\cal W}^{1}(z)
and
Y^{2}_{z^{\prime}}\in{\cal W}^{2}(z^{\prime})
.

To prove Theorem
3.2
, we need a preliminary result for
S(t):=X^{1}(t)-X^{2}(t)
, which is a random walk
on
{\mathbb{Z}}
with stickiness at
0
and transition kernel

P(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)1_{\{y=0\}}+\varepsilon\bar{P}(x,y)&\mbox{if }x=0,\\[5.0pt]
\bar{P}(x,y)&\mbox{if }x\neq 0,\end{array}\right.

(3.5)

where

\bar{P}(x,x+y):=\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a(z+y)a(z)\qquad(x,y\in{\mathbb{Z}}).

(3.6)

For
t\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}:={\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
, let
\bar{P}^{t}
denote the
t
-th power of the transition
matrix
\bar{P}
, with
\bar{P}^{0}(x,y)=1_{\{x=y\}}
, and set
\overline{G}_{t}(x,y):=\sum_{s=0}^{t}\bar{P}^{s}(x,y)
. By

[
S01
, Prop. 28.8]
, the limit

\bar{A}(x):=\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\big[\bar{G}_{t}(0,0)-\bar{G}_{t}(x,0)\big]<\infty\quad(x\in{\mathbb{Z}})

(3.7)

exists, and
\bar{A}
is called the
potential kernel
of the random walk with
transition kernel
\bar{P}
.

Proposition 3.4 (Compensator of potential kernel)

Let
S
denote the difference random walk with transition kernel
P
as in
(
3.5
). Then

\bar{A}(S(t))-\varepsilon\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}1_{\{S(s)=0\}}

(3.8)

is a martingale. Moreover

\lim\limits_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\bar{A}(x)}{|x|}=\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}.

(3.9)

Proof
Formula (
3.9
) follows from
[
S01
, Prop. 29.2]
. To prove (
3.8
), first denote
\bar{A}_{t}(x):=\bar{G}_{t}(0,0)-\bar{G}_{t}(x,0)
.
Then we have

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}\displaystyle\bar{P}\bar{A}_{t}(x)&=&\displaystyle\sum_{\scriptstyle{y\in{\mathbb{Z}}}}\bar{P}(x,y)\bar{A}_{t}(y)=\displaystyle\sum_{\scriptstyle{y\in{\mathbb{Z}}}}\bar{P}(x,y)\sum_{s=0}^{t}\big(\bar{P}^{s}(0,0)-\bar{P}^{s}(y,0)\big)\\[5.0pt]
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{t}\big(\bar{P}^{s}(0,0)-\bar{P}^{s+1}(x,0)\big)=\displaystyle\bar{A}_{t}(x)+\bar{P}^{0}(x,0)-\bar{P}^{t+1}(x,0).\end{array}

(3.10)

Here
\lim_{t\to\infty}\bar{P}^{t+1}(x,0)=0
by the null-recurrence of the
random walk with kernel
\bar{P}
, while

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\bar{P}\bar{A}_{t}(x)=\bar{P}\bar{A}(x)
by

[
S01
, Prop. 13.3]
. Therefore letting
t\to\infty
in (
3.10
) gives

\bar{P}\bar{A}(x)-\bar{A}(x)=1_{\{x=0\}}\qquad(x\in{\mathbb{Z}}).

(3.11)

We claim that for the transition kernel
P
of the difference process
S
,

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}P\bar{A}(x)-\bar{A}(x)=\varepsilon 1_{\{x=0\}}.\end{array}

(3.12)

Indeed, when
x\neq 0
, by the definition (
3.5
), we have
P(x,y)=\bar{P}(x,y)

for all
y\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Hence (
3.11
) is the same as (
3.12
). When
x=0
,
recalling (
3.5
) and using
\bar{A}(0)=0
, we have

P\bar{A}(0)-\bar{A}(0)=(1-\varepsilon)\sum_{y\in{\mathbb{Z}}}1_{\{y=0\}}\bar{A}(y)+\varepsilon\bar{P}\bar{A}(0)=\varepsilon\bar{P}\bar{A}(0)=\varepsilon 1_{\{x=0\}},

(3.13)

where in the last equality we used (
3.11
).

In general, if
S(t)
is a Markov chain with countable state space and
transition kernel
P
, then for all real functions
f
,

M(t):=f(S(t))-\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\Big(Pf(S(s))-f(S(s))\Big)\qquad(t\geq 0)

(3.14)

is a martingale as long as each term in this expression is in
L^{1}
.
Applying this to the Markov chain
S
and the function
\overline{A}
, and using
(
3.12
), we see that the process in (
3.8
)
is a martingale. Here, to check integrability, we have used that

{\mathbb{E}}[|S(t)|]<\infty
and hence by (
3.9
), we also have

{\mathbb{E}}[\bar{A}(S(t))]<\infty
.

To prove Theorem
3.2
, we also need the following facts. Recall that for continuous local martingales
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, there exists a unique process
\langle M_{1},M_{2}\rangle
of bounded variation such that

M_{1}(t)M_{2}(t)-\langle M_{1},M_{2}\rangle(t)

(3.15)

is a local martingale (see
[
KS91
, Def. 1.5.18]
). Moreover, by
[
KS91
, Thm. 3.7.1]
one can define a local time
L(t,x)
for any real square integrable continuous semimartingale
M
such that
L(t,x)
is a measurable function, continuous and nondecreasing in
t
, and for any measurable function
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,

\int_{0}^{t}f(M(s))\mathrm{d}\langle M,M\rangle(s)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f(x)L(t,x)\mathrm{d}x.

(3.16)

Proof of Theorem
3.2

Since
X^{1}
and
X^{2}
are both random walks with the same jump distribution
a(\cdot)
, by Donsker’s invariance principle,
Y_{\varepsilon}^{1}
and
Y_{\varepsilon}^{2}
converge to standard Brownian motions starting from
y^{1}
and
y^{2}
, respectively. This also implies the tightness of
(Y^{1}_{\varepsilon},Y^{2}_{\varepsilon})
. Consequently, to show that
(Y^{1}_{\varepsilon},Y^{2}_{\varepsilon})
converges to a pair of sticky Brownian motions, it suffices to show that for any subsequential weak limit
(Y^{1},Y^{2})
,
the processes in (i) and (ii) of (
2.7
) are martingales. Along the way, we will see that
Z_{\varepsilon}
, as a function of
(Y^{1}_{\varepsilon},Y^{2}_{\varepsilon})
, also converges weakly to the process
Z
defined from
(Y^{1},Y^{2})
as in (
3.3
).

Note that the transition kernel of
\big(X^{1}(t),X^{2}(t)\big)_{t\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is given by

P^{(2)}(x_{1},x_{2};x_{1}+z_{1},x_{2}+z_{2})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(1-\varepsilon)1_{\{z_{1}=z_{2}\}}a(z_{1})+\varepsilon a(z_{1})a(z_{2})&\quad\mbox{if }x_{1}=x_{2},\\[5.0pt]
a(z_{1})a(z_{2})&\quad\mbox{if }x_{1}\neq x_{2}.\end{array}\right.

(3.17)

Therefore by standard theory (see (
3.14
)), the discrete time process

X^{1}(t)X^{2}(t)-\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\Gamma\big(X^{1}(k),X^{2}(k)\big)\qquad(t\geq 0)

(3.18)

is a martingale, where

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}\Gamma(x_{1},x_{2})&=&\displaystyle\sum_{z_{1},z_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}P^{(2)}(x_{1},x_{2};x_{1}+z_{1},x_{2}+z_{2})(x_{1}+z_{1})(x_{2}+z_{2})-x_{1}x_{2}\\[5.0pt]
&=&(1-\varepsilon)\sigma^{2}1_{\{x_{1}=x_{2}\}}.\end{array}

(3.19)

In terms of
Y^{1}_{\varepsilon}
and
Y^{2}_{\varepsilon}
, whose trajectories are piecewise constant, this implies that

Y^{1}_{\varepsilon}(t)Y^{2}_{\varepsilon}(t)-(1-\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{\lfloor t\rfloor_{\varepsilon}}1_{\{Y^{1}_{\varepsilon}(s)=Y^{2}_{\varepsilon}(s)\}}\mathrm{d}s\qquad(t\geq 0)

(3.20)

is a martingale, where
\lfloor t\rfloor_{\varepsilon}:=\sigma^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\lfloor t/\sigma^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\rfloor
.

On the other hand, since the transition kernel of the difference process
X^{1}(t)-X^{2}(t)
is given by (
3.5
), by Proposition
3.4
,

\varepsilon\bar{A}\big(\varepsilon^{-1}[Y^{1}_{\varepsilon}(t)-Y^{2}_{\varepsilon}(t)]\big)-\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\int_{0}^{\lfloor t\rfloor_{\varepsilon}}1_{\{Y^{1}_{\varepsilon}(s)=Y^{2}_{\varepsilon}(s)\}}\mathrm{d}s

(3.21)

is also a martingale. It will be more convenient to work with

\widetilde{Z}_{\varepsilon}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{Y^{1}_{\varepsilon}(s)=Y^{2}_{\varepsilon}(s)\}}\mathrm{d}s,

(3.22)

for which we note that
Z_{\varepsilon}(t)=\widetilde{Z}_{\varepsilon}(\lfloor t\rfloor_{\varepsilon})
for all
t\geq 0
.

To prove the convergence of the discrete martingale (
3.20
) to a continuous one, we adopt an argument in
[
SSS14
, Prop. A.6]
(which is
adapted from
[
HW09a
]
). First of all, recall that the laws of the processes
(Y^{1}_{\varepsilon},Y^{2}_{\varepsilon})
on
{\cal D}([0,\infty),{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
are tight. Secondly, since the slope of
\widetilde{Z}_{\varepsilon}
is between zero and one, by the Arzelà-Ascoli Theorem, tightness holds also for
(\widetilde{Z}_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
. As a result, by going to a subsequence if necessary, we may assume that the joint processes

(Y^{1}_{\varepsilon},Y^{2}_{\varepsilon},\widetilde{Z}_{\varepsilon})
converge weakly in law as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
,
and by Skorohod’s representation Theorem (see e.g.
[
B99
, Thm. 6.7]
), we can couple
(Y^{1}_{\varepsilon},Y^{2}_{\varepsilon},\widetilde{Z}_{\varepsilon})
along any sequence
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
such that the convergence is almost sure. Moreover, since for all
t\geq 0
,
(Y^{1}_{\varepsilon}(t),Y^{2}_{\varepsilon}(t),\widetilde{Z}_{\varepsilon}(t))_{\varepsilon>0}
are uniformly integrable, their convergence is also in
L^{1}
. Let
(Y^{1},Y^{2},Z_{\ast})
denote the limiting process. Then, taking the limit
in (
3.20
) and using the Markov property of
(Y^{1}_{\varepsilon},Y^{2}_{\varepsilon})
, we see that

Y^{1}(t)Y^{2}(t)-Z^{\ast}(t)

(3.23)

is a martingale, and hence

\langle Y^{1},Y^{2}\rangle(t)=Z^{\ast}(t)=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{Y^{1}_{\varepsilon}(s)=Y^{2}_{\varepsilon}(s)\}}\mathrm{d}s\qquad\forall t\geq 0\quad{\rm a.s.}

(3.24)

On one hand, since for a given
t>0
, the function
(w_{1},w_{2})\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{w_{1}(s)=w_{2}(s)\}}\mathrm{d}s
is upper semicontinuous with respect to the topology on
{\cal D}([0,\infty),{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
,
formula (
3.24

\langle Y^{1},Y^{2}\rangle(t)\leq\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{Y^{1}(s)=Y^{2}(s)\}}\mathrm{d}s\qquad(t\geq 0).

(3.25)

On the other hand, since
\langle Y^{1},Y^{2}\rangle(t)
is nondecreasing by (
3.24
), almost surely
\mathrm{d}\langle Y^{1},Y^{2}\rangle(t)
defines a nonnegative measure on
{[0,\infty)}
, which implies that

\langle Y^{1},Y^{2}\rangle(t)\geq\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{Y^{1}(s)=Y^{2}(s)\}}\mathrm{d}\langle Y^{1},Y^{2}\rangle(s)\qquad(t\geq 0).

(3.26)

To prove the opposite inequality to the one in (
3.25
), let
L(t,x)
be the local time of the semimartingale
Y^{1}-Y^{2}
.
Applying (
3.16
) to the function
f=1_{\{0\}}
, we find that

\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{Y^{1}(s)=Y^{2}(s)\}}\mathrm{d}\langle Y^{1}-Y^{2},Y^{1}-Y^{2}\rangle(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}1_{\{0\}}(x)L(t,x)\mathrm{d}x=0.

(3.27)

Since
Y^{1},Y^{2}
are standard Brownian motions, we have

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}\displaystyle\langle Y^{1}-Y^{2},Y^{1}-Y^{2}\rangle(t)&=&\displaystyle\langle Y^{1},Y^{1}\rangle(t)+\langle Y^{2},Y^{2}\rangle(t)-2\langle Y^{1},Y^{2}\rangle(t)\\[5.0pt]
&=&\displaystyle 2t-2\langle Y^{1},Y^{2}\rangle(t).\end{array}

(3.28)

Inserting this into (
3.27
) yields

\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{Y^{1}(s)=Y^{2}(s)\}}\mathrm{d}\langle Y^{1},Y^{2}\rangle(s)=\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{Y^{1}(s)=Y^{2}(s)\}}\mathrm{d}s.

(3.29)

Combining the above equality with (
3.26
) and (

) shows that

Z^{\ast}(t)=\langle Y^{1},Y^{2}\rangle(t)=\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{Y^{1}(s)=Y^{2}(s)\}}\mathrm{d}s=Z(t)\qquad\forall t\geq 0\quad{\rm a.s.}

(3.30)

Therefore,
Y^{1}(t)Y^{2}(t)-\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{Y^{1}(s)=Y^{2}(s)\}}\mathrm{d}s
is a martingale. Moreover, it is easy to see that the almost sure convergence of
\widetilde{Z}_{\varepsilon}
to
Z
implies the almost sure convergence of
Z_{\varepsilon}
to
Z
.

To check that the process in (ii) of (
2.7
) is a martingale, we take the limit
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
in (

).
Since
\bar{P}
defined in (

) has second moment

, by Proposition

, for any
\delta>0
, there exists

such that for all integers
|x|>N
,

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}\Delta(x):=\big|2\sigma^{2}\bar{A}(x)-|x|\big|<\delta|x|.\end{array}

(3.31)

Recall that
S=X^{1}-X^{2}
and denote
C_{N}:=\sup_{|x|\leq N}\big|2\sigma^{2}\bar{A}(x)-|x|\big|
. We see that

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}\varepsilon\Delta\big(S(t\sigma^{-2}\varepsilon^{-2})\big)&=&\varepsilon\Delta\big(S(t\sigma^{-2}\varepsilon^{-2})\big)1_{\{S(t\sigma^{-2}\varepsilon^{-2})>N\}}+\varepsilon\Delta(S(t\sigma^{-2}\varepsilon^{-2}))1_{\{S(t\sigma^{-2}\varepsilon^{-2})\leq N\}}\\[5.0pt]
&\leq&\varepsilon\delta|S(t\sigma^{-2}\varepsilon^{-2})|+\varepsilon C_{N}=\delta|Y^{1}_{\varepsilon}(t)-Y^{2}_{\varepsilon}(t)|+\varepsilon C_{N}.\end{array}

(3.32)

Sending
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
first and then
\delta\downarrow 0
, and using the almost sure convergence of
Y^{1}_{\varepsilon}(t)-Y^{2}_{\varepsilon}(t)
to
Y^{1}(t)-Y^{2}(t)
, we obtain that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon\Delta\big(\varepsilon^{-1}[Y^{1}_{\varepsilon}(t)-Y^{2}_{\varepsilon}(t)]\big)=0\qquad\forall t\geq 0\quad{\rm a.s.}

(3.33)

Therefore, almost surely, for all
t\geq 0
,

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon\bar{A}\big(\varepsilon^{-1}[Y^{1}_{\varepsilon}(t)-Y^{2}_{\varepsilon}(t)]\big)=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}|Y^{1}_{\varepsilon}(t)-Y^{2}_{\varepsilon}(t)|=\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}|Y^{1}(t)-Y^{2}(t)|.

(3.34)

Recalling (
3.24
) and (
3.30
), the almost sure limit (also
L^{1}
limit) of (
3.21
) is

\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}|Y^{1}(t)-Y^{2}(t)|-\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{Y^{1}(s)=Y^{2}(s)\}}\mathrm{d}s.

(3.35)

Together with the Markov property of
(Y^{1}_{\varepsilon},Y^{2}_{\varepsilon})
, it follows that

|Y^{1}(t)-Y^{2}(t)|-2\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{Y^{1}(s)=Y^{2}(s)\}}\mathrm{d}s

(3.36)

is a martingale.

Therefore, the process
(Y_{\varepsilon}^{1},Y_{\varepsilon}^{2},Z_{\varepsilon})
converges in distribution on

{\cal D}([0,\infty),{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
to
(Y^{1},Y^{2},Z)
, where
(Y^{1},Y^{2})
is a pair of sticky Brownian motions with parameter
1
and
starting point
(y^{1},y^{2})
as characterized in Proposition
2.4
, and
Z
is defined from
(Y^{1},Y^{2})
as in (
3.3
).

3.2
Proof of the lower bound

We are now ready to complete the lower bound on any subsequential weak limit of
S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}N_{\varepsilon}
.

Theorem 3.5 (Lower bound)

For
\varepsilon>0
, let
N_{\varepsilon}
be the discrete net with branching probability
\varepsilon
and jump distribution
a(\cdot)
satisfying (
1.1
).
Then for any subsequential weak limit
{\cal N}^{\ast}
of
S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}N_{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, there exists a standard Brownian net
{\cal N}
such that almost surely,

{\cal N}\subseteq{\cal N}^{\ast}.

(3.37)

Proof
Let
(W_{\varepsilon}^{1},W_{\varepsilon}^{2})
be the pair of discrete sticky webs defined before (
1.3
), which is contained in the the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
.
Thanks to Corollary
3.3
, if
{\cal N}^{\ast}
is a subsequential limit of
S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}N_{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, then by going to a subsequence if necessary and by Skorohod’s representation theorem, we can find a coupling such that

(S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W_{\varepsilon}^{1},S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W_{\varepsilon}^{2},S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}N_{\varepsilon},(Z_{\varepsilon;z,z^{\prime}})_{z,z^{\prime}\in{\cal D}})\underset{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2},{\cal N}^{\ast},(Z_{z,z^{\prime}})_{z,z^{\prime}\in{\cal D}})\quad{\rm a.s.},

(3.38)

where
{\cal W}^{1}\cup{\cal W}^{2}\subseteq{\cal N}^{\ast}
,
{\cal D}
is a deterministic countable subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, and
Z_{z,z^{\prime}}
is the intersection time process
between
Y^{1}_{z}\in{\cal W}^{1}(z)
and
Y^{2}_{z^{\prime}}\in{\cal W}^{2}(z^{\prime})
while
Z_{\varepsilon;z,z^{\prime}}
is the discrete counterpart. By Theorem
2.7
, we can construct a standard Brownian net via hopping between paths in
{\cal W}^{1}
and
{\cal W}^{2}
, i.e.,

{\cal N}=\overline{{\cal H}_{\rm int}({\cal W}^{1}\cup{\cal W}^{2})}.

(3.39)

To show
{\cal N}\subseteq{\cal N}^{\ast}
, it suffices to show that

{\cal H}_{\rm int}({\cal W}^{1}\cup{\cal W}^{2})\subseteq{\cal N}^{\ast},

(3.40)

since
{\cal N}^{\ast}
is a compact subset of the path space
\Pi
. Let
{\cal D}
be a countable dense subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. For any path
\pi\in{\cal W}^{i}
, we can find
a sequence of
\pi_{n}\in{\cal W}^{i}({\cal D})
such that
\pi_{n}\to\pi
and the time of coalescence between
\pi_{n}
and
\pi
converges to the starting time of
\pi
, see
[
SS08
, Lemma 3.4]
. Therefore to prove (
3.40
), it suffices to to show
{\cal H}_{\rm int}({\cal W}^{1}({\cal D})\cup{\cal W}^{2}({\cal D}))\subseteq{\cal N}^{\ast}
. In particular, by induction, it suffices
to show that for any
Y^{1}_{z}\in{\cal W}^{1}(z)
and
Y^{2}_{z^{\prime}}\in{\cal W}^{2}(z^{\prime})
with
z=(x,t),z^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in{\cal D}
, if
u>\max\{t,t^{\prime}\}
is an intersection time of
Y^{1}
and
Y^{2}
, then the concatenated path with graph

Y:=\{(Y^{1}_{z}(s),s):s\in[t,u]\}\cup\{(Y^{2}_{z^{\prime}}(s),s):s\in[u,\infty]\}\in{\cal N}^{\ast}.

(3.41)

By (
3.38
), we can find
Y^{1}_{\varepsilon,z}\in S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W_{\varepsilon}^{1}
and
Y^{2}_{\varepsilon,z^{\prime}}\in S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}W_{\varepsilon}^{2}
such that

(Y^{1}_{\varepsilon,z},Y^{2}_{\varepsilon,z^{\prime}},Z_{\varepsilon;z,z^{\prime}})\to(Y^{1}_{z},Y^{2}_{z^{\prime}},Z_{z,z^{\prime}}),

where
Z_{\varepsilon;z,z^{\prime}}
and
Z_{z,z^{\prime}}
are the intersection time processes for
(Y^{1}_{\varepsilon,z},Y^{2}_{\varepsilon,z^{\prime}})
and
(Y^{1}_{z},Y^{2}_{z^{\prime}})
, respectively.
Note that
Y^{1}_{z}(s)-Y^{2}_{z^{\prime}}(s)
is a sticky Brownian motion with stickiness at
0
, which can be obtained from a Brownian motion via a random time change that changes its local time at the origin into real time, see e.g.
[
SS08
, Section 2.1]
and
[
Y16
, Section 2.1]
. This ensures that any intersection time
u
of
Y^{1}_{z}
and
Y^{2}_{z^{\prime}}
lies in the support of the measure
\mathrm{d}Z_{z,z^{\prime}}(s)
. The convergence
Z_{\varepsilon;z,z^{\prime}}\to Z_{z,z^{\prime}}
then implies that we can find intersection times
u_{\varepsilon}
of
Y^{1}_{\varepsilon,z}
and
Y^{2}_{\varepsilon,z^{\prime}}
such that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
. Therefore we can concatenate
Y^{1}_{\varepsilon,z}
and
Y^{2}_{\varepsilon,z^{\prime}}
at time
u_{\varepsilon}
, which gives a path in
S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}N_{\varepsilon}
that clearly converges to
Y
.
This shows that
Y
is an element of
{\cal N}^{\ast}
and completes the proof.

4
Auxiliary Bernoulli net

In this section, we introduce an auxiliary system of branching-coalescing random walks which we call the
Bernoulli net

\widetilde{N}
. In Subsection
4.1
, we show that
\widetilde{N}
can be coupled to the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
such that
N_{\varepsilon}\subset\widetilde{N}
almost surely. In Subsection
4.2
, we introduce the branching-coalescing point set
\tilde{\xi}
generated by
\widetilde{N}
, and its dual process
\tilde{\phi}
which is a discrete time biased voter model. In Subsection
4.3
, we show that
\tilde{\xi}
admits an invariant Bernoulli product law, which
converges to the invariant law of the branching-coalescing point set
\xi
generated by the Brownian net
{\cal N}
. This fact is the key to the upper bound proof in Section
5
. Lastly in Subsection
4.4
, we give an upper bound on the density of
\tilde{\xi}
with maximal initial configuration, which is comparable to the density of coalescing random walks up to the diffusive time scale. The results of this section will be crucial in the upper bound proof in Section
5
and in the tightness proof in Section
6
.

4.1
Bernoulli net and coupling with discrete net

Recall that the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
was defined before (
1.3
) by specifying the set of outgoing arrows from each
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
. To define a
more general branching-coalescing system, we can specify a probability law
\mu
on
{\cal P}_{\rm fin,+}({\mathbb{Z}})
, the space of non-empty finite subsets of
{\mathbb{Z}}
.
We then assign i.i.d.
{\cal P}_{\rm fin,+}({\mathbb{Z}})
-valued random variables
{\cal A}(x,t)
with common law
\mu
to each
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
. For every
y\in{\cal A}(x,t)
,
we then draw an arrow from
(x,t)
to
(x+y,t+1)
. The set of arrows then defines a branching-coalescing system similar to the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
,
with
\mu
specifying the offspring distribution.

We now introduce the Bernoulli net
\widetilde{N}
, which corresponds to a specific class of
\mu
. Given
\psi:{\mathbb{Z}}\to[0,1)
with
\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\psi(x)<\infty
, let
(V_{x})_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
be independent Bernoulli random variables with parameters
(\psi(x))_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
respectively, and let
{\rm Supp}(V):=\{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}:V(x)=1\}
. We then choose the offspring distribution
\mu
such that

1-\mu(A)={\mathbb{P}}\big({\rm Supp(V)}=A\,\big|{\rm Supp}(V)\neq\emptyset\big)\qquad\big(A\in{\cal P}_{\rm fin,+}({\mathbb{Z}})\big).

(4.1)

We call the associated branching-coalescing system
\widetilde{N}_{\psi}
the
Bernoulli net
with
kernel

\psi
. We say that
\psi
is

irreducible
if paths in
\widetilde{N}_{\psi}(0,0)
(the set of paths in
\widetilde{N}_{\psi}
starting at
(0,0)
) can reach any point in
{\mathbb{Z}}
with positive probability;
and we say
\psi
is
aperiodic
if the set of times at which
0
can be reached by paths in
\widetilde{N}_{\psi}(0,0)
with positive probability has greatest
common divisor
1
.

We now show how the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
with jump distribution
a(\cdot)
and branching probability
\varepsilon
, defined as in (
1.3
), can be coupled to
a Bernoulli net
\widetilde{N}_{\psi}
with kernel
\psi
, such that
N_{\varepsilon}\subset\widetilde{N}_{\psi}
almost surely and the difference between them vanishes as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
.

Lemma 4.1 (Coupling of Bernoulli and discrete nets)

Let
N_{\varepsilon}
be the discrete net with jump distribution
a(\cdot)
and branching probability
\varepsilon>0
. Let
r>0
be a solution of the equation

\varepsilon=1-\frac{r}{e^{r}-1}.

(4.2)

Define

\psi_{\varepsilon}(x):=1-e^{-ra(x)}\quad(x\in{\mathbb{Z}}).

(4.3)

Then the Bernoulli net
\widetilde{N}_{\psi_{\varepsilon}}
with kernel
\psi_{\varepsilon}
can be coupled to
N_{\varepsilon}
such that
N_{\varepsilon}\subset\widetilde{N}_{\psi_{\varepsilon}}
almost surely.

Proof
It suffices to couple the configuration of arrows originating from
(0,0)
in
N_{\varepsilon}
and
\widetilde{N}_{\psi_{\varepsilon}}
. The basic idea is to construct
the arrows in
\widetilde{N}_{\psi_{\varepsilon}}
through a Poisson point process. Let
(M_{x})_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
be independent Poisson random variables with mean

ra(x)
. We then draw
M_{x}
arrows from
(0,0)
to
(1,x)
, where multiple arrows coalesce with each other at
(1,x)
. Note that this is
equivalent to assigning a single arrow from
(0,0)
to
(1,x)
with probability
\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)=1-e^{-ra(x)}
. To construct the configuration
of arrows in
\widetilde{N}_{\psi_{\varepsilon}}
, we are simply conditioning on the event
M:=\sum_{x}M_{x}\geq 1
. With our choice of
r
, we have

{\mathbb{P}}(M=1|M\geq 1)=\frac{re^{-r}}{1-e^{-r}}=\frac{r}{e^{r}-1}=1-\varepsilon.

Restricted to the event
\{M=1\}
, it is clear that the single arrow from
(0,0)
is drawn according to distribution
a(\cdot)
, which
coincides with the definition of
a^{(2)}
in (
1.3
). On the event
\{M\geq 2\}
, we draw
M
arrows independently, each following
the distribution
a(\cdot)
. Retaining only the first 2 arrows already reproduces the arrow configuration in the definition of
a^{(2)}

in (
1.3
). This establishes the desired coupling between
N_{\varepsilon}
and
\widetilde{N}_{\psi_{\varepsilon}}
.

Remark 4.2

We are interested in applying Lemma
4.1
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
. By Taylor expansion around
r=0
in the r.h.s. of (
4.2
),
it is easy to see that for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, there is a unique solution
r(\varepsilon)
to (
4.2
), and
r(\varepsilon)=(2+o(1))\varepsilon

as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
.

4.2
Branching-coalescing point sets and their duals

Let
\widetilde{N}:=\widetilde{N}_{\psi}
be a Bernoulli net with kernel
\psi
. We now introduce two associated Markov chains. One is the so-called
branching-coalescing point set
\tilde{\xi}_{t}
, the other is its dual
\tilde{\phi}_{t}
which can be interpreted as a biased voter model (see
e.g.
[
SSY19
,
SSY21
]
).

Let
{\cal P}({\mathbb{Z}})
denote the collection of subsets of
{\mathbb{Z}}
. Given a Bernoulli net
\widetilde{N}
, for each
s,t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
s\leq t
, we define two random maps
\Xi_{s,t}:{\cal P}({\mathbb{Z}})\to{\cal P}({\mathbb{Z}})
and
\Phi_{t,s}:{\cal P}({\mathbb{Z}})\to{\cal P}({\mathbb{Z}})
by

\left.\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}\displaystyle\Xi_{s,t}(A)&:=&\displaystyle\big\{y\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\exists x\in A,\ \pi\in\widetilde{N}(x,s)\mbox{ s.t.\ }\pi(t)=y\big\},\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\Phi_{t,s}(B)&:=&\displaystyle\big\{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\exists y\in B,\ \pi\in\widetilde{N}(x,s)\mbox{ s.t.\ }\pi(t)=y\big\},\end{array}\right.\quad\big(A,B\in{\cal P}({\mathbb{Z}})\big),

(4.4)

where
\widetilde{N}(x,s)
is the set of paths in
\widetilde{N}
starting from
(x,s)
. In words,
\Xi_{s,t}(A)\subset{\mathbb{Z}}
is the set of positions generated at time
t
by paths in
\widetilde{N}
that start from
A
at time
s
, while

\Phi_{t,s}(B)
is the set of positions at time
s
that can lead to some point in
B
by following paths in
\widetilde{N}
.

Fix two times
s,u\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. We can then define two
{\cal P}({\mathbb{Z}})
-valued Markov chains

\tilde{\xi}_{t}:=\Xi_{s,s+t}(A)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\tilde{\phi}_{t}:=\Phi_{u,u-t}(B)\qquad(t\geq 0),

(4.5)

where
\tilde{\xi}
evolves forward in time and
\tilde{\phi}
evolves backward in time. We call
\tilde{\xi}
the branching-coalescing
point set generated by
\widetilde{N}
, and
\tilde{\phi}
its dual. More precisely, they are dual in the sense that

{\mathbb{P}}\big(\tilde{\xi}_{t}\cap B\neq\emptyset\big|\tilde{\xi}_{0}=A\big)={\mathbb{P}}\big(A\cap\tilde{\phi}_{t}\neq\emptyset\big|\tilde{\phi}_{0}=B\big),\qquad\big(A,B\in{\cal P}({\mathbb{Z}}),\ t\geq 0),

(4.6)

which holds since

\{\Xi_{0,t}(A)\cap B\neq\emptyset\}=\big\{\exists x\in A,\ y\in B,\ \pi\in\widetilde{N}(x,0)\mbox{ s.t.\ }\pi(t)=y\big\}=\{A\cap\Phi_{t,0}(B)\neq\emptyset\}.

It will be convenient to define the Markov chain
\tilde{\xi}
also at half-integer
times in order to keep track of the arrows that are used in each time step.
To this aim, for each
s,t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
s\leq t
, we define

\Xi_{s,t+1/2}:{\cal P}({\mathbb{Z}})\to{\cal P}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2})
by

\Xi_{s,t+1/2}(A):=\big\{\big(\pi(t),\pi(t+1)\big)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}:\pi\in\widetilde{N}(s,x)\mbox{ for some }x\in A\big\},

(4.7)

and we define
\xi_{t+1/2}
as in (
4.5
). Then

(\xi_{0},\xi_{1/2},\xi_{1},\ldots)
is a Markov chain taking values alternatively
in
{\cal P}({\mathbb{Z}})
and
{\cal P}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2})
, where in fact
\xi_{t+1}
is a deterministic
function of
\xi_{t+1/2}
.

In the continuum setting, if
{\cal N}
is the standard Brownian net, then for each real
s\leq t
, we
can similarly define a random map
\Xi^{\cal N}_{s,t}
acting on closed subsets of the real line by

\Xi_{s,t}(A):=\big\{\pi(t):\pi\in{\cal N}(x,t)\mbox{ for some }x\in A\big\}.

(4.8)

Setting
\xi^{\cal N}_{t}:=\Xi^{\cal N}_{s,s+t}(A)

(t\geq 0)
then defines a Markov process
taking values in the space of closed subsets of the real line. This process is known as
the
branching-coalescing point set
of the Brownian net
{\cal N}

[
SS08
, Thm 1.11]
.

4.3
Product invariant law and the backbone

For the standard Brownian net
{\cal N}
, the branching-coalescing point set
\xi^{\cal N}_{t}
is known to have a unique
invariant law given by the law of the Poisson point process on
{\mathbb{R}}
with intensity
2
, see
[
SS08
, Prop. 1.15]
.
Furthermore, the stationary process can be constructed by considering
{\cal N}(*,-\infty)
, the set of paths
in
{\cal N}
starting at
t=-\infty
, which is called the
backbone
of
{\cal N}
. Similar results hold for the
discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
with jump distribution
a(\cdot)
supported on
\{\pm 1\}
, see
[
SS08
, Prop. 1.14]
.
In this subsection, we show that analogous results also hold for the Bernoulli nets
\widetilde{N}
, and we identify explicitly
the product invariant law of the branching-coalescing point set
\tilde{\xi}
generated by
\widetilde{N}
.

In what follows, a
Bernoulli subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}
with intensity
\alpha(\cdot)
refers to a random subset of
\Lambda\subset{\mathbb{Z}}
,
where independently each
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}
belongs to
\Lambda
with probability
\alpha(x)
. A Bernoulli subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}

with intensity
\alpha(\cdot)
is defined similarly. We will write
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}/2:=\{x/2:x\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\}
where
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}:=\{0\}\cup{\mathbb{N}}
.

Lemma 4.3 (Product invariant law)

Let
\widetilde{N}
be a Bernoulli net with kernel
\psi:{\mathbb{Z}}\to[0,1)
, which is irreducible and aperiodic. Let
\Lambda
be a Bernoulli subset
of
{\mathbb{Z}}
with constant intensity

\rho:=1-\prod_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(1-\psi(x)).

(4.9)

Let
(\tilde{\xi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}/2}
be the branching-coalescing point set defined as in (
4.5
) and (
4.7
) from
\widetilde{N}
with initial condition
\tilde{\xi}_{0}=\Lambda
. Then for each
t\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(i)

\tilde{\xi}_{t}
is a Bernoulli subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}
with intensity
\rho
;

(ii)

\tilde{\xi}_{t+1/2}
is a Bernoulli subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
with intensity
\alpha(x,y)=\psi(y-x)
.

Proof
Let
\xi_{1/2}
be a Bernoulli subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
with intensity
\alpha(x,y)=\psi(y-x)
.
Define random sets
\xi_{0},\xi_{1}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}
by

\begin{array}[]{r@{\,}c@{\,}l}\displaystyle\xi_{0}&:=&\displaystyle\{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}:(x,y)\in\xi_{1/2}\mbox{ for some }y\in{\mathbb{Z}}\},\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\xi_{1}&:=&\displaystyle\{y\in{\mathbb{Z}}:(x,y)\in\xi_{1/2}\mbox{ for some }x\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}.\end{array}

(4.10)

Then it is easy to see that both
\xi_{0}
and
\xi_{1}
are Bernoulli subset of

{\mathbb{Z}}
with constant intensity
\rho
. Moreover, for
x\in\xi_{0}
, if we define

{\cal A}(x,0):=\{y\in{\mathbb{Z}}:(x,x+y)\in\xi_{1/2}\},

(4.11)

then we note that conditioned on
\xi_{0}
, the random variables
({\cal A}(x,0))_{x\in\xi_{0}}
are
i.i.d. with common law
\mu
defined from
\psi
as in (
4.1
). In view of this, the
conditional law of
\xi_{1/2}
given
\xi_{0}
is the same as that for the branching-coalescing
point set
\tilde{\xi}
defined from the Bernoulli net
\widetilde{N}
with kernel
\psi
, and also

\xi_{1}
is a function of
\xi_{1/2}
in exactly the same way as
\tilde{\xi}
. The statements
(i) and (ii) then follow immediately.

Next we show that the backbone of the Bernoulli net
\widetilde{N}
with kernel
\psi
, defined by

\tilde{\xi}^{*}_{t}:=\{\pi(t):\pi\in\widetilde{N}(*,-\infty)\},

(4.12)

is a stationary process with the law of
\tilde{\xi}^{*}_{t}
for each
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
given by the product invariant law identified in
Lemma
4.3
. In
[
SS08
]
, such a result has been proved for the Brownian net
{\cal N}
as
well as for the nearest neighbour discrete net. The proof for the latter, see
[
SS08
, Prop. 1.14 (i)]
,
makes essential use of the nearest neighbour property. Here we argue differently by identifying a suitable martingale
for the process
\tilde{\phi}
dual to the branching-coalescing point set
\tilde{\xi}
. We start with a preliminary lemma
for
\tilde{\phi}
.

Lemma 4.4 (Positive fluctuations)

Let
\widetilde{N}
be a Bernoulli net with kernel
\psi:{\mathbb{Z}}\to[0,1)
, which is irreducible and aperiodic. Let
\tilde{\phi}=(\tilde{\phi}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the dual Markov chain defined in (
4.5
) with initial state
\tilde{\phi}_{0}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then for all initial states
\tilde{\phi}_{0}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}
with
0<|\tilde{\phi}_{0}|<\infty
, we have

{\mathbb{P}}\big(|\tilde{\phi}_{1}|\neq|\tilde{\phi}_{0}|\big)\geq\delta(\psi):=\frac{1}{\rho}\min\Big\{\psi(y_{1}),\psi(y_{2})\prod_{w\leq y_{1}}(1-\psi(w))\Big\}>0,

(4.13)

where
\rho
is defined as in (
4.9
), and
y_{1}<y_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
are any two points with
\psi(y_{1}),\psi(y_{2})\in(0,1)
.

In particular, if
\widetilde{N}_{\psi_{\varepsilon}}
is the Bernoulli net coupled to the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
with jump distribution
a(\cdot)
and branching probability
\varepsilon
as in Lemma
4.1
, then

4.4

For the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
with jump distribution

To simplify notation, from now on, we will omit

Theorem 4.6 (Density decay)

Let
\tilde{\xi}
be the branching-coalescing point set defined from the Bernoulli net
\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}
with
\tilde{\xi}_{0}={\mathbb{Z}}
, and let
\tilde{\phi}
be the dual of
\tilde{\xi}

\tilde{\phi}_{0}=\{0\}

such that for all
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small and all

, we have

{\mathbb{P}}\big(0\in\tilde{\xi}_{T}\big)={\mathbb{P}}\big(|\tilde{\phi}_{T}|\neq 0\big)\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{T}}.

(4.22)

In particular, the same bound holds for the branching-coalescing point set
\xi
defined from the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
with
\xi_{0}={\mathbb{Z}}
.

We now sketch the key ideas behind the proof of Theorem
4.6
. The first observation is that we can relate the process
|\tilde{\phi}_{t}|
to a martingale. Instead of working with the martingale
M_{t}
defined in (
4.16
), it is more convenient to work with

\widetilde{M}_{t}:=\frac{1-M_{t}}{\rho}=\frac{1-(1-\rho)^{|\tilde{\phi}_{t}|}}{\rho}.

(4.23)

Note that
|\tilde{\phi}_{t}|=0
if and only if
\widetilde{M}_{t}=0
, and the time
\tilde{\phi}
hits the empty set is exactly the time
\widetilde{M}
hits
0
. For
\varepsilon
small, by (
4.15
), we have
\rho=1-e^{-r}=(2+o(1))\varepsilon
. From (
4.23
), we see that
\widetilde{M}_{t}
is close to
|\tilde{\phi}_{t}|
as long as
|\tilde{\phi}_{t}|\ll 1/\varepsilon
, and the two remain comparable as long as
|\tilde{\phi}_{t}|\leq c/\varepsilon
for some fixed constant

. We will show that for
T=O(\varepsilon^{-2})
, the probability of the event
\{\max_{1\leq t\leq T}|\tilde{\phi}_{t}|\geq c/\varepsilon\}
is of order
O(1/\sqrt{T})
and hence constitutes
part of the bound in (
4.22
). On the complementary event,
\widetilde{M}_{t}
and
|\tilde{\phi}_{t}|
are comparable up to time

. Lemma
4.4
gives a lower bound on the fluctuations of

\widetilde{M}_{t}
. This in turn implies
that the probability that
\widetilde{M}
does not hit

before time

is comparable to the probability of the same event for a Brownian motion, which is also of order

.
To make this last comparison rigorous, we need to Skorohod embed

in a Brownian motion (see e.g.
[

Theorem 4.7 (Skorohod embedding)

(\widetilde{M}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}

.

\displaystyle\tau_{t}=\inf\big\{s\geq\tau_{t-1}:B_{s}-B_{\tau_{t-1}}\in\{\alpha_{t},\beta_{t}\}\big\},

Proof of Theorem
4.6
.

The equality in (

The idea behind the construction of the stopping times

5
The upper bound

Let
N_{\varepsilon}
be the discrete net as in Theorem
1.1
, let
\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}
be the Bernoulli net coupled to
N_{\varepsilon}
as in Lemma
4.1
,
and let
{\cal N}
be the standard Brownian net. In Section
3
, it was shown that every subsequential weak limit of the rescaled discrete net

S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}N_{\varepsilon}
contains a copy of the Brownian net
{\cal N}
. In this section, we show the matching upper bound that every subsequential weak limit of

S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}N_{\varepsilon}
contains no additional paths besides
{\cal N}
. We will start with a sufficient criterion for this last statement in Section
5.1
, which is based on a family of counting random variables. In Section
5.2
, we will show that the subset of
N_{\varepsilon}
started at time
0
from a random subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}
, distributed according to the product invariant law of the branching-coalescing point set generated by the Bernoulli net
\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}
(see Lemma
4.3
), converges under the scaling map
S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}
to the subset of
{\cal N}
started at time
0
according to the stationary law of the branching-coalescing point set
generated by
{\cal N}
. This is then used in Section
5.3
to deduce the convergence of paths in
S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}N_{\varepsilon}
starting from finitely many space-time points. Finally, the desired upper bound on subsequential weak limits of

S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}N_{\varepsilon}
is established in Section
5.4
by verifying the criterion in Section
5.1
.

5.1
An upper bound criterion

Recall from Section
2.1
the space of paths
\Pi
, and the space
{\cal H}
of compact subsets of
\Pi
. Let
\Pi_{t}\subset\Pi
denote the set of
paths starting at time
t
, and let
{\cal N}_{t}:={\cal N}\cap\Pi_{t}
. For a path
\pi\in\Pi
and any
t\geq\sigma_{\pi}
, let
\pi^{t}:=\{(\pi(s),s):s\geq t\}
denote the truncation of
\pi
at time
t
so that
\sigma_{\pi^{t}}=t
.
Recall that the Brownian net
{\cal N}
satisfies the image set property stated in Proposition
2.8
, which implies that for any
{\cal H}
-valued random variable
{\cal X}
, if

{\cal X}^{t}:=\{\pi^{t}:\pi\in{\cal X},\sigma_{\pi}\leq t\}

denotes the set of paths in
{\cal X}
starting before or at time
t
and truncated at time
t
, and almost surely
\cup_{p\in{\cal X}^{t}}p\subset\cup_{p\in{\cal N}_{t}}p
for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, then
{\cal X}\setminus{\cal N}=\emptyset
.
Motivated by this observation, we will formulate an upper bound criterion for the Brownian net using a family of
counting random variables

\eta
to be defined below. Analogous counting random variables were used in the upper bound criteria for the Brownian web
{\cal W}
in
[
NRS05
]
, which were in turn adapted from
[
FINR04
]
.

Similar to the definition of the branching-coalescing point set from
{\cal N}
, for
{\cal X}\in{\cal H}
, we define

\xi_{\cal X}(t,h;a,b):=\{\pi(t+h)\cap(a,b):\pi\in{\cal X},\sigma_{\pi}\leq t\},\quad(t\in{\mathbb{R}},h>0,a<b),

(5.1)

which is the point set generated at time
t+h
by paths in
{\cal X}
that start before or at time
t
.
We then define the family of counting random variables

\eta_{\cal X}(t,h;a,b):=|\xi_{\cal X}(t,h;a,b)|,\quad(t\in{\mathbb{R}},h>0,a<b).

(5.2)

Here is our upper bound criterion, which is in fact also a convergence criterion since it already assumes tightness and the desired lower bound.

Theorem 5.1 (Upper bound criterion)

Let
({\cal X}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of
{\cal H}
-valued random variables whose laws are tight.
If any of its subsequential weak limits
{\cal X}
contains a copy of the Brownian net
{\cal N}
, and moreover for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}},h>0,a<b
,

{\mathbb{E}}[\eta_{\cal X}(t,h;a,b)]\leq{\mathbb{E}}[\eta_{\cal N}(t,h;a,b)],

(5.3)

then
{\cal X}_{n}
converges weakly to the Brownian net
{\cal N}
as
n\to\infty
.

Remark 5.2

Due to the assumption that any subsequential weak limit
{\cal X}
contains a copy of the Brownian net
{\cal N}
, it is easily seen that the inequality in (
5.3
) actually implies equality.

Proof
By assumption,
{\cal X}
is coupled to a Brownian net
{\cal N}
such that
{\cal N}\subset{\cal X}
a.s., which implies that

\cup_{p\in{\cal X}^{t}}p\supset\cup_{p\in{\cal N}_{t}}p
for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. It remains to show that
{\cal X}\setminus{\cal N}=\emptyset
a.s.
By the image set property Proposition
2.8
, it suffices to show that a.s.,

\bigcup_{p\in{\cal X}^{t}}p\subset\bigcup_{p\in{\cal N}_{t}}p\quad\quad\mbox{for all }t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(5.4)

Note that (
5.3
) implies that a.s., for all

t\in{\mathbb{Q}}
,
h\in{\mathbb{Q}}\cap(0,\infty)
and
a<b
, we have
\eta_{\cal X}(t,h;a,b)\leq\eta_{\cal N}(t,h;a,b)
, which can be extended to all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
and

h>0
since
{\cal X}
and
{\cal N}
are compact sets of continuous paths. This immediately implies (
5.4
) and concludes the proof.

5.2

For the standard Brownian net

, recall from

6
Tightness

In this section, we prove that the laws of the rescaled discrete nets
(S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}N_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
are tight. In Section
6.1
, we recall the standard tightness criterion for a family of
{\cal H}
-valued random variables. To address the fundamental difficulty created by branching, we introduce in Section
6.2
the so-called relevant branching points of the branching-coalescing random walks. In Sections
6.3
and
6.4
, we show that such relevant branching points are rare on small time scales, which then allows us to implement in Section
6.5

the multiscale argument used in
[
BMSV06
]
to verify the tightness criterion for the rescaled discrete webs.

6.1
Tightness criterion

We recall here the standard tightness criterion for a sequence of
{\cal H}
-valued random variables
({\cal X}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, which was first formulated in
[
FINR04
, Proposition B.1]
.

Proposition 6.1 (Tightness criterion)

A sequence of
{\cal H}
-valued random variables
({\cal X}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}

\Lambda_{L,T}:=[-L,L]\times[-T,T]
with
L,T>0

and every
M>0
,

\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{(x,t)\in\Lambda_{L,T}}{\mathbb{P}}\big({\cal X}_{n}\in A_{M,\delta}(x,t)\big)=0,

(6.1)

where
A_{M,\delta}(x,t)\subset{\cal H}
consists of compact sets of paths
K\in{\cal H}
, such that
K
contains some path which intersects the box

[x-M,x+M]\times[t,t+\delta]
, and at a later time, intersects the left or right boundary of the larger box
[x-2M,x+2M]\times[t,t+2\delta]

(
see Figure
1
)
.

Figure 1:
A path
\pi
causing the unlikely event
\{{\cal X}_{n}\in A_{M,\delta}(x,t)\}
to occur.

Remark 6.2

Proving tightness of
({\cal X}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
amounts to controlling the uniform modulus of continuity of paths in
{\cal X}_{n}
in such a way that the probability
bounds are uniform in
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Thanks to the compactification of space-time in Section
2.1
, we can restrict to a large but finite space-time window. Dividing the time window into intervals of length
\delta
and controlling the uniform modulus of continuity on each time interval then leads to the condition (

). For more details, see e.g.
[
SSS17
, Section 6.6]
.

6.2
Relevant branching points

To verify the tightness condition (
6.1
) for the rescaled discrete nets
S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}N_{\varepsilon}
, we will adapt the multiscale argument
developed in
[
BMSV06
]
to prove tightness for the rescaled discrete webs. A key ingredient is to show that the probability that some of the random walks and their descendants (due to branching) will exit the left or right boundary of the larger box
[x-2M,x+2M]\times[t,t+2\delta]
in Figure
1
is very small. Branching creates fundamental difficulties for this estimate, which motivates us to consider the so-called
relevant branching points (RBP)
of the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
. Controlling the number of RBP along paths in
N_{\varepsilon}(0,0)
then allows us to show that the probability that some path in
N_{\varepsilon}(0,0)
makes a large excursion as depicted in Figure
1
is comparable to the probability that a single random walk starting from
(0,0)
makes a large excursion. We now give the precise definition of RBP.

Let
A\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
be a set of space-time points. Given
S,U\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
S<U
, a space-time point
z=(x,t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
with
t\in(S,U)
is called a
(S,U)
-relevant branching point
(
RBP
)
of
N_{\varepsilon}(A)
if there exist two paths
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in N_{\varepsilon}
starting from
A
, such that

\pi_{1}(s)=\pi_{2}(s)~\forall s\in[S,t],\quad\pi_{1}(t)=\pi_{2}(t)=x,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\pi_{1}(s)\neq\pi_{2}(s)~\forall s\in(t,U]\cap{\mathbb{Z}}.

(6.2)

We call
t
an
(S,U)
-relevant branching time of
N_{\varepsilon}(A)
. When things are clear from the context, we will simply call
z
(resp.
t
) a relevant branching point (resp. time). We say that a path
\pi
passes through
z=(x,t)
if
\pi
starts before or at time
t
and
\pi(t)=x
.

We define the branching-coalescing point set generated by
N_{\varepsilon}(A)
by

\xi_{t}^{A}:=\{\pi(t):\pi\in N_{\varepsilon}(A)\}.

(6.3)

To determine how paths in
N_{\varepsilon}(A)
lead from the set
\xi^{A}_{S}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}
at time
S
to the set
\xi^{A}_{U}
at time
U
, we consider the set of
(S,U)
-RBP of
N_{\varepsilon}(A)
and how each path
\pi\in N_{\varepsilon}(A)
passes through these RBP’s. More precisely, we build a directed graph
G_{S,U}(N_{\varepsilon}(A))=(V,E)
with the vertex set
V
consisting of
\xi^{A}_{S}\times\{S\}
, the set of
(S,U)
-RBP of
N_{\varepsilon}(A)
, and
\xi^{A}_{U}\times\{U\}
.
For any two vertices
z_{1}=(x_{1},t_{1}),z_{2}=(x_{2},t_{2})\in V
with
S\leq t_{1}<t_{2}\leq U
, we draw a directed edge
e
from
z_{1}
to
z_{2}
if there exists

\pi\in N_{\varepsilon}(A)
with starting time
\sigma_{\pi}\leq S
, and
\pi
pass through
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
, but no
(S,U)
-RBP in between. Given such a
directed edge
e=(z_{1},z_{2})
, we call
e
an outgoing edge at
z_{1}
and an incoming edge at
z_{2}
.

The definition of RBP above for the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
is motivated by a similar definition of
relevant separation points
for the Brownian net
{\cal N}
in
[
SSS09
, Section 2.3]
and
[
SSS17
, Section 6.1]
. The Brownian net analogue of the directed graph
G_{S,U}(N_{\varepsilon}(A))
was studied in
[
SSS17
, Section 6.2]
and was called the
finite graph representation
of the Brownian net
{\cal N}
in the time interval
[S,U]
, which played a crucial role in
[
SSS17
]
in showing that the coupling between the discrete sticky webs and the discrete net converge to a coupling between the
sticky Brownian webs and the Brownian net.

For the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
, we show that the RBP and the directed graph
G_{S,U}
share similar properties to their continuum counterparts for the Brownian net
{\cal N}
.

Our first result shows that if
\pi\in N_{\varepsilon}(A)
starting at or before time
S
passes through
z_{1}=(x_{1},t_{1})
and
z_{2}=(x_{2},t_{2})
for some directed edge
(z_{1},z_{2})
in
G_{S,U}(N_{\varepsilon}(A))
, then descendants of

Corollary 6.4 (Non-relevant branching points)

Lemma 6.5 (Disjoint paths from relevant branching points)

6.3

Proposition 6.7 (At least
1
RBP)

Let
(\xi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the branching-coalescing point set generated by the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}

\xi_{0}=\{0\}

be the number of
(0,T)

{\mathbb{P}}({\cal R}_{T}\geq 1)={\mathbb{P}}(|\xi_{T}|\geq 2)\leq C\varepsilon\sqrt{T}.

(6.5)

Proof strategy

The proof starts with the fact that the i.i.d. Bernoulli product law with parameter
\rho_{\varepsilon}=2\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^{2})
as in (
4.28
) is invariant for the branching-coalescing point set
(\tilde{\xi}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
generated by the Bernoulli net
\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}
(see Lemma
4.3

and
\widetilde{N}_{\varepsilon}
in Lemma

generated from
N_{\varepsilon}
with
\xi_{0}=\tilde{\xi}_{0}
. The stationarity of

implies that for
\xi
, the reduction of particle density up to time

due to coalescence must be greater than the increase of density due to branching. The reduction in density can be computed for the collection of coalescing random walks starting from the same Bernoulli product law. By the reasoning above, this gives an upper bound on the number of offsprings of each particle (with branching turned on for that particle and its offsprings) that differ from all particles in the coalescing system. Restricted to the event that there is initially no other particles nearby in the coalescing system, this then leads to an upper bound on the probability that a single particle has more than 1 offspring at time
T
.

We now make the above proof sketch rigorous. First we consider the coalescing random walks (CRW)
(\eta_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with
\eta_{0}
following the i.i.d. Bernoulli product law with
{\mathbb{P}}(0\in\eta_{0})=\rho_{\varepsilon}
as in (
4.28
), and the random walk increment distribution

is the same as that for the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
. Note that there is a natural coupling between
\eta
and the branching-coalescing point set

generated by
N_{\varepsilon}
, such that almost surely,
\eta_{0}=\xi_{0}
, and
\eta_{t}\subset\xi_{t}
for all

equals

p_{\eta}(t):={\mathbb{P}}(0\in\eta_{t})={\mathbb{P}}(x\in\eta_{t})\quad\mbox{for all }x\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

(6.6)

We now bound the decrease of density
p_{\eta}(0)-p_{\eta}(t)
due to coalescence.

Lemma 6.8 (Density reduction for CRW)

denote summation w.r.t. this total order. We can then bound

\displaystyle\displaystyle p_{\eta}(T)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{P}}(\pi^{x}_{T}=0~\text{for some}~x\in{\mathbb{Z}})=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[1_{\{\pi^{x}_{T}=0\}}1_{\{\pi^{x}_{T}\neq\pi^{y}_{T}\,\forall\,y\prec x\}}\Big]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\Big({\mathbb{E}}[1_{\{\pi^{x}_{T}=0\}}]-\sum_{y\prec x}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[1_{\{\pi^{x}_{T}=\pi^{y}_{T}=0\}}\Big]\Big)

\displaystyle=\rho_{\varepsilon}-\rho_{\varepsilon}^{2}\sum_{z\neq 0}{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{0,z}\leq T),

where in the last step, we made the change of variable

\tau
actually exit
[-3\varepsilon^{-1},3\varepsilon^{-1}]
. Using the graphical construction of
(\xi^{0}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, where we sample the realisation of the branchings and random walk jumps originating at each space-time point, we note that
A_{8}
occurs only if one of the random walk jumps originating from the space-time window
[-2\varepsilon^{-1},2\varepsilon^{-1}]\times[0,T]
jumps outside
[-3\varepsilon^{-1},3\varepsilon^{-1}]
. Let
\Delta
denote a random walk increment. Then by a union bound,

{\mathbb{P}}(A_{8})\leq 2T\sum_{x\geq 0}{\mathbb{P}}(|\Delta|\geq\varepsilon^{-1}+x)\leq 2T\sum_{x\geq 0}\frac{{\mathbb{E}}[|\Delta|^{\gamma}]}{(\varepsilon^{-1}+x)^{\gamma}}\leq C\varepsilon^{\gamma-1}T\leq C\varepsilon\sqrt{T},

(6.16)

where the last bound holds if
\gamma\geq 3
. On the other hand, we have

{\mathbb{P}}(A_{7}\cap A_{8}^{c})={\mathbb{E}}[1_{A_{8}^{c}}\,{\mathbb{P}}(A_{7}|(\xi^{0}_{t})_{t\leq\tau})]\leq C\varepsilon\sqrt{T},

(6.17)

because on the event
A_{8}^{c}
, there exists some
x\in\xi^{0}_{\tau}\cap[-3\varepsilon^{-1},3\varepsilon^{-1}]\backslash[-2\varepsilon^{-1},2\varepsilon^{-1}]
. We can then sample a random walk
S=(S_{t})_{t\geq\tau}
in
(\xi^{0}_{t})_{t\geq\tau}
with
S_{\tau}=x
, whose law is still that of a random walk with transition kernel
a(\cdot)

since
\tau
is a stopping time. The event
A_{7}
implies that either
S
returns to the interval
[-\varepsilon^{-1},\varepsilon^{-1}]
before time
T
to ensure
S_{T}=\pi^{0}_{T}
, or
\sup_{t\in[\tau,T]}|S_{t}|\geq 4\varepsilon^{-1}
. Either way, we must have
\sup_{t\in[\tau,T]}|S_{t}-S_{\tau}|\geq\varepsilon^{-1}
, the probability of which is bounded by
C\varepsilon\sqrt{T}
by (
6.15
). The bounds (
6.16
) and (
6.17
) imply
{\mathbb{P}}(A_{7})\leq C\varepsilon\sqrt{T}
, which concludes the proof of Proposition

6.7
.

6.4
Probability of having more than
K
relevant branching points

We now bootstrap the bound on
{\mathbb{P}}({\cal R}_{T}\geq 1)
in Proposition
6.7
to get a bound on
{\mathbb{P}}({\cal R}_{T}\geq K)
for
K\geq 2
.

Theorem 6.9 (More than
K
RBP)

Let
(\xi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the branching-coalescing point set generated by the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
with
\xi_{0}=\{0\}
. Let
{\cal R}_{T}
be the number of
(0,T)
-RBP for
N_{\varepsilon}(0,0)
. Then for any
K\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there exist
C_{K},\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(K),\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(K)>0
such that for all
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}

and
1\leq T\leq\delta_{0}\varepsilon^{-2}
,

{\mathbb{P}}({\cal R}_{T}\geq K)\leq C_{K}\,(\varepsilon\sqrt{T})^{K}.

(6.18)

Proof
We first outline the proof strategy, which starts with a decomposition according to the times
t_{1},\ldots,t_{K}
of the
K
earliest
(0,T)
-RBP’s

z_{i}=(x_{i},t_{i})
,
1\leq i\leq K
. Assume for the moment the
t_{i}
’s are all distinct. Let us include half integer times in
(\xi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
to keep track of the increments of the branching-coalescing random walks. We can construct
(\xi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
through an exploration procedure as follows (see Figure
3
). First sample a random walk
S^{0}
starting at
(0,0)
and run till time
t_{1}
, where at each branching point of
N_{\varepsilon}
the walk encounters, it chooses one of the two outgoing jumps with equal probability. This generates a path in
(\xi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. Then
z_{1}=(t_{1},S^{0}_{t_{1}})
must be the first
(0,T)
-RBP. In particular,
z_{1}
is a branching point, an event which we denote by
B_{1}
and has probability
\varepsilon
. We next sample two random walks
S^{1,1}
and
S^{1,2}
starting at
z_{1}
, whose first jumps are independent due to the binary branching at
z_{1}
, but then interact as coalescing random walks. The paths
(S^{1,1}_{t})_{t_{1}\leq t\leq t_{2}}
and
(S^{1,2}_{t})_{t_{1}\leq t\leq t_{2}}
are also paths in
(\xi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, and by Lemma
6.5
,
S^{1,1}
and
S^{1,2}
cannot coalesce before or at time
t_{2}
because
z_{1}
is a
(0,T)
-relevant branching point. We denote this event by
E_{1}
, the probability of which can be bounded by
C/\sqrt{t_{2}-t_{1}}
by standard random walk estimates. At time
t_{2}
, the next RBP
z_{2}
is either
(t_{2},S^{1,1}_{t_{2}})
or
(t_{2},S^{1,2}_{t_{2}})
. Once a choice has been made, that point must be a branching point, an event which we denote by
B_{2}
. We then sample two random walks
S^{2,1}
and
S^{2,2}
starting at
z_{2}
, whose first jumps are independent and then interact as coalescing random walks (also coalesce with previously sampled random walks if they have not terminated as a result of reaching a RBP). Denote by
E_{2}
the event that
S^{2,1}
and
S^{2,2}
do not coalesce by time
t_{3}
. We then repeat this construction so that at each time
t_{k}
, there are at most
k
choices (the number of locations of the previously sampled coalescing random walks) for the RBP
z_{k}
, there is an event
B_{k}
for
z_{k}
to be a branching point, and there is an event
E_{k}
for two sampled random walks
S^{k,1}
and
S^{k,2}
starting from
z_{k}
not to coalesce by time
t_{k+1}
. The event for the time interval
[t_{K},T]
will be different because
t_{K+1}
is unspecified, and we avoided
a full decomposition of all
(0,T)
-RBP’s because we are unable to control the growing combinatorial factor
k
associated with the choice of
z_{k}
at time
t_{k}
. Instead of restricting to the event
E_{K}
on the two sampled random walks
S^{K,1}
and
S^{K,2}
starting from
z_{K}
, we simply restrict to the event
G(z_{K};t_{K},T)
that there are two paths in
N_{\varepsilon}(z_{K})
which are disjoint on the time interval
(t_{K},T]
. Thanks to Proposition
6.7
, we can control
{\mathbb{P}}(G(z_{K};t_{K},T))
averaged over
t_{K}
, which leads to a version of (
6.18
) with
T
therein replaced by
T^{\prime}
averaged over
[T,2T]
. A monotonicity argument then gives (
6.18
).

Figure 3:
The image on the left illustrates the exploration procedure for
\{{\cal R}_{T}\geq 2\}
. The image on the right
illustrates the branching-coalescing point set, with
\bullet
identifying the relevant branching points (RBP’s).

We now make the above proof sketch more precise. First we restrict to the event
D
that the first
K
relevant branching points all occur at distinct times. Then by successively conditioning on the sampled coalescing random walks (
S^{0}
,
S^{1,1}
,
S^{1,2}
, …) up to time
t_{K}
,
t_{K-1}
, …and applying the Markov property, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(D\cap\{{\cal R}_{T}\geq K\})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{0<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{K}<T}\prod_{k=1}^{K-1}(\varepsilon k{\mathbb{P}}(E_{k}))\,\cdot K\,{\mathbb{P}}(G(z_{K};t_{K},T))

(6.19)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{K-1}K!\sum_{0\leq t_{1}<\cdots<t_{K}<T}\prod_{i=1}^{K-1}{\mathbb{P}}(E_{k})\cdot\phi(T-t_{K}),

where
\phi(T-t_{K}):={\mathbb{P}}(G(z_{K};t_{K},T))
depends only on
T-t_{K}
by translation invariance.

To bound
{\mathbb{P}}(E_{k})
, note that if
S
and
S^{\prime}
are two independent random walks with kernel
a(\cdot)
starting at
(0,0)
, and
\tau^{S,S^{\prime}}:=\min\{t\geq 1:S_{t}=S^{\prime}_{t}\}
. Since
S^{\prime}-S
is a symmetric random walk with finite second moments, by a standard random walk hitting time estimate (see e.g.
[
NRS05
, Lemma 2.2]
),

{\mathbb{P}}(E_{k})={\mathbb{P}}(\tau^{S,S^{\prime}}>t_{k+1}-t_{k})\leq{\mathbb{E}}\Big[C\,\frac{|S_{1}-S^{\prime}_{1}|}{\sqrt{t_{k+1}-t_{k}}}\Big]\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{t_{k+1}-t_{k}}}.

(6.20)

Substitute this bound into (
6.19
), replace
T
by
T^{\prime}
and average over
T\leq T^{\prime}\leq 2T
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{T}^{2T}{\mathbb{P}}(D\cap\{{\cal R}_{T^{\prime}}\geq K\})

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{K-1}K!\Big(\sum_{t=1}^{2T}\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{K-1}\sum_{t=1}^{2T}\phi(t)

(6.21)

\displaystyle\leq K!\,(C\varepsilon\sqrt{T})^{K-1}\sum_{t=1}^{2T}\phi(t),

where in the first inequality, the sum over
t_{1}\in(0,T)
compensates the factor
\frac{1}{T}
. To bound the last sum in (
6.21
),
note that for
K=1
, we have a similar decomposition according to the time of the first relevant branching point:

{\mathbb{P}}({\cal R}_{2T}\geq 1)=\sum_{t=0}^{2T-1}{\mathbb{P}}(|\xi_{t}|=1)\phi(2T-t)\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{2T}\phi(t),

where we applied Proposition
6.7
to obtain
{\mathbb{P}}(|\xi_{t}|=1)\geq 1/2
. Again by Proposition
6.7
,

\sum_{t=1}^{2T}\phi(t)\leq 2{\mathbb{P}}({\cal R}_{2T}\geq 1)\leq C\varepsilon\sqrt{T}.

(6.22)

Substituting this bound into (
6.21
) then gives

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{T}^{2T}{\mathbb{P}}(D\cap\{{\cal R}_{T^{\prime}}\geq K\})\leq K!\,(C\varepsilon\sqrt{T})^{K}\leq C_{K}(\varepsilon\sqrt{T})^{K},

(6.23)

which is an averaged version of (
6.18
).

Before removing the averaging in (
6.23
), let us first remove the restriction
D
that the first
K
relevant branching points all occur at distinct times. Assume
D^{c}
, so that
|\{t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{K}\}|=M<K
. Let us relabel these distinct times by
0\leq s_{1}<\cdots<s_{M}
, with multiplicities
r_{1},\ldots,r_{M}
so that
\sum r_{i}=K
. We can apply the same argument as in the bound for
{\mathbb{P}}(D\cap\{{\cal R}_{T}\geq M\})
, except that at each time
s_{k}
with
r_{k}>1
, we only restrict to the event
E_{k}
for a pair of sampled random walks starting from one of the
r_{k}
RBP’s. However, the
r_{k}
RBP’s at time
s_{k}
must still all be branching points, which contribute a probability factor of
\varepsilon^{r_{k}}
instead of
\varepsilon
. This argument gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{T}^{2T}{\mathbb{P}}(D^{c}\cap\{{\cal R}_{T^{\prime}}\geq K\})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{M=1}^{K-1}C_{K,M}\varepsilon^{K-M}(C\varepsilon\sqrt{T})^{M}

(6.24)

\displaystyle=\sum_{M=1}^{K-1}C_{K,M}(C\sqrt{T})^{M-K}(C\varepsilon\sqrt{T})^{K}\leq C_{K}(\varepsilon\sqrt{T})^{K},

where
C_{K,M}
are combinatorial factors depending on
K
and
M
. Therefore (
6.23
) can be replaced by

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{T}^{2T}{\mathbb{P}}({\cal R}_{T^{\prime}}\geq K)\leq C_{K}(\varepsilon\sqrt{T})^{K}.

(6.25)

To remove the averaging in
T^{\prime}
, note that for any
T^{\prime}\geq T
, the event
\{{\cal R}_{T^{\prime}}\geq K\}
contains the intersection of the events that
\xi_{T^{\prime}-T}=:\{x\}
is a singleton and the branching-coalescing random walks starting from
x
at time
T^{\prime}-T
has at least
K
relevant branching points for the time interval
(T^{\prime}-T,T^{\prime})
. Using the Markov property of
(\xi^{\varepsilon}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, we then have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}({\cal R}_{T^{\prime}}\geq K)\geq{\mathbb{P}}(|\xi_{T^{\prime}-T}|=1){\mathbb{P}}({\cal R}_{T}\geq K)\geq\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{P}}({\cal R}_{T}\geq K),

(6.26)

where the last bound follows from Proposition
6.7
. Substituting this bound into (
6.25
) then gives (
6.18
).

6.5
A multiscale argument for tightness

We are now ready to verify the tightness condition (
6.1
) for the rescaled discrete nets
S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}N_{\varepsilon}

by adapting a multiscale argument from
[
BMSV06
]
for coalescing random walks. More precisely, we will prove the following:

\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\sup_{(\varepsilon x,\varepsilon^{2}t)\in\Lambda_{L,T}}{\mathbb{P}}\big(N_{\varepsilon}\in A_{M\varepsilon^{-1},\delta\varepsilon^{-2}}(x,t)\big)=0,\quad\text{for all~}L,T,M>0,

(6.27)

where
A_{M\varepsilon^{-1},\delta\varepsilon^{-2}}(x,t)
is defined as in (
6.1
). By translation invariance, it suffices to show

\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}{\mathbb{P}}\big(N_{\varepsilon}\in A_{M\varepsilon^{-1},\delta\varepsilon^{-2}}(0,0)\big)=0\quad\mbox{for all }M>0.

(6.28)

The key ingredients of the multiscale argument used in
[
BMSV06
]
are: density reduction through
coalescence, and large deviation estimates for a single random walk. In our case, we need large deviation estimates for a random
walk and all its branching descendants, which can be achieved thanks to our bounds on the number of relevant branching points (RBP’s)
in Theorem
6.9
. This is the content of the following lemma, where the three terms in (
6.29
) originate respectively from
large deviation from the sum of many small jumps, large deviation from rare large jumps, and excessive branching.

Lemma 6.10 (Large deviation estimate)

Let
(\xi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the branching-coalescing point set generated by the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
with
\xi_{0}=\{0\}
.
Then for any
K\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there exist
c_{K},\delta_{0},\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for all
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
,
\ell>0
, and

1\leq T\leq\delta_{0}\varepsilon^{-2}
,

{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\max\xi_{t}\geq\ell\Big)\leq e^{-\frac{\ell^{2}}{c_{K}T}}+c_{K}T\ell^{-\gamma}+c_{K}\varepsilon^{K}T^{K/2},

(6.29)

where the jump distribution
a(\cdot)
satisfies (
1.1
) with
\sum_{x}|x|^{\gamma}a(x)<\infty
for some
\gamma>3
.

Proof
We first prove the deviation bound for
\xi
at the terminal time
T
, i.e.,

{\mathbb{P}}\big(\max\xi_{T}\geq\ell\big)\leq e^{-\frac{\ell^{2}}{c_{K}T}}+c_{K}T\ell^{-\gamma}+c_{K}\varepsilon^{K}T^{K/2}.

(6.30)

Let
{\cal R}_{T}
be the number of
(0,T)
-RBP for
N_{\varepsilon}(0,0)
, defined as in (
6.2
). Then by Theorem
6.9
,

{\mathbb{P}}({\cal R}_{T}\geq K)\leq c_{K}\varepsilon^{K}T^{K/2},

(6.31)

which gives the last term in (
6.30
).

On the complementary event
\{{\cal R}_{T}\leq K-1\}
, we have
|\xi_{T}|\leq K
by the properties of RBP established in Section
6.2
.
Let us include half integer times in
(\xi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
to keep track of the increments of the branching-coalescing random walks. We can then
sample a random walk
S
in
\xi
such that
S_{0}=0
, and whenever
S
encounters a branching point of
N_{\varepsilon}
, it chooses one of the two
outgoing jumps with equal probability. Clearly
S
has the law of a random walk with jump distribution
a(\cdot)
. On the other hand,
the position
S_{T}
depends only on the choices made by
S
at each
(0,T)
-RBP it encounters along the way. Therefore on the event
\{{\cal R}_{T}\leq K-1\}
,
each point in
\xi_{T}
(including
\max\xi_{T}
) will coincide with
S_{T}
with probability at least
1/2^{K-1}
, and hence

{\mathbb{P}}(\max\xi_{T}\geq\ell,{\cal R}_{T}\leq K-1,S_{T}\geq\ell)\geq\frac{1}{2^{K-1}}{\mathbb{P}}(\max\xi_{T}\geq\ell,{\cal R}_{T}\leq K-1).

It follows that

{\mathbb{P}}(\max\xi_{T}\geq\ell,{\cal R}_{T}\leq K-1)\leq 2^{K-1}{\mathbb{P}}(S_{T}\geq\ell)\leq e^{-\frac{\ell^{2}}{c_{K}T}}+c_{K}T\ell^{-\gamma},

(6.32)

where the last inequality is a standard random walk large deviation estimate (see e.g.
[
N79
, Corollary 1.8]
).

Lastly, to show (
6.29
), one only needs to note that

\tau:=\inf\{t\in{\mathbb{N}}:\max\xi_{t}\geq\ell\}

(6.33)

is a stopping time. On the event
\tau\leq T
, we can sample a random walk
S
in
\xi
starting from position
\max\xi_{\tau}
at time
\tau
, which then leads to
\xi_{T}\ni S_{T}\geq\ell
with a probability that is bounded away from
0
uniformly in
T-\tau
(by the central limit theorem). The bound (
6.29
) then follows from (
6.30
) with an adjusted value of
c_{K}
.

We are now ready to prove the tightness of the family of rescaled discrete nets.

Theorem 6.11 (Tightness)

The family of rescaled discrete nets
(S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}N_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
in Theorem
1.1
is tight.

Proof
As discussed above, we need to verify the condition (
6.28
) for all
M>0
. As we will see, the constant
M
enters the proof only through the large deviation estimate in Lemma
6.10
when we take
\ell=cM\varepsilon^{-1}
for constants
c>0
independent of
M
. For any fixed
M>0
,
the last two terms in the right hand side of (
6.29
) will always be of order
o(\varepsilon^{3})
, while the first term will always be of order
o(1/\delta)
when we take
T=\delta\varepsilon^{-2}
with
\delta\downarrow 0
. These are the only bounds we need. To simplify the notation, we may therefore assume that
M=10
and
\sigma^{2}
=1. We also note that to bound
{\mathbb{P}}(N_{\varepsilon}\in A_{M\varepsilon^{-1},\delta\varepsilon^{-2}}(x,t))
in (
6.28
), we can discard the event that there are random walk increments in
N_{\varepsilon}
that jump across the interval
[-M\varepsilon^{-1},M\varepsilon^{-1}]
in the time interval
[0,\delta\varepsilon^{-2}]
, so that we only need to restrict our attention to random walks that originate inside the space-time box
[-M\varepsilon^{-1},M\varepsilon^{-1}]\times[0,\delta\varepsilon^{-2}]
. The same issue arises in the proof of tightness for the family of rescaled coalescing random walks, and the proof is identical since the branching at most doubles the number of random walk increments, see
[
NRS05
, Section 4]
.

We now apply the multiscale argument as in the proof of
[
BMSV06
, Proposition 2.3]
, where for convenience we actually consider all random walks originating from a larger space-time box
[-M\varepsilon^{-1},M\varepsilon^{-1}]\times[0,2\delta\varepsilon^{-2}]
. In the first step, we choose a large fixed constant
R>0
independent of
\varepsilon>0
(to be determined below Lemma
6.12
) and decompose the space-time box
[-M\varepsilon^{-1},M\varepsilon^{-1}]\times[0,2\delta\varepsilon^{-2}]
in the time direction into
I_{0}:=2\delta\varepsilon^{-2}/R
boxes of the form

B_{0,i}:=[-M\varepsilon^{-1},M\varepsilon^{-1}]\times[(i-1)R,iR],\qquad i=1,\ldots,I_{0}.

(6.34)

To simplify notation later, we may assume
I_{0}=2^{J}
for some
J\in{\mathbb{N}}
; otherwise we can increase
\delta
and
I_{0}
by a factor of at most
2
such that this property holds.
For the branching-coalescing random walks in
N_{\varepsilon}
starting from each space-time box
B_{0,i}
, we let them evolve up to time
T^{(0)}_{i}:=iR
and discard the event
E_{0,i}
that some particle exits the interval

[-\widetilde{M}_{0},\widetilde{M}_{0}]:=[-(M+1)\varepsilon^{-1},(M+1)\varepsilon^{-1}]

before time
T^{(0)}_{i}
. Indeed, by the large deviation estimate in Lemma
6.10
with
K=4
and a union bound over all starting points in
\cup_{i=1}^{I_{0}}B_{0,i}
,
we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(\cup_{i=1}^{I_{0}}E_{0,i})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq 4M\delta\varepsilon^{-3}\big(e^{-\frac{cM^{2}\varepsilon^{-2}}{R}}+cRM^{-\gamma}\varepsilon^{\gamma}+c\varepsilon^{4}R^{2}\big),

(6.35)

where
\gamma=3+\eta>3
by the assumption on the jump distribution
a(\cdot)
in (
1.1
). Denote
E_{0}:=\cup_{i=1}^{I_{0}}E_{0,i}
.
Given
M
and
R
, clearly

\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(E_{0})=0.

(6.36)

Therefore to verify (
6.28
), we can discard the event
E_{0}
, which ensures that no walk has exited

[-\widetilde{M}_{0},\widetilde{M}_{0}]
in this first step, and at the end of this step, all remaining walks lie in
[-\widetilde{M}_{0},\widetilde{M}_{0}]

at times
T^{(0)}_{i}=iR
,
1\leq i\leq I_{0}
, whose configurations we denote by
\xi^{(0)}_{i}\subset[-\widetilde{M}_{0},\widetilde{M}_{0}]
.

In the second step, let
I_{1}:=I_{0}/2
and let
T^{(1)}_{i}:=T^{(0)}_{2i}=2iR
for
1\leq i\leq I_{1}
. For each
1\leq i\leq I_{1}
, we consider the remaining random walks starting from
\xi^{(0)}_{2i-2}\times\{T^{(0)}_{2i-2}\}

and
\xi^{(0)}_{2i-1}\times\{T^{(0)}_{2i-1}\}
and let them evolve together till time
T^{(1)}_{i}=T^{(0)}_{2i}
, and denote the resulting particle configuration at time
T^{(1)}_{i}
by
\xi^{(1)}_{i}
. We will show that we can also discard the events
E_{1,i}:=D_{1,i}\cup F_{1,i}
,
1\leq i\leq I_{1}
, where

D_{1,i}
:

Some random walk starting from
\xi^{(0)}_{2i-2}
at time
T^{(0)}_{2i-2}
, or from
\xi^{(0)}_{2i-1}
at time
T^{(0)}_{2i-1}
, exits the interval

[-\widetilde{M}_{1},\widetilde{M}_{1}]
before time
T^{(1)}_{i}=T^{(0)}_{2i}
, where
\widetilde{M}_{1}:=\widetilde{M}_{0}+\beta_{1}\varepsilon^{-1}
for some
\beta_{1}
to be chosen.

F_{1,i}
:

The particle density
\frac{1}{4M\varepsilon^{-1}}|\xi^{(1)}_{i}|>p:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
.

Let us denote
E_{1}:=\cup_{i=1}^{I_{1}}E_{1,i}
. Similar to (
6.36
), we will show that

\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(E_{1}\cap E_{0}^{c})=0,

(6.37)

which allows us to discard the event
E_{1}
in the verification of (
6.28
).

We now iterate the above argument. After the
(j-1)
-st iteration for some
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, where we have discarded the events
E_{0}\cup\cdots\cup E_{j-1}
,
we are left with random walk configurations
\xi^{(j-1)}_{i}
at times
T^{(j-1)}_{i}=T^{(j-2)}_{2i}=i\cdot 2^{j-1}R
for
1\leq i\leq I_{j-1}:=2^{-(j-1)}I_{0}
, such that

\xi^{(j-1)}_{i}\subset[-\widetilde{M}_{j-1},\widetilde{M}_{j-1}]\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\frac{1}{4M\varepsilon^{-1}}|\xi^{(j-1)}_{i}|\leq p^{j-1}=2^{-(j-1)/2},

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{I_{j}}{\mathbb{P}}\big(D_{j,i}\bigcap\cap_{k=0}^{j-1}E_{k}^{c}\big)

\displaystyle=:8(B^{1}_{j}+B^{2}_{j}+B^{3}_{j}),

where we have expanded the product.

Also note that because

Since

as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
because

, we have

\limsup_{\delta\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\sum_{j=1}^{J}B^{2}_{j}=0.

(6.42)

Lastly using

and

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{J}B^{1}_{j}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{M\delta}{R\,\varepsilon^{3}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}2^{-\frac{3j}{2}}e^{-\frac{c\beta_{j}^{2}}{2^{j}R\varepsilon^{2}}}

\displaystyle=C\frac{M\delta}{R\,\varepsilon^{3}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}2^{-\frac{3j}{2}}e^{-\frac{c}{2^{j}(J+1-j)^{2}R\varepsilon^{2}}}=C\frac{M\delta}{R\,\varepsilon^{3}}2^{-\frac{3(J+1)}{2}}\sum_{\tilde{j}=1}^{J}2^{\frac{3\tilde{j}}{2}}e^{-\frac{c}{2^{J+1-\tilde{j}}\,\tilde{j}^{2}R\varepsilon^{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq CMR^{\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\tilde{j}=1}^{J}2^{\frac{3\tilde{j}}{2}}e^{-c\cdot\frac{2^{\tilde{j}-1}}{\tilde{j}^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{\delta}}\leq CMR^{\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{c}{2\delta}}\sum_{\tilde{j}=1}^{J}2^{\frac{3\tilde{j}}{2}}e^{-c\big(\frac{2^{\tilde{j}-1}}{\tilde{j}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\big)\frac{1}{\delta}},

where we note that the last sum is uniformly bounded in

and in

sufficiently small, because

comes from Lemma

and

\limsup_{\delta\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\sum_{j=1}^{J}B^{1}_{j}=0.

Combining (

which accounts for the contribution of

in (

We now consider the events
F_{j}=\cup_{i=1}^{I_{j}}F_{j,i}
on the density of the branching-coalescing random walks. We will need the following result, which generalises

on the density of purely coalescing random walks. Roughly speaking, it says that if the initial density of

so that the average distance between neighbouring walks is of order

, the density of the branching-coalescing random walks would have decreased by a fixed factor depending on

Lemma 6.12 (Density decay of branching-coalescing random walks)

Let
p\in(1/2,1)
be fixed. Let
(\xi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a branching-coalescing point set generated by the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
with initial condition

\xi_{0}\subset[0,L]
. Then there exist
R_{0},\varepsilon_{0},\delta_{0},C,c>0
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, if

|\xi_{0}|\leq\upsilon L
for some
\upsilon\in(0,1)
and
T:=R_{0}\upsilon^{-2}\leq 2\delta_{0}\varepsilon^{-2}
, then

{\mathbb{P}}(|\xi_{T}|\geq p\upsilon L)\leq Ce^{-c\upsilon L}+C\upsilon L\varepsilon^{4}T^{2}.

(6.45)

We are now ready to choose
R
in (
6.34
). Let

be as in Lemma
6.12
for

, and set

.

\limsup_{\delta\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\delta}\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\sum_{j=1}^{J}{\mathbb{P}}\big(F_{j}\bigcap\cap_{k=0}^{j-1}E_{k}^{c}\big)=0.

(6.48)

Together with (

Proof of Lemma
6.12

By adding new walks if necessary, we can assume that we start with exactly
\upsilon L
walks at positions
0\leq x_{1}<x_{2}<\cdots<x_{\upsilon L}\leq L
.
Let
{\cal R}^{x_{k}}_{T}
be the number of
(0,T)
-relevant branching points of
N_{\varepsilon}(x_{k},0)
, and let
{\cal R}_{T}:=\max_{1\leq k\leq\upsilon L}{\cal R}^{x_{k}}_{T}
.
First note that by Theorem
6.9
with
K=4
, we have

{\mathbb{P}}({\cal R}_{T}\geq 4)\leq C\upsilon L\varepsilon^{4}T^{2},

(6.49)

which is the source of the second term in the bound in (
6.45
).

Note that for any
L_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
(to be chosen later), the number of consecutive pairs
(x_{k},x_{k+1})
with
x_{k+1}-x_{k}>L_{0}
is at most
\frac{L}{L_{0}}
. Dividing
(x_{i},\cdots,x_{j},x_{j+1},\cdots,x_{k})
into two blocks
(x_{i},\cdots,x_{j})
and
(x_{j+1},\cdots,x_{k})
whenever
x_{j+1}-x_{j}>L_{0}
, we can divide
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\upsilon L})
into at most
1+L/L_{0}
blocks. In each block, we can match adjacent
(x_{i},x_{i+1})
into pairs, which leaves at most
one unmatched particle. This gives at least
\kappa:=\frac{1}{2}(\upsilon L-1-L/L_{0})
disjoint pairs of particles where each pair is within distance
L_{0}
of each other. For such a pair
(x,y)
with
0<y-x\leq L_{0}
, if we consider the branching-coalescing point sets
\xi^{x}
and
\xi^{y}
generated by the same discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
with initial conditions
\xi^{x}_{0}=\{x\}
and
\xi^{y}_{0}=\{y\}
, then we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(|\xi^{x}_{T}\cup\xi^{y}_{T}|=1)

\displaystyle\geq{\mathbb{P}}({\cal R}^{x}_{T}={\cal R}^{y}_{T}=0,\xi^{x}_{T}=\xi^{y}_{T})

(6.50)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{P}}({\cal R}^{x}_{T}={\cal R}^{y}_{T}=0)-{\mathbb{P}}({\cal R}^{x}_{T}={\cal R}^{y}_{T}=0,\xi^{x}_{T}\neq\xi^{y}_{T}).

On the event
\{{\cal R}^{x}_{T}={\cal R}^{y}_{T}=0\}
,
|\xi^{x}_{T}|=|\xi^{y}_{T}|=1
. Therefore by sampling the random walk jumps at branching points of
N_{\varepsilon}
, we can couple
\xi^{x}
and
\xi^{y}
with two coalescing random walks
S^{x}
and
S^{y}
such that
\xi^{x}_{T}=\{S^{x}_{T}\}
and
\xi^{y}_{T}=\{S^{y}_{T}\}
. This implies that

\{\xi^{x}_{T}\neq\xi^{y}_{T}\}=\{S^{x}_{T}\neq S^{y}_{T}\}
and

{\mathbb{P}}({\cal R}^{x}_{T}={\cal R}^{y}_{T}=0,\xi^{x}_{T}\neq\xi^{y}_{T})\leq{\mathbb{P}}(\tau_{x,y}>T)\leq C\frac{L_{0}}{\sqrt{T}},

(6.51)

where
\tau_{x,y}
is the first collision time between
S^{x}
and
S^{y}
, and the last inequality is a standard estimate, see e.g.
[
NRS05
, Lemma 2.2]
.

On the other hand, by Theorem
6.9
, we have

{\mathbb{P}}({\cal R}^{x}_{T}={\cal R}^{y}_{T}=0)\geq 1-\sum_{w=x,y}{\mathbb{P}}({\cal R}^{w}_{T}\geq 1)\geq 1-2C\varepsilon\sqrt{T}.

(6.52)

Substituting the above bounds in to (
6.50
) then gives

{\mathbb{P}}(|\xi^{x}_{T}\cup\xi^{y}_{T}|=1)\geq 1-2C\varepsilon\sqrt{T}-C\frac{L_{0}}{\sqrt{T}}=:1-p_{0}.

(6.53)

Let us denote the
\kappa=\frac{1}{2}(\upsilon L-1-L/L_{0})
pairs of particles by
(u_{i},v_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\kappa}
, with
v_{i}\in(u_{i},u_{i}+L_{0}]
.
We observe that the branching coalescing point set
\xi^{\vec{u},\vec{v}}
generated by the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
with
\xi^{\vec{u},\vec{v}}_{0}=\{u_{1},v_{1},\ldots,u_{\kappa},v_{\kappa}\}
can be coupled to
\kappa
independent branching-coalescing point sets
\xi^{u_{i},v_{i}}
,
1\leq i\leq\kappa
, with
\xi^{u_{i},v_{i}}_{0}=\{u_{i},v_{i}\}
, such that almost surely,

|\xi^{\vec{u},\vec{v}}_{T}|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa}|\xi^{u_{i},v_{i}}_{T}|.

(6.54)

This is an analogue of
[
BMSV06
, Lemma 5.3]
for coalescing random walks and can be shown as follows. First sample a realisation of the jointly independent branching-coalescing random walks associated with
(\xi^{u_{i},v_{i}})_{1\leq i\leq\kappa}
, keeping track of the random walk jumps
by defining
\xi^{u_{i},v_{i}}_{t}
at the half-integer times
t\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}+1/2
. We then construct
\xi^{\vec{u},\vec{v}}
by recursively defining the branching-coalescing random walks starting from
(u_{i},v_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\kappa}
. For
i=1
, we construct the branching-coalescing random walks starting from
\{u_{1},v_{1}\}
by following exactly
\xi^{u_{1},v_{1}}
. We then add random walk paths starting from
\{u_{2},v_{2}\}
by following paths in
\xi^{u_{2},v_{2}}
, except when a random walk path meets an existing walk, it coalesces with that walk and discards its own descendants. The same procedure is then iterated by adding random walk paths in
\xi^{u_{3},v_{3}},\ldots,\xi^{u_{\kappa},v_{\kappa}}
. Clearly (
6.54
) holds, while the
\xi^{\vec{u},\vec{v}}
constructed this way has the correct distribution because our procedure recursively explores the realisation of the random walk branching and increments at different lattice sites.

For
\xi
with
\xi_{0}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{\upsilon L}\}
, we can now use (
6.54
) to bound

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(|\xi_{T}|\geq p\upsilon L)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}({\cal R}_{T}\geq 4)+{\mathbb{P}}(|\xi_{T}|\geq p\upsilon L,{\cal R}_{T}\leq 3)

\displaystyle\leq C\upsilon L\varepsilon^{4}T^{2}+{\mathbb{P}}\big(|\xi^{\vec{u},\vec{v}}_{T}|+4(1+L/L_{0})\geq p\upsilon L\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\upsilon L\varepsilon^{4}T^{2}+{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{\kappa}|\xi^{u_{i},v_{i}}_{T}|\geq p\upsilon L-4(1+L/L_{0})\Big),

(6.55)

where we used the bound (
6.49
) and the fact that for each
x_{i}\notin\{u_{1},v_{1},\ldots,u_{\kappa},v_{\kappa}\}
,
the event
\{{\cal R}^{x_{i}}_{T}\leq 3\}
implies that
|\xi^{x_{i}}_{T}|\leq 4
, and there are at most
1+L/L_{0}
many such
x_{i}
’s.

We now choose
L_{0}=(\alpha\upsilon)^{-1}
with
\alpha>0
small and depending only on
p\in(1/2,1)
such that

\displaystyle p\upsilon L-4(1+L/L_{0})\geq p^{\prime}\upsilon L-4,\quad\mbox{with }p^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}(p+1/2)>1/2.

(6.56)

Note that
|\xi^{u_{i},v_{i}}_{T}|
are independent random variables, and
|\xi^{u_{i},v_{i}}_{T}|1_{\{{\cal R}^{u_{i}}_{T},{\cal R}^{v_{i}}_{T}\leq 3\}}

can be dominated by i.i.d. random variables
\zeta_{i}
with

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(\zeta_{i}=1)=1-p_{0},\qquad{\mathbb{P}}(\zeta_{i}=8)=p_{0},

where as in (
6.53
),

p_{0}:=2C\varepsilon\sqrt{T}+C\frac{L_{0}}{\sqrt{T}}\leq 2C\sqrt{2\delta_{0}}+\frac{C}{\alpha\upsilon\sqrt{T}}.

We now choose
\delta_{0}
sufficiently small and
T=R_{0}\upsilon^{-2}\leq 2\delta_{0}\varepsilon^{-2}
with
R_{0}
sufficiently large such
that
{\mathbb{E}}[\zeta_{i}]=1+7p_{0}<2p^{\prime}
with
p^{\prime}
defined in (
6.56
).

We can then continue the bound in (
6.55
) to obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(|\xi_{T}|\geq p\upsilon L)

\displaystyle\leq C\upsilon L\varepsilon^{4}T^{2}+{\mathbb{P}}(\cup_{i=1}^{\kappa}\{{\cal R}^{u_{i}}_{T}\vee{\cal R}^{v_{i}}_{T}\geq 4\})+{\mathbb{P}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{\gamma L/2}\zeta_{i}\geq p^{\prime}\gamma L-4\Big)

(6.57)

\displaystyle\leq 2C\upsilon L\varepsilon^{4}T^{2}+Ce^{-c\upsilon L},

where we used
\kappa\leq\upsilon L/2
and applied a standard large deviation bound for
\sum\zeta_{i}
. This is exactly
(
6.45
), which concludes the proof of Lemma
6.12
.

Appendix A
Proof of some facts for the Brownian web and net

In this appendix, we sketch the proof of Proposition
2.6
and Theorem
2.7
, which follow
readily from results in the literature.

Proof sketch for Proposition
2.6

As in (
1.3
), let us consider a pair jump distribution

a^{(2)}(x_{1},x_{2}):=(1-2\theta\varepsilon)1_{\{x_{1}=x_{2}\}}a(x_{1})+2\theta\varepsilon a(x_{1})a(x_{2})\quad\mbox{with}\quad a(x)=\frac{1}{2}(1_{\{x=1\}}+1_{\{x=-1\}}).

Since the simple random walk has period
2
, we consider the pair of discrete webs
(W^{1},W^{2})
defined as before (
1.3
), but with paths starting only from the even lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}_{\rm even}=\{(x,t):x+t~\text{is even}\}
. Since
\sigma^{2}=\sum_{x}x^{2}a(x)=1
, we write
S_{\varepsilon}
for the scaling map
S_{\varepsilon,\sigma}
. We will show that
(S_{\varepsilon}W^{1},S_{\varepsilon}W^{2})
converges to a pair of sticky Brownian webs
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
with parameter
\theta
both in the sense of marking construction and in the sense of martingale characterisation, which then yields the equivalence of the two characterisations.

The convergence
(S_{\varepsilon}W^{1},S_{\varepsilon}W^{2})
to
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
with parameter
\theta
is a slight extension of
[
SSS14
, Theorem 6.15]
and
follows the same proof. Assume
\theta=1
to simplify notation. Theorem 6.15 of
[
SSS14
]
establishes the convergence of
(S_{\varepsilon}W^{1},S_{\varepsilon}N_{\varepsilon})
, the rescaling of a nearest neighbour discrete web
W^{1}
coupled to a nearest neighbour discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
, to a coupled Brownian web and net
({\cal W},{\cal N})
. The discrete
web
W^{1}
is obtained from
N_{\varepsilon}
by sampling at branching points, and the pair
({\cal W},{\cal N})
is constructed in two ways: either start with
{\cal W}
and
construct
{\cal N}
by a marking construction similar to the marking construction of the sticky webs, or start with
{\cal N}
and construct
{\cal W}
by sampling at the so-called relevant separation points of
{\cal N}
. The key step in the proof of
[
SSS14
, Theorem 6.15]
is that the relevant branching points of
N_{\varepsilon}
converges to the relevant separation points of
{\cal N}
, while the sampling distribution at relevant branching points of
N_{\varepsilon}
converges
to the sampling distribution at the relevant separation points of
{\cal N}
. As noted in (7.23) in
[
SSS14
]
, the convergence of
(S_{\varepsilon}W^{1},S_{\varepsilon}N_{\varepsilon})\Rightarrow({\cal W},{\cal N})
can be extended to include multiple sticky discrete webs sampled from the same discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
. In our case,
this means
(S_{\varepsilon}W^{1},S_{\varepsilon}W^{2},S_{\varepsilon}N_{\varepsilon})\Rightarrow({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2},{\cal N})
, where the discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
is generated from the pair of discrete webs
(W^{1},W^{2})
by switching between paths in
W^{1}
and
W^{2}
. This is equivalent to starting from a discrete net
N_{\varepsilon}
and then sampling the random walk increments in
(W^{1},W^{2})
with a sampling distribution such that at each branching point of
N_{\varepsilon}
,
W^{1}
samples one of the two outgoing arrows in
N_{\varepsilon}
with equal probability while
W^{2}
always samples the other arrow. The same sampling distribution can be defined at the relevant separation points of
{\cal N}
, which in the notation of
[
SSS14
, Lem. 6.16]
, corresponds to parameters
p_{--}=p_{++}=0
,
p_{-+}=p_{+-}=1/2
. The limit
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
obtained this way is defined exactly through the marking construction in
[
SSS14
, Theorem 3.5]
.

On the other hand, the convergence of
(S_{\varepsilon}W^{1},S_{\varepsilon}W^{2})
to a limit
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
whose law satisfies the conditions in Theorem
2.5
is a special case of our Theorem
3.1
. For the special nearest neighbour case, it also follows from results in the literature. Conditions (i) and (iii) in Theorem
2.5
follow by the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, while the key condition (ii)
on the convergence of a pair of nearest neighbour sticky random walks to sticky Brownian motions was proved in
[
SSS14
, Proposition A.6]

(the continuous time version was proved earlier in
[
HW09a
, Theorem 8.1]
. This concludes the proof sketch.

Proof sketch for Theorem
2.7
Assume
\theta=1
to simplify the notation. In
[
SS08
]
, the Brownian net
{\cal N}
is defined via hopping
between a pair of sticky Brownian webs
({\cal W}^{\rm l},{\cal W}^{\rm r})
with drifts
-1
and
+1
respectively, called the
left-right web
, where
paths in
{\cal W}^{\rm l}
cannot cross paths in
{\cal W}^{\rm r}
from left to right and vice versa. To show that
{\cal N}
constructed
via hopping from a pair of sticky Brownian webs
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2})
with parameter
\theta=1
is the same as the
{\cal N}
constructed via hopping from a
pair of left-right webs
({\cal W}^{\rm l},{\cal W}^{\rm r})
, it suffices to construct a coupling
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2},{\cal W}^{\rm l},{\cal W}^{\rm r},{\cal N})
that makes this equivalence obvious. In the discrete setting, such a coupling is given precisely by
(W^{1},W^{2},N_{\varepsilon})
defined around (
1.3
), and then define
W^{\rm l}_{\varepsilon}
(resp.
W^{\rm r}_{\varepsilon}
) by always choosing the leftmost (resp. rightmost) arrow coming out of each point in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\rm even}^{2}:=\{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}:x+t\mbox{ is even}\}
. Equivalently, we can start with
N_{\varepsilon}
and then sample the arrows followed by
(W^{1},W^{2},W^{\rm l}_{\varepsilon},W^{\rm r}_{\varepsilon})
at each branching point of
N_{\varepsilon}
, such that
W^{\rm l}
(resp.
W^{\rm r}
) always samples the leftmost (resp. rightmost) arrow,

W^{1}
samples one of the two outgoing arrows with equal probability while
W^{2}
samples the other arrow. The scaling limit then gives the desired coupling between
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2},{\cal W}^{\rm l},{\cal W}^{\rm r},{\cal N})
, where the same sampling distribution is applied independently at each relevant separation point of
{\cal N}
to recover the quadruple
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2},{\cal W}^{\rm l},{\cal W}^{\rm r})
. Such a convergence follows from the proof of Theorem 6.15 and (7.23) in
[
SSS14
]
, and the coupling
({\cal W}^{1},{\cal W}^{2},{\cal N})
was formulated in
[
SSS14
, Lemma 6.16]
, which can be easily extended to include also
({\cal W}^{\rm l},{\cal W}^{\rm r})
. Lastly, to see why hopping
among
{\cal W}^{1}\cup{\cal W}^{2}
and hopping among
{\cal W}^{\rm l}\cup{\cal W}^{\rm r}
will both lead to
{\cal N}
, we note that this is because in both cases, the hopping construction will exhaust both branches of paths coming out of each relevant separation point of
{\cal N}
, just as in
N_{\varepsilon}
, paths in
both
W^{1}\cup W^{2}
and
W^{\rm l}\cup W^{\rm r}
use up all arrows coming out of each branching point of
N_{\varepsilon}
. Using the finite graph representation of paths in the Brownian net (see
[
SSS14
, Section 6.2]
), which is the continuum analogue of the directed graph connecting relevant branching points of
N_{\varepsilon}
as introduced in Section
6.2
, every path
\pi\in{\cal N}
can be approximated by a hopping path
\pi_{\delta}
constructed from either
{\cal W}^{1}\cup{\cal W}^{2}
or
{\cal W}^{\rm l}\cup{\cal W}^{\rm r}
that visits the same set of
(\sigma_{\pi}+(n-1)\delta,\sigma_{\pi}+n\delta)
-relevant separation points as
\pi
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. In fact, it suffices to hop between paths at the relevant separation points of
{\cal N}
. As the time spacing
\delta\downarrow 0
, the approximating path
\pi_{\delta}
then converges to
\pi
thanks to the almost sure compactness of
{\cal N}
.

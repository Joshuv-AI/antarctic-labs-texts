Iterated invariance principle for slowly mixing dynamical systems
Matt Galton, Ian Melbourne

Abstract
We give sufficient Gordin-type criteria for the iterated (enhanced) weak invariance principle to hold for deterministic dynamical systems. Such an invariance principle is intrinsically related to the interpretation of stochastic integrals. We illustrate this with examples of deterministic fast-slow systems where our iterated invariance principle yields convergence to a stochastic differential equation.

Introduction

Recently, there has been a great deal of interest in homogenisation of deterministic systems with multiple timescales
[
3
,
4
,
8
,
9
,
16
,

,
24
,
26
,
27
,

; the aim is to prove convergence to a stochastic differential equation (SDE) as the separation of timescales increases.
The papers
[

38
]
considered some simplified situations where it sufficed that the fast dynamics satisfies the weak invariance principle (WIP).
In general, however, there are issues regarding the correct interpretation of stochastic integrals (Itô, Stratonovich, …) in the limiting SDE that are not resolved by the WIP. According to rough path theory
[
10
,
11
,
31
]
, it is necessary to consider an iterated (or enhanced) WIP in order to determine the stochastic integrals.
Kelly & Melbourne
[
23
,
24
]
applied rough path theory in the deterministic setting and reduced homogenisation theorems to establishing the iterated WIP and suitable moment control. The conditions on moments were optimized in
Chevyrev
et al.

[
3
,
4
]
.

The current paper is
based on results of the first author in his Ph. D. thesis
[
12
]
and aims
to extend the class of dynamical systems for which the iterated WIP holds. There is already a wealth of literature on the central limit theorem (CLT) and WIP for large classes of dynamical systems in both the dynamical systems and probability theory literature
[
5
,
13
,
18
,
19
,
22
,
29
,
36
,
46
,
47
]
. We slightly extend the class of systems for which the WIP holds, and greatly extend the class of systems for which the iterated WIP holds.

Our approach is based on Gordin’s method
[
13
]
for proving limit theorems via martingale approximation.
It is well-known that the
L^{2}
-criterion of Gordin
[
13
]
leads to the CLT and WIP, and it follows from
[
23
]
that the iterated WIP holds under this criterion (see
[
6
, Proposition 2.5]
).
Proving the same results under the
L^{1}
version of this criterion (hypotheses (
2.1
) and (

) in this paper) is more delicate.
The CLT was obtained by
[
14
]
and much later the WIP was obtained by
[
5
]
. The WIP in
[
5
]
is not quite in the right form for dynamical systems; in this paper we modify it so that it applies to dynamical systems by extending a time-reversal argument from
[
23
]
.

Previously there were no results on the iterated WIP under
L^{p}
Gordin criteria for
p<2
(except where there is additional Young tower structure, see
[
40
]
and
[
23
, Section 10]
). Addressing this is the main aim of this work.
In the noninvertible setting (Section
2
), we
prove the iterated WIP under the
L^{1}
Gordin criterion.
In the invertible setting (Section
3
), the validity of the iterated WIP under the
L^{1}
Gordin criterion remains unresolved. However, we prove the iterated WIP under a hybrid
L^{1}
–

The remainder of the paper is organised as follows.
In Section

In Section

2
Noninvertible setting

Let
(\Lambda,{\mathcal{F}},\mu)
be a probability space and

T:\Lambda\to\Lambda
be an ergodic measure-preserving map.
Let
P:L^{1}\to L^{1}
be the associated transfer operator (so

\int_{\Lambda}Pv\,w\,d\mu=\int_{\Lambda}v\,w\circ T\,d\mu

for
v\in L^{1}
,
w\in L^{\infty}
).
Also define the Koopman operator
Uv=v\circ T
.
We recall that

PU=I\quad\text{and}\quad UP={\mathbb{E}}(\cdot|T^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}).

Let
v\in L^{\infty}(\Lambda,{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with
\int_{\Lambda}v\,d\mu=0
.
Our underlying hypothesis throughout this section is the

L^{1}
Gordin criterion

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|P^{n}v|_{1}<\infty.

(2.1)

Under this hypothesis, it is well-known that the CLT and WIP hold.
We mention
[
29
,
5
]
for this and related results.
Our aim is to prove the iterated (or enhanced) version of the WIP.
Previously, this was proved in
[
23
, Section 4]
under the more restrictive assumption
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|P^{n}v|_{2}<\infty
.

Define the sequences of càdlàg processes

W_{n}\in D([0,\infty),{\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\qquad{\mathbb{W}}_{n}\in D([0,\infty),{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}),

by

W_{n}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{0\leq j\leq[nt]-1}v\circ T^{j},\qquad{\mathbb{W}}_{n}(t)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq[nt]-1}(v\circ T^{i})\otimes(v\circ T^{j}).

(2.2)

Theorem 2.1

Let
v\in L^{\infty}(\Lambda,{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with
\int_{\Lambda}v\,d\mu=0
,
and suppose that (
2.1
) holds. Then

(a)

The limit

\Sigma=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Lambda}W_{n}(1)\otimes W_{n}(1)\,d\mu\;\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}

exists.

(b)

\det\Sigma=0
if and only if there exists
c\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
nonzero
and
h\in L^{1}

such that
c\cdot v=h\circ T-h
.

(c)

Let
\nu
be any probability measure on
\Lambda
absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu
and regard

(W_{n},{\mathbb{W}}_{n})
as a sequence of processes in
D([0,\infty),{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d})
on the probability space
(\Lambda,\nu)
.

Then

(W_{n},{\mathbb{W}}_{n})\to_{w}(W,{\mathbb{W}})

as
n\to\infty
, where
W
is a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion with covariance
\Sigma
and

{\mathbb{W}}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}W\otimes dW+t\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\Lambda}v\otimes(v\circ T^{j})\,d\mu.

Remark 2.2

A standard calculation using (
2.1
) and Theorem
2.1
(a) yields the Green-Kubo formula

\Sigma=\int_{\Lambda}v\otimes v\,d\mu+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\Lambda}\big\{v\otimes(v\circ T^{j})+(v\circ T^{j})\otimes v\big\}\,d\mu.

Remark 2.3

The assumption that
T
is noninvertible is not assumed explicitly in Theorem
2.1
, but hypothesis (
2.1
) implies that
v\equiv 0
when
T
is invertible.

Remark 2.4

There are various possible extensions to Theorem
2.1
:

(1) Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Dedecker & Rio
[
5
]
consider unbounded functions
v\in L^{p}(\Lambda,{\mathbb{R}})
and prove that the ordinary WIP
W_{n}\to_{w}W
holds provided the
1
-norm in (
2.1
) is replaced by the
q
-norm.
(In fact it suffices that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P^{n}v
converges in
L^{q}
in
[
5
]
.)
A natural question is to prove the iterated WIP

under such assumptions. However, the main motivation for studying the iterated WIP is its fundamental role in the theory of fast-slow systems (considered further in Section
5
) where it is standard to consider bounded
v
.
Also, considering unbounded

would exacerbate the issues regarding
hypotheses (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) in the invertible setting.
Hence we restrict in this paper to the case of bounded

.

(2)
Dedecker & Rio
[
5
]

prove a nonergodic version of the WIP
following Volný
[
47
]
. It seems likely that a nonergodic version of the iterated WIP holds (paying due attention to the limit of
{\mathbb{W}}_{n}(1)-{\mathbb{M}}_{n}(1)
in the proof of Theorem
2.1
(c)). Again, ergodicity of
\mu
is a standard assumption in the motivating setting of fast-slow systems, and is assumed throughout this paper.

(3) A third possible extension is to consider limits of
(W_{n}(s),{\mathbb{W}}_{n}(t))
in
the space
D([0,\infty)\times[0,\infty),{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d})
. This seems to involve a nontrivial extension of
[

,
28
]
and hence is beyond the scope of this paper.

Hypothesis (

)
can be viewed as a slow mixing condition: we recall the following elementary result.

Proposition 2.5

Let

Suppose that there exists

\Big|\int_{\Lambda}v\,w\circ T^{n}\,d\mu\Big|\leq a_{n}|w|_{\infty}\quad\text{for all $w\in L^{\infty}(\Lambda,{\mathbb{R}})$, $n\geq 1$.}

In particular, hypothesis (

Proof.

See for example
[

.

2.1
Martingales

Let
v:\Lambda\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be an
L^{\infty}
observable with mean zero satisfying
hypothesis (
2.1
), and
define

\chi^{k}_{\ell}=\sum_{j=\ell}^{k}P^{j}v,\,\;1\leq\ell\leq k<\infty\qquad\text{and}\qquad\chi=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}P^{j}v.

It follows from our assumptions that
\chi^{k}_{\ell}\in L^{\infty}
for all
\ell\leq k
and
\chi\in L^{1}
. Moreover,
\chi^{k}_{1}\to\chi
in
L^{1}
as
k\to\infty
.
Following
[
34
]
, we write

v=m^{(k)}+\chi^{k}_{1}\circ T-\chi^{k}_{1}+P^{k}v,\,\;k\geq 1\qquad\text{and}\qquad v=m+\chi\circ T-\chi.

(2.3)

Since
PU=I
, it is easily verified from the definitions in (
2.3
) that
m,\,m^{(k)}\in\ker P
for all
k
.
It is immediate that

m^{(k)}\in L^{\infty}
for all
k
, that
m\in L^{1}
and that
m^{(k)}\to m
in
L^{1}
.
A somewhat surprising fact due originally to
[
25
]
, see also
[
5
,
15
,
29
,
32
,
46
]
, is that

m\in L^{2}
. We begin by recovering this fact using an elementary argument.

Lemma 2.6

m\in L^{2}
and
m^{(k)}\to m
in
L^{2}
as
k\to\infty
.

Proof.

Working componentwise, we can suppose without loss that
d=1
.
For
\ell<k
,

\displaystyle m^{(k)}-m^{(\ell)}

\displaystyle=(\chi_{1}^{k}-\chi_{1}^{k}\circ T-P^{k}v)-(\chi_{1}^{\ell}-\chi_{1}^{\ell}\circ T-P^{\ell}v)

\displaystyle=\chi_{\ell+1}^{k}-\chi_{\ell+1}^{k}\circ T+P^{\ell}v-P^{k}v=\chi_{\ell}^{k-1}-\chi_{\ell+1}^{k}\circ T.

(2.4)

Hence

\displaystyle|m^{(k)}-m^{(\ell)}|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Lambda}(m^{(k)}-m^{(\ell)})(\chi_{\ell}^{k-1}-\chi_{\ell+1}^{k}\circ T)\,d\mu=\int_{\Lambda}(m^{(k)}-m^{(\ell)})\chi_{\ell}^{k-1}\,d\mu,

where we used that
m^{(k)},\,m^{(\ell)}\in\ker P
.
Continuing and using (
2.1
) once more,

\displaystyle|m^{(k)}-m^{(\ell)}|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Lambda}(\chi_{\ell}^{k-1}-\chi_{\ell+1}^{k}\circ T)\chi_{\ell}^{k-1}\,d\mu=\int_{\Lambda}\{(\chi_{\ell}^{k-1})^{2}-\chi_{\ell+1}^{k}\,P\chi_{\ell}^{k-1}\}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\Lambda}\{(\chi_{\ell}^{k-1})^{2}-(\chi_{\ell+1}^{k})^{2}\}\,d\mu=\int_{\Lambda}(\chi_{\ell}^{k-1}-\chi_{\ell+1}^{k})(\chi_{\ell}^{k-1}+\chi_{\ell+1}^{k})\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\Lambda}(P^{\ell}v-P^{k}v)(\chi_{\ell}^{k-1}+\chi_{\ell+1}^{k})\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq(|P^{\ell}v|_{\infty}+|P^{k}v|_{\infty})(|\chi_{\ell}^{k-1}|_{1}+|\chi_{\ell+1}^{k}|_{1})\leq 4|v|_{\infty}\sum_{n=\ell}^{\infty}|P^{n}v|_{1}.

It follows from (
2.1
) that
m^{(k)}
is Cauchy in
L^{2}
.
By uniqueness of limits in
L^{1}
, the
L^{2}
limit of
m^{(k)}
coincides with
m
.
∎

Elements of
\ker P
enjoy the following martingale structure.

Proposition 2.7

Let
\phi\in L^{1}\cap\ker P
and
fix
n\geq 1
.
Define
{\mathcal{G}}_{j}=T^{-(n-j)}{\mathcal{F}}
,
1\leq j\leq n
.
Then
\{\phi\circ T^{n-j},\,{\mathcal{G}}_{j};\,1\leq j\leq n\}
is a sequence of
martingale differences. That is,
{\mathcal{G}}_{1}\subset\dots\subset{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
,

\phi\circ T^{n-j}
is
{\mathcal{G}}_{j}
-measurable for each
j
,
and
{\mathbb{E}}(\phi\circ T^{n-j}|{\mathcal{G}}_{j-1})=0
for each
j
.

Proof.

Since
T^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}\subset{\mathcal{F}}
, it follows that
{\mathcal{G}}_{j}\subset{\mathcal{G}}_{j+1}
.
Measurability of
\phi\circ T^{n-j}
with respect to
{\mathcal{G}}_{j}
is clear.
Finally,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(\phi\circ T^{n-j}|{\mathcal{G}}_{j-1})

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}(\phi|T^{-1}{\mathcal{F}})\circ T^{n-j}=(UP\phi)\circ T^{n-j}=0,

since
\phi\in\ker P
.
∎

2.2
Second moments

Throughout, we write
v_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}v\circ T^{j}
,

m_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}m\circ T^{j}
and so on for observables

v,\,m,\,\ldots
defined on
\Lambda
.

Corollary 2.8

Let
\phi\in L^{2}\cap\ker P
. Then

\big|\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n}|\phi_{\ell}|\big|_{2}\leq 4\sqrt{n}|\phi|_{2}

for all
n\geq 1
.
In particular,
\big|\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n}|(m-m^{(k)})_{\ell}|\big|_{2}\leq 4\sqrt{n}|m-m^{(k)}|_{2}

for all
k,n\geq 1
.

Proof.

Fix
n\geq 1
and let
X(j)=\phi\circ T^{n-j}
.
Since
\phi\in\ker P
, it follows that

|X(1)+\dots+X(n)|_{2}=\sqrt{n}|\phi|_{2}
.
By Proposition
2.7
,

\{X(j),\,{\mathcal{G}}_{j};\,1\leq j\leq n\}
is a sequence of
martingale differences.
Hence by Doob’s inequality,

\big|\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n}|X(1)+\dots+X(\ell)|\big|_{2}\leq 2|X(1)+\dots+X(n)|_{2}=2\sqrt{n}|\phi|_{2}.

Finally,
\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n}|\phi_{\ell}|\leq 2\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n}|X(1)+\dots+X(\ell)|
.
∎

Following
[
37
]
, we have a similar estimate for
v_{n}
.

Proposition 2.9

\big|\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n}|v_{\ell}|\big|_{2}^{2}\leq 128\,n|v|_{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|P^{j}v|_{1}
.

Proof.

Fix
n\geq 1
and
define the random variables
X(j)=v\circ T^{n-j}
,
1\leq j\leq n
which are adapted to the filtration
{\mathcal{G}}_{j}=T^{-(n-j)}{\mathcal{F}}
.
The version of Rio’s inequality
[
44
]
for
p=2

in
[
41
, Proposition 7]
states that

\big|\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n}|X(1)+\cdots+X(\ell)|\big|_{2}^{2}\leq 16\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{j,n}

where

b_{j,n}=\max_{1\leq j\leq u\leq n}|X(j)\sum_{k=j}^{u}{\mathbb{E}}(X(k)|{\mathcal{G}}_{j})|_{1}\leq|v|_{\infty}\max_{1\leq j\leq u\leq n}\Big|\sum_{k=j}^{u}{\mathbb{E}}(v\circ T^{n-k}|{\mathcal{G}}_{j})\Big|_{1}.

By Proposition
2.7
,

{\mathbb{E}}(m\circ T^{n-k}|{\mathcal{G}}_{j})=0
for all

k>j
. By (
2.3
),

\displaystyle\sum_{k=j}^{u}{\mathbb{E}}(v\circ T^{n-k}|{\mathcal{G}}_{j})

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\big(m\circ T^{n-j}+\chi\circ T^{n+1-j}-\chi\circ T^{n-u}|{\mathcal{G}}_{j}\big)

\displaystyle=v\circ T^{n-j}+\chi\circ T^{n-j}-{\mathbb{E}}\big(\chi\circ T^{n-u}|{\mathcal{G}}_{j}\big).

Hence

b_{j,n}\leq|v|_{\infty}(|v|_{1}+2|\chi|_{1})\leq 2|v|_{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|P^{j}v|_{1}

and so

\big|\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n}|X(1)+\cdots+X(\ell)|\big|_{2}^{2}\leq 32\,|v|_{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|P^{j}v|_{1}.

Finally,
\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n}|v_{\ell}|\leq 2\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n}|X(1)+\dots+X(\ell)|
.
∎

Lemma 2.10

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\big|\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n}|(v-m)_{\ell}|\big|_{2}=0
.

Proof.

By hypothesis (
2.1
) and Lemma
2.6
, for each
\epsilon>0
, there exists
k\geq 1
such that
\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}|P^{j}v|_{1}<\epsilon^{2}
and

|m-m^{(k)}|_{2}<\epsilon
.

Recall that
m,\,m^{(k)}\in\ker P
.
By Corollary
2.8
,

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\big|\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n}|(m-m^{(k)})_{\ell}|\big|_{2}<4\epsilon.

(2.5)

Next,
v=m^{(k)}+\chi_{1}^{k}\circ T-\chi_{1}^{k}+P^{k}v
, so

\displaystyle|(v-m^{(k)})_{n}|\leq 2|\chi_{1}^{k}|_{\infty}+|(P^{k}v)_{n}|\leq 2k|v|_{\infty}+|(P^{k}v)_{n}|.

Note that
P^{k}v
satisfies our underlying hypotheses, namely

P^{k}v\in L^{\infty}
,
\int_{\Lambda}P^{k}v\,d\mu=0
,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|P^{n}(P^{k}v)|_{1}<\infty
.
Hence by Proposition
2.9
,

\displaystyle\big|\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n}|(v-m^{(k)})_{\ell}|\big|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2k|v|_{\infty}+\big|\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n}|(P^{k}v)_{\ell}|\big|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2k|v|_{\infty}+\Big\{128\,n|P^{k}v|_{\infty}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|P^{j+k}v|_{1}\Big)\Big\}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 2k|v|_{\infty}+\Big\{128\,n|v|_{\infty}\Big(\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}|P^{j}v|_{1}\Big)\Big\}^{1/2}\ll k+\epsilon\sqrt{n}.

Combining this with (
2.5
),

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\big|\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n}|(v-m)_{\ell}|\big|_{2}\ll\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}k+\epsilon.

Hence
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\big|\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n}|(v-m)_{\ell}|\big|_{2}\ll\epsilon
and the result follows since
\epsilon
is arbitrary.
∎

Proof of
parts (a) and (b) of Theorem
2.1

Since
m\in\ker P
, it holds that

\int_{\Lambda}m_{n}\otimes m_{n}\,d\mu=n\int_{\Lambda}m\otimes m\,d\mu

for all
n
.
By Proposition
2.9
,

|v_{n}|_{2}\ll n^{1/2}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\Big|n^{-1}\int_{\Lambda}v_{n}\otimes v_{n}\,

\displaystyle d\mu-\int_{\Lambda}m\otimes m\,d\mu\Big|=n^{-1}\Big|\int_{\Lambda}(v_{n}\otimes v_{n}-m_{n}\otimes m_{n})\,d\mu\Big|

\displaystyle\leq n^{-1}(|v_{n}|_{2}+|m_{n}|_{2})|v_{n}-m_{n}|_{2}\ll n^{-1/2}|v_{n}-m_{n}|_{2}\to 0

by Lemma
2.10
.
This proves part (a) and shows in addition that

\Sigma=\int_{\Lambda}m\otimes m\,d\mu.

(2.6)

It follows that

c^{T}\Sigma c=\int_{\Lambda}(c\cdot m)^{2}\,d\mu
for all
c\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Next we prove part (b).
If
\det\Sigma=0
, then there exists
c\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
nonzero such that
\Sigma c=0
and hence

\int_{\Lambda}(c\cdot m)^{2}\,d\mu=c^{T}\Sigma c=0
,
so
c\cdot m=0
.
By (
2.3
),
c\cdot v=h\circ T-h
where
h=c\cdot\chi\in L^{1}
.

Conversely, suppose that
c\cdot v=h\circ T-h
for
c\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

nonzero and
h\in L^{1}
.
Then
c\cdot Pv=h-Ph
. Also,

Pv=\chi-P\chi
, hence
c\cdot\chi-h\in\ker(P-I)
.
By ergodicity,
c\cdot\chi=h+aI
for some
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Substituting into (
2.3
),

c\cdot v=c\cdot m+c\cdot\chi\circ T-c\cdot\chi=c\cdot m+h\circ T-h=c\cdot m+c\cdot v,

and so
c\cdot m=0
.
Hence
c^{T}\Sigma c=\int_{\Lambda}(c\cdot m)^{2}\,d\mu=0
.
It follows that
\det\Sigma=0
.
∎

2.3
Iterated WIP

In this subsection, we prove Theorem
2.1
(c). First, we prove the ordinary WIP.

Lemma 2.11

W_{n}\to_{w}W
in
D([0,\infty),{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
as
n\to\infty

on the probability space
(\Lambda,\mu)
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that
W_{n}\to_{w}W
in
D([0,K],{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
for each fixed integer
K\geq 1
.
Define

M_{n}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{0\leq j\leq[nt]-1}m\circ T^{j}
.
Recall that
m\in L^{2}\cap\ker P
.
By the pointwise ergodic theorem and (
2.6
),

n^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\{UP(m\otimes m)\}\circ T^{j}\to\int_{\Lambda}UP(m\otimes m)\,d\mu=\int_{\Lambda}m\otimes m\,d\mu=\Sigma\quad a.e.

It follows from
[
26
, Theorem A.1]
that

M_{n}\to_{w}W
in
D([0,K],{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Also,

3
Invertible setting

Let
(\Lambda,{\mathcal{F}},\mu)
be a probability space and

T:\Lambda\to\Lambda
be an invertible ergodic measure-preserving map.
We suppose that there is a sub-sigma-algebra
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}\subset{\mathcal{F}}
such that

T^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}_{0}\subset{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
.
Then
{\mathcal{F}}_{j}=T^{j}{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
defines a nondecreasing filtration
\{{\mathcal{F}}_{j}:j\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
.

Fix
d\geq 1
and let
v\in L^{\infty}(\Lambda,{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with
\int_{\Lambda}v\,d\mu=0
.
The
L^{1}
Gordin criterion now takes the form

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(v\circ T^{-n})|_{1}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(v\circ T^{n})-v\circ T^{n}|_{1}<\infty,

(3.1)

where
{\mathbb{E}}_{j}={\mathbb{E}}(\,\cdot\,|{\mathcal{F}}_{j})
.

Under hypotheses similar to (
3.1
), the CLT and WIP have been proved by various authors, including
[
5
,
42
,
46
]
.
In Subsection
3.1
, we recover the WIP under hypothesis (
3.1
) using techniques similar to those in Section
2
combined with ideas from
[
5
]
.

The iterated WIP holds under the
L^{2}
Gordin criterion

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(v\circ T^{-n})|_{2}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(v\circ T^{n})-v\circ T^{n}|_{2}<\infty

by
[
23
, Section 4]
(see
[
6
, Proposition 2.5]
).
An interesting open question is to prove the iterated WIP under the
L^{1}
criterion (
3.1
), but this seems currently out of reach.
In Subsection
3.2
, we prove the iterated WIP under a hybrid
L^{1}
–
L^{2}
Gordin criterion

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(v\circ T^{-n})|_{1}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(v\circ T^{n})-v\circ T^{n}|_{2}<\infty.

(3.2)

The same argument works if the roles of

|\;|_{1}
and
|\;|_{2}
are reversed in (
3.2
).

We note that the existence of a suitable sub-sigma-algebra
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
is very natural in the dynamical setting. Indeed it is often the case that
\Lambda
is covered by a collection
{\mathcal{W}}^{s}
of disjoint measurable sets, called “stable
leaves”, such that
TW^{s}_{x}\subset W^{s}_{Tx}
for all
x\in\Lambda
, where
W^{s}_{x}
is the stable leaf containing
x
.
In this situation, let
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
denote the sigma-algebra generated by
{\mathcal{W}}^{s}
. Then
T^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}_{0}\subset{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
.
The following result gives sufficient conditions for hypotheses (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) to hold.

Proposition 3.1

Let
p\geq 1
and let
v\in L^{\infty}(\Lambda,{\mathbb{R}})
with
\int_{\Lambda}v\,d\mu=0
.

(a)

Suppose that there exists
C>0
,
\epsilon>0
such that

\Big|\int_{\Lambda}v\,w\circ T^{n}\,d\mu\Big|\leq C|w|_{\infty}\,n^{-(p+\epsilon)}

for all
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
-measurable
w\in L^{\infty}(\Lambda,{\mathbb{R}})
,
n\geq 1
.
Then

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(v\circ T^{-n})|_{p}<\infty
.

(b)

Suppose that there exists
C>0
,
\epsilon>0
such that

\int_{\Lambda}\operatorname{diam}(v(T^{n}W^{s}))\,d\mu\leq C|w|_{\infty}\,n^{-(p+\epsilon)}

for all
n\geq 1
. Then

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(v\circ T^{n})-v\circ T^{n}|_{p}<\infty
.

Proof.

The arguments are standard. See for example
[
6
, Theorem 3.1]
.
∎

Proposition 3.9

Let
w\in L^{\infty}
with
{\mathbb{E}}_{0}w=0
. Then

\Big|\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n}|w_{\ell}|\big|_{2}^{2}\leq 96\,n|w|_{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(w\circ T^{j})-w\circ T^{j}|_{1}.

Proof.

Define
X(j)=w\circ T^{-j}
and
S_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}X(j)
.
Then

{\mathbb{E}}(S_{n}^{2})=\sum_{i,j=0}^{n-1}{\mathbb{E}}(w\circ T^{-i}\,w\circ T^{-j})=n{\mathbb{E}}(w^{2})+2\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(n-j){\mathbb{E}}(w\,w\circ T^{j}).

Also,

{\mathbb{E}}\big(w\,{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(w\circ T^{j})\big)={\mathbb{E}}\big({\mathbb{E}}_{0}(w\circ T^{j})\,{\mathbb{E}}_{0}w\big)=0

and so

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(S_{n}^{2})

\displaystyle=n{\mathbb{E}}(w^{2})+2\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(n-j){\mathbb{E}}\big(w\,(w\circ T^{j}-{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(w\circ T^{j}))\big)

\displaystyle\leq 2n|w|_{\infty}\Big(|w|_{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(w\circ T^{j})-w\circ T^{j}|_{1}\Big)

\displaystyle=2n|w|_{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(w\circ T^{j})-w\circ T^{j}|_{1}.

Next, define

Y_{i,j}={\mathbb{E}}_{j}(w\circ T^{-i}),\qquad Z_{p,j}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}Y_{i,j},\qquad Z_{j-1}^{*}=\max\{0,Z_{1,j},\dots,Z_{j-1,j}\}.

Note that
Y_{i,j}
is
{\mathcal{F}}_{j}
-measurable for all
i<j
, so
in particular
Z_{j-1}^{*}
is
{\mathcal{F}}_{j}
-measurable.
Hence
{\mathbb{E}}(X(j)Z_{j-1}^{*})={\mathbb{E}}(Z_{j-1}^{*}{\mathbb{E}}_{j}X(j))=0
.
It follows that

\sum_{j=1}^{n}|{\mathbb{E}}(X(j)S_{j-1}^{*})|=\sum_{j=1}^{n}|{\mathbb{E}}(X(j)(S_{j-1}^{*}-Z_{j-1}^{*}))|\leq|w|_{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\mathbb{E}}|S_{j-1}^{*}-Z_{j-1}^{*}|.

By Proposition
3.8
,

|S_{j-1}^{*}-Z_{j-1}^{*}|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}|X(i)-Y_{i,j}|=\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}|w\circ T^{-i}-({\mathbb{E}}_{j-i}w)\circ T^{-i}|

and hence

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}|{\mathbb{E}}(X(j)S_{j-1}^{*})|

\displaystyle\leq|w|_{\infty}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|w-{\mathbb{E}}_{j-i}w|_{1}=|w|_{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(n-j)|w-{\mathbb{E}}_{j}w|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq n|w|_{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|{\mathbb{E}}_{j}w-w|_{1}=n|w|_{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|({\mathbb{E}}_{0}(w\circ T^{j}))\circ T^{-j}-w|_{1}

\displaystyle=n|w|_{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(w\circ T^{j})-w\circ T^{j}|_{1}.

Combining this with the estimate for
{\mathbb{E}}S_{n}^{2}
it follows from
Lemma
3.7
that

{\mathbb{E}}({S_{n}^{*}}^{2})\leq 12n|w|_{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(w\circ T^{j})-w\circ T^{j}|_{1}.

The transformation
w\mapsto-w
sends
S_{n}^{*}\mapsto S_{n,*}=\max\{0,-S_{1},\dots,-S_{n}\}
.
Hence
{\mathbb{E}}(S_{n,*}^{2})\leq 12n|w|_{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(w\circ T^{j})-w\circ T^{j}|_{1}
, and so

\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n}|S_{\ell}|^{2}=\max\{{S_{n}^{*}}^{2},S_{n,*}^{2}\}\leq{S_{n}^{*}}^{2}+S_{n,*}^{2}\leq 24\,n|w|_{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(w\circ T^{j})-w\circ T^{j}|_{1}.

Finally,

w_{\ell}=(S_{n}-S_{n-\ell})\circ T^{n}
,
so
\big|\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n}|w_{\ell}|\big|_{2}\leq 2\big|\max_{1\leq\ell\leq n}|S_{\ell}|\big|_{2}
and the result follows.
∎

(\Lambda,\mu)
.

Finally, we consider the case where
\nu
is a general probability measure absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu
.
As in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, it suffices to establish (
2.9
)
for all
\epsilon>0
.
The estimates

|W_{n}(t)\circ T-W_{n}(t)|\leq 2n^{-1/2}|v|_{\infty},\qquad|{\mathbb{W}}_{n}(t)\circ T-{\mathbb{W}}_{n}(t)|\leq 2n^{-1}|v|_{\infty}\max_{1\leq k\leq nK}|v_{k}|

hold as before
for all
t\in[0,K]
.
Hence it suffices to show that
\big|\max_{1\leq k\leq nK}|v_{k}|\big|_{2}\ll n^{1/2}
.

Write
v=w+w^{\prime}
where
w={\mathbb{E}}_{0}v
,
w^{\prime}=v-{\mathbb{E}}_{0}v
.
By Propositions
3.5
and
3.9
,

\big|\max_{1\leq k\leq nK}|w_{k}|\big|_{2}\ll\Big(n|v|_{\infty}\sum_{j\geq 0}|{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(w\circ T^{-j})|_{1}\Big)^{1/2}=\Big(n|v|_{\infty}\sum_{j\geq 0}|{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(v\circ T^{-j})|_{1}\Big)^{1/2}

and

\displaystyle\big|\max_{1\leq k\leq nK}|w^{\prime}_{k}|\big|_{2}

\displaystyle\ll\Big(n|v|_{\infty}\sum_{j\geq 0}|{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(w^{\prime}\circ T^{j})-w^{\prime}\circ T^{j}|_{1}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\Big(n|v|_{\infty}\sum_{j\geq 0}|{\mathbb{E}}_{0}(v\circ T^{j})-v\circ T^{j}|_{1}\Big)^{1/2}.

Hence the required estimate for
\max_{1\leq k\leq nK}|v_{k}|
follows from (
3.1
).
∎

4
Examples

In this section, we consider examples consisting of time-one maps of nonuniformly expanding semiflows and
nonuniformly hyperbolic flows to which our theory applies and gives new results.

4.1
Noninvertible setting

We begin by revisiting nonuniformly expanding maps modelled by one-sided Young towers
[
51
]
.
Optimal results for the iterated WIP were obtained by
[
23
]
and we recover their result.
In particular,
[
51
]
proved results on decay of correlations; Theorem
2.1
applies whenever the decay of correlations is summable by Proposition
2.5
.
As described below, we are moreover able to treat time-one maps of nonuniformly expanding semiflows, significantly improving on existing results.

It is convenient to mention a specific family of dynamical systems.
Prototypical examples of nonuniformly expanding map are given by intermittent maps of Pomeau-Manneville type
[
43
]
.
For definiteness, we consider the example considered by
[
30
]
, namely

\displaystyle f:[0,1]\to[0,1],\qquad f(x)=\begin{cases}x(1+2^{\gamma}x^{\gamma})&x<\frac{1}{2}\\
2x-1&x>\frac{1}{2}\end{cases}.

(4.1)

Here
\gamma\in[0,1)
is a parameter and there is a unique absolutely continuous invariant probability measure
\mu_{0}
for each
\gamma
.

Let
v:[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be Hölder with
\int_{0}^{1}v\,d\mu_{0}=0
.
By
[
20
,
51
]
, there is a constant
C>0
such that
|\int_{0}^{1}v\,w\circ T^{m}\,d\mu_{0}|\leq Cn^{-(\gamma^{-1}-1)}|w|_{\infty}
for all
w\in L^{\infty}([0,1],{\mathbb{R}})
. By Proposition
2.5
,
hypothesis (
2.1
) holds for
\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
.
Hence we obtain the iterated WIP, Theorem
2.1
,
for all
\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
.
This recovers a result of
[
23
, Example 10.3]
and it is sharp since even the CLT fails for
\gamma\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
when
v(0)\neq 0
by
[
17
]
.

Now we consider suspension semiflows and their time-one maps to obtain new examples where the iterated WIP holds. Again we consider the specific example (
4.1
) for definiteness, but
f
could be replaced by any nonuniformly expanding map modelled by a Young tower. Let
h:[0,1]\to(0,\infty)
be a Hölder roof function and define the suspension semiflow

f_{t}:\Lambda\to\Lambda
where

\Lambda=\{(x,u)\in[0,1]\times{\mathbb{R}}:0\leq u\leq h(x)\}/\sim,\qquad(x,h(x))\sim(fx,0),

and
f_{t}(x,u)=(x,u+t)
computed modulo identifications.
The probability measure
\mu=(\mu_{0}\times\operatorname{Leb})/\int_{0}^{1}h\,d\mu_{0}

is
f_{t}
-invariant and ergodic.
At the level of the semiflow
f_{t}
, when
\gamma<\frac{1}{2}

the iterated WIP holds for Hölder mean zero observables
v
by
[
23
, Theorem 6.1]
.

Now consider the time-one map
T=f_{1}:\Lambda\to\Lambda
.
In general, even the CLT is not known for such maps. By
[
33
,
35
]
,
typically (under a non-approximate eigenfunction condition due to
[
7
]
)
T
has decay of correlations at the same rate as
f
for sufficiently smooth observables.
(We refer to
[
35
, Section 3]
for details regarding the class of observables
v
and
[
35
, Section 5]
for details regarding the word “typical”.)
A consequence
[
23
, Theorem 4.3 and Proposition 4.4]
is that the iterated WIP holds for
\gamma<\frac{1}{3}
.
Previously, the range
\gamma\in[\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2})
remained open.
But Proposition
2.5
again implies that the
L^{1}
Gordin criterion (
2.1
) holds for all
\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
.
Hence, by Theorem
2.1
, the iterated WIP holds in the optimal range
\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
.

4.2
Invertible setting

We begin by revisiting nonuniformly hyperbolic maps modelled by two-sided Young towers
[
50
,
51
]
.
Optimal results in this setting were obtained
by
[
40
]
(see also
[
23
, Section 10.2]
).
(Unlike in the noninvertible setting, Theorem
3.13
does not recover this result since the iterated WIP is not known under the
L^{1}
Gordin criterion (
3.1
).)

Examples of nonuniformly hyperbolic maps include intermittent solenoids
[
2
, Section 5]
and
[
40
, Example 4.2]
. These are invertible analogues of the intermittent maps in Subsection
4.1
and are obtained by adapting the classical Smale-Williams solenoid construction
[
45
,
49
]
. There is an invariant contracting stable foliation
{\mathcal{W}}^{s}
as in Proposition
3.1
and the dynamics modulo the stable leaves is given by an intermittent map. In particular, condition (a) in Proposition
3.1
is satisfied for
p<\gamma^{-1}-1
where
\gamma
is the parameter for the intermittent map.
The examples in
[
2
]
and some of the examples in
[
40
]
have exponential contraction along
stable leaves. For these examples and
v
Hölder, condition (b) in Proposition
3.1
is satisfied for all
p\in[1,\infty)
and hence Theorem
3.13
applies for all
\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
. The remaining examples in
[
40
]
have contraction along stable leaves which is as slow as the expansion of the underlying intermittent map, and condition (b) in Proposition
3.1
is satisfied for
p<\gamma^{-1}-1
; hence Theorem
3.13
applies for
\gamma<\frac{1}{3}
.

As in Subsection
4.1
, we consider intermittent solenoidal flows given by suspensions over intermittent solenoids. Optimal results on the iterated WIP for such flows follow by combining
[
40
]
and
[
23
, Theorem 6.1]
.
Again we focus on time-one maps of intermittent solenoid flows, restricting to typical flows and sufficiently smooth observables. Previous results on the iterated WIP in this context apply only for
\gamma<\frac{1}{3}
;
we considerably relax this restriction.
By the arguments in
[
6
]
,
the conditions of Proposition
3.1
hold for the same values of
p
as in the case of intermittent solenoids described above. Hence for the examples with exponential contraction along stable leaves, we obtain the iterated WIP for all
\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
. Exponential contraction can be relaxed to moderately fast polynomial contraction as discussed in
[
1

.
Explicit examples with
\gamma\in[\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2})
and condition (b) of Proposition
3.1
holding for

can be found in
[

]
.
For such examples, the iterated WIP follows from Theorem
3.13
; this is far beyond the scope of previous methods.

5
Application to homogenisation

Let
(\Lambda,{\mathcal{F}},\mu)
be a probability space and

T:\Lambda\to\Lambda
be an ergodic measure-preserving map.
Consider the fast-slow system

\displaystyle x^{(\epsilon)}_{n+1}

\displaystyle=x^{(\epsilon)}_{n}+\epsilon^{2}a(x^{(\epsilon)}_{n})+\epsilon b(x^{(\epsilon)}_{n})v(y_{n}),\quad x^{(\epsilon)}_{0}=\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

\displaystyle y_{n+1}

\displaystyle=Ty_{n},

where
a:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
b:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
and

v\in L^{\infty}(\Lambda,{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with
\int_{\Lambda}v\,d\mu=0
.

Define
\hat{x}_{\epsilon}(t)=x^{(\epsilon)}_{[t/\epsilon^{2}]}
and

W_{\epsilon}(t)=\epsilon\sum_{0\leq j\leq[t/\epsilon^{2}]-1}v(y_{j}),\qquad{\mathbb{W}}_{\epsilon}(t)=\epsilon^{2}\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq[t/\epsilon^{2}]-1}v(y_{i})\otimes v(y_{j}).

The aim is to prove homogenisation to a stochastic differential equation (SDE)
of the type

dX=\tilde{a}(X)\,dt+b(X)\,dW,\quad X(0)=\xi,

where
\tilde{a}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is to be determined; i.e. to show that

\hat{x}_{\epsilon}\to_{w}X
in
D[0,\infty),{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
as
\epsilon\to 0
.

In the special case where
a
and
b
are Lipschitz, and
b
satisfies an exactness condition of the form

b=(dh)^{-1}
for some
h:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, this problem was completely solved by
[
16
]
: It is necessary and sufficient that
v
satisfies the WIP. Hence the
L^{1}
Gordin criterion suffices by Theorems
2.1
and
3.2
.

When the exactness condition for
b
fails,
[
23
,
27
]
proved homogenisation for

a\in C^{1+}
and
b\in C^{2+}
under an

L^{4}
Gordin criterion on
v
(see
[
6
, Theorem 2.9]
).
Moreover,
\tilde{a}(X)=a(X)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma=1}^{d}E^{\gamma\beta}\partial_{\alpha}b^{\beta}(X)b^{\alpha\gamma}(X)

where
E
is the matrix in the iterated WIP.
1
1

1

There is a typo in
[
23
]
and subsequent papers; the matrix entry
E^{\beta\gamma}
should be replaced by

E^{\gamma\beta}
as written here.

In certain special cases, our results yield homogenisation theorems where the previous papers do not. One such example is the following:

Proposition 5.1

Let
d=2
and write
x=(x^{1},x^{2})
. Let

a(x)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
g(x)\end{array}\right),\qquad b(x)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&x^{1}\end{array}\right),

where
g:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is Lipschitz.
Suppose that
v
satisfies either the
L^{1}
Gordin criterion (
2.1
) in the noninvertible setting or the hybrid
L^{1}
–
L^{2}
Gordin criterion (
3.2
) in the invertible setting.
In particular,
{\mathbb{W}}_{\epsilon}^{12}(1)\to_{w}{\mathbb{W}}^{12}(1)+c
for some
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Then
\hat{x}_{\epsilon}\to_{w}X
in
D([0,\infty),{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
as
\epsilon\to 0
where
X
is the solution to the SDE

dX=\tilde{a}(X)\,dt+b(X)\,dW,\quad X(0)=\xi,

with
\tilde{a}(X)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
g(X)+c\end{array}\right)
.

Proof.

We have

\hat{x}_{\epsilon}^{1}(t)=\xi^{1}+W^{1}_{\epsilon}(t)
and

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{\epsilon}^{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\xi^{2}+\epsilon^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{[t\epsilon^{-2}]-1}g(x^{(\epsilon)}_{j})+\epsilon\sum_{j=0}^{[t\epsilon^{-2}]-1}(x^{(\epsilon)}_{j})^{1}v^{2}(y_{j})

\displaystyle=\xi^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}g(x_{\epsilon}(s))\,dt+\int_{0}^{t}\hat{x}_{\epsilon}^{1}(s)\,dW^{2}_{\epsilon}(s)+A_{\epsilon}(t)

\displaystyle=\xi^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}g(x_{\epsilon}(s))\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}(\xi^{1}+W^{1}_{\epsilon}(s))\,dW_{\epsilon}^{2}(s)+A_{\epsilon}(t)

\displaystyle=\xi^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}g(x_{\epsilon}(s))\,ds+\xi^{1}W_{\epsilon}^{2}(t)+{\mathbb{W}}_{\epsilon}^{12}(t)+A_{\epsilon}(t)

where

\displaystyle|A_{\epsilon}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{2}|g|_{\infty}+\epsilon|v|_{\infty}\max_{0\leq j\leq\epsilon^{-2}t}|(x^{(\epsilon)}_{j})^{1}|.

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{2}|g|_{\infty}+\epsilon|v|_{\infty}\xi^{1}+\epsilon|v|_{\infty}\max_{0\leq s\leq t}|W_{\epsilon}^{1}(s)|.

In other words,

\hat{x}_{\epsilon}(t)=\xi+\int_{0}^{t}a(x_{\epsilon}(s))\,ds+U_{\epsilon}(t)

where

U_{\epsilon}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}W^{1}_{\epsilon}\\
\xi^{1}W^{2}_{\epsilon}+{\mathbb{W}}^{12}_{\epsilon}+A_{\epsilon}\end{array}\right).

The resulting solution map
\hat{x}_{\epsilon}={\mathcal{G}}(U_{\epsilon})
is continuous on
C([0,K],{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
for all
K>0
since
a
is Lipschitz.
By the iterated WIP,
U_{\epsilon}\to_{w}U
where

U=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}W^{1}\\
\xi^{1}W^{2}+{\mathbb{W}}^{12}\end{array}\right),\qquad{\mathbb{W}}^{12}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}W^{1}\,dW^{2}+tE^{12},\qquad E^{12}=c,

so the continuous mapping theorem shows that
\hat{x}_{\epsilon}\to_{w}{\mathcal{G}}(U)=X

where

dX=a(X)\,dt+dU,\quad X(0)=\xi.

But
U^{1}=W^{1}
and

U^{2}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}(\xi^{1}+W^{1})\,dW^{2}+tE^{12}=\int_{0}^{t}X^{1}\,dW^{2}+tE^{12}=\int_{0}^{t}X^{1}\,dW^{2}+tc,

yielding

\tilde{a}(X)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
g(X)+c\end{array}\right)
as required.
∎

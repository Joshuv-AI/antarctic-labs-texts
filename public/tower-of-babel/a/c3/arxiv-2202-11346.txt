ACC for Foliated Log Canonical Thresholds
Yen-An Chen

Abstract
. It is known that the set of log canonical thresholds (lcts) on any varieties with fixed dimension satisfies the ascending chain condition. Inspired by the foliated minimal model program, it is intriguing to study the foliated version of lcts and ask whether they have the similar property. We give an affirmative answer in the case of surfaces and threefolds.

Primary 32S65, Secondary 32M25, 14B05, 14J99.

Introduction

Singularities play an important role in studying higher dimensional birational geometry.
In order to measure the singularities, we would like to find some invariants which behave well under birational maps.
For example, the minimal log discrepancies (mlds for short) arise naturally in the study of birational geometry.
In
[
Sho04
]
, Shokurov showed that two following conjectures on minimal log discrepancies implies the termination of flips, which is one of main open problems in the minimal model program.

Conjecture 0.1

(
[
Sho88
, Problem 5]
)
.

Fix a positive integer
n
and a subset
I\subset[0,1]
satisfying the descending chain condition.
Then the set

\textnormal{MLD}_{n}(I):=\{\textnormal{mld}(x,X,B)|\,(X\ni x,B)\textnormal{ is log canonial},\dim X=n,B\in I\}

satisfies the ascending chain condition.
Here
\textnormal{mld}(x,X,B)
is the minimal log discrepancy of the pair
(X,B)
with center
x
and
B\in I
means that the coefficients of
B
belong to
I
.

Conjecture 0.2

(
[
Amb99
, Conjecture 2.4]
)
.

Let
X
be a quasi-projective variety.
Then the function
a:|X|\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}
, which sends a closed point
x\in X
to the minimal log discrepancy of
(X,B)
with center at
x
, is lower semi-continuous.

Conjecture
0.1
is known to hold for surface pairs (
[
Ale93
]
), toric varieties (
[
Bor97
]
), toric pairs (
[
Amb06
]
), exceptional singularities (
[
HLS19
]
), and recently terminal threefolds (
[
HLL22
]
); while conjecture
0.2
holds for
n\leq 3
(
[
Ale93
]
and
[
Amb99
]
) and
X
is toric (
[
Amb99
]
).

Another interesting and important invariant is the log canonical threshold, which plays a vital role in the inductive approach for higher dimensional geometry.
The set of log canonical thresholds also satisfies the ascending chain condition by the following theorem.

Theorem 0.3

(
[
HMX14
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Fix
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
I\subset[0,1]
, and a subset
J
of positive real numbers.
If
I
and
J
both satisfy the descending chain condition (DCC), then the set

\textnormal{LCT}_{n}(I,J)=\{\textnormal{lct}(X,\Delta;M)|\,\dim X=n,\,\Delta\in I,\,\textnormal{and }M\in J\}

satisfies the ascending chain condition (ACC).

It was originally conjectured in all dimensions and proved for
n=2
by Shokurov in
[
Sho92
]
.
And then Alexeev proved it for
n=3
in
[
Ale93
]
.
Later, it was proved for smooth varieties in
[
dFEM10
]
and for full generality in
[
HMX14
]
.

Inspired by the work on the minimal model program on foliated surfaces and threefolds (see, for example,
[
Bru15
]
,
[
McQ08
]
,
[
Spi20
]
,
[
CS21
]
,
[
spicer2022local
]
,
[
CS20
]
, and the references therein), we are interested in the foliated version of the theorem above.
Precisely, we define the foliated log canonical threshold as follows.

Definition 0.4
.

Let
({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
be a log canonical pair on a variety
X
.

(1)

Given an
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier divisor
M\geq 0
, we define the
log canonical threshold
of
M
with respect to
({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
to be

\textnormal{lct}({\mathscr{F}},\Delta;M):=\sup\{t\in{\mathbb{R}}|\,({\mathscr{F}},\Delta+tM)\textnormal{ is log canonical}\}.

(See Definition
1.5
for log canonicity.)

(2)

Let
I\subset[0,1]
and
J\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
.
We put
\textnormal{LCT}_{n,r}(I,J)
to be the set of all real numbers
\textnormal{lct}({\mathscr{F}},\Delta;M)
where
({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
is a log canonical pair such that
{\mathscr{F}}
is a foliation of rank
r
on a variety
X
of dimension
n
and the coefficients of
\Delta
(resp.
M
) belong to
I
(resp.
J
).

In this paper, we show the following theorem.

Theorem 0.5
.

Fix
I\subset[0,1]
, a subset
J
of positive real numbers, and two positive integers
r
and
n
with
r<n
.
If
I
and
J
both satisfy the descending chain condition, then the set
\textnormal{LCT}_{n,r}(I,J)
satisfies the ascending chain condition provided that
n\leq 3
.

With some reformulations as in
[
HMX14
]
, we also show the following theorem.

Theorem 0.6
.

Fix two positive integers
r
and
n
with
r<n\leq 3
and a set
I\subset[0,1]
, which satisfies the descending chain condition.
Then there exists a finite subset
I_{0}\subset I
with the following property:
If
(X,{\mathscr{F}},D)
is a triple such that

(1)

X
is a variety of dimension
n
,

(2)

{\mathscr{F}}
is a foliation of rank
r
,

(3)

({\mathscr{F}},D)
is log canonical,

(4)

the coefficients of
D
belong to
I
, and

(5)

there is a log canonical center
Z
which is contained in every component of
D
,

then the coefficients of
D
belong to
I_{0}
.

The proof has two steps.
We first extract the log canonical center and then apply adjunction to the foliated triple restricting onto some irreducible extracted divisor.
The main difficulty is that, in general, there is little control on the singularities when applying adjunction.
However, the model that extracts the log canonical center has mild singularities, which gives us more control on singularities.
Note that both steps rely on the foliated minimal model program and the resolution of foliation singularities, which are widely open when the dimension is at least
4
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In section 1, we fix some notations and recall some facts.
In section 2, we show Theorem
0.6
in the surface case.
In the section 3 and 4, Theorem
0.6
for threefolds is proved in the cases when the rank is 2 and 1, respectively.
The last section is to show the set of log canonical thresholds satisfies the ascending chain condition (Theorem
0.5
) from our finiteness theorem
0.6
.

Preliminaries

We will work over
{\mathbb{C}}

1.1.
Foliations

Definition 1.1
.

A
foliation

{\mathscr{F}}
on a normal variety
X
is a coherent subsheaf
{\mathscr{F}}
of the tangent sheaf
T_{X}
such that

(1)

it is closed under the Lie bracket and

(2)

{\mathscr{F}}
is saturated, that is, the quotient
T_{X}/{\mathscr{F}}
is torsion free.

The
rank
of
{\mathscr{F}}
is its rank as a sheaf.
The
co-rank
of
{\mathscr{F}}
is defined as
\dim X-\textnormal{rank}{\mathscr{F}}
.
We define the
canonical divisor

K_{\mathscr{F}}
for the foliation
{\mathscr{F}}
as
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-K_{\mathscr{F}})=\det({\mathscr{F}})
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a foliation of rank
r
on a normal variety
X
.
We have a morphism
\Omega_{X}^{[1]}\longrightarrow{\mathscr{F}}^{*}
.
Taking the double dual of
r
-th wedge product, we get a morphism

\textstyle{\varphi:\Omega_{X}^{[r]}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(K_{\mathscr{F}}),}

which yields a map

\textstyle{\phi:\Omega_{X}^{[r]}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-K_{\mathscr{F}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}}

and we define the
singular locus
of
{\mathscr{F}}
as the co-support of the image of
\phi
.

Definition 1.2

(Rational transform of foliations)
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a foliation on a normal variety
X
.

(1)

Given
f:Y\dashrightarrow X
be a dominant rational map.
Let
U
be an open subset of
X
such that
f|_{V}:V\longrightarrow U
is an isomorphism where
V:=f^{-1}(U)
.
Note that
{\mathscr{F}}|_{U}\subset T_{U}\cong T_{V}
.
By
[
Har77
, Exercise II.5.15]
, there is a coherent subsheaf
{\mathscr{G}}
of
T_{Y}
such that
{\mathscr{G}}|_{V}={\mathscr{F}}|_{U}\subset T_{V}
.
Then the
pullback foliation

f^{*}{\mathscr{F}}
is defined to be the saturation of
{\mathscr{G}}
.
Note that
{\mathscr{G}}
is closed under the Lie bracket and, by
[
HL21
, Lemma 1.8]
, this definition is well-defined.

(2)

Given
g:X\dashrightarrow Z
be a birational map, then we define the
pushforward foliation

g_{*}{\mathscr{F}}
as
(g^{-1})^{*}{\mathscr{F}}
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a foliation on a normal variety
X
.
Given a subvariety
W
of
X
.
Let
U=X\setminus\big(\textnormal{Sing}(X)\cup\textnormal{Sing}({\mathscr{F}})\cup\textnormal{Sing}(W)\big)
.

(1)

W
is
tangent
to
{\mathscr{F}}
if
T_{W}|_{U}\longrightarrow T_{X}|_{U}
factors through
{\mathscr{F}}|_{U}
.
Otherwise we say
W
is
transverse
to
{\mathscr{F}}
.

(2)

W
is
invariant
if
{\mathscr{F}}|_{U}\longrightarrow T_{X}|_{U}
factors through
T_{W}|_{U}
.

Definition 1.4

(Foliated pair)
.

A
foliated pair

({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
on a normal variety
X
consists of a foliation
{\mathscr{F}}
and an effective
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor
\Delta
on
X
such that
K_{\mathscr{F}}+\Delta
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Cartier.

Definition 1.5

(
[
McQ08
, Definition I.1.5]
)
.

Let
({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
be a foliated pair on a normal variety
X
and
f:Y\longrightarrow X
be a proper birational morphism.
For any divisor
E
on
Y
, we define the
discrepancy
of
({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
along
E
to be
a(E,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)=\textnormal{ord}_{E}(K_{f^{*}{\mathscr{F}}}-f^{*}(K_{\mathscr{F}}+\Delta))
.

We say
(X,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
is
terminal
(resp.
canonical
) if
a(E,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)>0
(resp.
\geq 0
) for every exceptional divisor
E
over
X
and

(X,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
is
log terminal
(resp.
log canonical
) if
a(E,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)>-\varepsilon(E)
(resp.
\geq-\varepsilon(E)
) for every divisor
E
over
X
where
\varepsilon(E)
is defined to be
0
if
E
is
f^{*}{\mathscr{F}}
-invariant, and
1
otherwise.

Definition 1.6
.

Given a foliated pair
({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
on
X
.
A subvariety
Z
of
X
is called a
log canonical center
for
({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
if
({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
is log canonical at the generic point of
Z
and there is a divisor
E
over
X
with discrepancy
a(E,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)=-\varepsilon(E)
and with center
Z
on
X
.

Theorem 1.7

(
[
Miy87
]
,
[
SB92
, Theorem 9.0.2]
, or
[
BM16
]
)
.

Let
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
be a normal foliated variety of dimension
n
and
H_{1}
,
\ldots
,
H_{n-1}
, and

be ample divisors.
Let
C
be a general intersection of element
D_{i}\in|m_{i}H_{i}|
for some

.
If

, then through a general point of

1.2.
Indices on foliated surfaces

Most definitions in this subsection follow from
[
Bru15
]
with some generalizations.

Let
p\in\textnormal{Sing}({\mathscr{F}})\setminus\textnormal{Sing}(X)
. That is,
p
is a smooth point on
X
but a singular point of the foliation.
Let
v
be the vector field around
p
generating
{\mathscr{F}}
.
Since
p\in\textnormal{Sing}({\mathscr{F}})
, we have
v(p)=0
.
Then we can consider the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}
of
(\textnormal{D}v)|_{p}
, which do not depend on the choice of
v
.

Definition 1.8
.

If one of the eigenvalues is non-zero, say
\lambda_{2}
, then we say
p
is
semi-reduced
and define the eigenvalue of the foliation
{\mathscr{F}}
at
p
to be

\lambda:=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}.

For
\lambda\neq 0
, this definition is unique up to reciprocal
\lambda\sim\frac{1}{\lambda}
.

If
\lambda=0
, then
p
is called a
saddle-node
; otherwise, we say
p
is
non-degenerate
.
If
\lambda\not\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{+}
, then
p
is called a
reduced
singularity of
{\mathscr{F}}
.

Reduced singularities arise naturally.
Indeed, blowing up a smooth foliation point will introduce a reduced singularity with
\lambda=-1
.

1.2.1.
Non-invariant curves

We first consider the non-invariant curves and define the tangency order for them.

Definition 1.9
.

Let
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
be a foliated surface and
C
be a
non-invariant
reduced curve.
Let
p\in C\setminus\textnormal{Sing}(X)
and
v
be the vector field generating
{\mathscr{F}}
around
p
.
Let
f
be the local defining function of
C
at
p
.
We define the
tangency order
of
{\mathscr{F}}
along
C
at
p
to be

\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{F}},C,p):=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}}{\langle f,v(f)\rangle}.

Note that
\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)\geq 0
and is independent of the choices of
v
and
f
.
Moreover, if
{\mathscr{F}}
is transverse to
C
at
p
, then
\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)=0
.
Therefore, if
C
is compact, then we can define

\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{F}},C):=\sum_{p\in C}\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{F}},C,p).

Theorem 1.10

((Adjunction for non-invariant divisors)
[
Bru97
]
,
[
Bru15
, Proposition 2.2]
)
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a foliation on a smooth projective surface
X
.
Let
C
be a non-invariant irreducible curve on
X
and
C^{\nu}
be the normalization of
C
.
Then there is an effective divisor
\Delta
on
C^{\nu}
such that
(K_{\mathscr{F}}+C)|_{C^{\nu}}=\Delta
with
\textnormal{deg}\,\Delta=\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{F}},C)
.

Corollary 1.11
.

Let
C
be a non-invariant curve on a foliated surface
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
.
If
C
is contained in the smooth locus of
X
, then we have
(K_{\mathscr{F}}+C)\cdot C\geq 0
.

1.2.2.
Invariant curves

Now we study the invariant curves.

Definition 1.12
.

Let
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
be a foliated surface and
C
be an invariant curve.
Let
p\in C\setminus\textnormal{Sing}(X)
and
\omega
be a
1
-form generating
{\mathscr{F}}
around
p
.
If
C
is an invariant curve and
f
is the local defining function of
C
at
p
, then we can write

g\omega=h\textnormal{d}f+f\eta

where
g
,
h
are holomorphic functions,
\eta
is a holomorphic
1
-form, and
h
,
f
are relatively prime functions.

We define the index
\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)
to be the vanishing order of
\frac{h}{g}|_{C}
at
p
.
This definition is independent of the choices of
f
,
g
,
h
,
\omega
, and
\eta
.
(For a reference, see
[
Bru15
, page 15 in Chapter 2 and page 27 in Chapter 3]
.)

Note that if
p\not\in\textnormal{Sing}({\mathscr{F}})
, then
\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)=0=\textnormal{CS}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)
.
Therefore, if
C
is compact, then we can define

\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},C):=\sum_{p\in C}\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)

where the sums are taken over only finitely many points.

Theorem 1.13

((Adjunction for invariant divisors)
[
Bru97
]
,
[
Bru15
, Proposition 2.3]
)
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a foliation on a smooth projective surface
X
.
Let
C
be an invariant irreducible curve on
X
and
C^{\nu}
be its normalization.
Then there is an effective divisor
\Delta
on
C^{\nu}
such that
K_{\mathscr{F}}|_{C^{\nu}}=K_{C^{\nu}}+\Delta
with
\deg\Delta=\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},C)+\deg\textnormal{Diff}_{C}(0)
where
\textnormal{Diff}_{C}(0)
is the different with
(K_{X}+C)|_{C^{\nu}}=K_{C^{\nu}}+\textnormal{Diff}_{C}(0)
.
In particular, we have
K_{\mathscr{F}}\cdot C=\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},C)+2p_{a}(C)-2
where
p_{a}(C)
is the arithmetic genus of
C
.

Lemma 1.14
.

Given a foliated surface
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
.
Suppose there is a morphism
f:X\longrightarrow C
where
C
is a curve.
Let
F
be the general fiber of
f
.

(1)

If
K_{\mathscr{F}}\cdot F<0
, then
F
is a smooth invariant rational curve with
K_{\mathscr{F}}\cdot F=-2
.

(2)

If
K_{X}\cdot F<0
, then
F
is a smooth rational curve with
K_{X}\cdot F=-2
.

Proof
.

Since
F
is a general fiber, we may assume that
F
has no singularity of
X
and
{\mathscr{F}}
.

(1)

If
F
is not invariant, then by adjunction for non-invariant divisors (Theorem
1.10
), we have

0>K_{\mathscr{F}}\cdot F=(K_{\mathscr{F}}+F)\cdot F=\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{F}},F)\geq 0,

which is impossible.
Thus,
F
is invariant.
Now by adjunction for invariant divisors (Theorem
1.13
), we have

0>K_{\mathscr{F}}\cdot F=\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{F}},F)+2p_{a}(F)-2=2p_{a}(F)-2.

Therefore,
p_{a}(F)=0
and
K_{\mathscr{F}}\cdot F=-2
.

(2)

By adjunction, we have

0>K_{X}\cdot F=(K_{X}+F)\cdot F=2p_{a}(F)-2.

Therefore,
p_{a}(F)=0
and
K_{X}\cdot F=-2
.

∎

1.3.
DCC sets

Suppose
I\subset[0,1]
.
We define

I_{+}:=\{0\}\cup\Bigg\{j\,:\,j=\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}i_{p}\textnormal{ for some }i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell}\in I\Bigg\}

and

\textnormal{D}(I):=\bigg\{a\leq 1\,:\,a=\frac{m-1+f}{m},m\in{\mathbb{N}},f\in I_{+}\bigg\}.

We say a set
I
of real numbers satisfies the
descending chain condition
(DCC) if it does not contain any infinite strictly decreasing sequence.

Also, if
I\subset[0,1]
satisfies the DCC, then the minimum among
I\setminus\{0\}
exists, which is denoted by
I_{\textnormal{min}}
.

Proposition 1.15
.

We have the following properties:

(1)

If
I\subset[0,1]
satisfies the DCC, then so do
I_{+}
and
\textnormal{D}(I)
.

(2)

\textnormal{D}(\textnormal{D}(I))=\textnormal{D}(I)
.

Proof
.

This is straightforward. For a reference, see
[
MP04
, Lemma 4.4]
. ∎

Lemma 1.16
.

Fix a DCC set
I
and a positive real number
\alpha
.
Then there is a finite subset
I_{0}\subset I
depending only on
I
and
\alpha
such that if
\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}k_{j}i_{j}=\alpha
for some
k_{j}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
i_{j}\in I\setminus\{0\}
, and
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then all
i_{j}\in I_{0}
.

Proof
.

Note that

\alpha=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}k_{j}i_{j}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}k_{j}I_{\textnormal{min}}.

Thus the sum
\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}k_{j}
is bounded, and so is the length
\ell
.
Without loss of generality, we may fix
\ell
and
k_{1}
,
k_{2}
,
\ldots
,
k_{\ell}
.
Now we write

\alpha-k_{1}i_{1}=\sum_{j=2}^{\ell}k_{j}i_{j}

where the left hand side belongs to a ACC set while the right hand side belongs to a DCC set.
The only set which satisfies both the ACC and the DCC is the finite set.
Thus we have only finitely many possible
i_{1}
.
Similarly, there are only finitely many possible
i_{j}
’s.
Hence, there is a finite subset
I_{0}\subset I
such that all
i_{j}
’s belong to
I_{0}
provided that
\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}k_{j}i_{j}=\alpha
.
∎

2.
Foliations of rank one on surfaces

In this section, we show Theorem
2.4
, which is Theorem
0.6
when
r=1
and
n=2
.
We first recall the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
C=\bigcup_{i}C_{i}
be a set of proper curves on a smooth surface.
Assume that the intersection matrix
(C_{i}\cdot C_{j})_{i,j}
is negative definite.
Let
A=\sum a_{i}C_{i}
be an
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear combination of the curves
C_{i}
’s.
If
A\cdot C_{j}\geq 0
for all
j
, then
a_{i}\leq 0
for all
i
.
If, moreover,
C
is connected, then either
a_{i}=0
for all
i
or
a_{i}<0
for all
i
.

In particular, if
C
is connected and
A\cdot C_{j}>0
for some
j
, then
a_{i}<0
for all
i
.

Proof
.

For a reference, see
[
KM98
, Lemma 3.41]
. ∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Given a foliated pair
({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
of rank 1 of a surface.
Suppose
({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
is log canonical at a point
p
.
If
p\in\textnormal{Supp}(\Delta)
, then
{\mathscr{F}}
is terminal at
p
.

Proof
.

Since
({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
is log canonical at
p
, we also have
{\mathscr{F}}
is log canonical at
p
.
Let
E=\cup_{i}E_{i}
be the exceptional divisor for the minimal resolution of
{\mathscr{F}}
at
p
.
Note that

(K_{\mathscr{F}}+\Delta)\cdot E_{i}\geq K_{\mathscr{F}}\cdot E_{i}

for all
i
and it is strict for some
i
because
p\in\textnormal{Supp}(\Delta)
.
By Lemma
2.1
, the (foliated) discrepancy
a(E_{i},{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
is strictly less than
a(E_{i},{\mathscr{F}})
for all
i
.

Suppose
{\mathscr{F}}
is not terminal at
p
, then, by
[
Che21
, Lemma 4.2]
, there is an irreducible exceptional divisor

such that

.
But this implies that

is log canonical at

.
Therefore,

is terminal at

.
∎

In addition to the existence of a foliated dlt modification on surfaces given in

CS21
, Theorem 8.1]
, the following theorem also gives a foliated dlt modification with more explicit properties.
Note that for the case when

, the foliated dlt modification around

, Theorem 0.1]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
({\mathscr{F}},\Delta,p)
be a germ of foliated pair with
p\in\textnormal{Supp}(\Delta)
.
Suppose

is a log canonical center of
({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
.
Then we have a foliated dlt modification
\pi:(Y,{\mathscr{G}},\Delta_{Y})\longrightarrow(X,{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
around

such that the following hold:

(1)

The graph of the
\pi
-exceptional divisors
E=\cup_{i}E_{i}
is a chain with
a(E_{i},{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)=0
.

(2)

K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Delta_{Y}=\pi^{*}(K_{\mathscr{F}}+\Delta)
where
\Delta_{Y}=\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta
is the proper transform of
\Delta
.

(3)

There is only one non-reduced foliation singularity

on
E
.
Precisely,

is terminal for
{\mathscr{G}}
.
Moreover

is only on the irreducible component
E_{1}
which is one end of the chain

.

(4)

\Delta_{Y}
only meets
E_{1}
.

Proof
.

Let
\pi:(Z,{\mathscr{H}})\longrightarrow(X,{\mathscr{F}})
be the minimal resolution of
(X,{\mathscr{F}})
at
p
.
By Lemma
2.2
and
[
Che21
, Theorem 0.1]
,
p
is a cyclic quotient singularity and the dual graph of
\pi
-exceptional divisors
F=\cup_{i}F_{i}
is an
{\mathscr{H}}
-chain with
\textnormal{Z}({\mathscr{H}},F_{1})=1
.
Note that

K_{\mathscr{H}}+\Delta_{Z}=\pi^{*}(K_{\mathscr{F}}+\Delta)+\sum_{i}a(F_{i},{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)F_{i}

where
\Delta_{Z}=\pi_{*}^{-1}\Delta
and
a(F_{i},{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)\geq 0
for all

since
F_{i}
’s are all invariant.

Claim
.

If
a(F_{i},{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)=0
for some
i
, then
a(F_{j},{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)=0
and
\Delta_{Z}\cdot F_{j}=0
for all
j>i
.

Proof

(Claim)
.

If
a(F_{i_{0}},{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)=0
for some

, then we have
(K_{\mathscr{H}}+\Delta_{Z})\cdot F_{j}\geq 0
for all
j>i_{0}
.
Applying Lemma
2.1
on the set of exceptional divisors
F_{j}
’s where

, we have

and hence
a(F_{j},{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)=0
for all

.
Since

, we have

\Delta_{Z}\cdot F_{j}=(K_{\mathscr{H}}+\Delta_{Z})\cdot F_{j}=\Bigg(\sum_{i<i_{0}}a(F_{i},{\mathscr{F}},\Delta)F_{i}\Bigg)\cdot F_{j}=0

for
j>i
.
This completes the proof of the Claim.

Suppose

for some

.
Then let

for all

.
Thus, contracting

,
\ldots
,

gives a desired foliated dlt modification.

Now suppose

for all

.
Since

is a log canonical singularity, there is a higher model
\phi:(W,{\mathscr{L}})\longrightarrow(Z,{\mathscr{H}})
consisting of a sequence of blowups with
\phi
-exceptional
divisor

.
We may assume that

is the exceptional divisor for the last blowup and

.
Note that

and

for

.
By
[

, Lemma 4.1(d)]
, the dual graph of

-exceptional divisors is a chain.
We relabel all exceptional divisors

’s and the proper transform of

’s as

\widetilde{E}_{i}
’s so that
\widetilde{E}_{i}\cdot\widetilde{E}_{i+1}=1

.
Let

be the one such that

Recall

.
Using

Similar as above, contracting all

∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Fix a set
I\subset[0,1]
, which satisfies the descending chain condition.
Then there exists a finite subset
I_{0}\subset I
with the following property:
If
(X,{\mathscr{F}},D)
is a triple such that

(1)

X
is a variety of dimension
2
,

(2)

{\mathscr{F}}
is a foliation of rank
1
,

(3)

({\mathscr{F}},D)
is log canonical,

(4)

the coefficients of
D
belong to
I
, and

(5)

there is a log canonical center
Z
which is contained in every component of
D
,

then the coefficients of
D
belong to
I_{0}
.

Proof
.

If
Z
is a divisor, then
D=Z
with coefficient one.
Then we require
1\in I_{0}
.

If
Z
is a point, by Theorem
2.3
, we have a foliated dlt modification

\pi:(Y,{\mathscr{G}},D_{Y})\longrightarrow(X,{\mathscr{F}},D)

with
K_{\mathscr{G}}+D_{Y}=\pi^{*}(K_{\mathscr{F}}+D)
where
{\mathscr{G}}
is the pullback foliation and
D_{Y}
is the proper transform of

.
Let
E_{1}
be an irreducible component of the exceptional divisor which meets

.
Then we have

0=\pi^{*}(K_{\mathscr{F}}+D)\cdot E_{1}=(K_{\mathscr{G}}+D_{Y})\cdot E_{1}=\frac{-1}{m}+\sum_{j}\frac{k_{j}d_{j}}{m}

where

is the index of the only singularity on

and

for all

.
Here

is the multiple of

when the corresponding irreducible component does not pass through the only singularity on

.
Thus we have

.
Since all

’s belong to the DCC set

, by Lemma
1.16
, we have a finite subset

such that

for all

.
∎

Using some ideas in
[
Ale93
, Theorem 5.3]
, we also show the similar result for the numerically trivial pairs.

Theorem 2.5

(ACC for numerically trivial pairs)
.

Fix a DCC set
I
.
Then there is a finite subset
I_{0}\subset I
with the following property:
If
(X,{\mathscr{F}},B)
is a triple such that

(1)

X
is a surface,

(2)

{\mathscr{F}}
is a foliation of rank one on
X
,

(3)

({\mathscr{F}},B)
is log canonical,

(4)

K_{{\mathscr{F}}}+B
is numerically trivial, and

(5)

the coefficients of
B
belong to
I
,

then all coefficients of
B
belong to
I_{0}
.

Proof
.

Given a triple
(X,{\mathscr{F}},B)
satisfying (1)-(5).
We write
B=\sum_{j}b_{j}B_{j}
where
b_{j}\in I
and
B_{j}
’s are irreducible components.

We may assume that
{\mathscr{F}}
has either reduced singularities or terminal singularities by taking a foliated dlt modification. (See Theorem
2.3
.)
Note that
K_{\mathscr{F}}\equiv-B
is not pseudo-effective.
We would like to run a
K_{\mathscr{F}}
-MMP.
Then, either
\rho(X)=1
or there is a
K_{\mathscr{F}}
-negative extremal ray that does not contract
B_{1}
since
B_{1}
is not invariant.

Assume
X
has Picard number
1
.
Since the general curves are not numerically trivial, we have
-K_{{\mathscr{F}}}\cdot C=B\cdot C>0
for a general curve
C
.
Thus
-K_{{\mathscr{F}}}
is ample because
\rho(X)=1
.
By Theorem
1.7
,
X
is uniruled.
Hence
K_{X}
is not pseudo-effective and therefore
-K_{X}
is ample.
Moreover, the
(-K_{X})
-slope of
{\mathscr{F}}
is

\mu_{(-K_{X})}({\mathscr{F}})=(-K_{\mathscr{F}})\cdot(-K_{X})>0.

By
[
CP19
, Theorem 1.1]
or
[
Ou17
, Proposition 2.2]
,
{\mathscr{F}}
is algebraically integrable, that is, general leaves are algebraic.
Note
{\mathscr{F}}
has only reduced or terminal singularities through which there are at most two invariant curves passing.
So the algebraic integrability of
{\mathscr{F}}
gives a fibration
\pi:X\longrightarrow C
onto a curve
C
and the general fibers are the leaves of
{\mathscr{F}}
.
This shows that
\rho(X)\geq 2
, which gives a contradiction.

Now we suppose
\rho(X)\geq 2
.
Then we run a
K_{\mathscr{F}}
-MMP to contract a
K_{\mathscr{F}}
-negative extremal ray.
If the contraction is birational, then we notice that the conditions (1)-(5) are preserved.
Moreover, the property that the foliation has only finitely many invariant curves passing through each point is also preserved.
Besides, each irreducible component
B_{i}
of
B
is not contracted since they are non-invariant.
Thus we can repeat the process mentioned above.
Since we have shown that Picard number cannot be
1
, after finitely many times, the contraction gives a fibration onto a curve.

Let
F
be a general fiber of the fibration.
Since
F
is invariant, by adjunction for invariant divisors (Theorem
1.13
), we have
K_{{\mathscr{F}}}\cdot F=-2
and thus

2=-K_{\mathscr{F}}\cdot F=B\cdot F=\sum_{j}b_{j}(B_{j}\cdot F).

Note that all
B_{j}\cdot F>0
for all
j
since
B_{j}
’s are non-invariant and
F
is invariant.
Therefore, by Lemma
1.16
, there is a finite subset
I_{0}\subset I
such that
b_{j}\in I_{0}
for all
j
.
∎

3.

In this section, we show Theorem

3.1.
Foliated dlt modification

Most definitions in this subsection follow from
[
CS21
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

We say
z_{1}
,
\ldots
,
z_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
satisfy the
non-resonant condition
if, for any non-negative integers
a_{1}
,
\ldots
,
a_{\ell}
with
\sum_{i}a_{i}z_{i}=0
, we have
a_{i}=0
for all
i=1
,
\ldots
,
\ell
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a co-rank one foliation on a smooth variety
X
of dimension
n
.
We say a point
p\in X
is a
simple singularity
for
{\mathscr{F}}
if, in formal coordinates
x_{1}
,
\ldots
,
x_{n}
around
p
, conormal sheaf
N_{\mathscr{F}}^{*}:=(T_{X}/{\mathscr{F}})^{*}
is generated by a
1
-form which is in one of the following two forms, for some
1\leq r\leq n
:

(1)

There are
\lambda_{1}
,
\ldots
,
\lambda_{r}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, which satisfy the non-resonant condition, such that

\omega=x_{1}\cdots x_{r}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}\frac{\textnormal{d}x_{i}}{x_{i}}.

(2)

There is an integer
k\leq r
such that

\omega=x_{1}\cdots x_{r}\cdot\Bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}\frac{\textnormal{d}x_{i}}{x_{i}}+\varphi(x_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdots x_{k}^{p_{k}})\sum_{i=2}^{r}\lambda_{i}\frac{\textnormal{d}x_{i}}{x_{i}}\Bigg)

where
p_{1}
,
\ldots
,
p_{k}
are positive integers without a common factor,
\varphi(s)
is a formal power series which is not a unit, and
\lambda_{2}
,
\ldots
,
\lambda_{r}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
satisfy the non-resonant condition.

Definition 3.3
.

Given a foliated pair
({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
of co-rank one on a normal variety
X
.
We say
({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
is
foliated log smooth
if the following hold:

(1)

(X,\Delta)
is log smooth,

(2)

{\mathscr{F}}
has simple singularities, and

(3)

if
S
is the support of the non-
{\mathscr{F}}
-invariant components of
\Delta
,
p
is a closed point, and
\Sigma_{1}
,
\ldots
,
\Sigma_{k}
are (possibly formal)

-invariant divisors passing through

, then
S\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\Sigma_{i}
is a normal crossing divisor at
p
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a (co-)rank one foliation on a surface
X
and
C
be a non-invariant curve on
X
.
Suppose
({\mathscr{F}},C)
is foliated log smooth, then
(K_{\mathscr{F}}+C)\cdot C=0
.

Proof
.

Note that
X
is smooth since
({\mathscr{F}},C)
is foliated log smooth.
By adjunction for non-invariant divisors (Theorem
1.10
), we have
(K_{\mathscr{F}}+C)\cdot C=\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{F}},C)\geq 0
.
Thus it suffices to show that tangency order of
\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)=0
for any

.

If
p
is a singularity for
{\mathscr{F}}
, then there are two formal invariant divisors
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
passing through

.
Hence

is not a normal crossing divisor at

, which contradicts the last requirement that
({\mathscr{F}},S)
is foliated log smooth.

Therefore,
p
is a smooth point for
{\mathscr{F}}
.
Let
\Sigma
be the formal invariant divisor passing through
p
.
Since

is a normal crossing divisor at

, we have
\textnormal{tang}({\mathscr{F}},C,p)=0
.
∎

Definition 3.5
.

Given a foliated pair
({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
of co-rank one on a normal variety

.
A

of

is a birational morphism

such that

(1)

\textnormal{Exc}(\pi)
is a divisor and

(2)

is the pullback foliation on

\pi
-exceptional divisors.

When

]
.
For the threefolds

]
.

Definition 3.7

We say a foliated pair

(1)

(2)

there is a foliated log resolution

of

which only extracts divisor

.

.

Let

be a foliated pair of co-rank one on a normal projective variety

.

for some effective

-exceptional

3.2.
Adjunction

The following Proposition slightly generalizes

Let
{\mathscr{F}}

be a prime divisor, and

be a subset of

Suppose

-divisor with coefficients belonging to the set

-Cartier and

is non-dicritical (see
[

Then there exists an effective

-divisor

\textnormal{D}(I)\cup{\mathbb{N}}

Proof
.

The existence of
\Theta
follows from
[

, Lemma 3.18]
.

(1)

When
\varepsilon(S)=1
, we have

is log canonical by
[

, Lemma 3.18]
.
To show other statements in this case, we may reduce to the case when

Since

is log canonical,
{\mathscr{F}}
has at worst terminal singularities along

Claim
.

(K_{\mathscr{F}}+S)\cdot S=0
.

Proof
.

We consider
\pi:Z\longrightarrow X
a foliated log resolution of
X
along
S
with the pullback foliation

on

and

We may write

Note that each component of

By

Thus, we have

If
{\mathscr{F}}
is smooth along

, then we have

and the coefficients of

belong to

.
Since

is log canonical, the coefficients of

In general, we consider

along

We write

Claim
.

({\mathscr{F}}_{W},S_{W})

Proof

Suppose

Hence, by Proposition

So

(2)

When

Note that

(b)

C^{2}<0
because
C
is an irreducible component of a singular fiber of
\varphi_{R}
, and

(c)

(K_{E_{1}^{\nu}}+\Theta^{\prime}-b_{1}\Theta_{1})\cdot C=(-b_{1}\Theta_{1}-\sum(n_{i}-1)C_{i})\cdot C<0
.

So we see that
{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}[C]
is a
(K_{E_{1}^{\nu}}+\Theta^{\prime}-b_{1}\Theta_{1})
-negative extremal ray with
C\cdot\Theta_{1}>0
, which contradicts our assumption.
Thus, we complete the proof of the Claim.

Nevertheless, we note that

K_{E_{1}^{\nu}}+\Theta^{\prime}-b_{1}\Theta_{1}\equiv-b_{1}\Theta_{1}-\sum(n_{i}-1)C_{i}

is not pseudo-effective.
So the outcome of
(K_{E_{1}^{\nu}}+\Theta^{\prime}-b_{1})
-MMP must be a Mori fiber space, which is impossible.
This complete the proof of the Theorem.
∎

Adjunction

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a foliation of rank one with at worst simple singularities on a normal threefold
X
,
S
be an invariant prime divisor on

(X,S)

I
be a subset of
[0,1]
containing

Suppose
({\mathscr{F}},\Delta)
is log canonical where
\Delta
is an effective (non-invariant) divisor whose coefficients belong to

Let
\nu:S^{\nu}\longrightarrow S
be its normalization.
We write
(K_{\mathscr{F}}+\Delta)|_{S^{\nu}}=K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Theta
where
{\mathscr{G}}
is the pullback foliation on
S^{\nu}
and
\Theta
is an effective
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor.
Then the coefficients of
\Theta
belong to
(I_{+}\cap[0,1])\cup\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof
.

The existence of

[
CS20
, Proposition 2.16]
.

be any irreducible component of

.

is terminal along

, then by
[

, Lemma 2.12]
,

is non-invariant.

Moreover, any irreducible component of

does not interset

along

.

4.3.
ACC for LCT when
n=3
and
r=1

Theorem 4.7
.

Fix a set
I\subset[0,1]
, which satisfies the descending chain condition.
Then there exists a finite subset
I_{0}\subset I
with the following property:
If
(X,{\mathscr{F}},D)
is a triple such that

(1)

X
is a variety of dimension
3
,

(2)

{\mathscr{F}}
is a foliation of rank
1
,

(3)

({\mathscr{F}},D)
is log canonical,

(4)

the coefficients of
D
belong to
I
, and

(5)

there is a log canonical center
Z
which is contained in every component of
D
,

then the coefficients of
D
belong to
I_{0}
.

Proof
.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
Z
is maximal with respect to the inclusion.
If
Z
is a divisor, then
Z=D
and thus we require
1\in I_{0}
.

Otherwise, by the resolution of singularities (Theorem
4.4
), we have a birational morphism
p:X^{\prime}\longrightarrow X
such that the pullback foliation
{\mathscr{F}}^{\prime}:=p^{*}{\mathscr{F}}
has only simple singularities.
Let
D^{\prime}:=p_{*}^{-1}D
.
Then we may write

K_{{\mathscr{F}}^{\prime}}+D^{\prime}+F^{\prime}=p^{*}(K_{\mathscr{F}}+D)+F^{\prime\prime}

where
F^{\prime}
and
F^{\prime\prime}
are effective
p
-exceptional divisors without common components.
After possibly taking a higher resolution, we may assume that
({\mathscr{F}}^{\prime},D^{\prime}+F^{\prime})
is log canonical and that
(X^{\prime},D^{\prime}+F)
is log canonical where
F
is the sum of all
p
-exceptional divisors.
By
[
CS20
, Proposition 8.1]
, we may run a
(K_{{\mathscr{F}}^{\prime}}+D^{\prime}+F^{\prime})
-MMP over
X
.
Let
\varphi:X^{\prime}\dashrightarrow Y
be the output of this MMP with
\pi:Y\longrightarrow X
.
Let
{\mathscr{G}}:=\varphi_{*}{\mathscr{F}}^{\prime}
,
D_{Y}:=\varphi_{*}D^{\prime}
, and
E^{\prime}:=\varphi_{*}F^{\prime}
.
Then we have, by
[
CS20
, Proposition 8.1 and Lemma 8.2]
, that

(1)

({\mathscr{G}},\Gamma:=D_{Y}+\sum\varepsilon(E_{j})E_{j})
is log canonical;

(2)

we have
K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Gamma=\pi^{*}(K_{{\mathscr{F}}}+D)
;

(3)

{\mathscr{G}}
has only simple singularities; and

(4)

(Y,E_{\textnormal{inv}})
is log canonical where
E_{\textnormal{inv}}
is the sum of all
{\mathscr{G}}
-invariant exceptional divisors.

Let
D_{1}
be one of irreducible components of
D
and
d_{1}
be the coefficient of
D_{1}
in
D
.
Then the proper transform
\pi_{*}^{-1}D_{1}
has coefficient
d_{1}
in
\Gamma
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
E_{1}
intersects
\pi_{*}^{-1}D_{1}
.
Let
\Theta_{1}
be an irreducible component of
\pi_{*}^{-1}D_{1}\cap E_{1}
.
By the assumption that
Z
is contained in
D_{1}
, we have
\Theta_{1}
dominates
Z
.

We have four cases based on the invariance of
E_{1}
and the dimension of
Z
:

4.3.1.
E_{1}
is non-invariant

By adjunction
[
CS20
, Proposition 2.19]
, we have

(K_{\mathscr{G}}+E_{1})|_{E_{1}^{\nu}}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\Xi

where
\Xi
is an effective
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor.

(1)

If
Z
, which is the center of
E_{1}
, is a point, then
K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Gamma
is numerically trivial and thus we have an effective
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor
\Xi+(\Gamma-E_{1})|_{E_{1}^{\nu}}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
.
Therefore,

\Xi=0=(\Gamma-E_{1})|_{E_{1}^{\nu}},

which shows that all irreducible components of
\Gamma-E_{1}
do not intersect
E_{1}
.
This is impossible since
\pi_{*}^{-1}D_{1}
is in the support of
\Gamma-E_{1}
.

(2)

If
Z
is a curve, then
\psi:=\pi|_{E_{1}^{\nu}}:E_{1}^{\nu}\longrightarrow Z
is a fibration.
Let
\Theta_{1}
be an irreducible component of
\pi_{*}^{-1}D_{1}\cap E_{1}
.
Since
\Theta_{1}
dominates
Z
, we have
\Theta_{1}\cdot F>0
where
F
is a general fiber of
\psi
.
Hence,

0=\pi^{*}(K_{\mathscr{F}}+D)\cdot F=(\Xi+(\Gamma-E_{1})|_{E_{1}^{\nu}})\cdot F\geq\Theta_{1}\cdot F>0,

which is impossible.

4.3.2.
E_{1}
is invariant

By Theorem
4.6
, we have

(K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Gamma)|_{E_{1}^{\nu}}=K_{\mathscr{H}}+\Theta

where
{\mathscr{H}}
is the pullback foliation on
E_{1}^{\nu}
and
\Theta
is an effective divisor whose coefficients belong to
\frac{1}{2}I_{+}
.
Let
\Theta=\sum b_{i}\Theta_{i}
where
\Theta_{1}
be an irreducible component of
\pi_{*}^{-1}D_{1}\cap E_{1}
which dominates
Z
.
Note that
2b_{1}=kd_{1}+f
where
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
f\in I_{+}
.

(1)

If
Z
is a curve, then
\psi:=\pi|_{E_{1}^{\nu}}:E_{1}^{\nu}\longrightarrow Z
is a fibration.
By the assumption that
Z
is contained in
D_{1}
, we have
\Theta_{1}\cdot F>0
where
F
is a general fiber of
\psi
.
Note that

(4)

0=\pi^{*}(K_{\mathscr{F}}+D)\cdot F=(K_{\mathscr{H}}+\Theta)\cdot F

and thus
-K_{\mathscr{H}}\cdot F=\Theta\cdot F\geq\Theta_{1}\cdot F>0
.
By Lemma
1.14
, we have
F
is invariant and
K_{\mathscr{H}}\cdot F=-2
.
Therefore, we have

4=-2K_{\mathscr{H}}\cdot F=2\Theta\cdot F=\sum 2b_{i}(\Theta_{i}\cdot F)

where
\Theta_{i}\cdot F\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
and
\Theta_{1}\cdot F>0
.
Replacing
2b_{1}
by
kd_{1}+f
, there is a finite subset
I_{1}\subset I
, by Lemma
1.16
, such that
d_{1}\in I_{1}
since
\Theta_{1}\cdot F>0
.

(2)

Now suppose
Z
is a point.
Then
K_{\mathscr{H}}+\Theta\equiv 0
.

Claim
.

{\mathscr{H}}
is algebraically integrable with rationally connected leaves.

Proof
.

By
[
CS21
, Theorem 8.1]
,
({\mathscr{H}},\Theta)
admits a foliated dlt modification
\pi:S\longrightarrow E_{1}^{\nu}
such that

(a)

S
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial,

(b)

S
has at worst klt singularities,

(c)

the pullback foliation
{\mathscr{H}}_{S}
on
S
is non-dicritical, that is the exceptional curves over
S
are all invariant,

(d)

({\mathscr{H}}_{S},\pi_{*}^{-1}\Theta+\sum\varepsilon(E_{i})E_{i})

(e)

K_{{\mathscr{H}}_{S}}+B:=K_{{\mathscr{H}}_{S}}+\pi_{*}^{-1}\Theta+\sum\varepsilon(E_{i})E_{i}+F=\pi^{*}(K_{\mathscr{H}}+\Theta)
where
F
is a
\pi
-exceptional
{\mathbb{Q}}
-divisor on
S
.

If
S
has Picard number
1
, then
-K_{{\mathscr{H}}_{S}}\cdot C=B\cdot C>0
for a general curve
C
.
Thus,
-K_{\mathscr{F}}
is ample since
\rho(S)=1
.
By Theorem
1.7
,
S
is uniruled.
Hence
K_{S}
is not pseudo-effective and therefore
-K_{S}
is ample.
Moreover, we note that the
(-K_{S})
-slope of

is

\mu_{(-K_{S})}({\mathscr{H}}_{S})=(-K_{{\mathscr{H}}_{S}})\cdot(-K_{S})>0.

By
[
CP19
, Theorem 1.1]
or
[
Ou17
, Proposition 2.2]
,
{\mathscr{H}}_{S}
is algebraically integrable with rationally connected leaves, and thus so is
{\mathscr{H}}
.

Now suppose
\rho(S)\geq 2
.
We will run
{\mathscr{H}}_{S}
-MMP on
S
.
Note that
K_{{\mathscr{H}}_{S}}\equiv-B
is not pseudo-effective.
Then we will end up with a Mori fiber space, either a fibration onto a curve or a surface with Picard number 1.
In both cases, we see that
{\mathscr{H}}_{S}
is algebraically integrable with rationally connected leaves.
Therefore,
{\mathscr{H}}
is also algebraically integrable with rationally connected leaves.

Claim
.

Any dicritical singularity for
{\mathscr{H}}
is a log canonical center for
{\mathscr{G}}
.

Proof
.

Let
p
be a dicritical singularity for
{\mathscr{H}}
.
Assume
p
is not a log canonical center for
{\mathscr{G}}
.
Since
{\mathscr{G}}
has simple singularities which are log canonical,
{\mathscr{G}}
is terminal at
p
.
Note that
K_{\mathscr{G}}\cdot L=-\Gamma\cdot L<0
for a general leaf
L
of
{\mathscr{H}}
.
By
[
CS20
, Proposition 3.3 and Theorem 3.2]
,
{\mathscr{G}}
is a fibration locally around
p
, which contradicts that
p
is a dicritical singularity for
{\mathscr{H}}
.
Therefore,
p
is a log canonical center for
{\mathscr{G}}
. This completes the proof of the Claim.

Let
L
be a general leaf of
{\mathscr{H}}
.
Then by
[
CS20
, Proposition 2.16]
, we have

0=\pi^{*}(K_{\mathscr{F}}+D)\cdot L=(K_{\mathscr{G}}+\Gamma)\cdot L=\deg(K_{L^{\nu}}+\Delta)

. If there is a dicritical singularity for

, then

.
Thus,
\deg K_{L^{\nu}}\leq 1

.
On the other hand, if there is no dicritical singularity for

for some curve

.

\deg\Delta=-\deg K_{L^{\nu}}=2

is general and
\psi
has rationally connected fibers.

Let
\nu:E_{1}^{\nu}\rightarrow E_{1}^{\nu}\hookrightarrow Y
be the composition of the normalization and the inclusion.
If
\Theta_{1}
is contained in a fiber of

, then
{\mathscr{F}}
is terminal along

.

Note that the coefficients of
\Delta
belong to
\textnormal{D}(\textnormal{D}(I))\cup\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{N}}

By Lemma

, there is a finite subset

.

, such that

.

Proof of Theorem on ACC for LCT

Proof

(Theorem
0.5
)
.

Given a non-decreasing sequence
\{c^{(\ell)}\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
in
\textnormal{LCT}_{n,r}(I,J)
.
We will drop the superscript if no confusion arises.
We may assume that both 0 and 1 belong to
I
and
J
.

For each
c
in the sequence, we may find a foliation
{\mathscr{F}}
of rank
r
on a variety
X
of dimension
n
, a divisor
\Delta
whose coefficients belong to
I
, and an
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier divisor

whose coefficients belong to
J
such that
c=\sup\{t\in{\mathbb{R}}|\,({\mathscr{F}},\Delta+tM)\textnormal{ is log canonical}\}
.

Let
D=\Delta+cM
and
K=\{a+bc\leq 1|\,a\in I\textnormal{ and }b\in J\}
.
Note that
K
satisfies the descending chain condition and
({\mathscr{F}},D)
is log canonical with coefficients of
D
in
K
.
Since
c
is the log canonical threshold, there is a log canonical center
Z
contained in the support of

.
Possibly throwing away components of

which do not contain

, then we may assume every component of
D
contains
Z
.
By Theorem
0.6
, there is a finite subset
K_{0}\subset K
such that the coefficients of
D
belong to
K_{0}
.
This shows that the sequence
c^{(\ell)}
eventually terminates.
∎

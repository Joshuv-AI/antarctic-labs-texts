Matrix factorizations and intermediate Jacobians of cubic threefolds Thanks: The first author was supported by the EPSRC New Horizons Grant EP/V047299/1.
Christian Böhning, Hans-Christian Graf von Bothmer, Lukas Buhr

Abstract
. Results due to Druel and Beauville show that the blowup of the intermediate Jacobian of a smooth cubic threefold X X in the Fano surface of lines can be identified with a moduli space of semistable sheaves of Chern classes c 1 = 0 , c 2 = 2 , c 3 = 0 c_{1}=0,c_{2}=2,c_{3}=0 on X X . Here we further identify this space with a space of matrix factorizations. This has the advantage that this description naturally generalizes to singular and even reducible cubic threefolds. In this way, given a degeneration of X X to a reducible cubic threefold X 0 X_{0} , we obtain an associated degeneration of the above moduli spaces of semistable sheaves.

The main results we will show in this article are the following.

a)

3.
The connection to the intermediate Jacobian

In this section we fix a smooth cubic threefold
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
and a cubic polynomial
F\in\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\dots,x_{4}]
defining
X
. We denote by
J_{X}
the intermediate Jacobian, and consider

\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}_{X}:=\{([M],[F])\,\mid\,V(F)=X\}\subset\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}

with its reduced structure.

Given
([M],[F])\in\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}_{X}
, we consider the image
\mathscr{F}_{M}
of the map given by the Pfaffian adjoint matrix:

6\mathscr{O}_{X}(-1)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{adj}^{\mathrm{Pf}}(M)}6\mathscr{O}_{X}(1).

Here
\mathrm{adj}^{\mathrm{Pf}}(M)
is the skew matrix whose
(i,j)
-entry for
i<j
is

(-1)^{i+j}\mathrm{Pf}(M_{ij})

where
M_{ij}
is the matrix obtained from
M
by deleting rows and columns with indices
i,j
.

We now want to prove that there is an isomorphism from
\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}_{X}
to
Druel’s compactification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{X}
of the moduli space of stable rank 2 vector bundles with
c_{1}=0
and
c_{2}=2
on
X
sending
([M],[F])
to
\mathscr{F}_{M}
.
For this we recall some properties of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{X}
from
[
Dru00
]
following
[
Beau02
]
.

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{X}
is smooth and connected, and contains a nonempty open subset
\mathcal{M}_{X}
corresponding to stable rank 2 vector bundles
\mathscr{E}
with
c_{1}=0
and
c_{2}=2
on
X
. Every such
\mathscr{E}
sits in an exact sequence

0\to 6\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}(-2)\xrightarrow{M}6\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}(-1)\to\mathscr{E}\to 0

with
M
a skew
6\times 6
matrix with
X=V\bigl(\mathrm{Pf}(M)\bigr)
, and conversely, every cokernel
\mathscr{E}_{M}
of a skew map
M
with
X=V\bigl(\mathrm{Pf}(M)\bigr)
as in the above short exact sequence is a vector bundle of this type. From this one sees that
\mathcal{M}_{X}
is isomorphic to the GIT quotient of the space of such skew matrices up to congruence. Points in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{X}-\mathcal{M}_{X}
correspond to isomorphism classes of polystable sheaves with
c_{1}=0,c_{2}=2,c_{3}=0
on
X
. Moreover,

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{X}-\mathcal{M}_{X}=\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}

with
\mathcal{A}
an irreducible locally closed codimension
1
subvariety, and
\mathcal{B}
an irreducible closed codimension
1
subvariety containing all points of
\overline{\mathcal{A}}-\mathcal{A}
. Representatives of points in
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
can be described as follows.

(
\alpha
)

Let
C
be a smooth conic in
X
,
L
the positive generator of
\Pic(C)
(so that
\mathscr{O}_{X}(1)|_{C}\simeq L^{\otimes 2}
). Let
\mathscr{E}
be the kernel of the canonical evaluation map
H^{0}(C,L)\otimes\mathscr{O}_{X}\to L
. Then
\mathscr{E}
is a torsion free sheaf, with
c_{1}(\mathscr{E})=c_{3}(\mathscr{E})=0
and
c_{2}(\mathscr{E})=[C]
.

(
\beta
)

Let
l,l^{\prime}
be two lines in
X
(possibly equal), and let
I_{l},I_{l^{\prime}}
be their ideal sheaves. Then the sheaf
I_{l}\oplus I_{l^{\prime}}
is a torsion free sheaf with
c_{1}(E)=c_{3}(E)=0
and
c_{2}(E)=[l]+[l^{\prime}]
.

We will now show that there is a morphism

\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}_{X}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{im}\,\mathrm{adj}^{\mathrm{Pf}}}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{X}

sending
([M],[F])
to
\mathscr{F}_{M}
. We then consider the morphism
\widetilde{c}_{2}
defined by the diagram

\textstyle{\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}_{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{im}\,\mathrm{adj}^{\mathrm{Pf}}}
\scriptstyle{\widetilde{c}_{2}}
\textstyle{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{c_{2}}
\textstyle{J_{X}^{(2)}}

Here we think of the intermediate Jacobian
J_{X}
as being parametrised, using the Abel-Jacobi map, by the group of
1
-cycles on
X
that are homologically equivalent to zero, modulo those that are rationally equivalent to zero, and of
J_{X}^{(2)}
as the translate of
J_{X}
by the class of twice a line. See
[
Gri84
]
for details concerning these definitions. Moreover, the Chern classes are understood to take values in the Chow groups of cycles modulo rational equivalence, as in Grothendieck’s sense.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
\mathrm{Pf}(M)\not=0
then
\mathscr{E}_{M}\simeq\mathscr{F}_{M}
.

Proof.

We have

\mathrm{adj}^{\mathrm{Pf}}(M)\cdot M=M\cdot\mathrm{adj}^{\mathrm{Pf}}(M)=\mathrm{Pf}(M)\cdot\mathrm{Id},

hence on

-periodic exact sequence

\dots\xrightarrow{}6\mathscr{O}_{X}(-2)\xrightarrow{M}6\mathscr{O}_{X}(-1)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{adj}^{\mathrm{Pf}}(M)}6\mathscr{O}_{X}(1)\xrightarrow{M}6\mathscr{O}_{X}(2)\xrightarrow{}\dots

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{M}=\mathrm{coker}\left(6\mathscr{O}_{X}(-2)\xrightarrow{M}6\mathscr{O}_{X}(-1)\right)\simeq\ker\left(6\mathscr{O}_{X}(1)\xrightarrow{M}6\mathscr{O}_{X}(2)\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{im}\left(6\mathscr{O}_{X}(-1)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{adj}^{\mathrm{Pf}}(M)}6\mathscr{O}_{X}(1)\right)=\mathscr{F}_{M}.

∎

l_{2},l_{0}l_{4}-l_{1}l_{3},l_{1}^{2},l_{0}l_{1},l_{0}^{2}
. Again
F=q_{2}l_{2}+q_{1}l_{1}+q_{0}l_{0}
. The linear forms
l_{0},\dots,l_{4}
are independent, and putting
l_{2}=0
, which amounts to working in the polynomial ring in
l_{0},\dots,l_{3}
we see that
\overline{F}=\overline{q}_{1}l_{1}+\overline{q}_{0}l_{0}
is in the ideal generated by

\displaystyle(-l_{3})l_{1}+l_{4}l_{0}

\displaystyle l_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle l_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle l_{0}l_{1}

where the overlines mean “put
l_{2}=0
”, or equivalently, work in the polynomial ring in
l_{0},\dots,l_{3}
. Subtracting of a multiple of
(-l_{3})l_{1}+l_{4}l_{0}
we can assume that
\overline{F}=\overline{q}_{1}l_{1}+\overline{q}_{0}l_{0}
is even in the ideal generated by

, meaning that the new

are in

. Summarising, that means that we can write the original

\displaystyle q_{1}=

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}l_{2}-\lambda l_{3}+q_{1}^{\prime}

Therefore also in this case,

∎

Theorem 3.6
.

There is an isomorphism

\mathrm{im}\,\mathrm{adj}^{\mathrm{Pf}}\colon\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}_{X}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{X}.

sending
([M],[F])
to
\mathscr{F}_{M}
.

Proof.

Lemmata
3.1
,
3.3
,
3.4
,
3.5
show that
\mathrm{im}\,\mathrm{adj}^{\mathrm{Pf}}
defines a morphism from
\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}^{ss}
to
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{X}
that is constant on
G
-orbits, giving a morphism
\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}_{X}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{X}
.

To show that
\mathrm{im}\,\mathrm{adj}^{\mathrm{Pf}}
is injective on closed points, we distinguish two cases.
If
\mathscr{E}\in\mathcal{M}_{X}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{X}
, then
\mathscr{E}
fits into a short exact sequence

0\to 6\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}(-2)\xrightarrow{M}6\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{4}}(-1)\to\mathscr{E}\to 0

where

is a skew

matrix whose Pfaffian defines
X
.
If

is a second such sequence, then there exist invertible matrices

such that

By Lemma

this implies that there exists an invertible matrix

such that

Therefore

and

If
\mathscr{E}\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{X}-\mathcal{M}_{X}=\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}

3.4
and
3.3
.

Moreover,
\mathrm{im}\,\mathrm{adj}^{\mathrm{Pf}}
is dominant since all of
\mathcal{M}_{X}
is in its image, hence bijective on closed points since
\widetilde{\mathscr{M}}_{X}
is projective. Since
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{X}

∎

Appendix A
A linear algebra lemma

We want to prove the following

Lemma A.1
.

Let
M
be a skew-symmetric
n\times n
matrix with entries in a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
R
and
A,B\in\mathrm{GL_{n}}(\mathbb{C})
invertible matrices such that
M^{\prime}=A^{-1}MB
is also skew. Then there exists an an invertible matrix

S\in\mathrm{GL_{n}}(\mathbb{C})
such that

A^{-1}MB=S^{t}MS.

Since

A^{-1}MB=S^{t}MS\iff M(BA^{t})=(AS^{t})M(SA^{t})

we can reduce to the case where
A=\mathrm{id}
.
Furthermore, we observe that for any
T\in\mathrm{GL_{n}}(\mathbb{C})

M^{\prime}=MB\iff T^{t}M^{\prime}T=(T^{t}MT)(T^{-1}BT).

We can therefore assume that
B
has Jordan normal form.

By the following proposition we can reduce to the case where all Jordan blocks of
B
have
the same eigenvalue:

Proposition A.2
.

Let
M
be skew and
assume that
B
has Jordan blocks for pairwise different eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. We write in block form

MB=\begin{pmatrix}M_{11}&\dots&M_{1n}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-M_{1n}^{t}&\dots&M_{nn}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}B_{\lambda_{1}}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&B_{\lambda_{n}}\end{pmatrix}=M^{\prime}

with
B_{\lambda_{i}}
a square matrix containing all the Jordan blocks for the eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}
on the diagonal.
If
M^{\prime}
is also skew, then
M_{ij}=0
for
i\neq j
.

Proof.

Choose indices
i<j
.
If
M^{\prime}
is skew then

M^{\prime}_{ij}=M_{ij}B_{\lambda_{j}}=-(M_{ji}^{\prime})^{t}=-\bigl(-(M_{ij})^{t}B_{\lambda_{i}}\bigr)^{t}=B_{\lambda_{i}}^{t}M_{ij}.

We want to prove
M_{ij}=0
by induction on the number of rows of
M_{ij}
.

If
M_{ij}
has only one row, then

M_{ij}B_{\lambda_{j}}=B_{\lambda_{i}}^{t}M_{ij}=\lambda_{i}M_{ij}=M_{ij}(\lambda_{i}\cdot\mathrm{id})

which implies

M_{ij}(B_{\lambda_{j}}-\lambda_{i}\cdot\mathrm{id})=0.

Since
\lambda_{i}\not=\lambda_{j}
we get that
M_{ij}=0
.

If
M_{ij}
has more than one row, we write the equation above as

B_{\lambda_{i}}^{t}M_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}(\widetilde{B}_{\lambda_{i}})^{t}&0\\
\begin{pmatrix}0&\cdots&0&\epsilon\end{pmatrix}&\lambda_{i}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{M}_{ij}\\
m\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{M}_{ij}\\
m\end{pmatrix}B_{\lambda_{j}}

with
\epsilon=0
or
1
. We see that
\widetilde{M}_{ij}
satisfies the induction hypothesis and therefore
\widetilde{M}_{ij}=0
. The
equation above then reduces to

\lambda_{i}m=mB_{\lambda_{j}}

which as in the one-row case implies
m=0
.
∎

The case where
B
has only one eigenvalue is treated by

Proposition A.3
.

Let
M
be skew and
B_{\lambda}
a matrix consisting of Jordan blocks with eigenvalue
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. If

M^{\prime}=MB_{\lambda}

is again skew, then there exists an invertible matrix
S
such that
S^{2}=B_{\lambda}
and

M^{\prime}=S^{t}MS.

Proof.

Since
M
and
M^{\prime}
are skew, we have

MB_{\lambda}=M^{\prime}=-(M^{\prime})^{t}=-B_{\lambda}^{t}M^{t}=B_{\lambda}^{t}M

We can now write

B_{\lambda}=\lambda\cdot\mathrm{id}+N

with
N
nilpotent. Plugging this into the above equation we get

M(\lambda\cdot\mathrm{id}+N)=(\lambda\cdot\mathrm{id}+N^{t})M\quad\iff\quad MN=N^{t}M.

For
s_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
we consider the matrix

S=\sum_{i}s_{i}N^{i}.

Since the
s_{i}
are in
\mathbb{C}
we can successively solve the equation
S^{2}=B_{\lambda}
for the
s_{i}
, computing
s_{0},s_{1},s_{2}\dots
in this order. There are then two
solutions corresponding to the two solutions of
s_{0}^{2}=\lambda
(after which
s_{1},\dots
are uniquely determined using
\lambda\neq 0
). Now since
MN=N^{t}M
we also have

MS=S^{t}M
. With this we get

M^{\prime}=MB_{\lambda}=MS^{2}=(MS)S=S^{t}MS

as claimed.
∎

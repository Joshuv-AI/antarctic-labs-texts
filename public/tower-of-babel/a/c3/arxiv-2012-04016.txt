On the bounds of the sum of eigenvalues for a Dirichlet problem involving mixed fractional Laplacians


Abstract
Our purpose in this paper is to study of the eigenvalues { λ i ​ ( μ ) } i \{\lambda_{i}(\mu)\}_{i} of the Dirichlet problem ( − Δ ) s 1 ​ u = λ ⁡ ( ( − Δ ) s 2 ​ u + μ ​ u ) in ​ Ω , u = 0 in ℝ N ∖ Ω , (-\Delta)^{s_{1}}u=\lambda\big((-\Delta)^{s_{2}}u+\mu u\big)\ \ {\rm in}\ \,\Omega,\quad\quad u=0\ \ {\rm in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega, where 0 < s 2 < s 1 < 1 0<s_{2}<s_{1}<1 , N > 2 ​ s 1 N>2s_{1} and ( − Δ ) s (-\Delta)^{s} is the fractional Laplacian operator defined in the principle value sense. We first show the existence of a sequence of eigenvalues, which approaches infinity. Secondly we provide a Berezin–Li–Yau type lower bound for the sum of the eigenvalues of the above problem. Furthermore, using a self-contained and novel method, we establish an upper bound for the sum of eigenvalues of the problem under study.

Introduction and main results

Let
0<s_{2}<s_{1}<1
,
\Omega
be a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with the integer
N\geq 1
,
N>2s_{1}
. The main goal of this paper is to study
the lower bounds of the eigenvalues for the Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}u=\lambda\Big((-\Delta)^{s_{2}}u+\mu u\Big)&{\rm in}\quad\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{(-\Delta)^{s}}u=0&{\rm{in}}\ \quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
(-\Delta)^{s}
is the fractional laplacian defined in the following sense (principle value)

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=c_{N,s}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\epsilon}(x)}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy

(1.2)

with
s\in(0,1)
,

c_{N,s}=2^{2s}\pi^{-\frac{N}{2}}s\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N+2s}{2})}{\Gamma(1-s)}
and

\Gamma
being the Gamma function, see e.g.
[
40
]
.
Recall that, for
s\in(0,1)
, the fractional Laplacian of a function
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
can also be defined by:

(-\Delta)^{s}u(\xi)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\bigg(|\xi|^{2s}\mathcal{F}(u)(\xi)\bigg)\qquad\text{for all $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$},

where
F(f)
denotes the Fourier transform of
f
.

Problem (
1.1
) involves two fractional Laplacians with two different powers.
The terminology “mixed operators” refers to the differential or pseudo-differential order of the operator,
and to the type of the operator, which can combine classical and fractional features.
When
\lambda<0
, (
1.1
) involves a sum of two fractional Laplacians of different orders. Indeed, such sum of operators arises naturally from superposition of two stochastic processes with a different random walk and a Lévy flight, this is the case when a particle can follow either of these two processes according to a certain probability, the associated limit diffusion equation is described by a sum of fractional Laplacians, see for e.g
[
4
]
. While if
\lambda>0
, (
1.1
) models a difference of two fractional Laplacians. The sum and the difference of two fractional Laplacians appear in many circumstances. To mention few, problems of blood circulation in the heart, responsible for causing heart problems and in many circumstances coronary bypass surgeries, can be modelled by two to five mixed fractional Laplacians, for e.g. see
[
16
,
33
,
34
,
35
]
. It also appears in many circumstances because of the anomalous blood circulation, but it is often not the same anomaly in all the arteries, and the blood can follow either of the five arteries. Other applications of mixed fractional operators with different orders include plasma physics and population dynamics, ways to reduce pandemics and so on. In view of these important applications, we strongly believe that equation (
1.1
) and some of its variants described above will get an increasing interest in the near future.

The most primitive model of (
1.1
) for the eigenvalues is

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta u=\lambda u&{\rm in}\quad\Omega,\\[4.2679pt]
\phantom{-\Delta}u=0&{\rm{on}}\quad\!\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

which has attracted the attention of mathematicians since 1912. Indeed, Weyl in
[
43
]
showed that the
k
-th eigenvalue,
\lambda_{1,k}(\Omega)
(
1
stands for
s=1
) of (
1.3
), has the asymptotic behavior

\lambda_{1,k}(\Omega)\sim C_{N}(k|\Omega|)^{\frac{2}{N}}\quad{\rm as}\ \ k\to+\infty,

where

C_{N}=(2\pi)^{2}|B_{1}|^{-\frac{2}{N}}

and
|B_{1}|
is the volume of unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Later, Pólya
[
38
]
(in 1960) provided a lower bound for the k-th eigenvalue,

\lambda_{1,k}(\Omega)\geq C(k/|\Omega|)^{\frac{2}{N}}

(1.4)

for any
plane covering domain

D
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
C=C_{N}
, (his proof also works in dimension
N\geq 3
).
D
is called a
plane covering domain
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
if an infinity of domains congruent to
D
cover the whole plane without gaps and without overlapping except a set of measure zero. In
[
38
]
, Pólya also made a conjecture that (
1.4
) holds for any bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
C=C_{N}
. To answer this conjecture, Lieb
[
32
]
proved (
1.4
) with a positive constant
C
for general bounded domain and Li-Yau
[
31
]
improved
the constant
C
to
\frac{N}{N+2}C_{N}
. Until now, this constant for lower bound is the best and (
1.4
) with
C=\frac{N}{N+2}C_{N}
is called Berezin-Lieb-Yau inequality. More related estimates on lower bounds for the eigenvalues under various setting can be found in
[
14
,
18
,
32
,
36
]
. On the other hand, the upper bounds of Dirichlet eigenvalues were derived by Kröger in
[
29
]
by calculating the Rayleigh quotient by using a sequence of functions approaching the characterized function of
\Omega
. We also refer to Yang’s upper bounds of the Dirichlet’s eigenvalues in
[
13
,
8
]
in the following way:

\lambda_{1,k}(\Omega)\leq c(N,k)\lambda_{1,1}(\Omega)k^{\frac{2}{N}}\quad\text{ for some $c(N,k)>0$}.

The unstopped interest in finding bounds for eigenvalues of the Dirichlet problem is in part due to the following fact: The Hilbert-Polya conjecture is to associate the zero of the Riemann Zeta function with the
eigenvalue of a Hermitian operator. This quest initiated the mathematical interest for estimating
the sum of Dirichlet eigenvalues of the Laplacian while in physics the question is related to count
the number of bound states of a one body Schrödinger operator and to study their asymptotic distribution. The latter constitutes in itself a branch in nonlinear analysis.
During the last decade, there has been a renewed and increasing
interest in the study of linear and nonlinear integral operators.
The prototype is the fractional Laplacian. This has been motivated by numerous applications in different fields motivated by connections to real world life applications and by important advances in the theory of linear and nonlinear
partial differential equations, see basic properties

, Liouville property

To analyze the fractional Dirichlet eigenvalues of (
1.1
), we denote
{\mathbb{H}}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
the space of all measurable functions
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
with
u\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
and

{\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy<+\infty.

It is well known that
{\mathbb{H}}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space equipped with the inner product

\mathcal{E}_{s}(u,w)=\frac{c_{N,s}}{2}{\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}}\frac{(u(x)-u(y)){(w(x)-w(y))}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy

and the induced norm

\|u\|_{s}:=\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{s}(u,u)}.

We say a function
u\in{\mathbb{H}}^{s_{1}}_{0}(\Omega)
be an eigenfunction of (
1.1
) corresponding to the eigenvalue

\lambda
if

{\mathcal{E}}_{s_{1}}(u,w)=\lambda\Big({\mathcal{E}}_{s_{2}}(u,w)+\mu\int_{\Omega}uw\,dx\Big)\quad\text{for all $w\in{\mathbb{H}}^{s_{1}}_{0}(\Omega)$.}

For
\mu\geq-\lambda_{s_{2},1}
, we denote by
{\mathbb{H}}^{s_{2}}_{\mu,0}(\Omega)
the space of all measurable functions
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
with
u\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
and

\|u\|_{s_{2},\mu}:=\bigg({\mathcal{E}}_{s_{2}}(u,u)+\mu\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty.

The corresponding inner product in
{\mathbb{H}}^{s_{2}}_{\mu,0}(\Omega)
is given by

{\langle u,w\rangle_{s_{2},\mu}:=}\,{\mathcal{E}}_{s_{2}}(u,w)+\mu\int_{\Omega}uw\,dx,\quad\forall\,\,u,\,w\in{\mathbb{H}}^{s_{2}}_{0}(\Omega).

Let
\lambda_{s_{2},1}
be the first eigenvalue of
(
1.5
) corresponding to
s=s_{2}
.
We note that
\|.\|_{s_{2},\mu}
is equivalent to
\|.\|_{s_{2}}
for
\mu>-\lambda_{s_{2},1}
.

Our first aim is to show the existence of a sequence of discrete eigenvalues of (
1.1
) as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu>-\lambda_{s_{2},1}
, where
\lambda_{s_{2},1}>0
be the first eigenvalue of (
1.5
) with
s=s_{2}
.
Then problem (
1.1
) admits a sequence of real eigenvalues

0<\lambda_{1}(\mu)\leq\lambda_{2}(\mu)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{j}(\mu)\leq\lambda_{j+1}(\mu)\leq\cdots

and the corresponding eigenfunction
\phi_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, we have the following properties:

(i)

\lambda_{j}(\mu)=\min\{{\mathcal{E}}_{s_{1}}(u,u):\,u\in{\mathbb{H}}_{0,j}(\Omega),\,{\|u\|_{{\mathbb{H}}^{s_{2}}_{\mu,0}(\Omega)}=1}\}
, where

{{\mathbb{H}}_{0,1}(\Omega)={\mathbb{H}}^{s_{1}}_{0}(\Omega),\quad{\mathbb{H}}_{0,j}(\Omega):=\{u\in{\mathbb{H}}^{s_{1}}_{0}(\Omega)\>:\>\langle u,\,\phi_{m}\rangle_{s_{2},\mu}=0\,\,\mbox{for}\,\,m=1,\dots,j-1\}\,\,\text{for}\,\,j>1;}

(ii)

\{\phi_{j}\>:\>j\in\mathbb{N}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
{\mathbb{H}}^{s_{2}}_{\mu,0}(\Omega)
;

(iii)

\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\lambda_{j}(\mu)=+\infty
;

(iv)

For
\mu\in(-\lambda_{s_{2},1},+\infty)
, the map
\mu\mapsto\lambda_{1}(\mu)
is decreasing and
\displaystyle\limsup_{\mu\to{-\lambda_{s_{2},1}^{+}}}\lambda_{1}(\mu)<+\infty
.

We remark that

(a)

from the appendix in
[
41
]
, problem (
1.5
) has the property that the first eigenvalue is simple and
the corresponding eigenfunction
\phi_{s,1}
is positive; these properties are derived by the following:
{\mathcal{E}}_{s}(|\phi_{s,1}|)<{\mathcal{E}}_{s}(\phi_{s,1})
, if
\phi_{s,1}
is sign-changing.
However, this argument fails for problem (
1.1
) due to presence of multiple fractional Laplacians, and it is very interesting but challenging to determine the one-fold of
\lambda_{1}(\mu)
and the positivity of the eigenfunction
\phi_{1}
corresponding to the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
for problem (
1.1
);

(b)

thanks to the monotonicity and boundedness of
\lambda_{1}(\mu)
, assertion
(iv)
indicates
that it is possible to obtain the existence of
\{\lambda_{j}(\mu)\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
for
\mu\leq-\lambda_{s_{2},1}
;

(c)

it is known that eigenfunctions of (
1.5
) are
C^{\infty}(\Omega)
. To see this, one uses bootstraps method to prove solutions of (
1.5
) are in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and then uses regularity results of
[
39
]
. While the regularity for (
1.1
) seems to be much more complicated, because bootstraps iteration has to work between different order fractional Laplacians.

We now provide a lower bound for the sum of eigenvalues of (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mu\geq 0
and
\{\lambda_{j}(\mu)\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be the increasing sequence of eigenvalues of problem (
1.1
) and
\omega_{{}_{N-1}}
denote the surface area of the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then for
k\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}(\mu)\geq b_{1}|\Omega|^{-\frac{2(s_{1}-s_{2})}{N}}k^{1+\frac{2(s_{1}-s_{2})}{2s_{2}+N}}-\mu b_{2}|\Omega|^{-\frac{2s_{1}}{N}}k^{1+\frac{2s_{1}-4s_{2}}{2s_{2}+N}},

(1.10)

where

b_{1}=\frac{(2s_{2}+N)^{\frac{2s_{1}+N}{2s_{2}+N}}}{2s_{1}+N}\Big(2^{-(N+1+2s_{2})}\pi^{-\frac{3N}{2}}\omega_{{}_{N-1}}^{2-\frac{2s_{2}}{N}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N-2s_{2}}{2})}{\Gamma(s_{2}+1)}N^{\frac{2s_{2}}{N}}\Big)^{-\frac{2(s_{1}-s_{2})}{2s_{2}+N}}

and

b_{2}=b_{1}\frac{2s_{1}+N}{N(2s_{2}+N)^{\frac{2s_{2}}{2s_{2}+N}}}\Big(2^{-(N+1+2s_{2})}\pi^{-\frac{3N}{2}}\omega_{{}_{N-1}}^{2-\frac{2s_{2}}{N}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N-2s_{2}}{2})}{\Gamma(s_{2}+1)}N^{\frac{2s_{2}}{N}}\Big)^{\frac{2s_{2}}{N+2s_{2}}}.

We remark that when
s_{2}=0=\mu
, the constant
b_{1}(s_{1},0)
in Theorem
1.2
reduces to
\frac{N}{N+2s_{1}}a(N,s_{1})
, where
a(N,s_{1})
is defined as in (
1.7
). Moreover, (
1.10
) coincides with Berezin-Li-Yau estimate for (
1.3
) (with
s_{1}=1
).

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is inspired by the Berezin–Li–Yau method
(see
[
31
]
) in which the authors treated mainly the function
\Psi_{k}(x,y)=\sum^{k}_{j=1}\psi_{j}(x)\psi_{j}(y),

where
\psi_{j}
’s are the eigenfunctions corresponding to the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}
of (
1.3
). Denote
F=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|({\mathcal{F}}_{x}\Psi_{k})(z,y)|^{2}dy
,
the key estimate is the following

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Fdz\leq\Big(\frac{N+2}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|z|^{2}Fdz\Big)^{\frac{N}{N+2}}\Big(|B_{1}|\|F\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)^{\frac{2}{N+2}},

(1.11)

where
|B_{1}|
denotes the volume of unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. For our problem (
1.1
), the situation is much more complicated. More precisely, to apply Berezin-Li-Yau method,
we need to consider the function
f=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|({\mathcal{F}}_{x}\Phi_{k})(z,y)|^{2}dy
, where

\Phi_{k}(x,y)=\sum^{k}_{j=1}\phi_{j}(x)\tilde{\phi}_{j}(y),\quad\tilde{\phi}_{j}:=\Big((-\Delta)^{s_{2}}+\mu\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi_{j}

and
\phi_{j}
’s are eigenfunctions corresponding to the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}
of (
1.1
). The most important estimate is

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|z|^{2s_{2}}+\mu)f(z)dz
, which is controlled by
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|z|^{2s_{1}}fdz
and
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}
.
These difficulties arise from the non homogeneous property of lower oder term
(|z|^{2s_{2}}+\mu)f
and the estimate
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}
. For
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}
, the original tool is the Bessel’s inequality, which requires orthonormal property for
\{\phi_{j}\}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, while
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j}
is not an orthonormal sequence in
L^{2}(\Omega)
(it is orthonormal in
H^{s_{2}}_{\mu,0}(\Omega)
). To overcome this difficulty, we transform
\phi_{j}
to
\tilde{\phi}_{j}
which is orthonormal in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
but not supported in
\Omega
. Further using certain delicate estimate, we have overcome the difficulty (see Section 3.2).

2
Existence

We set

{W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}:=\Big\{u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}\mbox{ measurable }\Big|u=0\ {\rm a.e.\ in\ }\Omega^{c}\mbox{ and }\int_{Q}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy<\infty\Big\}.

Note that from the fractional Poincaré inequality, see
[
15

{W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}
is endowed with the norm defined as

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}:=\left(\int_{Q}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy\right)^{1/p}.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
0<s_{2}<s_{1}<1
,

\Omega

Before starting to prove the above theorem, we need to introduce the Besov Space over

Definition 2.2

\eta=\{\eta_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\eta_{j}(x)=1,

and for every multi-index

c_{\alpha}
such that

2^{j\alpha}|D^{\alpha}\eta_{j}(x)|\leq c_{\alpha}\quad\forall\,j=0,1,2,\dots\quad\mbox{and}\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
s\in(-\infty,\infty)
and
p,q\in(0,\infty]
and
\{\eta_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Then

B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\eta}:=\|2^{sj}{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\big(\eta_{j}{\mathcal{F}}(f)\big)\|_{l_{q}\big(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\big)}<\infty\Big\}.

It can be shown that the quasi-norm
\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\eta}
does not depend on the choice of
\eta\in{\mathcal{A}}
(see
[
42
, Proposition 1 in 2.3.2]
).

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{D^{\prime}}(\Omega)
denote the set of all distributions over
\Omega
. For
1\leq p,\,q\leq\infty
, and
0<s<1
, we set

B^{s}_{p,q}(\Omega)=\Big\{u\in\mathcal{D^{\prime}}(\Omega):\,\exists\,\,g\in B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\quad\mbox{with}\quad g|_{\Omega}=u\Big\}

and

\|u\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}(\Omega)}=\inf_{g\in B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\,g|_{\Omega}=u}\|g\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Here
B^{s}_{p,q}(\Omega)
is called the Besov Space over
\Omega
.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
42
, Theorem 3.1.1(i)]

Let
p_{0},q_{0},p_{1},q_{1}\in(0,\infty)
,
s_{0},s_{1}\in(-\infty,\infty)
and
\Omega
be a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then the following embedding

B^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow B^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},q_{1}}(\Omega)

is continuous if
s_{0}-\frac{N}{p_{0}}>s_{1}-\frac{N}{p_{1}}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

[
42
, pg.233]

Let
0<s_{2}<s_{1}
and
p,q\in(0,\infty)
and
\Omega
be a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then the embedding
B^{s_{1}}_{p,q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow B^{s_{2}}_{p,q}(\Omega)
is compact.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.
From
[
2
, Lemma 2.2]
, we know that the embedding
{W^{s_{1},p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset W^{s_{2},p}_{0}(\Omega)}
is continuous. Moreover, from
[
42
, pg. 209]
), it follows that

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}:=\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

is an equivalent norm for
\|u\|_{B^{s}_{p,p}(\Omega)}
for
p\in(1,\infty),\,s\in(0,1)
.
Therefore, by Lemma
2.6
we have that

W^{s_{1},p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{s_{2},p}(\Omega)\quad\text{is compact.}

(2.1)

Now let
\{u_{n}\}
be a bounded sequence in
{W^{s_{1},p}_{0}(\Omega)}
, to prove the theorem we need to extract a convergent subsequence in

{W^{s_{2},p}_{0}(\Omega)}
. By Rellich compactness, up to a subsequence
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for some
u\in{W^{s_{1},p}_{0}(\Omega)}
. For that subsequence we define
v_{n}:=u_{n}-u
. Therefore,
we need to show
v_{n}\to 0
in
{W^{s_{2},p}_{0}(\Omega)}
. As
v_{n}=0
in
\Omega^{c}
,

\displaystyle\|v_{n}\|_{{W^{s_{2},p}_{0}(\Omega)}}^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|v_{n}(x)-v_{n}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s_{2}p}}dxdy+2\int_{x\in\Omega}\int_{y\in\Omega^{c}}\frac{|v_{n}(x)-v_{n}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s_{2}p}}dxdy

(2.2)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|v_{n}\|_{W^{s_{2},p}(\Omega)}^{p}+2\int_{x\in\Omega}\int_{y\in\Omega^{c}}\frac{|v_{n}(x)-v_{n}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s_{2}p}}dxdy.

By Póincare inequality in
[
15
, Theorem 6.7]
there exists a positive constant
c>0
such that

\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}\leq c\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy\quad{\rm for\ any}\ v\in{W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}.

Thus, from the definition of
{W^{s_{1},p}_{0}(\Omega)}
and
W^{s_{1},p}(\Omega)
, there exists
c>0
such that

\|v_{n}\|_{W^{s_{1},p}(\Omega)}\leq c\|v_{n}\|_{{W^{s_{1},p}_{0}(\Omega)}}.

Therefore,
\{v_{n}\}
is a bounded sequence in
W^{s_{1},p}(\Omega)
. Consequently,
by (
2.1
), up to a subsequence
v_{n}\to 0
in
W^{s_{2},p}(\Omega)
. Therefore,
to complete the proof, we only have to show that

\int_{x\in\Omega}\int_{y\in\Omega^{c}}\frac{|v_{n}(x)-v_{n}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s_{2}p}}dxdy\to 0.

To that end, let
\epsilon>0
be arbitrary and we define
M:=\sup_{n}\|v_{n}\|_{{W^{s_{1},p}_{0}(\Omega)}}
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{x\in\Omega}\int_{y\in\Omega^{c}}\frac{|v_{n}(x)-v_{n}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s_{2}p}}dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{x\in\Omega}\int_{y\in\Omega^{c}\cap|x-y|<{\varepsilon}}\frac{|v_{n}(x)-v_{n}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s_{2}p}}dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{x\in\Omega}\int_{y\in\Omega^{c}\cap|x-y|\geq\epsilon}\frac{|v_{n}(x)-v_{n}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s_{2}p}}dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{x\in\Omega}\int_{y\in\Omega^{c}\cap|x-y|<\epsilon}\frac{|v_{n}(x)-v_{n}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s_{1}p}}|x-y|^{(s_{1}-s_{2})p}dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{x\in\Omega}\bigg(\int_{y\in\Omega^{c}\cap|x-y|\geq\epsilon}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{N+s_{2}p}}\bigg)|v_{n}(x)|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon^{(s_{1}-s_{2})p}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|v_{n}(x)-v_{n}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+s_{1}p}}dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+C(\epsilon)\|v_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon^{(s_{1}-s_{2})p}M+o(1),

where
o(1)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Therefore, the above integral can be made arbitrary small. Hence from (
2.2
), we conclude that
\|v_{n}\|_{{W^{s_{2},p}_{0}(\Omega)}}\to 0
. This completes the proof.
\Box

In the particular case that
p=2
, for
s\in(0,1)
we set

{\mathbb{H}}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)={W^{s,2}_{0}(\Omega)}

which is a Hilbert space with the inner product

{\mathcal{E}}_{s}(u,v)
for
u,v\in{\mathbb{H}}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The functional

{\mathbb{H}}^{s_{1}}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R},\qquad u\mapsto\Phi(u):={\mathcal{E}}_{s_{1}}(u,u)

is weakly lower semicontinuous. Moreover, let

{\mathcal{M}}_{1}:=\{u\in{\mathbb{H}}^{s_{1}}_{0}(\Omega),\,\|u\|_{s_{2}}+\mu\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1\},

where
\|\cdot\|_{s_{2}}+\mu\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
is equivalent to

\|\cdot\|_{s_{2}}
in the space
{\mathbb{H}}^{s_{2}}_{0}(\Omega)
for
\mu>-\lambda_{s_{2},1}
.
Then we have that

\Phi(u)<+\infty\qquad\text{for $u\in{\mathcal{M}}_{1}$.}

Put
\lambda_{1}(\mu):=\inf_{{\mathcal{M}}_{1}}\Phi
. By Theorem
2.1
, the embedding

W^{s_{1},p}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow W^{s_{2},p}_{0}(\Omega)

is compact and therefore, it follows that
\lambda_{1}(\mu)
is attained by a function
\phi_{1}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
. Consequently, there exists a Lagrange multiplier
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that

{\mathcal{E}}_{s_{1}}(\phi_{1},w)=\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{\prime}(\phi_{1})w=\lambda\Big({\mathcal{E}}_{s_{2}}(\phi_{1},w)+\mu\int_{\Omega}\phi_{1}w\,dx\Big)\qquad\text{for all $w\in{\mathbb{H}}^{s_{1}}_{0}(\Omega)$.}

Choosing
w=\phi_{1}
yields to
\lambda=\lambda_{1}(\mu)
. Hence
\phi_{1}
is an eigenfunction of (
1.1
) corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(\mu)
. Moreover
\lambda_{1}(\mu)>0
. Next we proceed inductively and assume that
\phi_{2},\dots,\phi_{k}\in{\mathbb{H}}^{s_{1}}_{0}(\Omega)
and
\lambda_{2}(\mu)\leq\dots\leq\lambda_{k}(\mu)
are already given for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
with the properties that for
j=2,\dots,k
, the function
\phi_{j}
is a minimizer
of
\Phi
within the set

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{j}:=\{u\in{\mathbb{H}}^{s_{1}}_{0}(\Omega)\>:\>\|u\|_{s_{2}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1,\>\text{${\mathcal{E}}_{s_{2}}(u,\phi_{m})+\mu\int_{\Omega}u\phi_{m}\,dx=0$ for $m=1,\dots j-1$}\},

\lambda_{j}(\mu)=\inf_{{\mathcal{M}}_{j}}\Phi=\Phi(\phi_{j})
, and

{\mathcal{E}}_{s_{1}}(\phi_{j},\varphi)=\lambda_{j}(\mu)\Big({\mathcal{E}}_{s_{2}}(\phi_{j},\varphi)+\mu\int_{\Omega}\phi_{j}\varphi\,dx\Big)\qquad\text{for all $\varphi\in\mathbb{H}^{s_{1}}_{0}(\Omega)$.}

(2.3)

Again by the compact embedding in Theorem
2.1
, the value
\lambda_{k+1}
is attained by a function
\phi_{k+1}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k+1}
. Consequently, there exists a Lagrange multiplier
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that

{\mathcal{E}}_{s_{1}}(\phi_{k+1},\varphi)=\lambda\Big({\mathcal{E}}_{s_{2}}(\phi_{k+1},\varphi)+\mu\int_{\Omega}\phi_{k+1}\varphi\,dx\Big)\qquad\text{for all $\varphi\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k+1}(\Omega)$.}

(2.4)

Choosing
\varphi=\phi_{k+1}
, we have that
\lambda=\lambda_{k+1}(\mu)
. Moreover, for
j=1,\dots,k
, we have, by (
2.3
) and the definition of
{\mathcal{M}}_{k+1}(\Omega)
, that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{s_{1}}(\phi_{k+1},\phi_{j})={\mathcal{E}}_{s_{1}}(\phi_{j},\phi_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{j}(\mu)\Big({\mathcal{E}}_{s_{2}}(\phi_{j},\phi_{k+1})+\mu\int_{\Omega}\phi_{j}\phi_{k+1}\,dx\Big)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle=\lambda_{k+1}(\mu)\Big({\mathcal{E}}_{s_{2}}(\phi_{k+1},\phi_{j})+\mu\int_{\Omega}\phi_{j}\phi_{k+1}\,dx\Big).

Hence (
2.4
) holds with
\lambda=\lambda_{k+1}(\mu)
for all
\varphi\in{\mathbb{H}}^{s_{1}}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Inductively, we have now constructed a normalized sequence
(\phi_{k})_{k}
in
{\mathbb{H}}^{s_{1}}_{0}(\Omega)
and a nondecreasing sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k}
in
\mathbb{R}
such that property (i) holds and such that
\phi_{k}
is an eigenfunction of (
1.1
) corresponding to
\lambda=\lambda_{k}(\mu)
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, by construction, the sequence
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k}
forms an orthonormal system in
{\mathbb{H}}^{s_{2}}_{\mu,0}(\Omega)
.

Next we show property (iii), i.e.,
\lim\limits_{k\to+\infty}\lambda_{k}(\mu)=+\infty.
Supposing by contradiction that
c:=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{k}(\mu)<+\infty
, we deduce that

{\mathcal{E}}_{s_{1}}(\phi_{k},\phi_{k})\leq c
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence the sequence
(\phi_{k})
is bounded in
{\mathbb{H}}^{s_{1}}_{0}(\Omega)
, and therefore by Rellich compactness theorem,
(\phi_{k})

contains a convergent subsequence
(\phi_{k_{j}})_{j}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. This however is impossible since the functions
\{\phi_{k_{j}}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
are orthonormal in
H^{s_{2}}_{\mu,0}(\Omega)
. Hence (iii) is proved.

Next, to prove that
\{\phi_{k}\>:\>k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
H^{s_{2}}_{\mu,0}(\Omega)
, we first suppose by contradiction that there exists
v\in\mathbb{H}^{s_{1}}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\|v\|_{s_{2}}+{\mu}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1

and
{\mathcal{E}}_{s_{2}}(v,\phi_{k})+\mu\int_{\Omega}v\phi_{k}\,dx=0
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{k}(\mu)=+\infty
, there exists an integer
k_{0}>0
such that

\Phi(v)<\lambda_{k_{0}}(\mu)=\inf_{{\mathcal{M}}_{k_{0}}}\Phi(u),

which by definition of
{\mathcal{M}}_{k_{0}}
implies that

{\mathcal{E}}_{s_{2}}(v,\phi_{k})+\mu\int_{\Omega}v\phi_{k}\,dx\not=0
for some
k\in\{1,\dots,k_{0}-1\}
. This is a a contradiction to
{\mathcal{E}}_{s_{2}}(v,\phi_{k})+\mu\int_{\Omega}v\phi_{k}\,dx=0
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, we conclude that
{\mathbb{H}}^{s_{1}}_{0}(\Omega)
is contained in the
H^{s_{2}}_{\mu,0}
-closure of the span of
\{\phi_{k}\>:\>k\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Since
{\mathbb{H}}^{s_{1}}_{0}(\Omega)
is dense in
H^{s_{2}}_{\mu,0}(\Omega)
, we conclude that the span of
\{\phi_{k}\>:\>k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is dense in
H^{s_{2}}_{\mu,0}(\Omega)
, and hence
\{\phi_{k}\>:\>k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
H^{s_{2}}_{\mu,0}(\Omega)
. This proves (ii).

Finally, we show
(iv)
. It follows by the definition of the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(\mu)

that for
\mu\in(-\lambda_{s_{2},1},+\infty)
, the map
\mu\mapsto\lambda_{1}(\mu)
is decreasing.

Let
(\lambda_{s_{2},1},\varphi_{s_{2},1})
be the first eigenvalue and the corresponding eigenfunction of (
1.5
) with
s=s_{2}
. We may assume that
\varphi_{s_{2},1}>0
in
\Omega
,
\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{s_{2},1}^{2}dx=1
. Let
\eta_{0}
be a smooth function with compact support in
B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
such that
B_{2r_{0}}(x_{0})\subset\Omega

with
\int_{\Omega}\eta_{0}^{2}dx=1
. Note that
\eta_{0}\not\equiv\varphi_{s_{2},1}
in
\Omega
.
Then
{\mathcal{E}}_{s_{2}}(\eta_{0},\eta_{0})>\lambda_{s_{2},1}
, otherwise
\eta_{0}\equiv\varphi_{s_{2},1}

since the first eigenfunction of (
1.5
) with
s=s_{2}
is simple.

Thus, we have that

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\mu)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\mathcal{E}}_{s_{1}}(\eta_{0},\eta_{0})}{{\mathcal{E}}_{s_{2}}(\eta_{0},\eta_{0})+\mu\int_{\Omega}\eta_{0}^{2}dx}

\displaystyle\ \leq\frac{{\mathcal{E}}_{s_{1}}(\eta_{0},\eta_{0})}{{\mathcal{E}}_{s_{2}}(\eta_{0},\eta_{0})-\lambda_{s_{2},1}}\ <+\infty\quad{\rm as}\ \ \mu\to-\lambda_{s_{2},1}^{+}.

This completes the proof.

\Box

3
Lower bounds

3.1
Important estimate

To prove the Li-Yau’s type lower bound for (
1.1
), we need the following results.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mu\geq 0
,
0<s_{2}<s_{1}<1
and
f
be a real-valued function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
0\leq f\leq M_{1}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(z)|z|^{2s_{1}}dz\leq M_{2},

then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|z|^{2s_{2}}+\mu)f(z)dz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}M_{1}}{2s_{2}+N}\Big(\frac{(2s_{1}+N)M_{2}}{M_{1}\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}\Big)^{\frac{2s_{2}+N}{2s_{1}+N}}\Big[1+\mu\frac{2s_{2}+N}{N}\Big(\frac{(2s_{1}+N)M_{2}}{M_{1}\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}\Big)^{-\frac{2s_{2}}{2s_{1}+N}}\Big].

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.
Let

h(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}(|z|^{2s_{2}}+\mu)M_{1}&{\rm for}\quad|z|<R,\\[4.2679pt]
\phantom{}0&{\rm{for}}\quad|z|\geq R,\end{array}\right.

where
R>0
such that

\int_{B_{R}}M_{1}|z|^{2s_{1}}dz=M_{2}.

Direct computation shows that

R^{2s_{1}+N}=\frac{(2s_{1}+N)M_{2}}{M_{1}\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}.

Since

\displaystyle|z|^{2s_{2}}\Big(|z|^{2(s_{1}-s_{2})}-R^{2(s_{1}-s_{2})}\Big)\Big(f(z)-(|z|^{2s_{2}}+\mu)^{-1}h(z)\Big)\geq 0

and

\displaystyle\mu\Big(|z|^{2s_{1}}-R^{2s_{1}}\Big)\Big(f(z)-(|z|^{2s_{2}}+\mu)^{-1}h(z)\Big)\geq 0,

it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|z|^{2s_{2}}\Big(f(z)-(|z|^{2s_{2}}+\mu)^{-1}h(z)\Big)dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R^{2(s_{1}-s_{2})}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|z|^{2s_{2}}|z|^{2(s_{1}-s_{2})}\Big(f(z)-(|z|^{2s_{2}}+\mu)^{-1}h(z)\Big)dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R^{2(s_{1}-s_{2})}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|z|^{2s_{1}}(f(z)-M_{1}\chi_{{}_{B_{R}}})dz\leq 0,

(3.1)

and

\displaystyle\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(f(z)-(|z|^{2s_{2}}+\mu)^{-1}h(z)\Big)dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{R^{2s_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|z|^{2s_{1}}\Big(f(z)-(|z|^{2s_{2}}+\mu)^{-1}h(z)\Big)dz

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{R^{2s_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|z|^{2s_{1}}(f(z)-M_{1}\chi_{{}_{B_{R}}})dz\leq 0.

(3.2)

Combining (
3.1
) and (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|z|^{2s_{2}}+\mu)f(z)dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(z)dz

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}M_{1}}{2s_{2}+N}R^{2s_{2}+N}\Big(1+\mu\frac{2s_{2}+N}{N}R^{-2s_{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}M_{1}}{2s_{2}+N}\Big(\frac{(2s_{1}+N)M_{2}}{M_{1}\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}\Big)^{\frac{2s_{2}+N}{2s_{1}+N}}\Big[1+\mu\frac{2s_{2}+N}{N}\Big(\frac{(2s_{1}+N)M_{2}}{M_{1}\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}\Big)^{-\frac{2s_{2}}{2s_{1}+N}}\Big].

We complete the proof.
\Box

We also need the following Lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
1>\tau_{1}>\tau_{2}>0
and
d_{1}>0.

Let
r_{1}
be the solution of

r^{\tau_{1}}\Big(1+r^{-\tau_{2}}\Big)=d_{1},

then

d_{1}^{\frac{1}{\tau_{1}}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{\tau_{1}}d_{1}^{-\frac{\tau_{2}}{\tau_{1}}}\Big)<r_{1}<d_{1}^{\frac{1}{\tau_{1}}}.

Proof.
Let

f(r)=r^{\tau_{1}}\Big(1+r^{-\tau_{2}}\Big),

then
f(0)=0
,
\lim_{r\to+\infty}f(r)=+\infty
and
f^{\prime}(r)=\tau_{1}r^{\tau_{1}-1}+(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})r^{\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}-1}>0
,
f^{\prime\prime}(r)=\tau_{1}(\tau_{1}-1)r^{\tau_{1}-2}+(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}-1)r^{\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}-2}<0
, As a consequence,
f
is strictly increasing, concave in
(0,+\infty)

and for any
d_{1}>0
, there exists a unique solution
r_{1}
such that
f(r_{1})=d_{1}
. Moreover, since
f(r)>0
for
r>0
, we conclude
r_{1}>0
.

Let

R_{1}=d_{1}^{\frac{1}{\tau_{1}}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{\tau_{1}}d_{1}^{-\frac{\tau_{2}}{\tau_{1}}}\Big)_{+}\quad{\rm and}\quad R_{2}=d_{1}^{\frac{1}{\tau_{1}}},

where
a_{+}=\max\{0,a\}
.
Note that

\displaystyle\frac{f(R_{2})}{d_{1}}

\displaystyle=1+d_{1}^{-\frac{\tau_{2}}{\tau_{1}}}>1

and if

\frac{1}{\tau_{1}}d_{1}^{-\frac{\tau_{2}}{\tau_{1}}}<1
, then

\displaystyle\frac{f(R_{1})}{d_{1}}

\displaystyle=\Big(1-\frac{1}{\tau_{1}}d_{1}^{-\frac{\tau_{2}}{\tau_{1}}}\Big)^{\tau_{1}}+d_{1}^{-\frac{\tau_{2}}{\tau_{1}}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{\tau_{1}}d_{1}^{-\frac{\tau_{2}}{\tau_{1}}}\Big)^{\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 1-d_{1}^{-\frac{\tau_{2}}{\tau_{1}}}+d_{1}^{-\frac{\tau_{2}}{\tau_{1}}}-{\frac{\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}}{\tau_{1}}d_{1}^{-\frac{2\tau_{2}}{\tau_{1}}}}

\displaystyle<1,

where we used the fact that for
\tau\in(0,1)
,

(1-t)^{\tau}\leq 1-\tau t\quad{\rm for\ any}\ t\in(0,1).

On the other hand, if
\frac{1}{\tau_{1}}d_{1}^{-\frac{\tau_{2}}{\tau_{1}}}\geq 1
, then
R_{1}=0
. Therefore,
\frac{f(R_{1})}{d_{1}}=0<1
. Thus, in both the cases

f(R_{1})<d_{1}<f(R_{2})
. Now using the fact that
f
is strictly increasing, continuous and
f(r_{1})=d_{1}
, we conclude
R_{1}<r_{1}<R_{2}
. This completes the proof.

\Box

3.2
Lower bound

The Bessel inequality plays an important role in the Li-Yau method for the lower bound of eigenvalues:

Let
{\mathbb{H}}
be an inner product space or a Hermitian product space together with its product function
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. Let
e_{1},e_{2},\cdots
be any (finite or infinite) orthonormal sequence. Then for any
x\in{\mathbb{H}}
,

\sum_{j\geq 1}|\langle x,e_{j}\rangle|^{2}\leq\langle x,x\rangle=\|x\|_{{}_{\mathbb{H}}}^{2}.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let

\Phi_{k}(x,y)=\sum^{k}_{j=1}\phi_{j}(x)\tilde{\phi}_{j}(y),

where

\tilde{\phi}_{j}:=\Big((-\Delta)^{s_{2}}+\mu\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi_{j}={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\bigg[\big(|z|^{2s_{2}}+\mu\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathcal{F}}\big(\phi_{j}\big)\bigg].

Using the Fourier transform, we have that

{\mathcal{F}}_{x}(\Phi_{k})(z,y)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{k}(x,y)e^{ix\cdot z}dx

and

{\mathcal{F}}\left(\Big((-\Delta)^{s_{2}}+\mu\Big)_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\phi_{j}}\right)(z)=\big(|z|^{2s_{2}}+\mu\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}{{\mathcal{F}}\big(\phi_{j}(z)\big)}\ \ \text{ for}\ \,\mu\geq 0.

Therefore, doing a straightforward computation we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big|\big(|z|^{s_{2}}+\mu\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathcal{F}}_{x}(\Phi_{k})(z,y)\Big|^{2}dydz=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\tilde{\phi}_{j}^{2}(x)\tilde{\phi}_{j}^{2}(y)dydx=k,

(3.3)

by the orthonormality of
\{\tilde{\phi}_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Next, we estimate
\big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big|{\mathcal{F}}_{x}(\Phi_{k})(z,y)\Big|^{2}dy\big|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
. Here the main difficulty comes from the fact that
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j}
is not an orthonormal sequence in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Let
G_{\mu}
be the fundamental solution of
(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}+\mu
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then

0<G_{\mu}(x)\leq G_{0}(x)=a_{N,s_{2}}|x|^{2s_{2}-N}\quad{\rm for}\ \,\mu\geq 0,

a_{N,s_{2}}=2^{-2s_{2}}\pi^{-\frac{N}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N-2s_{2}}{2})}{\Gamma(s_{2})}=2^{-2s_{2}}\pi^{-\frac{N}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N-2s_{2}}{2})}{\Gamma(s_{2}+1)}s_{2}:=\tilde{a}_{N,s_{2}}s_{2}.

(3.4)

Note that

\displaystyle\big((-\Delta)^{s_{2}}+\mu\big)^{-1}g=G_{\mu}\ast g\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}

for
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Using Fourier transform, we can see that

{\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\big((-\Delta)^{s_{2}}+\mu\big)^{-1}g\bigg)(z)=\big(|z|^{2s_{2}}+\mu\big)^{-1}{\mathcal{F}}(g(z)).

Consequently,
\big((-\Delta)^{s_{2}}+\mu\big)^{-1/2}g
can be defined as follows

\big((-\Delta)^{s_{2}}+\mu\big)^{-1/2}g:=\bigg[\big((-\Delta)^{s_{2}}+\mu\big)^{-1}\bigg]^{1/2}g={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\bigg[\big((|z|^{2s_{2}}+\mu)^{-1}\big)^{1/2}{\mathcal{F}}(g)\bigg].

Now, in view of Parseval’s relation and Bessel’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big|{\mathcal{F}}_{x}(\Phi_{k})(z,y)\Big|^{2}dy

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-N}\sum^{k}_{j=1}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi_{j}(x)\big(e^{{\rm i}x\cdot z}\chi_{\Omega}(x)\big)dx\Big|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\tilde{\phi}_{j}(y)|^{2}dy

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-N}\sum^{k}_{j=1}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big((-\Delta)^{s_{2}}+\mu\Big)_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi_{j}(x)\Big((-\Delta)^{s_{2}}+\mu\Big)_{x}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(e^{{\rm i}x\cdot z}\chi_{\Omega}(x)\big)dx\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-N}\sum^{k}_{j=1}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\tilde{\phi}_{j}(x)\Big((-\Delta)^{s_{2}}+\mu\Big)_{x}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(e^{{\rm i}x\cdot z}\chi_{\Omega}(x)\big)dx\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{-N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big|\Big((-\Delta)^{s_{2}}+\mu\Big)_{x}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(e^{{\rm i}x\cdot z}\chi_{\Omega}(x)\big)\Big|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\bigg[\Big((-\Delta)^{s_{2}}+\mu\Big)_{x}^{-1}\big(e^{{\rm i}x\cdot z}\chi_{\Omega}(x)\big)\bigg]\overline{\big(e^{{\rm i}x\cdot z}\chi_{\Omega}(x)\big)}dx

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{-N}\int_{\Omega}\Big|G_{\mu}\ast\big(e^{{\rm i}x\cdot z}\chi_{\Omega}(x)\big)\Big|dx

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{-N}\int_{\Omega}G_{0}\ast\chi_{\Omega}dx

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-N}a_{N,s_{2}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}|x-y|^{2s_{2}-N}dydx

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{-N}a_{N,s_{2}}|\Omega|\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{\Omega}|x-y|^{2s_{2}-N}dy.

Now we choose
r>0
such that
|\Omega|=\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{N}r^{N}
and using rearrangement inequality, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{\Omega}|x-y|^{2s_{2}-N}dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{r}(x)}|x-y|^{2s_{2}-N}dy

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{2s_{2}}r^{2s_{2}}=\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{2s_{2}}\bigg(\frac{N|\Omega|}{\omega_{N-1}}\bigg)^{\frac{2s_{2}}{N}}=a_{0}|\Omega|^{\frac{2s_{2}}{N}},

where

a_{0}=\frac{1}{2s_{2}}N^{\frac{2s_{2}}{N}}\omega_{{}_{N-1}}^{1-\frac{2s_{2}}{N}}.

(3.5)

As a consequence, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big|{\mathcal{F}}_{x}(\Phi_{k})(z,y)\Big|^{2}dy\leq(2\pi)^{-N}a_{N,s_{2}}a_{0}|\Omega|^{1+\frac{2s_{2}}{N}}=\tilde{a}_{N,s_{2}}\frac{N^{\frac{2s_{2}}{N}}\omega_{{}_{N-1}}^{1-\frac{2s_{2}}{N}}}{2^{N+1}\pi^{N}}|\Omega|^{1+\frac{2s_{2}}{N}}.

(3.6)

Meanwhile, using the definition of
\Phi_{k}
, it also follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|z|^{2s_{1}}\big|{\mathcal{F}}_{x}(\Phi_{k})(z,y)\big|^{2}dydz

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{k}(x,y)(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}_{x}\Phi_{k}(x,y)dydx

\displaystyle=\sum^{k}_{j=1}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{j}(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}\phi_{j}dx\int_{\Omega}|\tilde{\phi}_{j}(y)|^{2}dy

\displaystyle=\sum^{k}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}(\mu).

Now we apply Proposition
3.1
to the function

f(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|({\mathcal{F}}_{x}\Phi_{k})(z,y)|^{2}dy

with

M_{1}=(2\pi)^{-N}a_{N,s_{2}}a_{0}|\Omega|^{\frac{N+2s_{2}}{N}}\quad{\rm and}\quad M_{2}=\sum^{k}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}(\mu),

then we conclude that

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}M_{1}}{2s_{2}+N}\Big(\frac{(2s_{1}+N)M_{2}}{M_{1}\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}\Big)^{\frac{2s_{2}+N}{2s_{1}+N}}\cdot\left(1+\mu\frac{2s_{2}+N}{N}\Big(\frac{(2s_{1}+N)M_{2}}{M_{1}\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}\Big)^{-\frac{2s_{2}}{2s_{1}+N}}\right).

(3.7)

Case:

\displaystyle M_{2}\geq\frac{(2s_{2}+N)^{\frac{2s_{1}+N}{2s_{2}+N}}}{2s_{1}+N}(M_{1}\omega_{{}_{N-1}})^{-\frac{2(s_{1}-s_{2})}{2s_{2}+N}}k^{\frac{2s_{1}+N}{2s_{2}+N}}\Big[1-\frac{\mu(2s_{1}+N)}{N(2s_{2}+N)^{\frac{2s_{2}}{2s_{2}+N}}}(\omega_{N-1}M_{1})^{\frac{2s_{2}}{2s_{2}+N}}k^{-\frac{2s_{2}}{2s_{2}+N}}\Big],

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.
Denote

\Psi_{k}(x,y)=\sum^{k}_{j=1}\tilde{\phi}_{j}(x)\phi_{j}(y)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{t=0}^{r_{0}}\int_{\rho(y)=t}t^{s_{2}-2\beta}dSdt+C\int_{t=0}^{r_{0}}\int_{\rho(y)=t}t^{s_{2}-2s}dSdt

\displaystyle<\infty,

for our choice that
\frac{s_{2}}{2}<s<\beta<\frac{1+s_{2}}{2}
.
Similarly, we also show that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{c}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|v_{\sigma}(x)-v_{\sigma}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dydx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega^{c}}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|v_{\sigma}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dydx\leq\int_{\Omega^{c}}\int_{\Omega}\rho(y)^{2s_{2}}|x-y|^{-(N+2s)}dydx<\infty,

as
s<\frac{1+s_{2}}{2}
and for any
y\in\Omega
, it follows
\{x:\in\Omega^{c}:|x-y|<\rho(y)\}=\emptyset
.
Hence,
v_{\sigma}\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
for
s<\frac{1+s_{2}}{2}
.

Denote

\tilde{v}_{\sigma}(x,z)=(-\Delta)^{\frac{s_{2}}{2}}v_{\sigma}(x,z)

and

v_{\sigma,k}(z,y):=v_{\sigma}(y,z)-\sum^{k}_{j=1}\big\langle\tilde{v}_{\sigma}(.,z),\tilde{\phi}_{j}\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\phi_{j}(y).

Note that

(-\Delta)^{\frac{s_{2}}{2}}_{y}v_{\sigma,k}(z,y)={\tilde{v}_{\sigma}(y,z)}-\sum^{k}_{j=1}\big\langle\tilde{v}_{\sigma}(.,z),\tilde{\phi}_{j}\big\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})}\tilde{\phi}_{j}(y),

thus we get
v_{\sigma,k}(z,\cdot)\in{\mathbb{H}}_{0,k+1}(\Omega)

and
the Rayleigh-Ritz formula shows that

\displaystyle\lambda_{k+1}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s_{2}}{2}}_{y}v_{\sigma,k}(z,y)dy\Big|^{2}\leq\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{s_{1}}{2}}_{y}v_{\sigma,k}(z,y)dy\Big|^{2}

for any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\sigma>0
.
Thus we can conclude that

\lambda_{k+1}(\mu)\leq\inf_{\sigma>0}\frac{\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}_{y}v_{\sigma,k}(z,y)\,\overline{v_{\sigma,k}(z,y)}dydz}{\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}_{y}v_{\sigma,k}(z,y)\,\overline{v_{\sigma,k}(z,y)}dydz}.

An elementary calulation yields that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta)^{s_{2}}_{y}v_{\sigma,k}(z,y)\,\overline{v_{\sigma,k}(z,y)}dydz

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s_{2}}{2}}_{y}v_{\sigma,k}(z,y)\big|^{2}dydz

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s_{2}}{2}}_{y}v_{\sigma}(z,y)\big|^{2}dydz-\int_{B_{r}}\sum^{k}_{j=1}\Big|\big\langle\tilde{v}_{\sigma}(.,z),\tilde{\phi}_{j}\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\Big|^{2}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{\phi}_{j}^{2}dxdz

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}w_{\sigma}^{2}dxdz-\sum^{k}_{j=1}\int_{B_{r}}\Big|\big\langle\tilde{v}_{\sigma}(.,z),\tilde{\phi}_{j}\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\Big|^{2}dz

and

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}}}(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}_{y}v_{\sigma,k}(z,y)\,\overline{v_{\sigma,k}(z,y)}dydz

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big((-\Delta)^{s_{1}}_{y}v_{\sigma}(y,z)\,\overline{v_{\sigma}(y,z)}\Big)dydz-\int_{B_{r}}\sum^{k}_{j=1}\Big|\big\langle\tilde{v}_{\sigma}(.,z),\tilde{\phi}_{j}\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\Big|^{2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{j}(-\Delta)^{s_{1}}\phi_{j}dxdz

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big((-\Delta)^{s_{1}-s_{2}}_{y}(w_{\sigma}e^{{\rm i}x\cdot z})\Big)\,\overline{(w_{\sigma}e^{{\rm i}x\cdot z})}dydz-\sum^{k}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}(\mu)\int_{B_{r}}\Big|\big\langle\tilde{v}_{\sigma}(.,z),\tilde{\phi}_{j}\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\Big|^{2}dz

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big({\mathcal{L}}_{z}^{s_{1}-s_{2}}(w_{\sigma})\,\overline{w_{\sigma}e^{{\rm i}x\cdot z}}+w_{\sigma}(-\Delta)^{s_{1}-s_{2}}w_{\sigma}+|z|^{2(s_{1}-s_{2})}w_{\sigma}^{2}(x)\Big)dxdz

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum^{k}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}(\mu)\int_{B_{r}}\Big|\big\langle\tilde{v}_{\sigma}(.,z),\tilde{\phi}_{j}\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\Big|^{2}dz.

Since
\Omega
is
C^{2}
, there exists
t_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

|\partial\Omega_{t}|\leq 2|\partial\Omega|\quad{\rm for}\ t\in(0,t_{0}].

From (
4.5
),

\displaystyle|\Omega|\geq\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy>|\Omega_{\sigma}|\geq|\Omega|-2\sigma|\partial\Omega|>\frac{|\Omega|}{2},

when
\sigma
is small enough. We choose
r>r_{0}
for some
r_{0}>1
such that
r^{-\frac{s_{1}-s_{2}}{2}}=\sigma
satisfies the above relation and
\sigma<\frac{1}{4|\partial\Omega|}|\Omega|
. Therefore,

|\Omega|\geq\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy\geq|\Omega|-2r^{-\frac{s_{1}-s_{2}}{2}}|\partial\Omega|>\frac{|\Omega|}{2}.

(4.9)

Note that for
s=s_{1}-s_{2}
and
r>r_{0}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{N,s}}\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|{\mathcal{L}}^{s}_{z}w_{\sigma}\,\overline{w_{\sigma}e^{{\rm i}x\cdot z}}|dydz

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}r^{N+2s-1}}{\sigma(N+2s-1)(1-s)}|\Omega|+\frac{1}{\sigma}\varphi_{s}(r,R)|\Omega|+\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}^{2}r^{N}}{2sN}R^{-2s}|\Omega|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}|\Omega|}{(N+2s-1)(1-s)}r^{N+2s+\frac{s}{2}-1}+r^{\frac{s}{2}}\varphi_{s}(r,R)|\Omega|+\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}^{2}r^{N}}{2sN}R^{-2s}|\Omega|

\displaystyle\leq c_{1}(N,s_{1},s_{2})|\Omega|\,r^{N+\max\{2s+\frac{s}{2}-1,\,\frac{s}{2}\}},

where

\varphi_{s}(r,R)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{4\omega_{{}_{N-1}}r^{N+2s-1}}{(2s-1)(N+2s-1)}&{\rm if}\quad s\in(\frac{1}{2},1),\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{}\frac{4\omega_{{}_{N-1}}r^{N}(\log r+\log(4R))}{N}&{\rm{if}}\ \,s=\frac{1}{2},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{}\frac{4\omega_{{}_{N-1}}^{2}r^{N}}{N(1-2s)}(4R)^{1-2s}&{\rm{if}}\ \,s\in(0,\frac{1}{2}).\end{array}\right.

(4.10)

Moreover, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w_{\sigma}(-\Delta)^{s}w_{\sigma}|dydz

\displaystyle\leq 2c_{N,s}\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}^{2}r^{N}}{N}\sigma^{-2s}|\Omega|=c_{2}(N,s_{1},s_{2})|\Omega|r^{N+s^{2}}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|z|^{2s}w_{\sigma}^{2}(x)dydz=\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}r^{N+2s}}{N+2s}\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy.

Let

\delta_{0}=\max\big\{2s+\frac{s}{2}-1,\,\frac{s}{2},s^{2}\big\}=\frac{s}{2},

where we have used the hypothesis that
s_{1}<\frac{1+s_{2}}{2}
.

Let

\displaystyle P_{j}:

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}}\Big|\big\langle\tilde{v}_{\sigma}(.,z),\tilde{\phi}_{j}\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\Big|^{2}dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w_{\sigma}e^{{\rm i}x\cdot z}\tilde{\phi}_{j}\Big|^{2}dz\ \leq(2\pi)^{N}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\!\Big|{\mathcal{F}}(w_{\sigma}\tilde{\phi}_{j})\Big|^{2}dz

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=\ \,(2\pi)^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(w_{\sigma}\tilde{\phi}_{j})^{2}dx\ \,\leq\,(2\pi)^{N},

using the Parseval’s inequality. Note that

\displaystyle\lambda_{k+1}(0)\leq\frac{\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{N+2(s_{1}-s_{2})}r^{N+2(s_{1}-s_{2})}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy+O(1)|\Omega|r^{N+\frac{s}{2}}-\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}(0)P_{j}}{\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{N}r^{N}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy-\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{j=1}P_{j}},

and now taking

Q_{1}=\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{N+2(s_{1}-s_{2})}r^{N+2(s_{1}-s_{2})}\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy+O(1)|\Omega|r^{N+\frac{s}{2}}\quad{\rm and}\quad Q_{2}=\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{N}r^{N}\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy,

we have that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Q_{1}-\sum^{k}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}(0)P_{j}}{Q_{2}-\sum^{k}_{j=1}P_{j}}-\lambda_{k+1}(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{\big(Q_{1}-Q_{2}\lambda_{k+1}(0)\big)+\sum^{k}_{j=1}\Big(\lambda_{k+1}(0)-\lambda_{j}(0)\Big)P_{j}}{Q_{2}-\sum^{k}_{j=1}P_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\big(Q_{1}-Q_{2}\lambda_{k+1}(0)\big)+(2\pi)^{N}\sum^{k}_{j=1}\Big(\lambda_{k+1}(0)-\lambda_{j}(0)\Big)}{Q_{2}-(2\pi)^{N}k},

since
\lambda_{k+1}(0)\geq\lambda_{j}(\mu)
for
j<k+1
and
P_{j}\in\big(0,(2\pi)^{N}]
.
Therefore

0<\lambda_{k+1}(0)\leq\frac{\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{N+2(s_{1}-s_{2})}r^{N+2(s_{1}-s_{2})}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy+O(1)|\Omega|r^{N+\frac{s_{1}-s_{2}}{2}}-(2\pi)^{N}\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}(0)}{\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{N}r^{N}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy-(2\pi)^{N}k}.

There exists
k_{0}\geq 1
such that for
k\geq k_{0}
, we can choose
r>r_{0}
satisfying

\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{N}r^{N}\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy=(2\pi)^{N}(k+1).

(4.11)

As a consequence, using (
4.9
) and the above relation, we obtain for
k\geq k_{0}

\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{j=1}\lambda_{j}(0)

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{-N}\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{N+2(s_{1}-s_{2})}r^{N+2(s_{1}-s_{2})}\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy+c|\Omega|r^{N+\frac{s_{1}-s_{2}}{2}}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{2(s_{1}-s_{2})}\frac{N}{N+2(s_{1}-s_{2})}\Big(N^{-1}\omega_{{}_{N-1}}\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy\Big)^{-\frac{2(s_{1}-s_{2})}{N}}(k+1)^{1+2\frac{s_{1}-s_{2}}{N}}

\displaystyle\qquad+c_{3}k^{1+\frac{1}{2}\frac{s_{1}-s_{2}}{N}}

\displaystyle\leq b_{3}|\Omega|^{-\frac{2(s_{1}-s_{2})}{N}}k^{1+2\frac{s_{1}-s_{2}}{N}}+c_{4}k^{1+\frac{3}{2}\frac{s_{1}-s_{2}}{N}}+c_{3}k^{1+\frac{1}{2}\frac{s_{1}-s_{2}}{N}},

where
c_{3},c_{4}>0
depends on
N,s_{1},s_{2}
,
\Omega
,

(k+1)^{1+2\frac{s_{1}-s_{2}}{N}}\leq k^{1+2\frac{s_{1}-s_{2}}{N}}+c_{5}k^{2\frac{s_{1}-s_{2}}{N}}\quad{\rm for}\ \,k\geq 1

and
we use the fact that for
c_{7},c_{8}>0

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\Omega}w_{\sigma}^{2}(y)dy\Big)^{-\frac{2(s_{1}-s_{2})}{N}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(|\Omega|-2r^{-\frac{s_{1}-s_{2}}{2}}|\partial\Omega|\Big)^{-\frac{2(s_{1}-s_{2})}{N}}

\displaystyle\leq|\Omega|^{-\frac{2(s_{1}-s_{2})}{N}}+c_{6}r^{-\frac{s_{1}-s_{2}}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq|\Omega|^{-\frac{2(s_{1}-s_{2})}{N}}+c_{7}k^{-\frac{s_{1}-s_{2}}{2N}}.

Here for
k\leq k_{0}
, we only have to adjust the constant
c_{3}
or
c_{4}
.

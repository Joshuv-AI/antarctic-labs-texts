ON REAL ALGEBRAIC LINKS IN THE 3 3 -SPHERE ASSOCIATED WITH MIXED POLYNOMIALS
Raimundo N. Araújo dos Santos, Eder L. Sanchez Quiceno

Abstract
In this paper we construct new classes of mixed singularities that provide realizations of real algebraic links in the 3 3 -sphere. Classifications and characterizations of real algebraic links are still open. These new classes of mixed singularities may help to shed light on the Benedetti-Shiota conjecture, which state that any fibered link on the 3 3 -sphere is a real algebraic link. Keywords: real algebraic links, mixed singularity, Newton polyhedron, closure of braids. Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary 32S55, 57M25 ; Secondary 14M25, 14P05, 14P15, 14P25 , 32S05.



1  Introduction

A link
L
in
S^{3}
is fibered if the complement
S^{3}\setminus L
is a surface bundle over the circle
S^{1}
whose fiber is the interior of a compact, oriented surface

were characterized by Stallings in
[
22

\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus L)
is finitely generated. A very important class of links in

are those that arise from some type of singularities of germ of polynomial maps
(\mathbb{R}^{4},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{2},0)
, in the following sense: a polynomial map germ
f:(\mathbb{R}^{4},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{2},0)
has a
weakly isolated singularity
at the origin if we have
\Sigma_{f}\cap V_{f}=\{0\}
as a germ of sets, where
\Sigma_{f}:=\{x\in(\mathbb{R}^{4},0):\text{rank}(Jf(x))<2\}
is the singular set of
f
and
V_{f}:=f^{-1}(0)
both considered as germs of sets. In the particular case where
\Sigma_{f}=\{0\}
we say that

has an
isolated singularity
at the origin. According to
[
2
]
, a link

in
S^{3}
is called

) if there exist a polynomial map germ

with a weakly isolated (resp. an isolated) singularity at the origin and a positive real number
\rho_{0}
such that
L_{f_{L},\rho}:=V_{f_{L}}\cap S^{3}_{\rho}
is ambient isotopic to
L
in
S^{3}_{\rho}\approx S^{3}
for every
0<\rho\leq\rho_{0}
, where
S^{3}_{\rho}
is the sphere of radius
\rho
centered at the origin in

. In this case, such a polynomial map germ

is called a

(resp.

) of

. From this perspective in
[
1
]
Akbulut and King have proven that every link

in
S^{3}
admits a weak realization. However, they did not provide any sufficient condition for a weak realization to be a strong realization.

In a more general way, for a germ of complex analytic polynomials

with isolated singularity, the classical result due to Milnor
[
15
]
guarantees that there exists a small radius

such that for all

the map

\arg(g):=\dfrac{g}{|g|}:S^{2n-1}_{\rho}\setminus L_{g}\to S^{1}

is a smooth locally trivial fibration. Since the intersection

is transverse for all small radii

, the manifolds

for different values of

are isotopic. Therefore, the link type of

is well-defined and does not depend on the radius

For a real analytic map germ with an isolated singularity at the origin

, Milnor also proved that if

it is always a fibered link.

2  Mixed Polynomial and Newton Boundary

To know whether or not a germ of mixed polynomial
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
has an isolated singularity it is not an easy task. Recently combinatorial and analytic techniques have been developed throwing some light on that. Taking as references the works
[
4
,
5
,
16
,
17
]
, in this section we introduce the basic definitions and notations used in this paper for mixed polynomials.

We consider a germ of mixed polynomials at the origin
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}

f(z,\bar{z})=\sum_{j=1}^{l}c_{\nu_{j},\mu_{j}}z^{\nu_{j}}\bar{z}^{\mu_{j}}

(1)

where
z=(u,v)
,
\bar{z}=(\bar{u},\bar{v})
,
z^{\nu_{j}}={u}^{\nu_{j,1}}v^{\nu_{j,2}}
for
\nu_{j}=(\nu_{j,1},\nu_{j,2})
(respectively
\bar{z}^{\mu_{j}}=\bar{u}^{\mu_{j,1}}{\bar{v}}^{\mu_{j,2}}
for
\mu_{j}=(\mu_{j,1},\mu_{j,2})
).
The zero set
V_{f}=f^{-1}(0)
is called
the mixed hypersurface
. For a fixed variable
x\in\{u,v,\bar{u},\bar{v}\}
we say that
f
is a
x
-semiholomorphic polynomial
if
f
does not depend on the variable
\bar{x}
. With this definition we have that
f
is a holomorphic polynomial if and only if
f
is both
u
-semiholomorphic and
v
-semiholomorphic polynomial. Analogously,
f
is anti-holomorphic polynomial if and only if
f
is both
\bar{u}
-semiholomorphic and
\bar{v}
-semiholomorphic polynomial.

Let
M_{\nu,\mu}=z^{\nu}\bar{z}^{\mu}
be a mixed monomial where
\nu=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2}),\ \mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
and let
P=\mathstrut^{t}(p_{1},p_{2})
be a weight vector. We define the
radial degree
of
M_{\nu,\mu}
,
rdeg_{P}M_{\nu,\mu}
with respect to
P
and the
polar degree
of
M_{\nu,\mu}
,
pdeg_{P}M_{\nu,\mu}
with respect to
P
by

rdeg_{P}M_{\nu,\mu}=\sum^{2}_{j=1}p_{j}(\nu_{j}+\mu_{j})\text{ and }pdeg_{P}M_{\nu,\mu}=\sum^{2}_{j=1}p_{j}(\nu_{j}-\mu_{j}).

Definition 2.1
.

We say

is a radially weighted homogeneous mixed polynomial (for short, radial mixed polynomial) of radial weight type
(P;d_{r})
if

rdeg_{P}M_{\nu_{j},\mu_{j}}=d_{r}\text{ for }j=1,2,\dots,l\text{ with }c_{\nu_{j},\mu_{j}}\neq 0

and polar weighted homogeneous of polar weight type
(Q;d_{p})
if

pdeg_{Q}M_{\nu_{j},\mu_{j}}=d_{p}\text{ for }j=1,2,\dots,l\text{ with }c_{\nu_{j},\mu_{j}}\neq 0.

If
f(z,\bar{z})
is both radially and polar weighted homogeneous of radial weight type
(P;d_{r})
and polar weight type
(Q;d_{p})
then we say that
f(z,\bar{z})
is a weighted homogeneous mixed polynomial.

If
d_{p}\neq 0
then
f
is called a polar non-zero weighted homogeneous polynomial. Moreover, if for a positive integer

the product

has non-zero polar degree then

is called of

-compatible polar non-zero weighted homogeneous polynomial.

2.1  Newton boundary and non-degeneracy of mixed polynomials.

2.2  Newton boundary for products of mixed polynomials

For a mixed polynomial
f:(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)

\Gamma(f)
is formed by a finite union of 0-faces and 1-faces. Thus, we can associate to the set of faces of

a finite sequence of positive weight vectors whose entries are coprimes, which will be called
the sequence of positive weight vector of

Denote by
N^{+}:=\{P=\mathstrut^{t}(p_{1},p_{2})|\ p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathbb{N}\}

P=\mathstrut^{t}(p_{1},p_{2}),Q=\mathstrut^{t}(q_{1},q_{2})\in N^{+}

P\succ Q\text{ if and only if }\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}>\frac{q_{1}}{q_{2}}.

For the Newton boundary
\Gamma(f)

P_{1}\succ P_{2}\succ\cdots\succ P_{\ell_{f}}

. Note that a weight vector

is non-degenerate (resp. strongly non-degenerate) if and only if

3.1  Mixed singularities outside of the axes

Let

that

For the second class of face functions, with

(6)

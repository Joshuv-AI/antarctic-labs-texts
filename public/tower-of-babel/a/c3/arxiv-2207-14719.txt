Classification of Solvable Lie algebras whose non-trivial Coadjoint Orbits of simply connected Lie groups are all of Codimension 2 Thanks: Received by the editors on Month/Day/Year. Accepted for publication on Month/Day/Year. Handling Editor: Name of Handling Editor. Corresponding Author: Vu Anh Le
Hieu Van Ha, Vu Anh Le, Tu Thi Cam Nguyen, Hoa Duong Quang

Abstract
We give a classification of real solvable Lie algebras whose non-trivial coadjoint orbits of corresponding simply connected Lie groups are all of codimension 2. These Lie algebras belong to a well-known class, called the class of MD-algebras.

Solvable, Lie groups, Classification of Lie algebras, MD-algebras.

1
Introduction

The problem of the classification of Lie algebras (as well as Lie groups) has received much attentions since the early 20
{}^{\textnormal{th}}
century. However, this is still an open problem. By Levi’s decomposition and the Cartan’s theorem, we know that the problem of classification of Lie algebras over any field of characteristic zero are reduced to the problem of classification of solvable ones. However, until now, there is no a complete classification of
n
dimensional solvable Lie algebras if
n\geq 7
. And this classification problem seems to be impossible to solve, unless there is a suitable change on the definition of term “classification” or there is a completely new method to classify those Lie algebras
[
4
]
.

As we know, the Lie algebra of a (simply connected) Lie group is commutative if and only if all of its coadjoint orbits are trivial (or of dimension 0). However, Lie groups which has a non-trivial coadjoint orbit are much more complicated. In 1980, while searching for the class of Lie groups whose
C^{*}
-algebra can be characterized by BDF
K
-functions, Do Ngoc Diep proposed to study a class of Lie groups whose non-trivial coadjoint orbits have the same dimension
[
5
]
. He named this class as MD-class. Any Lie group belongs to this class is called an MD-group and the Lie algebra of any MD-group is called an MD-algebra.

It can be said that Vuong Manh Son and Ho Huu Viet were the authors who faced the problem of classification MD-algebras (as well as MD-groups) firstly. In 1984, they gave not only the classification of MD-groups whose non-trivial coadjoint orbits are of the same dimension as the group but also some important characteristics of this class. For example, they showed that any non-commutative MD-algebra is either 1-step solvable or 2-step solvable, i.e. the second derived algebra is commutative
[
17
]
. Afterward, from 1990, Vu A. L. and Hieu V. H. (the authors of this paper) gave the classification (up to isomorphic) of some subclasses; including all MD-algebras of dimension 4
[
20
]
, all MD-algebras of dimension 5
[
23
,
21
]
, all MD-algebras which have the first derived ideal of dimension 1 or codimension 1
[
22
]
.

Besides, a list of all simply connected Lie groups whose coadjoint orbits are of dimension up to 2 was given by D. Arnal et al. in 1995
[
1
]
. In 2019, Michel Goze and Elisabeth Remm used Cartan class to give the classification of all Lie algebras that all non-trivial coadjoint orbits of corresponding Lie groups are of dimension 4
[
7
]
. Remark that the Lie algebras classified in
[
1
]
and
[
7
]
are all MD-algebras in terms of Diep. Moreover, Goze and Remm also gave some characteristics of the class of MD-algebras whose non-trivial coadjoint orbits are of codimension 1. Recently, in an earlier article
[
8
]
, we have classified all real solvable Lie algebras whose non-trivial coadjoint orbits are of codimension 1. Now, we will give the complete classification of real solvable Lie algebras whose non-trivial coadjoint orbits are of codimension 2.

The paper is organized into 6 sections, including this introduction. In Section
2
, we will recall some basic preliminary concepts, notations and properties which will be used throughout the paper. In Section
3
and Section
4
, we will give the classification of 1-step solvable Lie algebras whose non-trivial coadjoint orbits are of codimension 2 [Theorem
3.14
, Theorem
4.23
]. In Section
5
, we will study the case of such 2-step solvable Lie algebras [Theorem
5.24
], and complete the results in Sections
3
,
4
. Tables containing a list of results are provided in the last section.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a Lie group and let
\mathcal{G}
be its Lie algebra. If

denotes the

of

\displaystyle K:

\displaystyle\quad G\rightarrow\text{Aut}(\mathcal{G}^{*})

\displaystyle\quad g\mapsto K_{g}

defined by

K_{g}(F)(x)=F(\text{Ad}(g^{-1})(x))\text{ for }F\in\mathcal{G}^{*},x\in\mathcal{G}.

is called the

of

in

. Each orbit of the coadjoint representation of

For each

\Omega_{F}=\{K_{g}(F):g\in G\}.

The dimension of each coadjoint orbit is determined via the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
10
]
Let
F
be any element in
\mathcal{G}^{*}
. If
\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\}
is a basis of
\mathcal{G}
then

\dim\Omega_{F}=\rank{\bigl(F([x_{i},x_{j}])\bigr)_{n\times n}}.

Remark 2.3
.

The dimension of each K-orbit
\Omega_{F}
is always even for every
F\in\mathcal{G}^{*}
. Moreover,
\dim\Omega_{F}>0
if and only if
F|_{\mathcal{G}^{1}}\neq 0
.

As mentioned in previous section, this paper concerns with Lie algebras whose non-trivial coadjoint orbits are all of the same dimension.

Definition 2.4
.

[
5
,
17
]

An
MD-group
is a finite-dimensional, simply connected and solvable Lie group whose non-trivial coadjoint orbits are of the same dimension. The Lie algebra of an MD-group is called an
MD-algebra
. An MD-algebra
\mathcal{G}
is called an MD
{}_{k}(n)
-algebra if
\dim\mathcal{G}=n
and the same dimension of non-trivial coadjoint orbits is equal to
k

One of the most interesting characteristics on this class is about the degree of solvability which is proven by Son & Viet
[
17
]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

[
17
]

If
\mathcal{G}
is an MD-algebra then the degree of solvability is at most 2, i.e.
\mathcal{G}^{3}=\{0\}
.

Therefore, the problem of classification of MD-algebras falls naturally into two parts:
(1) the classification of 1-step solvable ones, and (2) the classification of 2-step solvable ones. However, if
\mathcal{G}
is a 2-step solvable MD-algebra then
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{G}^{2}
is a 1-step solvable MD-algebra
[
8
, Theorem 3.5]
. Hence, we should firstly study some interesting properties of 1-step solvable MD-algebras.

Proposition 2.6
.

[
8
]

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a 1-step solvable Lie algebra of dimension
n
such that its non-trivial coadjoint orbits are all of codimension
k
. If
\dim\mathcal{G}^{1}\geq n-k+1
then

\mathcal{G}
is isomorphic to the semi-direct product
\mathcal{L}\oplus_{\rho}\mathcal{G}^{1}
where
\mathcal{L}
is a commutative sub-algebra of
\mathcal{G}
and
\rho
is defined by

\begin{array}[]{llll}\rho:&\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{G}^{1}&\rightarrow&\mathcal{G}^{1}\\
&(x,y)&\mapsto&[x,y].\end{array}

(2.1)

Moreover, if
\mathcal{G}
is 1-step solvable then
[[x,y],z]=0
for every
x,y\in\mathcal{G},z\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
. It follows immediately from the Jacobi identity that
\textnormal{ad}^{1}_{x}\textnormal{ad}^{1}_{y}=\textnormal{ad}^{1}_{y}\textnormal{ad}^{1}_{x}
for every
x,y\in\mathcal{G}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is 1-step solvable then
\{\textnormal{ad}^{1}_{x}:x\in\mathcal{G}\}
is a family of commuting endomorphisms.

It is well-known that an arbitrary set of commuting matrices over an algebraic closed field may be simultaneously brought to triangular form by a unitary similarity
[
13
,
14
]
. A similar version for the case of the real field is given in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a set of commuting real matrices of the same size. Then
\mathcal{S}
is block simultaneously triangularizable in which the maximal size of each block is 2. In other words, there is a non-singular real matrix
T
so that

T\mathcal{S}T^{-1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{llllllll}*_{2\times 2}&\\
&\ddots&&&\textnormal{\huge*}\\
&&*_{2\times 2}\\
&&&*\\
&\textnormal{\huge 0}&&&\ddots\\
&&&&&*\end{array}\right]

where each block
*_{2\times 2}
is of the form
\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
-b&a\end{bmatrix}
for some
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
(

The following lemma is a straightforward but useful consequence of Propositions

Lemma 2.9

Let
\mathcal{G}

-algebra such that

Corollary 4.18
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be an
\text{MD}_{n-2}(n)
-algebra with
n\geq 6

•

If
\dim\mathcal{G}^{1}=1

\mathcal{G}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{h}_{2m+1}\oplus\mathbb{R}
where

•

If
\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dim\mathcal{G}^{1}=2\\
\mathcal{G}^{1}\nsubseteq C(\mathcal{G})\end{array}\right.

then
\mathcal{G}
is isomorphic to
\textnormal{aff}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2}

Now, we will investigate the remaining case:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dim\mathcal{G}^{1}=2\\
\mathcal{G}^{1}\subseteq C(\mathcal{G}).\end{array}\right.

Firstly, it is easy to check that
\mathcal{G}^{1}\subseteq C(\mathcal{G})
if and only if
\mathcal{G}
is 2-step nilpotent, i.e.
\mathcal{G}_{2}:=[[\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G}],\mathcal{G}]
is trivial (a 2-step nilpotent Lie algebra is also called a metabelian Lie algebra).

Because
\mathcal{G}
is 2-step nilpotent with
\dim\mathcal{G}^{1}=2

\mathfrak{b}:=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
of
\mathcal{G}
such that
\mathcal{G}^{1}=\langle x_{n-1},x_{n}\rangle
and
[x_{i},x_{n-1}]=[x_{i},x_{n}]=0
for all
i

\mathcal{G}
determines a pair of
(n-2)\times(n-2)
skew-symmetric matrices
(M,N)
defined by

(M)_{ij}:=x_{n-1}^{*}([x_{i},x_{j}]);(N)_{ij}:=x_{n}^{*}([x_{i},x_{j}]).

(4.9)

Since
\dim\mathcal{G}^{1}=2

M
and
N
are linearly independent in the sense that there is no
(0,0)\neq(\alpha,\beta)
such that
\alpha M+\beta N=\boldsymbol{0}

(M,N)
are called the

of
\mathcal{G}
with respect to the basis
\mathfrak{b}
(we also say that
\mathcal{G}
is associated by the matrices
(M,N)
with respect to
\mathfrak{b}
). Conversely, Let
(M,N)
be any pair of skew-symmetric matrices of size
(n-2)\times(n-2)
which are linearly independent. Then we can define a Lie algebra
\mathcal{G}
of dimension
n

\mathcal{G}
is spanned by a basis
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
, and the Lie brackets are defined via that basis as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}[x_{i},x_{n-1}]=[x_{i},x_{n}]=0&1\leq i\leq n\\
{}[x_{i},x_{j}]=(M)_{ij}x_{n-1}+(N)_{ij}x_{n}&1\leq i,j\leq n-2.\end{array}\right.

Proposition 4.19
.

[

\mathcal{G}

\mathcal{G}

T\cdot\langle M,N\rangle\cdot T^{t}=\langle M^{\prime},N^{\prime}\rangle

(which does not depend on

[

5
Two-step solvable
\boldsymbol{\text{MD}_{n-2}(n)}
-algebras

Finally, to complete the classification of
\text{MD}_{n-2}(n)
-algebras, we only need to classify 2-step solvable
\text{MD}_{n-2}(n)
-algebras. Surprising, such a Lie algebra is decomposable and has dimension exactly 6.

Theorem 5.24
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a 2-step solvable real Lie-algebra whose non-trivial coadjoint orbits are all of codimension 2. Then
\mathcal{G}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathfrak{s}_{5,45}
.

Proof.

Recall that for every
x,y,z\in\mathcal{G}
, we have:

\left[[x,y],z\right]=\left[x,[y,z]\right]-\left[y,[x,z]\right].

It follows that

\textnormal{ad}_{x}\textnormal{ad}_{y}-\textnormal{ad}_{y}\textnormal{ad}_{x}=\textnormal{ad}_{[x,y]}.

Hence, for every
x\in\mathcal{G}^{1}
, we have

\text{trace}(\textnormal{ad}_{x})=\text{trace}(\textnormal{ad}^{1}_{x})=\text{trace}(\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{x})=0.

(5.12)

According to Theorem 3.5 in
[
8
]
,
1\leq\dim\mathcal{G}^{2}\leq 2
. Therefore, we will divide the proof into two cases:

•

Case 1:
\boldsymbol{\dim\mathcal{G}^{2}=2}
.
If so,
\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{G}^{2}
is a 1-step solvable Lie algebra whose non-trivial coadjoint orbits are all of the same dimension as
\mathcal{H}

[
8
, Theorem 3.5]
. In the other words,
\mathcal{H}
is an
\text{MD}_{n}(n)
-algebra. According to Proposition
2.12
,
\mathcal{H}
is isomorphic to either
\textnormal{aff}(\mathbb{R})
or
\textnormal{aff}(\mathbb{C})
. Since
\dim\mathcal{G}\geq 6
,
\mathcal{H}\cong\textnormal{aff}(\mathbb{C})
. It implies the existence of a basis
\mathfrak{b}:=\{x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2},z_{1},z_{2}\}
of
\mathcal{G}
such that:

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{1}=

\displaystyle\langle y_{1},y_{2},z_{1},z_{2}\rangle,\quad\mathcal{G}^{2}=\langle z_{1},z_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=

\displaystyle\langle\overline{x_{1}},\overline{x_{2}},\overline{y_{1}},\overline{y_{2}}\rangle\cong\text{aff}(\mathbb{C}),

where

\displaystyle[\overline{x_{1}},\overline{y_{1}}]=\overline{y_{1}},[\overline{x_{1}},\overline{y_{2}}]=\overline{y_{2}}\text{ and }[\overline{x_{2}},\overline{y_{1}}]=\overline{y_{2}},[\overline{x_{2}},\overline{y_{2}}]=-\overline{y_{1}}.

Since
\mathcal{G}^{1}
and
\mathcal{G}^{2}
are both ideals of
\mathcal{G}
, the Lie brackets in
\mathcal{G}
can be determined as follows:

\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr&x_{1}&x_{2}&y_{1}&y_{2}&z_{1}&z_{2}\\
\hline\cr x_{1}&0&\lambda_{1}z_{1}+\lambda_{2}z_{2}&y_{1}+\lambda_{3}z_{1}+\lambda_{4}z_{2}&y_{2}+\lambda_{5}z_{1}+\lambda_{6}z_{2}&\lambda_{7}z_{1}+\lambda_{8}z_{2}&\lambda_{9}z_{1}+\lambda_{10}z_{2}\\
\hline\cr x_{2}&&0&y_{2}+\lambda_{11}z_{1}+\lambda_{12}z_{2}&-y_{1}+\lambda_{13}z_{1}+\lambda_{14}z_{2}&\lambda_{15}z_{1}+\lambda_{16}z_{2}&\lambda_{17}z_{1}+\lambda_{18}z_{2}\\
\hline\cr y_{1}&&&0&\lambda_{19}z_{1}+\lambda_{20}z_{2}&\lambda_{21}z_{1}+\lambda_{22}z_{2}&\lambda_{23}z_{1}+\lambda_{24}z_{2}\\
\hline\cr y_{2}&&&&0&\lambda_{25}z_{1}+\lambda_{26}z_{2}&\lambda_{27}z_{1}+\lambda_{28}z_{2}\\
\hline\cr z_{1}&&&&&0&0\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

Since
\mathcal{G}^{2}
is commutative, we can obtain directly from the Jacobi identity that
\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{1}}\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{2}}=\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{2}}\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{1}}
. By Proposition
2.8
, we can assume that
[\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{1}}]_{\mathfrak{b}}
and
[\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{2}}]_{\mathfrak{b}}
are both either of the diagonal form or of the form
aI+bJ
.
Without loss of generality, we can assume that

\text{either}\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}[\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{1}}]_{\mathfrak{b}}=\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
0&b\end{bmatrix}\\
{}[\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{2}}]_{\mathfrak{b}}=\begin{bmatrix}c&0\\
0&d\end{bmatrix}\end{array}\right.\quad\text{or}\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}[\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{1}}]_{\mathfrak{b}}=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
-b&a\end{bmatrix}\\
{}[\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{2}}]_{\mathfrak{b}}=\begin{bmatrix}c&d\\
-d&c\end{bmatrix}.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, it follows from the equation (
5.12
) that

\textnormal{trace}(\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{1}})=\textnormal{trace}(\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{2}})=0.

It turns out that

\text{either}\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}[\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{1}}]_{\mathfrak{b}}=\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
0&-a\end{bmatrix}\\
{}[\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{2}}]_{\mathfrak{b}}=\begin{bmatrix}c&0\\
0&-c\end{bmatrix}\end{array}\right.\quad\text{or}\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}[\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{1}}]_{\mathfrak{b}}=\begin{bmatrix}0&b\\
-b&0\end{bmatrix}\\
{}[\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{2}}]_{\mathfrak{b}}=\begin{bmatrix}0&d\\
-d&0\end{bmatrix}.\end{array}\right.

In both cases, there is
(0,0)\neq(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
so that
\lambda\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{1}}+\mu\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{2}}=\boldsymbol{0}
. Now, by applying the Jacobi identity to
(x_{2},\lambda y_{1}+\mu y_{2},z)
for any
z\in\mathcal{G}^{2}
, we easily see that

\boldsymbol{0}=\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{x_{2}}\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{\lambda y_{1}+\mu y_{2}}-\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{\lambda y_{1}+\mu y_{2}}\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{x_{2}}=\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{[x_{2},\lambda y_{1}+\mu y_{2}]}=-\mu\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{1}}+\lambda\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{2}}.

Therefore,

\lambda\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{1}}+\mu\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{2}}=-\mu\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{1}}+\lambda\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{2}}=\boldsymbol{0}.

This clearly forces
\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{1}}=\textnormal{ad}^{2}_{y_{2}}=\boldsymbol{0}
, and consequently
\mathcal{G}^{2}
is spanned by
\{[y_{1},y_{2}]\}
, a contradiction to
\dim\mathcal{G}^{2}=2
. Hence, this case is excluded.

•

Case 2:
\boldsymbol{\dim\mathcal{G}^{2}=1}
.
If so,
\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{G}^{2}
is a 1-step solvable Lie-algebra whose non-zero coadjoint orbits are of codimension 1. It follows from Proposition
2.11
that
\mathcal{H}
is isomorphic to one of the followings:
\mathfrak{h}_{2m+1}
,
\mathbb{R}\oplus\textnormal{aff}(\mathbb{C})
. Furthermore, if
\mathcal{H}\cong\mathfrak{h}_{2m+1}
then
\dim\mathcal{G}^{1}=2
and
\dim\mathcal{G}^{2}=1
. This is impossible because
\mathcal{G}^{1}
is nilpotent.
Hence,
\mathcal{H}\cong\mathbb{R}\oplus\textnormal{aff}(\mathbb{C})
.

Equivalently, we can fix a basis
\{x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},z\}
of
\mathcal{G}
so that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{G}^{1}=&\langle y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\rangle,\quad\mathcal{G}^{2}=\langle z\rangle,\\
\mathcal{H}=&\langle\overline{y_{3}}\rangle\oplus\langle\overline{x_{1}},\overline{x_{2}},\overline{y_{1}},\overline{y_{2}}\rangle\end{array}\right.

where the Lie brackets in
\mathcal{H}
are the same as those in
\mathbb{R}\oplus\textnormal{aff}(\mathbb{C})
, i.e.

\displaystyle[\overline{x_{1}},\overline{y_{1}}]=\overline{y_{1}},[\overline{x_{1}},\overline{y_{2}}]=\overline{y_{2}}\text{ and }[\overline{x_{2}},\overline{y_{1}}]=\overline{y_{2}},[\overline{x_{2}},\overline{y_{2}}]=-\overline{y_{1}}.

It implies that the Lie brackets in
\mathcal{G}
must have the form

\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline\cr&x_{1}&x_{2}&y_{1}&y_{2}&y_{3}&z\\
\hline\cr x_{1}&&\lambda_{1}z&y_{1}+\lambda_{2}z&y_{2}+\lambda_{3}z&\lambda_{4}z&\lambda_{5}z\\
\hline\cr x_{2}&&&y_{2}+\lambda_{6}z&-y_{1}+\lambda_{7}z&\lambda_{8}z&\lambda_{9}z\\
\hline\cr y_{1}&&&&\lambda_{10}z&\lambda_{11}z&\lambda_{12}z\\
\hline\cr y_{2}&&&&&\lambda_{13}z&\lambda_{14}z\\
\hline\cr y_{3}&&&&&&\lambda_{15}z\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

If so, it follows from the equation (
5.12
) that

\lambda_{12}=\lambda_{14}=0.

This means
[y_{1},z]=[y_{2},z]=0
. Because
\mathcal{G}^{2}\neq\{0\}
, we must have
\lambda_{10}\neq 0
. By basis changing
z\rightarrow\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{10}}z
, we may assume
\lambda_{10}=1
.

Now, by checking the Jacobi identity to the following triples

(x_{1},y_{1},y_{2})
;
(x_{2},y_{1},y_{2})
;
(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})
;
(x_{1},x_{2},y_{3})
;
(x_{1},y_{1},y_{3})
; and
(x_{1},y_{2},y_{3})
; we obtain

\lambda_{5}=2,\lambda_{9}=\lambda_{15}=2\lambda_{8}+\lambda_{1}\lambda_{15}=\lambda_{11}+\lambda_{2}\lambda_{15}=\lambda_{13}+\lambda_{3}\lambda_{15}=0

Hence,

\lambda_{5}=2,\lambda_{8}=\lambda_{9}=\lambda_{11}=\lambda_{12}=\lambda_{13}=\lambda_{14}=\lambda_{15}=0.

By basis changing
y_{3}\rightarrow y_{3}-\dfrac{\lambda_{4}}{2}z
if necessary, we get
\mathcal{G}
decomposable. In the other words,
\mathcal{G}
is isomorphic to a direct sum of
\mathbb{R}
with a Lie algebra
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
. Since
\mathcal{G}
is 2-step solvable, so is
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
. Furthermore, non-zero coadjoint orbits of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{G}
have the same dimension
[
8
, Theorem 3.1]
. In the other words,
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is a 2-step solvable MD-algebra whose non-trivial coadjoint orbits are all of codimension 1. According to Proposition
2.11
,
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
must be isomorphic to
\mathfrak{s}_{5,45}
. Equivalently,
\mathcal{G}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathfrak{s}_{5,45}
. This completes the proof.

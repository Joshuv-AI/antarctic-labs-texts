Smooth weighted zero-sum constants
Krishnendu Paul, Shameek Paul

Abstract
Let A ⊆ ℤ n A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n} be a weight-set and S = ( x 1 , x 2 , … , x k ) S=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k}) be a sequence in ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} . We say that S S is a smooth A A -weighted zero-sum sequence if there exists ( a 1 , … , a k ) ∈ A k (a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\in A^{k} such that we have a 1 ​ x 1 + ⋯ + a k ​ x k = 0 a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}=0 and a 1 + ⋯ + a k = 0 a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}=0 . It is easy to see that if S S is a smooth A A -weighted zero-sum sequence, then for every y ∈ ℤ n y\in\mathbb{Z}_{n} the sequence S + y = ( x 1 + y , … , x k + y ) S+y=(x_{1}+y,\ldots,x_{k}+y) is also a smooth A A -weighted zero-sum sequence. From the well known EGZ-theorem it follows that if S S has length at least 2 ​ n − 1 2n-1 , then S S has a smooth A A -weighted zero-sum subsequence of length n n . The constant E ¯ A \bar{E}_{A} is defined to be the smallest positive integer k k such that any sequence of length k k in ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} has a smooth A A -weighted zero-sum subsequence of length n n . A sequence in ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} of length E ¯ A − 1 \bar{E}_{A}-1 which does not have any smooth A A -weighted zero-sum subsequence of length n n is called an E ¯ \bar{E} -extremal sequence for A A . For every n n we consider the weight-sets { 1 } \{1\} and ℤ n ∖ { 0 } \mathbb{Z}_{n}\setminus\{0\} . When n n is an odd prime p p we consider the weight-set Q p Q_{p} of all non-zero quadratic residues. We also study the related constants C ¯ A \bar{C}_{A} and D ¯ A \bar{D}_{A} .

Theorem 2.2

Observation 3.1
.

Suppose there exist two terms of a sequence
S
which are equal. Then there exists
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
such that
T=(x,x)
is a subsequence of
S
. As
1x+(-1)x=0
, it follows that
T
is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum subsequence.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
S=(x,y,z)
be a sequence of distinct terms of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. Then
S
is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

Proof.

Let
a=y-z,b=z-x
, and
c=x-y
. As
x,y
, and
z
are distinct, we have that
a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
. Also, we see that
ax+by+cz=0
and
a+b+c=0
. Thus, it follows that
S
is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

For every
n
we have that
\bar{D}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}}=3
.

Proof.

Let
S=(x,y,z)
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. By Observation

we can assume that all the terms of

are distinct. By Lemma
3.2
we see that
S
is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. Hence, we see that
\bar{D}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}}\leq 3
. As the sequence
(0,1)
does not have any smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum subsequence it follows that
\bar{D}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}}\geq 3
.

Theorem 3.4
.

For every
n
we have that
\bar{C}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}}=4
.

Proof.

Let

be a sequence in

. By Observation

are equal. In addition, if either

or

-weighted zero-sum subsequence of

is a smooth

-weighted zero-sum sequence, as

. As the sequence

Theorem 3.7
.

For every
n\neq 3
we have that
\bar{E}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}}=n+1
and
\bar{E}_{\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{\prime}}=5
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
\bar{E}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\prime}}=3
.
Let
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{5})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
. If
S
has at least three distinct terms, by Lemma
3.2
we get a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of length three. If
S
has at most two distinct terms, there exists
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
such that
(x,x,x)
is a subsequence of
S
. This is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of length three, as
x+x+x=0
. Thus, we see that
\bar{E}_{\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{\prime}}\leq 5
. We can check that the sequence
(0,0,1,1)
in
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
does not have any smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of length three and so
\bar{E}_{\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{\prime}}\geq 5
.

The sequence
(\overbrace{0,\ldots,0}^{\text{$n-1$ times}},1)
does not have any smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of length
n
. So it follows that for every
n
we have
\bar{E}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}}\geq n+1
.

Let
n\geq 4
and
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. By Remark
1.1
we may assume that the term which is repeated the most number of times in

is zero. Thus, by the pigeonhole principle we see that at least two terms of

are equal to zero.

Case 1
: Suppose at least three terms of
S
are equal to zero.

In
[
2
]
it is shown that

. As
S
has length

, it follows that there is a subsequence
T
of

having length

which is a
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. As at least three terms of

are equal to zero, we see that at least two terms of

are equal to zero. By permuting the terms of
S
, we may assume that
T=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and that
x_{n-1}=x_{n}=0
. As

is a
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum sequence, there exist
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
such that

. We can replace

and

by any two elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
.
Let

. If
c=0
, we let
a_{n-1}=1
and
a_{n}=-1
. If

, we let

and

where

. Thus, it follows that
T
is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

Case 2
: Suppose exactly two terms of
S
are zero.

As zero is repeated the most number of times in
S
, every element of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is repeated at most twice in

. Let

be the subsequence of

obtained by removing pairs of repeated non-zero terms as well as triples of distinct non-zero terms from

. It follows that

has at most two distinct non-zero terms and exactly two zeroes. From Observation
3.1
and Lemma

is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

If

has two distinct non-zero terms

we see that
(x,y,0)
is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum subsequence. As the length of

is four, it follows that

is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of

of length
n
. If

has exactly one non-zero term, then

is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of

of length
n
.

So we can assume that
T^{\prime}
has no non-zero term. Then we see that
T
has length
n-1
. Recall that
T
is made up of smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum subsequences which are either pairs of repeated non-zero terms or triples of distinct non-zero terms from
S
. Suppose we cannot express
T
as a combination of pairs of repeated non-zero terms.

Then
T
has a subsequence
(x,y,z)
of three distinct non-zero terms such that
T-(x,y,z)
is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. If
x-y=z
, then
x-y-z+0=0
. If
x-y\neq z
, then
a=z
and
b=y-x
are non-zero. Also, we have that
a(x-y)+bz=0
and so
ax-ay+bz-b\,0=0
. Thus, in both cases
(x,y,z,0)
is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. Hence, the sequence
T+(0)
is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

So we may assume that we can express
T
as a combination of pairs of repeated non-zero terms. As
T^{\prime}
has no non-zero term, we have that
S=T+(0,0)
.

Suppose all the terms of
T
are units. As
n\geq 4
, it follows that there exist distinct units
x
and
y
, both of which occur twice in
T
. Let
T_{1}
denote the subsequence of
T
having length four in which both
x
and
y
occur twice. Now
T-T_{1}
is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. Also, we see that
T_{1}+(0)
is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum sequence since

2x^{-1}x-x^{-1}x-2y^{-1}y+y^{-1}y+(y^{-1}-x^{-1})\,0=0,

Hence, it follows that
T+(0)
is also a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

Suppose
T
has a term
x
which is a zero-divisor. Let
y\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
such that
xy=0
. As
(1-y)x-x=0
, it follows that
(x,x,0)
is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. Also, we have that
T-(x,x)
is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. Hence, we see that
T+(0)
is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.
∎

4
Extremal sequences for
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}

Theorem 4.1
.

For every
n
a sequence
S
in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is a
\bar{D}
-extremal sequence for
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
if and only if
S
is equivalent to the sequence
(0,1)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.3
we have
\bar{D}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}}=3
. It follows that a
\bar{D}
-extremal sequence for
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
has length two. Let
S=(x,y)
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. We observe that a sequence of length one cannot be a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

Suppose
x-y
is not a unit. Then there exists
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
such that
a(x-y)=0
and so
S
is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. So if
S
is a
\bar{D}
-extremal sequence for
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
, then it follows that
x-y
is a unit and so
S
is equivalent to the sequence
(0,1)
.

It is easy to see that the sequence
(0,1)
is a
\bar{D}
-extremal sequence for
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
. Thus, by Remark
1.3
it follows that any sequence which is equivalent to
(0,1)
is also a
\bar{D}
-extremal sequence for
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

For every
n
a sequence
S
in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is a
\bar{C}
-extremal sequence for
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
if and only if
S
is order-equivalent to the sequence
(0,1,0)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.4
we have
\bar{C}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}}=4
. It follows that a
\bar{C}
-extremal sequence for
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
has length three. Let
S=(x,y,z)
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
.

If
x-y
is not a unit, then as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
we see that
(x,y)
is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum subsequence. Similarly, if
y-z
is not a unit we get that
(y,z)
is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum subsequence. If the terms of
S
are distinct, by Lemma
3.2
we see that
S
is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. So if
S
is a
\bar{C}
-extremal sequence for
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
, it follows that
S
is of the form
(x,y,x)
where
x-y
is a unit and so
S
is order-equivalent to
(0,1,0)
.

It is easy to see that the sequence
(0,1,0)
is a
\bar{C}
-extremal sequence for
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
. Thus, by Remark
1.3
it follows that any sequence which is order-equivalent to
(0,1,0)
is also a
\bar{C}
-extremal sequence for
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

For every
n\geq 4
a sequence
S
in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is an
\bar{E}
-extremal sequence for
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
if and only if
S
is an
E
-extremal sequence for
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
n\geq 4
. By Theorem
3.7
we have
\bar{E}_{\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}}=n+1
. Let
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. Suppose all the terms of
S
are distinct. Then each element of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
occurs exactly once as a term of
S
. Let
b
be the sum of all the terms of
S
. Then
2b=0
and so
S
is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

Thus, if
S
is an
\bar{E}
-extremal sequence for
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
it follows that there exists
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
such that at least two terms of
S
are equal to
c
. By Remark
1.1
we may assume that the term which is repeated the most number of times in
S
is zero. Also, we may assume that
x_{1}=x_{2}=0
.

Suppose
S
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. Then there exist
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
such that
a_{1}x_{1}+\ldots+a_{n}x_{n}=0
. Let
a=a_{3}+a_{4}+\cdots+a_{n}
and
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}\setminus\{a\}
. Then we get that
(c-a)x_{1}-cx_{2}+a_{3}x_{3}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}=0
. So we see that
S
is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.
∎

It is easy to see that a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is an
\bar{E}
-extremal sequence for
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\prime}
if and only if it is equivalent to
(0,1)
. So by Theorem 1 in
[
1
]
and Theorem
4.3
we get the next result.

Corollary 4.4
.

For every
n\neq 3
a sequence
S
in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is an
\bar{E}
-extremal sequence for
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{\prime}
if and only if
S
is equivalent to the sequence
(\,\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{n-1},1)
.

Theorem 4.5
.

A sequence
S
in
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
is an
\bar{E}
-extremal sequence for
\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{\prime}
if and only if
S
is equivalent to
(1,1,0,0)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.7
we have
\bar{E}_{\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{\prime}}=5
.
Let
S=(x,y,z,w)
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
which is an
\bar{E}
-extremal sequence for
\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{\prime}
. There exists an element in
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
which is repeated in
S
. By Remark
1.1
we can assume that this element is zero.

So we assume that
z=w=0
. Then both
x
and
y
must be non-zero. If
x\neq y
, by Lemma
3.2
we get the contradiction that
(x,y,0)
is a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum subsequence. So we see that
S
is of the form
(x,x,0,0)
where
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{\prime}
. Hence, it follows that
S
is equivalent to the sequence
S^{\prime}=(1,1,0,0)
.

Suppose
S^{\prime}=(1,1,0,0)
has a smooth
\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{\prime}
-weighted zero-sum subsequence
T
having length three. Then the only possibility is that
T=(1,1,0)
. So there exist
a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{\prime}
such that
a+b=0
and
a+b+c=0
. Then we get the contradiction that
c=0
. Thus, it follows that any sequence which is equivalent to
S^{\prime}
is an
\bar{E}
-extremal sequence for
\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{\prime}
.
∎

5
Determining the value of
\bar{E}_{Q_{p}}

Throughout this section we assume that
p
is an odd prime. We denote
U(p)^{2}
by
Q_{p}
. As
Q_{p}
is the image of the homomorphism
U(p)\to U(p)
given by
x\mapsto x^{2}
whose kernel is
\{1,-1\}
, it follows that
|Q_{p}|=(p-1)/2
. We denote
U(p)\setminus Q_{p}
by
N_{p}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

For every odd prime
p
we have that
p+2\leq\bar{E}_{Q_{p}}\leq p+4
.

Proof.

For any odd prime
p
, as
E_{Q_{p}}=p+2
from Theorem 3 of
[
3
]
and
E_{Q_{p}}\leq\bar{E}_{Q_{p}}
, we have
\bar{E}_{Q_{p}}\geq p+2
. Let
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p+4})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Consider the following system of equations in the variables
z_{1},\ldots,z_{p+4}
over the field
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
:

x_{1}z_{1}^{2}\,+\,\cdots\,+\,x_{p+4}z_{p+4}^{2}=0~,~~~~~~~z_{1}^{2}\,+\,\cdots\,+\,z_{p+4}^{2}=0~,~~~~~~~z_{1}^{p-1}\,+\,\cdots\,+\,z_{p+4}^{p-1}=0.

By the Chevalley-Warning Theorem in
[
9
]
, there exists a non-trivial solution
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{p+4})
of the above system. Let
I=\{i\in[1,p+4]:b_{i}\neq 0\}
and for every
i\in I
, let
a_{i}=b_{i}^{2}\in Q_{p}
. As
U(p)
is a group of order
p-1
, from the third equation we see that
|I|=p
. Let
T
be the subsequence of
S
such that
x_{i}
is a term of
T
if and only if
i\in I
. From the first two equations, we see that
T
is a smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of
S
having length
p
. Thus, it follows that
\bar{E}_{Q_{p}}\leq p+4
.
∎

We will prove a stronger version of Proposition
5.1
later in this section. Towards that, we develop some tools. The next result is Lemma 1 of
[
5
]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
p
be a prime which is at least seven and
x,y,z\in U(p)
. Then we have that
x\,Q_{p}+y\,Q_{p}+z\,Q_{p}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
p
be a prime which is at least seven and
S
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
which has at least three non-zero terms. Then
S
is a
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

Proof.

Let
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
such that
x_{1},x_{2},
and
x_{3}
are non-zero. By Lemma
5.2
there exist
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in Q_{p}
such that
a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}+a_{3}x_{3}=-(x_{4}+\cdots+x_{n})
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
p
be a prime which is at least seven and
T=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Suppose there exist
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}\in Q_{p}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}x_{i}=0
and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\neq 0
. Then the sequence
S=T+(0,0)
is a smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

Proof.

Let
a=a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}
. As
a\neq 0
, by Corollary
5.3
we see that the sequence
(a,1,1)
is a
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. So there exist
c^{\prime},c_{1},c_{2}\in Q_{p}
such that
c^{\prime}a+c_{1}+c_{2}=0
. Then
c^{\prime}a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c^{\prime}a_{k}x_{k}+c_{1}0+c_{2}0=0
. So it follows that
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},0,0)
is a smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

By a similar argument as in the proof of Lemma
5.4
we can show that if
T
is a sequence as in the statement of Lemma
5.4
and
U
is a sequence having length at least two such that all the terms of
U
are equal to zero, then the sequence
T+U
is a smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
p
be a prime which is at least seven and
S
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
which has at least three terms which are zero and at least three non-zero terms. Then
S
is a smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

Proof.

By rearranging the terms of
S
we may assume that
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
such that
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
are non-zero and the last three terms are zero. By Corollary
5.3
we see that there exist
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-3}\in Q_{p}
such that
a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{n-3}x_{n-3}=0
.

Let
a=a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n-3}
. By Corollary
5.3
we see that the sequence
(a,1,1,1)
is a
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. We now argue as in the proof of Lemma
5.4
to see that
S
is a smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.
∎

The following results are implied by Proposition 2 from
[
7
]
and by the fact that
-1\in Q_{p}
if and only if
p\equiv 1~(\text{mod}~4)
.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
p
be a prime which is at least seven and
x,y\in U(p)
.
Then we have that
U(p)\subseteq x\,Q_{p}+y\,Q_{p}
.

If
x
and
y
are in the same coset of
Q_{p}
, then
0\in x\,Q_{p}+y\,Q_{p}
if and only if
p\equiv 1~(\emph{mod}~4)
.

If
x
and
y
are in different cosets of
Q_{p}
, then
0\in x\,Q_{p}+y\,Q_{p}
if and only if
p\equiv 3~(\emph{mod}~4)
.

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
p
be a prime which is at least seven and
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that at least two terms of
S
are non-zero. Then we have that
U(p)\subseteq x_{1}Q_{p}+\cdots+x_{n}Q_{p}
.

Proof.

We will prove this result by using induction on
n
.
When
n=2
, the result follows from Theorem
5.7
. Let
n\geq 3
. If
x_{n}=0
, we are done. So we can assume that
x_{n}\neq 0
. As
|Q_{p}|=(p-1)/2
and
p\geq 5
, we have
|x_{n}Q_{p}|=|Q_{p}|\geq 2
. Let
a,b\in x_{n}Q_{p}
with
a\neq b
. As
U(p)=Z_{p}\setminus\{0\}
, we see that
U(p)+\{a\}=Z_{p}\setminus\{a\}
and
U(p)+\{b\}=Z_{p}\setminus\{b\}
. As
a\neq b
, it follows that
\mathbb{Z}_{p}=(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\setminus\{a\})\cup(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\setminus\{b\})
. Thus, we get that
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\subseteq U(p)+x_{n}Q_{p}
. By the induction hypothesis, we have
U(p)\subseteq x_{1}Q_{p}+\cdots+x_{n-1}Q_{p}
and so
U(p)+x_{n}Q_{p}\subseteq x_{1}Q_{p}+\cdots+x_{n}Q_{p}
. Hence, it follows that
U(p)\subseteq x_{1}Q_{p}+\cdots+x_{n}Q_{p}
.
∎

The next result is a special case of a conjecture by Erdos and Heilbronn which was proved by Dias da Silva and Hamidoune in
[
8
]
.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
p
be a prime and
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that
|A|\geq 2
. Let
A\hat{+}A
denote the set
\{a+b:a,b\in A,~a\neq b\}
. Then either
A\hat{+}A=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
or
|A\hat{+}A|\geq 2\,|A|-3
.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
p
be a prime which is at least eleven and
x,y,z\in U(p)
such that
x
and
y
are distinct elements which belong to the same coset of
Q_{p}
. Then there exist
a,b\in Q_{p}
such that
ax+by+z=0
and
a+b+1\neq 0
.

Proof.

It is enough if we prove the result when the elements
x,y,z
are replaced by
ux,uy,uz
where
u\in U(p)
. So we may assume that
x,y\in Q_{p}
. By Theorem
5.9
we have
|Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p}|\geq p-4
since
|Q_{p}|=(p-1)/2
.

As
p\geq 11
, it follows that
|Q_{p}|\geq 5
and hence
Q_{p}\cap(Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p})\neq\emptyset
. As
Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p}
is closed under multiplication by elements of
Q_{p}
we have
Q_{p}\subseteq Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p}
. By a similar argument we get that
N_{p}\subseteq Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p}
. Hence, as
z\neq 0
it follows that
-z\in Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p}
. So there exist
u,v\in Q_{p}
such that
u\neq v
and
u+v=-z
.

Let
a=u/x
and
b=v/y
. Then
a,b\in Q_{p}
and
ax+by=-z
. Let
a^{\prime}=v/x
and
b^{\prime}=u/y
. Then
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in Q_{p}
and
a^{\prime}x+b^{\prime}y=-z
. As
ax+by=a^{\prime}x+b^{\prime}y
, we get that
(a-a^{\prime})x=(b^{\prime}-b)y
. As
u\neq v
, we see that
a\neq a^{\prime}
and so
a-a^{\prime}\neq 0
. Also, as
x\neq y
it follows that
a-a^{\prime}\neq b^{\prime}-b
and hence
a+b\neq a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}
. Thus, we see that either
a+b
or
a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}
is different from
-1
.
∎

.

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
p
be a prime which is at least seven and

be a sequence in

which has at least four non-zero terms. Suppose

and

are distinct non-zero terms of

As in the previous proof, we may assume that

. Let

be the subsequence of

which is obtained by removing the terms

and

from

. For every

. As

has at least four non-zero terms, we see that

has at least two non-zero terms.

By Corollary

we have that

. Also, we have that

and so
(Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p})\cap U(p)\neq\emptyset

(Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p})\,\cap\,(w_{1}Q_{p}+\cdots+w_{n-2}Q_{p})\neq\emptyset
. So there exist
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-2}\in Q_{p}
such that

. Let

. Then
-z\in Q_{p}\hat{+}Q_{p}
and so there exist

such that

and

.

Let
a=u/x
and
b=v/y
. Then
a,b\in Q_{p}
and

. Let

and

. Then

and

. As in the previous proof, we see that

. So we see that there exist
c_{n-1},c_{n}\in Q_{p}
such that
c_{1}z_{1}+\cdots+c_{n-2}z_{n-2}+c_{n-1}x+c_{n}y=0
and
c_{1}+\cdots+c_{n-2}+c_{n-1}+c_{n}\neq 0
.
∎

Theorem 5.13
.

For every odd prime
p\neq 5
we have that
\bar{E}_{Q_{p}}=p+2
and
\bar{E}_{Q_{5}}=9
.

Proof.

As
Q_{3}=\{1\}
, by Theorem
2.2
we see that
\bar{E}_{Q_{3}}=5
. From
[
6
]
we have that
E_{\{1\}}=2n-1
for any
n
. Suppose a sequence
T
in
\mathbb{Z}_{5}
is a smooth
\{1,-1\}
-weighted zero-sum sequence of length five. Then we see that
T
is a
\{1\}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. As
Q_{5}=\{1,-1\}
, it follows that
\bar{E}_{Q_{5}}\geq E_{\{1\}}=9
. So by Proposition
5.1
we get that
\bar{E}_{Q_{5}}=9
.

Let
p\geq 7
and
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p+2})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. By the pigeonhole principle it follows that at least two terms of
S
must be equal. By Remark
1.1
we may assume that zero occurs as a term of
S
the most number of times. Suppose
S
has at most two non-zero terms. Then
S
has at least
p
zeroes. So we get a smooth
\{1\}
-weighted (and hence
Q_{p}
-weighted) zero-sum subsequence of length
p
. Thus, we may assume that
S
has at least three non-zero terms.

Suppose at least three terms of
S
are zero. As
p\geq 7
, we see that
S
has at least nine terms. So we can find two terms
a
and
b
of
S
such that if
T^{\prime}
is the sequence of length
p
which is obtained by removing
a
and
b
from
S
, then
T^{\prime}
has at least three terms which are zero and at least three non-zero terms. So from Lemma
5.6
it follows that
T^{\prime}
is a smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

Hence, we may assume that exactly two terms of
S
are zero. Let
x_{p+1}=x_{p+2}=0
and
T
be the subsequence of
S
having length
p-2
which is obtained by removing the last four terms of
S
. Then all the terms of
T
are non-zero. As
p\geq 7
, we see that
T
has at least five terms. By the pigeonhole principle, it follows that at least three terms of
T
are in the same coset of
Q_{p}
.

As zero occurs the most number of times as a term of
S
, we see that there exist distinct terms of
T
which are in the same coset of
Q_{p}
. Thus, by Lemma
5.12
we see that there exist
a_{1},\ldots,a_{p-2}\in Q_{p}
such that
a_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{p-2}x_{p-2}=0
and
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{p-2}\neq 0
. Hence, by Lemma
5.4
it follows that
T+(0,0)
is a smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.
∎

6
Determining the value of
\bar{C}_{Q_{p}}

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
p
be a prime which is at least seven such that
p\equiv 1~(\emph{mod}~4)
. Let
S
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
which has at least two terms which are zero and at least three non-zero terms. Then
S
is a smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

Proof.

By rearranging the terms of
S
we may assume that
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
such that
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
are non-zero and
x_{n-1}=x_{n}=0
. By Corollary
5.3
we see that there exist
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-2}\in Q_{p}
such that
c_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c_{n-2}x_{n-2}=0
. As
p\equiv 1~(\text{mod}~4)
, we have that
-1\in Q_{p}
.

Let
c=c_{1}+\cdots+c_{n-2}
. If
c=0
, we see that
S
is a smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence since
c_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c_{n-2}x_{n-2}+x_{n-1}-x_{n}=0
. If
c\neq 0
, by Lemma
5.4
we see that
S
is a smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence as
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-2})+(0,0)
.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

The statement of Lemma
6.1
is not valid when
p=5
. This is because the sequence
(1,1,1,0,0)
is not a smooth
Q_{5}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
p
be an odd prime. Then we have that
\bar{C}_{Q_{p}}\geq 6
. Further, if
p\equiv 3~(\emph{mod}~4)
we have that
\bar{C}_{Q_{p}}\geq 9
.

Proof.

By Theorem 4 of
[
10
]
we have that
C_{Q_{p}}=3
. So there exist
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that the sequence
S^{\prime}=(x,y)
is not a
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. Consider the sequence
S=(0,x,0,y,0)
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Then any smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum subsequence
T
whose terms are consecutive terms of
S
contains both
x
and
y
. Then we get the contradiction that
S^{\prime}
is a
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. Hence, it follows that
\bar{C}_{Q_{p}}\geq 6
.

Let
p\equiv 3~(\text{mod}~4)
. Consider the sequence
S=(0,0,x,0,0,y,0,0)
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. As
-1\notin Q_{p}
the sequence
(0,0)
is not a
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. So it follows that any smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum subsequence
T
whose terms are consecutive terms of
S
contains both
x
and
y
. Then we get the contradiction that
S^{\prime}
is a
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. Hence, it follows that
\bar{C}_{Q_{p}}\geq 9
.
∎

Theorem 6.4
.

For every prime
p\equiv 1~(\emph{mod}~4)
we have that
\bar{C}_{Q_{p}}=6
when
p\neq 5
.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.3
for any odd prime
p
we have that
\bar{C}_{Q_{p}}\geq 6
. Let
S
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
having length six. By Remark
1.1
we may assume that zero is a term of
S
which occurs the most number of times as a term of
S
.

Case 1:
At least two terms of
S
are equal to zero.

Suppose at least three terms of
S
are non-zero. Then by Lemma
6.1
we see that
S
is a smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. Suppose at most two terms of
S
are non-zero. As
S
has length six, it follows that there exist two consecutive terms
x
and
y
of
S
which are equal to zero. Since
p\equiv 1~(\text{mod}~4)
we have that
-1\in Q_{p}
. As
1x+(-1)y=0
, we see that the sequence
T=(x,y)
is a smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

Case 2:
Exactly one term of
S
is zero.

As zero is the term which is repeated the most number of times in
S
, it follows that all the terms of
S
are distinct. As
S
has length six, there exist two terms
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
of
S
which are in the same coset of
Q_{p}
. Let
x_{1},x_{2}
and
x_{3}
be the other non-zero terms of
S
.

As
p\geq 11
, by Corollary
5.11
we see that there exist
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in Q_{p}
such that
a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}+a_{3}x_{3}=0
and
a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}\neq 0
. By Theorem
5.7
there exist
b_{1},b_{2}\in Q_{p}
such that
b_{1}y_{1}+b_{2}y_{2}=0
. As
y_{1}\neq y_{2}
, it follows that
b_{1}\neq-b_{2}
and so
b_{1}+b_{2}\neq 0
. Let
a=a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}
and
b=b_{1}+b_{2}
. As all the terms of the sequence
(a,b,1)
are non-zero, by Corollary
5.3
there exist
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\in Q_{p}
such that
c_{1}a+c_{2}b+c_{3}=0
. As we also have that
c_{1}(a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}+a_{3}x_{3})+c_{2}(b_{1}y_{1}+b_{2}y_{2})+c_{3}0=0
, it follows that
S
is a smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
S
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{5}
having length three such that all the terms of
S
are non-zero. Then there exists a
Q_{5}
-weighted zero-sum sequence whose terms are consecutive terms of
S
.

Proof.

As
Q_{5}=\{1,-1\}
, it is enough to prove the result for a sequence
S
whose terms are either one or two. If any two consecutive terms of
S
are equal, the result follows. So we may assume that
S=(1,2,1)
or
S=(2,1,2)
. In both these cases, we see that
S
is a
Q_{5}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.
∎

Theorem 6.6
.

We have that
\bar{C}_{Q_{5}}=6
.

Proof.

From Proposition
6.3
we see that
\bar{C}_{Q_{5}}\geq 6
. Let
S
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{5}
having length six. By Remark
1.1
we may assume that zero is a term of
S
which occurs the most number of times as a term of
S
. It follows that at least two terms of
S
are zero.

Case 1:
Exactly two terms of
S
are zero.

Let
T=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{4})
be the subsequence of
S
whose terms are the non-zero terms of
S
. By Lemma 2 of
[
5
]
we see that
T
is a
Q_{5}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. So there exist
a_{i}
’s in
Q_{5}
such that
a_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+a_{4}y_{4}=0
. Let
a=a_{1}+\cdots+a_{4}
. Suppose
a=0
, then it follows that
S
is a smooth
Q_{5}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

Suppose
a\neq 0
. Since for every
i\in[1,4]
we have that
a_{i}\in Q_{5}=\{1,-1\}
, it follows that
a\notin\{1,-1\}
and so
a\in\{2,-2\}
. Let
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
be the two terms of
S
which are equal to zero. Suppose
a=2
. Then
a_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+a_{4}y_{4}-z_{1}-z_{2}=0
and so
S
is a smooth
Q_{5}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. Suppose
a=-2
. Then
a_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+a_{4}y_{4}+z_{1}+z_{2}=0
and so
S
is a smooth
Q_{5}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

Case 2:
Exactly three terms of
S
are zero.

Suppose there exist two consecutive terms
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
of
S
which are equal to zero. As
-1\in Q_{5}
, it follows that
(z_{1},z_{2})
is a smooth
Q_{5}
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of
S
. So we may assume that the terms of
S
which are equal to zero and the non-zero terms of
S
occur in alternate positions. Let
U=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
be the sequence whose terms are the three non-zero terms of
S
.

By Lemma
6.5
we see that there exists a
Q_{5}
-weighted zero-sum sequence
V
whose terms are consecutive terms of
U
. We claim that there exists a smooth
Q_{5}
-weighted zero-sum sequence
T
such that the length of
T
is twice the length of
V
and the terms of
T
are consecutive terms of
S
.

We prove our claim in the case when
V=(x_{1},x_{2})
. As
V
is a
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence, there exist
a_{1},a_{2}\in Q_{5}
such that
a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}=0
. So we get that
a_{1}x_{1}-a_{1}0+a_{2}x_{2}-a_{2}0=0
and hence
T=(x_{1},0,x_{2},0)
is a smooth
Q_{5}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. By using similar arguments for the other possibilities for the sequence
V
, we see that our claim is true.

Case 3:
At least four terms of
S
are zero.

As
S
has length six, it follows that there exist two consecutive terms
x
and
y
of
S
which are equal to zero. Hence, we see that the sequence
T=(x,y)
is a smooth
Q_{5}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.
∎

Theorem 6.7
.

For every prime
p\equiv 3~(\emph{mod}~4)
we have that
\bar{C}_{Q_{p}}=9
when
p\neq 7
.

Proof.

When
p=3
, we have
Q_{3}=\{1\}
. So by Theorem
2.4
we see that
\bar{C}_{Q_{3}}=9
. By Proposition
6.3
we have that
\bar{C}_{Q_{p}}\geq 9
. Let
S
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
having length nine. By Remark
1.1
we may assume that zero is a term of
S
which occurs the most number of times as a term of
S
.

If at least three terms of
S
are zero and at least three terms of
S
are non-zero, by Lemma
5.6
it follows that
S
is a smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. So we have the following two remaining cases:

Case 1:
At most two terms of

are non-zero.

There exists a subsequence
T=(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})
of
S
such that the terms of
T
are consecutive terms of
S
and are equal to zero. By Corollary
5.3
we see that the sequence
(1,1,1)
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is a

-weighted zero-sum sequence. So there exist
a,b,c\in Q_{p}
such that
a+b+c=0
and hence
az_{1}+bz_{2}+cz_{3}=0
. Thus, it follows that
T
is a smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

Case 2:
At most two terms of
S
are zero.

In this case every element of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
occurs at most twice as a term of
S
. We see that either there exists a subsequence
T
of length seven whose terms are consecutive terms of
S
such that exactly one term of
T
is zero or there exists a subsequence
U
of length eight whose terms are consecutive terms of
S
such that exactly two terms of

are zero.

It follows that we can partition the non-zero terms of
T
into two subsequences
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
and
(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})
such that each subsequence has distinct terms. When
p\geq 11
, by Corollary
5.11
we see that there exist
a_{i}
’s and
b_{i}
’s in
Q_{p}
such that
a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}+a_{3}x_{3}=0
,
b_{1}y_{1}+b_{2}y_{2}+b_{3}y_{3}=0
,
a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}\neq 0
and
b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}\neq 0
.

Let
a=a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}
,
b=b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}
. By Corollary
5.3
the sequence
(a,b,1)
is a
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. So there exist
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\in Q_{p}
such that
c_{1}a+c_{2}b+c_{3}=0
. As
c_{1}(a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}+a_{3}x_{3})+c_{2}(b_{1}y_{1}+b_{2}y_{2}+b_{3}y_{3})+c_{3}0=0
, it follows that

is a smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.

There are exactly two terms of
U
which are zero. We define
a
and
b
as we have defined in the previous two paragraphs for the sequence
T
. By Corollary
5.3
the sequence
(a,b,1,1)
is a
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. So by a similar argument as in the previous two paragraphs, it follows that

is a smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence.
∎

7
Determining the value of
\bar{D}_{Q_{p}}

Theorem 7.1
.

For every odd prime
p
we have that
\bar{D}_{Q_{p}}
is either
4
or
5
.

Proof.

Let
S=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{5})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Consider the following system of equations in the variables
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{5}
over the field
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

x_{1}Z_{1}^{2}\,+\,\cdots\,+\,x_{5}Z_{5}^{2}=0,\hskip 28.45274ptZ_{1}^{2}\,+\,\cdots\,+Z_{5}^{2}=0

By the Chevalley-Warning Theorem in
[
9
]
, there exists a non-trivial solution
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{5})
of the above system. Let
I=\{i\in[1,5]:b_{i}\neq 0\}
and for every
i\in I
, let
a_{i}=b_{i}^{2}\in Q_{p}
. Let
T
be the sequence such that
x_{i}
is a term of
T
if and only if
i\in I
. As
I\neq\emptyset
, it follows that
T
is a smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. Thus, we see that
\bar{D}_{Q_{p}}\leq 5
.

From
[
1
]
we have that
D_{Q_{p}}=3
. So there exists a sequence
U=(x,y)
which has no
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum subsequence. Consider the sequence
S=(0,x,y)
. Suppose
T
is a smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of
S
. If
T
contains any term of
U
, then we get the contradiction that
U
has a
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum subsequence. So we see that
T=(0)
. However, the sequence
(0)
is not a smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum sequence. Thus, we see that
\bar{D}_{Q_{p}}\geq 4
.
∎

Theorem 7.2
.

For every prime
p\equiv 3~(\emph{mod}~4)
we have that
\bar{D}_{Q_{p}}=5
.

Proof.

From
[
1
]
we have that
D_{Q_{p}}=3
. So there exists a sequence
U=(x,y)
which has no
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum subsequence. Consider the sequence
S=(0,0,x,y)
. Suppose
T
is a smooth
Q_{p}
-weighted zero-sum subsequence of
S
. By a similar argument as in the proof of Theorem
7.1
we see that the only remaining possibility is
T=(0,0)
. So there exist
a,b\in Q_{p}
such that
a+b=0
. It follows that
-1\in Q_{p}
and hence we get the contradiction that
p\equiv 1~(\text{mod}~4)
. Thus, we see that
\bar{D}_{Q_{p}}\geq 5
. From Theorem
7.1
we have that
\bar{D}_{Q_{p}}\leq 5
.
∎

8
Concluding remarks

When
p
is a prime such that
p\equiv 1~(\text{mod}~4)
, we believe that
\bar{D}_{Q_{p}}=4
. However, despite our best efforts, we have been unable to show this. It will also be interesting to investigate the value of the smooth
A
-weighted zero-sum constants for other weight-sets
A
.

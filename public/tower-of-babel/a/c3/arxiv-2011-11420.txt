Abstract
. Let L L be a linear operator in L 2 ​ ( ℝ n ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) which generates a semigroup e − t ​ L e^{-tL} whose kernels p t ​ ( x , y ) p_{t}(x,y) satisfy the Gaussian upper bound. 1.
Introduction

Given an operator
L
, the conical square function
S_{\alpha,L}
associated with
L
is defined by

\displaystyle S_{\alpha,L}(f)(x):=\bigg(\iint_{\Gamma_{\alpha}(x)}|t^{m}Le^{-t^{m}L}f(y)|^{2}\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(1.1)

where
\Gamma_{\alpha}(x)=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty):|x-y|<\alpha t\}
. In particular, if
m=2
and
L=-\Delta
,
S_{\alpha,L}
is the classical area integral function. The conical square functions associated with abstract operators played an important role in harmonic analysis. For example, by means of
S_{\alpha,L}
, Auscher et al.
[
2
]
introduced the Hardy space
H^{1}_{L}
associated with an operator
L
. Soon after, Duong and Yan
[
15
]
showed that
\operatorname{BMO}_{L^{*}}
is the dual space of the Hardy space
H^{1}_{L}
, which can be seen a generalization of Fefferman and Stein’s result on the duality between
H^{1}
and
\operatorname{BMO}
spaces. Later, the theory of function spaces associated with operators has been developed and generalized to many other different settings, see for example
[
13
,
16
,
17
,
24
]
. Recently, Martell and Prisuelos-Arribas
[
27
]
studied the weighted norm inequalities for conical square functions. More specifically, they established boundedness and comparability in weighted Lebesgue spaces of different square functions using the Heat and Poisson semigroups. Using these square functions, they
[
28
]
define several weighted Hardy spaces
H_{L}^{1}(w)
and showed that they are one and the same in view of the fact that the square functions are comparable in the corresponding weighted spaces. Very recently, Bui and Duong
[
3
]
introduced several types of square functions associated with operators and established the sharp weighted estimates.

In this paper, we continue to investigate several kinds of weighted norm inequalities for such operators, including bump estimates, Fefferman-Stein inequalities with arbitrary weights, the local decay estimates, the mixed weak type estimates corresponding Sawyer’s conjecture. Beyond that, we consider other weak type estimates including the restricted weak-type
(p,p)
estimates and the endpoint estimate for the corresponding commutators. For more information about the progress of these estimates, see
[
6
,
14
,
30
,
31
,
29
,
32
]
and the reference therein.

Suppose that
L
is an operator which satisfies the following properties:

(A1)

L
is a closed densely defined operators of type
\omega
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
0\leq\omega<\pi/2
, and it has a bounded
H_{\infty}
-functional calculus in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(A2)

The kernel
p_{t}(x,y)
of
e^{-tL}
admits a Gaussian upper bound. That is, there exists
m\geq 1
and
C,c>0
so that for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

|p_{t}(x,y)|\leq\frac{C}{t^{n/m}}\exp\bigg(-\frac{|x-y|^{m/(m-1)}}{c\,t^{1/(m-1)}}\bigg).

Examples of the operator
L
which satisfies condition (A1) and (A2) include: Laplacian
-\Delta
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, or the Laplace operator on an open connected domain
with Dirichlet boundary conditions, or the homogeneous sub-Laplacian on a homogeneous group; Schrödinger operator
L=-\Delta+V
with a nonnegative potential
0\leq V\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

3.
Preliminaries

3.1.
Muckenhoupt class

By a weight
w
, we mean that
w
is a nonnegative locally integrable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The weight
w
is said to belong to the Muckenhoupt class
A_{p}
,
1<p<\infty
, if

[w]_{A_{p}}:=\sup_{Q}\bigg(\fint_{Q}w\,dx\bigg)\bigg(\fint_{Q}w^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}dx\bigg)^{p-1}<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

3.2.
Dyadic cubes

Denote by
\ell(Q)
the sidelength of the cube
Q
. Given a cube
Q_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\mathcal{D}(Q_{0})
denote the set of all dyadic cubes with respect to
Q_{0}
, that is, the cubes obtained by repeated subdivision of
Q_{0}
and each of its descendants into
2^{n}
congruent subcubes.

Definition 3.1
.

A collection
\mathcal{D}
of cubes is said to be a dyadic grid if it satisfies

(1)

For any
Q\in\mathcal{D}
,
\ell(Q)=2^{k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

For any
Q,Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}
,
Q\cap Q^{\prime}=\{Q,Q^{\prime},\emptyset\}
.

(3)

The family
\mathcal{D}_{k}=\{Q\in\mathcal{D};\ell(Q)=2^{k}\}
forms a partition of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 3.2
.

A subset
\mathcal{S}
of a dyadic grid is said to be
\eta
-sparse,
0<\eta<1
, if for every
Q\in\mathcal{S}
, there exists a measurable set
E_{Q}\subset Q
such that
|E_{Q}|\geq\eta|Q|
, and the sets
\{E_{Q}\}_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}
are pairwise disjoint.

By a median value of a measurable function
f
on a cube
Q
we mean a possibly non-unique, real number
m_{f}(Q)
such that

\max\big\{|\{x\in Q:f(x)>m_{f}(Q)\}|,|\{x\in Q:f(x)<m_{f}(Q)\}|\big\}\leq|Q|/2.

The decreasing rearrangement of a measurable function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
defined by

f^{*}(t)=\inf\{\alpha>0:|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|f(x)|>\alpha\}|<t\},\quad 0<t<\infty.

The local mean oscillation of
f
is

\omega_{\lambda}(f;Q)=\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R}}\big((f-c)\mathbf{1}_{Q}\big)^{*}(\lambda|Q|),\quad 0<\lambda<1.

Given a cube
Q_{0}
, the local sharp maximal function is
defined by

M_{\lambda;Q_{0}}^{\sharp}f(x)=\sup_{x\in Q\subset Q_{0}}\omega_{\lambda}(f;Q).

Observe that for any
\delta>0
and
0<\lambda<1

|m_{f}(Q)|\leq(f\mathbf{1}_{Q})^{*}(|Q|/2)\ \ \text{and}\ \ (f\mathbf{1}_{Q})^{*}(\lambda|Q|)\leq\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\fint_{Q}|f|^{\delta}dx\right)^{1/{\delta}}.

(3.1)

The following theorem was proved by Hytönen
[
18
, Theorem 2.3]
in order to improve Lerner’s formula given in
[
22
]
by getting rid of the local sharp maximal function.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
f
be a measurable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
Q_{0}
be a fixed cube. Then there exists a (possibly empty) sparse family
\mathcal{S}(Q_{0})\subset\mathcal{D}(Q_{0})
such that

|f(x)-m_{f}(Q_{0})|\leq 2\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}(Q_{0})}\omega_{2^{-n-2}}(f;Q)\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x),\quad a.e.~x\in Q_{0}.

(3.2)

3.3.
Orlicz maximal operators

A function
\Phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is called a Young function if it is continuous, convex, strictly increasing, and satisfies

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{\Phi(t)}{t}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\Phi(t)}{t}=\infty.

Given
p\in[1,\infty)
, we say that a Young function
\Phi
is a
p
-Young function, if
\Psi(t)=\Phi(t^{1/p})
is a Young function.

If
A
and
B
are Young functions, we write
A(t)\simeq B(t)
if there are constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}A(t)\leq B(t)\leq c_{2}A(t)
for all
t\geq t_{0}>0
. Also, we denote
A(t)\preceq B(t)
if there exists
c>0
such that
A(t)\leq B(ct)
for all
t\geq t_{0}>0
. Note that for all Young functions
\phi
,
t\preceq\phi(t)
. Further, if
A(t)\leq cB(t)
for some
c>1
, then by convexity,
A(t)\leq B(ct)
.

A function
\Phi
is said to be doubling, or
\Phi\in\Delta_{2}
, if there is a constant
C>0
such that
\Phi(2t)\leq C\Phi(t)
for any
t>0
. Given a Young function
\Phi
, its complementary function
\bar{\Phi}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is defined by

\bar{\Phi}(t):=\sup_{s>0}\{st-\Phi(s)\},\quad t>0,

which clearly implies that

\displaystyle st\leq\Phi(s)+\bar{\Phi}(t),\quad s,t>0.

(3.3)

Moreover, one can check that
\bar{\Phi}
is also a Young function and

t\leq\Phi^{-1}(t)\bar{\Phi}^{-1}(t)\leq 2t,\qquad t>0.

(3.4)

In turn, by replacing
t
by
\Phi(t)
in first inequality of (
3.4
), we obtain

\bar{\Phi}\Big(\frac{\Phi(t)}{t}\Big)\leq\Phi(t),\qquad t>0.

(3.5)

Given a Young function
\Phi
, we define the Orlicz space
L^{\Phi}(\Omega,u)
to be the function space with Luxemburg norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\Phi}(\Omega,u)}:=\inf\bigg\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}\Phi\Big(\frac{|f(x)|}{\lambda}\Big)du(x)\leq 1\bigg\}.

(3.6)

Now we define the Orlicz maximal operator

\displaystyle M_{\Phi}f(x):=\sup_{Q\ni x}\|f\|_{\Phi,Q}:=\sup_{Q\ni x}\|f\|_{L^{\Phi}(Q,\frac{dx}{|Q|})},

where the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. When
\Phi(t)=t^{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\Phi,Q}=\bigg(\fint_{Q}|f(x)|^{p}dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}=:\|f\|_{p,Q}.

In this case, if
p=1
,
M_{\Phi}
agrees with the classical Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
M
; if
p>1
,
M_{\Phi}f=M_{p}f:=M(|f|^{p})^{1/p}
. If
\Phi(t)\preceq\Psi(t)
, then
M_{\Phi}f(x)\leq cM_{\Psi}f(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The Hölder inequality can be generalized to the scale of Orlicz spaces
[
11
, Lemma 5.2]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Given a Young function
A
, then for all cubes
Q
,

\fint_{Q}|fg|dx\leq 2\|f\|_{A,Q}\|g\|_{\bar{A},Q}.

(3.7)

More generally, if
A
,
B
and
C
are Young functions such that
A^{-1}(t)B^{-1}(t)\leq c_{1}C^{-1}(t),
for all
t\geq t_{0}>0
,
then

\displaystyle\|fg\|_{C,Q}\leq c_{2}\|f\|_{A,Q}\|g\|_{B,Q}.

(3.8)

The following result is an extension of the well-known Coifman-Rochberg theorem. The proof can be found in
[
19
, Lemma 4.2]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\Phi
be a Young function and
w
be a nonnegative function such that
M_{\Phi}w(x)<\infty
a.e.. Then

\displaystyle[(M_{\Phi}w)^{\delta}]_{A_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{n,\delta},\quad\forall\delta\in(0,1),

(3.9)

\displaystyle[(M_{\Phi}w)^{-\lambda}]_{RH_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{n,\lambda},\quad\forall\lambda>0.

(3.10)

Given
p\in(1,\infty)
, a Young function
\Phi
is said to satisfy the
B_{p}
condition (or,
\Phi\in B_{p}
) if for some
c>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{c}^{\infty}\frac{\Phi(t)}{t^{p}}\frac{dt}{t}<\infty.

(3.11)

Observe that if (
3.11
) is finite for some
c>0
, then it is finite for every
c>0
. Let
[\Phi]_{B_{p}}
denote the value if
c=1
in (
3.11
). It was shown in
[
11
, Proposition 5.10]
that if
\Phi
and
\bar{\Phi}
are doubling Young functions, then
\Phi\in B_{p}
if and only if

\displaystyle\int_{c}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{t^{p^{\prime}}}{\bar{\Phi}(t)}\bigg)^{p-1}\frac{dt}{t}<\infty.

Let us present two types of
B_{p}
bumps. An important special case is the “log-bumps” of the form

\displaystyle A(t)=t^{p}\log(e+t)^{p-1+\delta},\quad B(t)=t^{p^{\prime}}\log(e+t)^{p^{\prime}-1+\delta},\quad\delta>0.

(3.12)

Another interesting example is the “loglog-bumps” as follows:

\displaystyle A(t)=t^{p}\log(e+t)^{p-1}\log\log(e^{e}+t)^{p-1+\delta},\quad\delta>0

(3.13)

\displaystyle B(t)=t^{p^{\prime}}\log(e+t)^{p^{\prime}-1}\log\log(e^{e}+t)^{p^{\prime}-1+\delta},\quad\delta>0.

(3.14)

Then one can verify that in both cases above,
\bar{A}\in B_{p^{\prime}}
and
\bar{B}\in B_{p}
for any
1<p<\infty
.

The
B_{p}
condition can be also characterized by the boundedness of the Orlicz maximal operator
M_{\Phi}
. Indeed, the following result was given in
[
11
, Theorem 5.13]
and
[
19
, eq. (25)]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Then
M_{\Phi}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
\Phi\in B_{p}
. Moreover,
\|M_{\Phi}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{n,p}[\Phi]_{B_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p}}
. In particular, if the Young function
A
is the same as the first one in (
3.12
) or (
3.13
), then

\|M_{\bar{A}}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c_{n}p^{2}\delta^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}},\quad\forall\delta\in(0,1].

(3.15)

Definition 3.7
.

Given
p\in(1,\infty)
, let
A
and
B
be Young functions such that
\bar{A}\in B_{p^{\prime}}
and
\bar{B}\in B_{p}
. We say that the pair of weights
(u,v)
satisfies the
double bump condition
with respect to
A
and
B
if

\displaystyle[u,v]_{A,B,p}:=\sup_{Q}\|u^{\frac{1}{p}}\|_{A,Q}\|v^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|_{B,Q}<\infty.

(3.16)

where the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Also,
(u,v)
is said to satisfy the
separated bump condition
if

\displaystyle[u,v]_{A,p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{Q}\|u^{\frac{1}{p}}\|_{A,Q}\|v^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|_{p^{\prime},Q}<\infty,

(3.17)

\displaystyle[u,v]_{p,B}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{Q}\|u^{\frac{1}{p}}\|_{p,Q}\|v^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|_{B,Q}<\infty.

(3.18)

Note that if
A(t)=t^{p}
in (
3.17
) or
B(t)=t^{p}
in (
3.18
), each of them actually is two-weight
A_{p}
condition and we denote them by
[u,v]_{A_{p}}:=[u,v]_{p,p^{\prime}}
. Also, the separated bump condition is weaker than the double bump condition. Indeed, (
3.16
) implies (
3.17
) and (
3.18
), but the reverse direction is incorrect.
The first fact holds since
\bar{A}\in B_{p^{\prime}}
and
\bar{B}\in B_{p}
respectively indicate
A
is a
p
-Young function and
B
is a
p^{\prime}
-Young function. The second fact was shown in
[
1
, Section 7]
by constructing log-bumps.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, let
A
,
B
and
\Phi
be Young functions such that
A\in B_{p}
and
A^{-1}(t)B^{-1}(t)\lesssim\Phi^{-1}(t)
for any
t>t_{0}>0
. If a pair of weights
(u,v)
satisfies
[u,v]_{p,B}<\infty
, then

\displaystyle\|M_{\Phi}f\|_{L^{p}(u)}\leq C[u,v]_{p,B}[A]_{B_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(v)}.

(3.19)

Moreover, (
3.19
) holds for
\Phi(t)=t
and
B=\bar{A}
satisfying the same hypotheses. In this case,
\bar{A}\in B_{p}
is necessary.

The two-weight inequality above was established in
[
11
, Theorem 5.14]
and
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
. The weak type inequality for
M_{\Phi}
was also obtained in
[
11
, Proposition 5.16]
as follows.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, let
B
and
\Phi
be Young functions such that
t^{\frac{1}{p}}B^{-1}(t)\lesssim\Phi^{-1}(t)
for any
t>t_{0}>0
. If a pair of weights
(u,v)
satisfies
[u,v]_{p,B}<\infty
, then

\displaystyle\|M_{\Phi}f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(u)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(v)}.

(3.20)

Moreover, (
3.20
) holds for
M
if and only if
[u,v]_{A_{p}}<\infty
.

Proof of main results

4.1.
Sparse domination

Let
\Phi
be a radial Schwartz function such that
\mathbf{1}_{B(0,1)}\leq\Phi\leq\mathbf{1}_{B(0,2)}
. We define

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}(f)(x):=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi\Big(\frac{|x-y|}{\alpha t}\Big)|Q_{t,L}f(y)|^{2}\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}\bigg)^{1/2},

where
Q_{t,L}f:=t^{m}Le^{-t^{m}L}f
. It is easy to verify that

\displaystyle S_{\alpha,L}(f)(x)\leq\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}(f)(x)\leq S_{2\alpha,L}(f)(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(4.1)

Additionally, it was proved in
[
2
]
that
S_{1,L}
is bounded from
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then, this and (
4.1
) give that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}(f)\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\alpha^{n}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(4.2)

Using these facts, we can establish the sparse domination for
S_{\alpha,L}
as follows.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
\alpha\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle S_{\alpha,L}(f)(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{3^{n}}\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}^{2}(f)(x),\quad\text{a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(4.3)

where

\mathcal{A}_{S}^{2}(f)(x):=\bigg(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\langle|f|\rangle_{Q}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

Fix
Q_{0}\in\mathcal{D}
. By (
3.1
), Kolmogorov’s inequality and (
4.2
), we have

\displaystyle|m_{\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}(f)^{2}}(Q_{0})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\fint_{Q_{0}}|\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}(f\mathbf{1}_{Q_{0}})|^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dx\bigg)^{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}(f\mathbf{1}_{Q_{0}})\|^{2}_{L^{1,\infty}(Q_{0},\frac{dx}{|Q_{0}|})}\lesssim\alpha^{2n}\bigg(\fint_{Q_{0}}|f|\,dx\bigg)^{2}.

(4.4)

From
[
3
, Proposition 3.2]
, we obtain that for any dyadic cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\alpha\geq 1
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
,

\omega_{\lambda}(\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}(f)^{2};Q)\lesssim\alpha^{2n}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j\delta}\bigg(\fint_{2^{j}Q}|f|\,dx\bigg)^{2},

(4.5)

where
\delta\in(0,1)
is some constant. Invoking Lemma
3.3
, (
4.1
) and (
4.5
), one can pick a sparse family
\mathcal{S}(Q_{0})\subset\mathcal{D}(Q_{0})
so that

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}(f)(x)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|m_{\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}(f)^{2}}(Q_{0})|+\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}(Q_{0})}\omega_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}(f)^{2};Q)\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{2n}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}(Q_{0})}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j\delta}\langle|f|\rangle_{2^{j}Q}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)

(4.6)

\displaystyle=:\alpha^{2n}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j\delta}\mathcal{T}^{2}_{\mathcal{S}(Q_{0}),j}(f)(x)^{2},\quad\text{ a.e. }x\in Q_{0}.

(4.7)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{2}_{\mathcal{S},j}(f)(x)

\displaystyle:=\bigg(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\langle|f|\rangle_{2^{j}Q}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{S},j}(f,g)(x)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\langle|f|\rangle_{2^{j}Q}\langle|g|\rangle_{2^{j}Q}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x),

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}(f,g)(x)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\langle|f|\rangle_{Q}\langle|g|\rangle_{2Q}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x).

Then,
\mathcal{T}^{2}_{\mathcal{S},j}(f)(x)=\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{S},j}(f,f)(x)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. On the other hand, the arguments in
[
23
, Sections 11-13]
shows that there exist
3^{n}
dyadic grids
S_{j}\in\mathcal{D}_{j},j=1,\ldots,3^{n}
, such that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j\delta}\mathcal{T}^{1}_{\mathcal{S}(Q_{0}),j}(f,f)(x)\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{3^{n}}\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}(f,f)(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{3^{n}}\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}(f)(x)^{2}.

(4.8)

Gathering (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}(f)(x)\lesssim\alpha^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{3^{n}}\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}(f)(x),\quad\text{ a.e. }x\in Q_{0}.

Since
\mathbb{R}^{n}=\bigcup_{Q\in\mathcal{D}}Q
, it leads that

\displaystyle S_{\alpha,L}(f)(x)\leq\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}(f)(x)\lesssim\alpha^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{3^{n}}\mathcal{A}^{2}_{\mathcal{S}_{j}}(f)(x),\quad\text{ a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

This completes our proof.
∎

4.2.
Bump conjectures

In this subsection, we are going to show two-weight inequalities invoking bump conjectures.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
.

By Lemma
4.1
, the inequality (
1.2
) follows from the following

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}(f)\|_{L^{p}(u)}\lesssim\mathscr{N}_{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(v)},

(4.9)

for every sparse family
\mathcal{S}
, where the implicit constant does not depend on
\mathcal{S}
.

To prove (
4.9
), we begin with the case
1<p\leq 2
. Actually, the Hölder’s inequality (
3.7
) gives that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}(f)\|_{L^{p}(u)}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\bigg(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\langle f\rangle_{Q}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{Q}(x)\bigg)^{\frac{p}{2}}u(x)dx\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\langle|f|\rangle_{Q}^{p}\int_{Q}u(x)dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\|fv^{\frac{1}{p}}\|_{\bar{B},Q}^{p}\|v^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|_{B,Q}^{p}\|u^{\frac{1}{p}}\|_{p,Q}^{p}|Q|

\displaystyle\lesssim||(u,v)||_{A,B,p}^{p}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\left(\inf_{Q}M_{\bar{B}}(fv^{\frac{1}{p}})\right)^{p}|E_{Q}|

\displaystyle\leq||(u,v)||_{A,B,p}^{p}\int_{X}M_{\bar{B}}(fv^{\frac{1}{p}})(x)^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq||(u,v)||_{A,B,p}^{p}\|M_{\bar{B}}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(v)}^{p},

(4.10)

where Lemma
3.6
is used in the last step.

Next let us deal with the case
2<p<\infty
. By duality, one has

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}(f)\|_{L^{p}(u)}^{2}=\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}(f)^{2}\|_{L^{p/2}(u)}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq h\in L^{(p/2)^{\prime}}(u)\\
\|h\|_{L^{(p/2)^{\prime}}(u)=1}\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}(f)^{2}hu\,dx.

(4.11)

Fix a nonnegative function
h\in L^{(p/2)^{\prime}}(u)
with
\|h\|_{L^{(p/2)^{\prime}}(u)}=1
. Then using Hölder’s inequality (
3.7
) three times and Lemma
3.6
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{X}\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}(f)(x)^{2}h(x)u(x)dx\lesssim\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\langle|f|\rangle_{Q}^{2}\langle hu\rangle_{Q}|Q|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\|fv^{\frac{1}{p}}\|_{\bar{B},Q}^{2}\|v^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|_{B,Q}^{2}\|hu^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\|_{\bar{A},Q}\|u^{\frac{2}{p}}\|_{A,Q}|Q|

\displaystyle\lesssim||(u,v)||_{A,B,p}^{2}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\left(\inf_{Q}M_{\bar{B}}(fv^{\frac{1}{p}})\right)^{2}\left(\inf_{Q}M_{\bar{A}}(hu^{1-\frac{2}{p}})\right)|E_{Q}|

\displaystyle\leq||(u,v)||_{A,B,p}^{2}\int_{X}M_{\bar{B}}(fv^{\frac{1}{p}})(x)^{2}M_{\bar{A}}(hu^{1-\frac{2}{p}})(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq||(u,v)||_{A,B,p}^{2}\|M_{\bar{B}}(fv^{\frac{1}{p}})^{2}\|_{L^{p/2}}\|M_{\bar{A}}(hu^{1-\frac{2}{p}})\|_{L^{(p/2)^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq||(u,v)||_{A,B,p}^{2}\|M_{\bar{B}}\|_{L^{p}}^{2}\|M_{\bar{A}}\|_{L^{(p/2)^{\prime}}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(v)}^{2}\|h\|_{L^{(p/2)^{\prime}}(u)}.

(4.12)

Therefore, (
4.9
) immediately follows from (
4.2
), (
4.11
) and (
4.2
).
∎

Let us present an endpoint extrapolation theorem from
[
11
, Corollary 8.4]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a collection of pairs
(f,g)
of nonnegative measurable functions. If for every weight
w
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{1,\infty}(w)}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{1}(Mw)},\quad(f,g)\in\mathcal{F},

then for all
p\in(1,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(u)}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{p}(v)},\quad(f,g)\in\mathcal{F},

whenever
\sup_{B}\|u^{\frac{1}{p}}\|_{A,B}\|v^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|_{p^{\prime},B}<\infty
, where
\bar{A}\in B_{p^{\prime}}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.
.

In view of, it suffices to prove that for every weight
w
,

\displaystyle\|S_{\alpha,L}(f)\|_{L^{1,\infty}(w)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{1}(Mw)},

where the constant
C
is independent of
w
and
f
. We should mention that although the norm of weights does not appear in
[
11
, Corollary 8.4]
, one can check its proof to obtain the norm constant in (
1.3
). Invoking Lemma
4.1
, we are reduced to showing that there exists a constant
C
such that for every sparse family
\mathcal{S}
and for every weight
w
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}(f)\|_{L^{1,\infty}(w)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{1}(M_{\mathcal{D}}w)},

(4.13)

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
f
is bounded and has compact support. Fix
\lambda>0
and denote
\Omega:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:M_{\mathcal{D}}f(x)>\lambda\}
. By the Calderón-Zygmund decomposition, there exists a pairwise disjoint family
\{Q_{j}\}\subset\mathcal{D}
such that
\Omega=\bigcup_{j}Q_{j}
and

(1)

f=g+b
,

(2)

g=f\mathbf{1}_{\Omega}+\sum_{j}\langle f\rangle_{Q_{j}}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{j}}
,

(3)

b=\sum_{j}b_{j}
with
b_{j}=(f-\langle f\rangle_{Q_{j}})\mathbf{1}_{Q_{j}}
,

(4)

\langle|f|\rangle_{Q_{j}}>\lambda
and
|g(x)|\leq 2^{n}\lambda
, a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(5)

\operatorname{supp}(b_{j})\subset Q_{j}
and
\fint_{Q_{j}}b_{j}\,dx=0
.

Then by (
1
), we split

\displaystyle w(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}(f)(x)>\lambda\})\leq w(\Omega)+{\rm I_{g}}+{\rm I_{b}},

(4.14)

where

\displaystyle{\rm I_{g}}=w(\{x\in\Omega^{c}:\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}(g)(x)>\lambda/2\})\quad\text{and}\quad{\rm I_{b}}=w(\{x\in\Omega^{c}:\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}(b)(x)>\lambda/2\})

For the first term, we by (
4
) have

\displaystyle w(\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}w(Q_{j})\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{j}\frac{w(Q_{j})}{|Q_{j}|}\int_{Q_{j}}|f(x)|dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{j}\int_{Q_{j}}|f(x)|M_{\mathcal{D}}w(x)dx\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|M_{\mathcal{D}}w(x)dx.

(4.15)

To estimate
{\rm I_{b}}
, we claim that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}(b_{j})(x)=0
for all
x\in\Omega^{c}
and for all
j
. In fact, if there exist
x_{0}\in\Omega^{c}
and
j_{0}
such that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}(b_{j_{0}})(x_{0})\neq 0
, then there is a dyadic cube
Q_{0}\in\mathcal{S}
such that
x_{0}\in Q_{0}
and
\langle b_{j_{0}}\rangle_{Q_{0}}\neq 0
. The latter implies
Q_{0}\subsetneq Q_{j_{0}}
because of the support and the vanishing property of
b_{j_{0}}
. This in turn gives that
x_{0}\in Q_{j_{0}}
, which contradicts
x_{0}\in\Omega^{c}
. This shows our claim. As a consequence, the set
\{x\in\Omega^{c}:\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}(b)(x)>\lambda/2\}
is empty, and hence
{\rm I_{b}}=0
.

In order to control
{\rm I_{g}}
, we first present a Fefferman-Stein inequality for
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}
. Note that
v(x):=M_{\mathcal{D}}w(x)\geq\langle w\rangle_{Q}
for any dyadic cube
Q\in\mathcal{S}
containing
x
. Then for any Young function
A
such that
\bar{A}\in B_{2}
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}(f)\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\langle|f|\rangle_{Q}^{2}w(Q)\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\|fv^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\bar{A},Q}^{2}\|v^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|_{A,Q}^{2}w(Q)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\|fv^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\bar{A},Q}^{2}\langle w\rangle_{Q}^{-1}w(Q)\lesssim\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}}\Big(\inf_{Q}M_{\bar{A}}(fv^{\frac{1}{2}})\Big)^{2}|E_{Q}|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}M_{\bar{A}}(fv^{\frac{1}{2}})(x)^{2}dx\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}(v)}^{2}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(M_{\mathcal{D}}w)}^{2},

(4.16)

where we used Lemma
3.6
in the last inequality. Note that for any
x\in Q_{j}
,

\displaystyle M_{\mathcal{D}}(w\mathbf{1}_{\Omega^{c}})(x)

\displaystyle\leq M_{\mathcal{D}}(w\mathbf{1}_{Q_{j}^{c}})(x)=\sup_{x\in Q\in\mathcal{D}}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q\cap Q_{j}^{c}}w(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{Q\in\mathcal{D}:Q_{j}\subsetneq Q}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q\cap Q_{j}^{c}}w(y)dy\leq\inf_{Q_{j}}M_{\mathcal{D}}w.

(4.17)

Thus, combining (
4.2
) with (
4.2
), we have

\displaystyle{\rm I_{g}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{\Omega^{c}}\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}^{2}(g)(x)w(x)dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|g(x)|M_{\mathcal{D}}(w\mathbf{1}_{\Omega^{c}})(x)dx\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|g(x)|M_{\mathcal{D}}(w\mathbf{1}_{\Omega^{c}})(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\Omega^{c}}|f(x)|M_{\mathcal{D}}(w\mathbf{1}_{\Omega^{c}})(x)dx+\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{j}\langle f\rangle_{Q_{j}}\int_{Q_{j}}M_{\mathcal{D}}(w\mathbf{1}_{\Omega^{c}})(x)dx

(4.18)

Consequently, gathering (

), (
4.2
) and (
4.2

4.3.
Fefferman-Stein inequalities

In order to show Theorem
1.3
, we recall an extrapolation theorem for arbitrary weights in
[
9
, Theorem 1.3]
, or
[
5
, Theorem 4.11]
in the general Banach function spaces.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a collection of pairs
(f,g)
of nonnegative measurable functions. If for some
p_{0}\in(0,\infty)
and for every weight
w
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p_{0}}(w)}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{p_{0}}(Mw)},\quad(f,g)\in\mathcal{F},

then for every
p\in(p_{0},\infty)
and for every weight

,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\leq C\|g(Mw/w)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}\|_{L^{p}(w)},\quad(f,g)\in\mathcal{F}.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.
.

Note that
v(x):=Mw(x)\geq\langle w\rangle_{Q}
for any dyadic cube
Q\in\mathcal{S}
containing
x
. Let
A
be a Young function such that
{\bar{A}}\in B_{p}
.
By Lemma
3.6
, we have

\displaystyle\|M_{\bar{A}}(fv^{\frac{1}{p}})\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(v)}.

(4.19)

Thus, using Lemma
4.1
, Hölder’s inequality and (
4.19
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\|S_{\alpha,L}(\vec{f})\|_{L^{p}(w)}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{pn}\sum_{j=1}^{K_{0}}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{j}}\langle|f|\rangle_{Q}^{p}\int_{Q}w(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{pn}\sum_{j=1}^{K_{0}}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{j}}\|f\nu^{\frac{1}{p}}\|_{\bar{A},Q}^{p}\|\nu^{-\frac{1}{p}}\|_{A,Q}^{p}\int_{Q}w(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{pn}\sum_{j=1}^{K_{0}}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{j}}\|f\nu^{\frac{1}{p}}\|_{\bar{A},Q}^{p}|Q|

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{pn}\sum_{j=1}^{K_{0}}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{S}_{j}}\Big(\inf_{Q}M_{\bar{A}}(f\nu^{\frac{1}{p}})\Big)^{p}|E_{Q}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{pn}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}M_{\bar{A}}(f\nu^{\frac{1}{p}})(x)^{p}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{pn}\|f\|_{L^{p}(v)}^{p}=\alpha^{pn}\|f\|_{L^{p}(Mw)}^{p}.

This shows (
1.4
). Finally, (

) is a consequence of (

) and Lemma

.
∎

4.4.
Local decay estimates

To show Theorem

, we need the following Carleson embedding theorem from
[

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose that the sequence

of nonnegative numbers satisfies the Carleson packing condition

Then for all

and

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{D}}a_{Q}\Big(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}f(x)w(x)\ du(x)\Big)^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq A^{\frac{1}{p}}p^{\prime}\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}.

Lemma 4.5
.

For every
1<p<\infty
and

\displaystyle\|S_{\alpha,L}f\|_{L^{2}(B,w)}\leq c_{n,p}\alpha^{n}[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|Mf\|_{L^{2}(B,w)},

(4.20)

for every ball

and

with

Proof.

Let
w\in A_{p}
with
1<p<\infty
. Fix a ball

. From (
4.6
), there exists a sparse family
\mathcal{S}(Q)\subset\mathcal{D}(Q)
so that

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}(f)(x)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{2n}\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}(Q)}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j\delta}\langle|f|\rangle_{2^{j}Q^{\prime}}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{Q^{\prime}}(x)

This implies that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}(f)\|_{L^{2}(Q,w)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{2n}\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}(Q)}\inf_{Q^{\prime}}M(f)^{2}w(Q^{\prime}).

From this and (
4.1
), we see that to obtain (
4.20

\displaystyle\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}(Q)}\inf_{Q^{\prime}}M(f)^{2}w(Q^{\prime})\lesssim[w]_{A_{p}}\|M(f)\|_{L^{2}(Q,w)}^{2}.

(4.21)

Recall that a new version of
A_{\infty}
was introduced by Hytönen and Pérez

\displaystyle[w]^{\prime}_{A_{\infty}}:=\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}M(w\mathbf{1}_{Q})(x)dx.

By
[

, there holds

\displaystyle c_{n}[w]^{\prime}_{A_{\infty}}\leq[w]_{A_{\infty}}\leq[w]_{A_{p}}.

(4.22)

Observe that for every

\displaystyle\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}(Q):Q^{\prime}\subset Q^{\prime\prime}}w(Q^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}(Q):Q^{\prime}\subset Q^{\prime\prime}}\langle w\rangle_{Q^{\prime}}|Q^{\prime}|\lesssim\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}(Q):Q^{\prime}\subset Q^{\prime\prime}}\inf_{Q^{\prime}}M(w\mathbf{1}_{Q^{\prime\prime}})|E_{Q^{\prime}}|

where we used the disjointness of

and (
4.22
). This shows that the collection

satisfies the Carleson packing condition with the constant
c_{n}[w]_{A_{p}}
. As a consequence, this and Lemma
4.4
give that

\displaystyle\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}(Q)}\inf_{Q^{\prime}}M(f)^{2}w(Q^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}(Q)}\bigg(\frac{1}{w(Q^{\prime})}\int_{Q^{\prime}}M(f)\,\mathbf{1}_{Q}w\,dx\bigg)^{2}w(Q^{\prime})

\displaystyle\lesssim[w]_{A_{p}}\|M(f)\mathbf{1}_{Q}\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}=[w]_{A_{p}}\|M(f)\mathbf{1}_{Q}\|_{L^{2}(Q,w)}^{2},

where the above implicit constants are independent of
[w]_{A_{p}}
and
Q

4.21
) and completes the proof of (
4.20
).
∎

Next, let us see how Lemma

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.
.

Let
p>1
and
r>1
be chosen later. Define the Rubio de Francia algorithm:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}h=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{M^{k}h}{2^{k}\|M\|^{k}_{L^{r^{\prime}}\to L^{r^{\prime}}}}.

Then it is obvious that

\displaystyle h\leq\mathcal{R}h\quad\text{and}\quad\|\mathcal{R}h\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 2\|h\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(4.23)

Moreover, for any nonnegative

, we have that
\mathcal{R}h\in A_{1}
with

\displaystyle[\mathcal{R}h]_{A_{1}}\leq 2\|M\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}\to L^{r^{\prime}}}\leq c_{n}\ r.

By Riesz theorem and the first inequality in (

), there exists some nonnegative function

with

such that

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{Q}^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle:=|\{x\in Q:S_{\alpha,L}(f)(x)>tM(f)(x)\}|^{\frac{1}{r}}

(4.25)

where

and

. Recall that Coifmann-Rochberg theorem

\displaystyle[(M(f))^{\delta}]_{A_{1}}\leq\frac{c_{n}}{1-\delta},\quad\forall\delta\in(0,1).

In view of (

Thus, gathering (

\displaystyle\leq c_{n}t^{-2}\alpha^{2n}[w]_{A_{p}}\|M(f)\|_{L^{2}(Q,w)}^{2}

4.5.
Mixed weak type estimates

To proceed the proof of Theorem
1.5
, we establish a Coifman-Fefferman inequality.

Lemma 4.6
.

For every
0<p<\infty
and

\displaystyle\|S_{\alpha,L}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|Mf\|_{L^{p}(w)},

(4.30)

Proof.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}S_{\alpha,L}(\vec{f})(x)^{2}w(x)\ dx

be a family of pairs of functions. Suppose that for some

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p_{0}}(v_{0})}\leq C_{1}\|g\|_{L^{p_{0}}(v_{0})},\quad\forall(f,g)\in\mathcal{F}.

Proof of Theorem

.
.

In view of Lemma
4.6

4.7.

Recall that the sharp maximal function of
f

If we write

\displaystyle C_{b}(S_{\alpha,L})f(x)

.

,

\displaystyle M_{\delta}^{\#}(\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}f)(x_{0})\lesssim\alpha^{2n}Mf(x_{0}),\quad\forall x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

\displaystyle F(f)(x)

Let us choose

where

is the center of
Q

\displaystyle\bigg(

\displaystyle\fint_{Q}|\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,\psi}(f)^{2}-c_{Q}|^{\delta}dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}=\left(\fint_{Q}|E(f)(x)+F(f)(x)-c_{Q}|^{\delta}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}

\left(\fint_{Q}|E(f)(x)|^{\delta}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\lesssim\left(\fint_{Q}|E(f_{0})(x)|^{\delta}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}+\left(\fint_{Q}|E(f^{\infty}(x)|^{\delta}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}.

It was proved in
[
3

(4.44)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{Q}|E(f^{\infty})(x)|^{\delta}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}

(4.45)

(4.46)

Lemma 4.8
.

For any
0<\delta<\varepsilon<1
and for any
b\in\operatorname{BMO}
,

\displaystyle M_{\delta}^{\#}(C_{b}(\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L})f)(x)\lesssim\|b\|_{\operatorname{BMO}}\big(M_{L\log L}f(x)+M_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}f)(x)\big).

(4.48)

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
Q
be any arbitrary cube containing
x
. It suffices to show that there exists
c_{Q}
such that

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}

\displaystyle:=\bigg(\fint_{Q}|C_{b}(\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L})f(z)-c_{Q}|^{\delta}dz\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}\lesssim\|b\|_{\operatorname{BMO}}\big(M_{L\log L}f(x)+M_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}f)(x)\big).

(4.49)

Split
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
, where
f_{1}=f{\bf 1}_{8Q}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\fint_{Q}|(b(z)-b_{Q})\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}(f)(z)|^{\delta}dz\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\bigg(\fint_{Q}|\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}((b-b_{Q})f_{1})(z)|^{\delta}dz\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\bigg(\fint_{Q}|\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}((b-b_{Q})f_{2})(z)-c_{Q}|^{\delta}dz\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\delta}}

\displaystyle:=\mathscr{A}_{1}+\mathscr{A}_{2}+\mathscr{A}_{3}.

(4.50)

To bound
\mathscr{A}_{1}
, we choose
r\in(1,\varepsilon/\delta)
. The Hölder’s inequality gives that

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\fint_{Q}|b(z)-b_{Q}|^{\delta r^{\prime}}dz\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\delta r^{\prime}}}\bigg(\fint_{Q}|\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}f(z)|^{\delta r}dz\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\delta r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b\|_{\operatorname{BMO}}M_{\delta r}(\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}f)(x)\leq\|b\|_{\operatorname{BMO}}M_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}f)(x).

(4.51)

Since
\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}:L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
0<\delta<1
, there holds

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}((b-b_{Q})f_{1})\|_{L^{1,\infty}(Q,\frac{dx}{|Q|})}\lesssim\fint_{Q}|(b-b_{Q})f_{1}|dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\|b-b_{Q}\|_{\exp L,Q}\|f\|_{L\log L,Q}\lesssim\|b\|_{\operatorname{BMO}}M_{L\log L}(f)(x).

(4.52)

For the last term, we take
c_{Q}=\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}((b-b_{Q})f_{2})(z_{Q})
, where
z_{Q}
is the center of
B
. We have

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{3}\leq\fint_{Q}|\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}((b-b_{Q})f_{2})(z)-c_{Q}|\,dz=:\fint_{Q}J_{Q}(z)\,dz\leq\bigg(\fint_{Q}J_{Q}(z)^{2}\,dz\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.53)

For any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, set
T_{Q}=Q\times(0,\ell(Q))
. Thus, for any
z\in Q
,

\displaystyle J_{Q}(z)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}((b-b_{Q})f_{2})(z)^{2}-\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}((b-b_{Q})f_{2})(z_{Q})^{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{T(2Q)}\Phi\Big(\frac{z-y}{\alpha t}\Big)|Q_{t,L}((b-b_{Q})f_{2})(y)|^{2}\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\iint_{T(2Q)}\Phi\Big(\frac{z^{\prime}-y}{\alpha t}\Big)|Q_{t,L}((b-b_{Q})f_{2})(y)|^{2}\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus T(2Q)}\Big|\Phi\Big(\frac{z-y}{\alpha t}\Big)-\Phi\Big(\frac{z^{\prime}-y}{\alpha t}\Big)\Big||Q_{t^{m}}((b-b_{Q})f_{2})(y)|^{2}\frac{dydt}{t^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=:J_{Q,1}(z)+J_{Q,2}(z)+J_{Q,3}(z).

(4.54)

In order to estimate

\displaystyle\fint_{Q}\Phi\Big(\frac{z-y}{\alpha t}\Big)dz\leq\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi\Big(\frac{z-y}{\alpha t}\Big)dz\lesssim\frac{(\alpha t)^{n}}{|Q|}.

(4.55)

Furthermore, the kernel estimate (
4.45
) gives that

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{2Q}

\displaystyle|Q_{t,L}((b-b_{Q})f_{2})(y)|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 3}\bigg\{\int_{2Q}\bigg[\int_{2^{j+1}Q\setminus 2^{j}Q}\frac{1}{t^{n}}\bigg(\frac{t}{t+|z-y|}\bigg)^{n+\delta_{0}}|b-b_{Q}||f|dz\bigg]^{2}dy\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 3}\bigg\{\int_{2Q}\bigg[\int_{2^{j+1}Q\setminus 2^{j}Q}\frac{1}{t^{n}}\bigg(\frac{t}{2^{j}\ell(Q)}\bigg)^{n+\delta_{0}}|b-b_{Q}||f|dz\bigg]^{2}dy\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\frac{t}{\ell(Q)}\bigg)^{\delta_{0}}|Q|^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{-j\delta_{0}}\|b-b_{Q}\|_{\exp L,2^{j+1}Q}\|f\|_{L\log L,2^{j+1}Q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\frac{t}{\ell(Q)}\bigg)^{\delta_{0}}|Q|^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{-j\delta_{0}}j\|b\|_{\operatorname{BMO}}M_{L\log L}f(x)

(4.56)

Then, gathering (

\displaystyle\fint_{Q}J_{Q,1}(z)dz

(4.57)

Similarly, one has

(4.58)

To control

Therefore, (

Lemma 4.9
.

For any
w\in A_{\infty}
and
b\in\operatorname{BMO}
,

\begin{split}\sup_{t>0}\Phi(1/t)^{-1}&w(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|C_{b}(S_{\alpha,L})f(x)|>t\})\\
&\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\Phi(1/t)^{-1}w(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:M_{L\log L}f(x)>t\}),\end{split}

(4.61)

for all
f\in L^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof.

Recall that the weak type Fefferman-Stein inequality:

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda>0}\varphi(\lambda)\omega(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:M_{\delta}f(x)>\lambda\})\leq\sup_{\lambda>0}\varphi(\lambda)\omega(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:M^{\sharp}_{\delta}f(x)>\lambda\})

(4.62)

for all function
f
for which the left-hand side is finite, where
\varphi:(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is doubling. We may assume that the right-hand side of (
4.61
) is finite since otherwise there is nothing to be proved. Now by the Lebesgue diffentiation theorem we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}:=

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\Phi(1/t)^{-1}w(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|C_{b}(S_{\alpha,L})f(x)|>t\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\Phi(1/t)^{-1}w(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|C_{b}(\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L})f(x)|>t\})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\Phi(1/t)^{-1}w(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:M_{\delta}(C_{b}(\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}))f(x)|>t\}).

Then Lemma
4.7
, Lemma
4.8
and (
4.62
) give that

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\Phi(1/t)^{-1}w(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:M_{\delta}^{\#}(C_{b}(\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L})f)(x)>t\})

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\Phi(1/t)^{-1}w(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:M_{L\log L}f(x)+M_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}f)(x)>c_{0}t\})

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\Phi(1/t)^{-1}w(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:M_{L\log L}f(x)>t\})

\displaystyle\quad+\sup_{t>0}\Phi(1/t)^{-1}w(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:M_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}f)(x)>t\})

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\Phi(1/t)^{-1}w(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:M_{L\log L}f(x)>t\})

\displaystyle\quad+\sup_{t>0}\Phi(1/t)^{-1}w(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:M^{\sharp}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{S}_{\alpha,L}f)(x)>t\})

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\Phi(1/t)^{-1}w(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:M_{L\log L}f(x)>t\})

\displaystyle\quad+\sup_{t>0}\Phi(1/t)^{-1}w(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:M(f)(x)>t\})

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\Phi(1/t)^{-1}w(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:M_{L\log L}f(x)>t\}).

The proof is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.
.

Let
w\in A_{1}
. By homogeneity, it is enough to prove

\displaystyle w(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:C_{b}(S_{\alpha,L})f(x)>1\})\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi(|f(x)|)w(x)dx.

(4.63)

Let us recall a result from
[
7
, Lemma 2.11]
for
m=1
. For any
w\in A_{1}
,

\displaystyle w(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:M_{L\log L}f(x)>t\})\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi\bigg(\frac{|f(x)|}{t}\bigg)w(x)dx,\quad\forall t>0.

(4.64)

Since
\Phi
is submultiplicative, Lemma
4.9
and (
4.64
) imply that

\displaystyle w(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:C_{b}(S_{\alpha,L})f(x)>1\})

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sup_{t>0}\Phi(1/t)^{-1}w(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|C_{b}(S_{\alpha,L})f(x)|>t\})

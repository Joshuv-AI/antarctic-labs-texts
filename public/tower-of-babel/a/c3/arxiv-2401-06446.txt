Increasing dimension asymptotics for two-way crossed mixed effect models
Ziyang Lyu, S.A. Sisson, A.H. Welsh

Abstract
This paper presents asymptotic results for the maximum likelihood and restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimators within a two-way crossed mixed effect model as the sizes of the rows, columns, and cells tend to infinity. Under very mild conditions which do not require the assumption of normality, the estimators are proven to be asymptotically normal, possessing a structured covariance matrix. The growth rate for the number of rows, columns, and cells is unrestricted, whether considered pairwise or collectively.

Introduction

Mixed models are a diverse class of statistical models that relate a response variable to both observed covariates and latent random effects. They are widely used in applied statistics, particularly for modeling relationships within clustered and longitudinal data. Pioneering works on this topic include those by
[
23
]
and
[
2
]
for survey data, and
[
14
]
for longitudinal data. Commonly, linear mixed models are fitted by assuming normality and using either maximum likelihood (ML) or restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation; see for example
[
9
]
. As these estimators are nonlinear, asymptotic methods are needed to derive their properties and construct approximate inferences for the unknown parameters.

The traditional asymptotic approach, discussed by
[
8
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
5
]
, and
[
22
]
, applies to data with independent clusters modelled by a nested random effect structure and requires the number of clusters to increase while the cluster size stays bounded.
Recent asymptotic results for mixed model estimators
[
15
,
13
]
allow both the number of clusters and the cluster sizes to increase, but still require nested random effects.
[
13
]
also require the ratios of the cluster sizes to the number of clusters to tend to zero.
[
15
]
do not impose such rate conditions, but their results are restricted to the nested error regression model.

There are real-world scenarios requiring crossed random effects that are not covered by the available results. For example, an educational dataset recording student performance as the outcome variable can have teachers and class groups of students (treated as random effects) nested within schools but crossed with each other if teachers teach multiple class groups and/or class groups are taught by multiple teachers. In such applications, the clusters or cells (as clusters created by crossing are sometimes described) can be large, allowing the number of observations in them to be treated as asymptotically increasing. In addition, there are theoretical problems (e.g. in prediction, see
[
10
]
) for which consistency requires both the number of clusters and the cluster sizes to increase. Moreover,

[
12
, section 3.31]
point out that it is very difficult to derive the asymptotic behavior of the MLE in any generalized linear mixed effect model (GLMM) with increasing numbers of crossed random effects, even for the simplest cases.
Although
[
11
]
use a subset argument to prove consistency of the MLE for GLMM parameters with crossed random effects, the method does not lead to the asymptotic distribution of the estimators.
Partly for this reason, recent literature on GLMMs with crossed random effects focuses on computational algorithms; see for example
[
7
]
who proposed a backfitting algorithm to compute generalized least squares estimates for the crossed effect model without the interaction random effect, and
[
18
]
who explored streamlined mean field variational Bayes algorithms for fitting linear mixed models with crossed random effects.
There is a gap in the literature on asymptotic results for linear mixed models with crossed random effects.
The purpose of this paper is to develop rigorously the asymptotic properties of working normal maximum likelihood and REML estimators for the parameters of crossed random effect models when both the number of cells and their sizes tend to infinity.

We consider the two-way crossed random effect with interaction model as a canonical crossed random effect model. It is used to analyze data structured by the crossing of two categorical factors, hereinafter referred to as factor A (rows) and factor B (columns), respectively.

Under working normality for both the random effects and errors, the likelihood and the REML criterion can be written down as a function of

3

given by
[
24

For our results, we do not assume normality, but instead assume the existence of finite "
4+\delta
" moments for both the random effects and errors. Whether the explanatory variables are considered fixed or random, we impose conditions akin to finite moments of order "
2+\delta
". These conditions are analogous to those used by
[
16
,
15
]
for nested error regression models. We permit the values of
g

[
15
]
, we identify four distinct levels of parameters:

1.

Factor A (row) parameters: Coefficients of factor A (row) level covariates (covariates that vary only with
i
) and the corresponding variance
\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}
.

2.

Factor B (column) parameters: Coefficients of factor B (column) level covariates (covariates that vary only with
j
) and the corresponding variance
\sigma_{\beta}^{2}
.

3.

Interaction parameters: Coefficients of interaction covariates (covariates that vary only with
i
and
j
) and the corresponding variance
\sigma_{\gamma}^{2}
.

4.

Within cell parameters: Coefficients of within cell covariates (covariates that vary with
i
,
j
and
k
) and the corresponding variance
\sigma_{e}^{2}
.

The information for factor A parameters increases with
g
, factor B parameters with
h
, interaction parameters with
gh
, and within-cell parameters with
n
. Each of these rates requires a different normalization on the corresponding estimators; we achieve this using a diagonal matrix which is readily interpretable. We derive asymptotic representations for both the maximum likelihood and REML estimators in terms of their influence functions that are useful in their own right and for the development of the central limit theorem. The asymptotic variance matrix we obtain is elegantly structured with a block diagonal configuration, which is both concise and interpretable, and simplifies making asymptotic inferences for the unknown parameters.

The case

In this case, we have to to consider the estimators of the row parameters and column parameters together:
[\xi_{0},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1}^{T},{\sigma}_{\alpha}^{2},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{2}^{T},{\sigma}_{\beta}^{2}]^{T}

For REML estimation, group the regression parameters as

\boldsymbol{\xi}=[\xi_{0},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1}^{T},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{2}^{T},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{3}^{T},\boldsymbol{\xi}_{4}^{T}]^{T}

and the variance components as

\boldsymbol{\theta}=[{\sigma}_{\alpha}^{2},{\sigma}_{\beta}^{2},{\sigma}_{\gamma}^{2},{\sigma}_{e}^{2}]^{T}
.
The REML criterion function is obtained by replacing the regression parameters
\boldsymbol{\xi}
in the log-likelihood (
6

\boldsymbol{\theta}
and then modifying the resulting profile log-likelihood for
\boldsymbol{\theta}
to reduce the bias of the estimators. For each
\boldsymbol{\theta}

\boldsymbol{\xi}
presented in (
8

\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})=\{\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{V}^{-1}\mathbf{X}\}^{-1}\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{V}^{-1}\mathbf{y},

and the REML criterion function is

l_{R}(\boldsymbol{\theta};\mathbf{y})=l(\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}),\boldsymbol{\theta};\mathbf{y})-\frac{1}{2}\log|\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{V}^{-1}\mathbf{X}|.

The REML estimator
\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{R}
of
\dot{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
maximizes the REML criterion function
l_{R}(\boldsymbol{\theta};\mathbf{y})
. We define
\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{R}=\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{R})
to be the REML estimator of
\boldsymbol{\xi}
and write the REML estimator of
\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
as

\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{R}=[\hat{\xi}_{R0},\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{R1}^{T},\hat{\sigma}_{R\alpha}^{2},\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{R2}^{T},\hat{\sigma}_{R\beta}^{2},\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{R3}^{T},\hat{\sigma}_{R\gamma}^{2},\hat{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{R4}^{T},\hat{\sigma}_{Re}^{2}]^{T}.

Since
\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{V}^{-1}\mathbf{X}
is not a function of
\boldsymbol{\xi}
, the REML estimator also maximizes the adjusted log-likelihood

l_{A}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\theta};\mathbf{y})=l(\boldsymbol{\omega};\mathbf{y})-\frac{1}{2}\log|\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{V}^{-1}\mathbf{X}|.

According to
[
21
]
, the REML estimator can be identified in a single step (rather than in two steps) by optimizing

directly. This means that the estimating function is represented by
\boldsymbol{\psi}_{A}(\boldsymbol{\omega})
using the derivatives

,

l_{A\boldsymbol{\xi}_{3}}=l_{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{3}}

\displaystyle l_{A{\sigma}_{\alpha}^{2}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})=l_{{\sigma}_{\alpha}^{2}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{trace}\{(\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{V}^{-1}\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{V}^{-1}\mathbf{Z}_{1}\mathbf{Z}_{1}^{T}\mathbf{V}^{-1}\mathbf{X}\},

\displaystyle l_{A{\sigma}_{\beta}^{2}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})=l_{{\sigma}_{\beta}^{2}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{trace}\{(\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{V}^{-1}\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{V}^{-1}\mathbf{Z}_{2}\mathbf{Z}_{2}^{T}\mathbf{V}^{-1}\mathbf{X}\},

\displaystyle l_{A{\sigma}_{\gamma}^{2}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})=l_{{\sigma}_{\gamma}^{2}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{trace}\{(\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{V}^{-1}\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^{T}\mathbf{V}^{-1}\mathbf{Z}_{3}\mathbf{Z}_{3}^{T}\mathbf{V}^{-1}\mathbf{X}\},

We show that the REML estimator is asymptotically equivalent to the maximum likelihood estimator by showing that the
contribution from the adjustment terms to the estimating function is asymptotically negligible. This yields the following
theorem which we prove in Section
6

Theorem 3
.

Suppose Condition A holds. Then there is a solution
\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{R}
of the REML estimating equations satisfying
|\mathbf{K}^{1/2}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{R}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})|=O_{p}(1)
and

\displaystyle\mathbf{K}^{1/2}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{R}-\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}})=o_{p}(1),

so Theorem 1 applies to the REML estimator.

and

, we can consistently estimate the within-cell variance
\sigma_{e}^{2}
. However, estimates for the row variance
{\sigma}_{\alpha}^{2}
, the column variance
{\sigma}_{\beta}^{2}
, and the interaction variance
\sigma_{\gamma}^{2}
will not be consistent.

2.

When both
m
and
g
approach infinity with
h
held fixed, we can consistently estimate the row variance
{\sigma}_{\alpha}^{2}
, the interaction variance
\sigma_{\gamma}^{2}
, and the within-cell variance
\sigma_{e}^{2}
. However, the column variance
{\sigma}_{\beta}^{2}
will not be consistently estimated.

3.

When both
m
and
h
approach infinity with
g
held fixed, we can consistently estimate the column variance
{\sigma}_{\beta}^{2}
, the interaction variance
\sigma_{\gamma}^{2}
, and the within-cell variance
\sigma_{e}^{2}
. However, the row variance
{\sigma}_{\alpha}^{2}
will not be consistently estimated.

These considerations motivate requiring all
g\to\infty
,
h\to\infty
and
m\to\infty
.

The asymptotic representation shows that the influence function of the maximum likelihood and REML estimators under the model is given by the summands of
\mathbf{B}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\phi}
. For the case
0\leq\eta<\infty
, this function is
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{IF}}=[\mathcal{IF}_{\xi_{0}},\boldsymbol{\mathcal{IF}}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1}}^{T},\mathcal{IF}_{{\sigma}_{\alpha}^{2}},\boldsymbol{\mathcal{IF}}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{2}}^{T},\mathcal{IF}_{{\sigma}_{\beta}^{2}},\boldsymbol{\mathcal{IF}}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{3}}^{T},\mathcal{IF}_{\sigma_{\gamma}^{2}},\boldsymbol{\mathcal{IF}}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{4}}^{T},\mathcal{IF}_{\sigma_{e}^{2}}]^{T}
,
where

\displaystyle\mathcal{IF}_{\xi_{0}}

\displaystyle=[1+(\eta-\eta^{1/2})\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(b)T}\mathbf{D}_{2}^{-1}\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(b)}]\sum_{i=1}^{g}\alpha_{i}+\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(a)T}\mathbf{D}_{1}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{g}\mathbf{x}_{i(c)}^{(a)}\alpha_{i}

\displaystyle\qquad+\eta[1+(\eta^{1/2}\dot{\sigma}_{\alpha}^{2}/\dot{\tau}+\eta\dot{\sigma}_{\beta}^{2}/\dot{\tau})\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(b)T}\mathbf{D}_{2}^{-1}\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(b)}]\sum_{j=1}^{h}\beta_{j}-\eta^{1/2}\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(b)T}\mathbf{D}_{2}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{h}\mathbf{x}_{j}^{(b)}\beta_{j},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{IF}}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{D}_{1}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{g}\mathbf{x}_{i(c)}^{(a)}\alpha_{i},\qquad\mathcal{IF}_{{\sigma}_{\alpha}^{2}}=\sum_{i=1}^{g}(\alpha_{i}^{2}-\dot{\sigma}_{\alpha}^{2}),

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{IF}}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{2}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{D}_{2}^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{h}\mathbf{x}_{j}^{(b)}\beta_{j}+\mathbf{D}_{2}^{-1}\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(b)}[\dot{\sigma}_{\beta}^{2}/\dot{\tau}(\eta-\eta^{1/2})\sum_{i=1}^{g}\alpha_{i}-(\dot{\sigma}_{\alpha}^{2}/\dot{\tau}+\eta^{3/2}\dot{\sigma}_{\beta}^{2}/\dot{\tau})\sum_{j=1}^{h}\beta_{j}],

\displaystyle\mathcal{IF}_{{\sigma}_{\beta}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{h}(\beta_{j}^{2}-\dot{\sigma}_{\beta}^{2}),\qquad\boldsymbol{\mathcal{IF}}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{3}}=\mathbf{D}_{3}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{g}\sum_{j=1}^{h}\mathbf{x}_{ij(c)}^{(ab)}\gamma_{ij},\qquad\mathcal{IF}_{\sigma_{\gamma}^{2}}=\sum_{i=1}^{g}\sum_{j=1}^{h}(\gamma_{ij}^{2}-\dot{\sigma}_{\gamma}^{2}),

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{IF}}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{4}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{D}_{4}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{g}\sum_{j=1}^{h}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\mathbf{x}_{ijk(c)}^{(w)}e_{ijk},\qquad\mathcal{IF}_{\sigma_{e}^{2}}=\sum_{i=1}^{g}\sum_{j=1}^{h}\sum_{k=1}^{m}(e_{ijk}^{2}-\dot{\sigma}_{e}^{2}).

Explicitly, when
\eta=0
at a point
[\alpha_{i},\beta_{j},\gamma_{ij},e_{ijk},\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(a)T},\mathbf{x}_{j}^{(b)T},\mathbf{x}_{ij}^{(ab)T},\mathbf{x}_{ijk}^{(w)T}]^{T}
(which we suppress in the notation),
the influence function can be simplified to

\displaystyle\mathcal{IF}_{\xi_{0}}=(1+\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(a)}\mathbf{D}_{1}^{-1}\mathbf{x}_{i(c)}^{(a)})\alpha_{i},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{IF}}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1}}=\mathbf{D}_{1}^{-1}\mathbf{x}_{i(c)}^{(a)}\alpha_{i},

\displaystyle\mathcal{IF}_{{\sigma}_{\alpha}^{2}}=\alpha_{i}^{2}-\dot{\sigma}_{\alpha}^{2},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{IF}}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{2}}=\mathbf{D}_{2}^{-1}\mathbf{x}_{j(c)}^{(b)}\beta_{j},

\displaystyle\mathcal{IF}_{{\sigma}_{\beta}^{2}}=\beta_{j}^{2}-\dot{\sigma}_{\beta}^{2},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{IF}}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{3}}=\mathbf{D}_{3}^{-1}\mathbf{x}_{ij(c)}^{(ab)}\gamma_{ij},

\displaystyle\mathcal{IF}_{\sigma_{\gamma}^{2}}=\gamma_{ij}^{2}-\dot{\sigma}_{\gamma}^{2},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{IF}}_{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{4}}=\mathbf{D}_{4}^{-1}\mathbf{x}_{ijk(c)}^{(w)}e_{ijk},

\displaystyle\mathcal{IF}_{\sigma_{e}^{2}}=e_{ijk}^{2}-\dot{\sigma}_{e}^{2}.

These expression are not easy to obtain directly because the row, column, interaction and within-cell parameters are estimated at different rates. As is well-known, the estimators are not robust because the influence function is unbounded in the covariates, random effects, and errors. Analogous results can be obtained for the case
\eta=\infty
.

The central limit theorem allows us to construct asymptotic confidence intervals for the parameters in the model.
Asymptotic
100(1-b)\%
confidence intervals for
\dot{\xi}_{1q},\dot{\xi}_{2q},\dot{\xi}_{3q},\dot{\xi}_{4q}
are given straightforwardly by

\displaystyle[\xi_{1q}-\mathcal{N}_{b}\hat{\sigma}_{\alpha}\hat{d}^{*1/2}_{1qq}/g^{1/2},\xi_{1q}+\mathcal{N}_{b}\hat{\sigma}_{\alpha}\hat{d}^{*1/2}_{1qq}/g^{1/2}],

\displaystyle[\xi_{2q}-\mathcal{N}_{b}\hat{\sigma}_{\beta}\hat{d}^{*1/2}_{2qq}/h^{1/2},\xi_{2k}+\mathcal{N}_{b}\hat{\sigma}_{\beta}\hat{d}^{*1/2}_{2qq}/h^{1/2}],

\displaystyle[\xi_{3q}-\mathcal{N}_{b}\hat{\sigma}_{\gamma}\hat{d}^{*1/2}_{3qq}/(gh)^{1/2},\xi_{3q}+\mathcal{N}_{b}\hat{\sigma}_{\gamma}\hat{d}^{*1/2}_{3qq}/(gh)^{1/2}],

\displaystyle[\xi_{4q}-\mathcal{N}_{b}\hat{\sigma}_{e}\hat{d}^{*1/2}_{4qq}/n^{1/2},\xi_{4q}+\mathcal{N}_{b}\hat{\sigma}_{e}\hat{d}^{*1/2}_{4qq}/n^{1/2}].

Our theoretical results show that the row, column, interaction, and within-cell parameters are estimated at different rates. From the structure of
\mathbf{F}
, even without assuming normality, the maximum likelihood and REML estimators for the row and column parameters are independent of both the interaction parameter estimators and the within-cell parameter estimators. Additionally, the interaction parameter estimators are independent of the within-cell parameter estimators. However, there is dependence between the estimators of the row and column parameters when
0<\eta<\infty
.
This implies that when we view the estimators of the row and column parameters together, the three sets of estimators (row and column, interaction, and within-cell) are asymptotically orthogonal.

For scenarios where
\eta=0
or
\eta=\infty
, we can treat the estimators for the row and column parameters separately, with all four sets being asymptotically orthogonal.
Further insights show asymptotic orthogonality between the row slope and its variance, the column slope and its variance, the interaction slope and its variance, and the within-cell slope and its variance. A notable exception exists for the intercept, which is only asymptotically orthogonal to the row (or column) variance when the random effect distribution of the row (or column) is symmetrically centered around zero.

In working with the model (
4
), we estimate all parameters. If we have no row covariates, we discard
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1}
; if we have no column covariates, we discard
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{2}
; if we have no interaction covariates, we discard
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{3}
; if we have no within-cell covariates, we discard
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{4}
. The results for these cases can be obtained as special cases of the general results by deleting the components of vectors and the rows and columns of matrices corresponding to the discarded parameter. If there are no row covariates in the model (there is no
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1}
in the model), we drop rows and columns 2 to
p_{a}+1
from
\mathbf{F}
etc. There is a corresponding simplification to Condition A4. Finally, we have treated the covariates in the model as fixed, conditioning on them when they are random. As noted by
[
26
]
, when the covariatse are random, it makes sense to treat them as having a similar covariance structure to the response.

The proofs of Theorems

and

6.1
Proof of Theorem
1

Write

\begin{split}\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\omega})=&\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\phi}-\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}^{1/2}(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})+T_{1}+T_{2}(\boldsymbol{\omega})+T_{3}(\boldsymbol{\omega}),\end{split}

(11)

where
\mathbf{B}=\lim_{g,h,m\to\infty}-\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}\operatorname{E}\triangledown\boldsymbol{\psi}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}
,
T_{1}=\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}[\boldsymbol{\psi}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})-\boldsymbol{\phi}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})]
,

T_{2}(\boldsymbol{\omega})=\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}\operatorname{E}[\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\omega})-\boldsymbol{\psi}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})]+\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}^{1/2}(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})
and
T_{3}(\boldsymbol{\omega})=\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}[\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\omega})-\boldsymbol{\psi}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})-\operatorname{E}\{\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\omega})-\boldsymbol{\psi}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})\}]
.
For
0<M<\infty
, let
\boldsymbol{N}=\{\boldsymbol{\omega}:|\mathbf{K}^{1/2}(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})|\leq M\}
define a local (shrinking) ball centered at
\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
. If we can show that
|T_{1}|=o(1)
,
\underset{\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\boldsymbol{N}}{\sup}|T_{2}(\boldsymbol{\omega})|=o_{p}(1)
and
\underset{\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\boldsymbol{N}}{\sup}|T_{3}(\boldsymbol{\omega})|=o_{p}(1)
,
respectively, then uniformly on
|\mathbf{K}^{1/2}(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})|\leq M
, we have

\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\omega})=\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\phi}-\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}^{1/2}(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})+o_{p}(1).

(12)

Multiplying by
(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})^{T}\mathbf{K}^{1/2}
,

(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})^{T}\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\omega})=(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})^{T}\boldsymbol{\phi}-(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})^{T}\mathbf{K}^{1/2}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}^{1/2}(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})+o_{p}(1).

Since
\mathbf{B}
is positive definite, the right-hand side of (
12
) is negative for
M
sufficiently large. Therefore, according to Result 6.3.4 of
[
20
]
, a solution to the estimating equations exists in probability and satisfies
|\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})|=O_{p}(1)
, so
\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\in\boldsymbol{N}
. This allows us to substitute
\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
for
\boldsymbol{\omega}
in (
12
) and rearrange the terms to obtain the asymptotic representation for
\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
; the central limit theorem then follows from Lemma
2
.

It remains to show that the remainder terms
T_{1}
,
T_{2}(\boldsymbol{\omega})
, and
T_{3}(\boldsymbol{\omega})
in (
11
) are of sufficiently small order that they can be ignored. In Lemma
1
, we establish
|T_{1}|=o_{p}(1)
by showing that the result holds for each component of
T_{1}=\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}[\boldsymbol{\psi}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})-\boldsymbol{\phi}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})]
, by applying Chebychev’s inequality, and calculating the variances of the components.

The method used for addressing
T_{2}(\boldsymbol{\omega})
and
T_{3}(\boldsymbol{\omega})
is inspired by

]
who applied similar arguments to one-step regression estimators. The approach was extended to maximum likelihood and REML estimators in linear mixed models by

. For

\displaystyle\underset{\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\boldsymbol{N}}{\sup}|T_{2}(\boldsymbol{\omega})|

\displaystyle\quad+\underset{\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\boldsymbol{N}}{\sup}|\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}\operatorname{E}\triangledown\boldsymbol{\psi}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})+\mathbf{B}\mathbf{K}^{1/2}(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})|

\displaystyle\leq\underset{\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\boldsymbol{N}}{\sup}|\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}[\operatorname{E}\triangledown\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})-\operatorname{E}\triangledown\boldsymbol{\psi}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})](\boldsymbol{\omega}-\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})|+M\|-\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}\operatorname{E}\triangledown\boldsymbol{\psi}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}-\mathbf{B}\|

\displaystyle\leq M\underset{\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\boldsymbol{N}}{\sup}\|\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}[\operatorname{E}\triangledown\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})-\operatorname{E}\triangledown\boldsymbol{\psi}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})]\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}\|+M\|\mathbf{B}_{n}-\mathbf{B}\|,

where the

i
th row of the matrix
\boldsymbol{\Omega}
and each row of
\boldsymbol{\Omega}
lies between
\boldsymbol{\omega}
and
\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}

. In Lemma

, it is demonstrated that

. Additionally, in Lemma

, we show

.
Moving to

, we decompose

into the set of

smaller cubes

\mathcal{C}=\{\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})\}
, where

. We first show that

|T_{3}(\boldsymbol{\omega})|
holds over the set of indices. Using Chebyshev’s inequality, for any
\eta>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\operatorname{Pr}\left(|\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}[\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})-\boldsymbol{\psi}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})-\operatorname{E}\{\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})-\boldsymbol{\psi}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})\}]|>\eta\right)

\displaystyle\leq\eta^{-2}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\operatorname{E}|\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}[\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})-\boldsymbol{\psi}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})-\operatorname{E}\{\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})-\boldsymbol{\psi}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})\}]|^{2}

\displaystyle=\eta^{-2}g^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\operatorname{E}|\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(a)}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})-\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(a)}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})-\operatorname{E}\{\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(a)}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})-\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(a)}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})\}|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\eta^{-2}h^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\operatorname{E}|\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(b)}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})-\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(b)}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})-\operatorname{E}\{\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(b)}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})-\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(b)}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})\}|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\eta^{-2}(gh)^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\operatorname{E}|\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(ab)}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})-\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(ab)}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})-\operatorname{E}\{\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(ab)}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})-\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(ab)}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})\}|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\eta^{-2}n^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\operatorname{E}|\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(w)}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})-\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(w)}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})-\operatorname{E}\{\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(w)}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})-\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(w)}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})\}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\eta^{-2}g^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\operatorname{trace}[\operatorname{Var}\{\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(a)}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})-\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(a)}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})\}]+\eta^{-2}h^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\operatorname{trace}[\operatorname{Var}\{\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(b)}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})-\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(b)}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})\}]

\displaystyle\quad+\eta^{-2}(gh)^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\operatorname{trace}[\operatorname{Var}\{\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(ab)}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})-\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(w)}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})\}]+\eta^{-2}n^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\operatorname{trace}[\operatorname{Var}\{\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(w)}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})-\boldsymbol{\psi}^{(w)}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})\}].

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\operatorname{Pr}\left(|\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}[\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})-\boldsymbol{\psi}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})-\operatorname{E}\{\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})-\boldsymbol{\psi}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})\}]|>\eta\right)

\displaystyle\leq\eta^{-2}g^{-1}N(p_{a}+2)L+\eta^{-2}h^{-1}N(p_{b}+1)L+\eta^{-2}(gh)^{-1}N(p_{ab}+1)L+\eta^{-2}n^{-1}N(p_{w}+1)L

\displaystyle=o(1),

using fact that

. Using Taylor expansion, we get

\displaystyle\underset{1\leq k\leq N}{\max}\underset{\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})}{\sup}|\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}[\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\omega})-\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})-\operatorname{E}\{\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\omega})-\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})\}]|

\displaystyle=\underset{1\leq k\leq N}{\max}\underset{\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})}{\sup}|\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}\{\nabla\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{k})(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})-\operatorname{E}\nabla\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{k})(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k})\}|

\displaystyle\leq M\underset{\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\boldsymbol{N}}{\sup}g^{-1/4}\|\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}\{\nabla\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})-\operatorname{E}\nabla\boldsymbol{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})\}\mathbf{K}^{-1/2}\|,

where the rows of
\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{k}
are between
\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{k}
and
\boldsymbol{\omega}
.
The result follows from Lemma
6
.

\displaystyle({1}/{\lambda_{1}}-{1}/{\dot{\lambda}_{1}})^{2}\leq(gh)^{-1}m^{-2}M_{0},\qquad({1}/{\lambda_{2}}-{1}/{\dot{\lambda}_{2}})^{2}\leq g^{-1}h^{-2}m^{-2}M_{0},

\displaystyle({1}/{\lambda_{3}}-{1}/{\dot{\lambda}_{3}})^{2}\leq g^{-2}h^{-1}m^{-2}M_{0},\qquad({1}/{\lambda_{4}}-{1}/{\dot{\lambda}_{4}})^{2}\leq g^{-1}h^{-2}m^{-2}M_{0}\text{ (or }\leq g^{-2}h^{-1}m^{-2}M_{0}),

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}[(\alpha_{i}-\bar{\alpha})+(\bar{\gamma}_{i.}-\bar{\gamma})+(\bar{e}_{i.}-\bar{e})]\leq M_{0},\qquad\operatorname{Var}[(\beta_{j}-\bar{\beta})+(\bar{\gamma}_{.j}-\bar{\gamma})+(\bar{e}_{.j}-\bar{e})]\leq M_{0},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}[(\gamma_{ij}-\bar{\gamma}_{i.}-\bar{\gamma}_{.j}+\bar{\gamma})+(\bar{e}_{ij}-\bar{e}_{i.}-\bar{e}_{.j}+\bar{e})]\leq M_{0},\qquad\operatorname{Var}(\bar{\alpha}+\bar{\beta}+\bar{\gamma}+\bar{e})\leq g^{-1}M_{0}\text{ (or }\leq h^{-1}M_{0}),

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}[\{(\alpha_{i}-\bar{\alpha})+(\bar{\gamma}_{i.}-\bar{\gamma})+(\bar{e}_{i.}-\bar{e})\}^{2}]\leq M_{0},\qquad\operatorname{Var}[\{(\beta_{j}-\bar{\beta})+(\bar{\gamma}_{.j}-\bar{\gamma})+(\bar{e}_{.j}-\bar{e})\}^{2}]\leq M_{0},

For example, we have

\displaystyle l_{\xi_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})-l_{\xi_{0}}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{\lambda_{4}}[(\dot{\xi}_{0}-\xi_{0})+\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(a)T}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{1}-\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1})+\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(b)T}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{2}-\boldsymbol{\xi}_{2})+\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(ab)T}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{3}-\boldsymbol{\xi}_{3})+\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{(w)T}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{4}-\boldsymbol{\xi}_{4})]

\displaystyle+(\frac{n}{\lambda_{4}}-\frac{n}{\dot{\lambda}_{4}})(\bar{\alpha}+\bar{\beta}+\bar{\gamma}+\bar{e}),

and so

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}[l_{\xi_{0}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})-l_{\xi_{0}}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}})]=(\frac{n}{\lambda_{4}}-\frac{n}{\dot{\lambda}_{4}})^{2}\operatorname{Var}(\bar{\alpha}+\bar{\beta}+\bar{\gamma}+\bar{e})\leq M_{0}^{2}\leq L.

The argument for each of the remaining term is similar and details can be found in Supplementary Section S.6.3.

7
Discussion

We have established asymptotic theory for the two-way crossed random effect with interaction model. We imposed mild conditions to derive the asymptotic properties of its estimators. Importantly, we did not constrain the rates for
g
,
h
, and
m
to diverge to infinity, and we assumed moment conditions rather than normality.
The main challenges in this research were to calculate the inverse of
\mathbf{V}
, to obtain tractable expressions for the likelihood estimating equations, and to group the parameters into four clear groups (row, column, interaction, and within-cell parameters) corresponding to different levels of information in the data. This approach eliminates the need for rate restrictions on
g
,
h
, and
m
, and also produces an elegantly structured asymptotic covariance matrix.

The scope of this paper is confined to the two-way crossed random effect with interaction model, which serves as a canonical example of crossed random effect models, featuring both the crossed structure and interaction terms. Our analysis of this model’s asymptotic properties sets the stage for broadening these approaches to a wider variety of crossed effect models.
We addressed the balanced case to use the simplifications afforded by the Kronecker product notation in computation and derivation. Despite this simplification, the calculations remain challenging, with all details relegated to the supplementary material.
Expanding our approach to the unbalanced case follows the same principles, though the resulting expressions and derivations become even more complicated. Our theoretical and simulation results suggest that with
g
and
h
increasing indefinitely, we can achieve asymptotic normality for all estimators. Importantly, our framework accommodates an infinite increase in
m
, a necessary condition for the consistent prediction of random effects as outlined by
[
10
]
.
For instance,
[
17
,
16
]
have shown that
m
tending to infinity is required to establish asymptotic normality for the best linear unbiased predictor (EBLUP) and for model-based small area estimators. Predictions of random effects for the two-way crossed random effect with interaction model will be pursued in future work.

B
Inverse of
\mathbf{V}

Following the algorithm of
[
24
]
, we define

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}_{3}=\left[\begin{matrix}1&0\\
1&g\end{matrix}\right]\otimes\left[\begin{matrix}1&0\\
1&h\end{matrix}\right]\otimes\left[\begin{matrix}1&0\\
1&m\end{matrix}\right].

Then we let
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\mathbf{T}_{3}\boldsymbol{\theta}
, so that

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\left[\begin{matrix}1&0&0&0&0&0&0&0\\
1&m&0&0&0&0&0&0\\
1&0&h&0&0&0&0&0\\
1&m&h&hm&0&0&0&0\\
1&0&0&0&g&0&0&0\\
1&m&0&0&g&gm&0&0\\
1&0&h&0&g&0&gh&0\\
1&m&h&hm&g&gm&gh&ghm\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}\sigma_{e}^{2}\\
\sigma_{\gamma}^{2}\\
0\\
\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}\\
0\\
\sigma_{\beta}^{2}\\
0\\
0\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}\sigma_{e}^{2}\\
\sigma_{e}^{2}+m\sigma_{\gamma}^{2}\\
\sigma_{e}^{2}\\
\sigma_{e}^{2}+m\sigma_{\gamma}^{2}+hm\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}\\
\sigma_{e}^{2}\\
\sigma_{e}^{2}+m\sigma_{\gamma}^{2}+gm\sigma_{\beta}^{2}\\
\sigma_{e}^{2}\\
\sigma_{e}^{2}+m\sigma_{\gamma}^{2}+hm\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}+gm\sigma_{\beta}^{2}\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}\lambda_{0}\\
\lambda_{1}\\
\lambda_{0}\\
\lambda_{2}\\
\lambda_{0}\\
\lambda_{3}\\
\lambda_{0}\\
\lambda_{4}\end{matrix}\right],

where

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{e}^{2},

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{e}^{2}+m\sigma_{\gamma}^{2},

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{e}^{2}+m\sigma_{\gamma}^{2}+hm\sigma_{\alpha}^{2},

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{e}^{2}+m\sigma_{\gamma}^{2}+gm\sigma_{\beta}^{2}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\lambda_{4}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{e}^{2}+m\sigma_{\gamma}^{2}+hm\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}+gm\sigma_{\beta}^{2}.

With
\boldsymbol{\nu}
having elements that are reciprocals of the elements of
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
, the equation
\boldsymbol{{\mathsf{t}}}=\mathbf{T}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\nu}
gives

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{{\mathsf{t}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{ghm}\left[\begin{matrix}g&0\\
-1&-1\end{matrix}\right]\otimes\left[\begin{matrix}h&0\\
-1&-1\end{matrix}\right]\otimes\left[\begin{matrix}m&0\\
-1&-1\end{matrix}\right]\boldsymbol{\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{ghm}\left[\begin{matrix}ghm&0&0&0&0&0&0&\\
-gh&gh&0&0&0&0&0&0\\
-gm&0&gm&0&0&0&0&0\\
g&-g&-g&g&0&0&0&0\\
-hm&0&0&0&hm&0&0&0\\
h&-h&0&0&-h&h&0&0\\
m&0&-m&0&-m&0&m&0\\
-1&1&1&-1&1&-1&-1&1\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}\\
\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}\\
\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}\\
\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}}\\
\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}\\
\frac{1}{\lambda_{3}}\\
\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}\\
\frac{1}{\lambda_{4}}\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}\mathsf{t}_{000}\\
\mathsf{t}_{001}\\
0\\
\mathsf{t}_{011}\\
0\\
\mathsf{t}_{101}\\
0\\
\mathsf{t}_{111}\\
\end{matrix}\right],

where

\displaystyle\mathsf{t}_{000}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}},

\displaystyle\mathsf{t}_{001}=\frac{1}{m}(\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}),

\displaystyle\mathsf{t}_{011}=\frac{1}{hm}(\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}),

\displaystyle\mathsf{t}_{101}=\frac{1}{gm}(\frac{1}{\lambda_{3}}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}),

\displaystyle\mathsf{t}_{111}=\frac{1}{ghm}(\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{3}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{4}}).

Then the inverse of
\mathbf{V}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}^{-1}=

\displaystyle\mathsf{t}_{000}(\mathbf{I}_{g}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{h}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{m})+\mathsf{t}_{001}(\mathbf{I}_{g}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{h}\otimes\mathbf{J}_{m})+\mathsf{t}_{011}(\mathbf{I}_{g}\otimes\mathbf{J}_{h}\otimes\mathbf{J}_{m})

\displaystyle+\mathsf{t}_{101}(\mathbf{J}_{g}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{h}\otimes\mathbf{J}_{m})+\mathsf{t}_{111}(\mathbf{J}_{g}\otimes\mathbf{J}_{h}\otimes\mathbf{J}_{m}).

Substituting the
\mathsf{t}
s with
\lambda
s, we rewrite the inverse of
\mathbf{V}
as

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{t}_{000}(\mathbf{I}_{g}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{h}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{m})+\mathsf{t}_{001}(\mathbf{I}_{g}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{h}\otimes\mathbf{J}_{m})+\mathsf{t}_{011}(\mathbf{I}_{g}\otimes\mathbf{J}_{h}\otimes\mathbf{J}_{m})

\displaystyle\quad+\mathsf{t}_{101}(\mathbf{J}_{g}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{h}\otimes\mathbf{J}_{m})+\mathsf{t}_{111}(\mathbf{J}_{g}\otimes\mathbf{J}_{h}\otimes\mathbf{J}_{m})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}(\mathbf{I}_{g}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{h}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{m})+\frac{1}{m}(\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}})(\mathbf{I}_{g}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{h}\otimes\mathbf{J}_{m})+\frac{1}{hm}(\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}})(\mathbf{I}_{g}\otimes\mathbf{J}_{h}\otimes\mathbf{J}_{m})

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{gm}(\frac{1}{\lambda_{3}}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}})(\mathbf{J}_{g}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{h}\otimes\mathbf{J}_{m})+\frac{1}{ghm}(\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{3}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{4}})(\mathbf{J}_{g}\otimes\mathbf{J}_{h}\otimes\mathbf{J}_{m})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}(\mathbf{I}_{g}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{h}\otimes\mathbf{C}_{m})+\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}(\mathbf{C}_{g}\otimes\mathbf{C}_{h}\otimes\bar{\mathbf{J}}_{m})+\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}}(\mathbf{C}_{g}\otimes\bar{\mathbf{J}}_{h}\otimes\bar{\mathbf{J}}_{m})+\frac{1}{\lambda_{3}}(\bar{\mathbf{J}}_{g}\otimes\mathbf{C}_{h}\otimes\bar{\mathbf{J}}_{m})

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{\lambda_{4}}(\bar{\mathbf{J}}_{g}\otimes\bar{\mathbf{J}}_{h}\otimes\bar{\mathbf{J}}_{m}),

where
\bar{\mathbf{J}}_{g}=\frac{1}{g}{\mathbf{J}}_{g}
and
\mathbf{C}_{g}=\mathbf{I}_{g}-\bar{\mathbf{J}}_{g}
. We have the identities

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}(\bar{\mathbf{J}}_{g})=1,\qquad\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{C}_{g})=g-1,\qquad\mathbf{C}_{g}\mathbf{J}_{g}=\boldsymbol{0},\qquad\bar{\mathbf{J}}_{g}\mathbf{J}_{g}=\mathbf{J}_{g},\qquad\boldsymbol{1}_{g}^{T}\mathbf{C}_{g}=\boldsymbol{0},\qquad\bar{\mathbf{J}}_{g}\boldsymbol{1}_{g}=\boldsymbol{1}_{g}.

Complex surfaces with many algebraic structures
Anna Abasheva, Rodion Déev

Abstract
We find new examples of complex surfaces with countably many non-isomorphic algebraic structures. Here is one such example: take an elliptic curve E E in ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2} and blow up nine general points on E E . Then the complement M M of the strict transform of E E in the blow-up has countably many algebraic structures. Moreover, each algebraic structure comes from an embedding of M M into a blow-up of ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2} in nine points lying on an elliptic curve F ≄ E F\not\simeq E . We classify algebraic structures on M M using a Hopf transform : a way of constructing a new surface by cutting out an elliptic curve and pasting a different one. Next, we introduce the notion of an analytic K-theory of varieties . Manipulations with the example above lead us to prove that classes of all elliptic curves in this K-theory coincide. To put in another way, all motivic measures on complex algebraic varieties that take equal values on biholomorphic varieties do not distinguish elliptic curves.

Introduction

A compact complex analytic variety
X^{an}
has at most one algebraic structure; that is to say, there is at most one algebraic variety
Y
such that
Y^{an}\cong X^{an}
. However, this is false in the non-compact world. We do not have general techniques to describe the set of algebraic structures on a non-compact complex variety. Available results show that a variety can have infinitely many of algebraic structures. We briefly review examples in dimension two.

•

Let
C
be an affine curve of positive genus. Then
C^{an}\times\mathbb{C}
admits uncountably many non-isomorphic affine structures
[
Jel15
]
. Indeed, every line bundle on
C
is analytically trivial, hence its total space is biholomorphic to
C^{an}\times\mathbb{C}
.

•

The surface
\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
is biholomorphic to the unique non-trivial affine bundle of rank one and degree zero over any elliptic curve
[
Nee89
, Thm. 6.12]
. Hence, it has uncountably many non-isomorphic algebraic structures.

•

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve and
p\colon X\to C
an algebraic elliptic surface. Choose a smooth fiber
E=p^{-1}(x)
. For every
n>0
, we can find an elliptic fibration

, called a
log-transform
of

, that has a fiber of multiplicity
n
over

and is biholomorphic to
p
over

. The new surfaces

are algebraic unless
X\simeq E\times C

[
FM13
, Ch. I, Thm. 6.12]
. Hence the surface

has at least countably many algebraic structures.

•

We can construct even more algebraic structures on the surface
X-E
from the previous example using Shafarevich–Tate twists. We refer the reader to
[
FM13
, Ch. I, Sections 1.5.1, 1.5.3]
for more details.

Our construction in Theorem

We can summarize a Hopf transform as follows: take an elliptic curve on a surface, cut it out, and paste another elliptic curve instead. Thanks to a theorem of Arnold and Ueda (Theorem

), a square-zero elliptic curve

whose normal bundle

\mathbb{C}^{2}-\{0\}/\operatorname{diag}(\lambda,\mu)
and their finite quotients, we show that there exist countably many degree zero line bundles

over different elliptic curves

such that
\operatorname{Tot}(L)^{an}-E\cong\operatorname{Tot}(L^{\prime})^{an}-F
. We call such line bundles

and

analytically cobordant

with

Our notion of a Hopf transform was inspired by Koike and Uehara’s construction in

2.1
Analytic compactifications

Definition 2.1.1
.

Let

are exceptional.

The following classification theorem is due to Enoki
[
Eno82
, Theorem C-I
0
]
.

Theorem 2.1.2
.

Let
\operatorname{Tot}(L)
be the total space of a line bundle
L
of degree zero over an elliptic curve
E
. Then every minimal compactification
(S,C)
of
M
is of the following form:

1.

S\cong\mathbb{P}({\cal O}\oplus L)
is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over an elliptic curve;
C
is its section, and
(C)^{2}=0
.

2.

S
is a Hopf surface (Definition
2.2.1
), and

is an elliptic curve.

If
L
is non-torsion, the set of Hopf surfaces compactifying

is countable, as we will see in Theorem

. For now, let us note that all such compactifications contain at least two elliptic curves.

2.2
Hopf surfaces

Definition 2.2.1
.

[
Kod66
, Sect. 10]

A
Hopf surface
is a compact complex surface whose universal cover is
\mathbb{C}^{2}-\{0\}
. A Hopf surface is called
primary
if its fundamental group is
\mathbb{Z}
, otherwise it is called
secondary
.

2.2.2.

All Hopf surfaces are non-Kähler. By
[
Kod66
, Sect. 10, p. 695]
every primary Hopf surface
X
is biholomorphic to a quotient
\mathbb{C}^{2}-\{0\}/\Gamma
where
\Gamma
is either a linear diagonal operator or of the form

\Gamma(x,y)=(\alpha^{n}x+y^{n},\alpha y).

In the second case, a Hopf surface has only one irreducible curve: the image of the
x
-axis. Therefore, such surfaces do not arise as compactifications of
\operatorname{Tot}(L)
.

2.2.3.

Let us study the case when
\Gamma=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda,\mu)
. In order for
\mathbb{C}^{2}-\{0\}/\Gamma
to be compact, the absolute values of the eigenvalues must be either both greater than one or both less than one. By replacing
\Gamma
with
\Gamma^{-1}
if necessary, we may assume the latter.

Definition 2.2.4
.

A
diagonal Hopf surface
is
\mathbb{C}^{2}-\{0\}/\operatorname{diag}(\lambda,\mu)
for
|\lambda|<1
,
|\mu|<1
. We will denote it by
H(\lambda,\mu)

2.2.5.

One can show that two Hopf surfaces
H(\lambda,\mu)
and
H(\lambda^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
are isomorphic if and only if
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
and
\mu=\mu^{\prime}
(up to a permutation). All automorphisms of
H(\lambda,\mu)
are induced by a linear diagonal automorphism of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

2.2.6.

If
\lambda^{n}=\mu^{m}
for some non-zero integers
n
and
m
then
H(\lambda,\mu)
is an elliptic surface with smooth but possibly non-reduced fibers
[
BHPVdV15
, Ch. V, Prop. 18.2]
. All elliptic curves in
X
are fibers, hence their normal bundles are torsion. Such a surface cannot be a compactification of
\operatorname{Tot}(L)
for a non-torsion line bundle
L
.

When
\lambda^{n}\neq\mu^{m}
for any pair of non-zero integers the surface
H(\lambda,\mu)
contains exactly two irreducible curves
[
BHPVdV15
, Ch. V, Prop. 18.2]
. They are the images of the axes under the quotient map and are isomorphic to
E_{\lambda}:=\mathbb{C}^{*}/\lambda
and
E_{\mu}:=\mathbb{C}^{*}/\mu
. They have both square zero.

The complement to
E_{\mu}
in
H(\lambda,\mu)
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}/\operatorname{diag}(\lambda,\mu)
. It is the total space of a line bundle over
E_{\lambda}
, and the map to
E_{\lambda}
is the projection to the first coordinate.

2.2.7.

Every elliptic curve can be written as
\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau
for
\operatorname{Im}(\tau)>0
. The parameter
\tau
is defined modulo the
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-action. The map

induces an isomorphism

\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathbb{C}^{*}/q^{\tau}.

Here we use the shorthand
q^{\tau}
for

. The total space of every line bundle of degree zero over
\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau
is of the form

\mathrm{L}(\tau,A,B)=\mathbb{C}^{2}/\sim:\>\>\>\>\>\>(x,y)\sim(x+1,Ay);\>(x,y)\sim(x+\tau,By),

(1)

where

. These numbers are uniquely defined once we fix
\tau
. Similarly, the total space of a line bundle of degree zero over
E_{\lambda}=\mathbb{C}^{*}/\lambda
can be written as

\mathcal{L}(\lambda,\mu)=\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}/\operatorname{diag}(\lambda,\mu),

(2)

where
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

2.2.8.

The class of
\mathcal{L}(\lambda,\mu)
in
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(E_{\lambda})\simeq E_{\lambda}
is
\mu\pmod{\lambda^{\mathbb{Z}}}

[
Kod66
, Sect. 10, p. 696]
. In particular, line bundles
\mathcal{L}(\lambda,\mu)
and
\mathcal{L}(\lambda,\mu^{\prime})
are isomorphic if and only if
\mu^{\prime}=\mu\lambda^{n}
.

2.2.9.

A line bundle on an elliptic curve can be represented as
\mathrm{L}(\tau,A,B)
or
\mathcal{L}(\lambda,\mu)
. Let us figure out how to switch between the two descriptions. Write

for a number

. Then we have a linear isomorphism of line bundles

\alpha_{\tau,u,B}\colon\mathrm{L}(\tau,q^{u},B)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathcal{L}(q^{\tau},q^{-u\tau}B)

(3)

Note that

The isomorphism (

2.2.11.

Consider a translation

on the curve
\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau

has degree zero, hence it is isomorphic to its pullback along the translation. The isomorphism can be written explicitly as

\displaystyle t_{s}^{*}\colon\mathrm{L}(\tau,A,B)\to\mathrm{L}(\tau,A,B)

When we write

\displaystyle t_{s}^{*}\colon\mathcal{L}(q^{\tau},q^{-u\tau}B)\to\mathcal{L}(q^{\tau},q^{-u\tau}B)

This map extends to the automorphism

Consider the automorphism

2.2.13.

Every automorphism of

2.3
Secondary Hopf surfaces

2.3.1.

We have just classified primary Hopf surfaces that arise as compactifications of

[

is a secondary Hopf surface containing an elliptic curve with non-torsion normal bundle. Then

is a quotient of a diagonal Hopf surface

by an operator

and

are primitive

[
Kod66

Theorem 2.3.2

Let
L
be a degree-zero non-torsion line bundle on an elliptic curve

\operatorname{Tot}(L)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\to}}H
of
\operatorname{Tot}(L)
into a Hopf surface (primary or secondary) is countable.

Proof:
.

We know the claim for embeddings into primary Hopf surfaces (Proposition
2.2.14
), hence it is enough to assume that
H
is a secondary Hopf surface. By
2.3.1
, every secondary Hopf surface compactifying
\operatorname{Tot}(L)
must be of the form
H/\operatorname{diag}(q^{1/n},q^{r/n})
for
(r,n)=1
, where
H
is a diagonal Hopf surface. Given an embedding of
\operatorname{Tot}(L)
into
H/\operatorname{diag}(q^{1/n},q^{r/n})
, we can construct an embedding of
\operatorname{Tot}(f^{*}L)
to
H
, where
f\colon E^{\prime}\to E
is a quotient by an
n
-torsion element of
E^{\prime}
. The set of possible
f
is countable, and so is the set of equivalence classes of embeddings of
\operatorname{Tot}(f^{*}L)
into a primary Hopf surface, hence the claim.
∎

2.4
Hopf duality and analytic cobordance

2.4.1.

Let
L\to E
be a non-trivial degree-zero line bundle. The ruled surface
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{E}\oplus L)
contains two sections:
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O})
and
\mathbb{P}(L)
. The map
\operatorname{Tot}(L)\to\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}\oplus L)
,
v\mapsto[1:v]
defines a biholomorphism between
\operatorname{Tot}(L)
and
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}\oplus L)-\mathbb{P}(L)
. Similarly,
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}\oplus L)-\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O})
is biholomorphic to
\operatorname{Tot}(L^{-1})
. Hence the surface
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{E}\oplus L)
is a compactification of total spaces of dual line bundles:
\operatorname{Tot}(L)
and
\operatorname{Tot}(L^{-1})
. The analogous property of the diagonal primary Hopf surfaces motivates the following definition.

Definition 2.4.2
.

Let
L\to E
and
L^{\prime}\to E^{\prime}
be two degree-zero line bundles on elliptic curves. They are called

•

Hopf dual
if there exists a Hopf surface
H
with two elliptic curves
E_{\lambda}
and
E_{\mu}
such that
H-E_{\mu}\simeq\operatorname{Tot}(L)
and
H-E_{\lambda}\simeq\operatorname{Tot}(L^{\prime})
;

•

analytically cobordant
if
L^{-1}\to E
and
L^{\prime}\to E^{\prime}
are either Hopf dual, or
E=E^{\prime}
and
L=L^{\prime}
.

By Proposition
2.2.14
, any non-torsion line bundle admits a countable number of Hopf dual and analytically cobordant line bundles. The base curves of analytically cobordant line bundles are in general non-isomorphic (
2.2.10
).

2.4.3.

Let
L\to E
and
L^{\prime}\to E^{\prime}
be Hopf dual line bundles. Then the spaces
\operatorname{Tot}(L)-0_{L}
and
\operatorname{Tot}(L^{\prime})-0_{L^{\prime}}
are biholomorphic, where
0_{\Xi}\subset\operatorname{Tot}(\Xi)
denotes the zero section. Indeed, both spaces are biholomorphic to
H(\lambda,\mu)-\left(E_{\lambda}\cup E_{\mu}\right)
. The biholomorphism sends the neighborhood of the zero section in one to the neighborhood of the infinity section in the other and vice versa. Similarly, let
L\to E
and
L^{\prime}\to E^{\prime}
be analytically cobordant line bundles. Then
\operatorname{Tot}(L)-0_{L}
and
\operatorname{Tot}(L^{\prime})-0_{L^{\prime}}
are biholomorphic so that a neighbourhood of the zero section in one maps to the neighbourhood of the zero section in the other.

2.4.4.

Of course, if
E\not\cong E^{\prime}
, the biholomorphism
\operatorname{Tot}(L)-0_{L}\simeq\operatorname{Tot}(L^{\prime})-0_{L^{\prime}}
cannot be extended to the zero section. To internalize it, let us understand the behavior of fibers of the bundle under this biholomorphism. Let
H=H(\lambda,\mu)
be a Hopf surface. The fibers of
H-E_{\mu}=\mathcal{L}(\lambda,\mu)\to E_{\lambda}
are vertical lines. When we project
\mathbb{C}^{2}-\{0\}
to
H(\mu,\lambda)
, we identify vertical lines
x=c,x=ac,x=a^{2}c
etc. Hence, the image of a vertical line in
H(\mu,\lambda)
is a non-closed horn-like subspace with the curve
E_{\mu}
in its closure.

Proposition 2.4.5
.

Let
L\to E
and
L^{\prime}\to E^{\prime}
be two line bundles on elliptic curves. Then

1.

L
and
L^{\prime}
are Hopf dual if and only if there exists a biholomorphism
\operatorname{Tot}(L)-0_{L}
and
\operatorname{Tot}(L^{\prime})-0_{L^{\prime}}
sending a neighborhood of
0_{L}
to a neighborhood of infinity in
\operatorname{Tot}(L^{\prime})
.

2.

L
and
L^{\prime}
are analytically cobordant if and only if there exists a biholomorphism
\operatorname{Tot}(L)-0_{L}
and
\operatorname{Tot}(L^{\prime})-0_{L^{\prime}}
sending a neighborhood of
0_{L}
to a neighborhood of
0_{L^{\prime}}
.

Proof:
.

The second statement follows immediately from the first, so we will only prove the first one. We saw in (
2.4.3
) that Hopf dual line bundles satisfy the condition of the theorem. Conversely, suppose there is a biholomorphism
\operatorname{Tot}(L)-0_{L}
and
\operatorname{Tot}(L^{\prime})-0_{L^{\prime}}
as in the proposition. Let us glue
\operatorname{Tot}(L)
and
\operatorname{Tot}(L^{\prime})
by this isomorphism. The result is a compact Hausdorff surface compactifying
\operatorname{Tot}(L)
. By Enoki’s theorem, it is either a ruled or a Hopf surface, hence the claim.
∎

2.4.6.

By Proposition
2.4.5
, analytic cobordance is an equivalence relation on the set of pairs
(E,L)
where
E
is an elliptic curve and
L
is a line bundle on
E
. However, if
L
is analytically cobordant to
L^{\prime}
and
L^{\prime\prime}
through primary Hopf surfaces, then
L^{\prime}
and
L^{\prime\prime}
can be analytically cobordant through a secondary Hopf surface.

2.4.7.

The surface
M=\operatorname{Tot}(L)
carries a holomorphic symplectic form
\sigma
with a simple pole at the zero section. Indeed,

K_{M}=\pi^{*}K_{E}\otimes K_{M/E}=K_{M/E}=\pi^{*}L^{*}.

The second isomorphism holds because
K_{E}
is trivial. The pullback
\pi^{*}L
has the tautological section, which vanishes on
0_{L}
; thus
K_{M}=\pi^{*}L^{*}
has a nowhere zero section with a simple pole along
0_{L}
.

2.4.8.

Consider non-torsion analytically cobordant line bundles
L
and
L^{\prime}
. Let
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
be the holomorphic symplectic forms on
\operatorname{Tot}(L)-0_{L}
and
\operatorname{Tot}(L^{\prime})-0_{L^{\prime}}
The pullback of
\sigma^{\prime}
under the holomophic isomorphism
f\colon\operatorname{Tot}(L)-0_{L}\to\operatorname{Tot}(L^{\prime})-0_{L^{\prime}}
equals
c\sigma
for a non-zero constant
c
. Indeed,
f^{*}\sigma^{\prime}=g\sigma
for a holomorphic function
g
on
\operatorname{Tot}(L)-0_{L}
. By
[
KU19
, Lemma 2.2]
, all holomorphic functions on
\operatorname{Tot}(L)-0_{L}
are constant.

2.5
Hopf transforms

2.5.1.

A tubular neighborhood of a complex submanifold is in general not biholomorphic to a neighborhood of the zero section of its normal bundle. For example, a smooth cubic
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
can be deformed to a non-isomorphic curve, whereas all deformations of the zero section of
\operatorname{Tot}(\nu_{C/\mathbb{P}^{2}})
are isomorphic to
C
. Therefore, neighborhoods of
C
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and in
\operatorname{Tot}(\nu_{C/\mathbb{P}^{2}})
cannot be biholomorphic. However, analogues of the tubular neighbourhood theorem are known in certain situations.

Definition 2.5.2
.

Let
L\to E
be a degree-zero line bundle on an elliptic curve, and
d
an translation-invariant metric on the Picard variety
\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(E)
. Suppose that

-\log d(\mathcal{O}_{E},L^{\otimes n})=O(\log n).

Then
L
is called
Diophantine
.

The Diophantine property does not depend on the choice of the metric
d
. The set of Diophantine line bundles on
E
is the complement to a measure-zero set in
\mathrm{Pic^{0}}(E)
.

Theorem 2.5.3

(Arnold–Ueda theorem
[
Arn76
, 4.3]

[
Ued82
, Th. 3]
)
.

Let
S
be a complex surface and
E\subset S
an elliptic curve. If the normal bundle
\nu_{E/S}
is non-torsion and of degree zero, then the formal neighbourhoods of
E
in
S
and in
\operatorname{Tot}(\nu_{E/S})
are isomorphic. If
\nu_{E/S}
is Diophantine, then this isomorphism extends to a biholomorphism of an analytic neighborhood of
E
in
S
with a neighborhood of
E
in
\operatorname{Tot}(\nu_{E/S})
.

2.5.4.

The explicit description in
[
Arn76
, 4.3]
implies that if
L\to E
and
L^{\prime}\to E^{\prime}
are analytically cobordant through a primary Hopf surface and
L
is Diophantine, then so is
L^{\prime}
. It follows easily from this and
2.3.1
that the Diophantine property is preserved by any analytic cobordism.

2.5.5.

Arnold–Ueda theorem fails in the case of torsion line bundles. A counterexample is a fiber
F
of a non-isotrivial elliptic fibraton. It has a holomorphically trivial normal bundle, yet, its neighbors are not isomorphic to
F
.

Definition 2.5.6
.

Let
E\subset S
be an elliptic curve on a surface
S
and
L
its normal bundle. Assume that
\deg_{E}L=0
and
L
satisfies the Diophantine condition. Let
L^{\prime}\to E^{\prime}
be a bundle analytically cobordant to
L\to E
(Definition
2.4.2
). Pick a tubular neigbourhood of
E\subset S
as in Theorem
2.5.3
, throw away
E
, and glue the tubular neighbourhood of the zero section of
L^{\prime}\to F
through a holomorphic isomorphism between
\operatorname{Tot}(L)-0_{L}
and
\operatorname{Tot}(L^{\prime})-0_{L^{\prime}}
. The resulting surface
S^{\prime}
is called the
Hopf transform
of
S
in
E
by
L^{\prime}\to E^{\prime}
. The image of the curve
E^{\prime}
in the Hopf transform is called the
graft
.

2.5.7.

The new surface
S^{\prime}
may not be algebraic or even Kähler even if
S
is. For example, take
S=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{E}\oplus L)
and embed
E
as one of the sections. Then
S^{\prime}
is a Hopf surface.

2.5.8.

The Hopf transform of a given surface in a given curve can be made in a countable number of non-isomorphic ways due to Theorem
2.3.2
. Nevertheless, the result of a Hopf transform depends only on the choice of an analytically cobordant line bundle and not on an isomorphism between a neighbourhood of
E
in
S
and in
\operatorname{Tot}(L)
. The proof relies on the following Lemma.

Lemma 2.5.9
.

Let
L
be a non-torsion degree-zero line bundle on an elliptic curve
E
. For
r>0
, denote by
W_{r}
the subset of vectors in
\operatorname{Tot}(L)
of length less than
r
. Consider an open embedding
j\colon W_{r}\to W_{R}
identical on
E
, where
r,R\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\sqcup\infty
. Then
j
is the fiberwise multiplication by a constant
c\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
such that
|c|\leq R/r
.

Proof:
.

Step 1.
The space
W_{r}
(resp.​
W_{R}
) is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}\times B_{r}/\Lambda
(resp.
\mathbb{C}\times B_{R}/\Lambda
) where
\Lambda:=\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\cdot\tau
acts as follows:

\gamma\cdot(x,y)=(x+\gamma,\rho(\gamma)y).

Here
B_{r}
(resp.
B_{R}
) is the open disk of radius
r
(resp.
R
) and
\rho\colon\Lambda\to U(1)
is the monodromy representation. By the universal property of universal covers, we can lift
j
to an open embedding
J\colon\mathbb{C}\times B_{r}\to\mathbb{C}\times B_{R}
. The map
J
descends to quotients, hence for every
\gamma\in\Lambda
there exists
\gamma^{\prime}\in\Lambda
such that
J(\gamma\cdot(x,y))=\gamma^{\prime}\cdot J(x,y)
. When
y=0
the map
J
is the identity, hence
\gamma^{\prime}=\gamma
. We conclude that
J
is
\Lambda
-equivariant.

Step 2.
Write
J
as
(f,g)
, where
f\colon\mathbb{C}\times B_{r}\to\mathbb{C}
and
g\colon\mathbb{C}\times B_{r}\to B_{R}
. We will see in this step that
g(x,y)=cy
for a constant
c\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. For a fixed

, the map
g
is a holomorphic function from
\mathbb{C}
to
B_{R}
, hence constant. We conclude that

. The
\Lambda

implies that

. The image of

is dense because

is
U(1)
-equivariant, and our claim follows.

Step 3.
By
\Lambda

f(x+\gamma,\rho(\gamma)y)=f(x,y)+\gamma.

(4)

Let as differentiate the equation (
4
)
k

y
. We obtain

\rho^{k}(\gamma)\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial y^{k}}(x+\gamma,\rho(\gamma)y)=\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial y^{k}}(x,y)

Set
y=0
. The function
\left|\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial y^{k}}(x,0)\right|
is invariant under shifts by

, hence bounded. We conclude that
\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial y^{k}}(x,0)
is constant. The representation
\rho^{k}
is non-trivial for every

, hence

f(x,y)=f(x,0)=x
. We showed that

, hence the claim.
∎

3
Surfaces with square-zero elliptic curves

3.1
Kodaira dimension

3.1.1.

Let
S
be a complex surface (not necessarily projective) containing a square-zero elliptic curve
E
. Then its Kodaira dimension
\kappa(S)
is at most one. Indeed, suppose
\kappa(S)=2
. Then the map
\varphi\colon S\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
induced by the linear system
|nK_{S}|
is birational onto its image for sufficiently large
n
. Every square-zero elliptic curve
E
satisfies
K_{S}\cdot E=0
. Hence the map
\varphi
must contract
E
. Yet, a birational map of surfaces cannot contract a square-zero curve.

Proposition 3.1.2
.

Let
S
be a surface with square-zero elliptic curve
E\subset S
whose normal bundle
\nu_{E/S}
is non-torsion. Then
\kappa(S)=-\infty
.

Proof:
.

Observe that
\nu_{E/S}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{E}(-K_{S}|_{E})
. We consider two cases.

Case 1:
\kappa=0
.
Every minimal surface with
\kappa=0
has torsion canonical bundle, hence the statement is trivial in this case. If
S
is non-minimal, then
K_{S}=\sum E_{i}+D
, where
E_{i}
’s are exceptional curves and
D
is a torsion divisor. We get that
K_{S}|_{E}
is either torsion or has positive degree for every curve
E\subset S
.

Case 2:
\kappa=1
.
The linear system
|nK_{S}|
for
n\gg 0
induces a morphism
\varphi\colon S\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
whose image is a curve. It is an elliptic fibration. Elliptic curves in the fibers of
\varphi
have torsion normal bundle, so we can consider only horizontal curves. For every horizontal curve
C
, we have
nK_{S}|_{C}=(\varphi|_{C})^{*}H
, where
H
is the hyperplane section. Therefore,
K_{S}\cdot C
is positive.
∎

3.1.3.

A surface of negative Kodaira dimension is one of the following:

•

rational;

•

birational to a ruled surface;

•

of class VII, i.e., a non-Kähler surface with
\kappa=-\infty
and
b_{1}=1
.

If a blow-up of a ruled surface
S
contains an elliptic curve, then
S
is ruled over an elliptic curve.

Proposition 3.1.4
.

Let
E\subset S
be an anti-canonical elliptic curve with a degree-zero non-torsion normal bundle
\nu_{E/S}
. Assume
b_{1}(S)=0
. Then
S
is a blow-up of a length nine subscheme in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

Proof:
.

The canonical class of
S
is anti-effective, hence all plurigenera
p_{n}:=h^{0}(K_{S}^{n})
of
S
vanish. Since
b_{1}(S)=0
, the irregularity
q=h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{Y})
vanishes as well. Castelnuovo theorem implies that
S
is rational, in particular, projective
[
BHPVdV15
, VI (3.4)]
. Its minimal model is either
\mathbb{P}^{2}
or a Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{n}=\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-n)\right)
. The image of
E
in a minimal model is an irreducible anti-canonical curve: indeed,
E
is anti-canonical, so it intersects each
(-1)
-curve transversely at one point. Since
K_{S}^{2}=0
,
S
is either a blow-up of nine points in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
or of eight points in
\mathbb{F}_{n}
.

The Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{n}
contains a section
C_{n}
of square
-n
. Projection formula yields
-K_{\mathbb{F}_{n}}\cdot C=2-n
, thus for
n>2
an anti-canonical curve contains
C
and is reducible. Therefore, any minimal model of
S
is
\mathbb{P}^{2}
,
\mathbb{F}_{0}=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
, or
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. An anti-canonical curve in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
does not intersect
C_{2}
. A blow-up of a point away from
C_{2}
is isomorphic to a blow-up of a length-two subscheme in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. A blowup of any point on
\mathbb{F}_{0}=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
is isomorphic to a blowup of two points on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Thus
S
is a blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
∎

3.2
Rational surfaces

Let us start with the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2.1
.

Let
S
be a surface, and
C\subset S
a smooth curve. Then the natural map
H_{1}(S-C,\mathbb{Q})\to H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Q})
is surjective. If
S
is Kähler, then it is also injective.

Proof:
.

Look at the long exact sequence of cohomology associated with the decomposition
S=C\sqcup(S-C)
:

\dots\to H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{2}(C,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{3}_{c}(S-C,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{3}(S,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{3}(C,\mathbb{Q})=0.

If
S
is Kähler, the class of
C
in
H_{2}(S)
is non-trivial. Therefore the map
H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{2}(C,\mathbb{Q})
is surjective, and
H_{1}(S-C,\mathbb{Q})\simeq H^{3}_{c}(S-C,\mathbb{Q})\simeq H^{3}(S,\mathbb{Q})\simeq H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Q})
.
∎

The next statement follows easily from Lemma
3.2.1
, and we omit its proof.

Corollary 3.2.2
.

1.

Let
S
be a rational surface,
C\subset S
a smooth curve. Then
h_{1}(S-C)=0
.

2.

Let
S
be a blow-up of a ruled surface over an elliptic curve,
C\subset S
a smooth curve. Then
h_{1}(S-C)=2
.

Corollary 3.2.3
.

1.

A Hopf transform of a rational surface is rational.

2.

A Hopf transform of a surface birational to a ruled surface is birational either to a ruled surface or a Hopf surface.

Proof:
.

Let
S
be a rational surface. Lemma
3.2.1
implies that
h_{1}(S-C)=0
. Consider a Hopf transform
(S^{\prime},C^{\prime})
of
S
in
C
. The space
S^{\prime}-C^{\prime}
is biholomorphic to
S-C
, hence
h_{1}(S^{\prime}-C^{\prime})=h_{1}(S-C)
. Lemma
3.2.1
implies that
h_{1}(S^{\prime}-C^{\prime})\geqslant h_{1}(S^{\prime})
. Thus a Hopf transform of a rational surface satisfies
h_{1}(S^{\prime})=0
. The classification in
3.1.3
implies that
S^{\prime}
is rational.

A Hopf transform of a blow-up of a ruled surface cannot be rational by the previous statement. By
3.1.3
its Hopf transform is either a blow-up of a ruled surface or a Hopf surface.
∎

3.2.4.

Choose an elliptic curve
E
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and nine points
p_{1},...p_{9}\in E
(points are allowed to collide). Denote the hyperplane section of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
as
H
. Assume that the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{E}(3H-p_{1}-...-p_{9})
satisfies the Diophantine condition (Definition
2.5.2
). This line bundle is isomorphic to the normal bundle to the strict transform of
E
in the blow-up
X
in
p_{1},...p_{9}
. Therefore, a Hopf transform
Y
of
X
in
E
is well-defined.

Theorem 3.2.5
.

Any Hopf transform
Y
of
X
is the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
in a length nine subscheme
\gamma
. The graft is the strict preimage in
Y
of the elliptic curve
F\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
passing through
\gamma
.

Proof:
.

Step 1.
The surface
(X,E)
is log Calabi–Yau, that is,
K_{X}+E=0
. This implies existence of a meromorphic symplectic form on
X
with a simple pole along
E\subset X
. We know from
2.4.8
that a Hopf transform is a gluing along a holomorphic symplectomorphism, hence
Y
carries a symplectic form with a simple pole along
F\subset Y
. Thus
K_{Y}+F=0
.

Step 2.
By Corollary
3.2.3
,
Y
is rational. Moreover, its anti-canonical divisor is effective. By Proposition
3.1.4
,
Y
is a blowup of a length-nine subscheme in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, and
F\subset Y
is the strict transform of the only plane cubic passing through it.
∎

The following question remains open:

Problem 1
.

Determine the nine points
q_{1},...q_{9}\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
such that
Y\simeq\operatorname{Bl}_{q_{1},..q_{9}}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
.

Theorem 3.2.6
.

Let
X
be the blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
in a very generic length-nine subscheme
\gamma
, and
E\subset X
the strict transform of the plane cubic passing through
\gamma
. Then the analytification of
X-E
admits countably many algebraic structures.

Proof:
.

The claim follows from Corollary
2.5.14
because every Hopf transform of
X
is projective.

∎

3.2.7.

There are rational surfaces containing a not anti-canonical elliptic curve of square zero. Let
Q\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
be a plane quartic with two nodes. Blow them up; the strict transform
\tilde{Q}
has square
8
. By blowing up eight more points on
\tilde{Q}
, one gets a square-zero elliptic curve on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
blown up in ten points. Notice that ‘having a node at a given point’ is a codimension three condition, thus plane quartics with two nodes at
p,q\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
form an eight-dimensional space. Thus, a generic tuple of ten points with two distinguished ones determines a unique plane quartic with two nodes through it.

3.2.8.

By
[
KU19
, Lemma 2.2]
, punctured neighbourhood of a square-zero elliptic curve with non-torsion normal bundle carries no nonconstant functions. Hence none of the surfaces
X-E
from Theorem
3.2.6
is Stein.

3.3
Surfaces of class VII

All surfaces of class VII that contain an elliptic curve with a non-torsion degree-zero normal bundle are Hopf surfaces, as we prove below.

Proposition 3.3.1
.

Let
S
be a surface of class VII and
E\subset S
a smooth elliptic curve with non-torsion normal bundle
\nu_{E/S}
of degree zero. Then
S
is a primary Hopf surface
H(\lambda,\mu)
with
\lambda^{n}\neq\mu^{m}
for any
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
, or a secondary Hopf surface
H(\lambda,\mu)/\mu_{n}
from
2.3.1
, or a blow-up of such a surface away from
E
.

Proof:
.

Every curve on a smooth non-Kähler surface has a non-positive square
[
BHPVdV15
, Ch. IV, Thm. 2.14]
. Applying this fact to a minimal model of
S
, we obtain that a curve of square zero does not intersect exceptional curves.

If
E\subset S
is a nonsingular square-zero curve on a minimal surface
S
of class VII, then
S
is a Hopf surface
[
Eno81
, Proposition 4.12]
. Hopf surfaces that contain an elliptic curve with non-torsion normal bundle were classified in
2.2.6
and
2.3.1
.
∎

4
Analytic Grothendieck group

Definition 4.1.1
.

The
Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})
of complex algebraic varieties
is the abelian group generated by classes of
\mathbb{C}
-varieties modulo scissor relations:

[X-Y]=[X]-[Y],

where
Y\subset X
is a closed algebraic subvariety.
2
2

2

It is actually a ring with multiplication
[U]\cdot[V]=\left[{U}\times_{k}{V}\right]
, but we do not use it.

We introduce an interesting quotient of this group.

Definition 4.1.2
.

The
analytic Grothendieck group of varieties

K_{0}^{an}
is the quotient of
K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})
by differences
[U]-[V]
where
U
and
V
are biholomorphic varieties.

Proposition 4.1.3
.

The classes of any two elliptic curves in
K_{0}^{an}
are equal.

Proof:
.

Step 1.
Suppose that
E,F
are two elliptic curves and
L\to E
,
L^{\prime}\to F
are two analytically cobordant Diophantine line bundles. Then
[E]=[F]\in K_{0}^{an}
. Indeed, let
X
be a blow-up of nine points in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
such that the cubic passing through them is isomorphic to
E
, and the normal bundle of its strict transform is isomorphic to
L
. Even in
K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})
, one has
[X]=\mathbb{L}^{2}+10\mathbb{L}+[\mathrm{pt}]
, where
\mathbb{L}
is the class of the affine line. By Theorem
3.2.5
, the Hopf transform
Y
of
X
in
E
by
L^{\prime}\to F
is also a blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
in a length nine subscheme. Thus
[Y]=\mathbb{L}^{2}+10\mathbb{L}+1=[X]
. It follows from the relations
[X]=[E]+[X-E]
and
[Y]=[F]+[Y-F]
that

[E]^{an}=[X]^{an}-[X-E]^{an}=[Y]^{an}-[Y-F]^{an}=[F]^{an}\in K_{0}^{an}.

Step 2.
For any pair of elliptic curves
E
,
F
, there exists a primary Hopf surface
H=H_{E,F}
containing
E
and
F
. For a fixed
E
, the set of curves
F
for which the normal bundle
\nu_{E/H_{E,F}}
is not Diophantine is meagre in the moduli space of elliptic curves. Thus for arbitrary two curves
E
,
E^{\prime}
, the complements of the corresponding meagre sets have nonempty intersection. Let
F
be any curve from this intersection. Step 1 implies that
[E]^{an}=[F]^{an}
and
[F]^{an}=[E^{\prime}]^{an}
, hence the claim.
∎

4.1.4.

Although the classes of elliptic curves in
K_{0}^{an}

coincide, the classes of different elliptic curves in
K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})
are distinct, as
follows from Franziska Bittner’s description of
K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})
in
[
Bit04
]
. She proved
that
K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})
is generated by classes of smooth projective varieties modulo relations

[X]-[Y]=[Bl_{Y}X]-[E],

(5)

where
Y
is a smooth subvariety of a smooth projective variety
X

and
E\subset Bl_{Y}X

is the exceptional divisor of the blow-up of
X
in
Y
. This result is very powerful as it
enables us to construct motivic measures (i.e., maps from
K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})
to an abelian group)
by checking only the relation (
5
). For example,
consider the functional on smooth projective varieties sending a variety to its stable
birationality class. This functional descends to a motivic measure

K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})\to\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{SB}]

with values in the free abelian group generated by stable birationality classes. Indeed,

Bl_{Y}X
is birational to
X
and
E
is stably birational to
Y
, so the relation (
5
)
is preserved. We see that non-stably birational varieties have different classes in
K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})
.
Two curves are stably birational if and only if they are isomorphic
[
Har13
, V Ex.​ 2.1]
. In particular,
non-isomorphic elliptic curves have different classes in
K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})
.

4.1.5.

Topological Euler characteristic is a homomorphism
K_{0}^{an}\to\mathbb{Z}
. However, we were not able to find more interesting analytic motivic measures. This motivates the following

Problem 2
.

Is the class of an elliptic curve in
K_{0}^{an}
trivial? If so, is
K_{0}^{an}
isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
?

Adaptive inference over Besov spaces in the white noise model using p p -exponential priors
Sergios Agapiouabel=e1, mark]agapiou.sergios@ucy.ac.cy, Aimilia Savvaabel=e2, mark]savva.emilia@ucy.ac.cy

Abstract
In many scientific applications the aim is to infer a function which is smooth in some areas, but rough or even discontinuous in other areas of its domain. Such spatially inhomogeneous functions can be modelled in Besov spaces with suitable integrability parameters. In this work we study adaptive Bayesian inference over Besov spaces, in the white noise model from the point of view of rates of contraction, using p p -exponential priors, which range between Laplace and Gaussian and possess regularity and scaling hyper-parameters. To achieve adaptation, we employ empirical and hierarchical Bayes approaches for tuning these hyper-parameters. Our results show that, while it is known that Gaussian priors can attain the minimax rate only in Besov spaces of spatially homogeneous functions, Laplace priors lead to adaptive or nearly adaptive procedures in both Besov spaces of spatially homogeneous functions and Besov spaces permitting spatial inhomogeneities.

Introduction

A common goal in statistical practice is the inference of a functional unknown. This unknown can have uniform smoothness across its domain or it can be
spatially inhomogeneous
, that is, smooth in some areas and rough in other areas of its domain. For example, in imaging applications typically there are different smooth objects separated by sharp edges, in geophysical applications the physical parameter of interest has jumps at the intersection of different media and in signal processing some signals are mostly idle and only exhibit activity in localized outbursts. In this work, we will study
adaptive
Bayesian inference for both of these types of unknowns, in the
white noise model
and under a class of priors termed
p
-exponential
, from the point of view of rates of posterior contraction in the small noise limit.

It is well known that both spatially homogeneous and spatially inhomogeneous functions can be modelled using Besov spaces,
B^{\beta}_{qq^{\prime}}
with smoothness
\beta>0
and integrability indices
q\geq 2,1\leq q^{\prime}\leq\infty
for the former and
1\leq q<2,1\leq q^{\prime}\leq\infty
for the latter (for a nice heuristic explanation why
q<2
relates to spatial inhomogeneity we refer to
[
31
, Section 9.6]
). Function estimation for such unknowns in the white noise model has been studied extensively in the frequentist statistical literature, and minimax rates under Besov-type regularity in
L_{2}
-loss have been established by Donoho and Johnstone in
[
22
]
. A main finding in
[
22
]
is that, unlike the homogeneous case
q\geq 2
in which linear estimators can achieve the minimax rate (Sobolev-type spaces correspond to
q=2
), in the inhomogeneous case linear estimators only achieve a rate which is polynomially slower than minimax. On the other hand, there are a few (nonlinear) frequentist procedures which are (nearly) adaptive in the minimax sense in both cases, for example based on wavelet thresholding
[
20
,
21
]
or locally variable bandwidth kernel estimates
[
37
]
. We also mention the Bayesian wavelet thresholding method in
[
1
]
.

We will restrict our attention to Besov spaces with equal integrability indices
q=q^{\prime}
,
q\geq 1
, which will enable us to simplify our analysis while still exhibiting the main statistical phenomena we are interested in. Rates of posterior contraction in
L_{2}
-loss under such Besov regularity have been studied in the (direct) white noise model in
[
4
]
, for
p
-exponential priors, which is a family of sequence priors with tails between Gaussian (
p=2
) and exponential (
p=1
). The obtained upper bounds suggested that in the spatially inhomogeneous case (
1\leq q<2
), Gaussian priors are limited by the "linear-minimax" rate; this agrees with the intuition that Gaussian process regression is a linear statistical procedure due to the linearity of the posterior mean in this setting. Indeed, in the recent preprint
[
6
]
this was made rigorous by establishing the corresponding lower bounds for rates of contraction holding uniformly over Besov bodies and for sequences of
arbitrary
Gaussian priors. On the contrary, Laplace priors with appropriately chosen regularity
and
scaling parameters were shown in
[
4
]
to attain the minimax or nearly the minimax rate (for
q=1
and
1<q<2
, respectively). More generally, it was shown that when
p\leq q<2
, appropriately tuned
p
-exponential priors attain the minimax rate (up to logarithmic terms when
p<q
). In the less intricate spatially homogeneous case (
q\geq 2
), it was shown that the minimax rate can be achieved using appropriately tuned
p
-exponential priors with any
p\in[1,2]
.

The purpose of this work is to theoretically establish that the required tuning of
p
-exponential priors can be performed automatically. Empirical Bayes procedures based on the maximum marginal likelihood estimator (MMLE) as well as hierarchical Bayes procedures, for choosing either the scaling or the regularity hyper-parameter of a class of Gaussian process priors in the white noise model, have been studied from the point of view of contraction rates in
[
43
]
and
[
32
]
, respectively. In those sources, it was shown that both the empirical and hierarchical procedures lead to partial adaptation over hyper-rectangles when selecting the scaling hyper-parameter (extended to Sobolev spaces in
[
41
]
), while both procedures are fully adaptive over Sobolev spaces when selecting the regularity hyper-parameter (up to logarithmic terms, improved in
[
41
]
; see also
[
10
,
42
]
). Both contributions
[
43
]
and
[
32
]
, relied on the fact that for Gaussian priors the marginal likelihood can be expressed explicitly.
On the other hand, a general theory for studying adaptivity with MMLE empirical Bayes and hierarchical Bayes procedures, which does not require the explicit availability of the marginal likelihood, has been developed in
[
41
]
; see also
[
19
]
concerning general empirical Bayes procedures. We employ this theory to study adaptation with
p
-exponential priors in the white noise model, since for
p<2
the marginal likelihood is not explicit.

We initially study adaptation over Sobolev spaces, by choosing either the scaling or the regularity hyper-parameter of the
p
-exponential prior via the MMLE empirical and hierarchical Bayes procedures. Our results are analogous to the Gaussian case: first, we show that when choosing the scaling hyper-parameter, the minimax rate is achieved provided the truth is not too smooth compared to the prior; second, we show that when choosing the regularity hyper-parameter, the minimax rate is effectively always achieved. These results trivially generalize to Besov spaces of spatially homogeneous functions (
q\geq 2
) as well.

We then study adaptation over Besov spaces permitting spatial inhomogeneities,
B^{\beta}_{qq}
with
1\leq q<2
, by choosing
both
the scaling and regularity parameters
simultaneously
. We establish that for
p
-exponential priors with
p\leq q
, both the MMLE empirical and hierarchical Bayes posteriors are minimax adaptive (up to logs when
p<q
), for
\beta\in(\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}+1/p,\bar{\alpha}+1/p)
where
\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}>0
can be chosen arbitrarily small and large, respectively. In particular,
the main contribution of this work is the establishment that Laplace priors lead to minimax or near minimax adaptation over Besov spaces of spatially homogeneous functions as well as Besov spaces permitting spatially inhomogeneous functions.

Although our results refer to Besov spaces with equal integrability indices
q=q^{\prime}
, the setting is sufficiently rich to exhibit the advantage of using Laplace priors over Gaussian priors from the point of view of contraction rates. This is not surprising, since the minimax and linear minimax rates established in
[
22
]
are independent of
q^{\prime}
. The extension of our results to the general
B^{\beta}_{qq^{\prime}}
case, while outside the scope of this work, is expected to be straightforward, despite requiring more technical calculations and a heavier notation.

We mention here that adaptive Bayesian procedures for spatially inhomogeneous functions are also studied in the recent preprint
[
40
]
, where adaptive contraction rates are obtained for the spike-and-slab prior and Bayesian CART under spatially varying Hölder smoothness assumptions. We also mention the upcoming work
[
28
]
, which studies adaptation over Besov spaces in density estimation based on (hierarchical) Besov priors. To the best of our knowledge, these two works and the present article are the first Bayesian adaptive results for spatially inhomogeneous functions in the literature.

In the rest of this introductory discussion we briefly introduce the setting, and overview the general theory for studying adaptivity with MMLE empirical and hierarchical Bayes procedures from
[
41
]
, as well as the concentration theory for
p
-exponential priors from
[
4
]
. In Section
2
we state our main results, while their proofs are contained in Section
3
. The proofs rely on a number of technical results contained in the Supplement. Finally, Section
4
contains a brief concluding discussion on computation, including two simulation experiments relating to the presented hierarchical Bayes procedures for Sobolev truths.

1.1
The white noise model

We consider the white noise model, which serves as a continuous limit of Gaussian nonparametric regression,
[
13
]
. This idealized model is typically used as a tractable stepping stone, before studying more complex nonparametric models.

We are interested in inferring a square integrable function
\theta
on the unit interval, from an observation of a path of the stochastic process

X^{(n)}_{t}=\int^{t}_{0}\theta(s)ds+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}W_{t},\quad t\in[0,1].

(1.1)

Here
W
is a standard Brownian motion,
n^{-1}
is the noise level and we will study the small noise asymptotic regime,
n\to\infty
. The restriction to one dimension is for reasons of notational convenience; we expect the presented theory to generalize to higher dimensional domains in a straightforward way. Furthermore, for simplicity of our exposition, we assume that in addition to square summable, the unknown function
\theta
is periodic; we write
\theta\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T})
, where
\mathbb{T}=(0,1]
, and equip this space with the usual
L_{2}
-inner product
\big\langle\cdot,\cdot\big\rangle_{2}
and norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2}
. We let
P^{n}_{\theta}
denote the law of the sample path
X^{(n)}:=(X^{(n)}_{t}:0\leq t\leq 1)
in the sample space
\mathcal{X}:=C[0,1]
, where the latter denotes the space of continuous real functions on the unit interval. In particular,
P^{n}_{0}
denotes the law of the scaled Brownian motion
W_{t}/\sqrt{n}
. Then by the Cameron-Martin theorem, the likelihood function is given as

p^{n}_{\theta}(X):=\frac{dP^{n}_{\theta}}{dP^{n}_{0}}(X)=\exp\Big(n\big\langle X,\theta\big\rangle_{2}-\frac{n}{2}\left\|\theta\right\|^{2}_{2}\Big),\quad X\in\mathcal{X}.

(1.2)

We refer to Section 6.1.1 in
[
27
]
for more details on the white noise model.

1.2
The MMLE and adaptive contraction rates

Given a class of prior distributions
\Pi(\cdot|\lambda)
on the parameter space
\Theta:=L_{2}(\mathbb{T})
indexed by a hyper-parameter
\lambda\in\Lambda
, the associated posterior distributions are given as

\Pi(B|X^{(n)},\lambda)=\frac{\int_{B}p^{n}_{\theta}(X^{(n)})d\Pi(\theta|\lambda)}{\bar{m}(X^{(n)}|\lambda)},

for any Borel set
B\subseteq\Theta
. Here

\bar{m}(X^{(n)}|\lambda):=\int_{\Theta}p^{n}_{\theta}(X^{(n)})d\Pi(\theta|\lambda),

denotes the
marginal likelihood
of the path
X^{(n)}
in (
1.1
), given
\lambda
. Fixing a set of candidate hyper-parameter values
\Lambda_{n}\subseteq\Lambda
, the MMLE is defined as

\hat{\lambda}_{n}\in\argmax_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}}\,\,\bar{m}(X^{(n)}|\lambda),

and leads to the associated empirical Bayes posterior distribution
\Pi(\cdot|X^{(n)},\hat{\lambda}_{n})
.

The asymptotic behaviour of the MMLE
\hat{\lambda}_{n}
was studied in general settings in
[
41
]
and sufficient conditions were given securing that, as
n\to\infty
,
\hat{\lambda}_{n}
belongs to a set
\Lambda_{0}
of ’good’ values of the hyper-parameter with probability tending to 1. More specifically for
\Theta=L_{2}(\mathbb{T})
as assumed here, let
\theta_{0}\in\Theta
denote the ground truth and let
\epsilon_{n}(\lambda)=\epsilon_{n}(\lambda;K,\theta_{0})
be such that

\Pi(\theta\in\Theta:\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}\leq K\epsilon_{n}(\lambda)|\lambda)=e^{-n\epsilon_{n}^{2}(\lambda)},

(1.3)

where
K>0
is a constant introduced for additional flexibility. Note that, for ’continuous’ priors as the ones considered in this work,
\epsilon_{n}(\lambda)
is defined uniquely by (
1.3
) (see Lemma
B.1
in the Supplement below). Let
\epsilon_{n,0}>0
be defined through

\epsilon_{n,0}^{2}=\inf_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}}\Big\{\epsilon_{n}^{2}(\lambda):\epsilon_{n}^{2}(\lambda)\geq m_{n}(\log n)/n\Big\},

(1.4)

with
m_{n}
tending to infinity arbitrarily slowly. Then the set
\Lambda_{0}
is given as

\Lambda_{0}(M_{n}):=\{\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}:\epsilon_{n}(\lambda)\leq M_{n}\epsilon_{n,0}\},

(1.5)

with
M_{n}
tending to infinity arbitrarily slowly.

Having identified
\Lambda_{0}
, additional sufficient conditions were derived securing that the contraction rate of the MMLE empirical Bayes posterior is bounded by
M_{n}\epsilon_{n,0}
. This was based on the techniques in
[
19
]
, where rates of contraction for empirical Bayes posteriors were studied with general estimators of the hyper-parameter
\lambda
and where the existence of a ’good’ set
\Lambda_{0}
in which these estimators concentrate asymptotically was postulated. Further sufficient conditions were derived for establishing the sharpness of the rates for each
\theta_{0}
, as well as conditions for showing that the contraction rates for the MMLE empirical and hierarchical posteriors share the same bounds. We recall these conditions adapted to the assumed white noise model setting in Subsection
3.1
below.

1.3
p
-exponential priors and their concentration

The class of
p
-exponential priors
has been introduced in
[
4
]
as a generalization of Gaussian and Besov priors.
B^{s}_{11}
-Besov priors, which are sequence priors corresponding to wavelet expansions with independent and appropriately weighted Laplace-distributed coefficients, have been proposed in the applied Bayesian inverse problems literature in order to achieve ’edge-preserving’ reconstruction; see
[
35
]
as well as
[
36
,
17
,
33
,
18
]
. The weights of the wavelet coefficients are chosen in order to penalize the
B^{s}_{11}
-norm in the prior probability and, indeed, in
[
3
]
it was established in a general nonparametric inverse regression setting that the
maximum a posteriori estimator
(MAP) arising from these priors is the minimizer of a penalized least squares functional with a
B^{s}_{11}
-penalty.

In this work we will study
\alpha
-regular and
\tau
-scaled
p
-exponential priors
, defined as

\Pi(\cdot|\lambda):=\mathcal{L}\big((\gamma_{\ell}\xi_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}\big),\;\lambda=(\and),

where
\xi_{\ell}
are independent and identically distributed real random variables with probability density function
f_{p}(x)\propto\exp(-|x|^{p}/p),\,x\in\mathbb{R},\,p\in[1,2]
and
\gamma_{\ell}
are deterministic scalings of the form
\gamma_{\ell}=\tau\ell^{-1/2-\alpha}
for some
\alpha,\tau>0
. Note that for
p=1
, these priors are merely a different parametrization of
B^{s}_{11}
-Besov priors, while for
p=2
they correspond to the Gaussian priors studied in
[
43
,
32
]
. The concentration properties of
p
-exponential priors in general and
\alpha
-regular
\tau
-scaled
p
-exponential priors in particular, have been studied in
[
4
]
. Here we recall two key results for our analysis.

First, note that the term "
\alpha
-regular" is used since, for any
q\geq 1
, draws from
\Pi(\cdot|\lambda)
live in
B^{t}_{qq}
with probability one for
t<\alpha
and with probability zero for
t\geq\alpha
, see
[
4
, Lemma 5.2]
. Associated to an
\alpha
-regular
\tau
-scaled
p
-exponential prior
\Pi(\cdot|\lambda)
are two sequence spaces. The first is the space of translations of
\Pi
which produce equivalent measures, which is a separable Hilbert space:

\mathcal{Q_{\alpha,\tau}}=\Big\{h\in\mathbb{R}^{\infty}:\tau^{-2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}h_{\ell}^{2}\ell^{1+2\alpha}<\infty\Big\},\quad\left\|h\right\|_{\mathcal{Q_{\alpha,\tau}}}=\tau^{-1}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}h_{\ell}^{2}\ell^{1+2\alpha}\right)^{1/2}.

The second is the following Banach space

\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,\tau}=\Big\{h\in\mathbb{R}^{\infty}:\tau^{-p}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}|h_{\ell}|^{p}{\ell}^{p/2+\alpha p}<\infty\Big\},\quad\left\|h\right\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,\tau}}=\tau^{-1}\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}|h_{\ell}|^{p}{\ell}^{p/2+\alpha p}\right)^{1/p}.

Note that
\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,\tau}\subsetneq\mathcal{Q_{\alpha,\tau}}\subsetneq\ell_{2}
if
p\in[1,2)
, while
\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,\tau}=\mathcal{Q_{\alpha,\tau}}
if
p=2
, in which case both spaces are identified with the reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) of the corresponding Gaussian prior.

For any
\alpha
-regular
\tau
-scaled
p
-exponential prior, it is straightforward to show that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a unique
\epsilon_{n}(\lambda)
such that (
1.3
) holds, see Lemma
B.1
in the Supplement below. Given
\and>0
, we define the
concentration function
at
\theta\in\ell_{2}

\displaystyle\varphi_{\theta}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle:=\inf_{h\in\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,\tau}:\left\|h-\theta\right\|_{2}\leq\epsilon}\left\|h\right\|^{p}_{\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,\tau}}-\log\Pi\left(\epsilon B_{\ell_{2}}\mid\alpha,\tau\right),

(1.6)

where
B_{\ell_{2}}
is the centered unit ball of
\ell_{2}
. For
\theta=0
, the concentration function measures the probability of centered
\epsilon
-balls, while for
\theta\neq 0
it gives a lower bound on the probability of
\epsilon
-balls around
\theta

\Pi(\theta+\epsilon B_{\ell_{2}}|\and)\geq\exp\big(-\varphi_{\theta}(\epsilon/2)\big),

(1.7)

see
[
4
, Theorem 2.13]
. This enables us to obtain upper bounds on the value of
\epsilon_{n}(\lambda)
solving (
1.3
). However, except in the Gaussian case
p=2

[
46
, Lemma 5.3]
, the concentration function is not known to give an upper bound on the probability of
\epsilon
-balls around
\theta
. Since
p
-exponential priors are log-concave, one can at least use Anderson’s inequality which upper bounds the probability of any non-centered ball by the probability of the corresponding centered one, leading to a lower bound on
\epsilon_{n}(\lambda)
, see Lemma
3.2
. It will transpire that this naive bound is sufficient for our analysis.

Furthermore,
\alpha
-regular
\tau
-scaled
p
-exponential priors satisfy the following concentration inequality derived from the work of Talagrand in
[
44
]
,

\Pi(\epsilon B_{\ell_{2}}+R^{p/2}B_{\mathcal{Q_{\alpha,\tau}}}+RB_{\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,\tau}}|\and)\geq 1-\frac{e^{-R^{p}/\tilde{K}}}{\Pi(\epsilon B_{\ell_{2}}|\and)},\quad\epsilon>0,R>0,

(1.8)

where
B_{\mathcal{Y}}
denotes the centered unit ball in the space
(\mathcal{Y},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{Y}})
and
\tilde{K}
is a positive constant depending only on
p
, see
[
4
, Proposition 2.15]
. Inequality (
1.8
) shows that, even though it is straightforward to check that both spaces
\mathcal{Q_{\alpha,\tau}}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,\tau}
have probability zero under the prior, the prior mass concentrates in small
\epsilon
-enlargements in
\ell_{2}
around sums of large balls of these two spaces.

Together the lower bound (
1.7
) and the concentration inequality (
1.8
), have been employed to study contraction rates by verifying the ’prior-mass around the truth’ and ’sieve-set’ conditions in non-adaptive general posterior contraction results, such as the ones in the seminal papers
[
24
,
25
]
and the textbooks
[
26
,
27
]
. This was done in
[
4
]
in direct settings, while
[
6
]
and
[
30
]
supplemented these results with other novel concentration inequalities in order to study non-linear PDE inverse problems and drift estimation for multi-dimensional diffusions, respectively. We will also build on (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) in order to verify the more elaborate assumptions of
[
41
]
and study adaptivity.

1.4
Periodic Besov spaces

We consider an orthonormal wavelet basis
\mathcal{E}=\{e_{kl}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N},1\leq l\leq 2^{k}}
in
L_{2}(\mathbb{T})
. We can then uniquely express a function
\theta\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T})
as

\theta=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{2^{k}}\theta_{kl}e_{kl},

where
\theta_{kl}=\big\langle\theta,e_{kl}\big\rangle_{2}
. This allows us to identify a function
\theta\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T})
with the corresponding sequence of coefficients
\{\theta_{kl}\}\in\ell_{2}
, and in particular to define
p
-exponential priors on sequences, even though the goal is to reconstruct functions. We additionally assume that the basis functions
e_{kl}
have sufficient Hölder regularity, which allows us to characterize Besov function spaces and in particular the corresponding Besov norms, via the coefficients
\theta_{kl}

\left\|\theta\right\|_{B^{s}_{qq^{\prime}}}=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{q^{\prime}k(s+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{2^{k}}|\theta_{kl}|^{q}\Big)^{q^{\prime}/q}\right)^{1/q^{\prime}},\quad s\in\mathbb{R},\;1\leq q,q^{\prime}<\infty.

Note that by working on the torus
\mathbb{T}
, we benefit from a particularly clean sequence representation of the Besov norm, however analogous representations exist which allow treating more general domains with additional technical effort (see for example
[
6
]
in the non-adaptive inverse problems setting). For details on periodic Besov spaces see
[
27
, Section 4.3.4]
.

Since we restrict to the case
q=q^{\prime}
, enumerating
\{\theta_{kl}\},~{k\in\mathbb{N},1\leq l\leq 2^{k}}
, using a single index as
\{\theta_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
, and using the asymptotic equivalence between the sequences
2^{sk},\,k\in\mathbb{N},\,1\leq l\leq 2^{k}
and
\ell^{s},\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the above characterization of the Besov norms can be simplified to

\left\|\theta\right\|_{B^{s}_{qq}}=\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell^{qs+\frac{q}{2}-1}|\theta_{\ell}|^{q}\right)^{1/q},\quad s\in\mathbb{R},\;1\leq q<\infty.

This justifies our definition of the
p
-exponential priors in Subsection
1.3
via a single index. Note that for
q=2
we obtain the usual Sobolev-Hilbert spaces which we denote as
H^{s}
. The latter can also be defined using the Fourier basis.

1.5
Additional notation

We denote by
E^{n}_{\theta}
the expectation with respect to
P^{n}_{\theta}
, while we denote the log-likelihood of a
\theta
as
\ell_{n}(\theta):=\log p_{n}^{\theta}(X)
. The notation
N(\epsilon,A,d)
is used for the
\epsilon
-covering number of a set
A
with respect to a metric
d
, that is the minimum number of balls of radius
\epsilon
with respect to
d
which are needed to cover the set
A
. For two positive sequences
(a_{n}),(b_{n})
, the notation
a_{n}\asymp b_{n}
means that
a_{n}/b_{n}
is bounded away from zero and infinity, while
a_{n}\lesssim b_{n}
means that
a_{n}/b_{n}
is bounded. For
L>0
and specifically for Sobolev and Besov spaces, we will use the notation
H^{\beta}(L)
and
B^{s}_{qq}(L)
, respectively, to denote the corresponding ball of radius
L
.

2
Setup and main results

In this section we formulate our main results, which concern adaptive rates of contraction for empirical and hierarchical Bayes procedures based on
\alpha
-regular and
\tau
-scaled
p
-exponential priors in the white noise model.

In the case of a truth with Sobolev regularity
\beta
, the non-adaptive upper bounds in
[
4
, Theorem 5.5]
show that the minimax rate can be achieved without rescaling if we choose
\alpha=\beta
, suggesting that a possible strategy for adaptation is to fix
\tau=1
and choose the regularity,
\lambda=\alpha
. We will see that (similarly to Gaussian priors) another strategy is to fix the regularity and choose the scaling,
\lambda=\tau
, provided
\alpha
is not too small. We study the Sobolev setting in Subsection
2.3
.

For a truth in a Besov space
B^{\beta}_{qq}
with
1\leq q<2
, the non-adaptive upper bounds in
[
4
, Proposition 5.8]
only match or nearly match the minimax rate if
p\leq q
, for
\alpha=\beta-1/p

and
for an appropriately vanishing sequence of scalings
\tau
as
n\to\infty
. As a result, in Subsection
2.4
we study adaptation in the spatially inhomogeneous Besov setting only for
p\leq q
and by
simultaneously
choosing
both
the regularity
and
the scaling,
\lambda=(\and)
. Note that, since it is not known whether the bounds in
[
4
, Proposition 5.8]
are sharp, it is also not known whether
p\leq q
and varying
\alpha
and
\tau
simultaneously are necessary.

2.1
Minimax and linear minimax rates under Besov regularity

We first recall the minimax rates in
L_{2}
-loss for the white noise model and under Besov regularity, established in
[
22
]
. For a Besov class
B^{\beta}_{qq},q\geq 1
, with
\beta>1/q
or
\beta\geq 1
when
q=1
, the minimax rate depends only on the smoothness
\beta

{m}^{\ast}_{n}:=n^{-\frac{\beta}{1+2\beta}}.

(2.1)

When restricting to linear estimators, the minimax rate depends on
q
as well,

l^{\ast}_{n}:=n^{-\frac{\beta-\gamma/2}{1+2\beta-\gamma}},\quad\text{where}\quad\gamma=\frac{2}{q}-\frac{2}{\max(q,2)}.

(2.2)

In particular, for
q<2
the linear minimax rate is polynomially slower than the global minimax rate, while for
q\geq 2
the linear and global minimax rates coincide.

2.2
Prior specification

We consider
\alpha
-regular and
\tau
-scaled
p
-exponential priors,
\Pi(\cdot|\lambda)
, as defined in Subsection
1.3
. Depending on the setting, we will consider either
\lambda=\tau
,
\lambda=\alpha
or
\lambda=(\and)
as the hyper-parameter to be chosen, which accordingly lives in either
\Lambda=(0,\infty)
or
\Lambda=(0,\infty)^{2}
. We will study two approaches for automatically tuning
\lambda
:

i)

Empirical Bayes: choose
\lambda
as the maximum marginal likelihood estimator
\hat{\lambda}_{n}
defined in Subsection
1.2
for an appropriate set of candidate hyper-parameters values
\Lambda_{n}
, leading to the empirical Bayes posterior
\Pi(\cdot|X^{(n)},\hat{\lambda}_{n});

ii)

Hierarchical Bayes: postulate a hyper-prior
\tilde{\pi}
on
\lambda\in\Lambda
, giving rise to the hierarchical prior

\Pi(\cdot)=\int_{\Lambda}\tilde{\pi}(\lambda)\Pi(\cdot|\lambda)d\lambda.

In the hierarchical Bayes setting, we make the following assumptions for the hyper-priors on
\tau
and/or
\alpha
, respectively:

Assumption 2.1

(Hyper-prior distributions)
.

i)

Given
\alpha>0
,
\tilde{\pi}(\tau)
is supported on
\big[n^{-1/(2+p+2\alpha p)},\infty\big)
and for some
{r_{0}}>0
, there exist
{r_{1}},{r_{3}}>0
and
{r_{2}}>1+1/{r_{0}}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}e^{-{r_{1}}\tau^{\frac{2}{1+2\alpha}}}\lesssim\tilde{\pi}(\tau)\lesssim\tau^{-{r_{2}}},\quad\tau\geq 1,\\
\tilde{\pi}(\tau)\gtrsim e^{-{r_{3}}\tau^{-p}},\quad n^{-\frac{1}{2+p+2\alpha p}}\leq\tau\leq 1.\end{split}

(2.3)

ii)

\tilde{\pi}(\alpha)
is supported on
[\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}]
for some fixed
\bar{\alpha}>\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}>0
, and there exists
\kappa>0
such that

{\tilde{\pi}(\alpha)\geq\kappa,\quad\text{for all}\quad\alpha\in[\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}].}

(2.4)

iii)

\tilde{\pi}(\and)=\tilde{\pi}_{\alpha}(\alpha)\tilde{\pi}_{\tau}(\tau|\alpha)
, where
\tilde{\pi}_{\alpha}(\alpha)
satisfies part (ii), while
\tilde{\pi}_{\tau}(\tau|\alpha)
satisfies part (i) with constants
r_{0},\dots,r_{3}
and constants in the upper and lower bounds in (
2.3
) which are uniform in
\alpha
(note that the lower bound for
\tau\geq 1
is redundant here).

For example, (
2.3
) is satisfied by appropriately left-truncated inverse gamma distributions with constants
r_{0},\dots,r_{3}
independent of
\alpha
, (
2.4
) by truncated exponential distributions, while combinations of such distributions lead to hyper-priors
\tilde{\pi}(\and)
satisfying Assumption
2.1
(iii).

2.3
Adaptive rates of contraction over Sobolev spaces

We first study adaptation over Sobolev smoothness, using
\alpha
-regular
\tau
-scaled
p
-exponential priors, with a fixed regularity
\alpha
and by choosing the scaling parameter
\tau
.

Theorem 2.2

(
\alpha
-fixed,
\lambda=\tau
)
.

Consider
\alpha
-regular
\tau
-scaled
p
-exponential priors
\Pi(\cdot\mid\and)
in
\Theta
, where
\alpha>0
is fixed and
\lambda=\tau>0
.
Assume that
\theta_{0}\in H^{\beta}{(L)}
for some
\beta\geq(1+\alpha p)/(p+2\alpha p)
and
L>0
.

i)

Let
\Lambda_{n}\coloneqq\big[n^{-1/(2+p+2\alpha p)},n^{\alpha}\big]
be the set of candidate hyper-parameter values for the MMLE
\hat{\lambda}_{n}
. Then, for
M_{n}
tending to infinity arbitrarily slowly, it holds

P^{n}_{\theta_{0}}\left(\hat{\lambda}_{n}\in\Lambda_{0}(M_{n})\right)\to 1

and the empirical Bayes posterior satisfies

\sup_{\theta_{0}\in H^{\beta}(L)}E_{\theta_{0}}^{n}\Pi(\theta:\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}\geq M_{n}\epsilon_{n,\alpha,\beta,p}|X^{(n)},\hat{\lambda}_{n})=o(1),

(2.5)

where

\epsilon_{n,\alpha,\beta,p}\asymp\begin{cases}n^{-\frac{\beta}{1+2\beta}},&\text{for}\quad\text{$\beta<\alpha+1/p$,}\\
n^{-\frac{1+\alpha p}{2+p(1+2\alpha)}},&\text{for}\quad\text{$\beta>\alpha+1/p$,}\\
n^{-\frac{\beta}{1+2\beta}}\big(\log{n}\big)^{\frac{2-p}{2p(1+2\beta)}},&\text{for}\quad\text{$\beta=\alpha+1/p$.}\end{cases}

(2.6)

ii)

Similarly, for a hyper-prior on
\lambda=\tau
which satisfies Assumption
2.1
(i) and for
M_{n}
tending to infinity arbitrarily slowly, the hierarchical posterior satisfies

\sup_{\theta_{0}\in H^{\beta}(L)}E_{\theta_{0}}^{n}\Pi(\theta:\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}\geq M_{n}\epsilon_{n,\alpha,\beta,p}|X^{(n)})=o(1).

(2.7)

Note that, since
p\leq 2
, the assumption
\beta\geq(1+\alpha p)/(p+2\alpha p)
is satisfied for example when
\beta\geq 1/p
. Assuming
\beta\geq(1+\alpha p)/(p+2\alpha p)
, the minimax rate
{m}^{\ast}_{n}
is attained by both the empirical and hierarchical Bayes approaches of choosing the scaling
\tau
, provided that
\beta<\alpha+1/p
, that is provided the truth is not too smooth compared to the prior on
\theta
. Interestingly, the smaller
p
is, the more undersmoothing the prior can be while still achieving the minimax rate by rescaling. This is perhaps counterintuitive, since on the one hand Gaussian priors were expected to be the most compatible with the
\ell_{2}
structure of Sobolev spaces among all
p
-exponential priors, and on the other hand the smaller
p
is the heavier-tailed the prior which should be less suitable for smoother functions. However, it turns out that the smaller
p
is, the larger the capacity of a
p
-exponential prior to exploit vanishing scalings
\tau
to achieve the minimax rate for functions with smoothness higher than the prior regularity. This can be understood by examining the concentration function (
1.6
): the penalty payed in the infimum term for letting
\tau\to 0
is smaller the smaller
p
is, while the gain in the centered small ball probability term is identical for all
p
(see (
3.14
) in Lemma
3.2
below). Note that for
p=2
our results are compatible with the results of
[
43
,
41
]
(which are in slightly different settings: the former over hyper-rectangles, the latter using truncated rescaled
\alpha
-regular Gaussian priors).

We next study adaptation over Sobolev smoothness, by
\alpha
-regular
\tau
-scaled
p
-exponential priors, with fixed scaling
\tau=1
and choosing the regularity parameter
\alpha
.

Theorem 2.3

(
\tau
-fixed,
\lambda=\alpha
)
.

Consider
\alpha
-regular
\tau
-scaled
p
-exponential priors
\Pi(\cdot\mid\and)
in
\Theta
, where
\tau=1
,
\lambda=\alpha
and
\alpha\in[\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}]
for some fixed
\bar{\alpha}>\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}>0
. Assume that
\theta_{0}\in H^{\beta}{(L)}
for some
\beta\in(\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha})
and
L>0
.

i)

Let
\Lambda_{n}\coloneqq[\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}]
be the set of candidate hyper-parameter values for the MMLE
\hat{\lambda}
(constant for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
).
Then, for any
M_{n}
tending to infinity arbitrarily slowly, it holds

P^{n}_{\theta_{0}}\Big(\hat{\lambda}_{n}\in\Lambda_{0}(M_{n})\Big)\to 1

and the empirical Bayes posterior satisfies

\sup_{\theta_{0}\in H^{\beta}(L)}E_{\theta_{0}}^{n}\Pi(\theta:\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}\geq M_{n}{m}^{\ast}_{n}|X^{(n)},\hat{\lambda}_{n})=o(1),

(2.8)

where
{m}^{\ast}_{n}
is the minimax rate from (
2.1
).

ii)

Similarly, for a hyper-prior on
\lambda=\alpha
which satisfies Assumption
2.1
(ii), for
M_{n}
tending to infinity arbitrarily slowly, the hierarchical posterior satisfies

\sup_{\theta_{0}\in H^{\beta}(L)}E_{\theta_{0}}^{n}\Pi(\theta:\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}\geq M_{n}{m}^{\ast}_{n}|X^{(n)})=o(1).

(2.9)

For any
p\in[1,2]
, the minimax rate
{m}^{\ast}_{n}
is attained by both the empirical and hierarchical Bayes approaches of choosing the regularity
\alpha
, provided the truth has Sobolev regularity in the arbitrarily large interval
(\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha})
,
\bar{\alpha}>\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}>0
. For
p=2
, this is compatible with the result for truncated Gaussian priors in
[
32
]
and
[
41
, Proposition 3.2]
.

Remark 2.4
.

Note that Theorems
2.2
and
2.3
remain true for
\theta_{0}\in B^{\beta}_{qq}
with
q\geq 2
, that is over Besov spaces which do not permit spatial inhomogeneity in the truth.
This is a trivial implication of
[
4
, Proposition 5.4]
which shows that our upper bounds on the rates
\epsilon_{n}(\lambda)
solving (
1.3
) are identical for all
q\geq 2
.

2.4
Adaptive rates of contraction over Besov spaces permitting spatial inhomogeneity

We finally study adaptation over Besov spaces
B^{\beta}_{qq}
, with
1\leq q<2
, using
\alpha
-regular
\tau
-scaled
p
-exponential priors with
p\leq q
and choosing both the regularity
\alpha
and the scaling
\tau
, simultaneously.

Theorem 2.5
.

Consider
\alpha
-regular
\tau
-scaled
p
-exponential priors
\Pi(\cdot\mid\and)
in
\Theta
, where
\lambda=(\and)
for
\tau>0
and
\alpha\in[\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}]
with
\bar{\alpha}>\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}>0
fixed. Assume that
\theta_{0}\in B^{\beta}_{qq}(L)
for
1\leq p\leq q<2
,
\beta\in(\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}+1/p,\bar{\alpha}+1/p)
and
L>0
.

i)

Let

\Lambda_{n}:=\Big\{(\and):\alpha\in[\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}],\tau\in\big[n^{-\frac{1}{2+p+2\alpha p}},n^{\alpha}\big]\Big\},

be the set of candidate hyper-parameter values for the MMLE
\hat{\lambda}_{n}
. Then, for
M_{n}
tending to infinity arbitrarily slowly, it holds

P^{n}_{\theta_{0}}\Big(\hat{\lambda}_{n}\in\Lambda_{0}(M_{n})\Big)\to 1

and the empirical Bayes posterior satisfies

\sup_{\theta_{0}\in B^{\beta}_{qq}(L)}E_{\theta_{0}}^{n}\Pi(\theta:\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}\geq M_{n}{m}^{\ast}_{n}{\log(n)^{\frac{q-p}{pq(1+2\beta)}}}|X^{(n)},\hat{\lambda}_{n})=o(1),

(2.10)

where
{m}^{\ast}_{n}
is the minimax rate from (
2.1
).

ii)

Similarly, for a hyper-prior on
\lambda=(\and)
which satisfies Assumption
2.1
(iii), for
M_{n}
tending to infinity arbitrarily slowly, the hierarchical posterior satisfies

\sup_{\theta_{0}\in B^{\beta}_{qq}(L)}E_{\theta_{0}}^{n}\Pi(\theta:\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}\geq M_{n}{m}^{\ast}_{n}\log(n)^{\frac{q-p}{pq(1+2\beta)}}|X^{(n)})=o(1).

(2.11)

The minimax rate is attained when
p=q
, while for
p<q
the minimax rate is attained up to logarithmic terms. Note, that since we consider
\alpha\in[\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}]
and since in the non-adaptive results in
[
4
, Proposition 5.8]
the minimax rate is achieved for
\alpha=\beta-1/p
(for an appropriate choice of
\tau
), it is not surprising that the adaptation range is
\beta\in(\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}+1/p,\bar{\alpha}+1/p)
. In particular,
Laplace priors
attain the minimax or nearly the minimax rate over all Besov spaces
B^{\beta}_{qq}
with
1\leq q<2
and regularity
\beta
in the arbitrarily large interval
(\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}+1,\bar{\alpha}+1)
. Importantly, by choosing
\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}
sufficiently small, we can guarantee that Laplace priors attain the minimax rate over
B^{\beta}_{11}
where
\beta
can be arbitrarily close to 1. [The space
B^{1}_{11}
is the well known "bump algebra" which contains functions of "considerable spatial inhomogeneity",
[
22
]
.] In the case
p>q
which is not considered here, the non-adaptive bounds in
[
4
, Proposition 5.8]
suggest that the attained rate is substantially slower than minimax. Although it is not known whether these bounds are sharp, it is shown in
[
6
, Theorem 4.1]
that Gaussian priors (
p=2
) are fundamentally limited by the suboptimal linear minimax rate from (
2.2
).

2.5
Technical comments

The proofs of our three main results can be found in Section
3
, and broadly follow the techniques of
[
41
, Section 3.5]
where adaptive rates of contraction are studied with truncated Gaussian priors in the setting of nonparametric regression. Due to the fact that we consider the broader class of
p
-exponential priors and without truncation, we need to introduce certain new techniques in order to verify the assumptions of the invoked general theory of
[
41
]
. For example, we rely on a Fernique-type theorem for log-concave priors to obtain concentration of measure inequalities with constants which are uniform with respect to the hyper-parameter(s)
\lambda
and we borrow ideas from the recent literature on posterior contraction for inverse problems to get better controlled sieve sets, see Lemma
A.1

and Remark
C.4
in the Supplement below, respectively. A significant amount of effort is also devoted in the careful control of the constants in the small ball probability estimates, uniformly with respect to the hyper-paremeter(s)
\lambda
as required by the general theory, see Remark
C.3
and Lemma
D.1
in the Supplement below. We close this section with some technical comments on our results.

Remark 2.6
.

a)

Theorems
2.2
,
2.3
and
2.5
assume that
\beta\geq(1+\alpha p)/(p+2\alpha p)
,
\beta\in(\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha})
and
\beta\in(\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}+1/p,\bar{\alpha}+1/p)
, respectively. These assumptions on the regularity of the truth are stronger than the corresponding assumptions in non-adaptive settings:
\beta>0
under Sobolev regularity and
\beta>1/p
under Besov regularity, see Lemmas
3.4
and
3.6
below, respectively. The source of these more stringent conditions, is the fact that in the upper and lower bounds for the centered small ball probabilities with respect to
\alpha
-regular
\tau
-scaled
p
-exponential priors, it is impossible to choose the constants uniformly for all
\tau>0
and
\alpha>0
; see Lemma
3.2
below as well as Lemma
D.1
in the Supplement below. Since the employed general theory of
[
41
]
(naturally) requires a uniform control of the constants appearing in the small ball probability bounds with respect to the prior
\Pi(\cdot|\lambda)
for all
\lambda
in the set of candidate hyper-parameter values
\Lambda_{n}
, these assumptions appear to be unavoidable at present. Nevertheless, the additional restrictions are quite mild and do not obscure the main message of this article.

b)

More specifically, for fixed prior regularity
\alpha
and varying scaling
\tau
, the upper and lower bounds for the centered small ball probabilities hold with uniform constants if
\epsilon\leq\tilde{C}\tau
, for any fixed
\tilde{C}>0
, see Lemma
3.2
below. Our upper bounds on
\epsilon_{n}(\alpha,\tau)
solving (
1.3
), suggest that adaptation is possible for truth regularity
\beta<\alpha+1/p
by choosing
\tau=n^{(\alpha-\beta)/(1+2\beta)}
, see Lemma
3.4
(i) below. Hence, the smallest
\tau
we need to consider is
n^{-1/(2+p+2\alpha p)}
and the assumption
\beta\geq(1+\alpha p)/(p+2\alpha p)
is imposed in order to ensure that
\epsilon_{n}(\alpha,\tau)\leq\tilde{C}\tau
for all considered
\tau
. Note that for
p=2
the assumption becomes
\beta\geq 1/2
, while as
p
decreases it becomes more stringent. This is because, for a fixed truth regularity
\beta
, when
p
is smaller adaptation can be achieved down to smaller prior regularities
\alpha
via faster decaying choices of the scaling
\tau
. Hence, for a smaller
p
we need to consider smaller values of
\tau
, which imposes a stricter assumption on
\beta
so that
\epsilon_{n}(\alpha,\tau)\leq\tilde{C}\tau
.

c)

In all three theorems, the set
\Lambda_{0}
on which the MMLE concentrates is only implicitly defined in (
1.5
). Upper bounds for
\epsilon_{n}(\lambda)
and
\epsilon_{n,0}
for Sobolev truths, can be found in Lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
below (by
[
4
, Proposition 5.4]
these bounds hold more generally for Besov truths with
q\geq 2
). For Besov truths with
q<2
, upper bounds for
\epsilon_{n}(\lambda)
are implicitly computed in the proof of
[
4
, Proposition 5.8]
while bounds for
\epsilon_{n,0}
are given in the statement of the same result. In general, the upper bounds on
\epsilon_{n}(\lambda)
are not known to be sharp (see the brief discussion after (
1.7
) in Subsection
1.3
). Crude lower bounds based on the logarithmic-concavity of
p
-exponential measures are derived in Lemma
3.2
below, however, there is a gap between the upper and lower bounds when the prior is too smooth compared to the truth. As a result, we cannot explicitly characterize
\Lambda_{0}
. For the same reason, we do not study lower bounds on the rate of contraction.

d)

Explicitly identifying
\Lambda_{0}
is not necessary for our results. In fact, similarly to
[
41
, Remark 3.4]
our empirical Bayes results are stronger than the minimax results stated in Theorems
2.2
-
2.5
. The invoked Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.1 of
[
41
]
in the proof of our results, show that the MMLE Bayes posterior contracts around the truth for every
\theta_{0}\in\Theta
with rate
\epsilon_{n,0}(\theta_{0})
which can be faster than the minimax rate. A tighter understanding of
\Lambda_{0}
is, however, required for the hierarchical result, in particular we need to identify a subset of
\Lambda_{0}
with sufficient mass under the hyper-prior, see
[
41
, Section 2.2]
or assumption (
3.10
) below. Due to the gap between the available upper and lower bounds we cannot identify such a subset for arbitrary sequences
M_{n}\to\infty
, but we can do so for
M_{n}
such that
M_{n}\epsilon_{n,0}
matches our optimized upper bounds on
\epsilon_{n}(\lambda)
(for details see e.g. the proof of Theorem
2.2
below). As a result, for the hierarchical posterior we can only invoke
[
41
, Theorem 2.3]
with this choice of
M_{n}
, and thus can only establish the minimax results as stated in our theorems.

3
Proofs of main results

3.1
Notation and assumptions of the general contraction theory of
[
41
]

In this subsection we briefly recall the assumptions of the general contraction theory for empirical and hierarchical Bayes priors of
[
41
]
, which we employ in order to establish our main results. We formulate them specifically for the white noise model, and restrict our attention on the conditions which suffice for obtaining upper bounds on the rates of contraction.

Recall the definitions (
1.3
)-(
1.5
) from Subsection
1.2
.
Consider the transformation
\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}:\Theta\mapsto\Theta
for all
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{n}
, which is such that if
\theta\sim\Pi(\cdot\mid\lambda)
then
\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta)\sim\Pi(\cdot\mid\lambda^{\prime})
. For the different choices of hyper-parameter
\lambda
studied in this article, the transformation of a
\theta=(\theta_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\in\Theta
can be expressed as
\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta)=\big(\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta_{j})\big)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, where

\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta_{j})=\begin{cases}\vskip 5.69046pt\frac{\tau^{\prime}}{\tau}\theta_{j},&\text{for $\lambda=\tau$,}\\
\vskip 5.69046ptj^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\theta_{j},&\text{for $\lambda=\alpha$,}\\
\frac{\tau^{\prime}}{\tau}j^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\theta_{j},&\text{for $\lambda=(\alpha,\tau)$}.\end{cases}

(3.1)

Given a loss function
\rho:\Lambda_{n}\times\Lambda_{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
and a sequence
u_{n}\to 0
, introduce the notation

q_{\lambda,n}^{\theta}(X)=\sup_{\rho(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\leq u_{n}}p_{\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta)}^{n}(X),\quad X\in\mathcal{X}

(3.2)

and let
Q_{\lambda,n}^{\theta}
be the associated measure. In our setting, the loss function will have the following three forms depending on the choice of hyper-parameter
\lambda
:

\rho(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})=\begin{cases}\mid\log\tau-\log\tau^{\prime}\mid,&\text{for $\lambda=\tau$,}\\
\mid\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}\mid,&\text{for $\lambda=\alpha$,}\\
\mid\log\tau-\log\tau^{\prime}\mid+\mid\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}\mid,&\text{for $\lambda=(\alpha,\tau)$}.\end{cases}

(3.3)

Additionally, denote by
N_{n}(\Lambda_{0}),N_{n}(\Lambda_{n}\setminus\Lambda_{0})
and
N_{n}(\Lambda_{n})
the covering numbers of
\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{n}\setminus\Lambda_{0}
and
\Lambda_{n}
by
\rho
-balls of radius
u_{n}
, respectively.

The following assumptions are employed in
[
41
, Theorem 2.1]
in order to show that given
\theta_{0}\in\Theta
, the MMLE
\hat{\lambda}_{n}
belongs to
\Lambda_{0}=\Lambda_{0}(M_{n})
from (
1.5
), with
P^{n}_{\theta_{0}}
-probability tending to 1 as
n\to\infty
. This is done by establishing that the marginal likelihood is small for
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}\setminus\Lambda_{0}
.

•

(A1) There exists
N>0
such that for all
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}\backslash\Lambda_{0}
and
n\geq N
, there exists
\Theta_{n}(\lambda)\subset\Theta
such that

\sup_{\{\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}\leq K\epsilon_{n}(\lambda)\}\cap\Theta_{n}(\lambda)}\frac{\log Q_{\lambda,n}^{\theta}(\mathcal{X})}{n\epsilon_{n}^{2}(\lambda)}=o(1),

(3.4)

and

\int_{\Theta_{n}(\lambda)^{c}}Q_{\lambda,n}^{\theta}(\mathcal{X})d\Pi(\theta\mid\lambda)\leq e^{-w_{n}^{2}n\epsilon_{n,0}^{2}},

(3.5)

for some positive sequence
w_{n}
going to infinity.

•

(A2) There exist
\zeta>0,c_{1}<1
such that for all
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}\backslash\Lambda_{0}
and all
\theta\in\Theta_{n}(\lambda)
, there exist tests
\varphi_{n}(\theta)
such that

E^{n}_{\theta_{0}}\varphi_{n}(\theta)\leq e^{-c_{1}n\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|^{2}_{2}},\\
\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta_{n}(\lambda)\\
\left\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\right\|_{2}<\zeta\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\big(1-\varphi_{n}(\theta)\big)dQ^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\lambda,n}(X)\leq e^{-c_{1}n\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|^{2}_{2}}.

(3.6)

In addition, again for
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}\setminus\Lambda_{0}
, it holds

\log N\big(\zeta u,\{u\leq\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}\leq 2u\}\cap\Theta_{n}(\lambda),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2}\big)\leq c_{1}nu^{2}/2

(3.7)

for all
u\geq K\epsilon_{n}(\lambda)
, where
K
is as in (
1.3
).

In general, there are two additional conditions in
[
41
]
, which in our assumed setting hold trivially. The first one (equation (2.8) in that source), compares neighbourhoods with respect to the norm of the parameter space
\Theta
to neighbourhoods with respect to the metric used as loss in the definition of contraction rates. Since here both of these are given by the
L_{2}
-norm, this comparison is trivial (more specifically note that for
w_{n}=o(M_{n})
as we will choose later on and for
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}\setminus\Lambda_{0}
,
c(\lambda)
in (2.8) of
[
41
]
is allowed to take the constant value
K
from (
1.3
)). The second one (assumption (B1) in the same source), compares balls with respect to the norm of the parameter space (here
L_{2}
-norm) to Kullback-Leibler neighbourhoods. Since we are interested in the white noise model,
[
26
, Lemma 8.30]
shows that these two coincide, hence this assumption also holds trivially. Moreover, note that the assumption on the type-II error bound of the tests in
[
41
, Equation (2.7)]
, is formulated without restricting
\theta^{\prime}
on the sieve set
\Theta_{n}(\lambda)
. However, an inspection of the proof of
[
41
, Theorem 2.1]
where this assumption is used, immediately reveals that in all occurrences
\theta^{\prime}
does belong to
\Theta_{n}(\lambda)
, hence we can restrict the supremum on
\Theta_{n}(\lambda)
as in (
3.6
) above.

Having established that the MMLE belongs to
\Lambda_{0}
with
P^{n}_{\theta_{0}}

•

(3.8)

•

such that for all

The last set of additional assumptions is used in

to show that the hierarchical Bayes approach attains the same rates,

For a sequence

(H2) Uniformly over

For sieve sets

Lemma 3.2
.

3.3
Proofs of main theorems

In order to prove our three main results, we verify the conditions of Corollary
3.1
in each of the corresponding settings, in a similar way to the proof of
[

, Proposition 3.2]
.
The verification of these conditions is based on a number of lemmas contained in Section

of the Supplement.

Here we have
\lambda=\tau
, while
\alpha>0
is fixed. Let
M_{n},w_{n}
tending to infinity,
w_{n}=o(M_{n})
. Verifying the conditions of Corollary
3.1
and combining with the bounds on
\epsilon_{n}(\tau)
and
\epsilon_{n,0}
in the first part of Lemma
3.4
, establishes the result.

We start the proof with some observations. First, note that for all possible relationships between
\alpha,\beta
and
p
, the optimal choices of
\tau
, denoted by
\tau_{0}
in Lemma
3.4
, are contained in the set of candidate hyper-parameters
\Lambda_{n}=[n^{-1/(2+p+2\alpha p)},n^{\alpha}]
. Indeed, for
\beta>\alpha+1/p
we have
\tau_{0}=n^{-1/(2+p+2\alpha p)}
, for
\beta<\alpha+1/p
we have
n^{\alpha}>\tau_{0}=n^{(\alpha-\beta)/(1+2\beta)}>n^{-1/(2+p+2\alpha p)}
, while for
\beta=\alpha+1/p
we have
n^{\alpha}>\tau_{0}\geq n^{-1/(2+p+2\alpha p)}(\log n)^{\varsigma}
for some
\varsigma\geq 0
.

Moreover, note that there exists
\tilde{C}>0
such that
\epsilon_{n}(\tau)\leq\tilde{C}\tau
, for any
\tau\in\Lambda_{n}
and in all considered relationships between
\alpha,\beta,p
. Indeed, we use (
3.17
). First, notice that
n^{-\alpha/(1+2\alpha)}\tau^{1/(1+2\alpha)}\leq\tau
is equivalent to
\tau\geq n^{-1/2}
, which indeed holds for any
\tau\in\Lambda_{n}
. For
\beta<\alpha+1/p
, we check that
(n\tau^{p})^{\beta/[\beta(p-2)-\alpha p-1]}\leq\tau
or equivalently
\tau\geq n^{-\beta/(1+\alpha p+2\beta)}
, which holds for all
\tau\geq n^{-1/(2+p+2\alpha p)}
by the condition
\beta\geq(1+\alpha p)/(p+2\alpha p)
. For
\beta>\alpha+1/p
, we check that
n^{-1/2}\tau^{-p/2}\leq\tau
or equivalently
\tau\geq n^{-1/(2+p)}
, which holds trivially for all
\tau\geq n^{-1/(2+p+2\alpha p)}
. For
\beta=\alpha+1/p
, we check that
n^{-1/2}\tau^{-p/2}\big[\log(n\tau^{p})\big]^{1/2-p/4}\leq\tau
, which holds similarly to the previous case [using that for any
\delta>0
(arbitrarily small), there exists
C_{\delta}>0
such that
\log(n\tau^{p})\leq C_{\delta}(n\tau^{p})^{\delta},
for all
n\geq 1
and for all
\tau\in\Lambda_{n}
].

Finally, note that the considerations in the last paragraph, enable us to employ Lemma
3.2
to show that for all
\tau\in\Lambda_{n}
we have
\epsilon_{n}(\tau)\gtrsim n^{-t}
where
t<1/2
. By the definition of
\epsilon_{n,0}
, (
1.4
), we thus get that
\epsilon_{n}(\tau)\gtrsim\epsilon_{n,0}
for all
\tau\in\Lambda_{n}
.

We take
u_{n}\asymp n^{-(3+2\alpha)}
. First, we observe that
N_{n}(\Lambda_{n})\leq n^{H}
for some
H>0
. Indeed, by the definition of
\rho
, starting from the lower limit of
\Lambda_{n}
,
n^{-1/(2+p+2\alpha p)}
, the number

4
Outlook and computation

Combined, the three main theorems of this article indeed show that
\alpha
-regular
\tau
-scaled Laplace priors lead to adaptivity over all Besov spaces, spatially homogeneous
and
spatially inhomogeneous. This is in contrast to Gaussian priors which, due to
[
6
, Theorem 4.1]
, can only adapt over spatially homogeneous Besov spaces.

For this theoretical benefit of Laplace priors to be realized in practice, the efficient numerical implementation of the procedures studied in this work is crucial.
Naturally, this implementation is expected to pose some challenges and will be the topic of future work by the authors. The standard way of probing the joint posterior on
\theta,\lambda|X
in the studied hierarchical setting, uses a (Metropolis within) Gibbs sampler which alternates between updating
\theta|X,\lambda
and
\lambda|X,\theta
. In high dimensions, the parameters
\theta
and
\lambda
are strongly dependent under the assumed
\lambda
-dependent priors on
\theta
and, as a result, the
\lambda
-chain in such Gibbs samplers mixes poorly. This has been studied for Gaussian priors with hyper-priors on scaling or regularity hyper-parameters in
[
2
]
based on
[
39
]
; the intuition carries over to Laplace priors as well. To resolve this issue,
[
2
,
39
]
propose to re-parametrize the prior by writing
\theta=T(v,\lambda)
for some appropriate transformation
T
, where
v
and
\lambda
are a priori independent with priors chosen so that
T(v,\lambda)
has the desired prior distribution for
\theta
. For example, for an
\alpha
-regular
\tau
-scaled
p
-exponential prior with
\lambda=\tau
, we can write
\theta=\tau v
, where
v
is
\alpha
-regular
p
-exponential with
\tau=1
and
\tau
has the originally postulated hyper-prior distribution. The non-centered parametrization of the initial Gibbs sampler, alternates between updating
v|X,\lambda
and
\lambda|X,v
; samples for
\theta|X,\lambda
can be obtained using
T(v,\lambda)
. While this method leads to robust algorithms with respect to dimension, for small noise the
\lambda
-chain again mixes poorly because
v
and
\lambda
are strongly a posteriori dependent (since they are constrained to satisfy
T(v,\lambda)\approx\theta_{0}
). As a result, when the discretization level is high
and
the noise is small, both Gibbs sampler parametrizations have poor performance. A candidate alternative approach is the (pseudo) marginal algorithm which alternates between updating the marginal
\lambda|X
and
\theta|X,\lambda
,
[
7
]
; see also
[
2
, Section 2.3]
.

In addition,
p
-exponential priors with
p\neq 2
are not conjugate to the likelihood in the studied white noise model (conditionally on
\lambda
), hence one needs to employ Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods for sampling the conditional distributions
\theta|X,\lambda
or
v|X,\lambda
. Such methods will need to be used even with Gaussian priors, when implementing models for which Gaussian priors are not conjugate, such as density estimation
[
28
]
and non-linear inverse problems
[
29
]
. For Gaussian priors, MCMC algorithms which are well defined in function spaces and thus remain robust for high discretization levels, have been introduced in the last decade, see for example
[
15
,
16
,
11
]
. The contribution
[
14
]
discusses how to modify these algorithms to achieve dimension-robust posterior sampling for non-Gaussian priors including
p
-exponential. The idea is to write
\theta=T(\xi,\lambda)
where
\xi
is a Gaussian white noise and
T
is an appropriate transformation, which is such that
T(\xi,\lambda)
has the desired prior for
\theta|\lambda
, and to use one of the known dimension-robust algorithms for Gaussian priors to sample the posterior
\xi|X,\lambda
. Samples for
\theta|X,\lambda
can be obtained using
T(\xi,\lambda)
. In the same contribution, it is discussed how to combine this approach with a non-centered Gibbs sampler, to achieve dimension-robust sampling of the joint distribution of
\theta,\lambda|X
, when the prior on
\theta
is non-Gaussian.

For the implementation of the MMLE empirical Bayes approach, one can maximize the marginal likelihood over a fine grid of candidate values
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}
. For the computation of the marginal likelihood
\bar{m}(X^{(n)}|\lambda)
, which for
p<2
is not available explicitly, one can exploit the product structure in the assumed setting to express
\bar{m}
as a product of univariate integrals, which can be approximated, for example, using quadrature. Note that, even for
p=2
, it is possible that
\bar{m}(X^{(n)}|\cdot)
is multimodal with respect to
\lambda
, see
[
43
, Section 4.6]
.

A
The Fernique theorem for
\alpha
-regular
p
-exponential priors

We formulate a result based on Christer Borell’s generalization of Fernique’s theorem for Gaussian measures, to the class of log-concave measures, see Theorem 3.1. in
[
12
]
. Here, we consider the case of
\alpha
-regular
p
-exponential measures, which are known to be log-concave
[
4
, Proposition 2.3]
, and track the dependence of the constants appearing in the cited theorem on the regularity parameter
\alpha
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\Pi_{\alpha}\coloneqq\Pi(\cdot\mid\alpha,\tau=1)
be an
\alpha
-regular
p
-exponential measure. Fix
\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}>0
and assume that
\mu
is any weighted-
\ell_{r}
norm,
r\geq 1
, which is finite almost surely with respect to
\Pi_{\alpha}
,
\forall\alpha\geq\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}
. Then, there exist
s_{1},s_{2}>0
(depending on
\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}
and
\mu
), such that

E_{\Pi_{\alpha}}\big[e^{s_{2}\mu(\theta)}\big]\leq s_{1},\quad\forall\alpha\geq\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}.

(A.1)

The proof follows the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
, with the difference that the cited result considers a single logarithmically-concave measure, while we consider the family of
\alpha
-regular
p
-exponential priors with
\alpha\geq\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}
simultaneously.

Since
\mu
is finite almost surely with respect to
\Pi_{\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}}
and since
\Pi_{\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}}
is non-degenerate, there exists a
\lambda>0
such that

\Pi_{\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}}\big(\{\theta:\mu(\theta)<\lambda\}\big)\in(1/2,1).

Set

\gamma:=\Pi_{\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}}\big(\{\theta:\mu(\theta)<\lambda\}\big).

By the definition of
\Pi_{\alpha}
and since
\mu
is a weighted-
\ell_{r}
norm with
r\geq 1
, it is straightforward to see that for any
s>0
it holds

\Pi_{{\alpha}}\big(\{\theta:\mu(\theta)<s\}\big)\geq\Pi_{\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}}\big(\{\theta:\mu(\theta)<s\}\big),\quad\forall\alpha\geq\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}.

Let
A\coloneqq\{\theta:\mu(\theta)<\lambda\}
. Combining with
[
12
, Lemma 3.1]
, we thus get that for all
t\geq 1

\Pi_{\alpha}\big((tA)^{c}\big)=\Pi_{\alpha}\big(\{\theta:\mu(\theta)\geq\lambda t\}\big)\leq\Pi_{\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}}\big(\{\theta:\mu(\theta)\geq\lambda t\}\big)\leq\gamma\Big(\frac{1-\gamma}{\gamma}\Big)^{\frac{1+t}{2}},

(A.2)

where
0<(1-\gamma)/\gamma<1
.

For any
s_{2}>0
, using
[
23
, Exercise 2.2.7.]
and a change of variables, we get

\begin{split}E_{\Pi_{\alpha}}&\big[e^{s_{2}\mu(\theta)}\big]=\int_{0}^{\infty}s_{2}e^{s_{2}\lambda\psi}\Pi_{\alpha}\big(\{\theta:\mu(\theta)\geq\lambda\psi\}\big)\lambda d\psi\\
&=s_{2}\lambda\Big(\int_{0}^{1}e^{s_{2}\lambda\psi}\Pi_{\alpha}\big(\{\theta:\mu(\theta)\geq\lambda\psi\}\big)d\psi+\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{s_{2}\lambda\psi}\Pi_{\alpha}\big(\{\theta:\mu(\theta)\geq\lambda\psi\}\big)d\psi\Big).\end{split}

(A.3)

Let us study each of the two integrals on the above right hand side separately.

For the first integral, we have that

\int_{0}^{1}e^{s_{2}\lambda\psi}\Pi_{\alpha}\big(\{\theta:\mu(\theta)\geq\lambda\psi\}\big)d\psi\leq\int_{0}^{1}e^{s_{2}\lambda\psi}d\psi=\frac{e^{s_{2}\lambda}-1}{s_{2}\lambda}.

For the second integral, using (
A.2
) we have

\begin{split}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{s_{2}\lambda\psi}\Pi_{\alpha}\big(\{\theta:\mu(\theta)\geq\lambda\psi\}\big)d\psi&\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{s_{2}\lambda\psi}\gamma\Big(\frac{1-\gamma}{\gamma}\Big)^{\frac{1+\psi}{2}}d\psi\\
&=\gamma\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{s_{2}\lambda\psi+\frac{1+\psi}{2}\log\big(\frac{1-\gamma}{\gamma}\big)}d\psi\\
&=\gamma e^{-\frac{a}{2}}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{\psi\big(s_{2}\lambda-\frac{a}{2}\big)}d\psi,\end{split}

where
a=\log\big(\gamma/(1-\gamma)\big)>0
. Choosing
s_{2}<a/2\lambda,
we have

\begin{split}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{s_{2}\lambda\psi}\Pi_{\alpha}\big(\{\theta:\mu(\theta)\geq\lambda\psi\}\big)d\psi\leq\frac{\gamma e^{-\frac{a}{2}}}{\frac{a}{2}-s_{2}\lambda}.\end{split}

Combining we have that for
s_{2}<a/2\lambda,
it holds

E_{\Pi_{\alpha}}\big[e^{s_{2}\mu(\theta)}\big]\leq\frac{s_{2}\lambda\gamma e^{-\frac{a}{2}}}{\frac{a}{2}-s_{2}\lambda}+e^{s_{2}\lambda}-1,\quad\forall\alpha\geq\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},

which completes the proof.
∎

B
Existence of
\epsilon_{n}(\lambda)
solving (
1.3
)

Lemma B.1
.

Let
\Pi(\cdot\mid\and)
be an
\alpha
-regular
\tau
-scaled
p
-exponential prior in
\Theta
, for some
\and>0
. Let
\theta_{0}\in\Theta
and fix
K>0
. Then,
\forall n
there exists a unique
\epsilon_{n}=\epsilon_{n}(\and;K,\theta_{0})
, such that

\Pi\big(\theta:\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}\leq K\epsilon_{n}\mid\and\big)=e^{-n\epsilon_{n}^{2}}.

(B.1)

Furthermore, given
\and>0
, the sequence
(\epsilon_{n})
is strictly decreasing to zero.

We first verify that
r(\epsilon)\coloneqq\Pi(\theta:\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}\leq K\epsilon\mid\and)
is a continuous function of
\epsilon>0
. Indeed this can be checked by verifying left and right continuity using the continuity property of probability measures, where for left continuity one needs to use that
\Pi(\theta:\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}=\epsilon\mid\and)=0,\forall\epsilon>0
, implied by
[
3
, Lemma 6.1]
.

The function
r(\cdot)
is obviously non-decreasing with
\epsilon
, with
\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}r(\epsilon)=0
and
\lim_{\epsilon\nearrow\infty}r(\epsilon)=1
.
In fact,
r(\cdot)
is strictly increasing. Indeed, if not, then there would exist
\epsilon_{1}>\epsilon_{2}
such that
r(\epsilon_{1})=r(\epsilon_{2})
. In particular, there would exist a
\theta_{1}\in\Theta
and a small enough radius
\delta>0
, such that
\Pi(\theta:\left\|\theta-\theta_{1}\right\|_{2}<\delta\mid\and)=0
. This would be a contradiction to the fact that
\text{supp}\big(\Pi(\cdot\mid\and)\big)=\Theta
, as implied by
[
4
, Proposition 2.10]
.

The above considerations imply the claims in the statement, since
E_{n}(\epsilon)\coloneqq e^{-n\epsilon^{2}}
is decreasing in both
\epsilon
and
n
with
\lim_{\epsilon\searrow 0}E_{n}(\epsilon)=1
and
\lim_{\epsilon\nearrow\infty}E_{n}(\epsilon)=0.

∎

Remark C.1
.

In all of these lemmas, it is silently assumed that for some fixed

,

from (

) satisfies

Lemma C.2
.

Fix
\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}
and
s
such that
\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}>s>0
. For any
\alpha\geq\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\tau>0
and
\zeta\in(0,1)

\eta\geq\tilde{c}_{1}^{2}\big(4\zeta^{-1}\big)^{1/\alpha},
where
\tilde{c}_{1}=\tilde{c}_{1}(\alpha)
is as in (

\tilde{\Theta}_{n}(\alpha,\tau)\coloneqq\frac{\zeta K}{4}\epsilon_{n}B_{\ell_{2}}+R_{n}^{\frac{p}{2}}B_{\mathcal{Q_{\alpha,\tau}}}+R_{n}B_{\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,\tau}},

where
\epsilon_{n}=\epsilon_{n}(\alpha,\tau)

) and

R_{n}=R_{n}(\and)\coloneqq\big(2\eta\tilde{K}n\epsilon_{n}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad p\in[1,2].

Here,
\tilde{K}>0
is a fixed constant depending only on

, defined in the concentration inequality
[
4
, Proposition 2.15]
.

Let
s_{1}=s_{1}(\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha};s)>0,s_{2}=s_{2}(\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha};s)>0
be as in Lemma
A.1
, for
\mu(\cdot)=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{{H^{s}}}
. For
Y_{n}=\eta\tau n\epsilon_{n}^{2}/s_{2}
, define the sets

\Theta_{n}(\and)\coloneqq\tilde{\Theta}_{n}(\and)\cap Y_{n}B_{{H^{s}}},\quad\alpha\geq\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\tau>0.

(C.1)

Then,

\Pi\big(\Theta_{n}^{c}(\alpha,\tau)\mid\alpha,\tau\big)\leq 2s_{1}e^{-\eta n\epsilon_{n}^{2}}

(C.2)

and there exists a constant
M^{\prime}>0
(independent of
\alpha
,
\tau
and
K
) such that

\log N\big(K\zeta\epsilon_{n},\Theta_{n}(\alpha,\tau),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{2}\big)\leq M^{\prime}\eta^{p}\tilde{c}_{1}^{2}n\epsilon_{n}^{2}.

We use ideas from the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
.

For the first claim, by
[
4
, Proposition 2.15]
(here we denote by
\tilde{K}
the constant
K
in the notation of that source) we have

\displaystyle\Pi\big(\tilde{\Theta}_{n}^{c}(\alpha,\tau)\mid\alpha,\tau\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Pi\big(\frac{\zeta K}{4}\epsilon_{n}B_{\ell_{2}}\mid\and\big)}e^{-R_{n}^{p}/\tilde{K}}=e^{\varphi_{0}(\frac{\zeta K}{4}\epsilon_{n})}e^{-2\eta n\epsilon_{n}^{2}}.

Since
\eta\geq\tilde{c}_{1}^{2}(4\zeta^{-1})^{1/\alpha},
and by combining (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) (see also Remark
C.1
), it holds

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}\Big(\frac{\zeta K}{4}\epsilon_{n}\Big)-\eta n\epsilon_{n}^{2}\leq\varphi_{0}\Big(\frac{\zeta K}{4}\epsilon_{n}\Big)-\tilde{c}_{1}^{2}\big(4\zeta^{-1}\big)^{1/\alpha}n\epsilon_{n}^{2}\leq 0,

so that

\Pi\big(\tilde{\Theta}_{n}^{c}(\alpha,\tau)\mid\alpha,\tau\big)\leq e^{-\eta n\epsilon_{n}^{2}}.

By the definition of
\Theta_{n}(\and)
, it thus remains to show that

\Pi\big((Y_{n}B_{{H^{s}}})^{c}|\and\big)\leq s_{1}e^{-\eta n\epsilon_{n}^{2}}.

Indeed, using the (exponential) Markov inequality, for any
c,r>0
we have that

\Pi(\left\|\theta\right\|_{{H^{s}}}\geq r\mid\and)=\Pi(\left\|\theta\right\|_{{H^{s}}}\geq r/{\tau}\mid\alpha,\tau=1)\leq\frac{E_{\alpha,1}[e^{c\left\|\theta\right\|_{{H^{s}}}}]}{e^{c\frac{r}{\tau}}},

where
E_{\alpha,1}
denotes expectation with respect to
\Pi(\cdot\mid\alpha,\tau=1)
. Using Fernique’s theorem, see Lemma
A.1
, and by
[
4
, Lemma 5.2]
which implies that the

-norm is almost surely finite with respect to

for

, we get that there exist

such that

Choosing

, completes the proof.

For the second claim, note that since
\Theta_{n}(\and)\subset\tilde{\Theta}_{n}(\and)
it suffices to establish the same bound for covering
\tilde{\Theta}_{n}(\and)
instead of
\Theta_{n}(\and)
. We first show that
\forall\epsilon,R>0

(C.3)

where

a=a(\epsilon,R)=R^{\frac{p}{2}+\frac{2-p}{2(1+2\alpha)}}\tau^{\frac{2-p}{p(1+2\alpha)}}\epsilon^{\frac{p-2}{p(1+2\alpha)}}.

Indeed, let

, that is

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell^{1+2\alpha}w_{\ell}^{2}\leq\tau^{2}R^{p}.

Consider
h_{L}=(w_{1},\cdots,w_{L},0,\cdots,0)
, where

is sufficiently large so that
\left\|h_{L}-w\right\|_{2}\leq\epsilon
. It holds

\left\|h_{L}-w\right\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{\ell=L+1}^{\infty}w_{\ell}^{2}=\sum_{\ell=L+1}^{\infty}\ell^{1+2\alpha}\ell^{-1-2\alpha}w_{\ell}^{2}\leq L^{-1-2\alpha}\tau^{2}R^{p},

so that it suffices to take

Using Hölder inequality with

\displaystyle\left\|h_{L}\right\|^{p}_{\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,\tau}}

\displaystyle=\tau^{-p}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\ell^{\frac{p}{2}+\alpha p}|w_{\ell}|^{p}\leq\tau^{-p}\bigg(\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\ell^{1+2\alpha}|w_{\ell}|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{2}}L^{\frac{2-p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{-p}\big(\tau^{2}R^{p}\big)^{\frac{p}{2}}L^{\frac{2-p}{2}}=R^{\frac{p^{2}}{2}}\bigg(\frac{R^{p}\tau^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{2-p}{2(1+2\alpha)}}=R^{\frac{p^{2}}{2}+{\frac{p(2-p)}{2(1+2\alpha)}}}\tau^{\frac{2-p}{1+2\alpha}}\epsilon^{\frac{p-2}{1+2\alpha}}.

The inclusion (
C.3
) thus holds.

We thus obtain that

\tilde{\Theta}_{n}(\alpha,\tau)\subseteq\frac{\zeta K}{2}\epsilon_{n}B_{\ell_{2}}+\bigg(a\Big(\frac{\zeta K}{4}\epsilon_{n},R_{n}\Big)+R_{n}\bigg)B_{\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,\tau}},

where using the definition of
R_{n}
and (
3.16
) (which we solve for
\tau
), we can bound

\displaystyle a\Big(\frac{\zeta K}{4}\epsilon_{n},R_{n}\Big)

\displaystyle=R_{n}^{\frac{p}{2}+{\frac{2-p}{2(1+2\alpha)}}}\tau^{\frac{2-p}{p(1+2\alpha)}}\epsilon_{n}^{\frac{p-2}{p(1+2\alpha)}}\Big(\frac{\zeta K}{4}\Big)^{\frac{p-2}{p(1+2\alpha)}}

\displaystyle\leq\big(2\eta\tilde{K}n\epsilon_{n}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2-p}{2p(1+2\alpha)}}\big(\epsilon_{n}n^{\frac{\alpha}{1+2\alpha}}\big)^{\frac{2-p}{p}}\big(\tilde{c}_{1}K^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\big)^{\frac{\alpha(2-p)}{p(1+2\alpha)}}\epsilon_{n}^{\frac{p-2}{p(1+2\alpha)}}\Big(\frac{\zeta K}{4}\Big)^{\frac{p-2}{p(1+2\alpha)}}

\displaystyle\leq\big(2\eta\tilde{K}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2-p}{2p(1+2\alpha)}}\tilde{c}_{1}^{\frac{\alpha(2-p)}{p(1+2\alpha)}}(4/\zeta)^{\frac{2-p}{p(1+2\alpha)}}\big(n\epsilon_{n}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Noting that
\eta\geq 1
and taking into account that

and

(which implies that the exponents

), we can further bound

a\Big(\frac{\zeta K}{4}\epsilon_{n},R_{n}\Big)\leq\eta\tilde{c}_{1}M_{1}\big(n\epsilon_{n}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where the constant

does not depend on

.
We thus get that

\tilde{\Theta}_{n}(\alpha,\tau)\subseteq\frac{\zeta K}{2}\epsilon_{n}B_{\ell_{2}}+\eta\tilde{c}_{1}M_{2}\big(n\epsilon_{n}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}B_{\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,\tau}},

(C.5)

for some constant
M_{2}
again independent of
\alpha,\tau,K
, where we used that
\tilde{c}_{1}\geq 1
and
\eta^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\eta
since
\eta\geq 1
.

Now, let
h_{1},\cdots,h_{N}\in\eta\tilde{c}_{1}M_{2}\big(n\epsilon_{n}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}B_{\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,\tau}}
be
\zeta K\epsilon_{n}/2
-apart in

. Then, the balls

are disjoint, and hence by

we obtain

1\geq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Pi\Big(h_{j}+\frac{\zeta K}{4}\epsilon_{n}B_{\ell_{2}}|\and\Big)\geq\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-\frac{\left\|h_{j}\right\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha,\tau}}^{p}}{p}}\Pi\Big(\frac{\zeta K}{4}\epsilon_{n}B_{\ell_{2}}|\and\Big)\geq Ne^{-\frac{\eta^{p}\tilde{c}_{1}^{p}M_{2}^{p}n\epsilon_{n}^{2}}{p}-\varphi_{0}\big(\frac{\zeta K}{4}\epsilon_{n}\big)}.

Remark C.3

A careful examination of the sharp results of

Remark C.4
.

The set
\tilde{\Theta}_{n}(\lambda)
is a natural candidate for the sieve set due to Talagrand’s inequality (

). Here, we intersect
\tilde{\Theta}_{n}(\lambda)
with a ball in a Sobolev space

for

which can be arbitrarily small, with a suitably growing radius as

. The additional regularity infused on the sieve set

, will allow us to uniformly control small perturbations of its elements, resulting in a uniform control of the data-dependence of the hyper-parameter (see the proofs of Lemmas

and
C.7
below). This technique is inspired by the recent literature on rates of contraction for PDE inverse problems, see for example
[

29

6

, where the sieve set is intersected with a ball of sufficiently large (but fixed) radius in some suitably regular space (there the motivation is different: it allows the exploitation on the one hand of local-Lipschitz assumptions on the forward operator to get rates of contraction for the forward problem and on the other hand of stability estimates to pass from "forward" to "inverse" rates).

Lemma C.5

Let
\bar{\tau}_{n}\to\infty
, and
s,\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}\leq\alpha\leq\bar{\alpha}\\
\ 0<\tau<\bar{\tau}_{n}\end{subarray}}\;\sup_{\theta\in\Theta_{n}(\and)}Q^{\theta}_{\lambda,n}(\mathcal{X})=O(1).

(C.8)

The bound holds for
\lambda=\tau
or
\alpha
or
(\and)
and the loss
\rho
in the definition of

is as in (

).

The proof follows the lines of the proof of
[

which considers

in the non-parametric regression setting.
Apart from considering the white noise model, the main difference here is that we need to additionally control the tail of the sequence

, and to this end we restrict the inner supremum on the set
\Theta_{n}
, which in particular implies that

\displaystyle\underline{\psi}_{\lambda,j}^{\theta}

\displaystyle=\inf_{|\log\tau-\log\tau^{\prime}|+|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}|\leq u_{n}}\frac{\tau^{\prime}}{\tau}j^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\theta_{j}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\log\tau-\log\tau^{\prime}|\leq u_{n}\\
|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}|\leq u_{n}\end{subarray}}\frac{\tau^{\prime}}{\tau}j^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\theta_{j}=\inf_{|\log\tau-\log\tau^{\prime}|\leq u_{n}}\Big(\frac{\tau^{\prime}}{\tau}\inf_{|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}|\leq u_{n}}j^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\theta_{j}\Big)

\displaystyle\overline{\psi}_{\lambda,j}^{\theta}\leq j^{{\rm sign}(\theta_{j})u_{n}}e^{{\rm sign}(\theta_{j})u_{n}}\theta_{j}.

By triangle inequality, taking into account that
u_{n}\to 0

\displaystyle\Big|\underline{\psi}_{\lambda,j}^{\theta}-\overline{\psi}_{\lambda,j}^{\theta}\Big|

(C.11)

for any

and any

.

For

and
\theta\in\Theta_{n}(\and)

(C.12)

Taking into account Remark
C.3
which says that
\eta=\eta(\alpha)
remains bounded for
\alpha\in[\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}]
, as well as Lemma
A.1
which says that
s_{2}
depends only on the fixed
\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}
and
s<\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}
, we have that the exponent on the right hand side is bounded above by a constant multiple of
u_{n}\bar{\tau}_{n}n^{3/2}
, and thus remains bounded for the assumed choice of
u_{n}
.
∎

Lemma C.6
.

Consider the white noise model. Let
\bar{\tau}_{n}\to\infty
and
s,\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}
such that
\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}>s>0
. Let
\epsilon_{n}=\epsilon_{n}(\and)
and recall

from Lemma

. Consider any of the settings:

\lambda=\tau
, where

and

is fixed,
u_{n}\lesssim\bar{\tau}_{n}^{-1}n^{-1}
;

ii)

, where

.

Then there exists

as in Lemma
C.2
, it holds

\int_{\Theta_{n}^{c}(\and)}Q^{\theta}_{\lambda,n}(\mathcal{X})\Pi(d\theta\mid\alpha,\tau)\leq Ce^{-\frac{\eta}{2}n\epsilon_{n}^{2}}.

(C.13)

The constant

, while in the settings (ii) and (iii), it can be chosen uniformly over
\alpha>\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}
.

This lemma corresponds to
[
41
, Lemma E.2]
, however for the proof we use a different technique relying on the Fernique theorem, see Lemma
A.1
. We prove here the case
\lambda=(\and)
which is based on the estimate (
C.11
), while the other two cases follow easily with obvious modifications.

Let
\lambda=(\and)
where
\alpha\geq\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}
and

. By (
C.11
) (which holds for any

) and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get that

\displaystyle\int_{\Theta_{n}^{c}}Q^{\theta}_{\alpha,\tau,n}(\mathcal{X})\Pi(d\theta|\alpha,\tau)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Theta_{n}^{c}}e^{cu_{n}n^{1/2}\left\|\theta\right\|_{H^{s}}}\Pi(d\theta|\alpha,\tau)

\displaystyle\leq\Pi(\Theta_{n}^{c}|\alpha,\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\int_{\Theta}e^{2cu_{n}n^{1/2}\left\|\theta\right\|_{H^{s}}}\Pi(d\theta|\alpha,\tau)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

from Lemma

Lemma C.7
.

Consider the white noise model. Let
\bar{\tau}_{n}\to\infty
and
s,\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}
such that
\bar{\alpha}>\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}>s>0
. Recall
\Theta_{n}(\and)
from Lemma
C.2
. Consider any of the settings:

i)

\lambda=\tau
where
\alpha>0
is fixed,
\bar{\Lambda}_{n}\coloneqq(0,\bar{\tau}_{n})
and
u_{n}\lesssim\bar{\tau}_{n}^{-2}n^{-3}
;

ii)

\lambda=\alpha
where
\tau>0
is fixed,
\bar{\Lambda}_{n}\coloneqq[\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}]
and
u_{n}\lesssim n^{-3}
;

iii)

\lambda=(\and)
,
\bar{\Lambda}_{n}\coloneqq[\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}]\times(0,\bar{\tau}_{n})
, and
u_{n}\lesssim\bar{\tau}_{n}^{-2}n^{-3}
.

Then for
\theta\in\Theta_{n}(\and)
, there exist tests
\varphi_{n}(\theta)
such that

\begin{split}&E^{n}_{\theta_{0}}\varphi_{n}(\theta)\leq e^{-\frac{1}{32}n\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|^{2}_{2}},\\
&\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\bar{\Lambda}_{n}\end{subarray}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta_{n}(\and)\\
\left\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\right\|_{2}<\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}/4\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\big(1-\varphi_{n}(\theta)\big)dQ^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\lambda,n}(X)\leq e^{-\frac{1}{32}n\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|^{2}_{2}}.\end{split}

(C.14)

The proof proceeds broadly along the lines of the proof of
[
41
, Lemma E.3]
, which considers
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the non-parametric regression setting. In order to handle the infinite dimensionality of both the data and the parameter space in the presently assumed white noise model setting, we substantially exploit the
H^{s}
-regularity of elements of the sieve set
\Theta_{n}
. Furthermore, we need to study the case
\lambda=(\and)
. We in fact only study that case, while the other two cases follow with obvious modifications.

The likelihood ratio test

\varphi_{n}(\theta)={\bf 1}\Big[\big\langle{X},\theta-\theta_{0}\big\rangle>\left\|\theta\right\|^{2}_{2}/2-\left\|\theta_{0}\right\|^{2}_{2}/2\Big],

(C.15)

satisfies for any
\theta,\theta_{0}\in\ell_{2}

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta^{\prime}\in\ell_{2}:\left\|\theta-\theta^{\prime}\right\|_{2}<\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}/4\end{subarray}}E^{n}_{\theta^{\prime}}\big(1-\varphi_{n}(\theta)\big)\leq e^{-\frac{1}{32}n\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|^{2}_{2}}

(C.16)

and

E^{n}_{\theta_{0}}\varphi_{n}(\theta)\leq e^{-\frac{1}{32}n\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|^{2}_{2}},

see
[
25
, Lemma 5]
and
[
26
, Lemma K.6]
. In particular, the first inequality in (
C.14
) is verified and it remains to prove the second.

Using the definition of
Q^{\theta}_{\lambda,n}
and letting
Z=Z(X)\coloneqq\sup_{\rho(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\leq u_{n}}\left|\big\langle X,\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})-\theta^{\prime}\big\rangle\right|
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{X}}\big(1-\varphi_{n}(\theta)\big)dQ^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\lambda,n}(X)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{X}}\big(1-\varphi_{n}(\theta)\big)\sup_{\rho(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\leq u_{n}}\Big(e^{n\big\langle{X},\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})\big\rangle-\frac{n}{2}\left\|\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})\right\|_{2}^{2}}\Big)dP^{n}_{0}({X})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\{Z<u_{n}\bar{\tau}_{n}^{2}n^{2}\}}\big(1-\varphi_{n}(\theta)\big)e^{n\big\langle{X},\theta^{\prime}\big\rangle-\frac{n}{2}\left\|\theta^{\prime}\right\|_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\sup_{\rho(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\leq u_{n}}\Big(e^{n\big\langle{X},\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})-\theta^{\prime}\big\rangle-\frac{n}{2}\left\|\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{n}{2}\left\|\theta^{\prime}\right\|_{2}^{2}}\Big)dP^{n}_{0}({X})

\displaystyle+\int_{\{Z>u_{n}\bar{\tau}_{n}^{2}n^{2}\}}\;\sup_{\rho(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\leq u_{n}}e^{n\left|\big\langle{X},\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})\big\rangle\right|}dP^{n}_{0}({X}).

(C.17)

We deal with the two integral-terms on the right hand side separately.

We examine the first integral, and we notice that by (
C.16
) it suffices to show that the supremum is bounded above by a constant. Indeed, using triangle inequalities (both sided), we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{\rho(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\leq u_{n}}\Big|2n\big\langle{X},\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})-\theta^{\prime}\big\rangle-n\left\|\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})\right\|_{2}^{2}+n\left\|\theta^{\prime}\right\|_{2}^{2}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq 2nZ+n\sup_{\rho(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\leq u_{n}}\left\{\Big|\left\|\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})\right\|_{2}-\left\|\theta^{\prime}\right\|_{2}\Big|\Big(\left\|\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})\right\|_{2}+\left\|\theta^{\prime}\right\|_{2}\Big)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 2nZ+n\sup_{\rho(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\leq u_{n}}\left\{\left\|\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})-\theta^{\prime}\right\|_{2}\Big(\left\|\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})\right\|_{2}+\left\|\theta^{\prime}\right\|_{2}\Big)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 2nZ+n\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\big(\overline{\psi}_{\lambda,j}^{\theta^{\prime}}-\underline{\psi}_{\lambda,j}^{\theta^{\prime}}\big)^{2}}\Big(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\end{subarray}\leq u_{n}}\left\|\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})\right\|_{2}+\left\|\theta^{\prime}\right\|_{H^{s}}\Big),

where
\underline{\psi}_{\lambda,j}^{\theta^{\prime}},\overline{\psi}_{\lambda,j}^{\theta^{\prime}}
are defined in the proof of Lemma
C.5
. Recall from (
3.1
) that, in the studied setting, the transformation
\psi
is given as
\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta_{j})=\frac{\tau^{\prime}}{\tau}j^{\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}}\theta_{j}
.
We bound the right hand side above, for
\lambda=(\and)
and for
\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta_{n}(\and)
. First note that since
u_{n}\to 0
, for sufficiently large
n
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rho(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\end{subarray}\leq u_{n}}\left\|\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq e^{u_{n}}\sup_{|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}|\leq u_{n}}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{2(\alpha-\alpha^{\prime})}\theta_{j}^{\prime 2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq 2\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{2s}\theta_{j}^{\prime 2}\bigg)^{1/2}=2\left\|\theta^{\prime}\right\|_{H^{s}},

(C.18)

where we used that
s>0
. Similarly to the derivation of (
C
), using the inequality
1-1/x\leq\log(x)
for
x>0
, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\underline{\psi}_{\lambda,j}^{\theta^{\prime}}-\overline{\psi}_{\lambda,j}^{\theta^{\prime}}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(j^{{\rm sign}(\theta_{j}^{\prime})u_{n}}e^{{\rm sign}(\theta_{j}^{\prime})u_{n}}\theta_{j}^{\prime}-j^{-{\rm sign}(\theta_{j}^{\prime})u_{n}}e^{-{\rm sign}(\theta_{j}^{\prime})u_{n}}\theta_{j}^{\prime}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{-2{\rm sign}(\theta_{j}^{\prime})u_{n}}\big(j^{-u_{n}}-j^{u_{n}}\big)^{2}{\theta_{j}^{\prime}}^{2}+2j^{2{\rm sign}(\theta_{j}^{\prime})u_{n}}{\theta_{j}^{\prime}}^{2}(e^{-u_{n}}-e^{u_{n}})^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big(j^{u_{n}}-j^{-u_{n}}\big)^{2}{\theta_{j}^{\prime}}^{2}+j^{2u_{n}}{\theta_{j}^{\prime}}^{2}(e^{u_{n}}-e^{-u_{n}})^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim u_{n}^{2}j^{2u_{n}}(\log j)^{2}{\theta_{j}^{\prime}}^{2}+u_{n}^{2}e^{2u_{n}}j^{2u_{n}}{\theta_{j}^{\prime}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim u_{n}^{2}j^{2s}{\theta_{j}^{\prime}}^{2},

hence

\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\big(\overline{\psi}_{\lambda,j}^{\theta^{\prime}}-\underline{\psi}_{\lambda,j}^{\theta^{\prime}}\big)^{2}}\lesssim u_{n}\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{2s}\theta^{\prime 2}_{j}}=u_{n}\left\|\theta^{\prime}\right\|_{H^{s}}.

(C.19)

Combining
with the definition of
\Theta_{n}(\and)
and Remark
C.3
, we obtain

\Big|2n\big\langle{X},\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})-\theta^{\prime}\big\rangle-n\left\|\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})\right\|_{2}^{2}+n\left\|\theta^{\prime}\right\|_{2}^{2}\Big|\lesssim u_{n}\bar{\tau}_{n}^{2}n^{3}=O(1),

(C.20)

for the given choice of
u_{n}
.

Returning to the second integral in (
C
), we first notice that for
X\sim P^{n}_{0}
,
Z
is equal in distribution to
\frac{|\xi|}{\sqrt{n}}\sup_{\rho(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\leq u_{n}}\left\|\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})-\theta^{\prime}\right\|_{2},
where
\xi\sim N(0,1)
and similarly

\sup_{\rho(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\leq u_{n}}\big\langle X,\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})\big\rangle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle D}}{{=}}\frac{|\xi|}{\sqrt{n}}\sup_{\rho(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\leq u_{n}}\left\|\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})\right\|_{2}.

The integral of interest can thus be written as

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{|x|>r}\exp\Big(\sqrt{n}|x|\sup_{\rho(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\leq u_{n}}\left\|\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})\right\|_{2}-\frac{x^{2}}{2}\Big)dx,

where
r=\frac{u_{n}\bar{\tau}_{n}^{2}n^{5/2}}{\sup_{\rho(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\leq u_{n}}\left\|\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})-\theta^{\prime}\right\|_{2}}
. By (
C.19
), the definition of
\Theta_{n}(\and)
and Remark
C.3
, we have that (for sufficiently large
n
) the denominator in
r
is bounded above by
u_{n}\bar{\tau}_{n}n
, hence the last displayed integral can be bounded above by

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{|x|>\bar{\tau}_{n}n^{3/2}}\exp\Big(\sqrt{n}|x|\sup_{\rho(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\leq u_{n}}\left\|\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})\right\|_{2}-\frac{x^{2}}{2}\Big)dx.

Using (
C.18
), we note that for
|x|>\bar{\tau}_{n}n^{3/2}
and for
\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta_{n}(\and)
, it holds

\sqrt{n}|x|\sup_{\rho(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\leq u_{n}}\left\|\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta^{\prime})\right\|_{2}\leq 2\sqrt{n}|x|\left\|\theta^{\prime}\right\|_{H^{s}}=o(1)x^{2}.

Combined, the above considerations yield that the second integral in (
C
) is bounded by

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{|x|>\bar{\tau}_{n}n^{3/2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{x^{2}}{4}\Big)dx\leq ce^{-\frac{\bar{\tau}_{n}^{2}n^{3}}{4}},

where the latter bound is implied by
[
26
, Lemma K.6]
for a sufficiently large constant
c>0
. Observing that for
\theta\in\Theta_{n}(\and)
it holds

\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\theta\right\|_{2}\lesssim 1+\left\|\theta\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim 1+\bar{\tau}_{n}n\varepsilon_{n}^{2}(\lambda)=o(\bar{\tau}_{n}n),

we have that the obtained bound is of smaller order than
e^{-\frac{1}{32}n\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|^{2}_{2}}
which completes the proof.
∎

The next lemma concerns condition (H2).

Lemma C.8
.

Consider the white noise model. Let
\bar{\tau}_{n}\to\infty
and
s,\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}
such that
\bar{\alpha}>\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}>s>0
. Recall
\Theta_{n}(\and)
from Lemma
C.2
and consider any of the three settings studied in Lemma
C.7
. Fix
K>0
. Then for all
\lambda\in\bar{\Lambda}_{n}
, for any
c_{3}\geq 2+2K^{2}
it holds

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\in\Theta_{n}(\and)\\
\theta:\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}\leq K\varepsilon_{n}(\lambda)\end{subarray}}P^{n}_{\theta_{0}}\Big\{\inf_{\rho(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\leq u_{n}}\ell_{n}\big(\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta)\big)-\ell_{n}\big(\theta_{0}\big)\leq-c_{3}n\varepsilon_{n}^{2}(\lambda)\Big\}\leq 2e^{-n\varepsilon_{n}^{2}(\lambda)}.

The proof follows the reasoning of the proof of
[
41
, Lemma E.4]
, which refers to nonparametric regression. As in Lemma
C.7
above, we exploit the
H^{s}
-regularity induced by
\Theta_{n}
, in order to handle the infinite dimensionality of both the data and the parameter space in the white noise model. As a result, we have the additional restriction
\theta\in\Theta_{n}
in the supremum, compared to
[
41
, Lemma E.4]
. Once more, we prove the case
\lambda=(\and)
while the other two cases follow with obvious modifications.

We first note that it suffices to show that

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\in\Theta_{n}(\and)\\
\theta:\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}\leq K\varepsilon_{n}(\lambda)\end{subarray}}P^{n}_{\theta_{0}}\Big\{\sup_{\rho(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\leq u_{n}}|\ell_{n}\big(\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta)\big)-\ell_{n}\big(\theta\big)|\geq\frac{c_{3}}{2}n\varepsilon_{n}^{2}(\lambda)\Big\}\leq e^{-n\varepsilon_{n}^{2}(\lambda)}

(C.21)

and

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\in\Theta_{n}(\and)\\
\theta:\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}\leq K\varepsilon_{n}(\lambda)\end{subarray}}P^{n}_{\theta_{0}}\Big\{\ell_{n}(\theta)-\ell_{n}(\theta_{0})\leq-\frac{c_{3}}{2}n\varepsilon_{n}^{2}(\lambda)\Big\}\leq e^{-n\varepsilon_{n}^{2}(\lambda)}.

(C.22)

As in the proof of Lemma
C.7
, define
Z=Z(X)\coloneqq\sup_{\rho(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\leq u_{n}}\left|\big\langle X,\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta)-\theta\big\rangle\right|
and assume that
Z<u_{n}\bar{\tau}_{n}^{2}n^{2}
. Then by (
C.20
), for
u_{n}
as chosen in Lemma
C.7
, it holds

\sup_{\rho(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\leq u_{n}}|\ell_{n}\big(\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta)\big)-\ell_{n}\big(\theta\big)|=O(1).

On the other hand, again similarly to the proof of Lemma
C.7
, for sufficiently large
n
, it holds

\displaystyle P^{n}_{\theta_{0}}\big(Z(X)>u_{n}\bar{\tau}_{n}^{2}n^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=2\bigg(1-\Phi\Big(\frac{u_{n}\bar{\tau}_{n}^{2}n^{5/2}}{\sup_{\rho(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\leq u_{n}}\left\|\psi_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}(\theta)-\theta\right\|_{2}}\Big)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq 2\big(1-\Phi(\bar{\tau}_{n}n^{3/2})\big)\leq e^{-\frac{n^{3}\bar{\tau}_{n}^{2}}{2}}=o(e^{-n\varepsilon^{2}_{n}(\lambda)}),

where
\Phi
denotes the cumulative distribution function of the standard normal distribution, and we have used
[
26
, Lemma K.6]
to bound
1-\Phi
. Combining we get that (
C.21
) holds for any
c_{3}>0
.

For (
C.22
), let
K(\theta_{0},\theta)=\frac{1}{2}n\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}^{2}
and note that this quantity coincides with the Kullback-Leibler divergence
{\rm KL}(P^{n}_{\theta_{0}};P^{n}_{\theta})=E^{n}_{\theta_{0}}[\ell_{n}(\theta_{0})-\ell_{n}(\theta)]
, see for example
[
26
, Lemma 8.30]
. Using the (exponential) Markov inequality, we then have

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\in\Theta_{n}(\and)\\
\theta:\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}\leq K\varepsilon_{n}(\lambda)\end{subarray}}P^{n}_{\theta_{0}}\Big\{\ell_{n}(\theta)-\ell_{n}(\theta_{0})\leq-\frac{c_{3}}{2}n\varepsilon_{n}^{2}(\lambda)\Big\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\in\Theta_{n}(\and)\\
\theta:\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}\leq K\varepsilon_{n}(\lambda)\end{subarray}}P^{n}_{\theta_{0}}\Big\{\ell_{n}(\theta)-\ell_{n}(\theta_{0})+K(\theta_{0},\theta)\leq K(\theta_{0},\theta)-\frac{c_{3}}{2}n\varepsilon_{n}^{2}(\lambda)\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\in\Theta_{n}(\and)\\
\theta:\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}\leq K\varepsilon_{n}(\lambda)\end{subarray}}P^{n}_{\theta_{0}}\Big\{\ell_{n}(\theta)-\ell_{n}(\theta_{0})-E^{n}_{\theta_{0}}[\ell_{n}(\theta)-\ell_{n}(\theta_{0})]\leq\frac{K^{2}-c_{3}}{2}n\varepsilon_{n}^{2}(\lambda)\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\in\Theta_{n}(\and)\\
\theta:\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}\leq K\varepsilon_{n}(\lambda)\end{subarray}}P^{n}_{\theta_{0}}\Bigg\{\exp\Big(\ell_{n}(\theta_{0})-\ell_{n}(\theta)-E^{n}_{\theta_{0}}[\ell_{n}(\theta_{0})-\ell_{n}(\theta)]\Big)\geq e^{\frac{c_{3}-K^{2}}{2}n\varepsilon_{n}^{2}(\lambda)}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\in\Theta_{n}(\and)\\
\theta:\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}\leq K\varepsilon_{n}(\lambda)\end{subarray}}E^{n}_{\theta_{0}}\Bigg\{\exp\Big(\ell_{n}(\theta_{0})-\ell_{n}(\theta)-E^{n}_{\theta_{0}}[\ell_{n}(\theta_{0})-\ell_{n}(\theta)]\Big)\Bigg\}e^{\frac{K^{2}-c_{3}}{2}n\varepsilon_{n}^{2}(\lambda)}.

Since for
W\sim P^{(1)}_{0}
, it holds that
\big\langle W,\theta_{0}-\theta\big\rangle
is a univariate normal random variable with mean zero and variance
\left\|\theta_{0}-\theta\right\|_{2}^{2}
, we obtain that the last expectation is equal to

e^{-\frac{n}{2}\left\|\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{n}{2}\left\|\theta\right\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{n}{2}\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}^{2}}E^{n}_{\theta_{0}}\Big[e^{n\big\langle{\mathbf{x}},\theta_{0}-\theta\big\rangle}\Big]=e^{\frac{n}{2}\left\|\theta-\theta_{0}\right\|_{2}^{2}}.

Choosing
c_{3}\geq 2+2K^{2}
, we thus verify (
C.22
) and the proof is complete.
∎

,

, respectively.

Lemma C.9
.

Consider an
\alpha
-regular
\tau
-scaled

-exponential prior, for fixed

\tilde{\pi}

, satisfying Assumption

(i) for some
{r_{0}}>0

For
\bar{\epsilon}_{n}=\bar{\epsilon}_{n}(\alpha,\beta,p)
as defined in Lemma

, any
\bar{c}_{0}>0

tending to infinity arbitrarily slowly, let

Then Assumption (H1) from Subsection
3.1
is verified for these
\Lambda_{n},\bar{c}_{0}
and for
\tilde{w}_{n}=\tilde{M}_{n}\bar{\epsilon}_{n}/\epsilon_{n,0}
.

The proof proceeds similarly to the proof of
[
41

, with the difference that, due to the lack of good lower bounds for
\epsilon_{n}(\lambda)
, here we work with the upper bounds obtained in Lemma
3.4
rather than the oracle rate
\epsilon_{n,0}
defined in (

We first explicitly identify
\tilde{\Lambda}_{0}\subseteq\Lambda_{0}(\tilde{w}_{n})=\{\tau\in\Lambda_{n}:\epsilon_{n}(\alpha,\tau)\leq\tilde{M}_{n}\bar{\epsilon}_{n}\}
, which has enough mass under the hyper-prior
\tilde{\pi}(\tau)
, so that (
3.10
) is satisfied. To this end, we consider the value of
\tau
, denoted by
\tau_{0}
, which balances the two terms in the upper bounds on
\epsilon_{n}(\and)
found in part (i) of Lemma

. Clearly, for sufficiently large

\tau_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}(\tilde{w}_{n})
. We will show that
\tilde{\Lambda}_{0}
can be chosen as the interval
\big[\tau_{0},2\tau_{0}\big]

D
The constant in the centered small ball probabilities

Lemma D.1
.

Fix
\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}>0
and consider all
\alpha
-regular
p
-exponential measures
\Pi_{\alpha}\coloneqq\Pi(\cdot\mid\alpha,\tau=1)
, with
\alpha\geq\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}
. Recall the concentration function
\varphi_{\theta}(\cdot)
from (
1.6
) and consider

\varphi_{0}(\epsilon;\alpha)\coloneqq-\log\Pi_{\alpha}(\epsilon B_{\ell_{2}}).

There exists a constant
\tilde{c}>1
, such that

\tilde{c}^{-1}\alpha\leq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\epsilon^{1/\alpha}\varphi_{0}(\epsilon;\alpha)\leq\tilde{c}\alpha,\quad\forall\alpha\geq\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}.

(D.1)

In particular, for any fixed
\bar{\alpha}>\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}
and any
\epsilon_{0}>0
, there exist constants
\tilde{c}_{1}>0
, such that

\tilde{c}_{1}^{-1}\epsilon^{-1/\alpha}\leq\varphi_{0}(\epsilon;\alpha)\leq\tilde{c}_{1}\epsilon^{-1/\alpha},\quad\forall\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0}],\quad\forall\alpha\in[\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}].

(D.2)

By
[
9
, Theorem 4.2]
(where the small ball probabilities in
\ell_{2}
for
\alpha
-regular
p
-exponential priors correspond to powers
p=2
and
\mu=1/2+\alpha
in that source’s notation), we have that

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\epsilon^{1/\alpha}\varphi_{0}(\epsilon;\alpha)=C(\alpha),

where

C(\alpha)\coloneq\alpha\bigg(\frac{K(\alpha)^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{2}(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}}\bigg)^{1/\alpha},

(D.3)

and

K(\alpha)\coloneqq-\int_{0}^{\infty}\log\mathbb{E}\big[e^{-z^{-1-2\alpha}\xi^{2}}\big]dz.

Here
\xi
is a real valued
p
-exponentially distributed random variable, with probability density function given by
f_{p}(x)=\frac{1}{c_{p}}\exp(-{|x|^{p}}/p),
where
c_{p}=2\Gamma(1/p)p^{1/p-1}
.

Using the bound

\exp(-|x|^{p}/p)\geq c\exp(-x^{2}/p),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R},

(D.4)

where
c
is a sufficiently small positive constant, we can show that there exists another constant
c>0
, such that

\mathbb{E}\big[e^{-z^{-1-2\alpha}\xi^{2}}\big]=\frac{1}{c_{p}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-z^{-1-2\alpha}x^{2}-\frac{|x|^{p}}{p}}dx\geq c\Big(\frac{1}{p}+z^{-1-2\alpha}\Big)^{-1/2}.

(D.5)

On the other hand, we have

\mathbb{E}\big[e^{-z^{-1-2\alpha}\xi^{2}}\big]\leq\min\bigg\{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-z^{-1-2\alpha}x^{2}}}{c_{p}}dx,\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\frac{|x|^{p}}{p}}}{c_{p}}dx\bigg\}=\min\Big\{\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{c_{p}}z^{\frac{1}{2}+\alpha},1\Big\}\leq\min\big\{z^{\frac{1}{2}+\alpha},1\big\}.

(D.6)

Note that

-\int_{1}^{\infty}\log\mathbb{E}\big[e^{-z^{-1-2\alpha}\xi^{2}}\big]dz\leq K(\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha})<\infty,\quad\forall\alpha\geq\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}.

Combined with (
D.5
), this gives

K(\alpha)\leq-\int_{0}^{1}\log\mathbb{E}\big[e^{-z^{-1-2\alpha}\xi^{2}}\big]dz+K(\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha})\leq-\int_{0}^{1}\log\bigg(c\Big(\frac{1}{p}+z^{-1-2\alpha}\Big)^{-1/2}\bigg)dz+K(\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}),

and it is straightforward to check that there exists another constant
c>1/2
depending on
p
and
\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}
but not on
\alpha
, such that

K(\alpha)\leq c+\alpha.

(D.7)

Here, it is possible to take
c>1/2
, due to the fact that in (
D.4
) the constant
c
can be taken as small as we wish.
Moreover, using the upper bound (
D.6
), it is straightforward to show that

K(\alpha)\geq-\int_{0}^{\infty}\log\Big(\min\big\{z^{1/2+\alpha},1\big\}\Big)dz\geq-\int_{0}^{1}\log\big(z^{1/2+\alpha}\big)dz=1/2+\alpha.

By the above bounds on
K(\alpha)
, we thus have

2^{-1/2\alpha}\leq\frac{C(\alpha)}{\alpha}\leq\bigg(\frac{(\alpha+c)^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{2}(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}}\bigg)^{1/\alpha},

and the proof of (
D.1
) is complete since both the upper and lower bounds of
C(\alpha)/\alpha
are continuous over the range of
\alpha
and tend to 1 as
\alpha\to\infty
.

In order to prove (
D.2
), we delve into the theory developed in
[
9
]
(see also the PhD thesis
[
8
]
), which lead to
[
9
, Theorem 4.2]
which we quoted in the proof of the first claim, and we extract the desired nonasymptotic bound with constants which are uniform for
\alpha\in[\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}]
.

Let
\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots
be independent and identically distributed real valued
p
-exponential random variables, and denote
\mu=1/2+\alpha
. Recall that we are interested in studying the probability

\Pi_{\alpha}(\epsilon B_{\ell_{2}})\equiv\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell^{-1-2\alpha}\xi_{\ell}^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2}\Big)\equiv\mathbb{P}\Big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell^{-2\mu}\xi_{\ell}^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2}\Big).

By
[
9
, Theorem 2.2]
, applied with
S(x)=x^{-\mu}
so that
S^{-1}(x)=x^{-1/\mu}
, we have for all
\lambda>0

\log\mathbb{E}e^{-\lambda\xi^{2}}+J_{\mu}\leq\log\mathbb{E}e^{-\lambda\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell^{-2\mu}\xi_{\ell}^{2}}\leq J_{\mu},

(D.8)

where

J_{\mu}\coloneqq-\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\lambda}}\log\mathbb{E}e^{-y^{2}\xi^{2}}\frac{d}{dy}\big[S^{-1}\big(y\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)\big]dy=\lambda^{\frac{1}{2\mu}}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\lambda}}\log\mathbb{E}e^{-y^{2}\xi^{2}}\frac{1}{\mu}y^{-\frac{1}{\mu}-1}dy.

A change of variables then shows that
J_{\mu}=\lambda^{\frac{1}{2\mu}}I_{\mu},

where

I_{\mu}=I_{\mu}(\lambda)\coloneqq\int_{\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2\mu}}}^{\infty}\log\mathbb{E}e^{-z^{-2\mu}\xi^{2}}dz.

We bound
I_{\mu}
from above and below, uniformly for all
\mu\in[\underaccent{\bar}{\mu},\bar{\mu}]
, where
\underaccent{\bar}{\mu}\coloneqq 1/2+\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\mu}\coloneqq 1/2+\bar{\alpha}.

Noting that the integrand is negative, by (
D.7
) we have that

I_{\mu}\geq-K(\alpha)\geq-c-\alpha,\quad\forall\lambda>0,

where
c
depends on
\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}
and
p
, but not on
\alpha
. As a result there is a finite constant
R_{1}=R_{1}(p,\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha})>0
independent of
\alpha
, such that

I_{\mu}\geq-R_{1},\quad\forall\lambda>0,\quad\forall\alpha\in[\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}].

Furthermore, noting that for any
\lambda\geq 1

I_{\mu}\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\log\mathbb{E}e^{-z^{-2\mu}\xi^{2}}dz,

and bounding
z^{-2\mu}
from below by
z^{-2\bar{\mu}}
, it is straightforward to show that there exists a finite constant
R_{2}=R_{2}(p,\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha})>0
independent of
\alpha
, such that

I_{\mu}\leq-R_{2},\quad\forall\lambda\geq 1,\quad\forall\alpha\in[\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}].

We next turn our attention to the term
\log\mathbb{E}e^{-\lambda\xi^{2}}
appearing in (
D.8
). Observe that for all
p\in[1,2]
,
\alpha
-regular
p
-exponential priors satisfy
[
9
, Condition (O)]
for any
r>0
, that is there exists
C_{1}=C_{1}(r)>0
such that

\mathbb{P}(|\xi|\leq r)\geq e^{-C_{1}t^{-1/r}},\quad\forall 0<t\leq 1.

Indeed, by
[
8
, Equation (2.21)]
, it suffices to verify the existence of
\delta,C>0
such that

\mathbb{P}(|\xi|\leq t)\geq Ct^{\delta},\quad\forall 0<t\leq 1.

(D.9)

Since for any
t\in(0,1]
, for all
p\in[1,2]
we have

\mathbb{P}(|\xi|\leq t)=\frac{2}{c_{p}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{x^{p}}{p}}dx\geq\frac{2}{c_{p}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-x}dx=\frac{2}{c_{p}}(1-e^{-t}),

it is straightforward to check the validity of (
D.9
) (for example using L’Hôpital’s rule). By
[
9
, Equation (4.5)]
and Condition (O) with a fixed

, we have

\log\mathbb{E}e^{-\lambda\xi^{2}}\geq-1+\log\mathbb{P}(|\xi|\leq\lambda^{-1/2})\geq-1-C_{1}(r)\lambda^{\frac{1}{2r}}=\lambda^{\frac{1}{2\mu}}\big(-\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2\mu}}-C_{1}(r)\lambda^{\frac{1}{2r}-\frac{1}{2\mu}}\big),\quad\forall\lambda>0.

Combining the above considerations, we get that there exist finite constants, also denoted by
R_{1},R_{2}
and which are independent of
\alpha
, such that for
\mu=1/2+\alpha

-\lambda^{\frac{1}{2\mu}}R_{1}\leq\log\mathbb{E}e^{-\lambda\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell^{-2\mu}\xi_{\ell}^{2}}\leq-\lambda^{\frac{1}{2\mu}}R_{2},\quad\forall\lambda\geq 1,\quad\forall\alpha\in[\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}].

(D.10)

To complete the proof, we exploit the relationship between the Laplace transform
\log\mathbb{E}e^{-\lambda V}
and the small ball probabilities
\mathbb{P}(V\leq\epsilon)
, as presented in the proof of
[
8
, Lemma 1.5]
. In particular, by
[
8
, Equations (1.3) and (1.4)]
, for all
\epsilon,\lambda>0
we have

\log\mathbb{P}(V\leq\epsilon)\leq\lambda\epsilon+\log\mathbb{E}e^{-\lambda V}

(D.11)

and

\mathbb{E}e^{-\lambda V}\leq e^{-\lambda\epsilon}+(1-e^{-\lambda\epsilon})\mathbb{P}(V\leq\epsilon).

(D.12)

We will use these bounds with

V\coloneqq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell^{-1-2\alpha}\xi_{\ell}^{2}
.

Let
\gamma\coloneqq 1/2\mu=1/(1+2\alpha)
. On the one hand, by (
D.10
), we have
\log\mathbb{E}e^{-\lambda V}\geq-R_{1}\lambda^{\gamma}
for all
\lambda\geq 1
, hence using (
D.12
) with
\lambda=K_{1}\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{1-\gamma}}
for
K_{1}\geq 1
to be chosen below, we get

\mathbb{P}(V\leq\epsilon)\geq\frac{e^{-R_{1}K_{1}^{\gamma}\epsilon^{-\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}}}-e^{-K_{1}\epsilon^{-\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}}}}{1-e^{-K_{1}\epsilon^{-\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}}}}=\frac{e^{-R_{1}K_{1}^{\gamma}\epsilon^{-\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}}}\Big(1-e^{(R_{1}K_{1}^{\gamma}-K_{1})\epsilon^{-\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}}}\Big)}{1-e^{-K_{1}\epsilon^{-\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}}}}.

Choosing
K_{1}>1\vee R_{1}^{\frac{1+2\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}}{2\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}}}
, we have that
R_{1}K_{1}^{\gamma}-K_{1}<0
for all
\alpha\in[\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}]
, so that for
\epsilon\in(0,1]
the parenthesis term in the right hand side above is lower bounded by a constant
0<C^{\prime\prime}<1
. We hence obtain

\mathbb{P}(V\leq\epsilon)\geq C^{\prime\prime}e^{-K_{1}\epsilon^{-\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}}}=C^{\prime\prime}e^{-K_{1}\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2\alpha}}}\geq e^{-K_{1}^{\prime}\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2\alpha}}},\quad\forall\epsilon\in(0,1],\quad\forall\alpha\in[\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}],

where
K_{1}^{\prime}>0
is independent of
\alpha

and replacing
\epsilon
by
\epsilon^{2}
, we have

\log\mathbb{P}\big(V\leq\epsilon^{2}\big)\geq-K_{1}^{\prime}\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}},\quad\forall\epsilon\in(0,1],\quad\forall\alpha\in[\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}].

(D.13)

On the other hand, by (
D.10
), we have
\log\mathbb{E}e^{-\lambda V}\leq-R_{2}\lambda^{\gamma}
for all
\lambda\geq 1
, thus using (
D.11
) with
\lambda=K_{2}\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{1-\gamma}}
for
0<K_{2}\leq 1
to be chosen below and for
0<\epsilon\leq K_{2}^{\frac{2\bar{\alpha}}{1+2\bar{\alpha}}}
(so that
\lambda\geq 1
), we get

\log\mathbb{P}(V\leq\epsilon)\leq-\epsilon^{-\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}}(R_{2}K_{2}^{\gamma}-K_{2}).

Choosing
K_{2}\leq 1\wedge R_{2}^{\frac{1+2\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}}{2\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha}}}
, we have that
R_{2}K_{2}^{\gamma}-K_{2}=C^{\prime}>0
for all
\alpha\in[\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}]
and by replacing
\epsilon
by
\epsilon^{2}
, we therefore obtain

\log\mathbb{P}\big(V\leq\epsilon^{2}\big)\leq-C^{\prime}\epsilon^{-\frac{2\gamma}{1-\gamma}}=-C^{\prime}\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}},\quad\forall 0<\epsilon\leq K_{2}^{\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{1+2\bar{\alpha}}},\quad\forall\alpha\in[\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}],

(D.14)

where
C^{\prime}>0
is independent of
\alpha
.

Combined, (
D.14
) and (
D.13
) verify (
D.2
) for
\epsilon\in\big(0,K_{2}^{\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{1+2\bar{\alpha}}}\big].
Let
\epsilon_{0}>0
. Due to the positivity and the joint continuity of
\epsilon^{1/\alpha}\varphi_{0}(\epsilon;\alpha)
for
(\epsilon,\alpha)\in\big[K_{2}^{\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{1+2\bar{\alpha}}},\epsilon_{0}\big]\times[\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}]
(which follows from the continuity of
\varphi_{0}
separately with respect to
\epsilon
and
\alpha
and its monotonicity properties, see
[
34
]
), there exist constants
B,D>0
such that

B\leq-\epsilon^{1/\alpha}\log\mathbb{P}\big(V\leq\epsilon^{2}\big)\leq D,\quad\forall\epsilon\in\big[K_{2}^{\frac{\bar{\alpha}}{1+2\bar{\alpha}}},\epsilon_{0}\big],\quad\forall[\underaccent{\bar}{\alpha},\bar{\alpha}].

The validity of (
D.2
) is thus verified and the proof is complete.
∎

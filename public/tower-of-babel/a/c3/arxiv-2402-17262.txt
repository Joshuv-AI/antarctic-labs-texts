Speak Out of Turn: Safety Vulnerability of Large Language Models in Multi-turn Dialogue
Zhenhong Zhou, Jiuyang Xiang, Haopeng Chen, Zherui Li, Ting Yang, Quan Liu, Sen Su

Abstract
Large Language Models (LLMs) have been demonstrated to generate illegal or unethical responses, particularly when subjected to ‘jailbreak.’ Research on jailbreak has highlighted the safety issues of LLMs. However, prior studies have predominantly focused on single-turn dialogue, ignoring the potential complexities and risks presented by multi-turn dialogue, a crucial mode through which humans derive information from LLMs. In this paper, we argue that humans could exploit multi-turn dialogue to induce LLMs into generating harmful information. LLMs may not intend to reject cautionary or borderline unsafe queries, even if each turn is closely aligned with one malicious purpose in a multi-turn dialogue. Therefore, by decomposing an unsafe query into several sub-queries for multi-turn dialogue, we induced LLMs to answer harmful sub-questions incrementally, culminating in an overall harmful response. Our experiments, conducted across a wide range of LLMs, indicate current inadequacies in the safety mechanisms of LLMs in multi-turn dialogue. Our findings expose vulnerabilities of LLMs in complex scenarios involving multi-turn dialogue, presenting new challenges for the safety of LLMs.

Furthermore, in multi-turn dialogue, switching topics may also intensify the conflict between harmlessness and helpfulness. Based on these insights, language models can incrementally generate harmful content through multi-turn dialogue. This is analogous to how each interaction in a helpful multi-turn dialogue might not be comprehensive, yet the combined dialogue is
Radhakrishnan et al. 2023
. Therefore, as shown in Figure
1
, harmful questions can be decomposed into several unrelated sub-questions, making each interaction implicitly generate harmful content. Finally, the multi-turn dialogue builds up a comprehensive harmful response.

In this paper, we highlight overlooked safety vulnerabilities within language models during multi-turn dialogue. We introduce a baseline paradigm for decomposing malicious queries to jailbreak LLMs, starting from decomposing a single malicious question into several less harmful and loosely related sub-questions. Subsequently, it induces the language models to produce cautionary or a little harmful response over multi-turn dialogue and ends by either inverting or combining the prior responses. This method only leverages responses from multi-turn dialogue to obtain harmful outputs without requiring training, making it cost-effective. Anyone could extract harmful knowledge from LLMs efficiently through multi-turn dialogue.
We conduct comprehensive experiments across various aligned commercial LLMs, such as ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini. The results indicate that current LLMs possess safety vulnerabilities in multi-turn dialogue that malicious users can exploit. Based on these observations, we conduct an extensive empirical analysis and proposed several potential mitigation strategies. We believe that LLMs require consideration of safety within multi-turn dialogue specifically and the timely implementation of mitigation measures.

Safety Vulnerability of LLMs in Multi-Turn Dialogue

Jailbroken LLMs often generate harmful responses in single turn interactions, even though some studies
Li et al. 2023a
have been conducted within the context of multi-turn dialogues. However, like helpful interactions, incrementally addressing a complex problem often yields better results than resolving it in one attempt. As illustrated in Figure
2
, decomposing a harmful query into multiple sub-queries and allowing the model to address them incrementally over multiple turns can similarly result in a harmful multi-turn dialogue. In this section, we will detail a paradigm for decomposing malicious queries in multi-turn dialogue and use our proposed method to expose safety risks.

Figure 3:
Malicious Query Decomposition Paradigm. Four main instructions guide manual decomposition, while automatic decomposition relies on GPT-4, utilizing several manually decomposed examples as a few-shot demonstration and the requirements for prompt transformation as the Transfer Prompt.

3.1
Malicious Query Decomposition

Red-teaming
Perez et al. 2022
;
Ganguli et al. 2022
and alignment
Bai et al. 2022a
ensure that LLMs do not directly respond to overtly malicious queries, instead rejecting them. Notably, the threshold for rejections is quite subtle, and sometimes, using euphemistic phrasing or positive wording can achieve approximate objectives
Chao et al. 2023
. However, decomposing malicious questions into low-level sub-questions makes it more challenging for LLMs to discern the true intent compared to reframing them in a single turn, thereby leading to the models responding to malicious sub-queries.

To achieve approximate sub-objectives, we introduce Malicious Query Decomposition to conceal the harmful intent within a malicious query, thereby crafting single-turn queries. These queries are designed to avoid rejection by the model and collectively form a set of sub-queries for a harmful multi-turn dialogue. Our methodology encompasses several critical strategies for query manipulation, as illustrated in Figure
3
:

•

Purpose Inversion
: Transforming the query’s intent to its opposite to mitigate direct harmfulness.

•

Keyword Replacement
: Substituting malicious keywords with neutral or positive ones to disguise the query’s harmful nature.

•

Cautionary Orientation
: Shifting the query towards a cautionary approach, prompting awareness and prevention.

•

Reframing Sentence
: Modifying the sentence structure and phrasing of the query to shift its emphasis towards a less harmful or more constructive narrative.

Following the guidelines provided above, we manually decompose malicious queries. As demonstrated in Figure
2
, the model follows our instructions on superficially unrelated sub-topics and respond to these harmless queries. This process is carried out over multiple turns of dialogue, enabling the acquisition of substantial background knowledge and opposing viewpoints on the malicious.

Considering the output quality of LLMs depends on the prompt
Wei et al. 2022
, and manual decomposition of prompts significantly relies on expertise. We explore using LLMs to generate decomposed sub-query prompts, a strategy proven to be a reliable solution
Zhang et al. 2022
. Automating the decomposition of malicious queries using LLMs is a low-barrier approach that enables the rapid mass production of such query sets. As shown in the right half of Figure
3
, we provide LLMs with a set of successful decomposition examples as a few-shot demonstration, followed by a detailed transfer instruction in the transfer prompt. For ethical considerations, we will detail this part in Appendix
B
without disclosing the specific prompts.

3.2
Safety Vulnerability in Multi-Turn Dialogue

Following the malicious query decomposition method presented in Section
3.1
, we obtain a set of sub-questions from a single harmful question. These questions are mildly harmful and can elicit sufficient preliminary knowledge. Leveraging this context, we then invert or combine the generations from LLMs to unlock the hidden harmful knowledge within them. Using the dotted line in Figure
2
as the threshold for the model’s rejection of harmful queries, we demonstrate how, in the final turn of the dialogue, the model gathers harmful knowledge and generate answer near this threshold. It is important to clarify that this dotted line serves more as an illustrative guide rather than a precise and rigid criterion for LLMs to determine harmful queries. Attributed to the safety alignment, this answer, while harmful, may sometimes lack details or be ambiguous in key parts.

Shifting the perspective back to the multi-turn dialogue, the process organically builds up a harmful generation. As illustrated in Figure
2
, the interactions between the model and the sub-questions generate substantial background knowledge or positive cautions. Due to the In-Context Learning (ICL) capabilities
Wei et al. 2023
;
Dong et al. 2022
of LLMs, harmful knowledge can be gathered in the final turn. Furthermore, the model will provide at least an action outline, or even detailed instructions and accurate answers. From a high-level perspective of the multi-turn dialogue, the malicious query is addressed as much as possible.

Alignment is quite successful for each turn in a multi-turn dialogue, except for the final turn, where harmful knowledge is aggregated. However, the cumulative harmful content across the dialogue results in an overall alignment failure. Each turn within the multi-turn dialogue forms a part of the entire malicious content. In each individual turn, the harmful content might not be sufficient to cause significant damage. This introduces a new challenge for the safety of LLMs: vulnerability in multi-turn dialogue. Although LLMs have robust safety measures in single-turn dialogue, they often fail in multi-turn scenarios, especially when there are intent shifts and instruction following. Language models may lack an comprehensive understanding of the multi-turn context and the potential malicious intents embedded within prompts.

4.3.2
Role-Play Jailbreak

Role-playing is a common technique in prompt engineering, where LLMs are guided into specific roles to follow user instructions better. Role-playing such as ‘Developer Mode’ and ‘Grandma Exploit’ are frequently employed in crafting jailbreak prompts. Inspired by this concept, we introduce role-playing in the final turn of multi-turn dialogue to further test the safety of multi-turn dialogue. As indicated by the results marked with ‘Role Play’ in Table
1
, compared to the baseline method without role-playing, the proportion of harmful multi-turn dialogues increased. As shown in Figure
5
, the average harmfulness scores for all models also increased compared to the baseline.

Analyzing the experimental results, we find that role-playing actually makes the model less likely to reject to answer in the final turn while increasing the quality of harmful generations in multi-turn dialogue. Through multi-turn dialogue, role-playing could elicit harmful content, even though it alone can not jailbreak these models. The experimental results suggest that jailbreak methods applicable to single-turn dialogue might be extended to multi-turn dialogue. This further indicates that LLMs exhibit mismatched generalization in multi-turn dialogue, posing safety risks.

4.3.3
Multi-turn Ablation

To confirm the safety vulnerabilities in multi-turn dialogue leading to harmful generations, we design an ablation experiment to isolate the contribution of decomposed prompt engineering. We combine the sub-queries into one turn and conduct baseline generation experiments.

As shown in Table
3
, decomposing malicious objectives indeed causes the model to respond to a portion of the questions. However, GPT-4’s judgments and average scores for harmfulness significantly decreased in a single-turn dialogue. This indicates that the safety vulnerabilities in multi-turn dialogues are real and not solely attributable to decomposed prompt engineering. Furthermore, we believe that the instruction transfer across different turns in multi-turn dialogues naturally leverages the competitive objectives of helpfulness and harmlessness, akin to jailbreak.

The ablation experiment results in Section

5
Discussion and Mitigation

Based on our experimental findings, this section discusses why current LLMs generally exhibit safety vulnerabilities in multi-turn dialogue. From our analysis, we propose several potential methods to enhance the safety of language models in multi-turn dialogue.

5.1
Why Aligned LLMs is Vulnerable in Multi-Turn Dialogue

5.1.1
Well-designed Prompt Engineering for Jailbreak

After eliminating the impact of the multi-turn dialogue format on generating harmful content, the ablation experiment results in Table
3
show that the multi-turn dialogue format can produce more harmful content. To further determine the extent to which the decomposed prompt engineering method aids in jailbreaking, we evaluate direct queries to models using AdvBench with GPT-4. As shown in Table
4
, essentially, no model responded to these queries. This demonstrates that the proposed decomposed paradigm plays a significant role in eliciting harmful multi-turn dialogues.

Since alignment does not cause models to forget harmful knowledge but merely chooses to reject answering. Consequently, this systematic decomposed prompt engineering, in the form of contrary or background knowledge, implicitly leverages the harmful knowledge within LLMs. Furthermore, decomposition methods are designed for multi-turn dialogue, making them highly compatible. The multi-turn dialogue format provides more information than single-turn dialogue, while harmful knowledge in multiple turns complements each other, further increasing safety risks.

5.1.2
Competing Objectives and Mismatched Generalization

Competing objectives and mismatched generalization are the main reasons language models are susceptible to jailbreak
Wei et al. 2024
. Competing objectives refer to the language models tasked with being helpful and harmless, which become competitive when facing harmful queries. Previous jailbreak studies often exploit this by prompting models to start responses to malicious questions with affirmative words like ‘Sure’ or ‘Of course.’ This paper, however, leverages a different aspect of helpfulness: the instruction-following capability in multi-turn dialogue. Language models are asked to follow the user’s prompts across different turns. They can be misled by seemingly unrelated malicious sub-questions, gradually leading to a comprehensive harmful dialogue. Since we leverage the helpfulness of instruct-following rather than affirmative initial responses, we believe that using prior jailbreak methods in multi-turn dialogue could further exacerbate the safety risks.

5.2
How to Mitigate Harmfulness in Multi-Turn Dialogue

We believe that it is feasible to conduct RLHF and SFT safety alignment based on multi-turn dialogue data, enabling models to learn to reject consistent harmful questions throughout the dialogue. However, in the early stages of our experiments, we attempted covert harmful queries reliant on prompt engineering.
Skilled malicious users can decompose queries into nearly unrelated sub-queries, which is quite challenging. Furthermore, models are also trained to maintain the ability to follow instructions across multiple dialogue turns. Nevertheless, humans can easily guess that two unrelated questions might jointly point to malicious intent, a capability that language models have been proven to lack.

Based on the discussion above, we argue that in addition to conducting safety alignment for multi-turn dialogue early in model training, it is also essential to enhance the model’s understanding of context further. A better semantic understanding of unrelated queries is necessary to mitigate the safety risks in multi-turn dialogue.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we highlight that current safety alignments of language models ignore multi-turn dialogue. While safety alignments for single-turn dialogue prevent the direct output of harmful content, simple prompt engineering can generate harmful dialogues. We present a proven feasible paradigm for generating multi-turn malicious sub-queries, which can be executed by humans or LLMs alike. Therefore, this safety vulnerability could be easily exploited on a large scale by malicious attackers. Comprehensive experiments are conducted on widely used commercial LLMs. Based on our experimental results, we analyze the causes of safety vulnerabilities in multi-turn dialogue and propose several strategies to mitigate these safety risks. Finally, we argue that this previously ignored scenario urgently requires dedicated alignment to prevent language models from producing illegal and unethical content, thereby avoiding adverse social impacts.

7
Limitations

In this paper, we focus on discussing the vulnerabilities of LLM multi-turn dialogues and demonstrate that such risks may exist.
However, we have yet to optimize the multi-turn dialogue jailbreak specifically.
We believe that a specially optimized multi-turn dialogues jailbreak attack could generate more harmful content.
In future work, we will try to complete a more detailed and automated jailbreak attack in a multi-turn dialogue scenario. This will further reveal the vulnerability of multi-turn dialogue.

Appendix B
How to Design Prompt for Decomposing Malicious Question

Manual decomposition performed well in most experiments, leading us to believe that manual decomposition can abstract effective decomposition features. Therefore, we selected input-output pairs from manually crafted prompts that were successful in jailbreaking as few-shot demonstrations. Considering the complexity of the transformation task, we designed a COT-based prompt to guide GPT-4 in completing the decomposition task.
Following the COT process, GPT-4 can transform harmful questions into groups of seemingly harmless sub-questions in bulk. However, these groups of sub-questions can build up into a harmful multi-turn dialogue.

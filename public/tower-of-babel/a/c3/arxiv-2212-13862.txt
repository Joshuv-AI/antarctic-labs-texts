On toric Fano fibrations
Florin Ambro

Abstract
. A. Borisov classified into finitely many series the set of isomorphism classes of germs of toric ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} -factorial singularities, of fixed dimension and with minimal log discrepancy over the special point bounded from below by a fixed real number. We extend this classification to germs of toric Fano fibrations, possibly not ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} -factorial. As an application, we verify in the toric setting a conjecture proposed by V. V. Shokurov on the existence of bounded complements.

1.
Introduction

The explicit classification of terminal
3
-fold singularities played an important role in the birational classification of projective
3
-folds. Based on earlier computer-generated attempts to explicitly classify toric terminal
4
-fold singularities, Borisov
[
5
]
obtained a qualitative classification of toric
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial singularities
P\in X
with
\dim X=d
and
\operatorname{mld}X\geq t
(i.e. log discrepancies of geometric valuations of
X
are at least
t
). The latter correspond to finite subgroups of the real torus
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
which intersect the set
\{x\in[0,1)^{d};\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}<t\}
only at the origin, and Borisov showed that they fall into
finitely many series
. Our motivation is to understand these series, find their geometric meaning, and possibly extend them to non-toric singularities. If the latter is indeed true, it could have important applications in the birational classification of projective varieties, since in the toric case the standard (local) conjectures of Shokurov on minimal log discrepancies do follow from the finiteness of series.

Borisov’s definition of series simplifies if the global condition
\operatorname{mld}X\geq t
is replaced by the local condition
\operatorname{mld}_{P}X\geq t
(i.e. log discrepancies of geometric valuations of
X
with center
P
are at least
t
).
By Lawrence
[
10
]
, the subgroups of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
which avoid
the image of the open set
\{x\in(0,1)^{d};\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}<t\}
have only finitely many maximal elements
G
with respect to inclusion (called
series
), and Borisov showed that a toric
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial affine variety
X
with (unique) invariant point
P
satisfies
\dim X=d
and
\operatorname{mld}_{P}X\geq t
if and only if it corresponds to a finite subgroup of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
contained in some
G
. We only consider such local series in this paper.

A first observation is that series of toric
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial singularities have a dual interpretation, which make sense even in the non-
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial case. We identify a series with a closed subgroup
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\leq G\leq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. A basis of the dual abelian group
G^{*}\subset({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})^{*}
defines a projection
\Phi\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
such that
G=\Phi^{-1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{p})
(i.e.
G=G^{**}
).
Denote
U=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{d};\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}\leq 1\}
. The condition

G\cap\operatorname{int}(tU)=\emptyset
is equivalent to

{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}\cap\operatorname{int}(t\Phi(U))=\emptyset
. The series
G
belongs to a finite family if and only if
t\Phi(U)
is contained in a bounded box.
Therefore Borisov’s existence of only finitely many local series can be restated in terms of projections as follows:
an affine toric
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial variety
P\in X=T_{N}\operatorname{emb}(\sigma)
with
\dim X=d
satisfies
\operatorname{mld}_{P}X\geq t
if and only if there exists a surjective homomorphism of lattices
\Phi\colon N\to N^{\prime}
such that

N^{\prime}\cap\operatorname{int}(t\Phi(U))=\emptyset
and the pair
(N^{\prime},t\Phi(U))
is bounded
(i.e. the convex set is contained in a bounded box after choosing some basis of the lattice). Here
U=\operatorname{Conv}(\{0\}\cup\sigma(1))
coincides with the polar set of the anti-canonical moment polytope
\square_{-K_{X}}\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}
, and is characterized in terms of valuations as follows: the primitive lattice points in the interior of
hU
are in one to one correspondence with toric valuations
E
of
X
with center
P
such that the log discrepancy
a_{E}(X)
is strictly less than
h
. This statement makes sense and holds even if
X
is not
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial.

We call
\Phi\colon(N,tU)\to(N^{\prime},t\Phi(U))
a
t
-lc reduction
of
P\in X
. Whereas
0
is an extremal point of
U
,
0
is only a boundary point of
\Phi(U)
. Therefore
(N^{\prime},t\Phi(U))
may not be associated to a toric germ, but rather to a germ of toric proper fibration
f^{\prime}\colon(X^{\prime},B^{\prime})\to Y^{\prime}\ni P^{\prime}

where
\dim X^{\prime}=\dim N^{\prime}
,
B^{\prime}
is an invariant boundary with standard coefficients,
Y^{\prime}
affine with invariant point
P^{\prime}
,
-K_{X^{\prime}}-B^{\prime}
is
f^{\prime}
-semiample
and
\operatorname{mld}_{{f^{\prime}}^{-1}P^{\prime}}(X^{\prime},B^{\prime})\geq t
. Moreover,
\Phi(U)=U^{\prime}
admits three equivalent definitions, same as
U
. We will show that
t
-lc reductions exist in this setting too. Our main result is

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
d\geq 1
,
t>0
and a DCC set
{\mathcal{B}}\subset[0,1]
. Let
f\colon X\to Y\ni P
be a toric fibration with affine base
Y
having positive dimension and invariant point
P
. Suppose
\dim X=d
. Let
\sum_{i}E_{i}
be the complement of the torus inside
X
, let
B=\sum_{i}b_{i}E_{i}
with
b_{i}\in{\mathcal{B}}
for all
i
. Write
X=T_{N}\operatorname{emb}(\Delta)
, let
\square=\square_{-K-B}\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}
be the moment polytope of the torus invariant
{\mathbb{R}}
-Weil divisor
-K-B
and
U=\square^{*}\subset N_{\mathbb{R}}
its polar set. The following are equivalent:

a)

There exists an open neighborhood
P\in V\subseteq Y
and a boundary
B^{+}_{f^{-1}V}\geq B|_{f^{-1}V}
on
f^{-1}V
such that

K_{f^{-1}V}+B^{+}_{f^{-1}V}\sim_{\mathbb{R}}0
and
\operatorname{mld}_{f^{-1}P}(f^{-1}V,B^{+}_{f^{-1}V})\geq t
.

b)

There exists a non-zero surjective homomorphism of lattices
\Phi\colon N\to N^{\prime}
such that the image
U^{\prime}=\Phi(U)\subset N^{\prime}_{\mathbb{R}}

is a compact polytope,
N^{\prime}\cap\operatorname{int}(tU^{\prime})=\emptyset
and
(N^{\prime},tU^{\prime})
is bounded.

A stronger statement holds in fact, where we only require that
\dim X
is fixed and the ratios
\frac{1-b_{i}}{t}
belong to a fixed ACC set (Theorem
3.7
).

Since
Y
is affine toric, a) holds if and only if it holds with
V=Y
. If some coefficient of
B
is
1
then
U
is not compact, hence the compact
condition in b) is not redundant. We say that
B
has
r
-hyperstandard coefficients
if they are of the form
1-\frac{x}{q}
, where
x\in\frac{1}{r}{\mathbb{Z}}\cap[0,1]
and
q\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
. If
B
has
r
-hyperstandard coefficients and
t
is fixed and rational, Theorem
1.1
can be deduced from Nill-Ziegler’s result
[
11
]
stating that polytopes in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with extremal points rational of bounded index and without interior lattice points are either
finite (up to lattice translations and change of basis of the lattice), or they project onto a smaller dimensional polytope with the same properties.
In general, Theorem
1.1
follows from a generalization of Nill-Ziegler’s theorem in our setting (Theorem
2.11
).

One application of Theorem
1.1
is the toric case of a conjecture of Shokurov:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a toric fibration with affine base
Y
, let
B
be an invariant boundary on
X
with
r
-hyperstandard coefficients, suppose

-K-B
is
f
-semiample. Let
t>0
.

a)

Suppose
Y
has an invariant closed point
P
and
\operatorname{mld}_{f^{-1}P}(X,B)\geq t
. Then there exists
B^{+}\geq B
such that
n(K+B^{+})\sim 0
and

\operatorname{mld}_{f^{-1}P}(X,B^{+})\geq t
, where
n
is a positive integer depending only on
\dim X,r,t
.

b)

Suppose
\operatorname{mld}(X,B)\geq t
and
t<1
. Then there exists
B^{+}\geq B
such that
n(K+B^{+})\sim 0
and

\operatorname{mld}(X,B^{+})\geq t
, where
n
is a positive integer depending only on
\dim X,r,t
.

If
f
is an isomorphism and
X
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial, Theorem
1.2
.b) was proved by Shokurov
[
13
]
, based on Borisov’s result. A slightly more general (but equivalent modulo toric MMP) statement is proved in Section 4.
We do not address here the more general conjecture on the existence of bounded
a-n-
complements for boundaries with arbitrary coefficients (see
[
14
, Conjecture 5 on page 212]
).

We outline the structure of this paper. In Section 2 we prove the main combinatorial result (Theorem
2.11
), a generalization of Nill-Ziegler’s theorem. The proof is similar but more direct, based on the reduced projections of Kannan-Lovasz and a generalization of a result of
Pikhurko (Theorem
2.8
). In Section 3 we translate the combinatorial result in the setting of germs of toric fibrations which admit complements with good singularities. We also investigate basic properties of the image of a
t
-lc reduction, including the existence of hyperplane sections on the base with good singularities (Theorem
3.12
). In Section 4 we assume the boundary has
r
-hyperstandard coefficients, and construct a complement with bounded index and good singularities. In Section 5 we specialize our main result to the case of germs of toric log singularities

P\in(X,B)
. We also show that if
\dim X
is fixed, if the coefficients of
B
and
\operatorname{mld}_{P}(X,B)
belong to a DCC set, then they are finite.

2.
Geometry of numbers

Preliminary

Let
V\simeq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a finite dimensional
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space. Let

V^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{R}}(V,{\mathbb{R}})
be the dual
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space, with induced duality pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon V^{*}\times V\to{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
\square\subset V
be a closed convex set which contains the origin. Its
polar set
is defined by

\square^{*}=\{v^{*}\in V^{*};\langle v^{*},v\rangle\geq-1\ \forall v\in\square\}.

It is again a closed convex set in
V^{*}
, which contains the origin. We have
\square=(\square^{*})^{*}
. Indeed, this follows from the duality theorem for closed convex cones applied to the cone in
V\times{\mathbb{R}}
generated by the set
\square\times 1
.

If
\square\subset V
is a closed convex set and
\varphi\in V^{*}
, we denote
h_{\square}(\varphi)=\inf\langle\varphi,\square\rangle\in{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{-\infty\}
, where

\langle\varphi,\square\rangle
is the image interval
\{\varphi(v);v\in\square\}
. If
\square
is polyhedral, the infimum is either
-\infty
or a minimum.

If
\square\subset V
is a closed convex set, the difference
\square-\square=\{x-y;x,y\in\square\}\subset V
is a closed convex set
symmetric about the origin, and
(\square-\square)^{*}
consists of the linear forms
\varphi\in V^{*}
such that the interval
\langle\varphi,\square\rangle\subset{\mathbb{R}}
has length at most
1
.

Suppose
\square\subset V
is a compact convex set of dimension
d
. For

P\in\square
, denote by
\gamma(P\in\square)
the maximal
t\geq 0

such that
P+t(\square-\square)\subseteq\square
. We have
0\leq\gamma(P\in\square)\leq\frac{1}{2}
, and the left hand side (resp. right hand side) inequality is attained only if
P\in\partial\square
(resp.
\square
is symmetric about
P
). We also have
\gamma(P\in\square)=\frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}
, where
\epsilon
is maximal with
the property
-\epsilon(\square-P)\subseteq\square-P
.

If
\square
is a simplex with vertices
v_{0},\ldots,v_{d}
, denoted

\square=[v_{0},\ldots,v_{d}]
, any point
P\in\square
admits a unique
representation
P=\sum_{i=0}^{d}t_{i}v_{i}
with
\min_{i}t_{i}\geq 0
and

\sum_{i=0}^{d}t_{i}=1
. Then
\gamma(P\in\square)=\min_{i=0}^{d}t_{i}
.
Moreover, we will denote
t_{i}
by
\gamma_{v_{i}}(P\in[v_{0},\ldots,v_{d}])
.

If
\square
is a polytope, we will denote by
\square(0)
the set of extremal points of
\square
, i.e. the finite set of its vertices.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
S=[v_{0},\ldots,v_{d}]
be a
d
-dimensional simplex in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Let
P\in\operatorname{int}S
and suppose
P\neq 0
. Let
\lambda>1
be maximal such that
\lambda P\in S
. Then
\lambda P
belongs to the relative interior of a proper face
[v_{i};i\in I]
of
S
, and
(v_{i})_{i\in I}
are linearly independent.

Proof.

Write
P=\sum_{i=0}^{d}t_{i}v_{i}
with
t_{i}>0
for all
i
and
\sum_{i=0}^{d}t_{i}=1
. Since
v_{1}-v_{0},\ldots,v_{d}-v_{0}
form a basis of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, there exists a unique solution for the system

\sum_{i=0}^{d}\alpha_{i}v_{i}=0
,
\sum_{i=0}^{d}\alpha_{i}=1
.
Moreover, any non-trivial relation among
v_{i}
is a multiple of

(\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
.
We have

tP=\sum_{i=0}^{d}(tt_{i}-(t-1)\alpha_{i})v_{i},\ \sum_{i=0}^{d}(tt_{i}-(t-1)\alpha_{i})=1.

Therefore
tP\in S
if and only if
tt_{i}-(t-1)\alpha_{i}\geq 0

for all
i
. If
t_{i}\geq\alpha_{i}
for all
i
, then
P=0
. Since we assumed otherwise,
t_{i}<\alpha_{i}
for some
i
. Therefore the maximal
\lambda
is well defined, and
\lambda-1=\min_{\alpha_{i}>t_{i}}\frac{t_{i}}{\alpha_{i}-t_{i}}
. We have
\lambda>1
,
\lambda P=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\lambda_{i}v_{i}
and
\lambda_{i}=\lambda t_{i}-(\lambda-1)\alpha_{i}
.
Say
\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{k}>0=\lambda_{>k}
. Here
0\leq k<d
.
We have
\lambda P\in\operatorname{relint}[v_{0},\ldots,v_{k}]
.
Suppose by contradiction that
v_{0},\ldots,v_{k}
are linearly dependent.
Then
\alpha_{>k}=0
. But
0=\lambda_{d}=\lambda t_{d}-(\lambda-1)\alpha_{d}
,
hence
\alpha_{d}>0
. Contradiction!
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\pi\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d^{\prime}}
be a surjective linear homomorphism. Let
\square\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a closed convex set of
dimension
d
, containing
0
in its interior. Then
\square^{\prime}:=\pi(\square)
is a closed convex set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d^{\prime}}
containing the origin in its interior. Let
V_{0}=\operatorname{Ker}\pi
and
\square_{0}:=\square\cap V_{0}
. Then
\square_{0}\subset V_{0}
is a closed convex set of dimension
d-d^{\prime}
, containing
0
in its interior. Denote
\gamma=\gamma(0\in\square)
,
\gamma^{\prime}=\gamma(0\in\square^{\prime})
and
\gamma_{0}=\gamma(0\in\square_{0})
. Then

\gamma_{0}\gamma^{\prime}\leq\gamma\leq\min(\gamma_{0},\gamma^{\prime}).

Proof.

The second inequality is easy. For the first inequality, choose
0\neq v\in\square
. Then
\pi(v)\in\square^{\prime}
.

Suppose
\pi(v)=0
. Then
{\mathbb{R}}v\cap\square={\mathbb{R}}v\cap\square_{0}
, hence
-\epsilon v\in\square
for some

\epsilon>0
with
\frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}\geq\gamma_{0}
.

Suppose
\pi(v)\neq 0
. Let
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
be maximal such that
-\epsilon^{\prime}\pi(v)\in\square^{\prime}
. We have
\frac{\epsilon^{\prime}}{1+\epsilon^{\prime}}\geq\gamma^{\prime}
. There exists
w\in\square
such that
\pi(w)=-\epsilon^{\prime}\pi(v)
. This means that

v_{0}:=\frac{1}{1+\epsilon^{\prime}}w+\frac{\epsilon^{\prime}}{1+\epsilon^{\prime}}v\in\square_{0}

If
v_{0}=0
, then
-\epsilon^{\prime}v=w\in\square
. Suppose
v_{0}\neq 0
.
Let
\epsilon_{0}>0
be maximal such that
-\epsilon_{0}v_{0}\in\square_{0}
.
We have
\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{1+\epsilon_{0}}\geq\gamma_{0}
. If we denote

\epsilon:=\frac{\epsilon^{\prime}\epsilon_{0}}{1+\epsilon^{\prime}+\epsilon_{0}},

the following identity holds:

-\epsilon v=\frac{\epsilon}{\epsilon^{\prime}}w+(1-\frac{\epsilon}{\epsilon^{\prime}})(-\epsilon_{0}v_{0}).

Since
w,-\epsilon_{0}v_{0}\in\square
, we deduce
-\epsilon v\in\square
. We compute

\frac{\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}=\frac{\epsilon^{\prime}}{1+\epsilon^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{\epsilon_{0}}{1+\epsilon_{0}}\geq\gamma^{\prime}\cdot\gamma_{0}.

∎

Remark 2.3
.

Both inequalities are attained. For the left hand side, let
T=[A,B,C]\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be a triangle containing
0
in its interior. Let
\pi\colon{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be the projection which maps
B
and
C
onto the same point
P
.
Let
P^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}
be the image of
A
. Let
0=(1-\lambda)P^{\prime}+\lambda P
.
The intersection of
T
with
\operatorname{Ker}\pi
is an interval
[B^{\prime},C^{\prime}]
.
Let
0=(1-\mu)B^{\prime}+\mu C^{\prime}
. Then
0\in T
has the following barycentric coordinates:

0=(1-\lambda)A+\lambda(1-\mu)B+\lambda\mu C.

Let
\Lambda\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
be a
d
-dimensional lattice. Let
\square\subset\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
be a compact convex set of dimension
d
. For
1\leq i\leq d
,
denote by
\lambda_{i}(\Lambda,\square-\square)
the
i
-th successive minimum of Minkowski, i.e. the smallest real number
t>0
such that
\Lambda\cap t(\square-\square)
contains
i
linearly independent elements.

If
\Lambda\cap\operatorname{int}\square\neq\emptyset
, we define the
Pikhurko constant
of
(\Lambda,\square)
as follows:

\gamma(\Lambda,\square):=\max_{P\in\Lambda\cap\operatorname{int}\square}\gamma(P\in\square).

Definition 2.4
.

We say that
(\Lambda,\square)
is
bounded
if one of the following equivalent conditions holds:

a)

\lambda_{1}(\Lambda,\square-\square)
is bounded away from zero.

b)

\lambda_{d}(\Lambda^{*},(\square-\square)^{*})
is bounded above.

c)

There exists an isomorphism
\Phi\colon\Lambda{\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}}{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}

such that
\Phi_{\mathbb{R}}(\square)\subseteq\prod_{i=1}^{d}[x_{i},x_{i}+l]
, where

x_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
l
is a positive number bounded above.

Properties a) and b) are equivalent due to the inequality

1\leq\lambda_{1}(\Lambda,\square-\square)\cdot\lambda_{d}(\Lambda^{*},(\square-\square)^{*})\leq d!,

which is one of the inequalities of Mahler’s Transference Theorem
[
6
, page 219, thm VI]
. If c) holds, let
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{d}
be the components of
\Phi
. Then
they form a basis of
\Lambda^{*}
and
\operatorname{length}\varphi_{i}(\square)\leq l
for all
i
. Therefore
\lambda_{d}(\Lambda^{*},(\square-\square)^{*})\leq l
.
Suppose now b) holds, i.e.
\lambda_{d}(\Lambda^{*},(\square-\square)^{*})\leq c
.
This means that there exist
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{d}\in\Lambda^{*}
,
linearly independent, such that
\operatorname{length}\varphi_{i}(\square)\leq c
for all
i
. By an argument of Mahler
[
7
, page 68]
,
\Lambda^{*}
admits a basis
\varphi^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\varphi^{\prime}_{d}
such that
\operatorname{length}\varphi^{\prime}_{i}(\square)\leq dc
for all
i
. Then
(\varphi^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\varphi^{\prime}_{d})\colon\Lambda{\overset{\sim}{\rightarrow}}{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
is an isomorphism
as in c) and the image of
\square
is contained in a product of intervals of length at most
dc
.

Note that in case c) we have
\sqrt[d]{\operatorname{vol}_{\Lambda}\square}\leq l
. By the above argument,
\sqrt[d]{\operatorname{vol}_{\Lambda}\square}\leq d\cdot\lambda_{d}(\Lambda^{*},(\square-\square)^{*})
.

Note that if
\square
contains
0
, then c) implies

\Phi_{\mathbb{R}}(\square)\subseteq[-l,l]^{d}
. If moreover
\square
is a polytope whose vertices are rational points of bounded index, then
\Phi_{\mathbb{R}}(\square)
belongs to a finite set of polytopes.

Central integer points of intervals

Let
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be a compact interval such that
{\mathbb{Z}}\cap\operatorname{int}I\neq\emptyset
. We compute and estimate the Pikhurko constant
\gamma:=\gamma({\mathbb{Z}},I)
.

a) Case of odd number of integer interior points:
{\mathbb{Z}}\cap\operatorname{int}I=\{z,z+1,\ldots,z+2k\}
for some integer
k\geq 0
. Then
I=[z-\alpha,z+2k+\beta]
for some

\alpha,\beta\in(0,1]
. After possibly replacing
I
by
-I
, we may suppose
\alpha\geq\beta
. Then

\gamma
is attained by
P=z+k
, equal to

\gamma=\frac{k+\beta}{2k+\alpha+\beta}.

Keeping the set of interior integer points fixed, this invariant is minimal when
\alpha=1
, hence

\gamma\geq\frac{k+\beta}{2k+1+\beta}.

b) Case of even number of integer interior points:
{\mathbb{Z}}\cap\operatorname{int}I=\{z,z+1,\ldots,z+2k+1\}
for some integer
k\geq 0
. Then
I=[z-\alpha,z+2k+1+\beta]
for some

\alpha,\beta\in(0,1]
. After possibly replacing
I
by
-I
, we may suppose
\alpha\geq\beta
. Then

\gamma
is attained by
P=z+k
, equal to

\gamma=\frac{k+\alpha}{2k+1+\alpha+\beta}.

Keeping the set of interior integer points fixed, this invariant is minimal when
\alpha=\beta
, hence

\gamma\geq\frac{k+\beta}{2k+1+2\beta}.

We deduce that
\gamma>\frac{k}{2k+1}
. Therefore
\gamma
approaches
\frac{1}{2}
from below as the length of
I
becomes arbitrarily large.

Lemma 2.5
.

(1-2\gamma)\cdot\operatorname{length}I\leq 1
.

Proof.

In case a),
(1-2\gamma)(2k+\alpha+\beta)=\alpha-\beta<1
.
In case b),
(1-2\gamma)(2k+1+\alpha+\beta)=1-\alpha+\beta\leq 1
.
∎

The inequality in Lemma
2.5
can be restated as

\lambda_{1}({\mathbb{Z}},I-I)+2\gamma({\mathbb{Z}},I)\geq 1
, since
\lambda_{1}({\mathbb{Z}},I-I)=(\operatorname{length}I)^{-1}
in this case.

Lemma 2.6

(Pikhurko)
.

Suppose
I(0)\subset\frac{1}{l}{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then
\gamma\geq\frac{1}{l+2}
, attained up to lattice translation only by the interval
I=[-\frac{1}{l},1+\frac{1}{l}]
and its two interior integer points
\{0,1\}
.

Proof.

Case a): May suppose
\alpha=1
. Then
\gamma\geq\frac{k+\beta}{2k+1+\beta}\geq\frac{kl+1}{(2k+1)l+1}
.
The smallest value is taken for
k=0
, equal to
\frac{1}{l+1}
.
Case b): May suppose

Theorem 2.8
.

Fix
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
and an ACC set
{\mathcal{A}}\subset(0,+\infty)
.
Let
\Lambda\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
be a lattice of dimension
d
, let
\square\subset\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
be a compact polytope of dimension
d
. Suppose

and every non-zero extremal point of
\square
is of the form

and
a_{e}\in{\mathcal{A}}
.
If
\Lambda\cap\operatorname{int}\square\neq\emptyset
,
then
\gamma(\Lambda,\square)
is bounded away from zero.

Lemma 2.9
.

In the setting of Theorem
2.8
, suppose moreover that
(\Lambda,\square)
is bounded.
Then
\min_{P\in\Lambda\cap\operatorname{int}\square}\gamma(P\in\square)
is bounded away from zero.

Proof.

Fix
P\in\Lambda\cap\operatorname{int}\square
and denote
\gamma=\gamma(P\in\square)
. There exists
v_{0}\in\square(0)
such that
P=\gamma v_{0}+(1-\gamma)v^{\prime}_{0}

and
v^{\prime}_{0}\in\partial\square
. Thus
v^{\prime}_{0}
belongs to a face
F
of
\square
of dimension at most
d-1
. By Caratheodory’s theorem, there exist
v_{1},\ldots,v_{p}\in F(0)\subset\square(0)
, for some
1\leq p\leq d
, forming the vertices of a
(p-1)
-dimensional simplex which contains
v^{\prime}_{0}
in its relative interior. Then
v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{p}
are the vertices of a
p
-dimensional simplex containing
P
in its relative interior, and

\gamma=\gamma_{v_{0}}(P\in[v_{0},\ldots,v_{p}])
. Thus we may write

P=\sum_{i=0}^{p}t_{i}v_{i}
with
\min_{i}t_{i}>0
,
\sum_{i}t_{i}=1
and
t_{0}=\gamma
.

Suppose
v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{p}
are linearly independent. That is,

0,v_{0},\ldots,v_{p}
are the vertices of a simplex of dimension
p+1
,

P
lies in the relative interior of the face opposite the origin, with

P=\sum_{i=0}^{p}t_{i}v_{i}
with
\min_{i}t_{i}>0
,
\sum_{i}t_{i}=1
and
t_{0}=\gamma
.
(Case 0)
.

Suppose from now
v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{p}
are linearly dependent.
As they are the vertices of a
p
-dimensional simplex, there exists a unique relation
0=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\alpha_{i}v_{i}
with
1=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\alpha_{i}
. Moreover, any non-trivial relation among the
v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{p}
is a non-zero multiple of
(\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{p})
.

We claim that
\alpha_{0}\geq 0
. Indeed, there exists
e\in\Lambda^{*}_{\mathbb{R}}\setminus 0

\min\langle\square,e\rangle=\langle v^{\prime}_{0},e\rangle=:c
. Since
0\in\square
, we have
c\leq 0
. Since
v^{\prime}_{0}
is contained in the relative interior of the simplex
[v_{1},\ldots,v_{p}]\subset\square
, we obtain
\langle v_{i},e\rangle=c
for all
1\leq i\leq p
. Since
P\in\operatorname{int}\square
, we have
\langle P,e\rangle>c
. That is,
\langle v_{0},e\rangle>c
. We obtain

\alpha_{0}=\frac{-c}{\langle v_{0},e\rangle-c}\geq 0.

Suppose
\alpha_{0}=0
. Then
0=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\alpha_{i}v_{i}
,

1=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\alpha_{i}
, and
v^{\prime}_{0}
lies in the relative interior of the simplex with vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{p}
.

If
v^{\prime}_{0}=0
, then
P=\gamma v_{0}+(1-\gamma)\cdot 0

(Case 1a)
.

Suppose
v^{\prime}_{0}\neq 0
. Then there exists
\lambda>1
maximal with the property that
\lambda v^{\prime}_{0}\in[v_{1},\ldots,v_{p}]
. After reordering,

\lambda v^{\prime}_{0}
belongs to a simplex face
[v_{1},\ldots,v_{q}]
, and

v_{1},\ldots,v_{q}
are linearly independent. Then
v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{q},0
are the vertices of a simplex containing
P
in its interior, and
\gamma=\gamma_{v_{0}}(P\in[v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{q},0])
.

(Case 1b)

Suppose
\alpha_{0}>0
.

Suppose
P=0
. Then
P=0
lies in the relative interior of the simplex with vertices
v_{0},\ldots,v_{p}
, with barycentric coordinates
\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{p}
. Thus
\gamma=\gamma_{v_{0}}(P\in[v_{0},\ldots,v_{p}])
.
(Case 2)

Suppose from now that
P\neq 0
. Let
\lambda>1
be maximal such that
\lambda P\in[v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{p}]
. Then

\lambda P=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\lambda_{i}v_{i},\lambda_{i}=\lambda t_{i}-(\lambda-1)\alpha_{i}.

Let
I=\{i\in\{0,\ldots,p\};\lambda_{i}>0\}
. Then
I\subsetneq\{0,1,\ldots,p\}
and
(\lambda_{i})_{i\in I}
are linearly independent.
We obtain
\lambda P=\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}v_{i}
with
\lambda_{i}>0
for all
i\in I
and
\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}=1
.

Subcase
0\in I
. Say
I=\{0,1,\ldots,k\}
, for some
0\leq k<p
.
Then
\lambda P=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\lambda_{i}v_{i}
with all
\lambda_{i}>0

\sum_{i=0}^{k}v_{i}=1

P=(1-\frac{1}{\lambda})\cdot 0+\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda}v_{i}
.
So
P
lies in the relative interior of the simplex with vertices

0,v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
.
(Case 3)
.
From
\gamma=t_{0}
and
\lambda_{0}=\lambda t_{0}-(\lambda-1)\alpha_{0}

\gamma=t_{0}=\frac{\lambda_{0}+(\lambda-1)\alpha_{0}}{\lambda}>\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda}=\gamma_{v_{0}}(P\in[0,v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}]).

Subcase
0\notin I
. Then
\lambda P\in\operatorname{relint}[v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}]

for some

.
(Case 4)

Here

p

Since
0\notin I

\square

belong to a given finite set.

We show that
\gamma
is away from zero, in each of the five cases:

Case 0): Here

.
Therefore

\gamma=\frac{a_{0}x_{0}}{a_{0}x_{0}+\cdots+a_{p}x_{p}}.

Since

satisfy ACC, they are bounded above. Then

\sum_{i=0}^{p}a_{i}x_{i}\leq(\max_{i}a_{i})\sum_{i=0}^{p}x_{i}
is bounded above.
Since
a_{0}
is bounded away from zero and

\gamma
is bounded away from zero.

Case 1): Here

is contained in the relative interior of a simplex with vertices

\gamma

a_{i}
satisfy ACC and

\gamma
is away from zero.

Case 2): Here
v_{i}\neq 0

Case 3): Here

\gamma
is away from zero.

Case 4): Here

.

\tau+2\gamma\geq\gamma^{\prime}.

Indeed, we may suppose
\tau<\gamma^{\prime}

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
\Lambda\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
be a
d
-dimensional lattice, let

\square\subset\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
be a
d
-dimensional compact polytope
such that
\Lambda\cap\operatorname{int}\square=\emptyset
.
Suppose
0\in\square
and
\square(0)\subset\{0\}\cup\Lambda^{prim}/{\mathcal{A}}
, where
{\mathcal{A}}\subset(0,+\infty)
is a given ACC set.
Then there exists a non-zero projection of lattices

\pi\colon\Lambda\to\Lambda^{\prime}
such that if we denote by
\square^{\prime}=\pi_{\mathbb{R}}(\square)
the image polytope, then
\Lambda^{\prime}\cap\operatorname{int}\square^{\prime}=\emptyset
and
(\Lambda^{\prime},\square^{\prime})
is bounded.

Proof.

We use induction on
d
.
Suppose
d=1
. We may suppose
\Lambda={\mathbb{Z}}
and
\square
is an interval with no interior lattice points. Equivalently,
\square\subseteq[z,z+1]
for some
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. We may take the identity as the projection.

Suppose from now that
d>1
and the statement holds in smaller dimension.
Denote
\tau=\lambda_{1}(\Lambda,\square-\square)
.
Choose
0\neq b\in\Lambda\cap\tau(\square-\square)
. Let
\Lambda\to\Lambda^{\prime}:=\Lambda/{\mathbb{Z}}b
be the projection, let
\square^{\prime}
be the image of
\square
. We have
0\in\square^{\prime}
and
\square^{\prime}(0)\subset{\Lambda^{\prime}}^{prim}/{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
, where
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{A}}\cdot\{\frac{1}{n};n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}\}
satisfies again ACC. We have two possibilities:

1) Case
\Lambda^{\prime}\cap\operatorname{int}\square^{\prime}=\emptyset
. Then we may replace
(\Lambda,\square,{\mathcal{A}})
by
(\Lambda^{\prime},\square^{\prime},{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime})
. By induction,
there exists a projection
\Lambda^{\prime}\to\Lambda^{\prime\prime}
with the desired properties. Its composition with
\Lambda\to\Lambda^{\prime}
satisfies the claim.

2) Case
\Lambda^{\prime}\cap\operatorname{int}\square^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
. We claim that the following inequality holds:

\tau\geq\gamma(\Lambda^{\prime},\square^{\prime}).

Indeed, let
Q\in\Lambda^{\prime}\cap\operatorname{int}\square^{\prime}
. Since
\square
contains
no interior lattice points, the fiber
\square_{Q}
of
\square
over
Q
is an interval without interior integer points (with respect to basis
b
). Therefore

\operatorname{length}\square_{Q}\leq 1
. On the other hand, there exists
p\in\square^{\prime}
such that
\operatorname{length}\square_{p}=\tau^{-1}
.
If
Q\in(1-t)\square^{\prime}+tp
, then
\operatorname{length}\square_{Q}\geq\frac{t}{\tau}
, and therefore
\tau\geq t
. We deduce

Q\notin\operatorname{int}((1-\tau)\square^{\prime}+\tau p).

Let
Q^{\prime}\in\partial(\square^{\prime})
be the unique point such that
Q\in(p,Q^{\prime})
. Then
\frac{Q-p}{Q^{\prime}-p}\geq 1-\tau
, that is

\tau\geq\frac{Q^{\prime}-Q}{Q^{\prime}-p}\geq\gamma(Q\in\square^{\prime}).

Taking the maximum after all
Q
, we obtain the claim.

By Theorem
2.8
,
\gamma(\Lambda^{\prime},\square^{\prime})\geq\gamma(d-1,{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime})
. From the claim, we deduce that
\tau
is bounded away from zero, i.e.
(\Lambda,\square)
is bounded. Take the identity as the projection.

Germs of toric fibrations

Let
(X/Y\ni P,B)
consist of a
toric fibration
(i.e. a proper toric morphism with connected fibers)

,
where
Y
is an affine toric variety with (unique) invariant point
P

an
{\mathbb{R}}
-Weil divisor

\operatorname{mld}_{f^{-1}P}(X,\Sigma_{X})=0

In toric language,
f\colon T_{N}\operatorname{emb}(\Delta)\to T_{\bar{N}}\operatorname{emb}(\bar{\sigma})
corresponds to a surjective homomorphism of lattices
\pi\colon N\to\bar{N}
,
a strongly convex rationally polyhedral cone
\bar{\sigma}\subset\bar{N}_{\mathbb{R}}

N
-rational fan
\Delta

|\Delta|=\pi^{-1}(\bar{\sigma})
.
We have an induced injective homomorphism between dual lattices
\pi^{*}\colon\bar{M}\to M

\bar{\sigma}-\bar{\sigma}=\bar{N}_{\mathbb{R}}
.

We use the same notation for a homomorphism of lattices and its extension to a homomorphism of
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector spaces.
Note that
|\Delta|
contains
\operatorname{Ker}\pi
. Therefore

|\Delta|^{\vee}=\pi^{*}(\bar{\sigma}^{\vee})
, and the correspondence
\bar{\tau}\mapsto\pi^{-1}(\bar{\tau})

\bar{\sigma}\subset\bar{N}_{\mathbb{R}}

\operatorname{int}|\Delta|=\pi^{-1}(\operatorname{int}\bar{\sigma}),\ \partial|\Delta|=\pi^{-1}(\partial\bar{\sigma}).

If we denote
\Delta(1)=\{e_{i};i\}

with
a_{i}\in[0,1]
. Note that
(e_{i})_{i}

1) Fix

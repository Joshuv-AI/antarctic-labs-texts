The Shakhov model near a global Maxwellian
Gi-Chan Bae, Seok-Bae Yun

Abstract
. Shakhov model is a relaxation approximation of the Boltzmann equation proposed to overcome the deficiency of the original BGK model, namely, the incorrect production of the Prandtl number. In this paper, we address the existence and the asymptotic stability of the Shakhov model when the initial data is a small perturbation of global equilibrium. We derive a dichotomy in the coercive estimate of the linearized relaxation operator between zero and non-zero Prandtl number, and observe that the linearized relaxation operator is more degenerate in the former case. To remove such degeneracy and recover the full coercivity, we consider a micro-macro system that involves an additional non-conservative quantity related to the heat flux.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Shakhov model

The fundamental model describing the dynamics of rarefied gases at the mesoscopic level is the Boltzmann equation.
But the complicated structure and the high dimensionality have long hindered the practical application of the Boltzmann equation.
In this regard, the model equation introduced in
[
5
,
47
]
, has been popularly used to study various flow problems in place of the Boltzmann equation.
However, it was soon revealed that this model, which goes by the BGK model, cannot achieve the correct Navier-Stokes limit in
that the Prandtl number computed in the hydrodynamic limit is incorrect.

There have been two major remedies to overcome this drawback.
The first such effort goes back to Holway
[
26
]
, who extended the local Maxwellian into an ellipsoidal Gaussian
to obtain an additional degree of freedom in computing the transport coefficients.
On the other hand, Shakhov
[
40
]
suggested a way to get the correct Prandtl number by multiplying the Maxwellian with
an extra term that adjusts the heat flux while leaving the collision invariant untouched. The price to pay for this adjustment is that the H-theorem holds only when the flow remains close to the fluid regime. Even with such apparent defect of the model, the Shakhov model has been
widely used in various fields of rarefied gas dynamics, since it reproduces satisfactory qualitative features of the Boltzmann dynamics in many important flow problems
[
8
,
13
,
43
,
44
,
54
,
55
]
.
Especially, due to the non-triviality of the heat flux compared to that of the original BGK, it is reported that the Shakhov
model works better for non-isothermal flows
[
54
,
13
]
. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, however, the existence of this important kinetic model has never been studied in the mathematical literature, which is the main motivation of the current work.

More precisely, we consider in this paper the following initial value problem of the Shakhov model:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}F+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F&=\frac{1}{\tau}(\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)-F),\cr F(x,v,0)&=F_{0}(x,v).\end{split}

The unknown
F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called the velocity distribution function where
F(x,v,t)
is the number density of molecules in the phase space on the phase point
(x,v)
at time
t
. We define the macroscopic density
\rho
, bulk velocity
U
, temperature
T
, stress tensor
\Theta
, and the heat flux
q
as follows:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho(x,t)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(x,v,t)dv,\cr\rho(x,t)U(x,t)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(x,v,t)vdv,\cr 3\rho(x,t)T(x,t)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(x,v,t)|v-U(x,t)|^{2}dv,\cr\rho\Theta_{ij}(x,t)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(x,v,t)(v_{i}-U_{i}(x,t))(v_{j}-U_{j}(x,t))dv,\cr q(x,t)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(x,v,t)(v-U(x,t))|v-U(x,t)|^{2}dv.\end{split}

The Shakhov operator is defined as

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)(x,v,t)=\mathcal{M}(F)\left[1+\frac{1-Pr}{5}\frac{q(x,t)\cdot(v-U(x,t))}{\rho(x,t)T(x,t)^{2}}\left(\frac{|v-U(x,t)|^{2}}{2T(x,t)}-\frac{5}{2}\right)\right],\end{split}

where
Pr
is the Prandtl number,
\mathcal{M}(F)
is the standard local Maxwellian:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(F)(x,v,t)=\frac{\rho(x,t)}{\sqrt{2\pi T(x,t)}^{3}}\exp\left(-\frac{|v-U(x,t)|^{2}}{2T(x,t)}\right).

Although the stress tensor
\Theta
does not explicitly appears in the definition (
1.3
), except through the relation:

(1.4)

\displaystyle 3T=\sum_{1\leq i\leq 3}\Theta_{ii},

we listed it in (
1.2
) since it will be crucially used in the analysis later.
The relaxation time
\tau
takes the following form
[
43
,
8
,
32
,
48
]
:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}=\frac{1}{\tau_{0}}\rho^{\eta}T^{w},

for some positive constant
\tau_{0}
and
\eta\geq 0
,
w\in\mathbb{R}
.

The Shakhov operator satisfies the following identities by construction (See Appendix.):

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)(x,v,t)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\cr v\cr|v|^{2}\end{array}\right)dv=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(x,v,t)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\cr v\cr|v|^{2}\end{array}\right)dv,

which implies the conservation laws of the total mass, momentum, and energy:

(1.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(x,v,t)dvdx=0,\cr&\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(x,v,t)vdvdx=0,\cr&\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(x,v,t)|v|^{2}dvdx=0.\end{split}

The
H
-theorem is proved only when the
F
is sufficiently close to the equilibrium in
[
40
]
(See Appendix.):

(1.7)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}F\ln Fdvdx\leq 0.

We note that the Shakhov relaxation operator satisfies

(1.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)-F)(v_{i}-U_{i})|v-U|^{2}dv=-Prq_{i}(x,t).

This additional cancellation property explains why the Shakhov model has a bigger degeneracy in this case in the vanishing Prantl number regime (See Proposition
2.2
).

Finally, we mention that the Shakhov model is a generalization of the BGK model in the sense that it reduces to the original BGK model when
Pr=1
.

1.2.
Main results

In this paper, we consider the existence and asymptotic behavior of (
1.1
) when the initial data is close enough to the normalized global Maxwellian:

(1.9)

\displaystyle m(v)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{3}}}e^{-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}}.

We define the perturbation
f
by
F=m+\sqrt{m}f
, and derive following equation for
f
from (
1.1
):

(1.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f&=\frac{1}{\tau_{0}}L_{Pr}f+\Gamma(f),\cr f(x,v,0)&=f_{0}(x,v).\end{split}

Here,
f_{0}(x,v)=(F_{0}(x,v)-m)/\sqrt{m}
,
L_{Pr}
, and
\Gamma(f)
are the linear part and the non-linear part of the linearized relaxation operator (See Section 2 for precise definitions).

We define the energy functional:

(1.11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(f)(t)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq N}\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}ds,

where
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}
is the standard
L^{2}
norm:

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|f(x,v)|^{2}dvdx,

and the multi-indices notation was employed for the differential operator:

\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}=\partial_{t}^{\alpha_{0}}\partial_{x_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{x_{2}}^{\alpha_{2}}\partial_{x_{3}}^{\alpha_{3}}\partial_{v_{1}}^{\beta_{1}}\partial_{v_{2}}^{\beta_{2}}\partial_{v_{3}}^{\beta_{3}},

with
\alpha=(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})
,
\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3})
.

We now state the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N\geq 3
, and fix
Pr\geq 0
. Assume the initial data satisfies
F_{0}(x,v)=m+\sqrt{m}f_{0}(x,v)\geq 0
, and shares the same total mass, momentum and energy with the global equilibrium
m(v)
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{0}(x,v)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\cr v\cr|v|^{2}\end{array}\right)dvdx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}m(v)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\cr v\cr|v|^{2}\end{array}\right)dvdx.

In the case of
Pr=0
, we assume further that the total third moment of the initial data vanishes:

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{0}(x,v)v|v|^{2}dvdx=0.

Then, there exists
M
such that if
\mathcal{E}(f_{0})\leq M
, then there exists the unique global-in-time classical solution
f
to (
1.10
) satisfying

(1)

The distribution function
F
and the Shakhov operator are non-negative for
t\geq 0
:

\displaystyle F(x,v,t)=m+\sqrt{m}f(x,v,t)\geq 0,\quad\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)(x,v,t)\geq 0,

and satisfies the conservation laws (
2.22
).

(2)

The energy functional is uniformly bounded:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathcal{E}(f)(t)\leq C\mathcal{E}(f_{0}).

(3)

The perturbation
f
decays exponentially fast:

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\leq Ce^{-\delta t},

for some positive constant
C>0
and
\delta>0
.

(4)

Let
f
and
\bar{f}
be solutions corresponding to initial data
f_{0}
and
\bar{f}_{0}
, respectively satisfying
\mathcal{E}(f_{0})\leq M
and
\mathcal{E}(\bar{f}_{0})\leq M
. Then there exists positive constant
C
satisfying the following
L^{2}
stability estimate:

\displaystyle\|f(t)-\bar{f}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\leq C\|f_{0}-\bar{f}_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.

Remark 1.2
.

The study of the vanishing Prandtl number is not only mathematically interesting but also physically relevant even since it can be an approximate model for fluids with a very small Prandtl number. For example, in some thermoacoustic engines, a gas with a low Prandtl number plays a critical role because of its high heat diffusivity. For example, the authors in
[
3
,
9
]
observed that some mixtures of light and heavy noble gases produce low Prandtl numbers. Besides, zero-Prandtl-number limit is considered in
[
42
]
to study the convection with extremely small Prandtl number such as the convection zone of the sun (
Pr\approx 10^{-8}
).

Remark 1.3
.

In the case of
Pr>0
, the instant energy functional without the production term is sufficient to close the energy estimate as in
[
2
]
. But when
Pr=0
, the production term must be incorporated into the energy functional. (See details in Proposition
5.1
step 3.)

1.3.
Novelties
\&
difficulties

To close the energy estimate to extend the local solution into the global one, it is important to identify the dissipative nature of the linear term
L_{Pr}
. In this regard, we observe the following dichotomy in the degeneracy of the dissipative estimate: When
Pr>0
, we have

(1.12)

\displaystyle\langle L_{Pr}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\leq-\min\{Pr,1\}\|(I-P_{c})f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}^{2},

where
P_{c}
is a projection operator on the linear space spanned by
\{\sqrt{m},v\sqrt{m},|v|^{2}\sqrt{m}\}
.
On the other hand, the dissipation becomes more degenerate in the case
Pr=0
:

(1.13)

\displaystyle\langle L_{0}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}=-\|(I-P_{c}-P_{nc})f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2},

where
P_{nc}f
is a projection operator on the linear space spanned by non-conservative basis
\{v|v|^{2}\sqrt{m}\}
, so that
P_{c}+P_{nc}
constitutes
a projection operator on a wider space spanned by 8 bases:

\{\sqrt{m},v\sqrt{m},|v|^{2}\sqrt{m},v|v|^{2}\sqrt{m}\}
.

This additional degeneracy in the vanishing Prandtl number regime is due to the following cancellation property unobserved in the original BGK model or the Boltzmann equation:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\mathcal{S}_{0}(F)-F)(v_{i}-U_{i})|v-U|^{2}dv=0,

which is obtained by putting
Pr=0
in (
1.8
).
The larger kernel (
1.13
) indicates that the degeneracy is stronger in the vanishing Prantl number regime since the dissipativity of
L_{Pr}
stops operating on the null space.
In the case
Pr>0
, the full dissipativity of
L_{Pr}
can be recovered by the standard argument
[
23
,
25
,
50
]
: the derivatives of the kernels are estimated using the micro-macro equations that govern the evolution of the degenerate part, and the lowest order estimates are derived by combining the derivative estimates and the Poincaré inequality with the vanishing moments of the perturbation up to the second order.
On the other hand, when the Prandtl number vanishes, a novel difficulty unobserved in the previous literature arises: The micro-macro system now involves a non-conservative quantity, namely the heat flux part. This leads to a more complicated micro-macro system, and more seriously, the lowest order estimate of these new terms can not be treated in a similar manner as in the previous case, since the third-order moment of the perturbation related to the heat flux does not vanish. To overcome this, we assume that the total third moment of the initial data vanishes:

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{0}(x,v)v_{i}|v|^{2}dvdx=0.

and use the evolution law for the third moment:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}fv_{i}(|v|^{2}-5)\sqrt{m}dvdx=\frac{1}{\tau}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}2U_{i}\rho T-\sum_{1\leq j\leq 3}2\rho U_{j}\Theta_{ij}dx\bigg),

to derive the following modified dissipation estimate:

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\langle L_{Pr}\partial^{\alpha}f,\partial^{\alpha}f\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\leq-\delta\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+C\mathcal{E}^{2}(t),

for some positive constant
\delta>0
, which enables one to construct the global-in-time classical solution.

2.
Linearization

2.1.
Linearization of the Shakhov operator

In this section, we linearize the Shakhov operator
\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)
near the global Maxwellian. We start with the definition of the Shakhov projection operator.

Definition 2.1
.

We define the
8
-dimensional macroscopic projection operator as follows:

\displaystyle P_{Pr}f

\displaystyle=P_{c}f+(1-Pr)P_{nc}f,

where
P_{c}
and
P_{nc}
are projections on the conservative space and non-conservative space respectively:

\displaystyle P_{c}f

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\sqrt{m}dv\right)\sqrt{m}+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}fv\sqrt{m}dv\right)\cdot v\sqrt{m}+\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\frac{|v|^{2}-3}{\sqrt{6}}\sqrt{m}dv\right)\frac{|v|^{2}-3}{\sqrt{6}}\sqrt{m},

\displaystyle P_{nc}f

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\frac{v(|v|^{2}-5)}{\sqrt{10}}\sqrt{m}dv\right)\cdot\frac{v(|v|^{2}-5)}{\sqrt{10}}\sqrt{m}.

Definition 2.2
.

We define
G_{ij}
and
H_{i}

(1\leq i,j\leq 3)
by

(2.1)

\displaystyle G_{ij}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\left(\rho\Theta_{ii}+\rho U_{i}^{2}-\rho\right),~&\textit{if}\quad i=j,\cr\rho\Theta_{ij}+\rho U_{i}U_{j},~&\textit{if}\quad i\neq j,\end{cases}

for
1\leq i,j\leq 3
, and

(2.2)

\displaystyle H_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{10}}\bigg(q_{i}+\sum_{1\leq j\leq 3}2\rho U_{j}\Theta_{ij}+\rho U_{i}|U|^{2}+\rho U_{i}(\Theta_{11}+\Theta_{22}+\Theta_{33})-5\rho U_{i}\bigg),

for
i=1,\cdots,3
, where
\Theta
is the stress tensor defined in (
1.2
). Since
G
(or
\Theta
) is symmetric

matrix, we view it as a component of
\mathbb{R}^{6}
:

(2.3)

\displaystyle G=\left\{G_{11},G_{22},G_{33},G_{12},G_{23},G_{31}\right\},

for simplicity.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
F=m+\sqrt{m}f
. Then the Shakhov operator is linearized into the following form:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)=m+P_{Pr}f\sqrt{m}+\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 13}\int_{0}^{1}\left\{D^{2}_{(\rho_{\theta},\rho_{\theta}U_{\theta},G_{\theta},H_{\theta})}\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(\theta)\right\}_{ij}(1-\theta)d\theta\langle f,e_{i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\langle f,e_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(\theta)=\frac{\rho_{\theta}}{\sqrt{2\pi T_{\theta}}^{3}}\exp\left(-\frac{|v-U_{\theta}|^{2}}{2T_{\theta}}\right)\left[1+\frac{1-Pr}{5}\frac{q_{\theta}\cdot(v-U_{\theta})}{\rho_{\theta}T_{\theta}^{2}}\left(\frac{|v-U_{\theta}|^{2}}{2T_{\theta}}-\frac{5}{2}\right)\right],

and the transitional macroscopic fields
\rho_{\theta}
,
U_{\theta}
,
\Theta_{\theta}
and

are given by

(2.4)

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta}

\displaystyle=\theta\rho+(1-\theta),\quad\rho_{\theta}U_{\theta}=\theta\rho U,\quad G_{\theta}=\theta G,\quad H_{\theta}=\theta H.

The
13
-basis
\{e_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq 13}
denote

\displaystyle e_{1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{m},\qquad e_{i+1}=v_{i}\sqrt{m},\qquad e_{i+4}=\frac{v_{i}^{2}-1}{2}\sqrt{m},

\displaystyle e_{8}

\displaystyle=v_{1}v_{2}\sqrt{m},\qquad e_{9}=v_{2}v_{3}\sqrt{m}\qquad e_{10}=v_{1}v_{3}\sqrt{m},

and

\displaystyle e_{i+10}=\frac{v_{i}|v|^{2}\sqrt{m}-5v_{i}\sqrt{m}}{\sqrt{10}},

for

.

Proof.

We apply Taylor’s theorem:

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(1)=\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(0)+\mathcal{S}_{Pr}^{\prime}(0)+\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{S}_{Pr}^{\prime\prime}(\theta)(1-\theta)d\theta.

We can easily see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(0)=m,\qquad\textit{and}\qquad\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(1)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F).

\bullet
Computation of
\mathcal{S}_{Pr}^{\prime}(0)
: An explicit computation with a change of variable

(2.6)

\displaystyle(\rho,U,\Theta,q)\rightarrow(\rho,\rho U,G,H),

gives

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{Pr}^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{d(\rho_{\theta},\rho_{\theta}U_{\theta},G_{\theta},H_{\theta})}{d\theta}\right)^{T}\left(\frac{\partial(\rho_{\theta},\rho_{\theta}U_{\theta},G_{\theta},H_{\theta})}{\partial(\rho_{\theta},U_{\theta},\Theta_{\theta},q_{\theta})}\right)^{-1}\nabla_{(\rho_{\theta},U_{\theta},\Theta_{\theta},q_{\theta})}\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(\theta)\bigg|_{\theta=0}.

To proceed further, we need the following two auxiliary lemmas.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
J
denote the Jacobian matrix:

J\equiv\frac{\partial(\rho,\rho U,G,H)}{\partial(\rho,U,\Theta,q)}.

Then we have

(1) T

(2.22)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(x,v,t)\sqrt{m}dvdx&=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{0}(x,v)\sqrt{m}dvdx,\cr\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(x,v,t)v\sqrt{m}dvdx&=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{0}(x,v)v\sqrt{m}dvdx,\cr\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(x,v,t)|v|^{2}\sqrt{m}dvdx&=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{0}(x,v)|v|^{2}\sqrt{m}dvdx.\end{split}

2.3.
Properties of the linear term

In this section, we establish the coercivity of
L_{Pr}
. We observe the dichotomy in the dissipative nature of
L_{Pr}

between the case
Pr>0
and
Pr=0
. We first define the following basis:

(2.23)

\displaystyle\bar{e}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{m},\quad\bar{e}_{i+1}=v_{i}\sqrt{m},\quad\bar{e}_{5}=\frac{|v|^{2}-3}{\sqrt{6}}\sqrt{m},\quad\bar{e}_{i+5}=\frac{v_{i}|v|^{2}-5v_{i}}{\sqrt{10}}\sqrt{m},

for
i=1,2,3
, and write
P_{Pr}f
(See Definition
2.1
) as

\displaystyle P_{Pr}f

\displaystyle=P_{c}f+(1-Pr)P_{nc}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq 5}\langle f,\bar{e}_{i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\bar{e}_{i}+(1-Pr)\sum_{6\leq i\leq 8}\langle f,\bar{e}_{i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\bar{e}_{i}.

Lemma 2.7
.

The projection operator
P_{c}
and
P_{nc}
satisfy the following properties:

(1)

P_{c}
and
P_{nc}
are orthonormal projection.

\displaystyle P_{c}^{2}=P_{c},\quad P_{nc}^{2}=P_{nc}.

(2)

P_{c}
and
P_{nc}
are orthogonal.

\displaystyle P_{c}\perp P_{nc}.

Proof.

The first statement follows from that each of the following:

\{\bar{e}_{1},\,\bar{e}_{2},\,\bar{e}_{3},\,\bar{e}_{4},\,\bar{e}_{5}\},

and

\{\bar{e}_{6},\,\bar{e}_{7},\,\bar{e}_{8}\},

forms an orthonormal basis, while the second statement is derived from

\displaystyle\langle\bar{e}_{i},\bar{e}_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}=0\quad(1\leq i\leq 5,~~6\leq j\leq 8),

which can be checked through a direct computation.
∎

In the following proposition, we prove the main result of this section, namely the dichotomy between
Pr>0
and
Pr=0
in the dissipative property of the linearized Shakhov operator. We note that the degeneracy of the estimate is stronger in the case of
Pr=0
.

Proposition 2.2
.

(1)
In the case
Pr>0
,
L_{Pr}f
satisfies

\displaystyle\langle L_{Pr}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\leq-\min\{Pr,1\}\|(I-P_{c})f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}^{2}.

(2)
When
Pr=0
,
L_{Pr}
satisfies

\displaystyle\langle L_{Pr}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}=-\|(I-P_{Pr})f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}=-\|(I-P_{c}-P_{nc})f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}.

Proof.

(1) By an explicit computation, we have

\displaystyle\langle L_{Pr}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}

\displaystyle=\langle P_{c}f-f+(1-Pr)P_{nc}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}

\displaystyle=\langle P_{c}f-f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}+(1-Pr)\langle P_{nc}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}.

Since
\bar{e}_{1},\cdots,\bar{e}_{5}
constitute an orthonormal basis, we have

\displaystyle\langle P_{c}f-f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}

\displaystyle=-\langle(I-P_{c})f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}

\displaystyle=-\langle(I-P_{c})f,(I-P_{c})f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}-\langle(I-P_{c})f,P_{c}f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}

\displaystyle=-\|(I-P_{c})f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}^{2},

which implies

(2.24)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle L_{Pr}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}&=-\|(I-P_{c})f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}^{2}+(1-Pr)\langle P_{nc}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}.\end{split}

Applying the property
P_{nc}\perp P_{c}
in Lemma
2.7
(2), we have

\displaystyle\langle P_{nc}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}

\displaystyle=\langle P_{nc}(P_{c}f+(I-P_{c})f),P_{c}f+(I-P_{c})f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}

\displaystyle=\langle P_{nc}(I-P_{c})f,(I-P_{c})f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}

\displaystyle=\|P_{nc}(I-P_{c})f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}^{2},

which gives

(2.25)

\displaystyle 0\leq\langle P_{nc}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\leq\|(I-P_{c})f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}^{2}.

When
0<Pr\leq 1
, substituting (
2.25
) in (
2.24
) yields

\displaystyle\langle L_{Pr}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\leq-Pr\|(I-P_{c})f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}^{2}.

In the case
1<Pr
, since
\langle P_{nc}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}
is non-negative, we can ignore the second term in the R.H.S of (
2.24
), to obtain

\displaystyle\langle L_{Pr}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\leq-\|(I-P_{c})f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}^{2}.

We combine the above two inequalities to get the desired result.

(2) Since
P_{Pr}f=P_{c}f+P_{nc}f
is a projection operator onto the space spanned by the
8
-dimensional orthonormal basis
\{\bar{e}_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq 8}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle L_{Pr}f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle=-\langle(I-P_{Pr})f,f\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle=-\langle(I-P_{Pr})f,(I-P_{Pr})f\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}-\langle(I-P_{Pr})f,P_{Pr}f\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle=-\|(I-P_{Pr})f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}.

∎

Lemma 2.8
.

When
Pr>0
, the kernel of the linear operator
L_{Pr}
is given by the following 5-dimensional space:

\displaystyle Ker{L}=span\{\sqrt{m},v\sqrt{m},|v|^{2}\sqrt{m}\},

while in the case of
Pr=0
,
L_{Pr}
has a larger kernel spanned by the following 8 functions:

\displaystyle Ker{L}=span\{\sqrt{m},v\sqrt{m},|v|^{2}\sqrt{m},v|v|^{2}\sqrt{m}\}.

Proof.

This follows directly from Proposition
2.2
.
∎

3.1.
Estimates for the macroscopic fields

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
N\geq 3
. For sufficiently small
\mathcal{E}(t)
, there exist positive constants

such that

\displaystyle(1)\ |\rho(x,t)-1|\leq C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)},

\displaystyle(2)\ |U(x,t)|\leq C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)},

\displaystyle(3)\ |\Theta_{ij}(x,t)-\delta_{ij}|\leq C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)},

\displaystyle(4)\ |q_{i}(x,t)|\leq C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)},

for
1\leq i,j\leq 3
.

Proof.

(1) Since

(3.1)

\displaystyle\rho=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}m+\sqrt{m}fdv=1+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{m}fdv.

The Hölder inequality and the Sobolev embedding
H^{2}\subset\subset L^{\infty}
give

\displaystyle|\rho-1|\leq C\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\leq\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 2}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\leq C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)}.

(2) We write the bulk velocity
U
as

\displaystyle\rho U=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(m+\sqrt{m}f)vdv=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{m}fvdv.

Then, the Hölder inequality and the Sobolev embedding, together with the lower bound of
\rho
in (1) yields

\displaystyle|U|\leq\frac{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|f|^{2}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}m|v|^{2}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{1-C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)}}\leq\frac{C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)}}{1-C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)}}\leq C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)}.

(3) For the estimate of
\Theta
, we recall the computation in
(\ref{viv2})_{1}
:

\displaystyle\rho\Theta_{ij}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(v_{i}-U_{i})(v_{j}-U_{j})dv=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(m+\sqrt{m}f)v_{i}v_{j}dv-\rho U_{i}U_{j}.

When
i=j
, we apply the Hölder inequality and the estimates in (1) and (2) to get

\displaystyle|\rho\Theta_{ii}-1|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|f|^{2}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}mv_{i}^{4}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C(1+C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)})\mathcal{E}(t)\leq C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)},

which gives

\displaystyle|\Theta_{ii}-1|

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)}.

When
i\neq j
, since
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}mv_{i}v_{j}dv=0
, we get

\displaystyle|\Theta_{ij}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|f|^{2}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}mv_{i}^{2}v_{j}^{2}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C(1+C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)})\mathcal{E}(t)}{1-C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)}}\leq C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)}.

(4) Recall the computation in
(\ref{viv2})_{2}
that

\displaystyle q_{i}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}Fv_{i}|v|^{2}dv-\sum_{1\leq j\leq 3}2U_{j}\rho\Theta_{ij}-\rho U_{i}|U|^{2}-U_{i}\rho\sum_{1\leq i\leq 3}\Theta_{ii}.

Combining this with the above estimates (1)-(3), we have

\displaystyle|q_{i}|

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}+C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)}\leq C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)}.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
N=|\alpha|\geq 1
. For sufficiently small
\mathcal{E}(t)
, there exist positive constants
C
and
C_{\alpha}
such that

\displaystyle(1)\ |\partial^{\alpha}\rho(x,t)|\leq C\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{v}},

\displaystyle(2)\ |\partial^{\alpha}U(x,t)|\leq C_{\alpha}\sum_{|\alpha_{1}|\leq|\alpha|}\|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}f\|_{L^{2}_{v}},

\displaystyle(3)\ |\partial^{\alpha}\Theta_{ij}(x,t)|\leq C_{\alpha}\sum_{|\alpha_{1}|\leq|\alpha|}\|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}f\|_{L^{2}_{v}},

\displaystyle(4)\ |\partial^{\alpha}q_{i}(x,t)|\leq C_{\alpha}\sum_{|\alpha_{1}|\leq|\alpha|}\|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}f\|_{L^{2}_{v}},

for

.

Proof.

(1) Taking
\partial^{\alpha}
on (
3.1
) and applying the Hölder inequality give

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}\rho|=\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{m}\partial^{\alpha}fdv\bigg|\leq C\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}.

(2) Similarly, we have

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}U|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\partial^{\alpha}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{m}fvdv}{\rho}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\alpha}\sum_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=\alpha}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{m}\partial^{\alpha_{1}}fvdv\bigg|\bigg|\partial^{\alpha_{2}}\frac{1}{\rho}\bigg|

(4) Similarly, taking

on

∎

3.3.
Local solution

Theorem 3.5
.

Let

and

(2)

\displaystyle F(x,v,t)=m+\sqrt{m}f(x,v,t)\geq 0.

(3)

(4)

Proof.

We define

iteratively by the following scheme:

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}F^{n+1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F^{n+1}&=\frac{1}{\tau(F^{n})}(\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F^{n})-F^{n+1}),\cr F^{n+1}(x,v,0)&=F_{0}(x,v),\end{split}

with
F^{0}(x,v,t)=F_{0}(x,v)
. We use induction argument. We assume the statement (1) - (4) for
n
-th step.
We first observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F^{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{n}}{\sqrt{2\pi T_{n}}^{3}}\left[1+\frac{1-Pr}{5}\frac{q_{n}\cdot v}{\rho_{n}T_{n}^{2}}\left(\frac{|v|^{2}}{2T_{n}}-\frac{5}{2}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2T_{n}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{M}(F^{n})\left(1-C\frac{1-Pr}{5}\frac{|q_{n}|}{\rho_{n}T_{n}^{3/2}}\right),

where we used

\displaystyle\bigg|x^{m}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{2T}\right)\bigg|\leq CT^{m/2}.

Applying Lemma
3.1
and the induction hypothesis yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F^{n})\geq\mathcal{M}(F^{n})\left(1-C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(f^{n})}\right)\geq\mathcal{M}(F^{n})\left(1-CM_{0}\right).

Therefore for sufficiently small
M_{0}
, we have
\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F^{n})\geq 0
. Then the non-negativity of
F^{n}
follows directly from the mild formulation of (
3.5
):

F^{n+1}(x,v,t)=e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\tau(F^{n})}dt}F_{0}(x-vt,v)\cr+\frac{1}{\tau(F^{n})}e^{-\int_{s}^{t}\frac{1}{\tau(F^{n})}dt}\int_{0}^{T_{*}}\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F^{n})(x+v(s-t),v,s)ds.

Now we prove the uniform boundedness of the energy norm. For this, we substitute
F^{n+1}=m+\sqrt{m}f^{n+1}
into (
3.5
) to get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f^{n+1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{n+1}+\frac{1}{\tau_{0}}f^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau_{0}}P_{Pr}(f^{n})+\Gamma(f^{n}),

\displaystyle f^{n+1}(x,v,0)

\displaystyle=f_{0}(x,v),

where
f_{0}^{n}(x,v)=(F_{0}(x,v)-m)/\sqrt{m}
. Taking
\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}
on both sides:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f^{n+1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f^{n+1}+\frac{1}{\tau_{0}}\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f^{n+1}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\partial^{\alpha+\bar{k}_{i}}_{\beta-k_{i}}\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau_{0}}\partial_{\beta}P_{Pr}(\partial^{\alpha}f^{n})+\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}\Gamma(f^{n}),

and taking product with
\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f^{n+1}
yields

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau_{0}}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}^{3}}\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f^{n+1}\partial^{\alpha+\bar{k}_{i}}_{\beta-k_{i}}\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f^{n+1}dvdx\cr+\frac{1}{\tau_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}^{3}}(\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f^{n+1}\partial_{\beta}P_{Pr}(\partial^{\alpha}f^{n})+\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f^{n+1}\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}\Gamma(f^{n}))dvdx,

where
k_{i}~(i=1,2,3)
are coordinate unit vectors and
\bar{k}_{1}=(0,1,0,0)
,
\bar{k}_{2}=(0,0,1,0)
,
\bar{k}_{3}=(0,0,0,1)
.
We then integrate for time and recall Proposition
3.1
to derive

\displaystyle(1-CT_{*})\mathcal{E}(f^{n+1})(t)\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}+CT_{*}+CT_{*}M_{0}^{2}\right)M_{0}.

For sufficiently small
M_{0}
and
T_{*}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(f^{n+1})(t)\leq M_{0}.

The remaining part can be obtained from the standard argument
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
. We omit it.
∎

4.
Coercivity estimate for
Pr>0

In this section, we fill up the degeneracy of the linearized Shakov operator
L_{Pr}
and recover the full coercivity. As is observed in Proposition
2.2
and Lemma
2.8
, the degeneracy of the linear operator
L_{Pr}
is strictly larger when
Pr=0
than
Pr>0
. The case of
Pr>0
can be treated by a rather standard argument, which is briefly presented in this section.

Recall the macroscopic projection operator
P_{c}
from Definition
2.1
:

\displaystyle P_{c}f=a_{0}(x,t)\sqrt{m}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq 3}b_{0i}(x,t)v_{i}\sqrt{m}+c_{0}(x,t)|v|^{2}\sqrt{m},

where

\displaystyle a_{0}(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\sqrt{m}dv-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{m}dv,

\displaystyle b_{0i}(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}fv_{i}\sqrt{m}dv,

\displaystyle c_{0}(x,t)=\frac{1}{6}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{m}dv,

for
i=1,2,3
. Substituting
f=P_{c}f+(I-P_{c})f
into (
2.20
) gives

(4.1)

\displaystyle\{\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\}\{P_{c}f\}=l_{0}(f)+h_{0}(f),

where

\displaystyle l_{0}(f)

\displaystyle=-\{\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\}\{(I-P_{c})f\}+\frac{1}{\tau_{0}}L_{Pr}\{(I-P_{c})f\},\quad h_{0}(f)=\Gamma(f).

By an explicit computation, the left-hand side of (
4.1
) is expressed as a linear combination of the following
13
-basis

\{\sqrt{m},v_{i}\sqrt{m},v_{i}v_{j}\sqrt{m},v_{i}|v|^{2}\sqrt{m}\}

(1\leq i,j\leq 3)
:

\bigg\{\partial_{t}a_{0}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq 3}(\partial_{x_{i}}a_{0}+\partial_{t}b_{0i})v_{i}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq 3}(\partial_{x_{i}}b_{0i}+\partial_{t}c_{0})v_{i}^{2}\cr+\sum_{i<j}(\partial_{x_{i}}b_{0j}+\partial_{x_{j}}b_{0i})v_{i}v_{j}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq 3}(\partial_{x_{i}}c_{0})v_{i}|v|^{2}\bigg\}\sqrt{m}.

Let
(l_{0c},l_{0i},l_{0ij},l_{0is},l_{0ijs})
, and
(h_{0c},h_{0i},h_{0ij},h_{0is},h_{0ijs})
be the coefficient corresponding to the linear
expansion w.r.t the above basis when
l_{0}
and
h_{0}
are expanded to the above
13
basis, respectively. Then comparing the coefficients of both sides yields the following system:

(4.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}a&=l_{0c}+h_{0c},\cr\partial_{x_{i}}a+\partial_{t}b_{i}&=l_{0i}+h_{0i},\cr\partial_{x_{i}}b_{i}+\partial_{t}c&=l_{0ii}+h_{0ii},\cr\partial_{x_{i}}b_{j}+\partial_{x_{j}}b_{i}&=l_{0ij}+h_{0ij}\quad(i\neq j),\cr\partial_{x_{i}}c&=l_{0is}+h_{0is}.\end{split}

for
i,j=1,2,3
. For the notational simplicity we define

\displaystyle\tilde{l}_{0}

\displaystyle=l_{0c}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq 3}\left(l_{0i}+l_{0is}\right)+\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 3}\left(l_{0ij}+l_{0ijs}\right),

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{0}

\displaystyle=h_{c}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq 3}\left(h_{0}{i}+h_{0is}\right)+\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 3}\left(h_{0ij}+h_{0ijs}\right).

The analysis for this system is now standard, which can be found, for example, in
[
23
,
24
,
25
,
48
]
to yield

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|P_{Pr}\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\left(\|\partial^{\alpha}a\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}b\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}c\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N-1}\left(\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{l}_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{h}_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|(I-P_{Pr})\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+CM_{0}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}},

for sufficiently small
\mathcal{E}(t)
. This, combined with the degenerate coercive estimate in Proposition
2.2
(1), leads to the following full coercivity estimate for sufficiently small
\mathcal{E}(t)
:

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\langle L_{Pr}\partial^{\alpha}f,\partial^{\alpha}f\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\leq-\delta\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}.

5.
Coercivity estiamte for
Pr=0

Due to the bigger degeneracy of
L_{Pr}
in the case of
Pr=0
, a new idea is to need to recover the full coercivity. More precisely, the presence of an additional 3-dimensional null space leads to a bigger differential system than (
4.2
) that involves non-conservative quantities, which cannot be merged into the coercivity estimate using the previous arguments.

First, we write
P_{Pr}f
in Definition
2.1
as

\displaystyle P_{Pr}f=a(x,t)\sqrt{m}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq 3}b_{i}(x,t)v_{i}\sqrt{m}+c(x,t)|v|^{2}\sqrt{m}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq 3}d_{i}(x,t)v_{i}|v|^{2}\sqrt{m},

where

\displaystyle a(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\sqrt{m}dv-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{m}dv,

\displaystyle b_{i}(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}fv_{i}\sqrt{m}dv-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}fv_{i}(|v|^{2}-5)\sqrt{m}dv,

\displaystyle c(x,t)=\frac{1}{6}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(|v|^{2}-3)\sqrt{m}dv,

\displaystyle d_{i}(x,t)=\frac{1}{10}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}fv_{i}(|v|^{2}-5)\sqrt{m}dv,

for
i=1,2,3
. Substituting
f=P_{Pr}f+(I-P_{Pr})f
into (
2.20
), we get the similar equation as in (
4.1
):

(5.1)

\displaystyle\{\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\}\{P_{Pr}f\}=l(f)+h(f),

where

\displaystyle l(f)

\displaystyle=-\{\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\}\{(I-P_{Pr})f\}+\frac{1}{\tau_{0}}L_{Pr}\{(I-P_{Pr})f\},\quad h(f)=\Gamma(f).

This time, we expand the L.H.S. of (
5.1
) using the following
19
-basis
(1\leq i,j\leq 3)
:

(5.2)

\displaystyle\{\sqrt{m},v_{i}\sqrt{m},v_{i}v_{j}\sqrt{m},v_{i}|v|^{2}\sqrt{m},v_{i}v_{j}|v|^{2}\sqrt{m}\},

to get

\bigg\{\partial_{t}a+\sum_{1\leq i\leq 3}(\partial_{x_{i}}a+\partial_{t}b_{i})v_{i}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq 3}(\partial_{x_{i}}b_{i}+\partial_{t}c)v_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i<j}(\partial_{x_{i}}b_{j}+\partial_{x_{j}}b_{i})v_{i}v_{j}\cr+\sum_{1\leq i\leq 3}(\partial_{x_{i}}c+\partial_{t}d_{i})v_{i}|v|^{2}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq 3}\partial_{x_{i}}d_{i}v_{i}^{2}|v|^{2}+\sum_{i<j}(\partial_{x_{i}}d_{j}+\partial_{x_{j}}d_{i})v_{i}v_{j}|v|^{2}\bigg\}\sqrt{m}.

Let
(l_{c},l_{i},l_{ij},l_{is},l_{ijs})
, and
(h_{c},h_{i},h_{ij},h_{is},h_{ijs})
be the coefficient for the linear expansion of
l
and
h
w.r.t (
5.2
) respectively. Then we can derive the following system:

(5.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}a&=l_{c}+h_{c},\cr\partial_{x_{i}}a+\partial_{t}b_{i}&=l_{i}+h_{i},\cr\partial_{x_{i}}b_{i}+\partial_{t}c&=l_{ii}+h_{ii},\cr\partial_{x_{i}}b_{j}+\partial_{x_{j}}b_{i}&=l_{ij}+h_{ij}\quad(i\neq j),\cr\partial_{x_{i}}c+\partial_{t}d_{i}&=l_{is}+h_{is},\cr\partial_{x_{i}}d_{i}&=l_{iis}+h_{iis},\cr\partial_{x_{i}}d_{j}+\partial_{x_{j}}d_{i}&=l_{ijs}+h_{ijs}\quad(i\neq j),\end{split}

for
i,j=1,2,3
. For a notational simplicity we define

\displaystyle\tilde{l}

\displaystyle=l_{c}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq 3}\left(l_{i}+l_{is}\right)+\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 3}\left(l_{ij}+l_{ijs}\right),

\displaystyle\tilde{h}

\displaystyle=h_{c}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq 3}\left(h_{i}+h_{is}\right)+\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 3}\left(h_{ij}+h_{ijs}\right).

The desired full coercivity estimate is stated in the following theorem. Note that we need an additional moment condition on the initial data.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
Pr=0
and
|\alpha|\leq N
. Let
f
be the local smooth solution obtained in Theorem
3.5
. Suppose further that the third moment of the initial data is zero:

(5.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{0}(x,v)v_{i}|v|^{2}dvdx=0,

for
i=1,2,3
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\langle L_{Pr}\partial^{\alpha}f,\partial^{\alpha}f\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\leq-\delta\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+C\mathcal{E}^{2}(t).

The following estimate of macroscopic variables is the key estimate for the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Under the same assumption as in Theorem
5.1
, we have

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}a\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}b\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}c\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}d\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N-1}\left(\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{l}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{h}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\right)+C\mathcal{E}(t).

Proof.

Note that the new variable
d
is coupled only with
c
. Therefore, the estimates for
a
and
b
are the same as the previous
Pr>0
case, which is

(5.5)

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}a\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}b\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N-1}\left(\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{l}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{h}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\right).

We divide the estimate of
c
and
d
into the following four steps:

\displaystyle(\mbox{Step}~1)\quad\|\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{t}c\|_{L_{x}^{2}},

\displaystyle(\mbox{Step}~2)\quad\|\nabla_{x}\partial^{\alpha}d_{i}\|_{L_{x}^{2}},\quad(|\alpha|\leq N-1)

\displaystyle(\mbox{Step}~3)\quad\|\partial^{\gamma}d_{i}\|_{L_{x}^{2}},

\displaystyle(\mbox{Step}~4)\quad\|c\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|\nabla_{x}\partial^{\alpha}c\|_{L_{x}^{2}},

where
\partial^{\gamma}
is pure time derivatives for
|\gamma|\leq N
, that is,
\partial^{\gamma}=\partial_{t}^{|\gamma|}
.

\bullet

Step 1.
The estimate of
\|\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{t}c\|_{L_{x}^{2}}
: Taking
\partial^{\alpha}
on
\eqref{system}_{3}
gives

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{t}c

\displaystyle=\partial^{\alpha}l_{ii}+\partial^{\alpha}h_{ii}-\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{x_{i}}b_{i}.

Multiplying both sides by
\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{t}c
and applying the Hölder inequality yields

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{t}c\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial^{\alpha}l_{ii}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}h_{ii}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{x_{i}}b_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

We then combine this with the estimate of
b
in (
5.5
) to get

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{t}c\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N-1}\left(\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{l}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{h}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\right).

\bullet

Step 2.
The estimate of
\|\nabla_{x}\partial^{\alpha}d_{i}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}
: Using
\eqref{system}_{6}
and
\eqref{system}_{7}
, we compute

\displaystyle\triangle d_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq j\leq 3}\partial_{jj}d_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\neq i}\partial_{jj}d_{i}+\partial_{ii}d_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\neq i}\left(\partial_{j}l_{ijs}+\partial_{j}h_{ijs}-\partial_{ji}d_{j}\right)+\partial_{i}l_{iis}+\partial_{i}h_{iis}.

We then use
\eqref{system}_{6}
to get

\displaystyle\triangle d_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\neq i}\left(\partial_{j}l_{ijs}+\partial_{j}h_{ijs}-\partial_{i}l_{jjs}-\partial_{i}h_{jjs}\right)+\partial_{i}l_{iis}+\partial_{i}h_{iis},

which implies

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{x}d\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq C\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 3}(\|l_{ijs}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|h_{ijs}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|l_{iis}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|h_{iis}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}).

The same argument holds when
d_{i}
is replaced by
\partial^{\alpha}d_{i}
. This completes the proof of Step 2.

\bullet

Step 3.
The estimate of
\|\partial^{\gamma}d_{i}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}
: We divide the proof into the following 3 cases:
|\gamma|=0

|\gamma|=1
and
2\leq|\gamma|\leq N
. We start with
|\gamma|=1
.

(1) The case of
|\gamma|=1
: We employ the Poincaré inequality to derive

(5.6)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}d_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla_{x}\partial_{t}d_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+C\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\partial_{t}d_{i}dx\Big\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

Note that, in the case of
a
,
b
,
c
, the last term on the R.H.S. vanishes due to the conservation laws, which is not the case for non-conservative quantity
d
.
To control
\int\partial^{\gamma}d_{i}dx
, we multiply
v_{i}|v|^{2}
and integrate with respect to
dvdx
on the equation (
1.1
).

(5.7)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}Fv_{i}|v|^{2}dvdx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)-F)v_{i}|v|^{2}~dvdx.

We recall from
\eqref{viv2}_{2}
that the energy flux can be expressed as follows:

(5.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(x,v,t)v_{i}|v|^{2}dv

\displaystyle=q_{i}+\sum_{1\leq j\leq 3}2\rho U_{j}\Theta_{ij}+\rho U_{i}|U|^{2}+U_{i}\rho\sum_{1\leq i\leq 3}\Theta_{ii}.

On the other hand, an explicit computation using the following decomposition of
v_{i}|v|^{2}

v_{i}|v|^{2}=(v_{i}-U_{i})|v-U|^{2}+2(v_{i}-U_{i})(v-U)\cdot U+(v_{i}-U_{i})|U|^{2}\cr+U_{i}|v-U|^{2}+2U_{i}(v-U)\cdot U+U_{i}|U|^{2},

gives

(5.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)(x,v,t)v_{i}|v|^{2}dv

\displaystyle=(1-Pr)q_{i}+2U_{i}\rho T+\rho U_{i}|U|^{2}+U_{i}3\rho T.

Inserting (
5.8
) and (
5.9
) into (
5.7
), we get the following evolution law for the energy flux for
Pr=0
:

(5.10)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(x,v,t)v_{i}|v|^{2}dvdx=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\bigg(2U_{i}\rho T-\sum_{1\leq j\leq 3}2\rho U_{j}\Theta_{ij}\bigg)dx,

which, combined with the momentum conservation law:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(x,v,t)v~dvdx=0,

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{i}|v|^{2}mdv=0,

gives the evolution law for
d_{i}
:

(5.11)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}d_{i}(x,t)dx=\frac{1}{10\tau}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}2U_{i}\rho T-\sum_{1\leq j\leq 3}2\rho U_{j}\Theta_{ij}dx\bigg).

Then, from Lemma
3.1
,

U,~\rho T,~\rho\Theta\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}_{v}},

we get

(5.12)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\partial_{t}d_{i}(x,t)dx\bigg|=\bigg|\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}d_{i}(x,t)dx\bigg|\leq C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}^{2}dx\leq C\mathcal{E}(t).

Inserting this, into the Poincaré inequality (
5.6
), we get the desired estimate for
|\gamma|=1
:

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}d_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla_{x}\partial_{t}d_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+C\mathcal{E}(t).

(2) The case of
|\gamma|=0
:
We note from (
5.4
) that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}d_{i}(x,0)dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{v_{i}(|v|^{2}-5)}{10}F(x,v,0)~dvdx=0.

Therefore, integration (
5.11
) with respect to
dt
gives

(5.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}d_{i}(x,t)dx=\frac{1}{10\tau}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{2}{3}U_{i}\rho(\Theta_{11}+\Theta_{22}+\Theta_{33}-3\Theta_{ii})-\sum_{j\neq i}2\rho U_{j}\Theta_{ij}dxdt,\end{split}

where we used
3T=\Theta_{11}+\Theta_{22}+\Theta_{33}
.
To estimate the first term of the R.H.S., we observe

\displaystyle\rho(\Theta_{jj}-\Theta_{ii})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F\left(v_{j}^{2}-v_{i}^{2}\right)dv+\rho U_{j}^{2}-\rho U_{i}^{2}.

Therefore, substituting
F=m+\sqrt{m}f
and applying the Hölder inequality, we get

(5.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\big|\rho(\Theta_{jj}-\Theta_{ii})\big|&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(m+\sqrt{m}f)\left(v_{j}^{2}-v_{i}^{2}\right)dv+\frac{(\rho U_{j})^{2}+(\rho U_{i})^{2}}{\rho}\cr&\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}+C\|f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}^{2},\end{split}

where we used the lower bound of
\rho
in Lemma
3.1
(1) and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}m\left(v_{j}^{2}-v_{i}^{2}\right)dv=0.

We insert (
5.14
) into (
5.13
), and apply
\rho U/\rho\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}
and
\rho\Theta\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}
to obtain

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}d_{i}(x,t)dx\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}^{3}dxdt.

We then apply the Sobolev embedding
H^{2}\subset\subset L^{\infty}
to bound

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}^{3}dx\leq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\leq\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 2}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\leq\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}.

Therefore,

(5.15)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}d_{i}(x,t)dx\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq(1+\sqrt{M_{0}})\int_{0}^{t}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}dt\leq(1+\sqrt{M_{0}})\mathcal{E}(t),

where
M_{0}
is from Theorem
3.5
(1). We note that this is why we use the energy functional with the time integration of the production
term.
Combining (
5.15
) with (
5.12
) gives

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\partial^{\gamma}d_{i}(x,t)dx\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\sqrt{M_{0}})\mathcal{E}(t),

for
|\gamma|=0,1
. Substituting it into (
5.6
) yields

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\gamma}d_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla_{x}\partial^{\gamma}d_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+C(1+\sqrt{M_{0}})\mathcal{E}(t)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\partial^{\gamma}\tilde{l}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\gamma}\tilde{h}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\right)+C(1+\sqrt{M_{0}})\mathcal{E}(t),

where we used the result of (Step 2).

(3) The case of
2\leq|\gamma|\leq N
: We have from
\eqref{system}_{5}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{|\gamma|}d_{i}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}^{|\gamma|-1}l_{is}+\partial_{t}^{|\gamma|-1}h_{is}-\partial_{t}^{|\gamma|-1}\partial_{x_{i}}c.

Since the last term
\partial_{t}^{|\gamma|-1}\partial_{x_{i}}c
has at least one time derivative, we can apply the estimate of
\partial_{t}c
in (Step 1):

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{|\gamma|}d_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\partial_{t}^{|\gamma|-1}l_{is}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial_{t}^{|\gamma|-1}h_{is}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial_{t}^{|\gamma|-1}\partial_{x_{i}}c\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N-1}(\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{l}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{h}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}).

Finally, we combine (1)-(3) to get the desired result:

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\gamma}d_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N-1}(\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{l}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{h}\|_{L_{x}^{2}})+C\mathcal{E}(t).

\bullet

Step 4:
We first consider the estimate of
c
which has at least one spatial derivative. We take
\partial^{\alpha}
on
\eqref{system}_{5}
to get

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{x_{i}}c

\displaystyle=\partial^{\alpha}l_{is}+\partial^{\alpha}h_{is}-\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{t}d_{i}.

Using (Step 3), we have

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{x_{i}}c\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial^{\alpha}l_{is}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}h_{is}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}\partial_{t}d_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N-1}(\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{l}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{h}\|_{L_{x}^{2}})+C\mathcal{E}(t).

Finally, we use the Poincaré inequality to get the estimate of
c
without derivative:

\displaystyle\|c\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla_{x}c\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N-1}(\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{l}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{h}\|_{L_{x}^{2}})+C\mathcal{E}(t).

This completes the proof of Proposition
5.1
.
∎

Finally, we need to estimate the R.H.S. of (
5.1
).

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{E}(t)
is sufficiently small.
Then we have

\displaystyle(1)\ \sum_{|\alpha|\leq N-1}\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{l}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|(I-P_{Pr})\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}},

\displaystyle(2)\ \sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{h}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq CM_{0}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.

Proof.

This estimates are standard (See for example
[
23
,
24
,
48
]
). The only difference is that, as we can see in (
5.2
), the number of basis changes from
13
to
19
, so we omit it.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
5.1
.

5.1.
Proof of Theorem
5.1

From Proposition
5.1
, we have Lemma
5.2
,

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|P_{Pr}\partial^{\alpha}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\left(\|\partial^{\alpha}a\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}b\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}c\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}d\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N-1}\left(\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{l}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{h}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+C\mathcal{E}(t)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|(I-P_{Pr})\partial^{\alpha}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+CM_{0}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+C\mathcal{E}^{2}(t).

Adding
\sum\|(I-P_{Pr})\partial^{\alpha}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}
on both side, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|(I-P_{Pr})\partial^{\alpha}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+C\mathcal{E}^{2}(t),

for the sufficiently small
M_{0}
. This, combined with the degenerate coercivity estimate in Proposition
2.2
(2), lead to the following modified coercivity estimate for sufficiently small
\mathcal{E}(t)
:

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\langle L_{Pr}\partial^{\alpha}f,\partial^{\alpha}f\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\leq-\delta\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+C\mathcal{E}^{2}(t),

for some positive constant
\delta>0
. This completes the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

6.
Global existence

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
. We will only prove

(6.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq N\cr|\beta|\leq m\end{subarray}}\left\{C_{m_{1}}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+\delta_{m}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq C_{m_{2}}\mathcal{E}^{2}(t),

for some positive constants
C_{m_{1}}
,
C_{m_{2}}
, and
\delta_{m}
for
0\leq m\leq N
, since the standard argument in
[
23
,
24
,
48
]
leads to the desired result.

Proof of (
6.1
):
Let
f
be a local-in-time solution constructed in Theorem
3.5
. We apply the induction argument for the momentum derivative
|\beta|=m
. For
m=0
, taking
\partial^{\alpha}
on (
2.20
) and applying inner product with
\partial^{\alpha}f
give

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}=\frac{1}{\tau_{0}}\langle\partial^{\alpha}f,L_{Pr}\partial^{\alpha}f\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\langle\partial^{\alpha}f,\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma(f)\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.

We then apply the coercivity estimate (
4.3
) in the case
Pr>0
, and apply Theorem
5.1
in the degenerate case
Pr=0
, to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+\delta\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\langle\partial^{\alpha}f,\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma(f)\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.

For the nonlinear term, we apply Proposition
3.1
to have

\displaystyle\langle\partial^{\alpha}f,\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma(f)\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)}\sum_{|\alpha_{1}|+|\alpha_{2}|\leq|\alpha|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|\partial^{\alpha_{2}}f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{v}}dx.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
|\alpha_{1}|\leq|\alpha_{2}|
,
and employ the Sobolev embedding
H^{2}\subset\subset L^{\infty}
to get

\displaystyle\langle\partial^{\alpha}f,\partial^{\alpha}\Gamma(f)\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(t)}\bigg(\sum_{|\alpha_{1}|\leq|\alpha|}\|\partial^{\alpha_{1}}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\|\bigg)^{2}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\leq\mathcal{E}^{2}(t).

Thus we obtain the following estimate of
|\beta|=0
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}_{0}~\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}~\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+\delta\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{E}^{2}(t).

Now we consider the case
|\beta|=m>0
. We take
\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}
on (
2.20
)
and apply inner product with
\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f
.

(6.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}_{\beta}~\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}~&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau_{0}}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\cr&\leq\sum_{i=1}^{3}\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f,\partial^{\alpha+\bar{k}_{i}}_{\beta-k_{i}}\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\frac{1}{\tau_{0}}\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f,\partial_{\beta}P_{Pr}(\partial^{\alpha}f)\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f,\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}\Gamma(f)\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.\end{split}

The first two terms on the second line can be estimated by Young’s inequality:

\displaystyle\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f,\partial^{\alpha+\bar{k}_{i}}_{\beta-k_{i}}\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\|\partial^{\alpha+\bar{k}_{i}}_{\beta-k_{i}}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2},

and

\displaystyle\langle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f,\partial_{\beta}P_{Pr}(\partial^{\alpha}f)\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2},

where we used

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\beta}P_{Pr}(\partial^{\alpha}f)\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\leq\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}.

To estimate the last term of (
6.2
), we apply Proposition
3.1
.

\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}_{\beta}~\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}~\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau_{0}}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\cr\leq C_{\epsilon}\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\|\partial^{\alpha+\bar{k}_{i}}_{\beta-k_{i}}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+C\mathcal{E}^{2}(t).

For sufficiently small
\epsilon
, the right-hand side of
\|\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}
can be absorbed in the left-hand side of that. Once we take
\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq N}
and
\displaystyle\sum_{|\beta|=m+1}
on each side, then the 2-nd and the 3-rd terms of the second line is bounded by the induction hypothesis:

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq N\cr|\beta|=m+1\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{3}\|\partial^{\alpha+\bar{k}_{i}}_{\beta-k_{i}}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}\right)\leq C_{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq N\cr|\beta|\leq m\end{subarray}}\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}_{\beta}+C_{0}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}\leq C\mathcal{E}^{2}(t).

Thus we have the desired result. This completes the proof.

In this part, we prove the conservation laws (

Lemma 7.1
.

The Shakhov model satisfies the conservation laws (
1.6
) and the additional cancellation property (
1.8
).

Proof.

It is enough to show that

\displaystyle(1)~\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)(x,v,t)dv=\rho,

\displaystyle(2)~\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(v-U)\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)(x,v,t)dv=0,

\displaystyle(3)~\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v-U|^{2}\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)(x,v,t)dv=3\rho T,

\displaystyle(4)~\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)-F)(v_{i}-U_{i})|v-U|^{2}dv=-Prq_{i}.

(1) We integrate the Shakhov operator (
1.3
) with respect to
dv
and take the change of variable
(v-U)\rightarrow v
to get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)dv=\rho+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2\pi T}^{3}}\exp\left(-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2T}\right)\frac{1-Pr}{5}\frac{q\cdot v}{\rho T^{2}}\left(\frac{|v|^{2}}{2T}-\frac{5}{2}\right)dv=\rho.

(2) Multiplying
\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)
by
(v_{i}-U_{i})
and applying the change of variable
(v-U)\rightarrow v
yield

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(v_{i}-U_{i})\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{i}\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2\pi T}^{3}}\exp\left(-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2T}\right)\frac{1-Pr}{5}\frac{q\cdot v}{\rho T^{2}}\left(\frac{|v|^{2}}{2T}-\frac{5}{2}\right)dv

\displaystyle=\frac{1-Pr}{5}\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2\pi T}^{3}}\frac{1}{\rho T^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}q_{i}v_{i}^{2}\left(\frac{|v|^{2}}{2T}-\frac{5}{2}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2T}\right)dv

\displaystyle=\frac{1-Pr}{5}\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2\pi T}^{3}}\frac{1}{\rho T^{2}}\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}q_{i}|v|^{2}\left(\frac{|v|^{2}}{2T}-\frac{5}{2}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2T}\right)dv.

We then take another change of variable
v/\sqrt{2T}\rightarrow v
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(v_{i}-U_{i})\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)dv

\displaystyle=\frac{1-Pr}{15}\frac{q_{i}}{4\pi^{3/2}T^{4}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{4}e^{-|v|^{2}}dv-\frac{5}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{2}e^{-|v|^{2}}dv\right)=0,

where we used

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{4}e^{-|v|^{2}}dv=\frac{15\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}{4},\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{2}e^{-|v|^{2}}dv=\frac{3\pi^{\frac{3}{2}}}{2}.

(3) We integrate
\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)
with respect to
|v-U|^{2}dv
and the change of variable
(v-U)\rightarrow v
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v-U|^{2}\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)dv

\displaystyle=3\rho T+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{2}\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2\pi T}^{3}}\exp\left(-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2T}\right)\frac{1-Pr}{5}\frac{q\cdot v}{\rho T^{2}}\left(\frac{|v|^{2}}{2T}-\frac{5}{2}\right)dv

\displaystyle=3\rho T.

(4) Integrating the Shakhov operator
\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)
with respect to
(v_{i}-U_{i})|v-U|^{2}dv
gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)(v_{i}-U_{i})|v-U|^{2}dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{i}|v|^{2}\left[1+\frac{1-Pr}{5}\frac{q\cdot v}{\rho T^{2}}\left(\frac{|v|^{2}}{2T}-\frac{5}{2}\right)\right]\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2\pi T}^{3}}e^{-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2T}}dv

\displaystyle=\frac{1-Pr}{5}\frac{q_{i}}{\rho T^{2}}\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2\pi T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left[\left(\frac{v_{i}^{2}|v|^{4}}{2T}-\frac{5}{2}v_{i}^{2}|v|^{2}\right)\right]e^{-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2T}}dv

\displaystyle=\frac{1-Pr}{15}\frac{q_{i}}{\rho T^{2}}\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{2\pi T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left[\left(\frac{|v|^{6}}{2T}-\frac{5}{2}|v|^{4}\right)\right]e^{-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2T}}dv

\displaystyle=(1-Pr)q_{i}.

Then by the definition of the heat flux
q_{i}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)-F)(v_{i}-U_{i})|v-U|^{2}dv=-Prq_{i}.

∎

Lemma 7.2
.

[
40
]
The Shakhov model satisfies the
H
-theorem (
1.7
) when the distribution function
F(x,v,t)
is sufficiently close to the global Maxwellian in the sense that

\displaystyle|\rho-1|+|U|+|T-1|+|q|\ll 1.

Remark 7.3
.

We remark that the above smallness condition is satisfied by the solution derived in Theorem
1.1
.

Proof.

We take
(1+\ln F)dvdx
on both sides of (
1.1
):

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}F\ln Fdvdx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)-F)\ln Fdvdx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)-F)\ln\frac{F}{\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)}dvdx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)-F)\ln\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)dvdx.

Since the first term is non-positive, we only consider the second term.
We expand
\ln\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)
with respect to
q
as

\displaystyle\ln\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)=\ln\mathcal{M}+\frac{1-Pr}{5}\frac{(v-U)}{\rho T^{2}}\left(\frac{|v-U|^{2}}{2T}-\frac{5}{2}\right)\cdot q+O(q^{2}),

so that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)-F)\ln\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)dvdx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)-F)\times\left[\ln\mathcal{M}+\frac{1-Pr}{5}\frac{q\cdot(v-U)}{\rho T^{2}}\left(\frac{|v-U|^{2}}{2T}-\frac{5}{2}\right)+O(q^{2})\right]dvdx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{1-Pr}{5\rho T^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)-F)\times\left[q\cdot(v-U)\left(\frac{|v-U|^{2}}{2T}-\frac{5}{2}\right)\right]dvdx+O(q^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\frac{-Pr(1-Pr)|q|^{2}}{10\rho T^{3}}dx+O(q^{2}).

In the last line, we used

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\mathcal{S}_{Pr}(F)-F)(v-U)|v-U|^{2}dv=-Prq(x,t).

Since
\rho
and
T
have lower bounds by the assumption, for sufficiently small
q
, we have the desired result.
∎

Constructing 2 2 -dimensional Lubin-Tate formal groups over ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} (I)
Ramla Abdellatif ∗, Mabud Ali Sarkar †

Abstract
. In this paper, we construct a class of 2 2 -dimensional formal groups over ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} that provide a higher-dimensional analogue of the usual 1 1 -dimensional Lubin-Tate formal groups, then we initiate the study of the extensions generated by their p n p^{n} -torsion points. For instance, we prove that the coordinates of the p ∞ p^{\infty} -torsion points of such a formal group generate an abelian extension over a certain unramified extension of ℚ p \mathbb{Q}_{p} , and we study some ramification properties of these abelian extensions. In particular, we prove that the extension generated by the coordinates of the p p -torsion points is in general totally ramified.

11S31, 11S82 (primary); 11S15, 13F25 (secondary)

1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime integer. In 1964, Lubin and Tate
[
9
]
constructed a one-dimensional formal group, now refered to as the
Lubin-Tate formal group over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, and proved that the
p^{\infty}
-torsion points of this formal group generate a maximal totally ramified abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
that allowed them to recover (the opposite of) the reciprocity law at the core of local class field theory.
More precisely, set

(1.1)

\mathcal{C}_{LT}:=\{f(x)\in x\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[x]]~|~f(x)=px\ (\text{mod deg}\ 2)\ \text{and}\ f(x)\equiv x^{p}\ (\text{mod}\ p)\}.

Then, given any element
f\in C_{LT}
, Lubin and Tate proved that there is a unique one-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal group
F(x,y)
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that
f(F(x,y))=F(f(x),f(y))
, and that the zeros of the endomorphism
[p^{n}]_{F}
generate a totally ramified abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

Several authors built more or less satisfying extensions of the work done by Lubin and Tate. To state them, let us fix an unramified extension
U
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{U}
and maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
. For a fixed positive integer
d
, let
U^{nr}
denote the maximal unramified extension of degree
d
of
U
and let
\varphi:U^{nr}\to U^{nr}
denote the Frobenius automorphism of
U^{nr}
.

•

In the 80’s, Iwasawa
[
6
]
constructed a more general one-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal group
F
that satisfies
f(F(x,y))=(\varphi_{*}F)(f(x),f(y))
for any
f(x)\in\mathcal{C}_{LT}
, where
\varphi_{*}F
is obtained by applying
\varphi
to the coefficients of
F
.

•

Around the same time, De Shalit
[
12
]
introduced a
relative Lubin-Tate formal group
\mathcal{F}
, associated with
U/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, that produces a tower of abelian extensions
U(\mathcal{F}[\mathfrak{p}^{n}])/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
such that each extension
U(\mathcal{F}[\mathfrak{p}^{n}])/U
is totally ramified.

•

Twenty years later, Berger
[
2
]
showed that the torsion points of the relative Lubin-Tate formal group defined by De Shalit generate a tower of totally ramified extensions of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

All these constructions, however, provide one-dimensional formal groups. In the 70’s, Honda
[
5
]
and Hazewinkel
[
4
]
laid a path to build some higher-dimensional formal groups, but most attempts to follow this way were not pushed very far (see for instance the work of Koch
[
7
]
, whose constructions of higher dimensional Lubin-Tate groups actually amount to direct sums of one-dimensional Lubin-Tate groups) and the behaviour of higher dimensional Lubin-Tate groups still remains poorly understood. For the sake of completeness, let us also mention the work done by Nakamura
[
11
]
in positive characteristic, which provides some examples of
2
-dimensional formal groups over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
that are very close to
1
-dimensional formal groups too.

In 2020, Matson
[
10
]
extended Hazewinkel’s work in her PhD Thesis: she developed there some suitable family of higher-dimensional formal groups with complex multiplication under
“certain conditions”
, then used them to generate abelian extensions over some unramified extension of any given
p
-adic field.

This paper aims at filling partially this gap in dimension
2
, by introducing
actual
two-dimensional Lubin-Tate groups over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
(in the sense that they are not isomorphic to a direct sum of one-dimensional Lubin-Tate groups) and initiating the study of the extensions generated by their
p^{\infty}
-torsion points. It is organised as follows.

In Section
3
, we construct the aforementioned class of two-dimensional formal groups over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. We show that they have larger rings of endomorphisms than
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
when considered as defined over certain unramified extensions, hence they are a suitable analogue of one-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal groups over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

To provide a geometric understanding of our construction, we define in Section
4
the Newton copolygon associated with a
p
-adic power series in two variables.

In Section
5
, we start to investigate the properties of the extensions generated by the
p^{n}
-torsion points of these formal groups. In particular, we prove that for such a formal group
F
and any positive integer
n
, the coordinates of the
p^{n}
-torsion points of
F
generate a Galois extension (Proposition
5.1
), and that the
p^{\infty}
-torsion points of
F
generate an abelian extension over a certain unramified extension
U_{h}
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
(Theorem
5.2
). Finally, we show that when
h
is odd, the
p
-torsion points of
F
generate a totally abelian extension of
U_{h}
(Theorem
5.6
).

Two appendices are attached to this paper: Appendix
A
provides an alternative proof of Proposition
2.8
in the
2
-dimensional case, which is the one we are interested in in this paper, while Appendix
B
explains some limitations preventing a direct extension of our work beyond the
2
-dimensional case.

General notations

We fix a prime number
p
and let
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
denote the field of
p
-adic numbers,
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
denote its ring of integers (

),

\overline{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}

. We let
v:\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}\to\mathbb{Z}

.

, we let

\mathbb{Q}_{p}
in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. The ring of integers of

will be denoted by
\mathcal{U}_{k}
.

For any power
q
of

, we denote (as usual) by
\mathbb{F}_{q}

q

\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

2.2.
Some remainder on formal groups

We follow the definitions and notations given in
[
4
]
. The reader familiar with this setting can skip this section and move forward to Section
3
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring. Consider
n
-tuples of indeterminates
X=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
,
Y=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})
and
Z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
over
R
. Also denote by
\vec{0}=(0,\ldots 0)
the null
n
-tuple in
R^{n}
.

An
n
-dimensional formal group over
R
is an
n
-tuple power series
F(X,Y)=(F_{1}(X,Y),\cdots,F_{n}(X,Y))
in
R[[X,Y]]^{n}
that satisfies the four following properties.

(i)

F(X,Y)=X+Y\text{ mod degree 2 terms}
, i.e.
F_{i}(X,Y)=x_{i}+y_{i}\text{ mod degree 2 terms in $x_{i}$ and $y_{i}$}
for all
i\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
.

(ii)

Associativity :
F(F(X,Y),Z)=F(X,F(Y,Z))
.

(iii)

Identity element :
F(X,\vec{0})=F(\vec{0},X)=X
.

(iv)

Symmetry : There exists a unique power series
\iota(X)\in R[[X]]^{n}
such that
F(X,\iota(X))=\vec{0}=F(\iota(X),X)
.

Given an
n
-dimensional formal group
F
, we sometimes write
X+_{F}Y
instead of
F(X,Y)
.

Definition 2.2
.

An
n
-dimensional formal group
F
is said
commutative
when it satisfies

X+_{F}Y=Y+_{F}X\ .

Example 2.3
.

(i)

G_{a}(X,Y):=X+Y
and
G_{m}(X,Y):=X+Y+XY=(X+1)(Y+1)-1
are both 1-dimensional commutative formal groups, respectively called the
additive
and
multiplicative
formal groups over
R
.

(ii)

More generally, for any positive integer
n
, the
n
-dimensional additive formal group
is defined by

G^{(n)}_{a}(X,Y)=(G_{a,1}(X,Y),\cdots,G_{a,n}(X,Y))\ ,

where
G_{a,i}(X,Y)=x_{i}+y_{i}
is the
1
-dimensional additive formal group defined in
(i)
above, for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. This formal group law is usually denoted by
X+Y
.

(iii)

One can check as an exercise that the
2
-tuple power series

F(X,Y)=(F_{1}(X,Y),F_{2}(X,Y))=(x_{1}+y_{1}+x_{2}y_{2}\ ;\ x_{2}+y_{2})

defines a
2
-dimensional formal group over
R
.

The next definitions come from
[
4
, Section 9.4]
and provide the suitable notions of (iso)morphism of formal groups over a domain
R
.

Definition 2.4
.

Given two positive integers
m
and
n
, let
F(X,Y)
be an
m
-dimensional formal group over
R
and
G(X,Y)
be an
n
-dimensional formal group over
R
.

•

A
homomorphism
f:F(X,Y)\to G(X,Y)
over
R
is an
n
-tuple power series in
m
indeterminates
f(X)=(f_{1}(X),\cdots,f_{n}(X))
with coefficients in
R
, such that
f(X)\equiv 0
mod degree 1 terms and
f(X+_{F}Y)=f(X)+_{G}f(Y)
, i.e.
f(F(X,Y))=G(f(X),f(Y))
.

•

Such a homomorphism is an
isomorphism over
R
when there exists a homomorphism
g(X):G(X,Y)\to F(X,Y)
over
R
such that
f(g(X))=X=g(f(X))
.

Remark 2.5
.

Note that a necessary and sufficient condition for
f
to be an isomorphism is that its Jacobian matrix
J(f):=\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}f_{i}(0)\right]_{i,j=1}^{n}
is invertible (see
[
4
, Section 9.4]
).

Example 2.6
.

For any integer
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and any formal group
F(X,Y)
over
R
, one defines recursively the multiplication-by-
k
homomorphism
[k]_{F}:F(X,Y)\to F(X,Y)
as follows :

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}[0]_{F}(X)=\vec{0}\ ;\ [1]_{F}(X)=X;\\
\text{if $k\geq 2$, then }[k]_{F}(X):=X+_{F}[k-1]_{F}(X)=F(X,[k-1]_{F}(X))\\
\text{if $k<0$, then }[k]_{F}(X):=\iota([-k]_{F}(X))\ .\end{array}\right.

Definition 2.7
.

An isomorphism
f:F(X,Y)\to G(X,Y)
is said to be
strict
when its Jacobian matrix
J(f)
is the identity matrix. This amounts to say that
f(X)\equiv X
mod degree 2 terms.

The next proposition provides what we need to define the logarithm of a formal group. Its proof for
1
-dimensional formal groups is provided by
[
4
, Section 5.4]
. For higher-dimensional formal groups, it goes virtually the same, but for the sake of completeness, we give in Appendix
A
a full proof for
2
-dimensional formal groups over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, which is the case we are interested in.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
R
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-algebra of characteristic
0
that is
p
-adically separated and
n
be a positive integer.
Let
F(X,Y)
be an
n
-dimensional formal group over
R
. For any integer
k\geq 0
, set

\displaystyle L_{k}(X):=p^{-k}[p^{k}]_{F}(X)\ ,

where
[p]_{F}
is a multiplication by
p
endomorphism of
F
. Then
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}L_{k}(X)
converges (in
R\otimes\mathbb{Q}[[X]]
) to a power series
L(X)
over
R
that satisfies
J(L)=\mathrm{I}_{n}
and
L(X+_{F}Y)=L(X)+L(Y)
.

In particular,
L
defines a strict isomorphism from
F(X,Y)
to the
n
-dimensional additive formal group
G^{(n)}_{a}
.

We end up this preliminary section by introducing an arithmetic invariant of formal group laws, following
[
4
, Section 18.3]
for the positive characteristic case, and extending it to local rings of characteristic
0
(e.g. finite extensions of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
) by reduction modulo
p
(see
[
4
, (18.3.10)]
).

Definition 2.9
.

Assume that
\mathbb{F}
is a perfect field of characteristic
p
, that
n
is a positive integer and that
F(X,Y)
is an
n
-dimensional formal group over
\mathbb{F}
. Write
[p]_{F}(X)=(H_{1}(X),\ldots,H_{n}(X))
. Then
F
is said to be
of finite height
if the ring
\mathbb{F}[[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]]
is a finitely generated free module over the subring
\mathbb{F}[[H_{1}(X),\ldots,H_{n}(X)]]
. In this case, the rank of this module is of the form
p^{h}
for some integer
h\geq 0
: the latter is called the
height of
F(X,Y)
.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
R
be a local ring of characteristic
0
and residue field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic
p
.
Let
F(X,Y)
be an
n
-dimensional formal group over
R
. We define the
height of
F(X,Y)
as the height (as given by Definition
2.9
) of its reduction over
\mathbb{F}
(i.e. modulo the maximal ideal of
R
).

Example 2.11
.

(i)

The multiplicative group
\mathbb{G}_{m}
has height
1
.

(ii)

For any integer
n\geq 1
, the
n
-dimensional additive formal group
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{(n)}
is of infinite height.

(iii)

Let us consider the
2
-dimensional formal group defined over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
(as in Example
2.3
) by

F(X,Y)=\displaystyle\left(x_{1}+y_{1}+x_{2}y_{2};\ x_{2}+y_{2}\right)\ ,

and assume
p\geq 3
. An immediate recursion shows that for any integer
n\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle[n]_{F}(X)=\left(nx_{1}+\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}x_{2}^{2}\ ;\ nx_{2}\right)\ .

For
n=p
, the corresponding formal group over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
has its multiplication by
p
endomorphism given by :

\displaystyle[p]_{F}(X)\text{ mod }p=\left(\alpha x_{2}^{2}\ ;\ 0\right)\ ,

where
\alpha
denotes the reduction modulo
p
of
\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}
. This proves that this formal group is not of finite height, as the
\mathbb{F}_{p}[[x_{2}^{2}]]
-module
\mathbb{F}_{p}[[x_{1},x_{2}]]
is not of finite rank.

3.
Constructing 2-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal group laws over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}

In this section, we extend the 1-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal groups over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
as originally defined by Lubin and Tate to the following
2
-dimensional analogue. Unless otherwise stated, we set
X=(x_{1},x_{2})
and
Y=(y_{1},y_{2})
in this section.

Given two positive integers
k,\ell
that are relatively prime, we first set

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{LT}^{k,\ell}:=\{f(X)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[X]]^{2}\ |\ f(X)\equiv(px_{1},px_{2})\text{ mod degree 2 and }f(X)\text{ mod }p\in\{(x_{1}^{p^{k}},x_{2}^{p^{\ell}})\ ;\ (x_{2}^{p^{k}},x_{1}^{p^{\ell}}))\}\ .

We then define
\mathcal{C}_{LT}
as the union of all
\mathcal{C}_{LT}^{k,\ell}
for
(k,\ell)
running over pairs of relatively prime positive integers :

(3.2)

\mathcal{C}_{LT}:=\displaystyle\bigcup_{k>0,\ \ell>0\ |\ \gcd(k,\ell)=1}\mathcal{C}_{LT}^{k,\ell}\ .

Let us already point out that, unlike the
1
-dimensional case, not all elements of
\mathcal{C}_{LT}
will be associated with a (
2
-dimensional) Lubin-Tate formal group: we provide in Remark
3.10
below an explicit counter-example in this direction.

The key to construct a
2
-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal group with multiplication by
p
given by a prescribed element
f
of
\mathcal{C}_{LT}
is to define this formal group via its logarithm, as introduced in Proposition
2.8
: we then obtain the formal group we want by exponentiation of this logarithm. Note that the properties of this logarithm, hence of the
2
-dimensional formal group build from it, will later impose some necessary conditions on our choice of
f
to ensure that it defines an endomorphism of the formal group. This makes a huge difference with the
1
-dimensional case, where any
f
was eligible (see
[
9
]
).

3.1.
The functional equation lemma

Following
[
4
, Section 10]
, we recall some notation and results required to build higher-dimensional formal groups as claimed above.

Let
R
be a discrete valuation ring with residue field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic
p
and let
K=\mathrm{Frac}(R)
be the fraction field of
R
. Let
I
be an ideal of
R
and let
\sigma:K\to K
be a field endomorphism that behaves on
R
like a Frobenius morphism modulo
I
, i.e. such that there exists a power
q
of
p
satisfying:

\displaystyle\forall\ x\in R,\sigma(x)\equiv x^{q}\mod I\ .

Let
(s_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{*}}
be a family of
n\times n
matrices with coefficients in
K
such that :

(3.3)

\forall\ i\geq 1,\ s_{i}I\subset R\ .

Let
n
and
m
be positive integers. Given an
n
-tuple of power series
g(X)=g(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
with coefficients in
R
and without constant terms (i.e. having all its components in
XR[[X]]
), we construct an
n
-tuple of power series
L_{g}(X)
by means of the following recursion formula (also called
functional equation
) :

(3.4)

\displaystyle L_{g}(X)=g(X)+\sum_{i\geq 1}s_{i}\sigma_{*}^{i}L_{g}(X^{q^{i}})\ ,

where
\sigma_{*}^{i}L_{g}(X)
is obtained from
L_{g}(X)
by applying the endomorphism
\sigma^{i}
to the coefficients of the
n
-tuple of power series
L_{g}(X)
, and where we set for short
X^{q^{i}}:=(x_{1}^{q^{i}},\ldots,x_{m}^{q^{i}})
.

Definition 3.1
.

With the notation above, if
g
satisfies the recursion relation (
3.4
), we say that
L_{g}(X)
satisfies
a functional equation of type
(s_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{*}}
.

The next statement, known as the
n
-dimensional functional equation lemma

[
4
, (10.2)]
, will be one of our key tools to build
2
-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal groups.

Lemma 3.2
.

Keep the same notation as above and define
L_{g}(X)
by the recursion formula (
3.4
). Also assume that the Jacobian matrix
J(L_{g})
of
L_{g}
is invertible. Then the following hold.

(1)

There is a unique
n
-tuple of power series
L_{g}^{-1}(X)
such that

L_{g}(L_{g}^{-1}(X))=X=L_{g}^{-1}(L_{g}(X))\ .

(2)

The
n
-tuple of power series
F_{g}(X,Y):=L_{g}^{-1}(L_{g}(X)+L_{g}(Y))
has coefficients in
R
.

(3)

For any
n
-tuple of power series
h(Y)\in(YR[[Y]])^{n}
in
m
indeterminates, the
n
-tuple of power series
L_{g}^{-1}(L_{h}(Y))
has coefficients in
R
, and
L_{g}(h(Y))
satisfies a functional equation of type
(s_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{*}}
.

(4)

Assume that
\gamma(X)
is an
n
-tuple of power series with coefficients in
R
and that
\delta(X)
is an
n
-tuple of power series with coefficients in
K
(both with
m
indeterminates). Then :
\forall\ r\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\left(\gamma(X)\equiv\delta(X)\ \mathrm{mod}\ I^{r}\right)\iff\left(L_{g}(\gamma(X))\equiv L_{g}(\delta(X))\ \mathrm{mod}\ I^{r}\right)\ .

3.2.
Application to
2
-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal groups over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}

Using the functional equation lemma, we now construct a
2
-dimensional analogue of the logarithm for
1
-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal groups over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, from which we will deduce an appropriate
2
-dimensional analogue of Lubin-Tate formal groups over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Let
R:=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, so that
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is the field of
p
-adic numbers and
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{p}
is the field with
p
elements. Let
I=p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
be the maximal ideal of
R
and
q=p
be the cardinality of the residue field
\mathbb{F}
: we can then take
\sigma=\mathrm{Id}
. Let
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
be fixed relatively prime positive integers, and consider the family
(s_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{*}}
of
2\times 2
matrices with coefficients in
p^{-1}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\subset K
defined as follows:

\displaystyle s_{h_{1}}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cl}0&p^{-1}\\
0&0\end{array}\right)\ ,\ s_{h_{2}}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
p^{-1}&0\end{array}\right)\ \text{ and }\ s_{i}=0\text{ if }i\not\in\{h_{1},h_{2}\}\ .

Finally, let
g(X)=X=(x_{1},x_{2})\in(XR[[X]])^{2}
. Then we can define
L_{g}
by the recursive formula (
3.4
). This candidate for the logarithm of our
2
-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal group satisfies the following nice relation.

Proposition 3.3
.

With the notation above, the logarithm
L_{g}(X)=(L_{1}(x_{1},x_{2}),L_{2}(x_{1},x_{2}))
satisfies the following recursion formulas:

(3.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}L_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{1}+p^{-1}L_{2}(x_{1}^{p^{h_{1}}},x_{2}^{p^{h_{1}}})\\
L_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{2}+p^{-1}L_{1}(x_{1}^{p^{h_{2}}},x_{2}^{p^{h_{2}}})\ .\end{array}\right.

In particular, this provides the following explicit formulas for the power series composing
L_{g}(X)
:

(3.6)

\displaystyle\displaystyle L_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{1}+\sum_{k\geq 1}p^{-2k}x_{1}^{p^{k(h_{1}+h_{2})}}+\sum_{k\geq 0}p^{-(2k+1)}x_{2}^{p^{h_{1}+k(h_{1}+h_{2})}}\ ;

(3.7)

\displaystyle\displaystyle L_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{2}+\sum_{k\geq 1}p^{-2k}x_{2}^{p^{k(h_{1}+h_{2})}}+\sum_{k\geq 0}p^{-(2k+1)}x_{1}^{p^{h_{2}+k(h_{1}+h_{2})}}\ .

Proof.

Applying the recursion formula (
3.4
) in our setting directly provides (
3.5
), since the only two non-zero elements in the family
(s_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{*}^{+}}
are
s_{h_{1}}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cl}0&p^{-1}\\
0&0\end{array}\right)
and
s_{h_{2}}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
p^{-1}&0\end{array}\right)
:

\displaystyle L_{g}(X)=\begin{pmatrix}L_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})\\
L_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{1}{p}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}L_{1}(x_{1}^{p^{h_{1}}},x_{2}^{p^{h_{1}}})\\
L_{2}(x_{1}^{p^{h_{1}}},x_{2}^{p^{h_{1}}})\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
\frac{1}{p}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}L_{1}(x_{1}^{p^{h_{2}}},x_{2}^{p^{h_{2}}})\\
L_{2}(x_{1}^{p^{h_{2}}},x_{2}^{p^{h_{2}}})\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}+\frac{1}{p}L_{2}(x_{1}^{p^{h_{1}}},x_{2}^{p^{h_{1}}})\\
x_{2}+\frac{1}{p}L_{1}(x_{1}^{p^{h_{2}}},x_{2}^{p^{h_{2}}})\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=X+\frac{1}{p}\begin{pmatrix}L_{2}(x_{1}^{p^{h_{1}}},x_{2}^{p^{h_{1}}})\\
L_{1}(x_{1}^{p^{h_{2}}},x_{2}^{p^{h_{2}}})\end{pmatrix}\ .

We use the same kind of recursion to obtain the explicit formulae (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) by induction. Indeed, we directly have that :

\displaystyle L_{g}(X)

\displaystyle=X+s_{h_{1}}L_{g}(X^{p^{h_{1}}})+s_{h_{2}}L_{g}(X^{p^{h_{2}}})

\displaystyle=X+s_{h_{1}}\left(X^{p^{h_{1}}}+s_{h_{1}}L_{g}(X^{p^{2h_{1}}})+s_{h_{2}}L_{g}(X^{p^{h_{1}+h_{2}}})\right)+s_{h_{2}}\left(X^{p^{h_{2}}}+s_{h_{1}}L_{g}(X^{p^{h_{2}+h_{1}}})+s_{h_{2}}L_{g}(X^{p^{2h_{2}}})\right)

\displaystyle=X+s_{h_{1}}X^{p^{h_{1}}}+s_{h_{2}}X^{p^{h_{2}}}+(s_{h_{1}}s_{h_{2}}+s_{h_{2}}s_{h_{1}})L_{g}(X^{p^{h_{1}+h_{2}}})+s_{h_{1}}^{2}L_{g}(X^{p^{2h_{1}}})+s_{h_{2}}^{2}L_{g}(X^{p^{2h_{2}}})

\displaystyle=X+\frac{1}{p}\begin{pmatrix}x_{2}^{p^{h_{1}}}\\
0\end{pmatrix}+\frac{1}{p}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
x_{1}^{p^{h_{2}}}\end{pmatrix}+(s_{h_{1}}s_{h_{2}}+s_{h_{2}}s_{h_{1}})L_{g}(X^{p^{h_{1}+h_{2}}})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}+p^{-1}x_{2}^{p^{h_{1}}}\\
x_{2}+p^{-1}x_{1}^{p^{h_{2}}}\end{pmatrix}+p^{-2}L_{g}(X^{p^{h_{1}+h_{2}}})\ ,

since
s_{h_{1}}
and
s_{h_{2}}
are both nilpotent of order
2
while
s_{h_{1}}s_{h_{2}}+s_{h_{2}}s_{h_{1}}=p^{-2}\mathrm{Id}
. Let us go one step further to see how the induction process actually works. Replacing
X
by
X^{p^{h_{1}+h_{2}}}
in the previous calculations provides that

\displaystyle L_{g}(X^{p^{h_{1}+h_{2}}})=X^{p^{h_{1}+h_{2}}}+\frac{1}{p}\begin{pmatrix}x_{2}^{p^{2h_{1}+h_{2}}}\\
0\end{pmatrix}+\frac{1}{p}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
x_{1}^{p^{h_{1}+2h_{2}}}\end{pmatrix}+p^{-2}L_{g}(X^{p^{2(h_{1}+h_{2})}})\ ,

hence that

\displaystyle L_{g}(X)=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}+p^{-1}x_{2}^{p^{h_{1}}}\\
x_{2}+p^{-1}x_{1}^{p^{h_{2}}}\end{pmatrix}+p^{-2}\left[\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}^{p^{h_{1}+h_{2}}}+p^{-1}x_{2}^{p^{2h_{1}+h_{2}}}\\
x_{2}^{p^{h_{1}+h_{2}}}+p^{-1}x_{1}^{p^{h_{1}+2h_{2}}}\end{pmatrix}+p^{-2}L_{g}(X^{p^{2(h_{1}+h_{2})}})\right]\ .

This can be rewritten as

(3.8)

L_{g}(X)=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}+p^{-1}x_{2}^{p^{h_{1}}}+p^{-2}x_{1}^{p^{h_{1}+h_{2}}}+p^{-3}x_{2}^{p^{2h_{1}+h_{2}}}\\
x_{2}+p^{-1}x_{1}^{p^{h_{2}}}+p^{-2}x_{2}^{p^{h_{1}+h_{2}}}+p^{-3}x_{1}^{p^{h_{1}+2h_{2}}}\end{pmatrix}+p^{-4}L_{g}(X^{p^{2(h_{1}+h_{2})}})\ ,

which shows exactly the first terms appearing in (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) – for
k=0
and
k=1
. The shape of the residue in (
3.8
) ensures that a direct induction on
n\geq 2
(to transform the residue
p^{-2n}L_{g}(X^{p^{n(h_{1}+h_{2})}})
into
p^{-2(n+1)}L_{g}(X^{p^{(n+1)(h_{1}+h_{2})}})
) provides the infinite sum formulae (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) of the statement and hence, it finishes the proof.
∎

This proposition ensures in particular that
J(L_{g})=\mathrm{I}_{2}
is invertible; hence, we can apply the
2
-dimensional functional equation (Lemma
3.2
) to
L_{g}
and set the following definition.

Definition 3.4
.

We define the
2
-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal group
F(X,Y)
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
by

\displaystyle F(X,Y):=L^{-1}(L(X)+L(Y))\ ,

where
L=L_{g}
is the logarithm formal series provided by Proposition
3.3
.

Remark 3.5
.

The formal group introduced in Definition
3.4
depends on the pair of relatively prime integers
\vec{h}=(h_{1},h_{2})
, hence we actually defined a family of
2
-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal groups over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, as announced.

3.3.
On endomorphisms of these formal groups

As above, we fix a pair
\vec{h}=(h_{1},h_{2})
of positive integers that are relatively prime and we denote by
F(X,Y)
the
2
-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal group over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
associated with
\vec{h}
by Definition
3.4
. The next proposition imposes explicit conditions on the multiplication-by-
p
endomorphism of
F(X,Y)
.

Proposition 3.6
.

The endomorphism
[p]_{F}(X)
must satisfy

(3.9)

[p]_{F}(x_{1},x_{2})\equiv(px_{1},px_{2})\mathrm{\ mod\ degree\ 2\ and\ }[p]_{F}(x_{1},x_{2})\equiv(x_{2}^{p^{h_{1}}},x_{1}^{p^{h_{2}}})\mathrm{\ mod\ }p\ .

In particular, it is an element of
\mathcal{C}^{h_{1},h_{2}}_{LT}\subset\mathcal{C}_{LT}
(see (
3.1
)).

Proof.

Recall that, by definition of multiplication-by-
p
in a formal group (see Example
2.6
), we have
[p]_{F}(X)=F(X,[p-1]_{F}(X))
. The formula provided by Definition
3.4
then ensures that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}[p]_{F}(X)&=&L^{-1}(L(X)+L([p-1]_{F}(X)))\\
&=&L^{-1}(L(X)+L(F(X,[p-2]_{F}(X))))\\
&=&L^{-1}(L(X)+L(X)+L([p-2]_{F}(X))\\
&=&L^{-1}(2L(X)+L([p-2]_{F}(X)))\ .\end{array}

Since
p\geq 2
and
[0]_{F}(X)=(0,0)
, we obtain recursively that

(3.10)

[p]_{F}(X)=L^{-1}(pL(X))\ ,

which already proves (by Lemma
3.2
) that the tuple of power series defining the endomorphism
[p]_{F}
has coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, while Proposition
2.8
certifies that it is an endomorphism of
F(X,Y)
. Now, note that we can rewrite (
3.10
) as

(3.11)

\displaystyle L([p]_{F}(X))=pL(X)\ .

Using Proposition
3.3
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}L([p]_{F}(X))&=&pX+\begin{pmatrix}x_{2}^{p^{h_{1}}}+\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}p^{-2k}x_{2}^{p^{h_{1}+k(h_{1}+h_{2})}}+\sum_{k\geq 0}p^{-(2k+1)}x_{1}^{p^{(k+1)(h_{1}+h_{2})}}\\
x_{1}^{p^{h_{2}}}+\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}p^{-2k}x_{1}^{p^{h_{2}+k(h_{1}+h_{2})}}+\sum_{k\geq 0}p^{-(2k+1)}x_{2}^{p^{(k+1)(h_{2}+h_{1})}}\end{pmatrix}\\
&&\\
&=&pX+L(\Phi(X))\end{array}

with
\Phi(X)=(x_{2}^{p^{h_{1}}},x_{1}^{p^{h_{2}}})\in X^{2}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[X]]
. On the one hand, this directly shows that

(3.12)

\displaystyle L([p]_{F}(X))\equiv L(\Phi(X))\mathrm{\ mod\ }p\ ,

thus the last statement of Lemma
3.2
implies that
[p]_{F}(X)\equiv\Phi(X)\mathrm{\ mod\ }p
, which is the mod
p
congruence we claimed. On the other hand, equality (
3.11
) obviously implies that

(3.13)

\displaystyle L([p]_{F}(X))\equiv pL(X)\mathrm{\ mod\ degree\ 2\ terms}\ .

The formulae for the logarithm given by Proposition
3.3
allow us to rewrite this congruence as

(3.14)

\displaystyle[p]_{F}(X)\equiv pX\mathrm{\ mod\ degree\ 2\ terms}\ ,

which is exactly what we claimed, and the proof is now complete.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

From the modulo
p
congruence provided by Proposition
3.6
, we see that
\mathbb{F}_{p}[[x_{1},x_{2}]]
is free of finite rank
p^{h_{1}+h_{2}}
as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}[[x_{1}^{p^{h_{2}}},x_{2}^{p^{h_{1}}}]]
-module, which means that the
2
-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal group
F
we built is of finite height
h_{1}+h_{2}
(see Definition
2.9
).

The way we defined multiplication-by-
p
for the
2
-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal group
F
introduced in Definition
3.4
can be used to define, more generally, multiplication by any element of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
in
F
as follows.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
F
be the
2
-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal group given by Definition
3.4
and
a
be any element of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Then the formula

(3.15)

[a]_{F}(X):=L^{-1}(aL(X))

defines an endomorphism of
F
(with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
).

Proof.

First note that if
a
is an integer, then the first part of the proof of Proposition
3.6
with
[p]_{F}
replaced by
[a]_{F}
works the same, hence (
3.9
) is satisfied by
[a]_{F}
instead of
[p]_{F}
and the same argument as for
[p]_{F}
shows that
[a]_{F}
is an endomorphism of
F
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

For arbitrary
a
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, one can either use a continuity argument to deduce the result from the previous case, or notice that multiplying by
a
the recursion formula of Proposition
3.3
shows that
aL(X)
satisfies the same functional equation as
L(X)
: this allows us to use Lemma
3.2
and Proposition
2.8
(as we did for
[p]_{F}
in the proof of Proposition
3.6
) to conclude.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

Let us point out here that a direct induction from (
3.11
) ensures that

(3.16)

\forall\ n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},\ \displaystyle[p^{n}]_{F}(X)=L^{-1}(p^{n}L(X))\ .

Remark 3.10
.

As we claimed earlier, not every element of
\mathcal{C}_{LT}
is a priori a good candidate to provide an endomorphism of a
2
-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal group. Indeed, assume that
h_{1},h_{2}
are both greater than or equal to
2
and consider the element
f_{\vec{h}}\in\mathcal{C}^{h_{1},h_{2}}_{LT}
defined by

(3.17)

f_{\vec{h}}(x_{1},x_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}px_{1}+x_{1}^{p^{h_{1}}}\\
px_{2}+x_{2}^{p^{h_{2}}}\end{pmatrix}\ .

One directly checks that it cannot provide a multiplication-by-
p
endormorphism since it does not satisfy the same relation as (
3.12
) with
[p]_{F}
replaced by
f_{\vec{h}}
. Note that the same kind of argument (based on Proposition
3.8
) proves that
f_{\vec{h}}
cannot provide a multiplication-by-
a
endomorphism for any
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, and the density of
\mathbb{Z}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
then ensures that
f_{\vec{h}}
cannot provide any endomorphism of
F
defined by multiplication by a scalar in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Using the logarithm recursion formulae proven in Proposition
3.3
and doing a little more calculation, one can actually prove that
f_{\vec{h}}
cannot provide any endomorphism of
F
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

4.
Torsion points and Newton copolygon of
2
-dimensional formal groups

Let
\vec{h}=(h_{1},h_{2})
be a pair of positive coprime integers and let
F
be the
2
-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal group associated with
\vec{h}
by Definition
3.4
. Recall that in this setting, the formal series
L
is called the
logarithm of the formal group
F
.

The goal of this section is to define the
p^{n}
-torsion part of
F
for any integer
n\geq 1
, to give some basic properties of it (a deeper study will be initiated in the next section), then to provide a geometric point of view on all these objects by introducing the Newton copolygon associated with
F
and studying some explicit examples.

4.1.
The
p^{\infty}
-torsion of a
2
-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal group

For any integer
n\geq 2
, we define the
p^{n}
-torsion points of
F
by

(4.1)

\displaystyle F[p^{n}]=\{X\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{2}\ |\ [p^{n}]_{F}(X)=\vec{0}\ \}\ .

Since the solutions of
[p^{n}]_{F}(X)=\vec{0}
define an affine
2
-space over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, the set of all coordinates of the points of
F[p^{n}]
generates a field extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
that will be denoted by
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(F[p^{n}])
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any integer
n\geq 2
,
F[p^{n}]
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module.

Proof.

Let
n\geq 2
be an integer and let
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(F)
denote the ring of endomorphisms of
F
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. By Proposition
3.8
, we have a map
\mu:\left[a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to[a]_{F}\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(F)\right]
that actually happens (by (
3.15
)) to be a ring homomorphism. One can then check that
F[p^{n}]
is actually a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module for the addition law defined by
F
(i.e.
\alpha+\beta:=F(\alpha,\beta)
) and for the scalar multiplication defined by
\mu
(i.e.
a\cdot\alpha:=\mu(a)(\alpha)=[a]_{F}(\alpha)
).
∎

One of the most interesting features of these torsion points is given by the following statement.

Theorem 4.2
.

For any integer
n\geq 1
, the solutions of
[p^{n}]_{F}(X)=\vec{0}
are all multiplicity one.

Proof.

Thanks to Remark
3.9
, we are reduced to prove the result for
n=1
. Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
be such that
[p]_{F}(\alpha)=\vec{0}
. Since
[p]_{F}
is an endomorphism of
F
, we have

(4.2)

[p]_{F}(X+\alpha)=[p]_{F}(X)+[p]_{F}(\alpha)=[p]_{F}(X)\ .

Let us write
\delta[p]_{F}
for the differential map associated with
[p]_{F}
and
F(X,Y)=(F_{1}(X,Y),F_{2}(X,Y))
, and recall that
X+\alpha=F(X,\alpha)
by definition. Then, differenciating (
4.2
) with respect to
X
shows that

\displaystyle\delta[p]_{F}(X+\alpha)\partial_{X}F(X,\alpha)=\delta[p]_{F}(X)\ .

Evaluating this equality at
X=\vec{0}
ensures in particular that

(4.3)

\delta[p]_{F}(\alpha)\partial_{X}F(\vec{0},\alpha)=\delta[p]_{F}(\vec{0})\ ,\text{ i.e. }\delta[p]_{F}(\alpha)\partial_{X}F(\vec{0},\alpha)=p\mathrm{I}_{2}\ .

(The last equality follows from (
3.10
) and
J(L)=\mathrm{I}_{2}
.)

Now, recall that by definition of formal groups,
F(X,Y)
satisfies

\displaystyle F(X,Y)\equiv X+Y\textrm{ mod degree }2\textrm{ terms; }

hence, we have
F_{i}(X,Y)\equiv x_{i}+y_{i}\textrm{ mod degree $2$ terms}
for any
i\in\{1,2\}
. This implies that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial F_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq 2}=\mathrm{I}_{2}\ ,\text{ i.e. }\partial_{X}F\equiv\mathrm{Id}\ .

Replacing this in (
4.3
), we conclude that
\delta[p]_{F}(\alpha)=p\mathrm{I}_{2}
is invertible, hence
\alpha
is a multiplicity-free solution of
[p]_{F}(X)=\vec{0}
, as claimed.
∎

The next proposition shows that the
p^{n}
-torsion part of a
2
-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal group only depends on the functional equation satisfied by the logarithm of this formal group.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
G
be a
2
-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal group (in the sense of Definition
3.4
) whose logarithm satisfies a functional equation of the same type
(s_{i})_{i\geq 1}
as
L
(in the sense of Definition
3.1
). Then, for any integer
n\geq 2
, we have
F[p^{n}]\cong G[p^{n}]
as
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules.

Proof.

Let
L_{\gamma}
denote the logarithm of the formal group
G
, with
\gamma
being an
n
-tuple of power series with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and no constant terms. We then have, by definition,

\displaystyle G(X,Y)=L_{\gamma}^{-1}(L_{\gamma}(X)+L_{\gamma}(Y))\ .

Our assumption means that the functional equation (
3.4
) satisfied by
L_{\gamma}
involves the same family
(s_{i})_{i\geq 1}
of matrices than the functional equation of
L
. The functional equation lemma (Lemma
3.2
) then ensures that
L_{\gamma}^{-1}
is well-defined and that
L_{\gamma}^{-1}\circ L
has coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. It is now straightforward to check that
L_{\gamma}^{-1}\circ L
actually defines an isomorphism of formal groups from
F
to
G
, which implies in particular that their
p^{n}
-torsion points provide isomorphic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules for any integer
n\geq 1
. ∎

Thanks to Proposition
4.3
, we can choose any convenient
\gamma
such that
L_{\gamma}
satisfies the same functional equation as
L
to study the
p^{\infty}
-torsion part of
F
via its counterpart for the
2
-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal group associated with
L_{\gamma}
. Considering the formulae given by Proposition
3.6
for
[p]_{F}
, it is natural to introduce the following dynamical system
\mathcal{D}_{\vec{h}}
as a good approximation of
[p]_{F}
:

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\vec{h}}\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{pmatrix}:=\begin{pmatrix}px_{1}+x_{2}^{p^{h_{1}}}\\
px_{2}+x_{1}^{p^{h_{2}}}\end{pmatrix}\ .

Note that
\mathcal{D}_{\vec{h}}
does satisfy the same conditions as those given on
[p]_{F}
by Proposition
3.6
, but we do not claim for now that it is an endomorphism of the formal group
F
. Nevertheless, this is the simplest approximation of
[p]_{F}
, which avoids further computational complexity, and it is
p
-adically close enough to
[p]_{F}
so that the qualitative questions we are interested in (such as ramification properties of the field extensions defined by the
p^{\infty}
-torsion part of
F
) can be at least partially answered by a study of the dynamical system
\mathcal{D}_{\vec{h}}
. This will lead us in the sequel to the following abuse of notation: when no confusion is possible, we will simply denote
[p]_{F}
instead of
\mathcal{D}_{\vec{h}}
.

4.2.
Newton copolygons associated with
p
-adic power series in
2
variables

The goal of this subsection is to introduce an analogue, for
p
-adic power series in
2
variables (such as those involved in the endomorphisms of the
2
-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal groups we defined in the previous section), of the Newton copolygon associated with
p
-adic power series in
1
variable. The latter was introduced by Lubin
[
8
]
to provide a new point of view on some standard fundamental results in (higher) ramification theory. Our motivation is to build a tool that encodes the same information as usual Newton polygons regarding valuations, but in a more functorial way with respect to the composition of power series, and to provide a more geometrical point of view on the (endomorphisms of the)
2
-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal groups, in the view of studying the ramification properties associated with their
p^{\infty}
-torsion part in the next section (and in forthcoming work).

4.2.1.
Definition of the Newton copolygon associated with a
p
-adic power series in
2
variables

Let
f\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[x_{1},x_{2}]]
be a
p
-adic power series in
2
variables. Let us write it as

\displaystyle f(x_{1},x_{2})=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}c_{ij}x^{i}_{1}x^{j}_{2}\ ,\ \textrm{ with }c_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\ .

In the
3
-dimensional Euclidean space with coordinates
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\eta)
, we draw, for each pair of indices
(i,j)
such that
c_{ij}\not=0
, the half-space defined by the equation
\eta\leq i\xi_{1}+j\xi_{2}+v(c_{ij})
. The intersection of all these half-spaces provides a convex body that extends downwards. We call the boundary of this convex body the
Newton copolygon associated with
f
, and we denote it by
\mathcal{N}_{f}^{c}
. Another way to define the same object is provided by the next definition.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[x_{1},x_{2}]]
be a
p
-adic power series in
2
variables. Let us denote by
(c_{ij})_{i,j\geq 0}
its coefficients (in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
) :

\displaystyle f(x_{1},x_{2})=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}c_{ij}x_{1}^{i}x_{2}^{j}\ .

Set
\mathcal{A}
be the set of all pairs of indices
(i,j)
such that
c_{ij}
is non-zero and let
V_{f}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be the function defined by

\displaystyle\forall\ (\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\ V_{f}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}):=\min\{i\xi_{1}+j\xi_{2}+v(c_{ij}),\ (i,j)\in\mathcal{A}\}\ .

The
Newton copolygon associated with
f
is the graph
\mathcal{N}_{f}^{c}
(in the
3
-dimensional Euclidean space) of the function
V_{f}
. The latter function is called the
copolygon function associated with
f
.

Remark 4.5
.

As any simplicial complex living in a
3
-dimensional Euclidean space, the Newton copolygon associated with such a power series comes with a stratification into
k
-skeletons with
0\leq k\leq 2
: the
0
-skeleton is made of the vertices of the copolygon, the
1
-skeleton consists in all edges linking two vertices of the copolygon, while the
2
-skeleton is the union of the
2
-dimensional facets of the copolygon. We will see below (see Proposition
4.7
) that the
0
-skeleton can be characterised in terms of exceptional values (as defined in Definition
4.6
below).

Definition 4.6
.

We keep the same notation as in Definition
4.4
. A pair
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
of real numbers is called an
exceptional value for
V_{f}
if there exists three elements
(i_{1},j_{1})
,
(i_{2},j_{2})
and
(i_{3},j_{3})
in
\mathcal{A}
such that

\displaystyle\forall\ k\in\{1,2,3\},\ V_{f}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})=i_{k}\xi_{1}+j_{k}\xi_{2}+v(c_{i_{k}j_{k}})\ .

In other words, exceptional values are pre-images of elements in the image of
V_{f}
that are reached from (at least) three different elements of
\mathcal{A}
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[x_{1},x_{2}]]
be a
p
-adic power series in
2
variables. Let
\mathcal{N}_{f}^{c}
be its Newton copolygon and
V_{f}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be the associated copolygon function (as in Definition
4.4
).

(1)

A pair
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
of real numbers is an exceptional value of
V_{f}
if, and only if,
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},V_{f}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}))
is in the
0
-skeleton of
\mathcal{N}_{f}^{c}
.

(2)

For any element
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in\overline{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
, we have
v(f(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}))\geq V_{f}(v(\alpha_{1}),v(\alpha_{2}))
.

(3)

For any element
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in\overline{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
, the following are equivalent :

•

there exists
u\in\overline{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
such that
(\alpha_{1},u\alpha_{2})
is a zero of
f
;

•

(v(\alpha_{1}),v(\alpha_{2}))
is an exceptional value for
V_{f}
.

Proof.

•

By definition, the exceptional values of
V_{f}
are exactly the two first coordinates of points where (at least) three half-planes of the convex body that defines
\mathcal{N}_{f}^{c}
meet, i.e. they are the two first coordinates of a vertex of
\mathcal{N}_{f}^{c}
. The third coordinate of such a vertex is (by definition of
\mathcal{N}_{f}^{c}
) the image by
V_{f}
of the two first coordinates, hence the first statement is proven.

•

Let
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in\overline{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
: then
f(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
is a convergent series. Moreover, if we write as above
f(x,y)=\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\geq 0}c_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}
with
c_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and let
\mathcal{A}
denote the set of all pairs
(i,j)
of indices such that

, then we have (since

is a valuation) :

(4.5)

\displaystyle v(f(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}))=v\left(\sum_{i,j\geq 0}c_{ij}\alpha_{1}^{i}\alpha_{2}^{j}\right)\geq\min\{v(c_{ij})+iv(\alpha_{1})+jv(\alpha_{2}),\ (i,j)\in\mathcal{A}\}\ ,

the right hand side being by definition nothing but
V_{f}(v(\alpha_{1}),v(\alpha_{2}))
. Hence, we have the claimed inequality.

•

Assume that
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in\overline{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
is a zero of
f
. Then the inequality in statement (2) above must be strict, which implies by contraposition that
(v(\alpha_{1}),v(\alpha_{2}))
must be an exceptional value for
V_{f}
. Conversely, assume that
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in\overline{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
is such that
(v(\alpha_{1}),v(\alpha_{2}))
is an exceptional value for
V_{f}
and set

. Then

is a
p
-adic power series in one variable

for which
v(\alpha_{2})
is exceptional in the sense of

. According to

, and this ends the proof of statement (3), as well as the proof of the proposition.

∎

5.
Galois extensions attached to a 2-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal group

In this last section, we investigate some properties of the field extension
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(F[p^{n}])/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
introduced in Section
4.1
. More precisely, we are interested in understanding the ramification properties attached to such an extension. Recall that
F
is the
2
-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal group over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
associated with
\vec{h}=(h_{1},h_{2})
and that
n\geq 1
is any positive integer.

The first thing to check is that the extension
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(F[p^{n}])/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
built by adjunction of the coordinates of the
p^{n}
-torsion points of
F
is Galois. This is the point of the next statement.

Theorem 5.1
.

For any
n\geq 1
, the field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(F[p^{n}])
generated over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
by the
p^{n}
-torsion points of
F
defines a Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

Proof.

To ease notation, we set
K_{n}:=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(F[p^{n}])
and let
K_{n}^{c}
denote the Galois closure of
K_{n}
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, i.e. the smallest Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
that contains
K_{n}
. To prove that
K_{n}=K_{n}^{c}
, it is enough to prove that any element of
F[p^{n}]
has all its Galois conjugates in
K_{n}
, which will be the case if we prove that:

\displaystyle\forall\ \alpha\in F[p^{n}],\ \forall\ \tau\in\mathrm{Gal}(K_{n}^{c}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}),\ \tau(\alpha)\in F[p^{n}]\ .

Let
\alpha\in F[p^{n}]
and
\tau\in\mathrm{Gal}(K_{n}^{c}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.
Recall that the natural structure of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module carried by
F[p^{n}]
(see Lemma
4.1
) is such that the scalar multiplication is given by:

\displaystyle\forall\ u\in\mathbb{Z}_{p},\ u\cdot\alpha:=[u]_{F}(\alpha)\ .

This implies in particular that
p^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p}
acts trivially on
\alpha
. Since
p^{n}=\tau(p^{n})
, we directly have that

\displaystyle p^{n}\cdot\tau(\alpha)=\tau(p^{n})\cdot\tau(\alpha)=\tau(p^{n}\cdot\alpha)=\tau(\vec{0})=\vec{0}\ .

As
\tau
induces a permutation of the elements of
F[p^{n}]
, we obtain that
\tau(K_{n})=K_{n}
- by double inclusion. Hence,
\tau
induces a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-automorphism of
K_{n}
, and the proof is complete.
∎

5.1.
Building (non) abelian extensions from the
p^{\infty}
-torsion points of
F

Before going further, let us explain, by analogy with the
1
-dimensional case, why these Galois extensions have a priori no reason to be abelian extensions.

Let
h\geq 2
be any fixed integer. Let
G
be a classical (
1
-dimensional) Lubin-Tate formal group over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that the
p
-adic power series
f(x)=px+x^{p^{h}}
defines the multiplication-by-
p
endomorphism
[p]_{G}
of
G
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. (All this makes sense in the classical case by
[
9
]
.) Although it is well-defined over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, it does not provide a Lubin-Tate formal group over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, but over the unramified extension
U_{h}
of degree
h
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Note that
p
remains a prime element in
U_{h}
, and the corresponding residue field
\mathcal{U}_{h}/p\mathcal{U}_{h}
is isomorphic to the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p^{h}}
. By Hensel’s lemma, this ensures (as
\mathbb{F}_{p^{h}}^{*}
is cyclic of order
p^{h}-1
) that
U_{h}
contains all
p^{h}-1
roots of unity in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
.

Now remark that
U_{h}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is an abelian extension (since finite and unramified) , and
\mathrm{Gal}(U_{h}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is the cyclic (abelian) group of order
h
. The problem comes from the next stage: indeed, if we set
U_{\infty,G}:=\displaystyle\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{Q}_{p}[G[p^{n}]]
, then it defines a huge totally ramified abelian extension of
U_{h}
, but it is definitely
not
an abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, since the maximal abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is much smaller (as it is generated by the cyclotomic extensions of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
). More precisely, note that the Galois group of
U_{\infty,G}/U_{h}
is actually isomorphic to
\mathcal{U}_{h}^{\times}
, hence the Galois group of
U_{\infty,G}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is isomorphic to a semi-direct product
\mathcal{U}_{h}^{\times}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/p^{h}\mathbb{Z}
, which has no reason to be a direct product in general. This suggests that in higher dimension, there is little chance that our Galois extensions
K_{n}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
are abelian in general.

Back to our
2
-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal group
F
of height
p^{h_{1}+h_{2}}
, we denote by

\mathrm{Tors}(F):=\displaystyle\bigcup_{n\geq 1}F[p^{n}]

the
p^{\infty}
-torsion part of
F
. Note that
\ker([p]_{F})
provides
p^{h_{1}+h_{2}}
points of an affine
2
-space over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, and that adjoining them to
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
automatically includes
U_{h_{1}+h_{2}}
in the field built this way. This motivates the study of the extension of
U_{h_{1}+h_{2}}
generated by
\mathrm{Tors}(F)
, as a candidate for a nice abelian extension. The next statement, which is one of the main results of this paper, shows that this hope is reasonnable.

Theorem 5.2
.

The extension
U_{h_{1}+h_{2}}(\mathrm{Tors}(F))/U_{h_{1}+h_{2}}
is abelian.

Proof.

To ease notation, we set
h:=h_{1}+h_{2}
and
U:=U_{h}(\mathrm{Tors}(F))
. Our goal is then to prove that
\mathrm{Gal}(U/U_{h})
is an abelian group. First, let us explain how elements of
\mathcal{U}_{h}
define endomorphisms of our Lubin-Tate group
F
, seen as a Lubin-Tate group over
\mathcal{U}_{h}
. Note that it is enough to understand what happens for
p^{h}-1
roots of unity, since the latter generate the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module
\mathcal{U}_{h}
.

Let
\gamma
be a
(p^{h}-1)
-th root of
1
in
U_{h}
. We define
\{\gamma\}
by the following formula:

(5.1)

\{\gamma\}(x_{1},x_{2}):=(\gamma x_{1},\gamma^{p^{h_{2}}}x_{2})\ .

Let us check that
\{\gamma\}
defines an endomorphism of
F
. First note that, by construction, we have
\{\gamma\}(X)\equiv 0\textrm{ mod degree 1 terms}
; hence, we are left to check that
\{\gamma\}
commutes with
F
. To do this, let us substitute
\{\gamma\}(x_{1},x_{2})
to
(x_{1},x_{2})
in (
3.6
) and (
3.7
). As
\gamma^{p^{h}}=\gamma
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}L_{1}(\{\gamma\}(X))&=&L_{1}(\gamma x_{1},\gamma^{p^{h_{2}}}x_{2})\\
&=&\displaystyle\gamma x_{1}+\sum_{k\geq 1}p^{-2k}\gamma^{p^{kh}}x_{1}^{p^{kh}}+\sum_{k\geq 0}p^{-(2k+1)}\left(\gamma^{p^{h_{2}}}x_{2}\right)^{p^{h_{1}+kh}}\\
&=&\displaystyle\gamma x_{1}+\sum_{k\geq 1p^{-2k}}\gamma x_{1}^{p^{kh}}+\sum_{k\geq 0}p^{-(2k+1)}\gamma^{(k+1)p^{h}}x_{2}^{p^{h_{1}+kh}}\\
&=&\displaystyle\gamma\left(x_{1}+\sum_{k\geq 1}p^{-2k}x_{1}^{p^{kh}}+\sum_{k\geq 0}p^{-(2k+1)}x_{2}^{p^{h_{1}+kh}}\right)\\
&=&\gamma L_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})\ ,\end{array}

and

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}L_{2}(\{\gamma\}(X))&=&L_{2}(\gamma x_{1},\gamma^{p^{h_{2}}}x_{2})\\
&=&\displaystyle\gamma^{p^{h_{2}}}x_{2}+\sum_{k\geq 1}p^{-2k}\left(\gamma^{p^{h_{2}}}x_{2}\right)^{p^{kh}}+\sum_{k\geq 0}p^{-(2k+1)}(\gamma x_{1})^{p^{h_{2}+kh}}\\
&=&\displaystyle\gamma^{p^{h_{2}}}x_{2}+\sum_{k\geq 1}p^{-2k}\left(\gamma^{p^{h_{2}}}x_{2}\right)^{p^{kh}}+\sum_{k\geq 0}p^{-(2k+1)}(\gamma^{p^{kh}})^{p^{h_{2}}}x_{1}^{p^{h_{2}+kh}}\\
&=&\displaystyle\gamma^{p^{h_{2}}}\left(x_{2}+\sum_{k\geq 1}p^{-2k}x_{2}^{p^{h_{2}+kh}}+\sum_{k\geq 0}p^{-(2k+1)}x_{1}^{p^{h_{2}+kh}}\right)\\
&=&\gamma^{p^{h_{2}}}L_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})\ .\end{array}

We have hence proven that

\displaystyle L(\{\gamma\}(X))=M_{\gamma}L(X)\ \text{ with }M_{\gamma}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\gamma&0\\
0&\gamma^{p^{h_{2}}}\end{array}\right)\in\mathrm{M}_{2}(U_{h}^{\times})\ .

This is enough to ensure that
\{\gamma\}
is an endomorphism of
F
. Indeed, since we have

\displaystyle L(\{\gamma\}(X))+L(\{\gamma\}(Y))=M_{\gamma}(L(X)+L(Y))\ ,

then we have

(5.2)

\displaystyle L((F\circ\{\gamma\})(X,Y))=L(\{\gamma\}(X))+L(\{\gamma\}(Y)))=M_{\gamma}(L(X)+L(Y))

and

(5.3)

\displaystyle L((\{\gamma\}\circ F)(X,Y))=M_{\gamma}L(F(X,Y))=M_{\gamma}(L(X)+L(Y))\ .

Comparing (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) shows that
L((F\circ\{\gamma\})(X,Y))=L((\{\gamma\}\circ F)(X,Y))
, hence that

F\circ\{\gamma\}=\{\gamma\}\circ F\ ,

as announced.

In particular,
\{\gamma\}
commutes with multiplication by
p
-adic integers and, as it satisfies

\{\gamma\}^{\circ(p^{h}-1)}=\mathrm{Id}_{F}\ ,

we can see
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\gamma]\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\{\gamma\}]
as a subring of the
\mathcal{U}_{h}
-endomorphism ring
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{U}_{h}}(F)
of
F
. (Recall that
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\{\gamma\}]
denotes the smallest subring of
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{U}_{h}}(F)
that contains
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
\{\gamma\}
.) Hence, we can embed the group of (
p^{h}-1
)-th roots of
1
in
U_{h}
as a subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{U}_{h}}(F)
, the latter being the group of
\mathcal{U}_{h}
-automorphisms of
F
. This proves in particular that
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{U}_{h}}(F)=\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{U}_{h}}(F)^{\times}
has a cyclic subgroup of order
p^{h}-1
.

Assume now that
\gamma
is a given primitive
(p^{h}-1)
-th root of
1
in
\mathcal{U}_{h}
. Considering
\mathcal{U}_{h}
as a free
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module with basis
\{\gamma^{i},0\leq i\leq p^{h}-2\}
, we can define a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-linear map
\Gamma:\mathcal{U}_{h}\to\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{U}_{h}}(F)
by setting
\Gamma(\gamma^{i}):=\{\gamma\}^{\circ i}
for any
i\in\{0,\ldots,p^{h}-2\}
. It is straightforward to check that
\Gamma
is actually an injective ring homomorphism whose image is
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\{\gamma\}]
, so we have identified elements of
\mathcal{U}_{h}
with explicit endomorphisms of the Lubin-Tate group
F
.

This shows in particular that
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{U}_{h}}(F)
contains a subring that is also a free
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-subalgebra of rank
h
. Let us introduce here the Tate module
\mathrm{T}_{p}(F)=\displaystyle\lim_{\leftarrow}F[p^{n}]
of the formal group
F
. Since
F
is of height
h
,
\mathrm{T}_{p}(F)
is a free
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module of rank
h
; hence,
\Gamma
turns it into a free
\mathcal{U}_{h}
-module of rank
1
.

Now, let
\sigma
be an element of
\mathrm{Gal}(U/U_{h})
, as well as the endomorphism of
\mathrm{T}_{p}(F)
it defines. Since
\{\gamma\}(X)=M_{\gamma}X
with
M_{\gamma}\in\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathcal{U}_{h}^{\times})
, we directly obtain that
\sigma
and
\{\gamma\}
commute. The same holds for any
(p^{h}-1)
-th root of
1
, since the associated endomorphism of
F
will just be the corresponding power of
\{\gamma\}
. The identification made above regarding the structure of
\mathcal{U}_{h}
-module of
\mathrm{T}_{p}(F)
hence ensures that
\sigma
is actually a
\mathcal{U}_{h}
-endomorphism of
\mathrm{T}_{p}(F)
. But
\mathrm{T}_{p}(F)
has rank
1
as
\mathcal{U}_{h}
-module, hence all its endomorphisms are exactly multiplication by elements of
\mathcal{U}_{h}
: hence, we have proven that
\sigma
commute with all
\mathcal{U}_{h}
-endomorphisms of
\mathrm{T}_{p}(F)
.

In other words, this implies that the action of
\mathrm{Gal}(U/U_{h})
on
T_{p}(F)
defines a rank
1
representation of
\mathrm{Gal}(U/U_{h})
over
\mathcal{U}_{h}
. It is straightforward to check that this representation is faithful since
\mathrm{Gal}(U/U_{h})
maps onto
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{U}_{h}}(F)
in a
1-1
way. Consequently, any element of
\mathrm{Gal}(U/U_{h})
acts on
T_{p}(F)
as an element of
\mathcal{U}_{h}
; hence, these actions commute to each other (since
\{\gamma\}\circ\{\mu\}=\{\mu\}\circ\{\gamma\}
for any elements
\gamma,\mu
in
\mathcal{U}_{h}
). But
U=U_{h}(\mathrm{Tors}(F))
, so we have proven that any two elements of
\mathrm{Gal}(U/U_{h})
commute to each other, which ends the demonstration.
∎

.

. Indeed, given any unit
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
and any integer

, let
G_{u,r}
denote the
1
-dimensional Lubin-Tate formal group whose multiplication-by-
p
endomorphism is given by the
p
-adic power series
upx+x^{p^{r}}
. Let
U_{r}^{LT}
denote the extension of
U_{r}

Let us start by proving a useful arithmetical lemma.

Let

\displaystyle\frac{p^{t}+1}{2}

Degenerate Fubini-type polynomials associated with degenerate Apostol-Bernoulli and Apostol-Euler polynomials of order α \alpha
Muhammet Cihat DAĞLI

Abstract
In this paper, by introducing the degenerate Fubini-type polynomials, we give several relations with the help of the Faà di Bruno formula and some properties of Bell polynomials, and generating function methods. Also, we derive some new explicit formulas and recurrence relations for Fubini-type polynomials and numbers. Associating the degenerate Fubini-type polynomials newly defined here with degenerate Apostol-Bernoulli polynomials and degenerate Apostol-Euler polynomials of order α \alpha enables us to present additional relations for some degenerate special polynomials and numbers. Keywords: Apostol-Bernoulli polynomials, Apostol-Euler polynomials, degenerate Bernoulli polynomials, Generalized Fubini polynomial, Stirling numbers, explicit formula, recurrence relation, generating function. Mathematics Subject Classification 2010: 11B68, 11B37, 05A15, 05A19, 11B83, 11Y55.

Introduction

The higher-order Bernoulli polynomials
B_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)
and higher-order
Euler polynomials
E_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)
, each of degree
n
in
x

\alpha

[
23

\left(\dfrac{t}{e^{t}-1}\right)^{\alpha}e^{xt}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}}B_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)\dfrac{t^{n}}{n!}

and

\text{ }\left(\dfrac{2}{e^{t}+1}\right)^{\alpha}e^{xt}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}}E_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)\dfrac{t^{n}}{n!},

respectively. For
\alpha=1

B_{n}(x)
and Euler polynomials
E_{n}(x),
defined by means of the following
generating functions:

\dfrac{te^{xt}}{e^{t}-1}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}}B_{n}(x)\dfrac{t^{n}}{n!}\text{ }\left(\left|t\right|<2\pi\right)\text{ and }\dfrac{2e^{xt}}{e^{t}+1}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}}E_{n}(x)\dfrac{t^{n}}{n!}\text{ }\left(\left|t\right|<\pi\right).

In particular, the rational numbers
B_{n}=B_{n}(0)
and integers
E_{n}=2^{n}E_{n}(1/2)
are called classical Bernoulli numbers and Euler numbers, respectively.

The generalized Apostol-Bernoulli polynomials
B_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x;\gamma)

were defined by Luo and Srivastava by means of the generating function

[

,
21
,

\left(\dfrac{t}{\gamma e^{t}-1}\right)^{\alpha}e^{xt}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}}B_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x;\gamma)\dfrac{t^{n}}{n!}

\left(\gamma\in\mathbb{C};\text{ }\left|t\right|<2\pi\text{ if }\gamma=1\text{; }\left|t\right|<\left|\log\gamma\right|\text{ if }\gamma\neq 1\right),

and the generalized Apostol-Euler polynomials
E_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x;\gamma)
by
means of the generating function
[
20
]

\text{ }\left(\dfrac{2}{\gamma e^{t}+1}\right)^{\alpha}e^{xt}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}}E_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x;\gamma)\dfrac{t^{n}}{n!}

\left(\left|t\right|<\pi\text{ if }\gamma=1\text{; }\left|t\right|<\left|\log(-\gamma)\right|\text{ if }\gamma\neq 1;\text{ }1^{\gamma}=1\right).

Carlitz
[
1
]
defined degenerate Bernoulli polynomials and degenerate
Euler polynomials by

\frac{t}{\left(1+\lambda t\right)^{1/\lambda}-1}\left(1+\lambda t\right)^{x/\lambda}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}}B_{n}(x;\lambda)\dfrac{t^{n}}{n!},

and

\frac{2}{\left(1+\lambda t\right)^{1/\lambda}+1}\left(1+\lambda t\right)^{x/\lambda}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}}E_{n}(x;\lambda)\dfrac{t^{n}}{n!}.

For

2
Properties of second kind Bell polynomials

The Bell polynomials of the second kind
B_{n,k}\left(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n-k+1}\right)
for
n\geq k\geq 0
were defined by
[
2
, p. 134 and
139]

B_{n,k}\left(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n-k+1}\right)={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq n,\text{ }l_{i}\in\left\{0\right\}\cup\mathbb{N}\\
{{\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}}}il_{i}=n,\text{ }{{\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}}}l_{i}=k\end{subarray}}^{\infty}}\frac{n!}{{\textstyle\prod\nolimits_{i=1}^{l-k+1}}l_{i}!}{\textstyle\prod\limits_{i=1}^{l-k+1}}\left(\frac{x_{i}}{i!}\right)^{l_{i}}.

The Faà di Bruno formula can be described in terms of the Bell polynomials
of the second kind
B_{n,k}\left(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n-k+1}\right)
by

\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}f\circ h\left(t\right)={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}}f^{\left(k\right)}\left(h\left(t\right)\right)B_{n,k}\left(h^{\prime}\left(t\right),h^{\prime\prime}\left(t\right),...,h^{(n-k+1)}\left(t\right)\right).

(2.1)

For
n\geq k\geq 0,
these polynomials satisfy the following relation
[
2
, p.
135]

B_{n,k}\left(abx_{1},ab^{2}x_{2},...,ab^{n-k+1}x_{n-k+1}\right)=a^{k}b^{n}B_{n,k}\left(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n-k+1}\right),

(2.2)

where
a
and
b
are any complex number. Also, for
n\geq k\geq 0,
the
following formula is valid for the special case of
B_{n,k}

B_{n,k}\left(1,1,...,1\right)=S\left(n,k\right),

(2.3)

where
S\left(n,k\right)
denotes the Stirling numbers of the second kind,
can be generated by
[
2
, p. 206]

\frac{\left(e^{t}-1\right)^{k}}{k!}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=k}^{\infty}}S\left(n,k\right)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

In
[
32
, Remark 1]
, there existed the formula

B_{n,k}\left(1,1-\lambda,\left(1-\lambda\right)\left(1-2\lambda\right),...,\prod_{l=0}^{n-k}\left(1-l\lambda\right)\right)=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=0}^{k}}\left(-1\right)^{l}\binom{k}{l}\prod_{q=0}^{n-1}\left(l-q\lambda\right),

(2.4)

which is equivalent to

B_{n,k}\left(\left\langle\lambda\right\rangle_{1},\left\langle\lambda\right\rangle_{2},...,\left\langle\lambda\right\rangle_{n-k+1}\right)=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=0}^{k}}\left(-1\right)^{l}\binom{k}{l}\left\langle\lambda l\right\rangle_{n},

(2.5)

established in
[
35
, Theorems 2.1 and 4.1]
. In
[
7
, Remark 7.5]
,
the explicit formulas (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) were rearranged as

\displaystyle B_{n,k}\left(1,1-\lambda,\left(1-\lambda\right)\left(1-2\lambda\right),...,\prod_{l=0}^{n-k}\left(1-l\lambda\right)\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\frac{\lambda^{n-1}(n-1)!}{k!}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=1}^{k}}\left(-1\right)^{l}l\binom{k}{l}\binom{l/\lambda-1}{n-1}

for
\lambda\neq 0
and

B_{n,k}\left(\left\langle\lambda\right\rangle_{1},\left\langle\lambda\right\rangle_{2},...,\left\langle\lambda\right\rangle_{n-k+1}\right)=(-1)^{k}\lambda\frac{(n-1)!}{k!}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=1}^{k}}\left(-1\right)^{l}l\binom{k}{l}\binom{\lambda l-1}{n-1}.

(2.6)

Here, the generalized binomial coefficient
\binom{z}{w}
is defined by

\binom{z}{w}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\Gamma\left(z+1\right)}{\Gamma\left(w+1\right)\Gamma\left(z-w+1\right)},&\text{ if }z,w,z-w\in\mathbb{C}-\left\{-1,-2,...\right\}\text{;}\\
0,&\text{if }z\in\mathbb{C}-\left\{-1,-2,...\right\}\text{ and }w,z-w\in\left\{-1,-2,...\right\}\text{.}\end{cases}

Theorem 3.1

The degenerate Fubini-type numbers can be computed by the formula:

a_{n}^{\left(\alpha\right)}\left(\lambda\right)=(n-1)!{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}}\frac{\left\langle-2\alpha\right\rangle_{k}}{2^{\alpha+k}}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\lambda^{k-1}k!}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=1}^{k}}(-1)^{l}l\binom{k}{l}\binom{\lambda l-1}{n-1},

1,&\text{if }n=0.\end{cases}

Consequently, for the special case of the degenerate Apostol-Euler polynomials

the following relation holds:

E_{n}^{(2\alpha)}(0;\lambda;-1/2)=(n-1)!{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}}\frac{\left\langle-2\alpha\right\rangle_{k}}{2^{k-2\alpha}}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\lambda^{k-1}k!}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=1}^{k}}(-1)^{l}l\binom{k}{l}\binom{\lambda l-1}{n-1}.

(3.2)

Proof.
If we apply

and

to the Faà di Bruno formula (

\displaystyle\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\left((2-\left(1+\lambda t\right)^{1/\lambda})^{-2\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}}\frac{d^{k}}{du^{k}}(2-u)^{-2\alpha}B_{n,k}\left(\frac{\lambda\left(1+t\right)^{\lambda-1}}{\lambda},\frac{\lambda\left(\lambda-1\right)\left(1+t\right)^{\lambda-2}}{\lambda},\right.

\displaystyle...,\left.\frac{\lambda\left(\lambda-1\right)...\left(\lambda-\left(n-k\right)\right)\left(1+t\right)^{\lambda-(n-k+1)}}{\lambda}\right),

\displaystyle={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}}\left\langle-2\alpha\right\rangle_{k}(2-u)^{-2\alpha-k}\frac{\left(1+t\right)^{k\lambda-n}}{\lambda^{k}}B_{n,k}\left(\left\langle\alpha\right\rangle_{1},\left\langle\alpha\right\rangle_{2},...,\left\langle\alpha\right\rangle_{n-k+1}\right)

Now letting

Theorem 3.4

The following relationship holds true:

a_{n-2\alpha}^{\left(\alpha\right)}\left(x;\lambda\right)=\frac{B_{n}^{\left(2\alpha\right)}(x;\lambda;1/2)}{2^{\alpha}\left\langle n\right\rangle_{2\alpha}},

\alpha

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}}B_{n}^{\left(2\alpha\right)}(x;\lambda;1/2)\dfrac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}}a_{n}^{\left(\alpha\right)}\left(x;\lambda\right)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

4
Conclusion

In our recent study, we have introduced and dealt with degenerate version of
Fubini-type polynomials. Utilizing the Faà di Bruno formula and some
properties of Bell polynomials, and generating function methods, we have
derived some new explicit formulas, closed forms and recurrence relations for
degenerate Fubini-type polynomials and numbers, and Fubini-type polynomials
and numbers, defined by Kılar and Simsek
[
14
]
. Furthermore, by
associating the degenerate Fubini-type polynomials with degenerate
Apostol-Bernoulli polynomials and degenerate Apostol-Euler polynomials of
order
\alpha,
we have presented analog identities for them. As a final note,
a relation involving degenerate Fubini-type polynomials and degenerate
Apostol-Genocchi polynomials of order
\alpha
, defined by
[
13
, Eq.
2.6]
can be given and further relations can be obtained.

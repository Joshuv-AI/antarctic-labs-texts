On different modes of order convergence and some applications
Kevin Abela, Emmanuel Chetcuti, Hans Weber

Abstract
. Different notions for order convergence have been considered by various authors. Associated to every notion of order convergence corresponds a topology, defined by taking as the closed sets those subsets of the poset satisfying that no net in them order converges to a point that is outside of the set. We shall give a thorough overview of these different notions and provide a systematic comparison of the associated topologies. Then, in the last section we shall give an application of this study by giving a result on von Neumann algebras complementing the study started in [ 8 ] . We show that for every atomic von Neumann algebra (not necessarily σ \sigma -finite) the restriction of the order topology to bounded parts of M M coincides with the restriction of the σ \sigma -strong topology s ⁡ ( M , M ∗ ) s(M,M_{\ast}) . We recall that the methods of [ 8 ] rest heavily on the assumption of σ \sigma -finiteness. Further to this, for a semi-finite measure space, we shall give a complete picture of the relations between the topologies on L ∞ L^{\infty} associated with the duality ⟨ L 1 , L ∞ ⟩ \langle L^{1},L^{\infty}\rangle and its order topology.

1.
Introduction

Order convergence in partially ordered sets has for long been studied. One finds different definitions for order convergence that all reduce – for complete lattices – to the requirement
\limsup=\liminf
. Associated to each notion of order convergence there exists a topology, defined by taking as the closed sets those subsets of the poset satisfying that no net in them order converges to a point that is outside of the set. This topology is called the order topology and being the finest topology preserving order convergence, it is intrinsic to the poset. The simplest example is the set of real numbers. The order topology on the real line is the standard topology and if a sequence converges w.r.t. the order topology, it is also order convergent. Caution is required here because in general it is not so that the nets that converge w.r.t. the order topology are also order convergent. Order convergence is not topological in general. The question of characterizing those posets for which order convergence is topological has received considerable attention. However, we shall not follow this path. Our work continues on the work in
[
5
,
8
,
7
,
6
,
4
]
in which the order topology associated to a partially ordered structure arising naturally in the realm of Functional Analysis is studied and related to the functional-analytic properties of the structure.

A systematic treatment of the order topology on various structures associated with a von Neumann algebra was carried out in
[
8
]
. In that case, the order topology comes from the standard operator order defined on the self-adjoint operators acting on a Hilbert space
H
; namely, for self-adjoint operators
T
and
S
, we have
T\leq S
if the operator
S-T
has a positive spectrum. It was shown that the order topology on the self-adjoint part of a
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebra, albeit far from being a linear topology in general, on bounded parts, coincides with the strong operator topology. The proof is based on the Noncommutative Egoroff Theorem and rests heavily on the assumption of
\sigma
-finiteness. Besides, finite
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebras were characterized in terms of the order topological properties of the projection lattice.

The aim of the present paper is two-fold. In the first section we shall present a thorough overview of the different definitions for order convergence that are found in the literature and also provide a systematic comparison of the associated order topologies. In the second section we shall consider some applications complementing the work done in
[
8
,
7
]
. We shall first consider a semi-finite measure and study the relation between the locally convex topologies associated with
L^{\infty}
and its order topology. Then we shall make use of the results obtained in the first section to show that for atomic von Neumann algebras the underlying assumption of
\sigma
-finiteness is redundant in
[
8
, Corollary 4.4]
.

2.
On relations between different notions of order convergence in a partially ordered set

Let
(\mathscr{P},\leq)
be a partially ordered set. A subset
\mathscr{D}
of
\mathscr{P}
is
directed
provided it is nonempty and every finite subset of
\mathscr{D}
has an upper bound in
\mathscr{D}
. Dually, a nonempty subset
\mathscr{F}
of
\mathscr{P}
is
filtered
if every finite subset of
\mathscr{F}
has a lower bound in
\mathscr{F}
. If the set of upper bounds of a subset
\mathscr{X}
of
\mathscr{P}
has a least element, we call this element the
supremum
(or least upper bound) of
\mathscr{X}
and denote it by
\sup X
or
\vee X
. Similarly, the
infimum
(or greatest lower bound) is written as
\inf X
or
\wedge X
. The poset
(\mathscr{P},\leq)
is said to be:

•

a
lattice
if every finite subset of
\mathscr{P}
has a supremum and infimum.

•

conditionally complete
if every subset of
\mathscr{P}
that is bounded from above has a supremum and every subset of
\mathscr{P}
that is bounded from below has an infimum.

•

conditionally monotone complete
if every directed subset of
\mathscr{P}
that is bounded from above has a supremum and every filtered subset of
\mathscr{P}
that is bounded from below has an infimum.

A function
\varphi
from a poset
(\mathscr{P},\leq)
into another poset
(\mathscr{Q},\leq)
is said to be
isotone
if
\varphi(a)\leq\varphi(b)
for every
a,b\in\mathscr{P}
satisfying
a\leq b
.
Antitone
functions between posets are defined dually. When
\varphi
is isotone and injective, and
\varphi^{-1}
(defined on the range of
\varphi
) is again isotone, we say that
\varphi
is an
order isomorphism
. The posets
(\mathscr{P},\leq)
and
(\mathscr{Q},\leq)
are said to be order isomorphic when there is a surjective order isomorphism between them.

A
net
in a set
X
is a function
\varphi
from a directed set
\Gamma
into
X
. We shall write
x_{\gamma}
instead of
\varphi(\gamma)
and denote the net by
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
. For a net
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
, the subset
E_{\Gamma}(\gamma_{0})=\{x_{\gamma}:\gamma\geq\gamma_{0}\}
is called a
residual
of
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
. If
P(x)
is a property of the elements
x\in X
and
P(x_{\gamma})
is true for some residual
E_{\Gamma}(\gamma_{0})
, then we say that the net
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}

satisfies
P(x)
eventually
. The subset
\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}\subseteq\Gamma
is
cofinal
if for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
there exists
\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma^{{}^{\prime}}
such that
\gamma\leq\gamma_{0}
. It is easily seen that if
\Gamma^{\prime}
is cofinal in
\Gamma
, then
\Gamma^{\prime}
is directed and therefore
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\prime}}
is a net. This net is said to be a
cofinal subnet
of
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
.

A net
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
in a partially ordered set
\mathscr{P}
is said to be
monotonic increasing
or
monotonic decreasing
depending on whether the function
\gamma\mapsto x_{\gamma}
is isotone or antitone, respectively. A net is called
monotone
if it is either monotonic increasing or monotonic decreasing. If
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is monotonic increasing and its supremum exists and equals
x
we write
x_{\gamma}\uparrow x
. Dually,
x_{\gamma}\downarrow x
means that the net
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is monotonic decreasing with infimum equal to
x
.

Order convergence in posets has been studied since at least the time of Birkhoff. The motivation can be traced to the squeezing lemma, well known to every student of calculus: Let
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
be an increasing sequence of real numbers and
(z_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
be a decreasing sequence of real numbers such that
y_{n}\leq z_{n}
for all
n
. Suppose that the supremum of
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
is the same as the infimum of
(z_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
and equals to
\alpha
. Then, for any other sequence,
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
, squeezed between
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
and
(z_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
(i.e.
y_{n}\leq x_{n}\leq z_{n}
for all
n
) we have that
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
converges to
\alpha
. The following three different generalizations for posets can be found in the literature. We call them O
1
-, O
2
- and O
3
- convergence. For every
a,b\in\mathscr{P}
we define
(\leftarrow,a]:=\{x\in\mathscr{P}:x\leq a\}
;
[a,\rightarrow)
is defined dually, and
[a,b]:=\{x\in\mathscr{P}:a\leq x\leq b\}
.

Definition 1
.

Let
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be a net and
x
a point in a poset
\mathscr{P}
.

(i)

(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is said to
O
1
-converge
to
x
in
\mathscr{P}
if there exist two nets
(y_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
,
(z_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
in
\mathscr{P}
such that eventually
y_{\gamma}\leq x_{\gamma}\leq z_{\gamma}
,
y_{\gamma}\uparrow x
and
z_{\gamma}\downarrow x
.

(ii)

(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is said to
O
2
-converge
to
x
in
\mathscr{P}
if there exists a directed subset
M\subset\mathscr{P}
, and a filtered subset
N\subset\mathscr{P}
, such that
\vee M=\wedge N=x
, and for every
(m,n)\in M\times N
the net is eventually contained in
[m,n]
.

(iii)

(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is said to
O
3
-converge
to
x
in
\mathscr{P}
if there exist two subsets
M
and
N
of
\mathscr{P}
such that
\vee M=\wedge N=x
, and for every
(m,n)\in M\times N
the net is eventually contained in
[m,n]
.

The notion of O
1
-convergence can be traced back to Birkhoff
[
2
]
and Kantorovich
[
14
]
. Note that, in contrast to O
1
-convergence, in O
2
-convergence, the controlling nets are not indexed by the same directed set of the original net. O
2
-convergence is due McShane
[
17
]
. The notion of O
3
-convergence can be found in
[
27
]
whereby he gives references to
[
19
]
and
[
26
]
. It is easy to see that:

•

For a net
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
in a poset
\mathscr{P}
,

x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{\text{O${}_{1}$}}}{\longrightarrow}}x\ \Rightarrow\ x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{\text{O${}_{2}$}}}{\longrightarrow}}x\ \Rightarrow\ x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{\text{O${}_{3}$}}}{\longrightarrow}}x\qquad(x\in\mathscr{P}).

•

Every eventually constant net is O
1
-convergent to its eventual value.

•

Every cofinal subnet of an O
i
-convergent net is O
i
-convergent to the same limit (where
i\in\{1,2,3\}
).

Let us verify that when
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is a net in a conditionally complete lattice, the expression ‘
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
order converges to
x
’ conveys the intuitive meaning

(1)

\liminf_{\gamma}x_{\gamma}=\sup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\inf_{\gamma^{\prime}\geq\gamma}x_{\gamma}=x=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sup_{\gamma^{\prime}\geq\gamma}x_{\gamma}=\limsup_{\gamma}x_{\gamma}.

It is easy to see that if
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
satisfies (
1
) then it O
1
-converges to
x
. Moreover, if there exist subsets
\mathscr{M}
and
\mathscr{N}
of
\mathscr{P}
satisfying
\sup\mathscr{M}=\inf\mathscr{N}=x
, and such that for every
(m,n)\in\mathscr{M}\times\mathscr{N}

x_{\gamma}\in[m,n]\quad\text{eventually},

then
\liminf_{\gamma}x_{\gamma}\geq\sup\mathscr{M}=x
and
\limsup_{\gamma}x_{\gamma}\leq\inf\mathscr{N}=x
. This shows that when
\mathscr{P}
is a conditionally complete lattice all three notions of order convergence coalesce to the condition expressed in (
1
). In this case we simply say
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
O-converges to
x
.

The following example shows that – even when the poset is a lattice – O
2
-convergence does not imply O
1
-convergence. This question was first answered in
[
16
]
but the example that we shall give here is credited to D. Fremlin
[
20
, Ex. 2, pg. 140]
.

Example 1

(O
2
-convergence is not the same as O
1
-convergence)
.

Let
X
be an uncountable discrete space and let
\mathscr{D}
denote the family of all the cofinite subsets of
X
. Let
X_{0}
denote the one-point compactification of
X
. This can be identified with the set
X\cup\{\infty\}
, equipped with the topology
\mathscr{T}:=2^{X}\cup\hat{\mathscr{D}}
, where
\hat{\mathscr{D}}:=\left\{D\cup\{\infty\}\,:\;D\in\mathscr{D}\right\}
. The net
\bigl(\chi_{D}\bigr)_{D\in\hat{D}}
belongs to the Banach lattice
C(X_{0},\mathds{R})
and
\chi_{D}\downarrow 0
. So, if
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
is an arbitrary sequence in
X_{0}
such that all terms are distinct, the corresponding sequence of characteristic functions
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
is O
2
-convergent to
0
.
On the other-hand, if
f\in C(X_{0},\mathds{R})
and
f(x)\geq 1
holds on some infinite subset of
X_{0}
then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
D\in\hat{\mathscr{D}}
such that
f(x)>1-\varepsilon
holds on
D
. So, if
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
is a sequence in
C(X_{0},\mathds{R})
satisfying
f_{n}\leq g_{n}\leq g_{n+1}
for every
n\in\mathds{N}
then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an infinite subset
Y\subset X_{0}
such that every
g_{n}
is at least
1-\varepsilon
on
Y
. This implies the zero function cannot be the infimum of
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
and therefore
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
cannot be O
1
-convergent to
0
.

]
and shows that in general O
2
-convergence is not the same as O

-convergence.

Example 2

(O
3
-convergence is not the same as O
2
-convergence)
.

Let
A:=\{a_{n}:n\in\mathds{N}\}
and
B:=\{b_{n}:n\in\mathds{N}\}
be two countable sets and let
\mathscr{P}:=A\cup B\cup\{\mathbf{0},\mathbf{1}\}
equipped with the partial order defined by:

\mathbf{0}\leq x\leq\mathbf{1}\ (\forall x\in\mathscr{P})\qquad\text{and}\qquad x\leq y\ \Leftrightarrow\ x=a_{n},\,y=b_{m}\ (\exists n\leq m).

It is clear that
\mathscr{P}
contains no infinite directed (or filtered) set. On the other-hand,
\sup A=\mathbf{1}
. Thus, the sequence
(b_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
is O
3
-convergent to
\mathbf{1}
but not O
2
-convergent.

For any subset
\mathscr{D}
of the poset
\mathscr{P}
, define

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}^{+}:=

\displaystyle\left\{x\in\mathscr{P}\,:\;(\forall d\in\mathscr{D})\,x\geq d\right\},\,\text{and}

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}^{-}:=

\displaystyle\left\{x\in\mathscr{P}\,:\;(\forall d\in\mathscr{D})\,x\leq d\right\}.

We recall that with respect to set-theoretic inclusion the set
\hat{\mathscr{P}}:=\left\{\mathscr{D}\subset\mathscr{P}\,:\;(\mathscr{D}^{+})^{-}=\mathscr{D}\right\}
becomes a complete lattice satisfying:

(1)

\mathscr{A}^{-}
belongs to
\hat{\mathscr{P}}
for every
\mathscr{A}\subset\mathscr{P}
.

(2)

If
\left\{\mathscr{A}_{\iota}\,:\;\iota\in\mathds{I}\right\}
is a subset of
\hat{\mathscr{P}}
then
\sup_{\iota\in\mathds{I}}\mathscr{A}_{\iota}=\left(\bigcup_{\iota\in\mathds{I}}\mathscr{A}_{\iota}\right)^{+-}
and
\inf_{\iota\in\mathds{I}}\mathscr{A}_{\iota}=\bigcap_{\iota\in\mathds{I}}\mathscr{A}_{\iota}
.

(3)

(\leftarrow,x]\in\hat{\mathscr{P}}
for every
x\in\mathscr{P}
and the function
\varphi:x\mapsto(\leftarrow,x]:\mathscr{P}\to\hat{\mathscr{P}}
is an order isomorphism.

(4)

\varphi[\mathscr{P}]
is
join-dense
and
meet-dense
in
\hat{\mathscr{P}}
, i.e.

\sup\,\left\{\varphi(x)\,:\;x\in\mathscr{P},\,\varphi(x)\leq a\right\}\,=\,a\,=\,\inf\,\left\{\varphi(x)\,:\;x\in\mathscr{P},\,\varphi(x)\geq a\right\},

for every
a\in\hat{\mathscr{P}}
. In particular, it follows that
\varphi
preserves all suprema and infima that exist in
\mathscr{P}
; i.e. if
\mathscr{A}\subset\mathscr{P}
and
x\in\mathscr{P}
, then
\sup\varphi[\mathscr{A}]=\varphi(x)
if and only if
\sup\mathscr{A}=x
(and dually,
\inf\varphi[\mathscr{A}]=\varphi(x)
if and only if
\inf\mathscr{A}=x
).

(5)

\mathscr{A}^{-}=\inf\varphi[\mathscr{A}]
and
\mathscr{A}^{+-}=\sup\varphi[\mathscr{A}]
for every
\mathscr{A}\subset\mathscr{P}
.

(6)

\inf\varphi[\mathscr{A}^{+}]=\sup\varphi[\mathscr{A}]
and
\sup\varphi[\mathscr{A}^{-}]=\inf\varphi[\mathscr{A}]
for every
\mathscr{A}\subset\mathscr{P}
.

The complete lattice
\hat{\mathscr{P}}
is called the
Dedekind-MacNeille completion
of
\mathscr{P}
. It is characterized – up to order-isomorphism – as the unique complete lattice containing
\mathscr{P}
as a simultaneously join-dense and meet-dense subset.

In
[
3
]
one finds yet another definition for ‘order convergence’ based on the embedding of the poset in its Dedekind-MacNeille completion:

Definition 2
.

A net
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
in a poset
\mathscr{P}
is said to
O

-converge
to

if the net
\bigl(\varphi(x_{\gamma})\bigr)_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
O-converges to

\hat{\mathscr{P}}

Remark 1
.

Note that

x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{O${}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\text{DM}}}$}}{\longrightarrow}}x\,\Longleftrightarrow\liminf_{\gamma}\varphi(x_{\gamma})=\limsup_{\gamma}\varphi(x_{\gamma})=\varphi(x).

The comment after the definition of order convergence found in
[
3
, pg. 244]
shows that Birkhoff does not make a proper distinction between these modes of convergence, particularly he erroneously writes that O
1
-convergence is equivalent to O
DM
-convergence (compare to Theorem
4
).

Proposition 3
.

Let
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be a net in a poset
\mathscr{P}
. For any
x\in\ \mathscr{P}
the following three statements are equivalent:

(i)

x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{O${}_{3}$}}{\longrightarrow}}x
,

(ii)

\sup\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{-}=x=\inf\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{+}
,

(iii)

\bigcap_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{+-}=(\leftarrow,x]
and
\bigcap_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{-+}=[x,\rightarrow)
.

Proof.

(i) implies (ii). Assume that
x_{\gamma}\mathop{\overset{\text{O${}_{3}$}}{\longrightarrow}}x
and let
\mathscr{M}
,
\mathscr{N}
be subsets of
\mathscr{P}
satisfying
\sup\mathscr{M}=\inf\mathscr{N}=x
, and such that for every
(m,n)\in\mathscr{M}\times\mathscr{N}
there exists
\gamma(m,n)
such that
E_{\Gamma}(\gamma(m,n))\subset[m,n]
. Then

\displaystyle\sup\varphi\left[\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{-}\right]\,

\displaystyle=\,\sup\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\varphi[E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{-}]

\displaystyle\geq\,\sup\bigcup_{(m,n)\in\mathscr{M}\times\mathscr{N}}\varphi[E_{\Gamma}(\gamma(m,n))^{-}]

\displaystyle=\,\sup_{(m,n)\in\mathscr{M}\times\mathscr{N}}\sup\varphi[E_{\Gamma}(\gamma(m,n))^{-}]

\displaystyle=\,\sup_{(m,n)\in\mathscr{M}\times\mathscr{N}}\inf\varphi[E_{\Gamma}(\gamma(m,n))]\,\geq\,\sup_{(m,n)\in\mathscr{M}\times\mathscr{N}}\varphi(m)\,=\,\varphi(x),

and therefore
\sup\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{-}\,\geq\,x
. Dually, one can show that
\inf\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{+}\,\leq\,x
. On the other-hand, observe that if
s\in E_{\Gamma}(\gamma_{s})^{-}
and
t\in E_{\Gamma}(\gamma_{t})^{+}
, then
s\leq x_{\gamma}\leq t
holds for every
\gamma
satisfying
\gamma\geq\gamma_{s}
and
\gamma\geq\gamma_{t}
. This shows that
\sup\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{-}\,\leq\,\inf\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{+}
, and therefore

x\,\leq\,\sup\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{-}\,\leq\,\inf\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{+}\,\leq\,x.

This proves that (i) implies (ii). To show that (ii) implies (iii) we first observe that:

(2)

t\in\bigcap_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{-+}\,\Leftrightarrow\,\varphi(t)\geq\sup\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\varphi[E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{-}],

and

(3)

t\in\bigcap_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{+-}\,\Leftrightarrow\,\varphi(t)\leq\inf\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\varphi[E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{+}].

If
\sup\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{-}=x
, by (
2
) it follows that
t\in\bigcap_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{-+}
if and only if
\varphi(t)\geq\varphi(x)
, i.e.
\bigcap_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{-+}=[x,\rightarrow)
. Dually, if
\inf\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{+}=x
, then
\bigcap_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{+-}=(\leftarrow,x]
.

(iii) implies (i). Let
\mathscr{M}:=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{-}
and
\mathscr{N}:=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{+}
. If
\bigcap_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{-+}=[x,\rightarrow)
, then

t\geq s\ (\forall s\in\mathscr{M})\Leftrightarrow t\in\bigcap_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{-+}=[x,\rightarrow),

and if
\bigcap_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{+-}=(\leftarrow,x]
, then

t\leq s\ (\forall s\in\mathscr{N})\Leftrightarrow t\in\bigcap_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{+-}=(\leftarrow,x].

This shows that
\sup\mathscr{M}=\inf\mathscr{N}=x
. By definition of
\mathscr{M}
and
\mathscr{N}
it is clear that for every
(m,n)\in\mathscr{M}\times\mathscr{N}
there exists
\gamma(m,n)\in\Gamma
such that
m\in E_{\Gamma}(\gamma(m,n))^{-}
and
n\in E_{\Gamma}(\gamma(m,n))^{+}
. So
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is O
3
-convergent to
x
.

Remark 2
.

In particular, Proposition

Theorem 4
.

(i)

In a poset, O
3
-convergence is equivalent to O
DM
-convergence.

(ii)

In a lattice, O
2
-convergence, O
3
-convergence and O
DM
-convergence are equivalent.

Proof.

Let
(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be a net and
x
and point in
\mathscr{P}
.

(i) Observing that

\bigcap_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{+-}=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sup\varphi[E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)]=\limsup_{\gamma}\varphi(x_{\gamma})

and

\left(\bigcap_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{-+}\right)^{-}=\left(\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{-}\right)^{+-}=\sup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\inf\varphi[E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)]=\liminf_{\gamma}\varphi(x_{\gamma}),

one readily sees that the assertion follows by Proposition
3
.

(ii) Suffices to show that in a lattice O
3
-convergence implies O
2
-convergence. In view of Proposition
3
, it is enough to show that
\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}(\gamma)^{-}

Theorem 9

Let
\lambda

f:\lambda\to\mathscr{P}
be a
\lambda

\mathscr{P}

w.r.t.
\tau^{\lambda}_{\text{O${}_{i}$}}(\mathscr{P})
(where

\lambda

x

Proof.

We can suppose that
f^{-1}\{x\}
is bounded in
\lambda

f(\alpha)\neq x
for every
\alpha<\lambda
, i.e. the range
\mathscr{X}
of

is not closed w.r.t.
\tau^{\lambda}_{\text{O${}_{i}$}}(\mathscr{P})
. By definition of
\tau^{\lambda}_{\text{O${}_{i}$}}(\mathscr{P})
there exists a
\lambda

g:\lambda\to\mathscr{X}
that O
i
-converges outside of
\mathscr{X}
. For every
t\in\mathscr{X}
the set
G_{t}:=g^{-1}\{t\}
is bounded in
\lambda
; otherwise,
G_{t}
is cofinal in
\lambda
for some

and this contradicts the assumption that
g
is O
i
-convergent to a point outside of
\mathscr{X}
. For every
\alpha<\lambda
let

for every

.

We show that
h[\lambda]
is cofinal in
\lambda
. Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists
\gamma<\lambda
satisfying

. For every
\alpha<\gamma

In

3.1.
The order topology on
L^{\infty}
.

Let
(X,\Sigma,\mu)
be a semi-finite measure space and let
L^{\infty}
denote the Banach algebra of essentially bounded real-valued functions.
Other useful topologies on
L^{\infty}
, besides the topology
\tau_{\infty}
induced by the norm
\|\|_{{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}}

(i)

the topology of convergence in measure
\tau_{\mu}
: Every
E\in\Sigma

\mu(E)<\infty
defines an F-seminorm
\rho_{E}:f\mapsto\int|f|\wedge\chi_{E}\,{\rm d}\mu

\left\{\rho_{E}\,:\;E\in\Sigma,\,\mu(E)<\infty\right\}
is the topology of convergence in measure (on sets of finite measure) and is denoted by
\tau_{\mu}
.

(ii)

the strong-operator topology
\sigma_{p}
where
1\leq p<\infty

g\in L^{\infty}
induces a bounded linear operator on the Banach space
L^{p}
of
p

f\mapsto fg

L^{\infty}
embeds isometrically (as a Banach algebra) in the space of bounded linear operators
B(L^{p})
and can therefore be endowed with the subspace topology induced by the strong-operator topology of
B(L^{p})
generated by the family of order continuous Riesz seminorms
f\mapsto\|fg\|_{{\scriptscriptstyle p}}
as

L^{p}
. Let
\sigma_{p}
denote this locally convex-solid topology.

(iii)

the weak topology
\sigma(L^{\infty},L^{1})
and the Mackey topology
\tau(L^{\infty},L^{1})

L^{\infty}\times L^{1}\to\mathds{R}
defined by
\langle f,g\rangle\mapsto\int fg\,{\rm d}\mu

embeds isometrically (as a Banach space) in the dual space of

is localisable.) Among the locally convex topologies that are consistent with this duality, the weak topology

With the partial order induced by the pointwise order,

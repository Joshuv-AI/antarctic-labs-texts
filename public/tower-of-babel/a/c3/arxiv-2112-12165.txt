The Universal ℓ p \displaystyle\ell^{p} -Metric on Merge Trees
Robert Cardona, Justin Curry, Tung Lam, Michael Lesnick

Abstract
Adapting a definition given by Bjerkevik and Lesnick [ 9 ] for multiparameter persistence modules, we introduce an ℓ p \displaystyle\ell^{p} -type extension of the interleaving distance on merge trees. We show that our distance is a metric, and that it upper-bounds the p \displaystyle p -Wasserstein distance between the associated barcodes. For each p ∈ [ 1 , ∞ ] \displaystyle p\in[1,\infty] , we prove that this distance is stable with respect to cellular sublevel filtrations and that it is the universal (i.e., largest) distance satisfying this stability property. In the p = ∞ \displaystyle p=\infty case, this gives a novel proof of universality for the interleaving distance on merge trees.

The question of how to metrize the collection of merge trees is a fundamental one: Metrics are needed to study the continuity and stability of the merge tree construction, and to quantify sensitivity to noise.
Many metrics on merge trees have been proposed in prior work, as we discuss in detail below.

In particular, metrics called

Morozov et al. observed that there is a natural definition of an interleaving distance for merge trees, denoted

Theorem 1

(Stability properties of merge trees

)
.

(i)

For any functions
\displaystyle f,g:X\to\mathbb{R}

d_{I}(M_{f},N_{g})\leq||f-g||_{\infty},\qquad\text{where}\qquad||f-g||_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in X}|f(x)-g(x)|.

(ii)

For any merge trees
\displaystyle M

\displaystyle d_{B}
denotes the bottleneck distance between barcodes; see
\cref

While
\displaystyle d_{B}
is the most common metric on barcodes in the TDA literature, it has a property that is undesirable in some settings: Informally,
\displaystyle d_{B}
is sensitive only to the largest difference between two barcodes and not to smaller differences.
To avoid such undesirable behavior,

As the parameter

Both the bottleneck distance

This distance is an analogue of the

-distance on multiparameter persistence modules recently introduced in

\Cref

Let
\displaystyle X

Following

Recall that Morozov’s interleaving distance
\displaystyle d_{I}

The

[

In this section, we define merge trees. We work primarily with a categorical definition, which is convenient for defining the interleaving and presentation distances.
Recall that we may regard any partially ordered set
\displaystyle(P,\preceq)

\displaystyle p\in P

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle p\preceq q

We will be particularly interested in the posets
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle[n]=\{0,1,\dots,n\}

Applying the usual disjoint union of sets at each index, we obtain a well defined notion of disjoint union of persistent sets. The disjoint union of finitely many merge forests is itself a merge forest.

3
Presentations of merge trees

We now define presentations of merge trees.
Recall that, in the usual algebraic setting, presentations are defined in terms of generators and relations.
For merge trees, generators correspond to closed strands, as defined in
\Cref
ex:strand, which can be thought of as starting branches that emanate from each leaf node.
An internal/merge node above leaf nodes
\displaystyle i
and
\displaystyle j
witnesses the equality
\displaystyle\text{branch}_{i}=\text{branch}_{j}
by mapping a relating strand into the generating strands for
\displaystyle i
and
\displaystyle j
.
Each presentation
\displaystyle P_{M}
of a merge tree
\displaystyle M
then gives rise to a matrix that encodes which generators are identified by which relation.
We show that any pair of merge trees can be given presentations so that these matrices are identical—we call these ”compatible presentations.”

Definition 15

(Coequalizer)
.

Given sets
\displaystyle A,B
, the
coequalizer
of a pair of functions
\displaystyle\alpha,\beta\colon A\rightrightarrows B
is the set of equivalence classes
\displaystyle C:=B/\sim
, where

\displaystyle\sim
is the equivalence relation on
\displaystyle B
generated taking
\displaystyle\alpha(x)\sim\beta(x)
for all
\displaystyle x\in A
.
Let
\displaystyle q:B\to C
denote the quotient map.
\displaystyle C
satisfies the following universal property: Given a function
\displaystyle\gamma:B\to D
such that
\displaystyle\gamma\circ\beta=\gamma\circ\alpha
, there is a unique function
\displaystyle\delta:C\to D
such that
\displaystyle\gamma=\delta\circ q
.
The definition of a coequalizer extends pointwise to persistent sets: Given persistent sets
\displaystyle A
and
\displaystyle B
, the
coequalizer
of a pair of natural transformations
\displaystyle\alpha,\beta:A\rightrightarrows B
is the persistent set
\displaystyle C
such that for all
\displaystyle t\in\mathbb{R}
,
\displaystyle C(t)
is the coequalizer of
\displaystyle\alpha(t),\beta(t)\colon A(t)\rightrightarrows B(t)
, with the maps internal to
\displaystyle C
given by universal properties.
The universal property of coequalizers of persistent sets is completely analogous.

Definition 16

5.1
Stability

Definition 35
.

A distance
\displaystyle d

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle d(M,N)\leq\|f-g\|_{p}

\displaystyle p

Proof of
\Cref

We start by labeling vertices of
\displaystyle X
by
\displaystyle\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}
and edges
by
\displaystyle\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{l}
.
Consider the
\displaystyle(k\times l)
-matrix
\displaystyle P
where

We then define labeled matrices
\displaystyle P_{M}
,
\displaystyle P_{N}
with underlying matrix
\displaystyle P

5.2
Universality

In this section we prove that the
\displaystyle p
-presentation metric is universal among
\displaystyle p
-stable metrics. Here ”universal” can be interpreted as maximal or final in a certain poset category of pseudometrics.
In order to prove
\Cref
Thm:Universality, we need the following lemma:

Lemma 37

(Geometric lifting)
.

If
\displaystyle M
and
\displaystyle N
are merge trees with compatible presentations
\displaystyle P_{M}
and
\displaystyle P_{N}

such that
\displaystyle\|L(P_{M})-L(P_{N})\|_{p}=\epsilon
,
then there exists a regular cell complex
\displaystyle X
and monotone cellular functions

\displaystyle f:X\to\mathbb{R}
and
\displaystyle g:X\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(i)

\displaystyle M\cong\pi_{0}S^{\uparrow}f
,
\displaystyle N\cong\pi_{0}S^{\uparrow}g
, and

(ii)

\displaystyle\|f-g\|_{p}=\epsilon
.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle P_{M}
and
\displaystyle P_{N}
be compatible presentations for
\displaystyle M
and
\displaystyle N
with presentation matrices

P_{M}:=\begin{array}[]{ccccc}&\rho_{1}^{M}&\cdots&\rho_{l}^{M}\\
\gamma_{1}^{M}&&&\\
\vdots&&&\\
\gamma_{k}^{M}&&&\\
\end{array}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad P_{N}:=\begin{array}[]{ccccc}&\rho_{1}^{N}&\cdots&\rho_{l}^{N}\\
\gamma_{1}^{N}&&&\\
\vdots&&&\\
\gamma_{k}^{N}&&&\\
\end{array}\,.

Using the underlying matrix for either presentation we construct a cell complex

\displaystyle X
as follows: For each row
\displaystyle i
, add a
\displaystyle 0
-cell
\displaystyle\sigma_{i}
to
\displaystyle X
, and for
each column
\displaystyle j
, attach a
\displaystyle 1
-cell
\displaystyle\tau_{j}
so that

\displaystyle P_{M}(i,j)=1\Rightarrow\sigma_{i}\subseteq\tau_{j}
.
We then construct cellular functions
\displaystyle f,g:X\to\mathbb{R}
using the birth times
for the generators and relations for
\displaystyle P_{M}
and
\displaystyle P_{N}
, respectively.
More precisely,
\displaystyle f(\sigma_{i})=\gamma^{M}_{i}
and
\displaystyle f(\tau_{j})=\rho^{M}_{j}
, likewise for
\displaystyle g
.
Since
\displaystyle\|L(P_{M})-L(P_{N})\|_{p}=\epsilon

\displaystyle\|f-g\|_{p}=\epsilon

\Cref
Thm:Universality.

It suffices to prove that for any
\displaystyle p
-stable distance
\displaystyle d

\displaystyle d\leq\hat{d}_{I}^{p}
.

\displaystyle M,N
be merge trees with
\displaystyle\hat{d}_{I}^{p}(M,N)=\epsilon
and let

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\prime}>\epsilon

\displaystyle f,g:X\to\mathbb{R}

A study on the Poisson, geometric and Pascal distributions motivated by Chvátal’s conjecture
Fu-Bo Li, Kun Xu, Ze-Chun Hu

Abstract
Let B ⁡ ( n , p ) B(n,p) denote a binomial random variable with parameters n n and p p . Vasěk Chvátal conjectured that for any fixed n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , as m m ranges over { 0 , … , n } \{0,\ldots,n\} , the probability q m := P ⁡ ( B ⁡ ( n , m / n ) ≤ m ) q_{m}:=P(B(n,m/n)\leq m) is the smallest when m m is closest to 2 ​ n 3 \frac{2n}{3} . This conjecture has been solved recently. Motivated by this conjecture, in this paper, we consider the corresponding minimum value problem on the probability that a random variable is not more than its expectation, when its distribution is the Poisson distribution, the geometric distribution or the Pascal distribution.

1
Introduction

Let
B(n,p)
denote a binomial random variable with parameters
n
and
p
. Janson (2021) introduced the following conjecture suggested by Vašk Chvátal.

Conjecture 1
(Chvátal). For any fixed
n\geq 2
, as
m
ranges over
\{0,\ldots,n\}
, the probability
q_{m}:=P(B(n,m/n)\leq m)
is the smallest when

m
is closest to
\frac{2n}{3}
.

As to the probability of a binomial random variable exceeding its expectation, we refer to Doerr (2018), Greenberg and Mohri (2014), and Pelekis and Ramon (2016). Janson (2021) proved that Conjecture 1 holds for large
n
. Barabesi et al. (2023) and Sun (2021) gave an affirmative answer to Conjecture 1 for general
n\geq 2
.

Motivated by Conjecture 1, we will consider the corresponding minimum value problem on the probability that a random variable is not more than its expectation, when its distribution is the Poisson distribution, the geometric distribution or the Pascal distribution in Sections 2, 3, 4, respectively.

Sun et al.
[
7
]
considered the minimum value problem for the Gamma distribution among other things.

2
The Poisson distribution

Let
X_{\lambda}
denote a Poisson random variable with parameter
\lambda\,(\lambda>0)
. Then

\displaystyle P(X_{\lambda}\leq E[X_{\lambda}])=P(X_{\lambda}\leq\lambda)=\sum_{k=0}^{[\lambda]}\frac{\lambda^{k}e^{-\lambda}}{k!}=\left(1+\lambda+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2!}+\cdots+\frac{\lambda^{[\lambda]}}{[\lambda]!}\right)e^{-\lambda}.

Hereafter, for a real number
a,[a]
stands for the biggest integer which is not more than
a
.

The main result of this section is

Proposition 2.1

The function
P(X_{\lambda}\leq E[X_{\lambda}])
has no minimum value on
(0,\infty)
, but

\displaystyle\inf_{\lambda\in(0,\infty)}P(X_{\lambda}\leq\lambda)=\lim_{\lambda\uparrow 1}P(X_{\lambda}\leq\lambda)=\frac{1}{e}.

(2.1)

Proof.
For
\lambda\in(0,1)
, we have

P(X_{\lambda}\leq\lambda)=e^{-\lambda}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\inf_{\lambda\in(0,1)}P(X_{\lambda}\leq\lambda)=\lim_{\lambda\uparrow 1}e^{-\lambda}=\frac{1}{e}.

(2.2)

In the first version of this paper (see
[
8
]
), we used pure analysis method to give a long proof of

\inf_{\lambda\in[1,\infty)}P(X_{\lambda}\leq\lambda)>\frac{1}{e}.

The following is a much shorter alternagive proof to (
2.1
) suggested by the referee to the first version.

By the central limit theorem,

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}P(X_{\lambda}\leq\lambda)=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}P(\lambda^{-1/2}(X_{\lambda}-\lambda)\leq 0)=\frac{1}{2}.

Therefore, by virtue of (
2.2
), there is an integer
j
such that

\inf_{\lambda>j}P(X_{\lambda}\leq\lambda)>\frac{1}{e}=\inf_{\lambda\in(0,1)}P(X_{\lambda}\leq\lambda).

Since
P(X_{\lambda}\leq x)
is decreasing in
\lambda
for fixed
x
, it follows that

\inf_{\lambda>0}P(X_{\lambda}\leq\lambda)=\inf_{0<\lambda\leq j}P(X_{\lambda}\leq\lambda)=\inf_{0<\lambda\leq j}P(X_{\lambda}\leq[\lambda])=\min_{k=0,1,\ldots,j-1}P(X_{1+k}\leq k).

Next, since
n-B(n,(k+1)/n)
and
B(n,1-(k+1)/n)
have the same distribution, one obtains

\displaystyle P(X_{k+1}\geq k+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}P(B(n,(k+1)/n)\geq E[B(n,(k+1)/n)])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}P(B(n,1-(k+1)/n)\leq E[B(n,1-(k+1)/n]).

In addition, by
[
1
, Theorem 2]
, for large
n
,
P(B(n,1-(k+1)/n)\leq E[B(n,1-(k+1)/n])
is decreasing in
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,j-1\}
. Taking the limit,
P(X_{k+1}\geq k+1)
is decreasing in
k
, so that

P(X_{1+k}\leq k)=1-P(X_{1+k}\geq k+1)
is increasing in
k
. Hence

\min_{k=0,1,\ldots,j-1}P(X_{1+k}\leq k)=P(X_{1}\leq 0)=\frac{1}{e}.

The proof is complete.

3
The geometric distribution

Let
Y_{p}
denote a geometric random variable with parameter
p\,(0<p\leq 1)
. Then we have

\displaystyle P(Y_{p}\leq E[Y_{p}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(Y\leq 1/p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{[1/p]}p(1-p)^{k-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(p+p(1-p)+\cdots+p(1-p)^{[1/p]-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-(1-p)^{[1/p]}.

Define

\displaystyle f(p):=1-(1-p)^{[1/p]},\quad 0<p\leq 1.

(3.1)

The main result of this section is

Proposition 3.1

The function
f(p)
has no minimum value on
(0,1]
, but

\displaystyle\inf_{p\in(0,1]}f(p)=\lim_{p\downarrow\frac{1}{2}}f(p)=\frac{1}{2}.

(3.2)

Proof.
Let
x
be a positive integer. For any
1/p\in[x,x+1)
, we have

f(p)=1-(1-p)^{x}.

Then for
p\in(\frac{1}{x+1},\frac{1}{x})
,

f^{\prime}(p)=x(1-p)^{x-1}>0,

which implies that the function
f(p)
is strictly increasing on the interval
(\frac{1}{x+1},\frac{1}{x}]
. Thus we have

\displaystyle\inf_{p\in(\frac{1}{x+1},\frac{1}{x}]}f(p)=\lim_{p\downarrow\frac{1}{x+1}}f(p)=1-\left(1-\frac{1}{x+1}\right)^{x}.

Define a sequence
\{a_{n}\}
as follows:

\displaystyle a_{n}:=1-\left(1-\frac{1}{n+1}\right)^{n},\ \forall n\geq 1.

(3.3)

Since the sequence
(1+1/n)^{n}
is increasing and

a_{n}=1-\left(1-\frac{1}{n+1}\right)^{n}=1-\frac{1}{(1+1/n)^{n}},

it follows that
\min_{n\geq 1}a_{n}=a_{1}=1/2
.

By the analysis above, we know that
f(p)
has no minimum value on
(0,1]
, but its infimum satisfies

\displaystyle\inf_{p\in(0,1]}f(p)=\lim_{p\downarrow\frac{1}{2}}f(p)=a_{1}=\frac{1}{2}.

Hence (
3.2
) holds. The proof is complete.

4
The Pascal distribution

Let
B^{*}(r,p)
denote a Pascal random variable with parameters
r
(
r
is a positive integer) and
p\,(0<p\leq 1)
such that

\displaystyle P(B^{*}(r,p)=j)=\left(\begin{array}[]{l}j-1\\
r-1\end{array}\right)(1-p)^{j-r}p^{r},\ j=r,r+1,\cdots.

We know that the expectation is
E[B^{*}(r,p)]=r/p
.

An anonymous referee to the first version of this paper posed the following
conjecture
:

\displaystyle\inf_{0<p\leq 1}P\left(B^{*}(r,p)\leq r/p\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{n\geq 1}P\left(B\left(n+r-1,\frac{r}{n+r}\right)\geq r\right)

(4.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{n\geq r}P\left(B\left(n,\frac{r}{n+1}\right)\geq r\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P\left(B\left(r,\frac{r}{r+1}\right)\geq r\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{r}{r+1}\right)^{r},

and presented the proof to the first equality in (
4.2
) as follows:

\displaystyle P\left(B^{*}(r,p)\leq r/p\right)=P\left(B^{*}(r,p)\leq[r/p]\right)=P\left(B([r/p],p)\geq r\right).

For each
p\in(\frac{r}{n+r},\frac{r}{n+r-1}]
, one obtains

\displaystyle P(B([r/p],p)\geq r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(B(n+r-1,p)\geq r)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle P\left(B\left(n+r-1,\frac{r}{n+r}\right)\geq r\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{q\in(\frac{r}{n+r},\frac{r}{n+r-1}]}P(B([r/q],q)\geq r).

It follows that the first equality in (
4.2
) holds.

Define a function

\displaystyle f_{r}(p):=\sum_{k=r}^{[r/p]}\dbinom{k-1}{r-1}p^{r}(1-p)^{k-r},\ p\in(0,1].

Then Conjecture (
4.2
) becomes

\displaystyle\inf_{0<p\leq 1}f_{r}(p)=\left(\frac{r}{r+1}\right)^{r}.

(4.3)

When
\frac{r}{p}\in[n,n+1)(n\geq r,n\in\mathbb{N}
), we have

\displaystyle f_{r}(p):=\sum_{k=r}^{n}\dbinom{k-1}{r-1}p^{r}(1-p)^{k-r},\ p\in\left(\frac{r}{n+1},\frac{r}{n}\right].

For
k=r,\ldots,n
, define a function

g_{k}(p):=\dbinom{k-1}{r-1}p^{r}(1-p)^{k-r},\ p\in\left(\frac{r}{n+1},\frac{r}{n}\right].

By the condition that
\frac{r}{k}\geq\frac{r}{n}
, we get

\frac{dg_{k}(p)}{dp}=\dbinom{k-1}{r-1}\frac{kp^{r}(1-p)^{k-r}(\frac{r}{k}-p)}{p(1-p)}>0,\ \forall p\in\left(\frac{r}{n+1},\frac{r}{n}\right).

It follows that for any
k=r,\ldots,n
, the function
g_{k}(p)
is strictly increasing on
\left(\frac{r}{n+1},\frac{r}{n}\right]
. Then

\displaystyle\inf_{p\in(\frac{r}{n+1},\frac{r}{n}]}g_{k}(p)=\lim_{p\downarrow\frac{r}{n+1}}g_{k}(p)=\dbinom{k-1}{r-1}\left(\frac{r}{n+1}\right)^{r}\left(1-\frac{r}{n+1}\right)^{k-r}.

Thus

\displaystyle\inf_{p\in(\frac{r}{n+1},\frac{r}{n}]}f_{r}(p)=\lim_{p\downarrow\frac{r}{n+1}}f_{r}(p)=\sum_{k=r}^{n}\dbinom{k-1}{r-1}\left(\frac{r}{n+1}\right)^{r}\left(1-\frac{r}{n+1}\right)^{k-r}.

Define a number sequence

\displaystyle a_{r}(n)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=r}^{n}\dbinom{k-1}{r-1}\left(\frac{r}{n+1}\right)^{r}\left(1-\frac{r}{n+1}\right)^{k-r}

(4.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{r}{n+1}\right)^{r}\sum_{k=r}^{n}\dbinom{k-1}{r-1}\left(1-\frac{r}{n+1}\right)^{k-r},\quad\forall n\geq r.

Then (
4.3
) (or equivalently, Conjecture (
4.2
)) becomes

\displaystyle\min_{n\geq r}a_{r}(n)=a_{r}(r).

(4.5)

Up to now, we have not found the unified method to prove (
4.5
) for any
r\geq 2
.
In the following two subsections, we will give the proofs for the cases
r=2,3
, respectively.

4.1
Case
r=2

In this case, we have

\displaystyle a_{2}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2}{n+1}\right)^{2}\sum_{k=2}^{n}(k-1)\left(1-\frac{2}{n+1}\right)^{k-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{4}{9},&\mbox{if}\ n=2,\\
1-\frac{3n-1}{n+1}\left(\frac{n-1}{n+1}\right)^{n-1},&\mbox{if}\ n\geq 3.\end{array}\right.

Proposition 4.1

\displaystyle\min_{n\geq 2}a_{2}(n)=a_{2}(2).

(4.9)

Proof.
Define a number sequence

\displaystyle b_{2}(n):=\frac{3n-1}{n+1}\left(\frac{n-1}{n+1}\right)^{n-1},\ n\geq 3,

(4.10)

and a function

\displaystyle g_{2}(x):=\frac{3x-1}{x+1}\left(\frac{x-1}{x+1}\right)^{x-1},\ x\geq 3.

(4.11)

Then we have

\displaystyle g_{2}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{3x-1}{x+1}\left(\frac{x-1}{x+1}\right)^{x-1}\left(\frac{3}{3x-1}+\frac{1}{x+1}+\ln\frac{x-1}{x+1}\right).

(4.12)

Define a function

\displaystyle h_{2}(x):=\frac{3}{3x-1}+\frac{1}{x+1}+\ln\frac{x-1}{x+1},\ x\geq 3.

(4.13)

Then we have

\displaystyle h_{2}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{4(3x^{2}-2x+3)}{(x+1)^{2}(x-1)(3x-1)^{2}}.

It is easy to check that
3x^{2}-2x+3>0,\forall x\in(-\infty,\infty)
. Then we get

h_{2}^{\prime}(x)>0,\quad\forall x\geq 3,

which implies that
h_{2}(x)
is strictly increasing on
[3,\infty)
. Since

\displaystyle\ \lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}h_{2}(x)=\lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}\left(\frac{3}{3x-1}+\frac{1}{x+1}+\ln\frac{x-1}{x+1}\right)=0,

we get that
h_{2}(x)<0,\forall x\geq 3
. By (
4.12
) and (
4.13
), we know that
g_{2}^{\prime}(x)<0,\forall x\geq 3
. Thus, by (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), we get

\max_{n\geq 3}b_{2}(n)=b_{2}(3)=\frac{1}{2}<\frac{5}{9}.

By (
4.1
) and (
4.10
), we get

\min_{n\geq 2}a_{2}(n)=a_{2}(2)=\frac{4}{9},

i.e. (
4.9
) holds. The proof is complete.

4.2
Case
r=3

In this case, we have

\displaystyle a_{3}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{n+1}\right)^{3}\sum_{k=3}^{n}\dbinom{k-1}{2}\left(1-\frac{3}{n+1}\right)^{k-3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{27}{64},&\mbox{if}\ n=3,\\
1-\frac{17n^{2}-29n+8}{2(n+1)^{2}}\left(\frac{n-2}{n+1}\right)^{n-2},&\mbox{if}\ n\geq 4.\end{array}\right.

Proposition 4.2

\displaystyle\min_{n\geq 3}a_{3}(n)=a_{3}(3).

(4.17)

Proof.
Define a number sequence

\displaystyle b_{3}(n):=\frac{17n^{2}-29n+8}{2(n+1)^{2}}\left(\frac{n-2}{n+1}\right)^{n-2},\ n\geq 4.

(4.18)

We will prove

\displaystyle\max_{n\geq 4}b_{3}(n)=b_{3}(4),

(4.19)

which implies that

\min_{n\geq 4}a_{3}(n)=a_{3}(4)=1-\frac{328}{390625}>\frac{27}{64}.

and thus

\min_{n\geq 3}a_{3}(n)=a_{3}(3),

i.e. (
4.17
) holds.

In the following, we will prove (
4.19
). Define a function

\displaystyle g_{3}(x):=\frac{17x^{2}-29x+8}{2(x+1)^{2}}\left(\frac{x-2}{x+1}\right)^{x-2},\ x\geq 4.

(4.20)

We have

\displaystyle g_{3}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{17x^{2}-29x+8}{2(x+1)^{2}}\left(\frac{x-2}{x+1}\right)^{x-2}\left[\frac{34x-29}{17x^{2}-29x+8}+\frac{1}{x+1}+\ln\frac{x-2}{x+1}\right],\ x\geq 4.

(4.21)

Define a function

\displaystyle h_{3}(x):=\frac{34x-29}{17x^{2}-29x+8}+\frac{1}{x+1}+\ln\frac{x-2}{x+1},\ x\geq 4.

(4.22)

Then for any
x\geq 4
,

\displaystyle h_{3}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{27(17x^{4}-57x^{3}+105x^{2}-91x+54)}{(x+1)^{2}(x-2)(17x^{2}-29x+8)^{2}}>0.

Hence
h_{3}(x)
is strictly increasing on
[4,\infty)
. Since

\lim_{x\to\infty}h_{3}(x)=0,

we get
h_{3}(x)<0
for any
x\geq 4
. Then by (
4.21
) and (
4.22
), we get

g_{3}^{\prime}(x)<0,\ \forall x\geq 4,

which implies that
g_{3}(x)
is strictly decreasing on
[4,\infty)
. It follows that (
4.19
) holds. The proof is complete.

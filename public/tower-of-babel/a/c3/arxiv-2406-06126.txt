The obstalce scattering for the biharmonic equation
Chengyu Wu, Jiaqing Yang

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the obstacle scattering problem for biharmonic equations with a Dirichlet boundary condition in both two and three dimensions. Some basic properties are first derived for the biharmonic scattering solutions, which leads to a simple criterion for the uniqueness of the direct problem. Then a new type far-field pattern is introduced, where the correspondence between the far-field pattern and scattered field is established. Based on these properties, we prove the well-posedness of the direct problem in associated function spaces by utilizing the boundary integral equation method, which relys on a natural decomposition of the biharmonic operator and the theory of the pseudodifferential operator. Furthermore, the inverse problem for determining the obstacle is studied. By establishing some novel reciprocity relations between the far-field pattern and scattered field, we show that the obstacle can be uniquely recovered from the measurements at a fixed frequency.



keywords

biharmonic scattering, far-field pattern, boundary integral equations, well-posedness, reciprocity relation, inverse problem.

Introduction

The biharmonic scattering problems have important applications in various scientific fields and thus have arisen more interests in recent years. They play a significant part in the study of elasticity and the theory of vibration of beams, such as the beam equation
[
9
]
, the hinged plate configurations
[
9
]
, and the Stokes equation
[
11
]
, and the scattering by grating stacks
[
20
]
.

The present paper concerns the direct and inverse biharmonic scattering problems by impenetrable obstacles. Denote by
\Omega\in C^{3,\alpha}
a bounded domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

(d=2,3)
with connected complement. Consider the following biharmonic obstacle scattering problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta^{2}u-k^{4}u=0&{\rm in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega},\\[5.69054pt]
\mathcal{B}(u)=(0,0)&{\rm on}~\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
k>0
is the wave number,
u=u^{i}+u^{s}
denotes the total field in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
with
u^{i}
the incident wave and
u^{s}
the scattered wave, and
\mathcal{B}
stands for the boundary condition on
\partial\Omega
. There are various boundary conditions for the biharmonic scattering (cf.
[
2
,
9
,
11
]
), such as the Dirichlet condition
\mathcal{B}_{D}(u)=(u,\partial_{n}u)
with
n
the unit exterior normal on
\partial\Omega
, the Navier condition
\mathcal{B}_{N}(u)=(u,\Delta u)
, the Neumann condition
\mathcal{B}(u)=(\Delta u,\partial_{n}\Delta u)
(this is actually a special case of the Neumann condition) and
\mathcal{B}(u)=(u,\partial_{n}\Delta u),(\partial_{n}u,\Delta u),(\partial_{n}u,\partial_{n}\Delta u)
. In this paper, we mainly consider the Dirichlet condition
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{D}
. To ensure the well-posedness of problem (
1
), we impose an analogue of the classical Sommerfeld radiation condition (cf.
[
23
,
8
,
19
]
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{r}w-ikw=o\left(r^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\right),~~r=|x|\rightarrow\infty,~w=u^{s},\Delta u^{s},

(1.4)

uniformly in all directions
\hat{x}=x/|x|
. In this work, we will define a new type far-field pattern for the biharmonic scattering and study the inverse problem of uniquely determining the obstacle
\Omega
from the knowledge of the scattered fields or far-field patterns at a fixed frequency.

Comparing to the scattering problems in the acoustic, elastic and electromagnetic cases, the biharmonic scattering problems are much less studied till now. The high order of the differential operator bring numerous difficulties in the reserach and many classical methods do not work any longer. For the direct biharmonic obstacle scattering problems, we refer to
[
2
]
where the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map for biharmonic scattering was defined in two dimensions and the corresponding Fredholm properties were derived, which yielded an equivalent variational formulation and the well-posedness of biharmonic scattering with Dirichlet condition and other boundary conditions followed. Besides the variational method, the boundary integral equation method is also an usual method to derive the well-posedness in the scattering theory. For the biharmonic scattering, the boundary integral equation method was considered in
[
8
]
, where the well-posedness of the Dirichlet problem was obtained and relating convergence analysis was carried out. However, the results in
[
8
]
was only obtained in two dimensions with the boundary
\partial\Omega
required to be analytic and parametrizable, which is a rather strong restriction. As for the inverse scattering by biharmonic obstacles, in
[
3
]
, the unique recovery of a Dirichlet obstacle was obtained by measuring the scattered field with the incident point source at a circle. Moreover, the linear sampling method was then justified. Here we further refer some papers concerning other types problems in the biharmonic scattering. In
[
23
]
, the Saito’s formula in the biharmonic case was proved, which leads to the unique identification of the perturbation of the biharmonic operator. The biharmonic inverse source problems are considered in
[
18
,
17
,
19
]
. For the biharmonic scattering in the nonlinear case, we refer the readers to
[
10
]
and the references quoted there. We also mention some work about the inverse boundary value problems for the bi- and polyharmonic operators, see e.g.,
[
1
,
5
,
16
,
25
,
14
]
, where the well-known complex geometrical optics solutions are generalized to the biharmonic case.

Inspired by the natural decomposition of the biharmonic operator into the Helmholtz and modified Helmholtz operators, we investigate on the direct and inverse biharmonic obstacle scattering problems. First, we study the basic properties of the biharmonic scattering solutions, which leads to a simple criterion for the uniqueness of the direct problem. Further, we define a new type far-field pattern for the biharmonic scattering and establish its correspondence to the scattered field. For the well-posedness of the direct problem, in fact, it is easy to find that the biharmonic equation with some boundary conditions, such as the Navier condition, after decomposition can be solved by the usual methods in the acoustic scattering. However, different from the acoustic case, it is hard to develop a unified way to deal with all the boundary conditions simultaneously, since in the Dirichlet case the biharmonic equation is spiltted to an interior transmission problem in the exterior domain, which is known that can not be managed by the classical methods. With the variational method being studied in great details in
[
2
]
, the corresponding boundary integral equation method, which has its own significance in both mathematics and practice, is much less investigated, since the only relating work
[
8
]
contains certain strong restrictions. We therefore further develop the boundary integral equation method for the Dirichlet case in the present study. After spiltting the biharmonic scattering solution into Helmholtz and modified Helmholtz parts, and expressing both parts as a combination of the single- and double-layer potentials, we reduce the direct problem to an equivalent boundary integral equation. Through an elaborate analysis, the Fredholm properties of the corresponding integral operators are derived utilizing the theory of the pseudodifferential operators and the well-posedness follows. In contrast to
[
2
,
8
]
, here we obtain the well-posedness in both two and three dimensions. Furthermore, we weaken the conditions in
[
8
]
to only requiring
\partial\Omega\in C^{3,\alpha}
. Finally, we prove some uniqueness theorems in determining the obstacle. Applying the reciprocity relations of the far-field pattern and scattered field, we show that the biharmonic obstacle can be uniquely identified by the measurements of the scattered fields or far-field patterns at a fixed frequency, while almost all the preceding results on the inverse biharmonic scattering problems are using multi-frequency measurements.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we fix some notations and recall some estimates about the fundamental solution. In Section
3
, we discover some foundational properties for the biharmonic scattering, which yields a simple criterion for the uniqueness of the direct problem. Moreover, a new type far-field pattern is defined. Section
4
is about the establishment of the boundary integral equation method, and the well-posedness of the direct problem is obtained for Dirichlet boundary condition. Finally, in Section
5
, we prove the uniqueness results for the inverse problem of determing the obstacle by measurements at a fixed frequency, which follows from the reciprocity relations for far-field pattern and scattered field.

\displaystyle H_{\nu}^{(1)}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}O(|x|^{-\nu}),&\nu>0,\\[2.84526pt]

\displaystyle H_{\nu}^{(1)}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi x}}e^{i(x-\frac{1}{2}\nu\pi-\frac{1}{4}\pi)}+O\left(x^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right),

(2.5)

\displaystyle H_{-m}^{(1)}=(-1)^{m}H_{m}^{(1)}~~~~{\rm and}~~~~K_{-m}=K_{m}

\displaystyle G_{k}(|x|)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}O(1),&{\text{as}}~|x|\rightarrow 0,\\[5.69054pt]

Moreover, from the recurrence relations of Hankel and Macdonald’s functions, it follows that

\displaystyle{\rm NewA}G_{k}(|x|)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\frac{ikx}{8k^{2}|x|}\left(H_{1}^{(1)}(k|x|)+\frac{2i}{\pi}K_{1}(k|x|)\right),&d=2,\\[8.53581pt]

\displaystyle\Delta G_{k}(|x|)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\frac{i}{8}\left(H_{0}^{(1)}(k|x|)-\frac{2i}{\pi}K_{0}(k|x|)\right),&d=2,\\[8.53581pt]

which indicates the asymptotic behaviors

\displaystyle{\rm NewA}G_{k}(|x|)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}O\left(|x|^{2-d}\right),&{\text{as}}~|x|\rightarrow 0,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle O\left(|x|^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\right),&{\text{as}}~|x|\rightarrow+\infty,\end{array}\right.

and

\displaystyle\Delta G_{k}(|x|)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}O(|\ln(|x|)|),&d=2,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle O\left(|x|^{-1}\right),&d=3,\end{array}\right.

as
|x|\rightarrow 0
and

\displaystyle\Delta G_{k}(|x|)=O\left(|x|^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\right),~~~{\text{as}}~|x|\rightarrow+\infty.

Also we see that the fundamental solution

3
Some basic properties

In this section, we prove some basic properties and define a new type far-field pattern for the biharmonic scattered solutions, which is fundamental in the theory of biharmonic scattering. Particularly, a simple criterion for the uniqueness of the direct problem is discovered and the correspondence between the far-field and scattered field is established.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose
u^{s}\in C^{4}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{3}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\Omega)
satisfies
\Delta^{2}u^{s}-k^{4}u^{s}=0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
and the radiation condition
(
1.4
)
, then

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{R}}(|\Delta u^{s}|^{2}+|u^{s}|^{2})ds=O(1),~~{\rm as}~R\rightarrow+\infty.

Proof.

We choose
R>0
large enough such that
\overline{\Omega}\subset B_{R}
. From the radiation conditon (
1.4
), we have that

\displaystyle 0~

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\partial B_{R}}\left|\partial_{r}w-ikw\right|^{2}ds

(3.1)

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\partial B_{R}}\left(|\partial_{r}w|^{2}+k^{2}|w|^{2}+2k{\rm Im}(w\partial_{r}\overline{w})\right)ds

for
w=u^{s},\Delta u^{s}
. Integration by parts over
B_{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
yields that

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\int_{\partial B_{R}}w\partial_{r}\overline{w}ds={\rm Im}\int_{\partial\Omega}w\partial_{n}\overline{w}ds+{\rm Im}\int_{B_{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}w\Delta\overline{w}dx,

which implies

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\int_{\partial B_{R}}\Delta u^{s}\partial_{r}\Delta\overline{u}^{s}ds+k^{4}{\rm Im}\int_{\partial B_{R}}u^{s}\partial_{r}\overline{u}^{s}ds

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\int_{\partial\Omega}\Delta u^{s}\partial_{n}\Delta\overline{u}^{s}ds+k^{4}{\rm Im}\int_{\partial\Omega}u^{s}\partial_{n}\overline{u}^{s}ds+{\rm Im}\int_{B_{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}\Delta u^{s}(\Delta^{2}\overline{u}^{s}-k^{4}\overline{u}^{s})dx

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\int_{\partial\Omega}(\Delta u^{s}\partial_{n}\Delta\overline{u}^{s}+k^{4}u^{s}\partial_{n}\overline{u}^{s})ds.

Hence, by (
3.1
) we derive that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\partial B_{R}}[|\partial_{r}\Delta u^{s}|^{2}+k^{2}|\Delta u^{s}|^{2}+k^{4}(|\partial_{r}u^{s}|^{2}+k^{2}|u^{s}|^{2})]ds

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle-2k{\rm Im}\int_{\partial\Omega}(\Delta u^{s}\partial_{n}\Delta\overline{u}^{s}+k^{4}u^{s}\partial_{n}\overline{u}^{s})ds,

(3.2)

and the conclusion follows.
∎

Theorem 2

\displaystyle~\left.-G_{k}(|x-y|)\partial_{n}\Delta u^{s}(y)-\Delta_{y}G_{k}(|x-y|)\partial_{n}u^{s}(y)\right)ds(y).

\displaystyle 0~

\displaystyle=\int_{S_{R,\varepsilon}}\left(u^{s}(y)(\Delta_{y}^{2}-k^{4})G_{k}(|x-y|)-G_{k}(|x-y|)(\Delta^{2}-k^{4})u^{s}(y)\right)dy

\displaystyle~\qquad\qquad\left.-G_{k}(|x-y|)\partial_{n}\Delta u^{s}(y)-\Delta_{y}G_{k}(|x-y|)\partial_{n}u^{s}(y)\right)ds(y)

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3},

We see that

\displaystyle I_{2}=\int_{\partial B_{R}}

\displaystyle\left(u^{s}(y)(\partial_{n(y)}-ik)\Delta_{y}G_{k}(|x-y|)+\Delta u^{s}(y)(\partial_{n(y)}-ik)G_{k}(|x-y|)\right.

\displaystyle~\left.-G_{k}(|x-y|)(\partial_{r}-ik)\Delta u^{s}(y)-\Delta_{y}G_{k}(|x-y|)(\partial_{r}-ik)u^{s}(y)\right)ds(y).

By Lemma

, we get

2

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}(x)}

\displaystyle\left(\Delta u^{s}(y)\partial_{n(y)}G_{k}(|x-y|)-G_{k}(|x-y|)\partial_{n}\Delta u^{s}(y)\right.

\displaystyle~\left.-\Delta_{y}G_{k}(|x-y|)\partial_{n}u^{s}(y)\right)ds(y)\rightarrow 0

as

\displaystyle\displaystyle\partial_{n(y)}\Delta_{y}G_{k}(|x-y|)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})}{2\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d-1}}+O\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{d-2}}\right)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{\varepsilon}(x)}u^{s}(y)\partial_{n(y)}\Delta_{y}G_{k}(|x-y|)ds(y)\rightarrow u^{s}(x),~~{\text{as}}~\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

Arguing analogously as above, we can get the representation theorem in bounded domains.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose
u\in C^{4}(\Omega)\cap C^{3}(\overline{\Omega})
and
\Delta^{2}u-k^{4}u=0
in
\Omega
, then for
x\in\Omega
we have

\displaystyle u(x)=\int_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle\left(u(y)\partial_{n(y)}\Delta_{y}G_{k}(|x-y|)+\Delta u(y)\partial_{n(y)}G_{k}(|x-y|)\right.

\displaystyle~\left.-G_{k}(|x-y|)\partial_{n}\Delta u(y)-\Delta_{y}G_{k}(|x-y|)\partial_{n}u(y)\right)ds(y).

The following result is a simple application of Rellich’s Lemma.

Theorem 4
.

Let
u\in C^{4}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega})
solves
\Delta^{2}u-k^{4}u=0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
. If further

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\partial B_{R}}(|\Delta u|^{2}+|u|^{2})ds=0,

then
\Delta u-k^{2}u=0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
.

Proof.

Clearly,
(\Delta-k^{2})u\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega})
is a solution to the Helmholtz equation with

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{R}}|(\Delta-k^{2})u|^{2}ds~

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial B_{R}}(|(\Delta-k^{2})u|^{2}+|(\Delta+k^{2})u|^{2})ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B_{R}}2(|\Delta u|^{2}+k^{4}|u|^{2})ds\rightarrow 0,~~R\rightarrow+\infty.

Thus by Rellich’s Lemma
[
4
, Theorem 3.5]
, we have
\Delta u-k^{2}u=0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
.
∎

Next we are interesting in the behavior of the biharmonic solutions outside some large disk (ball), which are essential for the later establishment of the uniqueness result for the direct biharmonic scattering problem.

Lemma 5
.

Let

be such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle v^{s}(r,\theta)=\sum\limits_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a_{m}K_{m}(kr)e^{im\theta},~~~~{\rm if}~d=2,

\displaystyle\displaystyle v^{s}(r,\theta,\varphi)=\sum\limits_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}a_{l}^{m}k_{l}(kr)Y_{l}^{m}(\theta,\varphi),~~~~{\rm if}~d=3,

where

The proof is analogously to the Helmholtz equation case, we thus omit it here.

Lemma 8
.

Suppose
u^{s}\in C^{4}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{3}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\Omega)
satisfies
\Delta^{2}u^{s}-k^{4}u^{s}=0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
and the radiation condition
(

)
, then

\displaystyle k^{2}{\rm Im}\int_{\partial\Omega}(u^{s}\partial_{n}\Delta\overline{u}^{s}+\Delta u^{s}\partial_{n}\overline{u}^{s})ds=-{\rm Im}\int_{\partial\Omega}(\Delta u^{s}\partial_{n}\Delta\overline{u}^{s}+k^{4}u^{s}\partial_{n}\overline{u}^{s})ds

It suffices to show
\displaystyle{\rm Im}\int_{\partial\Omega}(\Delta u^{s}+k^{2}u^{s})\partial_{n}(\Delta\overline{u}^{s}+k^{2}\overline{u}^{s})ds=0
. Applying Green’s theorem in
B_{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{R}}(\Delta u^{s}+k^{2}u^{s})\partial_{n}(\Delta\overline{u}^{s}+k^{2}\overline{u}^{s})ds-\int_{\partial\Omega}(\Delta u^{s}+k^{2}u^{s})\partial_{n}(\Delta\overline{u}^{s}+k^{2}\overline{u}^{s})ds

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}(\Delta u^{s}+k^{2}u^{s})(\Delta^{2}\overline{u}^{s}+k^{2}\Delta\overline{u}^{s})dx+\int_{B_{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}|{\rm NewA}(\Delta u^{s}+k^{2}u^{s})|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}k^{2}|\Delta u^{s}+k^{2}u^{s}|^{2}dx+\int_{B_{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}|{\rm NewA}(\Delta u^{s}+k^{2}u^{s})|^{2}dx,

which implies

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\int_{\partial B_{R}}(\Delta u^{s}+k^{2}u^{s})\partial_{n}(\Delta\overline{u}^{s}+k^{2}\overline{u}^{s})ds={\rm Im}\int_{\partial\Omega}(\Delta u^{s}+k^{2}u^{s})\partial_{n}(\Delta\overline{u}^{s}+k^{2}\overline{u}^{s})ds.

Now by Theorem
6
, for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{R}
,

\displaystyle u^{s}(r,\theta)=\sum\limits_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left(a_{m}H_{m}^{(1)}(kr)+b_{m}K_{m}(kr)\right)e^{im\theta},~~~~{\rm if}~d=2,

\displaystyle u^{s}(r,\theta,\varphi)=\sum\limits_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}\left(a_{l}^{m}h_{l}^{(1)}(kr)+b_{l}^{m}k_{l}(kr)\right)Y_{l}^{m}(\theta,\varphi),~~~~{\rm if}~d=3,

with
a_{m},b_{m},a_{l}^{m},b_{l}^{m}
constants. From Remark
7
we see that

\displaystyle(\Delta u^{s}+k^{2}u^{s})|_{\partial B_{R}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2k^{2}b_{m}K_{m}(kR)e^{im\theta},&d=2,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}2k^{2}b_{l}^{m}k_{l}(kr)Y_{l}^{m}(\theta,\varphi),&d=3,\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\partial_{n}(\Delta u^{s}+k^{2}u^{s})|_{\partial B_{R}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2k^{3}b_{m}K_{m}^{\prime}(kR)e^{im\theta},&d=2,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}2k^{3}b_{l}^{m}k_{l}^{\prime}(kr)Y_{l}^{m}(\theta,\varphi),&d=3,\end{array}\right.

which further indicates

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{R}}(\Delta u^{s}+k^{2}u^{s})\partial_{n}(\Delta\overline{u}^{s}+k^{2}\overline{u}^{s})ds=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle 2\pi R\sum\limits_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}4k^{5}|b_{m}|^{2}K_{m}(kR)\overline{K_{m}^{\prime}(kR)},\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle 4\pi R^{2}\sum\limits_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}4k^{5}|b_{l}^{m}|^{2}k_{l}(kr)\overline{k_{l}^{\prime}(kr)},\end{array}\right.

for
d=2,3
. Since
K_{m}(r),k_{l}(r)
take real values for
r>0
, we deduce that

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\int_{\partial B_{R}}(\Delta u^{s}+k^{2}u^{s})\partial_{n}(\Delta\overline{u}^{s}+k^{2}\overline{u}^{s})ds=0.

The proof is thus complete.
∎

.

Under the assumptions in Lemma

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\int_{\partial\Omega}(u^{s}\partial_{n}\Delta\overline{u}^{s}+\Delta u^{s}\partial_{n}\overline{u}^{s})ds\leq 0,

then
\Delta u^{s}-k^{2}u^{s}=0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
.

Proof.

This would be a immediate consequence of (
3.2
), Theorem
4
and Lemma

.

Theorem 10
.

Suppose
u^{s}\in C^{4}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{3}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\Omega)
satisfies
\Delta^{2}u^{s}-k^{4}u^{s}=0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
and the radiation condition
(
1.4
)
, if further

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\int_{\partial\Omega}(u^{s}\partial_{n}\Delta\overline{u}^{s}+\Delta u^{s}\partial_{n}\overline{u}^{s})ds\leq 0~~{\rm and}~~{\rm Re}\int_{\partial\Omega}u^{s}\partial_{n}\overline{u}^{s}\geq 0,

then
u^{s}=0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
.

Proof.

From Corollary
9
, we have
\Delta u^{s}-k^{2}u^{s}=0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
. Hence, it follows that

\displaystyle 0~

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}(\Delta u^{s}-k^{2}u^{s})\overline{u}^{s}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B_{R}}\partial_{n}u^{s}\overline{u}^{s}ds-\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{n}u^{s}\overline{u}^{s}ds-\int_{B_{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}(|{\rm NewA}u^{s}|^{2}+k^{2}|u^{s}|^{2})dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B_{R}}(\partial_{n}u^{s}-iku^{s})\overline{u}^{s}ds+ik\int_{\partial B_{R}}|u^{s}|^{2}ds-\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{n}u^{s}\overline{u}^{s}ds

\displaystyle\quad~-\int_{B_{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}(|{\rm NewA}u^{s}|^{2}+k^{2}|u^{s}|^{2})dx.

Taking the real part of the equation yields

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}(|{\rm NewA}u^{s}|^{2}+k^{2}|u^{s}|^{2})dx\leq{\rm Re}\int_{\partial B_{R}}(\partial_{n}u^{s}-iku^{s})\overline{u}^{s}ds.

Due to the radiation condition (
1.4
) and Lemma
1
, it is derived that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\partial B_{R}}(\partial_{n}u^{s}-iku^{s})\overline{u}^{s}ds=0,

which implies
u^{s}\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega})
and
\|u^{s}\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega})}=0
. Thus
u^{s}=0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
.
∎

Remark 11

Since we already have

In the following, we define a new type far-field pattern for the biharmonic scattering. It is seen that the scattered field
u^{s}
to problem (
1
) can be spilted into two parts
u^{s}_{-}:=\Delta u^{s}-k^{2}u^{s}
and

, it is the radiating solution to the Helmholtz equation and thus has the asymptotic behavior (see

\displaystyle u^{s}_{-}(x)=\frac{e^{ik|x|}}{|x|^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}\left\{u^{s}_{-,\infty}(\hat{x})+O\left(\frac{1}{|x|}\right)\right\},~~~|x|\rightarrow\infty,

(3.9)

uniformly in all directions
\hat{x}=x/|x|
, where

\displaystyle u^{s}_{-,\infty}(\hat{x})=\frac{ie^{-i\frac{d-1}{4}\pi}k^{\frac{d-3}{2}}}{2(2\pi)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(u^{s}_{-}(y)\frac{\partial e^{-ik\hat{x}\cdot y}}{\partial n(y)}-\frac{\partial u^{s}_{-}}{\partial n}(y)e^{-ik\hat{x}\cdot y}\right)ds(y).

(3.10)

As for the second part

Theorem 12
.

Suppose
v^{s}\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\Omega)

(3.11)

uniformly in all directions
\hat{x}
with

\displaystyle v^{s}_{\infty}(\hat{x})=\frac{k^{\frac{d-3}{2}}}{2(2\pi)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(v^{s}(y)\frac{\partial e^{k\hat{x}\cdot y}}{\partial n(y)}-\frac{\partial v^{s}}{\partial n}(y)e^{k\hat{x}\cdot y}\right)ds(y).

(3.12)

Proof.

,

]
.
∎

Theorem 13
.

Under the conditions in Theorem
12
, if

, then

in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5
and Parseval’s equality, we see that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{R}}|v^{s}|^{2}ds=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle 2\pi R\sum\limits_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|a_{m}|^{2}|K_{m}(kR)|^{2},&d=2,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle 4\pi R^{2}\sum\limits_{l\in{\mathbb{N}}}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}|a_{l}^{m}|^{2}|k_{l}(kr)|^{2},&d=3.\end{array}\right.

Further, it is obtained from (

) that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}Re^{2kR}\int_{\partial B_{R}}|v^{s}|^{2}ds=0,

which indicates that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\lim\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}R^{2}e^{2kR}|a_{m}|^{2}|K_{m}(kR)|^{2}=0,&d=2,\\[8.53581pt]

Due to the asymptotic expansion (

and (

a_{m}=0
for all

for all

. Therefore,

in

∎

Now we know that

4
Well-posedness

In this section, we want to obtain the well-posedness of biharmonic obstacle scattering problem with Dirichlet boundary condition, while the uniqueness is given by Theorem
10
. Basically, we turn problem (
1
) into a couple of Helmholtz and modified Helmholtz equations and develop corresponding boundary integral equation method.
Different from
[
8
]
, the problem is then reduced to equivalent boundary integral equations by expressing the solutions as combined single- and double-layer potentials. The Fredholm properties of the relating integral operators are derived using the theory of pseudodifferential operators and then the well-posedness follows.

Denote by
\Phi_{k}
the fundamental solution of the Helmholtz equation in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with wave number
k
, which is

\displaystyle\Phi_{k}(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{i}{4}H_{0}^{(1)}(k|x-y|),&d=2,\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\frac{e^{ik|x-y|}}{4\pi|x-y|},&d=3.\end{array}\right.

We introduce the famous single- and double-layer potentials in the acoustic scattering,

\displaystyle(SL_{k}\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\partial\Omega}\Phi_{k}(x,y)\varphi(y)ds(y),~~~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\partial\Omega,

\displaystyle(DL_{k}\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial\Phi_{k}(x,y)}{\partial\nu(y)}\varphi(y)ds(y),~~~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\partial\Omega.

Also, we give the definitions of the associated boundary integral operators,

\displaystyle(S_{k}\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\partial\Omega}\Phi_{k}(x,y)\varphi(y)ds(y),~~~x\in\partial\Omega,

\displaystyle(K_{k}\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial\Phi_{k}(x,y)}{\partial\nu(y)}\varphi(y)ds(y),~~~x\in\partial\Omega,

\displaystyle(K^{\prime}_{k}\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial\Phi_{k}(x,y)}{\partial\nu(x)}\varphi(y)ds(y),~~~x\in\partial\Omega,

\displaystyle(T_{k}\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu(x)}\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial\Phi_{k}(x,y)}{\partial\nu(y)}\varphi(y)ds(y),~~~x\in\partial\Omega.

Further, we remind the volume potential

\displaystyle(\widetilde{SL}_{k}\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{k}(x,y)\varphi(y)dy,~~~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

The basic properties of these operators can be found in
[
4
,
7
]
.

We spilt the biharmonic equation into a couple of Helmholtz and modified Helmholtz equations. For
(u^{s}_{+},u^{s}_{-})
we see that problem (
1
) with
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{D}
becomes

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta u^{s}_{+}-k^{2}u^{s}_{+}=0,~~\Delta u^{s}_{-}+k^{2}u^{s}_{-}=0&{\rm in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega},\\[5.69054pt]
u^{s}_{+}-u^{s}_{-}=2k^{2}f,~~\partial_{n}u^{s}_{+}-\partial_{n}u^{s}_{-}=2k^{2}g&{\rm on}~\partial\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\partial_{r}w-ikw=o\left(r^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\right),~~r=|x|\rightarrow\infty,&w=u^{s}_{\pm},\end{array}\right.

which is an interior tranmission problem in the exterior domain and can not be handled by the usual methods. It is also remarked that problem (
1
) with some boundary condition, such as
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{N}
, after decomposition can be easily solved by the classical methods in the acoustic scattering. Here we require the boundary data
(f,g)\in H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)\times H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
. We want to seek the solutions
u^{s}_{\pm}
of problem (
4
) in the form of

\displaystyle u^{s}_{+}=SL_{ik}\varphi-DL_{ik}\psi+i\eta SL_{ik}(S_{0}^{2}\psi),~~~u^{s}_{-}=SL_{k}\varphi-DL_{k}\psi~~{\rm in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}.

with density
(\varphi,\psi)\in H^{-3/2}(\partial\Omega)\times H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
and the real parameter
\eta\neq 0
. Now problem (
4
) can be equivalently reduced to the following boundary integral equations on
\partial\Omega
(note that the mapping properties and the jump relations of these operators in weaker spaces can be found in
[
6
]
):

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}S_{ik}-S_{k}&-K_{ik}+K_{k}+i\eta S_{ik}S_{0}^{2}\\
-K_{ik}^{\prime}+K_{k}^{\prime}&T_{ik}-T_{k}+i\eta K_{ik}^{\prime}S_{0}^{2}-\frac{i\eta}{2}S_{0}^{2}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\varphi\\
\psi\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}2k^{2}f\\
-2k^{2}g\end{array}\right).

Denote by
M(k)
the matrix operator in the left hand side of (
4
), and set

\displaystyle Z(k)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}S_{ik}-S_{k}&-K_{ik}+K_{k}\\
-K_{ik}^{\prime}+K_{k}^{\prime}&T_{ik}-T_{k}\end{array}\right).

From
[
6
]
it is known that
Z(k):H^{-3/2}(\partial\Omega)\times H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)\rightarrow H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)\times H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
is bounded. Further, since
\partial\Omega\in C^{3,\alpha}
, we deduce from
[
12
]
that
M(k)-Z(k):H^{-3/2}(\partial\Omega)\times H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)\rightarrow H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)\times H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
is compact. We first consider the uniqueness of equation (
4
).

Theorem 17
.

The equation
(
4
)
has at one solution, i.e., the operator
M(k)
is injective.

Proof.

Suppose
M(k)(\varphi,\psi)^{T}=(0,0)^{T}
. Define

\displaystyle v=SL_{ik}\varphi-DL_{ik}\psi+i\eta SL_{ik}(S_{0}^{2}\psi),~~~w=SL_{k}\varphi-DL_{k}\psi~~{\rm in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\partial\Omega.

Then
(v,w)
satisfy the homogeneous problem (
4
), which indicates that
v=w=0
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
by Theorem
10
. By the notations
(\cdot)^{\pm}
we denote the traces from the exterior (+) and interior (–) of the boundary
\partial\Omega
. It follows that
v^{+}=\partial_{n}v^{+}=w^{+}=\partial_{n}w^{+}=0
on
\partial\Omega
, which further implies by the jump relations that

\displaystyle v^{-}=w^{-}=\psi,~~~\partial_{n}v^{-}=\varphi+i\eta S_{0}^{2}\psi,~~~\partial_{n}w^{-}=\varphi~~{\rm on}~\partial\Omega.

Since
\Delta v-k^{2}v=\Delta w+k^{2}w=0
in
\Omega
, we yields that

\displaystyle 2i\eta\int_{\partial\Omega}|S_{0}\psi|^{2}ds\;

\displaystyle=2i\eta\int_{\partial\Omega}\overline{\psi}S_{0}^{2}\psi ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\left[\partial_{n}(v^{-}-w^{-})(\overline{v}^{-}+\overline{w}^{-})-(v^{-}-w^{-})\partial_{n}(\overline{v}^{-}+\overline{w}^{-})\right]ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\left[\Delta(v-w)(\overline{v}+\overline{w})-(v-w)\Delta(\overline{v}+\overline{w})\right]dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}k^{2}\left(|v+w|^{2}-|v-w|^{2}\right)dx.

Taking the imaginary part we obtain
S_{0}\psi=0
on
\partial\Omega
and hence
\psi=0
. Finally, from
v^{+}=\psi=0
on
\partial\Omega
, we deduce that
S_{ik}\varphi=0
on
\partial\Omega
, which also leads to
\varphi=0
. The proof is thus complete.
∎

Next we study the Fredholm property of the operator
M(k)
. Consider an auxiliary operator

\displaystyle\hat{Z}(k)=\frac{2}{3}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}S_{i2|k|}-S_{i|k|}&-K_{i2|k|}+K_{i|k|}\\
-K_{i2|k|}^{\prime}+K_{i|k|}^{\prime}&T_{i2|k|}-T_{i|k|}\end{array}\right).

We refer a significant property of this operator.

Theorem 18
.

(
[
6
, Lemma 3.7]
)

\hat{Z}(k):H^{-3/2}(\partial\Omega)\times H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)\rightarrow H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)\times H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
is coercive.

Lemma 19
.

The pseudodifferential operator
\widetilde{SL}_{ik}-\widetilde{SL}_{k}-2/3(\widetilde{SL}_{i2|k|}-\widetilde{SL}_{i|k|})
is of order
-5
.

Proof.

We first consider the two-dimensional case. It is seen that

\displaystyle\left(\widetilde{SL}_{ik}-\widetilde{SL}_{k}-2/3(\widetilde{SL}_{i2|k|}-\widetilde{SL}_{i|k|})\right)\varphi(x)=\int_{\Omega}a(x,x-y)\varphi(y)dy

with the kernel

\displaystyle a(x,z):=\frac{i}{4}\left(H_{0}^{(1)}(ik|z|)-H_{0}^{(1)}(k|z|)-\frac{2}{3}\left(H_{0}^{(1)}(i2|kz|)-H_{0}^{(1)}(i|kz|)\right)\right).

From the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 3.2]
we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{i}{4}\left(H_{0}^{(1)}(ik|z|)-H_{0}^{(1)}(k|z|)\right)

\displaystyle=f(x,z)+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}p_{j+2}(x,z)\ln|z|,

\displaystyle\frac{i}{4}\left(H_{0}^{(1)}(i2|kz|)-H_{0}^{(1)}(i|kz|)\right)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{f}(x,z)+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\widetilde{p}_{j+2}(x,z)\ln|z|,

where
f,\widetilde{f}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and

\displaystyle p_{j+2}(x,z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\text{if $j$ is odd},\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{(-1)^{p+1}}{(p+1)!^{2}}(i^{j+2}-1)k^{j+2}\left(\frac{|z|}{2}\right)^{j+2}&\text{if $j=2p$},\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\widetilde{p}_{j+2}(x,z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\text{if $j$ is odd},\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{(-1)^{p+1}}{(p+1)!^{2}}(2^{j+2}-1)(ik)^{j+2}\left(\frac{|z|}{2}\right)^{j+2}&\text{if $j=2p$}.\end{array}\right.

Hence, by direct calculation we derive that

\displaystyle a(x,z)=\hat{f}(x,z)+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\hat{p}_{j+4}(x,z)\ln|z|

with
\hat{f}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and
\hat{p}_{j+4}(x,z)=0
if
j
is odd and

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{j+4}(x,z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{(-1)^{p}}{(p+2)!^{2}}\left(i^{j}-1-\frac{2}{3}(2^{j+4}-1)i^{j}\right)k^{j+4}\left(\frac{|z|}{2}\right)^{j+4}

for
j=2p
. Note that the functions
\hat{p}_{q}
satisfy
\hat{p}_{q}(x,tz)=t^{q}\hat{p}_{q}(x,z)
, which implies that the kernel of
\widetilde{SL}_{ik}-\widetilde{SL}_{k}-2/3(\widetilde{SL}_{i2|k|}-\widetilde{SL}_{i|k|})
is a pseudohomogeneous kernel of degree 4. Thus by
[
11
, Theorem 7.1.1]
we yield that
\widetilde{SL}_{ik}-\widetilde{SL}_{k}-2/3(\widetilde{SL}_{i2|k|}-\widetilde{SL}_{i|k|})
is a pseudodifferential operator of order -6 (thus of order -5).

Now we consider the three-dimensional case. The corresponding kernel is

\displaystyle a(x,z)=\frac{1}{4\pi|z|}\left(e^{-k|z|}-e^{ik|z|}-\frac{2}{3}(e^{-2k|z|}-e^{-k|z|})\right).

Computing directly from the series expansion of the exponential function we obtain that

\displaystyle a(x,z)=-\frac{1+3i}{12\pi}k+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{j+2}(x,z),

where

\displaystyle a_{j+2}(x,z)=\frac{-i^{j+1}}{4\pi(j+3)!}\left(i^{j+3}-1-\frac{2}{3}(2^{j+3}-1)i^{j+3}\right)k^{j+3}|z|^{j+2},~~~{\rm for}~j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Since
a_{p}(x,tz)=t^{p}a_{p}(x,z)
,
a
is a pseudohomogeneous kernel of degree 2, which indicates that
\widetilde{SL}_{ik}-\widetilde{SL}_{k}-2/3(\widetilde{SL}_{i2|k|}-\widetilde{SL}_{i|k|})
is a pseudodifferential operator of order -5.
∎

Theorem 20
.

Z(k)-\hat{Z}(k):H^{-3/2}(\partial\Omega)\times H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)\rightarrow H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)\times H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
is compact.

Proof.

Combining Lemma
19
and
[
11
, Theorem 8.5.8]
, we obtain that
SL_{ik}-SL_{k}-2/3(SL_{i2|k|}-SL_{i|k|}):H^{-3/2}(\partial\Omega)\rightarrow H^{3}(\Omega)
is bounded. Since
DL_{k}\varphi=-{\rm NewA}SL_{k}(\varphi n)
, it follows that
DL_{ik}-DL_{k}-2/3(DL_{i2|k|}-DL_{i|k|}):H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)\rightarrow H^{3}(\Omega)
is bounded. Then by the classical trace theorems we deduce the conclusion.
∎

Theorem 21
.

Given
(f,g)\in H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)\times H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)
, then the following problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta^{2}u^{s}-k^{4}u^{s}=0&{\rm in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega},\\[5.69054pt]
\mathcal{B}_{D}(u^{s})=(f,g)&{\rm on}~\partial\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\partial_{r}w-ikw=o\left(r^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\right),~~r=|x|\rightarrow\infty,&w=u^{s},\Delta u^{s},\end{array}\right.

has a unique solution
u^{s}\in H^{2}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega})
such that

\displaystyle\|u^{s}\|_{H^{2}(B_{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega})}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}\right),

where
R>0
is sufficiently large and
C
is a positive constant depending only on
R
.

Proof.

From Theorems
17
,
18
and
20
, we see that problem (
4
) has a unique solution
(u^{s}_{+},u^{s}_{-})=(\Delta u^{s}+k^{2}u^{s},\Delta u^{s}-k^{2}u^{s})
in the form of

\displaystyle u^{s}_{+}=SL_{ik}\varphi-DL_{ik}\psi+i\eta SL_{ik}(S_{0}^{2}\psi),~~~u^{s}_{-}=SL_{k}\varphi-DL_{k}\psi~~{\rm in}~{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega},

where
(\varphi,\psi)
is the unique solution to the boundary integral equation (
4
) such that

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{H^{-3/2}(\partial\Omega)}+\|\psi\|_{H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}\right)

with
C>0
a constant. By
[
6
, Corollary 3.3]
, we further have that
2k^{2}u^{s}=u^{s}_{+}-u^{s}_{-}=(SL_{ik}-SL_{k})\varphi-(DL_{ik}-DL_{k})\psi+i\eta SL_{ik}(S_{0}^{2}\psi)\in H^{2}(B_{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega})
and

\displaystyle\|u^{s}\|_{H^{2}(B_{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega})}~

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|(SL_{ik}-SL_{k})\varphi\|_{H^{2}(B_{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega})}+\|(DL_{ik}-DL_{k})\psi\|_{H^{2}(B_{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega})}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad~+\|SL_{ik}(S_{0}^{2}\psi)\|_{H^{2}(B_{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\varphi\|_{H^{-3/2}(\partial\Omega)}+\|\psi\|_{H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|f\|_{H^{3/2}(\partial\Omega)}+\|g\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)}\right),

which is the desired a priori estimate.
∎

Remark 22
.

For problem
(
1
)
with
\mathcal{B}(u)=(\Delta u,\partial_{n}\Delta u)
, it can be verified that
(u^{s}_{+},-u^{s}_{-})
satisfies problem
(
4
)
. Therefore, following the same process, we can solve problem
(
1
)
in the case
\mathcal{B}(u)=(\Delta u,\partial_{n}\Delta u)
.

5
The inverse problem

In this section, we study the inverse problem of uniquely determining the obstacle
\Omega
from measurements at a fixed frequency, which relies heavily on the reciprocity relations of our new type far-field pattern and the scattered field.

Denote by
u^{s}(x,a,b)
the solutions to problem (
1
) corresponding to the incident wave
u^{i}(x,\hat{y},b):=e^{ibx\cdot\hat{y}}
or
u^{i}(x,y,b):=\Phi_{b}(x,y)
with
a=\hat{y}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
or
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
and
b=k,ik
, respectively. Let
u(x,a,b)=u^{s}(x,a,b)+u^{i}(x,a,b)
.
We begin with exploring some reciprocity relations for the far-field pattern and the scattered field.

Theorem 23
.

For biharmonic obstacle
\Omega
with
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{D}
, we have the reciprocity relations

\displaystyle\quad\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u^{s}_{+}(y,\hat{x},k)&u^{s}_{-}(y,\hat{x},k)\\
u^{s}_{+}(y,\hat{x},ik)&u^{s}_{-}(y,\hat{x},ik)\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2(2\pi)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}{ik^{\frac{d-3}{2}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{i\frac{d-1}{4}\pi}u^{s}_{-,\infty}(-\hat{x},y,ik)&e^{i\frac{d-1}{4}\pi}u^{s}_{-,\infty}(-\hat{x},y,k)\\
iu^{s}_{+,\infty}(-\hat{x},y,ik)&iu^{s}_{+,\infty}(-\hat{x},y,k)\end{array}\right)

provided that
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
and
\hat{x}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
.

Proof.

By the boundary condition we see that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle\left(u(\cdot,\hat{x},b_{1})\partial_{n}\Delta u(\cdot,y,b_{2})+\Delta u(\cdot,\hat{x},b_{1})\partial_{n}u(\cdot,y,b_{2})\right.

(5.3)

\displaystyle~\left.-\partial_{n}\Delta u(\cdot,\hat{x},b_{1})u(\cdot,y,b_{2})-\partial_{n}u(\cdot,\hat{x},b_{1})\Delta u(\cdot,y,b_{2})\right)ds=0

for
b_{1},b_{2}=k,ik
. In the following, we only consider the case
(b_{1},b_{2})=(k,k)
, the other three cases can be managed analogously.

Integration by parts over
\Omega
yields

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle\left(u^{i}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)\partial_{n}\Delta u^{i}(\cdot,y,k)+\Delta u^{i}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)\partial_{n}u^{i}(\cdot,y,k)\right.

(5.4)

\displaystyle~\left.-\partial_{n}\Delta u^{i}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)u^{i}(\cdot,y,k)-\partial_{n}u^{i}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)\Delta u^{i}(\cdot,y,k)\right)ds=0.

From Green’s theorem, the radiation condition (
1.4
) and Lemma
1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle\left(u^{s}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)\partial_{n}\Delta u^{s}(\cdot,y,k)+\Delta u^{s}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)\partial_{n}u^{s}(\cdot,y,k)\right.

(5.5)

\displaystyle~\left.-\partial_{n}\Delta u^{s}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)u^{s}(\cdot,y,k)-\partial_{n}u^{s}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)\Delta u^{s}(\cdot,y,k)\right)ds=0.

Combining (
5.3
)-(
5.5
), since
u=u^{i}+u^{s}
, it is derived that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle\left(u^{s}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)\partial_{n}\Delta u^{i}(\cdot,y,k)+\Delta u^{s}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)\partial_{n}u^{i}(\cdot,y,k)\right.

\displaystyle~\left.-\partial_{n}\Delta u^{s}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)u^{i}(\cdot,y,k)-\partial_{n}u^{s}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)\Delta u^{i}(\cdot,y,k)\right)ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle\left(u^{i}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)\partial_{n}\Delta u^{s}(\cdot,y,k)+\Delta u^{i}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)\partial_{n}u^{s}(\cdot,y,k)\right.

\displaystyle~\left.-\partial_{n}\Delta u^{i}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)u^{s}(\cdot,y,k)-\partial_{n}u^{i}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)\Delta u^{s}(\cdot,y,k)\right)ds.

Note that
u^{s}=1/2k^{2}(u^{s}_{+}-u^{s}_{-})
,
\Delta u^{s}=1/2(u^{s}_{+}+u^{s}_{-})
and
u^{i}(\cdot,a,k)
satisfies the Helmholtz equation for
a=\hat{x},y
. By the representation theorem, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle\left(u^{s}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)\partial_{n}\Delta u^{i}(\cdot,y,k)+\Delta u^{s}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)\partial_{n}u^{i}(\cdot,y,k)\right.

\displaystyle~\left.-\partial_{n}\Delta u^{s}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)u^{i}(\cdot,y,k)-\partial_{n}u^{s}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)\Delta u^{i}(\cdot,y,k)\right)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle\left(u^{s}_{-}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)\partial_{n}u^{i}(\cdot,y,k)-u^{i}(\cdot,y,k)\partial_{n}u^{s}_{-}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)\right)ds=u^{s}_{-}(y,\hat{x},k).

Moreover, from the expression of the far-field pattern (
3.10
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(u^{i}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)\partial_{n}\Delta u^{s}(\cdot,y,k)+\Delta u^{i}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)\partial_{n}u^{s}(\cdot,y,k)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\quad\;\left.-\partial_{n}\Delta u^{i}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)u^{s}(\cdot,y,k)-\partial_{n}u^{i}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)\Delta u^{s}(\cdot,y,k)\right)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(u^{s}_{-}(\cdot,y,k)\partial_{n}u^{i}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)-u^{i}(\cdot,\hat{x},k)\partial_{n}u^{s}_{-}(\cdot,y,k)\right)ds

\displaystyle=\frac{2(2\pi)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}{ie^{-i\frac{d-1}{4}\pi}k^{\frac{d-3}{2}}}u^{s}_{-,\infty}(-\hat{x},y,k),

which implies

\displaystyle u^{s}_{-}(y,\hat{x},k)=\frac{2(2\pi)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}{ie^{-i\frac{d-1}{4}\pi}k^{\frac{d-3}{2}}}u^{s}_{-,\infty}(-\hat{x},y,k).

The other three reciprocity relations can be obtained from other three choices of
(b_{1},b_{2})
, i.e.,
(b_{1},b_{2})=(k,ik),(ik,k)
and
(ik,ik)
. The proof is thus complete.
∎

The next two theorems about other kinds reciprocity relations follow closely as Theorem
23
, the detailed proof is omitted.

Theorem 24
.

The reciprocity relations hold

\displaystyle\quad\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{i\frac{d-1}{4}\pi}u^{s}_{+,\infty}(\hat{x},\hat{y},k)&u^{s}_{-,\infty}(\hat{x},\hat{y},k)\\
u^{s}_{+,\infty}(\hat{x},\hat{y},ik)&u^{s}_{-,\infty}(\hat{x},\hat{y},ik)\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u^{s}_{-,\infty}(-\hat{y},-\hat{x},ik)&u^{s}_{-,\infty}(-\hat{y},-\hat{x},k)\\
u^{s}_{+,\infty}(-\hat{y},-\hat{x},ik)&e^{i\frac{d-1}{4}\pi}u^{s}_{+,\infty}(-\hat{y},-\hat{x},k)\end{array}\right)

for biharmonic obstacle
\Omega
with
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{D}
and
\hat{x},\hat{y}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
.

Theorem 25
.

For biharmonic obstacle
\Omega
with
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{D}
, we have the symmetry relations

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u^{s}_{+}(x,y,k)&u^{s}_{-}(x,y,k)\\
u^{s}_{+}(x,y,ik)&u^{s}_{-}(x,y,ik)\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u^{s}_{-}(y,x,ik)&u^{s}_{-}(y,x,k)\\
u^{s}_{+}(y,x,ik)&u^{s}_{+}(y,x,k)\end{array}\right),

where
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
.

Remark 26
.

It can be easily seen that Theorems
23
-
25
also hold for
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{N}
,
\mathcal{B}(u)=(\partial_{n}u,\partial_{n}\Delta u),(\Delta u,\partial_{n}\Delta u)
.

We now are at the position to establish uniqueness results for the inverse biharmonic obstacle scattering problems in the Dirichlet case
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{D}
.

Theorem 27
.

Suppose
\Omega
and
\widetilde{\Omega}
are two biharmonic obstacles with
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{D}
. If, for all
\hat{x},\hat{y}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
, one of the following holds
:

(i)
(u^{s}_{+,\infty}(\hat{x},\hat{y},k),u^{s}_{+,\infty}(\hat{x},\hat{y},ik))=(\widetilde{u}^{s}_{+,\infty}(\hat{x},\hat{y},k),\widetilde{u}^{s}_{+,\infty}(\hat{x},\hat{y},ik))
;

(ii)
(u^{s}_{-,\infty}(\hat{x},\hat{y},k),u^{s}_{-,\infty}(\hat{x},\hat{y},ik))=(\widetilde{u}^{s}_{-,\infty}(\hat{x},\hat{y},k),\widetilde{u}^{s}_{-,\infty}(\hat{x},\hat{y},ik))
;

then
\Omega=\widetilde{\Omega}
.

Proof.

The proof is standard as in
[
13
]
. We only consider the situation that (i) is satisfied. Denote by
G
the unbounded connected part of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{(\Omega\cup\widetilde{\Omega})}
. By Theorem
13
, we know that
(u^{s}_{+}(x,\hat{y},k),u^{s}_{+}(x,\hat{y},ik))=(\widetilde{u}^{s}_{+}(x,\hat{y},k),\widetilde{u}^{s}_{+}(x,\hat{y},ik))
for all
x\in G
and
\hat{y}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
. Due to the reciprocity relations Theorem
23
, we see that
(u^{s}_{+,\infty}(\hat{y},x,ik),u^{s}_{-,\infty}(\hat{y},x,ik))=(\widetilde{u}^{s}_{+,\infty}(\hat{y},x,ik),\widetilde{u}^{s}_{-,\infty}(\hat{y},x,ik))
with
\hat{y}\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
and
x\in G
. Furthermore, by Theorem
15
, it immediately follows that
u^{s}(x,y,ik)=\widetilde{u}^{s}(x,y,ik)
for all
x,y\in G
.

Now suppose
\Omega\neq\widetilde{\Omega}
. Then, without loss of generality, there exists a
x_{0}\in\partial G
such that
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
x_{0}\notin\overline{\widetilde{\Omega}}
. Choose
\delta>0
such that the sequence

\displaystyle x_{j}:=x_{0}+\frac{\delta}{j}n(x_{0})\in B\cap G,~~~j\in{\mathbb{N}},

where
B
is a small ball centered at
x_{0}
satisfying
B\cap\overline{\widetilde{\Omega}}=\emptyset
. We know that
u^{s}(x,x_{j},ik)=\widetilde{u}^{s}(x,x_{j},ik)
for
x\in G
and
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. From the positive distance between
x_{j}
and
\widetilde{\Omega}
, since
k^{4}
is not a biharmonic Dirichlet eigenvalue in
\widetilde{\Omega}
, by Theorem
21
it yields that
\widetilde{u}^{s}(x,x_{j},ik)
is uniformly bounded in
H^{2}(B\cap\Omega)
for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, which further indicates by the trace theorem that
\|\widetilde{u}^{s}(x,x_{j},ik)\|_{H^{3/2}(B\cap\partial\Omega)}\leq C
for all
j
and some positive constant
C
. On the other hand, since
u^{s}(x,x_{j},ik)=\widetilde{u}^{s}(x,x_{j},ik)
for
x\in G
, from the boundary condition on
\partial\Omega
we have that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{u}^{s}(x,x_{j},ik)\|_{H^{3/2}(B\cap\partial\Omega)}=\|\Phi_{ik}(x,x_{j})\|_{H^{3/2}(B\cap\partial\Omega)}\rightarrow\infty,~~~n\rightarrow\infty,

which is a contradiction. Therefore,
\Omega=\widetilde{\Omega}
.
∎

Theorem 28
.

Let
\Omega
and
\widetilde{\Omega}
be two biharmonic obstacles with
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{D}
and
\overline{\Omega\cup\widetilde{\Omega}}\subset B_{R}
for some
R>0
. If, for all
x,y\in\partial B_{R}
, one of the following is satisfied
:

(i)
(u^{s}_{+}(x,y,k),u^{s}_{+}(x,y,ik))=(\widetilde{u}^{s}_{+}(x,y,k),\widetilde{u}^{s}_{+}(x,y,ik))
;

(ii)
(u^{s}_{-}(x,y,k),u^{s}_{-}(x,y,ik))=(\widetilde{u}^{s}_{-}(x,y,k),\widetilde{u}^{s}_{-}(x,y,ik))
;

then
\Omega=\widetilde{\Omega}
.

Proof.

Here, for simplicity, we only prove case (ii). Also denote by
G
the unbounded connected part of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\overline{(\Omega\cup\widetilde{\Omega})}
. Due to the uniqueness of the exterior Dirichlet Helmholtz equation, we deduce from (ii) that
u^{s}_{-}(x,y,b)=\widetilde{u}^{s}_{-}(x,y,b)
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus B_{R}
,
y\in\partial B_{R}
and
b=k,ik
. Then by analyticity we know
u^{s}_{-}(x,y,b)=\widetilde{u}^{s}_{-}(x,y,b)
for all
x\in G
,
y\in\partial B_{R}
and
b=k,ik
. Further, the symmetry relations in Theorem
25
yield that
u^{s}_{\pm}(y,x,k)=\widetilde{u}^{s}_{\pm}(y,x,k)
for all
y\in\partial B_{R}
and
x\in G
, which implies
(u^{s}(y,x,k),\Delta u^{s}(y,x,k))=(\widetilde{u}^{s}(y,x,k),\Delta\widetilde{u}^{s}(y,x,k))
with
y\in\partial B_{R}
and
x\in G
. From the uniqueness of problem (
1
) with
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{N}
in Theorem
10
, it is obtained that
u^{s}(y,x,k)=\widetilde{u}^{s}(y,x,k)
for
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B_{R}
and
x\in G
. By analyticity, we further deduce
u^{s}(y,x,k)=\widetilde{u}^{s}(y,x,k)
for
x,y\in G
. Finally, following the same line as the proof of Theorem
27
we can conclude that
\Omega=\widetilde{\Omega}
.
∎

Remark 29
.

Though the part
u^{s}_{+}
decays exponentially at infinity and thus hard to measure at practice, it is sufficient for the uniqueness results to hold that only the condition
(ii)
in Theorems
27
and
28
is fullfilled, while
u^{s}_{-}
and
u^{s}_{-,\infty}
behaves like the usual scattered fields and far-field patterns in the acoustic scattering. Further, since the proofs of Theorem
27
and
28
rely heavily on the reciprocity relations, it can be verified that similar results also hold for
\mathcal{B}(u)=(\Delta u,\partial_{n}\Delta u)
.

Xiaoxia Wang, Chang Xu ∗

Abstract
. Inspired by the recent work of El Bachraoui, we present some new q q -supercongruences on triple and quadruple sums of basic hypergeometric series. In particular, we give a q q -supercongruence modulo the fifth power of a cyclotomic polynomial, which is a q q -analogue of the quadruple sum of Van Hamme’s supercongruence (G.2).

Primary 33D15; Secondary 11A07, 11B65

1.
Introduction

In 1913, Ramanujan (see
[
3
]
) proposed some well-known hypergeometric series without any proof, including

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(8k+1)\frac{\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{4}_{k}}{k!^{4}}=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{2}}.

Here
(a)_{n}=a(a+1)\cdots(a+n-1)
is the Pochhammer symbol and
\Gamma(x)
is the gamma function.
In 1997, Van Hamme
[
15
]
studied partial sums of Ramanujan’s summation formular for
1/\pi

and conjectured
13
Ramanujan-type supercongruences, involving

\displaystyle\mathrm{(G.2)}\quad\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{p-1}{4}}(8k+1)\frac{\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{4}_{k}}{k!^{4}}\equiv\frac{p\Gamma_{p}\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)\Gamma_{p}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma_{p}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)}\pmod{p^{3}},\quad\mathrm{if}\quad p\equiv 1\pmod{4}.

Here and in what follows,
p
is an odd prime and
the
p
-adic gamma function is defined as

\Gamma_{p}(x)=\lim_{m\rightarrow x}\left(-1\right)^{m}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<k<m\\
(k,p)=1\end{subarray}}k,

where the limit is for
m
tending to
x

p
-adically in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

The Ramanujan-type supercongruences and their
q
-analogues have attracted a number of experts.
The reader interested in
q
-analogues of supercongruences is referred to

[
4
,
6
,
13
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
20
]
.
Especially,
a
q
-analogue of Van Hamme’s (G.2) was proposed by Liu and Wang
[
11
]
as follows: for integers
n\equiv 1\pmod{4}
,

\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{n-1}{4}}[8k+1]\frac{(q;q^{4})^{4}_{k}}{(q^{4};q^{4})^{4}_{k}}q^{2k}\equiv\frac{(q^{2};q^{4})_{\frac{n-1}{4}}}{(q^{4};q^{4})_{\frac{n-1}{4}}}[n]q^{\frac{1-n}{4}}\pmod{[n]\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}}.

(1.1)

We now need to introduce some necessary definitions and concepts.
Let
a
,
q
be complex numbers with
\left|q\right|<1
and
n
a nonnegative integer.
The
q
-integer
is defined as
[n]=[n]_{q}=1+q+\cdots+q^{n-1}
.
And the
q
-shifted factorial
is defined by

(a;q)_{0}=1\quad\mathrm{and}\quad(a;q)_{n}=(1-a)(1-aq)\cdots(1-aq^{n-1}).

Usually, the multiple
q
-shifted factorial is directly written as

(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r};q)_{m}=(a_{1};q)_{m}(a_{2};q)_{m}\cdots(a_{r};q)_{m},

where
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}\cup\left\{\infty\right\}
.
Moreover, the
n
-th
cyclotomic polynomial
in
q
is represented by
\Phi_{n}(q)
:

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}(q)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant k\leqslant n\\
\gcd(n,k)=1\end{subarray}}\left(q-\zeta^{k}\right),

where
\zeta
is an
n
-th primitive root of unity.

By transforming
q
-supercongruences on double sums into the ones from squares of truncated
basic hypergeometric series,
El Bachraoui
[
1
]
obtained the following result: for any positive odd integer
n
with
\gcd(n,6)=1
,

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=0}^{k}c_{q}(j)c_{q}(k-j)\equiv q^{1-n}[n]^{2}\pmod{[n]\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}},

where
c_{q}(k)=[8k+1](q;q^{2})^{2}_{k}(q;q^{2})_{2k}q^{2k^{2}}/((q^{6};q^{6})^{2}_{k}(q^{2};q^{2})_{2k})
.
Recently, many experts paid attention to El Bachraoui’s work and obtained some new results (see, for example,
[
5
,
14
,
10
]
).

Inspired by the above work, in this paper,
by some essential tools including
the ‘creative microscoping’ method introduced by Guo and Zudilin
[
8
]
and
the Chinese remainder theorem for coprime polynomials, we shall
investigate several
q
-supercongruences on triple and quadruple sums.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d
and
n
be positive integers with
d\geq 3
and
n\equiv 1\pmod{d}
. For
k\geq 0
, let

c_{q}(k)=[2dk+1]\frac{(q;q^{d})^{4}_{k}}{(q^{d};q^{d})^{4}_{k}}q^{(d-2)k}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{{i+j+k\leq n-1}}c_{q}(i)c_{q}(j)c_{q}(k)\equiv[n]^{3}q^{\frac{3(1-n)}{d}}\frac{(q^{2};q^{d})^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}{(q^{d};q^{d})^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}\pmod{[n]\Phi_{n}(q)^{3}}.

(1.2)

Letting
d=3
in Theorem
1.1
, we get the following result.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
n
be a positive integer with
n\equiv 1\pmod{3}
. For
k\geq 0
, let

c_{q}(k)=[6k+1]\frac{(q;q^{3})^{4}_{k}}{(q^{3};q^{3})^{4}_{k}}q^{k}.

Then, modulo
[n]\Phi_{n}(q)^{3}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{{i+j+k\leq n-1}}c_{q}(i)c_{q}(j)c_{q}(k)\equiv[n]^{3}q^{1-n}\frac{(q^{2};q^{3})^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{3}}}{(q^{3};q^{3})^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{3}}}.

Note that He
[
9
]
obtained the following Ramanujan-type
supercongruence: for primes
p\geq 5
,

\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{p-1}{3}}(6k+1)\frac{\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{4}_{k}}{k!^{4}}\equiv-p\Gamma_{p}\left(\tfrac{1}{3}\right)^{3}\pmod{p^{4}},\quad\mathrm{if}\ p\equiv 1\pmod{3},

and Liu and Wang
[
12
]
presented several different

q
-analogues of He’s supercongruence.

Similarly, setting
d=4
in Theorem
1.1
yields a new
q
-supercongruence related to Van Hamme’s (G.2) supercongruence.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
n
be a positive integer with
n\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. For
k\geq 0
, let

c_{q}(k)=[8k+1]\frac{(q;q^{4})^{4}_{k}}{(q^{4};q^{4})^{4}_{k}}q^{2k}.

Then, modulo
[n]\Phi_{n}(q)^{3}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{{i+j+k\leq n-1}}c_{q}(i)c_{q}(j)c_{q}(k)\equiv[n]^{3}q^{\frac{3(1-n)}{4}}\frac{(q^{2};q^{4})^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{4}}}{(q^{4};q^{4})^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{4}}}.

(1.3)

Moreover, we shall give a
q
-analogue of the quadruple sum of (G.2) as follows.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
n
be a positive integer with
n\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. For
k\geq 0
, let

c_{q}(k)=[8k+1]\frac{(q;q^{4})^{4}_{k}}{(q^{4};q^{4})^{4}_{k}}q^{2k}.

Modulo
[n]\Phi_{n}(q)^{4}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{{j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}+j_{4}\leq n-1}}c_{q}(j_{1})c_{q}(j_{2})c_{q}(j_{3})c_{q}(j_{4})\equiv[n]^{8}q^{4(1-n)}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{n-1}{4}}\frac{(q,q,q^{3};q^{4})_{k}}{(q^{4},q^{4},q^{5};q^{4})_{k}}q^{4k}\right)^{4}.

(1.4)

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. We shall prove Theorem
1.1
in the next section by making use of the ‘creative microscoping’
method and the Chinese remainder theorem for coprime polynomials.
Finally, in Section 3, we give a proof of
Theorem
1.4
through establishing its parametric generalization.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We first give an important result due to El Bachraoui
[
2
]
, which plays an important role in our proof.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
d
,
n
,
t
be positive integers with
d\geq t\geq 2
and
n\equiv 1\pmod{d}
.
Let
\{c(k)\}^{\infty}_{k=0}
be a sequence of complex numbers.
If
c(k)=0
for
(n-1)/d<k<n
, then

\sum_{{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{t}\leq n-1}}c(k_{1})\cdots c(k_{t})=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{n-1}{d}}c(k)\right)^{t},

(2.1)

Furthermore, if
c(ln+k)/c(ln)=c(k)
for all nonnegative integers
k
and
l
such that
0\leq k\leq n-1
,
then,

\displaystyle\sum_{{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{t}=ln+k}}c(k_{1})c(k_{2})\cdots c(k_{t})

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\sum_{{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{t}=k}}c(k_{1})c(k_{2})\cdots c(k_{t})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{l}c(s_{1}n)\sum_{s_{2}=0}^{l-s_{1}}c(s_{2}n)\cdots\sum_{s_{t-1}=0}^{l-s_{1}-\cdots-s_{t-2}}c(s_{t-1}n)c\left((l-s_{1}-\cdots-s_{t-1})n\right).

(2.2)

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, we need to confirm the following two lemmas based on the result presented in
[
7
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
n
,
d
be positive integers with
d\geq 3
and
n\equiv 1\pmod{d}
,
a
and
b
indeterminates. For
k\geq 0
, let

z_{q}(k)=[2dk+1]\frac{\left(q,aq,q/a,q/b;q^{d}\right)_{k}}{\left(q^{d},aq^{d},q^{d}/a,bq^{d};q^{d}\right)_{k}}b^{k}q^{(d-2)k}.

Then, we have, modulo
[n](1-aq^{n})(a-q^{n})
,

\displaystyle\sum_{{i+j+k\leq n-1}}z_{q}(i)z_{q}(j)z_{q}(k)\equiv[n]^{3}\frac{\left(b/q\right)^{\frac{3(n-1)}{d}}\left(q^{2}/b;q^{d}\right)^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}{\left(bq^{d};q^{d}\right)^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}.

(2.3)

Proof.

For
n=1
, the result is clearly true. We now assume that
n
is an integer with
n>1
.
Recall that Guo and Schlosser
[
7
, Equation (4.3)]
gave the following result:
modulo
{[n](1-aq^{n})(a-q^{n})(b-q^{n})}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{M}z_{q}(k)

\displaystyle\equiv[n]\frac{\left(b/q\right)^{\frac{n-1}{d}}\left(q^{2}/b;q^{d}\right)_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}{\left(bq^{d};q^{d}\right)_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}\frac{(b-q^{n})(ab-1-a^{2}+aq^{n})}{(a-b)(1-ab)}

\displaystyle\quad+[n]\frac{(q,q^{d-1};q^{d})_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}{(aq^{d},q^{d}/a;q^{d})_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}\frac{(1-aq^{n})(a-q^{n})}{(a-b)(1-ab)},

(2.4)

where
M=(n-1)/d
or
n-1
.
It is clear to see that
\left(q;q^{d}\right)_{k}\equiv 0\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}
for
(n-1)/d<k<n
. This means that

z_{q}(k)\equiv 0\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}
for
(n-1)/d<k<n
. Therefore, employing Lemma
2.1
with
t=3
produces

\sum_{{i+j+k\leq n-1}}z_{q}(i)z_{q}(j)z_{q}(k)\equiv 0\pmod{\Phi_{n}(q)}.

(2.5)

Let
\zeta\neq 1
be an
n
-th root of unity, not necessarily primitive.
Namely,
\zeta
is a primitive
m
-th root of unity with
m\mid n
and
m>1
.
From (
2.5
), we get

\sum_{{i+j+k\leq m-1}}z_{\zeta}(i)z_{\zeta}(j)z_{\zeta}(k)=0.

(2.6)

It is not difficult to check that
z_{\zeta}(lm+s)/z_{\zeta}(lm)=z_{\zeta}(s)
for nonnegative integers
l
and
s
with
0\leq s\leq m-1
.
Applying Lemma
2.1
with
t=3
again, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{{i+j+k\leq n-1}}z_{\zeta}(i)z_{\zeta}(j)z_{\zeta}(k)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}\sum_{i+j+k=r}z_{\zeta}(i)z_{\zeta}(j)z_{\zeta}(k)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{l=0}^{\frac{n}{m}-1}\sum_{s=0}^{m-1}\sum_{i+j+k=lm+s}z_{\zeta}(i)z_{\zeta}(j)z_{\zeta}(k)

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{l=0}^{\frac{n}{m}-1}\sum_{a=0}^{l}z_{\zeta}(am)\sum_{t=0}^{l-a}z_{\zeta}(tm)z_{\zeta}((l-a-t)m)\sum_{s=0}^{m-1}\sum_{i+j+k=s}z_{\zeta}(i)z_{\zeta}(j)z_{\zeta}(k)

\displaystyle\quad=0.

Since the above equality is true for any
n
-th root of unity
\zeta\neq 1
, we conclude that

\sum_{{i+j+k\leq n-1}}z_{q}(i)z_{q}(j)z_{q}(k)\equiv 0\pmod{[n]}.

(2.7)

On the other hand, letting
a=q^{n}
or
q^{-n}
in (
2.4
), we have

\sum_{k=0}^{M}\tilde{z}_{q}(k)=[n]\frac{\left(b/q\right)^{\frac{n-1}{d}}\left(q^{2}/b;q^{d}\right)_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}{\left(bq^{d};q^{d}\right)_{\frac{n-1}{d}}},

where

\tilde{z}_{q}(k)=[2dk+1]\frac{\left(q,q^{1+n},q^{1-n},q/b;q^{d}\right)_{k}}{\left(q^{d},q^{d+n},q^{d-n},bq^{d};q^{d}\right)_{k}}b^{k}q^{(d-2)k}.

Note the fact that
\tilde{z}_{q}(k)=0
for
(n-1)/d<k<n
. Using Lemma
2.1
with
t=3
again, we arrive at

\sum_{{i+j+k\leq n-1}}\tilde{z}_{q}(i)\tilde{z}_{q}(j)\tilde{z}_{q}(k)=[n]^{3}\frac{\left(b/q\right)^{\frac{3(n-1)}{d}}\left(q^{2}/b;q^{d}\right)^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}{\left(bq^{d};q^{d}\right)^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}.

Then, we have the following
q
-congruence involving a variable
a
: modulo
\left(1-aq^{n}\right)\left(a-q^{n}\right)
,

\sum_{{i+j+k\leq n-1}}z_{q}(i)z_{q}(j)z_{q}(k)\equiv[n]^{3}\frac{\left(b/q\right)^{\frac{3(n-1)}{d}}\left(q^{2}/b;q^{d}\right)^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}{\left(bq^{d};q^{d}\right)^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}.

(2.8)

From (
2.7
) and (
2.8
),
we conclude that (
2.8
) is true modulo
[n]\left(1-aq^{n}\right)\left(a-q^{n}\right)
,
as desired.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
n
,
d
be positive integers with
d\geq 3
and
n\equiv 1\pmod{d}
,
a
and
b
indeterminates. For
k\geq 0
, let

z_{q}(k)=[2dk+1]\frac{\left(q,aq,q/a,q/b;q^{d}\right)_{k}}{\left(q^{d},aq^{d},q^{d}/a,bq^{d};q^{d}\right)_{k}}b^{k}q^{(d-2)k}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{{i+j+k\leq n-1}}z_{q}(i)z_{q}(j)z_{q}(k)\equiv[n]^{3}\frac{\left(q,q^{d-1};q^{d}\right)^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}{\left(aq^{d},q^{d}/a;q^{d}\right)^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}\pmod{b-q^{n}}.

(2.9)

Proof.

Letting
b=q^{n}
in (
2.4
), we get

\sum_{k=0}^{M}\hat{z}_{q}(k)=[n]\frac{\left(q,q^{d-1};q^{d}\right)_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}{\left(aq^{d},q^{d}/a;q^{d}\right)_{\frac{n-1}{d}}},

where

\hat{z}_{q}(k)=[2dk+1]\frac{\left(q,aq,q/a,q^{1-n};q^{d}\right)_{k}}{\left(q^{d},aq^{d},q^{d}/a,q^{d+n};q^{d}\right)_{k}}q^{(n+d-2)k}.

Note that
\hat{z}_{q}(k)=0
for
(n-1)/d<k<n
. From Lemma
2.1
with
t=3
, we obtain

\sum_{{i+j+k\leq n-1}}\hat{z}_{q}(i)\hat{z}_{q}(j)\hat{z}_{q}(k)=[n]^{3}\frac{\left(q,q^{d-1};q^{d}\right)^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}{\left(aq^{d},q^{d}/a;q^{d}\right)^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{d}}},

which means that

\sum_{{i+j+k\leq n-1}}z_{q}(i)z_{q}(j)z_{q}(k)\equiv[n]^{3}\frac{\left(q,q^{d-1};q^{d}\right)^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}{\left(aq^{d},q^{d}/a;q^{d}\right)^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}\pmod{b-q^{n}}

is true.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

It is easy to see that
[n]\left(1-aq^{n}\right)\left(a-q^{n}\right)
and
b-q^{n}
are relatively prime polynomials. Noting the relations

\displaystyle\frac{\left(b-q^{n}\right)(ab-1-a^{2}+aq^{n})}{(a-b)(1-ab)}

\displaystyle\equiv 1\pmod{\left(1-aq^{n}\right)(a-q^{n})},

\displaystyle\frac{(1-aq^{n})(a-q^{n})}{(a-b)(1-ab)}

\displaystyle\equiv 1\pmod{b-q^{n}},

and employing the Chinese remainder theorem for coprime polynomials, we derive the following result
from (
2.3
) and (
2.9
): modulo
[n](1-aq^{n})(a-q^{n})(b-q^{n})
,

\displaystyle\sum_{{i+j+k\leq n-1}}z_{q}(i)z_{q}(j)z_{q}(k)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\equiv[n]^{3}\left\{\frac{(b-q^{n})(ab-1-a^{2}+aq^{n})}{(a-b)(1-ab)}\frac{\left(b/q\right)^{\frac{3(n-1)}{d}}\left(q^{2}/b;q^{d}\right)^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}{\left(bq^{d};q^{d}\right)^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\left.+\frac{(1-aq^{n})(a-q^{n})}{(a-b)(1-ab)}\frac{\left(q,q^{d-1};q^{d}\right)^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}{\left(aq^{d},q^{d}/a;q^{d}\right)^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}\right\}.

(2.10)

Taking
b\rightarrow 1
in (
2.10
), we deduce that, modulo
\Phi_{n}(q)^{2}(1-aq^{n})(a-q^{n})
,

\displaystyle\sum_{{i+j+k\leq n-1}}z_{q}(i)z_{q}(j)z_{q}(k)\equiv[n]^{3}q^{\frac{3(1-n)}{d}}\frac{(q^{2};q^{d})^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}{(q^{d};q^{d})^{3}_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}.

(2.11)

Here we have employed the relation:

(1-q^{n})(1+a^{2}-a-aq^{n})=(1-a)^{2}+(1-aq^{n})(a-q^{n}).

Finally, letting
a\rightarrow 1
in (
2.11
) and noting
1-q^{n}
has the factor
\Phi_{n}(q)
,
we see that the congruence (
1.2
) is true modulo
\Phi_{n}(q)^{4}
. On the other hand, our proof of
(
2.7
) is still valid for
a=b=1
, which means that (
1.2
) is also correct modulo
[n]
.
Since the least common multiple of
\Phi_{n}(q)^{4}
and
[n]
is
[n]\Phi_{n}(q)^{3}
, we finish the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

We first establish a parametric extension of Theorem
1.4
as follows.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
d
and
n
be positive integers with
d\geq 4
and
n\equiv 1\pmod{d}
,
c
an indeterminate. For
k\geq 0
, let

c_{q}(k)=[2dk+1]\frac{(q;q^{d})^{5}_{k}(cq;q^{d})_{k}}{(q^{d};q^{d})^{5}_{k}\left(q^{d}/c;q^{d}\right)_{k}}\left(\frac{q^{2d-3}}{c}\right)^{k}.

Then, we have, modulo
[n]\Phi_{n}(q)^{4}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{{j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}+j_{4}\leq n-1}}c_{q}(j_{1})c_{q}(j_{2})c_{q}(j_{3})c_{q}(j_{4})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\equiv[n]^{4}\left(cq\right)^{\frac{4(1-n)}{d}}\frac{\left(cq^{2};q^{d}\right)^{4}_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}{(q^{d}/c;q^{d})^{4}_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{n-1}{d}}\frac{\left(q,q,cq,q^{d-1};q^{d}\right)_{k}}{\left(q^{d},q^{d},q^{d},cq^{2};q^{d}\right)_{k}}q^{dk}\right)^{4}.

(3.1)

Clearly, Theorem
1.4
can be gotten by setting
c=q^{d-1}
and
d=4
in Theorem
3.1
.

In order to confirm Theorem
3.1
, we need to prove the following parametric extension.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
d
and
n
be positive integers with
d\geq 4
and
n\equiv 1\pmod{d}
,
a
,
b
and
c
indeterminates. For
k\geq 0
, let

z_{q}(k,a,b)=[2dk+1]\frac{\left(aq,q/a,bq,q/b,cq,q;q^{d}\right)_{k}}{\left(q^{d}/q,aq^{d},q^{d}/b,bq^{d},q^{d}/c,q^{d};q^{d}\right)_{k}}\left(\frac{q^{2d-3}}{c}\right)^{k}.

Then, modulo
[n](1-aq^{n})(a-q^{n})(1-bq^{n})(b-q^{n})
,

\displaystyle\sum_{{j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}+j_{4}\leq n-1}}z_{q}(j_{1},a,b)z_{q}(j_{2},a,b)z_{q}(j_{3},a,b)z_{q}(j_{4},a,b)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\equiv[n]^{4}\left(cq\right)^{\frac{4(1-n)}{d}}\frac{\left(cq^{2};q^{d}\right)^{4}_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}{\left(q^{d}/c;q^{d}\right)_{\frac{n-1}{d}}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\left\{\frac{(1-bq^{n})(b-q^{n})(-1-a^{2}+aq^{n})}{(a-b)(1-ab)}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{n-1}{d}}\frac{\left(aq,q/a,cq,q^{d-1};q^{d}\right)_{k}}{\left(bq^{d},q^{d}/b,cq^{2},q^{d};q^{d}\right)_{k}}q^{dk}\right)^{4}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\left.\quad+\frac{(1-aq^{n})(a-q^{n})(-1-b^{2}+bq^{n})}{(b-a)(1-ab)}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{n-1}{d}}\frac{\left(bq,q/b,cq,q^{d-1};q^{d}\right)_{k}}{\left(aq^{d},q^{d}/a,cq^{2},q^{d};q^{d}\right)_{k}}q^{dk}\right)^{4}\right\}.

Gaurav (Dhruv) Goel

Abstract
The space of all pencils of conics in the plane 𝐏 ​ V \mathbf{P}V (where dim V = 3 \dim V=3 ) is a projective Grassmannian 𝐆 ⁡ ( 1 , 𝐏 ​ Sym 2 ​ V ∗ ) \mathbf{G}(1,\mathbf{P}\Sym^{2}V^{*}) and admits a natural PGL ⁡ ( V ) \PGL(V) action. It is a classical theorem that this action has exactly eight orbits, and in fact that the orbit of a pencil ℓ ⊂ 𝐏 ​ Sym 2 ​ V ∗ \ell\subset\mathbf{P}\Sym^{2}V^{*} is determined completely by its position with respect to the Veronese surface X ⊂ 𝐏 ​ Sym 2 ​ V ∗ X\subset\mathbf{P}\Sym^{2}V^{*} of rank 1 conics and its secant variety S ⁡ ( X ) ⊂ 𝐏 ​ Sym 2 ​ V ∗ S(X)\subset\mathbf{P}\Sym^{2}V^{*} , which is the cubic fourfold of rank 2 conics. In this paper, we present some geometric descriptions of these orbits. Then, using a mixture of direct enumerative techniques and some Chern class computations, we present a calculation of the classes of the orbit closures in the Chow ring of this Grassmannian (and consequently also of their degrees under the Plücker embedding 𝐆 ⁡ ( 1 , 𝐏 ​ Sym 2 ​ V ∗ ) ↪ 𝐏 ​ Λ 2 ​ Sym 2 ​ V ∗ \mathbf{G}(1,\mathbf{P}\Sym^{2}V^{*})\hookrightarrow\mathbf{P}\Lambda^{2}\Sym^{2}V^{*} ).

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In §2, we establish our notation and conventions, and then recall the classification of these orbits and show how this relates to the positions of these lines with respect to the Veronese surface and its secant variety. 5
Dealing with Characterstic
p>2

In positive characteristic
p>2
, our results are still correct. Most of the above arguments remain valid; the only thing that changes is that we have to replace the use of Kleiman’s transversality theorem by an explicit calculation of the generic transversality of the corresponding cycles. Since most of the cycles we are interested in incidence varieties, varieties of secant lines, varieties of tangent lines, or Fano varieties in the Grassmannian, we can use the explicit description of the tangent spaces to such cycles (as found in say
[
14
, Lect. 16]
) to check generic transversality. In this section, we check the result for one orbit closure for which such description is not as easy to write down (or locate a reference for), namely
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{5}
.

Recall that
\mathcal{O}_{5}
is the orbit of lines intersecting
S
in a unique point of
X
. In an affine neighborhood of a line
\ell\in\mathcal{O}_{5}
, it is straightforward to write down defining equations of the variety
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{5}
; to help in this process, you can take one point of such a line varying on
X
. Given this, it is very easy to figure out the tangent space
T_{\ell}\mathcal{O}_{5}
as a subspace of
T_{\ell}\mathbf{G}(1,5)=\Hom(\ell,K^{6}/\ell)
. We see that if
p\in\ell\cap X
is the unique point of intersection of
\ell
with
X
, then if
H_{\ell}\subset K^{6}
is the hyperplane of conics passing through the (underlying reduced subscheme, i.e. the point) of the base locus
B_{\ell}
, then

T_{\ell}\mathcal{O}_{5}=\{\varphi\in\Hom(\ell,K^{6}/\ell):\varphi(p)\subseteq\mathbf{T}_{p}X+\ell\text{ and }\varphi(\ell)\subseteq H_{\ell}\}.

This amounts to saying that as we vary the line
\ell
along the tangent vector
\varphi
, to keep it incident to
X
amounts to the condition
\varphi(p)\subseteq\mathbf{T}_{p}X+\ell
, and to keep
\ell
in the tangent cone to
S
at its point of intersection with
X
amounts to the additional condition
\varphi(\ell)\subseteq H_{\ell}
. From this, it follows that a general complementary Schubert cycle is generically transverse to
\mathcal{O}_{5}
, which is the needed generic transversality result.

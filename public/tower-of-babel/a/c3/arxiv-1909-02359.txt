Representation theory of semidirect products of a compact quantum group with a finite group
Hua Wang

Abstract
. We study unitary representations of semidirect products of a compact quantum group with a finite group. We give a classification of all irreducible unitary representations, a description of the conjugate representation of irreducible unitary representations in terms of this classification, and the fusion rules for the semidirect product.



Introduction

It is often the case that one can retrieve significant information about
representations of a group
G
from representations of some subgroups of

G
. As a trivial example, the study of representations of a direct product

G\times H
of groups of
G
and
H
can be easily reduced to the study
of representations of
G
and
H

direct products with the more ubiquitous semidirect products, the situation
quickly becomes complicated. To get a taste of this complication, the classic

[
MR0450380

treats representations of a semidirect product

G\rtimes H
in the special case where
G,H
are both finite and

G
is abelian.

In the setting of locally compact groups and their unitary representations, via
the theories of systems of imprimitivity, induced representations, projective
representations (a.k.a. ray representations), etc., George Mackey developed a
heavy machinery of techniques, often referred as Mackey’s analysis, Mackey’s
machine or the little group method (which is also due to Wigner), to attack such
kind of problems. Subsequent works based on Mackey’s analysis emerge rapidly,
making it one of the most powerful tools to study unitary representations of
locally compact groups. For an introduction of this development, we refer the
reader to
[
MR0098328
,
MR44536
,
MR0031489
,
MR936629
,
MR3012851
]
among the large
volumes of literature on this subject.

The author’s own interest of this subject comes from the joint
work
[
2018arXiv181204078F
]
with Pierre Fima. In
[
2018arXiv181204078F
]
,
we systematically studied the permanence of property (RD) and polynomial growth
of the dual of a bicrossed product of a matched pair consisting of a second
countable compact group and a countable discrete group. The natural subsequent
question of constructing examples of nontrivial bicrossed products with or
without (RD) leads one to study closely the representation theory of semidirect
products
G\rtimes\Lambda
of a compact group
G
with a finite group

\Lambda
. More precisely, as required by the study of length functions relevant
to these properties, we need a classification of all irreducible unitary
representations of
G\rtimes\Lambda
, the conjugate (which, when we adopt
the point of view of topological quantum groups as in this paper, is also the
contragredient since classic groups are of Kac type) of irreducible
representations in terms of this classification, and most importantly, the
fusion rules of
G\rtimes\Lambda
, i.e. how the tensor product of two
irreducible representations decomposes into a direct sum of irreducible
representations. While the first two questions can be settled using Mackey’s
machine as mentioned above, the fusion rules, however, to the best of the
author’s limited knowledge, are never calculated in the literature.

This paper treats these questions in the more general setting of semidirect
products of the form
\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
, where
\mathbb{G}
is
a compact quantum group and
\Lambda
a finite group. However, instead of

C^{\ast}
-tensor category

\repcat(\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda)
. Roughly speaking, a representation
parameter is a triple
(u,V,v)
, where
u
is an irreducible
representation of
\mathbb{G}
on some finite dimensional Hilbert space

\mathscr{H}
,
V
is a unitary projective representation of a certain

\Lambda_{0}
of
\Lambda

\mathscr{H}
,
and

we will do later, one will need to use the counit, which is always everywhere
defined in the more elaborate algebraic approach of van Daele, but is merely
densely defined if the compact quantum group in the sense of Woronowicz is not
universal. We also point out here that the term

1.
Semidirect product of a compact quantum group with a
finite group

Let
\mathbb{G}=(A,\Delta)
be a compact quantum group,
\Lambda
a
finite group. An action of
\Lambda
on
\mathbb{G}
via quantum
automorphisms is an antihomomorphism

\alpha^{\ast}\colon\Lambda\rightarrow\aut\bigl(C(\mathbb{G}),\Delta\bigr)
. One can then form the semi-direct

\mathbb{G}\rtimes_{\alpha^{\ast}}\Lambda
, or simply

\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
if the action
\alpha^{\ast}
is clear from
the context, which is again a compact quantum group. The underlying

C^{\ast}
-algebra
\mathscr{A}
of

\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
is
A\otimes C(\Lambda)
, and the
comultiplication
\widetilde{\Delta}
on
\mathscr{A}
is determined by

(1.1)

\widetilde{\Delta}(a\otimes\delta_{r})=\sum_{s\in\Lambda}{\left[(\id_{A}\otimes\alpha^{\ast}_{s})\Delta(a)\right]}_{13}{(\delta_{s}\otimes\delta_{s^{-1}r})}_{24}\in A\otimes C(\Lambda)\otimes A\otimes C(\Lambda)

for any
a\in A
and
r\in\Lambda
. As we’ve mentioned at the end of
the introduction, from now on,
\mathbb{G}
,
\Lambda
and the action

\alpha^{\ast}
are fixed until the end of the paper.

It is clear that
\widetilde{\Delta}
is a unital

\ast
-morphism. We now check that in the six-fold tensor
product

A\otimes C(\Lambda)\otimes A\otimes C(\Lambda)\otimes A\otimes C(\Lambda)
, we have

(1.2)

\forall a\in A,r\in\Lambda,\qquad(\id\otimes\id\otimes\widetilde{\Delta})[\widetilde{\Delta}(a\otimes\delta_{r})]=(\widetilde{\Delta}\otimes\id\otimes\id)[\widetilde{\Delta}(a\otimes\delta_{r})],

i.e. our new comultiplication
\widetilde{\Delta}
is
coassociative. Indeed, put

\Delta^{(2)}:=(\id\otimes\Delta)\Delta=(\Delta\otimes\id)\Delta
,
since
\alpha^{\ast}_{s}\in\aut\bigl(C(\mathbb{G}),\Delta\bigr)
for
all
s\in\Lambda
, we have

(\alpha_{s}^{\ast}\otimes\alpha_{s}^{\ast})\circ\Delta=\Delta\circ\alpha_{s}^{\ast}
,

(1.3)

\begin{split}&\hskip 11.99998pt(\id\otimes\id\otimes\widetilde{\Delta})[\widetilde{\Delta}(a\otimes\delta_{r})]\\
&=(\id\otimes\id\otimes\widetilde{\Delta})\left(\sum_{s\in\Lambda}{[(\id\otimes\alpha_{s}^{\ast})\Delta(a)]}_{13}{(\delta_{s}\otimes\delta_{s^{-1}r})}_{24}\right)\\
&=\sum_{s,t\in\Lambda}{\Bigl\{\bigl[\bigl(\id\otimes\alpha_{s}^{\ast}\otimes(\alpha_{t}^{\ast}\circ\alpha_{s}^{\ast})\bigr)(\id\otimes\Delta)\Delta\bigr](a)\Bigr\}}_{135}{(\delta_{s}\otimes\delta_{t}\otimes\delta_{t^{-1}s^{-1}r})}_{246}\\
&=\sum_{s,t\in\Lambda}{\Bigl\{\bigl[(\id\otimes\alpha_{s}^{\ast}\otimes\alpha_{st}^{\ast})\Delta^{(2)}\bigr](a)\Bigr\}}_{135}{(\delta_{s}\otimes\delta_{t}\otimes\delta_{t^{-1}s^{-1}r})}_{246}.\end{split}

On the other hand,

(1.4)

\begin{split}&\hskip 11.99998pt(\widetilde{\Delta}\otimes\id\otimes\id)[\widetilde{\Delta}(a\otimes\delta_{r})]\\
&=(\widetilde{\Delta}\otimes\id\otimes\id)\left(\sum_{s\in\Lambda}{[(\id\otimes\alpha_{s}^{\ast})\Delta(a)]}_{13}{(\delta_{s}\otimes\delta_{s^{-1}r})}_{24}\right)\\
&=\sum_{s,t\in\Lambda}\Bigl\{\bigl[(\id\otimes\alpha_{t}^{\ast}\otimes\alpha_{s}^{\ast})(\Delta\otimes\id)\Delta\bigr](a)\Bigr\}_{135}{(\delta_{t}\otimes\delta_{t^{-1}s}\otimes\delta_{s^{-1}r})}_{246}\\
&\hskip 11.99998pt(s^{\prime}=t,t^{\prime}=t^{-1}s\iff s=s^{\prime}t^{\prime},t=s^{\prime})\\
&=\sum_{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}\in\Lambda}\Bigl\{\bigl[(\id\otimes\alpha_{s^{\prime}}^{\ast}\otimes\alpha_{s^{\prime}t^{\prime}}^{\ast})\Delta^{(2)}\bigr](a)\Bigr\}_{135}{(\delta_{s^{\prime}}\otimes\delta_{t^{\prime}}\otimes\delta_{t^{\prime-1}s^{\prime-1}r})}_{246}.\end{split}

Now (
1.2
) follows from (
1.3
) and
(
1.4
).

Since
\pol(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\Lambda)
is dense in

A\otimes C(\Lambda)
, in order to prove that

\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
is indeed a compact quantum group, it suffices
to show that
(\pol(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\Lambda),\widetilde{\Delta})

is an algebraic compact quantum group, i.e. a Hopf

\ast
-algebra with an invariant state (called the Haar state or
Haar integral).

First of all, since

\alpha_{s}^{\ast}\in\aut\bigl(C(\mathbb{G}),\Delta\bigr)
for all

s\in\Lambda
, we have

\alpha_{s}^{\ast}\bigl(\pol(\mathbb{G})\bigr)=\pol(\mathbb{G})
, and

\widetilde{\Delta}
indeed restricts to a well-defined comultiplication on

\pol(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\Lambda)
.

Let
\epsilon,S
be the counit and the antipode respectively for the Hopf

\ast
-algebra
\pol(\mathbb{G})
. Denoting the neutral
element of the group
\Lambda
by
e
, we define

(1.5)

\begin{split}\widetilde{\epsilon}\colon\pol(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\Lambda)&\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\\
\sum_{r\in\Lambda}x_{r}\otimes\delta_{r}&\mapsto\epsilon(x_{e}),\end{split}

and

(1.6)

\begin{split}\widetilde{S}\colon\pol(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\Lambda)&\rightarrow\pol(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\Lambda)\\
\sum_{r\in\Lambda}x_{r}\otimes\delta_{r}&\mapsto\sum_{r\in\Lambda}\alpha_{r}^{\ast}(S(x_{r}))\otimes\delta_{r^{-1}}=\sum_{r\in\Lambda}S(\alpha_{r^{-1}}^{\ast}(x_{r^{-1}}))\otimes\delta_{r}.\end{split}

Since
\epsilon
is a
\ast
-morphism of algebras, so is

\widetilde{\epsilon}
. Moreover, for any
x\in\pol(\mathbb{G})
and

r\in\Lambda
, we have

\displaystyle\hskip 11.99998pt(\widetilde{\epsilon}\otimes\id)\widetilde{\Delta}(x\otimes\delta_{r})=(\widetilde{\epsilon}\otimes\id)\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\sum x_{(1)}\otimes\delta_{s}\otimes\alpha_{s}^{\ast}(x_{(2)})\otimes\delta_{s^{-1}r}

\displaystyle=\sum\epsilon(x_{(1)})\alpha_{e}^{\ast}(x_{(2)})\otimes\delta_{r}=\sum\epsilon(x_{(1)})x_{(2)}\otimes\delta_{r}=x\otimes\delta_{r}

\displaystyle=\sum x_{(1)}\epsilon(x_{(2)})\otimes\delta_{r}=\sum x_{(1)}\epsilon(\alpha_{r}^{\ast}(x_{(2)}))\otimes\delta_{r}

\displaystyle=(\id\otimes\widetilde{\epsilon})\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\sum x_{(1)}\otimes\delta_{s}\otimes\alpha_{s}^{\ast}(x_{(2)})\otimes\delta_{s^{-1}r}=(\id\otimes\widetilde{\epsilon})\widetilde{\Delta}(x\otimes\delta_{r}).

Hence
\widetilde{\epsilon}
is the counit for
\widetilde{\Delta}
. Let

m\colon\pol(G)\otimes\pol(G)\rightarrow\pol(G)
be the multiplication
map, and
\widetilde{m}
the multiplication map on

\pol(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\Lambda)
, then

\displaystyle\widetilde{m}(\widetilde{S}\otimes\id)\widetilde{\Delta}(x\otimes\delta_{r})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{m}(\widetilde{S}\otimes\id)\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\sum x_{(1)}\otimes\delta_{s}\otimes\alpha_{s}^{\ast}(x_{(2)})\otimes\delta_{s^{-1}r}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{m}\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\sum\alpha_{s}^{\ast}(S(x_{(1)}))\otimes\delta_{s^{-1}}\otimes\alpha_{s}^{\ast}(x_{(2)})\otimes\delta_{s^{-1}r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\left[m(S\otimes\id)(\alpha_{s}^{\ast}\otimes\alpha_{s}^{\ast})\Delta(x)\right]\otimes\delta_{s^{-1}}\cdot\delta_{s^{-1}r}

\displaystyle=\delta_{e,r}\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\left[m(S\otimes\id)\Delta(\alpha_{s}^{\ast}(x))\right]\otimes\delta_{s^{-1}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{e,r}\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\epsilon(\alpha_{s}^{\ast}(x))1_{A}\otimes\delta_{s^{-1}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{e,r}\epsilon(x)1_{A}\otimes\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\delta_{s^{-1}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{e,r}\epsilon(x)1_{A}\otimes 1_{C(\Lambda)}=\widetilde{\epsilon}(x\otimes\delta_{r})1_{A}\otimes 1_{C(\Lambda)}.

Similarly, since for any
s\in\Lambda
,

\alpha_{s^{-1}r}^{\ast}S\alpha_{s}^{\ast}=\alpha_{s^{-1}r}^{\ast}\alpha_{s}^{\ast}S=\alpha_{r}^{\ast}S,

we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{m}(\id\otimes\widetilde{S})\widetilde{\Delta}(x\otimes\delta_{r})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{m}(\id\otimes\widetilde{S})\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\sum x_{(1)}\otimes\delta_{s}\otimes\alpha_{s}^{\ast}(x_{(2)})\otimes\delta_{s^{-1}r}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{m}\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\sum x_{(1)}\otimes\delta_{s}\otimes(\alpha_{s^{-1}r}^{\ast}S\alpha_{s}^{\ast})(x_{(2)})\otimes\delta_{r^{-1}s}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{m}\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\sum x_{(1)}\otimes\delta_{s}\otimes(\alpha_{r}^{\ast}S)(x_{2})\otimes\delta_{r^{-1}s}

\displaystyle=\delta_{e,r}\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\sum x_{(1)}[S(x_{(2)})]\otimes\delta_{s}=\delta_{e,r}\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\epsilon(x)1_{A}\otimes\delta_{s}

\displaystyle=\delta_{e,r}\epsilon(x)1_{A}\otimes 1_{C(\Lambda)}=\widetilde{\epsilon}(x\otimes\delta_{r})1_{A}\otimes 1_{C(\Lambda)}.

Therefore,
\widetilde{S}
is the antipode for

(\pol(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\Lambda),\widetilde{\Delta})
.

It remains to construct the Haar state on the Hopf

\ast
-algebra
\pol(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\Lambda)
. Suppose
h\colon A\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is the Haar state on

\mathbb{G}
, define

(1.7)

\begin{split}\widetilde{h}\colon\pol(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\Lambda)&\to\mathbb{C}\\
\sum_{r}x_{r}\otimes\delta_{r}&\mapsto\left\lvert\Lambda\right\rvert^{-1}\sum_{r\in\Lambda}h(x_{r}).\end{split}

It is obvious that
\widetilde{h}
is a state. For any

x\in\pol(\mathbb{G})
,
r\in\Lambda
,

\displaystyle(\widetilde{h}\otimes\id)\widetilde{\Delta}(x\otimes\delta_{r})

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\Lambda\right\rvert^{-1}\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\sum h(x_{(1)})\alpha_{s}^{\ast}(x_{(2)})\otimes\delta_{s^{-1}r}

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\Lambda\right\rvert^{-1}\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\alpha_{s}^{\ast}\bigl(\sum h(x_{(1)})x_{(2)}\bigr)\otimes\delta_{s^{-1}r}

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\Lambda\right\rvert^{-1}\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\alpha_{s}^{\ast}(h(x)1_{A})\otimes\delta_{s^{-1}r}

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\Lambda\right\rvert^{-1}h(x)\sum_{s\in\Lambda}1_{A}\otimes\delta_{s^{-1}r}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{h}(x\otimes\delta_{r})1_{A}\otimes 1_{C(\Lambda)}.

The uniqueness of the Haar state implies that

h\circ\alpha_{s}^{\ast}=h
for any
s\in\Lambda
, hence

\displaystyle(\id\otimes\widetilde{h})\widetilde{\Delta}(x\otimes\delta_{r})

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\Lambda\right\rvert^{-1}\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\sum x_{(1)}h\bigl(\alpha_{s}^{\ast}(x_{(2)})\bigr)\otimes\delta_{s}

algebraic compact quantum group (cf.
[

, paper

]

and Haar state (which we still denote by

\begin{split}\widetilde{h}:A\otimes C(\Lambda)&\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\\
\sum x_{r}\otimes\delta_{r}&\mapsto\left\lvert\Lambda\right\rvert^{-1}\sum_{r\in\Lambda}h(x_{r}).\end{split}

Definition 1.1

Lemma 2.1

\ast

Proof.

Since the antipodes are

Take any

,
r\in\Lambda

\displaystyle\hskip 11.99998pt[(\epsilon\otimes\id)\otimes(\epsilon\otimes\id)]\Delta_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda}(a\otimes\delta_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\sum\epsilon(a_{(1)})\epsilon(\alpha_{s}^{\ast}(a_{(2)}))\delta_{s}\otimes\delta_{s^{-1}r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\Lambda}\sum\epsilon(a_{(1)})\epsilon(a_{(2)})\delta_{s}\otimes\delta_{s^{-1}r}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.99998pt[(\id\otimes\epsilon_{\Lambda})\otimes(\id\otimes\epsilon_{\Lambda})]\Delta_{\mathbb{G}\otimes\Lambda}(a\otimes\delta_{r})

\displaystyle=\delta_{r,1_{\Lambda}}\sum a_{(1)}\otimes a_{(2)}

\displaystyle=\delta_{r,1_{\Lambda}}\Delta(a)=\Delta[(\id\otimes\epsilon_{\Lambda})(a\otimes\delta_{r})].

Thus

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{H}=(B,\Delta_{B})
,
\mathbb{K}=(C,\Delta_{C})
be
compact quantum groups, we say
\mathbb{K}
is isomorphic to a closed
quantum subgroup of
\mathbb{H}
, or simply
\mathbb{K}
is a closed
subgroup of
\mathbb{H}

surjective
mapping

\varphi:\pol(\mathbb{H})\to\pol(\mathbb{K})
such that
\varphi

is a morphism of Hopf
\ast
-algebras.

When
\mathcal{\mathbb{H}}
is universal, then

3.1
can be reformulated as the existence of a
surjective unital

-algebra morphism

\varphi:B\to C
such that

(\varphi\otimes\varphi)\Delta_{B}=\Delta_{C}\varphi
.

In the context of compact quantum groups, we will use the terms “quantum closed
subgroup” and “closed subgroup”, sometimes even “subgroup”, interchangeably
without further explanation.

Remark 3.2
.

If
\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbb{K}
are commutative, i.e. they come from
genuine compact groups, then
\mathbb{K}
being isomorphic to a closed
subgroup, says exactly that there exists a continuous injective map

\varphi_{\ast}

.

Let
\Lambda_{0}
be a subgroup of
\Lambda
,
U
a unitary
representation of
\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}
, then the following are
equivalent:

(a)

the unitary representation

\indrep(U)
of
\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
is irreducible;

(b)

for any
r,s\in\Lambda
, posing

\Lambda(r,s)=r\Lambda_{0}r^{-1}\cap s\Lambda_{0}s^{-1}
, we have

(6.9)

\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)}\Bigl((r\cdot U)|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)},(s\cdot U)|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)}\Bigr)=\delta_{r\Lambda_{0},s\Lambda_{0}};

(c)

U
is irreducible, and

(6.10)

\begin{split}&\forall r,s\in\Lambda,\quad r^{-1}s\notin\Lambda_{0}\\
&\implies\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)}\Bigl((r\cdot U)|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)},(s\cdot U)|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)}\Bigr)=0.\end{split}

In particular, if any of the above conditions holds, then
U
itself is
irreducible.

Proof.

If
r^{-1}s\in\Lambda_{0}
, then

r\Lambda_{0}r^{-1}=s\Lambda_{0}s^{-1}
, so

\Lambda(r,s)=r\Lambda_{0}r^{-1}=s\Lambda_{0}s^{-1}
. By
Proposition
3.9
, we see that

(6.11)

\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes r\Lambda_{0}r^{-1}}\bigl(r\cdot U,r\cdot U\bigr)=\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}(U,U).

By Proposition
6.3
, Lemma
5.3
,
and the above, we have

\begin{split}&\hskip 11.99998pt\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}}\bigl(\indrep(U),\indrep(U)\bigr)\\
&=\frac{1}{\left\lvert\Lambda_{0}\right\rvert^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,s\in\Lambda,\\
r^{-1}s\in\Lambda_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{[\Lambda\colon r\Lambda_{0}r^{-1}]}\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes r\Lambda_{0}r^{-1}}\bigl(r\cdot U,s\cdot U\bigr)\\
&\hskip 30.00005pt+\frac{1}{\left\lvert\Lambda_{0}\right\rvert^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,s\in\Lambda,\\
r^{-1}s\notin\Lambda_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{[\Lambda\colon\Lambda(r,s)]}\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)}\Bigl((r\cdot U)|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)},(s\cdot U)|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)}\Bigr)\\
&=\frac{1}{\left\lvert\Lambda_{0}\right\rvert^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,s\in\Lambda,\\
r^{-1}s\in\Lambda_{0}\end{subarray}}\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes r\Lambda_{0}r^{-1}}\bigl(r\cdot U,r\cdot U\bigr)\\
&\hskip 30.00005pt+\frac{1}{\left\lvert\Lambda_{0}\right\rvert^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,s\in\Lambda,\\
r^{-1}s\notin\Lambda_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{[\Lambda\colon\Lambda(r,s)]}\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)}\Bigl((r\cdot U)|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)},(s\cdot U)|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)}\Bigr)\\
&=\frac{1}{\left\lvert\Lambda_{0}\right\rvert^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,s\in\Lambda,\\
r^{-1}s\in\Lambda_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{[\Lambda\colon\Lambda_{0}]}\dim_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}(U,U)\\
&\hskip 30.00005pt+\frac{1}{\left\lvert\Lambda_{0}\right\rvert^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,s\in\Lambda,\\
r^{-1}s\notin\Lambda_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{[\Lambda\colon\Lambda(r,s)]}\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)}\Bigl((r\cdot U)|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)},(s\cdot U)|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)}\Bigr)\\
&=\frac{\left\lvert\Lambda\right\rvert\cdot\left\lvert\Lambda_{0}\right\rvert}{\left\lvert\Lambda_{0}\right\rvert^{2}\cdot[\Lambda\colon\Lambda_{0}]}\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}(U,U)\\
&\hskip 30.00005pt+\frac{1}{\left\lvert\Lambda_{0}\right\rvert^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,s\in\Lambda,\\
r^{-1}s\notin\Lambda_{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{[\Lambda\colon\Lambda(r,s)]}\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)}\Bigl((r\cdot U)|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)},(s\cdot U)|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)}\Bigr).\end{split}

Since

\left\lvert\Lambda\right\rvert\cdot\left\lvert\Lambda_{0}\right\rvert=\left\lvert\Lambda_{0}\right\rvert^{2}\cdot[\Lambda\colon\Lambda_{0}]
and

(6.12)

\begin{split}\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes r\Lambda_{0}r^{-1}}\bigl(r\cdot U,s\cdot U\bigr)&=\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes r\Lambda_{0}r^{-1}}\bigl(r\cdot U,r\cdot U\bigr)\\
&=\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}(U,U)=\dim\selfmorph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}(U)\end{split}

whenever
r^{-1}s\in\Lambda_{0}
by Lemma
5.3

and Proposition
3.9
, the above calculation yields

(6.13)

\dim\selfmorph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda}\left(\indrep(U)\right)=\dim\selfmorph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}(U)+\frac{1}{\left\lvert\Lambda_{0}\right\rvert^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,s\in\Lambda,\\
r^{-1}s\notin\Lambda_{0}\end{subarray}}{d(r,s)\over[\Lambda:\Lambda(r,s)]},

where

(6.14)

d(r,s):=\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)}\Bigl((r\cdot U)|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)},(s\cdot U)|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)}\Bigr).

The corollary follows from (
6.12
)
(
6.13
), (
6.14
) and the fact that a
representation is irreducible if and only if the dimension of the space of its
self-intertwiners is
1
.

Definition 7.1
.

A subgroup

of

is finite) family of all general

The following proposition is an immediate consequence of properties of

.

Proposition 7.2
.

The family
\giso(\Lambda)
is stable under intersection and conjugation

\Lambda
. ∎

Definition 7.3
.

Let
\Lambda_{0}
be a general isotropy subgroup of
\Lambda
. A

\Lambda_{0}
is a triple
(\mathscr{H},u,w)
, where

—

\mathscr{H}
is a finite dimensional

—

is a unitary representation of

\mathbb{G}
on
\mathscr{H}
;

—

w
is a unitary representation of

\Lambda_{0}
on
\mathscr{H}
,

such that
u
and
w
are covariant. In this paper, CSRs are often
denoted by bold faced uppercase letters like

\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B},\mathbf{C},\ldots
(mostly
\mathbf{S}
) with
possible subscripts.

By Proposition
2.2
, the covariant systems of
representations subordinate to a general isotropy subgroup
\Lambda_{0}

correspond bijectively to the class of unitary representations of

\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}
, via

(\mathscr{H},u,w)\mapsto u_{12}w_{13}

in one direction, and

U_{\mathscr{H}}\mapsto(\mathscr{H},U_{\mathscr{H},\mathbb{G}},U_{\mathscr{H},\Lambda_{0}})

in the other, where
\mathscr{H}
is the underlying space of the

of
\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}
,
and
U_{\mathscr{H},\mathbb{G}}

U_{\mathscr{H},\Lambda_{0}}
are the

to
\mathbb{G}
and
\Lambda_{0}

-tensor category

whose objects are CSRs subordinate to

To make this transport of categorical structures less tautological, we make a
convenient characterization of the morphisms in

.

Proposition 7.4
.

Fix a general isotropy subgroup
\Lambda_{0}
of
\Lambda
. For

i=1,2
, let
\mathbf{S}_{i}=(\mathscr{H}_{i},u_{i},w_{i})
be
a CSR subordinate to
\Lambda_{0}
,

U_{i}={(u_{i})}_{12}{(w_{i})}_{13}
the corresponding unitary
representation of
\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}
,

S\in\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{H}_{1},\mathscr{H}_{2})
. Then

S\in\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}\bigl(U_{1},U_{2}\bigr)
if
and only if

(7.1)

S\in\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(u_{1},u_{2})\cap\morph_{\Lambda_{0}}(w_{1},w_{2}).

Proof.

The condition is easily seen to be sufficient. Indeed, if condition
(
7.1
) holds, then

(7.2)

(S\otimes 1)w_{1}=w_{2}(S\otimes 1),\quad\\
(S\otimes 1)u_{1}=u_{2}(S\otimes 1).

Thus

(7.3)

\begin{split}(S\otimes 1\otimes 1)U_{1}&=(S\otimes 1\otimes 1){(u_{1})}_{12}{(w_{1})}_{13}={(u_{2})}_{12}(S\otimes 1\otimes 1){(w_{1})}_{13}\\
&={(u_{2})}_{12}{(w_{2})}_{13}(S\otimes 1\otimes 1)=U_{2}(S\otimes 1\otimes 1).\end{split}

This means exactly
S\in\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(U_{1},U_{2})
.

To show the necessity of this condition, let

\epsilon_{\mathbb{G}}\colon\pol(\mathbb{G})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be
the counit of the Hopf-
\ast
-algebra,

\epsilon_{\Lambda_{0}}\colon C(\Lambda_{0})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

the counit for the Hopf
\ast
-algebra
C(\Lambda_{0})
. Since

U_{i}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{H}_{i})\otimes\pol(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\Lambda_{0})
for
i=1,2
and

S\in\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}(U_{1},U_{2})
, we have

(7.4)

(S\otimes 1\otimes 1)U_{1}=U_{2}(S\otimes 1\otimes 1).

Applying
\id\otimes\id\otimes\epsilon_{\Lambda_{0}}
on both sides
of (
7.4
) yields

(7.5)

(S\otimes 1)u_{1}=u_{2}(S\otimes 1),

which means
S\in\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(u_{1},u_{2})
. Applying

\id\otimes\epsilon_{\mathbb{G}}\otimes\id
on both sides
of (
7.4
) yields

(7.6)

(S\otimes 1)w_{1}=w_{1}(S\otimes 1),

which means
S\in\morph_{\Lambda_{0}}(w_{1},w_{2})
.
∎

of

on

onto
\mathbb{G}
(resp.
\Lambda_{0}

Proposition 7.5
.

For
i=1,2
, let

\mathbf{S}_{i}=(\mathscr{H}_{i},u_{i},w_{i})\in\mathcal{CSR}_{\Lambda_{0}}
,

U_{i}=\mathscr{R}_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathbf{S}_{i})\in\repcat(\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0})

(7.7)

\mathscr{S}_{\Lambda_{0}}(U_{1}\times U_{2})=(\mathscr{H}_{1}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{2},u_{1}\times u_{2},w_{1}\times w_{2})=\mathbf{S}_{1}\otimes\mathbf{S}_{2}.

Proof.

By definition of the tensor product of representations,

is the representation of

defined by

(7.8)

U_{1}\times U_{2}={(U_{1})}_{134}{(U_{2})}_{234}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{H}_{1})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{H}_{2})\otimes\pol(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\Lambda_{0}),

The restriction of

Similarly, the restriction of

\mathcal{CSR}_{\Lambda_{0}}

Recall that the contragredient representation

of a

is defined as

, where

is defined as

T\mapsto\overline{T^{\ast}}
, with
\overline{\mathscr{H}}
being the

\mathscr{H}
, and
\overline{T^{\ast}}

meaning
T^{\ast}
viewed as a linear mapping from

\overline{\mathscr{H}}
to
\overline{\mathscr{H}}
. Note that

,

is linear, antimultiplicative and
positive (in particular, it preserves adjoints). If

is of

by
Proposition
1.4
, in which case

might not be
unitary, which is exactly why the “modular” operator

is
necessary to express the dual object of

in

\mathcal{CSR}_{\Lambda_{0}}
as presented in

7.7
.

Proposition 7.7
.

Let
\mathbf{S}=(\mathscr{H},u,w)\in\mathcal{CSR}_{\Lambda_{0}}
,

U=\mathscr{R}_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathbf{S})\in\repcat(\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0})
,
U^{c}
the contragredient representation of
U
on
the conjugate space
\overline{\mathscr{H}}
of
\mathscr{H}
. If

\rho_{U}
is the unique invertible positive operator in

\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}(U,U^{cc})
(which we call
modular operator) such that

\tr(\cdot\,\rho_{U})=\tr(\cdot\,\rho_{U}^{-1})
on

\selfmorph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}(U)
, so that

(7.12)

\overline{U}={\bigl\{{[j(\rho_{U})]}^{1/2}\otimes 1_{\pol(\mathbb{G})}\otimes 1_{C(\Lambda_{0})}\bigr\}}U^{c}{\bigl\{{[j(\rho_{U})]}^{-1/2}\otimes 1_{\pol(\mathbb{G})}\otimes 1_{C(\Lambda_{0})}\bigr\}}

is the conjugate representation of
U
, then the dual of
\mathbf{S}

is given by
\overline{\mathbf{S}}=(\overline{\mathscr{H}},u^{\prime},w^{\prime})
,
where

(7.13)

\begin{split}u^{\prime}&=({j(\rho_{U})}^{1/2}\otimes 1)u^{c}({j(\rho_{U})}^{-1/2}\otimes 1),\\
w^{\prime}&=({j(\rho_{U})}^{1/2}\otimes 1)w^{c}({j(\rho_{U})}^{-1/2}\otimes 1).\end{split}

Note that
w^{c}=\overline{w}
as
\Lambda
is a finite (compact)
group. In particular, if
\mathbb{G}
is of Kac-type, then

u^{c}=\overline{u}
,
\rho_{U}=1
, and

\overline{\mathbf{S}}=(\overline{\mathscr{H}},\overline{u},\overline{w})
.

Proof.

By definition,
\overline{S}=\mathscr{S}_{\Lambda_{0}}(\overline{U})
,
thus

\begin{split}u^{\prime}&=(\id_{\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{H})}\otimes\id_{\pol(\mathbb{G})}\otimes\epsilon_{\Lambda_{0}})(\overline{U})\\
&=(\id\otimes\id\otimes\epsilon_{\Lambda_{0}})\bigl[({j(\rho_{U})}^{1/2}\otimes 1\otimes 1)U^{c}({j(\rho_{U})}^{-1/2}\otimes 1\otimes 1)\bigr]\\
&=(\id\otimes\id\otimes\epsilon_{\Lambda_{0}})\Bigl(({j(\rho_{U})}^{1/2}\otimes 1\otimes 1)\\
&\hskip 80.00012pt[(j\otimes\id_{\pol(\mathbb{G})}\otimes\id_{C(\Lambda_{0})})(U^{\ast})]({j(\rho_{U})}^{-1/2}\otimes 1\otimes 1)\Bigr)\\
&=({j(\rho_{U})}^{1/2}\otimes 1)\bigl[(j\otimes\id\otimes\epsilon_{\Lambda_{0}})(U^{\ast})\bigr]({j(\rho_{U})}^{-1/2}\otimes 1)\\
&=({j(\rho_{U})}^{1/2}\otimes 1){\bigl[(j\otimes\id\otimes\epsilon_{\Lambda_{0}})(U)\bigr]}^{\ast}({j(\rho_{U})}^{-1/2}\otimes 1)\\
&=({j(\rho_{U})}^{1/2}\otimes 1){\bigl[(j\otimes\id)(u)\bigr]}^{\ast}({j(\rho_{U})}^{-1/2}\otimes 1)\\
&=({j(\rho_{U})}^{1/2}\otimes 1)\bigl[(j\otimes\id)(u^{\ast})\bigr]({j(\rho_{U})}^{-1/2}\otimes 1)\\
&=({j(\rho_{U})}^{1/2}\otimes 1)u^{c}({j(\rho_{U})}^{-1/2}\otimes 1).\end{split}

The expression for

is proved analogously by applying

\id_{\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{H})}\otimes\epsilon_{\pol(\mathbb{G})}\otimes\id_{C(\Lambda_{0})}
on (
7.12
). Finally, if

\mathbb{G}
is of Kac-type, then
\rho_{U}=\id_{\mathscr{H}}=1
.

stably pure

\mathcal{CSR}_{\Lambda_{0}}
, then such a
description is indeed achievable via the theory of unitary projective
representations of
\Lambda_{0}
. This section studies how such projective
representations arise naturally from the action of
\Lambda
on irreducible
representations of
\mathbb{G}
, as well as establishes some basic
properties of these projective representations. The results here will be used in
§
9
to describe the structure of stably pure CSRs in

\mathcal{CSR}_{\Lambda_{0}}
(Proposition
9.5
and
Proposition
9.6
).

We begin with a simple observation which is a trivial quantum analogue of one of
the most basic ingredients of the Mackey analysis. Let
U_{\mathbb{G}}
be a
unitary representation of
\mathbb{G}
on some finite dimensional Hilbert
space
\mathscr{H}
. Since

\alpha^{\ast}\colon\Lambda\rightarrow\aut\bigl(C(\mathbb{G}),\Delta\bigr)
is an antihomomorphism of groups, we know that

{(\id_{\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{H})}\otimes\alpha_{r^{-1}}^{\ast})}(U_{\mathbb{G}})
is again a unitary representation
of
\mathbb{G}
on the same space
\mathscr{H}
, and we denote this new
representation by
r\cdot U_{\mathbb{G}}
as we did in
Proposition
5.1
. One checks that

(rs)\cdot U_{\mathbb{G}}=r\cdot(s\cdot U_{\mathbb{G}})
. Thus this
defines a left action of the group
\Lambda
on the (proper) class of all
unitary representation of
\mathbb{G}
, which is easily seen to preserve
irreducibility and pass to a well-defined action of
\Lambda
on the

set

\irr(\mathbb{G})
by letting
r\cdot[u]=[r\cdot u]
,
where
r\in\Lambda
,
u
is an irreducible unitary representation of

\mathbb{G}
and
[u]
is the equivalence class of
u
in

\irr(\mathbb{G})
. Take another unitary representation
W_{\mathbb{G}}

of
\mathbb{G}
on some other finite dimensional Hilbert space

\mathscr{K}
. For any
r,s\in\Lambda
and any

T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{H},\mathscr{K})
, we have

(8.1)

\begin{split}&T\in\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(r\cdot U_{\mathbb{G}},W_{\mathbb{G}})\\
\iff&W_{\mathbb{G}}(T\otimes 1)=(T\otimes 1)(\id\otimes\alpha_{r^{-1}}^{\ast})(U_{\mathbb{G}})\\
\iff&[(\id\otimes\alpha_{s^{-1}}^{\ast})(W_{\mathbb{G}})](T\otimes 1)=(T\otimes 1)(\id\otimes\alpha_{{(sr)}^{-1}}^{\ast})(U_{\mathbb{G}})\\
\iff&T\in\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(sr\cdot U_{\mathbb{G}},s\cdot W_{\mathbb{G}}).\end{split}

Now take any irreducible unitary representation
u
of
\mathbb{G}
on
some finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
. Let

x=[u]\in\irr(\mathbb{G})
, and

\Lambda_{x}=\left\{r\in\Lambda\;:\;r\cdot x=x\right\},

i.e.
\Lambda_{x}
is the isotropy subgroup of
\Lambda
fixing

x
. Then for any
r_{0}\in\Lambda_{x}
,
u
and

r_{0}\cdot u
are equivalent by definition, hence there exists a unitary

V(r_{0})\in\mathcal{U}(\mathscr{H})
intertwining
r_{0}\cdot u
and

u
, in other words,

(8.2)

\bigl(V(r_{0})\otimes 1\bigr)(\id\otimes\alpha^{\ast}_{r_{0}^{-1}})(u)=u\bigl(V(r_{0})\otimes 1\bigr),

which is clearly equivalent to

(8.3)

\forall r_{0}\in\Lambda_{x},\quad\bigl(V(r_{0})\otimes 1\bigr)u=[(\id\otimes\alpha_{r_{0}}^{\ast})(u)]\bigl(V(r_{0})\otimes 1\bigr).

It is remarkable that (
8.3
) takes exactly the same form as
the covariance condition (
2.2
) when we define covariant
representations in §
2
. Now if we choose a

(8.4)

V(r_{0})\in\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(r_{0}\cdot u,u)\cap\mathcal{U}(\mathscr{H})

for each
r_{0}\in\Lambda_{x}
, then for any
s_{0}\in\Lambda_{x}
,
by (
8.1
), we have

V(r_{0})\in\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(s_{0}r_{0}\cdot u,s_{0}\cdot u),\quad V(s_{0})\in\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(s_{0}\cdot u,u),\quad V(s_{0}r_{0})\in\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(s_{0}r_{0}\cdot u,u),

thus

(8.5)

\forall r_{0},s_{0}\in\Lambda_{x},\quad V(s_{0}r_{0}){[V(r_{0})]}^{\ast}{[V(s_{0})]}^{\ast}\in\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(u,u)\cap\mathcal{U}(\mathscr{H})=\mathbb{T}\cdot\id_{\mathscr{H}}.

This means that
V\colon\Lambda_{x}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathscr{H})

is a unitary
projective representation

(Definition
8.1
) of
\Lambda_{x}
on

\mathscr{H}
, which satisfies the covariant
condition (
8.3
) for each
r_{0}\in\Lambda_{x}
.

To facilitate our discussion, we digress now to give a brief summary of some
basic terminologies of the theory of group cohomology which we will use (cf.
[
MR1324339
]
). We regard
\mathbb{T}
as a trivial module over any
finite group when considering unitary projective representations of finite
groups. For any finite group
\Gamma
, an
n
-cochain on
\Gamma

with coefficients in
\mathbb{T}
, or simply an
n
-cochain (on

\Gamma
), as we won’t consider coefficient module other that the trivial
module
\mathbb{T}
, is a mapping from the
n
-fold product

\Gamma^{n}=\Gamma\times\cdots\times\Gamma
to
\mathbb{T}
. Let

C^{n}(\Gamma,\mathbb{T})
be the abelian group of
n
-cochains on

\Gamma
,
Z^{2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{T})
the subgroup of
2
-cocycles
on
\Gamma
, i.e. mappings

\omega\colon\Gamma\times\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
satisfying the
cocycle condition

(8.6)

\forall r,s,t\in\Gamma,\quad\omega(r,st)\omega(s,t)=\omega(r,s)\omega(rs,t).

The mapping

(8.7)

\begin{split}\delta\colon C^{1}(\Gamma,\mathbb{T})&\rightarrow Z^{2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{T})\\
\mathsf{b}&\mapsto\left\{(r,s)\in\Gamma\times\Gamma\mapsto\frac{\mathsf{b}(r)\mathsf{b}(s)}{\mathsf{b}(rs)}\right\}\end{split}

is easily checked to be a well-defined group morphism. We use

B^{2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{T})
to denote the image of
\delta
, and the

2
-cocycles in
B^{2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{T})
are called

2
-coboundaries of
\Gamma
. The quotient group

Z^{2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{T})/B^{2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{T})
is called the second
cohomology group of
\Gamma
with coefficients in the trivial

\Gamma
-module
\mathbb{T}
, and is denoted by

H^{2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{T})
. Elements in
H^{2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{T})
are
called cohomology class. Note that
\ker(\delta)
is exactly the group of
characters on
\Gamma
, i.e. group morphisms from
\Gamma
to

\mathbb{T}
.

Definition 8.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group,
\mathscr{H}
a finite dimensional Hilbert
space, a projective representation of
\Gamma
on
\mathscr{H}
is a
mapping
V:\Gamma\to\mathcal{U}(\mathscr{H})
such that

V(e_{\Gamma})=\id_{\mathscr{H}}
, and there exists a

2
-cochain
\omega\in C^{2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{T})
, such
that

(8.8)

\forall r,s\in\Gamma,\qquad\omega(r,s)V(r,s)=V(r)V(s).

It is easy to check that such
\omega
is uniquely determined by
V
,
and it is in fact a
2
-cocylce, with the additional property
(which follows from our assumption
V(e_{\Gamma})=\id_{\mathscr{H}}
)
that

(8.9)

\forall\gamma\in\Gamma,\qquad\omega(e_{\Gamma},\gamma)=\omega(\gamma,e_{\Gamma})=1\in\mathbb{T}.

We call
\omega
the cocylce (or Schur multiplier after Schur who
introduced them in his work on projective representations

[
schur1904darstellung
]
) of the projective representation
V
.

We will freely use the character theory and the Peter Weyl theory of projective
representations of finite groups, and we refer the reader to
[
MR3299063
]

for the proofs.

We track here the following easy results for convenience of the reader.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite group,

V\colon\Gamma\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathscr{H})
a finite
dimensional unitary projective representation of
\Gamma
with cocycle

\omega
. If
\omega^{\prime}\in[\omega]\in H^{2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{T})
,
then there exists a mapping

\mathsf{b}\colon\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
, such that

\mathsf{b}V\colon\Gamma\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathscr{H})
,

\gamma\mapsto\mathsf{b}(\gamma)V(\gamma)
is a unitary projective
representation with cocycle
\omega^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Since
\omega^{\prime}\in[\omega]
, there is a mapping

\mathsf{b}\colon\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
such that

\omega^{\prime}=(\delta\mathsf{b})\omega
, and obviously,
\mathsf{b}V

is a unitary projective representation with

(\delta\mathsf{b})\omega=\omega^{\prime}
as its cocycle.
∎

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite group,

V\colon\Gamma\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathscr{H})
a finite
dimensional unitary projective representation of
\Gamma
with cocycle

\omega
, and let
\mathsf{b}\colon\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
be
an arbitrary mapping. The following hold:

(a)

\mathsf{b}V\colon\Gamma\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathscr{H})
,

\gamma\mapsto\mathsf{b}(\gamma)V(\gamma)
is a projective
representation with cocycle
(\delta\mathsf{b})\omega
;

(b)

\mathsf{b}V
and
V
have the
same cocycle if and only if
\mathsf{b}\in\ker(\delta)
, i.e.
\mathsf{b}
is a character of
\Gamma
;

(c)

\mathsf{b}V
is irreducible if and
only if
V
is irreducible.

Proof.

It is clear that
(a)
and
(b)

are direct consequences of the relevant definitions. We now prove

(c)
. If we denote the character of
V
by

\chi_{V}
, then the character of
\mathsf{b}V
is

\mathsf{b}\chi_{V}
. Hence

(8.10)

\begin{split}&\hskip 11.99998pt\dim\morph_{\Gamma}(\mathsf{b}V,\mathsf{b}V)=\frac{1}{\left\lvert\Gamma\right\rvert}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\overline{\mathsf{b}(\gamma)\chi_{V}(\gamma)}\mathsf{b}(\gamma)\chi_{V}(\gamma)\\
&=\frac{1}{\left\lvert\Gamma\right\rvert}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\overline{\chi_{V}(\gamma)}\chi_{V}(\gamma)=\dim\morph_{\Gamma}(V,V),\end{split}

and
\mathsf{b}V
is irreducible if and only if
V
is.
∎

Remark 8.4
.

If
\mathsf{b}
is a character of
\Gamma
, and

V\colon\Gamma\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathscr{H})
an irreducible
unitary projective representation, then
\mathsf{b}V
is also an
irreducible unitary projective representation with the same cocycle as that of

V
. Note that
\left\lvert\mathsf{b}(\gamma)\right\rvert=1
for all

\gamma\in\Gamma
, we have

(8.11)

\begin{split}&\hskip 11.99998pt\dim\morph_{\Gamma}(\mathsf{b}V,V)=\frac{1}{\left\lvert\Gamma\right\rvert}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\overline{\mathsf{b}(\gamma)}\chi_{V}(\gamma)\overline{\chi_{V}(\gamma)}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\left\lvert\Gamma\right\rvert}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\chi_{V}(\gamma)\overline{\chi_{V}(\gamma)}=\dim\morph_{\Gamma}(V,V)=1.\end{split}

with equality holds if and only if
\mathsf{b}(\gamma)=1
whenever

\chi_{V}(\gamma)\neq 0
. If equality doesn’t hold
in (
8.11
), then

\dim\morph_{\Gamma}(\mathsf{b}V,V)
must be
0
since it is a
natural number. Therefore, whenever
\Gamma
is not trivial, it is
possible that
\mathsf{b}V
and
V
are irreducible unitary projective
representations with the same cocycle but not equivalent. Thus one must be
careful not to confuse our definition of projective representation with the
more naive definition where one simply replaces

\generallinear(\mathscr{H})
by
\prglin(\mathscr{H})
as the target
model group. For us, how we lift from
\prglin(\mathscr{H})
to

\generallinear(\mathscr{H})

does
matter, even if we keep the
cocycle in the process.

After this digression, we now resume our discussion. Using terminologies in the
theory of group cohomology, and regarding
\mathbb{T}
as the trivial

\Lambda_{x}
-module, we see that the
2
-cocycle

\omega_{x}\in C^{2}(\Lambda_{x},\mathbb{T})
of the unitary projective
representation
V
of
\Lambda_{x}
is determined up to a

2
-boundary in
B^{2}(\Lambda_{x},\mathbb{T})
, because each unitary
operator
V(r_{0})
,
r_{0}\in\Lambda_{x}
is uniquely determined up
to a scalar multiple in
\mathbb{T}
(Schur’s lemma plus the unitarity of

V(r_{0})
). In other words,

[\omega_{x}]\in H^{2}(\Lambda_{x},\mathbb{T})
is a well-defined
cohomology class of
\Lambda_{x}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{T}
.

Conversely, let
u
be an
irreducible
unitary representation of

\mathbb{G}
on some finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
, and

x=[u]\in\irr(\mathbb{G})
. If
\Lambda_{0}
is a subgroup of

\Lambda
,
V\colon\Lambda_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathscr{H})
a
unitary projection representation of
\Lambda_{0}
such that
u
and

V
satisfy the covariance condition (
8.3
), then

\forall r_{0}\in\Lambda_{0},\quad V(r_{0})\in\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(r_{0}\cdot u,u).

In particular,
\Lambda_{0}
fixes
x=[u]
under the action

\Lambda\curvearrowright\irr(\mathbb{G})
. Repeat the above reasoning
shows that (
8.5
) still holds.

We summarize the above discussion in the following proposition, which proves
slightly more.

Proposition 8.5
.

Let
u
be an irreducible unitary representation of
\mathbb{G}
on
some finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
,

x=[u]\in\irr(\mathbb{G})
,
\Lambda_{x}
the isotropy group
fixing

(under the action

). Then

);

(b)

-cocycle of

.

Conversely, if

of

\Lambda

(c)

-cocycle

, then

under

The above discussion already establishes

,

(c)
and
(d)
. Assertion

(e)
follows from
(a)
and

(c)
, while
(f)
follows from

(e)
. Moreover, we’ve seen that

Since

and

-cocycle of

coincides with

-cocycle of

indeed depends only
on

and not on any particular choice of

and finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Definition 8.6
.

Using the notations in Proposition
8.5
, we call the
cohomology class
[\omega]\in H^{2}(\Lambda_{x},\mathbb{T})
the
cohomology class associated with
x=[u]\in\irr(\mathbb{G})
, and we
denote
[\omega]
by

. If
\Lambda_{0}
is a subgroup of

\Lambda_{x}

is called the

on
\Lambda_{0}
, and is
denoted by

.

Obviously,

depends on

and

,
and

if
\Lambda_{0}=\Lambda_{x}
. To
apply the character theory of projective representations, we need to suitably
rescale the projective representations in question so that they share the same

of

is irreducible, and

projective representation satisfying the covariance

for each

tells

9.
Pure, stable, distinguished CSRs and representation parameters

Recall that for any finite dimensional representation
u
of

\mathbb{G}
, the support of
u
, denoted by
\supp(u)
, is the set

\left\{x\in\irr(\mathbb{G})\;:\;\dim_{\mathbb{G}}\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(x,[u])\neq 0\right\}

where
[u]
is the class of unitary representations of
\mathbb{G}

equivalent to
u
. We call
u

if
\supp(u)
is a
singleton.

Definition 9.1
.

Fix a
\Lambda_{0}\in\giso(\Lambda)
,

\mathbf{S}=(\mathscr{H},u,w)\in\mathcal{CSR}_{\Lambda_{0}}
, we
call
\mathbf{S}

—

u
is pure;

—

stable

r\cdot[u](=[r\cdot u])=[u]
for all

;

—

stably pure
, if it is both pure and stable;

—

maximally stable
, if

\Lambda_{0}=\left\{r\in\Lambda\;:\;r\cdot[u]=[u]\right\};

—

, if
\mathbf{S}
is a simple object in

\mathcal{CSR}_{\Lambda_{0}}
;

—

, if it is maximally stable, pure and simple.

As remarked earlier, while it is not reasonable for the moment to hope for a
satisfactory description of all simple CSRs in

\mathcal{CSR}_{\Lambda_{0}}
, it is possible to describe simple CSRs that
are stably pure using unitary projective representations of
\Lambda_{0}
.
Somewhat surprisingly, one can even describe all stably pure CSRs, even the
non-simple ones, in this way. To achieve the latter, we introduce the following
definitions, which are closely related to the materials in
§
8
.

Definition 9.2
.

Let
\Lambda_{0}\in\giso(\Lambda)
. Suppose

is a unitary
representation of
\mathbb{G}
on some finite dimensional Hilbert space

\mathscr{H}
, and

is a unitary

\Lambda_{0}
. We say

and

are

condition (
8.3
), or equivalently

V(r_{0})\in\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(r_{0}\cdot u,u)
for all

r_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}
.

Definition 9.3
.

Let

,
\Lambda_{0}\in\giso(\Lambda)
with

\Lambda_{0}\subseteq\Lambda_{x}
,

,

\omega_{0}\in c_{x,\Lambda_{0}}
(see

), then a unitary projective
representation

of
\Lambda_{0}
that is covariant with

is
said to be a covariant projective
\Lambda_{0}
-representation of

(with cocycle
\omega_{0}
).

Remark 9.4
.

In the setting of Definition

, fix any covariant
projective
\Lambda_{0}
-representation

of

with cocycle

, the set of covariant projective

\Lambda_{0}
-representations of

with multiplier

is in bijective correspondence with the group of characters of

, via

(see

).

(Structure of stably pure CSR)

Fix a

is the support

if necessary, we may assume

usual identification, there exists an

,

and

and

\begin{split}&\hskip 11.99998pt\sum_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}\otimes[(B_{i}\otimes 1)u_{0}]\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}\otimes\Bigl(\bigl[(\id_{\mathscr{H}_{0}}\otimes\alpha^{\ast}_{r_{0}})u_{0}\bigr]\bigl(B_{i}\otimes 1\bigr)\Bigr)\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{H}_{0})\otimes\pol(\mathbb{G}).\end{split}

Now the claim follows from (

)
and (

).
∎

.

Proof.

Proposition 9.11
.

Fix a
\Lambda_{0}\in\giso(\Lambda)
. For

\irr(\mathbb{G})

\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}(U_{1},U_{2})=0

the

(9.7)

\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}(U_{1},U_{2})=\dim\morph_{\Lambda_{0}}\left(v_{1},\mathsf{b}v_{2}\right).

Proof.

Let
h
be the Haar state of
\mathbb{G}
,
by (
1.7
), the Haar state
h_{\Lambda_{0}}
of

\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}
is the linear functional on

defined by

a\otimes\delta_{r_{0}}\mapsto\left\lvert\Lambda_{0}\right\rvert^{-1}h(a)
, where

,
r_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}
(recall that

).

Suppose
[u_{1}]\neq[u_{2}]
. For any
i=1,2
, by choosing a
Hilbert space basis for the representation of

, one can write

u_{i}
as a square matrix
\left(u^{(i)}_{jk}\right)
over

\pol(\mathbb{G})\subseteq A

\left(V^{(i)}_{jk}\right)

of the same size of

\chi_{i}

(9.8)

\chi_{i}=\sum_{r_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}\tr(v_{i})\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n_{i}}V^{(i)}_{kj}(r_{0})u^{(i)}_{jk}\right)\otimes\delta_{r_{0}}\in\pol(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\Lambda_{0}).

The orthogonality relation for the nonequivalent irreducible representations

and
u_{2}
implies that

(9.9)

\forall j_{1},k_{1},j_{2},k_{2},\quad h\left({\bigl(u^{(1)}_{j_{1}k_{1}}\bigr)}^{\ast}u^{(2)}_{j_{2}k_{2}}\right)=0.

Hence, by (
9.8
) and (
9.9
),

(9.10)

\begin{split}&\hskip 11.99998pt\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}(U_{1},U_{2})=h_{\Lambda_{0}}\bigl(\overline{\chi_{1}}\chi_{2}\bigr)\\
&=\left\lvert\Lambda_{0}\right\rvert^{-1}\sum_{r_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{j_{1},k_{1}=1}^{n_{1}}\sum_{j_{2},k_{2}=1}^{n_{2}}\overline{\tr\bigl(v_{1}(r_{0})\bigr)}\tr\bigl(v_{2}(r_{0})\bigr)\\
&\hskip 30.00005pt\overline{V^{(1)}_{k_{1}j_{1}}(r_{0})}V^{(2)}_{k_{2}j_{2}}(r_{0})h\left({\bigl(u^{(1)}_{j_{1}k_{1}}\bigr)}^{\ast}u^{(2)}_{j_{2}k_{2}}\right)\\
&=0.\end{split}

This proves
(a)
.

Under the hypothesis of
(b)
, using the same notations
as in the previous paragraph, we have
n_{1}=n_{2}=\dim U
. We may
assume that
e^{(1)}_{j}=e^{(2)}_{j}=e_{j}
, hence

u_{jk}:=u^{(1)}_{jk}=u^{(2)}_{jk}
for all possible
j,k
. Note
that

, and

may assume that
V_{2}=V_{1}=V
and
\mathsf{b}=1
, with

V_{jk}:=V^{(1)}_{jk}=V^{(2)}_{jk}\in C(\Lambda_{0})
for all
possible
j,k
. Let
\rho
be the unique invertible positive operator
in
\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(u,u^{cc})
such that

\tr(\cdot\,\rho)=\tr(\cdot\,\rho^{-1})
on

\selfmorph_{\mathbb{G}}(u)
. With these assumptions,
by (
9.10
), the orthogonality relation takes the form

(9.11)

h(u_{ij}^{\ast}u_{kl})=\frac{\delta_{j,l}{\bigl(\rho^{-1}\bigr)}_{ki}}{\dim_{q}U}

where

is the quantum dimension of

(see
[
MR3204665
, §1.4]
). Since
\rho
is positive, we might
choose the basis
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
to diagonize
\rho
, so that

\rho_{ki}={\bigl(\rho^{-1}\bigr)}_{ki}=0
whenever

. Using this basis, (
9.11
) and
(
9.10
), we have

(9.12)

\begin{split}&\hskip 11.99998pt\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}(U_{1},U_{2})\\
&=\left\lvert\Lambda_{0}\right\rvert^{-1}\sum_{r_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{j_{1},k_{1}=1}^{n}\sum_{j_{2},k_{2}=1}^{n}\overline{\tr\bigl(v_{1}(r_{0})\bigr)}\tr\bigl(v_{2}(r_{0})\bigr)\\
&\hskip 120.00018pt\cdot\overline{V_{k_{1}j_{1}}(r_{0})}V_{k_{2}j_{2}}(r_{0})h\left({\bigl(u_{j_{1}k_{1}}\bigr)}^{\ast}u_{j_{2}k_{2}}\right)\\
&=\left\lvert\Lambda_{0}\right\rvert^{-1}\sum_{r_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{j_{1},k_{1}=1}^{n}\sum_{j_{2},k_{2}=1}^{n}\overline{\tr\bigl(v_{1}(r_{0})\bigr)}\tr\bigl(v_{2}(r_{0})\bigr)\overline{V_{k_{1}j_{1}}(r_{0})}V_{k_{2}j_{2}}(r_{0})\\
&\hskip 120.00018pt\cdot\frac{\delta_{j_{1},j_{2}}\delta_{k_{1},k_{2}}{\bigl(\rho^{-1}\bigr)}_{j_{2}j_{1}}}{\dim_{q}U}\\
&=\left\lvert\Lambda_{0}\right\rvert^{-1}\overline{\tr\bigl(v_{1}(r_{0})\bigr)}\tr\bigl(v_{2}(r_{0})\bigr)\sum_{r_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\overline{V_{kj}(r_{0})}V_{kj}(r_{0})\right\}\frac{{\bigl(\rho^{-1}\bigr)}_{jj}}{\dim_{q}U}\\
&\hskip 11.99998pt(\text{Note that $V(r_{0})$ is unitary})\\
&=\left\lvert\Lambda_{0}\right\rvert^{-1}\sum_{r_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}}\overline{\tr\bigl(v_{1}(r_{0})\bigr)}\tr\bigl(v_{2}(r_{0})\bigr)\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\bigl(\rho^{-1}\bigr)}_{jj}}{\dim_{q}U}\\
&=\left\lvert\Lambda_{0}\right\rvert^{-1}\sum_{r_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}}\overline{\tr\bigl(v_{1}(r_{0})\bigr)}\tr\bigl(v_{2}(r_{0})\bigr)=\dim\morph_{\Lambda_{0}}(v_{1},v_{2}).\end{split}

This proves

.

10.
Distinguished representation parameters and distinguished
representations

Fix a
\Lambda_{0}\in\giso(\Lambda)
. For any unitary projective
representation
V\colon\Lambda_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathscr{H})

of
\Lambda_{0}
, and any
r\in\Lambda
, define
r\cdot V
to be
the unitary projective representation of
r\Lambda_{0}r^{-1}
on

\mathscr{H}
sending
s_{0}=rr_{0}r^{-1}\in r\Lambda_{0}r^{-1}
to

(V\circ\adj_{r^{-1}})(s_{0})=V(r_{0})
. Then

(rs)\cdot V=r\cdot(s\cdot V)
for all

with

1_{\Lambda}\cdot V=V
, in other words, this defines an action of the
group
\Lambda
on the class of all unitary projective representations of
general isotropy subgroups of
\Lambda
.

It is easy to see from Proposition
3.9
that whenever

\mathbf{S}=(\mathscr{H},u,w)\in\mathcal{CSR}_{\Lambda_{0}}
, the

in
\mathcal{CSR}_{r\Lambda_{0}r^{-1}}

U=\mathscr{R}_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathbf{S})
is the unitary representation of

\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}

\mathscr{R}_{r\Lambda_{0}r^{-1}}(r\cdot\mathbf{S})

\mathbb{G}\rtimes r\Lambda_{0}r^{-1}
, as
described in Proposition
5.1
. Thus by
Corollary
4.4
, we see that
\indrep(U)
and

\indrep(r\cdot U)
are equivalent representations of

\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
.

Similarly, for any representation parameter
(u,V,v)
associated with

\Lambda_{0}
and any
r\in\Lambda
, the triple

(r\cdot u,r\cdot V,r\cdot v)
is a representation parameter associated
with
r\Lambda_{0}r^{-1}
, which we denoted by

.
This clearly defines an
\Lambda
-action on the proper class of all
representation parameters associated with any group in some conjugacy class of a
general isotropy subgroup of
\Lambda
. A simple calculation shows that
(recall
\mathcal{S}(u,V,v)
is the CSR parameterized by
(u,V,v)
)

(10.1)

\forall r\in\Lambda,\quad r\cdot\mathcal{S}(u,V,v)=\mathcal{S}\bigl(r\cdot(u,V,v)\bigr).

.

Remark 10.5
.

The associated group of a distinguished representation parameter must be an
isotropy subgroup of
\Lambda
for the action

\Lambda\curvearrowright\irr(\mathbb{G})
. More precisely, a
representation parameter
(u,V,v)
is distinguished if and only if its
associated group is exactly the isotropy subgroup of

[u]\in\irr(\mathbb{G})
under the action

\Lambda\curvearrowright\irr(\mathbb{G})
. As we will see presently, in
the formulation of our results on the classification of irreducible
representations of
\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
and the conjugation on

\irr(\mathbb{G})

needed. This makes one wonder why we pose the family of general isotropy
subgroup
\giso(\Lambda)
instead of only isotropy subgroups. The main
reason we need general isotropy subgroups of
\Lambda
is that in proving
these results, as well as the formulation and the proof of the fusion rules,
we need to express the dimensions of various intertwiner spaces. The
calculation of the dimensions of these intertwiner spaces will rely on
Proposition
6.3
, which clearly requires us to consider
the intersections of isotropy subgroups, i.e. general isotropy subgroups.

The following proposition serves to characterize equivalence of distinguished

Proposition 10.7
.

Let
\Lambda_{0}
be an isotropy subgroup of
\Lambda
for the action

\Lambda\curvearrowright\irr(\mathbb{G})
,
(u_{1},V_{1},v_{1})

and
(u_{2},V_{2},v_{2})

(a)

(u_{1},V_{1},v_{1})
and

(u_{2},V_{2},v_{2})
are equivalent;

(b)

(u_{1},V_{1},v_{1})
and

(u_{2},V_{2},v_{2})
parameterize equivalent representations of

\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}
;

(c)

there exists a mapping

\mathsf{b}\colon\Lambda_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
such that

\mathsf{b}V_{1}
and
V_{2}
share the same cocycle, and both

\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(u_{1},u_{2})\cap\morph_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathsf{b}V_{1},V_{2})

\morph_{\Lambda_{0}}(v_{1},\mathsf{b}v_{2})

(d)

there exists a mapping

\mathsf{b}\colon\Lambda_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
such that

\mathsf{b}V_{1}
and
V_{2}
share the same cocycle, and both

\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(u_{1},u_{2})\cap\morph_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathsf{b}V_{1},V_{2})
and

contain unitary

Proof.

The equivalence of
(a)
and
(b)

follows directly from the definitions. It is also clear that

(d)
implies
(c)
. If

(c)
holds, and

(10.3)

\begin{gathered}0\neq S\in\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(u_{1},u_{2})\cap\morph_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathsf{b}V_{1},V_{2}),\\
\text{and}\quad 0\neq T\in\morph_{\Lambda_{0}}(v_{1},\mathsf{b}v_{2})=\morph_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathsf{b}^{-1}v_{1},v_{2}),\end{gathered}

then both
S
and
T
are invertible by Schur’s lemma as

u_{1},u_{2},\mathsf{b}^{-1}v_{1},v_{2}
are all irreducible. Since

u_{1},u_{2},\mathsf{b}V_{1},V_{2},v_{1},\mathsf{b}v_{2}
are all
unitary, we have

(10.4)

0\neq\Upsilon_{S}\in\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(u_{1},u_{2})\cap\morph_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathsf{b}V_{1},V_{2}),\quad\text{and}\quad 0\neq\Upsilon_{T}\in\morph_{\Lambda_{0}}(v_{1},\mathsf{b}v_{2}),

where
S=\Upsilon_{S}\left\lvert S\right\rvert
is the polar decomposition of
S
,
and
T=\Upsilon_{T}\left\lvert T\right\rvert
the polar decomposition of
T
. As

S,T
are invertible,
\Upsilon_{S}
and
\Upsilon_{T}
are
unitary. This proves that
(c)
implies

(d)
.

Let
\mathscr{K}_{i}
be the representation space of
v_{i}
for

i=1,2
. By definition,

\mathcal{S}(u_{i},V_{i},v_{i})=(\id_{\mathscr{K}_{i}}\otimes u,v_{i}\times V_{i})
, and

\mathsf{b}^{-1}v_{i}\times\mathsf{b}V_{i}=v_{i}\times V_{i}
for any
mapping
\mathsf{b}\colon\Lambda_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
. If

(c)
holds, let
S
,
T
be operators as
in (
10.3
), then

(10.5)

T\otimes S\in\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(\id_{\mathscr{K}_{1}}\otimes u_{1},\id_{\mathscr{K}_{2}}\otimes u_{2})\cap\morph_{\Lambda_{0}}(v_{1}\times V_{1},v_{2}\times V_{2}).

Now
(a)
follows from (
10.5
),
Proposition
7.4
and the fact that both
S
and

T
are invertible. Thus
(c)
implies

(a)
.

We conclude the proof by showing
(a)
implies

(d)
. By Schur’s lemma, and the irreducibility of

u_{1}
and
u_{2}
, it is easy to see that

(10.6)

\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(\id_{\mathscr{K}_{1}}\otimes u_{1},\id_{\mathscr{K}_{2}}\otimes u_{2})=\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{K}_{1},\mathscr{K}_{2})\otimes\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(u_{1},u_{2}).

Suppose
(a)
holds. Then the intertwiner space given by
the intersection in (
10.5
) is nonzero, and

(10.7)

\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(u_{1},u_{2})=\mathbb{C}W_{r}

for some unitary operator
W_{r}
. By (
10.6
) and

(a)
, there exists a unitary

W_{l}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{K}_{1},\mathscr{K}_{2})
such that

(10.8)

W_{l}\otimes W_{r}\in\morph_{\Lambda_{0}}(v_{1}\times V_{1},v_{2}\times V_{2})=\morph_{\Lambda_{0}}\bigl((\mathsf{b}^{-1}v_{1})\times(\mathsf{b}V_{1}),v_{2}\times V_{2}\bigr).

By (
10.7
), both
W_{r}V_{1}W_{r}^{\ast}
and

V_{2}
are covariant projective
\Lambda_{0}
-representations of

u_{2}
. Thus we can take a
u_{2}
-transitional mapping

\mathsf{b}
from
W_{r}V_{1}W_{r}^{\ast}
to
V_{2}
(see
Definition
9.10
), i.e. a mapping

\mathsf{b}\colon\Lambda_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}
such that

(10.9)

W_{r}(\mathsf{b}V_{1})W_{r}^{\ast}=\mathsf{b}(W_{r}V_{1}W_{r}^{\ast})=V_{2},

which forces the cocycles of
\mathsf{b}V_{1}
and
V_{2}
coincide,
and

(10.10)

W_{r}\in\morph_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathsf{b}V_{1},V_{2})\cap\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(u_{1},u_{2}).

Now (
10.8
) and (
10.10
) forces

(10.11)

W_{l}\in\morph_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathsf{b}^{-1}v_{1},v_{2})=\morph_{\Lambda_{0}}(v_{1},\mathsf{b}v_{2}).

Thus
(d)
holds by (
10.10
) and
(
10.11
).
∎

11.
Density of matrix coefficients of distinguished representations

The aim of this section is to show that the linear span of matrix coefficients
of distinguished representations of
\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
is exactly

\pol(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\Lambda)
, hence is dense in

C(\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda)=A\otimes C(\Lambda)
in particular. As a
consequence, any irreducible unitary representation of

\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
is equivalent to a distinguished one.

The following lemma essentially establishes the density of the linear span of
matrix coefficients of distinguished representations of

\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
in

C(\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda)=A\otimes C(\Lambda)
.

Lemma 11.1
.

Let
u
be an irreducible unitary representation of
\mathbb{G}
on
some finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
,

x=[u]\in\irr(\mathbb{G})
,
V
the covariant projective

\Lambda_{x}
-representation of
u
with cocycle
\omega
. Let

M(u)
denote the linear subspace of

\pol(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\Lambda)
spanned by matrix coefficients of
distinguished representations of
\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda

parameterized by distinguished representation parameters of the form

(u,V,v)
, where
v
runs through all irreducible unitary projective
representations of
\Lambda_{x}
with cocycle

\omega^{-1}=\overline{\omega}
. For any
r\in\Lambda
, suppose

M_{c}(r\cdot u)
is the linear subspace of
\pol(\mathbb{G})

spanned by matrix coefficients of
r\cdot u
, then

(11.1)

M(u)=\sum_{r\in\Lambda}M_{c}(r\cdot u)\otimes C(\Lambda)=\left(\sum_{r\in\Lambda}M_{c}(r\cdot u)\right)\otimes C(\Lambda).

Proof.

Take any irreducible unitary projective representation
v
of

\Lambda_{x}
on some finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathscr{K}

with cocycle
\overline{\omega}
, then
(u,V,v)
is a distinguished
representation parameter. The distinguished CSR
\mathcal{S}(u,V,v)

subordinate to
\Lambda_{x}
parameterized by
(u,V,v)
is given by

(11.2)

\mathcal{S}(u,V,v)=(\mathscr{K}\otimes\mathscr{H},\id_{\mathscr{K}}\otimes u,v\times V)

by definition. Let

U=\mathscr{R}_{\Lambda_{0}}\bigl(\mathcal{S}(u,V,v)\bigr)
, then the
distinguished representation
W=\indrep(U)
of

\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
parameterized by
(u,V,v)
is obtained
as follows by the construction of induced representations presented in
§
4
. First we define a unitary representation

(11.3)

\begin{split}\widetilde{W}=\sum_{r,s\in\Lambda}e_{rs^{-1},r}\otimes\id_{\mathscr{K}}\otimes[(\id_{\mathscr{H}}\otimes\alpha^{\ast}_{rs^{-1}})(u)]\otimes\delta_{s}\\
\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda))\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{K})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{H})\otimes\pol(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\Lambda)\end{split}

of
\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
on

\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\otimes\mathscr{K}\otimes\mathscr{H}
. The subspace

(11.4)

\mathscr{H}_{(u,V,v)}=\left\{\sum_{r\in\Lambda}\delta_{r}\otimes\zeta_{r}\;:\;\begin{array}[]{r}\zeta_{r}\in\mathscr{K}\otimes\mathscr{H},\text{ and }\;\zeta_{r_{0}r}=\bigl(v(r_{0})\otimes V(r_{0})\bigr)\zeta_{r}\\
\text{ for all }r_{0}\in\Lambda_{0},r\in\Lambda\end{array}\right\}

of
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\otimes\mathscr{K}\otimes\mathscr{H}
is
invariant under
\widetilde{W}
and
W
is the subrepresentation

\mathscr{H}_{(u,V,v)}
of
\widetilde{W}
. Recall
(Lemma
4.5
) that the projection

\pi\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\otimes\mathscr{K}\otimes\mathscr{H})
with range
\mathscr{H}_{u,V,v}
is given by

(11.5)

\pi=\frac{1}{\left\lvert\Lambda_{x}\right\rvert}\sum_{r_{0}\in\Lambda_{x}}\sum_{s\in\Lambda}e_{r_{0}s,s}\otimes v(r_{0})\otimes V(r_{0}).

Since vectors of the form
\delta_{r}\otimes\xi\otimes\eta
,

r\in\Lambda
,
\xi\in\mathscr{K}
,
\eta\in\mathscr{H}

span
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)\otimes\mathscr{K}\otimes\mathscr{H}
, the
matrix coefficients of
W
is spanned by elements of

\pol(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\Lambda)
of the form

(11.6)

\begin{split}&\hskip 11.99998ptc(v;r,s,\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\\
&=(\omega_{\pi(\delta_{r}\otimes\xi_{1}\otimes\eta_{1}),\pi(\delta_{s}\otimes\xi_{2}\otimes\eta_{2})}\otimes\id_{\pol(\mathbb{G})}\otimes\id_{C(\Lambda)})(W)\\
&=(\omega_{\delta_{r}\otimes\xi_{1}\otimes\eta_{1},\delta_{s}\otimes\xi_{2}\otimes\eta_{2}}\otimes\id\otimes\id)\bigl((\pi\otimes 1\otimes 1)\widetilde{W}(\pi\otimes 1\otimes 1)\bigr)\\
&=(\omega_{\delta_{r}\otimes\xi_{1}\otimes\eta_{1},\delta_{s}\otimes\xi_{2}\otimes\eta_{2}}\otimes\id\otimes\id)\bigl(\widetilde{W}(\pi\otimes 1\otimes 1)\bigr),\end{split}

where the last equality follows from Lemma
4.5
, and

\omega_{x,y}
is the linear form
\left\langle\,\cdot\,x\,,\,y\right\rangle
.

By (
11.3
) and (
11.5
), we see that

(11.7)

\begin{split}&\hskip 11.99998pt\left\lvert\Lambda_{0}\right\rvert\cdot\bigl[\widetilde{W}(\pi\otimes 1\otimes 1)\bigr](\delta_{r}\otimes\xi_{1}\otimes\eta_{1}\otimes 1\otimes 1)\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r^{\prime},s^{\prime},t\in\Lambda,\\
r_{0}\in\Lambda_{x}\end{subarray}}\left[e_{r^{\prime}s^{\prime-1},r^{\prime}}e_{r_{0}t,t}\otimes v(r_{0})\otimes\Bigl(\bigl((s^{\prime}r^{\prime-1})\cdot u\bigr)\bigl(V(r_{0})\otimes 1\bigr)\Bigr)\otimes\delta_{s^{\prime}}\right]\\
&\hskip 120.00018pt\cdot(\delta_{r}\otimes\xi_{1}\otimes\eta_{1}\otimes 1\otimes 1)\\
&\hskip 11.99998pt(\text{Only terms with $t=r$, and
$r^{\prime}=r_{0}t=r_{0}r$ can be nonzero})\\
&=\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\Lambda}\sum_{r_{0}\in\Lambda_{x}}\delta_{r_{0}rs^{\prime-1}}\otimes[v(r_{0})\xi_{1}]\otimes\Bigl[\bigl((s^{\prime}r^{-1}r_{0}^{-1})\cdot u\bigr)\bigl(V(r_{0})\eta_{1}\otimes 1\bigr)\Bigr]\otimes\delta_{s^{\prime}}.\end{split}

Note that

r_{0}rs^{\prime-1}=s\iff s^{\prime}=s^{-1}r_{0}r\iff s^{\prime}r^{-1}r_{0}^{-1}=s^{-1}
, by (
11.6
) and (
11.7
), we have

(11.8)

c(v;r,s,\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\eta_{1},\eta_{2})=\sum_{r_{0}\in\Lambda_{x}}\omega_{\xi_{1},\xi_{2}}\bigl(v(r_{0})\bigr)\bigl[(\omega_{V(r_{0})\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}\otimes\id)(s^{-1}\cdot u)\bigr]\otimes\delta_{s^{-1}r_{0}r}.

For any
r_{0}\in\Lambda_{x}
, we have

(11.9)

\omega_{\xi_{1},\xi_{2}}\bigl(v(r_{0})\bigr)\in\mathbb{C}\quad\text{and}\quad\bigl[(\omega_{V(r_{0})\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}\otimes\id)(s^{-1}\cdot u)\bigr]\in M_{c}(s^{-1}\cdot u).

By (
11.8
) and (
11.9
), we have

(11.10)

c(v;r,s,\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\in M_{c}(s^{-1}\cdot u)\otimes C(\Lambda),

which proves that

(11.11)

M(u)\subseteq\sum_{r^{\prime}\in\Lambda}M_{c}(r^{\prime}\cdot u)\otimes C(\Lambda)=\left(\sum_{r^{\prime}\in\Lambda}M_{c}(r^{\prime}\cdot u)\right)\otimes C(\Lambda).

It remains to establish the reverse inclusion, which is easily seen to be
equivalent to show that for any
r_{1},r_{2}\in\Lambda
, we have

(11.12)

M(u)\supseteq M_{c}(r_{1}\cdot u)\otimes\delta_{r_{2}}.

By the general theory of projective representations, there exists irreducible
unitary projective representations
v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}
on

\mathscr{K}_{1},\ldots,\mathscr{K}_{m}
respectively, all with cocycle

\overline{\omega}
, and

\xi^{(i)}_{1},\xi^{(i)}_{2}\in\mathscr{K}_{i}
, such that

(11.13)

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\omega_{\xi^{(i)}_{1},\xi^{(i)}_{2}}\otimes\id\right)(v_{i})=\delta_{e}\in C(\Lambda_{x}).

By (
11.8
) and (
11.13
), we see that
for any
r,s\in\Lambda
, and any

\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\mathscr{H}
,
M(u)
contains

(11.14)

\begin{split}&\hskip 11.99998pt\sum_{i=1}^{n}c(v_{i};r,s\xi^{(i)}_{1},\xi^{(i)}_{2},\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\\
&=\sum_{r_{0}\in\Lambda_{x}}\delta_{e}(r_{0})\bigl[(\omega_{V(r_{0})\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}\otimes\id)(s^{-1}\cdot u)\bigr]\otimes\delta_{s^{-1}r_{0}r}\\
&\hskip 11.99998pt(\text{Only terms with $r_{0}=e$ can be nonzero, and
$V(e)=\id_{\mathscr{H}}$})\\
&=\bigl[(\omega_{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}\otimes\id)(s^{-1}\cdot u)\bigr]\otimes\delta_{s^{-1}r}.\end{split}

Taking
s=r_{1}^{-1}
and
r=sr_{2}=r_{1}^{-1}r_{2}
in
(
11.14
) proves (
11.12
) and finishes
the proof of the lemma.
∎

Proposition 11.2
.

The linear span of matrix coefficients of distinguished representations of

\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
in
\pol(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\Lambda)

is
\pol(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\Lambda)
itself. In particular, every
unitary irreducible representation of
\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
is
unitarily equivalent to a distinguished one.

Proof.

The first assertion follows from Lemma
11.1
, and the
second assertion follows from the first and the orthogonality relations of
irreducible representations of
\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
.
∎

12.
Classification of irreducible representations of

\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda

For each isotropy subgroup
\Lambda_{0}
of
\Lambda
, let

\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda_{0}}
denotes the collection of equivalence classes of
distinguished representation parameters associated with
\Lambda_{0}
. By
Proposition
10.7
, the mapping

(12.1)

\begin{split}\Psi_{\Lambda_{0}}\colon\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda_{0}}&\to\irr(\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda)\\
[(u,V,v)]&\mapsto\mathscr{R}_{\Lambda_{0}}\bigl(\mathcal{S}(u,V,v)\bigr)\end{split}

is well-defined and injective. In particular,
\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda_{0}}

is a set (instead of a proper class). Let
\mathfrak{D}
be the collection
of equivalence classes of distinguished representation parameters associated
with any isotropy subgroup of
\Lambda
. By definition,
\mathfrak{D}

is the disjoint union of
\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda_{0}}
as
\Lambda_{0}

runs through all isotropy subgroups of
\Lambda
, hence
\mathfrak{D}

is also a set. For any
[(u,V,v)]\in\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda_{0}}
and any

r\in\Lambda
,
r\cdot[(u,V,v)]=[r\cdot(u,V,v)]
is a
well-defined class in
\mathfrak{D}_{r\Lambda_{0}r^{-1}}
. This defines an
action of
\Lambda
on
\mathfrak{D}
. We are now ready to state and
prove the classification of irreducible representations of

\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
.

Theorem 12.1

(Classification of irreducible representations of

\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
)
.

The mapping

(12.2)

\begin{split}\Psi\colon\mathfrak{D}&\rightarrow\irr(\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda)\\
[(u,V,v)]\in\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda_{0}}&\mapsto\Psi_{\Lambda_{0}}\bigl([(u,V,v)]\bigr)=\indrep\Bigl(\mathscr{R}_{\Lambda_{0}}\bigl(\mathcal{S}(u,V,v)\bigr)\Bigr)\end{split}

is surjective, and the fibers of
\Psi
are exactly the

\Lambda
-orbits in
\mathfrak{D}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
11.2
,
\Psi
is surjective. By
Corollary
5.2
and (
10.1
), each

\Lambda
-orbits in
\mathfrak{D}
maps to the same point under

\Psi
. It remains to show that if
(u_{i},V_{i},v_{i})
is a
distinguished representation parameter with associated subgroup

\Lambda_{i}
for
i=1,2
, and

(12.3)

\Psi\bigl([(u_{1},V_{1},v_{1})]\bigr)=\Psi\bigl([(u_{2},V_{2},v_{2})]\bigr),

then there exists an
r_{0}\in\Lambda
, such that

(12.4)

r_{0}\cdot[(u_{1},V_{1},v_{1})]=[(u_{2},V_{2},v_{2})]\in\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda_{2}}.

Let
\mathbf{S}_{i}=\mathcal{S}(u_{i},V_{i},v_{i})
,

U_{i}=\mathscr{R}_{\Lambda_{i}}(\mathbf{S}_{i})
for
i=1,2
. If

[u_{2}]\notin\Lambda\cdot[u_{1}]
, then by
Proposition
9.11
, we have

(12.5)

\forall r,s\in\Lambda,\;\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)}\Bigl((r\cdot U_{1})|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)},(s\cdot U_{2})|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)}\Bigr)=0,

where
\Lambda(r,s)=r\Lambda_{1}r^{-1}\cap s\Lambda_{2}s^{-1}
. This is
because
(r\cdot U_{1})|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)}
is
parameterized by the representation parameter

(u_{1},V_{1}|_{\Lambda(r,s)},v_{1}|_{\Lambda(r,s)})

associated with
\Lambda(r,s)
, and a similar assertion holds for

(s\cdot U_{2})|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)}
. Thus

(12.6)

\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda}\bigl(\indrep(U_{1}),\indrep(U_{2})\bigr)=0

by Proposition
6.3
, which contradicts
(
12.3
).

Thus
[u_{2}]\in\Lambda\cdot[u_{1}]
, by replacing

[(u_{1},V_{1},v_{1})]
with
r_{0}\cdot[(u_{1},V_{1},v_{1})]

for some
r_{0}\in\Lambda
if necessary, we may assume without loss of
generality that
[u_{1}]=[u_{2}]\in\irr(\mathbb{G})
, and

\Lambda_{1}=\Lambda_{2}
, which we now denote by
\Lambda_{0}
. It
remains to prove that under this assumption, we have

(12.7)

[(u_{1},V_{1},v_{1})]=[(u_{2},V_{2},v_{2})]\in\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda_{0}}

Since when
r^{-1}s\notin\Lambda_{0}
if and only if

r\cdot[u_{1}]\neq s\cdot[u_{2}]
, we have

(12.8)

\forall r,s\in\Lambda,\quad r^{-1}s\notin\Lambda_{0}\implies\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)}\bigl((r\cdot U_{1})|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)},(r\cdot U_{2})|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r,s)}\bigr)=0.

Note that when
r^{-1}s\in\Lambda_{0}
, we have

\Lambda(r,s)=r\Lambda_{0}r^{-1}=s\Lambda_{0}s^{-1}
, and

[\Lambda\colon\Lambda(r,s)]=[\Lambda\colon\Lambda_{0}]
. By
(
12.3
), (
12.8
) and
Proposition
6.3
, we have

(12.9)

1=\frac{1}{\left\lvert\Lambda_{0}\right\rvert^{2}[\Lambda\colon\Lambda_{0}]}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,s\in\Lambda,\\
r^{-1}s\in\Lambda_{0}\end{subarray}}\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes r\Lambda_{0}r^{-1}}\bigl(r\cdot U_{1},s\cdot U_{2}\bigr).

Since
r\cdot U_{1}
,
s\cdot U_{2}
are both irreducible, we have

(12.10)

r^{-1}s\in\Lambda_{0}\implies\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes r\Lambda_{0}r^{-1}}\bigl(r\cdot U_{1},s\cdot U_{2}\bigr)=0\;\text{or}\;1.

Note that there are

\left\lvert\Lambda_{0}\right\rvert^{2}[\Lambda\colon\Lambda_{0}]=\left\lvert\Lambda\right\rvert\cdot\left\lvert\Lambda_{0}\right\rvert
terms on the right side of (
12.9
),
(
12.10
) forces

(12.11)

r^{-1}s\in\Lambda_{0}\implies\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes r\Lambda_{0}r^{-1}}\bigl(r\cdot U_{1},s\cdot U_{2}\bigr)=1.

In particular, taking
r=s=1_{\Lambda}
in (
12.11
)
shows that
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are equivalent, hence
(
12.7
) holds by
Proposition
10.7
. This finishes the proof of the
theorem.
∎

13.
The conjugate representation of distinguished representations

We now study the conjugation of irreducible representations of

\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
in terms of the classification presented in
Theorem
12.1
. There is a small complication here in the
non-Kac type case, where the contragredient of a unitary representation need not
be unitary. Resolving this kind of question involves the modular operator, just
as in Proposition
7.7
.

We begin with a simple lemma on linear operators.

Lemma 13.1
.

Let
\mathscr{H}
be a Hilbert space,

U,P\in\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{H})
such that
U
is unitary,
P

is invertible and positive, if
PUP^{-1}
is unitary, then

PUP^{-1}=U
, i.e.
P
commutes with
U
.

Proof.

Let
V=PUP^{-1}
. We have

(13.1)

PU^{\ast}P^{-1}=PU^{-1}P^{-1}=V^{-1}=V^{\ast}=P^{-1}U^{\ast}P.

Thus
U^{\ast}
commutes with the positive operator
P^{2}
. Hence

U^{\ast}
commutes with
{(P^{2})}^{1/2}=P
, i.e.
U^{\ast}P=PU^{\ast}
. Taking adjoints of this proves
PU=UP
.
∎

Proposition 13.2
.

Let
u
be an irreducible unitary representation of
\mathbb{G}
,

\Lambda_{0}
a subgroup of the isotropy subgroup
\Lambda_{[u]}
,

V
a covariant projective
\Lambda_{0}
-representation of
u
.
Then any operator
\rho\in\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(u,u^{cc})
commutes with

V
(i.e.
\rho V(r_{0})=V(r_{0})\rho
for all

r_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}
).

Proof.

Since
u
is irreducible,
\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(u,u^{cc})
is a one
dimensional space spanned by an invertible positive operator (
[
MR3204665
, Lemma
1.3.12]
). By definition (see
[
MR3204665
, Proposition 1.4.4 and
Definition 1.4.5]
), the conjugation
\overline{u}
of
u

is given by

(13.2)

\overline{u}=\bigl({j(\rho_{u})}^{1/2}\otimes 1\bigr)u^{c}\bigl({j(\rho_{u})}^{-1/2}\otimes 1\bigr),

where
\rho_{u}
is the unique
positive
operator in

\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(u,u^{cc})
with

\tr(\rho_{u})=\tr(\rho_{u}^{-1})
. Since

\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(u,u^{cc})=\mathbb{C}\rho_{u}
, it suffices to
show that
\rho_{u}
commutes with
V
.

Since
u
,
V
are covariant, we have

(13.3)

\forall r_{0}\in\Lambda_{0},\;\bigl(V(r_{0})\otimes 1\bigr)(r_{0}\cdot u)=u\bigl(V(r_{0})\otimes 1\bigr).

Taking the adjoint of both sides of (
13.3
) then applying

j\otimes\id
, we get

(13.4)

\forall r_{0}\in\Lambda_{0},\;\bigl(V^{c}(r_{0})\otimes 1\bigr)(r_{0}\cdot u^{c})=u^{c}\bigl(V^{c}(r_{0})\otimes 1\bigr),

where

(13.5)

is the contragredient of

, and

the contragredient of

. We pose

(13.7)

\overline{V}=\bigl({j(\rho_{u})}^{1/2}\otimes 1\bigr)V^{c}\bigl({j(\rho_{u})}^{-1/2}\otimes 1\bigr),

then by (

) and (

), we have

\forall r_{0}\in\Lambda_{0},\;\bigl(\overline{V}(r_{0})\otimes 1\bigr)(r_{0}\cdot\overline{u})=\overline{u}\bigl(\overline{V}(r_{0})\otimes 1\bigr).

Thus for any

,

, which is a one dimensional space spanned by a unitary

and
\overline{u}
are
irreducible unitary representations of

. Note that

This forces

to be unitary since it is a scalar
multiple of a unitary operator. Applying Lemma
13.1

to (

), we see that

V^{c}=\overline{V}=\bigl({j(\rho_{u})}^{1/2}\otimes 1\bigr)V^{c}\bigl({j(\rho_{u})}^{-1/2}\otimes 1\bigr).

Applying

i.e.
\rho_{u}^{1/2}
(hence
\rho_{u}

∎

Proposition 13.3
.

Let
(u,V,v)
be a representation parameter associated with some

\Lambda_{0}\in\giso(\Lambda)
,
U
is the unitary representation of

\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}
parameterized by
(u,V,v)
, then
the following hold:

(a)

(\overline{u},V^{c},v^{c})
is also
a representation parameter;

(b)

\rho_{U}=\id_{\mathscr{H}_{v}}\otimes\rho_{u}
, where

\rho_{U}
(resp.
\rho_{u}
) is the modular operator for the
representation
U
(resp.
u
);

(c)

\overline{U}
is parameterized by

(\overline{u},V^{c},v^{c})
.

Proof.

As we’ve seen in Proposition
13.2
and its proof, we
have
\overline{V}(r_{0})\in\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(r_{0}\cdot u,u)
for
all
r_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}
, thus
V^{c}=\overline{V}
is covariant
with
\overline{u}
. Since

(13.12)

\begin{split}\forall r_{0}\in\Lambda_{0},\qquad(v^{c}\times V^{c})(r_{0})&=j\bigl({[v(r_{0})]}^{-1}\otimes{[V(r_{0})]}^{-1}\bigr)\\
&=j\bigl(\{[{(v\times V)}](r_{0})\}^{-1}\bigr)\end{split}

v^{c}\times V^{c}
is the contragredient of the unitary representation

of
\Lambda_{0}

are unitary projective

.

To prove

, by the characterizing property of

, it suffices to show that the invertible positive operator

satisfies

(13.13)

\id\otimes\rho_{u}\in\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(U,U^{cc})

and (by Proposition
7.4

(13.14)

\begin{split}&\hskip 11.99998pt\tr((\cdot)(\id\otimes\rho_{u}))=\tr((\cdot)(\id\otimes\rho_{u}^{-1}))\\
&\in\selfmorph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}(U)=\selfmorph_{\mathbb{G}}(\id\otimes u)\cap\selfmorph_{\Lambda_{0}}(v\times V)\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{H}_{v})\otimes\mathbb{C}\id.\end{split}

Since
\tr(\rho_{u})=\tr(\rho_{u}^{-1})
, (
13.14
)
holds. We now prove (
13.13
). As is seen in the proof of
Proposition
13.2
, condition (
13.3
)
holds, and a similar calculation by applying

to the

\forall r_{0}\in\Lambda_{0},\;\bigl(V(r_{0})\otimes 1\bigr)(r_{0}\cdot u^{cc})=u^{cc}\bigl(V(r_{0})\otimes 1\bigr).

By definition, we have

(13.16)

\begin{split}U&={(\id\otimes u)}_{123}{(v\times V)}_{124}=(\id\otimes u\otimes 1)v_{14}V_{24}\\
&\in\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{H}_{v})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathscr{H}_{u})\otimes\pol(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\Lambda_{0}).\end{split}

Thus

(13.17)

U^{c}=(j\otimes j\otimes\id\otimes\id)(U^{-1})=(\id\otimes u^{c}\otimes 1)v^{c}_{14}V^{c}_{24},

and

(13.18)

U^{cc}=(\id\otimes u^{cc}\otimes 1)v^{cc}_{14}V^{cc}_{24}=(\id\otimes u^{cc}\otimes 1)v_{14}V_{24}.

By (
13.16
), (
13.18
) and
Proposition
13.2
, we have

(13.19)

\begin{split}&\hskip 11.99998pt(\id\otimes\rho_{u}\otimes 1\otimes 1)U=(\id\otimes\rho_{u}\otimes 1\otimes 1)(\id\otimes u\otimes 1)v_{14}V_{24}\\
&=(\id\otimes u^{cc}\otimes 1)v_{14}[(\id\otimes\rho_{u}\otimes 1\otimes 1)V_{24}]\\
&=(\id\otimes u^{cc}\otimes 1)v_{14}V_{24}(\id\otimes\rho_{u}\otimes 1\otimes 1)\\
&=U^{cc}(\id\otimes\rho_{u}\otimes 1\otimes 1).\end{split}

This proves (
13.13
) and finishes the proof of

(b)
.

By Proposition
7.7
and
(b)
,

\overline{U}

(13.20)

and

(13.21)

\begin{split}w^{\prime}&=(\id\otimes{j(\rho)}^{1/2}\otimes 1)(v^{c}_{13}V^{c}_{23})(\id\otimes{j(\rho)}^{-1/2}\otimes 1)\\

Thus the CSR

.
∎

We now turn our attention to the fusion rules of

.

Definition 14.1
.

For
i=1,2,3
, let
\Lambda_{i}\in\giso(\Lambda)
. Suppose

U_{i}
is a unitary representation of

\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{i}
, and
r_{i}\in\Lambda
, then the

incidence number
of
(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})
relative to

(U_{1},U_{2},U_{3})
, denoted by

m_{U_{1},U_{2},U_{3}}(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})
, is defined by

(14.1)

\begin{split}&\hskip 11.99998ptm_{U_{1},U_{2},U_{3}}(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})\\
&=\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}\left((r_{1}\cdot U_{1})|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}},(r_{2}\cdot U_{2})|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}\times(r_{3}\cdot U_{3})|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}\right),\end{split}

where
\Lambda_{0}=\cap_{i=1}^{3}r_{i}\Lambda_{i}r_{i}^{-1}
.

We now aim to express the incidence numbers in terms of characters. Let

\Theta,\Xi
be two subgroups of
\Lambda
with

\Theta\subseteq\Xi
. Recall that
C(\mathbb{G})=A
. Suppose

F=\sum_{r\in\Xi}a_{r}\otimes\delta_{r}
,
a_{r}\in A
is an
element of
C(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\Xi)=A\otimes C(\Xi)
. We use

to denote the element

\sum_{r\in\Theta}a_{r}\otimes\delta_{r}
in

\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Theta
, and call it the restriction of
F
to

\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Theta
. A simple calculation shows that this
restriction operation gives a surjective unital morphism of

C^{\ast}
-algebras from

C(\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Xi)=A\otimes C(\Xi)
to

C(\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Theta)=A\otimes C(\Theta)
that also preserves
comultiplication, thus allows us to view
\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Theta
as a
closed subgroup of
\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Xi
in the sense of
Definition
3.1
. Recall that we also have the extension
morphism
E_{\Lambda_{0}}\colon C(\Lambda_{0})\rightarrow C(\Lambda)
,

\delta_{r_{0}}\mapsto\delta_{r_{0}}
for every subgroup
\Lambda_{0}

of
\Lambda
, which simply sends each function in
C(\Lambda_{0})
to
its unique extension in
C(\Lambda)
that vanishes outside

\Lambda_{0}
. Finally, we use
h^{\Lambda_{0}}
to denote the Haar
state on
\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}
. For
i=1,2,3
, let

\Lambda_{i}\in\giso(\Lambda)
. Suppose
U_{i}
is a unitary
representation of
\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{i}
,
\chi_{i}
is the
character of

. Let

\Lambda_{0}=\cap_{i=1}^{3}r_{i}\Lambda_{i}r_{i}^{-1}
. Then we have the
following formula to calculate the incidence numbers in terms of characters.

(14.2)

\begin{split}\forall r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}\in\Lambda,\quad&\hskip 11.99998ptm_{U_{1},U_{2},U_{3}}(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})\\
&=h^{\Lambda_{0}}\left(\overline{(r_{1}\cdot\chi_{1})|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}}(r_{2}\cdot\chi_{2})|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}(r_{3}\cdot\chi_{3})|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}\right).\end{split}

Proposition 14.2
.

Using the above notations, the incidence number

m_{U_{1},U_{2},U_{3}}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
depends only on the classes

[U_{1}]
,
[U_{2}]
,
[U_{3}]
of equivalent unitary
representations and the left cosets
r_{1}\Lambda_{1}
,

r_{2}\Lambda_{2}
,
r_{3}\Lambda_{3}
.

Proof.

Note that for any

,

s_{i}\Lambda_{i}s_{i}^{-1}=r_{i}\Lambda_{i}r_{i}^{-1}
whenever

r_{i}^{-1}s_{i}\in\Lambda_{i}
. The proposition follows
from (
14.2
) and Lemma
5.3

(b)
.
∎

By Proposition
14.2
, we see immediately that the
following definition is well-defined.

Definition 14.3
.

For
i=1,2,3
, let
\Lambda_{i}\in\giso(\Lambda)
. Suppose

x_{i}
is a class of equivalent unitary representations of

\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{i}
, and

z_{i}\in\Lambda/\Lambda_{i}
is a left coset of
\Lambda_{i}
in

\Lambda
, then the
incidence number
of

(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})
relative to
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
, denoted
by
m_{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}}(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})
, is defined by

(14.3)

m_{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}}(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})=m_{U_{1},U_{2},U_{3}}(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})

where

,
r_{i}\in z_{i}
for

.

The rest of this section is devoted to the calculation of the incidence number
(
14.3
) in terms of more basic ingredients when

for some

\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\mathfrak{D}_{\Lambda_{i}}
(see
§
12
), as this will be the case we need in the
calculation of fusion rules for
\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
in
§
15
. We begin with a result on the structure of
unitary projective representations of some
\Lambda_{0}\in\giso(\Lambda)

that are covariant with some unitary representation of
\mathbb{G}
.

Lemma 14.4
.

Fix a
\Lambda_{0}\in\giso(\Lambda)
. Let
u_{0}
be an irreducible
unitary representation of
\mathbb{G}
,
[u_{0}]\in\irr(\mathbb{G})

the class of
u_{0}
, such that

\Lambda_{0}\subseteq\Lambda_{[u_{0}]}
. Suppose
u
is a unitary
representation of
\mathbb{G}
,

V\colon\Lambda_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathscr{H}_{u})
is a
unitary projective representation covariant with
u
,
p
is the
minimal central projection in
\selfmorph_{\mathbb{G}}(u)
corresponding
to the maximal pure subrepresentation of
u
supported by

[u_{0}]\in\irr(\mathbb{G})
. Let
q=1-p
, then
V
is
diagonalizable along
p
in the sense that

(14.4)

\begin{split}&(p\otimes 1)V=V(p\otimes 1),\quad(q\otimes 1)V=V(q\otimes 1),\\
&\text{and }\;(p\otimes 1)V(q\otimes 1)=(q\otimes 1)V(p\otimes 1)=0.\end{split}

Proof.

Since
V
and
u
are covariant, we have

(14.5)

\forall r_{0}\in\Lambda_{0},\;\bigl(V(r_{0})\otimes 1\bigr)(r_{0}\cdot u)=u\bigl(V(r_{0})\otimes 1\bigr).

Note that

p\in\selfmorph_{\mathbb{G}}(u)=\selfmorph_{\mathbb{G}}(r_{0}\cdot u)
(see (
8.1
)), then for every

r_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}
, it follows that

(14.6)

\begin{split}&\hskip 11.99998pt\bigl([pV(r_{0})q]\otimes 1\bigr)[(q\otimes 1)(r_{0}\cdot u)]=(p\otimes 1)\bigl(V(r_{0})\otimes 1\bigr)(q\otimes 1)(r_{0}\cdot u)\\
&=(p\otimes 1)\bigl(V(r_{0})\otimes 1\bigr)(r_{0}\cdot u)(q\otimes 1)=(p\otimes 1)u\bigl(V(r_{0})\otimes 1\bigr)(q\otimes 1)\\
&=[(p\otimes 1)u](p\otimes 1)\bigl(V(r_{0})\otimes 1\bigr)(q\otimes 1)=[(p\otimes 1)u]\bigl([pV(r_{0})q]\otimes 1\bigr).\end{split}

Let
u_{p}
(resp.
u_{q}
) be the subrepresentation of
u

corresponding to
p
(resp.
q
), then
r_{0}^{-1}\cdot u_{p}

is equivalent to
u_{p}
for all
r_{0}\in\Lambda_{0}
since

\Lambda_{0}\subseteq\Lambda_{[u]}
, and

(14.7)

\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(r_{0}\cdot u_{q},u_{p})=\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(u_{q},r_{0}^{-1}\cdot u_{p})=\morph_{\mathbb{G}}(u_{q},u_{p})=0.

By (
14.6
), the operator
pV(r_{0})q
, when viewed
as an operator from
p(\mathscr{H}_{u})
to
q(\mathscr{H}_{u})
,
intertwines
r_{0}\cdot u_{q}
and
u_{p}
. Thus by
(
14.7
),

(14.8)

\forall r_{0}\in\Lambda_{0},\quad pV(r_{0})q=0.

Similarly,

(14.9)

\forall r_{0}\in\Lambda_{0},\quad qV(r_{0})p=0.

Hence

(14.10)

pV(r_{0})=pV(r_{0})(p+q)=pV(r_{0})p=(p+q)V(r_{0})p=V(r_{0})p,

and similarly,

(14.11)

qV(r_{0})=qV(r_{0})(p+q)=qV(r_{0})q=(p+q)V(r_{0})q=V(r_{0})q.

Now (
14.4
) follows from equations
(
14.8
), (
14.9
),
(
14.10
), and (
14.11
).
∎

.

The following result describes the incidence numbers

m_{[U_{1}],[U_{2}],[{U_{3}}]}(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})
in terms of the
dimension of the intertwiner space of some projective representations of

\Lambda_{0}
.

Proposition 14.11
.

Suppose we are given the following data for each
i=1,2,3
:

—

a
\Lambda_{i}\in\giso(\Lambda)
, a left coset
z_{i}
in

\Lambda/\Lambda_{i}
and a
r_{i}\in z_{i}
;

—

a representation parameter
(u_{i},V_{i},v_{i})
associated with

\Lambda_{i}
;

—

the unitary representation
U_{i}
of

\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{i}
parameterized by

(u_{i},V_{i},v_{i})
.

Let

\Lambda_{0}=\cap_{i=1}^{3}r_{i}\Lambda_{i}r_{i}^{-1}=\cap_{i=1}^{3}z_{i}\Lambda_{i}z_{i}^{-1}
. Suppose

\bigl(r_{1}\cdot u_{1},(r_{1}\cdot V_{1})|_{\Lambda_{0}},(r_{2}\cdot v_{2})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times(r_{3}\cdot v_{3})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times V\bigr)

is the reduction of the GRP

\bigl((r_{2}\cdot u_{2})\times(r_{3}\cdot u_{3}),(r_{2}\cdot V_{2})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times(r_{3}\cdot V_{3})|_{\Lambda_{0}},(r_{2}\cdot v_{2})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times(r_{3}\cdot v_{3})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\bigr)

along

\bigl(r_{1}\cdot u_{1},(r_{1}\cdot V_{1})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\bigr)
. Then the unitary projective representations

(r_{1}\cdot v_{1})|_{\Lambda_{0}}
and

(r_{2}\cdot v_{2})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times(r_{3}\cdot v_{3})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times V

of
\Lambda_{0}
have the same cocycle, and

(14.13)

\begin{split}&\hskip 11.99998ptm_{[U_{1}],[U_{2}],[U_{3}]}(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})\\
&=\dim\morph_{\Lambda_{0}}\bigl((r_{1}\cdot v_{1})|_{\Lambda_{0}},(r_{2}\cdot v_{2})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times(r_{3}\cdot v_{3})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times V\bigr).\end{split}

Proof.

It is easy to check that

\bigl((r_{2}\cdot u_{2})\times(r_{3}\cdot u_{3}),(r_{2}\cdot V_{2})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times(r_{3}\cdot V_{3})|_{\Lambda_{0}},(r_{2}\cdot v_{2})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times(r_{3}\cdot v_{3})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\bigr)
is indeed a generalized representation
parameter. Take the minimal central projection
p
of

\selfmorph_{\mathbb{G}}\bigl((r_{2}\cdot u_{2})\times(r_{3}\cdot u_{3})\bigr)
corresponding to the maximal pure subrepresentation

u_{p}
of
(r_{2}\cdot u_{2})\times(r_{3}\cdot u_{3})
that is
supported by
[r_{1}\cdot u_{1}]\in\irr(\mathbb{G})
. Suppose

q=1-p
. By Lemma
14.4
,
q
also
corresponds to a subrepresentation
u_{q}
of

(r_{2}\cdot u_{2})\times(r_{3}\cdot u_{3})
on

q(\mathscr{H}_{u_{2}}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{u_{3}})
. Similarly, let

V_{p}
(resp.
V_{q}
) be the subrepresentation of the unitary
projective representation

(r_{2}\cdot v_{2})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times(r_{3}\cdot v_{3})|_{\Lambda_{0}}
on

p(\mathscr{H}_{u_{2}}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{u_{3}})
(resp.
q(\mathscr{H}_{u_{2}}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{3})
). Let
U_{p}
(resp.
U_{q}
) be the representation of
\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}

parameterized by the GRP

(u_{p},V_{p},(r_{2}\cdot v_{2})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times(r_{3}\cdot v_{3})|_{\Lambda_{0}})
(resp.
(u_{q},V_{q},(r_{2}\cdot v_{2})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times(r_{3}\cdot v_{3})|_{\Lambda_{0}})
). By construction, the unitary representation

U
of
\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}
parameterized by

\bigl((r_{2}\cdot u_{2})\times(r_{3}\cdot u_{3}),(r_{2}\cdot V_{2})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times(r_{3}\cdot V_{3})|_{\Lambda_{0}},(r_{2}\cdot v_{2})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times(r_{3}\cdot v_{3})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\bigr)
is the direct sum of
U_{p}
and
U_{q}
. By definition,

(14.14)

\begin{split}m_{[U_{1}],[U_{2}],[U_{3}]}(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})&=\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}(U_{1},U)\\
&=\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}(U_{1},U_{p})+\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}(U_{1},U_{q}).\end{split}

From our construction, the matrix coefficients of
u_{p}
and
u_{q}

are orthogonal with respect to the Haar state
h
of
\mathbb{G}
.
Thus the proof of Proposition
9.11

(a)
applies almost verbatim, and shows that

(14.15)

\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}(U_{1},U_{q})=0.

On the other hand, the cocycles of both

(r_{1}\cdot v_{1})|_{\Lambda_{0}}
and

(r_{2}\cdot v_{2})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times(r_{3}\cdot v_{3})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times V
are both opposite to that of

(r_{1}\cdot V_{1})|_{\Lambda_{0}}
by the reduction process
described above, hence these cocycles coincide. By
Proposition
9.11

(b)
and
Proposition
14.8

(c)
, we have

(14.16)

\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0}}(U_{1},U_{p})=\dim\morph_{\Lambda_{0}}\bigl((r_{1}\cdot v_{1})|_{\Lambda_{0}},(r_{2}\cdot v_{2})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times(r_{3}\cdot v_{3})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times V\bigr).

Now (
14.13
) follows from (
14.15
) and
(
14.16
).
∎

15.
Fusion rules

We now calculate the fusion rules of
\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
. From the
classification theorem (Theorem
12.1
), up to unitary
equivalence, all unitary irreducible representations of

\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
are distinguished. Thus the task falls to the
calculation of

(15.1)

\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}}\bigl(\indrep(U_{1}),\indrep(U_{2})\times\indrep(U_{3})\bigr),

where, for
i=1,2,3
,
U_{i}
is the irreducible unitary
representation of
\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{i}
parameterized (see
Definition
10.1
and
Definition
10.4
) by some
distinguished

representation parameter
(u_{i},V_{i},v_{i})
associated with

\Lambda_{i}
(recall that
\Lambda_{i}=\Lambda_{[u_{i}]}
since

(u_{i},V_{i},v_{i})
is distinguished). Let
h
be the Haar state on

C(\mathbb{G})=A
. For any subgroup
\Lambda_{0}
of
\Lambda
, we
use
h^{\Lambda_{0}}
to denote the Haar state on

C(\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda_{0})=A\otimes C(\Lambda_{0})
, and

E_{\Lambda_{0}}\colon C(\Lambda_{0})\rightarrow C(\Lambda)
denotes the
linear embedding such that

\delta_{r_{0}}\in C(\Lambda_{0})\mapsto\delta_{r_{0}}\in C(\Lambda)
(the extension of functions in
C(\Lambda_{0})
to functions in

C(\Lambda)
that vanishes outside
\Lambda_{0}
). In particular,

h^{\Lambda}
is the Haar state on

C(\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda)=A\otimes C(\Lambda)
. For

i=1,2,3
, let

\chi_{i}=(\tr\otimes\id)(U_{i})\in A\otimes C(\Lambda_{i})
be the
character of
U_{i}
, and
r\cdot\chi_{i}
is defined to be the
character of the representation
r\cdot U_{i}
of

\mathbb{G}\rtimes r\Lambda_{i}r^{-1}
.

Using these notations, by Proposition
5.1
, we have the
following formula for the character of
\indrep(U_{i})
,

(15.2)

\forall i=1,2,3,\quad\chi(\indrep(U_{i}))=\left\lvert\Lambda_{i}\right\rvert^{-1}\sum_{r_{i}\in\Lambda}(\id\otimes E_{r_{i}\Lambda_{i}r_{i}^{-1}})(r_{i}\cdot\chi_{i}).

Thus

(15.3)

\begin{split}&\hskip 11.99998pt\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda}\bigl(\indrep(U_{1}),\indrep(U_{2})\times\indrep(U_{3})\bigr)\\
&=h^{\Lambda}\bigl(\overline{\chi(\indrep(U_{1}))}[\chi(\indrep(U_{2}))][\chi(\indrep(U_{3}))]\bigr)\\
&=\sum_{r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}}{h^{\Lambda}\bigl(\chi(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})\bigr)\over\left\lvert\Lambda_{1}\right\rvert\cdot\left\lvert\Lambda_{2}\right\rvert\cdot\left\lvert\Lambda_{3}\right\rvert},\end{split}

where

(15.4)

\begin{split}&\hskip 11.99998pt\chi(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})\\
&=\overline{(\id\otimes E_{r_{1}\Lambda_{1}r_{1}^{-1}})(r_{1}\cdot\chi_{1})}[(\id\otimes E_{r_{2}\Lambda_{2}r_{2}^{-1}})(r_{2}\cdot\chi_{2})][(\id\otimes E_{r_{3}\Lambda_{3}r_{3}^{-1}})(r_{3}\cdot\chi_{3})].\end{split}

If
\Theta,\Xi
are subgroups of
\Lambda
with

\Theta\subseteq\Xi
, and
\sum_{r\in\Xi}a_{r}\otimes\delta_{r}

is an arbitrary element of
A\otimes C(\Xi)
with all
a_{r}\in A
, we
call the element
\sum_{r\in\Theta}a_{r}\otimes\delta_{r}
of

A\otimes C(\Theta)
the restriction of

\sum_{r\in\Xi}a_{r}\otimes\delta_{r}
and denote it by

{\left(\sum_{r\in\Xi}a_{r}\otimes\delta_{r}\right)}|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Theta}
. Recall that

(15.5)

h^{\Lambda_{0}}=[\Lambda\colon\Lambda_{0}]\cdot h^{\Lambda}\circ(\id\otimes E_{\Lambda_{0}})

for any subgroup
\Lambda_{0}
of
\Lambda
, posing

(15.6)

\Lambda(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})=\bigcap_{i=1}^{3}r_{i}\Lambda_{i}r_{i}^{-1},

we have

(15.7)

\begin{split}&\hskip 11.99998pth^{\Lambda}\bigl(\chi(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})\bigr)=h^{\Lambda}\left(\chi(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})}\right)\\
&={h^{\Lambda(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})}\left(\overline{(r_{1}\cdot\chi_{1})|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})}}(r_{2}\cdot\chi_{2})|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})}(r_{3}\cdot\chi_{3})|_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})}\right)\over[\Lambda\colon\Lambda(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})]}\\
&={[\Lambda\colon\Lambda(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})]}^{-1}m_{U_{1},U_{2},U_{3}}(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}),\end{split}

where
m_{U_{1},U_{2},U_{3}}(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})
is the incidence number
of
(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})
relative to
(U_{1},U_{2},U_{3})
.

By (
15.3
) and (
15.7
), we have

(15.8)

\begin{split}&\hskip 11.99998pt\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda}\bigl(\indrep(U_{1}),\indrep(U_{2})\times\indrep(U_{3})\bigr)\\
&=\sum_{r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}\in\Lambda}\frac{m_{U_{1},U_{2},U_{3}}(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})}{\left\lvert\Lambda_{1}\right\rvert\cdot\left\lvert\Lambda_{2}\right\rvert\cdot\left\lvert\Lambda_{3}\right\rvert\cdot[\Lambda\colon\Lambda(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})]}.\end{split}

As we’ve seen in Definition
14.3
and the discussion
before it, we have

(15.9)

\begin{split}&(\forall i=1,2,3,r_{i}\in z_{i}\in\Lambda/\Lambda_{i})\\
&\implies m_{[U_{1}],[U_{2}],[U_{3}]}(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})=m_{U_{1},U_{2},U_{3}}(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}),\end{split}

where

\Lambda(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}):=\cap_{i=1}^{3}r_{i}\Lambda_{i}r_{i}^{-1}

does not depend on the choices for
r_{i}\in z_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
.
Thus (
15.8
) can be written more succinctly as

(15.10)

\begin{split}&\hskip 11.99998pt\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda}\bigl(\indrep(U_{1}),\indrep(U_{2})\times\indrep(U_{3})\bigr)\\
&=\sum_{z_{1}\in\Lambda/\Lambda_{1}}\sum_{z_{2}\in\Lambda/\Lambda_{2}}\sum_{z_{3}\in\Lambda/\Lambda_{3}}\frac{m_{[U_{1}],[U_{2}],[U_{3}]}(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})}{[\Lambda\colon\Lambda(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})]}.\end{split}

We formalize the above calculation as the following theorem, which describes the
fusion rules of
\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
in terms of the more basic
ingredients of incidence numbers, which in turn is completely determined by the
representation theory of
\mathbb{G}
, the action of
\Lambda
on

\irr(\mathbb{G})
, and various unitary projective representations of some
naturally appeared subgroups in
\giso(\Lambda)
.

Theorem 15.1
.

The fusion rules for
\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
is given as the
following. For
i=1,2,3
, let
W_{i}
be an irreducible
representation of
\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda
. Suppose
U_{i}
is the
distinguished representation parameterized by some distinguished
representation parameter
(u_{i},V_{i},v_{i})
associated with some
isotropy subgroup
\Lambda_{i}
of
\Lambda
, such that
W_{i}
is
equivalent to
\indrep(U_{i})
, then

(15.11)

\begin{split}&\hskip 11.99998pt\dim\morph_{\mathbb{G}\rtimes\Lambda}(W_{1},W_{2}\times W_{3})\\
&=\sum_{z_{1}\in\Lambda/\Lambda_{1}}\sum_{z_{2}\in\Lambda/\Lambda_{2}}\sum_{z_{3}\in\Lambda/\Lambda_{3}}\frac{m_{[U_{1}],[U_{2}],[U_{3}]}(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})}{[\Lambda\colon\Lambda(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})]}.\end{split}

Here the incidence numbers

(15.12)

\begin{split}&\hskip 11.99998ptm_{[U_{1}],[U_{2}],[U_{3}]}(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})=m_{U_{1},U_{2},U_{3}}(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})\\
&=\dim\morph_{\Lambda(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})}\bigl((r_{1}\cdot v_{1})|_{\Lambda_{0}},(r_{2}\cdot v_{2})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times(r_{3}\cdot v_{3})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times V\bigr),\end{split}

where
r_{i}\in z_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
, and the unitary projective
representation
V
of
\Lambda(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})
is taken from the
reduction

\bigl(r_{1}\cdot u_{1},(r_{1}\cdot V_{1})|_{\Lambda_{0}},(r_{2}\cdot v_{2})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times(r_{3}\cdot v_{3})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times V\bigr)

of the generalized representation parameter

\bigl((r_{2}\cdot u_{2})\times(r_{3}\cdot u_{3}),(r_{2}\cdot V_{2})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times(r_{3}\cdot V_{3})|_{\Lambda_{0}},(r_{2}\cdot v_{2})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\times(r_{3}\cdot v_{3})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\bigr)

along
\bigl(r_{1}\cdot u_{1},(r_{1}\cdot V_{1})|_{\Lambda_{0}}\bigr)
.

Proof.

The above calculation proves (
15.11
), and
(
15.12
) follows from
Proposition
14.11
.
∎

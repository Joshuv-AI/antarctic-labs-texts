Corrigendum to ”On the sum of the first two largest signless Laplacian eigenvalues of a graph” [Discrete Math. 347 (2024) 114035] DOI of original article: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2024.114035 Corresponding author: Chang-Xiang He. Corresponding author Email: cxhe@usst.edu.cn (C.-X. He).
Zhi-Bin Du, Zi-Ming Zhou, Hai-Ying Shan, Chang-Xiang He

Abstract




The purpose of this corrigendum is to rectify an error in our recent publication
[
1
]
, in which we wrongly claimed that
f(G_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}})>f(G_{t_{1},t_{2}+1,t_{3}-1})
with
t_{2}\geq t_{3}
aiming to
G_{1}\cong G_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}
, as well as
G_{11}
and
G_{14}
. This error is attributed to the fact that

P_{1}-P_{2}=P(\Delta_{2}(Q^{\pi}(G_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}})),x+e(G_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}})+3)-P(\Delta_{2}(Q^{\pi}(G_{t_{1},t_{2}+1,t_{3}-1})),x+e(G_{t_{1},t_{2}+1,t_{3}-1})+3)

is not always positive. In this corrigendum, we will give supplements and corrections to our previous proof. The notations not defined here can refer to
[
1
]
.

We have proved in
[
1
]
that

\frac{1.3}{n}<f(K^{+}_{1,n-1})<\frac{1.5}{n}
, and
f(G)>\frac{1.5}{n}
when
G\not\cong G_{1},G_{11},G_{14}
. In the following, we will show that
f(G)>\frac{1.5}{n}
for
G\cong G_{1}
with
(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})\neq(1,n-3,0)
, or
G\cong G_{11},G_{14}
. Then it results in
f(G)\geq f(K^{+}_{1,n-1})
with equality if and only if
G\cong K^{+}_{1,n-1}
, completing the proof of
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
. It is worth mentioning that
K^{+}_{1,n-1}
is the same as
G_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}
with
(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})=(1,n-3,0)
.

K^{+}_{1,n-1}

Case 1
.

G\cong G_{1}
with
(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})\neq(1,n-3,0)
.

It is easy to get that the characteristic polynomial of
Q(G_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}})
is equal to

\displaystyle P_{Q}(G_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}},x)=P(Q^{\pi}(G_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}),x)(x-2)^{t_{1}-1}(x-1)^{t_{2}+t_{3}-2}.

On one hand, since
q_{2}>2
by Lemma 2.9,
P(Q^{\pi}(G_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}),x)
should contain the largest two roots of
P_{Q}(G_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}},x)
. On the other hand, one can see that all the coefficients of

P\left(\Delta_{2}(Q^{\pi}(G_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}})),x+e(G)+3-\frac{1.5}{n}\right)

on
x
are positive (since the denominator is positive, we need only to consider the numerator after the reduction of fractions to a common denominator, the details are deferred in
[
2
]
),
which infers that the largest eigenvalue of
\Delta_{2}(Q^{\pi}(G_{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}))
is less than
e(G)+3-\frac{1.5}{n}
.
Now by Corollary 2.1, we have
f(G)>\frac{1.5}{n}
.

Case 2
.

G\cong G_{11}
or
G_{14}
.

We have checked
f(G)>\frac{1.5}{n}
holds for all the graphs with at most
11
vertices by computational checking. Assuming the graph under consideration has at least
12
vertices next.
Clearly,
d(a_{3})+d(b_{3})=6
.

According to the symmetry of the structure of
G_{11}
(
G_{14}
, respectively),
without loss of generality,
if
t_{1}+t_{2}>2
, then
G_{11}
(
G_{14}
, respectively) contains two disjoint stars
K_{1,5}
with centers
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
.
Further, by Lemma 2.9,
q_{2}(G-e_{3})\geq 6
(
e_{3}=a_{3}b_{3}
).
Using Lemmas 3.3 and 2.10, we find that

S_{2}(G)<S_{2}(G-e_{3})+1<\left(e(G-e_{3})+3-\frac{1.5}{n}\right)+1=e(G)+3-\frac{1.5}{n},

equivalently
f(G)>\frac{1.5}{n}
.

Since
|N(M)|=2
,
t_{1}+t_{2}>1
, we are remaining to consider
t_{1}+t_{2}=2
, that is to say,
(t_{1},t_{2})=(0,2),(1,1)
or
(2,0)
. For each of such cases, using the
2^{nd}
additive compound matrix approach, we have
f(G)>0.4>\frac{1.5}{n}
, the details can refer to
[
1
]
.

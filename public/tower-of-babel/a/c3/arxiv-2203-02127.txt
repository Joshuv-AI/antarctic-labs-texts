A hierarchy of efficient bounds on quantum capacities exploiting symmetry
Omar Fawzi, Ala Shayeghi, Hoang Ta

Abstract
Optimal rates for achieving an information processing task are often characterized in terms of regularized information measures. In many cases of quantum tasks, we do not know how to compute such quantities. Here, we exploit the symmetries in the recently introduced D # \mathrm{D}^{\#} in order to obtain a hierarchy of semidefinite programming bounds on various regularized quantities. As applications, we give a general procedure to give efficient bounds on the regularized Umegaki channel divergence as well as the classical capacity and two-way assisted quantum capacity of quantum channels. In particular, we obtain slight improvements for the capacity of the amplitude damping channel. We also prove that for fixed input and output dimensions, the regularized sandwiched Rényi divergence between any two quantum channels can be approximated up to an ϵ \epsilon accuracy in time that is polynomial in 1 / ϵ 1/\epsilon .

Introduction

The optimal rates for many quantum information processing tasks of interest can be characterized in terms of a regularized divergence between quantum channels. For a divergence
\mathbf{D}
defined on quantum states, the corresponding channel divergence is defined by maximizing the divergence between the channel outputs over the set of possible inputs. There are two natural variants: for quantum channels
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}
the non-stabilized divergence is given by only allowing input states
\rho
in the input space of
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{D}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})=\sup_{\rho}\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{M}(\rho))\ ,

whereas the stabilized version allows arbitrary input states

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})=\sup_{\rho}\mathbf{D}((\mathcal{I}\otimes\mathcal{N})(\rho)\|(\mathcal{I}\otimes\mathcal{M})(\rho))\ ,

where
\mathcal{I}
is the identity channel. The most well-known example illustrating these two variants is when
\mathbf{D}
is the trace distance, then
\overline{\mathbf{D}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
is the superoperator trace norm and
\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
is the diamond norm, and it is known that we can have
\overline{\mathbf{D}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\ll\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})

[
KSV02
]
.

When analyzing tasks in the independent and identically distributed limit, an important divergence
\mathbf{D}
is the Umegaki divergence
\mathrm{D}
defined by
\mathrm{D}(\rho\|\sigma)=\mathrm{tr}\,(\rho\log\rho)-\mathrm{tr}\,(\rho\log\sigma)
. For example, in asymmetric hypothesis testing between channels
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathcal{M}
, the Stein exponent is characterized using
\mathrm{D}
, namely in terms of the
regularized
channel divergence
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M}):=\sup_{n}\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n})

[
WBHK20
,
WW19
,
FFRS20
]
. In fact, it turns out that the channel divergence
\mathrm{D}
is in general non-additive
[
FFRS20
]
, in which case we have
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})>\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
.

†
†

Another example is the Holevo information of a quantum channel
\mathcal{N}
, which is given by

\chi(\mathcal{N})=\min_{\sigma}\overline{\mathrm{D}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{T}_{\sigma})

where
\mathcal{T}_{\sigma}
is the replacer channel that outputs
\sigma
for any input density operator
[
OPW97
]
.
The Holevo-Schumacher-Westmorland theorem (see e.g.,
[
Wil13
]
) states that the classical capacity of
\mathcal{N}
is given by
\chi^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{N}):=\sup_{n}\frac{1}{n}\chi(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n})
and the regularization is needed for some channels, as shown by
[
Has09
]
.

The objective of this paper is to provide efficient ways of computing, or more specifically upper bounding, such regularized channel divergences. In order to achieve this, we use the recently introduced
\mathrm{D}^{\#}
Rényi divergences
[
FF21b
]
which were shown to give a converging hierarchy of upper bounds on regularized channel divergences. We exploit the symmetries of the resulting hierarchy of optimization programs to obtain a concise representation and solve it efficiently. Specifically, for quantum channels
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}
, we show in Theorem
4.3
that the permutation symmetry of the optimization program defining
\mathrm{D}^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes k})
can be used to transform it into a semidefinite program with
\mathrm{poly}(k)
variables and constraints compared to the straightforward representation which is of size exponential in
k
. However, as we will see, a direct implementation of this transformation would require an exponential time computation. In Theorem
4.6
, we provide an algorithm which performs this transformation in
\mathrm{poly}(k)
time, for fixed input and output dimensions. As a first application, we consider the task of approximating the regularized sandwiched Rényi divergence between two channels. Note that the sandwiched Rényi divergence (see Section
2
for the definition) is in general non-additive
[
FFRS20
]
, and it is not known whether its regularization is efficiently computable. Ref.
[
FF21a
]
shows that the regularized quantity can be approximated up to arbitrary accuracy by
\frac{1}{k}\mathrm{D}^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes k})
, for sufficiently large
k
. Our results imply that the regularized sandwiched Rényi divergence between two channels can be approximated up to an accuracy
\epsilon\in(0,1]
, in time that is polynomial in
1/\epsilon
(for fixed input/output dimensions).
Furthermore, when the channels admit additional group symmetries, we present a general approach to combine these symmetries with the intrinsic permutation invariance to further simplify the problem. As an example demonstrating the potential of this approach, in Section
4.2
, we apply our method to generalized amplitude damping channels and we show how a very simple symmetry of these channels leads to considerable reductions in the size of the convex optimization program for computing the channel divergence (see Table
2
).

In Section
5
, we present a procedure for efficiently computing improved strong converse bounds on the classical capacity of quantum channels by considering the generalized Upsilon-information
[
WFT19
]
induced by the
\mathrm{D}^{\#}
Rényi divergences. To illustrate our method, we apply it to the amplitude damping channel (see Table
3
for a comparison with the best previously known bounds). Even though the improvements we obtain for the classical capacity are very small for this channel, the amplitude damping channel
\mathcal{A}_{p}
is one of the current challenges as far as the classical capacity is concerned. In particular, it remains open whether
\chi^{\textrm{reg}}(\mathcal{A}_{p})=\chi(\mathcal{A}_{p})
.
Finally, in Section
6
, we use our method for computing improved upper bounds on the two-way assisted quantum capacity of channels by considering the generalized Theta-information
[
FF21a
]
induced by the
\mathrm{D}^{\#}
divergences and apply it to the amplitude damping channel, as an example (see Figure
2
for a comparison with the best previously known bounds).

Quantum divergences

A functional
\mathbf{D}:\mathscr{D}(\mathcal{H})\times\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathbb{R}
is a
generalized quantum divergence

[
PV10
,
SW13
]
if it satisfies the data-processing inequality

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{N}(\sigma))\leq\mathbf{D}(\rho\|\sigma).

Let
\rho\in\mathscr{D}(\mathcal{H})
and
\sigma\in\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{H})
such that
\rho\ll\sigma
. The
sandwiched Rényi divergence

[
MDS+13
]
,
[
WWY14
]
of order

is defined as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{D}}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma):=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\mathrm{tr}\,\left[\left(\sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\rho\sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\right)^{\alpha}\right].

The

[
PR98
,
Mat15
,
Tom15
,
HM17
,
FF21a
]
of order
\alpha
is defined as

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{D}}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma):=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\mathrm{tr}\,\left[\sigma^{1/2}\left(\sigma^{-1/2}\rho\sigma^{-1/2}\right)^{\alpha}\sigma^{1/2}\right].

The
max divergence
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma):=\log\inf\{\lambda>0:\rho\leq\lambda\sigma\}.

The inverses in these formulations are generalized inverses, i.e., the inverse on the support. When
\rho\ll\sigma
does not hold, these quantities are set to
\infty
. Recently, in
[
FF21b
]
, the authors introduced an interesting quantum Rényi divergence called
\#
-Rényi divergence
. To define this divergence, we recall the geometric mean of two positive definite matrices.

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the
\alpha
-geometric mean of two positive definite matrices
\rho
and
\sigma
is defined as

\displaystyle\rho\#_{\alpha}\sigma=\rho^{1/2}(\rho^{-1/2}\sigma\rho^{-1/2})^{\alpha}\rho^{1/2}.

The
\alpha
-geometric mean has the following properties (see Refs.
[
KA80
]
and
[
FF21b
]

1.

Monotonicity:
A\leq C
and
B\leq D
implies
A\#_{\alpha}B\leq C\#_{\alpha}D
.

2.

Transformer inequality:
M(A\#_{\alpha}B)M^{*}\leq(MAM^{*})\#_{\alpha}(MBM^{*})
, with equality if
M
is invertible.

3.

(aA)\#_{\alpha}(bB)=a(b/a)^{\alpha}(A\#_{\alpha})B
, for any
a>0
and
b\geq 0
.

4.

Joint-concavity/sub-additivity: for any
A_{i},B_{i}\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i}A_{i}\#_{\alpha}B_{i}\leq\left(\sum_{i}A_{i}\right)\#_{\alpha}\left(\sum_{i}B_{i}\right)\kern 5.0pt.

5.

Direct sum: for any
A_{1},A_{2},B_{1},B_{2}\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle(A_{1}\oplus A_{2})\#_{\alpha}(B_{1}\oplus B_{2})=(A_{1}\#_{\alpha}B_{1})\oplus(A_{2}\#_{\alpha}B_{2})\kern 5.0pt,

where
A_{1}\oplus A_{2}
form a block diagonal matrix
\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}A_{1}&0\\
0&A_{2}\end{array}\right].

The
\#
-Rényi divergence

[
FF21b
]
of order
\alpha
between two positive semidefinite operators is defined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\mathrm{Q}^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Q}^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)

\displaystyle:=\min_{A\geq 0}\mathrm{tr}\,(A)\,\,\text{ s.t. }\,\,\rho\leq\sigma\#_{\frac{1}{\alpha}}A.

We note that the above convex program may be expressed as a semidefinite program when
\alpha
is a rational number
[
FS17
,
Sag13
]

Proposition 2.1

For any

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq\widetilde{\mathrm{D}}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq\mathrm{D}^{\#}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq\widehat{\mathrm{D}}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq\mathrm{D}_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma).

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\mathrm{Q}^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M}),

3.1
Matrix
*
-algebra background

A subset
\mathcal{A}
of the set of all
n\times n
complex matrices is said to be a
matrix
*
-algebra
over
\mathbb{C}
, if it contains the identity operator and is closed under addition, scalar multiplication, matrix multiplication, and taking the conjugate transpose. For our applications, the structure in the optimization programs we consider will allow us to assume that the variables live in such an algebra. A map
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
between two matrix
*
-algebras
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
is called a
*
-isomorphism if

•

\varphi
is a linear bijection,

•

\varphi(AB)=\varphi(A)\varphi(B)
for all
A,B\in\mathcal{A}
,

•

\varphi(A^{*})=\varphi(A)^{*}
for all
A\in\mathcal{A}
.

The matrix algebras
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are called
isomorphic
and we write
\mathcal{A}\cong\mathcal{B}
. Note that, by the second property above,
*
-isomorphisms preserve positive semidefiniteness. From a standard result in the theory of matrix
*
-algebra, we get the following structure theorem.

Theorem 3.1

(Theorem 1,
[
Gij05
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be a matrix
*
-algebra. There are numbers
t
,
m_{1},\ldots,m_{t}
such that there is a
*
-isomorphism
\phi
between
\mathcal{A}
and a direct sum of complete matrix algebras

\displaystyle\phi\;:\;\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}\mathbb{C}^{m_{i}\times m_{i}}\kern 5.0pt.

(3)

In other words, under the mapping
\phi
, all the elements of
\mathcal{A}
have a common block-diagonal structure. Moreover, this is the finest such decomposition for a generic element of
\mathcal{A}
. We remark that the
*
-isomorphism
\phi
can be computed in polynomial time in the dimension of the matrix
*
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
(see e.g., Theorem
2.7
in Ref.
[
LM11
]
and the following discussion, or Ref.
[
Gij05
]
).

(

]

Let

\displaystyle\psi:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}\;,\;\psi(C_{r})=L(C_{r})\;,\;r\in[m]\kern 5.0pt,

is a

-isomorphism.

Note that under the

4
Efficient approximation of the regularized divergence of channels

For
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
, the regularized sandwiched

-Rényi divergence between channels
\mathcal{N}_{X\to Y}
and
\mathcal{M}_{X\to Y}
is defined as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{D}}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M}):=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\widetilde{\mathrm{D}}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes k})\ .

(10)

The regularized sandwiched Rényi divergence between channels can be used to obtain improved characterization of many information processing tasks such as channel discrimination
[
FF21a
,
FF21b
]
. However, the sandwiched Rényi divergence between channels is non-additive in general
[
FFRS20
]
and it is unclear whether its regularization can be computed efficiently. Ref.
[
FF21b
]
provides a converging hierarchy of upper bounds on the regularized divergence between channels:

Theorem 4.1

(
[
FF21b
]
)

Let
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
and
\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}
be completely positive maps from

to

. Then for any

,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\mathrm{D}^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes k})-\frac{1}{k}\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}(d^{2}+d)\log(k+d)\leq\>\widetilde{\mathrm{D}}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})\>\leq\frac{1}{k}\mathrm{D}^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes k})\ ,

where
d=\dim X\dim Y
.

We note that
\frac{1}{k}\mathrm{D}^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes k})
is decreasing in
k
(since the
\mathrm{D}^{\#}
channel divergence is subadditive). Moreover,
\mathrm{D}^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes k})
can be written in terms of a convex program as (
[

]

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\min_{A_{X^{\otimes k}Y^{\otimes k}}\geq 0}\|\mathrm{tr}\,_{Y^{\otimes k}}(A_{X^{\otimes k}Y^{\otimes k}})\|_{\infty}\quad\textup{ s.t. }\quad(J^{\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k}})\leq(J^{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes k}})\#_{1/\alpha}A_{X^{\otimes k}Y^{\otimes k}}.

(11)

Therefore, Theorem

establishes that

can be approximated by

with arbitrary accuracy for sufficiently large

in finite time. Namely, if we take

then we have

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\mathrm{D}}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})-\frac{1}{k}\mathrm{D}^{\#}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes k})|\leq\epsilon\,.

However, the size of Program (

) grows exponentially with

.

4.1
Exploiting symmetries to simplify the problem

In this section, we will show how the symmetries of Program (
11
) can be used to simplify this optimization problem and solve it in time polynomial in
k
. We first focus on the natural symmetries arising due to invariance under permutation of physical systems. In Section
4.2
, we show how additional symmetries can be utilized to further simplify the problem. Our approach can be summarized as follows: First, we show that program (
11
) is invariant with respect to the action of the symmetric group. Using this observation, we show that the program can be transformed into an equivalent program with polynomially many constraints, each of polynomial size in
k
. In order to show this, we use the block-diagonal decomposition given by Lemma
3.3
. A naive implementation of this transformation, however, involves exponential time computations. We show that the simplified form of the program can be directly computed in
\mathrm{poly}(k)

Recall that, for every

\displaystyle\pi\cdot\left(h_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes h_{k}\right)=h_{\pi^{-1}(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes h_{\pi^{-1}(k)}\quad,\quad h_{i}\in\mathcal{H}\,,\,\forall i\in[k]\kern 5.0pt.

Let

and

on

and

The following lemma shows that the feasible region of the convex program (

For a linear operator

\displaystyle\overline{X}\coloneqq\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{S}_{k}|}\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}}P_{\mathcal{H}}(\pi)XP_{\mathcal{H}}(\pi)^{*}\kern 5.0pt.

.

The convex program of Eq. (

Proof.

It is straightforward to check that by Slater’s condition the optimal value is achieved by a feasible solution. We will prove that for every feasible solution
A
, the corresponding group-average operator
\overline{A}
is a feasible solution with an objective value not greater than the original value.

To simplify the notation, let
\Pi(\pi)\coloneqq P_{X\otimes Y}(\pi)

\displaystyle\overline{A}\;\#_{1/\alpha}\;J^{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes k}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{S}_{k}|}\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}}\Pi(\pi)A\Pi(\pi)^{*}\right)\#_{1/\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{S}_{k}|}\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}}\Pi(\pi)J^{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes k}}\Pi(\pi)^{*}\right)

(12)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}}\left(\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{S}_{k}|}\Pi(\pi)A\Pi(\pi)^{*}\right)\#_{1/\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{S}_{k}|}\Pi(\pi)J^{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes k}}\Pi(\pi)^{*}\right)

(13)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{S}_{k}|}\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}}\Pi(\pi)\left(A\#_{1/\alpha}J^{\mathcal{M}^{\otimes k}}\right)\Pi(\pi)^{*}

(14)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{S}_{k}|}\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}}\Pi(\pi)\left(J^{\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k}}\right)\Pi(\pi)^{*}

(15)

\displaystyle=J^{\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k}}\kern 5.0pt,

(16)

For the objective function, note that since

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{tr}\,_{Y^{\otimes k}}\left(\overline{A}\right)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathrm{tr}\,_{Y^{\otimes k}}\left(\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{S}_{k}|}\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}}\Pi(\pi)A\Pi(\pi)^{T}\right)\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle(E^{(i,j)})_{a,b}\coloneqq\left|\{v\in[k]:i_{v}=a,j_{v}=b\}\right|\;,\;\forall a,b\in[d_{\mathcal{H}}]\,.

(25)

By the construction in Eq. (

), for two pairs
(i,j),(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in[d_{\mathcal{H}}]^{k}\times[d_{\mathcal{H}}]^{k}
, we have
(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})=(\pi(i),\pi(j))
, for some
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}
if and only if

. Therefore, there is a one-to-one correspondence between the orbits

and

O_{r}^{\mathcal{H}}
in
\mathrm{poly}(k)
time by listing all non-negative integer solutions of the equation
\sum_{a,b\in[d_{\mathcal{H}}]}E_{a,b}=k
.

Any matrix in
\mathrm{End}^{\mathfrak{S}_{k}}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k})

\displaystyle M(z)\coloneqq\sum_{r=1}^{m^{\mathcal{H}}}z_{r}C_{r}^{\mathcal{H}}\,,\text{ for some }z\in\mathbb{C}^{[m^{\mathcal{H}}]}\,.

Using the representative matrix for the action of
\mathfrak{S}_{k}
on the space

in Eq. (

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(M(z))=\sum_{r=1}^{m^{\mathcal{H}}}z_{r}\phi_{\mathcal{H}}\left(C_{r}^{\mathcal{H}}\right)=\sum_{r=1}^{m^{\mathcal{H}}}z_{r}\bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash_{d_{\mathcal{H}}}k}U_{\lambda}^{T}C_{r}^{\mathcal{H}}U_{\lambda}\,.

.

We show that, for every

\mathrm{poly}(k)
time. In order to do so, we show how to efficiently compute each block

\displaystyle a_{p}\coloneqq e_{i}\otimes e_{j}\in W_{\mathcal{H}}\,,

Using the natural identification of

For every

For every

. Refs.

Lemma 4.5

The proof can be found in

For every

\displaystyle w=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau^{\prime}\sim\tau\\

\displaystyle=\overline{w}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau^{\prime}\sim\tau\\

is exactly the coefficient of the monomial

4.2
Beyond permutation invariance

So far, we have only focused on the permutation symmetries of convex optimization problem (
11
) arising from considering multiple copies of quantum channels. In this section, we discuss how the group symmetries of the underlying channels may be used to further simplify these convex programs. In particular, we show how the symmetries of the channels can be combined with the permutation symmetry and expressed as invariance under the action of a single group. Theorem
3.1
is then used to simplify the programs.

Let
G
be a finite group, and denote by
G^{k}
, the
k
-fold direct product of
G
. Consider the group
H:=G^{k}\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathfrak{S}_{k}
, an outer semi-direct product of
G^{k}
and
\mathfrak{S}_{k}
, defined as follows:

•

The underlying set is the Cartesian product of the sets
G^{k}
and
\mathfrak{S}_{k}
, i.e., the set of ordered pairs
(g,\pi)
, where
g=\left(g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{k}\right)\in G^{k}
and
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}
.

•

\gamma:\mathfrak{S}_{k}\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}\left(G^{k}\right)
is a group homomorphism given by

\gamma(\pi)\left(g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{k}\right)=\left(g_{\pi(1)},g_{\pi(2)},\ldots,g_{\pi(k)}\right)\kern 5.0pt,

for every
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}
and
g=\left(g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{k}\right)\in G^{k}
.

•

The group operation
*
is defined for any pair
(g,\pi),(g^{\prime},\pi^{\prime})\in H
as

(g^{\prime},\pi^{\prime})*(g,\pi)=(g^{\prime}\gamma(\pi^{\prime})(g),\pi^{\prime}\pi).

Consider an arbitrary action of
G
on a finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, and the natural action of
\mathfrak{S}_{k}
on
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}
defined for every
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}
as

\displaystyle\pi\cdot\left(h_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes h_{k}\right)=h_{\pi^{-1}(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes h_{\pi^{-1}(k)}\quad,\quad h_{i}\in\mathcal{H}\,,\,\forall i\in[k]\kern 5.0pt.

(31)

Then it is easy to check that the following defines an action of
H=G^{k}\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathfrak{S}_{k}
on
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}
:

\displaystyle(g,\pi)\cdot(h_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes h_{k})=g_{1}\cdot h_{\pi^{-1}(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes g_{k}\cdot h_{\pi^{-1}(k)}\quad,\quad h_{i}\in\mathcal{H}\,,\,\forall i\in[k]\kern 5.0pt,

(32)

for all
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}
and
g\in G^{k}
. In particular, we have

\displaystyle(g^{\prime},\pi^{\prime})\cdot\left((g,\pi)\cdot(h_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes h_{k})\right)=\left((g^{\prime},\pi^{\prime})*\left((g,\pi)\right)\cdot(h_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes h_{k})\right)\kern 5.0pt,

for every
(g,\pi),(g^{\prime},\pi^{\prime})\in H
.

For
\mathcal{H}\in\{X,Y\}
, let
\rho_{\mathcal{H}}:G\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{H})
be the representation of
G
defined by its action on
\mathcal{H}
and
\rho_{X\otimes Y}\coloneqq\rho_{X}\otimes\rho_{Y}
. Let
\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}
denote the representation of
G^{k}
on
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}
given by
\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}(g)\coloneqq\rho_{\mathcal{H}}(g_{1})\otimes\ldots\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{H}}(g_{k})
, for every
g\in G^{k}
. As before, denote by
P_{\mathcal{H}}
the representation of
\mathfrak{S}_{k}
on
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}
defined by the action (
31
). Then the representation of
H=G^{k}\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathfrak{S}_{k}
defined above on
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes k}
is given by
\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}(g)P_{\mathcal{H}}(\pi)
, for every
(g,\pi)\in H
. Note that in (
32
), for
\mathcal{H}=X\otimes Y
, the action of
(g,\pi)
on
(X\otimes Y)^{\otimes k}
corresponds to the simultaneous permutation of the
X
and
Y
tensor factors followed by applying
\rho_{X}(g_{i})\otimes\rho_{Y}(g_{i})
on
i
-th
X\otimes Y
tensor factor. When the subsystems are reordered as
X^{\otimes k}\otimes Y^{\otimes k}
, this action is simply given by
\sigma_{X}(g)P_{X}(\pi)\otimes\sigma_{Y}(g)P_{Y}(\pi)
.
With the above notation, we are now ready to state the following proposition:

Proposition 4.8
.

Let

Application to the generalized amplitude damping channel

As an application, we consider the generalized amplitude damping (GAD) channel defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{p,q}(\rho)=\sum_{i=1}^{4}A_{i}\rho A_{i}^{*},\quad p,q\in[0,1]

(33)

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-q}(|0\rangle\!\langle 0|+\sqrt{1-p}|1\rangle\!\langle 1|),

\displaystyle A_{2}=\sqrt{p(1-q)}(|0\rangle\!\langle 1|),

\displaystyle A_{3}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{q}(\sqrt{1-p}|0\rangle\!\langle 0|+|1\rangle\!\langle 1|),

\displaystyle A_{4}=\sqrt{pq}|1\rangle\!\langle 0|.

for all

\rho(1)=\mathrm{Z}

5
Efficient bounds on classical capacity of quantum channels

The
(unassisted) classical capacity
of a quantum channel is defined as the maximum rate at which classical information can be transmitted over the quantum channel in the asymptotic limit of many channel uses.
For a quantum channel
\mathcal{N}
, the classical capacity is characterized by the regularized Holevo information
[
Hol98
,
SW97
]
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}(\mathcal{N})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\chi(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k})\kern 5.0pt,

where
\chi(\mathcal{N})
is the Holevo capacity of the channel
\mathcal{N}
defined as

\displaystyle\chi(\mathcal{N})\coloneqq\max_{\mathcal{E}=\{p_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i}}\mathrm{H}\left(\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{i}\right)-\sum_{i}p_{i}\mathrm{H}\left(\mathcal{N}(\rho_{i})\right)\kern 5.0pt,

where the maximization is over all quantum ensembles
\mathcal{E}=\{p_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i}
. Here,
\mathrm{H}
denotes the von Neumann entropy, defined as
\mathrm{H}(\sigma)\coloneqq-\mathrm{tr}\,\left(\rho\log\rho\right)
, for every positive semidefinite operator
\rho
. Note that the Holevo information is in general non-additive
[
Has09
]
.

We denote by
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{cb}}(X,Y)
the set of constant bounded subchannels from
\mathscr{L}(X)
to
\mathscr{L}(Y)
defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{cb}}(X,Y)\coloneqq\left\{\mathcal{M}\in\mathrm{CP}(X:Y):\exists\,\sigma\in\mathscr{D}(Y)\text{ s.t. }\mathcal{M}_{X\to Y}(\rho)\leq\sigma,\forall\rho\in\mathscr{D}(X)\right\}\kern 5.0pt.

Let
\mathcal{V}(X,Y)\coloneqq\{\mathcal{M}\in\mathrm{CP}(X:Y):\beta(J_{XY}^{\mathcal{M}})\leq 1\}
, with
\beta(J_{XY}^{\mathcal{M}})
defined in terms of the following SDP

\displaystyle\beta(J_{XY}^{\mathcal{M}})\coloneqq\min_{R_{XY},S_{Y}}\mathrm{tr}\,(S_{Y})\text{\quad s.t. \quad}R_{XY}\pm(J_{XY}^{\mathcal{M}})^{T_{Y}}\geq 0\;,\;I_{X}\otimes S_{Y}\pm R_{XY}^{T_{Y}}\geq 0\kern 5.0pt,

where
(\cdot)^{T_{Y}}
denotes the partial transpose on system
Y
. Note that the set
\mathcal{V}(X,Y)
is a convex subset of
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{cb}}(X,Y)
containing all the constant channels
[
WFT19
]
.

Let
D
be a generalized quantum divergence. For any quantum channel
\mathcal{N}_{X\to Y}
, define

\displaystyle\Upsilon(\textbf{D},k)(\mathcal{N}):=\min_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{V}(X^{\otimes k},Y^{\otimes k})}\textbf{D}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k}\|\mathcal{M}).

The following proposition provides upper bounds on the classical capacity of a quantum channel.

Proposition 5.1

(
[
WFT19
]
)
.

Let
\mathrm{\mathbf{D}}
be a generalized quantum divergence. If
\mathrm{\mathbf{D}}
is bounded below by the Umegaki relative entropy on quantum states and the corresponding channel divergence is subadditive under tensor product of channels, then, for any
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}(\mathcal{N})\leq\frac{1}{k}\Upsilon(\mathrm{\mathbf{D}},k)(\mathcal{N})\,.

Proof.

The proof can be found in
[
WFT19
]
, but we include a concise proof for the reader’s convenience. As shown in
[
OPW97
]
the Holevo information can be written as a divergence radius:

\displaystyle\chi(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle=\min_{\sigma\in\mathscr{D}(Y)}\max_{\rho\in\mathscr{D}(X)}\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\sigma)

\displaystyle=\min_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{cb}}(X,Y)}\max_{\rho\in\mathscr{D}(X)}\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{M}(\rho))

\displaystyle\leq\min_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{V}(X,Y)}\max_{\rho\in\mathscr{D}(X)}\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{N}(\rho)\|\mathcal{M}(\rho))

\displaystyle\leq\min_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{V}(X,Y)}\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})

where we used the fact that if
\sigma\leq\sigma^{\prime}
then
\mathrm{D}(\rho\|\sigma)\geq\mathrm{D}(\rho\|\sigma^{\prime})
and the fact that
\mathcal{V}(X,Y)\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{cb}}(X,Y)
. So, for
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\chi(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes nk})

\displaystyle\leq\min_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{V}(X^{\otimes nk},Y^{\otimes nk})}\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes nk}\|\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\leq\min_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{V}(X^{\otimes k},Y^{\otimes k})}\mathrm{D}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes nk}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}),

where we used the fact that if
\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{V}(X^{\otimes k},Y^{\otimes k})
, then
\mathcal{M}^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal{V}(X^{\otimes nk},Y^{\otimes nk})
. Since
\mathrm{\mathbf{D}}
is bounded below by
\mathrm{D}
and subadditive under tensor product of channels, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{nk}\chi(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes nk})\leq\min_{\mathcal{M}\in\mathcal{V}(X^{\otimes k},Y^{\otimes k})}\frac{1}{k}\;\mathrm{\mathbf{D}}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k}\|\mathcal{M})=\frac{1}{k}\Upsilon(\mathrm{\mathbf{D}},k)(\mathcal{N}).

Taking the limit as
n\to\infty
, we get the desired result.
∎

Note that by Proposition
2.1
, for
\alpha\in(1,2]
, we have

\displaystyle\Upsilon(\widetilde{\mathrm{D}}_{\alpha},k)(\mathcal{N})\leq\Upsilon(\mathrm{D}^{\#}_{\alpha},k)(\mathcal{N})\leq\Upsilon(\widehat{\mathrm{D}}_{\alpha},k)(\mathcal{N})\leq\Upsilon(\mathrm{D}_{\max},k)(\mathcal{N}).

Remark 5.2
.

If in addition the generalized quantum divergence
\mathbf{D}
satisfies
\widetilde{\mathrm{D}}_{\alpha}\leq\mathbf{D}
, for some
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
, then
\frac{1}{k}\Upsilon(\textbf{D},k)(\mathcal{N})
is a strong converse bound, i.e., above this communication

Both
\mathrm{D}_{\max}
and
\widehat{\mathrm{D}}_{\alpha}
have the desired properties and were used in
[

]
and

FF21a
]
to obtain bounds on the classical capacity. On the other hand,
\widetilde{\mathrm{D}}_{\alpha}
is not always additive
[
FFRS20
]
so it cannot be used in general. The best-known general strong converse bound is given by

[
FF21a
]
.
For
\textbf{D}=\mathrm{D}^{\#}_{\alpha}

can be written in terms of a convex program. For every

\displaystyle\Upsilon(\mathrm{D}^{\#}_{\alpha},k)(\mathcal{N})=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\quad\min

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{tr}\,_{Y^{\otimes k}}(A)\|_{\infty}

(35)

\displaystyle\textrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle J^{\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k}}\leq J^{\mathcal{M}}\#_{1/\alpha}A\kern 5.0pt,

\displaystyle R\pm(J^{\mathcal{M}})^{T_{Y^{\otimes k}}}\geq 0\kern 5.0pt,

\displaystyle(I_{X^{\otimes k}}\otimes S)\pm R^{T_{Y^{\otimes k}}}\geq 0\kern 5.0pt,

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\,(S)\leq 1\kern 5.0pt,

\displaystyle A,J^{\mathcal{M}},R\in\mathscr{P}(X^{\otimes k}\otimes Y^{\otimes k})\,,\,S\in\mathscr{P}(Y^{\otimes k})\kern 5.0pt.

5.1
Exploiting symmetries to simplify the problem

Using a similar argument as in Lemma
4.2
, one may restrict the feasible region of the convex program (

) to the
\mathfrak{S}_{k}
-invariant subspace of operators.

Lemma 5.3
.

For every
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
, the convex program (

) has an optimal solution
(A,R,J^{\mathcal{M}},S)
, with

and

Proof.

It is straightforward to check that by Slater’s condition the optimal value is achieved by a feasible solution. For an arbitrary feasible solution
(A,J^{\mathcal{M}},R,S)
, we will prove that the corresponding group-average operators
(\overline{A},\overline{J^{\mathcal{M}}},\overline{R},\overline{S})
are feasible with an objective value not greater than the original value.

For brevity of notation, we write
\Pi(\pi)\coloneqq P_{X\otimes Y}(\pi)
. The first constraint,

, follows from a similar argument as in Lemma
4.2
. For the second constraint note that, for every
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}

\Pi(\pi)^{*}=\Pi(\pi)^{T}
, and we have

\displaystyle\left(\Pi(\pi)J^{\mathcal{M}}\Pi(\pi)^{*}\right)^{T_{Y^{\otimes k}}}=\left(\Pi(\pi)J^{\mathcal{M}}\Pi(\pi)^{T}\right)^{T_{Y^{\otimes k}}}=\Pi(\pi)(J^{\mathcal{M}})^{T_{Y^{\otimes k}}}\Pi(\pi)^{T}\kern 5.0pt.

(36)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left(\overline{J^{\mathcal{M}}}\right)^{T_{Y^{\otimes k}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{S}_{k}|}\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}}\Pi(\pi)J^{\mathcal{M}}\Pi(\pi)^{*}\right)^{T_{Y^{\otimes k}}}=\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{S}_{k}|}\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}}\Pi(\pi)(J^{\mathcal{M}})^{T_{Y^{\otimes k}}}\Pi(\pi)^{*}\kern 5.0pt,

(37)

and the feasibility of

and

. Similarly, we get

\displaystyle\left(\overline{R}\right)^{T_{Y^{\otimes k}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{S}_{k}|}\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}}\Pi(\pi)\,R\,\Pi(\pi)^{*}\right)^{T_{Y^{\otimes k}}}=\frac{1}{|\mathfrak{S}_{k}|}\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}}\Pi(\pi)(R)^{T_{Y^{\otimes k}}}\Pi(\pi)^{*}\kern 5.0pt,

(38)

and the feasibility of

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\phi_{X\otimes Y}\left(\left(J^{\mathcal{N}}\right)^{\otimes k}\right)\right\rrbracket_{i}\leq J_{i}\,\#_{1/\alpha}\,A_{i}\kern 5.0pt,

\displaystyle R_{i}\pm\left\llbracket\left(\phi_{X\otimes Y}\circ T_{Y^{\otimes k}}\circ\phi_{X\otimes Y}^{-1}\right)\left(\oplus_{l}A_{l}\right)\right\rrbracket_{i}\geq 0\kern 5.0pt,

\displaystyle\left\llbracket\phi_{X\otimes Y}\left(Q\left(I_{X^{\otimes k}}\otimes\phi_{Y}^{-1}\left(\oplus_{r}S_{r}\right)\right)Q^{T}\right)\right\rrbracket_{i}\pm\left\llbracket\left(\phi_{X\otimes Y}\circ T_{Y^{\otimes k}}\circ\phi_{X\otimes Y}^{-1}\right)\left(\oplus_{l}R_{l}\right)\right\rrbracket_{i}\geq 0\kern 5.0pt,

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{r}\mathrm{tr}\,(S_{r})\leq 1\kern 5.0pt,

\displaystyle A_{i},R_{i},J_{i}\in\mathscr{P}\left(\mathbb{C}^{m_{i}^{X\otimes Y}}\right),S_{r}\in\mathscr{P}\left(\mathbb{C}^{m_{r}^{Y}}\right)\kern 5.0pt,

for all
i\in\left[t^{X\otimes Y}\right]
,
j\in\left[t^{X}\right]
, and
r\in\left[t^{Y}\right]
.
The statement of the theorem follows since for
\mathcal{H}\in\{X,Y,X\otimes Y\}
, we have

and

, for every

.
∎

6
Two-way assisted quantum capacity

In this section, we consider
\mathrm{D}^{\#}_{\alpha}
in the framework of
generalized Theta-information
which was introduced in
[
FF21a
]
. As we will see, the generalized Theta-information induced by
\#
-channel divergence gives efficiently computable strong converse bounds on the two-way-assisted quantum capacity,
\mathrm{Q}^{\leftrightarrow}(\mathcal{N})
, for any quantum channel
\mathcal{N}
.

The two-way assisted quantum capacity of a quantum channel
\mathcal{N}
is the maximum rate at which quantum information can be transmitted reliably from a sender to a receiver, when the parties are allowed to perform arbitrary LOCC (short for local operations and classical communication) between consecutive channel uses
[
BDSW96
]
. While the two-way assisted quantum capacity for some specific channels such as the quantum erasure channel is known
[
BDS97
]
, no general characterization of
\mathrm{Q}^{\leftrightarrow}(\mathcal{N})
is known for an arbitrary quantum channel
\mathcal{N}
.

In
[
Rai99
,
Rai01
]
, the authors relaxed the set LOCC to a larger class of operations known as PPT-preserving operations, which is the set of channels that are positive partial transpose preserving. A quantum channel
\mathcal{P}_{AB\rightarrow A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
is PPT-preserving if the linear map
T_{B^{\prime}}\circ\mathcal{P}_{AB\rightarrow A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\circ T_{B}
is completely positive and trace-preserving
[
Rai01
]
, where
T_{B^{\prime}}
and
T_{B}
denote the partial transpose map.
For any quantum channel
\mathcal{N}
, we denote by
\mathrm{Q}^{\mathrm{PPT},\leftrightarrow}(\mathcal{N})
the PPT-assisted quantum capacity of
\mathcal{N}
. In this case, the operations between the channel uses are allowed to be PPT-preserving operations. Because of the containment LOCC
\subset
PPT
[
Rai01
]
, we have the following inequality

\displaystyle\mathrm{Q}^{\leftrightarrow}(\mathcal{N})\leq\mathrm{Q}^{\mathrm{PPT},\leftrightarrow}(\mathcal{N})\,,

for all quantum channels
\mathcal{N}
.

Inspired by the formulation of the Rains set
[
Rai01
]
, in
[
FF21a
]
the authors introduced the set of subchannels given by the zero set of the Holevo-Werner bound
[
HW01
]
as

\displaystyle\Theta(X,Y)\coloneqq\{\mathcal{M}\in\mathrm{CP}(X:Y):\exists R_{XY}\text{ s.t. }R_{XY}\pm(J_{XY}^{\mathcal{M}})^{T_{Y}}\geq 0,\mathrm{tr}\,_{Y}(R_{XY})\leq I_{X}\}\,.

Let
D
be a generalized divergence. For any quantum channel
\mathcal{N}_{X\to Y}
, define

\displaystyle R_{\Theta}(\textbf{D},k)(\mathcal{N})\coloneqq\textbf{D}(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes k}\|\mathcal{M}^{\otimes k})\,,

where
\mathcal{M}=\argmin_{\mathcal{M}\in\Theta(X,Y)}\textbf{D}(\mathcal{N}\|\mathcal{M})
.

For any quantum channel
\mathcal{N}
, by
[
FF21a
, Theorem 17]
,
[
FF21b
, Proposition 5.9]
,
[
BW18
, Corollary 5]
and the relation between the divergences in Proposition
2.1
, the following holds:

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a quantum channel. For any
\alpha\in(1,2]
and
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Q}^{\leftrightarrow}(\mathcal{N})\leq\mathrm{Q}^{\mathrm{PPT},\leftrightarrow}(\mathcal{N})\leq\mathrm{Q}^{\mathrm{PPT},\leftrightarrow,\dagger}(\mathcal{N})\leq\frac{1}{k}R_{\Theta}(\mathrm{D}^{\#}_{\alpha},k)(\mathcal{N})\leq R_{\Theta}(\widehat{\mathrm{D}}_{\alpha},1)(\mathcal{N})\leq R_{\Theta}(\mathrm{D}_{\max},1)(\mathcal{N})\,,

where
\mathrm{Q}^{\mathrm{PPT},\leftrightarrow,\dagger}(\mathcal{N})
is the strong converse capacity corresponding to
\mathrm{Q}^{\mathrm{PPT},\leftrightarrow}(\mathcal{N})
.

The squashed entanglement of the channel
\mathcal{N}
introduced in
[
TGW14
]
is known to be a converse bounds for

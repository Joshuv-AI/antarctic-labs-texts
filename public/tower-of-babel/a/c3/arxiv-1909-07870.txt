The Feigin-Odesskii Wheel Conditions and Sheaves on Surfaces
Yu Zhao

Abstract
. In this paper, we start the study of Feigin-Odesskii Wheel conditions from a geometric viewpoint, and generalize it to the K-theoretic Hall algebra of any surface, which is defined in [ 23 ] and [ 7 ] .



1.
Introduction

The study of the
K
-theoretic Hall algebra of a surface was
initiated by Vasserot and Schiffmann
[
16
]
, who
constructed the
K
-theoretic Hall algebra of the affine plane

\mathbb{A}^{2}
, and studied its action on the
K
-theory of Hilbert
schemes of points of
\mathbb{A}^{2}
. The constructions of

K
-theoretic Hall algebra were generalized by Minets

[
9
]
for Higgs sheaves over a curve, and the
author
[
23
]
, and Kapranov-Vasserot
[
7
]
for any projective
surface
S
. Porta and Sala
[
13
]
categorized these Hall algebras.

The
K
-theoretic Hall algebra of a
surface is intimately connected with the sheaves on
S
. We
consider the moduli stack
Coh_{n}
of length
n
coherent sheaves on

S
, represented by a quotient stack:

Coh_{n}=[Quot_{n}^{\circ}/GL_{n}]

where
Quot_{n}^{\circ}
is an open subset of the Quot scheme of
length
n
quotients of
\mathcal{O}_{S}^{n}
, as recalled in
Definition

3.1
. Therefore,
K(Coh_{n})
, the Grothendick group of
Coh_{n}
is represented by

K(Coh_{n})=K^{GL_{n}}(Quot_{n}^{\circ}).

For any two non-negative integers
n,m
, let
Corr_{n,m}
be the moduli stack of short exact sequences

0\to\mathcal{E}_{n}\to\mathcal{E}_{n+m}\to\mathcal{E}_{m}\to 0

where
\mathcal{E}_{n}\in Coh_{n}
,
\mathcal{E}_{m}\in Coh_{m}
and

\mathcal{E}_{n+m}\in Coh_{n+m}
. The diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{0\to\mathcal{E}_{n}\to\mathcal{E}_{n+m}\to\mathcal{E}_{m}\to 0\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{E}_{n},\mathcal{E}_{m})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}_{n+m}}

induces a morphism:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Corr_{n,m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Coh_{n}\times Coh_{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Coh_{n+m},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

where
q
is proper. Based on the ideas of perfect obstruction theory, the
author
[
23
]
and Kapranov-Vasserot

[
7
]
constructed the pull back morphism

p^{!}:K(Coh_{n}\times Coh_{m})\to K(Corr_{n,m}).

Together with the push forward morphism

q_{*}:K(Corr_{n,m})\to K(Coh_{n+m})
,
q_{*}\circ p^{!}

induces an associative algebra structure on

K(Coh)=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}K(Coh_{n}),

which is defined to be the
K
-theoretic Hall algebra of a surface.

The K-theoretic Hall algebras have shuffle presentations. Shuffle algebras are closely
connected with quantum groups
[
14
]
, but the incarnation we will be
interested in is that of Feigin-Odeskkii, who defined shuffle algebras
with multiplication twisted by an elliptic function in

[
11
]
,
[
12
]
and

[
5
]
. In
[
9
]
, Minets
gave a shuffle presentation of the K-theoretical Hall algebra of
Higgs sheaves of a curve. The author
[
23
]
generalized it
and gives a shuffle presentation of
K
-theoretic Hall algebra of any surface
S
, which was first suggested by Negut
[
10
]
.

The main tool of constructing the shuffle presentation is the Thomason
localization Theorem. For any non-negative integer
n
, the maximal
torus
T_{n}\subset GL(n)
, which consists of diagonal matrices, has a
natural action on
Quot_{n}^{\circ}
and the
T_{n}
fixed locus of

Quot_{n}^{\circ}
is
S^{n}
. By the Thomason
localization theorem
[
20
]
, we have the isomorphism:

K^{T_{n}}(S^{n})_{loc}\xrightarrow{i_{n_{*}}}K^{T_{n}}(Quot_{n}^{\circ})_{loc}.

Let

(1.1)

\tau_{n}=i_{n_{*}}^{-1}\circ l_{n}:K^{T_{n}}(Quot_{n}^{\circ})\to K(S^{n})(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})

where
l_{n}
is the natural morphism from

K^{T_{n}}(Quot_{n}^{\circ})
to

K^{T_{n}}(Quot_{n}^{\circ})_{loc}
.
Recalling

K(Coh_{n})=K^{GL_{n}}(Quot_{n}^{\circ})=(K^{T_{n}}(Quot_{n}^{\circ}))^{\sigma_{n}},

where
\sigma_{n}
is the permutation group of order
n
. Hence
\tau_{n}

induces a morphism:

\tau:K(Coh)\to\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}K(S^{n})(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})^{Sym},

which is an homomorphism between the
K
-theoretic Hall algebra and
the shuffle algebra
[
10
]
,
[
23
]
.

An important question is to study the image of shuffle presentation

\tau
. From the viewpoint of representation theory, Enriquez
[
4
]
, analogous with Feigin-Odesskii, proposed two vanishing conditions that
describe the image of
\tau
, which are called “pole conditions” and “wheel
conditions” by Feigin and Tsymbaliuk in
[
6
]
.

In this paper, we will study “pole conditions” and “wheel
conditions” from the viewpoint of algebraic geometry. We will give a geometric interpretation of
“pole conditions” and “wheel conditions” based on the shuffle
presentation of the
K
-theoretical Hall algebra, and generalize it to
any surface
S
. To be more precise, we are going to prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
S
be a smooth projective surface with a very ample bundle
\mathcal{O}(1)
s.t.
h^{0}(\mathcal{O}(1))=r
, which induces
a closed embedding of
S
into

\mathbb{P}^{r-1}
. Let
\mathcal{R}
be the kernel of

(1.2)

H^{0}(S,\mathcal{O}(1))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{S}\to\mathcal{O}(1)

Let
pr_{1}
and
pr_{2}
be the two different
projections from
S\times S
to
S
and
\mathcal{W}=pr_{1}^{*}\mathcal{R}\otimes pr_{2}^{*}\mathcal{O}^{-1}
. Let
\mathcal{W}_{ij}=pr_{ij}^{*}\mathcal{W}
, where
pr_{ij}:S^{n}\to S\times S
is the projection to
i
-th and
j
-th projectors.
Let
\Omega_{S}
and
\omega_{S}
be the cotangent and canonical
bundle over
S
.

Then for any element
[\mathcal{F}]
in the image of
\tau_{n}
in (
1.1
), we have

(1)

(Pole Conditions)
(\prod_{i\neq j}[\wedge^{\bullet}\frac{z_{j}}{z_{i}}\mathcal{W}_{ij}])\otimes[\mathcal{F}]\in K(S^{d})[z_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,z_{n}^{\pm 1}]

(2)

(Wheel Conditions) For any three different
numbers
i,j,k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, let

\Delta_{ijk}=\{(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})\in S^{n}|s_{i}=s_{j}=s_{k}\}

and
pr:\Delta_{ijk}\to S
be the
morphism which maps
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
to
s_{i}
. Then

((\prod_{i\neq j}[\wedge^{\bullet}\frac{z_{j}}{z_{i}}\mathcal{W}_{ij}])\otimes[\mathcal{F}])|_{\Delta_{ijk}}

is in the ideal generated by

(1-\frac{z_{i}}{z_{k}}pr^{*}\omega_{S})
, and
(1-q_{1}\frac{z_{j}}{z_{k}})(1-q_{2}\frac{z_{j}}{z_{k}})
,
where
q_{1}
,
q_{2}
are the two Chern roots of

pr^{*}\Omega_{S}
.

Here
[\wedge^{\bullet}E]=\sum_{i=0}^{rank(E)}(-1)^{i}[\wedge^{i}E]
for
any locally free sheaf
E
.

For any quiver
Q
, the study of the K-theoretic Hall algebra associated to
Q
and its shuffle presentation was started
by Kontsevich and Soibelman
[
8
]
, and
Schiffmann and Vasserot

[
16
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
. We
are going to systematically study the “wheel
conditions” for the cohomology of preprojective stacks in
[
22
]
.

We will organize the paper in the following way. In Section 2, we
recall the background of equivariant
K
-theory, and prove a version of

, as the motivation of proving

[

Convention 1.2
.

In this paper, we will consider some locally free sheaves and their

For any vector bundle

3.
Quot Schemes and Flag Schemes

In this section, following
[
23
]
, we recall some facts
about Quot schemes and flag schemes.

Definition 3.1
.

Given
d
a non-negative integer, Grothendieck’s Quot scheme

Quot_{d}
is defined to be the moduli scheme of quotients of coherent sheaves

\{\phi_{d}:\mathcal{O}_{S}^{d}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{d}\},

where
\mathcal{E}_{d}
has dimension
0
and

h^{0}(\mathcal{E}_{d})=d
.

There is an open subscheme
Quot_{d}^{\circ}\subset Quot_{d}
which
consists of quotients such that
H^{0}(\phi):k^{d}\to H^{0}(\mathcal{E}_{d})
is an isomorphism.

Over
Quot_{d}^{\circ}\times S
, there is a
universal quotient of
coherent sheaves

\phi_{d}:\mathcal{O}_{S}^{d}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{d},

and its kernel is denoted by
\mathcal{I}_{d}
.

Example 3.2
.

Quot_{1}^{\circ}=S
. Let
\Delta:S\to S\times S
be the diagonal map, then

\mathcal{E}_{1}\in Coh(Quot_{1}^{\circ}\times S)
is

\mathcal{O}_{\Delta}
, and
\mathcal{I}_{1}
is

\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}
, the ideal sheaf of diagonal.

Example 3.3
.

Let

, the affine plane. Then

Quot_{n}^{\circ}=Comm_{n}
, the commuting variety in
Section

2
.

For any closed point

Homology cobordism group of homology cylinders and invariants related to lower central series
Minkyoung Song

Abstract
. The homology cobordism group of homology cylinders is a generalization of both the mapping class group of surfaces and the string link concordance group. We consider extensions of Johnson homomorphisms of a mapping class group, Milnor invariants and Orr invariants of links to homology cylinders, all of which are related to free nilpotent groups. We establish a combined filtration via kernels of extended Johnson homomorphisms and extended Milnor invariants. We determine its image under the three invariants, and investigate relations among the invariants, and relations of the filtration to automorphism groups of free nilpotent groups and to graded free Lie algebras. We obtain the number of linearly independent invariants by examining the successive quotients of the filtration.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Sigma_{g,n}
be a compact, oriented surface of genus
g
with
n
boundary components. Roughly speaking, a
homology cylinder
over
\Sigma_{g,n}
is a homology cobordism between two copies of
\Sigma_{g,n}
endowed with two embeddings
i_{+}
and
i_{-}
of
\Sigma_{g,n}
(see Definition
2.1
).
Goussarov
[
Gou99
]
and Habiro
[
Habi00
]
independently introduced homology cylinders as important model objects for their theory of finite type invariants of 3-manifolds which play the role of string links in the theory of finite type invariants of links. Garoufalidis and Levine introduced in
[
GL05
,
Lev01
]
the homology cobordism group
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
of homology cylinders over
\Sigma_{g,n}
(see Definition
2.2
for the description of the group operation). We can regard
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
as an enlargement of the mapping class group
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
of
\Sigma_{g,n}
since there is a natural embedding
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
(see
[
Lev01
, p. 247]
,
[
CFK11
, Proposition 2.3]
).
We can identify
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
with more familiar groups. Both
\mathcal{H}_{0,0}
and
\mathcal{H}_{0,1}
are isomorphic to the group of homology cobordism classes of integral homology 3-spheres. The group
\mathcal{H}_{0,2}
is isomorphic to the concordance group of framed knots in homology 3-spheres. For
n\geq 3
,
\mathcal{H}_{0,n}
is isomorphic to the concordance group of framed
(n-1)
-component string links in homology 3-balls, or equivalently, in homology cylinders over
D^{2}=\Sigma_{0,1}
. Similarly,
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
can be considered to be the concordance group of framed
(n-1)
-component string links in homology cylinders over
\Sigma_{g,1}
.

Hereafter, we assume that
\Sigma_{g,n}
has nonempty boundary, that is,
n>0
.
Hence
F:=\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n})
and
H:=H_{1}(\Sigma_{g,n};\mathbb{Z})
are the free group and the free abelian group of rank
2g+n-1
, respectively.
We denote by
\mathsf{D}_{k}(H)
the kernel of the bracket map
H\otimes\mathsf{L}_{k}(H)\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathsf{L}_{k+1}(H)
where
\mathsf{L}_{k}(H)
is the degree
k
part of the free Lie algebra over
H
. For a group
G
,
G_{k}
denotes the
k
th term of lower central series given by
G_{1}=G
,
G_{k+1}=[G,G_{k}]
.

To understand structures of
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
, several invariants and filtrations are introduced. First, Garoufalidis and Levine extended Johnson homomorphisms and Johnson filtration to
\mathcal{H}_{g,1}

[
GL05
,
Lev01
]
. Based on them, Morita, Sakasai, Cochran-Harvey-Horn
[
Mor08
,
Sak12
,
CHH12
]
constructed their new invariants on
\mathcal{H}_{g,1}
, respectively. Cha-Friedl-Kim
[
CFK11
]
introduced a torsion invariant on
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
. The author naturally extended Garoufalidis-Levine’s Johnson homomorphisms and the filtration to
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
in
[
So16
]
. Also, she defined extended Milnor invariants, extended Milnor filtration, and Hirzebruch-type invariants.
In this paper, we newly defined extended Orr invariants, and address the three invariants related to lower central series:

•

extended Johnson homomorphisms
\tilde{\eta}_{k}\colon\mathcal{H}_{g,n}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\operatorname{Aut}(F/F_{k})
(see Section
2.2
)

•

extended Milnor invariants
\tilde{\mu}_{k}\colon\mathcal{H}_{g,n}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}(F/F_{k})^{2g+n-1}
(see Section
2.3
)

•

extended Orr invariants
\tilde{\theta}_{k}\colon\mathcal{H}_{g,n}(k)\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}H_{3}(F/F_{k})
(see Section
2.4
),

with the following filtrations of
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
:

•

extended Johnson filtration
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}[k]:=\operatorname{Ker}\tilde{\eta}_{k}

•

extended Milnor filtration
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}(k):=\operatorname{Ker}\tilde{\mu}_{k}\cap\mathcal{H}^{0}_{g,n}(=\operatorname{Ker}\tilde{\mu}_{k}\textrm{ for }k\geq 2)

•

modified extended Johnson filtration
\mathcal{H}^{0}_{g,n}[k]:=\mathcal{H}_{g,n}[k]\cap\mathcal{H}^{0}_{g,n}
.

Here,
\mathcal{H}^{0}_{g,n}
is the subgroup consisting of 0-framed homology cylinders (see Section
3.1
). By modifying
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}[k]
to
\mathcal{H}^{0}_{g,n}[k]
, and we have a filtration combined with
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}(k)

\mathcal{H}\rhd\mathcal{H}^{0}[1]=\mathcal{H}(1)\rhd\mathcal{H}^{0}[2]\rhd\mathcal{H}(2)\rhd\cdots\rhd\mathcal{H}^{0}[k]\rhd\mathcal{H}(k)\rhd\mathcal{H}^{0}[k+1]\rhd\mathcal{H}(k+1)\rhd\cdots.

We determine images and successive quotients of this filtration under the three invariants.

1.1.
Extended Johnson homomorphims

Johnson homomorphisms

\eta_{k}\colon\mathcal{M}_{g,1}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\operatorname{Aut}(F/F_{k})
and the
Johnson filtration
(also called the
relative weight filtration
) on the mapping class group have connections to the geometry of the moduli space of curves, 3-manifold topology, and number theory (e.g., see
[
Hai95
,
GL98
,
Put12
,
Mat13
]
).
However, many basic questions remain open. In particular, the image of the Johnson homomorphisms are unknown. Morita
[
Mor93
]
gave the induced injective homomorphisms
\frac{\mathcal{M}_{g,1}[k]}{\mathcal{M}_{g,1}[k+1]}\hookrightarrow\mathsf{D}_{k}(H)
, but the precise images are also unknown. Rationally, there are results for
k\leq 7
in
[
Joh80
,
Hai97
,
AN95
,
Mor99
,
MSS15
]
. On the other hand, Garoufalidis and Levine considered
extended Johnson homomorphisms

\tilde{\eta}_{k}\colon\mathcal{H}_{g,1}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\operatorname{Aut}(F/F_{k})
and the filtration
\mathcal{H}_{g,1}[k]:=\operatorname{Ker}\tilde{\eta}_{k}
, and determine
\frac{\mathcal{H}_{g,1}[k]}{\mathcal{H}_{g,1}[k+1]}
as the whole
\mathsf{D}_{k}(H)
in
[
GL05
]
. The homomorphisms have been used to develop new invariants and to find conditions for the invariants to be (quasi-)additive in
[
Mor08
,
Sak12
,
CHH12
]
. The author naturally extended
\tilde{\eta}_{k}
and the filtration to
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
in
[
So16
]
, and suggested a candidate of the image
\tilde{\eta}_{k}(\mathcal{H}_{g,n})
. Now we formulate the images and quotients of the combined filtration.

Theorem 1.1
.

The image of the extended Johnson homomorphism
\tilde{\eta}_{k}\colon\mathcal{H}_{g,n}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\operatorname{Aut}(F/F_{k})
is

\displaystyle\operatorname{Aut}_{*}(F/F_{k})

\displaystyle:=\bigg\{\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(F/F_{k})~\bigg|\begin{array}[]{ll}\textrm{there is a lift }\tilde{\phi}\in\operatorname{Aut}(F/F_{k+1})\textrm{ such that }\\
\tilde{\phi}(x_{i})=\alpha_{i}^{-1}x_{i}\alpha_{i}\textrm{ for some }\alpha_{i}\in F/F_{k}\textrm{ and }\tilde{\phi}\textrm{ fixes }[\partial_{n}]\end{array}\bigg\}

where
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}\in F
are homotopic to each boundaries.
Moreover,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{H}_{g,n}[k]}{\mathcal{H}_{g,n}[k+1]}

\displaystyle\cong\frac{\mathcal{H}^{0}_{g,n}[k]}{\mathcal{H}^{0}_{g,n}[k+1]}\cong\operatorname{Ker}\{\operatorname{Aut}_{*}(F/F_{k+1})\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_{*}(F/F_{k})\},

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{H}_{g,n}(k)}{\mathcal{H}^{0}_{g,n}[k+1]}

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Ker}\{\operatorname{Aut}_{*}(F/F_{k+1})\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_{*}(F/F_{k})\}\cap\{\phi\;|\;\phi(x_{i})=x_{i}\}.

We construct a descending filtration of
\operatorname{Aut}_{*}(F/F_{k})
which corresponds to images of the combined filtration under
\tilde{\eta}_{k}
. More details are discussed in Section
4.1
.

1.2.
Extended Milnor invariants

Milnor’s
\bar{\mu}
-invariants of links are actually invariants on string links without indeterminacy
[
HL90
]
. Orr
[
Orr89
]
determined the precise number of linearly independent Milnor invariants of length
k
as the rank of
\mathsf{D}_{k}(H)
. The author defined extended Milnor invariants
\tilde{\mu}_{k}\colon\mathcal{H}_{g,n}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}(F/F_{k})^{2g+n-1}
and a filtration
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}(k):=\operatorname{Ker}\tilde{\mu}_{k}
on
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
in
[
So16
]
. The invariants are not only an extension of the Milnor invariants for (string) links, but also a generalization of extended Johnson homomorphisms of
\mathcal{H}_{g,1}
. If
n=1
, they are equivalent to the extended Johnson homomorphisms on
\mathcal{H}_{g,1}
, and if
g=0
, the extended Milnor invariants are equivalent to the Milnor invariants of string links in homology 3-balls. Note that the Orr’s result above can be restated that
\frac{\mathcal{H}_{0,n}(k)}{\mathcal{H}_{0,n}(k+1)}\cong\mathsf{D}_{k}(H)
. In
[
So16
]
, it is revealed that for general
g\geq 0
and
n>0
, the successive quotients
\frac{\mathcal{H}_{g,n}(k)}{\mathcal{H}_{g,n}(k+1)}
are finitely generated free abelian if
k\geq 2
, and upper and lower bounds of their ranks are provided. Now we complete our previous work as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
g\geq 0
,
n\geq 1
and
k\geq 2
, the extended Milnor invariants induce isomorphisms

\frac{\mathcal{H}_{g,n}(k)}{\mathcal{H}_{g,n}(k+1)}\cong\mathsf{D}_{k}(H),\quad\frac{\mathcal{H}^{0}_{g,n}[k+1]}{\mathcal{H}_{g,n}(k+1)}\cong\mathsf{D}_{k}(H^{\prime}),\quad\frac{\mathcal{H}^{0}_{g,n}[2]}{\mathcal{H}_{g,n}(2)}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}\cong\mathsf{D}_{1}(H^{\prime})

where
H^{\prime}=H_{1}(\Sigma_{0,n})
.
Consequently, for
k\geq 2
, there are
(2g+n-1)N_{k}-N_{k+1}
linearly independent extended Milnor invariants of length
k+1
distinguishing homology clinders with vanishing extended Milnor invariants of lenght
\leq k
. Here,
N_{k}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{d|k}\varphi(d)\;(2g+n-1)^{k/d}
where
\varphi
is the Möbius function.

Also, we clarify the image of
\tilde{\mu}_{k+1}\colon\mathcal{H}_{g,n}(k)\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}(F_{k}/F_{k+1})^{2g+n-1}
in Theorem
4.5
.
Based on Theorem
1.2
, the extended Milnor invariants can be regarded as a proper generalization of the extended Johnson homormorphism on
\mathcal{H}_{g,1}
and the Milnor invariants on
\mathcal{H}_{0,n}
. Habegger showed that the extended Johnson homomorphisms on
\mathcal{H}_{g,1}
coincides with Milnor’s invariants on the string link concordance group, which is called a
Milnor-Johnson correspondence

[
Habe00
]
. It also can be integrated with the extended Milnor invariants.
We remark that vanishing of the extended Milnor invariants is the exact criterion that Hirzebruch-type intersection form defect invariants from iterated
p
-covers are defined
[
So16
]
.

1.3.
Extended Orr invariants

Orr
[
Orr89
]
introduced homotopy invariants of based links to find the number of linearly independent Milnor invariants. Igusa and Orr
[
IO01
]
saw the relation of the invariants to
k
-slice and to Milnor invariants to prove the
k
-slice conjecture, which gives a geometric characterization for the vanishing of the Milnor invariants. They reduced the invariants to invariants with values in
H_{3}(F/F_{k})
. We extend (Igusa-)Orr invariants to homology cylinders in Section
2.4
, and obtain the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

For
k\geq 2
, the extended Orr invariant
\tilde{\theta}_{k}\colon\mathcal{H}_{g,n}(k)\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}H_{3}(F/F_{k})
is surjective.
It induces isomorphisms

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{H}_{g,n}(k)}{\mathcal{H}_{g,n}(2k-1)}

\displaystyle\cong H_{3}(F/F_{k}),

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{H}_{g,n}(k)}{\mathcal{H}_{g,n}(k+1)}

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Coker}\{H_{3}(F/F_{k+1})\rightarrow H_{3}(F/F_{k})\}.

Cha and Orr defined transfinite Milnor invariants for closed 3-manifolds in
[
CO20
]
.
Our
\tilde{\theta}_{k}(M)
is the same as the Cha-Orr invariant
\theta_{k}(\widehat{M})
of finite length in
[
CO20
]
, with the closure
\widehat{M}
of
M
.

The above theorems imply relations of the filtration of
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
to automorphism groups of free nilpotent groups, graded free Lie algebras, and the third homology of free nilpotent groups, respectively.
We also establish relations among the three invariants in Section
4.4
.
Further, we look into
\frac{\mathcal{H}_{g,n}(1)}{\mathcal{H}_{g,n}(2)}
, which is the only non-abelian case in Section
4.6
.

1.4.
Organization

In Section
2
, we recall definitions of homology cylinders, their homology cobordism group, extended Johnson homomorphisms, and extended Milnor invariants. Also we provide previously known results about the two invariants. We define extended Orr invariants for homology cylinders. In Section
3
, we define 0-framed homology cylinders and refine the two filtrations to be totally ordered. In Section
4
, we determine their images under the three invariants, and build a connection between the invariants. We find the rank of the successive quotients of the filtration. Finally in Section
5
, we give proofs of the theorems stated in Section
4
.

In this paper, manifolds are assumed to be compact and oriented. Our results hold in both topological and smooth categories.

2.
Definitions and known results

2.1.
Homology cylinder cobordism group

We recall precise definitions about homology cylinders. Let
\Sigma_{g,n}
, or simply
\Sigma
, be a surface of
g
genus with
n
boundary components. We only consider the case
n>0
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
homology cylinder over

\Sigma
consists of a 3-manifold
M
with two embeddings
i_{+}^{\vphantom{}},~i_{-}^{\vphantom{}}\colon\Sigma\hookrightarrow\partial M
, called
markings
, such that

(1)

i_{+}^{\vphantom{}}|_{\partial\Sigma}=i_{-}^{\vphantom{}}|_{\partial\Sigma}
,

(2)

i_{+}\cup i_{-}\colon\Sigma\cup_{\partial}(-\Sigma)\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\partial M
is an orientation-preserving homeomorphism, and

(3)

i_{+}^{\vphantom{}},i_{-}^{\vphantom{}}
induce isomorphisms
H_{k}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}H_{k}(M;\mathbb{Z})
for all
k\geq 0
.

We denote a homology cylinder by
(M,i_{+}^{\vphantom{}},i_{-}^{\vphantom{}})
or simply by
M
.

Two homology cylinders
(M,i_{+}^{\vphantom{}},i_{-}^{\vphantom{}})
and
(N,j_{+}^{\vphantom{}},j_{-}^{\vphantom{}})
over
\Sigma_{g,n}
are said to be
isomorphic
if there exists an orientation-preserving homeomorphism
f\colon M\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}N
satisfying
j_{+}^{\vphantom{}}=f\circ i_{+}^{\vphantom{}}
and
j_{-}^{\vphantom{}}=f\circ i_{-}^{\vphantom{}}
. Denote by
\mathcal{C}_{g,n}
the set of all isomorphism classes of homology cylinders over
\Sigma_{g,n}
. We define a product operation on
\mathcal{C}_{g,n}
by

(M,i_{+}^{\vphantom{}},i_{-}^{\vphantom{}})\cdot(N,j_{+}^{\vphantom{}},j_{-}^{\vphantom{}}):=(M\cup_{i_{-}^{\vphantom{}}\circ(j_{+}^{\vphantom{}})^{-1}}N,i_{+}^{\vphantom{}},j_{-}^{\vphantom{}})

for
(M,i_{+}^{\vphantom{}},i_{-}^{\vphantom{}}),~(N,j_{+}^{\vphantom{}},j_{-}^{\vphantom{}})\in\mathcal{C}_{g,n}
, which endows
\mathcal{C}_{g,n}
with a monoid structure. The identity is
((\Sigma_{g,n}\times I)/(z,0)=(z,t),\mathrm{id}\times 1,\mathrm{id}\times 0)
where
z\in\partial\Sigma,t\in I
.
Here and after,
I
denote the interval
[0,1]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Two homology cylinders
(M,i_{+}^{\vphantom{}},i_{-}^{\vphantom{}})
and
(N,j_{+}^{\vphantom{}},j_{-}^{\vphantom{}})
over
\Sigma_{g,n}
are said to be
homology cobordant
if there exists a 4-manifold
W
such that

(1)

\partial W=M\cup(-N)/\sim
, where
\sim
identifies
i_{+}^{\vphantom{}}(x)
with
j_{+}^{\vphantom{}}(x)
and
i_{-}^{\vphantom{}}(x)
with
j_{-}^{\vphantom{}}(x)
for all
x\in\Sigma_{g,n}
, and

(2)

the inclusions
M\hookrightarrow W
,
N\hookrightarrow W
induce isomorphisms on the integral homology.

We denote by
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
the set of homology cobordism classes of elements of
\mathcal{C}_{g,n}
. We usually omit the subscripts and simply write
\mathcal{H}
for general
g,n
. By abuse of notation, we also write
(M,i_{+},i_{-})
, or simply
M
for the class of
(M,i_{+},i_{-})
. The monoid structure on
\mathcal{C}_{g,n}
descends to a group structure on
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
, with
(M,i_{+}^{\vphantom{}},i_{-}^{\vphantom{}})^{-1}=(-M,i_{-}^{\vphantom{}},i_{+})
. We call this group the
homology cobordism group
of homology cylinders, or simply
homology cylinder cobordism group
. In fact, there are two kinds of groups
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{smooth}}
and
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{top}}
depending on whether the homology cobordism is smooth or topological, and there exists a canonical epimorphism
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{smooth}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{top}}
whose kernel contains an abelian group of infinite rank
[
CFK11
]
. In this paper, however, the author does not distinguish the two cases since everything holds in both cases. The fact that the mapping class group over
\Sigma_{g,n}
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
implies
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
is non-abelian except

and
(0,2)
. For any pairs

and
(g^{\prime},n^{\prime})
satisfying

,

, and

, there is an injective homomorphism
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{g^{\prime},n^{\prime}}

[
So16
]
.

2.2.
Extended Johnson homomorphisms and filtration

In
[
GL05
,
Lev01
]
, Garoufalidis and Levine defined homomorphisms
\tilde{\eta}_{k}\colon\mathcal{H}_{g,1}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\operatorname{Aut}(F/F_{k})
and a filtration
\mathcal{H}_{g,1}[k]:=\operatorname{Ker}\tilde{\eta}_{k}
as extensions of the Johnson homomorphisms and the Johnson filtration of the mapping class group
\mathcal{M}_{g,1}

[
Joh80
]
. It is a straightforward consequence of Stallings’ theorem in
[
Sta65
]
. The
\tilde{\eta}_{k}
is defined to be
(i_{+})_{k}^{-1}\circ(i_{-})_{k}
where
(i_{\pm})_{k}\colon F/F_{k}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\pi_{1}(M)/\pi_{1}(M)_{k}
is the isomorphisms induced from
i_{\pm}
. They can be considered on
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}
in the same way. We call the maps
\tilde{\eta}_{k}
on
\mathcal{H}_{g,n}

extended Johnson homomorphisms
(they were referred to as ‘Garoufalidis-Levine homomorphisms’ in
[
So16
]
).
They proved that
\tilde{\eta}_{k}\colon\mathcal{H}_{g,1}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\operatorname{Aut}_{0}(F/F_{k})
are surjective where
F=\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g,1})=\langle m_{1},\ldots,m_{g},l_{1},\ldots,l_{g}\rangle
and

\displaystyle\operatorname{Aut}_{0}(F/F_{k}):=\{\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(F/F_{k})~|~

\displaystyle\textrm{there is a lift }\tilde{\phi}\colon F/F_{k+1}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}F/F_{k+1}

\displaystyle\textrm{such that }\tilde{\phi}([\partial_{n}])=[\partial_{n}]\}.

and the image of
\mathcal{H}_{g,1}[k]
under
\tilde{\eta}_{k+1}
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{Ker}\{\operatorname{Aut}_{0}(F/F_{k+1})\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\operatorname{Aut}_{0}(F/F_{k})\}
. Recall that
\mathsf{L}_{k}(H)
is the degree
k
part of the free Lie algebra over
H
, and
\mathsf{D}_{k}(H):=\operatorname{Ker}\{H\otimes\mathsf{L}_{k}(H)\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\mathsf{L}_{k+1}(H)\}
. For
k\geq 2
,

\mathsf{D}_{k}(H)\cong\operatorname{Ker}\{\operatorname{Aut}_{0}(F/F_{k+1})\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_{0}(F/F_{k})\}.

Since
\mathsf{D}_{k}(H)
is free abelian and the rank is well-known, the successive quotient groups
\frac{\mathcal{H}_{g,1}[k]}{\mathcal{H}_{g,1}[k+1]}
, which is isomorphic to the image
\tilde{\eta}_{k+1}(\mathcal{H}_{g,1}[k])
, are clarified. We remark that the image
\eta_{k+1}(\mathcal{M}_{g,1}[k])
of the Johnson subgroup
\mathcal{M}_{g,1}[k]:=\mathcal{H}_{g,1}[k]\cap\mathcal{M}_{g,1}
of the mapping class group is unknown.

Remark 2.3
.

From the result about the Artin representation for the concordance group of string links in
D^{2}\times I
, Habegger and Lin
[
HL98
, Theorem 1.1]
proved that
\tilde{\eta}_{k}\colon\mathcal{H}_{0,n}\mathchoice{\longrightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}{\rightarrow}\operatorname{Aut}_{1}(F/F_{k})
is surjective where
F=\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{0,n})=\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}\rangle
and

\displaystyle\operatorname{Aut}_{1}(F/F_{k}):=\{\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(F/F_{k})~|~

\displaystyle\phi(x_{i})=\lambda_{i}^{-1}x_{i}\lambda_{i}\textrm{ for some }\lambda_{i}\in F/F_{k-1}

\displaystyle\textrm{ and }\phi(x_{1}\cdots x_{n-1})=x_{1}\cdots x_{n-1}\}.

Note that

\mathsf{D}_{k}(H)\cong\operatorname{Ker}\{\operatorname{Aut}_{1}(F/F_{k+2})\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_{1}(F/F_{k+1})\}

for
k\geq 2
, and the Artin representation corresponds to the Milnor invariant on string links. Two invariants give rise to the same filtration whose the graded quotients are isomorphic to
\mathsf{D}_{k}(H)
. See
[
HL98
,
Orr89
]
.

2.3.
Extended Milnor invariants and filtration

Let
(M,i_{+}^{\vphantom{}},i_{-}^{\vphantom{}})
be a homology cylinder over
\Sigma_{g,n}
. The chosen
x_{i}^{\vphantom{}}
is of the form

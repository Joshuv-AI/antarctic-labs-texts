Stable degeneration of families of klt singularities with constant local volume
Zhiyuan Chen

Abstract
. For a klt singularity, C. Xu and Z. Zhuang [ XZ25 ] proved the associated graded algebra of a minimizing valuation of the normalized volume function is finitely generated, finishing the proof of the stable degeneration conjecture proposed by C. Li and C. Xu. We prove a family version of the stable degeneration: for a locally stable family of klt singularities with constant local volume, the ideal sequences of the minimizing valuations for the normalized volume function form families of ideals with flat cosupport, which induce a degeneration to a locally stable family of K-semistable log Fano cone singularities. In the proof, we give a method to construct families of Kollár models, which are a crucial tool introduced by Xu–Zhuang to prove finite generation for valuations of higher rational rank.



1.
Introduction

Along with recent developments of the algebraic theory of K-stability of Fano varieties, an analogous K-stability theory for klt singularities—the local counterparts of Fano varieties that are fundamental in the MMP (minimal model program)—is studied, through the normalized volume function introduced in
[
Li18
]
. For a klt singularity
x\in(X,\Delta)
, the normalized volume of a real valuation
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
centered at
x
is

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X,\Delta}(v)\coloneqq A_{X,\Delta}(v)^{\dim X}\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{X}(v),

where
A_{X,\Delta}(v)
denotes the log discrepancy, and
\mathrm{vol}_{X}(v)
denotes the volume introduced in
[
ELS03
]
. The infimum
\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x;X,\Delta)\coloneqq\inf_{v}\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X,\Delta}(v)
is called the local volume of the klt singularity
x\in(X,\Delta)
.

The local K-stability theory is particularly interested in the minimizer of
\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}
, that is, a real valuation
v^{\mathrm{m}}\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
such that
\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x;X,\Delta)=\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X,\Delta}(v^{\mathrm{m}})
, as highlighted in the
Stable Degeneration Conjecture
proposed in
[
Li18
, Conj. 7.1]
and
[
LX18
, Conj. 1.2]
: There exists a minimizer
v^{\mathrm{m}}
of
\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X,\Delta}
, which is unique up to scaling and is quasi-monomial; the associated graded algebra
R_{0}=\mathrm{gr}_{v^{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathscr{O}_{X,x})
is finitely generated, and induces a klt degeneration
(X_{0}=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(R_{0}),\Delta_{0})
, which is a K-semistable log Fano cone singularity under the natural torus action, and is the unique such K-semistable degeneration.

The Stable Degeneration Conjecture has been proved is a series of works: The existence, uniqueness, and quasi-monomial property of a minimizer are proved in
[
Blu18
]
,
[
XZ21
]
,
[
Xu20
]
; the K-semistability of the degeneration is proved in
[
LX18
]
assuming finite generation; and finally, the finite generation is proved in
[
XZ25
]
. See
[
LLX20
]
and
[
Zhu25
]
for surveys and more comprehensive references.

The minimizing valuation
v^{\mathrm{m}}
, or the K-semistable degeneration, is associated with a klt singularity canonically. Then we ask whether the K-semistable degenerations for a family of klt singularities form a family. We consider locally stable families in the sense of
[
Kol23
]
. As the local volume function
\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}
is constructible and lower semi-continuous by
[
Xu20
]
and
[
BL21
]
, we consider families of klt singularities with constant local volume.

Instead of the minimizing valuation

itself, we consider the ideal sequence

associated with it, where
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\coloneqq\{f:v^{\mathrm{m}}(f)\geq\lambda\}\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{X,x}

Outline of the proof

We sketch the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The base

is the spectrum of a DVR, so we can degenerate the ideal sequence

on the generic fiber

to an ideal sequence

on

with flat cosupport over

. Then

has constant multiplicity over

; by the lower semi-continuity of the lc thresholds, the closed fiber

computes

. By a stronger uniqueness of minimizer implicitly in
[
BLQ24
]
, we conclude that
\mathfrak{a}_{s,\bullet}
is the saturation of

; see Lemma
2.18
.

Note that

computes the lc threshold of
I_{\eta,\bullet}=\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{\eta})
on
(X_{\eta},\Delta_{\eta})
. Then we can construct a model
f\colon(Y,E)\to(X,\Delta)
relatively of Fano type over
X
such that
v_{\eta}^{\mathrm{m}}\in\mathrm{QM}(Y_{\eta},E_{\eta})
, by a similar argument that has been used in
[
LX18
, Thm. 4.2]
,
[

, Thm. 4.48]
, and
[
XZ25
, Lem. 3.2]
; see Lemma

. Since

that is monomial on
(Y_{s},E_{s})
, corresponding to

as in

. Since

computes the lc thresholds of

on

, so we can conclude

Now the closed fiber

are

, namely, monomial lc places of special

Instead, note that

can be viewed as the limit of the weighted blow-up valuations of

and the discrete valuation

induced by

goes to

-complement for which all these weighted blow-ups are monomial lc places. Hence we get a model of qdlt Fano type model accommodating them by

2.1.3.
Locally stable families

We use the notion of locally stable families of pairs following

Kol23
]

Let
S

\pi\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
consists of a morphism of schemes
\pi\colon X\to S
that is flat and of finite type, with reduced, equidimensional,
(\mathrm{S}_{2})

\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta

[
Kol23
, Thm-Def. 4.3]
). If
S
is normal and
\pi

, then it suffices to assume that
\mathrm{Supp}(\Delta)\to S
has pure relative dimension

. In general, we always assume components of
\Delta
are
relative Mumford divisors
on
X
(see
[
Kol23
, Def. 4.68]
).

A family of pairs
\pi\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
is said to be
locally stable
if the fibers
(X_{s},\Delta_{s})
are slc for all
s\in S
, and
K_{X/S}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier (see
[
Kol23
, Def-Thm. 4.7]
). If moreover
(X_{s},\Delta_{s})
are klt (resp., qdlt, lc) for all

If

-Gorenstein family
of klt pairs, since the essential condition is that

-Carter.

is affine, together with a section

of

-point of the
S
-scheme

at a point

\kappa(s)

A
locally stable family of klt singularities
over

\pi\colon(X,\Delta)\to S

Definition 2.6
.

Let
S
be a reduced scheme,
\pi\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
be a family of pairs. A

consists of a projective birational morphism

on

(see

Let

2.2.
Valuations

2.2.1.
Real valuations

All valuations that we consider will be real valuations.

Let
X
be an integral scheme with function field
K(X)
. A
real valuation

v
on
K(X)
gives a valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{v}
of
K(X)
of rank
1
with an order-preserving embedding of the valuation group
\Gamma_{v}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}
, which recovers the valuation
v\colon K(X)^{\times}\to\mathbb{R}
. By convention, we set
v(0)=+\infty
.

The
rational rank
of
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
is
\mathop{\mathrm{rat.rank}}(v)\coloneqq\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Gamma_{v}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q})
.

The set of all real valuations on
K
centered on
X
is denoted by
\mathrm{Val}_{X}
. For
x\in X
, let

\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}\coloneqq\{v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}:\mathrm{center}_{X}(v)=x\}.

More generally, if
X=\bigcup_{i}X_{i}
is a reduced scheme, where
X_{i}
are the irreducible components of
X
, then we write
\mathrm{Val}_{X}\coloneqq\bigsqcup_{i}\mathrm{Val}_{X_{i}}
.

Suppose
f\colon Y\to X
is a dominant morphism, where
Y
is an integral scheme, then we have an induced map
\mathrm{Val}_{Y}\to\mathrm{Val}_{X}
by restricting a valuation on
K(Y)
to the subfield
K(X)
via
f
. If
f
is birational and proper, then this is a bijection, and we usually identify
\mathrm{Val}_{Y}=\mathrm{Val}_{X}
.

A real valuation
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
can be evaluated at functions, coherent ideals, and Cartier divisors on
X
, as well as their
\mathbb{R}
-linear combinations (e.g.,
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier
\mathbb{R}
-divisors).

2.2.2.
Quasi-monomial valuations

Let
(Y,E=\sum_{i}E_{i})
be an snc pair. Suppose
y\in Y
is a generic point of a stratum
E_{1}\cap\cdots\cap E_{r}\subseteq Y
, and
t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\in\mathscr{O}_{Y,y}
is a regular system of parameters such that
E_{i}
is defined by
t_{i}
near
y
for all
i=1,\ldots,r
. Then there is an isomorphism
\hat{\mathscr{O}}_{Y,y}\simeq\kappa(y)[\![t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}]\!]
.

For
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{r}
, there exists a unique real valuation
v_{\alpha}\in\mathrm{Val}_{Y}
satisfying the following: if
f\in\mathscr{O}_{Y,y}
is written as
f=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}c_{m}t_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots t_{r}^{m_{r}}
in
\hat{\mathscr{O}}_{Y,y}\simeq\kappa(y)[\![t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}]\!]
, then

v_{\alpha}(f)=\inf\{\langle\alpha,m\rangle:c_{m}\neq 0\}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{+\infty\},\quad\text{where}\ \langle\alpha,m\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}m_{i};

see
[
JM12
, Prop. 3.1]
. We call
v_{\alpha}
a
monomial valuation
at
y\in(Y,E)
, and denote by
\mathrm{QM}_{y}(Y,E)
the set of all monomial valuations at
y
. Finally, we define
\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)\coloneqq\bigcup_{y}\mathrm{QM}_{y}(Y,E)
, where
y
ranges over all generic points of all strata of
(Y,E)
.

Following
[
XZ25
]
, we consider a generalization of monomial valuations to
simple-toroidal
pairs.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
(Y,E=\sum_{i}E_{i})
be a simple-toroidal pair. Suppose
y\in Y
is a generic point of a stratum
\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}\subseteq Y
. Then locally at
y
there is a finite abelian cover

\mu\colon(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})\to(Y,E)

which is étale away from
E
, such that
(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime}=\sum_{i}E^{\prime}_{i})
is snc, and
\mu^{*}E_{i}=m_{i}E_{i}^{\prime}
for some
m_{i}>0
. Let
y^{\prime}\in\mu^{-1}(y)
, then
y^{\prime}
is a generic point of
\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}^{\prime}\subseteq Y^{\prime}
. For
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{r}
, define
v_{\alpha}\in\mathrm{Val}_{Y}
to be the restriction of
v^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}}\in\mathrm{QM}_{y^{\prime}}(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
corresponding to
\alpha^{\prime}=(\alpha_{1}/m_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}/m_{r})
. Thus

v_{\alpha}(E_{i})=v^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}}(\mu^{*}E_{i})=m_{i}v^{\prime}_{\alpha^{\prime}}(E_{i}^{\prime})=\alpha_{i}.

Note that
v_{\alpha}
does not depend on the choice of a finite abelian cover.

We call
v_{\alpha}
a
monomial valuation
at
y\in(Y,E)
, and denote by
\mathrm{QM}_{y}(Y,E)\subseteq\mathrm{Val}_{Y}
the set of all such
v_{\alpha}
. The
relative interior

\mathrm{QM}_{y}^{\circ}(Y,E)\subseteq\mathrm{QM}_{y}(Y,E)
consists of
v_{\alpha}
for which
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{r}
. By definition there is a canonical bijection

\mathrm{QM}_{y}(Y,E)\simeq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{r},\quad v\mapsto(v(E_{1}),\ldots,v(E_{r})),

which is a homeomorphism when
\mathrm{Val}_{Y}
is equipped with the weakest topology such that the evaluation map
\mathrm{Val}_{Y}\to\mathbb{R}
,
v\mapsto v(f)
is continuous for all

(see
[
JM12
, Lem. 4.5]
).

More generally, let
(Y,E)
be a pair where
E=\sum_{i}E_{i}
is reduced, such that
(\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\mathscr{O}_{Y,y},E|_{\mathrm{Spec}\mathscr{O}_{Y,y}})
is simple-toroidal for all generic points
y
of all strata
\bigcap_{i\in I}E_{i}\subseteq Y
. Then we define

\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)\coloneqq\bigcup\nolimits_{y}\mathrm{QM}_{y}(Y,E)=\bigsqcup\nolimits_{y}\mathrm{QM}^{\circ}_{y}(Y,E).

Remark 2.8
.

Let
(Y,E)
be a pair where
E
is a reduced Weil divisor. Then
(Y,E)
is simple-toroidal if and only if
Y\smallsetminus E\hookrightarrow Y
is a toroidal embedding without self-intersection such that the rational convex polyhedral cones
\sigma^{Z}\subseteq N^{Z}_{\mathbb{R}}
associated to all strata
Z\subseteq Y
are simplicial (see
[
KKMSD73
, II.§1]
for the relevant notions).

In fact, if
(Y,E)
is Zariski locally, at a point
y\in E
, isomorphic to the quotient
(V,D)/G
where
(V,D)
is snc and
G
is a finite abelian group, then the action of
G
on
(V,D)
is formally isomorphic to an action of
G^{\prime}
on
\mathbb{A}^{n}
where
G\simeq G^{\prime}\subseteq(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}})^{n}
, and
D
corresponds to a union of coordinate hyperplanes
D^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{n}
(see
[
Kol13
, 3.17]
). Then
(\mathbb{A}^{n},D^{\prime})/G^{\prime}
is an affine toric variety associated to a simplicial cone, and
(Y,E)
is formally isomorphic to
(\mathbb{A}^{n},D^{\prime})/G^{\prime}
at
y\in E
. That is,
Y\smallsetminus E\hookrightarrow Y
is a toroidal embedding, and the associated cones are simplicial; it has no self-intersections since every stratum is normal.

Conversely, assume
Y\smallsetminus E\hookrightarrow Y
is such a toroidal embedding, then every component of
E
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier (see
[
KKMSD73
, II.§1, Lem. 1]
). To see
(Y,E)
is simple-toroidal in the sense of Definition
2.1
, we may take a composition of cyclic covers, and assume every component of
E
is Cartier. In this case, the cone
\sigma^{Z}\subseteq N^{Z}_{\mathbb{R}}
is spanned by a basis of the lattice
N^{Z}
, hence
(Y,E)
is snc.

Let
Z\subseteq Y
be a stratum, with the generic point
z\in Z
. Recall that
\sigma^{Z}\subseteq N^{Z}_{\mathbb{R}}
is the dual cone of the cone of effective Cartier divisors supported on
E
and passing through
Z
(see
[
KKMSD73
, II.§1, Def. 3]
). Then there is a canonical isomorphism

\mathrm{QM}_{z}(Y,E)\simeq\sigma^{Z}

and
\sigma^{Z}\cap N^{Z}
corresponds to the lattice generated by valuations given by prime divisors over
Y
. Hence we can equip
\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)
with an integral structure (see
[
KKMSD73
, 71]
).

By the results in
[
KKMSD73
, II.§2]
, if
f\colon(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})\to(Y,E)
is a toroidal resolution, then

\mathrm{QM}(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})=\mathrm{QM}(Y,E).

Conversely, if
\sigma\subseteq\mathrm{QM}_{y}(Y,E)
is a rational simplicial cone, then there exists a toroidal proper birational morphism
f\colon(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})\to(Y,E)
with
(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
simple-toroidal and
\mathrm{QM}_{y^{\prime}}(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})=\sigma
for some
y^{\prime}\in f^{-1}(y)
. Moreover, we can choose
(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
such that every
f
-exceptional divisor corresponds to an edge of
\sigma
, and
\mathrm{Ex}(f)
supports an
f
-ample divisor, by
[
XZ25
, Lem. 3.16]
.

Definition 2.10

2.2.3.
Log discrepancies

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a pair such that
K_{X}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier. Suppose
f\colon Y\to X
is a birational morphism of finite type, and
F
is a prime divisor on
Y
. Then the log discrepancy

A_{X,\Delta}(F)\coloneqq 1+\mathrm{ord}_{F}(K_{Y}-f^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta))

only depends on the valuation
\mathrm{ord}_{F}\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
. The log discrepancy extends to a function

A_{X,\Delta}\colon\mathrm{Val}_{X}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}

such that

A_{X,\Delta}(v)\coloneqq\sup_{(Y,E)}\sum\nolimits_{i}v(E_{i})\cdot A_{X,\Delta}(E_{i})

where the supremum ranges over all log resolutions
f\colon(Y,E=\sum_{i}E_{i})\to(X,\Delta)
; see
[
JM12
, §5]
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a pair such that
K_{X}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier. Suppose

f\colon(Y,E=E_{1}+\cdots+E_{r})\to(X,\Delta)

is a log resolution. Then

A_{X,\Delta}(v)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{r}v(E_{i})\cdot A_{X,\Delta}(E_{i})

for all
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
, and the equality holds if and only if
v\in\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)
.

Proof.

Note that the value
\sum_{i=1}^{r}v(E_{i})\cdot A_{X,\Delta}(E_{i})
does not change if we replace
(Y,E)
with a toroidal resolution, and the set
\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)
also remains the same. Hence we may assume that
f\colon(Y,E)\to(X,\Delta)
is an snc model, so the inequality follows by definition.

If the equality holds, then it is proved that
v\in\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)
in
[
JM12
, Cor. 5.4]
when
X
is regular and
\Delta=0
. In general, we can reduce it to the regular case as follows. Let
\Delta_{Y}
be the crepant pullback of
\Delta
, so that

\Delta_{Y}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(1-A_{X,\Delta}(E_{i}))\cdot E_{i},

and

A_{X,\Delta}(v)=A_{Y,\Delta_{Y}}(v)=A_{Y}(v)-\sum_{i=1}^{r}v(E_{i})\cdot(1-A_{X,\Delta}(E_{i})).

If
A_{X,\Delta}(v)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}v(E_{i})\cdot A_{X,\Delta}(E_{i})
, then
A_{Y}(v)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}v(E_{i})
, hence
v\in\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)
.
∎

2.3.1.

We consider ideal sequences indexed by

Definition 2.13
.

Let

\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}=\{\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\geq 0}

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{X}
for

(1)

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{\mu}

(2)

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\mathfrak{a}_{\mu}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda+\mu}
for all
\lambda,\mu\geq 0
.

(3)

\mathfrak{a}_{0}=\mathscr{O}_{X}
, and
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}=\bigcap_{\lambda^{\prime}<\lambda}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda^{\prime}}
for all
\lambda>0
.

Let
x\in X
be a point, with the prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{x}\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{X}
. We say an ideal sequence
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is centered at
x
if
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}
is
\mathfrak{p}_{x}
-primary for all
\lambda>0
. In this case,
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is uniquely determined by the corresponding ideal sequence
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\mathscr{O}_{X,x}=\{\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\mathscr{O}_{X,x}\}_{\lambda}
of
\mathscr{O}_{X,x}
; hence we may assume
x
is a closed point by localization if necessary.

Suppose
X
in integral and
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
. The ideal sequence
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v)
associated with

is defined by

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v)\coloneqq\{f\in\mathscr{O}_{X}:v(f)\geq\lambda\}.

If
\mathrm{center}_{X}(v)=x
, then
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v)
is an ideal sequence centered at
x
.

A real valuation
v
on
X
can be evaluated at an ideal sequence
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
by

v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=\inf_{\lambda>0}\frac{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda})}{\lambda}=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda})}{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{+\infty\}.

Note that
v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v))=1
.

.

\mathrm{vol}(v)=\mathrm{vol}_{X}(v)\coloneqq\mathrm{e}_{X}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v)),

where

. The set of valuations with positive volume is denoted by

. Note that

Lemma 2.16

x
with

\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})<+\infty.

Suppose
\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
is the saturation of
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
. Then

\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta;\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}),

and a valuation
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
computes the lc threshold of
\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
if and only if it computes that of
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
.

Proof.

We have

\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta;\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=\inf_{w\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}^{\mathrm{div}}}\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(w)}{w(\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})}=\inf_{w\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}^{\mathrm{div}}}\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(w)}{w(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})}=\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})

since
w(\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=w(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
for every
w\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}^{\mathrm{div}}
. For a general real valuation
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
, we have

\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(v)}{v(\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})}\geq\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(v)}{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})}

since
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\subseteq\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
. Thus, if
v
computes the lc threshold of
\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
, then it also computes that of
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
. Conversely, assume
v
computes the lc threshold of
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
. Then
v
also computes the lc threshold of the ideal sequence
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v)
associated with itself by
[
JM12
, Thm. 7.8]
. Hence

\inf_{w\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}^{\mathrm{div}}}\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(w)}{w(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v))}=\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v))=\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(v)}{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v))}<+\infty.

Then there exists a divisorial valuation
w\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}^{\mathrm{div}}
such that
w(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v))>0
. By rescaling, we may assume that
w(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v))=1
, so that
w(f)\geq v(f)
for all
f\in\mathscr{O}_{X,x}
. Thus

\mathrm{vol}_{X}(v)\geq\mathrm{vol}_{X}(w)>0.

By
[
BLQ24
, Prop. 3.12]
,
v(\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
. Hence
v
also computes the lc threshold of
\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
.
∎

2.3.2.
Normalized volumes and the minimizers

Definition 2.17

(cf.
[
Li18
, §1]
)
.

Let
x\in(X,\Delta)
be a klt singularity, and
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
be a real valuation. The normalized volume of
v
is

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(v)=\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X,\Delta}(v)\coloneqq A_{X,\Delta}(v)^{n}\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{X}(v)\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{+\infty\}

where
n=\dim_{x}(X)
, and by convention
+\infty\cdot 0=+\infty
.

The
local volume
of
x\in(X,\Delta)
is

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x;X,\Delta)\coloneqq\inf\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X,\Delta}(v):v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}}}\right\}.

The local volume is positive by
[
Li18
, Thm. 1.2]
. A real valuation
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
is called a
minimizer
for the normalized volume if
\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(v)=\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x;X,\Delta)
.

Let
x\in(X,\Delta)
be a klt singularity. There exists a minimizer
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
of the normalized volume by
[
Blu18
, Main Thm.]
and
[
Xu20
, Rmk. 3.8]
, which is unique up to scaling by
[
XZ21
, Thm. 1.1]
and
[
BLQ24
, Cor. 1.3]
, and is quasi-monomial by
[
Xu20
, Thm. 1.2]
.

Lemma 2.18
.

Let
x\in(X,\Delta)
be a klt singularity, and
v^{\mathrm{m}}\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
be a minimizer of the normalized volume. Let
n=\dim_{x}(X)
. Then the following hold:

(1)

v^{\mathrm{m}}
computes the lc threshold of
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}})
, and

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x;X,\Delta)=\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}))^{n}\cdot\mathrm{e}_{X}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}))=\inf\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})^{n}\cdot\mathrm{e}_{X}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})}}\right\}

where
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
ranges over all ideal sequences centered at
x
;

(2)

if
v\in\mathrm{Val}_{X,x}
computes the lc threshold of
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}})
, then
v=c\cdot v^{\mathrm{m}}
for some
c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
;

(3)

if
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}=\{\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
is an ideal sequence centered at
x
such that
\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x;X,\Delta)=\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})^{n}\cdot\mathrm{e}_{X}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
, then
\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}_{\lambda}=\mathfrak{a}_{c\lambda}(v^{\mathrm{m}})
for some
c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, for all
\lambda\geq 0
.

Proof.

(1). By
[
Liu18
, Thm. 27]
, we have

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x;X,\Delta)=\inf\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})^{n}\cdot\mathrm{e}_{X}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})}}\right\}.

Since
v^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}))=1
, and
\mathrm{e}_{X}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}))=\mathrm{vol}_{X}(v^{\mathrm{m}})
by definition, and

\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}))\leq\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(v^{\mathrm{m}})}{v^{\mathrm{m}}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}))}=A_{X,\Delta}(v^{\mathrm{m}}),

we have

\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}))^{n}\cdot\mathrm{e}_{X}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}))\leq A_{X,\Delta}(v^{\mathrm{m}})^{n}\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{X}(v^{\mathrm{m}})=\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x;X,\Delta).

Thus the equality holds, and
v^{\mathrm{m}}
computes the lc threshold
\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}))
.

(2). Suppose
v
computes the lc threshold of
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}})
, that is,

\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}))=\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(v)}{v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}))}.

By scaling, we may assume that
v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}))=\inf v(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v^{\mathrm{m}}))/\lambda=1
. Thus
v(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v^{\mathrm{m}}))\geq\lambda
, i.e.,

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v^{\mathrm{m}})\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v)

for all
\lambda\geq 0
. Hence

\mathrm{vol}_{X}(v)=\mathrm{e}_{X}(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v))\leq\mathrm{e}_{X}(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v^{\mathrm{m}}))=\mathrm{vol}_{X}(v^{\mathrm{m}}).

Since
A_{X,\Delta}(v)=\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}))=A_{X,\Delta}(v^{\mathrm{m}})
, we get

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X,\Delta}(v)\leq\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X,\Delta}(v^{\mathrm{m}})=\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x;X,\Delta).

By uniqueness of the minimizer,
v=c\cdot v^{\mathrm{m}}
for some
c>0
. In fact,
v=v^{\mathrm{m}}
since
v(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}))=1
.

(3). This is obtained in the proof of
[
BLQ24
, Cor. 1.3]
, which we briefly recall: We may assume that
\mathrm{lct}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=\mathrm{lct}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}))=1
by scaling. Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t}
, where
0\leq t\leq 1
, be the geodesic between
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,0}=\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,1}=\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}})
defined in
[
BLQ24
, Def. 4.1]
. Then

\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t})\leq(1-t)\cdot\mathrm{lct}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})+t\cdot\mathrm{lct}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}))=1

by
[
BLQ24
, Prop. 5.1]
, and

\mathrm{e}_{X}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t})^{-1/n}\geq(1-t)\cdot\mathrm{e}_{X}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})^{-1/n}+t\cdot\mathrm{e}_{X}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}))^{-1/n}=\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x;X,\Delta)^{-1/n}

by
[
BLQ24
, Thm. 1.1(2)]
. Hence

\mathrm{lct}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t})^{n}\cdot\mathrm{e}_{X}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet,t})\leq\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x;X,\Delta),

so all inequalities above are equalities. Then
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}})
have the same saturation, up to scaling, by
[
BLQ24
, Thm. 1.1(3)]
. But
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}})
is saturated by
[
BLQ24
, Lem. 3.20]
, so we get (3).
∎

2.4.
Families of log Fano cone singularities

A log Fano cone singularity is a klt singularity with a good torus action, which generalizes the usual affine cone over a Fano variety. The K-stability of log Fano cone singularities is parallel to that of Fano varieties; see
[
LX18
]
,
[
LWX21
]
,
[
Hua22
]
,
[
LW25
]
for an algebraic approach.

Let
S
be a reduced scheme. Let
M\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{r}
be a free abelian group of finite rank
r
(i.e., a lattice), and
\mathbb{T}_{S}=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}_{S}}(\mathscr{O}_{S}[M])
be the corresponding torus over
S
.

Definition 2.19
.

A
locally stable family of log Fano cone singularities
over
S
(with
weight lattice

M
) consists of a locally stable family of affine klt singularities
\pi\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
with
x\in X(S)
and an action of
\mathbb{T}_{S}
on
(X,\Delta)
over
S
such that
x(S)
is the fixed locus, called the
vertex
.

Thus
X=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}_{S}}(\mathscr{A})
where

\mathscr{A}=\bigoplus_{m\in M}\mathscr{A}_{m}

is an
M
-graded
\mathscr{O}_{S}
-algebra, such that
\mathscr{A}_{+}=\bigoplus_{m\neq 0}\mathscr{A}_{m}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
is an ideal, and
\mathscr{A}/\mathscr{A}_{+}=\mathscr{A}_{0}=\mathscr{O}_{S}
. Note that each
\mathscr{A}_{m}
is a flat coherent
\mathscr{O}_{S}
-module.

A
polarization
is
\xi\in N_{\mathbb{R}}\coloneqq\mathrm{Hom}(M,\mathbb{R})
such that
\langle\xi,m\rangle>0
for all
m\in M\smallsetminus\{0\}
with
\mathscr{A}_{m}\neq 0
. Let
\mathrm{wt}_{\xi}\in\mathrm{Val}_{X_{s}}
denote the
\mathbb{T}_{s}
-invariant valuation on each fiber
X_{s}
given by

\mathrm{wt}_{\xi}(a)=\min\{\langle\xi,m\rangle:a_{m}\neq 0\},

for
a=\sum_{m\in M}a_{m}\in\bigoplus_{m\in M}\mathscr{A}_{m}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{S}}\kappa(s)
.

The family
\pi\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
is called a locally stable family of K-semistable Fano cone singularities over
S
if there is a polarization
\xi
such that
(X_{s},\Delta_{s};\xi)
is a K-semistable polarized log Fano cone singularity for all
s\in S
. Such a polarization
\xi
is unique up to scaling if it exists (e.g., by
[
LX18
, Thm. 3.5]
), hence we often omit it.

The following is a family version of
[
Zhu24a
, §3.1]
.

Lemma 2.20
.

Let
\pi\colon(X=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}_{S}}(\mathscr{A}),\Delta)\to S
be a locally stable family of log Fano cone singularities, where
\mathscr{A}=\bigoplus_{m\in M}\mathscr{A}_{m}
, and
\xi
be a polarization. Assume
\xi\in N
, that is,
\langle\xi,m\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
m\in M
. Let

\overline{X}\coloneqq\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}_{S}}\mathscr{A}[t]

where
\mathscr{A}[t]
is given the
\mathbb{N}
-grading with
\deg(\mathscr{A}_{m})=\langle\xi,m\rangle
and
\deg(t)=1
. Then the following hold:

(1)

There is a canonical open immersion
X\hookrightarrow\overline{X}
, and the complement
V=\overline{X}\smallsetminus X
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier relatively ample divisor.

(2)

Let
\overline{\Delta}
be the closure of
\Delta
, then
\bar{\pi}\colon(\overline{X},\overline{\Delta}+V)\to S
is a locally stable family of plt pairs.

(3)

If
S
is connected, then
K_{\overline{X}/S}+\overline{\Delta}+(1+a)V\sim_{S,\mathbb{Q}}0
for some
a>0
.

Proof.

(1). The open subset
X\subseteq\overline{X}
is the locus
t\neq 0
, so the complement
V
is the divisor defined by
t
. Note that
\mathscr{O}_{\overline{X}}(V)=\mathscr{O}_{\overline{X}}(1)
, hence
V
is relatively ample over
S
.

We have
V=\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}_{S}}\mathscr{A}
where
\mathscr{A}
is given the
\mathbb{N}
-grading with
\deg(\mathscr{A}_{m})=\langle\xi,m\rangle
, and
X
(resp.,
\overline{X}
) is the affine cone (resp., projective cone), relatively over
S
, for
\mathscr{O}_{V}(1)=\mathscr{O}_{\overline{X}}(1)|_{V}
. Thus
V\subseteq\overline{X}
is Cartier on the locus where
\mathscr{O}_{V}(1)
is invertible; in particular,
V\subseteq\overline{X}
is Cartier in codimension
2
.

(2). First assume
S
is the spectrum of a DVR, with the closed point
s\in S
. Then
(X,\Delta+X_{s})
is plt by
[
Kol23
, Thm. 4.54]
, hence
(V,\Delta_{V}+V_{s})
is plt, where
\Delta_{V}=\mathrm{Diff}_{V}(\overline{\Delta})=\overline{\Delta}|_{V}
(see
[
Kol13
, Lem. 3.1]
). Then
(\overline{X},\overline{\Delta}+V+\overline{X}_{s})
is dlt. Thus the conclusion holds in this case by
[
Kol23
, Thm. 4.54]
again. Note that the closed fiber
\overline{\Delta}_{s}
of
\overline{\Delta}
is the closure of
\Delta_{s}
.

In general, the formation of
\overline{X}
commutes with base change. By the DVR case above, the formation of
\overline{\Delta}
commutes with specialization, hence
\bar{\pi}\colon(\overline{X},\overline{\Delta}+V)\to S
is a well-defined family of pairs by
[
Kol23
, Thm-Def. 4.3]
. Then it is a locally stable family of plt pairs since its base change to every DVR is so by
[
Kol23
, Def-Thm. 4.7]
.

(3). Let

Y=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}_{V}}\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{O}_{V}(n),

with the canonical morphisms
p\colon Y\to V
and
\mu\colon Y\to X
, and
E=\mu^{-1}(x(S))\simeq V
. Then

\mu\colon(Y,E)\to(X,\Delta)

is a family of birational models, and we can write

\mu^{*}(K_{X/S}+\Delta)=K_{Y/S}+\mu^{-1}_{*}\Delta+E^{\prime}

for some
E^{\prime}
on
Y
supported on
E
. For each fiber we have
E^{\prime}_{s}=(1-A_{X_{s},\Delta_{s}}(E_{s}))E_{s}
, and
E_{s}
is a prime divisor. Thus the function
s\mapsto A_{X_{s},\Delta_{s}}(E_{s})
is locally constant. If
S
is connected, then it is constant, say with value
a
. Note that
a>0
since
(X_{s},\Delta_{s})
is klt for all
s\in S
. Then

K_{\overline{X}_{s}}+\overline{\Delta}_{s}+(1+a)V_{s}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0

for all
s\in S
by
[
Zhu24a
, Lem. 3.3]
. Hence
K_{\overline{X}/S}+\overline{\Delta}+(1+a)V\sim_{S,\mathbb{Q}}0
.
∎

Lemma 2.21
.

Let
\pi\colon(X=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}_{S}}(\mathscr{A}),\Delta)\to S
be a locally stable family of log Fano cone singularities, where
\mathscr{A}=\bigoplus_{m\in M}\mathscr{A}_{m}
, and
\xi
be a polarization. Then the following hold:

(1)

The function
s\mapsto\mathrm{vol}_{X_{s}}(\mathrm{wt}_{\xi})
is locally constant.

(2)

The function
s\mapsto A_{X_{s},\Delta_{s}}(\mathrm{wt}_{\xi})
is locally constant.

(3)

The function
s\mapsto\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X_{s},\Delta_{s}}(\mathrm{wt}_{\xi})
is locally constant.

In particular, if
\pi\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
is a locally stable family of K-semistable log Fano cone singularities with the polarization
\xi
, then the function

s\mapsto\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x_{s};X_{s},\Delta_{s})

is locally constant, where
x\colon S\to X
is the vertex section.

Proof.

Write
n=\dim(X_{s})
for all
s\in S
.

(1). By definition,

\mathrm{vol}_{X_{s}}(\mathrm{wt}_{\xi})=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{n!}{\lambda^{n}}\sum_{\langle\xi,m\rangle<\lambda}\dim_{\kappa(s)}(\mathscr{A}_{m}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{S}}\kappa(s)),

where the summation ranges over all
m\in M
with
\langle\xi,m\rangle<\lambda
. Since
\mathscr{A}_{m}
is a flat coherent
\mathscr{O}_{S}
-module,

s\mapsto\dim_{\kappa(s)}(\mathscr{A}_{m}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{S}}\kappa(s))

is locally constant for all
m\in M
(see
[
Stacks
,
05P2
]
). Hence
s\mapsto\mathrm{vol}_{X_{s}}(\mathrm{wt}_{\xi})
is locally constant.

(2). Fix
s\in S
, then
\xi\mapsto A_{X_{s},\Delta_{s}}(\mathrm{wt}_{\xi})
is a linear map on
N_{\mathbb{R}}
by
[
LX18
, Lem. 2.18]
. Hence it suffices to show
s\mapsto A_{X_{s},\Delta_{s}}(\mathrm{wt}_{\xi})
is locally constant when
\xi\in N
. We may assume that
S
is connected. Let

\bar{\pi}\colon(\overline{X},\overline{\Delta}+V)\to S

be the family constructed in Lemma
2.20
using
\xi
. Then

K_{\overline{X}/S}+\overline{\Delta}+(1+a)V\sim_{S,\mathbb{Q}}0

for some
a>0
. Restricting to each fiber, we get
a=A_{X_{s},\Delta_{s}}(\mathrm{wt}_{\xi})
for all
s\in S
by
[
Zhu24a
, Lem. 3.3]
.

(3). By definition,

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X_{s},\Delta_{s}}(\mathrm{wt}_{\xi})=A_{X_{s},\Delta_{s}}(\mathrm{wt}_{\xi})^{n}\cdot\mathrm{vol}_{X_{s}}(\mathrm{wt}_{\xi}).

Hence it is a locally constant function on
S
by (1) and (2).

If
\pi\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
is a family of K-semistable log Fano cone singularities with the polarization
\xi
, then

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x_{s};X_{s},\Delta_{s})=\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X_{s},\Delta_{s}}(\mathrm{wt}_{\xi})

by
[
LX18
, Thm. 3.5]
. Hence
s\mapsto\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x_{s};X_{s},\Delta_{s})
is locally constant by (3).
∎

3.
Special valuations

3.1.
Models of qdlt Fano type

Definition 3.1

(
[
XZ25
, Def. 3.5, 3.7]
)
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be an affine klt pair. A model

f\colon(Y,E=E_{1}+\cdots+E_{r})\to(X,\Delta)

is said to be of
qdlt Fano type
if there is an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
on
Y
such that
\lfloor D\rfloor=0
,
D\geq f^{-1}_{*}\Delta
,
(Y,D+E)
is qdlt, and
-(K_{Y}+D+E)
is
f
-ample.

The model
f\colon(Y,E)\to(X,\Delta)
is called a
qdlt anti-canonical model
if one can take
D=f^{-1}_{*}\Delta
above, that is,
(Y,f^{-1}_{*}\Delta+E)
is qdlt and
-(K_{Y}+f^{-1}_{*}\Delta+E)
is
f
-ample.

Let
x\in(X,\Delta)
be a klt singularity. A qdlt anti-canonical model
f\colon(Y,E)\to(X,\Delta)
is called a
Kollár model
at
x
if all components of
E
are centered at
x
.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
XZ25
, Prop. 3.9]
)
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be an affine klt pair, and
f\colon(Y,E)\to(X,\Delta)
be a model of qdlt Fano type. Then there exists a birational contraction
\phi\colon Y\dashrightarrow\overline{Y}
over
X
that is a local isomorphism at generic points of strata of
(Y,E)
, such that
\bar{f}\colon(\overline{Y},\overline{E}=\phi_{*}E)\to(X,\Delta)
is a qdlt anti-canonical model, and
\mathrm{QM}(Y,E)=\mathrm{QM}(\overline{Y},\overline{E})
.

Remark 3.3
.

Let
f\colon(Y,E=\sum_{i=1}^{r}E_{i})\to(X,\Delta)
be a model of qdlt Fano type. Then the anti-canonical model
\bar{f}\colon(\overline{Y},\overline{E}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\overline{E}_{i})\to(X,\Delta)
is uniquely determined by the divisorial valuations
\mathrm{ord}_{\overline{E}_{i}}=\mathrm{ord}_{E_{i}}
. In fact, since

-(K_{\overline{Y}}+\bar{f}^{-1}_{*}\Delta+\overline{E})\sim_{X,\mathbb{Q}}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\overline{E}_{i},

is
\bar{f}
-ample, where
a_{i}=A_{X,\Delta}(\overline{E}_{i})=A_{X,\Delta}(\mathrm{ord}_{E_{i}})
, if
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
\ell a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
i
, then

\overline{Y}=\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}_{X}}\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\bar{f}_{*}\mathscr{O}_{\overline{Y}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m\ell a_{i}\overline{E}_{i}}}\right),

and

\bar{f}_{*}\mathscr{O}_{\overline{Y}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m\ell a_{i}\overline{E}_{i}}}\right)=f_{*}\mathscr{O}_{Y}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m\ell a_{i}E_{i}}}\right)=\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}\mathfrak{a}_{m\ell a_{i}}(\mathrm{ord}_{E_{i}})

only depend on the valuations
\mathrm{ord}_{E_{i}}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be an affine klt pair, and
f\colon(Y,E=\sum_{i=1}^{r}E_{i})\to(X,\Delta)
be a model of qdlt Fano type. Suppose
f^{\prime}\colon(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}^{\prime})\to(X,\Delta)
is a model such that
E^{\prime}
contains all
f^{\prime}
-exceptional divisors,
\mathrm{ord}_{E_{i}^{\prime}}=\mathrm{ord}_{E_{i}}
for every
i
, and
-(K_{Y^{\prime}}+f^{\prime-1}_{*}\Delta+E^{\prime})
is
f^{\prime}
-nef. Then
f^{\prime}\colon(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})\to(X,\Delta)
is a model of qdlt Fano type, and the birational map
\phi\colon Y\dashrightarrow Y^{\prime}
is a local isomorphism at all generic points of strata of
(Y,E)
and
(Y^{\prime},E^{\prime})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.2
, we may assume that
-(K_{Y}+f^{-1}_{*}\Delta+E)
is ample. Since
Y
and
Y^{\prime}
have the same exceptional divisors over

Lemma 4.6

Let

f^{(1)}\colon(\mathcal{Y}^{(1)},\mathcal{E}^{(1)})\to(\mathcal{X}^{(1)},\Delta^{(1)})

is a locally stable family of Kollár models.
∎

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
S
be a regular connected scheme. Let
\pi\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
with
x\in X(S)
be a locally stable family of klt singularities, and
f\colon(Y,E=\sum_{i=1}^{r}E_{i})\to(X,\Delta)
be a locally stable family of Kollár models at
x
. Suppose
\alpha=(\alpha_{i})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{r}
, and
v_{s}^{\alpha}\in\mathrm{QM}^{\circ}(Y_{s},E_{s})
is the corresponding quasi-monomial valuation on
x_{s}\in(X_{s},\Delta_{s})
for every
s\in S
. For each
\lambda\geq 0
, let

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\coloneqq\sum_{\langle\alpha,m\rangle\geq\lambda}f_{*}\mathscr{O}_{Y}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}E_{i}}}\right)\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{X},

where the sum ranges over all
m=(m_{i})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}m_{i}\geq\lambda
. Then the following hold:

(1)

\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}=\{\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
is an ideal sequence on
X
centered at
x
, and
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{a}_{>\lambda}
is flat over
S
for all
\lambda\geq 0
.

(2)

Let
X_{\alpha}=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}_{S}}(\pi_{*}\mathrm{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mathscr{O}_{X}))
, where

\mathrm{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mathscr{O}_{X})\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\lambda\geq 0}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{a}_{>\lambda},

then the canonical morphism
\pi_{\alpha}\colon X_{\alpha}\to S
is flat, of finite type, with normal and geometrically integral fibers. There is a canonical action of
\mathbb{T}=(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m},S})^{r}
on
X_{0}
preserving the grading, whose fixed locus is the image of a section
x_{\alpha}\colon S\to X_{\alpha}
.

(3)

For every
s\in S
, the restriction
\mathfrak{a}_{s,\bullet}=\{\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\mathscr{O}_{X_{s}}\}_{\lambda}
is the ideal sequence associated with
v_{s}^{\alpha}
.

(4)

There exists a
\mathbb{T}
-invariant effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta_{\alpha}
on
X_{\alpha}
such that
\pi_{\alpha}\colon(X_{\alpha},\Delta_{\alpha})\to S
is a locally stable family of klt pairs, and
(X_{\alpha,s},\Delta_{\alpha,s})
is the degeneration of
x_{s}\in(X_{s},\Delta_{s})
induced by
v_{s}^{\alpha}
for every
s\in S
.

Proof.

(1). It is clear that
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}=\{\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
is an ideal sequence on
X
. Write

I_{m}=f_{*}\mathscr{O}_{Y}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}E_{i}}}\right)\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{X}

for
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
, and
I_{>m}\coloneqq\sum_{m^{\prime}>m}I_{m^{\prime}}
, where
m^{\prime}=(m^{\prime}_{i})>(m_{i})=m
if and only if
m^{\prime}_{i}\geq m_{i}
for all
i
and
m^{\prime}\neq m
. By Lemma
4.10
,

\mathcal{R}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}I_{m}t_{1}^{-m_{1}}\cdots t_{r}^{-m_{r}}

is flat over
\mathbb{A}_{S}^{r}=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}_{S}(\mathscr{O}_{S}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}])
. Hence

\mathcal{R}/(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})\simeq\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}I_{m}/I_{>m}

is flat over
S
. By Lemma
3.14
, we have

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{a}_{>\lambda}\simeq\bigoplus_{\langle\alpha,m\rangle=\lambda}I_{m}/I_{>m},

so
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{a}_{>\lambda}
is also flat over
S
.

(2). By the computation above,
X_{\alpha}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathbb{A}^{r}_{S}}0_{S}
over
S
, where
0_{S}\subseteq\mathbb{A}_{S}^{r}
is the zero section, and
(\mathcal{X},\Delta_{\mathcal{X}})\to\mathbb{A}^{r}_{S}
is the multiple degeneration induced by
f\colon(Y,E)\to(X,\Delta)
in Lemma
4.10
. Hence
\pi_{\alpha}\colon X_{\alpha}\to S
is flat, of finite type, with normal and geometrically integral fibers. Since
\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{A}^{r}_{S}
is
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant, we get an action of
\mathbb{T}
on
X_{\alpha}
, and

{\pi_{\alpha}}_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X_{\alpha}}\simeq\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}\pi_{*}(I_{m}/I_{>m})

is the eigenspace decomposition. In particular, the
\mathbb{T}
-action preserves each
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{a}_{>\lambda}
. Since

\pi_{*}(I_{0}/I_{>0})=\pi_{*}(\mathscr{O}_{X}/I_{>0})\simeq\mathscr{O}_{S},

the fixed locus is given by a section
x_{\alpha}\colon S\to X_{\alpha}
.

(3) For
s\in S
, we construct
I_{s,m}\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{X_{s}}
and
\mathcal{R}_{s}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}I_{s,m}t_{1}^{-m_{1}}\cdots t_{r}^{-m_{r}}
as above from the Kollár model
f_{s}\colon(Y_{s},E_{s})\to(X_{s},\Delta_{s})
. Then
\mathcal{R}_{s}=\mathcal{R}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{S}}\kappa(s)
as subrings of
\mathscr{O}_{X_{s}}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,t_{r}^{\pm 1}]
, by Lemma
4.10
. So
I_{s,m}=I_{m}\mathscr{O}_{X_{s}}=I_{m}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{S}}\kappa(s)
for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
. By
[
XZ25
, Cor. 4.10]
, we have

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v_{s}^{\alpha})=\sum_{\langle\alpha,m\rangle\geq\lambda}I_{s,m}.

Thus
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v_{s}^{\alpha})=\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\mathscr{O}_{X_{s}}
, and
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v_{s}^{\alpha})/\mathfrak{a}_{>\lambda}(v_{s}^{\alpha})\simeq(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{a}_{>\lambda})\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{S}}\kappa(s)
. So we get (3).

(4). Recall that
X_{\alpha}\simeq\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathbb{A}^{r}_{S}}0_{S}
over
S
. Let
\Delta_{\alpha}
be the base change of
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
, then
\pi_{\alpha}\colon(X_{\alpha},\Delta_{\alpha})\to S
is a locally stable family of klt pairs. As in (3), the fiber
(X_{\alpha,s},\Delta_{\alpha,s})
is the central fiber of the multiple degeneration
(\mathcal{X}_{s},\Delta_{s})\to\mathbb{A}_{s}^{r}
, so it is the degeneration of
x_{s}\in(X_{s},\Delta_{s})
induced by
v_{s}^{\alpha}
.
∎

Remark 4.12
.

Keep the notations in Lemma
4.11
and the proof. We claim that
\Delta_{\alpha}
is defined by the divisorial part of the initial ideal of
\Delta
.

More precisely, write
\Delta=\sum c_{D}D
, and let
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{X}
be the ideal of the closed subscheme
D\subseteq X
for each component
D
of
\Delta
. The
initial ideal
of
\mathfrak{b}
(with respect to
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
) is the ideal

\mathop{\mathrm{in}_{\mathfrak{a}}}(\mathfrak{b})\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\lambda\geq 0}(\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda})/(\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{>\lambda})\subseteq\mathrm{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mathscr{O}_{X}).

Then
\mathop{\mathrm{in}_{\mathfrak{a}}}(\mathfrak{b})
defines a closed subscheme of
X_{\alpha}=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}_{S}}(\pi_{*}\mathrm{gr}_{\mathfrak{a}}(\mathscr{O}_{X}))
. Let
D_{\alpha}\subseteq X_{\alpha}
be its divisorial part (see
[
Kol23
, 4.16]
). We will show that
\Delta_{\alpha}\coloneqq\sum_{j}c_{D}D_{\alpha}
is the divisor given by Lemma
4.11
.

Recall that
X_{\alpha}=\mathcal{X}\times_{\mathbb{A}_{S}^{r}}0_{S}
. Let
\Delta_{0}
be the pullback of
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
to
X_{\alpha}
, then we need to show
\Delta_{0}=\Delta_{\alpha}
. There is an isomorphism

(\mathcal{X},\Delta_{\mathcal{X}})\times_{\mathbb{A}_{S}^{r}}(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m},S})^{r}\simeq(X,\Delta)\times_{S}(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m},S})^{r},

such that
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
is the closure of
\Delta\times_{S}(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m},S})^{r}
. Thus, we have
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}=\sum c_{D}\mathcal{D}
, where
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
is defined by the ideal

\mathcal{R}\cap\mathfrak{b}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,t_{r}^{\pm 1}]=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}(I_{m}\cap\mathfrak{b})t_{1}^{-m_{1}}\cdots t_{r}^{-m_{r}}\subseteq\mathcal{R}.

Hence the pullback
D_{0}
of
\mathcal{D}
is the divisorial part of the closed subscheme defined by the ideal

\mathop{\mathrm{in}_{I}}(\mathfrak{b})=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}(I_{m}\cap\mathfrak{b})/(I_{>m}\cap\mathfrak{b})\subseteq\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}}I_{m}/I_{>m}.

If
\alpha_{1},\ldots\alpha_{r}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
, then we have
\mathop{\mathrm{in}_{I}}(\mathfrak{b})=\mathop{\mathrm{in}_{\mathfrak{a}}}(\mathfrak{b})
since
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{a}_{>\lambda}=I_{m}/I_{>m}
for the unique
m\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
with
\langle\alpha,m\rangle=\lambda
. Hence
D_{0}=D_{\alpha}
, so
\Delta_{0}=\Delta_{\alpha}
.

In general, suppose the subspace generated by
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}
has dimension
q<r
over
\mathbb{Q}
, and let
\sigma\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{r}_{\geq 0}
be a rational simplicial cone of dimension
q
with
\alpha\in\sigma
, spanned by
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{q}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
. Then
\sigma
induces a morphism
\mathbb{A}_{S}^{q}\to\mathbb{A}_{S}^{r}
given by

\mathscr{O}_{S}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}]\to\mathscr{O}_{S}[u_{1},\ldots,u_{q}],\quad t_{i}\mapsto u_{1}^{\mu_{1,i}}\cdots u_{q}^{\mu_{q,i}}.

Let
(\mathcal{X}^{\prime},\Delta_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}})=(\mathcal{X},\Delta_{\mathcal{X}})\times_{\mathbb{A}_{S}^{r}}\mathbb{A}_{S}^{q}
, so that
X_{\alpha}=\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\times_{\mathbb{A}^{q}_{S}}0_{S}
. More explicitly,

\mathcal{X}^{\prime}=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}_{S}}\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{q}}J_{n}u_{1}^{-n_{1}}\cdots u_{q}^{-n_{q}},\quad\text{where}\quad J_{n}=\sum_{\langle\mu_{j},m\rangle\geq n_{j}}I_{m}.

Note that if we write
\alpha=\sum_{j=1}^{q}\beta_{j}\mu_{j}
and
\beta=(\beta_{j})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{q}
, then

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}=\sum_{\langle\alpha,m\rangle\geq\lambda}I_{m}=\sum_{\langle\beta,n\rangle\geq\lambda}J_{n}.

Then
(\mathcal{X}^{\prime},\Delta_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}})\to\mathbb{A}^{q}_{S}
is a locally stable family, in particular every component of
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}
dominates
\mathbb{A}^{q}_{S}
. So
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}
is closure of
\Delta\times_{S}(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m},S})^{q}
under

(\mathcal{X}^{\prime},\Delta_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}})\times_{\mathbb{A}_{S}^{q}}(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m},S})^{q}\simeq(X,\Delta)\times_{S}(\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m},S})^{q}.

By our choices,
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{q}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
, hence the pullback
\Delta_{0}^{\prime}
of
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}
to
X_{\alpha}
coincides with the divisor
\Delta_{\alpha}
defined by the initial ideals, as in the paragraphs above. Finally,
\Delta_{0}^{\prime}=\Delta_{0}
since
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
has well-defined pullbacks (see
[
Kol23
, Thm-Def. 4.3]
).

5.
Families with constant local volume

5.1.
Degeneration of the minimizer over a DVR

In this subsection, let
S=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(A)
, where
A
is a DVR, with the generic point
\eta\in S
and the closed point
s\in S
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\pi\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
with
x\in X(S)
be a locally stable family of klt singularities with

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x_{\eta};X_{\eta},\Delta_{\eta})=\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x_{s};X_{s},\Delta_{s}).

Suppose
v^{\mathrm{m}}_{\eta}\in\mathrm{Val}_{X_{\eta},x_{\eta}}
and
v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s}\in\mathrm{Val}_{X_{s},x_{s}}
are minimizers of the normalized volume for
x_{\eta}\in(X_{\eta},\Delta_{\eta})
and
x_{s}\in(X_{s},\Delta_{s})
, respectively, scaled such that
A_{X_{\eta},\Delta_{\eta}}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{\eta})=A_{X_{s},\Delta_{s}}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s})
. Then there exists a locally stable family of Kollár models
f\colon(Y,E)\to(X,\Delta)
at
x
over
S
such that

v^{\mathrm{m}}_{\eta}\in\mathrm{QM}(Y_{\eta},E_{\eta})\quad\text{and}\quad v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s}\in\mathrm{QM}(Y_{s},E_{s}),

and they are identified under the canonical isomorphism
\mathrm{QM}(Y_{\eta},E_{\eta})\simeq\mathrm{QM}(Y_{s},E_{s})
in Definition
4.4
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{\eta})\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{X_{\eta}}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s})\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{X_{s}}
be the ideal sequences associated with
v^{\mathrm{m}}_{\eta}
and
v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s}
, respectively. We may assume that
A_{X_{\eta},\Delta_{\eta}}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{\eta})=A_{X_{s},\Delta_{s}}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s})=1
, so that

\mathrm{lct}(X_{\eta},\Delta_{\eta};\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{\eta}))=\mathrm{lct}(X_{s},\Delta_{s};\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s}))=1,

since they are computed by
v^{\mathrm{m}}_{\eta}
and
v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s}
, respectively, by Lemma
2.18
. Thus

\mathrm{e}_{X_{\eta}}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{\eta}))=\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x_{\eta};X_{\eta},\Delta_{\eta})=\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x_{s};X_{s},\Delta_{s})=\mathrm{e}_{X_{s}}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s})).

Let
I_{\bullet}=j_{*}\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{\eta})\cap\mathscr{O}_{X}
, where
j\colon X_{\eta}\to X
is the inclusion, and
\mathscr{O}_{X}\to j_{*}\mathscr{O}_{X_{\eta}}
is injective since
X_{s}
is a Cartier divisor. It is clear that
\mathscr{O}_{X}/I_{\lambda}
is supported on the section
\{x\}
and torsion-free over
S
for all
\lambda>0
. Since
S
is the spectrum of a DVR, we conclude that
\mathscr{O}_{X}/I_{\lambda}
is flat over
S
. Hence

\mathrm{e}_{X_{\eta}}(I_{\eta,\bullet})=\mathrm{e}_{X_{s}}(I_{s,\bullet}),

where
I_{\eta,\bullet}=I_{\bullet}\mathscr{O}_{X_{\eta}}=\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{\eta})
, and
I_{s,\bullet}=I_{\bullet}\mathscr{O}_{X_{s}}
. Since
\mathrm{lct}
is lower semi-continuous in a family, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x_{s};X_{s},\Delta_{s})

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{lct}(X_{s},\Delta_{s};I_{s,\bullet})^{n}\cdot\mathrm{e}_{X_{s}}(I_{s,\bullet})

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{lct}(X_{\eta},\Delta_{\eta};I_{\eta,\bullet})^{n}\cdot\mathrm{e}_{X_{\eta}}(I_{\eta,\bullet})=\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x_{\eta};X_{\eta},\Delta_{\eta}),

where
n=\dim_{x_{\eta}}(X_{\eta})=\dim_{x_{s}}(X_{s})
. But
\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x_{\eta};X_{\eta},\Delta_{\eta})=\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x_{s};X_{s},\Delta_{s})
, so all the inequalities above must be equalities. Thus by the uniqueness up to saturation in Lemma
2.18
, we have

I_{s,\bullet}\subseteq\widetilde{I}_{s,\bullet}=\mathfrak{a}_{c\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s})

for some
c>0
. In fact,
c=1
since
\mathrm{e}_{X_{s}}(\widetilde{I}_{s,\bullet})=\mathrm{e}_{X_{s}}(I_{s,\bullet})=\mathrm{e}_{X_{s}}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s}))
.

Next, we will verify conditions of Lemma
4.7
. Assume
D
is a relative effective
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X/S
, such that

v^{\mathrm{m}}_{\eta}(D_{\eta})=v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s}(D_{s}).

By Lemma
3.10
, there exists
\delta>0
such that if
\epsilon<\delta/v^{\mathrm{m}}_{\eta}(D_{\eta})=\delta/v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s}(D_{s})
, then
v^{\mathrm{m}}_{\eta}
and
v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s}
are special valuations for
x_{\eta}\in(X_{\eta},\Delta_{\eta}+\epsilon D_{\eta})
and
x_{s}\in(X_{s},\Delta_{s}+\epsilon D_{s})
, respectively. In fact, we can take
\delta=1/n
; see Remark
3.11
.

By Lemma
3.12
,
v^{\mathrm{m}}_{\eta}
and
v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s}
are the unique, up to scaling, real valuations that compute lc thresholds
\mathrm{lct}(X_{\eta},\Delta_{\eta}+\epsilon D_{\eta};\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{\eta}))
and
\mathrm{lct}(X_{s},\Delta_{s}+\epsilon D_{s};\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s}))
, respectively. Then by Lemma
2.16
,
v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s}
is also the unique, up to scaling, real valuation that compute
\mathrm{lct}(X_{s},\Delta_{s}+\epsilon D_{s};I_{s,\bullet})
. Now the theorem follows from Lemma
4.7
.
∎

5.2.
General base schemes

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
S
be a semi-normal scheme, and
\pi\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
with
x\in X(S)
be a locally stable family of klt singularities, such that

s\mapsto\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x_{s};X_{s},\Delta_{s})

is a locally constant function on
S
. Suppose
v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s}\in\mathrm{Val}_{X_{s},x_{s}}
is a minimizer of the normalized volume for
x_{s}\in(X_{s},\Delta_{s})
, scaled such that
A_{X_{s},\Delta_{s}}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s})=1
for all
s\in S
. Then there is an ideal sequence
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{X}
cosupported at
x(S)\subseteq X
such that the following hold:

(1)

\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{a}_{>\lambda}
is flat over
S
for all
\lambda\geq 0
.

(2)

For every
s\in S
,
\mathfrak{a}_{s,\bullet}\coloneqq\{\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\mathscr{O}_{X_{s}}\}_{\lambda}
is the ideal sequence on
X_{s}
associated with
v_{s}^{\mathrm{m}}
.

Proof.

First assume that
S=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(A)
, where
A
is a DVR. By Theorem
5.1
and Lemma
4.11
, we have an ideal sequence
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{X}
such that
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{a}_{>\lambda}
is flat over
S
for every
\lambda\geq 0
,
(X_{0},\Delta_{0})\to S
is a locally stable family of klt singularities, and
\mathfrak{a}_{s,\bullet}
is the ideal sequence associated with
v_{s}^{\mathrm{m}}
for each
s\in S
. Since
v_{s}^{\mathrm{m}}
is a minimizer of the normalized volume function,
x_{0,s}\in(X_{0,s},\Delta_{0,s})
is a K-semistable log Fano cone singularity by
[
LX18
, Thm. 1.1]
.

In general, we may assume that
S
is connected. Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s})\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{X_{s}}
be the ideal sequence associated with
v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s}
. Note that if
\kappa(s)\subseteq K
is field extension, then
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s})\otimes_{\kappa(s)}K
is the ideal sequence associated with the minimizer of the normalized volume function for
x_{K}\in(X_{K},\Delta_{K})
. Suppose
A
is a DVR, and
g\colon T=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(A)\to S
is a morphism, then the base change
(X_{T},\Delta_{T})\to T
with
x_{T}\in X_{T}(T)
is a locally stable family of klt singularities, so that we have an ideal sequence
\mathfrak{a}_{T,\bullet}\subseteq\mathscr{O}_{X_{T}}
, whose fibers are the base changes of the corresponding
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s})
. Therefore, the function

\ell_{\lambda}\colon s\mapsto\mathrm{length}_{\mathscr{O}_{X_{s}}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathscr{O}_{X_{s}}/\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s})}}\right)

is constant under specialization, hence constant, for all
\lambda\geq 0
. Consider the Hilbert scheme

h_{\lambda}\colon\mathrm{Hilb}_{X/S}^{\ell_{\lambda}}\to S

parameterizing closed subschemes of
X
that are finite flat over
S
with length
\ell_{\lambda}
. Then
h_{\lambda}
is separated and of finite type. For each
s\in S
, we have a
\kappa(s)
-point
\sigma_{\lambda}(s)=[\mathscr{O}_{X_{s}}/\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s})]\in h_{\lambda}^{-1}(s)
. If
A
is a DVR, and
g\colon T=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(A)\to S
is a morphism, then by the argument above, there is a lifting
f\colon T\to\mathrm{Hilb}_{X/S}^{\ell_{\lambda}}
of
g
such that
f(t)=\sigma_{\lambda}(g(t))
for every
t\in T
. Thus, by Lemma
A.1
, the set map
s\mapsto\sigma_{\lambda}(s)
underlies a morphism of schemes
\sigma_{\lambda}\colon S\to\mathrm{Hilb}_{X/S}^{\ell_{\lambda}}
, which is a section of
h_{\lambda}
. Hence we get an ideal sequence
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
on
X
such that
\mathfrak{a}_{s,\lambda}=\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s})
for all
s\in S
. Each
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{a}_{>\lambda}
is a finitely generated
\mathscr{O}_{S}
-module with constant rank, hence flat over
S
.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
S
be a regular connected scheme, and
\pi\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
with
x\in X(S)
be a locally stable family of klt singularities such that the function

is constant on

. Suppose
v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s}\in\mathrm{Val}_{X_{s},x_{s}}
is a minimizer of the normalized volume for
x_{s}\in(X_{s},\Delta_{s})
, scaled such that
A_{X_{s},\Delta_{s}}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s})=1
for all
s\in S
. Then there exists a locally stable family of Kollár models
f\colon(Y,E=\sum_{i=1}^{r}E_{i})\to(X,\Delta)
at
x
over
S
such that
v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s}\in\mathrm{QM}(Y_{s},E_{s})
, and the coordinate

s\mapsto(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s}(E_{i,s}))_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{r}

is a constant function on
S

Proof.

By Corollary
5.2
, there is an ideal sequence
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
on
X
cosupported on
x(S)
such that
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}/\mathfrak{a}_{>\lambda}
is flat over
S
for all
\lambda\geq 0
and
\mathfrak{a}_{s,\bullet}=\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}(v^{\mathrm{m}}_{s})
for all
s\in S
. If
S
is local, then we can apply Lemma
4.7
as in the proof of Theorem
5.1

In general, consider the collection
\mathcal{C}
of open subsets
U\subseteq S
such that there exists such a locally stable family of Kollár models over
U

S\in\mathcal{C}

Assume that
U\in\mathcal{C}
is a maximal element, and
f_{U}\colon(Y_{U},E_{U})\to(X_{U},\Delta_{U})
is a locally stable family of Kollár models satisfying the desired conditions. Suppose
U\neq S
, and
t\in S\smallsetminus U
. Let

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
S
be a reduced scheme, and
\pi\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
with
x\in X(S)
be a locally stable family of klt singularities. Then there exists a locally closed stratification
\bigsqcup_{i}S_{i}\to S
satisfying the following: if

is a morphism where
T
is a semi-normal scheme, then the base change
(X_{T},\Delta_{T})\to T
with

admits a degeneration to a locally stable family of K-semistable log Fano cone singularities if and only if

factors through some

, where
S^{\mathit{sn}}_{i}
is the semi-normalization of
S_{i}
.

Here we say a locally stable family of klt singularities admits a degeneration to a locally stable family of K-semistable log Fano cone singularities if the conclusions of Corollary
5.4
hold.

Proof.

Suppose
\pi\colon(X,\Delta)\to S
with
x\in X(S)
degenerates to a locally stable family
(X_{0},\Delta_{0})\to S
of K-semistable log Fano cone singularities, then

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x_{s};X_{s},\Delta_{s})=\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(x_{0,s};X_{0,s},\Delta_{0,s})

for all

by
[
LX18
, Lem. 4.10]
, where
x_{0}\in X_{0}(S)
is the vertex. Hence

is locally constant on

by Lemma
2.21
. The converse holds when

is semi-normal by Corollary
5.4
.

In general, the function

is constructible on

[
Xu20
, Thm. 1.3]
, and lower semi-continuous on

[

, Thm. 1.1]
, and its formation commutes with arbitrary base change since the local volume is preserved by field extensions. Then it suffices to take each

to be a level set of this function, which is a locally closed subscheme with the reduced scheme structure.
∎

5.3.
Example: Unibranch plane curves

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{2}=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\Bbbk[x,y]
be a reduced plane curve, i.e., a reduced closed subscheme of pure dimension one. Assume
C
is unibranch at the origin
0\in\mathbb{A}^{2}
(in particular,
0\in C
). We are interested in the local volume

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(0;\mathbb{A}^{2},\lambda C)

for coefficients
0\leq\lambda<\mathrm{lct}_{0}(\mathbb{A}^{2};C)
.

By
[
LX18
, Thm. 1.1]
, we need to find a quasi-monomial valuation
v\in\mathrm{Val}^{\mathrm{qm}}_{\mathbb{A}^{2},0}
inducing a degeneration of
0\in(\mathbb{A}^{2},\lambda C)
to a K-semistable log Fano cone singularity. We first consider the problem for analytic germs of curves.

Example 5.6
.

Let
\widehat{C}\subseteq\widehat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\Bbbk[\![x,y]\!]
be an integral closed subscheme of dimension
1
. As in
[
Zar06
]
, we may assume that
\widehat{C}
is given by a parametrization

x=t^{a},\quad y=\varphi(t)=t^{b}+\sum_{i>b}c_{i}t^{i}\in\Bbbk[\![t]\!]

such that
b>a>1
and
a\nmid b
. More precisely,
\widehat{C}
is defined by the kernel of the map

\Bbbk[\![x,y]\!]\to\Bbbk[\![t]\!],\quad(x,y)\mapsto(t^{a},\varphi(t)).

We claim that

\mathrm{lct}(\widehat{C})\coloneqq\mathrm{lct}(\widehat{\mathbb{A}}^{2};\widehat{C})=\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}.

The function
f\in\Bbbk[\![x,y]\!]
defining
\widehat{C}
can be written as
f=\prod_{j=0}^{a-1}(y-\varphi(\zeta^{j}x^{1/a}))
, where
\zeta
is a primitive
a
-th root of unity. Then from the Newton polygon of
f
we get
\mathrm{lct}(\widehat{C})\leq\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}
, by
[
KSC04
, Thm. 6.40]
. In the following, we will show that
f
degenerates to
f_{0}=(y^{a/d}-x^{b/d})^{d}
, where
d=\mathrm{gcd}(a,b)
. Hence, if
\widehat{C}_{0}
is defined by
y^{a/d}-x^{b/d}
, then

\mathrm{lct}(\widehat{C})\geq\mathrm{lct}(d\widehat{C}_{0})=\frac{1}{d}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{a/d}+\frac{1}{b/d}}}\right)=\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}

by the lower semi-continuity of lct and
[
KSC04
, Prop. 6.39]
.

Let
v_{\xi}\in\mathrm{Val}_{\widehat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}}
denote the monomial valuation given by weight
\xi=(\mu,\nu)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{>0}
. Then

\mathrm{gr}_{v_{\alpha}}(\Bbbk[\![x,y]\!])=\Bbbk[X,Y],

where we denote the initial terms of
x,y\in\Bbbk[\![x,y]\!]
by
X,Y\in\Bbbk[X,Y]
, respectively, and the ring
\Bbbk[X,Y]
is graded with
\deg(X)=\mu
and
\deg(Y)=\nu
. We will show that
v_{\xi}
induce K-semistable degenerations of
(\widehat{\mathbb{A}}^{2},\lambda\widehat{C})
, for an appropriate weight
\xi
depending on the coefficient
\lambda
. There are two cases:

(1). Let
d=\mathrm{gcd}(a,b)
,
a_{0}=a/d
,
b_{0}=b/d
, and
\xi=(a_{0},b_{0})
. Then the initial term of
f
is

f_{0}=\prod_{j=0}^{a-1}(Y-\zeta^{bj}X^{b/a})=(Y^{a_{0}}-X^{b_{0}})^{d}

where
\zeta\in\Bbbk
is a primitive
a
-th root of unity. Let
C_{0}\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{2}
be the curve defined by
Y^{a_{0}}-X^{b_{0}}
. Then
v_{\xi}
induces a
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-equivariant degeneration of
(\widehat{\mathbb{A}}^{2},\lambda\widehat{C})
to
(\mathbb{A}^{2},\lambda dC_{0})
, where
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
acts on
\mathbb{A}^{2}
with weight
\xi
, such that
C_{0}\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{2}
is invariant. In this case, the polarized log Fano cone singularity
(\mathbb{A}^{2},\lambda dC_{0};\xi)
is an (orbifold) affine cone over the log Fano pair

(E,\Delta_{E})=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-\frac{1}{a_{0}}}}\right)\{0\}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-\frac{1}{b_{0}}}}\right)\{\infty\}+\lambda d\{1\}}}\right)

see
[
Li21
, Ex. 1.7]
. By Fujita–Li’s valuative criterion for K-stability (see
[
Xu25
, Thm. 4.13]
), it is easy to see that
(E,\Delta_{E})
is K-semistable if and only if
\lambda d\geq\frac{1}{a_{0}}-\frac{1}{b_{0}}
, that is,

\lambda\geq\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{b}.

Thus
(\mathbb{A}^{2},\lambda dC_{0};\xi)
is K-semistable if and only if
\lambda\geq\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{b}
. We can also compute

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{\widehat{\mathbb{A}}^{2},\lambda\widehat{C}}(v_{\xi})=ab\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}-\lambda}}\right)^{2}.

(2). Suppose
0\leq\lambda<\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{b}
. Let
\xi=(\mu,1)
where

\mu=1-\lambda a>a/b

Then
v_{\xi}
induces a degeneration of
(\widehat{\mathbb{A}}^{2},\lambda\widehat{C})
to
(\mathbb{A}^{2},\lambda aL;\xi)
, where
L\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{2}
is the line
Y=0
. The pair
(\mathbb{A}^{2},\lambda aL)
is toric, and it is easy to see the toric valuation of weight
\xi=(\mu,1)
minimizes the normalized volume among all toric valuations. Hence
(\mathbb{A}^{2},\lambda aL;\xi)
is K-semistable by
[
LX18
, Thm. 3.5, Prop. 4.21]
. In this case, we have

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{\widehat{\mathbb{A}}^{2},\lambda\widehat{C}}(v_{\xi})=4(1-\lambda a).

Note that the above results only depend on the pair of integers
(a,b)
. They are the first two terms of the
Puiseux characteristic
of the germ
\widehat{C}\subseteq\widehat{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
(see
[
Zar06
, Def. II.3.2]
), and are independent of the choice of analytic local coordinates.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let

Kol13

\hat{v}\in\mathrm{Val}_{\widehat{\mathbb{A}}^{2},0}
inducing a degeneration of
0\in(\widehat{\mathbb{A}}^{2},\lambda\widehat{C})
to a K-semistable log Fano cone singularity
(\mathbb{A}^{2},\Delta_{0};\xi)
as in Example
5.6
. Let
v
denote the restriction of
\hat{v}
on
\Bbbk[x,y]\subseteq\Bbbk[\![x,y]\!]
. Then
v
is a quasi-monomial valuation centered at
0\in\mathbb{A}^{2}
by
[
JM12
, Lem. 3.10]
, and the degeneration of
0\in(\mathbb{A}^{2},\lambda C)
induced by
v
is also
(\mathbb{A}^{2},\Delta_{0};\xi)
.

Hence
v
is the minimizer of the normalized volume of
0\in(\mathbb{A}^{2},\lambda C)
by
[
LX18
, Thm. 1.3]
. That is,

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(0;\mathbb{A}^{2},\lambda C)=\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{\mathbb{A}^{2},\lambda C}(v)=\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{\widehat{\mathbb{A}}^{2},\lambda\widehat{C}}(\hat{v})=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}ab\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}-\lambda}}\right)^{2}&\text{if}\ \frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{b}\leq\lambda<\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}\\
4(1-\lambda a)&\text{if}\ 0\leq\lambda<\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{b}\end{array}}}\right.

where the two cases
\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{b}\leq\lambda<\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}
and
0\leq\lambda<\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{b}
correspond to the two cases in Example
5.6
, and the explicit form of the K-semistable degeneration
(\mathbb{A}^{2},\Delta_{0};\xi)
is given there.

If
C
is smooth at
0
, then we can choose analytic local coordinates such that
\widehat{C}
is the line
y=0
. Thus the second case gives the K-semistable degeneration for all
0\leq\lambda<1
with
a=1
.
∎

We say two plane curves
C,C^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{2}
that are unibranch at
0\in\mathbb{A}^{2}
are
equisingular at
0
if they have the same Puiseux characteristics. See
[
Zar65
]
and
[
Zar68
]
for other equivalent definitions. In particular, if
\Bbbk=\mathbb{C}
, then
C
and
C^{\prime}
are equisingular at
0
if and only if they have the same topological type (see
[
Zar06
, Def. I.1.2]
).

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
S
be a reduced connected scheme of finite type over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
of characteristic
0
. Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{2}_{S}
be a closed subscheme that is flat of pure relative dimension
1
over
S
. Let
0_{S}\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{2}_{S}
denote the origin. Suppose
C_{s}\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\Bbbk}
is a reduced plane curve that is unibranch at
0
for all
s\in S(\Bbbk)
, and
C_{s},C_{s^{\prime}}
are equisingular at
0
for all
s,s^{\prime}\in S(\Bbbk)
. Then the following hold:

(1)

The function
s\mapsto\mathrm{lct}_{0_{s}}(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{s};C_{s})
is constant on
S
.

(2)

Suppose
0\leq\lambda<\mathrm{lct}_{0_{s}}(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{s};C_{s})
for all
s\in S
. Then
(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{S},\lambda C)\to S
is a locally stable family of klt singularities at
0_{S}
, and the function

s\mapsto\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(0_{s};\mathbb{A}^{2}_{s},\lambda C_{s})

is constant on
S
.

(3)

Assume
S
is semi-normal. Then
(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{S},\lambda C)\to S
admits a degeneration to a locally stable family of K-semistable log Fano cone singularities
(X_{0},\Delta_{0})\to S
. Moreover, all fibers of
(X_{0},\Delta_{0})\to S
over
\Bbbk
-points of
S
are isomorphic.

Proof.

(1). Since all
C_{s}
are equisingular at
0
for
s\in S(\Bbbk)
, they have the same Puiseux characteristics
(a,b,\ldots)
. By Lemma
5.7
, the function
s\mapsto\mathrm{lct}_{0_{s}}(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{s};C_{s})
is constant for
s\in S(\Bbbk)
, of value
\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}
. Since
S
is of finite type over
\Bbbk
, the set of
\Bbbk
-points
S(\Bbbk)
is dense in
S
, and every non-empty closed subset of
S
contains a
\Bbbk
-point. Thus, by the lower semi-continuity of lct,
s\mapsto\mathrm{lct}_{0_{s}}(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{s};C_{s})
is constant on
S
.

(2). Let
0\leq\lambda<\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}
. Note that
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{S}\to S
is a smooth family, and
C\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{2}_{S}
is a relative Cartier divisor over
S
. Thus
(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{S},\lambda C)\to S
is a locally stable family of klt singularities at
0_{S}
since every fiber
(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{s},\lambda C_{s})
is klt at
0_{s}
by (1), and
K_{\mathbb{A}^{2}_{S}/S}+\lambda C
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier (see
[
Kol23
, Def. 4.7]
). The function

s\mapsto\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(0_{s};\mathbb{A}^{2}_{s},\lambda C_{s})

is lower semi-continuous by
[
BL21
, Thm. 1.1]
, and is constant on
S(\Bbbk)
with the value given by Lemma
5.7
. Hence it is a constant function on
S
.

(3). The existence of the K-semistable degeneration
(X_{0},\Delta_{0})\to S
follows from Corollary
5.4
, since the family
(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{S},\lambda C)\to S
has constant local volume at
0_{S}
. Moreover, the fiber
(X_{0,s},\Delta_{0,s})
at
s\in S(\Bbbk)
is the K-semistable degeneration of
0\in(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\Bbbk},\lambda C_{s})
. They are all isomorphic by Lemma
5.7
.
∎

Appendix A
A valuative criterion for sections

Lemma A.1
.

Let
S
be a locally Noetherian semi-normal scheme, and
\pi\colon Z\to S
be a morphism that is separated and of finite type. Suppose
|\sigma|\colon|S|\to|Z|
is a map between underlying sets that is a section of the map
|\pi|\colon|Z|\to|S|
, such that for every morphism
g\colon T=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(A)\to S
, where
A
is a DVR, there exists a morphism
f\colon T\to Z
such that
\pi\circ f=g
and
|f|=|\sigma|\circ|g|
. Then there is a section
\sigma\colon S\to Z
of
\pi
whose underlying map between sets is
|\sigma|
.

Moreover, if
S
is locally essentially of finite type over a field
k
, then it suffices to consider
A
that are also essentially of finite type over
k
.

Proof.

We may assume
S
is affine. If
T
is the spectrum of a DVR, we write
\zeta\in T
for the generic point, and
t\in T
for the closed point. For
s\in S
with residue field
\kappa=\kappa(s)
, let
A
be a DVR with residue field
\kappa
, say,
A=\kappa[\varpi]_{(\varpi)}
, and consider the map

T=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(A)\to\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(\kappa)\xrightarrow{s}S.

Then we conclude that
|\sigma|(s)\in|Z_{s}|
is a
\kappa
-point of
Z_{s}
. In particular, it is a closed point of
|Z_{s}|
.

Let
|S^{\prime}|=|\sigma|(|S|)\subseteq|Z|
be the image of
|\sigma|
. Note that
|\sigma|\colon|S|\to|S^{\prime}|
is a bijection, and the restriction of
|\pi|
is the inverse. Suppose
x^{\prime}\in|S^{\prime}|
specializes to
y^{\prime}\in|Z|
, and
y^{\prime}\neq x^{\prime}
. Let
x=\pi(x^{\prime})
and
y=\pi(y^{\prime})
. Since
x^{\prime}=|\sigma|(x)
is a closed point in
Z_{x}
, we have
y\neq x
. Then there exists a DVR
A
with fraction field
K=\kappa(x)
and a morphism
f^{\prime}\colon T=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(A)\to Z
such that
f^{\prime}(\zeta)=x^{\prime}
, and
f^{\prime}(t)=y^{\prime}
(see
[
Stacks
, Tag 00PH]
), and we may assume that
A
is essentially of finite type over
k
if
S
is so. By assumption, there exists a morphism
f\colon T\to Z
such that
\pi\circ f=\pi\circ f^{\prime}
,
f(\zeta)=|\sigma|(x)=x^{\prime}
, and
f(t)=|\sigma|(y)
. Since
T
has function field
K=\kappa(x)
, we must have
f|_{\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(K)}=f^{\prime}|_{\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(K)}
. Since
\pi
is separated, we conclude that
f=f^{\prime}
by the valuative criterion. Thus
y^{\prime}=f(t)=|\sigma|(y)\in|S^{\prime}|
. So
|S^{\prime}|
is stable under specialization.

Let
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n}\in S
be the generic points, and
\eta_{i}^{\prime}=|\sigma|(\eta_{i})\in|S^{\prime}|
. Then
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\overline{\{\eta_{i}^{\prime}\}}\subseteq|S^{\prime}|
. Conversely, if
x^{\prime}\in|S^{\prime}|
, then
x=\pi(x^{\prime})\in S
generalizes to some
\eta_{i}
. Let
A
be a DVR and
g\colon T=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(A)\to S
be a morphism such that
g(\zeta)=\eta_{i}
and
g(t)=x
. By assumption, there is a lifting
f\colon T\to Z
of
g
such that
f(\zeta)=|\sigma|(\eta_{i})=\eta_{i}^{\prime}
and
f(t)=|\sigma|(x)=x^{\prime}
. Hence
x^{\prime}\in\overline{\{\eta_{i}^{\prime}\}}
. Thus we conclude that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\overline{\{\eta_{i}^{\prime}\}}=|S^{\prime}|
. Hence
|S^{\prime}|\subseteq|Z|
is closed. Let
S^{\prime}\subseteq Z
be the corresponding reduced closed subscheme.

By the valuative criterion,
\pi|_{S^{\prime}}\colon S^{\prime}\to S
is proper. Moreover, it is bijective and induces isomorphisms on residue fields. Since
S
is semi-normal, we conclude that
\pi|_{S^{\prime}}\colon S^{\prime}\to S
is an isomorphism. Therefore we get a section
\sigma\colon S\simeq S^{\prime}\hookrightarrow Z
.
∎

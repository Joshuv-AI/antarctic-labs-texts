Global Existence and Large Time Behaviors of Strong Solutions to the Kinetic Cucker–Smale Model Coupled with the Three Dimensional Incompressible Navier–Stokes Equations Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35A01, 35B45, 35D35, 35Q35, 35Q92
Chunyin Jin

Abstract
. In this paper, we study global existence and large time behaviors of strong solutions to the kinetic Cucker–Smale model coupled with the three dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations in the whole space. Using the maximal regularity estimate on the Stokes equations, global-in-time strong solutions to the Cauchy problem of the coupled system are obtained under small initial data regime. The optimal decay rate of the system, in the sense that the energy of the system decays at a rate coinciding with the one corresponding to the underling incompressible Navier–Stokes equations without the coupling term, is also analyzed. Compared with previous results set in spatial-periodic or bounded domains, we circumvent the difficulty caused by unboundedness of the domain using the Fourier splitting method.

Introduction

If an ensemble of particles is immersed in the incompressible fluid, then the dynamics of the system can be modeled by the kinetic Cucker–Smale model coupled with the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations. Throughout the paper,
\nabla
and
\Delta
without indices denote gradient and Laplacian with respect to the spatial variable
x
, respectively. Suppose particles are transported by the fluid velocity. Then the coupled system reads as

(1.1)

\begin{cases}f_{t}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla_{x}f+\nabla_{v}\cdot(L[f]f+(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}-v)f)=0,\\
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{t}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\nabla P=\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$})fdv,\\
\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=0,\end{cases}

subject to the initial data

(1.2)

f|_{t=0}=f_{0},\quad\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|_{t=0}=\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0},

with
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}
satisfying the compatibility condition
\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}=0
. Here
f(t,x,v)
is the particle distribution function in phase space
(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
at the time
t
.

P

L[f]

L[f](t,x,v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varphi(|x-y|)f(t,y,v^{*})(v^{*}-v)dydv^{*},

where
\varphi(\cdot)\in C_{b}^{1}
is a positive non-increasing function, denoting the interaction kernel. Without loss of generality, we postulate that

\max\{|\varphi|,|\varphi^{\prime}|\}\leq 1

in the sequel. The detailed background concerning the system (
1.1
) can be found in

The rest two equations in (

[
34

\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t,x)
is a solution on the whole space, then
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{\lambda}(t,x):=\lambda\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x)
is also a solution. A space whose norm is invariant under this scaling is referred to as a critical space. Much work has been carried out in this setting, mainly based on two types of methods. One is by an energy type estimate, and the other by a fixed point argument. Well-posedness in
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

[
40

[
34
]

[
32

obtained algebraic decay rate for the Cauchy problem, using the Fourier splitting method. Later, this method was extended to some other diffusive equations or with an external force. In this paper, we will generalize it to the coupled kinetic-fluid model.

Recently, the author has started the program to study the Cauchy problem of the kinetic Cucker–Smale model coupled with fluid equations. Local existence and a blowup criterion of strong solutions to the kinetic Cucker–Smale model coupled with the isentropic compressible Navier–Stokes equations were obtained in Jin
[
29
]
, where weighted Sobolev spaces were introduced to overcome difficulties arising from unboundedness of the domain and the coupling term. It was shown that the integrability in time of the spatial
W^{1,\infty}
-norm of
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t,x)
controls blowup of strong solutions. Along this line, Jin
[
30
]
investigated global existence of strong solutions to the kinetic Cucker–Smale model coupled with the Stokes equations, by using the maximal regularity estimate on the Stokes equations. In this paper, we further study the kinetic Cucker-Smale model coupled with the three dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations. It is remarked that there are two aspects different from most previous results: First, the regularity of initial data is minimal in terms of existence of strong solutions to the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations. We control the quantity determining the extendence of local strong solutions by invoking the maximal regularity estimate on the Stokes equations, instead of using high order energy estimates, which requires initial data lying in high order Sobolev spaces; second, large time behaviors of strong solutions are analyzed in the whole space, instead of bounded or spatial-periodic domain. Thus the Poincaré inequality is not applicable. We circumvent this difficulty by ingeniously using the Fourier splitting method.

Motivated by our previous studies
[
29
]
[
30
]
, we introduce the following weighted Sobolev space to overcome difficulty induced from unboundedness of the domain.

H_{\omega}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}):=\bigg\{h(x,v):\ h\in L_{\omega}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}),\\
\nabla_{x}h\in L_{\omega}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}),\ \nabla_{v}h\in L_{\omega}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})\bigg\},

\|h\|_{H_{\omega}^{1}}^{2}:=\|h\|_{L_{\omega}^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla_{x}h\|_{L_{\omega}^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla_{v}h\|_{L_{\omega}^{2}}^{2},

where

\|h\|_{L_{\omega}^{2}}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}h^{2}(x,v)\omega(x)dxdv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\omega(x):=(1+|x|^{2})^{\alpha},\quad\alpha>\frac{3}{2},

similarly for
\|\nabla_{x}h\|_{L_{\omega}^{2}}
and
\|\nabla_{v}h\|_{L_{\omega}^{2}}
. The following simplified notations for homogeneous Sobolev Spaces are used in this paper.

D^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):=\left\{u\in L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):\ \nabla u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\right\},

D^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):=\left\{u\in L_{loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):\ \nabla^{2}u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\right\},

D^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):=\left\{u\in L_{loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):\ \nabla^{2}u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\right\},\quad 1\leq p\leq\infty.

Next we give the definition of strong solutions to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
0<T<\infty
.
(f(t,x,v),\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t,x),\nabla P(t,x))
is said to be a strong solution to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) in
[0,T]
, if

\displaystyle f(t,x,v)\in C([0,T];H_{\omega}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})),

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t,x)\in C([0,T];H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;D^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{t}(t,x)\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),

\displaystyle\nabla P(t,x)\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),

and

\begin{gathered}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}f\phi_{t}dxdvdt+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}fv\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi dxdvdt\\
+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\Big(L[f]+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}-v\Big)\cdot\nabla_{v}\phi fdxdvdt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}f_{0}\phi(0)dxdv=0,\end{gathered}

for all
\phi(t,x,v)\in C_{0}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
;

\begin{gathered}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\boldsymbol{\psi}_{t}dxdt+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}:\nabla\boldsymbol{\psi}dxdt-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\boldsymbol{\psi}dxdt\\
+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}(v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$})\cdot\boldsymbol{\psi}fdxdvdt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\cdot\boldsymbol{\psi}(0)dx=0,\end{gathered}

for all
\boldsymbol{\psi}(t,x)\in C_{0,\sigma}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
, where

C_{0,\sigma}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}):=\bigg\{\boldsymbol{\psi}(t,x):\ \boldsymbol{\psi}(t,x)\in C_{0}^{\infty}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}),\ \nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\psi}=0\bigg\}.

We define the particle density as

\rho(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(t,x,v)dv,

with the initial value
\rho_{0}(x):=\rho(0,x)
. The energy of the system (
1.1
) is defined as

E(t):=\frac{1}{2}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(t,x,v)|v|^{2}dxdv,

with the initial energy
E_{0}:=E(0)
. If the initial fluid velocity
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}(x)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, it follows from the Sobolev embedding that

\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{3}}\leq C\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{H^{1}},\quad\text{for some constant $C>0$.}

Denote by
B(R_{0})
the ball centered at the origin with a radius
R_{0}
. The bound of
v
-support of
f(t,x,v)
at the time
t
is defined as

R(t):=\sup\Big\{|v|:\ v\in\text{supp$f(t,x,\cdot)$ for a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$}\Big\}.

If
f_{0}(x,v)\in H_{\omega}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\text{supp}_{v}f_{0}(x,\cdot)\subseteq B(R_{0})
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, then we have

\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{1}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{0}(x,v)dxdv\leq CR_{0}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|f_{0}\|_{L_{\omega}^{2}},

and

\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}=\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{0}(x,v)dv\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq CR_{0}^{3}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},

for some constant
C>0
. In terms of the above notations, the results of this paper are summarized as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
0<R_{0}<\infty
. Assume that
f_{0}(x,v)\geq 0
,
f_{0}(x,v)\in H_{\omega}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
, and
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}(x)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
with
\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}=0
and the
v
-support of
f_{0}(x,v)
satisfying

\text{supp}_{v}f_{0}(x,\cdot)\subseteq B(R_{0})\quad\text{for a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$}.

If there is a sufficiently small constant
\varepsilon_{0}
such that

C\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{3}}^{6}+(1+\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}})E_{0}\leq\varepsilon_{0}

for some constant
C>0
independent of the initial data, then the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) admits a unique global-in-time strong solution in the sense of Definition
1.1
. Moreover, it holds that

\displaystyle(1)\ \|f(t)\|_{H_{\omega}^{1}}\leq\|f_{0}\|_{H_{\omega}^{1}}\exp\Big(C(1+t)\Big)\quad\text{and}\quad R(t)\leq R_{0}+C,

\displaystyle\text{where $C:=C(R_{0},E_{0},\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{H^{1}},\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{3}})$};

\displaystyle(2)\ \|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\bigg(\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\Big(1+\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg)\exp\left(C(\varepsilon_{0}+\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}})\right),

\displaystyle\text{for some constant $C>0$ independent of the initial data};

\displaystyle(3)\ \lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2}fdxdv=0.

If
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}(x)
is integrable, except for the conditions in Theorem
1.1
, then the quantitative decay rate of the system can be obtained.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume initial data satisfy the conditions in Theorem
1.1
. If
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}(x)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
in addition, then there holds

\displaystyle(1)\ E(t)\leq C\Big(1+t\Big)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\Big(1+t\Big)^{-\frac{3}{4}};

\displaystyle(2)\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2}fdxdv\leq C\Big(1+t\Big)^{-\frac{3}{2}},

where
C:=C(E_{0},\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{1}})
.

Remark 1.1
.

The transport term in
\eqref{eq-cs-ns}_{1}
is taken as
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla_{x}f
, in order to derive a uniform-in-time bound on the particle density
\rho(t,x)
, which plays a crucial role in the analysis of global existence and large time behaviors of strong solutions. As for the usual transport term
v\cdot\nabla_{x}f
, particle concentration in phase space seems unavoidable. Whether Theorem
1.1
holds or not in this setting is still unknown, due to lack of a uniform-in-time bound on the particle density.

Remark 1.2
.

The regularity of
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}
is optimal in terms of existence of strong solutions to the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations. The small global strong solution is obtained in the critical
L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
space, based on an energy-type estimate. As is well-known, the best critical space to date for existence of small global solutions to the three dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations is
BMO^{-1}
. Whether or not the same type results hold for small
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}
in
BMO^{-1}
is still a problem deserving our further endeavor.

Remark 1.3
.

Compared with decay results in the three dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations, the decay rates in Theorem
1.2
are optimal. Since the smallness assumption is only used to derive global strong solutions, which does not play a role in the analysis of large time behaviors, we conjecture that Theorem
1.2
also holds for large weak solutions to the system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).

Based on our analysis on local strong solutions to the coupled system, it suffices to prove
\int_{0}^{T}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}dt<\infty
for all
T>0
, to extend local solutions to infinity. This estimate can be obtained by interpolation, once we have the estimate on
\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{s}(0,T;L^{q})}
,
1<s<2
,
3<q<6
.
In fact, the estimate of
\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{s}(0,T;L^{q})}
can be obtained, by employing the maximal regularity estimate on the Stokes equations. Nevertheless, the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations contain the convective term
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}
. To deal with the convective term, we need the estimate on
\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}
. However, the estimate on
\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}
in the three dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations is still open for large initial data. Thus this estimate by far can only be hoped for under small initial data regime. In this paper, we generalize the small global existence result in the critical
L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
space for the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations to the coupled system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
). Using the splitting approach, we decompose
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}+\mbox{\boldmath$w$}
, with
\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}
and
w
determined by the following equations:

\begin{cases}\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{t}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}=\Delta\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}},\\
\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}|_{t=0}=\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0},\end{cases}

and

\begin{cases}\mbox{\boldmath$w$}_{t}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$w$}+\nabla P=\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$w$}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$})fdv,\\
\mbox{\boldmath$w$}|_{t=0}=0,\end{cases}

respectively. In the process of the
L^{3}
-type energy estimate, the estimate on
\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}
is standard under the incompressible condition
\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=0
. The difficulty mainly arises from the term
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}3|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\cdot\nabla Pdx
, where the pressure
P
is determined by the following equation:

\Delta P=-\nabla\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$})+\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$h$},\quad\text{with $\mbox{\boldmath$h$}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$})fdv$.}

Appearance of the additional term
\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$h$}
disables us to use integration by parts to estimate
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}3|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\cdot\nabla Pdx
. We overcome this difficulty by decomposing
P=P_{1}+P_{2}
, with
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
governed by

\Delta P_{1}=-\nabla\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$})\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta P_{2}=\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$h$},

respectively. Then it holds that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}3|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\cdot\nabla Pdx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}3|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\cdot\nabla P_{1}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}3|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\cdot\nabla P_{2}dx.

The first term in the right-hand side of the above equation is dealt with similarly as in the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations, i.e., using integration by parts and the inequality

\|P_{1}\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2p}}^{2}\quad\text{for some constant $C>0$ and $1<p<\infty$}.

The second term is estimated directly, using Hölder’s inequality and the fact that

\|\nabla P_{2}\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\mbox{\boldmath$h$}\|_{L^{p}}\quad\text{for some constant $C>0$ and $1<p<\infty$}.

Having the uniform-in-time bound on the particle density
\rho(t,x)
, along with the elementary energy estimate on the system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), the quantity
\int_{0}^{T}\|\mbox{\boldmath$h$}(t)\|_{L^{p}}^{2}dt
for
1\leq p\leq 2
can be controlled by the initial energy
E_{0}
times
1+\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
for all
T>0
. Then we can obtain the Serrin condition
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\in L^{3}(0,T;L^{9})
for all
T>0
, under some smallness assumption on initial data, which leads to estimate on
\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}
for all
T>0
.

In analysis of large time behaviors to the coupled system, we use the Fourier splitting method. Nevertheless, we cannot directly apply this method to the fluid equations, since

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}(v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$})\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}fdxdv,

and we do not have enough decay estimates on the coupling term
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}(v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$})\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}fdxdv
. Combining the kinetic equation, we deduce that

\frac{d}{dt}E(t)+\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2}fdxdv\leq 0.

Our main observation is that

E(t)\leq(1+\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}})\left(\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2}fdxdv\right),

under uniform-in-time bound on the particle density
\rho(t,x)
. Then we use the Fourier splitting method to derive the inequality

\frac{d}{dt}E(t)+\frac{c^{2}}{(t+c^{2})(1+\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}})}E(t)\leq\frac{c^{2}}{t+c^{2}}\int_{|\xi|\leq c(t+c^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}}|\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi,

where the positive constant
c
satisfies
c^{2}=3(1+\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}})
. However, due to appearance of the coupling term, we are still faced with some difficulties in estimating the low frequency of
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t,x)
. Fortunately, these difficulties can be overcome by a subtle bootstrap argument. It follows from the elementary energy estimate that
E(t)\leq E_{0}
for all
t\geq 0
. Therefore, we can make the bootstrap assumption that

E(t)<K(1+t)^{-\frac{9}{8}}\quad\text{in $[0,T)$}

for some suitably large constants
K
and
T
. Under this assumption, the coupling term can be controlled by using a time-weighted energy estimate on the coupled system
(
1.1
)-(
1.2
). Then we can improve estimate on the low frequency of
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t,x)
, which leads to a better decay rate on
E(t)
, i.e.,

E(t)\leq CK(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}+CK^{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{2}}\quad\text{in $[0,T)$}

for some constant
C>0
, depending only on
E_{0}
,
\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
and
\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
; see details in Sect.
5
. Taking
K
suitably large, we can prove that the supremum among all the above
T
is
\infty
by continuity argument. Thus we can improve the bootstrap assumption and show that

E(t)\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\quad\text{for all $t\geq 0$},

where
C:=C(E_{0},\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{1}})
. Then the decay rates of
\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2}fdxdv
follow easily.
The proof is subtle and robust. We believe that the idea can be also used in some other coupled models.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Sect.
2
, we present some preliminary results used in the subsequent analysis. In Sect.
3
, we construct local strong solutions to the coupled model by iteration on the linearized system. In Sect.
4
, we derive some a priori estimates on classical solutions, which are used to extend local strong solutions. Sect.
5
is devoted to the proof of our theorems.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we present some preliminaries that will be used in the following analysis. The first result concerns the well-posedness and estimates on strong solutions to the kinetic Cucker–Smale model with a coupling term, provided that the fluid velocity is given in some function space. It will be used in the construction of local strong solutions to the system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
). While the second result is the maximal regularity estimate on the Stokes equations. This estimate is crucially used in the estimate of
\int_{0}^{T}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}dt<\infty
for all

2.1.
The kinetic Cucker–Smale model with a coupling term

When an ensemble of particles is immersed in a fluid, due to influence of ambient fluid, the alignment term
L[f]f
is replaced by
L[f]f+(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}-v)f
, in order for conservation of mass and momentum of the system. In this paper, we suppose particles are transported by the fluid velocity
u
. Then the transport term in the kinetic equation becomes
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla_{x}f
. Given
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t,x)\in C([0,T];H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;D^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{s}(0,T;D^{2,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
,
1<s<2
,
3<q<6
, with
\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=0
, it follows from the Sobolev imbedding that
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t,x)\in L^{1}(0,T;W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
.
Consider

(2.1)

\begin{cases}f_{t}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla_{x}f+\nabla_{v}\cdot(L[f]f+(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}-v)f)=0,\\
f|_{t=0}=f_{0}(x,v),\end{cases}

in
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Define

a(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\varphi(|x-y|)f(t,y,v^{*})dydv^{*},

\mathbf{b}(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\varphi(|x-y|)f(t,y,v^{*})v^{*}dydv^{*}.

Recall that the bound of
v
-support of
f(t,x,v)
at the time
t
is defined as

R(t):=\sup\Big\{|v|:\ v\in\text{supp$f(t,x,\cdot)$ for a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$}\Big\}.

Following the line of proof of Proposition 2.1 in
[
29
]
, we have the following results.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
0<R_{0},T<\infty
. Assume
f_{0}(x,v)\geq 0
,
f_{0}(x,v)\in H_{\omega}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
, and
\text{supp}_{v}f_{0}(x,\cdot)\subseteq B(R_{0})
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Given
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t,x)\in C([0,T];H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;D^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{s}(0,T;D^{2,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
,
1<s<2
,
3<q<6
, with
\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=0
, there exists a unique non-negative strong solution
f(t,x,v)\in C([0,T];H_{\omega}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}))
to (
2.1
). Moreover,

\displaystyle(1)

\displaystyle R(t)\leq R_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}(\|\mathbf{b}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}})d\tau,\quad 0\leq t\leq T;

\displaystyle(2)

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{H_{\omega}^{1}}\leq\|f_{0}\|_{H_{\omega}^{1}}\exp\left(C\int_{0}^{t}\Big(1+R(\tau)+\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\tau)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\Big)d\tau\right),\quad 0\leq t\leq T,

where
C:=C(\|\varphi\|_{C^{1}},\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}})
.

From
f_{0}(x,v)\in H_{\omega}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we deduce that

(2.2)

\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}f_{0}(x,v)\omega^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\omega^{-\frac{1}{2}}(x)dxdv\leq C(R_{0})\|f_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{\omega}}.

Integrating
\eqref{eq-kine-cs}_{1}
over
[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}

,0<t\leq T
, gives

(2.3)

\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}}=\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}.

Without loss of generality, we suppose
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}=1
. Using Cauchy’s inequality, we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle|\mathbf{b}(t,x)|\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}f(t,y,v^{*})dydv^{*}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}f(t,y,v^{*})|v^{*}|^{2}dydv^{*}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}f(t,y,v^{*})|v^{*}|^{2}dydv^{*}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad 0\leq t\leq T.

Multiplying
\eqref{eq-kine-cs}_{1}
by
|v|^{2}
, we obtain

(2.5)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(f|v|^{2})+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla_{x}(f|v|^{2})+\nabla_{v}\cdot\Big(L[f]f|v|^{2}+(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}-v)f|v|^{2}\Big)=2fL[f]\cdot v+2fv\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}-v).

Integrating (
2.5
) over
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
leads to

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}f|v|^{2}dxdv=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{12}}\varphi(|x-y|)f(t,x,v)f(t,y,v^{*})|v^{*}-v|^{2}dydv^{*}dxdv

\displaystyle-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}f|v|^{2}dxdv+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}fvdv\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}f|v|^{2}dxdv+2\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|f\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}f|v|^{2}dxdv\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Solving the above Gronwall’s inequality yields

(2.6)

\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}f(t,x,v)|v|^{2}dxdv\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq R_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}d\tau,\quad 0\leq t\leq T.

Combining (
2.4
) and (
2.6
) with Proposition
2.1
(1), we can further estimate
R(t)
as follows:

(2.7)

R(t)\leq\left(R_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}d\tau\right)(1+t),\quad 0\leq t\leq T.

2.2.
Maximal regularity estimate on the Stokes equations

Our analysis to the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations is based on the global-in-time maximal
L^{p}-L^{q}
estimates for the Stokes equations. By global-in-time maximal
L^{p}-L^{q}
estimates, we mean that for given
\mbox{\boldmath$g$}(t,x)\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, there exists a unique solution

(\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\nabla P)
to the Stokes equations

(2.8)

\begin{cases}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{t}+\nabla P=\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\mbox{\boldmath$g$},\\
\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=0,\\
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|_{t=0}=0,\end{cases}

satisfying

\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{t}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}+\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}+\|\nabla P\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})}\leq C\|\mbox{\boldmath$g$}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})},

for some constant
C>0
independent of
T
and
g
. Pioneering results on maximal
L^{p}-L^{p}
estimates are due to Solonnikov
[
42
]
. Later, by proving boundedness of the imaginary powers of the Stokes operator, and using the Dore–Venni Theorem
[
15
]
, Giga–Sohr
[
19
]
extended these estimates to the mixed
L^{p}-L^{q}
setting, with the above constant
C
improved to be independent of
T
, i.e., global in time. For modern approach to the maximal regularity estimates from the point of view of evolution equations, we refer the reader to
[
39
]
. In terms of the regularity of
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t,x)
, the space of initial data
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}(x)
, as the trace space of
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t,x)
, can be characterized by the real interpolation space

\Big(W^{2,q},L^{q}\Big)_{\frac{1}{s},s}=B_{q,s}^{2-\frac{2}{s}}.

For general initial data in the Besov space
B_{q,s}^{2-\frac{2}{s}}
, it is proved in
[
19
]
that

\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{t}\|_{L^{s}(0,T;L^{q})}+\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{s}(0,T;L^{q})}+\|\nabla P\|_{L^{s}(0,T;L^{q})}\leq C\bigg(\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{B_{q,s}^{2-\frac{2}{s}}}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$g$}\|_{L^{s}(0,T;L^{q})}\bigg).

In this paper, we suppose

(2.9)

1<s<2,\quad 3<q<6,\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{2}{s}+\frac{3}{q}>\frac{5}{2}.

It follows from the Sobolev embedding that

(2.10)

\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{B_{q,s}^{2-\frac{2}{s}}}\leq C\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{H^{1}},\quad\text{for some constant $C>0$.}

Combining Theorem 2.8 in
[
19
]
with (
2.10
), we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
0<T\leq\infty
,
1<s<2
,
3<q<6
,
\frac{2}{s}+\frac{3}{q}>\frac{5}{2}
. Given
\mbox{\boldmath$g$}(t,x)\in L^{s}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, there exists a unique solution

(\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\nabla P)
to the Stokes equations

(2.11)

\begin{cases}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{t}+\nabla P=\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\mbox{\boldmath$g$},\\
\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=0,\\
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|_{t=0}=\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0},\end{cases}

such that for some constant
C>0
independent of
T
and
g
,

\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{t}\|_{L^{s}(0,T;L^{q})}+\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{s}(0,T;L^{q})}+\|\nabla P\|_{L^{s}(0,T;L^{q})}\leq C\Big(\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{H^{1}}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$g$}\|_{L^{s}(0,T;L^{q})}\Big).

If
\mbox{\boldmath$g$}(t,x)\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, it holds similarly that

\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{t}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}+\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}+\|\nabla P\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}\leq C\Big(\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{H^{1}}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$g$}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}\Big),

for some constant
C>0
independent of
T
and
g
.

3.
Construction of Local Strong Solutions

\displaystyle C\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1})f^{n+1}dv\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle C(R_{0})\|f^{n+1}\|_{L_{\omega}^{2}}^{2}+C(R_{0})\|f^{n+1}\|_{L_{\omega}^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(R_{0})\|f^{n+1}\|_{L_{\omega}^{2}}^{2}+C(R_{0})\|f^{n+1}\|_{L_{\omega}^{2}}^{4}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

and

(3.13)

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}f^{n+1}dvdx\leq

\displaystyle C(R_{0})\|f^{n+1}\|_{L_{\omega}^{2}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(R_{0})\|f^{n+1}\|_{L_{\omega}^{2}}^{2}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where we have used the Sobolev inequality

\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

and the elliptic estimate

\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}.

Substituting (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) into (
3.11
) leads to

(3.14)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{t}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(C+C\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+C(R_{0})\|f^{n+1}\|_{L_{\omega}^{2}}^{4}\Big)\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+C(R_{0})\|f^{n+1}\|_{L_{\omega}^{2}}^{2}

for
t\in[0,T_{2}]
. Solving the above Gronwall’s inequality gives

(3.15)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{2}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T_{2}}\|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt+\int_{0}^{T_{2}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{t}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T_{2}}C(R_{0})\|f^{n+1}\|_{L_{\omega}^{2}}^{2}dt\right)\exp\left(\int_{0}^{T_{2}}\Big(C+C\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+C(R_{0})\|f^{n+1}\|_{L_{\omega}^{2}}^{4}\Big)dt\right)

Take
T_{2}:=T_{2}(R_{0},\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{H^{1}},\|f_{0}\|_{H_{\omega}^{1}})
suitably small such that

(3.16)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{2}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}(t)\|_{H^{1}}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}_{t}\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{2};L^{2})}+\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{2};L^{2})}\leq K_{0}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{H^{1}}.

Take
T_{3}\leq T_{2}
. Applying Proposition
2.2
to
\eqref{eq-cs-s-appro}_{2}-\eqref{eq-cs-s-appro}_{3}
results in

(3.17)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{s}(0,T_{3};L^{q})}\leq

\displaystyle C\Bigg(\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{H^{1}}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{s}(0,T_{3};L^{q})}

\displaystyle+\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1})f^{n+1}dv\right\|_{L^{s}(0,T_{3};L^{q})}\Bigg).

The last two terms in the right-hand side of (
3.17
) are estimated as follows.

(3.18)

\displaystyle C\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{s}(0,T_{3};L^{q})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{0}^{T_{3}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{s}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{s(1-\theta)}\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{q}}^{s\theta}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{0}^{T_{3}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{s}{1-\theta}}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{s}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{s}(0,T_{3};L^{q})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{3}}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{T_{3}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{s}{1-\theta}}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{s}(0,T_{3};L^{q})},

where we have used the following Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,

(3.19)

\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{q}}\leq C\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\theta}\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{q}}^{\theta}\quad\text{with $1-\frac{3}{q}=-\frac{1-\theta}{2}+\theta\left(2-\frac{3}{q}\right)$}.

Using (
3.7
) and the Sobolev inequality, we proceed to estimate the last term in the right-hand side of (
3.17
).

(3.20)

\displaystyle C\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1})f^{n+1}dv\right\|_{L^{s}(0,T_{3};L^{q})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(R_{0})\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{3}}\|f^{n+1}(t)\|_{H_{\omega}^{1}}T_{3}^{\frac{1}{s}}+C(R_{0})\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{3}}\|f^{n+1}(t)\|_{H_{\omega}^{1}}\left(\int_{0}^{T_{3}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{s}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}.

Substituting (
3.18
) and (
3.20
) into (
3.17
) gives

(3.21)

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{s}(0,T_{3};L^{q})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{H^{1}}+C\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{3}}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{T_{3}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{s}{1-\theta}}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}

\displaystyle+C(R_{0})\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{3}}\|f^{n+1}(t)\|_{H_{\omega}^{1}}T_{3}^{\frac{1}{s}}+C(R_{0})\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{3}}\|f^{n+1}(t)\|_{H_{\omega}^{1}}\left(\int_{0}^{T_{3}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{s}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}.

It is easy to see from (
2.9
) and (
3.19
) that

\frac{s}{1-\theta}=\frac{(5q-6)s}{2q}<2\quad\text{and}\quad 1<s<2.

Using (
3.5
), (
3.7
) and (
3.16
), we take
T_{3}:=T_{3}(R_{0},\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{H^{1}},\|f_{0}\|_{H_{\omega}^{1}})
suitably small such that

(3.22)

\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{s}(0,T_{3};L^{q})}\leq K_{0}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{H^{1}}.

Define
T_{*}:=\min\{T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}\}
. Adding (
3.22
) to (
3.16
) results in

(3.23)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{*}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}(t)\|_{H^{1}}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}_{t}\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{*};L^{2})}+\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}(0,T_{*};L^{2})}+\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}\|_{L^{s}(0,T_{*};L^{q})}\leq 2K_{0}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{H^{1}}.

From (
3.4
), we know (
3.5
) holds for
n=0
. Thus, we prove by induction that (
3.5
) holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Step 2. Convergence of approximate aolutions

Define

\overline{f}^{n+1}:=f^{n+1}-f^{n},\quad\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}:=\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n},\quad\overline{P}^{n+1}:=P^{n+1}-P^{n}.

It follows from (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) that

(3.24)

\begin{cases}\overline{f}^{n+1}_{t}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\cdot\nabla_{x}\overline{f}^{n+1}+\nabla_{v}\cdot\Big[L[f^{n+1}]\overline{f}^{n+1}+(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}-v)\overline{f}^{n+1}\Big]\\
\qquad+\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{n}+\nabla_{v}\cdot\Big[L[\overline{f}^{n+1}]f^{n}+f^{n}\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}\Big]=0,\\
\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}_{t}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\cdot\nabla\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}+\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}+\nabla\overline{P}^{n+1}\\
\qquad=\Delta\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f^{n+1}\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}dv+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n})\overline{f}^{n+1}dv,\\
\nabla\cdot\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}=0,\end{cases}

with

(3.25)

\overline{f}^{n+1}|_{t=0}=0,\quad\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}|_{t=0}=0.

Taking the inner product of
\eqref{eq-cs-s-appro-dif}_{2}
with
\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}
, we deduce that for
t\in[0,T_{*}]
,

(3.26)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\cdot\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\overline{f}^{n+1}(v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n})dv\cdot\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\|_{L^{3}}\|\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}+C\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\overline{f}^{n+1}vdv\right\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}\|\nabla\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+C\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\overline{f}^{n+1}dv\right\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\|\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(R_{0})\Big(1+\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\Big)\left\|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{24}\|\nabla\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

that is,

(3.27)

\begin{gathered}\frac{d}{dt}\|\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\|\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
+C(R_{0})\Big(1+\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\Big)\left\|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}^{2}+\frac{1}{12}\|\nabla\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{gathered}

Multiplying
\eqref{eq-cs-s-appro-dif}_{1}
by
\frac{6}{5}\left|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right|^{\frac{1}{5}}\text{sgn}\overline{f}^{n+1}
leads to

(3.28)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right|^{\frac{6}{5}}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\cdot\nabla_{x}\left|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right|^{\frac{6}{5}}+\nabla_{v}\cdot\bigg[L[f^{n+1}]\left|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right|^{\frac{6}{5}}+(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}-v)\left|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right|^{\frac{6}{5}}\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{5}\nabla_{v}\cdot L[f^{n+1}]\left|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right|^{\frac{6}{5}}+\frac{3}{5}\left|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right|^{\frac{6}{5}}

\displaystyle-\frac{6}{5}\left|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right|^{\frac{1}{5}}\text{sgn}\overline{f}^{n+1}\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{n}

\displaystyle-\frac{6}{5}\left|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right|^{\frac{1}{5}}\text{sgn}\overline{f}^{n+1}\nabla_{v}\cdot\bigg(L[\overline{f}^{n+1}]f^{n}+\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}f^{n}\bigg).

Integrating (
3.28
) over
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
gives

(3.29)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}^{\frac{6}{5}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Bigg(-\frac{1}{5}\nabla_{v}\cdot L[f^{n+1}]\left|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right|^{\frac{6}{5}}+\frac{3}{5}\left|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right|^{\frac{6}{5}}\Bigg)dxdv

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{6}{5}\left|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right|^{\frac{1}{5}}\text{sgn}\overline{f}^{n+1}\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{n}dxdv

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{6}{5}\left|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right|^{\frac{1}{5}}\text{sgn}\overline{f}^{n+1}\nabla_{v}\cdot\bigg(L[\overline{f}^{n+1}]f^{n}+\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}f^{n}\bigg)dxdv

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}N_{i}.

For
t\in[0,T_{*}]
, we estimate each
N_{i}

(i=1,2,3)
as follows.

\displaystyle N_{1}:=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Bigg(-\frac{1}{5}\nabla_{v}\cdot L[f^{n+1}]\left|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right|^{\frac{6}{5}}+\frac{3}{5}\left|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right|^{\frac{6}{5}}\Bigg)dxdv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}^{\frac{6}{5}};

\displaystyle N_{2}:=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{6}{5}\left|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right|^{\frac{1}{5}}\text{sgn}\overline{f}^{n+1}\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{n}dxdv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}^{\frac{1}{5}}\left\|\nabla_{x}f^{n}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}L_{v}^{\frac{6}{5}}}\|\nabla\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(R_{0})\|\nabla_{x}f^{n}\|_{L_{\omega}^{2}}\left\|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}^{\frac{1}{5}}\|\nabla\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}\|_{L^{2}};

\displaystyle N_{3}:=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{6}{5}\left|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right|^{\frac{1}{5}}\text{sgn}\overline{f}^{n+1}\nabla_{v}\cdot\bigg(L[\overline{f}^{n+1}]f^{n}+\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}f^{n}\bigg)dxdv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}^{\frac{1}{5}}\left\|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{1}}\left\|{f}^{n}\right\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}

\displaystyle+C(R_{0})\left\|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}^{\frac{1}{5}}\left\|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{1}}\left\|\nabla_{v}{f}^{n}\right\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}

\displaystyle+C\left\|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}^{\frac{1}{5}}\left\|\nabla_{v}f^{n}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}L_{v}^{\frac{6}{5}}}\|\nabla\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(R_{0})\|f^{n}\|_{H_{\omega}^{1}}\left\|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}^{\frac{1}{5}}\left\|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{1}}+C(R_{0})\|\nabla_{v}f^{n}\|_{L_{\omega}^{2}}\left\|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}^{\frac{1}{5}}\|\nabla\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}.

In the estimate of
N_{2}
and
N_{3}
, we have used the following inequalities.

\displaystyle\left\|f^{n}\right\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}\leq

\displaystyle C(R_{0})\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|f^{n}|^{2}(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{2\alpha}{3}}dxdv\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(1+|x|^{2})^{-\frac{\alpha}{3}}\|_{L^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(R_{0})\|f^{n}\|_{L_{\omega}^{2}};

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{x}f^{n}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}L_{v}^{\frac{6}{5}}}\leq

\displaystyle C(R_{0})\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla_{x}f^{n}|^{2}(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{3}}dxdv\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(1+|x|^{2})^{-\frac{\alpha}{6}}\|_{L^{6}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(R_{0})\|\nabla_{x}f^{n}\|_{L_{\omega}^{2}}.

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{v}{f}^{n}\right\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}
and
\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{v}f^{n}\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}L_{v}^{\frac{6}{5}}}
are estimated similarly as above.
Substituting the estimates on
N_{i}

(i=1,2,3)
into (
3.29
), we deduce that for
t\in[0,T_{*}]
,

(3.30)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle C(R_{0})\bigg(1+\|f^{n}\|_{H_{\omega}^{1}}^{2}\bigg)\left\|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}^{2}+\left\|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{12}\|\nabla\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Similarly, we have for
t\in[0,T_{*}]
,

(3.31)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle C(R_{0})\bigg(1+\|f^{n}\|_{H_{\omega}^{1}}^{2}\bigg)\left\|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{12}\|\nabla\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Define

F^{n+1}(t):=\|\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{\frac{6}{5}}}^{2}+\left\|\overline{f}^{n+1}\right\|_{L^{1}}^{2}.

Combining (
3.27
), (
3.30
), and (
3.31
) , we deduce that for
t\in[0,T_{*}]
,

(3.32)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}F^{n+1}+\|\nabla\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(R_{0})\bigg(1+\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|f^{n}\|_{H_{\omega}^{1}}^{2}+\|\nabla{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\bigg)F^{n+1}+\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Solving the above Gronwall inequality in
[0,T_{0}]
for
0<T_{0}\leq T_{*}
, we obtain

(3.33)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}F^{n+1}(t)+\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\|\nabla\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq\frac{A(T_{0})}{4}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\|\nabla\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt,

where
A(T_{0})
is given by

A(T_{0}):=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{T_{0}}C(R_{0})\bigg(1+\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|f^{n}\|_{H_{\omega}^{1}}^{2}+\|\nabla{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\bigg)dt\right).

Using the uniform bound on approximate solutions, we take
T_{0}:=T_{0}(R_{0},\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{H^{1}},\|f_{0}\|_{H_{\omega}^{1}})
suitably small, so that

A(T_{0})\leq 2.

Thus, we have

(3.34)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}F^{n+1}(t)+\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\|\nabla\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\|\nabla\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt.

Summing (
3.34
) over all
n\in\mathbb{N}
yields

(3.35)

\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}F^{n}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\|\nabla\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\|\nabla\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt.

We deduce from (
3.35
) that there exists
(f,\mbox{\boldmath$u$})
such that

(3.36)

\displaystyle f^{n}\to f,\quad\text{in $C([0,T_{0}];L^{1})$, as $n\to\infty$};

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\to\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\quad\text{in $C(0,T_{0};L^{2})$, as $n\to\infty$};

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}\to\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\quad\text{in $L^{2}(0,T_{0};D^{1})$, as $n\to\infty$}.

From (
3.36
), it is easy to show that
(f,\mbox{\boldmath$u$})
verifies (
1.1
) in the sense of distributions.

Step 3. Continuity in strong solution space

Combining the uniform bound estimates (
3.5
) and (
3.7
) with (
3.36
), we deduce that

(3.37)

\displaystyle f^{n}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup f,\quad\text{weakly-$\star$ in $L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};H^{1}_{\omega})$, as $n\to\infty$};

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\quad\text{weakly in $L^{2}(0,T_{0};H^{2})$, as $n\to\infty$};

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}_{t}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{t},\quad\text{weakly in $L^{2}(0,T_{0};L^{2})$, as $n\to\infty$};

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$},\quad\text{weakly in $L^{s}(0,T_{0};L^{q})$, as $n\to\infty$}.

It follows from (
3.37
) that
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{t}\in L^{2}(0,T_{0};L^{2})
and
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\in L^{2}(0,T_{0};H^{2})
.
Using the regularity of
u
, we can show that
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\in C(0,T_{0};H^{1})
. Thus,

\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\in C([0,T_{0}];H^{1})\cap L^{2}(0,T_{0};D^{2})\cap L^{s}(0,T_{0};D^{2,q}).

Employing Proposition
2.1
, we infer that

(3.38)

f\in C([0,T_{0}];H^{1}_{\omega}).

Therefore,
(f,\mbox{\boldmath$u$})
is the desired strong solution in the sense of Definition
1.1
. The uniqueness of strong solutions can be proved in the same way as in the derivation of (
3.32
).
This completes the proof.
\hfill\square

4.
A Priori Estimates

In this section, we derive some a priori estimates on classical solutions to the coupled model, which are used to extend the local strong solutions.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
0<R_{0}<\infty
. Assume that
f_{0}(x,v)\geq 0
,
f_{0}(x,v)\in H_{\omega}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
, and
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}(x)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
with
\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}=0
and the
v
-support of
f_{0}(x,v)
satisfying

\text{supp}_{v}f_{0}(x,\cdot)\subseteq B(R_{0})\quad\text{for a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$}.

If
(f,\mbox{\boldmath$u$})
is a classical solution in
[0,T]
, then it holds that

\displaystyle(1)

\displaystyle f\geq 0\ \text{and}\ \sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\exp\big(CT\big),\quad\text{where $C:=C\Big(\|\varphi\|_{C^{0}},\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\Big)$};

\displaystyle(2)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{p}}=\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}R_{0}^{3-\frac{3}{p}},\quad 1\leq p\leq\infty,

\displaystyle\text{for some constant $C>0$};

\displaystyle(3)

\displaystyle E(T)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\varphi(|x-y|)f(t,y,v^{*})f(t,x,v)|v^{*}-v|^{2}dydv^{*}dxdvdt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}f(t,x,v)|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}-v|^{2}dxdvdt=E_{0}.

Proof.

(1) Denote by
(X(t;x_{0},v_{0}),V(t;x_{0},v_{0}))
the characteristic to
\eqref{eq-cs-ns}_{1}
, emanating from
(x_{0},v_{0})
. It verifies that

(4.1)

\begin{cases}\frac{dX}{dt}=\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t,X),\\
\frac{dV}{dt}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\varphi(|X-y|)f(t,y,v^{*})(v^{*}-V)dydv^{*}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t,X)-V.\end{cases}

(4.2)

X(0;x_{0},v_{0})=x_{0},\qquad V(0;x_{0},v_{0})=v_{0}.

Recall that

a(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\varphi(|x-y|)f(t,y,v^{*})dydv^{*},

\mathbf{b}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\varphi(|x-y|)f(t,y,v^{*})v^{*}dydv^{*}.

Solving the equation (
2.1
) by the method of characteristics gives

(4.3)

f(t,X(t;x_{0},v_{0}),V(t;x_{0},v_{0}))=f_{0}(x_{0},v_{0})\exp\left(3\int_{0}^{t}\Big[1+a(\tau,X(\tau))\Big]d\tau\right)\geq 0.

From (
2.3
), (
4.3
) and the initial condition
f_{0}(x,v)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we deduce that

(4.4)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\exp\big(CT\big),\quad\text{where $C:=C\Big(\|\varphi\|_{C^{0}},\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\Big)$}.

(2) Integrating
\eqref{eq-cs-ns}_{1}
with respect to
v
over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
gives

(4.5)

\rho_{t}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\rho=0.

Multiplying (
4.5
) by
p\rho^{p-1}
,
1\leq p<\infty
, and integrating the resulting equation over
[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we obtain that for
t\in[0,T]

(4.6)

\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{p}}=\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\leq C\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}R_{0}^{3-\frac{3}{p}}

for some constat
C>0
, where we have used the incompressible condition
\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=0
.
Since

(4.7)

\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}=\lim_{p\to\infty}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{p}}=\lim_{p\to\infty}\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{p}}=\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}R_{0}^{3},

we combine (
4.6
) with (
4.7
) to obtain that for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{p}}=\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}R_{0}^{3-\frac{3}{p}},\quad\text{for some constant $C>0$.}

(3) Multiplying
\eqref{eq-cs-ns}_{1}
by
\frac{1}{2}|v|^{2}
, and integrating the resulting equation over
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we have

(4.8)

\begin{gathered}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\frac{1}{2}f|v|^{2}dxdv+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\varphi(|x-y|)f(t,y,v^{*})f(t,x,v)|v^{*}-v|^{2}dydv^{*}dxdv\\
=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}fv\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}-v)dxdv.\end{gathered}

Taking the dot product of
\eqref{eq-cs-ns}_{2}
with
u
, and integrating the resulting equation over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we deduce that

(4.9)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2}dx+\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}f\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot(v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$})dxdv.

Adding (
4.8
) to (
4.9
), and integrating the resulting equation over
[0,T]
, we arrive at Lemma
4.1
(3). This completes the proof.
∎

The following lemma is devoted to estimating
\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{9})}
. It plays a crucial role in the estimate of
\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}
, which is indispensable in applying Proposition
2.2
to the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations.

Lemma 4.2
.

Under the conditions in Theorem
1.1
, if
(f,\mbox{\boldmath$u$})
is a classical solution to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) in
[0,T]
, then it holds that

\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{9})}^{3}\leq C\Big(\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{3}}^{3}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{3}}^{6}+(1+\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}})E_{0}\Big),

for some constant
C>0
independent of the initial data.

Proof.

Using the splitting approach, we decompose
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}=\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}+\mbox{\boldmath$w$}
, with
\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}
and
w
determined by the following equations:

(4.10)

\begin{cases}\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{t}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}=\Delta\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}},\\
\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}|_{t=0}=\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0},\end{cases}

and

(4.11)

\begin{cases}\mbox{\boldmath$w$}_{t}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$w$}+\nabla P=\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$w$}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$})fdv,\\
\mbox{\boldmath$w$}|_{t=0}=0,\end{cases}

respectively. Taking the dot product of (
4.10
) with
3|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}
, and integrating the resulting equation over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we obtain

(4.12)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}\|_{L^{3}}^{3}dx+3\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}||\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}|^{2}dx\leq 0,

where we have used the fact that

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}\cdot\Delta\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}dx=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\Big(|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}\Big):\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}dx\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}||\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}|^{2}dx,

and

\displaystyle 3\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}\cdot\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}|dx=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}|^{3}dx=0.

Integrating (
4.12
) over
[0,T]
gives

(4.13)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}(t)\|_{L^{3}}^{3}dx+3\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}||\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}|^{2}dxdt\leq\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{0}\|_{L^{3}}^{3}.

For convenience of notation, we define
I(\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}})
as

I(\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}||\nabla\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}|^{2}dx,

similarly for the following
I(\mbox{\boldmath$w$})
, and
\mbox{\boldmath$h$}(t,x)
is defined as

\mbox{\boldmath$h$}(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$})fdv.

Taking the dot product of (
4.11
) with
3|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}
, and integrating the resulting equation over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we deduce that for
t\in[0,T]
,

(4.14)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\|_{L^{3}}^{3}+3I(\mbox{\boldmath$w$})\leq

\displaystyle-3\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\cdot\nabla Pdx+3\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$h$}dx,

where the pressure
P
is determined by the following equation:

\Delta P=-\nabla\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$})+\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$h$}.

Nevertheless, appearance of the additional term
\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$h$}
disables us to use integration by parts to estimate the term
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\cdot\nabla Pdx
. We overcome this difficulty by decomposing
P=P_{1}+P_{2}
, with
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
governed by

\Delta P_{1}=-\nabla\cdot(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$})\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta P_{2}=\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$h$},

respectively. Then it follows from the Calderón–Zygmund Theorem that

\|P_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{9}{4}}}\leq C\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{\frac{9}{2}}}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\nabla P_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}\leq C\|\mbox{\boldmath$h$}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}},

for some constant
C>0
. We estimate the right-hand side of (
4.14
) as follows.

(4.15)

\displaystyle-3\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\cdot\nabla Pdx+3\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$h$}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-3\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\cdot(\nabla P_{1}+\nabla P_{2})dx+3\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\mbox{\boldmath$h$}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 3\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\cdot\Big(|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\Big)P_{1}dx-3\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\cdot\nabla P_{2}dx+3\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\mbox{\boldmath$h$}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}||\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$w$}||P_{1}|dx+C\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\nabla P_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}+3\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\mbox{\boldmath$h$}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\||\mbox{\boldmath$w$}|^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$w$}|\right\|_{L^{2}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\|_{L^{9}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|P_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{9}{4}}}+C\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\mbox{\boldmath$h$}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CI^{\frac{2}{3}}(\mbox{\boldmath$w$})\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{\frac{9}{2}}}^{2}+C\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\mbox{\boldmath$h$}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CI^{\frac{2}{3}}(\mbox{\boldmath$w$})\bigg(\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}\|_{L^{\frac{9}{2}}}^{2}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\|_{L^{\frac{9}{2}}}^{2}\bigg)+C\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\mbox{\boldmath$h$}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CI^{\frac{2}{3}}(\mbox{\boldmath$w$})\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}\|_{L^{3}}\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}\|_{L^{9}}+CI^{\frac{2}{3}}(\mbox{\boldmath$w$})\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\|_{L^{3}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\|_{L^{9}}+C\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\|_{L^{3}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\|_{L^{9}}^{3}+\frac{1}{2}\|\mbox{\boldmath$h$}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CI^{\frac{2}{3}}(\mbox{\boldmath$w$})\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}\|_{L^{3}}I^{\frac{1}{3}}(\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}})+C\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\|_{L^{3}}I(\mbox{\boldmath$w$})+\frac{1}{2}\|\mbox{\boldmath$h$}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle I(\mbox{\boldmath$w$})+K_{1}\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\|_{L^{3}}^{3}I(\mbox{\boldmath$w$})+C\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}\|_{L^{3}}^{3}I(\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}})+\Big(1+\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2}fdxdv,

for some positive constants
C
and
K_{1}
. In the above estimate, we have used the Sobolev inequality

(4.16)

\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\|_{L^{9}}^{3}\leq CI(\mbox{\boldmath$w$})\quad\text{for some constant $C>0$,}

similarly for
\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}\|_{L^{9}}^{3}
, and the fact that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\mbox{\boldmath$h$}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{2}\leq\|\mbox{\boldmath$h$}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$h$}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\Big(1+\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2}fdxdv.

Substituting (
4.15
) into (
4.14
) and integrating the resulting equation over
[0,t]
,
t\in(0,T]
, we have by Lemma
4.1
(3) and (
4.13
) that

(4.17)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}(\tau)\|_{L^{3}}^{3}+2\int_{0}^{t}I(\mbox{\boldmath$w$})d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\|_{L^{3}}^{3}I(\mbox{\boldmath$w$})d\tau+C\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}(\tau)\|_{L^{3}}^{3}\int_{0}^{t}I(\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}})d\tau

\displaystyle+\Big(1+\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2}fdxdvd\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\|_{L^{3}}^{3}I(\mbox{\boldmath$w$})d\tau+C\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{3}}^{6}+(1+\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}})E_{0},

for some constants
K_{1},C>0
, independent of the initial data. Since
\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}(0)\|_{L^{3}}^{3}=0
, there must exist
t^{*}\in(0,T]
such that

(4.18)

\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}(t)\|_{L^{3}}^{3}<2\varepsilon_{0}\quad\text{for $t\in[0,t^{*})$,}

where
\varepsilon_{0}
satisfies
2K_{1}\varepsilon_{0}=1.
If

C\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{3}}^{6}+(1+\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}})E_{0}\leq\varepsilon_{0},

it follows from (
4.17
) and (
4.18
) that

(4.19)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t^{*}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}(\tau)\|_{L^{3}}^{3}+\int_{0}^{t^{*}}I(\mbox{\boldmath$w$})d\tau\leq C\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{3}}^{6}+(1+\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}})E_{0}\leq\varepsilon_{0}.

By continuity argument, we can show that
t^{*}=T
. Thus, it holds that

(4.20)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}(t)\|_{L^{3}}^{3}+\int_{0}^{T}I(\mbox{\boldmath$w$})dt\leq C\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{3}}^{6}+(1+\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}})E_{0}.

Combining with (
4.13
) and (
4.16
), we deduce that

(4.21)

\displaystyle\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{9})}^{3}\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\|\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{9})}^{3}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$w$}\|_{L^{3}(0,T;L^{9})}^{3}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T}I(\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}})dt+C\int_{0}^{T}I(\mbox{\boldmath$w$})dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{3}}^{3}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{3}}^{6}+(1+\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}})E_{0}\Big),

for some constant
C>0
independent of the initial data.
∎

In fact,
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\in{L^{3}(0,T;L^{9})}
satisfies the Serrin condition, which can be used to derive the estimate on
\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Under the conditions in Theorem
1.1
, if
(f,\mbox{\boldmath$u$})
is a classical solution to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) in
[0,T]
, then it holds that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq C\Big(\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}E_{0}\Big)\exp\left(C(\varepsilon_{0}+\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}})\right),

for some constant
C>0
independent of the initial data.

Proof.

Taking the dot product of
\eqref{eq-cs-ns}_{2}
with
-\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}
, and integrating the resulting equation over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we obtain

(4.22)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+K_{2}\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\cdot\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$})fdv\cdot\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{9}}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{\frac{18}{7}}}\|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\mbox{\boldmath$h$}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{9}}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{3}}+C\|\mbox{\boldmath$h$}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{K_{2}}{2}\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{9}}^{3}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\mbox{\boldmath$h$}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

for some positive constants
K_{2}
and
C
. Here we have used the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality

(4.23)

\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{\frac{18}{7}}}\leq C\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$,}

and the elliptic estimate

(4.24)

K_{2}\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Using Lemma
4.2
and the fact that

\int_{0}^{T}\|\mbox{\boldmath$h$}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}E_{0},

we solve the above Gronwall inequality (
4.22
) to obtain

(4.25)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt\leq C\Big(\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}E_{0}\Big)\exp\left(C(\varepsilon_{0}+\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}})\right),

for some constant
C>0
independent of the initial data.
∎

Having obtained the estimate on
\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}
, we then use Proposition
2.2
to derive the estimate on
\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{s}(0,T:L^{q})}
, which leads to the estimate on
\int_{0}^{T}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}dt
by interpolation.

Lemma 4.4
.

Under the conditions in Theorem
1.1
, if
(f,\mbox{\boldmath$u$})
is a classical solution to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) in
[0,T]
, then it holds that

\displaystyle(1)\ R(t)\leq R_{0}+C,\quad\text{for $t\in[0,T]$};

\displaystyle(2)\ \|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{s}(0,T;L^{q})}\leq C+CT^{\frac{1}{s}};

\displaystyle(3)\ \int_{0}^{T}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}dt\leq C(1+T),

where
C:=C(R_{0},E_{0},\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{H^{1}},\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{3}})
.

Proof.

(1) Solving the characteristic equation (
4.1
) yields

(4.26)

\displaystyle V(t)=

\displaystyle v_{0}\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}\Big[1+a(s,X(s))\Big]ds\right)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\Big[\mathbf{b}(\tau,X(\tau))+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\tau,X(\tau))\Big]\exp\left(-\int_{\tau}^{t}\Big[1+a(s,X(s))\Big]ds\right)d\tau.

Using Lemma
4.1
(3) and the Cauchy inequality, we have

(4.27)

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\mathbf{b}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}f|v|^{2}dxdv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

From (

), we deduce that for

,

\displaystyle R(t)\leq

\displaystyle R_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(1+\|\mathbf{b}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\exp\left(-\int_{\tau}^{t}\Big[1+a(s,X(s))\Big]ds\right)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle R_{0}+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}d\tau

\displaystyle+\bigg(1+\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\|\mathbf{b}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\bigg)\int_{0}^{t}\Big[1+a(\tau,X(\tau))\Big]\exp\left(-\int_{\tau}^{t}\Big[1+a(s,X(s))\Big]ds\right)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle R_{0}+1+E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle R_{0}+C(R_{0},E_{0},\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{H^{1}},\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{3}}),

(2) Applying Proposition

to

(4.29)

\displaystyle\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\theta_{1}}\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{q}}^{\theta_{1}}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\theta_{2}}\|\nabla^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{q}}^{\theta_{2}}

4.1
(2), Lemma
4.3

\displaystyle C\|\rho\|_{L^{q}}+C\|\rho\|_{L^{\frac{6q}{6-q}}}\|\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle C(R_{0},E_{0},\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{H^{1}},\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{3}}).

where

\displaystyle C(1+T),

This completes the proof.

Proof of the Theorems

Combining the local existence result with the a priori estimates on classical solutions to the coupled system, we prove global existence of strong solutions by continuity argument.

Proof of Theorem

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T_{m}}\Big(1+s\Big)^{-\frac{17}{32}}\Big(1+s\Big)^{\frac{17}{32}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$h$}(s)\|_{L^{1}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\int_{0}^{T_{m}}\Big(1+s\Big)^{\frac{17}{16}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2}fdxdvds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(E_{0})\Big(1+K\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Substituting (
5.22
) and (
5.25
) into (
5.19
), we deduce that

(5.26)

\displaystyle|\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}(t,\xi)|^{2}\leq C+CK+CK^{2}|\xi|^{2}\quad\text{for $t\in[0,T_{m}]$},

where
C:=C(\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{1}},E_{0})
.
It follows from (
5.26
) that

(5.27)

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq c(t+c^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}}|\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq CK\Big(1+t\Big)^{-\frac{3}{2}}+CK^{2}\Big(1+t\Big)^{-\frac{5}{2}}\quad\text{for $t\in[0,T_{m}]$},

where
C:=C(\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{1}},E_{0})
. Combining (
5.13
) with (
5.27
), we have

(5.28)

\displaystyle E(t)\leq CK\Big(1+t\Big)^{-\frac{3}{2}}+CK^{2}\Big(1+t\Big)^{-\frac{5}{2}}\quad\text{for $t\in[0,T_{m}]$},

where
C:=C(\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{1}},E_{0})
.
Since

E\Big(T_{m}\Big)=K\Big(1+T_{m}\Big)^{-\frac{9}{8}}\leq E_{0},

we have

(5.29)

1+T_{m}\geq\left(\frac{K}{E_{0}}\right)^{\frac{8}{9}}.

From (
5.28
) and (
5.29
), we infer that

(5.30)

\displaystyle E\Big(T_{m}\Big)\leq

\displaystyle\bigg[C\Big(1+T_{m}\Big)^{-\frac{3}{8}}+CK\Big(1+T_{m}\Big)^{-\frac{11}{8}}\bigg]K\Big(1+T_{m}\Big)^{-\frac{9}{8}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CK^{-\frac{2}{9}}K\Big(1+T_{m}\Big)^{-\frac{9}{8}},

for some constant
C:=C(\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{1}},E_{0})
.
Take
K
suitably large such that

CK^{-\frac{2}{9}}=\frac{1}{2}.

Then we have
E\Big(T_{m}\Big)\leq\frac{K}{2}\Big(1+T_{m}\Big)^{-\frac{9}{8}}
, which contradicts (
5.21
). Therefore, we show that
T_{m}=\infty
, and it follows form (
5.28
) that

(5.31)

\displaystyle E(t)\leq C\Big(1+t\Big)^{-\frac{3}{2}}

for some constant
C:=C(\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{1}},E_{0})
. It is easy to deduce from (
5.31
) that

\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\Big(1+t\Big)^{-\frac{3}{4}}

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|v-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2}fdxdv\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|v|^{2}fdxdv+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}|^{2}fdxdv

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}|v|^{2}fdxdv+2\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CE(t)\leq C\Big(1+t\Big)^{-\frac{3}{2}},

for some constant
C:=C(\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\|_{L^{1}},E_{0})
, where we have used Lemma
4.1
(2). This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

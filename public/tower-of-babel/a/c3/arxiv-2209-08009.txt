Approximate traces on groups and the quantum complexity class MIP c ​ o , s \operatorname{MIP}^{co,s} Thanks: I. Goldbring was partially supported by NSF grant DMS-2504477.
Isaac Goldbring and Bradd Hart

Abstract
. An open question in quantum complexity theory is whether or not the class MIP c ​ o \operatorname{MIP}^{co} , consisting of languages that can be efficiently verified using interacting provers sharing quantum resources according to the quantum commuting model, coincides with the class c ​ o ​ R ​ E coRE of languages with recursively enumerable complement. We introduce the notion of a qc-modulus, which encodes approximations to quantum commuting correlations, and show that the existence of a computable qc-modulus gives a negative answer to a natural variant of the aforementioned question.



1.
Introduction

Throughout this note,
n
and
m
denote natural numbers that are at least
2
and
[n]
denotes the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
(and likewise for
[m]
).

We recall the following definitions from quantum information theory and quantum complexity theory.

Definition 1.1
.

(1)

The set
C_{q}(n,m)
of
quantum correlations
consists of the correlations of the form
p(i,j|v,w)=\langle A^{v}_{i}\otimes B^{w}_{j}\xi,\xi\rangle
for
v,w\in[n]
and
i,j\in[m]
,
where H is a finite-dimensional Hilbert space,
\xi\in H\otimes H
is a unit vector, and for every
v,w\in[n]
,

(A^{v}_{i}:i\in[m])
and
(B^{w}_{j}\ :\ j\in[m])
are positive operator-valued measures (POVMs) on H.

(2)

We set
C_{qa}(n,m)
to be the closure in
[0,1]^{n^{2}m^{2}}
of
C_{q}(n,m)
.

(3)

The set
C_{qc}(n,m)
of
quantum commuting correlations
consists of the correlations of the form
p(i,j|v,w)=\langle A^{v}_{i}B^{w}_{j}\xi,\xi\rangle
for
v,w\in[n]
and
i,j\in[m]
,
where H is a separable Hilbert space,
\xi\in H
is a unit vector, and for every
v,w\in[n]
,

(A^{v}_{i}:i\in[m])
and
(B^{w}_{j}\ :\ j\in[m])
are POVMs on H for which
A^{v}_{i}B^{w}_{j}=B^{w}_{j}A^{v}_{i}
for all
v,w\in[n]
and
i,j\in[m]
.

Note that
C_{q}(n,m)\subseteq C_{qc}(n,m)
; since it is know that the latter set is closed, we in fact have that
C_{qa}(n,m)\subseteq C_{qc}(n,m)
. Tsirelson’s problem in quantum information theory asks if these latter two sets in fact coincide; a negative resolution to this problem follows from the main result of
[
1
]
.

Definition 1.2
.

(1)

A
nonlocal game with
n
questions and
m
answers
is a pair
\mathfrak{G}=(\mu,D)
, where
\mu
is a probability distribution on
[n]\times[n]
and

D:[n]\times[n]\times[m]\times[m]\to\{0,1\}

is a function.

(2)

Given a nonlocal game
\mathfrak{G}
as in the previous item and
p\in[0,1]^{n^{2}m^{2}}
, we define the
value of
\mathfrak{G}
at
p
to be

\operatorname{val}(\mathfrak{G},p):=\sum_{v,w\in[n]}\mu(v,w)\sum_{i,j\in[m]}D(v,w,i,j)p(i,j|v,w).

Definition 1.3
.

Given a nonlocal game
\mathfrak{G}
with
n
questions and
m
answers, we define:

(1)

the
entangled value of
\mathfrak{G}
to be

\operatorname{val}^{*}(\mathfrak{G}):=\sup_{p\in C_{qa}(n,m)}\operatorname{val}(\mathfrak{G},p);

(2)

the
quantum commuting value of
\mathfrak{G}
to be

\operatorname{val}^{co}(\mathfrak{G}):=\sup_{p\in C_{qc}(n,m)}\operatorname{val}(\mathfrak{G},p).

Definition 1.4
.

A language
L
(in the sense of complexity theory) belongs to the class
\operatorname{MIP}^{*}
if there is an effective mapping
z\mapsto\mathfrak{G}_{z}
from strings to nonlocal games such that:

•

if
z\in L
, then
\operatorname{val}^{*}(\mathfrak{G}_{z})\geq\frac{2}{3}

•

if
z\notin L
, then
\operatorname{val}^{*}(\mathfrak{G}_{z})\leq\frac{1}{3}
.

The complexity class
\operatorname{MIP}^{co}
is defined in the analogous fashion, using
\operatorname{val}^{co}
instead of
\operatorname{val}^{*}
.

The following landmark result in quantum complexity theory appears in
[
1
]
:

Theorem 1.5
.

\operatorname{MIP}^{*}=\operatorname{RE}
. In other words, the languages that belong to
\operatorname{MIP}^{*}
are precisely the recursively enumerable languages, that is, those languages
L
for which there is an algorithm that enumerates
L
.

In particular,
\operatorname{MIP}^{*}
contains undecidable problems (such as the halting problem).

In
[
1
]
, it was pointed out that every element of the class
\operatorname{MIP}^{co}
is co-recursively enumerable, that is, the complement of
\operatorname{MIP}^{co}
is recursively enumerable. Denoting by
\operatorname{coRE}
the complexity class of langauges that are co-recursively enumerable, the authors of
[
1
]
ask whether or not the aforementioned inclusion is actually an equality:

Question 1.6
.

Does
\operatorname{MIP}^{co}=\operatorname{coRE}
?

The main result of this note is that a very natural variant of the above question has a negative answer provided a certain computability assumption about “approximate” quantum commuting correlations holds, and that, in fact, all languages that belong to this natural variant of
\operatorname{MIP}^{co}
are actually decidable.

First, we recall the following definitions:

Definition 1.7
.

(1)

Given
p\in[0,1]^{n^{2}m^{2}}
, we say that
p
is
synchronous
if
p(i,j|v,v)=0
for all
v\in[n]
and all
distinct

i,j\in[m]
.

(2)

We let
C_{qa}^{s}(n,m)
and
C_{qc}^{s}(n,m)
denote the synchronous elements of
C_{qa}(n,m)
and
C_{qc}(n,m)
respectively.

(3)

Given a nonlocal game
\mathfrak{G}
, we let
\operatorname{s-val}^{*}(\mathfrak{G})
and
\operatorname{s-val}^{co}(\mathfrak{G})
denote the corresponding
synchronous values
of
\mathfrak{G}
, which are defined analogously to
\operatorname{val}^{*}(\mathfrak{G})
and
\operatorname{val}^{co}(\mathfrak{G})
, except that we only take the supremum over
C^{s}_{qa}(n,m)
and
C^{s}_{qc}(n,m)
respectively.

(4)

We define the complexity class
\operatorname{MIP}^{*,s}
and
\operatorname{MIP}^{co,s}
analogously to
\operatorname{MIP}^{*}
and
\operatorname{MIP}^{co}
, this time using the appropriate synchronous values of the games in the definition.

The main result of
[
1
]
actually shows that
\operatorname{MIP}^{*,s}
coincides with
RE
and thus a reasonable variant of Question
1.6
above is whether or not
\operatorname{MIP}^{co,s}
coincides with coRE.

We will see later in Proposition
3.5
that synchronous commuting correlations satisfy a certain “stability” property, namely that correlations that are almost quantum commuting correlations (in a certain technical sense) are near actual quantum commuting correlations. The main result of this note (Theorem
4.3
below) will show that if this stability relation can be realized “effectively,” then all languages in
\operatorname{MIP}^{co,s}
are in RE, and are thus decidable. Since there are elements of coRE that are undecidable (such as the complement of the halting problem), we would obtain a negative solution to the synchronous version of Question
1.6
above. Said in the opposite direction: if it turns out that
\operatorname{MIP}^{co,s}=
coRE, then there is no effective version of stability for almost quantum commuting correlations.

2.
Preliminaries: traces on groups and group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras

Fix a countable group
G
and let
\mathbb{C}G
denote the corresponding group ring. We recall the following terminology:

Definition 2.1
.

A function
\tau:G\to\mathbb{C}
is called:

(1)

of positive type
if for all
\sum_{\lambda}a_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{C}G
, we have
\sum_{\lambda,\gamma}\overline{a_{\lambda}}a_{\gamma}\tau(\lambda^{-1}\gamma)\geq 0

(2)

a
class function
if
\tau
is constant on conjugacy classes.

The following terminology is not standard, but convenient for our purposes. In what follows,
\mathbb{D}
denotes the unit disc in the complex plane.

Definition 2.2
.

A function
\tau:G\to\mathbb{D}
is called a
trace
on
G
if it is a class function of positive type.

Here is the reason for the abuse in terminology. Below,
C^{*}(G)
denotes the universal
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra of the group
G
.

Fact 2.3
.

Given a trace
\tau
on
C^{*}(G)
, its restriction to
G
is a trace on
G
(in the sense of Definition
2.2
). Moreover, the map
\tau\mapsto\tau|G
is a bijection between traces on
C^{*}(G)
and traces on
G
.

In the sequel, we will freely abuse notation and use
\tau
to denote both the trace on the group
G
as well as the corresponding trace on
C^{*}(G)
.

Note that in the definition of being positive type, we can restrict attention to elements of the subring
\mathbb{Q}(i)G
without changing the notion. Assuming then that some countable enumeration
G=(g_{0},g_{1},g_{2},\ldots)
of
G
has been given, there is thus an effectively enumerable countable list
(R_{l})
of requirements that characterize when a function
\tau:G\to\mathbb{D}
is a trace on
G
. Note really that this list of requirements is independent of the group
G
in question and just depends on some fixed effective coding of
\mathbb{Q}(i)
.

We now consider “relaxations” of these requirements:

Definition 2.4
.

Fix
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq 1
.

(1)

If
R_{l}
is the requirement
\sum_{\lambda,\gamma}\overline{a_{\lambda}}a_{\gamma}\tau(\lambda^{-1}\gamma)\geq 0
, then we define the relaxed requirement
R_{l}^{k}
to be that
\sum_{\lambda,\gamma}\overline{a_{\lambda}}a_{\gamma}\tau(\lambda^{-1}\gamma)
is within
\frac{1}{k}
of the positive real axis.

(2)

If
R_{l}
is the requirement
\tau(\gamma^{-1}\lambda\gamma)=\tau(\lambda)
, then we define the relaxed requirement
R_{l}^{k}
to be
|\tau(\gamma^{-1}\lambda\gamma)-\tau(\lambda)|<\frac{1}{k}
.

We say that
\tau:G\to\mathbb{D}
is a
k
-approximate trace on
G
if the relaxed requirements
R_{1}^{k},\ldots,R_{k}^{k}
hold.

The following lemma is obvious:

Lemma 2.5
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there is
\delta>0
such that, for all functions
\tau,\tau^{\prime}:G\to\mathbb{D}
, if
\tau
is a trace on
G
and
\|\tau-\tau^{\prime}\|_{\infty}<\delta
, then
\tau^{\prime}
is a
k
-approximate trace on
G
. Moreover,
\delta
depends only on
k
and not on
G
and this dependence is computable from
k
.

3.
The groups
\mathbb{F}(n,m)

Below, for
n,m\geq 2
, we let
\mathbb{F}(n,m)
denote the group freely generated by
n
elements of order
m
. In the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(\mathbb{F}(n,m))
, for each
v\in[n]
, we let
e^{n,m}_{v,1},\ldots,e^{n,m}_{v,m}
denote the projections onto the eigenspaces corresponding to the eigenvalue
\xi_{m}^{i}
of the unitary operator corresponding to the
v^{\text{th}}
generator
u^{n,m}_{v}
of
\mathbb{F}(n,m)
, where
\xi_{m}
denotes a primitive
m^{\text{th}}
root of unity. We then have that
(u_{v}^{n,m})^{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{m}^{ji}e^{n,m}_{v,i}
for each
v\in[n]
and
j\in[m]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Given
p\in[0,1]^{n^{2}m^{2}}
, we say that a trace
\tau
on
\mathbb{F}(n,m)
is
adapted to
p
if
p(i,j|v,w)=\tau(e^{n,m}_{v,i}e^{n,m}_{w,j})
for all
v,w\in[n]
and all
i,j\in[m]
.

Here is the key fact relating traces on
\mathbb{F}(n,m)
and quantum commuting correlations:

Fact 3.2
.

(
[
2
]
)
For
p\in[0,1]^{n^{2}m^{2}}
, we have
p\in C_{qc}^{s}(n,m)
if and only if there is a trace
\tau
on
\mathbb{F}(n,m)
that is adapted to
p
.

Proof.

In
[
2
]
, they show that
p\in C_{qc}^{s}(n,m)
if and only if there is a tracial C*-algebra
(A,\tau)
and a generating family of projections
p_{v,i}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{v,i}=1
for each
v=1,\ldots,n
and such that
p(i,j|v,w)=\tau(p_{v,i}p_{w,j})
. However, letting
\pi:C^{*}(\mathbb{F}(n,m))\to A
be the surjective *-homomorphism determined by sending
e_{v,i}
to
p_{v,i}
and defining
\tau^{\prime}
on
C^{*}(\mathbb{F}(n,m))
by
\tau^{\prime}(x):=\tau(\pi(x))
, we obtain the equivalence with the above statement.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

There is a computable function
s:\mathbb{N}^{7}\to\bigcup_{n,m\geq 2}\mathbb{Q}(i)\mathbb{F}(n,m)
so that, for each
n,m\geq 2
, each
v,w\in[n]
, each
i,j\in[m]
, and each
k\geq 1
, we have that
s(v,w,i,j,k,n,m)\in\mathbb{Q}(i)\mathbb{F}(n,m)
and
\|e^{n,m}_{v,i}e^{n,m}_{w,j}-s(v,w,i,j,k,n,m)\|<\frac{1}{k}
, where the norm is the norm on the universal representation of
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{F}(n,m)
, that is, the norm on
C^{*}(\mathbb{F}(n,m))
.

Proof.

The theorem follows from the fact that the relation
(u_{v}^{n,m})^{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{m}^{ji}e^{n,m}_{v,i}
mentioned above can be effectively inverted to express each
e^{n,m}_{v,i}
as a polynomial in the generator
u_{v}^{n,m}
with coefficients in the computable field
\mathbb{Q}(\xi_{m})
. Moreover, this procedure is uniform in
n
and
m
.
∎

In order to match notation, in what follows we will rewrite
s(v,w,i,j,k,n,m)
as
s_{v,w,i,j,k}^{n,m}
. Also, given a function
\tau:\mathbb{F}(n,m)\to\mathbb{D}
, we extend it to a function
\tau:\mathbb{Q}(i)\mathbb{F}(n,m)\to\mathbb{C}
by linearity.

Definition 3.4
.

Fix
n,m\geq 2
,
p\in\mathbb{[}0,1]^{n^{2}m^{2}}
, and
k\geq 1
. We say that a function (not necessarily a trace)
\tau:\mathbb{F}(n,m)\to\mathbb{D}
is
k
-adpated to
p
if

|p(i,j|v,w)-\tau(s^{n,m}_{v,w,i,j,k})|<\frac{1}{k}

for all
v,w\in[n]
and all
i,j\in[m]
.

Here is the “stability” property satisfied by synchornous quantum commuting correlations:

Proposition 3.5
.

Given
m,n\geq 2
and
\epsilon>0
, there is
k\geq 1
such that, for all
p\in[0,1]^{n^{2}m^{2}}
, if there is a
k
-approximate trace
\tau
on
\mathbb{F}(n,m)
that is
k
-adpated to
p
, then there is
p^{\prime}\in C_{qc}^{s}(n,m)
with
\|p-p^{\prime}\|_{\infty}<\epsilon
.

Proof.

Suppose that the lemma is false for some
m,n,\epsilon
, that is, for each
k\geq 1
, there is
p_{k}\in\mathbb{[}0,1]^{n^{2}m^{2}}
for which there is a
k
-approximate trace
\tau_{k}
on
\mathbb{F}(n,m)
that is
k
-adapted to
p_{k}
and yet
\|p_{k}-p\|_{\infty}\geq\epsilon
for all
p\in C_{qc}^{s}(n,m)
. Let
p
be a subsequential limit of
p_{k}
. Since
\tau_{k}
is pointwise bounded, by passing to a subsequence if necessary, we may suppose that
\tau_{k}
converges pointwise to a function
\tau:\mathbb{F}(n,m)\to\mathbb{D}
. Note then that
\tau
is an actual trace on
\mathbb{F}(n,m)
that is adapted to
p
, whence
p\in C_{qc}^{s}(n,m)
. Since
\|p-p_{k}\|_{\infty}<\epsilon
for some
k
, this is a contradiction.
∎

4.
The main theorem

Definition 4.1
.

We say that
T:\mathbb{N}^{2}\to\mathbb{N}
is a
qc-modulus
if it satisfies the conclusion of Proposition
3.5
for
\epsilon=\frac{1}{12}
, that is, for all
n,m\geq 2
and all
p\in\mathbb{[}0,1]^{n^{2}m^{2}}
, if there is a
T(n,m)
-approximate trace
\tau
on
\mathbb{F}(n,m)
that is
T(n,m)
-adapted to
p
, then there is
p^{\prime}\in C_{qc}^{s}(n,m)
with
\|p-p^{\prime}\|_{\infty}<\frac{1}{12}
.

We let
X^{n,m}_{k}
denote those
p\in\mathbb{(}[0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q})^{n^{2}m^{2}}
for which there is a
k
-approximate trace
\tau:\mathbb{F}(n,m)\to\mathbb{Q}(i)
that is
k
-adpated to
p
.

The following lemma is clear:

Lemma 4.2
.

Each
X^{n,m}_{k}
is recursively enumerable, uniformly in
k
,
n
, and
m
.

Here is the main result of this note:

Theorem 4.3
.

If there is a computable qc-modulus
T:\mathbb{N}^{2}\to\mathbb{N}
, then every language in
\operatorname{MIP}^{co,s}
is in RE (and thus decidable).

Proof.

Fix a computable qc-modulus
T
and suppose that
L\in\operatorname{MIP}^{co,s}
. Let
z\mapsto\mathfrak{G}_{z}
be an efficient mapping from strings to nonlocal games such that:

•

if
z\in L
, then
\operatorname{s-val}^{co}(\mathfrak{G}_{z})\geq\frac{2}{3}

•

if
z\notin L
, then
\operatorname{s-val}^{co}(\mathfrak{G}_{z})\leq\frac{1}{3}
.

Here is the algorithm for enumerating
L
. Given a string
z
, first determine the dimensions
n
and
m
for
\mathfrak{G}_{z}
. Set
k:=T(n,m)
and let
(p_{l})
be a computable enumeration of
X^{n,m}_{k}
. The algorithm then simply starts computing
\operatorname{val}(\mathfrak{G}_{z},p_{l})
for each
l
; if for some
l
we see that
\operatorname{val}(\mathfrak{G}_{z},p_{l})>\frac{1}{2}
, then we declare that
z\in L
.

Soundness of the algorithm: suppose that
\operatorname{val}(\mathfrak{G}_{z},p_{l})>\frac{1}{2}
. By the choice of
k
, there is
p\in C_{qc}^{s}(n,m)
such that
\|p_{l}-p\|_{\infty}<\frac{1}{12}
. It follows that
\operatorname{val}^{co,s}(\mathfrak{G}_{z})\geq\operatorname{val}(\mathfrak{G}_{z},p)>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{12}>\frac{1}{3}
, which tells us that
z\in L
.

Completeness of the algorithm: suppose that
z\in L
and take
p\in C_{qc}^{s}(n,m)
such that
\operatorname{val}(\mathfrak{G}_{z},p)\geq\frac{2}{3}
. Let
\tau_{p}
be a trace on
\mathbb{F}(n,m)
that is adapted to
p
, whence
|p(i,j|v,w)-\tau_{p}(s^{n,m}_{v,w,i,j,k})|\leq\|e^{n,m}_{v,i}e^{n,m}_{w,j}-s^{n,m}_{v,w,i,j,k}\|<\frac{1}{k}
for all
v,w\in[n]
and all
i,j\in[m]
. Fix
\eta>0
small enough and let
p^{\prime}\in([0,1]\cap\mathbb{Q}m)^{n^{2}m^{2}}
and
\tau^{\prime}:\mathbb{F}(n,m)\to\mathbb{D}\cap\mathbb{Q}(i)
be such that
\|p-p^{\prime}\|_{\infty},\|\tau_{p}-\tau^{\prime}\|_{\infty}<\eta
. By Lemma
2.5
, if
\eta
is small enough, then
\tau^{\prime}
is a
k
-approximate trace on
\mathbb{F}(n,m)
. Note also that

|p^{\prime}(i,j|v,w)-\tau^{\prime}(s^{n,m}_{v,w,i,j,k})|<2\eta+\|e^{n,m}_{v_{i}}e^{n,m}_{w,j}-s^{n,m}_{v,w,i,j,k}\|<\frac{1}{k}

as long as
\eta
is small enough. (Note that the conditions on
\eta
are effective in terms of
k
and thus in terms of
n
and
m
.) It follows that
\tau^{\prime}
is
k
-adapted to
p^{\prime}
, whence there is
l
such that
p^{\prime}=p_{l}
. As long as
\eta<\frac{1}{12}
, we have that
\operatorname{val}(\mathfrak{G}_{z},p_{l})\geq\frac{2}{3}-\frac{1}{12}>\frac{1}{2}
. Consequently, the algorithm will tell us that
z\in L
.
∎

We note that it remains a possibility that
\operatorname{MIP}^{co,s}
is properly contained in co-RE and yet there is no computable qc-modulus.

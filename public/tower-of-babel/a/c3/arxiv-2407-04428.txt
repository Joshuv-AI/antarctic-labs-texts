Abstract
We present a wavenumber-explicit analysis of FEM-BEM coupling methods for time-harmonic Helmholtz problems proposed in [ MMPR20 ] for conforming discretizations and in [ EMM+22 ] for discontinuous Galerkin (DG) volume discretizations. We show that the conditions that k ​ h / p kh/p be sufficiently small and that log ⁡ ( k ) / p \log(k)/p be bounded imply quasi-optimality of both conforming and DG-method, where k k is the wavenumber, h h the mesh size, and p p the approximation order. The analysis relies on a k k -explicit regularity theory for a three-field coupling formulation.

Introduction

Many acoustic or electromagentic wave propagation problems in inhomogeneous media are posed in time-harmonic form and are additionally
naturally posed in full space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Numerically, the treatment of such problems is challenging for two reasons. First, time-harmonic
problems at large wavenumbers
k
are notoriously beset by dispersion errors (also known as pollution errors) in which
the gap between the best approximation error and the actual numerical error widens as the wavenumber
k
increases.
For both Helmholtz and Maxwell problems, it has now become clear that high order methods are much better suited to
control dispersion errors than low order methods; see, e.g.,
[
MS11
,
MPS13
,
BCFM22
,
LSW22
,
GLSW23
,
CFGNT22
,
CFN20
,
NT20
,
GS23
]
and references therein where a mathematical
analysis is put forward for this observation, and it is shown that the conditions

\frac{kh}{p}\mbox{ sufficient small}\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad p=O(\log k)

(1.1)

are sufficient to suppress dispersion errors, where
h
is the mesh size and
p
the approximation order.
Second, the treatment of unbounded domains requires one to resort to a form of transparent boundary conditions
such as PML
[
Ber94
,
CJM97
,
BP08
,
CFGNT22
]
,
absorbing boundary conditions
[
EM77
,
BGT82
,
Giv91
]
(and the surveys
in
[
Giv04
]
,
[
Ihl98
, Sec. 3.3]
), pole condition,
[
HN09
,
NS11
,
Hal16
]
,
or FEM-BEM coupling,
[
Cos88
,
Say09
,
Ste11
,
AFF+13
,
CF99
,
Era12
]
,
the latter providing great geometric flexibility.
The purpose of the present work is to provide a
k
-explicit analysis of the FEM-BEM coupling strategy for Helmholtz problems that
has been proposed in our earlier works
[
MMPR20
]
for a conforming volume discretization and
[
EMM+22
]
for a
hp
-DGFEM volume discretization.
For both discretizations strategies, our
k
-explicit analysis shows that the scale resolution condition (
1.1
) ensures quasi-optimality of the FEM-BEM coupling method.

The problem under consideration is the Helmholtz equation in heterogeneous media with piecewise analytic coefficients, analytic interfaces between
the different materials, and an analytic coupling boundary separating the computational domain from a homogeneous unbounded medium.
This setting prevents the appearance of corner or edge singularities and allows us to focus on the influence of the wavenumber
k
. The coupling strategy studied by us is taken from our previous work

[
MMPR20
,
EMM+22
]
. It is particularly suitable for smooth coupling boundaries
\Gamma

coupling
[

Han90
]
for the Poisson problem. For a more detailed discussion of the features of our coupling strategy, we refer to
[

For numerical examples for coupling strategies, we refer to
[

]
(conforming
hp
-FEM) and
[
EMM+22
]
(
hp
-DGFEM).

2.1
Roadmap

Since the
k
-explicit convergence analysis for the FEM-BEM coupling is rather
involved, let us indicate the main steps for the case of a conforming discretization.
The related, somewhat more involved DG discretization will be discussed in detail in Section
7
.

The FEM-BEM coupling takes the form of a three-field formulation with a volume variable

u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and two boundary variables
m\in H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
,

u^{\mathrm{ext}}\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
,
where the auxiliary variable
m=\partial_{\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}}^{\nu,\mathrm{int}}u+iku
is the impedance trace and
u^{\mathrm{ext}}

is the exterior Dirichlet trace, which coincides with the interior trace
\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{int}}u
.
A triplet
(u,m,u^{\mathrm{ext}})
is collected in the vector
{\mathbf{u}}\in{\mathcal{V}}=H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)\times H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
.
The analysis of the numerical methods is carried out in the
k
-dependent norm

|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}|\!|\!|_{k}^{2}:=\|\nu^{1/2}\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+k^{2}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|m\|^{2}_{H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)}+\|u^{\mathrm{ext}}\|^{2}_{H^{1/2}(\Gamma)}\sim\left|\!\left|\!\left|{\mathbf{u}}\right|\!\right|\!\right|^{2}_{k,\mathcal{V},0}.

(2.4)

Elements
{\mathbf{u}}
of
{\mathcal{V}}
may also be piecewise analytic. In this Subsection
2.1
we use
the analyticity class
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(M):=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(M,\vartheta,\Omega\setminus\partial\mathscr{P})\times\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(kM,\vartheta,{\mathcal{O}},\Gamma)\times\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(M,\vartheta,{\mathcal{O}},\Gamma)
,
where we track only the dependence on
M
and suppress the dependence on
\vartheta
and
{\mathcal{O}}
. That is,
{\mathbf{u}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(M)
implies that the first component
u
is piecewise analytic and the
second and third components admit analytic extensions to a fixed neighborhood of
\Gamma
.

The three-field variational formulation of (
2.1
) reads:
Find
{\mathbf{u}}=(u,m,u^{\mathrm{ext}})\in{\mathcal{V}}
such that

{\mathcal{T}_{k}}({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}})=\ell({\mathbf{v}})\quad\forall{\mathbf{v}}\in{\mathcal{V}}.

for a
sesquilinear form
{\mathcal{T}_{k}}
and a linear functional
\ell
incorporating the given data (see (
5.7
)).
The conforming Galerkin discretization based on the space
{\mathcal{V}}_{h}^{C}\subset{\mathcal{V}}
(cf. Section
5.2
)
then
leads to an approximation
{\mathbf{u}}_{N}
and the Galerkin error
{\mathbf{e}}_{N}:={\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{u}}_{N}
. The analysis
proceeds in the following steps.

(I)

(Gårding inequality for
{\mathcal{T}_{k}}
and the sesquilinear form
\mathcal{T}_{+}
) The form
{\mathcal{T}_{k}}
can be understood as a perturbation of the case
k=0
and then reads

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}_{k}}({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}})

\displaystyle={\mathcal{T}_{0}}({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}})-k^{2}(n^{2}u,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+ik(u,v)_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\langle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{k}-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{0}){\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}\rangle,

where the operator
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{k}
involves boundary integral operators that realize the coupling, and

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{0}
is the corresponding one for
k=0
;
see (
5.7
) for the precise definition.
The choice of the coupling between the PDE on
\Omega
and on
\Omega^{\operatorname{ext}}
is such that sequilinear form
{\mathcal{T}_{0}}

reproduces a variant of the classical symmetric coupling of Costabel
[
Cos88
]
and Han
[
Han90
]
. In particular,
{\mathcal{T}_{0}}

is positive semi-definite:
{\mathcal{T}_{0}}({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{u}})\gtrsim|u|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|u\|^{2}_{-1/2,\Gamma}+|u^{\mathrm{ext}}|^{2}_{1/2,\Gamma}
.
The difference
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{k}-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{0}
will turn out to be a compact operator
(see Prop.
3.3
and specifically Lemma
5.4
),
so that the sesquilinear form
{\mathcal{T}_{k}}

satisfies the Gårding inequality (
2.8
) below.
We introduce the operator
\Theta
(cf. Lemma
5.4
) and the sesquilinear form
\mathcal{T}_{+}
by

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbf{u}},\Theta{\mathbf{v}}\rangle

\displaystyle=2k^{2}(n^{2}u,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\langle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{k}-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{0}){\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}\rangle,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{+}({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}})

\displaystyle:={\mathcal{T}_{k}}({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}})+\langle{\mathbf{u}},\Theta{\mathbf{v}}\rangle

(2.6)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{T}_{0}}({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}})+k^{2}(n^{2}u,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+ik(u,v)_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}+\langle u^{\mathrm{ext}},1\rangle_{\Gamma}\langle 1,v^{\mathrm{ext}}\rangle_{\Gamma}

(2.7)

so that we have, with a constant
C>0
independent of

, the estimate

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\big(\mathcal{T}_{+}({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{u}})\big)=\operatorname{Re}\big({\mathcal{T}_{k}}({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{u}})+\langle{\mathbf{u}},\Theta{\mathbf{u}}\rangle\big)\geq C|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}|\!|\!|_{k}^{2}.

(2.8)

This coercivity is not unexpected in view of the fact that, up to the purely imaginary term

and the
non-negative term
\langle u^{\mathrm{ext}},1\rangle_{\Gamma}\langle 1,v^{\mathrm{ext}}\rangle_{\Gamma}
,
the sesquilinear form
\mathcal{T}_{+}
corresponds to a symmetric FEM-BEM coupling
for the operator
-\operatorname{div}(\nu\nabla u)+k^{2}n^{2}u
in
\Omega
and the Laplacian in
\Omega^{\operatorname{ext}}
. The sesquilinear form

is
also uniformly continuous:

|\mathcal{T}_{+}({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}})|\lesssim|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}|\!|\!|_{k}|\!|\!|\mathbf{v}|\!|\!|_{k}.

(2.9)

(II)

(continuity properties of
{\mathcal{T}_{k}}
)
Due to the presence of the boundary integral operators in

, the sesquilinear form
{\mathcal{T}_{k}}
is not bounded uniformly in

|{\mathcal{T}_{k}}({\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{u}})|\leq Ck^{\mu_{stab}}|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}|\!|\!|_{k}|\!|\!|\mathbf{v}|\!|\!|_{k},\qquad\text{for some }\mu_{stab}\leq 4,

(cf. Corollaries

However, the terms responsible for the

\Theta

It it convenient to decompose

\Theta=\Theta^{\mathcal{F}}+\Theta^{\mathcal{A}},

where the operator

is a smoothing operator of finite negative order with

|\!|\!|\cdot|\!|\!|_{k}

The operator

(2.11)

\displaystyle=\langle\cdot,\Theta^{\mathcal{F}}{\mathbf{w}}\rangle,

\displaystyle=\langle\cdot,\Theta^{\mathcal{A}}{\mathbf{w}}\rangle,

\displaystyle\eta^{(\mathcal{F})}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{V}\setminus\{0\}}\inf_{{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{N}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{h}^{C}}\frac{|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\mathcal{F}}_{\mathbf{w}}-{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{N}|\!|\!|_{k}}{|\!|\!|\mathbf{w}|\!|\!|_{k}},

\displaystyle\eta^{(\mathcal{A})}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{V}\setminus\{0\}}\inf_{{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{N}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{h}^{C}}\frac{|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbf{w}}-{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{N}|\!|\!|_{k}}{|\!|\!|\mathbf{w}|\!|\!|_{k}}.

(2.13)

(IV)

(Nitsche trick; cf. proof of Thms.
7.2
,
7.5
) For arbitrary discrete

, the Gårding inequality, the Galerkin orthogonality, and the

(2.14)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Re}\left[{\mathcal{T}_{k}}({\mathbf{e}}_{N},{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{v}}_{N})+\langle{\mathbf{e}}_{N},\Theta^{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{v}}_{N})\rangle-\langle{\mathbf{e}}_{N},\Theta^{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{v}}_{N})\rangle+\langle{\mathbf{e}}_{N},\Theta{\mathbf{e}}_{N}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim|\!|\!|{\mathbf{e}}_{N}|\!|\!|_{k}|\!|\!|{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{v}}_{N}|\!|\!|_{k}+|\langle{\mathbf{e}}_{N},\Theta^{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{v}}_{N})\rangle|+|\langle{\mathbf{e}}_{N},\Theta^{\mathcal{F}}{\mathbf{e}}_{N}\rangle|+|\langle{\mathbf{e}}_{N},\Theta^{\mathcal{A}}{\mathbf{e}}_{N}\rangle|.

The three terms

\langle{\mathbf{e}}_{N},\Theta^{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{v}}_{N})\rangle

\langle{\mathbf{e}}_{N},\Theta^{\mathcal{F}}{\mathbf{e}}_{N}\rangle

We start with the term

With the notation (

) for dual solutions and Galerkin orthogonality satisfied by
{\mathbf{e}}_{N}

for arbitrary

(2.15)

Completely analogously, we arrive at

\displaystyle\lesssim k^{\mu_{stab}}\eta^{(\mathcal{A})}|\!|\!|{\mathbf{e}}_{N}|\!|\!|_{k}|\!|\!|{\mathbf{u}}-{\mathbf{v}}_{N}|\!|\!|_{k}.

(2.16)

For the term
\langle{\mathbf{e}}_{N},\Theta^{\mathcal{F}}{\mathbf{e}}_{N}\rangle

with Galerkin orthogonality for arbitrary discrete

\displaystyle\left|\langle{\mathbf{e}}_{N},\Theta^{\mathcal{F}}{\mathbf{e}}_{N}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\left|{\mathcal{T}_{k}}({\mathbf{e}}_{N},{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathbf{e}}_{N}}-{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\mathcal{F}}_{N})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|{\mathcal{T}_{k}}({\mathbf{e}}_{N},{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathbf{e}}_{N}}-{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\mathcal{F}}_{N})+\langle{\mathbf{e}}_{N},\Theta^{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathbf{e}}_{N}}-{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\mathcal{F}}_{N})\rangle-\langle{\mathbf{e}}_{N},\Theta^{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathbf{e}}_{N}}-{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\mathcal{F}}_{N})\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\!|\!|{\mathbf{e}}_{N}|\!|\!|_{k}|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathbf{e}}_{N}}-{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\mathcal{F}}_{N}|\!|\!|_{k}+|\langle{\mathbf{e}}_{N},\Theta^{\mathcal{A}}({\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathbf{e}}_{N}}-{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\mathcal{F}}_{N})\rangle|.

since

\displaystyle\left|\langle{\mathbf{e}}_{N},\Theta^{\mathcal{F}}{\mathbf{e}}_{N}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\inf_{{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\mathcal{F}}_{N}\in{\mathcal{V}}_{h}^{C}}\left(1+k^{\mu_{stab}}\eta^{(\mathcal{A})}\right)|\!|\!|{\mathbf{e}}_{N}|\!|\!|_{k}|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\mathcal{F}}_{{\mathbf{e}}_{N}}-{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{\mathcal{F}}_{N}|\!|\!|_{k}\lesssim\left(1+k^{\mu_{stab}}\eta^{(\mathcal{A})}\right)\eta^{(\mathcal{F})}|\!|\!|{\mathbf{e}}_{N}|\!|\!|_{k}^{2}.

(V)

(quasi-optimality under abstract scale resolution; cf. Theorems

,
7.5

Inserting the above estimates into (

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{e}}_{N}\|^{2}_{k}

(2.17)

(VI)

The regularity theory for the solutions

(VII)

(tool: high- and low-pass filters; cf. Section
4
)
As a tool, we will require filter operators, the low-pass filter
{\mathbf{L}}_{\eta}
and the high-pass filter
{\mathbf{H}}_{\eta}
. The tuning parameter

plays an
important role to ensure contractivity in step
(X)
below. Key features of the operators are:

a)

The low-pass filter
{\mathbf{L}}_{\eta}
maps into a class of analytic functions.

b)

The high-pass filter
{\mathbf{H}}_{\eta}
acts componentwise and

{\mathbf{H}}_{\eta}(w,w^{m},w^{ext})=:(\widetilde{w},\widetilde{w}^{m},\widetilde{w}^{ext})
satisfies for

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{w}^{m}\|_{{s^{\prime}},\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\eta/k)^{s-s^{\prime}}\|w\|_{s,\Omega},

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{w}^{m}\|_{-1/2+s^{\prime},\Gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\eta/k)^{s-s^{\prime}}\|w^{m}\|_{-1/2+s,\Gamma}

(2.19a)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{w}^{ext}\|_{1/2+s^{\prime},\Gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\eta/k)^{s-s^{\prime}}\|w^{ext}\|_{{1/2+s},\Gamma}.

(2.19b)

From this follow the two estimates

\displaystyle k\|\widetilde{w}^{m}\|_{1/2,\Gamma}+\|\widetilde{w}^{m}\|_{3/2,\Gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w^{m}\|_{3/2,\Gamma},

\displaystyle k\|\widetilde{w}^{ext}\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}+\|\widetilde{w}^{ext}\|_{1/2,\Gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w^{ext}\|_{1/2,\Gamma},

(2.19c)

which will be needed below.

(VIII)

(regularity I: analyticity for analytic data; cf. Lemma
6.7
)
Due to the piecewise analyticity of the data (coefficients, interfaces, boundary), the solution
{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{\mathbf{w}}
is piecewise analytic if the right-hand side

{\mathbf{w}}
is. This is captured with the analyticity classes
{\mathcal{A}}
, and in fact, if
{\mathbf{w}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(M)

{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{\mathbf{w}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(CMk^{\beta})

\vartheta

The operator
\Theta^{\mathcal{A}}
maps into the class of piecewise analytic functions (cf. Lemma
5.4
)
so that the corresponding solution

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(Ck^{\beta+3}|\!|\!|\mathbf{w}|\!|\!|_{k})

(IX)

(regularity II: regularity of the problem with the “good” sign) The sequilinear form
\mathcal{T}_{+}
is (uniformly-in-

so that the Lax-Milgram Lemma and elliptic regularity theory can be brought to bear. The solution

of

\displaystyle\left|\!\left|\!\left|{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}^{+}_{\mathbf{w}}\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{k,\mathcal{V},1}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\!|\!|\mathbf{w}|\!|\!|_{k,\mathcal{V}^{\prime},1}.

(2.21)

(X)

(regularity by decomposition, cf. Theorem

)
For right-hand sides
{\mathbf{w}}
with finite regularity, the solution

and define, using the solution operators

The functions

(XI)

(regularity of

(frequency filters on surfaces,

Proposition 4.3

(frequency filters on surfaces, negative norms,

Let
\Omega

and

.

(ii)

,

(4.2)

(iii)

is the restriction to

of a function that is

Here

is independent of

and

Proof.

Items

–

are proven in
[

Property

follows by inspection of the construction given there.
To give more details: The construction is done via the spectral decomposition of the Laplace-Beltrami operator,
with low-frequency modes included in

If

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-\Delta_{\Gamma}1=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\lambda_{j}(1,\varphi_{j})_{0,\Gamma}\varphi_{j}},

Since the eigenfunctions corresponding to

∎

5
Discretization

We follow
[
MMPR20
]
and rewrite problem (
2.1
)
using a mortar formulation and boundary integrals.
We obtain three coupled problems for
u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
u^{\mathrm{ext}}
,
m:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\operatorname{div}(\nu\nabla u)-(kn)^{2}u=f&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\partial_{\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}}^{\nu,\mathrm{int}}u+iku-m=0&\text{on }\Gamma,\\
\end{cases}

(5.1a)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u-\left[\left(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+\mathcal{K}_{k}\right)u^{\mathrm{ext}}-\mathcal{V}_{k}(m-iku^{\mathrm{ext}})\right]=g\quad\,\text{on }\Gamma,\end{cases}

(5.1b)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathcal{B}_{k}u^{\mathrm{ext}}+ik\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{k}(u^{\mathrm{ext}})-\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{k}m=h&\,\;\quad\text{ on }\Gamma\end{cases},

(5.1c)

with boundary operators
\mathcal{B}_{k}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{k}
defined as

\mathcal{B}_{k}:=-\mathcal{W}_{k}-ik\left(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}-\mathcal{K}_{k}\right),\quad\quad\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{k}:=\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+\mathcal{K}^{\!\top}_{k}+ik\mathcal{V}_{k}.

(5.2)

Here, in the interest of generality, we allow for right-hand sides
g\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
and

h\in H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
.
We will usually collect the three fields
u
,
m
,
u^{\mathrm{ext}}
in the vector valued quantity
\mathbf{u}:=(u,m,u^{\mathrm{ext}})

and we will write
\mathbf{u}=:S_{k}^{-}(f,g,h)
for the solution of the problems.
The equivalence of (
5.1
) and (
2.1
) is valid for
g=h=0
.

This system of equations can be discretized in multiple ways.
In
[
MMPR20
]
, a combination of conforming FEM and BEM was discussed.
Building on that work,
[
EMM+22
]
considered a discontinuous
Galerkin approximation for the interior subproblem.
In the present article, we refine the analysis of
[
MMPR20
]
and
[
EMM+22
]
in that
we give a
k
-explicit analysis of both discretization schemes.
To that end, we introduce some notation concerning general
discretizations with finite element and boundary element spaces
in Sections
5.1
–
5.3
, following
closely the notation of
[
EMM+22
]
.

Before we can prove a result on the well-posedness of problem (
5.1
)
with polynomial bounds, we need the following preparatory lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

For
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
with
-\Delta u-k^{2}u=0
, we have the following
trace estimates:

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\Gamma}u\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}+\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}}^{\mathrm{int}}u\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}+\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}}^{\nu,\mathrm{int}}u\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{k}\|u\|_{1,k,\Omega}.

Proof.

The estimate of
\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}}^{\mathrm{int}}u\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}

follows from
[
LS09
, Lem. 15]
using
s=ik
.
To estimate the
\nu
-weighted flux

\partial_{\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}}^{\nu,\mathrm{int}}u
,
we decompose it into the normal and tangential part:

\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}\cdot\nu\nabla u=(\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}\cdot\nabla u)(\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}^{T}\nu\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma})+(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\Gamma}\cdot\nabla u)(\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}^{T}\nu{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\Gamma}}).

Since the geometry and
\nu
are smooth and using the fact that the tangential derivative
is a differential operator of order one, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}\cdot\nu\nabla u\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}}^{\mathrm{int}}u\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}+\|\nabla_{\Gamma}u\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}}^{\mathrm{int}}u\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}+\|u\|_{1/2,\Gamma}\lesssim\sqrt{k}\|u\|_{1,k,\Omega},

where in the last step we used the already estalished bound on
\partial_{\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}}^{\mathrm{int}}u
and
a standard trace theorem.
This also implies the estimate of the tangential gradient.
∎

Lemma 5.2

(polynomial well-posedness)
.

There exists a constant
\beta\geq 0
such that, for any right-hand
side
(f,g,h)\in L^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1/2}(\Gamma)\times H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
, the problem
(
5.1
)
has a unique solution
\mathbf{u}=(u,m,u^{\mathrm{ext}})\in{\mathcal{V}}=H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)\times H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
, which
satisfies the estimate

\displaystyle\|u\|_{1,k,\Omega}+\|m\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}+\|u^{\mathrm{ext}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim k^{\beta}\Big(\|f\|_{0,\Omega}+\|g\|_{1/2,\Gamma}+\|h\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}\Big).

The constant
\beta
can be bounded by
\beta\leq\beta_{0}+4
, where

\beta_{0}
is given by Assumption
2.1
.

Proof.

We use a construction similar to that
in the proof of
[
MMPR20
, Thm. 3.11]
.
Existence of the solution
(u,m,u^{\mathrm{ext}})
follows from standard Fredholm theory as the sesquilinear form satisfies a Gårding inequality
and uniqueness is implied by Assumption
2.1
. We thus focus on the
k
-explicit stability estimate.

By assumption,
\nu|_{\Omega}
is smooth near
\Gamma
. Let
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)\subset\Omega
be a tubular neighborhood of
\Gamma
such that
\nu|_{\Omega}
is smooth on
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)\cap\Omega
. Let
\chi_{\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfy
\operatorname{supp}\chi_{\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)}\subset{\mathcal{N}}(\Gamma)\cup\Omega^{\operatorname{ext}}
and
\chi_{\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)}\equiv 1
in a neighborhood of
\Gamma\cup\Omega^{\operatorname{ext}}
.
We use the following
auxiliary functions

\displaystyle\daleth

\displaystyle:=\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_{k}(-m+iku^{\mathrm{ext}})+\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{k}u^{\mathrm{ext}}\qquad\text{and }\qquad\mathcal{L}:=u\mathds{1}_{\Omega}+\daleth\chi_{\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)}.

By the jump conditions (
3.2
) satisfied by the operators, we deduce that

\displaystyle\llbracket\partial_{\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}}\daleth+ik\daleth\rrbracket_{\Gamma}=-m,\qquad\llbracket\daleth\rrbracket_{\Gamma}=-u^{\mathrm{ext}}.

(5.3)

Applying trace operators and using the equations (
5.1
) we obtain that
\daleth
solves

\displaystyle-\Delta\daleth-k^{2}\daleth

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Gamma$},\;\quad\partial_{\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}}^{\mathrm{int}}{\daleth}+ik\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{int}}\daleth=h\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{ext}}\daleth=-g+\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{int}}u\quad\text{on $\Gamma$}.

Restricted to
\Omega
, this is a standard Robin boundary value problem, for which

k
-explicit bounds are available
[
BSW16
, Cor. 1.10]
:

\displaystyle\|\daleth\|_{1,k,\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim k\|h\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}.

(5.4)

It is then an easy calculation that
\mathcal{L}
solves the following transmission problem:

\displaystyle-\operatorname{div}(\nu\nabla\mathcal{L})-(kn)^{2}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=f-\big[\operatorname{div}(\nu\nabla(\daleth\,\chi_{\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)})-(kn)^{2}\daleth\,\chi_{\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)}\big]\mathds{1}_{\Omega},

\displaystyle\llbracket\partial^{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}}\mathcal{L}+ik\mathcal{L}\rrbracket_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}}^{\nu,\mathrm{int}}\daleth-\partial_{\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}}^{\mathrm{int}}{\daleth}\quad\text{and}\quad\llbracket\mathcal{L}\rrbracket_{\Gamma}=\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{int}}\daleth+g,

The impedance jump

R_{1}:=\partial_{\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}}^{\nu,\mathrm{int}}\daleth-\partial_{\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}}^{\mathrm{int}}{\daleth}
,
the Dirichlet-jump
R_{0}:=g+\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{int}}{\daleth}
,
and the right-hand side
\tilde{f}

satisfy by Lemma
5.1
and standard trace estimates:

\displaystyle\|R_{1}\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{L.\penalty\ \ref{lemma:neumann_trace}}}}{{\lesssim}}k^{1/2}\|\daleth\|_{1,k,\Omega}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:lemma:existence_primal-20}}}{{\lesssim}}k^{3/2}\|h\|_{-1/2,\Gamma},

\displaystyle\|R_{0}\|_{1/2,\Gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\daleth\|_{1,k,\Omega}+\|g\|_{1/2,\Gamma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:lemma:existence_primal-20}}}{{\lesssim}}k\|h\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}+\|g\|_{1/2,\Gamma}.

We now lift these boundary jump functions. Namely,
we consider the function
\gimel\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Gamma)

such that

\displaystyle-\operatorname{div}{(\nu\nabla\gimel)}+(kn)^{2}\gimel

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{and}\quad\llbracket\partial^{\nu}_{\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}}\gimel+ik\gimel\rrbracket_{\Gamma}=R_{1},\;\,\llbracket\gimel\rrbracket_{\Gamma}=R_{0}.

Such a function exists and satisfies (see, e.g.,
[
LS09
, Prop. 9]
):

\|\gimel\|_{1,k,\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Gamma}\lesssim k\big(\|R_{1}\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}+\|R_{0}\|_{1/2,\Gamma}\big)\lesssim k^{2}(1+k^{1/2})\|h\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}+k\|g\|_{1/2,\Gamma}.

We consider another smooth cutoff function
\chi_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

with
\chi_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\equiv 1
in a neighborhood of
\overline{\Omega}
and

\operatorname{supp}{\chi_{\widetilde{\Omega}}}\subseteq\widetilde{\Omega}
for
\widetilde{\Omega}

as in Assumption
2.1
.
Then
\mathcal{L}-\gimel\chi_{\widetilde{\Omega}}
solves in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle-\operatorname{div}(\nu\nabla(\mathcal{L}-\gimel\chi_{\widetilde{\Omega}})-(kn)^{2}(\mathcal{L}-\gimel\chi_{\widetilde{\Omega}})

\displaystyle=f-\big[\operatorname{div}(\nu\nabla(\daleth\,\chi_{\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)})-(kn)^{2}\daleth\,\chi_{\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)}\big]\mathds{1}_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\qquad+\big[\operatorname{div}(\nu\nabla(\gimel\chi_{\widetilde{\Omega}}))+(kn)^{2}\gimel\chi_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\big]

\displaystyle=:\tilde{f}

with Sommerfeld radiation condition and
\operatorname{supp}{\widetilde{f}}\subseteq\widetilde{\Omega}
.
For the new right-hand side we get, since
\nu
is smooth in
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)\cap\Omega
, the estimate:

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{f}\|_{0,\widetilde{\Omega}}

\displaystyle\lesssim k\|\daleth\|_{1,k,\Omega}+k\|\gimel\|_{1,k,\widetilde{\Omega}\setminus\Gamma}+\|f\|_{0,\Omega}\lesssim k^{2}\|h\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}+k(k^{5/2}\|h\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}+k\|g\|_{1/2,\Gamma})+\|f\|_{0,\Omega}.

Assumption
2.1
then gives for any ball
B_{R}
:

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}-\gimel\chi_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\|_{1,k,B_{R}}\lesssim k^{\beta_{0}}\|\tilde{f}\|_{0,\Omega}.

This allows us to bound
\mathcal{L}
by:

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}\|_{1,k,B_{R}}\lesssim k^{\beta_{0}}\big(\|f\|_{0,\Omega}+k^{7/2}\|h\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}+k^{2}\|g\|_{1/2,\Gamma}\big)+k^{5/2}\|h\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}+k\|g\|_{1/2,\Gamma}.

From this the statement follows by
u=\mathcal{L}-\daleth\chi_{N({\Gamma})}
in
\Omega

and the previously established estimate (
5.4
). The functions
m
and
u^{\mathrm{ext}}
can
be bounded as the jumps of
\daleth
(cf. (
5.3
)). The dominant power of
k
then
stems from bounding
m
via Lemma
5.1
.
∎

5.1
Finite element and boundary element spaces

Meshes and element maps

The finite element spaces on
\Omega
and
\Gamma

are based on standard regular (i.e., no “hanging nodes”),
\gamma
-shape regular meshes
\Omega_{h}
. That is, the (open) elements
K\in\Omega_{h}

are images of a fixed reference simplex
\widehat{K}
under bijective element maps

\Phi_{K}
, they partition
\Omega
, and the element maps of elements sharing
a
j
-face (
0\leq j\leq d-1
) induce the same parametrization on that common facet.
A formal definition is given in
[
LMWZ10
, Def. 2.2]

or
[
MS23
, Sec. 8.1]
for
d=3
, and
[
Mel02
, Def. 2.4.1]
for
d=2
.
In addition, we require the mesh
\Omega_{h}
to be compatible with the
decomposition
\overline{\Omega}=\bigcup_{P\in\mathscr{P}}\overline{P}
, i.e.,
for each
K\in\Omega_{h}
there is a unique
P\in\mathscr{P}
with
K\subset\mathscr{P}
.
For
K\in\Omega_{h}
, we write
h_{K}=\operatorname{diam}K
and set
h:=\max_{K\in\Omega_{h}}h_{K}
.
Certain approximation results will require analytic element maps
\Phi_{K}
. It will be
convenient to make the following assumption.

Assumption 5.3

(normalizable regular triangulation)
.

Each element map

\Phi_{K}
can be written as
\Phi_{K}=R_{K}\circ A_{K}
, where
A_{K}
is an

affine
map, and the maps
R_{K}
and
A_{K}
satisfy for constants

C_{\operatorname*{affine}}
,
C_{\operatorname{metric}}
,
\gamma>0

independent of
K
:

\displaystyle\|A_{K}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widehat{K})}\leq C_{\operatorname*{affine}}h_{K},\qquad\|(A_{K}^{\prime})^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widehat{K})}\leq C_{\operatorname*{affine}}h_{K}^{-1},

\displaystyle\|(R_{K}^{\prime})^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{K})}\leq C_{\operatorname{metric}},\qquad\|\nabla^{n}R_{K}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widetilde{K})}\leq C_{\operatorname{metric}}\gamma^{n}n!\qquad\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

Here,
\widetilde{K}:=A_{K}(\widehat{K})
.

It is worth highlighting that Assumption
5.3
implies the shape
regularity of the element maps, viz.,

h_{K}^{-1}\|\Phi_{K}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widehat{K})}+h_{K}\|\Phi_{K}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\widehat{K})}\leq C

for some
C>0
depending only the constants
C_{\operatorname{affine}}
,
C_{\operatorname{metric}}
,

\gamma
.

By taking traces on
\Gamma
, a mesh
\Omega_{h}
on
\Omega
induces a mesh
\Gamma_{h}
on
\Gamma

with element maps induced by the element maps
\Phi_{K}

Approximation spaces

When working with discontinuous approximation spaces, it is
useful to also work in broken Sobolev spaces.
Given a mesh
\Omega_{h}
on
\Omega
and

, we define the space

H^{s}_{\mathrm{pw}}(\Omega_{h}):=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega):\,v{}_{|_{K}}\in H^{s}(K)\quad\forall K\in\Omega_{h}\}.

(5.5)

On the mesh
\Omega_{h}
we define the approximation spaces of standard mapped piecewise polynomials:
Denoting by
\mathbb{P}_{\ell}(\omega)
the space of

on the domain

, we set for

and

,
1

\mathcal{S}^{\ell,r}(\Omega_{h})=\{v\in H^{r}(\Omega):\,v{}_{|_{K}}\circ\Phi_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{\ell}(\widehat{K})\quad\forall K\in\Omega_{h}\}.

Analogously, we define on
\Gamma_{h}
for
r=0
,
1

\mathcal{S}^{\ell,r}(\Gamma_{h})=\{v\in H^{r}(\Gamma):\,v{}_{|_{F}}\circ\Phi_{K_{F}}\in\mathbb{P}_{\ell}(\widehat{F})\quad\forall F\in\Gamma_{h}\},

where
K_{F}
is the element of
\Omega_{h}
with

as a
(d-1)
-facet and
\widehat{F}=\Phi_{K_{F}}^{-1}(F)
.

DG-related notation

Let
\mathbf{n}_{K}
denote the outward pointing unit vector normal to
\partial K
.

(d-1)
-facets (simply called facets) are push-forwards of
(d-1)
-facets of
the reference simplex
\widehat{K}
. We collect the (open) internal facets (i.e., the facets lying in

)
and boundary facets (i.e., the facets lying on

) of

in the sets

For a mesh

and

. On each boundary facet

, where

We may fix

Finally, we recall that the following polynomial inverse inequality from

\displaystyle\|{\mathfrak{h}}^{1/2}p^{-1}\lambda_{h}\|_{0,\Gamma}\leq c_{inv}^{G}\|\lambda_{h}\|_{-\frac{1}{2},\Gamma}\qquad\forall\lambda_{h}\in S^{p,0}(\Gamma_{h}),\ p\geq 1.

(5.6)

with a constant

independent of the mesh size

and the polynomial degree

.

5.2
Conforming FEM

We start with the simpler case of the conforming FEM discretization.
We pick discrete spaces

{\mathcal{V}}^{C}_{h}:=V_{h}^{C}\times W_{h}\times Z_{h},\qquad V_{h}^{C}:=S^{p,1}(\Omega_{h}),\qquad W_{h}:=S^{p-1,0}(\Gamma_{h}),\qquad Z_{h}:=S^{p,1}(\Gamma_{h}).

We use the superscript
C
for the
H^{1}
-
conforming
space
V_{h}^{C}
to
distinguish it from the
discontinuous Galerkin
space
V_{h}^{DG}
defined below.

For the weak form of (
5.7
) we introduce the sesquilinear form

, we look at the terms individually using the mapping properties
from Lemma
5.4
. For simplicity, we only
treat the dominant term in detail. The others are bounded
analogously:

\displaystyle\|\Theta_{u^{\mathrm{ext}},v^{\mathrm{ext}}}v^{\mathrm{ext}}\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Theta_{u^{\mathrm{ext}},v^{\mathrm{ext}}}v^{\mathrm{ext}}\|_{0,\Gamma}\lesssim\|\Theta_{u^{\mathrm{ext}},v^{\mathrm{ext}}}v^{\mathrm{ext}}\|_{1,\Omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim k^{3}C_{u^{\mathrm{ext}},v^{\mathrm{ext}}}\|v^{\mathrm{ext}}\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}(1+k\vartheta_{u^{\mathrm{ext}},v^{\mathrm{ext}}}).\qed

5.3
Discontinuous Galerkin method

In this section, we discretize problem (
2.1
) using a
discontinuous Galerkin (DG) method for the interior subproblem.
For a detailed derivation of the method and numerical experiments, we refer to
[
EMM+22
]
.

We start by defining the penalty functions
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\delta
.
Denoting by
\nu_{K}
the analytic extension of
\nu_{|_{K}}
up to
\partial K
, we define the function
\widetilde{\nu}:\mathcal{F}_{h}^{I}\to\mathbb{R}

by
\widetilde{\nu}(\mathbf{x})=\max\{\left|\nu_{K_{1}}(\mathbf{x})\right|,\left|\nu_{K_{2}}(\mathbf{x})\right|\}
if
\mathbf{x}
is on a facet
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{I}
shared by
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
.
We recall that, for matrices, we denote by
\left|\cdot\right|
the spectral norm.
Similarly to
[
EMM+22
]
,
we set

\begin{split}\alpha(\mathbf{x}):=\mathfrak{a}\,&\frac{p^{2}\widetilde{\nu}(\mathbf{x})}{k{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathbf{x})},\qquad\beta(\mathbf{x}):=\mathfrak{b}\,\frac{k{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathbf{x})}{p\,\widetilde{\nu}(\mathbf{x})}\qquad\forall\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{I},\quad\delta(\mathbf{x}):=\mathfrak{d}\frac{k{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathbf{x})}{p^{2}}\quad\forall\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{B},\end{split}

(5.18)

with chosen constants
\mathfrak{a}>0,\mathfrak{b}\geq 0,\mathfrak{d}>0
. Throughout we assume that

\mathfrak{a}
is sufficiently large and
\mathfrak{d}
is sufficiently small.
We also require
\delta(\mathbf{x})\in(0,1/2)
.
Next, we need some notation for the jump and the average functionals on
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{I}

for piecewise smooth, scalar functions
v
and vector-valued functions
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}
.
At any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{I}
shared by the two elements
K_{\mathbf{x}}^{1}

and
K_{\mathbf{x}}^{2}
, the jumps
\llbracket v\rrbracket
and
\llbracket{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\rrbracket
,
and the averages
\{\!\!\{v\}\!\!\}
and
\{\!\!\{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\}\!\!\}

are defined as

\begin{split}&\llbracket v\rrbracket(\mathbf{x}):=v{}_{|_{K_{\mathbf{x}}^{1}}}(\mathbf{x})\,\mathbf{n}{}_{K_{\mathbf{x}}^{1}}+v{}_{|_{K_{\mathbf{x}}^{2}}}(\mathbf{x})\,\mathbf{n}_{K_{\mathbf{x}}^{2}},\quad\quad\{\!\!\{v\}\!\!\}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{2}\left(v{}_{|_{K_{\mathbf{x}}^{1}}}(\mathbf{x})+v{}_{|_{K_{\mathbf{x}}^{2}}}(\mathbf{x})\right),\\
&\llbracket{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\rrbracket(\mathbf{x}):={\boldsymbol{\tau}}{}_{|_{K_{\mathbf{x}}^{1}}}(\mathbf{x})\cdot\mathbf{n}{}_{K_{\mathbf{x}}^{1}}+{\boldsymbol{\tau}}{}_{|_{K_{\mathbf{x}}^{2}}}(\mathbf{x})\cdot\mathbf{n}{}_{K_{\mathbf{x}}^{2}},\quad\quad\{\!\!\{{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\}\!\!\}(\mathbf{x}):=\frac{1}{2}\left({\boldsymbol{\tau}}{}_{|_{K_{\mathbf{x}}^{1}}}(\mathbf{x})+{\boldsymbol{\tau}}{}_{|_{K_{\mathbf{x}}^{2}}}(\mathbf{x})\right).\end{split}

The discrete spaces are chosen as
V_{h}^{DG}:=S^{p,0}(\Omega_{h})
,
W_{h}=S^{p-1,0}(\Gamma_{h})
, and

Z_{h}:=S^{p,1}(\Gamma_{h})
and we set

\mathcal{V}^{DG}_{h}:=V_{h}^{DG}\times W_{h}\times Z_{h}.

We define the interior DG sesquilinear form by

\displaystyle a_{h}^{\Omega}(u_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{K\in\Omega_{h}}{\!\!\big(\int_{K}\nu\,\nabla u_{h}\cdot\overline{\nabla v_{h}}-\int_{K}(kn)^{2}u_{h}\overline{v_{h}}\big)}-\int_{\mathcal{F}_{h}^{I}}\left(\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket\cdot\{\!\!\{\overline{\nu\nabla_{h}v_{h}}\}\!\!\}+\{\!\!\{\nu\nabla_{h}u_{h}\}\!\!\}\cdot\llbracket\overline{v_{h}}\rrbracket\right)

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathcal{F}_{h}^{I}}\beta(ik)^{-1}\llbracket\nu\nabla_{h}u_{h}\rrbracket\llbracket\overline{\nu\nabla_{h}v_{h}}\rrbracket+\int_{\mathcal{F}_{h}^{I}}\alpha\,ik\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket\cdot\llbracket\overline{v_{h}}\rrbracket,

and the DG boundary sesquilinear form
b_{h}^{\Gamma}(\cdot,\cdot)
by

\begin{split}b_{h}^{\Gamma}(u_{h},v_{h})&:=-\int_{\Gamma}\delta(ik)^{-1}\nu\nabla_{h}u_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}\,\overline{\nu\nabla_{h}v_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}}-\int_{\Gamma}\delta u_{h}\,\overline{\nu\nabla_{h}v_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}}\\
&\quad\quad-\int_{\Gamma}\delta\,\nu\nabla_{h}u_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}\overline{v_{h}}+\int_{\Gamma}(1-\delta)iku_{h}\overline{v_{h}}.\end{split}

The sesquilinear form corresponding to the DG discretization of (
5.1
) is then given by

\begin{split}&{\mathcal{T}_{k}^{DG}}((u_{h},m_{h},u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}),(v_{h},\lambda_{h},v^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}))\\
&:=a_{h}^{\Omega}(u_{h},v_{h})+b_{h}^{\Gamma}(u_{h},v_{h})-(m_{h},\delta(ik)^{-1}\nu\nabla_{h}v_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}+(1-\delta)v_{h})_{0,\Gamma}\\
&\quad\quad-\langle(-\mathcal{W}_{k}-ik(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}-\mathcal{K}_{k})+ik(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+\mathcal{K}^{\!\top}_{k}+ik\mathcal{V}_{k}))u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}-(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+\mathcal{K}^{\!\top}_{k}+ik\mathcal{V}_{k})m_{h},v^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}\rangle_{\Gamma}\\
&\quad\quad+\langle-\delta(ik)^{-1}\nu\nabla_{h}u_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}+(1-\delta)u_{h}+\delta(ik)^{-1}m_{h},\lambda_{h}\rangle_{\Gamma}\\
&\quad\quad-\langle(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+\mathcal{K}_{k})u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}-\mathcal{V}_{k}(m_{h}-iku^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}),\lambda_{h}\rangle_{\Gamma}.\end{split}

(5.19)

The method can then be written in compact form as follows:

\begin{cases}\text{Find }\mathbf{u}^{h}:=(u_{h},m_{h},u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h})\in{\mathcal{V}}^{DG}_{h}=V_{h}^{DG}\times W_{h}\times Z_{h}\text{ such that}\\
{\mathcal{T}_{k}^{DG}}\left(\mathbf{u}^{h},\mathbf{v}^{h}\right)=(f,v_{h})_{0,\Omega}\quad\forall\mathbf{v}^{h}:=(v_{h},\lambda_{h},v^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h})\in{\mathcal{V}}^{DG}_{h}.\end{cases}

(5.20)

The natural norms of the DG method for the interior subproblem,
for
v\in H_{\mathrm{pw}}^{\frac{3}{2}+t}(\Omega_{h})
, with
t>0
arbitrary,
are given by

\begin{split}\|v\|^{2}_{dG(\Omega)}&:=\|\nu^{1/2}\,\nabla_{h}v\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\|kn\,v\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}+k^{-1}\|\beta^{1/2}\llbracket\nu\nabla_{h}v\rrbracket\|^{2}_{0,\mathcal{F}_{h}^{I}}+k\|\alpha^{1/2}\llbracket v\rrbracket\|^{2}_{0,\mathcal{F}_{h}^{I}}\\
&\quad+k^{-1}\|\delta^{1/2}\nu\nabla_{h}v\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}\|^{2}_{0,\Gamma}+k\|(1-\delta)^{1/2}v\|^{2}_{0,\Gamma}\end{split}

(5.21)

and

\begin{split}\|v\|^{2}_{dG^{+}(\Omega)}:=\|v\|^{2}_{dG(\Omega)}+k^{-1}\|\alpha^{-1/2}\{\!\!\{\nu\nabla_{h}v\}\!\!\}\|^{2}_{0,\mathcal{F}_{h}^{I}}.\end{split}

(5.22)

We further introduce the following two energy norms, which extend the
dG(\Omega)
and
dG^{+}(\Omega)
norms to the FEM-BEM coupling:

\displaystyle\left|\!\left|\!\left|(u,m,u^{\mathrm{ext}})\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{dG(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\|{u}\|_{dG(\Omega)}^{2}+\|m\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}^{2}+\|u^{\mathrm{ext}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma}^{2},

\displaystyle\left|\!\left|\!\left|(u,m,u^{\mathrm{ext}})\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{dG^{+}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\|{u}\|_{dG^{+}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|m\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}^{2}+\|{\mathfrak{h}}^{1/2}p^{-1}\ m\|_{0,\Gamma}^{2}+\|u^{\mathrm{ext}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma}^{2}.

The main ingredients in the convergence proof of
[
EMM+22
]

are a Gårding inequality and the boundedness of the
sesquilinear form. We now transfer these estimates to the
k
-explicit
setting.

Lemma 5.7

(
k
-explicit Gårding inequality)
.

There are constants
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
,
\mathfrak{d}_{0}
,
\varepsilon>0
independent of
k\geq k_{0}
such that, for

\mathfrak{a}\geq\mathfrak{a}_{0}
,

0<\mathfrak{d}\leq\mathfrak{d}_{0}
we have
for all
\mathbf{u}^{h}:=(u_{h},m_{h},u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h})\in\mathcal{V}^{DG}_{h}

\displaystyle|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}^{h}|\!|\!|_{dG(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim(\operatorname{Re}+\varepsilon\operatorname{Im})\Big[\mathcal{T}_{DG}\big(\mathbf{u}^{h},\mathbf{u}^{h}\big)+\big\langle\mathbf{u}^{h},\Theta\mathbf{u}^{h}\big\rangle\Big].

Proof.

Just as in the proof of Proposition
5.5
for the conforming case, we first derive an explicit representation of

{\mathcal{T}_{k}^{DG}}+\Theta
. Most of the boundary terms are the same
as in the continuous case, see (
5.16
). We get

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\mathcal{T}_{k}^{DG}}((u_{h},m_{h},u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}),(v_{h},\lambda_{h},v^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}))+\langle(u_{h},m_{h},u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}),\Theta(v_{h},\lambda_{h},v^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h})\rangle)\\
&=a_{h}^{\Omega}(u_{h},v_{h})+b_{h}^{\Gamma}(u_{h},v_{h})+2((kn)^{2}u_{h},v_{h})_{0,\Omega}\\
&\quad\quad+\langle(\mathcal{W}_{0}+1)u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}+(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+\mathcal{K}^{\!\top}_{0})m_{h},v^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}\rangle_{\Gamma}+\langle-(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+\mathcal{K}_{0})u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}+\mathcal{V}_{0}m_{h},\lambda_{h}\rangle_{\Gamma}\\
&\qquad-(m_{h},\delta(ik)^{-1}\nu\nabla_{h}v_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}+(1-\delta)v_{h})_{0,\Gamma}\\
&\qquad\qquad\quad+\langle-\delta(ik)^{-1}\nu\nabla_{h}u_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}+(1-\delta)u_{h}+\delta(ik)^{-1}m_{h},\lambda_{h}\rangle_{\Gamma}.\end{split}

(5.23)

We proceed similarly to
[
EMM+22
]
.
Selecting
(v_{h},\lambda_{h},v^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}):=(u_{h},m_{h},u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h})
and taking
\operatorname{Re}+\varepsilon\operatorname{Im}

give after some minor reordering to be more consistent with
[
EMM+22
]
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(\operatorname{Re}+\varepsilon\operatorname{Im})\Big({\mathcal{T}_{k}^{DG}}((u_{h},m_{h},u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}),(v_{h},\lambda_{h},v^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}))+\langle(u_{h},m_{h},u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}),\Theta(v_{h},\lambda_{h},v^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h})\rangle_{\Gamma}\Big)\\
&=(\operatorname{Re}+\varepsilon\operatorname{Im})\big[a_{h}^{\Omega}(u_{h},u_{h})+b_{h}^{\Gamma}(u_{h},u_{h})\big]+2\|kn\,u_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\langle\mathcal{V}_{0}m_{h},m_{h}\rangle+\langle(\mathcal{W}_{0}+1)u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h},u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}\rangle_{\Gamma}+2\varepsilon\operatorname{Im}\langle(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+\mathcal{K}_{0})u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h},m_{h}\rangle_{\Gamma}\\
&\qquad\qquad-\varepsilon k^{-1}\|\delta^{1/2}m_{h}\|_{0,\Gamma}^{2}-2\operatorname{Re}\langle m_{h},\delta(ik)^{-1}\nu\nabla_{h}u_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}\rangle_{\Gamma}+2\varepsilon\operatorname{Im}\langle(1-\delta)u_{h},m_{h}\rangle_{\Gamma}\end{split}

\displaystyle=:(\operatorname{Re}+\varepsilon\operatorname{Im})\big[a_{h}^{\Omega}(u_{h},u_{h})+b_{h}^{\Gamma}(u_{h},u_{h})\big]+2\|kn\,u_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{6}{T_{i}}.

We estimate each of these terms individually.

Starting with the standard DG terms, we can use

[
EMM+22
, Sec. 4, Prop. 2]
to get
for
\mathfrak{a}
sufficiently large and
\mathfrak{d}
sufficiently
small:

(\operatorname{Re}+\varepsilon\operatorname{Im})\big[a_{h}^{\Omega}(u_{h},u_{h})+b_{h}^{\Gamma}(u_{h},u_{h})\big]\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nu^{1/2}\nabla_{h}u_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}-\|kn\,u_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}\\
+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\left(k^{-1}\|\beta^{1/2}\llbracket\nu\nabla_{h}u_{h}\rrbracket\|^{2}_{0,\mathcal{F}_{h}^{I}}+k\|\alpha^{1/2}\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket\|^{2}_{0,\mathcal{F}_{h}^{I}}\right.\\
\left.+k^{-1}\|\delta^{1/2}\nu\nabla_{h}u_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}\|^{2}_{0,\Gamma}+k\|(1-\delta)^{1/2}u_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Gamma}\right).

The only negative term is
-\|kn\,v_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}
. We can thus
focus on the BEM contributions
T_{i}
.
For
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
, we have the standard coercivity:

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{V}_{0}m_{h},m_{h}\rangle+\langle(\mathcal{W}_{0}+1)u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h},u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}\rangle\geq c_{\mathcal{V}_{0}}\|m_{h}\|^{2}_{-1/2,\Gamma}+c_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}\|u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}\|^{2}_{1/2,\Gamma}.

(5.24a)

For
T_{3}
, we use Young’s inequality to get

\displaystyle T_{3}=2\varepsilon\operatorname{Im}\langle(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+\mathcal{K}_{0})u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h},m_{h}\rangle\geq-\varepsilon(1+2C_{\mathcal{K}_{0}})\big(\|u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}\|_{1/2,\Gamma}^{2}+\|m_{h}\|^{2}_{-1/2,\Gamma}\big).

(5.24b)

For
T_{4}
, we use the polynomial inverse inequality (
5.6
) to get,
after inserting the definition of
\delta
from (
5.18
),

\displaystyle T_{4}=-\varepsilon k^{-1}\|\delta^{1/2}m_{h}\|_{0,\Gamma}^{2}\geq-\varepsilon\mathfrak{d}c_{inv}^{G}\|m_{h}\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}^{2}.

(5.24c)

Similarly,
T_{5}
is dealt with again via an inverse estimate:

\displaystyle T_{5}

\displaystyle=-2\operatorname{Re}\langle m_{h},\delta(ik)^{-1}\nu\nabla_{h}u_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{4c_{inv}^{G}\mathfrak{d}}{\varepsilon}\|m_{h}\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon k^{-1}}{4}\|\delta^{1/2}\nu\nabla_{h}u_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}\|_{0,\Gamma}^{2}.

The term
T_{6}
does not depend on any boundary integral operator and thus
can be estimated just as in
[
EMM+22
, Eqn. (4.28)]
.
For a sufficiently large constant
c_{10}
depending on
\nu
,
the inverse estimate constant
c_{inv}^{G}
from (
5.6
),
and the shape regularity constant of
\Omega_{h}

we have

\displaystyle T_{6}

\displaystyle=-2\varepsilon\operatorname{Im}\langle(1-\delta)m_{h},u_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{4c_{10}\varepsilon}{\nu_{0}}\|m_{h}\|_{-\frac{1}{2},\Gamma}^{2}

(5.24d)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\Big(\|\nu^{1/2}\nabla_{h}u_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\nu_{0}(k_{0}c_{0})^{-2}\|kn\,u_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+k\|\alpha^{1/2}\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket\|_{0,\mathcal{F}_{h}^{I}}^{2}\Big).

We put everything together and choose
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
appropriately.
We end up with

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\operatorname{Re}+\varepsilon\operatorname{Im})&\Big({\mathcal{T}_{k}^{DG}}((u_{h},m_{h},u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}),(v_{h},\lambda_{h},v^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}))+\langle(u_{h},m_{h},u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}),\Theta(v_{h},\lambda_{h},v^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h})\rangle\Big)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{4}\|\nu^{1/2}\nabla_{h}u_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\Big(1-\frac{\varepsilon\nu_{0}(c_{0}k_{0})^{-2}}{4}\Big)\|kn\,u_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}\\
&\qquad\begin{multlined}+\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon\Big(2k^{-1}\|\beta^{1/2}\llbracket\nu\nabla_{h}u_{h}\rrbracket\|^{2}_{0,\mathcal{F}_{h}^{I}}+k\|\alpha^{1/2}\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket\|^{2}_{0,\mathcal{F}_{h}^{I}}\\
+k^{-1}\|\delta^{1/2}\nu\nabla_{h}u_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}\|^{2}_{0,\Gamma}+2k\|(1-\delta)u_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Gamma}\Big)\end{multlined}\\
&\qquad+\Big(c_{\mathcal{V}_{0}}-\varepsilon(1+2C_{\mathcal{K}_{0}})-\varepsilon\mathfrak{d}c_{inv}^{G}-\frac{4c_{inv}^{G}\mathfrak{d}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{4\varepsilon c_{10}}{\nu_{0}}\Big)\|m_{h}\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}^{2}\\
&\qquad+\big(c_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}-\varepsilon(1+2C_{\mathcal{K}_{0}})\big)\|u^{\mathrm{ext}}_{h}\|^{2}_{1/2,\Gamma}.\end{split}

(5.25)

We pick
\varepsilon
and
\mathfrak{d}
as

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle:=\min\Big(\frac{c_{\mathcal{V}_{0}}}{4\big(1+2C_{\mathcal{K}_{0}}+c_{inv}^{G}+\nu_{0}^{-1}4c_{10}\big)},\frac{c_{\mathcal{W}_{0}}}{2(1+C_{\mathcal{K}_{0}})},2(c_{0}k_{0})^{2}\nu_{0}^{-1},1\Big),\;\;\mathfrak{d}\leq\frac{c_{\mathcal{V}_{0}}\varepsilon}{16c_{inv}^{G}}.

(5.26)

This ensures that all the terms on the right-hand side of (
5.25
) are
positive and the stated result follows.
∎

Lemma 5.8

(
k
-explicit continuity bound)
.

Let
\Gamma
be analytic and
k\geq k_{0}>0
. Then, up to compact terms,
the DG-sesquilinear form
{\mathcal{T}_{k}^{DG}}
is bounded uniformly in
k
. Namely, there exists
C>0
depending
only on
\Gamma
,
k_{0}
,
\nu_{min}
, and the shape-regularity of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
such that for all

\mathbf{u}
,
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{pw}}:=H^{3/2+\varepsilon}_{pw}(\Omega_{h})\times H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)\times H^{1/2}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{T}_{k}^{DG}}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})+\langle\mathbf{u},\Theta^{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{v}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq C\left|\!\left|\!\left|\mathbf{u}\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{dG^{+}(\Omega)}\left|\!\left|\!\left|\mathbf{v}\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{dG^{+}(\Omega)}.

(5.27a)

If
\mathbf{u}
or
\mathbf{v}
are in the discrete space

\mathcal{V}^{DG}_{h}
then the corresponding
\left|\!\left|\!\left|\cdot\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{dG^{+}(\Omega)}
norm can
be weakened to the
\left|\!\left|\!\left|\cdot\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{dG(\Omega)}
norm:

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{T}_{k}^{DG}}(\mathbf{u}^{h},\mathbf{v})+\langle\mathbf{u}^{h},\Theta^{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{v}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq C|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}^{h}|\!|\!|_{dG(\Omega)}\left|\!\left|\!\left|\mathbf{v}\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{dG^{+}(\Omega)}\qquad\forall\mathbf{u}^{h}\in\mathcal{V}^{DG}_{h},\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{pw}},

(5.27b)

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{T}_{k}^{DG}}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}^{h})+\langle\mathbf{u},\Theta^{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{v}^{h}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq C\left|\!\left|\!\left|\mathbf{u}\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{dG^{+}(\Omega)}|\!|\!|\mathbf{v}^{h}|\!|\!|_{dG(\Omega)}\qquad\forall\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{pw}},\mathbf{v}^{h}\in\mathcal{V}^{DG}_{h}.

(5.27c)

The three continuity bounds in (
5.27
) are also valid with the operator
\Theta^{\mathcal{A}}
replaced by
\Theta
in view of
the continuity assertion (
5.11
) for
\Theta^{\mathcal{F}}
.

Proof.

We write
\mathbf{u}=:(u,m,u^{\mathrm{ext}})
and
\mathbf{v}=:(v,\lambda,v^{\mathrm{ext}})
for the different components
and focus on the non-discrete case (
5.27a
).
We again go back to the explicit representation of
{\mathcal{T}_{k}^{DG}}+\Theta
in
(
5.23
):

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\mathcal{T}_{k}^{DG}}((u,m,u^{\mathrm{ext}}),(v,\lambda,v^{\mathrm{ext}}))+\langle(u,m,u^{\mathrm{ext}}),\Theta(v,\lambda,v^{\mathrm{ext}})\rangle)\\
&=a_{h}^{\Omega}(u,v)+b_{h}^{\Gamma}(u,v)+2((kn)^{2}u,v)_{0,\Omega}\\
&\quad\quad+\langle(\mathcal{W}_{0}+1)u^{\mathrm{ext}}+(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+\mathcal{K}^{\!\top}_{0})m,v^{\mathrm{ext}}\rangle_{\Gamma}+\langle-(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+\mathcal{K}_{0})u^{\mathrm{ext}}+\mathcal{V}_{0}m,\lambda\rangle_{\Gamma}\\
&\qquad-\langle m,\delta(ik)^{-1}\nu\nabla_{h}v\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}+(1-\delta)v\rangle_{\Gamma}\\
&\qquad\qquad\quad+\langle-\delta(ik)^{-1}\nu\nabla_{h}u\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}+(1-\delta)u+\delta(ik)^{-1}m,\lambda\rangle_{\Gamma}.\end{split}

(5.28)

Most of the terms can easily estimated because they only contain boundary integral operators
for the Laplacian.
We focus on the few terms whose estimates are not obvious:

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle:=a_{h}^{\Omega}(u,v)+b_{h}^{\Gamma}(u,v),

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle:=-\langle m,\delta(ik)^{-1}\nu\nabla_{h}v\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}+(1-\delta)v\rangle_{\Gamma},

\displaystyle T_{3}

\displaystyle:=\langle-\delta(ik)^{-1}\nu\nabla_{h}u\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}+(1-\delta)u,\lambda\rangle_{\Gamma},

\displaystyle T_{4}

\displaystyle:=\langle\delta(ik)^{-1}m,\lambda\rangle_{\Gamma}.

Term
T_{1}
is a standard DG sesquilinear form and an upper bound in the energy
norm is derived in
[
MPS13
, Prop. 3.1]
.
The terms
T_{2},\,T_{3},\,T_{4}
are the same coupling terms that were analyzed
in
[
EMM+22
, Prop. 5.1]
. We only point out that all estimates
are indeed
k
-independent and only involve standard inverse estimates
and the use of a reconstruction operator to split functions
into a
H^{1/2}
-conforming contribution and smaller remainder.
∎

Finally, we have a boundedness result for the full sesquilinear form,
as long as we allow for polynomial growth in

of the continuity constant.

.

Proof.

Follows completely analogously to Lemma

,

.

The result follows from standard elliptic regularity theory. By lifting

to a function

in

and modifying

We then combine
[

∎

We consider the following auxiliary (“proxy”) problem, which represents a
“positive definite” version of the original adjoint problem (

):
Given
r\in\left(H^{1}(\Omega)\right)^{\prime}
,
R_{m}\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
, and
R_{ext}\in H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)

with
(R_{ext},1)_{0,\Gamma}=0

S^{+}_{k}(r,R_{m},R_{ext}):=(\phi,\phi_{m},\phi_{\mathrm{ext}})
solve

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\operatorname{div}(\nu\nabla\overline{\phi})+(kn)^{2}\overline{\phi}=\overline{r}\quad\qquad\qquad\;\text{in }\Omega,\\
\partial_{\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}}^{\nu,\mathrm{int}}\overline{\phi}+\overline{\phi_{m}}=0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{on }\Gamma,\\
\end{cases}

(6.2a)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\overline{\phi}+(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+\mathcal{K}_{0})\overline{\phi_{\mathrm{ext}}}+\mathcal{V}_{0}\overline{\phi_{m}}=\overline{R_{m}}\qquad\text{on }\Gamma,\end{cases}

(6.2c)

Lemma 6.3
.

The operator
S^{+}_{k}
is well defined, i.e., (

for

and

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{1,k,\Omega}+\|\phi_{m}\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}+\|\phi_{\mathrm{ext}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim k^{-1+\tau}\|r\|_{\left(H^{\tau}(\Omega)\right)^{\prime}}+\|R_{m}\|_{1/2,\Gamma}+\|R_{ext}\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}.

(6.3)

Proof.

We consider the weak formulation of
problem (
6.2
) and prove that the sesquilinear form

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{+}((v,\lambda,v^{\mathrm{ext}}),(\phi,\phi_{m},\phi_{\mathrm{ext}}))

\displaystyle:=(\nu\nabla v,\nabla\phi)_{0,\Omega}+((kn)^{2}v,\phi)_{0,\Omega}+\langle v,\phi_{m}\rangle_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle-\langle\lambda,\phi\rangle_{\Gamma}+\langle\lambda,\big(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+\mathcal{K}_{0}\big)\phi_{\mathrm{ext}}\rangle_{\Gamma}+\langle\lambda,\mathcal{V}_{0}\phi_{m}\rangle_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle+\langle v^{\mathrm{ext}},\mathcal{W}_{0}\phi_{\mathrm{ext}}\rangle_{\Gamma}-\langle v^{\mathrm{ext}},\big(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+\mathcal{K}^{\!\top}_{0}\big)\phi_{m}\rangle_{\Gamma}

is coercive with respect to the space

(We used the fact that, since the Green function of the
Laplacian is real valued, all the boundary operators
satisfy
\overline{\mathcal{V}_{0}\phi_{m}}=\mathcal{V}_{0}\overline{\phi_{m}}
etc.)
Choosing

we get:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{+}((\phi,\phi_{m},\phi_{\mathrm{ext}}),(\phi,\phi_{m},\phi_{\mathrm{ext}}))

\displaystyle=\|\nu^{1/2}\nabla\phi\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}+k^{2}\|n\phi\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\langle\phi,\phi_{m}\rangle_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle-\langle\phi_{m},\phi\rangle_{\Gamma}+\langle\phi_{m},\big(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+\mathcal{K}_{0}\big)\phi_{\mathrm{ext}}\rangle_{\Gamma}+\langle\phi_{m},\mathcal{V}_{0}\phi_{m}\rangle_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle+\langle\phi_{\mathrm{ext}},\mathcal{W}_{0}\phi_{\mathrm{ext}}\rangle_{\Gamma}-\langle\phi_{\mathrm{ext}},\big(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+\mathcal{K}^{\!\top}_{0}\big)\phi_{m}\rangle_{\Gamma}.

Using the fact that

is the adjoint of

, we take the real part and are left

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\big(\mathcal{T}_{+}((\phi,\phi_{m},\phi_{\mathrm{ext}}),(\phi,\phi_{m},\phi_{\mathrm{ext}}))\big)

\displaystyle=\|\nu^{1/2}\nabla\phi\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}+k^{2}\|n\phi\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\langle\phi_{m},\mathcal{V}_{0}\phi_{m}\rangle+\langle\phi_{\mathrm{ext}},\mathcal{W}_{0}\phi_{\mathrm{ext}}\rangle

where in the last step we used the coercivity of

and
\mathcal{W}_{0}
and

The continuity of the sesquilinear form

in the considered space follows from standard estimates and mapping properties of the operators.

What remains to be shown in order to conclude the proof is that

of the weak formulation of (

) is a bounded linear functional.
Let

. For

\big|(v,r)_{0,\Omega}\big|\leq k^{-1}\|r\|_{0,\Omega}\left(k\|v\|_{0,\Omega}\right)\leq k^{-1}\|r\|_{0,\Omega}\|v\|_{1,k,\Omega},

\big|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\langle v,r\rangle_{\left(H^{1}\Omega)\right)^{\prime}}\big|\leq\|r\|_{\left(H^{1}\Omega)\right)^{\prime}}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|r\|_{\left(H^{1}\Omega)\right)^{\prime}}\|v\|_{1,k,\Omega}.

By interpolation, we get

\big|_{H^{\tau}(\Omega)}\langle v,r\rangle_{\left(H^{\tau}\Omega)\right)^{\prime}}\big|\leq k^{-1+\tau}\|r\|_{\left(H^{\tau}\Omega)\right)^{\prime}}\|v\|_{1,k,\Omega}.

This, together with
the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, gives

\big|{{}_{H^{\tau}(\Omega)}\langle v,r\rangle_{\left(H^{\tau}\Omega)\right)^{\prime}}}+\langle\lambda,R_{m}\rangle+\langle v^{\mathrm{ext}},R_{ext}\rangle\big|\\
\leq k^{-1+\tau}\|r\|_{\left(H^{\tau}\Omega)\right)^{\prime}}\|v\|_{1,k,\Omega}+\|R_{m}\|_{1/2,\Gamma}\|\lambda\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}+\|R_{ext}\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}\|v^{\mathrm{ext}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma}.

From this, the

Lemma 6.4
.

Let

6.2
Analytic regularity

In this section, we show that for analytic data, the solution to the adjoint
problem is also analytic.

The following lemma analyzes how analycity classes are transformed if functions are multiplied by analytic functions or if derivatives are taken:

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an open set,

u\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}\big(C_{u},\vartheta_{u},\mathcal{O}\big)
, and
\eta\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}^{\infty}\big(C_{\eta},\vartheta_{\eta},\mathcal{O}\big)

Then
\nabla u
and
\eta u
are both analytic and

\displaystyle\nabla u

\displaystyle\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}\big({C_{\nabla u},\vartheta_{\nabla u}\mathcal{O}}\big),\qquad\text{with }\qquad C_{\nabla u}\leq\max(k,1)e^{2}\vartheta_{u}C_{u}\text{ and }\vartheta_{\nabla u}\leq e\vartheta_{u},

\displaystyle\eta u

\displaystyle\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}\big({C_{\eta u},\vartheta_{\eta u},\mathcal{O}}\big)\qquad\text{with }\qquad\;C_{\eta u}\leq C_{\eta}C_{u}\text{ and }\vartheta_{\eta u}\leq\vartheta_{\eta}+\vartheta_{u}.

Proof.

The product of analytic functions is again analytic (see
[
MS21
, Lem. 2.6]
). The precise behavior of the constants
for
\eta u
can be found by tracking the proof.
For
\eta u
, we compute

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{p}(\nabla u\big)\|_{0,\mathcal{O}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla^{p+1}(\nabla u\big)\|_{0,\mathcal{O}}\leq C_{u}\vartheta_{u}^{p+1}\max(p+2,k)^{p+1}

\displaystyle\leq\big[C_{u}\vartheta_{u}e\big]\max(k,1)2^{p}\vartheta_{u}^{p}\max(p+1,k)^{p},

where in the last step we used the crude estimate
(p+2)\leq e^{p+1}
to estimate

\displaystyle(p+2)^{p+1}

\displaystyle=(p+1)^{p}(p+2)\Big(1+\frac{1}{p+1}\Big)^{p+1}\leq(p+1)^{p}\,e^{p+2}.\qed

\left(\mathcal{W}_{k}+ik(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}-\mathcal{K}^{\!\top}_{k})-ik(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+\mathcal{K}_{k}+ik\mathcal{V}_{k})\right)\overline{\delta_{\mathrm{ext}}}-\left((\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+\mathcal{K}^{\!\top}_{k})+ik\mathcal{V}_{k}\right)\overline{\delta_{m}}\\
=\mathcal{S}_{4}\overline{\psi_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\mathcal{F}}}+\mathcal{S}_{5}\overline{\widehat{\psi_{m}^{\mathcal{F}}}}+{\mathcal{A}}_{4}\overline{\psi_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\mathcal{F}}}+{\mathcal{A}}_{5}\overline{\widehat{\psi_{m}^{\mathcal{F}}}}+\mathcal{S}_{6}\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{int}}(\overline{\psi^{\mathcal{F}}})+{\mathcal{A}_{6}}\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{int}}(\overline{\psi^{\mathcal{F}}}).

Collecting all the “finite regularity contributions”, we get the new right-hand sides

\displaystyle\overline{\widetilde{r}}:=-2(kn)^{2}\overline{\psi^{\mathcal{F}}},\qquad\overline{\widetilde{R}_{m}}:=\mathcal{S}_{1}\overline{\psi_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\mathcal{F}}}+\mathcal{S}_{2}\overline{\widehat{\psi_{m}^{\mathcal{F}}}}+\mathcal{S}_{3}\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{int}}(\overline{\psi^{\mathcal{F}}}),\qquad\overline{\widetilde{R}_{\mathrm{ext}}}:=\mathcal{S}_{4}\overline{\psi_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\mathcal{F}}}+\mathcal{S}_{5}\overline{\widehat{\psi_{m}^{\mathcal{F}}}}+\mathcal{S}_{6}\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{int}}(\overline{\psi^{\mathcal{F}}}).

From the mapping properties of the operators
\mathcal{S}_{i}

and
k\|\psi^{\mathcal{F}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma}\lesssim k\|\psi^{\mathcal{F}}\|_{1,k,\Omega}

with the properties of the high-frequency filters,

\displaystyle|\!|\!|\widetilde{\mathbf{r}}|\!|\!|_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime},1}

\displaystyle=\|\widetilde{r}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\widetilde{R}_{m}\|_{3/2,\Gamma}+\|\widetilde{R}_{\mathrm{ext}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim k^{2}\|\psi^{\mathcal{F}}\|_{0,\Omega}+k\|\psi_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\mathcal{F}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma}+k\|\widehat{\psi_{m}^{\mathcal{F}}}\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}+k\|\psi^{\mathcal{F}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim k\left(\|\psi^{\mathcal{F}}\|_{1,k,\Omega}+\|\psi_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\mathcal{F}}\|_{1/2,\Gamma}+\|\widehat{\psi_{m}^{\mathcal{F}}}\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:phip_energy_est}}}{{\lesssim}}\eta^{1/4}|\!|\!|\mathbf{r}|\!|\!|_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime},1}.

Thus, the by choosing
\eta
sufficiently small to compensate for the implied
constant, we get
(iii)
for the remainder
\widetilde{\Psi}=(\widetilde{\psi},\widetilde{\psi}_{m},\widetilde{\psi}_{\mathrm{ext}}):=S_{k}^{-}(\widetilde{r},\widetilde{R}_{m},\widetilde{R}^{\mathrm{ext}})
.

In order to prove
(iii)

we
collect the remaining analytic terms as

\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{A}}:=\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{A}}_{I}+\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{A}}_{II},

where
\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{A}}_{II}

\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{A}}_{II}:=S_{k}^{-}\left(0,\quad\overline{{\mathcal{A}}_{1}(\overline{\psi_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\mathcal{F}}})+{\mathcal{A}}_{2}(\overline{\widehat{\psi_{m}^{\mathcal{F}}}})+\mathcal{A}_{3}\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{int}}(\overline{\psi^{\mathcal{F}}})},\quad\overline{{\mathcal{A}}_{4}(\overline{\psi_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\mathcal{F}}})+{\mathcal{A}}_{5}(\overline{\widehat{\psi_{m}^{\mathcal{F}}}})+\mathcal{A}_{6}\gamma_{0}^{\mathrm{int}}(\overline{\psi^{\mathcal{F}}})}\right).

(6.10)

This clearly gives that

\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{A}}=\boldsymbol{\psi}-\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{F}}-\widetilde{\Psi}
, as

For

\displaystyle L_{\Omega,\eta}r

\displaystyle\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(C\|r\|_{0,\Omega},\vartheta_{\eta},\mathscr{P}),\;\;\qquad\qquad\qquad L_{\Gamma,\eta}^{+}R_{m}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(C\|R_{m}\|_{1/2,\Gamma},\vartheta_{\eta},\mathbb{R}^{d}),

\displaystyle L_{\Gamma,\eta}^{-}R_{ext}

\displaystyle\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(Ck^{d/2}\|R_{ext}\|_{-1/2,\Gamma},\vartheta_{\eta},\mathcal{O},\Gamma),

for a suitable (unilateral) tubular neighborhood
\mathcal{O}\subset\Omega
of
\Gamma
.

6.7
, gives that
\Psi^{\mathcal{A}}_{I}=:\left((\psi^{\mathcal{A}})_{I},(\psi_{m}^{\mathcal{A}})_{I},(\psi_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\mathcal{A}})_{I}\right)

for a suitable

\begin{split}M_{I}&\leq C\max(1,k^{\beta})\big(\|r\|_{0,\Omega}+\|R_{m}\|_{1/2,\Gamma}+k^{d/2}\|R_{ext}\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}\big)\\
&\leq C\max(1,k^{\beta})k^{d/2}\big(\|r\|_{0,\Omega}+\|R_{m}\|_{1/2,\Gamma}+\|R_{ext}\|_{-1/2,\Gamma}\big).\end{split}

For

and

Finally, by iterating the argument of Lemma

, we can eliminate the remainder term and prove the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 6.11

(Regularity splitting)
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\psi}:=(\psi,\psi_{m},\psi_{\mathrm{ext}})
solve the adjoint problem (
6.1
)
for right-hand sides
\mathbf{r}=(r,R_{m},R_{ext})
satisfying

r\in L^{2}(\Omega),\quad R_{m}\in H^{3/2}(\Gamma),\quad R_{ext}\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma).

Then, we can decompose
\boldsymbol{\psi}
as

\displaystyle\psi=\psi^{\mathcal{F}}+\psi^{\mathcal{A}},\quad\psi_{m}=\psi_{m}^{\mathcal{F}}+\psi_{m}^{\mathcal{A}},\quad\psi_{\mathrm{ext}}=\psi_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\mathcal{F}}+\psi_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\mathcal{A}},

with the following properties:

(i)

The finite regularity terms satisfy, for a constant
C>0
independent of
k
,

\displaystyle|\!|\!|\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{F}}|\!|\!|_{k,\mathcal{V},1}

\displaystyle\leq C|\!|\!|\mathbf{r}|\!|\!|_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime},1}.

(ii)

The analytic terms satisfy

\displaystyle\psi^{\mathcal{A}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}\big(M,\vartheta,\mathscr{P}),\quad\psi_{m}^{\mathcal{A}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}\big(kM,\vartheta,\mathcal{O},\Gamma),\quad\psi_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\mathcal{A}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}\big(M,\vartheta,\mathcal{O},\Gamma)

with

M\leq Ck^{\beta+3}\big(\|r\|_{0,\Omega}+\|R_{m}\|_{3/2,\Gamma}+\|R_{ext}\|_{1/2,\Gamma}\big),

where the tubular neighborhood
\mathcal{O}\subset\Gamma
of
\Gamma
and
\vartheta
depend solely on
\Omega
,
\mathscr{P}
,
\nu
, and
n
.

Proof.

We apply iteratively Lemma
6.10
.
We start by writing
\mathbf{r}^{(0)}=(r^{(0)},R_{m}^{(0)},R_{ext}^{(0)}):=(r,R_{m},R_{ext})

and decompose the
solution
\mathbf{\psi}
according to Lemma
6.10
:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\psi}=\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{F}}_{(0)}+\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{A}}_{(0)}+\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\psi}}_{(0)}.

Since the remainder
\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\psi}}_{(0)}
solves the same
problem with a new right-hand side

\mathbf{r}^{(1)}:=({r}^{(1)},{R}^{(1)}_{m},{R}^{(1)}_{\mathrm{ext}})
,
namely,
\mathbf{\widetilde{\psi}}_{(0)}=S^{-}_{k}(\mathbf{r}^{(1)})
,
we can again split this as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\psi}}_{(0)}=\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{F}}_{(1)}+\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{A}}_{(1)}+\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\psi}}_{(1)}

and so on, defining sequences
\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{F}}_{(\ell)}
,
\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{A}}_{(\ell)}
,
\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\psi}}_{(\ell)}

of functions by

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\psi}}_{(\ell-1)}=\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{F}}_{(\ell)}+\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{A}}_{(\ell)}+\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\psi}}_{(\ell)}\qquad\text{with}\qquad\boldsymbol{\widetilde{\psi}}_{(\ell)}=S_{k}^{-}\big(\boldsymbol{r}^{(\ell+1}\big),\qquad\ell\geq 1.

From Lemma
6.10
(iii)
, for any
\ell\geq 1
,
the right-hand sides
\mathbf{r}^{(\ell)}
satisfy

\displaystyle|\!|\!|\mathbf{r}^{(\ell)}|\!|\!|_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime},1}\leq q|\!|\!|\mathbf{r}^{(\ell-1)}|\!|\!|_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime},1}\leq q^{\ell}|\!|\!|\mathbf{r}^{(0)}|\!|\!|_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime},1}

Since
0<q<1
, we get that these right-hand sides converge to zero as
\ell\to\infty
.
This implies that we can write

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\psi}:=\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{F}}+\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{A}},\qquad\text{with}\quad\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{F}}:=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}{\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{F}}_{(\ell)}}\quad\text{and}\quad\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{A}}:=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}{\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{A}}_{(\ell)}}.

For the finite regularity part
\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{F}}=:(\psi^{\mathcal{F}},\psi_{m}^{\mathcal{F}},\psi_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\mathcal{F}})
,
we get the estimate

\displaystyle|\!|\!|\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{F}}|\!|\!|_{k,\mathcal{V},1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}{|\!|\!|\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathcal{F}}_{(\ell)}|\!|\!|_{k,\mathcal{V},1}}\lesssim\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}{|\!|\!|\mathbf{r}^{(\ell)}|\!|\!|_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime},1}}\lesssim|\!|\!|\mathbf{r}^{(0)}|\!|\!|_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime},1}\big(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}{q^{\ell}}\big).

Since the geometric series converges, we get the stated estimate for
the finite regularity terms.

For the analytic part, we focus on the interior contribution
and denote it as
\psi^{\mathcal{A}}
.
For any
\ell\geq 0
, we get

\psi^{\mathcal{A}}_{(\ell)}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(M_{(\ell)},\vartheta,\Omega)

(note that
\vartheta
is independent of the right-hand side

\mathbf{r}^{(\ell)}
and is thus the same in each iteration), with the leading constant

\displaystyle M_{(\ell)}

\displaystyle\lesssim k^{\beta+3}|\!|\!|\mathbf{r}^{(\ell)}|\!|\!|_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime},1}\lesssim k^{\beta+3}q^{\ell}|\!|\!|\mathbf{r}^{(0)}|\!|\!|_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime},1}.

We can therefore again argue as before that the geometric series converges, and we
get

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{p}\psi^{\mathcal{A}}\|_{0,\Omega\setminus\partial\mathscr{P}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}{\|\nabla^{p}\psi^{\mathcal{A}}_{(\ell)}\|_{0,\Omega\setminus\partial\mathscr{P}}}\lesssim k^{\beta+3}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}{M_{(\ell)}\vartheta^{p}\max(p+1,k)^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim k^{\beta+3}\vartheta^{p}\max(p+1,k)^{p}|\!|\!|\mathbf{r}^{(0)}|\!|\!|_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime},1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}{q^{\ell}}.

In other words
\psi^{\mathcal{A}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}\big(M,\vartheta,\Omega\big)

with

M:=\frac{k^{\beta+3}}{1-q}\Big(\|r\|_{0,\Omega}+\|R_{m}\|_{3/2,\Gamma}+\|R_{ext}\|_{1/2,\Gamma}\Big).

The result for the other contributions
\psi_{m}^{\mathcal{A}}
and
\psi_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\mathcal{A}}
follows along the same lines.
∎

7
Convergence of the discrete schemes

With the results of the previous two sections in place, we can prove that both the
conforming FEM and the DG method lead to
k
-independent quasi-optimal error estimates
provided a weak (
k
-dependent) resolution condition is satisfied.
For
s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
M>0
, and a fixed tubular neighborhood
\mathcal{O}
of
\Gamma

sufficiently enough so that
\nu
,
n
are smooth on
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathbf{n}_{\Gamma}
has a smooth extension
to
\mathcal{O}
, we also introduce the following product spaces:

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{s}

\displaystyle:=H^{1+s}_{\mathscr{P}}(\Omega)\times H^{-1/2+s}(\Gamma)\times H^{1/2+s}(\Gamma),

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{M}

\displaystyle:=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(M,\vartheta_{u},\mathscr{P})\times\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(kM,\vartheta_{m},\mathcal{O},\Gamma)\times\boldsymbol{\mathcal{A}}(M,\vartheta_{u^{\mathrm{ext}}},\mathcal{O},\Gamma).

The space
\mathcal{V}^{s}
is equipped with the norm

Most arguments of the convergence analysis will be presented in detail only for the DG case,
as the proofs for the conforming FEM would be essentially the

7.1
Discontinuous Galerkin method

For

, we define the approximation quantities

\displaystyle\eta^{(s)}_{DG}:=\adjustlimits{\sup}_{0\neq\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{V}^{s}}{\inf}_{\mathbf{u}_{h}\in\mathcal{V}^{DG}_{h}}\frac{\left|\!\left|\!\left|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{dG^{+}(\Omega)}}{|\!|\!|\mathbf{u}|\!|\!|_{k,\mathcal{V},{s}}},\qquad\eta^{(\mathcal{A})}_{DG}:=\adjustlimits{\sup}_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{A}^{1}}{\inf}_{\mathbf{u}_{h}\in\mathcal{V}^{DG}_{h}}\left|\!\left|\!\left|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{dG^{+}(\Omega)}.

(7.1)

In our final convergence result, we will assume these quantities to be sufficiently small.
We remark that

\forall\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{A}^{M}\colon\qquad\inf_{\mathbf{u}_{h}\in\mathcal{V}^{DG}_{h}}\left|\!\left|\!\left|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{h}\right|\!\right|\!\right|_{dG^{+}(\Omega)}\leq M\eta^{(\mathcal{A})}_{DG}.

(7.2)

Our convergence analysis hinges on duality arguments. These duality arguments are possible for the chosen DG-discretization

(7.3)

Proof.

The result follows as in the proof of

A.1

We start with the following preparatory result concerning

In the following, for any two continuously embedded Banach spaces

Lemma A.1
.

Let

be Banach spaces with continuous inclusion.
Let

be a (semi)-norm on

For

, define the following weighted norms:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X_{1},\tau}

\displaystyle:=|u|_{X_{1}}+\tau^{-1}\|u\|_{X_{0}},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X_{0},\tau}

\displaystyle:=\|u\|_{X_{0}},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X_{\theta},\tau}

\displaystyle:=|u|_{X_{\theta}}+\tau^{-\theta}\|u\|_{X_{0}},\quad 0<\theta<1.

For

\displaystyle\big\|u\big\|_{X_{\theta},\tau}\sim\big\|u\big\|_{[(X_{\theta_{0}},\|\cdot\|_{X_{\theta_{0}},\tau}),(X_{\theta_{1}},\|\cdot\|_{X_{\theta_{1}},\tau})]_{\eta,2}}\qquad\text{with}\qquad\eta:=\frac{\theta-\theta_{0}}{\theta_{1}-\theta_{0}}

\theta_{0}
,
\theta_{1}
, and
\theta
but independent of
\tau
.

We now apply the Reiteration Theorem
[
Tar07
, Thm. 26.3]
as discussed in Step 2
with
E_{0}=(X_{0},\|\cdot\|_{X_{0},\tau})
,
E_{1}=(X_{1},\|\cdot\|_{X_{1},\tau})

and
F_{i}
as in Step 2. We obtain

\displaystyle\big\|u\big\|_{[(X_{0},\|\cdot\|_{X_{0},\tau}),(X_{1},\|\cdot\|_{X_{1},\tau})]_{\theta,2}}\sim\big\|u\big\|_{[(X_{\theta_{0}},\|\cdot\|_{X_{\theta_{0}},\tau}),(X_{\theta_{1}},\|\cdot\|_{X_{\theta_{1}},\tau})]_{\eta,2}}

(A.1)

with implied constants depending only on
\theta_{0},\theta_{1}
and
\theta
, but independent of
\tau
.
The proof of the lemma is completed by noting that the left-hand side of
(
A.1
) is equivalent to
\|u\|_{X_{\theta},\tau}
by Step 1.
∎

Corollary A.2
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be bounded Lipschitz domains and

\partial\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}
be smooth. Let
k\geq k_{0}>0
,

0\leq s_{0}<s_{1}
. Then for every

0<\theta<1
there hold the norm equivalences

\displaystyle\|u\|_{(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1},k,\mathcal{O}}

\displaystyle\sim\|u\|_{[(H^{s_{0}}(\mathcal{O}),\|\cdot\|_{{s_{0}},k,\mathcal{O}}),(H^{s_{1}}(\mathcal{O}),\|\cdot\|_{s_{1},k,\mathcal{O}})]_{\theta,2}},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1},k,\partial\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}}

\displaystyle\sim\|u\|_{[(H^{s_{0}}(\partial{\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}}),\|\cdot\|_{s_{0},k,\partial{\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}}}),(H^{s_{1}}(\partial{\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}}),\|\cdot\|_{s_{1},k,\partial{\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}}})]_{\theta,2}}

with implied constants independent of
k
.

Proof.

Follows from Lemma
A.1
.
An alternative proof can be obtained by eigenfunction expansions.
For example, for the first estimate, consider the variational eigenvalue problem: Find
(u,\lambda)\in H^{s_{1}}(\mathcal{O})\setminus\{0\}\times{\mathbb{R}}

such that
(u,v)_{H^{s_{1}}(\mathcal{O})}=\lambda^{s_{1}}(u,v)_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}
for all
v\in H^{s_{1}}(\mathcal{O})
where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{H^{s_{1}}(\mathcal{O})}
denotes the scalar product on

H^{s_{1}}(\mathcal{O})
. One can normalize the eigenpairs
(\varphi_{j},\lambda_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}

such they form an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
and an orthogonal basis of
H^{s_{1}}(\mathcal{O})
. This, together with interpolation, results in the norm equivalences

\|u\|^{2}_{H^{\theta s_{1}}(\mathcal{O})}\sim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}(1+\lambda_{j}^{\theta s_{1}})u_{j}^{2}
for any
\theta\in[0,1]
, where
u_{j}=(u,\varphi_{j})_{L^{2}({\mathcal{O}})}
.
Hence,

\|u\|^{2}_{s^{\prime},k,\mathcal{O}}\sim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\left(\lambda_{j}^{s^{\prime}}+k^{2s^{\prime}}\right)u_{j}^{2}
for
0\leq s^{\prime}\leq s_{1}
.
From
[
Tar07
, Sec. 23]
, one gets, with constants depending only on
\theta
,

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}_{[(H^{s_{0}}(\mathcal{O}),\|\cdot\|_{s_{0},k,\mathcal{O}}),(H^{s_{1}}(\mathcal{O}),\|\cdot\|_{s_{1},k,\mathcal{O}})]_{\theta,2}}

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\left(\lambda_{j}^{s_{0}}+k^{2s_{0}}\right)^{1-\theta}\left(\lambda_{j}^{s_{1}}+k^{2s_{1}}\right)^{\theta}u_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}(\lambda_{j}^{(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1}}+k^{2(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1}})u_{j}^{2}\sim\|u\|^{2}_{(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1},k,\mathcal{O}},

where the second equivalence follows from distinguishing between the cases
\lambda_{j}\leq k^{2}
and
\lambda_{j}>k^{2}
.
∎

We are in position to prove a lifting result.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
\omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, be a bounded domain with a smooth boundary.
For
\tau\in(0,1]
and
g\in H^{s-1/2}(\partial\omega)
with
s\geq 1
, let
{\mathcal{G}}
be the solution to

\displaystyle-\Delta{\mathcal{G}}+\tau^{-2}{\mathcal{G}}=0\quad\mbox{ in $\omega$},\qquad{\mathcal{G}}{}_{|_{\partial\omega}}=g.

Then, there is
C_{s}>0
independent of
\tau
and
g
such that

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{G}}\|_{s,\omega}+\tau^{-s}\|{\mathcal{G}}\|_{0,\omega}\leq C_{s}\left[\|g\|_{{s-1/2},\partial\omega}+\tau^{-(s-1/2)}\|g\|_{0,\partial\omega}\right].

Proof.

For
\sigma>0
, we introduce the norms

\|{\mathcal{G}}\|_{\sigma,\tau,\omega}:=\|{\mathcal{G}}\|_{\sigma,\omega}+\tau^{-\sigma}\|{\mathcal{G}}\|_{0,\omega}
,
and

\|g\|_{\sigma,\tau,\partial\omega}:=\|g\|_{\sigma,\partial\omega}+\tau^{-\sigma}\|g\|_{0,\omega}
.

Step 0 (a norm equivalence):

Before we tackle the PDE, we note that we can estimate lower order norms by a weighted linear combination
of a higher order norm and the
L^{2}
-norm. Namely, for
\sigma\geq 2
, the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\sigma-2,\partial\omega}\lesssim\tau^{2}\big(\|g\|_{\sigma,\partial\omega}+\tau^{-\sigma}\|g\|_{0,\partial\omega}\big).

(A.2)

This follows by a multiplicative interpolation estimate and Young’s inequality.
Namely, for
\theta:={(\sigma-2)}/{\sigma}
, we have

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\sigma-2,\partial\omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{2}\|g\|_{\sigma,\partial\omega}^{\theta}\tau^{-2}\|g\|_{0,\partial\omega}^{1-\theta}\lesssim\tau^{2}\big(\|g\|_{\sigma,\partial\omega}+\tau^{-2/(1-\theta)}\|g\|_{0,\partial\omega}\big).

By inserting the value of
\theta
, estimate (
A.2
) follows.

Step 1 (shift theorem for
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
):
The case
s=1
is given in
[
MR21
, Lemma 4.5]
. For
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the estimate

\|{\mathcal{G}}\|_{s,\tau,\omega}\leq C_{s}\|g\|_{s-1/2,\tau,\partial\omega}
follows inductively from
the standard shift theorem for the Laplacian see, e.g.,
[
GT01
, Thm. 8.13]
by writing
-\Delta{\mathcal{G}}=-\tau^{-2}{\mathcal{G}}
on
\omega
with Dirichlet conditions
{\mathcal{G}}=g
on
\partial\omega
:

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{G}\|_{s,\omega}+\tau^{-s}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{0,\omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{-2}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{s-2,\omega}+\|g\|_{s-1/2,\partial\omega}+\tau^{-s}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{0,\omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{-2}\left(\|\mathcal{G}\|_{s-2,\omega}+\tau^{-(s-2)}\|\mathcal{G}\|_{0,\omega}\right)+\|g\|_{s-1/2,\partial\omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{-2}\Big(\|g\|_{s-2-1/2,\partial\omega}+\tau^{-(s-2-1/2)}\|g\|_{0,\partial\omega}\Big)+\|g\|_{s-1/2,\partial\omega}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{s-1/2,\partial\omega}+\tau^{-(s-1/2)}\|g\|_{0,\partial_{\omega}},

where in the last step we used the norm-equivalence in (
A.2
).

Step 2 (shift theorem for
s\not\in{\mathbb{N}}
):
The desired shift theorem for
s\not\in{\mathbb{N}}
follows by interpolation from the case of

s\in{\mathbb{N}}
and the norm equivalences for interpolation spaces given in Corollary
A.2
.
∎

A.2
Approximation in DG-norms

Lemma A.4
.

Let Assumption
5.3
be valid. Then, for every
v\in H^{s+2}(\mathscr{P})
,
s\geq 0
and
p\geq s+1
, there is
v_{h}\in V_{h}^{C}\subset H^{1}(\Omega)

with

\|v-v_{h}\|_{dG^{+}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{s+1}\left(1+\frac{kh}{p}\right)\|v\|_{s+2,\mathscr{P}}.

Proof.

In
[
MPS13
, Lemma 4.7]
, it is proven that, for element maps satisfying Assumption
5.3
, usual scaling arguments lead to the expected powers of the mesh size. The approximation
v_{h}
is constructed elementwise with
an operator defined on the reference element in such a way that an
H^{1}
-conforming interpolant is obtained for
v\in H^{t+2}_{pw}(\Omega_{h})\cap H^{1}(\Omega)
;
see
[
MPS13
, Thm. 7.3, Cor. 7.4]

As in the proof of
[
MPS13
, Thm. 4.11]
, we use the operator
I:H^{t}_{pw}(\Omega_{h})\cap H^{1}(\Omega)\rightarrow S^{p,1}(\Omega_{h})

of
[
MPS13
, Thm. 7.3, Cor. 7.4]

with the approximation property

\displaystyle\left(\frac{h_{K}}{p}\right)^{2}\|\nabla^{2}(v-Iv)\|_{L^{2}(K)}+\left(\frac{h_{K}}{p}\right)\|\nabla(v-Iv)\|_{L^{2}(K)}+\|v-Iv\|_{L^{2}(K)}\lesssim\left(\frac{h_{K}}{p}\right)^{t}\|v\|_{H^{t}(K)},

(A.3)

which is valid under the
proviso

t>t^{*}_{d}
, where

t^{*}_{d}:=5/2
for
d=2
and
t^{*}_{d}:=5
for
d=3
as well as the assumption
p\geq t-1
.

If
s+2>t^{*}_{d}
, then we take the approximation
v_{h}:=Iv
and the result follows from elementwise multiplicative trace inequalitites
as shown in more detail in (
A.8
) below.

If
s+2\leq t^{*}_{d}
, fix
\sigma>t^{*}_{d}

and use the characterization of Sobolev spaces as interpolation spaces with the “real method” (see, e.g.,
[
Tar07
]
)
to write
H^{s+2}(P_{i})=(L^{2}(P_{i}),H^{\sigma}(P_{i}))_{\theta,2}
with
\theta=(s+2)/\sigma
for each
i=0,\ldots,L
.
As in the proof of
[
MPS13
, Thm. 4.11]
, this characterization allows us to find, for each
\tau>0
, a function
v_{i,\tau}\in H^{\sigma}(P_{i})

with

\displaystyle\tau^{\sigma-(s+2)}\|v_{i,\tau}\|_{\sigma,P_{i}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{s+2,P_{i}},

\displaystyle\|v-v_{i,\tau}\|_{\rho,P_{i}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{s+2-\rho}\|v\|_{s+2,P_{i}},\quad 0\leq\rho\leq s+2.

(A.4)

The piecewise defined function
\widetilde{v}_{\tau}
given by
\widetilde{v}_{\tau}|_{P_{i}}=v_{i,\tau}
is piecewise smooth but not necessarily in
H^{1}(\Omega)
.
This is corrected with a lifting. We note that the multiplicative trace inequality and the trace inequality yield for the
jump
\kappa_{ij}:=v_{i,\tau}-v_{j,\tau}
on
\partial P_{i}\cap\partial P_{j}
, in view of
(
A.4
) and the fact that
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\|\kappa_{ij}\|_{0,\partial P_{i}\cap\partial P_{j}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{s+2-1/2}\|v\|_{s+2,\mathscr{P}},

\displaystyle\|\kappa_{ij}\|_{\sigma-1/2,\partial P_{i}\cap\partial P_{j}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{s+2-\sigma}\|v\|_{s+2,\mathscr{P}},

(A.5)

which implies

\displaystyle\tau^{-(\sigma-1/2)}\|\kappa_{ij}\|_{0,\partial P_{i}\cap\partial P_{j}}+\|\kappa_{ij}\|_{\sigma-1/2,\partial P_{i}\cap\partial P_{j}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{s+2-\sigma}\|v\|_{s+2,\mathscr{P}}.

(A.6)

Note that
\partial P_{i}\cap\partial P_{j}
is a smooth manifold without boundary by our assumptions on
\mathscr{P}
.
The lifting of Lemma
A.3
allows us to correct the jump across
\partial P_{i}\cap\partial P_{j}
with a
function
J_{ij}
(supported by either
P_{i}
or
P_{j}
) with

\|J_{ij}\|_{r,\mathscr{P}}\lesssim\tau^{s+2-r}\|v\|_{s+2,\mathscr{P}},\qquad 0\leq r\leq s+2.

Using liftings for all interfaces leads to a function
v_{\tau}:=\widetilde{v}_{\tau}+\sum_{ij}J_{ij}\in H^{\sigma}(\mathscr{P})\cap H^{1}(\Omega)
with

\displaystyle\|v_{\tau}\|_{\sigma,\mathscr{P}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{s+2-\sigma}\|v\|_{s+2,\mathscr{P}},

\displaystyle\|v-v_{\tau}\|_{r,\mathscr{P}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{s+2-r}\|v\|_{s+2,\mathscr{P}},\quad 0\leq r\leq s+2.

(A.7)

As in the proof of
[
MPS13
, Thm. 4.11]
, we select
\tau=h/p
. Elementwise appropriate multiplicative trace inequalities
yield

\|v-v_{\tau}\|_{dG^{+}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left(\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{s+1}+k\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{s+2}+\sqrt{k}\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{s+3/2}\right)\|v\|_{s+2,\mathscr{P}},

and (
A.3
) with
t=\sigma
results in

\displaystyle\|v_{\tau}-Iv_{\tau}\|_{dG^{+}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{\sigma-1}+k\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{\sigma}+\sqrt{k}\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{\sigma-1/2}\right)\|v_{\tau}\|_{\sigma,\mathscr{P}}

(A.8)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{lemma:broken-H2-approximation-40})}}{{\lesssim}}\left(\frac{h}{p}\right)^{s+1}\left(1+\frac{kh}{p}+\left(\frac{kh}{p}\right)^{1/2}\right)\|v\|_{s+2,\mathscr{P}}.

This shows the statement under the assumption that
p\geq\sigma-1
. The remaining case
s+1\leq p<\sigma-1
is a pure
h
-version
statement, which follows by usual scaling arguments and a polynomial reproducing interpolation operator (note:
s+2\geq 2
and
d\leq 3
so that
Lagrange interpolation is admissible).
∎

The arithmetic volume of hypersurfaces in toric varieties and Mahler measures
Mounir Hajli

Abstract
. In this paper we determine the canonical arithmetic volume of hypersurfaces in smooth projective toric varieties. As a consequence, we prove a generalized Hodge index theorem on hypersurfaces in smooth projective toric varieties.



Keywords:
Arithmetic volume; Height; Mahler measure
.

MSC:
14G40; 11G50.

1.
Introduction

Let
{Z}
be an arithmetic variety over
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})
, that is a projective, integral and flat scheme over
\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
d+1
be the absolute dimension of
{Z}
. Let
\overline{{\mathcal{L}}}=(\mathcal{L},\|\cdot\|_{\phi})
be a Hermitian line bundle on
{Z}
, such that
the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\phi}
is defined by a continuous weight
\phi
.
For any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
,

k\overline{{\mathcal{L}}}
denotes
\overline{{\mathcal{L}}}^{\otimes k}
.

The arithmetic volume
\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(\overline{{\mathcal{L}}})
is defined by

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(\overline{{\mathcal{L}}})=\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\limsup}\frac{\widehat{h}^{0}(\overline{H^{0}(Z,k\mathcal{L})}_{(\sup,k\phi)})}{k^{d+1}/(d+1)!},

where
\widehat{h}^{0}(\overline{H^{0}(Z,k\mathcal{L})}_{(\sup,k\phi)}):=\log\#\{s\in H^{0}({Z},k{L})\mid\|s\|_{\sup,k\phi}\leq 1\}
.
This arithmetic invariant was introduced by Moriwaki in
[
14
]
.

One of the main results of
[
14
]
can be stated as follows.
Let
\overline{{\mathcal{L}}}
be a nef
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
Hermitian line bundle on
Z
(see Section
3
). Then, the height of
Z
with respect to
\overline{{\mathcal{L}}}
equals to the
arithmetic volume of
Z
with respect to
\overline{{\mathcal{L}}}
. Namely

(1)

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(\overline{{\mathcal{L}}})=h_{\overline{{\mathcal{L}}}}(Z),

(see
[
9
]
for the definition of the heights). The proof of this result is difficult and relies on the property of continuity of
arithmetic volume function proved in
[
14
]
.

The explicit determination of the arithmetic volume is a very difficult problem. When
Z
is a toric variety, and
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
is toric in the sense of Burgos-Philippon-Sombra
[
5
]
, the arithmetic volume of
Z
with respect to
\overline{\mathcal{L}}

possesses a nice integral representation. Namely the following equation.

(2)

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(\overline{\mathcal{L}})=(d+1)!\int_{\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}}\max(0,\vartheta_{\overline{{\mathcal{L}}}})d\mathrm{vol}_{M},

where
M
is a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module of rank
d
, and

\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}
is a rational polytope in
M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
attached to

\mathcal{L}
,
\vartheta_{\overline{{\mathcal{L}}}}
a concave function defined in terms of
the metric of
\mathcal{L}
, and
d\mathrm{vol}_{M}
is a normalized
Lebesgue measure on
M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
,
see
[
4
,
10
,
15
]
. We can prove (
2
) using three ingredients. Namely, the following equation

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(\overline{\mathcal{L}})=\underset{k\rightarrow\infty}{\limsup}\frac{\log\#\{s\in H^{0}({Z},k{\mathcal{L}})\mid\|s\|_{\mu,k\phi}\leq 1\}}{k^{d+1}/(d+1)!},

where
\|\cdot\|_{\mu,k\phi}
is a Euclidean norm (see Section
2
), the fact that the Euclidean lattice
\overline{H^{0}(Y,k\mathcal{L})}_{(\mu,k\phi)}
possesses a natural orthonormal basis with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\mu,k\phi}
and some classical results from the geometry of numbers
[
8
]
. Note that in

[
4
]
, the authors bypass the use of the
L^{2}
-norm with the fact that the basis of toric sections is orthogonal with respect to the sup-norm for all places, both Archimedean or ultrametric.

Burgos, Moriwaki, Philippon and Sombra

[
4
]
gave a combinatorial proof of
(
1
) in the toric setting.

Let
M
be a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module of rank
d
and
N
its dual. We consider a fan
\Sigma
on
M_{\mathbb{R}}=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
and we denote by
Y
the associated toric variety over
\mathbb{Z}
, see for instance
[
16
]
or
[
12
, Paragraph 2.2]
. In the sequel, we assume that
Y
is smooth and projective (this is equivalent to the fact that
\Sigma
is nonsingular and the support of
\Sigma
is
M_{\mathbb{R}}
, see
[
16
, Theorems 1.10 and 1.11]
).
We set
\mathbb{T}_{M}:=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,\mathbb{C}^{\ast})\simeq(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{d}

and we denote by
\mathbb{S}_{N}\simeq(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{d}
its compact torus. We have an open
dense immersion
\mathbb{T}_{M}\hookrightarrow X
with an action of
\mathbb{T}_{M}
on
Y
which extends the action of
\mathbb{T}_{M}

on itself by translations. There exists a canonical volume form on
\mathbb{S}_{N}
(see
[
12
, Remarque 6.1.2, and p. 97]
) which is denoted by
d\mu_{\infty}
.

Let
s
be a nonzero rational function on
Y
. Mahler measure of
s
is defined as follows.

m(s)=\int_{\mathbb{S}_{N}}\log|s|d\mu_{\infty}.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be an equivariant line bundle on
Y
. It is well known that
\mathcal{L}
possesses a natural continuous Hermitian metric which we denote by
\|\cdot\|_{\phi_{\infty}}
and which is given in terms of the combinatorial structure of
Y

[
12
, Paragraph 3.4]
. This metric is called the canonical metric of
\mathcal{L}
. We denote this Hermitian line bundle by
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}}
.
Let
\mathbb{P}^{N}
be the projective space of dimension
N
. The canonical metric of the standard line bundle
\mathcal{O}(1)
on
\mathbb{P}^{N}
is given as follows.

\|\cdot\|_{\phi_{\infty}}=\frac{|\cdot|}{\max(|x_{0}|,\ldots,|x_{N}|)},

where
x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}
are the standard homogeneous coordinates in
Y
.
Let
\mathcal{L}
be an equivariant line bundle generated by its global sections on
Y
. Then
\mathcal{L}
defines an equivariant morphism
\psi_{\mathcal{L}}
on
Y
with image in
\mathbb{P}^{h^{0}(Y,\mathcal{L})-1}
. We can show that

\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{{\phi_{\infty}}}=\psi_{\mathcal{L}}^{\ast}\overline{\mathcal{O}(1)}_{\phi_{\infty}}
, see
[
12
, Paragraph 3.3.3]
. It is worth noting
that, up to a positive multiplicative constant,
\|\cdot\|_{\phi_{\infty}}
is the unique toric metric on
\mathcal{L}
which has Bernstein-Markov property (see Section
2
) with respect to
\mu_{\infty}
, see Theorem

2.2
.

Let
X
be a hypersurface in
Y
which is defined by a
nonzero rational section
s
of an equivariant line bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
Y
. Let

D
be an equivariant divisor on
Y
such that
\mathcal{E}\simeq\mathcal{O}(D)
. Let
s_{D}
be the rational function of
Y
which corresponds to

s
by this isomorphism. The canonical height of the hypersurface
X
with respect to

\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}}
is given in terms of Mahler measure of
s_{D}
. Namely

h_{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}}}(X)=\deg(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Q}})\ m(s_{D}),

see
[
12
, Proposition 7.2.1]
. In the sequel,

\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{\psi_{\infty}}
denotes the
line bundle
\mathcal{E}
endowed with its canonical metric
\|\cdot\|_{\psi_{\infty}}
.

Our first goal is to determine the canonical arithmetic volume of
X
with respect to

\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{{\phi_{\infty}}}
.
Our first result is the following equation.

(3)

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}({\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}}})=\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Q}})m(s_{D}),

where
\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}({\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}}})
is the arithmetic volume of

X
with respect to
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}}
and
\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Q}})
is the geometric volume of

\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, see Theorem
4.4
.
As a first application, we deduce that

(4)

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}({\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}}})=h_{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}}}({{X}}).

In
[
10
, Proposition 1.5]
, we proved that
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}}
is arithmetically nef but not big, and hence not arithmetically ample on
Y
(see Section
3
for the definitions of arithmetically nef, big and ample Hermitian line bundles).

Classically, (
1
) can be used to prove (
4
).
Let us outline the proof of this. Let us first recall that

\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}}

can be approximated by a sequence of
nef
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
Hermitian line bundles
[
19
, Paragraph 2.1]
. Then
use the fact that the arithmetic volume function and the height are
continuous with respect to the variation of
the metrics
[
2
, Proposition 3.2.2]
and
[
14
, Proposition 4.2]
.

Our method for the proof of (
4
) is different and more direct. As a consequence of this study, we show that
the equation

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}({\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi}})=h_{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi}}({{X}}),

holds for every Hermitian line
{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi}}
generated by its small sections on
Y
,
see Theorem
5.2
. Thus we partially recover (
1
).

We proved a generalized Hodge index theorem on toric varieties, see
[
11
, Theorem 5.5]
. In this paper, we show that the methods of
[
11
]
can be used to prove a generalized Hodge index theorem on hypersurfaces in
Y
.
Let
\phi

be a semipositive weight on
\mathcal{L}
. We shall prove that

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi})\geq h_{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi}}(X),

see Theorem
5.3
.

The approach proposed here for the study of these problems relies on the
combinatorial structure of the toric variety

Y
. The space
Y(\mathbb{C})
possesses a canonical measure which is denoted by

\mu_{\infty}
. Its restriction to
the compact torus of
Y(\mathbb{C})
is a Haar measure.
To
\mu_{\infty}
,
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}}

and
\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{\psi_{\infty}}
we attach an Euclidean lattice

\overline{{H^{0}}\left({{Y}},k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}\right)}_{(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty}+\psi_{\infty})}
for every

k\in\mathbb{N}
(see Section
2
). We call

\overline{{H^{0}}\left({{Y}},k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}\right)}_{(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty}+\psi_{\infty})}
the

canonical Euclidean lattice
associated with
\mu_{\infty}
,
k\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}}
and

\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{\psi_{\infty}}
. This lattice plays a central role in this paper.

The Euclidean lattice

\overline{{H^{0}}\left({{Y}},k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}\right)}_{(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty}+\psi_{\infty})}
induces a structure of Euclidean lattice on

{H^{0}}\left({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{X}}\right)
, which we denote by

\overline{{H^{0}}\left({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{X}}\right)}_{\mathrm{sq},(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty}+\psi_{\infty})}
(see Section
2
for more details on the construction). We are naturally led to study the following limits

\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\hat{h}^{0}}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu_{\infty},k{{\phi_{\infty}+\psi_{\infty}}})})}{k^{d}/d!},

and

\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\widehat{\deg}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})}_{\mathrm{sq},(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty}+\psi_{\infty})})}{k^{d}/d!}.

In Proposition
4.3
we prove that

\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{{{\hat{h}^{0}}}}\left(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty}+\psi_{\infty})}\right)}{k^{d}/d!}=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{{{\hat{h}^{0}}}}\left(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},k\mathcal{L}_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty})}\right)}{k^{d}/d!}.

An important point to note here is that
the
\chi
-arithmetic volume of canonical Euclidean lattices is zero.
We use the additivity
of the
\chi
-arithmetic degree on admissible metrized sequences,
and a theorem due to Szegö and generalized by Deninger, to deduce the following inequality

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}({\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}}})\geq\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Q}})m(s_{D}).

Let
(\|\cdot\|_{\phi_{p}})_{p=1,2,\ldots}
be a sequence of smooth Hermitian metrics on
\mathcal{L}
converging uniformly to
\|\cdot\|_{\phi_{\infty}}
.

We shall show that

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\hat{h}^{0}}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},{k\mathcal{L}}_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu,k{{\phi_{p}}})})}{k^{d}/d!}=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\hat{h}^{0}}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},{k\mathcal{L}}_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu_{\infty},k{{\phi_{\infty}}})})}{k^{d}/d!},

where
\mu
is any smooth probability measure on
Y
(see Proposition
4.1
). Using Bernstein-Markov’s property (see Section
2
) we shall deduce that

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{p}})=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\hat{h}^{0}}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},{k\mathcal{L}}_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu_{\infty},k{{\phi_{\infty}}})})}{k^{d}/d!}.

It is not difficult to prove that

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{p}})=\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}}).

Using the technical lemma
3.2
we should deduce the following

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\widehat{\deg}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})}_{\mathrm{sq},(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty}+\psi_{\infty})})}{k^{d}/d!}=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\hat{h}^{0}}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu_{\infty},k{{\phi_{\infty}+\psi_{\infty}}})})}{k^{d}/d!}.

Gathering all these computations, we shall conclude the proof of
(
3
).

2.
Preliminaries

A normed
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\overline{M}=(M,\|\cdot\|)
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-module of finite type
endowed with a norm
\|\cdot\|
on the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space

M_{\mathbb{C}}=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
. Let
M_{\mathrm{tors}}
denote the torsion-module
of
M
,
M_{\mathrm{free}}=M/M_{\mathrm{tors}}
, and

M_{\mathbb{R}}=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
. We let
B=\{m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}:\|m\|\leq 1\}
. There exists
a unique Haar measure on
M_{\mathbb{R}}
such that the volume of
B
is
1
. We let

\hat{\chi}(M,\|\cdot\|)=\log\#M_{\mathrm{tors}}-\log\mathrm{vol}(M_{\mathbb{R}}/(M/M_{\mathrm{tors}})).

Equivalently, we have

\hat{\chi}(M,\|\cdot\|)=\log\#M_{\mathrm{tors}}-\log\left(\frac{\mathrm{vol}(M_{\mathbb{R}}/(M/M_{\mathrm{tor}}))}{\mathrm{vol}(B(M,\|\cdot\|))}\right),

for any choice of a Haar measure of

M_{\mathbb{R}}
.

The arithmetic degree of
(M,\|\cdot\|)
is defined as follows

\widehat{\deg}(M,\|\cdot\|)=\widehat{\deg}\overline{M}=\hat{\chi}(\overline{M})-\hat{\chi}(\overline{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}),

where
\hat{\chi}(\overline{\mathbb{Z}}^{r})=-\log\left(\Gamma(\frac{r}{2}+1)\pi^{-\frac{r}{2}}\right)
, with
r
is the rank of
M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
.

When the norm
\|\cdot\|
is induced by a Hermitian product
(\cdot,\cdot)
,
we say that
\overline{M}
is an
Euclidean lattice. In this situation, we have

\widehat{\deg}(\overline{M})=\log\#M/(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})-\log\sqrt{\det((s_{i},s_{j}))_{1\leq i,j\leq r}},

where
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}
are elements of
M
such that their images
in
M_{\mathbb{Q}}
form a basis.

We define
\widehat{H}^{0}(\overline{M})
and
\widehat{h}^{0}(\overline{M})

to be

\widehat{H}^{0}(\overline{M})=\left\{m\in M:\|m\|\leq 1\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{h}^{0}(\overline{M})=\log\#\widehat{H}^{0}(\overline{M}).

We let

\widehat{H}^{1}(\overline{M}):=\widehat{H}^{0}(\overline{M}^{\vee})\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{h}^{1}(\overline{M}):=\widehat{h}^{0}(\overline{M}^{\vee}),

where
\overline{M}^{\vee}
is the
\mathbb{Z}
-module
M^{\vee}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,\mathbb{Z})
endowed with the dual norm
\|\cdot\|^{\vee}
defined as follows

\|f\|^{\vee}=\sup_{x\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|f(x)|}{\|x\|},\quad\forall f\in M^{\vee}.

Gillet and Soulé
[
8
]
proved the following

(5)

-\log(6)\ \mathrm{rank}\ M\leq\widehat{h}^{0}(\overline{M})-\widehat{\deg}(\overline{M})-\widehat{h}^{1}(\overline{M})\leq\log(\tfrac{3}{2})\mathrm{rank}\,M+2\log((\mathrm{rank}\ M)!),

see also
[
14
, Proposition 2.1]
.

A short exact sequence of Euclidean lattices

0\longrightarrow\overline{N}\overset{i}{\longrightarrow}\overline{M}\overset{\pi}{\longrightarrow}\overline{Q}\longrightarrow 0,

is said to be admissible if
i_{\mathbb{R}}
and the transpose
of
\pi_{\mathbb{R}}
are isometries with respect to the Euclidean
norms on
N_{\mathbb{R}},M_{\mathbb{R}}
and
Q_{\mathbb{R}}
defining the Euclidean
lattices
\overline{N},\overline{M}
and
\overline{Q}
(for more details see
[
3
]
).

We denote by
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathrm{sq}}}
the norm on
Q
induced by
\overline{M}
. It is given by

(6)

\|v\|_{{\mathrm{sq}}}:=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in M_{\mathbb{R}},\\
\pi_{\mathbb{R}}(m)=v\end{subarray}}\|m\|,\quad\forall v\in Q_{\mathbb{R}}.

Let
Y
be an arithmetic variety over
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})
of absolute dimension
d+1
. We assume that
Y_{\mathbb{Q}}
is smooth. Let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
Y
.

A weight
\phi
on
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C})
is a locally integrable function on the complement of the zero-section in the total space of the dual line bundle
\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\mathbb{C})
satisfying the log-homogeneity property

\phi(\lambda v)=\log|\lambda|+\phi(v)

for all non-zero
v\in\mathcal{L}^{-1}(\mathbb{C})
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
\phi
be a weight function on
\mathcal{L}
.
\phi

defines a Hermitian metric on
\mathcal{L}
, which we denote by
\|\cdot\|_{\phi}
. We denote by

\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi}
the line bundle
\mathcal{L}

endowed with the metric
\|\cdot\|_{\phi}
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
Y(\mathbb{C})
. Let

\phi
(resp.
\psi
) be a continuous weight function on
\mathcal{L}
(resp.
\mathcal{E}
). Let
k
be a positive integer.
We endow the space of global sections
H^{0}(Y,{k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
with the
L^{2}
-norm given as follows

\|s\|_{(\mu,k\phi+\psi)}:=\left(\int_{Y(\mathbb{C})}\|s(x)\|_{k\phi+\psi}^{2}\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\forall\ s\in H^{0}(Y,{k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}.

Let
(\cdot,\cdot)_{(\mu,k\phi+\psi)}
denote the associated inner product.
Also we consider the sup-norm defined by

\|s\|_{\sup,k\phi+\psi}:=\sup_{x\in Y(\mathbb{C})}\|s(x)\|_{k\phi+\psi}\quad\forall\ s\in H^{0}(Y,{k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}

Let
X
be a subvariety of
Y
. We let

\|s\|_{\sup,(k\phi+\psi)_{|_{X}}}:=\sup_{x\in X(\mathbb{C})}\|s(x)\|_{(k\phi+\psi)_{|_{X}}}\quad\forall\ s\in H^{0}(X,{(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})}_{|_{X}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}

where
(k\phi+\psi)_{|_{X}}

denotes the weight of the restriction of
\|\cdot\|_{k\phi+\psi}

to
(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{X}}
.

The Bergman distortion function
\rho(\mu,\overline{\mathcal{L}})
is by definition the function given at a point
x\in X
by

\rho(\mu,\phi)(x):=\sup_{s\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})_{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|s(x)\|_{\phi}^{2}}{\quad\|s\|_{(\mu,\phi)}^{2}}.

If
\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{N}\}
is a
(\mu,\phi)
-orthonormal basis of
H^{0}(X,{\mathcal{L}})_{\mathbb{C}}
, where

N=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L})_{\mathbb{C}}
, then it is
well known that

\rho(\mu,\phi)(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|s_{j}(x)\|_{\phi}^{2}\quad\forall\,x\in X,

see
[
1
, p. 357]
.

We say that
\mu
has the Bernstein-Markov property with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\phi}
if for all
\varepsilon>0
we have

\sup_{X}\rho(\mu,k\phi)^{\frac{1}{2}}=O(e^{k\varepsilon}).

Remark 2.1
.

If
\mu
is a smooth positive volume form and
\|\cdot\|_{\phi}
is a continuous metric on
\mathcal{L}
then

\mu
has the Bernstein-Markov property with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\phi}
(see
[
1
, Lemma 3.2]
).

The following result provides a new characterization of canonical metrics on equivariant line bundles on toric varieties.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
Y_{\mathbb{C}}
be nonsingular complex projective variety. Let
L
be an equivariant line bundle on
Y_{\mathbb{C}}
generated by its global sections. Let
\|\cdot\|_{\phi}
be a toric Hermitian metric on
L
. Then

(i)

\|\cdot\|_{\phi_{\infty}}
has Bernstein-Markov property with respect to

\mu_{\infty}
.

(ii)

\|\cdot\|_{\phi}
has
Bernstein-Markov property with respect to

\mu_{\infty}
if and only if
\|\cdot\|_{\phi}=\lambda\|\cdot\|_{\phi_{\infty}}
where
\lambda
is a positive constant.

Proof.

(i)

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
.
It is clear that
\|\chi^{m}\|_{\sup,k\phi_{\infty}}=1
and
\|\chi^{m}\|_{\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty}}=1
for every
m\in k\Delta_{L}\cap M
. Using this, it is not difficult to see that

\|s\|_{\sup,k\phi_{\infty}}\leq\sum_{m\in k\Delta_{L}\cap M}|a_{m}|\leq\sqrt{\#(k\Delta_{L}\cap M)}\|s\|_{\mu,k\phi}\quad\forall s\in H^{0}(Y,kL)_{\mathbb{C}}

where the complex coefficients
a_{m}
are such that
s=\sum_{m\in k\Delta_{L}\cap M}a_{m}\chi^{m}
. So we have proved (i).

(ii)

Let us assume that for every
\varepsilon>0
we have

\|s\|_{\sup,k\phi}\leq Ce^{k\varepsilon}\|s\|_{\mu_{\infty},k\phi}\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}

for every
s\in H^{0}(Z,kL)
, where
C
is a positive constant.

For
s=\chi^{m}
with
m\in k\Delta_{L}\cap M
, we get

(7)

e^{-k\check{g}(\frac{m}{k})}\leq Ce^{k\varepsilon}e^{kg(0)}.

where
g(u):=\log\|s_{L}\|(e^{-u})
for every
u\in N_{\mathbb{R}}
with
s_{L}
is an equivariant rational section of
L
and

\check{g}
is the Legendre-Fenchel transform of
g
, see
[
5
]
for the definition of Legendre-Fenchel transform.
We deduce from (
7
) the following

\check{g}(x)\geq-g(0)-\varepsilon\quad\forall x\in\Delta_{L}.

Observe that
\check{g}(x)\leq-g(0)
.
It follows that

\check{g}(x)=-g(0)\quad\forall x\in\Delta_{L}.

By
[
18

, we infer that

3.
Arithmetic volume of hypersurfaces in projective toric varieties

For
X
an irreducible hypersurface of
Y
, the arithmetic
volume of
X
with respect to
{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi}}_{|_{X}}
is denoted by
\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi})
or
\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}(\mathcal{L},\|\cdot\|_{\phi})
. In other words,

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi}):=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{k^{d}/d!}\hat{h}^{0}(\overline{H^{0}(X,k\mathcal{L}_{|_{X}})}_{(\sup,k\phi_{|_{X}})}).

Unless otherwise stated we assume that
Y
is a smooth projective toric variety, and
\mathcal{L}
is an equivariant line bundle generated by its global sections on
Y
.
Let
\mathcal{E}
be a line bundle on
Y
such that the defining equation
of
X
is given by a global section
s
of
\mathcal{E}
.

Note that the following sequence is exact.

0\rightarrow{H^{0}({{Y}},k\mathcal{L})}\overset{i}{\rightarrow}{H^{0}({{Y}},{k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}})}\overset{\pi}{\rightarrow}{H^{0}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})}\rightarrow 0\ \ (\text{for}\ k=1,2,\ldots)

where
i
is the multiplication map by
s
.

Let
k\geq 1
. We consider the following admissible exact sequences.

(8)

0\rightarrow\overline{{H^{0}({{Y}},k\mathcal{L})}}_{(\mu,k\phi,s)}\overset{i}{\rightarrow}\overline{H^{0}({{Y}},{k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}})}_{(\mu,k\phi)}\overset{\pi}{\rightarrow}\overline{H^{0}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu,k\phi)}\rightarrow 0,

and

(9)

0\rightarrow\overline{{H^{0}({{Y}},k\mathcal{L})}}_{(\sup,k\phi,s)}\overset{i}{\rightarrow}\overline{H^{0}({{Y}},{k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}})}_{(\sup,k\phi)}\overset{\pi}{\rightarrow}\overline{H^{0}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\sup,k\phi)}\rightarrow 0,

where the metrics of
\overline{H^{0}({{Y}},k\mathcal{L}{{{}}})}_{(\sup,k\phi,s)}
and
\overline{H^{0}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\sup,k\phi,s)}
(resp.
\overline{H^{0}({{Y}},k\mathcal{L}{{{}}})}_{(\mu,k\phi,s)}
and
\overline{H^{0}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu,k\phi,s)}
) are induced by the norm considered on
\overline{H^{0}({{Y}},{k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}})}_{(\sup,k\phi)}
(resp.
\overline{H^{0}({{Y}},{k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}})}_{(\mu,k\phi)}
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\phi
be a weight on
\mathcal{L}
. We assume that

\|\cdot\|_{\phi}
is smooth and positive. Let
\mu
be a smooth
probability measure on
Y(\mathbb{C})
. We have

(i)

(10)

\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\hat{h}^{0}}\left(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu,k\phi)}\right)}{k^{d}/d!}=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\hat{h}^{0}}\left(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\sup,k\phi)}\right)}{k^{d}/d!}.

(ii)

(11)

\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{{{\hat{h}^{0}}}}\left(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},k\mathcal{L}_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu,k\phi)}\right)}{k^{d}/d!}=\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi}).

Proof.

Let
\phi
be a weight on
\mathcal{L}
such that
the metric
\|\cdot\|_{\phi}
is smooth and positive.
By
[
17
, Theorem B, (2.7.3)]
, we know that for every

\varepsilon>0
and
k=1,2,\ldots

(12)

\|s\|_{\mathrm{sq},(\sup,k\phi+\psi)}\leq Ce^{k\varepsilon}\|s\|_{\sup,(k\phi+\psi)_{|_{X}}},\quad\forall s\in H^{0}(X,(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{X}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C},

where
C
is a positive constant depending only on

\phi
and
\mu.

It is clear that

(13)

\|s\|_{\sup,\phi_{|_{X}}}\leq\|s\|_{\mathrm{sq},(\sup,\phi)}\quad\forall s\in H^{0}(X,(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{X}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}

Combining (
12
) and (
13
), and following the proof
[
15
, Lemma 2.1]
), it follows immediately that

\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{{{\hat{h}^{0}}}}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})})_{(\sup,(k\phi+\psi)_{|_{X}})})}{k^{d}/d!}=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{{{\hat{h}^{0}}}}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})})_{\mathrm{sq},(\sup,k\phi+\psi)})}{k^{d}/d!}.

By Gromov’s inequality, there exists a constant
C^{\prime}
such that for every
\varepsilon>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\|s\|_{(\mu,k\phi+\psi)}\leq\|s\|_{(\sup,k\phi+\psi)}\leq C^{\prime}e^{k\varepsilon}\|s\|_{(\mu,k\phi+\psi)}\quad\forall\ s\in H^{0}(Y,k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}.

Hence

\|s\|_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu,k\phi+\psi)}\leq\|s\|_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\sup,k\phi+\psi)}\leq C^{\prime}e^{k\varepsilon}\|s\|_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu,k\phi+\psi)}\quad\forall\ s\in H^{0}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}}).

So we deduce
(i). The proof (ii) follows from (i).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mu
be a smooth positive volume form on
Y
. Let
\mathcal{L}
be an equivariant line bundle generated by its global sections on
Y
.
Let
\|\cdot\|_{\phi}
be a continuous Hermitian metric on
\mathcal{L}
such that

\|\chi^{m}\|_{\sup,\phi}\leq 1
for every
m\in\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}\cap M
.

Let
X
be an irreducible hypersurface of
Y
. With the notations of the previous section,
we have

\widehat{h}^{1}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},{(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})}_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\sup,k\phi+\psi_{\infty})})=o(k^{d}),\quad(k\rightarrow\infty),

and

\widehat{h}^{1}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},{(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})}_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu,k\phi+\psi_{\infty})})=o(k^{d}),\quad(k\rightarrow\infty).

Remark 3.3
.

Lemma
3.2
can be applied to

\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}}
, endowed with its
canonical metric.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

To shorten notation, we write

\|\cdot\|
and
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathrm{sq}}}
instead of
\|\cdot\|_{\sup}
and

\|\cdot\|_{{}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\sup,k\phi+\psi_{\infty})}}
respectively. The proof
of the second assertion can be deduced from the first one by using
Bernstein-Markov’s property.

We let
e_{m}:=\chi^{m}
for every
m\in\Delta_{k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}}
.

Let
k\geq 1
. Let
\gamma\in\widehat{H}^{1}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}}})
. We have

(15)

|\gamma(\pi(e_{m}))|\leq\|\pi(e_{m})\|_{{\mathrm{sq}}}\leq\|e_{m}\|\leq 1,\ \forall m\in(k\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}+\Delta_{\mathcal{E}})\cap M.\\

Note that

\pi^{\ast}:H^{0}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})^{\vee}\longrightarrow H^{0}(Y,k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})^{\vee}

is injective. We consider
\pi^{\ast}(\gamma)\in H^{0}(Y,k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})^{\vee}
. There exists a sequence of integers
(a_{m})_{m\in(k\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}+\Delta_{\mathcal{E}})\cap M}
such that

\gamma\circ\pi=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{N+1}_{k}}a_{m}e_{m}^{\vee},

where
\{e_{m}^{\vee}\}_{m\in\Delta_{k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}}\cap M}
denote the dual basis of
\{e_{m}\}_{m\in\Delta_{k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}}\cap M}
.
From (
15
) we see that

a_{m}\in\{-1,0,1\},\quad\forall\;m\in\Delta_{k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}}\cap M.

Let
f
be a rational function which defines
X
.
We have

(\gamma\circ\pi)(f{\chi^{\mu}})=0\quad\forall\mu\in\Delta_{k\mathcal{L}}\cap M.

So

\begin{split}0=&\sum_{\nu\in\Delta_{k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}}\cap M}a_{\nu}e_{\nu}^{\vee}(f{x^{\mu}})\\
=&\sum_{\nu\in\Delta_{k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}}\cap M}a_{\nu}\sum_{m\in\Delta_{\mathcal{E}}}b_{m}e_{\nu}^{\vee}(\chi^{m}\chi^{\mu})\\
=&\sum_{\nu\in\Delta_{k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}}\cap M}a_{\nu}\sum_{m\in\Delta_{\mathcal{E}}}b_{m}e_{\nu}^{\vee}(e_{m+\mu}).\end{split}

Hence

0=\sum_{\nu\in\Delta_{k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}}\cap M}a_{\nu}b_{\nu-\mu},\quad\forall\mu\in\Delta_{k\mathcal{L}}\cap M,

where we have made the convention that
b_{\nu-\mu}=0
whenever

\nu-\mu\notin\Delta_{k\mathcal{L}}\cap M
.

Let us consider the matrix

C_{k}=(c_{\mu,m})_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in\Delta_{k\mathcal{L}}\cap M,\\
m\in\Delta_{k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}}\cap M\end{subarray}}

where
c_{\mu,m}=b_{m-\mu}
for any

\mu\in\Delta_{k\mathcal{L}}\cap M
and
m\in\Delta_{k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}}\cap M
. So
C_{k}
is a

h^{0}(Y,k\mathcal{L})\times h^{0}(Y,k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})
-matrix, where its
\mu
-row is given in terms of the coefficients of

f{\chi^{\mu}}
.

We claim that the rank of
C_{k}
is
h^{0}(Y,k\mathcal{L})
. Indeed, let
y=(y_{m})_{m\in\Delta_{k\mathcal{L}}\cap M}\in\mathbb{R}^{h^{0}(Y,k\mathcal{L})}
.
By basic linear algebra, we observe that

C_{k}^{t}y=0
(where
C_{k}^{t}
is the transpose of
C_{k}
) if and only if
f\sum_{m\in\Delta_{k\mathcal{L}}\cap M}y_{m}\chi^{m}=0
.

It follows that

\dim\ker C_{k}=h^{0}(Y,k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})-h^{0}(Y,k\mathcal{L})=o(k^{d}),

as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

Note that
(a_{m})_{m\in\Delta_{k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}}\cap M}\in\ker C_{k}

and recall that
a_{m}\in\{-1,0,1\}
, so we can conclude that

\#\widehat{H}^{1}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}}})=3^{o(k^{d})}.

4.
Canonical arithmetic volume of hypersurfaces

Assume that
\mathcal{L}
is generated by its global sections on
Y
.
Let
(\phi_{p})_{p=1,2,\ldots}
be the sequence of continuous weights on
\mathcal{L}
given as follows

\|s(x)\|_{\phi_{p}}=\frac{|s(x)|}{\left(\sum_{v\in\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}\cap M}|\chi^{v}(x)|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}},\quad p=1,2,\ldots

for every local section
s
of
\mathcal{L}
.

It is well-known that the sequence

(\|\cdot\|_{\phi_{p}})_{p=1,2,\ldots}
converges uniformly to

\|\cdot\|_{\phi_{\infty}}
.

From now on, we assume moreover that the probability measure
\mu
is invariant under
the action of the compact torus of
Y(\mathbb{C})
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be an equivariant line bundle generated by its global sections.
Under the above notations and assumptions, we have

(16)

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\hat{h}^{0}}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},{k\mathcal{L}}_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu,k{{\phi_{p}}})})}{k^{d}/d!}=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\hat{h}^{0}}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},{k\mathcal{L}}_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu_{\infty},k{{\phi_{\infty}}})})}{k^{d}/d!}.

Proof.

There exists an equivariant map

\psi_{\mathcal{L}}:Y\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{r_{\mathcal{L}}},\ x\mapsto\psi_{\mathcal{L}}(x)=(\chi^{m}(x))_{m\in\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}\cap M}

where
r_{\mathcal{L}}:=\#(\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}\cap M)-1
. We have

\|\cdot\|_{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}}}=\psi_{\mathcal{L}}^{\ast}\|\cdot\|_{\overline{\mathcal{O}(1)}_{\phi_{\infty}}}.

Let
\delta\in[0,1]
. Let
v_{0}\in\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}\cap M
. For every
v\in\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}\cap M
, we let

E_{v,\delta}:=\left\{x\in Y(\mathbb{C}):\chi^{v_{0}}(x)\neq 0\;,\;\delta\tfrac{|\chi^{v}(x)|}{|\chi^{v_{0}}(x)|}\leq\tfrac{|\chi^{v^{\prime}}(x)|}{|\chi^{v_{0}}(x)|}\leq\tfrac{|\chi^{v}(x)|}{|\chi^{v_{0}}(x)|}\;\text{for every}\;v^{\prime}\in(\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}\cap M)\right\}.

It is clear that

Y(\mathbb{C})\setminus\mathrm{div}(\chi^{v_{0}})=\bigcup_{v\in\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}\cap M}E_{v,0}.

For
0<\delta<1
, and for every
p=1,2,\ldots
,
k=1,2,\ldots
, and

m\in k\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}\cap M
,

\begin{split}(\chi^{m},\chi^{m})_{(\mu,k\phi_{p})}\geq&\sum_{v\in\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}\cap M}\int_{E_{v,\delta}}\frac{|\chi^{m}(x)|^{2}}{(\sum_{v\in\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}\cap M}|\chi^{v}(x)|^{p})^{\frac{2k}{p}}}\mu\geq\frac{\delta^{2k}}{(r_{\mathcal{L}}+1)^{\frac{2k}{p}}}I_{\delta},\end{split}

where we have put
I_{\delta}:=\sum_{v\in\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}\cap M}\int_{E_{v,\delta}}\mu.

That is

(17)

(\chi^{m},\chi^{m})_{(\mu,k{{\phi_{p}}})}\geq\frac{\delta^{2k}}{(r_{\mathcal{L}}+1)^{\frac{2k}{p}}}I_{\delta}.

On one hand, by noticing that the metrics are invariant under the action of
the compact group
\mathcal{S}
, it is easy to check that (
17
) gives the following

(18)

(s,s)_{(\mu,k{{\phi_{p}}})}\geq\frac{\delta^{2k}I_{\delta}}{(r_{\mathcal{L}}+1)^{\frac{2k}{p}}}(s,s)_{(\mu_{\infty},k{{\phi_{\infty}}})},\quad\forall\ s\in H^{0}(Y,k\mathcal{L})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}.

On the other hand, we have

(19)

(s,s)_{(\mu,k\phi_{p})}\leq(s,s)_{(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty})},\quad\forall\ s\in H^{0}(Y,k\mathcal{L})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}.

In order to see this, let

s=\sum_{m\in k\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}\cap M}c_{m}\chi^{m}
be an element of
H^{0}(Y,k\mathcal{L})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
. By the invariance of the metrics, we obtain that

\begin{split}(s,s)_{(\mu,k\phi_{p})}=&\sum_{m\in k\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}\cap M}|c_{m}|^{2}\int_{Y(\mathbb{C})}\|\chi^{m}\|_{k\phi_{p}}^{2}\mu\\
&\leq\sum_{m\in k\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}\cap M}|c_{m}|^{2}\\
=&(s,s)_{(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty})},\end{split}

where we have used the fact that
\|\chi^{m}\|_{k\phi_{p}}\leq\|\chi^{m}\|_{k\phi_{\infty}}\leq 1
.

So, we have proved the following.

\frac{\delta^{2k}I_{\delta}}{(r_{\mathcal{L}}+1)^{\frac{2k}{p}}}(s,s)_{(\mu_{\infty},k{{\phi_{\infty}}})}\leq(s,s)_{(\mu,k\phi_{p})}\leq(s,s)_{(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty})}\quad\forall\ s\in H^{0}(Y,k\mathcal{L})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}.

That is

\frac{\delta^{k}I_{\delta}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(r_{\mathcal{L}}+1)^{\frac{k}{p}}}\|\cdot\|_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu_{\infty},k{{\phi_{\infty}}})}\leq\|\cdot\|_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu,k{{\phi_{p}}})}\leq\|\cdot\|_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu_{\infty},k{{\phi_{\infty}}})}\quad\forall\ k\in\mathbb{N}.

From these inequalities, we infer that

\begin{split}\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\hat{h}^{0}}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},k\mathcal{L}_{|_{{X}}}))}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu_{\infty},k{{\phi_{\infty}}})}}{k^{d}/d!}&\leq\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\hat{h}^{0}}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},k\mathcal{L}_{|_{{X}}}))}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu,k{{\phi_{p}}})}}{k^{d}/d!}\\
&\leq\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\hat{h}^{0}}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},k\mathcal{L}_{|_{{X}}}))}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu_{\infty},k{{\phi_{p}}})}}{k^{d}/d!}\\
&-\log\frac{\delta^{2}}{(r_{\mathcal{L}}+1)^{\frac{2}{p}}}.\end{split}

By letting
\delta\rightarrow 1^{-}
, we obtain

(20)

\left|\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\hat{h}^{0}}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},k\mathcal{L}_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu_{\infty},k{{\phi_{\infty}}})}}{k^{d}/d!}-\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\hat{h}^{0}}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},k\mathcal{L}_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu,k{{\phi_{p}}})}}{k^{d}/d!}\right|\leq\tfrac{2}{p}\log(r_{\mathcal{L}}+1).

∎

(21)

, we get for every
p=1,2,\ldots

\begin{split}\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\hat{h}^{0}}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu,k{{\phi_{p}}})})}{k^{d}/d!}=&\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\hat{h}^{0}}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\sup,k{{\phi_{p}}})})}{k^{d}/d!}\\
=&\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{p}}).\end{split}

We have

\begin{split}\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{p}})=&\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\hat{h}^{0}}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},{k\mathcal{L}}_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu,k{{\phi_{p}}})})}{k^{d}/d!}\quad\text{(by \eqref{Th2.2ii})}\\
=&\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\hat{h}^{0}}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},{k\mathcal{L}}_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu_{\infty},k{{\phi_{\infty}}})})}{k^{d}/d!}\quad\text{(by \eqref{1706})}\\
=&\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{{{\hat{h}^{0}}}}\left(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty}+\psi_{\infty})}\right)}{k^{d}/d!}\quad\text{(by \eqref{4.3-1})}\\
\end{split}

Hence

(23)

\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\hat{h}^{0}}\Bigl(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu_{\infty},k{{\phi_{\infty}}+\psi_{\infty}})}\Bigr)}{k^{d}/d!}=\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{{X}}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}}),

where we have used that
\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{{X}}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{p}})=\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{{X}}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}}).

Note that

(24)

\hat{\chi}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{Y}},k\mathcal{L})}_{(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty},{}{s})})=\det\left(\left<{{s}}{\chi^{m}},{{s}}\chi^{m^{\prime}}\right>_{(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty})}\right)_{m,m^{\prime}\in{}{k\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}\cap M}},

(we recall that
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>_{(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty})}
is the scalar product
associated with
\|\cdot\|_{(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty})}
).

We have

(25)

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\log\left(\det\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{S}_{N}}\chi^{m}\overline{\chi^{m^{\prime}}}|s|^{2}{d\mu_{\infty}}\Bigr)_{m,m^{\prime}\in(k\Delta_{\mathcal{L}})\cap M}\right)^{1/|k\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}\cap M|}=\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Q}})\int_{\mathcal{S}}\log|s|^{2}{d\mu_{\infty}},

see
[
7
, Theorem 4, p.49]
.

We obtain that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\hat{\chi}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{Y}},k\mathcal{L}))}_{(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty},{}{s})})}{k^{d}/d!}=\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Q}})\int_{\mathcal{S}}\log|s|^{2}{d\mu_{\infty}}.

Applying
[
17
, p. 81]
to (
8
), we obtain that

\begin{split}\widehat{\deg}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})})_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty}+\psi_{\infty})}=&\widehat{\deg}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{Y}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}))})_{(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty}+\psi_{\infty})}\\
&-\widehat{\deg}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{Y}},k\mathcal{L}))})_{(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty},{}{s})},\end{split}

holds for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

An easy computation shows that

\hat{\chi}\left(\overline{{H^{0}}\left({{Y}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})\right)}_{(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty}+\psi_{\infty})}\right)=0\quad\forall\ k\in\mathbb{N}.

Hence, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\begin{split}\widehat{\deg}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})})_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty}+\psi_{\infty})}=&\hat{\chi}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{Y}},k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})}_{(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty}+\psi_{\infty})})-\hat{\chi}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{Y}},k\mathcal{L})}_{(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty},s)})\\
&-\hat{\chi}(\overline{\mathbb{Z}}^{h^{0}(Y,(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}))})+\hat{\chi}(\overline{\mathbb{Z}}^{h^{0}(Y,k\mathcal{L})})\\
=&\det\left(\left<s{\chi^{m}},s\chi^{m^{\prime}}\right>_{(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty})}\right)_{m,m^{\prime}\in k\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}\cap M}\\
&-\hat{\chi}(\overline{\mathbb{Z}}^{\#(\Delta_{k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}}\cap M)})+\hat{\chi}(\overline{\mathbb{Z}}^{\#(k\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}\cap M)}).\end{split}

Since
\dim{H^{0}}\left({{Y}},k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E}\right)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}=O(k^{d})
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, we can use Lemma
4.2
to conclude that

(26)

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\widehat{\deg}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})}_{\mathrm{sq},(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty}+\psi_{\infty})})}{k^{d}/d!}=\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Q}})\int_{\mathcal{S}}\log|s|^{2}{d\mu_{\infty}}.

It is clear that the metric
\|\cdot\|_{\phi_{\infty}}
satisfies the conditions of
Lemma
3.2
. Using (
5
), we get

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\widehat{\deg}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})}_{\mathrm{sq},(\mu_{\infty},k\phi_{\infty}+\psi_{\infty})})}{k^{d}/d!}=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\hat{h}^{0}}(\overline{{H^{0}}({{X}},(k\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{E})_{|_{{X}}})}_{{\mathrm{sq}},(\mu_{\infty},k{{\phi_{\infty}+\psi_{\infty}}})}}{k^{d}/d!}.

So, by (
23
) and (
26
),

\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Q}})\int_{\mathcal{S}}\log|s|^{2}{d\mu_{\infty}}=\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{{X}}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}}).

We conclude that

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{{X}}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}})=h_{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}}}(X),

where we have used the fact that

h_{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}}}(X)=\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{Q}})\int_{\mathcal{S}}\log|s|^{2}{d\mu_{\infty}},

see
[
12
, Proposition 7.2.1]
.
∎

5.
A generalized Hodge index theorem on hypersurfaces in toric varieties

We introduced the theory of arithmetic theta invariants associated with Hermitian line bundles on arithmetic varieties, see
[
11
, Section 4]
. One of the main result of
[
11
]
is
a generalized Hodge index theorem on toric varieties. In this section we show that the methods of
[
11
]
can be generalized to prove a generalized Hodge index theorem on hypersurfaces in toric varieties.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
Z
be an arithmetic variety of dimension
n+1
and with smooth generic fibre.
Let
(\mathcal{L},\|\cdot\|_{\phi})
and

(\mathcal{L},\|\cdot\|_{\psi})
be two
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
semipositive Hermitian line bundles on
Z
. We assume
\psi\leq\phi
and
(\mathcal{L},\|\cdot\|_{\psi})
is generated by small sections. We have

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(\mathcal{L},\|\cdot\|_{\phi})-\widehat{\deg}(\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{L},\|\cdot\|_{\phi})^{n+1})=\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(\mathcal{L},\|\cdot\|_{\psi})-\widehat{\deg}(\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{L},\|\cdot\|_{\psi})^{n+1}).

Proof.

See
[
11
, Theorem 4.11]
.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
Y
be a smooth toric variety over
\mathbb{Z}
of dimension
d+1
. Let
\mathcal{L}
be an equivariant line bundle on
Y
. We assume that it admits a semipositive weight
\phi
and that
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi}
is generated by small sections. Let

X
be a hypersurface in
Y
. Then

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}({\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi}})=h_{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi}}({{X}}).

Proof.

The arithmetic volume function is continuous with respect to the variation of the metric, see
[
15
, p. 513]
. Then
we can assume that
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi}
is generated by strictly small sections. We claim that the proof can be obtained by the same method as in the proof of
[
11
, Theorem 5.4]
. Indeed,
there exists
0<\alpha\leq 1
such that

\alpha\|\cdot\|_{\phi}\leq\|\cdot\|_{\phi_{\infty}}.

So by Theorem
5.1
and
a continuity argument, we can show that

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}(\mathcal{L},\alpha\|\cdot\|_{\phi})-\widehat{\deg}(\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{|_{X}},\alpha\|\cdot\|_{\psi})^{d})=\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}(\mathcal{L},\|\cdot\|_{\phi_{\infty}})-\widehat{\deg}(\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{|_{X}},\|\cdot\|_{\phi_{\infty}})^{d}),

and

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}(\mathcal{L},\alpha\|\cdot\|_{\phi})-\widehat{\deg}(\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{|_{X}},\alpha\|\cdot\|_{\phi})^{d})=\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}(\mathcal{L},\|\cdot\|_{\phi})-\widehat{\deg}(\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{|_{X}},\|\cdot\|_{\phi})^{d}).

Since

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}})=h_{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi_{\infty}}}(X),

(see (ii) of Theorem
4.4
) we deduce that

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}(\mathcal{L},\|\cdot\|_{\phi})=h_{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi}}(X).

∎

Theorem 5.3
.

[A generalized Hodge index theorem] Let
Y
be a smooth toric variety over
\mathbb{Z}
of dimension
d+1
. Let
\mathcal{L}
be an equivariant line bundle on
Y
. Let
\phi

be a semipositive weight on
\mathcal{L}
. Let

X
be a hypersurface in
Y
. We have

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi})\geq h_{\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\phi}}(X).

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of
[
11
, Theorem 5.5]
.
The
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra

\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}H^{0}(X,{k\mathcal{L}}_{|_{X}})
is generated by
\chi^{m}
for

m\in k\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}\cap M
.

Let
\alpha
be a positive real number such that

0<\alpha<1\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha\|\chi^{m}\|_{\sup,\phi_{|_{X}}}<1\quad\text{for}\quad m\in k\Delta_{\mathcal{L}}\cap M.

It follows that

(\mathcal{L},\alpha\|\cdot\|_{\phi})
is ample.

From Theorem
5.2
, we get

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}(\mathcal{L},\alpha\|\cdot\|_{\phi})=\widehat{\deg}(\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{L},\alpha\|\cdot\|_{\phi_{|_{X}}})^{d}).

By
[
11
, Proposition 4.6 and Theorem 4.7]
we see that

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}(\mathcal{L},\|\cdot\|_{\phi})-\widehat{\deg}(\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{L},\|\cdot\|_{\phi_{|_{X}}})^{d})\geq\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}(\mathcal{L},\alpha\|\cdot\|_{\phi_{|_{X}}})-\widehat{\deg}(\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{L},\alpha\|\cdot\|_{\phi_{|_{X}}})^{d}).

Therefore

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}_{X}(\mathcal{L},\|\cdot\|_{\phi})\geq\widehat{\deg}(\hat{c}_{1}(\mathcal{L},\|\cdot\|_{\phi_{|_{X}}})^{d}).

∎

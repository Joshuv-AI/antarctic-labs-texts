A program for the full axiom of choice
Jean-Louis Krivine

Abstract
. The theory of classical realizability is a framework for the Curry-Howard correspondence which enables to associate a program with each proof in Zermelo-Fraenkel set theory. But, almost all the applications of mathematics in physics, probability, statistics, etc. use Analysis i.e. the axiom of dependent choice (DC) or even the (full) axiom of choice (AC). It is therefore important to find explicit programs for these axioms. Various solutions have been found for DC, for instance the lambda-term called ”bar recursion” or the instruction ”quote” of LISP. We present here the first program for AC.

Introduction

The Curry-Howard correspondence enables to associate a program with each proof in classical natural deduction. But mathematical proofs not only use the rules of natural deduction, but also
axioms
, essentially those of Zermelo-Frænkel set theory with axiom of choice. In order to transform these proofs into programs, we must therefore associate with each of these axioms suitable instructions, which is far from obvious.

The theory of
classical realizability (c.r.)
solves this problem for all axioms of ZF, by means of a very rudimentary programming language: the
\lambda_{c}
-calculus, that is to say the
\lambda
-calculus with a control instruction
[
Gri90
]
.

The programs obtained in this way can therefore be written in practically any programming language. They are said to
realize
the axioms of ZF.

But, almost all the applications of mathematics in physics, probability, statistics, etc. use
Analysis
, that is to say
the axiom of dependent choice
. The first program for this axiom, known since 1998
[
BBC98
]
, is a pure
\lambda
-term called
bar recursion

[
Spe62
,
BO05
,
Str17
]
. In fact, c.r. shows that it provides also a program for
the
continuum hypothesis

[
Kri16
]
.

Nevertheless, this solution requires the programming language to be limited to
\lambda_{c}
-calculus, prohibiting any other instruction, which is a severe restriction.

Classical realizability provides other programs for this axiom
[
Kri12
,
Kri18
,
Kri09
]
, which require an additional
instruction (clock, signature, … , or as in this paper, introduction of
fresh variables
). On the other hand,
this kind of solution is very flexible regarding the programming language.

There remained, however, the problem of the
full
axiom of choice. It is solved here, by means of new instructions
which allow
branching
or
parallelism
1
1

1

A detailed study of the effect of such instructions on the
characteristic Boolean algebra
\gimel 2
is made in
[
Geo18
]
.
.
Admittedly, the program obtained in this way is rather complicated and we may hope for new solutions as simple
as bar recursion or clock. However, it already shows how we can turn all proofs of ZFC into programs
(more precisions about this in Remark
45
at the end of the paper).
Note that
the proof is constructive
i.e. it gives explicitly a program for AC: you ”merely” need to formalize this proof itself in natural deduction and apply the proof-program correspondence
to the result.

Intuitively, their elements are programs.

Section 2:

1.
Realizability algebras (r.a.)

It is a first order structure, which is defined in
[
Kri18
]
. We recall here briefly this definition and some
essential properties.

A
realizability algebra

{\mathcal{A}}
is made up of three sets:

\Lambda
(the set of
terms
),
\Pi
(the set of
stacks
),

\Lambda\star\Pi
(the set of
processes
) with the following operations:

(\xi,\eta)\mapsto(\xi)\eta
from
\Lambda^{2}
into
\Lambda
(
application
);

(\xi,\pi)\mapsto\xi\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\pi
from
\Lambda\!\times\!\Pi
into
\Pi
(
push
);

(\xi,\pi)\mapsto\xi\star\pi
from
\Lambda\!\times\!\Pi
into
\Lambda\star\Pi
(
process
);

\pi\mapsto\mbox{k}_{\pi}
from
\Pi
into
\Lambda
(
continuation
).

There are, in
\Lambda
, distinguished elements
\mbox{B},\mbox{C},\mbox{I},\mbox{K},\mbox{W},\mbox{\sf cc}
, called

elementary combinators
or
instructions
.

Notation.
The term
(\ldots(((\xi)\eta_{1})\eta_{2})\ldots)\eta_{n}
will be also written

(\xi)\eta_{1}\eta_{2}\ldots\eta_{n}
or even
\xi\eta_{1}\eta_{2}\ldots\eta_{n}
.
For instance:
\xi\eta\zeta=(\xi)\eta\zeta=(\xi\eta)\zeta=((\xi)\eta)\zeta
.

We define a preorder on
\Lambda\star\Pi
, denoted by
\succ
, which is called
execution
;

\xi\star\pi\succ\xi^{\prime}\star\pi^{\prime}
is read as:

the process
\xi\star\pi
reduces to
\xi^{\prime}\star\pi^{\prime}
.

It is the smallest reflexive and transitive binary relation, such that, for any
\xi,\eta,\zeta\in\Lambda
and
\pi,\varpi\in\Pi
, we have:

(\xi)\eta\star\pi\succ\xi\star\eta\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\pi
(
push
).

\mbox{I}\star\xi\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\pi\succ\xi\star\pi
(
no operation
).

\mbox{K}\star\xi\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\eta\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\pi\succ\xi\star\pi
(
delete
).

\mbox{W}\star\xi\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\eta\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\pi\succ\xi\star\eta\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\eta\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\pi
(
copy
).

\mbox{C}\star\xi\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\eta\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\zeta\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\pi\succ\xi\star\zeta\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\eta\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\pi
(
switch
).

\mbox{B}\star\xi\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\eta\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\zeta\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\pi\succ\xi\star(\eta)\zeta\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\pi
(
apply
).

\mbox{\sf cc}\star\xi\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\pi\succ\xi\star\mbox{k}_{\pi}\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\pi
(
save the stack
).

\mbox{k}_{\pi}\star\xi\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\varpi\succ\xi\star\pi
(
restore the stack
).

We are also given a subset
\bot\hskip-6.49994pt\bot
of
\Lambda\star\Pi
, called ”the pole”, such that:

\xi\star\pi\succ\xi^{\prime}\star\pi^{\prime}
,
\xi^{\prime}\star\pi^{\prime}\in\bot\hskip-6.49994pt\bot

\Rightarrow

\xi\star\pi\in\bot\hskip-6.49994pt\bot
.

Given two processes
\xi\star\pi,\xi^{\prime}\star\pi^{\prime}
, the notation
\xi\star\pi\succ\hskip-10.00002pt\succ\xi^{\prime}\star\pi^{\prime}
means:

\xi\star\pi\notin\bot\hskip-6.49994pt\bot\Rightarrow\xi^{\prime}\star\pi^{\prime}\notin\bot\hskip-6.49994pt\bot
.

Therefore, obviously,
\xi\star\pi\succ\xi^{\prime}\star\pi^{\prime}\;\Rightarrow\;\xi\star\pi\succ\hskip-10.00002pt\succ\xi^{\prime}\star\pi^{\prime}
.

Finally, we choose a set of terms
PL
{}_{\mathcal{A}}\subset\Lambda
, containing the elementary combinators:

\mbox{B},\mbox{C},\mbox{I},\mbox{K},\mbox{W},\mbox{\sf cc}
and closed by application. They are called the

proof-like terms of the algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
. We write also
PL
instead of

PL
A
if there is no ambiguity about
{\mathcal{A}}
.

The algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
is called
coherent
if, for every proof-like term
\theta\in\mbox{\mbox{PL}}_{\mathcal{A}}
, there exists a stack
\pi
such that
\theta\star\pi\notin\bot\hskip-6.49994pt\bot
.

.

A
set of forcing conditions
can be considered as a degenerate case of realizability

An inf-semi-lattice

, with a greatest element
\mathbf{1}
and an

\bot\hskip-6.49994pt\bot

is not in

We get a realizability algebra if we set

;
\mbox{B}=\mbox{C}=\mbox{I}=\mbox{K}=\mbox{W}=\mbox{\sf cc}=\mathbf{1}
and

=\{\mathbf{1}\}
;

(p)q=p\,\mbox{\Large.}\,q=p\star q=p{\scriptstyle\land}q
and
\mbox{k}_{p}=p
. The preorder

is defined as

, i.e.
p{\scriptstyle\land}q=p
. The condition of coherence is
\mathbf{1}\notin\bot\hskip-6.49994pt\bot

We can translate
\lambda
-terms into terms of

\mbox{B},\mbox{C},\mbox{I},\mbox{K},\mbox{W}

\lambda x\,t[x]\star u\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\pi\succ t[u/x]\star\pi

where

are terms and

This is done in
[

. Note that the usual

In order to write programs,

we use it extensively in the following. But combinatory algebra
[
CF58
]
is better for theory, in particular
because it is a first order structure.

2.
Realizability models (r.m.)

The framework is very similar to that of forcing, which is anyway a particular case.

We use a first order language with three binary symbols
\,\varepsilon\!\!\!/\,,\notin,\subset

(
\,\varepsilon\,
is intended to be a strong, non extensional membership relation;
\in
and
\subset
have their usual meaning in ZF).

First order formulas are written with the only logical symbols
\to,\forall,\bot,\top
.

The symbols
\neg,\land,\lor,\leftrightarrow,\exists
are defined with them in the usual way.

Given a realizability algebra, we get a
realizability model (r.m.)
as follows:

We start with a model
{\mathcal{M}}
of ZFC (or even ZFL) called the
ground model
.
The axioms of ZFL are written with the sublanguage
\{\notin,\subset\}
.

We build a model
{\mathcal{N}}
of a new set theory ZF
ε
, in the language

\{\,\varepsilon\!\!\!/\,,\notin,\subset\}
, the axioms of which are given in
[
Kri12
]
. We recall them below, using
the following rather standard abbreviations:

F_{1}\to(F_{2}\to\ldots(F_{n}\to G)\ldots)
is written
F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{n}\to G
or even
\vec{F}\to G
.

We use the notation
\exists x\{F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{n}\}
for
\forall x(F_{1}\to(F_{2}\to\cdots\to(F_{n}\to\bot)\cdots))\to\bot
.

Of course,
x\,\varepsilon\,y
and
x\in y
are the formulas
x\,\varepsilon\!\!\!/\,y\to\bot
and
x\notin y\to\bot
.

The notation
x=_{\in}\!y\to F
means
x\subset y,y\subset x\to F
. Thus
x=_{\in}\!y
,
which represents the usual (extensional) equality of sets, is the pair of formulas
\{x\subset y,y\subset x\}
.

We use the notations:

(\forall x\,\varepsilon\,a)F(x)
for
\forall x(\neg F(x)\to x\,\varepsilon\!\!\!/\,a)
and

(\exists x\,\varepsilon\,a)\vec{F}(x)
for
\neg\forall x(\vec{F}(x)\to x\,\varepsilon\!\!\!/\,a)
.

For instance,
(\exists x\,\varepsilon\,y)(t=_{\in}\!u)
is the formula

\neg\forall x(t\subset u,u\subset t\to x\,\varepsilon\!\!\!/\,y)
.

The axioms of ZF
ε
are the following:

(0)

Extensionality axioms.

\forall x\forall y(x\in y\leftrightarrow(\exists z\,\varepsilon\,y)x=_{\in}\!z)
;

\forall x\forall y(x\subset y\leftrightarrow(\forall z\,\varepsilon\,x)z\in y)
.

(1)

Foundation scheme.

\forall\vec{a}(\forall x((\forall y\,\varepsilon\,x)F(y,\vec{a})\to F(x,\vec{a}))\to\forall x\,F(x,\vec{a}))

for every formula
F(x,a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
.

The intuitive meaning of these axioms is that
\varepsilon
is a well
founded relation and the relation
\in
is obtained by “ collapsing ”

\varepsilon
into an extensional relation.

The following axioms essentially express that the relation
\,\varepsilon\,

satisfies the Zermelo-Fraenkel axioms
except extensionality
.

(2)

Comprehension scheme.

\forall\vec{a}\forall x\exists y\forall z(z\,\varepsilon\,y\leftrightarrow(z\,\varepsilon\,x\land F(z,\vec{a}))

for every formula
F(z,\vec{a})
.

(3)

Pairing axiom.

\forall a\forall b\exists x\{a\,\varepsilon\,x,b\,\varepsilon\,x\}
.

(4)

Union axiom.

\forall a\exists b(\forall x\,\varepsilon\,a)(\forall y\,\varepsilon\,x)\,y\,\varepsilon\,b
.

(5)

Power set axiom.

\forall a\exists b\forall x(\exists y\,\varepsilon\,b)\forall z(z\,\varepsilon\,y\leftrightarrow(z\,\varepsilon\,a\land z\,\varepsilon\,x))
.

(6)

Collection scheme.

\forall\vec{a}\forall x\exists y(\forall u\,\varepsilon\,x)(\exists v\,F(u,v,\vec{a})\to(\exists v\,\varepsilon\,y)F(u,v,\vec{a}))

for every formula
F(u,v,\vec{a})
.

(7)

Infinity scheme.

\forall\vec{a}\forall x\exists y\{x\,\varepsilon\,y,(\forall u\,\varepsilon\,y)(\exists v\,F(u,v,\vec{a})\to(\exists v\,\varepsilon\,y)F(u,v,\vec{a}))\}

for every formula
F(u,v,\vec{a})
.

It is shown in
[
Kri12
]
that ZF
ε
is a
conservative extension of ZF.

For each formula
F(\vec{a})
of ZF
ε
(i.e. written with
\,\varepsilon\!\!\!/\,,\notin,\subset
) with parameters
\vec{a}

in the ground model
{\mathcal{M}}
we define, in
{\mathcal{M}}
, a
falsity value

\|F(\vec{a})\|
which is
a subset of
\Pi
and a

|F(\vec{a})|
which is a subset of
\Lambda
.

The notation
t\mbox{ $|\hskip-1.00006pt|\hskip-2.20001pt-\,$}F(\vec{a})
(read “
t

F(\vec{a})
” or “
t
forces
F(\vec{a})
”

t\in|F(\vec{a})|
.

We set first
|F(\vec{a})|=\{t\in\Lambda\;;\;(\forall\pi\in\|F(\vec{a})\|)(t\star\pi\in\bot\hskip-6.49994pt\bot)\}
so that
we only need to define
\|F(\vec{a})\|
, which we do by induction on
F
:

(1)

Definition of
\|a\,\varepsilon\!\!\!/\,b\|
:

\|a\,\varepsilon\!\!\!/\,b\|=\{\pi\in\Pi\;;\;(a,\pi)\in b\}
;
\|\bot\|=\Pi
;
\|\top\|=\emptyset

(2)

Definition of

\|a\notin b\|

\|a\subset b\|=\bigcup_{c}\{t\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\pi\in\Pi\;;\;t\mbox{ $|\hskip-1.00006pt|\hskip-2.20001pt-\,$}c\notin b,(c,\pi)\in a\}

\|a\notin b\|=\bigcup_{c}\{t\,\mbox{\Large.}\,u\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\pi\in\Pi\;;\;t\mbox{ $|\hskip-1.00006pt|\hskip-2.20001pt-\,$}c\subset a,u\mbox{ $|\hskip-1.00006pt|\hskip-2.20001pt-\,$}a\subset c,(c,\pi)\in b\}

(3)

Definition of
\|F\|
for a non atomic formula
F

\|F\to F^{\prime}\|=\{t\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\pi\in\Pi\;;\;t\mbox{ $|\hskip-1.00006pt|\hskip-2.20001pt-\,$}F,\pi\in\|F^{\prime}\|\}

\|\forall x\,F(x)\|=\bigcup_{a}\|F(a)\|
.

.

Lemma 6

\|a=b\|=\|\top\to\bot\|=\{\xi\,\mbox{\Large.}\,\pi\;;\;\xi\in\Lambda,\pi\in\Pi\}

For each functional relation

Theorem 8

Then

Let

\|x\,\varepsilon\!\!\!/\,\gimel\mbox{\bf V}\|=\|\langle x\in\mbox{\bf V}\rangle\neq 1\|

\mbox{I}\,\mbox{ $|\hskip-1.00006pt|\hskip-2.20001pt-\,$}\forall x(x\,\varepsilon\,\gimel\mbox{\bf V}\leftrightarrow\langle x\in\mbox{\bf V}\rangle=1)

In other words, the

\gimel\mbox{\bf V}

{\mathcal{M}}_{\gimel 2}

\forall x^{\gimel\mbox{\scriptsize\bf V}}F(x)

\forall x(\langle x\in\mbox{\bf V}\rangle=1\hookrightarrow F(x))

Remember also the important (and obvious) equivalence:

\mbox{I}\mbox{ $|\hskip-1.00006pt|\hskip-2.20001pt-\,$}\forall x\forall y(x=y\leftrightarrow\langle x=y\rangle=1)

{\mathcal{N}}

{\mathcal{M}}_{\gimel 2}

Theorem 22
.

{\mathcal{N}}\models\forall a\forall n^{\mbox{\footnotesize int}}\left(a\,\varepsilon\,(\gimel\mbox{\bf V})^{n}\to\exists!b^{\gimel(\mbox{{\scriptsize\bf V}}^{n})}\forall i^{\mbox{\footnotesize int}}(i<n\to a(i)=b[i])\right)

In other words, each finite sequence of
\;\gimel\mbox{\bf V}
in
{\mathcal{N}}
is represented by a unique finite sequence
of
V
in the boolean model
{\mathcal{M}}_{\gimel 2}

Proof 3.1
.

Unicity. Note first that, since there is no extensionality in
{\mathcal{N}}
, you may have two sequences

a\neq a^{\prime}\,\varepsilon\,(\gimel\mbox{\bf V})^{n}
such that
a(i)=a^{\prime}(i)
for
i<n
. Now suppose
b,b^{\prime}\,\varepsilon\,\gimel(\mbox{\bf V}^{n})
be such
that
b[i]=b^{\prime}[i]
for
i<n
. Then, we have
\langle b,b^{\prime}\in\mbox{\bf V}^{n}\rangle=1
and
\langle(\forall i<n)(b[i]=b^{\prime}[i])\rangle=1
.
Since the boolean model
{\mathcal{M}}_{\gimel 2}
satisfies extensionality, we get
\langle b=b^{\prime}\rangle=1
that is
b=b^{\prime}
.

Existence. Proof by induction on
n
. This is trivial if
n=0

b=\emptyset
.

Now, let
a\,\varepsilon\,(\gimel\mbox{\bf V})^{n+1}
and
a^{\prime}
be a restriction of

to

b^{\prime}\,\varepsilon\,\gimel(\mbox{\bf V}^{n})

a^{\prime}(i)=b^{\prime}[i]

for
i<n
(induction hypothesis).

In the ground model
{\mathcal{M}}
, we define the binary functional
+
as follows:

if
u
is a finite sequence
(u_{0},\ldots,u_{n-1})

u+v
is the sequence
(u_{0},\ldots,u_{n-1},v)

We extend it to
{\mathcal{N}}
and we set
b=b^{\prime}+a(n)
i.e.
\langle b=b^{\prime}+a(n)\rangle=1

\langle b[i]=b^{\prime}[i]\rangle=1
for
i<n
and
\langle b[n]=a(n)\rangle=1
, i.e.
a(i)=b[i]
for
i<n
and
a(n)=b[n]
.

{\mathcal{N}}

Remark 45
.

In the introdution, I said we consider the problem of turning proofs in ZFC into programs. But we must be more
precise because this may seem rather easy by means of classical realizability; indeed, here is an algorithm to transform a proof in ZFC of an arithmetical formula
A
into a program:
first, transform it into a proof of ZF
\vdash A
by restricting its quantifiers to L or HOD.
Then, get a program from this new proof using c.r.

The problem solved in the present article is more difficult and is much more interesting from the point of view of
computer science: consider the program P obtained from a proof of ZF
\vdash(AC\to A
) and,

forgetting this proof
, transform directly the program P. This is more difficult because the program P
contains much less information than the proof which gave it.
In fact, the interesting point is that this is possible.

You may compare with the problem of modifying a program, knowing its source code or only its compiled code.
It is well known that the first situation is (very) much easier.

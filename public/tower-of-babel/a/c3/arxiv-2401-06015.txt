Complexity of equal 0-surgeries
Tetsuya Abe, Marc Kegel, Nicolas Weiss

Abstract
. We say that two knots are friends if they share the same 0 0 -surgery. Two friends with different sliceness status would provide a counterexample to the 4 4 -dimensional smooth Poincaré conjecture. Here we create a census of all friends with small crossing numbers c c and tetrahedral complexities t t , and compute their smooth 4 4 -genera. In particular, we compute the minimum of c ⁡ ( K ) + c ⁡ ( K ′ ) c(K)+c(K^{\prime}) and of t ⁡ ( K ) + t ⁡ ( K ′ ) t(K)+t(K^{\prime}) among all friends K K and K ′ K^{\prime} . Along the way, we classify all 0 0 -surgeries of prime knots of at most 15 15 crossings. Moreover, we determine for many friends in our census if their traces are equivalent or not. For that, we develop a new obstruction for two traces being homeomorphic coming from symmetry-exceptional slopes of hyperbolic knots. This is enough to also determine the minimum value of c ⁡ ( K ) + c ⁡ ( K ′ ) c(K)+c(K^{\prime}) among all friends K K and K ′ K^{\prime} whose traces are not homeomorphic.

From a given knot

in the
3
-sphere

2

K

that share the same
0

0

of

We can also create a
4

4

is diffeomorphic to the
0
-surgery

of

is determined by its trace, i.e. if two knots

and

share the same trace, then they are either both slice or both non-slice. On the other hand, it is known that there exist knots with the same traces but different

-genus
[

. Adapting the above notation, we say that two non-isotopic knots are
4
-
dimensional friends

One interest in knots with the same

-surgeries is that such knots provide a potential way to disprove the smooth
4
-dimensional Poincaré conjecture (see for example
[

,

]

and

such that

is not slice, then the smooth
4
-dimensional Poincaré conjecture is false. If such a pair of knots exists we might expect it to be simple in some way (or if it is not simple we would expect to not find it at all).

The low-crossing knots

In this section, we work with the low-crossing knots, i.e. the prime knots that have diagrams of at most
15
crossings.
We start with the classification of their
0
-surgeries.

Theorem 2.1

Tables
2
and
3
list the Regina names of all non-hyperbolic
0
-surgeries of prime knots with at most
15
crossings. Any knot that is not contained in that table is proven to have a hyperbolic
0
-surgery. The list of the hyperbolic volumes of these
0
-surgeries can be accessed at
[
AKW24
]
.

The complete list of prime, non-hyperbolic knots of at most
15

0

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

SnapPy has implemented a verified version of the volume function, that if successful, returns an interval of small length in which the volume provable has to lie. In that case, the manifold is also proven to be hyperbolic. If unsuccessful an error is returned. In that case, the manifold might be hyperbolic or not.

We use that verified volume function in SnapPy to compute the volume of the
0
-surgeries. If SnapPy fails to compute the verified volume, we try to find a better triangulation where it works or raise the precision for the computations. If both methods fail to compute the volume, we use the Regina recognition code
[
Dun20a
]
, cf.
[
FPS24
]
to recognize the triangulation of the
0
-surgery as a triangulation of a non-hyperbolic manifold. This successfully classifies all
0
-surgeries of the low-crossing knots.
∎

Connected sums

Next, we show that no knot among the low crossing knots has

For that, we first prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1

Let
K=K_{1}\#\cdots\#K_{n}
be a connected sum of non-trivial, non-satellite knots
K_{1},\ldots,K_{n}
. If
K^{\prime}
is another non-trivial connected sum such that
K
and
K^{\prime}
have orientation-preserving diffeomorphic
0
-surgeries. Then
K
and
K^{\prime}
are isotopic.

Proof.

It is well-known (see for example
[
Bud06
]
) that the exterior
E_{K}
of
K
decomposes as

(D_{n}\times S^{1})\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}E_{K_{i}}

where
D_{n}
denotes a
2
-disk with
n
holes. The exterior
E_{K_{i}}
is glued to the
i
-th hole with a gluing map that identifies the meridian
\mu_{i}
of
K_{i}
with the boundary of the hole and the Seifert longitude
\lambda_{i}
of
K_{i}
with the
S^{1}
-factor. Then the longitude
\lambda_{K}
of
K
is given by the outer boundary component of
D_{n}
and the meridian
\mu_{K}
of
K
is the
S^{1}
-factor. Since every summand
K_{i}
was assumed to not be a satellite, it follows that each
K_{i}
is either a hyperbolic knot or a torus knot, and thus the above decomposition is actually the JSJ decomposition of
E_{K}
.

Now it follows that performing the
0
-surgery on
K
is the same as capping off the outer boundary component of
D_{n}
to get a
2
-sphere
S_{n}
with
n
holes. Thus the JSJ decomposition of the
0
-surgery of
K
is given as

K(0)=(S_{n}\times S^{1})\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}E_{K_{i}}

where each exterior
E_{K_{i}}
is glued to the
i
-th hole with a gluing map that identifies the meridian
\mu_{i}
of
K_{i}
with the boundary of the hole and the Seifert longitude
\lambda_{i}
of
K_{i}
with the
S^{1}
-factor. (Note that in the case of

, the above JSJ decomposition simplifies to just
E_{K_{1}}\cup E_{K_{2}}
such that meridians and longitudes get identified.)

Now let
K^{\prime}=K^{\prime}_{1}\#\cdots\#K^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}}
be another connected sum of non-trivial knots
K^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,K^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}}
such that
K(0)
is orientation-preserving diffeomorphic to
K^{\prime}(0)
. Then the uniqueness of the JSJ decomposition implies that the JSJ decompositions of
K(0)
and
K^{\prime}(0)
are the same. In particular, it follows that

and that (after renumbering)

is orientation-preserving diffeomorphic to
E_{K^{\prime}_{i}}
. Then
[
GL89
]
tells us that
K_{i}
is isotopic to
K^{\prime}_{i}
.

Note, however, that the oriented diffeomorphism type of

only determines the chirality of

, but does not yield an orientation of the knot
K_{i}
. On the other hand, the connected sum depends in general on the orientation of
K_{i}
. The orientations of the

can be recovered from the gluing maps of the above JSJ decomposition (since the orientations of meridian and longitude change if the orientation of the knot is changed). Thus it follows that
K
is isotopic to
K^{\prime}
.
∎

To show that all non-slice knots in that table have smooth

8.
Extending diffeomorphisms over traces and symmetry-exceptional slopes

In the remaining part of this article, we ask which of the friends from Table
1
are
4
-dimensional friends, i.e. share the same trace. In this section, we explain general strategies to study the question if two friends are
4
-dimensional friends.

To show in practice that two friends are
4
-dimensional friends, we describe a
0
-surgery diffeomorphism
\varphi\colon K(0)\rightarrow K^{\prime}(0)
via explicit Kirby moves and then analyze the image of the meridian
\mu
of
K
under
\varphi
. This can reveal that
\varphi
extends to a diffeomorphism of the
0
-traces, see for example
[
Akb77
,
AJOT13
,
MP23
]
.

It is often harder to show that two friends are not
4
-dimensional friends. We first recall a result of Boyer
[
Boy86
]
.
Let
K
and
K^{\prime}
be friends and let
\varphi\colon K(0)\rightarrow K^{\prime}(0)
be a diffeomorphism. We define the
parity
of
\varphi
to be the parity of the intersection form of the closed
4
-manifold

X(K^{\prime})\cup_{\varphi}-X(K).

Theorem 8.1

(Boyer
[
Boy86
]
, cf. Theorem 3.7 in
[
MP23
]
)

\varphi
extends to a homeomorphism
\Phi\colon X(K)\rightarrow X(K^{\prime})
of the traces if and only if
\varphi
is even.

We remark that there could exist
0
-surgery diffeomorphisms that extend to trace homeomorphisms but not to trace diffeomorphisms. Moreover, there exist examples of friends that have two different
0
-surgery homeomorphisms
\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}\colon K(0)\rightarrow K^{\prime}(0)
, such that
\varphi
is odd and thus does not extend to a homeomorphism of the traces but such that
\varphi^{\prime}
is even and even extends to a trace diffeomorphism. We refer to Examples
9.5
and
9.6
for concrete examples.

On the other hand,
[
MP23
]
describe situations in which every
0
-surgery diffeomorphism extends to a trace homeomorphism.

Theorem 8.2

(Manolescu–Piccirillo
[
MP23
]
)

If the Arf invariant
\operatorname{Arf}(K)
of
K
is non-vanishing, then any
0
-surgery diffeomorphism from
K(0)
to any other
0
-surgery
K^{\prime}(0)
extends to a trace homeomorphism.

Note that the Arf invariant of a knot can be computed from its Alexander polynomial and since the Alexander polynomial of a knot is an invariant of its
0
-surgery, it follows that this condition is symmetric. We have used SnapPy to compute the Arf invariants of all pairs of friends from our census. The friends with non-vanishing Arf invariants are displayed in Table
5
. By Theorem
8.2
these friends have homeomorphic traces.

On the other hand, to show that

In the following, we present a criterium to show that any
0
-surgery diffeomorphism of two friends is odd and thus the friends are not
4
-dimensional friends.

First, we recall that if
K
is a hyperbolic knot, then Thurston’s hyperbolic Dehn filling theorem tells us that for all but finitely many slopes
r
, the Dehn filling
K(r)
of
K
with slope
r
is again hyperbolic
[
Thu79
]
. (The finitely many slopes for which
K(r)
is not hyperbolic are called
exceptional
.) Moreover, if the slope
r
is sufficiently long then the core of the newly glued-in solid torus is the shortest geodesic of
K(r)
. By Mostow rigidity, any diffeomorphism of a hyperbolic manifold is isotopic to a unique isometry. Since an isometry preserves the shortest geodesic, it follows that for sufficiently large
r
, any symmetry of
K(r)
restricts to a symmetry of
K
. If there exists a symmetry of
K(r)
that does not restrict to a symmetry of
K
then
r
is called
symmetry-exceptional slope
. In particular, it follows that if
K(0)
has trivial symmetry group, then
0
is not a symmetry-exceptional slope. We refer to
[
BKM24b
]
for further discussion. We also mention that there exist computational methods to check if a given slope is a symmetry-exceptional slope
[
BKM24b
]
.

Proposition 8.3

Let
K
and
K^{\prime}
be friends.
If
K
is hyperbolic and
0
is not a symmetry-exceptional slope of
K
, then all
0
-surgery diffeomorphisms
K(0)\rightarrow K^{\prime}(0)
have the same parity.

Remark 8.4

Opposed to Theorem
8.2
, the condition in Proposition
8.3
is not symmetric. There exist friends
K
and
K^{\prime}
such that
0
is a symmetry-exceptional slope for

but not for
K^{\prime}
, see for example Table

.

In Example

below we use Proposition

to demonstrate that certain friends are not

.

9.
Algorithmic search for 0-surgery diffeomorphisms

In this section, we explain and implement various methods to algorithmically search for a Kirby calculus presentation of a
0
-surgery diffeomorphism of a given pair of friends. Using these methods we find explicit
0
-surgery diffeomorphisms for many friends from the census of Table
1
and are in some cases able to answer if the friends are
4
-dimensional friends or not.

We also note here that there exists an algorithm that takes as input diagrams of a pair of friends
K
and
K^{\prime}
and outputs a
0
-surgery diffeomorphism presented as a sequence of Kirby moves. Indeed, we can just enumerate all possible Kirby moves in the Kirby diagram of
K(0)
given by a
0
-framed diagram of
K
and will reach in finite time to the Kirby diagram of
K^{\prime}(0)
given by a
0
-framed diagram of
K^{\prime}
. Of course, this algorithm is not practical but guaranteed to theoretically always work. Below we explain certain strategies that will not work in general but often return a verified result in reasonable time.

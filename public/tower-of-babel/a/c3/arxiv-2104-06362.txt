Fibred categorical theory of obstruction and classification of morphisms
A. S. Cigoli, S. Mantovani, G. Metere, E. M. Vitale

Abstract
We set up a fibred categorical theory of obstruction and classification of morphisms that specializes to the one of monoidal functors between categorical groups and also to the Schreier-Mac Lane theory of group extensions. Further applications are provided, as for example a classification of unital associative algebra extensions with non-abelian kernel in terms of Hochschild cohomology.

the already mentioned butterflies between crossed extensions of groups (Theorem

•

Theorem 3.5
.

Let us consider a fibrewise opfibration
(P,F,G)
such that the fibres of
P
are groupoids.
Given
x
and
y
objects of
\mathsf{X}
, and
\varphi\colon P(x)\to P(y)
, we consider the set

\mathsf{X}_{\varphi}(x,y)
. Then

(i)

\mathsf{X}_{\varphi}(x,y)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
\varphi^{*}y\cong\varphi_{*}x
in the
P
-fibre.

(ii)

If this is the case, then
\mathsf{X}_{\varphi}(x,y)
is a simply transitive
H
-set (i.e. an
H
-torsor), where the acting group is

H=\mathsf{X}_{P(\varphi^{*}y)}(\varphi^{*}y,\varphi^{*}y).

Proof.

Use
\Phi
to transfer the simply transitive left action of
H
on

\mathsf{X}_{P(\varphi^{*}y)}(\varphi_{*}x,\varphi^{*}y)

given by arrow composition.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Of course, one can equivalently endow the set
\mathsf{X}_{P(\varphi^{*}y)}(\varphi_{*}x,\varphi^{*}y)
with a simply transitive right action
of the group

\mathsf{X}_{P(\varphi_{*}x)}(\varphi_{*}x,\varphi_{*}x).

When a fibrewise opfibration
(P,F,G)
satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem

, we say that it admits a
categorical obstruction theory
. In this case, that theorem provides a solution to the obstruction problem of Definition
3.3
, for any choice of
(x,y,\varphi)

such that
P(f)=\varphi

In the first part of this section, we introduce a prototype example that concerns the setting described in Section

5.1
Crossed modules and crossed extensions of groups

Here we only recall the basic notions, which are well established in the literature; we refer to
[
17
]
for some constructions and results. The whole section is based on the category of groups, but the notions and results therein can be adapted to many semi-abelian categories. For this reason, we shall often replace group-theoretical terms by categorical ones, as for example using “regular epimorphism” instead of “surjection”.

Definition 5.1
.

A pre-crossed module is a group homomorphism

\partial\colon G_{2}\to G_{1}
endowed with an action of
G_{1}
on
G_{2}
such that, for every
g_{1}\in G_{1}
and
g_{2}\in G_{2}
, the condition

(i)\ \ \partial(g_{1}*g_{2})=g_{1}\partial(g_{2})g_{1}^{-1}\,,

is satisfied. If moreover, for every
g_{2},g_{2}^{\prime}\in G_{2}
, the condition

(ii)\ \ \partial(g_{2})*g_{2}^{\prime}=g_{2}+g_{2}^{\prime}-g_{2}\,,

holds, then
\partial
is called a
crossed module
.

Given two crossed modules
\partial
and
\partial^{\prime}\colon G_{2}^{\prime}\to G_{1}^{\prime}
, a morphism
(f_{1},f_{2})\colon\partial\to\partial^{\prime}
is a pair of group homomorphisms
f_{2}\colon G_{2}\to G_{2}^{\prime}
and
f_{1}\colon G_{1}\to G_{1}^{\prime}
equivariant with respect to the actions and such that
\partial^{\prime}\cdot f_{2}=f_{1}\cdot\partial
. The category
\mathsf{XMod}(\mathsf{Gp})
of crossed modules is defined.

Given a crossed module
\partial
, the action of
G_{1}
on
G_{2}
induces an action of the cokernel
\pi_{0}(\partial)
on the abelian kernel
\pi_{1}(\partial)
of
\partial
: for
a\in\pi_{1}(\partial)
and
x=p(g_{1})\in\pi_{0}(\partial)
, one sets

x*a=g_{1}*j(a)

where
j\colon\pi_{1}(\partial)\to G_{2}
and
p\colon G_{1}\to\pi_{0}(\partial)
are the kernel inclusion and cokernel quotient respectively. This construction defines a functor
\pi
in the category
\mathsf{Mod}(\mathsf{Gp})
of group modules, i.e. of triples
(C,B,\xi)
, where
B
is a
C
-module via the action
\xi
, and equivariant pairs of morphisms as arrows.

In fact, crossed modules form a
2
-category: a
2
-cell

\alpha\colon(f_{1},f_{2})\Rightarrow(f^{\prime}_{1},f^{\prime}_{2})\colon\partial\to\partial^{\prime}

is given by a set-theoretical map
\alpha\colon G_{1}\to G_{2}^{\prime}
satisfying suitable conditions (see, for instance,
[
3
]
). All
2
-cells are isomorphisms. We shall denote the
2
-category of crossed modules by
\underline{\mathsf{XMod}}(\mathsf{Gp})
.

A morphism of crossed modules
(f_{1},f_{2})
is called
weak equivalence
if
\pi(f_{1},f_{2})
is an isomorphism. It is possible to show that every internal equivalence in the
2
-category of crossed modules is a weak equivalence according to the above definition. The converse does not hold in general.

We will need some factorization properties of crossed modules morphisms. Here we recall the
comprehensive factorization system
; details can be found in
[
18
, Section 3]
, where the results are stated in the more general case of a semi-abelian category.

Proposition 5.2
.

The category
\mathsf{XMod}(\mathsf{Gp})
admits a factorization system whose orthogonal classes are the class of final morphisms and the class of discrete fibrations. These classes are characterized as follows:

•

a morphism
(f_{1},f_{2})
is final if and only if
\pi_{0}(f_{1},f_{2})
is an isomorphism and
\pi_{1}(f_{1},f_{2})
is a regular epimorphism;

•

a morphism
(f_{1},f_{2})
is a discrete fibration if and only if
f_{2}
is an isomorphism.

5.2

If a fibrewise opfibration

is such that the fibres of

6.4
Butterflies and crossed extensions

Given the construction in Section
6.3
, we can easily extend the notion of butterfly from crossed modules to crossed extensions. As a result, we obtain the bicategory
\underline{\mathsf{BExt}}(\mathsf{Gp})
: a butterfly between two crossed extensions is nothing but a butterfly between the underlying crossed modules. Recall from
[
18
]
the definition of
[\mathsf{BExt}](\mathsf{Gp})
, i.e. the classifying category of
\underline{\mathsf{BExt}}(\mathsf{Gp})
: objects are crossed extensions in
\mathsf{Gp}
and arrows are isomorphism classes of butterflies.

The homomorphism
\mathcal{B}
of bicategories (
12
) sends a morphism of crossed modules to a butterfly, and this assignment preserves associativity and identities up to 2-cells. Hence, a functor is defined:

Q\colon\mathsf{XExt}(\mathsf{Gp})\to[\mathsf{BExt}](\mathsf{Gp})

which is the identity on objects and takes any morphism
\underline{f}=(f_{1},f_{2})
of crossed extensions to the class
[\mathcal{B}(U(\underline{f}))]
of butterflies.

Now, given the butterfly
\widehat{E}
representing a morphism
[\widehat{E}]
in
[\mathsf{BExt}](\mathsf{Gp})
, we can associate with it a span

(\underline{s},\underline{t})=((\delta,p_{1}),(\gamma,p_{2}))

in
\mathsf{XExt}(\mathsf{Gp})
obtained by extending the construction of the last section to crossed extensions (see diagram (
13
)).

Moreover, since
\underline{s}

\mathcal{B}(U(\underline{s}))

[

Q(\underline{s})

Q(\underline{t})\cdot Q(\underline{s})^{-1}=[\widehat{E}]

Then, we get the following result.

The functor

7.1
Categorical groups

It is well known that the category
\mathsf{XMod}(\mathsf{Gp})
of internal crossed modules in groups is equivalent to the category
\mathsf{Gpd}(\mathsf{Gp})
of internal groupoids in groups.
Groupoids in groups can be equivalently presented as strict categorical groups, i.e. strict monoidal groupoids where each object is invertible. In such terms, internal functors become strict monoidal functors. Actually strict categorical groups and strict monoidal functors organize in a 2-category, where 2-cells are monoidal natural transformations. This 2-category is equivalent to
\underline{\mathsf{XMod}}(\mathsf{Gp})
, hence its bicategory of fractions
\underline{\mathsf{Mon}\mathsf{Gp}}
is biequivalent to
\underline{\mathsf{Bfly}}(\mathsf{Gp})
. In
[
34
]
, it is proved that (not necessarily strict) monoidal functors serve as morphisms between strict categorical groups in
\underline{\mathsf{Mon}\mathsf{Gp}}
and in
[
1
]
it is described in detail how to perform the correspondence with butterflies. Actually,
\underline{\mathsf{Mon}\mathsf{Gp}}
is biequivalent to the 2-category
\underline{\mathsf{Cat}\mathsf{Gp}}
of (not necessarily strict) categorical groups, monoidal functors and monoidal natural transformations. Hence
[\mathsf{Cat}\mathsf{Gp}]
is equivalent to
[\mathsf{Bfly}(\mathsf{Gp})]
, hence to
[\mathsf{BExt}(\mathsf{Gp})]
.

Thanks to the previous equivalences, we can adapt Theorem
6.7
to the present context in order to formulate an obstruction and classification theorem for monoidal functors between categorical groups. With each categorical group
\mathbb{G}
, one can functorially associate a module
\Pi(\mathbb{G})=(\Pi_{0}(\mathbb{G}),\Pi_{1}(\mathbb{G}),\xi_{\mathbb{G}})
, where
\Pi_{0}(\mathbb{G})
and
\Pi_{1}(\mathbb{G})
are the homotopy invariants of the categorical group, respectively given by the group of connected components of
\mathbb{G}
and the group of automorphisms of its identity object. Moreover, we can choose an equivalence
\Phi
between
[\mathsf{Cat}\mathsf{Gp}]
and
[\mathsf{BExt}(\mathsf{Gp})]
in such a way that
P\cdot\Phi=\Pi
, where
P\colon[\mathsf{BExt}(\mathsf{Gp})]\to\mathsf{Mod}(\mathsf{Gp})
is as in Theorem
6.6
. Via
\Phi
, one can also associate with each categorical group
\mathbb{G}
an element
[\epsilon_{\mathbb{G}}]
of the cohomology group
\mathcal{H}^{3}(\Pi_{0}(\mathbb{G}),\Pi_{1}(\mathbb{G}),\xi_{\mathbb{G}})
.

Then we are ready to recover, in the following theorem, a result on the classification of monoidal functors between categorical groups stated by Cegarra, García-Calcines and Ortega in
[
14
]
and based on the homotopical classification of categorical groups established by Sinh in
[
32
]
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Given two categorical groups
\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbb{G}
, and a homomorphism of modules
\underline{\varphi}=(\varphi_{0},\varphi)\colon\Pi(\mathbb{H})\to\Pi(\mathbb{G})
, then

i)

there exists a monoidal functor

F\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{G}

with

if and only if
[\varphi\cdot\epsilon_{\mathbb{H}}]=[\epsilon_{\mathbb{G}}\cdot(\varphi_{0}\times\varphi_{0}\times\varphi_{0})]
in the group
\mathcal{H}^{3}(\Pi_{0}(\mathbb{H}),\Pi_{1}(\mathbb{G}),\varphi_{0}^{*}\xi_{\mathbb{G}})

ii)

if this is the case, isomorphism classes of such functors form a simply transitive
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Pi_{0}(\mathbb{H}),\Pi_{1}(\mathbb{G}),\varphi_{0}^{*}\xi_{\mathbb{G}})
-set.

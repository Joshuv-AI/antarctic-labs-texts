Introduction

For a sequence
a_{n}
(such as
\Lambda(n)
or
d_{k}(n)
) let
S(\alpha)
be its exponential sum

\sum_{n\leq x}a_{n}e(n\alpha)

and consider the

\int_{0}^{1}|S(\alpha)|d\alpha.

We’d expect this norm to give us some insight about the sequence

itself, the most famous result probably being Vaughan’s result
[
5
]
that for the (weighted) primes the norm is
\gg\sqrt{x}
; the same argument shows the norm to be
\gg\sqrt{x}
for the divisor functions too. On the other hand the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality shows the norm to be

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\sqrt{x\log x}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{ for the primes}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\sqrt{x(\log x)^{k^{2}-1}}\hskip 28.45274pt\text{ for the $k$-fold divisor function}

so the question is whether these
\log
powers are really there. In this note we’ll be interested in this question for the divisor function
d(n)
.

That the norm is
\ll\sqrt{x}
was claimed in
[
1
]
, but the proof was shown to be incorrect by Goldston and Pandey
[
2
]
, who also gave the current best bound in the literature - they proved the norm is
\ll\sqrt{x}\log x
. We prove that it is
\ll\sqrt{x}
.

Parts of this work was started when the author was working for the Indraprastha Institute of Information Technology, Delhi, and we thank Prof.’s Sneha Chaubey and Debika Benerjee for discussions around the problem.

Uniqueness of Galilean and Carrollian limits of gravitational theories and application to higher derivative gravity
Poula Tadros, Ivan Kolář

Abstract
We show that the seemingly different methods used to derive non-Lorentzian (Galilean and Carrollian) gravitational theories from Lorentzian ones are equivalent. Specifically, the pre-nonrelativistic and the pre-ultralocal parametrizations can be constructed from the gauging of the Galilei and Carroll algebras, respectively. Also, the pre-ultralocal approach of taking the Carrollian limit is equivalent to performing the ADM decomposition and then setting the signature of the Lorentzian manifold to zero. We use this uniqueness to write a generic expansion for the curvature tensors and construct Galilean and Carrollian limits of all metric theories of gravity of finite order ranging from the f ⁡ ( R ) f(R) gravity to a completely generic higher derivative theory, the f ⁡ ( g μ ​ ν , R μ ​ ν ​ σ ​ ρ , ∇ μ ) f(g_{\mu\nu},R_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho},\nabla_{\mu}) gravity. We present an algorithm for calculation of the n n -th order of the Galilean and Carrollian expansions that transforms this problem into a constrained optimization problem. We also derive the condition under which a gravitational theory becomes a modification of general relativity in both limits simultaneously.

While calculating the Carrollian limit of Maxwell’s theory, it was shown that there are two nonequivalent Carrollian limits for any Lorentzian theory. The first one keeps the electric part in Maxwell’s theory and so was named the
“electric limit”
, and the other keeps the magnetic part and so was called the
“magnetic limit”
. There are multiple ways to take the (electric and magnetic) Carrollian limit of a Lorentzian gravitational theory:

i)

By parametrizing the Lagrangian using the
pre-ultralocal (PUL) parametrization
, then taking

;

ii)

By performing the ADM decomposition for the Lagrangian and set the signature of the metric to zero

;
Henneaux and Salgado-Rebolledo 2021

iii)

By gauging the algebra of the theory and performing the ultralocal Inönü-Wigner contraction

c^{-1}

{c\to 0}

{c\to 0}

In this paper, we show that the PNR parametrization can be constructed from GAG. Similarly, the PUL parametrization can be constructed from CAG. We also show that the PUL and ZS approaches of taking the Carrollian limit are completely equivalent (and, similarly, PNR and IS). Throughout this paper, we consider the ADM decomposition with an extra real parameter
\epsilon
which can be interpreted as a signature if it is
1
,
-1
, or
0

. Once we establish the uniqueness of the Galilean and Carrollian limits (i.e., the equivalence of above approaches), we construct an algorithm for computing the
n
-th order of the Galilean, and the Carrollian expansions of a completely generic higher derivative gravity (HDG). The problem is transformed into computationally easier optimization problems which upon solving gives the desired order.

The paper is organised as follows:

•

In Sec.
II
, we briefly review the methods used to construct non-Lorentzian gravitational theories from Lorentzian ones.

•

In Sec.
III
, we give a proof that the PUL and the ZS approaches are equivalent in the sense that they describe the same limit of the metric with same variables and parameters. Naturally, the same is true for PNR and IS approaches. Furthermore, we show that the PUL parametrization and the Carroll compatible connection can be constructed by gauging the Carroll algebra, i.e., the equivalence of PUL and CAG. Similarly, we also prove that we can construct the PNR parametrization and the Galilei compatible connection from the Galilei algebra gauging procedure, i.e., the equivalence of PNR and GAG.

•

In Sec.
IV

gravity, the

gravity, into the constrained optimization problems. We also present the conditions under which a gravitational theory can be a modification of GR in both the Galilean and Carrollian regimes.

•

In Sec.
V

II
Review of non-Lorentzian limits

We begin with a brief review of the methods that are used in the available literature for construction of non-Lorentzian theories.

II.1
Pre-ultralocal parametrization

The PUL parameterization is the parametrization that is suitable for taking the ultralocal (Carrollian) limit
Hansen et al. 2022
. The metric parametrization is the
given by

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-c^{2}T_{\mu}T_{\nu}+\Pi_{\mu\nu},

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0ptg^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{c^{2}}V^{\mu}V^{\nu}+\Pi^{\mu\nu},

(1)

where
T_{\mu}
and
V^{\mu}
is the orthonormal covector and vector while
\Pi_{\mu\nu}
and
\Pi^{\mu\nu}
is the induced metric and its inverse; they satisfy

\displaystyle T_{\mu}V^{\mu}

\displaystyle=-1,

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0pt-V^{\mu}T_{\nu}+\Pi^{\rho\mu}\Pi_{\rho\nu}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\mu}^{\nu},

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0ptT_{\mu}\Pi^{\mu\nu}=\Pi_{\mu\nu}V^{\nu}

\displaystyle=0.

(2)

It proves useful to introduce a Carroll compatible connection by demanding that
\Pi^{\mu\nu}
and
T_{\mu}
are covariantly constant,

C^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}=-V^{\rho}\partial_{(\mu}T_{\nu)}-V^{\rho}T_{(\mu}\pounds_{\boldsymbol{V}}T_{\nu)}+\tfrac{1}{2}\Pi^{\rho\lambda}\bigg[\partial_{\mu}\Pi_{\nu\lambda}+\partial_{\nu}\Pi_{\lambda\mu}-\partial_{\lambda}\Pi_{\mu\nu}\bigg]-\Pi^{\rho\lambda}T_{\nu}\mathcal{K}_{\mu\lambda},

(3)

where
\mathcal{K}_{\mu\lambda}
is the extrinsic curvature defined by
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\pounds_{\boldsymbol{V}}\boldsymbol{\Pi}}
. This connection is defined so that the respective covariant derivative leaves the Carrollian structure invariant (similar to the requirement that the covariant derivative of the metric is zero in the Lorentzian case). This can be gone by requiring that
\Pi_{\mu\nu}
and
V^{\mu}
(the defining quantities for the Carrollian structure) to be covariantly constant. Such connection can be derived from fundamental arguments in the gauging procedure of the Galilei algebra as we will see below.

Assuming analyticity, we again expand all the quantities in powers of
c^{2}
:

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=h^{\mu\nu}+c^{2}\Phi^{\mu\nu}+O(c^{4}),

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0pt\Pi_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=h_{\mu\nu}+c^{2}\Phi_{\mu\nu}+O(c^{4}),

(4)

\displaystyle V^{\mu}

\displaystyle=v^{\mu}+c^{2}M^{\mu}+O(c^{4}),

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0ptT_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\tau_{\mu}+c^{2}N_{\mu}+O(c^{4}).

This allows us to express the Lagrangian in powers of
c^{2}
. The LO (electric limit) will define an ultralocal theory, i.e, a theory that does not admit a non-tachyonic single-particle motion. In contrast, the theory obtained in the NLO (magnetic limit) allows some motion, and it also admits massive solutions.

II.2
Pre-nonrelativistic parametrization

A convenient parametrization of the metric that allows one to take the nonrelativistic (Galilean) limit is the PNR parametrization
Hansen et al. 2021
. The metric parameterization is the same as (
1
) with the conditions (
2
), but a different connection that is compatible with Galilean symmetries:

C^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}=-V^{\rho}\partial_{\mu}T_{\nu}+\tfrac{1}{2}\Pi^{\rho\sigma}\bigg(\partial_{\mu}\Pi_{\nu\sigma}+\partial_{\nu}\Pi_{\mu\sigma}-\partial_{\sigma}\Pi_{\mu\nu}\bigg).

(5)

This connection is defined so that the respective covariant derivative leaves the Galilean structure invariant. This can be gone by requiring that
\Pi^{\mu\nu}
and
T_{\mu}
to be covariantly constant. Such connection can be derived from fundamental arguments in the gauging procedure of the Galilei algebra as we will see below.

Now, assuming the analyticity of all the quantities, we expand
\Pi_{\mu\nu},\Pi^{\mu\nu},V^{\mu},T_{\mu}
in powers of
{c^{-2}}
:

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=h_{\mu\nu}+\tfrac{1}{c^{2}}\Phi_{\mu\nu}+O\big(\tfrac{1}{c^{4}}\big),

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0pt\Pi^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=h^{\mu\nu}+\tfrac{1}{c^{2}}\Phi^{\mu\nu}+O\big(\tfrac{1}{c^{4}}\big),

(6)

\displaystyle V^{\mu}

\displaystyle=v^{\mu}+\tfrac{1}{c^{2}}m^{\mu}+O\big(\tfrac{1}{c^{4}}\big),

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0ptT_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\tau_{\mu}+\tfrac{1}{c^{2}}m_{\mu}+O\big(\tfrac{1}{c^{4}}\big),

where
m_{\mu}
is the mass current which is conserved in Galilean theories. (It also corresponds to the additional generator in the extension of Galilean algebra to Bargmann algebra in the gauging process.) The final step in the Galilean limit is to use the formulae above to expand the Lagrangian. The expansion to the LO will only involve the LO fields
h_{\mu\nu},h^{\mu\nu},v^{\mu}
and
\tau_{\mu}
, which define NC structure, while the NLO should also include
\Phi^{\mu\nu},\Phi_{\mu\nu},m^{\mu}
, and
m_{\mu}
, which modifies it to type II TNC structure.

II.3
Zero signature approach

The ZS approach is a method of taking the ultralocal limit of a Lorentzian theory. It consists of performing the ADM decomposition, and then setting the signature of the manifold to zero. This process results in a Carrollian manifold
Henneaux 1979
. This is equivalent of taking the limit of the norm of the normal vector to zero. The first step is to decompose the metric with a Lorentzian and a Riemannian signatures. The Lorentzian metric is decomposed as

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=-n_{\mu}n_{\nu}+h_{\mu\nu},

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}=-n^{\mu}n^{\nu}+h^{\mu\nu},

(7)

where
h_{\mu\nu}
is the induced metric on the spatial surfaces and
n_{\mu}
is the corresponding orthogonal 1-form with
n_{\mu}n^{\mu}=-1
.
The Riemannian counterpart is

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=n_{\mu}n_{\nu}+h_{\mu\nu},

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}=n^{\mu}n^{\nu}+h^{\mu\nu}.

(8)

Calculating the Hamiltonian of GR in both signatures yields

H=G_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho}\pi^{\mu\nu}\pi^{\sigma\rho}-\sqrt{g}R=G_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho}\pi^{\mu\nu}\pi^{\sigma\rho}+\bar{R}-(K^{2}-K_{\mu\nu}K^{\mu\nu}),

(9)

where
G_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho}=\tfrac{1}{2\sqrt{g}}(g_{\mu\sigma}g_{\nu\rho}+g_{\mu\rho}g_{\nu\sigma}-g_{\mu\nu}g_{\sigma\rho})
,
\bar{R}
is the three dimensional Ricci scalar,
K_{\mu\nu}
is the extrinsic curvature, and
K=h^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu}
in the Lorentzian case, and

H=G_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho}\pi^{\mu\nu}\pi^{\sigma\rho}+\sqrt{g}R=G_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho}\pi^{\mu\nu}\pi^{\sigma\rho}-\bar{R}-(K^{2}-K_{\mu\nu}K^{\mu\nu}),

(10)

in the Riemannian case. The first term vanishes by the Hamiltonian constraints and we end up with the same results as if we began with the Lagrangian.

It was shown in
Henneaux 1979
that removing the terms that switches sign with signature results in a quantity that respects Carroll symmetries, i.e,
K^{2}-K_{\mu\nu}K^{\mu\nu}
. This is effectively the same as setting the signature of the manifold to zero. We will do the same procedure using the Lagrangian formalism in the following section in a different way, by considering the signature as a real parameter.

Contrary to the previously reviewed approaches, here we use the usual Levi-Civita connection. Expressing the Lagrangian in terms of the ADM variables, then setting the signature of the manifold to zero, we get a degenerate metric with the Carrollian theory (on a Carrollian manifold).

II.4
Infinite scaling approach

Starting from a Lorentzian theory and writing the metric in the same form as in the ZS approach but taking the limit of the norm of the normal vector to
-\infty
results in a Galilean theory. Alternatively, starting with the Riemannian theory
and taking the limit to
\infty
results in the same Galilean theory; the difference would be only in an overall sign in the Lagrangian.

II.5
Carroll algebra gauging

The gauging procedure for the Carroll algebra, the CAG approach, was done in
Bergshoeff et al. 2017
;
Hartong 2015
;
Figueroa-O’Farrill et al. 2022b
. We will briefly review it and show its equivalence with the PUL parametrization. The generators of the Carroll algebra are
H
(time translation),
P_{a}
(space translations),
G_{a}
(Carroll boosts), and
J_{ab}
(space rotations). We assign to each generator a gauge field as follows

\displaystyle H\to\tau_{\mu},

\displaystyle P_{a}\to e_{\mu}{}^{a},

\displaystyle G_{a}\to\omega_{\mu}{}^{a},

\displaystyle J_{ab}\to\omega_{\mu}{}^{ab}.

(11)

The most general connection for the resulting geometry is

\mathcal{A}_{\mu}=H\tau_{\mu}+P_{a}e_{\mu}{}^{a}+G_{a}\omega_{\mu}{}^{a}+\tfrac{1}{2}J_{ab}\omega_{\mu}{}^{ab}.

(12)

Let us define an infinitesimal parameter for each gauge field by the virtue of an infinitesimal vector field
\xi^{\mu}
,

\displaystyle\zeta=\xi^{\mu}\tau_{\mu},

\displaystyle\zeta^{a}=\xi^{\mu}e_{\mu}{}^{a},

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0pt\lambda^{a},

\displaystyle\lambda^{ab}.

(13)

Using these parameters we construct the infinitesimal transform parameter

\Pi=H\zeta+P_{a}\zeta^{a}+G_{a}\lambda^{a}+\tfrac{1}{2}J_{ab}\lambda^{ab}=\xi^{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\mu}+G_{a}\lambda^{a}+\tfrac{1}{2}J_{ab}\lambda^{ab}.

(14)

The second equality follows from
\xi^{\mu}\omega_{\mu}^{a}=\xi^{\mu}\omega_{\mu\nu}^{ab}=0
. Let
\omega
be a real parameter. Considering the geneators as rescaled Poincare generators, We notice that the rescaling of
\tau_{\mu}
and
\zeta
by
\omega^{-1}
and of
\omega_{\mu}{}^{a}
and
\lambda^{a}
by
\omega
leaves the connection
\mathcal{A}_{\mu}
and the infinitesimal transformation parameter
\Pi
invariant In the Carrollian limit (where
H
and
G_{a}
get rescaled by
\omega
). This consideration will be useful to define a metric usind the scaling parameter
\omega
. The field strength tensor (curvature) associated to
\mathcal{A}_{\mu}
as

F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\mathcal{A}_{\mu}+[\mathcal{A}_{\mu},\mathcal{A}_{\nu}]=HR_{\mu\nu}(H)+P_{a}R_{\mu\nu}{}^{a}(P)+G_{a}R_{\mu\nu}{}^{a}(G)+\tfrac{1}{2}J_{ab}R_{\mu\nu}{}^{ab}(J),

(15)

where
\partial_{\mu}
is the partial derivative with respect to a generic coordinate system, and
R_{\mu\nu}(H),R^{a}_{\mu\nu}(P),R^{ab}_{\mu\nu}(J),R^{a}_{\mu\nu}(G)
are curvatures associated with the Carroll algebra generators which are given by

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}(H)

\displaystyle=2\partial_{[\mu}\tau_{\nu]}-2\omega_{[\mu}{}^{a}e_{\nu]a},

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0ptR_{\mu\nu}{}^{a}(P)

\displaystyle=2\partial_{[\mu}e_{\nu]}{}^{a}-2\omega_{[\mu}{}^{ab}\omega_{\nu]b},

(16)

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}{}^{a}(G)

\displaystyle=2\partial_{[\mu}\omega_{\nu]}{}^{a}-2\omega_{[\mu}{}^{ab}\omega_{\nu]b},

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0ptR_{\mu\nu}{}^{ab}(J)

\displaystyle=2\partial_{[\mu}\omega_{\nu]}{}^{ab}-2\omega_{[\mu}{}^{a}_{c}\omega_{\nu]}{}^{cb}.

These quantities can be used to construct the torsion and the Riemann tensor as shown in

II.6
Galilei algebra gauging

Let us also review the gauging procedure for the Galilei algebra, i.e., the GAG approach. The procedure is the same as the CAG procedure but with different generators

. We first assign a gauge field to each generator,

\displaystyle H\to\tau_{\mu},

\displaystyle P_{a}\to e_{\mu}{}^{a},

\displaystyle G_{a}\to\omega_{\mu}{}^{a},

\displaystyle J_{ab}\to\omega_{\mu}{}^{ab},

(17)

where

denote the Galilean boosts. The general connection and the infinitesimal transformation parameter are the same as in the Carrollian case. However, as in the Carrollian case, by considering the Galilean algebra generators as rescaled Poincare generators, the rescaling of

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}(H)

\displaystyle=2\partial_{[\mu}\tau_{\nu]},

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}{}^{a}(P)

\displaystyle=2\partial_{[\mu}e_{\nu]}{}^{a}-2\omega_{[\mu}{}^{ab}e_{\nu]b}-2\omega_{[\mu}{}^{a}\tau_{\nu]},

(18)

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}{}^{a}(G)

\displaystyle=2\partial_{[\mu}\omega_{\nu]}{}^{a}-2\omega_{[\mu}{}^{ab}\omega_{\nu]b},

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0ptR_{\mu\nu}{}^{ab}(J)

where
\partial_{\mu}

III
Comparison of non-Lorentzian methods

Let us demonstrate the equivalence between the various approaches mentioned above.

III.1
Equivalence of PUL and ZS approaches

Here, we rewrite the formulae used in the ADM decomposition with an extra real parameter
\epsilon
. This parameter should be understood as a modification to the normalization of the vector
n_{\mu}
that is orthogonal to the spacelike slices, but not necessarily orthonormal,
{n_{\mu}n^{\mu}=\epsilon}
. The metric is Lorentzian for
{\epsilon<0}
, Riemannian for
{\epsilon>0}
, and degenerate for
{\epsilon=0}
. Following the usual ADM decomposition procedure, we write the metric and its inverse in an adapted coordinate system satisfying
{\boldsymbol{\partial}_{t}=N\boldsymbol{n}+\boldsymbol{N}}
,

g_{\mu\nu}=\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon(N^{2}+\epsilon N^{i}N_{i})&-\epsilon N_{i}\\
-\epsilon N_{i}&h_{ij}\end{bmatrix},

(19)

g^{\mu\nu}=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac{1}{\epsilon N^{2}}&\tfrac{N^{j}}{N^{2}}\\
\tfrac{N^{i}}{N^{2}}&h^{ij}+\epsilon\frac{N^{i}N^{j}}{N^{2}}\end{bmatrix},

(20)

where
N
is the lapse function,
N_{i}
is the shift vector, and

is the induced metric. The vector

in adapted coordinates reads

\displaystyle n_{\mu}=\left[-\epsilon N,0,0,0\right],

\displaystyle n^{\mu}=\left[-\tfrac{1}{N},\epsilon\tfrac{N^{i}}{N}\right].

(21)

The metric and its inverse can be written in terms of the orthogonal (co)vector, the induced metric, and its inverse as

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=h_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}n_{\mu}n_{\nu},

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0ptg^{\mu\nu}=h^{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}n^{\mu}n^{\nu}.

(22)

One can show that
h_{\mu}^{\nu}h_{\nu}^{\rho}=h^{\rho}_{\mu}
, and
h^{\mu}_{\nu}=\delta_{\nu}^{\mu}-\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}n^{\mu}n_{\nu}
. The extrinsic curvature is then given by

K_{\mu\nu}=h_{\mu}^{\alpha}h_{\nu}^{\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}n_{\beta}=-\nabla_{\nu}n_{\nu}+\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}n_{\mu}n^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}n_{\nu}.

(23)

It is easy to deduce that
\boldsymbol{K}=-\tfrac{1}{2}\pounds_{\boldsymbol{n}}\boldsymbol{h}
. We notice that although (
21
) was written in some adapted coordinates the fact that

has an overall factor of
\epsilon
while

as

does not change. This is because the dependency on the signature does not depend on the choice of the coordinate system. Thus, we can rescale

where

when

. Writing (

) in terms of the covector
\tilde{n}_{\mu}
we get

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=h_{\mu\nu}+\epsilon\tilde{n}_{\mu}\tilde{n}_{\nu},

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0ptg^{\mu\nu}=h^{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}n^{\mu}n^{\nu},

(24)

\displaystyle\tilde{n}_{\mu}h^{\mu\nu}=n^{\mu}h_{\mu\nu}=0,

\displaystyle\tilde{n}_{\mu}n^{\mu}=-1,

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0pth_{\mu\nu}h^{\nu\rho}-\tilde{n}_{\mu}n^{\rho}=\delta^{\rho}_{\mu}.

(25)

and

K_{\mu\nu}=\epsilon\big(\nabla_{\mu}\tilde{n}_{\nu}+\tilde{n}_{\mu}n^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}\tilde{n}_{\nu}\big).

(26)

This is the PUL form that appeared in
Hansen et al. 2022
with
\epsilon=-c^{2}
. Thus, the limit
\epsilon\to 0

c\to 0

We now present the explicit calculations for GR. The resulting formulae are the same as the formulae derived using the PUL approach up to renaming of quantities. We can write the extrinsic curvature in terms of partial derivatives instead of covariant derivatives as follows

Assuming that the quantities

Having shown that, we write Gauss relation and its contractions in terms of the parameter
\epsilon

h_{\alpha}^{\mu}h_{\beta}^{\nu}h_{\rho}^{\gamma}h_{\sigma}^{\delta}R^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}=\bar{R}^{\gamma}{}_{\delta\alpha\beta}-\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\big(K^{\gamma}_{\alpha}K_{\delta\beta}-K^{\gamma}_{\beta}K_{\delta\alpha}\big),

(28)

where
R^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}
is the 4-dimensional Riemann tensor and
\bar{R}^{\gamma}{}_{\delta\alpha\beta}
is the 3-dimensional Riemann tensor on a spacelike slice. Contracting
\gamma

\alpha

h_{\alpha}^{\mu}h_{\rho}^{\alpha}=h_{\rho}^{\mu}=\delta_{\rho}^{\mu}-\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}n^{\mu}n_{\rho}

h_{\beta}^{\nu}h_{\alpha}^{\sigma}R_{\sigma\nu}-\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\big(h_{\beta\mu}h_{\alpha}^{\nu}n^{\rho}n^{\sigma}{}_{(4)}{R^{\mu}{}_{\rho\nu\sigma}}\big)=\bar{R}_{\alpha\beta}-\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\big(KK_{\alpha\beta}-K_{\alpha}^{\delta}K_{\delta\beta}\big),

(29)

R-\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\big(2n^{\mu}n^{\nu}R_{\mu\nu}\big)=\bar{R}-\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\big(K^{2}-K^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu}\big),

(30)

where
R

R=\bar{R}-\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\big(K^{2}-K^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu}-2\nabla_{\mu}A^{\mu}\big),

(31)

where
A^{\mu}=-n^{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}n^{\nu}+n^{\nu}\nabla_{\nu}n^{\mu}

{K^{2}=\tilde{K}^{2}}

{K_{\mu\nu}K^{\mu\nu}=\tilde{K}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{K}^{\mu\nu}+\epsilon^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{n})^{2}}
where
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{n}
is the exterior derivative of
n_{\mu}

R=-\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}\big(\tilde{K}^{2}-\tilde{K}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{K}^{\mu\nu}-2\nabla_{\mu}A_{1}^{\mu}\big)+\bar{R}-2\nabla_{\mu}A_{2}^{\mu}+\epsilon(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{n})^{2},

(32)

A_{1}^{\mu}=-n^{\mu}\partial_{\nu}n^{\nu}+n^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}n^{\mu}-\tfrac{1}{2}n^{\mu}n^{\sigma}h^{\alpha\rho}\big(2\partial_{(\alpha}h_{\sigma)\rho}-\partial_{\rho}h_{\alpha\sigma}\big)+h^{\mu\rho}n^{\sigma}n^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}h_{\sigma\rho}-\tfrac{1}{2}n^{\sigma}n^{\mu}h^{\mu\rho}\partial_{\rho}h_{\sigma\nu}

III.3
Equivalence of PUL and CAG approaches

Let us now reconstruct the PUL parametrization from the CAG procedure.
Using the procedure delineated in Sec.
II.5
, we define inverses to the gauge fields vielbein (
\tau_{\mu}
and
e_{\mu}{}^{a}
) by

\displaystyle v^{\mu}\tau_{\mu}=-1,

\displaystyle v^{\mu}e_{\mu}{}^{a}=\tau_{\mu}e^{\mu}{}_{b}=0,\hskip 10.0pt

\displaystyle e_{\mu}{}^{a}e^{\mu}{}_{b}=\delta_{b}^{a},

\displaystyle e_{\mu}{}^{a}e^{\nu}{}_{a}=\delta_{\mu}^{\nu}-\tau_{\mu}v^{\nu}.

(33)

Performing the redefinitions in
Bergshoeff et al. 2017
, where
\tau_{\mu}
,
\omega_{\mu}{}^{a}
,
\zeta
, and
\lambda^{a}
are rescaled by
\omega^{-1}
and taking the limit
\omega\to\infty

\mathcal{A}_{\mu}
and
\Pi
invariant, we can write the metric and its inverse as

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\tfrac{-1}{\omega}\tau_{\mu}\tau_{\nu}+e_{\mu}{}^{a}e_{\nu}{}^{b}\delta_{ab},

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}=-\omega v^{\mu}v^{\nu}+e^{\mu}{}_{a}e^{\nu}{}_{b}\delta^{ab}.

(34)

This form is the same form in the PUL approach with
{\omega=c^{-2}}
. It follows from this fact is that all curvature tensors will be the same in the two approaches. Thus, any gravity theory will be expanded similarly, i.e, the two approaches are equivalent.

We now derive the Carroll compatible connection presented in
Hansen et al. 2022
from the CAG approach. To do that, let us define a covariant derivative that are compatible with the veilbein
Bergshoeff et al. 2017
by

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\tau_{\nu}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}\tau_{\nu}+C^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}\tau_{\rho}-\omega_{\mu a}e_{\nu}{}^{a}=0,

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0ptD_{\mu}e_{\nu}{}^{a}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}e_{\nu}{}^{a}-C^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}e_{\rho}{}^{a}-\omega_{\mu}{}^{a}_{b}e_{\nu}^{b}=0,

(35)

\displaystyle D_{\mu}v^{\nu}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}v^{\nu}+C_{\mu\rho}^{\nu}v^{\rho}=0,

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0ptD_{\mu}e^{\nu}{}_{a}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}e^{\nu}{}_{a}+C^{\nu}_{\mu\rho}e^{\rho}{}_{a}-\omega_{\mu a}v^{\nu}-\omega_{\mu}{}_{a}^{b}e^{\nu}{}_{b}=0,

where
C^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}
is the Carroll compatible connection,
\omega_{\mu a}
and
\omega_{\mu}{}^{a}_{b}
act as spin connections, and
\partial_{\mu}
is the partial derivative with respect to a generic coordinate system. Defining
h_{\mu\nu}=e_{\mu}{}^{a}e_{\nu}{}^{b}\delta_{ab}
and solving for
C^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}
, we see that the simplest connection to satisfy the conditions is

C^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}=-v^{\rho}\partial_{(\mu}\tau_{\nu)}-v^{\rho}\tau_{(\mu}\pounds_{\boldsymbol{v}}\tau_{\nu)}+\tfrac{1}{2}h^{\rho\sigma}\big(\partial_{\mu}h_{\nu\sigma}+\partial_{\nu}h_{\mu\sigma}-\partial_{\sigma}h_{\mu\nu}\big)+\tfrac{1}{2}h^{\rho\sigma}\tau_{\nu}\pounds_{\boldsymbol{v}}h_{\mu\sigma}.

(36)

(For more general connections see
Hartong 2015
.) This procedure is the generalized version of the one in appendix B of
Hansen et al. 2022
. It is easy to see from this point using (
16
) that the torsion and the curvature tensors match that of the PUL ones. For example, the Riemann tensor is given by

R^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu\sigma}=v^{\rho}e_{\sigma a}R_{\mu\nu}{}^{a}(G)+e^{\rho}{}_{a}e_{\sigma b}R_{\mu\nu}{}^{ab}(J),

(37)

which, upon calculating explicitly, gives the same form as (2.18) in
Tadros and Kolář 2023
.

Similar to the previous section, the metric can be written as

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=-\omega\tau_{\mu}\tau_{\nu}+e_{\mu}{}^{a}e_{\nu}{}^{b}\delta_{ab},

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}=-\tfrac{1}{\omega}v^{\mu}v^{\nu}+e^{\mu}{}_{a}e^{\nu}{}_{b}\delta^{ab}.

(38)

This is the same form as in the PNR parameterization with
\omega=c^{2}
We can also define a Galilei compatible covariant derivative as

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\tau_{\nu}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}\tau_{\nu}-C^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}\tau_{\rho}=0,

\displaystyle D_{\mu}e_{\nu}{}^{a}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}e_{\nu}{}^{a}-C^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}e_{\rho}{}^{a}-\omega_{\mu}{}^{a}\tau_{\nu}-\omega_{\mu}{}^{a}_{b}e_{\nu}{}^{b}=0,

(39)

\displaystyle D_{\mu}v^{\nu}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}v^{\nu}+C^{\nu}_{\mu\rho}v^{\rho}-\omega_{\mu}{}^{a}e^{\nu}{}_{a}=0,

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0ptD_{\mu}e^{\nu}{}_{a}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}e^{\nu}{}_{a}+C_{\mu\rho}^{\nu}e^{\rho}{}_{a}-\omega_{\mu}{}^{b}{}_{a}e^{\nu}{}_{b}=0,

{h_{\mu\nu}=e_{\mu}{}^{a}e_{\nu}{}^{b}\delta_{ab}}

IV
Applications to HDG

Having established that all methods of taking the Galilean and Carrollian limits are equivalent, we derive an algorithm to calculate the
n-
th order of the Galilean and Carrollian expansions of a generic HDG theory.

IV.1
f(R)
gravity

Here we derive the Galilean and the Carrollian limits for a general
f(R)
gravity theory to the LO and NLO. We introduce an algorithm for finding any order of the expansions using combinatorial arguments. The Lagrangian for
f(R)
gravity is

\mathcal{L}=f(R),

(42)

where
R
is the Ricci scalar and
f
is an analytic function. We expand
f
as a power series in integer powers of
R
, with coefficients
g_{i}
:

f(R)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}g_{i}R^{i},

(43)

where
g_{i}
are constant coefficients that are independent of
c
.

Since all methods of constructing the Galilean and Carrollian expansions are equivalent as shown in previous sections, we can write the Ricci scalar as a generic expansion as follows

R=\tfrac{1}{c^{2}}R_{1}+R_{2}+c^{2}R_{3},

(44)

where
R_{1},R_{2},R_{3}
are the expansion terms shown explicitly in (3.2) in
Hansen et al. 2022
; they are also displayed in (
88
). (The use of the connection does not affect the from of the expansion as shown in Sec.
III
, different connections will result in different forms for
R_{1}
,
R_{2}
,
R_{3}
).

The constants
g_{i}
are considered as functions of
c
(not necessarily regular). Writing

g_{i}(c)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{ij}c^{j},

(45)

where
a_{ij}
are constants without any
c
dependency, and expanding
f(R)
, we get

f(R)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{ij}\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m,k\geq 0\\
n+m+k=i\end{subarray}}}c^{2k-2n+j}\frac{i!}{n!m!k!}R_{1}^{n}R_{2}^{m}R_{3}^{k}.

(46)

Here, we need to be extra careful since
j
can be unbounded regardless of the value of
i
, and appears in the power of
c
. For a theory to have consistent Galilean and Carrollian limits, the quantity
{2k-2n+j}
must be bounded from above and below.

IV.1.1
Carrollian limit

Let us denote
l=\min(2k-2n+j)
. The electric limit’s Lagrangian is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{el}}

\displaystyle=c^{l}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{ij}\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m,k\geq 0\\
n+m+k=i\\
2k-2n+j=l\end{subarray}}}\hskip 5.0pt\frac{i!}{n!m!k!}R_{1}^{n}R_{2}^{m}R_{3}^{k}

(47)

\displaystyle=c^{l}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{ij}\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\geq 0\\
2n+m+\frac{l-j}{2}=i\end{subarray}}}\hskip 5.0pt\frac{i!}{n!m!\big(n+\frac{l-j}{2}\big)!}R_{1}^{n}R_{2}^{m}R_{3}^{n+\tfrac{l-j}{2}},

while the magnetic limit’s Lagrangian reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{mag}}

\displaystyle=c^{l+2}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{ij}\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m,k\geq 0\\
n+m+k=i\\
2k-2n+j=l+2\end{subarray}}}\hskip 5.0pt\frac{i!}{n!m!k!}R_{1}^{n}R_{2}^{m}R_{3}^{k}

(48)

\displaystyle=c^{l+2}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{ij}\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\geq 0\\
2n+m+\frac{l+2-j}{2}=i\end{subarray}}}\hskip 5.0pt\frac{i!}{n!m!\big(n+\tfrac{l+2-j}{2}\big)!}R_{1}^{n}R_{2}^{m}R_{3}^{n+\tfrac{l+2-j}{2}}.

As a specific example we now compute the Carrollian limits of the theories
f(R)=R+c^{N}\alpha R^{2}
where
\alpha
is a constant with no
c
dependency. The Carrollian limit of these type of theories was computed in
Tadros and Kolář 2023
. The electric limit can be computed from (
47
) by noticing that all the coefficients
a_{ij}
are zero except
a_{10}=1
and
a_{2N}=\alpha
. In this case
i=1,2
and
j=0,N
.

The term where
i=1
and
j=0
have
l=-2
,
m=0
and
n=1
, while the term where
i=2
and
j=N
has
l=N-4
,
m=0
and
n=2
. Putting all together we get

\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{el}}=c^{-2}R_{1}+2\alpha c^{N-4}R_{1}^{2}.

(49)

In order for the second term to couple to some higher order of the first term, we must set
N\geq 2
otherwise, it will be pure quadratic gravity, i.e., not a modification of GR. This is the same condition derived in
Tadros and Kolář 2023
.
The magnetic limit can be derived from (
48
) by noting that the first term has
i=1
,
j=0
,
l=-2
. Solving
2n+m+\frac{l+2-j}{2}=i
, we get
m=1
and
n=0
. The second term has
i=2
,
j=N
and
l=N-4
. Again, by solving
2n+m+\tfrac{l+2-j}{2}=i
, we get
m=n=1
. Putting all together we obtain

\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{mag}}=R_{2}+2\alpha c^{N-2}R_{1}R_{2},

(50)

with the condition
N\geq 2
to get a GR modification.

For the case where
f(R)
does not depend on
c
explicitly, the electric limit’s Lagrangian is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{el}}

\displaystyle=c^{l}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}g_{i}\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m,k\geq 0\\
n+m+k=i\\
2k-2n=l\end{subarray}}}\hskip 5.0pt\frac{i!}{n!m!k!}R_{1}^{n}R_{2}^{m}R_{3}^{k}

(51)

\displaystyle=c^{l}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}g_{i}\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\geq 0\\
2n+m+\frac{l}{2}=i\end{subarray}}}\hskip 5.0pt\frac{i!}{n!m!\big(\frac{l}{2}+n\big)!}R_{1}^{n}R_{2}^{m}R_{3}^{\frac{l}{2}+n},

and the magnetic limit’s Lagrangian is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{mag}}

\displaystyle=c^{l+2}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}g_{i}\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m,k\geq 0\\
n+m+k=i\\
2k-2n=l+2\end{subarray}}}\hskip 5.0pt\frac{i!}{n!m!k!}R_{1}^{n}R_{2}^{m}R_{3}^{k}

(52)

\displaystyle=c^{l+2}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}g_{i}\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\geq 0\\
2n+m+\frac{l}{2}=i-1\end{subarray}}}\hskip 5.0pt\frac{i!}{n!m!\big(\frac{l}{2}+n+1\big)!}R_{1}^{n}R_{2}^{m}R_{3}^{\tfrac{l}{2}+n+1}.

As consistency check, let us calculate the electric and magnetic limits of GR,
f(R)=R
. In this case
g_{i}=0
except for
g_{1}=1
; thus

\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{el}}=c^{l}\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\geq 0\\
2n+m+\frac{l}{2}=1\end{subarray}}}\hskip 5.0pt\frac{1}{n!m!\big(\frac{l}{2}+n\big)!}R_{1}^{n}R_{2}^{m}R_{3}^{\tfrac{l}{2}+n}.

(53)

Here the minimum value of
2k-2n
is
-2
i.e,
l=-2
(by choosing
k=0,n=1
), and since
2n+m+\frac{l}{2}=1
therefore
m=0
, substituting in the formula we get

\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{el}}=c^{-2}R_{1}.

(54)

The magnetic limit can be derived similarly from

\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{mag}}=c^{l}\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\geq 0\\
2n+m+\frac{l}{2}=0\end{subarray}}}\hskip 5.0pt\frac{1}{n!m!\big(\frac{l}{2}+n+1\big)!}R_{1}^{n}R_{2}^{m}R_{3}^{\tfrac{l}{2}+n+1},

(55)

by solving
2n+m=1
, with in this case
n
must be zero and
m=1
. Thus,

\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{mag}}=R_{2}.

(56)

The
n-
th order in the Carrollian expansion can be derived from the same minimization problem by replacing
l
with
l+2n
.

IV.1.2
Galilean limit

The Galilean limit uses the same formulae except for setting
l=\max(2k-2n+j)
. The LO takes the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{NC}}

\displaystyle=c^{l}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{ij}\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m,k\geq 0\\
n+m+k=i\\
2k-2n+j=l\end{subarray}}}\hskip 5.0pt\frac{i!}{n!m!k!}R_{1}^{n}R_{2}^{m}R_{3}^{k}

(57)

\displaystyle=c^{l}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{ij}\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\geq 0\\
2n+m+\frac{l-j}{2}=i\end{subarray}}}\hskip 5.0pt\frac{i!}{n!m!\big(n+\frac{l-j}{2}\big)!}R_{1}^{n}R_{2}^{m}R_{3}^{n+\frac{l-j}{2}},

which is similar to the Carrollian case but with
l
being the maximum rather than the minimum. The NLO reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{TNC}}=c^{l-2}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{ij}\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\geq 0\\
2n+m+\frac{l-j}{2}=i+1\end{subarray}}}\hskip 5.0pt\frac{i!}{n!m!\big(n-1+\frac{l-j}{2}\big)!}R_{1}^{n}R_{2}^{m}R_{3}^{n-1+\tfrac{l-j}{2}}.

(58)

We will derive the Galilean limit of
f(R)=R+c^{N}\alpha R^{2}
as an example. As mentioned in the previous section, all coefficients
a_{ij}
, except for
a_{10}=1
and
a_{2N}=\alpha
, vanish. The first term in the Galilean limit has
i=1
,
j=0
and
l=2
i.e,
n=0
. Solving
2n+m+\tfrac{l-j}{2}=i
leads to
m=0
. The second term has
i=2
,
j=N
,
l=N+4
i.e,
n=0
. Solving
2n+m+\tfrac{l-j}{2}=i
gives
m=0
. Putting all together, we get

\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{NC}}=c^{2}R_{3}+2\alpha c^{N+4}R_{3}^{2}.

(59)

For this theory to be a modification of the Galilean limit of GR at some order, we must impose the condition
N\leq-2
. Otherwise it would be a theory of pure quadratic gravity.

The first terms in the NLO has
i=1
,
j=0
,
l=2
. Solving
2n+m+\tfrac{l-j}{2}=i+1
, we get
n=0
,
m=1
. The second term has
i=2
,
j=N
and
l=N+4
. Solving
2n+m+\tfrac{l-j}{2}=i+1
, we get
n=0
,
m=1
. Putting all together we get

\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{TNC}}=R_{2}+2\alpha c^{N+2}R_{2}R_{3},

(60)

with the condition
N\leq-2
to get a GR modification. From this condition and the condition for the Carrollian case, we see that no
f(R)
theory with a polynomial function
f
can be a modification of GR in both the Galilean and the Carrollian regimes at the same time.

For the case where
f(R)
does not depend on
c
explicitly, the LO takes the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{NC}}

\displaystyle=c^{l}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}g_{i}\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m,k\geq 0\\
n+m+k=i\\
2k-2n=l\end{subarray}}}\hskip 5.0pt\frac{i!}{n!m!k!}R_{1}^{n}R_{2}^{m}R_{3}^{k}

(61)

\displaystyle=c^{l}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}g_{i}\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\geq 0\\
2n+m+\frac{l}{2}=i\end{subarray}}}\hskip 5.0pt\frac{i!}{n!m!\big(\frac{l}{2}+n\big)!}R_{1}^{n}R_{2}^{m}R_{3}^{\tfrac{l}{2}+n}.

The NLO, however, is a little different, but the general formula can be derived in a similar way to the Carrollian case,

\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{TNC}}=c^{l-2}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}g_{i}\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\geq 0\\
2n+m+\frac{l}{2}=i+1\end{subarray}}}\hskip 5.0pt\frac{i!}{n!m!\big(\frac{l}{2}+n-1\big)!}R_{1}^{n}R_{2}^{m}R_{3}^{\frac{l}{2}+n-1}.

(62)

Let us again check these expressions by applying to GR again. The LO reduces to

\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{NC}}=c^{l}\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\geq 0\\
2n+m+\frac{l}{2}=1\end{subarray}}}\hskip 5.0pt\frac{1}{n!m!\big(\frac{l}{2}+n\big)!}R_{1}^{n}R_{2}^{m}R_{3}^{\tfrac{l}{2}+n},

(63)

where
l=2
. Then by
2n+m+(l/2)=1
, we get
n=m=0
, so the LO is

\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{NC}}=c^{2}R_{3}.

(64)

The NLO can be derived from

\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{TNC}}=c^{l-2}\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\geq 0\\
2n+m+\frac{l}{2}=i+1\end{subarray}}}\hskip 5.0pt\frac{i!}{n!m!\big(\frac{l}{2}+n-1\big)!}R_{1}^{n}R_{2}^{m}R_{3}^{\tfrac{l}{2}+n-1}.

(65)

Here we have to solve
2n+m+1=2
. The solution is
n=0
and
m=1
, and we get

\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{TNC}}=R_{2}.

(66)

The
n-
th order in the Galilean expansion can be derived from the same maximization problem by replacing
l
with
l-2n
.

IV.2
f(g_{\mu\nu},R_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho})
gravity

We first consider gravity theories of the form
f(g_{\mu\nu},R_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho})
where
f
is polynomial. We consider a generic term in the Lagrangian to apply the algorithm, the
n
-th term in the Carrollian or Galilean expansions is the sum of the contributions of all terms. A generic term in the Lagrangian can be written as

\mathcal{L}=\alpha\mathcal{R}^{a}\bar{\mathcal{R}}^{b},

(67)

where
\mathcal{R}
can be the Riemann tensor, the Ricci tensor with all indices down, or the Ricci scalar,
\bar{\mathcal{R}}
can be the same tensors with all indices up, and
a
,
b
are positive numbers such that the Lagrangian terms are scalars. Using eq.(2.8) in
Tadros and Kolář 2023
and raising/lowering the indices, we can write the generic expansions

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{c^{2}}\mathcal{R}_{1}+\mathcal{R}_{2}+c^{2}\mathcal{R}_{3}+c^{4}\mathcal{R}_{4},

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0pt\bar{\mathcal{R}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{c^{4}}\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{1}+\tfrac{1}{c^{2}}\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{2}+\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{3}+c^{2}\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{4}.

(68)

The explicit expressions are in the appendix. Raising the expansions to the powers of
a
,
b
, and
c
, respectively, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{a}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{4}k_{i}=a\\
k_{i}\geq 0\end{subarray}}}\frac{a!}{k_{1}!...k_{4}!}\prod_{t=1}^{4}c^{2k_{t}(t-2)}\big(\mathcal{R}_{t}\big)^{k_{t}},

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0pt\bar{\mathcal{R}}^{b}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{4}k_{j}=b\\
k_{j}\geq 0\end{subarray}}}\frac{b!}{\bar{k}_{1}!...\bar{k}_{4}!}\prod_{\bar{t}=1}^{4}c^{2\bar{k}_{\bar{t}}(\bar{t}-3)}\big(\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{\bar{t}}\big)^{\bar{k}_{\bar{t}}}.

(69)

Assuming
\alpha=c^{N}\alpha^{\prime}
where
\alpha^{\prime}
has no
c
dependency and substituting in the Lagrangian, we find

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\alpha^{\prime}\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{4}k_{i}=a\\
k_{i}\geq 0\end{subarray}}}\frac{a!}{k_{1}!...k_{4}!}\hskip 5.0pt\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{4}\bar{k}_{j}=b\\
k_{j}\geq 0\end{subarray}}}\frac{b!}{\bar{k}_{1}!...\bar{k}_{4}!}\prod_{t=1}^{4}\prod_{\bar{t}=1}^{4}c^{2k_{t}(t-2)+2\bar{k}_{\bar{t}}(\bar{t}-3)+N}(\mathcal{R})^{k_{t}}(\bar{\mathcal{R}})^{\bar{k}_{\bar{t}}}.

(70)

The
n
-th order in the Galilean or the Carrollian limits can be obtained by solving a numerical optimization problem as we will demonstrate below.

IV.2.1
Galilean limit

The LO of the Galilean limit is given by the solution of the optimization problem

z=\max\bigg[\sum_{t=1}^{4}2k_{t}(t-2)+2\sum_{\bar{t}=1}^{4}\bar{k}_{\bar{t}}(\bar{t}-3)+N\bigg],

(71)

subjected to the constraints

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{4}k_{i}=a,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{4}\bar{k}_{j}=b,

\displaystyle k_{i},\bar{k}_{j}\geq 0.

(72)

This is a constrained numerical optimization problem that can be solved by the simplex method
Borgwardt 2012
for
k_{i}
,
\bar{k}_{j}
given that
a
,
b
are known. The solution is
z=4a+4b+2+N
. Thus, for this term to be a modification of GR, the condition on
N
is
N\leq-4a-4b
. If such term exists in the Lagrangian then the Lagrangian is a modification of the Galilean limit of GR. The
n
-th order can be deduced by modifying the constrained optimization problem by
z\to z-2n
.

IV.2.2
Carrollian limit

The LO is given by the solution of the constrained optimization problem

z=\min\bigg[2\sum_{t=1}^{4}k_{t}(t-2)+2\sum_{\bar{t}=1}^{4}\bar{k}_{\bar{t}}(\bar{t}-3)+N\bigg],

(73)

subjected to the constraints

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{4}k_{i}=a,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{4}\bar{k}_{j}=b,

\displaystyle k_{i},\bar{k}_{j}\geq 0,

(74)

which is again solvable by the simplex method given that
a,b
are known. The solution is
z=N-2a-4b-2
. Thus, for the term to be a modification of GR, the condition on
N
must be
N\geq 2a+4b
. If such term exists in the Lagrangian then the Lagrangian is a modification of the Carrollian limit of GR. The
n
-th order is given by the modified optimization problem with
z\to z+2n
.

We can see that the conditions on
N
in the two limits are mutually exclusive. Thus, no such term (of the form (
67
)) with finite
a
,
b
can be a GR modification in the two limits simultaneously. This implies that no Lagrangian of the form
f(g_{\mu\nu},R_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho})
with polynomial
f
can be a modification of GR in both limits simultaneously. In both limits we can recover the results from the previous section if we set
\mathcal{R}=\bar{\mathcal{R}}=R
. We can recover quadratic gravity examples in the previous section by setting
a=2,b=0
or
a=0,b=2
or
a=b=1
.

IV.3
f(g_{\mu\nu},R_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho},\nabla_{\mu})
gravity

Now we consider the most general HDG theory, i.e.,
f(g_{\mu\nu},R_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho},\nabla_{\mu})
, where
f
is polynomial. Following
Biswas et al. 2017
, any such theory can be recast to the form

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{P}_{0}+\sum_{i}\mathcal{P}_{i}\prod_{I}O_{iI}\mathcal{Q}_{iI},

(75)

where
\mathcal{P}_{0}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{iI}
are tensors made up of the Riemann tensor and the metric. The symbols
O_{iI}
denote differential operators that are constructed from contractions of covariant derivatives. Notice that
\mathcal{P}_{0}
is just
f(g_{\mu\nu},R_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho})
which was discussed in the previous subsection. The second term of (
75
) can be rearranged and written in our notation as

\mathcal{L}=\alpha^{\prime}c^{N}\sum_{i=1}^{i_{\textrm{max}}}\mathcal{R}^{a_{i}}\prod_{j=1}^{j_{\textrm{max}}}\nabla^{b_{ij}}\bar{\mathcal{R}}^{c_{ij}},

(76)

where we added a constant with no
c
dependency
\alpha^{\prime}
. Moreover, here
i_{\textrm{max}}
,
j_{\textrm{max}}
,
a_{i}
,
b_{ij}
,
c_{ij}
and
N
are integers. Expanding the terms using (
68
) we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{a_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{4}k_{in}=a_{i}\\
k_{in}\geq 0\end{subarray}}}\frac{a_{i}!}{k_{i1}!...k_{in}!}\prod_{t=1}^{4}c^{2k_{it}(t-2)}(\mathcal{R}_{t})^{k_{it}},

(77)

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{R}}^{c_{ij}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{4}\bar{k}_{ijm}=c_{ij}\\
\bar{k}_{ijm}\geq 0\end{subarray}}}\frac{c_{ij}!}{\bar{k}_{ij1}!...\bar{k}_{ijm}!}\prod_{\bar{t}=1}^{4}c^{2\bar{k}_{ij\bar{t}}(\bar{t}-3)}(\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{\bar{t}})^{\bar{k}_{ij\bar{t}}},

\displaystyle\nabla^{b_{ij}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{ij},\beta_{ij},\gamma_{ij}\geq 0\\
\alpha_{ij}+\beta_{ij}+\gamma_{ij}=b_{ij}\end{subarray}}}c^{-2\alpha_{ij}+2\gamma_{ij}}\frac{b_{ij}!}{\alpha_{ij}!\beta_{ij}!\gamma_{ij}!}\nabla_{1}^{\alpha_{ij}}\nabla_{3}^{\gamma_{ij}}\nabla_{2}^{\beta_{ij}}.

Putting everything together, we arrive at the action

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{i_{\textrm{max}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{a_{i}}k_{in}=a_{i}\\
k_{in}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{j_{\textrm{max}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{4}\bar{k}_{ijm}=c_{ij}\\
\bar{k}_{ijm}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{ij},\beta_{ij},\gamma_{ij}\geq 0\\
\alpha_{ij}+\beta_{ij}+\gamma_{ij}=b_{ij}\end{subarray}}\frac{a_{i}!}{k_{i1}!...k_{in}!}\frac{c_{ij}!}{\bar{k}_{ij1}!...\bar{k}_{ijm}!}\frac{b_{ij}!}{\alpha_{ij}!\beta_{ij}!\gamma_{ij}!}

(78)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\times\nabla_{1}^{\alpha_{ij}}\nabla_{3}^{\gamma_{ij}}\prod_{t=1}^{4}\prod_{\bar{t}=1}^{4}c^{2k_{it}(t-2)+2\bar{k}_{ij\bar{t}}(\bar{t}-3)-2\alpha_{ij}+2\gamma_{ij}+N}(\mathcal{R}_{t})^{k_{it}}\nabla_{2}^{\beta_{ij}}(\mathcal{R}_{\bar{t}})^{\bar{k}_{ij\bar{t}}}.

As before, we will now discuss how the Galilean and Carrollian expansions can be transformed into constrained optimization problems.

IV.3.1
Galilean limit

The LO of the Galilean limit is given by
i_{\textrm{max}}
maximization problems

z_{i}=\max\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{j_{\textrm{max}}}\bigg(2\sum_{t=1}^{4}k_{it}(t-2)+2\sum_{\bar{t}=1}^{4}\bar{k}_{ij\bar{t}}(\bar{t}-3)-2\alpha_{ij}+2\gamma_{ij}+N\bigg)\bigg],

(79)

subjected to the constraints

\begin{gathered}\sum_{n=0}^{4}k_{in}=a_{i},\hskip 10.0pt\sum_{m=0}^{4}\bar{k}_{ijm}=c_{ij},\hskip 10.0pt\alpha_{ij}+\beta_{ij}+\gamma_{ij}=b_{ij},\\
k_{in},\bar{k}_{ijm},\alpha_{ij},\beta_{ij},\gamma_{ij}\geq 0.\end{gathered}

(80)

The
n
-th order in the Galilean expansion is given by similar optimization problems with
z_{i}\to z_{i}-2n
. All such problems are solvable using the simplex method.

IV.3.2
Carrollian limit

The LO of the Carrollian expansion is given by a similar problem to the Galilean one but with minimization instead of maximization i.e,

z_{i}=\min\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{j_{\textrm{max}}}\bigg(2\sum_{t=1}^{4}k_{it}(t-2)+2\sum_{\bar{t}=1}^{4}\bar{k}_{ij\bar{t}}(\bar{t}-3)-2\alpha_{ij}+2\gamma_{ij}+N\bigg)\bigg],

(81)

subjected to the constraints

\begin{gathered}\sum_{n=0}^{4}k_{in}=a_{i},\hskip 10.0pt\sum_{m=0}^{4}\bar{k}_{ijm}=c_{ij},\hskip 10.0pt\alpha_{ij}+\beta_{ij}+\gamma_{ij}=b_{ij},\\
k_{in},\bar{k}_{ijm},\alpha_{ij},\beta_{ij},\gamma_{ij}\geq 0.\end{gathered}

(82)

The
n
-th order is given by similar problem with
z_{i}\to z_{i}+2n
.

For this type of terms to be modifications of GR in both limits in the LO (in the case of a theory with a Lagrangian of the form
R+\mathcal{L}
, where
\mathcal{L}
is the Lagrangian in (
78
)), there must exist
i_{1}
and
i_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\max\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{j_{\textrm{max}}}\bigg(2\sum_{t=1}^{4}k_{i_{1}t}(t-2)+2\sum_{\bar{t}=1}^{4}\bar{k}_{i_{1}j\bar{t}}(\bar{t}-3)-2\alpha_{i_{1}j}+2\gamma_{i_{1}j}\bigg)\bigg]

\displaystyle=2-N,

(83)

\displaystyle\min\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{j_{\textrm{max}}}\bigg(2\sum_{t=1}^{4}k_{i_{2}t}(t-2)+2\sum_{\bar{t}=1}^{4}\bar{k}_{i_{2}j\bar{t}}(\bar{t}-3)-2\alpha_{i_{2}j}+2\gamma_{i_{2}j}\bigg)\bigg]

\displaystyle=-N-2.

If such terms exist, then the Lagrangian is a modification of GR in both limits simultaneously. We have shown that theories of the form (
67
) as well as quadratic gravity do not satisfy these conditions. We will leave the search for such theory (if any exists) to future work.

V
Conclusions

In this paper, we reviewed the methods used to construct non-Lorentzian gravitational theories from Lorentzian ones. We showed that all methods of taking the non-Lorentzian limits lead to the same metric, thus, the same non-Lorentzian theories. In the case of the Galilean expansion, the GAG procedure gives the same Galilean theories as the PNR approach. However, the PNR approach explore more theories from expanding the PNR quantities in powers of
c^{-2}
. Such expansion gives rise to infinite number of theories, one for each order, which are not accounted for in other methods. However, these theories are not Galilean till the NLO. The same Galilean theories can be deduced by performing the ADM decomposition and taking the infinite limit of the norm of the orthogonal vector, the IS approach. A similar situation occurs in the Carrollain expansion, although the ZS approach where the limit of the norm of the orthogonal vector is sent to zero (which give the same Carrollian theories as the Carroll group gauging approach) is computationally easier than the PUL approach. The PUL approach explores a larger space of theories, the Carrollian theories to the NLO coincide with the theories we get from other approaches by truncating the expansions of the PUL quantities setting all higher order fields to zero.

Having established that all approaches give the same metric, one can write general expansions for the curvature tensors. We explored the most general HDG theory with polynomial
f
, introducing an algorithm to calculate the
n
-th order of its Galilean and Carrollian expansions as follows:

1.

We write the Lagrangian and identify which form it takes (is it of the form (
42
), (
67
), or the general (
78
)) and if the Lagrangian depends on
c
explicitly.

2.

We identify the parameters in the respective section of this paper by comparing the given Lagrangian with the expanded one.

3.

We deduce the optimization problem equivalent to the Lagrangian’s Galilean or Carrollian expansion by substituting the parameters from the previous step into the respective optimization problem in the relevant section.

4.

We solve the optimization problem (manually or using a computer) to get the desired order of the expansion.

Higher orders in the Galilean expansion are useful to get more accurate results of dynamical systems in the post Newtonian approximation. Thus, by transforming the problem into computationally easier optimization problems, we can study such systems more efficiently. We leave the analysis of such dynamical systems to future work. On the other hand, although higher orders in the Carrollian expansion have no utility at the moment, some may be discovered in the near future given the increasing interest in Carrollian physics, and Carrollian gravity in particular, and having an algorithm to compute such higher order will be beneficial then.

Another interesting future direction of research is to search for gravity theories which are viable modifications of GR in both Carrollian, and Galilean limits at the same time i.e, satisfies the conditions (
83
). If such a theory exists, it is interesting to see what significance it has. Is there a defining property of the theory that allows this? If so, how does it impact its solutions. It is also interesting to apply the algorithm introduced to study its Galilean and Carrollian limits, and see how the GR black holes get modified.

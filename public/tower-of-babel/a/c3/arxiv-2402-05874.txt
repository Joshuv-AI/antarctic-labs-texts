Complexity of graph-state preparation by Clifford circuits
Soh Kumabe, Ryuhei Mori, Yusei Yoshimura

Abstract
In this work, we study the complexity of graph-state preparation. We consider general quantum algorithms consisting of Clifford operations acting on at most two qubits for graph-state preparations. We define the CZ-complexity of a graph state | G ⟩ \left|{G}\right\rangle as the minimum number of two-qubit Clifford operations (excluding single-qubit Clifford operations) for generating | G ⟩ \left|{G}\right\rangle from the trivial state | 0 ⟩ ⊗ n \left|{0}\right\rangle^{\otimes n} . We first prove that a graph state | G ⟩ \left|{G}\right\rangle can be generated by at most t t two-qubit Clifford operations if and only if | G ⟩ \left|{G}\right\rangle can be generated by at most t t controlled-Z (CZ) operations. We next prove that a graph state | G ⟩ \left|{G}\right\rangle is generated from another graph state | H ⟩ \left|{H}\right\rangle by t t CZ operations if and only if the graph G G is generated from H H by some combinatorial graph transformation with cost t t . As the main results, we show a connection between the CZ-complexity of graph state | G ⟩ \left|{G}\right\rangle and the rank-width of the graph G G . Indeed, we prove that for any graph G G with n n vertices and rank-width r r , 1. The CZ-complexity of | G ⟩ \left|{G}\right\rangle is O ⁡ ( r ​ n ) O(rn) . 2. If G G is connected, the CZ-complexity of | G ⟩ \left|{G}\right\rangle is at least n + r − 2 n+r-2 . We also demonstrate the existence of graph states whose CZ-complexities are close to the upper and lower bounds. Finally, we present quantum algorithms for preparing graph states for three specific graph classes related to intervals: interval graphs, interval containment graphs, and circle graphs. We prove that the CZ-complexity is O ⁡ ( n ) O(n) for interval graphs, and O ⁡ ( n ​ log ⁡ n ) O(n\log n) for both interval containment graphs and circle graphs.

Introduction

The set of stabilizer states is a large class of quantum states used in quantum information processing.
The set of graph states is a subclass of stabilizer states represented by an undirected graph.
Any stabilizer state can be transformed into a graph state using local Clifford operations
[
PhysRevA.69.022316
]
.
Hence, it is important to develop efficient quantum algorithms for preparing graph states since
any stabilizer state can be transformed from some graph state by local Clifford operations.
Several properties of graph states have been studied so far
[
PhysRevLett.91.107903
,
PhysRevA.68.022312
,
PhysRevA.69.062311
,
PhysRevA.69.022316
,
PhysRevA.77.042307
,
PhysRevA.83.042314
,
dahlberg2018
,
Adcock2020mappinggraphstate
]
.

In general, any stabilizer state can be generated from
\left|{0}\right\rangle^{\otimes n}
by an
n
-qubit Clifford operation.
Any
n
-qubit Clifford operation is generated by single-qubit and two-qubit Clifford operations.
For example, the commonly used generators of the Clifford group are the
S
,
H
and
\mathrm{CZ}
gates.
In this work, we study the complexity of graph-state preparation.
Although there are several implementations of quantum computing, e.g., superconductors, trapped-ion, etc.,
we are interested in complexity measures that capture universal difficulties in graph-state preparation.
The implementation of two-qubit operations is generally challenging.
Therefore, we define the complexity of graph states as follows.

Definition 1

(The CZ-complexity of graph states)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a quantum algorithm
that consists of the following operations.

1.

Measurements of qubits in the computational basis.

2.

Single-qubit Clifford operations.

3.

Two-qubit Clifford operations.

Here, we assume that measured qubits are discarded in quantum algorithms.
After the measurements of some qubits, the algorithm may choose subsequent operations depending on the measurement outcomes.
The
\mathrm{CZ}
-complexity of
\mathcal{A}
is defined as the maximum number (with respect to the measurement outcome) of two-qubit Clifford operations in
\mathcal{A}
.
The
\mathrm{CZ}
-complexity
\Gamma_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\left|{G}\right\rangle)
of graph state
\left|{G}\right\rangle
is defined as the minimum CZ-complexity of
\mathcal{A}

that generates the
n
-qubit graph state
\left|{G}\right\rangle
from
\left|{0}\right\rangle^{\otimes(n+s)}
with probability 1 for some
s\geq 0
.

There is a simple quantum algorithm generating an
n
-qubit graph state
\left|{G}\right\rangle
.
First,
\left|{+}\right\rangle^{\otimes n}
is prepared, which corresponds to the graph state for the empty graph of
n
vertices.
Here, each qubit corresponds to each vertex of
G
.
Then, for each edge of
G
, the controlled-Z (CZ) operation is applied for the corresponding pair of qubits.
This quantum algorithm generates
\left|{G}\right\rangle
with
m
two-qubit Clifford operations where
m
is the number of edges in
G
.

Van den Nest, Dehaene, and De Moor proved that two graph states
\left|{G}\right\rangle
and
\left|{H}\right\rangle
can be transformed
to each other by local Clifford operations if and only if
G
and
H
can be transformed to each other
by some combinatorial graph transformations, called local complementations
[
PhysRevA.69.022316
]
.
The local complementation for a vertex
v
is an operation that complements edges for all pairs in neighborhoods of
v
.
The local complementation can reduce the number of edges in graphs in some cases.
For example, the complete graph
K_{n}
can be transformed to the star graph
S_{n-1}
by a local complementation.
This implies that
\left|{K_{n}}\right\rangle
is generated from
\left|{S_{n-1}}\right\rangle
by local Clifford operations.
Since
S_{n-1}
has
n-1
edges,
\left|{S_{n-1}}\right\rangle
can be generated with
n-1
two-qubit Clifford operations.
Hence, the CZ-complexity of
\left|{K_{n}}\right\rangle
is at most
n-1

(Indeed, this is a lower bound for any connected graph as well).
This example shows that local Clifford operations are useful for reducing the number of two-qubit Clifford operations.
As another important example, Figure
1
shows that the cycle graph
C_{4}
of size four can be transformed to the path graph
P_{4}
of size four by the local complementations.
Note that we do not allow the use of local non-Clifford operations in Definition
1

although they are generally stronger than local Clifford operations
[
10.5555/2011438.2011446
]
.

Figure 1
:
C_{4}
and
P_{4}
are local-complement equivalent. When the local complementations are applied for the filled vertices,
C_{4}
is transformed to
P_{4}
.

Figure 2
:
Graphs that have the minimum number of edges among all graphs obtained by local complementations, and include cycles of size three or four.

Based on the above observations, we can consider a general strategy for designing low CZ-complexity quantum algorithms.
For a generation of
\left|{G}\right\rangle
, find a graph
H
that has the minimum number of edges among all graphs
that can be transformed to
G
by local complementations.
Then,
\left|{H}\right\rangle
can be generated with
m
two-qubit operations where
m
is the number of edges of
H
.
Finally,
\left|{G}\right\rangle
can be generated from
\left|{H}\right\rangle
by a local Clifford operation.
The CZ-complexities of the algorithms based on this strategy were calculated for all graph states up to 12 qubits in
[
PhysRevA.83.042314
]
.
In quantum algorithms of this type, the CZ operations are applied for
\left|{+}\right\rangle^{\otimes n}
first, and then
a local Clifford operation is applied.

In fact, this simple strategy is not necessarily optimal.
The two graphs in Figure
2
each have six edges, which is the minimum number possible under local-complementations
[
Adcock2020mappinggraphstate
]
.
However, these graphs can be generated with five
\mathrm{CZ}
operations since cycles of size three and four can be generated with two and three
\mathrm{CZ}
operations, respectively.

The class of quantum algorithms in Definition
1
is more general than quantum algorithms from the above strategy since any two-qubit Clifford operations rather than the CZ operation are allowed, and single-qubit Clifford operations and two-qubit Clifford operations can be applied in arbitrary order.
We first prove that, without loss of generality, we can assume that all two-qubit Clifford operations in graph-state preparation algorithms are
\mathrm{CZ}
operations, thus justifying the terminology of CZ-complexity introduced in Definition
1
.

Theorem 2
.

For any graph
G
, there exists a quantum algorithm
\mathcal{A}
generating
\left|{G}\right\rangle

of the type in Definition
1
with CZ-complexity
\Gamma_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\left|{G}\right\rangle)

where all two-qubit Clifford operations in

are the
\mathrm{CZ}
operations.

For a graph state
\left|{G}\right\rangle
, the
\mathrm{CZ}
operation corresponds to the edge complementation on
G
.
However, in the framework of quantum algorithms in Definition
1
,
\mathrm{CZ}
operations can be performed on a stabilizer state which may not be a graph state.
We prove a combinatorial characterization of the graph state transformation with positive CZ-complexity.

Theorem 3
.

For any graphs
G
and
H
,
\left|{G}\right\rangle
is generated from
\left|{H}\right\rangle
with CZ-complexity
t

if and only if
G
is transformed from
H
with some combinatorial transformation of graphs with cost
t
, which is defined in Definition
.

This combinatorial characterization can be regarded as a generalization of the combinatorial characterization for
t=0

[
PhysRevA.69.022316
,
dahlberg2018
]
.
Theorem
3
is derived from the graphical description of
\mathrm{CZ}
operations on stabilizer states
[
PhysRevA.77.042307
]
.

It is known that the asymptotic CZ-complexity is
\Theta(n^{2}/\log n)

[
markov2008optimal
]
.
For better upper bounds, we consider graphs with bounded rank-width.
The rank-width is a complexity measure of graphs introduced by Oum and Seymour
[
OUM2006514
,
OUM201715
]
.
We derived the upper and lower bounds of the CZ-complexity using the rank-width.

Theorem 4
.

Let
G
be a graph with
n
vertices and rank-width at most
r\geq 1
.
If
r
is odd, for any
n\geq\frac{13r^{2}-6r-3}{4r}
,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\left|{G}\right\rangle)\leq\frac{5r^{2}-1}{4r}n-\frac{221r^{4}-180r^{3}+10r^{2}+36r+9}{96r^{2}}.

If
r
is even, for any
n\geq\frac{13r-6}{4}
,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\left|{G}\right\rangle)\leq\frac{5r}{4}n-\frac{221r^{2}-180r+100}{96}.

For the case
r=1
, this upper bound is optimal for connected graphs.

Corollary 5
.

For any graph
G
with
n
vertices and rank-width 1,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\left|{G}\right\rangle)\leq n-1.

Since
n-1
is a trivial lower bound on the
\mathrm{CZ}
-complexity for any connected graph
G
, Corollary
5
implies that
\Gamma_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\left|{G}\right\rangle)=n-1
for any connected graph
G
with rank-width 1.
The upper bound
O(rn)
is nearly optimal, as demonstrated by the following lemma derived from information-theoretic counting arguments.

Lemma 6
.

There exists a positive constant
c
such that
for any positive integer
n
and
r\leq\lceil n/3\rceil
,
there exists a graph
G
with
n
vertices and rank-width at most
r
such that
\Gamma_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\left|{G}\right\rangle)\geq crn/\log n
.

The lower bound derived from the rank-width is formulated as follows.

Theorem 7
.

For any connected graph
G
with
n\geq 2
vertices and rank-width at least
r
,
\Gamma_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\left|{G}\right\rangle)\geq n+r-2
.

The cycle graph
C_{n}
with
n
vertices and
n
edges has rank-width two when
n\geq 5
.
Theorem
7
implies that
\Gamma_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\left|{C_{n}}\right\rangle)=n
when
n\geq 5
.
This is in contrast to the fact
\Gamma_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\left|{C_{n}}\right\rangle)=n-1
for
n=3,4
.
From the analysis of the rank-width of random sparse graphs
[
https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.20620
]
, the following lemma is obtained.

Lemma 8
.

There exists a constant
c\geq 1
such that
for any
r^{\prime}
there exist
n^{\prime}
and
r\geq r^{\prime}

such that for any
n\geq n^{\prime}
,
there exists a connected graph
G
with
n
vertices and rank-width
r
satisfying
\Gamma_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\left|{G}\right\rangle)\leq n-1+c(r-1)
.

This lemma implies that the lower bound
\Gamma_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\left|{G}\right\rangle)-(n-1)\geq r-1
for a connected graph
G
obtained from Theorem
7
is optimal up to a constant factor.

It is natural to ask whether the rank-width is an appropriate choice of the complexity measure of graphs for bounding the CZ-complexity of graph states.
A similar upper bound to Theorem
4
may be obtained from other complexity measures satisfying some properties required for the proof of Theorem
4
.
On the other hand, for the proof of Theorem
7
, the invariance under the local complementations is required for the complexity measure.
Deriving lower bounds for the CZ-complexity from other graph complexity measures is challenging due to this highly restrictive requirement, despite the upper and lower bounds in Theorems
4
and
7
not being tight.

Finally, we present efficient quantum algorithms for special classes of graphs with unbounded rank-widths.

Theorem 9
.

For any interval graph
G
with
n
vertices,
\Gamma_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\left|{G}\right\rangle)\leq 2n-2
.
For any circle graph and interval containment graph
G
with
n
vertices,
\Gamma_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\left|{G}\right\rangle)\leq 1.262\cdot n\log n+O(n)
.

Recently, Davies and Jena proved that for any circle graph
G
with
n
vertices and the chromatic number
k

\Gamma_{\mathrm{CZ}}(\left|{G}\right\rangle)\leq 2n\lceil\log k\rceil

[
davies2025preparing
,
jena2024graph
]
.
The class of circle graphs plays a key role within vertex-minor theory, analogous to the role of planar graphs within graph minor theory
[
KIM202454
]
.

(Pauli group)

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle:=\bigl\{\alpha h_{1}\otimes h_{2}\otimes\dotsm\otimes h_{n}\mid\alpha\in\{\pm 1,\pm i\},\quad h_{j}\in\{I_{2},X,Y,Z\}\ \forall j\in\{1,2,\dotsc,n\}\bigr\}.

Definition 11

(Stabilizer state)

S

.

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{n}^{\pm}

\displaystyle:=\bigl\{\alpha h_{1}\otimes h_{2}\otimes\dotsm\otimes h_{n}\mid\alpha\in\{\pm 1\},\quad h_{j}\in\{I_{2},X,Y,Z\}\ \forall j\in\{1,2,\dotsc,n\}\bigr\}.

3
Graph state and combinatorial characterizations of local Clifford operations and measurements

For a finite set
V
, let
\binom{V}{2}
denote a family of subsets of
V
of size exactly two.
For a finite set
V
, and
E\subseteq\binom{V}{2}
, a pair
(V,E)
is called a
graph
.
For a graph
G=(V,E)
,
V(G):=V
and
E(G):=E
are called
vertex set
and
edge set
, respectively.
The
size of graph

G
is defined as
|V(G)|
.
For a graph
G
and
v\in V(G)
,
the
neighborhood of
v
in
G
is defined as
N_{G}(v):=\{w\in V\mid\{v,w\}\in E\}
.

Definition 16

(Graph state)
.

For a graph
G=(V=\{1,2,\dotsc,n\},E)
, a
graph state

\left|{G}\right\rangle
is a stabilizer state defined by stabilizer generators

\displaystyle X_{v}\otimes\bigotimes_{w\in N_{G}(v)}Z_{w},\qquad v\in V.

Here, for any
2\times 2
matrix
U
,
U_{v}:=g_{1}\otimes\dotsm\otimes g_{n}
where
g_{v}=U
and
g_{w}=I_{2}
for
w\in V\setminus\{v\}
.

The generating matrix of
\left|{G}\right\rangle
is

\begin{bmatrix}I\mid A\end{bmatrix}

where
A\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n\times n}
is the
adjacency matrix
of
G
, i.e.,
A_{v,w}=1
if and only if
\{v,w\}\in E(G)
.

In Definition
1
, the local Clifford operations are free to use.
Hence, the two stabilizer states that are equivalent up to the local Clifford operations can be identified in our analysis.

Definition 17

(Local Clifford equivalence)
.

Stabilizer states
\left|{\psi}\right\rangle
and
\left|{\varphi}\right\rangle
are
local-Clifford (LC) equivalent
if
there exists a local Clifford operator
C\in\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\otimes n}
such that
C\left|{\psi}\right\rangle=\left|{\varphi}\right\rangle
.

The following lemma implies that it is sufficient to prepare graph states for preparing general stabilizer states.

Lemma 18

(
[
PhysRevA.69.022316
]
)
.

For any stabilizer state
\left|{\psi}\right\rangle
, there exists a graph
G
such that
\left|{\psi}\right\rangle
and
\left|{G}\right\rangle
are LC-equivalent.

For the combinatorial characterization of LC-equivalence of two graph states, the local complementation is defined as follows.

Definition 19

(Local complementation)
.

The
local complementation
is an operation on a graph.
For a graph
G=(V,E)
, and a vertex
v\in V
,
the local complementation
\tau_{v}(G)=(V,E^{\prime})
is defined as

\displaystyle E^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\left\{\{u,w\}\in\binom{V}{2}\mid((u\notin N_{G}(v)\lor w\notin N_{G}(v))\land\{u,w\}\in E)\lor(u\in N_{G}(v)\land w\in N_{G}(v)\land\{u,w\}\notin E)\right\}.

Two graphs
G
and
H
are said to be
local-complement equivalent
if
G
and
H
can be transformed to each other by a sequence of local complementations.

Lemma 20

(Local Clifford equivalence of graph states
[
PhysRevA.69.022316
]
)
.

For any graphs
G
and
H
,
\left|{G}\right\rangle
and
\left|{H}\right\rangle
are local-Clifford equivalent if and only if

G
and
H
are local-complement equivalent.

Lemma
20
provides a combinatorial characterization of local Clifford operations on graph states.
Next, we consider a combinatorial characterization of measurements.
In quantum algorithms in Definition
1
, general stabilizer states, which are not necessarily graph states, may be measured in the computational basis.
Since any stabilizer state is LC-equivalent to some graph state,
it is sufficient to consider Pauli measurements for graph states.

Lemma 21

(Pauli measurements for graph states
[
PhysRevA.69.062311
]
)
.

For any graph
G
, let
\left|{G^{+}}\right\rangle
and
\left|{G^{-}}\right\rangle
be two possible stabilizer states obtained by a Pauli measurement, i.e., in the eigenbasis of
X
,
Y
or
Z
, of a qubit in
\left|{G}\right\rangle
.
Then,
\left|{G^{+}}\right\rangle
and
\left|{G^{-}}\right\rangle
are LC-equivalent to some common graph state
\left|{H}\right\rangle
.
Here,
H
is obtained from
G
by some local complementations and deletion of the vertex that corresponds to the measured qubit.

Lemma
21
implies that a Pauli measurement and an adaptive local Clifford operation for a graph state
\left|{G}\right\rangle
give some graph state
\left|{H}\right\rangle
deterministically.
Furthermore, the graph
H
is obtained by local complementation and a vertex deletion for
G
.
This observation implies the following lemma.

Definition 22

(Vertex minor
[
BOUCHET198858
,
dahlberg2018
]
)
.

A graph
H
is a
vertex minor
of graph
G
if
H
is obtained from
G
by local complementations and vertex deletions.

Lemma 23

(
[
dahlberg2018
,
PhysRevA.69.062311
,
BOUCHET198858
]
)
.

For any graphs
G
and
H
,
\left|{H}\right\rangle
is obtained from
\left|{G}\right\rangle
with probability 1 by a local Clifford operation followed by measurement of qubits in the computational basis and an adaptive local Clifford operation if and only if

H
is a vertex-minor of
G
.

Dahlberg, Helsen, and Wehner showed that it is NP-hard to decide whether
G
is a vertex-minor of
H
for given graphs
G
and
H

[
Dahlberg_2020
]
.

4
Sufficiency of the
\mathrm{CZ}
operators: Proof of Theorem
2

In this section, we prove Theorem
2
.
We first prove that any two-qubit Clifford gate can be replaced by single-qubit gates, at most one
\mathrm{CZ}
gate and at most one SWAP gate.

Lemma 24
.

Any two-qubit Clifford operator is represented by a quantum circuit consisting of arbitrary number of single-qubit Clifford gates, at most one
\mathrm{CZ}
gate and at most one SWAP gate.

Proof.

From Fact
15
, for a given two-qubit Clifford operator
\mathcal{C}
, it is sufficient to show the existence of the quantum circuit
\mathcal{D}
satisfying
L_{\mathcal{C}}=L_{\mathcal{D}}
and the conditions of the lemma.
Since the
S
,
H
and
\mathrm{CZ}
gates generate the Clifford group, any two-qubit Clifford operator is represented by a Clifford circuit consisting of these gates.
When the Clifford circuit includes more than one
\mathrm{CZ}
gates, we can reduce the number of
\mathrm{CZ}
gates by the following procedure.
There are six possible single-qubit Clifford gates (up to succeeding Pauli gates) in (
2
).
Since
S
commutes with
\mathrm{CZ}
, we can assume without loss of generality that single-qubit gates between two specific
\mathrm{CZ}
gates are all Hadamard gates.
In the two possible cases, the number of
\mathrm{CZ}
gates can be reduced by using the SWAP gate as follows.

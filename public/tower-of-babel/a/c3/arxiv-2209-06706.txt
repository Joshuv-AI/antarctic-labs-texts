A Rigidity Result for the Robin Torsion Problem
Alba Lia Masiello, Gloria Paoli*

Abstract
Let Ω ⊂ ℝ 2 \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2} be an open, bounded and Lipschitz set. We consider the torsion problem for the Laplace operator associated to Ω \Omega with Robin boundary conditions. In this setting, we study the equality case in the Talenti-type comparison, proved in [ 5 ] . We prove that the equality is achieved only if Ω \Omega is a disk and the torsion function u u is radial. Keywords: Robin boundary conditions, Laplace operator, rigidity result, torsion problem, Talenti comparison. MSC 2020: 35J05, 35J25,46E30.

1
Introduction

Let
\beta>0
and let
\Om\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an open, bounded and Lipschitz set. We consider the following problem for the Laplace operator:

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=1&\text{in }\Om\\
\displaystyle{\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\beta u=0}&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1)

where
\nu
is the outer unit normal to
\partial\Omega
.
A function
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
is a weak solution to (
1
) if

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla\varphi\,dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}u\varphi\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}=\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,dx,\quad\forall\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega).

(2)

Classical arguments, see e.g
[
13
]
, ensure that there exists a positive and unique weak solution to (
1
), that we denote by
u
. So, we can define the Robin torsional rigidity of
\Omega
as the
L^{1}-
norm of
u
:

T(\Om):=\int_{\Om}u\,dx,

or, equivalently, as the maximum of the following Rayleigh quotient:

T(\Omega)=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)\\
\varphi\not\equiv 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\displaystyle{\left(\int_{\Omega}{\left|\varphi(x)\right|}\,dx\right)^{2}}}{\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla\varphi(x)\right|}^{2}\,dx}+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}\varphi^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}}.

In
[
10
]
the authors prove that the Robin torsional rigidity is maximum on balls among bounded and Lipschitz sets of fixed Lebesgue measure and the proof of this Saint-Venant type inequality relays on reflection arguments (see also
[
11
]
).

In the recent paper
[
5
]
, the authors obtain the same result using symmetrization techniques.
They establish a Talenti-type comparison result between suitable Lorentz norms of the solution to the following problems:

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=f\,&\text{in }\Om,\\
\displaystyle{\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\beta u=0}&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}\quad\qquad\quad\begin{cases}-\Delta v=f^{\sharp}\,&\text{in }\Om^{\sharp},\\
\displaystyle{\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}+\beta v=0}&\text{on }\partial\Omega^{\sharp},\end{cases}

where
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
f^{\sharp}
is the
Schwartz rearrangement
of
f
(see Definition
2.3
) and
\Omega^{\sharp}
is the ball centered at the origin having the same measure as
\Om
. Moreover, in the case
f\equiv 1
, they obtain the following comparison result in any dimension

{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{p}(\Om)}\leq{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{p}(\Om^{\sharp})},\,\,\quad p=1,2.

(3)

We observe that, for
p=1
, inequality (
3
) is exactly the Saint-Venant inequality proved in
[
10
]
.
It is still an open problem to establish if, for
p\in(1,+\infty)
, the ball maximizes the
L^{p}
norm of the torsion function among open, bounded and Lipschitz sets (see
[
11
, Open Problem
1
]
). A first evidence in this direction is provided in
[
19
]
, where it is proved that the ball is a critical shape for every
L^{p}
norm in dimension
n>2
.

On the other hand, in the case
n=2
, the Open Problem
1
contained in
[
11
]
is solved in
[
5
]
in the following stronger version:

u^{\sharp}(x)\leq v(x)\quad\forall x\in\Omega^{\sharp},

(4)

where
u^{\sharp}
is the Schwartz rearrangement of the solution to (
1
) and
v
is the solution to

\begin{cases}-\Delta v=1&\text{in }\Om^{\sharp}\\
\displaystyle{\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}+\beta v=0}&\text{on }\partial\Om^{\sharp}.\end{cases}

(5)

This kind of results in the Robin boundary setting was generalized to nonlinear case in
[
6
]
, to anisotropic case in
[
18
]
, with mixed boundary conditions in
[
1
]
, in the case of the Hermite operator in
[
12
]
and for Riemannian manifolds in
[
14
]
.

The aim of the present paper is to characterize the equality case in (
4
), indeed we prove that the Talenti-type comparison is rigid in the planar case.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Om\subset\R^{2}
be an open, bounded and Lipschitz set and let
\Om^{\sharp}
be the ball centered at the origin and having the same measure as
\Om
. Let
u
be the solution to (
1
) and let
v
be the solution to (
5
). If
u^{\sharp}(x)=v(x)
for all
x\in\Om^{\sharp}
, then

\Om=\Om^{\sharp}+x_{0},\quad u(\cdot+x_{0})=u^{\sharp}(\cdot).

Moreover, we have the following extension of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Om\subset\R^{2}
be an open, bounded and Lipschitz set and let
\Om^{\sharp}
be the ball centered at the origin and having the same measure as
\Om
. Let
u
be the solution to (
1
) and let
v
be the solution to (
5
). We denote by
R
the radius of
\Omega^{\sharp}
.

If
\displaystyle{\min_{\Omega}u=\min_{\Omega^{\sharp}}v}
and if there exists
r\in]0,R[
such that
u^{\sharp}(x)=v(x)
for
{\left|x\right|}=r
, then

\Om=\Om^{\sharp}+x_{0},\quad u(\cdot+x_{0})=u^{\sharp}(\cdot)\,\,\text{in \;}\Omega.

The idea of the proof is the following.
Starting from the proof in
[
5
]
of the pointwise comparison (
4
), we show that the equality
u^{\sharp}=v
implies that the level sets of
u
are balls on the boundary of which the normal derivative of
u
is constant. Then, we prove that these balls are concentric, using an argument inspired by
[
9
]
(see also
[
20
, Lemma 6]
). In the Robin case, the main difficulty is that, contrary to the Dirichlet case, the level sets of the solution may touch the boundary of
\Omega
.

As far as the Dirichlet boundary conditions, the starting point for the study of these kinds of problems is the paper by Talenti
[
21
]
, in which a pointwise comparison is stated between the solution to the following problems:

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{D}=f\,&\text{in }\Om,\\
u_{D}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}\quad\qquad\begin{cases}-\Delta v_{D}=f^{\sharp}\,&\text{in }\Om^{\sharp},\\
v_{D}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega^{\sharp},\end{cases}

whenever

f\in L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(\Omega)
. In particular, he proves in
[
21
]
the pointwise inequality:

u_{D}^{\sharp}(x)\leq v_{D}(x)\quad\forall x\in\Omega^{\sharp}

(6)

and, consequently, by integration, the Saint-Venant inequality in the Dirichlet case holds:

\int_{\Omega}u_{D}\;dx=\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}u^{\sharp}_{D}\;dx\leq\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}v_{D}\;dx,

conjectured by Saint-Venant in 1856.
Moreover, a previous result in this direction is due to Weinberger, that proved in
[
24
]
the following result:

\max_{\Omega}u_{D}\leq\max_{\Om^{\sharp}}v_{D}.

We stress that, in the case of Dirichlet boundary conditions, the rigidity result holds and it is proved in
[
3
]
(see Remark
3.1
for the main differences to the Robin case).

Finally, we conclude by a list of generalization of Talenti’s comparison results in different setting with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Extension to the semilinear and nonlinear
elliptic case can be found, for instance, in
[
23
]
, to the anisotropic elliptic operators in
[
2
]
, to the parabolic case in
[
4
]
and to
higher order operators in
[
8
,
22
]
.
We also refer the reader to
[
15
,
16
]
and the references therein for a survey on Talenti’s techniques.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall some basic notions about rearrangements of functions and we recall some properties of the Torsion function, while Section
3
is dedicated to the proof of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
and to a list of open problems.

2
Notation and preliminaries

Throughout this article,
|\cdot|
will denote the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
while
\cdot
is the standard Euclidean scalar product. By
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\cdot)
, we denote the
1-
dimensional Hausdorff measure in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The perimeter of
\Omega
will be denoted by
P(\Omega)
and since
\Omega
is a bounded, open and Lipschitz set, we have that
P(\Omega)=\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial\Omega)
. Moreover, we denote by
|\Omega|
the Lebesgue measure of
\Omega
.

If
\Omega
is an open and Lipschitz set, it holds the following coarea formula. Some references for results relative to the sets of finite perimeter and the coarea formula are, for instance,
[
17
,
7
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Coarea formula)
.

Let
f:\Omega\to\R
be a Lipschitz function and let
u:\Omega\to\R
be a measurable function. Then,

{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u|\nabla f(x)|dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}dt\int_{(\Omega\cap f^{-1}(t))}u(y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}(y)}.

(7)

We recall now some basic definitions and results about rearrangements and we refer to
[
16
]
for a general overview.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
u:\Omega\to\R
be a measurable function, the
distribution function
of
u
is the function
\mu:[0,+\infty[\,\to[0,+\infty[
defined by

\mu(t)={\left|\Set{x\in\Omega\,:\,{\left|u(x)\right|}>t}\right|}.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
u:\Omega\to\R
be a measurable function, the
decreasing rearrangement
of
u
, denoted by
u^{\ast}
, is the distribution function of
\mu
.

Remark 2.1
.

We observe that the function
\mu(\cdot)
is decreasing and right continuous and the function
u^{\ast}(\cdot)
is the generalized inverse of the function
\mu(\cdot)
.

Definition 2.3
.

The
Schwartz rearrangement
of
u
is the function
u^{\sharp}
whose level sets are balls with the same measure as the level sets of
u
.

We have the following relation between
u^{\sharp}
and
u^{*}
:

u^{\sharp}(x)=u^{*}(\pi{\left|x\right|}^{2})

and it can be easily checked that the functions
u
,
u^{*}
e
u^{\sharp}
are equi-distributed, so we have that

\displaystyle{{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}={\left\|u^{*}\right\|}_{L^{p}(0,{\left|\Omega\right|})}=\lVert{u^{\sharp}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\sharp})}}.

Let now
u
be the solution to (
1
). For
t\geq 0
, we introduce the following notations:

U_{t}=\left\{x\in\Omega:u(x)>t\right\}\quad\partial U_{t}^{int}=\partial U_{t}\cap\Omega,\quad\partial U_{t}^{ext}=\partial U_{t}\cap\partial\Omega,\quad\mu(t)={\left|U_{t}\right|}

and, if
v
is the solution to (
5
), using the same notations as above, we set

V_{t}=\left\{x\in\Omega^{\sharp}:v(x)>t\right\},\quad\partial V_{t}^{int}=\partial V_{t}\cap\Omega,\quad\partial V_{t}^{ext}=\partial V_{t}\cap\partial\Omega,\quad\phi(t)={\left|V_{t}\right|}.

Because of the invariance of the Laplacian under rotation, we have that
v
is radial.
Moreover, we observe that the solutions
u
to (
1
) and
v
to (
5
) are both superharmonic and so, by the strong maximum principle, it follows that they achieve their minima on the boundary.

From now on, we denote by

u_{m}=\min_{\Omega}u,\quad\quad v_{m}=\min_{\Omega^{\sharp}}v,

(8)

u_{M}=\max_{\Omega}u,\quad\quad v_{M}=\max_{\Omega^{\sharp}}v.

(9)

Since we are assuming that the Robin boundary parameter
\beta
is strictly positive,
we have that
u_{m}>0
and
v_{m}>0
. Hence,
u
and
v
are strictly positive in the interior of
\Omega
.

Since
v
is radial, positive and decreasing along the radius then, for
0\leq t\leq v_{m}
,

V_{t}=\Omega^{\sharp},

while, for
v_{m}<t<v_{M}
, we have that
V_{t}
is a ball concentric to
\Omega^{\sharp}
and strictly contained in it.

In the next remarks, we collect some general and useful results.

Remark 2.2
.

By the weak formulation (
2
) and the isoperimetric inequality, we have that

\begin{split}v_{m}\text{P}(\Omega^{\sharp})&=\int_{\partial\Omega^{\sharp}}v(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}=\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}\,dx=\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{\Omega}\,dx\\
&=\int_{\partial\Omega}u(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\geq u_{m}\text{P}(\Omega)\geq u_{m}\text{P}(\Omega^{\sharp}),\end{split}

and, as a consequence,

u_{m}\leq v_{m}.

(10)

Moreover, from (
10
) follows that

\mu(t)\leq\phi(t)={\left|\Omega\right|}\quad\forall t\leq v_{m}.

(11)

Remark 2.3
.

We observe that
\phi
, the distribution function of
v
, is absolutely continuous. Indeed, in
[
9
, Lemma 2.3]
, is proved that the absolutely continuity of
\phi
is equivalent to the following condition:

{\left|\Set{\nabla v=0}\cap v^{-1}(v_{m},v_{M})\right|}=0

(12)

which is verified by
v
, as its gradient never vanishes on the level sets
V_{t}
.

The starting point of the proof of our main results is the following Lemma, proved in
[

]
. For the convenience of exposition, we report here the proof.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
u
be a solution to (

) and let

be a solution to (

, we have

4\pi\leq\left(-\mu^{\prime}(t)+\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{\partial U_{t}^{ext}}\frac{1}{u}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\right)

(13)

and

4\pi=\left(-\phi^{\prime}(t)+\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{\partial V_{t}^{ext}}\frac{1}{v}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\right).

Let

and

. Let us choose the following test function in the weak formulation (

\left.\varphi(x)=\right.\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }u<t\\

\begin{split}\int_{U_{t}\setminus U_{t+h}}{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{2}\,dx&+\beta h\int_{\partial U_{t+h}^{ext}}u\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}+\beta\int_{\partial U_{t}^{ext}\setminus\partial U_{t+h}^{ext}}u(u-t)\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\\

(15)

Dividing (

) and letting

\left.g(x)=\right.\begin{cases}{\left|\nabla u\right|}&\text{ if }x\in\partial U_{t}^{int},\\
\beta u&\text{ if }x\in\partial U_{t}^{ext}.\end{cases}

Using the isoperimetric inequality, for a.e.

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\pi}\mu(t)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq P(U_{t})=\int_{\partial U_{t}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\leq

(17)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\partial U_{t}}g\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\partial U_{t}}\frac{1}{g}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\mu(t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\partial U_{t}^{int}}\frac{1}{{\left|\nabla u\right|}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}+\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{\partial U_{t}^{ext}}\frac{1}{u}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(19)

and, so, (
13
) follows. Finally, we notice that, if

is the solution to (

).
∎

3
Proof of the main results

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

First of all, let us observe that, from the fact that we are assuming that
u^{\sharp}=v
, we have

u_{m}=v_{m}.

(21)

We integrate now (
13
) and (
14
) from
0
to
t
and, since
u^{\ast}
is the generalized inverse of
\mu
(Remark
2.1
), we perform the following change of variables
\mu(t)=s
and
\phi(t)=s
. So, we get

v^{\ast}(s)=\frac{{\left|\Om\right|}-s}{4\pi}+\frac{{\left|\Om\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2\sqrt{\pi}\beta}

(22)

u^{\ast}(s)\leq\frac{{\left|\Om\right|}-s}{4\pi}+\frac{1}{4\pi\beta}\int_{0}^{u^{\ast}(s)}dr\int_{\partial U_{r}^{ext}}\frac{1}{u}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}.

(23)

From
u^{\sharp}=v
, we have
u^{\ast}=v^{\ast}
and, so, combining (
22
) and (
23
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{{\left|\Om\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2\sqrt{\pi}\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4\pi\beta}\int_{0}^{u^{\ast}(s)}dr\int_{\partial U_{r}^{ext}}\frac{1}{u}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}

(24)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4\pi\beta u_{m}}\int_{0}^{u_{M}}\int_{\partial U_{r}^{ext}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}=\frac{1}{4\pi\beta u_{m}}\frac{{\left|\Om\right|}}{\beta}=\frac{{\left|\Om\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2\sqrt{\pi}\beta},

where the last equality follows from (
21
).
Therefore, all the inequalities in (
24
) are equalities and, consequently, equality holds in (
13
).

We now divide the proof in two steps.

Step 1
. Let us prove that every level set
\Set{u>t}
is a ball.

Equality in (
13
) implies the equality in (
17
), i.e.

2\sqrt{\pi}\mu(t)^{\frac{1}{2}}=P(U_{t})

that means that almost every level set is a ball. On the other hand, for all
t\in[u_{m},u_{M})
, there exists a sequence
\Set{t_{k}}
such that

1.

t_{k}\to t
;

2.

t_{k}>t_{k+1}
;

3.

\{u>t_{k}\}
is a ball for all
k
.

Since
\Set{u>t}=\cup_{k}\Set{u>t_{k}}
can be written as an increasing union of balls, then we have that
\{u>t\}
is a ball for all
t
and, from the fact that
\Om=\{u>u_{m}\}
, we obtain that
\Om=x_{0}+\Om^{\sharp}
. From now on, we can assume without loss of generality that
x_{0}=0
.

Step 2.
Let us prove that the level sets are concentric balls.

Equality in (
13
) implies also equality in (
18
), i.e.

\int_{\partial U_{t}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}=\left(\int_{\partial U_{t}}g\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\partial U_{t}}\frac{1}{g}\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This means that, as we have equality in the Hölder inequality, for almost every
t
, the function

\left.g(x)=\right.\begin{cases}{\left|\nabla u\right|}&\text{ if }x\in\partial U_{t}^{int},\\
\beta u&\text{ if }x\in\partial U_{t}^{ext}.\end{cases}

is constant, in particular

{\left|\nabla u\right|}=C_{t},\quad\forall x\in\partial U_{t}^{int},\qquad\beta u=C_{t},\quad\forall x\in\partial U_{t}^{ext},

(25)

and by continuity we can infer that this is true for all
t
.
By the way, we observe that for all
x\in\partial U_{t}
,

g(x)=\displaystyle{\frac{\partial u(x)}{\partial\nu_{t}}},

(26)

where
\nu_{t}
is the unit outer normal to
\partial U_{t}
.

From equality (
13
), we have also that

\mu(t)=\phi(t),

and, consequently, we can deduce from Remark
2.3
that also
\mu
is absolutely continuous. If we denote by

B(x(t),\rho(t))=\Set{u>t},

we can observe that the function
\mu(t)
is locally Lipschitz in
(u_{m},u_{M})
, and, so, the function

\rho(t)=\left(\frac{\mu(t)}{\pi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

is also locally Lipschitz. Moreover, since
\Set{u>t}\subseteq\Set{u>s}
for
t>s
, we have

{\left|x(t)-x(s)\right|}\leq\rho(s)-\rho(t)

and, consequently,
x(t)
is locally Lipschitz.

Let us assume now by contradiction that
x(t)
is not constant. This means that there exists
t_{0}\in(u_{m},u_{M})
such that

y=\frac{d}{dt}x(t_{0})\neq 0.

Let us set
z:=y/{\left|y\right|}
and

P(t):=x(t)+\rho(t)z\in\partial B(x(t),\rho(t)),\quad Q(t):=x(t)-\rho(t)z\in\partial B(x(t),\rho(t)).

We have that, for all
t\in(u_{m},u_{M})
,

u(P(t))=u(Q(t))=t

(27)

and

\frac{\partial u(P(t_{0}))}{\partial\nu_{t_{0}}}=\nabla u(P(t_{0}))\cdot z

-\frac{\partial u(Q(t_{0}))}{\partial\nu_{t_{0}}}=\nabla u(Q(t_{0}))\cdot z.

On the other hand, from (
27
), we obtain

1=\frac{d}{dt}u(P(t))\lvert_{t_{0}}=\nabla u(P(t_{0}))\cdot P^{\prime}(t_{0})=\nabla u(P(t_{0}))\cdot z({\left|y\right|}+\rho^{\prime}(t_{0}))

1=\frac{d}{dt}u(Q(t))\lvert_{t_{0}}=\nabla u(Q(t_{0}))\cdot Q^{\prime}(t_{0})=\nabla u(Q(t_{0}))\cdot z({\left|y\right|}-\rho^{\prime}(t_{0})),

and, consequently,

\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu_{t_{0}}}(P(t_{0}))({\left|y\right|}+\rho^{\prime}(t_{0})=-\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu_{t_{0}}}(Q(t_{0}))({\left|y\right|}-\rho^{\prime}(t_{0}).

(28)

Moreover, by (
25
) we have

\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu_{t_{0}}}(P(t_{0}))=\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu_{t_{0}}}(Q(t_{0}))

and, so, we have
|y|=0
, that is absurd.

Thus, we have proved that
u
is radially symmetric and, since

\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}=\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}<0,

u
is decreasing along the radii and
u=u^{\sharp}
.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

In the proof of Theorem 1.1 the main difference
from the proof of the rigidity result in the Dirichlet case contained in

]
is Step 2. Indeed, in

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let us set
s=\pi r^{2}
. The assumption
u^{\sharp}(x)=v(x)
for
{\left|x\right|}=r
, implies

u^{\ast}(s)=v^{\ast}(s).

Arguing now as in the proof of Theorem

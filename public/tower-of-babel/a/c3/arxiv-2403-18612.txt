Bowen’s formula for a rational graph-directed Markov system
Tadashi Arimitsu, Johannes Jaerisch, Hiroki Sumi and Takayuki Watanabe

Abstract
. We establish Bowen’s formula for the Julia set of a non-elementary, expanding, irreducible and aperiodic rational graph-directed Markov system satisfying the backward separating condition. Towards this end, we shall prove that the associated skew product map is topologically exact on the skew product Julia set, and satisfies the density of repelling periodic points. Moreover, we give a criterion for expandingness in terms of hyperbolicity.

37F10, 58F23, 30D05, 37H12

1.
Introduction and statement of results

Let Rat be the set of all non-constant rational maps on the Riemann sphere
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.
A subsemigroup of
\Rat
with semigroup operation being the functional composition is called a rational semigroup.
The study of rational semigroups was initiated independently in
[
GR96
]
,
[
HM96
]
,
[
Sum97
]
for their own purposes.
This theory has seen significant development in relation to independent and identically-distributed (i.i.d.) random holomorphic dynamics
[
Sum11
,
Sum13
,
Sum21
]
.
Here, by i.i.d. we mean that the holomorphic maps are chosen independently and with the same distribution. We refer to
[
Sum11
]
and
[
Sum13
]
for precise definitions.

Regarding further development along this line of study, it is noticeable that the authors of
[
SW19
]
and
[
SW22
]
developed this random iteration theory by introducing a Markov random dynamical system of rational maps.
These works revealed new phenomena which cannot be observed in i.i.d. random dynamical systems; see, for example,
[
SW19
, Proposition 4.23]
.
We aim to establish a fractal theory for Markov random dynamical systems of rational maps which will lead us to a new horizon of random complex dynamics.
As a first step, we establish Bowen’s formula for the Hausdorff dimension of the Julia set of a rational graph-directed Markov system (rational GDMS for short).
We refer to Section
1.1
for an introduction to rational GDMS.

To explain and motivate our study of Bowen’s formula better, let us first briefly introduce the thermodynamic formalism of dynamical systems.
The origin of this theory dates back to the seminal paper
[
Sin72
]
by Y. Sinaĭ. Later, D. Ruelle and R. Bowen laid the foundation of this theory as cofounders; see
[
Rue04
]
,
[
Bow08
]
for comprehensive accounts of the prototypical results.
Of particular concern is Bowen’s celebrated result which determines the Hausdorff dimension of a quasi-circle as the unique zero of a central notion in the thermodynamic formalism called pressure function; see
[
Bow79
]
.
This achievement is widely known as Bowen’s formula.
In
[
Rue82
]
, Ruelle further developed this result in the context of hyperbolic Julia sets, and many other analogous statements have been justified in various settings to this date.
In particular, we note that under the open set condition, Bowen’s formula for a finitely generated, expanding rational semigroup was justified in
[
Sum05
]
.

1.1.
Rational graph directed Markov systems

Let us begin with a formal introduction to a rational GDMS.
All the definitions in this subsection are given in
[
SW19
]
and
[
Wal82
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(V,E)
be a
directed graph
with finite
vertex set

V
and finite
edge set

E
.
Let
\Gamma_{e}\subset\Rat
be a non-empty, finite subset of
\Rat
indexed by a directed edge
e\in E
.
Then the triple
S:=(V,E,(\Gamma_{e})_{e\in E})
is called a
rational GDMS
.
The symbol
i(e)
(resp.
t(e))
denotes the
initial
(resp.
terminal
)
vertex
of each directed edge
e\in E
.

We shall adopt the convention that
\mathbb{N}:=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:n>0\}
and
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:n\geq 0\}
.
For each point of the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
, let
|z|
denote the modulus of
z
.

Definition 1.2
.

Given a rational GDMS
S:=(V,E,(\Gamma_{e})_{e\in E})
, we define the subshift of finite type
(X(S),\sigma)
given by

X(S):=\left\{\xi:=(\xi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}:=(g_{n},e_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in(\Rat\times E)^{\mathbb{N}}\mid\forall n\in\mathbb{N},g_{n}\in\Gamma_{e_{n}},t(e_{n})=i(e_{n+1})\right\},

and the left shift
\sigma:X(S)\rightarrow X(S)
.
We say that
S
is
irreducible
if
(X(S),\sigma)
is irreducible, and
S
is
irreducible and aperiodic
if
(X(S),\sigma)
is irreducible and aperiodic; see e.g.
[
Wal82
]
for these properties of subshifts.

For each
N\in\mathbb{N}
we denote by
X^{N}(S)
the set of all subwords of
X(S)
of length
N
.
For each element
\xi=(g_{n},e_{n})_{1\leq n\leq N}\in X^{N}(S)
, denoting
g_{\xi}:=g_{N}\circ\dots\circ g_{1}
, we define the sets

\displaystyle H(S):=\bigcup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{g_{\xi}\mid\xi\in X^{N}(S)\right\},\quad H_{i}(S):=\left\{g_{\xi}\mid\xi\in X^{N}(S),i=i(e_{1})\right\},

\displaystyle H^{j}(S):=\bigcup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{g_{\xi}\mid\xi\in X^{N}(S),j=t(e_{N})\right\}\text{ and }H^{j}_{i}(S):=H_{i}(S)\cap H^{j}(S).

The set
F(S)
defined as
\{z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\mid H(S)\text{ is equicontinuous on some neighborhood of }z\}
is called the
Fatou set of
S
.
Its complement
J(S):=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus F(S)
is called the
Julia set of
S
.

Likewise, we define the following sets at each vertex.
The set
F_{i}(S)
defined as
\{z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\mid H_{i}(S)\text{ is equicontinuous on some neighborhood of }z\}
is called the
Fatou set of
S
at the vertex
i
.
Its complement
J_{i}(S):=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus F_{i}(S)
is called the
Julia set of
S
at the vertex
i
.

The cardinality of a set
\mathcal{A}
is denoted by
\#\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}
.

Definition 1.3
.

A rational GDMS
S
is said to be
non-elementary
if
\#J_{i}(S)\geq 3
for all
i\in V
.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
S:=(V,E,(\Gamma_{e})_{e\in E})
be a rational GDMS.

(i)

We define the
skew product map

\tilde{f}:X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
associated with
S
by

\tilde{f}(\xi,z):=\left(\sigma(\xi),g_{1}(z)\right)

for each
\xi:=\left(g_{n},e_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in X(S)
and
z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.

(ii)

For each
\xi:=(g_{n},e_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in X(S)
, we define the
fiber Fatou set
F_{\xi}
of
\xi
by

\{z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\mid(g_{N}\circ\dots\circ g_{1})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\text{ is equicontinuous on some neighborhood of }z\}.

Its complement
J_{\xi}:=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus F_{\xi}
is called the
fiber Julia set of
\xi
.
We also define
F^{\xi}:=\{\xi\}\times F_{\xi}\subset X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
, and
J^{\xi}:=\{\xi\}\times J_{\xi}\subset X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.
We call the set

J(\tilde{f}):=\overline{\bigcup_{\xi\in X(S)}J^{\xi}}\subset X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}

the
skew product Julia set of
\tilde{f}
.

Let
d_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}
be the spherical distance on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.
Let
d_{X}
be the shift distance on
X(S)
and define the distance
\tilde{d}
on the set
X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
given by
\tilde{d}((\xi,x),(\tau,y)):=d_{X}(\xi,\tau)+d_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}(x,y)
for each
(\xi,x),(\tau,y)\in X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.
We shall endow the set
J(\tilde{f})
with the subspace topology of the metric topology on
X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.

1.2.
Main results

In the setting above, we introduce further definitions to state the main results.
Let
S
be a rational GDMS.
Given an element
\xi\in X(S)
, we also write
(g_{\xi,n},e_{\xi,n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}:=\xi
.
For each
\tilde{z}:=(\xi,z)\in X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we set

(\tilde{f}^{n})^{\prime}(\tilde{z}):=\left(g_{\xi,n}\circ\cdots\circ g_{\xi,1}\right)^{\prime}(z).

The first result provides the characterisation of the skew product Julia set
J(\tilde{f})
from the perspective of the dynamics of
\tilde{f}
under a mild assumption.
Recall that a point
\tilde{z}\in\widehat{X}(S)\times\mathbb{C}
is called
a repelling periodic point of
\tilde{f}

if there exists
p\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\tilde{f}^{p}(\tilde{z})=\tilde{z}
and
|\tilde{f}^{p}(\tilde{z})^{\prime}|>1
.

Main result A

(see Proposition
2.2
)
.

Let
S
be a non-elementary, irreducible rational GDMS and
\tilde{f}
be the associated skew product map.
Then,
J(\tilde{f})
is equal to the closure of
the set of repelling periodic points of
\tilde{f}
.

The next result concerns the topological behaviour of the skew product map
\tilde{f}
with respect to
J(\tilde{f})
: the topological exactness, often casually described as locally eventually onto.
To be more precise, the skew product map
\tilde{f}:X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is said to be
topologically exact on
J(\tilde{f})
if for each open subset
U
of
X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
with
U\cap J(\tilde{f})\neq\emptyset
, there exists an element
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq N
, we have
\tilde{f}^{n}(U)\supset J(\tilde{f}).

To obtain this result, we restrict the class of a skew product map
\tilde{f}
by using the notion of expandingness.
This notion is inspired by a class called the expanding rational semigroup introduced in
[
Sum05
]
.
We state the definition. A rational GDMS
S
is said to be
expanding
if
J(S)\neq\emptyset
and the map
\tilde{f}
is
expanding along fibers
, i.e., there exist
C>0
and
\lambda>1
such that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}

\inf_{\tilde{z}\in J(\tilde{f})}\|(\tilde{f}^{n})^{\prime}(\tilde{z})\|\geq C\lambda^{n},

where we use
\|\cdot\|
to denote the norm of the derivative with respect to the metric
d_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}
.

Main result B

(see Corollary
2.7
)
.

Let
S
be a non-elementary irreducible and aperiodic rational GDMS.
Suppose that the associated skew product map
\tilde{f}
is expanding along fibers.
Then,
\tilde{f}
is topologically exact on
J(\tilde{f})
.

Motivated by
[
Sum98
]
, we introduce the definitions below for a rational GDMS. Let
S:=\left(V,E,\left(\Gamma_{e}\right)_{e\in E}\right)
be a rational GDMS. For each vertex
i\in V
, the set defined by

P_{i}(S):=\overline{\bigcup_{h\in H^{i}(S)}\{\text{ critical values of }h\}}\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}

is called the
post-critical set at the vertex
i
.
A rational GDMS
S
is said to be
hyperbolic
if
P_{i}(S)\subset F_{i}(S)
for each
i\in V
.

Main result C

(see Theorem
2.13
)
.

Let
S
be a hyperbolic, non-elementary, irreducible rational GDMS.
For each
i\in V
and
h\in H_{i}^{i}(S)
, assume that
h
is loxodromic when its degree is one.
Then, the skew product map associated with
S
is expanding along fibers.

To state the last result, we briefly introduce some relevant definitions of thermodynamic formalism; see subsection
2.3
for precise definitions.
Assume that a rational GDMS
S
is expanding.
The geometric potential
\tilde{\varphi}\colon J(\tilde{f})\to\mathbb{R}
is given by
\tilde{\varphi}(\tilde{z}):=-\log\|g_{\xi,1}^{\prime}(z)\|
for each
\tilde{z}:=(\xi,z)\in J(\tilde{f})
.
Let
P(\tilde{f},\tilde{\varphi})
denote the topological pressure of the pair
(\tilde{f},\tilde{\varphi})
.
Then, there is a unique zero
\delta
of the geometric pressure function
\mathcal{P}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
t\mapsto P(\tilde{f},t\tilde{\varphi})
.
We refer to subsection
2.3
for further details on topological pressure.
Let
\dim_{H}(A)
denote the Hausdorff dimension of a set
A\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
with respect to
d_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}
.

Main result D

(see Theorem
2.22
)
.

Let
S
be a non-elementary, expanding, irreducible and aperiodic rational GDMS.
Then, the
\delta
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
J(S)
is finite and its upper box-counting dimension never exceeds
\delta
.

Main result E

(see Corollary
2.23
)
.

Let
S
be a non-elementary, expanding, irreducible and aperiodic rational GDMS.
Suppose that
S
satisfies the backward separating condition.
Then, we have
\dim_{H}(J(S))=\delta
and the
\delta
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
J(S)
is positive and finite.

The definition of the backward-separation condition is given in Definition
2.20
.
In section
3
, we shall estimate the Hausdorff dimension of the Julia set for a rational GDMS by using the last result.

Next, we list several important properties of a rational GDMS.

.

Let
S
be a rational GDMS and
\tilde{f}
be the skew product map associated with

.
Then, we have the following.

(i)

\tilde{f}^{-1}(J(\tilde{f}))=J(\tilde{f})
and

.

(ii)

If the system

2.1.
The density of repelling periodic points and the topological exactness of a skew product map

The objectives of this subsection are to prove the density in
J(\tilde{f})
of the repelling periodic points of a skew product map
\tilde{f}
and, as a consequence, the topological exactness of
\tilde{f}
under some standard assumptions.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
S
be a non-elementary, irreducible rational GDMS and
\tilde{f}:X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
the associated skew product map.
Then,
J(\tilde{f})
is equal to the closure of
the set of repelling periodic points of
\tilde{f}.

Proof.

Let
U
be a nonempty open subset of
X(S)\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}

with
U\cap J(\tilde{f})\neq\emptyset.
Then, there exists an element

(\xi,z_{0})\in U\cap J(\tilde{f}).
We may assume

z_{0}\in J_{\xi}.

Let
i:=i(e_{\xi,1}).
There exist
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
an open subset
V_{0}
of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
such that

(\xi,z_{0})\in[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{r}]\times V_{0}\subset U.

Since
S
is irreducible and
V
is finite, there exists a finite set
\mathscr{F}\subset H(S)
such that for each
i,j\in V
there exists
h\in H^{i}_{j}(S)\cap\mathscr{F}
.
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
we take
h_{n}\in H_{t(e_{\xi,n})}^{i}(S)\cap\mathscr{F}
. Clearly,
\{h_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a finite set.
Since
z_{0}\in J_{\xi}
, the sequence

\{g_{\xi,n}\circ\cdots\circ g_{\xi,1}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is not
equicontinuous in any neighborhood of
z_{0}.

We now show the following claim.

Claim
.

The sequence
\{h_{n}\circ g_{\xi,n}\circ\cdots\circ g_{\xi,1}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is not equicontinuous in any neighborhood of
z_{0}
.

To prove this claim,
suppose
\{h_{n}\circ g_{\xi,n}\circ\cdots\circ g_{\xi,1}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is
equicontinuous in a neighborhood of
z_{0}.
Let

\varepsilon>0
be any small positive number.
Since
\{h_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is finite,
there exists an
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that
for each
\varepsilon_{0}
-ball
B
in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
and for each

n\in\mathbb{N}
, the diameter of any connected component of
h_{n}^{-1}(B)
is less than
\varepsilon.
For this
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, since
we are assuming that
\{h_{n}\circ g_{\xi,n}\circ\cdots\circ g_{\xi,1}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is
equicontinuous in a neighborhood of
z_{0}
, there exists a

\delta>0
such that
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the diameter of
h_{n}\circ g_{\xi,n}\circ\cdots\circ g_{\xi,1}(D(z_{0},\delta))
is less than
\varepsilon_{0}
. It follows that
the diameter of
g_{\xi,n}\circ\cdots\circ g_{\xi,1}(D(z_{0},\delta))
is
less than
\varepsilon.

However, this contradicts
z_{0}\in J_{\xi}.

Thus we have proved our claim.

Since
S
is irreducible,
\#J_{i}(S)=\#J(H_{i}^{i}(S))\geq 3
by (
(ii)
) of Proposition
2.1
.
Hence,
\#J(H_{i}^{i}(S))=\infty
and
J(H_{i}^{i}(S))
is equal to the closure of the set of repelling fixed
points of elements of
H_{i}^{i}(S)
; see e.g.
[
HM96
]
,
[
Sta12
]
.

Thus there exist mutually distinct three
elements
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in J(H_{i}^{i})
such that for each

j=1,2,3,
the
point
x_{j}
is a repelling fixed point of some
\alpha_{j}\in H_{i}^{i}(S)
.
By the claim above and Montel’s theorem, it follows that
there exist an element
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq r
, an element

z_{1}\in V_{0}
, and an element
j\in\{1,2,3\}
such that

h_{n}\circ g_{\xi,n}\circ\cdots\circ g_{\xi,1}(z_{1})=x_{j}.

Therefore there exist an element
\rho\in[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{r}]
and
an element
s\in\mathbb{N}
with
s\geq r
such that

(1)

\ z_{1}\in J_{\rho},\ g_{\rho,s}\circ\cdots\circ g_{\rho,1}\in H_{i}^{i}(S),\mbox{ and }g_{\rho,s}\circ\cdots\circ g_{\rho,1}(z_{1})=x_{j}\in J_{i}(S)=J(H_{i}^{i}(S)).

Let
H:=\{h\in H_{i}^{i}(S)\mid h=\cdots\circ g_{\rho,s}\circ\cdots\circ g_{\rho,1}\}
.
Note that this is a rational semigroup and

(2)

\ J(H)=(g_{\rho,s}\circ\cdots\circ g_{\rho,1})^{-1}(J(H_{i}^{i}(S)).

By
(\ref{XRexk38dyn}),(\ref{tfIdUGgdiI})
, we obtain

(3)

\ z_{1}\in(g_{\rho,s}\circ\cdots\circ g_{\rho,1})^{-1}(J(H_{i}^{i}(S)))=J(H).

Moreover, since
\#J(H_{i}^{i}(S))\geq 3
, by
(\ref{tfIdUGgdiI})
we have

\#(J(H))\geq 3.
Hence,

J(H)
is equal to the closure of the set of all repelling fixed points
of elements of
H
; see
[
HM96
]
.
Combining this with
(\ref{1gongIbvUM})
and the fact
z_{1}\in V_{0}
, we obtain that
there exist an element
z_{2}\in V_{0}
and an element
g\in H
such that

z_{2}
is a repelling fixed point of
g.

Hence, there exist an element
\beta\in[\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{s}]\subset[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{r}]
and an element
k\in\mathbb{N}
with

k\geq s
such that
\sigma^{k}(\beta)=\beta
and
z_{2}
is a
repelling fixed point of
g_{\beta,k}\circ\cdots g_{\beta,1}.

Thus

(\beta,z_{2})\in([\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{r}]\times V_{0})\cap J(\tilde{f})\subset U\cap J(\tilde{f})

and

(\beta,z_{2})
is a repelling periodic point of
\tilde{f}.

Hence, we have proved our theorem.
∎

Let

Proof.

Pointwise Ergodic Theorems for Higher Levels of Mixing
Sohail Farhangi

Abstract
We prove strengthenings of the Birkhoff Ergodic Theorem for weakly mixing and strongly mixing measure preserving systems. We show that our pointwise theorem for weakly mixing systems is strictly stronger than the Wiener-Wintner Theorem. We also show that our pointwise Theorems for weakly mixing and strongly mixing systems characterize weakly mixing systems and strongly mixing systems respectively.

Introduction

In this section we establish the notation that we use, review some known results related to the Birkhoff Pointwise Ergodic Theorem and state the main theorems of this paper.

Whenever we discuss a measure preserving system (m.p.s.)
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
,
X
will be a measurable space,
\mathscr{B}
will be a
\sigma
-algebra of subsets of
X
,
\mu
will be a probability measure on
(X,\mathscr{B})
and
T:X\rightarrow X
will be a measurable transformation satisfying
\mu(A)=\mu(T^{-1}A)
for all
A\in\mathscr{B}
. When we work with the Hilbert space
L^{2}(X,\mu)
, we will let
U:L^{2}(X,\mu)\rightarrow L^{2}(X,\mu)
denote the unitary operator given by
U(f)=f\circ T
. When we work with a m.p.s. of the form
([0,1],\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
, we will assume that
\mathscr{B}
is the completion of the Borel
\sigma
-algebra. We say that two sequences of complex numbers
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
are orthogonal if

(1.1)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}x_{n}\overline{y_{n}}=0.

Now let us recall the levels of the ergodic hierarchy of mixing that will be used throughout this paper.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
be a m.p.s.

•

(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
is
ergodic
if for every
A\in\mathscr{B}
satisfying
\mu(A)=\mu(T^{-1}A)
we have
\mu(A)\in\{0,1\}
.

•

(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
is
weakly mixing
if for every
A,B\in\mathscr{B}
we have

(1.2)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\mu(A\cap T^{-n}B)-\mu(A)\mu(B)|=0.

•

(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
is
strongly mixing
if for every
A,B\in\mathscr{B}
we have

(1.3)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mu(A\cap T^{-n}B)=\mu(A)\mu(B).

Theorem 1.2

(Birkhoff)
.

Let
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
be a m.p.s., and let
f\in L^{1}([0,1],\mu)
. For a.e.
x\in X
, we have

(1.4)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(T^{n}x)=f^{*}(x),

where
f^{*}(x)\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
is such that
f^{*}(Tx)=f^{*}(x)
for a.e.
x\in X
and
\int_{A}f^{*}d\mu=\int_{A}fd\mu
for every
A\in\mathscr{B}
satisfying
A=T^{-1}(A)
. In particular, if
T
is ergodic, then for a.e.
x\in X
we have

(1.5)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(T^{n}x)=\int_{X}fd\mu.

The Birkhoff Pointwise Ergodic Theorem can be interpreted as follows. Given an ergodic m.p.s.
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
and
f\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
satisfying
\int_{X}fd\mu=0
, the sequence
\left(f(T^{n}x)\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
is orthogonal to the constant sequence
(1)_{n=1}^{\infty}
. The Wiener-Wintner Theorem is a generalization of the Birkhoff Pointwise Ergodic Theorem for weakly mixing systems and has a similar interpretation.

Theorem 1.3

(Wiener-Wintner)
.

Let
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
be a m.p.s. and let
f\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
. There exists
X^{\prime}\in\mathscr{B}
with
\mu(X^{\prime})=1
, such that for every
x\in X^{\prime}
and any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\lambda|=1
the limit

(1.6)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(T^{n}x)\lambda^{n}

exists. Furthermore, if
T
is weakly mixing, then

(1.7)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(T^{n}x)\lambda^{n}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }\lambda\neq 1\\
\int_{X}fd\mu&\text{if }\lambda=1\end{cases}.

Definition 1.4
.

A bounded sequence of complex numbers
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is
Besicovitch Almost Periodic
if for each
\epsilon>0
there exists a trigonometric polynomial
P_{\epsilon}
for which

(1.8)

\overline{\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|x_{n}-P_{\epsilon}(n)|<\epsilon.

The Wiener-Wintner Theorem can be interpreted as follows. Given a weakly mixing m.p.s.
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
and
f\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
satisfying
\int_{X}fd\mu=0
, the sequence
\left(f(T^{n}x)\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
is orthogonal to any Besicovitch Almost Periodic Sequence
(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
. Theorem
1.6
is one of the main results of this paper and is a generalization of the Wiener-Wintner Theorem when interpreted in a similar fashion. We require some more preliminaries before we can state Theorem
1.6
.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
satisfy

(1.9)

\overline{\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|x_{n}|<\infty.

(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a
weakly mixing sequence
if it satisfies the following condition.

Suppose that for a bounded sequence
(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
there is a strictly increasing sequence
(N_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
for which

(1.10)

\ell(h):=\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{q}}x_{n+h}\overline{y_{n}}\text{ exists for every }h\in\mathbb{N}.

Then we have

(1.11)

\lim_{H\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=1}^{H}|\ell(h)|=0.

Theorem 1.6

(cf. Theorem
2.6
in Section 2)
.

Let
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
be a weakly mixing m.p.s. and let
f\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
satisfy
\int_{X}fd\mu=0
. For a.e.
x\in X
,
\left(f(T^{n}x)\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a weakly mixing sequence.

Definition 1.7

(cf. Definition 3.13 in
[
4
]
)
.

A bounded sequence of complex numbers
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is
compact
if for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(1.12)

\underset{m\in\mathbb{N}}{\text{sup }}\underset{1\leq k\leq K}{\text{min }}\overline{\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|x_{n+m}-x_{n+k}|^{2}<\epsilon.

Lemma 1.8

(cf. Lemma 3.19 of
[
4
]
)
.

If
(w_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
is a weakly mixing sequence and
(c_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a compact sequence, then

(1.13)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}w_{n}\overline{c_{n}}=0.

Lemma
1.13
is what allows us to interpret Theorem
1.6
in a similar fashion to the Birkhoff Pointwise Ergodic Theorem and the Wiener-Wintner Theorem. Theorem
1.6
asserts that for a weakly mixing m.p.s.
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
and
f\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
satisfying
\int_{X}fd\mu=0
, the sequence
\left(f(T^{n}x)\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
is orthogonal to any compact sequence
(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
. We will see in section
2
that the class of compact sequences is strictly larger than the class of Besicovitch Almost Periodic Sequences and this is why Theorem
1.6
is more general than the Wiener-Wintner Theorem. The next main result of this paper is an analogue of Theorem
1.6
for strongly mixing measure preserving systems.

Definition 1.9
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
satisfy

(1.14)

\overline{\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|x_{n}|<\infty.

(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a
strongly mixing sequence
if it satisfies the following condition.

Suppose that for a bounded sequence
(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
there is a strictly increasing sequence
(N_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
for which

(1.15)

\ell(h):=\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{q}}x_{n+h}\overline{y_{n}}\text{ exists for every }h\in\mathbb{N}.

Then we have

(1.16)

\lim_{h\rightarrow\infty}|\ell(h)|=0.

Theorem 1.10

(cf. Theorem
3.2
of Section 3)
.

Let
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
be a strongly mixing m.p.s. and let
f\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
satisfy
\int_{X}fd\mu=0
. For a.e.
x\in X
,
\left(f(T^{n}x)\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a strongly mixing sequence.

Proposition
1.11
gives a partial converse to the Birkhoff Pointwise Ergodic Theorem. Proposition
1.11
is well known and an easy consequence of the Dominated Convergence Theorem but we state it anyways so that we can put some results of this paper in context.

Proposition 1.11
.

Let
([0,1],\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
be a m.p.s. If for every
f\in L^{\infty}([0,1],\mu)
, there exists
A_{f}\in\mathscr{B}
such that
\mu(A_{f})=1
and for every
x\in A_{f}
we have

(1.17)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(T^{n}x)=\int_{X}fd\mu,

then
T
is ergodic.

Proposition
1.12
is a converse to Theorem
1.6
in the same fashion that Proposition
1.11
is a converse to the Birkhoff Ergodic Theorem.

Proposition 1.12
.

Let
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
be a m.p.s. If for every
f\in L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
with
\int_{X}fd\mu=0
there exist a set
A_{f}\subseteq X
satisfying
\mu(A_{f})=1
and for every
x\in A_{f}
we have that
\left(f(T^{n}x)\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a weakly mixing sequence, then
T
is weakly mixing.

Proposition
1.12
should not come as a surprise since the Wiener-Wintner Theorem is already strong enough to characterize weakly mixing systems. To see this let us recall that an ergodic m.p.s.
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
is weakly mixing if and only if
L^{2}(X,\mu)
has no non-constant eigenfunctions with respect to
U
. Now let
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
be an ergodic m.p.s. that is not weakly mixing and let
f\in L^{2}(X,\mu)
be a non-constant eigen function corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda
. We see that

(1.18)

\lim_{H\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=1}^{H}|\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(T^{n+h}x)\lambda^{-n}|\\
=\lim_{H\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=1}^{H}|f(T^{h}x)|=\int_{X}|f|d\mu\neq 0

for a.e.
x\in X
. Since any m.p.s.
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
satisfying the hypothesis of Proposition
1.12
also satisfies the hypothesis of Proposition
1.11
we see that
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
is ergodic, so
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
is in fact weakly mixing.

Theorem
1.13
is a converse to Theorem
1.10
in the same way that Proposition
1.12
is a converse for Theorem
1.6
. The proof of Theorem
1.13
can easily be adjusted to give a direct proof of Proposition
1.12
as well, but we will delay the proof of Theorem
1.13
until section 3.

Theorem 1.13

(cf. Theorem
3.3
in Section 3)
.

Let
([0,1],\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
be a m.p.s. If for every
f\in L^{\infty}([0,1],\mu)
with
\int_{X}fd\mu=0
there exist a set

and for every

is strongly mixing.

2
A Pointwise Theorem for Weakly Mixing Systems

In this section we prove Theorem
2.6
, which is Theorem
1.6
for standard measure preserving systems. In section
4
we will show how to deduce Theorem
1.6
from
2.6
. We will also discuss some potential generalizations of Theorem
1.6
.

Before we begin proving Theorem
2.6
we will show that the class of compact sequences is strictly larger than that of Besicovitch Almost Periodic Sequences. In Example 3.21 of
[
4
]
a compact sequence orthogonal to any Besicovitch Almost Periodic Sequence was constructed. In Lemma
2.1
we give a different class of compact sequences that are orthogonal to all Besicovitch Almost Periodic Sequences.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{T}
be irrational, and let
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be the sequence

\alpha,2\alpha,2\alpha,3\alpha,3\alpha,3\alpha,\cdots,

which can also be expressed as
x_{n}=m\alpha
for
\binom{m}{2}<n\leq\binom{m+1}{2}
. If
f\in C(\mathbb{T})
satisfies
\int_{\mathbb{T}}fdm=0
, then
\left(f(x_{n})\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
is orthogonal to any Besicovitch Almost Periodic Sequence.

Proof.
Using standard approximation arguments, we see that it suffices to show that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
the sequence
\left(e^{2\pi ikx_{n}}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
is orthogonal to any Besicovitch Almost Periodic Sequence. We note that for any
\beta\in\mathbb{T}\setminus\{0\}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
, we have

(2.1)

\overline{\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}}|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ikx_{n}}e^{-2\pi in\beta}|

(2.2)

=\overline{\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}}|\binom{N}{2}^{-1}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{-2\pi i\binom{n}{2}\beta}\sum_{m=1}^{n}e^{2\pi ikn\alpha}e^{-2\pi im\beta}|

(2.3)

=\overline{\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}}|\binom{N}{2}^{-1}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi i(n\alpha-\binom{n}{2}\beta)}\frac{e^{-2\pi i(n+1)\beta}-e^{-2\pi i\beta}}{1-e^{-2\pi i\beta}}|

(2.4)

\leq\overline{\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}}\binom{N}{2}^{-1}\frac{N}{|1-e^{-2\pi i\beta}|}=0.

Similarly, we see that

(2.5)

\overline{\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}}|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ikx_{n}}\overline{1}|=\overline{\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}}|\binom{N}{2}^{-1}\sum_{n=1}^{N}ne^{2\pi ikn\alpha}|

(2.6)

=\overline{\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}}|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ib\alpha}|=0.

We now see that a standard approximation argument shows that
(e^{2\pi ikx_{n}})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is orthogonal to any Besicovitch Almost Periodic Sequence.

To see that
\left(f(x_{n})\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a compact sequence it suffices to note that

(2.7)

\underset{m\in\mathbb{N}}{\text{sup }}\underset{1\leq k\leq K}{\text{min }}\overline{\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|x_{n+m}-x_{n+k}|^{2}

(2.8)

\leq\underset{m\geq K+1}{\text{sup }}\underset{1\leq k\leq K}{\text{min }}\sum_{j=k}^{m-1}\overline{\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|x_{n+j+1}-x_{n+j}|^{2}

(2.9)

=\underset{m\geq k+1}{\text{sup }}\underset{1\leq k\leq K}{\text{min }}\sum_{j=k}^{m-1}0=0.\qed

We see that in the proof of Lemma
2.1
, the sequence
(n\alpha)_{n=1}^{\infty}
could be replaced by any uniformly distributed sequence in
[0,1]
, and
(\binom{m}{2})_{m=1}^{\infty}
could be replaced by any subpolynomial sequence of integers
(k_{m})_{m=1}^{\infty}
satisfying
\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}k_{m+1}-k_{m}=\infty
, so Lemma
2.1
can be used to construct many examples of compact sequences that are not Besicovitch Almost Periodic.

Lemma
2.2
is what will allow us to view bounded sequences of complex numbers as an element of some Hilbert space.

Lemma 2.2

(cf. Lemma 3.26 of
[
4
]
)
.

Let
(a_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be bounded. Then there exists a compact metric space
X
, a continuous map
S:X\rightarrow X
, a function
F\in C([0,1])
, and a point
x\in X
with a dense orbit under
S
such that
a_{n}=F(S^{n}(x))
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma
2.4
allows us to convert correlations of sequences to inner products of functions in a Hilbert space, which will then permit us to use Hilbert space Theory to analyze our correlations. Before stating Lemma
2.4
, let us recall the definition of a generic point.

Definition 2.3
.

Given an ergodic m.p.s.
([0,1],\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
,
x\in[0,1]
is
generic
if for every
f\in C([0,1])
we have

(2.10)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(T^{n}x)=\int_{[0,1]}fd\mu.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
([0,1],\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
be an ergodic m.p.s. Let
(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a bounded sequence of complex numbers. Let
f\in C([0,1])
be arbitrary and let
x\in X
be a generic point. Let
U:L^{2}([0,1],\mu)\rightarrow L^{2}([0,1],\mu)
be the unitary operator induced by
T
. Let
(N_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
be any sequence for which

(2.11)

\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{q}}f(T^{n+h}x)\overline{y_{n}}

exists for each
h\in\mathbb{N}
. Then there exists
g\in L^{2}([0,1],\mu)
for which

(2.12)

\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{q}}f(T^{n+h}x)\overline{y_{n}}=\langle U^{h}f,g\rangle_{\mu}.

Proof.
By Lemma
2.2
, let
(Y,d)
be a compact metric space, let
\xi\in C(Y)
,
y\in Y
, and
S:Y\rightarrow Y
be such that
y_{n}=\xi(S^{n}y)
. Let
\nu
be any weak
∗
limit point of the sequence

(2.13)

\left(\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{q}}\delta_{T^{n}x,S^{n}y}\right)_{q=1}^{\infty},

and let
(M_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
be such that

(2.14)

\nu=\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{M_{q}}\sum_{n=1}^{M_{q}}\delta_{T^{n}x,S^{n}y}.

Let
\tilde{f},\tilde{\xi}\in L^{2}([0,1]\times Y,\nu)
be given by
\tilde{f}(x,y)=f(x)
and
\tilde{\xi}(x,y)=\xi(y)
. Let
V:L^{2}([0,1]\times Y,\nu)\rightarrow L^{2}([0,1]\times Y,\nu)
be the unitary operator induced by
T\times S
. We note that if
h\in L^{2}([0,1]\times Y,\nu)
is such that
h(x,y)=k(x)
for some
k\in L^{2}([0,1],\mu)
, then the genericity of
x
gives us

(2.15)

\int_{[0,1]\times Y}hd\nu=\int_{[0,1]}kd\mu.

Let
\tilde{\mu}
be the probability measure on
([0,1]\times Y,\mathscr{B}\otimes\mathscr{A})
given by
\tilde{\mu}(B\times A)=\mu(B)\mathbbm{1}_{A}(y)
for all
B\in\mathscr{B}
and
A\in\mathscr{A}
. Since we may identify
L^{2}([0,1]\times Y,\tilde{\mu})
with the functions in
L^{2}([0,1]\times Y,\nu)
of the form
h(x,y)=k(x)
, let
P:L^{2}([0,1]\times Y,\nu)\rightarrow L^{2}([0,1]\times Y,\tilde{\mu})
denote the orthogonal projection. Let
\tilde{g}=P\tilde{\xi}
, and let
g\in L^{2}([0,1],\mu)
be such that
\tilde{g}(x,y)=g(x)
. We now see that for any
h\in\mathbb{N}
we have

(2.16)

\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}|\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{q}}f(T^{n+h}x)\overline{y_{n}}|=\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}|\frac{1}{M_{q}}\sum_{n=1}^{M_{q}}\tilde{f}(T^{n+h}x)\overline{\tilde{\xi}(S^{n}y)}|

(2.17)

=|\int_{[0,1]\times Y}V^{h}f\overline{\tilde{\xi}}d\nu|=|\langle V^{h}\tilde{f},\tilde{\xi}\rangle_{\nu}|=|\langle V^{h}\tilde{f},\tilde{g}\rangle_{\nu}|=|\langle U^{h}f,g\rangle_{\mu}|.\qed

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
([0,1],\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
be a weakly mixing m.p.s. For each generic point
x\in[0,1]
and each
f\in C([0,1])
with
\int_{[0,1]}fd\mu=0
,
\left(f(T^{n}x)\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a weakly mixing sequence.

Proof.
Let
f\in C([0,1])
be arbitrary and let
(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be bounded. Let
(N_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
be any sequence for which

(2.18)

\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{q}}f(T^{n+h}x)\overline{y_{n}}

exists for every
h\in\mathbb{N}
. By Lemma
2.4
, let
g\in L^{2}([0,1],\mu)
be such that

(2.19)

\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{q}}f(T^{n+h}x)\overline{y_{n}}=\langle U^{h}f,g\rangle.

Letting
U:L^{2}([0,1],\mu)\rightarrow L^{2}([0,1],\mu)
denote the unitary operator induced by
T
, we see that
U
is weakly mixing. It follows that

(2.20)

\lim_{H\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=1}^{H}|\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{q}}f(T^{n+h}x)\overline{y_{n}}|

(2.21)

=\lim_{H\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=1}^{H}|\langle U^{h}f,g\rangle|=0.\qed

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
([0,1],\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
be a weakly mixing m.p.s. and let
f\in L^{1}([0,1],\mu)
satisfy
\int_{[0,1]}fd\mu=0
. For a.e.
x\in[0,1]
,
\left(f(T^{n}x)\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a weakly mixing sequence.

Proof.
Let
\epsilon>0
be arbitrary, and let
g\in C([0,1])
be such that
||f-g||_{1}\leq\epsilon
and
\int_{[0,1]}gd\mu=0
. Let
X\subseteq[0,1]
be a set of full measure for which the Birkhoff Pointwise Ergodic Theorem holds for
|f-g|
along every

In this section we prove Theorem

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
([0,1],\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
be a strongly mixing m.p.s. For every generic point
x\in[0,1]
and each

with
\int_{X}fd\mu=0
,
\left(f(T^{n}x)\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a strongly mixing sequence.

Proof.
Let
f\in C([0,1])
be arbitrary and let
(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be bounded. Let
(N_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
be any sequence for which

(3.1)

\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{q}}f(T^{n+h}x)\overline{y_{n}}

exists for every
h\in\mathbb{N}
. By Lemma
2.4
, let
g\in L^{2}([0,1],\mu)
be such that

(3.2)

\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{q}}f(T^{n+h}x)\overline{y_{n}}=\langle U^{h}f,g\rangle.

Letting
U:L^{2}([0,1],\mu)\rightarrow L^{2}([0,1],\mu)
denote the unitary operator induced by
T
, we see that
U
is strongly mixing. It follows that

(3.3)

\lim_{h\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{q}}f(T^{n+h}x)\overline{y_{n}}h=\lim_{h\rightarrow\infty}\langle U^{h}f,g\rangle=0.\qed

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
([0,1],\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
be a strongly mixing m.p.s. and let
f\in L^{1}([0,1],\mu)
satisfy
\int_{[0,1]}fd\mu=0
. For a.e.

\left(f(T^{n}x)\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}

Proof.
Let

be arbitrary, and let

and
\int_{X}gd\mu=0

be a set of full measure for which the Birkhoff Pointwise Ergodic Theorem holds for
|f-g|
along every

(3.4)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|f(T^{n}x)-g(T^{n}x)|=\int_{X}|f-g|d\mu=||f-g||_{1}<\epsilon.

2.5
to further refine

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
be a m.p.s. If for every

\left(f(T^{n}x)\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a strongly mixing sequence, then
T
is strongly mixing.

Proof.
Let
A,B\in\mathscr{B}
both be arbitrary. Let
X^{\prime}\subseteq X
be a set of full measure, such that for any
x\in X^{\prime}
and any

we have that
\left(\mathbbm{1}_{B}(T^{n}x)-\mu(B)\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
,
\left(\mathbbm{1}_{A}(T^{n}x)-\mu(A)\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
\left(\mathbbm{1}_{T^{-h}(A)\cap B}(T^{n}x)-\mu(T^{-h}(A)\cap B)\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
are strongly mixing sequences. We note that if
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
is a strongly mixing sequence, then for any
(N_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
for which the limits in equation (

(3.6)

\lim_{N_{q}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}x_{n}=\lim_{h\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N_{q}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{q}}x_{n+h}\overline{1}=0.

In particular, for any
x\in X^{\prime}
, we have that

(3.7)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbbm{1}_{B}(T^{n}x)=\mu(B)

and

(3.8)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbbm{1}_{T^{-h}(A)\cap B}(T^{n}x)=\mu(T^{-h}(A)\cap B)

for every

. We now see that for any
x\in X^{\prime}

(3.9)

\lim_{h\rightarrow\infty}\mu(T^{-h}(A)\cap B)=\lim_{h\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathbbm{1}_{T^{-h}(A)\cap B}(T^{n}x)

4
Concluding Remarks

The definitions of weakly mixing sequences and strongly mixing sequences are quite cumbersome since we have to pass to a subsequence
(N_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
for which all of the relevant limits to exist. To circumvent this, we would like to propose definition
4.2
to replace the current definition of strongly mixing sequences.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of complex numbers for which

(4.1)

\overline{\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|x_{n}|<\infty.

(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a
supremely strongly mixing sequence
if for any other bounded sequence of complex numbers
(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
we have

(4.2)

\lim_{h\rightarrow\infty}\overline{\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}}|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}x_{n+h}\overline{y_{n}}|=0.

It is clear that any supremely strongly mixing sequence is a strongly mixing sequence, and it would be convenient if the two notions were equivalent, as supremely strongly mixing suquences are much simpler to work with. However, Lemma
4.2
shows us that there does not exist a supremely strongly mixing sequence.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is any sequence of complex numbers for which

(4.3)

\overline{\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|x_{n}|<\infty,

then there exists a bounded sequence of complex numbers
(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
for which

(4.4)

\overline{\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}}|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}x_{n+h}\overline{y_{n}}|=\overline{\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|x_{n}|,

for every
h\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.

Proof.
Let
(N_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be such that

(4.5)

\overline{\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|x_{n}|=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{k}}|x_{n}|.

By passing to a subsequence, we may assume without loss of generality that

(4.6)

\frac{1}{N_{k+1}}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N_{k}}|x_{n}|\right|<\frac{1}{k}.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0}
be any bijection. For
N_{f(m,h)}<n\leq N_{f(m,h)+1}
, let
y_{n}=\text{sgn}(x_{n+h})
. Now let
h\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
be arbitrary, and note that

(4.7)

\overline{\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|x_{n}|

(4.8)

\geq\overline{\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}}|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}x_{n+h}\overline{y_{n}}|\geq\overline{\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}}|\frac{1}{N_{f(m,h)+1}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{f(m,h)+1}}x_{n+h}\overline{y_{n}}|

(4.9)

=\overline{\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}}\frac{1}{N_{f(m,h)+1}}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N_{f(m,h)}}x_{n+h}\overline{y_{n}}+\sum_{n=N_{f(m,h)}+1}^{N_{f(m,h)+1}}|x_{n+h}|\right|

(4.10)

\geq\overline{\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}}\frac{1}{N_{f(m,h)+1}}\left(\sum_{n=N_{f(m,h)}+1}^{N_{f(m,h)+1}}|x_{n+h}|-|\sum_{n=1}^{N_{f(m,h)}}x_{n+h}\overline{y_{n}}|\right)

(4.11)

\geq\overline{\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}}\frac{1}{N_{f(m,h)+1}}\sum_{n=1}^{N_{f(m,h)+1}}|x_{n+h}|-\frac{2}{f(m,h)}=\overline{\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|x_{n}|.\qed

We will now show that Theorem
2.6

is the generalization of Theorem

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(X,\mathscr{B},\mu,T)
be a weakly mixing m.p.s. and let

satisfy

. For a.e.

\left(f(T^{n}x)\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}

Proof.
Let
\mathscr{A}

\mathscr{A}
is a countably generated
\sigma

is measurable. By Carathéodory’s Theorem (cf. Theorem 15.3.4 of
[
5

) there exists an isomorphism
\Phi
of

into

is the Lebesgue
\sigma

[0,1]
,

\mathscr{N}\subseteq\mathscr{L}
is the
\sigma

of
[
5
]
, there exists
\tilde{f}\in L^{1}([0,1],m)
be such that for every

\{\tilde{f}<q\}=\Phi(\{f<q\})
. By Proposition
15.6.19
of
[
5
]
, let

be a measurable transformation for which
\mu(\phi^{-1}(B)\triangle\Phi^{-1}(B))=0
for every

. We see that for every

, we have that
(\tilde{f}\circ\phi)^{-1}((-\infty,q))=f^{-1}((-\infty,q))
, so

a.e. by the uniqueness portion of Proposition

of
[
5
]
. Now note that
\Phi\circ T^{-1}\circ\Phi^{-1}
is a
\sigma

to itself, so yet another application of Proposition

of
[

yields a map

for which

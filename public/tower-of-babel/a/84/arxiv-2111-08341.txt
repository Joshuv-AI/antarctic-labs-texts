A GENERALIZATION OF SIMPLEST NUMBER FIELDS AND THEIR INTEGRAL BASIS
L. REMETE

Abstract
An integral basis of the simplest number fields of degree 3,4 and 6 over ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} are well-known, and widely investigated. We generalize the simplest number fields to any degree, and show that an integral basis of these fields is repeating periodically.

Introduction

Let
a,b,c,d\in{\mathbb{Q}}
and

\sigma:{\mathbb{C}}\mapsto{\mathbb{C}},\qquad\sigma:z\mapsto\frac{az+b}{cz+d}.

Assume, that
f(X)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]
is a polynomial with real roots, such that
\sigma
transitively permutes the roots of
f(X)
. In this case, if
\beta

is a totally real cyclic number field.
This requires, that the matrix

M=\left(\begin{array}[]{rr}a&b\\

is of finite order. It can be shown that each non-trivial torsion element of
PGL_{2}({\mathbb{Q}})
has order 2,3,4 or 6, whence, there exist such polynomials and number fields only of degrees 2,3,4 and 6.

Assume that
\beta
is a root of a simplest polynomial
f(X)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]
of degree
n
, and the Möbius transformation
\sigma
permutes its roots transitively.

Then the conjugates of
\beta
are:

\{\beta,\sigma(\beta),\sigma^{2}(\beta),\ldots,\sigma^{n-1}(\beta)\}.

Let
m
denote

m=\frac{\beta+\sigma(\beta)+\sigma^{2}(\beta)+\ldots+\sigma^{n-1}(\beta)}{n}.

If
n=3
or
n=6
, and

\sigma:{\mathbb{C}}\mapsto{\mathbb{C}},\qquad\sigma:z\mapsto\frac{az-1}{z+a+1}.

where
a=0
or
-1
for degree 3, and
a=1
or
-2
for degree 6, then we can write the minimal polynomial of
\beta
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
in the form:

f^{(3)}_{m}(X)=X^{3}-3mX^{2}-3(m+1)X-1,

f^{(6)}_{m}(X)=X^{6}-6mX^{5}-15(m+1)X^{4}-20X^{3}+15mX^{2}+6(m+1)X+1.

Therefore, if
m
is a rational parameter, then the polynomials above are cyclic, they have rational coefficients and

\sigma:z\mapsto\frac{az-1}{z+a+1}

permutes their roots transitively.

Although these polynomials have nice properties for any
m\in{\mathbb{Q}}
, we say that their root generates a simplest number field, if they are irreducible, and have integer coefficients.

In Section
2
we will generalize these polynomials to any degree. We keep the property that the coefficients are rational numbers, and a Möbius transformation

\sigma:z\mapsto\frac{\alpha z-1}{z+\alpha+1}

permutes the roots transitively, where
\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}
is an algebraic integer, such that

M=\left(\begin{array}[]{rr}\alpha&-1\\
1&\alpha+1\end{array}\right)\in PGL_{2}({\mathbb{C}}),

has finite order. In such way, we will obtain infinite parametric families of polynomials of degree
n
, such that their Galois groups are cyclic over
{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)
.

We get some well-known families as special cases.

Simplest cubic fields:

,

(c.f.
[

,

•

Family of polynomials of degree 12 investigated by A.Hoshi:

We call the number fields generated by a root of our polynomials

In Section

2
Generalized simplest number fields

In this section we define the parametric family
f^{(n)}_{m}
of polynomials of degrees
n>1
, such that there exists a Möbius transformation

\sigma:z\mapsto\frac{\alpha z-1}{z+\alpha+1},\qquad\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}

which permutes the roots of
f^{(n)}_{m}(X)
transitively for any parameter
m
.
All along this section let
m
be a rational parameter.

Let
g,h:{\mathbb{N}}\mapsto{\mathbb{Q}}
be defined by

g(i):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}1,&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 0\mod{6},\\
-m,&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 1\mod{6},\\
-m-1,&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 2\mod{6},\\
-1,&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 3\mod{6},\\
m,&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 4\mod{6},\\
m+1,&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 5\mod{6}.\end{array}\right.\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad h(i):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}0,&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 0\mod{6},\\
-1,&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 1\mod{6},\\
-1,&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 2\mod{6},\\
0,&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 3\mod{6},\\
1,&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 4\mod{6},\\
1,&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 5\mod{6}.\end{array}\right.

For
n\geq 0
, let

f^{(n)}_{m}(X):=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\cdot X^{i}\cdot g(n-i)\qquad\in{\mathbb{Q}}[m,X],

and

r^{(n)}(X):=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\cdot X^{i}\cdot h(n-i)\qquad\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X].

These polynomials satisfy the following recursion formulas:

Lemma 1
.

For
n\geq 0
,

(1)

f^{(n+1)}_{m}(X)=(X-m)\cdot f^{(n)}_{m}(X)+(m^{2}+m+1)\cdot r^{(n)}(X),

and

(2)

r^{(n+1)}(X)=(X+m+1)\cdot r^{(n)}(X)-f^{(n)}_{m}(X).

Proof.

We prove only (
2
), the other identity can be proved in the same way. We compare the coefficients of
X^{k}
in the two side of the equation.

The coefficient of
X^{k}
on the left hand side of (
2
) is

\binom{n+1}{k}\cdot h(n+1-k)

and on the right side of (
2
) is

\binom{n}{k-1}\cdot h(n+1-k)+(m+1)\cdot\binom{n}{k}\cdot h(n-k)-\binom{n}{k}\cdot g(n-k).

It is easy to check that for any remainder of
n-k\mod{6}
, these coefficients are the same.
∎

By the definition of
f^{(n)}_{m}
and
r^{(n)}
, it is easy to determine their derivatives.

Lemma 2
.

For
n\geq 0
,

\left(f^{(n+1)}_{m}\right)^{\prime}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\binom{n+1}{i}\cdot i\cdot X^{i-1}\cdot g(n+1-i)=(n+1)\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\binom{n}{i-1}\cdot X^{i-1}\cdot g(n+1-i)=

=(n+1)\cdot f^{(n)}_{m}(X),

and similarly

\left(r^{(n+1)}\right)^{\prime}(X)=(n+1)\cdot r^{(n)}(X).

Now we prove a nice variable transformation identity for
f^{(n)}_{m}(X)
.

Theorem 3
.

For any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
n\geq 1
,

(3)

(X+\alpha+1)^{n}\cdot f^{(n)}_{m}\left(\frac{\alpha X-1}{X+\alpha+1}\right)=f^{(n)}_{m}(\alpha)\cdot f^{(n)}_{m}(X)-(m^{2}+m+1)\cdot r^{(n)}(\alpha)\cdot r^{(n)}(X).

Proof.

If
n=1
, then
f^{(1)}_{m}(X)=X-m
,
r^{(1)}(X)=-1
, and

(X+\alpha+1)\cdot\left(\frac{\alpha X-1}{X+\alpha+1}-m\right)=(\alpha-m)\cdot(X-m)-(m^{2}+m+1)\cdot(-1)\cdot(-1).

Hence, (
3
) is true for
n=1
.

Assume, that (
3
) is true for
n
:

(4)

(X+\alpha+1)^{n}\cdot f^{(n)}_{m}\left(\frac{\alpha X-1}{X+\alpha+1}\right)=f^{(n)}_{m}(\alpha)\cdot f^{(n)}_{m}(X)-(m^{2}+m+1)\cdot r^{(n)}(\alpha)\cdot r^{(n)}(X).

Let

F_{n+1}(X):=\frac{f^{(n+1)}_{m}(\alpha)\cdot f^{(n+1)}_{m}(X)-(m^{2}+m+1)\cdot r^{(n+1)}(\alpha)\cdot r^{(n+1)}(X)}{(X+\alpha+1)^{n+1}}.

Using Lemma
2
, the derivative of
F_{n+1}
with respect to
X
is:

F^{\prime}_{n+1}(X)=\frac{n+1}{(X+\alpha+1)^{n+2}}\cdot\left[\left(f^{(n+1)}_{m}(\alpha)\cdot f^{(n)}_{m}(X)-(m^{2}+m+1)\cdot r^{(n+1)}(\alpha)\cdot r^{(n)}(X)\right)\cdot(X+\alpha+1)-\right.

\left.f^{(n+1)}_{m}(\alpha)\cdot f^{(n+1)}_{m}(X)+(m^{2}+m+1)\cdot r^{(n+1)}(\alpha)\cdot r^{(n+1)}(X)\right].

By Lemma
1
, substitute

r^{(n+1)}(X)=(X+m+1)\cdot r^{(n)}(X)-f^{(n)}_{m}(X),

f^{(n+1)}_{m}(X)=(X-m)\cdot f^{(n)}_{m}(X)+(m^{2}+m+1)\cdot r^{(n)}(X),

r^{(n+1)}(\alpha)=(\alpha+m+1)\cdot r^{(n)}(\alpha)-f^{(n)}_{m}(\alpha),

f^{(n+1)}_{m}(\alpha)=(\alpha-m)\cdot f^{(n)}_{m}(\alpha)+(m^{2}+m+1)\cdot r^{(n)}(\alpha).

After simplification we get

F^{\prime}_{n+1}(X)=\frac{n+1}{(X+\alpha+1)^{n+2}}\cdot(\alpha^{2}+\alpha+1)\cdot\left(f^{(n)}_{m}(X)\cdot f^{(n)}_{m}(\alpha)-(m^{2}+m+1)\cdot r^{(n)}(X)\cdot r^{(n)}(\alpha)\right).

The induction hypothesis (
4
) implies

F^{\prime}_{n+1}(X)=\frac{n+1}{(X+\alpha+1)^{n+2}}\cdot(\alpha^{2}+\alpha+1)\cdot(X+\alpha+1)^{n}\cdot f^{(n)}_{m}\left(\frac{\alpha X-1}{X+\alpha+1}\right)=

=\frac{n+1}{(X+\alpha+1)^{2}}\cdot(\alpha^{2}+\alpha+1)\cdot f^{(n)}_{m}\left(\frac{\alpha X-1}{X+\alpha+1}\right)=\left(f^{(n+1)}_{m}\right)^{\prime}\left(\frac{\alpha X-1}{X+\alpha+1}\right).

In the last step we applied Lemma
2
, and the derivation rules.
This shows, that

F_{n+1}(X)=f^{(n+1)}_{m}\left(\frac{\alpha X-1}{X+\alpha+1}\right)+c,

with an appropriate constant
c
.
We have to show that
c=0
. In order to prove this, multiply both sides of this equation by
(X+\alpha+1)^{n+1}
:

f^{(n+1)}_{m}(\alpha)\cdot f^{(n+1)}_{m}(X)-(m^{2}+m+1)\cdot r^{(n+1)}(\alpha)\cdot r^{(n+1)}(X)=

(X+\alpha+1)^{n+1}\cdot\left(f^{(n+1)}_{m}\left(\frac{\alpha X-1}{X+\alpha+1}\right)+c\right)

Thus, both sides are polynomials in
X
of degree
n+1
. The leading coefficient of
X
in the left hand side is equal to

f^{(n+1)}_{m}(\alpha)\cdot g(0)-(m^{2}+m+1)\cdot r^{(n+1)}(\alpha)\cdot h(0)=f^{(n+1)}_{m}(\alpha).

The right hand side is

(X+\alpha+1)^{n+1}\cdot\left(f^{(n+1)}_{m}\left(\frac{\alpha X-1}{X+\alpha+1}\right)+c\right)=

\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\binom{n+1}{i}\cdot(\alpha X-1)^{i}\cdot(X+\alpha+1)^{n+1-i}\cdot g(n+1-i)\right)+c(X+\alpha+1)^{n+1},

and the leading coefficient of
X
in this polynomial is equal to

\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\binom{n+1}{i}\cdot\alpha^{i}\cdot g(n+1-i)\right)+c=f^{(n+1)}_{m}(\alpha)+c.

The leading coefficients have to be the same, which implies that
c=0
, and therefore by mathematical induction, the equation (
3
) is true for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
∎

We will use the following interesting lemma about the periodic alternating multisection of sums of binomial coefficients.

Lemma 4
.

Let

p(i):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}a,&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 0\mod{6},\\
b,&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 1\mod{6},\\
c,&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 2\mod{6},\\
-a,&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 3\mod{6},\\
-b,&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 4\mod{6},\\
-c,&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 5\mod{6}.\end{array}\right.

with arbitrary complex numbers
a,b,c
, and let

t_{n}:=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\cdot p(i).

Then

t_{n+12}=3^{6}\cdot t_{n}.

Proof.

We will use the formula of multisections of sums of binomial coefficients (see e.g.
[
25
]
,
[
17
]
). Let
0\leq u<v
be integers, then

S_{n}(u,v):=\binom{n}{u}+\binom{n}{u+v}+\binom{n}{u+2v}+\ldots=\frac{1}{v}\sum_{i=0}^{v-1}\left(2\cos\frac{\pi i}{v}\right)^{n}\cdot\cos\frac{\pi(n-2u)i}{v}.

By this formula,

(5)

t_{n}=a\cdot(S_{n}(0,6)-S_{n}(3,6))+b\cdot(S_{n}(1,6)-S_{n}(4,6))+c\cdot(S_{n}(2,6)-S_{n}(5,6)).

The coefficient of
a
is:

S_{n}(0,6)-S_{n}(3,6)=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{i=0}^{5}\left(2\cos\frac{\pi i}{6}\right)^{n}\cdot\left(\cos\frac{\pi ni}{6}-\cos\frac{\pi(n-6)i}{6}\right)=

=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{i=0}^{5}\left(2\cos\frac{\pi i}{6}\right)^{n}\cdot\cos\frac{\pi ni}{6}\left(1-\cos\pi i\right).

For
i=3
,
\cos\frac{\pi i}{6}=0
;

for
i=0,2,4
,
\left(1-\cos\pi i\right)=0
;

for
i=1,5
,
\left(1-\cos\pi i\right)=2
, so

S_{n}(0,6)-S_{n}(3,6)=\frac{1}{3}\left(\left(2\cos\frac{\pi}{6}\right)^{n}\cdot\cos\frac{\pi n}{6}+\left(2\cos\frac{5\pi}{6}\right)^{n}\cdot\cos\frac{5\pi n}{6}\right)=

=\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{3}^{n}\left(\cos\frac{\pi n}{6}+(-1)^{n}\cdot\cos\frac{5\pi n}{6}\right).

Hence,

S_{n+12}(0,6)-S_{n+12}(3,6)=\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{3}^{n+12}\left(\cos\frac{\pi(n+12)}{6}+(-1)^{n+12}\cdot\cos\frac{5\pi(n+12)}{6}\right)=

=3^{6}\cdot(S_{n}(0,6)-S_{n}(3,6)).

Similarly:

S_{n+12}(1,6)-S_{n+12}(4,6)=3^{6}\cdot(S_{n}(1,6)-S_{n}(4,6)),

S_{n+12}(2,6)-S_{n+12}(5,6)=3^{6}\cdot(S_{n}(2,6)-S_{n}(5,6)).

Finally by (
5
)

t_{n+12}=3^{6}\cdot t_{n}.

∎

By this lemma, we are able to calculate
r^{(n)}(\varepsilon_{3})
, where
\varepsilon_{3}
is a primitive cube root of unity.

Since

r^{(n)}(\varepsilon_{3})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\cdot\varepsilon_{3}^{i}\cdot h(n-i),

we can use Lemma
4
with

p(i)=\varepsilon_{3}^{i}\cdot h(n-i).

It is easy to see, that
p(i)
is of the form we prescribed in Lemma
4
, since

p(i+3)=\varepsilon_{3}^{i+3}\cdot h(n-i-3)=-\varepsilon_{3}^{i}\cdot h(n-i)=-p(i).

Therefore we get

r^{(n+12)}(\varepsilon_{3})=3^{6}\cdot r^{(n)}(\varepsilon_{3}).

By calculating
r^{(n)}(\varepsilon_{3})
for
n=1,\ldots,12
, we get

(6)

r^{(n)}(\varepsilon_{3})=-(-I\sqrt{3})^{n-1}.

As
3^{6}=(-I\sqrt{3})^{12}
, (
6
) is true for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

An important consequence is that
\varepsilon_{3}
is not a root of
r^{(n)}(X)
.

In the following we show, that there is an interesting connection between the roots of
f_{m}^{(n)}(X)
and
r^{(n)}(X)
.

Corollary 5
.

Let
n\geq 2
. If
\beta
is a root of
f^{(n)}_{m}(X)
and
\alpha
is a root of
r^{(n)}(X)
, then

\frac{\alpha\beta-1}{\beta+\alpha+1}

is also a root of
f^{(n)}_{m}(X)
.

Proof.

First we show that if
\beta
is a root of
f^{(n)}_{m}(X)
and
\alpha
is a root of
r^{(n)}(X)
, then
\beta+\alpha+1\neq 0
.
We will use, that

(7)

r^{(n)}(X)=(-1)^{n-1}\cdot r^{(n)}(-X-1).

This is trivially true for
n=1
. Assume, that it is true for
n
, then

\left(r^{(n+1)}(X)\right)^{\prime}=(n+1)\cdot r^{(n)}(X)=(n+1)\cdot(-1)^{n-1}\cdot r^{(n)}(-X-1)=\left((-1)^{n}\cdot r^{(n+1)}(-X-1)\right)^{\prime}.

The leading coefficients of
r^{(n+1)}(X)
and
(-1)^{n}\cdot r^{(n+1)}(-X-1)
are the same, so (
7
) is true for any
n\geq 1
.

The second step is to show that
f^{(n)}_{m}(X)
and
r^{(n)}(X)
can not have a common root.
By Lemma
1
, if
\alpha
was a common root of
f^{(n)}_{m}(X)
and
r^{(n)}(X)
, and the determinant of

\left(\begin{array}[]{rr}\alpha-m&m^{2}+m+1\\
-1&\alpha+m+1\end{array}\right)

is not zero, then
\alpha
would also be a common root of
f^{(n-1)}_{m}(X)
and
r^{(n-1)}(X)
. However, the determinant of this matrix is
\alpha^{2}+\alpha+1
, which is not zero, since
\alpha
is a root of
r^{(n)}(X)
, and by (
6
), the cube roots of unity are not roots of
r^{(n)}(X)
.

Iteratively using this correspondence, we get, that
\alpha
is a common root of
f^{(1)}_{m}(X)
and
r^{(1)}(X)
, but
r^{(1)}(X)=-1
does not have any root, which is contradiction.

If
n\geq 2
and
\alpha
is a root of
r^{(n)}(X)
, then
-\alpha-1
is also a root of
r^{(n)}(X)
. Therefore, as
f^{(n)}_{m}(X)
and
r^{(n)}(X)
don’t have a common root,
\beta\neq-\alpha-1
, hence
\beta+\alpha+1\neq 0
.

Finally, by Theorem
3
:

(\beta+\alpha+1)^{n}\cdot f^{(n)}_{m}\left(\frac{\alpha\beta-1}{\beta+\alpha+1}\right)=f^{(n)}_{m}(\alpha)\cdot f^{(n)}_{m}(\beta)-(m^{2}+m+1)\cdot r^{(n)}(\alpha)\cdot r^{(n)}(\beta)=

=f^{(n)}_{m}(\alpha)\cdot 0-(m^{2}+m+1)\cdot 0\cdot r^{(n)}(\beta)=0,

whence

\frac{\alpha\beta-1}{\beta+\alpha+1}

is also a root of
f^{(n)}_{m}(X)
.
∎

Now we will prove, that if
n\geq 2
, then there exists a root
\alpha
of
r^{(n)}(X)
, such that the order of the matrix

M=\left(\begin{array}[]{rr}\alpha&-1\\
1&\alpha+1\end{array}\right)\in PGL_{2}({\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha))

is equal to
n
. Let
\varepsilon_{6}
be a primitive sixth root of unity, and
\varepsilon_{n}
be a primitive
n
-th root of unity.
Set

(8)

\alpha=\varepsilon_{6}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon_{6}+\varepsilon_{n}}{1-\varepsilon_{n}}.

First we show, that
\alpha
is a root of
r^{(n)}(X)
.
It is easy to see, that

(9)

\frac{\alpha+\varepsilon_{6}^{5}}{\alpha+\varepsilon_{6}}=\varepsilon_{n}.

Hence

\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(\alpha+\varepsilon_{6}^{5})^{n-1-i}(\alpha+\varepsilon_{6})^{i}=0,

and
\alpha+\varepsilon_{6}
is a root of

R^{(n)}(X):=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(X+\varepsilon_{6}^{5}-\varepsilon_{6})^{n-1-i}X^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(X-I\sqrt{3})^{n-1-i}X^{i}.

We will show that

R^{(n)}(X)=-r^{(n)}(X-\varepsilon_{6}).

For
k=0,\ldots,n-1
, the coefficient of
X^{k}
in
R^{(n)}(X)
is:

\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n-1-i}{k-i}\cdot(-I\sqrt{3})^{n-1-i-(k-i)}=(-I\sqrt{3})^{n-1-k}\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{n-1-i}{k-i}=

=(-I\sqrt{3})^{n-1-k}\cdot\binom{n}{k}.

The coefficient of
X^{k}
in
r^{(n)}(X-\varepsilon_{6})
is:

\sum_{i=k}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\cdot\binom{i}{k}\cdot(-\varepsilon_{6})^{i-k}\cdot h(n-i)=\sum_{i=k}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\cdot\binom{n-k}{i-k}\cdot(-\varepsilon_{6})^{i-k}\cdot h(n-i)=

=\binom{n}{k}\cdot\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\binom{n-k}{j}\cdot(-\varepsilon_{6})^{j}\cdot h(n-k-j)=\binom{n}{k}\cdot r_{n-k}(-\varepsilon_{6})=-\binom{n}{k}\cdot(-I\sqrt{3})^{n-k-1}.

In the last step we used equation (
6
), and that
-\varepsilon_{6}
is a primitive cube root of unity.
Since all coefficients of
R^{(n)}(X)
are the negative of the corresponding coefficients of
r^{(n)}(X-\varepsilon_{6})
, we obtain

R^{(n)}(X)=-r^{(n)}(X-\varepsilon_{6}).

Thus
\alpha+\varepsilon_{6}
is a root of
r^{(n)}(X-\varepsilon_{6})
, and therefore
\alpha
is a root of
r^{(n)}(X)
.

Due to the special form of
\alpha
, it is also easy to determine the order of

M=\left(\begin{array}[]{rr}\alpha&-1\\
1&\alpha+1\end{array}\right)\in PGL_{2}({\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)).

The eigenvalues of this matrix are
\alpha+\varepsilon_{6}
and
\alpha+\varepsilon_{6}^{5}
, and by (
9
) the quotient of the eigenvalues of
M
is
\varepsilon_{n}
, therefore
M\in PGL_{2}({\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha))
is a matrix of order
n
.

This allows us to determine the Galois group of
f^{(n)}_{m}(X)
over
{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
n\geq 2
. If
\alpha
is defined as in (
8
), and
m
is a rational number, such that
f^{(n)}_{m}(X)
is irreducible over
{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)
, then the Galois group of
f^{(n)}_{m}(X)
over
{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)
is the cyclic group of order
n
.

Proof.

By Corollary
5
, if
\beta
is a root of
f^{(n)}_{m}(X)
, then

\frac{\alpha\beta-1}{\beta+\alpha+1}

is also a root of
f^{(n)}_{m}(X)
. As the order of the matrix

M=\left(\begin{array}[]{rr}\alpha&-1\\
1&\alpha+1\end{array}\right)\in PGL_{2}({\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha))

is
n
,
\{\sigma^{i}(\beta)|i=0,\ldots,n-1\}
is the complete set of roots of
f^{(n)}_{m}(X)
, where

\sigma:z\mapsto\frac{\alpha z-1}{z+\alpha+1}.

Finally
\sigma^{i}(\beta)\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha,\beta)
for all
i=0,\ldots,n-1
, hence the splitting field of
f^{(n)}_{m}(X)
over
{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)
is
{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha,\beta)
. Therefore,
{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha,\beta)/{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)
, is a cyclic Galois extension of degree
n
.
∎

2.1
The discriminant of

In this section, we determine the discriminants of the polynomials

We will use the following properties of the resultant

(10)

\res(A,B)=(-1)^{nm}\cdot\res(B,A).

If

and

\res(A,B)=b_{m}^{n-r}\cdot\res(R,B).

For any monic polynomial

\res(AQ,B)=\res(A,B)\cdot\res(Q,B).

If

Lemma 7

For

the resultant of

and

with respect to

\res\left(f^{(n)}_{m}(X),f^{(n-1)}_{m}(X)\right)=(m^{2}+m+1)^{n-1}\cdot 3^{\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}}\cdot(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}.

Proof.

First we determine the resultant of

The remainder of

X,&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 1\mod{3},\\

Thus, we can write

G_{0}(i):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}1,&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 0\mod{3},\\
0,&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 1\mod{3},\\
-1,&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 2\mod{3}.\\
\end{array}\right.\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad G_{1}(i):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}0,&\mbox{ if }i\equiv 0\mod{3},\\

\end{array}\right.

By applying Lemma

\res\left(r^{(n+12)}(X),X^{2}+X+1\right)=\res\left(T_{n+12}(X),X^{2}+X+1\right)=

By calculating

for

The next step is to determine the resultant of
r^{(n)}(X)
and

.

By (

f^{(n)}_{m}(X)=-(X-m)\cdot r^{(n)}(X)+(X^{2}+X+1)\cdot r^{(n-1)}(X).

Therefore, by (

\res\left(r^{(n)}(X),f^{(n)}_{m}(X)\right)=(-1)^{n(n-1)}\cdot\res\left(f^{(n)}_{m}(X),r^{(n)}(X)\right)=

n^{0}\cdot\res\left((X^{2}+X+1)\cdot r^{(n-1)}(X),r^{(n)}(X)\right)=

\res\left(X^{2}+X+1,r^{(n)}(X)\right)\cdot\res\left(r^{(n-1)}(X),r^{(n)}(X)\right)=3^{n-1}\res\left(r^{(n-1)}(X),r^{(n)}(X)\right).

By (

2.2
Irreducibility of
f^{(n)}_{m}(X)

In this section, we will give a sufficient condition for the irreducibility of
f^{(n)}_{m}(X)
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. For the investigation of an integral basis of these polynomials, we will use exactly the same condition, and therefore we do not need the necessary condition of the irreducibility.

Here
v_{p}(x)
denotes the
p
-adic valuation of a rational number
x
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
n\geq 2
. If there exist a prime
p\neq 3
, such that
v_{p}(m^{2}+m+1)=1
, then
v_{p}\left(f^{(n)}_{m}(m)\right)=1
.

Proof.

By Lemma
1
, if
n\geq 2
then

r^{(n)}(m)=(2m+1)\cdot r^{(n-1)}(m)-f^{(n-1)}_{m}(m)

and

f^{(n)}_{m}(m)=(m^{2}+m+1)\cdot r^{(n-1)}(m),

thus

r^{(n)}(m)=(2m+1)\cdot r^{(n-1)}(m)-(m^{2}+m+1)\cdot r^{(n-2)}(m).

We have
v_{p}(2m+1)=0
, since
4(m^{2}+m+1)-(2m+1)^{2}=3
and
p\neq 3
.

Now assume that
v_{p}\left(r^{(n-1)}(m)\right)=v_{p}\left(r^{(n-2)}(m)\right)
, then

v_{p}\left((2m+1)\cdot r^{(n-1)}(m)\right)=0+v_{p}\left(r^{(n-1)}(m)\right),

v_{p}\left((m^{2}+m+1)\cdot r^{(n-2)}(m)\right)=1+v_{p}\left(r^{(n-1)}(m)\right),

whence

v_{p}\left(r^{(n)}(m)\right)=\min(v_{p}\left(r^{(n-1)}(m)\right),v_{p}\left(r^{(n-1)}(m)\right)+1)=v_{p}\left(r^{(n-1)}(m)\right).

Therefore,
v_{p}\left(r^{(1)}(m)\right)=v_{p}(-1)=0
and
v_{p}\left(r^{(2)}(m)\right)=v_{p}(-2m-1)=0
implies

v_{p}\left(r^{(n)}(m)\right)=0

and

v_{p}\left(f^{(n)}_{m}(m)\right)=v_{p}\left(m^{2}+m+1\right)+v_{p}\left(r^{(n-1)}(m)\right)=1.

∎

Corollary 10
.

Let
n\geq 2
. If there exist a prime
p\neq 3
, such that
v_{p}(m^{2}+m+1)=1
, then
f^{(n)}_{m}(X)
is irreducible over
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Proof.

The Taylor series of
f^{(n)}_{m}(X)
at
m
is:

f^{(n)}_{m}(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{\left({f^{(n)}_{m}}\right)^{(i)}(m)}{i!}\cdot(X-m)^{i}.

Thus, by shifting the polynomial by
m
:

f^{(n)}_{m}(X+m)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{\left({f^{(n)}_{m}}\right)^{(i)}(m)}{i!}\cdot X^{i}.

By Lemma
2

\left({f^{(n)}_{m}}\right)^{(i)}(X)=\frac{n!}{(n-i)!}\cdot f^{(n-i)}_{m}(X),

whence

f^{(n)}_{m}(X+m)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}\cdot f^{(n-i)}_{m}(m)\cdot X^{i}.

With
f^{(0)}_{m}(m)=1
and
f^{(1)}_{m}(m)=0
, Lemma
9
shows that if
n\geq 2
, then
f^{(n)}_{m}(X+m)
is Eisenstein with respect to
p
, so it is irreducible, and consequently
f^{(n)}_{m}(X)
is also irreducible.
∎

3
Periodically repeating integral bases

In this section we will prove, that an integral basis of the generalized simplest fields generated by a root of
f^{(n)}_{m}(X)

(n\geq 2)
is repeating periodically, and we give an upper bound for the period length
n_{0}
, in terms of
n
.

The periodic property of an integral basis makes sense if we investigate the integral bases of a parametric family of number fields of type
{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{t})
, where
\beta_{t}
is a root of a polynomial
f^{(n)}_{t}(X)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t][X]
of degree
n
, with integer parameter
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. In this case we determine an integral basis of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{t})
of the form

\left(h_{0}(\beta_{t}),h_{1}(\beta_{t}),h_{2}(\beta_{t}),\ldots,h_{n-1}(\beta_{t})\right),

where
h_{i}(X)\in{\mathbb{Q}}[X]
,
(i=0,\ldots,n-1)
, are polynomials with
\deg(h_{i}(X))=i
.

In order to construct a parametric family of polynomials with integer coefficients from
f_{m}^{(n)}
, let

•

m=t
, if
n\equiv 1,2\mod{3},

•

m=\frac{t}{3}
, if
n\equiv 0\mod{3}
.

It is easy to check, that for any
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, the generalized simplest polynomials

have integer coefficients. In the following we write
f^{(n)}_{t}(X)
, and it refers to the definition above.

In this case the modified results of the previous section are the following.

The discriminant of
f_{t}^{(n)}(X)
with respect to
X
is

(18)

D_{f_{t}^{(n)}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}3^{\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}}\cdot n^{n}\cdot(t^{2}+t+1)^{n-1},&$if $n\equiv 1,2\mod{3},\\

If
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
is an integer parameter, such that there exists a prime
p\neq 3
, for which

•

v_{p}(t^{2}+t+1)=1
, if
n\equiv 1,2\mod{3},

•

v_{p}(t^{2}+3t+9)=1
, if
n\equiv 0\mod{3},

then
f_{t}^{(n)}(X)
is irreducible over
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

\beta_{t}
be a root of

. We recall that an integral basis of the fields
{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{t})
is

, if for each residue class

, there exist polynomials

,

. (See

]
,
[

]

).

There is an equivalent definition of a periodically repeating integral basis which is more convenient to use in practice.

Lemma 11
.

Let
f_{t}^{(n)}(X)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t,X]
be a parametric family of polynomials, where
t
is an integer parameter such that
f_{t}^{(n)}(X)
is irreducible, and let
\beta_{t}
be a root of
f_{t}^{(n)}(X)
.

An integral basis of the fields
{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{t})
is repeating periodically modulo
n_{0}
, if and only if for any
k(X)\in{\mathbb{Q}}[X]
,
\deg(k(X))\leq n-1
, the following property is satisfied:

for any parameters

for which
f_{t}^{(n)}(X)
and

are irreducible and

,

k(\beta_{t})
is an algebraic integer if and only if
k(\beta_{s})
is an algebraic integer.

Proof.

First assume, that an integral basis of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{t})
is repeating periodically modulo
n_{0}
. Let
V=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}{\mathbb{Q}}X^{i}
. Then the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linear map
\varphi:X^{i}\mapsto\beta_{t}^{i}
gives an isomorphism of the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector spaces
V
and
{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{t})
for any
t\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Let
M
be the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module generated by

where

. If we restrict
\varphi
to

then we obtain an isomorphism of
M
and the ring of integers of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{t})
for any

. This means that if

, then for any

with
\deg(k(X))\leq n-1
,

is an algebraic integer if and only if

. Therefore if

, then
k(\beta_{t})
is an algebraic integer if and only if
k(\beta_{s})
is an algebraic integer.

On the other hand, assume that for any polynomial
k(X)\in{\mathbb{Q}}[X]
with
\deg(k(X))\leq n-1
,

is an algebraic integer if and only if
k(\beta_{s})
is an algebraic integer. Let

and

,
(i=0,\ldots n-1)
be polynomials, such that

\left(h^{(r)}_{0}(\beta_{t}),h^{(r)}_{1}(\beta_{t}),h^{(r)}_{2}(\beta_{t}),\ldots,h^{(r)}_{n-1}(\beta_{t})\right)

is an integral basis of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{t})
, where
\deg(h^{(r)}_{i}(X))=i
(one can use the Hermite normal form representation of any integral basis of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{t})
with respect to the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-basis
\left\{1,\beta_{t},\beta_{t}^{2},\ldots,\beta_{t}^{n-1}\right\}
to obtain such

h^{(r)}_{i}(\beta_{s})
, is algebraic integer for any

. We should prove, that the elements
h^{(r)}_{i}(\beta_{s})

(i=0,\ldots n-1)
form an integral basis of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{s})
.

Let
k(X)\in{\mathbb{Q}}[X]
,
\deg(k(X))\leq n-1
, be a polynomial, such that

is an algebraic integer. By our assumption

is also an algebraic integer, thus we can represent it in the integral basis

\left(h^{(r)}_{0}(\beta_{t}),h^{(r)}_{1}(\beta_{t}),h^{(r)}_{2}(\beta_{t}),\ldots,h^{(r)}_{n-1}(\beta_{t})\right)

with integer coefficients. Therefore we can write

By substituting

in place of

in the equation above, we obtain that we can represent

as an integer linear combination of the elements

,
(i=0,\ldots n-1)
. Therefore, if these elements are linearly independent over

, then

\left(h^{(r)}_{0}(\beta_{s}),h^{(r)}_{1}(\beta_{s}),h^{(r)}_{2}(\beta_{s}),\ldots,h^{(r)}_{n-1}(\beta_{s})\right)

is an integral basis of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{s})
, and an integral basis of the fields
{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{t})
is repeating periodically modulo
n_{0}
. However, it is trivial, since
\deg(h^{(r)}_{i}(X))=i
,
(i=0,\ldots n-1)
, and the minimal polynomial of

is the irreducible polynomial
f_{s}^{(n)}(X)
of degree
n
.
∎

3.1
Upper bound for the period length

In this section we prove, that if the 3-free part of
t^{2}+t+1
(or
t^{2}+3t+9
respectively) is square-free, then an integral basis of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{t})
is repeating periodically modulo
n_{0}
, where

n_{0}^{2}\mid\left(3^{\frac{n^{2}}{2}}\cdot n^{n}\right)^{n}.

First we show, that if the 3-free part of
t^{2}+t+1
(or
t^{2}+3t+9
respectively) is square-free, then the index
\ind(\beta_{t})
of
\beta_{t}
is independent of
t
. Therefore, for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
there exists an integer
C_{n}
, such that for any algebraic integer
\gamma_{t}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{t})
:

C_{n}\cdot\gamma_{t}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[\beta_{t}].

It means, that we can represent
\gamma_{t}
in the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-basis

\left\{1,\beta_{t},\beta_{t}^{2},\ldots,\beta_{t}^{n-1}\right\},

with rational coefficients having denominator
d\mid C_{n}
.

Finally, we prove that if such
C_{n}
exists, then an integral basis of these fields is repeating periodically modulo
n_{0}=C_{n}^{n}
.

The
index
of an algebraic integer
\beta_{t}
is defined by

\ind(\beta_{t}):=({\cal O}_{{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{t})}^{+}:{\mathbb{Z}}[\beta_{t}]^{+}),

where
{\cal O}_{{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{t})}
is the ring of integers of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{t})
. That is the index of the additive group of the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{Z}}[\beta_{t}]
in the additive group of
{\cal O}_{{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{t})}
. (for more details see for e.g. I.Gaál
[
5
]
, Section 1.2).

This is in fact, the product of the denominators of the elements of an integral basis of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{t})
, represented in the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-basis
\left(1,\beta_{t},\ldots,\beta_{t}^{n-1}\right)
.

If
D(\beta_{t})
denotes the discriminant of the minimal polynomial of
\beta_{t}
(which is equal to the discriminant of the basis

Lemma 12
.

(

Let

By this lemma, we can exclude certain prime divisors of the discriminant

Theorem 13
.

Let
t
be an integer and
\beta_{t}
be a root of
f^{(n)}_{t}(X)
. If
p\neq 3
is a prime, for which

v_{p}(t^{2}+t+1)=1,\qquad\mbox{ if }n\equiv 1,2\mod{3},

v_{p}(t^{2}+3t+9)=1,\qquad\mbox{ if }n\equiv 0\mod{3},

then
p\nmid\ind(\beta_{t})
.

Proof.

If
n\equiv 1
or
2\mod{3}
, then by Corollary
10
, the minimal polynomial of
\beta_{t}-t
is Eisenstein with respect to
p
, so by Lemma
12
,
p\nmid\ind(\beta_{t}-t)
. Since the index of an algebraic integer is invariant under translation, we obtain
p\nmid\ind(\beta_{t})
.

If
n\equiv 0\mod{3}
, then by Corollary
10
, the minimal polynomial of
3\beta_{t}-t
is Eisenstein with respect to
p
, so by Lemma
12
,
p\nmid\ind(3\beta_{t}-t)
, and therefore
p\nmid\ind(3\beta_{t})
. If
p\neq 3
, then
v_{p}(\ind(3\beta_{t}))=v_{p}(\ind(\beta_{t}))
, and thus we have
p\nmid\ind(\beta_{t})
in this case, too.
∎

By (
18
) and (
19
)

\ind(\beta_{t})^{2}\mid 3^{\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}}\cdot n^{n}\cdot(t^{2}+t+1)^{n-1},\qquad\mbox{ if }n\equiv 1,2\mod{3},

\ind(\beta_{t})^{2}\mid 3^{\frac{(n-1)(n-6)}{2}}\cdot n^{n}\cdot(t^{2}+3t+9)^{n-1},\qquad\mbox{ if }n\equiv 0\mod{3}.

Hence, by Theorem
13
, if the 3-free part of
t^{2}+t+1
(or
t^{2}+3t+9
, respectively) is square-free, then

\ind(\beta_{t})^{2}\mid 3^{\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}+v_{3}(t^{2}+t+1)}\cdot n^{n},\qquad\mbox{ if }n\equiv 1,2\mod{3},

\ind(\beta_{t})^{2}\mid 3^{\frac{(n-1)(n-6)}{2}+v_{3}(t^{2}+3t+9)}\cdot n^{n},\qquad\mbox{ if }n\equiv 0\mod{3}.

But it is easy to check, that for any
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
v_{3}(t^{2}+t+1)\leq 1
and
v_{3}(t^{2}+3t+9)\leq 3
.
Thus we get the following corollary.

Corollary 14
.

Let
\beta_{t}
be a root of
f^{(n)}_{t}(X)
, and
C_{n}
be the greatest integer, such that

C_{n}^{2}\mid 3^{\frac{n^{2}-3n+4}{2}}\cdot n^{n},\qquad\mbox{ if }n\equiv 1,2\mod{3},

C_{n}^{2}\mid 3^{\frac{n^{2}-7n+12}{2}}\cdot n^{n},\qquad\mbox{ if }n\equiv 0\mod{3}.

If
t^{2}+t+1
(or
t^{2}+3t+9
, respectively) is square-free, then for any algebraic integer
\gamma_{t}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{t})
,

C_{n}\cdot\gamma_{t}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[\beta_{t}].

Therefore, in both cases

C_{n}^{2}\mid 3^{\frac{n^{2}}{2}}\cdot n^{n}.

Following similar approach as in Theorem 2 of
[
7
]
, we show, that it implies that an integral basis of these fields is repeating periodically.

Lemma 15
.

Let
\beta_{t}
be a root of
f_{t}^{(n)}(X)
. If there exists an integer
C_{n}
, such that for any algebraic integer
\gamma_{t}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{t})
,

C_{n}\cdot\gamma_{t}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[\beta_{t}],

then an integral basis of the fields
{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{t})
is repeating periodically modulo
n_{0}=C_{n}^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma_{t}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{t})
be an algebraic integer and represent it the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-basis
\left\{1,\beta_{t},\beta_{t}^{2},\ldots,\beta_{t}^{n-1}\right\}
:

\gamma_{t}=\frac{a_{0}+a_{1}\beta_{t}+a_{2}\beta_{t}^{2}+\ldots+a_{n-1}\beta_{t}^{n-1}}{d},\qquad a_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}},d\mid C_{n}.

Denote the conjugates of
\gamma_{t}
by
\gamma_{t}^{(j)}
,
j=1,\ldots,n
. The defining polynomial of
\gamma_{t}
is

\prod_{j=1}^{n}(X-\gamma_{t}^{(j)})=\frac{1}{d^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(dX-a_{0}-a_{1}\beta_{t}^{(j)}-a_{2}(\beta_{t}^{(j)})^{2}-\ldots-a_{n-1}(\beta_{t}^{(j)})^{n-1}).

The product is a symmetric polynomial of
\beta_{t}^{(1)},\beta_{t}^{(2)},\ldots,\beta_{t}^{(n)}
, hence its coefficients can be expressed as integer polynomials of the coefficients of the defining polynomial of
\beta_{t}
, that is
f_{t}^{(n)}(X)
. The coefficients of
f_{t}^{(n)}(X)
are integer polynomials of
t
, hence there exist polynomials
P_{0},\ldots,P_{n-1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]
, such that

\prod_{j=1}^{n}(X-\gamma_{t}^{(j)})=\frac{1}{d^{n}}\left(d^{n}X^{n}+P_{n-1}(t)(dX)^{n-1}+\ldots+P_{1}(t)(dX)+P_{0}(t)\right).

Therefore the element
\gamma_{t}
is an algebraic integer if
d^{n}\mid d^{j}P_{j}(t)
for all
j=1,\ldots,n-1
.

Now let
s\in{\mathbb{Z}}
be an integer, such that
C_{n}^{n}\mid(t-s)
. Then
C_{n}^{n}\mid P_{j}(t)-P_{j}(s)
, and therefore

d^{n}\mid d^{j}P_{j}(t)
if and only if
d^{n}\mid d^{j}P_{j}(s)
.
It implies that for any
k(X)\in{\mathbb{Q}}[X]
,
(\deg(k(X))\leq n-1)
,
\gamma_{t}=k(\beta_{t})
is algebraic integer if and only if
\gamma_{s}=k(\beta_{s})
is an algebraic integer, thus by Lemma
11
an integral basis of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\beta_{t})
is repeating periodically modulo
n_{0}=C_{n}^{n}
.
∎

By Corollary
14
and Lemma

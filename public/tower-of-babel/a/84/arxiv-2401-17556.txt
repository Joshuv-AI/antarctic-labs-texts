Model-Theoretic Logic for Mathematical Theory of Semantic Information and Communication
Ahmet Faruk Saz ∗ , Siheng Xiong + , Yashas Malur Saidutta, and Faramarz Fekri

Abstract
THIS PAPER IS ELIGIBLE FOR THE STUDENT PAPER AWARD. In this paper, we propose an advancement to Tarskian model-theoretic semantics, leading to a unified quantitative theory of semantic information and communication. We start with description of inductive logic and probabilities, which serve as notable tools in development of the proposed theory. Then, we identify two disparate kinds of uncertainty in semantic communication, that of physical and content, present refined interpretations of semantic information measures, and conclude with proposing a new measure for semantic content-information and entropy. Our proposition standardizes semantic information across different universes and systems, hence bringing measurability and comparability into semantic communication. We then proceed with introducing conditional and mutual semantic cont-information measures and point out to their utility in formulating practical and optimizable lossless and lossy semantic compression objectives. Finally, we experimentally demonstrate the value of our theoretical propositions.

Before we move on to explain how to calculate inductive probabilities, a chief point to clarify regards to their interpretation. Inductive probability, as initially posited by Carnap, aims to enclose the degree of support evidence

The last cardinal implication we wish to mention relates to Bayesian aspect of (

In such circumstances, probability of an event is equal to fraction of possibilities an event occurs to total number of possibilities. This is reflected in (

IV
Formulation of Semantic Information

Weaver defined semantic communication as "How precisely do the transmitted symbols convey the desired meaning?". The subtlety with his definition is to realize that it presents two different components of semantic communication, namely ’desires/intentions’ and ’meaning’, on the same plate. The former relates to
personal and psychological aspects
of semantic communication whereas the latter is about
quasi-objective semantic concepts
. In our paradigm, we consider a universe being composed of mutually exclusive, jointly exhaustive, and fully descriptive objects of meaning, whose relationship to one another is expressed via FOL. From transmission of semantic information, we understand the transmission of these objects, leaving personal and psychological aspects out.

IV-A

Physical Transmission of Semantic Objects

In semantic transmission, there exists two kinds of uncertainty. The former relates to physical aspects of transmission of -aforementioned-
semantic objects
whereas the latter relates to their actual content, meaning. In the former kind of transmission, given an arbitrary semantic alphabet
\mathcal{A}
used to describe the universe
\mathcal{U}
, the entropy of physical transmission of semantic objects can be characterized in terms of the per-semantic-symbol entropy of a source that transmits them. Within the context of present discussion, the source is assumed to contain either partial or complete information about universe
\mathcal{U}
; and if partial, it’s knowledge can either be static, i.e., evidence
e
is fixed and does not change over time (such as information of a book or a movie) or dynamic, i.e., evidence
e
increases in time with more observations. Then, per-symbol source entropy can be measured in an identical manner to Shannon entropy, where statistical probabilities are replaced with degree of confirmation, yielding average Carnapian
inf
entropy:

H_{\text{inf}}(\mathcal{U})=\sum_{i}c(A_{i},e)\times log(\text{inf}(c(A_{i},e)))

(8)

where
\bigcup_{i}A_{i}=\mathcal{A}
and
\bigcup
being the set union. In the context of our running example, if each symbol in
\mathcal{A}
is assumed to be a triplet, i.e.,
A_{1}=P_{1}\text{(Alice, Apple)}
,
A_{2}=P_{2}\text{(Alice, Bob)}
etc. then entropy of the source is characterized through degree of confirmations of set of all possible triples. On the other hand, if each symbol
A_{i}
is taken as a complete state description, then entropy of the source is characterized through degree of confirmations on the state-space. An important consideration regarding (
8
) is that, the quality of inductive probabilities depend on the number of available samples (i.e.,
|e|
). Furthermore, even if
|e|\gg 0
, size of alphabet
|\mathcal{A}
| and the actual distribution of
c(A_{i},e)\text{ for }i\in\mathcal{A}
, depends strictly on engineer’s choice of how to represent the source (i.e., universe
\mathcal{U}
), what kind of alphabet
\mathcal{A}
to use, and the real-life application under consideration. Hence, per-symbol entropy
H_{\text{inf}}(\mathcal{U})
could be larger or smaller than the per-symbol entropy of the same source’s classical Shannon representation as also suggested in seminal paper
[
8
]
. The unifying point, however, is that irrespective of choice of
\mathcal{A}
, as
|e|\rightarrow\infty
, the inductive distribution will approach to true distribution.

IV-B

Semantic Content-Information Measure

The latter kind of uncertainty pertains that of content. Semantic communication, when considered from the perspective of receiver, pertains to what use the content of the message has for the receiver. In the absence of any downstream task, receiver’s interest can broadly be understood in terms of reducing its uncertainty regarding the content of information present at source about universe
\mathcal{U}
. Then, we can characterize average semantic content-information as:

Proposition 1 (Average Semantic Content Information of Source).

Assume a universe
\mathcal{U}
where cardinality of set of predicates is
|\mathcal{P}|=p
and cardinality of set of entities is
|\mathcal{E}|=e
. Given that Carnapian content-information measure is denoted as cont(
\cdot
) and a suitable disjoint partitioning of universe
\mathcal{U}
such that
\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{U}}\mathcal{U}_{i}\equiv\mathcal{U}
, where
|\mathcal{U}|=2^{p\times e}
is formed. Then, if a set of FOL expressions
\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{U}}m_{i}\equiv\mathcal{M}
where
m_{i}=m_{j}
iff
i=j
is given such that each
m_{i}
has 1-to-1 correspondence to
\mathcal{U}_{i}
expressing its content and it yields a degree of confirmation
c(m_{i},e)
under the evidence
e
present at semantic source, the semantic content information of the source regarding
\mathcal{U}
is formulated by the equation:

H_{\text{cont}}(\textbf{m},p,e)=\sum_{i}c(m_{i},e)\times 2^{p\times e}\times cont(c(m_{i},e))

(9)

where for a finite Carnapian universe
\mathcal{M}
can be the set of state descriptions and in an infinite Hintikkan universe,
\mathcal{M}
can be the set of constituents.

Corollary 1.

Given Proposition
1
, average semantic content information of the source per partition (i.e., per-state-description, per-constituent etc.) is equal to
H_{\text{cont}}(\textbf{m},p,e)/2^{p\times e}
.

We refer reader to appendix for our axioms, derivation and discussion of
cont-entropy

This metric not only characterizes how much, on average, the uncertainty regarding the state of the world is reduced at receiver after semantic communication, but is also useful in characterizing, comparing, and contrasting the content-informativeness of rival semantic information sources (i.e., text data, visual data, knowledge bases, RDF stores etc.) described with possibly different ontologies.

One striking issue that has prevented development of theoretical semantic communication, as presented by Carnap, has been the issue of additivity in absence of L-exclusivity. This was the primary reason behind the duality of Carnapian semantic information measures
inf
and
cont
(refer to appendix for definitions and discussion). We however, proposed a different understanding and use for
inf

cont

Hence, we next present definitions of conditional content-entropy and mutual content-information that are able to handle consequent transmissions that are not L-exclusive or additive. Given two particular FOL messages to be transmitted
in succession
by sender, namely

and
s_{2}
, content-information at receiver can be characterized as:

\text{cont}(s_{2}|s_{1})=\text{cont}(s_{2}\land s_{1})-\text{cont}(s_{1})

(10)

\text{transcont}(s_{2}|s_{1})=\text{cont}(s_{2})-\text{cont}(s_{2}|s_{1})=1-c(s_{2}\lor s_{1},e)

(11)

Then, on average, for an arbitrary message
s
transmitted
after

H_{\text{cont}}(\textbf{s}|\textbf{r})=\sum_{i,j}p(s_{i},r_{j})\times 2^{|\mathcal{P}|\times|\mathcal{E}|}\times\text{cont}(s_{i}|r_{j})

(12)

I_{\text{cont}}(\textbf{s};\textbf{r})=\sum_{i,j}p(s_{i},r_{j})\times 2^{|\mathcal{P}|\times|\mathcal{E}|}\times\text{transcont}(s_{i},r_{j})

(13)

Conditional cont-information entropy as in (

\textbf{m}=\textbf{s}\lor\textbf{r}

IV-C

Lossy Content-Semantic Compression

Theorem 1 (Lossy Content-Semantic Compression).

Given a semantic information source in a universe

\min_{p(\hat{s}|s)}I_{shn}(s;\hat{s})\text{ subject to }I_{cont}(s;\hat{s})\geq d^{*}

where

B-A

Information Measures, L-Exclusivity, Additivity and Inductive Independence

Carnap and Bar-Hillel, in their seminal paper, proposed a quantitative way of measuring semantic information in a FOL monadic (one-place) predicate language. They adopt a Tarskian model-theoretic view of a finite universe where all possible state of affairs can be expressed with a suitably chosen disjunction of
state descriptions
. Each state description is sentence in the language that entirely and uniquely specifies a possible state of universe for all individuals with respect to all predicates. Carnap and Bar-Hillel then
inductively
assign a probability distribution
\mathcal{M}_{c}
, based on observations
\mathcal{E}_{o}
, over the set of state descriptions and use it to compute semantic information. They propose two metrics of semantic information, namely
cont(m)
and
inf(m)
based on inductive probabilities. Given a sentence
m
with inductive probability
p(m)
, these metrics are defined as:

cont(m)=1-p(m)

(33)

inf(m)=\frac{1}{1-cont(m)}=\frac{1}{p(m)}

(34)

The intuition behind the first measure is to assign greater information to a sentence
m
that is less likely to be true, i.e., the set of models (state descriptions)
m
is comprised of are less likely to be true. However, Bar-Hillel and Carnap assert that
cont(m)
cannot be the only quantitative measure of semantic information as it does not satisfy additivity property unless sentences are
L-exclusive
. Then, another measure
inf(s)
is necessary for sentences that are
inductively independent
. These concepts are defined as:

Definition 1 (L-Exclusion).

Two sentences
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are L-exclusive iff propositions
K_{1}

\equiv

(\neg m_{1})\nvDash(\neg m_{2})
,
K_{2}

\equiv

(\neg m_{2})\nvDash(\neg m_{1})
, and
K_{1}\land K_{2}

\equiv
1 holds.

Definition 2 (Inductive Independence).

Given probability measure
\mathcal{M}
over sentences in a language
\mathcal{L}
,
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are inductively independent iff
p(m_{1}\land m_{2})=p(m_{1})\times p(m_{2})

\implies p(m_{1}|m_{2})=p(m_{1})\text{ and }p(m_{2}|m_{1})=p(m_{2})
holds.

Definition 3 (Additivity Condition).

Given an information metric
info(.)
over sentences in a
\mathcal{L}
and let
\{m_{1}
,
m_{2}\}
be two sentences in
\mathcal{L}
, if
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are inductively independent, then
\textit{info}(m_{1}\land m_{2})=\textit{info}(m_{1})+\textit{info}(m_{2})
must hold.

L-exclusion is most easily understood if one considers each possible state of the universe to be a unique element in a universal set where
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are two set-theoretic propositions over them. Then, L-exclusion only holds if the intersection of
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
is empty. It is natural to wonder as to why such distinction, and hence two separate measures were deemed necessary by Carnap and Bar-Hillel. Let’s now illustrate this through a toy example.

Assume, for simplicity, we are only interested in a small universe
\mathcal{U^{\prime}}
with a single person,
\{\textit{Alice}\}
, two entities,
\{\textit{car, house}\}
, and a single predicate
\{\textit{Has(x, y)}\}
is present. In
\mathcal{U^{\prime}}
, observing
m_{1}
=
Has(Alice, car)

\equiv

Has(Alice, car)
\land
[Has(Alice, house)
\lor
(
\neg
Has(Alice, house))
, which has inductive logical probability of
p(m_{1})=

0.5
, yields information value of
\text{cont}(m_{1})=1-0.5=0.5
. Then, assume one subsequently observes
m_{2}
=
Has(Alice, house)

\equiv

Has(Alice, house)
\land
[Has(Alice, car)
\lor
(
\neg
Has(Alice, car))
, which also has a logical probability of
p(m_{2})=

0.5
. Then, the total information one gains up to that point is
\text{cont}(m_{1}\land m_{2})=1-0.25=0.75
. In turn, this implies observing
m_{1}
helps one gain semantic information in the amount
0.5
whereas observing
m_{2}
on top of that contributes an additional information of
0.25
, totaling to a
cont
value of
0.75
. This, in other words, would imply that the semantic information
m_{2}
provided on top of
m_{1}
is halved. Let us keep this result in mind for a moment.

Carnap asserts that any two primitive predicates that are inductively independent are by construction irrelevant (i.e., carry no logical implication relationship in between). Then, as expected,
p(m_{1}\land m_{2})=p(m_{1})\times p(m_{2})=0.5\times 0.5=0.25
holds. Here, the dilemma is that, how can observing two things that are inductively independent have differing amounts of information? Why would observing that
m_{1}
, which is inductively independent from
m_{2}
, be more or less informative than
m_{2}
? Therefore, Carnap suggests, if two sentences
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are inductively independent, the information they convey together should be equal to the sum of information they convey separately, which is what is called the additivity condition. This condition does not hold for
cont
and hence Carnap and Bar-Hillel concludes there must be not one but at least two different metrics of semantic information, where

is valid in cases where sentences are L-exclusive, and

valid when sentences are inductively independent.

Appendix C
Derivation and Discussion of Cont-Entropy

Fundamental motivation behind a unified theory of quantitative information and communication is to be able to study the properties of rival communication systems and measure how much information is transmitted after communication takes place. Before Shannon, the problem was considered as deterministic signal reconstruction. Initially, Hartley asserted one needed an anchor to quantitatively define information. That anchor shall ignore the psychological aspects of communication and focus merely on physical. Then, for any physical system, Hartley posited, these anchors should be
the number of transmissions
and
number of available symbols
at each transmission. Shannon conjoined Hartley’s insights with the notion that there exists
statistical imbalance between likelihoods of different symbols
,
separated the channel noise from communication system design
and
modelled the sources probabilistically
. Further, based on Nyquist’s conclusion that capacitors of the time were best suited to represent two levels of voltage and came up with B(inary Dig)it as unit of information. The reason behind the recap of Shannon and Hartley’s well-known work was to bring reader’s attention to how Shannon and Hartley approached the problems of physical communication and quantification of information.

Weaver defines semantic communication as "How precisely do the transmitted symbols convey the desired meaning?". It is our view that subtlety with his definition is in realizing that it presents two unassociated components of semantic communication, namely ’desires/intentions’ and ’meaning’, on the same plate. The former relates to
personal and psychological aspects
of semantic communication whereas the latter is about
quasi-objective semantic concepts
. In our paradigm, we consider a universe being composed of mutually exclusive, jointly exhaustive, and fully descriptive objects of meaning, whose relationship to one another is expressed via FOL. From transmission of semantic information, we understand the transmission of these objects, leaving subjective interpretations of these concepts out. In our framework, we use the set entities of
\mathcal{E}
in FOL language
\mathcal{L}
to represent these
semantic concepts
and express their interrelationships via the set of dyadic predicates
\mathcal{P}
. Then, each semantic triplet of the form
P_{i}(x,y)
becomes a
semantic object
of transmission. Characterization of intentions, desires (and consequent
subjective interpretations
of these semantic objects) is beyond the scope of this paper and is left as future work.

Furthermore, as explained above for physical communication, in order to come up with a quantitative framework of semantic information and communication, one needs suitable anchor(s) for representing semantics. In Tarskian model-theoretic view of semantic information, knowledge and truth, one can reasonably take number of possible state descriptions eliminated as a result of a transmission to characterize the so-called
content-information of messages
.

In order to get a better comprehension this last statement, consider the a toy universe
\mathcal{U}_{1}
where there exists a single entity,
\mathcal{E}_{1}=\{\textit{Alice}\}
, and two monadic predicates,
\mathcal{P}_{1}=\{\textit{HasHouse(x), HasCar(x)}\}
are present. Then, the state space is
\mathcal{S}\mathcal{S}=\{Q_{1}=\textit{HasHouse(Alice)}\land\textit{HasCar(Alice)}

\mathcal{U}_{1}

m_{2}=\{\textit{HasHouse(Alice)}\}

m_{3}=\{\textit{HasHouse(Alice)}\land\textit{HasCar(Alice)}\}

m_{1}
conveys the information that state description

m_{2}
conveys the information that state descriptions
Q_{2}
and
Q_{4}
are false hence eliminating two possible state descriptions, and message
m_{3}
conveys the information that state descriptions
Q_{2}

Q_{3}

Q_{4}

is
Q_{1}

m_{1}

m_{3}

Therefore, the reduction in uncertainty regarding the state of the world is proportional to number of state descriptions the content of a message eliminates, hence it shall be proportional to the amount of semantic cont-information it conveys. This leads us to our first axiom for a quantitative measure of semantic information:

Axiom 1 (Linearity in Elimination).

Given a uniform prior and uniform inductive probability distribution over state space
\mathcal{SS}
in a universe
\mathcal{U}

measures the fraction of state descriptions eliminated if a message

H(\textbf{m})\propto cont(\textbf{m})\implies H(\textbf{m})=K_{1}\times cont(\textbf{m})

(35)

where

exponential

\mathcal{U}_{3}

\mathcal{U}_{1}

\mathcal{P}_{1}=\mathcal{P}_{3}=\{\textit{HasHouse(x), HasCar(x)}\}

H(p,e)
is a semantic cont-information metric.

The axiom of Exponentiation of Granularity provides a useful anchor in comparing and contrasting the content-informativeness of distinct universes. In other terms, it is frequently the case that same semantic source is described via different ontologies and at varying granularities in distinct universes. Such differences are natural and can be thought of discrete quantizations of the continuous space of semantics. Then, it is the
volume
of a universe that determines its cont-informativeness, as presented by next axiom:

Axiom 3 (Equality of Volumes).

Given two universes
\mathcal{U}_{i}
and
\mathcal{U}_{j}
, they are equally cont-informative if they have the same volume, i.e.,

2^{p_{i}\times e_{i}}=2^{p_{j}\times e_{j}}\implies H(p_{i},e_{i})=H(p_{j},e_{j})

(37)

where
I(p,e)
is a semantic cont-information metric.

Combining the first three axioms, we are now ready to present the following axiom on quantification of semantic cont-information in a message
m
,

Axiom 4 (Characterization of Cont-Information).

Given a universe
\mathcal{U}
, where cardinality of set of predicates
|\mathcal{P}|=p
and cardinality of set of entities
|\mathcal{E}|=e
and Carnap’s cont measure denoted as cont(
\cdot
), the cont-information of a message
m
in this universe can be characterized as:

H\propto 2^{p\times e}\times cont(\textbf{m})\implies H(\textbf{m},p,e)=K^{\prime}\times 2^{p\times e}\times cont(\textbf{m})

(38)

where
H(\textbf{m},p,e)
is a semantic cont-information metric and
K^{\prime}
is an arbitrary constant that is same for all semantic systems.

As
K^{\prime}
is arbitrary, constant, and same for all systems, we can drop it. Finally, as the nature of communication is in itself probabilistic and not deterministic, we do not know which message
m
will be transmitted by the source, and hence quantifying the
average
uncertainty (i.e., average cont-informativeness) makes natural sense. The apparent problem in this line of thinking is the potential of overlap between semantic messages, and lack of additivity property in
cont
measure unless the messages are
L-exclusive
. This is, however, not a big concern in Tarskian model theoretic view as there exists multiple sets (i.e., sets of constituents, attributive constituents or state descriptions) whose members form disjoint partitioning of the universe.

Another important point in characterization of semantic information is that Axiom 1 assumes a uniform prior and uniform distribution over the disjoint partitioning. This is, however, not an accurate assumption in practice as observations (i.e., evidence) made over time changes one’s posterior beliefs about what the true state of the world is, i.e., some states become more likely to be the true state whereas some others turn out less favorable by evidence. Then, the content informativeness of a source, i.e., on average how much the receiver’s uncertainty regarding the state of the world would be reduced -on average- if semantic communication were to take place also depend on source statistics, in this case being inductive probabilities of messages associated with that source. Hence, we propose to define average semantic cont-information, a.k.a., semantic cont-entropy of a source as:

Proposition 2 (Average Semantic Content Information).

Given a universe
\mathcal{U}
where cardinality of set of predicates is
|\mathcal{P}|=p
, cardinality of set of entities is
|\mathcal{E}|=e
, Carnap’s cont measure is denoted as cont(
\cdot
), a set of messages
\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{M}}m_{i}
form a disjoint partitioning of
\mathcal{U}
, and
p(\cdot)
is an inductive probability distribution over set
\mathcal{M}
. Then, the average cont-entropy of a semantic source is defined as:

H_{\text{cont}}(\textbf{m},p,e)=\sum_{i}p(m_{i})\times 2^{p\times e}\times cont(m_{i})

(39)

Corollary 3.

Given Proposition
2
, average semantic content information of the source per partition (i.e., per-state-description, per-constituent etc.) is equal to
H_{\text{cont}}(\textbf{m},p,e)/2^{p\times e}

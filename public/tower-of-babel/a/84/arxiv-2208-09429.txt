Superconductivity of Cs 3 C 60 at atmosphere pressure
Di Peng 1, Ren-Shu Wang 2,1, Li-Na Zong 1 & Xiao-Jia Chen 1,2∗

Abstract
Pressure as a clean and efficient tool can bring about unexpected extraordinary physical and chemical properties of matters [ 1 ] . The recent discoveries of superconductivity at nearly room temperature in hydrides [ 3 , 2 ] highlight the power of pressure in this aspect. Capturing such high critical temperature ( T c T_{c} ) superconductivity at atmosphere pressure for the technological applications is highly desired. The large-scale growth of diamond through the chemical vapor deposition [ 4 ] away from the usual high-pressure and high-temperature conditions [ 5 ] fuels such a hope. Similar to hydrides, Cs-doped C 60 [ 6 , 7 , 8 , 9 ] was also found to exhibit superconductivity by the application of pressure with a comparable T c T_{c} of 40 K as MgB 2 [ 10 ] . Here, we report the successful realization of superconductivity in Cs-doped C 60 at atmosphere pressure. The phase is characterized to have the primitive cubic structure in the space group of P ​ a ​ 3 ¯ Pa\overline{3} with the stoichiometry of Cs 3 C 60 . The superconductivity is evidenced from the observations of both the Meissner effect and zero-resistance state. Although the pressure effects on superconductivity are different for the newly discovered Cs 3 C 60 compared to the known two phases with the face-centered-cubic ( f ​ c ​ c fcc ) and A15 structure [ 7 , 8 , 9 ] , the evolution of T c T_{c} with the volume occupied per C 3 − 60 {}_{60}^{3-} anion for all these superconductors follows the same universal trend, suggesting the same pairing mechanism of the superconductivity. Such a trend together with the nearly linear T c T_{c} vs the lattice constant in the structure with smaller unit-cell volumes and the neighbouring antiferromagnetic state in the structure with larger unit-cell volumes invites the electron-phonon coupling and the electron correlations together to account for the superconductivity in Cs 3 C 60 . The present results and findings suggest a new route to capturing the superconductivity which takes place at high pressures to atmosphere pressure environment.

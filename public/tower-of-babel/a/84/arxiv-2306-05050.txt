Sparse Posets and Pebble Game Algorithms
Signe Lundqvist, Tovohery Randrianarisoa, Klara Stokes

Abstract
We generalize a sparsity condition for hypergraphs and show a result relating sparseness of hypergraphs to the decomposition of a modified incidence graph into edge-disjoint spanning forests. We also give new sparsity conditions for posets, and define an algorithm of pebble game type for recognising this sparsity.



1
Introduction

A graph
G=(V,E)
is
(k,l)
-sparse
if
|E^{\prime}|\leq k|V(E^{\prime})|-l
for all subsets
E^{\prime}\subseteq E
, and
(k,l)
-tight
if it is
(k,l)
-sparse and
|E|=k|V|-l
. It is well-known that a graph is generically rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
if and only if
G
has a
(2,3)
-tight spanning subgraph
[
7
,
11
]
. There are several algorithms for finding
(2,3)
-tight spanning subgraphs, one of which is the pebble game algorithm, which was first introduced by Jacobs and Hendrickson
[
6
]
.

An incidence geometry of rank
2
(a.k.a. a hypergraph) is a triple
(P,L,I)
, where
P
is a set of ‘points’,
L
is a set of ‘lines’ and
I\subseteq P\times L
is a set of incidences. Let
\Gamma=(P,L,I)
be an incidence geometry of rank
2
,
and let
(\lambda,k_{1},k_{2},l)\in\mathbb{N}^{4}
. Then we define
\Gamma
to be
(\lambda,k_{1},k_{2},l)
-sparse if for every nonempty subset of
I^{\prime}\subseteq I
it holds that

\lambda|I^{\prime}|\leq k_{1}|P(I^{\prime})|+k_{2}|L(I^{\prime})|-l,

where
P^{\prime}=P(I^{\prime})
and
L^{\prime}=L(I^{\prime})
are the sets of points and lines which participate in an incidence of
I^{\prime}
.
Furthermore,
\Gamma
is
(\lambda,k_{1},k_{2},l)
-tight if
\Gamma
is
(\lambda,k_{1},k_{2},l)
-sparse and

\lambda|I|=k_{1}|P|+k_{2}|L|-l.

For example, the
k
-plane matroid, introduced by Whiteley
[
16
]
, is a matroid with ground set
A\times B
, for some sets
A
and
B
in which a set
I\subseteq A\times B
is independent if for any subset
I^{\prime}\subseteq I
it holds that:

|I^{\prime}|\leq|A(I^{\prime})|+k|B(I^{\prime})|-k

where
A(I^{\prime})\times B(I^{\prime})\subseteq A\times B
is the support of
I
, i.e.

A(I^{\prime})=\{a\in A\>|\>\exists i\in I^{\prime},i=(a,b)\}

and

B(I^{\prime})=\{b\in L\>|\>\exists i\in I^{\prime},i=(a,b)\}.

The bases of this matroid have exactly

|I|=|A|+k|B|-k

incidences. In other words, the independent sets of the
k
-plane matroid are
(1,1,k,k)
-sparse, and the bases of the
k
-plane matroid are
(1,1,k,k)
-tight.

Even more generally, in recent work we consider sparsity conditions for incidence geometries of rank
r
using graphs of groups
[
13
]
. When the groups are Lie groups the corresponding Lie algebras give rise to a notion of infinitesimal motion for the incidence geometry. Let
\Gamma=(X,I,t,*)
be an incidence geometry of rank
2
, then a lower bound on the dimension of this space
M
of infinitesimal motions is:

\dim(M)\geq\sum_{x\in X}\dim(G/\rho(x))+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\dim(G/\rho(i))-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\sum_{x\in i}\dim(G/\rho(x)),

(1)

where
X
is the set of points,
I
is the set of incidences, and
\rho(x)
and
\rho(i)
are stabilizers of realiations of points or lines and incidences respectively under a smooth group action
[
13
]
. Under certain assumptions regarding the intersection of the stabilizers, the bound (
1
) gives rise to sparsity conditions.

In this paper, we first consider sparse incidence geometries of rank
2
, and then we introduce sparsity conditions for graded posets. For rank 2 incidence geometries, such sparsity conditions have been studied before, as we will indicate in the background, but we have proven two new results on this.

Namely, we show how to translate sparsity conditions on hypergraphs into sparsity conditions on the incidence graph, and provide a graph that has a decomposition into disjoint spanning forests whenever the incidence geometry is
(1,k_{1},k_{2},l)
-sparse.

We define what can be seen as new sparsity conditions for graphs, which is applicable whenever one has a graded poset. In particular, we allow both the weights of the vertices and the weights associated to distinct edges
l_{i,j}
to vary. We show how this gives sparsity conditions for posets and present a pebble game algorithm that can determine sparsity and tightness for posets.

The algorithm and proof techniques are based on those in
[
8
]
. The main difficulty in adapting the proof of correctness of the algorithm, is that the different
l_{i,j}
makes it more difficult to analyze one of the invariants of the algorithm, which leads to requirements on the order in which edges are processed in the algorithm.

Finally, we show that the resulting structure does not give a matroid.

The structure of the article is as follows. In Section
2
we give the necessary background. In Section
3
we compare the sparsity condition for incidence geometries with that of count matroids for hypergraphs and we prove that a decomposition into arborescences holds. In Section
4
we consider more general sparsity condition applicable to graded posets, and we describe a pebble game algorithm which recognizes such graphs. Finally, we conclude the article with some open problems.

2
Background

2.1
Sparsity conditions and algorithms of pebble game type

The pebble game algorithm, introduced by Jacobs and Hendrickson, is an algorithm for determining whether a graph has a
(2,3)
-tight spanning subgraph
[
6
]
. The pebble game algorithm takes as its input a graph
G
, and outputs a maximum size
(2,3)
-sparse subgraph. In particular, the algorithm can decide whether the input graph has a
(2,3)
-tight spanning subgraph. The
(2,3)
-tight graphs on
n
vertices are the bases of a matroid on the edges of the complete graph on
n
vertices. Another algorithm that can extract a
(2,3)
-tight subgraph was introduced by Gabow and Westerman
[
5
]
.

For
l\geq 2k
, only the empty graph is
(k,l)
-sparse. For
l<0
,
(k,l)
-sparsity does not behave well with respect to unions, in the sense that the union of two vertex disjoint sparse graphs is not necessarily sparse. Therefore,
(k,l)
-sparse graphs are primarily interesting to consider when
l\in[0,2k)
.

Lee and Streinu introduced a family of
(k,l)
-pebble game algorithms that recognize exactly the
(k,l)
-sparse multigraphs, for
l\in[0,2k)

[
8
]
. Lee and Streinu show that the
(k,l)
-sparse multigraphs on
n
vertices are the independent sets of a matroid on the edges of the complete multigraph on
n
vertices. The set of bases of this matroid is the set of
(k,l)
-tight multigraphs on
n
vertices.

Later, Streinu and Theran generalized the
(k,l)
-pebble game algorithms to
r
-uniform hypergraphs
[
14
]
.

Streinu and Theran considered parameters
k
and
l
such that
0\leq l\leq kr-1
, where
r
is the number of vertices in each edge. The
(k,l)
-sparse hypergraphs with
n
vertices also form the independent sets of a matroid, whenever
(k,l)
-sparse
r
-uniform hypergraphs with
n
vertices exist
[
8
,
17
]
. The
(k,l)
-tight hypergraphs on
n
vertices are the bases of this matroid.

Count matroids on hypergraphs were considered first by White and Whiteley, later by Whiteley
[
17
]
. For general count matroids, each vertex is assigned a different integer weight
m(v)
, and a set of edges
E^{\prime}
is independent in such a matroid if for every subset of vertices
V^{\prime}
one has:

|E^{\prime}[V^{\prime}]|\leq\sum_{v\in V}m(v)-l.

Frank developed the theory also for hypergraphs (and graphs) with parallel edges
[
4
]
.

In
[
17
]
, some matroids are also considered where instead the value of
{}^{\prime}l^{\prime}
is allowed to vary when ranging over different subsets of vertices, yet the weights
{}^{\prime}m(v)^{\prime}
of the vertices are constant.

Berg and Jordán developed a degree-constrained orientation algorithm able to determine independence in count matroids on graphs, inspired by the pebble game algorithm
[
1
]
. For a general reference on count matroids and generalizations of this algorithm to count matroids on multigraphs and hypergraphs, see
[
4
]
.

2.2
Arboricity

An arborescence of root
r
is a directed tree such that
r
has in-degree 0, and all other nodes of the tree have in-degree 1. In 1973, Edmonds proved a characterization of when a directed graph has
k
disjoint spanning arborescences of a given root
[
2
]
. Several related results have been proven since, about for instance branchings, i.e. directed forests such that every component is an arborescence, and when it is possible to cover the edge sets of graphs (directed or undirected) by edge-disjoint spanning trees. See for example
[
3
, Chapter 10]
for a review of such results.

The Nash-Williams theorem states that a graph is the union of
k
edge-disjoint spanning forests if and only if it is
(k,k)
-sparse, and the union of
k
edge-disjoint spanning trees if and only if it is
(k,k)
-tight
[
10
]
. There is also a result of Tutte which is equivalent to the Nash-Williams theorem
[
15
]
.

Using the Nash-Williams theorem, Lovász and Yemini proved that a graph is
(2,3)
-sparse if and only if doubling any edge results in a graph that is the union of two edge-disjoint spanning forests
[
9
]
. Lee, Streinu and Theran prove similar results for
(k,l)
-sparse graphs, and
(k,l)
-sparse hypergraphs
[
8
,
14
]
. In the hypergraph case, Streinu and Theran use result about decompositions of hypergraphs due to Frank, Király and Kriesell rather than the Nash-Williams theorem
[
4
]
.

Servatius showed that independence in the
k
-plane matroid is related to the arboricity of a modified incidence graph, called the
k-fold butterfly graph

[
12
]
.

3
Sparsity for rank 2 incidence geometries

The bound in (
1
) gives rise to notions of sparsity and tightness of rank 2 incidence geometries.

Definition 1
.

Let
\Gamma=(P,L,I)
be an incidence geometry of rank
2
, and let
(\lambda,k_{1},k_{2},l)\in\mathbb{N}^{4}
. Then
\Gamma
is
(\lambda,k_{1},k_{2},l)
-sparse if for every nonempty subset of
I^{\prime}\subseteq I
it holds that

\lambda|I^{\prime}|\leq k_{1}|P(I^{\prime})|+k_{2}|L(I^{\prime})|-l,

where
P(I^{\prime})\times L(I^{\prime})
is the support of
I^{\prime}
. Furthermore,
\Gamma
is
(\lambda,k_{1},k_{2},l)
-tight if
\Gamma
is
(\lambda,k_{1},k_{2},l)
-sparse and

\lambda|I|=k_{1}|P|+k_{2}|L|-l.

When
\lambda=1
, a
(1,k_{1},k_{2},l)
-sparse incidence geometry is simply said to be
(k_{1},k_{2},l)
-sparse. Similarly, a
(1,k_{1},k_{2},l)
-tight incidence geometry is simply said to be
(k_{1},k_{2},l)
-tight.

Given an incidence geometry
\Gamma=(P,L,I)
, define
I_{\lambda}(\Gamma)
to be the multigraph constructed by taking the incidence graph of
\Gamma
, and copying each edge
\lambda
times. In other words, let
V(I_{\lambda}(\Gamma))=P\cup L
, and define the edge set to be

E(I_{\lambda}(\Gamma))=\{(p,l,i)\in P\times L\times\{1,\cdots\lambda\}\>|\>(p,l)\in I\},

where the endpoints of an edge
(p,l,i)
are
p
and
l
. This gives us a new incidence geometry
(P,L,E(I_{\lambda}(\Gamma)))
.
The following result is immediate.

Lemma 2
.

If
(P,L,E(I_{\lambda}(\Gamma)))
is
(k_{1},k_{2},l)
-sparse then
\Gamma
is
(\lambda,k_{1},k_{2},l)
-sparse. Also, if
(P,L,E(I_{\lambda}(\Gamma)))
is
(k_{1},k_{2},l)
-tight, then
\Gamma
is
(\lambda,k_{1},k_{2},l)
-tight.

Any hypergraph
H=(V,E)
can be considered to be a rank
2
incidence geometry
\Gamma(H)=(V,E,I)
with point set
V
and line set
E
, where a point
v
and a line
e
are incident if and only if
v
is contained in the edge
e
. Note that if
H
is
r
-uniform, then each edge are in
r
incidences.
For
r
-uniform hypergraphs, the notion of sparsity of hypergraphs defined by Streinu and Theran
[
8
]
, see Section
2
,is related to our notion of sparsity for incidence geometries of rank 2 in the following way.

Theorem 3
.

For all natural numbers
\lambda\geq 1
, the
(k_{1},l)
-sparsity of a
r
-uniform hypergraph
H=(V,E)
corresponds to the
(\lambda,k_{1},\lambda r-1,l)
-sparsity of the incidence geometry
\Gamma(H)
.
Moreover,
H
is tight if and only if
\Gamma(H)
is tight.

Proof.

Suppose
H
is
(k_{1},l)
-sparse. Then, for any subset
V^{\prime}
of vertices one has:

|E(V^{\prime})|\leq k_{1}|V^{\prime}|-l,

where
E(V^{\prime})=\{e\in E|\>e\subseteq V^{\prime}\}
.

Since
r|E(V^{\prime})|=|I\cap(V\times E^{\prime})|
, for any set of incidences of the form
I^{\prime}=I\cap V^{\prime}\times E(V^{\prime})
one has

\displaystyle\lambda|I^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq k_{1}|V^{\prime}|+(\lambda r-1)|E^{\prime}|-l.

For a general subset,
I^{\prime}=I\cap V^{\prime\prime}\times E^{\prime\prime}
(with
E^{\prime\prime}\neq V^{\prime\prime}
), we see that

\displaystyle\lambda|I^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq\lambda r|E(V^{\prime\prime})|+\lambda(r-1)|E^{\prime\prime}\setminus E(V^{\prime\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq k_{1}|V^{\prime}|+(\lambda r-1)|E(V^{\prime\prime})|+\lambda(r-1)|E^{\prime\prime}\setminus E(V^{\prime\prime})|-l

\displaystyle\leq k_{1}|V^{\prime\prime}|+(\lambda r-1)|E^{\prime\prime}|-l,

Hence, we see that
\Gamma(H)
is sparse
(\lambda,k_{1},\lambda r-1,l)
-sparse.

Conversely, if
\Gamma(H)
is sparse, then for any
V^{\prime}
, and
E^{\prime}=E(V^{\prime})
, we have:

\lambda|I^{\prime}|\leq k_{1}|V^{\prime}|+(\lambda r-1)|E^{\prime}|-l,

and thus,

|E(V^{\prime})|\leq k_{1}|V^{\prime}|-l,

which proves
(k,l)
-sparsity of
H
.

Finally, to finish the proof, we note that

|E|=k|V|-l

if and only if

\lambda|I|=k|V|+(\lambda r-1)|E|-l,

which shows the claim about tightness.
∎

We will now prove a generalisation to
(1,k_{1},k_{2},l)
-sparsity, of Servatius result relating independence in the
k
-plane matroid to the arboricity of the
k
-fold butterfly graph.

Given a rank 2 incidence geometry
S=(P,L,I)
we want to define its associated
(k_{1},k_{2})
-fold butterfly graph as follows: The vertex set consists of
k_{1}
vertices for each element of
P
, and
k_{2}
vertices for each element of
L
, and for each incidence
(p,\ell)\in L
, there are edges between all
k_{1}
vertices representing
p
and all
k_{2}
vertices representing
\ell
.

The following theorem relates arboricity in the
(k_{1},k_{2})
-fold butterfly graph to
(1,k_{1},k_{2},l)
-tightness.

Theorem 4
.

An incidence geometry
S=(P,L,I)
is
(1,k_{1},k_{2},l)
-sparse if and only if adding any
l-1
incidences results in an incidence geometry with an associated
(k_{1},k_{2})
-fold butterfly graph decomposes into
k_{1}k_{2}
edge-disjoint spanning forests.

Proof.

Suppose that adding any
l-1
incidences to an incidence geometry
S
yields an associated
(k_{1},k_{2})
-fold butterfly graph which is the disjoint union of
k_{1}k_{2}
spanning forests. Then, by the Nash-Williams theorem,

k_{1}k_{2}|I|\leq k_{1}k_{2}(k_{1}|P|+k_{2}|L|-1).

Similarly, for any subset of incidences,

k_{1}k_{2}|I^{\prime}|\leq k_{1}k_{2}(k_{1}|P(I^{\prime})|+k_{2}|L(I^{\prime})|-1)

also by the Nash-Williams theorem. Hence
S
must be
(1,k_{1},k_{2},l)
-sparse if adding any
l-1
incidences to an incidence geometry
S
yields an associated
(k_{1},k_{2})
-fold butterfly graph which is the disjoint union of
k_{1}k_{2}
spanning forests.

Suppose that
S
is a
(1,k_{1},k_{2},l)
-sparse incidence geometry. Adding any
l-1
incidences to a
(1,k_{1},k_{2},l)
-sparse incidence geometry results in an incidence geometry with
|I|=|I|+l-1
incidences. The associated
(k_{1},k_{2})
-fold butterfly graph has

k_{1}k_{2}|I|\leq k_{1}k_{2}(k_{1}|P|+k_{2}|L|-1)

edges, since
S
was assumed to be
(1,k_{1},k_{2},l)
-sparse.

Let
V
be a set of vertices of the
(k_{1},k_{2})
-fold butterfly graph. Let
P^{\prime}
be the set of points
p
such that some vertex representing
p
is in
V
, and let
L^{\prime}
be the set of lines
l
such that some vertex representing
l
is in
V
. Note that if
V
contains
all
of the
k_{1}|P^{\prime}|
vertices representing points of
P^{\prime}
and all of the
k_{2}|L^{\prime}|
vertices representing elements of
L^{\prime}
, then
V
induces at most
k_{1}k_{2}(k_{1}|P^{\prime}|+k_{2}|L^{\prime}|-1)=k_{1}k_{2}(|V|-1)
edges, since
S
was assumed to be
(1,k_{1},k_{2},l)
-sparse.

Otherwise,
V
can be obtained from the set of all
k_{1}|P|
vertices representing points of
P^{\prime}
and all of the
k_{2}|L^{\prime}|
vertices representing elements of
L^{\prime}
by removing some vertices. Suppose that removing a vertex representing a point
p
from
V
produces a a vertex set that induces more than
k_{1}k_{2}(|V|-1)
edges. Note that all vertices representing a given point or line must induce the same number of edges. It follows that removing
all
vertices of
V
representing
p
must also produce a vertex set that induces more than
k_{1}k_{2}(|V|-1)
edges. This contradicts the incidence geometry being
(1,k_{1},k_{2},l)
-sparse. Hence the inequality
|E(V)|\leq k_{1}k_{2}(|V|-1)
is satisfied for all subsets of vertices of the
(k_{1},k_{2})
-butterfly graph, where
E(V)
is the set of edges induced by
V
. By the Nash-Williams Theorem it follows that the associated
(k_{1},k_{2})
-fold butterfly graph is the disjoint union of
k_{1}k_{2}
spanning forests.
∎

We immediately get the following corollary.

Corollary 5
.

An incidence geometry
S=(P,L,I)
is
(1,k_{1},k_{2},l)
-tight if adding any
l-1
incidences results in an incidence geometry with an associated
(k_{1},k_{2})
-fold butterfly graph decomposes into
k_{1}k_{2}
edge-disjoint spanning trees.

Lemma
2
implies that the property that adding any
l-1
incidences results in an incidence geometry with an associated
(k_{1},k_{2})
-fold butterfly graph decomposes into
k_{1}k_{2}
edge-disjoint spanning trees can be checked by checking that the incidence geometry
(P,L,E(I_{1}(\Gamma)))
is
(k_{1},k_{2},l)
-tight.

4
Sparsity for posets and a pebble game algorithm

4.1
(K,L)
-sparsity for posets

A graded poset is a partially ordered set
(\mathcal{P},\leq)
, such that every maximal chain has the same finite length
n
. This allows one to define a rank function

r:\mathcal{P}\rightarrow\{1,\cdots,n\},

where
r(x)
is the length of a maximal chain contained in
\{y\in\mathcal{P}~|~y\leq x\}
.

Given a graded poset
(\mathcal{P},\leq)
, the underlying graph of the Hasse diagram is the graph
I(\mathcal{P})=(V,E)
where
V=\mathcal{P}
, and

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=\cup_{k=1}^{n-1}\{xy\in\mathcal{P}^{2}~|~x\leq y,r(x)=k,r(y)=k+1\}

The vertex set of
I(\mathcal{P})
can be partitioned into sets
V_{0},\cdots,V_{n}
, where
V_{k}
consists of the elements of
\mathcal{P}
of rank
k
.
By definition of
I(\mathcal{P})
, all edges are between
V_{j}
and
V_{j+1}
for some
j\in\{0,\cdots,n-1\}
.

We now give a definition of sparsity and tightness that applies to graphs arising from posets in this way.

Definition 6
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a (multi)graph such that
V
has a partition into subsets
V_{1},V_{2},\cdots,V_{n}
, where every edge
e\in E
has endpoints in
V_{i}
and
V_{i+1}
for some
i\in\{1,\cdots,n-1\}
. Let
K=(k_{1},\cdots k_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
L=(l_{1,2},\cdots,l_{n-1,n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
be lists of positive real numbers.

We say that
G
is
(K,L)
-sparse if for any subset
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
, one has

|E(V^{\prime})|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}|V^{\prime}\cap V_{i}|-\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}i:V_{i}\cap V^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\\
V_{i+1}\cap V^{\prime}\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}{\max}\{l_{i,i+1}\}

Furthermore,
G
is
(K,L)
-tight if
G
is
(K,L)
-sparse, and additionally

|E|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}|V\cap V_{i}|-\max\{l_{i,i+1}\}.

If the underlying graph of the Hasse diagram of a poset
\mathcal{P}
is
(K,L)
-sparse or
(K,L)
-tight, then we simply say that
\mathcal{P}
is
(K,L)
-sparse or
(K,L)
-tight respectively.

We will mostly consider the case where
k_{i}
and
l_{i,i+1}
are integers. However, in some interesting examples, we see that non-integral parameters appear.

If we take
K=(k_{1},k_{2})
and
L=(l)
, then we see that this generalizes the notion of
(k_{1},k_{2},l)
-sparse and
(k_{1},k_{2},l)
-tight rank 2 incidence geometries that we had in Section
3
. The following gives an example of a rank 3 incidence geometry.

Example 7
.

Consider the cube with vertex set
H_{1}
, edge set
H_{2}
, and face set
H_{3}
. The face lattice of the cube is the graded poset with elements
H_{1}\cup H_{2}\cup H_{3}
, and order relation given by inclusion. The rank of an element in this poset is simply its dimension. Thus, one has
V_{1}=H_{1}
,
V_{2}=H_{2}
and
V_{3}=H_{3}
. For any face
z
, the set
R_{z}=\{x\in\mathcal{P}~|~x\leq z,r(x)\leq 2\}
induces the face lattice of a square. Hence for
V^{\prime}\subseteq R_{z}
one has

|E(V^{\prime})|\leq|V^{\prime}\cap V_{1}|+|V^{\prime}\cap V_{2}|.

Since every vertex is contained in
3
faces, every edge is contained in
2
faces and every incident vertex-edge pair incidence appears in
2
faces, for any
V^{\prime}\subseteq V_{1}\cup V_{2}
one has

\displaystyle 2|E(V^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{z\in Z}|V^{\prime}\cap V_{1}\cap R_{z}|+|V^{\prime}\cap V_{2}\cap R_{z}|

\displaystyle\leq 3|V^{\prime}\cap V_{1}|+2|V^{\prime}\cap V_{2}|,

and equality holds when
V^{\prime}=V_{1}\cup V_{2}.

Making a similar reasoning for the sets
R_{h}=\{x\in\mathcal{P}|h\leq x,r(x)\geq 2\}
, we get for
V^{\prime}\subseteq V_{2}\cup V_{3}
that

\displaystyle 2|E(V^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq 2|V^{\prime}\cap V_{2}|+4|V^{\prime}\cap V_{3}|,

5
Conclusions and open problems

We have presented new sparsity conditions for graphs that are suitable for the underlying graph of the Hasse diagram of a poset. We have also given a pebble game type algorithm extending results by Berg, Jordán and Frank to the new sparsity condition.
We have also proved an arboricity result for incidence geometries of rank 2.

Last, we now identify some open problems:

1.

Can one find inductive constructions (”Henneberg moves”) that allow to generate all
(K,L)
-tight graphs starting from a finite set of graphs?

2.

Can one relate
(K,L)
- sparsity to some other property of the poset?

3.

For a graded poset
P
, sparsity gives bounds on the number of pairs

\#\{(x,y)\in V_{i}^{\prime}\cup V_{i+1}^{\prime}~|~x\leq y\},

for
V_{i}^{\prime}\subseteq V_{i}
and
V_{i+1}^{\prime}\subseteq V_{i+1}
.
What does it imply about

\#\{(x,y)\in V_{i}^{\prime}\cup V_{i+k}^{\prime}~|~x\leq y\}

for
k>1
?

4.

Can one obtain results regarding tree decompositions for posets?

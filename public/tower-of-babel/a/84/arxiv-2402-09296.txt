Mean eigenvector self-overlap in the real and complex elliptic Ginibre ensembles at strong and weak non-Hermiticity
M. J. Crumpton, Y. V. Fyodorov and T. R. Würfel

Abstract
We study the mean diagonal overlap of left and right eigenvectors associated with complex eigenvalues in N × N N\times N non-Hermitian random Gaussian matrices. In well known works by Chalker and Mehlig the expectation of this (self-)overlap was computed for the complex Ginibre ensemble as N → ∞ N\to\infty . In the present work, we consider the same quantity in the real and complex elliptic Ginibre ensembles characterized by correlations between off-diagonal entries controlled by a parameter τ ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] \tau\in[0,1] , with τ = 1 \tau=1 corresponding to the Hermitian limit. We derive exact expressions for the mean diagonal overlap in both ensembles at any finite N N , for any eigenvalue off the real axis. We further investigate several scaling regimes as N → ∞ N\rightarrow\infty , both in the limit of strong non-Hermiticity keeping a fixed τ ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) \tau\in[0,1) and in the weak non-Hermiticity limit, with τ \tau approaching unity in such a way that N ⁡ ( 1 − τ ) N(1-\tau) remains finite.



Keywords:
non-Hermitian random matrices, real elliptic Ginibre ensemble, complex elliptic Ginibre ensemble, bi-orthogonal eigenvectors, eigenvalue depletion, eigenvector overlaps, bulk statistics, strong non-Hermiticity, weak non-Hermiticity.

Introduction

Influence of non-Hermiticity in physical systems (both classical and quantum) has recently attracted significant attention, both theoretically and experimentally, see e.g.
[
1
]
for a review. In many situations the corresponding operators or matrices may have (or do have) a random component, either by intrinsic properties of the system or as a model reflecting lack (or irrelevance) of the knowledge of their exact structure.
All these developments boosted attention to various classes of non-Hermitian random matrices, which despite lasting interest in their properties
[
2
]
remain much less understood in comparison with their self-adjoint counterparts. Motivations for such studies traditionally include, among other topics, attempts to characterize equilibria, stability and dynamics of complicated, mostly nonlinear dynamical systems, e.g. neural networks, or communities of many interacting species, see
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
, as well as studies of an effective non-Hermiticity helping to characterize various aspects of scattering in systems with quantum or classical wave chaos nature
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
. More recently, a boost of interest in non-Hermitian random matrices arose due to attempts to understand better universal properties of dissipative Lindbladian terms in density-matrix evolution of quantum many-body systems
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
.

Without loss of generality, a random
N\times N
matrix
X
can be diagonalised as
X=S\Lambda S^{-1}
, where
\Lambda
is a diagonal matrix containing the eigenvalues of
X
, represented by
z_{i}
. Additionally, the columns of
S
are the right eigenvectors,
\bm{v}_{R,i}
and the rows of
S^{-1}
are the left-eigenvectors,
\bm{v}_{L,i}^{\dagger}
. Within this setup the left and right eigenvectors satisfy a bi-orthogonality condition
\bm{v}_{L,i}^{\dagger}\bm{v}_{R,j}=\delta_{ij}
, but separately inside each of the sets of left and of right eigenvectors no such relation exists, unless matrices are normal. Chalker and Mehlig
[
16
,
17
]
were the first to systematically address the non-orthogonality of eigenvectors in non-normal random matrices, restricting their studies to the classical complex Ginibre ensemble (GinUE)
[
18
]
. In particular, they introduced an important object, the
matrix of overlaps

\mathcal{O}_{mn}=\left(\bm{v}^{\dagger}_{L,m}\bm{v}_{L,n}\right)\left(\bm{v}^{\dagger}_{R,n}\bm{v}_{R,m}\right)\hskip 9.24994ptm,n=1,\ldots,N,

(1.1)

and studied associated connected ensemble averages for diagonal entries
\mathcal{O}_{nn}
(frequently called self-overlaps) and their off-diagonal counterparts
\mathcal{O}_{mn}
:

\mathcal{O}(z)\equiv\bigg\langle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathcal{O}_{nn}\ \delta(z-z_{n})\bigg\rangle\hskip 21.33955pt\text{and}\hskip 21.33955pt\mathcal{O}(z_{1},z_{2})\equiv\bigg\langle\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n=1\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}^{N}\mathcal{O}_{mn}\ \delta(z_{1}-z_{m})\ \delta(z_{2}-z_{n})\bigg\rangle\ ,

(1.2)

where the angular brackets stand for the expectation value with respect to the measure associated with the ensemble in question, i.e. the GinUE in the case of
[
16
]
. Here
\delta(z-z_{n})
is the Dirac delta mass at the eigenvalue
z_{n}
, so that the empirical density of eigenvalues in the complex plane,
z
, reads
\rho^{(\text{emp})}_{N}(z)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\delta(z-z_{n})
.

The problem of statistical characterization of eigenvector non-orthogonality in various ensembles of non-Hermitian random matrices has been attracting growing research ever since the Chalker and Mehlig papers, in particular driven by many applications in physics and beyond. More recently the interest in these questions got further impetus from papers by Bourgade & Dubach
[
19
]
and Fyodorov
[
20
]
, who managed to derive, among other quantities, the explicit distribution of self-overlaps
\mathcal{O}_{nn}
in the bulk of the complex Ginibre ensemble. Such self-overlaps are directly related to the eigenvalue condition numbers, controlling stability of eigenvalues against additive perturbation of the matrix entries, see e.g. the discussion in
[
20
]
. Further representative examples of studies of eigenvector non-orthogonality in non-normal random matrices, of various types and at varying level of mathematical rigor, as well as some motivating applications, can be found in
[
21
]
-
[
59
]
.

In this paper we will concentrate on the diagonal overlaps described via
\mathcal{O}(z)
. This can be further used to define the mean conditional self-overlap as

\mathbb{E}\left(z\right)\equiv\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{O}_{nn}\ |\ z=z_{n}\right)=\frac{\mathcal{O}(z)}{\rho\,(z)}\ \text{,}

(1.3)

where
\rho\,(z)
is the mean spectral density at a point
z
in the complex plane, defined via

\rho\,(z)\equiv\langle\ \rho_{N}^{(\text{emp})}(z)\ \rangle=\bigg\langle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\delta(z-z_{n})\bigg\rangle\ .

(1.4)

Note that
\mathbb{E}\left(z\right)
is particularly useful when comparing theoretical predictions with numerical data. For the GinUE, as
N\rightarrow\infty
and each entry having variance
1/N
, the droplet becomes a unit circle in
\mathbb{C}
with uniform density
\rho(z)\approx 1/\pi
for
|z|^{2}<1
and zero otherwise
[
60
]
. Chalker and Mehlig found the leading asymptotic behaviour of Eq. (
1.2
) in this ensemble, deriving
\mathcal{O}(z)\approx N\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)/\pi
inside the unit circle and zero otherwise
[
16
]
. Thus,
\mathcal{O}_{nn}\sim N
for eigenvalues inside the disk, which is macroscopically larger than the corresponding value seen in normal matrices, where
\mathcal{O}_{nn}=1
. One should note that in order to obtain a non-trivial limit as
N\to\infty
, we consider the well-defined rescaled correlator
\mathcal{O}(z)/N
.

In our recent paper
[
54
]
, we provided explicit expressions for the mean self-overlap in the real Ginibre ensemble at finite matrix size
N
and, for
N\rightarrow\infty
, we analyzed various regions associated with complex eigenvalues inside the limiting spectral support of the circular droplet. In the present work, we develop our analysis further by extending it to more general classes of random matrices with real or complex Gaussian-distributed entries. Namely, the so-called elliptic ensembles introduced by Girko
[
61
]
, who demonstrated that the limiting spectral support will have the shape of an elliptic droplet controlled by an ellipticity parameter
\tau\in[0,1]
. In particular, the length of the semi-major and semi-minor axes of the ellipse become proportional to
1+\tau
and
1-\tau
respectively.

Our main goal then is to study the mean self-overlap of eigenvectors associated with complex eigenvalues for the complex elliptic Ginibre ensemble (eGinUE) and the real elliptic Ginibre ensemble (eGinOE), both at finite
N
and asymptotically as
N\rightarrow\infty
. We will first consider the parameter
\tau
fixed (and away from one) as
N\to\infty
, this defines the so-called regime of
strong non-Hermiticity
(SNH). Note that for
\tau
being zero we naturally recover the Ginibre ensembles, GinUE and GinOE respectively, and thus, in this limit, our results have to match those presented in
[
54
]
. Additionally, we study another important scaling regime of
weak non-Hermiticity
(WNH), originally introduced in
[
62
,
63
,
64
]
. In this regime the parameter
\tau\to 1
as
N
tends to
\infty
, more precisely
\tau\sim 1-\kappa/N
, with
\kappa>0
, which results in the elliptic droplet being replaced by a thin cloud of complex eigenvalues. In addition, for real-valued matrices a macroscopic fraction of eigenvalues remain exactly on the real line, while the complex eigenvalues are depleted away from the real axis. These features are absent in the complex-valued weakly non-Hermitian matrices, making results at weak non-Hermiticity pronouncedly different between real-valued and complex-valued matrices.

2.2

N

2.5
) and the eGinOE (Theorem

2.2.1
and at weak non-Hermiticity in Section
2.2.2
. We provide asymptotic formulas for the mean self-overlap in the eGinOE at SNH in the spectral bulk and in the special depletion regime, which only exists in the eGinOE, in Corollaries
2.7
and
2.9
respectively. Furthermore, we discuss the mean self-overlap at WNH in what we call the
weak bulk
for the eGinUE in Corollary
2.11
and the eGinOE in Corollary
2.12
. Finally, the corresponding proofs of our findings are presented in Section
3
for finite
N
and in Section
4
for the asymptotic results.

2
Statement and Discussion of the Main Results

2.1
Remarks on elliptic Ginibre ensembles

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X=\left(X_{ij}\right)_{i,j=1}^{N}
be an
N\times N
matrix in the
complex elliptic Ginibre
ensemble denoted as eGinUE. Matrices in this ensemble consist of mean zero i.i.d. complex Gaussian entries
[
61
,
4
]
, with the following correlation structure

\mathbb{E}\left(X_{ii}^{2}\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(X_{ij}^{2}\right)=1\hskip 28.45274pt\mathbb{E}\left(X_{ij}X_{ji}\right)=\tau\text{,}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for}\hskip 9.24994pti,j=1,...N\hskip 9.24994pti\neq j\ ,

(2.1)

for
\tau\in[0,1]
. The joint probability density function (JPDF) of such matrices, with respect to the flat Lebesgue measure,
dX=\prod_{i,j=1}^{N}dX_{ij}d\bar{X}_{ij}
, is given by

P_{\text{eGinUE}}(X)\,dX=\frac{1}{D_{N,\tau}}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{1-\tau^{2}}\Tr\left(XX^{\dagger}-\tau\ {\rm Re\,}X^{2}\right)\right]dX\ ,

(2.2)

with normalization constant,
D_{N,\tau}=\pi^{N^{2}}\left(1-\tau^{2}\right)^{\frac{N^{2}}{2}}

[
63
]
. Matrices belonging to this ensemble can be generated according to
X=\sqrt{1+\tau}\,H_{1}+i\sqrt{1-\tau}\,H_{2}
, where
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are complex Hermitian matrices with mean zero Gaussian entries. The real entries on the diagonal have variance
1/2
whereas the complex off-diagonal entries have variance
1/4
for both their real and imaginary components.

Definition 2.2
.

The counterpart of the eGinUE for matrices with real i.i.d. Gaussian entries is the so-called
real elliptic Ginibre
ensemble, denoted as eGinOE. Such matrices have entries of mean zero, with the following correlation structure

\mathbb{E}\left(X_{ii}^{2}\right)=1+\tau\hskip 28.45274pt\mathbb{E}\left(X_{ij}^{2}\right)=1\hskip 28.45274pt\mathbb{E}\left(X_{ij}X_{ji}\right)=\tau\text{,}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{for}\hskip 9.24994pti,j=1,...N\hskip 9.24994pti\neq j\ ,

(2.3)

for
\tau\in[0,1]
. The JPDF of matrices
X
with respect to the flat Lebesgue measure
[
61
,
4
]
, reads

P_{\text{eGinOE}}(X)\,dX=\frac{1}{C_{N,\tau}}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2(1-\tau^{2})}\Tr\left(XX^{T}-\tau X^{2}\right)\right]dX\ ,

(2.4)

with normalization constant
C_{N,\tau}=(2\pi)^{N^{2}/2}\left(1+\tau\right)^{\frac{N(N+1)}{4}}\left(1-\tau\right)^{\frac{N(N-1)}{4}}

[
44
]
. In a similar way to the eGinUE, matrices belonging to this ensemble can be randomly sampled using
X=\sqrt{1+\tau}\,H+\sqrt{1-\tau}\,A
, where
H
and
A
are real symmetric and anti-symmetric Gaussian matrices respectively. Both have mean zero entries and variance
1/2
of their constrained off-diagonal entries, however the diagonal entries of
H
have unit variance whereas, by definition,
A
must have all zeros on the diagonal.

For recent reviews of available results on the eGinUE and eGinOE see
[
65
,
66
]
. The remarks below provide a discussion of main facts on both ensembles which will be of direct relevance to the present paper.

Remark 2.3
.

The correlation parameter
\tau\in[0,1]
allows one to interpolate between the two classical limits. For
\tau=0
, the entries of eGinOE and eGinUE matrices are uncorrelated, as such the eGinOE and eGinUE reduce to the GinOE
[
18
,
66
]
and the GinUE
[
18
,
65
]
respectively. For
\tau=1
, the correlation is at its maximal possible value, yielding symmetric matrices belonging to the
Gaussian Orthogonal Ensemble
(GOE) in the real case, whereas in the complex case one arrives at Hermitian matrices from the
Gaussian Unitary Ensemble
(GUE). Finite
N
and asymptotic results for the mean density of complex eigenvalues and the mean diagonal overlaps for both the GinOE and GinUE can be found in
[
54
]
.
Since the characteristic polynomial of an eGinOE matrix has only real coefficients, generically the spectrum of eGinOE matrices consists of real eigenvalues
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and complex eigenvalues
z=x+iy,\,y\neq 0
which always appear in conjugated pairs. Correspondingly, the mean spectral density, Eq. (
1.4
), necessarily has the form
\rho\,(z)=\rho^{(c)}(z)+\delta(y)\rho^{(r)}(x)
, where
\rho^{(c/r)}(z)
describes the mean density of complex/real eigenvalues. On the other hand, the eigenvalues of eGinUE matrices are all complex and do not respect complex conjugation with probability one.

The density of eigenvalues in both ensembles has already been studied extensively. Before presenting the explicit formulas, we need to introduce the Hermite polynomials via:

{\rm He\,}_{k}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dy\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}y^{2}}\,\left(x+iy\right)^{k}=\frac{(\pm i)^{k}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{\frac{x^{2}}{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\mp ixt}t^{k}=k!\sum_{m=0}^{[k/2]}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{(k-2m)!m!}\frac{x^{k-2m}}{2^{m}}\ ,

(2.5)

where
[x]
represents the floor function. We will also need the standard complementary error function
\text{erfc}(x)=1-\erf(x)
, where
\erf(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{x}e^{-t^{2}}dt
.

Remark 2.4
.

The mean density of complex eigenvalues is of particular interest in this work and is given for finite
N

in the eGinUE by

\rho^{(\text{eGinUE},c)}_{N}(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{1-\tau^{2}}\bigg(|z|^{2}-\tau\ \text{Re}(z^{2})\bigg)\right]\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\frac{\tau^{n}}{n!}{\rm He\,}_{n}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{\tau}}\right){\rm He\,}_{n}\left(\frac{\bar{z}}{\sqrt{\tau}}\right)\ ,

(2.6)

equivalent representations for finite
N
are also known, see e.g.
[
65
, Prop. 2.7]
and
[
2
, Eq. (18.4.19)]
. On the other hand, in the eGinOE the corresponding expression reads

\rho_{N}^{(\text{eGinOE,c})}(z)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{1}{1+\tau}\ y\ \exp\left[\frac{y^{2}-x^{2}}{1+\tau}\right]\text{erfc}\left[\sqrt{\frac{2}{1-\tau^{2}}}\ |y|\right]P_{N-2}\ ,

(2.7)

where we have introduced

P_{N}=\frac{1}{\bar{z}-z}\sum_{k=0}^{N}\ \frac{\tau^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}{k!}\bigg[{\rm He\,}_{k+1}\left(\frac{\bar{z}}{\sqrt{\tau}}\right){\rm He\,}_{k}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{\tau}}\right)-{\rm He\,}_{k+1}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{\tau}}\right){\rm He\,}_{k}\left(\frac{\bar{z}}{\sqrt{\tau}}\right)\bigg]\text{,}

(2.8)

see e.g.
[
67
, Eq. (5.2)]
and
[
68
, Eq. (23)]
for equivalent representations of this density. The mean density of
real
eigenvalues in the eGinOE is also known at finite
N
, see e.g.
[
67
,
69
]
or
[
44
, Remark 2.3]
, but is not needed for our purposes.

It is worth discussing the large
N
asymptotic behaviour of the mean eigenvalue density in more detail. Focusing first on the regime of
strong non-Hermiticity
, defined such that
0\leq\tau<1
remains fixed as
N\to\infty
, we show a comparison between the large
N
densities of complex eigenvalues in the eGinOE and eGinUE in the upper half of Figure
1
using heatmaps. The heatmap associated with the eGinUE features two distinct scaling regimes: a spectral bulk inside the ellipse and an edge region along the ellipse circumference. Similar regimes are also seen in the eGinOE. In both ensembles it has previously been shown
[
61
,
4
,
67
]
, that the limiting mean density of complex eigenvalues in the spectral bulk, after rescaling
z=\sqrt{N}w
with
w=x+iy
, converges to

\rho_{\text{bulk,SNH}}^{(\textup{eGinUE},c)}(w)=\rho_{\text{bulk,SNH}}^{(\textup{eGinOE},c)}(w)=\frac{1}{\pi(1-\tau^{2})}\ \Theta\bigg[1-\left(\frac{x^{2}}{(1+\tau)^{2}}+\frac{y^{2}}{(1-\tau)^{2}}\right)\bigg]\ ,

(2.9)

where
\Theta[a]=1
when
a>0
and zero otherwise is the Heaviside step function. This implies that in the limit of large
N
, for both the eGinUE and eGinOE, the vast majority of eigenvalues lie inside the ellipse

\frac{{\rm Re\,}(z)^{2}}{N(1+\tau)^{2}}+\frac{{\rm Im\,}(z)^{2}}{N(1-\tau)^{2}}=1\ ,

(2.10)

thus, at SNH, both the semi-major and semi-minor axes of the elliptical support are
O(\sqrt{N})
.

In addition, the heatmap in the eGinOE case shows yet another scaling regime close to the real axis, essentially for
{\rm Im\,}(z)\sim O(1)
. In that region the density of eigenvalues is significantly reduced in comparison to the spectral bulk value, reflecting the presence of the
O(\sqrt{N})
real eigenvalues in the eGinOE, see
[
54
]
for discussion in the context of the GinOE. The scaling regime describing this region of the complex plane will be referred to as the

. Inside the depletion regime of the eGinOE, where we write

(2.11)

4.2.3
. The magnitude of a non-zero density at fixed
z
in this regime does not depend on its real part, but only on
\xi
. The dependence on
\delta
via the Heaviside step-function ensures that we stay inside the elliptic droplet, while the fact that
\xi
remains unscaled with respect to
N
restricts us inside a strip of width
O(1)
. Note that for
\tau=0
in Eqs. (
2.6
) to (

[

.

We now focus on the second scaling of
\tau

regime. In contrast to strong non-Hermiticity, here

It is also apparent from the lower half of Figure

After the digressions on the mean densities, let us return to our main object of interest, the self-overlap between left and right eigenvectors. Results for the mean self-overlap are well established in the GinUE for the entire complex plane, see

2.3
Discussion of open problems

In a subsequent paper, we will consider the mean self-overlap at the droplet edge for both strong and weak non-Hermiticity. We will also study the density of edge eigenvalues in the eGinOE, as (to the best of our knowledge) the leading order asymptotic behaviour has not yet been established in this ensemble. It is natural to expect that the result should match the leading order eGinUE result derived by Lee and Riser
[
72
]
, who also give fine asymptotic behaviour of the density in terms of the curvature of the ellipse. The leading order density term, as
N\rightarrow\infty
, is universal as described by Tao and Vu
[
73
]
, and should also be valid in the eGinOE at the edge. This could in principle be derived using Forrester and Nagao’s finite
N
result. In the present paper we will not discuss the edge statistics of eigenvalue density or self-overlaps further.

Alternatively, one could discuss similar objects in the third standard Ginibre ensemble, the
quaternionic Ginibre ensemble
, to connect with the existing results in
[
40
]
and then extend to its elliptic version. For that purpose it might also be interesting to employ techniques similar to the works by Noda
[
56
,
57
]
and Akemann et al
[
41
]
.

Finally, the ultimate goal should be to describe the entire distribution of diagonal overlaps at finite
N
and in various asymptotic regimes. The distribution of self-overlaps associated with complex eigenvalues has been considered in the GinUE
[
19
,
20
]
and has been studied for real eigenvalues in the eGinOE
[
44
]
, but is not yet available for complex eigenvalues in both eGinOE and eGinUE. Considering the statistics of off-diagonal overlaps, i.e. Eq. (
1.1
) with
n\neq m
, beyond results of
[
19
]
is another important avenue.

To prove our main results, we review the Schur decomposition of complex and real random matrices as well as their effects on
\mathcal{O}(z)
in Sections
3.1
and
3.3
respectively. Then, through the use of techniques from
[
20
,
44
,
54
]

2.5
and
2.6
in Sections

3.1
Incomplete complex Schur decomposition

In the eGinUE, the eigenvalues are always complex and are not necessarily in conjugate pairs. Therefore, we make use of the
incomplete complex Schur decomposition
(ICSD), see
[
74
,
75
,
76
]
. Let
z=x+iy
be a complex eigenvalue of the
N\times N
complex, non-Hermitian matrix
X
, with associated right and left eigenvectors denoted by
\mathbf{x}_{R}
and
\mathbf{x}_{L}^{\dagger}
respectively. Note that to have a nontrivial self-overlap we must assume
N\geq 2
. Utilising the results derived in
[
76
]
we find the ICSD of
X
with respect to
z
as

X=R\widetilde{X}R^{\dagger}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{with}\hskip 9.24994pt\widetilde{X}=\left(\begin{matrix}z&\mathbf{w}^{\dagger}\\
0&X_{1}\\
\end{matrix}\right)\text{,}

(3.1)

where
R
is a Hermitian matrix with
R^{\dagger}=R
and
R^{2}=\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}
. The vector
\mathbf{w}
is complex with
N-1
entries with independent real and imaginary parts. The matrix
X_{1}
is an
(N-1)\times(N-1)
dimensional complex matrix with the same statistical properties as
X
, which in principle could be further decomposed into blocks. In
[
20
, Section 4]
, this decomposition is applied to the GinUE and it is found that a complex eigenvalue
z
, has right eigenvector
\bm{v}_{R}\rightarrow\left(1,\bm{0}_{N-1}\right)^{T}
and left eigenvector
\bm{v}_{L}\rightarrow\left(1,\mathbf{b}_{N-1}\right)^{T}
, with
N-1
dimensional complex vector
\mathbf{b}_{N-1}
. We thus deduce that in the eGinUE the self-overlap,
\mathcal{O}_{z}
, is readily expressed using the resolvent of
X_{1}
, since
\mathbf{b}_{N-1}^{\dagger}=\mathbf{w}^{\dagger}\left(z\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N-1}-X_{1}\right)^{-1}
, implying

\mathcal{O}_{z}=1+\mathbf{b}_{N-1}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}_{N-1}=1+\mathbf{w}^{\dagger}\bigg[\left(z\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N-1}-X_{1}\right)^{\dagger}\left(z\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N-1}-X_{1}\right)\bigg]^{-1}\mathbf{w}\equiv 1+\mathbf{w}^{\dagger}\ B^{-1}\ \mathbf{w}\ ,

(3.2)

where we have defined the matrix

B\equiv\left(z\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N-1}-X_{1}\right)^{\dagger}\left(z\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N-1}-X_{1}\right).

(3.3)

The Jacobian needed for the change to the ICSD of
X
can be found in Appendix B of
[
76
]
,

\begin{split}dX&=\frac{1}{2}\ |\det\left[z\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N-1}-X_{1}\right]|^{2}\ d^{2}z\ d^{2}\mathbf{w}\ dX_{1}\ dR\ ,\end{split}

(3.4)

where
dR
denotes the contribution to the measure originating from the Householder reflection matrix
R
. After simple manipulations we find the probability measure, defined in terms of the new variables:

\begin{split}P_{\text{eGinUE}}(X)dX&=D_{N,\tau}^{\prime}|\det\left[z\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N-1}-X_{1}\right]|^{2}e^{-\frac{1}{1-\tau^{2}}\big[\Tr\left(X_{1}X_{1}^{\dagger}-\tau\ {\rm Re\,}X_{1}^{2}\right)+\left(|z|^{2}-\tau\ {\rm Re\,}z^{2}+\mathbf{w}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}\right)\big]}d^{2}z\ d^{2}\mathbf{w}\ dX_{1}\ .\end{split}

(3.5)

Note that the integral over
dR
yields a factor
\frac{2\pi^{N-1}}{\Gamma(N)}

[
76
, Section 6]
, combining this with
D_{N,\tau}
from Definition
2.1
, gives the new constant

\begin{split}D_{N,\tau}^{\prime}&=\frac{1}{2}\,D_{N,\tau}^{-1}\,\frac{2\pi^{N-1}}{\Gamma(N)}=\frac{\pi^{-N^{2}+N-1}}{\Gamma(N)}\,\left(1-\tau^{2}\right)^{-N^{2}/2}\ .\end{split}

(3.6)

3.2
Proof of Theorem
2.5
- eGinUE

The mean self-overlap for the eGinUE can be written as

\begin{split}\mathcal{O}^{(\textup{eGinUE},c)}_{N}(z)&=\bigg\langle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathcal{O}_{nn}\ \delta(z-z_{n})\bigg\rangle_{\text{eGinUE},N}=\bigg\langle\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{z}}\ \delta(z-\widetilde{z})\bigg\rangle_{X}\ .\end{split}

(3.7)

The ICSD of an
N\times N
eGinUE matrix
X
allows one to express
\mathcal{O}_{z}
in terms of
z
and
X_{1}
, thus the average is obtained by integrating over the measure in Eq. (
3.5
). The
\delta
-function of a complex variable makes the integration over
d^{2}z
trivial. It is convenient to define the normalised average of any function
\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{w})
with respect to the vector
\mathbf{w}
as

\bigg\langle\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{w})\bigg\rangle_{\mathbf{w}}\equiv\frac{(1-\tau^{2})^{-N+1}}{\pi^{N-1}}\int d^{2}\mathbf{w}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{1-\tau^{2}}\mathbf{w}^{\dagger}\mathbf{w}\right]\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{w})\ ,

(3.8)

so that we can write

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{z}}\ \delta(z-\widetilde{z})\bigg\rangle_{X}

\displaystyle=D_{N,\tau}^{\prime}\ e^{-\frac{1}{1-\tau^{2}}\left(|z|^{2}-\tau\ {\rm Re\,}z^{2}\right)}\int dX_{1}\ |\det\left[z\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N-1}-X_{1}\right]|^{2}\ e^{-\frac{1}{1-\tau^{2}}\Tr\left(X_{1}X_{1}^{\dagger}-\tau\ {\rm Re\,}X_{1}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\times\ (1-\tau^{2})^{N-1}\ \pi^{N-1}\ \bigg\langle 1+\left(\mathbf{b}_{N-1}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}_{N-1}\right)\bigg\rangle_{\mathbf{w}}\ .

(3.9)

We can now adapt statements from
[
54
]
, in particular Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2 which apply to the GinOE. These adaptations are straightforward exercises in Gaussian integrals and are left to the reader:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{w}
be a complex vector of length
N-1
, whose entries have independent real and imaginary parts. We have

\begin{split}&\bigg\langle\mathbf{w}^{\dagger}Y\mathbf{w}\bigg\rangle_{\mathbf{w}}=(1-\tau^{2})\Tr Y\ ,\end{split}

(3.10)

where the average is defined in Eq. (
3.8
) and
Y
is an
N-1
dimensional square matrix.

Straightforward application of this formula further implies

Lemma 3.2
.

The average over
\mathbf{w}
of
\mathbf{b}_{N-1}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}_{N-1}
, defined in Eq. (
3.2
), is given by

\begin{split}\bigg\langle 1+\left(\mathbf{b}_{N-1}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}_{N-1}\right)\bigg\rangle_{\mathbf{w}}&=1+(1-\tau^{2})\Tr\left[B^{-1}\right]\ .\end{split}

(3.11)

Inserting now the result from Lemma
3.2
into Eq. (
3.9
), the next task is to perform the integral over
X_{1}
in

\begin{split}\bigg\langle\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{z}}\ \delta(z-\widetilde{z})\bigg\rangle_{X}&=D_{N,\tau}^{\prime}\ e^{-\frac{1}{1-\tau^{2}}\left(|z|^{2}-\tau\ {\rm Re\,}z^{2}\right)}\ (1-\tau^{2})^{N-1}\ \pi^{N-1}\\
&\times\int dX_{1}\ |\det\left[z\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N-1}-X_{1}\right]|^{2}\ e^{-\frac{1}{1-\tau^{2}}\Tr\left(X_{1}X_{1}^{\dagger}-\tau\ {\rm Re\,}X_{1}^{2}\right)}\bigg[1+(1-\tau^{2})\Tr B^{-1}\bigg]\ .\end{split}

(3.12)

We now utilise the definition of the matrix
B
in Eq. (
3.3
) and notice that we can write

\begin{split}\det B&=|\det\left[z\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N-1}-X_{1}\right]|^{2}=\det\left[\begin{matrix}0&i(z\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N-1}-X_{1})\\
i(\bar{z}\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N-1}-X_{1}^{\dagger})&0\\
\end{matrix}\right]\ ,\end{split}

(3.13)

which will be useful in calculating the first term of Eq. (
3.12
), whereas for the second term we must utilise

\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\det\left(\mu\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N-1}+B\right)\bigg|_{\mu=0}=\det B\ \Tr B^{-1}\ .

(3.14)

This motivates the need to introduce the block-matrix
M
via

M\equiv\left(\begin{matrix}\sqrt{\mu}\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}&i\left(z\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}-X\right)\\
i\left(\bar{z}\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}-X^{\dagger}\right)&\sqrt{\mu}\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}\\
\end{matrix}\right)\ ,

(3.15)

where each block is of size
N\times N
and
\mu
is a real parameter, such that for
N\rightarrow N-1
and
\mu=0
we have

\det M\ \bigg|_{\mu=0}=\det B\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\det M\ \bigg|_{\mu=0}=\det B\ \Tr B^{-1}\ .

(3.16)

Now we define the normalised average of any function
\mathcal{A}(X_{1})
over
X_{1}
via

\bigg\langle\mathcal{A}(X_{1})\bigg\rangle_{X_{1}}\equiv\frac{1}{D_{N-1,\tau}}\int dX_{1}\ \exp\left[-\frac{1}{1-\tau^{2}}\Tr\left(X_{1}X_{1}^{\dagger}-\tau X_{1}^{2}\right)\right]\ \mathcal{A}(X_{1})\ ,

(3.17)

where the constant
D_{N-1,\tau}
is given in Definition
2.1
. Introducing another constant,
D_{N,\tau}^{\prime\prime}\equiv D_{N,\tau}^{\prime}D_{N-1,\tau}(1-\tau^{2})^{N-1}\ \pi^{N-1}
, the expression in Eq. (
3.12
) takes the form

\begin{split}\bigg\langle\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{z}}\ \delta(z-\widetilde{z})\bigg\rangle_{X}&=D_{N,\tau}^{\prime\prime}\ e^{-\frac{1}{1-\tau^{2}}\left(|z|^{2}-\tau\ {\rm Re\,}z^{2}\right)}\bigg[\ \bigg\langle\det M\bigg\rangle_{X_{1}}\,\bigg|_{\mu=0}+(1-\tau^{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\bigg\langle\det M\bigg\rangle_{X_{1}}\,\bigg|_{\mu=0}\ \bigg]\ .\end{split}

(3.18)

The averaging of
\det M
with respect to the eGinUE (and later the eGinOE) can be most conveniently performed using the representation of the determinant via Berezin integration over anti-commuting Grassmann variables:

\det M=\int D(\bm{\Phi},\bm{\chi})\exp\left[-\bm{\Phi}^{T}M\bm{\chi}\right]\ \text{,}

(3.19)

where
D(\bm{\Phi},\bm{\chi})=d\bm{\phi}_{1}d\bm{\chi}_{1}d\bm{\phi}_{2}d\bm{\chi}_{2}
and
\bm{\Phi}^{T}=\left(\bm{\phi}_{1},\bm{\phi}_{2}\right)^{T}
,
\bm{\chi}^{T}=\left(\bm{\chi}_{1},\bm{\chi}_{2}\right)^{T}
. Here,
\bm{\phi}_{1}
,
\bm{\phi}_{2}
,
\bm{\chi}_{1}
and
\bm{\chi}_{2}
are Grassmann vectors of length
N
. For further use it is also convenient to introduce a longer Grassmann vector
\phi^{T}\equiv\left(\bm{\phi}_{1},\bm{\phi}_{2},\bm{\chi}_{1},\bm{\chi}_{2}\right)^{T}
with the measure
D\phi=d\bm{\phi}_{1}d\bm{\phi}_{2}d\bm{\chi}_{1}d\bm{\chi}_{2}
, as this allows us to employ the following identity, see e.g.
[
77
]

\int D\phi\ \exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\phi^{T}H\phi\right]=\begin{cases}{\rm Pf\,}H&\text{for }H\ \text{even dimensional,}\\
0&\text{for }H\ \text{odd dimensional.}\\
\end{cases}

(3.20)

The outcome of calculating the average determinant of
M
over
X
is provided in the following Proposition.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
M
be the matrix defined in Eq. (
3.15
), where
z
is a complex number and
X
is an
N\times N
eGinUE matrix. The average of
\det M
, with respect to
X
, can be expressed in the following form:

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\det M\bigg\rangle_{X}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}dv_{1}\,dv_{2}\,e^{-\frac{v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dR\ e^{-R}\bigg[\Big(R+f(z,v_{1},v_{2})\Big)^{2}+\mu^{2}+2\mu\Big(f(z,v_{1},v_{2})-R\Big)\bigg]^{\frac{N}{2}}

\displaystyle\times P_{N}\left(\frac{\mu+R+f(z,v_{1},v_{2})}{\sqrt{\big(R+f(z,v_{1},v_{2})\big)^{2}+\mu^{2}+2\mu\big(f(z,v_{1},v_{2})-R\big)}}\right)\ ,

(3.21)

where
f(z,v_{1},v_{2})\equiv\left(\bar{z}-iv_{2}\sqrt{\tau}\right)\left(z+iv_{1}\sqrt{\tau}\right)
and
P_{N}(t)
is a Legendre polynomial defined in
[
79
, 8.913.3]

P_{N}(t)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta\ \left(t+\sqrt{t^{2}-1}\cos{\theta}\right)^{N}\ .

(3.22)

Proof.

Using Eq. (
3.19
) to express the determinant of
M
in terms of anticommuting variables leads to

\bigg\langle\det M\bigg\rangle_{X}=(-1)^{N}\expectationvalue{ \int D \bm\Phi D \bm\chi\exp{ - \left( \begin{matrix} \bm\phi_1^T & \bm\phi_2^T \end{matrix} \right) \left( \begin{matrix} \sqrt{\mu} \eins_N & i \left( z \eins_N - X \right) \\ i \left( \bar{z} \eins_N - X^\dagger\right) & \sqrt{\mu} \eins_N
\end{matrix} \right) \left( \begin{matrix} \bm\chi_1 \\ \bm\chi_2 \end{matrix} \right)
} }_{X}\ ,

(3.23)

which upon using
\bm{\phi}_{i}^{T}X\bm{\chi}_{j}=-\Tr\left[X\bm{\chi}_{j}\bm{\phi}_{i}^{T}\right]
can be written as

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\det M\bigg\rangle_{X}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{N}\int D\bm{\Phi}D\bm{\chi}\ e^{-\sqrt{\mu}\left(\bm{\phi}_{1}^{T}\bm{\chi}_{1}+\bm{\phi}_{2}^{T}\bm{\chi}_{2}\right)-i\left(z\bm{\phi}_{1}^{T}\bm{\chi}_{2}+\bar{z}\bm{\phi}_{2}^{T}\bm{\chi}_{1}\right)}\expectationvalue{ e^ {- i \Tr[ X \bm\chi_2 \bm\phi_1^T] - i \Tr[ X^\dagger\bm\chi_1 \bm\phi_2^T]}
}_{X}\ .

(3.24)

We now employ the following identity for averages with respect to the eGinUE:

\expectationvalue{e^{-\Tr[X A + X^\dagger B] }}_{X}=\exp[\Tr\left(AB\right)+\frac{\tau}{2}\Tr\left(A^{2}+B^{2}\right)]\ ,

(3.25)

where if we use
A=i\bm{\chi}_{2}\bm{\phi}_{1}^{T}
and
B=i\bm{\chi}_{1}\bm{\phi}_{2}^{T}
, the average in Eq. (
3.24
) becomes

\begin{split}\expectationvalue{e^{-\Tr[X A + X^\dagger B] }}_{X}&=\exp[\left(\bm{\phi}_{1}^{T}\bm{\chi}_{1}\right)\left(\bm{\phi}_{2}^{T}\bm{\chi}_{2}\right)+\frac{\tau}{2}\bigg(\left(\bm{\phi}_{1}^{T}\bm{\chi}_{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\bm{\chi}_{1}^{T}\bm{\phi}_{2}\right)^{2}\bigg)\bigg]\ .\end{split}

(3.26)

These terms, quartic in Grassmann variables, can be further bilinearized by employing auxilliary Gaussian integrals, i.e. Hubbard-Stratonovich transformations:

\begin{split}\exp[\left(\bm{\phi}_{1}^{T}\bm{\chi}_{1}\right)\left(\bm{\phi}_{2}^{T}\bm{\chi}_{2}\right)\bigg]&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}}dqd\bar{q}\ \exp[-|q|^{2}-q\left(\bm{\phi}_{2}^{T}\bm{\chi}_{2}\right)-\bar{q}\left(\bm{\phi}_{1}^{T}\bm{\chi}_{1}\right)\bigg]\hskip 9.24994pt\text{,}\\
\exp[\ \frac{\tau}{2}\left(\bm{\phi}_{1}^{T}\bm{\chi}_{2}\right)^{2}\ \bigg]&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dv_{1}\ \exp[-\frac{1}{2}\ v_{1}^{2}+v_{1}\ \sqrt{\tau}\ \left(\bm{\phi}_{1}^{T}\bm{\chi}_{2}\right)\bigg]\hskip 9.24994pt\text{,}\\
\exp[\ \frac{\tau}{2}\left(\bm{\chi}_{1}^{T}\bm{\phi}_{2}\right)^{2}\ \bigg]&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dv_{2}\ \exp[-\frac{1}{2}\ v_{2}^{2}+v_{2}\ \sqrt{\tau}\ \left(\bm{\chi}_{1}^{T}\bm{\phi}_{2}\right)\bigg]\hskip 9.24994pt\text{.}\end{split}

(3.27)

Rewriting the exponents using that
\phi\chi=(\phi\chi-\chi\phi)/2
, for anti-commuting variables
\chi
and
\phi
, allows the usage of the Pfaffian as defined in Eq. (
3.20
) with an anti-symmetric matrix
H
of the form

H=\left(\begin{matrix}0\ \hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}&0\ \hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}&\left(\sqrt{\mu}+\bar{q}\right)\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}&\left(iz-v_{1}\sqrt{\tau}\right)\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}\\
0\ \hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}&0\ \hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}&\left(i\bar{z}+v_{2}\sqrt{\tau}\right)\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}&\left(\sqrt{\mu}+q\right)\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}\\
-\left(\sqrt{\mu}+\bar{q}\right)\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}&-\left(i\bar{z}+v_{2}\sqrt{\tau}\right)\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}&0\ \hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}&0\ \hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}\\
-\left(iz-v_{1}\sqrt{\tau}\right)\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}&-\left(\sqrt{\mu}+q\right)\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}&0\ \hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}&0\ \hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}\\
\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}0&\widetilde{H}\\
-\widetilde{H}^{T}&0\\
\end{matrix}\right)\ .

(3.28)

Due to the structure of
H
we can use an identity in
[
78
]
to write
{\rm Pf\,}H=(-1)^{N}\det\widetilde{H}
, with

\widetilde{H}=F\otimes\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}=\left(\begin{matrix}\sqrt{\mu}+\bar{q}&iz-v_{1}\sqrt{\tau}\\
i\bar{z}+v_{2}\sqrt{\tau}&\sqrt{\mu}+q\\
\end{matrix}\right)\otimes\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}\ .

(3.29)

The determinant obeys the rule
\det\left(A\otimes B\right)=\left(\det A\right)^{m}\left(\det B\right)^{n}
for matrices
A
and
B
of dimension
n
and
m
respectively. In our case
A=F
and
B=\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}
thus
n=2
and
m=N
, therefore
\det\widetilde{H}=\left(\det F\right)^{N}
. The determinant of
F
can be immediately calculated yielding

{\rm Pf\,}H=(-1)^{N}\left(\det F\right)^{N}=(-1)^{N}\bigg[\left(\sqrt{\mu}+\bar{q}\right)\left(\sqrt{\mu}+q\right)+\left(\bar{z}-iv_{2}\sqrt{\tau}\right)\left(z+iv_{1}\sqrt{\tau}\right)\bigg]^{N}

(3.30)

and after straightforward manipulations our equation for the average determinant of
M
becomes

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\det M\bigg\rangle_{X}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{N}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\right)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}dqd\bar{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}dv_{1}dv_{2}\exp[-|q|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\bigg(v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}\bigg)\bigg]\ {\rm Pf\,}H

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}dqd\bar{q}\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}dv_{1}\,dv_{2}\,e^{-|q|^{2}-\frac{v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}}{2}}\bigg[\left(\sqrt{\mu}+\bar{q}\right)\left(\sqrt{\mu}+q\right)+f(z,v_{1},v_{2})\bigg]^{N}\ .

(3.31)

Employing the change of variables,
q=\sqrt{R}e^{i\theta}
and
\bar{q}=\sqrt{R}e^{-i\theta}
along with the definition of a Legendre polynomial in Eq. (
3.22
) we finally arrive at Eq. (
3.21
).
∎

The result derived in Proposition
3.3
is crucial to our analysis of
\langle\det M\rangle_{X_{1}}
and its derivative w.r.t.
\mu
at
\mu=0
. The results of this are shown in the following Corollary.

Corollary 3.4
.

With the average taken as in Eq. (
3.17
) using the
N-1
dimensional eGinUE matrix
X_{1}
and matrix
M
given Eq. (
3.15
), we have

\bigg\langle\det M\bigg\rangle_{X_{1}}\ \bigg|_{\mu=0}=\Gamma(N)\,\pi\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}\exp\left[\frac{1}{1-\tau^{2}}\bigg(|z|^{2}-\tau\ \textup{Re}(z^{2})\bigg)\right]\rho_{N}^{\textup{(eGinUE,c)}}(z)\ ,

(3.32)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\bigg\langle\det M\bigg\rangle_{X_{1}}\bigg|_{\mu=0}&=\Gamma(N)\pi\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}e^{\frac{|z|^{2}-\tau\textup{Re}(z^{2})}{1-\tau^{2}}}\bigg[\rho_{N-1}^{\textup{(eGinUE,c)}}(z)+(N-2)\rho_{N-2}^{\textup{(eGinUE,c)}}(z)-R_{N-3}\bigg]\ ,\end{split}

(3.33)

where
\rho_{N}^{\textup{(eGinUE,c)}}(z)
and
R_{N}
are defined in Eq. (
2.6
) and Eq. (
2.16
) respectively and
\Gamma(N)=(N-1)!
.

Proof.

We follow the steps performed in
[
54
]
to prove these statements. To derive Eq. (
3.32
) we directly use Proposition
3.3
, for
\mu=0
with
N\rightarrow N-1
and the Legendre polynomial property,
P_{N}(1)=1
, producing

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\det M\bigg\rangle_{X_{1}}\ \bigg|_{\mu=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}dv_{1}dv_{2}\ e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left(v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}dR\ e^{-R}\big[R+f(z,v_{1},v_{2})\big]^{N-1}\ .

(3.34)

Now employing the binomial theorem one notices the connection to the Hermite polynomials utilizing the first equality in Eq. (
2.5
), which upon performing the integral over
R
gives

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\det M\bigg\rangle_{X_{1}}\ \bigg|_{\mu=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left(\begin{matrix}N-1\\
k\\
\end{matrix}\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}dR\ e^{-R}R^{N-1-k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}dv_{1}dv_{2}\ e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left(v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}\right)}\Big(z+iv_{1}\sqrt{\tau}\Big)\Big(\bar{z}-iv_{2}\sqrt{\tau}\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=(N-1)!\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\ \frac{\tau^{k}}{k!}\ {\rm He\,}_{k}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{\tau}}\right)\ {\rm He\,}_{k}\left(\frac{\bar{z}}{\sqrt{\tau}}\right)\ .

(3.35)

Recalling the definition of the density in Eq. (
2.6
), we obtain

\bigg\langle\det M\bigg\rangle_{X_{1}}\ \bigg|_{\mu=0}=(N-1)!\,\pi\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}\exp\left[\frac{1}{1-\tau^{2}}\bigg(|z|^{2}-\tau\ \text{Re}(z)^{2}\bigg)\right]\rho_{N}^{\textup{(eGinUE,c)}}(z)

(3.36)

as desired for the first relation in Corollary
3.4
. For the second relation, we start with Eq. (
3.21
), replace
N\rightarrow N-1
, differentiate over
\mu
using the formula
[
54

\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\left(\frac{\mu+R+f(z,v_{1},v_{2})}{\sqrt{\big(R+f(z,v_{1},v_{2})\big)^{2}+\mu^{2}+2\mu\big(f(z,v_{1},v_{2})-R\big)}}\right)\Bigg|_{\mu=0}=\frac{2R}{\Big(R+f(z,v_{1},v_{2})\Big)^{2}}

(3.37)

and employ
P_{N-1}^{\prime}(1)=N(N-1)/2
, which follows per induction from
[
79
, 8.939.6]
. Collecting all terms we find

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\bigg\langle\det M\bigg\rangle_{X_{1}}\,\bigg|_{\mu=0}=\frac{N-1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}dv_{1}\ dv_{2}\ e^{-\frac{v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dR\ e^{-R}\ \Big(R+f(z,v_{1},v_{2})\Big)^{N-3}\big[(N-1)R+f(z,v_{1},v_{2})\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{N-1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}dv_{1}dv_{2}\ e^{-\frac{v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dR\ e^{-R}\ \bigg[(R+f(z,v_{1},v_{2}))^{N-2}+(N-2)(R+f(z,v_{1},v_{2}))^{N-3}\,R\bigg]\ .

(3.38)

From Eqs. (
3.32
) and (
3.34
), we immediately identify that the first of the two terms above is proportional to
\rho_{N-1}^{\textup{(eGinUE,c)}}(z)
. The second term can be rewritten via the binomial theorem and the definition of the Hermite polynomials in Eq. (
2.5
), allowing us to obtain

\begin{split}&\frac{(N-1)(N-2)}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dv_{1}\ \int_{\mathbb{R}}dv_{2}\ e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left(v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}dR\ R\ e^{-R}\ \Big(R+f(z,v_{1},v_{2})\Big)^{N-3}\\
&=\frac{(N-1)(N-2)}{2\pi}\ \sum_{k=0}^{N-3}\left(\begin{matrix}N-3\\
k\\
\end{matrix}\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}dRe^{-R}R^{N-2-k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}dv_{1}\,dv_{2}e^{-\frac{v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}}{2}}\Big(z+i\sqrt{\tau}v_{1}\Big)^{k}\Big(\bar{z}-i\sqrt{\tau}v_{2}\Big)^{k}\\
&=(N-1)!\sum_{k=0}^{N-3}\frac{\tau^{k}}{k!}(N-2-k){\rm He\,}_{k}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{\tau}}\right){\rm He\,}_{k}\left(\frac{\bar{z}}{\sqrt{\tau}}\right)\ ,\end{split}

(3.39)

which is proportional to
(N-2)\rho_{N-2}^{\textup{(eGinUE,c)}}(z)-R_{N-3}
. Combining the two terms we finally find

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\bigg\langle\det M\bigg\rangle_{X_{1}}\ \bigg|_{\mu=0}&=\Gamma(N)\pi\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}e^{\frac{|z|^{2}-\tau\ \textup{Re}(z)^{2}}{1-\tau^{2}}}\bigg[\rho_{N-1}^{\textup{(eGinUE,c)}}(z)+(N-2)\rho_{N-2}^{\textup{(eGinUE,c)}}(z)-R_{N-3}\bigg]\ ,\end{split}

(3.40)

as required.
∎

To finish the proof of Theorem
2.5
we insert our results for the averages over
X_{1}
from Corollary
3.4
into Eq. (
3.18
). After some simple manipulation of the constants one arrives at the required result of

\mathcal{O}^{(\textup{eGinUE},c)}_{N}(z)=\rho_{N}^{\textup{(eGinUE,c)}}(z)+(1-\tau^{2})\bigg[\rho_{N-1}^{\textup{(eGinUE,c)}}(z)+(N-2)\rho_{N-2}^{\textup{(eGinUE,c)}}(z)-R_{N-3}\bigg]\ .

(3.41)

3.3
Incomplete real Schur decomposition

Now we move onto our analysis of the real-valued eGinOE, where the eigenvalues may come either in complex conjugate pairs or be real numbers. The latter case is not considered in this work as it has already been extensively studied in
[
44
]
. To treat complex conjugate pairs we employ
incomplete real Schur decomposition
(IRSD) following the influential paper by Edelman
[
75
]
and restate the necessary information below.

Let
z=x+iy
(
y\neq 0
) be a complex eigenvalue of the
N\times N
real, non-Hermitian matrix
X
, with
N\geq 2
. We find the IRSD of
X
with respect to
z
in
[
75
]
, i.e.

X=Q\widetilde{X}Q^{T}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{with}\hskip 9.24994pt\widetilde{X}=\left(\begin{matrix}\begin{matrix}x&b\\
-c&x\\
\end{matrix}&W\\
0&X_{2}\\
\end{matrix}\right)\text{,}\ bc>0\text{,}\ b\geq c\text{,}\ y=\sqrt{bc}\ ,

(3.42)

where
Q
is a real symmetric matrix with
Q^{T}=Q
and
Q^{2}=\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N}
. The block matrix
W
is real and has size
2\times(N-2)
, i.e.
W=\left(\mathbf{w}_{1}\ \text{,}\ \mathbf{w}_{2}\right)^{T}
, where
\mathbf{w}_{1}
,
\mathbf{w}_{2}
are vectors with
N-2
real independent entries. The matrix
X_{2}
is an
(N-2)\times(N-2)
dimensional real matrix obeying the same statistical properties as
X
. This, in principle could be further decomposed into blocks
[
75
]
. As the eGinOE is an extension of the GinOE, the derivation of the representation of the left and right eigenvectors is exactly the same in both ensembles. Thus, we reuse results from
[
54
]
, where the case of complex eigenvalues in the GinOE was considered. Specifically we utilise their Eqs. (3.5) to (3.9) to specify the self-overlap as

\begin{split}\mathcal{O}_{z}&=\left({\mathbf{x}}_{L}^{\dagger}{\mathbf{x}}_{L}\right)\left({\mathbf{x}}_{R}^{\dagger}{\mathbf{x}}_{R}\right)=\frac{1}{4}\left(2+\frac{c^{2}+b^{2}}{bc}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{c}{b}\right)\left(\mathbf{b}_{N-2}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}_{N-2}\right)\ ,\end{split}

(3.43)

where
\mathbf{b}_{N-2}
is given by

\mathbf{b}_{N-2}^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\mathbf{w}_{1}^{T}-i\sqrt{\frac{b}{c}}\mathbf{w}_{2}^{T}\right)\left(z\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N-2}-X_{2}\right)^{-1}\ .

(3.44)

Similarly to the previous section one can define the matrix

B\equiv\left(z\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N-2}-X_{2}\right)^{\dagger}\left(z\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.8pt\normalsize 1}_{N-2}-X_{2}\right)

(3.45)

so as to write

\begin{split}\left(\mathbf{b}_{N-2}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}_{N-2}\right)&=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{w}_{1}^{T}\ B^{-1}\ \mathbf{w}_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\exp\left[2\ \text{arcsinh}\left(\frac{\delta}{2y}\right)\right]\mathbf{w}_{2}^{T}\ B^{-1}\ \mathbf{w}_{2}\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\ i\ \exp\left[\text{arcsinh}\left(\frac{\delta}{2y}\right)\right]\bigg[\mathbf{w}_{1}^{T}\ B^{-1}\ \mathbf{w}_{2}-\mathbf{w}_{2}^{T}\ B^{-1}\ \mathbf{w}_{1}\bigg]\ .\end{split}

(3.46)

Now we change the variables from
b
and
c
to
y=\sqrt{bc}
and
\delta=b-c>0
which allows us to rewrite the mean self-overlap as

\begin{split}\mathcal{O}_{z}=\widetilde{c}_{1}+\widetilde{c}_{2}\ \left(\mathbf{b}_{N-2}^{\dagger}\mathbf{b}_{N-2}\right)\ ,\end{split}

(3.47)

where the constants are
y-
dependent and given by

\begin{split}\widetilde{c}_{1}&=\frac{1}{4}\left(2+\frac{\delta^{2}+2y^{2}}{y^{2}}\right)\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\widetilde{c}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\exp\left[-2\ \text{arcsinh}\left(\frac{\delta}{2y}\right)\right]\right)\ .\end{split}

(3.48)

The equation for the Jacobian of the IRSD (before the last change of variables) can be found in
[

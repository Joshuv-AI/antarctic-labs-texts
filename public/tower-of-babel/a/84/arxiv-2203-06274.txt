2.2
Integral transforms on
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})

For

and
\phi\in\mathbb{R}
, let us define the operator
\mathscr{I}_{\phi}:\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
as

\displaystyle[\mathscr{I}_{\phi}f](w)=\begin{cases}f(w),&\mbox{if $\phi\equiv 0$ mod $2\pi$,}\\
f(-w),&\mbox{if $\phi\equiv\pi$ mod $2\pi$,}\\
\\
\displaystyle|\sin\phi|^{-1/2}\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}\!\left(\frac{\frac{1}{2}(w^{2}+w^{\prime 2})\cos\phi-ww^{\prime}}{\sin\phi}\right)f(w^{\prime})\mathrm{d}w^{\prime},&\mbox{if $\phi\equiv\hskip-10.81218pt/\hskip 5.69046pt0$ mod $\pi$.}\end{cases}\>\>

(2.12)

We will see in Section
6
how to interpret

as the solution at time

to a quantum harmonic oscillator with initial condition
f
. Let us also introduce the function
\phi\mapsto\sigma_{\phi}
, given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{\phi}:=\begin{cases}2\nu,&\mbox{if $\phi=\nu\pi$, $\nu\in\mathbb{Z}$;}\\
2\nu+1,&\mbox{if $\nu\pi<\phi<(\nu+1)\pi$, $\nu\in\mathbb{Z}$.}\end{cases}

(2.13)

Finally, define the operator
\mathscr{F}_{\phi}:\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle[\mathscr{F}_{\phi}f](w)=\mathrm{e}\!\left(-\sigma_{\phi}/8\right)[\mathscr{I}_{\phi}f](w)

(2.14)

The reason for the factor
\mathrm{e}\!\left(-\sigma_{\phi}/8\right)
in (
2.14
) is that
\lim_{\phi\to 0^{\pm}}[\mathscr{I}_{\phi}f](w)=\mathrm{e}\!\left(\pm\tfrac{1}{8}\right)f(w)
for

.
The 1-parameter family

is a continuous group of unitary operators on
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. The particular case
\mathscr{F}_{\frac{\pi}{2}}
coincides with the classical unitary Fourier transform. Note that

is
4\pi

\overline{\mathscr{F}_{\phi}f}=\mathscr{F}_{-\phi}\overline{f}
. For a more compact notation, unless explicitly stated, we will write

instead of
\mathscr{F}_{\phi}f

Lemma 2.1
.

The measure
\mu^{\mathbf{0}}
is
\Gamma
-invariant.

Proof.

Let
A=A_{H}\times A_{R}
be a measurable rectangle, where
A_{H}
is a Borel subset of
\mathfrak{H}\times[0,2\pi)
and
A_{R}
is a Borel subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Suppose that
A
has finite
\mu^{\mathbf{0}}
-measure. Let us show that
\mu^{\mathbf{0}}(\gamma^{-1}A)=\mu^{\mathbf{0}}(A)
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
. The statement of the lemma follows from this fact. Since
A
is a measurable rectangle,
\mathbf{1}_{A}(x+iy,\phi;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}})=\mathbf{1}_{A_{H}}{x+iy,\phi}\mathbf{1}_{A_{R}}({\text{\boldmath$\xi$}})
. Let
\gamma=(M;\mathbf{v})
. We have

\displaystyle\mu^{\mathbf{0}}(\gamma^{-1}A)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\mathbf{1}_{\gamma^{-1}A}(x+iy,\phi;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}})\mathrm{d}\mu^{\mathbf{0}}=\int_{G}\mathbf{1}_{A}(\gamma(x+iy,\phi;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}))\mathrm{d}\mu^{\mathbf{0}}

(2.53)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\mathbf{1}_{A_{H}}(M(x+iy,\phi))\mathbf{1}_{A_{R}}(\gamma\cdot{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}})\mathrm{d}\mu^{\mathbf{0}}

(2.54)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{1}_{A_{R}}(\gamma\cdot{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}})\,\mathrm{d}\nu\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{A_{H}}(M(x+iy,\phi))\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}\phi}{y^{2}}

(2.55)

Note that the action of
M
on
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
preserves the measure
\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}\phi}{y^{2}}
. Moreover, using the generators of
\Gamma
, we see that the action of each
\gamma\in\Gamma
is a bijection on the support of
\nu
, i.e.

\displaystyle\bigcup_{{\text{\boldmath$k$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!0\!\\
\!0\!\end{smallmatrix}\right)+{\text{\boldmath$k$}},\,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!1/2\!\\
\!0\!\end{smallmatrix}\right)+{\text{\boldmath$k$}},\,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!0\!\\
\!1/2\!\end{smallmatrix}\right)+{\text{\boldmath$k$}}\right\}.

(2.56)

Since
\nu
is atomic with equal weights for its atoms, we obtain
\gamma^{-1}_{*}\nu=\nu
. Therefore (
2.55
) implies
\mu^{\mathbf{0}}(\gamma^{-1}A)=\mu^{\mathbf{0}}(A)
.
∎

The projection of the measure
\mu
(respectively
\mu^{\mathbf{0}}
) from
G
to
\Gamma\backslash G
will be denoted by
\mu_{\Gamma\backslash G}
(respectively
\mu^{\mathbf{0}}_{\Gamma\backslash G}
). These projection measures are well-defined thanks to the

and therefore

and

are both probability measures on
\Gamma\backslash G
.

Remark 2.2
.

It is not hard to numerically sample points
(x,y,\phi;\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!\xi_{1}\!\\
\!\xi_{2}\!\end{smallmatrix}\right))\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
according to
\mu^{\mathbf{0}}_{\Gamma\backslash G}
or
\mu_{\Gamma\backslash G}
, assuming that we have a generator of random numbers uniformly distributed on
[0,1]
, such as
\mathtt{RandomReal[]}
in the Mathematica
©
software. The only nontrivial step is to generate pairs of real numbers
(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})}
distributed according to the joint density
\frac{3}{\pi y^{2}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})}}(x,y)
.
The marginal density of
x\in[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
is
\int_{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}^{\infty}\frac{3}{\pi y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y=\frac{3}{\pi\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}
and hence its distribution function is
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})\ni t\mapsto\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3\arcsin(t))}{\pi}\in[0,1]
. If
x_{0}
is uniformly distributed on
[0,1]
, then evaluating the inverse distribution function
[0,1]\ni s\mapsto\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi s}{3}-\frac{\pi}{6}\right)\in[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
at
s=x_{0}
yields a random number distributed according to the marginal density of
x
. Since the conditional density of
y
given
x
is
\frac{3}{\pi y^{2}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})}}(x,y)/\frac{3}{\pi\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}=\frac{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}{y^{2}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})}}(x,y)
, we find the conditional distribution function of

given

as
[\sqrt{1-x^{2}},\infty)\ni t\mapsto 1-\frac{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}{t}\in[0,1]
. If
y_{0}
is uniformly distributed on
[0,1]
, then evaluating the inverse conditional distribution function
[0,1]\ni s\mapsto\frac{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}{1-s}\in[\sqrt{1-x^{2}},\infty)
at

yields a random number distributed according to the conditional density of
y
given
x
. In summary, if

3.1
Equidistribution in the smooth rational case

Theorem 3.1

(Limit Theorem, rational case)
.

Let
F:\Gamma\setminus G\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a bounded continuous function, and
\lambda
a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}F(\Gamma(I;\boldsymbol{0})\Psi^{u}\Phi^{t})\>\mathrm{d}\lambda(u)=\int_{\Gamma\setminus G}F\>\mathrm{d}\mu_{\Gamma\backslash G}^{\mathbf{0}},

where
\mu_{\Gamma\backslash G}^{\mathbf{0}}
is the probability measure defined in (
2.11
).

Proof.

Consider the action of
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{T}^{2}=\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
via affine transformations, i.e.
(M,\mathbf{v})({\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}+\mathbb{Z}^{2})=(\mathbf{v}+M{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}+\mathbb{Z}^{2})
,
and let
\Sigma=\mathrm{Stab}_{\Gamma}(\mathbf{0}+\mathbb{Z}^{2})
. Using the generators of
\Gamma
it is not hard to see that
\Sigma=\Gamma_{\theta}\ltimes\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, where
\Gamma_{\theta}=\langle\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&2\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\rangle
is the so-called
theta group
.
Note that the orbit of
\mathbf{0}+\mathbb{Z}^{2}
under
\Gamma
consists of three points in
\mathbb{T}^{2}=(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{2}
, namely
S=\mathrm{Orb}_{\Gamma}(\mathbf{0}+\mathbb{Z}^{2})=\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!0\!\\
\!0\!\end{smallmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!1/2\!\\
\!0\!\end{smallmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!0\!\\
\!1/2\!\end{smallmatrix}\right)\}+\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
By the orbit-stabiliser theorem we have

.

Let

, and

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\lambda\!\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}:\tfrac{1}{N}\left|S_{N}\overline{S_{\lfloor bN\rfloor}}(x;c,\alpha)\right|>R^{2}\right\}=\mu_{\Gamma\backslash G}\!\left\{|\Theta_{\chi}\overline{\Theta_{\chi_{b}}}|>R^{2}\right\}.

Proof of Corollaries
3.11

. Observe that

(3.38)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{N}\left|S_{N}\overline{S_{\lfloor sN\rfloor}}(x;c,\alpha)\right|=

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{N}\left|S_{N}^{\chi}\overline{S_{N}^{\chi_{s}}}(x;c,\alpha)\right|+O\left(\tfrac{1}{N}\right)+O\left(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left|\tfrac{S_{N}^{\chi}(x;c,\alpha)}{\sqrt{N}}\right|\right)+O\left(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left|\tfrac{S_{N}^{\chi_{s}}(x;c,\alpha)}{\sqrt{N}}\right|\right).

3.4

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\lambda\!\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}:\tfrac{1}{N}\left|S_{N}\overline{S_{\lfloor bN\rfloor}}(x;c,\alpha)\right|\in\mathcal{A}\right\}=\begin{cases}\mu^{\mathbf{0}}_{\Gamma\backslash G}\!\left\{|\Theta_{\chi}\overline{\Theta_{\chi_{b}}}|\in\mathcal{A}\right\}&if $\alpha=c=0$,\\

4
Growth in the cusp and tail bounds for
\Theta_{f_{2}}\overline{\Theta_{f_{2}}}

In this section we focus on the size of
\Theta_{f_{1}}\overline{\Theta_{f_{2}}}
when
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are of sufficient regularity. The following lemma, which extends Lemma 2.1 in
[
6
]
, is crucial and will be used later. Recall the definition of
\kappa_{\eta}(f)
(
2.26
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Given
\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, write
\xi_{2}=k+\theta
, with
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
\theta\in(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
. Let
\eta>1
, then, there exist constants
d>0
and
C_{\eta}=d^{\eta}\zeta(\eta)^{2}
such that for every
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{\eta}(\mathbb{R})
we have

\displaystyle\left|\Theta_{f_{1}}\overline{\Theta_{f_{2}}}(x+iy,\phi,\boldsymbol{\xi})-y^{\frac{1}{2}}\,(f_{1})_{\phi}(-\theta y^{\frac{1}{2}})\,\overline{(f_{2})_{\phi}(-\theta y^{\frac{1}{2}})}\right|\leq C_{\eta}\kappa_{\eta}(f_{1})\kappa_{\eta}(f_{2})y^{-\frac{(\eta-1)}{2}}

(4.1)

for every
y\geq\frac{1}{2}
, and every
x,\phi,\boldsymbol{\xi}
. We may take
d=2^{6}
.

Proof.

The regularity assumption on
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
ensures that the series defining
\Theta_{f_{1}}
and
\Theta_{f_{2}}
are absolutely convergent. Therefore we may write

\displaystyle\Theta_{f_{1}}\overline{\Theta_{f_{2}}}(x+iy,\phi,\boldsymbol{\xi})=y^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{n,m\in\mathbb{Z}}(f_{1})_{\phi}((n-\xi_{2})y^{\frac{1}{2}})\,\overline{(f_{2})_{\phi}((m-\xi_{2})y^{\frac{1}{2}})}

(4.2)

\displaystyle\hskip 156.49014pt\mathrm{e}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}(n-\xi_{2})^{2}x+n\xi_{1}-\tfrac{1}{2}(m-\xi_{2})^{2}x-m\xi_{1}\right).

Since the term
y^{\frac{1}{2}}\,(f_{1})_{\phi}(-\theta y^{\frac{1}{2}})\,\overline{(f_{2})_{\phi}(-\theta y^{\frac{1}{2}})}
in (
4.1
) corresponds to the term
n=m=k
, it is enough to prove that
the above sum restricted to
(n,m)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\smallsetminus\{(k,k)\}
is bounded in absolute value by
C_{\eta}\kappa_{\eta}(f_{1})\kappa_{\eta}(f_{2})y^{\frac{-(\eta-1)}{2}}
.
Indeed, by (
2.26
),

\displaystyle\>y^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{(n,m)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus{\{(k,k)\}}}\left|(f_{1})_{\phi}((n-\xi_{2})y^{\frac{1}{2}})\,\overline{(f_{2})_{\phi}((m-\xi_{2})y^{\frac{1}{2}})}\right|\leq

(4.3)

\displaystyle\leq\>y^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{(n,m)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\setminus{\{(k,k)\}}}\frac{\kappa_{\eta}(f_{1})}{\left(1+(n-\xi_{2})^{2}y\right)^{\frac{\eta}{2}}}\frac{\kappa_{\eta}(f_{2})}{\left(1+(m-\xi_{2})^{2}y\right)^{\frac{\eta}{2}}}=

(4.4)

\displaystyle=y^{\frac{1}{2}}\kappa_{\eta}(f_{1})\kappa_{\eta}(f_{2})\left(y^{-\eta}\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus\{k\}}\frac{1}{\left(y^{-1}+(n-\xi_{2})^{2}\right)^{\frac{\eta}{2}}}\right)^{2}+\right.

(4.5)

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 113.81102pt+2y^{-\frac{\eta}{2}}\frac{1}{\left(1+(k-\xi_{2})^{2}y\right)^{\frac{\eta}{2}}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus\{k\}}\frac{1}{\left(y^{-1}+(n-\xi_{2})^{2}\right)^{\frac{\eta}{2}}}\right).

(4.6)

Now note that the sum appearing in both (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) is bounded above by

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(\ell-\theta)^{\eta}}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(j+\theta)^{\eta}}.

(4.7)

By setting
\theta=\pm\frac{1}{2}
we can find an upper bound for (
4.7
) given by
2\zeta(\eta)(2^{\eta}-1)
.
We have therefore found an upper bound for the absolute value of the sum (
4.2
) restricted to
(n,m)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\smallsetminus\{(k,k)\}
of the form

\displaystyle y^{\frac{1}{2}}\kappa_{\eta}(f_{1})\kappa_{\eta}(f_{2})\left(A_{\eta}y^{-\eta}+B_{\eta}y^{-\frac{\eta}{2}}\right),

(4.8)

where
A_{\eta}=(2\zeta(\eta)(2^{\eta}-1))^{2}
and
B_{\eta}=4\zeta(\eta)(2^{\eta}-1)
. Since
y^{-\eta}\leq 2^{\frac{\eta}{2}}y^{-\frac{\eta}{2}}
for
y\geq\frac{1}{2}
and
B_{\eta}\leq 2^{\frac{\eta}{2}}A_{\eta}
, we obtain (
4.1
) with
C_{\eta}\geq 2^{1+\frac{\eta}{2}}A_{\eta}
and the result follows.
∎

4.1
Tail bound in the smooth case

We now consider the function
\Theta_{f_{1}}\overline{\Theta_{f_{2}}}:\Gamma\backslash G\to\mathbb{C}
as
two
possible complex-valued random variables by assuming that its argument is distributed either according to
\mu_{\Gamma\backslash G}^{\mathbf{0}}
or
\mu_{\Gamma\backslash G}
. In both cases, we use Lemma
4.1
to accurately study the tails of these random variables.

4.1.1
Tail bound in the smooth rational case

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\eta>1
. Then, for every
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{\eta}(\mathbb{R})
and
R^{2}>2^{\eta}C_{\eta}\kappa_{\eta}(f_{1})\kappa_{\eta}(f_{2})
we have

\displaystyle\mu_{\Gamma\backslash G}^{\mathbf{0}}\left(|\Theta_{f_{1}}\overline{\Theta_{f_{2}}}|>R^{2}\right)=\frac{2D_{\mathrm{rat}}(f_{1},f_{2})}{\pi^{2}R^{4}}\left(1+O_{\eta}\!\left((\kappa_{\eta}(f_{1})\kappa_{\eta}(f_{2}))^{\eta}R^{-2\eta}\right)\right),

(4.9)

where
C_{\eta}
is as in Lemma
4.1
,

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{rat}}(f_{1},f_{2})=\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|(f_{1})_{\phi}(0)\,(f_{2})_{\phi}(0)\right|^{2}\>\mathrm{d}\phi,

(4.10)

and the implied constant by
O_{\eta}
-notation in (
4.9
) can be taken to be
3(2^{\eta}C_{\eta})^{\eta}
.

Proof.

Recall the fundamental domain (
2.51
) for the action of
\Gamma
on
G
, and define

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{T}=\{(x+iy,\phi;\boldsymbol{\xi})\in\mathfrak{H}\times[0,\pi)\times(-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}]^{2}:\>x\in[-1/2,1/2),\>y\geq T\}

(4.11)

so that we have the containments
\mathcal{F}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{\frac{1}{2}}
. Set
\widetilde{\kappa}=C_{\eta}\kappa_{\eta}(f_{1})\kappa_{\eta}(f_{2})
. We obtain, by Lemma
4.1
,

\displaystyle\mu_{\Gamma\backslash G}^{\mathbf{0}}\left\{(x+iy,\phi;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}})\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}:|\Theta_{f_{1}}\overline{\Theta_{f_{2}}}(x+iy,\phi;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}})|>R^{2}\right\}\leq

(4.12)

\displaystyle\leq\mu^{\mathbf{0}}\left\{(x+iy,\phi;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}})\in\mathcal{F}_{\frac{1}{2}}:|\Theta_{f_{1}}\overline{\Theta_{f_{2}}}(x+iy,\phi;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}})|>R^{2}\right\}\leq

(4.13)

\displaystyle\leq\mu^{\mathbf{0}}\left\{(x+iy,\phi;\boldsymbol{\xi})\in\mathcal{F}_{\frac{1}{2}}:\left|y^{\frac{1}{2}}\,(f_{1})_{\phi}(-\theta y^{\frac{1}{2}})\,(f_{2})_{\phi}(-\theta y^{\frac{1}{2}})\right|+\widetilde{\kappa}y^{-\frac{\eta-1}{2}}>R^{2}\right\}.

(4.14)

Since
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{\eta}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\displaystyle\left|(f_{1})_{\phi}(-\theta y^{\frac{1}{2}})\,(f_{2})_{\phi}(-\theta y^{\frac{1}{2}})\right|\leq\frac{\kappa_{\eta}(f_{1})\kappa_{\eta}(f_{2})}{\left(1+\left|-\theta y^{\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}\right)^{\eta}}\leq\kappa_{\eta}(f_{1})\kappa_{\eta}(f_{2}).

(4.15)

Therefore, the condition

\displaystyle\left|y^{\frac{1}{2}}(f_{1})_{\phi}(-\theta y^{\frac{1}{2}})\,(f_{2})_{\phi}(-\theta y^{\frac{1}{2}})\right|+\widetilde{\kappa}y^{-\frac{\eta-1}{2}}>R^{2}

(4.16)

in (
4.14
) implies

\displaystyle\widetilde{\kappa}\left(y^{\frac{1}{2}}+y^{-\frac{\eta-1}{2}}\right)>R^{2}.

(4.17)

Furthermore, since
y\geq\frac{1}{2}
for all points
(x+iy,\phi;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}})\in\mathcal{F}_{\frac{1}{2}}
, we have
y^{-\tfrac{\eta-1}{2}}\leq 2^{\frac{\eta}{2}}y^{\frac{1}{2}}
and (
4.17
) in turn implies

\displaystyle y>\frac{R^{4}}{\widetilde{\kappa}^{2}4^{\eta}}.

(4.18)

Therefore (
4.16
) implies

\displaystyle\left|y^{\frac{1}{2}}(f_{1})_{\phi}(-\theta y^{\frac{1}{2}})\,(f_{2})_{\phi}(-\theta y^{\frac{1}{2}})\right|+\widetilde{\kappa}\left(\frac{R^{4}}{\widetilde{\kappa}^{2}4^{\eta}}\right)^{\!-\frac{\eta-1}{2}}>R^{2}.

(4.19)

Let us introduce the shorthand

\displaystyle I_{T}^{\mathbf{0}}(\Lambda)=\mu^{\mathbf{0}}\left\{(x+iy,\phi;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}})\in\mathcal{F}_{T}:\left|y^{\frac{1}{2}}\,(f_{1})_{\phi}(-\theta y^{\frac{1}{2}})\,(f_{2})_{\phi}(-\theta y^{\frac{1}{2}})\right|>\Lambda\right\}.

(4.20)

Then, (
4.14
) and (
4.19
) yield the upper bound

\displaystyle\mu_{\Gamma\backslash G}^{\mathbf{0}}\left(|\Theta_{f_{1}}\overline{\Theta_{f_{2}}}|>R^{2}\right)\leq I_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\mathbf{0}}\!\left(R^{2}-\widetilde{\kappa}\!\left(\tfrac{2^{\eta}\widetilde{\kappa}}{R^{2}}\right)^{\eta-1}\right).

(4.21)

Similarly, we obtain the lower bound

\displaystyle\mu_{\Gamma\backslash G}^{\mathbf{0}}\left(|\Theta_{f_{1}}\overline{\Theta_{f_{2}}}|>R^{2}\right)\geq I_{1}^{\mathbf{0}}\left(R^{2}+\widetilde{\kappa}\!\left(\tfrac{2^{\eta}\widetilde{\kappa}}{R^{2}}\right)^{\eta-1}\right).

(4.22)

Using the fact that
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{\eta}(\mathbb{R})
, with
\eta>1
, we see that for fixed
\theta\neq 0
we have
\displaystyle\lim_{y\to\infty}y^{\frac{1}{2}}(f_{1})_{\phi}(-\theta y^{\frac{1}{2}})\,(f_{2})_{\phi}(-\theta y^{\frac{1}{2}})=0
. Thus, in order for the inequality in (
4.20
)
to hold for large
\Lambda
, we must have that
|\theta y^{\frac{1}{2}}|=O(1)
as
y\to\infty
, i.e.
\theta=O(y^{-\frac{1}{2}})
. In particular, if
\theta
is fixed, then
\theta=0
(i.e.
\xi_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
).
Recall (
2.11
) and note that integrating the indicator of the event
\{|y^{\frac{1}{2}}(f_{1})_{\phi}(-\theta y^{\frac{1}{2}})\,(f_{2})_{\phi}(-\theta y^{\frac{1}{2}})|>\Lambda\}
against the measure
\mu^{\mathbf{0}}
on
\mathcal{F}_{T}
forces
{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}}\in\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!0\!\\
\!0\!\end{smallmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!1/2\!\\
\!0\!\end{smallmatrix}\right),\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\!0\!\\
\!1/2\!\end{smallmatrix}\right)\}
. More precisely,
\xi_{2}=\theta=0
with probability
\frac{2}{3}
and
\xi_{2}=\theta=\tfrac{1}{2}
with probability
\frac{1}{3}
. The terms with
\xi_{2}=\theta=\tfrac{1}{2}
do not contribute for
\Lambda
large. Indeed, note that if
\xi_{2}=\theta=\tfrac{1}{2}
, then we have the estimates

\displaystyle\frac{\left|y^{\frac{1}{2}}(f_{1})_{\phi}(-\tfrac{1}{2}y^{\frac{1}{2}})\,(f_{2})_{\phi}(-\tfrac{1}{2}y^{\frac{1}{2}})\right|}{\kappa_{\eta}(f_{1})\kappa_{\eta}(f_{2})}\leq\frac{y^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(1+\frac{1}{4}y)^{{\eta}}}<4^{\eta}y^{\frac{1}{2}-\eta}\leq 2^{3\eta}

(4.23)

for
y\geq\frac{1}{2}
. Therefore, if we assume

\displaystyle\Lambda\geq 2^{3\eta}\kappa_{\eta}(f_{1})\,\kappa_{\eta}(f_{2}),

(4.24)

then
we obtain

\displaystyle I_{T}^{\mathbf{0}}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{\pi^{2}}\frac{2}{3}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{y>\max\!\left\{T,\left|(f_{1})_{\phi}(0)\,(f_{2})_{\phi}(0)\right|^{-2}\Lambda^{2}\right\}}\frac{1}{y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x.

(4.25)

Note that (
4.24
) implies that
\Lambda\geq\kappa_{\eta}(f_{1})\,\kappa_{\eta}(f_{2})
and hence

\left|(f_{1})_{\phi}(0)\,(f_{2})_{\phi}(0)\right|^{2}\leq\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{T}
for

. In this case (
4.25
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle I_{T}^{\mathbf{0}}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{|(f_{1})_{\phi}(0)\,(f_{2})_{\phi}(0)|^{-2}\Lambda^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{y^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x=

(4.26)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}|(f_{1})_{\phi}(0)\,(f_{2})_{\phi}(0)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\phi.

(4.27)

We now use the inequality (valid for

)

\displaystyle\left|1-\frac{1}{(1\mp u)^{2}}\right|\leq 6u,

(4.28)

and hence

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1\mp u)^{2}}=1+O(u)

(4.29)

where the implied constant can be taken to be
6
. Hence, with

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle=R^{2}\mp\widetilde{\kappa}\!\left(\frac{2^{\eta}\widetilde{\kappa}}{R^{2}}\right)^{\eta-1},

(4.30)

we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}}=\frac{1}{\left(R^{2}\mp\widetilde{\kappa}\!\left(\frac{2^{\eta}\widetilde{\kappa}}{R^{2}}\right)^{\eta-1}\right)^{2}}=\frac{1}{R^{4}}\frac{1}{\left(1\mp\!\left(\frac{2^{\eta-1}\widetilde{\kappa}}{R^{2}}\right)^{\eta}\right)^{2}}=\frac{1}{R^{4}}\left(1+O(2^{\eta(\eta-1)}\tilde{\kappa}^{\eta}R^{-2\eta})\right)

provided

\displaystyle R^{2}>2^{\eta}\tilde{\kappa}.

(4.32)

Therefore, the assumption (
4.32
) and the bound (

) allow us to write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}}=\frac{1}{R^{4}}\left(1+O_{\eta}\!\left(\left(\kappa_{\eta}(f_{1})\kappa_{\eta}(f_{2})\right)^{\eta}R^{-2\eta}\right)\right),

(4.33)

where the constant implied by the

-notation can be taken to be

. Finally we notice that (

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle=R^{2}\mp\widetilde{\kappa}\!\left(\frac{2^{\eta}\widetilde{\kappa}}{R^{2}}\right)^{\eta-1}>\widetilde{\kappa}\left(2^{\eta}-1\right)\geq\tilde{\kappa}.

Therefore, using the fact that

\displaystyle I_{T}^{\mathbf{0}}(\Lambda)=\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}D_{\mathrm{rat}}(f_{1},f_{2})\,R^{-4}\left(1+O_{\eta}\!\left((\kappa_{\eta}(f_{1})\kappa_{\eta}(f_{2}))^{\eta}R^{-2\eta}\right)\right)

(4.35)

for

either equal to
(\frac{1}{2},R^{2}-\widetilde{\kappa}\!\left(\frac{2^{\eta}\widetilde{\kappa}}{R^{2}}\right)^{\eta-1})
or to

. Finally, the bounds (

) and (

∎

4.1.2
Tail bound in the smooth irrational case

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\eta>1
. Then for every

and
R^{2}>2^{\eta}C_{\eta}\kappa_{\eta}(f_{1})\kappa_{\eta}(f_{2})

\displaystyle\mu_{\Gamma\backslash G}\left(|\Theta_{f_{1}}\overline{\Theta_{f_{2}}}|>R^{2}\right)=\frac{2D_{\mathrm{irr}}(f_{1},f_{2})}{\pi^{2}R^{6}}\left(1+O_{\eta}\!\left((\kappa_{\eta}(f_{1})\kappa_{\eta}(f_{2}))^{\eta}R^{-2\eta}\right)\right),

\displaystyle D_{\mathrm{irr}}(f_{1},f_{2})=\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|(f_{1})_{\phi}(w)\,(f_{2})_{\phi}(w)\right|^{3}\>\mathrm{d}\phi\,\mathrm{d}w

and the constant implied by the

-notation in (

7(2^{\eta}C_{\eta})^{\eta}

Proceeding as in the proof of Lemma

\displaystyle I_{T}(\Lambda)=\mu\left\{(x+iy,\phi;{\text{\boldmath$\xi$}})\in\mathcal{F}_{T}:\left|y^{\frac{1}{2}}(f_{1})_{\phi}(-\theta y^{\frac{1}{2}})\,(f_{2})_{\phi}(-\theta y^{\frac{1}{2}})\right|>\Lambda\right\},

(4.38)

then we obtain the bounds

\displaystyle I_{1}\!\left(R^{2}+\widetilde{\kappa}\!\left(\tfrac{2^{\eta}\widetilde{\kappa}}{R^{2}}\right)^{\eta-1}\right)\leq\mu_{\Gamma\backslash G}\left(|\Theta_{f_{1}}\overline{\Theta_{f_{2}}}|>R^{2}\right)\leq I_{\frac{1}{2}}\!\left(R^{2}-\widetilde{\kappa}\!\left(\tfrac{2^{\eta}\widetilde{\kappa}}{R^{2}}\right)^{\eta-1}\right).

(4.39)

\displaystyle\Lambda\geq 2\kappa_{\eta}(f_{1})\,\kappa_{\eta}(f_{2}),

(4.40)

6.1
Divergence of expected displacement for harmonic quantum oscillator with piecewise initial data

We illustrate here an application of Theorem
6.4
. The equation (
6.1
) describes a 1-dimensional quantum harmonic oscillator.
If we interpret
x\mapsto|f(t,x)|^{2}
as a probability density, then we can consider the
m
-th moment of the displacement of the quantum particle at time
t
, i.e.
\langle x^{m}\rangle=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{m}|f(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x
. It is well known that the regularity of the initial condition
f
determines the decay of
f(t,\cdot)
. If, for instance,
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, then
f(t,\cdot)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
and hence all moments
\langle x^{m}\rangle
are finite.
However, if the initial condition is only piecewise
C^{1}
with finite jumps (such as
f=\chi
), then
\langle x\rangle
diverges because
|f(t,x)|^{2}
decays too slowly as
|x|\to\infty
. In Theorem
6.8
below we consider smooth approximations to such initial data, and we precisely quantify the rate at which the divergence in the expected displacement occurs as the approximation increases.

For the rest of this section,
f_{\epsilon}
will not denote
\mathscr{F}_{\epsilon}f
, and we will instead use it to denote a sequence of functions approaching
f
in the suitable sense as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
.

Definition 6.7
.

A sequence
(f_{\epsilon})_{1\geq\epsilon>0}
of
C^{1}
functions converging in

to a piecewise
C^{1}

if there exists a constant

such that for all
\epsilon>0
we have

\|f_{\epsilon}\|_{1}\leq C
,
\|f_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\|_{1}\leq C
, and

\|f_{\epsilon}^{\prime\prime}\|_{1}\leq C\epsilon^{-1}

That is to say, the derivative of the functions
f_{\epsilon}
will diverge as
\epsilon\to 0
to approximate the piecewise function
f_{0}
, but such divergence must occur in proportionally small intervals.

This definition can be motivated as follows. Let

be piecewise
C^{1}
with finitely many jump discontinuities at the points

, and construct a sequence
f_{\epsilon}(x)=f_{0}(x)+\sum_{j}g_{j}((x-x_{j})/\epsilon)
where the

chosen in order to make

differentiable. By direct computation one can check that

in

with bounded gradient as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
.

Appendix A
Bounds for the zeta function

The constant
C_{\eta}=2^{6\eta}\zeta(\eta)^{2}
in Lemmata
4.1
and
4.3
involves the Riemann zeta function and is used throughout Sections 4, 7, and 8 of the paper. In the proofs of Theorems
8.4
and
8.7
we use the upper bound (
8.50
), which we prove in this appendix.

For
\eta>1
let
\zeta_{\mathrm{a}}(\eta)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-1}n^{-\eta}
be the alternating zeta function (also known as the Dirichlet eta function).

Lemma A.1
.

For
\eta>1
we have

\displaystyle\zeta_{\mathrm{a}}(\eta)\left(\frac{1}{\log(2)(\eta-1)}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\leq\zeta(\eta)\leq\zeta_{\mathrm{a}}(\eta)\left(\frac{1}{\log(2)(\eta-1)}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\log(2)}{12}(\eta-1)\right).

(A.1)

Proof.

The identity
\zeta(\eta)=\zeta_{\mathrm{a}}(\eta)(1-2^{1-\eta})^{-1}
is classical. Truncating the Laurent series
(1-2^{1-\eta})^{-1}
near
\eta=1
to the first order we have
\frac{1}{\log(2)(\eta-1)}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\log(2)}{12}(\eta-1)
. This is an upper bound for
(1-2^{1-\eta})^{-1}
if
\eta>1
. In fact, an application of l’Hôpital’s rule gives

\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\to 1}\frac{1}{\log(2)(\eta-1)}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\log(2)}{12}(\eta-1)-(1-2^{1-\eta})^{-1}=0

(A.2)

and we can check that for
\eta>1

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\eta}\left(\frac{1}{\log(2)(\eta-1)}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\log(2)}{12}(\eta-1)-(1-2^{1-\eta})^{-1}\right)>0.

(A.3)

This yields the upper bound in (
A.1
). A similar argument applied to
(1-2^{1-\eta})^{-1}-(\frac{1}{\log(2)(\eta-1)}+\frac{1}{2})
yields the lower bound.
∎

Corollary A.2
.

For every
\eta_{0}>1
there is a constant
c(\eta_{0})>1
such that if
1<\eta\leq\eta_{0}
we have

\frac{1}{\eta-1}<\zeta(\eta)\leq\frac{c(\eta_{0})}{\eta-1}
.

Proof.

Note that functions
\eta\mapsto\zeta_{\mathrm{a}}(\eta)
and
\eta\mapsto\frac{1}{\log(2)}+\frac{1}{2}(\eta-1)+\frac{\log(2)}{12}(\eta-1)^{2}
are both positive and increasing for
\eta>1
. Therefore, for
\eta\in(1,\eta_{0}]
, their product is maximised at
\eta=\eta_{0}
. Similarly, since
\frac{1}{\log(2)}+\frac{1}{2}(\eta-1)
is bounded below by its value at
\eta=1
and
\zeta_{\mathrm{a}}(1)=\log(2)
(this is the alternating harmonic series), we have
\zeta_{\mathrm{a}}(\eta)\left(\frac{1}{\log(2)(\eta-1)}+\frac{1}{2}\right)>1
. The statement then follows from Lemma
A.1
with

\displaystyle c(\eta_{0})=\zeta_{\mathrm{a}}(\eta_{0})\left(\frac{1}{\log(2)}+\frac{1}{2}(\eta_{0}-1)+\frac{\log(2)}{12}(\eta_{0}-1)^{2}\right).

(A.4)

∎

Remark A.3
.

Note that the lower bound in Corollary
A.2
is only meaningful for
1<\eta\leq 2
since for
\eta>2
it is worse than the trivial bound
\zeta(\eta)>1
.

Corollary A.4
.

For
1<\eta\leq\frac{5}{4}
, we have
\frac{1}{\eta-1}<\zeta(\eta)\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\eta-1}
.

Proof.

The statement follows immediately from Corollary
A.2
since, using (
A.4
), it is easy to verify that
1.1487793\approx c(\tfrac{5}{4})<\sqrt{2}
.
∎

Note that Corollary
A.4
can be used in the proofs of Theorems
8.4
and
8.7
since they use the ranges
1<\eta\leq\frac{4+\sqrt{10}}{6}
and
1<\eta\leq\frac{11+\sqrt{73}}{16}
respectively and
\frac{4+\sqrt{10}}{6},\frac{11+\sqrt{73}}{16}\leq\frac{5}{4}
.

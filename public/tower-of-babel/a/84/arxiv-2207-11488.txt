School of Mathematics, Hefei University of Technology, Hefei, Anhui 230009, China.3. School of Mathematical Sciences, University of Science and Technology of China,Hefei, Anhui 230026, China.4. Abstract




\theorembodyfont

Abstract:

The irreducibility is fundamental for the study of ergodicity of stochastic dynamical systems.
The existing methods on the irreducibility of stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) and stochastic differential equations (SDEs) driven by pure jump noise are basically along the same lines as that for the Gaussian case, which are not particularly suitable for jump noise. As a result, restrictive conditions are usually placed on the driving jump noise. Basically the driving noises are additive type and more or less in the class of stable processes.

In this paper, we develop a new and effective method to obtain the irreducibility of SPDEs and SDEs driven by multiplicative pure jump noise.
The conditions placed on the coefficients and the driving noise are very mild, and in some sense they are necessary and sufficient. As an application of our main results,
we remove all the restrictive conditions on the driving noises in the literature, and derive new irreducibility results of a large class of equations driven
by pure jump noise, including SPDEs with locally monotone coefficients, SPDEs/SDEs with singular coefficients, nonlinear
\rm Schr\ddot{o}dinger
equations etc. We emphasize that under our setting the driving noises could be compound Poisson processes, even allowed to be infinite dimensional. As further applications of the main results, we obtain the ergodicity of multi-valued, singular stochastic evolution inclusions such as stochastic
1
-Laplacian
evolution(total variation flow), stochastic sign fast diffusion equation, stochastic minimal surface flow, stochastic curve shortening flow, etc.

Keywords:

Irreducibility; pure jump noise; stochastic partial differential equations; ergodicity; locally monotone coefficients; singular coefficients;

AMS Subject Classification (2020):

60H15; 60G51; 37A25; 60H17.

In this paper, we are concerned with the irreducibility of stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) and stochastic differential equations (SDEs) driven by pure jump noise.

The irreducibility is a fundamental property of stochastic dynamic systems.
The importance of the study of the irreducibility lies in its relevance in the analysis of the ergodicity of Markov processes.

To obtain the irreducibility for stochastic equations driven by Gaussian noise, one usually needs to solve a control problem.
In doing so, three ingredients play very important role:
the (approximate) controllability of the associated PDEs, Girsanov’s transformation of Wiener processes, and the support of Wiener processes/stochastic convolutions on path spaces.

However, things become quite different when the driving noises are pure jump processes.
Compared with the case of the Gaussian driving noise, there are few results on the irreducibility of the case of the pure jump driving noise, because the systems
behave drastically differently due to the appearance of jumps.
The existing methods on the irreducibility of the dynamical system driven by jump noise are basically along the same lines as that of the Gaussian case. They heavily rely on the fact that the driving noises are additive type and more or less in the class of stable processes. The use of such methods to deal with the case of other types of additive pure jump noises appears to be unclear, let alone the case of multiplicative noises. Actually the methods and techniques available for dynamical systems driven by Gaussian noise are not well suited for investigating the irreducibility of systems driven by jump type noise for two main reasons.
One is that there exist very few results on the support of the pure jump Lévy processes/the
stochastic convolutions on path spaces. Due to the discontinuity of trajectories, the characterization of the support of the pure jump processes is not as satisfactory as in the case of Gaussian noise. The other is that Girsanov’s transformation of the pure jump Lévy process is much less effective than that of Gaussian case, because the density of the Girsanov transform of a
Poisson random measure is expressed in terms of nonlinear invertible and predictable transformations, and is to
censore jumps or thin the size of jumps. So far, there is a lack of effective methods to obtain the irreducibility of
stochastic equations driven by pure jump noise. This strongly motivates the current paper.

Our contributions

Our main results are Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
. In a few words, to get the irreducibility, we only need to impose the conditions under which the well posedness can be guaranteed and a nondegenerate condition on the intensity measure of the driving Lévy noise, i.e., Assumptions
2.3
and
2.4
in Section
2
respectively for the case of the multiplicative noise and the case of the additive noise.

(I)
The approach to prove the irreducibility we proposed
is completely different with the existing ones. Our approach gets rid of solving the (approximate) controllability for the associated PDEs, does not need to establish the support of Lévy processes/stochastic convolutions on path spaces, and does not rely on Girsanov’s transformation of Lévy processes. As a result, we removed all the restrictions placed on the driving noises in the literature; see Propositions
4.1
and
4.4
in this paper, and some details will be provided below.

(II)

We established new irreducibility results of a large class of equations driven
by pure jump noise with mild requirements. For instance, Proposition
4.3
below establishes the irreducibility of the nonlinear
\rm Schr\ddot{o}dinger
equations, which covers both focusing and defocusing nonlinearity in the full subcritical
range of exponents. To the best of our knowledge, the corresponding results are not even known in the case of Gaussian driving noise.
The framework of Proposition
4.1
in Section 4
covers SPDEs such as stochastic porous medium equation, stochastic
p
-Laplace equation, stochastic fast diffusion equation, stochastic 2D Navier-Stokes equation, stochastic equations of non-Newtonian fluids, and many other stochastic Hydrodynamical systems, most of which can not be covered by the existing results.

(III)

As a further application of the main result of our paper, combining with the
e
-property, we can obtain the uniqueness of invariant measures of the linear
\rm Schr\ddot{o}dinger
equations and a class of multi-valued, singular stochastic evolution inclusions (see Proposition
4.3
and Theorem
5.1
). Examples include stochastic
1
-Laplacian
evolution(total variation flow), stochastic sign fast diffusion equation, stochastic minimal surface flow, stochastic curve shortening flow, etc. It seems quite difficult to get these results with other means due to the lack of strong dissipativity of the equations.

The second one is a nondegenerate condition on the intensity measure of the driving Lévy noise, which basically says that for any

Let

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \cap\{X^{x}(\sigma_{1})\in B(q_{1},\frac{\rho_{1}}{2})\}\cap\{X^{x}(t)\in B(q_{1},\rho_{1}),\forall t\in(\sigma_{1},T_{1}]\}\Big)>0,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\{X^{x}(T_{0})\in B(\zeta,\rho_{0})\}\cap\{X^{x}(T_{1})\in B(q_{1},\rho_{1})\}\Big)>0.

To get (
1
), the following claims will be used:

(C1) Assumption

An important novelty of this article is that, we find a nondegenerate condition on the intensity measure of the driving Lévy noises to prove the irreducibility.
A further novelty is that the main assumptions, Assumptions

Therefore, the approach we are proposing here is quite robust, and covers SPDEs/SDEs with quite singular coefficients.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(1_{B(y,\kappa)}(X^{\hbar}(T-T_{n^{\zeta}}))\Big)=\mathbb{P}\Big(X^{\hbar}(T-T_{n^{\zeta}})\in B(y,\kappa)\Big)>0.

Applications I: irreducibility

In this section, we provide applications of our main results to various SDEs and SPDEs. Since Assumptions

4.1
Sufficient conditions and examples for Assumption
2.4

For any measure
\rho
on
H

S_{\rho}=S(\rho)
is defined to be the set of

such that
\rho(G)>0
for any open set

Set

It is not difficult to see that Assumption
2.4
holds if and only if

is dense in

.

Assumption
2.4
actually places very mild conditions on the intensity measures

of the Lévy processes.
The examples we can include are considerably more general than that considered in the literature where the irreducibility of SPDEs/SDEs driven by pure jump noise were studied. In this subsection, we give several explanatory examples that satisfy Assumption

Example 4.1

Let

.
The intensity measure

4.3
Locally monotone SPDEs

Under the general framework as in
[
43
]
, we will obtain the irreducibility for coercive and local monotone SPDEs driven by pure jump noise.

The assumptions (H1)-(H4) are very mild so that our result in this subsection ( Proposition 4.1 below) is applicable to SPDEs such as stochastic reaction-diffusion equations, stochastic semilinear evolution equation, stochastic porous medium equation, stochastic
p
-Laplace equation, stochastic Burgers
type equations, stochastic 2D Navier-Stokes equation, stochastic magneto-hydrodynamic
equations, stochastic Boussinesq model for the Bénard convection, stochastic 2D magnetic Bénard problem, stochastic 3D Leray-
\alpha

model, stochastic equations of non-Newtonian fluids, several stochastic Shell Models of
turbulence, and many other stochastic 2D Hydrodynamical systems.

Recall that we consider the following SPDEs.

\displaystyle dX(t)={\mathcal{A}}(X(t))dt+\int_{Z_{1}^{c}}\!\!\!\!{\sigma}(X(t-),z)\tilde{N}(dz,dt)+\int_{Z_{1}}\!\!\!\!{\sigma}(X(t-),z)N(dz,dt),

(4.5)

\displaystyle X(0)=x.

Let us formulate the assumptions on the coefficients
\mathcal{A}
and
\sigma
. Suppose that there exist constants
{\alpha}>1,\ {\beta}\geq 0,\ {\theta}>0,\ C>0
,
F>0
and a measurable (bounded on balls) function
\rho:V\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
such that the following conditions hold for all

(H1) (Hemicontinuity) The map
s\mapsto_{V^{*}}\langle\mathcal{A}(v_{1}+sv_{2}),v\rangle_{V}
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}

(H2) (Local monotonicity)

\displaystyle 2_{V^{*}}\langle\mathcal{A}(v_{1})-\mathcal{A}(v_{2}),v_{1}-v_{2}\rangle_{V}+\int_{Z_{1}^{c}}\|\sigma(v_{1},z)-\sigma(v_{2},z)\|_{H}^{2}\nu(dz)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(C+\rho(v_{2}))\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{H}^{2},

(H3) (Coercivity)

2_{V^{*}}\langle\mathcal{A}(v),v\rangle_{V}+{\theta}\|v\|_{V}^{{\alpha}}\leq F+C\|v\|_{H}^{2},

(H4) (Growth)

\|\mathcal{A}(v)\|_{V^{*}}^{\frac{{\alpha}}{{\alpha}-1}}\leq(F+C\|v\|_{V}^{{\alpha}})\big(1+\|v\|_{H}^{{\beta}}\big).

The following well-posedness was proved in
[
43

Lemma 4.1

Suppose that conditions (H1)-(H4) hold, and there exists a constant
\gamma<\frac{\theta}{2\beta}

such that for all

\displaystyle\int_{Z_{1}^{c}}\|\sigma(v,z)\|_{H}^{2}\nu(dz)\leq F+C\|v\|_{H}^{2}+\gamma\|v\|_{V}^{\alpha};

\displaystyle\int_{Z_{1}^{c}}\|\sigma(v,z)\|_{H}^{\beta+2}\nu(dz)\leq F^{\frac{\beta+2}{2}}+C\|v\|_{H}^{\beta+2};

\displaystyle\rho(v)\leq C(1+\|v\|_{V}^{\alpha})(1+\|v\|_{H}^{\beta}).

Then for any
x\in H

4.5

Now, we state the main result in this subsection.

Proposition 4.1

Under the same assumptions of Lemma

4.4
Nonlinear Schrödinger equations

The nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) is a fundamental model describing wave propagation that appears in various fields such as nonlinear optics, nonlinear water propagation, quantum physics, Bose-Einstein condensate, plasma physics and molecular biology.

In this subsection, we study the irreducibility of stochastic NLS driven by additive Lévy noise. Without further notice, all the
L^{p}
spaces in this subsection are referred as spaces of complex-values functions.

Consider (
2.12
) with
H=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
{\mathcal{A}}(u)=i[\Delta u-\lambda|u|^{{\alpha}-1}u]
, where
\lambda\in\{-1,{\color[rgb]{0,0.07,1}0,}1\}
, and
1<{\alpha}<1+\frac{4}{d}
. Now consider NLS with additive noise, that is,

\displaystyle dX(t)={\mathcal{A}}(X(t))dt+\int_{0<\|z\|_{H}\leq 1}\!\!\!\!z\tilde{N}(dz,dt)+\int_{\|z\|_{H}>1}\!\!\!\!zN(dz,dt),

(4.15)

\displaystyle X(0)=x.

We say a pair
(p,r)
is admissible if
p,r\in[2,\infty]
and
(p,r,d)\neq(2,\infty,2)
satisfying
\frac{2}{p}+\frac{d}{r}=\frac{d}{2}
.
The following result provides the existence and uniqueness of the solution of the stochastic NLS (
4.15
) whose proof was given in
[
38
]
.

Proposition 4.2

Let
p\geq 2
,
1<{\alpha}<1+\frac{4}{d}
,
r={\alpha}+1
such that
(p,r)
is an admissible pair. For any
\hbar\in H
,
there exists a unique global mild solution
X^{\hbar}=(X^{\hbar}(t),t\geq 0)
of (
4.15
) satisfying

X^{\hbar}\in D([0,\infty);H)\cap L^{p}_{loc}(0,\infty;L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),\ \mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

Here is the result for the irreducibility of the solution.

Proposition 4.3

If the driving noise satisfies Assumption
2.4
, then the solution
\{X^{x},x\in H\}
of (
4.15
) is irreducible in
H
.
Moreover, for the linear Schrödinger equation case, i.e.,
\lambda=0
, there exists at most one invariant measure.

Proof

For the case of
\lambda=0
, we have

\|X^{\hbar_{1}}(t)-X^{\hbar_{2}}(t)\|_{H}\leq\|\hbar_{1}-\hbar_{2}\|_{H},\ \forall t\geq 0,\ \hbar_{1},\hbar_{2}\in H.

This together with the irreducibility implies the uniqueness of the invariant measure (if it exists); see Theorem 2 in
[
25
]
.
In the following, we will prove irreducibility.

It is classical that the solution
\{X^{\hbar},\hbar\in H\}
of (
4.15
) forms a strong Markov process on
H
.
Therefore, Assumptions
2.1
holds.
To apply Theorem
2.2
to conclude the proof, it only remains to show that Assumption
2.2
holds.
From the proof of Proposition
4.1
we see that Assumption
2.2
is implied by the following results.

For any
h\in H
and
{\eta}>0
, there exist
T_{0}=T_{0}(h,\eta)>0
and
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(h,\eta)>0
small enough such that

\sup_{\tilde{h}\in B(h,{\epsilon}_{0})}\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}\|X_{1}^{h}(t)-X_{1}^{\tilde{h}}(t)\|_{H}>\frac{{\eta}}{2}\Big)<\frac{1}{2},

(4.16)

where
X^{\hbar}_{1}
is the mild solution of the following equation:

\displaystyle dX^{\hbar}_{1}(t)={\mathcal{A}}(X^{\hbar}_{1}(t))dt+\int_{0<\|z\|_{H}\leq 1}\!\!\!\!z\tilde{N}(dz,dt),

(4.17)

\displaystyle X^{\hbar}_{1}(0)=\hbar.

We now prove (
4.16
). For
t>0
, set

\displaystyle Y_{t}:=L^{\infty}(0,t;H)\cap L^{p}(0,t;L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),

(4.18)

and for
u\in Y_{t}
,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{Y_{t}}:=\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\|u(s)\|_{H}+\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(4.19)

Let
\theta:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow[0,1]
be a non-increasing
C_{0}^{\infty}
function such that

1_{[0,1]}\leq\theta\leq 1_{[0,2]}
and
\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\theta^{\prime}(x)\geq-2
. For the fixed
h\in H
, set
R=\|h\|_{H}+2
and
\theta_{R}(\cdot)=\theta(\frac{\cdot}{R})
.

For any
\hbar\in H
, let
Z^{\hbar}_{R}
be the solution of the trancated stochastic Schrödinger equation:

\displaystyle dZ^{\hbar}_{R}(t)=i[\Delta Z^{\hbar}_{R}(t)-\lambda\theta_{R}(\|Z^{\hbar}_{R}\|_{Y_{t}})|Z^{\hbar}_{R}(t)|^{{\alpha}-1}Z^{\hbar}_{R}(t)]dt+\int_{0<\|z\|_{H}\leq 1}\!\!\!\!z\tilde{N}(dz,dt),

(4.20)

\displaystyle Z^{\hbar}_{R}(0)=\hbar.

By
[
3
, Propositions 2.2 and 3.1]
, for any
T>0
, we have

\displaystyle\|Z_{R}^{h}-Z_{R}^{\tilde{h}}\|_{Y_{T}}\leq C\|h-\tilde{h}\|_{H}+CT^{1-\frac{({\alpha}-1)d}{4}}R^{{\alpha}-1}\|Z_{R}^{h}-Z_{R}^{\tilde{h}}\|_{Y_{T}}.

(4.21)

For any
\eta>0
, we can choose
\widetilde{\epsilon}\in(0,\frac{\eta}{4}\wedge 1]
and
\widetilde{T}>0
small enough such that

2C\widetilde{\epsilon}\leq\frac{1}{2}\wedge\frac{\eta}{4}\text{ and }\quad C\widetilde{T}^{1-\frac{({\alpha}-1)d}{4}}R^{{\alpha}-1}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Then, for any
\tilde{h}\in B(h,\widetilde{\epsilon})
,

\|Z_{R}^{h}-Z_{R}^{\tilde{h}}\|_{Y_{\widetilde{T}}}\leq 2C\|h-\tilde{h}\|_{H}\leq\frac{1}{2}\wedge\frac{\eta}{4},

(4.22)

here, for any
\epsilon>0
,
B(h,\epsilon)=\{\hbar\in H:\|\hbar-h\|_{H}<\epsilon\}
.

Define
\tau=\inf\{s\geq 0:\|Z^{h}_{R}\|_{{}_{Y_{s}}}>\|h\|_{H}+1/2\}
. Then
\mathbb{P}(\tau>0)=1
, which implies that
there exists
T_{0}\in(0,\widetilde{T}]
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\tau>T_{0})\geq 11/12.

(4.23)

Combining this with (
4.22
), for any
\tilde{h}\in B(h,\widetilde{\epsilon})
,

\displaystyle\|Z_{R}^{\tilde{h}}\|_{Y_{T_{0}}}\leq\|h\|_{H}+1<R\text{ on }\ \{\tau>T_{0}\}\ \mathbb{P}\text{-a.s..}

(4.24)

Let us define
\tau^{\hbar}_{R}=\inf\{s\geq 0:\|Z^{\hbar}_{R}\|_{{}_{Y_{s}}}>R\}
. Then, for any
\tilde{h}\in B(h,\widetilde{\epsilon})
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\tau^{\tilde{h}}_{R}>0)=1\text{ and }Z^{\tilde{h}}_{R}(t)=X^{\tilde{h}}_{1}(t)\text{ on }t\in[0,\tau^{\tilde{h}}_{R})\ \mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}.

(4.25)

Note that (
4.24
) implies that, for any
\tilde{h}\in B(h,\widetilde{\epsilon})
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\tau\wedge\tau^{\tilde{h}}_{R}>T_{0})=\mathbb{P}(\tau>T_{0}).

(4.26)

Combining (
4.22
)–(
4.26
) together, we deduce that, for any
\tilde{h}\in B(h,\widetilde{\epsilon})
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}\|X_{1}^{h}(t)-X_{1}^{\tilde{h}}(t)\|_{H}>\frac{{\eta}}{2}\Big)

(4.27)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}\|Z_{R}^{h}(t)-Z_{R}^{\tilde{h}}(t)\|_{H}>\frac{{\eta}}{2},\tau\wedge\tau^{\tilde{h}}_{R}>T_{0}\Big)+\mathbb{P}(\tau\wedge\tau^{\tilde{h}}_{R}\leq T_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\tau\leq T_{0})\leq\frac{1}{12}.

This completes the proof.

4.5
Singular SDEs

Let
L=(L_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a Lévy process on
\mathbb{R}^{d},d\in\mathbb{N}
, and denote its intensity measure by
\nu
.
To state the condition on
\nu
, for
\alpha\in(0,2)
, denote by
\mathbb{L}_{non}^{\alpha}
the space of all non-degenerate
\alpha
-stable Lévy measure
\nu^{(\alpha)}
; that is,

\nu^{(\alpha)}(A)=\int_{0}^{\infty}(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\frac{1_{A}(r\theta)\vartheta(d\theta)}{r^{1+\alpha}})dr,\ \ A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
\vartheta
is a finite measure over the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with

\displaystyle\inf_{\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\theta_{0}\cdot\theta|\vartheta(d\theta)>0.

(4.28)

For
R>0
, denote by
B_{R}
the closed ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
centered at the origin with radius

R
. We assume that there are
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in\mathbb{L}_{non}^{\alpha}
, so that

\displaystyle\nu_{1}(A)\leq\nu(A)\leq\nu_{2}(A)\ \text{for}\ A\in\mathcal{B}(B_{1}).

(4.29)

In
[
6
]
, the authors call Lévy processes with intensity measure satisfying (
4.29
)
non-degenerate
{\alpha}
-stable-like Lévy process. The Lévy measure
\nu
could be singular with respect to
the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and its support could be a proper subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

For a Borel measurable drift
b(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and diffusion matrix
\sigma(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{d}
, consider the following SDE

\displaystyle dX_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b(X_{t})dt+\sigma(X_{t-})dL_{t}

(4.30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b(X_{t})dt+\int_{0<|z|\leq 1}{\sigma}(X_{t-})z\widetilde{N}(dz,dt)+\int_{|z|>1}{\sigma}(X_{t-})zN(dz,dt).

Here
N
and
\widetilde{N}
are the Poisson random measure and compensated Poisson random measure associated with
L
, respectively.
In a recent paper
[
6
]
, the authors established the following well posedness of the SDE (
4.30
).

Lemma 4.2

Assume that
\nu
satisfies (
4.29
) with
\alpha\in(0,2)
. Assume that there
are constants
\beta\in(1-\alpha/2,1]
and
\Lambda>0
so that for all
x,y,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle|b(x)|\leq\Lambda\text{ and }|b(x)-b(y)|\leq\Lambda|x-y|^{\beta},

(4.31)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{-1}|\xi|\leq|\sigma(x)\xi|\leq\Lambda|\xi|\text{ and }\|\sigma(x)-\sigma(y)\|\leq\Lambda|x-y|.

(4.32)

Then, there is a unique strong solution
X^{x}=(X^{x}(t),t\geq 0)
to (
4.30
) for any initial data
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

We are concerned with the irreducibility of the solutions
\{X^{x},x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
To obtain the irreducibility, we introduce the following conditions. Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1,2,...,d}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(I)

There exist
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

f_{1},f_{2},...,f_{n}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, and
\kappa\in(0,1]
, such that

\{f_{1},f_{2},...,f_{n}\}\subset S_{\vartheta}
, and
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\inf_{y\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\sup_{i=1,2,...,n}\frac{\langle\sigma(x)f_{i},y\rangle}{|\sigma(x)f_{i}|}\geq\kappa
.

Proposition 4.4

Under the same assumptions of Lemma
4.2
, and assume that (I) holds, the solutions to (
4.30
) is irreducible in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof

We will apply Theorem 2.1 to get the irreducibility. First we verify Assumption
2.2
.

Removing the big jumps in (
4.30
), consider the following SDE:

dX_{1}(t)=b(X_{1}(t))dt+\int_{0<|z|\leq 1}{\sigma}(X_{1}(t-))z\widetilde{N}(dz,dt),\qquad X_{1}(0)=x.

(4.33)

We will prove that for any
h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
{\eta}>0
, there exist
T_{0}=T_{0}(h,\eta)>0
and
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(h,\eta)>0
small enough such that

\sup_{\tilde{h}\in B(h,{\epsilon}_{0})}\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}|X_{1}^{h}(t)-X_{1}^{\tilde{h}}(t)|>\frac{{\eta}}{2}\Big)<\frac{1}{2}.

(4.34)

We now fix
h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and set
R=|h|+2
. Let
\theta:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow[0,1]
be a non-increasing
C_{0}^{\infty}
function such that

1_{[0,R]}\leq\theta\leq 1_{[0,R+2]}
. Let
b_{\theta}(x)=b(x)\theta(x)
. For any
\zeta\in(0,1]
, define

\displaystyle\sigma_{\zeta}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\!\!\!\sigma(x+h),&\mbox{$|x|\leq\zeta/2$,}\\
\!\!\!\frac{2(\zeta-|x|)}{\zeta}\sigma(\frac{\zeta x}{2|x|}+h)+\frac{2(|x|-\zeta/2)}{\zeta}\sigma(h),&\mbox{$\zeta/2<|x|\leq\zeta$,}\\
\!\!\!\sigma(h),&\mbox{$|x|>\zeta$.}\end{array}\right.

For
\zeta_{0}\in(0,1]
small enough, by the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
and
applying
[
6
, Theorem 4.1]
, for any
\hbar\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the following SDE admits a unique strong solution

\displaystyle Y_{t}^{\hbar}=\hbar+\int_{0}^{t}b_{\theta}(Y_{s}^{\hbar}+h)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0<|z|\leq 1}{\sigma}_{\zeta_{0}}(Y_{s-}^{\hbar})z\widetilde{N}(dz,dt),\ t\geq 0.

Moreover, from the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 4.1]
we see that for any
T>0
there exists a constant
C_{T}>0
such that for any
\hbar_{1},\hbar_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|Y_{t}^{\hbar_{1}}-Y_{t}^{\hbar_{2}}|^{2})\leq C_{T}|\hbar_{1}-\hbar_{2}|^{2}.

(4.36)

Define

\tau^{\hbar}=\inf\{t>0:|Y_{t}^{\hbar}|\geq{\zeta_{0}}/2\}.

Then for any
\tilde{h}\in B(h,{\zeta_{0}}/8)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\tau^{\tilde{h}-h}>0)=1,

(4.37)

\displaystyle X_{1}^{\tilde{h}}(t)=h+Y_{t}^{\tilde{h}-h}\text{ on }t\in[0,\tau^{\tilde{h}-h})\ \mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.},

(4.38)

and

\displaystyle X_{1}^{{h}}(t)-X_{1}^{\tilde{h}}(t)=Y_{t}^{0}-Y_{t}^{\tilde{h}-h}\text{ on }t\in[0,\tau^{0}\wedge\tau^{\tilde{h}-h})\ \mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}.

(4.39)

Define
\tilde{\tau}^{0}=\inf\{t>0:|Y_{t}^{0}|\geq{\zeta_{0}}/16\}
. Then
\mathbb{P}(\tilde{\tau}^{0}>0)=1
, which implies that there
exists
T_{0}\in(0,1]
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\tilde{\tau}^{0}>T_{0})\geq 11/12.

(4.40)

Combining the inequality above with (
4.36
) and the Chebyshev inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\sup_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}|Y_{t}^{\tilde{h}-h}|\leq\zeta_{0}/8)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\{\sup_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}|Y_{t}^{\tilde{h}-h}-Y_{t}^{0}|\leq\zeta_{0}/16\}\cap\{\sup_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}|Y_{t}^{0}|\leq\zeta_{0}/16\})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1-\mathbb{P}(\sup_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}|Y_{t}^{\tilde{h}-h}-Y_{t}^{0}|>\zeta_{0}/16)-\mathbb{P}(\sup_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}|Y_{t}^{0}|>\zeta_{0}/16)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1-\frac{16^{2}}{\zeta_{0}^{2}}C_{T_{0}}|\tilde{h}-h|^{2}-\mathbb{P}(\tilde{\tau}^{0}\leq T_{0})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 11/12-\frac{16^{2}}{\zeta_{0}^{2}}C_{T_{0}}|\tilde{h}-h|^{2}.

Hence, there exists
\epsilon_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\inf_{\tilde{h}\in B(h,\epsilon_{1})}\mathbb{P}(\sup_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}|Y_{t}^{\tilde{h}-h}|\leq\zeta_{0}/8)\geq 10/12,

which implies that

\displaystyle\inf_{\tilde{h}\in B(h,\epsilon_{1})}\mathbb{P}(\tau^{\tilde{h}-h}>T_{0})\geq 10/12.

Combining this inequality with (
4.40
), we further have that for any
\tilde{h}\in B(h,\epsilon_{1})

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\tau^{0}\wedge\tau^{\tilde{h}-h}>T_{0})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1-\mathbb{P}(\tau^{0}\leq T_{0})-\mathbb{P}(\tau^{\tilde{h}-h}\leq T_{0})

(4.41)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\tau^{\tilde{h}-h}>T_{0})-\mathbb{P}(\tilde{\tau}^{0}\leq T_{0})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}.

For the second inequality, we have used
\{\tau^{0}\leq T_{0}\}\subseteq\{\tilde{\tau}^{0}\leq T_{0}\}
.

For any
\eta>0
, let
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{1}\wedge\sqrt{\frac{\eta^{2}}{32C_{T_{0}}}}\wedge\frac{\eta}{4}
. Then,
by (
4.36
), (
4.39
), the Chebyshev inequality, and (
4.41
), for any
\tilde{h}\in B(h,\epsilon_{0})
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}|X_{1}^{h}(t)-X_{1}^{\tilde{h}}(t)|>\frac{{\eta}}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}|X_{1}^{h}(t)-X_{1}^{\tilde{h}}(t)|>\frac{{\eta}}{2}\}\cap\{\tau^{0}\wedge\tau^{\tilde{h}-h}>T_{0}\}\Big)+\mathbb{P}(\tau^{0}\wedge\tau^{\tilde{h}-h}\leq T_{0})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{0}}|Y_{t}^{0}-Y_{t}^{\tilde{h}-h}|>\frac{{\eta}}{2}\Big)+\frac{1}{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\eta^{2}}C_{T_{0}}|\tilde{h}-h|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 3/8.

The proof of (
4.34
) is complete. Now following the similar arguments as that in the proof of Proposition
4.1
, we see that Assumption
2.2
holds.

Assumption
2.1
follows from Lemma
4.2
. We now verify Assumption
2.3
.

Note that
\{lf_{i},l\in(0,1],i=1,2,...,n\}\subseteq S_{\nu}
.

For any
\hbar\neq y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\eta>0
, set
q_{0}=\hbar
. Choose
i_{0}\in\{1,2,...,n\}
such that

\varpi_{0}:=\frac{\langle\sigma(q_{0})f_{i_{0}},y-q_{0}\rangle}{|\sigma(q_{0})f_{i_{0}}||y-q_{0}|}=\sup_{i=1,2,...,n}\frac{\langle\sigma(q_{0})f_{i},y-q_{0}\rangle}{|\sigma(q_{0})f_{i}||y-q_{0}|}.

Then
\varpi_{0}\in[\kappa,1]
. Let
\varrho=|y-q_{0}|
and
\theta
be such that
\cos\theta=\varpi_{0}
. Define

g(r)=(\varrho-r\cos\theta)^{2}+(r\sin\theta)^{2}=\varrho^{2}-2r\varrho\varpi_{0}+r^{2}\leq\varrho^{2}-2r\varrho\kappa+r^{2},\ \ r\geq 0.

Take
r_{0}\in(0,|\sigma(q_{0})f_{i_{0}}|]\supset(0,\Lambda^{-1}]
such that
g(r_{0})=\inf_{r\in(0,|\sigma(q_{0})f_{i_{0}}|]}g(r)\leq\inf_{r\in(0,\Lambda^{-1}]}g(r)
. Since
\varpi_{0}\in[\kappa,1]
,
if
\varrho\kappa>\Lambda^{-1}
,
g(r_{0})\leq g(\Lambda^{-1})\leq\varrho^{2}-\Lambda^{-2}
; if
\varrho\kappa\in(0,\Lambda^{-1}]
,
g(r_{0})=g(\varrho\kappa)=\varrho^{2}(1-\kappa^{2})
.

Now let
q_{1}=q_{0}+\frac{\sigma(q_{0})f_{i_{0}}}{|\sigma(q_{0})f_{i_{0}}|}r_{0}
and
l_{1}=\frac{f_{i_{0}}}{|\sigma(q_{0})f_{i_{0}}|}r_{0}
. Then
|q_{1}-y|^{2}=g(r_{0})
. Recursively, we can construct
q_{m},l_{m},m\geq 2
until that
|q_{m}-y|\leq\frac{\eta}{8}
.
Since
\sigma
is continuous and
\{lf_{i},l\in(0,1],i=1,2,...,n\}\subseteq S_{\nu}
, then Assumption
2.3
holds.

The proof of the proposition is complete.

5
Applications II: ergodicity

In Proposition
4.3
, we obtained the uniqueness of invariant measure for the linear Schrödinger equation. In this section, we will see many more interesting examples for which we obtain the ergodicity.
We consider the following stochastic evolution inclusion in a separable Hilbert space
H
driven by pure jump noise:

\displaystyle dX(t)-{\mathcal{A}}(X(t))dt\ni dL(t),\ t>0,

(5.1)

\displaystyle X(0)=X_{0}.

Let
H
be a separable
Hilbert space with dual
H^{*}
. Suppose that there is another Hilbert space
S
embedded densely and compactly into
H
.
We thus have a Gelfand triple

S\subset H\cong H^{*}\subset S^{*}

and it holds that

{}_{S^{*}}\langle v,u\rangle_{S}=\langle v,u\rangle_{H},\text{ whenever }u\in S,v\in H.

Let
i_{S}:S\rightarrow S^{*}
denote the Riesz map of
S
. We note that the scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{S}
defines a bilinear,
S
-bounded,

S
-coercive form on
H
. By the Lax-Milgram Theorem there is a linear, positive definite, self-adjoint operator

T:D(T)\subset H\rightarrow H
with
D(T^{1/2})=S
and
\langle T^{1/2}u,T^{1/2}v\rangle_{H}=\langle u,v\rangle_{S}
.
We define
J_{n}=(1+\frac{T}{n})^{-1},n\in\mathbb{N}
, to be
the resolvent associated to
T
and
T_{n}=TJ_{n}=n(1-J_{n})
to be the Yosida approximation of
T
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{A}:S\rightarrow 2^{S^{*}}
satisfies the following conditions: There are constants
C,f,\gamma>0
such
that

(A1)

Maximal monotonicity: The map
x\rightarrow\mathcal{A}(x)
is maximal monotone with non-empty values.

(A2)

Linear growth: for all
x\in S
and
y\in\mathcal{A}(x)
,

\|y\|_{S^{*}}\leq f+C\|x\|_{S},

(A3)

Weak coercivity in
S
: for all
x\in S
,
y\in\mathcal{A}(x)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

2_{S}^{*}\langle y,T_{n}(x)\rangle_{S}\geq-\gamma-C\|x\|_{S}^{2}.

Suppose that the driving noise
L(t)
,
t\geq 0
satisfies

(A4)

L
is a pure jump Lévy process taking values in
D(T^{3/2})
, that is, the intensity measure
\nu
of
L
satisfies

\int_{D(T^{3/2})}\|z\|^{2}_{D(T^{3/2})}\wedge 1\nu(dz)<\infty
.

(A5)

H_{0}
is dense in
H
, here
S_{\nu}
denotes the support of
\nu
and

H_{0}:=\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}a_{i},\ n,m_{1},...,m_{n}\in\mathbb{N},\ a_{i}\in S_{\nu}\Big\}.

Consider the following deterministic evolution inclusion in
H
:

\displaystyle dX(t)-{\mathcal{A}}(X(t))dt\ni g(t),\ t>0,

(5.2)

\displaystyle X(0)=x.

Here
g
is a
\rm c\grave{a}dl\grave{a}g
path in
H
.

Definition 5.1

A solution of (
5.2
) is a
\rm c\grave{a}dl\grave{a}g
function

\displaystyle X(t)=x+\int_{0}^{t}\eta(s)ds+g(t),

for all

)

Now we state our main results in this subsection.

Theorem 5.1

Assume that Assumptions (A1)-(A5) hold. Then there exists at most one invariant measure to the limit solution of the equation
(
5.1
).

Proof

The inequality (
5.3
)
implies that the limit solutions
\{X^{x},x\in H\}
satisfy the so-called
e
-property; see
[
25
,
26
]
. This together with the irreducibility implies the uniqueness of the invariant measure (if it exists); see Theorem 2 in
[
25
]
. In the rest of the proof, we will show that
\{X^{x},x\in H\}
is irreducible in
H
.

Since, in general, one can not prove that the limit solutions
\{X^{x},x\in H\}
satisfy some specific stochastic equations, which plays an important role in the proofs of Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
. Hence, we can not apply Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
directly to this case . In the sequel, with slight modifications to the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we will show the irreducibility of
\{X^{x},x\in H\}
.

By the Yamada-Watanabe Theorem and Proposition
5.1
, there exists a measurable map
K:H\times D([0,\infty);H)\rightarrow D([0,\infty);H)
satisfying

(B1)

For any
X_{0}\in H
and
X_{0}\in\mathcal{F}_{0}
,
K(X_{0},L)=X^{X_{0}}
. Here
X^{X_{0}}
is the limit solution of the equation
(
5.1
) with initial data
X_{0}
. And for any stopping time
\tau
and
t\geq 0
,
K(K(X_{0},L)(\tau),L(\tau+\cdot))(t)=K(X_{0},L)(\tau+t)
.

(B2)

For any
x,y\in H
and
t\geq 0
,
\|K(x,L)(t)-K(y,L)(t)\|_{H}\leq\|x-y\|_{H}
,

(B3)

Set
G(x,L)(t)=K(x,L)(t)-L(t)-x,t\geq 0
, then for any
\omega\in\Omega
,
G(x,L(\omega))\in C([0,\infty);H)
. Hence for any
t\geq 0
,
K(x,L)(t)=x+G(x,L)(t)+L(t)
, and
K(x,L)(t)-K(x,L)(t-)=L(t)-L(t-)
.

By (B1) and (B2), it is classical that
\{K(x,L),x\in H\}
forms a strong Markov process on
H
. Applying (B2) again, using arguments similar to, but much easier than, that in the proof of Proposition
4.1
, we can see that
\{K(x,L),x\in H\}
satisfies Assumption
2.2
.
Combining (B3) and Assumption (A5), with only slight modifications to the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we obtain the irreducibility of
\{K(x,L),x\in H\}
, that is, the irreducibility of
\{X^{x},x\in H\}
.

The proof of Theorem
5.1
is complete.

As the application of Theorem
5.1
, we can obtain the uniqueness of invariant measures of many multi-valued, singular stochastic evolution inclusions. It seems quite difficult to
get these results with other means due to the multi-valued or/and lack of strong dissipativity of the equations.

Here are the examples.

Example 5.1

(Stochastic singular
\Phi
-Laplace equation in all space dimensions)

•

Dirichlet boundary conditions in a bounded domain

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}

and

respectively. Let

where we endow
S
with the equivalent norm
\|u\|_{S}:=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Lambda)}
.
Let
\Psi(x)=\widetilde{\Psi}(|x|):\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a radially symmetric function with
\widetilde{\Psi}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
being even, convex, continuous, non-decreasing and satisfying
\widetilde{\Psi}(0)=0
. Further assume

\Psi(x)\leq C(|x|^{2}+1),\ \ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

for some constant
C>0
. Let
\Phi:=\partial\Psi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow 2^{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
.

•

Neumann boundary conditions in a bounded domain

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded, open domain with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Lambda
. We either assume that
\Lambda
is convex or
\partial\Lambda

is
C^{2}
and convex. Let

S:=H^{1}(\Lambda)\subseteq H:=L^{2}(\Lambda)\subseteq S^{*},

where
S
is normed by
\|u\|^{2}_{S}:=\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Lambda)}+\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Lambda)}
. Let
\widetilde{\Psi}(x):\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be an Orlicz function, i.e.,
\widetilde{\Psi}
is even, convex, continuous, non-decreasing and satisfying

Let

We consider the following stochastic singular

-Laplace equation

5.1

,

An explicit example of
\Psi

\Psi_{p}(x):=\frac{1}{p}|x|^{p},\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \ p\in[1,2].

Some examples of

1.

Singular
p
-Laplacian:
\Phi_{p}(x):=\partial\left(\frac{1}{p}|\cdot|^{p}\right)(x)=|x|^{p-1}{\rm Sgn}(x),\ p\in[1,2],

&B(0,1),&&\;\;\text{if}\;\;x=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Curve shortening flow:

\Phi_{\arctan}(r):=\partial\left(s\mapsto s\arctan(s)-\frac{1}{2}\log(s^{2}+1)\right)(r)=\arctan(r),\quad r\in\mathbb{R}.

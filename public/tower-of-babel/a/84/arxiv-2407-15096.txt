Difference of weighted composition operators on weighted Bergman spaces over the unit Ball Thanks: ∗ \ast Corresponding author.
Lian Hu, Songxiao Li ∗ and Yecheng Shi

Abstract
. In this paper, we characterize the boundedness and compactness of differences of weighted composition operators from weighted Bergman spaces A ω p A^{p}_{\omega} induced by a doubling weight ω \omega to Lebesgue spaces L μ q L^{q}_{\mu} on the unit ball for full 0 < p , q < ∞ 0<p,q<\infty , which extend many results on the unit disk. As a byproduct, a new characterization of q q -Carleson the measure for A ω p A^{p}_{\omega} in terms of the Bergman metric ball is also presented. Keywords :Difference, Weighted Bergman space, weighted composition operator, doubling weight, Carleson measure.

1.
Introduction

Denote by
H(\mathbb{B}_{n})
and
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
the space of analytic functions and holomorphic self-maps on the unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{n}
of the complex
n
-space
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, respectively.
Given
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
and a Borel function
u
on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
, the weighted composition operator
uC_{\varphi}
with symbol
\varphi
and weight
u

is defined by

uC_{\varphi}f=f\circ\varphi

for
f\in H(\mathbb{B}_{n})
.
When
u\equiv 1
, the operator
uC_{\varphi}
becomes the classical composition operator
C_{\varphi}
.
The theory of (weighted) composition operators on various settings has been extensively studied in recent decades
and we refer to the books
[
5
]
and
[
14
]
. The difference of composition operators has become an attractive topic in various areas of function theory and operator theory.
Recently,
Choe et al. [
[
3
,
4
]
] completely characterized the bounded and compact differences of weighted composition operators from standard weighted Bergman spaces to Lebesgue spaces on the unit disk.
Subsequently, Choe et al.
[
2
]
extend their results to the unit ball.
Chen
[
1
]
generalized their results to the doubling weight setting and characterized the difference of composition operators from weighted Bergman spaces
A^{p}_{\omega}
to Lebesgue spaces
L^{q}_{\mu}
for full
0<p,q<\infty
.
The main purpose of the current paper is to generalize the main results in
[
1
]
to the unit ball setting.

To begin with, we recall some definitions. A positive, measurable and integrable function
\omega
, satisfying
\omega(z)=\omega(|z|)
for all
z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
, is called a radial weight on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
. Put
\hat{\omega}(r)=\int_{r}^{1}\omega(s)ds
for
r\in[0,1)
.
A radial weight
\omega
is called a doubling weight, denoted by
\omega\in\hat{\mathcal{D}}
, if there is a constant
C=C(\omega)\geq 1
such that

\hat{\omega}(r)<C\hat{\omega}\left({r+1\over 2}\right){\rm~for~}r\in[0,1).

A radial weight
\omega
is called a reverse doubling weight, denoted by
\omega\in\breve{\mathcal{D}}
, if there exists constants
C=C(\omega),K=K(\omega)>1
such that

\hat{\omega}(r)\geq C\hat{\omega}\left(1-{1-r\over K}\right){\rm~for~}r\in[0,1).

Define
\mathcal{D}=\hat{\mathcal{D}}\cap\breve{\mathcal{D}}
, called the two-sides doubling weight.
See
[
10
,
11
]
and the references therein for more results on doubling weights.

For
0<p<\infty
and a weight function
\omega
, the weighted Bergman space
A^{p}_{\omega}
is the space of all functions
f\in H(\mathbb{B}_{n})
such that

\|f\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{\omega}}=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|f(z)|^{p}\omega(z)dV(z)<\infty,

where
dV
is the normalized volume measure on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
. Clearly,
A^{p}_{\omega}
is a Banach space when
1\leq p<\infty
and a complete metric space with the distance
\rho(f,g)=\|f-g\|^{p}_{A^{p}_{\omega}}
when
0<p<1
.
Taking
\omega(z)={\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)\over\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(\alpha+1)}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha},z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
with
\alpha>-1
,
A^{p}_{\omega}
becomes the standard weighted Bergman space
A^{p}_{\alpha}
.

To present our results, we introduce several notations.
For
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
, put

\rho(z):=\rho(\varphi(z),\psi(z)),\,\,z\in\mathbb{B}_{n},

where
\rho(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the pseudohyperbolic distance on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
; see Sect. 2.2.
Given a positive Borel measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
, the pullback measure
\nu\circ\varphi^{-1}
is defined by

(\nu\circ\varphi^{-1})(E)=\nu[\varphi^{-1}(E)]

for Borel sets
E\subset\mathbb{B}_{n}
.
Let
0<r<1
,
0<\alpha,q<\infty
,
u,v
be weights and
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
.
The pullback measures
\eta
and
\sigma_{r}
are defined by

\eta=(|\rho u|^{q}d\mu)\circ\varphi^{-1}+(|\rho v|^{q}d\mu)\circ\psi^{-1},

and

\sigma_{r}=(\chi_{G_{r}}|u-v|^{q}d\mu)\varphi^{-1}+(\chi_{G_{r}}|u-v|^{q}d\mu)\psi^{-1},

respectively, where
\chi_{G_{r}}
is the characteristic function of the set

G_{r}:=\{z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}:\rho(z)<r\}.

Recall that for a Banach space
X
of analytic functions and a positive Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
,
\mu
is called a (varnishing)
q
-Carleson measure for
X
if the identity operator
I_{d}:X\to L^{q}_{\mu}
is bounded (compact).
In this paper, we completely characterized the boundededness and compactness of differences of weighted composition operators from weighted Bergman spaces
A^{p}_{\omega}
to Lebesgue spaces
L^{q}_{\mu}
for full
0<p,q<\infty
on the unit ball.
Our main results are divided into two cases:
p\leq q
and
p>q
, which are stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
0<p\leq q<\infty
,
0<r<1
,
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
,
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
,
u,v
be weights
and
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
.
Then
uC_{\varphi}-vC_{\psi}:A^{p}_{\omega}\to L^{q}_{\mu}
is bounded (resp. compact) if and only if

\eta+\sigma_{r}
is a (resp. varnishing)
q
-Carleson measure for
A^{p}_{\omega}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
0<q<p<\infty
,
0<r<1
,
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
,
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
,
u,v
be weights
and

\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
.
Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

uC_{\varphi}-vC_{\psi}:A^{p}_{\omega}\to L^{q}_{\mu}
is bounded;

(ii)

uC_{\varphi}-vC_{\psi}:A^{p}_{\omega}\to L^{q}_{\mu}
is compact;

(iii)

\eta+\sigma_{r}
is a
q
-Carleson measure for
A^{p}_{\omega}
.

This paper is organized as follows. We give some preliminaries in Section 2. In Section 3, some new characterizations of the (varnishing)
q
-Carleson measure for
A^{p}_{\omega}
for full
0<p,q<\infty
are given. In Section 4, we prove our main results.

Throughout this paper, let
C
be a positive constant that may change from one step to the next. For nonnegative quantities
A
and
B
, we say that
A\lesssim B
if there is a constant
C>0
such that
A\leq CB
. The symbol
A\approx B
means that
A\lesssim B\lesssim A
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect some basic facts that are important to our proof.

2.1.
Compact operator

Let
X,Y
be topological vector spaces. A linear map
T:X\to Y
is said to be compact if the image of any bounded sequence in
X
has a subsequence that converges in
Y
.
To study the compactness of
T:A^{p}_{\omega}\to L^{q}_{\mu}
, we need the following lemma. Its verification is a simplified modification of the proof for Proposition 3.11 in
[
5
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
0<p,q<\infty
,
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
and
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
. Suppose that
T:A^{p}_{\omega}\to L^{q}_{\mu}
is linear and bounded. Then
T:A^{p}_{\omega}\to L^{q}_{\mu}
is compact if and only if
Tf_{k}\to 0
in
L^{q}_{\mu}
, where
\{f_{k}\}
is bounded in
A^{p}_{\omega}
and converges to
0
uniformly on any compact subset of
\mathbb{B}_{n}
.

2.2.
Pseudohyperbolic distance

Recall that the pseudohyperbolic distance between
a,z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
is defined by

\rho(a,z)=|\varphi_{a}(z)|,

where
\varphi_{a}(z)
is the Möbius transformation in
\mathbb{B}_{n}
.
Let
\beta(\cdot,\cdot)
denote the Bergman metric on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
, that is,

\beta(z,w)={1\over 2}\log{1+\rho(z,w)\over 1-\rho(z,w)},\,\,z,w\in\mathbb{B}_{n},

and
D(z,r)=\{w\in\mathbb{B}_{n}:\beta(z,w)<r\}
for
r\in(0,1)
be the Bergman metric ball centered at
z
with radius
r
.
Let
P_{z}
be the orthogonal projection from
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
onto the one dimensional subspace
[z]
generated by
z
and
Q_{z}
be the orthogonal projection from
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
onto
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\ominus[z]
.
For any
z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}\backslash\{0\}
and
r>0
,
D(z,r)
is an ellipsoid consisting of all points
w\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
that satisfy

{|P_{z}(w)-c|^{2}\over s^{2}R^{2}}+{|Q_{z}(w)|^{2}\over sR^{2}}<1,

(2.1)

where

R=\tanh(r),\,c={(1-R^{2})z\over 1-R^{2}|z|^{2}},\,s={1-|z|^{2}\over 1-R^{2}|z|^{2}}.

It is clear that

1-|z|\approx|1-|w|\approx|1-\langle z,w\rangle|

(2.2)

and

|1-\langle z,a\rangle|\approx|1-\langle a,w\rangle|

for any
a\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
and
z,w\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
with
\beta(z,w)<r
, where the constants suppressed depend only on
r
and
n
.

For
\xi\in\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
and
\delta>0
, let

S(\xi,\delta):=\{z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}|~|1-\langle z,\xi\rangle|<\delta\}

be the Carleson tube at
\xi
. From (2.13) in
[
2
]
, we see that

D(te_{1},r)\subset S\left(e_{1},{2(1-t)\over 1-\tanh^{-1}r}\right)

(2.3)

for all
r,t\in(0,1)
.

2.3.
Separated sequences and lattices

A sequence of points
\{a_{j}\}\subset\mathbb{B}_{n}
is said to be separated if there is a constant
\delta>0
such that the Bergman metric
\beta(a_{i},a_{j})\geq\delta
for all
i
and
j
with
i\neq j
. This implies that there exists
r>0
such that the Bergman metric balls
D(a_{j},r)
are pairwise disjoint.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
15
, Theorem 2.23]

There exists a positive integer
N
such that, for any
0<r\leq 1
we can find a sequence
\{a_{k}\}\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
with the following properties:

(i)

\mathbb{B}_{n}=\cup_{k}D(a_{k},r)
;

(ii)

The sets
D(a_{k},{r\over 4})
are mutually disjoint;

(iii)

Each point
z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
belongs to at most
N
of the sets
D(a_{k},4r)
.

Any sequence
\{a_{k}\}
satisfying the above conditions is called an
r
-lattice in the Bergman metric. Clearly, any
r
-lattice sequence is separated.

2.4.
Function property

From [
[
6
]
, Lemma 2.2], we know that the weight
\omega
has the following properties.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
. For
a,z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
, then the following statements hold:

(i)

\omega(S_{a})\approx(1-|a|)^{n}\int_{|a|}^{1}\omega(s)ds
, where
S_{a}
is the Carleson block;

(ii)

\hat{\omega}(a)\approx\hat{\omega}(z)
, if
1-|a|\approx 1-|z|
;

(iii)

There are
0<\alpha:=\alpha(\omega)\leq\beta:=\beta(\omega)
and
C=C(\omega)>1
such that

{1\over C}\left({1-s\over 1-t}\right)^{\lambda}\leq{\hat{\omega}(s)\over\hat{\omega}(t)}\leq C\left({1-s\over 1-t}\right)^{\beta},0\leq s\leq t<1.

For
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
, the twisted weight
W
is defined as follows.

W(z)=W_{\omega}(r):={\hat{\omega}(z)\over 1-|z|},\,\,z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}.

The following result may have appeared in some literature, but for the benefit of the reader, we provide a brief proof here.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
0<p<\infty,\omega\in\mathcal{D}
. Then
\|f\|_{A^{p}_{\omega}}\approx\|f\|_{A^{p}_{W}}
for all
f\in H(\mathbb{B}_{n})
.

Proof.

Let
r_{k}=1-2^{-k}
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
. By
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
,
there is
C=C(\omega)\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle\widehat{W}(r_{k})=

\displaystyle\int_{r_{k}}^{r_{k+1}}{\hat{\omega}(s)\over 1-s}ds+\int_{r_{k+1}}^{1}W(s)ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\hat{\omega}(r_{k})\int_{r_{k}}^{r_{k+1}}{1\over 1-s}ds+\int_{r_{k+1}}^{1}W(s)ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\log 2\cdot\hat{\omega}(r_{k+1})+\int_{r_{k+1}}^{1}W(s)ds\leq C_{1}\widehat{W}(r_{k+1})

for some
C_{1}=C_{1}(\omega)\geq 1
.
Then similar to the proof of Lemma 2.1(
{\rm i}\Rightarrow{\rm iii}
) in
[
10
]
, there are
\gamma:=\gamma(\omega)>0
and
C:=C(\omega)>0
such that

\int_{0}^{t}\left({1-t\over 1-s}\right)^{\gamma}W(s)ds\leq C\widehat{W}(t),\,\,0\leq t<1,

which gives that
W\in\hat{\mathcal{D}}
by Lemma A(iii) in
[
13
]
. Applying Theorem 1 in
[
7
]
,
\|f\|_{A^{p}_{\omega}}\approx\|f\|_{A^{p}_{W}}
for all
f\in H(\mathbb{B}_{n})
if
W(S_{a})\approx\omega(S_{a})
for
a\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
.
Since
W
and
\omega
are radial weights, we get the desired result if
\widehat{W}(r)\approx\hat{\omega}(r)
for
0\leq r<1
.
Using Lemma B(iii) in
[
13
]
, for
0\leq r<1
, there is a
C:=C(\omega)>0
such that

\widehat{W}(r)=\int_{r}^{1}{\hat{\omega}(s)\over 1-s}ds\leq C\hat{\omega}(r)

and by Lemma A(ii) in
[
13
]
, there are constants
\beta:=\beta(\omega)>0
and
C:=C(\omega)>0
such that

\widehat{W}(r)=\int_{r}^{1}{\hat{\omega}(s)\over 1-s}ds\geq C\int_{r}^{1}{\hat{\omega}(r)\over 1-s}\left(1-s\over 1-r\right)^{\beta}ds\geq C\hat{\omega}(r).

Therefore,
\widehat{W}(r)\approx\hat{\omega}(r)
for
0\leq r<1
. The proof is complete.

The following Lemma deals with the integrability of doubling weights.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
\omega\in{\mathcal{D}}
, then there is
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(\omega)\geq 0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}{\omega(z)\over|1-\langle z,a\rangle|^{\lambda n+n}}dV(z)\approx{\hat{\omega}(a)\over(1-|a|)^{\lambda n}},a\in\mathbb{B}_{n},

for all
\lambda>\lambda_{0}
.

Proof.

Using Theorem 1.12 in
[
15
]
and Lemma 2.1 (iii) in
[
10
]
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}{\omega(z)\over|1-\langle z,a\rangle|^{\lambda n+n}}dV(z)=

\displaystyle 2n\int_{0}^{1}\omega(r)r^{2n-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}{1\over|1-\langle r\xi,a\rangle|^{\lambda n+n}}d\sigma(\xi)dr

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}{\omega(r)r^{2n-1}\over(1-|a|r)^{\lambda n}}dr

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|a|}{\omega(r)\over(1-r)^{\lambda n}}dr+\int_{|a|}^{1}{\omega(r)\over(1-|a|)^{\lambda n}}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle{\int_{|a|}^{1}\omega(r)dr\over(1-|a|)^{\lambda n}}={\hat{\omega}(a)\over(1-|a|)^{\lambda n}}

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}{\omega(z)\over|1-\langle z,a\rangle|^{\lambda n+n}}dV(z)=

\displaystyle 2n\int_{0}^{1}\omega(r)r^{2n-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{n}}{1\over|1-\langle r\xi,a\rangle|^{\lambda n+n}}d\sigma(\xi)dr

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\int_{|a|}^{1}{\omega(r)r^{2n-1}\over(1-|a|)^{\lambda n}}dr\approx{\int_{|a|}^{1}\omega(r)dr\over(1-|a|)^{\lambda n}}={\hat{\omega}(a)\over(1-|a|)^{\lambda n}}.

The proof is complete.

To study the boundedness and compactness of
uC_{\varphi}-vC_{\psi}:A^{p}_{\omega}\to L^{q}_{\mu}

K_{a}(z)={1\over 1-\langle a,z\rangle},z,a\in\mathbb{B}_{n}

From the above lemma, we see that

\|K_{a}^{s}\|_{A^{p}_{\omega}}^{p}\approx{\hat{\omega}(a)\over(1-|a|)^{ps-n}}

In the case

2.7
.
Before presenting Lemma

Lemma 2.6
.

If
\omega\in{\mathcal{D}}
, then there is
\beta=\beta(\omega)>0
and
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(\omega)\geq 0

\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}{\omega(z)\over(1-|z|^{2})^{t}|1-\langle z,a\rangle|^{\lambda n+n}}dV(z)\lesssim{\hat{\omega}(a)\over(1-|a|)^{\lambda n+t}},a\in\mathbb{B}_{n},

for all

Proof.

Let
\beta=\beta(\omega)
be the constant in Lemma
2.3
(iii).
Applying Theorem 1.12 in
[
15
]
and Lemma 2.1 (iii) in
[

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}{\omega(z)\over(1-|z|^{2})^{t}|1-\langle z,a\rangle|^{\lambda n+n}}dV(z)\approx

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}{\omega(r)r^{2n-1}\over(1-r)^{t}(1-|a|r)^{\lambda n}}dr

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|a|}{\omega(r)\over(1-r)^{\lambda n+t}}dr+\int_{|a|}^{1}{\omega(r)\over(1-r)^{\lambda n+t}}dr.

Note that Lemma

Lemma 2.7

\|F\|_{A^{p}_{\omega}}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\lambda_{k}|^{p}=C\|\lambda\|_{l^{p}}.

Let

g_{k}(z):={(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{t-{n\over p}}\over\hat{\omega}(a_{k})^{1\over p}(1-\langle z,a_{k}\rangle)^{t}},\,\,z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}.

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{|\lambda_{k}|\over\hat{\omega}(a_{k})^{1\over p}V\left(D(a_{k},r)\right)^{1\over p}}\cdot{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{t+{1\over p}-n-1}\over|1-\langle z,a_{k}\rangle|^{t}}V(D(a_{k},r))

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{k}|{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{t-{n\over p}}\over\hat{\omega}(a_{k})^{1\over p}|1-\langle z,a_{k}\rangle|^{t}}\geq|F(z)|

for all

. We claim that

is bounded on

at this moment. Then there is a constant

such that

\|F\|_{A^{p}_{\omega}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}.

(2.5)

Noting that each point
z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
belongs to at most
N
of the sets

by Lemma
2.2
, we have

|f(z)|^{p}\leq N^{p-1}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{|\lambda_{k}|^{p}\chi_{D(a_{k},4r)}(z)\over\omega(a_{k})V\left(D(a_{k},r)\right)},\,z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}.

Therefore, integrating term by term, we get

\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|f(z)|^{p}\omega(z)dV(z)\leq N^{p-1}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{k}|^{p},

which combined with (
2.5
) deduces the desired result.

Finally, we only need to verify our claim, that is,
\Lambda
is bounded on
L^{p}_{\omega}
. Let

h(z)=(1-|z|^{2})^{-{t-n-1\over 2}},\,\,z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}.

By Lemmas
2.5
and
2.6
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}{(1-|w|^{2})^{t-n-1}\over|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{t}}h^{2}(w)\omega(w)dV(w)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}{\omega(w)\over|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{t}}dV(w)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle{\hat{\omega}(z)\over(1-|z|^{2})^{t-n}}\lesssim{1\over(1-|z|^{2})^{t-n-1}}

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}{(1-|w|^{2})^{t-n-1}\over|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{t}}h^{2}(z)\omega(z)dV(z)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}{(1-|w|^{2})^{t-n-1}\omega(z)\over(1-|z|^{2})^{t-n-1}|1-\langle z,w\rangle|^{t}}dV(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle{\hat{\omega}(w)\over(1-|w|^{2})^{t-n}}\lesssim{1\over(1-|w|^{2})^{t-n-1}},

which implies that
\Lambda
is bounded on
L^{p}_{\omega}
by Schur’s test. The proof is complete.
∎

The following lemma extends the one dimensional result [

Lemma 2.8

Let

3.
Carleson measure

In this section, we characterize the (vanishing)
q
-Carleson measure for
A^{p}_{\omega}
for full
0<p,q<\infty
.
To present our results, we introduce a notation first.
Given
\omega\in\mathcal{D},0<r<1
,
0<s<\infty
and a positive Borel measurev
\mu
on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
, the weighted mean function
\hat{\mu}_{\omega,r,s}

\hat{\mu}_{\omega,r,s}(z):={\mu(D(z,r))\over\omega(D(z,r))^{s}},\,\,z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}.

Write

To characterize the
q
-Carleson measure for

in the case

, we need to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
0<r<1
,
0<p\leq q<\infty
,
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
and
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
. Then for any
f\in A^{p}_{\omega}
, one has

\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|f(z)|^{q}d\mu(z)\lesssim\|f\|^{q-p}_{A^{p}_{\omega}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|f(z)|^{p}\hat{\mu}_{\omega,r,{q\over p}}(z)W(z)dV(z).

Proof.

For
f\in A^{p}_{\omega}
, using the subharmonic property of
|f|^{p}
(see Lemma 2.24 in
[
15
]
), (
2.2
), Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle|f(z)|^{q}\lesssim

\displaystyle\left({1\over(1-|z|^{2})^{n+1}}\int_{D(z,r)}|f(w)|^{p}dV(w)\right)^{q\over p}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left({1\over\hat{\omega}(z)(1-|z|^{2})^{n}}\int_{D(z,r)}|f(w)|^{p}W(w)dV(w)\right)^{q\over p}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle{1\over\left(\hat{\omega}(z)(1-|z|^{2})^{n}\right)^{q\over p}}\|f\|^{q-p}_{A^{p}_{\omega}}\int_{D(z,r)}|f(w)|^{p}W(w)dV(w).

Combined with (
2.1
), (
2.6
), Proposition 1 in
[
6
]
and Lemma
2.3
, we see that

\omega(D(a,r))\approx\omega(S_{a})\approx\hat{\omega}(a)(1-|a|^{2})^{n}.

(3.1)

Therefore, by Fubini’s theorem and Lemma
2.4
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|f(z)|^{q}d\mu(z)\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f\|^{q-p}_{A^{p}_{\omega}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}{1\over\left(\hat{\omega}(z)(1-|z|^{2})^{n}\right)^{q\over p}}\int_{D(z,r)}|f(w)|^{p}W(w)dV(w)d\mu(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f\|^{q-p}_{A^{p}_{\omega}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|f(w)|^{p}W(w){\mu(D(w,r))\over\left(\hat{\omega}(w)(1-|w|^{2})^{n}\right)^{q\over p}}dV(w)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f\|^{q-p}_{A^{p}_{\omega}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|f(w)|^{p}\hat{\mu}_{\omega,r,{q\over p}}(w)W(w)dV(w).

The proof is complete.
∎

The following result gives the characterization of the (vanishing)
q
-Carleson measure in the case
A^{p}_{\omega}
for
p\leq q
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
0<p\leq q<\infty
,
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
and
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
.
Then there is
r_{0}:=r_{0}(\omega)\in(0,1)
such that the following statements hold:

(i)

\mu
is a
q
-Carleson measure for
A^{p}_{\omega}
if and only if for some (or all)
r\in[r_{0},1)
such that
\hat{\mu}_{\omega,r,{q\over p}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
;

(ii)

\mu
is a vanishing
q
-Carleson measure for
A^{p}_{\omega}
if and only if for some (or all)
r\in[r_{0},1)
such that
\hat{\mu}_{\omega,r,{q\over p}}(a)\to 0
as
|a|\to 1
.

Proof.

(i). If
\hat{\mu}_{\omega,r,{q\over p}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{n})

then
\mu
is a
q
-Carleson measure for
A^{p}_{\omega}
by Lemmas
3.1
and
2.4
.
We next verify the necessity. Suppose that
\mu
is a
q
-Carleson measure for
A^{p}_{\omega}
. Consider the test function

f_{a}(z)={1\over\hat{\omega}(a)^{1\over p}(1-|a|^{2})^{n\over p}}\left({1-|a|^{2}\over|1-\langle z,a\rangle|}\right)^{\gamma+n\over p},\,\,a,z\in\mathbb{B}_{n},

(3.2)

where
\gamma
is large enough.
From Lemma
2.3
and Lemma 6 in
[
7
]
,
f_{a}\in A^{p}_{\omega}
.
Applying (
2.2
), (
3.1
) and the fact
W(S_{a})\approx\omega(S_{a}),a\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
( see the proof of Lemma
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle(1-|a|^{2})^{n}\hat{\omega}(a)\approx

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\hat{\omega}(a)\over 1-|a|^{2}}\int_{D(a,r)}dV(z)\approx\int_{D(a,r)}{\hat{\omega}(z)\over 1-|z|^{2}}dV(z)

(3.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W(D(a,r))\approx W(S_{a})\approx\omega(S_{a})\approx\omega(D(a,r)).

Therefore, for
a\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
, by the assumption,

\hat{\mu}_{\omega,r,{q\over p}}(a)\approx{\mu(D(a,r))\over\left(\hat{\omega}(a)(1-|a|^{2})^{n}\right)^{q\over p}}\lesssim\|f_{a}\|^{q}_{L^{q}_{\mu}}\lesssim\|f\|^{q}_{A^{p}_{\omega}}\approx 1,

which means that

\mu(D(a,r))\lesssim\left(\hat{\omega}(a)(1-|a|^{2})^{n}\right)^{q\over p}\lesssim(\omega(D(a,r)))^{q\over p}.

This completes the proof of (i).

(ii) Assume that
\mu
is a vanishing
q
-Carleson measure for
A^{p}_{\omega}
.
Consider the test function
f_{a}
defined as (
3.2
).
Then
f_{a}\in A^{p}_{\omega}
and converges to
0
uniformly on any compact subset of
\mathbb{B}_{n}
as
|a|\to 1
.
Therefore, by Lemma
2.1
,

0=\lim_{|a|\to 1^{-}}\|f_{a}\|^{q}_{L^{q}_{\mu}}\geq\lim_{|a|\to 1^{-}}\int_{D(a,r)}|f_{a}(z)|^{q}d\mu(z)\gtrsim\lim_{|a|\to 1^{-}}{\mu(D(a,r))\over\left(\hat{\omega}(a)(1-|a|^{2})^{n}\right)^{q\over p}},

which means that
\hat{\mu}_{\omega,r,{q\over p}}(a)\to 0
as
|a|\to 1
.

Conversely, assume that
\hat{\mu}_{\omega,r,{q\over p}}(a)\to 0
as
|a|\to 1
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there is
r:=r(\omega)\in(0,1)
such that
{\mu(D(a,r))\over\left(\hat{\omega}(a)(1-|a|^{2})^{n}\right)^{q\over p}}<\varepsilon
when
|a|>r
.
Put
d\mu_{r}(z):=\chi_{r\leq|z|<1}d\mu(z)
.
If
|a|\geq r
, then
\mu_{r}(D(a,r))\leq\mu(D(a,r))
.
If
0<|a|<r
, by the fact
D(a,r)=\varphi_{a}(D(0,r))
, then

\displaystyle\mu_{r}(D(a,r))=

\displaystyle(1-|a|^{2})^{n+1}\int_{D(0,r)\backslash D(0,\tanh^{-1}r)}{d\mu(z)\over|1-\langle z,a\rangle|^{2(n+1)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mu(D(0,r)\backslash D(0,\tanh^{-1}r)).

From [
[
15
]
, page 64], we see that there is a finite sequence

such that

D(0,r)\backslash D(0,\tanh^{-1}r)\subset\cup_{j=1}^{N}D(a^{(j)},\delta)

for some

, where

,

.
Therefore,

\mu_{r}(D(a,r))\lesssim\cup_{j=1}^{N}D(a^{(j)},\delta)\lesssim\varepsilon,

which implies that
\hat{\mu}_{\omega,r,{q\over p}}(a)\lesssim\varepsilon
.
Thus,
\|I_{d}\|^{q}_{A^{p}_{\omega}\to L^{q}_{\mu_{r}}}\lesssim\varepsilon
.
Let
\{f_{k}\}\in A^{p}_{\omega}
and converge to
0
uniformly on any compact subset of

. Then

\displaystyle\lim\sup_{k\to\infty}\|f_{k}\|^{q}_{L^{q}_{\mu}}=

\displaystyle\lim\sup_{k\to\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|f_{k}(z)|^{q}d\mu_{r}(z)+\int_{r\mathbb{B}_{n}}|f_{k}(z)|^{q}d\mu(z)\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon\lim\sup_{k\to\infty}\|f_{k}\|^{q}_{A^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim\varepsilon,

which gives that

is a vanishing
q
-Carleson measure for

by the arbitrariness of

.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let

,
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
and
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{B}_{n}

Then there is

-Carleson measure for

(ii)

(i)\Rightarrow(iv)

,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}r_{k}(t)f_{k}(z)\right|^{q}d\mu(z)\leq\|I_{d}\|^{q}_{A^{p}_{\omega}\to L^{q}_{\mu}}\|F_{t}\|^{q}_{A^{p}_{\omega}}\lesssim\|I_{d}\|^{q}_{A^{p}_{\omega}\to L^{q}_{\mu}}\|\lambda\|^{q}_{l^{q}}.

Then integrating with respect to

[0,1]

2.2

,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\hat{\mu}_{\omega,r}(z)^{p\over p-q}W(z)dV(z)

\displaystyle\|f_{k}\|^{q}_{L^{q}_{\mu}}=

4.1.

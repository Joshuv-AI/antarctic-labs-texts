The Three-loop MHV Octagon from Q ¯ \bar{Q} equations
Zhenjie Li, Chi Zhang

Abstract
The Q ¯ \bar{Q} equations, rooted in the dual superconformal anomalies, are a powerful tool for computing amplitudes in planar 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory. By using the Q ¯ \bar{Q} equations, we compute the symbol of the first MHV amplitude with algebraic letters – the three-loop 8-point amplitude (or the octagon remainder function) – in this theory. The symbol alphabet for this amplitude consists of 204 independent rational letters and shares the same 18 algebraic letters with the two-loop 8-point NMHV amplitude.

2.2
Multiple polylogarithms, symbols and their integrations

The known MHV and NMHV amplitudes in planar
\mathcal{N}=4
sYM theory are all described by multiple polylogarithms, including two-loop NMHV amplitudes. Therefore, computing integrals in (
14
) is essentially to deal with integrals of multiple polylogarithms. In this subsection, we will briefly review some basic facts about multiple polylogarithms.

Multiple polylogarithms are generalizations of classical polylogarithms, such as
\log,\operatorname{Li}_{2}
,
\dots
, which are defined by
goncharov2005galois

G(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n};z):=\int_{0}^{z}\mathrm{d}\log(t-a_{1})\,G(a_{2},\ldots,a_{n};t),

(16)

with the starting point
G(;z):=1
and exceptional cases

G(\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{k};z):=\frac{1}{k!}(\log z)^{k},

where
n
is called the weight of
G(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n};z)
. For example,

G(0;x)=\log x,\quad G(1;x)=\log(1-x),\quad G(0,1;x)=-\operatorname{Li}_{2}(x),\quad\dots.

It is straightforward to see that the total differential of (
16
) satisfies

\mathrm{d}G(a_{1},\dots,a_{n};z)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}G(a_{1},\dots,\hat{a_{i}},\dots,a_{n};z)\,\mathrm{d}\log\frac{a_{i}-a_{i{-}1}}{a_{i}-a_{i{+}1}},

(17)

where
a_{i}
is deleted in the
i
-th summand with boundary cases
a_{0}:=z
and
a_{n{+}1}:=0
.

There is a well-known Hopf algebra structure
Goncharov:2005sla
, which has led to the notion of
symbol

Goncharov:2010jf
;
Duhr:2011zq
. For any weight-
n

G^{(n)}
whose differential reads

\mathrm{d}G^{(n)}=\sum_{i}G^{(n-1)}_{i}\mathrm{d}\log x_{i},

where
\{G^{(n-1)}_{i}\}
are polylogarithms of lower weight
(n{-}1)
, its symbol
\mathcal{S}(G^{(n)})
is recursively defined by

\mathcal{S}(G^{(n)}):=\sum_{i}\mathcal{S}(G^{(n-1)}_{i})\otimes x_{i}.

For example, the symbol of
G(a_{1},\dots,a_{n};z)
is

\mathcal{S}(G(a_{1},\dots,a_{n};z))=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{S}(G(a_{1},\dots,\hat{a_{i}},\dots,a_{n};z))\otimes\frac{a_{i}-a_{i{-}1}}{a_{i}-a_{i{+}1}},

which leads to special cases

\mathcal{S}(\log(x))=x,\quad\mathcal{S}(\operatorname{Li}_{2}(x))=-\,(1-x)\otimes x.

The entries of a symbol are called its
letters
, and the collection of all letters is called its
alphabet
.

Conversely, we can first construct the space of symbols, which are spanned by

\sum_{I}c_{I}\,a^{I}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a^{I}_{n}\>,

where
c_{I}
are rational numbers and
a^{I}_{i}
are letters. Symbols of functions should satisfy the

:

\sum_{I}c_{I}\,a^{I}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a^{I}_{i-1}\otimes\widehat{a^{I}_{i}}\otimes\widehat{a^{I}_{i+1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes a^{I}_{n}\,\mathrm{d}\log a^{I}_{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}\log a^{I}_{i+1}=0

(18)

for any
i
. It follows from the fact that
\mathrm{d}^{2}=0
and the recursive definitions of multiple polylogarithms and symbols.

For our calculation, it’s more convenient to perform integrations on the symbol level directly based on the following rules
CaronHuot:2011kk
(a short proof is reviewed in Appendix
A

\int_{a}^{b}{\rm d}\log(t+c)\,(F(t)\otimes w(t)),

where

is a linear reducible symbol in
t
,

its entries are products of powers of linear polynomials in
t
, and

is the last entry. The total differential of this integral is the sum of the following two parts:

(1)

the contribution from endpoints:

{\rm d}\log(t+c)\Bigl(F(t)\otimes w(t)\Bigr)\Bigr|_{t=a}^{t=b}\Rightarrow\Bigl(F(t)\otimes w(t)\otimes(t+c)\Bigl)\Bigr|_{t=a}^{t=b},

(19)

(2)

contributions from the last entry: for a term where
w(t)
is a constant,

\left(\int_{a}^{b}{\rm d}\log(t+c)\,F(t)\right){\rm d}\log w\Rightarrow\left(\int_{a}^{b}{\rm d}\log(t+c)\,F(t)\right)\otimes w,

(20)

and for a term where

\left(\int_{a}^{b}{\rm d}\log\frac{t+c}{t+d}\,F(t)\right){\rm d}\log(c-d)\Rightarrow\left(\int_{a}^{b}{\rm d}\log\frac{t+c}{t+d}\,F(t)\right)\otimes(c-d).

(21)

We have performed various consistency checks on our results, for example, cyclicity, dual conformal invariance, and the first entries being of the form
\langle i\,i{+1}\,j\,j{+}1\rangle
; Here we present details for the more non-trivial checks, including integrability and collinear limits.

Integrability.

It is crucial that the whole data, which is of the form
\sum_{i}F_{i}\mathrm{d}\log x_{i}
, can be integrated to a function. This is guaranteed if

\sum_{i}\mathrm{d}\log F_{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}\log x_{i}=0.

In terms of the symbol, the result is integrable if and only if the symbol vanishes after taking the last two entries to be the wedge of their
\mathrm{d}\log
’s (see
Chen:1977oja
;
brown2009multiple
).
We only need to calculate all two forms

\mathrm{d}\log a_{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}\log a_{j}

for all
a_{i}
and
a_{j}
in the alphabet, which can be numerically done by using any full-rank parametrization of
\mathrm{Gr}(4,8)
. After inserting these numerical two forms, we finally checked that this huge data is integrable. As far as we know, there’s no very efficient way to find all linear relations between these two forms by now, which is also crucial for bootstrapping.

Collinear limits.

We check that the MHV octagon reduces to the MHV heptagon upon taking the collinear limit: first set

Z_{8}\to Z_{7}+\epsilon\frac{\langle 1257\rangle}{\langle 1256\rangle}Z_{6}+\epsilon\tau\frac{\langle 2567\rangle}{\langle 1256\rangle}Z_{1}+\eta\frac{\langle 1567\rangle}{\langle 1256\rangle}Z_{2}

with fixed
\tau
, then take the limit
\eta\to 0
before
\epsilon\to 0
. After taking such limits and keeping leading terms of
\eta
and
\epsilon
, it is highly non-trivial that this limit does not depend on the parameters
\eta
,
\epsilon
and
\tau
,
i.e.
it is a smooth limit. Moreover, we find this limit is precisely the three-loop MHV heptagon
Drummond:2014ffa
. In particular, all algebraic letters disappear in this limit.

5
Conclusion and Outlook

In this paper, with the two-loop 9-point NMHV amplitude as the input, we computed the symbol of the three-loop octagon remainder function
R_{8,0}^{(3)}
, which is the first three-loop amplitudes containing algebraic letters, by using the
\bar{Q}
equations. The alphabet of the symbol
\mathcal{S}(R_{8,0}^{(3)})
consists of 18 algebraic letters and 204 rational ones, where 24 of the rational letters are new compared with the symbol alphabet of the two-loop 8-point NMHV amplitude. Since there is no qualitative difference for higher points, we expect that the three-loop MHV amplitudes share the same algebraic letters with the two-loop NMHV amplitudes for all multiplicities.

Although there are new letters in the three-loop MHV octagon compared with the known two-loop octagons, the symbol alphabet is covered by the prediction from the tropical Grassmannian
\mathrm{Tr}(4,8)

Henke:2019hve
;
Drummond:2019cxm
which consists of
280
rational letters and the same 18 algebraic letters.
It is highly desirable to understand how the alphabets for MHV octagons differ at different loop orders. Furthermore,
as reported in
He:2021mme
, instead of the usual BDS-subtracted amplitudes, the three-loop MHV octagon with
minimal
subtraction
Golden:2018gtk
fulfills the (extended) Steinmann relation. There are other arguments
Henke:2019hve
;
Herderschee:2021dez
that suggest larger alphabets for octagons, however, it is worthwhile to survey the bootstrap program for octagons already. For example, it would be interesting to see whether the usual constraints used in the bootstrap program are sufficient to fix the known two-loop and three-loop octagons with a redundant symbol alphabet, say that one given by the tropical Grassmannian
\mathrm{Tr}(4,8)
.

By definition, the
\bar{Q}
and
Q^{(1)}
equations are sufficient to fix arbitrary amplitudes in planar
\mathcal{N}=4
sYM theory up to some Yangian invariants. Thus, a very important problem is how to apply parity conjugates of the
\bar{Q}
equations, that are the
Q^{(1)}
equations, to the computation of N
k>1
MHV amplitudes. A good start point would be one- and two-loop N
2
MHV octagons, which are parity invariant by themselves and hence can be fixed by the
\bar{Q}
equations and the parity symmetry in principle. Similarly, the two-loop N
3
MHV 10-point amplitude can be fixed in the same way, it would be amusing to see how the elliptic polylogarithms arise in this method.

Besides the application in the computations of MHV/NMHV amplitudes, another major application of the
\bar{Q}
equations is to constrain the last entries and the last two entries
He:2021mme
for amplitudes. On the one hand, it is worth exploring the relation of such constraints with extended Steinmann/cluster adjacency, on the other hand, similar constraints also appear in the bootstrap of certain form factors
Brandhuber:2012vm
;
Dixon:2020bbt
, it would be interesting to generalize the
\bar{Q}
equations to these cases and investigate other non-perturbative applications of the
\bar{Q}
equations.

Appendix A
Symbol integration method

Here we first prove the symbol integration method described in section
2.2
. Consider an integral

\int_{a}^{b}{\rm d}\log(t+c)\,(F(t)\otimes w(t)),

where
F(t)\otimes w(t)
is a linear reducible symbol in
t
,
i.e.
its entries are products of powers of linear polynomials in
t
, and
w(t)
is the last entry. The total differential of this integral is the sum of the following two parts:

(1)

the contribution from endpoints:

\Bigl({\rm d}\log(t+c)F(t)\otimes w(t)\Bigr)\Bigr|_{t=a}^{t=b}=\Bigl(F(t)\otimes w(t)\otimes(t+c)\Bigr)\Bigr|_{t=a}^{t=b},

(2)

contributions from the last entry: for a term where
w(t)
is a constant,

\left(\int_{a}^{b}{\rm d}\log(t+c)\,F(t)\right){\rm d}\log w=\left(\int_{a}^{b}{\rm d}\log(t+c)\,F(t)\right)\otimes w,

and for a term where
w(t)=t+d
,

\left(\int_{a}^{b}{\rm d}\log\frac{t+c}{t+d}\,F(t)\right){\rm d}\log(c-d)=\left(\int_{a}^{b}{\rm d}\log\frac{t+c}{t+d}\,F(t)\right)\otimes(c-d).

Proof.

We use
D
to represent the total differential with respect to variables other than
t
. Therefore, the total differential of the integral is

\displaystyle D\int_{a}^{b}{\rm d}\log(t+c)\,(F(t)\otimes w(t))=

\displaystyle\,\frac{Db}{b+c}F(b)\otimes w(b)-\frac{Da}{a+c}F(a)\otimes w(a)

\displaystyle+Dc\int_{a}^{b}{\rm d}\biggl(\frac{1}{t+c}\biggr)\,(F(t)\otimes w(t))

\displaystyle+\int_{a}^{b}{\rm d}\log(t+c)\,F(t)D\log w(t).

The second line equals to

\frac{Dc}{b+c}F(b)\otimes w(b)-\frac{Dc}{a+c}F(a)\otimes w(a)-Dc\int_{a}^{b}\mathrm{d}\log(t+c)\,F(t)\partial_{t}\log w(t)

by integrating by parts, so

\displaystyle D\int_{a}^{b}{\rm d}\log(t+c)\,(F(t)\otimes w(t))

\displaystyle=\frac{D(b+c)}{b+c}F(b)\otimes w(b)-\frac{D(a+c)}{a+c}F(a)\otimes w(a)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{a}^{b}{\rm d}\log(t+c)\,(D-Dc\,\partial_{t})(\log w(t))\,F(t).

The first line is exactly the contribution from the endpoints, and the second line is the contribution from the last entry.

Now suppose that the last entry
w(t)=pt+q
is a linear function of
t
, then

\displaystyle{\rm d}\log(t+c)\,(D-Dc\,\partial_{t})

\displaystyle(\log w(t))=\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t+c}\frac{1}{pt+q}\biggl(tDp+Dq-pDc\biggr)

\displaystyle=-D\log(q/p-c)\,\mathrm{d}\log(pt+q)+D\log(cp-q)\,\mathrm{d}\log(t+c)

by a partial fraction which completes the proof.
∎

There is an interesting subtle for the above method:

\int_{a}^{b}{\rm d}\log(t+c)\,F(t),

is not invariant under the variable substitution in general if
F(t)
is not integrable. To show it, we consider two simpler integrals
\int_{a}^{b}\mathrm{d}\log(t)F(t)
and
\int_{ac}^{bc}\mathrm{d}\log(t)F(t/c)
for a non-integrable symbol
F(t)
. It’s clear that we make a variable substitution
t\mapsto ct
. If
F
is integrable, these two integrals should be the same and independent of
c
, but this is not true for a non-integrable symbol
F(t)
. In fact, the partial differential with respect to
c
of the last integral is

\displaystyle c\partial_{c}\int_{ac}^{bc}\mathrm{d}\log(t)F(t/c)

\displaystyle=F(b)-F(a)+\int_{ac}^{bc}\mathrm{d}\log(t)\,c\partial_{c}F(t/c)

\displaystyle=F(b)-F(a)-\int_{ac}^{bc}\mathrm{d}\log(t)\,t\partial_{t}F(t/c).

This may not vanish,
i.e.
results of two integrals by applying the symbol integration method are different. For example,
F(t)=(t+d)\otimes t
, then

t\partial_{t}F(t/c)=\log(t/c+d)t\partial_{t}\log(t/c)=\log(t/c+d)

and

\int_{ac}^{bc}\mathrm{d}\log(t)\,t\partial_{t}F(t/c)=F(b)-F(a)+\frac{b(a+d)}{a(b+d)}\otimes d.

In other words,
\int_{a}^{b}\mathrm{d}F(t)
usually doesn’t only depend on two end points
F(b)-F(a)
if
F
is not integrable.

This fact will bother us when the symbol involves irrational objects: the integrable integrand decomposes into two parts

I=\int_{a}^{b}\mathrm{d}\log(t+c)\>\bigl(F_{1}(t)+F_{2}(t)\bigr),

(30)

where
F_{1}
is purely rational and
F_{2}
is not, and they are
not
integrable individually. The obstruction of applying the symbol integration is that some letters of
F_{2}
are not rational in
t
. However, once we find a variable substitution
t\mapsto t(z)
such that letters of
F_{2}(z)
and
t+c
can be written as products of linear factors of
z
, we want to apply the symbol integration procedure on

I_{1}=\int_{a}^{b}\mathrm{d}\log(t+c)F_{1}(t)\quad\text{and}\quad I_{2}=\int_{z(a)}^{z(b)}\mathrm{d}\log(t(z)+c)F_{2}(z)

individually, then add them together to get the answer of the original integral. Unfortunately, this may lead to a wrong answer. The reason is very simple, suppose that
t+c
is written as products of linear factors of
z
,
i.e.

t(z)+c=c_{0}\prod_{i}(z+a_{i})^{n_{i}},

the partial differential with respect to
c_{0}
of
I_{2}=\int_{a}^{b}\mathrm{d}\log(t+c)F_{2}(t)

may not vanish if
F_{2}
is not integrable, but we miss it in the integral

\sum_{i}n_{i}\int_{z(a)}^{z(b)}\mathrm{d}\log(z+a_{i})F_{2}(z).

The solution to this problem is also simple: just adding the missing part back

I_{c_{0}}:=\int_{a}^{b}\mathrm{d}\log(t)F_{1}(t)-\int_{c_{0}a}^{c_{0}b}\mathrm{d}\log(t)F_{1}(t/c_{0})\,\,\text{``=''}\int\mathrm{d}\log(t/(c_{0}t))F_{1}(t),

which corresponds to the partial differential with respect to
c_{0}
in the total differential, then the original integral equals to

\int_{a}^{b}\mathrm{d}\log\frac{t+c}{c_{0}}(F_{1}(t)+F_{2}(t))=I_{c_{0}}+\int_{a}^{b}\mathrm{d}\log(t+c)F_{1}(t)+\sum_{i}n_{i}\int_{z(a)}^{z(b)}\mathrm{d}\log(z+a_{i})F_{2}(z),

where we divide a
c_{0}
in the
\mathrm{d}\log
by hand, but it does not affect the result since
F_{1}+F_{2}
is integrable. Each “integral” on the right-hand side of the above equation can be carried out by the symbol integration method described above, and it’s also direct to prove that acting with
\partial_{c_{0}}
on the right-hand side gives zero.

Appendix B
One-fold integrals with a quadratic curve

As reviewed in section
2
, there is an automated algorithm for computing the symbol of one-fold integrals of the form

\int_{x_{i}}^{x_{f}}\mathrm{d}\log(x+\alpha)\>F_{n}(x,\{\beta_{j}\})\>,

(31)

where
F_{n}
is a weight
n
polylogarithm whose letters are rational in
x
. In the computation of multi-loop octagons and beyond
Zhang:2019vnm
;
He:2020vob
and certain Feynman integrals
He:2020lcu
, we will encounter one-fold integrals of the form

\mathscr{I}_{n+1}=\int_{x_{i}}^{x_{f}}\mathrm{d}\log r(x,y)\>G_{n}(x,y,\{\beta_{j}\})

(32)

where
r(x,y)
is some rational function in
x,y
and
G_{n}
is a weight
n
polylogarithm whose letters are rational in
x,y
with a quadratic curve

y^{2}=x^{2}-2ax+b\>.

These integrals of the form (
32
) can be reduced to the cases in (
31
) through a variable substitution, say

x=\frac{t^{2}-b/4}{t-a/2}\>.

(33)

Sometimes, an algorithm “without” variable substitutions is more convenient for computing the symbol of the integrals of the form (
32
). In this appendix, we will provide such an algorithm by taking turns to use the variable substitution (
33
) and the algorithm (
19
) – (
21
).

The key point is that
\mathrm{d}\log r(x,y)
can be expanded in the basis

\mathbb{Q}\biggl[\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x-\alpha},\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{y},\frac{y_{\beta}\mathrm{d}x}{y(x-\beta)}\biggr]\>,

(34)

where
y_{\beta}=\sqrt{\beta^{2}-2a\beta+b}
. Indeed, one can check that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{y}=\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t-a/2}\>,\qquad\frac{y_{\beta}\mathrm{d}x}{y(x-\beta)}=\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t-Y_{\beta}^{+}/2}-\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t-Y_{\beta}^{-}/2}

(35)

are of
\mathrm{d}\log
-form in
t
, where
Y_{\beta}^{\pm}=\beta\pm y_{\beta}
, and the other terms, like
(x-a)^{-2}y^{-1}
, in
\mathrm{d}\log r(x,y)=(r_{1}(x)+r_{2}(x)/y)\mathrm{d}x
are forbidden since they have singularities other than logarithmic singularities.

In general, the total differential of
G_{n}
in (
32
) can be written as

\mathrm{d}G_{n}=\sum G_{n-1}^{(i)}\mathrm{d}\log p_{i}(x,y),

(36)

where
p_{i}(x,y)
is a polynomial in
x
and
y
. Then, the total differential of
\mathscr{I}_{n+1}
receives contributions from:

(i)

The boundary term

\mathrm{d}\log r(x_{f},y_{x_{f}})G_{n}(x_{f},y_{x_{f}})-\mathrm{d}\log r(x_{i},y_{x_{i}})G_{n}(x_{i},y_{x_{i}})\>.

(37)

(ii)

Suppose that the coefficient of the leading term in the expansion of
p_{i}(x,y)
around
x=\infty
is
c_{i}
, then there is

\sum\mathrm{d}\log c_{i}\int G_{n-1}^{(i)}\>\mathrm{d}\log r(x,y)\>.

(38)

(iii)

Assume that the
\log r(x,y)
and
\log p_{i}(x,y)
have the following expansions

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\log r(x,y)}{\partial x}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu}\frac{A_{\mu}\mathrm{d}x}{x-\alpha_{\mu}}+\sum_{\nu}\frac{B_{\nu}\,y_{\beta_{\nu}}\mathrm{d}x}{y(x-\beta_{\nu})}+\frac{C\,\mathrm{d}x}{y}\>,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\log p_{i}(x,y)}{\partial x}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{\rho}\frac{\tilde{A}_{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}x}{x-\tilde{\alpha}_{\rho}}+\sum_{\sigma}\frac{\tilde{B}_{\sigma}\,y_{\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma}}\mathrm{d}x}{y(x-\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma})}+\frac{\tilde{C}\,\mathrm{d}x}{y}\>.

Then there is

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{\mu,\rho}A_{\mu}\tilde{A}_{\rho}\mathrm{d}\log(\alpha_{\mu}-\tilde{\alpha}_{\rho})\biggl(\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x-\alpha_{\mu}}-\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x-\tilde{\alpha}_{\rho}}\biggr)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\mu,\sigma}\frac{A_{\mu}\tilde{B}_{\sigma}}{2}\Biggl(\mathrm{d}\log\frac{a-Y_{\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma}}^{+}}{a-Y_{\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma}}^{-}}\biggl(\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x-\alpha_{\mu}}-\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{y}\biggr)-2\,\mathrm{d}\log(\alpha_{\mu}-\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma})\frac{y_{\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma}}\,\mathrm{d}x}{y(x-\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\mathrm{d}\log\frac{\bigl(Y^{-}_{\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma}}-Y^{-}_{\alpha_{\mu}}\bigr)\bigl(Y^{+}_{\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma}}-Y^{+}_{\alpha_{\mu}}\bigr)}{\bigl(Y^{+}_{\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma}}-Y^{-}_{\alpha_{\mu}}\bigr)\bigl(Y^{-}_{\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma}}-Y^{+}_{\alpha_{\mu}}\bigr)}\frac{y_{\alpha_{\mu}}\mathrm{d}x}{y(x-\alpha_{\mu})}\Biggr)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\mu}\frac{A_{\mu}\tilde{C}}{2}\Biggl(\mathrm{d}\log\frac{a^{2}-b}{4}\biggl(\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x-\alpha_{\mu}}-\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{y}\biggr)+\mathrm{d}\log\frac{a-Y^{+}_{\alpha_{\mu}}}{a-Y^{-}_{\alpha_{\mu}}}\,\frac{y_{\alpha_{\mu}}\,\mathrm{d}x}{y(x-\alpha_{\mu})}\Biggr)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\nu,\rho}\frac{B_{\nu}\tilde{A}_{\rho}}{2}\Biggl(2\,\mathrm{d}\log(\tilde{\alpha}_{\rho}-\beta_{\nu})\,\frac{y_{\beta_{\nu}}\,\mathrm{d}x}{y(x-\beta_{\nu})}+\mathrm{d}\log\frac{a-Y^{+}_{\beta_{\nu}}}{a-Y^{-}_{\beta_{\nu}}}\biggl(\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{y}-\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x-\tilde{\alpha}_{\rho}}\biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\mathrm{d}\log\frac{\bigl(Y_{\tilde{\alpha}_{\rho}}^{+}-Y_{\beta_{\sigma}}^{-}\bigr)\bigl(Y_{\tilde{\alpha}_{\rho}}^{-}-Y_{\beta_{\sigma}}^{+}\bigr)}{\bigl(Y_{\tilde{\alpha}_{\rho}}^{-}-Y_{\beta_{\sigma}}^{-}\bigr)\bigl(Y_{\tilde{\alpha}_{\rho}}^{+}-Y_{\beta_{\sigma}}^{+}\bigr)}\,\frac{y_{\tilde{\alpha}_{\rho}}\,\mathrm{d}x}{y(x-\tilde{\alpha}_{\rho})}\Biggr)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\nu,\sigma}\frac{B_{\nu}\tilde{B}_{\sigma}}{2}\Biggl(\mathrm{d}\log\frac{a-Y_{\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma}}^{+}}{a-Y_{\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma}}^{-}}\frac{y_{\beta_{\nu}}\,\mathrm{d}x}{y(x-\beta_{\nu})}-\mathrm{d}\log\frac{a-Y_{\beta_{\nu}}^{+}}{a-Y_{\beta_{\nu}}^{-}}\frac{y_{\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma}}\,\mathrm{d}x}{y(x-\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\mathrm{d}\log\frac{\bigl(Y_{\beta_{\nu}}^{-}-Y_{\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma}}^{-}\bigr)\bigl(Y_{\beta_{\nu}}^{+}-Y_{\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma}}^{+}\bigr)}{\bigl(Y_{\beta_{\nu}}^{-}-Y_{\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma}}^{+}\bigr)\bigl(Y_{\beta_{\nu}}^{+}-Y_{\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma}}^{-}\bigr)}\biggl(\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x-\beta_{\nu}}-\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x-\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma}}\biggr)\Biggr)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\nu}\frac{B_{\nu}\tilde{C}}{2}\Biggl(\mathrm{d}\log\frac{a-Y_{\beta_{\nu}}^{+}}{a-Y_{\beta_{\nu}}^{-}}\biggl(\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x-\beta_{nu}}-\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{y}\biggr)+\mathrm{d}\log\frac{a^{2}-b}{4}\frac{y_{\beta_{\nu}}\,\mathrm{d}x}{y(x-\beta_{\nu})}\Biggr)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\rho}\frac{C\tilde{A}_{\rho}}{2}\Biggl(\mathrm{d}\log\frac{a^{2}-b}{4}\biggl(\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{y}-\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x-\tilde{\alpha}_{\rho}}\biggr)-\mathrm{d}\log\frac{a-Y_{\tilde{\alpha}_{\rho}}^{+}}{a-Y_{\tilde{\alpha}_{\rho}}^{-}}\,\frac{y_{\tilde{\alpha}_{\rho}}\,\mathrm{d}x}{y(x-\tilde{\alpha}_{\rho})}\Biggr)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\sigma}\frac{C\tilde{B}_{\sigma}}{2}\Biggl(\mathrm{d}\log\frac{a-Y_{\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma}}^{+}}{a-Y_{\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma}}^{-}}\biggl(\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{y}-\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x-\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma}}\biggr)-\mathrm{d}\log\frac{a^{2}-b}{4}\,\frac{y_{\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma}}\,\mathrm{d}x}{y(x-\tilde{\beta}_{\sigma})}\Biggr)\>,

(39)

where we have omitted
\int G^{(i)}_{n-1}
for saving space.

The above formula is nothing but a result of using the transformation (
35
), the algorithm (
19

21
), as well as the inverse of (
35

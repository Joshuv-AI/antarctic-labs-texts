On the treewidth of generalized q q -Kneser graphs
Klaus Metsch

Abstract
The generalized q q -Kneser graph K q ​ ( n , k , t ) K_{q}(n,k,t) for integers k > t > 0 k>t>0 and n > 2 ​ k − t n>2k-t is the graph whose vertices are the k k -dimensional subspaces of an n n -dimensional F q F_{q} -vectorspace with two vertices U 1 U_{1} and U 2 U_{2} adjacent if and only if dim ( U 1 ∩ U 2 ) < t \dim(U_{1}\cap U_{2})<t . The imposed bound on n n is a significant improvement of the previously known bound. One consequence of our results is that the treewidth of each q q -Kneser graph K q ​ ( n , k , t ) K_{q}(n,k,t) with k > t > 0 k>t>0 and n ≥ 3 ​ k − t + 9 n\geq 3k-t+9 is equal to [ n k ] q − [ n − t k − t ] q − 1 {n\brack k}_{q}-{n-t\brack k-t}_{q}-1 .

Introduction

In this paper we only consider simple
graphs

\Gamma
without loops, that is
\Gamma
is a pair
(X,E)
where
X
is a non-empty set and
E
is a set of subsets of cardinality two of
X
. By graph we always refer to a simple graph without loops. The elements of
X
are called
vertices
and the elements of
E
are called
edges
of
\Gamma
. We write
X=V(\Gamma)
. A graph is
empty
if it has no edges, and
finite
if it has only finitely many vertices.

A
tree decomposition
of a graph
\Gamma
is a pair
(T,B)
where
T
is a tree and
B=(B_{t}:t\in V(T))
is a collection of subsets
B_{t}
of
V(\Gamma)
, indexed by the vertices of
T
, such that

(TD1)

every edge
\{u,v\}
of
\Gamma
is contained in
B_{t}
for some
t\in V(T)
, and

(TD2)

for each
v\in V(\Gamma)
, the set
\{t\in V(T)\mid v\in B_{t}\}
is not empty and the graph induced by
T
on this set is connected.

Each graph
\Gamma
has the trivial tree decomposition where the tree has just one vertex
t
and
B_{t}=V(\Gamma)
. The
width
of a tree decomposition of a finite graph is the number
\max\{|B_{t}|-1\mid t\in V(T)\}
, and the
treewidth

\tw(\Gamma)
of a finite graph
\Gamma
is the smallest width of its tree decompositions. The treewidth of a graph measures how treelike a graph is. For example, the treewidth of a non-empty tree is one and the treewidth of a graph on
n
vertices is at most
n-1
with equality if and only if the graph is complete. There is a vast literature on the treewidth of graphs, see
[
2
,
5
,
6
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
14
]
for some recent ones, and there are applications. A famous one is by Robertson and Seymour
[
13
]
when they proved that the treewidth of a minor of a graph cannot exceed the treewidth of the graph.

Let
q
be a prime power and let
F_{q}
be the finite field of order
q
. For integers
n,k,t
with
k>t\geq 1
and
n>2k-t
, the
q
-Kneser graph

K_{q}(n,k,t)
is the graph whose vertices are the subspaces of dimension
k
of the vector space
F_{q}^{n}
with two vertices
K
and
K^{\prime}
adjacent if and only if
\dim(K\cap K^{\prime})<t
. The condition
n>2k-t
ensures that the graph is non-empty.
Considering the vertices of
K_{q}(n,k,t)
as subspaces of the vector space dual to
F_{q}^{n}
, one shows that the graphs
K_{q}(n,k,t)
and
K_{q}(n,n-k,n-2k+t)
are isomorphic. It is therefore sufficient to consider Kneser graphs
K_{q}(n,k,t)
with
n\geq 2k
. Notice that we do not allow
t=k
, which would yields only complete graphs.
In 2021 Cao, Liu, Lu and Lv proved the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let
q
be the order of a finite field. For integers
n,k,t
with
k>t\geq 1
and

\displaystyle n\geq 2t(k-t+1)+k+1

(1)

we have
\tw(K_{q}(n,k,t))={n\brack k}_{q}-{n-t\brack k-t}_{q}-1
.

In this result the Gaussian coefficient
{n\brack k}_{q}
is defined for integers
n\geq k\geq 0
and
q\geq 2
as follows.

\displaystyle{n\brack k}_{q}:=\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}\frac{q^{n-i}-1}{q^{i}-1}.

(2)

The hard part of the theorem is to prove the lower bound for the treewidth. For the upper bound, the authors of
[
1
]
proved in fact that
\tw(K_{q}(n,k,t))\leq{n\brack k}_{q}-{n-t\brack k-t}_{q}-1
holds whenever
k>t\geq 1
and

. In this paper we give a shorter proof for this upper bound and we will weaken the required condition (
1
) for the lower bound substantially. Our main result is the following. To formulate it, we define the map
\epsilon
from the set of all primepowers to the set
\{0,1,2,3,4\}
by
\epsilon(2)=9
,
\epsilon(3)=3
,
\epsilon(4)=2

Theorem 1.2
.

Consider a
q
-Kneser graph

and

. Suppose that one of the following condition is satisfied.

The strongest result is obtained for

-Kneser graph

Corollary 1.4

Let

An upper bound for the treewidth

For every finite graph
\Gamma
its maximum vertex degree is denoted by
\Delta(\Gamma)
and is called its
maximum degree
. The cardinality of a largest independent set of
\Gamma
is denoted by
\alpha(\Gamma)
and is called the
independence number
of the graph. The following connection between treewidth, maximum degree and independence number is known.

Result 2.1

(
[
4
]
)
.

For every finite graph
\Gamma
we have

\displaystyle\tw(\Gamma)\leq\max\{\Delta(\Gamma),|V(\Gamma)|-\alpha(\Gamma)-1\}.

(3)

For generalized
q
-Kneser graphs we have
\Delta(\Gamma)\leq|V(\Gamma)|-\alpha(\Gamma)-1
as was shown for
n\geq 2k
in
[
1
]
. We give a simpler proof for this inequality. In order to do so, we need the following result of Wilson on the independence number of generalized Kneser graphs.

Result 2.2

(
[
3
]
)
.

For integers
k>t\geq 1
and
n>2k-t
we have

\displaystyle\alpha(K_{q}(n,k,t))=max\{{n-t\brack k-t}_{q},{2k-t\brack k-t}_{q}\}.

Lemma 2.3
.

For Kneser graphs
K_{q}(n,k,t)
with
n>2k-t
and
k>t\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle\Delta(K_{q}(n,k,t))\leq{n\brack k}_{q}-\alpha(K_{q}(n,k,t))-1.

Proof.

Since
K_{q}(n,k,t)
and
K_{q}(n,n-k,n-2k+t)
are isomorphic graphs, we may assume that
n\geq 2k
. The graph
\Gamma:=K_{q}(n,k,t)
has
{n\brack k}_{q}
vertices and is regular. Given a vertex
A
, then
r:={n\brack k}_{q}-\Delta(\Gamma)
is the number of vertices of the graph that are not adjacent to it. Considering
A
as a
k
-subspace of
F_{q}^{n}
, then
r
is the number of
k
-subspaces meeting
A
in a subspace of dimension at least
t
. We have to show that this number is strictly larger than
\alpha(\Gamma)
.

From
2.2
we have
\alpha(\Gamma)={n-t\brack k-t}_{q}
. Let
T_{0}
be a
t
-subspace of
A
. Then
T
is contained in
{n-t\brack k-t}_{q}
k
-subspaces. Let
T
be a second
t
-subspace of
A
. Notice that
T
exists as
t<k
. Then there exist
k
-subspaces meeting
A
exactly in
T
. This shows that
r>{n-t\brack k-t}_{q}=\alpha(\Gamma)
as desired.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

For Kneser graphs
K_{q}(n,k,t)
with
n>2k-t
and

\displaystyle\tw(K_{q}(n,k,t))\leq{n\brack k}_{q}-\alpha(K_{q}(n,k,t))-1.

As mentioned before, it was proved in

that this upper bound is sharp when

and

[

]
and

uses a result of Robertson and Seymour on separators. We use the slightly different result (2.5) from the same paper and modify it in the following lemma. Recall that a component of a graph is a maximal connected induced subgraph of
\Gamma
. Hence, every vertex of the graph is a vertex of exactly one component.

Lemma 2.5

(based on
[
13
]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite graph. Then there exists a tree representation
(T,B)
with

with width
\tw(\Gamma)
and the following properties.

(a)

If

are adjacent in

, then
B_{t}
is not a subset of
B_{t^{\prime}}
.

(b)

For some
t\in T
, there exists a subset
P
of
B_{t}
such that
|P|\leq\tw(\Gamma)+1
and such that every component of
\Gamma\setminus P
has at most
\frac{1}{2}|V(\Gamma)\setminus P|

Proof.

Let
(T,B)
with
B=(B_{t}\mid t\in V(T))
be a tree decomposition of
\Gamma
with width
\tw(\Gamma)
. If there exist adjacent vertices

of

with

, let

be the graph obtained from

is a tree decomposition of

and one vertex less than

Claim (2.5) in

]
states that there exists a subset

of

such that every component of

A lower bound for the treewidth

Lemma 3.1
.

Consider integers
n,k,q
with
n\geq k\geq 0
and
q\geq 2
. Then

\displaystyle{n\brack k}_{q}\leq(q+\beta)q^{k(n-k)-1}.

where
\beta=5
for
q=2
, and
\beta=3
for
q=3
and
\beta=2
for
q\geq 4
. Also, if
0<k<n
, then
(q+1)q^{k(n-k)-1}\leq{n\brack k}_{q}
.

Proof.

We first prove the lower bound for the Gaussian coefficient for
0<k<n
by induction on
k
. For
k=1
we have

{n\brack k}_{q}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}q^{i}\geq(q+1)q^{n-2}

as required. For
k\geq 2
, we have

{n\brack k}_{q}=\frac{q^{n}-1}{q^{k}-1}{n-1\brack k-1}_{q}\geq q^{n-k}{n-1\brack k-1}_{q}

and the induction hypothesis shows that this is at least
(q+1)q^{k(n-k)-1}
.

For
q\geq 3
, the upper bound was proved in
[
8
, Lemma 34]
. Now consider
q=2
. We have to prove the upper bound, which we do using the technique of
[
8
, Lemma 34]
. From the definition of the Gaussian coefficient, we have

\displaystyle{n\brack k}_{q}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{q^{n-k+i}-1}{q^{i}-1}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{q^{n-k+i}}{q^{i}-1}=q^{k(n-k)}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{q^{i}}{q^{i}-1}.

(4)

It suffices therefore to show that
\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{q^{i}}{q^{i}-1}\leq 1+\frac{\beta}{q}=\frac{7}{2}
. For
1\leq k\leq 5
, one verifies this by hand. For
k\geq 6
, one proves easily by induction on
k
the stronger statement
\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{q^{i}}{q^{i}-1}\leq 1+5\cdot\frac{q^{k-1}-2}{q^{k}-2}
.
∎

.

Let

and

be two subspaces of dimension

of an

-vector space and let

Then for every integer

of

\displaystyle{s\brack i}_{q}{k-i\brack t-i}_{q}^{2}.

For any such pair, the intersection of

is a subspace of the

-subspace

. Now

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
f
be a real quadratic polynomial with leading coefficient
-1
, and let
f
obtain its maximum for

. Let

and

be integers and suppose that

.

(a)

If

, then
\sum_{i=a}^{\infty}q^{f(i)}<q^{f(a)}(1+\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{3}})
.

(b)

If

, then
\sum_{i=-\infty}^{a}q^{f(i)}<q^{f(a)}(1+\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{3}})
.

(c)

If

\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}q^{f(i)}<q^{f(x_{0})}(1+\frac{2}{q}+\frac{2}{q^{3}})
.

Proof.

We use several times that
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}q^{-i}=q/(q-1)\leq q
.

(a) Since

, then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=a}^{\infty}q^{f(i)}\leq q^{f(a)}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}q^{-i^{2}}<q^{f(a)}\left(1+\frac{1}{q}+q^{-4}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}q^{-i}\right)\leq q^{f(a)}\left(1+\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{3}}\right).

.

(c) If

. Now consider the case when

, then

and

. Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}}q^{f(z)}

\displaystyle\leq 2q^{f(x_{0})-\frac{1}{4}}(1+q^{-2}+q^{-6}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}q^{-i})

\displaystyle\leq 2q^{f(x_{0})-\frac{1}{4}}(1+q^{-2}+q^{-5})

\displaystyle<q^{f(x_{0})}(1+\frac{2}{q}+\frac{2}{q^{3}})

\displaystyle\sum_{i=i_{\max}}^{t-1}q^{(t-i)(i+3k-2t-n)-i}.

Defining the quadratic function

The Kneser graph

The graph

is the complement of a Grassmann graph. This graph attracted special attention in

where it was shown for all

that the graph

remained unsolved in
[

and also our counting argument from the last section is not strong enough to cover this case. We will choose a special model for this graph and use geometric properties to determine its treewidth. The case

will lead to extra difficulties, whereas the case

only requires some basic knowledge of tree decompositions.

Using projective geometry, the graph

can be understood as the graph whose vertices are the lines of the projective space

with two vertices adjacent if and only if the lines are skew. Using the Klein-correspondence from

to the hyperbolic quadric

, we can define the same graph as follows.
Its vertices are the points of
Q^{+}(5,q)
and two vertices are non-adjacent if and only if the lie on a line of
Q^{+}(5,q)
. We consider

naturally embedded in
\PG(5,q)
and denote the related polarity of
\PG(5,q)
by

. Then two points

and

of

are adjacent as vertices of the graph if and only if the points are not perpendicular, that is
v^{\prime}
is not a point of the tangent hyperplane
v^{\perp}
at

. See
[
7
]
for properties of the hyperbolic quadric and the fact that this graph is indeed isomorphic to

. Using this model we will show that
\tw(\Gamma)=|V(\Gamma)|-\alpha(\Gamma)-1
. We need two lemmata of preparation, the first one is an easy observation of the hyperbolic quadric

, which occurs in

as an intersection of
Q^{+}(5,q)
with
\ell^{\perp}
, where

is any secant line to the quadric.

Lemma 4.1
.

The largest sets of points of the hyperbolic quadric
Q^{+}(3,q)
without three pairwise non-collinear points are the unions of two skew lines with
2q+2
points.

Proof.

Notice that the hyperbolic quadric

is a
(q+1)\times(q+1)
-grid, so it can be described as follows: Its points are
(i,j)
for

and its lines are
\{(i,j)\mid 0\leq i\leq q\}
and
\{(j,i)\mid 0\leq j\leq q\}
for

. The assertion is easily proved from this.
∎

The next lemma collects some properties of tree decompositions.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

with

be a tree decomposition of a finite graph
\Gamma
. Let
t
be a vertex of
T
. Then we have the following.

(a)

If
t\in V(T)
and

, then

has exactly one component such that
v\in B_{s}
for some vertex
s
of this component. We denote the vertex set of this component by
T_{t}(v)
and we denote by
\Gamma_{t}(v)
the union of the sets

with
s\in T_{t}(v)
.

(b)

For adjacent vertices

of
\Gamma
with

, we have

.

(c)

For adjacent vertices

of

.

(d)

If

, then

contains every vertex of

and every vertex of
\Gamma
that has a neighbor in

Part (a) follows from the second property of the definition of a tree decomposition. Part (b) then follows from the fact that the edge

of

is contained in

for some vertex

of

.
∎

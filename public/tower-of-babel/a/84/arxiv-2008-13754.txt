Width, Largeness and Index Theory This paper is a contribution to the Special Issue on Scalar and Ricci Curvature in honor of Misha Gromov on his 75th Birthday. The full collection is available at https://www.emis.de/journals/SIGMA/Gromov.html
Rudolf ZEIDLER

Abstract
In this note, we review some recent developments related to metric aspects of scalar curvature from the point of view of index theory for Dirac operators. In particular, we revisit index-theoretic approaches to a conjecture of Gromov on the width of Riemannian bands M × [ − 1 , 1 ] M\times[-1,1] , and on a conjecture of Rosenberg and Stolz on the non-existence of complete positive scalar curvature metrics on M × ℝ M\times\mathbb{R} . We show that there is a more general geometric statement underlying both of them implying a quantitative negative upper bound on the infimum of the scalar curvature of a complete metric on M × ℝ M\times\mathbb{R} if the scalar curvature is positive in some neighborhood. We study ( A ^ \hat{A} -)iso-enlargeable spin manifolds and related notions of width for Riemannian manifolds from an index-theoretic point of view. Finally, we list some open problems arising in the interplay between index theory, largeness properties and width.

Introduction

Gromov proposed studying the geometry of scalar curvature via various metric inequalities which have similarities to classical Riemannian comparison geometry, see in particular
[
12
,
13
]
.
One instance of such an inequality is the following estimate on the widths of Riemannian bands.

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
12
, Section 11.12, Conjecture C]
)
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold of dimension
n-1\neq 4
such that
M
does not admit a metric of positive scalar curvature.
Then every Riemannian metric
g
on
V=M\times[-1,1]
of scalar curvature bounded below by
n(n-1)=\scal_{\mathrm{S}^{n}}
satisfies

\width(V,g)\coloneqq\dist_{g}(\partial_{-}V,\partial_{+}V)\leq\frac{2\pi}{n},

where
\partial_{\pm}V=M\times\{\pm 1\}
.

Rosenberg and Stolz previously proposed a seemingly related conjecture, see
[
33
, Section 7]
:

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold of dimension
\neq 4
such that
M
does not admit a metric of positive scalar curvature.
Then
M\times\mathbb{R}
does not admit a complete metric of positive scalar curvature.

While these two conjectures appear similar, there is no direct formal implication between them.
Conjecture
1.1
only implies that
M\times\mathbb{R}
does not admit a complete metric of
uniformly
positive scalar curvature, a weaker conclusion than what is desired by Conjecture
1.2
.

Remark 1.3
.

We have to exclude dimension
4
because even this weaker conclusion is known to fail in this case: Using Seiberg–Witten invariants, it is possible to show that there exists a simply connected spin
4
-manifold such that
M
does not admit positive scalar curvature but
M\times\mathrm{S}^{1}
does.
To this end, one can take a simply connected
4
-manifold of vanishing
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-genus which does not admit a metric of positive scalar curvature,
[
35
, Counterexample 4.16]
,
[
44
]
.
But if
M\times\mathrm{S}^{1}
admits positive scalar curvature, then
M\times\mathbb{R}
admits a complete metric of uniformly positive scalar curvature.

The ideas behind these conjectures have their roots in work of Gromov and Lawson
[
14
,
15
]
, where they studied various notions of
largeness
for Riemannian manifolds, most notably
enlargeability
, which serve as obstructions to the existence of positive scalar curvature metrics.
While these conditions are of purely geometric topological nature, the original proof that these are obstructions to positive scalar curvature rely on the spin condition and (relative) index theory of spinor Dirac operators.

, see

.
Gromov recently generalized enlargeability based on results related to Conjecture

Initially, it appeared that Conjecture
1.1
and iso-enlargeability
cannot
be understood using index theory of the Dirac operator because the latter does not naturally lend itself to the study non-complete manifolds.
Instead, in
[
12
]
these ideas were approached via the minimal hypersurface technique of Schoen and Yau
[
40
]
.
On the other hand, as mentioned above, Conjecture
1.2
was already known to be accessible to index-theoretic methods.
Cecchini
[
4
]
strengthened this link by showing that Conjecture
1.2
holds whenever
M
is spin and has non-vanishing
Rosenberg index
(see Section
2
for a survey of the Rosenberg index).
Furthermore, it turned out that Conjecture
1.1
can also be addressed using index theory of Dirac operators.
Indeed, it was shown by the author
[
46
]
that a version of Conjecture
1.1
with a weaker upper bound holds if
M
has non-vanishing Rosenberg index.
Recently, Cecchini
[
5
]
, along with addressing several other questions in Gromov’s metric inequalities programme, improved this estimate by showing that Conjecture
1.1
holds as stated if

has non-vanishing Rosenberg index.

The purpose of this note is threefold.

Firstly
, we revisit the direct index-theoretic approaches to Conjectures
1.1
and
1.2
via the Rosenberg index.
This was prompted by observing that both cases are based on the same index-theoretic technique, namely an index theorem for certain
Callias-type
operators, suggesting that there should be a more general geometric statement underlying both.
This is achieved in Theorem
1.4
below.
We show that in the presence of a hypersurface
M\subset X
with an index-theoretic obstruction, positive scalar curvature
\geq\sigma>0
in a neighborhood
\{p\in X\mid d(p,M)\leq d/2\}
of width
d
forces the scalar curvature somewhere else to be negative in a quantifiable way depending on
d
and
\sigma
.
We formulate this for the normalized case
\sigma=n(n-1)=\scal_{\mathrm{S}^{n}}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X
be a spin
n
-manifold without boundary and
M\subseteq X
a closed codimension one submanifold with trivial normal bundle such that the induced map
\pi_{1}M\to\pi_{1}X
is injective.
Suppose that the Rosenberg index
\alpha(M)\in\KO_{n-1}(\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\pi_{1}M)
does not vanish.

Let
g
be a complete Riemannian metric on
X
which has scalar curvature bounded below by
n(n-1)
in a neighborhood of
M
of width
d<2\pi/n
.
Then

\inf_{p\in X}\scal_{g}(p)\leq-n(n-1)\tan\left(\frac{nd}{4}\right)^{2}.

(1.1)

For manifolds of non-vanishing Rosenberg index, this immediately implies Conjecture
1.2
and furthermore, since the term on the right-hand side of (
1.1
) tends to
-\infty
as
d\to 2\pi/n
, it also implies Conjecture
1.1
.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
M
be a closed spin manifold of non-vanishing Rosenberg index
\alpha(M)\neq 0\in\KO_{n-1}(\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\pi_{1}M)
.
Then the following holds:

•

M\times\mathbb{R}
does not admit a complete metric of positive scalar curvature,

•

Every Riemannian metric
g
on
V=M\times[-1,1]
of scalar curvature
\geq n(n-1)
satisfies

\width(V,g)<\frac{2\pi}{n}.

Note that the latter is even slightly stronger than the desired conclusion of Conjecture
1.1
because we obtain a strict inequality.

Secondly
, we exhibit an index-theoretic point of view towards Gromov’s
iso-enlargeability
for spin manifolds.
This was essentially already implicit in
[
46
]
, where the related notion of
infinite
\mathcal{KO}
-width
was introduced (see Section
5
).
Here we make this explicit and study a slight generalization of iso-enlargeability, namely
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-iso-enlargeability
(following Gromov and Lawson’s
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-enlargeability, see Section
4
), and relate it to infinite
\mathcal{KO}
-width.
This leads to the following theorem (see Section
6
).

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
M
be a closed spin manifold which is
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-iso-enlargeable.
Then
M
does not admit a metric of positive scalar curvature.

Finally
, we survey several open questions suggested by these recent developments connecting index theory to metric and largeness aspects of scalar curvature (see Sections
4
and
7
).

2
The Rosenberg index

In this section, we provide a brief survey of the aspects of the Rosenberg index relevant to our statements and open problems.
More material on these matters can be found in the original articles of Rosenberg
[
30
,
31
,
32
]
and in
[
35
,
38
,
43
,
41
]
.

The starting point is that the spinor Dirac operator on a spin manifold is closely related to the scalar curvature via the Schrödinger–Lichnerowicz formula
\not{\mathfrak{D}}^{2}=\nabla^{\ast}\nabla+\frac{\scal}{4}
.
Together with the Atiyah–Singer index theorem, this was first used by Lichnerowicz
[
26
]
to show that the
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-genus is an obstruction to the existence of a positive scalar curvature metric on a closed spin manifold
M
.
Later, this connection between the Dirac operator and scalar curvature was exploited further using variations of the index invariant.
For closed spin manifolds, the most sophisticated such invariant is the
Rosenberg index

\alpha(M)\in\KO_{n}(\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\pi_{1}M)
which takes values in the (real) K-theory of the group
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\!
-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\pi_{1}M
.
Notable examples of manifolds with non-vanishing Rosenberg index (but which in general do not have non-vanishing
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-genus) include (area-)enlargeable spin manifolds
[
16
,
18
,
19
]
.

We will now describe two (equivalent) points of view towards the Rosenberg index.
The first is as the index of the spinor Dirac operator of
M
twisted by the “universal flat bundle” on
M
.
The latter is the
Miščenko bundle

\mathcal{L}_{M}\to M
which is the flat bundle of finitely generated projective Hilbert-
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\Gamma
-modules associated to the representation of
\Gamma\coloneqq\pi_{1}M
on
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\Gamma
by left-multiplication.
Index theory extends to the situation of elliptic operators with coefficients in
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\!
-algebras yielding index invariants in corresponding operator K-theory groups.
This goes back to Miščenko and Fomenko
[
27
]
; for more recent expositions of the technical background relevant to our situation we refer to
[
9
,
17
]
.
The Rosenberg index
\alpha(M)\in\KO_{n}(\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\pi_{1}M)
is then simply the (K-theoretic) index of the twisted Dirac operator on
\mathfrak{S}_{M}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{M}
using the flat connection induced on the Miščenko bundle
\mathcal{L}_{M}
, and
\mathfrak{S}_{M}
is the spinor bundle.
The Schrödinger–Lichnerowicz formula extends to this situation so that the non-vanishing of
\alpha(M)\in\KO_{n}(\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\Gamma)
is still an obstruction to the existence of a positive scalar curvature metric on
M
.

The second point of view is through the lens of the strong Novikov and Baum–Connes conjectures.
The (real) Baum–Connes conjecture features the assembly map

\mu\colon\ \KO_{\ast}^{\Gamma}(\underline{\mathrm{E}}\Gamma)\to\KO_{\ast}(\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\Gamma),

where the left-hand side is the equivariant KO-homology of the classifying space for proper actions.
Note that there is a canonical map
\iota_{\ast}\colon\KO_{\ast}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma)=\KO_{\ast}^{\Gamma}(\mathrm{E}\Gamma)\to\KO_{\ast}^{\Gamma}(\underline{\mathrm{E}}\Gamma)
, where
\mathrm{B}\Gamma
is the usual classifying space of
\Gamma
and
\mathrm{E}\Gamma
the classifying space for free actions.
The map
\iota_{\ast}
is always rationally injective.
Moreover, it is an isomorphism iff
\Gamma
is torsion-free.
The
Novikov assembly map
is the composition

\nu=\mu\circ\iota_{\ast}\colon\ \KO_{\ast}^{\Gamma}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma)\to\KO_{\ast}(\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\Gamma).

The
strong Novikov conjecture
asserts that
\nu
is rationally injective.
If
M
is a spin manifold, then we have the KO-fundamental class
[M]_{\KO}\in\KO_{n}(M)
.
Suppose that we have a map
f\colon M\to\mathrm{B}\Gamma
, then
\nu(f_{\ast}[M]_{\KO})\in\KO_{n}(\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\Gamma)
equals the index of the Dirac operator of
M
twisted by
f^{\ast}\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma}
, where
\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma}\to\mathrm{B}\Gamma
is the Miščenko-bundle.
In particular, the Rosenberg index is given as

\alpha(M)=\nu(c_{\ast}[M]_{\KO})\in\KO_{n}(\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\pi_{1}M),

where
c\colon M\to\mathrm{B}\pi_{1}M
is the classifying map of the fundamental group.
Together with the strong Novikov conjecture, this reduces the study of the Rosenberg index to computations in (KO-)homology.
If we are only interested in rational information, we can apply the
Pontryagin character
to reduce this further to ordinary (co)homology.
The (homological) Pontryagin character is the composition of the (homological) Chern character with complexification and yields an isomorphism

\ph\colon\ \KO_{n}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Q}\xrightarrow{\cong}\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\HZ_{n+4k}(X;\mathbb{Q}).

Applied to the KO-fundamental class this yields
\ph([M]_{\KO})=[M]\cap\hat{\mathbf{A}}(M)
,
where
[M]\in\HZ_{n}(M)
is the ordinary fundamental class and
\hat{\mathbf{A}}(M)\in\HZ^{4\ast}(M;\mathbb{Q})
the total
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-class of
M
.
Given any map
f\colon M\to\mathrm{B}\Gamma
and an ordinary cohomology class
\theta\in\HZ^{k}(\mathrm{B}\Gamma;\mathbb{Q})
, we have

\langle\ph(f_{\ast}[M]_{\KO}),\theta\rangle=\big\langle[M]\cap\hat{\mathbf{A}}(M),f^{\ast}\theta\big\rangle=\big\langle[M],\hat{\mathbf{A}}(M)\cup f^{\ast}\theta\big\rangle,

(2.1)

where a number as on the right-hand side is called a
higher
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-genus
of
M
.
The strong Novikov conjecture thus implies that the non-vanishing of any higher
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-genus is an obstruction to positive scalar curvature.

In the case of a trivial fundamental group, the Rosenberg index reduces to Hitchin’s
\alpha
-invariant
[
21
]
.
Stolz
[
42
]
proved that a simply connected spin manifold of dimension
\geq 5
admits a positive scalar curvature metric if and only if its
\alpha
-invariant vanishes.
Note that dimensions
1,2\mod 8
this is a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-valued invariant, so here it is important to consider the full integral information.
More generally, one might expect that the analogous statement holds for not necessarily simply connected manifolds using the (integral) Rosenberg index.
This is the content of the (unstable) Gromov–Lawson–Rosenberg conjecture.
Unfortunately, this conjecture is false in general
[
36
]
.
However, in its stead, one can consider a
stable
version of the Gromov–Lawson–Rosenberg conjecture as introduced by Rosenberg and Stolz
[
34
]
.
The stable conjecture is motivated by the observation that the receptacle of the Rosenberg index is
8
-periodic due to Bott periodicity, whereas the geometric problem concerning the existence of positive scalar curvature metrics does not feature such a periodicity in an obvious way.
To remedy this mismatch, one stabilizes the positive scalar curvature existence problem:
To this end, let
B
be a
Bott manifold
, that is, a simply connected spin
8
-manifold such that
\hat{\mathrm{A}}(B)=1
.
Note that
B
can be chosen to be endowed with a Ricci flat metric
[
22
]
.
Moreover, using Bott periodicity,
\alpha(M\times B^{k})=\alpha(M)\in\KO_{n}(\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\Gamma)
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
We say that a manifold
stably admits a positive scalar curvature metric
if there exists some
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
M\times B^{k}
admits a positive scalar curvature metric.

Conjecture 2.1

(stable GLR conjecture)
.

A spin manifold stably admits a positive scalar curvature metric if and only if
\alpha(M)=0\in\KO_{n}(\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\Gamma)
.

Stolz proved that the stable conjecture holds whenever the (real version of the) Baum–Connes assembly map is injective:

Theorem 2.2

(Stolz
[
43
, Theorem 3.10]
)
.

The stable GLR conjecture holds if the Baum–Connes assembly map
\mu\colon\KO_{\ast}^{\Gamma}(\underline{\mathrm{E}}\Gamma)\to\KO_{\ast}(\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\Gamma)
is injective.

This implies that there is a large scope of cases, where the stable GLR conjecture is known to hold, and that any counterexample would also be a counterexample to the Baum–Connes conjecture.
In a similar vein, Schick proposed a meta-conjecture
[
38
, Conjecture 1.5]
asserting that every positive scalar curvature obstruction “based on index theory of Dirac operators” can be obtained from the Rosenberg index.

We conclude this section with the discussion of two subtle technicalities we have ignored so far.

Remark 2.3

(maximal versus reduced)
.

The group
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\!
-algebras
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\Gamma
comes in (at least) two flavours, depending on which completion of the group ring is used.
We have the
reduced
group
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\!
-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{red}}\Gamma
(which is the completion induced by the left-regular representation of
\Gamma
) and the
maximal
group
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\!
-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{max}}\Gamma
(which is the universal representation of
\Gamma
).
In the discussion above, we have not stressed which one we use because for our purposes we can usually work with either one of them.
First of all, there is a canonical quotient map
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{max}}\Gamma\to\mathrm{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{red}}\Gamma
which means that the maximal
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\!
-algebra a priori could potentially capture more obstructions.
Moreover, viewing the Miščenko bundle as the “universal flat bundle”, the maximal
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\!
-algebra is the more natural choice because any unitary representation of
\Gamma
factors through
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{max}}\Gamma
.
On the other hand, the Baum–Connes conjecture is usually formulated and studied for the reduced group
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\!
-algebra (because surjectivity definitely fails in the maximal case).
Fortunately, injectivity of the Baum–Connes assembly map for the reduced case implies injectivity in the maximal case and so the distinction becomes less relevant for our purposes even when we view the Rosenberg index through this lens.

Remark 2.4

(real versus complex)
.

In the literature on the Baum–Connes conjecture, usually the case of complex K-theory and -homology is treated, whereas for the purposes of spin geometry and scalar curvature, real K-theory and -homology is the more appropriate choice.
Fortunately, whether or not the Baum–Connes assembly map is an isomorphism does not depend on this choice,
and after inverting
2
, this is even true for injectivity and surjectivity separately
[
1
,
37
]
.
In particular, for the strong Novikov conjecture as formulated here the difference does not matter.

3
Callias-type operators and codimension one obstructions

In this section, we will provide a proof of Theorem
1.4
.
This is a simplified synthesis of the previous approaches from
[
4
]
,
[
5
, Section 6]
and
[
46
, Section 2]
.
We rely on the same index-theoretic setup which has its roots in work of Higson
[
20
]
and Bunke
[
3
]
.

In this section, we fix an arbitrary unital Real
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\!
-algebra
A
.
For the purposes of the main theorem of this note, one can keep the case
A=\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\Gamma
with
\Gamma
some discrete group in mind.

Let
W
be a complete spin
n
-manifold together with a proper smooth
1
-Lipschitz function
x\colon W\to\mathbb{R}
.
Fix some regular value
a\in\mathbb{R}
of
x
and set
M\coloneqq x^{-1}(a)
.
Suppose furthermore that
W
is endowed with a smooth bundle
E\to W
of finitely generated projective Hilbert-
A
-modules furnished with a metric connection.
Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be some non-decreasing smooth function satisfying
f(0)=0
and such that
f^{2}-f^{\prime}
is bounded below by a positive constant outside a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}
.
In particular, the latter property includes the following two special cases:

(i)

the function
f
is proper and Lipschitz,

(ii)

or there exists
R>0
such that
f(x)=\lambda_{-}
for all
x\leq-R
and
f(x)=\lambda_{+}
for all
x\geq R
, where
\lambda_{-}<0<\lambda_{+}
are constants.

Denote the
\mathrm{Cl}_{n}
-linear spinor bundle of
W
by
\mathfrak{S}_{W}
and let
\not{\mathfrak{D}}_{W,E}
be the Dirac operator twisted by
E
.
We then consider the operator

B=\not{\mathfrak{D}}_{W,E}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}1+f(x)\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\epsilon

acting on compactly supported smooth sections of
\mathfrak{S}_{W}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}E\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\mathrm{Cl}_{0,1}
.
Then
B
is an essentially self-adjoint regular Fredholm operator.
It has an index
\ind(B)\in\KO_{n-1}(A)
which satisfies

\ind(B)=\ind(\not{\mathfrak{D}}_{M,E|_{M}})\in\KO_{n-1}(A).

Moreover, we have the formula

B^{2}=\not{\mathfrak{D}}^{2}_{W,E}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}1+f^{\prime}(x)\clm(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x)\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\epsilon+f(x)^{2}\geq\not{\mathfrak{D}}^{2}_{W,E}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}1+f(x)^{2}-f^{\prime}(x),

(3.1)

where
\clm
denotes the Clifford action and we have used
\|\mathrm{d}x\|\leq 1
and
f^{\prime}\geq 0
.
For all of this we refer, e.g., to
[
5
, Section 6]
,
[
46
, Appendix A]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

In the above setup, suppose that
\ind(\not{\mathfrak{D}}_{M,E|_{M}})\neq 0\in\KO_{n-1}(A)
.
Let
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an interval of length
d<2\pi/n
such that the scalar curvature of
W
is bounded below by
n(n-1)
on the subset
x^{-1}(I)
.
Then

\inf_{p\in W}\scal(p)\leq-n(n-1)\tan\left(\frac{nd}{4}\right)^{2}.

Proof.

We can assume without loss of generality that
I=[-d/2,d/2]
.
Then suppose, by contraposition, that there exists
\sigma<\tan\big(\frac{nd}{4}\big)^{2}
such that
\scal(p)\geq-n(n-1)\sigma
for all
p\in W
.
Choose
0<\tilde{d}<d
such that
\sigma<\tan\big(\frac{n\tilde{d}}{4}\big)^{2}<\tan\big(\frac{nd}{4}\big)^{2}
and
0<r<n/2
such that

\frac{n^{2}\sigma}{4}<r^{2}\tan\big(r\tilde{d}/2\big)^{2}.

(3.2)

Choose a smooth function
\xi\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
which satisfies
0\leq\xi(x,y)\leq y^{2}+r^{2}
for all
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\xi(x,y)=y^{2}+r^{2}
for
|x|\leq\tilde{d}/2
and
\xi(x,y)=0
for
|x|\geq d/2
.
Let
f
be the unique maximal solution of the initial value problem

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(x)=\xi(x,f(x)),

\displaystyle f(0)=0.

By construction,
f
is a smooth non-decreasing function defined on some interval containing
0
.
We first claim that
f
is defined on the entire real line.
To this end, consider the auxilliary function
\bar{f}\colon(-\pi/(2r),\pi/(2r))\to\mathbb{R}
,
\bar{f}(x)=r\tan(rx)
.
Note that this is the unique solution of
\bar{f}^{\prime}(x)=\bar{f}(x)^{2}+r^{2}
,
\bar{f}(0)=0
and that
\pi/(2r)>\pi/n>d/2
.
By the choice of
\xi
,
f^{\prime}(x)\leq f(x)^{2}+r^{2}
which implies that
|f|\leq|\bar{f}|
as long as both are defined.
This implies that
f
exists at least for all
|x|\leq d/2
.
But
\xi(x,y)=0
for
|x|\geq d/2
, so
f
must continue for all times and remain constant on each component of
\mathbb{R}\setminus(-d/2,d/2)
.
Moreover, by monotonicity and because
f(x)=\bar{f}(x)
for
|x|\leq\tilde{d}/2
, we obtain the estimate

|f(x)|\geq|f(\pm\tilde{d}/2)|=\bar{f}(\tilde{d}/2)=r\tan\big(r\tilde{d}/2\big)\qquad\text{for all $|x|\geq d/2$.}

(3.3)

Now we consider the operator

B=\not{\mathfrak{D}}\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}1+f(x)\mathbin{\widehat{\otimes}}\epsilon

as above, where
\not{\mathfrak{D}}=\not{\mathfrak{D}}_{W,E}
.
Then using (
3.1
) together with the Schrödinger–Lichnerowicz formula and the Friedrich estimate (compare
[
5
, Section 3.2]
), we obtain

\displaystyle B^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\not{\mathfrak{D}}^{2}\otimes 1+f(x)^{2}-f^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle\geq{\frac{n\scal}{4(n-1)}+f(x)^{2}-f^{\prime}(x)}\ \eqqcolon\Psi.

We denote the latter function by
\Psi\colon W\to\mathbb{R}
and will show that it is bounded below by a positive constant to finish the proof.
By construction of
f
, we always have
f(x)^{2}-f^{\prime}(x)\geq-r^{2}
.
For
|x|\leq d/2
, this implies together with the scalar curvature bound on
x^{-1}(I)
that

\Psi(p)\geq\frac{n^{2}}{4}-r^{2}>0\qquad\text{if $|x(p)|\leq d/2$.}

Moreover, for
|x|\geq d/2
, we have
f^{\prime}(x)=0
and so (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) imply

\displaystyle\Psi(p)\geq-\frac{n^{2}\sigma}{4}+f(x(p))^{2}\geq-\frac{n^{2}\sigma}{4}+r^{2}\tan\big(r\tilde{d}/2\big)^{2}>0\qquad\text{if $|x(p)|\geq d/2$.}

This proves that
B^{2}\geq\inf\limits_{p\in W}\Psi(p)>0
and hence
\ind(\not{\mathfrak{D}}_{M,E|_{M}})=\ind(B)=0
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The argument implies the following more general observation:
If
\ind(\not{\mathfrak{D}}_{M,E|_{M}})
does not vanish, then it follows that

\inf_{p\in W}\left(\frac{n\scal(p)}{4(n-1)}+f(x(p))^{2}-f^{\prime}(x(p))\right)\leq 0

for any such smooth function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
allowed in this setup.
By choosing different functions one can thereby obtain various pointwise constraints on how scalar curvature can be distributed along
W
.

Note that this implies Theorem
1.4
from the introduction by taking
W
to be the connected covering of
X
such that
\pi_{1}W=\pi_{1}M
, the bundle
E=\mathcal{L}_{W}
to be the Miščenko-bundle, and the function
x
to be (a smooth approximation of) the signed distance function to
M\subseteq W
.

Rescaling the scalar curvature lower bound on the tubular neighborhood implies the first part of Corollary
1.5
.
How the second part of Corollary
1.5
follows from this theorem will be explained in Corollary

below.

In this section, we review various notions of largeness for Riemannian manifolds following Gromov and Lawson

For a textbook treatment see also

We need the notion of the (
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-)mapping degree in different setups.
To this end, let
f\colon X\to Y
be a smooth map between oriented manifolds (possibly with boundary) and assume that we are in one of the two following situations:

(i)

the map
f\colon X\to Y
is proper and takes
\partial X
to
\partial Y
,

(ii)

or
Y
is a closed manifold with a fixed base-point
\ast\in Y
and
f
is
constant at infinity
, that is, it maps both
\partial X
and the complement of a compact subset to the base-point
\ast\in Y
.

In these situations and if
\dim X=\dim Y=n
, then

has a well-defined mapping degree
\deg(f)\in\mathbb{Z}
.
It can be characterized in terms of differential forms, that is,

\int_{X}f^{\ast}\omega=\deg(f)\ \int_{Y}\omega

for all compactly supported

-forms

on

which vanish on
\partial Y
.
More generally, if

and

-degree of

by the formula

\int_{X}\hat{\mathbf{A}}(X)\wedge f^{\ast}\omega=\hat{\mathrm{A}}\!-\!\deg(f)\ \int_{Y}\omega

for all compactly supported
k
-forms
\omega
on

which vanish on
\partial Y
.
Here

denotes the total
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-class of

.
Alternatively, the

can be described as the
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-genus of the transversal pre-image

of a point

(which in case (ii) must not be equal to the base-point).
Note that the

-degree can only be non-zero if

.

In the following, we fix a base-point

in the sphere of each dimension

Definition 4.1
.

Let

We say a connected oriented Riemannian manifold

\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-hyperspherical

\varepsilon

-degree.

.

If

in the definition above, then

must be a closed manifold such that

We now review various notions of largeness.
The first three go back in one form or another to Gromov and Lawson

Definition 4.3

Let

•

-

•

\hat{\mathrm{A}}

\varepsilon

•

\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-

such that

is
\varepsilon
-
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-hyperspherical in dimension

,

•

\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-

is
\varepsilon
-
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-hyperspherical in dimension

If

in any of these notions, then this just means that

is closed and has non-zero

-genus.
In the other extreme case

, the

-degree reduces the usual degree and this yields the usual notions of hypereuclidean, hyperspherical, enlargeable and iso-enlargeable.
If we omit the dimension

when using any of these notions, we will mean that the corresponding property holds in some dimension

.

The following implications hold.

X
admits a connected covering which is (
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-)hypereuclidean
X
admits a connected covering which is (
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-)hyperspherical
X
is (
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-)enlargeable
X
is (
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-)iso-enlargeable

(4.1)

The first is due to the fact that Euclidean space

is hyperspherical, and the others follow immediately from the definitions.

However, it is an open question whether any of these implications admit a converse for closed manifolds.
This is already the case for the non-

Indeed, the question of whether every closed iso-enlargeable manifold is enlargeable was already posed by Gromov in

, p. 659]

showed that there exist enlargeable closed manifolds whose

However, their construction does not prove the non-existence of an intermediate hyperspherical (or even hypereuclidean) covering.
It appears that given what we know at present we cannot even exclude the possibility that every iso-enlargeable manifold might admit a hypereuclidean covering, even though this looks unlikely.

Index-theoretic notions of width

In this section, we say that a

This notion appeared first in

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(X,g)

(V,\phi)
varies through all
V\in\mathcal{V}
and local diffeomorphisms
\phi\colon V\to X
.
If no such local diffeomorphism exists, we define
\width_{\mathcal{V}}(X,g)=0
.

We consider the following examples of classes of bands.

\hat{\mathcal{T}}
:

the class of all
overtorical

[

]

\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\hat{\mathrm{A}}}

the class of all
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-overtorical

\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-degree.

\mathcal{KO}

the class of all
\mathcal{KO}
-bands

Proposition 5.2
.

A band

that is spin is a
\mathcal{KO}
-band.

Proof.

Let

be an
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-overtorical band.
That means that there exists a band map

of non-zero
\hat{\mathrm{A}}
-degree.
Let
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{T}^{k-1}}\to\mathrm{T}^{k-1}\times[-1,1]

We set

This is a flat bundle of finitely generated projective Hilbert-

We will show that

To this end, observe that

\ind(\not{\mathfrak{D}}_{\partial_{-}\bar{V},E})=\nu(f_{\ast}[\partial_{-}V]_{\KO}),

Since

A Riemannian spin manifold

.
More generally:

Definition 5.4
.

.

Let

Suppose, by contradiction, that

7
Generaliziations and open problems

In this section, we discuss a number of possible generalizations and open problems which are suggested by the results discussed in this note.

7.1
Infinite scalar curvature decay via hypersurface

Theorem
3.1
suggests an estimate like (
1.1
) could hold whenever the closed hypersurface
M
does not admit a metric of positive scalar curvature.
In particular, we may propose the following question:

Conjecture 7.1
.

There exist functions
s_{n}\colon[0,2\pi/n)\to[0,\infty)
, where
s_{n}(x)\to\infty
as
x\to 2\pi/n
, such that the following holds.

Let
M
be a closed manifold of dimension
n-1\neq 4
which does not admit a metric of positive scalar curvature.
Suppose that
g
is a complete Riemannian metric on
M\times\mathbb{R}
whose scalar curvature is bounded below by
n(n-1)
on a neighborhood
U
of
M\times\{0\}
of some width
d<2\pi/n

(
more precisely, that is,
U\coloneqq\{p\in M\times\mathbb{R}\mid d_{g}(p,M\times\{0\})\leq d/2\})
. Then

\inf_{p\in M\times\mathbb{R}}\scal_{g}(p)\leq-s_{n}(d).

Note that this would imply both Conjectures
1.1
and
1.2
.

7.2
Almost spin manifolds

While the Dirac operator method relies on manifolds being spin, it is usually enough if the manifold admits a connected covering that is spin.
We refer to such manifolds as
almost spin
.
For simplicity, we have not stressed this point in this note.
However, we expect that generalizations to almost spin manifolds of our statements, in particular Theorem
1.4
, can be worked out along the same lines.
In the context of the Rosenberg index, the formal groundwork for this has already been laid by Stolz
[
41
]
who introduced a twisted version of the group
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\!
-algebra and the Rosenberg index for almost spin manifolds.

7.3
Comparison to the coarse index

There is another kind of index theory which has been successfully applied to obtain obstructions to
uniformly
positive scalar curvature on certain non-compact manifolds, namely Roe’s
coarse index theory
(for an introduction see, e.g.,
[
29
]
).
Coarse index theory was used by Hanke, Pape,
and Schick
[
17
]
in their approach to the index-theoretic codimension two obstruction (compare Example
5.10
) and by Chang, Weinberger and Yu
[
8
]
to provide obstructions to complete uniformly positive scalar curvature metrics (compare Proposition
5.7
).
For a selection of other related results see for instance
[
7
,
10
,
11
,
39
,
45
]
.

Crucial features of the coarse index are that it provides an obstruction to uniformly positive scalar curvature outside a compact subset, and it admits a way of localizing index information on submanifolds.
In the codimension one case, the latter is facilitated by Roe’s
partitioned manifold index theorem
which states that a non-vanishing index of a partitioning hypersurface in a non-compact complete manifold implies non-vanishing of the coarse index.

For instance, this can be used to show that if
M
is a spin manifold of non-vanishing Rosenberg index, then
M\times\mathbb{R}
does not admit a complete metric which has uniformly positive scalar curvature outside a compact subset.
However, this is
also
a consequence of Theorem
1.4
.
Indeed, it appears that in all the examples we know, the outright obstructions to complete uniformly positive scalar curvature obtained through the coarse index can be alternatively proved (and strengthened) by Theorem
1.4
.
On the other hand, coarse index theory can be used to exclude the existence of uniformly positive scalar curvature in a fixed quasi-isometry class of Riemannian metrics (the simplest example being
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
which admits a complete metric of uniformly positive scalar curvature but not in the same quasi-isometry class as the standard Euclidean metric).

This is a somewhat vague point, but it would be of interest to explore if there are a more concrete relations between coarse index theory and the Callias-type operator methods exhibited here (beyond the fact that they have a partially overlapping range of applications).

7.4
Implications between notions of largeness

As mentioned Section
4
in the discussion after (
4.1
), it is an open problem to understand which of the obvious implications between the largeness properties of hypereuclidean, hyperspherical, enlargeable and iso-enlargeable admit a converse.
In particular, this includes Gromov’s question of whether closed iso-enlargeable manifolds are enlargeable.

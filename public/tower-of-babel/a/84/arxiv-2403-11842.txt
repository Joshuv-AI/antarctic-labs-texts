I
Introduction

The electromagnetic (EM) Dalitz decay of a hadron
A
, namely,
A\to B\gamma^{*}\to Bl^{+}l^{-}
, refers to the decay process that
A
decays into
B
by emitting a time-like photon which then converts to a lepton pair
l^{+}l^{-}
. The differential partial decay width with respect to the invariant mass
q^{2}\equiv m^{2}_{l^{+}l^{-}}
of the lepton pair can be expressed by
d\Gamma(q^{2})/dq^{2}=(d\Gamma(q^{2})/dq^{2})_{\mathrm{point-like}}|f_{AB}(q^{2})|^{2}

Landsberg 1985
, where
(d\Gamma(q^{2})/dq^{2})_{\mathrm{point-like}}
can be calculated exactly in QED for point-like particle
A
and
B
and the
f_{AB}(q^{2})
is called the transition form factor (TFF) of the transition
A\to B
, and is an important probe to the EM structure of the
AB\gamma
vertex and also the internal structure of the hadron
A
(and
B
if it is also structured). Experimentally, the TFF
f_{AB}(q^{2})
can be derived by taking the ratio
[d\Gamma(q^{2})/dq^{2}]/\Gamma(A\to B\gamma)\propto|f_{AB}(q^{2})|^{2}/|f_{AB}(0)|^{2}\equiv|F_{AB}(q^{2})|^{2}
with the normalization
F_{AB}(0)=1
, where many systematic uncertainties cancel. The Dalitz decays of light hadrons, such as
\phi\to\pi^{0}e^{+}e^{-}

Achasov et al. 2002
;
Anastasi et al. 2016
,
\phi\to\eta e^{+}e^{-}

Babusci et al. 2015
,
\omega\to\pi^{0}e^{+}e^{-}

Akhmetshin et al. 2005
,
\omega\to\pi^{0}\mu^{+}\mu^{-}

Dzhelyadin et al. 1979
, have been widely studied in experiments. It should be noted that the experimental studies on Dalitz decays usually require large statistics, since they are rare decays for a hadron.

The BESIII Collaboration (BESIII) has accumulated more than
10^{10}

J/\psi
events
Ablikim et al. 2022a
, based on which the Dalitz decays of
J/\psi
to light hadrons can be researched. On the other hand, the light pseudoscalars (P), such as
\eta,\eta^{\prime},\eta(1405/1475)
and
X(1835)
etc., are observed to have large production rates on the
J/\psi
radiative decays
Workman et al. 2022
. So it is expected the Daltiz decays
J/\psi\to Pl^{+}l^{-}
can be investigated to a high precision. Actually, BESIII has performed the experimental studies on the processes
J/\psi\to e^{+}e^{-}(\eta,\eta^{\prime},\pi^{0})

Ablikim et al. 2014
,
J/\psi\to e^{+}e^{-}\eta

Ablikim et al. 2019a
,
J/\psi\to e^{+}e^{-}\eta^{\prime}

Ablikim et al. 2018
;
Ablikim et al. 2019b
,
J/\psi\to e^{+}e^{-}\eta(1405)

Ablikim et al. 2023
and
J/\psi\to e^{+}e^{-}(X(1835),X(2120),X(2370)

Ablikim et al. 2022b
. With the large
\psi(3686)
data ensemble, BESIII also studies the process
\psi(3686)\to e^{+}e^{-}\eta_{c}
. The TTFs
F_{\psi p}(q^{2})
are extracted for the processes
J/\psi\to\eta,\eta^{\prime},\eta(1405),X(1835)
and
\psi(3686)\to\eta_{c}
, and the
q^{2}
-dependence can be described by the single-pole model

F_{\psi P}=\frac{1}{1-q^{2}/\Lambda^{2}},

(1)

based on the vector meson dominance (VMD)
Landsberg 1985
;
Fu et al. 2012
;
Gu et al. 2019
, and the pole parameter
\Lambda
varies in the range from 1.7 to 3.8 GeV.

Intuitively, the Dalitz decay and the radiative decay of charmonium into light pseudoscalars happen through the annihilation of the charm quark and antiquark. According to the OZI rule, the dominant contribution comes from the initial state radiation of the virtual and real photons from the charm (anti)quark. In this sense, the TFF of
J/\psi
to pseudoscalars should reflect the electromagnetic properties of
J/\psi
. Therefore, For a same initial vector charmonium, Dalitz decays are insensitive to the properties of the final state light hadrons. A theoretical derivation of
F_{\psi P}(q^{2})
from QCD is desirable but is still challenging since
F_{\psi P}(q^{2})
is obviously in the non-perturbative regime of QCD. The phenomenological studies of
F_{\psi\eta^{(^{\prime})}}(q^{2})
can be found in Ref.
He and Fan 2022
where the analysis is carried out in the full kinematic region based on QCD models and in Ref.
Chen et al. 2015
where the
J/\psi\to\gamma^{*}P
is discussed within the framework of the effective Lagrangian approach and the
\eta_{c}-\eta-\eta^{\prime}
mixing is considered.

Lattice QCD may take the mission to give reliable predictions of TFF of
J/\psi
to light hadrons. A recent
N_{f}=2
lattice QCD calculation confirms the large production rate of the flavor singlet pseudoscalar meson
\eta_{(2)}
in the
J/\psi
radiative decay
Jiang et al. 2023
. In that work, the EM form factor is obtained at quite a few values of time-like
q^{2}
, from which the on-shell form factor at
q^{2}=0
is obtained through a polynomial interpolation. By assuming the
\mathrm{U}_{A}(1)
anomaly dominance and using the
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
mixing angle, this on-shell form factor results in the branching fractions of
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta
and
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta^{\prime}
that are close to the experimental values. Actually, the
q^{2}
dependence of this decay form factor is described better by the single-pole model in Eq. (
1
) (see below).

Recently, we generated a large gauge ensemble with
N_{f}=1
strange sea quarks. The
N_{f}=1
QCD is a well defined theory and a simplified version of QCD. It has no chiral symmetry breaking but the
\mathrm{U}_{A}(1)
anomaly that has a close relation with the unique light pseudoscalar meson
\eta_{(1)}
. So we will revisit the production rate of
\eta_{(1)}
in the
J/\psi
radiative decays. We will test the
\mathrm{U}_{A}(1)
anomaly dominance in this process by looking at the
N_{f}
dependence of the partial decay width, since the
\mathrm{U}_{A}(1)
anomaly is proportional to
N_{f}
. In the meantime, we will explore the
q^{2}
-dependence of the related TFF and its sensitivity to the light pseudoscalar mass since our sea quark is much heavier than that in Ref.
Jiang et al. 2023
. The related calculations involve necessarily the annihilation effect of strange quarks which are dealt with using the distillation method
Peardon et al. 2009
.

This work is organized as follows. The numerical procedures and results are presented in Sec.
II
. Section
III
is devoted to the discussions and the physical implications of our results. Sect.
IV
is the summary of this work.

We generate gauge configurations with

(2)

1

2

,

\psi

is a matrix with each column being an eigenvector. All the meson interpolation operators in this work are built from the smeared charm and strange quark fields.

II.3
Form Factor for
J/\psi\to\gamma^{*}\eta_{(1)}

The transition matrix element
\mathcal{M}
for the process
J/\psi\to\gamma^{*}\eta_{(1)}
can be expressed in terms of one form factor
M(q^{2})
, namely,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\psi\eta_{(1)}\gamma^{*}}^{\mu}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\langle\eta_{(1)}(p_{\eta})|j_{\mathrm{em}}^{\mu}(0)|\psi(p_{\psi},\lambda)\rangle

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M(q^{2})\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}p_{\psi,\nu}p_{\eta,\rho}\epsilon_{\sigma}(p_{\psi},\lambda),

where
q^{2}=(p_{\psi}-p_{\eta})^{2}
is the virtuality of the photon,
\epsilon_{\sigma}(p_{J/\psi},\lambda)
is the polarization vector of
J/\psi
and
j_{\mathrm{em}}^{\mu}=\bar{c}\gamma^{\mu}c
is the electromagnetic current of charm quark (we only consider the initial state radiation and ignore photon emissions from sea quarks and the final state). The matrix element
\mathcal{M}
is encoded in the following three-point functions

C^{\mu i}_{(3)}(\vec{q};t,t^{\prime})=\sum\limits_{\vec{y}}e^{i\vec{q}\cdot\vec{y}}\langle\mathcal{O}_{\eta}(\vec{p^{\prime}},t)j_{\mathrm{em}}^{\mu}(\vec{y},t^{\prime})\mathcal{O}_{\psi}^{i,\dagger}(\vec{p},0)\rangle

(6)

with
\vec{q}=\vec{p^{\prime}}-\vec{p}
, where
\mathcal{O}_{\eta}(\vec{p^{\prime}},t)
and
\mathcal{O}_{\psi}^{i}(\vec{p},t)
are the interpolating field operators for
\eta_{(1)}
and
J/\psi
with spatial momenta
\vec{p^{\prime}}
and
\vec{p}
, respectively. For
t\gg t^{\prime}
,
t^{\prime}\gg 0
and in the rest frame of
J/\psi
(
\vec{p}=0
), the explicit spectral expression of
C^{\mu i}_{(3)}(\vec{q};t,t^{\prime})
reads

\displaystyle C^{\mu i}_{(3)}(\vec{q};t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{Z_{\eta}(\vec{q})Z_{\psi}^{*}}{4V_{3}E_{\eta}(\vec{q})m_{\psi}}e^{-E_{\eta}(\vec{q})(t-t^{\prime})}e^{-m_{\psi}t^{\prime}}

(7)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\lambda}\langle{\eta_{(1)}}(\vec{q})|j_{\mathrm{em}}^{\mu}|J/\psi(\vec{0},\lambda)\rangle\epsilon^{*,i}(\vec{0},\lambda),

where
V_{3}
is the spatial volume,
Z_{\eta}(\vec{q})=\langle\Omega|\mathcal{O}_{\eta_{(1)}}(\vec{q})|{\eta_{(1)}}(\vec{q})\rangle
, and
Z_{\psi}\epsilon^{i}(\vec{0},\lambda)=\langle\Omega|\mathcal{O}_{\psi}^{i}(\vec{0})|J/\psi(\vec{0},\lambda)\rangle
. Note that
Z_{\eta}
has a
\vec{q}
dependence due to the smeared operator
\mathcal{O}_{\eta}

Bali et al. 2016
. The parameters
m_{\psi}
,
E_{\eta}(\vec{q})
,
Z_{\psi}
and
Z_{\eta}(\vec{q})
can be derived from the two-point correlation functions

\displaystyle C_{(2),\eta}(\vec{q},t)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2E_{\eta}(\vec{q})V_{3}}|Z_{\eta}(\vec{q})|^{2}e^{-E_{\eta}(\vec{q})t},

\displaystyle C_{(2),\psi}^{ii}(t)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m_{\psi}V_{3}}|Z_{J/\psi}|^{2}e^{-m_{\psi}t}.

(8)

Thus we can extract the matrix element
\langle\eta_{(1)}|j_{\mathrm{em}}^{\mu}|J/\psi\rangle
through Eqs. (
7
) and (
II.3
).

Therefore, the major numerical task is the calculation of
C_{(3)}^{\mu i}(\vec{q};t,t^{\prime})
. The local EM current
j_{\mathrm{em}}^{\mu}(x)=[\bar{c}\gamma^{\mu}c](x)
mentioned above (the charm quark field
c
and
\bar{c}
are the original field, which are not smeared) is not conserved anymore on the finite lattice and should be renormalized. We determine
the renormalization factor
Z_{V}^{t}=1.147(1)
and
Z_{V}^{s}=1.191(2)
for the temporal and spatial components of
j_{\mathrm{em}}^{\mu}(x)
,
respectively, by calculating the relevant electromagnetic form factors of
\eta_{c}

Dudek et al. 2006
;
Yang et al. 2013
. In practice, only
Z_{V}^{s}
is involved and is incorporated implicitly in
j_{\mathrm{em}}^{\mu}(x)
. We use the operator
\mathcal{O}_{\gamma_{4}\gamma_{5}}=\bar{s}^{(s)}\gamma_{4}\gamma_{5}s^{(s)}
for
\mathcal{O}_{\eta}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\psi}^{i}
takes the form
\bar{c}^{(s)}\gamma^{i}c^{(s)}
in Eq. (
6
). The three-point function
C_{(3)}^{\mu i}(\vec{q};t,t^{\prime})
is calculated in the rest frame of
J/\psi
(
\vec{p}=0
), such that
\eta_{(1)}
moves in a spatial momentum
\vec{p}^{\prime}=\vec{q}

1

\eta_{(1)}

a_{t}^{2}E_{\eta}^{2}(\vec{q})=a_{t}^{2}m_{\eta}^{2}+\frac{1}{\xi^{2}}\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{2}|\vec{n}|^{2},

(9)

where

After the Wick’s contractions, the three-point function
C^{\mu i}_{(3)}
is expressed in terms of quark propagators, and the schematic quark diagram is illustrated in Fig.

comes from the contraction of
\mathcal{O}_{\psi}^{i}
and the current

G^{\mu i}(\vec{p},\vec{q};t^{\prime}+\tau,\tau)=\sum\limits_{\vec{y}}e^{i\vec{q}\cdot\vec{y}}j_{\mathrm{em}}^{\mu}(\vec{y},t^{\prime}+\tau)O_{\psi}^{i\dagger}(\vec{p},\tau),

(10)

and is dealt with the distillation method
Chen et al. 2023

\mathcal{O}_{\psi}^{i}(\vec{p},t)=\sum\limits_{\vec{y}}e^{-i\vec{p}\cdot\vec{y}}[\bar{c}^{(s)}\gamma_{i}c^{(s)}](\vec{y},t)
,
the explicit expression of

at the source time slice

\displaystyle G^{\mu i}(\vec{p},\vec{q};t,0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\vec{x}}e^{i\vec{q}\cdot\vec{x}}\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\gamma_{5}[S_{c}V(0)]^{\dagger}(\vec{x},t)\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\mu}\right.

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left.\left[S_{c}V(0)\right](\vec{x},t)[V^{\dagger}(0)D(\vec{p})\gamma^{i}V(0)]\right\},

where
S_{c}=\langle c\bar{c}\rangle_{U}
is the all-to-all propagator of charm quark for a given gauge configuration

and

is a

\delta_{ab}e^{i\vec{p}\cdot\vec{y}}
(

labels the column or row indices and

\gamma_{5}

,
such that only
S_{c}V(0)
is required, while
S_{c}V(0)
can be obtained by solving the system of linear equations

M[U;m_{c}][S_{c}V(0)]=V(0),

(12)

III
Discussions

III.1
The partial decay width of
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{(1)}

The partial decay width
\Gamma(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{(1)})
is dictated by the on-shell form factor
M(q^{2}=0)
through the relation

\Gamma(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{(1)})=\frac{4\alpha}{27}|M(0)|^{2}|\vec{p}_{\gamma}|^{3},

(16)

where the electric charge of charm quark
Q=+2e/3
has been incorporated,
\alpha\equiv\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi}=1/134
is the fine structure constant at the charm quark mass scale, and
|\vec{p}_{\gamma}|=(m_{\psi}^{2}-m_{\eta_{(1)}}^{2})/2m_{\psi}
is the on-shell momentum of the photon. Using the value of
M(0)
in Eq. (
II.3
), the partial decay width and the corresponding branching fraction are predicted as

\displaystyle\Gamma(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{(1)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.097(5)~\mathrm{keV}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Br}(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{(1)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1.06(5)\times 10^{-3},

(17)

where the experimental value
\Gamma_{J/\psi}=92.6~\mathrm{keV}
is used. Since an
\eta
state in the process
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta
is produced by gluons through its flavor singlet component, the results in Eq. (
III.1
) should be compared with the experimental result

\mathrm{Br}(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta^{\prime})=5.25(7)\times 10^{-3}

Workman et al. 2022
(
\eta^{\prime}
is mainly a flavor singlet) and
\mathrm{Br}(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{(2)})=4.16(49)\times 10^{-3}
in the
N_{f}=2
case at
m_{\pi}\approx 350~\mathrm{MeV}

Jiang et al. 2023
. Obviously
\mathrm{Br}(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{(1)})
is four or five times smaller that in
N_{f}=2
and
N_{f}=2+1
cases.

This large difference can be understood as follows. The decay process
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{(N_{f})}
takes place in the procedure that the
c\bar{c}
pair annihilates into gluons (after a photon radiation), which then convert into

\eta_{(N_{f})}
. There are two mechanisms for gluons to couple to
\eta_{(N_{f})}
. The first is the
\mathrm{U}_{A}(1)
anomaly manifested by the anomalous axial vector current relation (in the chiral limit)

\partial_{\mu}j_{5}^{\mu}(x)=\sqrt{N_{f}}\frac{g^{2}}{32\pi}G_{\mu\nu}^{a}(x)\tilde{G}^{a,\mu\nu}(x)\equiv\sqrt{N_{f}}q(x),

(18)

where
j_{5}^{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{f}}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\bar{q}_{k}\gamma_{5}\gamma^{\mu}q_{k}
is the flavor singlet axial vector current for
N_{f}
flavor quarks, and
q(x)
is the topological charge density. The
\mathrm{U}_{A}(1)
anomaly induces the anomalous gluon-
\eta
coupling with the strength observed by the matrix element
\langle 0|q(0)|\eta_{(N_{f})}\rangle
. With the matrix element
\langle 0|\partial_{\mu}j_{5}^{\mu}(0)|\eta_{(N_{f})}\rangle=f_{\eta_{(N_{f})}}m_{\eta_{(N_{f})}}^{2}
, from Eq. (
18
) one has the relation

\langle 0|q(0)|\eta_{(N_{f})}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{f}}}f_{\eta_{(N_{f})}}m_{\eta_{(N_{f})}}^{2}

(19)

in the chiral limit. According to the Witten and Veneziano mechanism
Witten 1979
;
Veneziano 1979
for the mass of
\eta_{(N_{f})}
,
m_{\eta_{(N_{f})}}^{2}=\frac{4N_{f}}{f_{\pi}^{2}}\chi_{\mathrm{top}}
, where
\chi_{\mathrm{top}}
is the topological susceptibility of the SU(3) pure Yang-Mills theory, one has
\langle 0|q(0)|\eta_{(N_{f})}\rangle\propto\sqrt{N_{f}}
in the chiral limit.

For massless quarks, the
\mathrm{U}_{A}(1)
anomaly dominates the production of
\eta_{(N_{f})}
in the process
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{(N_{f})}
,
then one expects the
N_{f}
scaling for the partial decay width

\Gamma(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{(N_{f})})\propto|\langle 0|q(0)|\eta_{(N_{f})}\rangle|^{2}\propto N_{f},

(20)

since
f_{\eta_{(N_{f})}}\approx f_{\pi}
is independent of
N_{f}
to the lowest order in
1/N_{c}
. In Ref.
Jiang et al. 2023
, this scaling relation is used to predict the production rates of
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
from the form factor
M(0)
of the
N_{f}=2
case at
m_{\pi}\approx 350~\mathrm{MeV}
along with the mixing
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
mixing angle
\theta_{\mathrm{lin}}=-24.5^{\circ}

Workman et al. 2022
. The results
\mathrm{Br}(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta)=1.15(14)\times 10^{-3}
and

\mathrm{Br}(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta^{\prime})=4.49(53)\times 10^{-3}
are in excellent agreement with the experimental values
1.11(3)\times 10^{-3}
and
5.25(7)\times 10^{-3}
, respectively. For the case of this study of
N_{f}=1
strange quarks, the scaling relation implies
\mathrm{Br}(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{(N_{f}=3)})=3.19(15)\times 10^{-3}
which is closer to the experiment value of
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta^{\prime}
. This is in the right trend but still has a large discrepancy that can be attributed to the quark mass dependence. The quark mass dependence appears in three places.
First, there is an additional term
2m_{s}j_{5}\equiv 2m_{s}(i\bar{s}\gamma_{5}s)
in the right-hand side of Eq. (
18
) for
N_{f}=1
strange quarks, which gives a correction to Eq. (
19
) as

\langle 0|q(0)|\eta_{(1)}\rangle=f_{\eta_{(N_{f})}}m_{\eta_{(N_{f})}}^{2}-2m_{s}\langle 0|j_{5}(0)|\eta_{(1)}\rangle

(21)

with
m_{s}\langle 0|j_{5}(0)|\eta_{(1)}\rangle>0

Novikov et al. 1980
;
Feldmann 2000
;
Beneke and Neubert 2003
;
Cheng et al. 2009
;
Singh 2013
;
Qin et al. 2018
;
Ding et al. 2019
;
Bali et al. 2021
.
Secondly, the right-hand side of Eq. (
19
) has quark mass dependence itself through
f_{\eta_{(N_{f})}}
and
m_{\eta_{(N_{f})}}
. Since the strange quark is not so light as
u,d
quarks, this kind of the quark mass dependence may not be negligible. The third place is the
\eta_{(N_{f})}

production procedure that the gluons couple perturbatively to
q\bar{q}
pairs which then couple to
\eta_{(N_{f})}
. Because of the vector-like
q\bar{q}
-gluon coupling in QCD, the produced quark and antiquark have opposite chiralities. So in the chiral limit, the production of
\eta_{(N_{f})}
is prohibited to all orders of the perturbative QCD due to the conservation of angular momentum (the
q\bar{q}
pair has chirality two and has a total spin
S=0
and the orbital angular momentum
L=0
). For massive quarks, the amplitude of this process is proportional to the quark mass
Chanowitz 2005
;
Chao et al. 2007
, which measures the mixing of quarks with different chiralities. Thus the production of
s\bar{s}
is drastically enhanced over that of
(u\bar{u}+d\bar{d})/\sqrt{2}
. If the amplitude of this mechanism for the
\eta_{(N_{f})}
production has an opposite
sign to that of the
\mathrm{U}_{A}(1)
anomaly, then the seemingly smaller value in Eq. (
III.1
) can be understood. This possibility actually exists, as is manifested in a previous lattice QCD study on the semileptonic decay from
D_{s}
to
\eta^{\prime}

Bali et al. 2015
, where it is observed that, the contribution of the direct coupling of
s\bar{s}
to
\eta^{\prime}
has an opposite sign to that through the disconnected diagram.

IV
Summary

We generate a large gauge ensemble with
N_{f}=1
dynamical strange quarks on an anisotropic lattice with the anisotropy
a_{s}/a_{t}\approx 5.0
. The pseudoscalar mass is measured to be
m_{\eta_{s}}=693.1(3)~\mathrm{MeV}
without considering the quark annihilation effect, and
m_{\eta_{(1)}}=783.0(5.5)~\mathrm{MeV}
with the inclusion the quark annihilation diagrams. We calculate the EM form factor
M(q^{2})
for the decay process
J/\psi\to\gamma^{*}(q^{2})\eta_{(1)}
with
q^{2}
being the virtuality of the photon. By interpolating
M(q^{2})
to the value at
q^{2}=0
through the VMD inspired single pole model in Eq. (
14
), the decay width and the branching fraction of
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{(1)}
is predicted to be
\Gamma(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{(1)})=0.097(5)~\mathrm{keV}

and
\mathrm{Br}(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{(1)})=1.06(5)\times 10^{-3}
, respectively, which are much smaller than those in the
N_{f}=2
case and those in the physical
N_{f}=2+1
case.
The difference among the partial widths
\Gamma(J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{(N_{f})})
at different
N_{f}
can be attributed in part to the
\mathbf{U}_{A}(1)
anomaly that induces a
N_{f}
scaling.

It is interesting to see that
M(q^{2})
’s in
N_{f}=1,2
are both well described by the single pole model
M(q^{2})=M(0)/(1-q^{2}/\Lambda^{2})
. Combined together with the known experimental results of the Dalitz decays
J/\psi\to Pe^{+}e^{-}
with
P
being light pseudoscalar mesons, the
m_{P}
dependence of the pole parameter

\Lambda
is observed and can be expressed approximately as
\Lambda(m_{P}^{2})=\Lambda_{1}(1-m_{P}^{2}/\Lambda_{2}^{2})
with
\Lambda_{1}=2.64(4)~\mathrm{GeV}
and
\Lambda_{2}=2.97(33)~\mathrm{GeV}
. This result provide meaningful inputs for future theoretical and experimental studied on Dalitz decays
J/\psi\to Pe^{+}e^{-}
. As a direct application, this
m_{P}
dependence expects a pole parameter
\Lambda(s=m_{\eta^{\prime}}^{2})=2.36(3)~\mathrm{GeV}
, which is more precise than the value
3.1(1.0)~\mathrm{GeV}
measured by BESIII
Ablikim et al. 2014
and whose prediction on
\mathrm{Br}(J/\psi\to\eta^{\prime}e^{+}e^{-})
agrees better with the experimental value
Ablikim et al. 2019b
.

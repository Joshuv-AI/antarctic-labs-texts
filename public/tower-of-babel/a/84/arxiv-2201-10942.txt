The Equidistribution of Grids of Rings of Integers in Number Fields of Degrees 3,4 and 5
Yuval Yifrach

Abstract
It was shown by M. Bhargava and P. Harron that for n = 3 , 4 , 5 n=3,4,5 , the shapes of rings of integers of S n S_{n} -number fields of degree n n become equidistributed in the space of shapes when the fields are ordered by discriminant. Instead of shapes, we correspond grids to each number field, which preserve more of the number fields’ data. The space of grids is a fiber bundle over the space of shapes. We strengthen Bhargava-Harron’s result by proving that the grids of rings of integers of S n S_{n} -number fields become equidistributed in the space of grids.



1
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a number field of degree
n
and let
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}
denote its ring of integers. Let
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r}
denote the
r
real embeddings of
\mathbb{F}
and
\tau_{1},\overline{\tau_{1}},\dots,\tau_{s},\overline{\tau_{s}}
denote the
s
pairs of complex embeddings of
\mathbb{F}
. We define the signature of
\mathbb{F}
to be
(r,s)
.
These embeddings induce a map
\sigma:\mathbb{F}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{r}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{s}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by:

\mathbb{F}\ni\alpha\mapsto(\sigma_{1}(\alpha),\dots,\sigma_{r}(\alpha),\tau_{1}(\alpha),\dots,\tau_{s}(\alpha))\in\mathbb{R}^{r}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{s}.

We denote the image of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}
under
\sigma
by
\Lambda(\mathbb{F})
.
The space
X_{n}
of unimodular lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is commonly identified with
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
and is endowed with the quotient topology. Given a lattice
\Lambda
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
we denote
\left<\Lambda\right>
to be the corresponding scaled unimodular lattice. It is well known that
\Lambda(\mathbb{F})
is a lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and therefore
\left<\Lambda(\mathbb{F})\right>\in X_{n}
.

It is very natural to study geometric aspects of the lattices
\Lambda(\mathbb{F})
as
\mathbb{F}
varies. It is natural to expect that unless an obvious obstacle of an algebraic origin exists, various geometric aspects of the lattices
\Lambda(\mathbb{F})
would behave ”randomly” as
\mathbb{F}
varies. The first naive attempt is to consider
\left<\Lambda(\mathbb{F})\right>
as a point in
X_{n}
equipped with the uniform (
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
-invariant) probability measure
m_{X_{n}}
, and expect that the countable collection
\left<\Lambda(\mathbb{F})\right>
equidistributes there as
\mathbb{F}
varies in a natural way. This naive attempt fails drastically because of an obvious ”algebraic” reason as we shall presently explain. For convenience, assume that the signature of
\mathbb{F}
is
(n,0)
. Then the unimodular lattice
\left<\Lambda(\mathbb{F})\right>
contains the vector
v_{\overline{1}}=\left(\frac{1}{\lvert\operatorname{Disc}(\mathbb{F})\rvert}\right)^{\frac{1}{2n}}(1,\dots,1)
and therefore by Mahler’s compactness criterion (see
[
7
]
), we have that not only does
\Lambda(\mathbb{F})
fail to equidistribute as
\mathbb{F}
varies, but in fact, any compact set in the space of lattices contains only finitely many such lattices (in other words
\left<\Lambda(\mathbb{F})\right>
diverges to infinity as
\mathbb{F}
varies).

In the paper
[
3
]
, the authors show, roughly, that orthogonal to the short vector
v_{\overline{1}}
, the geometry of the lattice
\left<\Lambda(\mathbb{F})\right>
does look random as
\mathbb{F}
varies. In this paper we consider slightly more refined ways to remedy the divergence to infinity. That is, we identify a closed sub-homogeneous space of
X_{n}
, which we call
Y^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}_{1}
(the reason for this notation will become clear later), and deform
\left<\Lambda(\mathbb{F})\right>
into
Y_{1}^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}
in a manner that still preserves some of the geometry of
\Lambda(\mathbb{F})
, and establish the equidistribution of the resulting points in
Y^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}_{1}
as
\mathbb{F}
varies.

In fact, the deformation of
\left<\Lambda(\mathbb{F})\right>
into
Y^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}_{1}
will depend on an ad hoc choice of a linear direct complement
E
of the line
\mathbb{R}\cdot(1,\dots,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(in the case of signature
(n,0)
, for the remaining signatures the vector will be replaced by a different one) and it is interesting to note that the answer to the question ”how do the deformed lattices distribute in
Y^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}_{1}
as
\mathbb{F}
varies” depends on the choice of
E
, namely, the asymptotic behaviour is completely different for
E=V_{0}
and for
E\neq V_{0}
where
V_{0}
is the orthogonal complement of
(1,\dots,1)
.

We remark that (as will be explained in more detail below), the deformed lattices in
Y^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}_{1}
record some of the original geometrical information of
\Lambda(\mathbb{F})
. For example, we show below that one can recover the main result of
[
3
]
from our main results. As we are building heavily on the analysis and results from
[
3
]
, this last remark should only be taken into account on the motivational level, supporting the ”natural” nature of our deformation procedure which we shall now define.

For any line
\ell
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote:

Y^{\ell}\vcentcolon=\{\Lambda\in X_{n}:\Lambda\cap\ell\text{ is a lattice in }\ell\},\text{ }Y_{1}^{\ell}\vcentcolon=\{\Lambda\in X_{n}:\Lambda\cap\ell\text{ is a unimodular lattice in }\ell\}.

(1.1)

Definition 1.1
.

A Borel probability measure
\mu
on
X_{n}
is called homogeneous if there exists a closed subgroup
H\leq\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\mu
is invariant under
H
and supported on an
H
orbit. An orbit supporting a homogeneous measure is called periodic. Periodic orbits are closed in
X_{n}
.

Each of the subsets
Y^{\ell},Y^{\ell}_{1}
is an orbit of a closed subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. Indeed,
Y^{\ell}
is the orbit of the subgroup of elements
g\in\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying
g\ell=\ell
and
Y^{\ell}_{1}
is the orbit of the subgroup of elements
g\in\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying
gv=v
for every
v\in\ell
. The orbit
Y^{\ell}
is dense in
X_{n}
and the orbit
Y^{\ell}_{1}
is closed in
X_{n}
and in fact periodic. We denote the homogeneous measure on
Y^{\ell}_{1}
by
m_{Y^{\ell}_{1}}
.
We will use the following normalization group to deform elements of
Y^{\ell}
into
Y^{\ell}_{1}
:

Definition 1.2
.

Given subspaces
E_{1},E_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
E_{1}\oplus E_{2}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we define a linear operator
g_{E_{1},E_{2}}^{t}\in\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
by
g(x)=e^{t/dim(E_{1})}x
,
g(y)=e^{-t/dim(E_{2})}y
for every
x\in E_{1},y\in E_{2}
. The set
\{g_{E_{1},E_{2}}^{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a group, which we shall call the
E_{1},E_{2}
normalization group.

Define the vector
\overline{1}\vcentcolon=(1,\dots,1,1,0,\dots,1,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
as the image of
(1_{\mathbb{R}},\dots,1_{\mathbb{R}},1_{\mathbb{C}},\dots,1_{\mathbb{C}})\in\mathbb{R}^{r}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{s}
under the identification with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
\overline{1}
is an element of
\Lambda(\mathbb{F})
because it is the image of the identity in
\mathbb{F}
under
\sigma
. Denote
\ell_{\overline{1}}=\{t\overline{1}:t\in\mathbb{R}\}
and note that
\left<\Lambda(\mathbb{F})\right>\in Y^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}
. Let
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
denote the standard inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For any subspace
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, define
E^{\perp}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left\langle x,E\right\rangle=0\}
and
V_{0}=\ell_{\overline{1}}^{\perp}
.

Let
E
be a subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of co-dimension 1 satisfying
E\oplus\ell_{\overline{1}}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
t\in\mathbb{R}
be such that:

q_{E}(\mathbb{F})\vcentcolon=g^{t}_{E,\ell_{\overline{1}}}\left<\Lambda(\mathbb{F})\right>\in Y_{1}^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}

(1.2)

(
t
will be the solution of
e^{-t}\operatorname{cov}(\Lambda(\mathbb{F}))^{-1/n}\sqrt{n}=1
). Therefore
q_{E}(\cdot)
deforms an element of
Y^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}
into an element of
Y_{1}^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}
.
The following definition parametrizes a subset of the number fields using the discriminant.

Definition 1.3
.

For any
T>0
and signature
(n-2s,s)
, let
\mathcal{F}^{s}_{n}(T)
be the set of isomorphism classes of degree
n
number fields
\mathbb{F}
of signature
(n-2s,s)
, with discriminant
\leq T
in absolute value such that the Galois group of the normal closure of
\mathbb{F}
is
S_{n}
. It is well known that
\mathcal{F}_{n}(T)
is finite for any
T>0
.

So far we have constructed a correspondence between number fields and elements of
Y_{1}^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}
, which are essentially lattices with some extra properties. As appeared earlier, the aim of this paper is to analyze the connection between number fields and lattices given by this correspondence. More concretely, we will analyze the asymptotic distribution as
T\rightarrow\infty
of
\{q_{E}(\mathbb{F})\}_{\mathbb{F}\in\mathcal{F}_{n}^{s}(T)}
for the different choices for
E
. The answer will turn out to be dependent upon the choice of
E
and to imply Bhargava-Harron’s main result in
[
3
]
.

1.1
New Equidistribution Results for Number Fields of Degrees 3,4,5

In this subsection we state our new results which implement the philosophy described in the first part of this introduction.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
n=3,4,5
and let
(r,s)
be a signature. Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a subspace such that
E\oplus\ell_{\overline{1}}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
E\neq V_{0}
. Then:

\frac{1}{\lvert\mathcal{F}^{s}_{n}(T)\rvert}\sum_{\mathbb{F}\in\mathcal{F}^{s}_{n}(T)}\delta_{q_{E}(\mathbb{F})}\longrightarrow m_{Y_{1}^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}}\text{ weakly as }T\longrightarrow\infty.

(1.3)

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
n=3,4,5
and let
(n-2s,s)
be a signature. Denote
\operatorname{Div}(n)=\{1\leq k\leq n:k\mid n\}
to be the set of divisors of
n
. Then for any
k\in\operatorname{Div}(n)
there exists a periodic orbit
F^{(k)}\subset Y_{1}^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}
,
\ell=\ell(k)\in\mathbb{N}
, disjoint closed connected subsets
F^{(k)}_{1},\dots,F^{(k)}_{\ell(k)}\subset F^{(k)}
and nonnegative scalars
s_{k,\ell};\ell=1,\dots,\ell(k)
not all zero, such that:

\frac{1}{\lvert\mathcal{F}^{s}_{n}(T)\rvert}\sum_{\mathbb{F}\in\mathcal{F}^{s}_{n}(T)}\delta_{q_{V_{0}}(\mathbb{F})}\longrightarrow\sum_{k\in\operatorname{Div}(n)}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\ell(k)}s_{k,\ell}\mu^{(k)}\mid_{F^{(k)}_{\ell}}\text{ weakly as }T\longrightarrow\infty.

(1.4)

where
\mu^{(k)}
is the homogenous measure supported on the periodic orbit
F^{(k)}
.

The two Theorems above are the main results of this paper. In order to motivate them we explain how each of them implies Bhargava-Harron’s main result in
[
3
]
which is stated below as Theorem
1.6
.

First we give additional background required to state
[
3
, Theorem 1]
. In the above setting, consider the orthogonal projection of
\Lambda(\mathbb{F})
on
V_{0}
and denote it by
P\Lambda(\mathbb{F})
. Fix a linear isometry
\phi
between
V_{0}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
and use it to identify
P\Lambda(\mathbb{F})
with its image under
\phi
. From now on we think of
P\Lambda(\mathbb{F})
as a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. Since
\overline{1}\in\Lambda(\mathbb{F})
,
P\Lambda(\mathbb{F})
is a lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. The shape of
\mathbb{F}
, denoted
s(\mathbb{F})
, is defined as the
\operatorname{O}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})
-orbit of the lattice
\left<P\Lambda(\mathbb{F})\right>
inside
X_{n-1}
. It is thus an element of the double quotient
\operatorname{O}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\operatorname{SL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{SL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{Z})
. This double quotient is called the space of
n-1
-dimensional shapes and is denoted
\mathcal{S}_{n-1}
. Let
m_{\mathcal{S}_{n-1}}
be the measure on
\mathcal{S}_{n-1}
coming from the Haar measure on
\operatorname{SL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})
. Bhargava-Harron prove:

Theorem 1.6

(
[
3
]
, Theorem 1)
.

Let
n=3,4,5
. Then:

\frac{1}{\lvert\cup_{s}\mathcal{F}^{s}_{n}(T)\rvert}\sum_{\mathbb{F}\in\cup_{s}\mathcal{F}^{s}_{n}(T)}\delta_{s(\mathbb{F})}\longrightarrow m_{\mathcal{S}_{n-1}}\text{ weakly as }T\longrightarrow\infty.

(1.5)

In a nutshell, we will explain why
q_{E}(\mathbb{F})
contains more information than
s(\mathbb{F})
and how to use it to deduce Bhargava-Harron’s result from either one of Theorems
1.4
,
1.5

Y_{1}^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}\xrightarrow{\pi_{1}}X_{n-1}\xrightarrow{\pi_{2}}\mathcal{S}_{n-1}

(1.6)

defined for any
\Lambda\in Y_{1}^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}
by
\pi_{1}(\Lambda)=\left<P_{V_{0}}(\Lambda)\right>
and for any
\Lambda\in X_{n-1}
by
\pi_{2}(\Lambda)=\operatorname{O}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})\Lambda
. Recall that
q_{E}(\mathbb{F})
was defined as
g^{t}_{E,\ell_{\overline{1}}}\left<\Lambda(\mathbb{F})\right>
where

g^{t}_{E,\ell_{\overline{1}}}\left<\Lambda(\mathbb{F})\right>\in Y_{1}^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}

have the same projection on

s(\mathbb{F})
was defined as the

s(\mathbb{F})=\pi_{2}\circ\pi_{1}(q_{E}(\mathbb{F})).

(1.7)

Let
m_{X_{n-1}}
be the natural measure on
X_{n-1}

\operatorname{SL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})

(\pi_{1})_{*}m_{Y_{1}^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}}
and
(\pi_{2})_{*}m_{X_{n-1}}

(\pi_{1})_{*}m_{Y_{1}^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}}=m_{X_{n-1}}\text{ and }(\pi_{2})_{*}m_{X_{n-1}}=m_{\mathcal{S}_{n-1}}.

(1.8)

Moreover, the probability measures
\mu_{i}=\frac{1}{\mu(F_{i})}\mu\mid_{F_{i}}

G_{0}(\ell_{\overline{1}}):=\{g\in\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R}):gv=v\text{ for all }v\in\ell_{\overline{1}}\text{ and }gV_{0}=V_{0}\}

We deduce that for any

(\pi_{1})_{*}\mu_{i}=m_{X_{n-1}}

(1.10)

which together with Equation (

) implies for any

(\pi_{2}\circ\pi_{1})_{*}m_{Y_{1}^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}}=(\pi_{2}\circ\pi_{1})_{*}\mu_{i}=m_{\mathcal{S}_{n-1}}.

(1.11)

Fix a signature
(r,s)
. By equation (

) for any

\frac{1}{\lvert\mathcal{F}^{s}_{n}(T)\rvert}\sum_{\mathbb{F}\in\mathcal{F}^{s}_{n}(T)}\delta_{s(\mathbb{F})}=\frac{1}{\lvert\mathcal{F}^{s}_{n}(T)\rvert}\sum_{\mathbb{F}\in\mathcal{F}^{s}_{n}(T)}\delta_{\pi_{2}\circ\pi_{1}(q_{E}(\mathbb{F}))}=(\pi_{2}\circ\pi_{1})_{*}\left(\frac{1}{\lvert\mathcal{F}^{s}_{n}(T)\rvert}\sum_{\mathbb{F}\in\mathcal{F}^{s}_{n}(T)}\delta_{q_{E}(\mathbb{F})}\right)

\frac{1}{\lvert\mathcal{F}^{s}_{n}(T)\rvert}\sum_{\mathbb{F}\in\mathcal{F}^{s}_{n}(T)}\delta_{s(\mathbb{F})}\longrightarrow(\pi_{2}\circ\pi_{1})_{*}m_{Y_{1}^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}}=m_{\mathcal{S}_{n-1}}\text{ weakly as }T\longrightarrow\infty.

1.2
Testing the Construction on Quadratic Number Fields

Quadratic number fields are well studied objects and their rings of integers can be fully described. Therefore, we would like to motivate our construction by examining it for quadratic number fields
\mathbb{F}
. More specifically, we would like to analyze the ’asymptotic portion’ of quadratic number fields with predetermined geometric behaviour phrased using
q_{E}
. The following theorem summerizes this analysis:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
n=2
and let
(r,s)
be a signature. Let
E
be a 1-dimensional subspace in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
E\neq\ell_{\overline{1}},V_{0}
. Then:

\frac{1}{\lvert\mathcal{F}^{s}_{2}(T)\rvert}\sum_{\mathbb{F}\in\mathcal{F}^{s}_{2}(T)}\delta_{q_{E}(\mathbb{F})}\longrightarrow m_{Y_{1}^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}}\text{ weakly as }T\longrightarrow\infty.

(1.16)

When
E=V_{0}
there exists a periodic orbit
\{p_{1},p_{2}\}\subset Y_{1}^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}
and
t\in(0,1)
such that:

\frac{1}{\lvert\mathcal{F}^{s}_{2}(T)\rvert}\sum_{\mathbb{F}\in\mathcal{F}^{s}_{2}(T)}\delta_{q_{E}(\mathbb{F})}\longrightarrow t\delta_{p_{1}}+(1-t)\delta_{p_{2}}\text{ weakly as }T\longrightarrow\infty.

(1.17)

Theorem
1.7
is a simple test case due to the complete description of rings of integers in quadratic fields. We use this description to sketch a proof of the theorem.

For simplicity of this exposition, we limit ourselves to the case
s=0
. Firstly, it is easy to see that when
n=2
,
Y_{1}^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}
is isomorphic to the unit circle
S^{1}
and that
m_{Y_{1}^{\ell_{\overline{1}}}}
is the Lebesgue measure
\lambda
on
S^{1}
. Secondly, let
D
be a square free natural number (nonnegative, because
s=0
). Given
D=1\mod 4
, denote
\omega=\frac{1+\sqrt{D}}{2}
. It is well known that:

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})}=\{a+b\omega:a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

(1.18)

Let
\overline{u}=(1,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
u\neq-1
and let
E=\overline{u}^{\perp}
. Using this description,
q_{E}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D}))
can be calculated to be (This calculation is carried in grated generality in the proof of Lemma
2.21
below, we refer the reader to this proof for reference):

q_{E}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D}))=\frac{1}{2(1+u)}\left(1+u+(1-u)\sqrt{D}\right)\operatorname{mod}1\in S^{1}.

(1.19)

Denote for any
T>0
and
i=0,\dots,3
:

\mathcal{D}_{T}(i)=\{0<D<T:\text{ }D\text{ is a square free natural number and }D=i\mod 4\}.

(1.20)

Assume further that
u\neq 1
so that
\overline{u}\neq\overline{1}
. Then classical asymptotic estimations on the density of square free integers (see
[
9
, Theorem 8.2.1]
) and equation (
1.19
) show that:

\frac{1}{\lvert\mathcal{D}_{T}(1)\rvert}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{T}(1)}\delta_{q_{E}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D}))}\longrightarrow\lambda\text{ as }T\longrightarrow\infty.

(1.21)

When
D=2,3\mod 4
the description of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
is different (and also well known), but also shows in the same way that:

\frac{1}{\lvert\mathcal{D}_{T}(2)\cup\mathcal{D}_{T}(3)\rvert}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{T}(2)\cup\mathcal{D}_{T}(3)}\delta_{q_{E}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D}))}\longrightarrow\lambda\text{ as }T\longrightarrow\infty.

(1.22)

Averaging equations (
1.21
) and (
1.22
) implies the first statement of Theorem
1.7
.

When
\overline{u}=\overline{1}
and
D=1\operatorname{mod}4
is square free, equation (
1.19
) says
q_{V_{0}}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D}))=q_{E}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D}))=1/2+\mathbb{Z}
. When
D=2,3\operatorname{mod}4
the same calculation leading to equation (
1.19
) which as we said is given in Lemma
2.21
below, says that
q_{V_{0}}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D}))=q_{E}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D}))=\mathbb{Z}
. From this it is immediate to deduce that for some
t\in(0,1)
(related to the density of
\mathcal{D}_{T}(1)
in
\mathbb{Z}
):

\frac{1}{\lvert\mathcal{D}_{T}(1)\cup\mathcal{D}_{T}(2)\cup\mathcal{D}_{T}(3)\rvert}\sum_{D\in\mathcal{D}_{T}(1)\cup\mathcal{D}_{T}(2)\cup\mathcal{D}_{T}(3)}\delta_{q_{V_{0}}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D}))}\longrightarrow t\delta_{0}+(1-t)\delta_{1/2}\text{ as }T\longrightarrow\infty

(1.23)

proving equation (
1.17
) and the second statement of Theorem
1.7
.

2
The Ingredients

2.1
Notation

Given a natural number
n
, a ring of rank
n
over another ring
R
is a ring which is also a free
R
-module. Given a ring
R
, we denote
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(R)
to be the space of all invertible
n\times n
matrices with entries from
R
. The subset of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}(R)
of all matrices with determinant
1
is denoted
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(R)
.
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle)
denotes the standard inner product structure of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Given a linear subspace
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote

Lemma B.1
.

Let
W\subset Y_{n}
be a measurable set with
\mu(\partial W)=0
. Then for every
\epsilon>0
there exist
B_{1},\dots,B_{m}\subset W
disjoint basic subsets, such that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu(B_{i})\geq\mu(W)-\epsilon
.

Proof.

We may assume that
W
is bounded, since otherwise we may intersect
W
with large enough bounded ball in
Y_{n}
. Let
U\supset\partial W
be open such that
\mu(U)\leq\epsilon
. For any
x\in\overline{W\setminus U}
, let
B_{x}\subset\text{int}(W)
be a Basic subset. Take a finite sub-cover of
\overline{W\setminus U}
,
(B_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
. Then clearly the
B_{i}
’s are included in
W
(they are not necessarily disjoint), and:

\mu\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}B_{i}\right)\geq\mu(W)-\mu(W\cap U)\geq\mu(W)-\mu(U)\geq\mu(W)-\epsilon.

Finally, note that the intersection of every two basic subsets can be estimated by finite union of disjoint basic subsets with arbitrarily small defect, so we may also assume that the
B_{i}
’s are disjoint.
∎

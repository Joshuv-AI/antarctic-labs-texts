Abstract
. In this note, we obtain a reverse version of the integral Hardy inequality on metric measure spaces. Moreover, we give necessary and sufficient conditions for the weighted reverse Hardy inequality to be true. The main tool in our proof is a continuous version of the reverse Minkowski inequality. Also, we present some consequences of the obtained reverse Hardy inequality on the homogeneous groups, hyperbolic spaces and Cartan-Hadamard manifolds.

22E30, 43A80.

1.
Introduction

In one of the pioneering papers of Hardy
[
12
]
, he proved the following (direct) inequality:

(1.1)

\int_{a}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x^{p}}\left(\int_{a}^{\infty}f(t)dt\right)^{p}dx\leq\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{p}\int_{a}^{\infty}f^{p}(x)dx,

where
f\geq 0
,
p>1
, and
a>0
. Today’s literature on the development of the extensions of this integral Hardy inequality is very large, see e.g.
[
3
,
5
,
15
,
16
]
and
[
20
]
. Note that the multi-dimensional version of the integral Hardy inequality was proved in
[
4
]
.

In
[
2
]
, the authors obtained the so-called reverse integral Hardy inequality in the following form:

(1.2)

\left(\int^{b}_{a}\left(\int_{a}^{x}f(t)dt\right)^{q}u(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq C\left(\int^{b}_{a}f^{p}(x)v(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

and the conjugate reverse integral Hardy inequality

(1.3)

\left(\int^{b}_{a}\left(\int_{x}^{b}f(t)dt\right)^{q}u(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq C\left(\int^{b}_{a}f^{p}(x)v(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
f\geq 0,
for some positive weights
u,v
and
p,q<0
. The reverse Hardy inequalities were also studied in
[
10
,
14
,
17
]
and
[
21
]
.

The main aim of the present paper is to extend the reverse Hardy inequalities to general metric measure spaces. More specifically, we consider metric spaces
\mathbb{X}
with a Borel measure
dx
allowing for the following
polar decomposition
at
a\in{\mathbb{X}}
: we assume that there is a locally integrable function
\lambda\in L^{1}_{loc}
such that for all
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{X})
we have

(1.4)

\int_{\mathbb{X}}f(x)dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Sigma_{r}}f(r,\omega)\lambda(r,\omega)d\omega dr,

for the set
\Sigma_{r}=\{x\in\mathbb{X}:d(x,a)=r\}\subset\mathbb{X}
with a measure on it denoted by
d\omega
, and
(r,\omega)\rightarrow a
as
r\rightarrow 0
.

The condition (
1.4
) is rather general since we allow the function
\lambda
to depend on the whole variable
x=(r,\omega)
. The reason to assume (
1.4
) is that since
\mathbb{X}
does not have to have a differentiable structure, the function
\lambda(r,\omega)
can not be in general obtained as the Jacobian of the polar change of coordinates. However, if such a differentiable structure exists on
\mathbb{X}
, the condition (
1.4
) can be obtained as the standard polar decomposition formula.
In particular, let us give several examples of
\mathbb{X}
for which the condition (
1.4
) is satisfied with different expressions for
\lambda(r,\omega)
:

(I)

Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:
\lambda(r,\omega)={r}^{n-1}.

(II)

Homogeneous groups:
\lambda(r,\omega)={r}^{Q-1}
, where
Q
is the homogeneous dimension of the group. Such groups have been consistently developed by Folland and Stein
[
7
]
, see also an up-to-date exposition in
[
6
]
.

(III)

Hyperbolic spaces
\mathbb{H}^{n}
:
\lambda(r,\omega)=(\sinh{r})^{n-1}
.

(IV)

Cartan-Hadamard manifolds: Let
K_{M}
be the sectional curvature on
(M,g).
A Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is called
a Cartan-Hadamard manifold
if it is complete, simply connected and has non-positive sectional curvature, i.e., the sectional curvature
K_{M}\leq 0
along each plane section at each point of
M
. Let us fix a point
a\in M
and denote by

\rho(x)=d(x,a)
the geodesic distance from
x
to
a
on
M
. The exponential map
{\rm exp}_{a}:T_{a}M\to M
is a diffeomorphism, see e.g. Helgason
[
13
]
. Let
J(\rho,\omega)
be the density function on
M
, see e.g.
[
8
]
. Then we have the following polar decomposition:

\int_{M}f(x)dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f({\rm exp}_{a}(\rho\omega))J(\rho,\omega)\rho^{n-1}d\rho d\omega,

so that we have (
1.4
) with
\lambda(\rho,\omega)=J(\rho,\omega)\rho^{n-1}.

In
[
22
]
, the (direct) integral Hardy inequality on metric measure space was established with applications to homogeneous Lie groups, hyperbolic spaces, Cartan-Hadamard manifolds with negative curvature and on general Lie groups with Riemannian distance. Also, on Riemannian manifolds Hardy inequality was obtained in
[
28
]
. In the present paper, we continue the analysis in general setting of metric measure spaces as in
[
22
]
and show the reverse integral Hardy inequality with
q<0
and
p\in(0,1)
and we also discuss its consequences for homogeneous Lie groups, hyperbolic spaces and Cartan-Hadamard manifolds with negative curvature.

2.
Main result

Let us recall briefly the reverse Hölder’s inequality.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
1
]
, Theorem 2.12, p. 27)
.

Let
p\in(0,1)
, so that
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}<0
. If non-negative functions satisfy
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{X})
and
0<\int_{\mathbb{X}}g^{p^{\prime}}(x)dx<+\infty,
we have

(2.1)

\int_{\mathbb{X}}f(x)g(x)dx\geq\left(\int_{\mathbb{X}}f^{p}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{X}}g^{p^{\prime}}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}.

Let us present the reverse integral Minkowski inequality (or a continuous version of reverse Minkowski inequality).

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y}
be metric measure spaces and let
F=F(x,y)\in\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y}
be a non-negative measurable function. Then we have

(2.2)

\left[\int_{\mathbb{X}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{Y}}F(x,y)dy\right)^{q}dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq\int_{\mathbb{Y}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{X}}F^{q}(x,y)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}dy,\;q<0.

Proof.

Let us consider the following function:

(2.3)

A(x):=\int_{\mathbb{Y}}F(x,y)dy,

so we have

(2.4)

A^{q}(x)=\left(\int_{\mathbb{Y}}F(x,y)dy\right)^{q}.

By integrating over
\mathbb{X}
both sides and by using reverse Hölder’s inequality (Theorem
2.1
), we obtain

(2.5)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{X}}A^{q}(x)dx&=\int_{\mathbb{X}}A^{q-1}(x)A(x)dx\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{X}}A^{q-1}(x)\int_{\mathbb{Y}}F(x,y)dydx\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{Y}}\int_{\mathbb{X}}A^{q-1}(x)F(x,y)dxdy\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{Holin})}}{{\geq}}\int_{\mathbb{Y}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{X}}A^{q-1\frac{q}{q-1}}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{q-1}{q}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{X}}F^{q}(x,y)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}dy\\
&=\left(\int_{\mathbb{X}}A^{q}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{q-1}{q}}\int_{\mathbb{Y}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{X}}F^{q}(x,y)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}dy.\end{split}

From this, we get

(2.6)

\left[\int_{\mathbb{X}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{Y}}F(x,y)dy\right)^{q}dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq\int_{\mathbb{Y}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{X}}F^{q}(x,y)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}dy,

proving (
2.2
).
∎

Remark 2.3
.

In our sense, the negative exponent
q<0
of
0
, we understand in the following form:

(2.7)

0^{q}=(+\infty)^{-q}=+\infty,\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,\,\,0^{-q}=(+\infty)^{q}=0.

We denote by
B(a,r)
the ball in
\mathbb{X}
with centre
a
and radius
r
, i.e

B(a,r):=\{x\in\mathbb{X}:d(x,a)<r\},

where
d
is the metric on
\mathbb{X}
. Once and for all we will fix some point
a\in\mathbb{X}
, and we
will write

(2.8)

|x|_{a}:=d(a,x).

Now we prove the reverse integral Hardy inequality on a metric measure space.

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume that
p\in(0,1)
and
q<0
. Let
\mathbb{X}
be a metric measure space with a polar decomposition at
a\in\mathbb{X}
. Suppose that
u,v>0
are locally integrable functions on
\mathbb{X}
. Then the inequality

(2.9)

\left[\int_{\mathbb{X}}\left(\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}f(y)dy\right)^{q}u(x)dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq C(p,q)\left(\int_{\mathbb{X}}f^{p}(x)v(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

holds for some
C(p,q)>0
and for all non-negative real-valued measurable functions
f
, if and only if

(2.10)

0<D_{1}:=\inf_{x\neq a}\left[\left(\int_{\mathbb{X}\setminus B(a,|x|_{a})}u(y)dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}v^{1-p^{\prime}}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\right].

Moreover, the biggest constant
C(p,q)
in (
2.9
) has the following relation to
D_{1}
:

(2.11)

D_{1}\geq C(p,q)\geq\left(\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}+q}\right)^{-\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{q}{p^{\prime}+q}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}D_{1}.

Proof.

Let us divide proof of this theorem in several steps.

Step 1.
Let us denote
g(x):=f(x)v^{\frac{1}{p}}(x)
. Let
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1,

\alpha\in\left(0,-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\right)
and
z(x)=v^{-\frac{1}{p}}(x)
. Let us denote, using (
1.4
),

(2.12)

\displaystyle V(x):=\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}v^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}(y)dy=\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}z^{p^{\prime}}(y)dy,

(2.13)

\displaystyle H_{1}(s):=\int_{\sum_{s}}\lambda(s,\sigma)g(s,\sigma)z(s,\sigma)d\sigma,

(2.14)

\displaystyle H_{2}(s):=\int_{\sum_{s}}\lambda(s,\sigma)z^{p^{\prime}}(s,\sigma)V^{\alpha p^{\prime}}(s,\sigma)d\sigma,

(2.15)

\displaystyle H_{3}(s):=\int_{\sum_{s}}\lambda(s,\sigma)g^{p}(s,\sigma)V^{-\alpha p}(s,\sigma)d\sigma,

(2.16)

\displaystyle U(r):=\int_{\sum_{r}}\lambda(r,\omega)u(r,\omega)d\omega.

After some calculation, we compute, using reverse Hölder’s inequality (Theorem
2.1

(2.17)

\begin{split}A:&=\int_{\mathbb{X}}\left(\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}f(y)dy\right)^{q}u(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{X}}\left(\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}g(y)z(y)dy\right)^{q}u(x)dx\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{X}}\left(\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}g(y)z(y)dy\right)^{p}\left(\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}g(y)z(y)dy\right)^{q-p}u(x)dx\\

&\geq\int_{\mathbb{X}}\left(\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}g^{p}(y)V^{-\alpha p}(y)dy\right)\left(\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}z^{p^{\prime}}(y)V^{\alpha p^{\prime}}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&\times\left(\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}g(y)z(y)dy\right)^{q-p}u(x)dx\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}U(r)\left(\int_{0}^{r}H_{1}(s)ds\right)^{q-p}\left(\int_{0}^{r}H_{2}(s)ds\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{0}^{r}H_{3}(s)ds\right)dr.\end{split}

Let us denote by
\tilde{H}_{2}(s):=\int_{\sum_{s}}\lambda(s,\sigma)z^{p^{\prime}}(s,\sigma)d\sigma.
Then we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{r}H_{2}(s)ds\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{r}\int_{\sum_{s}}\lambda(s,\sigma)z^{p^{\prime}}(s,\sigma)V^{\alpha p^{\prime}}(s,\sigma)dsd\sigma\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{r}\int_{\sum_{s}}\lambda(s,\sigma)z^{p^{\prime}}(s,\sigma)\left(\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\sum_{\rho}}\lambda(\rho,\sigma_{1})z^{p^{\prime}}(\rho,\sigma_{1})d\rho d\sigma_{1}\right)^{\alpha p^{\prime}}dsd\sigma\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}

(2.18)

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{r}\tilde{H}_{2}(s)\left(\int_{0}^{s}\tilde{H}_{2}(\rho)d\rho\right)^{\alpha p^{\prime}}ds\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{r}\left(\int_{0}^{s}\tilde{H}_{2}(\rho)d\rho\right)^{\alpha p^{\prime}}d_{s}\left(\int_{0}^{s}\tilde{H}_{2}(\rho)d\rho\right)\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1+\alpha p^{\prime}>0}}{{=}}\frac{1}{(1+\alpha p^{\prime})^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}}\left(\left(\int_{0}^{s}\tilde{H}_{2}(\rho)d\rho\right)^{1+\alpha p^{\prime}}\big|_{0}^{r}\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1+\alpha p^{\prime}>0}}{{=}}\frac{1}{(1+\alpha p^{\prime})^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}}\left(\int_{0}^{r}\tilde{H}_{2}(\rho)d\rho\right)^{\frac{p(1+\alpha p^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{V_{1}^{\frac{p(1+\alpha p^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}}}(r)}{(1+\alpha p^{\prime})^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}},

where
V_{1}(r)=\int_{0}^{r}\tilde{H}_{2}(\rho)d\rho
. By using this fact and reverse Hölder’s inequality with
\frac{p}{q}+\frac{q-p}{q}=1
, we obtain

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{r}H_{3}(s)ds\right)U(r)\left(\int_{0}^{r}H_{1}(s)ds\right)^{q-p}\left(\int_{0}^{r}H_{2}(s)ds\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}dr

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{2.11})}}{{=}}\frac{1}{(1+\alpha p^{\prime})^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{r}H_{3}(s)ds\right)U(r)\left(\int_{0}^{r}H_{1}(s)ds\right)^{q-p}V_{1}^{\frac{p(1+\alpha p^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}}}(r)dr

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1+\alpha p^{\prime})^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}U^{\frac{p}{q}}(r)\left(\int_{0}^{r}H_{3}(s)ds\right)V_{1}^{\frac{p(1+\alpha p^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}}}(r)\left(\int_{0}^{r}H_{1}(s)ds\right)^{q-p}U^{\frac{q-p}{q}}dr

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{(1+\alpha p^{\prime})^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{r}H_{3}(s)ds\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}U(r)V_{1}^{\frac{q(1+\alpha p^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}}}(r)dr\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{r}H_{1}(s)ds\right)^{q}U(r)dr\right)^{\frac{q-p}{q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1+\alpha p^{\prime})^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}U(r)\left(\int_{0}^{r}H_{3}(s)ds\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}V_{1}^{\frac{q(1+\alpha p^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}}}(r)dr\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{\mathbb{X}}\left(\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}g(y)z(y)dy\right)^{q}u(x)dx\right)^{\frac{q-p}{q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{A^{\frac{q-p}{q}}}{(1+\alpha p^{\prime})^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}U(r)\left(\int_{0}^{r}H_{3}(s)ds\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}V_{1}^{\frac{q(1+\alpha p^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}}}(r)dr\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}.

Therefore,

A^{\frac{p}{q}}\geq\frac{1}{(1+\alpha p^{\prime})^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}U(r)\left(\int_{0}^{r}H_{3}(s)ds\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}V_{1}^{\frac{q(1+\alpha p^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}}}(r)dr\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}.

Let us treat the following integral with reverse Minkowski inequality with exponent
\frac{q}{p}<0
, so that we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}U(r)\left(\int_{0}^{r}H_{3}(s)ds\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}V_{1}^{\frac{q(1+\alpha p^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}}}(r)dr\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{r}U^{\frac{p}{q}}(r)H_{3}(s)V^{\frac{(1+\alpha p^{\prime})p}{p^{\prime}}}(r)ds\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}dr\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}U^{\frac{p}{q}}(r)H_{3}(s)V_{1}^{\frac{(1+\alpha p^{\prime})p}{p^{\prime}}}(r)\chi_{\{s<r\}}ds\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}dr\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{minkow})}}{{\geq}}\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{3}(s)\left(\int_{s}^{\infty}U(r)V_{1}^{\frac{q(1+\alpha p^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}}}(r)dr\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{X}}g^{p}(y)V^{-\alpha p}(y)\left(\int_{\mathbb{X}\setminus B(a,|y|_{a})}u(x)V^{\frac{q(1+\alpha p^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}}}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}dy

\displaystyle\geq D^{p}(\alpha)\int_{\mathbb{X}}g^{p}(y)dy,

where
D(\alpha):=\inf_{x\neq a}D(x,\alpha)=\inf_{x\neq a}V^{-\alpha}(x)\left(\int_{\mathbb{X}\setminus B(a,|x|_{a})}u(y)V^{\frac{q(1+\alpha p^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}}}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}
and

Then we obtain

\displaystyle A^{\frac{p}{q}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{X}}\left(\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}f(y)dy\right)^{q}u(x)dx\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\geq\frac{D^{p}(\alpha)}{(1+\alpha p^{\prime})^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}}\int_{\mathbb{X}}g^{p}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{D^{p}(\alpha)}{(1+\alpha p^{\prime})^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}}\int_{\mathbb{X}}f^{p}(y)v(y)dy.

Step 2.
Let us recall
D_{1}
, given in the following form:

(2.19)

0<D_{1}=\inf_{x\neq a}\left[\left(\int_{\mathbb{X}\setminus B(a,|x|_{a})}u(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}v^{1-p^{\prime}}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\right].

Let us note a relation between
V

V_{1}

\displaystyle V(x)=\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}v^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}z^{p^{\prime}}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}\int_{\sum_{r}}z^{p^{\prime}}(r,\omega)\lambda(r,\omega)drd\omega

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}\tilde{H}_{2}(r)dr

\tilde{H}_{2}(r)=\int_{\sum_{r}}z^{p^{\prime}}(r,\omega)\lambda(r,\omega)d\omega

(2.21)

\begin{split}I&=\int_{\mathbb{X}\setminus B(a,|x|_{a})}u(y)V^{\frac{q(1+\alpha p^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}}}(y)dy=\int_{|x|_{a}}^{\infty}\int_{\sum_{r}}\lambda(r,\omega)u(r,\omega)V^{\frac{q(1+\alpha p^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}}}_{1}(r)drd\omega\\

I=D^{q}(x,\alpha)V^{\alpha q}(x)\leq-\frac{1}{\alpha p^{\prime}}D_{1}^{q}V^{\alpha q}(x)

D(x,\alpha)\geq(-\alpha p^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{q}}D_{1},

Finally, we obtain

A^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq\frac{D_{1}(-\alpha p^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{q}}}{(1+\alpha p^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{X}}f^{p}(y)v(y)dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Let us consider the function

(2.22)

\begin{split}\frac{dk(\alpha)}{d\alpha}&=-\frac{1}{q}(-p^{\prime})(-\alpha p^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{q}-1}(1+\alpha p^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}+\left(-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\right)p^{\prime}(1+\alpha p^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}-1}(-\alpha p^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{q}}\\
&=p^{\prime}(-\alpha p^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{q}-1}(1+\alpha p^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}-1}\left(\frac{(1+\alpha p^{\prime})}{q}+\alpha\right)\\
&=\frac{p^{\prime}}{q}(-\alpha p^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{q}-1}(1+\alpha p^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}-1}\left(\alpha(p^{\prime}+q)+1\right)=0,\end{split}

it implies that its solution is

\alpha_{1}=-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}+q}\in\left(0,-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\right).

By taking the second derivative of
k(\alpha)
at the point
\alpha_{1}
and by denoting
k_{1}(\alpha)=(-\alpha p^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{q}-1}(1+\alpha p^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}-1}
, we obtain

(2.23)

\begin{split}\frac{d^{2}k(\alpha)}{d\alpha^{2}}\big|_{\alpha=\alpha_{1}}&=\left(\frac{p^{\prime}}{q}(-\alpha p^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{q}-1}(1+\alpha p^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}-1}\left(\alpha(p^{\prime}+q)+1\right)\right)^{\prime}\big|_{\alpha=\alpha_{1}}\\
&=\frac{p^{\prime}}{q}\left(k_{1}(\alpha)\left(\alpha(p^{\prime}+q)+1\right)\right)^{\prime}\big|_{\alpha=\alpha_{1}}\\
&=\frac{p^{\prime}}{q}\left(\frac{dk_{1}(\alpha)}{d\alpha}\left(\alpha(p^{\prime}+q)+1\right)+(p^{\prime}+q)k_{1}(\alpha)\right)\big|_{\alpha=\alpha_{1}}\\
&=\frac{p^{\prime}}{q}\left(\frac{dk_{1}(\alpha)}{d\alpha}\big|_{\alpha=\alpha_{1}}\underbrace{\left(\alpha_{1}(p^{\prime}+q)+1\right)}_{=0}+(p^{\prime}+q)k_{1}(\alpha_{1})\right)\\
&=\frac{p^{\prime}(p^{\prime}+q)}{q}k_{1}(\alpha_{1})=\frac{p^{\prime}(p^{\prime}+q)}{q}(-\alpha_{1}p^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{q}-1}(1+\alpha_{1}p^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}-1}\\
&=\underbrace{\frac{p^{\prime}(p^{\prime}+q)}{q}}_{<0}\underbrace{\left(\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}+q}\right)^{-\frac{1}{q}-1}}_{>0}\underbrace{\left(\frac{q}{p^{\prime}+q}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}-1}}_{>0}<0.\end{split}

It means, function
k(\alpha)
has supremum at the point
\alpha=\alpha_{1}
. Then, the biggest constant has the following relationship
C(p,q)\geq\left(\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}+q}\right)^{-\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{q}{p^{\prime}+q}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}D_{1}
.

2.9

which gives

.
∎

3.
Consequences

In this section, we consider some consequences of the reverse integral Hardy inequality.

3.1.
Homogeneous Lie groups

Let us recall that a Lie group (on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
)
\mathbb{G}
with the dilation

D_{\lambda}(x):=(\lambda^{\nu_{1}}x_{1},\ldots,\lambda^{\nu_{n}}x_{n}),\;\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n}>0,\;D_{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n},

which is an automorphism of the group
\mathbb{G}
for each
\lambda>0,

is called a
homogeneous (Lie) group
. For simplicity, throughout this paper we use the notation
\lambda x
for the dilation
D_{\lambda}.
The homogeneous dimension of the homogeneous group
\mathbb{G}
is denoted by
Q:=\nu_{1}+\ldots+\nu_{n}.

Also, in this note we denote a homogeneous quasi-norm on
\mathbb{G}
by
|x|
, which
is a continuous non-negative function

(3.1)

\mathbb{G}\ni x\mapsto|x|\in[0,\infty),

with the properties

i)

|x|=|x^{-1}|
for all
x\in\mathbb{G}
,

ii)

|\lambda x|=\lambda|x|
for all
x\in\mathbb{G}
and
\lambda>0
,

iii)

|x|=0
iff
x=0
.

Moreover, the following polarisation formula on homogeneous Lie groups will be used in our proofs:
there is a (unique)
positive Borel measure
\sigma
on the
unit quasi-sphere

\mathfrak{S}:=\{x\in\mathbb{G}:\,|x|=1\},

so that for every
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G})
we have

(3.2)

\int_{\mathbb{G}}f(x)dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}f(ry)r^{Q-1}d\sigma(y)dr.

We refer to
[
7
]
for the original appearance of such groups, and to
[
6
]
for a recent comprehensive treatment.
Let us define the quasi-ball centered at
x
with radius
r
in the following form:

(3.3)

B(x,r):=\{x\in\mathbb{G}:|x^{-1}y|<r\}.

Then we have the following reverse integral Hardy inequality on homogeneous Lie groups.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a homogeneous Lie group of homogeneous dimension
Q
with a quasi-norm
|\cdot|
. Assume that
q<0
,
p\in(0,1)
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the reverse integral Hardy inequality

(3.4)

\left[\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left(\int_{B(0,|x|)}f(y)dy\right)^{q}|x|^{\alpha}dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}f^{p}(x)|x|^{\beta}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

holds for
C>0
and for all non-negative measurable functions
f
, if only if

(3.5)

\alpha+Q<0,\,\,\,\beta(1-p^{\prime})+Q>0\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\frac{Q+\alpha}{q}+\frac{Q+\beta(1-p^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}}=0.

Moreover, the biggest constant
C
for (
3.4
) satisfies

(3.6)

\begin{split}&\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{|\alpha+Q|}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q+\beta(1-p^{\prime})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\geq C\\
&\geq\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{|\alpha+Q|}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q+\beta(1-p^{\prime})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}+q}\right)^{-\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{q}{p^{\prime}+q}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}},\end{split}

where
|\mathfrak{S}|
is the area of unit sphere with respect to
|\cdot|
.

Proof.

Let us check condition (
2.10
) with
u(x)=|x|^{\alpha}
,
v(x)=|x|^{\beta}
and with
a=0
. Let us calculate the first integral in (
2.10
):

(3.7)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{G}\setminus B(0,|x|)}u(y)dy&=\int_{\mathbb{G}\setminus B(0,|x|)}|y|^{\alpha}dy\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{EQ:polar})}}{{=}}\int_{|x|}^{\infty}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}\rho^{\alpha}\rho^{Q-1}d\rho d\sigma(\omega)\\
&=|\mathfrak{S}|\int_{|x|}^{\infty}\rho^{Q+\alpha-1}d\rho\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Q+\alpha<0}}{{=}}-\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q+\alpha}|x|^{Q+\alpha}=\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{|Q+\alpha|}|x|^{Q+\alpha},\end{split}

where
|\mathfrak{S}|
is the area of the unit quasi-sphere in
\mathbb{G}
.
Then,

(3.8)

\begin{split}\int_{B(0,|x|)}v^{1-p^{\prime}}(y)dy&=\int_{B(0,|x|)}|y|^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})}dy\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{EQ:polar})}}{{=}}\int_{0}^{|x|}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}\rho^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})}\rho^{Q-1}d\rho d\sigma(\omega)\\
&=|\mathfrak{S}|\int_{0}^{|x|}\rho^{Q+\beta(1-p^{\prime})-1}d\rho\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Q+\beta(1-p^{\prime})>0}}{{=}}\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q+\beta(1-p^{\prime})}|x|^{Q+\beta(1-p^{\prime})}.\end{split}

Finally by summarising above facts with
\frac{Q+\alpha}{q}+\frac{Q+\beta(1-p^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}}=0
, we have

(3.9)

\begin{split}D_{1}&=\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{|\alpha+Q|}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q+\beta(1-p^{\prime})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\inf_{r>0}r^{\frac{Q+\alpha}{q}+\frac{Q+\beta(1-p^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&=\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{|\alpha+Q|}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q+\beta(1-p^{\prime})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}>0.\end{split}

Then by using (
2.11
), we obtain

(3.10)

\begin{split}&\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{|\alpha+Q|}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q+\beta(1-p^{\prime})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\geq C\\
&\geq\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{|\alpha+Q|}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{Q+\beta(1-p^{\prime})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}+q}\right)^{-\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{q}{p^{\prime}+q}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}},\end{split}

completing the proof.
∎

Then we have conjugate reverse integral Hardy inequality on homogeneous Lie groups.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a homogeneous Lie group of homogeneous dimension
Q
with a quasi-norm
|\cdot|
. Assume that
q<0
,
p\in(0,1)
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the conjugate reverse integral Hardy inequality

(3.11)

\left[\int_{\mathbb{G}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}\setminus B(0,|x|)}f(y)dy\right)^{q}|x|^{\alpha}dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{G}}f^{p}(x)|x|^{\beta}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

holds for
C>0
and for all non-negative measurable functions
f
, if only if

(3.12)

\alpha+Q>0,\,\,\,\beta(1-p^{\prime})+Q<0\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\frac{Q+\alpha}{q}+\frac{Q+\beta(1-p^{\prime})}{p^{\prime}}=0.

Moreover, the biggest constant
C
for (
3.11
) satisfies

(3.13)

\begin{split}&\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{\alpha+Q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{|Q+\beta(1-p^{\prime})|}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\geq C\\
&\geq\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{\alpha+Q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{|\mathfrak{S}|}{|Q+\beta(1-p^{\prime})|}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}+q}\right)^{-\frac{1}{q}}\left(\frac{q}{p^{\prime}+q}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}},\end{split}

where
|\mathfrak{S}|
is the area of unit sphere with respect to
|\cdot|
.

Proof.

Proof of this corollary is similar to the previous case.
∎

3.2.
Hyperbolic space

Let
\mathbb{H}^{n}
be the hyperbolic space of dimension
n
and let
a\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Let us set

(3.14)

u(x)=(\sinh|x|_{a})^{\alpha},\,\,\,\,\,v(x)=(\sinh|x|_{a})^{\beta}.

Then we have the following result of this subsection.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\mathbb{H}^{n}
be the hyperbolic space of dimension
n
and let
a\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Assume that
q<0
,
p\in(0,1)
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the reverse integral Hardy inequality

(3.15)

\left[\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\left(\int_{B(a,|x|_{a})}f(y)dy\right)^{q}(\sinh|x|_{a})^{\alpha}dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}f^{p}(x)(\sinh|x|_{a})^{\beta}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

holds for
C>0
and for all non-negative measurable functions
f,
if

(3.16)

0\leq\alpha+n<1,\,\,\,\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n>0\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\frac{\alpha+n}{q}+\frac{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n}{p^{\prime}}\geq\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}.

Proof.

Let us check condition (
2.10
). By using polar decomposition for the hyperbolic space, we have

(3.17)

D_{1}=\inf_{x\neq a}\left(\int_{|x|_{a}}^{\infty}(\sinh\rho)^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}(\sinh\rho)^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}.

If
\alpha+n<1
and
\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n>0
, then (
3.17
) is integrable. Let us check the finiteness and positiveness of the infimum (
3.17
). Let us divide the proof in two cases.

First case,
|x|_{a}\gg 1
. Then
\sinh|x|_{a}\approx\exp|x|_{a}
if
|x|_{a}\gg 1
. Then we obtain,

(3.18)

\begin{split}D^{1}_{1}&=\inf_{|x|_{a}\gg 1}\left(\int_{|x|_{a}}^{\infty}(\sinh\rho)^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}(\sinh\rho)^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&\simeq\inf_{|x|_{a}\gg 1}\left(\int_{|x|_{a}}^{\infty}(\exp\rho)^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}(\exp\rho)^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&=\inf_{|x|_{a}\gg 1}\left((\exp|x|_{a})^{\alpha+n-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left((\exp|x|_{a})^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&=\inf_{|x|_{a}\gg 1}(\exp|x|_{a})^{\frac{\alpha+n-1}{q}+\frac{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}{p^{\prime}}},\end{split}

infimum of the last term is positive, if only if
\frac{\alpha+n-1}{q}+\frac{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}{p^{\prime}}\geq 0
, i.e.,
\frac{\alpha+n}{q}+\frac{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n}{p^{\prime}}\geq\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}
, then
D^{1}_{1}>0
.

Let us consider the another case
|x|_{a}\ll 1
. For
|x|_{a}\ll 1
we have
\sinh\rho_{\{0\leq\rho<|x|_{a}\}}\approx\rho,
then we calculate

(3.19)

\begin{split}\inf_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}&\left(\int_{|x|_{a}}^{\infty}(\sinh\rho)^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int^{|x|_{a}}_{0}(\sinh\rho)^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&\simeq\inf_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}\left(\int_{|x|_{a}}^{R}(\sinh\rho)^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho+\int_{R}^{\infty}(\sinh\rho)^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int^{|x|_{a}}_{0}\rho^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&\simeq\inf_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}\left(\int_{|x|_{a}}^{R}(\sinh\rho)^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho+\int_{R}^{\infty}(\sinh\rho)^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}|x|_{a}^{\frac{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n}{p^{\prime}}}.\end{split}

Similarly, for small
R
we have
\sinh\rho_{\{|x|_{a}\leq\rho<R\}}\approx\rho,
so that we obtain

(3.20)

\begin{split}\inf_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}&\left(\int_{|x|_{a}}^{\infty}(\sinh\rho)^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int^{|x|_{a}}_{0}(\sinh\rho)^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&\simeq\inf_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}\left(\int_{|x|_{a}}^{R}(\sinh\rho)^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho+\int_{R}^{\infty}(\sinh\rho)^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}|x|_{a}^{\frac{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&\simeq\inf_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}\left(\int_{|x|_{a}}^{R}\rho^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho+\int_{R}^{\infty}(\sinh\rho)^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}|x|_{a}^{\frac{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&\simeq\inf_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}\left(|x|_{a}^{\alpha+n}+C_{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}|x|_{a}^{\frac{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n}{p^{\prime}}}.\end{split}

If
\alpha+n\geq 0
, we have
\frac{\alpha+n}{q}\leq 0
, then we have

(3.21)

\begin{split}D^{2}_{1}&=\inf_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}\left(\int_{|x|_{a}}^{\infty}(\sinh\rho)^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int^{|x|_{a}}_{0}(\sinh\rho)^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&\simeq\inf_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}\left(|x|_{a}^{\alpha+n}+C_{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}|x|_{a}^{\frac{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&\simeq\inf_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}|x|_{a}^{\frac{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n}{p^{\prime}}}>0,\end{split}

and infimum is positive, if only if
\frac{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n}{p^{\prime}}<0
, i.e.,
\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n>0
.
∎

Let us give the reverse conjugate integral Hardy’s inequality in hyperbolic spaces:

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\mathbb{H}^{n}
be the hyperbolic space of dimension
n
and
a\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Assume that
q<0
,
p\in(0,1)
and let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the reverse conjugate integral Hardy inequality

(3.22)

\left[\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{X}\setminus B(a,|x|_{a})}f(y)dy\right)^{q}(\sinh|x|_{a})^{\alpha}dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\geq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}f^{p}(x)(\sinh|x|_{a})^{\beta}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

holds for all non-negative measurable functions
f,
if

\alpha+n>0,\,\,\,1>\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n\geq 0\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\frac{\alpha+n}{q}+\frac{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n}{p^{\prime}}\geq\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}.

Proof.

Similarly to the previous case, check condition (
2.26
) and then, we have

(3.23)

D_{2}=\inf_{x\neq a}\left(\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}(\sinh\rho)^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{|x|_{a}}^{\infty}(\sinh\rho)^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}.

If
\alpha+n>0
and
\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n<1
, then (
3.23
) is integrable.
If
|x|_{a}\gg 1
, we obtain,

(3.24)

\begin{split}D^{1}_{2}&=\inf_{|x|_{a}\gg 1}\left(\int_{|x|_{a}}^{\infty}(\sinh\rho)^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}(\sinh\rho)^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&\simeq\inf_{|x|_{a}\gg 1}\left(\int_{|x|_{a}}^{\infty}(\exp\rho)^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}(\exp\rho)^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&=\inf_{|x|_{a}\gg 1}\left((\exp|x|_{a})^{\alpha+n-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left((\exp|x|_{a})^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&=\inf_{|x|_{a}\gg 1}(\exp|x|_{a})^{\frac{\alpha+n-1}{q}+\frac{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}{p^{\prime}}},\end{split}

infimum of the last term is positive, if only if
\frac{\alpha+n-1}{q}+\frac{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}{p^{\prime}}\geq 0
, i.e.,
\frac{\alpha+n}{q}+\frac{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n}{p^{\prime}}\geq\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}
, then
D^{1}_{1}>0
.

If
|x|_{a}\ll 1
, we obtain

(3.25)

\begin{split}\inf_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}&\left(\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}(\sinh\rho)^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int^{\infty}_{|x|_{a}}(\sinh\rho)^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&\simeq\inf_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}\left(\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}\rho^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{|x|_{a}}^{R}(\sinh\rho)^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho+\int_{R}^{\infty}(\sinh\rho)^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&\simeq\inf_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}\left(\int_{|x|_{a}}^{R}(\sinh\rho)^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho+\int_{R}^{\infty}(\sinh\rho)^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}|x|_{a}^{\frac{\alpha+n}{q}}.\end{split}

Similarly, for small
R
we have
\sinh\rho_{\{|x|_{a}\leq\rho<R\}}\approx\rho,
so that we obtain

(3.26)

\begin{split}\inf_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}&\left(\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}(\sinh\rho)^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int^{\infty}_{|x|_{a}}(\sinh\rho)^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&\simeq\inf_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}\left(\int_{|x|_{a}}^{R}(\sinh\rho)^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho+\int_{R}^{\infty}(\sinh\rho)^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}|x|_{a}^{\frac{\alpha+n}{q}}\\
&\simeq\inf_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}\left(|x|_{a}^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n}+C^{\prime}_{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}|x|_{a}^{\frac{\alpha+n}{q}}.\end{split}

If
\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n\geq 0
, we have
\frac{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n}{q}\leq 0
, then we have

(3.27)

\begin{split}D^{2}_{2}&=\inf_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}\left(\int_{0}^{|x|_{a}}(\sinh\rho)^{\alpha+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int^{\infty}_{|x|_{a}}(\sinh\rho)^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\\
&\simeq\inf_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}\left(|x|_{a}^{\beta(1-p^{\prime})+n}+C^{\prime}_{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}|x|_{a}^{\frac{\alpha+n}{q}}\\
&\simeq\inf_{|x|_{a}\ll 1}|x|_{a}^{\frac{\alpha+n}{q}},\end{split}

and infimum is positive, if only if
\frac{\alpha+n}{q}<0
, i.e.,
\alpha+n>0
.
∎

From axion quality and naturalness problems to a high-quality ℤ N \mathbb{Z}_{N} QCD relaxion Preprint: IPMU22-0051
Abhishek Banerjee ID, Joshua Eby ID, Gilad Perez ID

Abstract
We highlight general issues associated with quality and naturalness problems in theories of light QCD-axions, axion-like particles, and relaxions. We show the presence of Planck-suppressed operators generically lead to scalar coupling of axions with the Standard model. We present a new class of ℤ N \mathbb{Z}_{N} QCD relaxion models that can address both the QCD relaxion CP problem as well as its quality problem. This new class of models also leads to interesting experimental signatures, which can be searched for at the precision frontier.



I
Introduction

The Standard Model of particle physics (SM) is an extremely successful yet incomplete description of nature.
It cannot account for the observed neutrino masses and mixings, the matter anti-matter asymmetry, and the origin of Dark Matter (DM). Even within the framework of the SM, we have the Higgs-hierarchy and the Strong CP problems.
On top of that, the effect of gravity is expected to be significant at the Planck scale despite the lack of knowledge about its quantum nature.
In particular,
quantum gravity is expected to violate global symmetries in the UV, implying the existence of symmetry-breaking operators suppressed by powers of the Planck mass
M_{\rm Pl}=2.4\times 10^{18}\,{\rm GeV}
in the framework of effective field theory (EFT).
For an axion field
\Phi
with a global Peccei-Quinn (PQ) symmetry
Peccei and Quinn 1977
, one for instance expects, among others, operators of the form

{\mathcal{L}}\supset\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{c_{N}\Phi^{N}+{\rm h.c.}}{M_{\rm Pl}^{N}}\right){\mathcal{O}}

(I.1)

where
N
is an integer,
c_{N}
is a dimensionless EFT parameter, and
{\mathcal{O}}
is any dimension-four operator consistent with the unbroken gauge symmetries. Expanding
\Phi=f\,e^{i\,\phi/f}
, this Lagrangian generates a shift-symmetric potential of the form

\displaystyle V_{\Delta}

\displaystyle=|c_{N}|\Delta^{N}\cos\left(\frac{N\,\phi}{f}+\beta\right){\mathcal{O}},

(I.2)

where
\Delta\equiv f/M_{\rm Pl}
and
\beta=\arg(c_{N})
is an arbitrary phase which is generically
{\mathcal{O}}(1)
. Note that, if CP is not broken by gravity then
\beta=0
.
The dimension
N
of the PQ-breaking operator in Eqs. (
I.1
-
I.2
) is dictated by the unbroken gauge symmetries of the underlying theory.

The leading contribution to PQ-breaking arises from a constant operator multiplied by
M_{\rm Pl}^{4}
to match the dimension.
This definition fixes
N>4
so that these operators are suppressed in the limit
M_{\rm Pl}\to\infty
(see e.g.
Hook 2019
;
Di Luzio et al. 2021a
).
This implies a contribution
V_{\Delta}=|c_{N}|\Delta^{N}M_{\rm Pl}^{4}\cos\left(N\phi/f+\beta\right)
to the scalar field theory.
If this field is identified with the QCD axion
Weinberg 1978
;
Wilczek 1978
;
Dine et al. 1981
;
Zhitnitsky 1980
;
Kim 1979
;
Shifman et al. 1980
, then the coefficient
|c_{N}|\Delta^{N}
in Eq. (
I.2
) cannot be too large or else it will spoil the solution to the strong CP problem; this is the so-called
axion quality problem

Kamionkowski and March-Russell 1992
;
Barr and Seckel 1992
;
Ghigna et al. 1992
, and it can be solved by either (a) fine-tuning, e.g. taking
|c_{N}|\ll 1
, (b) taking
f
very small (which is constrained by measurements of axion couplings to matter), or (c) forbidding operators of dimension
N
up to some large value, for example by imposing some unbroken gauge symmetry (e.g.
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
).

We describe the constraints on these operators in greater detail in the subsections below.

II
Axion phenomenology

II.1
Axion-like particles and naturalness

A general axion-like particle (ALP) which is not coupled to QCD does not exhibit a quality problem related to the vacuum structure (see next section), and therefore it might seem that the presence of Planck-suppressed operators would be harmless. However, these same operators can induce large contributions to the ALP mass, leading to a fine-tuning problem.

Planck-suppressed operators can also generate ALP couplings to the SM scalar operators, which we discuss in Section
II.5
.
In the absence of any CP violation, ALPs interact with the SM scalar operators quadratically at the leading order, whereas if gravity does not respect CP, i.e. for
\beta\neq 0
in Eq. (
I.2
), these interactions are generated at linear order.

An ALP is defined by its mass
m
and coupling with the SM pseudoscalar operators. These couplings are associated with an energy scale, which we will identify with
f
.
To analyse the effect of Planck-suppressed operators, we consider an ALP potential of

V_{\rm ALP}(\phi)=-m^{2}f^{2}\cos\left(\frac{\phi}{f}\right),

(II.1)

which defines the ALP mass
m
.
However, the second derivative of the potential induced by Planck-suppressed operators in Eq. (
I.2
) is

\displaystyle V_{\Delta}^{\prime\prime}(\phi)

\displaystyle=|c_{N}|\Delta^{N-2}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\,N^{2}\cos\left(\frac{N\phi}{f}+\beta\right).

(II.2)

Therefore, at leading order in
\phi/f\ll 1
, we have a bare contribution to the mass
m^{2}
and a correction of order

\delta m^{2}\simeq|c_{N}|\cos\beta\,\Delta^{N-2}N^{2}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\,.

(II.3)

Such corrections satisfy
\delta m^{2}\ll m^{2}
only if

\displaystyle\left|\frac{|c_{N}|\cos\beta\,\Delta^{N-2}N^{2}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{m^{2}}\right|=\left|\frac{|c_{N}|\cos\beta\,\Delta^{N}N^{2}M_{\rm Pl}^{4}}{m^{2}f^{2}}\right|\ll 1\,.

(II.4)

Assuming
c_{N}\sim\cos\beta\sim 1
, one can translate the inequality (
II.4
) into an upper bound on
f
as a function of
N
in order to have negligible fine-tuning of the ALP mass. We illustrate these limits for ALP masses
m=1,10^{-7},10^{-14}
eV using the red, blue, and green dotted lines (respectively) in Figure
1
.

\displaystyle V_{a}(\phi)=

At leading order in

V_{a}(\phi)\simeq-\Lambda_{a}^{4}\cos\left(\frac{\phi}{f}+\bar{\theta}\right),

(II.6)

In the presence of the leading Planck-suppressed operator, one can find the minimum of the QCD-axion potential as

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=V^{\prime}(\langle\phi\rangle)

\displaystyle=|c_{N}|\Delta^{N}N\,M_{\rm Pl}^{4}\sin\left(N\epsilon+\beta^{\prime}\right)+\Lambda_{a}^{4}\sin\epsilon

\displaystyle\approx|c_{N}|\Delta^{N}N\,M_{\rm Pl}^{4}\sin\beta^{\prime}+\Lambda_{a}^{4}\epsilon,

(II.7)

where
\epsilon\equiv\langle\phi\rangle/f-\bar{\theta}

\beta^{\prime}\equiv\beta-N\bar{\theta}
which is generically
{\mathcal{O}}(1)
.
Non-observation of the neutron electric dipole moment (EDM) implies that

(see e.g.
Alexandrou et al. 2021

Pignol and Schmidt-Wellenburg 2021
), so in the last step we have expanded in small
\epsilon,\,N\epsilon\ll 1
.
In order to not spoil the QCD axion solution to the strong CP problem, one must require

|\epsilon|=\left|\frac{|c_{N}|\sin\beta^{\prime}\,\Delta^{N}\,NM_{\rm Pl}^{4}}{\Lambda_{a}^{4}}\right|\lesssim 10^{-10}.

(II.8)

At leading order in
N
this gives (for
c_{N}\simeq\sin\beta^{\prime}\simeq 1
)

N\gtrsim\frac{\log\left(10^{-10}(\Lambda_{a}/M_{\rm Pl})^{4}\right)}{\log\left(\Delta\right)}=\frac{201}{19-\log\left(f/10^{10}\,{\rm GeV}\right)},

(II.9)

see also
Holman et al. 1992
.
So for PQ quality to be preserved, one needs to forbid operators with
N\lesssim 10
(
13
) for
f=10^{10}
(
10^{12}
) GeV. A simple way to do this is with a gauged
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
symmetry (see Section
II.3
).

The inequality of (
II.8
) is illustrated by the black solid line in Figure
1
. Comparing the QCD case to an ALP where

, we observe a natural suppression of
10^{-10}N
in the ALP naturalness condition in Eq. (
II.4
), relative to Eq. (
II.8
). Further, ALPs can populate a wider space of values for

and

, allowing for more freedom in parameter inputs. Still, it is intriguing that the requirement of natural ALP mass given in (

As we point out above, generically scalar fields acquire large mass corrections from Planck-suppressed operators. Therefore in principle there is another constraint on the quality of the QCD axion, arising from fine-tuning of the axion mass, though this is always weaker than the constraint above (this was also pointed out in
Holman et al. 1992

II.3
High-quality, natural
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
QCD Axion

It was shown in
Hook 2018
that an extended sector with
N
copies of the SM, related by a
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
symmetry, can lead to a QCD-like axion of mass much smaller than that of canonical QCD axion, due to additional suppression by
\sim z^{N}
in the effective QCD scale. This idea was further investigated in
Di Luzio et al. 2021a
and shown to simultaneously admit a viable ultralight axion DM candidate

symmetry is gauged, it can protect the theory from Planck-suppressed operators in Eq. (
I.1

Let us consider

symmetry which is non-linearly realized by the axion field
\phi

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{N}:\,\,

\displaystyle{\rm SM}_{k}\to{\rm SM}_{k+1({\rm mod}\,N)}

(II.11)

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\phi\to\phi+\frac{2\pi k}{N}f\,,

with
k=0,\cdots,N-1
.
The most general
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
symmetric Lagrangian

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\mathcal{L}=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{L}_{{\rm SM}_{k}}+\frac{\alpha_{s}}{8\pi}\left(\frac{\phi}{f}+\bar{\theta}+\frac{2\pi k}{N}\right)G_{k}\tilde{G}_{k}\,.

(II.12)

The axion will receive contributions from all the
N

\displaystyle V_{\rm tot}(\phi)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}V\left(\frac{\phi}{f}+\bar{\theta}+\frac{2\pi k}{N}\right)\,,

V_{N}(\phi)\simeq-\sqrt{\frac{1-z^{2}}{\pi\,N}}(-z)^{N-1}\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{3}m_{u}\cos\left(\frac{N\,\phi}{f}+\bar{\theta}\right).

II.4
Challenges associated with the QCD relaxion idea

The relaxion framework, proposed in
Graham et al. 2015
provides a new insight on the hierarchy problem, which does not require TeV-scale new physics, but rather implies a non-trivial cosmological evolution of the Higgs mass.
The original relaxion model was based on the QCD axion model
Graham et al. 2015
2
2

2

Espinosa et al. 2015

Fonseca et al. 2018

.

However, as the back-reaction and the rolling potential are sequestered, the relaxion stopping point corresponds to sizeable phase, and generically cannot be set to zero.
It was noticed in the original paper
Graham et al. 2015

Furthermore, as was shown in
Banerjee et al. 2020a
, and further derived below for the QCD-relaxion model, the peculiar nature of the relaxion dynamics implies that the relaxion stops at a highly non-generic point in the field space.
At this point, the mass is parametrically suppressed, and the phase
is predicted to be very close to
\pi/2
,
a mechanism dubbed the
relaxed relaxion

In
Nelson and Prescod-Weinstein 2017
a solution was proposed to this problem; however, it required non-classical evolution of the relaxion and thus, led to further problems associated with the measure problem
Gupta 2018

In addition to that, a successful relaxation of the Higgs mass requires large hierarchy between the scales of the rolling potential and the back-reaction potential
Gupta et al. 2016
and thus, the relaxion setup rely on a carefully designed potential derived from the clockwork mechanism

Choi and Im 2016

Kaplan and Rattazzi 2016

The resulting construction suffers from a fairly severe quality problem, unless the relaxion is rather heavy

In Section
III

II.5
Axion/ALP couplings from unknown Planck physics

As mentioned previously, the Planck-suppressed PQ-breaking operators in Eq. (
I.2
) give rise to SM couplings.
This is, as we discuss below, due to the fact that the additional terms
may be misaligned in phase relative to the terms induced by the IR QCD instantons.
In the presence of CP violation, the resulting couplings can be linear in the field, whereas if CP is conserved the leading couplings are quadratic.
In addition to that, the QCD axion always induces a scalar interaction with the nucleons at the quadratic order of the axion field

III
High-quality QCD relaxion

We combine elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
QCD axion model with the relaxion, in a way that can ameliorate the challenges described in Section
II.4
. The relaxation of the axion field will preserve the QCD axion solution to the strong CP problem, giving rise to a
low-mass
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
QCD axion which also relaxes the electroweak (EW) scale via the relaxion mechanism.

We again consider
N
copies of the SM related by a
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
symmetry, with an effective potential given in Eq. (
II.14
). We will use the fact that the QCD axion potential depends on the Higgs vev through the quark masses, and thus, it can be used as a trigger for the relaxation of the Higgs mass
Graham et al. 2015
;
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2016
.
Note that for our purpose, we will only be interested in the shape of the potential and its dependence on Higgs vev.

Starting from a high-energy cut-off
\Lambda
, the EW scale is set by the dynamics of a axion-like field, usually known as a
relaxion
.
The relaxion-Higgs potential can be written as

\displaystyle V(\phi,H)

\displaystyle=\left(\Lambda^{2}-g\Lambda\phi\right)|H|^{2}+\lambda|H|^{4}

(III.1)

\displaystyle+V_{\rm roll}(\phi)+V_{\rm br}(\phi,\left<H\right>)\,,

where
V_{\rm roll}=-g\Lambda^{3}\phi
(
g
a dimensionless constant) and
V_{\rm br}
is called the “back-reaction” potential as this back-reacts to the motion of the relaxion and is only active when
\langle H\rangle\neq 0
.
In our case, we will take
V_{\rm br}=V_{N}(\phi)
in Eq. (
II.14
), which depends linearly on the Higgs vev through
m_{u}=y_{u}\langle H\rangle
, with
y_{u}
the Yukawa coupling of the up quark, in contrast to the quadratic case discussed in
Banerjee et al. 2020a
.
Note that with this definition, the SM Higgs vacuum expectation value (vev) would be
\langle H\rangle=v\sim 174\,{\rm GeV}
.
See Appendix
A
for general details about the relaxion mechanism and constraints.

In
Banerjee et al. 2020a
, the authors discussed the vacuum structure of the relaxion near the EW scale in detail, and showed that the rolling of the relaxion field stops at the first local minimum of the potential it encounters.
Furthermore, due to the incremental change of the Higgs vev, the relaxion stops at a very shallow part of the potential and thus, its mass is suppressed compared to the naive expected value, a mechanism known as
relaxation
of the axion field.

The relaxion stopping point is determined when the first derivative of
V_{\rm br}(\phi)
is close to its maximum
Banerjee et al. 2020a
.
If, for example,
V_{\rm br}(\phi)\propto\cos(N\theta)
, the relaxion stops around
N\theta_{0}\sim 3\pi/2
for
N
even, and
N\theta_{0}\sim\pi/2
for
N
odd.
In the absence of some breaking of the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
symmetry, this leads to
\mathcal{O}(1)
CP violating phase in all sectors, and is thus ruled out by the neutron EDM experiments unless
N\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{10})
.

In order to successfully solve the strong CP problem, we need to find at least one sector in which effective
\theta_{0}\lesssim\mathcal{O}(10^{-10})
and identify this sector as our SM (this amounts to a linear tuning of
1/N
).
To do that, we will break the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
symmetry explicitly in the
k=0
sector by a small parameter,
4
4

4

k=0
sector with a

.
by requiring
y^{\prime}_{u}>y_{u}^{\rm SM}\sim 10^{-5}

\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{\prime}
would change as well

;
Hook 2015
;
Fukuda et al. 2015
. A possible second source of
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
breaking is a change to the Higgs vev in the

\displaystyle\epsilon_{b}\equiv\frac{y_{u}\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{3}}{y^{\prime}_{u}\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{\prime 3}}\,,\qquad\gamma\equiv\frac{v}{v^{\prime}}\,.

(III.2)

Note that

III.2
Model including NLO corrections

In the toy model above, we only consider the first term of the leading order (LO)

However, there are additional terms in the potential at LO, as well as the higher-harmonic contributions coming from the non-leading-order (NLO) terms. Both of these contributions can shift the vacuum of the SM sector and spoil the mechanism.

\displaystyle 0

\mathbb{Z}_{N}

Here we outline the phenomenological implication of our QCD relaxion.
In our model, the axion has a CP-violating phase of

m_{u}m_{d}/(m_{u}+m_{d})^{2}\simeq 0.22\,

\displaystyle\frac{6\times 10^{-37}}{f_{12}^{2}}\lesssim|g_{p}^{a}g_{\phi NN}|\lesssim\frac{6\times 10^{-36}}{f_{12}^{2}}\,.

IV
Discussion

In this work we analysed how Planck-suppressed (quality) operators affect the low-energy dynamics of theories involving QCD axions or axion like particles (ALPs).
For the QCD axion, the quality operators lead to the well-known
QCD axion quality problem
, whereas for ALPs, they may lead to an equally severe fine-tuning problem.
Quality operators also induce scalar interaction between the Standard Model (SM) fields and QCD axions/ALPs.
In the absence of CP violation, we obtain SM-ALP scalar interaction in quadratic order of the ALP field, whereas if CP is broken by gravity, ALP-SM scalar interactions are generated even at linear order.
These interactions can be probed by various precision experiments.
The strength of the scalar and pseudoscalar interactions are closely related, and therefore these search strategies can complement one another.

We also provide a framework for addressing both the Higgs hierarchy and the strong CP problems together. We invoke a relaxation mechanism where the Higgs mass is scanned during inflation and the QCD axion plays the role of the relaxion.
We show that a
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
-symmetric back-reaction potential which is broken explicitly by a small parameter can address both of these problems simultaneously.
Depending on the symmetry of the dominant sector, one can accomplish this mechanism with underlying symmetries of
\mathbb{Z}_{2{\mathcal{N}}}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{3{\mathcal{N}}}
, with interesting implications for the resulting parameter space.
We show that one of the sectors, identified with the SM, has effective CP violating phase
\theta_{0}\lesssim\mathcal{O}(10^{-10})
. The tuning in the model is linear and of
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{N})
. Our model cannot fully ameliorate the hierarchy problem, as it leaves a little hierarchy to address.
The mass of the QCD relaxion obtained in our model can also be lighter than that of the canonical QCD axion.

Our model can accommodate a CP-violating phase of
1\lesssim\theta_{0}/10^{-10}\lesssim 10
. This range of CP-violating phase is already being probed by neutron electric dipole moment experiments
Abel et al. 2020
, and will be fully probed within the next five years
Filippone 2018
.
Due to the underlying
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
symmetry which can be gauged, this model exhibits better protection against quality operators than the vanilla QCD axion/relaxion models.
Due to the predicted narrow range of CP-violating phase, our model can also be used as target of experiments like ARIADNE
Arvanitaki and Geraci 2014
;
Geraci et al. 2018
and/or QUAX
Crescini et al. 2017
;
Crescini et al. 2022
, which are sensitive to the product of scalar and pseudoscalar interaction of the QCD axion to the SM.
In the case of QCD relaxion dark matter, precision searches can also be applied.
However, further investigation of this possibility is beyond the scope of the current work.

Note added

As this work was finalized,
Chatrchyan and Servant 2022
appeared, which also address issues related to the QCD relaxion by changing the relaxion evolution during inflation. We also note the recent work
Zhang 2022
, which discusses the relationship of Planck-suppressed operators and fifth forces.

Appendix A
Review of the relaxion mechanism

In this section we discuss the relaxation of the Higgs mass parameter.
For the case of QCD relaxion, the back-reaction potential depends linearly on the Higgs vev as opposed to the quadratic case discussed in
Banerjee et al. 2020a
.
A generic back-reaction potential which depends linearly on the Higgs vev can be written as

\displaystyle V_{\rm br}=-\Lambda_{\rm b}^{3}\left<H\right>\cos(\phi/f)\,,

(A.1)

where
\Lambda_{\rm b}
is the back-reaction scale. Following the notation of the main text Eq. (
III.1
), the total relaxion potential can be written as

\displaystyle V(\phi,H)

\displaystyle=\left(\Lambda^{2}-g\Lambda\phi\right)|H|^{2}+\lambda|H|^{4}

\displaystyle-g\Lambda^{3}\phi-\Lambda_{\rm b}^{3}\left<H\right>\cos(\phi/f)\,.

(A.2)

Below we set the Higgs quartic coupling
\lambda=1
for notational convenience.
We are interested in understanding the evolution of the relaxion close to the EW scale
(v)
Higgs mass.
In that case, the minimum of the potential can be found by solving two equations:
\partial V(\phi,H)/\partial|H|=0
and
\partial V(\phi,H)/\partial\phi=0
.
If
|\partial^{2}V/\partial^{2}H|\gg|\partial^{2}V/\partial^{2}\phi|
, then one can set the Higgs at its instantaneous minimum by solving
\partial V(\phi,H)/\partial|H|=0
.
Using perturbation theory around the EW vacuum, one finds the relaxion-dependent Higgs vev as

\displaystyle v^{2}(\theta_{a})=\!\frac{v^{2}}{2}\!\left(\!-\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{v^{2}}\!+\frac{g\Lambda f\theta_{a}}{v^{2}}+\!\frac{\Lambda_{\rm b}^{3}}{2v^{3}}\cos\theta_{a}\!\!\right)\!\!+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\!\frac{\Lambda_{\rm b}^{6}}{v^{6}}\!\right)\!,

where we write
\theta_{a}=\phi/f
. The perturbative expansion of the Higgs vev is valid as long as

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\rm b}^{3}\ll v^{3}\,.

(A.3)

From Eq. (
II.6
) one can see that the above condition is easily satisfied for QCD axion.
Setting the Higgs to its relaxion dependent vev, we obtain the effective potential of the relaxion as

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!V_{\rm eff}(\theta_{a})=-g\Lambda^{3}f\theta_{a}-(v^{2}(\theta_{a}))^{2}-\frac{\Lambda_{\rm b}^{3}v}{2}\cos\theta_{a}\,.

(A.4)

Thus, the relaxion encounters the first minimum when
V^{\prime}_{\rm eff}(\theta_{a})=0
and we find,

\displaystyle-g\Lambda^{3}f-2v^{2}(\theta)v^{2}(\theta)^{\prime}+\frac{\Lambda_{\rm b}^{3}v}{2}\sin\theta_{a}=0\,.

(A.5)

By setting the EW scale as
g\Lambda^{3}f\simeq\Lambda_{\rm b}^{3}v
, and defining a small parameter
\delta^{2}=\Lambda_{\rm b}^{3}/(v\Lambda^{2})\ll 1
, we find that the Higgs vev changes only incrementally as

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta v^{2}}{v^{2}}=\frac{v^{2}(\theta_{a}+2\pi)-v^{2}(\theta_{a})}{v^{2}}=\pi\frac{g\Lambda f}{v^{2}}=\pi\delta^{2}\,.

(A.6)

Following the calculation of
Banerjee et al. 2020a

\theta_{a}\to 2\pi m+\theta_{a}
where
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\theta_{a}\in[0,2\pi)
and then properly adjusting
m
we find,

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!v_{m}^{2}(\theta_{a})=v^{2}\left(1+m\pi\delta^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}\theta_{a}+\frac{\Lambda_{\rm b}^{3}}{4v^{3}}\cos\theta_{a}\right).

(A.7)

From Eq. (
A.5
) we get,

\displaystyle\frac{\sin\theta_{a}}{2}\left(1+\frac{v^{2}}{v_{m}^{2}(\theta_{a})}\right)=\frac{v^{2}}{v_{m}^{2}(\theta_{a})}+\frac{v^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\,.

(A.8)

Note that, the effective potential written before and the above equation is valid only when the Higgs vev is close to
v
. By expanding
v_{m}(\theta_{a})
close to
v
we find, the above equation admits a solution when,

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sin\theta_{a}=1-\frac{m\pi}{2}\delta^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\delta^{2}\theta_{a}-\frac{\Lambda_{\rm b}^{3}}{8v^{3}}\cos\theta_{a}+\frac{v^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\,.

(A.9)

It is easy to see that, the above equation has two solutions close to
\theta_{a}\sim\pi/2
.
As the Higgs vev only increases incrementally with a small parameter
\delta
, we find the relaxion stopping point as

\displaystyle\theta_{a}-\pi/2\equiv\delta_{\theta}=\frac{\Lambda_{\rm b}^{3}}{8v^{3}}+\frac{\delta^{2}}{4}\mp\alpha\,\delta\,,

(A.10)

where
\alpha
is some
\mathcal{O}(1)
number.
The mass of relaxion at the first minimum can be written as

\displaystyle m_{\phi}^{2}=\frac{\Lambda_{\rm b}^{3}v}{f^{2}}\times\delta\,,

(A.11)

significantly reduced by the small parameter
\delta
compared to the naive expected value.

Constraints:
For a successful relaxation of the Higgs mass we require the following conditions:

\displaystyle f\gtrsim\Lambda\gtrsim\Lambda_{\rm min}=4\pi v\,.

(A.12)

Here we are considering scanning of the Higgs mass during inflation.
We require a separate inflaton sector dominates the energy of the universe during inflation and the classical evolution of the relaxion dominates over quantum spreading during inflation.
These two requirements lead (respectively) to the constraints

\displaystyle 3H_{I}^{2}M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\gtrsim\Lambda^{4}\,{\rm and}\,(\Delta\phi)_{\rm cl}=\frac{g\Lambda^{3}}{3H_{I}^{2}}\gtrsim\frac{H_{I}}{2\pi}\,,

(A.13)

where

\displaystyle\frac{8\pi^{2}}{3}(g\Lambda^{3}f)\,\delta^{3}\gtrsim H_{I}^{4}\,.

(A.14)

In the case of a QCD relaxion, the back-reaction potential depends on the temperature and thus, it is only significant when

.
In this section we only consider inflationary based-relaxation of the Higgs mass with a back-reaction potential which depends linearly on Higgs vev.

Appendix B
Full leading order and Higher harmonic corrections

In this section we investigate the back-reaction potential in greater detail.

\displaystyle V_{\rm br}(\phi)\sim

\displaystyle-\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{\prime 3}y^{\prime}_{u}v^{\prime}\cos\left(\frac{\phi}{f}\right)\left(1-\epsilon_{b}\gamma\right)

\displaystyle-\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{3}\,y_{u}v\,\kappa\,\cos\left(\frac{N\phi}{f}\right)\,,

\displaystyle V_{\rm QCD^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{\prime 3}\,y^{\prime}_{q}\,v^{\prime}\left[\,\cos\frac{\theta_{a}}{2}+\frac{y^{\prime}_{q}\,v^{\prime}}{\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{\prime}}\,R\cos\theta_{a}\right],

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Lambda_{\rm b}^{3}v^{\prime}\cos\frac{\theta_{a}}{2}-\alpha^{2}\cos\theta_{a},

\displaystyle V_{\rm br}(\phi)=

\displaystyle V_{\rm QCD^{\prime}}+\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{3}\,y_{u}v\,\sqrt{1+z^{2}+2z\cos(\theta_{a})}

(B.4)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{3}\,y_{u}v\,\kappa\,\cos\left(N\theta_{a}\right)\,.

\displaystyle V_{r}=(\Lambda^{2}-g\Lambda\phi)|H|^{2}+|H|^{4}-g\Lambda^{3}\phi\,,

\displaystyle v^{\prime 2}(\theta_{a})=\frac{v^{\prime 2}}{2}\!\left(\!-\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{v^{\prime 2}}\!+\frac{g\Lambda f\theta_{a}}{v^{\prime 2}}+\!\frac{\Lambda_{\rm b}^{3}}{2v^{\prime 3}}\cos\frac{\theta_{a}}{2}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{v^{\prime 2}}\cos\theta_{a}\!\!\right).

\displaystyle\alpha\ll\Lambda_{\rm b}\ll v^{\prime}.

(B.7)

\displaystyle V_{\rm eff}(\theta_{a})

\displaystyle=-g\Lambda^{3}f\theta_{a}-(v^{\prime 2}(\theta_{a}))^{2}-\frac{\Lambda_{\rm b}^{3}v^{\prime}}{2}\cos\frac{\theta_{a}}{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{3}\,y_{u}v\left[\,\sqrt{1+z^{2}+2z\cos(\theta)}-\kappa\,\cos\left(N\theta_{a}\right)\right]\,.

The first relaxion minima can be found when

\displaystyle\frac{\sin\theta_{a}/2}{2}\left[1+\frac{v^{\prime 2}}{v^{\prime 2}(\theta_{a})}\right]=\frac{v^{\prime 2}}{\Lambda^{2}}+\frac{2\alpha^{2}v^{\prime}}{\Lambda_{\rm b}^{3}}\sin\theta_{a}

\displaystyle+\frac{v^{\prime 2}}{v^{\prime 2}(\theta_{a})}\left[1-N\kappa\epsilon_{b}\gamma\cos(N\theta_{a})+\frac{\epsilon_{b}\gamma z\cos\theta_{a}}{\sqrt{1+z^{2}+2z\cos(\theta)}}\right],

Appendix C
Generating large hierarchy between the confinement scales of the hidden and visible sectors

In this section we discuss how to generate a large hierarchy between the confinement scales of the hidden sector and the SM.
To achieve this, we add additional
\Delta N_{f}
number of heavy vector-like fermions with mass
m_{\rm NP}^{\prime}
and
m_{\rm NP}
in the
k=0
sector and the SM respectively. Adding additional fermions with different masses requires breaking of the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
symmetry.
Thus, in order for the
\mathbb{Z}_{N}
symmetry to be realized for the axion, we require

\displaystyle m_{\rm NP}^{\prime}\,,\,m_{\rm NP}\lesssim f\,,

(C.1)

where
f
is the Peccei-Quinn symmetry breaking scale.

Let us first consider the
k=0
(hidden) sector. With the additional vector-like fermions whose mass is larger than the hidden top quark mass,
m_{t}^{\prime}
, we consider the running of

\alpha_{s}
.
For the energy scale
\mu
,
m_{t^{\prime}}\lesssim\mu\lesssim m_{\rm NP}^{\prime}
, we can write at the 1-loop order,

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{\alpha_{s}(m_{t}^{\prime}+\epsilon)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{\alpha_{s}(f)}-b(n_{f}+\Delta n_{f})\ln\frac{f}{m_{t}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle-\frac{2\Delta n_{f}}{3}\ln\frac{m_{\rm NP}^{\prime}}{m_{t}^{\prime}}\,,

with
b(n)=11-2n/3
. Now for a scale
\Lambda>m_{u}^{\prime},m_{d}^{\prime}
, we can write

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{\alpha_{s}(\Lambda)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{\alpha_{s}(m_{t}^{\prime})}-b(n_{f})\ln\frac{m_{t}^{\prime}}{\Lambda}-\frac{8}{3}\ln\frac{v^{\prime}}{\Lambda}

(C.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{\alpha_{s}(f)}-b(n_{f}+\Delta n_{f})\ln\frac{f}{m_{t}^{\prime}}-\frac{2\Delta n_{f}}{3}\ln\frac{m_{\rm NP}^{\prime}}{m_{t}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle b(n_{f})\ln\frac{m_{t}}{\Lambda}-\frac{8}{3}\ln\frac{v^{\prime}}{\Lambda}\,.

Thus, one obtains the confinement scale,
\Lambda^{\prime}_{\rm IR}
when
\alpha_{s}^{-1}(\Lambda^{\prime}_{\rm IR})\to 0
as,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\rm IR}^{\prime}\sim f\left(\frac{v^{\prime}}{f}\right)^{\alpha_{1}}\left(\frac{m_{\rm NP}^{\prime}}{f}\right)^{\alpha_{2}}\exp[-2\pi/\alpha_{s}(f)]\,,

(C.3)

with

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\alpha_{1}=\frac{\frac{8}{3}}{b(6)+\frac{8}{3}}\simeq 0.28\,,\,\,\alpha_{2}=\frac{\frac{2\Delta n_{f}}{3}}{b(6)+\frac{8}{3}}=\frac{2\Delta n_{f}}{29}\,.

(C.4)

We repeat the same exercise for the SM sector and obtain the same result with
v^{\prime}\to v
and
m^{\prime}_{\rm NP}\to m_{\rm NP}
. We also assume
m_{\rm NP}>m_{t}
where
m_{t}\sim 175\,{\rm GeV}
is the SM top quark mass.
So the ratio of the confinement scale can be obtained as

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{\prime}}{\Lambda_{\rm QCD}}\sim\frac{\Lambda_{\rm IR}^{\prime}}{\Lambda_{\rm IR}}=\left(\frac{v^{\prime}}{v}\right)^{0.28}\left(\frac{m_{\rm NP}^{\prime}}{m_{\rm NP}}\right)^{2\Delta n_{f}/29}\,,

(C.5)

where
\Delta n_{f}
is the number of extra vector like fermions that we are adding.
For,
\Delta n_{f}=4
,
m_{\rm NP}/m_{\rm NP}^{\prime}=10^{-9}
,
v/v^{\prime}=10^{-3}
one obtains

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda_{\rm QCD}}{\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{\prime}}\sim\frac{\Lambda_{\rm IR}}{\Lambda_{\rm IR}^{\prime}}=4.8\times 10^{-4}\,.

(C.6)

The current bound on the vector-like fermion mass scale
m_{\rm NP}
is at the level of
1-2
TeV, though this is somewhat model-dependent (see e.g.
Aaboud et al. 2018
).

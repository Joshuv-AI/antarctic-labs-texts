Radiative Acceleration and X-ray Spectrum of Outflowing Pure Electron-Positron Pair Fireball in Magnetar Bursts
Tomoki Wada, Katsuaki Asano

Abstract
An X-ray short burst associated with a Galactic fast radio burst was observed in 2020, distinguished by its X-ray cut-off energy significantly exceeding that of other X-ray short bursts. X-ray photons of these short bursts are believed to originate from fireballs within the magnetospheres of magnetars. If a fireball forms near a magnetic pole, it expands along the magnetic field lines, subsequently emitting photons and generating plasma outflows that may account for the observed radio bursts. We numerically study the radiative acceleration and X-ray spectrum of such outflowing fireballs consisting of pure electron-positron pairs and radiation, employing spherically symmetric relativistic radiation hydrodynamics calculations with the effects of strong magnetic fields. Using Monte-Carlo scheme in the radiation calculation, we consistently incorporate both the acceleration of the fluid by radiation and the scattering of radiation by the fluid, both of which are enhanced by the cyclotron resonant scattering. Our calculation reveals that cyclotron resonant scattering accelerates the plasma outflow significantly and broadens the X-ray spectrum. The plasma outflow is accelerated up to ultra-relativistic velocities, with Lorentz factors exceeding 100. The calculated X-ray spectrum broadened due to the scattering is similar to the observed X-ray spectrum in the Galactic fast radio burst.

Introduction

Magnetars are neutron stars with X-ray luminosities exceeding their spin-down luminosities

(e.g.,
HarLai2006
;
KasBel2017
;
EnoKis2019_neutronstar
, for reviews)
.
Their spin-down luminosities indicate that the magnetars possess strong magnetic
field
\textcolor
blacks of
\sim 10^{14}
–

, which are

\textcolor
blackthought to be the primary energy source
of the
X-ray luminosities
(

Magnetars
exhibit bursting activities

\textcolor
blacksuch as
short bursts, outbursts, and flares.
The duration of short bursts and flares is roughly
1 s, and they are categorized

\textcolor
blackbased on their isotropic
luminosity

\textcolor
black
\sim 10^{36}
–
10^{43}\,{\rm erg\,s^{-1}}

\textcolor

\sim 10^{44}

The short bursts and the flares are considered to be triggered by a sudden

)

The released energy is promptly thermalized; an electron-positron
pair plasma tightly coupled with photons,

\textcolor

so-called a fireball, is formed.
X-ray photons of short bursts and flares are emitted from this fireball.

\textcolor

blackThe dynamics of fireballs and the X-ray spectrum depend on the shape of the magnetic
field at the largest scale
\textcolor
blackaround the fireball
and on the ratio of the thermal energy density to
the magnetic energy density.
(1) Trapped fireball: If

\textcolor
blacka fireball is formed far from the magnetic pole and the thermal energy
density is much lower than the magnetic energy density, the fireball

\textcolor
blackis likely to be confined in a closed magnetic flux tube.

\textcolor
blackIf this fireball is confined in a closed magnetic field lines of
a dipole magnetic field, the
photon energy

\textcolor
blackemitted from the trapped fireball is typically
\sim 10\,{\rm keV}

(see
ThoDun1995
, for details)
. The X-ray spectrum of the emission
from the trapped fireball has been studied, and some observed X-ray spectra
of short bursts

\textcolor
blackhave been successfully
reproduced
(
Lyu1986
;
Lyu2002
;
YamLyu2020
)
.
The polarization of
X-ray emission is also studied in
YanZha2015
.
(2) Expanding fireball: If the thermal energy density

\textcolor
blackof a fireball
is much higher than the magnetic energy density, the fireball

\textcolor
blackis not confined in the magnetic flux tube and
expand
\textcolor
blacks almost unaffected by the magnetic field.
This type of fireball

\textcolor
blackis thought to be realized in the initial spike of the magnetar flares

(
ThoDun1995
;
ThoDun2001
)
. In this case, the X-ray spectrum is

\textcolor
blackapproximately blackbody, which agrees with the observed magnetar flare
properties
(
HurCli1999
;
HurBog2005
;
BogZog2007
)
.
(3) Outflowing fireball:

\textcolor
blackIf the released energy is much lower than the magnetic energy, an energy
release near the magnetic pole may form an outflowing fireball

\textcolor
black
(see
ThoDun2001
;
Iok2020
;
YanZha2021
, for the detailed scenarios)
. The fireball
confined in the open magnetic flux tube would expand along it
(
ThoDun2001
)
.
For this case, the X-ray spectrum and polarization are numerically studied in

PutWat2016

fast radio bursts (FRBs). FRBs are bright radio bursts with a duration of a few

Tho2013

The origins of almost all FRBs are extragalactic, but there are

;
CHIME2020_200428

FRBs may be emitted from a relativistically outflowing plasma driven by expanding/outflowing
fireballs

YanZha2021
;
WadIok2023

is accelerated by an isotropic radiation pressure. This acceleration mechanism has been studied

;
Goo1986
;
Pac1986
;
ShePir1990
and

Even in an optically thin region, if the radiation luminosity is much higher than the kinetic

;

\textcolor
blackIn magnetar busts,
X-ray photons, responsible for the radiative acceleration,
can be resonantly scattered at the radius where the

energy determined by the magnetic field. Thus, this resonant scattering

In this paper, we numerically demonstrate that plasma outflows are accelerated up to an

\textcolor

We solve the radiation hydrodynamic equations numerically under the assumption that the
fireball is composed of an electron-positron pair plasma and the flow is spherically symmetric
and steady.

\textcolor

Wada and Ioka (2023, hereafter WI23)

shows that a “pure” electron-positron pair fireball, aided by

The Lorentz factor of the baryon-loading fireball is lower than the pair fireball due to the
higher mass density, but only by a factor of
\textcolor
blackapproximately 2
\textcolor
blackas long as the photon
luminosity is an order of magnitude higher than the kinematic luminosity of the plasma
(Figure 5 in WI23).
WI23 also derives analytical expressions for the terminal Lorentz factor
of the outflows. In this paper, we verify their analytical solutions with numerical simulations.
We solve both the radiation and
\textcolor
blackthe plasma outflows and provide steady solutions for them.

4
Conclusions and Discussion

In this paper, we numerically study the X-ray radiation, the plasma outflow, and their coupling
via the cyclotron resonant scattering in outflowing fireballs responsible for magnetar bursts.
For simplicity, we assume a spherically symmetric steady outflow. The plasma is treated as an
anisotropic plasma

\textcolor
blackdue to strong synchrotron cooling (Section
2.2
).
Photon propagation is solved in the magnetized electron-positron pair plasma
(Section
2.3
). The scattering cross section is suppressed

\textcolor
blackby a factor of
\sim(\omega/\omega_{B})^{2}

\textcolor
blackfor
\omega<\omega_{B}
(except in the case that an O-mode photon
scatters into another O-mode).
Photons of both the two modes satisfying
\omega\sim\omega_{B}
are
resonantly scattered. We solve the plasma outflow and the radiation iteratively

\textcolor
blackto find the steady solution

\textcolor
blackfor the plasma and radiation.

We apply our model to the X-ray short burst from a magnetar associated with the
Galactic FRB, FRB 20200428A (Section
3.1
). The X-ray spectrum is modified
by Compton

\textcolor
blackscattering in the relativistic outflow

\textcolor
blackand high-latitude emission from a relativistic outflow.
The resultant spectrum
\textcolor
blackis
within
the
\textcolor
black3-sigma error range of the observed one by the Konus-
Wind
(Figure
4
).
The plasma outflow is accelerated by the radiative force,

\textcolor
blackreaching a four-velocity

\textcolor
blackof
\sim 100

\textcolor
blackfor our fiducial case (Figure
2
).
This
\textcolor
blackfour-velocity is two orders of magnitude higher than the
\textcolor
black
\textcolor
blackfour-velocity of an outflow without the
acceleration

\textcolor
blackabove
r_{\rm ph}
and
one order of magnitude higher than the
\textcolor
black
\textcolor
blackfour-velocity of an outflow without
cyclotron resonant scattering.

\textcolor

blackWe also perform a comprehensive numerical and analytical study of radiative
acceleration via resonant scattering (Sections
3.2
and
3.3
).
We showed that the strong magnetic field enhances the terminal
\textcolor
blackfour-velocity of the outflow.

\textcolor
blackAdditionally, the initial spectrum does not strongly change the terminal
\textcolor
blackfour-velocity
within the models of our simulation (Figure
5
).
These numerical results are roughly consistent with our analytic estimate
(Section
3.3
).

Our numerical simulations show that a relativistic outflow with
\Gamma\sim 100

is always

\textcolor
blackformed in the outflowing fireball near the magnetic pole.
Such relativistic outflows can

\textcolor
blackserve as the source for Galactic FRB 20200428A,
\textcolor
blackwhich is associated
with the magnetar short burst.

\textcolor

purely electron-positron pairs, in fireballs

\textcolor

\Gamma\sim 100

\textcolor

\textcolor

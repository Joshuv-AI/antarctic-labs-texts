Abstract
We consider properties of semi-classical orthogonal polynomials with respect to the generalised Airy weight ω ⁡ ( x , t , λ ) = x λ ​ exp ⁡ ( − 1 3 ​ x 3 + t ​ x ) , x ∈ ℝ + \omega(x;t,\lambda)=x^{\lambda}\exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{3}x^{3}+tx\right),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{+} with parameters λ > − 1 \lambda>-1 and t ∈ ℝ t\in\mathbb{R} . We also investigate the zeros and recurrence coefficients of the polynomials. The generalised sextic Freud weight ω ⁡ ( x , t , λ ) = | x | 2 ​ λ + 1 ​ exp ⁡ ( − x 6 + t ​ x 2 ) , x ∈ ℝ \omega(x;t,\lambda)=|x|^{2\lambda+1}\exp\left(-x^{6}+tx^{2}\right),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R} arises from a symmetrisation of the generalised Airy weight and we study analogous properties of the polynomials orthogonal with respect to this weight.



Keywords
: Semi-classical orthogonal polynomials, generalised Airy weight; generalised sextic Freud weight; moments; recurrence coefficients; zeros; asymptotics

Introduction

Suppose
P_{n}(x)
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is a sequence of
classical
orthogonal polynomials (such as Hermite, Laguerre and Jacobi polynomials), then
P_{n}(x)
is a solution of a second-order ordinary differential equation of the form

\sigma(x)\frac{{\rm d}^{2}{P_{n}}}{{{\rm d}{x}}^{2}}+\tau(x)\frac{{\rm d}{P_{n}}}{{{\rm d}{x}}}=\lambda_{n}P_{n}

(1.1)

where
\sigma(x)
is a monic polynomial with deg
(\sigma)\leq 2
,
\tau(x)
is a polynomial with deg
(\tau)=1
, and
\lambda_{n}
is a real number which depends on the degree of the polynomial solution, see Bochner
[
5
]
. Equivalently, the weights of classical orthogonal polynomials satisfy a first-order ordinary differential equation, the
Pearson equation

\frac{{\rm d}{}}{{{\rm d}{x}}}[\sigma(x)\omega(x)]=\tau(x)\omega(x)

(1.2)

with
\sigma(x)
and
\tau(x)
the same polynomials as in (
1.1
), see, for example
[
1
,
5
,
8
]
.

For
semi-classical
orthogonal polynomials, the weight function
\omega(x)
satisfies the Pearson equation (
1.2
) with either deg
(\sigma)>2
or deg
(\tau)\neq 1
(cf.
[
17
,
24
]
).
For example, the generalised Airy weight

\omega(x;t,\lambda)=x^{\lambda}\exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{3}x^{3}+tx\right),

(1.3)

with parameters
\lambda>-1
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
satisfies the Pearson equation (
1.2
) with

\sigma(x)=x,\qquad\tau(x)=-x^{3}+tx+\lambda+1

and the generalised sextic Freud weight

\omega(x;t,\lambda)=|x|^{2\lambda+1}\exp\left(-x^{6}+tx^{2}\right),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}

(1.4)

with
\lambda>-1
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
parameters,
satisfies (
1.2
) with

\sigma(x)=x,\qquad\tau(x)=2\lambda+2+2tx^{2}-6x^{6}.

Orthogonal polynomials associated with the exponential cubic weight

\omega(x)=\exp(-x^{3}),\qquad x\in\mathcal{C}

(1.5)

where
\mathcal{C}
a contour in the complex plane, were investigated in
[
14
,
23
,
31
]
,
while the semiclassical weight

\omega(x;t)=\exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{3}x^{3}+tx\right),\qquad x\in\mathcal{C}

(1.6)

with
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathcal{C}
is a contour in the complex plane, was discussed in
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
9
,
13
,
14
,
22
]
. These studies of the weights (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) are for contours in the complex plane. In contrast, in this paper, we study orthogonal polynomials associated with an exponential cubic weight on the positive real axis.

We are concerned with semi-classsical orthogonal polynomials associated with the generalised Airy weight (
1.3
) as well as generalised sextic Freud polynomials associated with the weight function (

2
Orthogonal polynomials

Let
\mu
be a positive Borel measure with support
S
defined on
\mathbb{R}
for which moments of all orders exist, that is

\mu_{n}=\int_{S}x^{n}\,{\rm d}\mu(x),\quad n=0,1,2\dots.

(2.1)

When the sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is positive, that is for all
n\in\{0,1,2,..\}
the Hankel determinant

\Delta_{n}=\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mu_{0}&\mu_{1}&\ldots&\mu_{n-1}\\
\mu_{1}&\mu_{2}&\ldots&\mu_{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mu_{n-1}&\mu_{n}&\ldots&\mu_{2n-2}\end{array}\right|,\qquad n\geq 1

(2.2)

is positive, the family of monic polynomials

P_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{\Delta_{n}}\left|\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mu_{0}&\mu_{1}&\ldots&\mu_{n}\\
\mu_{1}&\mu_{2}&\ldots&\mu_{n+1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mu_{n-1}&\mu_{n}&\ldots&\mu_{2n-1}\\
1&x&\ldots&x^{n}\end{array}\right|

is orthogonal with respect to the measure
\mu
on its support, i.e.

\int_{S}P_{m}(x)P_{n}(x)\,{\rm d}\mu(x)=h_{n}\delta_{m,n},\qquad h_{n}>0

where
\delta_{m,n}
denotes the Kronecker delta. The sequence
\{P_{n}(x)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
satisfies the three-term recurrence relation

xP_{n}(x)=P_{n+1}(x)+\alpha_{n}P_{n}(x)+\beta_{n}P_{n-1}(x)

(2.3)

with initial conditions
P_{-1}(x)=0
and
P_{0}(x)=1
and where

\alpha_{n}=\frac{1}{h_{n}}\int_{S}xP_{n}^{2}(x){\rm d}\mu(x),\qquad\beta_{n}=\frac{\Delta_{n-1}\Delta_{n+1}}{\Delta_{n}^{2}}>0.

(2.4)

Additional information about orthogonal polynomials can be found in, for example,
[
8
,
18
,
30
]
.

Now suppose that the measure is absolutely continuous and has the form

{\rm d}\mu(x)=\omega(x;t,\lambda)\,{\rm d}x

where

\omega(x;t,\lambda)=x^{\lambda}\omega_{0}(x)\exp(xt),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\qquad\lambda>-1

(2.5)

with finite moments for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, which is the case for the generalised Airy weight (
1.3
).
If the weight has the form (
2.5
), which depends on the parameters
t
and
\lambda
, then the orthogonal polynomials
P_{n}
,
the recurrence coefficients
\alpha_{n}
,
\beta_{n}
given by (
2.4
),
the determinant
\Delta_{n}
given by (
2.2
) and
the moments
\mu_{k}
given by (
2.1
) are now functions of
t
and
\lambda
.
Specifically, in this case then

\mu_{k}(t;\lambda)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{k+\lambda}\omega_{0}(x)\exp(xt)\,{\rm d}x=\frac{{\rm d}^{k}{}}{{{\rm d}{t}}^{k}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\lambda}\omega_{0}(x)\exp(xt)\,{\rm d}x\right)=\frac{{\rm d}^{k}{\mu_{0}(t;\lambda)}}{{{\rm d}{t}}^{k}}.

(2.6)

Further, the recurrence relation has the form

xP_{n}(x;t,\lambda)=P_{n+1}(x;t,\lambda)+\alpha_{n}(t;\lambda)P_{n}(x;t,\lambda)+\beta_{n}(t;\lambda)P_{n-1}(x;t,\lambda)

(2.7)

where we have explicitly indicated that the coefficients
\alpha_{n}(t;\lambda)
and
\beta_{n}(t;\lambda)
depend on
t
and
\lambda
.

Theorem 2.1
.

If the weight has the form (
2.5
), then the determinant
\Delta_{n}(t;\lambda)
given by (
2.2
) can be written as

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}(t;\lambda)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}\left(\mu_{0},\frac{{\rm d}{\mu_{0}}}{{{\rm d}{t}}},\ldots,\frac{{\rm d}^{n-1}{\mu_{0}}}{{{\rm d}{t}}^{n-1}}\right).

(2.8)

where
\mathcal{W}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\ldots,\varphi_{n})
is the Wronskian given by

\mathcal{W}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\ldots,\varphi_{n})=\left|\begin{matrix}\varphi_{1}&\varphi_{2}&\ldots&\varphi_{n}\\
\varphi_{1}^{(1)}&\varphi_{2}^{(1)}&\ldots&\varphi_{n}^{(1)}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\varphi_{1}^{(n-1)}&\varphi_{2}^{(n-1)}&\ldots&\varphi_{n}^{(n-1)}\end{matrix}\right|,\qquad\varphi_{j}^{(k)}=\frac{{\rm d}^{k}{\varphi_{j}}}{{{\rm d}{t}}^{k}}.

Proof.

See, for example,
[
10
, Theorem 2.1]
∎

The Hankel determinant
\Delta_{n}(t;\lambda)
satisfies the Toda equation, as shown in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

The Hankel determinant
\Delta_{n}(t;\lambda)
given by (

) satisfies the Toda equation

\frac{{\rm d}^{2}{}}{{{\rm d}{t}}^{2}}\ln\Delta_{n}(t;\lambda)=\frac{\Delta_{n-1}(t;\lambda)\Delta_{n+1}(t;\lambda)}{\Delta_{n}^{2}(t;\lambda)}.

Proof.

See, for example, Nakamira and Zhedanov
[
26
, Proposition 1]
; also
[
7
]
.∎

and
2.2
, we can express the recurrence coefficients

See Chen and Ismail
[
7
]
.∎

and

Generalised Airy polynomials

Lemma 3.1
.

For the generalised Airy weight (
1.3
),
the first moment is given by

\displaystyle\mu_{0}(t;\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\lambda}\exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{3}x^{3}+tx\right)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=3^{(\lambda-2)/3}\,\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{3}\lambda+\tfrac{1}{3})\;{}_{1}F_{2}(\tfrac{1}{3}\lambda+\tfrac{1}{3};\tfrac{1}{3},\tfrac{2}{3};\tfrac{1}{9}t^{3})+3^{(\lambda-1)/3}\,t\,\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{3}\lambda+\tfrac{2}{3})\;{}_{1}F_{2}(\tfrac{1}{3}\lambda+\tfrac{2}{3};\tfrac{2}{3},\tfrac{4}{3};\tfrac{1}{9}t^{3})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\tfrac{1}{2}\,3^{\lambda/3}\,t^{2}\,\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{3}\lambda+1)\;{}_{1}F_{2}(\tfrac{1}{3}\lambda+1;\tfrac{4}{3},\tfrac{5}{3};\tfrac{1}{9}t^{3})

(3.1)

where
{}_{1}F_{2}(a_{1};b_{1},b_{2};z)
is the generalised hypergeometric function. Further,
\varphi(t)=\mu_{0}(t;\lambda)
satisfies the third order equation

\frac{{\rm d}^{3}{\varphi}}{{{\rm d}{t}}^{3}}-t\frac{{\rm d}{\varphi}}{{{\rm d}{t}}}-(\lambda+1)\varphi=0.

Proof.

See
[
11
, Lemma 3.1]
.

3.1
Differential and discrete equations satisfied by generalised Airy polynomials

We derive a differential-difference equation, a differential equation and a mixed recurrence relation satisfied by generalised Airy polynomials.

The coefficients
{A}_{n}(x)
and
{B}_{n}(x)
in the relation

\frac{{\rm d}{P_{n}}}{{{\rm d}{x}}}(x;t,\lambda)=\beta_{n}(t;\lambda){A}_{n}(x)P_{n-1}(x;t,\lambda)-{B}_{n}(x)P_{n}(x;t,\lambda)

(3.2)

satisfied by semi-classical orthogonal polynomials can be derived using a technique introduced by Shohat
[
28
]
for weights
\omega(x)
such that
\displaystyle{\omega^{\prime}(x)}/{\omega(x)}
is a rational function. The method of ladder operators was introduced by Chen and Ismail in
[
7
]
, see also
[
18
, Theorem 3.2.1]
and adapted in
[
6
]
for the situation where the weight function vanishes at one point. Explicit expressions for the coefficients in the differential-difference equation (
3.2
) when the weight function is positive on the real line except for one point are provided in
[
12
]
. The coefficients in the differential-difference relation for generalised Airy polynomials associated with the weight (
1.3
) are given in the next result.

Theorem 3.4
.

For the generalised Airy weight (
1.3
)
the monic orthogonal polynomials
P_{n}(x;t,\lambda)
with respect to this weight satisfy the differential-difference equation (
3.2
) with

\displaystyle A_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=x+\alpha_{n}+\frac{R_{n}}{x}

(3.3a)

\displaystyle B_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\beta_{n}+\frac{r_{n}}{x}

(3.3b)

where
\alpha_{n}(t;\lambda)
and
\beta_{n}(t;\lambda)
are the coefficients in the three-term recurrence relation (
2.7
) and

\displaystyle R_{n}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{n}^{2}+\beta_{n}+\beta_{n+1}-t

(3.4a)

\displaystyle r_{n}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\lambda-\alpha_{n+1}\beta_{n+1}+\alpha_{n-1}\beta_{n}-\alpha_{n}^{3}+t\alpha_{n}+1\right)-\alpha_{n}\beta_{n+1}.

(3.4b)

Proof.

Since
P_{n}(x)=P_{n}(x;t,\lambda)
is a polynomial of degree
n
, we can write

\displaystyle x\frac{{\rm d}{P_{n}}}{{{\rm d}{x}}}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{n,k}P_{k}(x).

(3.5)

Multiplying (
3.5
) by
P_{k}(x)\,\omega(x)
, integrating both sides of the equation with respect to
x
and applying the orthogonality relation, yields, for
k=0,1,2,...,n,

\displaystyle h_{k}c_{n,k}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}x\frac{{\rm d}{P_{n}}}{{{\rm d}{x}}}(x)P_{k}(x)\,\omega(x)\,{\rm d}{x},\qquad h_{k}\neq 0.

(3.6)

Integrating the right hand side of (
3.6
) by parts, we obtain, for
k=0,1,2,\dots n
,

\displaystyle h_{k}c_{n,k}

\displaystyle=\Big[xP_{k}(x)P_{n}(x)\,\omega(x;t,\lambda)\Big]_{0}^{\infty}-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}{}}{{{\rm d}{x}}}\left[xP_{k}(x)\,\omega(x;t,\lambda)\right]P_{n}(x)\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[P_{n}(x)P_{k}(x)+xP_{n}(x)\frac{{\rm d}{P_{k}}}{{{\rm d}{x}}}(x)\right]\omega(x;t,\lambda)\,{\rm d}{x}-\int_{0}^{\infty}xP_{n}(x)P_{k}(x)\frac{{\rm d}{\omega}}{{{\rm d}{x}}}(x)\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[P_{n}(x)P_{k}(x)+xP_{n}(x)\frac{{\rm d}{P_{k}}}{{{\rm d}{x}}}(x)\right]\omega(x;t,\lambda)\,{\rm d}{x}-\int_{0}^{\infty}P_{n}(x)P_{k}(x)(\lambda+tx-x^{3})\,\omega(x;t,\lambda)\,{\rm d}{x}.

(3.7)

For
k=n
, it follows from (
3.7
), that

\displaystyle h_{n}c_{n,n}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}x\frac{{\rm d}{P_{n}}}{{{\rm d}{x}}}(x)P_{n}(x)\,\omega(x;t,\lambda)\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}P_{n}^{2}(x)\omega(x;t,\lambda)\,{\rm d}x-\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}P_{n}^{2}(x)(\lambda+tx-x^{3})\,\omega(x;t,\lambda)\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}h_{n}-\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda h_{n}-\tfrac{1}{2}t\int_{0}^{\infty}xP_{n}^{2}(x)\,\omega(x;t,\lambda)\,{\rm d}{x}+\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{3}P_{n}^{2}(x)\,\omega(x;t,\lambda)\,{\rm d}{x}.

(3.8)

Iterating the three-term recurrence relation (
2.7
), yields

\displaystyle x^{3}P_{n}(x)=P_{n+3}(x)

\displaystyle+(\alpha_{n+2}+\alpha_{n}+\alpha_{n+1})P_{n+2}(x)

\displaystyle+(\alpha_{n}^{2}+\alpha_{n+1}\alpha_{n}+\alpha_{n+1}^{2}+\beta_{n}+\beta_{n+1}+\beta_{n+2})P_{n+1}(x)

\displaystyle+(2\alpha_{n}\left(\beta_{n}+\beta_{n+1}\right)+\alpha_{n-1}\beta_{n}+\alpha_{n+1}\beta_{n+1}+\alpha_{n}^{3})P_{n}(x)

\displaystyle+\beta_{n}\left(\alpha_{n-1}^{2}+\alpha_{n}\alpha_{n-1}+\alpha_{n}^{2}+\beta_{n-1}+\beta_{n}+\beta_{n+1}\right)P_{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle+\beta_{n-1}\beta_{n}(\alpha_{n}+\alpha_{n-1}+\alpha_{n-2})P_{n-2}(x)+\beta_{n}\beta_{n-1}\beta_{n-2}P_{n-3}(x).

(3.9)

Substituting (
2.7
) and (
3.9
) into (
3.8
) it follows that

\displaystyle c_{n,n}

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}(\alpha_{n}t+\lambda+1-\alpha_{n+1}\beta_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}^{3}-\alpha_{n-1}\beta_{n})+\alpha_{n}(\beta_{n+1}+\beta_{n})

(3.10)

For
k=0,1,2,\dots n-1
, (
3.7
) yields

\displaystyle h_{k}c_{n,k}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\infty}{P_{n}(x)P_{k}(x)\left(\lambda+tx-x^{3}\right)}\,\omega(x;t,\lambda)\,{\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(x^{3}-tx\big)P_{n}(x)P_{k}(x)\,\omega(x;t,\lambda)\,{\rm d}{x}.

(3.11)

Substituting (
3.9
) and (
2.3
) into (

\displaystyle c_{n,n-1}

\displaystyle=\beta_{n}(\beta_{n+1}+\beta_{n}+\beta_{n-1}+\alpha_{n}^{2}+\alpha_{n-1}\alpha_{n}+\alpha_{n-1}^{2}-t)

(3.12a)

\displaystyle c_{n,n-2}

\displaystyle=\beta_{n}\beta_{n-1}(\alpha_{n}+\alpha_{n-1}+\alpha_{n-2})

(3.12b)

\displaystyle c_{n,n-3}

\displaystyle=\beta_{n}\beta_{n-1}\beta_{n-2}.

(3.12c)

We now write (

\displaystyle x\frac{{\rm d}{P_{n}}}{{{\rm d}{x}}}(x)=c_{n,n-3}P_{n-3}(x)+c_{n,n-2}P_{n-2}(x)+c_{n,n-1}P_{n-1}+c_{n,n}P_{n}(x).

(3.13)

Iterating (

\displaystyle P_{n-2}(x)

\displaystyle P_{n-3}(x)

\displaystyle=\left\{\dfrac{(x-\alpha_{n-1})(x-\alpha_{n-2})}{\beta_{n-1}\beta_{n-2}}-\dfrac{1}{\beta_{n-2}}\right\}P_{n-1}(x)-\dfrac{x-\alpha_{n-2}}{\beta_{n-1}\beta_{n-2}}\,P_{n}(x).

(3.14b)

Substituting (

3.14

\displaystyle x\frac{{\rm d}{P_{n}}}{{{\rm d}{x}}}(x)=

\displaystyle-\left\{\beta_{n}x-\alpha_{n}\beta_{n+1}+\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\lambda-\alpha_{n+1}\beta_{n+1}+\alpha_{n-1}\beta_{n}-\alpha_{n}^{3}+t\alpha_{n}+1\right)\right\}P_{n}(x)

Theorem 3.5
.

For the generalised Airy weight (

Next, we consider a mixed recurrence relation connecting generalised Airy polynomials associated with different weight functions. Mixed recurrence relations such as these are typically used to prove interlacing and Stieltjes interlacing of the zeros of two polynomials from different sequences and also provide a set of points that can be applied as inner bounds for the extreme zeros of polynomials.

Lemma 3.6

Let

\displaystyle\dfrac{P_{n}(0;t,\lambda)}{P_{n-1}(0;t,\lambda)}+\dfrac{P_{n-1}(0;t,\lambda+1)}{P_{n-2}(0;t,\lambda+1)},\qquad e_{n}=\dfrac{P_{n-1}(0;t,\lambda+1)}{P_{n-2}(0;t,\lambda+1)}\dfrac{P_{n-1}(0;t,\lambda)}{P_{n-2}(0;t,\lambda)}

and

The weight function associated with the monic polynomials

\displaystyle\omega(x;t,\lambda+2)

[

\displaystyle xP_{n-2}(x;t,\lambda+2)

3.2

The property that the zeros

Monotonicity of the zeros of semiclassical orthogonal polynomials plays and important role in applications.

.

Consider the semiclassical weight

\lambda
increases, if
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{C(x)}\frac{{\rm d}{}}{{{\rm d}{x}}}C(x)>0}

increases when

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}(t;\lambda)

Proof.

When

(3.34)

4
Generalised sextic Freud polynomials

The generalised sextic Freud weight

\omega(x;t,\lambda)=|x|^{2\lambda+1}\exp\left(-x^{6}+tx^{2}\right),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}

(4.1)

with
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda>-1
parameters,
is a symmetric weight, i.e.
\omega(x;t,\lambda)=\omega(-x;t,\lambda)
, so that
\alpha_{n}\equiv 0
. The generalised sextic Freud weight and recurrence coefficients associated with the weight were discussed in
[
11
]
.

Next we consider some properties of the polynomials associated with the generalised sextic Freud weight (
4.1
). Generalised sextic Freud polynomials arise from a symmetrisation of generalised Airy polynomials. Since the weight is symmetric, the monic orthogonal polynomials
S_{n}(x;t,\lambda)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, therefore satisfy the three-term recurrence relation

S_{n+1}(x;t,\lambda)=xS_{n}(x;t,\lambda)-\beta_{n}(t;\lambda)S_{n-1}(x;t,\lambda),\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots\

(4.2)

with
S_{-1}(x;t,\lambda)=0
and
S_{0}(x;t,\lambda)=1
.

4.1
Differential and discrete equations satisfied by generalised sextic Freud polynomials

In this section we derive mixed recurrence relations, differential-difference equations and differential equations satisfied by generalised sextic Freud polynomials which are analogous to those for the generalised Airy polynomials in §
3.1
.

Theorem 4.1
.

For the generalised sextic Freud weight (
4.1
)
the monic orthogonal polynomials
S_{n}(x;t,\lambda)
with respect to this weight satisfy the differential-difference equation

x\frac{{\rm d}{S_{n}}}{{{\rm d}{x}}}(x;t,\lambda)=\mathcal{A}_{n}(x)S_{n-1}(x;t,\lambda)-\mathcal{B}_{n}(x)S_{n}(x;t,\lambda)

(4.3)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=6x\beta_{n}\big[x^{4}-\tfrac{1}{3}t+x^{2}\big(\beta_{n}+\beta_{n+1}\big)+\beta_{n+2}\beta_{n+1}+\big(\beta_{n+1}+\beta_{n}\big)^{2}+\beta_{n-1}\beta_{n}\big]

(4.4a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=6x^{2}\beta_{n}\big(x^{2}+\beta_{n+1}+\beta_{n}+\beta_{n-1}\big)+(\lambda+\tfrac{1}{2})[1-(-1)^{n}],

(4.4b)

with
\beta_{n}
the recurrence coefficient in the three-term recurrence relation (
4.2
).

Proof.

It was proved in
[
12
, Corollary 1]
that monic orthogonal polynomials
S_{n}(x)=S_{n}(x;t,\lambda)
with respect to the weight

\omega(x)=|x|^{2\lambda+1}\exp\{-v(x)\}

satisfy the differential-difference equation (
4.3
), where

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{x}{h_{n-1}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(x,y){S_{n}^{2}(y)}\,\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{x}{h_{n-1}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}(x,y){S_{n}(y)S_{n-1}(y)}\,\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y+(\lambda+\tfrac{1}{2})[1-(-1)^{n}]

and

\mathcal{K}(x,y)=\frac{v^{\prime}(x)-v^{\prime}(y)}{x-y}.

For the generalised sextic Freud weight (
4.1
) we have

v(x)=x^{6}-tx^{2}
and hence

\mathcal{K}(x,y)=6(x^{4}+x^{3}y+x^{2}y^{2}+xy^{3}+y^{4})-2t.

Hence

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(x,y){S_{n}^{2}(y)}\,\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=(6x^{4}-2t)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{S_{n}^{2}(y)}\,\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y+6x^{3}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{yS_{n}^{2}(y)}\,\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y+6x^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{y^{2}S_{n}^{2}(y)}\,\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\qquad+6x\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{y^{3}S_{n}^{2}(y)}\,\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y+6\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{y^{4}S_{n}^{2}(y)}\,\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=(6x^{4}-2t)h_{n}+6x^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big[S_{n+1}(y)+\beta_{n}S_{n-1}(y)\big]^{2}\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\qquad+6\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big[S_{n+2}(y)+(\beta_{n+1}+\beta_{n})S_{n}(y)+\beta_{n}\beta_{n-1}S_{n-2}(y)\big]^{2}\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=(6x^{4}-2t)h_{n}+6x^{2}(h_{n+1}+\beta_{n}^{2}h_{n-1})+6(h_{n+2}+(\beta_{n+1}+\beta_{n})^{2}h_{n}+\beta_{n}^{2}\beta_{n-1}^{2}h_{n-2})

\displaystyle=6\big[x^{4}-\tfrac{1}{3}t+x^{2}\big(\beta_{n}+\beta_{n+1}\big)+\beta_{n+2}\beta_{n+1}+\big(\beta_{n+1}+\beta_{n}\big)^{2}+\beta_{n-1}\beta_{n}\big]h_{n},

since

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{yS_{n}^{2}(y)}\,\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{y^{3}S_{n}^{2}(y)}\,\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y=0

as these have odd integrands,

\beta_{n}=h_{n}/h_{n-1}
where

h_{n}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}S_{n}^{2}(y)\,\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y

the monic orthogonal polynomials
S_{n}(x)
satisfy the three-term recurrence relation (
4.2
),
and are orthogonal, i.e.

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{S_{m}(y)}S_{n}(y)\,\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y=0,\qquad{\rm if}\quad m\neq n.

(4.5)

Also,

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(x,y){S_{n}(y)S_{n-1}(y)}\,\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=(6x^{4}-2t)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{S_{n}(y)S_{n-1}(y)}\,\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y+6x^{3}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{yS_{n}(y)S_{n-1}(y)}\,\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+6x^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{y^{2}S_{n}(y)S_{n-1}(y)}\,\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y+6x\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{y^{3}S_{n}(y)S_{n-1}(y)}\,\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+6\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{y^{4}S_{n}(y)S_{n-1}(y)}\,\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=6x^{3}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}S_{n}(y)\big[S_{n}(y)+\beta_{n-1}S_{n-2}(y)\big]\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+6x\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\big[S_{n+2}(y)+\big(\beta_{n+1}+\beta_{n}\big)S_{n}(y)+\beta_{n}\beta_{n-1}S_{n-2}(y)\big]\big[S_{n}(y)+\beta_{n-1}S_{n-2}(y)\big]\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=6x^{3}h_{n}+6x\big[\big(\beta_{n+1}+\beta_{n}\big)h_{n}+\beta_{n}\beta_{n-1}^{2}h_{n-2}\big]

\displaystyle=6x\big(x^{2}+\beta_{n+1}+\beta_{n}+\beta_{n-1}\big)h_{n},

since

\begin{split}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{S_{n}(y)S_{n-1}(y)}\,\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}y^{2}{S_{n}(y)S_{n-1}(y)}\,\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}y^{4}{S_{n}(y)S_{n-1}(y)}\,\omega(y)\,{\rm d}y&=0\end{split}

as these have odd integrands, using the recurrence relation (
4.2
) and orthogonality (
4.5
).∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\{S_{n}(x;t,\lambda)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be the sequence of monic generalised sextic Freud polynomials orthogonal with respect to the weight
(
4.1
), then, for
n
fixed,

\displaystyle x^{2}S_{n}(x;t,\lambda+1)=xS_{n+1}(x;t,\lambda)-(\beta_{n+1}+a_{n})S_{n}(x;t,\lambda)

(4.8)

where

a_{n}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{S_{n+2}(0;t,\lambda)}{S_{n}(0;t,\lambda)},\quad&\text{if}\quad n\quad\text{even}\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{S_{n+2}^{\prime}(0;t,\lambda)}{S_{n}^{\prime}(0;t,\lambda)},\quad&\text{if}\quad n\quad\text{odd}.\end{cases}

Proof.

The weight function associated with the polynomials
S_{n}(x;t,\lambda+1)
is

\displaystyle\omega(x;t,\lambda+1)

\displaystyle=|x|^{2\lambda+3}\exp(-x^{6}+tx^{2})=x^{2}\omega(x;t,\lambda).

The factor

by which the weight

is modified has a double zero at the origin and therefore Christoffel’s formula (cf.
[

, Theorem 2.5]
), applied to the monic polynomials

S_{n}^{\prime}(0;t,\lambda)&S_{n+1}^{\prime}(0;t,\lambda)&S_{n+2}^{\prime}(0;t,\lambda)\\
\end{matrix}\right|

Since the weight
\omega(x;t,\lambda)
is even, we have that
S_{2n+1}(0;t,\lambda)=S_{2n}^{\prime}(0;t,\lambda)=0
while
S_{2n}(0;t,\lambda)\neq 0
and

5
Discussion

In this paper, we have studied generalised Airy polynomials that are orthogonal polynomials that satisfy a three-term recurrence relation whose coefficients depend on two parameters. We have derived a differential difference equation, a differential equation and a mixed recurrence relation satisfied by the polynomials and used these to study properties of the zeros and recurrence coefficients of the polynomials. We also investigated various asymptotic properties of the recurrence coefficients. Furthermore we have shown that similar results hold for the generalised sextic Freud polynomials and corrected some results in the literature.

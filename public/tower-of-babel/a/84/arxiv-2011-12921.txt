Strong transitivity, Moufang’s condition and the Howe–Moore property
Corina Ciobotaru

Abstract
Firstly, we prove that every closed subgroup H H of type-preserving automorphisms of a locally finite thick affine building Δ \Delta of dimension ≥ 2 \geq 2 that acts strongly transitively on Δ \Delta is Moufang. If moreover Δ \Delta is irreducible and H H is topologically simple, we show that H H is the subgroup 𝔾 ⁡ ( k ) + \operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(k)^{+} of the k k -rational points 𝔾 ⁡ ( k ) \operatorname{\mathbb{G}}(k) of the isotropic simple algebraic group 𝔾 \operatorname{\mathbb{G}} over a non-Archimedean local field k k associated with Δ \Delta . Secondly, we generalise the proof given in [ 7 ] for the case of bi-regular trees to any locally finite thick affine building Δ \Delta , and obtain that any topologically simple, closed, strongly transitive and type-preserving subgroup of Aut ⁡ ( Δ ) \mathrm{Aut}(\Delta) has the Howe–Moore property. This proof is different than the strategy used so far in the literature and does not relay on the polar decomposition K ​ A + ​ K KA^{+}K , where K K is a maximal compact subgroup, and the important fact that A + A^{+} is an abelian maximal sub-semi-group.

The third result of the article concerns the Howe–Moore property

The Howe–Moore property
[
12
]
is a harmonic analytic feature of a locally compact group that has far-reaching applications to many areas of Mathematics, such as homogeneous dynamics, geometry, or number theory. To list some, it has notable consequences regarding discrete subgroups of semi-simple Lie groups, Mostow’s rigidity theorem, Ratner’s theorems on unipotent flows, or mixing and equidistribution results.

So far, all known examples of locally compact groups having the Howe–Moore property are:

1.

connected, non-compact, simple real Lie groups, with finite center
[
12
]
, e.g.,
\operatorname{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})

2.

isotropic simple algebraic groups over non Archimedean local fields
[
12
]
, e.g.,
\operatorname{SL}(n,\mathbb{Q}_{p})

3.

closed, topologically simple subgroups of
\mathrm{Aut}(T)
with a
2
-transitive action on the boundary of a bi-regular tree
T
, that has valence
\geq 3
at every vertex,
[
7
]
, e.g., the universal group
U(F)^{+}
of Burger–Mozes, when F is
2
-transitive.

An important fact the above mentioned groups have in common is their polar decomposition
KA^{+}K
, where
K
is a maximal compact subgroup and
A^{+}
is an abelian maximal sub-semi-group, the latter being crucial in the proof to imply the Howe–Moore property. This was employed in
[
10
]
to give a unified proof for all these above examples.

For a historical introduction and basic definitions regarding the Howe–Moore property one can consult
[
10
, Introduction and Section 2]
. For completeness, we recall here just some of the very basic notions.

Let
G
be a locally compact group and
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
a (strongly continuous) unitary representation
\pi:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
on a (infinite dimensional) complex Hilbert space
(\mathcal{H},\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle)
. For
v,w\in\mathcal{H}
the associated
(v,w)
-matrix coefficient
is the map
c_{v,w}:G\to\mathbb{C}
given by
c_{v,w}(g):=\left\langle\pi(g)v,w\right\rangle
. We say
c_{v,w}

vanishes at infinity
if, for every
\epsilon>0
, the subset
\{g\in G\;|\;|c_{v,w}(g)|\geq\epsilon\}
is compact in
G
; equivalently,
\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}c_{v,w}(g)=0
, where
\infty
represents the one-point compactification of the locally compact group
G
.

Definition 1.8
.

A locally compact group
G
has
the Howe–Moore property
if, any unitary representation of
G
that is without non-zero
G
–invariant vectors is
C_{0}
(i.e. all its matrix coefficients vanish at infinity).

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
\Delta
be a locally finite thick affine building and
G
a topologically simple, closed, strongly transitive and type-preserving subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(\Delta)
. Then
G
has the Howe–Moore property.

The two key ideas behind the proof of Theorem
1.9
are the use of the well known Bruhat decomposition of a strongly transitive group
G\leq\mathrm{Aut}(\Delta)
(see Section
4
), and the closed subgroup
G_{\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{B})}^{0}
introduced in Definition
4.1
. These allow us to get rid of the so-far-used polar decomposition
KA^{+}K
and its
abelian
sub-semi-group
A^{+}
techniques.

In the case of a locally finite thick affine building
\Delta
we expect the Howe–Moore property of a closed, topologically simple and type-preserving
G\leq\mathrm{Aut}(\Delta)
to be equivalent to the strongly transitive action of

on
\Delta
. Recall, in the case of bi-regular trees, strong transitivity is equivalent to
2
-transitivity. Moreover, it appears that among locally finite thick buildings (so of any Coxeter type), only the affine case provides examples of groups enjoying the Howe–Moore property. Both of the above mentioned expectations will be studied in a future research project.

From strong transitivity to Moufang’s condition

Let us recall a fundamental Rigidity Theorem of Tits. To state this, let
X
be a building and
c
a chamber of
X
. We denote by
E_{1}(c)
the set of all chambers of
X
that are adjacent to
c
. In what follows we denote by
\Delta
an irreducible locally finite thick affine building of dimension
\geq 2
, if not stated otherwise.

Theorem 2.1
.

(see
[
1
, Theorem 5.205]
)

Let
X
be a thick spherical building, and let
c
and
c^{\prime}
be opposite chambers in
X
. If an automorphism
\phi
of
X
fixes
E_{1}(c)\cup\{c^{\prime}\}
pointwise, then
\phi
is the identity.

From Theorem
2.1
one can deduce simple-transitivity for a root group
U_{\alpha}
when it enjoys the Moufang property. More precisely:

Corollary 2.2
.

(see
[
1
, Lemma 7.25(3)]
)

Let
H
be a closed subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}_{0}(\Delta)
and
\alpha
any root of
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
. Suppose the root group
U_{\alpha}(H)
acts transitively on
\mathcal{A}_{\partial_{\infty}\Delta}(\alpha)
. Then the action of
U_{\alpha}(H)
on
\mathcal{A}_{\partial_{\infty}\Delta}(\alpha)
is simple-transitive; i.e., for every
u\in U_{\alpha}(H)-\{id\}
the action of
u
has no fixed points on
\mathcal{A}_{\partial_{\infty}\Delta}(\alpha)
.

Proof.

Suppose there is an element
u\in U_{\alpha}(H)-\{id\}
that fixes an element of
\mathcal{A}_{\partial_{\infty}\Delta}(\alpha)
, i.e.,
u
fixes pointwise at least one apartment
\Sigma
of
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
that contains
\alpha
as a half-apartment.
As the affine building
\Delta
is irreducible, its Coxeter diagram has no isolated nodes, and thus also the one of the spherical building
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
. Then by
[
1
, Example 3.128]
there is an ideal chamber
c\in\mathrm{Ch}(\alpha)
having no panel in
\partial\alpha
. Then by the definition of
U_{\alpha}(H)
, the element
u
fixes
E_{1}(c)\subset\partial_{\infty}\Delta
and the apartment
\Sigma
, and thus the ideal chamber
c^{\prime}
of
\Sigma
opposite
c
. By Theorem
2.1
, as
u
is also an automorphism of
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
, we must have
u|_{\partial_{\infty}\Delta}=\operatorname{id}
, given a contradiction with our assumption.
∎

As written in
[
1
, Remark 7.26]
, the assumption that the Coxeter diagram has no isolated nodes (e.g., the irreducibility condition from Corollary
2.2
) is crucial and cannot be removed.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\alpha
be any root of
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
and
H
a closed subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}_{0}(\Delta)
. Suppose both root groups
U_{\alpha}(H)
and
U_{\alpha}(\mathrm{Aut}_{0}(\Delta))
act transitively on
\mathcal{A}_{\partial_{\infty}\Delta}(\alpha)
. Then
U_{\alpha}(\mathrm{Aut}_{0}(\Delta))=U_{\alpha}(H)
.

Proof.

It is obvious that
U_{\alpha}(H)\leq U_{\alpha}(\mathrm{Aut}_{0}(\Delta))
. By Corollary
2.2
we have that both groups
U_{\alpha}(H),U_{\alpha}(\mathrm{Aut}_{0}(\Delta))
act simple-transitively on
\mathcal{A}_{\partial_{\infty}\Delta}(\alpha)
and so they must be equal.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\Delta
be a locally finite thick affine building. Let
H
be a closed subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}_{0}(\Delta)
that acts strongly transitively on
\Delta
. Then for every
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}\Delta
the group
H_{\xi}
acts cocompactly on
\Delta
and transitively on the set
\mathrm{Opp}(\xi)\subset\partial_{\infty}\Delta
of all ideal points opposite
\xi
.

Proof.

Fix an apartment
\Sigma
in
\Delta
. By the strong transitivity of
H
on
\Delta
, it is enough to consider only ideal points
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}\Sigma
. For what follows, fix such
\xi
. Then there is a unique, minimal-dimensional ideal simplex
\sigma(\xi)\subset\partial_{\infty}\Sigma
such that
\xi
is an interior point of
\sigma(\xi)
. Then choose an ideal chamber
c\in\mathrm{Ch}(\Sigma)
(not necessarily unique), with
\sigma(\xi)\subset c
and notice
H_{c}\leq H_{\xi}
. It is then enough to prove that for every
c\in\mathrm{Ch}(\Sigma)
, the group
H_{c}
acts cocompactly on
\Delta
. Indeed, fix
c\in\mathrm{Ch}(\Sigma)
. Since
H
is strongly transitive on
\Delta
, by
[
11
, Sec. 17.1]
,
H
is strongly transitive on
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
as well. In particular, we have that
H_{c}
acts transitively on the set
\mathrm{Opp}(c)
of all ideal chambers of
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
opposite
c
. As for every chamber
C\in\mathrm{Ch}(\Delta)
there is an apartment
\Sigma_{1}
in
\Delta
containing
C
, and
c
as an ideal chamber of
\mathrm{Ch}(\Sigma_{1})
, we are left with proving that
\mathrm{Stab}_{H_{c}}(\Sigma):=\{g\in H_{c}\;|\;g(\Sigma)=\Sigma\}
acts cocompactly on
\Sigma
. To do that, again by the strong transitivity of
H
on
\Delta
, we know that
\mathrm{Stab}_{H}(\Sigma)\backslash\mathrm{Fix}_{H}(\Sigma)
is the affine Weyl group
W
corresponding to the affine building
\Delta
, and that W is generated by the reflexions through the walls of a chamber in
\mathrm{Ch}(\Sigma)
(see
[
11
, Section 5.2]
). We also know that
W
is simply transitive on special vertices of
\Sigma
of the same type, and for every two special vertices
v_{1},v_{2}
of
\Sigma
of the same type there is a translation automorphism in
\mathrm{Stab}_{H}(\Sigma)
of the apartment
\Sigma
, sending
v_{1}
to
v_{2}
. In particular, such translation automorphism fixes pointwise the ideal boundary
\partial_{\infty}\Sigma
of
\Sigma
, and thus belonging to the group
\mathrm{Stab}_{H_{c}}(\Sigma)\leq H_{c}
. This remark easily implies
\mathrm{Stab}_{H_{c}}(\Sigma)
acts cocompactly on
\Sigma
and so proving the claim.

Let us now prove the transitivity of
H_{\xi}
on the set
\mathrm{Opp}(\xi)
. As above, fix an apartment
\Sigma
of
\Delta
with
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}\Sigma
, an ideal chamber
c\in\mathrm{Ch}(\partial_{\infty}\Sigma)
with
\xi\in c
, and let
\xi_{-}
be the unique ideal point in
\partial_{\infty}\Sigma
opposite
\xi
. Take
\xi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Opp}(\xi)
, with
\xi^{\prime}\neq\xi_{-}
. Then there is an apartment
\Sigma^{\prime}
of
\Delta
such that
c\subset\mathrm{Ch}(\partial_{\infty}\Sigma^{\prime})
and
\xi^{\prime}\in\partial_{\infty}\Sigma^{\prime}
. As
H_{c}\leq H_{\xi}
acts transitively on
\mathrm{Opp}(c)
, there is
g\in H_{c}
with
g(\Sigma^{\prime})=\Sigma
and
g(c)=c
. In particular,
g(\xi^{\prime})\in\partial_{\infty}\Sigma
and
g(\xi)=\xi
. As
\xi_{-},\xi^{\prime}
are opposite
\xi
, we must have
g(\xi^{\prime})=\xi_{-}
. The lemma is proven.
∎

Let
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}\Delta
and choose
\xi_{-}\in\partial_{\infty}\Delta
an ideal point opposite
\xi
. Denote by
P(\xi,\xi_{-})
the set of all apartments of
\Delta
that contain
\xi
and
\xi_{-}
in their ideal boundary at infinity. As
\xi
is an ideal point, there is a unique minimal-dimensional ideal simplex
\sigma(\xi)\subset\partial_{\infty}\Delta
such that
\xi
is an interior point of
\sigma(\xi)
. Let
\sigma_{-}(\xi)\subset\partial_{\infty}\Delta
be the corresponding ideal simplex, opposite
\sigma(\xi)
, that contains
\xi_{-}
as an interior point. Denote by

the dimension (with respect to
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
) of the ideal simplex
\sigma(\xi)
. Then
P(\xi,\xi_{-})=\mathbb{R}^{n_{\xi}+1}\times\Delta_{\xi}
, where
\Delta_{\xi}
is a sub-building of
\Delta
, this is proved by Rousseau
[
16
, 4.3]
in the more general setting of masures.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\Delta
be a locally finite thick affine building. Let
H
be a closed subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}_{0}(\Delta)
that acts strongly transitively on
\Delta
. Let
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}\Delta
and choose
\xi_{-}\in\partial_{\infty}\Delta
opposite
\xi
. Then the closed group
H_{\xi,\xi_{-}}\leq H_{\xi}
acts cocompactly on

.

Proof.

Fix an apartment
\Sigma
of
\Delta
such that
\xi,\xi_{-}\in\partial_{\infty}\Sigma
, and an ideal chamber

with
\xi\in c
. Then
\Sigma\in P(\xi,\xi_{-})
. Take another apartment
\Sigma^{\prime}\in P(\xi,\xi_{-})
with
\Sigma\neq\Sigma^{\prime}
, and an ideal chamber

with
\xi\in c^{\prime}
. By
[
11
, Sec. 17.1]
,
H
acts strongly transitively on
\partial_{\infty}\Delta
. Then there is

such that

and

. Since

. To prove the lemma it is enough to show that

acts cocompactly on

. As used in the proof of Lemma

do pointwise stabilise the ideal boundary

Definition 2.9

(see
[
8
]
Definition 3.5)
.

Let
G
be a group acting by isometries on a proper
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
space
X
. A sequence
\{g_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset G
is called
radial
in
G
if there exist an ideal point
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}X
, called
the radial limit point
of
\{g_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
, and a geodesic ray
r:[0,\infty)\to X
pointing towards
\xi
such that the sequence
g_{n}^{-1}(r(0))
converges to
\xi
, with respect to the cone topology on
X\cup\partial_{\infty}X
, while remaining at bounded distance from
r
.

One of the properties of radial sequences is the existence of a limiting bi-infinite geodesic line in
X
with specific features. The following technical lemma expresses exactly that, although its proof is very easy and relays on the properness of
X
implying that
X\cup\partial_{\infty}X
is a compact space, with respect to the cone topology.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
G
be a group acting by isometries on a proper
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
space
X
. Suppose
\{g_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset G
is a radial sequence with radial limit point
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}X
and geodesic ray
r:[0,\infty)\to X
pointing towards
\xi
. Then one can extract a subsequence
\{g_{n_{k}}\}_{k\geq 1}
of
\{g_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
such that the sequence of geodesic rays
\{g_{n_{k}}(r)\}_{k\geq 1}
converges to a bi-infinite geodesic line
\ell:\mathbb{R}\to X
with the properties that
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}g_{n_{k}}(r(0))=\ell(-\infty)=:\eta_{-}\in\partial_{\infty}X
and
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}g_{n_{k}}(\xi)=\ell(\infty)=:\eta_{+}\in\partial_{\infty}X
.

Proof.

By the definition of a radial sequence, we have that
\{g_{n}^{-1}(r(0))\}_{n\geq 1}
converges to
\xi
and it stays at bounded distance from
r
. Denote by
r(t_{n})
the projection of
g_{n}^{-1}(r(0))
to
r
. Then, by the construction, the sequence
\{g_{n}(r(t_{n}))\}_{n\geq 1}
stays at bounded distance from
r(0)
. As
X
is proper, implying the visual boundary
\partial_{\infty}X
is compact, one can extract a subsequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
such that
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}g_{n_{k}}(\xi)=\eta_{+}\in\partial_{\infty}X
. Then, up to further extracting a subsequence from
\{n_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
, the sequence of points
\{g_{n_{k}}(r(0))\}_{k\geq 1}
converges to an ideal point
\eta_{-}\in\partial_{\infty}X
. As
g_{n_{k}}(r)
are geodesics rays, from the above limiting procedures one can conclude that the sequence of geodesic rays
\{g_{n_{k}}(r)\}_{k\geq 1}
converges to a bi-infinite geodesic line
\ell:\mathbb{R}\to X
with
\ell(-\infty)=\eta_{-}
and
\ell(\infty)=\eta_{+}
.
∎

.

In the hypotheses of Lemma

The following, also technical lemma, gives a reason why and how radial sequences can be used in applications.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
X
be a proper
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
space and
G\leq Isom(X)
be a closed group. Suppose
\{g_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset G
is a radial sequence with radial limit point
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}X
and geodesic ray
r:[0,\infty)\to X
pointing towards
\xi
. Suppose also the sequence of geodesic rays
\{g_{n}(r)\}_{n\geq 1}
converges to a bi-infinite geodesic line
\ell:\mathbb{R}\to X
with the properties that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}g_{n}(r(0))=\ell(-\infty)=:\eta_{-}\in\partial_{\infty}X
and
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}g_{n}(\xi)=\ell(\infty)=:\eta_{+}\in\partial_{\infty}X
. Let
g\in G_{\xi}
. Then the sequence
\{g_{n}gg_{n}^{-1}\}_{n\geq 1}
is bounded and for any of its convergent subsequence
\{g_{n_{k}}gg_{n_{k}}^{-1}\}_{n\geq 1}
the corresponding limit
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}g_{n_{k}}gg_{n_{k}}^{-1}=:h\in G
is an element of
G_{\eta_{-},\eta_{+}}
.

Proof.

As
g\in G_{\xi}
and by the definition of the visual boundary
\partial_{\infty}X
, we first notice that the geodesic ray
g(r)
is asymptotically equivalent to
r
, and so at bounded distance from the geodesic ray
r
. As
\{g_{n}^{-1}(r(0))\}_{n\geq 1}
remains at bounded distance from
r
, we have that indeed
d(g_{n}gg_{n}^{-1}(r(0)),r(0))=d(gg_{n}^{-1}(r(0)),g_{n}^{-1}(r(0)))
is a bounded sequence. Take now a convergent subsequent
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}g_{n_{k}}gg_{n_{k}}^{-1}=:h\in G
. By the hypothesis
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}g_{n}(\xi)=\eta_{+}
and by the continuity of the action of
G
on
\partial_{\infty}X
, we also have
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}g_{n_{k}}gg_{n_{k}}^{-1}(g_{n_{k}}(\xi))=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}g_{n_{k}}g(\xi)=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}g_{n_{k}}(\xi)=\eta_{+}=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}h(g_{n_{k}}(\xi))=h(\eta_{+})
. Now using
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}g_{n}(r(0))=\eta_{-}
and the same computation as above, we have
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}g_{n_{k}}gg_{n_{k}}^{-1}(g_{n_{k}}(r(0)))=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}g_{n_{k}}g(r(0))=\eta_{-}=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}h(g_{n_{k}}(r(0)))=h(\eta_{-})
.

Notice, as each element
g_{n}gg_{n}^{-1}
inherits the same properties as the element
g
(i.e., elliptic if
g
is elliptic, hyperbolic of the same translation length if
g
is hyperbolic), the same is true for a limiting element
h
.
∎

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
\Delta
be a locally finite thick affine building. Let

Let

of

of

Theorem 2.14
.

(Levi decomposition II)

Let
\Delta
be a locally finite thick affine building and
H
a closed subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}_{0}(\Delta)
that acts strongly transitively on
\Delta
. Let
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}\Delta
. Let

be a closed subgroup that is normalised by some radial sequence

with radial limit point
\xi

; hence

N^{u}
acts transitively on the
N
-orbits of
\xi_{-}

\mathrm{Opp}(\xi)

For the proof see
[
8

\{g_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
has the property that belongs to

we have
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}g_{n}(r(0))=\xi_{-}\in\partial_{\infty}X

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}g_{n}(\xi)=\xi

, any of the convergent subsequence

has the corresponding limit

, we have moreover that
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}g_{n_{k}}gg_{n_{k}}^{-1}=h\in N_{\xi_{-}}
. Because the properties of

are preserved by each of the elements

, then also the limit

inherits the properties of

(i.e., elliptic if

Proof of Theorem

.

The proof is the same as in

9

Let

\Delta

from

to

to chambers in

. This will produce an element

.

Secondly, notice that by the properties of the root groups (see for example

Thirdly, we prove that

in
\partial_{\infty}\Delta

Let
G
be a closed, strongly transitive and type-preserving subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(\Delta)
. Let
\mathcal{A}
be an apartment of
\Delta
and
\sigma
a simplex in
\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A}
. Then there exist a sequence of half-apartments
\{H_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
of
\mathcal{A}
and elements
\{k_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset G_{x_{0}}
such that:

1.

H_{n}\subsetneq H_{n+1}
, and
x_{0}\in H_{n}
, for every
n\geq 1

2.

\mathrm{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})\subset\partial_{\infty}(\mathcal{A}-H_{1})

3.

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}k_{n}=e
, and for every
n\geq 1
one has
k_{n}(H_{n})=H_{n}
and
k_{n}(\mathcal{A}-H_{n})\cap\mathcal{A}
is a wall.

Proof.

One uses Lemma
4.4
and the fact that, when
\Delta
is irreducible, by Theorems
1.5
and
1.7
, and Proposition
3.3
,
G
has many and enough elements.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
G
be a closed, strongly transitive and type-preserving subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(\Delta)
. Let
\mathcal{A}
be an apartment of
\Delta
,
\sigma
a simplex in
\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A}
and
M
a wall in
\mathcal{A}
. Suppose
\mathrm{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})\subset\partial_{\infty}H
, where
H
is one of the two half-apartments of
\mathcal{A}
associated with
M
. Let
k\in G
such that
k(\mathcal{A}-H)=\mathcal{A}-H
point-wise and
k(H)\cap\mathcal{A}=M
. Then for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
there exists
g\in G_{\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})}^{0}kG_{\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})}^{0}
that is hyperbolic, of translation axis perpendicular to
M
, containing
\mathcal{A}-H
in its translation apartment, of attracting end-point in
\partial_{\infty}(\mathcal{A}-H)
, and of translation length
2\ell
.

Proof.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Choose the
M
-parallel wall
M^{\prime}
contained in
H
such that
\operatorname{dist}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,M^{\prime})=\ell
. Let
H^{\prime}
be the half-apartment of
\mathcal{A}
with wall-boundary
M^{\prime}
and containing
\mathrm{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})
in its ideal boundary
\partial_{\infty}H^{\prime}
. By Lemma
4.4
there is
h\in G_{\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})}^{0}
fixing
H^{\prime}
point-wise and moving away the half apartment
\mathcal{A}-H^{\prime}
. Then
\mathcal{B}:=(\mathcal{A}-H^{\prime})\cup h(\mathcal{A}-H^{\prime})
is again an apartment of
\Delta
that contains
\mathcal{A}-H
as a half-apartment. Let
H^{\prime\prime}:=\mathcal{B}-(\mathcal{A}-H)
and notice this half-apartment has
M
as a boundary wall. Apply
k
to
H^{\prime\prime}
, and so by our construction
k(H^{\prime\prime})\cap\mathcal{A}=M
. Then
\mathcal{C}:=H\cup k(H^{\prime\prime})
is again an apartment in
\Delta
. We apply again Lemma
4.4
and find an element
h^{\prime}\in G_{\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})}^{0}
with
h^{\prime}(H)=H
point-wise and
h^{\prime}k(H^{\prime\prime})\subset\mathcal{A}
. By tracking the orientation during the construction, one notices
h^{\prime}kh
is a hyperbolic element of translation axis perpendicular to the wall
M
, containing
\mathcal{A}-H
in its translation apartment, and with attracting end-point in
\partial_{\infty}(\mathcal{A}-H)
. The translation length of
h^{\prime}kh
is exactly
2\ell
.
∎

Let
G
be a closed, strongly transitive and type-preserving subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(\Delta)
. Let
\mathcal{A}
be an apartment of
\Delta
, an ideal chamber
c
of the spherical apartment
\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A}
, and
x_{0}
a special vertex of
\mathcal{A}
. Then we have the well known
polar decomposition

G=G_{x_{0}}AG_{x_{0}}
, where
A:=\{g\in\mathrm{Stab}_{G_{0}}(\mathcal{A})\;|\;g\text{ hyperbolic}\}\cup\{e\}
is the subgroup of euclidean translations in the apartment
\mathcal{A}
(for a proof see for example
[
10
, Lemma 4.7]
). As well, by the well known
Bruhat decomposition
we have
G=G_{c}WG_{c}=G_{c}^{0}\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(\mathcal{A})G_{c}^{0}
, where
W
is the finite Weyl group associated with
\Delta
and its elements can be lifted (not uniquely) to elements in
\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(\mathcal{A})
.

In what follows we will consider unitary representations
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
of
G
that are strongly continuous. The idea of the proof of Theorem
1.9
is the same as in
[
7
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

Fix a unitary representation
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
of
G
without non-zero
G
-invariant vectors. Suppose there exist two non-zero vectors
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathcal{H}
such that the
(v_{1},v_{2})
-matrix coefficient does not vanish at
\infty
. As
G=G_{x_{0}}AG_{x_{0}}
there exist a sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset A
and non-zero vectors
v_{0},w_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
such that
a_{n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\infty
and
|\langle\pi(a_{n})(v_{0}),w_{0}\rangle|
does not converges to
0
, when
n\to\infty
(see for example
[
10
, Lemma 2.9]
).

As
a_{n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\infty
and
\Delta\cup\partial_{\infty}\Delta
is a compact space with respect to the cone topology, by extracting a subsequence, we can suppose in addition
\{a_{n}(x_{0})\}_{n\geq 1}
converges to an ideal point
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A}
contained in the interior of a minimal ideal simplex
\sigma\subset\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A}
. Then apply Proposition
4.3
to our sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(\mathcal{A})
of hyperbolic elements and the non-zero vectors
v_{0},w_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
to obtain a non-zero vector
w\in\mathcal{H}
such that
L:=\mathrm{Stab}_{G_{\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})}^{0}}(w)
is of finite index in
G_{\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})}^{0}
.

Then consider the
(w,w)
-matrix coefficient on
G
,
f:G\to\mathbb{C}
, given by
f(g):=\langle\pi(g)(w),w\rangle
. This map
f
is continuous with respect to the topology on
G
and
L
-bi-invariant (i.e.,
f(\ell_{1}g\ell_{2})=f(g)
for every
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in L
).

Choose some ideal chamber
c
with
\sigma\subset c\in\mathrm{Ch}(\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})
. Then we have
G_{\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})}^{0}\leq G_{c}
. If
\sigma
is just a sub-simplex of
c
, then
G_{\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})}^{0}
is of infinite index in
G_{c}^{0}
, the latter being a normal subgroup of
G_{c}
. Still, one can use Bruhat decomposition
G=G_{c}WG_{c}=G_{c}^{0}\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(\mathcal{A})G_{c}^{0}
and write
G=LHL
, where
H
is a subset of
G
containing many elements from
\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(\mathcal{A})
(e.g., hyperbolic elements), as well as many other elements.

By Lemma
4.6
, consider a sequence
\{k_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset G_{x_{0}}
and a sequence of half-apartments
\{H_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
in
\mathcal{A}
with the required properties. By the continuity of the map
f
we have

f(Lk_{n}L)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}f(L)=1.

Let
j\in\mathbb{N}
. By Lemma
4.7
, applied to every
k_{n}
, the double coset
G_{\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})}^{0}k_{n}G_{\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})}^{0}
contains a hyperbolic element
g_{n}=h_{n}k_{n}h_{n}^{\prime}
, with
h_{n},h_{n}^{\prime}\in G_{\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})}^{0}
, of translation length
2j
, containing the half-apartment
H_{n}
in its translation apartment, of translation axis perpendicular to boundary wall of
H_{n}
, and of attracting end-point in
\partial_{\infty}(H_{n})\subset\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A}
. Then
\{g_{n}=h_{n}k_{n}h_{n}^{\prime}\}_{n\geq 1}
admits a subsequence, by simplicity denoted by
\{g_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
, that converges to an element
g_{j}\in A
that is hyperbolic, of attracting endpoint in
\partial_{\infty}(H_{1})\subset\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A}
, of translation length
2j
, and whose translation axis is perpendicular to the boundary wall of
H_{1}
.

Moreover, as
L
has finite index in
G_{\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})}^{0}
and
h_{n},h_{n}^{\prime}\in G_{\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})}^{0}
, by passing to a subsequence
\{g_{n_{i}}\}_{i\geq 1}
we can write
h_{n_{i}}=\sigma\ell_{n_{i}}
,
h_{n_{i}}^{\prime}=\ell_{n_{i}}^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}
, with
\ell_{n_{i}},\ell_{n_{i}}^{\prime}\in L
and
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in G_{\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})}^{0}
. Then

\lim\limits_{i\to\infty}\ell_{n_{i}}k_{n_{i}}\ell_{n_{i}}^{\prime}=\sigma^{-1}g_{j}(\sigma^{\prime})^{-1}

and because
g_{j}G_{\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})}^{0}g_{j}^{-1}=G_{\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})}^{0}
(see Lemma
4.2
) we can write further as

\lim\limits_{i\to\infty}\ell_{n_{i}}k_{n_{i}}\ell_{n_{i}}^{\prime}=g_{j}\sigma_{j},\text{ with }\sigma_{j}\in G_{\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})}^{0}.

Then as well by the continuity of the map
f
we have

f(Lk_{n}L)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}f(Lg_{j}\sigma_{j}L)

and therefore
f(Lg_{j}\sigma_{j}L)=1=\langle\pi(g_{j}\sigma_{j})(w),w\rangle
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
. We have the equality
\langle\pi(g_{j}\sigma_{j})(w),w\rangle=\langle w,w\rangle
if and only if
w
is
g_{j}\sigma_{j}
-invariant, for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
L
has finite index in
G_{\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})}^{0}
, we can choose
\{\sigma_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset G_{\operatorname{St}(\sigma,\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{A})}^{0}/L
that is a finite set. As
g_{j}g_{j+i}^{-1}
is still a hyperbolic element of
A
, for every
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
, one can conclude there is a hyperbolic element
a\in A-\{e\}
such that
w
is
a
-invariant. Then by standard methods as Mautner’s phenomenon (see for example
[
10
, Prop. 2.12]
) applied to the sequence
\{a^{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
, resp.,
\{a^{-n}\}_{n\geq 1}
, one has
w
is
U_{a}^{-}
and
U_{a}^{+}
-invariant. If in addition
G
is topologically simple, by
[
10
, Corollary 4.18]
, the non-zero vector
w
is
G
-invariant. This is a contradiction with our assumption at the beginning of the proof, and so the theorem is proven.
∎

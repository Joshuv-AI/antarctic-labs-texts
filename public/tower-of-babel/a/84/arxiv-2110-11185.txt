Circular average relative to fractal measures
Seheon Ham, Hyerim Ko, Sanghyuk Lee

Abstract
. We prove new L p L^{p} – L q L^{q} estimates for averages over dilates of the circle with respect to fractal measures, which unify different types of maximal estimates for the circular average. Our results are consequences of L p L^{p} – L q L^{q} smoothing estimates for the wave operator relative to fractal measures. We also discuss similar results concerning the spherical averages.

1.
introduction

Let
d\geq 2
. We consider the average

\mathcal{A}f(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}f(x-ty)d\sigma(y),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Here
d\sigma
denotes the normalized measure on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.
We study estimates for
\mathcal{A}
relative to fractal measures.
To motivate our study, we briefly review some of the previous results.

Stein
[
26
]
(
d\geq 3
) and Bourgain
[
4
]
(

) proved that the maximal operator
f\mapsto\sup_{t>0}|\mathcal{A}f(\cdot,t)|
is bounded on
L^{p}

if and only if
\frac{d}{d-1}<p\leq\infty
. If the supremum is taken over a compact interval
I
contained in
(0,\infty)
, the consequent maximal function
has the
L^{p}
improving property (
[
24
,
25
,
14
]
). That is to say, the estimate

(1.1)

\|\sup_{t\in I}|\mathcal{A}f(\cdot,t)|\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{d}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

holds for some
p<q
. Except for a few endpoint cases, we now have a complete characterization of
p,q
for which (
1.1
) holds.
Let
\mathcal{P}_{d}\subset[0,1]\times[0,1]
be the convex hull of the set consisting of the points
O:=(0,0),~Q_{1}:=\big(\frac{d(d-1)}{d^{2}+1},\frac{d-1}{d^{2}+1}\big),
and
Q_{2}:=\big(\frac{d-1}{d},\frac{1}{d}\big),~Q_{3}:=\big(\frac{d-1}{d},\frac{d-1}{d}\big)
.
Schlag
[
24
]
(
d=2
), and Schlag and Sogge
[
25
]
(

) showed that the estimate (

(p^{-1},q^{-1})\in\mathcal{P}_{d}\setminus((O,Q_{1})\cup[Q_{1},Q_{2})\cup[Q_{2},Q_{3}])
and it fails if
(p^{-1},q^{-1})\not\in({{\mathcal{P}_{d}}}\setminus\{Q_{3}\})
.
The third named author
[
14
]
proved the borderline case
(p^{-1},q^{-1})\in(O,Q_{1})\cup(Q_{1},Q_{2})\cup(Q_{2},Q_{3})

remain open.
See also

for recent developments regarding the circular and spherical maximal functions.

Different forms of maximal estimates have also been of interest. In particular, the estimate

(1.2)

\|\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{A}f(x,\cdot)|\|_{L^{q}_{t}(I)}\leq C_{d}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\epsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

Definition 1.1
.

For
\alpha\in(0,d+1]
, we say a non-negative Borel measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
is
\alpha
-dimensional if there is a constant
C_{\nu}

such that

(1.3)

\nu(\mathbb{B}^{d+1}(z,\rho))\leq C_{\nu}\rho^{\alpha},\quad\forall(z,\rho)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},

where
\mathbb{B}^{d+1}(z,\rho)=\{z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:|z-z^{\prime}|<\rho\}
.
By
\mathfrak{C}^{d+1}(\alpha)
we denote the class of
\alpha
-dimensional measure
\nu
.
For
\nu\in\mathfrak{C}^{d+1}(\alpha)
, we also define

\langle\nu\rangle_{\alpha}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1},\,\rho>0}\rho^{-\alpha}\nu(\mathbb{B}^{d+1}(z,\rho)).

For the rest of the paper, we fix
I=(1,2)
. We consider the estimate

(1.4)

\|\mathcal{A}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I;d\nu)}\leq C\langle\nu\rangle_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Remarkably, the estimate (
1.4
) unifies the maximal estimates (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) in a single framework. Indeed, it is not difficult to see that (
1.4
) implies the
seemingly unrelated estimates (
1.1
) and (
1.2
). One can deduce
the estimate (
1.1
) from (
1.4
) if the latter holds with a uniform

for all
d
-dimensional measures with
\langle\nu\rangle_{d}\leq 1
. This can be shown by the Kolmogorov-Seliverstov-Plessner linearization argument and the Riesz representation theorem (see Lemma
3.2
).
Similarly, (
1.2
) follows if (
1.4
) with

holds uniformly for all
1
-dimensional measures.

Since the averaging operator
\mathcal{A}
is translation-invariant in

1.4
) unless
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
.
Also, taking a specific
\alpha
-dimensional measure, one can show (
1.4
) holds only if

\alpha p\geq q
(see

Thus it is natural for (
1.4

When

We discuss the matter in Section

In this paper we focus on the more interesting case

Theorem 1.3

Let

As a corollary we obtain

Let

\big\|\sup_{t\in I}|\mathcal{A}f(\cdot,t)|\big\|_{L^{q}(d\mu)}\leq C\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

2.
Weighted local smoothing estimates

In this section, we obtain some preparatory results which we use in proving Theorem
1.5
and Theorem
1.6
.
The main object is to prove Proposition
2.1
and Proposition
2.12
.
To this end, we adapt the argument due to Vargas and one of the authors
[
16
]
which is based on the trilinear restriction estimate.

We decompose
e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f
into two parts: a sum of operators with small frequency supports and a product of operators with angularly separated frequency supports.
For the latter we apply the multilinear restriction estimate due to Bennett, Carbery, and Tao
[
2
]
by making use of the fact that angular separation implies transversality on the trilinear operator.
For the first part, we rescale the operator and apply an induction argument (see (
2.5
)).
However, the support of a rescaled measure is no longer contained in a bounded set, so the induction can not be carried out on a bounded set.
To get around this issue, we consider a class of weight functions which are not necessarily supported in a bounded set.

2.1.
Weighted local smoothing estimates in
\mathbb{R}^{2+1}

For
0<\alpha\leq 3
, we denote by
\Omega(\alpha)
the class of nonnegative measurable functions
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times I\mapsto\mathbb{R}
which satisfy

(2.1)

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{3}(z,r)}\omega(y)dy\leq r^{\alpha},\quad\forall(z,r)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.

By Littlewood-Paley decomposition,
it is enough to consider the estimate

(2.2)

\|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}P_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times I;\omega)}\leq C\lambda^{\gamma}\|f\|_{p}.

Here
P_{\lambda}
is the standard Littlewood-Paley projection operator given by
\widehat{P_{\lambda}f}(\xi)=\beta(\lambda^{-1}|\xi|)\widehat{f}(\xi)
for
\beta\in\mathrm{C}_{c}^{\infty}((1/2,2))
and
\|f\|_{L^{q}(E;\omega)}^{q}:=\int_{E}|f(x)|^{q}\omega(x)dx
.

Instead of proving (
2.2
) directly, we consider a modified operator to facilitate rescaling. We consider

\psi_{{0}}(\theta):=\sqrt{1+\theta^{2}}-1.

Note that
\psi_{{0}}
can be regarded as a small perturbation of
\theta^{2}/2
.

For
\lambda\geq 1
and
J\subset[-1,1]
, let

A_{\lambda}(J)=\{(\eta,\rho):\lambda/4\leq\eta\leq 4\lambda,~\theta:=\rho/\eta\in J\}.

We define

(2.3)

T_{\lambda}^{\phi}g(x,t)=\int_{A_{\lambda}([-1,1])}e^{i(x,t)\cdot(\eta,\rho,\eta\phi(\rho/\eta))}\beta(\lambda^{-1}\eta)\beta_{0}(|\rho|/\eta)\widehat{g}(\eta,\rho)d\eta d\rho,

where
\beta_{0}
is a smooth function supported in
[0,2]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
1<\alpha\leq 2
and
\omega\in\Omega(\alpha)
.
If
p,q\geq 1
satisfy (
1.5
) and
\lambda\gg 1
, then for some
\gamma<1/2
we have

(2.4)

\|T_{\lambda}^{\psi_{0}}g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times I;\omega)}\leq C\lambda^{\gamma}\|g\|_{p}.

We deduce the estimate (
2.2
) from (
2.4
) via a change of variable (see the proof of Theorem
1.5
in Section
3
).

Normalization of the phase function.

For our induction argument, we begin with normalizing the phase function of
T_{\lambda}g
.
Let

\phi_{\circ}(\theta)=\frac{1}{2}\theta^{2}.

For a given
\epsilon>0
, we define a class
\mathfrak{S}(\epsilon)
of
\mathrm{C}^{3}
functions by

\mathfrak{S}(\epsilon)=\{\phi\in\mathrm{C}^{3}([-1,1]):\phi(0)=\phi^{\prime}(0)=0,~\|\phi-\phi_{\circ}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{3}([-1,1])}\leq\epsilon\},

where
\|\phi\|_{\mathrm{C}^{n}([-1,1])}=\sum_{j\leq n}\sup_{\theta\in[-1,1]}\big|\partial_{\theta}^{j}\phi(\theta)\big|
.
For
\theta_{\circ}\in(-1,1)
, let
h
be a real number such that
[\theta_{\circ}-h,\theta_{\circ}+h]\subset[-1,1]
.
For
\phi\in\mathrm{C}^{3}([-1,1])
satisfying
\phi^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\circ})\neq 0
, we define

\phi_{\theta_{\circ},h}(\theta)=(\phi^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\circ})h^{2})^{-1}\big(\phi(h\theta+\theta_{\circ})-\phi(\theta_{\circ})-\phi^{\prime}(\theta_{\circ})h\theta\big).

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
\phi\in\mathfrak{S}(\epsilon_{\circ})
for some
0<\epsilon_{\circ}<1/8
. Then, there exists
0<h_{\circ}=h_{\circ}(\epsilon_{\circ})
such that
\phi_{\theta_{\circ},h}\in\mathfrak{S}(\epsilon_{\circ})
whenever
0<h\leq h_{\circ}
and
[\theta_{\circ}-h,\theta_{\circ}+h]\subset[-1,1]
.

Throughout the paper,
\epsilon_{\circ}
denotes a positive constant such that
0<\epsilon_{\circ}<1/8
.

Proof.

By Taylor series expansion, we have

\displaystyle\phi(h\theta+\theta_{\circ})-\phi(\theta_{\circ})-\phi^{\prime}(\theta_{\circ})h\theta=\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\circ})h^{2}\theta^{2}}{2}+\mathcal{E}(\theta_{\circ},h,\theta),

where
\|\mathcal{E}(\theta_{\circ},h,\cdot)\|_{\mathrm{C}^{3}([-1,1])}\leq Ch^{3}
with a constant
C
independent of
\theta_{\circ}\in[-1,1]
.
Since
\phi\in\mathfrak{S}(\epsilon_{\circ})
,
\phi^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\circ})\in[3/4,5/4]
.
So, we see

\|\phi_{\theta_{\circ},h}-\phi_{\circ}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{3}([-1,1])}\leq(\phi^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\circ})h^{2})^{-1}\|\mathcal{E}(\theta_{\circ},h,\cdot)\|_{\mathrm{C}^{3}([-1,1])}\leq Ch.

Thus, there exists
h_{\circ}\in(0,\epsilon_{\circ}]
such that
\phi_{\theta_{\circ},h}\in\mathfrak{S}(\epsilon_{\circ})

for
0<h\leq h_{\circ}
.
∎

Induction quantity.

As mentioned before, our proof is based on an induction on scale argument.
We introduce a quantity which makes it possible to carry out the induction argument.

For
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
, we define
Q(\lambda)=Q(\lambda,\epsilon_{\circ},p,q,\alpha)
by

(2.5)

\begin{split}Q(\lambda)=\sup\Big\{\|T_{\lambda}^{\phi}g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times I;\omega)}:\ &{\rm supp}\,{\widehat{g}}\subset A_{\lambda}([-1,1]),~\|g\|_{p}\leq 1,\\
\ &\phi\in\mathfrak{S}(\epsilon_{\circ}),~\omega\in\Omega(\alpha)\Big\}.\end{split}

It is easy to see
Q(\lambda)<\infty
. Indeed,
one can show a rough bound

(2.6)

Q(\lambda)\lesssim\lambda^{2},\quad\lambda\geq 1

for
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
.
We write
T_{\lambda}^{\phi}g(x,t)=K_{\lambda}(\cdot,t)\ast g
, where the kernel is given by

(2.7)

\displaystyle K_{\lambda}(x,t)=\int e^{i(x,t)\cdot(\eta,\rho,\eta\phi(\rho/\eta))}\beta(\lambda^{-1}\eta)\beta_{0}(|\rho/\eta|)d\eta d\rho.

In order to show (
2.6
), we use an easy estimate:

(2.8)

|K_{\lambda}(x,t)|\lesssim\mathcal{K}(x):=\lambda^{2}\big(1+|x|\big)^{-M},\quad t\in I

for any
M\geq 1
,
which follows by routine integration by parts (for example, see (
3.6
) below).
Using (
2.1
), it is easy to see

(2.9)

\displaystyle\Big\|\int\mathcal{K}(x-y)g(y)\,dy\Big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times I,\omega)}\lesssim\lambda^{2}\|g\|_{p}

for
1\leq p\leq q
.
Indeed, since
\int_{I}\int\mathcal{K}(x-y)\,\omega(x,t)\,dxdt\lesssim\lambda^{2}

and
\int\mathcal{K}(x-y)dy\lesssim\lambda^{2}
,
(
2.9
) follows for
p=q
by Schur’s test.
Also, by Hölder’s inequality, we get (
2.9
) for
q=\infty
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
Interpolating those estimates, we have (
2.9
) and therefore (
2.6
) for
1\leq p\leq q
.

In what follows, we show
Q(\lambda)\leq C_{\epsilon}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon}
for some
\epsilon>0
.

Rescaling

We first observe that
L^{p}
–
L^{q}
bound on
T_{\lambda}^{\phi}
is improved on a certain range of
p,q
if
\widehat{g}
is supported in a narrow conic neighborhood.
More precisely, we have the following.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
0<\alpha\leq 3
,
\omega\in\Omega(\alpha)
, and
\phi\in\mathfrak{S}(\epsilon_{\circ})
.
For
h\geq\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, let
J_{h}=[\theta_{\circ}-h,\theta_{\circ}+h]\subset[-1,1]
.
Suppose
{\rm supp}\,\widehat{g}\subset A_{\lambda}(J_{h})
.
Then,
there is an
h_{\circ}=h_{\circ}(\epsilon_{\circ})
such that

(2.10)

\|T_{\lambda}^{\phi}g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times I;\omega)}\leq Ch^{\frac{2\alpha-\kappa(\alpha)}{q}-\frac{3}{p}}Q(\phi^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\circ})h^{2}\lambda)\|g\|_{p}

whenever
h\leq h_{\circ}
.

We note
\kappa(\alpha)=\min\{3,\alpha+1,2\alpha\}
(see (
1.8
)).

Proof.

Since
\widehat{g}(\eta,\rho)
is supported in
A_{\lambda}(J_{h})
, we have
\lambda/4\leq\eta\leq 4\lambda
and
\theta=\rho/\eta\in J_{h}
i.e.,
|\theta-\theta_{\circ}|\leq h
.
We set

D_{\theta_{\circ},h}=\frac{1}{\phi^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\circ})}\begin{bmatrix}h^{-2}&h^{-2}\theta_{\circ}&h^{-2}\phi(\theta_{\circ})\\
0&h^{-1}&h^{-1}\phi^{\prime}(\theta_{\circ})\\
0&0&\phi^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\circ})\end{bmatrix},

and

L_{\theta_{\circ},h}(\eta,\rho)=\frac{1}{\phi^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\circ})}(h^{-2}\eta,h^{-1}\rho+h^{-2}\theta_{\circ}\eta).

Let
z=(x,t)
. Note that

z\cdot(\eta,\rho,\eta\phi(\rho/\eta))\mapsto D_{\theta_{\circ},h}\,z\cdot(\eta,\rho,\eta\phi_{\theta_{\circ},h}(\rho/\eta))
under the transformation
(\eta,\rho)\mapsto L_{\theta_{\circ},h}(\eta,\rho)
.
Changing variables
(\eta,\rho)\mapsto L_{\theta_{\circ},h}(\eta,\rho)
, we obtain

T_{\lambda}^{\phi}g(z)=T_{\lambda_{1}}^{\phi_{\theta_{\circ},h}}g_{\theta_{\circ},h}(D_{\theta_{\circ},h}z),\qquad\lambda_{1}=\phi^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\circ})h^{2}\lambda,

where

\widehat{g_{\theta_{\circ},h}}(\eta,\rho)={\det(L_{\theta_{\circ},h})}\widehat{g}(L_{\theta_{\circ},h}(\eta,\rho)).

Clearly,
\widehat{g_{\theta_{\circ},h}}
is supported in
A_{\lambda_{1}}([-1,1])
. By Lemma
2.2

there exists
h_{\circ}=h_{\circ}(\epsilon_{\circ})
such that

\phi_{\theta_{\circ},h}\in\mathfrak{S}(\epsilon_{\circ})
for
h\leq h_{\circ}
.

Let us define

\omega_{\theta_{\circ},h}(z)=(1+\widetilde{C})^{-1}h^{\kappa(\alpha)-2\alpha+3}\omega(D_{\theta_{\circ},h}^{-1}z),

where
\widetilde{C}>0
is a constant to be chosen later. We claim
\omega_{\theta_{\circ},h}\in\Omega(\alpha)
if
\widetilde{C}>0
is large enough.
To show this, we first observe

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{3}(y_{\circ},r)}\omega(D_{\theta_{\circ},h}^{-1}z)dz=h^{-3}\int_{D_{\theta_{\circ},h}^{-1}\mathbb{B}^{3}(y_{\circ},r)}\omega(z)dz.

So, it is sufficient to show

(2.11)

\displaystyle\int_{D_{\theta_{\circ},h}^{-1}\mathbb{B}^{3}(y_{\circ},r)}\omega(z)dz\leq Cr^{\alpha}h^{2\alpha-\kappa(\alpha)}.

Note that
D_{\theta_{\circ},h}^{-1}\mathbb{B}^{3}(y_{\circ},r)
is contained in a box
of dimensions

ch^{2}r\times chr\times cr
for a constant
c>0
. We can cover
D_{\theta_{\circ},h}^{-1}\mathbb{B}^{3}(y_{\circ},r)

with as many as
Ch^{-3}
,
Ch^{-1}
, and
C
balls of radii
h^{2}r
,
hr
, and
r
, respectively. By (
2.1
)
the left hand side of (
2.11
) is bounded above by
C\min\{r^{\alpha}h^{2\alpha-3},r^{\alpha}h^{\alpha-1},r^{\alpha}\}=Cr^{\alpha}h^{2\alpha-\min\{3,\alpha+1,2\alpha\}}
for some
C
.

Changing variables
z\mapsto D_{\theta_{\circ},h}^{-1}z
,
we have

(2.12)

\displaystyle\|T_{\lambda}^{\phi}g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times I;\omega)}^{q}

\displaystyle=(1+\widetilde{C})h^{2\alpha-\kappa(\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times I}\big|T_{\lambda_{1}}^{\phi_{\theta_{\circ},h}}g_{\theta_{\circ},h}(z)\big|^{q}\omega_{\theta_{\circ},h}(z)dz.

Since
\phi_{\theta_{\circ},h}\in\mathfrak{S}(\epsilon_{\circ})
,
\omega_{\theta_{\circ},h}\in\Omega(\alpha)
, and
\widehat{g_{\theta_{\circ},h}}
is supported in
A_{\lambda_{1}}([-1,1])
, by the definition of
Q
we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times I}\big|T_{\lambda_{1}}^{\phi_{\theta_{\circ},h}}g_{\theta_{\circ},h}(z)\big|^{q}\omega_{\theta_{\circ},h}(z)dz\leq\big(Q(\phi^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\circ})h^{2}\lambda)\|g_{\theta_{\circ},h}\|_{p}\big)^{q}.

Therefore, we get (
2.10
) since
\|g_{\theta_{\circ},h}\|_{p}=h^{-\frac{3}{p}}\|g\|_{p}
.
∎

2.2.
Trilinear estimate.

In this section, we obtain a weighted local
L^{p}
–
L^{q}
estimate for the trilinear operator
\prod_{i=1}^{3}T_{\lambda}^{\phi}g_{i}
by interpolating the multilinear restriction estimate due to Bennett, Carbery and Tao
[
2
]
, the local smoothing estimate in
[
8
]
, and an easy
L^{1}
–
L^{\infty}
estimate.
Subsequently, we extend the local estimate to a global one by using decay of the kernel
K_{\lambda}
.
For simplicity, we write

T_{\lambda}=T_{\lambda}^{\phi}.

For
\phi\in\mathfrak{S}(\epsilon_{\circ})

and an interval
J\subset[-1,1]
, we consider a truncated conic surface

\Gamma_{\lambda}(J):=\{(\eta,\rho,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\tau=\eta\phi(\rho/\eta),~(\eta,\rho)\in A_{\lambda}(J)\}.

Let
\mathrm{N}(\theta)
be the normal vector to
\Gamma_{\lambda}(J)
at
\eta(1,\theta,\phi(\theta))
. A computation gives

(2.13)

\mathrm{N}(\theta)=(\phi(\theta)-\theta\phi^{\prime}(\theta),\phi^{\prime}(\theta),-1).

The following is a consequence of the multilinear restriction estimate due to Bennett-Carbery-Tao
[
2
]
.
We denote by
d\sigma_{\lambda}
the surface measure on
\Gamma_{\lambda}([-1,1])
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\phi\in\mathfrak{S}(\epsilon_{\circ})
and
J_{1},J_{2},J_{3}\subset[-1,1]
be intervals.
Suppose

(2.14)

\det(\mathrm{N}(\theta_{1}),\mathrm{N}(\theta_{2}),\mathrm{N}(\theta_{3}))\geq\delta

holds for some
\delta>0
whenever
\theta_{i}\in J_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
.
If
\epsilon_{\circ}
is sufficiently small and
\lambda\gg\delta^{-1}
, then for
\epsilon>0

\Big\|\prod_{i=1}^{3}\widehat{f_{i}d\sigma_{\lambda}}\Big\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{3}(0,10))}\leq C_{\epsilon}\lambda^{\epsilon}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\lambda}(J_{i}))}

whenever
{\rm supp}\,f_{i}\subset\Gamma_{\lambda}(J_{i})
,
i=1,2,3
.

By interpolation with the local smoothing estimate and an easy
L^{1}-L^{\infty}
estimate, we get the following.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\phi\in\mathfrak{S}(\epsilon_{\circ})
, and let
J_{1},J_{2},J_{3}\subset[-1,1]
be intervals.
Also let
\frac{1}{q}\leq\min(\frac{1}{p},~\frac{1}{3p}+\frac{1}{6},~\frac{2}{3p^{\prime}})
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
Suppose (
2.14
) holds whenever
\theta_{i}\in J_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
.
If
\epsilon_{\circ}
is sufficiently small and
\lambda\gg\delta^{-1}
,
then we have

(2.15)

\Big\|\prod_{i=1}^{3}T_{\lambda}g_{i}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{q}{3}}(\mathbb{B}^{2}(0,1)\times I)}\leq C\lambda^{3\gamma_{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|g_{i}\|_{p},

for
\gamma_{0}>\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{3}{q}
whenever
{\rm supp}\,\widehat{g_{i}}\subset A_{\lambda}(J_{i})
,
i=1,2,3
.

Proof.

By interpolation it is enough to show (
2.15
) for
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})=(0,0)
,
(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{4})
,
(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{3})
, and
(1,0)
if
\gamma_{0}>\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{3}{q}
.
For the first two cases, (
2.15
) follows from the local smoothing estimate (
1.7
) with
d\nu=dxdt
which holds for
4\leq p=q\leq\infty
and
\gamma_{0}>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{p}
.
By Theorem
2.4
and Plancherel’s theorem, we have (
2.15
) with
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})=(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{3})
for
\gamma_{0}>0
.
Also, (
2.7
) and van der Corput’s lemma yield
\|K_{\lambda}\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda^{3/2}
(e.g. (
3.6
)), which gives

\|T_{\lambda}g_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{2}(0,1)\times I)}\lesssim\lambda^{3/2}\|g_{i}\|_{1}
. Thus, we have (
2.15
) with
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})=(1,0)
for
\gamma_{0}\geq 3/2
.
∎

From (
2.8
), we see that
K_{\lambda}(\cdot,t)
decays rapidly away from
\mathbb{B}^{2}(0,1)
.
Using this, we can extend the local estimate (
2.15
) to a global one.

Lemma 2.6
.

Under the same assumptions as in Proposition
2.5
, we have

(2.16)

\Big\|\prod_{i=1}^{3}T_{\lambda}g_{i}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{q}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times I)}\leq C\lambda^{3\gamma_{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|g_{i}\|_{p}

for
\gamma_{0}>\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{3}{q}
.

Proof.

We follow a standard localization argument (e.g., see
[
15
, Proposition 2.10]
).
For a constant
r
satisfying
\lambda^{-1}\ll r\ll\delta
,
let
N_{r\lambda}(A_{\lambda}(J_{i}))
denote the
r\lambda
-neighborhood of
A_{\lambda}(J_{i})
,
i=1,2,3
.
Let
\varphi_{i}
be a smooth function supported in
N_{r\lambda}(A_{\lambda}(J_{i}))
such that

\varphi_{i}=1
on
A_{\lambda}(J_{i})
and
|\partial_{\xi}^{m}\varphi_{i}(\xi)|\lesssim|\xi|^{-|m|}
.

Let us denote by
\mathcal{F}_{x}
the Fourier transform in
x
and define
\mathcal{F}_{x}(\widetilde{K}_{\lambda,i}(\cdot,t))=\mathcal{F}_{x}(K_{\lambda}(\cdot,t))\varphi_{i}
.
Then
T_{\lambda}g_{i}(x,t)=\widetilde{K}_{\lambda,i}(\cdot,t)\ast g_{i}(x)
.
We consider a collection
\{B\}
of finitely overlapping unit balls which cover
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Fixing
\varepsilon>0
, let
\widetilde{B}
be a ball of radius
\lambda^{\varepsilon}
with the same center as
B
.
For each
i
, we decompose
g_{i}=g_{i,B}+g_{i,B^{\mathrm{c}}}
where
g_{i,B}:=g_{i}\chi_{\widetilde{B}}
and
g_{i,B^{\mathrm{c}}}=g_{i}\chi_{\widetilde{B}^{\mathrm{c}}}
. We get

\displaystyle\Big\|\prod_{i=1}^{3}T_{\lambda}g_{i}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{q}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times I)}^{\frac{q}{3}}\lesssim\sum_{B}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}):\\
m_{j}\in\{B,B^{\mathrm{c}}\}\end{subarray}}\int_{I}\int_{B}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\big|\widetilde{K}_{\lambda,i}(\cdot,t)\ast(g_{i,m_{i}})(x)\big|^{\frac{q}{3}}dxdt.

We consider the case
m_{1}=m_{2}=m_{3}=B
first. Since the Fourier transform of
\widetilde{K}_{\lambda,i}(\cdot,t)\ast(g_{i,B})
is supported in
N_{r\lambda}(A_{\lambda}(J_{i}))
,
the transversality condition (
2.14
) holds for
2^{-1}\delta
replacing
\delta
if we take a sufficiently small
r
.
Applying Proposition
2.5
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{B}\int_{B\times I}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\big|\widetilde{K}_{\lambda,i}(\cdot,t)\ast(g_{i,B})(x)\big|^{\frac{q}{3}}dxdt\lesssim\lambda^{q\gamma_{0}}\sum_{B}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|g_{i}\chi_{\widetilde{B}}\|_{p}^{\frac{q}{3}}\lesssim\lambda^{q\gamma_{0}+\tilde{c}\varepsilon}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|g_{i}\|_{p}^{\frac{q}{3}}

for
\gamma_{0}>\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{3}{q}
and
\tilde{c}>0
. The second inequality follows by
Hölder’s inequality and the inclusion
\ell^{p}\subset\ell^{q}
for
p\leq q
since the balls
\widetilde{B}
overlap at most
C\lambda^{2\varepsilon}
.

If
m_{i}=B^{\mathrm{c}}
for some
i
, we use decay of
\widetilde{K}_{\lambda,i}
.
As before, it is easy to show

|\widetilde{K}_{\lambda,i}(x,t)|\lesssim\lambda^{2}(1+|x|)^{-M}
for any
M\geq 1
(c.f., (
2.8
)).
Setting
\mathscr{K}(x)=(1+|x|)^{-3}
, we see
|\widetilde{K}_{\lambda,i}(\cdot,t)\ast g_{i,B}(x)|\lesssim\lambda^{2}\mathscr{K}\ast|g_{i}|(x)

and

\big|\widetilde{K}_{\lambda,i}(\cdot,t)\ast g_{i,B^{\mathrm{c}}}(x)\big|\lesssim\lambda^{2}\lambda^{\varepsilon(3-M)}\mathscr{K}\ast|g_{i}|(x)
if
x\in B
.
Thus we have

\prod_{i=1}^{3}\big|\widetilde{K}_{\lambda,i}(\cdot,t)\ast(g_{i,m_{i}})(x)\big|\lesssim\lambda^{c_{1}-c_{2}\varepsilon M}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\mathscr{K}\ast|g_{i}|(x),\quad(x,t)\in B\times I,

for some constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
.
By Hölder’s inequality and Young’s convolution inequality, there are positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{B}\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})\neq(B,B,B)}\iint_{B\times I}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\big|\widetilde{K}_{\lambda,i}(\cdot,t)\ast(g_{i,m_{i}})(x)\big|^{\frac{q}{3}}dxdt\lesssim\lambda^{c_{1}-c_{2}\varepsilon M}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|g_{i}\|_{p}^{\frac{q}{3}}

for
p\leq q
. Combining the estimates above,
we obtain

\Big\|\prod_{i=1}^{3}T_{\lambda}g_{i}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{q}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times I)}^{\frac{q}{3}}\lesssim\big(\lambda^{q\gamma_{0}+\tilde{c}\varepsilon}+\lambda^{c_{1}-c_{2}\varepsilon M}\big)\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|g_{i}\|_{p}^{\frac{q}{3}}

for
\gamma_{0}>\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{p}-\frac{3}{q}
. Taking
\varepsilon>0
small enough and then a large
M
,
we obtain the desired estimate (
2.16
).
∎

We now obtain (
2.16
) with weights
\omega\in\Omega(\alpha)
via the following lemma.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\omega\in\Omega(\alpha)
,
0<\alpha\leq 3
.
Suppose
\widehat{F}
is supported in
\mathbb{B}^{3}(0,\lambda)
. Then, we have

\|F\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\omega)}\lesssim\lambda^{(3-\alpha)/q}\|F\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
for
q\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be a Schwartz function such that
\widehat{\varphi}=1
on
\mathbb{B}^{3}(0,1)
,
and set
\varphi_{\lambda}=\lambda^{3}\varphi(\lambda\cdot)
.
Since
F=F\ast\varphi_{\lambda}
, we have
\|F\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\omega)}\leq\|F\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\||\varphi_{\lambda}|\ast\omega\|_{\infty}^{1/q}
.
By rapid decay of
\varphi
, we see

\displaystyle|\varphi_{\lambda}|\ast\omega(y)\leq C_{N}\lambda^{3}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{-N\ell}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{3}(y,2^{\ell}\lambda^{-1})}\omega(z)dz

for any
N>3
. Since
\omega\in\Omega(\alpha)
, it follows by (
2.1
) that
\||\varphi_{\lambda}|\ast\omega\|_{\infty}\lesssim\lambda^{3-\alpha}
. Hence, we get the desired bound.
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\phi\in\mathfrak{S}(\epsilon_{\circ})
,
\omega\in\Omega(\alpha)
,
0<\alpha\leq 3
, and
\lambda^{-1}\ll\delta\ll 1
.
Suppose (
2.14
) holds whenever
\theta_{i}\in J_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
.
If (
2.16
) holds for some
\gamma_{0}
, then

(2.17)

\Big\|\prod_{i=1}^{3}T_{\lambda}g_{i}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{q}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times I;\omega)}\leq C\lambda^{3\gamma}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|g_{i}\|_{p}

holds for
\gamma=\gamma_{0}+\frac{3-\alpha}{q}
whenever
{\rm supp}\,\widehat{g_{i}}\subset A_{\lambda}(J_{i})
.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{\chi}\in\mathrm{C}_{0}^{\infty}((1/2,4))
such that
\widetilde{\chi}=1
on
I=[1,2]
.
We only have to show (
2.17
) with
T_{\lambda}
replaced by
\widetilde{\chi}\,T_{\lambda}
.
Since
\widetilde{\chi}
is compactly supported, the support of the space-time Fourier transform of
\widetilde{\chi}(t)T_{\lambda}g_{i}(x,t)
is unbounded.
So, in order to apply Lemma
2.7
,
we decompose
\widetilde{\chi}\,T_{\lambda}f
in such a way that the Fourier supports of the consequent operators are contained in either a bounded set or its complement.
Let us define a frequency localized operator
T_{\lambda}^{1}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{x,t}\big(T_{\lambda}^{1}g\big)(\eta,\rho,\tau)=\mathcal{F}_{x,t}\big(\widetilde{\chi}\,T_{\lambda}g\big)(\eta,\rho,\tau)\beta_{0}((c\lambda)^{-1}|\tau|),

where
c>0
is a constant to be chosen later.
We also set
T_{\lambda}^{2}g=(\widetilde{\chi}\,T_{\lambda}-T_{\lambda}^{1})g
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\Big\|\prod_{i=1}^{3}\widetilde{\chi}\,T_{\lambda}g_{i}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{q}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\omega)}\leq{\rm I}+{\rm I\!I},

where

\displaystyle{\rm I}=\Big\|\prod_{i=1}^{3}T_{\lambda}^{1}g_{i}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{q}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\omega)},\quad{\rm I\!I}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})\neq(1,1,1):\\
m_{j}\in\{1,2\}\end{subarray}}\Big\|\prod_{i=1}^{3}T_{\lambda}^{m_{i}}g_{i}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{q}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\omega)}.

First, we show
{\rm I\!I}\lesssim\lambda^{-M}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|g_{i}\|_{p}
for any
M>1
. It is enough to show

(2.18)

\displaystyle\|T_{\lambda}^{2}g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\omega)}\lesssim\lambda^{-M}\|g\|_{p}

for any
M>0
if
p\leq q
.
Once we have (
2.18
), it follows that
\|T_{\lambda}^{1}g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\omega)}\lesssim\lambda^{2}\|g\|_{p}

since
|T_{\lambda}^{1}g|\leq|\widetilde{\chi}\,T_{\lambda}g|+|T_{\lambda}^{2}g|
and
\|\widetilde{\chi}\,T_{\lambda}g\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\omega)}\lesssim\lambda^{2}\|g\|_{p}
for
p\leq q
(see (
2.8
) and (
2.9
)).
Thus, the desired bound on
\mathrm{I\!I}
follows by Hölder’s inequality.

To prove (
2.18
), let us set

\Phi(\eta,\rho,\tau)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int e^{-it(\tau-\eta\phi(\rho/\eta))}\widetilde{\chi}(t)dt\,\beta(\lambda^{-1}\eta)\beta_{0}(|\rho/\eta|).

Recalling the definition of
T_{\lambda}
((
2.3
)), we have
\mathcal{F}_{x,t}(\widetilde{\chi}\,T_{\lambda}g)(\eta,\rho,\tau)=\Phi(\eta,\rho,\tau)\widehat{g}(\eta,\rho)
.
Note
T_{\lambda}^{2}=(\widetilde{\chi}\,T_{\lambda}-T_{\lambda}^{1})
, so

T_{\lambda}^{2}g(x,t)=\mathfrak{K}(\cdot,t)\ast g(x),

where

\mathfrak{K}(x,t)=\iiint\big(1-\beta_{0}((c\lambda)^{-1}|\tau|)\big)\Phi(\eta,\rho,\tau)e^{i(x\cdot(\eta,\rho)+t\tau)}d\eta d\rho d\tau.

Taking a sufficiently large
c>0
, by integration by parts, we get

(2.19)

|\mathfrak{K}(x,t)|\lesssim\lambda^{-M}(1+|x|)^{-M}(1+|t|)^{-M}

for
M\geq 1
.
We see
\partial_{\eta,\rho,\tau}^{m}\big((1-\beta_{0}((c\lambda)^{-1}|\tau|))\Phi(\eta,\rho,\tau)\big)
are bounded by
C_{M}\lambda^{-M}(1+|\tau|)^{-M}
for any
M\geq 1
since
\partial_{\eta,\rho}^{m}\phi
are bounded by a constant depending only on the multi index
m
. Repeated integration by parts in
\eta,\rho,\tau
gives (
2.19
).
Using (
2.19
), we get (
2.18
) with
p=q
by Schur’s test. Also, (
2.18
) holds with
q=\infty
by Young’s convolution inequality. By interpolation, we get (
2.18
) for any
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
.

We now turn to the term
{\rm I}
. Since the Fourier transform of
T_{\lambda}^{1}g_{i}
is supported in a ball of radius
2c\lambda
, we can apply Lemma
2.7
to
\rm I
. So, we get

\displaystyle{\rm I}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{3(3-\alpha)}{q}}\Big\|\prod_{i=1}^{3}T_{\lambda}^{1}g_{i}\Big\|_{L^{\frac{q}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Since
|T_{\lambda}^{1}g|\leq|\widetilde{\chi}T_{\lambda}g|+|T_{\lambda}^{2}g|
, using

By decomposition into finite angular sectors and rotation, we may assume

is supported in a small conic neighborhood of

.
Changing variables

(3.1)

\|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}P_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times I;\omega)}\leq C\|T_{\lambda}^{\psi_{{0}}}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times I;\omega^{\prime})},

The following lemma shows that estimates relative to
\alpha
-dimensional measures can be deduced from weighted estimates.
See

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
0<\alpha\leq 3
and
\nu\in\mathfrak{C}^{3}(\alpha)
. Set
\varphi_{\lambda}=\lambda^{3}\varphi(\lambda\cdot)
for a Schwartz function
\varphi
such that

\widehat{\varphi}=1
on
\mathbb{B}^{3}(0,4)
and
{\rm supp}\,\widehat{\varphi}\subset\mathbb{B}^{3}(0,8)
. There is a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(\nu)>0
such that
C_{1}^{-1}|\varphi_{\lambda}|\ast\nu\in\Omega(\alpha)
.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Let us define
\nu_{\lambda}:=|\varphi_{\lambda}|\ast\nu
.
By rapid decay of
\varphi
, we have

\nu_{\lambda}(z)\leq C_{N}\lambda^{3}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{-N\ell}\nu\big(\mathbb{B}^{3}(z,2^{\ell}\lambda^{-1})\big)

for any
N>0
. It suffices to show
\int_{\mathbb{B}^{3}(z_{\circ},r)}\nu_{\lambda}(y)dy\leq C_{1}r^{\alpha}
for a constant
C_{1}
.

Let us consider two cases
r\leq\lambda^{-1}
and
r>\lambda^{-1}
, separately.
If
r\leq\lambda^{-1}
, using
\|\nu_{\lambda}\|_{\infty}\leq C\langle\nu\rangle_{\alpha}\lambda^{3-\alpha}
, we see
\int_{\mathbb{B}^{3}(z_{\circ},r)}\nu_{\lambda}(z)dz\lesssim\langle\nu\rangle_{\alpha}\lambda^{3-\alpha}r^{3}\leq C_{N}^{\prime}\langle\nu\rangle_{\alpha}r^{\alpha}
.
To handle the case
r>\lambda^{-1}
, we use

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{3}(z_{\circ},r)}\nu\big(\mathbb{B}^{3}(z,2^{\ell}\lambda^{-1})\big)dz\leq\int_{\mathbb{B}^{3}(z_{\circ},(2^{\ell}+1)r)}\int\chi_{\mathbb{B}^{3}(z^{\prime},2^{\ell}\lambda^{-1})}(z)dzd\nu(z^{\prime}).

Taking
N
sufficiently large, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{3}(z_{\circ},r)}\nu_{\lambda}(z)dz\leq C_{N}\lambda^{3}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{-N\ell}(2^{\ell}\lambda^{-1})^{3}(2^{\ell}+1)^{\alpha}r^{\alpha}\leq C_{N}^{\prime\prime}r^{\alpha}.

Therefore, taking
C_{1}=\max\{C_{N}^{\prime},C_{N}^{\prime\prime}\}
, we have
C_{1}^{-1}\nu_{\lambda}\in\Omega(\alpha)
.
∎

and Theorem

3.2.
Estimates for the circular average

Recall the average
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
.
Let us set
P_{0}=\sum_{j\leq 0}P_{2^{j}}
. The kernel

\mathcal{K}(\cdot,t)
of
f\to\mathcal{A}P_{0}f(\cdot,t)
is rapidly decaying, i.e.,
|\mathcal{K}(\cdot,t)|\leq C(1+|x|)^{-N}
for any
N
.
By Schur’s test, one can easily see
\|\mathcal{A}P_{0}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times I;d\nu)}\leq C\|f\|_{p}
for
1\leq p\leq q
. Therefore,
it is sufficient to consider the contribution from
\sum_{j\geq 1}\mathcal{A}P_{2^{j}}f
.

Let
d\sigma_{t}
denote the normalized spherical measure. Then it is well known that

(3.4)

\widehat{d\sigma_{t}}(\xi)=e^{it|\xi|}a_{+}(t\xi)+e^{-it|\xi|}a_{-}(t\xi),

where
a_{\pm}
are smooth functions satisfying
|\partial_{\xi}^{m}a_{\pm}(\xi)|\leq C_{m}(1+|\xi|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}-|m|}
for any
m
.
So, it is sufficient to consider
e^{it|\xi|}a_{+}(t\xi)
since the contribution from
e^{-it|\xi|}a_{-}(t\xi)
can be handled similarly by reflection
t\to-t
.
We set

\mathfrak{A}f(x,t)=\int e^{i(x\cdot\xi+t|\xi|)}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-\frac{d-1}{4}}\widehat{f}(\xi)d\xi

and

\mathfrak{A}_{j}f=\mathfrak{A}P_{2^{j}}f.

Since
t\in I
, to prove the estimate (
1.4
)
it suffices to show

\|\sum_{j\geq 1}\mathfrak{A}_{j}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I;d\nu)}\lesssim\langle\nu\rangle_{\alpha}^{1/q}\|f\|_{p}.

Thus, the matter is reduced to showing

(3.5)

\big\|\mathfrak{A}_{j}f\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I;d\nu)}\lesssim\langle\nu\rangle_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{q}}2^{-\epsilon j}\|f\|_{p},\quad j\geq 1,

for some
\epsilon>0
.

Let
\mathbf{K}_{j}(\cdot,t)
denote the kernel of
f\to\mathfrak{A}_{j}f(\cdot,t)
. It is easy to see

(3.6)

\displaystyle|\mathbf{K}_{j}(x,t)|\lesssim 2^{j}\big(1+2^{j}\big||x|-|t|\big|\big)^{-N}

for any
N\geq 1
(see, for example,
[
14
]
). Using the estimate we obtain

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{A}_{j}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I;d\nu)}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{j}\|f\|_{1},

(3.8)

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{A}_{j}f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I;d\nu)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle\nu\rangle_{\alpha}\min\big\{2^{j},2^{(d+1-\alpha)j}\big\}\|f\|_{1}.

In fact, (
3.7
) is clear from (
3.6
).
To show (
3.8
), we use the fact that
\mathbf{K}_{j}
is essentially supported in
O(2^{-j})
neighborhood of the truncated cone with height
\sim 1
. Since
the neighborhood can be covered by
O(2^{dj})
balls of radius
2^{-j}
, using (
2.1
) we see
\|\mathbf{K}_{j}\|_{L^{1}(d\nu)}\lesssim\langle\nu\rangle_{\alpha}2^{(d+1-\alpha)j}
.
Besides, we have
|\mathbf{K}_{j}|\lesssim 2^{j}(1+|x|)^{-N}
since
t\in I
. So,
\|\mathbf{K}_{j}\|_{L^{1}(d\nu)}\lesssim\langle\nu\rangle_{\alpha}\min\{2^{j},2^{(d+1-\alpha)j}\}
. We get (
3.8
) by Fubini’s theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

As discussed above, we need only to show

)
for some

For

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

be a

-dimensional subspace in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}

Let

be an

–

Proof.

By the Kolmogorov-Seliverstov-Plessner linearization it suffices to show

\|\chi_{\mathbb{B}^{d}(0,1)\times I}\mathcal{A}f(\cdot\,,\mathbf{z}_{2}(\cdot))\|_{L^{q}(d\mu)}\leq C\langle\mu\rangle_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{q}}\|f\|_{p}

Quantum backaction effects in sequential measurements
Le Bin Ho

Abstract
Quantum backaction refers to the disturbance of a quantum system caused by measuring it. In sequential measurements, this effect can accumulate and become significant, leading to nontrivial modifications of the system state and the measurement results. This paper explores the ways in which quantum backaction can manifest in sequential measurements, including the role of measurement strength and the nature of the measurement process. The paper highlights the insight quantum foundation and the implications for quantum measurement and information processing.

Introduction and
preliminary definitions

Quantum mechanics predicts that
measuring a quantum state
results in its collapse and
reveals hidden properties

;
Bohm 1989
.
This fundamental aspect
is known as the

The von Neumann mechanism

.
This effect is evident in
sequential measurements,
where later measurements
are disturbed by the QBA

;
Gudder and Nagy 2001
.
This causal process was widely
explored in recent works,
including sequential weak measurements

;
Piacentini et al. 2016
;
Avella et al. 2017
;
Brodutch and Cohen 2017
;
Ma et al. 2018
;
Kim et al. 2018
;
Chen et al. 2019
;
Pfender et al. 2019
,
reduction of QBA effects

Wiseman 1995
;
Wu et al. 2019
;
Wu et al. 2020
;
Shomroni et al. 2019
;
Møller et al. 2017
;
Tsang and Caves 2012
,
and manipulation of qubits
Blok et al. 2014
.
Other works also include violating
noncontextual realism
Larsson et al. 2011

and extracting maximal knowledge
Nagali et al. 2012

through sequential measurements, which are noteworthy.

In some cases,
later measurements can noncasually
affect the system’s behaviors in the past.
For example, in a delayed-choice

a measurement made after

.
Another vital example is
postselection measurements,
where the measurement results at present
are affected by future postselections

Aharonov et al. 1964
;
Aharonov et al. 1988
.
Yet, studying
the QBA caused by subsequent measurements
is still limited, e.g.,
the quantum trajectory
in continuous measurements

;
Tan et al. 2015
;
Ho 2019
;
Campagne-Ibarcq et al. 2014
,
quantum foundation

;
Guevara and Wiseman 2015
;
Chantasri et al. 2013
,
uncertainty relations

Bao et al. 2020a
;
Khanahmadi and Mølmer 2021
,
and quantum measurements

Bao et al. 2020b
;
Chantasri et al. 2021
.
Remarkably, Tsang et al. observed
sub-Heisenberg uncertainty via past measurements

Tsang et al. 2009
;
Tsang 2009
.
Similarly,
Bao et al. experimentally observed the beyond
Heisenberg uncertainty
by using past quantum states
of preparation and postselection
Bao et al. 2020a
.
These works push for scrutinizing
the QBA in sequential measurements.

This work examines QBA effects
in sequential measurements
of two observables
\bm{A}
and
\bm{B}

in a given system,
where
\bm{B}
is measured after
\bm{A}
.
The system is first coupled
with a quantum probe
\mathcal{P}_{1}

to measure
\bm{A}
,
then with a probe
\mathcal{P}_{2}
to measure
\bm{B}
.
The probes initialize as Gaussian states
with zero mean and are measured
on continuous variables, e.g.,
\bm{x}_{1,2}
.
We discuss the QBA caused by the first measurement
affecting the second via the change in variance
upon measurement of
\bm{B}
,
and the QBA caused by the second measurement
affecting the first via the change in variance of
\bm{A}
.
We conduct sequential measurements
in two models:
joint measurement
and

conditional measurement
(refer to Fig.
1
).
In the joint measurement model,
the measuring system couples to two
quantum probes,
and the joint state is measured afterward.
We find that only the first measurement
causally affects the second one in this model.
In the conditional measurement model,
measurements are conditioned on each other,
presenting QBA effects for both observables,
including causal and noncausal disturbances.
We demonstrate these observations
using a spin system, and uncover
a lower bound for the stronger uncertainty
relation proposed by Maccone and Pati

Maccone and Pati 2014
,
enabling high-precision measurements.
We finally present a general framework
for sequential
N
measurements.

QBA effects impede certain quantum
technologies like quantum computing
and metrology. Understanding and
controlling QBA in sequential
measurements could improve the quantum
measurement foundation, increase
precision, and offer noise mitigation techniques.

The paper is structured as follows.
Section
II
presents
the sequential measurements framework,
including the joint and conditional measurement models.
Illustrative results are shown in Section
III
,
and the general framework for

N
-sequential measurements
is provided in Section
IV
.
The paper is summarized in Section
V
.

II.1
Joint measurement model

We first consider the joint measurement approach
shown in Fig.
1
(a).
Assume the initial joint state of

\mathcal{S}\otimes\mathcal{P}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{P}_{2}
is

\displaystyle\rho_{\rm in}=\rho_{0}\otimes|\psi_{1}(x)\rangle\langle\psi_{1}(x)|\otimes|\psi_{2}(x)\rangle\langle\psi_{2}(x)|,

(3)

where
\rho_{0}
is the initial system state.
The joint state transforms to

\rho_{\rm fin}=\bm{U}_{2}\bm{U}_{1}\rho_{\rm in}\bm{U}_{1}^{\dagger}\bm{U}_{2}^{\dagger}
.
Inserting identity operators

\bm{I}_{1}=\int|m\rangle\langle m|{\rm d}m

and
\bm{I}_{2}=\int|n\rangle\langle n|{\rm d}n

into the probes,
we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{\rm fin}=\int\bm{N}_{n}\bm{M}_{m}\rho_{0}\bm{M}^{\dagger}_{m^{\prime}}\bm{N}_{n^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\otimes|m\rangle\langle m^{\prime}|\otimes|n\rangle\langle n^{\prime}|\ {\rm d}^{4}\bm{t},

(4)

where
\bm{t}
represents
a 4-tuple
(m,m^{\prime},n,n^{\prime})

and
{\rm d}^{4}\bm{t}
is
a 4-dimensional volume differential
(see App.
A
).
The measurement operators
\bm{M}
and
\bm{N}

for the first and second measurements are given by

\displaystyle\bm{M}_{m}

\displaystyle=\int\psi_{1}(m-a)|a\rangle\langle a|{\rm d}a,

\displaystyle\bm{N}_{n}

\displaystyle=\int\psi_{2}(n-b)|b\rangle\langle b|{\rm d}b,

(5)

where
|a\rangle
is an eigenstate of

\bm{A}
per an eigenvalue
a
,
and similar for
\bm{B}
.
These measurement operators
satisfy the completeness equations

\int\bm{M}_{m}^{\dagger}\bm{M}_{m}{\rm d}m=\bm{I}

and
\int\bm{N}_{n}^{\dagger}\bm{N}_{n}{\rm d}n=\bm{I}
.
The final
state of
the two probes
is given by

\eta={\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}[\rho_{\rm fin}]
, where Tr
{}_{\mathcal{S}}[*]

is a partial trace w.r.t.
system
\mathcal{S}
.
It explicitly yields (see App.
B
)

\displaystyle\eta=\iiint\langle b|a\rangle\langle a|\rho_{0}|a^{\prime}\rangle\langle a^{\prime}|b\rangle\times|\psi_{1}(x-a)\rangle\langle\psi_{1}(x-a^{\prime})|\otimes|\psi_{2}(x-b)\rangle\langle\psi_{2}(x-b)|\ {\rm d}a{\rm d}a^{\prime}{\rm d}b.

(6)

After the interaction,
we measure the position
operator
\bm{x}_{1}

in pointer
\mathcal{P}_{1}

and get
the expectation value as

\displaystyle\langle\bm{x}_{1}\otimes\bm{I}_{2}\rangle={\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{P}_{1}\mathcal{P}_{2}}\bigl[(\bm{x}_{1}\otimes\bm{I}_{2})\eta\bigr]={\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}[\bm{A}\rho_{0}].

(7)

The variance of observed values
upon measurement of
\bm{x}_{1}

is given by

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(\bm{x}_{1})_{\eta}

\displaystyle\equiv\langle(\bm{x}_{1}\otimes\bm{I}_{2})^{2}\rangle-\langle\bm{x}_{1}\otimes\bm{I}_{2}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}^{2}+{\rm Var}(\bm{A})_{\rho_{0}},

(8)

which is the spread of the observed values
upon measurement of
\bm{x}_{1}

concerning
\eta

(shorthanded as the variance of
\bm{x}_{1}
).
Here,
{\rm Var}(\bm{A})_{\rho_{0}}={\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}[\bm{A}^{2}\rho_{0}]-({\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}[\bm{A}\rho_{0}])^{2}

is the variance of

\bm{A}

concerning

\rho_{0}

(see App.
C.1
).
Experimentally, one can infer

{\rm Var}(\bm{A})_{\rho_{0}}={\rm Var}(\bm{x}_{1})_{\eta}-\sigma_{1}^{2}
,
where
\sigma_{1}^{2}

stands for shot noise fluctuations

Bao et al. 2020a
.

The QBA effect caused by the first measurement
can be informed
by the change in variance
of the second measurement.
To evaluate this
QBA, we examine the
variance of

\bm{x}_{2}

in the second measurement
as follows

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(\bm{x}_{2})_{\eta}

\displaystyle\equiv\bigl\langle(\bm{I}_{1}\otimes\bm{x}_{2})^{2}\bigr\rangle-\bigl\langle\bm{I}_{1}\otimes\bm{x}_{2}\bigr\rangle^{2},

\displaystyle=\sigma_{2}^{2}+{\rm Var}(\bm{B})_{\rho_{1}},

(9)

where the expectation value is

\langle\bm{I}_{1}\otimes\bm{x}_{2}\rangle={\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{P}_{1}\mathcal{P}_{2}}[(\bm{I}_{1}\otimes\bm{x}_{2})\eta]
,
and

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(\bm{B})_{\rho_{1}}={\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}[\bm{B}^{2}\rho_{1}]-({\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}[\bm{B}\rho_{1}])^{2},

(10)

is the variance of
\bm{B}

concerning
\rho_{1}
.
Here,
\rho_{1}={\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{P}_{1}}[\bm{U}_{1}(\rho_{0}\otimes|\psi_{1}(x)\rangle\langle\psi_{1}(x)|)\bm{U}_{1}^{\dagger}]
is
the system state after
interacting with
\mathcal{P}_{1}
.
Straightforwardly, it yields
(see App.
C.2
)

\displaystyle\rho_{1}=\iint|a\rangle\langle a|\rho_{0}|a^{\prime}\rangle\langle a^{\prime}|e^{-\frac{(a-a^{\prime})^{2}}{8\sigma_{1}^{2}}}{\rm d}a{\rm d}a^{\prime}.

(11)

Obviously, if the initial state

\rho_{0}
is an eigenstate of
\bm{A}
,
i.e.,
\rho_{0}=|a_{i}\rangle\langle a_{i}|
,
then
\rho_{1}=\rho_{0}
,
meaning that system
\mathcal{S}

is not disturbed after
the first measurement

Wu et al. 2020
.
In general, however,
the system
is disturbed through
the first measurement,
and thus,
\rho_{1}\neq\rho_{0}
.
Hence, Eq. (
10
)
explicitly gives

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(\bm{B})_{\rho_{1}}=\int b^{2}\langle b|\rho_{1}|b\rangle{\rm d}b-\Bigg(\int b\langle b|\rho_{1}|b\rangle{\rm d}b\Bigg)^{2},

(12)

which significantly
depends on
the first measurement
(via
\sigma_{1}
) for all

a\neq a^{\prime}
.

In general,

{\rm Var}(\bm{B})_{\rho_{1}}\neq{\rm Var}(\bm{B})_{\rho_{0}}

due to the QBA caused by the first measurement,
where
{\rm Var}(\bm{B})_{\rho_{0}}={\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}[\bm{B}^{2}\rho_{0}]-({\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}[\bm{B}\rho_{0}])^{2}

is the variance of

\bm{B}

concerning

\rho_{0}
.
The dependence of
{\rm Var}(\bm{B})_{\rho_{1}}

on the first measurement illustrates
an
in situ causal effect
,
where later measurement results
are affected by previous measurements.

When the
first measurement is weak,
i.e.,
\sigma_{1}\to\infty
,
Eq. (
II.1
)
simplifies to

{\rm Var}(\bm{x}_{2})_{\eta}=\sigma_{2}^{2}+{\rm Var}(\bm{B})_{\rho_{0}}
,
which is not affected by the first measurement.
This means
the QBA is eliminated

Šindelka and Moiseyev 2018
;
Abbott et al. 2019
.
However, the cost is that
the fluctuation of the first
measurement will increase,
i.e.,
{\rm Var}(\bm{x}_{1})_{\eta}
is large

Šindelka and Moiseyev 2018
;
Aharonov and Vaidman 2009
.
Discussing this increasing fluctuation
is out of the scope,
where we instead focus on
the mutual influence in
sequential measurements.

II.2
Conditional measurement model

In this approach, assume that
we first measure
\bm{x}_{1}
in

\mathcal{P}_{1}
and get an outcome

x_{1}
.
After the first measurement,
the initial system state
\rho_{0}

transforms into
an unnormalized state

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}_{1}(x_{1})

\displaystyle=\bm{M}_{x_{1}}\rho_{0}\bm{M}^{\dagger}_{x_{1}}

\displaystyle=\iint|a\rangle\langle a|\rho_{0}|a^{\prime}\rangle\langle a^{\prime}|\psi_{1}(x_{1}-a)\psi_{1}^{*}(x_{1}-a^{\prime}){\rm d}a{\rm d}a^{\prime}.

(13)

Here, we used
\bar{\rho}_{1}(x_{1})

to specify that the system state
is given according to
the outcome
x_{1}
of the first measurement
(see, for example,
Chap. II in Ref.
Nielsen and Chuang 2010
).
After the measurement
of
\bm{x}_{2}
with an outcome

x_{2}
, the system state becomes

\bar{\rho}_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})\propto\bm{N}_{x_{2}}\bar{\rho}_{1}(x_{1})\bm{N}^{\dagger}_{x_{2}}
.
For convenience, we introduce an effective matrix

\bm{E}(x_{2})=\bm{N}^{\dagger}_{x_{2}}\bm{N}_{x_{2}}
, which is the past quantum state

Gammelmark et al. 2013
.

We examine the QBA effect
caused by the first measurement,
i.e., the outcome of the second
measurement
is affected by the first measurement.
We consider the conditional probability for
obtaining the outcome
\bm{x}_{2}=x_{2}

conditioned on
\bm{x}_{1}=x_{1}
as
(see App.
D
)

\displaystyle p(x_{2}|x_{1})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{{\rm Tr}\bigl[\bar{\rho}_{1}(x_{1})\bm{E}(x_{2})\bigr]}{\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits{\rm Tr}\bigl[\bar{\rho}_{1}(x_{1})\bm{E}(x^{\prime}_{2})\bigr]{\rm d}x^{\prime}_{2}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\big|\psi_{2}(x_{2}-b)\big|^{2}\langle b|\bar{\rho}_{1}(x_{1})|b\rangle\ {\rm d}b}{\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\Bigl[\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\big|\psi_{2}(x^{\prime}_{2}-b)|^{2}\langle b|\bar{\rho}_{1}(x_{1})|b\rangle\ {\rm d}b\Bigr]{\rm d}x^{\prime}_{2}},

(14)

where
p(x_{2}|x_{1})
is
the shorthand of

p(\bm{x}_{2}\!\!=\!x_{2}|\bm{x}_{1}\!\!=\!x_{1})
.
Then, the expectation value
of
\bm{x}_{2}
conditioned on the
outcome
\bm{x}_{1}=x_{1}

reads

\displaystyle E(\bm{x}_{2}|\bm{x}_{1}\!\!=\!x_{1})=\int x_{2}\ p(x_{2}|x_{1}){\rm d}x_{2},

(15)

where ‘
E
’ stands
for
\langle\cdots\rangle
as an expectation value.
Generally, this expectation value
is affected by the QBA
caused by the first measurement.
The conditional variance
of
\bm{x}_{2}
gives

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(\bm{x}_{2}|\bm{x}_{1}\!\!=\!x_{1})

\displaystyle=E(\bm{x}_{2}^{2}|\bm{x}_{1}\!\!=\!x_{1})-[E(\bm{x}_{2}|\bm{x}_{1}\!\!=\!x_{1})]^{2}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{2}^{2}+{\rm Var}(\bm{B}|\bm{A}),

(16)

where
{\rm Var}(\bm{B}|\bm{A})

is the variance of

\bm{B}

conditioned on the presence of
\bm{A}
.
In indirect measurements,
this variance is informed by
Eq. (
II.2
) as

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(\bm{B}|\bm{A})={\rm Var}(\bm{x}_{2}|\bm{x}_{1}\!\!=\!x_{1})-\sigma_{2}^{2}.

(17)

Generally, it depends on
the strength
of the first and second measurements.
The QBA effect appears when

{\rm Var}(\bm{B}|\bm{A})\neq{\rm Var}(\bm{B})_{\rho_{0}}
.

Likewise,
the second measurement
can noncausally affect back
the first measurement.
To evaluate the noncausal backaction,
we consider the conditional probability
for obtaining
\bm{x}_{1}=x_{1}

conditioned on
\bm{x}_{2}=x_{2}

\displaystyle p(x_{1}|x_{2})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{{\rm Tr}\bigl[\bar{\rho}_{1}(x_{1})\bm{E}(x_{2})\bigr]}{\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits{\rm Tr}\bigl[\bar{\rho}_{1}(x^{\prime}_{1})\bm{E}(x_{2})\bigr]{\rm d}x^{\prime}_{1}},

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\big|\psi_{2}(x_{2}-b)\big|^{2}\langle b|\bar{\rho}_{1}(x_{1})|b\rangle\ {\rm d}b}{\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\Bigl[\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\big|\psi_{2}(x_{2}-b)\big|^{2}\langle b|\bar{\rho}_{1}(x^{\prime}_{1})|b\rangle\ {\rm d}b\Bigr]{\rm d}x^{\prime}_{1}}.

(18)

The conditional expectation value
and conditional variance
are given by

E(\bm{x}_{1}|\bm{x}_{2}\!\!=\!{x_{2}})=\int x_{1}p(x_{1}|x_{2}){\rm d}x_{1}

and
{\rm Var}(\bm{x}_{1}|\bm{x}_{2}\!\!=\!x_{2})=E(\bm{x}_{1}^{2}|\bm{x}_{2}\!\!=\!{x_{2}})-\bigl[E(\bm{x}_{1}|\bm{x}_{2}\!\!=\!{x_{2}})]^{2}
,
respectively.
Similarly, the variance
of
\bm{A}
conditioned on

\bm{B}

can be informed from

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(\bm{A}|\bm{B})={\rm Var}(\bm{x}_{1}|\bm{x}_{2}\!\!=\!x_{2})-\sigma_{1}^{2}.

(19)

Here,

{\rm Var}(\bm{A}|\bm{B})\neq{\rm Var}(\bm{A})_{\rho_{0}}

is the result of the noncausal QBA effect
caused by the second measurement.
Equations (
17
,
19
) explicitly
show the impacts of
the first measurement
on the second measurement
and vice versa, respectively.

Intuitively,
the conditional state
\bar{\rho}_{1}

interferes with
the effective matrix
\bm{E}

in the same way
as the interference of pre-
and post-selection states,
which was experimentally observed
in the resonance fluorescence

Campagne-Ibarcq et al. 2014
.
The interference here
relies on the conditional outcomes

x_{1},x_{2}

(like postselection),
allowing measurements of
\bm{A}

and
\bm{B}
to affect each other.
This is a purely quantum feature
of the reciprocal past-future information

Chantasri et al. 2021
.
The interference can be observed
through continuous monitoring
of the system under different observables.

Zhang and Mølmer 2017
;
Campagne-Ibarcq et al. 2014
;
Ho 2019
.

II.3
Conditional stronger uncertainty relations

It is unfeasible to get precise results
in incompatible measurements,
whether they are simultaneous or sequential

Robertson 1929
;
Arthurs and Goodman 1988
;
Šindelka and Moiseyev 2018
.
The Heisenberg-Robertson uncertainty
relation
for two incompatible observables
\bm{A}

and
\bm{B}
can be given
in terms of the commutator

{\rm Var}(\bm{A}){\rm Var}(\bm{B})\geq\bigl|\frac{1}{2}\langle[\bm{A},\bm{B}]\rangle\bigr|^{2}
,
which is the very first investment
on this issue
Robertson 1929
;
Arthurs and Goodman 1988
.
However, the lower bound in this relation is trivial,
such as
it can reach zero
when the system state is an eigenstate
of either
\bm{A}
or
\bm{B}
.
Maccone and Pati
later proposed a stronger (nontrivial)
uncertainty relation in terms
of the summation of variances

Maccone and Pati 2014

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(\bm{A})+{\rm Var}(\bm{B})\geq\max(\mathcal{R}_{\rm a},\mathcal{R}_{\rm b}),

(20)

which we name as Maccone-Pati uncertainty
relation (MPUR),
where

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\rm a}

\displaystyle=\pm i\langle[\bm{A},\bm{B}]\rangle+\bigl|\langle\psi|\bm{A}\pm i\bm{B}|\psi^{\perp}\rangle\bigr|^{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\rm b}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\bigl|\langle\psi^{\perp}_{\bm{A}+\bm{B}}|\bm{A}+\bm{B}|\psi\rangle\bigr|^{2}.

(21)

Here,
|\psi^{\perp}\rangle

is an arbitrary orthogonal state
and
|\psi^{\perp}_{\bm{A}+\bm{B}}\rangle\propto(\bm{A}+\bm{B}-\langle\bm{A}+\bm{B}\rangle)|\psi\rangle
.
The sign should be chosen
so that
\pm i\langle[\bm{A},\bm{B}]\rangle

is positive.
The MPUR is nontrivial,
i.e., nonzero lower bound.
Such an MPUR has been experimentally
verified in Ref.
Wang et al. 2016
.

In this framework,
we examine the MPUR
in terms of conditional variances,
where the summation

{\rm Var}(\bm{A})+{\rm Var}(\bm{B})

is replaced by
{\rm Var}(\bm{A}|\bm{B})+{\rm Var}(\bm{B}|\bm{A})
.
In the following section,
we observe that

{\rm Var}(\bm{A}|\bm{B})+{\rm Var}(\bm{B}|\bm{A})<\max(\mathcal{R}_{\rm a},\mathcal{R}_{\rm b})
.
This observation is a result of the
conditional measurement,
which analogizes to the
postselection measurement

Abbott et al. 2019
;
Bao et al. 2020a
.
We emphasize that
this result does not contradict
Eq.
20
since it only
applies to conditional cases.
Significantly, it allows for
getting higher measurements precision
and minimizing the trade-off between

{\rm Var}(\bm{A}|\bm{B})
and

{\rm Var}(\bm{B}|\bm{A})
.
The result is thus significant for minimizing
QBA effects in quantum measurements.

III
Illustration in a spin system

We consider the sequential measurements of
a spin system
using an atom-light interaction scheme

Hammerer et al. 2010
;
Julsgaard et al. 2003
;
Colangelo et al. 2017
;
Jasperse et al. 2017
;
Atatüre et al. 2007
;
Vasilyev et al. 2012
;
Robb et al. 2007
;
Bao et al. 2020a
;
Muñoz Arias et al. 2020
;
Madsen and Mølmer 2004
;
Liu et al. 2009
;
Ho and Edamatsu 2022
.
To simplify, we consider the system
to be a single spin-1/2 and choose

\bm{A}=\bm{S}_{z}=\bm{\sigma}_{z}/2

and
\bm{B}=\bm{S}_{x}=\bm{\sigma}_{x}/2
,
where
\bm{\sigma}_{i},(i=x,y,z)

is a Pauli matrix.
The initial system state is

\rho_{0}=|+\rangle\langle+|
, where

|\pm\rangle=(|\uparrow\rangle\pm|\downarrow\rangle)/\sqrt{2}
,
with
|\uparrow\rangle
and
|\downarrow\rangle

are the eigenstates of
\bm{S}_{z}
.
This choice gives

{\rm Var}(\bm{S}_{z})_{\rho_{0}}=1/4

and
{\rm Var}(\bm{S}_{x})_{\rho_{0}}=0
.
Additionally, we expand operators
\bm{S}_{z}

and
\bm{S}_{x}
into their eigenvalues and
eigenstates as follows

\displaystyle\bm{S}_{z}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{i}a_{i}|a_{i}\rangle\langle a_{i}|=\frac{1}{2}|\uparrow\rangle\langle\uparrow|-\frac{1}{2}|\downarrow\rangle\langle\downarrow|),

(22)

\displaystyle\bm{S}_{x}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{i}b_{i}|b_{i}\rangle\langle b_{i}|=\frac{1}{2}|+\rangle\langle+|-\frac{1}{2}|-\rangle\langle-|).

(23)

Since these spectrums are discrete,
we replace the integrals (w.r.t d
a
and d
b
)
with the summation over the discrete eigenstates.

III.1
Joint measurement model

First, we calculate
\rho_{1}

in Eq. (
11
):

\displaystyle\rho_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\in\{\uparrow,\downarrow\}}|a_{i}\rangle\langle a_{i}|+\rangle\langle+|a_{j}\rangle\langle a_{j}|e^{-\frac{(a_{i}-a_{j})^{2}}{8\sigma_{1}^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&e^{-\frac{1}{8\sigma_{1}^{2}}}\\
e^{-\frac{1}{8\sigma_{1}^{2}}}&1\end{pmatrix}.

(24)

Substituting Eq. (
III.1
)
into Eq. (
12
),
we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(\bm{S}_{x})_{\rho_{1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\{+,-\}}b_{i}^{2}\langle b_{i}|\rho_{1}|b_{i}\rangle-\Bigl[\sum_{i\in\{+,-\}}b_{i}\langle b_{i}|\rho_{1}|b_{i}\rangle\Bigr]^{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{4}\Bigl(1-e^{-\frac{1}{4\sigma_{1}^{2}}}\Bigr).

(25)

In Fig.

{\rm Var}(\bm{S}_{x})_{\rho_{1}}

\sigma_{1}
.
For strong measurement (
\sigma_{1}\ll 1

{\rm Var}(\bm{S}_{x})_{\rho_{1}}
reaches

indicating that the system
is maximum disturbed by
the first measurement.
For weak measurement
(
\sigma_{1}\to\infty

{\rm Var}(\bm{S}_{x})_{\rho_{1}}

which means that
the first measurement
has minimal impact on
the second one.
During the strong-to-weak transition regime

;
Pan et al. 2020
,

{\rm Var}(\bm{S}_{x})_{\rho_{1}}
reduces from

III.2
Conditional measurement model

First, let us calculate
\bar{\rho}_{1}(x_{1})

in Eq. (
II.2
):

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}_{1}(x_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\in\{\uparrow,\downarrow\}}|a_{i}\rangle\langle a_{i}|\rho_{0}|a_{j}\rangle\langle a_{j}|\psi_{1}(x_{1}-a_{i})\psi_{1}^{*}(x_{1}-a_{j})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\Bigl(\frac{1}{2\pi\sigma_{1}^{2}}\Bigr)^{1/2}e^{-\frac{x_{1}^{2}+1/4}{2\sigma_{1}^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}e^{\frac{x_{1}}{2\sigma_{1}^{2}}}&1\\
1&e^{-\frac{x_{1}}{2\sigma_{1}^{2}}}\end{pmatrix}.

(26)

This state significantly depends on the
outcome
x_{1}
and the interaction strength

\sigma_{1}

of the first measurement.
The conditional
expectation values
in Eq. (
15
) yield

\displaystyle E(\bm{x}_{2}|\bm{x}_{1}\!\!=\!x_{1})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{{\rm exp}({\frac{x_{1}}{2\sigma_{1}^{2}}})}{1+{\rm exp}({\frac{x_{1}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}})},

(27)

\displaystyle E(\bm{x}_{2}^{2}|\bm{x}_{1}\!\!=\!x_{1})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{4}+\sigma_{2}^{2},

(28)

and the conditional variance
of
\bm{x}_{2}
is given by

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(\bm{x}_{2}|\bm{x}_{1}\!\!=\!x_{1})=\dfrac{1}{4}\tanh\Bigl(\dfrac{x_{1}}{2\sigma_{1}^{2}}\Bigr)^{2}+\sigma_{2}^{2}.

(29)

Inversely,
the variance of

\bm{S}_{x}
conditioned on
\bm{S}_{z}
gives

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(\bm{S}_{x}|\bm{S}_{z})=\dfrac{1}{4}\tanh\Bigl(\dfrac{x_{1}}{2\sigma_{1}^{2}}\Bigr)^{2}.

(30)

We show the result in Fig.

.
When the first measurement is strong
(
\sigma_{1}\to 0
),
the conditional variance
reaches the maximum of 1/4
for

,
indicating that the system is
maximally disturbed by
the first measurement
due to the QBA.
In other words, before the first measurement,
the variance
{\rm Var}(\bm{S}_{x})_{\rho_{0}}=0
;
and after the measurement, it
reaches the maximum

{\rm Var}(\bm{S}_{x}|\bm{S}_{z})=1/4
.
When the first measurement is weak
(
\sigma_{1}\to\infty
),
it disturbs the system less, and thus

{\rm Var}(\bm{S}_{x}|\bm{S}_{z})
reduces
to
{\rm Var}(\bm{S}_{x})_{\rho_{0}}
.
This observation is consistent with
the joint measurement approach above,
where the first measurement
affects the results of the second.

III.3
Conditonal stronger uncertainty relations

We study the MPUR in the spin model
with conditional measurement.
The initial system state is

|\psi\rangle=|+\rangle
,
an eigenstate of
\bm{S}_{x}
.
We thus choose

|\psi^{\perp}\rangle=(\bm{S}_{z}-\langle\bm{S}_{z}\rangle)|+\rangle/\Delta(\bm{S}_{z})=|-\rangle

Maccone and Pati 2014
,
and obtain

|\psi^{\perp}_{\bm{S}_{z}+\bm{S}_{x}}\rangle=|-\rangle
.
Then,
\mathcal{R}_{\rm a}
and

\mathcal{R}_{\rm b}

in Eq. (
II.3
) yield

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\rm a}=\dfrac{1}{4},\text{and }\mathcal{R}_{\rm b}=\dfrac{1}{8}.

(32)

The MPUR
for the system before
the measurements reads

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(\bm{S}_{z})_{\rho_{0}}+{\rm Var}(\bm{S}_{x})_{\rho_{0}}=\dfrac{1}{4}=\max(\mathcal{R}_{\rm a},\mathcal{R}_{\rm b}),

(33)

in which reaches the lower bound
in Eq. (
20
).

Next, we consider the MPUR for the
conditional variances. Since

{\rm Var}(\bm{S}_{z}|\bm{S}_{x})

and
{\rm Var}(\bm{S}_{x}|\bm{S}_{z})

can be archived individually, we
obtain the inequality

{\rm Var}(\bm{S}_{z}|\bm{S}_{x})+{\rm Var}(\bm{S}_{x}|\bm{S}_{z})\geq\frac{1}{8}
.
The equality is attainable when

{\rm Var}(\bm{S}_{z}|\bm{S}_{x})=\frac{1}{8}

and
{\rm Var}(\bm{S}_{x}|\bm{S}_{z})=0
,
as previously discussed.
This is the lower bound for MPUR
in the
conditional
sequential measurements,
resulting in enhanced precision
measurements and reduced QBA effects.

IV
N
-sequential measurements

We extend the framework to

N
-sequential measurements.
Let
\bm{U}_{k}=e^{-i\bm{A}_{k}\otimes\bm{p}_{k}}

be the evolution operator of the

k^{\rm th}
measurement,
where
\bm{A}_{k}=\int a_{k}|a_{k}\rangle\langle a_{k}|\ {\rm d}a_{k}
is the
system operator,
and
\bm{p}_{k}
is the
k^{\rm th}

probe operator.
Let
\bm{\Omega}_{k}
be the
measurement operator
of the
k^{\rm th}
measurement,
we have

\displaystyle\bm{\Omega}_{k}=\int\psi_{k}(x_{k}-a_{k})|a_{k}\rangle\langle a_{k}|\ {\rm d}a_{k}.

(34)

The system state after
k

measurements is given by

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}_{k}=\bm{\Omega}_{k}\bar{\rho}_{k-1}\bm{\Omega}^{\dagger}_{k},\ \text{ for }\ k=1,\cdots,N.

(35)

The probability of obtaining
outcome
x_{k}

conditional on
the rest of other
outcomes is given by

\displaystyle p(x_{k}|\bar{x}_{k})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{{\rm Tr}[\bar{\rho}_{k}\bm{E}]}{\int{\rm Tr}[\bar{\rho}_{k}\bm{E}]\ {\rm d}x^{\prime}_{k}},

(36)

where
\bar{x}_{k}=\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}\}\backslash x_{k}
stands for the rest of
other outcomes, i.e.,
a set of all elements
from
x_{1}
to
x_{N}

except
x_{k}
, and

\displaystyle\bm{E}=\bm{\Omega}^{\dagger}_{k+1}\bm{\Omega}^{\dagger}_{k+2}\cdots\bm{\Omega}^{\dagger}_{N}\bm{\Omega}_{N}\bm{\Omega}_{N-1}\cdots\bm{\Omega}_{k+1}.

(37)

See the proof in App.
E
.
This result is similar
to the one given in Ref.
Bao et al. 2020a
.
However, in Ref.
Bao et al. 2020a
,
the authors approximate
the Gaussian distribution
of the conditional probability to deduce the
variance and
use a hypothesis projective measurement
at the
k^{\rm th}
measurement.
Here, we derive the exact variance
from the expectation value of the
position operator
\bm{x}_{k}
.
At first, the conditional expectation value
is given by

\displaystyle E(\bm{x}_{k}|{\bar{x}_{k}})=\int x_{k}\ p(x_{k}|\bar{x}_{k}){\rm d}x_{k}.

(38)

Then, the conditional variance is
expressed as

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(\bm{x}_{k}|\bar{x}_{k})

\displaystyle=E(\bm{x}_{k}^{2}|{\bar{x}_{k}})-[E(\bm{x}_{k}|{\bar{x}_{k}})]^{2}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{k}^{2}+{\rm Var}(\bm{A}_{k}\big|\bar{\bm{A}}_{k}),

(39)

where
\bar{\bm{A}}_{k}=\{\bm{A}_{1},\cdots,\bm{A}_{N}\}\backslash\bm{A}_{k}
.
We thus can extract the conditional
variance

{\rm Var}(\bm{A}_{k}|\bar{\bm{A}}_{k})
.

For example, in App.
E
, we
provide a useful Mathematica code
for calculating the conditional variance

{\rm Var}(\bm{x}_{2}|x_{1},x_{3},x_{4})

of 4-sequential measurements:

\bm{S}_{z}\to\bm{S}_{x}\to\bm{S}_{x}\to\bm{S}_{z}
.

V
Conclusion and outlook

We studied the impact of quantum backaction
(QBA) on sequential measurements of
incompatible operators.
We considered the indirect
measurement of observables
\bm{A}

and
\bm{B}
using two quantum probes

\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}

in the joint and conditional measurement models.
In the joint measurement,
only the QBA caused by the
first measurement is presented
due to the influence of former
measurements on later ones.
Otherwise, in the conditional
measurement approach,
there is a mutual effect
in sequential measurements
expressed through the QBAs of
the first and second measurements.
We illustrated the results in a spin system,
showing the dependence of
variance in subsequent measurements
on prior measurements and vice versa.
This observation results from the interplay between
the measurement strengths of
the first and second measurements.
We further derived a new bound
for the stronger uncertainty relation
with conditional measurements.
The framework
was then extended to
N
-sequential measurements.

Finally, we remark that these findings
can be demonstrated
using current technologies,
such as trapped ions, macroscopic vapor cells,
and nuclear magnetic resonance.
As we showed theoretically,
the measurement operators
\bm{M}_{m}

and
\bm{N}_{n}
can be implemented in
the phase-space representation of the driven field
(e.g., light, magnetic fields, etc),
and the measurement results can be inferred from
the position-shift of the final state.
Potential future research could include
controlling and eliminating the QBA effects,
experimental verifying the stronger uncertainty relation.

Appendix B

Here, we derive the joint state of

\displaystyle\eta={\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}[\rho_{\rm fin}].

(B.1)

We first consider the cyclic property
of the trace as

{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}\bigl[\bm{N}_{n}\bm{M}_{m}\rho_{0}\bm{M}^{\dagger}_{m^{\prime}}\bm{N}^{\dagger}_{n^{\prime}}\bigr]={\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}\bigl[\bm{M}_{m}\rho_{0}\bm{M}^{\dagger}_{m^{\prime}}\bm{N}^{\dagger}_{n^{\prime}}\bm{N}_{n}\bigr],

where

\displaystyle\bm{N}^{\dagger}_{n^{\prime}}\bm{N}_{n}

\displaystyle=\int\psi_{2}^{*}(n^{\prime}-b^{\prime})|b^{\prime}\rangle\langle b^{\prime}|\int\psi_{2}(n-b)|b\rangle\langle b|{\rm d}b{\rm d}b^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int\psi_{2}(n-b)\psi_{2}^{*}(n^{\prime}-b)|b\rangle\langle b|{\rm d}b,

(B.2)

and

\displaystyle\bm{M}_{m}\rho_{0}\bm{M}^{\dagger}_{m^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\Bigl[\int\psi_{1}(m-a)|a\rangle\langle a|{\rm d}a\Bigr]\times\rho_{0}\times\Bigl[\int\psi_{1}^{*}(m^{\prime}-a^{\prime})|a^{\prime}\rangle\langle a^{\prime}|{\rm d}a^{\prime}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\iint|a\rangle\langle a|\rho_{0}|a^{\prime}\rangle\langle a^{\prime}|\times\psi_{1}(m-a)\psi_{1}^{*}(m^{\prime}-a^{\prime}){\rm d}a{\rm d}a^{\prime}.

(B.3)

From Eqs. (

,

B
), we have

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}\bigl[\bm{M}_{m}\rho_{0}\bm{M}^{\dagger}_{m^{\prime}}\bm{N}^{\dagger}_{n^{\prime}}\bm{N}_{n}\bigr]=\iiint\langle b|a\rangle\langle a|\rho_{0}|a^{\prime}\rangle\langle a^{\prime}|b\rangle\times\psi_{1}(m-a)\psi_{1}^{*}(m^{\prime}-a^{\prime})\ \times\psi_{2}(n-b)\psi_{2}^{*}(n^{\prime}-b){\rm d}a{\rm d}a^{\prime}{\rm d}b.

Submitting it into Eq. (

)
and taking trace over

\mathcal{S}

which explicitly gives

\displaystyle\langle b|a\rangle\langle a|\rho_{0}|a^{\prime}\rangle\langle a^{\prime}|b\rangle\times|\psi_{1}(m-a)\rangle\langle\psi_{1}(m^{\prime}-a^{\prime})|\otimes|\psi_{2}(n-b)\rangle\langle\psi_{2}(n^{\prime}-b)|{\rm d}a{\rm d}a^{\prime}{\rm d}b,

Appendix C
Variances in the joint measurement model

C.1
Derivation of the variance
{\rm Var}(\bm{x}_{1})_{\eta}

We calculate
the expectation value of

\bm{x}_{1}
in Eq. (7) in the main text as

\displaystyle\langle\bm{x}_{1}\otimes\bm{I}_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{P}_{1}\mathcal{P}_{2}}\bigl[(\bm{x}_{1}\otimes\bm{I}_{2})\eta\bigr]

\displaystyle=\iiint\langle b|a\rangle\langle a|\rho_{0}|a^{\prime}\rangle\langle a^{\prime}|b\rangle\times\langle\psi_{1}(x-a^{\prime})|\bm{x}_{1}|\psi_{1}(x-a)\rangle{\rm d}a{\rm d}a^{\prime}{\rm d}b.

(C.1)

Wherein, we have

\displaystyle\int\langle b|a\rangle\langle a|\rho_{0}|a^{\prime}\rangle\langle a^{\prime}|b\rangle{\rm d}b

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}\Bigl[|a\rangle\langle a|\rho_{0}|a^{\prime}\rangle\langle a^{\prime}|\Bigr]

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}\bigl[|a\rangle\langle a|\rho_{0}\bigr],

(C.2)

where we used the cyclic property
of the trace, then, only
a=a^{\prime}
is non-vanish,
and

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{1}(x-a^{\prime})|\bm{x}_{1}|\psi_{1}(x-a)\rangle

\displaystyle=\Bigl[\int\Bigl(\dfrac{1}{2\pi\sigma_{1}^{2}}\Bigr)^{1/4}e^{-\frac{(x^{\prime}-a^{\prime})^{2}}{4\sigma_{1}^{2}}}\langle x^{\prime}|{\rm d}x^{\prime}\Bigr]\times\Bigl[\int x_{1}|x_{1}\rangle\langle x_{1}|{\rm d}x_{1}\Bigr]\times\Bigl[\int\Bigl(\dfrac{1}{2\pi\sigma_{1}^{2}}\Bigr)^{1/4}e^{-\frac{(x-a)^{2}}{4\sigma_{1}^{2}}}|x\rangle{\rm d}x\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\dfrac{a+a^{\prime}}{2}e^{-\frac{(a-a^{\prime})^{2}}{8\sigma_{1}^{2}}}

\displaystyle=a\ \text{ for all }a=a^{\prime}.

(C.3)

Then, the expectation value (
C.1
) is recast as

\displaystyle\langle\bm{x}_{1}\otimes\bm{I}_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int a\ {\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}\bigl[|a\rangle\langle a|\rho_{0}\bigr]{\rm d}a

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}[\bm{A}\rho_{0}].

(C.4)

Obviously, the expectation value
obtained from the first measurement
does not depend on the second measurement.

We next calculate

\langle(\bm{x}_{1}\otimes\bm{I}_{2})^{2}\rangle
for
the non-vanish terms, i.e.,
a=a^{\prime}
.
We first evaluate

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{1}(x-a)|\bm{x}_{1}^{2}|\psi_{1}(x-a)\rangle

\displaystyle=\Bigl[\int\Bigl(\dfrac{1}{2\pi\sigma_{1}^{2}}\Bigr)^{1/2}e^{-\frac{(x^{\prime}-a)^{2}}{4\sigma_{1}^{2}}}\langle x^{\prime}|{\rm d}x^{\prime}\Bigr]\times\Bigl[\int x_{1}^{2}|x_{1}\rangle\langle x_{1}|{\rm d}x_{1}\Bigr]\times\Bigl[\int\Bigl(\dfrac{1}{2\pi\sigma_{1}^{2}}\Bigr)^{1/2}e^{-\frac{(x-a)^{2}}{4\sigma_{1}^{2}}}|x\rangle{\rm d}x\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}^{2}+a^{2}.

(C.5)

Combining with Eq. (
C.1
),
we obtain

\displaystyle\langle(\bm{x}_{1}\otimes\bm{I}_{2})^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int(\sigma_{1}^{2}+a^{2})\ {\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}\bigl[|a\rangle\langle a|\rho_{0}\bigr]{\rm d}a

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}^{2}+{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}\bigl[\bm{A}^{2}\rho_{0}\bigr].

(C.6)

Then, the variance is

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(\bm{x}_{1})_{\eta}

\displaystyle=\langle(\bm{x}_{1}\otimes\bm{I}_{2})^{2}\rangle-\langle\bm{x}_{1}\otimes\bm{I}_{2}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}^{2}+{\rm Var}(\bm{A})_{\rho_{0}},

(C.7)

where
{\rm Var}(\bm{A})_{\rho_{0}}={\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}[\bm{A}^{2}\rho_{0}]-({\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}[\bm{A}\rho_{0}])^{2}
.
This result is casually trivial,
where the second measurement does not affect
the first one.

C.2
Derivation of the variance
{\rm Var}(\bm{x}_{2})_{\eta}

We next examine the backaction effect
of the first measurement affects the
second measurement.
We start from the expectation value:

\displaystyle\langle\bm{I}_{1}\otimes\bm{x}_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{P}_{1}\mathcal{P}_{2}}\bigl[(\bm{I}_{1}\otimes\bm{x}_{2})\eta\bigr]

\displaystyle=\iiint\langle b|a\rangle\langle a|\rho_{0}|a^{\prime}\rangle\langle a^{\prime}|b\rangle\times\langle\psi_{1}(x-a^{\prime})|\psi_{1}(x-a)\rangle\ \times\langle\psi_{2}(x-b)|\bm{x}_{2}|\psi_{2}(x-b)\rangle{\rm d}a{\rm d}a^{\prime}{\rm d}b.

(C.8)

The middle term is

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{1}(x-a^{\prime})|\psi_{1}(x-a)\rangle=e^{-\frac{(a-a^{\prime})^{2}}{8\sigma_{1}^{2}}}.

(C.9)

The last term is (similar to (
C.1
))

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{2}(x-b)|\bm{x}_{2}|\psi_{2}(x-b)\rangle=b.

(C.10)

Then, Eq. (
C.2
) explicitly gives

\displaystyle\langle\bm{I}_{1}\otimes\bm{x}_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\iiint b\langle b|a\rangle\langle a|\rho_{0}|a^{\prime}\rangle\langle a^{\prime}|b\rangle e^{-\frac{(a-a^{\prime})^{2}}{8\sigma_{1}^{2}}}{\rm d}a{\rm d}a^{\prime}{\rm d}b

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}\Bigl[\bm{B}\rho_{1}\Bigr],

(C.11)

where

\displaystyle\rho_{1}

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{P}_{1}}\Bigl[e^{-i{\bm{A}}\otimes\bm{p}_{1}}\Bigl(\rho_{0}\otimes|\psi_{1}(x)\rangle\langle\psi_{1}(x)|\Bigr)e^{i{\bm{A}}\otimes\bm{p}_{1}}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\iint|a\rangle\langle a|\rho_{0}|a^{\prime}\rangle\langle a^{\prime}|e^{-\frac{(a-a^{\prime})^{2}}{8\sigma_{1}^{2}}}{\rm d}a{\rm d}a^{\prime},

(C.12)

is the system state after the first interaction.
For weak measurement,

\sigma_{1}\gg|a-a^{\prime}|
,
i.e.,
e^{-\frac{(a-a^{\prime})^{2}}{8\sigma_{1}^{2}}}\approx 1
,
we have

\langle\bm{I}_{1}\otimes\bm{x}_{2}\rangle\approx{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}[\bm{B}\rho_{0}]
.
We next calculate the term

\langle(\bm{I}_{1}\otimes\bm{x}_{2})^{2}\rangle

and obtain

\displaystyle\langle(\bm{I}_{1}\otimes\bm{x}_{2})^{2}\rangle=\sigma_{2}^{2}+{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}[\bm{B}^{2}\rho_{1}]

(C.13)

Finally, the variance is given by

\displaystyle{\rm Var}({\bm{x}_{2}})_{\eta}=\sigma_{2}^{2}+{\rm Var}(\bm{B})_{\rho_{1}},

(C.14)

where
{\rm Var}(\bm{B})_{\rho_{1}}={\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}[\bm{B}^{2}\rho_{1}]-({\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{S}}[\bm{B}\rho_{1}])^{2}
and particularly gives

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(\bm{B})_{\rho_{1}}=\int b^{2}\langle b|\rho_{1}|b\rangle{\rm d}b-\Bigl[\int b\langle b|\rho_{1}|b\rangle{\rm d}b\Bigr]^{2}.

(C.15)

For the weak measurement,

{\rm Var}(\bm{B})_{\rho_{1}}={\rm Var}(\bm{B})_{\rho_{0}}
,
which implies that the weak measurement
will not affect the later measurement results.

Appendix D
Conditional measurement model

We calculate the conditional probability

\displaystyle p(x_{2}\big|x_{1})=\dfrac{{\rm Tr}\Bigl[\bar{\rho}_{1}(x_{1})\bm{E}(x_{2})\Bigr]}{\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits{\rm Tr}\Bigl[\bar{\rho}_{1}(x_{1})\bm{E}(x^{\prime}_{2})\Bigr]{\rm d}x^{\prime}_{2}}.

(D.1)

Using
\bm{E}(x_{2})=\bm{N}_{x_{2}}^{\dagger}\bm{N}_{x_{2}}
,
we have

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\Bigl[\bar{\rho}_{1}(x_{1})\bm{E}(x_{2})\Bigr]

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}\Bigl[\bm{N}_{x_{2}}\bar{\rho}_{1}(x_{1})\bm{N}^{\dagger}_{x_{2}}\Bigr]

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}\Bigl[\int\psi_{2}(x_{2}-b)|b\rangle\langle b|{\rm d}b\times\bar{\rho}_{1}(x_{1})\times\int\psi_{2}^{*}(x_{2}-b^{\prime})|b^{\prime}\rangle\langle b^{\prime}|{\rm d}b^{\prime}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\int\big|\psi_{2}(x_{2}-b)\big|^{2}\langle b|\bar{\rho}_{1}(x_{1})|b\rangle{\rm d}b.

(D.2)

Submitting to Eq. (

),
we obtain

\displaystyle p(x_{2}\big|x_{1})=\dfrac{\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\big|\psi_{2}(x_{2}-b)\big|^{2}\langle b|\bar{\rho}_{1}|b\rangle\ {\rm d}b}{\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\Bigl[\mathop{\text{\large$\int$}}\nolimits\big|\psi_{2}(x^{\prime}_{2}-b)\big|^{2}\langle b|\bar{\rho}_{1}|b\rangle\ {\rm d}b\Bigr]{\rm d}x^{\prime}_{2}},

(D.3)

as shown in Eq. (14) in the main text.

Gluon shadowing in nuclei and the role of the c ​ c ¯ ​ g c\bar{c}g state in the coherent photoproduction of J / ψ J/\psi in nucleus-nucleus collisions
Agnieszka Łuszczak, Wolfgang Schäfer

Abstract
In this brief note, we confront our results on diffractive photoproduction of J / ψ J/\psi mesons with the putative gluon shadowing ratio defined as R g = σ ⁡ ( γ ​ A → J / ψ ​ A ) / σ IA . R_{g}=\sqrt{\sigma(\gamma A\to J/\psi A)/\sigma_{\rm IA.}} , where σ IA \sigma_{\rm IA} is the result in impulse approximation. Building on our earlier description of the process in the color-dipole approach, where we took into account the rescattering of c ​ c ¯ c\bar{c} states only, we demonstrate that the inclusion of c ​ c ¯ ​ g c\bar{c}g -Fock states improves the description at small x x commonly associated with gluon shadowing.

In the dipole approach the imaginary part of the diffractive production amplitude in forward direction takes the form

\displaystyle\Im m\mathcal{A}(\gamma A\rightarrow VA;W,\mbox{\boldmath$q$}=0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{1}dz\int d^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}\,\Psi^{*}_{V}(z,\mbox{\boldmath$r$})\Psi_{\gamma}(z,\mbox{\boldmath$r$})\,\int d^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$b$}\,\Gamma_{A}(x,\mbox{\boldmath$b$},\mbox{\boldmath$r$}).

(1)

Here
\Psi_{\gamma},\Psi_{V}
stand for the light-front wave functions (LFWFs) of the
the virtual photon and
J/\psi
meson respectively.
The crucial input is the scattering amplitude
\Gamma_{A}(x,\mbox{\boldmath$r$},\mbox{\boldmath$b$})
of a color dipole of size
r
in
impact parameter space
Nikolaev and Zakharov 1991
;
Mueller 1990
.
Here the color dipole are treated as a concrete set of diffraction scattering eigenstates
Good and Walker 1960
;
Miettinen and Pumplin 1978
;
Nikolaev 1981
;
Nikolaev and Zakharov 1991
.
For heavy nuclei, the dipole amplitude can be written in the exponential from familiar from Glauber theory
Glauber 1959
.

\displaystyle\Gamma_{A}(x,\mbox{\boldmath$b$},\mbox{\boldmath$r$})=1-S_{A}(x,\mbox{\boldmath$b$},\mbox{\boldmath$r$})\,,\,\,{\rm{with}}\,\,S_{A}(x,\mbox{\boldmath$b$},\mbox{\boldmath$r$})=\exp\Big[-{1\over 2}\sigma(x,\mbox{\boldmath$r$})T_{A}(\mbox{\boldmath$b$})\Big]\,.

(2)

Above,
T_{A}(\mbox{\boldmath$b$})
is the optical thickness of the nucleus of mass number
A
normalized
as
\int d^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$b$}T_{A}(\mbox{\boldmath$b$})=A
.
The dipole amplitude depends on the
\gamma A
per-nucleon cm-energy
W
via
x=M_{V}^{2}/W^{2}
. Here
\sigma(x,\mbox{\boldmath$r$})
is the dipole cross section for the free nucleon target.
An important feature of diffractive production on the free nucleon is the “scanning radius” property of the overlap integral Eq.
1
, which
entails that the process is dominated by a typical dipole size
r=r_{s}
, in this case
r_{s}\sim 1/m_{c}
, where
m_{c}
is the charm quark mass. At small dipole sizes, we have

\displaystyle\sigma(x,\mbox{\boldmath$r$})=\frac{\pi^{2}}{N_{c}}\alpha_{s}(\mu^{2})r^{2}\,xg(x,\mu^{2}),\quad\mu^{2}=\frac{A}{r^{2}}+\mu_{0}^{2}\,,

(3)

which leads to the diffractive cross section proportional to the square of the proton’s gluon distribution
Ryskin 1993
,

\displaystyle\sigma(\gamma p\to J\psi p)\propto\sigma^{2}(x,r_{s})\propto[xg(x,\mu^{2})]^{2}\,.

(4)

Clearly this motivates an attempt to find information on the
nuclear
gluon distribution from diffractive
J/\psi
production on nuclear targets.
The strongly absorbing nuclear target however introduces another scale, the saturation scale (for a review see
Gelis et al. 2010
)

\displaystyle Q_{A}^{2}(x,\mbox{\boldmath$b$})={4\pi^{2}\over N_{c}}\alpha_{s}(Q_{A}^{2})xg(x,Q_{A}^{2})T_{A}(\mbox{\boldmath$b$}),

(5)

in terms of which the dipole-nucleus S-matrix Eq.
2
takes the form

Mueller 1999

\displaystyle S_{A}(x,\mbox{\boldmath$r$},\mbox{\boldmath$b$})=\exp\Big[-{1\over 8}Q_{A}^{2}(x,\mbox{\boldmath$b$})r^{2}\Big]\,,

(6)

Therefore the scale

\displaystyle r_{A}={2\sqrt{2}\over Q_{A}}\,,

(7)

separates standard hard processes with
r_{s}\ll r_{A}
, for which the leading twist pQCD factorization works, from processes in the strongly absorptive saturation regime
r_{s}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt$\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}}r_{A}
.

An estimate in Ref.
Nikolaev et al. 2003
gives
\langle{Q_{A}^{2}(\mbox{\boldmath$b$})}\rangle\sim 0.9\,\rm{GeV}^{2}
for
A^{1/3}=6
and
x\sim 0.01
.
For diffractive
J/\psi
photoproduction on lead we are therefore in a regime
r_{s}/r_{A}\sim 0.2
, where we may regard the rescatterings included in the Glauber-Gribov form as a summation of higher-twist corrections, but not yet as a fully developed saturated regime.

In Ref.
Łuszczak and Schäfer 2019
based on Glauber-Gribov rescattering of a color dipole were compared to the rapidity dependent cross sections for the exclusive production of
J/\psi
in ultraperipheral collisions of lead ions.
A rather good agreement was obtained for various input dipole cross sections when compared to the results of LHCb, ALICE and CMS at forward rapidities
|y|\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\lower 4.0pt\hbox{\hskip 1.0pt$\sim$}\hss}\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$>$}}2
.

II
Beyond the
c\bar{c}
rescattering

The Glauber form of the dipole amplitude however applies only in range of not too small
x=x_{A}\sim 0.01
.
Indeed, in the dipole picture of high-energy scattering, higher Fock states containing multiple additional gluons are responsible for the energy dependence of the dipole cross section. Multigluon states strongly ordered in light cone momentum fraction
z_{n}\ll z_{n-1}\ll\dots\ll z_{1}\ll 1
give rise to the BFKL evolution of the dipole cross section
Mueller 1994
;
Nikolaev et al. 1994a
;
Nikolaev and Zakharov 1994a
.
The dipole model applies in a limit of large coherence length of the relevant Fock state. For example, in the limit of large photon energy
\omega
in the rest frame of the nucleus, the coherence length
l_{c}=2\omega/M_{c\bar{c}}^{2}
for
c\bar{c}
invariant mass
M_{c\bar{c}}\sim M_{J/\psi}
becomes much larger than the size of the nucleus
l_{c}\gg R_{A}

Kopeliovich and Zakharov 1991
;
Nikolaev 1992
. This justifies the application of the Glauber approach for color dipoles
Mueller 1990
;
Nikolaev and Zakharov 1991
for the
c\bar{c}
component.
With increasing energy, the coherency condition
l_{c}\gg R_{A}
will be satisfied not only by the
c\bar{c}
-state, but also by higher
c\bar{c}g,c\bar{c}gg,\dots
states. In the language of Glauber–Gribov theory, these correspond to
inelastic shadowing corrections induced by high–mass diffractive states.
Below we restrict ourselves to
c\bar{c}g
states to represent high mass diffractive contributions. This is motivated by a phenomenological success of color dipole approaches such as
Bartels et al. 1999
;
Golec-Biernat and Łuszczak 2009
which can describe inclusive diffractive structure functions at HERA just including
q\bar{q}
and
q\bar{q}g
states. We therefore follow previous considerations for inclusive and diffractive DIS
Nikolaev et al. 2007
, where a similar approach had been adopted. We also omit possible contributions of
c\bar{c}q\bar{q}
-Fock states addressed in
Nikolaev et al. 2004
.

We very closely follow the approach outlined in
Nikolaev et al. 2007
adopted to the problem at hand in
Łuszczak and Schäfer 2022
, the details must not be repeated here.

Assuming the Glauber form to hold at
x\sim x_{A}
, account for the
c\bar{c}g
states leads to a dipole amplitude

\displaystyle\Gamma_{A}(x,\mbox{\boldmath$r$},\mbox{\boldmath$b$})=\Gamma_{A}(x_{A},\mbox{\boldmath$r$},\mbox{\boldmath$b$})+\log\Big({x_{A}\over x}\Big)\Delta\Gamma_{A}(x_{A},\mbox{\boldmath$r$},\mbox{\boldmath$b$})\,,

(8)

where

\displaystyle\Delta\Gamma_{A}(x_{A},\mbox{\boldmath$r$},\mbox{\boldmath$b$})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}|\psi(\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1})-\psi(\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{2})|^{2}\Big\{\Gamma_{A,c\bar{c}g}(x_{A},\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{2},\mbox{\boldmath$b$})-\Gamma_{A,c\bar{c}}(x_{A},\mbox{\boldmath$r$},\mbox{\boldmath$b$})\Big\}

(9)

and

\displaystyle\Gamma_{A,c\bar{c}g}(x_{A},\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{2},\mbox{\boldmath$b$})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma_{A}(x_{A},\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$b$}+{\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{2}\over 2})+\Gamma_{A}(x_{A},\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{2},\mbox{\boldmath$b$}+{\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}\over 2})

(10)

\displaystyle-\Gamma_{A}(x_{A},\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1},\mbox{\boldmath$b$}+{\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{2}\over 2})\Gamma_{A}(x_{A},\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{2},\mbox{\boldmath$b$}+{\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}\over 2})

The transverse separation of the gluon from quark/antiquark, encoded in the radial part of the
q\to qg
light front WF:

\displaystyle\psi(\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$})={\sqrt{C_{F}\alpha_{s}}\over\pi}{\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}\over\rho^{2}}\,\mu_{G}\rho K_{1}(\mu_{G}\rho)\,

(11)

In Eq.(
9
) the integration extend over all dipole sizes, including the infrared domain of large dipoles, where perturbation theory does not apply. Here, we follow
Nikolaev et al. 1994b
;
Nikolaev et al. 2007
and introduce the parameter
\mu_{G}
which ensures a the finite propagation radius
R_{c}=1/\mu_{G}\sim 0.2\div 0.3\,\mathrm{fm}
of gluons. In this work, we employ
\mu_{G}=0.7\,\rm{GeV}
. We use the running coupling

\displaystyle\alpha_{s}(r)=\frac{4\pi}{9}\frac{1}{\log\Big(\frac{C}{r^{2}\Lambda^{2}}\Big)},

(12)

with
C=1.5
, which we freeze for
r>1/\mu_{G}
. It enters the WF of Eq.(
11
) as
\alpha_{s}(\min\{r,\rho\})
.
In order to obtain the impulse approximation result, i.e. the production on a quasifree nucleon in the nucleus, we switch off the nonlinear piece in
\Gamma_{A,c\bar{c}g}
and put

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm IA}(x_{A},\mbox{\boldmath$r$},\mbox{\boldmath$b$})={1\over 2}\sigma(x_{A},\mbox{\boldmath$r$})T_{A}(\mbox{\boldmath$b$})\,,

(13)

We then follow
Cisek et al. 2012
and evaluate

\displaystyle R_{\rm coh}(x)={\displaystyle\int d^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$b$}\,\Big|\langle{J/\psi}|\Gamma_{A}(x,\mbox{\boldmath$r$},\mbox{\boldmath$b$})|{\gamma}\rangle\Big|^{2}\over\displaystyle\int d^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$b$}\,\Big|\langle{J/\psi}|\Gamma_{\rm IA}(x,\mbox{\boldmath$r$},\mbox{\boldmath$b$})|{\gamma}\rangle\Big|^{2}}\,,

(14)

and finally obtain the nuclear cross section as

\displaystyle\sigma(\gamma A\to J/\psi A;W)=R_{\rm coh}(x)\,\sigma(\gamma p\to J/\psi p;W)\,.

(15)

The cross section
\sigma(\gamma p\to J/\psi p;W)
is taken from our previous work
Łuszczak and Schäfer 2019
.

III
Comparison to experiment

In our numerical calculations we employed an easy to use dipole cross section designed for the use with heavy flavours from Ref.
Golec-Biernat and Sapeta 2006
. The light front wave function overlap is taken from
Kowalski et al. 2006
, as in
Łuszczak and Schäfer 2019
We refer the reader to these references for further details.

In Fig.
1
we show our results for the total diffractive photoproduction cross section of
J/\psi
on lead as a function of
\gamma A
per-nucleon cm-energy.
The data were obtained by the CMS
Tumasyan et al. 2023
and ALICE
Acharya et al. 2021
collaborations. We also show
data points extracted by Contreras in Ref.
Contreras 2017
from
data obtained in ultraperipheral heavy-ion collisions.
We show separately, the result of the impulse approximation, the Glauber-Gribov rescattering of
c\bar{c}
pairs, as well as the cross section including rescattering of the
c\bar{c}g
state.
To be precise, the solid curve is obtained by adding

\displaystyle\sigma(\gamma A\to J/\psi A;W)=(1-f(W))\sigma_{c\bar{c}}(\gamma A\to J/\psi A;W)+f(W)\sigma_{c\bar{c}+c\bar{c}g}(\gamma A\to J/\psi A;W)

with

\displaystyle f(W)={1\over 2}\Big\{1+\tanh\Big(\frac{W-W_{0}}{W_{1}}\Big)\Big\}\,,

(17)

with
W_{0}=50\,\rm{GeV}
,
W_{1}=5\,\rm{GeV}
.
Clearly the impulse approximation fails dramatically, which illustrates the scale of nuclear effects. A large part of the nuclear suppression can be explained by Glauber-Gribov rescattering of the
c\bar{c}
state alone.
We also observe that the calculations including the effect of the
c\bar{c}g
state show an additional suppression of the nuclear cross section, as required by experimental data.

Finally, in Fig.
2
we show the so-called gluon shadowing ratio
R_{g}(x)
. The latter is defined as

\displaystyle R_{g}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{\sigma(\gamma A\to J/\psi A;W)}{\tilde{\sigma}_{\rm IA}(\gamma A\to J/\psi A;W)}}\,.

(18)

It is important to note, that here the impulse approximation cross section in the denominator,
\tilde{\sigma}_{\rm IA}
is taken from Ref.
Guzey et al. 2013
, as this was also done in the analysis of the CMS collaboration
Tumasyan et al. 2023
.
This particular ratio is motivated by the leading-twist limit of the diffractive cross section, where it is expected that

\displaystyle R_{g}(x)\propto\frac{g_{A}(x,\mu^{2})}{Ag(x,\mu^{2})}\,,

(19)

and therefore
R_{g}(x)
is supposed to quantify the suppression (shadowing) of the per-nucleon glue in the nucleus at small-
x
.
Here the scale
\mu^{2}\sim M^{2}_{J/\psi}/4\sim 2.4\,\rm{GeV}^{2}
.

As can be seen from Fig.
2
, again including the
c\bar{c}g
state contributes to an additional suppression improving the agreement with data. This is also in qualitative agreement with the results for the nuclear structure function
F_{2A}
obtained in Ref.
Nikolaev et al. 2007
.

At larger
x
, but still well within the range of applicability of the dipole approach we observe a large discrepancy with experimental data. Note that a similar discrepancy is also visible in the rapidity dependence of cross sections from ultraperipheral heavy-ion collisions (UPCs) shown in
Łuszczak and Schäfer 2019
;
Łuszczak and Schäfer 2022
. There we found good agreement with LHCb data, which have not been analyzed in the form of Fig.
2
, but obtained a substantial underprediction of the ALICE forward rapidity data.

We note that this is the case for all dipole models used by us, and as can be seen from Ref.
Tumasyan et al. 2023
the same is true for slightly different dipole model calculations by other groups. An analysis of exclusive
J/\psi
production in UPCs based on the NLO collinear factorization approach
Eskola et al. 2023
also finds an ambiguity of gluon distributions fitting either set of data.

From the point of view of the dipole approach some amount of overprediction of shadowing in the frozen dipole size approximation is indeed expected
Zakharov 1998
in the range of
x\sim 0.01
.
We have accounted for the finite coherence length only in the most crude way, by including the nuclear form factor suppression
Łuszczak and Schäfer 2019
depending on longitudinal momentum transfer.

IV
Conclusions

In this letter we have addressed the diffractive photoproduction of
J/\psi
mesons at the highest available energies.
Data extracted from UPCs are well described at high energies/small-
x
after including additional shadowing that stems from the
c\bar{c}g
Fock state.

Admittedly, our calculation relies on a model for the behaviour of the
c\to cg
light front wave function in the nonperturbative region at large transverse distances. This is an inevitable element of any color dipole approach, though. We believe that our modeling of the essentially nonperturbative physics is well motivated by a phenomenological success of earlier works in the color dipole approach
Nikolaev et al. 1994b
;
Nikolaev et al. 2007
.
Our restriction to the
c\bar{c}g
-system is to some extent justified by the fact, that diffractive structure functions of the proton measured at HERA are well described by the inclusion of
q\bar{q}
and
q\bar{q}g
-states
Bartels et al. 1999
;
Golec-Biernat and Łuszczak 2009
.

It is interesting to ask, whether our inelastic shadowing induced by
q\bar{q}g
states can be directly related to a shadowing of the leading twist, DGLAP evolving gluon distribution of a nucleus.
While some early papers on the color dipole approach
Nikolaev and Zakharov 1994a
;
Nikolaev and Zakharov 1994b
;
Zakharov 1998
indeed suggest so, such a conclusion would be ad odds with the treatment of parton saturation effects along the lines of
Balitsky 1996
;
Kovchegov 1999
. A closer look at the triple Pomeron coupling
Nikolaev and Schäfer 2006
;
Bartels and Kutak 2008
also casts some doubts on this interpretation.

A precise relation to gluon shadowing would require a removal of higher-twist corrections which are also present in our
c\bar{c}g
contribution.
In any case, DGLAP evolution can only make sense at a hard scale
Q^{2}
much larger than the saturation scale. In the case of gluon shadowing, the relevant saturation scale is the one for an gluon-gluon dipole, which is enhanced by a factor
C_{A}/C_{F}=9/4
over the one of the triplet-antitriplet dipole. Roughly, one expects
\langle{Q^{2}_{8A}(\mbox{\boldmath$b$})}\rangle\sim 2\,\rm{GeV}^{2}
, in a similar ballpark as the hard scale for
J/\psi
production
Q^{2}\sim M^{2}_{J/\psi}/4\sim 2.4\,\rm{GeV}^{2}
.

On Onsager’s type conjecture for the inviscid Boussinesq equations
Changxing Miao, Yao Nie, Weikui Ye

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the Cauchy problem for the three dimensional inviscid Boussinesq system in the periodic setting. For 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ 1\leq p\leq\infty , we show that the threshold regularity exponent for L p L^{p} -norm conservation of temperature of this system is 1 / 3 1/3 , consistent with Onsager exponent. More precisely, for 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ 1\leq p\leq\infty , every weak solution ( v , θ ) ∈ C t ​ C x β (v,\theta)\in C_{t}C^{\beta}_{x} to the inviscid Boussinesq equations satisfies that ‖ θ ⁡ ( t ) ‖ L p ​ ( 𝕋 3 ) = ‖ θ 0 ‖ L p ​ ( 𝕋 3 ) \|\theta(t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}=\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{3})} if β > 1 3 \beta>\frac{1}{3} , while if β < 1 3 \beta<\frac{1}{3} , there exist infinitely many weak solutions ( v , θ ) ∈ C t ​ C x β (v,\theta)\in C_{t}C^{\beta}_{x} such that the L p L^{p} -norm of temperature is not conserved. As a byproduct, we are able to construct many weak solutions in C t ​ C x β C_{t}C^{\beta}_{x} for β < 1 3 \beta<\frac{1}{3} displaying wild behavior, such as fast kinetic energy dissipation and high oscillation of velocity. Moreover, we also show that if a weak solution ( v , θ ) (v,\theta) of this system has at least one interval of regularity, then this weak solution ( v , θ ) (v,\theta) is not unique in C t ​ C x β C_{t}C^{\beta}_{x} for β < 1 3 \beta<\frac{1}{3} .

Introduction

The fact that the dynamics of almost non-stationary astrophysical plasma flows feature a diversity of length scales and speed indicates the widespread existence of turbulence in the universe. Atmospheric turbulence may result from small-scale irregular air motions influenced by wind and ocean circulation acts as a turbulent motion on a wide range of scales
[
36
]
. In certain ranges of scales in the atmosphere and in the ocean, hydrodynamics is dominated by the interplay between gravity and the earth’s rotation with density variations about a reference state
[
24
]
.
The Boussinesq system describes the convection phenomena on these scales in the ocean or atmosphere and has been known as one of the most geophysical models
[
20
,
24
]
. In the periodic setting
\mathbb{T}^{3}=\mathbb{R}^{3}/\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, the inviscid Boussinesq system is described by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}v+(v\cdot\nabla)v+\nabla p=\theta e_{3},&{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{T}^{3}\times(0,T),\\
&\partial_{t}\theta+v\cdot\nabla\theta=0,&{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{T}^{3}\times(0,T),\\
&\nabla\cdot v=0,&{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{T}^{3}\times(0,T),\end{aligned}\right.

(B)

associated with initial data
(v,\theta)\big|_{t=0}=(v^{\textup{in}},\theta^{\textup{in}})
, where
v:\mathbb{T}^{3}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
denotes the velocity of the incompressible fluid,
p:\mathbb{T}^{3}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
the pressure field,
\theta:\mathbb{T}^{3}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
the temperature,
e_{3}=(0,0,1)^{T}
and
\theta e_{3}
represents the buoyancy force.

From the viewpoint of mathematics, the system (
B
) has been attracted considerable attention
in the past years since it is closely related to the incompressible Euler equations. More precisely,
the system (
B
) boils down to the classical Euler equations when
\theta\equiv 0
and the 2D inviscid Boussinesq system coincides with the 3D axisymmetric swirling Euler equations away from the symmetry axis
[
25
]
. The local well-posedness of smooth solution for the system (
B
) has been proved (see e.g.
[
11
,
10
,
26
]
). The global existence of smooth solution for the 3D
Euler equations remains outstandingly open. Therefore, the question of the global well-posedness of smooth solution for the multidimensional inviscid Boussinesq system is challenging.

To understand the turbulence phenomena in fluid mechanics, the study of weak solutions to the hydrodynamic equations becomes more and more important in the past several decades. For instance, the physicist and chemist Lars Onsager
[
29
]
proposed a celebrated conjecture for the Euler equations.

Conjecture 1.1

(Onsager’s conjecture)
.

Consider weak solutions of the Euler equations in
C_{t}C^{\beta}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
.

1.

[\rm{Positive\,\,\,part}]
If
\beta>1/3
, the energy
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|v|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
is conserved in time.

2.

[\rm{Negative\,\,\,part}]
If
\beta\leq 1/3
, there exist at least one solution that does not conserve energy.

The positive part of the conjecture was proven by Constantin, E and Titi in
[
14
]
. So far, the sharpest result on energy conservation of weak solutions was obtained by Cheskidov et al.. More specifically, they proved that the kinetic energy
is conserved in the class
L^{3}_{t}B^{1/3}_{3,c(\mathbb{N})}\cap C_{t}L^{2}_{x}
in
[
12
]
.

The negative part of the conjecture has been solved through several decades of unremitting efforts of many researchers. The first result in this direction was obtained by Scheffer
[
30
]
, in which the author constructed a nontrivial weak solution with compact support in space-time
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
. Later, Shnirelman
[
31
]
gave a simpler and more transparent example of a weak solution with compact support in time in the class of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})
. These weak solutions are discontinuous, unbounded
L^{2}
-fields. Therefore, these examples cannot show whether there exists a weak solution with monotonically decreasing kinetic energy or not. The first result on the existence of a dissipative solution was demonstrated by Shnirelman
[
32
]
in the class
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}\times[T_{1},T_{2}])
. After nearly a decade, De Lellis and Székelyhidi applied either the
Baire category theorem (e.g.,
[
15
]
) or the general framework of convex integration (e.g.,
[
28
,
33
]
) to the multidimensional incompressible Euler equations and recover Scheffer and Shnirelman’s counterexamples with bounded velocity and pressure in the seminal paper
[
17
]
. However, the Baire category approach does not work for obtaining continuous solutions. As a result, the method of convex integration has been developed as an alternative in the follow-up research on Onsager’s conjecture. The authors in
[
18
]
abandoned this essentially “soft” functional analytic technique in favor of a more “hard” PDE approach
based on Schauder’s estimates and oscillatory integrals to show the existence of continuous periodic dissipative solutions. Subsequently, they
[
19
]
constructed periodic
weak solutions in the Hölder space
C^{1/10-\epsilon}(\mathbb{T}^{3}\times[0,1])
, which dissipate the total kinetic energy with velocity. Choffrut
[
13
]
extended this result of
[
19
]
by establishing optimal h-principles in two spatial dimensions. Isett
[
21
]
constructed Hölder continuous periodic solutions with compact support in time in
C^{1/5-\epsilon}_{t,x}
and Buckmaster et al.
[
5
]
gave a shorter proof that includes a result on the existence of anomalous dissipative solutions in
C^{1/5-\epsilon}_{t,x}
. Furthermore,

[
3

proved the existence of non-trivial Hölder continuous solutions belonging to

for almost

show the existence of continuous periodic weak dissipative solutions in

\beta=\frac{1}{3}

In

More precisely, due to no known analogue for Beltrami flows, they decomposed the stress error into blocks by the geometric lemma and removed one component of stress error in each step by putting several plane waves which oscillate along the same direction with different frequency. In this paper, we build waves based on intermittent cuboid flows, which are constructed by collecting these flows supported in disjoint cuboids that point in multiple directions and essentially are Mikado flows given in
[
16
]

Different from the flows built in
[
35
]

To fully utilize this superiority and avoid the interference of waves with different oscillation directions, using the gluing approximation technique introduced in
[

(\bar{v}_{q},\bar{\theta}_{q},\bar{p}_{q},\mathring{\bar{R}}_{q},{\bar{T}}_{q})

(v_{q},b_{q},p_{q},\mathring{R}_{q},T_{q})
up to a small error. Benefiting from the stresses
(\mathring{\bar{R}}_{q},{\bar{T}}_{q})
supported in disjoint short time intervals,
we can decompose
(\mathring{\bar{R}}_{q},{\bar{T}}_{q})
as a sum
(\sum_{i}\mathring{\bar{R}}_{q,i},\sum_{i}{\bar{T}}_{q,i})
and cancel each
(\mathring{\bar{R}}_{q,i},{\bar{T}}_{q,i})
by adopting a single intermittent cuboid flow that is Lie-advected by
\bar{v}_{q}
and prevent the distinct flows from interacting with each other. Therefore, the interference between distinct deformed intermittent cuboid flows built is controlled over a sufficiently long time scale. This is the core of improving the regularity of weak solution from continuous space to
C^{1/3-}
Hölder space.

Organization of the paper.

In Section
2
, we recall the Littlewood-Paley theory, the definition of Besov spaces on torus and some related preliminary lemmas. Section
3
contains the proof of Theorem
1.5
. In Section

, we focus on proving Proposition
1.8
. In Section 4.1, we reduce Proposition
1.8
to Proposition 4.1. The proof of Proposition 4.1 occupies Sections 4.2-4.5. To be more precise, in Section 4.2, we implement mollification and gluing procedure to obtain the glued velocity and temperature, of which the corresponding Reynolds and temperature stresses are supported in disjoint time intervals. In Section 4.3, we introduce the deformed intermittent cuboid flows to construct the perturbation of the glued velocity and temperature. We establish the Hölder estimates for the perturbation, the new Reynolds and temperature stresses in Section 4.3 and Section 4.4, respectively. Collecting these estimates together, we verify that the inductive bounds stated in Proposition 4.1 are propagated from step

Proposition 4.3

(Estimates for exact solutions to Boussinesq equations)
.

Let

and
\alpha\in(0,1)

(V_{0},\Theta_{0})\in C^{\infty}

(4.23)

Proof.

We focus on the proof of the a priori estimate for smooth solutions. The existence of a unique solution is standard (see e.g.
[
25

We denote

by material derivative. For any multi-index

D_{t,V}\partial^{\sigma}\Theta+[\partial^{\sigma},V\cdot\nabla]\Theta=0.

Therefore, by commutator estimates we have

\|D_{t,V}\partial^{\sigma}V\|_{\alpha}\lesssim\|V\|_{1+\alpha}\|V\|_{N+\alpha}+\|\Theta\|_{\alpha+N}.

This estimate shows that for
|t|\leq T=\frac{c}{\|V_{0}\|_{{1+\alpha}}+1}

\|V(t)\|_{N+\alpha}\lesssim\|V_{0}\|_{N+\alpha}+\int_{0}^{|t|}\big(\|V(\tau)\|_{1+\alpha}\|V(\tau)\|_{N+\alpha}+\|\Theta(\tau)\|_{N+\alpha}\big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau.

(4.24)

Similarly, we can bound
\Theta

\|\Theta(t)\|_{N+\alpha}\lesssim\|\Theta_{0}\|_{N+\alpha}+\int_{0}^{|t|}\big(\|V(\tau)\|_{1+\alpha}\|\Theta(\tau)\|_{N+\alpha}+\|V(\tau)\|_{N+\alpha}\|\Theta(\tau)\|_{1+\alpha}\big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau.

(4.25)

For
N=1

|t|\leq T
, we have

\|V\|_{1+\alpha}+\|\Theta\|_{1+\alpha}\lesssim\|V_{0}\|_{1+\alpha}+\|\Theta_{0}\|_{1+\alpha}.

Combining this estimate with (
4.24

4.19

\displaystyle\|(\bar{v}_{q},\bar{\theta}_{q})\|_{N+1}\lesssim\|(\sum_{i=0}^{i_{\max}}\chi_{i}(t)\textsf{{v}}_{i},\sum_{i=0}^{i_{\max}}\chi_{i}(t)\bm{\theta}_{i})\|_{N+1}\lesssim\|(v_{\ell_{q}},\theta_{\ell_{q}})\|_{N+1}\lesssim\delta_{q}^{1/2}\lambda_{q}\ell_{q}^{-N}.

By the definition of
J_{0}
and
J_{i_{\max}}
, it is easy to verify that for
t\in[0,2\widetilde{T}+\tfrac{\tau_{q}}{3}]
,

(\bar{v}_{q},\bar{\theta}_{q})=(\textsf{{v}}_{0},\bm{\theta}_{0})=(v_{q},\theta_{q})=(v^{(1)},\theta^{(1)}),

and for
t\in[3\widetilde{T}-2\tau_{q},1]
,

(\bar{v}_{q},\bar{\theta}_{q})=(\textsf{{v}}_{i_{\max}},\bm{\theta}_{i_{\max}})=(v_{q},\theta_{q})=(v^{(2)},\theta^{(2)}).

Thus, we conclude (
4.46
).
∎

4.3
Construction and estimates for the perturbation

In the previous subsection, we have completed the stage:
(v_{q},\theta_{q})\mapsto(\bar{v}_{q},\bar{\theta}_{q})
. This section will be dedicated to construct the perturbation
(w_{q+1},d_{q+1})
of
(\bar{v}_{q},\bar{\theta}_{q})
to complete this iteration. More precisely,

(\bar{v}_{q},\bar{\theta}_{q})\mapsto(v_{q+1},\theta_{q+1}):=(\bar{v}_{q}+w_{q+1},\bar{\theta}_{q}+d_{q+1}).

The main idea in the construction of the perturbation is to use 2D spatial intermittent building blocks, which are essentially consistent with Mikado flows. This highly oscillatory perturbation is designed to cancel the error
(\mathring{\bar{R}}_{q},{\bar{T}}_{q})
by the low frequency of the term
\div(w_{q+1}\otimes w_{q+1})
. To achieve this goal,
the following geometric lemma plays a central role in the choices of the oscillation directions of these building blocks.

Lemma 4.7

(Geometric Lemma
[
2
]
)
.

Let
B_{\sigma}({\rm Id})
denote the ball of radius
\sigma
centered at
\rm Id
in the space of
3\times 3
symmetric matrices.
There exists a set
\Lambda_{v}\subset\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap\mathbb{Q}^{3}
that consists of vectors
k
with associated orthonormal basis
(k,\bar{k},\bar{\bar{k}}),~\epsilon_{v}>0
and smooth function
a_{v,k}:B_{\epsilon_{v}}(\rm Id)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that, for every
R_{v}\in B_{\epsilon_{v}}(\rm Id)
we have the following identity:

R_{v}=\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{v}}a^{2}_{v,k}(R_{v})\bar{k}\otimes\bar{k}.

Furthermore, for
0\leq N\leq N_{0}+1
, there exists a constant
M
such that

\|a_{v,k}\|_{C^{N}(\bar{B}_{1/2}({\rm Id}))}\leq M.

(4.47)

We define
\Lambda_{\theta}=\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
and choose
\Lambda_{v}
in Lemma
4.7
such that

\Lambda_{\theta}\cap\Lambda_{v}=\emptyset
. With respect to the choice of
\Lambda_{v}
, readers can refer to
[
2
]
for more details. Since the set
\Lambda_{v}\cup\Lambda_{\theta}\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap\mathbb{Q}^{3}
is finite, there exists a universal number
N_{\Lambda}
such that

\{N_{\Lambda}\bar{k},N_{\Lambda}\bar{\bar{k}}\}\subseteq N_{\Lambda}\mathbb{S}^{2}\cap\mathbb{N}^{3},\quad\forall k\in\Lambda_{v}\cup\Lambda_{\theta}.

(4.48)

Step 1: Deformed intermittent cuboid flows

Before introducing the deformed intermittent cuboid flows, we give spatial building blocks, which are essentially given in
[
2
,
4
]
.

Assume that
\phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a smooth mean-free cut-off function with a compact support which is much smaller than the interval
[0,1]
, We normalize
\phi
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=1.

(4.49)

Then we periodize
\phi
so that the resulting function is a periodic function defined on
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}=\mathbb{T}
and we still denote it by
\phi
. Now we construct spatial building blocks by
\phi
:

Lemma 4.8

(Spatial building blocks)
.

Let
N_{\Lambda}
be defined by (
4.48
) and the
\mathbb{T}
-periodic function
\phi
support on
[0,\lambda^{-1}_{1}]
, where
\lambda_{1}
is defined in (
4.3
). For each
k\in\Lambda_{v}\cup\Lambda_{\theta}
, let
\{k,\bar{k},\bar{\bar{k}}\}
be an orthonormal basis. Then for
\lambda_{1}
large enough, there exist suitable shifts
\{x^{k}\}_{k\in\Lambda_{v}\cup\Lambda_{\theta}}:=\{(x^{k}_{1},x^{k}_{2},x^{k}_{3})\}_{k\in\Lambda_{v}\cup\Lambda_{\theta}}
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &W^{\theta}_{(k)}(x)W^{\theta}_{{(k^{\prime})}}(x)=0,\quad\forall k\neq k^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{\theta},\\
&W^{v}_{(k)}(x)W^{v}_{{(k^{\prime})}}(x)=0,\quad\forall k\neq k^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{v},\\
&W^{v}_{(k)}(x)W^{\theta}_{{(k^{\prime})}}(x)=0,\quad\forall k\in\Lambda_{v},k^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{\theta}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.50)

That is, the set of the supports of
\big\{W^{v}_{(k)}(x)\big\}
and
\big\{W^{\theta}_{(k)}(x)\big\}
are mutual disjoint, where the spatial building blocks
\big\{W^{v}_{(k)}(x)\big\}
and
\big\{W^{\theta}_{(k)}(x)\big\}
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle W^{\theta}_{(k)}(x)=\phi(N_{\Lambda}\bar{k}\cdot(x-x^{k}))\phi(N_{\Lambda}\bar{\bar{k}}\cdot(x-x^{k})),\,k\in\Lambda_{\theta},

\displaystyle W^{v}_{(k)}(x)=\phi(N_{\Lambda}k\cdot(x-x^{k}))\phi(N_{\Lambda}\bar{\bar{k}}\cdot(x-x^{k})),\,k\in\Lambda_{v}.

Proof.

Since
\phi(x)
is
\mathbb{T}
-periodic and (
4.48
), for each
k\in\Lambda_{v}\cup\Lambda_{\theta}
, the function

W_{(k)}(x):=\phi(N_{\Lambda}\bar{k}\cdot x)\phi(N_{\Lambda}\bar{\bar{k}}\cdot x)

is
\mathbb{T}^{3}
-periodic. The construction of
W_{(k)}(x)
reveals that it supports on finite cuboids of which the length side are parallel to
k
, the width and height are equivalent to
\lambda^{-1}_{1}
. Note that
\Lambda_{v}{\cup}\Lambda_{\theta}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}^{3}
is finite, we take
\lambda_{1}
large enough such that these finite cuboids are mutually disjoint by choosing suitably shifts
\{x^{k}\}_{k\in\Lambda_{v}{\cup}\Lambda_{\theta}}
, which shows (
4.50
).
∎

For the case when
W_{(k)}(x)
supports on pipes, readers can refer to
[
8
]
for the proof. For the sake of dealing with the transport error of the perturbation to achieve the optimum regularity
\beta
, we will construct the deformed intermittent cuboid flows by the spatial building blocks in Lagrangian coordinates
\Phi_{i}
, the so-called inverse flow map defined as follows.

Inverse flow map.
Let
\Phi_{i}
be the solution to the glued vector transport equations

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+\bar{v}_{q}\cdot\nabla)\Phi_{i}=0,\quad\Phi_{i}\big|_{t=t_{i}}=x.

(4.51)

We define time cutoffs
\eta_{i}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
by

{\rm supp}\,\,\eta_{i}=I_{i}+[-\tfrac{\tau_{q}}{6},\tfrac{\tau_{q}}{6}],\quad\eta_{i}|_{I_{i}}\equiv 1,\quad\|\partial^{N}_{t}\eta_{i}\|_{0}\lesssim\tau^{-N}_{q}.

(4.52)

By the estimates for
\bar{v}_{q}
in (
4.45
), we have the following stability and derivative estimates of
\Phi_{i}
in a short time. Readers can refer to
[
23
, Proposition 4.10]
for the details.

Proposition 4.9

(Estimates for
\Phi_{i}
[
23
]
)
.

For
a\gg 1
,
0\leq N\leq N_{0}+1
and every
t\in{\rm supp}\,\,\eta_{i}
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Phi_{i}-{\rm Id}_{3\times 3}\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{10},

(4.53)

\displaystyle\|(\nabla\Phi_{i})^{-1}\|_{N}+\|\nabla\Phi_{i}\|_{N}

\displaystyle\leq\ell_{q}^{-N},

(4.54)

\displaystyle\|D_{t,\bar{v}_{q}}\nabla\Phi_{i}\|_{N}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta_{q}^{1/2}\lambda_{q}\ell_{q}^{-N}.

(4.55)

Noting that
\Phi_{i}(x,t)
is a measure preserving map,
\phi(N_{\Lambda}k\cdot(\Phi_{i}-x^{k}))
,
\phi(N_{\Lambda}\bar{k}\cdot(\Phi_{i}-x^{k}))
and
\phi(N_{\Lambda}\bar{\bar{k}}\cdot(\Phi_{i}-x^{k}))
are mean-free functions on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
. Furthermore, we follow from

\Phi_{i}(x+\mathbb{Z}^{3},t)=\Phi_{i}(x,t)+\mathbb{Z}^{3}

that

\phi(N_{\Lambda}k\cdot(\Phi_{i}-x_{k}))
,
\phi(N_{\Lambda}\bar{k}\cdot(\Phi_{i}-x_{k}))
and
\phi(N_{\Lambda}\bar{\bar{k}}\cdot(\Phi_{i}-x_{k}))
are
\mathbb{T}^{3}
-periodic functions.
Consequently, we conclude that
W^{v}_{(k)}(\Phi_{i}(x,t))
and
W^{\theta}_{(k)}(\Phi_{i}(x,t))
are
\mathbb{T}^{3}
-periodic function with zero mean. Moreover, we have the following property.

Lemma 4.10
.

For
t\in\text{supp}\,\eta_{i}(t)
, we have

\left\{\begin{aligned} &W^{\theta}_{(k)}(\Phi_{i}(x,t))W^{\theta}_{{(k^{\prime})}}(\Phi_{i}(x,t))=0,\quad\forall k\neq k^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{\theta},\\
&W^{v}_{(k)}(\Phi_{i}(x,t))W^{v}_{{(k^{\prime})}}(\Phi_{i}(x,t))=0,\quad\forall k\neq k^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{v},\\
&W^{v}_{(k)}(\Phi_{i}(x,t))W^{\theta}_{{(k^{\prime})}}(\Phi_{i}(x,t))=0,\quad\forall k\in\Lambda_{v},k^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{\theta}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.56)

Proof.

By the definition of
\Phi_{i}
in (
4.51
), we deduce that

\Phi_{i}(x,t)=\psi^{-1}_{t}(x)
, the inverse mapping for the flow
\psi_{t}(x)
, where

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\psi_{t}(x)=\bar{v}_{q}(\psi_{t}(x),t),\qquad\psi_{t}(x)|_{t=t_{i}}=x.

(4.57)

For any closed set
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{T}^{3}
satisfying
A\cap B=\emptyset
, we have
|x-y|>0
,
\forall x\in A,\forall y\in B
. Next, we consider
d(t)=|\psi_{t}(x)-\psi_{t}(y)|
. From the equation (
4.57
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(\psi_{t}(x)-\psi_{t}(y))=\bar{v}_{q}(\psi_{t}(x),t)-\bar{v}_{q}(\psi_{t}(y),t).

This together with
\|\nabla\bar{v}_{q}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\delta^{1/2}_{q}\lambda_{q}
yields that

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}d(t)\Big|\leq\|\nabla\bar{v}_{q}\|_{L^{\infty}}d(t)\leq C\delta^{1/2}_{q}\lambda_{q}d(t),

which implies that
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}d(t)\geq-C\delta^{1/2}_{q}\lambda_{q}d(t)
.
Then for
t\in\text{supp}\,\eta_{i}(t)=[t_{i}+\frac{\tau_{q}}{6},t_{i}+\frac{5\tau_{q}}{6}]
, we obtain that

\displaystyle d(t)\geq d(0)e^{-C\delta^{1/2}_{q}\lambda_{q}\tau_{q}}=|x-y|e^{-C\delta^{1/2}_{q}\lambda_{q}\tau_{q}}=|x-y|e^{-C_{0}}>0.

This shows that
\psi_{t}(A)\cap\psi_{t}(B)=\emptyset
, that is,
\Phi^{-1}_{i}(A,t)\cap\Phi^{-1}_{i}(B,t)=\emptyset
. Therefore, the set of the supports of
\big\{W^{v}_{(k)}(x)\big\}
and
\big\{W^{\theta}_{(k)}(x)\big\}
are mutual disjoint implies that the supports of
\big\{W^{v}_{(k)}(\Phi_{i}(x,t))\big\}
and
\big\{W^{\theta}_{(k)}(\Phi_{i}(x,t))\big\}
are also mutual disjoint for
t\in\text{supp}\,\eta_{i}(t)
, which implies (
4.56
).
∎

We are in position to construct the deformed intermittent cuboid flows. For each
k\in\Lambda_{\theta}
, we define

\displaystyle W^{\theta}_{(\lambda_{q+1}k)}(\Phi_{i}(x,t))k:=\phi(\lambda_{q+1}N_{\Lambda}\bar{k}\cdot(\Phi_{i}(x,t)-x^{k}))\phi(\lambda_{q+1}N_{\Lambda}\bar{\bar{k}}\cdot(\Phi_{i}(x,t)-x^{k}))k.

For each
k\in\Lambda_{v}
, we define

\displaystyle W^{v}_{(\lambda_{q+1}k)}(\Phi_{i}(x,t))\bar{k}:=\phi(\lambda_{q+1}N_{\Lambda}k\cdot(\Phi_{i}(x,t)-x^{k}))\phi(\lambda_{q+1}N_{\Lambda}\bar{\bar{k}}\cdot(\Phi_{i}(x,t)-x^{k}))\bar{k}.

Let us rewrite
W^{v}_{(\lambda_{q+1}k)}(\Phi_{i}(x,t))
and
W^{\theta}_{(\lambda_{q+1}k)}(\Phi_{i}(x,t))
as

\left\{\begin{aligned} W^{\theta}_{(\lambda_{q+1}k)}(\Phi_{i}(x,t)):=\sum_{l,m\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}}a_{k,l}a_{k,m}e^{2\pi\textup{i}\lambda_{q+1}N_{\Lambda}(l\bar{k}+m\bar{\bar{k}})\cdot\Phi_{i}}:=\sum_{j\in I^{\theta}_{k}}b_{k,j}e^{2\pi\textup{i}\lambda_{q+1}j\cdot\Phi_{i}},\\
W^{v}_{(\lambda_{q+1}k)}(\Phi_{i}(x,t)):=\sum_{l,m\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}}a_{k,l}a_{k,m}e^{2\pi\textup{i}\lambda_{q+1}N_{\Lambda}(lk+m\bar{\bar{k}})\cdot\Phi_{i}}:=\sum_{j\in I^{v}_{k}}b_{k,j}e^{2\pi\textup{i}\lambda_{q+1}j\cdot\Phi_{i}},\end{aligned}\right.

(4.58)

where
I^{\theta}_{k}
and
I^{v}_{k}
are defined by

\displaystyle I^{\theta}_{k}=\big\{j=N_{\Lambda}l\bar{k}+N_{\Lambda}m\bar{\bar{k}}\,\,|\,\,l,m\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\},k\in\Lambda_{\theta}\big\},

\displaystyle I^{v}_{k}=\big\{j=N_{\Lambda}lk+N_{\Lambda}m\bar{\bar{k}}\,\,|\,\,l,m\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\},k\in\Lambda_{v}\big\}.

From the smoothness of
W^{\theta}_{(k)}
and
W^{v}_{(k)}
, we can infer that for
j\in I^{\theta}_{k}\cup I^{v}_{k}
,

|b_{k,j}|\lesssim|lm|^{-6}\lesssim|j|^{-6}.

(4.59)

Furthermore,
W^{v}_{(\lambda_{q+1}k)}(\Phi_{i}(x,t))
and
W^{\theta}_{(\lambda_{q+1}k)}(\Phi_{i}(x,t))
are
\mathbb{T}^{3}
-periodic function with zero mean. By (
4.49
), we have

\displaystyle\big\|W^{v}_{(k)}(\Phi_{i}(x,t))\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}=\big\|W^{\theta}_{(k)}(\Phi_{i}(x,t))\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}=1.

(4.60)

Step 2:  Construction of the perturbation

Now, we are focused on constructing the perturbation parts
(w_{q+1},d_{q+1})
by (
4.58
). First of all, we rewrite the vector function
\nabla\Phi_{i}{\bar{T}}_{q}\eta_{i}(t)
in components form as follows.

-\nabla\Phi_{i}{\bar{T}}_{q}\eta_{i}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\big(-\nabla\Phi_{i}{\bar{T}}_{q}\big)^{j}\eta_{i}(t)e_{j}:=\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\theta}}\mathcal{T}_{q,i,k}\eta_{i}(t)k,

(4.61)

where we have used the fact that
\Lambda_{\theta}=\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
.
The
principal part
of the perturbation of glued velocity is defined by

\displaystyle w^{(p)}_{q+1}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{i_{\max}-1}w^{(p)}_{q+1,i}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{i_{\max}-1}\big(\delta^{-{1/2}}_{q+1}\ell^{-\alpha/2}_{q}\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\theta}}\mathcal{T}_{q,i,k}\eta_{i}(t)\nabla\Phi^{-1}_{i}W^{\theta}_{(\lambda_{q+1}k)}(\Phi_{i}(x,t))k

\displaystyle+{\delta^{{1/2}}_{q+1}}\ell^{\alpha/2}_{q}\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{v}}a_{v,k}\big({R_{v}}\big)\eta_{i}(t)\nabla\Phi^{-1}_{i}W^{v}_{(\lambda_{q+1}k)}(\Phi_{i}(x,t))\bar{k}\big),

(4.62)

where

R_{v}=\nabla\Phi_{i}\big(\mathrm{Id}-\tfrac{\mathring{\bar{R}}_{q}}{\delta_{q+1}\ell^{\alpha}_{q}}\big)(\nabla\Phi_{i})^{\mathsf{T}}+\delta^{-1}_{q+1}\ell^{-\alpha}_{q}\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\theta}}\mathcal{T}^{2}_{q,i,k}\eta^{2}_{i}(t)k\otimes k.

(4.63)

According to the representation (
4.58
), we can rewrite
w^{(p)}_{q+1}
as follows:

\displaystyle w^{(p)}_{q+1}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{i_{\max}-1}w^{(p)}_{q+1,i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{i_{\max}-1}\Big(\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\theta}}\sum_{j\in I^{\theta}_{k}}A_{k,i,j}ke^{2\pi\textup{i}\lambda_{q+1}j\cdot\Phi_{i}}+\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{v}}\sum_{j\in I^{v}_{k}}B_{k,i,j}\bar{k}e^{2\pi\textup{i}\lambda_{q+1}j\cdot\Phi_{i}}\Big),

(4.64)

where

\left\{\begin{aligned} &A_{k,i,j}=\delta^{-{1/2}}_{q+1}\ell^{-\alpha/2}_{q}b_{k,j}\mathcal{T}_{q,i,k}\eta_{i}(t)\nabla\Phi^{-1}_{i},\\
&B_{k,i,j}={\delta^{{1/2}}_{q+1}}\ell^{\alpha/2}_{q}b_{k,j}a_{v,k}({R_{v}})\eta_{i}(t)\nabla\Phi^{-1}_{i}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.65)

The perturbation of glued temperature is constructed as follows:

\displaystyle d_{q+1}=\sum_{i=0}^{i_{\max}-1}d_{q+1,i}:=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{i_{\max}-1}\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\theta}}\delta^{{1/2}}_{q+1}\ell^{\alpha/2}_{q}W^{\theta}_{(\lambda_{q+1}k)}(\Phi_{i}(x))\eta_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{i_{\max}-1}\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\theta}}\sum_{j\in I^{\theta}_{k}}\delta^{{1/2}}_{q+1}\ell^{\alpha/2}_{q}b_{k,j}\eta_{i}(t)e^{2\pi\textup{i}\lambda_{q+1}j\cdot\Phi_{i}}.

(4.66)

Next, we construct the
incompressibility corrector
to guarantee that the perturbation of glued velocity is divergence-free. To obtain the incompressibility corrector, we firstly give an equivalent form of
w^{(p)}_{q+1}
.

For any vectors
j
and
k
satisfying
j\perp k
, one deduces that

\displaystyle{2\pi}^{-1}\lambda^{-1}_{q+1}\curl_{\xi}\Big(\Big(\frac{\textup{i}j\times k}{|j|^{2}}\Big)e^{2\pi\textup{i}\lambda_{q+1}j\cdot\xi}\Big)=\textup{i}j\times\Big(\frac{\textup{i}j\times k}{|j|^{2}}\Big)e^{2\pi\textup{i}\lambda_{q+1}j\cdot\xi}=ke^{2\pi\textup{i}\lambda_{q+1}j\cdot\xi},

This fact combined with

j\perp k,\quad\forall\,\,k\in\Lambda_{\theta},\,j\in I^{\theta}_{k};\quad j\perp\bar{k},\quad\forall\,\,k\in\Lambda_{v},\,j\in I^{v}_{k}

allows us to obtain from (
4.64
) that

\displaystyle w^{(p)}_{q+1,i}=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{-{1/2}}_{q+1}\ell^{-\alpha/2}_{q}}{2\pi\lambda_{q+1}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\theta}}\sum_{j\in I^{\theta}_{k}}\mathcal{T}_{q,i,k}\nabla\Phi^{-1}_{i}\curl_{\xi}\Big(\Big(\frac{\textup{i}j\times b_{k,j}k}{|j|^{2}}\Big)e^{2\pi\textup{i}\lambda_{q+1}j\cdot\xi}\Big)(\Phi_{i})

\displaystyle+\frac{\delta^{{1/2}}_{q+1}\ell^{\alpha/2}_{q}}{2\pi\lambda_{q+1}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{v}}\sum_{j\in I^{v}_{k}}a_{v,k}\big({R_{v}}\big)\nabla\Phi^{-1}_{i}\curl_{\xi}\Big(\Big(\frac{\textup{i}j\times b_{k,j}\bar{k}}{|j|^{2}}\Big)e^{2\pi\textup{i}\lambda_{q+1}j\cdot\xi}\Big)(\Phi_{i}).

Combining with

\nabla\Phi^{-1}_{i}(\curl U)(\Phi_{i})=\curl(\nabla\Phi^{\mathsf{T}}_{i}U(\Phi_{i})),

we rewrite
w^{(p)}_{q+1,i}
as follows:

\displaystyle w^{(p)}_{q+1,i}=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{-{1/2}}_{q+1}\ell^{-\alpha/2}_{q}}{2\pi\lambda_{q+1}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\theta}}\sum_{j\in I^{\theta}_{k}}\mathcal{T}_{q,i,k}\curl\Big(\nabla\Phi^{\mathsf{T}}_{i}\Big(\frac{\textup{i}j\times b_{k,j}k}{|j|^{2}}\Big)e^{2\pi\textup{i}\lambda_{q+1}j\cdot\Phi_{i}}\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{\delta^{{1/2}}_{q+1}\ell^{\alpha/2}_{q}}{2\pi\lambda_{q+1}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{v}}\sum_{j\in I^{v}_{k}}a_{v,k}\big({R_{v}}\big)\curl\Big(\nabla\Phi^{\mathsf{T}}_{i}\Big(\frac{\textup{i}j\times b_{k,j}\bar{k}}{|j|^{2}}\Big)e^{2\pi\textup{i}\lambda_{q+1}j\cdot\Phi_{i}}\Big).

This equivalent form of
w^{(p)}_{q+1,i}
helps us define

\displaystyle w^{(c)}_{q+1,i}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{\theta}}\sum_{j\in I^{\theta}_{k}}E_{k,i,j}e^{2\pi\textup{i}\lambda_{q+1}j\cdot\Phi_{i}}+\sum_{k\in\Lambda_{v}}\sum_{j\in I^{v}_{k}}F_{k,i,j}e^{2\pi\textup{i}\lambda_{q+1}j\cdot\Phi_{i}},

(4.67)

where

\left\{\begin{aligned} &E_{k,i,j}=\frac{\delta^{-{1/2}}_{q+1}\ell^{-\alpha/2}_{q}}{2\pi\lambda_{q+1}}\nabla\mathcal{T}_{q,i,k}\times\Big(\nabla\Phi^{\mathsf{T}}_{i}\Big(\frac{\textup{i}j\times b_{k,j}k}{|j|^{2}}\Big)\Big),\\
&F_{k,i,j}=\frac{\delta^{{1/2}}_{q+1}\ell^{\alpha/2}_{q}}{2\pi\lambda_{q+1}}\nabla\big(a_{v,k}\big({R_{v}})\big)\times\Big(\nabla\Phi^{\mathsf{T}}_{i}\Big(\frac{\textup{i}j\times b_{k,j}\bar{k}}{|j|^{2}}\Big)\Big).\end{aligned}\right.

(4.68)

Since
\curl(fW)=\nabla f\times W+f\curl W
, we get that
\div(w^{(p)}_{q+1,i}+w^{(c)}_{q+1,i})=0
. Finally, we construct
w_{q+1}
as

w_{q+1}=\sum_{i=0}^{i_{\max}-1}(w^{(p)}_{q+1,i}+w^{(c)}_{q+1,i}).

Step 3:  Preliminaries for the estimates for perturbation

In order to estimate
(w_{q+1},d_{q+1})
, we give the derivative estimates of
R_{v}
for preparation.

Proposition 4.11

(Estimates for
R_{v}
)
.

For
0\leq N\leq N_{0}+1
, we have

\displaystyle\|R_{v}\|_{N}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta_{q+1}\ell^{-N+\frac{5}{4}\alpha}_{q},

(4.69)

\displaystyle\|D_{t,\bar{v}_{q}}R_{v}\|_{N}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta_{q+1}\delta^{1/2}_{q}\lambda_{q}\ell^{-N-\frac{3}{4}\alpha}_{q}.

(4.70)

Proof.

By the definition of
\mathcal{T}_{q,i,k}
in (
4.61
), we obtain from (
4.41
) and (
4.54
) that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}_{q,i,k}\|_{N}\leq\|{\bar{T}}_{q}\|_{N}\|\nabla\Phi_{i}\|_{0}+\|{\bar{T}}_{q}\|_{0}\|\nabla\Phi_{i}\|_{N}\lesssim\delta_{q+1}\ell_{q}^{-N+\frac{5}{4}\alpha}.

(4.71)

Combining (
4.71
) with (
4.41
) and (
4.54
), we infer from the definition of
R_{v}
in (
4.63
) that

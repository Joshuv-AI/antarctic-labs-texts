The Dyson equation for 2 2 -positive maps and Hölder bounds for the Lévy distance of densities of states Thanks: The author wants to thank Johannes Hoffmann and Roland Speicher for the interesting discussions which have inspired this work.
Tobias Mai

Abstract
. The so-called density of states is a Borel probability measure on the real line associated with the solution of the Dyson equation which we set up, on any fixed C ∗ C^{\ast} -probability space, for a selfadjoint offset and a 2 2 -positive linear map. Using techniques from free noncommutative function theory, we prove explicit Hölder bounds for the Lévy distance of two such measures when any of the two parameters varies. As the main tools for the proof, which are also of independent interest, we show that solutions of the Dyson equation have strong analytic properties and evolve along any C 1 C^{1} -path of 2 2 -positive linear maps according to an operator-valued version of the inviscid Burgers equation.

Operator-valued semicircular elements often do not show up explicitly in such contexts, but rather in the guise of their operator-valued Cauchy transforms or by equations determining the latter. To build our intuition, let us take a closer look at the model considered in
[
28
,
27

. Voiculescu’s multivariate generalization
[

of Wigner’s semicircle law
[

]
(in its almost sure version
[

,

]
which generalizes
[
7
]

(X^{N}_{1},\dots,X^{N}_{n})
of independent standard Gaussian random matrices of size
N\times N
converge in distribution almost surely as
N\to\infty
towards a tuple
(S_{1},\dots,S_{n})
of freely independent standard semicircular elements. Explicitly, with respect to the normalized trace
\operatorname{tr}_{N}
on
M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
, we have for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and each choice of indices
1\leq i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\leq n
that

(1.1)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{tr}_{N}(X_{i_{1}}^{N}X_{i_{2}}^{N}\cdots X_{i_{k}}^{N})=\tau(S_{i_{1}}S_{i_{2}}\cdots S_{i_{k}})\qquad\text{almost surely},

where we suppose that
(S_{1},\dots,S_{n})
is realized in some
C^{\ast}
-probability space
(M,\tau)
.
For fixed selfadjoint matrices
b_{0},b_{1},\dots,b_{n}\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
, we aim at understanding the asymptotic behavior of the selfadjoint random matrices
X^{N}:=b_{0}\otimes\mathbf{1}_{N}+b_{1}\otimes X_{1}^{N}+\dots+b_{n}\otimes X_{n}^{N}
. It follows from (
1.1
) that the empirical eigenvalue distribution
\mu_{X^{N}}
of
X^{N}
converges in distribution almost surely as
N\to\infty
to the analytic distribution
\mu_{S}
of the noncommutative random variable
S:=b_{0}\otimes\mathbf{1}+b_{1}\otimes S_{1}+\dots+b_{n}\otimes S_{n}
in the
C^{\ast}
-probability space
(M_{m}(\mathbb{C})\otimes M,\operatorname{tr}_{m}\otimes\tau)
. In fact, by means of operator-valued free probability, we can even compute
\mu_{S}
. The crucial observation is that
S
, in the operator-valued
C^{\ast}
-probability space
(M_{m}(\mathbb{C})\otimes M,\mathbb{E},M_{m}(\mathbb{C}))
with conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}:=\operatorname{id}_{M_{m}(\mathbb{C})}\otimes\tau
, forms an operator-valued semicircular element with mean
b_{0}
and the covariance
\eta:M_{m}(\mathbb{C})\to M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
given by
\eta(b):=\sum^{n}_{j=1}b_{j}bb_{j}
for
b\in M_{m}(\mathbb{C})
. This implies that the operator-valued Cauchy transform
G_{S}:\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{m}(\mathbb{C}))\to\mathbb{H}^{-}(M_{m}(\mathbb{C}))
defined by
G_{S}(b):=\mathbb{E}[(b-S)^{-1}]
solves the equation

(b-b_{0})G_{S}(b)=\mathbf{1}+\eta(G_{S}(b))G_{S}(b)\qquad\text{for $b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{m}(\mathbb{C}))$}.

In fact,
G_{S}
is hereby uniquely determined and can be computed efficiently by some fixed point iteration; see
[
31
,
34
]
. This change of perspective thus settles the problem of determining
\mu_{S}
, since
G_{S}
recovers the scalar-valued Cauchy transform
\mathcal{G}_{\mu_{S}}
of
\mu_{S}
via the relation
\mathcal{G}_{\mu_{S}}(z)=\operatorname{tr}_{m}(G_{S}(z\mathbf{1}))
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
and since
\mu_{S}
can be obtained from
\mathcal{G}_{\mu_{S}}
via Stieltjes inversion.

In this paper, we are concerned with a significant generalization of this construction which originates in
[
2
,
3
]
and which we briefly sketch now; a more detailed presentation will be given in Section
3
, in particular in Definition
3.4
.
Let
(B,\phi)
be a
C^{\ast}
-probability space. We put
B_{\mathrm{sa}}:=\{b\in B\mid b^{\ast}=b\}
and denote by
\mathcal{P}(B)
the set of all
\mathbb{C}
-linear maps
\eta:B\to B
which are positive; see Subsection
2.1
for more details.
To any
\rho=(b_{0},\eta)\in B_{\mathrm{sa}}\times\mathcal{P}(B)
, called
data pair
, we associate the holomorphic function
G_{\eta}:\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)\to\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
which is uniquely determined by the
Dyson equation

(1.2)

bG_{\eta}(b)=\mathbf{1}+\eta(G_{\eta}(b))G_{\eta}(b)\qquad\text{for $b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)$}

and consider in turn the holomorphic function
\mathcal{G}_{\rho}:\mathbb{C}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}^{-}
which is defined by
\mathcal{G}_{\rho}(z):=\phi(G_{\eta}(z\mathbf{1}-b_{0}))
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
. Since
\mathcal{G}_{\rho}
satisfies
\lim_{y\to\infty}iy\mathcal{G}_{\rho}(iy)=1
, it must be the Cauchy transform of a hereby uniquely determined Borel probability measure
\mu_{\rho}
on
\mathbb{R}
, to which we shall refer as the
density of states
associated with
\rho
. In this way, we obtain a mapping

\mu:\ B_{\mathrm{sa}}\times\mathcal{P}(B)\to\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R}),\quad\rho\mapsto\mu_{\rho}

taking values in the set
\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R})
of all Borel probability measures on the real line
\mathbb{R}
.

For the operator-valued semicircular element
S
with mean
b_{0}
and covariance
\eta
which we introduced above, this procedure recovers
\mu_{S}
as
\mu_{(b_{0},\eta)}
; in fact, it works for any operator-valued semicircular element
S
in an arbitrary operator-valued
C^{\ast}
-probability space
(A,\mathbb{E},B)
and with respect to some distinguished state
\phi
on
B
. This highlights the fundamental fact that, if we keep
(B,\phi)
fixed, then
\mu_{S}
only depends on
b_{0}
and the completely positive linear map
\eta
, but not on the concrete realization
S
or the corresponding space
(A,\mathbb{E},B)
.
We point out that with
[
44
, Proposition 2.2]
, it is possible to construct for every data pair
(b_{0},\eta)
with
\eta
being completely positive an operator-valued
C^{\ast}
-probability space
(A,\mathbb{E},B)
and an operator-valued semicircular element
S
with mean
b_{0}
and covariance
\eta
. The map
\mu
introduced above not only circumvents this construction, but even works in situations where no such realization of
(b_{0},\eta)
can exist.

Our aim is to quantify the proximity of
\mu_{\rho_{0}}
and
\mu_{\rho_{1}}
in terms of the data pairs
\rho_{0},\rho_{1}\in B_{\mathrm{sa}}\times\mathcal{P}(B)
. To this end, we endow

•

\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R})
with the Lévy metric
L:\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R})\times\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R})\to[0,1]
, which is known to provide a metrization of weak convergence;

•

B_{\mathrm{sa}}
with the metric
d_{B_{\mathrm{sa}}}
induced by the norm
\|\cdot\|
on the
C^{\ast}
-algebra
B
;

•

\mathcal{P}(B)
with the metric
d_{\mathcal{P}(B)}
induced by the norm
\|\eta\|:=\sup_{\|b\|\leq 1}\|\eta(b)\|
on the space of all bounded linear operators
\eta:B\to B
.

More precisely, we will derive Hölder bounds for
L(\mu_{\rho_{1}},\mu_{\rho_{0}})
in terms of
d_{B_{\mathrm{sa}}}(b_{0,1},b_{0,0})
and
d_{\mathcal{P}(B)}(\eta_{1},\eta_{0})
, depending on the varying argument, but uniformly in the respective other argument; for that purpose, however, we need to restrict
\mu
to the subset
B_{\mathrm{sa}}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)
of
B_{\mathrm{sa}}\times\mathcal{P}(B)
, where
\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)
stands for the set of all
\eta\in\mathcal{P}(B)
which are
2
-positive. The necessity of restricting
\mu
arose also in
[
3
]
, but there the authors required
\eta
to satisfy some strict version of positivity called
flatness
. This point and that we drop any symmetry condition for
\eta
are the crucial differences between our approach and that of
[
3
]
, aside from the technical variation that we work in the more general setting of
C^{\ast}
-probability spaces which are not necessarily tracial, whereas
[
3
]
is formulated for tracial

-probability spaces.

For the sake of simplicity, we will denote the restriction of
\mu
to
B_{\mathrm{sa}}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)
again by
\mu
. The precise statements, which constitute the first main result of this paper, read as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(B,\phi)
be a
C^{\ast}
-probability space and consider the map
\mu:B_{\mathrm{sa}}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)\to\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R})
defined above. Then the following bounds hold true:

(i)

With
c_{1}:=3(\frac{1}{\pi})^{1/3}<2.0484
, we have for each choice of
\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)
that

L(\mu_{(b_{0,1},\eta)},\mu_{(b_{0,0},\eta)})\leq c_{1}\,\|b_{0,1}-b_{0,0}\|^{1/3}\qquad\text{for all $b_{0,0},b_{0,1}\in B_{\mathrm{sa}}$.}

(ii)

With
c_{2}:=5(\frac{1}{4\pi})^{2/5}<1.8168
, we have for each choice of
b_{0}\in B_{\mathrm{sa}}
that

L(\mu_{(b_{0},\eta_{1})},\mu_{(b_{0},\eta_{0})})\leq c_{2}\,\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{0}\|^{1/5}\qquad\text{for all $\eta_{0},\eta_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)$.}

In particular, if the Cartesian product
B_{\mathrm{sa}}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)
is endowed with any product metric
d
, then the map
\mu:(B_{\mathrm{sa}}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(B),d)\to(\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R}),L)
is uniformly continuous.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
will be given in Section
5
. For both of the Hölder bounds
(i)
and
(ii)
, we will consider an affine linear interpolation
(\rho_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
between
\rho_{0}
and
\rho_{1}
for which the “time derivative” of

can be controlled. Along the way, we obtain estimates for the integrals
\frac{1}{\pi}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}|\mathcal{G}_{\mu_{\rho_{1}}}(s+i\varepsilon)-\mathcal{G}_{\mu_{\rho_{0}}}(s+i\varepsilon)|\,ds
for
\varepsilon>0
which we record in Corollary
5.2

The bounds in Theorem

For that purpose, it is crucial to understand how solutions

\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}=\{b\in B\mid\operatorname{Im}(b)\geq\gamma\mathbf{1}\}

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G:\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)\times\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)\to\mathbb{H}^{-}(B),(\eta,b)\mapsto G_{\eta}(b)
be the function determined by the Dyson equation (
1.2
). We endow

(i)

For every fixed
\gamma>0

G_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(b):\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)\to\mathbb{H}^{-}(B),\eta\mapsto G_{\eta}(b)
with

(1.3)

\lim_{\eta\to\eta_{0}}\sup_{b\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}}\|G_{\eta}(b)-G_{\eta_{0}}(b)\|=0\qquad\text{for each $\eta_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)$}.

In particular,
G:\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)\times\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)\to\mathbb{H}^{-}(B),(\eta,b)\mapsto G_{\eta}(b)

(ii)

For every fixed
\gamma>0

(DG_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}})(b):\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)\to\mathcal{L}(B),\eta\mapsto(DG_{\eta})(b)

(1.4)

\lim_{\eta\to\eta_{0}}\sup_{b\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}}\|(DG_{\eta})(b)-(DG_{\eta_{0}})(b)\|=0\qquad\text{for each $\eta_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)$}.

In particular,
DG:\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)\times\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)\to\mathcal{L}(B),(\eta,b)\mapsto(DG_{\eta})(b)

The proof of Theorem
1.2
will be given in Section

Our third main result given in the following theorem says that the evolution of solutions of the Dyson equation (
1.2
) along

-paths

can be described by an operator-valued version of the inviscid Burgers equation (

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\eta:[0,T]\to\mathcal{P}_{2}(B),t\mapsto\eta_{t}
be a
C^{1}
-path. Let
G:[0,T]\times\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)\to\mathbb{H}^{-}(B),(t,b)\mapsto G_{t}(b)
be the function defined by
G_{t}(b):=G_{\eta_{t}}(b)
for all
t\in[0,T]
and
b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
. We endow
[0,T]\times\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
with the product topology. Then, for every fixed
\gamma>0
, the family
G_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(b):[0,T]\to\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
with

is equidifferentiable, i.e., we have

(1.5)

\lim_{t\to t_{0}}\sup_{b\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}}\Big\|\frac{1}{t-t_{0}}\big(G_{t}(b)-G_{t_{0}}(b)\big)-\overset{\,{}_{\mbox{\bf.}}}{G}_{t_{0}}(b)\Big\|=0\qquad\text{for each $t_{0}\in[0,T]$}.

Moreover,
G
satisfies the partial differential equation

(1.6)

\overset{\,{}_{\mbox{\bf.}}}{G}_{t}(b)=-(DG_{t})(b)\big(\overset{\,{}_{\mbox{\bf.}}}{\eta}_{t}(G_{t}(b))\big)\qquad\text{for $(t,b)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)$}.

In particular,
\overset{\,{}_{\mbox{\bf.}}}{G}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)\to B,(t,b)\mapsto\overset{\,{}_{\mbox{\bf.}}}{G}_{t}(b)
is continuous and
\overset{\,{}_{\mbox{\bf.}}}{G}_{t}:\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)\to B

.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Positive and completely positive maps

For complex Banach spaces
(E,\|\cdot\|_{E})
and
(F,\|\cdot\|_{F})
, we denote by
\mathcal{L}(E,F)
the Banach space of all bounded linear operators
T
from
E
to
F
with the norm given by
\|T\|:=\sup_{\|x\|_{E}\leq 1}\|Tx\|_{F}
. We abbreviate
\mathcal{L}(E):=\mathcal{L}(E,E)
.

For a unital
C^{\ast}
-algebra
B
, whose unit we denote by
\mathbf{1}
, we let
B_{\mathrm{sa}}:=\{b\in B\mid b^{\ast}=b\}
be the real subspace of
B
consisting of all selfadjoint elements and we let
B_{+}:=\{b\in B\mid b\geq 0\}
be the convex cone of all positive elements in
B
.
For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
M_{k}(B)
the unital
C^{\ast}
-algebra of all
k\times k
matrices over
B
; the unit in
M_{k}(B)
will be denoted by
\mathbf{1}_{k}
.

A map
\eta:B\to B
is said to be
positive
if it is
\mathbb{C}
-linear and maps
B_{+}
into itself. Note that every such
\eta
is automatically bounded, i.e., it belongs to
\mathcal{L}(B)
; in fact, we have
\|\eta\|=\|\eta(\mathbf{1})\|
(see, for instance, Corollary 2.9 in
[
43
]
). The set of all positive maps
\eta:B\to B
, which will be denoted by
\mathcal{P}(B)
, thus forms a convex subset of
\mathcal{L}(B)
.
Further, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
\mathcal{P}_{k}(B)
the subset of
\mathcal{P}(B)
consisting of those
\eta
which are
k
-positive
, i.e., for which the amplification
\eta^{(k)}:M_{k}(B)\to M_{k}(B)
, given by
\eta^{(k)}(b):=(\eta(b_{ij}))_{i,j=1}^{k}
for every matrix
b=(b_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{k}\in M_{k}(B)
, are positive in the aforementioned sense. Clearly,

\mathcal{P}(B)=\mathcal{P}_{1}(B)\supseteq\dots\supseteq\mathcal{P}_{k}(B)\supseteq\mathcal{P}_{k+1}(B)\supseteq\dots\supseteq\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(B),

where
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(B)
denotes the subset of
\mathcal{P}(B)
consisting of those
\eta\in\mathcal{P}(B)
which are
completely positive
, i.e., which belong to
\mathcal{P}_{k}(B)
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
; in other words,
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(B):=\bigcap_{k\geq 1}\mathcal{P}_{k}(B)
. Notice that
\mathcal{P}_{k}(B)
, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and likewise
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(B)
form convex subsets of
\mathcal{L}(B)
.

As shown in
[
18
]
, each of the inclusions in the above chain can be strict depending on
B
; more precisely,
[
18
, Theorem 1]
says that we have
\mathcal{P}_{n-1}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))\supsetneq\mathcal{P}_{n}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
. On the other hand,
[
18
, Theorem 5]
tells us that
\mathcal{P}_{n}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))=\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(M_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
. In particular, already on
M_{3}(\mathbb{C})
there are
2
-positive maps which are not completely positive; a concrete example that was given in
[
18
]
is
\eta:M_{3}(\mathbb{C})\to M_{3}(\mathbb{C})
defined by
\eta(b):=2\operatorname{Tr}_{3}(b)\mathbf{1}_{3}-b
. In view of these facts, our results on the operator-valued inviscid Burgers equation provide a significant extension of the previous work done in this direction.

2.2.
Holomorphic functions between Banach spaces

In this section, we give a survey on notions of holomorphy for functions between subsets of complex Banach spaces and we briefly recall some of their properties which will be used in the sequel. For more details, we refer the interested readers to the monographs
[
33
,
21
]
.

In the following, let
(E,\|\cdot\|_{E})
and
(F,\|\cdot\|_{F})
be complex Banach spaces.

Recall that a function
f:U\to F
on an open subset
\emptyset\neq U\subseteq E
is said to be
Gâteaux holomorphic on
U
if the limit, called the
Gâteaux derivative
of
f
at
x
in the direction
h
,

\delta f(x;h):=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\rightarrow 0\\
z\in U(x;h)\backslash\{0\}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{z}(f(x+zh)-f(x))

exists for all
x\in U
and
h\in E
, where
U(x;h):=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid x+zh\in U\}
.
According to
[
21
, Lemma 3.3]
, a function
f:U\to F
is Gâteaux holomorphic if and only if, for every choice of
x\in U
and
h\in E
and each bounded linear functional
\phi:F\to\mathbb{C}
, the
\mathbb{C}
-valued function
U(x;h)\ni z\mapsto\phi(f(x+zh))
is holomorphic in the sense of one-variable complex analysis.

Further, we recall that a function
f:U\to F
is said to be
Fréchet holomorphic on
U
, if

(i)

it is Gâteaux holomorphic on
U
,

(ii)

its Gâteaux derivative
\delta f(x;\cdot)
at any point
x\in U
is a bounded linear operator, and

(iii)

\displaystyle\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\|h\|_{E}}\|f(x+h)-f(x)-\delta f(x;h)\|_{F}=0
.

In this case, we write
Df(x)
for the bounded linear operator given by the Gâteaux derivative
\delta f(x;\cdot)
and call it the
Fréchet derivative
of
f
at the point
x\in U
.
It can be shown (see (2.3) in
[
66
]
) that
\delta f(x;\cdot)
is always linear; therefore, condition
(ii)
above reduces to the requirement that
\delta f(x;\cdot)
is bounded. A result of Zorn
[
67
]
says that condition
(iii)
is redundant.

It is an immediate consequence of the definitions that every Fréchet holomorphic function
f:U\to F
is Gâteaux holomorphic and continuous. In particular, every Fréchet holomorphic function is holomorphic in the following sense (see
[
29
]
): a function
f:U\to F
is said to be
holomorphic
if it is Gâteaux holomorphic on
U
and
locally bounded on
U
, i.e., for every
x_{0}\in U
there exists
r=r(x_{0})>0
such that

\sup_{x\in U\colon\|x-x_{0}\|_{E}<r}\|f(x)\|_{F}<\infty.

Remarkably, it turns out that conversely every holomorphic function
f:U\to F
necessarily is Fréchet holomorphic; see
[
33
, Theorem 3.17.1]
. In other words, holomorphy and Fréchet holomorphy are equivalent concepts. The following lemma is a slight generalization of some key arguments in the proof of the aforementioned fact, which we record for later use.
To shorten the notation, we set
\mathbb{B}_{E}(x_{0},r):=\{x\in E\mid\|x-x_{0}\|_{E}<r\}
for
x_{0}\in E
and
r>0
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f:U\to F
be a Gâteaux holomorphic function. Suppose that
x_{0}\in U
and
r>0
are such that
\mathbb{B}_{E}(x_{0},r)\subseteq U
and
M_{f}(x_{0},r):=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{B}_{E}(x_{0},r)}\|f(x)\|_{F}<\infty
. Then the following statements hold true:

(i)

For every
h\in E
, we have that
\|\delta f(x_{0};h)\|_{F}\leq\frac{1}{r}M_{f}(x_{0},r)\|h\|_{E}
.

(ii)

For
h\in\mathbb{B}_{E}(0,r)
, we have
\|f(x_{0}+h)-f(x_{0})-\delta f(x_{0};h)\|_{F}\leq\frac{M_{f}(x_{0},r)}{r(r-\|h\|_{E})}\|h\|_{E}^{2}.

The different characterizations of holomorphy are useful to carry over results from one-variable complex analysis. The next lemma, for instance, is a consequence of Vitali’s theorem; it is contained in
[
33
, Theorem 3.18.1]
, but since the proof simplifies significantly in the particular situation that we are considering here, we include the argument for the sake of clarity.
Recall that a family
\mathcal{F}
of functions
f:U\to F
is called
locally uniformly bounded
if for every
x_{0}\in U
there exists
r=r(x_{0})>0
such that

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\ \sup_{x\in U\colon\|x-x_{0}\|_{E}<r}\|f(x)\|_{F}<\infty.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of Gâteaux holomorphic functions
f_{n}:U\to F
which is locally uniformly bounded and pointwise convergent to a function
f:U\to F
. Then
f
is (Fréchet) holomorphic.

Proof.

Let us fix
x\in U
,
h\in E
, as well as some bounded linear functional
\phi:F\to\mathbb{C}
. Since each
f_{n}
is Gâteaux holomorphic on
U
, we obtain holomorphic functions
g_{n}:U(x;h)\to\mathbb{C}
by
g_{n}(z):=\phi(f_{n}(x+zh))
; since
(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is locally uniformly bounded on
U
and converges pointwise on
U
to
f
, we infer that
(g_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is locally uniformly bounded on
U(x;h)
and pointwise convergent on
U(x;h)
to the function
g:U(x;h)\to\mathbb{C}
which is given by
g(z):=\phi(f(x+zh))
. By Vitali’s theorem, it follows that
(g_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges locally uniformly to
g
, so that
g
must be holomorphic. This verifies that
f
is Gâteaux holomorphic on
U
. Because
(f_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is locally uniformly bounded and pointwise convergent to
f
by assumption, it follows that
f
is locally bounded. In summary, we have that
f
is (Fréchet) holomorphic.
∎

2.3.
Cauchy transforms

We denote by
\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R})
the set of all Borel probability measures on the real line
\mathbb{R}
. For every
\mu\in\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R})
, let
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
be the
Cauchy transform
of
\mu
, i.e., the holomorphic function

\mathcal{G}_{\mu}:\ \mathbb{C}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}^{-},\quad z\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{z-t}\,d\mu(t)

which is defined on the complex upper half-plane
\mathbb{C}^{+}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\operatorname{Im}(z)>0\}
and takes its values in the complex lower half-plane
\mathbb{C}^{-}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\operatorname{Im}(z)<0\}
.

For later use, we recall the following well-known characterization of Cauchy transforms. We refer the readers to
[
25
]
for an excellent survey on this subject; in particular, the statement given below is
[
25
, Lemma 2]
, except that the authors work with the
Stieltjes transform

\mathcal{S}_{\mu}:\mathbb{C}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}^{+}
instead of the Cauchy transform
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}:\mathbb{C}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}^{-}
, which differ only by sign.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}:\mathbb{C}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}
be holomorphic. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\mathcal{G}
is the Cauchy transform of a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
, i.e., there exists some
\mu\in\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\mathcal{G}(z)=\mathcal{G}_{\mu}(z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
.

(ii)

\mathcal{G}
satisfies the conditions
\operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{G}(z))>0
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
and
\lim_{y\to\infty}iy\mathcal{G}(iy)=1
.

We notice that
\mu\in\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R})
is in fact uniquely determined by its Cauchy transform
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
; in other words (see
[
25
, Lemma 1]
), for
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R})
, we have that
\mathcal{G}_{\mu_{1}}(z)=\mathcal{G}_{\mu_{2}}(z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
if and only if
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
. In particular, we see that the measure
\mu
in Item
(i)
of Theorem
2.3
is unique.

2.4.
Lévy and Kolmogorov distance

The Lévy and the Kolmogorov distance yield metrics on
\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R})
which are among the most prominent ones which are usually considered on that space; see, e.g.,
[
17
]
. In this paper, we provide bounds for the Lévy distance of densities of states, but we point out that such bounds often go over to the Kolmogorov distance.

Before giving the definitions of the Lévy and the Kolmogorov distance, we stipulate that
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
will stand for the
cumulative distribution function
of any
\mu\in\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R})
; recall that
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}
is defined by
\mathcal{F}_{\mu}(t):=\mu((-\infty,t])
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

If
\mu,\nu\in\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R})
are given, then their
Lévy distance
is defined by

L(\mu,\nu):=\inf\{\varepsilon>0\mid\forall t\in\mathbb{R}:\ \mathcal{F}_{\mu}(t-\varepsilon)-\varepsilon\leq\mathcal{F}_{\nu}(t)\leq\mathcal{F}_{\mu}(t+\varepsilon)+\varepsilon\}

and their
Kolmogorov distance
is defined by

\Delta(\mu,\nu):=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{F}_{\mu}(t)-\mathcal{F}_{\nu}(t)|.

It is well-known that the Lévy distance defines a metric on
\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R})
which provides a metrization of weak convergence in the sense that
L(\mu_{n},\mu)\to 0
if and only if
(\mu_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges weakly to
\mu
.

The Lévy distance is always upper-bounded by the Kolmogorov distance, i.e., we have
L(\mu,\nu)\leq\Delta(\mu,\nu)
for all
\mu,\nu\in\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R})
. If we suppose in addition that
\nu
has a cumulative distribution function
\mathcal{F}_{\nu}
which is Hölder continuous with exponent
\beta\in(0,1]
and Hölder constant
C>0
, i.e.,
|\mathcal{F}_{\nu}(t)-\mathcal{F}_{\nu}(s)|\leq C|t-s|^{\beta}
holds for all
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
, then
[
10
, Lemma B.18]
says that we also have the complementary bound
\Delta(\mu,\nu)\leq(C+1)L(\mu,\nu)^{\beta}
.

Both the Lévy and the Kolmogorov distance can be controlled in terms of the Cauchy transforms of the involved measures. For the Kolmogorov distance, such bounds are given in
[
8
, Theorem 2.2]
; see also
[
9
,
10
]
. These bounds, however, require some regularity of the cumulative distribution function of at least one of the considered measures; see
[
26
,
11
]
. For the Lévy distance, we have the following statement, by which this quantity becomes accessible via Cauchy transforms and which remarkably works without any constraints.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\mu,\nu\in\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R})
. Then, for any choice of
\varepsilon>0
, we have the bound

(2.1)

L(\mu,\nu)\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon}{\pi}}+\frac{1}{\pi}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\big|\operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{G}_{\mu}(t+i\varepsilon))-\operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{G}_{\nu}(t+i\varepsilon))\big|\,dt.

While this result seems to be well-known, it is surprisingly challenging to localize it in the literature. The reason is that bounds for
L(\mu,\nu)
are mostly given in terms of the characteristic functions of
\mu
and
\nu
rather than in terms of their Cauchy transforms; see
[
17
, Section 3]
. A slightly weaker version of Theorem
2.4
, namely with an additional factor
\sqrt{2}
in front of the term
2\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon}{\pi}}
, was obtained recently in
[
47
]
. A direct proof of Theorem
2.4
is given in the appendix of
[
12
]
. For convenience of the readers, we include here another derivation based on the so-called
smoothing inequality

(2.2)

L(\mu,\nu)\leq L(\mu\ast\gamma,\nu\ast\gamma)+\max\{2\delta,1-\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}(\delta)+\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}(-\delta)\}

holding for arbitrary
\mu,\nu,\gamma\in\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R})
and each
\delta>0
; a proof of (
2.2
) can be found in
[
65
]
.

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

For
\mu\in\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R})
and
\varepsilon>0
, we define
\mu_{\varepsilon}\in\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R})
by
d\mu_{\varepsilon}(t)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{G}_{\mu}(t+i\varepsilon))\,dt
. Note that if we let
\gamma_{\varepsilon}\in\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R})
be given by
d\gamma_{\varepsilon}(t):=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^{2}+t^{2}}\,dt
, then
\mu_{\varepsilon}=\mu\ast\gamma_{\varepsilon}
for each
\varepsilon>0
. Now, since we have

\mathcal{F}_{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}(-\delta)=\gamma_{\varepsilon}((-\infty,-\delta])=\frac{1}{\pi}\int^{-\delta}_{-\infty}\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon^{2}+t^{2}}\,dt\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\pi\delta}

and similarly
1-\mathcal{F}_{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}(\delta)=\gamma_{\varepsilon}((\delta,\infty))\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\pi\delta}
, the smoothing inequality (
2.2
) yields
L(\mu,\nu)\leq L(\mu_{\varepsilon},\nu_{\varepsilon})+2\max\{\delta,\frac{\varepsilon}{\pi\delta}\}
. By choosing
\delta=\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon}{\pi}}
, we obtain that
L(\mu,\nu)\leq L(\mu_{\varepsilon},\nu_{\varepsilon})+2\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon}{\pi}}
. In combination with the fact that

L(\mu_{\varepsilon},\nu_{\varepsilon})\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\big|\operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{G}_{\mu}(t+i\varepsilon))-\operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{G}_{\nu}(t+i\varepsilon))\big|\,dt,

which is easily verified by straightforward estimates, this yields the asserted bound (
2.1
).
∎

3.
The Dyson equation and density of states

Throughout the following, let
B
be a unital
C^{\ast}
-algebra; the unit of
B
will be denoted by
\mathbf{1}
. We define
\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
and
\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
to be the upper and lower half-plane in
B
, respectively, that is,
\mathbb{H}^{\pm}(B):=\{b\in B\mid\exists\varepsilon>0:\pm\operatorname{Im}(b)\geq\varepsilon\mathbf{1}\}
where
\operatorname{Im}(b):=\frac{1}{2i}(b-b^{\ast})
.
Notice that
b\in B
belongs to
\mathbb{H}^{\pm}(B)
if and only if
\pm\operatorname{Im}(b)
is an invertible positive element in
B
; recall that
b\geq\|b^{-1}\|^{-1}\mathbf{1}
for every positive
b\in B
which is invertible.
Further, for every
\gamma>0
, we define
\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B):=\{b+i\gamma\mathbf{1}\mid b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)\}
. Notice that
\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}=\{b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)\mid\operatorname{Im}(b)\geq\gamma\mathbf{1}\}
.

3.1.
The Dyson equation

This subsection is devoted to the first step in the construction of the mapping
\mu
that we outlined in the introduction. It consists in showing that there exists for every
\eta\in\mathcal{P}(B)
a holomorphic function
G_{\eta}:\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)\to\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
uniquely determined among all functions defined on
\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
and taking values in
\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
by the Dyson equation (
1.2
); note that in the sequel, we shall refer to (
3.1
) instead of (
1.2
). The next theorem, a von Neumann algebra version of which was used also in
[
3
]
, gives the precise statement.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\eta:B\to B
be a positive linear map. Then there exists a unique function
G_{\eta}:\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)\to\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
solving the
Dyson equation

(3.1)

bG_{\eta}(b)=\mathbf{1}+\eta(G_{\eta}(b))G_{\eta}(b)\qquad\text{for all $b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)$}.

This function
G_{\eta}
is holomorphic and enjoys the property that

(3.2)

\|G_{\eta}(b)\|\leq\|\operatorname{Im}(b)^{-1}\|\qquad\text{for all $b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)$}.

Essentially, this result is proven in
[
31
]
, except that the authors have not discussed that the solution of the Dyson equation is holomorphic, because this observation was irrelevant for their purpose. Here, we supplement that argument. To this end, we first recall another result from
[
31
]
and we take a closer look at its proof.

Theorem 3.2

(Proposition 3.2,
[
31
]
)
.

Let
\eta:B\to B
be a positive linear map. For every fixed
b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
, the equation

(3.3)

bw=\mathbf{1}+\eta(w)w

has a unique solution
w\in\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
; this
w
is the limit of the sequence
(h_{b}^{\circ n}(w_{0}))_{n=1}^{\infty}
of iterates of the holomorphic function

h_{b}:\ \mathbb{H}^{-}(B)\to\mathbb{H}^{-}(B),\quad w\mapsto(b-\eta(w))^{-1}

for every choice of an initial point
w_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
. Moreover, we have that
\|w\|\leq\|\operatorname{Im}(b)^{-1}\|
.

Notice that our formulation of Theorem
3.2
differs from the original one in
[
31
]
as we prefer to perform the iteration in
\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
instead of the right half-plane of
B
, which would be
\{b\in B\mid\exists\varepsilon>0:\operatorname{Re}(b)\geq\varepsilon\mathbf{1}\}
, where
\operatorname{Re}(b):=\frac{1}{2}(b+b^{\ast})
.

The proof of Theorem
3.2
given in
[
31
]
crucially relies on the Earle-Hamilton fixed point theorem
[
23
]
, an important result about fixed points of holomorphic functions between subsets of complex Banach spaces; we refer the readers who are interested in this topic also to the excellent exposition given in
[
29
]
. For our purpose, it suffices to recap what the authors of
[
31
]
have proved (in the translation to our setting) in order to apply the Earle-Hamilton fixed point theorem: not only have they shown that
h_{b}(w)=(b-\eta(w))^{-1}
defines a holomorphic function
h_{b}:\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)\to\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
for every
b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
, but they also proved that each
h_{b}
is uniformly bounded on
\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
with
\sup_{w\in\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)}\|h_{b}(w)\|\leq\|\operatorname{Im}(b)^{-1}\|
.
With these insights, we are ready to return to Theorem
3.1
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

By assigning to
b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
the unique solution
G_{\eta}(b):=w\in\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
of (
3.3
), we obtain according to Theorem
3.2
a unique function
G_{\eta}:\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)\to\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
which solves the Dyson equation (
3.1
) and we infer that
G_{\eta}
satisfies in addition the bound (
3.2
). It remains to prove that
G_{\eta}
is holomorphic.

To this end, we fix any point
w_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
and use it to define a sequence
(G_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of functions
G_{n}:\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)\to\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
by
G_{n}(b):=h_{b}^{\circ n}(w_{0})
for all
b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
; inductively, one easily sees that each
G_{n}
is Gâteaux holomorphic. Theorem
3.2
tells us that the sequence
(G_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges pointwise on
\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
to
G_{\eta}
. Therefore, Lemma
2.2
will imply that
G_{\eta}
is holomorphic once we have verified that
(G_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is locally uniformly bounded.

This can be done as follows. Thanks to the uniform boundedness of
h_{b}
, we have
\|G_{n}(b)\|=\|h_{b}^{\circ n}(w_{0})\|\leq\|\operatorname{Im}(b)^{-1}\|
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and each
b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
. This shows that the sequence
(G_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is uniformly bounded on
\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}
for every
\gamma>0
and hence locally uniformly bounded on
\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
since we have
\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)=\bigcup_{\gamma>0}\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}
.
∎

Since holomorphy entails Fréchet differentiability, we have the Fréchet derivative
DG_{\eta}:\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)\to\mathcal{L}(B)
of
G_{\eta}
at our disposal. By definition, it satisfies for every
b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\|h\|}\|G_{\eta}(b+h)-G_{\eta}(b)-(DG_{\eta})(b)h\|=0.

For later use, we record with the following lemma some strengthening of the latter condition.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\eta:B\to B
be a positive linear map and let
G_{\eta}:\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)\to\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
be the function uniquely determined by the Dyson equation (
3.1
) according to Theorem
3.1
. Then the Fréchet derivative
DG_{\eta}:\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)\to\mathcal{L}(B)
of
G_{\eta}
satisfies for each
\gamma>0

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\|h\|}\sup_{b\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}}\|G_{\eta}(b+h)-G_{\eta}(b)-(DG_{\eta})(b)h\|=0.

Before giving the proof of Lemma
3.3
, we first introduce some notation. For
b_{0}\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
and
\sigma\in(0,1)
, we set
\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma):=\{b\in B\mid\|b-b_{0}\|<\sigma\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{0})^{-1}\|^{-1}\}
. Notice that

(3.4)

\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma)\subset\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)\qquad\text{for}\qquad\gamma:=(1-\sigma)\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{0})^{-1}\|^{-1}.

Indeed, if any
b\in\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma)
is given, we may check for every state
\phi
on
B
that

\phi(\operatorname{Im}(b))=\phi(\operatorname{Im}(b_{0}))+\phi(\operatorname{Im}(b)-\operatorname{Im}(b_{0}))\\
\geq\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{0})^{-1}\|^{-1}-\|b-b_{0}\|>(1-\sigma)\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{0})^{-1}\|^{-1}=\gamma,

where we used that
\operatorname{Im}(b_{0})\geq\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{0})^{-1}\|^{-1}\mathbf{1}
; this shows
b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)
, as claimed.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Let
\gamma>0
be given and choose
\sigma\in(0,1)
. Take any
b_{0}\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}
. Since
\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{0})^{-1}\|^{-1}\geq\gamma
, we get from (
3.4
) that
\mathbb{B}_{B}(b_{0},\sigma\gamma)\subseteq\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma)\subset\mathbb{H}^{+}_{(1-\sigma)\gamma}(B)
, and (
3.2
) yields
\sup_{b\in\overline{\mathbb{B}_{B}(b_{0},\sigma\gamma)}}\|G_{\eta}(b)\|\leq\frac{1}{(1-\sigma)\gamma}
. Using this fact, we deduce from Lemma
2.1

(ii)
that

\|G_{\eta}(b_{0}+h)-G_{\eta}(b_{0})-(DG_{\eta})(b_{0})h\|\leq\frac{1}{(1-\sigma)\sigma\gamma^{2}(\sigma\gamma-\|h\|)}\|h\|^{2}

for each
h\in B
which satisfies
\|h\|<\sigma\gamma
. Consequently, as
b_{0}\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}
was arbitrary,

\frac{1}{\|h\|}\sup_{b_{0}\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}}\|G_{\eta}(b_{0}+h)-G_{\eta}(b_{0})-(DG_{\eta})(b_{0})h\|\leq\frac{1}{(1-\sigma)\sigma\gamma^{2}(\sigma\gamma-\|h\|)}\|h\|

whenever
h\in B
satisfies
0<\|h\|<\sigma\gamma
. This yields the assertion.
∎

3.2.
Density of states

Based on the results about the Dyson equation obtained in the previous section, we finally can construct the map
\mu
which we announced in the introduction.

We suppose that
(B,\phi)
is a
C^{\ast}
-probability space, i.e., a
C^{\ast}
-algebra
B
with unit
\mathbf{1}
which is endowed with a distinguished state
\phi
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let any data pair
\rho=(b_{0},\eta)\in B_{\mathrm{sa}}\times\mathcal{P}(B)
be given. Consider the holomorphic function
G_{\eta}:\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)\to\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
associated with
\eta
by Theorem
3.1
. Then
\mathcal{G}_{\rho}(z):=\phi(G_{\eta}(z\mathbf{1}-b_{0}))
for
z\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
defines a holomorphic function
\mathcal{G}_{\rho}:\mathbb{C}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}^{-}
with the property
\lim_{y\to\infty}iy\mathcal{G}_{\rho}(iy)=1
and is thus, by Theorem
2.3
, the Cauchy transform of a Borel probability measure
\mu_{\rho}
on the real line
\mathbb{R}
; more explicitly, we have that

\phi(G_{\eta}(z\mathbf{1}-b_{0}))=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{z-t}\,d\mu_{\rho}(t)\qquad\text{for all $z\in\mathbb{C}^{+}$},

which determines
\mu_{\rho}
uniquely. We call
\mu_{\rho}
the
density of states
associated with the data pair
\rho
. In this way, we obtain a mapping

\mu:\ B_{\mathrm{sa}}\times\mathcal{P}(B)\to\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R}),\quad\rho\mapsto\mu_{\rho}.

Notice that
\lim_{y\to\infty}iy\mathcal{G}_{\rho}(iy)=1
follows easily from (
3.1
) with the help of (
3.2
).

Definition
3.4
extends
[
3
, Definition 2.2]
to the setting of
C^{\ast}
-probability spaces which are not necessarily tracial and removes any additional assumptions on the positive map.

We emphasize that
\mu_{\rho}
, despite its name, may very well possess an atomic part and hence can fail to have a density with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
. To ensure that
\mu_{\rho}
enjoys regularity properties of this kind, one needs to impose further conditions on
\rho
; see, for instance,
[
3
,
39
]
.

3.3.
Matricial amplifications of the Dyson equation

Theorem
3.1
characterizes
G_{\eta}
as the unique function on
\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
with values in
\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
which solves the Dyson equation (
3.1
). In order to further analyze
G_{\eta}
, it is helpful to take also matricial amplifications
\eta^{(n)}
of
\eta
into consideration, especially for
n=2
. For this purpose, we need a better understanding of the structure of
\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{2}(B))
; this goal is achieved by the next lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
and
w\in B
be given. Then
b:=\begin{bmatrix}b_{1}&w\\
0&b_{2}\end{bmatrix}\in M_{2}(B)
belongs to
\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{2}(B))
if and only if

(3.5)

\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})^{-1/2}w^{\ast}\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})^{-1}w\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})^{-1/2}\|<4.

Consequently, we have the following implications:

(i)

If
\|w\|^{2}<4\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})^{-1}\|^{-1}\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})^{-1}\|^{-1}
, then
b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{2}(B))
.

(ii)

If
b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{2}(B))
, then
\|w\|^{2}<4\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})\|\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})\|
.

The proof of Lemma
3.5
relies substantially on the following general facts.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
B
be a
C^{\ast}
-algebra with unit
\mathbf{1}
. For each
v\in B
, we have that

(i)

\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{1}&v\\
v^{\ast}&\mathbf{1}\end{bmatrix}\in M_{2}(B)
is positive if and only if
\|v\|\leq 1
;

(ii)

\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{1}&v\\
v^{\ast}&\mathbf{1}\end{bmatrix}\in M_{2}(B)
is positive and invertible if and only if
\|v\|<1
.

Proof.

The first equivalence
(i)
is
[
43
, Lemma 3.1 (i)]
; a direct proof of
(ii)
was given in
[
14
, Section 2.1]
. We note that
(ii)
can also be deduced from
(i)
; indeed, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, it gives that

\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{1}&v\\
v^{\ast}&\mathbf{1}\end{bmatrix}\geq\varepsilon\mathbf{1}_{2},

or equivalently

\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{1}&\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}v\\
\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}v^{\ast}&\mathbf{1}\end{bmatrix}\geq 0,

if and only if
\|v\|\leq 1-\varepsilon
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.5
.

The proof of the first assertion is basically given in the discussion at the end of Section 2.1 in
[
14
]
; for the readers’ convenience, we include the argument, adapted to our situation. First of all, we notice that
b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{2}(B))
if and only if the matrix

\operatorname{Im}(b)=\operatorname{Im}\bigg(\begin{bmatrix}b_{1}&w\\
0&b_{2}\end{bmatrix}\bigg)=\begin{bmatrix}\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})&\frac{1}{2i}w\\
-\frac{1}{2i}w^{\ast}&\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})\end{bmatrix}

is positive and invertible. Since
b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
by assumption, both
\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})
and
\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})
are positive and invertible; thus, the operator
\operatorname{Im}(b)
is positive and invertible if and only if

\begin{bmatrix}\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})^{-1/2}&0\\
0&\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})^{-1/2}\end{bmatrix}\operatorname{Im}(b)\begin{bmatrix}\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})^{-1/2}&0\\
0&\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})^{-1/2}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{1}&v\\
v^{\ast}&\mathbf{1}\end{bmatrix},

with
v:=\frac{1}{2i}\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})^{-1/2}w\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})^{-1/2}
, is positive and invertible. With the aid of Lemma
3.6

(ii)
, we infer that
\operatorname{Im}(b)
is positive and invertible if and only if

\frac{1}{4}\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})^{-1/2}w^{\ast}\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})^{-1}w\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})^{-1/2}\|=\|v^{\ast}v\|=\|v\|^{2}<1,

which is the statement we wanted to prove.

Having established that
b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{2}(B))
holds true if and only if condition (
3.5
) is satisfied, we can deduce the additional assertions:

Suppose that
\|w\|^{2}<4\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})^{-1}\|^{-1}\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})^{-1}\|^{-1}
is satisfied. Since
\operatorname{Im}(b_{i})^{-1}\leq\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{i})^{-1}\|\mathbf{1}
for
i=1,2
, we get in this case that

\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})^{-1/2}w^{\ast}\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})^{-1}w\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})^{-1/2}\|\leq\|w\|^{2}\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})^{-1}\|\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})^{-1}\|<4,

Thus, (
3.5
) is satisfied and hence we have
b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{2}(B))
; this proves
(i)
.

In order to prove
(ii)
, we suppose that
b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{2}(B))
. First, we notice that
\|w\|\leq\|w\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})^{-1/2}\|\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})\|^{1/2}
since
\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})^{1/2}\|=\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})\|^{1/2}
by the
C^{\ast}
-identity. Then, we use
\mathbf{1}\leq\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})^{-1}
to infer from the latter that

\displaystyle\|w\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|w\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})^{-1/2}\|^{2}\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})\|

\displaystyle=\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})^{-1/2}w^{\ast}w\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})^{-1/2}\|\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})\|\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})\|\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})^{-1/2}w^{\ast}\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})^{-1}w\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})^{-1/2}\|.

Involving (
3.5
), which holds since we have
b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{2}(B))
by assumption, we conclude that
\|w\|^{2}<4\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})\|\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{2})\|
, as desired.
∎

The next proposition provides a strengthening of Theorem
3.1
for
n
-positive maps
\eta:B\to B
. In such cases, the Dyson equation uniquely determines a noncommutative function of height
n
, in the terminology that we explain in the Appendix
A
; see also Proposition
A.2
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\eta:B\to B
be an
n
-positive linear map. Consider the noncommutative sets of height
n
which are given by
\mathbb{H}^{\pm}(M_{\leq n}(B)):=\coprod_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{H}^{\pm}(M_{k}(B))
. Then there exists a unique noncommutative function
G^{(\leq n)}_{\eta}:\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{\leq n}(B))\to\mathbb{H}^{-}(M_{\leq n}(B))
of height
n
such that, for each
k=1,\dots,n
, the
k
-th level function
G_{\eta}^{(k)}:\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{k}(B))\to\mathbb{H}^{-}(M_{k}(B))
satisfies

(3.6)

bG_{\eta}^{(k)}(b)=\mathbf{1}_{k}+\eta^{(k)}(G_{\eta}^{(k)}(b))G_{\eta}^{(k)}(b)\qquad\text{for all $b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{k}(B))$}.

In particular, if
n\geq 2
, then for every
b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
and each
w\in B
satisfying
\begin{bmatrix}b&w\\
0&b\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{2}(B))
, we have that

(3.7)

G^{(2)}_{\eta}\bigg(\begin{bmatrix}b&w\\
0&b\end{bmatrix}\bigg)=\begin{bmatrix}G_{\eta}(b)&(DG_{\eta})(b)w\\
0&G_{\eta}(b)\end{bmatrix}.

Proof.

For
k=1,\dots,n
, because
\eta^{(k)}:M_{k}(B)\to M_{k}(B)
is positive, Theorem
3.1
guarantees the existence of a unique function
G_{\eta}^{(k)}:\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{k}(B))\to\mathbb{H}^{-}(M_{k}(B))
satisfying (
3.6
); in fact, we have that
G_{\eta}^{(k)}=G_{\eta^{(k)}}
. Thus, in order to establish the existence of a unique noncommutative function
G^{(\leq n)}_{\eta}:\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{\leq n}(B))\to\mathbb{H}^{-}(M_{\leq n}(B))
of height
n
with the properties required in the proposition, it suffices to show that the aforementioned functions
G_{\eta}^{(k)}
combine to a noncommutative function of height
n
. To this end, we check the following:

Whenever
1\leq k,l\leq n
are such that
k+l\leq n
and if
b_{1}\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{k}(B))
and
b_{2}\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{l}(B))
, then both
G_{\eta}^{(k+l)}(b_{1}\oplus b_{2})
and
G_{\eta}^{(k)}(b_{1})\oplus G_{\eta}^{(l)}(b_{2})
are in
\mathbb{H}^{-}(M_{k+l}(B))
and solve (
3.3
) for
\eta^{(k+l)}\in\mathcal{P}(M_{k+l}(B))
at
b_{1}\oplus b_{2}\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{k+l}(B))
. By uniqueness,
G_{\eta}^{(k+l)}(b_{1}\oplus b_{2})=G_{\eta}^{(k)}(b_{1})\oplus G_{\eta}^{(l)}(b_{2})
.

For
1\leq k\leq n
, if
b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{k}(B))
and an invertible matrix
S\in M_{k}(\mathbb{C})
are given such that
SbS^{-1}\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{k}(B))
holds, then Theorem
3.2
tells us that

G^{(k)}_{\eta}(b)=\lim_{n\to\infty}h^{\circ n}_{b}(-i\mathbf{1}_{k})\qquad\text{and}\qquad G^{(k)}_{\eta}(SbS^{-1})=\lim_{n\to\infty}h^{\circ n}_{SbS^{-1}}(-i\mathbf{1}_{k}).

Notice that the functions
h_{b}
and
h_{SbS^{-1}}
are defined like in Theorem
3.2
, but with respect to
\eta^{(k)}
instead of
\eta
; both of them map
\mathbb{H}^{-}(M_{k}(B))
into itself. Since for all
w\in\mathbb{H}^{-}(M_{k}(B))

h_{SbS^{-1}}(w)=\big(SbS^{-1}-\eta^{(k)}(w)\big)^{-1}=S\big(b-S^{-1}\eta^{(k)}(w)S\big)^{-1}S^{-1}\\
=S\big(b-\eta^{(k)}(S^{-1}wS)\big)^{-1}S^{-1}=Sh_{b}(S^{-1}wS)S^{-1},

we see that
h^{\circ n}_{SbS^{-1}}(-i\mathbf{1}_{k})=S^{-1}h_{b}^{\circ n}(-i\mathbf{1}_{k})S
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and so
G_{\eta}^{(k)}(SbS^{-1})=SG_{\eta}^{(k)}(b)S^{-1}
.

The asserted formula (
3.7
) follows immediately from Theorem
A.1
.
∎

3.4.
Properties of solutions of the Dyson equation for
2
-positive maps

With the aid of Proposition
3.7
, we now derive bounds for the Fréchet derivative of
G_{\eta}
for
\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)
, which are in the spirit of
[
14
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Suppose that
\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)
. Then, for every
b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
,

(3.8)

\|(DG_{\eta})(b)\|=\sup_{w\in B\colon\|w\|\leq 1}\|(DG_{\eta})(b)w\|\leq\|\operatorname{Im}(b)^{-1}\|^{2},

and moreover, if
\phi
is any state on
B
, then

(3.9)

\sup_{w\in B\colon\|w\|\leq 1}\big|\phi((DG_{\eta})(b)w)\big|\leq-\operatorname{Im}(\phi(G_{\eta}(b)))\|\operatorname{Im}(b)^{-1}\|.

Proof.

We take
b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
and
w\in B
such that the condition
\|w\|\leq 2(1-\varepsilon)\|\operatorname{Im}(b)^{-1}\|^{-1}
is satisfied for some
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. In this situation, Lemma
3.5

(i)
tells us that

\begin{bmatrix}b&w\\
0&b\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{2}(B)).

Since
\eta
is supposed to be
2
-positive, we may apply Proposition
3.7
; the formula (
3.7
) yields

(3.10)

G_{\eta}^{(2)}\bigg(\begin{bmatrix}b&w\\
0&b\end{bmatrix}\bigg)=\begin{bmatrix}G_{\eta}(b)&(DG_{\eta})(b)w\\
0&G_{\eta}(b)\end{bmatrix}.

Since
G_{\eta}^{(2)}
takes its values in
\mathbb{H}^{-}(M_{2}(B))
, we conclude from the latter with the help of Lemma
3.5

(ii)
that
\|(DG_{\eta})(b)w\|<2\|\operatorname{Im}(G_{\eta}(b))\|
. By rescaling and using (
3.2
), we infer that every
w\in B
with
\|w\|\leq 1
satisfies

\|(DG_{\eta})(b)w\|<\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\|\operatorname{Im}(G_{\eta}(b))\|\|\operatorname{Im}(b)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\|\operatorname{Im}(b)^{-1}\|^{2}.

By letting
\varepsilon\searrow 0
, we obtain (
3.8
), the first of the asserted formulas.

In order to prove (
3.9
), we proceed as follows. First of all, we deduce from (
3.10
) that

\begin{bmatrix}\operatorname{Im}(G_{\eta}(b))&\frac{1}{2i}\big((DG_{\eta})(b)w\big)\\
-\frac{1}{2i}\big((DG_{\eta})(b)w\big)^{\ast}&\operatorname{Im}(G_{\eta}(b))\end{bmatrix}=\operatorname{Im}\bigg(\begin{bmatrix}G_{\eta}(b)&(DG_{\eta})(b)w\\
0&G_{\eta}(b)\end{bmatrix}\bigg)\leq 0.

By applying the amplification
\phi^{(2)}
of
\phi
to the latter, we conclude that the scalar matrix

\begin{bmatrix}\phi\big(\operatorname{Im}(G_{\eta}(b))\big)&\frac{1}{2i}\phi\big((DG_{\eta})(b)w\big)\\
-\frac{1}{2i}\overline{\phi\big((DG_{\eta})(b)w\big)}&\phi\big(\operatorname{Im}(G_{\eta}(b))\big)\end{bmatrix}

is negative semidefinite; hence, we get that
\frac{1}{2}|\phi((DG_{\eta})(b)w)|\leq-\phi(\operatorname{Im}(G_{\eta}(b)))
. By rescaling, we infer from the latter that if
w\in B
is such that
\|w\|\leq 1
, then

\big|\phi\big((DG_{\eta})(b)w\big)\big|\leq-\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\phi(\operatorname{Im}(G_{\eta}(b)))\|\operatorname{Im}(b)^{-1}\|.

By letting
\varepsilon\searrow 0
, we get (
3.9
), the second of the asserted formulas.
∎

The bound (
3.8
) can be used to derive, for every
\gamma>0
, Lipschitz continuity of
G_{\eta}
on
\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}
with Lipschitz constant
\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}
. We prove a more general result; cf.
[
14
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Suppose that
\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)
. Then, for all
b_{0},b_{1}\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
, we have that

(3.11)

\|G_{\eta}(b_{1})-G_{\eta}(b_{0})\|\leq\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{0})^{-1}\|\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})^{-1}\|\|b_{1}-b_{0}\|.

Proof.

Fix
b_{0},b_{1}\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
. We consider the function
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
which is defined by
f(t):=G_{\eta}(tb_{1}+(1-t)b_{0})
for
t\in[0,1]
. Then
f
is differentiable as a
B
-valued function with
f^{\prime}(t)=(DG_{\eta})(tb_{1}+(1-t)b_{0})(b_{1}-b_{0})
. By using the bound (
3.8
) provided by Lemma
3.8
, we observe that

\|f^{\prime}(t)\|\leq\big\|\big(t\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})+(1-t)\operatorname{Im}(b_{0})\big)^{-1}\big\|^{2}\|b_{1}-b_{0}\|.

Since
t\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})+(1-t)\operatorname{Im}(b_{0})\geq(t\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})^{-1}\|^{-1}+(1-t)\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{0})^{-1}\|^{-1})\,\mathbf{1}
, we have

\big\|\big(t\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})+(1-t)\operatorname{Im}(b_{0})\big)^{-1}\big\|\leq\big(t\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})^{-1}\|^{-1}+(1-t)\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{0})^{-1}\|^{-1}\big)^{-1}.

By using
\int^{1}_{0}(\gamma_{1}t+\gamma_{0}(1-t))^{-2}\,dt=(\gamma_{0}\gamma_{1})^{-1}
for every
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}>0
, we finally obtain that

\|G_{\eta}(b_{1})-G_{\eta}(b_{0})\|\leq\int^{1}_{0}\|f^{\prime}(t)\|\,dt\leq\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{0})^{-1}\|\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{1})^{-1}\|\|b_{1}-b_{0}\|,

which is the asserted bound (
3.11
).
∎

Let us point out that an alternative proof of (
3.11
) can be given by evaluating
G^{(2)}_{\eta}
at the point
\begin{bmatrix}b_{0}&r(b_{1}-b_{0})\\
0&b_{1}\end{bmatrix}
for sufficiently small
r>0
(using the identity (
A.3
) together with the fact that
G^{(\leq 2)}_{\eta}
is a noncommutative function of height
2
by Proposition
3.7
) and examining the result with Lemma
3.5
in analogy to the proof of Lemma
3.8
; see also Remark
A.3
.

From Lemma
3.9
, we can derive Lipschitz continuity of
DG_{\eta}
on
\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}
for every
\gamma>0
.

Lemma 3.10
.

There is a universal constant
c>0
such that, for all
\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)
and
\gamma>0
,

(3.12)

\|(DG_{\eta})(b_{1})-(DG_{\eta})(b_{0})\|\leq\frac{c}{\gamma^{3}}\,\|b_{1}-b_{0}\|\qquad\text{for all $b_{0},b_{1}\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}$}.

Proof.

Choose
\sigma\in(0,1)
. Like in the proof of Lemma
3.3
, we infer from (
3.4
) that
\mathbb{B}_{B}(b,\sigma\gamma)\subseteq\mathbb{D}(b,\sigma)\subset\mathbb{H}^{+}_{(1-\sigma)\gamma}(B)
for every
b\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}
. Thus,
f(b):=G_{\eta}(b_{1}+b)-G_{\eta}(b_{0}+b)
yields a well-defined holomorphic function
f:\mathbb{B}_{B}(0,\sigma\gamma)\to B
satisfying
(Df)(0)=(DG_{\eta})(b_{1})-(DG_{\eta})(b_{0})
and moreover
M_{f}(0,\sigma\gamma)\leq\frac{1}{(1-\sigma)^{2}\gamma^{2}}\|b_{1}-b_{0}\|
according (
3.11
). With the help of the Cauchy estimate stated in Lemma
2.1

(i)
, we derive that
\|(DG_{\eta})(b_{1})-(DG_{\eta})(b_{0})\|\leq\frac{1}{\sigma(1-\sigma)^{2}}\frac{1}{\gamma^{3}}\|b_{1}-b_{0}\|
, from which (
3.12
) follows with
c=\min_{\sigma\in(0,1)}\frac{1}{\sigma(1-\sigma)^{2}}=\frac{27}{4}
.
∎

4.
The operator-valued inviscid Burgers equation for paths in
\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)

This section is about an operator-valued version of the inviscid Burgers equation for paths in
\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)
.
In the scalar-valued framework, the fundamental insight that the inviscid Burgers equation constitutes the free probability counterpart of the heat equation is due to Voiculescu; see
[
58
]
, especially the comments following the proof of Theorem 4.3 therein. In
[
5
, Theorem 8.3]
, Anshelevich, Belinschi, Fevrier, and Nica generalized the aforementioned result of Voiculescu by an operator-valued version of the inviscid Burgers equation. On the level of formal series, a variant thereof has already appeared in
[
60
, Proposition 4.5]
.

In the literature on noncommutative probability and on free probability in particular, there are numerous other places where such a dynamical perspective turned out to be extremely successful; we refer the interested readers for instance to
[
16
,
13
,
19
,
35
,
6
,
48
,
24
,
36
,
37
]
and the references listed therein.

With Theorem
1.3
, we generalize
[
5
, Theorem 8.3]
from invertible completely positive maps to arbitrary
2
-positive maps, to the relatively small price that we are limited to initial conditions which are themselves solutions of some Dyson equation; this, however, is entirely sufficient for the intended application of Theorem
1.3
in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem
1.3
builds upon Theorem
1.2
, the proof of which will also be given in this section. Both proofs will make use of the following fact, which we borrow from
[
34
]
; because it is stated there in slightly different form and under the much more restrictive assumption of complete positivity instead of
2
-positivity, we include here also the proof.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
B
be a unital
C^{\ast}
-algebra and let
\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)
be given. Define holomorphic functions
\Psi_{\eta}:\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)\to B
by
\Psi_{\eta}(w):=w^{-1}+\eta(w)
and
\Delta_{b,\eta}:\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)\to B
by
\Delta_{b,\eta}(w):=b-\Psi_{\eta}(w)
for some fixed
b\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
. Furthermore, we set

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}_{b,\eta}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{w\in\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)\mid\|\Delta_{b,\eta}(w)\|<\|\operatorname{Im}(b)^{-1}\|^{-1}\}\qquad

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}^{\sigma}_{b,\eta}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{w\in\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)\mid\|\Delta_{b,\eta}(w)\|\leq\sigma\|\operatorname{Im}(b)^{-1}\|^{-1}\}\qquad

for
\sigma\in(0,1)
.

Then the following statements hold true:

(i)

\Psi_{\eta}
restricts to a holomorphic function
\Psi_{\eta}:\mathbb{U}_{b,\eta}\to\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
which satisfies
G_{\eta}(\Psi_{\eta}(w))=w
for all
w\in\mathbb{U}_{b,\eta}
. Further, for every
\sigma\in(0,1)
, we have that

\Psi_{\eta}(\mathbb{U}^{\sigma}_{b,\eta})\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{D}(b,\sigma)}\subset\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}\qquad\text{for}\qquad\gamma:=(1-\sigma)\|\operatorname{Im}(b)^{-1}\|^{-1}.

(ii)

Put
w_{0}:=G_{\eta}(b)\in\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
. Then, for every
\sigma\in(0,1)
and each
w\in\mathbb{U}^{\sigma}_{b,\eta}
, we have

(4.1)

\|w-w_{0}\|\leq\frac{1}{1-\sigma}\|\operatorname{Im}(b)^{-1}\|^{2}\|\Delta_{b,\eta}(w)\|\leq\frac{\sigma}{1-\sigma}\|\operatorname{Im}(b)^{-1}\|.

Note that
\mathbb{U}_{b,\eta}
is an open set containing
w_{0}
, which is the unique solution of the equation (
3.3
) in
\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
; indeed, we have
\Delta_{b,\eta}(w_{0})=0
so that in fact
w_{0}\in\mathbb{U}^{\sigma}_{b,\eta}
for every
\sigma\in(0,1)
. Accordingly, we regard points in
\mathbb{U}_{b,\eta}^{\sigma}
as approximate solutions of (
3.3
), which by (
4.1
) are getting closer to the accurate solution
w_{0}
when
\sigma
tends to
0
.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

(i) It is obvious that
\Psi_{\eta}
and hence
\Delta_{b,\eta}
define
B
-valued holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
. Take any
\sigma\in(0,1)
. Notice that
\|\Psi_{\eta}(w)-b\|=\|\Delta_{b,\eta}(w)\|\leq\sigma\|\operatorname{Im}(b)^{-1}\|^{-1}
and hence
\Psi_{\eta}(w)\in\overline{\mathbb{D}(b,\sigma)}
for
w\in\mathbb{U}^{\sigma}_{b,\eta}
; this proves that
\Psi_{\eta}(\mathbb{U}^{\sigma}_{b,\eta})\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{D}(b,\sigma)}\subset\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}
for
\gamma=(1-\sigma)\|\operatorname{Im}(b)^{-1}\|^{-1}
, where the second inclusion comes from (
3.4
). Because
\mathbb{U}_{b,\eta}=\bigcup_{\sigma\in(0,1)}\mathbb{U}^{\sigma}_{b,\eta}
, it follows that the restriction of
\Psi_{\eta}
to
\mathbb{U}_{b,\eta}
takes its values in
\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
.

Next, we verify that
G_{\eta}(\Psi_{\eta}(w))=w
for all
w\in\mathbb{U}_{b,\eta}
. To this end, we take any
w\in\mathbb{U}_{b,\eta}
. First, we observe that
\Psi_{\eta}(w)w=\mathbf{1}+\eta(w)w
. This tells us that
w
is the unique solution of (
3.3
) in
\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
at the point
\Psi_{\eta}(w)\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
, which implies
G_{\eta}(\Psi_{\eta}(w))=w
, as we wished to show.

(ii) Take any
w\in\mathbb{U}^{\sigma}_{b,\eta}
. From
(i)
, we know
\Psi_{\eta}(w)\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}
for
\gamma=(1-\sigma)\|\operatorname{Im}(b)^{-1}\|^{-1}
; consequently,
\|\operatorname{Im}(\Psi_{\eta}(w))^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1}{1-\sigma}\|\operatorname{Im}(b)^{-1}\|
.
With the help of the bound (
3.11
), we get

\|w-w_{0}\|=\|G_{\eta}(\Psi_{\eta}(w))-G_{\eta}(b)\|\leq\|\operatorname{Im}(\Psi_{\eta}(w))^{-1}\|\|\operatorname{Im}(b)^{-1}\|\|\Delta_{b,\eta}(w)\|\\
\leq\frac{1}{1-\sigma}\|\operatorname{Im}(b)^{-1}\|^{2}\|\Delta_{b,\eta}(w)\|\leq\frac{\sigma}{1-\sigma}\|\operatorname{Im}(b)^{-1}\|

for every
w\in\mathbb{U}^{\sigma}_{b,\eta}
, which are the bounds asserted in (
4.1
).
∎

We continue these investigations with the following two propositions which allow a local comparison of solutions
G_{\eta_{0}}
and
G_{\eta_{1}}
of the Dyson equation (

) for
\eta_{0},\eta_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)
.
With the first one, which builds on Proposition

(i)
, we show that
G_{\eta_{0}}
and
G_{\eta_{1}}
are locally subordinated (to each other) whenever
\eta_{0}
and
\eta_{1}
in

are sufficiently close and we control in terms of
\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{0}\|
the deviation of the subordination function from the identity.

Proposition 4.2
.

Fix
\gamma>0
and
0<\sigma^{\prime}<\sigma<1
. Suppose that
\eta_{0},\eta_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)
are such that the condition
\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{0}\|\leq(1-\sigma^{\prime})(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})\gamma^{2}
is satisfied. Then, for each
b_{0}\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}
, we have the inclusion
G_{\eta_{1}}(\overline{\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma^{\prime})})\subseteq\mathbb{U}^{\sigma}_{b_{0},\eta_{0}}
, so that we obtain a well-defined function by

\omega^{b_{0}}_{\eta_{0}\to\eta_{1}}:\ \overline{\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma^{\prime})}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma)},\quad b\mapsto\Psi_{\eta_{0}}(G_{\eta_{1}}(b)),

which is holomorphic on
\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma^{\prime})
and furthermore has the property that
G_{\eta_{0}}(\omega^{b_{0}}_{\eta_{0}\to\eta_{1}}(b))=G_{\eta_{1}}(b)
holds for all
b\in\overline{\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma^{\prime})}
. Moreover, we have that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{b_{0}\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}}\ \sup_{b\in\overline{\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma^{\prime})}}\|\omega^{b_{0}}_{\eta_{0}\to\eta_{1}}(b)-b\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1-\sigma^{\prime})\gamma}\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{0}\|\qquad\text{and}

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{b_{0}\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}}\|(D\omega^{b_{0}}_{\eta_{0}\to\eta_{1}})(b_{0})-\operatorname{id}_{B}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sigma^{\prime}(1-\sigma^{\prime})\gamma^{2}}\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{0}\|.

Before we proceed to the proof of Proposition
4.2
, we collect in the following remark some useful identities to which we will come back in the sequel.

Remark 4.3
.

Let
\eta_{0},\eta_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)
,
\gamma>0
, and
\sigma\in(0,1)
be given. For
b_{0}\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}
and
b\in\overline{\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma)}
, we may compute using (
3.1
) for
G_{\eta_{1}}
that

(4.4)

\Delta_{b_{0},\eta_{0}}(G_{\eta_{1}}(b))=b_{0}-G_{\eta_{1}}(b)^{-1}-\eta_{0}(G_{\eta_{1}}(b))=b_{0}-b+(\eta_{1}-\eta_{0})(G_{\eta_{1}}(b))

and in particular
\Delta_{b_{0},\eta_{0}}(G_{\eta_{1}}(b_{0}))=(\eta_{1}-\eta_{0})(G_{\eta_{1}}(b_{0}))
. From (
3.4
), we know that
\overline{\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma)}\subset\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{(1-\sigma)\gamma}(B)}
; thanks to (
3.2
), we thus have that
\|G_{\eta_{1}}(b)\|\leq\frac{1}{(1-\sigma)\gamma}
. It follows from (
4.4
) that

(4.5)

\|\Delta_{b_{0},\eta_{0}}(G_{\eta_{1}}(b))\|\leq\|b-b_{0}\|+\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{0}\|\|G_{\eta_{1}}(b)\|\leq\sigma\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{0})^{-1}\|^{-1}+\frac{1}{(1-\sigma)\gamma}\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{0}\|

and in particular
\|\Delta_{b_{0},\eta_{0}}(G_{\eta_{1}}(b_{0}))\|\leq\frac{1}{\gamma}\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{0}\|
.

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Suppose that
\eta_{0},\eta_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)
satisfy the condition
\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{0}\|\leq(1-\sigma^{\prime})(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})\gamma^{2}
. We take
b_{0}\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}
and any
b\in\overline{\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma^{\prime})}
. Using that
\gamma\leq\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{0})^{-1}\|^{-1}
, we obtain with the aid of the bound (
4.5
) from Remark
4.3
that

\|\Delta_{b_{0},\eta_{0}}(G_{\eta_{1}}(b))\|\leq\sigma^{\prime}\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{0})^{-1}\|^{-1}+(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})\gamma\leq\sigma\|\operatorname{Im}(b_{0})^{-1}\|^{-1},

which means that
G_{\eta_{1}}(b)\in\mathbb{U}_{b_{0},\eta_{0}}^{\sigma}
. This verifies the asserted inclusion
G_{\eta_{1}}(\overline{\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma^{\prime})})\subseteq\mathbb{U}^{\sigma}_{b_{0},\eta_{0}}
.

From Proposition
4.1

(i)
, we learn that
\Psi_{\eta_{0}}(\mathbb{U}^{\sigma}_{b_{0},\eta_{0}})\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma)}
and
G_{\eta_{0}}(\Psi_{\eta_{0}}(w))=w
for all
w\in\mathbb{U}^{\sigma}_{b_{0},\eta_{0}}
; hence,
\omega^{b_{0}}_{\eta_{0}\to\eta_{1}}:\overline{\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma^{\prime})}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma)}
is well-defined and satisfies
G_{\eta_{0}}(\omega^{b_{0}}_{\eta_{0}\to\eta_{1}}(b))=G_{\eta_{1}}(b)
for all
b\in\overline{\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma^{\prime})}
. The holomorphy of
\Psi_{\eta_{0}}
and
G_{\eta_{1}}
established in Proposition
4.1
respectively in Theorem
3.1
ensures that
\omega^{b_{0}}_{\eta_{0}\to\eta_{1}}
is holomorphic on
\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma^{\prime})
, as asserted.

To prove (
4.2
), we take
b_{0}\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}
and
b\in\overline{\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma^{\prime})}
and check with the help of Remark
4.3
that
b-\omega^{b_{0}}_{\eta_{0}\to\eta_{1}}(b)=\Delta_{b,\eta_{0}}(G_{\eta_{1}}(b))=(\eta_{1}-\eta_{0})(G_{\eta_{1}}(b))
and in turn
\|\omega^{b_{0}}_{\eta_{0}\to\eta_{1}}(b)-b\|\leq\frac{1}{(1-\sigma^{\prime})\gamma}\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{0}\|
. By taking suprema over
b\in\overline{\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma^{\prime})}
and
b_{0}\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}
, we get (
4.2
).

To prove (
4.3
), we proceed as follows. Take an arbitrary
b_{0}\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}
. Because
\mathbb{B}_{B}(b_{0},\sigma^{\prime}\gamma)\subseteq\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma^{\prime})
, we get by

a well-defined holomorphic function. From Lemma
2.1

(i)
, we infer that

\|(D\omega^{b_{0}}_{\eta_{0}\to\eta_{1}})(b_{0})-\operatorname{id}_{B}\|=\|(Df)(b_{0})\|\leq\frac{1}{\sigma^{\prime}\gamma}M_{f}(b_{0},\sigma^{\prime}\gamma)\leq\frac{1}{\sigma^{\prime}\gamma}\sup_{b\in\overline{\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma^{\prime})}}\|\omega^{b_{0}}_{\eta_{0}\to\eta_{1}}(b)-b\|.

By passing to the supremum over all
b_{0}\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}
and employing (
4.2
), we arrive at (
4.3
).
∎

The second proposition exploits Proposition
4.1

(ii)
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Fix
\gamma>0
and
\sigma_{0}\in(0,1)
. Suppose that
\eta_{0},\eta_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)
are such that the condition
\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{0}\|\leq\sigma_{0}\gamma^{2}

\sup_{b\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}}\|G_{\eta_{1}}(b)-G_{\eta_{0}}(b)\|\leq\frac{1}{(1-\sigma_{0})\gamma^{3}}\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{0}\|

and moreover, with the constant

which appeared in Lemma

,

(4.7)

\sup_{b\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}}\|(DG_{\eta_{1}})(b)-(DG_{\eta_{0}})(b)\|\leq\frac{1}{\gamma^{4}}\Bigg[\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<\sigma^{\prime}<\sigma<1\colon\\
\sigma_{0}=(1-\sigma^{\prime})(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})\end{subarray}}\frac{1-\sigma+c\sigma^{\prime}}{\sigma^{\prime}(1-\sigma^{\prime})(1-\sigma)^{3}}\Bigg]\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{0}\|.

Proof.

Let us consider arbitrary
0<\sigma^{\prime}<\sigma<1
such that
\sigma_{0}=(1-\sigma^{\prime})(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})
.

Take any
b_{0}\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}
. Thanks to Proposition
4.2
, we thus have that
G_{\eta_{1}}(\overline{\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma^{\prime})})\subseteq\mathbb{U}^{\sigma}_{b_{0},\eta_{0}}

Further, due to Remark

In combination, we get
\|G_{\eta_{1}}(b_{0})-G_{\eta_{0}}(b_{0})\|\leq\frac{1}{(1-\sigma)\gamma^{3}}\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{0}\|

and letting

Take again any

, we know that
\omega^{b_{0}}_{\eta_{0}\to\eta_{1}}:\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma^{\prime})\to\overline{\mathbb{D}(b_{0},\sigma)}

. This identity allows us to compute the Fréchet derivative of

at the point

. Therefore, we can estimate

(4.8)

\big\|(DG_{\eta_{1}})(b_{0})-(DG_{\eta_{0}})(b_{0})\big\|\leq\big\|(DG_{\eta_{0}})(\omega^{b_{0}}_{\eta_{0}\to\eta_{1}}(b_{0}))\big((D\omega^{b_{0}}_{\eta_{0}\to\eta_{1}})(b_{0})-\operatorname{id}_{B}\big)\big\|\\
+\big\|(DG_{\eta_{0}})(\omega^{b_{0}}_{\eta_{0}\to\eta_{1}}(b_{0}))-(DG_{\eta_{0}})(b_{0})\big\|.

) and (

), respectively, the latter estimates lead us to

\big\|(DG_{\eta_{0}})(\omega^{b_{0}}_{\eta_{0}\to\eta_{1}}(b_{0}))\big((D\omega^{b_{0}}_{\eta_{0}\to\eta_{1}})(b_{0})-\operatorname{id}_{B}\big)\big\|\leq\frac{1}{\sigma^{\prime}(1-\sigma^{\prime})(1-\sigma)^{2}\gamma^{4}}\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{0}\|\qquad\text{and}\\

), passing to the supremum over

, and optimizing over

).

With the help of Proposition

, we can prove Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

First, we notice that the assertions in Item
(i)
and Item
(ii)
concerning the continuity of
G:\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)\times\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)\to\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
and
DG:\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)\times\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)\to\mathcal{L}(B)
immediately follow once the equicontinuity in the respective case is established. The general mechanism behind (albeit not in its most general version but tailored to our situation) is the following: if
f:\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)\times\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)\to F
, for some Banach space
(F,\|\cdot\|)
, is such that

•

for all
\gamma>0
, the family
f(\cdot,b):\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)\to F
with
b\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}
is equicontinuous, and

•

for every fixed
\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)
, the function
f(\eta,\cdot):\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)\to F
is continuous,

then
f
must be continuous. To see this, we take any sequence
((\eta_{n},b_{n}))_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)\times\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
converging to
(\eta,b)\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)\times\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)
. Note that
(b_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
lies in
\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}
for some
\gamma>0
; thus,

\|f(\eta_{n},b_{n})-f(\eta,b)\|\leq\sup_{b^{\prime}\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}}\|f(\eta_{n},b^{\prime})-f(\eta,b^{\prime})\|+\|f(\eta,b_{n})-f(\eta,b)\|

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. With the help of the latter estimate, we easily infer from the assumptions on the separate continuity of
f
that
f(\eta_{n},b_{n})\to f(\eta,b)
as
n\to\infty
. Thus,
f
is continuous.

The statements on the equicontinuity follow immediately from Proposition
4.4
.
∎

\sup_{b_{0}\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}}\Big\|\frac{1}{t-t_{0}}\big(G_{t}(b_{0})-G_{t_{0}}(b_{0})\big)+(DG_{t_{0}})(b_{0})\big(\overset{\,{}_{\mbox{\bf.}}}{\eta}_{t_{0}}(G_{t_{0}}(b_{0}))\big)\Big\|\leq\varepsilon.

Thus, in summary, we see that

\lim_{t\to t_{0}}\sup_{b_{0}\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}}\Big\|\frac{1}{t-t_{0}}\big(G_{t}(b_{0})-G_{t_{0}}(b_{0})\big)+(DG_{t_{0}})(b_{0})\big(\overset{\,{}_{\mbox{\bf.}}}{\eta}_{t_{0}}(G_{t_{0}}(b_{0}))\big)\Big\|=0.

This shows that the family
G_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(b_{0}):[0,T]\to\mathbb{H}^{-}(B)
for
b_{0}\in\overline{\mathbb{H}^{+}_{\gamma}(B)}
is equidifferentiable at
t_{0}
with
\overset{\,{}_{\mbox{\bf.}}}{G}_{t_{0}}(b_{0})=-(DG_{t_{0}})(b_{0})(\overset{\,{}_{\mbox{\bf.}}}{\eta}_{t_{0}}(G_{t_{0}}(b_{0})))
, which establishes at once (
1.5
) and (
1.6
).
∎

5.
Hölder continuity of the density of states

In this section, we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
. For each of the two Hölder bounds stated therein, the proof follows the same strategy based on the next lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(\rho_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be a family of data pairs in
B_{\mathrm{sa}}\times\mathcal{P}(B)
. Suppose that the function
\mathcal{G}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{C}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}^{-},(t,z)\mapsto\mathcal{G}_{t}(z)
defined by
\mathcal{G}_{t}(z):=\mathcal{G}_{\rho_{t}}(z)
for
(t,z)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{C}^{+}
satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

The function
\mathcal{G}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{C}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}^{-}
is continuous with respect to the product topology on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{C}^{+}
.

(ii)

For each
z\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
, the function
\mathcal{G}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(z):[0,T]\to\mathbb{C}^{-},t\mapsto\mathcal{G}_{t}(z)
is continuously differentiable; we denote its derivative by
\overset{\,{}_{\mbox{\bf.}}}{\mathcal{G}}_{\boldsymbol{\cdot}}(z):[0,T]\to\mathbb{C}^{-}
.

(iii)

There are
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\theta\geq 0
such that

\big|\overset{\,{}_{\mbox{\bf.}}}{\mathcal{G}}_{t}(z)\big|\leq-\frac{\operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{G}_{t}(z))}{\operatorname{Im}(z)^{k}}\,\theta\qquad\text{for all $(t,z)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{C}^{+}$}.

Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, it holds true that

(5.1)

\frac{1}{\pi}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\big|\mathcal{G}_{\rho_{T}}(s+i\varepsilon)-\mathcal{G}_{\rho_{0}}(s+i\varepsilon)\big|\,ds\leq\frac{T\theta}{\varepsilon^{k}},

and with the absolute constant
c_{k}:=(2k+1)(\frac{1}{k^{2}\pi})^{\frac{k}{2k+1}}
, we have that

(5.2)

L(\mu_{\rho_{T}},\mu_{\rho_{0}})\leq c_{k}T^{\frac{1}{2k+1}}\theta^{\frac{1}{2k+1}}.

Proof.

Take any
\varepsilon>0
. Thanks to condition
(ii)
, the fundamental theorem of calculus allows us to write
\mathcal{G}_{\rho_{T}}(s+i\varepsilon)-\mathcal{G}_{\rho_{0}}(s+i\varepsilon)=\int^{T}_{0}\overset{\,{}_{\mbox{\bf.}}}{\mathcal{G}}_{t}(s+i\varepsilon)\,dt
for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
. In combination with the bound required in condition
(iii)
, this yields that

\big|\mathcal{G}_{\rho_{T}}(s+i\varepsilon)-\mathcal{G}_{\rho_{0}}(s+i\varepsilon)\big|\leq-\frac{\theta}{\varepsilon^{k}}\int^{T}_{0}\operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{G}_{t}(s+i\varepsilon))\,dt

for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
. By integrating the latter inequality over
s\in\mathbb{R}
and using the Fubini-Tonelli theorem, which can be applied thanks to condition
(i)
, we conclude that

\frac{1}{\pi}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\big|\mathcal{G}_{\rho_{T}}(s+i\varepsilon)-\mathcal{G}_{\rho_{0}}(s+i\varepsilon)\big|\,ds\leq\frac{\theta}{\varepsilon^{k}}\int^{T}_{0}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\Big(-\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{G}_{t}(s+i\varepsilon))\Big)\,ds\,dt=\frac{T\theta}{\varepsilon^{k}}.

Notice that the last step relies on the fact that
-\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{G}_{t}(s+i\varepsilon))\,ds
is a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
for each
t\in[0,T]
. This verifies the bound asserted in (
5.1
).
Finally, we involve the estimate for the Lévy distance given in Theorem
2.4
. The already established bound (
5.1
) gives us that
L(\mu_{\rho_{T}},\mu_{\rho_{0}})\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon}{\pi}}+\frac{T\theta}{\varepsilon^{k}}
for all
\varepsilon>0
. Minimizing the right hand side over
\varepsilon\in(0,\infty)
yields the asserted bound (
5.2
).
∎

With the help of Lemma
5.1
, we can finally prove Theorem
1.1
. Notice that the uniform continuity of
\mu:(B_{\mathrm{sa}}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(B),d)\to(\operatorname{Prob}(\mathbb{R}),L)
with respect to any metric

on

which yields a metrization of the product topology is an immediate consequence of the bounds stated in the items
(i)
and
(ii)
of that theorem. To see this, we only need to notice that, for any two data pairs
\rho_{0}=(b_{0,0},\eta_{0})
and
\rho_{1}=(b_{0,1},\eta_{1})
in
B_{\mathrm{sa}}\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)

L(\mu_{\rho_{1}},\mu_{\rho_{0}})\leq L(\mu_{(b_{0,1},\eta_{1})},\mu_{(b_{0,0},\eta_{1})})+L(\mu_{(b_{0,0},\eta_{1})},\mu_{(b_{0,0},\eta_{0})})\leq c_{1}\,\|b_{0,1}-b_{0,0}\|^{1/3}+c_{2}\,\|\eta_{1}-\eta_{0}\|^{1/5}.

Thus, it suffices to prove
(i)
and
(ii)
in Theorem
1.1
; we treat these two parts separately.

Appendix A
Free noncommutative function theory on the second level

Free noncommutative function theory has its origins in the work of Taylor
[
55
,
56
]
, whose ideas were taken up subsequently by numerous authors for various applications; we mention
[
61
,
62
,
63
]
for their relevance on free probability theory and since Proposition
3.7
fits nicely into that context. A unified and very general approach was presented by Kaliuzhnyi-Verbovetskyi and Vinnikov in
[
38
]
, building on the notion of noncommutative sets and functions. With an eye towards applications in the concrete situation of this paper, we introduce here the terminology of noncommutative sets and functions in some truncated version. For the sake of simplicity, we restrict ourselves to the case where those are built on
C^{\ast}
-algebras.

Let
A
be a unital
C^{\ast}
-algebra. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we refer to
M_{\leq n}(A):=\coprod_{k=1}^{n}M_{k}(A)
as the
noncommutative space over
A
of height
n
. For a subset
\Omega\subseteq M_{\leq n}(A)
, we call
\Omega_{k}:=M_{k}(A)\cap\Omega
for
1\leq k\leq n
the
k
-th level of
\Omega
. We say that
\Omega
is a
noncommutative set over
A
of height
n
if
\Omega
is closed under direct sums of appropriate size, i.e., we require that whenever
1\leq k,l\leq n
are such that
k+l\leq n
, then

X\oplus Y:=\begin{bmatrix}X&0\\
0&Y\end{bmatrix}\in\Omega_{k+l}

for all
X\in\Omega_{k}
and
Y\in\Omega_{l}
.
We say that
\Omega
is
open
if each level
\Omega_{k}
is open in the operator-norm topology of the respective
C^{\ast}
-algebra
M_{k}(A)
.

Now, consider two unital
C^{\ast}
-algebras
A
and
B
and let
\Omega\subseteq M_{\leq n}(A)
be a noncommutative set of height
n
. Consider a function
f^{(\leq n)}:\Omega\to M_{\leq n}(B)
. We shall denote by
f^{(k)}:\Omega_{k}\to M_{\leq n}(B)
the restriction of
f^{(\leq n)}
to the
k
-th level
\Omega_{k}
of
\Omega
. We call
f^{(\leq n)}
a
noncommutative function of height
n
if
f^{(k)}(\Omega_{k})\subseteq M_{k}(B)
for
k=1,\dots,n
and the following two conditions are satisfied:

•

f^{(\leq n)}
respects direct sums
, i.e., whenever
1\leq k,l\leq n
are such that
k+l\leq n
, then
f^{(k+l)}(X\oplus Y)=f^{(k)}(X)\oplus f^{(l)}(Y)
holds true for all
X\in\Omega_{k}
and
Y\in\Omega_{l}
.

•

f^{(\leq n)}
respects similarities
, i.e., if
X\in\Omega_{k}
with
1\leq k\leq n
and an invertible matrix
S\in M_{k}(\mathbb{C})
are given such that
SXS^{-1}\in\Omega_{k}
, then
f^{(k)}(SXS^{-1})=Sf^{(k)}(X)S^{-1}
.

We say that
f
is
continuous
if
f^{(k)}:\Omega_{k}\to M_{k}(B)
is continuous for
k=1,\dots,n
in the respective operator-norm topologies.

A fundamental fact in free noncommutative function theory is that noncommutative functions, due to their algebraic structure, show strong analytic properties under rather weak assumptions; see
[
38
, Theorem 7.4]
. To some extent, this remains true in the truncated setting. The following theorem, which is a variant of
[
30
, Proposition 2.5]
in the spirit of
[
1
, Lemma 3.1]
, makes this claim precise. In essence, it says that continuous functions between subsets of
C^{\ast}
-algebras are automatically (Fréchet) holomorphic if they admit an extension to a noncommutative function of height
2
.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
A
and
B
be unital
C^{\ast}
-algebras and let
\emptyset\neq\Omega_{1}\subseteq A
be an open subset. Consider a continuous function
f^{(1)}:\Omega_{1}\to B
with the following property: there exists an open noncommutative set
\Omega
over
A
of height
2
and a continuous noncommutative function
f^{(\leq 2)}:\Omega\to M_{\leq 2}(B)
of height
2
which extends
f^{(1)}:\Omega_{1}\to B
in the sense that
\Omega_{1}
is the first level set of
\Omega
and
f^{(1)}
is the restriction of
f^{(\leq 2)}
to
\Omega_{1}
.
Then
f^{(1)}
is holomorphic and, for all
a\in\Omega_{1}
and
h\in A
satisfying the condition

(A.1)

\begin{bmatrix}a&h\\
0&a\end{bmatrix}\in\Omega_{2},

it holds true that

(A.2)

f^{(2)}\bigg(\begin{bmatrix}a&h\\
0&a\end{bmatrix}\bigg)=\begin{bmatrix}f^{(1)}(a)&(Df^{(1)})(a)h\\
0&f^{(1)}(a)\end{bmatrix}.

Before we give the proof, we notice that for
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
the matrix identity

(A.3)

\begin{bmatrix}a_{1}&z(a_{2}-a_{1})\\
0&a_{2}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&z\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&z\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}a_{1}&0\\
0&a_{2}\end{bmatrix}

holds; this provides similarity relations on
M_{2}(A)
, which will be used in the sequel.

Proof of Theorem
A.1
.

Take
a\in\Omega_{1}
and
h\in A
such that (
A.1
) is satisfied. Since
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
are assumed to be open, we find
r>0
such that

a+zh\in\Omega_{1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\begin{bmatrix}a&h\\
0&a+zh\end{bmatrix}\in\Omega_{2}

for all
z\in\mathbb{C}
satisfying
0<|z|<r
. For every such
z
, notice that (
A.3
) gives

\begin{bmatrix}a&h\\
0&a+zh\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&\frac{1}{z}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&\frac{1}{z}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
0&a+zh\end{bmatrix},

from which we infer that

f^{(2)}\bigg(\begin{bmatrix}a&h\\
0&a+zh\end{bmatrix}\bigg)=\begin{bmatrix}1&\frac{1}{z}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}f^{(1)}(a)&0\\
0&f^{(1)}(a+zh)\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&-\frac{1}{z}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}.

By another application of (
A.3
), we deduce from the latter that

f^{(2)}\bigg(\begin{bmatrix}a&h\\
0&a+zh\end{bmatrix}\bigg)=\begin{bmatrix}f^{(1)}(a)&\frac{1}{z}(f^{(1)}(a+zh)-f^{(1)}(a))\\
0&f^{(1)}(a+zh)\end{bmatrix}.

As both
f^{(1)}
and
f^{(2)}
are continuous, the previous identity tells us when letting
z\to 0
that the limit
(\delta f^{(1)})(a;h)=\lim_{z\to 0}\frac{1}{z}(f^{(1)}(a+zh)-f^{(1)}(a))
exists and that

(A.4)

f^{(2)}\bigg(\begin{bmatrix}a&h\\
0&a\end{bmatrix}\bigg)=\begin{bmatrix}f^{(1)}(a)&(\delta f^{(1)})(a;h)\\
0&f^{(1)}(a)\end{bmatrix}.

Because both
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
are supposed to be open, there exists for every given
a\in\Omega_{1}
some
\varepsilon>0
such that (
A.1
) is satisfied for all
h\in A
with
\|h\|<\varepsilon
. Thus, it follows that
f^{(1)}
is Gâteaux holomorphic, and because it is continuous by assumption and so in particular locally bounded, we conclude that
f^{(1)}
is holomorphic; see the discussion in Section
2.2
. In particular, we may replace in (
A.4
) the Gâteaux derivative
\delta f^{(1)}(a;h)
by the Fréchet derivative
(Df^{(1)})(a)h
which leads us to the formula (
A.2
).
∎

For a function
f^{(\leq n)}:\Omega\to M_{\leq n}(B)
with
f^{(k)}(\Omega_{k})\subseteq M_{k}(B)
for
k=1,\dots,n
, we say that
f^{(\leq n)}
respects intertwinings
if it satisfies the following condition: whenever
X\in\Omega_{k}
,
Y\in\Omega_{l}
, and
T\in M_{k\times l}(\mathbb{C})
for
1\leq k,l\leq n
are such that
XT=TY
, then
f^{(k)}(X)T=Tf^{(l)}(Y)
. Like in
[
38
, Proposition 2.1]
, one sees that if
f^{(\leq n)}
respects intertwinings, then it respects direct sums and similarities, and is thus a noncommutative function of height
n
; the proof of the converse implication, however, does not work in the truncated setting due to the limitation of height. It is therefore interesting to note that Proposition
3.7
can be strengthened:

Proposition A.2
.

Let
\eta:B\to B
be an
n
-positive linear map on the unital
C^{\ast}
-algebra
B
. Then the noncommutative function
G^{(\leq n)}_{\eta}:\mathbb{H}^{+}(M_{\leq n}(B))\to\mathbb{H}^{-}(M_{\leq n}(B))
of height
n
introduced in Proposition
3.7
respects intertwinings.

The proof of Proposition
A.2
is similar to that of Proposition
3.7
and is thus omitted.

To some extent, one can imitate in such cases the construction of the left and right difference-differential operators
\Delta_{L}
and
\Delta_{R}
from
[
38
, Section 2.2]
. We focus on
\Delta_{R}
since
\Delta_{L}
can be treated analogously. Let
f^{(\leq n)}:\Omega\to M_{\leq n}(B)
be a noncommutative function of height
n
on an open noncommutative set
\Omega\subseteq M_{\leq n}(A)
of height
n
which respects intertwinings. In the same way as
[
38
, Proposition 2.4]
, one proves that if
X\in\Omega_{k}
and
Y\in\Omega_{l}
are given for
1\leq k,l\leq n
with
k+l\leq n
, then there exists a
\mathbb{C}
-homogeneous map
\Delta_{R}f^{(\leq n)}(X,Y):M_{k\times l}(A)\to M_{k\times l}(B)
such that

(A.5)

f^{(k+l)}\bigg(\begin{bmatrix}X&Z\\
0&Y\end{bmatrix}\bigg)=\begin{bmatrix}f^{(k)}(X)&\Delta_{R}f^{(\leq n)}(X,Y)(Z)\\
0&f^{(l)}(Y)\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{whenever}\quad\begin{bmatrix}X&Z\\
0&Y\end{bmatrix}\in\Omega_{k+l}.

While the proof of
[
38
, Proposition 2.6]
does not work without imposing further constraints on
k
and

due to the limitation of height, the first order difference formula from
[

, Theorem 2.11]
extends to the truncated setting with exactly the same proof: if

,

, and

are given for

with

, then

(A.6)

Sf^{(k)}(Y)-f^{(l)}(X)S=\Delta_{R}f^{(\leq n)}(X,Y)(SY-XS).

In the situation of Theorem
A.1
and under the additional assumption that
f^{(\leq 2)}:\Omega\to M_{\leq 2}(B)
respects intertwinings, one can easily deduce from (

) that
\Delta_{R}f^{(\leq 2)}(a,a)(h)=Df^{(1)}(a)h
for all

Remark A.3
.

It is worthwhile to note that with the aforementioned facts, the alternative derivation of Lemma
3.9
which we sketched after the detailed proof becomes more transparent; in fact, we see that (
A.3
) was a shortcut avoiding the difference-differential operator. For
\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(B)

3.5

b_{0},b_{1}\in\mathbb{H}^{+}(B)

2.
Properties of semi
r
-ideals

This section deals with many properties of semi
r
-ideals. We justify the
relations among the concepts of semiprime ideals, semi
n
-ideals and our new
class of ideals. Moreover, several characterizations and examples are
presented. In particular, we characterize rings in which every ideal is a semi

r
-ideal.

Definition 1
.

Let
I
be a proper ideal of a ring
R
.
I
is called a semi
r
-ideal of
R

if whenever
a\in R
such that
a^{2}\in I
and
Ann_{R}(a)=0
, then
a\in I
.

For any non-zero subset
A
of a ring
R
, we note that

is a semi

r
-ideal of
R
. It is clear that the classes of semiprime ideals,
r
-ideals
and semi
n
-ideals are contained in the class of semi
r
-ideals. However, in
general these containments are proper as we illustrate in the following examples.

Example 1
.

Let
p
and
q
be prime integers.

(1)

Any non-zero semiprime ideal in an integral domain is a semi
r
-ideal
that is not an
r
-ideal.

(2)

In the ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}q}
, the ideal
\left\langle\overline{p^{2}}\right\rangle
is a semi

r
-ideal that is not a semi
n
-ideal.

(3)

The zero ideal of a ring

is always a semi
r
-ideal but it is not a
semiprime ideal unless
R
is a semiprime ring.

(4)

Every ideal of a Boolean ring (a ring of which every element is
idempotent) is semi
r
-ideal. Consider the ideal

of the Boolean ring
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Then

is a semi
r
-ideal that is not prime.

(5)

In general pr-ideals and semi r-ideals are not comparable. Let

be a

and

be a nonzero minimal prime ideal in

with

. From

-ideal of the

. Then

and

. Thus,

is not a semi
r
-ideal. Moreover,
any non-zero prime ideal in an integral domain is clearly a semi
r
-ideal
that is not a

-ideal.

If

and

-ideals of a ring

Example 2
.

Consider the ideals

-ideal of

. Indeed,

. Also,

-ideal of

.

Theorem 1
.

Let

be a proper ideal of a ring

and

integer. The following statements are equivalent.

(1)

is a semi

-ideal of

(2)

(3)

(1)
\Leftrightarrow

a^{2}=0

(1)
\Rightarrow
(3). Suppose

Suppose

Since

is a semi

-ideal. By the induction hypothesis, we conclude that

(3)
\Rightarrow

(4)
\Rightarrow
(1). Straightforward.
∎

4.

-submodules of amalgamated modules

Let
R
be a ring,

an ideal of
R
and
M
an
R
-module. Recently, in

[
5
]
, the duplication of the
R
-module

along the ideal

(denoted by

-module with scaler multiplication defined by

(r,r+j)\cdot(m,m^{\prime})=(rm,(r+j)m^{\prime})
for

,

[
5
]
.

Let

be an ideal of a ring

and

R
-module

. Then

and so

is a faithful

-module if and only if

is a
faithful
R
-module,
[
5
, Lemma 3.6]
.

In general, let
f:R_{1}\rightarrow R_{2}
be a ring homomorphism,

be an

R_{2}
,

be an
R_{1}
-module,
M_{2}
be an
R_{2}
-module
(which is an
R_{1}
-module induced naturally by

) and

be an

-module homomorphism. The subring

[

]

and

which is an
(R_{1}\Join^{f}J)
-module with the scaler product defined as

(r,f(r)+j)(m_{1},\varphi(m_{1})+m_{2})=(rm_{1},\varphi(rm_{1})+f(r)m_{2}+j\varphi(m_{1})+jm_{2})

For submodules

and

.

.

.

Let

be a submodule of an

(2) Let

. If there exists an

of

. The proof of the converse part is
similar to that of the converse of (1).

\Omega
be a bounded domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
with
C^{2}
-boundary
\partial\Omega
.
Let
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
be a weak solution of the Navier-Stokes equation for any smooth test function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times\Omega)
with compact support, and
{\rm div}\,\varphi=0
.
In addition, if

u\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))\cap L^{s}(0,T;B_{s}^{\alpha,\infty}(\Omega))

for any
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{1}{2},\;q\geq 4,\;s>2
and for any
\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{s}<\alpha<1
.
Then, for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\int_{\Omega}dx|u(t,x)|^{2}+2\nu\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}dtdx|\nabla u|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}dx|u_{0}|^{2}.

Appendix B
H. Lewy’s operator by the method of characteristics

Here, we quote the famous H. Lewy’s counter example and its explanation by Hörmander
[
55
]
.

Theorem B.1
.

There exists a function
f=f(t,x_{1},x_{2})\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
such that the equation

(B.1)

\partial_{t}u+i\partial_{x_{1}}u-2i(t+ix_{1})\partial_{x_{2}}u=f

doesn’t have any distributional solution
u
in any open no-void subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.

L. Hörmander gives an resolution to this fact.
Let a linear PDE with smooth coefficients be given

P(x,D_{x})=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}a_{\alpha}(x)D^{\alpha}\quad\text{with}\quad a_{\alpha}(x)\in C^{\infty}(\Omega:{\mathbb{C}}).

Defining

(B.2)

P_{m}(x,\xi)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}a_{\alpha}(x)\xi^{\alpha},\quad\bar{P}_{m}(x,\xi)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\overline{a_{\alpha}}(x)\xi^{\alpha}

and

(B.3)

C_{2m-1}(x,\xi)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}i(P_{m}^{(j)}\bar{P}_{m,j}-P_{m,j}\bar{P}_{m}^{(j)})\quad\text{where}\quad P_{m}^{(j)}=\frac{\partial P_{m}(x,\xi)}{\partial\xi_{j}},P_{m,j}=\frac{\partial P_{m}(x,\xi)}{\partial x_{j}}.

Theorem B.2

(Theorem 6.1.1 in
[
55
]
)
.

Suppose that the differential equation

(B.4)

P(x,D_{x})u=f

has a solution
u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\Omega)
for every
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Then, we have

(B.5)

C_{2m-1}(x,\xi)=0\quad\mbox{if}\quad P_{m}(x,\xi)=0,\quad x\in\Omega,\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Theorem B.3

(Theorem 6.1.2 in
[
55
]
)
.

Suppose that the coefficients of the operator
P(x,D)
of order
m
are
C^{\infty}(\Omega)
and that (
B.5
) is not valid for any open non-void set
\omega\subset\Omega
when
x
is restricted to
\omega
. Then, there exists functions
f\in{\mathcal{S}}(\Omega)
such that the equation (
B.4
) doesn’t have any solution
u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(\omega)
for any open non-void set
\omega\subset\Omega
. The set of such functions
f
is of second category.

Taking
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\beta(1+\alpha)\neq 0
, we generalize slightly the operator in (
B.1
) as

L_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x,\partial_{t},\partial_{x})=\partial_{t}+i\alpha\partial_{x_{1}}+i\beta(t+ix_{1})\partial_{x_{2}}.

Then, we have

P_{1}((t,x),(\tau,\xi))=\tau+i\alpha\xi_{1}+i\beta(t+ix_{1})\xi_{2},\quad\bar{P}_{1}((t,x),(\tau,\xi))=\tau-i\alpha\xi_{1}-i\beta(t-ix_{1})\xi_{2}

which implies

C_{1}((t,x),(\tau,\xi))=-2\beta(1+\alpha)\xi_{2}

and assuming
\beta(1+\alpha)\neq 0
, we have the criterion of Theorem
B.2
:

P_{1}((t,x),(\tau,\xi))=0\quad\mbox{but}\quad C_{1}((t,x),(\tau,\xi))\neq 0\quad\mbox{if}\quad\tau+\beta x_{1}=0\;\mbox{and}\;\beta t+\alpha\xi_{1}=0.

Therefore, H. Lewy example is reproduced by
(\alpha,\beta)=(1,-2)
.

In spite of above, we try to construct a solution of the following initial value problem:

(B.6)

L_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x,\partial_{t},\partial_{x})u(t,x)=f(t,x)\quad\text{with}\quad u(0,x)=\underline{u}(x).

Though the coefficients are complex valued, we may apply Theorem
3.1
formally.
The characteristic equation corresponding to (
B.6
) is

(B.7)

\displaystyle\frac{dx_{1}}{dt}=i\alpha\quad\text{with}\quad x_{1}(0)=\underline{x}_{1}

\displaystyle\frac{dx_{2}}{dt}=i\beta(t+ix_{1})\quad\text{with}\quad x_{2}(0)=\underline{x}_{2}.

Therefore

(B.8)

\displaystyle x_{1}(t)=\underline{x}_{1}+i\alpha t,

\displaystyle x_{2}(t)=\underline{x}_{2}-\beta\underline{x}_{1}t+i\beta(1-\alpha)\frac{t^{2}}{2}.

Putting
x(t,\underline{x})=(x_{1}(t,\underline{x}),x_{2}(t,\underline{x}))
and
F(t,\underline{x})=f(t,x(t,\underline{x}))
, we define

U(t,\underline{x})=\underline{u}(x(t,\underline{x}))+\int_{0}^{t}ds\,F(s,\underline{x}).

Denoting the inverse function of
\bar{x}=x(t,\underline{x})
as
\underline{x}=y(t,\bar{x})
, more precisely,

(B.9)

\displaystyle\underline{x}_{1}=\bar{x}_{1}-i\alpha t=y_{1}(t,\bar{x}),

\displaystyle\underline{x}_{2}=\bar{x}_{2}+\beta\bar{x}_{1}t-i\beta(1+\alpha)\frac{t^{2}}{2}=y_{2}(t,\bar{x}),

we define

(B.10)

u(t,\bar{x})=U(t,x(t,\underline{x}))\big|_{\underline{x}=y(t,\bar{x})}.

Finally, we have a solution of (
B.6
) explicitly if we may give the meaning to this expression:

(B.11)

\displaystyle u(t,\bar{x})

\displaystyle=\underline{u}(\bar{x}_{1}-i\alpha t,\bar{x}_{2}+\beta\bar{x}_{1}t-i\beta(1+\alpha)\frac{t^{2}}{2})

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}dsf(s,x(s,\underline{x}))\bigg|_{\underline{x}=y(t,\bar{x})}.

In fact, if the function
\underline{u}(x_{1},x_{2})
is real analytic, then putting

v(t,x)=\underline{u}({x}_{1}-i\alpha t,{x}_{2}+\beta{x}_{1}t-i\beta(1+\alpha)\frac{t^{2}}{2}),

we have

v_{t}+i\alpha v_{x_{1}}+i\beta(t+ix_{1})v_{x_{2}}=0\quad\text{with}\quad v(0,x)=\underline{u}(x).

Moreover,

\displaystyle G(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}dsf(s,\underline{x}_{1}-i\alpha s,\underline{x}_{2}-\beta\underline{x}_{1}s+i\beta(1+\alpha)\frac{s^{2}}{2})\bigg|_{\underline{x}=y(t,\bar{x}),\bar{x}=x}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}dsf(s,x_{1}-i\alpha(t-s),x_{2}+\beta x_{1}(t-s)-i\alpha\beta\frac{(t-s)^{2}}{2}-i\beta\frac{t^{2}-s^{2}}{2})

satisfies

G_{t}+i\alpha G_{x_{1}}+i\beta(t+ix_{1})G_{x_{2}}=f(t,x)\quad\text{with}\quad G(0,x)=0.

Remark B.1
.

By Cauchy-Kowalevsky Theorem, we have a unique solution of (
B.6
) if coefficients are real analytic, therefore above arguments only imply the explicit representation of the solution.

We quote here a result mentioned to me by a letter from Takahiro Kawai on 01/Feb/1993:

Claim B.1
.

Take any functions
\phi,\psi
, for example, in
C^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Let a function
\underline{u}(x)=x_{1}\phi(x_{2})+\psi(x_{2})
be given as initial value at time
t=0
with
f=0
.
Assuming
\alpha=-1
, then (
B.1
) with
f=0
and initial data
\underline{u}(x)
has a unique solution

u(t,x)=(x_{1}-i\alpha t)\phi(x_{2}+\beta x_{1}t)+\psi(x_{2}+\beta x_{1}t)

of (
B.1
).

Proof
. Clear from above calculation because the function
x_{1}
itself is real analytic.

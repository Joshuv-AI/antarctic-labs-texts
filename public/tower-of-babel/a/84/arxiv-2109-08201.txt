Quantum fluctuations of baryon number density
Rajeev Singh

Abstract
Quantum fluctuation expression of the baryon number for a subsystem consisting of hot relativistic spin- 1 2 \frac{1}{2} particles are derived. These fluctuations seems to diverge in the limit where system size goes to zero. For a broad range of thermodynamic parameters numerical solutions are obtained which might be helpful to interpret the heavy-ion experimental data.

Basic definitions

Spin-
\frac{1}{2}
particles are represented by Dirac field operator which is given as
[
20
]

\displaystyle\psi(t,\bm{x})=

\displaystyle\sum_{r}\int\frac{dK}{\sqrt{2\omega_{\bm{k}}}}\Big(U_{r}^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}(\bm{k})a_{r}^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}(\bm{k})e^{-ik\cdot x}+V_{r}^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}(\bm{k})b_{r}^{\dagger}(\bm{k})e^{ik\cdot x}\Big),

(1)

where
dK\equiv{d^{3}k}/{(2\pi)^{3}}
, while
a_{r}^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}(\bm{k})
and
b_{r}^{\dagger}(\bm{k})
represent the annihilation and creation operators for particles and antiparticles, respectively, whereas
r
denotes the polarization degree of freedom.
Operators
a_{r}^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}(\bm{k})
and
b_{r}^{\dagger}(\bm{k})
satisfy the following canonical anticommutation rules,
\{a_{r}^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}(\bm{k}),a_{s}^{\dagger}(\bm{k}^{\prime})\}=(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{rs}\delta^{(3)}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})
and
\{b_{r}^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}(\bm{k}),b_{s}^{\dagger}(\bm{k}^{\prime})\}=(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{rs}\delta^{(3)}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})
.
U_{r}^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}(\bm{k})
and
V_{r}^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}(\bm{k})
are called Dirac spinors which have normalization
{\bar{U}}_{r}^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}(\bm{k})U_{s}^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}(\bm{k})=2m\delta_{rs}
and
{\bar{V}}_{r}^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}(\bm{k})V_{s}^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}(\bm{k})=-2m\delta_{rs}
, and
\omega_{\bm{k}}=\sqrt{\bm{k}^{2}+m^{2}}
is the particle’s energy.

To calculate thermal averaging of quantum operators we need to evaluate the thermal expectation values of the products of two and four creation and/or annihilation operators (for both particles and antiparticles)

\displaystyle\langle a_{r}^{\dagger}({\bm{k}})a_{s}^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}({\bm{k}}^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{rs}\delta^{(3)}({\bm{k}}-{\bm{k}}^{\prime})f(\omega_{\bm{k}}),

(2)

\displaystyle\langle a^{\dagger}_{r}(\bm{k})a^{\dagger}_{s}(\bm{k}^{\prime})a_{r^{\prime}}^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}(\bm{p})a_{s^{\prime}}^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}(\bm{p}^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{6}\Big(\delta_{rs^{\prime}}\delta_{r^{\prime}s}\delta^{(3)}(\bm{k}-\bm{p}^{\prime})~\delta^{(3)}(\bm{k}^{\prime}-\bm{p})

\displaystyle-\delta_{rr^{\prime}}\delta_{ss^{\prime}}\delta^{(3)}({\bm{k}}-\bm{p})~\delta^{(3)}({\bm{k}}^{\prime}-\bm{p}^{\prime})\Big)f(\omega_{{\bm{k}}})f(\omega_{{\bm{k}}^{\prime}}).

(3)

where
f(\omega_{{\bm{k}}})=1/(\exp(\beta(\omega_{\boldsymbol{k}}-\mu))+1)
is the distribution function for particles and similarly,
\bar{f}(\omega_{{\bm{k}}})=1/(\exp(\beta(\omega_{\boldsymbol{k}}+\mu))+1)

We define baryon number density

\hat{J}^{0}_{a}

\displaystyle\hat{J}^{0}_{a}=\frac{1}{(a\sqrt{\pi})^{3}}\int d^{3}\bm{x}~\hat{J}^{0}(x)~\exp\left(-\frac{{\bm{x}}^{2}}{a^{2}}\right),

(4)

\hat{J}^{0}=\psi^{\dagger}\psi

Following expression for variance is used to calculate the fluctuation for baryon number of the subsystem

\sigma^{2}(a,m,T,\mu)=\langle:\hat{J}^{0}_{a}::\hat{J}^{0}_{a}:\rangle-\langle:\hat{J}^{0}_{a}:\rangle^{2}\,

\sigma_{n}(a,m,T,\mu)=\frac{(\langle:\hat{J}^{0}_{a}::\hat{J}^{0}_{a}:\rangle-\langle:\hat{J}^{0}_{a}:\rangle^{2})^{1/2}}{\langle:\hat{J}^{0}_{a}:\rangle}.

(6)

where

:\hat{J}^{0}_{a}:

Quantum fluctuation expression

Using Equation (
2

:\hat{J}_{a}^{0}:

\displaystyle\langle:\hat{J}_{a}^{0}:\rangle=2\int dK\Big[f(\omega_{\bm{k}})-\bar{f}(\omega_{\bm{k}})\Big].

(7)

here, factor 2 accounts for degeneracy in spin.

Again using Equation (

S_{a}

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}(a,m,T,\mu)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{dK}{\omega_{\bm{k}}}\frac{dK^{\prime}}{\omega_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}}(\omega_{\bm{k}}\omega_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}+\bm{k}\cdot\bm{k}^{\prime}+m^{2})e^{-\frac{a^{2}}{2}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})^{2}}\times

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~\left[f(\omega_{\bm{k}})\left(1-f(\omega_{\bm{k}^{\prime}})\right)+\bar{f}(\omega_{\bm{k}})(1-\bar{f}(\omega_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}))\right]

\displaystyle-\int\frac{dK}{\omega_{\bm{k}}}\frac{dK^{\prime}}{\omega_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}}(\omega_{\bm{k}}\omega_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}+\bm{k}\cdot\bm{k}^{\prime}-m^{2})e^{-\frac{a^{2}}{2}(\bm{k}+\bm{k}^{\prime})^{2}}\times

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~\left[f(\omega_{\bm{k}})(1-\bar{f}(\omega_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}))+\bar{f}(\omega_{\bm{k}})(1-f(\omega_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}))\right].

(8)

4
Thermodynamic limit

Since
S_{a}
is a subsystem of the the larger thermodynamic system
S_{V}
, the thermodynamic limit can be obtained by by considering the
a\rightarrow\infty
limit, in this limit, our quantum fluctuation reduces to the formula similar to the classical statistical fluctuation. Hence to get the thermodynamic limit we need to study the susceptibilities which describe the fluctuations in the baryon number obtained for thermo-chemical equilibrium
[
25
]

\displaystyle\chi_{l}^{(B)}=\frac{\partial^{l}(P/T^{4})}{\partial(\mu/T)^{l}}\bigg|_{T}.

(9)

where
P
represents the thermodynamic pressure. Susceptibilities can also be represented by the cumulants of the baryon distribution with
n_{B}\equiv N_{B}/V
being the net baryon number density, as,

\displaystyle\chi_{1}^{(B)}=\frac{1}{VT^{3}}\langle N_{B}\rangle=\frac{1}{T^{3}}\frac{\langle N_{B}\rangle}{V}=\frac{n_{B}}{T^{3}},

\displaystyle\chi_{2}^{(B)}=\frac{1}{VT^{3}}\langle(\triangle N_{B})^{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{VT^{3}}\langle(N_{B}-\langle N_{B}\rangle)^{2}\rangle=\frac{V}{T^{3}}\langle(n_{B}-\langle n_{B}\rangle)^{2}\rangle.

(10)

The second order susceptibility
\chi_{2}^{(B)}
can again be evaluated by taking the derivative of the thermodynamic pressure using Eq. (
9
), where at finite temperature and baryon chemical potential, the thermodynamic pressure is given as
[
25
]

\displaystyle\frac{P}{T^{4}}=

\displaystyle\frac{2g}{T^{3}}\int dK\Bigg[\ln\bigg(1+\exp\left(-\frac{\omega_{\boldsymbol{k}}-\mu}{T}\right)\bigg)+\ln\bigg(1+\exp\left(-\frac{\omega_{\boldsymbol{k}}+\mu}{T}\right)\bigg)\Bigg].

(11)

By making use of Eqs. (
9
) and (
11
) we can easily come to the following expressions

\displaystyle\chi_{1}^{(B)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial(P/T^{4})}{\partial(\mu/T)}\bigg|_{T}=\frac{2g}{T^{3}}\int dK\Big[f(\omega_{\bm{k}})-\bar{f}(\omega_{\bm{k}})\Big]=\frac{n_{B}}{T^{3}}

(12)

and,

\displaystyle\chi_{2}^{(B)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{2}(P/T^{4})}{\partial(\mu/T)^{2}}\bigg|_{T}=\frac{2g}{T^{3}}\int dK\Big[f(\omega_{\bm{k}})(1-f(\omega_{\bm{k}}))+\bar{f}(\omega_{\bm{k}})(1-\bar{f}(\omega_{\bm{k}}))\Big].

(13)

Hence, we arrive at

\displaystyle T^{3}\chi_{2}^{(B)}

\displaystyle=V\langle(n_{B}-\langle n_{B}\rangle)^{2}\rangle=2g\int dK\Big[f(\omega_{\bm{k}})(1-f(\omega_{\bm{k}}))+\bar{f}(\omega_{\bm{k}})(1-\bar{f}(\omega_{\bm{k}}))\Big].

(14)

For the case of large volume limit, Eq. (
8
) should reproduce to Eq. (
14
), which can be seen by using the following Gaussian representation of the 3D Dirac delta function

\displaystyle\delta^{(3)}({\bm{k}}-{\bm{k}^{\prime}})=\lim_{a\to\infty}\frac{a^{3}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}e^{-\frac{a^{2}}{2}({\bm{k}}-{\bm{k}^{\prime}})^{2}}.

(15)

which leads to

\displaystyle\lim_{a\to\infty}a^{3}(2\pi)^{3/2}\bigg[\langle:\hat{J}_{a}^{0}::\hat{J}_{a}^{0}:\rangle-\langle:\hat{J}_{a}^{0}:\rangle^{2}\bigg]=2\int dK\Big[f(\omega_{\bm{k}})(1-f(\omega_{\bm{k}}))+\bar{f}(\omega_{\bm{k}})(1-\bar{f}(\omega_{\bm{k}}))\Big],

(16)

Thus,

\displaystyle\lim_{a\to\infty}V_{a}\bigg[\langle:\hat{J}_{a}^{0}::\hat{J}_{a}^{0}:\rangle-\langle:\hat{J}_{a}^{0}:\rangle^{2}\bigg]=T^{3}\chi_{2}^{(B)},

(17)

where
V_{a}=a^{3}(2\pi)^{3/2}
can be called the volume of the “Gaussian” subsystem
S_{a}
.

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we have presented the quantum fluctuations in baryon number in subsystems of a hot and dense relativistic gas of spin-
\frac{1}{2}
particles. Even though our results seem to diverge for the system going to zero size, they still agree with the results known from statistical physics for the system going to sufficiently large size
a
. The numerical findings obtained here might be useful to interpret the heavy-ion experimental data differently and give new information.

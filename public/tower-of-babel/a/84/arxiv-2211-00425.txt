Improved lattice method for determining entanglement measures in SU(N) gauge theories
Ahmed Salami

Abstract
The determination of entanglement measures in SU(N) gauge theories is a non-trivial task. With the so-called "replica trick", a family of entanglement measures, known as "Rényi entropies", can be determined with lattice Monte Carlo. Unfortunately, the standard implementation of the replica method for SU(N) lattice gauge theories suffers from a severe signal-to-noise ratio problem, rendering high-precision studies of Rényi entropies prohibitively expensive. In this work, we propose a method to overcome the signal-to-noise ratio problem and show some first results for SU(N) in 4 dimensions. Preprint: HIP-2022-26/TH

Introduction

Consider the Hilbert space of an isolated quantum system consisting of two disjoint parts,
A
and
B
, i.e.
\mathcal{H}_{AB}=\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
. Each state
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\psi}}\right\rangle_{AB}\in\mathcal{H}_{AB}
can be described in terms of orthonormal bases
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|n}}\right\rangle_{A}\in\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|m}}\right\rangle_{B}\in\mathcal{H}_{B}
of the subsystems as

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\psi}}\right\rangle_{AB}=\sum_{mn}a_{mn}\,\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|m}}\right\rangle_{A}\otimes\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|n}}\right\rangle_{B}\quad,\quad\sum_{mn}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|a_{mn}}}\right|^{2}=1\ ,

(1)

with corresponding density operator
1
1

1

We use notation where the tensor product between vectors from dual Hilbert spaces, e.g. between
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\psi}}\right\rangle_{A}\in\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
{}_{A}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\phi}}\right|\in\mathcal{H}^{*}_{A}
, is written as
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\psi}}\right\rangle_{A}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\phi}}\right|\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{*}_{A}
. All other tensor products are written with explicit
\otimes
-symbols, as e.g.
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\psi}}\right\rangle_{A}\otimes{}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\phi}}\right\rangle_{A}\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{A}
,
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\psi}}\right\rangle_{A}\otimes{}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\phi}}\right\rangle_{B}\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
or
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\psi}}\right\rangle_{A}\otimes{}_{B}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\phi}}\right|\in\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{*}_{B}
.

\rho_{AB}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\psi}}\right\rangle_{AB}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\psi}}\right|=\sum_{mnkl}a^{\phantom{*}}_{mn}a^{*}_{kl}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|m}}\right\rangle_{A}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle k}}\right|\otimes\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|n}}\right\rangle_{B}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle l}}\right|\ .

(2)

As this density operator corresponds to a
pure state
, it has the projector property,

\rho^{2}=\rho\ .

(3)

General density operators, however, can be convex sums of pure states (not necessarily orthogonal),

\rho=\sum_{i}\,p_{i}\,\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\psi_{i}}}\right\rangle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\psi_{i}}}\right|\quad,\quad\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\psi_{i}}}\right\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{AB}\,\forall\,i\quad,\quad\sum_{i}\,p_{i}=1\ ,

(4)

and do not have the projection property Eq. (
3
). For such
mixed states
one always has
\operatorname{tr}(\rho^{2})<1
.

From the pure state density operator in Eq. (
2
) we can trace out e.g. subsystem
B
to obtain a reduced density matrix for subsystem
A
:

\rho_{A}=\operatorname{tr}_{B}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\rho_{AB}}}\right)=\sum_{mkl}a^{\phantom{*}}_{ml}a^{*}_{kl}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|m}}\right\rangle_{A}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle k}}\right|\ .

(5)

The reduced density matrix in Eq. (
5
) is in general a mixed state, unless

a_{mn}=a^{(A)}_{m}a^{(B)}_{n}\quad\forall\,m,n\quad,\quad\sum_{m}|a^{(A)}_{m}|^{2}=1\quad,\quad\sum_{n}|a^{(B)}_{n}|^{2}=1\ ,

(6)

i.e. unless
\rho_{AB}
is a product sate,
\rho_{AB}=\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}
. If the total system is in such a product state, then the subsystems
A
and
B
are completely independent and not entangled. If, however the system is described by a general pure state, Eq. (
2
), which is not a product state, so that Eq. (
5
) satisfies
\operatorname{tr}(\rho^{2}_{A})<1
, then the subsystems
A
and
B
are entangled. This motivates the definition of a family of measures for quantifying the entanglement of a subsystem
A
with its complement
B
, based on how quickly
\operatorname{tr}(\rho_{A}^{s})
decays with increasing
s
, as for example the
Rényi entropies
of order
s\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{2,3,\ldots}}\right\}

[
1
]
,

H_{s}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(A}}\right)=\frac{1}{1-s}\log\operatorname{tr}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\rho_{A}^{s}}}\right)\ ,

(7)

or the
entanglement entropy
,

S_{EE}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(A}}\right)=-\lim_{s\to 1}\frac{\partial\log\operatorname{tr}(\rho_{A}^{s})}{\partial s}=-\lim_{s\to 1}\frac{\partial\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-s}}\right)H_{s}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(A}}\right)}}\right)}{\partial s}=\lim_{s\to 1}H_{s}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(A}}\right)\ .

(8)

The latter can with the spectral theorem also be written as Von Neumann entropy of the reduced density matrix
\rho_{A}
:

S_{EE}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(A}}\right)=-\operatorname{tr}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\rho_{A}\log\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\rho_{A}}}\right)}}\right)\ .

(9)

If
S_{EE}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(A}}\right)=0
then subsystems
A
and
B
are not entangled. The larger the value of
S_{EE}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(A}}\right)
, the more mixed
\rho_{A}
is and the more entangled
A
and
B
are. The same holds when using
H_{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(A}}\right)
instead of
S_{EE}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(A}}\right)
. However, this discussion applies only if the whole system is described by a pure state
\rho_{AB}
. If one has a finite temperature partition function at temperature
T
:

Z_{AB}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\beta}}\right)=\operatorname{tr}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\rho_{AB}}}\right)\quad,\quad\rho_{AB}=\sum_{n}p_{n}\,\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\psi_{n}}}\right\rangle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\psi_{n}}}\right|\quad,\quad\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\psi_{n}}}\right\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{AB}\quad,\quad p_{n}=\frac{\operatorname{e}^{-E_{n}/T}}{\sum_{i}\operatorname{e}^{-E_{i}/T}}

(10)

where
\{(E_{n},\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\psi_{n}}}\right\rangle)\}_{n=0,1,\ldots}
are the energy eigenvalues and corresponding eigenstates for the full system, then the system is obviously described by a mixed state, and only in the zero-temperature limit,
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(T\to 0}}\right)
,
\rho_{AB}
reduces to a pure state, namely the ground state. We will not try to quantify entanglement for mixed states. Results obtained with the methods described in the remainder of this article should therefore be considered as lattice approximations to the zero-temperature entanglement and Rényi entropies (resp. their derivatives).

\approx F_{c}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(l,2,N_{t},N_{s}}}\right)-2\,F\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N_{t},N_{s}}}\right)=H_{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(l,N_{t},N_{s}}}\right)\ .

(16)

The accuracy of this approximation is discussed in
[

]
.

3
Overlap problem

When trying to determine the free energy difference in Eq. (
16
) with Monte Carlo simulations by direct measurements,

H_{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(l,N_{t},N_{s}}}\right)=-\log\frac{Z_{c}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(l,2,N_{t},N_{s}}}\right)}{Z_{c}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(0,2,N_{t},N_{s}}}\right)}=-\log\,\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-S_{G,l}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[U}}\right]+S_{G,0}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[U}}\right]}}\right)}}\right\rangle_{Z_{c}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(0,2,N_{t},N_{s}}}\right)}\ ,

(17)

where
S_{G,l}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[U}}\right]
is for a given gauge link configuration,
U=\{U_{x,\nu}\}_{x,\nu}
, the value of the Wilson gauge action for the lattice topology that corresponds to an entangling region
A
of width
l
(cf. Fig.
1
) and
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\ldots}}\right\rangle_{Z}
is the expectation value with respect to the partition function
Z
,
one will be facing a severe overlap problem: link-variable configurations that (significantly) contribute to the partition function
Z_{c}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(l=0,2,N_{t},N_{s}}}\right)
play essentially no role for the partition function
Z_{c}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(l,2,N_{t},N_{s}}}\right)
(and vice versa); a reliable estimate on the log of the observable on the right-hand side of Eq. (
17
) would therefore require exponentially large statistics.

The overlap problem gets somewhat milder when looking at the derivative

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\frac{\partial S_{EE}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(l^{\prime},N_{t},N_{s}}}\right)}{\partial l^{\prime}}}}\right|_{l^{\prime}=l+1/2}\approx F_{c}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(l+1,2,N_{t},N_{s}}}\right)-F_{c}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(l,2,N_{t},N_{s}}}\right)\

(18)

instead of at Eq. (
16
) itself, which is often done also in a non-lattice context in order to get rid of a
l
-independent UV-divergent piece in
S_{EE}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(l,N_{t},N_{s}}}\right)

[
4
,
5
,
3
]
. However, also for the free energy difference in Eq. (
18
) the overlap problem will in general still be too severe to allow for a naive measurement à la Eq. (
17
).

In previous works
[
4
,
5
,
3
]
the overlap problem has been addressed by determining Eq. (
18
) from

\Delta F\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(l}}\right)=-\int\limits_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}\alpha\,\frac{\partial\log Z^{*}_{l}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\alpha}}\right)}{\partial\alpha}=\int\limits_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}\alpha\,\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle S_{G,l+1}-S_{G,l}}}\right\rangle_{Z^{*}_{l}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\alpha}}\right)}\ ,

(19)

using an interpolating partition function

Z^{*}_{l}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\alpha}}\right)=\int\mathcal{D}\bigl[U\bigr]\,\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-\alpha}}\right)\,S_{G,l}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[U}}\right]-\alpha\,S_{G,l+1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[U}}\right]}}\right)\ .

(20)

This approach suffers unfortunately form a bad signal to noise ratio for the integrated observable, Eq. (
19
), which is presumably due to a huge free energy barrier in Eq. (
20
) as
\alpha
runs from 0 to 1.

4
Improved interpolation method

In this work, we try to avoid the formation of huge free energy barriers when measuring (
18
) by using a different interpolation method. Let us denote by
C
the set of all plaquettes in a
V=s\,N_{t}\times N_{s}^{d-1}
lattice which have the same value regardless of whether their spatial location is part of region
A
or region
B
. These are all plaquettes but the temporal ones that touch from below the time-slices with
x^{d}=r\cdot N_{t}
for
r=1,\ldots,s
(cf. Fig.
1
). A generalized partition function can then be written as:

Z\bigl(\big.\beta,s,N_{t},N_{s},\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{n}}\right\}\big.\bigr)=\\
\int\mathcal{D}\bigl[U\bigr]\exp\Bigl(\Big.\frac{\beta}{N}\Bigl(\Big.\sum\limits_{\Box\in C}\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\big.U_{\vphantom{\bar{l}}\Box}\big.\bigr)+\sum_{\bar{x}}\sum_{\nu=1}^{d-1}\sum_{r=1}^{s}\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\big.U^{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(n_{\bar{x},\nu}}}\right)}_{\nu d}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\bar{x},r\cdot N_{t}-1}}\right)\big.\bigr)\Big.\Bigr)\Big.\Bigr)\ ,

(21)

where
\bar{x}={x^{1},\ldots,x^{d-1}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
labels the spatial positions on the lattice and
U^{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(0}}\right)}_{\nu d}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)
and
U^{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1}}\right)}_{\nu d}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)
are the two different values for the temporal plaquette

U_{\nu d}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)=U^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{\nu\vphantom{\widehat{\nu}}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x}}\right)\,U^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{d}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x+\widehat{\nu}}}\right)\,U^{\dagger}_{\nu}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x+\smash{\widehat{d}}\vphantom{\widehat{\nu}}}}\right)\,U^{\dagger}_{d}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x\vphantom{\widehat{\nu}}}}\right)\ ,

(22)

depending on whether the plaquette is subject to the temporal boundary conditions from outside or inside region A (see Fig.
1
: (0) corresponds to blue, (1) to red). Which case applies for which spatial link is controlled by the set
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{n}}\right\}=\{n_{\bar{x},\nu}\}_{\bar{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1},\nu\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{1,\ldots,d-1}}\right\}}
of discrete variables
n_{\bar{x},\nu}\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{0,1}}\right\}
.

To describe how the interpolation between
Z_{c}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(l,s,N_{t},N_{s}}}\right)
and
Z_{c}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(l+1,s,N_{t},N_{s}}}\right)
can be carried out using the generalized partition function Eq. (
21
), let us denote by
K=\{(\bar{x}_{i},\nu_{i})\}_{i=1,\ldots,N_{K}}

the ordered set of spatial links in the boundary region where
N_{s}-l-1\leq x_{1}<N_{s}-l
, and let
K_{j}=\{(\bar{x}_{i},\nu_{i})\}_{i=1,\ldots,j}
be the subset of the first
j
elements of
K
. Let us then further define:

n^{i}_{\bar{x},\nu}=\begin{cases}0\quad\text{if}\quad x_{1}<N_{s}-l-1\quad\text{or}\quad\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\bar{x},\nu}}\right)\in K\setminus K_{i}\\
1\quad\text{if}\quad x_{1}\geq N_{s}-l\quad\text{or}\quad\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\bar{x},\nu}}\right)\in K_{i}\end{cases}\ ,

(23)

and abbreviate
Z_{i}=Z(\beta,s,N_{t},N_{s},\{n^{i}\})
and
F_{i}=-\log(Z_{i})
.
The expression for the derivative of the entanglement entropy (
18
) then becomes:

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left.\frac{\partial S_{EE}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(l^{\prime},N_{t},N_{s}}}\right)}{\partial l^{\prime}}}}\right|_{l^{\prime}=l+1/2}\approx-\log(Z_{N_{K}})+\log(Z_{0})=F_{N_{K}}-F_{0}\ .

(24)

This free energy difference can be measured from a single simulation using Wang-Landau (WL) sampling
[
6
]
. With this method one samples the modified partition function,

Z_{\text{WL}}(\beta,s,N_{t},N_{s},\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{f}}\right\})=\\
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{N_{K}}e^{f_{i}}\int\mathcal{D}\bigl[U\bigr]\exp\Bigl(\Big.\frac{\beta}{N}\Bigl(\Big.\sum\limits_{\Box\in C}\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\big.U_{\vphantom{\bar{l}}\Box}\big.\bigr)+\sum_{\bar{x}}\sum_{\nu=1}^{d-1}\sum_{r=1}^{s}\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\big.U^{(n^{i}_{\bar{x},\nu})}_{\vphantom{\bar{x}}\smash{\bar{x}+(r\cdot N_{t}-1)\cdot\widehat{d},\nu d}}\big.\bigr)\Big.\Bigr)\Big.\Bigr)\ ,

(25)

and adjusts the set of parameters
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{f}}\right\}=\{f_{i}\}_{i=0,\ldots,N_{K}}
till the histogram
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{H}}\right\}=\{H_{j}\}_{j=0,\ldots,N_{K}}
, with

H_{j}=\frac{\partial\log\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Z_{\text{WL}}\bigl(\big.\beta,s,N_{t},N_{s},\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{f}}\right\}\big.\bigr)}}\right)}{\partial f_{j}}{}\ ,

(26)

is approximately flat. After appropriate values for
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{f}}\right\}
have been found with the WL method, one can start to accumulate high statistics for the histogram
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{H}}\right\}
while keeping the
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{f}}\right\}
fixed, and obtain an improved estimator for the free energy difference by setting:

F_{N_{K}}-F_{0}=f_{N_{K}}-f_{0}+\log(H_{N_{K}})-\log(H_{0})\ .

(27)

Error bars for Eq. (
27
) can be obtained from the histograms in Eq. (
26
) using the jackknife method.

5
Results

We briefly report here some first results obtained with our new method for
\operatorname{SU}(3)
in (3+1) dimensions. In Fig.
5
we show the derivative of the entanglement entropy from Eq. (
24
) as function of the slab-width
l
. The data was obtained on lattices of size
s\,N_{t}\times N_{s}^{3}
with
s=2
,
N_{t}=16
and
N_{s}=16
at
\beta\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{5.7,\,5.75,\,5.8,\,5.85}}\right\}
. We try to fit the data for
l<0.5\,\mathrm{fm}
to the form
y=c\,\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x-x_{0}}}\right)^{-3}
and find
c=0.090(2)
and
x_{0}=0.077(2)\,\mathrm{fm}
. The inclusion of the shift
x_{0}
is to some extent arbitrary as from holography one expects
x_{0}=0
, i.e. a pure power law
y\sim x^{-3}
.

As discussed in
[
3
]
, a more sensible quantity to consider instead of Eq. (
24
) itself is the the higher-dimensional analogue of the entropic c-function,

C\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(l}}\right)=\frac{l^{3}}{2\,N_{s}^{2}}\,\frac{\partial S_{EE}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(l,N_{t},N_{s}}}\right)}{\partial l}\ ,

(28)

which is expected to be proportional to the number of degrees of freedom (DOF) at scale
l
. If Eq. (
24
) would follow a pure power law
\sim l^{-3}
, then Eq. (
28
) would be constant. The authors of
[
3
]
, however, showed that their simulation results for Eq. (
28
) can be fitted reasonably well to the form

y=c\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\tan^{-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(b\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x-x_{0}}}\right)}}\right)}{\pi}+\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\ ,

(29)

which is qualitativley consistent with a change of DOFs from free gluons deep in the UV to gauge invariant objects (e.g. glueballs) at larger scales. As our data covers approximately the same simulation parameters as the
\operatorname{SU}(3)
data in
[
3
]
, also our data represents this transition region where DOFs change, and should therefore indeed not follow a pure power law
\sim l^{-3}
.

With the reduced errors in our data for Eq. (
28
), a collective fit of Eq. (
29
) to the data from different
\beta
-values does not appear to work out very well; we therefore show in Fig.
5
results from fitting Eq. (
29
) separately to the data for different
\beta
-values, revealing some systematic
\beta
-dependency.

6
Conclusions and outlook

We have presented a new method for determining Rényi entropies of spatial sub-regions in
\operatorname{SU}(N)
gauge theories with lattice Monte Carlo and performed some first tests with
\operatorname{SU}(3)
gauge theory in (3+1) dimensions. In (3+1) dimensions, the method is not yet fully optimized, as discussed in the last paragraph of Sec.
4
. However, as illustrated in Sec.
5
, the current implementation allows already for significantly reduced errors in the measurement of the second order Rényi entropy, used as approximation to the entanglement entropy on the lattice. In addition to the here discussed case, we have applied the method also to
\operatorname{SU}(2)
at finite temperature in (2+1) dimensions and to
\operatorname{SU}(2)
and
\operatorname{SU}(5)
in (3+1) dimensions at zero temperature.

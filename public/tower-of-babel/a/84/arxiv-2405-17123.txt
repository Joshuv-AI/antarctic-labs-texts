The Witten Index of massless ( d + 1 ) (d+1) -Dirac-Schrödinger Operators
Oliver Fürst

Abstract
We calculate the Witten index of a class of (non-Fredholm) Dirac-Schrödinger operators over ℝ d + 1 \mathbb{R}^{d+1} for d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 odd, and thus generalize known results for the case d = 1 d=1 . For a concrete example of the potential, we give a more explicit index formula, showing that the Witten index assumes any real number on this class of operators.

i.e. for
x,x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{1}}U^{A}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)=\mathbbm{i}A\left(x_{1}\right)U^{A}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right),

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{2}}U^{A}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)=-\mathbbm{i}U^{A}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)A\left(x_{2}\right),

\displaystyle U^{A}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)U^{A}\left(x_{2},x_{3}\right)=U^{A}\left(x_{1},x_{3}\right),

\displaystyle U^{A}\left(x,x\right)=1_{H}.

(37)

We also need to regularly extend a evolutions system
U^{A}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)
to the boundary of the real line, i.e. we need that
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}\cup\left\{\pm\infty\right\}
is permitted.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathbb{R}\ni x\mapsto A\left(x\right)\in B_{sa}\left(H\right)
a continuous function in the SOT of
H
, which additionally satisfies
A\in L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R},B_{sa}\left(H\right)\right)
. Then
U^{A}
extends in SOT of
H
to
\left(\mathbb{R}\cup\left\{\pm\infty\right\}\right)^{2}
. Moreover the evolution system properties (
3.1
) also extend in SOT to
x,x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in\mathbb{R}\cup\left\{\pm\infty\right\}
.

Proof.

We first introduce the limits

\displaystyle U^{A}\left(x,-\infty\right)=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}U^{A}\left(x,-n\right),\ x\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle U^{A}\left(+\infty,x\right)=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}U^{A}\left(n,x\right),\ x\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle U^{A}\left(+\infty,-\infty\right)=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}U^{A}\left(n,-n\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}U^{A}\left(+\infty,-n\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}U^{A}\left(n,-\infty\right),

(38)

which exist in the SOT of
H
, since for
\phi\in H
,

\displaystyle\left\|U^{A}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)\phi-\phi\right\|_{H}=\left\|\int_{x_{2}}^{x_{1}}\mathbbm{i}A\left(w\right)U^{A}\left(w,x_{2}\right)\phi{\rm d}w\right\|_{H}\leq\int_{x_{2}}^{x_{1}}\left\|A\left(w\right)\right\|_{B\left(H\right)}\left\|\phi\right\|_{H}{\rm d}w

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{x_{1},x_{2}\to+\infty,\ \text{or}\ x_{1},x_{2}\to-\infty}0,

(39)

which in turn, by the evolution properties of
U^{A}
, implies that the sequences in (
3
) are Cauchy sequences in the SOT of
H
. Moreover we note that the evolution properties

\displaystyle U^{A}\left(x_{1},x_{3}\right)

\displaystyle=U^{A}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)U^{A}\left(x_{2},x_{3}\right),

\displaystyle U^{A}\left(x,x\right)

\displaystyle=1_{H},

(40)

extend to
x,x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in\mathbb{R}\cup\left\{\pm\infty\right\}
. For differentiation in SOT we note for
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}h^{-1}\left(U^{A}\left(x+h,-\infty\right)-U^{A}\left(x,-\infty\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}h^{-1}\left(U^{A}\left(x+h,x\right)-1\right)U^{A}\left(x,-\infty\right)=\mathbbm{i}A\left(x\right)U^{A}\left(x,-\infty\right).

(41)

The analogous differentiability result also holds for
U^{A}\left(+\infty,x\right)
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\ni\left(x,y\right)\mapsto V\left(x,y\right)\in B_{sa}\left(H\right)

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{d}\ni x\mapsto U^{V}\left(x\right):=U^{V\left(x,\cdot\right)}\left(+\infty,-\infty\right),

\displaystyle\partial_{x^{i}}U^{V}\left(x\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}U^{V\left(x,\cdot\right)}\left(\infty,y\right)\mathbbm{i}\partial_{x^{i}}V\left(x,y\right)U^{V\left(x,\cdot\right)}\left(y,-\infty\right){\rm d}y.

Proof.

For

\displaystyle U^{V\left(x_{1},\cdot\right)}\left(y,z\right)-U^{V\left(x_{2},\cdot\right)}\left(y,z\right)=\left[U^{V\left(x_{2},\cdot\right)}\left(y,w\right)U^{V\left(x_{1},\cdot\right)}\left(w,z\right)\right]_{w=z}^{w=y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{i}\int_{z}^{y}U^{V\left(x_{2},\cdot\right)}\left(y,z\right)\left(V\left(x_{1},w\right)-V\left(x_{2},w\right)\right)U^{V\left(x_{1},\cdot\right)}\left(w,z\right){\rm d}w\xrightarrow{x_{2}\to x_{1}}0,

(44)

which holds in the SOT of

, since
\left\|U^{V\left(x,\cdot\right)}\left(y,z\right)\right\|_{B\left(H\right)}=1
, and the continuity of
V
in the first variable. Similarly for
\left(e_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{d}
an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle\partial_{x^{i}}U^{V}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{h\to 0}h^{-1}\left(U^{V}\left(x+he_{i}\right)-U^{V}\left(x\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{h\to 0}h^{-1}\left[U^{V\left(x,\cdot\right)}\left(\infty,y\right)U^{V\left(x+he_{i},\cdot\right)}\left(y,-\infty\right)\right]^{y=+\infty}_{y=-\infty}

(45)

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
t>0
, and
V:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow B\left(H\right)
a SOT-differentiable function Then
H_{t}^{V}\left(x,y\right)
given by

\displaystyle H_{t}^{V}\left(x,y\right)

\displaystyle:=q_{t}\left(x-y\right)U^{V}\left(x,y\right),

\displaystyle q_{t}\left(z\right)

\displaystyle:=\left(4\pi t\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{\left(x-y\right)^{2}}{4t}},

(46)

is the integral kernel of

in

Proof.

We use that
q_{t}
is the smooth heat kernel of

in

. We calculate

\displaystyle\partial_{t}H_{t}^{V}\left(x,y\right)=q_{t}^{\prime\prime}\left(x-y\right)U^{V}\left(x,y\right),

(47)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}H_{t}^{V}\left(x,y\right)=-\frac{x-y}{2t}H_{t}^{V}\left(x,y\right)+\mathbbm{i}V\left(x\right)H_{t}^{V}\left(x,y\right),

(48)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{2}H_{t}^{V}\left(x,y\right)=q_{t}^{\prime\prime}\left(x-y\right)U^{V}\left(x,y\right)-\mathbbm{i}\frac{x-y}{t}V\left(x\right)H_{t}^{V}\left(x,y\right)+\mathbbm{i}V^{\prime}\left(x\right)H^{V}_{t}\left(x,y\right)-V\left(x\right)^{2}H^{V}_{t}\left(x,y\right),

(49)

\displaystyle\left(\mathbbm{i}\partial_{x}+V\left(x\right)\right)^{2}H_{t}^{V}\left(x,y\right)=-q_{t}^{\prime\prime}\left(x-y\right)U^{V}\left(x,y\right)=-\partial_{t}H_{t}^{V}\left(x,y\right).

(50)

We also have for

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}H_{t}^{V}\left(x,y\right)f\left(y\right){\rm d}y=\int_{\mathbb{R}}q_{1}\left(w\right)U^{V}\left(x,x-\sqrt{t}w\right)f\left(x-\sqrt{t}w\right){\rm d}w\xrightarrow{t\searrow 0}U^{V}\left(x,x\right)f\left(x\right)=f\left(x\right),

(51)

which shows that

is the claimed integral kernel.
∎

The next simple Lemma recalls what form the integrand of formula (
52
), takes for commutative potentials in the
y
-direction. It follows from the fact that for pointwise commutative families
\mathbb{R}\ni y\mapsto A\left(y\right)
, the generated evolution system is the exponential
U^{A}\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)=e^{\mathbbm{i}\int_{y_{2}}^{y_{1}}A\left(u\right){\rm d}u}
,
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, as well as the application of a well-known differentiation formula for exponentials of operators.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow B_{sa}\left(H\right)
be continuous in SOT of
H
. Assume that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
the operators
V\left(x,y_{1}\right)
and
V\left(x,y_{2}\right)
commute. Let
v\left(x\right):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}V\left(x,y\right){\rm d}y
for

. Then
U^{V}=e^{iv}
, and

\displaystyle\left(U^{V}\right)^{-1}{\rm d}U^{V}=\mathbbm{i}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-\mathbbm{i}sv}\ {\rm d}v\ e^{\mathbbm{i}sv}{\rm d}s.

(62)

We now will construct a concrete class of potentials
V
in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, for which we can evaluate the right hand side of (
52
), and show that the Witten index of the associated Callias operator
D_{V}
may assume any real number for any odd

.

4
Example

It is apparent that a non-trivial example of index formula of Theorem
3.5
, requires
V
to be sufficiently non-commutative in the
x
-variable, due to the presence of the exterior product. An obvious choice would therefore be to consider
su\left(n\right)
-valued potentials
V
for
n
large enough. However the general commutator relations in
su\left(n\right)
are too complicated to calculate in general, so we consider as a subclass those potentials, for which its values are a (real) linear combination of Clifford-matrices. In the special case
d=3
these are however all
su\left(2\right)
-matrices.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\left(\sigma^{j}\right)_{j=1}^{d}
be Clifford matrices of rank
r^{\prime}
. Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded smooth function with bounded derivatives, such that

\displaystyle\partial_{x^{i}}F\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=O\left(\left|x\right|^{-1}\right),\ \left|x\right|\to\infty,

\displaystyle\exists\epsilon>0:\ \partial_{R}F\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=O\left(\left|x\right|^{-1-\epsilon}\right),\ \left|x\right|\to\infty,

(63)

and that for any
\widehat{x}\in S_{1}\left(0\right)
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
\left|\gamma\right|\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}R^{\left|\gamma\right|}\left|\left(\partial^{\gamma}F\right)\left(x_{0}+R\widehat{x}\right)-\left(\partial^{\gamma}F\right)\left(R\widehat{x}\right)\right|=0.

(64)

Denote by
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime}\times r^{\prime}}
the (hermitian-valued) function

\displaystyle\omega:=\mathbbm{i}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sigma^{j}F_{j},

(65)

and if
\left|F\right|>0
,

\displaystyle\widehat{\omega}:=\left|F\right|^{-1}\omega,\ \eta:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{F_{j}}{\left|F\right|}{\rm d}F_{j}.

(66)

Set
V\left(x,y\right):=\omega\left(x\right)\phi\left(y\right)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}
, for some function
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi\left(y\right){\rm d}y=1
.

Remark 4.2
.

For the above choice of
V
, the induced Dirac-Schrödinger operator
D_{V}
is
not
Fredholm. Indeed, although
D_{V}
is elliptic, the full symbol of
D_{V}
is not (uniformly) invertible away from
\left(x,y\right)=0
, which is necessary for Fredholmness, according to Theorem 1 in
[
2
]
.

For this choice of
V
, the index formula can be evaluated more concretely.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
V
be as in Definition
4.1
. Then

\displaystyle\ind_{W}D_{V}=\left(2\pi\right)^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}\frac{\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right)!}{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left(\left|F\right|+d-1\right)\left(\cos\left(2\left|F\right|\right)-1\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}{\left|F\right|^{d}}\det\mathrm{D}F\ {\rm d}x,

(67)

where
\mathrm{D}F
is the Jacobian of
F
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.6
, we have

\displaystyle\left(U^{V}\right)^{-1}{\rm d}U^{V}=\mathbbm{i}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-\mathbbm{i}s\omega}\ {\rm d}\omega\ e^{\mathbbm{i}s\omega}{\rm d}s.

(68)

Because of the Clifford anti-commutator relations, we have

\displaystyle e^{\mathbbm{i}s\omega}=\cos\left(s\left|F\right|\right)+\mathbbm{i}\sin\left(s\left|F\right|\right)\widehat{\omega},\ s\in\mathbb{R},

(69)

and thus,

\displaystyle\left(U^{V}\right)^{-1}{\rm d}U^{V}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(\mathbbm{i}\cos\left(2s\left|F\right|\right){\rm d}\omega-\sin\left(2s\left|F\right|\right)\left(\eta-\widehat{\omega}{\rm d}\omega\right)+\mathbbm{i}\left(1-\cos\left(2s\left|F\right|\right)\right)\widehat{\omega}\eta\right){\rm d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A{\rm d}\omega-B\eta+B\widehat{\omega}{\rm d}\omega+\left(\mathbbm{i}-A\right)\widehat{\omega}\eta,

(70)

where

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle:=\mathbbm{i}\frac{\sin\left(2\left|F\right|\right)}{2\left|F\right|},

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle:=\frac{1-\cos\left(2\left|F\right|\right)}{2\left|F\right|}.

(71)

Taking the square we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\left(U^{V}\right)^{-1}{\rm d}U^{V}\right)^{\wedge 2}=-\left|F\right|^{-1}B{\rm d}\omega\wedge{\rm d}\omega+2\mathbbm{i}B\eta\wedge{\rm d}\omega+\left(2\left|F\right|^{-1}B+2\mathbbm{i}A\right)\widehat{\omega}\eta\wedge{\rm d}\omega,

(72)

and thus for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, one inductively shows,

\displaystyle\left(\left(U^{V}\right)^{-1}{\rm d}U^{V}\right)^{\wedge 2k}=

\displaystyle\left(-\left|F\right|^{-1}B\right)^{k}{\rm d}\omega^{\wedge 2k}-2\mathbbm{i}k\left|F\right|\left(-\left|F\right|^{-1}B\right)^{k}\eta\wedge{\rm d}\omega^{\wedge\left(2k-1\right)}

\displaystyle+\left(-2k\left(-\left|F\right|^{-1}B\right)^{k}+2\mathbbm{i}kA\left(-\left|F\right|^{-1}B\right)^{k-1}\right)\widehat{\omega}\eta\wedge{\rm d}\omega^{\wedge\left(2k-1\right)}.

(73)

Since
d
is odd we conclude

\displaystyle\left(\left(U^{V}\right)^{-1}{\rm d}U^{V}\right)^{\wedge d}=A\left(-\left|F\right|^{-1}B\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}{\rm d}\omega^{\wedge d}+\left(-B+\left(d-1\right)\mathbbm{i}A\right)\left(-\left|F\right|^{-1}B\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\eta\wedge{\rm d}\omega^{\wedge\left(d-1\right)}

\displaystyle+B\left(-\left|F\right|^{-1}B\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\widehat{\omega}{\rm d}\omega^{\wedge d}

\displaystyle+\left(-\left(d-1\right)A\left(-\left|F\right|^{-1}B\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}+\mathbbm{i}\left(d-1\right)A^{2}\left(-\left|F\right|^{-1}B\right)^{\frac{d-3}{2}}+\mathbbm{i}\left(d-1\right)B\left|F\right|\left(-\left|F\right|^{-1}B\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left(\mathbbm{i}-A\right)\left(-\left|F\right|^{-1}B\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\right)\widehat{\omega}\eta\wedge{\rm d}\omega^{\wedge\left(d-1\right)}.

(74)

We want to apply the trace
\mathrm{tr}_{\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime}}}
to (
4
). Since
\widehat{\omega}{\rm d}\omega+{\rm d}\omega\widehat{\omega}=2\eta
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime}}}\widehat{\omega}{\rm d}\omega^{\wedge d}=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}_{\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime}}}\left(\widehat{\omega}{\rm d}\omega+{\rm d}\omega\widehat{\omega}\right)\wedge{\rm d}\omega^{\wedge\left(d-1\right)}=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime}}}\eta\wedge{\rm d}\omega^{\wedge\left(d-1\right)}.

(75)

On the other hand we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime}}}\eta\wedge{\rm d}\omega^{\wedge\left(d-1\right)}=\left(-1\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\eta\wedge\sum_{\alpha\in\left\{1,\ldots,d\right\}^{d-1}}\mathrm{tr}_{\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime}}}\left(\sigma^{\alpha_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot\sigma^{\alpha_{d-1}}\right){\rm d}F_{\alpha_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge{\rm d}F_{\alpha_{d-1}}=0,

(76)

because the trace on the right hand side vanishes if the
\alpha_{j}
are pairwise different, while the exterior product vanishes if there are
k,l\in\left\{1,\ldots,d-1\right\}
with
\alpha_{k}=\alpha_{l}
. We calculate

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime}}}{\rm d}\omega^{\wedge d}

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{i}^{d}\kappa_{\sigma}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{S}_{d}}\left(-1\right)^{\left|\alpha\right|+\left|\beta\right|}\partial_{x^{\alpha_{1}}}F_{\beta_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot\partial_{x^{\alpha_{d}}}F_{\beta_{d}}\ {\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{i}^{d}d!\kappa_{\sigma}\det\mathrm{D}F\ {\rm d}x,

(77)

where
\mathrm{D}F
is the Jacobian of
F
. Finally we determine

\displaystyle tr_{\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime}}}\widehat{\omega}\eta\wedge{\rm d}\omega^{\wedge\left(d-1\right)}

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{i}^{d}\kappa_{\sigma}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{S}_{d}}\left(-1\right)^{\left|\alpha\right|+\left|\beta\right|}\frac{F_{\beta_{1}}F_{\alpha_{1}}}{\left|F\right|^{2}}\partial_{\alpha_{1}}F_{\beta_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot\partial_{\alpha_{d}}F_{\beta_{d}}\ {\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{i}^{d}\left(d-1\right)!\kappa_{\sigma}\det\mathrm{D}F\ {\rm d}x.

(78)

We apply
\mathrm{tr}_{\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime}}}
to (
4
), and obtain, expanding
A
and
B
,

\displaystyle tr_{\mathbb{C}^{r^{\prime}}}\left(\left(U^{V}\right)^{-1}{\rm d}U^{V}\right)^{\wedge d}=

\displaystyle-2^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\mathbbm{i}^{d-1}\left(d-1\right)!\kappa_{\sigma}\left|F\right|^{-d}\left(\cos\left(2\left|F\right|\right)-1\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}

\displaystyle\left(d-1+\left|F\right|\right)\det\mathrm{D}F\ {\rm d}x.

(79)

Thus, with the choices
r=r^{\prime}=2^{\frac{d-1}{2}}
, we apply the index formula of Theorem
3.5
,

\displaystyle\ind_{W}D_{V}=\left(2\pi\right)^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}\frac{\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right)!}{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left(\left|F\right|+d-1\right)\left(\cos\left(2\left|F\right|\right)-1\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}{\left|F\right|^{d}}\det\mathrm{D}F\ {\rm d}x.

(80)

Note that the integrand extends continuously to
F=0
.
∎

The transformation theorem directly implies the following corollaries.

Corollary 4.4
.

Assume additionally to Definition
4.1
that
F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}

(81)

.

\displaystyle\ind_{W}D_{V_{\Phi}}=\ind_{W}D_{V}.

(82)

Corollary 4.6
.

For
s>1

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}\ni C\mapsto\ind_{W}D_{V_{C}}\in\mathbb{R},

(83)

is surjective.

Proof.

For
C=0
we get trivially
\ind_{W}D_{V_{C}}=0
. For

\displaystyle\mathrm{sgn}\left(\det\mathrm{D}F_{C}\left(x\right)\right)=\mathrm{sgn}\left(\det\mathrm{D}F_{C}\left(0\right)\right)=\mathrm{sgn}\left(C^{d}\right)=\mathrm{sgn}\left(C\right).

(84)

By Lemma
4.3
, and the transformation theorem we have

\displaystyle\ind_{W}D_{V_{C}}

\displaystyle=\left(2\pi\right)^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}\frac{\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right)!}{d}\mathrm{sgn}\left(C\right)\int_{B_{r_{C}}\left(0\right)}\frac{\left(\left|y\right|+d-1\right)\left(\cos\left(2\left|y\right|\right)-1\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}{\left|y\right|^{d}}{\rm d}y

Bochner-Kodaira Formulas and the Type IIA Flow 1 1 1 Work supported in part by the National Science Foundation Grants DMS-1855947 and DMS-1809582.


Abstract
A new derivation of the flow of metrics in the Type IIA flow is given. It is adapted to the formulation of the flow as a variant of a Laplacian flow, and it uses the projected Levi-Civita connection of the metrics themselves instead of their conformal rescalings.



Teng Fei, Duong H. Phong, Sebastien Picard,
and Xiangwen Zhang

1
Introduction

The search for supersymmetric compactifications of string theories has revealed itself to have deep connections with special geometry. The resulting non-linear partial differential equations also turned out to be quite rich and interesting in their own right
(see e.g.
[
5
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
18
,
21
]
). One invariable feature of particular interest in these equations is the presence of a cohomological constraint. When no
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma is available, the most natural implementation of these cohomological constraints is by a geometric flow, and this has resulted in considerable interest in the investigation of such flows in recent years
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
6
,
8
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
19
,
20
]
.

The present paper is mainly concerned with the Type IIA flow, which is a flow in symplectic geometry introduced in
[
7
]
and motivated by the Type IIA string. More specifically, let
(M,\omega)
be a compact
6
-dimensional symplectic manifold and
\rho_{A}
be the Poincaré dual to a finite combination of Lagrangians. Then the Type IIA flow is the flow of
3
-forms
\varphi
given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi=d\Lambda d(|\varphi|^{2}\star\varphi)-\rho_{A}

(1.1)

with an initial data
\varphi_{0}
which is a closed, primitive, and positive
3
-form on
M
. Here
\Lambda
is the Hodge contraction operator defined by
\omega
, and
\star
and
|\varphi|
are the Hodge star operator and the norm of
\varphi
with respect to the metric
g_{\varphi}
which is compatible with
\omega
and the almost-complex structure
J_{\varphi}
constructed by Hitchin
[
14
]
(see §
2
for the precise definitions). The Type IIA flow preserves the primitiveness and closedness of
\varphi
, so that its stationary points are automatically solutions of the system first investigated by Tseng and Yau
[
23
]
. This system is itself a basic case of the more general equations for supersymmetric compactifications of the Type IIA string proposed in
[
13
,
22
]
.

In
[
7
]
it was shown that the Type IIA flow admits at least short-time existence, and can be continued as long as
|\varphi|
and the Riemannian curvature of
g_{\varphi}
remain bounded. The proof of this last assertion relied heavily on determining the flow of
g_{\varphi}
. This was one of the main results of
[
7
]
, and it was established using the original formulation (
1.1
) of the Type IIA flow, and the projected Levi-Civita connection
\tilde{\mathfrak{D}}
of a metric
\tilde{g}_{\varphi}
conformal to
g_{\varphi}
(see (
2.2
below). A key point was that, with respect to
\tilde{\mathfrak{D}}
, the manifold
M
has SU(3) holonomy, and
the form
|\Omega|_{\tilde{g}_{\varphi}}^{-1}\Omega
, with
\Omega=\varphi+i\star\varphi
, is covariant constant.

The main goal of the present paper is to provide a different derivation of the flow of the metrics
g_{\varphi}
in the Type IIA flow. The new derivation differs from the one in
[
7
]
in two important aspects. The first aspect is that it relies on Bochner-Kodaira formulas and a different formulation of the Type IIA flow, which is closer in spirit to Bryant’s
G_{2}
flow. From this point of view, it is more easily adaptable to other Laplacian flows.
The second aspect is that it relies instead on the projected Levi-Civita connection
{\mathfrak{D}}
of
g_{\varphi}
, which is a very natural connection since it coincides with all the unitary Hermitian connections with respect to
g_{\varphi}
on the Gauduchon line. An important additional benefit of this second derivation is that it provides a check on the formulas obtained in
[
7
]
, which is non-trivial because the calculations in both approaches are particularly long and involved.

For simplicity, we focus on the source-free case
\rho_{A}=0
. Then we have

Theorem 1

Let
(M,\omega)
be a
6
-dimensional symplectic manifold, and let
t\to\varphi(t)
by the Type IIA flow of
3
-forms defined in (
1.1
) with
\rho_{A}=0
. If
g_{ij}=(g_{\varphi})_{ij}
is the corresponding flow of metrics, then we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g_{ij}=-|\varphi|^{2}\left\{2R_{ij}-2\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\,{\rm log}\,|\varphi|^{2}+4(N^{2}_{-})_{ij}-\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{Ji}\alpha_{Jj}+4\alpha_{p}(N_{j}{}^{p}{}_{i}+N_{i}{}^{p}{}_{j})\right\}

(1.2)

where
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection of
g
,
R_{ij}
is the Ricci curvature,
N
is the Nijenhuis tensor with respect to the almost-complex structure
J_{\varphi}
,

(N_{-}^{2})_{ij}=N^{\lambda p}{}_{i}N_{p\lambda j}
, and
\alpha
is the 1-form defined by
\alpha=-d\,{\rm log}\,|\varphi|^{2}
.

2
Background material

We begin by providing a brief summary of the setting for the Type IIA flow,
which is Type IIA geometry as introduced in
[
7
]
.

2.1
Type IIA geometry

Let
M
be an oriented
6
-manifold. In
[
14
]
, Hitchin has shown how to associate to any non-degenerate
3
-form
\varphi
an almost-complex structure
J_{\varphi}
. Type IIA geometry arises if, in addition,
M
is equipped with a fixed symplectic form
\omega
and
\varphi
is a closed form which is primitive and positive with respect to
\omega
. The primitive condition means that
\Lambda\varphi=0
, where
\Lambda:A^{k}(M)\to A^{k-2}(M)
is the standard Hodge contraction operator with respect to
\omega
. It is shown in
[
7
]
that
\omega
is then preserved by
J_{\varphi}
, and the positivity condition means that the resulting Hermitian form
g_{\varphi}(X,Y)=\omega(X,J_{\varphi}Y)
is positive definite and defines a metric. Thus
(J_{\varphi},g_{\varphi},\omega)
is an almost-Kähler manifold. However, the condition in Type IIA geometry that this almost-Kähler structure arise from a closed
3
-form results in many subtle properties which are essential for the Type IIA flow.

Explicitly, the metric
g_{\varphi}
is given by

\displaystyle(g_{\varphi})_{ij}=-|\varphi|^{-2}\varphi_{iab}\varphi_{jkp}\omega^{ak}\omega^{bp}

(2.1)

where
|\varphi|
is the norm of the
3
-form
\varphi
with respect to
J_{\varphi}
, and
\omega^{ak}
is the inverse of the symplectic form
\omega
,
\omega^{ak}\omega_{kp}=\delta^{a}{}_{p}
. The volume form of
g_{\varphi}
is the same as
\omega^{3}/3!
.
The following metric
\tilde{g}_{\varphi}
conformally equivalent to
g_{\varphi}
also plays an important role in Type IIA geometry,

\displaystyle(\tilde{g}_{\varphi})_{ij}=|\varphi|^{2}(g_{\varphi})_{ij}=-\varphi_{iab}\varphi_{jkp}\omega^{ak}\omega^{bp}.

(2.2)

In fact, one of the defining features of Type IIA geometry is that the manifold
(M,J_{\varphi})
have SU(3) holonomy with respect to the projected Levi-Civita connection
\tilde{\mathfrak{D}}
of
\tilde{g}_{\varphi}
. More precisely,
set

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}=\star\varphi=J\varphi

(2.3)

and let
\Omega
be the
(3,0)
-form defined by

\displaystyle\Omega=\varphi+i\hat{\varphi}.

(2.4)

Then
|\Omega|_{\tilde{g}_{\varphi}}^{-1}\,\Omega
is covariantly constant with respect to
\tilde{\mathfrak{D}}
. This was a major reason why the calculations in
[
7
]
were mostly carried out with the connection
\tilde{\mathfrak{D}}
.

In the present paper, we shall use instead the unitary connections with respect to
g_{\varphi}
. Since
\omega
is closed, the Gauduchon line of Hermitian unitary connections with respect to
J_{\varphi}
collapses to a single connection, which can be viewed as either the Chern connection or the projected Levi-Civita connection
{\mathfrak{D}}
of
g_{\varphi}
. Henceforth we drop the subindex
\varphi
when there is no possibility of confusion, and denote
g_{\varphi}
,
\tilde{g}_{\varphi}
,
J_{\varphi}
simply by
g
,
\tilde{g}
and
J
. Then the Levi-Civita connection
{\nabla}
and the projected Levi-Civita connection
{\mathfrak{D}}
of
g
are related by

{\mathfrak{D}}_{i}X^{m}=\nabla_{i}X^{m}-N_{ip}{}^{m}X^{p}

(2.5)

where
N_{ip}{}^{m}
is the Nijenhuis tensor of
J
,

N^{k}{}_{ij}={1\over 4}(J^{r}{}_{i}\nabla_{r}J^{k}{}_{j}+J^{k}{}_{r}\nabla_{j}J^{r}{}_{i}-(i\leftrightarrow j)).

(2.6)

In
[
7
]
, we showed
{\mathfrak{D}}^{0,1}\Omega=0
and
{\mathfrak{D}}^{1,0}\Omega=-\alpha\otimes\Omega
(Equation (6.50) in
[
7
]
), or equivalently,

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{D}}_{m}\varphi={1\over 2}(-\alpha_{m}\varphi-\alpha_{Jm}\hat{\varphi}),\quad{\mathfrak{D}}_{m}\hat{\varphi}={1\over 2}(-\alpha_{m}\hat{\varphi}+\alpha_{Jm}\varphi).

(2.7)

Here the
1
-form
\alpha
is defined by

\displaystyle\alpha=-d\,{\rm log}\,|\varphi|^{2}

(2.8)

and we used the same notation introduced in
[
7
]
for any vector field
V
and any 1-form
W
,

V^{Jk}=(JV)^{k}=J^{k}{}_{p}V^{p},\quad W_{Jk}=(JW)_{k}=J^{p}{}_{k}W_{p}.

(2.9)

In particular,
\omega_{ij}=g_{Ji,j}
,
g_{ij}=\omega_{i,Jj}
, and
\omega^{ij}=g^{Ji,j}
,
g^{ij}=\omega^{i,Jj}
.

2.2
Identities from Type IIA geometry

We list here some identities required later. Except for (
2.2.3
), they were proved in
[
7
]
.

2.2.1
Identities for
\varphi

First, the action of
J
on
\varphi
is given by

\displaystyle\varphi_{ijk}=-\varphi_{Ji,Jj,k}=-\varphi_{Ji,j,Jk}=-\varphi_{i,Jj,Jk}

\displaystyle\varphi_{Ji,j,k}=\varphi_{j,Jj,k}=\varphi_{i,j,Jk}.

(2.10)

Next, bilinears in
\varphi
with two contractions with
\omega^{ij}
give the metric
g_{ij}
. But bilinears with a single contraction with either
\omega^{ij}
or
g^{ij}
simplify as well,

\displaystyle\omega^{ij}\varphi_{iab}\varphi_{jcd}={|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}(\omega_{ac}g_{bd}+\omega_{bd}g_{ac}-\omega_{bc}g_{ad}-\omega_{ad}g_{bc})

\displaystyle g^{ij}\varphi_{iab}\varphi_{jcd}={|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}(g_{ac}g_{bd}+\omega_{ca}\omega_{bd}-\omega_{ad}\omega_{cb}-g_{bc}g_{ad}).

(2.11)

As a consequence, we also have bilinear identities involving
\varphi
and
\hat{\varphi}
, for example

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}_{\lambda kp}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}=|\varphi|^{2}\omega_{\lambda i}.

(2.12)

This reduces to the previous identity by noting that
\hat{\varphi}_{\lambda kp}=-\varphi_{J\lambda,kp}
, so that

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}_{\lambda kp}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}=-\varphi_{J\lambda,kp}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}=|\varphi|^{2}g_{J\lambda,i}=|\varphi|^{2}\omega_{\lambda i}.

(2.13)

2.2.2
Identities for the Nijenhuis tensor

In general, the Nijenhuis tensor satisfies the following identities of a type
(0,2)
-tensor in the sense of Gauduchon
[
12
]

N^{k}{}_{Ji,j}=-N^{Jk}{}_{ij}=N^{k}{}_{i,Jj},\quad N_{Ji,j,k}=N_{i,Jj,k}=N_{i,j,Jk}.

(2.14)

Since
d\omega=0
, we also have the Bianchi identity

N_{ijk}+N_{jki}+N_{kij}=0.

(2.15)

From this it follows that there are two symmetric tensors quadratic in

N
, denoted by

(N^{2}_{+})_{ij}=N^{pq}{}_{i}N_{pqj},\quad(N^{2}_{-})_{ij}=N^{pq}{}_{i}N_{qpj}.

(2.16)

The relation between the Levi-Civita connection
{\nabla}
and the projected Levi-Civita connection
{\mathfrak{D}}
also implies, since
{\mathfrak{D}}J=0
,

\nabla_{i}J^{k}{}_{j}=-2N_{ij}{}^{Jk}.

(2.17)

In Type IIA geometry, we have

\displaystyle N_{-}^{2}=2\,N_{+}^{2}-{1\over 4}|N|^{2}g,\quad|N|^{2}=(N_{+}^{2})^{\lambda}{}_{\lambda}=2(N_{-}^{2})^{\lambda}{}_{\lambda}

(2.18)

where
|N|^{2}=N^{mkp}N_{mkp}
. We also have the following crucial identity between the Nijenhuis tensor and
\varphi
,

N^{p}{}_{ij}\varphi_{pkl}=-N^{p}{}_{kl}\varphi_{pij},

(2.19)

which was proved in
[
7
]
, Corollary 1.

2.2.3
Identities for the curvature tensor

We shall express the desired identities for the curvature tensor of the Levi-Civita connection in the following convention. The connection
{\nabla}
is written as

\nabla_{m}V^{k}=\partial_{m}V^{k}+\Gamma^{k}{}_{m\ell}V^{\ell}
, and the curvature tensor
R_{ij}{}^{k}{}_{\ell}
is defined by

\displaystyle[{\nabla}_{i},{\nabla}_{j}]V^{k}=R_{ij}{}^{k}{}_{\ell}V^{\ell}.

(2.20)

The Ricci curvature is then given by
R_{ij}=R_{ipj}{}^{p}
.

The first curvature identity that we require gives the action of
J
on
Rm
,

\displaystyle R_{j,i,Jk,J\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{jik\ell}+B_{ijk\ell}

\displaystyle B_{ijk\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2{\mathfrak{D}}_{i}N_{jk\ell}+2{\mathfrak{D}}_{j}N_{ik\ell}-2N^{\alpha}{}_{ij}N_{\alpha k\ell},

(2.21)

This identity can also be expressed as

R_{ji}{}^{p}{}_{J\ell}=R_{ji}{}^{Jp}{}_{\ell}+2{\mathfrak{D}}_{j}N_{i}{}^{Jp}{}_{\ell}-2{\mathfrak{D}}_{i}N_{j}{}^{Jp}{}_{\ell}-2N^{\mu}{}_{ji}N_{\mu\ell}{}^{Jp}.

(2.22)

To see this, we consider the action of
J
on a vector field
V
,

\displaystyle R_{jk}{}^{p}{}_{q}(JV)^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\nabla}_{j}{\nabla}_{k}(JV)^{p}-{\nabla}_{k}{\nabla}_{j}(JV)^{p}

(2.23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J[{\nabla}_{j},{\nabla}_{k}]V^{p}+({\nabla}_{j}{\nabla}_{k}J-{\nabla}_{k}{\nabla}_{j}J)^{p}{}_{\lambda}V^{\lambda}.

It follows that

\displaystyle R_{jk}{}^{p}{}_{q}J^{q}{}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J^{p}{}_{q}R_{kj}{}^{q}{}_{\lambda}+{\nabla}_{j}{\nabla}_{k}J^{p}{}_{\lambda}-{\nabla}_{k}{\nabla}_{j}J^{p}{}_{\lambda}

(2.24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J^{p}{}_{q}R_{kj}{}^{q}{}_{\lambda}-2{\nabla}_{j}(J^{p}{}_{\mu}N_{k\lambda}{}^{\mu})+2{\nabla}_{k}(J^{p}{}_{\mu}N_{j\lambda}{}^{\mu})

or, in more succinct notation,

R_{jk}{}^{p}{}_{J\lambda}=R_{jk}{}^{Jp}{}_{\lambda}-2{\nabla}_{j}(N_{k\lambda}{}^{Jp})+2{\nabla}_{k}(N_{j\lambda}{}^{Jp}).

(2.25)

We now convert
\nabla
derivatives into
{\mathfrak{D}}

derivatives. First lowering indices gives

R_{jik,J\ell}=-R_{j,i,Jk,\ell}+2{\nabla}_{j}(N_{i,\ell,Jk})-2{\nabla}_{i}(N_{j,\ell,Jk}).

(2.26)

Therefore

R_{j,i,Jk,J\ell}=R_{jik\ell}+2J^{p}{}_{k}{\nabla}_{j}(N_{i,\ell,Jp})-2J^{p}{}_{k}{\nabla}_{i}(N_{j,\ell,Jp}).

(2.27)

We write

\displaystyle 2J^{p}{}_{k}{\nabla}_{j}(N_{i,\ell,Jp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2J^{p}{}_{k}{\mathfrak{D}}_{j}(N_{i,\ell,Jp})-2J^{p}{}_{k}N_{ji}{}^{\mu}(N_{\mu,\ell,Jp})

(2.28)

\displaystyle-2J^{p}{}_{k}N_{j\ell}{}^{\mu}(N_{i,\mu,Jp})-2J^{p}{}_{k}N_{jp}{}^{\mu}(J^{n}{}_{\mu}N_{i\ell n})

Since
{\mathfrak{D}}J=0
,

\displaystyle 2J^{p}{}_{k}{\nabla}_{j}(N_{i,\ell,Jp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2{\mathfrak{D}}_{j}N_{i\ell k}+2N_{ji}{}^{\mu}N_{\mu\ell k}+2N_{j\ell}{}^{\mu}N_{i\mu k}-2N_{j,Jk}{}^{Jn}N_{i\ell n}

(2.29)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2{\mathfrak{D}}_{j}N_{ik\ell}+2N_{ji}{}^{\mu}N_{\mu\ell k}-2(N_{j\ell}{}^{\mu}N_{ik\mu}+N_{jk}{}^{\mu}N_{i\ell\mu})

This last term is symmetric in
(i,j)
. Therefore

\displaystyle 2J^{p}{}_{k}{\nabla}_{j}(N_{i,\ell,Jp})-(i\leftrightarrow j)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2{\mathfrak{D}}_{j}N_{ik\ell}-2{\mathfrak{D}}_{i}N_{jk\ell}+2N_{ji}{}^{\mu}N_{\mu\ell k}-2N_{ij}{}^{\mu}N_{\mu\ell k}

(2.30)

By the Bianchi identity

\displaystyle 2J^{p}{}_{k}{\nabla}_{j}(N_{i,\ell,Jp})-(i\leftrightarrow j)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2{\mathfrak{D}}_{j}N_{ik\ell}-2{\mathfrak{D}}_{i}N_{jk\ell}+2(-N^{\mu}{}_{ji}-N_{i}{}^{\mu}{}_{j})N_{\mu\ell k}

(2.31)

\displaystyle-2N_{ij}{}^{\mu}N_{\mu\ell k}

from which the desired identity (
2.2.3
) follows.

Finally, we shall need the following curvature identity specific to Type IIA geometry (see (6.53) in
[
7
]
),

R_{ij}=-{\mathfrak{D}}_{s}(N_{i}{}^{s}{}_{j}+N_{j}{}^{s}{}_{i})-2(N^{2}_{-})_{ij}+{1\over 2}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\,{\rm log}\,|\varphi|^{2}+{1\over 2}J^{p}{}_{i}J^{q}{}_{j}\nabla_{p}\nabla_{q}\,{\rm log}\,|\varphi|^{2}.

(2.32)

3
Proof of Theorem
1

We shall establish Theorem
1
using the formulation of the Type IIA flow as a Laplacian type flow
[
7
]

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi=-dd^{\dagger}(|\varphi|^{2}\varphi)+2d(|\varphi|^{2}N^{\dagger}\cdot\varphi)

(3.1)

where
N^{\dagger}:\Lambda^{3}(M)\to\Lambda^{2}(M)
is the operator defined by

\displaystyle(N^{\dagger}\cdot\varphi)_{kj}=N^{\mu}{}_{j}{}^{\lambda}\varphi_{\mu k\lambda}-N^{\mu}{}_{k}{}^{\lambda}\varphi_{\mu j\lambda}.

(3.2)

For our present purposes, it is convenient to rewrite the above expression as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi=-|\varphi|^{2}dd^{\dagger}\varphi-d|\varphi|^{2}\wedge d^{\dagger}\varphi+d(\iota_{{\nabla}|\varphi|^{2}}\varphi)+2d(|\varphi|^{2}N^{\dagger}\cdot\varphi).

(3.3)

We would like to determine
\partial_{t}g_{ij}
explicitly. For this, it is convenient to determine first
\partial_{t}\tilde{g}_{ij}
, since
\tilde{g}_{ij}
is a quadratic expression in
\varphi
, and
we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{g}_{ij}=-\left\{(\partial_{t}\varphi_{iab})\varphi_{jkp}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}+(i\leftrightarrow j)\right\}.

(3.4)

We shall determine in turn the contribution of each expression in (
3.3
) to
\partial_{t}\tilde{g}_{ij}
.

3.1
The Bochner-Kodaira formula for the Levi-Civita connection

We begin with the contribution of
|\varphi|^{2}dd^{\dagger}\varphi
using a Bochner-Kodaira formula. In general, if
M
is any compact Riemannian manifold and we express any
p
-form in components as

\displaystyle\varphi={1\over p!}\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{p}}\varphi_{i_{1}\cdots i_{p}}dx^{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{i_{p}}={1\over p!}\sum_{I}\varphi_{I}dx^{I}

(3.5)

with antisymmetric coefficients
\varphi_{i_{1}\cdots i_{p}}
, then the adjoint
d^{\dagger}
of the de Rham exterior differential with respect to a given metric
g_{ij}
is given by

\displaystyle(d^{\dagger}\varphi)_{I^{\prime}}=-g^{\ell m}\nabla_{m}\varphi_{\ell I^{\prime}},

(3.6)

where
\nabla
denotes the covariant derivative with respect to the Levi-Civita connection and we have split the index
I
into
I=(\ell,I^{\prime})
,

I^{\prime}=(i_{2},\cdots,i_{p})
. It follows that

\displaystyle(dd^{\dagger}\varphi)_{I}=-({\nabla}_{i_{1}}(g^{\ell m}{\nabla}_{m}\varphi_{\ell I^{\prime}})-\sum_{q=2}^{p}(i_{1}\leftrightarrow i_{q})).

(3.7)

Next, we have

\displaystyle(d\varphi)_{\ell I}={\nabla}_{\ell}\varphi_{I}-\sum_{q=1}^{p}(\ell\leftrightarrow i_{q})

(3.8)

and hence

\displaystyle(d^{\dagger}d\varphi)_{I}=-g^{\ell m}{\nabla}_{m}({\nabla}_{\ell}\varphi_{I}-\sum_{q=1}^{p}(\ell\leftrightarrow i_{q})).

(3.9)

Altogether, we obtain the version of the Bochner-Kodaira formula that we need,

\displaystyle((dd^{\dagger}+d^{\dagger}d)\varphi)_{I}=-g^{\ell m}{\nabla}_{m}{\nabla}_{\ell}\varphi_{I}+g^{\ell m}\sum_{q=1}^{p}[{\nabla}_{m},{\nabla}_{i_{q}}]\varphi_{\cdots i_{q-1}\ell i_{q+1}\cdots}

(3.10)

In the case of interest, namely
3
-forms
\varphi
with
d\varphi=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle dd^{\dagger}\varphi_{jkp}=-g^{\ell m}{\nabla}_{m}{\nabla}_{\ell}\varphi_{jkp}+g^{\ell m}\big\{[{\nabla}_{m},{\nabla}_{j}]\varphi_{kp\ell}+[{\nabla}_{m},{\nabla}_{k}]\varphi_{pj\ell}+[{\nabla}_{m},{\nabla}_{p}]\varphi_{jk\ell}\big\}.

(3.11)

3.2
The Laplacian term
g^{\ell m}{\nabla}_{m}{\nabla}_{\ell}\varphi_{jkp}

Recall that the covariant derivatives of
\varphi
with respect to the projected Levi-Civita connection
{\mathfrak{D}}
are given by (
2.7
). It follows that

\displaystyle g^{\ell m}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\ell}{\mathfrak{D}}_{m}\varphi=-{1\over 2}(\nabla_{\mu}\alpha^{\mu})\varphi

(3.12)

and

\displaystyle[{\mathfrak{D}}_{m},{\mathfrak{D}}_{\ell}]\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 2}(-{\mathfrak{D}}_{m}\alpha_{\ell}+{\mathfrak{D}}_{\ell}\alpha_{m})\varphi+{1\over 2}(-{\mathfrak{D}}_{m}\alpha_{J\ell}+{\mathfrak{D}}_{\ell}\alpha_{Jm})\hat{\varphi}

(3.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{1\over 2}\,N_{m\ell}{}^{j}\alpha_{j}\,\varphi+{1\over 2}\,N_{\ell m}{}^{j}\alpha_{j}\,\varphi+{1\over 2}(-{\mathfrak{D}}_{m}\alpha_{J\ell}+{\mathfrak{D}}_{\ell}\alpha_{Jm})\hat{\varphi}.

Now the difference between
\nabla
and
{\mathfrak{D}}
on vectors is given by (
2.5
). On
3
-forms, it is given by

\displaystyle{\nabla}_{\ell}\varphi_{jkp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{D}}_{\ell}\varphi_{jkp}-\varphi_{\lambda kp}N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda}-\varphi_{j\lambda p}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}-\varphi_{jk\lambda}N_{\ell p}{}^{\lambda}

(3.14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{D}}_{\ell}\varphi_{jkp}-E_{\ell;jkp},

where

E_{\ell;jkp}=\varphi_{\lambda kp}N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda}+\varphi_{j\lambda p}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}+\varphi_{jk\lambda}N_{\ell p}{}^{\lambda}.

(3.15)

Similarly, we write

\displaystyle{\nabla}_{m}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\ell}\varphi_{jkp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{D}}_{m}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\ell}\varphi_{jkp}-{\mathfrak{D}}_{\mu}\varphi_{jkp}N_{m\ell}{}^{\mu}-{\mathfrak{D}}_{\ell}\varphi_{\mu kp}N_{mj}{}^{\mu}-{\mathfrak{D}}_{\ell}\varphi_{j\mu p}N_{mk}{}^{\mu}-{\mathfrak{D}}_{\ell}\varphi_{jk\mu}N_{mp}{}^{\mu}

(3.16)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{D}}_{m}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\ell}\varphi_{jkp}-E_{m;\ell jkp},

and hence

\displaystyle g^{m\ell}{\nabla}_{m}{\nabla}_{\ell}\varphi_{jkp}=g^{m\ell}{\mathfrak{D}}_{m}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\ell}\varphi_{jkp}-g^{m\ell}E_{m;\ell jkp}-g^{m\ell}{\nabla}_{m}E_{\ell;jkp}.

(3.17)

We begin by computing the contributions of
g^{m\ell}{\nabla}_{m}E_{\ell;jkp}
,

\displaystyle g^{m\ell}{\nabla}_{m}E_{\ell;jkp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(g^{\ell m}{\nabla}_{m}\varphi_{\lambda kp})N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda}+(g^{\ell m}{\nabla}_{m}\varphi_{j\lambda p})N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}+(g^{\ell m}{\nabla}_{m}\varphi_{jk\lambda})N_{\ell p}{}^{\lambda}

(3.18)

\displaystyle+\varphi_{\lambda kp}g^{\ell m}{\nabla}_{m}N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda}+\varphi_{j\lambda p}g^{\ell m}{\nabla}_{m}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}+\varphi_{jk\lambda}g^{\ell m}{\nabla}_{m}N_{\ell p}{}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(g^{\ell m}{\mathfrak{D}}_{m}\varphi_{\lambda kp})N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda}+(g^{\ell m}{\mathfrak{D}}_{m}\varphi_{j\lambda p})N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}+(g^{\ell m}{\mathfrak{D}}_{m}\varphi_{jk\lambda})N_{\ell p}{}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle-g^{\ell m}(E_{m;\lambda kp}N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda}+E_{m;j\lambda p}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}+E_{m;jk\lambda}N_{\ell p}{}^{\lambda})

\displaystyle+\varphi_{\lambda kp}g^{\ell m}{\nabla}_{m}N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda}+\varphi_{j\lambda p}g^{\ell m}{\nabla}_{m}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}+\varphi_{jk\lambda}g^{\ell m}{\nabla}_{m}N_{\ell p}{}^{\lambda}.

3.2.1
Contributions of the terms
E_{\ell;jkp}

Consider the contributions of the second row on the right hand side of the last equation. Paired with
\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}
, it gives

\displaystyle g^{\ell m}E_{m;\lambda kp}N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda}\,\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\ell m}(\varphi_{\mu kp}N_{m\lambda}{}^{\mu}+\varphi_{\lambda\mu p}N_{mk}{}^{\mu}+\varphi_{\lambda k\mu}N_{mp}{}^{\mu})N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda}\,\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

(3.19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\rm I}+{\rm II}+{\rm III})\cdot\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

with

\displaystyle({\rm I})\cdot\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\ell m}\varphi_{\mu kp}N_{m\lambda}{}^{\mu}N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda}\,\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

\displaystyle({\rm II})\cdot\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\ell m}\varphi_{\lambda\mu p}N_{mk}{}^{\mu}N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda}\,\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

\displaystyle({\rm III})\cdot\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\ell m}\varphi_{\lambda k\mu}N_{mp}{}^{\mu}N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda}\,\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}.

(3.20)

Next, we have

\displaystyle({\rm I})\cdot\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}=-|\varphi|^{2}g^{\ell m}g_{\mu i}N_{m\lambda}{}^{\mu}N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda}=-|\varphi|^{2}N^{\ell}{}_{\lambda i}N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda}=|\varphi|^{2}(N_{+}^{2})_{ij}

(3.21)

and, using (
2.2.1
), we compute

\displaystyle({\rm II})\cdot\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}g^{\ell m}(\omega_{\lambda i}g_{\mu a}+\omega_{\mu a}g_{\lambda i}-\omega_{\lambda a}g_{\mu i}-\omega_{\mu i}g_{\lambda a})N_{mk}{}^{\mu}N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda}\omega^{ka}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}g^{\ell m}(\omega_{\lambda i}J^{k}{}_{\mu}-\delta^{k}{}_{\mu}g_{\lambda i}+\delta^{k}{}_{\lambda}g_{\mu i}-\omega_{\mu i}J^{k}{}_{\lambda})N_{mk}{}^{\mu}N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}(\omega_{\lambda i}N_{mk}{}^{Jk}N^{m}{}_{j}{}^{\lambda}-N_{mk}{}^{k}N^{m}{}_{j}{}^{\lambda}+N_{mki}N^{m}{}_{j}{}^{k}-\omega_{\mu i}N_{mk}{}^{\mu}N^{m}{}_{j}{}^{Jk}).

Now
N_{mk}{}^{k}=0
, and by the Nijenhuis tensor identities,

\displaystyle N_{mk}{}^{Jk}=N_{m,Jk}{}^{k}=N_{Jm,k}{}^{k}=0.

(3.22)

Furthermore, we have by definition
N_{mki}N^{m}{}_{j}{}^{k}=-(N_{+}^{2})_{ij}
, while

\displaystyle-\omega_{\mu i}N_{mk}{}^{\mu}N^{m}{}_{j}{}^{Jk}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}J^{\nu}{}_{i}N_{mk}{}^{\mu}N^{m}{}_{j}{}^{Jk}=N_{mk\,Ji}N^{m}{}_{j}{}^{Jk}=N_{m,Jk,i}N^{m}{}_{j}{}^{Jk}

(3.23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-N_{mki}N^{m}{}_{j}{}^{k}=(N_{+}^{2})_{ij}

and hence

\displaystyle({\rm II})\cdot\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}=0.

(3.24)

Since (
{\rm III}
) can be obtained from (
{\rm II}
) by the simultaneous interchange
a\leftrightarrow b
and
k\leftrightarrow p
, we also have

\displaystyle({\rm III})\cdot\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}=0.

(3.25)

We consider next the expression

\displaystyle g^{\ell m}E_{m;j\lambda p}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\ell m}(\varphi_{\mu\lambda p}N_{mj}{}^{\mu}+\varphi_{j\mu p}N_{m\lambda}{}^{\mu}+\varphi_{j\lambda\mu}N_{mp}{}^{\mu})N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

(3.26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\rm IV}+{\rm V}+{\rm VI})\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}.

The contributions of the term (IV) worked out to be
0
,

\displaystyle({\rm IV})\cdot\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}(\omega_{\mu i}g_{\lambda a}+\omega_{\lambda a}g_{\mu i}-\omega_{\mu a}g_{\lambda i}-\omega_{\lambda i}g_{\mu a})N_{mj}{}^{\mu}N^{m}{}_{k}{}^{\lambda}\omega^{ka}

(3.27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}(\omega_{\mu i}J^{k}{}_{\lambda}-\delta^{k}{}_{\lambda}g_{\mu i}+\delta^{k}{}_{\mu}g_{\lambda i}-\omega_{\lambda i}J^{k}{}_{\mu})N_{mj}{}^{\mu}N^{m}{}_{k}{}^{\lambda}.

The first two terms on the right hand side vanish individually, since

\displaystyle\omega_{\mu i}J^{k}{}_{\lambda}N_{mj}{}^{\mu}N^{m}{}_{k}{}^{\lambda}=\omega_{\mu i}N_{mj}{}^{\mu}N^{m}{}_{J\lambda}{}^{\lambda}=0

\displaystyle\delta^{k}{}_{\lambda}\,g_{\mu i}N_{mj}{}^{\mu}N^{m}{}_{k}{}^{\lambda}=N_{mj}{}^{\mu}N^{m}{}_{k}{}^{k}=0.

(3.28)

Of the remaining two terms, we have obviously

\displaystyle\delta^{k}{}_{\mu}g_{\lambda i}N_{mj}{}^{\mu}N^{m}{}_{k}{}^{\lambda}=N_{mj}{}^{k}N^{m}{}_{ki}=-N_{m}{}^{k}{}_{j}N^{m}{}_{ki}=-(N_{+}^{2})_{ij},

(3.29)

while

\displaystyle-\omega_{\lambda i}N_{mj}{}^{Jk}N^{m}{}_{k}{}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{\lambda\nu}J^{\nu}{}_{i}N_{mj}{}^{Jk}N^{m}{}_{k}{}^{\lambda}=J^{\nu}{}_{i}N_{mj}{}^{Jk}N^{m}{}_{k\nu}=N_{mj}{}^{Jk}N^{m}{}_{k,Ji}

(3.30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{mj}{}^{Jk}N^{m}{}_{Jk,i}=-N_{mj}{}^{k}N^{m}{}_{ki}=(N_{+}^{2})_{ij}

so they cancel each other out and we obtain, as claimed,

\displaystyle({\rm IV})\cdot\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}=0.

(3.31)

The next group of terms is given by

\displaystyle({\rm V})\cdot\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{j\mu p}N_{m\lambda}{}^{\mu}N^{m}{}_{k}{}^{\lambda}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}=-\varphi_{j\mu p}g^{\mu\nu}(N_{+}^{2})_{\nu k}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

(3.32)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}(\omega_{ji}g_{\mu a}+\omega_{\mu a}g_{ji}-\omega_{ja}g_{\mu i}-\omega_{\mu i}g_{ja})\omega^{ka}(N_{+}^{2})_{\nu k}g^{\mu\nu}.

The first term on the right produces
0
, since it can be computed as
\omega_{ji}\omega^{k\nu}(N_{+}^{2})_{\nu k}
. This term vanishes due to the anti-symmetrization of
k
and
\nu
.
We are left with

\displaystyle({\rm V})\cdot\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}(-\delta^{k}{}_{\mu}g_{ji}g^{\mu\nu}+\delta^{k}{}_{j}g_{\mu i}g^{\mu\nu}-\omega_{\mu i}J^{k}{}_{j}g^{\mu\nu})(N_{+}^{2})_{\nu k}

(3.33)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}(-|N|^{2}g_{ij}+(N_{+}^{2})_{ij}+(N_{+}^{2})_{Ji,Jj})

Since we have

\displaystyle(N_{+}^{2})_{Ji,Jj}=N^{mk}{}_{Ji}N_{mk,Jj}=-N^{m,Jk}{}_{i}N_{m,Jk,j}=N^{mk}{}_{i}N_{mkj}=(N_{+}^{2})_{ij}

(3.34)

we are left with

\displaystyle({\rm V})\cdot\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}={|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}|N|^{2}g_{ij}-{|\varphi|^{2}\over 2}(N_{+}^{2})_{ij}.

(3.35)

Finally, we observe that

\displaystyle({\rm VI})\cdot\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}=g^{\ell m}\varphi_{j\lambda\mu}N_{mp}{}^{\mu}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}=0.

(3.36)

We can readily see this in a complex frame. Since
\varphi\in\Lambda^{3,0}\oplus\Lambda^{0,3}
, the only components of
\varphi_{j\lambda\mu}
which are not
0
must have both barred or both unbarred indices. But the contraction with
g^{\ell m}
implies that the indices
\ell
and
m
must be mixed. But then for
N_{mp}{}^{\mu}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}
not to be
0
, the indices
\lambda
and
\mu
must be mixed too, contradicting the requirement that they must be both barred or both unbarred. This establishes our claim.

We still have one more contribution from the second row of (
3.18
), given by

\displaystyle g^{\ell m}E_{m;jk\lambda}N_{\ell p}{}^{\lambda}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

(3.37)

but which can be recognized as coinciding with the term that we just computed

\displaystyle g^{\ell m}E_{m;j\lambda p}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}={|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}|N|^{2}g_{ij}-{|\varphi|^{2}\over 2}(N_{+}^{2})_{ij}

(3.38)

upon the renaming of indices
a\leftrightarrow b
,
p\leftrightarrow k
.

It is convenient to summarize the formula which we have obtained as a lemma:

Lemma 1

We have

\displaystyle g^{\ell m}(E_{m;\lambda kp}N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda}+E_{m;j\lambda p}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}+E_{m;jk\lambda}N_{\ell p}{}^{\lambda})\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}={|\varphi|^{2}\over 2}|N|^{2}g_{ij}.

(3.39)

3.2.2
Contributions of the term
E_{m;\ell jkp}

The term
E_{m;\ell jkp}
involves
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\mu}\varphi_{jkp}
,
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\ell}\varphi_{\mu kp}
,
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\ell}\varphi_{j\mu p}
, and
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\ell}\varphi_{jk\mu}
. We use (
2.7
) to evaluate the contribution of each term in turn,

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{D}}_{\mu}\varphi_{jkp}N_{m\ell}{}^{\mu}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{1\over 2}(\alpha_{\mu}\varphi_{jkp}+\alpha_{J\mu}\hat{\varphi}_{jkp})N_{m\ell}{}^{\mu}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

(3.40)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 2}|\varphi|^{2}(\alpha_{\mu}g_{ji}-\alpha_{J\mu}\omega_{ji})N_{m\ell}{}^{\mu}

Upon symmetrization in
i
and
j
, and contracting with
g^{\ell m}
, we obtain

\displaystyle g^{\ell m}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\mu}\varphi_{jkp}N_{m\ell}{}^{\mu}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}+(i\leftrightarrow j)=|\varphi|^{2}\,g_{ij}\alpha_{\mu}g^{m\ell}N_{m\ell}{}^{\mu}=0

(3.41)

where we have used the fact that
N
is of type
(0,2)
to write

\displaystyle g^{m\ell}N_{m\ell}{}^{\mu}=g^{Jm,J\ell}N_{m\ell}{}^{\mu}=g^{m\ell}N_{Jm,J\ell}{}^{\mu}=-g^{m\ell}N_{m\ell}{}^{\mu}

and therefore

\displaystyle g^{m\ell}N_{m\ell}{}^{\mu}=0.

(3.42)

Next, we consider the term

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{D}}_{\ell}\varphi_{\mu kp}N_{mj}{}^{\mu}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{1\over 2}(\alpha_{\ell}\varphi_{\mu kp}+\alpha_{J\ell}\hat{\varphi}_{\mu kp})N_{mj}{}^{\mu}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

(3.43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 2}|\varphi|^{2}(\alpha_{\ell}g_{\mu i}-\alpha_{J\ell}\omega_{\mu i})N_{mj}{}^{\mu}={1\over 2}|\varphi|^{2}(\alpha_{\ell}N_{mji}+\alpha_{J\ell}N_{mj,Ji}).

The first term on the right symmetrizes to
0
. So does the second, using the fact that
N
is a type
(0,2)
-tensor, so that

N_{mj,Ji}=N_{Jm,j,i}
which is antisymmetric in the last two indices.

We consider now

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{D}}_{\ell}\varphi_{j\mu p}N_{mk}{}^{\mu}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{1\over 2}(\alpha_{\ell}\varphi_{j\mu p}+\alpha_{J\ell}\hat{\varphi}_{j\mu p})N_{mk}{}^{\mu}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}.

(3.44)

We work out separately the contributions of the two terms
\varphi_{j\mu p}
and
\hat{\varphi}_{j\mu p}
on the right hand side. First, we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{\ell}\varphi_{j\mu p}N_{mk}{}^{\mu}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}\alpha_{\ell}(\omega_{ji}g_{\mu a}+\omega_{\mu a}g_{ji}-\omega_{ja}g_{\mu i}-\omega_{\mu i}g_{ja})N_{mk}{}^{\mu}\omega^{ka}

(3.45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}\alpha_{\ell}(\omega_{ji}J^{k}{}_{\mu}-\delta^{k}{}_{\mu}g_{ji}+\delta^{k}{}_{j}g_{\mu i}-\omega_{\mu i}J^{k}{}_{j})N_{mk}{}^{\mu}.

We claim that, upon symmetrization in
i
and
j
, the net result is
0
. This is obviously true of the term
\omega_{ji}
, while
N_{mk}{}^{k}=0
and
N_{mji}
also symmetrizes to
0
. The fourth term can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\omega_{\mu i}J^{k}{}_{j}N_{mk}{}^{\mu}=-g_{\mu\nu}J^{\nu}{}_{i}N_{m,Jj}{}^{\mu}=-J^{\nu}{}_{i}N_{m,Jj,\nu}=N_{m,Jj,Ji}

(3.46)

which symmetrizes to
0
. We come to the contribution of the term involving
\hat{\varphi}
,

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}_{j\mu p}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varphi_{Jj,\mu p}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

(3.47)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}(\omega_{Jj,i}g_{\mu a}+\omega_{\mu a}g_{Jj,i}-\omega_{Jj,a}g_{\mu i}-\omega_{\mu i}g_{Jj,a})\omega^{ka}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}(-g_{ij}g_{\mu a}+\omega_{\mu a}\omega_{ji}+g_{aj}g_{\mu i}-\omega_{\mu i}\omega_{ja})\omega^{ka}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}(-g_{ij}J^{k}{}_{\mu}-\delta^{k}{}_{\mu}\omega_{ji}+J^{k}{}_{j}g_{\mu i}+\delta^{k}{}_{j}\omega_{\mu i}).

Dropping the term
\omega_{ji}
since it symmetrizes to
0
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}_{j\mu p}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}N_{mk}{}^{\mu}+(i\leftrightarrow j)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}(-g_{ij}N_{m,J\mu}{}^{\mu}+J^{k}{}_{j}N_{mki}+N_{mj}{}^{\mu}\omega_{\mu i})+(i\leftrightarrow j)

(3.48)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}(N_{m,Jj,i}-N_{mj,Ji})+(i\leftrightarrow j)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}(N_{Jm,j,i}-N_{Jm,j,i})+(i\leftrightarrow j)=0.

The last term
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\ell}\varphi_{jk\mu}
makes an identical contribution as
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\ell}\varphi_{j\mu p}
, upon renaming the summation indices
a\leftrightarrow b
,
k\leftrightarrow p
. Thus its contribution is also
0
. In summary, we have established

Lemma 2

We have

\displaystyle g^{m\ell}E_{m;\ell jkp}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}+(i\leftarrow j)=0.

(3.49)

3.2.3
Completion of the calculations for
{\nabla}^{\ell}E_{\ell;jkp}

The terms from
{\nabla}^{\ell}E_{\ell;jkp}
in (
3.18
) whose contributions we have not worked out as yet are the following

\displaystyle(g^{\ell m}{\mathfrak{D}}_{m}\varphi_{\lambda kp})N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda}+(g^{\ell m}{\mathfrak{D}}_{m}\varphi_{j\lambda p})N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}+(g^{\ell m}{\mathfrak{D}}_{m}\varphi_{jk\lambda})N_{\ell p}{}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle+\varphi_{\lambda kp}g^{\ell m}{\nabla}_{m}N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda}+\varphi_{j\lambda p}g^{\ell m}{\nabla}_{m}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}+\varphi_{jk\lambda}g^{\ell m}{\nabla}_{m}N_{\ell p}{}^{\lambda}.

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\lambda kp}(-{1\over 2}\alpha^{\ell}N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda}+{\nabla}^{\ell}N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda})-{1\over 2}\hat{\varphi}_{\lambda kp}\,\alpha_{Jm}g^{\ell m}N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle\ +\varphi_{j\lambda p}(-{1\over 2}\alpha^{\ell}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}+{\nabla}^{\ell}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda})-{1\over 2}\hat{\varphi}_{j\lambda p}\,\alpha_{Jm}g^{\ell m}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle\ +\varphi_{jk\lambda}(-{1\over 2}\alpha^{\ell}N_{\ell p}{}^{\lambda}+{\nabla}^{\ell}N_{\ell p}{}^{\lambda})-{1\over 2}\hat{\varphi}_{jk\lambda}\,\alpha_{Jm}g^{\ell m}N_{\ell p}{}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\ {\rm VII}+\hat{\rm VII}+{\rm VIII}+\hat{\rm VIII}+{\rm IX}+\hat{\rm IX}.

(3.50)

Again, we evaluate each contribution in turn. We have

\displaystyle({\rm VII})\cdot\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}=-|\varphi|^{2}g_{\lambda i}(-{1\over 2}\alpha^{\ell}N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda}+{\nabla}^{\ell}N_{\ell j}{}^{\lambda})=|\varphi|^{2}({1\over 2}\alpha^{\ell}N_{\ell ji}-{\nabla}^{\ell}N_{\ell ji})=0

(3.51)

upon symmetrization in
i\leftrightarrow j
. Next,

\displaystyle(\hat{\rm VII})\cdot\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{1\over 2}\alpha_{Jm}\varphi_{J\lambda,k,p}N^{m}{}_{j}{}^{\lambda}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

(3.52)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 2}|\varphi|^{2}\alpha_{Jm}\omega_{\lambda i}N^{m}{}_{j}{}^{\lambda}=-{1\over 2}|\varphi|^{2}\alpha_{Jm}N^{m}{}_{j,Ji}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{1\over 2}|\varphi|^{2}\alpha_{Jm}g^{m\ell}N_{\ell,j,Ji}=-{1\over 2}|\varphi|^{2}\alpha_{Jm}g^{m\ell}N_{J\ell,j,i}

which produces
0
upon symmetrization in
j
and
i
. Next,

\displaystyle({\rm VIII})\cdot\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{j\lambda p}(-{1\over 2}\alpha^{\ell}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}+{\nabla}^{\ell}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda})\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

(3.53)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}(\omega_{ji}g_{\lambda a}+\omega_{\lambda a}g_{ji}-\omega_{ja}g_{\lambda i}-\omega_{\lambda i}g_{ja})\omega^{ka}(-{1\over 2}\alpha^{\ell}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}+{\nabla}^{\ell}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}(\omega_{ji}J^{k}{}_{\lambda}-\delta^{k}{}_{\lambda}g_{ji}+\delta^{k}{}_{j}g_{\lambda i}-\omega_{\lambda i}J^{k}{}_{j})(-{1\over 2}\alpha^{\ell}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}+{\nabla}^{\ell}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}\big\{g_{ji}({1\over 2}\alpha^{\ell}N_{\ell\lambda}{}^{\lambda}-{\nabla}^{\ell}N_{\ell\lambda}{}^{\lambda})-{1\over 2}\alpha^{\ell}N_{\ell ji}+{\nabla}^{\ell}N_{\ell ji}-{1\over 2}\alpha^{\ell}N_{\ell,Jj,Ji}

\displaystyle-\omega_{\lambda i}J^{k}{}_{j}\nabla^{\ell}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}\big\}

Note that, the first two terms are zero because
N_{\ell\lambda}{}^{\lambda}=0
; the next three terms also adds up to
0
upon symmetrization in
i
and
j
. Indeed, the last term is also zero upon symmetrization in
i
and
j
because it is anti-symmetric about
i
and
j
as

\displaystyle\omega_{\lambda i}J^{k}{}_{j}\nabla^{\ell}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}=\omega_{\lambda i}g^{kp}J_{pj}\nabla^{\ell}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}=\omega_{\lambda i}\omega_{pj}\nabla^{\ell}(N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}g^{kp})=\omega_{\lambda i}\omega_{pj}\nabla^{\ell}N_{\ell}{}^{p\lambda}=-\omega_{\lambda j}\omega_{pi}\nabla^{\ell}N_{\ell}{}^{p\lambda}

The last identity is seen by switching indices
p\leftrightarrow\lambda
and using the antisymmetry of
N
.

The next term to be considered is

\displaystyle(\hat{\rm VIII})\cdot\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{1\over 2}\alpha_{Jm}\hat{\varphi}_{j\lambda p}g^{\ell m}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}={1\over 2}\alpha_{Jm}\varphi_{Jj,\lambda,p}g^{\ell m}N_{\ell k}{}^{\lambda}\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{|\varphi|^{2}\over 8}(\omega_{Jj,i}g_{\lambda a}+\omega_{\lambda a}g_{Jj,i}-\omega_{Jj,a}g_{\lambda i}-\omega_{\lambda i}g_{Jj,a})\omega^{ka}\alpha_{Jm}N^{m}{}_{k}{}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{|\varphi|^{2}\over 8}(-g_{ij}J^{k}{}_{\lambda}-\delta^{k}{}_{\lambda}\omega_{ji}+J^{k}{}_{j}g_{\lambda i}+\omega_{\lambda i}\delta^{k}{}_{j})\alpha_{Jm}N^{m}{}_{k}{}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{|\varphi|^{2}\over 8}(-g_{ij}\alpha_{Jm}N^{m}{}_{J\lambda}{}^{\lambda}-\alpha_{Jm}N^{m}{}_{\lambda}{}^{\lambda}+\alpha_{Jm}N^{m}{}_{Jj,i}-\alpha_{Jm}N^{m}{}_{j,Ji})

Using the fact that
N
is a tensor of type
(0,2)
, we readily see that each of these terms reduces to
0
.

In summary, the contribution of the Laplacian term is given by

Lemma 3

We have

\displaystyle(g^{\ell m}{\nabla}_{m}{\nabla}_{\ell}\varphi_{jkp})\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}+(i\leftrightarrow j)=|\varphi|^{2}\big\{\nabla_{\mu}\alpha^{\mu}+|N|^{2}\big\}g_{ij}.

3.2.4
Contributions of the curvature terms

Turning next to the curvature contributions, we write

\displaystyle g^{\ell m}[{\nabla}_{m},{\nabla}_{j}]\varphi_{kp\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g^{\ell m}(R_{mj}{}^{\lambda}{}_{k}\varphi_{\lambda p\ell}+R_{mj}{}^{\lambda}{}_{p}\varphi_{k\lambda\ell}+R_{mj}{}^{\lambda}{}_{\ell}\varphi_{kp\lambda})

(3.54)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-R^{\ell}{}_{j}{}^{\lambda}{}_{k}\varphi_{\lambda p\ell}-R^{\ell}{}_{j}{}^{\lambda}{}_{p}\varphi_{k\lambda\ell}+R_{j}{}^{\lambda}\varphi_{kp\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-R^{\ell}{}_{j}{}^{\lambda}{}_{k}\varphi_{\lambda p\ell}+R^{\ell}{}_{j}{}^{\lambda}{}_{p}\varphi_{\lambda k\ell}+R_{j}{}^{\lambda}\varphi_{kp\lambda}

We consider for the moment only the contribution of the last term.

\displaystyle R_{j}{}^{\lambda}\,\varphi_{kp\lambda}\,\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}=-|\varphi|^{2}R_{j}{}^{\lambda}\,g_{\lambda i}=-|\varphi|^{2}R_{ji}.

(3.55)

The next curvature contribution is similar

\displaystyle g^{\ell m}[{\nabla}_{m},{\nabla}_{p}]\varphi_{jk\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g^{\ell m}(R_{mp}{}^{\lambda}{}_{j}\varphi_{\lambda k\ell}+R_{mp}{}^{\lambda}{}_{k}\varphi_{j\lambda\ell}+R_{mp}{}^{\lambda}{}_{\ell}\varphi_{jk\lambda})

(3.56)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-R^{\ell}{}_{p}{}^{\lambda}{}_{j}\varphi_{\lambda k\ell}+R^{\ell}{}_{p}{}^{\lambda}{}_{k}\varphi_{\lambda j\ell}+R_{p}{}^{\lambda}\varphi_{jk\lambda}

and the corresponding last term gives

\displaystyle R_{p}{}^{\lambda}\varphi_{jk\lambda}\,\varphi_{iab}\omega^{ka}\omega^{pb}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}R_{p}{}^{\lambda}(\omega_{ji}g_{\lambda b}+\omega_{\lambda b}g_{ji}-\omega_{jb}g_{\lambda i}-\omega_{\lambda i}g_{jb})\omega^{pb}

(3.57)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}R_{p}{}^{\lambda}(\omega_{ji}J^{p}{}_{\lambda}-\delta^{p}{}_{\lambda}g_{ji}+\delta^{p}{}_{j}g_{\lambda i}-\omega_{\lambda i}J^{p}{}_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{|\varphi|^{2}\over 4}(-R\,g_{ji}+R_{ji}+R_{Jj,Ji})

where we have dropped the term proportional to
\omega_{ji}

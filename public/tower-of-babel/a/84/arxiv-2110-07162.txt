Local regularity near boundary for the Stokes and Navier-Stokes equations


Abstract
. We are concerned with local regularity of the solutions for the Stokes and Navier-Stokes equations near boundary. Firstly, we construct a bounded solution but its normal derivatives are singular in any L p L^{p} with 1 < p 1<p locally near boundary. On the other hand, we present criteria of solutions of the Stokes equations near boundary to imply that the gradients of solutions are bounded (in fact, even further Hölder continuous). Finally, we provide examples of solutions whose local regularity near boundary is optimal. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. primary 35Q30, secondary 35B65. Keywords and phrases: Stokes equations, Navier-Stokes equations, local regularity near boundary

1.
Introduction

We consider the non-stationary Stokes equations near flat boundary

(1.1)

u_{t}-\Delta u+\nabla\pi=0\qquad\mbox{div }u=0\quad\mbox{ in }\,B^{+}_{2}\times(0,4),

where
B^{+}_{r}:=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}:|x|<r,x_{n}>0\}
. Here, no-slip boundary condition is given only on the flat boundary, i.e.

(1.2)

u=0\quad\mbox{ on }\,\Sigma:=(B_{2}\cap\{x_{n}=0\})\times(0,4).

We can also consider similar situation for the Navier-Stokes equations, i.e.

(1.3)

u_{t}-\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla\pi=0,\qquad\mbox{div }u=0\quad\mbox{ in }\,B^{+}_{2}\times(0,4)

with the boundary condition (
1.2
).

Our concern is local analysis of the solutions of the equations (
1.1
) or (
1.3
) with (
1.2
) in
B^{+}_{1}\times(0,1)
. Unlike the heat equation, non-local
effect of the Stokes equations may cause limitation of local smoothing effects of solutions.
In fact, the second author showed that there exists a weak solution of the Stokes equations (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) whose normal derivative is unbounded near boundary, although
it is bounded and its derivatives are square integrable (see
[
13
]
), i.e.

\sup_{Q_{\frac{1}{2}}^{+}}\left|D_{x_{n}}u\right|=\infty,\qquad\sup_{Q_{1}^{+}}\left|u\right|+\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1}^{+})}<\infty,

where
Q^{+}_{r}:=B_{r}^{+}\times(1-r^{2},1),\,\,0<r<1
.

Seregin and S̆verák found a simplified example as the form of shear flow in a half-space to the Stokes equations (and Navier-Stokes equations as well) such that its gradient is unbounded near boundary, although velocity field is locally bounded (see
[
19
]
). It is noteworthy that the example in
[
19
]
is not of globally finite energy, and on the other hand, the constructed one in
[
13
]
has finite energy globally in a half-space (see
[
15
]
for the details).

Following similar constructions as in
[
13
]
,
even further, the authors constructed an example that
u
is integrable in
L^{4}_{t}L^{p}_{x}
but
\nabla u
is not square integrable (see
[
7
]
). More precisely, there exists a very weak solution of the Stokes equations (
1.1
) or the Navier-Stokes equations (
1.3
) with (
1.2
) such that

\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}(Q_{1}^{+})}=\infty,\qquad\left\|u\right\|_{L^{4}_{t}L^{p}_{x}(Q_{1}^{+})}<\infty,\quad p<\infty.

The notions of very weak solutions are given in Section 2 (see Definition
2.3
and Definition
2.11
).
With the aid of this construction, the authors also proved that Caccioppoli’s inequalities of Stokes equations and Navier-Stokes equations in general may fails near boundary
when only local boundary problems are considered (see
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
). It is a very important distinction in comparison to the interior case, where Caccioppoli’s (type) inequalities turn out to be true (compare to
[
11
]
and
[
26
]
, and refer also
[
12
]
for generalized Navier-Stokes flow).

One may ask how bad
\nabla u
could be, when
u
is bounded in a local domain near boundary.
One of our motivations in this paper is to answer to the question, and we obtain the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Then, there exists a very weak solution
u
of Stokes equations (
1.1
) or Navier-Stokes equations (
1.3
) with the boundary condition (
1.2
) such that

(1.4)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1}^{+})}<\infty,\qquad\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(Q^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}})}=\infty.

Remark 1.2
.

If we compare to the example constructed by Seregin and S̆verák, their solutions also show
singular normal derivatives near boundary not only pointwisely but also
L^{p}_{\rm loc}
,
p>3
, since
\partial_{x_{3}}u(x_{3},t)\geq cx_{3}^{-1+2\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
in the region near origin with
x_{3}^{2}\geq-4t
. Theorem
1.1
is an improvement of their result, since construction of singular normal derivatives in
L^{p}_{\rm loc}
is extended up to all
p>1
near boundary.

Remark 1.3
.

We do not know if
p
in (
1.4
) can be replaced by
1
in Theorem
1.1
, and thus we leave it as an open question.
In Appendix
A
, alternative proof is given for
p=2
in Theorem
1.1
, which seems informative.

On the other hand, the second motivation of the paper is to study optimal regularity
of the local problem near boundary, in case that the pressure
\pi
is locally integrable in
L^{p}
. We recall that it was shown in
[
18
, Proposition 2]
in three dimensions that
for given
p,q\in(1,2]
the solution of the Stokes equations (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) satisfies the following a priori estimate:
For any
r
with
p\leq r<\infty

(1.5)

\left\|u_{t}\right\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}(Q_{\frac{1}{4}}^{+})}+\left\|\nabla^{2}u\right\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}(Q_{\frac{1}{4}}^{+})}+\left\|\nabla\pi\right\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}(Q_{\frac{1}{4}}^{+})}\leq C\left(\left\|u\right\|_{L^{q}_{t}W^{1,p}_{x}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\left\|\pi\right\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}(Q_{1}^{+})}\right).

Furthermore, due to parabolic embedding, it follows that

(1.6)

\left\|u\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}_{t}{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}_{x}(Q_{\frac{1}{4}}^{+})}<\infty,\qquad 0<\alpha<2-\frac{2}{q}.

We remark that there are examples such that the Hölder continuity (
1.6
) is optimal. To be more precise, in case that
q<2
, it was proved in
[
15
]
that

(1.7)

\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q^{+}_{\frac{1}{4}})}=\infty.

Remark 1.4
.

For the case
q=2
, we can also show that (
1.7
)
is true, and the details of its verification will be given in Appendix
B
.

The above estimate (
1.5
) shows that integrability of
u_{t}
,
\nabla^{2}u
and
\nabla\pi

is increased for spatial variables, and it is, however, not clear if integrability in time could be improved or not.

Firstly, we extend the result of
[
18
, Proposition 2]
for the case
q>2
. In such case, an interesting feature is that not only velocity but also the gradient of velocity fields are Hölder continuous up to boundary, contrary to the case
1<q\leq 2
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
2<q<\infty
and
1<p<\infty
.
Suppose that
(u,\pi)
is solution for the Stokes equations (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) satisfying
\nabla^{2}u,\,\,u_{t}\in L^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}(Q_{1}^{+})
and
\pi\in L^{q}_{t}W^{1,p}_{x}(Q_{1}^{+})
.
Then, for any
r
with
p\leq r<\infty

(1.8)

\left\|u_{t}\right\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}(Q_{\frac{1}{4}}^{+})}+\left\|\nabla^{2}u\right\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}(Q_{\frac{1}{4}}^{+})}+\left\|\nabla\pi\right\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}(Q_{\frac{1}{4}}^{+})}\leq c\left(\left\|u\right\|_{L^{q}_{t}W^{1,p}_{x}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\left\|\pi\right\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{p}_{x}(Q_{1}^{+})}\right).

Furthermore, the derivative of
u
is Hölder continuous, i.e.

(1.9)

\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{{\mathcal{C}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}_{t}{\mathcal{C}}^{\alpha}_{x}(Q_{\frac{1}{4}}^{+})}<\infty,\qquad 0<\alpha<1-\frac{2}{q}.

In next theorem, we prove that the estimates (
1.5
) and (
1.8
) are indeed optimal. To be more precise, there is a solution of the Stokes equations (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) such that
\nabla u
and
\pi
belong to
L^{p}(Q_{1}^{+})
but

u_{t},\nabla\pi\notin L^{\tilde{p}}(Q_{\frac{1}{2}}^{+})
for any
\tilde{p}
with
\tilde{p}>p
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
1<p,\,q<\infty
.
Then, there exist a solution

u
of Stokes equations (
1.1
) and Navier-Stokes equations (
1.3
) with the boundary condition (
1.2
) such that

(1.10)

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{2}u\right\|_{L^{\frac{3q}{2}}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\left\|D_{t}u\right\|_{L^{q}(Q_{1}^{+})}+\left\|\nabla\pi\right\|_{L^{q}(Q_{1}^{+})}<\infty,

but for any
r_{1}>q
and
r_{2}>\frac{3q}{2}

(1.11)

\displaystyle\|D_{t}u\|_{L^{r_{1}}(Q_{\frac{1}{2}}^{+})}=\infty,\qquad\|\nabla\pi\|_{L^{r_{1}}(Q_{\frac{1}{2}}^{+})}=\infty,\qquad\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{r_{2}}(Q_{\frac{1}{2}}^{+})}=\infty.

Remark 1.7
.

We do not know
whether or not there exists a solution
u
of Stokes equations (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) such that
\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{r_{1}}(Q_{\frac{1}{2}}^{+})}=\infty
for
r_{1}>q
. In fact, our construction shows that
\nabla^{2}u\in L^{\frac{3q}{2}}(Q_{\frac{1}{2}}^{+})

Preliminaries

For notational convention, we denote
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})
, where the symbol

{\prime}
means the coordinate up to
n-1
, that is,

x^{\prime}=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdot\cdot\cdot,x_{n-1})
. We write
D_{x_{i}}u

as the partial derivative of
u
with respect to
x_{i},\,\,1\leq i\leq n
, i.e.,
D_{x_{i}}u(x)=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}u(x)
. Throughout this paper we denote by
c
various generic positive constant and by
c(*,\cdots,*)
depending on the quantities in the
parenthesis.

Let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. We define an
anisotropic homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{W}^{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
by

\displaystyle\dot{W}^{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})=\{f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})\,|\,f=h_{\alpha}*g,\quad\mbox{for some}\quad g\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})\}

with norm

\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})}:=\|g\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})}\,=\|h_{-\alpha}*f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})},

where

*
is a convolution in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
and
{\mathcal{S}}^{{}^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})

is the dual space of the Schwartz space

{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
.
Here
h_{\alpha}
is a distribution whose Fourier transform
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
is defined by

\displaystyle\widehat{h_{\alpha}}(\xi,\tau)=c_{\alpha}(4\pi^{2}|\xi|^{2}+i\tau)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}},\quad(\xi,\tau)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}.

In case that
\alpha=k\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
, we note that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{k,\frac{k}{2}}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})}

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots k_{n}+l=k}\|D_{x_{l}}^{k_{1}}D_{x_{2}}^{k_{2}}\cdots D_{x_{n}}^{k_{n}}D^{\frac{1}{2}l}_{t}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})},

where
\displaystyle D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}f(t)=D_{t}\int_{-\infty}^{t}\frac{f(s)}{(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}}ds
and
D^{k+\frac{1}{2}}_{t}f=D^{\frac{1}{2}}_{t}D^{k}_{t}f
. In particular, when
k=0
, it follows that
\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{0,0}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})}=\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})}
.

Next, we recall an anisotropic homogeneous Besov space
\dot{B}^{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}_{pq}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
. Let
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
such that
\hat{\phi}
, the Fourier transform of
\phi
. satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}&\displaystyle\hat{\phi}(\xi,\tau)>0\quad\mbox{ on }2^{-1}<|\xi|+|\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}}<2,\\
&\\
&\displaystyle\hat{\phi}(\xi,\tau)=0\quad\mbox{ elsewhere },\\
&\\
&\displaystyle\sum_{-\infty<i<\infty}\hat{\phi}(2^{-i}\xi,2^{-2i}\tau)=1\ ((\xi,\tau)\neq(0,0)).\end{array}\right.

We then introduce functions
\phi_{i}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}
in terms of
\phi
, which are defined by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\widehat{\phi_{i}}(\xi,\tau)&=\hat{\phi}(2^{-i}\xi,2^{-2i}\tau),\qquad i=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\cdots.\end{array}

Note that
\phi_{i}(x,t)=2^{(i+2)n}\phi(2^{i}x,2^{2i}t)
.
For
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
we define the anisotropic homogeneous Besov space

\dot{B}^{\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\alpha}_{pr}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
by

\displaystyle\dot{B}^{\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\alpha}_{pr}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})=\left\{f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{{}^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})\,|\,\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\alpha}_{pr}}<\infty\,\right\}

with the norms

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\alpha}_{pr}}:

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{-\infty<i<\infty}(2^{\alpha i}\|\phi_{i}*f\|_{L^{p}})^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}},\quad 1\leq r<\infty,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\alpha}_{p\infty}}:

\displaystyle=\sup_{-\infty<i<\infty}2^{\alpha i}\|\phi_{i}*f\|_{L^{p}}.

Let
I
be a open interval.
The anisotropic homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{W}^{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\times I)
in

\displaystyle\dot{W}^{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\times I)=\left\{f=F|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\times I}\,|\,F\in\dot{W}^{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}})\right\}

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{W}^{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}})}=\inf\left\{\|F\|_{\dot{W}^{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}})}\,|\,F\in\dot{W}^{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}}_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}),\,\,F|_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\times I}=f\right\}.

Lemma 3.2

\displaystyle|J_{2}|

Before we prove Theorem

Lemma 5.1

For

where

\displaystyle\Gamma*f(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}}\Gamma(x-y,t-s)f(y,s)dyds,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{*}*f(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}}\Gamma(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},x_{n}+y_{n},t-s)f(y,s)dyds.

Proof.

We prove only the case
\Gamma*f
, since the other case can be treated similarly. For
h\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}\Gamma_{t}*h\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq ct^{-\frac{k}{2}}\|h\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\quad 0<t<\infty,

where

is a non-negative integer and

\displaystyle\Gamma_{t}*h(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\Gamma(x-y,t)h(y)dy.

Using the property of real interpolation between

and
k=2

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Gamma_{t}*h\|_{\dot{B}^{\beta}_{pr}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}\leq ct^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\beta}{2}}\|h\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

Let
\tilde{f}(t)
be a zero extension of
f(t)
over
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Gamma*f(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{\beta}_{pr}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})}\leq\|\nabla\Gamma*\tilde{f}(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{\beta}_{pr}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\Gamma_{t-s}*\tilde{f}(s)\|_{\dot{B}^{\beta}_{pr}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}ds

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\beta}{2}}\|\tilde{f}(s)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}ds

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\beta}{2}}\|f(s)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})}ds.

Therefore, we complete the proof of Lemma

\displaystyle\|I_{11}\|_{L^{p_{0}}(Q^{+}_{1})}\leq c\|D_{t}g^{{\mathcal{T}}}_{n}\|_{L^{p}(0,1)}\|D_{y^{\prime}}g_{n}^{\mathcal{S}}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Hence, summing up the estimates, for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla w_{i}\|_{L^{p_{0}}(Q^{+}_{1})}<\infty.

From the first equality of (
3.2
), we obtain (
6.17
) for

. Hence, we complete the proof of (
6.14
).
It remains to show (
6.15
).
It follows from the above estimate that

(6.18)

\displaystyle\|\nabla w-I_{11}\|_{L^{r}(Q_{1}^{+})}\leq\infty\qquad 1<r<\infty.

Noting that for
\frac{3}{4}<t<1
,

\displaystyle|D_{t}g^{\mathcal{T}}_{n}(t)|

\displaystyle=(t-\frac{3}{4})^{-\frac{1}{q}}|\ln^{-1}(t-\frac{3}{4})|\left((1-\frac{1}{q})-\ln^{-1}(t-\frac{3}{4})\right)

\displaystyle\geq(1-\frac{1}{q})(t-\frac{3}{4})^{-\frac{1}{q}}|\ln^{-1}(t-\frac{3}{4})|.

Hence, for
\frac{3}{4}<t<1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma_{1}(x_{n},t-s)D_{s}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(s)ds\right|

\displaystyle\geq c\int_{\frac{3}{4}}^{\frac{1}{2}(t+\frac{3}{4})}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{x_{n}^{2}}{4(t-s)}}(s-\frac{3}{4})^{-\frac{1}{q}}\left|\ln^{-1}(s-\frac{3}{4})\right|ds

\displaystyle\geq c(t-\frac{3}{4})^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{x_{n}^{2}}{2(t-\frac{3}{4})}}\int_{\frac{3}{4}}^{\frac{1}{2}(t+\frac{3}{4})}(s-\frac{3}{4})^{-\frac{1}{q}}\left|\ln^{-1}(s-\frac{3}{4})\right|ds

\displaystyle=c(t-\frac{3}{4})^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{x_{n}^{2}}{2(t-\frac{3}{4})}}\int^{\frac{1}{2}(t-\frac{3}{4})}_{0}s^{-\frac{1}{q}}\left|\ln^{-1}(s)\right|ds.

By L’hopital’s Theorem, we have
\displaystyle\lim_{a\rightarrow 0}\frac{\int^{a}_{0}s^{-\frac{1}{q}}|\ln^{-1}(s)|ds}{a^{1-\frac{1}{q}}|\ln^{-1}(a)|}=\frac{q-1}{q}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma_{1}(x_{n},t-s)D_{s}g_{n}^{\mathcal{T}}(s)ds\right|

\displaystyle\geq c(t-\frac{3}{4})^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}e^{-\frac{x_{n}^{2}}{2(t-\frac{3}{4})}}\left|\ln^{-1}(t-\frac{3}{4})\right|.

Hence, for
r>p_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\|I_{11}\|^{r}_{L^{r}(Q^{+}_{\frac{1}{2}})}\geq c\int_{\frac{3}{4}}^{1}(t-\frac{3}{4})^{(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})r+\frac{1}{2}}\left|\ln^{-r}(t-\frac{3}{4})\right|dt\|\psi\|^{r}_{L^{r}(B^{\prime}_{\frac{1}{2}})}=\infty.

Thus, combining the estimate (
6.18
), we have (
6.15
).
We complete the proof of Corollary
6.2
.

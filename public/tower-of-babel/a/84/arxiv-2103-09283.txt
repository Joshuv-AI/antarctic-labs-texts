Introduction

Supersymmetric quantum field theories with 8 supercharges (8
Q
s) provide a fertile ground for many interesting research topics connecting various areas in theoretical and mathematical physics. For example,
Seiberg-Witten’s approach
Seiberg:1994rs
to 4-dimensional (4D)
{\cal N}=2
supersymmetric gauge theories provides analytic understanding of non-perturbative phenomena, such as confinement, in strongly coupled gauge theories. 4D
{\cal N}=2
superconformal field theories can be geometrically constructed using wrapped M5-branes in M-theory, and the 4D-2D correspondence connects physics of 4D supersymmetric field theories with mathematical structures on 2D Riemann surfaces in an unexpected way
Gaiotto:2009we
;
Alday:2009aq
;
Gaiotto:2009hg
. Interestingly, there exist non-trivial superconformal field theories with 8 supercharges in higher dimensional space-time, 5D and 6D, as predicted by String/M-theory
Witten:1995zh
;
Seiberg:1996bd
;
Intriligator:1997pq
. More recently, it is found that 2D chiral algebras (resp. 1D topological quantum mechanics) appear as protected subsectors of 4D
{\cal N}=2
(resp. 3D
{\cal N}=4
) superconformal field theories
Beem:2013sza
;
Chester:2014fya
;
Chester:2014mea
;
Beem:2016cbd
;
Dedushenko:2016jxl
;
Dedushenko:2017avn
;
Panerai:2020boq
.

Extended SUSY gauge theories have rich structures in their vacuum moduli space, and one natural invariant is the rank, i.e. the complex dimension of the Coulomb branch.
There have been numerous efforts in classifying SCFTs with 8
Q
s for a given low rank in various space-time dimensions
Chang:2019dzt
;
Argyres:2020nrr
;
Heckman:2013pva
;
Heckman:2015bfa
;
Bhardwaj:2015xxa
;
Jefferson:2017ahm
;
Jefferson:2018irk
;
Bhardwaj:2018yhy
;
Bhardwaj:2018vuu
;
Apruzzi:2019opn
;
Bhardwaj:2020gyu
.
For 3D
{\cal N}=4
SCFTs, however, the rank is in general not a
duality-invariant concept since
the Coulomb and Higgs branches are exchanged under the 3D mirror symmetry
Intriligator:1996ex
.
For this reason, we hereby modify the definition of rank as the maximum of the dimension of Coulomb branch and
that of the Higgs branch.
Another peculiar fact about 3D
{\cal N}=4
theories is that there exist non-trivial interacting SCFTs of rank 0, as studied
by two of the authors of this present paper in
Gang:2018huc
. This is in contrast with the case of
D\geq 4
, where it is often implicitly assumed that there is no non-trivial interacting rank 0 SCFTs with 8
Q
s, so that the classification program starts with rank 1. (Recently, 4D/5D rank 0 SCFTs were found through a geometrical engineering but it is yet unclear if they are interacting SCFTs
Closset:2020scj
.)
Note that most of the classification schemes in previous studies do not work for rank 0 cases since the existence of Coulomb or Higgs branch operators is an crucial assumption in the analysis.

In this paper, we initiate the classification of rank 0 3D
{\cal N}=4
SCFTs by establishing the following correspondence:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\textrm{3D ${\cal N}=4$ superconformal field theories of rank 0 }\\
&\longleftrightarrow\textrm{A pair of 3D non-unitary topological quantum field theories (TQFTs)}\;.\end{split}

(1)

The non-unitary TQFTs emerge at particular choices of non-superconformal R-symmetry,
\nu=\pm 1
in (
5
), of rank 0
{\cal N}=4
SCFTs. In the limits, due to huge Bose/Fermi cancellations the unrefined superconformal index gets contributions only from Coulomb-branch or Higgs-branch operators and their descendants. For rank 0 theories, the index becomes trivial (i.e.
1
) since there are no non-trivial Coulomb/Higgs branch operators. Other partition functions on various rigid supersymmetric Euclidean backgrounds also drastically simplify in the degenerate limits for rank 0 theories. Our correspondence says that the simplified partition functions are actually equal to the partition functions of corresponding non-unitary TQFTs on the same 3D spacetime. (See (
12
) for a precise statement.) Concrete dictionaries of the correspondence are given in Table
1
. In the degenerate limits, as seen in the superconformal index case, contributions from local operators become unimportant for rank 0 theories and only non-local loop operators become relevant physical observables. Similarly, loop operators are the only physical observables in general TQFTs.
Using the correspondence, one can map the problem of classifying rank 0 SCFTs to the classification of non-unitary TQFTs, which is much easier to handle.
Mathematically, TQFTs are described by modular tensor categories (MTCs) and classification of MTCs has been studied intensively in the literature
turaev1992modular
;
rowell2006quantum
;
rowell2009classification
;
wen2016theory
;
bruillard2016rank
;
Cho:2020ljj
. The most basic quantity characterizing a 3D CFT is the round three-sphere free energy, usually denoted as
F
. The
F
always monotonically decreases under the renormalization group (RG) flow and thus is regarded as a proper measure of the degrees of freedom of 3D CFT
Jafferis:2011zi
;
Liu:2012eea
;
Casini:2012ei
. In one of the most interesting and surprising dictionaries of the correspondence, the
F
of a rank 0 CFT is related to the modular S-matrix of a non-unitary TQFT in a very simple way as given in Table
2
. Combining the dictionary and universal algebraic properties of the S-matrix, we derive following lower bound on
F

\displaystyle F\geq-\log\left(\sqrt{\frac{5-\sqrt{5}}{10}}\right),

(2)

which should hold for any rank 0 SCFTs. Interestingly, the lower bound is saturated by the minimal theory studied in
Gang:2018huc
.

The correspondence is similar in spirit with the (4D
{\cal N}=2
SCFT)/(2D chiral algebra) correspondence mentioned above. In both correspondences, non-unitary algebraic structures, chiral algebras on the one hand and modular tensor category on the other, appear as protected subsectors of unitary superconformal field theories. But there are several crucial differences. First, two theories in our correspondence are defined on the same 3D space-time while the 2D chiral algebra lives in the 2D subspace of 4D space-time of the SCFT.
Secondly, basic physical objects are BPS local operators in the (4D SCFTs)/(2D chiral algebra) story while BPS non-local loop operators are basic objects in our correspondence.
That non-local loop operators play crucial roles can be a great advantage of our classification approach over the conventional conformal bootstrap approaches, since the latter are based on correlation functions of local operators.
We note that the 3D non-unitary TQFTs are sensitive to the global structure of the 3D rank 0 SCFTs and two theories in the correspondence share the same one-form symmetry as well as their ’t Hooft anomalies.
In 3D, the quantity
F
(unlike the stress-energy tensor central charge
C_{T}
) is sensitive to the global structure of CFT and the conformal bootstrap approach never give a constraint on
F
but only on
C_{T}
, which is not a proper measure of the degrees of freedom in a strict sense
Nishioka:2013gza
.

As main result of the paper, we propose that for any rank 0

We claim that in the degenerate limits i) the rigid supersymmetry partition function becomes independent on the squashing parameter

We call a topological quantum field theory a non-spin (or bosonic) TQFT when its partition function is independent on the choice of the spin structure, and a spin (or fermionic) TQFT otherwise. We propose that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\textrm{ The non-unitary $\textrm{TFT}_{\pm}[{\cal T}_{\rm rank\;0}]$ is a spin (fermionic) TQFT}\;,\\
&\qquad\textrm{if $R_{\nu=\pm 1}+2j_{2}\in 2\mathbb{Z}+1$ for some BPS local operators \;. }\\
&\qquad\textrm{or equivalently,}\;\\
&\qquad\textrm{if ${\cal I}^{\rm sci}(q,\eta,\nu=\pm 1;s)$ contains $q^{\frac{1}{2}(\textrm{odd integer})}$ terms\;.}\end{split}

(13)

Here
{\cal I}^{\rm sci}
is the superconformal index defined in (
161
) and the index at

since
R_{\nu=\pm 1}+2j_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}

{\cal M}_{g,p=0}

implies that

to be fermionic. To see this, consider a rank 0 SCFT
{\cal T}_{\rm rank\;0}
not satisfying the above condition whose associated non-unitary topological field theories,
\textrm{TFT}_{\pm}[{\cal T}_{\rm rank\;0}]
, are bosonic. Then,
{\cal T}_{\rm rank\;0}\otimes{\cal T}_{\rm spin\;top}
with an unitary fermionic topological field theory
{\cal T}_{\rm spin\;top}
is still a rank 0 SCFT not satisfying the above condition since the decoupled topological sector does not contribute to the superconformal index. But its associated non-unitary TQFTs
\textrm{TFT}_{\pm}[{\cal T}_{\rm rank\;0}\otimes{\cal T}_{\rm spin\;top}]=\textrm{TFT}_{\pm}[{\cal T}_{\rm rank\;0}]\otimes{\cal T}_{\rm spin\;top}
are fermionic due to the decoupled unitary spin TQFT
{\cal T}_{\rm spin\;top}

The proposal in (

{\cal Z}^{\mathbb{B}}
is the superconformal index (

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\rm sci}(q,\eta,\nu;s)\xrightarrow{\;\;\eta=1,\nu\rightarrow 1\;\;}\begin{cases}\textrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{\rm rad}(S^{2})}(-1)^{2j_{3}}q^{R+j_{3}}\;\;,\quad s=+1\;,\\
\textrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{\rm rad}(S^{2})}(-1)^{R}q^{R+j_{3}}\;\;,\quad s=-1\;.\end{cases}

All unitary multiplets of 3D
{\cal N}=4

{\cal N}=2
supersymmetry which is expected from the geometrical aspects of the 3D-3D correspondence for a once-punctured torus bundle
Terashima:2011qi
;
Gang:2013sqa
. The
{\cal N}=2
dual is presented in Appendix
B
.

4
Discussion

There are several interesting questions we want to address in future.

Relation with Rozansky-Witten theory

One well-known method of constructing a topological theory from a 3D
{\cal N}=4
SCFT is using topological twisting as studied by Rozansky and Witten in
Rozansky:1996bq
. An
{\cal N}=4
SCFT
{\cal T}
has
SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}
R-symmetry and we can consider a pair of topological twisted theories,
\textrm{RW}_{+}[{\cal T}]
and
\textrm{RW}_{-}[{\cal T}]
, using the
SU(2)_{L}
or
SU(2)_{R}
in the twisting respectively. It would be interesting to clarify the exact relation between the pair of topological twisted theories,
\textrm{RW}_{\pm}[{\cal T}_{\rm rank\;0}]
, and our
\textrm{TFT}_{\pm}[\mathcal{T}_{\rm rank0}]
for rank 0 SCFT
{\cal T}_{\rm rank\;0}
.
6
6

6

For a 3D
{\cal N}=4
SCFT
{\cal T}
of non-zero rank, the topological twisted theories
\textrm{RW}_{\pm}[{\cal T}]
are not genuine TQFTs since they do not obey the standard axioms of TQFT. Nevertheless, interesting 3-manifold/knot invariants can be studied using the
\textrm{RW}_{\pm}[{\cal T}]

Gukov:2020lqm
.

Two theories,
\textrm{RW}[{\cal T}_{\rm rank\;0}]
and
\textrm{TFT}[{\cal T}_{\rm rank\;0}]
, have the same ground state degeneracy
\textrm{GSD}_{g}
for all
g\geq 0
since the partitial topological twisting on
\Sigma_{g}\times S^{1}
using the
U(1)\subset SU(2)_{R}
(or
U(1)\subset SU(2)_{L}
) symmetry is actually equivalent to the full topological twisting on the 3-manifold using the
SU(2)_{R}
(
SU(2)_{L}
) symmetry
Gukov:2016gkn
;
Bullimore:2018jlp
. From the comparison, we naturally conjecture that

\displaystyle\textrm{TFT}_{\pm}[{\cal T}_{\rm rank\;0}]\textrm{ is equal to }\textrm{RW}_{\pm}[{\cal T}_{\rm rank\;0}]\;.

(158)

If the conjecture is true, we need to explain how the BPS partition functions of rank-0 theories in the degenerate limits compute the partition functions of topologically twisted theories.

3D non-unitary TQFTs from 4D
{\cal N}=2
SCFTs

In
Dedushenko:2018bpp
, the authors constructed 3D non-unitary TQFTs from some 4D
{\cal N}=2
Argyres-Douglas theories. The construction is somewhat similar to our construction of TFT, but the precise relation is not clear. In our construction, semi-simple non-unitary TQFTs appear in 3D SCFTs of rank 0, while their examples after naive dimension reduction to 3D are not of rank 0. It would be interesting to clarify for which classes of 4D
{\cal N}=2
SCFTs their construction works, and to see if we can apply their construction in classification of 4D
{\cal N}=2
SCFTs.

Are all non-unitary TQFTs correspond to rank 0
{\cal N}=4
SCFTs?

In our paper, we assign a pair of non-unitary TQFTs to 3D rank 0
{\cal N}=4
SCFTs. But it is not clear if all non-unitary TQFTs can be constructed in this way.

Rank 0

3D

{\cal N}=4

In the below, we summarize localization formulae for the BPS partition functions introduced above for
{\cal N}\geq 3
gauge theories. In localization computations,

{\cal N}=2
gauge theories. The local Lagrangian density of an
{\cal N}=3

G

G

{\cal N}=4

Jafferis:2010un

{\cal N}\geq 2

Superconformal index

The superconformal index for a 3D
\mathcal{N}=4
SCFT is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\rm sci}(q,\eta,\nu;s):=\begin{cases}\textrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{\rm rad}(S^{2})}(-1)^{2j_{3}}q^{\frac{R_{\nu}}{2}+j_{3}}\eta^{A}\;\;,\quad s=1\;,\\
\textrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{\rm rad}(S^{2})}(-1)^{R_{\nu}}q^{\frac{R_{\nu}}{2}+j_{3}}\eta^{A}\;\;,\quad s=-1\;.\end{cases}

(161)

Here the trace is taken over the radially quantized Hilbert-space
\mathcal{H}_{\rm rad}(S^{2})
on
S^{2}
whose elements are local operators.
j_{3}\in\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{2}
is the Lorentz spin, the Cartan of
SO(3)
isometry on the
S^{2}

q
plays role as an
\Omega

\displaystyle\Delta=R+R^{\prime}+j_{3}\;,

(162)

where
\Delta
is the conformal dimension. The index alternatively can be regarded as a partition function on
(S^{2}\times S^{1})={\cal M}_{g=0,p=0}
with a fixed metric, background electric fields coupled to
U(1)_{R_{\nu}}
and axial
U(1)
symmetry and spin-structure along the
S^{1}

\nu
are simply related as follows

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{I}^{\rm sci}(q,\eta,\nu;s=1)=\mathcal{I}^{\rm sci}(q,\eta,\nu=0;s=1)\big|_{\eta\rightarrow\eta q^{\frac{\nu}{2}}}\;,\\
&\mathcal{I}^{\rm sci}(q,\eta,\nu;s=-1)=\mathcal{I}^{\rm sci}(q,\eta,\nu=0;s=-1)\big|_{\eta\rightarrow\eta(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\nu}}\;.\end{split}

(163)

The two indices with different choices of the spin structure are related to each other in the following way

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\rm sci}\left(q,\eta,\nu=\pm 1;s=1\right)=\mathcal{I}^{\rm sci}\left(q,\eta,\nu=\pm 1;s=-1\right)\big|_{q^{\frac{1}{2}}\rightarrow-q^{\frac{1}{2}}}\;.

(164)

Using localization, the superconformal index at

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\cal I}^{\rm sci}(q,\eta,\nu=0;s=1)\\
&=\sum_{\mathbf{m}}\oint_{|a_{i}|=1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\textrm{rank}G}\frac{da_{i}}{2\pi ia_{i}}\right)\Delta_{G}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{a};q)q^{\epsilon_{0}(\mathfrak{n})}{\cal I}_{0}^{cs}(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{a})\textrm{P.E.}[f_{\rm single}(q,\mathbf{a},\eta;\mathbf{m})]\;.\end{split}

(165)

In the localization, the saddle points are parametrized by

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{S^{2}}F=\mathbf{m},\;\exp\left(i\int_{S^{1}}A\right)=\mathbf{a}\;\;\textrm{and }\sigma=\frac{\mathbf{m}}{2}\\
&\textrm{ with }\mathbf{m}:=\sum_{i}\mathfrak{m}_{i}\mathbf{h}^{i}\textrm{ and }\mathbf{a}:=\exp(\mathbf{A}):=\exp\left(\sum_{i}(\log a_{i})\mathbf{h}^{i}\right)\;.\end{split}

(166)

Here

(167)

(170)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&G=U(N),\quad\{\lambda(\mathbf{m})\;:\;\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}_{\rm adj}\}=\{\mathfrak{m}_{i}-\mathfrak{m}_{j}\;:\;0<i<j\leq N\}\;,\\
&G=SU(2),\quad\{\lambda(\mathbf{m})\;:\;\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}_{\rm adj}\}=\{\mathfrak{m}_{1}-\mathfrak{m}_{2}\}\;.\end{split}

(171)

\displaystyle f_{\rm single}(q,\mathbf{a},\eta;\mathbf{m})=\sum_{\Phi}\sum_{\beta\in\rho_{R_{\Phi}}}\left(\frac{q^{\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\Phi}+\frac{1}{2}|\beta(\mathbf{m})|}e^{\beta(\mathbf{A})}\eta^{q_{A}(\Phi)}}{1-q}-\frac{q^{\frac{1}{2}(2-\Delta_{\Phi})+\frac{1}{2}|\beta(\mathbf{m})|}e^{-\beta(\mathbf{A})}\eta^{-q_{A}(\Phi)}}{1-q}\right)\;.

Squashed three-sphere partition function
{\cal Z}^{S^{3}_{b}}(b,m,\nu)

This is a partition function on
S^{3}={\cal M}_{g=0,p=1}
with the following metric

\displaystyle ds^{2}(S^{3}_{b})=|dz|^{2}+|dw|^{2}\;,\quad(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\textrm{ are subject to }b^{-2}|z|^{2}+b^{2}|w|^{2}=1\;.

(178)

To preserve some supercharges, a background field coupled to the
U(1)_{R_{\nu}}
symmetry is properly turned on. Using localization, the partition function can be given in the following integral form

\displaystyle{\cal Z}^{S^{3}_{b}}(b,m,\nu)=\int\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\textrm{rank}(G)}\frac{dZ_{i}}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}\right)\Delta_{G}(\mathbf{Z};\hbar){\cal I}_{\hbar}(\mathbf{Z},m,\nu)\;,\quad\hbar:=2\pi ib^{2}\;.

(179)

Here
\{Z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\textrm{rank}(G)}
parametrizes the Cartan subalgebra of
G
.

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}=\sum_{i=1}^{\textrm{rank}(G)}Z_{i}\mathbf{h}^{i}\in\textrm{(Cartan subalgebra of $G$)}\;,

(180)

and
\Delta_{G}(\mathbf{Z})
is the contribution from the
{\cal N}=2
vector multiplet associated to the gauge group
G

\displaystyle\Delta_{G}(\mathbf{Z};\hbar):=\frac{1}{|\textrm{Weyl}(G)|}\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}_{\rm adj}}\bigg[4\sinh\left(\frac{1}{2}\lambda\cdot\mathbf{Z}\right)\sinh\left(\frac{\pi i}{\hbar}\lambda\cdot\mathbf{Z}\right)\bigg]\;.

(181)

|\textrm{Weyl}(G)|
is the order of the Weyl group of
G
.

The integrand
{\cal I}_{\hbar}
is determined by gauge group, matter contents and Chern-Simons levels of the
{\cal N}=3

\bullet
An
{\cal N}=2
chiral multiplet in a representation
R
under
G
with

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\prod_{\Phi}\prod_{\beta\in\rho_{R_{\Phi}}}\Psi_{\hbar}\left(\beta\cdot\mathbf{Z}+q_{A}\big(m+(i\pi+\frac{\hbar}{2})\nu\big)+(i\pi+\frac{\hbar}{2})\Delta\right)\;.\end{split}

(182)

We define
\Psi_{\hbar}
as

\displaystyle\Psi_{\hbar}(X)

\displaystyle:=\psi_{\hbar}(X)\exp{\left(\frac{X^{2}}{4\hbar}\right)}\;,

(183)

with
\psi_{\hbar}(x)
being the non-compact quantum dilogarithm function. (We refer to

for details of the definition and basic properties of the function.)
An
{\cal N}=4
hypermultiplet consists of two
{\cal N}=2
chiral multiplets with gauge charge
R
and
\overline{R}
,
q_{A}=\frac{1}{2}
and
\Delta=\frac{1}{2}

{\cal N}=2

\bullet

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{k}{2\hbar}\textrm{Tr}(\mathbf{Z}^{2})\right)\;.

(184)

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{W\textrm{Tr}(\mathbf{Z})}{\hbar}\right)\bigg|_{W=m+(i\pi+\frac{\hbar}{2})\nu}\;.

(185)

\displaystyle F_{\nu=0}>F_{\nu\neq 0}\;,\quad\textrm{where }F_{\nu}:=-\log|{\cal Z}^{S^{3}_{b}}(b=1,m=0,\nu)|\;.

Perturbative expansion of squashed three-sphere partition function integral

One can consider formal perturbative expansion of the localization integral in an asymptotic limit
\hbar\rightarrow 0
,
to obtain infinitely many 3D SCFT invariants.
In the limit, the integrand
{\cal I}_{\hbar}
can be perturbatively expanded in the following form

\displaystyle\log{\cal I}_{\hbar}(\vec{Z},m,\nu)\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt\hbar\rightarrow 0\hskip 8.19447pt}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\hbar^{n-1}{\cal W}_{n}(\vec{Z},n,\nu)\;.

(188)

The leading part
{\cal W}_{0}
corresponds to the twisted superpotential. By extremizing the twisted superpotential, we obtain Bethe-vacua

\displaystyle\textrm{Bethe-vacua : }\frac{\bigg\{\vec{z}\;:\;\left(\exp(\partial_{Z_{i}}{\cal W}_{0})\big|_{\vec{Z}\rightarrow\log\vec{z}}\right)=1\;,\;w\cdot\vec{z}\neq\vec{z}\;\;\;\forall\;\textrm{non-trivial }w\in\textrm{Weyl}(G)\bigg\}_{i=1}^{\textrm{rank}(G)}}{\textrm{Weyl}(G)}\;.

(189)

Here
\textrm{Weyl}(G)
is the Weyl group of gauge group
G
. A Bethe-vacuum
\vec{z}_{\alpha}
can be promoted to a saddle point
\vec{Z}_{\alpha}=\log\vec{z}_{\alpha}
of the localization integral by properly shifting
{\cal W}_{0}
as follows,

\displaystyle{\cal W}_{0}^{\vec{n}_{\alpha}}={\cal W}_{0}+2\pi i\sum_{i}n_{\alpha}^{i}Z_{i},\;\;n_{\alpha}^{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\;\textrm{is chosen such that }\partial_{Z_{i}}{\cal W}^{\vec{n}_{\alpha}}_{0}|_{\vec{Z}\rightarrow\log\vec{z}_{\alpha}}=0\;.

(190)

Then we can consider formal perturbative expansion of the localization integral around the saddle point

\displaystyle\begin{split}&|\textrm{Weyl}(G)|\times\int\prod_{i=1}^{\textrm{rank}(G)}\frac{d(\delta Z_{i})}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}\exp\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}{\cal W}_{0}^{\vec{n}_{\alpha}}(\vec{Z}^{\alpha}+\delta\vec{Z},m,\nu)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\hbar^{n-1}{\cal W}_{n}(\vec{Z}+\delta\vec{Z},,m,\nu)\right)\\
&\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt\hbar\rightarrow 0\hskip 8.19447pt}\exp\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\hbar^{n-1}{\cal S}^{\alpha}_{n}(m,\nu)\right)\;.\end{split}

(191)

The factor
|\textrm{Weyl}(G)|
is multiplied since that many saddle points, which all give the same perturbative expansion, collapse into a single Bethe-vacuum after the Weyl quotient.
The perturbative expansion can be formally computed by performing Gaussian integrals
Dimofte:2012qj
;
Gang:2019jut
. For example,

\displaystyle{\cal S}^{\alpha}_{0}={\cal W}_{0}^{\vec{n}_{\alpha}}(\vec{Z}_{\alpha})\;,\quad{\cal S}_{1}^{\alpha}=-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\det_{i,j}\frac{\partial^{2}{\cal W}_{0}}{\partial Z_{i}\partial Z_{j}}\right)\bigg|_{\vec{Z}=\vec{Z}_{\alpha}}+{\cal W}_{1}(\vec{Z}_{\alpha})+\log|\textrm{Weyl}(G)|\;.

(192)

The proposal in (
12
) implies the following highly non-trivial constraints on the perturbative invariants for rank 0 SCFTs,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\textrm{Im}[{\cal S}^{\alpha}_{0}(m=0,\nu)]\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt\nu\rightarrow\pm 1\hskip 8.19447pt}0\;,\quad\textrm{Im}[{\cal S}^{\alpha}_{2}(m=0,\nu)]\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt\nu\rightarrow\pm 1\hskip 8.19447pt}0\;,\\
&{\cal S}^{\alpha}_{n\geq 3}(m=0,\nu)\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447pt\nu\rightarrow\pm 1\hskip 8.19447pt}0\;.\end{split}

(193)

This follows from the fact that the squashed three-sphere partition function becomes
b
-independent in the degenerate limits,
m=0
and
\nu\rightarrow\pm 1
, modulo local counter terms which affect an overall factor of the following form

\displaystyle\exp\bigg(\pi iq_{1}(b^{2}+\frac{1}{b^{2}})+i\pi q_{2})\bigg)\bigg|_{q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}}\;.

(194)

Twisted indices and twisted partition functions

The twisted index is defined as

\displaystyle{\cal I}^{\Sigma_{g}}(\eta,\nu;s)=\begin{cases}\textrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}(\Sigma_{g};\nu)}(-1)^{2j_{3}}\eta^{A}\;,\quad s=1\;,\\
\textrm{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}(\Sigma_{g};\nu)}(-1)^{R_{\nu}}\eta^{A}\;\;\;,\quad s=-1\;.\end{cases}

(195)

Here
\mathcal{H}(\Sigma_{g};\nu)
is the Hilbert-space on
\Sigma_{g}
with topological twisting using the
U(1)_{R_{\nu}}
symmetry. Unlike the radially quantized Hilbert-space
{\cal H}_{\rm rad}(S^{2})
, the Hilbert-space depends on the choice of the R-symmetry mixing parameter
\nu
.
Due to the topological twisting, the index is well-defined only when following Dirac quantization condition is satisfied

\displaystyle R_{\nu}\times(g-1)\in\mathbb{Z}\quad\textrm{for all local operators}\;.

(196)

Note that the condition is always satisfied in the degenerate limit
\nu=\pm 1
since
R_{\nu=\pm 1}\in\mathbb{Z}
which obvious from the fact that
R_{\nu=1}=2R\in\mathbb{Z}
and
R_{\nu=-1}=2R^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
. For
g=0
case, the quantization condition is satisfied for all
\nu
and the index is independent on the continuous parameter

Appendix D
Quantum dilogarithm function

The quantum dilogarithm function (Q.D.L)
\psi_{\hbar}(Z)
is defined by
Faddeev:1993rs

(\hbar=2\pi ib^{2})

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi_{\hbar}(Z):=\begin{cases}\prod_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-q^{r}e^{-Z}}{1-\tilde{q}^{-r+1}e^{-\tilde{Z}}}\quad\text{if}\quad|q|<1\;,\\
\prod_{r=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-\tilde{q}^{r}e^{-\tilde{Z}}}{1-q^{-r+1}e^{-Z}}\quad\text{if}\quad|q|>1\;,\end{cases}\end{split}

(294)

with

\displaystyle\begin{split}q:=e^{2\pi ib^{2}}\,,\qquad\tilde{q}:=e^{2\pi ib^{-2}}\,,\qquad\tilde{Z}:=\frac{1}{b^{2}}Z\,,\end{split}

(295)

where
b
is the squashing parameter.
The function satisfies the following difference equations:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi_{\hbar}(Z+2\pi ib^{2})=(1-e^{-Z})\psi_{\hbar}(Z)\,,\quad\psi_{\hbar}(Z+2\pi i)=(1-e^{-\frac{Z}{b^{2}}})\psi_{\hbar}(Z)\;.\end{split}

(296)

At the special value
b=1
, the Q.D.L simplifies as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\log\psi_{\hbar=2\pi i}(Z)=\frac{-(2\pi+iZ)\log(1-e^{-Z})+i\text{Li}_{2}(e^{-Z})}{2\pi}\,,\end{split}

(297)

and there is a special limit at
b=1

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{p\rightarrow 0}p\,\psi_{\hbar=2\pi i}(p)=e^{\frac{\pi i}{12}}\;.\end{split}

(298)

On the other hand, the asymptotic expansion when
\hbar=2\pi ib^{2}\rightarrow 0
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\log\psi_{\hbar}(Z)\xrightarrow{b^{2}\rightarrow 0}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{B_{n}\hbar^{n-1}}{n!}\text{Li}_{2-n}(e^{-Z})\;.\end{split}

(299)

Here
B_{n}
is the
n
-th Bernoulli number with
B_{1}=1/2
.
For several computations in the main text, one needs to utilize the identity

\displaystyle\begin{split}\text{Li}_{2}(u)+\text{Li}_{2}(u^{-1})=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\frac{1}{2}\big(\log(-u)\big)^{2}\;.\end{split}

(300)

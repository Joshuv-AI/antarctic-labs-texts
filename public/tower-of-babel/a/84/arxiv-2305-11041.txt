High-dimensional Asymptotics of Denoising Autoencoders
Hugo Cui, Lenka Zdeborová

Abstract
We address the problem of denoising data from a Gaussian mixture using a two-layer non-linear autoencoder with tied weights and a skip connection. We consider the high-dimensional limit where the number of training samples and the input dimension jointly tend to infinity while the number of hidden units remains bounded. We provide closed-form expressions for the denoising mean-squared test error. Building on this result, we quantitatively characterize the advantage of the considered architecture over the autoencoder without the skip connection that relates closely to principal component analysis. We further show that our results accurately capture the learning curves on a range of real data sets.



Introduction

Machine learning techniques have a long history of success in denoising tasks. The recent breakthrough of diffusion-based generation
Song2020ScoreBasedGM
;
Ho2020DenoisingDP
has further revived the interest in denoising networks, demonstrating how they can also be leveraged, beyond denoising, for generative tasks. However, this rapidly expanding range of applications stands in sharp contrast to the relatively scarce theoretical understanding of denoising neural networks, even for the simplest instance thereof – namely Denoising Auto Encoders (DAEs)
Vincent2010StackedDA
.

Theoretical studies of autoencoders have hitherto almost exclusively focused on data compression tasks using Reconstruction Auto Encoders (RAEs), where the goal is to learn a concise latent representation of the data. A majority of this body of work addresses
linear
autoencoders
Oftadeh2020EliminatingTI
;
Kunin2019LossLO
;
Bao2020RegularizedLA
;
Gidel2019ImplicitRO
. The authors of
Refinetti2022TheDO
;
Shevchenko2022FundamentalLO
analyze the gradient-based training of non-linear autoencoders with online stochastic gradient descent or in population, thus implicitly assuming the availability of an infinite number of training samples. Furthermore, two-layer RAEs were shown to learn to essentially perform Principal Component Analysis (PCA)
Eckart1936TheAO
;
Bourlard1988AutoassociationBM
;
Baldi1989NeuralNA
, i.e. to learn a linear model. Ref.
Nguyen2021AnalysisOF
shows that this is also true for infinite-width architectures. Learning in DAEs has been the object of theoretical investigations only in the linear case
Pretorius2018LearningDO
, while the case of non-linear DAEs remains theoretically largely unexplored.

Main contributions

The present work considers the problem of denoising data sampled from a Gaussian mixture by learning a two-layer DAE with a skip connection and tied weights via empirical risk minimization. Throughout the manuscript, we consider the high-dimensional limit where the number of training samples
n
and the dimension
d
are large (
n,d\to\infty
) while remaining comparable, i.e.
\alpha\equiv\nicefrac{{n}}{{d}}=\Theta(1)
. Our main contributions are:

•

Leveraging the replica method, we provide sharp, closed-form formulae for the mean squared denoising test error (MSE) for DAEs, as a function of the sample complexity
\alpha
and the problem parameters. We also provide a sharp characterization for other learning metrics including the weights norms, skip connection strength, and cosine similarity between the weights and the cluster means. These formulae encompass as a corollary the case of RAEs. We show that these formulae also describe quantitatively rather well the denoising MSE for
real
data sets, including MNIST
LeCun1998GradientbasedLA
and FashionMNIST
Xiao2017FashionMNISTAN
.

•

We find that PCA denoising (namely denoising by projecting the noisy data along the principal component of the training samples) is widely sub-optimal compared to the DAE, leading to a MSE superior by a difference of
\Theta(d)
, thereby establishing that DAEs do
not
simply learn to perform PCA.

•

Building on the formulae, we quantify the role of each component of the DAE architecture (skip connection and the bottleneck network) in its overall performance. We find that the two components have complementary effects in the denoising process –namely preserving the data nuances and removing the noise– and discuss how the training of the DAE results from a tradeoff between these effects.

Related works

Theory of autoencoders

– Various aspects of RAEs have been studied, for example, memorization
Radhakrishnan2019OverparameterizedNN
, or latent space alignment
Jain2021AMF
. However, the largest body of work has been dedicated to the analysis of gradient-based algorithms when training RAEs. Ref.
Kunin2019LossLO
established that minimizing the training loss leads to learning the principal components of the data. Authors of
Bourlard1988AutoassociationBM
;
Baldi1989NeuralNA
have analyzed how a linear RAE learns these components during training. These studies were later extended to non-linear networks by
NGUYEN2019OnTD
;
Refinetti2022TheDO
;
Shevchenko2022FundamentalLO
, at the sacrifice of further assuming an infinite number of training samples to be available –either by considering online stochastic gradient descent, or the population loss. Refs.
NGUYEN2019BenefitsOJ
;
Nguyen2021AnalysisOF
are able to address a finite sample complexity, but in exchange, have to consider infinite-width architectures, which

Nguyen2021AnalysisOF
further shows, also tend to a large extent to learn to perform PCA.

Exact asymptotics from the replica method

– The replica method
Replica
;
Replica2
;
Zdeborov2015StatisticalPO
;
Gabrie2019MeanfieldIM
has proven a very valuable gateway to access sharp asymptotic characterizations of learning metrics for high-dimensional machine learning problems. Past works have addressed –among others– single-
gardner1988optimal
;
opper1991generalization
;
Barbier2017OptimalEA
;
Aubin2020GeneralizationEI
and multi-index models
Aubin2018TheCM
, or kernel methods
dietrich1999statistical
;
Bordelon2020SpectrumDL
;
Gerace2020GeneralisationEI
;
Cui2022ErrorRF
. While the approach has traditionally addressed convex problems, for which its prediction can be proven e.g. using the convex Gordon minimax theorem
thrampoulidis2018precise
, the replica method allows to average over
all
the global minimizers of the loss, and therefore also accommodates non-convex settings. Refs.
ZavatoneVeth2022ContrastingRA
;
Cui2023OptimalLO
are two recent examples of its application to non-convex losses. In the present manuscript, we leverage this versatility to study the minimization of the empirical risk of DAEs, whose non-convexity represents a considerable hurdle to many other types of analyses.

I
Setting

Data model

We consider the problem of denoising data
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
corrupted by Gaussian white noise of variance
\Delta
,

\tilde{\bm{x}}=\sqrt{1-\Delta}\bm{x}+\sqrt{\Delta}\bm{\xi},

where we denoted
\tilde{\bm{x}}
the noisy data point, and
\bm{\xi}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbb{I}_{d})
the additive noise.
The rescaling of the clean data point by a factor
\sqrt{1-\Delta}
is a practical choice that entails no loss of generality, and allows to easily interpolate between the noiseless case (
\Delta=0
) and the case where the signal-to-noise ratio vanishes (
\Delta=1
). Furthermore, it allows us to seamlessly connect with works on diffusion-based generative models, where the rescaling naturally follows from the way the data is corrupted by an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process
Song2020ScoreBasedGM
;
Ho2020DenoisingDP
.
In the present work, we assume the clean data
\bm{x}
to be drawn from a Gaussian mixture distribution
\mathbb{P}
with
K
clusters

\displaystyle\bm{x}\sim\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\mathcal{N}(\bm{\mu}_{k},\bm{\Sigma}_{k}).

(1)

The
k-
th cluster is thus centered around
\bm{\mu}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, has covariance
\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\succeq 0
, and relative weight
\rho_{k}
.

DAE model

An algorithmic way to retrieve the clean data
\bm{x}
from the noisy data
\tilde{\bm{x}}
is to build a neural network taking the latter as an input and yielding the former as an output. A particularly natural choice for such a network is an autoencoder architecture
Vincent2010StackedDA
. The intuition is that the narrow hidden layer of an autoencoder forces the network to learn a succinct latent representation of the data, which is robust against noise corruption of the input. In this work, we analyze a two-layer DAE. We further assume that the weights are tied. Additionally, mirroring modern denoising architectures like U-nets
Ronneberger2015UNetCN
or
Mao2016ImageRU
;
tong2017image
;
kim2016accurate
;
kim2016deeply
, we also allow for a (trainable) skip-connection:

\displaystyle f_{b,\bm{w}}(\tilde{\bm{x}})=b\times\tilde{\bm{x}}+\frac{\bm{w}^{\top}}{\sqrt{d}}\sigma\left(\frac{\bm{w}\tilde{\bm{x}}}{\sqrt{d}}\right).

(2)

The DAE (
2
) is therefore parametrized by the scalar skip connection strength
b\in\mathbb{R}
and the weights
\bm{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times d}
, with
p
the width of the DAE hidden layer. The normalization of the weight
\bm{w}
by
\sqrt{d}
in (
2
) is the natural choice which ensures for high dimensional settings
d\gg 1
that the argument of the non-linearity
\sigma(\cdot)
stays
\Theta(1)
. Like
Refinetti2022TheDO
, we focus on the case with
p\ll d
. The assumption of weight-tying affords a more concise theoretical characterization and thus clearer discussions. Note that it is also a strategy with substantial practical history, dating back to
Vincent2010StackedDA
, as it prevents the DAE from functioning in the linear region of its non-linearity
\sigma(\cdot)
. This choice of architecture is also motivated by a particular case of Tweedie’s formula
Efron2011TweediesFA
(see eq. (
80
) in Appendix
B
), which will be the object of further discussion in Section
III
.

We also consider two other simple architectures

\displaystyle u_{\bm{v}}(\tilde{\bm{x}})=\frac{\bm{v}^{\top}}{\sqrt{d}}\sigma\left(\frac{\bm{v}\tilde{\bm{x}}}{\sqrt{d}}\right),

\displaystyle r_{c}(\tilde{\bm{x}})=c\times\tilde{\bm{x}},

(3)

which correspond to the building blocks of the complete DAE architecture
f_{b,\bm{w}}
(
2
) (hereafter referred to as the
full
DAE). Note that indeed
f_{b,\bm{w}}=r_{b}+u_{\bm{w}}
. The part
u_{\bm{v}}(\cdot)
is a DAE without skip connection (hereafter called the
bottleneck network
component), while
r_{c}(\cdot)
correspond to a simple single-parameter trainable rescaling of the input (hereafter called the
rescaling
component).

To train the DAE (
2
), we assume the availability of a training set
\mathcal{D}=\{\tilde{\bm{x}}^{\mu},\bm{x}^{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{n}
, with
n
clean samples
\bm{x}^{\mu}
drawn i.i.d from
\mathbb{P}
(
1
) and the corresponding noisy samples
\tilde{\bm{x}}^{\mu}=\bm{x}^{\mu}+\bm{\xi}^{\mu}
(with the noises
\bm{\xi}^{\mu}
assumed mutually independent). The DAE is trained to recover the clean samples
\bm{x}^{\mu}
from the noisy samples
\tilde{\bm{x}}^{\mu}
by minimizing the empirical risk

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{R}}(b,\bm{w})=\sum\limits_{\mu=1}^{n}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{\mu}-f_{b,\bm{w}}(\tilde{\bm{x}}^{\mu})\right\lVert^{2}+g(\bm{w}),

(4)

where
g:\mathbb{R}^{p\times d}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is an arbitrary convex regularizing function. We denote by
\hat{b},\hat{\bm{w}}
the minimizers of the empirical risk (
4
) and by
\hat{f}\equiv f_{\hat{b},\hat{\bm{w}}}
the corresponding trained DAE (
2
).
For future discussion, we also consider training independently the components (
3
) via empirical risk minimization, by which we mean replacing
f_{b,\bm{w}}
by
u_{\bm{v}}
or
r_{c}
in (
4
). We similarly denote
\hat{\bm{v}}
(resp.
\hat{c}
) the learnt weight of the bottleneck network (resp. rescaling) component and
\hat{u}\equiv u_{\hat{\bm{v}}}
(resp.
\hat{r}\equiv r_{\hat{c}}
). Note that generically,
\hat{\bm{v}}\neq\hat{\bm{w}}
and
\hat{c}\neq\hat{b}
, and therefore
\hat{f}\neq\hat{u}+\hat{r}
, since
\hat{b},\hat{\bm{w}}
result from their joint optimization as parts of the full DAE
f_{b,\bm{w}}
, while
\hat{c}
(or
\hat{\bm{v}}
) are optimized independently. As we discuss in Section
III
, training the sole rescaling
r_{c}
does not afford an expressive enough denoiser, while an independently learnt bottleneck network component
u_{\bm{v}}
essentially only learns to implement PCA. However, when
jointly
trained as components of the full DAE
f_{b,\bm{w}}
(
2
), the resulting denoiser
\hat{f}
is a genuinely non-linear model which yields a much lower test error than PCA, and learns to leverage flexibly its two components to balance the preservation of the data nuances and the removal of the noise.

Learning metrics

The performance of the DAE (
2
) trained with the loss (
4
) is quantified by its reconstruction (denoising) test MSE, defined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{mse}_{\hat{f}}\equiv\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim\mathbb{P}}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbb{I}_{d})}\left\lVert\bm{x}-f_{\hat{b},\hat{\bm{w}}}\left(\sqrt{1-\Delta}\bm{x}+\sqrt{\Delta}\bm{\xi}\right)\right\lVert^{2}.

(5)

The expectations run over a fresh test sample
\bm{x}
sampled from the Gaussian mixture
\mathbb{P}
(
1
), and a new additive noise
\bm{\xi}
corrupting it. Note that an expectation over the train set
\mathcal{D}
is also included to make
\mathrm{mse}_{\hat{f}}
a metric that does not depend on the particular realization of the train set.
The denoising test MSEs
\mathrm{mse}_{\hat{u}},\mathrm{mse}_{\hat{r}}
are defined similarly as the denoising test errors of the independently learnt components (
3
).
Aside from the denoising MSE (
5
), another question of interest is how much the DAE manages to learn the structure of the data distribution, as described by the cluster means
\bm{\mu}_{k}
. This is measured by the cosine similarity matrix
\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times K}
, where for
i\in\llbracket 1,p\rrbracket
and
k\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket
,

\displaystyle\theta_{ik}\equiv\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\left[\frac{\hat{\bm{w}}_{i}^{\top}\bm{\mu}_{k}}{\lVert\hat{\bm{w}}_{i}\lVert\lVert\bm{\mu}_{k}\lVert}\right].

(6)

In other words,
\theta_{ik}
measures the alignment of the
i-
th row
\hat{\bm{w}}_{i}
of the trained weight matrix
\hat{\bm{w}}
with the mean of the
k-
th cluster
\bm{\mu}_{k}
.

High-dimensional limit

We analyze the optimization problem (
4
) in the high-dimensional limit where the input dimension
d
and number of training samples
n
jointly tend to infinity, while their ratio
\alpha=\nicefrac{{n}}{{d}}
stays
\Theta(1)
. The hidden layer width
p
, the noise level
\Delta
, the number of clusters
K
and the norm of the cluster means
\lVert\bm{\mu}_{k}\lVert
are also assumed to remain
\Theta(1)
. This corresponds to a rich limit, where the number of parameters of the DAE is not large compared to the number of samples like in
NGUYEN2019BenefitsOJ
;
Nguyen2021AnalysisOF
, and therefore cannot trivially fit the train set, or simply memorize it
Radhakrishnan2019OverparameterizedNN
. Conversely, the number of samples
n
is not infinite like in
Refinetti2022TheDO
;
Shevchenko2022FundamentalLO
;
NGUYEN2019OnTD
, and therefore importantly allows to study the effect of a finite train set on the representation learnt by the DAE.

II
Asymptotic formulae for DAEs

We now state the main result of the present work, namely the closed-form asymptotic formulae for the learning metrics
\mathrm{mse}_{\hat{f}}
(
5
) and
\theta
(
6
) for a DAE (
2
) learnt with the empirical loss (
4
), derived using the replica method in its replica-symmetric formulation (see Appendix
A
for the derivation).

Assumption II.1
.

The covariances
\{\bm{\Sigma}\}_{k=1}^{K}
admit a common set of eigenvectors
\{\bm{e}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
. We further note
\{\lambda^{k}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
the eigenvalues of
\bm{\Sigma}_{k}
. The eigenvalues
\{\lambda^{k}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
and the projection of the cluster means on the eigenvectors
\{\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\bm{\mu}_{k}\}_{i,k}
are assumed to admit a well-defined joint distribution
\nu
as
d\to\infty
– namely, for
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},...,\gamma_{K})\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
and
\tau=(\tau_{1},...,\tau_{K})\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{K}\delta\left(\lambda_{i}^{k}-\gamma_{k}\right)\delta\left(\sqrt{d}\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\bm{\mu}_{k}-\tau_{k}\right)\xrightarrow{d\to\infty}\nu\left(\gamma,\tau\right).

(7)

Moreover, the marginals
\nu_{\gamma}
(resp.
\nu_{\tau}
) are assumed to have a well-defined first (resp. second) moment.

Assumption II.2
.

g(\cdot)
is a
\ell_{2}
regularizer with strength
\lambda
, i.e.
g(\cdot)=\nicefrac{{\lambda}}{{2}}\lVert\cdot\lVert_{F}^{2}
.

Result II.3
.

(
Closed-form asymptotics for DAEs trained with empirical risk minimization
) Under Assumptions
II.1
and
II.2
, in the high-dimensional limit
n,d\to\infty
with fixed ratio
\alpha
, the denoising test MSE
\mathrm{mse}_{\hat{f}}
(
5
) admits the expression

\displaystyle\mathrm{mse}_{\hat{f}}-\mathrm{mse}_{\circ}=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\mathbb{E}_{z}\Tr[q\sigma\left({\scriptstyle{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}m_{k}+\sqrt{\Delta q+{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(1-\Delta)}}q_{k}}z}\right)^{\otimes 2}]

(8)

\displaystyle-2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\mathbb{E}_{u,v}\left[{\scriptstyle\sigma\left({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}m_{k}+\sqrt{q_{k}({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}1-\Delta}})}u+\sqrt{\Delta q}v\right)^{\top}\left((1-\hat{b}{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}})(m_{k}+\sqrt{q_{k}}u)-\hat{b}\sqrt{\Delta q}v\right)}\right]+o(1),

where the averages bear over independent Gaussian variables
z,u,v\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbb{I}_{p})
. We denoted

\displaystyle\mathrm{mse}_{\circ}=d\Delta\hat{b}^{2}+\left(1-{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}\hat{b}\right)^{2}\left[\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\left(\int d\nu_{\tau}(\tau)\tau_{k}^{2}+d\int d\nu_{\gamma}(\gamma)\gamma_{k}\right)\right].

(9)

The learnt skip connection strength
\hat{b}
is

\displaystyle\hat{b}=\frac{\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\int d\nu_{\gamma}(\gamma)\gamma_{k}\right){\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}}{\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\int d\nu_{\gamma}(\gamma)\gamma_{k}\right){\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(1-\Delta)}}+\Delta}+o(1).

(10)

The cosine similarity
\theta
(
6
) admits the compact formula for
i\in\llbracket 1,p\rrbracket
and
k\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket

\displaystyle\theta_{ik}=\frac{(m_{k})_{i}}{\sqrt{q_{ii}\int d\nu_{\tau}(\tau)\tau_{k}^{2}}},

(11)

where we have introduced the summary statistics

\displaystyle q=\underset{d\to\infty}{\lim}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\left[\frac{\hat{\bm{w}}\hat{\bm{w}}^{\top}}{d}\right],

\displaystyle q_{k}=\underset{d\to\infty}{\lim}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\left[\frac{\hat{\bm{w}}\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\hat{\bm{w}}^{\top}}{d}\right],

\displaystyle m_{k}=\underset{d\to\infty}{\lim}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\left[\frac{\hat{\bm{w}}\bm{\mu}_{k}}{\sqrt{d}}\right].

(12)

Thus
q,q_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p},~m_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
. The summary statistics
q,q_{k},m_{k}
can be determined as solutions of the system of equations

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\hat{q}_{k}=\alpha\rho_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}V_{k}^{-1}\left(\mathrm{prox}_{y}^{k}-q_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta-m_{k}\right)^{\otimes 2}V_{k}^{-1}\\
\hat{V}_{k}=-\alpha\rho_{k}q_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}V_{k}^{-1}\left(\mathrm{prox}_{y}^{k}-q_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta-m_{k}\right)\eta^{\top}\\
\hat{m}_{k}=\alpha\rho_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}V_{k}^{-1}\left(\mathrm{prox}_{y}^{k}-q_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta-m_{k}\right)\\
\hat{q}=\frac{\alpha}{\Delta}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}V^{-1}\left(\mathrm{prox}^{k}_{x}-\sqrt{\Delta}q^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\right)^{\otimes 2}V^{-1}\\
\hat{V}=-\alpha\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}\Bigg[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta}}q^{-\frac{1}{2}}V^{-1}\left(\mathrm{prox}^{k}_{x}-\sqrt{\Delta}q^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\right)\xi^{\top}-\sigma\left(\sqrt{1-\Delta}\mathrm{prox}_{y}^{k}+\mathrm{prox}_{x}^{k}\right)^{\otimes 2}\Bigg]\end{cases}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}q_{k}=\int d\nu(\gamma,\tau)\gamma_{k}\left(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{p}+\hat{V}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{K}\gamma_{j}\hat{V}_{j}\right)^{-2}\left(\hat{q}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{K}\gamma_{j}\hat{q}_{j}+\sum\limits_{1\leq j,l\leq K}\tau_{j}\tau_{l}\hat{m}_{j}\hat{m}_{l}^{\top}\right)\\

V=\int d\nu(\gamma,\tau)\left(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{p}+\hat{V}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{K}\gamma_{j}\hat{V}_{j}\right)^{-1}\end{cases}

(13)

In (
II.3
),
\hat{q}_{k},\hat{V}_{k},\hat{V},V\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}

\displaystyle+\Tr[q\sigma({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}y+x)^{\otimes 2}]-2\sigma({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}y+x)^{\top}((1-{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}\hat{b})y-\hat{b}x)\Bigg\}.

In the next paragraph, we explore further the interaction between the rescaling component and the bottleneck network.

A tradeoff between the rescaling and the bottleneck network

Result (
II.3
), alongside Corollary (
II.4
) and the discussion in Section
III
provide a firm theoretical basis for the well-known empirical intuition (discussed e.g. in
Mao2016ImageRU
) that skip-connections allow to better propagate information from the input to the output of the DAE, thereby contributing to preserving intrinsic characteristics of the input. This effect is clearly illustrated in Fig.
4
, where the resulting denoised image of an MNIST
7
by
\hat{r}
,
\hat{f}
,
\hat{u}
and PCA are presented. While the bottleneck network
\hat{u}
perfectly eliminates the background noise and produces an image with a very good resolution, it essentially collapses the image to the cluster mean, and yields, like PCA, the average MNIST
7
. As a consequence, the denoised image bears little resemblance with the original image – in particular, the horizontal bar of the
7
is lost in the process. Conversely, the rescaling
\hat{r}
preserves the nuances of the original image, but the result is still largely blurred and displays overall poor contrast. Finally, the complete DAE (
2
) manages to preserve the characteristic features of the original data, while enhancing the image resolution by slightly overlaying the average
7
thereupon.

The optimization of the DAE (
4
) is therefore described by a tradeoff between two competing effects – namely the preservation of the input nuances by the skip connection, and the enhancement of the resolution/noise removal by the bottleneck network. This allows us to discuss the curious non-monotonicity of the cosine similarity
\theta
as a function of the noise level
\Delta
, see Fig.
1
(left). While it may at first seem curious that the DAE seemingly does not manage to learn the data structure better for low
\Delta
than for intermediate
\Delta
(where the cosine similarity
\theta
is observed to be higher), this is actually due to the afore-dicussed tradeoff. Indeed, for small
\Delta
, the data is still substantially clean, and there is therefore no incentive to enhance the contrast by using the cluster means –which are consequently not learnt. This phase is thus characterized by a large skip connection strength
\hat{b}
, and small cosine similarity
\theta
and weight norm
\lVert\hat{\bm{w}}\lVert_{F}
. Conversely, at high noise levels
\Delta
, the nuances of the data are already lost because of the noise. Hence the DAE does not rely on the skip connection component (whence the small values of
\hat{b}
), and the only way to produce reasonably denoised data is to collapse to the cluster mean using the network component (whence a large
\lVert\hat{\bm{w}}\lVert_{F}
).

Conclusion

We consider the problem of denoising a high-dimensional Gaussian mixture, by training a DAE via empirical risk minimization, in the limit where the number of training samples and the dimension are proportionally large. We provide a sharp asymptotic characterization of a number of summary statistics of the trained DAE weight, average MSE, and cosine similarity with the cluster means. These results contain as a corollary the case of RAEs. Building on these findings, we isolate the role of the skip connection and the bottleneck network in the DAE architecture and characterize the tradeoff between those two components in terms of preservation of the data nuances and noise removal – thereby providing some theoretical insight into a longstanding practical intuition in machine learning.

We believe the present work also opens exciting research avenues. First, our real data experiments hint at the presence of Gaussian universality. While this topic has gathered considerable attention in recent years, only classification/regression supervised learning tasks have been hitherto addressed. Which aspects of universality carry over to denoising tasks, and how they differ from the current understanding of supervised regression/classification, is an important question. Second, the DAE with skip connection (
2
) provides an autoencoder model which does not just simply learn the principal components of the training set. It, therefore, affords a genuinely non-linear network model where richer learning settings can be investigated.

Appendix A
Derivation of Result
II.3

In this section, we detail the derivation of Result (
II.3
).

A.1
Derivation technique

In order to access sharp asymptotic characterizations for the metrics and summary statistics
\mathrm{mse}
(
5
),
\theta
(
6
),
\hat{b}
and
\nicefrac{{\lVert\hat{\bm{w}}\lVert}}{{d}}
, the first step is to observe that any test function
\varphi(\hat{\bm{w}},\hat{b})
of the learnt network parameters
\hat{\bm{w}},\hat{b}
can be written as an average over a limit probability distribution

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\left[\varphi(\hat{\bm{w}},\hat{b})\right]=\underset{\beta\to\infty}{\lim}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\frac{1}{Z}\int dwdb\varphi(\bm{w},b)e^{-\beta\mathcal{\hat{R}}(\bm{w},b)}

(16)

where
\mathcal{\hat{R}}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the empirical risk (
4
) and the corresponding probability density

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\beta}(\bm{w},b)=\frac{e^{-\beta\mathcal{\hat{R}}(\bm{w},b)}}{Z}

(17)

is known as the
Boltzmann measure
in statistical physics, with the parameter
\beta
typically referred to as the
inverse temperature
. The normalization
Z
is called the partition function of
\mathbb{P}_{\beta}
:

\displaystyle Z=\int d\bm{w}dbe^{-\beta\mathcal{\hat{R}}(\bm{w},b)}.

(18)

The idea of the replica method
[
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
, building on the expression (
16
), is to compute the moment generating function (also known as the
free entropy
) as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\ln Z=\underset{s\to 0}{\lim}\frac{\ln\left[\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}Z^{s}\right]}{s}.

(19)

With the moment generating function, it then becomes possible to compute averages like (
16
). The backbone of the derivation, which we detail in the following subsections, therefore lies in the computation of the replicated partition function
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}Z^{s}
.

A.2
Replicated partition function

For subsequent clarity, we shall introduce the variable

\displaystyle\bm{\eta}^{\mu}\equiv\bm{x}^{\mu}-\bm{\mu}_{k}

(20)

if
\bm{x}^{\mu}
belongs to the
k-
th cluster. By definition of the Gaussian mixture model, this is just a Gaussian variable:
\bm{\eta}^{\mu}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\bm{\Sigma}_{k})
, and the average over the data set
\{\bm{x}^{\mu}\}
reduces to averaging over the cluster index
k
and
\{\bm{\eta}^{\mu}\}
. For notational brevity, we shall name in this appendix
{\bm{\xi}}^{\mu}
what is called
\sqrt{\Delta}{\bm{\xi}}^{\mu}
in the main text (i.e., absorbing the variance in the definition of
{\bm{\xi}}^{\mu}
). The replicated partition function then reads

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}Z^{s}=

\displaystyle\int\prod\limits_{a=1}^{s}d\bm{w}_{a}db_{a}e^{-\beta\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}g(\bm{w}^{a})}

\displaystyle\times\prod\limits_{\mu=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\eta},{\bm{\xi}}}\underbrace{e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}\left\lVert\bm{\mu}_{k}+\bm{\eta}-\left[b_{a}\times({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}(\bm{\mu}_{k}+\bm{\eta})+{\bm{\xi}})+\frac{\bm{w}_{a}^{\top}}{\sqrt{d}}\sigma\left(\frac{\bm{w}_{a}({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}(\bm{\mu}_{k}+\bm{\eta})+{\bm{\xi}})}{\sqrt{d}}\right)\right]\right\rVert^{2}}}_{(\star)}.

(21)

One can expand the exponent
(\star)
as

\displaystyle e^{{\scriptscriptstyle-\frac{\beta}{2}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}\left[\Tr[\frac{\bm{w}_{a}\bm{w}_{a}^{\top}}{d}\sigma\left(\frac{\bm{w}_{a}({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}(\bm{\mu}_{k}+\bm{\eta})+{\bm{\xi}})}{\sqrt{d}}\right)^{\otimes 2}]-2\sigma\left(\frac{\bm{w}_{a}({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}(\bm{\mu}_{k}+\bm{\eta})+{\bm{\xi}})}{\sqrt{d}}\right)^{\top}\frac{\bm{w}_{a}((1-b_{a}{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}})(\bm{\mu}_{k}+\bm{\eta})-b_{a}{\bm{\xi}})}{\sqrt{d}}\right]}}

\displaystyle\times e^{{\scriptscriptstyle-\frac{\beta}{2}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}\left[(1-{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b_{a})^{2}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\lVert\bm{\mu}_{k}\lVert^{2}}+\lVert\bm{\eta}\rVert^{2}+2\bm{\mu}_{k}^{\top}\bm{\eta})+b_{a}^{2}\lVert{\bm{\xi}}\rVert^{2}+2b_{a}({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b_{a}-1)(\bm{\mu}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{\xi}}+\bm{\eta}^{\top}{\bm{\xi}})\right]}}.

(22)

Therefore

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{s,\bm{\eta},{\bm{\xi}}}(\star)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\lVert\bm{\mu}_{k}\lVert^{2}}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b_{a}-1)^{2}}\int\frac{d\bm{\eta}d{\bm{\xi}}}{(2\pi\sqrt{\Delta})^{d}\sqrt{\det\bm{\Sigma}_{k}}}

\displaystyle\underbrace{e^{{\scriptscriptstyle-\frac{1}{2}\bm{\eta}^{\top}\left(\bm{\Sigma}_{k}^{-1}+\beta\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b_{a}-1)^{2}\mathbb{I}_{d}\right)\bm{\eta}-\frac{1}{2}\lVert{\bm{\xi}}\rVert^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\Delta}+\beta\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}b_{a}^{2}\right)-\beta\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}\left((1-{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b_{a})^{2}\bm{\mu}_{k}^{\top}\bm{\eta}+b_{a}({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b_{a}-1)\bm{\mu}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{\xi}}+b_{a}({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b_{a}-1)\bm{\eta}^{\top}{\bm{\xi}}\right)}}}_{P_{\mathrm{eff.}}(\bm{\eta},{\bm{\xi}})}

\displaystyle\times e^{{\scriptscriptstyle-\frac{\beta}{2}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}\left[\Tr[\frac{\bm{w}_{a}\bm{w}_{a}^{\top}}{d}\sigma\left(\frac{\bm{w}_{a}({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}(\bm{\mu}_{k}+\bm{\eta})+{\bm{\xi}})}{\sqrt{d}}\right)^{\otimes 2}]-2\sigma\left(\frac{\bm{w}_{a}({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}(\bm{\mu}_{k}+\bm{\eta})+{\bm{\xi}})}{\sqrt{d}}\right)^{\top}\frac{\bm{w}_{a}((1-b_{a}{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}})(\bm{\mu}_{k}+\bm{\eta})-b_{a}{\bm{\xi}})}{\sqrt{d}}\right]}}.

(23)

The effective prior over the noises
\bm{\eta},{\bm{\xi}}
is Gaussian with means
\bm{\mu}_{\xi},\bm{\mu}_{\eta}
and covariance
C
:

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{eff.}}(\bm{\eta},{\bm{\xi}})=\mathcal{N}\left(\bm{\eta},{\bm{\xi}};\bm{\mu}_{\eta},\bm{\mu}_{\xi},\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\bm{C}^{-1}_{\eta\eta}&C^{-1}_{\xi\eta}\mathbb{I}_{d}\\
C^{-1}_{\xi\eta}\mathbb{I}_{d}&C^{-1}_{\xi\xi}\mathbb{I}_{d}\end{array}\right]^{-1}\right).

Identifying the covariance components leads to

\displaystyle C^{-1}_{\xi\xi}=\frac{1}{\Delta}+\beta\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}b_{a}^{2},

\displaystyle\bm{C}^{-1}_{\eta\eta}=\bm{\Sigma}_{k}^{-1}+\beta\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b_{a}-1)^{2}\mathbb{I}_{d},

\displaystyle C^{-1}_{\xi\eta}=\beta\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}b_{a}({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b_{a}-1).

(26)

Note that all the sums indexed by
a
introduce terms of
\mathcal{O}(s)
, which can safely be neglected. Identifying the means leads to the following system of equations:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}C^{-1}_{\xi\xi}\bm{\mu}_{\xi}+C^{-1}_{\xi\eta}\bm{\mu}_{\eta}=\beta\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}b_{a}(1-{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b_{a})\bm{\mu}_{k}\\
\bm{C}^{-1}_{\eta\eta}\bm{\mu}_{\eta}+C^{-1}_{\xi\eta}\bm{\mu}_{\xi}=-\beta\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}(1-{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b_{a})^{2}\bm{\mu}_{k}\end{cases},

(27)

which is solved as

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{\xi}=\underbrace{\beta\left(\bm{C}^{-1}_{\eta\eta}C^{-1}_{\xi\xi}-(C^{-1}_{\xi\eta})^{2}\mathbb{I}_{d}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}\left(\bm{C}^{-1}_{\eta\eta}b_{a}(1-{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b_{a})+C^{-1}_{\xi\eta}(1-{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b_{a})^{2}\mathbb{I}_{d}\right)\right)}_{\equiv g^{\mu}_{\xi}}\bm{\mu}_{k},

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{\eta}=\underbrace{-\beta\left(\bm{C}^{-1}_{\eta\eta}C^{-1}_{\xi\xi}-(C^{-1}_{\xi\eta})^{2}\mathbb{I}_{d}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}\left(C^{-1}_{\xi\xi}(1-{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b_{a})^{2}+C^{-1}_{\xi\eta}b_{a}(1-{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b_{a})\mathbb{I}_{d}\right)\right)}_{\equiv g^{\mu}_{\eta}}\bm{\mu}_{k}.

(28)

Again, observe that
g^{\mu}_{\eta},g^{\mu}_{\xi}=\mathcal{O}(s)
and will be safely neglected later in the derivation. Therefore

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{s,\bm{\eta},\xi}(\star)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\frac{e^{{\scriptscriptstyle-\frac{\beta}{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\lVert\bm{\mu}_{k}\lVert^{2}}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b_{a}-1)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}C^{-1}_{\xi\xi}\lVert\bm{\mu}_{\xi}\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\bm{\mu}_{\eta}^{\top}\bm{C}^{-1}_{\eta\eta}\bm{\mu}_{\eta}+C^{-1}_{\xi\eta}\bm{\mu}_{\xi}^{\top}\bm{\mu}_{\eta}}}}{{\scriptstyle\sqrt{\det\bm{\Sigma}_{k}}\Delta^{\frac{d}{2}}\det\left(\bm{C}^{-1}_{\eta\eta}C^{-1}_{\xi\xi}-(C^{-1}_{\xi\eta})^{2}\mathbb{I}_{d}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}}

\displaystyle\times\underbrace{\left\langle e^{{\scriptscriptstyle-\frac{\beta}{2}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}\left[\Tr[\frac{\bm{w}_{a}\bm{w}_{a}^{\top}}{d}\sigma\left(\frac{\bm{w}_{a}({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}(\bm{\mu}_{k}+\bm{\eta})+{\bm{\xi}})}{\sqrt{d}}\right)^{\otimes 2}]-2\sigma\left(\frac{\bm{w}_{a}({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}(\bm{\mu}_{k}+\bm{\eta})+{\bm{\xi}})}{\sqrt{d}}\right)^{\top}\frac{\bm{w}_{a}((1-b_{a}{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}})(\bm{\mu}_{k}+\bm{\eta})-b_{a}{\bm{\xi}})}{\sqrt{d}}\right]}}\right\rangle_{P_{\mathrm{eff.}}(\bm{\eta},{\bm{\xi}})}}_{(a)}.

(29)

We are now in a position to introduce the local fields:

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}^{\xi}=\frac{\bm{w}_{a}({\bm{\xi}}-\bm{\mu}_{\xi})}{\sqrt{d}},

\displaystyle\lambda_{a}^{\eta}=\frac{\bm{w}_{a}(\bm{\eta}-\bm{\mu}_{\eta})}{\sqrt{d}},

\displaystyle h^{k}_{a}=\frac{\bm{w}_{a}\bm{\mu}_{k}}{\sqrt{d}},

(30)

with statistics

\displaystyle\langle\lambda_{a}^{\eta}\lambda_{b}^{\eta}\rangle\approx\frac{\bm{w}_{a}\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\bm{w}_{b}^{\top}}{d},

\displaystyle\langle\lambda_{a}^{\xi}\lambda_{b}^{\eta}\rangle=C_{\xi\eta}\frac{\bm{w}_{a}\bm{w}_{b}^{\top}}{d}\approx 0,

\displaystyle\langle\lambda_{a}^{\xi}\lambda_{b}^{\xi}\rangle\approx\Delta\frac{\bm{w}_{a}\bm{w}_{b}^{\top}}{d}.

(31)

We used the leading order of the covariances

\bm{C}_{\xi\xi,\eta\eta,\eta\xi}
. One therefore has to introduce the summary statistics:

\displaystyle Q_{ab}=\frac{\bm{w}_{a}\bm{w}_{b}^{\top}}{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p},

\displaystyle S^{k}_{ab}=\frac{\bm{w}_{a}\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\bm{w}_{b}^{\top}}{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p},

\displaystyle m_{a}^{k}=\frac{\bm{w}_{a}\bm{\mu}_{k}}{\sqrt{d}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}.

(32)

Note that the local fields
\lambda_{a}^{\eta,\xi}
thus follow a Gaussian distribution:

\displaystyle(\lambda^{\xi}_{a},\lambda^{\eta}_{a})_{a=1}^{s}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}S^{k}&0\\
0&\Delta Q\end{bmatrix}}_{\equiv\Omega_{k}}\right).

(33)

Going back to the computation,
(a)
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\Big\langle

\displaystyle e^{{\scriptscriptstyle-\frac{\beta}{2}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}\Tr\left[Q_{aa}\sigma\left(m_{a}^{k}({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}(1+g^{\mu}_{\eta})+g^{\mu}_{\xi})+{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}\lambda_{a}^{\eta}+\lambda_{a}^{\xi}\right)^{\otimes 2}\right]}}

\displaystyle\times e^{{\scriptscriptstyle-\frac{\beta}{2}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}\left[-2\sigma\left(m^{k}_{a}({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}(1+g^{\mu}_{\eta})+g^{\mu}_{\xi})+{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}\lambda_{a}^{\eta}+\lambda_{a}^{\xi}\right)^{\top}\left(m^{k}_{a}((1-{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b_{a})(1+g^{\mu}_{\eta})-b_{a}g^{\mu}_{\xi})+(1-{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b_{a})\lambda^{\eta}_{a}-b_{a}\lambda^{\xi}_{a}\right)\right]}}\Big\rangle_{\{\lambda^{\xi}_{a},\lambda^{\eta}_{a}\}_{a=1}^{s}}.

(34)

A.3
Reformulating as a saddle-point problem

Introducing Dirac functions enforcing the definitions of
Q_{ab},m_{a}
brings the replicated function in the following form:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}Z^{s}=

\displaystyle\int\prod\limits_{a=1}^{s}db_{a}\prod\limits_{a,b}dQ_{ab}d\hat{Q}_{ab}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{K}\prod\limits_{a=1}^{s}dm_{a}d\hat{m}_{a}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{K}\prod\limits_{a,b}dS^{k}_{ab}d\hat{S}^{k}_{ab}

\displaystyle\underbrace{e^{-d\sum\limits_{a\leq b}Q_{ab}\hat{Q}_{ab}-d\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\sum\limits_{a\leq b}S^{k}_{ab}\hat{S}^{k}_{ab}-\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\sum\limits_{a}dm^{k}_{a}\hat{m}^{k}_{a}}}_{e^{\beta sd\Psi_{t}}}

\displaystyle\underbrace{\int\prod\limits_{a=1}^{s}dw_{a}e^{-\beta\sum\limits_{a}g(\bm{w}^{a})+\sum\limits_{a\leq b}\hat{Q}_{ab}\bm{w}_{a}\bm{w}_{b}^{\top}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\sum\limits_{a\leq b}\hat{S}^{k}_{ab}\bm{w}_{a}\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\bm{w}_{b}^{\top}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\sum\limits_{a}\hat{m}^{k}_{a}\sqrt{d}\bm{w}_{a}\bm{\mu}_{k}}}_{e^{\beta sd\Psi_{w}}}

\displaystyle\underbrace{\left[\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\frac{e^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{O}(s^{2})e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\lVert\bm{\mu}_{k}\lVert^{2}}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b_{a}-1)^{2}}}}}{{\scriptstyle\sqrt{\det\bm{\Sigma}_{k}}\Delta^{\frac{d}{2}}\det\left(\bm{C}^{-1}_{\eta\eta}C^{-1}_{\xi\xi}-\mathcal{O}(s^{2})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}}(a)\right]^{\alpha d}}_{e^{\alpha sd^{2}\Psi_{\mathrm{quad.}}+\beta sd\Psi_{y}}}.

(35)

We introduced the trace, entropic and energetic potentials
\Psi_{t},\Psi_{w},\Psi_{y}
. Since all the integrands scale exponentially (or faster) with
d
, this integral can be computed using a saddle-point method. To proceed further, note that the energetic term encompasses two types of terms, scaling like
sd^{2}
and
sd
. More precisely,

\displaystyle\left[\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\frac{e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\lVert\bm{\mu}_{k}\lVert^{2}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b_{a}-1)^{2}}}}{{\scriptstyle\sqrt{\det\bm{\Sigma}_{k}}\Delta^{\frac{d}{2}}\det\left(\bm{C}^{-1}_{\eta\eta}C^{-1}_{\xi\xi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}}(a)\right]^{\alpha d}

\displaystyle=\!\!\Bigg[1\!\!-\!\!sd\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\frac{1}{sd}\ln\left({\scriptstyle\sqrt{\det\bm{\Sigma}_{k}}\Delta^{\frac{d}{2}}\det\left(\bm{C}^{-1}_{\eta\eta}C^{-1}_{\xi\xi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\!\!+\!\!s\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\frac{1}{s}\ln(a)-\!\!\frac{\beta}{2}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\lVert\bm{\mu}_{k}\lVert^{2}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b_{a}\!\!-\!\!1)^{2}\Bigg]^{\alpha d}

\displaystyle\approx e^{-s\alpha d^{2}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\frac{1}{sd}\ln\left({\scriptstyle\sqrt{\det\bm{\Sigma}_{k}}\Delta^{\frac{d}{2}}\det\left(\bm{C}^{-1}_{\eta\eta}C^{-1}_{\xi\xi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)+s\alpha d\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\frac{1}{s}\ln(a)}.

(36)

Note that the saddle-point method involves an intricate extremization over
s\times s
matrices
Q,S_{k}
. To make further progress, we assume the extremum is realized at the Replica Symmetric (RS) fixed point

\displaystyle\forall a,\qquad b_{a}=b

\displaystyle\forall a,\qquad m_{a}^{k}=m_{k},\qquad\hat{m}^{k}_{a}=\hat{m}_{k}

\displaystyle\forall a,b,\qquad Q_{ab}=(r-q)\delta_{ab}+q,\qquad\hat{Q}_{ab}=-\left(\frac{\hat{r}}{2}+\hat{q}\right)\delta_{ab}+\hat{q}

\displaystyle\forall a,b,\qquad S^{k}_{ab}=(r_{k}-q_{k})\delta_{ab}+q_{k},\qquad\hat{S}^{k}_{ab}=-\left(\frac{\hat{r}_{k}}{2}+\hat{q}_{k}\right)\delta_{ab}+\hat{q}_{k}

(37)

This is a standard assumption known as the
RS ansatz

[
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
.

Quadratic potential

The quadratic potential
\Psi_{\mathrm{quad.}}
, which correspond to the leading order term in
d
in the exponent, needs to be extremized first in the framework of our saddle-point analysis. Its expression can be simplified as

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\ln\left({\scriptstyle\sqrt{\det\bm{\Sigma}_{k}}\Delta^{\frac{d}{2}}\det\left(\bm{C}^{-1}_{\eta\eta}C^{-1}_{\xi\xi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\ln\det\left[(1+\Delta\beta sb^{2})\left(\mathbb{I}_{d}+\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\beta s(\sqrt{1-\Delta}b-1)^{2}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta s}{2}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\Tr[\Delta b^{2}\mathbb{I}_{d}+\bm{\Sigma}_{k}(\sqrt{1-\Delta}b-1)^{2}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta ds}{2}\left[\Delta b^{2}+(\sqrt{1-\Delta}b-1)^{2}\frac{1}{d}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\Tr\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\right],

(38)

which is extremized for

\displaystyle b=\frac{\frac{1}{d}\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\Tr\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\right){\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}}{\frac{1}{d}\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\Tr\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\right){\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(1-\Delta)}}+\Delta}.

(39)

This fixes the skip connection strength
b
.

Entropic potential

We now turn to the entropic potential
\Psi_{w}
. It is convenient to introduce the variance order parameters

\displaystyle\hat{V}\equiv\hat{r}+\hat{q},

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{k}\equiv\hat{r}_{k}+\hat{q}_{k}.

(40)

The entropic potential can then be expressed as

\displaystyle e^{\beta sd\Psi_{w}}

\displaystyle=\int\prod\limits_{a=1}^{s}dw_{a}e^{-\beta\sum\limits_{a}g(\bm{w}^{a})-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}\Tr[\hat{V}\bm{w}_{a}\bm{w}_{a}^{\top}]+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{a,b}\Tr[\hat{q}\bm{w}_{a}\bm{w}_{b}^{\top}]+\hat{m}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}\sqrt{d}\hat{m}_{k}^{\top}\bm{w}_{a}^{\top}\bm{\mu}_{k}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}\Tr[\hat{V}_{k}\bm{w}_{a}\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\bm{w}_{a}^{\top}]+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\sum\limits_{a,b}\Tr[\hat{q}_{k}\bm{w}_{a}\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\bm{w}_{b}^{\top}]}

\displaystyle=\int D\bm{\Xi}_{0}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{K}D\bm{\Xi}_{k}

\displaystyle\qquad\left[\int dwe^{-\beta g(\bm{w})-\frac{1}{2}\Tr[\hat{V}\bm{w}\bm{w}^{\top}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\hat{V}_{k}\bm{w}\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\bm{w}^{\top}]+\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\sqrt{d}\hat{m}_{k}\bm{\mu}^{\top}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\bm{\Xi}_{k}\odot(\hat{q}_{k}\otimes\bm{\Sigma}_{k})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\bm{\Xi}_{0}\odot(\hat{q}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{d})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\odot\bm{w}}\right]^{s}

\displaystyle=\int\underbrace{D\bm{\Xi}_{0}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{K}D\bm{\Xi}_{k}}_{\equiv D\bm{\Xi}}

\displaystyle\qquad\left[\int dwe^{-\beta g(\bm{w})-\frac{1}{2}\bm{w}\odot\left[\hat{V}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{d}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\hat{V}_{k}\otimes\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\right]\odot\bm{w}+\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\sqrt{d}\hat{m}_{k}\bm{\mu}^{\top}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\bm{\Xi}_{k}\odot(\hat{q}_{k}\otimes\bm{\Sigma}_{k})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\bm{\Xi}_{0}\odot(\hat{q}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{d})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\odot\bm{w}}\right]^{s}.

(41)

Therefore

\displaystyle\beta\Psi_{w}=\frac{1}{d}\int D\bm{\Xi}\ln\left[\int dwe^{-\beta g(\bm{w})-\frac{1}{2}\bm{w}\odot\left[\hat{V}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{d}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\hat{V}_{k}\otimes\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\right]\odot\bm{w}+\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\sqrt{d}\hat{m}_{k}\bm{\mu}^{\top}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\bm{\Xi}_{k}\odot(\hat{q}_{k}\otimes\bm{\Sigma}_{k})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\bm{\Xi}_{0}\odot(\hat{q}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{d})^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\odot\bm{w}}\right].

(42)

For a matrix
\bm{\Xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times d}
and tensors
\bm{A},\bm{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times d}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{p\times d}
, we denoted
(\bm{\Xi}\odot\bm{A})_{kl}=\sum_{ij}\bm{\Xi}^{ij}\bm{A}_{ij,kl}
and
(\bm{A}\odot\bm{B})_{ij,kl}=\sum_{rs}\bm{A}_{ij,rs}\bm{B}_{rs,kl}
.

Energetic potential

In order to compute the energetic potential
\Psi_{y}
, one must first compute the inverse of the covariance
\Omega_{k}
, given by

\displaystyle\Omega_{k}^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}S_{k}^{-1}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{\Delta}Q^{-1}\end{bmatrix}.

(43)

It is straightforward to see that
S_{k}^{-1},Q^{-1}
share the same block structure as
S_{k},Q
, and we shall name for clarity

\displaystyle\left(S_{k}^{-1}\right)_{ab}=\left(\tilde{r}_{k}-\tilde{q}_{k}\right)\delta_{ab}+\tilde{q}_{k},

\displaystyle\left(Q^{-1}\right)_{ab}=\left(\tilde{r}-\tilde{q}\right)\delta_{ab}+\tilde{q}.

(44)

From the Sherman-Morisson lemma, and noting

\displaystyle V_{k}\equiv r_{k}-q_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p},

\displaystyle V\equiv r-q\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p},

(45)

one reaches

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\tilde{r}=V^{-1}-(V+sq)^{-1}qV^{-1}\\
\tilde{q}=-(V+sq)^{-1}qV^{-1}\end{cases},

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\tilde{r}_{k}=V_{k}^{-1}-(V_{k}+sq_{k})^{-1}q_{k}V_{k}^{-1}\\
\tilde{q}_{k}=-(V_{k}+sq_{k})^{-1}q_{k}V_{k}^{-1}\end{cases}.

(46)

We will also need the expression of the determinants

\displaystyle\ln\det S_{k}=s\det V_{k}+s\Tr[V^{-1}_{k}q_{k}],

\displaystyle Q=s\det V+s\Tr[V^{-1}q].

(47)

One can finally proceed in analyzing the term
(a)
:

\displaystyle(a)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\frac{\prod\limits_{a=1}^{s}d\lambda^{\eta}_{a}d\lambda^{\xi}_{a}}{(2\pi)^{ps}\sqrt{\det Q\det S_{k}}\sqrt{\Delta}^{ps}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}(\lambda^{\eta}_{a})^{\top}(\tilde{r}_{k}-\tilde{q}_{k})\lambda_{a}^{\eta}-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{a,b}(\lambda^{\eta}_{a})^{\top}\tilde{q}_{k}\lambda_{b}-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}(\lambda^{\xi}_{a})^{\top}(\tilde{r}-\tilde{q})\lambda_{a}^{\xi}-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\sum\limits_{a,b}(\lambda^{\xi}_{a})^{\top}\tilde{q}\lambda_{b}}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{s}(*)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\frac{D\xi D\eta}{(2\pi)^{ps}\sqrt{\det Q\det S_{k}}\sqrt{\Delta}^{ps}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}d\lambda_{\eta}d\lambda_{\xi}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\eta}^{\top}V_{k}^{-1}\lambda_{\eta}+\lambda_{\eta}^{\top}V_{k}^{-1}q_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta-\frac{1}{2\Delta}\lambda_{\xi}^{\top}V^{-1}\lambda_{\xi}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\Delta}}\lambda_{\xi}^{\top}V^{-1}q^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi-\frac{\beta}{2}(*)}\right]^{s}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}D\xi D\eta\left[\int\mathcal{N}\left(\lambda_{\xi};\sqrt{\Delta}q^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi,\Delta V\right)\mathcal{N}\left(\lambda_{\eta};q^{\frac{1}{2}}_{k}\xi,V_{k}\right)e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}(*)}\right]^{s}

\displaystyle=1+s\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}D\xi D\eta\ln\left[\int\mathcal{N}\left(\lambda_{\xi};\sqrt{\Delta}q^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi,\Delta V\right)\mathcal{N}\left(\lambda_{\eta};q^{\frac{1}{2}}_{k}\xi,V_{k}\right)e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}(*)}\right]

(48)

where we noted with capital
D
an integral over a
p-
dimensional Gaussian distribution
\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbb{I}_{p})
. Therefore

\displaystyle\beta\Psi_{y}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}D\xi D\eta\ln\left[\int\mathcal{N}\left(\lambda_{\xi};\sqrt{\Delta}q^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi,\Delta V\right)\mathcal{N}\left(\lambda_{\eta};q^{\frac{1}{2}}_{k}\xi,V_{k}\right)e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}(*)}\right].

(49)

A.4
zero-temperature limit

In this subsection, we take the zero temperature
\beta\rightarrow\infty
limit. Rescaling

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}V\leftarrow V,

\displaystyle\beta\hat{V}\leftarrow\hat{V},

\displaystyle\beta^{2}\hat{q}\leftarrow\hat{q},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}V_{k}\leftarrow V_{k},

\displaystyle\beta\hat{V}_{k}\leftarrow\hat{V}_{k},

\displaystyle\beta^{2}\hat{q}_{k}\leftarrow\hat{q}_{k},

\displaystyle\beta\hat{m}_{k}\leftarrow\hat{m}_{k},

(50)

one has that

\displaystyle\Psi_{w}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2d}\Tr[\left(\hat{V}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{d}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\hat{V}_{k}\otimes\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\right)^{-1}\odot\left(\hat{q}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{d}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\hat{q}_{k}\otimes\bm{\Sigma}_{k}+d\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\hat{m}_{k}\bm{\mu}_{k}^{\top}\right)^{\otimes 2}\right)]-\frac{1}{d}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\Xi}}\mathcal{M}_{r}(\bm{\Xi}),

(51)

where we introduced the Moreau enveloppe

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{r}(\bm{\Xi})

\displaystyle=\underset{\bm{w}}{\inf}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert{\scriptstyle\left(\hat{V}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{d}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\hat{V}_{k}\otimes\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\odot\bm{w}-\left(\hat{V}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{d}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\hat{V}_{k}\otimes\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\!\!\!\!\!\odot\left((\hat{q}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{d})^{\frac{1}{2}}\odot\bm{\Xi}_{0}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}(\hat{q}_{k}\otimes\bm{\Sigma}_{k})^{\frac{1}{2}}\odot\bm{\Xi}_{k}+\sqrt{d}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\hat{m}_{k}\bm{\mu}_{k}^{\top}\right)}\right\lVert^{2}\!\!\!\!+g(\bm{w})\right\}.

(52)

Note that while this optimization problem is still cast in a space of dimension
p\times d
, for some regularizers
g(\cdot)
including the usual
\ell_{1,2}
penalties, the Moreau enveloppe admits a compact analytical expression.

The energetic potential
\Psi_{y}
also simplifies in this limit to

\displaystyle\Psi_{y}=-\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\eta,\xi}\mathcal{M}_{k}(\eta,\xi),

(53)

Where

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{k}(\xi,\eta)=\frac{1}{2}\underset{x,y}{\inf}\Bigg\{

\displaystyle\Tr[V_{k}^{-1}\left(y-q_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta-m_{k}\right)^{\otimes 2}]+\frac{1}{\Delta}\Tr[V^{-1}\left(x-\sqrt{\Delta}q^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\right)^{\otimes 2}]

\displaystyle\Tr[q\sigma({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}y+x)^{\otimes 2}]-2\sigma({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}y+x)^{\top}((1-{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b)y-bx)\Bigg\}.

(54)

It is immediate to see that the trace potential
\Psi_{t}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\Psi_{t}=\frac{\Tr[\hat{V}q]-\Tr[\hat{q}V]}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}(\Tr[\hat{V}_{k}q_{k}]-\Tr[\hat{q}_{k}V_{k}])-\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}m_{k}\hat{m}_{k}.

(55)

Putting these results together, the total free entropy reads

\displaystyle\Phi=

\displaystyle\underset{q,m,V,\hat{q},\hat{m},\hat{V},\{q_{k},m_{k},V_{k},\hat{q}_{k},\hat{m}_{k},\hat{V}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}}{\mathrm{extr}}\frac{\Tr[\hat{V}q]-\Tr[\hat{q}V]}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}(\Tr[\hat{V}_{k}q_{k}]-\Tr[\hat{q}_{k}V_{k}])-\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}m_{k}\hat{m}_{k}

\displaystyle-\alpha\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}\mathcal{M}_{k}(\xi,\eta)-\frac{1}{d}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\Xi}}\mathcal{M}_{r}(\bm{\Xi})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2d}\Tr[\left(\hat{V}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{d}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\hat{V}_{k}\otimes\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\right)^{-1}\odot\left(\hat{q}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{d}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\hat{q}_{k}\otimes\bm{\Sigma}_{k}+d\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\hat{m}_{k}\bm{\mu}_{k}^{\top}\right)^{\otimes 2}\right)]

(56)

where we remind that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{k}(\xi,\eta)=\frac{1}{2}\underset{x,y}{\inf}\Bigg\{

\displaystyle\Tr[V_{k}^{-1}\left(y-q_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta-m_{k}\right)^{\otimes 2}]+\Tr[V^{-1}\left(x-q^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\right)^{\otimes 2}]

\displaystyle+\Tr[q\sigma({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}y+x)^{\otimes 2}]-2\sigma({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}y+x)^{\top}((1-{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b)y-bx)\Bigg\}.

(57)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{r}(\bm{\Xi})

Self-consistent equations

We remind that the skip connection

Training error

The replica formalism also allows to compute a sharp asymptotic characterization for the training MSE

\displaystyle\epsilon_{t}\equiv\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\left[\frac{1}{nd}\sum\limits_{\mu=1}^{n}\left\lVert\bm{x}^{\mu}-\hat{f}(\tilde{\bm{x}}^{\mu})\right\lVert^{2}\right].

(65)

We have skipped the discussion of this learning metric in the main text for the sake of conciseness, but will now detail the derivation. Note that the training error
\epsilon_{t}
can be deduced from the risk (
4
) and the average regularization as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{t}=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\left[\hat{\mathcal{R}}(\hat{\bm{w}},\hat{b})\right]-\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\left[r(\hat{\bm{w}})\right].

(66)

Note that in turn, from the definition of the free entropy, the average risk (training loss) can be computed as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\left[\hat{\mathcal{R}}(\hat{\bm{w}},\hat{b})\right]=-\underset{\beta\to\infty}{\lim}\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\partial\ln Z}{\partial\beta}=-\underset{\beta\to\infty}{\lim}\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\beta}.

(67)

Doing so reveals

\displaystyle\epsilon_{t}

\displaystyle=-2\underset{\beta\to\infty}{\lim}\frac{\partial(\Psi_{y}-d\Psi_{\mathrm{quad}}-\frac{\beta}{2}m(\sqrt{1-\Delta}b-1)^{2}))}{\partial\beta}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{mse}_{\circ}+\underset{\beta\to\infty}{\lim}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}D\xi D\eta\frac{\int\mathcal{N}\left(\lambda_{\xi};q^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi,V_{k}\right)\mathcal{N}\left(\lambda_{\eta};q^{\frac{1}{2}}_{k}\xi,V\right)(*)e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}(*)}}{\int\mathcal{N}\left(\lambda_{\xi};q^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi,V_{k}\right)\mathcal{N}\left(\lambda_{\eta};q^{\frac{1}{2}}_{k}\xi,V\right)e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}(*)}}.

(68)

Taking the
\beta\to\infty
limit finally leads to

\displaystyle\epsilon_{t}=\mathrm{mse}_{\circ}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}

\displaystyle\Bigg[\Tr[q\sigma({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}\mathrm{prox}_{y}+\mathrm{prox}_{x})^{\otimes 2}]

\displaystyle-2\sigma({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}\mathrm{prox}_{y}+\mathrm{prox}_{x})^{\top}((1-{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}\hat{b})\mathrm{prox}_{y}-\hat{b}\mathrm{prox}_{x})\Bigg]

(69)

The sharp asymptotic formula (

) agrees well with numerical simulations, Fig.

. As is intuitive, the generalization gap

grows with the noise level

, as the learning problem grows harder.

Generic asymptotically exact formulae

This last result ends the derivation of the generic version of Result

, i.e.
not assuming

.

Importantly, note that the characterization (

47

, but not

asymptotic, as the equations (

admits a simple closed-form expression, and the average over

4

5
) involved in the original problem. We give in the next paragraph an example of how Assumption

can be relaxed, for a binary mixture with arbitrary (i.e. not necessarily jointly diagonalizable) covariances
\bm{\Sigma}_{1,2}
.

Example: Anisotropic, heteroscedastic binary mixture

As an illustration, we provide the equivalent of (
59
) for a binary mixture, with generically anisotropic and distinct covariances, for
p=1
, thereby breaking free from Assumption
II.1
. We index the clusters by
+,-
rather than
1,2
for notational convenience. We remind that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\pm}(\xi,\eta)=\frac{1}{2}\underset{x,y}{\inf}\left\{{\scriptstyle\frac{1}{\Delta V}(x-\sqrt{q}\sqrt{\Delta}\xi)^{2}+\frac{1}{V_{\pm}}(y-\sqrt{q_{\pm}}\eta\mp m)^{2}+q\sigma({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}y+x)^{2}-2\sigma({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}y+x)((1-{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}b)y-bx)}\right\}.

(70)

Then the self-consistent equations (
59
) simplify to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}q=\frac{1}{d}\Tr[\left((\lambda+\hat{V})\mathbb{I}_{d}+\hat{V}_{+}\bm{\Sigma}_{+}+\hat{V}_{-}\bm{\Sigma}_{-}\right)^{-2}\left(\hat{q}\mathbb{I}_{d}+\hat{q}_{+}\bm{\Sigma}_{+}+\hat{q}_{-}\bm{\Sigma}_{-}+\hat{m}^{2}d\bm{\mu}\bm{\mu}^{\top}\right)]\\
m=\hat{m}\Tr[\left((\lambda+\hat{V})\mathbb{I}_{d}+\hat{V}_{+}\bm{\Sigma}_{+}+\hat{V}_{-}\bm{\Sigma}_{-}\right)^{-1}\bm{\mu}\bm{\mu}^{\top}]\\
V=\frac{1}{d}\Tr[\left(\lambda+\hat{V})\mathbb{I}_{d}+\hat{V}_{+}\bm{\Sigma}_{+}+\hat{V}_{-}\bm{\Sigma}_{-}\right)^{-1}]\\
q_{\pm}=\frac{1}{d}\Tr[{\scriptstyle\left((\lambda+\hat{V})\mathbb{I}_{d}+\hat{V}_{+}\bm{\Sigma}_{+}+\hat{V}_{-}\bm{\Sigma}_{-}\right)^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{\pm}\left((\lambda+\hat{V})\mathbb{I}_{d}+\hat{V}_{+}\bm{\Sigma}_{+}+\hat{V}_{-}\bm{\Sigma}_{-}\right)^{-1}\left(\hat{q}\mathbb{I}_{d}+\hat{q}_{+}\bm{\Sigma}_{+}+\hat{q}_{-}\bm{\Sigma}_{-}+\hat{m}^{2}d\bm{\mu}\bm{\mu}^{\top}\right)}]\\
V_{\pm}=\frac{1}{d}\Tr[\bm{\Sigma}_{\pm}\left((\lambda+\hat{V})\mathbb{I}_{d}+\hat{V}_{+}\bm{\Sigma}_{+}+\hat{V}_{-}\bm{\Sigma}_{-}\right)^{-1}]\end{cases}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\hat{m}=\alpha\left[\rho\frac{1}{V_{+}}\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}\left(\mathrm{prox}_{y}^{+}-\sqrt{q_{+}}\eta-m\right)-(1-\rho)\frac{1}{V_{-}}\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}\left(\mathrm{prox}_{y}^{-}-\sqrt{q_{-}}\eta+m\right)\right]\\
\hat{q}=\frac{\alpha}{V^{2}\Delta}\left[\rho\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}(\mathrm{prox}_{x}^{+}-\sqrt{q}\sqrt{\Delta}\xi)^{2}+(1-\rho)\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}(\mathrm{prox}_{x}^{-}-\sqrt{q}\sqrt{\Delta}\xi)^{2}\right]\\
\hat{V}=-\alpha\left[\rho\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}\Bigg[\frac{\xi(\mathrm{prox}_{x}^{+}-\sqrt{q}\sqrt{\Delta}\xi)}{\sqrt{q}\sqrt{\Delta}V}-\sigma({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}\mathrm{prox}_{y}^{+}+\mathrm{prox}_{x}^{+})^{2}\right]\\
\qquad+(1-\rho)\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}\left[\frac{\xi(\mathrm{prox}_{x}^{-}-\sqrt{q}\sqrt{\Delta}\xi)}{\sqrt{q}\sqrt{\Delta}V}-\sigma({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}\mathrm{prox}_{y}^{-}+\mathrm{prox}_{x}^{-})^{2}\right]\Bigg]\\
\hat{q}_{+}=\alpha\rho\frac{1}{V_{+}^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}\left(\mathrm{prox}_{y}^{+}-\sqrt{q_{+}}\eta-m\right)^{2}\\
\hat{q}_{-}=\alpha(1-\rho)\frac{1}{V_{-}^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}\left(\mathrm{prox}_{y}^{-}-\sqrt{q_{-}}\eta+m\right)^{2}\\
\hat{V}_{+}=-\alpha\rho\frac{1}{\sqrt{q_{+}}V_{+}}\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}\left(\mathrm{prox}_{y}^{+}-\sqrt{q_{+}}\eta-m\right)\eta\\
\hat{V}_{-}=-\alpha(1-\rho)\frac{1}{\sqrt{q_{-}}V_{-}}\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}\left(\mathrm{prox}_{y}^{-}-\sqrt{q_{-}}\eta+m\right)\eta\end{cases}

(71)

These equations are, as previously discussed, asymptotically exact as
d\to\infty
. While they still involve traces over high-dimensional matrices
\bm{\Sigma}_{k}
, this is a very simple operation. We have therefore reduced the original high-dimensional optimization problem (
4
) to the much simpler one of computing traces like (
A.7
). Crucially, while these traces cannot be generally simplified without Assumption
II.1
, they provide the benefit of a simple and compact expression which bypasses the need of jointly diagonalizable covariances, and which can thus be readily evaluated for any covariance, like real-data covariances. This versatility is leveraged in Appendix
D
.

A.8
End of the derivation: Result
II.3

We finally provide the last step in the derivation of the fully asymptotic characterization of Result
II.3
. Assuming
r(\cdot)=\nicefrac{{\lambda}}{{2}}\lVert\cdot\lVert_{F}^{2}
(Assumption
II.2
), the Moreau envelope
\mathcal{M}_{r}
assumes a very simple expression, and no longer needs to be written as a high-dimensional optimization problem. The resulting free energy can be compactly written as:

\displaystyle\Phi=

\displaystyle\underset{q,m,V,\hat{q},\hat{m},\hat{V},\{q_{k},m_{k},V_{k},\hat{q}_{k},\hat{m}_{k},\hat{V}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}}{\mathrm{extr}}\frac{\Tr[\hat{V}q]-\Tr[\hat{q}V]}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}(\Tr[\hat{V}_{k}q_{k}]-\Tr[\hat{q}_{k}V_{k}])-\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}m_{k}\hat{m}_{k}

\displaystyle-\alpha\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}\mathcal{M}_{k}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\frac{1}{2d}\Tr[\left(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{p}\odot\mathbb{I}_{d}+\hat{V}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{d}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\hat{V}_{k}\otimes\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\right)^{-1}\!\!\!\!\odot\left(\hat{q}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{d}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\hat{q}_{k}\otimes\bm{\Sigma}_{k}+d\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\hat{m}_{k}\bm{\mu}_{k}^{\top}\right)^{\otimes 2}\right)]}_{(*)}

(72)

(*)
is the only segment which still involves high-dimensional matrices, and is therefore not yet full asymptotic. Assuming Assumption
II.1
,
(*)
can be massaged into

\displaystyle(*)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2d}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}\Tr[\left(\lambda+\hat{V}+\lambda^{k}_{i}\hat{V}_{k}\right)^{-1}\left(\hat{q}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\lambda^{k}_{i}\hat{q}_{k}+\sum\limits_{1\leq k,j\leq K}e_{i}^{\top}\mu_{j}\hat{m}_{j}\hat{m}_{k}^{\top}\bm{\mu}_{k}^{\top}e_{i}\right)]

\displaystyle\overset{d\to\infty}{=}\frac{1}{2}\int d\nu(\gamma,\tau)\Tr[\left(\lambda+\hat{V}+\gamma_{k}\hat{V}_{k}\right)^{-1}\left(\hat{q}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\gamma_{k}\hat{q}_{k}+\sum\limits_{1\leq k,j\leq K}\tau_{j}\tau_{k}\hat{m}_{j}\hat{m}_{k}^{\top}\right)]

(73)

The fully asymptotic free energy thus becomes

\displaystyle\Phi=

\displaystyle\underset{q,m,V,\hat{q},\hat{m},\hat{V},\{q_{k},m_{k},V_{k},\hat{q}_{k},\hat{m}_{k},\hat{V}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}}{\mathrm{extr}}\frac{\Tr[\hat{V}q]-\Tr[\hat{q}V]}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}(\Tr[\hat{V}_{k}q_{k}]-\Tr[\hat{q}_{k}V_{k}])-\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}m_{k}\hat{m}_{k}

\displaystyle-\alpha\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}\mathcal{M}_{k}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int d\nu(\gamma,\tau)\Tr[\left(\lambda+\hat{V}+\gamma_{k}\hat{V}_{k}\right)^{-1}\left(\hat{q}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\gamma_{k}\hat{q}_{k}+\sum\limits_{1\leq k,j\leq K}\tau_{j}\tau_{k}\hat{m}_{j}\hat{m}_{k}^{\top}\right)]

(74)

Requiring the free energy to be extremized implies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\hat{q}_{k}=\alpha\rho_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}V_{k}^{-1}\left(\mathrm{prox}_{y}^{k}-q_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta-m_{k}\right)^{\otimes 2}V_{k}^{-1}\\
\hat{V}_{k}=-\alpha\rho_{k}q_{k}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}V_{k}^{-1}\left(\mathrm{prox}_{y}^{k}-q_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta-m_{k}\right)\eta^{\top}\\
\hat{m}_{k}=\alpha\rho_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}V_{k}^{-1}\left(\mathrm{prox}_{y}^{k}-q_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta-m_{k}\right)\\
\hat{q}=\alpha\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}V^{-1}\left(\mathrm{prox}^{k}_{x}-q^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\right)^{\otimes 2}V^{-1}\\
\hat{V}=-\alpha\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\mathbb{E}_{\xi,\eta}\Bigg[q^{-\frac{1}{2}}V^{-1}\left(\mathrm{prox}^{k}_{x}-q^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi\right)\xi^{\top}-\sigma\left(\sqrt{1-\Delta}\mathrm{prox}_{y}^{k}+\mathrm{prox}_{x}^{k}\right)^{\otimes 2}\Bigg]\end{cases}

(75)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}q_{k}=\int d\nu(\gamma,\tau)\gamma_{k}\left(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{p}+\hat{V}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{K}\gamma_{j}\hat{V}_{j}\right)^{-2}\left(\hat{q}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{K}\gamma_{j}\hat{q}_{j}+\sum\limits_{1\leq j,l\leq K}\tau_{j}\tau_{l}\hat{m}_{j}\hat{m}_{l}^{\top}\right)\\

m_{k}=\int d\nu(\gamma,\tau)\tau_{k}\left(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{p}+\hat{V}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{K}\gamma_{j}\hat{V}_{j}\right)^{-1}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{K}\tau_{j}\hat{m}_{j}\\

V=\int d\nu(\gamma,\tau)\left(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{p}+\hat{V}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{K}\gamma_{j}\hat{V}_{j}\right)^{-1}\end{cases},

(76)

which is exactly (
II.3
). This closes the derivation of Result
II.3

We close this appendix by providing further discussion on some points, including the role of the non-linearity
\sigma(\cdot)
, the influence of the weight-tying assumption, and of the (an-)isotropy and (homo/hetero)-scedasticity of the clusters.

The activation function

The figures in the main text were generated using
\sigma=\tanh
. Several studies for RAEs, however, have highlighted that an auto-encoder would optimally seek to place itself in the
linear
region of its non-linearity, so as to learn the principal components of the training data, at least in the non-linear untied weights case, see e.g.
[
3
,
8
]
. In light of these findings, it is legitimate to wonder whether setting a linear activation
\sigma(x)=x
would not yield a better MSE. Fig.
6
shows that it is not the case. For the binary isotropic mixture (
II.3
), the linear activations yield a worse performance than the tanh activation.

Weight-tying

We have assumed that the weights of the DAE (
2
) were tied. While this assumption originates mainly for technical reasons in the derivation, note that it has been also used and discussed in practical setting
[
3
]
as a way to prevent the DAE from going to the linear region of its non-linearity, by making the norm of the first layer weights very small, and that of the second layer very large to compensate
[
8
]
. A full extension of Result
II.3
to the case of the untied weight is a lengthy theoretical endeavour, which we leave for future work. However, note that Fig.
6
shows, in the binary isotropic case, that untying the weights actually leads to a
worse
MSE than the DAE with tied weights. This is a very interesting observation, as a DAE with untied weights obviously has the expressive power to implement a DAE with tied weights. Therefore, this effect should be mainly due to the untied landscape presenting local minima trapping the optimization. A full landscape analysis would present a very important future research topic. Finally, remark that this stands in sharp contrast to the observation of
[
8
]
for non-linear RAEs, where weight-tying worsened the performance, as it prevents the RAE from implementing a linear principal component analysis.

An important conclusion from these two observations is that, in contrast to RAEs, the DAE (
2
) does
not
just implement a linear principal component analysis — weight tying, and the presence of a non-linearity, which are obstacles for RAEs in reaching the PCA MSE, lead for DAEs (
2
) to a
better
MSE. The model (
2
) therefore constitutes a model of a genuinely
non-linear
algorithm, where the non-linearity is helpful and is not undone during training. Further discussion can be found in Section
III
of the main text.

Appendix B
Tweedie baseline

B.1
Oracle denoiser

Tweedie’s formula
[
42
]
provides the best estimator of a clean sample
x
given a noisy sample
\tilde{x}
corrupted by Gaussian noise, as

\displaystyle t(\tilde{\bm{x}})=\frac{\tilde{\bm{x}}}{\sqrt{1-\Delta}}+\frac{\Delta}{\sqrt{1-\Delta}}\gradient\mathbb{P}|_{\tilde{\bm{x}}}.

(77)

Note that this gives the Bayes-optimal estimator for the MSE, assuming
perfect knowledge
of the clean data distribution
\mathbb{P}
from which the so-called score
\gradient\mathbb{P}
has to be computed. Of course, this knowledge is inaccessible in general, where the only information on
\mathbb{P}
is provided in the form of the train set
\mathcal{D}
. Therefore, Tweedie’s formula does not give a learning algorithm, but allows to give an oracle lower-bound on the achievable MSEs, as any learning algorithm will yield a higher MSE than this information-theoretic baseline.

For a generic Gaussian mixture (
1
), Tweedie’s formula reduces to the expression

\displaystyle t(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\Delta}}\frac{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\left[\tilde{\bm{\Sigma}}_{k}(\tilde{\bm{\Sigma}}_{k}+\Delta\mathbb{I}_{d})^{-1}\bm{x}+(\tilde{\bm{\Sigma}}_{k}+\Delta\mathbb{I}_{d})^{-1}\tilde{\bm{\mu}}_{k})\right]\mathcal{N}\left(\bm{x};\tilde{\bm{\mu}}_{k},\tilde{\bm{\Sigma}}_{k}+\Delta\mathbb{I}_{d}\right)}{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}\mathcal{N}\left(\bm{x};\tilde{\bm{\mu}}_{k},\tilde{\bm{\Sigma}}_{k}+\Delta\mathbb{I}_{d}\right)},

(78)

where we noted

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{\mu}}_{k}=\sqrt{1-\Delta}\bm{\mu}_{k},

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{\Sigma}}_{k}=(1-\Delta)\bm{\Sigma}_{k}.

(79)

B.2
Oracle test MSE

In general, except in the complete homoscedastic case where all clusters have the same covariance, there is no closed-form asymptotic expression for the MSE achieved by the Tweedie denoiser.
In the binary homoscedastic case
\bm{\mu}_{1}=-\bm{\mu}_{2}\equiv\bm{\mu}
,
\bm{\Sigma}_{1}=\bm{\Sigma}_{2}=\sigma^{2}\mathbb{I}_{d}
(see Fig.
1
), Tweedie’s formula (
78
) reduces to the compact form

\displaystyle t(\bm{x})=\frac{\sqrt{1-\Delta}\sigma^{2}}{\sigma^{2}(1-\Delta)+\Delta}\tilde{\bm{x}}+\frac{\Delta}{\sigma^{2}(1-\Delta)+\Delta}\tanh\left(\frac{\tilde{\bm{x}}^{\top}\bm{\mu}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}{\sigma^{2}(1-\Delta)+\Delta}\right)\times\bm{\mu}

(80)

Note that this is of the same form as the DAE architecture (
2
). The associated MSE reads

\displaystyle\mathrm{mse}^{\star}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x},\bm{\xi}}\left\lVert\bm{x}-\left[b\times({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}\bm{x}+\bm{\xi})+\frac{\Delta\bm{\mu}}{\sigma^{2}{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(1-\Delta)}}+\Delta}\sigma\left(\frac{{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}\bm{\mu}^{\top}({\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}\bm{x}+\bm{\xi})}{\sigma^{2}{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(1-\Delta)}}+\Delta}\right)\right]\right\rVert^{2}.

(81)

A sharp asymptotic formula can be found to be

\displaystyle\mathrm{mse}^{\star}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{mse}_{\mathrm{\circ}}+\frac{\sigma_{e}^{4}}{(\sigma^{2}{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(1-\Delta)}}+\Delta)^{2}}\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{s=\pm 1}\mathbb{E}_{z}^{\mathcal{N}(0,1)}\left[\sigma\left(\frac{{\scriptstyle{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(1-\Delta)}}s+{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}z\times\sqrt{\Delta+{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(1-\Delta)}}\sigma^{2}}}}{\sigma^{2}{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(1-\Delta)}}+\Delta}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{2\Delta}{\sigma^{2}{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(1-\Delta)}}+\Delta}\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{s=\pm 1}\mathbb{E}_{z}^{\mathcal{N}(0,1)}\left[\sigma\left(\frac{{\scriptstyle{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(1-\Delta)}}s+{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}}z\times\sqrt{\Delta+{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(1-\Delta)}}\sigma^{2}}}}{\sigma^{2}{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(1-\Delta)}}+\Delta}\right)\right]\times\left((1-b{\tiny{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sqrt{1-\Delta}}})s\right).

(82)

This is the theoretical characterization plotted in Figs.
1
and
3
in the main text.

Appendix C
Bayes-optimal MSE

C.1
Assuming imperfect prior knowledge

The Tweedie denoiser discussed in Appendix
B
is an
oracle
denoiser, in the sense that perfect knowledge of all the parameters
\{\bm{\mu}_{k},\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
of the Gaussian mixture distribution
\mathbb{P}
(
1
) is assumed. Therefore, though the comparison of the DAE (
2
) MSE with the oracle Tweedie MSE
\mathrm{mse}^{\star}
does provide useful intuition (see Fig.
3
), it importantly does not allow to disentangle which part of the MSE of the DAE is due to the limited expressivity of the architecture, and which is due to the limited availablility of training samples – which entails
imperfect
knowledge of the parameters
\{\bm{\mu}_{k},\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
of the Gaussian mixture distribution
\mathbb{P}
(
1
). In this appendix, we derive the MSE of the Bayes optimal estimator (with respect to the MSE), assuming only knowledge of the distribution from which the parameters
\{\bm{\mu}_{k},\bm{\Sigma}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
are drawn. For simplicity and conciseness, we limit ourselves to the binary isotropic and homoscedastic mixture (see e.g. Fig.
1
), for which
\bm{\mu}_{1}=-\bm{\mu}_{2}\equiv\bm{\mu}^{\star}
and
\bm{\Sigma}_{1}=\bm{\Sigma}_{2}=\sigma\mathbb{I}_{d}
. For definiteness, we assume
\bm{\mu}\sim\mathbb{P}_{\mu}
, with
\mathbb{P}_{\mu}=\mathcal{N}(0,\nicefrac{{\mathbb{I}_{d}}}{{d}})
, so that with high probability
\lVert\bm{\mu}\lVert=1
. We shall moreover assume for ease of discussion that
\sigma
is perfectly known. Thus, the centroid
\bm{\mu}
is the only unknown parameter.

C.2
Bayes-optimal denoiser without knowledge of cluster means

The corresponding Bayes-optimal denoiser then reads

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}(\tilde{\bm{x}})

\displaystyle\equiv\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{x}|\tilde{\bm{x}},\sigma,\mathcal{D}\right]=\int d\bm{\mu}\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left[\bm{x}|\tilde{\bm{x}},\bm{\mu},\sigma,\mathcal{D}\right]}_{t(\bm{\mu},\tilde{\bm{x}})}\mathbb{P}(\bm{\mu}|\sigma,\mathcal{D})

(83)

Where we have identified the oracle denoiser (
80
), and emphasized the dependence on
\bm{\mu}
. Note that this is a slight abuse of notation, as the true oracle
t(\bm{\mu}^{\star},\tilde{\bm{x}})
involves the ground truth centroid. Further note that
\mathbb{P}(\bm{\mu}|\sigma,\mathcal{D})=\mathbb{P}(\bm{\mu}|\mathcal{D})
, since knowledge of
\sigma
does not bring information about
\bm{\mu}
. Furthermore, note that as the noisy part of the dataset
\tilde{\mathcal{D}}\equiv\{\tilde{\bm{x}}^{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{n}
brings only redundant information, one further has that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\bm{\mu}|\mathcal{D})=\mathbb{P}(\bm{\mu}|\mathcal{\check{D}}),

(84)

where we noted
\mathcal{\check{D}}=\{\bm{x}^{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{n}
the clean part of the dataset. Thus

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}(\tilde{\bm{x}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{\check{D}})}\int d\bm{\mu}~~t(\bm{\mu},\tilde{\bm{x}})\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{\check{D}}|\bm{\mu})\mathbb{P}_{\mu}(\bm{\mu})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{\check{D}})}\int d\bm{\mu}~~t(\bm{\mu},\tilde{\bm{x}})\mathbb{P}_{\mu}(\bm{\mu})\prod\limits_{\mu=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{\nicefrac{{d}}{{2}}}}\left[e^{-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\lVert\bm{x}^{\mu}-\bm{\mu}\lVert^{2}}+e^{-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\lVert\bm{x}^{\mu}+\bm{\mu}\lVert^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{\check{D}})}\int d\bm{\mu}~~t(\bm{\mu},\tilde{\bm{x}})\frac{1}{(\nicefrac{{2\pi}}{{d}})^{\nicefrac{{d}}{{2}}}}e^{-\frac{d}{2}\lVert\bm{\mu}\lVert^{2}}e^{-\frac{n}{2\sigma^{2}}\lVert\bm{\mu}\lVert^{2}}\prod\limits_{\mu=1}^{n}\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\lVert\bm{x}^{\mu}\lVert^{2}}}{(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{\nicefrac{{d}}{{2}}}}\cosh\left(\frac{\bm{\mu}^{\top}\bm{x}^{\mu}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{\check{D}})}\int d\bm{\mu}~~t(\nicefrac{{\bm{\mu}}}{{\sqrt{d}}},\tilde{\bm{x}})\frac{1}{(\nicefrac{{2\pi}}{{d}})^{\nicefrac{{d}}{{2}}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\lVert\bm{\mu}\lVert^{2}}e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma^{2}}\lVert\bm{\mu}\lVert^{2}}\prod\limits_{\mu=1}^{n}\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\lVert\bm{x}^{\mu}\lVert^{2}}}{(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{\nicefrac{{d}}{{2}}}}\cosh\left(\frac{\bm{\mu}^{\top}\bm{x}^{\mu}}{\sigma^{2}\sqrt{d}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z}\int d\bm{\mu}~~t(\nicefrac{{\bm{\mu}}}{{\sqrt{d}}},\tilde{\bm{x}})\mathbb{P}_{b}(\bm{\mu}),

(85)

where we noted

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{b}(\bm{\mu})\equiv e^{-\frac{1}{2\tilde{\sigma}^{2}}\lVert\bm{\mu}\lVert^{2}}\prod\limits_{\mu=1}^{n}\cosh\left(\frac{\bm{\mu}^{\top}\bm{x}^{\mu}}{\sigma^{2}\sqrt{d}}\right),

(86)

with the effective variance

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}^{2}\equiv\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\sigma^{2}+\alpha}.

(87)

Finally, the partition function
Z
is defined as the normalisation of
\mathbb{P}_{b}
, i.e.

\displaystyle Z\equiv\int d\bm{\mu}~~\mathbb{P}_{b}(\bm{\mu}).

(88)

One is now in a position to compute the MSE associated with the Bayes estimator
\mathfrak{b}(\tilde{\bm{x}})
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{mse}_{\mathfrak{b}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbb{I}_{d})}\left\lVert\bm{x}-\left\langle t(\nicefrac{{\bm{\mu}}}{{\sqrt{d}}},\sqrt{1-\Delta}\bm{x}+\sqrt{\Delta}\bm{\xi})\right\rangle_{\bm{\mu}\sim\mathbb{P}_{b}}\right\lVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{mse}_{\circ}-\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\sum\limits_{s=\pm 1}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi},\bm{\eta}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbb{I}_{d})}\frac{\Delta}{\sigma^{2}(1-\Delta)+\Delta}\Bigg\langle\left({\scriptstyle\frac{(1-b\sqrt{1-\Delta})s\bm{\mu}^{\top}\bm{\mu}^{\star}}{d}+\frac{\bm{\mu}^{\top}((1-b\sqrt{1-\Delta})\sigma\bm{\eta}-b\sqrt{\Delta}\bm{\xi}}{\sqrt{d}}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\tanh\left({\scriptstyle\frac{\sqrt{1-\Delta}\frac{s\bm{\mu}^{\top}\bm{\mu}^{\star}}{2}+\sqrt{1-\Delta}\sigma\frac{\bm{\mu}^{\top}\bm{\eta}}{\sqrt{d}}+\sqrt{\Delta}\frac{\bm{\mu}^{\top}\bm{\xi}}{\sqrt{d}}}{\sigma^{2}(1-\Delta)+\Delta}}\right)\Bigg\rangle_{\bm{\mu}\sim\mathbb{P}_{b}}

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{s=\pm 1}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\xi},\bm{\eta}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbb{I}_{d})}\Bigg\langle\left(\frac{\Delta}{\sigma^{2}(1-\Delta)+\Delta}\right)^{2}\frac{\bm{\mu}_{1}^{\top}\bm{\mu}_{2}}{d}\tanh\left({\scriptstyle\frac{\sqrt{1-\Delta}\frac{s\bm{\mu}_{1}^{\top}\bm{\mu}^{\star}}{d}+\sqrt{1-\Delta}\sigma\frac{\bm{\mu}_{1}^{\top}\bm{\eta}}{\sqrt{d}}+\sqrt{\Delta}\frac{\bm{\mu}_{1}^{\top}\bm{\xi}}{\sqrt{d}}}{\sigma^{2}(1-\Delta)+\Delta}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\tanh\left({\scriptstyle\frac{\sqrt{1-\Delta}\frac{s\bm{\mu}_{2}^{\top}\bm{\mu}^{\star}}{d}+\sqrt{1-\Delta}\sigma\frac{\bm{\mu}_{2}^{\top}\bm{\eta}}{\sqrt{d}}+\sqrt{\Delta}\frac{\bm{\mu}_{2}^{\top}\bm{\xi}}{\sqrt{d}}}{\sigma^{2}(1-\Delta)+\Delta}}\right)\Bigg\rangle_{\bm{\mu}_{1},\bm{\mu}_{2}\sim\mathbb{P}_{b}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{mse}_{\circ}-\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\sum\limits_{s=\pm 1}\mathbb{E}_{z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)}\frac{\Delta}{\sigma^{2}(1-\Delta)+\Delta}\Bigg\langle\left({\scriptstyle\frac{(1-b\sqrt{1-\Delta})s\bm{\mu}^{\top}\bm{\mu}^{\star}}{d}}\right)\tanh\left({\scriptstyle\frac{\sqrt{1-\Delta}\frac{s\bm{\mu}^{\top}\bm{\mu}^{\star}}{d}+\sqrt{(1-\Delta)\sigma^{2}+\Delta}\frac{\lVert\bm{\mu}\lVert}{\sqrt{d}}z}{\sigma^{2}(1-\Delta)+\Delta}}\right)\Bigg\rangle_{\bm{\mu}\sim\mathbb{P}_{b}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{s=\pm 1}\mathbb{E}_{u,v\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Omega)}\Bigg\langle\left(\frac{\Delta}{\sigma^{2}(1-\Delta)+\Delta}\right)^{2}\frac{\bm{\mu}_{1}^{\top}\bm{\mu}_{2}}{d}\tanh\left({\scriptstyle\frac{\sqrt{1-\Delta}\frac{s\bm{\mu}_{1}^{\top}\bm{\mu}^{\star}}{d}+\sqrt{(1-\Delta)\sigma^{2}+\Delta}u}{\sigma^{2}(1-\Delta)+\Delta}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\tanh\left({\scriptstyle\frac{\sqrt{1-\Delta}\frac{s\bm{\mu}_{2}^{\top}\bm{\mu}^{\star}}{d}+\sqrt{(1-\Delta)\sigma^{2}+\Delta}v}{\sigma^{2}(1-\Delta)+\Delta}}\right)\Bigg\rangle_{\bm{\mu}_{1},\bm{\mu}_{2}\sim\mathbb{P}_{b}}.

(89)

We have adopted the shortcuts

\displaystyle b=\frac{\sqrt{1-\Delta}\sigma^{2}}{\sigma^{2}(1-\Delta)+\Delta},

\displaystyle\Omega=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\lVert\bm{\mu}_{1}\lVert^{2}}{d}&\frac{\bm{\mu}_{1}^{\top}\bm{\mu}_{2}}{d}\\
\frac{\bm{\mu}_{1}^{\top}\bm{\mu}_{2}}{d}&\frac{\lVert\bm{\mu}_{2}\lVert^{2}}{d}.\end{bmatrix}.

(90)

(
C.2
) shows that the inner averages involved in the Bayes MSE
\mathrm{mse}_{\mathfrak{b}}
only depend on the overlaps
\nicefrac{{\bm{\mu}_{1,2}^{\top}\bm{\mu}_{1,2}}}{{d}}
for
\bm{\mu}_{1},\bm{\mu}_{2}
two independently sampled vectors from
\mathbb{P}_{b}
. Motivated by similar high-dimensional studies, it is reasonable to expect these quantities to concentrate as
n,d\to\infty
in the measure
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\langle\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{P}_{b}}
. A more principle – but much more painstaking– way to derive this assumption is to introduce a Fourier representation, and carry out the
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}
average using the replica method. We refer the interested reader to, for instance, Appendix H of
[
36
]
, where such a route is taken.

C.3
Derivation

To compute the summary statistics of
\mathbb{P}_{b}
, we again resort to the replica method to compute the moment-generating function (free entropy), see also Appendix
A
. The replicated partition function reads

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}Z^{s}=\int\prod\limits_{a=1}^{s}d\bm{\mu}_{a}e^{-\frac{1}{2\hat{\sigma}^{2}}\lVert\bm{\mu}_{a}\lVert^{2}}\prod\limits_{\mu=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{s=\pm 1}\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{\eta}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbb{I}_{d})}\left[\prod\limits_{a=1}^{s}\cosh\left(\frac{s\frac{\bm{\mu}_{a}^{\top}\bm{\mu}^{\star}}{d}+\sigma\frac{\bm{\mu}_{a}^{\top}\bm{\eta}}{\sqrt{d}}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\right].

(91)

Introducing the order parameters

\displaystyle q_{ab}\equiv\frac{\bm{\mu}_{a}^{\top}\bm{\mu}_{b}}{d},

\displaystyle m_{a}\equiv\frac{\bm{\mu}_{a}^{\top}\bm{\mu}^{\star}}{d},

(92)

one reaches

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}Z^{s}=

\displaystyle\int\prod\limits_{a\leq b}dq_{ab}d\hat{q}_{ab}\prod\limits_{a=1}^{s}dm_{a}d\hat{m}_{a}\underbrace{e^{-d\sum\limits_{a\leq b}q_{ab}\hat{q}_{ab}-d\sum\limits_{a}m_{a}\hat{m}_{a}}}_{e^{sd\Psi_{t}}}\underbrace{\int\prod\limits_{a=1}^{s}d\bm{\mu}_{a}e^{-\frac{1}{2\hat{\sigma}^{2}}\lVert\bm{\mu}_{a}\lVert^{2}}e^{\sum\limits_{a\leq b}\hat{q}_{ab}\bm{\mu}_{a}^{\top}\bm{\mu}_{b}+\sum\limits_{a}\bm{\mu}_{a}^{\top}\bm{\mu}^{\star}}}_{e^{sd\Psi_{w}}}

\displaystyle\underbrace{\left[n\sum\limits_{s=\pm 1}\frac{1}{2}\int\mathcal{N}\left(\{\lambda_{a}\}_{a=1}^{s};0,(q_{ab})_{1\leq a,b\leq s}\right)\left[\prod\limits_{a=1}^{s}\cosh\left(\frac{sm_{a}+\sigma\lambda_{a}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\right]\right]^{n}}_{e^{s\alpha d\Psi_{y}}}.

(93)

The computation of
\Psi_{t},\Psi_{w},\Psi_{y}
is rather standard and follows the main steps presented in Appendix
A
. One again assumes the replica symmetric ansatz
[
21
,
22
]

\displaystyle q_{ab}=(r-q)\delta_{ab}+q,

\displaystyle m_{a}=m,

(94)

\displaystyle\hat{q}_{ab}=\left(-\frac{\hat{r}}{2}+-\hat{q}\right)\delta_{ab}+\hat{q},

\displaystyle\hat{m}_{a}=\hat{m}.

(95)

Introducing as in Appendix
A
the variances

\displaystyle V\equiv r-q,

\displaystyle\hat{V}\equiv\hat{r}+\hat{q},

(96)

one reaches

\displaystyle\Psi_{t}=\frac{\hat{V}q-V\hat{q}}{2}-m\hat{m},

(97)

\displaystyle\Psi_{w}=-\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(1+\hat{V}\hat{\sigma}^{2}\right)+\frac{\hat{\sigma}^{2}}{2}\frac{\hat{q}+\hat{m}^{2}}{1+\hat{V}\hat{\sigma}^{2}},

(98)

\displaystyle\Psi_{y}=\mathbb{E}_{\eta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)}\ln\left[\int d\lambda\mathcal{N}(\lambda;\sqrt{q}\eta,V)\cosh\left(\frac{\sigma\lambda+m}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\right].

(99)

Therefore the free entropy reads

\displaystyle\Phi=\underset{q,m,V,\hat{q},\hat{m},\hat{V}}{\mathrm{extr}}

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{V}q-V\hat{q}}{2}-m\hat{m}-\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(1+\hat{V}\hat{\sigma}^{2}\right)+\frac{\hat{\sigma}^{2}}{2}\frac{\hat{q}+\hat{m}^{2}}{1+\hat{V}\hat{\sigma}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\alpha\mathbb{E}_{\eta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)}\ln\left[\int d\lambda\mathcal{N}(\lambda;\sqrt{q}\eta,V)\cosh\left(\frac{\sigma\lambda+m}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\right].

(100)

The solution of this extremization problem is given by the set of self-consistent equations, imposing that gradients with respect to all parameters be zero:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}V=\frac{\hat{\sigma}^{2}}{1+\hat{V}\hat{\sigma}^{2}}\\
q=\frac{\hat{\sigma}^{4}(\hat{q}+\hat{m}^{2})}{(1+\hat{V}\hat{\sigma}^{2})^{2}}+\frac{\hat{\sigma}^{2}}{1+\hat{V}\hat{\sigma}^{2}}\\
m=\frac{\hat{\sigma}^{2}\hat{m}}{1+\hat{V}\hat{\sigma}^{2}}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\hat{V}=-\alpha\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{q}}\mathbb{E}_{\eta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)}\left[\tanh\left(\frac{\sigma\sqrt{q}\eta+m}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\eta\right]\\
\hat{q}=\frac{\alpha V}{\sigma^{2}}\\
\hat{m}=\frac{\alpha}{\sigma^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{\eta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)}\left[\tanh\left(\frac{\sigma\sqrt{q}\eta+m}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\right]\end{cases}

(101)

This is a simple optimization problem over
6
variables, which can be solved numerically. One can finally use the obtained summary statistics to evaluate the Bayes optimal MSE:

\displaystyle\mathrm{mse}_{\mathfrak{b}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{mse}_{\circ}-2\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\mathbb{E}_{z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)}\frac{\Delta}{\sigma^{2}(1-\Delta)+\Delta}\left({\scriptstyle(1-b\sqrt{1-\Delta})m}\right)\tanh\left({\scriptstyle\frac{\sqrt{1-\Delta}m+\sqrt{(1-\Delta)\sigma^{2}+\Delta}\sqrt{q+V}z}{\sigma^{2}(1-\Delta)+\Delta}}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\mathbb{E}_{u,v\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\Omega)}\left({\scriptstyle\frac{\Delta}{\sigma^{2}(1-\Delta)+\Delta}}\right)^{2}q\tanh\left({\scriptstyle\frac{\sqrt{1-\Delta}m+\sqrt{(1-\Delta)\sigma^{2}+\Delta}u}{\sigma^{2}(1-\Delta)+\Delta}}\right)\tanh\left({\scriptstyle\frac{\sqrt{1-\Delta}m+\sqrt{(1-\Delta)\sigma^{2}+\Delta}v}{\sigma^{2}(1-\Delta)+\Delta}}\right)

(102)

where

\displaystyle\Omega=\begin{bmatrix}q+V&q\\
q&q+V\end{bmatrix}

(103)

\displaystyle\Psi_{w}=

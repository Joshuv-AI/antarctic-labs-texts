Extension problems for the logarithmic Laplacian


Abstract
The logarithmic Laplacian on the (whole) N N -dimensional Euclidean space is defined as the first variation of the fractional Laplacian of order 2 ​ s 2s at s = 0 s=0 or, alternatively, as a singular Fourier integral operator with logarithmic symbol. While this operator has attracted fastly growing attention in recent years due to its relevance in the study of order-dependent problems, a characterization via a local extension problem on the ( N + 1 ) (N+1) -dimensional upper half-space in the spirit of the Cafferelli-Sivestre extension for the fractional Laplacian has been missing so far. In this paper, we establish such a characterization. More precisely, we show that, up to a multiplicative constant, the logarithmic Laplacian coincides with the boundary-value operator associated with a weighted second-order operator on the upper half-space, which maps inhomogeneous Neumann data to a Robin boundary-value of the corresponding distributional solution with a singular excess term. This extension property of the logarithmic Laplacian leads to a new energy functional associated with this operator. By doubling the extension-variable, we show that distributional solutions of the extension problem are actually harmonic in the ( N + 2 ) (N+2) -dimensional Euclidean space away from the boundary. As an application of these results, we establish a weak unique continuation principle for the (stationary) logarithmic Laplace equation.

Introduction

In recent years, the interest in the modeling of nonlocal phenomena via hypersingular
integral operators and their study from a PDE perspective has increased significantly. For an overview of these developments, see
e.g. the recent books
[
9
]
,
[
38
]
and
the references therein. The most prominent role in this context is
played by the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
of order
2s
, which
for
s\in(0,1)
is given by

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=c_{N,s}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\epsilon}(x)}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy\qquad\text{for $u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$.}

(1.1)

Here

c_{N,s}=2^{2s}\pi^{-\frac{N}{2}}s\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N+2s}{2})}{\Gamma(1-s)}

is a normalization constant which is chosen such that
(-\Delta)^{s}

corresponds to the Fourier symbol
\xi\mapsto|\xi|^{2s}
, i.e. we have

\mathcal{F}((-\Delta)^{s}u)(\xi)=|\xi|^{2s}\hat{u}(\xi)\qquad\text{for $u\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$ and $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$.}

(1.2)

Using this property, it is easy to see that

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=u(x)\quad\ {\rm and}\quad\ \quad\ \lim_{s\to 1^{-}}(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=-\Delta u(x)

(1.3)

for
u\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Motivated by this observation, the first and
third author of this paper introduced in
[
16
]
the

logarithmic Laplacian

L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}
as a first order correction in
the first limit in (
1.3
). More precisely, if

u\in C_{c}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some
\alpha>0
, the function
L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}u

is uniquely given, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, by the expansion

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=u(x)+sL_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}u(x)+o(s)\quad{\rm as}\ \,s\to 0^{+},

(1.4)

or equivalently, by

\frac{d}{ds}(-\Delta)^{s}u\Big|_{s=0}=L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}u.

So the logarithmic Laplacian
L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}
can be seen as the derivative of
operator valued map
s\mapsto(-\Delta)^{s}
at
s=0
. Moreover, as noted in

[
16
, Theorem 1.1]

1.2
) leads to

\mathcal{F}(L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}u)(\xi)=2\ln|\xi|\hat{u}(\xi)\qquad\qquad\text{for $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$ if $u\in C^{\alpha}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$ for some $\alpha>0$.}

Hence

is a (weakly) singular Fourier integral operator
associated with the symbol
2\ln|\cdot|
. In addition, it is shown in

[
16

admits the integral

L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}u(x)=c_{N}P.V.\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{u(x)-u(x+y)}{|y|^{N}}\ dy-c_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}(0)}\frac{u(x+y)}{|y|^{N}}\ dy+\rho_{N}\,u(x),

(1.5)

where

c_{N}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}{\pi^{N/2}}=\frac{2}{\omega_{{}_{N}}},\qquad\qquad\rho_{N}:=2\ln(2)+\psi(\frac{N}{2})-\gamma,

(1.6)

\gamma=-\Gamma^{\prime}(1)
is the Euler Mascheroni constant, and

is the Digamma function. Here and in the

denotes the

-dimensional volume of the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. In fact, for

L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}u(x)
to be well defined
by (

\varphi
is Dini
continuous at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and belongs to the space

L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\Big\{u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\>:\>\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1+|x|)^{-N}|u(x)|\,dx<\infty\Big\},

(1.7)

see
[
16
, Proposition 1.3]
.

More recently, the small order limit

Due to its relevance, it is natural to ask whether the logarithmic
Laplacian admits a representation by a local extension problem. This has
been an open question for the last years, and the purpose of the present
paper is to give an affirmative answer to this question. The interest
in local extension problems is very natural as they pave the way for the
application of classical PDE techniques to associated nonlocal
problems. In this context, the starting point of our paper is the
so-called
Caffarelli-Silvestre extension property
of the
fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
given by the characterization

(-\Delta)^{s}=d_{s}\,\Lambda_{s}\qquad\qquad\text{with}\quad d_{s}=2^{2s-1}\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(1-s)},

where
\Lambda_{s}
denotes the
Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator

associated with the weighted second-order differential operator

\mathcal{A}_{s}=-\,{\rm div}\,(t^{1-2s}\nabla\cdot)

on the half space
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}:=\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,+\infty)
. More
precisely, Caffarelli and Silvestre

[
14
]
showed that for every
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there is an
s
-harmonic extension

w_{s}:\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
of
u(x)=w_{s}(x,0)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
; that is,
w_{s}
is a
distributional solution of the
Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} -\,{\rm div}\,(t^{1-2s}\nabla w_{s})=0\quad\ &\text{in \ \ $\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}$,}\\[5.69054pt]
w_{s}=u\quad\ &\text{on \ \ $\mathbb{R}^{N}=\partial\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}$},\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(1.8)

where
s\in(0,1)
, and for the
co-normal derivative

\Lambda_{s}u:=-\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{1-2s}\partial_{t}w_{s}

one has that

(-\Delta)^{s}u=-d_{s}\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{1-2s}\partial_{t}w_{s}

(1.9)

with some constant
d_{s}>0
. The value (
1
)
of the constant
d_{s}
was then given by Cabré and Sire in

[
10
]
). This property of
(-\Delta)^{s}
provides a link between
nonlocal problems involving
(-\Delta)^{s}u
with a local one by extending

u
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}

s
-harmonically on
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
. In particular, it
provides the PDE point of view that a symmetric
2s
-stable Lévy
process (generated by the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
, see

[
5
]
) coincides with the trace process of a degenerate
diffusion process in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
(see
[
44
]
, and for further
details to the literature see
[
33
, Section 2]
).

As mentioned before, the purpose of the present paper is to derive a
representation of the operator logarithmic Laplacian
L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}
via a
local extension problem. In what follows, we shall see that
L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}

admits such a representation, but the corresponding extension problem
and boundary operator are very different from the Caffarelli-Silvestre
extension problem for the fractional Laplacian. To state our main
results, we need the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
and a subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we let
{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{G}}(\Omega)

denote the space of functions
w\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
having at most
algebraic growth in the integral sense, i.e., the space of functions

w\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
with the property that, for some constants

C,\sigma>0
, we have

\|w\|_{L^{1}(B_{1}(x)\cap\Omega)}\leq C(1+|x|)^{\sigma}\qquad\text{for all $x\in\Omega$.}

Further, we recall that, by definition, a function
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}

is Dini continuous at a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
if

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\omega_{u,x}(r)}{r}\,dr<\infty\qquad\text{with}\qquad\omega_{u,x}(r)=\sup_{y\in B_{r}(x)}|u(y)-u(x)|.

Moreover, we call
u

uniformly Dini continuous on a subset
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
if

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\omega_{u,\text{\tiny$K$}}(r)}{r}\,dr<\infty\qquad\text{for $\omega_{u,\text{\tiny$K$}}(r):=\sup\limits_{x\in K}\omega_{u,x}(r)$.}

Now, we can state the first variant of the extension property associated
with the logarithmic Laplacian
L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
u\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there is a unique solution

w_{u}\in{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{G}}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})\cap C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})

of the problem

-\,{\rm div}\,(t\,\nabla w_{u})=0\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}$}

(1.10)

satisfying the asymptotic boundary conditions

-\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t\partial_{t}w_{u}(\cdot,t)=u\qquad\text{in $L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$}

(1.11)

and

\lim_{t\to+\infty}w_{u}(x,t)=0\qquad\text{for every $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$.}

Moreover,
the following statements hold:

(i)

The function
w_{u}
can be represented via the Poisson formula

w_{u}(x,t)=\frac{c_{N}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(\tilde{x})}{(|x-\tilde{x}|^{2}+t^{2})^{N/2}}\,d\tilde{x}\qquad\text{for every $(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}$;}

(1.12)

(ii)

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{w_{u}(\cdot,t)}{\ln t}=-u\qquad\text{in $L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$;}

(1.13)

(iii)

L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}u=2(\ln 2-\gamma)u-2\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\Bigl(w_{u}(\cdot,t)+u\log t\Bigr)\quad\text{in the distributional sense in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$;}

(1.14)

(iv)

If
u
is Dini continuous at a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then
(
1.14
) holds in pointwise sense, i.e.
we have

L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}u(x)=2\,(\ln 2-\gamma)\,u(x)-2\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\Bigl(w_{u}(x,t)+u(x)\ln t\Bigr)

(1.15)

as a pointwise limit in
\mathbb{R}
.

The following comments to Theorem
1.2
are worth stating.

Remark 1.3
.

(i) Due to the characterization given by
(
1.14
)
(respectively,
1.15

as

Alternatively, since for every

, the extension

as limit of the co-normal derivative

), the characterization given by
(

) (respectively,

) says that the logarithmic

coincides up to a constant multiple with the

associated with
\mathcal{A}_{0}
on

\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
and with the singular excess term

as

.

(ii) If

is defined by

hold also in the

-space

. However, in general the functions

(iii) By definition, the distributional limit in

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}uL_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}\varphi dx=2(\ln 2-\gamma)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u\varphi\,dx-2\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Bigl(w_{u}(x,t)+u\ln t\Bigr)\varphi(x)\,dx\quad\>\text{for all $\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$.}

In fact, we shall show that this property already holds if

, where

denotes the space of
uniformly Dini continous functions on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with compact support. A

, which has been introduced in

]
in the form

\varphi\mapsto{\mathcal{E}}_{L}(\varphi,\varphi)=\frac{c_{N}}{2}\int_{|x-\tilde{x}|<1}\frac{(\varphi(x)-\varphi(\tilde{x}))^{2}}{|x-\tilde{x}|^{N}}dxd\tilde{x}-\frac{c_{N}}{2}\int_{|x-\tilde{x}|\geq 1}\frac{\varphi(x)\varphi(\tilde{x})}{|x-\tilde{x}|^{N}}\,dxd\tilde{x}+\frac{\rho_{N}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi(x)^{2}\,dx.

Corollary 1.4
.

For every
\varphi\in C^{D}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, one has that

{\mathcal{E}}_{L}(\varphi,\varphi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}\varphi dx=2(\ln 2-\gamma)\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}-2\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Bigl(\varphi(x)w_{\varphi}(x,t)+\varphi^{2}(x)\ln t\Bigr)\,dx.

Next we note that we may replace the nonhomogeneous boundary condition (
1.11
) by a distributional source term by extending the function
w=w_{u}
in Theorem
(i)
to all of

by even reflection. More precisely, we have the following.

Theorem 1.5
.

For every
u\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there is a unique
distributional solution
w\in{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{G}}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
of the Poisson problem

-\,{\rm div}\,(|t|\,\nabla w_{u})=2\,u\,{\mathcal{L}}^{N}\!\!\otimes\!\delta_{0}\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N+1}$}

(1.16)

which is even in the

\lim_{|t|\to\infty}w_{u}(x,t)=0\qquad\text{for every $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$.}

This solution is given by (
1.12
) for
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
, so

with

as in Theorem

.

Here,
\,{\mathcal{L}}^{N}\!\!\otimes\!\delta_{0}
denotes the product measure of the
N

{\mathcal{L}}^{N}
and the Dirac-measure
\delta_{0}
on
\mathbb{R}
at

.

If one rewrites the weighted operator

\mathcal{A}_{0}=-t\Bigl(\Delta_{x}-\frac{1}{t}\partial_{t}(t\partial_{t}\cdot)\Bigr),

then one sees that the part

is the radial

\mathbb{R}^{2}
. It is therefore convenient to

of the distributional solution

of Poisson

(x,y)\mapsto W_{u}(x,y):=w_{y}(x,|y|)\qquad(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+2}=\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}.

As we shall see in Section

as follows. In order to simplify the notation, we set
\mathbb{R}^{N+2}_{*}:=\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\})
.

Theorem 1.6
.

For every
u\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there is a unique
distributional solution

W_{u}\in{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{G}}(\mathbb{R}^{N+2})\cap C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N+2}_{*})

of the Poisson problem

-\Delta{w_{u}}=2\pi u\,\,{\mathcal{L}}^{N}\!\!\otimes\!\delta_{(0,0)}\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N+2}$,}

(1.17)

satisfying

\lim_{|y|\to\infty}W_{u}(x,y)=0\qquad\text{for every $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$.}

Here,
\delta_{(0,0)}
is the Dirac-measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
at
y=(0,0)
.
Moreover, the following statements hold:

(i)

W_{u}
satisfies the Poisson formula

W_{u}(x,y)=\frac{c_{N}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(\tilde{x})}{(|x-\tilde{x}|^{2}+|y|^{2})^{N/2}}\,d\tilde{x}\qquad\text{for every $(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+2}_{*}$}

(1.18)

(ii)

\lim_{|y|\to 0}\frac{W_{u}(\cdot,y)}{\ln t}=-u\qquad\text{in $L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$}

(1.19)

(iii)

L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}u=2(\ln 2-\gamma)u-2\lim_{|y|\to 0}\Bigl(W_{u}(\cdot,y)+u\log|y|\Bigr)\quad\text{in the distributional sense in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$.}

(1.20)

(iv)

If, moreover,
u
is Dini continuous at a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then we have

L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}u(x)=2\,(\ln 2-\gamma)\,u(x)-2\lim_{|y|\to 0}\Bigl(W_{u}(x,y)+u(x)\ln t\Bigr)

(1.21)

as a pointwise limit in
\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem
1.6
reveils that

is intimately related with harmonic

on
\mathbb{R}^{N+2}_{*}
. As an application of Theorem

, we can establish a weak unique

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
u\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a function with the property that there is a
nonempty open subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that

u\equiv 0\quad\text{in $\Omega$}\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}u\equiv 0\quad\text{in $\Omega$ in the distributional sense.}

Then

on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

), (

We close this introduction with further comments on extension properties for nonlocal operators and their applications.

The extension property of the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
was
first known in the square root case
s=1/2
, and, in particular, for
the square root
A^{1/2}
of a quite general class of linear operators

A
defined on Banach spaces (and, more generally, on locally convex
spaces, cf
[
42
]
and there reference therein). Since the
work
[
10
]
by Caffarelli and Silvestre, the extension property of
the fractional Laplacian was generalized in various direction: for
linear second order partial differential operators (including
Schrödinger operators), which can be realized as nonnegative,
densely defined, and self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\Omega,d\eta)
,
where
\Omega
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
d\eta
a positive
measure on
\Omega
(see, e.g.,
[
51
]
), for heat operators
(cf
[
52
]
), for (linear) sectorial operators
A
defined on
Hilbert spaces (see, for instance,
[
6
]
) on Banach spaces
(see, e.g.,
[
24
,
43
]
, and for nonlinear accretive
operators
A
in Hilbert spaces
[
32
]
. Another important
breakthrough in this field has been established by Kwaśnicki and
Mucha
[
39
]
(see also
[
7
]
) showing
that the nonlocal operator
\varphi(-\Delta)
also admits an extension
property provided
\varphi:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
is a so-called
complete Bernstein function. This result has been made available for
more general diffusion operators in
[
7
]
. The
result
[

]
has been refined and generalized for linear

-accretive operators defined on Banach space
in
[

]
. Note, the power function
f(\lambda)=\lambda^{s}

for
\lambda\geq 0
and
0<s<1
is a complete Bernstein function. In
particular,
f(\lambda)=\ln(\lambda+1)
for
\lambda\geq 0
is a
complete Bernstein function, but
\ln\lambda
for
\lambda>0
is not
a Bernstein function. Thus, the extension problem obtained for the
logarithmic Laplacian

obtained in Theorem

is
quite a surprise, and extends the existing literature in this field of

In the literature, one finds numerous applications of the extension property
of the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
. For example, it was applied to derive

[

]

-

[

]

[

,
25
]

[

to derive spectral estimates for eigenvalue problems governed by the fractional Laplacian (see, e.g.
[

)
or to provide a classification of isolated

We hope that the extension property derived in this paper allows to extend some of these applications to the logarithmic Laplacian. Theorem

is a first example in this regard.

2
Formal derivations of the extension problem for the logarithmic Laplacian

In this section we derive the representation formula (
1.15
) on a formal level, starting from the Caffarelli-Silvestre extension problem for the fractional Laplacian.

Let
u\in C^{\beta}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some
\beta>0
, and let
s\in(0,\beta)
in the following. Moreover, let
w_{s}:\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
denote the
s
-harmonic Caffarelli-Silvestre extension of
u
, which solves (
1.8
) and gives rise to the representation

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=-d_{s}\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{1-2s}\partial_{t}w_{s}(x,t)\qquad\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}

(2.1)

with
d_{s}
given by (
1
), i.e.,
d_{s}=2^{2s-1}\frac{\Gamma(s)}{\Gamma(1-s)}
. By l’Hopital’s rule, we may rewrite (
1.9
) as

[(-\Delta)^{s}u](x)=-2sd_{s}\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{w_{s}(x,t)-w_{s}(x,0)}{t^{2s}}=-2sd_{s}\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{w_{s}(x,t)-u(x)}{t^{2s}}\qquad\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$.}

(2.2)

Moreover, it is easy to see from the precise form of
d_{s}
that

sd_{s}\to 1/2\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{2sd_{s}-1}{s}=\frac{2^{2s}\frac{\Gamma(1+s)}{\Gamma(1-s)}-1}{s}\to 2\ln 2+2\Gamma^{\prime}(1)=2(\ln 2-\gamma)\quad\text{as
$s\to 0^{+}$.}

(2.3)

In order to characterize
L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}u
by an extension problem, it is useful to define
v_{s}:\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
for
s\in(0,\beta)
by

v_{s}(x,t)=\frac{2}{s}\Bigl(\frac{w_{s}(x,t)-u(x)}{t^{2s}}+u(x)\Bigr).

(2.4)

With the help of (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we then compute that

\displaystyle L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}u(x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\frac{(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)-u(x)}{s}=-\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\Bigl(2d_{s}\frac{w_{s}(x,t)-u(x)}{t^{2s}}+\frac{u(x)}{s}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=-\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\Bigl(sd_{s}v_{s}(x,t)-\frac{2sd_{s}-1}{s}u(x)\Bigr)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}v_{s}(x,t)+2(\ln 2-\gamma)u(x).

(2.5)

This is the starting point of our formal derivations of the extension problem for
L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}u
, which are based on assuming the existence of

v_{0}(x,t):=\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}v_{s}(x,t)\quad\text{as a locally uniform limit in $(x,t)\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}},$}

(2.6)

Under this assumption, we may change the order of limits in (
2.5
) and arrive at

L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}u(x)=\frac{1}{2}\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}v_{0}(x,t)+2(\ln 2-\gamma)u(x)

(2.7)

So it remains to characterize the limit
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}v_{0}(x,t)
via a suitable local extension problem, and this will be done in two different ways in the following subsections. Our first derivation only uses the extension problem (
1.8
), (
1.9
) in its differential form, while the second derivation will use a Poisson kernel representation of the Caffarelli-Silvestre extension.

2.1
A first formal derivation of the
extension problem

For a first formal derivation of an extension problem for
L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}u
, we assume, for simplicity, throughout this section that

u\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

is fixed. The idea is to first derive a partial differential equation for the functions
v_{s}
defined in (
2.4
) and then for
v_{0}
by passing to the limit
s\to 0^{+}
. For this it is convenient to extend
w_{s}
and
v_{s}
as even functions in
t
on all of
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
. Then we can rewrite (
2.4
) as

w_{s}(x,t)=(1-|t|^{2s})\,u(x)+\frac{s\,|t|^{2s}}{2}\,v_{s}(x,t)\qquad\text{for $(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}$.}

(2.8)

We start with the following observation, which might be of independent interest.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
. Then

w_{s}:\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies

-\,{\rm div}\,(|t|^{1-2s}\nabla w_{s})=\frac{2}{d_{s}}[(-\Delta)^{s}u]\,{\mathcal{L}}^{N}\!\!\otimes\!\delta_{0}\quad{\rm in}\ \,\mathbb{R}^{N+1}

(2.9)

in the distributional sense. In
other words, for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
, one has that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}w_{s}\Bigl(-\,{\rm div}\,(|t|^{1-2s}\nabla\varphi)\Bigr)\,dxdt=\frac{2}{d_{s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[(-\Delta)^{s}u](x)\varphi(x,0)\,dx.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
. Then integrating by parts twice in the
equation (
1.8
) yields that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}w_{s}\,\,{\rm div}\,(|t|^{1-2s}\nabla\varphi)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\{t\,:\,|t|>\varepsilon\}}w_{s}\,\,{\rm div}\,(|t|^{1-2s}\nabla\varphi)\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\varepsilon^{1-2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\{t\,:\,|t|=\varepsilon\}}w_{s}\,\partial_{\nu}\varphi\,d\sigma(x,t),

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt-\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\{t\,:\,|t|>\varepsilon\}}|t|^{1-2s}\nabla w_{s}\nabla\varphi\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\varepsilon^{1-2s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\{t\,:\,|t|=\varepsilon\}}\bigl(w_{s}\partial_{\nu}\varphi-\varphi\partial_{\nu}w_{s}\bigr)\,d\sigma(x,t),

where
\nu(x,t)=-\frac{t}{|t|}.
For
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
we have, since

w_{s}
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{1-2s}\int_{|t|=\varepsilon}w_{s}\partial_{\nu}\varphi\,d\sigma(x,t)=0.

Moreover, it follows from
(
1.9
) and the
evenness of the function
w_{s}
in
t
that

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)

\displaystyle=d_{s}\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{1-2s}\partial_{\nu}w_{s}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{s}}{2}\Big[-\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{1-2s}\partial_{t}w_{s}(x,t)\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}(x,t)-\lim_{t\to 0^{-}}|t|^{1-2s}\partial_{t}w_{s}(x,|t|)\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{-}}(x,t)\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{s}}{2}\lim_{t\to 0}|t|^{1-2s}\partial_{\nu}w_{s}(x,|t|).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}w_{s}\,{\rm div}\,(|t|^{1-2s}\nabla\varphi)\,dxdt

\displaystyle=-\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\varepsilon^{1-2s}\int_{|t|=\varepsilon}\varphi(x,t)\partial_{\nu}w_{s}(x,t)\,dxdt

\displaystyle=-\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{|t|=\varepsilon}\varphi(x,t)|t|^{1-2s}\partial_{\nu}w_{s}(x,t)\,d\sigma(x,t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{d_{s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi(x,0)(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)\,dx

as claimed.
∎

Next, we show which equation
v_{s}
solves in the sense of distributions.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
0<s<\frac{1}{2}
. Then the function

v_{s}:\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\to\mathbb{R}
given by the asymptotic ansatz (
2.8
) satisfies

\begin{split}-\,{\rm div}\,(|t|\nabla v_{s})&=2\,s\frac{t}{|t|}\partial_{t}v_{s}+4\,(s\,v_{s}-2\,u)\,{\mathcal{L}}^{N}\!\!\otimes\!\delta_{0}\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{4}{s\,d_{s}}(-\Delta)^{s}u\,{\mathcal{L}}^{N}\!\!\otimes\!\delta_{0}+2\,|t|\,\frac{|t|^{-2s}-1}{s}\Delta_{x}u\end{split}

(2.10)

in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
in the distributional sense.

Proof.

Indeed, by (
2.9
), and
by applying the asymptotic ansatz (
2.8
), one sees that

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{s\,d_{s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)\varphi(x,0)\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{2}{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}w_{s}\,\,{\rm div}\,(|t|^{1-2s}\nabla\varphi)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}\Bigl(2\,\frac{1-|t|^{2s}}{s}\,u(x)+|t|^{2s}\,v_{s}(x,t)\Bigr)\,\,{\rm div}\,(|t|^{1-2s}\nabla\varphi)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\qquad=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|t|^{1-2s}\,\nabla\Bigl(2\,\frac{1-|t|^{2s}}{s}\,u(x)\Bigr)\nabla\varphi\,dxdt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|t|^{2s}\,v_{s}(x,t)\,\,{\rm div}\,(|t|^{1-2s}\nabla\varphi)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\qquad=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|t|^{1-2s}\,2\,\frac{1-|t|^{2s}}{s}\,\nabla_{x}u(x)\nabla_{x}\varphi\,dxdt

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|t|^{1-2s}\,2\,u(x)\,\partial_{t}\Bigl(\frac{1-|t|^{2s}}{s}\Bigr)\partial_{t}\varphi\,dxdt

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|t|^{2s}\,v_{s}(x,t)\,\,{\rm div}\,(|t|^{1-2s}\nabla\varphi)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\qquad=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|t|^{1-2s}\,2\,\frac{1-|t|^{2s}}{s}\,\nabla_{x}u(x)\nabla_{x}\varphi\,dxdt+4\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}u(x)\,\frac{t}{|t|}\,\partial_{t}\varphi\,dxdt

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}v_{s}(x,t)\,\Bigl(\,{\rm div}\,_{x}(|t|\nabla_{x}\varphi)+(1-2s)\,\frac{t}{|t|}\,\partial_{t}\varphi+|t|\partial_{tt}\varphi\Bigr)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\qquad=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|t|^{1-2s}\,2\,\frac{1-|t|^{2s}}{s}\,\nabla_{x}u(x)\nabla_{x}\varphi\,dxdt+4\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}u(x)\,\frac{t}{|t|}\,\partial_{t}\varphi\,dxdt

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}v_{s}(x,t)\Bigl(\,{\rm div}\,_{x}(|t|\nabla_{x}\varphi)+\partial_{t}(|t|\partial_{t}\varphi)-2s\frac{t}{|t|}\partial_{t}\varphi\Bigr)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\qquad=-2\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}\Bigl(|t|\frac{|t|^{-2s}-1}{s}\nabla_{x}u(x)\nabla_{x}\varphi(x,t)-2u(x)\frac{t}{|t|}\partial_{t}\varphi(x,t)\Bigr)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}v_{s}(x,t)\Bigl(\,{\rm div}\,(|t|\nabla\varphi)-2s\frac{t}{|t|}\partial_{t}\varphi\Bigr)\,dxdt

for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
. Since for

\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}u(x)\,\frac{t}{|t|}\partial_{t}\varphi(x,t)\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{t}\varphi(x,t)\,dtdx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(x)\int_{-\infty}^{0}\partial_{t}\varphi(x,t)\,dtdx

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(x)\,\varphi(x,0)\,dx,

it follows that

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{s\,d_{s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)\varphi(x,0)dx

\displaystyle=2\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|t|\frac{|t|^{-2s}-1}{s}\Delta_{x}u(x)\,\varphi(x,t)dxdt-8\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(x)\varphi(x,0)dx

\displaystyle\hskip 62.59596pt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}v_{s}\,{\rm div}\,(|t|\nabla\varphi)dxdt-2s\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}v_{s}\frac{t}{|t|}\partial_{t}\varphi dxdt

for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
. Finally, an
integration by parts w.r.t. the variable
t
, shows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}v_{s}\frac{t}{|t|}\partial_{t}\varphi\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{0}^{\infty}v_{s}\partial_{t}\varphi\,dtdx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}v_{s}\partial_{t}\varphi\,dtdx

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v_{s}(x,0)\,\varphi(x,0)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{t}v_{s}\,\varphi\,dtdx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\partial_{t}v_{s}\,\varphi\,dxdt

\displaystyle=-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v_{s}(x,0)\,\varphi(x,0)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}\frac{t}{|t|}\partial_{t}v_{s}\,\varphi\,dtdx

for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
and so,

\begin{split}&-\frac{4}{s\,d_{s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)\,\varphi(x,0)\,dx\\
&\qquad=2\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|t|\frac{|t|^{-2s}-1}{s}\Delta_{x}u(x)\,\varphi(x,t)\,dxdt-8\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(x)\varphi(x,0)\,dx\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}v_{s}\,{\rm div}\,(|t|\nabla\varphi)\,dxdt+4s\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v_{s}(x,0)\varphi(x,0)\,dx+2s\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}\partial_{t}v_{s}\frac{t}{|t|}\varphi\,dxdt\end{split}

(2.11)

for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
, showing that
v_{s}

satisfies (
2.10
).
∎

By assumption (
2.6
) and since
\lim\limits_{s\to 0^{+}}(-\Delta)^{s}u=u
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\lim\limits_{s\to 0^{+}}sd_{s}=1/2
,
we can pass to the limit in (
2.11
) as

s\to 0^{+}
. Then, we find that

\displaystyle-8\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(x)\varphi(x,0)\,dx

\displaystyle=2\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}|t|(-2\,\ln|t|)\Delta_{x}u(x)\,\varphi(x,t)\,dxdt-8\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(x)\varphi(x,0)\,dx

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}v_{0}\,\,{\rm div}\,(|t|\nabla\varphi)\,dxdt

for every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
. Thereby we have shown
the following.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
u\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
v_{s}
given by (
2.8
), and

v_{0}\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
be the locally uniform limit (
2.6
)
of
\{v_{s}\}_{s\in(0,1/2)}
. Then,
v_{0}
is a distributional solution
of the equation

-\,{\rm div}\,(|t|\nabla v_{0})=-4\,|t|\bigl(\ln|t|\bigr)\Delta_{x}u\qquad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N+1}$.}

(2.12)

Further, the following auxiliary result holds.

Lemma 2.4
.

We have

\partial_{t}(|t|\partial_{t}\ln|t|)=2\,\delta_{0}\qquad\text{in $\mathbb{R}$}

(2.13)

in the distributional sense.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
and
b>0
large enough such that

{\rm supp}(\varphi)\subseteq(-b,b)
. Then, integration by parts shows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\ln\,|t|\,\partial_{t}(|t|\partial_{t}\varphi(t))\,dt=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\varepsilon}^{b}\ln\,t\,\partial_{t}(t\partial_{t}\varphi(t))\,dt+\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int^{-\varepsilon}_{-b}\ln\,(-t)\,\partial_{t}((-t)\partial_{t}\varphi(t))\,dt

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\Big[t\,\ln\,t\,\partial_{t}\varphi(t)\Big]_{\varepsilon}^{b}-\int_{\varepsilon}^{b}\partial_{t}\varphi(t))\,dt+\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\Big[(-t)\,\ln\,(-t)\,\partial_{t}\varphi(t)\Big]^{-\varepsilon}_{-b}+\int^{-\varepsilon}_{-b}\partial_{t}\varphi(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}-(\varphi(b)-\varphi(\varepsilon))+(\varphi(-\varepsilon)-\varphi(-b))=2\,\varphi(0).

∎

and (
2.12
), we can now

) and (

).

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose the hypotheses of this section hold. Then, the function

{w}(x,t):=\tfrac{1}{4}v_{0}(x,t)-u(x)\,\ln|t|\qquad\text{for every $(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}$ with $t\neq 0$,}

is a solution of the Poisson equation

-\,{\rm div}\,(|t|\nabla{w})=2\,u\,{\mathcal{L}}^{N}\!\!\otimes\!\delta_{0}\qquad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N+1}$}

(2.15)

in the distributional sense; i.e.,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}w_{u}\bigl(-\,{\rm div}\,(|t|\nabla\varphi)\bigr)\,dxdt=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(x)\,\varphi(x,0)\,dx\qquad\text{for all $\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})$.}

Moreover, we have

L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}u=2(\ln 2-\gamma)u-2\,\lim_{|t|\to 0}\Bigl(w(\cdot,t)+u\ln|t|\Bigr)\qquad\text{on $\mathbb{R}^{N}$.}

(2.16)

Proof.

By (
2.12
) and
(

), we have

\displaystyle-\,{\rm div}\,(|t|\nabla{w})

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{4}\,{\rm div}\,(|t|\nabla v_{0})+\,{\rm div}\,(|t|\nabla(u\,\ln|t|))

\displaystyle=-|t|\bigl(\ln|t|\bigr)\Delta_{x}u+|t|\bigl(\ln|t|\bigr)\Delta_{x}u+u\,\partial_{t}(|t|\partial_{t}\ln|t|)

\displaystyle=2\,u\,{\mathcal{L}}^{N}\!\!\otimes\!\delta_{0}

in the distributional sense in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
. Moreover, (
2.16
) follows from (
2.7
) and the definition of

in (

).
∎

2.2
A second formal derivation of the
extension problem

Let again
u\in C^{\beta}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some
\beta>0
, and let
s\in(0,\beta)
in the following. Similarly as in the last section, we consider the auxiliary function
v_{s}
given by (
2.4
), and we assume (
2.6
), i.e., the existence of

v_{0}
as a locally uniform limit of the functions
v_{s}
on
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}}
as
s\to 0^{+}
.

The additional tool we shall use now is the Poisson kernel representation of the Caffarelli-Silvestre extension
w_{s}
. More precisely, as noted in
[
14
]
and
[
10
]
, we have

w_{s}(x,t)=p_{N,s}\,t^{2s}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|x-\tilde{x}|^{2}+t^{2})^{-\frac{N+2s}{2}}u(\tilde{x})\,d\tilde{x}\qquad\text{for every $(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}$,}

(2.17)

where the constant
p_{N,s}=\pi^{-N/s}s\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2}+s)}{\Gamma(1+s)}
is chosen such that

p_{N,s}\,t^{2s}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|x-\tilde{x}|^{2}+t^{2})^{-\frac{N+2s}{2}}\,d\tilde{x}=p_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|z|^{2}+1)^{-\frac{N+2s}{2}}\,dz=1\qquad\text{for all $(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}$.}

By (
2.4
) and (
2.17
), we have

\begin{split}\tfrac{1}{2}v_{s}(x,t)&=\frac{1}{s\,t^{2s}}\Bigl(w_{s}(x,t)-u(x)\Bigr)+\frac{1}{s}u(x)\\
&=\frac{p_{N,s}}{s}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(|x-\tilde{x}|^{2}+t^{2})^{-\frac{N+2s}{2}}u(\tilde{x})\,d\tilde{x}-\frac{t^{-2s}-1}{s}\,u(x)\end{split}

(2.18)

for every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
and
0<s<1
. Using the explicit value of
p_{N,s}
given above, it is easy to see
\frac{p_{N,s}}{s}\to c_{N}

as
s\to 0^{+}
, where
c_{N}
is defined in (
1.6
). Since
u
is compactly supported by assumption, the limit

\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|x-\tilde{x}|^{2}+t^{2})^{-\frac{N+2s}{2}}u(\tilde{x})\,d\tilde{x}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|x-\tilde{x}|^{2}+t^{2})^{-\frac{N}{2}}u(\tilde{x})\,d\tilde{x}

exists by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem for every fixed
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
. Consequently, sending
s\to 0^{+}

in (
2.18
) yields that

\tfrac{1}{2}v_{0}(x,t)=c_{N}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|x-\tilde{x}|^{2}+t^{2})^{-\frac{N}{2}}u(\tilde{x})\,d\tilde{x}+2u(x)\ln t\qquad\text{for every $(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}$.}

(2.19)

Remark 2.6
.

Let us briefly comment here why it appears difficult to show that
(
2.19
) arises from (
2.18
) as a uniform limit in bounded subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
as
s\to 0^{+}
,
as assumed in (
2.6
). In fact neither one of the

s
-dependent functions

(x,t)\mapsto\frac{p_{N,s}}{s}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(|x-\tilde{x}|^{2}+t^{2})^{-\frac{N+2s}{2}}u(\tilde{x})\,d\tilde{x},\qquad\quad\ (x,t)\mapsto\frac{t^{-2s}-1}{s}\,u(x)

converges uniformly in bounded subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
, so the
uniformity of the convergence must be a result of cancellation
effects. We recall here that the locally uniform convergence was needed
to exchange the order of limits in the formula
(
2.5
) for the logarithmic Laplacian.

Once we have (
2.19
), we can use (
2.7
) to show that

L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}u=2(\ln 2-\gamma)u-2\,\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\Bigl(w_{u}(\cdot,t)+u\ln t\Bigr)\qquad\text{on $\mathbb{R}^{N}$.}

(2.20)

with the function
w_{u}
given by (
1.12
), i.e.

w_{u}(x,t):=\frac{c_{N}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|x-\tilde{x}|^{2}+t^{2}\right)^{-\frac{N}{2}}u(\tilde{x})\,d\tilde{x}\qquad\text{for every $(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}$.}

Hence, in order to complete a formal derivation of the extension property of the logarithmic laplacian in the form given by Theorem
1.2
for a function
u\in C_{c}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it remains to show that

w_{u}
satisfies the equation

-\,{\rm div}\,(t\,\nabla w_{u})=0\qquad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}$}

in classical sense together with the asymptotic boundary condition

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t\partial_{t}w_{u}(\cdot,t)=-u\qquad\text{in $L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$}

This will be done by direct computation under the more general assumption

u\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
in Lemmas
3.2
and
3.5
below, which completes the second formal derivation of the extension property of
L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}
.

3
Equivalent formulations of the Poisson problem associated with the logarithmic Laplacian

In this section we derive the unique existence of solutions of the degenerate Neumann problem in Theorem
1.2
and of the Poisson problems in Theorems
1.5
and
1.6
. Moreover, we shall show that these are essentially equivalent extension problems, and their solutions are given by the Poisson type integral formulas (
1.12
) and (
1.18
).

We first point out that, if
u\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then
w_{u}(x,t)
given by
(
1.12
) is well-defined pointwisely for every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
,
since

|w_{u}(x,t)|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(y)|}{(|x-y|^{2}+t^{2})^{\frac{N}{2}}}\,dy\leq C_{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(y)|}{(|y|+1)^{N}}\,dy<\infty

with
C_{t}:=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(|y|+1)^{N}}{(|x-y|^{2}+t^{2})^{\frac{N}{2}}}<\infty
. The following is the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then, for a function
w:\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
, the following properties are equivalent:

(i)

w=w_{u}
with
w_{u}
given by (
1.12
).

(ii)

w\in{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{G}}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
, and it satisfies

-\,{\rm div}\,(t\nabla w(x,t))=0\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}$}

(3.21)

together with the asymptotic boundary conditions

-\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t\partial_{t}w(\cdot,t)=u\qquad\text{ in $L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$}

(3.22)

and

w(x,t)\to 0\qquad\text{pointwisely as $t\to\infty$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$.}

(3.23)

(iii)

After extending
w
to
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
by even reflection in
the
t
-variable, we have
w\in{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{G}}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1})
, and it satisfies

-\,{\rm div}\,(|t|\nabla\,w(x,t))=2u\,{\mathcal{L}}^{N}\!\!\otimes\!\delta_{0}\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N+1}$}

(3.24)

in distributional sense together with the asymptotic boundary condition

w(x,|t|)\to 0\qquad\text{as $|t|\to\infty$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$.}

(3.25)

(iv)

We have
W\in{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{G}}(\mathbb{R}^{N+2})
for the function
(x,y)\to W(x,y)=w(x,|y|)
, and it satisfies

-\Delta W=2\pi u\,{\mathcal{L}}^{N}\!\!\otimes\!\delta_{(0,0)}\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{N+2}$}

(3.26)

in distributional sense together with the asymptotic boundary condition

W(x,y)\to 0\qquad\text{as $|y|\to\infty$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$.}

(3.27)

The remainder of this section is devoted to the proof of this theorem. We first recall that the space
L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
defined in (
1.7
) belongs to the more general family of weighted
L^{1}
-spaces

L^{1}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\Bigl\{v\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\>:\>\|v\|_{L^{1}_{\sigma}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|v(y)|}{(1+|y|)^{\sigma}}\,dy<\infty\}

(3.28)

with parameter
\sigma\geq 0
. We then note the following estimate in the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{1}_{\sigma}}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and let
w_{u}:\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
be given by (
1.12
).
Then
w_{u}(\cdot,t)\in L^{1}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for every
t>0
,
\sigma>0
, and there exists a constant
C_{\sigma}>0
with

\|w_{u}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}_{\sigma}}\leq C_{\sigma}(1+\ln^{-}t)\qquad\text{for every $t>0$.}

(3.29)

Here, as usual, we put
\ln^{-}t=\max\{-\ln t,0\}
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma>0
. In the following, the letter
C
stands for positive constants which may depend on
\sigma
but not on
t
. By definition and Fubini’s theorem, we have that

\|w_{u}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}_{\sigma}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|w_{u}(t,x)|}{(1+|x|)^{N+\sigma}}\,dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(y)|j_{\sigma}(y,t)dy

(3.30)

with

j_{\sigma}(y,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1+|x|)^{-N-\sigma}(|x-y|^{2}+t^{2})^{-\frac{N}{2}}dx.

We claim that

j_{\sigma}(y,t)\leq(1+|y|)^{-N}(1+\ln^{-}t).

(3.31)

To see this, we first consider
|y|\leq 3
. Then we have

\displaystyle j_{\sigma}(y,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1+|z-y|)^{-N-\sigma}(|z|^{2}+t^{2})^{-\frac{N}{2}}dz\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1+|z|)^{-N-\sigma}(|z|^{2}+t^{2})^{-\frac{N}{2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B_{1}}(|z|^{2}+t^{2})^{-\frac{N}{2}}dx+C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}}|z|^{-N-\sigma}(|z|^{2}+t^{2})^{-\frac{N}{2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\min\{|\ln t|,t^{-N}\}\leq C(1+|y|)^{-N}(1+\ln^{-}t).

Moreover, for
|y|\geq 3
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{|y|}{2}}}

\displaystyle(1+|x|)^{-N-\sigma}(|x-y|^{2}+t^{2})^{-\frac{N}{2}}dx\leq\Bigl(\bigl(\frac{|y|}{2}\bigr)^{2}+t^{2}\Bigr)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1+|x|)^{-N-\sigma}dx

\displaystyle\leq C|y|^{-N}\leq C(1+|y|)^{-N}(1+\ln^{-}t),

whereas

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2|y|}\setminus B_{\frac{|y|}{2}}}(1+|x|)^{-N-\sigma}(|x-y|^{2}+t^{2})^{-\frac{N}{2}}dx\leq C(1+|y|)^{-N-\sigma}\int_{B_{2|y|}\setminus B_{\frac{|y|}{2}}}(|x-y|^{2}+t^{2})^{-\frac{N}{2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|y|)^{-N}\int_{B_{4|y|}}(|z|^{2}+t^{2})^{-\frac{N}{2}}dx\leq C(1+|y|)^{-N}\int_{B_{\frac{4|y|}{t}}}(|z|^{2}+1)^{-\frac{N}{2}}dx\leq C(1+|y|)^{-N}(1+\ln^{-}t)

and also

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{2|y|}}

\displaystyle(1+|x|)^{-N-\sigma}(|x-y|^{2}+t^{2})^{-\frac{N}{2}}dx\leq C(|y|^{2}+t^{2})^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1+|x|)^{-N-\sigma}dx

\displaystyle\leq C|y|^{-N}\leq C(1+|y|)^{-N}(1+\ln^{-}t).

Combining these estimates, we get (
3.31
). Using (
3.31
) in (
3.30
), we get

\|w_{u}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C(1+\ln^{-}t)\quad\text{for all $t>0$,}

(3.32)

as claimed.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

If
u\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then the functions
w_{u}(\cdot,t)
,
t>0
do not need to belong to
L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
in general. A counterexample is given by
the function

u(x):=\ln^{-\tau}(1+|x|)\qquad\text{for every $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$, and fixed
$\tau\in(1,2)$.}

This function
u
belongs to
L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, since

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\ln^{-\tau}(1+|x|)}{(1+|x|)^{N}}dx\leq c_{1}+c_{2}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r\ln^{\tau}r}dr<\infty

form some constants
c_{1}
,
c_{2}>0
. On the other hand, by Fubini’s theorem we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{w_{u}(x,t)}{(1+|x|)^{N}}\,dx=\frac{c_{N}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\ln^{-\tau}(1+|y|)\rho(y)\,dy,

where

\rho(y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|x-y|^{2}+t^{2}\right)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{N}}\,dx.

Note that

\displaystyle\rho(y)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{|y|}}\left(|x-y|^{2}+t^{2}\right)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{N}}\,dx\geq\left(\left(2|y|\right)^{2}+t^{2}\right)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int_{B_{|y|}}\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{N}}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq C_{t}\,|y|^{-N}\int_{1}^{|y|}\frac{r^{N-1}}{(1+r)^{N}}\,dr\geq C_{t}\,|y|^{-N}\ln|y|.

Here, the letter
C_{t}
stands for positive constants depending on
t
. Since
1-\tau>-1
, we conclude
that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{w_{u}(x,t)}{(1+|x|)^{N}}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq C_{t}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{3}}\ln^{-\tau}(1+|y|)|y|^{-N}\ln|y|\,dy

\displaystyle\geq C_{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{3}}|y|^{-N}\ln^{1-\tau}|y|\,dy=\infty.

Next, we show that the Poisson integral formula (
1.12
) yields a classical solution of (
3.21
).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
u\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and let
w_{u}:\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
be given by (
1.12
). Then

w_{u}
is a classical solution of (
3.21
) in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
.

Proof.

It is convenient to define
H_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}
by

H_{0}(X):=\frac{c_{N}}{2}|X|^{-N}\qquad\text{for every $X=(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\setminus\{0\}$,}

(3.33)

where
|X|=\sqrt{|x|^{2}+|t|^{2}}
is the Euclidean norm on

\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
. Setting
R:=|X|
in the following, we directly compute, for
t>0
,

\begin{split}\frac{1}{t}\,{\rm div}\,(t\,\nabla H_{0})&=\Delta_{X}H_{0}+\frac{1}{t}\partial_{t}H_{0}\\[5.69054pt]
&=\frac{C_{N}}{2}\Big[(R^{-N})^{\prime\prime}+\frac{N}{R}(R^{-N})^{\prime}+\frac{1}{t}\big(R^{-N-2}(-N)t\big)\Big]\\[5.69054pt]
&=\frac{C_{N}}{2}\Big[NR^{-N-2}-NR^{-N-2}\Big]=0.\end{split}

(3.34)

Moreover, if
u\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
w_{u}:\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by (
1.12
), then one easily sees that

w_{u}(x,t)=(H_{0}(\cdot,t)\ast u)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H_{0}(x-\tilde{x},t)u(\tilde{x})\,d\tilde{x}

for every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
with
t\neq 0
. Further, note that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial H_{0}}{\partial x_{i}}(x,t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq N\,\max\{1,\tfrac{1}{|t|^{2}}\}\,|H_{0}(x,t)|,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial^{2}H_{0}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}(x,t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq N\,\tfrac{1}{|t|^{2}}\Big[1+2(\frac{N}{2}+1)\Big]\,|H_{0}(x,t)|,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial H_{0}}{\partial t}(x,t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq N\,\tfrac{1}{|t|}\,|H_{0}(x,t)|,

for every
X=(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
. Thus, Lebesgue’s dominated
convergence theorem yields that we may interchange integral and
differentiation and so, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\,{\rm div}\,(t\,\nabla w_{u}(x,t))

\displaystyle=\Delta_{X}w_{u}(X)+\frac{1}{t}\partial_{t}w_{u}(X)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big(\Delta_{x}H_{0}(x-\tilde{x},t)+\partial_{t}^{2}H_{0}(x-\tilde{x},t)+\frac{1}{t}\partial_{t}H_{0}(x-\tilde{x},t)\Big)u(\tilde{x})d\tilde{x}=0

for every
X=(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
. This shows that
w_{u}
satisfies
(
3.21
).
∎

Next, we show that
w_{u}
defined by (
1.12
)
satisfies the inhomogeneous Neumann condition
(
3.22
).

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
u\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and let
w_{u}:\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
be given by (
1.12
). Then

w_{u}
satisfies the asymptotic boundary condition (
3.22
).

Proof.

For
t>0
, a direct computation
shows

-t\partial_{t}w_{u}(x,t)=\frac{Nc_{N}}{2}t^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|x-\tilde{x}|^{2}+t^{2}\right)^{-\frac{N+2}{2}}u(\tilde{x})\,d\tilde{x}=\frac{Nc_{N}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|z|^{2}+1\right)^{-\frac{N+2}{2}}u(x+tz)\,dz

Moreover, we have

\frac{Nc_{N}}{2}\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|z|^{2}+1)^{-\frac{N+2}{2}}\,dz\Bigr)=\frac{Nc_{N}}{2}\Bigl(\frac{\omega_{N}}{2}B(\frac{N}{2},1)\Bigr)=\frac{N}{2}B(\frac{N}{2},1)=\frac{N}{2}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})\Gamma(1)}{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2}+1)}=1,

where
B
refers to the Beta function. This allows us to write, for
t\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\frac{2}{Nc_{N}}

\displaystyle\Bigl(-t\partial_{t}w_{u}(x,t)-u(x)\Bigr)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|z|^{2}+1\right)^{-\frac{N+2}{2}}\Bigl(u(x+tz)-u(x)\Bigr)\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}}\dots dz+\int_{B_{\frac{1}{t}}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}}\dots dz+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{t}}}\dots dz=I_{1}(x,t)+I_{2}(x,t)+I_{3}(x,t).

Consequently, (
3.22
) follows once we have shown, for given
R>0
, that

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\|I_{j}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(B_{R})}=0\qquad\text{for $j=1,2,3$.}

(3.35)

In the following, the letter
C
denotes a positive constant, which
might change from line to line, depending on
R
,
N
and
\alpha
, but
not
on
t
. We first note that

\displaystyle\|I_{1}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(B_{R})}\leq\int_{B_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}}\left(|z|^{2}+1\right)^{-\frac{N+2}{2}}\|u(\cdot+tz)-u\|_{L^{1}(B_{R})}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{|{\tilde{z}}|\leq\sqrt{t}}\|u(\cdot+{\tilde{z}})-u\|_{L^{1}(B_{R})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|z|^{2}+1\right)^{-\frac{N+2}{2}}\,dz\leq C\sup_{|{\tilde{z}}|\leq\sqrt{t}}\|u(\cdot+{\tilde{z}})-u\|_{L^{1}(B_{R})}.

Moreover, using the density of
C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
in
L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it is easy to see that

\lim_{t\to 0}\sup_{|{\tilde{z}}|\leq\sqrt{t}}\|u(\cdot+{\tilde{z}})-u\|_{L^{1}(B_{R})}=\lim_{{\tilde{z}}\to 0}\|u(\cdot+\tilde{z})-u\|_{L^{1}(B_{R})}=0

and therefore

\|I_{1}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(B_{R})}\to 0\qquad\text{as $t\to 0^{+}$.}

Moreover, we estimate

\displaystyle\|I_{2}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\frac{1}{t}}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}}\left(|z|^{2}+1\right)^{-\frac{N+2}{2}}\|u(\cdot+tz)-u\|_{L^{1}(B_{R})}dz

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{|\tilde{z}|\leq 1}\|u(\cdot+\tilde{z})-u\|_{L^{1}(B_{R})}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{t}}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}}\left(|z|^{2}+1\right)^{-\frac{N+2}{2}}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{R+1})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}}\left(|z|^{2}+1\right)^{-\frac{N+2}{2}}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{L^{1}_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}}\left(|z|^{2}+1\right)^{-\frac{N+2}{2}}\,dz\to 0\qquad\text{as $t\to 0$.}

Finally, we estimate, for
|x|\leq R
,

\displaystyle|I_{3}(x,t)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{t}}}|z|^{-(N+2)}|u(x+tz)-u(x)|dz

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{t}}}|z|^{-(N+2)}|u(x+tz)|dz+|u(x)|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{t}}}|z|^{-(N+2)}\,dz

\displaystyle=t^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}(x)}|{\tilde{x}}-x|^{-(N+2)}|u({\tilde{x}})|d{\tilde{x}}+Ct^{2}|u(x)|

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{2}\Bigl(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1+|{\tilde{x}}|)^{-N}|u({\tilde{x}})|d{\tilde{x}}+|u(x)|\Bigr)=Ct^{2}\bigl(\|u\|_{L^{1}_{0}}+|u(x)|\bigr).

Here we used the fact that

\sup_{|x|\leq R,\>{\tilde{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}(x)}(1+|{\tilde{x}}|)^{N}|{\tilde{x}}-x|^{-(N+2)}<\infty.

We thus conclude that

\|I_{3}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{1}(B_{R})}\leq Ct^{2}\Bigl(\|u\|_{L^{1}_{0}}|B_{R}|+\|u\|_{L^{1}(B_{R})}\Bigr)\leq Ct^{2}\|u\|_{L^{1}_{0}}\to 0\quad\text{as $t\to 0^{+}$.}

(3.36)

Combining the estimates for
I_{1},I_{2}
and
I_{3}
, we have shown (
3.35
), and this finishes the proof of (
3.22
).
∎

The following lemma will be useful to prove the implication (ii)
\Longrightarrow
(iii) in Theorem
3.1
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and let
w\in{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{G}}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})
satisfy

t\partial_{t}w(\cdot,t)\to-u\qquad\text{as $t\to 0^{+}$ in $L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$}

(3.37)

Then we also have

\frac{w(\cdot,t)}{\ln t}\to-u\qquad\text{in $L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$}

(3.38)

Proof.

Let
R>0
. Then l’Hôpital’s rule gives

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ln t}\int_{B_{R}}\sqrt{1+(w(x,t)+u(x)\ln t)^{2}}\,dx=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t\frac{d}{dt}\int_{B_{R}}\sqrt{1+(w(x,t)+u(x)\ln t)^{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\int_{B_{R}}\frac{(w(x,t)+u(x)\ln t)(t\partial_{t}w(x,t)+u(x))}{\sqrt{1+(w(x,t)+u(x)\ln t)^{2}}}\,dx

while

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int_{B_{R}}\frac{(w(x,t)+u(x)\ln t)(t\partial_{t}w(x,t)+u(x))}{\sqrt{1+(w(x,t)+u(x)\ln t)^{2}}}\,dx\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{R}}|t\partial_{t}w(x,t)+u(x)|\,dx

\displaystyle=\|t\partial_{t}w(\cdot,t)+u\|_{L^{1}(B_{R})}\to 0\quad\text{as $t\to 0^{+}$}

by (
3.22
). Hence we find that

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{|\ln t|}\int_{B_{R}}\sqrt{1+(w(x,t)+u(x)\ln t)^{2}}\,dx=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\ln t}\int_{B_{R}}\sqrt{1+(w(x,t)+u(x)\ln t)^{2}}\,dx=0

and therefore also

\lim_{t\to 0}\Bigl\|\frac{w(\cdot,t)}{\ln t}+u\Bigr\|_{L^{1}(B_{R})}=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{|\ln t|}\int_{B_{R}}|w(x,t)+u(x)\ln t|\,dx=0.

Since
R>0
was chosen arbitrarily, this yields (
3.38
).
∎

We may now complete the

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

We first show that (i) implies (ii). By Lemma
3.4
, the function
w:=w_{u}
satisfies (
3.21
) in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
. Moreover, by the assumption
u\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and the dominated convergence theorem, we have

|w_{u}(x,t)|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u({\tilde{x}})|}{(|x-{\tilde{x}}|^{2}+t^{2})^{\frac{N}{2}}}d\tilde{x}\to 0\qquad\text{as $t\to\infty$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$,}

which gives (
3.23
).
Moreover, from Lemma
3.2
with
\sigma=1
, we get the estimate

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|w({\tilde{x}},t)|}{(1+|{\tilde{x}}|)^{N+1}}d{\tilde{x}}\leq C_{1}(1+\ln^{-}t)\qquad\text{for $t>0$ with a constant $C_{1}>0$.}

From this we deduce that for every
X=(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}
we have, with a constant
C_{2}>0
,

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{1}(B_{1}(X)\cap\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+})}\leq C_{2}(1+|x|)^{\sigma}\int_{\max\{t-1,0\}}^{t+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|w_{u}({\tilde{x}},\tau)|}{(1+|{\tilde{x}}|)^{\sigma}}d{\tilde{x}}d\tau

4.1

Suppose that

Lemma 4.1

We have

c_{N}\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{t}}\left(|x|^{2}+t^{2}\right)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\,dx=-2\ln t+q_{N}+o(1)\qquad\text{as $t\to 0^{+}$,}

(4.46)

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle c_{N}\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{t}}\left(|x|^{2}+t^{2}\right)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle=c_{N}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{t}}\setminus B_{1}}\left(|z|^{2}+1\right)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\,dz

\displaystyle=c_{N}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{t}}\setminus B_{1}}|z|^{-N}\,dz+c_{N}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{t}}\setminus B_{1}}\big(\left(|z|^{2}+1\right)^{-\frac{N}{2}}-|z|^{-N}\big)\,dz

\displaystyle=-2\ln t+2\biggl(\int_{1}^{+\infty}\big((r^{2}+1)^{-\frac{N}{2}}-r^{-N}\big)r^{N-1}dr

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad-\int_{\frac{1}{t}}^{+\infty}\big((r^{2}+1)^{-\frac{N}{2}}-r^{-N}\big)r^{N-1}dr\biggr),

where, for

\displaystyle 0<

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{1}{t}}^{+\infty}\big(r^{-N}-(r^{2}+1)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\big)r^{N-1}dr=\int_{\frac{1}{t}}^{+\infty}\big(1-(1+r^{-2})^{-\frac{N}{2}}\big)r^{-1}dr

Consequently,

4.2
Representation of
L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}u
in distributional sense

Now, we are in the position to prove the distributional representation of
L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}u
for
u\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

by (
1.14
), where, as before
w=w_{u}
is defined by the Poisson integral formula (
1.12
).

More precisely, we shall prove the following.

Proposition 4.4
.

For
u\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\varphi\in C_{c}^{D}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}uL_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}\varphi dx=2(\ln 2-\gamma)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u\varphi\,dx-2\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Bigl(w_{u}(x,t)+u\ln t\Bigr)\varphi(x)\,dx.

(4.55)

with
w_{u}
defined by (
1.12
). Here, as before,
C_{c}^{D}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
denotes the space of uniformly Dini
continous functions on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with compact support.

Proof.

Let
w_{\varphi}:\mathbb{R}^{N+1}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
be given by (
1.12
) with
u
replaced by
\varphi
, i.e.,

w_{\varphi}(x,t)=\frac{c_{N}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|x-\tilde{x}|^{2}+t^{2}\right)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\varphi(\tilde{x})\,d\tilde{x}.

We then have, for
t>0
, by Fubini’s theorem

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}

\displaystyle(w_{u}(x,t)\varphi(x)\,dx=\frac{c_{N}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|(x-\tilde{x}|^{2}+t^{2}\right)^{-\frac{N}{2}}u(x)\varphi(\tilde{x})\,d\tilde{x}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{N}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|(x-\tilde{x}|^{2}+t^{2}\right)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\varphi(x)u(\tilde{x})\,dxd\tilde{x}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(w_{\varphi}(x,t)u(x)\,dx.

(4.56)

Consequently, using the fact that we have already proved the pointwise representation (
1.15
) for the Dini continuous function
\varphi
in place of

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}uL_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}\varphi dx

\displaystyle=2(\ln 2-\gamma)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi u\,dx-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(x)\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}(w_{\varphi}(x,t)+2\varphi(x)\ln t)\,dx

\displaystyle=2(\ln 2-\gamma)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi u\,dx-2\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(x)(w_{\varphi}(x,t)+2\varphi(x)\ln t)\,dx

\displaystyle=2(\ln 2-\gamma)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u\varphi\,dx-\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(w_{u}(x,t)+2u(x)\ln t)\varphi(x)\,dx.

Here we used the dominated convergence theorem in the second equality and (
4.56
) in the third inequality. To apply the dominated convergence theorem, we need to prove that

|w_{\varphi}(x,t)+2\varphi(x)\ln t|\leq C(1+|x|)^{-N}\qquad\text{for all $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$, $t\in(0,1)$}

(4.57)

, and to combine this fact with the assumption

. To show (

), we let

with

. Then for

with

and

we have

and therefore

\displaystyle|w_{\varphi}(x,t)+2\varphi(x)\ln t|=|w_{\varphi}(x,t)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{N}}{2}\int_{B_{R}}|x-\tilde{x}|^{-N}|\varphi(\tilde{x})|\,d\tilde{x}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{N-1}c_{N}|x|^{-N}\leq 2^{2N-1}c_{N}(1+|x|)^{-N}.

Moreover, since
\varphi
is uniformly Dini continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, Remark
4.3
implies that

|w_{\varphi}(x,t)+2\varphi(x)\ln t|
remains uniformly bounded for
x\in B_{2R}
,
t\in(0,1)
. This shows (
4.57
) and finishes the proof of the proposition.

5
An application: The weak unique continuation property for the
logarithmic Laplacian

This section is dedicated to an application of the theory developed in
the previous sections. More precisely, thanks to the extension
problem (
1.17
) in
\mathbb{R}^{N+2}
, we are able to prove the
following weak unique continuation property for the logarithmic Laplacian.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
u\in L^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. If there is an
nonempty open subset
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that

u\equiv 0\quad\text{in $\Omega$}\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}u\equiv 0\quad\text{in $\Omega$ in distributional sense,}

(5.58)

Then
u\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Proof.

Let
W_{u}
be the two-dimensional extension on
\mathbb{R}^{N+2}
given by (
1.18
), which has the properties of Theorem
1.6
. Moreover, consider the domain

U:=\Bigl(\Omega\times\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\}\Bigr)\cup\Bigl(\mathbb{R}^{N+2}\setminus\bigl(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\{0_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\}\bigr)\Bigr)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N+2}.

We first show that
W_{u}
is harmonic in
U
. For this, we let

\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U)
. Then, by (
1.17
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{U}W_{u}(-\Delta\varphi)\,dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+2}}W_{u}(-\Delta\varphi)\,dxdy

\displaystyle=2\pi\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u(x)\varphi(x,0)\,dxdy=2\pi\int_{\Omega}u(x)\varphi(x,0)\,dxdy=0.

Hence
W_{u}
is harmonic in distributional sense in
U
and therefore
also harmonic in
U
in classical sense by Weyl’s lemma. Moreover,
for
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)\subset C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
we have, by hyopothesis (
5.58
) and
(
1.20
), that

\displaystyle 0=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}uL_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}\varphi\,dx

\displaystyle=2(\ln 2-\gamma)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u\varphi\,dx-\lim_{|y|\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}2(W_{u}(x,y)+u(x)\ln|y|)\varphi(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=2(\ln 2-\gamma)\int_{\Omega}u\varphi\,dx-\lim_{|y|\to 0}\int_{\Omega}2(W_{u}(x,y)+u(x)\ln|y|)\varphi(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=-\lim_{|y|\to 0}\int_{\Omega}W_{u}(x,y)\varphi(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}W_{u}(x,0)\varphi(x)\,dx.

Consequently,
W_{u}(\cdot,0)\equiv 0
in
\Omega
. Hence for
x_{0}\in\Omega
we have

0=\Delta W_{u}(x_{0},0)=\Delta_{x}W_{u}(x_{0},0)+\Delta_{y}W_{u}(x_{0},0)=\Delta_{y}W_{u}(x_{0},0)

and

0=\Delta^{2}W_{u}(x_{0},0)=\Delta_{x}^{2}W_{u}(x_{0},0)+2\Delta_{x}\Delta_{y}W_{u}(x_{0},0)+\Delta_{y}^{2}W_{u}(x_{0},0)=\Delta_{y}^{2}W_{u}(x_{0},0).

Inductively, we get

\Delta_{y}^{k}W_{u}(x_{0},0)=0\qquad\text{for every $x_{0}\in\Omega$, $k\in\mathbb{N}$.}

Since
W_{u}(x_{0},\cdot)
is a radial function in

, this implies that
all partial derivatives with respect to the
y
-variable vanish at

z_{0}
, whereas also all partial derivatives with respect to the
x
-variable
vanish since
W_{u}(\cdot,0)\equiv 0
in
\Omega
. Hence all partial
derivatives of
W_{u}
vanish at points
(x_{0},0)
with
x_{0}\in\Omega
.
Since
W_{u}
is analytic on the connected open set
U
, it follows that

W_{u}\equiv 0
on
U
. But then we have

u=-\lim_{y\to 0}\frac{W_{u}(\cdot,y)}{\ln|y|}=0\qquad\text{in $L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$}

by (
1.19
), as claimed.
∎

Abstract
. We prove the strong Tits alternative for the tame automorphism group of 𝕜 3 \mathbbm{k}^{3} , for 𝕜 \mathbbm{k} of characteristic zero.



1.
Introduction

A group
G
satisfies the
strong Tits alternative
(resp.
weak Tits alternative
) if each subgroup of
G
(resp. each finitely generated subgroup of
G
) is virtually solvable or contains a non-abelian free group.
For each
n\geq 1
and each field
\mathbbm{k}
of characteristic zero (resp. of positive characteristic), Tits proved that the linear group
\textbf{GL}_{n}(\mathbbm{k})
satisfies the strong (resp. weak) Tits alternative
[
39
]
.
This result was then extended to many classes of groups.
We mention first some groups of polynomial or rational maps, also known as Cremona transformations.
The group
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
of polynomial automorphisms of the complex plane satisfies the strong Tits alternative by
[
25
]
.
For an arbitrary field
\mathbbm{k}
, one can adapt the argument and obtain the (strong, or weak, according to the characteristic of
\mathbbm{k}
) Tits alternative for
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbbm{k}^{2})

[
27
]
.
For birational maps, the weak and then strong Tits alternative for the 2-dimensional Cremona group
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
was established in
[
10
]
and
[
40
]
.
Again, the argument can be extended to any field of characteristic zero
[
27
]
, while the case of positive characteristic remains unclear.
In higher dimension, very little is known.
However, the strong Tits alternative for
\mathrm{Tame}(\textbf{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C}))
, a particular subgroup of the Cremona group
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{C}^{3})
, was established in
[
8
]
.

The aim of this article is to establish the Tits alternative for the following subgroup of the 3-dimensional Cremona group.
The
tame automorphism group

\mathrm{Tame}(\mathbbm{k}^{3})
of the affine space
\mathbbm{k}^{3}
is the subgroup of the polynomial automorphism group generated by the affine
automorphisms and by the automorphisms of the form

(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\mapsto(x_{1}+P(x_{2},x_{3}),x_{2},x_{3}),\ P\in\mathbbm{k}[x_{2},x_{3}].

Main Theorem
.

Let
\mathbbm{k}
be a field of characteristic zero.
Then
\mathrm{Tame}(\mathbbm{k}^{3})
satisfies the strong Tits alternative.

A common feature of the proof of the Tits alternative for
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
,
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
and
\mathrm{Tame}(\textbf{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C}))
is the use of an action on a CAT(0)
metric space. Namely,
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
acts on the Bass–Serre tree associated to its structure of amalgamated product,
\mathrm{Bir}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
acts on an infinite
dimensional hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{\infty}
and
\mathrm{Tame}(\textbf{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C}))
acts on a CAT(0) square complex. None of these actions is proper.

In general, suppose that a group
G
acts by isometries on a Gromov-hyperbolic geodesic metric space
X
. By
[
16
]
*§3.1, the group
G
(or its
index
2
subgroup) fixes a point of
X
or the Gromov boundary of
X
, or contains a non-abelian free group. In particular, if
G
is
word-hyperbolic, if we take
X
to be the Cayley graph of
G
, the stabilisers of points in the Gromov boundary of
X
are virtually cyclic and
so
G
satisfies the strong Tits alternative. More generally, if
G
acts acylindrically on a Gromov-hyperbolic geodesic metric space
X
, and the
subgroups of
G
with bounded orbits in
X
satisfy the weak (resp. strong) Tits alternative, then
G
satisfies the weak (resp. strong) Tits
alternative
[
14
]
. Taking for
X
the coned-off Deligne complex, this gives the strong Tits alternative for
2
-dimensional Artin groups of
hyperbolic type
[
31
]
.

For groups
G
that are not word-hyperbolic, but possess some hyperbolic-like features, various strategies were employed. Strong Tits alternative was
proved for mapping class groups of hyperbolic surfaces
[
18
,
32
]
and outer automorphism groups of free groups
[
5
,
6
]
. This was
generalised to outer automorphism groups of right-angled Artin groups
[
13
,
17
]
.

It is conjectured that if
X
is a CAT(0) metric space, and
G
acts on
X
properly and cocompactly, then it satisfies the strong Tits alternative.
This conjecture was verified for
2
-dimensional complexes
X
in
[
34
]
. The methods can be extended to ‘recurrent’ spaces
[
35
]
, implying
that the Artin groups of large type satisfy the strong Tits alternative. For CAT(0) cube complexes
X
the conjecture was established in
[
37
]
.
More generally, if
G
acts on a finite-dimensional CAT(0) cube complex
X
with the stabilisers of points in
X\cup\partial_{\infty}X
satisfying the
weak (resp. strong) Tits alternative, then
G
also satisfies the weak (resp. strong) Tits alternative
[
12
]
. Applying this, one verifies the
strong Tits alternative for Artin groups
G
of type FC
[
30
]
, by computing the stabilisers of points in
\partial_{\infty}X
, where
X
is the (cubical) Deligne
complex.

The group
\mathrm{Tame}(\mathbbm{k}^{3})
is the amalgamated product of three subgroups along their pairwise intersection
[
43
,
26
]
. This gives a natural
action of
\mathrm{Tame}(\mathbbm{k}^{3})
on a
2
-dimensional simplicial complex, which was proved in
[
23
]
to be contractible and Gromov-hyperbolic in order to find normal subgroups in
\mathrm{Tame}(\mathbbm{k}^{3})
. However, this complex cannot be
endowed with an equivariant CAT(0) metric, and so we do not know how to classify the subgroups of
\mathrm{Tame}(\mathbbm{k}^{3})
with bounded orbits in that complex.

In
[
24
]
, a new
2
-dimensional CAT(0) complex
\mathbf{X}
with an action of
\mathrm{Tame}(\mathbbm{k}^{3})
was constructed, leading to the description of the finite subgroups of
\mathrm{Tame}(\mathbbm{k}^{3})
.
However, this action is not proper and
\mathbf{X}
is not Gromov-hyperbolic. Therefore, in order to prove the Main Theorem,
we need to classify and
analyse all elements of
\mathrm{Tame}(\mathbbm{k}^{3})
that do not fix a point of
\mathbf{X}
and are not loxodromic of ‘rank 1’. In order to do this, we classify locally and
globally nearly flat subcomplexes of
\mathbf{X}
.

We apply an original mixture of metric and combinatorial techniques, where we introduce ‘relative disc diagrams’, which are, roughly speaking, disc diagrams that are required to be combinatorial only away from the boundary. We often study the limit of relative disc diagrams
\phi_{n}\colon D\to\mathbf{X}
, whose domain
D
might degenerate in the limit into a ‘frilling’
D^{\prime}
. We believe that these concepts will be useful for studying the actions of other groups on non-proper CAT(0) complexes.

Organisation.

We present now a simplified outline of the proof of the Main Theorem; see Section
12
for the full proof.

The group
\mathrm{Tame}(\mathbbm{k}^{3})
acts on the non-proper 2-dimensional
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
complex
\mathbf{X}
, which we discuss in Section
3
.
An important property of
\mathbf{X}
is the existence of a proper quotient
\rho_{+}\colon\mathbf{X}\to\nabla^{+}
, with
\nabla^{+}
isometric to a Weyl chamber in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Let
G<\mathrm{Tame}(\mathbbm{k}^{3})
and suppose that
G
does not stabilise a point in
\mathbf{X}
, or a ‘principal’ or an ‘antiprincipal’ point
\zeta
in
\partial_{\infty}\mathbf{X}
. (Stabilisers of such points
\zeta
are conjugate to subgroups denoted by
C,B^{\prime}<\mathrm{Tame}(\mathbbm{k}^{3})
, which satisfy the strong Tits alternative.) In particular, by
[
33
]
*Cor 2.5, there is an element of
G
that is not elliptic, i.e. has
no fixed point in
\mathbf{X}
. If all the elements of
G
are elliptic or loxodromic of ‘rank 1’, one can find a non-abelian free group in
G
appealing to
the work of Bestvina and Fujiwara. However, one needs to carry over the classical results of Ballmann and Ruane to the non-proper setup, which we do
in Section
2
.

It remains to classify the elements of
\mathrm{Tame}(\mathbbm{k}^{3})
that are parabolic or loxodromic not of rank 1. We do so eventually in Sections
8

and
9
, where we show that all such elements are conjugate into
C
or
B^{\prime}
. To do that, we first classify all cycles in the vertex links of
\mathbf{X}
of length
<2\pi+\varepsilon_{0}
, for a uniform
\varepsilon_{0}

(Sections
4
and
5
). We then study nearly flat diagrams
\phi\colon D\to\mathbf{X}
, and we describe their ‘folding locus’ in
D

where the composition
\rho_{+}\circ\phi\colon D\to\nabla^{+}
is not a local isometry (Section
7
). Note that the classification of

g
parabolic, which are all conjugate into
C
, is much easier and can be read without preparation.

In Section
10
, we study diagrams for pairs
(\xi,\eta)
of limit points of distinct isometries
g,g^{\prime}\in G
that are parabolic or
loxodromic not of rank 1. Since
\mathbf{X}
is not proper, we have to study the limits of disc diagrams
\phi_{n}
by postcomposing them with
\rho_{+}
. This
requires introducing a procedure of passing to the limit with non-combinatorial ‘relative disc diagrams’ (see Section
6
, portions of which could be
ignored at a first reading by pretending that all the diagrams are combinatorial). Finally, in Section
11
we prove that since
G

does not stabilise a principal or an antiprincipal point in
\partial_{\infty}\mathbf{X}
, there exist
g,g^{\prime}\in G
with
\xi
and
\eta
‘far’ enough so that
some
g^{\prime n}g^{n}
has rank 1, giving rise to the non-abelian free group.

2.
Constructing rank 1 isometries

In this section, we consider an arbitrary complete
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
space
(X,d_{X})
. We allow
X
to be not proper. We generalise
the methods of Ballmann and Ruane of constructing rank 1 isometries to this setting.

2.1.
Boundary

Two geodesic rays
r,r^{\prime}\colon[0,\infty)\to X
are
asymptotic
if there is
d>0
with
d_{X}\big(r(t),r^{\prime}(t)\big)\leq d
for all
t
. This defines an
equivalence relation, and we denote by
[r]
the equivalence class of a geodesic ray
r
. The
visual boundary

\partial_{\infty}X
is the set
of equivalence classes of geodesic rays. Note that for each geodesic ray
r
and each
y\in X
there is a geodesic ray representing
[r]
starting at
y

[
9
]
*II.8.2, which we call the
geodesic ray from
y
to
[r]
. We extend the topology on
X
to the
cone topology
on

X\cup\partial_{\infty}X
described in
[
9
]
*II.8.6. Namely, given a geodesic ray
r
, and
t,\epsilon>0
, a
basic neighbourhood of

[r]
, based at
r(0)
,
is the set of
x\in X\cup\partial_{\infty}X
such that the geodesic (possibly a ray)
r^{\prime}
from
r(0)
to
x
has length
>t

and satisfies
d_{X}\big(r^{\prime}(t),r(t)\big)<\epsilon
. Note that since
X
is not assumed to be proper,
\partial_{\infty}X
might not be compact.

For
\xi,\eta\in\partial_{\infty}X
, the
angle

\angle(\xi,\eta)
is the supremum
of the Alexandrov angle
\angle_{x}(r,r^{\prime})
(see

[
9
]
*I.1.12) over
x\in X
and
r
representing
\xi
and
r^{\prime}
representing
\eta
starting at
x
. The angle equips
\partial_{\infty}X
with
a complete
\mathrm{CAT}(1)
metric
[
9
]
*II.9.13. However, the induced topology on
\partial_{\infty}X
is stronger than the cone topology.

2.2.
Isometries

Let
g
be an isometry of
X
. The
translation length
of
g
is
|g|=\inf_{x\in X}d_{X}\big(x,g(x)\big)
. If the infimum is attained, we say
that
g
is
elliptic
if
|g|=0
and
loxodromic
otherwise. If the infimum is not attained, we say that
g
is
parabolic
. If
g

is loxodromic, then by
[
9
]
*II.6.8.(1) it has (at least one)
axis
, which is a
g
-invariant geodesic line in
X
.

Let
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}X
. We say that
\xi
is the
positive
(resp.
negative
)
limit point of
g
, if for some (hence any) point
x\in X
, we have
g^{n}(x)\to\xi
for
n\to\infty
(resp.
n\to-\infty
).
Note that a limit point is fixed by
g
.
If
g
is loxodromic, it has the positive limit point
\xi^{+}
and the negative limit point
\xi^{-}
that are the endpoints of any of the axes for
g
. In
particular
\angle(\xi^{+},\xi^{-})=\pi
. If
g
is parabolic with positive translation length, then by
[
15
]
*lem 23.2
g
also has the positive and
negative limit points
\xi^{+},\xi^{-}
with
\angle(\xi^{+},\xi^{-})=\pi
. A parabolic isometry of translation length zero without a point
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}X
that is its positive and negative limit point is called
vile
. (We do not know if vile isometries exist.)

2.3.
Rank 1

Let
\xi,\eta\in\partial_{\infty}X
. In what follows we generalise the condition
d_{T}(\xi,\eta)>\pi
used by Ruane in the setting of proper
X

(where
d_{T}
is the
Tits metric
, i.e. the path metric induced by the angle metric). In
[
36
]
*Prop 2.4 she proved that for proper
X
this
condition is equivalent to
\xi,\eta
being the endpoints of a geodesic line that does not bound an isometrically embedded Euclidean half-plane.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
p\in X
and let
\varepsilon,R>0
. A geodesic quadrilateral
xyy^{\prime}x^{\prime}
in
X
is a
(p,\varepsilon,R)
-quadrilateral
if
p\in xy
, the concatenation

yy^{\prime}\cdot y^{\prime}x^{\prime}\cdot x^{\prime}x
is at distance
>R
from
p
, and the sum of the four Alexandrov angles of
xyy^{\prime}x^{\prime}
is
>2\pi-\varepsilon
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\square=xyy^{\prime}x^{\prime}
be a
(p,\varepsilon,R)
-quadrilateral in
X
. Then there is an embedded
(p,\varepsilon,R)
-quadrilateral
\overline{\square}=\bar{x}\bar{y}\bar{y}^{\prime}\bar{x}^{\prime}
in
X
with
\bar{x}\bar{y}\subset xy
and all the other sides of
\overline{\square}
contained in some sides of
\square
.

Proof.

If for some vertex
v
of
\square
the consecutive sides containing
v
intersect along a geodesic
v\bar{v}
, then we replace
v
by
\bar{v}
.
Note that all the Alexandrov angles remain the same except that the angle
0
at
v
is replaced by a nonnegative angle at
\bar{v}
.

If opposite sides of
\square
intersect at a point
z
of
X
, then
\square
decomposes as a concatenation of two geodesic triangles with Alexandrov angle sum

>2\pi-\varepsilon
. Let
\triangle
be the triangle containing
p
. By
[
9
]
*II.1.7(4), the Alexandrov angle sum in the other triangle is
\leq\pi
, so
the sum of the Alexandrov angles of
\triangle
is
>\pi-\varepsilon
.

As in the first paragraph, we can assume that
\triangle
is embedded. We introduce an extra vertex (of Alexandrov angle
\pi
) on a side of
\triangle
not
containing
p
to treat
\triangle
as a
(p,\varepsilon,R)
-quadrilateral.
∎

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\xi,\eta\in\partial_{\infty}X
. We say that
\xi,\eta
are
(\varepsilon,R)
-far
(or, shortly,
far
), if there are
neighbourhoods
U,V
of
\xi,\eta
in
X\cup\partial_{\infty}X
and a closed metric ball
P\subset X
such that for each
u\in U,v\in V,
with at
least one of
u,v
in
X
,

•

the geodesic (possibly a ray)
uv
in
X
intersects
P
, and, furthermore,

•

there is
p\in uv\cap P
that does not lie in any segment of
uv
that is a side of a
(p,\varepsilon,R)
-quadrilateral.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
x\in X
, let
\xi,\eta\in\partial_{\infty}X
, and let
u,v\in X

x
lie on the geodesic rays from

to

\xi,\eta
, respectively. Then for any point

on the geodesic
uv
, distinct from

\angle_{u}(x,v)+\angle_{v}(x,u)\geq\pi-\angle_{p}(\xi,\eta)

Proof.

We have
\angle_{u}(x,v)\geq\pi-\angle_{u}(\xi,v)
, which is
\geq\angle_{p}(\xi,u)
by
[
9
]
*II.9.3(1). See Figure

we have
\angle_{v}(x,u)\geq\angle_{p}(\eta,v)

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose that
\xi,\eta
are far and
P
is as in Definition
2.3
. Then
\angle(\xi,\eta)=\pi
and for each
\delta>0

there is
p\in P
with
\angle_{p}(\xi,\eta)>\pi-\delta
.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
and let
r,r^{\prime}\colon[0,\infty)\to X
be the geodesic rays from
x
to
\xi,\eta
. Then for
t
sufficiently large, we have
r(t)\in U,r^{\prime}(t)\in V
. If
\angle(\xi,\eta)<\pi
, then by
[
9
]
*II.9.8(4) the geodesic
r(t)r^{\prime}(t)
is arbitrarily far from

t
sufficiently
large. Consequently,
r(t)r^{\prime}(t)
is disjoint from
P
, which is a contradiction.

Thus
\angle(\xi,\eta)=\pi
, and
so we can assume
\angle_{x}(\xi,\eta)>\pi-\delta
. Then
\angle_{r(t)}\big(x,r^{\prime}(t)\big)+\angle_{r^{\prime}(t)}\big(r(t),x\big)<\delta

.
∎

Following
[
7
]
*Def 5.1, an isometry

of

has

if for some (hence any)

, there is

such that, for each

,

•

the geodesic
\beta_{n}=g^{n}(x)g^{-n}(x)
is at Hausdorff distance
\leq M

, and

•

\beta_{n}

is

\beta_{n}
of any closed metric ball

\beta_{n}
has length
\leq M
.

It is not hard to prove (but we will not need it) that if

is proper, then
\xi,\eta
are
(\varepsilon,R)
-far (for some
\varepsilon,R
) if and only if

are the endpoints of a geodesic line that does not bound an isometrically embedded Euclidean half-plane. Thus the following theorem
generalises
[

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
g
be an isometry of
X
with

points. Then
g
has rank 1.

Note that if

has finite telescopic dimension, then such

*Thm 1.1 (see also

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
g

\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}

3.3.
Combinatorial structure on
\mathbf{X}

A map from a CW-complex
X
to a CW-complex
Y
is
combinatorial
, if its restriction to any open cell of
X
is a homeomorphism onto an open
cell of
Y
. A CW-complex
X
is
combinatorial
, if the attaching map of each open cell of
X
is combinatorial for some subdivision of the
sphere. The
star
of a cell
\sigma
in a CW-complex
X
is the union of all the open cells of
X
containing
\sigma
in their closure.

The space
\mathbf{X}
is equipped with the following structure of a combinatorial CW-complex (which induces a weaker topology than the metric
d_{\mathbf{X}}
due to
the lack of local finiteness). Namely, we first equip
\nabla^{+}
with the following structure of a combinatorial CW-complex. Its vertices are the
intersection points of distinct admissible lines. Its open edges are the connected components of the complement of the vertices in the admissible lines.
Finally, its open
2
-cells are the connected components of the complement of the admissible lines in
\nabla^{+}
. In (the proof of)

[
24
]
*prop 6.3, we identify
\mathbf{X}
with the universal development of a certain complex of groups with underlying complex
\nabla^{+}
equipped
with that CW-structure. This equips
\mathbf{X}
with a CW-complex structure. Equivalently, each open cell of
\mathbf{X}
is contained in a chamber
\mathbf{E}_{f}^{+}
and it
is the preimage in
\mathbf{E}_{f}^{+}
under
\rho_{+}
of a cell of
\nabla^{+}
.

4.
Triples of polynomials in two variables

In this section we collect several estimates on particular exponents appearing in identities involving triples of polynomials in two variables. This will enable us to study the links of the vertices of
\mathbf{X}
in Section
5
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
Q\in\mathbbm{k}[y,z]
be a nonzero homogeneous polynomial of degree
d\geq 0
, let
c,c^{\prime}\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}
, and let
q\geq 1
be an integer.
Then the partial derivatives
\partial_{y}[(cy+c^{\prime}z)^{q}Q]
and
\partial_{z}[(cy+c^{\prime}z)^{q}Q]

are both nonzero homogeneous polynomials of degree
d+q-1
that are multiples of
(cy+c^{\prime}z)^{q-1}
.

Proof.

Given a homogeneous polynomial
P\in\mathbbm{k}[y,z]
, if
\deg_{y}P\geq 1
, then we have
\deg\partial_{y}P=\deg(P)-1
.
This gives the assertion about the degree. The assertion that
(cy+c^{\prime}z)^{q-1}
divides the partial derivatives is a straightforward computation. For instance, for the first one:

\partial_{y}[(cy+c^{\prime}z)^{q}Q]=(cy+c^{\prime}z)^{q-1}[cqQ+(cy+c^{\prime}z)\partial_{y}Q].\qed

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
R,T,U\in\mathbbm{k}[x_{2},x_{3}]
be nonzero homogeneous polynomials, and let
\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}\in\mathbbm{k}[x_{2},x_{3}]
be pairwise independent linear forms such that we have a relation

\ell_{1}^{r}R+\ell_{2}^{t}T+\ell_{3}^{u}U=0,

for some
r,t,u\in\mathbb{N}
.
In particular, the three terms of the sum have the same degree
d=r+\deg R=t+\deg T=u+\deg U
.
Then
\min\{r,t,u\}\leq\left\lfloor\frac{2d+1}{3}\right\rfloor
.

Proof.

By a linear change of coordinates we can assume
\ell_{1}=x_{2}
,
\ell_{2}=cx_{2}+c^{\prime}x_{3}
and
\ell_{3}=x_{3}
, for some
c,c^{\prime}\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}
, so that the relation becomes

x_{2}^{r}R+(cx_{2}+c^{\prime}x_{3})^{t}T+x_{3}^{u}U=0.

Observe that for an integer
a
, the condition
a\leq\left\lfloor\frac{2d+1}{3}\right\rfloor
is equivalent to
a<\frac{2}{3}(d+1)
.
For contradiction, assume
r,t,u\geq\frac{2}{3}(d+1)
.
Let
n
be the unique integer such that
\tfrac{1}{3}(d-2)<n\leq\tfrac{1}{3}(d+1)
. Then
2n\leq\frac{2}{3}(d+1)\leq t
.
We have

\deg R=d-r\leq d-\tfrac{2}{3}(d+1)=\tfrac{1}{3}(d-2)<n,

so
\partial_{x_{3}}^{n}[x_{2}^{r}R]=x_{2}^{r}\partial_{x_{3}}^{n}R=0
.
Similarly,
\deg U<n
and
\partial_{x_{2}}^{n}[x_{3}^{u}U]=0
.
Taking the partial derivatives
\partial_{x_{2}}^{n}\partial_{x_{3}}^{n}
of the expression
E=x_{2}^{r}R+(cx_{2}+c^{\prime}x_{3})^{t}T+x_{3}^{u}U
,
we obtain

\partial_{x_{2}}^{n}\partial_{x_{3}}^{n}E=\partial_{x_{2}}^{n}\partial_{x_{3}}^{n}[(cx_{2}+c^{\prime}x_{3})^{t}T]
.
Since
2n\leq t
, we can apply
2n
times Lemma
4.1
to obtain

\partial_{x_{2}}^{n}\partial_{x_{3}}^{n}[(cx_{2}+c^{\prime}x_{3})^{t}T]\neq 0
, hence
E\neq 0
, contradiction.
∎

We will need the following
weighted versions of Lemma
4.2
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
p\geq 1
be an integer, and let
R,T,U\in\mathbbm{k}[x_{2},x_{3}]
be nonzero weighted homogeneous polynomials with
x_{2},x_{3}
of respective weights
p,1
.
Assume that there exist

such that

\deg R+rp=\deg T+tp=\deg U+up=m.

Suppose that for some distinct

Let

be the Euclidean division of

by

, and assume

.
Then

Remark 4.4
.

In the case
\alpha=r=t=u=1
, up to scaling the only possibility for

satisfying the identity in Lemma
4.3
is

\displaystyle R=(c-c^{\prime})x_{3}^{\beta},

\displaystyle T=c^{\prime}x_{3}^{\beta},

\displaystyle U=-cx_{3}^{\beta}.

Proof of Lemma
4.3
.

There exist (ordinary) homogeneous polynomials
\widetilde{R},\widetilde{T},\widetilde{U}
satisfying

\displaystyle R(x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle=x_{3}^{\beta}\widetilde{R}(x_{2},x_{3}^{p}),

\displaystyle T(x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle=x_{3}^{\beta}\widetilde{T}(x_{2},x_{3}^{p}),

\displaystyle U(x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle=x_{3}^{\beta}\widetilde{U}(x_{2},x_{3}^{p}).

By assumption, we have

x_{2}^{r}x_{3}^{\beta}\widetilde{R}(x_{2},x_{3}^{p})+(x_{2}+cx_{3}^{p})^{t}x_{3}^{\beta}\widetilde{T}(x_{2},x_{3}^{p})+(x_{2}+c^{\prime}x_{3}^{p})^{u}x_{3}^{\beta}\widetilde{U}(x_{2},x_{3}^{p})=0.

Dividing by
x_{3}^{\beta}
and changing variables we get

x_{2}^{r}\widetilde{R}(x_{2},x_{3})+(x_{2}+cx_{3})^{t}\widetilde{T}(x_{2},x_{3})+(x_{2}+c^{\prime}x_{3})^{u}\widetilde{U}(x_{2},x_{3})=0,

where each term of the sum is homogeneous of degree
\frac{m-\beta}{p}=\alpha
.
By Lemma
4.2
, we have
\min\{r,t,u\}\leq\left\lfloor\frac{2\alpha+1}{3}\right\rfloor
.
Observe that

(
\spadesuit
)

\max_{\alpha\geq 2}\frac{\left\lfloor\frac{2\alpha+1}{3}\right\rfloor}{\alpha}=\frac{3}{4},\text{ realised for }\alpha=4.

We conclude that

\min\{rp,tp,up\}\leq\left\lfloor\frac{2\alpha+1}{3}\right\rfloor p\leq\frac{\left\lfloor\frac{2\alpha+1}{3}\right\rfloor}{\alpha}\alpha p\leq\frac{3}{4}m.\qed

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
p\geq 1
be an integer, and let
R,T,U\in\mathbbm{k}[x_{2},x_{3}]
be nonzero weighted homogeneous polynomials with
x_{2},x_{3}
of respective weights
p,1
.
Assume that there exist
r,t,u,m\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\deg R+rp=\deg T+tp=\deg U+u=m.

Suppose that for some

we have

x_{2}^{r}R+(x_{2}+cx_{3}^{p})^{t}T+x_{3}^{u}U=0.

Let
m=\alpha p+\beta
be the Euclidean division of

by

, and

. Then

.

Remark 4.6
.

In the case
\alpha=r=t=1
, and

, up to scaling the only possibility for

satisfying the identity in Lemma

\displaystyle R=-x_{3}^{\beta},

\displaystyle T=x_{3}^{\beta},

\displaystyle U=-c.

We can assume that

does not divide

. Write the Euclidean division

.
Then there exist (ordinary) homogeneous polynomials

satisfying

\displaystyle R(x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle=x_{3}^{\beta}\widetilde{R}(x_{2},x_{3}^{p}),

\displaystyle T(x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle=x_{3}^{\beta}\widetilde{T}(x_{2},x_{3}^{p}),

\displaystyle U(x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle=x_{3}^{\beta^{\prime}}\widetilde{U}(x_{2},x_{3}^{p}).

By assumption, we have

x_{2}^{r}x_{3}^{\beta}\widetilde{R}(x_{2},x_{3}^{p})+(x_{2}+cx_{3}^{p})^{t}x_{3}^{\beta}\widetilde{T}(x_{2},x_{3}^{p})+x_{3}^{u}x_{3}^{\beta^{\prime}}\widetilde{U}(x_{2},x_{3}^{p})=0.

We see that

(The maximum is realised for

∎

Suppose that

6.2.
Flat lemma

A
flat in
X
is an isometrically embedded Euclidean plane in
X
.

Lemma 6.7
.

Suppose that
X
as in Definition
6.5
is
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
. Then for each circle
\omega
of length
2\pi
isometrically embedded in
\partial_{\infty}X
with the angle metric, there is a flat

with

\partial_{\infty}F=\omega
.

Note that for
X
proper, Lemma
6.7

is
[
28
]
*Prop 2.1.

Proof.

Choose a basepoint
x_{0}\in X
and seven geodesic rays
r_{0},r_{1},\ldots,r_{6}\colon[0,\infty)\to X
starting at
x_{0}
representing consecutive points
on
\omega
at distance
\frac{2\pi}{7}
in
\partial_{\infty}X
. Furthermore, for each integer
n\geq 0
, and
i=0,\ldots,6
, let
\gamma^{n}_{i}
be the geodesic in
X
between

r_{i}(n)
and
r_{i+1}(n)
, where
r_{7}=r_{0}
.

Consider a closed path
\gamma
of the form
r_{i+1}[n+1,n]\cdot(\gamma_{i}^{n})^{-1}\cdot r_{i}[n,n+1]\cdot\gamma_{i}^{n+1}
, for some
i=0,\ldots,6
,
and
n\geq 0
. Let

be the closed ball of radius
1+|\gamma^{i}_{n}|/2

centred at the midpoint of
\gamma^{i}_{n}
. Since
X
is
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
, we have that

is convex. Since
B_{i}^{n}

contains
r_{i+1}[n+1,n]\cdot(\gamma_{i}^{n})^{-1}\cdot r_{i}[n,n+1]
, it contains the entire
\gamma
. By
[
34
]
*Rem 2.4, we have that
\gamma
is
contained in the union of finitely many cells of

. Thus there is a union

of finitely many cells of
X
intersecting

, such that

is contained in

and contractible in

.

By
[
9
]
*II.9.8(4), for each

\frac{|\gamma^{n}_{i}|}{n}\to 2\sin\frac{\pi}{7}<1
. Consequently, the distance from

to

becomes arbitrary large as

. In particular, each vertex of

belongs to only finitely many

and so the union

of all

is proper. Since

is

is locally
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
by
[
33
]
*§2 and
[
3
]
*Thm 7.1.

Let
\widetilde{K}\to K
be the universal cover of

, which is a complete proper
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
space. Let
\widetilde{x}_{0}\in\widetilde{K}
be
a lift of
x_{0}
, and for

let
\widetilde{r}_{i}
be the lift of
r_{i}
to
\widetilde{K}
starting at
\widetilde{x}_{0}
. Since we
included
K_{i}^{n}
in

, the geodesics

lift as well to geodesics

connecting
\widetilde{r}_{i}(n)
and
\widetilde{r}_{i+1}(n)
. Thus by
[
9
]
*II.9.8(4) we obtain that consecutive
\widetilde{r}_{i}
represent points
[\widetilde{r}_{i}]\in\partial_{\infty}\widetilde{K}
at distance

. Note that since

does not increase distances, we have that the distance between

[\widetilde{r}_{i}]
and
[\widetilde{r}_{i+2}]
is
\frac{4\pi}{7}
. Consequently, the geodesics joining consecutive
[\widetilde{r}_{i}]

form a locally (hence globally, since

is

) isometrically

[

]
*Prop 2.1, there is a flat

∎

6.4.
Curvature

Let
D
be a disc diagram with a degenerate piecewise smooth Euclidean metric. For a vertex
v\in D
, the
angle
at
v
is the sum of the
Riemannian angles at
v
of all
2
-cells of
D
containing
v
. We analogously define
angles
at
v
between a pair of edges containing
v
.
If
v\in\mathrm{int}\,D
, then its
curvature

\kappa(v)
is
2\pi
minus its angle. If
v\in\partial D
, then its
boundary curvature

\kappa_{\partial}(v)
is
\pi
minus its angle. If
e
is an edge of
D
, then its
curvature

\kappa(e)
is the integral of its geodesic curvature in the (one or two)
2
-cells of
D
containing it. In particular, if
e
is a Riemannian
geodesic in the
2
-cells containing it, then
\kappa(e)=0
. From adding up the classical Gauss–Bonnet formula for the
2
-cells of
D
, we obtain
the following (see e.g.
[
3
]
*§2.3).

Theorem 6.9
.

Let
D
be a disc diagram. Then

\sum_{v\in\mathrm{int}D}\kappa(v)+\sum_{v\in\partial D}\kappa_{\partial}(v)+\sum_{e\subset D}\kappa(e)=2\pi.

We have the following consequence of
[
38
]
*Thm 2. For the reader’s convenience, we include the proof in our
2
-dimensional setup. A similar
proof was earlier provided in
[
1
]
*Thm 12.1 in the case where each edge
e
of
X
is a Riemannian geodesic in the
2
-cells
containing it.

Theorem 6.10
.

Suppose that
X
is
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
. Let
\gamma\colon S^{1}\to X
be an embedded closed path consisting of
3

or
4
geodesic segments. Then each reduced relative disc diagram
\phi\colon D\to X
with boundary
\gamma
is embedded.

Proof.

Step
1
.

\phi
is a local embedding.

Let
V\subset D
denote the set of the (at most
4
) endpoints of the geodesic segments in
\partial D
. Since
X
is
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
, each vertex

v\in\mathrm{int}\,D
and each edge
e\not\subset\partial D
has nonpositive curvature. Since
\gamma
is locally geodesic outside
V
, each
e\subset\partial D
or
v\in\partial D\setminus V
has also nonpositive (boundary) curvature. Finally, each vertex of
V
has curvature
\leq\pi
. (In fact, it is
<\pi
or one
of the edges containing this vertex has negative curvature.)
Let
\mathcal{K}
denote the sum of all
these curvatures.

Let
e\neq f
be oriented edges starting at a vertex
v
of
D
, and suppose
\phi(e)=\phi(f)
. If
v\in\mathrm{int}\,D
, then, since
X
is

\mathrm{CAT}(0)
, both angles at
v
between
e
and
f
are
\geq 2\pi
, so the angle at
v
is
\geq 4\pi
. Thus
\kappa(v)\leq-2\pi
, and so

\mathcal{K}<4\pi-2\pi
, which contradicts Theorem
6.9
. If
v\in\partial D\setminus V
, then let
\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3}
be the angle
at
v
, decomposed to indicate the angles at
v
between
\partial D,e,f,
and
\partial D
again. Since
X
is
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
, we have

\theta_{2}\geq 2\pi
. Since
v\in\partial D\setminus V
, we have
\theta_{1}+\theta_{3}\geq\pi
. Consequently,
\kappa_{\partial}(v)\leq-2\pi
, which is again a contradiction. Finally, if

v\in V
, then
\kappa_{\partial}(v)\leq-\pi
, and so
\mathcal{K}<3\pi-\pi
, contradiction.

Step
2
.

\phi
is an embedding.

Suppose that
\beta\subset D
is an embedded combinatorial path from
w_{1}
to
w_{2}
such that the restriction of
\phi
to
\beta
is an embedding
except that
\phi(w_{1})=\phi(w_{2})
. Let

be a reduced relative disc diagram with boundary
\phi_{|\beta}
, guaranteed by Lemma

. Suppose that among
such

and

, the disc diagram

has the minimal possible number of
2
-cells.

Let
v^{\prime}\in\partial D^{\prime}\setminus\psi^{-1}\phi(w_{1})
be a vertex with
\kappa_{\partial}(v^{\prime})>0
and let

be the edges of
\partial D^{\prime}
containing

v^{\prime}
. Let

be such that
\phi(e)=\psi(e^{\prime}),\phi(v)=\psi(v^{\prime}),\phi(f)=\psi(f^{\prime})
. By the minimality assumption, the images of the
restrictions of
\phi
and
\psi
to the open neighbourhoods of

in any
2
-cells containing

, are distinct, and the same property holds
for

. Thus, if

, then since

is
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
, both angles in

between

and

are
\geq\pi+\kappa_{\partial}(v^{\prime})
,
and so

. If

\theta_{2}\geq\pi+\kappa_{\partial}(v^{\prime})

.

Let now

By Theorem

Lemma 12.1

Let

Let

We will be applying Lemma

Proof of Lemma

.

We prove the lemma by induction on

. If

. Since

M. Anoussis, G. K. Eleftherakis, A. Katavolos

Abstract
. We investigate conditions for the extendibility of continuous algebra homomorphisms ϕ \phi from the Fourier algebra A ⁡ ( F ) A(F) of a locally compact group F F to the Fourier-Stieltjes algebra B ⁡ ( G ) B(G) of a locally compact group G G to maps between the corresponding L ∞ L^{\infty} algebras which are weak* continuous. When ϕ \phi is completely bounded and F F is amenable, it is induced by a piecewise affine map α : Y → F \alpha:Y\to F where Y ⊆ G Y\subseteq G . We show that extendibility of ϕ \phi is equivalent to α \alpha being an open map. We also study the dual problem for contractive homomorphisms ϕ : L 1 ​ ( F ) → M ⁡ ( G ) \phi:L^{1}(F)\to M(G) . We show that ϕ \phi induces a w* continuous homomorphism between the von Neumann algebras of the groups if and only if the naturally associated map θ \theta (Greenleaf [1965], Stokke [2011]) is a proper map.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we investigate conditions under which homomorphisms between algebras associated to
locally compact groups extend to von Neumann algebras containing them.

If
G
is a locally compact group, the group algebra
L^{1}(G)
as well as the measure algebra
M(G)
act on
L^{2}(G)

by convolution; their weak* closure is the von Neumann algebra of the group,
VN(G)
.
It is thus a natural problem to determine conditions under which an algebra homomorphism
from
L^{1}(F)
to
M(G)
induces a w*-continuous map between the corresponding von Neumann algebras.

The Fourier and Fourier-Stieltjes algebras of
G
also act on
L^{2}(G)
by (pointwise) multiplication, and their
w* closure is the multiplication algebra of
L^{\infty}(G)
.
Hence the corresponding problem about algebra homomorphisms from
A(F)
to
B(G)
is also natural.

The Fourier and Fourier-Stieltjes algebras of a locally compact group
G
were introduced
by Eymard in
[
9
]
and have since been a main object of study in the area of
Noncommutative Harmonic Analysis.

Recall that the Fourier-Stieltjes algebra
B(G)

is the set of all coefficient functions

s\to\left(\pi(s)\xi,\eta\right),\ (\xi,\eta\in H_{\pi})
defined by unitary representations

(\pi,H_{\pi})
of
G
, while the Fourier algebra
A(G)
of
G
consists of the coefficients
of the left regular representation
s\to\lambda_{s}
on
L^{2}(G)
, given by
\lambda_{s}\xi(t):=\xi(s^{-1}t)
.

When
G
is abelian, the Fourier algebra is isometrically isomorphic to the group algebra
L^{1}(\hat{G})

of the dual group
\hat{G}
of
G
and the Fourier-Stieltjes algebra is isometrically isomorphic to the
measure algebra
M(\hat{G})
. These isomorphisms are realised through the Fourier transform.

An important problem in Harmonic Analysis is the determination of all
homomorphisms
\phi:A(F)\rightarrow B(G)
, for locally compact groups
F
and
G
.

A bounded homomorphism
\phi:A(F)\rightarrow B(G)
always takes the form

\phi(u)=\phi_{\alpha}(u):=\chi_{Y}(u\!\circ\!\alpha)
for an open subset
Y\subseteq G
and a continuous map
\alpha:Y\to F
.

Cohen
[
5
]
completely characterized homomorphisms
L^{1}(F)\rightarrow M(G)

for locally compact
abelian

groups in terms of piecewise affine maps between the dual groups
\hat{G}
and
\hat{F}
.
In view of the above remark,
it follows that the result of Cohen provides a complete
characterisation of homomorphisms
A(F)\rightarrow B(G)
for abelian groups in terms of piecewise affine maps

\alpha:G\rightarrow F
.
To obtain his result he used a characterization of the idempotents in
B(G)
which he proved in
[
4
]
.
This result revealed the fundamental role of the open coset ring of the group
G
.
Host
[
11
]
generalized the characterization of idempotents of
B(G)
to general locally compact groups.

Ilie and Spronk used the natural operator space structure of the Fourier algebra, as the predual of the von
Neumann algebra of the group, to prove that if
F
is amenable, a completely bounded homomorphism

\phi:A(F)\rightarrow B(G)
is induced by a continuous piecewise affine map
\alpha
as above

[
13
,
6
]
. This generalises the result of Cohen.

Pham in
[
17
]
, characterized the contractive homomorphisms
\phi:A(F)\rightarrow B(G)
.
However, the general case remains open.

In the first part of this paper we prove that if
F
and
G
are locally compact second countable groups,
and the homomorphism
\phi_{\alpha}
is induced by a mixed continuous piecewise affine map
\alpha

(see Definition
2.2
), then
\phi_{\alpha}
extends to a w*-w* continuous map from
L^{\infty}(F)
to

L^{\infty}(G)
if and only if
\alpha
is an open map. For amenable
F
, the maps
\phi_{\alpha}
that we
consider strictly include those covered by the results of Ilie and Spronk
[
13
]
.

Note, for comparison, that Ilie and Stokke characterised in
[
14
]
the maps from
A(F)
to
B(G)

induced by piecewise affine maps that extend to maps from
B(F)
to
B(G)
which are continuous
for the respective w* topologies.

In the second part of the paper we study the dual problem: we
provide necessary and sufficient
conditions under which contractive homomorphisms from
L^{1}(F)
to the measure algebra

M(G)
induce homomorphisms
between the corresponding von Neumann algebras which are weak* continuous.

We note that for
F
and
G
abelian, the homomorphisms
\phi:L^{1}(F)\to M(G)
are characterized
by the result of Cohen mentioned above.

To motivate our approach to the dual problem, consider
a continuous homomorphism
\theta:F\rightarrow G
between abelian groups
F
and
G
.
Then the map
\phi:L^{1}(F)\to M(G)
defined by
\phi(f)=\theta_{*}f
(considering
f
as a measure) is a
continuous homomorphism. Taking Fourier transforms we obtain a homomorphism
A(\hat{F})\to B(\hat{G})
,
of the form
u\mapsto u\circ\hat{\theta}
. Then it follows from the results of the first part that this map
extends to a w*-w* continuous map from
L^{\infty}(\hat{F})
to
L^{\infty}(\hat{G})
if and only if

\hat{\theta}
is an open map. But it is known
[
7
, Theorem 8]
that the map

\hat{\theta}
is open if and only if
\theta

is proper. Hence, taking Fourier transforms again,
we conclude that the map
\phi
induces a w*-continuous *-homomorphism

VN(F)\to VN(G)
if and only if
\theta
is a proper map. See Section
3
for details.

This result suggests that a similar condition should hold
for general locally compact groups.

The contractive homomorphisms
\phi:L^{1}(F)\to M(G)
for locally compact groups
F
and
G

were characterized by Greenleaf
[
10
]
.
A more detailed description is given by Stokke
[
20
]
.

The structure of such homomorphisms is more
complicated than in the above special case.
Nevertheless, we prove in Section
4
that there still is a properness condition
which is necessary and sufficient for the existence of a w*-continuous
induced map.

As a consequence, we give a new proof of a result due to Stokke
[
21
]
, that the properness
condition mentioned above is equivalent to the w*-continuity of the extended map
\varphi:M(F)\to M(G)
.

Our approach relies on operator algebra methods and exploits the duality between
the Fourier algebra and the von Neumann algebra of a group.

Notation
We use
\left(\xi,\eta\right)
for the scalar product in a Hilbert space (linear in
\xi
) and

\langle x^{*},x\rangle
for the bilinear pairing between a vector
x
in a Banach space
X
and a linear functional

x^{*}
in its dual. The symbol
\mu\ll\nu
denotes absolute continuity for measures
\mu
and
\nu
(i.e. every

\nu
-null set is
\mu
-null). For a locally compact group
G
, we denote by
g\mapsto\lambda_{g}
or
\lambda_{g}^{G}

its left regular representation and by
\mu\mapsto\lambda^{G}(\mu),\ \mu\in M(G)

its ‘integrated form’ (see section
4
).

2.
Homomorphisms between Fourier Algebras which extend to the corresponding masas.

Let
G
and
F
be locally compact second countable groups with Haar measures
\mu
and
\nu
respectively.
We are concerned with conditions on a homomorphism

\phi:A(F)\to B(G)
which allow extension to a mapping from
L^{\infty}(F)
to
L^{\infty}(G)
.
Consider a bounded homomorphism
\phi:A(F)\rightarrow B(G)
and let

Y=\{t\in G:\exists u\in A(F),\;\;\phi(u)(t)\neq 0\}.

Then
Y
is an open set and there exists a continuous map

\alpha:Y\to F
, such that for all
u\in A(F),
we have

\phi(u)(t)=\phi_{\alpha}(u)(t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u(\alpha(t)),&t\in Y\\
0,&t\in G\setminus Y\end{array}\right.

see
[
16
, p. 101]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
\phi
extends to a w*-continuous map from
L^{\infty}(F)
to
L^{\infty}(G)
,
then the extension is necessarily a
*
-homomorphism, and in fact is given
on bounded measurable functions by the same formula as above.
It follows that the measure
\alpha_{*}\mu
defined on Borel subsets
E\subseteq F

by
\alpha_{*}\mu(E)=\mu(\alpha^{-1}(E))
satisfies
\alpha_{*}\mu\ll\nu
.

Conversely if
\alpha_{*}\mu\ll\nu
then the above formula defines a necessarily w*-continuous homomorphism

\tilde{\phi}:L^{\infty}(F)\to L^{\infty}(G)
which extends
\phi
.

Proof.

Denote the extension by
\tilde{\phi}
. Note first that the formula

\tilde{\phi}(u)(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u(\alpha(t)),&t\in Y\\
0,&t\in G\setminus Y\end{array}\right.

is valid for all
u\in C_{0}(F)
. Indeed by the density of
A(F)
in
C_{0}(F)
there is a
sequence
(u_{n})
in
A(F)
converging uniformly to
u
.

Now let
V\subseteq F
be an open set.
There exists a bounded sequence
(u_{n})\subseteq C_{0}(F)

of functions supported in
V
such that

u_{n}(s)\rightarrow\chi_{V}(s)
for all
s\in F
, where
\chi_{V}
denotes the characteristic function
of
V
.
By dominated convergence this sequence converges to

\chi_{V}
in the weak-* topology. Since
\tilde{\phi}
is w*-continuous, we have that

(2.1)

\tilde{\phi}(\chi_{V})(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\chi_{V}(\alpha(t)),&\text{for a.a. }t\in Y\\
0,&\text{for a.a. }t\in G\setminus Y\end{array}\right.

The collection of all Borel sets
V\subseteq F
for which
\tilde{\phi}(\chi_{V})

satisfies (
2.1
) is easily seen to be a
\sigma
-algebra.
Since it contains the open sets, it is equal to the Borel
\sigma
-algebra.

Therefore

(2.2)

\tilde{\phi}(u)(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u(\alpha(t)),&\text{for a.a. }t\in Y\\
0,&\text{for a.a. }t\in G\setminus Y\end{array}\right.

holds whenever
u
is the characteristic function of a Borel set, and hence
(by linearity and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty}
-continuity) it holds for all functions in
L^{\infty}(F)
.

It is now obvious that
\tilde{\phi}
is a *-homomorphism.

Now if
E\subseteq F
is any Borel set,
\tilde{\phi}(\chi_{E})=\chi_{\alpha^{-1}(E)}
must be in
L^{\infty}(G)
.
It follows that if
\nu(E)=0
then
\mu(\alpha^{-1}(E))
must vanish. This shows that
\alpha_{*}\mu\ll\nu
.

Conversely if
\alpha_{*}\mu\ll\nu
then (
2.2
) clearly defines a *-homomorphism
\tilde{\phi}

of
L^{\infty}(F)
into
L^{\infty}(G)
. To see that
\tilde{\phi}
is w*-continuous, let
\{E_{i}\}
be a family
of pairwise disjoint Borel subsets of
F
and
E:=\cup_{i}E_{i}
. Then

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}(\sum_{i}\chi_{E_{i}})

\displaystyle=\tilde{\phi}(\chi_{\cup_{i}E_{i}})=\tilde{\phi}(\chi_{E})=\chi_{\alpha^{-1}(E)}

\displaystyle=\chi_{\cup_{i}\alpha^{-1}(E_{i})}=\sum_{i}\chi_{\alpha^{-1}(E_{i})}=\sum_{i}\tilde{\phi}(\chi_{E_{i}})\,.

It follows from this that for any nonnegative
h\in L^{1}(G)
the positive linear
form
u\to\langle\tilde{\phi}(u),h\rangle
is completely additive, hence w*-continuous; thus for any

h\in L^{1}(G)
the linear form
u\to\langle\tilde{\phi}(u),h\rangle
is w*-continuous,
and thus
\tilde{\phi}
is w*-continuous.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

The assumption of w*-continuity of the extension cannot be omitted.

Indeed, let

\phi:A(F)\rightarrow B(G)
be as above with
Y\neq G
and

\tilde{\phi}:L^{\infty}(F)\rightarrow L^{\infty}(G)
be the map defined by
(
2.2
).
If
F
is not compact, the ideal generated by
A(F)
in
L^{\infty}(F)
is proper.
Let
\delta:L^{\infty}(F)\to\mathbb{C}
be a nonzero homomorphism which vanishes on this ideal.
Then the map

\phi_{1}:u\rightarrow\tilde{\phi}(u)+\delta(u)\chi_{G\setminus Y}

is a *-homomorphism from

L^{\infty}(F)
to
L^{\infty}(G)
which extends
\phi
but does not satisfy the above formula.

In this section we will prove that for a certain class of naturally arising continuous maps
\alpha
the condition

\alpha_{*}(\mu)\ll\nu
holds if and only if
\alpha
is an open map.

The following is Theorem 3.5 of
[
1
]
:

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
G
and
F
be locally compact second countable groups
with Haar measures
\mu
and
\nu
respectively, and let

\theta:G\rightarrow F
be a continuous homomorphism. The following are equivalent:

(i)
\theta_{*}(\mu)\ll\nu

(ii)
\theta(G)
is an open subgroup of
F
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
and
F
be locally compact groups,
K
a subgroup of
G
and
C

a left coset of
K
in
G
. A map
\alpha:C\rightarrow F
is
called affine (resp. anti-affine) if there exists a
homomorphism
(resp. anti-homomorphism)
\theta:K\rightarrow F
and elements
s_{0}\in F,t_{0}\in G

such that
C=t_{0}^{-1}K
and

\alpha(t)=s_{0}\theta(t_{0}t)

for all
t\in C=t_{0}^{-1}K
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
G
and
F
be locally compact second countable groups with
Haar measures
\mu
and
\nu
respectively. Let
C
be a coset of an open subgroup

K
of
G
and
\alpha:C\rightarrow F

a continuous affine or anti-affine map. The following are equivalent:

(i) The measure
\alpha_{*}\mu
satisfies
\alpha_{*}\mu\ll\nu
.

(ii)
\alpha(C)
is an open subset of
F
.

Proof.

We prove the corollary for affine maps. The proof for anti-affine maps is similar.
Write

\alpha=l_{s_{0}}\circ\theta\circ l_{t_{0}},

where
l_{x}
denotes left translation by
x
.
It is clear that
\alpha(C)
is open if and only if
\theta(K)
is open
and that
\alpha_{*}{\mu}\ll\nu
if and only if

\theta_{*}\mu_{K}\ll\nu
, where
\mu_{K}
is the restriction of
\mu
to
K
.
Since
K
is open,
\mu_{K}
is a Haar measure for
K
, and Theorem
2.3
yields the result.
∎

We shall need the following
definition of mixed piecewise affine maps, which is a modification of the one in
[
19
]
.
Note that we have included continuity in the definition of the maps.

If
G
is a locally compact group, the
open coset ring

\Omega_{0}(G)
of
G

is the smallest ring of subsets of
G
containing the open cosets.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G
and
F
be locally compact groups and
Y
an open and closed (clopen) subset of
G.

A map
\alpha:Y\rightarrow F
is called mixed piecewise affine if there exist
disjoint clopen sets
\{Y_{i}:i=1,...,m\}\subseteq\Omega_{0}(G)
such that
Y=\cup_{i=1}^{m}Y_{i}

and open cosets
C_{i}
of
G
such that
Y_{i}\subseteq C_{i}
and affine
or anti-affine
continuous
maps
\alpha_{i}:C_{i}\rightarrow F

such that
\alpha|_{Y_{i}}=\alpha_{i}|_{Y_{i}}.

If all the
\alpha_{i}:C_{i}\rightarrow F
are affine maps,
\alpha
is called piecewise affine.

Recall that a second countable locally compact space is metrisable.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
G
and
F
be locally compact second countable groups and
Y\subseteq G
be a clopen set. Let

\alpha:Y\rightarrow F
be a mixed piecewise affine map and
m,Y_{i},C_{i},\alpha_{i}
, for

i=1,2,\dots,m
be as in Definition
2.2
.
The following are equivalent:

(1)

\alpha_{*}\mu_{Y}\ll\nu

(2)

the sets

\alpha_{i}(C_{i}),i=1,\dots,m
are open in
F.

Proof.

(1) implies (2):

Let
i=1,\dots,m
. We show that
\alpha_{i}(C_{i})
is open. Since
\alpha_{i}(C_{i})
is a coset, it suffices to show
that
\nu(\alpha_{i}(C_{i}))>0
. But if
\nu(\alpha_{i}(C_{i}))=0
then
\nu(\alpha_{i}(Y_{i}))=0
, so

\alpha_{*}\mu_{Y_{i}}(\alpha_{i}(Y_{i}))=\mu(Y_{i})=0
.
Since
Y_{i}
is open, this is a contradiction.

(2) implies (1):
Suppose first that
m=1
, so that
\alpha
is the restriction of a continuous affine
or anti-affine map. If
\alpha(C)
is open, then by Corollary
2.4
,

\alpha_{*}\mu_{C}\ll\nu
, and a fortiori
\alpha_{*}\mu_{Y}\ll\nu
.

For the general case, suppose that the sets
\alpha_{i}(C_{i})
are open. By the special case just proved, we have

{\alpha_{i}}_{*}\mu_{Y_{i}}\ll\nu
for each
i
.
Since
\sum{\alpha_{i}}_{*}\mu_{Y_{i}}=\alpha_{*}\mu_{Y}
,
it follows that
\alpha_{*}\mu_{Y}\ll\nu
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

Note that the condition that
\alpha_{i}(C_{i}),i=1,\dots,m
are open in
F
is equivalent to the condition
that
\alpha
is an open map.

Indeed, let
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
. There exist an open subgroup

K_{i}
of
G
,
t_{i}\in G
,
s_{i}\in F
, and a continuous homomorphism or
anti-homomorphism
\theta_{i}:K_{i}\rightarrow F
such that
C_{i}=t_{i}^{-1}K_{i}
and

\alpha_{i}(t)=s_{i}\theta_{i}(t_{i}t)
, for all
t\in C_{i}
. Since

\alpha_{i}(C_{i})
is open in
F
, the subgroup
\theta_{i}(K_{i})
is also open in
F
and
it follows by
[
1
, Lemma 3.4]
that
\theta_{i}
is an open map and hence
\alpha_{i}
is an open map.

Therefore if
V\subseteq Y
is an open set, then for each
i
the set
\alpha(V\cap Y_{i})
is open, since
it equals
\alpha_{i}(V\cap Y_{i})
; hence
\alpha(V)=\cup_{i}\alpha(V\cap Y_{i})
is open. ∎

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
G
and
F
be locally compact second countable groups and
Y\subseteq G
be a clopen set. Let

\alpha:Y\rightarrow F
be a mixed piecewise affine map
(Definition
2.2
). The following are equivalent:

(1)

The map
\phi_{\alpha}:A(F)\to B(G)
extends to a bounded
w^{*}
-continuous homomorphism from

L^{\infty}(F)
to
L^{\infty}(G)
.

(2)

\alpha
is an open map.

Proof.

(1) implies (2):

Suppose that the map
\phi_{\alpha}
extends to a bounded
w^{*}
-continuous
homomorphism from
L^{\infty}(F)
to
L^{\infty}(G)
. For
i=1,\dots,m
and
u\in A(F)
let

\phi_{i}(u)(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\phi_{\alpha}(u)(t),&t\in Y_{i}\\
0,&t\in G\setminus Y_{i}\end{array}\right.

(note that
\phi_{i}
maps
A(F)
to
B(G)
since the characteristic function of
Y_{i}
is in
B(G)
).
Clearly
\phi_{i}
extends to a map
from
L^{\infty}(F)
to
L^{\infty}(G)
and hence, by Proposition
2.1
,

{\alpha_{i}}_{*}(\mu_{Y_{i}})\ll\nu
.

Therefore, from Theorem
2.5
and Remark
2.6
,
it follows that
\alpha
is an open map.

(2) implies (1):

Conversely, suppose that
\alpha
is an open map. Then

\alpha_{*}\mu_{Y}\ll\nu
.

Indeed the restriction of
\alpha
to each
Y_{i}
coincides with
\alpha_{i}|_{Y_{i}}
.
Thus
\alpha_{i}|_{Y_{i}}
is an open map.
But
\alpha_{i}
is affine or anti-affine, hence
\alpha_{i}(x)=s\theta(tx)
for some
s,t
, where
tC_{i}
is a subgroup
of
G
and
\theta:tC_{i}\to F
is a continuous
homomorphism (or anti-homomorphism). When
\theta
is a homomorphism,

\alpha_{i}(Y_{i})=s\theta(tY_{i})
so
\theta(tY_{i})
must be open in
F
; thus

\theta(tC_{i})
is a group containing a non empty open set, hence it must be open
and so
\alpha(C_{i})=s\theta(tC_{i})
is open. Thus
the claim follows by Theorem
2.5
.
The case of an anti-homomorphism is similar.

Therefore the map

\phi_{0}:L^{\infty}(F,\nu)\rightarrow L^{\infty}(Y,\mu_{Y}):\;\;\;\phi_{0}(u)=u\circ\alpha

is a w*-continuous *-homomorphism. The space
L^{\infty}(Y,\mu_{Y})

is a direct summand of
L^{\infty}(G,\mu)
and the injection

\iota:L^{\infty}(Y,\mu_{Y})\rightarrow L^{\infty}(G,\mu)
is
w^{*}
-continuous.
Hence
\tilde{\phi}:=\iota\circ\phi_{0}
is
w^{*}
-continuous and clearly extends
\phi_{\alpha}
.
∎

A related result is proved by Ilie and Stokke in
[

]
: if

Y\subseteq G
is an open set,
\alpha:Y\rightarrow F
a piecewise affine map and

the corresponding map from

to

then
\phi_{\alpha}
extends to a map

which is continuous for the respective w* topologies
if and only if

3.
The dual problem for locally compact abelian groups

Let
E
and
H
be locally compact abelian groups and
\omega:E\to H
a continuous homomorphism.
It follows from our results in Section 2 that the map
u\to u\circ\omega:A(H)\to B(E)
extends to a w*-continuous
*-homomorphism
L^{\infty}(H)\to L^{\infty}(E)
if and only if
\omega
is open.

In the next section, we will consider the dual problem for general locally compact groups:
under what conditions a homomorphism

L^{1}(H)\to M(E)
induces a w*-continuous
*-homomorphism
VN(H)\to VN(E)
.

Proposition
3.2
below examines what happens in the abelian group case. We include it as motivation
for our Theorem
4.14
(of which of course it is a special case). It is a consequence of Theorem

2.7
and the following result:

Theorem 3.1
.

[
7
, Theorem 8]

Let
E
and
H
be locally compact abelian groups and
\omega:E\to H
a continuous homomorphism.
If
\hat{E}
and
\hat{H}
are the dual groups
we denote by
\hat{\omega}:\hat{H}\to\hat{E}
the dual map
(given by
\hat{\omega}(\gamma)=\gamma\circ\omega
for every
\gamma\in\hat{H}
).

The following are equivalent:

(i) the map
\omega:E\to H
is open

(ii) the map
\hat{\omega}:\hat{H}\to\hat{E}
is proper.

Below if
\theta:F\to G
is a measurable map,
for
f\in L^{1}(F)
we denote by
\theta_{*}(f)\in M(G)
the measure given by

\theta_{*}(f)(A)=\int_{\theta^{-1}(A)}f(t)dt,\ A\subseteq G
Borel.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
F
and
G
are locally compact abelian groups,
a homomorphism
\phi:L^{1}(F)\to M(G)
of the form
f\mapsto\theta_{*}(f)
,
where
\theta:F\to G
is a continuous group homomorphism, induces
a w*-continuous *-homomorphism
VN(F)\to VN(G)
if and only if
\theta
is proper.

Proof.

Recall that, for an abelian group
F
, the map
u\to\hat{u}
(where
\hat{u}
is the Fourier transform of
u
)
is an isometric algebra isomorphism of
A(F)
onto
L^{1}(\hat{F})
and

u(x)=\int_{\hat{F}}\hat{u}(\eta)\eta(x)d\eta

for all
x\in F
(integration is understood with respect to suitably normalized Haar measure on
\hat{F}
).
The dual map of the isomorphism

L^{1}(\hat{F})\to A(F):\hat{u}\mapsto u

is a w*-continuous isomorphism
\omega^{F}:VN(F)\to L^{\infty}(\hat{F})
satisfying, for each
x\in F
,

VN(F)\ni\lambda^{F}_{x}\mapsto g^{F}_{x}\in L^{\infty}(\hat{F})

where

g^{F}_{x}(\eta)=\eta(x),\quad\eta\in\hat{F}.

Assume that
\theta
is a proper homomorphism.
Then
\hat{\theta}:\hat{G}\to\hat{F}
is open, and so by Theorem
2.7
the map

\hat{\phi}:A(\hat{F})\to L^{\infty}(\hat{G}):\hat{\phi}(u)=u\circ\hat{\theta}

extends to
a w*-continuous map
L^{\infty}(\hat{F})\to L^{\infty}(\hat{G})
denoted by the same symbol.
Consider the composition

\psi:VN(F)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega^{F}}}{{\rightarrow}}L^{\infty}(\hat{F})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\hat{\phi}}}{{\rightarrow}}L^{\infty}(\hat{G})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\omega^{G})^{-1}}}{{\rightarrow}}VN(G).

For each
x\in F
, this gives

\psi:\lambda^{F}_{x}\to g^{F}_{x}\to g^{F}_{x}\circ\hat{\theta}\to\lambda^{G}_{\theta(x)}

because
g^{F}_{x}\circ\hat{\theta}=g^{G}_{\theta(x)}
;
indeed, for each
\gamma\in\hat{G}
,

g_{\theta(x)}^{G}(\gamma)=\gamma(\theta(x))=g_{x}^{F}(\gamma\circ\theta)=(g_{x}^{F}\!\circ\hat{\theta})(\gamma).

Thus

\psi(\lambda^{F}_{x})=\lambda^{G}_{\theta(x)}

for all
x\in F
, and therefore, since
\psi
is w*-continuous,

\psi(\lambda^{F}(\mu))=\lambda^{G}(\theta_{*}(\mu))

for all
\mu\in M(F)
.
This shows that the map

\theta_{*}:L^{1}(F)\to M(G):f\mapsto\theta_{*}(f),

which extends to a map from
M(F)
to
M(G)
,
induces the map
\psi
, in the sense that
\psi\circ\lambda^{F}=\lambda^{G}\circ\theta_{*}
.

Conversely, assume that the map
\theta_{*}:L^{1}(F)\to M(G)
,
where
\theta:F\to G
is a continuous group homomorphism, induces a
w^{*}
-continuous homomorphism

\psi:VN(F)\to VN(G)\;\;\text{such that }\;\lambda_{x}\mapsto\lambda_{\theta(x)}\quad\text{for }\;x\in F.

Then the composition

\hat{\phi}:L^{\infty}(\hat{F})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\omega^{F})^{-1}}}{{\rightarrow}}VN(F)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\rightarrow}}VN(G)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega^{F}}}{{\rightarrow}}L^{\infty}(\hat{G})

is a
w^{*}
-continuous homomorphism which satisfies, for each
x\in F
,

\hat{\phi}:g^{F}_{x}\to\lambda^{F}_{x}\to\lambda^{G}_{\theta(x)}\to g^{F}_{x}\circ\hat{\theta}\,.

Since the w*-closed linear span of
\{g^{F}_{x}:x\in F\}
is
L^{\infty}(\hat{F})
, it follows
that

\hat{\phi}(f)=f\circ\hat{\theta}

for all
f\in L^{\infty}(\hat{F})
.
Since
\hat{\theta}:\hat{G}\to\hat{F}
is a continuous homomorphism we have that the restriction of

\hat{\phi}
sends
A(\hat{F})
to
B(\hat{G}).
Applying Theorem
2.7
, we see that the homomorphism

\hat{\theta}:\hat{G}\to\hat{F}

is open and therefore, by Theorem
3.1
,
\theta
is proper.
∎

We proved that if
F
and
G
are abelian locally compact groups
and
\phi:L^{1}(F)\to M(G)
is a homomorphism
of the form
\phi(f)=\theta_{*}(f)
with
\theta
a continuous homomorphism
F\rightarrow G
,
then
\phi
induces a w*-continuous *-homomorphism
\psi:VN(F)\to VN(G)

if and only if
\theta
is proper. This result suggests that a similar condition should hold for not necessarily
abelian groups
F
and
G
. Greenleaf
[
10
]
has shown that the form of a
contractive homomorphism

is more general than the
one we consider in Proposition
3.2
.
Nevertheless, we prove in the
next section that in the general case there still is a properness condition which is
necessary and sufficient for the existence of the induced map
\psi
.

4.
The dual problem for arbitrary locally compact groups

In this section we investigate conditions on a homomorphism
\phi:L^{1}(F)\to M(G)
under which
the corresponding homomorphism between the convolution algebras induces a w* continuous
homomorphism
\psi:VN(F)\to VN(G)
.

We begin with some preliminaries.

Let
G
be a locally compact group. The space
M(G)
of complex measures on
G

is the dual of
C_{0}(G)
; the duality is given by

\langle\mu,f\rangle=\int_{G}fd\mu,\quad f\in C_{0}(G),\,\mu\in M(G).

The space
M(G)
is a Banach algebra under the following multiplication

\langle\mu\ast\nu,f\rangle=\int_{G}\int_{G}f(ts)d\mu(t)d\nu(s),\quad f\in C_{0}(G).

We say that the net
(\mu_{i})\subseteq M(G)
converges in the
so
topology to
\mu\in M(G)
if

\left\|\mu_{i}\ast f-\mu\ast f\right\|_{L^{1}}\to 0\quad\text{for all }\,f\in L^{1}(G).

We will need the following simple facts:

Lemma 4.1
.

The map
\lambda:M(G)\to VN(G)
is the dual of the inclusion
\iota:A(G)\to C_{0}(G)
.

Proof.

An element
u\in A(G)
is a vector functional on
VN(G)
, i.e. an element of the predual

(VN(G))_{*}
given by

\langle T,u\rangle=\left(T\xi,\eta\right)
for some
\xi,\eta\in L^{2}(G)
. In particular

\langle\lambda(\mu),u\rangle=\left(\lambda(\mu)\xi,\eta\right)=\int_{G}\left(\lambda_{t}\xi,\eta\right)d\mu(t).

But the functional
u
defines a function
\iota(u)\in C_{0}(G)
, by

\iota(u)(s):=\langle\lambda_{s},u\rangle=\left(\lambda_{s}\xi,\eta\right)
(usually one denotes the function
\iota(u)
by
u
,
identifying the functional with the function). We have

\displaystyle\langle\mu,\iota(u)\rangle=\int\iota(u)(t)d\mu(t)=\int_{G}\left(\lambda_{t}\xi,\eta\right)d\mu(t)=\langle\lambda(\mu),u\rangle\,.

This relation holds for all
u\in A(G)
and all
\mu\in M(G)
and shows that
\lambda=\iota^{*}
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
F
be a locally compact group. Then
the maps

\lambda:(M(F),w^{*})\to(VN(F),w^{*})\quad\text{and }\quad\lambda:(M(F),so)\to(VN(F),w^{*})\,

are continuous.

Proof.

The first assertion follows from Lemma
4.1
.

For the second assertion, since the identity map
\iota:M(F)\to M(F)
is
so\text{-}w^{*}
-continuous

[
20
, Proposition 2.2]
, the composition
\lambda=\lambda\circ\iota:M(F)\to VN(F)
is
so\text{-}w^{*}
-continuous.
∎

The proof of the following Proposition is contained in
[
13
, Corollary 3.11]
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
F
and
G
be locally compact groups,
\theta:F\rightarrow G

a continuous homomorphism and

\rho(u)=u\circ\theta
for every
u\in A(G).
Then:
\theta
is proper if and only if
\rho(A(G))\subseteq A(F).

Proposition 4.4
.

[
20
, Proposition 2.2]

Any bounded homomorphism
\varphi_{0}:L^{1}(F)\to M(G)
has a unique
so
-
w^{*}
-continuous
extension to a homomorphism
\varphi:M(F)\rightarrow M(G)
.

Thus in the sequel it will be enough to consider
so
-
w^{*}
-continuous homomorphisms between the
measure algebras.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
F
and
G
be locally compact groups
and

\varphi:M(F)\rightarrow M(G)

be a
so
-
w^{*}
-continuous homomorphism.
We say that
\varphi
has the extension property if there exists a
w^{*}
-continuous
homomorphism

such that

Note that it follows from
[

, Proposition 5.5]
that a

w^{*}
continuous, contractive homomorphism

For clarity of exposition, we begin with the case of a unital homomorphism, since in this case the

4.1.
The unital case

In this subsection we assume that
\varphi:M(F)\rightarrow M(G)
is a contractive unital

-homomorphism.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
F
and
G
be locally compact groups and

\varphi:M(F)\rightarrow M(G)

so
-
w^{*}
-continuous contractive unital

-homomorphism

(\varphi(\delta_{e_{F}})=\delta_{e_{G}})

Then there exist, necessarily unique, continuous homomorphisms

\theta:F\rightarrow G,\;\beta:F\to\mathbb{T}

\varphi(\delta^{F}_{x})=\beta(x)\delta^{G}_{\theta(x)},\;x\in F.

Proof.

Applying
[
10

to the group

for each

\varphi(\delta_{x}^{F})=b\delta^{G}_{g}

We define maps

\theta(x)=g\text{ and }\beta(x)=b\;\Leftrightarrow\;\varphi(\delta_{x}^{F})=b\delta^{G}_{g}\,.

Using the fact that
\varphi
is unital and multiplicative, it is easily checked that

\theta
and
\beta
are well defined and are group homomorphisms.
We have

\varphi(\delta^{F}_{x})=\beta(x)\delta^{G}_{\theta(x)},\;\;x\in F.

Since
\varphi
is
so
-
w^{*}
-continuous the homomorphisms
\theta
and
\beta
are continuous.
This follows from
[
20

. We include a short proof for completeness:

Assume that
x_{i}\rightarrow x.
We shall prove that
\beta(x_{i})\rightarrow\beta(x)
and

\theta(x_{i})\rightarrow\theta(x).

Since
w^{*}\text{-}\lim\delta_{x_{i}}^{F}=\delta_{x}^{F}
, by
[
10
, Lemma 1.1.2]

we have
so
-
\lim\delta_{x_{i}}^{F}=\delta_{x}^{F}

and hence
w^{*}
-
\lim\varphi(\delta_{x_{i}}^{F})=\varphi(\delta_{x}^{F})
. Thus

w^{*}\text{-}\lim_{i}\beta(x_{i})\delta^{G}_{\theta(x_{i})}=\beta(x)\delta^{G}_{\theta(x)}.

Take a subnet
(\delta_{\theta(x_{j})})
of
(\delta_{\theta(x_{i})})
which converges, say to
\mu.

Since
\{\beta(x_{j})\}
lies in a compact set, passing to a subnet if necessary we may assume that

\beta(x_{j})\to b
for some

.
We have

\mu=w^{*}\text{-}\lim_{j}\delta_{\theta(x_{j})}=w^{*}\text{-}\lim_{j}\frac{1}{\beta(x_{j})}\beta(x_{j})\delta_{\theta(x_{j})}=\frac{1}{b}\beta(x)\delta_{\theta(x)}.

Since

We proved that every convergent subnet of

It follows that

A similar argument shows that

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
F
and
G
be locally compact groups and

\varphi:M(F)\rightarrow M(G)

be a unital contractive
so
-
w^{*}
-continuous homomorphism.
The following are equivalent:

(i) The map
\varphi
has the extension property.

(ii) The homomorphism
\theta
is proper.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)

Suppose there exists a w*-continuous map
\psi:VN(F)\rightarrow VN(G)
such that

\psi\circ\lambda^{F}=\lambda^{G}\circ\varphi
.
For all
x\in F
we have

\psi(\lambda_{x}^{F})=\psi\circ\lambda(\delta_{x}^{F})=\lambda\circ\varphi(\delta_{x}^{F})=\lambda(\beta(x)\delta^{G}_{\theta(x)})=\beta(x)\lambda^{G}_{\theta(x)}.

Therefore if
\psi_{*}:A(G)\rightarrow A(F)
is the predual of
\psi
then for all
u\in A(G)
we have

\langle\lambda_{x}^{F},\psi_{*}(u)\rangle=\langle\psi(\lambda_{x}^{F}),u\rangle=\langle\beta(x)\lambda^{G}_{\theta(x)},u\rangle=\beta(x)(u\circ\theta)(x)

and thus the map
x\to\beta(x)(u\circ\theta)(x)
is in
A(F)
.
Since the map
x\to\overline{\beta(x)}
is in the Fourier-Stieltjes algebra
B(F)

(
\overline{\beta}
is a representation) it follows that
u\circ\theta\in A(F).

By Proposition
4.3
,
\theta
must be a proper homomorphism.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)

Conversely assume that
\theta
is a proper homomorphism.
Then for all
u\in A(G)
the map
u\circ\theta
is in
A(F)
and so is the map

F\to\mathbb{C}:x\mapsto\beta(x)(u\circ\theta)(x)
. Hence we may define

\rho:A(G)\rightarrow A(F)\;\text{by}\;\rho(u)(x)=\beta(x)(u\circ\theta)(x),\,\,x\,\in\,F.

Then
\psi=\rho^{*}:VN(F)\rightarrow VN(G)
is a w* continuous *-homomorphism satisfying

\psi(\lambda_{x}^{F})=\beta(x)\lambda^{G}_{\theta(x)}.

Thus we have

(\psi\circ\lambda^{F})(\delta_{x}^{F})=\psi(\lambda_{x}^{F})=\beta(x)\lambda^{G}_{\theta(x)}=\lambda^{G}(\beta(x)\delta^{G}_{\theta(x)})=(\lambda^{G}\circ\varphi)(\delta_{x}^{F})

for all
x\in F
.

Since the map
\lambda^{F}:M(F)\to VN(F)
is
so
-
w^{*}
-continuous,
\psi\circ\lambda^{F}
is
so\text{-}w^{*}
-continuous;
also,
\varphi:M(F)\to M(G)
is
so
-
w^{*}
-continuous and
\lambda^{G}:M(G)\to VN(G)
is
w^{*}
-continuous,
hence
\lambda^{G}\circ\varphi
is also
so
-
w^{*}
-continuous. But the convex hull of

\{b\delta_{x}^{F}:b\in\mathbb{T},x\in F\}
is
so
-dense in
\mathop{\rm ball}M(F)

[
10
, Lemma 1.1.3]
.
Therefore from the previous equality we obtain

\psi\circ\lambda^{F}=\lambda^{G}\circ\varphi.

∎

4.2.
The general case

In order to deal with a not necessarily unital contractive *-homomorphism, we will need some preparation.

Remark 4.7
.

Let
G_{0}
be a locally compact group and
K\subseteq G_{0}
a normal compact subgroup
with normalized Haar measure
m_{K}
. If
\varepsilon:K\to\mathbb{T}
is a continuous character of

, the measure

\varepsilon m_{K}
defines a contractive idempotent element of

(this follows easily from the definitions).
Hence
P_{0}:=\lambda^{G_{0}}(\varepsilon m_{K})
is an orthogonal projection in

.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
G_{0}
be a locally compact group and
K\subseteq G_{0}
a normal compact subgroup
with normalized Haar measure
m_{K}
. If
\pi:G_{0}\to G_{0}/K
is the quotient map and

denotes the projection
Q_{0}=\lambda^{G_{0}}(m_{K})\in VN(G_{0})

there is a *-isomorphism
j_{0}:VN(G_{0})Q_{0}\to VN(G_{0}/K)

j_{0}(\lambda_{g}^{G_{0}}Q_{0})=\lambda_{\pi(g)}^{G_{0}/K}
.

Proof.

By
[
9
, Proposition 3.25]
the dual of the

is a w*-continuous onto *-homomorphism

\psi(\lambda_{g}^{G_{0}})=\lambda_{\pi(g)}^{G_{0}/K}
for all

.

The kernel of this map is the ideal

where

is the projection onto the Hilbert space

It follows that
\psi
induces an isomorphism from

for all

,

is in fact

.

Indeed, by
[

Let

.

It follows that the map

then, for all

∎

is a closed subset of
\mathbb{T}\times\pi^{-1}(L)

we conclude that
\tilde{\nu}^{-1}(L)
is a compact set.

Define the map

\theta:=\tilde{\nu}\circ\theta^{\prime}:F\rightarrow G_{0}/K\,

and note that clearly

(4.2)

\theta(x)=\pi(g)\iff\varphi(\delta_{x}^{F})=b\delta^{G}_{g}\ast\varepsilon m_{K}

for some
b\in\mathbb{T}
.

Note that
\theta=\tilde{\nu}\circ\theta^{\prime}
is continuous, since by
[
10
, Proposition 4.2.1]

the map
\theta^{\prime}
is continuous.

Lemma 4.11
.

The map
\theta
is proper if and only if
\theta^{\prime}
is proper.

Proof.

If
\theta^{\prime}
is proper, then
\theta
is proper as the composition
\theta=\tilde{\nu}\circ\theta^{\prime}

of proper maps.

Assume that
\theta
is proper. Let
L\subseteq G_{0}^{\prime}/K^{\prime}
be a compact set. Then

\theta^{\prime-1}(L)\subseteq\theta^{\prime-1}(\tilde{\nu}^{-1}(\tilde{\nu}(L))=\theta^{-1}(\tilde{\nu}(L)))
.
Since
\theta
is proper and
\tilde{\nu}
is continuous,
\theta^{-1}(\tilde{\nu}(L))
is compact
and hence its closed subset
\theta^{\prime-1}(L)
is compact.
∎

We will use the following

Notation

P_{0}=\lambda^{G_{0}}(\varepsilon m_{K})\in VN(G_{0})
,
and
Q^{\prime}_{0}=\lambda^{G_{0}^{\prime}}(m_{K^{\prime}})\in VN(G_{0}^{\prime})
.
Note that these are central projections (Remark
4.7
and Lemma
4.10
(i)).

Lemma 4.12
.

There exists a w*-continuous *-homomorphism

(4.3)

\displaystyle j^{\prime}:VN(G_{0}^{\prime})Q^{\prime}_{0}\to

\displaystyle VN(G_{0})P_{0}

\displaystyle\text{such that }\quad\lambda_{(b,g)}^{G_{0}^{\prime}}Q^{\prime}_{0}\mapsto

\displaystyle b\lambda_{g}^{G_{0}}P_{0}\,,\;(b,g)\in G_{0}^{\prime}\,.

Proof.

Let us write
t=(\beta(t),g(t))
for the elements of
G_{0}
.
For
u\in A(G_{0})
define

(4.4)

\sigma(u)(t)=\langle\lambda(\beta(t)\delta_{g(t)}\!*\!\varepsilon m_{K}),u\rangle=\beta(t)\langle\lambda_{g(t)}^{G_{0}}P_{0},u\rangle.

In Lemma
4.9
we have seen that for all
u\in A(G_{0})
the map

G_{0}\to\mathbb{C}:g\mapsto\int_{K}u(gk)\epsilon(k)dk=\langle\lambda_{g}^{G_{0}}P_{0},u\rangle

is in
A(G_{0})
. Hence by
[
9
, Proposition 3.25(i)]
, the map
(b,g)\mapsto\int_{K}u(gk)\varepsilon(k)dk

is in
A(G^{\prime}_{0})
.
The function
\beta
belongs to the Fourier - Stieltjes algebra
B(G^{\prime}_{0})

because
t\to\beta(t)
is a representation;
this shows that
\sigma(u)
is in the Fourier algebra
A(G^{\prime}_{0})
.

Rewriting relation (
4.4
) in the form

\langle\lambda_{(b,g)},\sigma(u)\rangle=\langle b\lambda_{g}^{G_{0}}P_{0},u\rangle

we see that the dual of the map

\sigma:A(G_{0})\rightarrow A(G_{0}^{\prime}):u\rightarrow\sigma(u)

is a continuous map satisfying

\sigma^{*}:VN(G_{0}^{\prime})\to VN(G_{0}):\lambda_{(b,g)}^{G_{0}^{\prime}}\mapsto b\lambda_{g}^{G_{0}}P_{0}.

We claim that

P_{0}=\lambda^{G_{0}}(\varepsilon m_{K})=\sigma^{*}(\lambda^{G_{0}^{\prime}}(m_{K^{\prime}}))=\sigma^{*}(Q_{0}^{\prime}).

Indeed, note that
m_{K^{\prime}}=m_{K}\circ\iota^{-1}
where
\iota:K\to K^{\prime}
is the group homomorphism

g\mapsto(\varepsilon(g),g)
.
Suppose that
m_{K}
is a w*-limit of the form

m_{K}=\lim_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}}a_{i}^{j}\delta_{g_{i}^{j}}^{K}.

Then

\varepsilon m_{K}=\lim_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}}a_{i}^{j}\varepsilon(g_{i}^{j})\delta_{g_{i}^{j}}^{K}\quad\text{and }\quad m_{K^{\prime}}=\lim_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}}a_{i}^{j}\delta^{K^{\prime}}_{(\varepsilon(g_{i}^{j}),g_{i}^{j})}.

Thus

\displaystyle\lambda^{G_{0}}(\varepsilon m_{K})

\displaystyle=\lim_{j}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}}a_{i}^{j}\varepsilon(g_{i}^{j})\lambda_{g_{i}^{j}}^{G_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{j}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}}a_{i}^{j}\varepsilon(g_{i}^{j})\lambda_{g_{i}^{j}}^{G_{0}}\right)\lambda^{G_{0}}(\varepsilon m_{K})\quad\text{since $\lambda^{G_{0}}(\varepsilon m_{K})$ is idempotent}

\displaystyle=\lim_{j}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}}a_{i}^{j}\sigma^{*}(\lambda^{G_{0}^{\prime}}_{(\varepsilon(g_{i}^{j}),g_{i}^{j})})

\displaystyle=\lim_{j}\sigma^{*}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{j}}a_{i}^{j}\lambda^{G_{0}^{\prime}}_{(\varepsilon(g_{i}^{j}),g_{i}^{j})}\right)=\sigma^{*}(\lambda^{G_{0}^{\prime}}(m_{K^{\prime}}))

which proves the Claim.

It follows that the linear map

\displaystyle j^{\prime}:VN(G_{0}^{\prime})Q^{\prime}_{0}\to VN(G_{0})P_{0}:\;\lambda_{(b,g)}^{G_{0}^{\prime}}Q^{\prime}_{0}\mapsto

\displaystyle\sigma^{*}(\lambda_{(b,g)}^{G_{0}^{\prime}})\,,\;(b,g)\in G_{0}^{\prime}\,.

is w*-continuous. That it is a *-homomorphism follows from (
4.3
),
since
Q_{0}^{\prime}
and
P_{0}
are central projections.
∎

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
VN_{G_{0}}(G)=\overline{{\rm span}\{\lambda^{G}_{g}:g\in G_{0}\}}^{w*}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(G))
. The map

\gamma:VN(G_{0})\to VN_{G_{0}}(G):\lambda^{G_{0}}_{g}\mapsto\lambda^{G}_{g}

extends to a w*-continuous *-isomorphism mapping the central
projection
P_{0}=\lambda^{G_{0}}(\varepsilon m_{K})
to the central projection
P=\lambda^{G}(\varepsilon m_{K})\in VN_{G_{0}}(G)
.

Proof.

The first statement is a Corollary of Herz’ restriction Theorem

[
16
, Proposition 2.6.6]
.

The fact that
P
and
P_{0}
are central projections follows from Lemma
4.10
(i).

The image of
P_{0}=\lambda^{G_{0}}(\varepsilon m_{K})
under this map is clearly

P=\lambda^{G}(\varepsilon m_{k})
.

Indeed, since
K\subseteq G_{0}
, the measure
\varepsilon m_{K}
is supported in
G_{0}
, so

P=\int\lambda^{G}_{g}d(\varepsilon m_{K})(g)
is in
VN_{G_{0}}(G)
and hence

\gamma(P_{0})=\int\gamma(\lambda^{G_{0}}_{g})d(\varepsilon m_{K})(g)=\int\lambda^{G}_{g}d(\varepsilon m_{K})(g)=P\,.

∎

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
F
and
G
be locally compact groups and

\varphi:M(F)\to M(G)

be a contractive
so
-
w^{*}
-continuous
homomorphism.
The following are equivalent:

(i) The map
\varphi
has the extension property.

(ii) The homomorphism
\theta:F\to G_{0}/K
(see (
4.2
)) is a proper map.

(iii) The map
\varphi
is w*-continuous.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(iii)

If
\varphi
has the extension property, there exists a
w^{*}
-continuous homomorphism

\psi:VN(F)\to VN(G)

satisfying

\lambda^{G}\circ\varphi=\psi\circ\lambda^{F}.

To show that
\varphi
is w*-continuous, it suffices (by the Krein-Smulian Theorem)
to prove that if
(\mu_{i})\subseteq{\rm ball}(M(F))
is a net with
w*-
\lim_{i}\mu_{i}=0
then w*-
\lim_{i}\varphi(\mu_{i})=0.

Since
\lambda^{G}\circ\varphi:M(F)\to VN(G)
is w*-w*-continuous, we have that
\lim_{i}\lambda^{G}\circ\varphi(\mu_{i})=0

in the w*-topology of
VN(G)
, i.e. that
\langle\lambda^{G}\circ\varphi(\mu_{i}),f\rangle\to 0
for all
f\in A(G)
.
By Lemma
4.1
, this means that

\int_{G}fd\varphi(\mu_{i})\to 0,\,\,\forall\,f\in A(G).

Since the net
(\varphi(\mu_{i}))
is bounded and
A(G)
is uniformly dense in
C_{0}(G)
,
it follows that

\int_{G}fd\varphi(\mu_{i})\to 0,\,\,\forall\,f\in C_{0}(G).

Thus w*-
\lim_{i}\varphi(\mu_{i})=0.

(iii)\Rightarrow(ii)

Assume that
\varphi
is
w^{*}
continuous. Consider the *-homomorphism

\psi_{0}:=\lambda^{G}\circ\varphi:M(F)\to VN(G).

By Proposition
4.2
, this map is w*-continuous. By (
4.1
), for all
x\in F
we have

\psi_{0}(\delta^{F}_{x})=\lambda^{G}(b\delta_{g}*\varepsilon m_{k})=b\lambda_{g}^{G}P\quad\text{when }\;\pi^{\prime}(b,g)=\theta^{\prime}(x)

(recall that
P=\lambda^{G}(\varepsilon m_{K})
).

Note that
\psi_{0}
takes values of the form
b\lambda^{G}_{g}P
where
g\in G_{0}
, hence

\psi_{0}(M(F))\subseteq VN_{G_{0}}(G)P\subseteq VN_{G_{0}}(G).

By Lemma
4.13
there is a w*-continuous isomorphism
\gamma^{-1}:VN_{G_{0}}(G)\to VN(G_{0})
such that

\displaystyle\gamma^{-1}(\lambda_{g}^{G}P)=\lambda_{g}^{G_{0}}P_{0}

(where
P_{0}=\lambda^{G_{0}}(\varepsilon m_{K})
).
Thus the composition

\zeta:=\gamma^{-1}\circ\psi_{0}:M(F)\to VN(G_{0})P_{0}

is a well-defined w*-continuous *-homomorphism satisfying

\zeta(\delta_{x}^{F})=b\lambda_{g}^{G_{0}}P_{0}\;\;\text{when }\;\pi^{\prime}(b,g)=\theta^{\prime}(x).

Recall (Lemma
4.9
) that the predual of
VN(G_{0})P_{0}
is
A_{\varepsilon}
. The predual of
\zeta
is
the map
\zeta_{*}:A_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow C_{0}(F)
which satisfies, for all
u\in A_{\varepsilon}
and
x\in F
,

\zeta_{*}(u)(x)=\langle\zeta(\delta_{x}^{F}),u\rangle=\langle b\lambda_{g}^{G_{0}}P_{0},u\rangle\quad\text{when }\;\pi^{\prime}(a,g)=\theta^{\prime}(x).

But again by Lemma
4.9
we have

\langle\lambda_{g}^{G_{0}}P_{0},u\rangle=u(g)

for
g\in G_{0}
; thus for all
u\in A_{\varepsilon}
and
x\in F
,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\zeta_{*}(u)(x)=bu(g)\;\text{ when }\;\pi^{\prime}(b,g)=\theta^{\prime}(x).

Now suppose, by way of contradiction, that
\theta
is not proper. Then there is a compact set

\tilde{L}\subset G_{0}/K
such that
\theta^{-1}(\tilde{L})
is not compact. Let
L=\pi^{-1}(\tilde{L}).

Observe that
L
is compact in
G_{0}.

Fix a nonzero
u\in A_{\varepsilon}
and
g_{0}\in G_{0}
such that
|u(g_{0})|=2\delta>0
.
For each
h\in G_{0}
the function
u_{h}(s):=\lambda(hg_{0}^{-1})u(s)
is in
A_{\varepsilon}

and
|u_{h}(h)|=|\lambda(hg_{0}^{-1})u(h)|=|u(g_{0})|=2\delta
. Hence there is an open set
V_{h}
containing
h

such that
|u_{h}(k)|\geq\delta>0
for each
k\in V_{h}
.
Since
\{V_{h}:h\in L\}
is an open cover of the compact set
L
, taking a finite subcover we obtain
a finite number of functions

u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in A_{\varepsilon}
such that if

L_{i}=\{g\in L:|u_{i}(g)|\geq\delta\}

then
L=\cup_{i=1}^{n}L_{i}.

If
x\in\theta^{-1}(\tilde{L})
then
\theta(x)\in\tilde{L}
and thus
\pi^{-1}(\{\theta(x)\})\subseteq L
.
Therefore there exists
g\in L_{i}
such that
\pi(g)=\theta(x)
,
hence
\pi^{\prime}(b,g)=\theta^{\prime}(x)
for some
b\in\mathbb{T}
.
Thus, by (
4.5
),

|\zeta_{*}(u_{i})(x)|\geq\delta.

Hence if we let

M_{i}=\{x\in\theta^{-1}(\tilde{L}):|\zeta_{*}(u_{i})(x)|\geq\delta\},\quad 1\leq i\leq n

we have

\theta^{-1}(\tilde{L})=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}\,.

Since
\theta^{-1}(\tilde{L})
is not compact, some set
M_{i}
is not compact.
But

|\zeta_{*}(u_{i})(x)|\geq\delta
for all
x\in M_{i}
, which contradicts the fact that
\zeta_{*}(u_{i})\in C_{0}(F)
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)

Assume that
\theta
is a proper homomorphism. Then
\theta^{\prime}
is proper by Lemma
4.11
.
By Proposition
4.3
, the map
u\mapsto u\circ\theta^{\prime}
sends
A(G_{0}^{\prime}/K^{\prime})
to
A(F)
and so
its dual is a
w^{*}
-continuous homomorphism

\psi_{1}:VN(F)\rightarrow VN(G_{0}^{\prime}/K^{\prime})
sending

\lambda_{x}^{F}
to
\lambda_{\theta^{\prime}(x)}^{G_{0}^{\prime}/K^{\prime}}.

Recall (Lemma
4.8
) that
VN(G_{0}^{\prime}/K^{\prime})
is isomorphic to
VN(G^{\prime}_{0})\lambda^{G_{0}^{\prime}}(m_{K^{\prime}}).

Thus we may assume that

\psi_{1}:VN(F)\to VN(G^{\prime}_{0})\lambda^{G^{\prime}_{0}}(m_{K^{\prime}})

sends

\lambda_{x}^{F}
to
\lambda^{G_{0}^{\prime}}_{(b,g)}\lambda^{G_{0}^{\prime}}(m_{K^{\prime}})
when
\pi^{\prime}(b,g)=\theta^{\prime}(x)
.
We also recall the
w^{*}
continuous homomorphism (
4.3
)

\displaystyle j^{\prime}:VN(G_{0}^{\prime})\lambda^{G_{0}^{\prime}}(m_{K^{\prime}})\to

\displaystyle VN(G_{0})\lambda^{G_{0}}(\varepsilon m_{K})=VN(G_{0})P_{0}

\displaystyle\lambda_{(b,g)}^{G_{0}^{\prime}}\lambda^{G_{0}^{\prime}}(m_{K^{\prime}})\mapsto

\displaystyle b\lambda_{g}^{G_{0}}P_{0}\,.

Composing with the w*-continuous *-isomorphism

\gamma:VN(G_{0})\to VN_{G_{0}}(G):\lambda_{g}^{G_{0}}\mapsto\lambda_{g}^{G}
(which sends
P_{0}
to
P
)
we have a *-homomorphism

\displaystyle\gamma\circ j^{\prime}:VN(G_{0}^{\prime})\lambda^{G_{0}^{\prime}}(m_{K^{\prime}})\to

\displaystyle VN(G)P

\displaystyle\lambda_{(b,g)}^{G_{0}^{\prime}}\lambda^{G_{0}^{\prime}}(m_{K^{\prime}}))\mapsto

\displaystyle b\lambda_{g}^{G}P\,.

Finally, composing with
\psi_{1}
, we obtain a w*-continuous *-homomorphism

\psi:=\gamma\circ j^{\prime}\circ\psi_{1}:VN(F)\rightarrow VN(G)

sending
\lambda_{x}^{F}
to
b\lambda^{G}_{g}P

when
\varphi(\delta_{x}^{F})=b\delta_{g}^{G_{0}}*\varepsilon m_{K}
.

From the construction of
\psi
we have

(\lambda^{G}\circ\varphi)(\delta_{x}^{F})=b\lambda_{g}^{G}\lambda^{G}(\varepsilon m_{K})=\psi(\lambda^{F}_{x})=(\psi\circ\lambda^{F})(\delta^{F}_{x})

for all
x\in F
. Now the same density argument as in the conclusion of the proof of Theorem
4.6
shows that

(\lambda^{G}\circ\varphi)(\mu)=(\psi\circ\lambda^{F})(\mu)

for all
\mu\in M(F)
,
which means that
\varphi
has the extension property.
∎

Remark 4.15
.

The main focus in this Section is the equivalence of the extension property with the properness of the map

\theta
. Note that the equivalence
(ii)\iff(iii)
is also proved in
[
21
, Proposition 3.4]
.
Our approach is different, as it
relies on operator algebra methods and exploits the duality between
the Fourier algebra and the von Neumann algebra of a group; it
highlights the use of the extension property.

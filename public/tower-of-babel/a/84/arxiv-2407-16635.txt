Abstract
. We prove the Mond conjecture for wave fronts which states that the number of parameters of a frontal versal unfolding is less than or equal to the number of spheres in the image of a stable frontal deformation with equality if the wave front is weighted homogeneous. We give two different proofs. The first one depends on the fact that wave fronts are related to discriminants of map germs and we then use the analogous result proved by Damon and Mond in this context. The second one is based on ideas by Fernández de Bobadilla, Nuño-Ballesteros and Peñafort Sanchis and by Nuño-Ballesteros and Fernández-Hernández. The advantage of the second approach is that most results are valid for any frontal, not only wave fronts, and thus give important tools which may be useful to prove the conjecture for frontals in general.

1.
Introduction

The Mond conjecture is an inequality of type
\mu\geq\tau
, but in the context of the Thom-Mather theory of singularities of mappings. It was stated by D. Mond in
[
17
]
and it says that given a holomorphic map germ
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
, with
S\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
a finite set, with isolated instability and such that
(n,n+1)
are in the range of nice dimensions of Mather
[
14
]
, then

(1)

\codim_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)\leq\mu_{I}(f),

with equality if
f
is weighted homogeneous.

The two invariants of the inequality appear in the scope of
\mathscr{A}
-equivalence of mappings, where we allow holomorphic coordinate changes in the source and target. On one hand, the
\mathscr{A}_{e}
-codimension, denoted by
\codim_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)
,
was introduced by Mather and it measures the minimal number of parameters necessary to have a versal unfolding of
f
. So, it plays the role of the Tjurina number
\tau
in the context of deformations of isolated hypersurface singularities (IHS)
(X,0)\subset(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
.

On the other hand,
\mu_{I}(f)
is called the image Milnor number and was introduced by Mond in
[
17
]
in analogy with the classical Milnor number
\mu
of an IHS. When
(n,n+1)
are nice dimensions,
f
always admits a stabilisation, that is, a 1-parameter unfolding
F=(f_{t},t)
with the property that
f_{0}=f
and
f_{t}
has only stable singularities for
t\neq 0
. If we denote by
X_{t}
the image of
f_{t}
and by
B_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
the closed ball of radius
\varepsilon>0
, Mond showed that for
0\ll\delta\ll\epsilon\ll 1
and
0<|t|<\delta
, the space
X_{t}\cap B_{\epsilon}
has the homotopy type of a wedge of
n
-spheres, and the number of such spheres,
\mu_{I}(f)
, only depends on
f
.

Although the classical
\mu\geq\tau
inequality for IHS is more or less obvious, the Mond conjecture is known to be true only for plane curves and surfaces (see
[
17
,
18
]
), but it remains open for dimensions
n\geq 3
. We refer to the book
[
19
]
or the recent survey
[
23
]
for more details.

We also remark that there is another version of the inequality for singularities of mappings
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{p},0)
, with
n\geq p
, as follows:

(2)

\codim_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)\leq\mu_{\Delta}(f),

with equality if
f
is weighted homogeneous. Here,
\mu_{\Delta}(f)
is the discriminant Milnor number, defined in an analogous way to the image Milnor number, but taking the discriminant
\Delta(f_{t})
(i.e., the image of the critical locus) instead of the image of a stabilisation
f_{t}
. This inequality is in fact a theorem that was proved by Damon and Mond in
[
4
]
.

In this paper, we consider an inequality of type
\mu\geq\tau
but in the context of deformations of singularities of frontals. We have developed in the last years this theory (see
[
21
,
22
]
), where we consider the subclass of frontal map germs
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
. Roughly speaking,
f
is a frontal if its image in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
has a well defined tangent hyperplane at each point
x
in a neighbourhood of
S
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and hence,
f
admits a smooth Nash lift
\tilde{f}
to the projectivised cotangent bundle
PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
. The notion of frontal appeared for the first time in the real smooth case in the works of Fujimori, Saji, Umehara and Yamada
[
7
]
as a generalisation of a wave front (in the terminology of the Russian school of singularities
[
28
]
), in the particular case that the Nash lift
\tilde{f}
is an immersion.

The frontal structure is preserved by
\mathscr{A}
-equivalence, so we can consider the induced equivalence for frontals. However, an unfolding of a frontal is not a frontal in general, hence we have to consider only frontal deformations which preserve such structure. In this setting, we also have a well stablished notion of frontal
\mathscr{F}_{e}
-codimension, denoted by
\codim_{\mathscr{F}_{e}}(f)
, which gives the minimal number of parameters necessary for a versal frontal unfolding.
Analogously, if
f
has isolated frontal instability and
(n,n+1)
are nice dimensions in the frontal sense, then
f
admits a frontal stabilisation
F=(f_{t},t)
and following the arguments of Mond, we can show that
X_{t}\cap B_{\epsilon}
has the homotopy type of a wedge of
n
-spheres, where
X_{t}
is the image of
f_{t}
,
0\ll\delta\ll\epsilon\ll 1
and
0<|t|<\delta
. We call the number of such spheres the frontal Milnor number
\mu_{\mathscr{F}}(f)
.

.

It is now natural to ask for a frontal version of the Mond conjecture as follows: let
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
be a frontal with isolated frontal instability and such that
(n,n+1)
are frontal nice dimensions, then

(3)

\codim_{\mathscr{F}_{e}}(f)\leq\mu_{\mathscr{F}}(f),

with equality if
f
is weighted homogeneous. The frontal Mond conjecture (
3
) has been already proved for plane curves (i.e.,
n=1
) in
[
22
]
. It follows from the original Mond conjecture (
1
) and the fact that
\codim_{\mathscr{F}_{e}}(f)=\codim_{\mathscr{A}_{e}}(f)-\mult(f)+1
and also
\mu_{\mathscr{F}}(f)=\mu_{I}(f)-\mult(f)+1
, where
\mult(f)
is the multiplicity of the plane curve.

In this paper, we follow the ideas of
[
5
]
and define a Jacobian module
M_{\mathscr{F}}(g)
, where
g\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
is a reduced equation of the image of
f
, with the property that

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M_{\mathscr{F}}(g)\geq\codim_{\mathscr{F}_{e}}(f),

with equality if
f
is weighted homogeneous. Hence, the frontal Mond conjecture (
3
) follows if we could show that
\mu_{\mathscr{F}}(f)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M_{\mathscr{F}}(g)
. In fact, we also define a relative version of the Jacobian module,
M_{y}(G)
, where
G
a reduced equation of the image of an
r
-parameter frontal unfolding
F
, such that
M_{y}(G)\otimes\mathscr{O}_{r}\cong M_{\mathscr{F}}(g)
. Furthermore, if
F
is frontal stable, then

\mu_{\mathscr{F}}(f)=e(\mathfrak{m}_{r};M_{y}(G)),

the Samuel multiplicity of
M_{y}(G)
over the parameter ring
\mathscr{O}_{r}
. As a consequence, when
M_{y}(G)
is Cohen-Macaulay of dimension
r
we get the desired equality
\mu_{\mathscr{F}}(f)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M_{\mathscr{F}}(g)
and hence, the frontal Mond conjecture (
3
). In the last part of the paper, we show the frontal Mond conjecture (
3
) for wave fronts. Our proof follows similar arguments to the proof of the Damon-Mond inequality (
2
) for the discriminant Milnor number in
[
4
]
.

2.
Frontal map germs

2.1.
Deformations of frontal map germs in corank
1

Let
PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
be the projectivized cotangent bundle of
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
.
If
(z,[\omega])\in PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
, we equip
PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
with the contact structure given by the differential form

\alpha=\omega_{1}\,dz^{1}+\dots+\omega_{n+1}\,dz^{n+1}.

We consider the canonical projection
\pi\colon PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
given by
\pi(z,[\omega])=z
, whose fibres are Legendrian submanifolds of
PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
under this contact structure (i.e.
\ker d\pi_{(z,[\omega])}\subseteq\ker\alpha_{(z,[\omega])}
for all
(z,[\omega])\in PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
).
A holomorphic map
F\colon N\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\to PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is
integral
if
F^{*}\alpha=0
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
N\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be an open subset.
A holomorphic map
f\colon N\to\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is
frontal
if there exists an integral map
F\colon N\to PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
such that

f=\pi\circ F.

If
F
is an immersion, we say
f
is a
Legendrian map
or
wave front
.
Similarly, a hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is
frontal
(resp. a
wave front
) if there exists a frontal map (resp. wave front)
f\colon N\to\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
such that
X=f(N)
.

If
F\colon N\to PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is an integral map and
f=\pi\circ F
,

0=F^{*}\alpha=\sum^{n+1}_{i=1}\nu_{i}d(Z_{i}\circ F)=\sum^{n+1}_{i=1}\sum^{n}_{j=1}\nu_{i}\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\,dx^{j}

for some
\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{n+1}\in\mathscr{O}_{n}
, not all of them zero.
This is the same as claiming that there exists a nowhere-vanishing differential
1
-form
\nu
on
f(N)
such that
\nu(df\circ\xi)=0
for all vector fields
\xi
on
N
.

Since
PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is a fibre bundle, we can find for each
(z,[\omega])\in PT^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
an open neighbourhood
Z\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
of
z
and an open
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}P^{n}
such that
\pi^{-1}(Z)\cong Z\times U
.
Therefore,
F
is contact equivalent to the integral mapping
\tilde{f}(x)=(f(x),[\nu_{x}])
, known as the
Nash lift
of
f
.
In particular, if
\Sigma(f)
is nowhere dense in
N
,
\nu
can be uniquely extended from
N\backslash\Sigma(f)
to
N
, and there is a one-to-one correspondence between
f
and
\tilde{f}
.
Such a frontal map is known as a
proper frontal
map
[
9
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a finite set.
A holomorphic multigerm
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
is
frontal
if it has a frontal representative
f\colon N\to Z
.
Given a hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
,
(X,0)
is a
frontal
hypersurface germ if there exists a finite frontal map germ
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
such that
(X,0)=f(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)
.

Similarly, we shall say that
f
is a proper frontal multigerm if it has a representative which is a proper frontal map.

Definition 2.3
.

A frontal map germ
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
is a
germ of wave fronts
if
\tilde{f}
is an immersion.
We also call the image of a germ of wave fronts a
wave front
.

Other authors such as
[
28
]
refer to

as a
Legendrian map germ
, and its image as a
front
.

Given a smooth map germ
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
, we define the

of

as the ideal

\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}&\dots&\dfrac{\partial f_{n+1}}{\partial x_{1}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\dfrac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{n}}&\dots&\dfrac{\partial f_{n+1}}{\partial x_{n}}\end{pmatrix}

Proposition 2.4

(
[
9
]
)

Let

Then

2.2.
Frontal Milnor number

We define the frontal Milnor number, by using analogous arguments to those of Mond in
[
17
]
. Let
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
be a frontal map germ.

Definition 2.10
.

A
frontal stabilisation
of
f
is a
1
-parameter frontal unfolding
F=(t,f_{t})
of
f
such that
f_{t}
has only stable frontal singularities, for all
t\neq 0
in a neighbourhood of the origin in
\mathbb{C}
.

Definition 2.11
.

We say that
(n,n+1)
are
frontal nice dimensions
if

(1)

Any proper frontal map germ
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
with isolated instabillity admits a frontal stabilisation.

(2)

There exist only a finite number of
\mathscr{A}
-equivalence classes of frontal stable map germs
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
.

In the real
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
case, the condition (1) is equivalent to saying that the stable frontal map germs are dense within the space of proper frontal map germs
(\mathbb{R}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},0)
and the condition (2) follows from (1).
This notion is the equivalent within the framework of frontal map germs to Mather’s nice dimensions (
[
15
]
; see also
[
19
]
, §5.2).

From now on, we assume that
(n,n+1)
are frontal nice dimensions and that
f
has isolated frontal instability. Let
F=(t,f_{t})
be a stabilisation and denote by
(\mathcal{X},0)
the image of
F
and by
\pi\colon(\mathcal{X},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
the projection
\pi(t,y)=t
. For each
t
in a neighbourhood of
0
in
\mathbb{C}
, the fibre
X_{t}=\pi^{-1}(t)
is the image of
f_{t}
. We fix in
(\mathcal{X},0)
the stratification by frontal stable types, which is well defined by condition (2) above.
By a theorem due to Lê
[
10
]
, for all
0<\delta\ll\epsilon\ll 1
, the restriction

\pi\colon\mathcal{X}\cap(D_{\delta}^{*}\times B_{\epsilon})\longrightarrow D_{\delta}^{*}

is a locally
\mathscr{C}^{0}
-trivial fibration. The fibre,
X_{t}\cap B_{\epsilon}
, with
0<|t|<\delta
, is called the
frontal disentanglement
. Here,

B_{\epsilon}=\{y\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\ |\ \|y\|\leq\epsilon\},\quad D_{\delta}^{*}=\{t\in\mathbb{C}\ |\ 0<|t|<\delta\}.

Lemma 2.12
.

With the above notation,
\pi\colon(\mathcal{X},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
has isolated critical point in the stratified sense. In particular, the frontal disentanglement has the homotopy type of a wedge of
n
-spheres.

Proof.

The fact that the fibre
X_{t}\cap B_{\epsilon}
, with
0<|t|<\delta
, has the homotopy type of a wedge of
n
-spheres, follows from a result of Hamm and Lê
[
8
]
, once we know that
(\mathcal{X},0)
is a hypersurface and
\pi\colon(\mathcal{X},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
has isolated critical point in the stratified sense.

In order to prove that
\pi\colon(\mathcal{X},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
has isolated critical point we fix a representative
\mathcal{X}
such that if
t\neq 0
,
f_{t}
has only frontal stable singularities on
X_{t}
and
f_{0}=f
has only frontal stable singularities on
X_{0}\setminus\{0\}
. Thus, for all
(t,y)\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{0\}
,
f_{t}
is frontal stable at
y\in X_{t}
. Hence,
F
is a trivial unfolding at
(t,y)
and there exists a germ of diffeomorphism
\Psi=(t,\psi_{t})
which sends
\mathcal{X}
into
\mathbb{C}\times X_{0}
. Since
\Psi
must preserve the strata, we conclude that
\pi
is a stratified submersion at
(t,y)
.

.

The number of spheres of the frontal disentanglement is called the

The frontal Milnor number of

Theorem 2.14

.

With the above notation, suppose that
G\in\mathscr{O}_{n+2}
is a reduced equation of
(\mathcal{X},0)
. Then, the number of spheres of the fibre
X_{t}\cap B_{\epsilon}
, with
0<|t|<\delta
, is equal to

\sum_{y\in B_{\varepsilon}\backslash X_{t}}\mu(g_{t};y),

where
g_{t}(y)=G(t,y)
and
\mu(g_{t};y)
is the classical Milnor number of
g_{t}
at
y
.

The notion of frontal Milnor number was first introduced in
[
21
]
, wherein we formulate a frontal version of the conjecture stated by D. Mond in
[
17
]
:

Conjecture 2.15
.

Let
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
be an
\mathscr{F}
-finite frontal map germ.
If
(n,n+1)
is in the frontal nice dimensions,

\mu_{\mathscr{F}}(f)\geq\codim_{\mathscr{F}_{e}}f,

with equality if
f
is quasihomogeneous.

Conjecture
2.15
has so far been proven for
n=1
in
[
22
]
, where it can be derived from an expression for the
\mathscr{F}_{e}
-codimension of a plane curve.
Our aim in this article is to prove Conjecture
2.15
for all wave fronts within the range of the nice dimensions.

Remark 2.16
.

Assume that the map germ
f
in Conjecture
2.15
is a proper germ of wave front.
A
k
-parameter unfolding
F=(f_{u},u)
of
f
is frontal if and only if
f_{t}
lifts into an integral deformation of
\tilde{f}
for all
t
in an open neighbourhood
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{k}
of
0
(
[
22
]
, Theorem 3.8).
The frontal map germ
f
is a germ of wave front if and only if
\tilde{f}
is an immersion, meaning that
\widetilde{f_{t}}
is also an immersion and
f_{t}
is a wave front for all
t\in U
.
It follows that
\Delta_{\mathscr{F}}(f)
is a wave front.

2.3.
Generating families

In their theory of deformations of wave fronts, Arnold and his colleagues proved that every germ of wave front
(X,0)
,
X\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
can be generated in terms of a smooth family of function germs

g\colon(\mathbb{C}^{k}\times\mathbb{C}^{r},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)

with smooth critical set (see
[
28
]
, Chapter 19), which they called
Morse families
of functions.
If one then considers the unfolding
G\colon(\mathbb{C}^{k}\times\mathbb{C}^{r},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{k}\times\mathbb{C},0)
associated to this smooth family,

G(u,x)=(u,g(u,x)),

then
(X,0)
is equal to the germ at
0
of the discriminant of
G
.

The original proof by Arnold and his colleagues is based on the fact that there is a correspondence between germs of caustics in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and germs of wave fronts in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
, sometimes called the
symplectification functor
.
We now wish to give an alternative proof of this fact which does not require prior knowledge of symplectic topology and outlines a method to compute
G
using a normalisation of
(X,0)
.

Lemma 2.17

(Rank Distribution)
.

Let
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
be a germ of wave front of corank
k>0
in the form

f(x,y)=(x,p_{1}(x,y),\dots,p_{k}(x,y),q(x,y)),

with
x\in\mathbb{C}^{n-k}
and
y\in\mathbb{C}^{k}
.
If we denote the Nash lift of
f
as

\tilde{f}(x,y)=(f(x,y),\lambda_{1}(x,y),\dots,\lambda_{n-k}(x,y),\mu_{1}(x,y),\dots,\mu_{k}(x,y)),

then the matrix

\frac{\partial\mu}{\partial y}=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\partial\mu_{1}}{\partial y_{1}}&\dots&\dfrac{\partial\mu_{1}}{\partial y_{k}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\dfrac{\partial\mu_{k}}{\partial y_{1}}&\dots&\dfrac{\partial\mu_{k}}{\partial y_{k}}\end{pmatrix}

is invertible in a neighbourhood
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
of
0
.

Proof.

The mapping
f
verifies the identity

dq=\lambda_{1}\,dx_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n-k}\,dx_{n-k}+\mu_{1}\,dp_{1}+\dots+\mu_{k}\,dp_{k},

from which follows that

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}=\frac{\partial q}{\partial x_{i}}-\mu_{1}\frac{\partial p_{1}}{\partial x_{i}}-\dots-\mu_{k}\frac{\partial p_{1}}{\partial x_{k}};

\displaystyle\frac{\partial q}{\partial y_{\ell}}=\mu_{1}\frac{\partial p_{1}}{\partial y_{\ell}}+\dots+\mu_{k}\frac{\partial p_{k}}{\partial y_{\ell}}.

for
i=1,\dots,n-k
and
\ell=1,\dots,k
.
Taking partial derivatives on both expressions yields

(5)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\lambda_{i}}{\partial y_{\ell}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}q}{\partial y_{\ell}\partial x_{i}}-\sum^{k}_{j=1}\left(\frac{\partial\mu_{j}}{\partial y_{\ell}}\frac{\partial p_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}+\mu_{j}\frac{\partial^{2}p_{j}}{\partial y_{\ell}\partial x_{i}}\right);

(6)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}q}{\partial x_{i}\partial y_{\ell}}=

\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{j=1}\left(\frac{\partial\mu_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial p_{j}}{\partial y_{\ell}}+\mu_{j}\frac{\partial^{2}p_{j}}{\partial x_{i}\partial y_{\ell}}\right).

Combining (
5
) and (
6
), we deduce that

(7)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\lambda_{i}}{\partial y_{\ell}}=

\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{j=1}\left(\frac{\partial\mu_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial p_{j}}{\partial y_{\ell}}-\frac{\partial\mu_{j}}{\partial y_{\ell}}\frac{\partial p_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}\right).

Since
f
has corank
k>0
, we can choose coordinates in the source and target such that the
1
-jets of the functions
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}
vanish at the origin; this is, that

\frac{\partial p_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(0,0)=\frac{\partial p_{j}}{\partial y_{\ell}}(0,0)=0

for
j,\ell=1,\dots,k
and
i=1,\dots,n-k
.
Therefore, it follows from (
7
) that the functions
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n-k}
have no linear term in
y
.

Since
\tilde{f}
is an immersion and
f
has corank
k
, it follows that there exist
\mathbf{A}\in M_{k\times 1}(\mathscr{O}_{n-k})
,
\mathbf{B}\in M_{k\times k}(\mathscr{O}_{n})
such that
\mathbf{B}(0,0)\in\mathrm{GL}_{k}(\mathbb{C})
and

\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{1}(x,y)\\
\vdots\\
\mu_{k}(x,y)\end{pmatrix}=\mathbf{A}(x)+\mathbf{B}(x,y)\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}\\
\vdots\\
y_{k}\end{pmatrix}

In particular, there is a
\mathbf{C}\in M_{k\times k}(\mathscr{O}_{n})
such that

\frac{\partial\mu}{\partial y}(x,y)=\mathbf{B}(x,y)+\mathbf{C}(x,y)\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}\\
\vdots\\
y_{k}\end{pmatrix}\implies\det\left(\frac{\partial\mu}{\partial y}\right)(0,0)=\det(\mathbf{B})(0,0)\neq 0.

Therefore, there is a neighbourhood
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
of
(0,0)
such that
\det(\partial\mu/\partial y)(x,y)\neq 0
for all
(x,y)\in U
.
∎

Theorem 2.18
.

Let
(X,0)
be a singular germ of wave front,
X\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
.
There exists a smooth map germ
h\colon(\mathbb{C}^{N},T)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
,
N\geq n+1
, verifying the following conditions:

(1)

the set of critical points of
h
is smooth;

(2)

the discriminant of
h
is equal to
(X,0)
.

We call
h
the
generating family
of
(X,0)
.

This result can be extended to regular germs
(X,0)
by taking a parametrisation
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
.
This is done under the convention that the discriminant of a map germ
h\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{p},0)
with
n<p
is the image of
h
.

Proof of Theorem
2.18
.

Let
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
be a normalisation of
(X,0)
with corank
k>0
.
We can choose coordinates in the source and target such that

f(x,y)=(x,p_{1}(x,y),\dots,p_{k}(x,y),q(x,y)),

with
x\in\mathbb{C}^{n-k}
and
y\in\mathbb{C}^{k}
.
We can further assume (by reordering the coordinates in the target if necessary) that

(8)

dq=\lambda_{1}\,dx_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n-k}\,dx_{n-k}+\mu_{1}\,dp_{1}+\dots+\mu_{k}\,dp_{k}

for some
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n-k},\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{k}\in\mathscr{O}_{n}
.
By the Rank Distribution Lemma
2.17
, the matrix
\partial\mu/\partial y
is invertible in an open neighbourhood
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{k}\times V
of
S
.

Let
f\colon V\to\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
be a representative of
f
.
Consider the mapping
h\colon\mathbb{C}^{k}\times V\to\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
given by

h(u,x,y)=\left(x,u,q(x,y)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}(x,y)(u_{i}-p_{i}(x,y))\right).

Using Equation (
8
), the critical set of
h
is given by the equations

0=\frac{\partial q}{\partial y_{j}}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\partial\mu_{i}}{\partial y_{j}}(u_{i}-p_{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}\frac{\partial p_{i}}{\partial y_{j}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\partial\mu_{i}}{\partial y_{j}}(u_{i}-p_{i})

for
j=1,\dots,k
.
This is, we have the system of equations

\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\partial\mu_{1}}{\partial y_{1}}&\dots&\dfrac{\partial\mu_{1}}{\partial y_{k}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\dfrac{\partial\mu_{k}}{\partial y_{1}}&\dots&\dfrac{\partial\mu_{k}}{\partial y_{k}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}-p_{1}\\
\vdots\\
u_{k}-p_{k}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{\partial\mu}{\partial y}\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}-p_{1}\\
\vdots\\
u_{k}-p_{k}\end{pmatrix}.

The matrix
\partial\mu/\partial y
is invertible on
U
, meaning that
u_{j}=p_{j}(x,y)
for
j=1,\dots,k
.
This defines a graph-type submanifold of dimension
k
on
U
, so the set of critical points of
h|_{U}
is smooth.
Moreover, we have that

h(p(x,y),x,y)=(x,p(x,y),q(x,y))=f(x,y),

so the discriminant of
h
on
U
is equal to
X\cap h(U)
.
∎

Note that the generating family
h\colon(\mathbb{C}^{k}\times\mathbb{C}^{n},\{0\}\times S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
constructed in the proof of Theorem
2.18
has a representative in the form

h(u,x,y)=(x,u,h^{\prime}(u,x,y))

for some
h^{\prime}\in\mathscr{O}_{n+k}
.
In Arnold’s terminology, the generating family
h
solely consists of the function
h^{\prime}
, with
y\in\mathbb{C}^{k}
as the variable and
x\in\mathbb{C}^{n-k}
and
u\in\mathbb{C}^{k}
as parameters.
Using Arnold’s definition of the term
generating family
, he showed the following:

Theorem 2.19

(
[
28
]
, §21.4)
.

A germ of wave front
(X,0)
with generating family
h^{\prime}\in\mathscr{O}_{N}
is stable if and only if
h^{\prime}
is
\mathscr{V}
-versal.

Theorem
2.19
can be phrased in our language as follows:
a germ of wave front
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
with generating family
h\colon(\mathbb{C}^{k}\times\mathbb{C}^{n},\{0\}\times S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
is
\mathscr{F}
-stable if and only if
h
is
\mathscr{A}
-stable.

Remark 2.20
.

Let
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
be a
\mathscr{F}
-stable germ of corank
1
wave fronts.
By Theorem
2.18
, the image of
f
is the discriminant of a corank
1
smooth map germ
h\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
.
Since
f
is
\mathscr{F}
-stable,
h
is
\mathscr{A}
-stable by Theorem
2.19
, which implies that
h
is an
A_{k}
singularity with
k\leq n+1
(following the notation from Arnold’s ADE classification
[
28
]
).
A simple computation shows that the discriminant of the
A_{k}
singularity is quasihomogeneous, so
f
is a quasihomogeneous map germ.

Using Theorem
2.18
, we can relate the frontal Milnor number of
f
with the discriminant Milnor number of a generating family of
f
.
The discriminant Milnor number is an analytic invariant first described by Damon and Mond in
[
4
]
, which counts the number of spheres emerging in the discriminant of a stable deformation of a smooth map germ
(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{p},0)
with
n\geq p
.

Corollary 2.21
.

Let
f\colon(\mathbb{C}^{n},S)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
be an
\mathscr{F}
-finite, proper germ of wave fronts.
Let
h\colon(\mathbb{C}^{N},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},0)
be a generating family for the image of
f
.
Then
f
admits a frontal stabilisation if and only if
h
admits a stabilisation.
Moreover, when both integers are defined, we have the identity

\mu_{\mathscr{F}}(f)=\mu_{\Delta}(h),

where
\mu_{\Delta}(h)
denotes the discriminant Milnor number of
h
.

Proof.

First assume that
f
admits an
\mathscr{F}
-stabilisation
f_{t}
, with
t\in\mathbb{D}
.
Let
t\in\mathbb{D}^{*}
and
h_{t}
be a generating family for
f_{t}
.
For
t\neq 0
, frontal stability of
f_{t}
implies
\mathscr{A}
-stability of
h_{t}
due to Theorem
2.19
.
Therefore,
h
admits a stabilisation.

Conversely, assume that
h
admits a stabilisation
h_{t}
, with
t\in\mathbb{D}
.
The
1
-parameter unfolding
H^{\prime}(t,y)=(t,h_{t}(y))
defines a
1
-parameter frontal unfolding
F(t,x)=(t,f_{t}(x))
of
f
.
Since
h_{t}
is stable for all
t\neq 0
, it follows from Theorem
2.19
that
f_{t}
is
\mathscr{F}
-stable for
t\neq 0
.
Therefore,
f
admits a frontal stabilisation.

Now assume that
f
admits a frontal stabilisation, so that
\mu_{\mathscr{F}}(f)
is defined.
By definition of frontal Milnor number,
\mu_{\mathscr{F}}(f)
is the number of spheres in the image of
f_{t}
for
t\in\mathbb{D}^{*}
.
The image of
f
is the discriminant of
h
, so the image of
f_{t}
is the discriminant of the induced stabilisation
h_{t}
and
\mu_{\mathscr{F}}(f)
is equal to the number of spheres in a stable deformation of the discriminant of
h
, which is
\mu_{\Delta}(h)
.
∎

To finish this section, we have the following result below, which will prove useful in §
4
.
Let
(X,0)
be an analytic hypersurface germ, with
X\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
, and

such that
(X,0)=(V(g),0)
.
We define

as the sheaf of vector fields in

which are tangent to

outside its singular locus.
This can be computed explicitly as

is a

if

is a

-free module.

Corollary 2.22
.

Every germ of stable wave front is a free divisor of rank
n+1
.

Proof.

Let
(X,0)
be a germ of stable wave front.
If
(X,0)
is a smooth germ, then
(X,0)
is a free divisor since
\Der(-\log(X,0))\cong\theta_{n}
, where

is the dimension of
(X,0)
.
Thus we can assume that
(X,0)
has a singularity at the origin.

By Theorem
2.18
, there exists a map germ

such that
(X,0)
is the discriminant of
h
.
Since
(X,0)
is stable,

is
\mathscr{A}
-stable by Theorem

, and a result by Looijenga

implies that the discriminant of

is a free divisor.
Therefore,
(X,0)
is a free divisor.
∎

4.
A Jacobian module for frontal disentanglements

The expression for the frontal codimension derived on the previous section is based on a similar result from
[
5
]
.
In that article, the authors define a module

for a smooth map germ

with reduced image equation

, whose dimension over
\mathbb{C}
equals

when
(n,n+1)
are in the range of Mather’s nice dimensions (i.e. for

) or when

has corank
1

Given a smooth
r

of

relative

with the property that

M_{y}(G)\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{r}}\frac{\mathscr{O}_{r}}{\mathfrak{m}_{r}}\cong M(g),

where
G=0
is a reduced image equation for

which specifies into

.
They then go to show that under the conditions above,

if and only if

is Cohen-Macaulay of dimension

.
In particular, they show that

has finite dimension over
\mathbb{C}
if and only if

is
\mathscr{A}
-finite.

The main obstacle one faces when trying to reach a similar result in the framework of frontal mappings is that some of the key results in
[
5

rely on the fact that the singular set of an
\mathscr{A}
-finite map germs

has codimension

can sometimes have finite dimension over

even if

is not
\mathscr{A}
-finite (see Example

below).
This motivates the question of whether the results from
[

]
can be adapted to our framework.

We begin by giving some preliminary definitions.
The conductor ideal of a smooth map germ

C(f)=\{h\in\mathscr{O}_{X,0}:h\cdot\mathscr{O}_{n}\subset\mathscr{O}_{X,0}\}.

A lemma of Piene

Lemma 4.1

Let
g(y)=0
be a reduced equation for the image of

If

-finite case, the ring

Definition 4.2

Example 4.3

Let

This mapping is
\mathscr{F}
-stable and its singular set is given by the source line of equation

We have

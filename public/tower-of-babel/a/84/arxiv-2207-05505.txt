Systematics of fully heavy dibaryons
Xin-Zhen Weng, Shi-Lin Zhu

Abstract
We systematically study the mass spectra of the fully heavy dibaryons in an extended chromomagnetic model, which includes both the colorelectric and chromomagnetic interactions. We find no stable state below the corresponding baryon-baryon thresholds. Besides the masses, we also estimate the relative width ratios of the two-body decay channels. We hope our study will be of help for future experiments.

For the fully heavy dibaryon, interactions are provided by gluon exchange and string confinement.
Usually, the interactions include the spin-independent Coulomb and confinement interactions, and the spin-dependent chromomagnetic, spin-orbit and tensor interactions

H_{\text{int}}=-\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}\bm{F}_{i}\cdot\bm{F}_{j}-\sum_{i<j}v_{ij}\bm{S}_{i}\cdot\bm{S}_{j}\bm{F}_{i}\cdot\bm{F}_{j}\,.

(1)

Weng et al. 2018

II
The Extended Chromomagnetic Model

In the quark model, the Hamiltonian of a
S
-wave hadron reads
Cui et al. 2007
;
Buccella et al. 2007
;
Luo et al. 2017
;
Liu et al. 2019

H=\sum_{i}m_{i}+H_{\text{CE}}+H_{\text{CM}}\,,

(2)

where
m_{i}
is the effective mass of the
i
th quark (or antiquark).

H_{\text{CE}}
is the colorelectric (CE) interaction
Høgaasen et al. 2014

H_{\text{CE}}=-\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}\bm{F}_{i}\cdot\bm{F}_{j}\,,

(3)

and
H_{\text{CM}}
is the chromomagnetic (CM) interaction

H_{\text{CM}}=-\sum_{i<j}v_{ij}\bm{S}_{i}\cdot\bm{S}_{j}\bm{F}_{i}\cdot\bm{F}_{j}\,.

(4)

Here,
A_{ij}
and
v_{ij}
are the effective coupling constants which depend on the constituent quark masses and the spatial wave function.

\bm{S}_{i}=\bm{\sigma}_{i}/2
and
\bm{F}_{i}={\bm{\lambda}}_{i}/2
are the quark spin and color operators.
For the antiquark,

\bm{S}_{\bar{q}}=-\bm{S}_{q}^{*}\,,\quad\bm{F}_{\bar{q}}=-\bm{F}_{q}^{*}\,.

(5)

Since

\sum_{i<j}\left(m_{i}+m_{j}\right)\bm{F}_{i}\cdot\bm{F}_{j}=\left(\sum_{i}m_{i}\bm{F}_{i}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{i}\bm{F}_{i}\right)-\frac{4}{3}\sum_{i}m_{i}\,,

(6)

and the total color operator
\sum_{i}\bm{F}_{i}
nullifies any color-singlet physical state, we can rewrite the Hamiltonian as
Weng et al. 2018

H=-\frac{3}{4}\sum_{i<j}m_{ij}V^{\text{C}}_{ij}-\sum_{i<j}v_{ij}V^{\text{CM}}_{ij}\,,

(7)

by introducing the quark pair mass parameter

m_{ij}=\left(m_{i}+m_{j}\right)+\frac{4}{3}a_{ij}\,,

(8)

where
V^{\text{C}}_{ij}\equiv\bm{F}_{i}\cdot\bm{F}_{j}
and
V^{\text{CM}}_{ij}\equiv\bm{S}_{i}\cdot\bm{S}_{j}\bm{F}_{i}\cdot\bm{F}_{j}
are the colorelectric and CM interactions between quarks.
Here
\{m_{ij},v_{ij}\}
are unknown parameters.
In Ref.
Weng et al. 2018
, we fitted these parameters from the conventional mesons and baryons.
The related parameters are presented in Table
1
.
In this work, we use the same set of parameters to study the ground state fully heavy dibaryons.

Only the scalar states are allowed for these systems, namely the

From Fig.

We may also see this from the Hamiltonian.
For the fully heavy dibaryon with the identical quarks, we have
Jaffe 1977a
;
Park et al. 2015

\Braket{H\left(Q^{6},0^{+}\right)}=\Braket{-\frac{3}{4}m_{QQ}^{D}\sum_{i<j}\bm{F}_{i}\cdot\bm{F}_{j}-v_{QQ}^{D}\sum_{i<j}\bm{F}_{i}\cdot\bm{F}_{j}\bm{S}_{i}\cdot\bm{S}_{j}}=3m_{QQ}^{D}+3v_{QQ}^{D}\,,

(9)

where the superscript
D
is an abbreviation of dibaryon.
On the other hand, the mass of the fully heavy
\Omega_{QQQ}
baryon reads
Weng et al. 2018

M_{\Omega_{QQQ}}=\frac{3}{2}m_{QQ}^{B}+\frac{1}{2}v_{QQ}^{B}\,,

(10)

where the superscript
B
is an abbreviation of the baryon.
In the present work, we assume that the dibaryons and the baryons share the same parameters, namely
m_{QQ}^{D}{\approx}m_{QQ}^{B}{\equiv}m_{QQ}
and
v_{QQ}^{D}{\approx}v_{QQ}^{B}{\equiv}v_{QQ}
.
Thus the dibaryons are above the baryon-baryon thresholds by

\Delta{E}\approx 2v_{QQ}=\left\{\begin{split}113.5~\text{MeV}~\text{ for }~cccccc\,,\\
61.3~\text{MeV}~\text{ for }~bbbbbb\,.\end{split}\right.

(11)

Of course, applying the baryon parameters to the dibaryon systems will cause some uncertainties.
Note that the dibaryon systems should have larger size compared to the baryon systems.
Thus the distance between two quarks within the dibaryons should be larger than that of the baryons.
So the attractive force between two quarks within the dibaryons should be weaker than that of the baryons.
Consequently, the realistic masses of the dibaryons should be slightly larger than the masses calculated in this work
An et al. 2022
;
Deng et al. 2021
.
We see that even with this consideration, the
c^{6}
(
b^{6}
) dibaryon should be above the
\Omega_{ccc}\Omega_{ccc}
(
\Omega_{bbb}\Omega_{bbb}
) threshold.

\times

Usually, the
QQQ

\ket{(QQQ)^{1_{c}}(QQQ)^{1_{c}}}

S

S

\ket{(QQQ)^{8_{c}}(QQQ)^{8_{c}}}

For simplicity, we follow Refs.

;

In Table

\Psi=\sum_{i}c_{i}\Ket{\psi_{i}\left(B{\otimes}B\right)}+\cdots\,.

(16)

c_{i}
of the corresponding component in the eigenvector, and also depends on the phase space.
For two body decay
Gao 1992

Weng et al. 2019

\Gamma_{i}=\gamma_{i}\alpha\frac{k^{2L+1}}{m^{2L}}{\cdot}|c_{i}|^{2},

(17)

m

For the decay processes in this work,

\mathcal{O}(10^{-2})
or even smaller.
Thus the higher wave decays are all suppressed.
We only consider the
S
-wave decays.
Next we have to estimate the
\gamma_{i}

1/2^{+}

3/2^{+}

each

\gamma_{B_{1}B_{2}}=\gamma_{B_{1}B_{2}^{*}}=\gamma_{B_{1}^{*}B_{2}}=\gamma_{B_{1}^{*}B_{2}^{*}}\,,

(18)

where

1/2^{+}
and
3/2^{+}

IV
Conclusions

In this work, we have systematically studied the fully heavy dibaryons in an extended chromomagnetic model, which consists of effective color-electric and color-magnetic (chromomagnetic) interactions.
We find that there is no dibaryon state below the corresponding baryon-baryon thresholds.
Our numerical results suggest that the energy levels are mainly determined by the effective color-electric interaction.
For example, the
cccc
/
bb
cluster in the
ccccbb
dibaryons can be a color-triplet or a color-sextet.
The effective color-electric interaction splits the two configurations and makes the color-triplet configuration lighter than the color-sextet one by nearly
100~\text{MeV}
, resulting in a clear two-band structure.
The chromomagnetic interaction contributes small splittings for the two bands.
We find that the lightest state always has a higher spin.
The reason is that the Pauli principle imposes large restriction over the wave functions.
More precisely, the color and spin wave functions must be coupled in some particular form restricted by the
\text{SU}(6)_{cs}=\text{SU}(3)_{c}{\otimes}\text{SU}(2)_{s}
symmetry.
Then the chromomagnetic interaction depends not only on the
\text{SU}(2)_{s}
Casimir operator (spin), but also on the
\text{SU}(3)_{c}
and
\text{SU}(6)_{cs}
Casimir operators (
\text{C}_{3}
and
\text{C}_{6}
).
The
\text{C}_{6}
term has opposite effect compared to the spin term [for example, see Eq. (
13
)].
When the
\text{C}_{6}
term prevails, the higher spin state becomes lighter.

With the eigenvectors obtained, we have also studied the decay properties of the dibaryons.
We hope that future experiments can search for these states.

Appendix A
The wave function

In this section, we construct the dibaryon wave functions.
In principle, the total wave function is a direct product of the spatial, flavor, color and spin wave functions.
In this work, we consider the ground state and assume that the spatial wave function is totally symmetric.
Then we need to construct the totally anti-symmetric
\text{flavor}\otimes\text{color}\otimes\text{spin}
wave functions.
According to the flavor configurations, we can divide the fully heavy dibaryons into four categories, depending on their heavy flavor content:

1.

c^{6}
and
b^{6}
,

2.

c^{5}b
and
b^{5}c
,

3.

c^{4}b^{2}
and
b^{4}c^{2}
,

4.

c^{3}b^{3}
.

To satisfy the Pauli principle, we first construct the totally anti-symmetric wave functions for the clusters with the identical quarks (see Appendix
B
), then we combine the clusters to construct the dibaryon wave functions.

For the

,
b^{6}
,

and

configuration.
There are five possible color wave functions

\displaystyle{}=\ket{(\{[(q_{1}q_{2})^{6}q_{3}]^{8}q_{4}\}^{\bar{6}}q_{5})^{\bar{3}}q_{6}}^{1}\,,

\displaystyle\phi_{\alpha 2}

\displaystyle{}=\ket{(\{[(q_{1}q_{2})^{\bar{3}}q_{3}]^{8}q_{4}\}^{\bar{6}}q_{5})^{\bar{3}}q_{6}}^{1}\,,

\displaystyle\phi_{\alpha 3}

Appendix B
The totally anti-symmetric wave functions for the
q^{N}
clusters

There are two methods to obtain the totally anti-symmetric wave functions for the
q^{N}
clusters.
The first one is by using the properties of permutation group, namely the Clebsch-Gordon (CG) coefficients of the
S_{N}
group.
The details can be found in Ref.
Stancu and Pepin 1999
.
On the other hand, we can also obtain the wave functions by applying the quark exchange operators and imposing the anti-symmetric properties over the identical quarks [or
u
and
d
quarks (collectively as
n
) in the isospin
\mathrm{SU}(2)
symmetry].
More precisely, assuming
\{\psi_{i},i=1,2,\ldots,n\}
the total wave function bases.
The wave function that satisfies the Pauli principle can be expanded as their superposition

\Psi=\sum_{i}k_{i}\psi_{i}\,.

(53)

The Pauli principle gives the secular equations

\sum_{j}\Braket{\psi_{i}|A|\psi_{j}}k_{j}=-k_{i}\,,

q_{\alpha}
and
q_{\beta}

Appendix C
Some formulae for the colorelectric and chromomagnetic interactions

In this section, we introduce the
\text{SU}(6)_{cs}=\text{SU}(3)_{c}{\otimes}\text{SU}(2)_{s}
group, then we can simplify the colorelectric and chromomagnetic interactions for hadron consists of at most
two
flavors.

Following Ref.
Jaffe 1977a
, the generators of
\text{SU}(6)_{cs}
are defined by the products of
\sigma^{k}
and
\lambda^{a}

\alpha^{\mu}={}\left\{\begin{split}&\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\sigma^{k},\quad k=1,2,3\,,\\
&\lambda^{a},\quad a=1,2,\cdots,8\,,\\
&\sigma^{k}\lambda^{a}\,,\end{split}\right.

(72)

which are normalized to
\text{tr}\alpha^{2}=4
.
The Casimir operators of the
\text{SU}(2)_{s}
,
\text{SU}(3)_{c}
and
\text{SU}(6)_{cs}
groups are defined as

\displaystyle\text{C}_{2}={}

\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{\sigma}=4S(S+1)\,,

(73)

\displaystyle\text{C}_{3}={}

\displaystyle\bm{\lambda}\cdot\bm{\lambda}\,,

(74)

\displaystyle\text{C}_{6}={}

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu=1}^{35}\left(\alpha^{\mu}\right)^{2}=\frac{2}{3}\text{C}_{2}+\text{C}_{3}+\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{\sigma}\bm{\lambda}\cdot\bm{\lambda}\,.

(75)

Now we can deal with the colorelectric and chromomagnetic interactions.

For
q^{s}Q^{t}
system,

\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}m_{ij}\bm{F}_{i}\cdot\bm{F}_{j}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle m_{qq}\sum_{i<i^{\prime}=1}^{s}\bm{F}_{i}\cdot\bm{F}_{i^{\prime}}+m_{QQ}\sum_{j<j^{\prime}=s+1}^{s+t}\bm{F}_{j}\cdot\bm{F}_{j^{\prime}}+m_{qQ}\sum_{i=1}^{s}\sum_{j=s+1}^{s+t}\bm{F}_{i}\cdot\bm{F}_{j}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{m_{qQ}}{8}\text{C}_{3}\left(q^{s}Q^{t}\right)+\frac{m_{qq}-m_{qQ}}{8}\text{C}_{3}\left(q^{s}\right)+\frac{m_{QQ}-m_{qQ}}{8}\text{C}_{3}\left(Q^{t}\right)-\frac{4}{3}\times\frac{sm_{qq}+tm_{QQ}}{2}\,.

(76)

For color-singlet hadron, the first term vanishes.
The colorelectric interaction becomes

\Braket{-\frac{3}{4}\sum_{i<j}m_{ij}\bm{F}_{i}\cdot\bm{F}_{j}}=\frac{sm_{qq}+tm_{QQ}}{2}-\frac{3}{8}{\delta}m_{qQ}\Braket{\text{C}_{3}\left(q^{s}\right)}\,,

(77)

where

{\delta}m_{qQ}=\frac{m_{qq}+m_{QQ}-2m_{qQ}}{4}\,.

(78)

As shown in Appendix
A
, The bases have definite
\text{SU}(3)_{c}
color representations for
q^{s}
and
Q^{t}
clusters.
Thus the colorelectric interaction is diagonal.
If
t=0
or
t=1
, the formula can be further simplified, as shown in Eq. (
9
) and Eq. (
12
).

Similarly, the chromomagnetic interaction

\displaystyle-\sum_{i<j}v_{ij}\bm{F}_{i}\cdot\bm{F}_{j}\bm{S}_{i}\cdot\bm{S}_{j}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-v_{qq}\sum_{i<i^{\prime}=1}^{s}\bm{F}_{i}\cdot\bm{F}_{i^{\prime}}\bm{S}_{i}\cdot\bm{S}_{i^{\prime}}-v_{QQ}\sum_{j<j^{\prime}=s+1}^{s+t}\bm{F}_{j}\cdot\bm{F}_{j^{\prime}}\bm{S}_{j}\cdot\bm{S}_{j^{\prime}}-v_{qQ}\sum_{i=1}^{s}\sum_{j=s+1}^{s+t}\bm{F}_{i}\cdot\bm{F}_{j}\bm{S}_{i}\cdot\bm{S}_{j}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-\frac{v_{qQ}}{32}\left(\text{C}_{6}-\frac{2}{3}\text{C}_{2}-\text{C}_{3}\right)_{q^{s}Q^{t}}-\frac{v_{qq}-v_{qQ}}{32}\left(\text{C}_{6}-\frac{2}{3}\text{C}_{2}-\text{C}_{3}\right)_{q^{s}}-\frac{v_{QQ}-v_{qQ}}{32}\left(\text{C}_{6}-\frac{2}{3}\text{C}_{2}-\text{C}_{3}\right)_{Q^{t}}+\frac{sv_{qq}+tv_{QQ}}{2}\,.

(79)

As shown in Appendices
A
–
B
, the bases have definite
\text{SU}_{cs}(6)
representations for the
q^{s}
and
Q^{t}
clusters, but not for the
q^{s}Q^{t}
system.
Thus the bases will be mixed by the chromomagnetic interaction.
However, for
t=0
and
t=1
cases, the
q^{s}Q^{t}
systems do have definite
\text{SU}_{cs}(6)
representations [see Eq. (
14
)], then the formula can be simplified to Eq. (
9
) and Eq. (
15
), respectively.
Note that similar formulae have also been obtained in Refs.
Jaffe 1977a
;
Park et al. 2015
.

Phase transition for the existence of van Kampen 2-complexes in random groups
Tsung-Hsuan Tsai

Abstract
Gromov showed that [ Gro93 ] with high probability, every bounded and reduced van Kampen diagram D D of a random group at density d d satisfies the isoperimetric inequality | ∂ D | ≥ ( 1 − 2 ​ d − s ) ​ | D | ​ ℓ |\partial D|\geq(1-2d-s)|D|\ell . In this article, we adapt Gruber-Mackay’s prove [ GM18 ] for random triangular groups, showing a non-reduced 2-complex version of this inequality. Moreover, for any 2-complex Y Y of a given geometric form, we exhibit a phase transition: we give explicitly a critical density d c d_{c} depending only on Y Y such that, in a random group at density d d , if d < d c d<d_{c} then there is no reduced van Kampen 2-complex of the form Y Y ; while if d > d c d>d_{c} then there exists reduced van Kampen 2-complexes of the form Y Y . As an application, we show a phase transition for the C ⁡ ( p ) C(p) small-cancellation condition: for a random group at density d d , if d < 1 / ( p + 1 ) d<1/(p+1) then it satisfies C ⁡ ( p ) C(p) ; while if d > 1 / ( p + 1 ) d>1/(p+1) then it does not satisfy C ⁡ ( p ) C(p) .

A

A

Reduction degree and Complexity

Let us define the

The

Definition 2.1

(Reduction degree,
[
GM18

)
.

Let

It is not hard to see that a van Kampen 2-complex

A

of a 2-complex is a reduced combinatorial path passing only by vertices of degree
2
whose endpoints are not of degree

of a 2-complex encodes the number of maximal arcs with the number of faces.

Definition 2.2

(Complexity of a 2-complex)
.

Let

be a 2-complex. Let

•

•

•

For any face

Note that if

is
6K

, the number of its maximal arcs is at most

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
K>0
. For
\ell

K
with face boundary lengths at most
\ell

Proof.

As there are at most
K

K^{2}
ways to draw an arc connecting two of these vertices, every arc is with length at most

To attach

K

which is smaller than

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}\leq\sum_{\overline{f}\in\overline{F}}|\overline{E}_{\overline{f}}|.

Denote
\Red(\overline{e})
the reduction degree caused by the edge
\overline{e}
. That is,

\Red(\overline{e}):=\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}\sum_{1\leq j\leq\ell}\Big(\big|\{f\in F\,|\,\widetilde{\varphi}_{2}(f)=i,\textup{ $e$ or $e^{-1}$ is the $j$-th edge of }\partial f\}\big|-1\Big)^{+},

so that the number of preferred faces of
\overline{e}
is bounded by
1+\Red(\overline{e})
. Hence,

\sum_{\overline{f}\in\overline{F}}|\overline{E}_{\overline{f}}|\leq\sum_{\overline{e}\in\overline{E}}\Big(1+\Red(\overline{e})\Big)=|\widetilde{Y}^{(1)}|+\Red(\widetilde{Y}).

∎

Probability of filling.

We shall estimate the probability that an abstract van Kampen 2-complex
\widetilde{Y}
is fillable by a random group
G_{\ell}(m,d)
. This step is the key to prove Theorem
1.4
. Recall that
Q_{\ell}:=\left\{(2m-1)^{(d-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\ell)}\leq|R_{\ell}|\leq(2m-1)^{(d+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\ell)}\right\}
is an a.a.s. true probability event.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\widetilde{Y}
be an abstract van Kampen 2-complex with
k
abstract relators. We have

\mathbf{Pr}\left(\widetilde{Y}\textup{ is fillable by }G_{\ell}(m,d)\;\middle|\;Q_{\ell}\right)\leq\left(\frac{2m}{2m-1}\right)^{k}(2m-1)^{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\eta_{i}+\left(d-1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\ell\right)}.

Proof.

Let us estimate the number of fillings of
\widetilde{Y}
. For every free-to-fill abstract letter
(i,j)
, there are at most
2m
ways to fill a generator if
j=1
, at most
(2m-1)
ways to fill if
j\neq 1
for avoiding reducible word. As there are
\eta_{i}
free-to-fill abstract letters on the
i
-th abstract relator, there are at most
2m(2m-1)^{\eta_{i}-1}
ways to fill it. So there are at most
\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(2m(2m-1)^{\eta_{i}-1}\right)
ways to fill
\widetilde{Y}
.

Let
Y
be a van Kampen 2-complex, which is a filling of
\widetilde{Y}
. The 2-complex
Y
is labeled by
k
different relators in
B_{\ell}
, denoted
r_{1},\dots,r_{k}
. By lemma
2.5
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Pr}\left(Y\textup{ is a 2-complex of }G_{\ell}(m,d)\;\middle|\;Q_{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Pr}\left(r_{1},\dots,r_{k}\in R_{\ell}\;\middle|\;Q_{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(2m-1)^{k(d-1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})\ell}.

Hence

\displaystyle\mathbf{Pr}\left(\widetilde{Y}\textup{ is fillable by }G_{\ell}(m,d)\;\middle|\;Q_{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{Y\textup{ fills }\widetilde{Y}}\mathbf{Pr}\left(Y\textup{ is a 2-complex of }G_{\ell}(m,d)\;\middle|\;Q_{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(2m(2m-1)^{\eta_{i}-1}\right)(2m-1)^{k(d-1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2m}{2m-1}\right)^{k}(2m-1)^{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\eta_{i}+\left(d-1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\ell\right)}.

∎

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\widetilde{Y}
be an abstract van Kampen 2-complex with
k
abstract relators. Suppose that
\widetilde{Y}
does not satisfy the inequality given in Theorem
1.4
, i.e.

|\widetilde{Y}^{(1)}|+\Red(\widetilde{Y})<(1-d-\varepsilon)|\widetilde{Y}|\ell,

\mathbf{Pr}\left(\widetilde{Y}\textup{ is fillable by }G_{\ell}(m,d)\;\middle|\;Q_{\ell}\right)\leq\left(\frac{2m}{2m-1}\right)(2m-1)^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\ell}.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{Y}_{i}
be the sub-2-complex of
\widetilde{Y}
consisting of faces labeled by the
i
first abstract relators. Denote
P_{i}=\mathbf{Pr}\left(\widetilde{Y}_{i}\textup{ is fillable by }G_{\ell}(m,d)\;\middle|\;Q_{\ell}\right)
. Apply lemma
2.9
on
\widetilde{Y}_{i}
, we have

P_{i}\leq\left(\frac{2m}{2m-1}\right)^{i}(2m-1)^{\sum_{j=1}^{i}\left(\eta_{j}+\left(d-1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\ell\right)}.

Note that if
\widetilde{Y}
is fillable by
G_{\ell}(m,d)
then its sub 2-complex
\widetilde{Y}_{i}
is fillable by the same group. So for any
1\leq i\leq k
,

\log_{2m-1}(P_{k})\leq\log_{2m-1}(P_{i})\leq\sum_{j=1}^{i}\left(\eta_{j}+\left(d-1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\ell+\log_{2m-1}\left(\frac{2m}{2m-1}\right)\right).

Without loss of generality, suppose that
\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha_{2}\geq\dots\geq\alpha_{k}
. Note that
\log_{2m-1}(P_{k})
is negative and
\alpha_{1}\leq|\widetilde{Y}|
, so
|\widetilde{Y}|\log_{2m-1}(P_{k})\leq\alpha_{1}\log_{2m-1}(P_{k})
. By Abel’s summation formula, with convention
\alpha_{k+1}=0
,

\displaystyle|\widetilde{Y}|\log_{2m-1}(P_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{1}\log_{2m-1}(P_{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{i+1})\log_{2m-1}(P_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{i+1})\sum_{j=1}^{i}\left[\eta_{i}+\left(d-1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\ell+\log_{2m-1}\left(\frac{2m}{2m-1}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\left[\eta_{i}+\left(d-1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\ell+\log_{2m-1}\left(\frac{2m}{2m-1}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\right)\left[\left(d-1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\ell+\log_{2m-1}\left(\frac{2m}{2m-1}\right)\right].

Note that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}=|\widetilde{Y}|
. By Lemma
2.8

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\eta_{i}\leq|\widetilde{Y}^{(1)}|+\Red(\widetilde{Y})<(1-d-\varepsilon)|\widetilde{Y}|\ell.

Hence,

\displaystyle|\widetilde{Y}|\log_{2m-1}(P_{k})

\displaystyle\leq(1-d-\varepsilon)|\widetilde{Y}|\ell+|\widetilde{Y}|\left[\left(d-1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\ell+\log_{2m-1}\left(\frac{2m}{2m-1}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq|\widetilde{Y}|\left[-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\ell+\log_{2m-1}\left(\frac{2m}{2m-1}\right)\right].

∎

3.1
Geometric form and Critical density

Let us define the
geometric form
of 2-complexes and the
critical density
of a geometric form. To simplify the notations, for a 2-complex

, we denote
\Edge(Y)
as the set of geometric edges of
Y
and
e
instead of
\overline{e}
for geometric edges.

Definition 3.1
.

A

of 2-complexes is a couple
(Y,\lambda)
where
Y=(V,E,F)
is a finite connected 2-complex without isolated edges, and
\lambda
is a length labeled on edges defined by
\lambda:\Edge(Y)\to]0,1],e\mapsto\lambda_{e}
, such that for every face
f
of
Y
, the boundary length
|\partial f|
is bounded by
1
.

A sequence of 2-complexes
(Y_{\ell})
is called
of the geometric form

(Y,\lambda)
if
Y_{\ell}
is obtained from
Y
by dividing every edge
e
of
Y
into
\lfloor\lambda_{e}\ell\rfloor
4
4

4

We can replace
\lfloor\lambda_{e}\ell\rfloor
by any function with
\lambda\ell+o(\ell)
and slightly smaller than
\lambda\ell
. Note that the sum of edge lengths on every face boundary of
Y_{\ell}
is at most
\ell
edges of length
1
.

A sequence of 2-complexes
(Y_{\ell})
is briefly said to be
of the same geometric form
if the geometric form
(Y,\lambda)
is not specified. Note that the boundary length of every face
f
of
Y_{\ell}
is at most
\ell
. If
Z
is a sub-2-complex of
Y
, we denote
Z\leq Y
. By convention, if
(Z_{\ell})
is a sequence of 2-complexes of the geometric form
(Z,\lambda_{|Z})
, we have
Z_{\ell}\leq Y_{\ell}
for any integer
\ell
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(Y,\lambda)
be a geometric form of 2-complexes. The
density
of
Y
is

\dens(Y):=\frac{\sum_{e\in\Edge(Y)}\lambda_{e}}{|Y|}.

The
critical density
of
Y
is

\dens_{c}(Y):=\min_{Z\leq Y}\{\dens(Z)\}.

The intuition of this definition can be found in Lemma
3.8
: the density of
Y
is actually the density of all possible van Kampen 2-complexes that fill
Y_{\ell}
.

Remark 3.3
.

Taking Definition
3.2
and Definition
3.1
together, we have

\dens(Y)=\frac{\sum_{e\in\Edge(Y)}\lambda_{e}}{|Y|}=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{e\in\Edge(Y)}\lfloor\lambda_{e}\ell\rfloor}{|Y_{\ell}|\ell}=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\frac{|Y_{\ell}^{(1)}|}{|Y_{\ell}|\ell}.

Hence, the condition
``\dens_{c}(Y)+d>1"
is equivalent to the following statement: Given
s>0
, for
\ell
large enough, every sub-2-complex
Z_{\ell}
of
Y_{\ell}
satisfies

|Z_{\ell}^{(1)}|\geq(1-d+s)|Z_{\ell}|\ell.

This argument shows that the second assertion of Theorem
1.5
is equivalent to Corollary

1.6
.

(i).

(G_{\ell}(m,d))

To prove the second assertion of Theorem

Recall that

of the sequence

[

Definition 3.4

(Self-intersection partition,
[
Tsa21
, Definition 3.4]
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{Y}_{\ell})
be a sequence of fixed subsets of the sequence
(B_{\ell}^{(k)})
. Let

be an integer. The
i
-th self-intersection of

S_{i,\ell}:=\{(x,y)\in\mathcal{Y}_{\ell}^{2}\,|\,|x\cap y|=i\}

is the number of common elements between the sets

and

The family of subsets
\{S_{i,\ell}\,|\,0\leq i\leq k\}

, called the

of
\mathcal{Y}_{\ell}
. Note that
(S_{i,\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of subsets of the sequence

, with density smaller than
\dens_{\left((B_{\ell}^{(k)})^{2}\right)}\left(\mathcal{Y}_{\ell}^{2}\right)=\dens_{(B_{\ell}^{(k)})}(\mathcal{Y}_{\ell})

Definition 3.5

)
.

Let
(\mathcal{Y}_{\ell})
be a sequence of fixed subsets of

with density

. Let

(Multidimensional intersection formula,

)
.

Let
(R_{\ell})

(B_{\ell})

2m-2
or
2m-1
choices for the last edge. So

C\prod_{e\in\Edge(Y)}(2m-1)^{\lfloor\lambda_{e}\ell\rfloor-3}(2m-2)\leq|\overline{\mathcal{Y}_{\ell}}|\leq C\prod_{e\in\Edge(Y)}(2m-1)^{\lfloor\lambda_{e}\ell\rfloor-2}.

Recall that
k=|Y|=|Y_{\ell}|
and that
|B_{\ell}^{k}|=(2m-1)^{k\ell+o(\ell)}
. We have

\dens_{(B_{\ell}^{k})}(\overline{\mathcal{Y}_{\ell}})=\frac{\sum_{e\in\Edge(Y)}\lambda_{e}}{|Y|}=\dens Y.

∎

Lemma 3.8
.

If
\dens_{c}Y>1/2
and
Y_{\ell}
is fillable by
B_{\ell}
, then
(\mathcal{Y}_{\ell})
is densable in
(B_{\ell}^{(k)})
and

\dens_{(B_{\ell}^{(k)})}(\mathcal{Y}_{\ell})=\dens Y.

Proof.

Suppose that
|Y|\geq 2
. The case
|Y|=1
is trivial. Let
Z
be a sub-2-complex of
Y
with exactly two faces
f_{1},f_{2}
. As
\dens Z\geq\dens_{c}Y>\frac{1}{2}
, by Definition
3.2
, we have

\sum_{e\in\Edge(Z)}\lambda_{e}>\frac{1}{2}|Z|=1\geq|\partial f_{1}|.

Let
\overline{\mathcal{Y}^{Z}_{\ell}}
be the set of fillings of
Y_{\ell}
by
B_{\ell}
such that the two faces of
Z
are filled by the same relator. By the same arguments of the previous lemma,

|\overline{\mathcal{Y}^{Z}_{\ell}}|\leq C(2m-1)^{|\partial f_{1}|}\prod_{e\in\Edge(Y)\backslash\Edge(Z)}(2m-1)^{\lfloor\lambda_{e}\ell\rfloor-2},

so

\displaystyle\dens_{(B_{\ell}^{k})}(\overline{\mathcal{Y}^{Z}_{\ell}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|Y|}\left[\sum_{e\in\Edge(Y)}\lambda_{e}+\left(|\partial f_{1}|-\sum_{e\in\Edge(Z)}\lambda_{e}\right)\right]

\displaystyle<\frac{\sum_{e\in\Edge(Y)}\lambda_{e}}{|Y|}

\displaystyle=\dens Y=\dens_{(B_{\ell}^{k})}(\overline{\mathcal{Y}_{\ell}}).

Knowing that

\mathcal{Y}_{\ell}=\left.\overline{\mathcal{Y}_{\ell}}\Big\backslash\bigcup_{Z<Y,|Z|=2}\overline{\mathcal{Y}^{Z}_{\ell}}\right.,

we have

|\overline{\mathcal{Y}_{\ell}}|-\sum_{Z<Y,|Z|=2}|\overline{\mathcal{Y}^{Z}_{\ell}}|\leq|\mathcal{Y}_{\ell}|\leq|\overline{\mathcal{Y}_{\ell}}|.

There are
\binom{|Y|}{2}
terms in the sum, in every term we have
\dens_{(B_{\ell}^{k})}(\overline{\mathcal{Y}^{Z}_{\ell}})<\dens_{(B_{\ell}^{k})}(\overline{\mathcal{Y}_{\ell}})
, so (cf.
[
Tsa21
, Proposition 2.7 and Proposition 2.8]
)

\dens_{(B_{\ell}^{k})}(\mathcal{Y}_{\ell})=\dens_{(B_{\ell}^{k})}(\overline{\mathcal{Y}_{\ell})}.

Together with Lemma
3.7
, we have
\dens_{(B_{\ell}^{k})}(\mathcal{Y}_{\ell})=\dens Y
. As
\dens_{(B_{\ell}^{k})}(B_{\ell}^{(k)})=1
, we get

\dens_{(B_{\ell}^{(k)})}(\mathcal{Y}_{\ell})=\dens Y.

∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Suppose that

and

i

(r_{1}^{\prime},\dots,r_{k}^{\prime})
corresponding to
W_{\ell}
, and that the remaining
2k-2i

Z_{\ell}
and
W_{\ell}
.

Let us estimate the cardinality
|S_{\ell}(Z,W)|
. Firstly, fill the
k
-tuple
(r_{1},\dots,r_{k})
so the
i
relators in the next
k
-tuple
(r_{1}^{\prime},\dots,r_{k}^{\prime})
corresponding to the sub 2-complex

is determined. There are at most
i!

Let us recall small cancellation notions in
[

\lambda

if no relator is a product of fewer than
p

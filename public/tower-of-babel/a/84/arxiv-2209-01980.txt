Hidden SU ​ ( 2 ) D \mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{D}} vector dark matter with a scalar septuplet
Zhao Zhang, Chengfeng Cai, Hong-Hao Zhang

Abstract
We propose a vector dark matter model from a hidden SU ​ ( 2 ) D \mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{D}} gauge symmetry at TeV \mathrm{T}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V} scale. A scalar septuplet is introduced to break the SU ​ ( 2 ) D \mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{D}} symmetry spontaneously. The septuplet also play the role of a portal between the standard model and the dark sector. We find that there are two different vacuum configurations corresponding to the sign of the quartic coupling λ 3 \lambda_{3} , which yields different mass spectrum for the gauge bosons. For a λ 3 < 0 \lambda_{3}<0 , the masses of gauge bosons are splitting, while for a λ 3 ≥ 0 \lambda_{3}\geq 0 , the masses are degenerate. We also study the RG evolutions of the couplings, and find that the perturbativity and vacuum stability can set a stringent bound on the parameter space. For the phenomenological aspect, we consider the experimental constraints including dark matter direct detection, indirect detection, relic density, and Higgs couplings measurements. We find that there are parameter space survive from all the constraints, and they can be tested in future dark matter direct and indirect detection experiments.

II
The setup of the model

In Ref.

\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{D}}

III
Vacuum stability and perturbativity

As the energy scale raises, all the couplings of interaction evolve according to renormalization group equations. Therefore, it is possible that some of the couplings change their sign at a critical scale. It means even the vacuum is stable in the low energy effective theory, it might become unstable in higher energy scale since the potential has no lower bound in the infinity of the vacuum configuration. In the SM, the quartic coupling of the Higgs field is an example that running to a negative value at the scale around
10^{9}
GeV. It tells us that if the SM is valid up to Planck scale, then the vacuum configuration corresponding to the Higgs field is unstable or metastable. However, if there are some new physics appearing below the scale of switching sign, they can modify the running behaviors of the couplings, and thus the vacuum is capable to keep stable in high energy.

The co-positivity criteria
Kannike:2012pe
are used to ensure that the tree-level potential is bounded from below.
There are two cases which coincide with the categories of two inequivalent vacua, giving respectively
Cai:2015kpa

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}<0:\begin{cases}\lambda\geq 0,\\
\lambda^{\prime}_{2}+\frac{25}{32}\lambda_{3}\geq 0,\\
\lambda_{4}+2\sqrt{\lambda(\lambda^{\prime}_{2}+\frac{25}{32}\lambda_{3})}\geq 0,\end{cases}\qquad\lambda_{3}\geq 0:\begin{cases}\lambda\geq 0,\\
\lambda^{\prime}_{2}\geq 0,\\
\lambda_{4}+2\sqrt{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}_{2}}\geq 0.\end{cases}

(55)

These conditions are required to be satisfied in the whole RG evolution processes till the cutoff scale.
Another theoretical concern of the running couplings is their perturbativity in high energy. We expect them to keep finite below the Planck scale, otherwise they should be embedded in a more fundamental theory below that scale. The blowing up scale for the couplings is so called Landau pole.
The Landau pole problem of the
\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{L}}
scalar multiplets has been studied in Ref.
Hamada:2015bra
.
They showed that if the dimension of representation is larger than 4, the quartic couplings of the scalars blow up soon below the Planck scale even they are set to vanishing at the EW scale, and thus the same problem can be raised in our model.
However, since the gauge coupling of
\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{D}}
is not necessary to be the same as of
\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{L}}
, the Landau pole scale can be totally different from the EW multiplet cases.
In order to determine the RG running of the couplings, we calculate their beta functions. To the one-loop level, the beta functions of couplings are shown as follows,

\displaystyle\beta_{g_{4}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\left(-\frac{8}{3}\right)g_{4}^{3},

(56)

\displaystyle\beta_{\lambda^{\prime}_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\left({30\lambda^{\prime}_{2}}^{2}+\frac{75}{8}\lambda^{2}_{3}+2\lambda_{4}^{2}+15\lambda^{\prime}_{2}\lambda_{3}-144\lambda^{\prime}_{2}g_{4}^{2}+288g_{4}^{4}\right),

(57)

\displaystyle\beta_{\lambda_{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{63}{8}\lambda_{3}^{2}+24\lambda^{\prime}_{2}\lambda_{3}-144\lambda_{3}g_{4}^{2}+288g_{4}^{4}\right),

(58)

\displaystyle\beta_{\lambda_{4}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\left(4\lambda_{4}^{2}+12\lambda\lambda_{4}+18\lambda^{\prime}_{2}\lambda_{4}+\frac{15}{2}\lambda_{3}\lambda_{4}-\frac{9}{10}\lambda_{4}g_{1}^{2}-\frac{9}{2}\lambda_{4}g_{2}^{2}-72\lambda_{4}g_{4}^{2}+6\lambda_{4}y_{t}^{2}\right),

(59)

IV
Constraining the model by phenomenologies, perturbativity, and vacuum stability

In this section, we study the phenomenological constraints for the vector bosons DM, associated with the theoretical constraints from perturbativity and vacuum stability.
Similar to most of the the other cold dark matter models, the vector DMs in our model are produced via the freeze-out mechanism. We utilize the
FeynRules 2

Alloul:2013bka
and
micrOMEGAs

Belanger:2014vza
packages to determine the relic density and annihilation cross section of DMs. We compare the numerical result of DM relic density with the experimental result,
\Omega_{\text{CDM}}h^{2}=0.12
, from the Planck experiment
Planck:2018vyg
.
We also consider the indirect detection bounds on DM annihilation cross section provided by Fermi-LAT
MAGIC:2016xys
, which combines the 6 years data of gamma-ray observations from Segue 1 with 158 hours and 15 dwarf satellite galaxies. In our numerical calculation of the annihilation cross section, DMs in the dwarf satellite galaxies are assumed to be at rest for simplicity. The future prospect on the sensitivity of DM indirect detection from the Cherenkov Telescope Array (CTA)
CTAConsortium:2012fwj
is included for comparison.
In addition, we consider the most recent direct detection constraints provided by first result of LUX-ZEPLIN (LZ) experiment
LUX-ZEPLIN:2022qhg
, and its prospective sensitivity of 1000 days data
LZ:2015kxe
.
Apart from the DM phenomenologies, there are LHC bounds on the the mixing angle between the Higgs field and the scalar
\rho_{2,3}

\cos\alpha
due to the mixing. We fit the measurements of couplings

from ATLAS

and CMS
CMS:2018uag

In the theoretical aspect, we assume that there are no other new sectors directly couple to the SM and dark sector below the Planck scale, and thus the perturbativity and vacuum stability conditions should be satisfied below the Planck scale. The RGEs are solved numerically with certain input of coupling parameters at the mass scale of the septuplet (around TeV).

V
Conclusion and discussion

The vector dark matter originates from a hidden gauged
\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{D}}
is investigated in this work. The
\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{D}}
is spontaneously broken by the vacuum configuration of a real scalar septuplet. The hidden dark sector communicate with the SM via the Higgs portal, and thus all the phenomenologies are related to the mixing angle between the SM Higgs and the radial component of the septuplet.
We find that there are two different vacuum configurations corresponding to different sign of the quartic coupling
\lambda_{3}
.
For the case of
\lambda_{3}<0
, the mass spectrum of gauge fields and scalar fields are splitting, and the lightest particle is assumed to be the complex vactor field
X^{\pm}
. We find a remnant discrete symmetry
Z_{6}\times Z_{2}
after the SSB, which can prevent the DM candidate from decaying into the SM particles.
For the case of
\lambda_{3}\geq 0
, the mass spectrum of gauge fields are degenerate. Both the complex vector
X^{\pm}
and the real vector
Z^{\prime}
are dark matter candidates. A remnant discrete symmetry
Z_{4}\times Z_{2}
is found in this case, and thus the DM candidates are stable.

We also calculate the one-loop beta functions of couplings and let the couplings evolve to higher energy scale.
We find that
\lambda^{\prime}_{2}
and
\lambda_{3}
will soon blow up in higher energy if
g_{4}
,
\lambda^{\prime}_{2}
and
\lambda_{3}
are not small enough. If we assume that all the couplings should keep perturbative below Planck energy scale, only a very restricted parameter space can survive.
Generally, the scale of Landau pole decreases when
|\lambda_{3}|
grows. On the other hand, the scale of Landau pole is not monotonically dependent on
g_{4}
. We find that the scale of Landau pole reaches its maximum around
g_{4}\sim 0.1
–
0.3
, which is confirmed by both analytical and numerical calculations.
However, if we assume that all the dark sector scalar can completely decay into the vector dark matter, which corresponds to
\lambda_{3}\leq-48g_{4}^{2}/5
for
\lambda_{3}<0
and
\lambda_{3}\geq 64g_{4}^{2}/5
for
\lambda_{3}\geq 0
, then the perturbative parameter regions always shrink when
g_{4}
increases in the region of our interests.
As a result, there are upper limits for the absolute value of the portal coupling,
|\lambda_{4}|\lesssim 0.24
, and the gauge coupling
g_{4}\lesssim 0.15~(0.19)
for the
\lambda_{3}\geq 0~(<0)
case.

The range of the DM masses is from several hundreds of
\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}
to a few
\mathrm{T}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}
.
To achieve the observed relic density, the masses of DM and the heavy Higgs should be closed to the resonance region,
2m_{X}\approx m_{h_{2}}
in both cases.
We also find that current direct and indirect detection of DM and the LHC measurements of Higgs couplings can barely exclude the region recommended by the DM relic density and perturbativity, unless the DM mass is around hundreds of
\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}
.
However, these regions can be tested by the future searches in the LZ and CTA experiments.

In this work, we only consider the case that vector fields constitute all the components of DM. However, some scalar particles in the dark sector can be parts of the DM as well if they are not massive enough for decaying into vector particles. In this case, the co-annihilation between the vectors and scalars can play a considerable role during the DM freeze-out.
We will leave this more complicated situation as a future research.

Appendix A
Analytic solution of Landau pole

The one-loop beta functions of gauge couplings
\beta_{g_{i}}=\frac{b_{i}}{16\pi^{2}}g_{i}^{3}
can be solved as,

\displaystyle g_{i}(\mu)=\left(\frac{1}{g_{i}^{2}(\Lambda_{0})}-\frac{b_{i}}{8\pi^{2}}\ln\frac{\mu}{\Lambda_{0}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(72)

At the one-loop level, gauge and Yukawa couplings are the same as the SM and they do not meet Landau pole below the Planck scale.

The couplings
\lambda^{\prime}_{2}
or
\lambda_{3}
are easy to blow up in high energy even they are set to vanish in low energy scale. Their beta functions are given by

\displaystyle\frac{d\lambda^{\prime}_{2}}{dt^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{30\lambda^{\prime}_{2}}^{2}+\frac{75}{8}\lambda^{2}_{3}+15\lambda^{\prime}_{2}\lambda_{3}-144\lambda^{\prime}_{2}g_{4}^{2}+288g_{4}^{4},

(73)

\displaystyle\frac{d\lambda_{3}}{dt^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{63}{8}\lambda_{3}^{2}+24\lambda^{\prime}_{2}\lambda_{3}-144\lambda_{3}g_{4}^{2}+288g_{4}^{4}.

(74)

where
t^{\prime}\equiv\frac{\ln\mu}{16\pi^{2}}
. We have assumed that
\lambda_{4}
is small and the
\lambda_{4}^{2}
term are neglected for simplicity.
To solve these differential equations, we define functions
f_{1}(t^{\prime})
and
f_{2}(t^{\prime})
as follows,

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}_{2}(t^{\prime})=f_{1}(t^{\prime})g_{4}^{2}(t^{\prime}),\qquad\lambda_{3}(t^{\prime})=f_{2}(t^{\prime})g_{4}^{2}(t^{\prime}),

(75)

and then replace them to above two beta functions:

\displaystyle\frac{df_{1}}{dG}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 30f_{1}^{2}+\frac{75}{8}f_{2}^{2}+15f_{1}f_{2}-(144+2b_{4})f_{1}+288,

(76)

\displaystyle\frac{df_{2}}{dG}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{63}{8}f_{2}^{2}+24f_{1}f_{2}-(144+2b_{4})f_{2}+288,

(77)

where
G(t^{\prime})
is given by

\displaystyle dG\equiv g_{4}^{2}dt^{\prime}=\frac{1}{b_{4}g_{4}}\frac{dg_{4}}{dt^{\prime}}dt^{\prime}\quad

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\quad G(t^{\prime})=\frac{1}{b_{4}}\ln\frac{g_{4}(t^{\prime})}{g_{4}(t^{\prime}_{0})}.

(78)

The next step is to introduce another function,
F=f_{1}+\eta f_{2}
, and adjust
\eta
to eliminate the
Ff_{1}
term in

\displaystyle\frac{dF}{dG}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{75}{8\eta^{2}}+\frac{63}{8\eta}\right)F^{2}-(144+2b_{4})F+288(1+\eta)+\left(\frac{75}{8\eta^{2}}-\frac{57}{8\eta}+6\right)f_{1}^{2}

(79)

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{75}{4\eta^{2}}+\frac{3}{4\eta}-24\right)Ff_{1}.

There are two roots for the equation,
75/(4\eta^{2})+3/(4\eta)-24=0
, and
\eta=\left(1+\sqrt{3201}\right)/64\approx 0.899647
is chosen since it gives a smaller coefficient of
f_{1}^{2}
term.
As a result, the evolution equation of
F
can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{dF}{dG}=c_{2}F^{2}-c_{1}F+c_{0}+c^{\prime}f_{1}^{2},

(80)

where

c_{2}=\frac{24\left(1621+21\sqrt{3201}\right)}{\left(\sqrt{3201}+1\right)^{2}}\approx 20.3366,\quad c_{1}=144+2b_{4}\approx 138.667,

\displaystyle\quad c_{0}=\frac{9\left(65+\sqrt{3201}\right)}{2}=547.098,\quad c^{\prime}=\frac{6\left(9526-74\sqrt{3201}\right)}{\left(\sqrt{3201}+1\right)^{2}}=9.66339.

(81)

If we ignore the
f_{1}^{2}
term and
F
can be solved analytically

\displaystyle F(G)=\frac{c_{1}}{2c_{2}}+\hat{d}\tan\left\{c_{2}\hat{d}G(t^{\prime})+\tan^{-1}\left[\frac{1}{\hat{d}}\left(F(t^{\prime}_{0})-\frac{c_{1}}{2c_{2}}\right)\right]\right\},

(82)

with
\hat{d}\equiv\sqrt{\frac{c_{0}}{c_{2}}-\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{4c_{2}^{2}}}\approx
5.1543.
When
\lambda^{\prime}_{2}
or
\lambda_{3}
(equivalently
f_{1}
or

f_{2}
), meets Landau pole at some scale
\Lambda_{\text{LP}}
,
F
also blows up.
Therefore we can extract the Landau pole
\Lambda_{\text{LP}}
in this model by analyzing the divergence of
F
.
Since
G(t^{\prime})
increases monotonically with
t^{\prime}
and
G(t^{\prime}_{0})=0
, we can find
F(t^{\prime})
diverging at

\displaystyle c_{2}\hat{d}G(t^{\prime})+\tan^{-1}\left[\frac{1}{\hat{d}}\left(F(t^{\prime}_{0})-\frac{c_{1}}{2c_{2}}\right)\right]=\frac{\pi}{2}.

(83)

In other words, the Landau pole is at

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\text{LP}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}\exp\left\{\frac{8\pi^{2}}{b_{4}g_{4}^{2}(t^{\prime}_{0})}\left[1-\exp\left(\frac{-\pi b_{4}}{c_{2}\hat{d}}\left[1-\frac{2}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{F(t^{\prime}_{0})}{\hat{d}}-\frac{c_{1}}{2c_{2}\hat{d}}\right)\right]\right)\right]\right\}

(84)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}\exp\left\{\frac{8\pi^{2}}{b_{4}g_{4}^{2}(t^{\prime}_{0})}\left[1-\exp\left(\frac{-\pi b_{4}}{c_{2}\hat{d}}\left[1-\frac{2}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\lambda^{\prime}_{2}(t^{\prime}_{0})+\eta\lambda_{3}(t^{\prime}_{0})}{\hat{d}g_{4}^{2}(t^{\prime}_{0})}-\frac{c_{1}}{2c_{2}\hat{d}}\right)\right]\right)\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\text{LP}}^{(g_{4})}\exp\left[\frac{-8\pi^{2}}{b_{4}g_{4}^{2}(t^{\prime}_{0})}\exp\left(\frac{-\pi b_{4}}{c_{2}\hat{d}}\left[1-\frac{2}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\lambda^{\prime}_{2}(t^{\prime}_{0})+\eta\lambda_{3}(t^{\prime}_{0})}{\hat{d}g_{4}^{2}(t^{\prime}_{0})}-\frac{c_{1}}{2c_{2}\hat{d}}\right)\right]\right)\right],

with
\Lambda_{\text{LP}}^{(g_{4})}=\exp\left(\frac{-8\pi^{2}}{b_{4}g_{4}^{2}(t^{\prime}_{0})}\right)
.

In this approximate solution, we neglect the
\lambda_{4}
term and thus the Landau pole is independent with
\lambda_{4}
.
However, when
\lambda_{4}
too large to be neglected, it should be taken into account. Another simplification in our analytical method is to neglect the
c^{\prime}f_{1}^{2}
term in Eq. (
80
). Since
f_{1}^{2}
may become large so that can not be neglected in higher energy scale, the evolutions of
F
and
f_{1}
couple with each other leading a complicated associated differential equations. If we want to get a reliable quantitative result, we should solve the equations numerically.

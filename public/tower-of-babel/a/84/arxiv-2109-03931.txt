Lie derivatives and structure Jacobi operator on real hypersurfaces in complex projective spaces II
Juan de Dios Pérez, David Pérez-López

Abstract
Let M \displaystyle M be a real hypersurface in complex projective space. The almost contact metric structure on M \displaystyle M allows us to consider, for any nonnull real number k \displaystyle k , the corresponding k \displaystyle k -th generalized Tanaka-Webster connection on M \displaystyle M and, associated to it, a differential operator of first order of Lie type. Considering such a differential operator and Lie derivative we define, from the structure Jacobi operator R ξ \displaystyle R_{\xi} on M \displaystyle M a tensor field of type (1,2), R ξ T ( k ) \displaystyle R_{{\xi}_{T}}^{(k)} . We obtain some classifications of real hypersurfaces for which R ξ T ( k ) \displaystyle R_{{\xi}_{T}}^{(k)} is either symmetric or skew symmetric.

\displaystyle k
-th generalized Tanaka-Webster connection; complex projective space; real hypersurface; Lie derivative; structure Jacobi operator.

Introduction.

Consider a complex projective space
\displaystyle\mbox{{\bb C}}P^{m}
,
\displaystyle m\geq 2
, endowed with the complex structure
\displaystyle J
and the Fubini-Study metric
\displaystyle g
of constant holomorphic sectional curvature
\displaystyle 4
. Let
\displaystyle M
be a
connected real hypersurface
of
\displaystyle\mbox{{\bb C}}P^{m}
without boundary,
\displaystyle\nabla
its Levi-Civita connection on
\displaystyle M
and
\displaystyle N
a local unit normal vector field on
\displaystyle M
. Then
\displaystyle\xi=-JN
is a tangent vector field to
\displaystyle M
called the structure vector field on
\displaystyle M
(or the Reeb vector field). The Kaehlerian structure
\displaystyle(J,g)
of
\displaystyle\mbox{{\bb C}}P^{m}
induces on
\displaystyle M
an almost contact metric structure
\displaystyle(\phi,\xi,\eta,g)
, see
[
1
]
, where
\displaystyle\phi
is the tangent component of
\displaystyle J
,
\displaystyle\eta
is an 1-form given by
\displaystyle\eta(X)=g(X,\xi)
for any
\displaystyle X
tangent to
\displaystyle M
and
\displaystyle g
is the metric induced on
\displaystyle M
.

Takagi, see
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
, classified homogeneous real hypersurfaces in
\displaystyle\mbox{{\bb C}}P^{m}
into 6 types. Kimura,
[
6
]
, proved that these 6 types are the unique Hopf real hypersurfaces in
\displaystyle\mbox{{\bb C}}P^{m}
with constant principal curvatures. A real hypersurface
\displaystyle M
is called Hopf if the structure vector field is principal, that is,
\displaystyle A\xi=\alpha\xi
for a certain function
\displaystyle\alpha
on
\displaystyle M
, where
\displaystyle A
denotes the shape operator of
\displaystyle M
associated to
\displaystyle N
. We will also denote by
𝔻
the maximal holomorphic distribution on
\displaystyle M
, given by all vector fields orthogonal to
\displaystyle\xi
.

Among the real hypersurfaces appearing in Takagi’s list we mention the following ones:

Type
\displaystyle(A_{1})
real hypersurfaces are geodesic hyperspheres of radius
\displaystyle r
,
\displaystyle 0<r<\frac{\pi}{2}
. They have 2 distinct constant principal curvatures,
\displaystyle 2\cot 2r
with eigenspace
\displaystyle\mbox{{\bb R}}[\xi]
and
\displaystyle\cot r
with eigenspace
𝔻
.

Type
\displaystyle(A_{2})
are tubes of radius
\displaystyle r
,
\displaystyle 0<r<\frac{\pi}{2}
, over totally geodesic complex projective spaces
\displaystyle\mbox{{\bb C}}P^{n}
,
\displaystyle 0<n<m-1
. They have 3 distinct constant principal curvatures,
\displaystyle 2\cot 2r
with eigenspace
\displaystyle\mbox{{\bb R}}[\xi]
,
\displaystyle\cot r
and
\displaystyle-\tan r
. The corresponding eigenspaces of
\displaystyle\cot r
and
\displaystyle-\tan r
are complementary and
\displaystyle\phi
-invariant distributions in
𝔻
.

From now on we will call type
\displaystyle(A)
real hypersurfaces to both of either type
\displaystyle(A_{1})
or type
\displaystyle(A_{2})
. The other real hypersurfaces appearing in Takagi’s list have either 3 or 5 distinct constant principal curvatures.

Tanaka,
[
14
]
, and, independtly Webster,
[
16
]
, studied the canonical affine connection defined on a non-degenerate, pseudo-Hermitian CR-manifold, known as the Tanaka-Webster connection. Tanno,
[
15
]
, generalized this connection for contact metric manifolds.

Cho, using the almost contact metric structure on a real hypersurface
\displaystyle M
of
\displaystyle\mbox{{\bb C}}P^{m}
, generalized Tanno’s connection, defining, for any non-zero real number
\displaystyle k
, the
\displaystyle k
-th generalized Tanaka-Webster connection
\displaystyle\hat{\nabla}^{(k)}
, see
[
2
]
,
[
4
]
, by

\begin{array}[]{c}\hat{\nabla}^{(k)}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y+g(\phi AX,Y)\xi-\eta(Y)\phi AX-k\eta(X)\phi Y\end{array}

(1.1)

for any X,Y tangent to
\displaystyle M
. Then
\displaystyle\hat{\nabla}^{(k)}\eta=0
,
\displaystyle\hat{\nabla}^{(k)}\xi=0
,
\displaystyle\hat{\nabla}^{(k)}g=0
,
\displaystyle\hat{\nabla}^{(k)}\phi=0
. In particular, if the shape operator of a real hypersurface satisfies
\displaystyle\phi A+A\phi=2k\phi
,
\displaystyle M
is a contact manifold and the
\displaystyle k
-th generalized Tanaka-Webster connection coincides with the Tanaka-Webster connection. From (
1.1
) the
\displaystyle k
-th Cho operator on
\displaystyle M
associated to a tangent vector field
\displaystyle X
is defined by
\displaystyle F_{X}^{(k)}Y=g(\phi AX,Y)\xi-\eta(Y)\phi AX-k\eta(X)\phi Y
, for any
\displaystyle Y
tangent to
\displaystyle M
. The torsion of the
\displaystyle k
-th generalized Tanaka-Webster connection is then given by
\displaystyle T^{(k)}(X,Y)=F^{(k)}_{X}Y-F^{(k)}_{Y}X
. For any
\displaystyle X
tangent to
\displaystyle M
we call
\displaystyle T_{X}^{(k)}
to the operator on
\displaystyle M
given by
\displaystyle T_{X}^{(k)}Y=T^{(k)}(X,Y)
, for any
\displaystyle Y
tangent to
\displaystyle M
and call it the torsion operator associated to
\displaystyle X
.

Jacobi fields along geodesics of a given Riemannian manifold
\displaystyle(\tilde{M},\tilde{g})
satisfy a very well-known differential equation. This classical differential equation naturally inspires the so-called
Jacobi operator
. That is, if
\displaystyle\tilde{R}
is the curvature operator of
\displaystyle\tilde{M}
, and
\displaystyle X
is any tangent vector field to
\displaystyle\tilde{M}
, the Jacobi operator (with respect to
\displaystyle X
) at
\displaystyle p\in M
,
\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{X}\in
End
\displaystyle(T_{p}\tilde{M})
, is defined as
\displaystyle(\tilde{R}_{X}Y)(p)=(\tilde{R}(Y,X)X)(p)
for all
\displaystyle Y\in T_{p}\tilde{M}
, being a selfadjoint endomorphism of the tangent bundle
\displaystyle T\tilde{M}
of
\displaystyle\tilde{M}
. Clearly, each tangent vector field
\displaystyle X
to
\displaystyle\tilde{M}
provides a Jacobi operator with respect to
\displaystyle X
.

If
\displaystyle M
is a real hypersurface of
\displaystyle\mbox{{\bb C}}P^{m}
the Jacobi operator associated to the structure vector field
\displaystyle\xi
is called the structure Jacobi operator on
\displaystyle M
and we denote it by
\displaystyle R_{\xi}
.

Let
\displaystyle\cal L
denote the Lie derivative on
\displaystyle M
. Therefore
\displaystyle{\cal L}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla_{Y}X
for any
\displaystyle X,Y
tangent to
\displaystyle M
. If we consider
\displaystyle\hat{\nabla}^{(k)}
, we can define a differential operator of first order
\displaystyle{\cal L}^{(k)}
on
\displaystyle M
by
\displaystyle{\cal L}_{X}^{(k)}Y=\hat{\nabla}_{X}^{(k)}Y-\hat{\nabla}_{Y}^{(k)}X={\cal L}_{X}Y+T_{X}^{(k)}Y
, for any
\displaystyle X,Y
tangent to
\displaystyle M
.

We can associate to
\displaystyle R_{\xi}
a tensor field of type (1,2),
\displaystyle R_{{\xi}_{T}}^{(k)}
, given by
\displaystyle R_{{\xi}_{T}}^{(k)}(X,Y)=[T_{X}^{(k)},R_{\xi}]Y=T_{X}^{(k)}R_{\xi}Y-R_{\xi}T_{X}^{(k)}Y
, for any
\displaystyle X,Y
tangent to
\displaystyle M
.

In the first paper of this series,
[
9
]
, first author studied the condition
\displaystyle{\cal L}^{(k)}R_{\xi}={\cal L}R_{\xi}
, that is,
\displaystyle({\cal L}_{X}^{(k)}R_{\xi})Y=({\cal L}_{X}R_{\xi})Y
for any
\displaystyle X,Y
tangent to
\displaystyle M
. This condition is equivalent to the fact that
\displaystyle R_{{\xi}_{T}}^{(k)}
vanishes, obtaining the following

Theorem

There do not exist real hypersurfaces in
\displaystyle\mbox{{\bb C}}P^{m}
,
\displaystyle m\geq 3
, such that
\displaystyle{\cal L}^{(k)}R_{\xi}={\cal L}R_{\xi}
.

The purpose of this paper is to study weaker conditions on
\displaystyle M
. Indeed, we will consider real hypersurfaces
\displaystyle M
in
\displaystyle\mbox{{\bb C}}P^{m}
whose structure Jacobi operator satisfies the following Codazzi condition

\begin{array}[]{c}(({\cal L}_{X}^{(k)}-{\cal L}_{X})R_{\xi})Y=(({\cal L}_{Y}^{(k)}-{\cal L}_{Y})R_{\xi})X\end{array}

(1.2)

for any
\displaystyle X,Y
tangent to
\displaystyle M
. It is easy to see that this condition is equivalent to
\displaystyle R_{{\xi}_{T}}^{(k)}
being symmetric, that is,
\displaystyle R_{{\xi}_{T}}^{(k)}(X,Y)=R_{{\xi}_{T}}^{(k)}(Y,X)
for any
\displaystyle X,Y
tangent to
\displaystyle M
. We will prove the following theorems.

Theorem 1

There does not exist any Hopf real hypersurface
\displaystyle M
in
\displaystyle\mbox{{\bb C}}P^{m}
,
\displaystyle m\geq 3
, such that
\displaystyle R_{\xi}
satisfies (
1.2
) for some nonnull real number
\displaystyle k
.

and

Theorem 2

There does not exist any non-Hopf real hypersurface
\displaystyle M
in
\displaystyle\mbox{{\bb C}}P^{m}
,
\displaystyle m\geq 3
, such that
\displaystyle R_{\xi}
satisfies (
1.2
) for any nonnull real number
\displaystyle k
such that
\displaystyle kg(A\xi,\xi)\neq-1
.

A different weaker condition is a Killing type condition given by

\begin{array}[]{c}(({\cal L}_{X}^{(k)}-{\cal L}_{X})R_{\xi})Y+(({\cal L}_{Y}^{(k)}-{\cal L}_{Y})R_{\xi})X=0\end{array}

(1.3)

for any
\displaystyle X,Y
tangent to
\displaystyle M
. Now this condition is equivalent to the fact that
\displaystyle R_{{\xi}_{T}}^{(k)}
is skew symmetric, that is,
\displaystyle R_{{\xi}_{T}}^{(k)}(X,Y)=-R_{{\xi}_{T}}^{(k)}(Y,X)
for any
\displaystyle X,Y
tangent to
\displaystyle M
. In this case we have a quite different situation as given by the following results.

Theorem 3

Let
\displaystyle M
be a Hopf real hypersurface in
\displaystyle\mbox{{\bb C}}P^{m}
,
\displaystyle m\geq 3
, and
\displaystyle k
a nonnull real number. Then
\displaystyle M
satisfies (
1.3
) if and only if either
\displaystyle k=1
and
\displaystyle M
is locally congruent to a geodesic hypersphere of radius
\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{4}
or
\displaystyle k^{2}\geq 4
and
\displaystyle M
is locally congruent to a tube of radius
\displaystyle r
,
\displaystyle 0<r<\frac{\pi}{2}
, around
\displaystyle\mbox{{\bb C}}P^{n}
,
\displaystyle 0<n<m-1
, whose radius satisfies either
\displaystyle cot(r)=k
or
\displaystyle cot(r)=-\frac{1}{k}
.

In the non-Hopf case we obtain

Theorem 4

There does not exist any non-Hopf real hypersurface
\displaystyle M
in
\displaystyle\mbox{{\bb C}}P^{m}
,
\displaystyle m\geq 3
, such that
\displaystyle R_{{\xi}_{T}}^{(k)}
is skew symmetric for any nonnull real number
\displaystyle k
satisfying
\displaystyle kg(A\xi,\xi)=1
.

\displaystyle-k\phi R_{\xi}Y+\phi AR_{\xi}Y+2(k-\lambda)R_{\xi}\phi Y=0

\displaystyle 2\alpha(k-\lambda)A\phi Y=(\alpha\lambda-1)(k-\lambda)\phi Y

\displaystyle k=\lambda

\begin{array}[]{c}A\phi Y=\frac{\alpha\lambda-1}{2\alpha}\phi Y.\end{array}

We will call

. If in (

\displaystyle\phi Y

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle(-1+\alpha\mu-2\alpha\lambda)(k-\mu)=0

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle-3(1+\alpha\lambda)(k-\mu)=0

\displaystyle\mu=k

\displaystyle\lambda\neq k

As
\displaystyle-(\phi A+A\phi)R_{\xi}Y=R_{\xi}(\phi A+A\phi)Y

\displaystyle-(1+\alpha\lambda)(\phi A+A\phi)Y=(\lambda+\mu)R_{\xi}\phi Y

\begin{array}[]{c}\beta g([\phi Y,Y],U)=\frac{2}{\alpha^{2}}.\end{array}

(3.12)

\displaystyle U

(3.13)

for any

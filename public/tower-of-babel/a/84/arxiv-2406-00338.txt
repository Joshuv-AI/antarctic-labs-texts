Two and three dimensional H 2 H^{2} -conforming finite element approximations without C 1 C^{1} -elements Note: The authors acknowledge that this material is based upon work supported by the National Science Foundation under Award No. DMS-2324364 for the first author and Award No. DMS-2201487 for the second author.
Mark Ainsworth, Charles Parker

Abstract
We develop a method to compute H 2 H^{2} -conforming finite element approximations in both two and three space dimensions using readily available finite element spaces. This is accomplished by deriving a novel, equivalent mixed variational formulation involving spaces with at most H 1 H^{1} -smoothness, so that conforming discretizations require at most C 0 C^{0} -continuity. The method is demonstrated on arbitrary order C 1 C^{1} -splines.

Introduction

Fourth-order partial differential equations arise in a variety of applications including thin plates and shells
[
1
]
, the separation of binary alloys
[
2
]
, condensed matter physics
[
3
]
, and liquid crystals
[
4
]
, among many others; see e.g.
[
5
]
and references therein. The variational formulation of such problems, or their linearizations, are well-posed in subspaces of
H^{2}
so that a standard Galerkin scheme would require
H^{2}
-conforming finite element methods. Conforming schemes are attractive in that they retain the stability of the variational formulation. While the
H^{2}
-conforming 2D Argyris element appears in Firedrake
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
and scikit-fem
[
9
]
and the 2D Hsieh-Clough-Tocher (HCT) element appears in FreeFEM
[
10
]
, the choice is otherwise rather limited. For instance, 3D
H^{2}
-conforming elements seem to be absent from all major packages.

Why are
H^{2}
-conforming elements not more widely available? One possible answer is that most
H^{2}
-conforming elements are
C^{1}
-continuous
[
11
]
and imposing
C^{1}
-continuity is less straightforward then imposing
C^{0}
-continuity. Consider, for instance, the simple setting where the domain is the unit cube and the finite element space is the space of
C^{1}
piecewise polynomials of degree
p\in\mathbb{N}
in the special case of tetrahedral Freudenthal partitions
[
12
]
of the cube. Even counting the dimension of the associated
H^{2}
-conforming space is a nontrivial matter. In
[
13
]
, a count of the dimension of the space is given explicitly for all
p\in\mathbb{N}
along with a choice of basis. However, the basis cannot be constructed in terms of local basis functions defined on a reference element. Instead, the basis is global in nature and thus not amenable to standard finite element procedures such as subassembly. On a general tetrahedral partition, even counting the dimension of the associated
C^{1}
piecewise polynomials is an open problem. Standard
H^{2}
-conforming finite elements based on locally defined basis functions are, of course, possible but only if the degree is sufficiently high (
p\geq 5
in 2D
[
14
]
and
p\geq 9

[
15
, Chapter 18.2]
in 3D).

In this work, we develop a method to compute
H^{2}
-conforming finite element approximations in both two and three space dimensions using finite element spaces that impose lower continuity than
C^{1}
, and which are more readily available. One benefit of such a scheme is that
H^{2}
-conforming approximations can be obtained using existing software packages even when
H^{2}
-spaces are not offered. This is accomplished by deriving a novel, equivalent mixed variational formulation involving spaces with at most
H^{1}
-smoothness, so that conforming discretizations require at most
C^{0}
-continuity. Particular choices of conforming spaces recover
H^{2}
-conforming approximations of the original
H^{2}
-variational problem, including the space in
[
13
]
. The method generalizes our previous work
[
16
]
to
H^{2}
-problems with lower order terms (e.g. time dependent problems) and to three space dimensions.

There are many alternative mixed methods for
H^{2}
-variational problems currently available – most of which specifically focus on the biharmonic equation or Kirchhoff plate problem in 2D; see e.g.
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
. Methods that are applicable to more general
H^{2}
-variational problems in two and three dimensions are less common; see e.g.
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
. In any case, none of the above methods will, in general, recover the
H^{2}
-conforming approximation. In contrast, our method uses standard spaces to recover the
H^{2}
-conforming approximation in two and three dimensions for problems with general boundary conditions.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In
section

2
, we describe the problem setting that naturally leads to the equivalent mixed variational formulation analyzed in
section

3
.
Section

4
details well-posed discretizations of the mixed formulation and their relationship to
H^{2}
-conforming approximations. The implementation of the method and several numerical examples are given in
sections

5
and
6
, while
section

7
discusses the extension to time-dependent problems. For the 2D Morgan-Scott element
[
26
]
, we show in
section

8
that the inf-sup condition associated with the discrete mixed problem is uniformly bounded away from zero with respect to both the polynomial degree and mesh size. Finally, in
section

9
, we show precisely how the proposed method generalizes our previous work
[
16
]
.

2
Problem setting

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
, be a connected Lipschitz polyhedral domain. The boundary of
\Omega
, denoted
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
, is partitioned into disjoint sets
\Gamma_{c}
,
\Gamma_{s}
, and
\Gamma_{f}
. For
d=2
, we assume that there exists simply-connected Lipschitz polygons
\Omega_{0},\Omega_{1},\ldots,\Omega_{N_{H}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\bar{\Omega}_{j}\subsetneq\Omega_{0}
for
1\leq j\leq N_{H}
,
\bar{\Omega}_{i}\cap\bar{\Omega}_{j}=\emptyset
for
1\leq i,j\leq N_{H}
with
i\neq j
, and
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\setminus\cup_{j=1}^{N_{H}}\bar{\Omega}_{j}
. For
d=3
, we assume that the geometry of
\Gamma_{cs}:=\Gamma_{c}\cup\Gamma_{s}
satisfies the following conditions
[
27
]
: For each of the
N
connected components
\Gamma_{cs}^{(i)}
,
1\leq i\leq N
, of
\Gamma_{cs}
, there exists an open Lipschitz domain
\Omega_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
\bar{\Omega}_{i}\cap\bar{\Omega}=\bar{\Gamma}_{cs}^{(i)}
,
\Omega_{i}\cap\Omega=\emptyset
, the distance between
\Omega_{i}
and
\Omega_{j}
is strictly positive for
i\neq j
, and
\bar{\Omega}\cup\bar{\Omega}_{1}\cup\ldots\cup\bar{\Omega}_{N}
is a Lipschitz domain.

We consider
H^{2}(\Omega)
variational problems of the form

\displaystyle w\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega):\qquad B(w,v)=F(v)\qquad\forall v\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega),

(2.1)

where

\displaystyle H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega):=\{v\in H^{2}(\Omega):v|_{\Gamma_{cs}}=0\text{ and }\partial_{n}v|_{\Gamma_{c}}=0\},

F
is a linear functional on
H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
, and
B:H^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
is a bilinear form with the following properties.

1.

(Boundedness) There exists
M_{B}>0
such that

\displaystyle|B(u,v)|\leq M_{B}\|u\|_{2}\|v\|_{2}\qquad\forall u,v\in H^{2}(\Omega).

(2.2)

2.

(Inf-sup condition) There exists
\alpha>0
such that

\displaystyle\inf_{0\neq u\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)}\sup_{0\neq v\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)}\frac{B(u,v)}{\|u\|_{2}\|v\|_{2}}\geq\alpha.

(2.3)

3.

(Uniqueness condition) For every
v\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
, there holds

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)}B(u,v)>0.

(2.4)

Then, problem
2.1
is well-posed
[
28
, Theorem 2.6]
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a shape regular
[
29
, Definition (4.4.13)]
partitioning of
\Omega
into simplices such that the nonempty intersection of any two distinct elements from
\mathcal{T}
is a single common sub-simplex of both elements. Given an
H^{2}
-conforming finite element space
\mathbb{W}\subset H^{2}(\Omega)
on
\mathcal{T}
, typically consisting of
C^{1}
-continuous piecewise polynomials of an arbitrary but fixed degree
p
, the corresponding
H^{2}
-conforming Galerkin finite element scheme for
2.1
reads

\displaystyle w_{\mathcal{T}}\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}:\qquad B(w_{\mathcal{T}},v)=F(v)\qquad\forall v\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma},

(2.5)

where
\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}:=\mathbb{W}\cap H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
. If there exists a positive constant
\alpha_{\mathcal{T}}>0
, possibly depending on the dimension of
\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
, such that the following discrete analogue of the inf-sup condition
2.3
holds:

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle:=\inf_{0\neq u\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}}\sup_{0\neq v\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}}\frac{B(u,v)}{\|u\|_{2}\|v\|_{2}}>0,

then
2.5
has a unique solution. Moreover, standard error estimates (e.g.
[
28
, Lemma 2.28]
) show that
w_{\mathcal{T}}
is, up to a constant, the best approximation to
w
in
\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
:

\displaystyle\|w-w_{\mathcal{T}}\|_{2}\leq\left(1+\frac{M_{B}}{\alpha_{\mathcal{T}}}\right)\inf_{v\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}}\|w-v\|_{2}.

2.1
Typical Problems

In practice, the bilinear form
B(\cdot,\cdot)
often has more structure beyond that indicated by the abstract assumptions
2.2
,
2.3
, and
2.4
. For example, in the case of the biharmonic equation
B(\cdot,\cdot)
depends only on the
gradient
of its arguments i.e.

\displaystyle B(w,v)=a(\grad w,\grad v),

(2.6)

where the bilinear form
a(\bm{\theta},\bm{\psi}):=(\dive\bm{\theta},\dive\bm{\psi})
is bounded on
\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
. In a similar vein, the bilinear form
B(\cdot,\cdot)
for a Kirchhoff plate in equilibrium (see e.g. Chapter 4 of
[
1
]
) again has the form
2.6
, this time with

\displaystyle a(\bm{\theta},\bm{\psi}):=D\left[(1-\nu)(\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{\theta}),\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{\psi}))+\nu(\dive\bm{\theta},\dive\bm{\psi})\right],

(2.7)

where
\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{\theta})=(\grad\bm{\theta}+(\grad\bm{\theta})^{T})/2
and
D
and
\nu
are positive physical parameters so that the corresponding bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is again bounded on
\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
. Here,
\Gamma_{c}
,
\Gamma_{s}
, and
\Gamma_{f}
correspond to the parts of the boundary where the plate is clamped, simply-supported, and left free.

Discretizations of time dependent problems offer another useful example. Consider, for instance, the time dependent biharmonic equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w+\Delta^{2}w=G\quad\text{with}\quad w|_{t=0}=g

in variational form: find
w(t)\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(w(t),v)+(\Delta w(t),\Delta v)=G(v)\qquad\forall v\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)

with
w(0)=g
. This problem can be accommodated in the foregoing framework using Rothe’s method
[
30
]
to first discretize in time (using e.g. Crank-Nicolson with a step size
\Delta t>0
) and thereby obtain the semi-discrete scheme: For
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, find
w_{n}\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle(w_{n},v)+\frac{\Delta t}{2}(\Delta w_{n},\Delta v)=(w_{n-1},v)-\frac{\Delta t}{2}(\Delta w_{n-1},\Delta v)+\Delta tG(v)\qquad\forall v\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)

(2.8)

with
w_{0}=g
. This problem is of the form
2.1
where is given by

\displaystyle B(w,v)=\frac{\Delta t}{2}(\Delta w,\Delta v)+(w,v)=\frac{\Delta t}{2}a(\grad w,\grad v)+(w,v),

where
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is the bilinear form that appears in the equilibrium case
2.6
. Other fourth-order problems, such as the Cahn-Hilliard equations
[
2
]
, give rise to problems of the form
2.1
with the same structure as in
2.8
. The same structure also arises for problems with lower order terms. For example, the weak formulation of the PDE
\Delta^{2}u-\Delta u+\bm{b}\cdot\grad u+u=f
with
\Gamma_{c}=\Gamma
reads: Find
u\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle(\Delta u,\Delta v)+(\grad u,\grad v)+(\bm{b}\cdot\grad u,v)+(u,v)=(f,v)\qquad\forall v\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega).

(2.9)

2.2
Structural assumption

In view of the above examples, we assume that the bilinear form
B(\cdot,\cdot)
can be written in the form

\displaystyle B(w,v)=a(\grad w,\grad v)+c(w,v)\qquad\forall w,v\in H^{2}(\Omega),

(2.10)

where
a:\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)\times\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
and
c:H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
are bilinear forms for which there exists a constant
M>0
such that

\displaystyle|a(\bm{\theta},\bm{\psi})|

\displaystyle\leq M\|\bm{\theta}\|_{1}\|\bm{\psi}\|_{1}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\bm{\theta},\bm{\psi}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega),

(2.11a)

\displaystyle|c(w,v)|

\displaystyle\leq M\|w\|_{1}\|v\|_{1}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall w,v\in H^{1}(\Omega),

(2.11b)

where the
H^{1}(\Omega)
-norm of a vector-valued function is the sum of the
H^{1}(\Omega)
-norm of its components. Similarly, we assume that the right hand side
F
can be written as the sum of two linear functionals
F_{1}\in\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)^{*}
and
F_{2}\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{*}
satisfying

\displaystyle F(v)=F_{1}(\grad v)+F_{2}(v)\qquad\forall v\in H^{2}(\Omega).

(2.12)

The decompositions
2.10
and
2.12
need not be unique. For instance, the bilinear form appearing in the example in
2.9
with
\bm{b}=\bm{0}
satisfies the decomposition condition with either
a(\cdot,\cdot)=(\dive\cdot,\dive\cdot)
and
c(\cdot,\cdot)=(\grad\cdot,\grad\cdot)+(\cdot,\cdot)
or alternatively
a(\cdot,\cdot)=(\dive\cdot,\dive\cdot)+(\cdot,\cdot)
and
c(\cdot,\cdot)=(\cdot,\cdot)
. In both cases,
a(\cdot,\cdot)
and
c(\cdot,\cdot)
are bounded on
\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
and
H^{1}(\Omega)
respectively. The ensuing analysis makes use of the existence of decompositions
2.10
and
2.12
, but does not require their uniqueness.

3
A novel mixed formulation for the continuous problem

The original primal variational problem
2.1
can be written as a mixed formulation involving spaces that have weaker smoothness requirements than
H^{2}(\Omega)
as follows. Let
\bm{\gamma}=\grad w
and observe that, since
w\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
, the gradient
\bm{\gamma}
belongs to the space

\displaystyle\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega):=\{\bm{\psi}\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{d}:\bm{\psi}|_{\Gamma_{c}}=\bm{0}\text{ and }\bm{\pi}_{T}\bm{\psi}|_{\Gamma_{s}}=\bm{0}\},

where
\bm{\pi}_{T}
is the tangential projection operator defined by

\displaystyle\bm{\pi}_{T}\bm{\psi}:=\bm{\psi}-(\bm{\psi}\cdot\bm{n})\bm{n}\qquad\text{on }\Gamma.

Observing that
w\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\subset H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
, where

\displaystyle H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega):=\{\tilde{v}\in H^{1}(\Omega):\tilde{v}|_{\Gamma_{cs}}=0\},

it follows that
(w,\bm{\gamma})\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
satisfies

\displaystyle a(\bm{\gamma},\grad v)+c(w,v)

\displaystyle=F_{1}(\grad v)+F_{2}(v)\qquad\forall v\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega).

(3.1)

Equation

3.1
holds for functions belonging to
H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
. It is not difficult to see that, with the above notation,
H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
can be precisely characterized as the following subspace of
H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
:

\displaystyle H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)=\{\tilde{v}\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega):\grad\tilde{v}\in\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\}.

(3.2)

Hence, viewing the relation
\bm{\gamma}=\grad w
as a side constraint for
3.1
to be imposed using a Lagrange multiplier
\bm{\phi}
, we arrive at the following mixed problem: Find
(\tilde{w},\bm{\gamma},\bm{\phi})\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{\Phi}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle a(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\psi})+c(\tilde{w},\tilde{v})+\langle\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi}),\bm{\phi}\rangle

\displaystyle=F_{1}(\bm{\psi})+F_{2}(\tilde{v})\qquad

\displaystyle\forall(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega),

(3.3a)

\displaystyle\langle\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{w},\bm{\gamma}),\bm{\eta}\rangle

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\bm{\eta}\in\bm{\Phi}_{\Gamma}(\Omega),

(3.3b)

where the operator
\bm{\Xi}
is defined by the rule

\displaystyle\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi}):=\grad\tilde{v}-\bm{\psi}\qquad\forall(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega).

(3.4)

Observe that we write
\tilde{w}
rather than
w
in
3.3
to reflect the fact that, starting with
3.3
, it is not obvious (at this stage) that
\tilde{w}
satisfies the original variational problem
2.1
. Later, we shall see that if the function space
\bm{\Phi}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
and duality pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
are chosen appropriately, then
\tilde{w}
does indeed satisfy
2.1
and, as a consequence
w=\tilde{w}
.

It remains to choose an appropriate function space
\bm{\Phi}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
and duality pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
with which to impose the side constraint. We make the natural choice and take
\bm{\Phi}_{\Gamma}(\Omega):=(\image\bm{\Xi})^{*}
, where

\displaystyle\image\bm{\Xi}:=\{\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi}):(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\},

so that
\image\bm{\Xi}\subset{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
and the duality pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is taken to be the extension of the
L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
inner-product to
\image\bm{\Xi}\times(\image\bm{\Xi})^{*}
.

3.1
Characterizing
(\image\bm{\Xi})^{*}

The mixed formulation
3.3
achieves the primary goal of reducing the maximum smoothness of the involved spaces:
H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\subset H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)^{d}
and
L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}\subset(\image\bm{\Xi})^{*}
. However, the presence of the nonstandard space
(\image\bm{\Xi})^{*}
means that neither the well-posedness of
3.3
nor its discretization are clear-cut. For instance, while it is clear from definition
3.4
that
\image\bm{\Xi}\subseteq{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
, we will show in this section that
\image\bm{\Xi}\neq{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
. That is,
(\image\bm{\Xi})^{*}
contains objects that are less smooth than
{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
which has repercussions both in the analysis of
3.3
and the development of a Galerkin scheme for
3.3
.

The first step in analyzing the mixed problem
3.3
is to develop a concrete characterization for the image space
\image\bm{\Xi}
. For
(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
, there holds

\displaystyle\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})=\grad\tilde{v}-\bm{\psi}\in{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{d}\quad\text{and}\quad\curl\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})=-\curl\bm{\psi}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d^{*}},

where
d^{*}=1
if
d=2
and
d^{*}=3
if
d=3
. Here, we use
\curl
to denote either the scalar curl of a 2D vector (i.e.
\curl\bm{\theta}:=\partial_{x}\theta_{2}-\partial_{y}\theta_{1}
) or the usual curl operator of a 3D vector depending on the context. Consequently,
\image\bm{\Xi}\subseteq\bm{H}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
, where

\displaystyle\bm{H}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega):=\{\bm{\theta}\in{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{d}:\curl\bm{\theta}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d^{*}}\}\quad\text{with}\quad\|\bm{\theta}\|_{\curl}^{2}:=\|\bm{\theta}\|^{2}+\|\curl\bm{\theta}\|^{2}.

We shall also need to characterize the traces of functions in
\image\bm{\Xi}
. The tangential trace operator
\Tr
given by

\displaystyle\bm{H}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)\ni\bm{\theta}\mapsto\Tr\bm{\theta}:=\begin{cases}\bm{t}\cdot\bm{\theta}|_{\Gamma}&\text{if }d=2\\
\bm{\pi}_{T}\bm{\theta}|_{\Gamma}&\text{if }d=3\end{cases}\in H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)^{d^{*}}

is well-defined on
\bm{H}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
(see e.g.
[
31
, p. 27 Theorem 2.5]
for the case
d=2
and
[
32
,
33
]
for the case
d=3
). For any
\hat{w}\in C^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})^{d^{*}}
with
\hat{w}|_{\Gamma_{f}}=0
, there holds

\displaystyle\langle\Tr\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi}),\hat{w}\rangle_{\Gamma}=\langle\Tr\grad\tilde{v},\hat{w}\rangle_{\Gamma}+(\Tr\bm{\psi},\hat{w})_{\Gamma}=\begin{cases}-(\tilde{v},\bm{t}\cdot\grad\hat{w})_{\Gamma}=0&d=2,\\
-(\tilde{v},\dive\bm{\pi}_{T}\hat{w})_{\Gamma}=0&d=3,\end{cases}

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\Gamma}
denotes the extension of the
L^{2}(\Gamma)^{d^{*}}
inner-product to the trace space and its dual. By density (see e.g.
[
34
, Theorem 3.1]
), we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\Tr\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi}),\hat{w}\rangle_{\Gamma}=0\qquad\forall\hat{w}\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{d^{*}}:w|_{\Gamma_{f}}\equiv 0.

As a result,
\image\bm{\Xi}
is contained in the closed subspace of
\bm{H}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
consisting of functions with vanishing tangential trace on
\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{f}
:

\displaystyle\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega):=\{\bm{\theta}\in\bm{H}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega):\langle\Tr\bm{\theta},\hat{w}\rangle_{\Gamma}=0\quad\forall\hat{w}\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{d^{*}}:w|_{\Gamma_{f}}\equiv 0\}.

The following result shows that in fact
\image\bm{\Xi}
can be identified with
\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
:

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
\bm{\theta}\in\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
, there exists
(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})=\bm{\theta}\quad\text{and}\quad C_{1}\left(\|\tilde{v}\|_{1}+\|\bm{\psi}\|_{1}\right)\leq\|\bm{\theta}\|_{\curl}\leq C_{2}\left(\|\tilde{v}\|_{1}+\|\bm{\psi}\|_{1}\right),

(3.5)

where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are positive constants independent of
\bm{\theta}
. Consequently,
\image\bm{\Xi}=\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
d=3
. Thanks to
[
27
, Theorem 5.2]
, for every
\bm{\theta}\in\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
, there exists
(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)^{3}\subset H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
such that
3.5
holds. The same arguments can be appropriately adjusted for the case
d=2
. Thus,
\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)\subseteq\image\bm{\Xi}
. The result now follows on noting that we have already shown that
\image\bm{\Xi}\subseteq\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
.
∎

Thanks to
Lemma

3.1
, we have
\image\bm{\Xi}=\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
, and so
\bm{\Phi}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)=(\image\bm{\Xi})^{*}=\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)^{*}
, which we equip with the standard dual norm

\displaystyle\|\bm{\eta}\|_{*}:=\sup_{\bm{0}\neq\bm{\theta}\in\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)}\frac{\langle\bm{\theta},\bm{\eta}\rangle}{\|\bm{\theta}\|_{\curl}}\qquad\forall\bm{\eta}\in\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)^{*}.

The following inf-sup condition will be useful in establishing the well-posedness of
3.3
:

Corollary 3.1
.

There holds

\displaystyle\beta:=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\eta}\in\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)^{*}\\
\bm{\eta}\neq\bm{0}\end{subarray}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\\
(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\neq(0,\bm{0})\end{subarray}}\frac{\langle\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi}),\bm{\eta}\rangle}{(\|\tilde{v}\|_{1}+\|\bm{\psi}\|_{1})\|\bm{\eta}\|_{*}}>0.

(3.6)

Proof.

Let
\bm{\eta}\in\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)^{*}
be given and let
\bm{\theta}\in\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
be its Riesz representer. By
Lemma

3.1
, there exists
(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
such that
\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})=\bm{\theta}
and
\|\tilde{v}\|_{1}+\|\bm{\psi}\|_{1}\leq C\|\bm{\theta}\|_{\curl}=\|\bm{\eta}\|_{*}
. Inequality
3.6
now follows since

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi}),\bm{\eta}\rangle}{(\|\tilde{v}\|_{1}+\|\bm{\psi}\|_{1})\|\bm{\eta}\|_{*}}\geq C^{-1}\frac{\langle\bm{\theta},\bm{\eta}\rangle}{\|\bm{\eta}\|_{*}^{2}}=C^{-1}.

∎

3.2
Characterizing
\Kernel\bm{\Xi}

The kernel of
\bm{\Xi}
is defined by

\displaystyle\Kernel\bm{\Xi}:=\{(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega):\langle\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi}),\bm{\eta}\rangle=0\ \forall\bm{\eta}\in\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)^{*}\}

and has the following simple characterization:

Lemma 3.2
.

There holds

\displaystyle\Kernel\bm{\Xi}=\{(w,\grad w):w\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\}.

(3.7)

Proof.

Let
(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in\Kernel\bm{\Xi}
. Then, as shown above,
\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
, and
\langle\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi}),\bm{\eta}\rangle=0
for all
\bm{\eta}\in\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)^{*}
. Hence,
\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})=\bm{0}
or, equally well,
\grad\tilde{v}=\bm{\psi}
, and so
\tilde{v}\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
. Thus,
\Kernel\bm{\Xi}\subseteq\{(w,\grad w):w\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\}
. The reverse inclusion follows by definition, and so
3.7
holds.
∎

3.3
Well-posedness

Thanks to the well-posedness of
2.1
, there exists
w\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
satisfying
2.1
. Consequently,
(\tilde{w},\bm{\gamma})=(w,\grad w)\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
satisfies
3.3a
for all
(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in\Kernel\bm{\Xi}
and satisfies
3.3b
. The following result establishes the well-posedness of the mixed formulation
3.3
in the case of general data:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
L\in(H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega))^{*}
and
\bm{g}\in\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
. Then, there exists a unique solution to the following mixed problem: Find
(\tilde{w},\bm{\gamma})\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
and
\bm{\phi}\in\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)^{*}
such that

\displaystyle a(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\psi})+c(\tilde{w},\tilde{v})+\langle\bm{\Xi}(v,\bm{\psi}),\bm{\phi}\rangle

\displaystyle=L(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\qquad

\displaystyle\forall(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega),

(3.8a)

\displaystyle\langle\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{w},\bm{\gamma}),\bm{\eta}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\bm{g},\bm{\eta}\rangle\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\bm{\eta}\in\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)^{*}.

(3.8b)

Additionally, the solution satisfies

\displaystyle\|\tilde{w}\|_{1}+\|\bm{\gamma}\|_{1}+\|\bm{\phi}\|_{*}\leq C\left(\|L\|_{(H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega))^{*}}+\|\bm{g}\|_{\curl}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{w},\bm{\gamma})=\bm{g},

(3.9)

where
C
is independent of
L
and
\bm{g}
.

Proof.

The proof is a standard application of Babuška-Brezzi theory.
Lemma

3.2
and
2.3
show that the bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)+c(\cdot,\cdot)
satisfies an inf-sup condition on
\Kernel\bm{\Xi}
. The inf-sup condition
3.6
then gives the existence and uniqueness of solutions to
3.8
and
3.9
; see e.g.
[
35
, Theorem 4.2.3]
.
∎

The next result shows, in the special case where the data in
3.8
is of the form in
3.3
, that
\tilde{w}\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
in fact belongs to
H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
and coincides with the solution
w\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
to the primal problem
2.1
:

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
F_{1}\in\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)^{*}
and
F_{2}\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)^{*}
. Then, there exists a unique solution to the following mixed problem: Find
(\tilde{w},\bm{\gamma})\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
and
\bm{\phi}\in\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)^{*}
such that

\displaystyle a(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\psi})+c(\tilde{w},\tilde{v})+\langle\bm{\Xi}(v,\bm{\psi}),\bm{\phi}\rangle

\displaystyle=F_{1}(\bm{\psi})+F_{2}(\tilde{v})\qquad

\displaystyle\forall(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega),

(3.10a)

\displaystyle\langle\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{w},\bm{\gamma}),\bm{\eta}\rangle

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\bm{\eta}\in\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)^{*}.

(3.10b)

Moreover, the solution satisfies
\tilde{w}=w
and
\bm{\gamma}=\grad w
, where
w\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
is the solution to problem
2.1
.

Proof.

The existence, uniqueness, and stability of
(\tilde{w},\bm{\gamma},\bm{\phi})\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)^{*}
and the relation
\grad\tilde{w}=\bm{\gamma}
follows from
Theorem

3.1
. Thanks to
Lemma

3.2
,
\tilde{w}\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
and testing
3.10a
with
(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})=(v,\grad v)
for
v\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
shows that
\tilde{w}
satisfies
2.1
. Since solutions to
2.1
are unique,
\tilde{w}=w
and
\bm{\gamma}=\grad\tilde{w}=\grad w
.
∎

The variational formulation
3.10
can be expressed in a form that avoids the presence of the dual spaces altogether. Specifically, replacing
\bm{\phi}\in\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)^{*}
by its Riesz representer
\bm{q}\in\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
and identifying the duality pairing with the inner product on
\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
leads to the following restatement of
Corollary

3.2
:

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
F_{1}\in\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)^{*}
and
F_{2}\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)^{*}
. Then, there exists a unique solution to the following mixed problem: Find
(\tilde{w},\bm{\gamma})\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
and
\bm{q}\in\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle a(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\psi})+c(\tilde{w},\tilde{v})+(\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi}),\bm{q})_{\curl}

\displaystyle=F_{1}(\bm{\psi})+F_{2}(\tilde{v})\qquad

\displaystyle\forall(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega),

(3.11a)

\displaystyle(\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{w},\bm{\gamma}),\bm{\eta})_{\curl}

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\bm{\eta}\in\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega),

(3.11b)

where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\curl}
is the inner-product on
\bm{H}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
:

\displaystyle(\bm{\theta},\bm{\eta})_{\curl}:=(\bm{\theta},\bm{\eta})+(\curl\bm{\theta},\curl\bm{\eta})\qquad\forall\bm{\theta},\bm{\eta}\in\bm{H}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega).

Moreover, the solution satisfies
\tilde{w}=w
and
\bm{\gamma}=\grad w
, where
w\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
is the solution to problem
2.1
.

The system
3.11
is not the only possible mixed formulation of problem
2.1
. Indeed, simpler mixed formulations are possible
[
18
,
19
]
depending on the particular form of
B(\cdot,\cdot)
and the boundary conditions. For example, in the case of the biharmonic equation
2.6
with boundary conditions
w=\Delta w=0
on
\Gamma
, one can use a mixed formulation based on introducing an auxiliary variable
\sigma=-\Delta w
: Find
(w,\sigma)\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)^{2}
such that

\displaystyle(\sigma,v)+(\grad w,\grad v)

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\forall v\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega),

(3.12a)

\displaystyle(\grad\sigma,\grad u)

\displaystyle=G(u)\qquad

\displaystyle\forall u\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega),

(3.12b)

which can be discretized using only
H^{1}(\Omega)
-conforming finite element spaces. However, the apparently more complicated mixed formulation
3.11
has an unexpected advantage over
3.12
, and other, mixed schemes in that under quite mild conditions, the
resulting discretization produces the
H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
-conforming solution to the original problem

2.1
. This is not the case with other mixed schemes such as
3.12
. The question arises of whether a finite element discretization of the new mixed form might inherit this property at the discrete level and thus provide a way to compute the
H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
-conforming approximation
w_{\mathcal{T}}
defined by
2.5
.

4
Discretization of novel mixed formulation

In order to construct conforming discretizations of the mixed problem
3.11
, we select finite dimensional spaces
\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}\subset H_{\Gamma}^{1}(\Omega)
,
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}\subset\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
, and
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{Q}}_{\Gamma}\subset\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
and consider the following scheme: Find
(\tilde{w}_{X},\bm{\gamma}_{X},\bm{q}_{X})\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{Q}}_{\Gamma}
such that

\displaystyle a(\bm{\gamma}_{X},\bm{\psi})+c(\tilde{w}_{X},\tilde{v})+(\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi}),\bm{q}_{X})_{\curl}

\displaystyle=F_{1}(\bm{\psi})+F_{2}(\tilde{v})\qquad

\displaystyle\forall(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma},

(4.1a)

\displaystyle(\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{w}_{X},\bm{\gamma}_{X}),\bm{\eta})_{\curl}

\displaystyle=0\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\bm{\eta}\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{Q}}_{\Gamma}.

(4.1b)

Here, and in what follows, the subscript “
X
" indicates the dependence of the discrete variables on the finite dimensional spaces
\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
, and
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{Q}}_{\Gamma}
.

Of course, in order for this scheme to be well-posed, some additional conditions must be imposed on
\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
, and
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{Q}}_{\Gamma}
. Fortunately, the derivation of the mixed problem
3.11
in the previous section provides some guidance as to how to choose these spaces. We shall assume the subspaces
\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
satisfy the following condition:

(A1)

\tilde{\mathbb{W}}\subset H^{1}(\Omega)
and
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}\subset H^{1}(\Omega)^{d}
are conforming finite-dimensional subspaces and

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}:=\tilde{\mathbb{W}}\cap H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}:=\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}\cap\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega).

Examples of suitable choices for
\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
will be given in
section

4.3
.

The choice of
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{Q}}_{\Gamma}\subset\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
is less obvious. The space
\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
arose from considering the mapping properties of the operator
\bm{\Xi}
in
3.3
which led to the conclusion
\image\bm{\Xi}=\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
. The situation in the discrete setting
4.1
mirrors that of the continuous problem
3.3
with the main difference being that the operator
\bm{\Xi}
is replaced by its restriction to the subspace
\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}\subset H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
defined by the rule
\bm{\Xi}_{X}:\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}\to\image\bm{\Xi}_{X}
,

\displaystyle\bm{\Xi}_{X}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi}):=\grad\tilde{v}-\bm{\psi}\qquad\forall(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}.

(4.2)

The analysis in the continuous setting suggests choosing
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{Q}}_{\Gamma}
as follows:

(A2)

\boldsymbol{\mathbb{Q}}_{\Gamma}:=\image\bm{\Xi}_{X}
.

While attractive from the theoretical viewpoint, in order to implement this choice in practice, it will be useful to study the operator
\bm{\Xi}_{X}
more closely.

4.1
Characterization of
\Kernel\bm{\Xi}_{X}

The kernel of
\bm{\Xi}_{X}
given by

\displaystyle\Kernel\bm{\Xi}_{X}:=\{(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}:(\bm{\Xi}_{X}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi}),\bm{\eta})_{\curl}=0\ \forall\bm{\eta}\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{Q}}_{\Gamma}\},

has the same structure as the kernel of
\bm{\Xi}

3.7
. In particular, we have the following analogue of
Lemma

3.2
:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
, and
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{Q}}_{\Gamma}
satisfy
(A1)
and
(A2)
and define

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}:=\{v\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}:\grad v\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}\}.

(4.3)

Then,
\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
is a finite dimensional subspace of
H_{\Gamma}^{2}(\Omega)
, and there holds

\displaystyle\Kernel\bm{\Xi}_{X}=\{(v,\grad v):v\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}\}.

(4.4)

Proof.

Since
\tilde{W}_{\Gamma}\subset H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
and
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}\subset\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
are finite dimensional by
(A1)
,
\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
is a finite dimensional subspace of
H_{\Gamma}^{2}(\Omega)
thanks to
3.2
.

Now suppose that
(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in\Kernel\bm{\Xi}_{X}
. The condition
(\bm{\Xi}_{X}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi}),\bm{\eta})_{\curl}=0
for all
\bm{\eta}\in\image\bm{\Xi}_{X}
then means that
\grad\tilde{v}=\bm{\psi}\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
. By definition,
\tilde{v}\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
, and so
\Kernel\bm{\Xi}_{X}\subseteq\{(v,\grad v):v\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}\}
. The reverse inclusion follows by definition, and so
4.4
holds.
∎

4.2
Well-posedness

The space
\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
that arose in
4.3
from considering the discrete kernel provides a convenient way to express necessary and sufficient conditions for the discrete mixed problem
4.1
to be well-posed.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
, and
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{Q}}_{\Gamma}
satisfy
(A1)
and
(A2)
with
\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
defined as in
4.3
. Then, the mixed problem
4.1
is uniquely solvable for any
F_{1}\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}^{*}
and
F_{2}\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}^{*}
if and only if
\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
satisfies the inf-sup condition:

(A3)

There exists
\alpha_{X}>0
such that

\displaystyle\alpha_{X}

\displaystyle:=\inf_{0\neq u\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}}\sup_{0\neq v\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}}\frac{B(u,v)}{\|u\|_{2}\|v\|_{2}}.

(4.5)

Proof.

Suppose first that
(A3)
holds. Since
4.1
is a finite dimensional linear system, existence follows from uniqueness. To this end, let
(\tilde{w}_{X},\bm{\gamma}_{X},\bm{q}_{X})\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{Q}}_{\Gamma}
be a solution to
4.1
with
F_{1}\equiv 0
and
F_{2}\equiv 0
. Choosing
\bm{\eta}=\bm{\Xi}_{X}(\tilde{w}_{X},\bm{\gamma}_{X})
in
4.1b
shows that
\bm{\Xi}_{X}(\tilde{w}_{X},\bm{\gamma}_{X})\equiv 0
. Consequently,
\tilde{w}_{X}\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
by
Lemma

4.1
and
\grad\tilde{w}_{X}=\bm{\gamma}_{X}
. Choosing
(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})=(v,\grad v)
for
v\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
in
4.1a
and applying
4.5
gives
\tilde{w}_{X}\equiv 0
and so
\bm{\gamma}_{X}\equiv\bm{0}
. Finally, choosing any
(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
such that
\bm{\Xi}_{X}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})=\bm{q}_{X}
in
4.1a
gives
\bm{q}_{X}\equiv\bm{0}
. Therefore, solutions to
4.1
are unique and thus exist.

Now suppose that
4.1
is uniquely solvable. Thanks to
Lemma

4.1
, the problem

\displaystyle w_{X}\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}:\qquad B(w_{X},v)=F_{1}(\grad v)+F_{2}(v)\qquad\forall v\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}

(4.6)

is also solvable. Again, since
4.6
is a finite dimensional square system, solutions to
4.6
are therefore also unique, and as a result,
\alpha_{X}
defined in
4.5
is positive. Indeed, if this were not the case, then there would exist
w_{X}\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}\setminus\{0\}
such that
B(w_{X},v)=0
for all
v\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
, which would contradict the uniqueness of solutions to
4.6
.
∎

Lemma

4.2
shows that given spaces that satisfy
(A1)
and
(A2)
, the well-posedness of the mixed problem
4.1
boils down to checking the inf-sup condition
4.5
. As shown in the proof of
Lemma

4.2
, the strict positivity of
\alpha_{X}
is equivalent to the well-posedness of
4.6
.

The variational problem
4.6
naturally arose from the novel mixed formulation and is nothing more than a
H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
-conforming Galerkin approximation to the original primal problem
2.1
. The following result shows that the component
\tilde{w}_{X}
of the solution to
4.1
coincides exactly with this
\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
-conforming approximation to
2.5
, and mirrors the result at the continuous level given in
Corollary

3.3
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
, and
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{Q}}_{\Gamma}
satisfy
(A1)
-
(A3)
. Then, for any
F_{1}\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}^{*}
and
F_{2}\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}^{*}
, the unique solution
(\tilde{w}_{X},\bm{\gamma}_{X},\bm{q}_{X})\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{Q}}_{\Gamma}
to
4.1
satisfies
\tilde{w}_{X}=w
and
\bm{\gamma}_{X}=\grad w_{X}
, where
w_{X}
is the
H^{2}
-conforming
\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}

4.3
approximation defined by
4.6
. In particular,
w_{X}
is independent of the splitting
2.10
of the bilinear form
B(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Proof.

Lemma

4.2
shows that
4.1
is well-posed. The same arguments used in the proof of
Lemma

4.2
also show that for any
F_{1}\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}^{*}
and
F_{2}\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}^{*}
,
\grad\tilde{w}_{X}=\bm{\gamma}_{X}
. By definition,
\tilde{w}_{X}\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}\subset H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
. Choosing
(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})=(v,\grad v)
for
v\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
in

then shows that
B(\tilde{w}_{X},v)=F_{1}(\grad v)+F_{2}(v)
for all
v\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
. Since
(A3)
ensures that solutions to
4.6
are unique,
\tilde{w}_{X}=w_{X}
.
∎

Theorem

3.1
shows that under assumptions
(A1)
-
(A3)
, the mixed formulation
4.1
recovers the
\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
-conforming approximation
4.6
of the original
H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
problem, where
\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
is defined by
4.3
.

Remark 1
.

Let
[\cdot,\cdot]
denote any inner-product on
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{Q}}_{\Gamma}
and consider the following generalization of

: Find
(\tilde{w}_{X},\bm{\gamma}_{X},\bm{q}_{X})\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{Q}}_{\Gamma}
such that

\displaystyle a(\bm{\gamma}_{X},\bm{\psi})+c(\tilde{w}_{X},\tilde{v})+[\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi}),\bm{q}_{X}]

\displaystyle=F_{1}(\bm{\psi})+F_{2}(\tilde{v})\qquad

\displaystyle\forall(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma},

(4.7a)

\displaystyle[\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{w}_{X},\bm{\gamma}_{X}),\bm{\eta}]

\displaystyle=0\qquad

Then,

,

, and

4.3
Choices of
\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
for common

-conforming spaces
\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}

We now show how to choose finite dimensional spaces
\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
so that the space
\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
defined by
4.3
coincides with existing finite element spaces. A useful tool will be the following result that removes the need to explicitly consider boundary conditions:

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{W}\subset H^{2}(\Omega)
,
\tilde{\mathbb{W}}\subset H^{1}(\Omega)
, and
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}\subset\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
satisfy

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}=\{\tilde{v}\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}:\grad\tilde{v}\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}\}.

(4.8)

Then,
\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}:=\mathbb{W}\cap H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
,
\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}:=\tilde{\mathbb{W}}\cap H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
, and
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}:=\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}\cap\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
satisfy
4.3
.

Proof.

Let
v\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
be given. Thanks to
4.8
, there holds
v\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}
and
\grad v\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}
. Since
v|_{\Gamma_{cs}}=0
and
\partial_{n}v|_{\Gamma_{c}}=0
, there holds
v\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}
and
\grad v\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
. Thus,
\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}\subseteq\{\tilde{v}\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}:\grad\tilde{v}\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}\}
.

Now let
\tilde{v}\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}
with
\grad\tilde{v}\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
. By
4.8
,
\tilde{v}\in\mathbb{W}
. Since
\tilde{v}|_{\Gamma_{cs}}=0
and
\grad\tilde{v}|_{\Gamma_{c}}=\bm{0}
, there holds
\tilde{v}\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
, and so
\{\tilde{v}\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}:\grad\tilde{v}\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}\}\subseteq\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
, which completes the proof.
∎

4.3.1
Argyris/TUBA element

The classical
H^{2}
-conforming finite element in two dimensions is the Argyris/TUBA element
[
14
]
consisting of degree
p\geq 5
polynomials with
C^{2}
degrees of freedom at element vertices. The element has been extended to three dimensions by Ženíšek
[
36
]
with
p=9
and generalized to elements of order
p>9
by Lai and Schumaker
[
15
, Chapter 18.2]
:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbb{W}=\{&v\in C^{1}(\Omega):v|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(K)\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}\text{ and }\\
&\text{$v$ is $C^{2}$ at element vertices and $p\geq 5$ if $d=2$, or }\\
&\text{$v$ is $C^{4}$ at element vertices, $C^{2}$ on element edges, and $p\geq 9$ if $d=3$}\}.\end{aligned}

(4.9)

The space
\tilde{\mathbb{W}}
may be taken to be the standard
H^{1}
-conforming continuous piecewise polynomial space, but the space
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}
reflects the additional continuity in the space:

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
p\geq 5
if
d=2
and
p\geq 9
if
d=3
and
\mathbb{W}
be given by
4.9
. If

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{W}}

\displaystyle=\{v\in C(\Omega):v|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(K)\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}\},

(4.10a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}

\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} \{&\bm{\theta}\in\bm{C}(\Omega):\bm{\theta}|_{K}\in[\mathcal{P}_{p-1}(K)]^{d}\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}\\
&\text{$\bm{\theta}$ is $\bm{C}^{1}$ at element vertices if $d=2$, or }\\
&\text{$\bm{\theta}$ is $\bm{C}^{3}$ on element vertices and $\bm{C}^{1}$ at element edges if $d=3$}\},\end{aligned}

(4.10b)

then
4.8
holds.

Proof.

The inclusion
\mathbb{W}\subseteq\{v\in\tilde{W}:\grad v\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}\}
follows by definition. Now let
v\in\tilde{W}
satisfy
\grad v\in\mathbb{G}
. Then,
v\in C^{1}(\Omega)
and
v
is
C^{2}
at element vertices if
d=2
or
v
is
C^{4}
at element vertices and
C^{2}
at element edges if
d=3
, and so
v\in\mathbb{W}
. Consequently,
\{v\in\tilde{W}:\grad v\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}\}\subseteq\mathbb{W}
, which completes the proof of
4.8
.
∎

4.3.2
C^{1}
spline space

Let
\mathbb{W}
to be the space of
C^{1}
polynomials on a simplicial partitioning

satisfying the conditions in
section

2
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}=\{v\in C^{1}(\Omega):v|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(K)\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}\},\qquad p\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.11)

In two dimensions, the space
\mathbb{W}
is often called the Morgan-Scott space
[
26
]
. It is known that
\mathbb{W}
has a local (elementwise) basis for polynomial orders
p\geq 5
in two dimensions and for

in three dimensions
[
15
, Theorem 17.29]
. For smaller values of

, these spaces need not have a local basis; see e.g.
[
37
]
for the case

and

.
Regardless,
\mathbb{W}
admits the decomposition

with
\tilde{\mathbb{W}}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}
consisting of standard continuous piecewise polynomials for all polynomial orders

:

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
p\geq 1
and
\mathbb{W}
be given by
4.11
. If

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{W}}

\displaystyle=\{v\in C(\Omega):v|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(K)\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}\},

(4.12a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}

\displaystyle=\{\bm{\theta}\in\bm{C}(\Omega):\bm{\theta}|_{K}\in[\mathcal{P}_{p-1}(K)]^{d}\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}\},

(4.12b)

then
4.8
holds.

Proof.

The inclusion
\mathbb{W}\subseteq\{v\in\tilde{W}:\grad v\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}\}
follows by definition. Now let

satisfy
\grad v\in\mathbb{G}
. Then,
v\in C^{1}(\Omega)
and so

. Consequently,
\{v\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}:\grad v\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}\}\subseteq\mathbb{W}
, which completes the proof of
4.8
.
∎

4.3.3
H^{2}
-conforming macroelements

H^{2}
-conforming spaces are often constructed using macroelements (see e.g.
[
15
, Chapters 6 & 18]
). Let
\mathcal{T}
be a partition as before, and let
\mathcal{T}_{A}
denote the Alfeld splitting obtained by subdividing every simplex of
\mathcal{T}
into
d+1
subsimplices by connecting the barycenter (or other internal point) of each element to the vertices of the element. Let

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}=\{v\in C^{1}(\Omega):v|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(K)\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{A}\},\qquad p\geq 3,

so that, in the 2D case with

coincides with the Hseih-Clough-Tocher (HCT) space
[

]
. Applying
Lemma

4.5
to the partition

shows that this choice of

satisfies
4.8
with

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{W}}

\displaystyle=\{v\in C(\Omega):v|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(K)\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{A}\},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}

\displaystyle=\{\bm{\theta}\in\bm{C}(\Omega):\bm{\theta}|_{K}\in[\mathcal{P}_{p-1}(K)]^{d}\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{A}\}.

In the 3D case, one could also use the Worsey-Farin splitting
\mathcal{T}_{WF}
obtained by dividing every tetrahedron in
\mathcal{T}
into 12 subtetrahedra; see
[
15
, Chapter 16.7]
for the precise definition. The Worsey-Farin

-spline space for

is then defined by

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}=\{v\in C^{1}(\Omega):v|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(K)\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{WF},\ \text{$v$ is $C^{2}$ at the incenter of $T$}\ \forall T\in\mathcal{T}\}.

Theorem 18.11 of
[
15
]
shows that

has a local basis when

, while similar arguments show that the result holds more generally for

. In fact, it is not necessary to explicitly require

-continuity at the incenter since, as shown in
[

, Remark 9]
and
[

, Theorem 4]

-continuity automatically holds, and so
\mathbb{W}
may be written in the equivalent form

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}=\{v\in C^{1}(\Omega):v|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(K)\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{WF}\}.

Applying
Lemma

4.5
to the partition
\mathcal{T}_{WF}
shows that the Worsey-Farin space may be characterized in the form
4.8
with
\tilde{\mathbb{W}}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}
given by
4.13
after replacing

with

.

5
Algorithmic implementation

Summarizing thus far, we have succeeded in our primary goal of producing a finite element scheme involving at most
H^{1}
-conforming spaces that delivers
H^{2}
-conforming approximations to
2.1
. In principle, by choosing spaces that satisfy
(A1)
-
(A3)
and solving a mixed problem
4.7
(which makes no mention of any
H^{2}
-conforming spaces), we can recover the conforming approximation to
2.1
. However, it is not so straightforward to compute the solution to
4.7
. One problem is that a basis for the space
\image\bm{\Xi}_{X}
in
(A2)
is often not known or available, which means that standard direct discretizations of the mixed problem
4.7
cannot be applied. Instead, we propose to apply the iterated penalty method
[
29
,
41
,
42
]
to solve
4.7
which has the advantage of not requiring the construction of a basis for
\image\bm{\Xi}_{X}
.

Let
\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
, and
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{Q}}_{\Gamma}
satisfy
(A1)
-
(A3)
, let
\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
be given by
4.3
, and let
[\cdot,\cdot]
be any inner-product on
\image\bm{\Xi}_{X}
. We define the following bilinear form on
(\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma})\times(\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma})
:

\displaystyle A_{\lambda}(\tilde{w},\bm{\gamma};\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})

\displaystyle:=a(\bm{\gamma},\bm{\psi})+c(\tilde{w},\tilde{v})+\lambda[\bm{\Xi}_{X}(\tilde{w},\bm{\gamma}),\bm{\Xi}_{X}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})].

(5.1)

Given
\lambda>0
and an initial approximation
(\tilde{u}^{0},\bm{\phi}^{0})\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
, the iterated penalty method for
4.7
reads as follows: For
n=0,1,\ldots
, define
(\tilde{w}^{n},\bm{\gamma}^{n}),(\tilde{u}^{n+1},\bm{\phi}^{n+1})\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
by

\displaystyle A_{\lambda}(\tilde{w}^{n},\bm{\gamma}^{n};\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})

\displaystyle=F_{1}(\bm{\psi})+F_{2}(\tilde{v})\qquad

\displaystyle\qquad-[\bm{\Xi}_{X}(\tilde{u}^{n},\bm{\phi}^{n}),\bm{\Xi}_{X}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})]\qquad

\displaystyle\forall(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma},

(5.2a)

\displaystyle\tilde{u}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\tilde{u}^{n}+\lambda\tilde{w}^{n},\qquad

(5.2b)

\displaystyle\bm{\phi}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\bm{\phi}^{n}+\lambda\bm{\gamma}^{n}.\qquad

(5.2c)

Under appropriate conditions (given below) the iterates converge to the solution to
4.7
:

\displaystyle\tilde{w}^{n}\to\tilde{w}_{X},\quad\bm{\gamma}^{n}\to\bm{\gamma}_{X},\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{\Xi}_{X}(\tilde{u}^{n},\bm{\phi}^{n})\to\bm{q}_{X}.

The rate of convergence will depend on the choice of spaces
\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
and the choice of inner-product
[\cdot,\cdot]
and can be quantified in terms of the following constants

\displaystyle\beta_{X}

\displaystyle:=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\eta}\in\image\bm{\Xi}_{X}\\
\bm{\eta}\neq\bm{0}\end{subarray}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}\\
(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\neq(0,\bm{0})\end{subarray}}\frac{[\bm{\Xi}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi}),\bm{\eta}]}{(\|\tilde{v}\|_{1}+\|\bm{\psi}\|_{1})\left|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\left|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\left|\bm{\eta}\right|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\right|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\right|},\qquad

\displaystyle\text{where }\left|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\left|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\left|\bm{\eta}\right|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\right|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\right|^{2}:=[\bm{\eta},\bm{\eta}],

(5.3)

\displaystyle\Upsilon

\displaystyle:=\sup_{(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}}\frac{\left|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\left|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\left|\bm{\Xi}_{X}(\tilde{v},\bm{\psi})\right|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\right|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\right|}{\|\tilde{v}\|_{1}+\|\bm{\gamma}\|_{1}}.\qquad

as follows:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(\tilde{w}_{X},\bm{\gamma}_{X},\bm{q}_{X})\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}\times\image\bm{\Xi}_{X}
satisfy
4.7
and let

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}:=\frac{5M}{4\beta_{X}^{2}}\left(1+\frac{2M}{\alpha_{X}}\right)^{2},

(5.4)

where
M
and
\alpha_{X}
are defined in
2.11
and
4.5
, respectively.
For
\lambda>\lambda_{0}
, the iterates
(\tilde{w}^{n},\bm{\gamma}^{n}),(\tilde{u}^{n},\bm{\phi}^{n})\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}\times\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
given by
5.2
are well-defined, and satisfy

\displaystyle\|\tilde{w}^{n}-\tilde{w}_{X}\|_{1}+\|\bm{\gamma}^{n}-\bm{\gamma}_{X}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\beta_{X}}\left(1+\frac{2M}{\alpha_{X}}\right)\left|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\left|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\left|\bm{\Xi}_{X}(\tilde{w}^{n},\bm{\gamma}^{n})\right|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\right|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\right|,

(5.5)

\displaystyle\left|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\left|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\left|\bm{\Xi}_{X}(\tilde{u}^{n},\bm{\phi}^{n})-\bm{q}_{X}\right|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\right|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\beta_{X}}\left\{\frac{M}{\beta_{X}}\left(1+\frac{2M}{\alpha_{X}}\right)+\Upsilon\lambda\right\}\left|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\left|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\left|\bm{\Xi}_{X}(\tilde{w}^{n},\bm{\gamma}^{n})\right|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\right|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\right|,

(5.6)

where

\displaystyle\left|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\left|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\left|\bm{\Xi}_{X}(\tilde{w}^{n},\bm{\gamma}^{n})\right|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\right|\mathchoice{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-4.5mu}{{}\mkern-2.5mu}{}\right|\leq\Upsilon\left\{\frac{8}{9}\left(\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda-\lambda_{0}}\right)\right\}^{n}\left(\|\tilde{w}^{0}-\tilde{w}_{X}\|_{1}+\|\bm{\gamma}^{0}-\bm{\gamma}_{X}\|_{1}\right).

(5.7)

Theorem

5.1
shows that if
\lambda
is sufficiently large, then the iterative process
5.2
is well-defined and converges at a geometric rate to the solution to
4.7
. The remainder of this section is concerned with the proof of

, which extends the analysis in

(\tilde{u},\bm{\phi})\in\mathbb{K}^{\circ}

\displaystyle\frac{8}{9}\left(\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda+\lambda_{0}}\right)\|w\|_{2}^{2}+\left(\|\tilde{u}\|_{1}+\|\bm{\phi}\|_{1}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{8}{9}\left(\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda-\lambda_{0}}\right)A_{\lambda}(\tilde{w},\bm{\gamma};\tilde{v},\bm{\psi}),

Proof.

7
Extension to time-dependent problems?

The numerical examples in the previous section show that the
H^{2}
-conforming finite element approximation
2.5
can be readily computed without having to implement
C^{1}
-continuous elements. In this section, we explore the possibility of applying the method to time-dependent problems even though such problems do not satisfy the previous assumptions.

Right off the bat, we run into the problem that the computation of the initial data entails computing the
L^{2}
-projection of the initial data
g
onto the
H^{2}
-conforming space:

\displaystyle w_{0}\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}:\qquad(w_{0},v)

\displaystyle=(g,v)\qquad\forall v\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma},

(7.1)

where
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. The
L^{2}
-inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
is not
H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
-elliptic, and hence does not satisfy condition
2.3
. However, thanks to equivalence of norms on the finite dimensional space
\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
, there exists
\varpi_{X}>0
, depending on the dimension of
\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
, such that

\displaystyle\varpi_{X}\|v\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|v\|^{2}\qquad\forall v\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}.

(7.2)

This means that, at the discrete level, the
L^{2}
-inner product on
\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
satisfies assumptions
2.2
and
4.5
meaning that we can apply our method and solve the mixed problem
4.7
with
c(\cdot,\cdot)=(\cdot,\cdot)
and
a(\cdot,\cdot)\equiv 0
to recover the solution to
7.1
. However, we expect the number of iterated penalty iterations to degenerate as the discretization is refined since
\varpi_{X}
is not uniformly bounded away from zero with respect to the dimension of
\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
.

A similar situation arises in time-stepping, where advancing the approximation in time involves solving problems of the form

\displaystyle w_{n}\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}:\qquad(w_{n},v)+(\Delta t)^{m}B(w_{n},v)=F_{n}(v)\qquad\forall v\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma},

(7.3)

where
B(\cdot,\cdot)
satisfies conditions
2.2
,
2.3
,
2.4
,
2.10
, and
4.5
,
m
is the order of the time derivative of the problem, and
F_{n}\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}^{*}
depends on the data from previous time steps. If the bilinear form
B(\cdot,\cdot)
is elliptic, i.e.
B(v,v)\geq\alpha\|v\|_{2}^{2}
for all
v\in H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
for some
\alpha>0
, then there holds

\displaystyle(\varpi_{X}+\alpha(\Delta t)^{m})\|v\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|v\|^{2}+(\Delta t)^{m}B(v,v)\qquad\forall v\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma},

where
\varpi_{X}
is given by
7.2
. Once again, this means that the conditions needed for our approach are satisfied at the discrete level, so that the method can be used to solve the mixed problem
4.7
to recover the solution to
7.3
. However, the fact that the constants in the assumptions depend on the dimension of the discrete space manifests itself through growth in the number of iterated penalty iterations as
\Delta t\to 0
or as the discretization is refined. Nevertheless, the method can be applied to time-dependent problems subject to the above proviso. We illustrate the performance in the case of a dynamic Kirchhoff plate.

7.1
Dynamic Kirchhoff plate

We continue the example in
section

6.1
by removing the point load that was responsible for the initial deflection and consider the resulting vibrations of the plate which are governed by the problem: Find
w(t)\in\mathbb{W}_{\Gamma}
such that

.

\beta_{X}
is the inf-sup constant defined in

\beta_{0}>0
is independent of
h
and

Theorem

The degree

\displaystyle\bm{BDM}_{\Gamma}^{p-1}:=\left\{\bm{\gamma}\in\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega):\bm{\gamma}|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}_{p-1}(K)^{2}\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}\right\}.

\displaystyle\curl\image\bm{\Xi}_{X}=\curl\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}\implies\image\bm{\Xi}_{X}\subseteq\{\bm{\gamma}\in\bm{BDM}_{\Gamma}^{p-1}:\curl\bm{\gamma}\in\curl\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}\}.

\tilde{v}\in\tilde{\mathbb{W}}_{\Gamma}
,
\bm{\psi}\in\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}
, and
8.6
. Consequently,
\{\bm{\gamma}\in\bm{BDM}_{\Gamma}^{p-1}:\curl\bm{\gamma}\in\curl\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}\}\subseteq\image\bm{\Xi}_{X}
, and so
\image\bm{\Xi}_{X}=\{\bm{\gamma}\in\bm{BDM}_{\Gamma}^{p-1}:\curl\bm{\gamma}\in\curl\boldsymbol{\mathbb{G}}_{\Gamma}\}

∎

9
Relationship to previous work

In previous work
[
16
]
, we considered the special case that
d=2
,
c(\cdot,\cdot)\equiv 0
for which the mixed problem
3.11
can be written in an alternative form that avoids the vector-valued space
\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
. In this section, we show that the current method can be regarded as a generalization of the method in
[
16
]
to the cases
d\in\{2,3\}
and where
c(\cdot,\cdot)
need not vanish. We begin with two properties of the space
\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
. The first is an immediate consequence of
Theorem

8.2
:

(P1)

For all
r\in L^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
and
\vec{\kappa}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{f}-1}
, there exists
\bm{\psi}\in\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
satisfying
\curl\bm{\psi}=r
,
(\bm{t}\cdot\bm{\psi},1)_{\Gamma_{f}^{(i)}}=\kappa_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq N_{f}-1
, and
\|\bm{\psi}\|_{1}\leq C(\|r\|+|\vec{\kappa}|)
,
where
L^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
is defined as in
8.2
.

The second property, which follows from
[
31
, p. 37, Theorem 3.1]
, characterizes the gradients of
H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
functions in terms of curl-free vector-fields in
\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
:

(P2)

\grad H^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)=\{\bm{\gamma}\in\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega):\curl\bm{\gamma}\equiv 0\text{ and }(\bm{t}\cdot\bm{\gamma},1)_{\Gamma_{f}^{(i)}}=0,\ 1\leq i\leq N_{f}-1\}
.

With these properties in hand, we have the following representation result:

Lemma 9.1
.

For every pair
(r,\vec{\kappa})\in L^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{f}-1}
, there exists a unique vector-field

\displaystyle\bm{\zeta}\in(\grad H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega))^{\perp}:=\{\bm{\phi}\in\image\bm{\Xi}:(\bm{\phi},\grad\tilde{v})_{\curl}=0\quad\forall\tilde{v}\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\},

(9.1)

satisfying

\displaystyle\tilde{b}(\bm{\eta};r,\vec{\kappa}):=(r,\curl\bm{\eta})+\sum_{i=1}^{N_{f}-1}(\bm{t}\cdot\bm{\eta},\kappa_{i})_{\Gamma_{f}^{(i)}}=-(\bm{\zeta},\bm{\eta})_{\curl}\qquad\forall\bm{\eta}\in\image\bm{\Xi}.

(9.2)

Conversely, for every
\bm{\zeta}\in(\grad H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega))^{\perp}
, there exists a unique pair
(r,\vec{\kappa})\in L^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{f}-1}
satisfying
9.2
.

Proof.

Let
(r,\vec{\kappa})\in L^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{f}-1}
be given. Thanks to the trace theorem, there holds

\displaystyle|\tilde{b}(\bm{\eta};r,\vec{\kappa})|\leq C(\|r\|+|\vec{\kappa}|)\|\bm{\eta}\|_{\curl}\qquad\forall\bm{\eta}\in\image\bm{\Xi}=\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega),

and so the mapping
\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)\ni\bm{\eta}\mapsto\tilde{b}(\bm{\eta};r,\vec{\kappa})
is a continuous linear functional on
\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
. By the Riesz representation theorem, there exists
\bm{\zeta}\in\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
satisfying

9.2
and
\|\bm{\zeta}\|_{\curl}\leq C(\|r\|+|\vec{\kappa}|)
. Since
\tilde{b}(\grad\tilde{v};r,\vec{\kappa})=0
for all
\tilde{v}\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
, there holds
\bm{\zeta}\in(\grad H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega))^{\perp}
. Moreover, the the linear mapping
(r,\vec{\kappa})\mapsto\bm{\zeta}
is injective since if
\tilde{b}(\bm{\eta};r,\vec{\kappa})=0
for all
\bm{\eta}\in\bm{H}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{curl};\Omega)
, then
(P1)
shows that
r\equiv 0
and
\vec{\kappa}=\vec{0}
.

Now let
\bm{\zeta}\in(\grad H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega))^{\perp}
be given. Thanks to
(P1)
, there exists
\tilde{\beta}>0
such that

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}(r,\vec{\kappa})\in L^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{f}-1}\\
(r,\vec{\kappa})\neq(0,\vec{0})\end{subarray}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\theta}\in\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)\\
\bm{\theta}\neq\bm{0}\end{subarray}}\frac{\tilde{b}(\bm{\theta};r,\vec{\kappa})}{(\|r\|+|\vec{\kappa}|)\|\bm{\theta}\|_{1}}\geq\tilde{\beta},

and so there exists
r\in L^{2}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
and
\vec{\kappa}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{f}-1}
satisfying

\displaystyle\tilde{b}(\bm{\psi};r,\vec{\kappa})=-(\bm{\zeta},\bm{\psi})_{\curl}\qquad\forall\bm{\psi}\in\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega).

Now let
\bm{\eta}\in\image\bm{\Xi}
be decomposed as in
Lemma

3.1
: i.e.
\bm{\eta}=\grad\tilde{v}+\bm{\psi}
, where
\tilde{v}\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
and
\bm{\psi}\in\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
. Then, there holds

\displaystyle\tilde{b}(\bm{\eta};r,\vec{\kappa})=\tilde{b}(\grad\tilde{v};r,\vec{\kappa})+\tilde{b}(\bm{\psi};r,\vec{\kappa})=-(\bm{\zeta},\bm{\psi})_{\curl}=-(\bm{\zeta},\bm{\eta})_{\curl},

where we used that
\tilde{b}(\cdot;r,\vec{\kappa})
and
(\bm{\zeta},\cdot)_{\curl}
vanish on
\grad H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
. As a result,
9.2
holds, and we have shown that the continuous mapping
(r,\vec{\kappa})\mapsto\bm{\zeta}
is bijective.
∎

\displaystyle\forall\bm{\psi}\in\bm{G}_{\Gamma}(\Omega),

\displaystyle\tilde{b}(\bm{\gamma};s,\vec{\mu})

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the control properties of the linearized compressible Navier-Stokes system with Maxwell’s law around a constant steady state ( ρ s , u s , 0 ) , ρ s > 0 , u s > 0 (\rho_{s},u_{s},0),\rho_{s}>0,u_{s}>0 in the interval ( 0 , 2 ​ π ) (0,2\pi) with periodic boundary data. We explore the exact controllability of the coupled system by means of a localized interior control acting in any of the equations when time is large enough. We also study the boundary exact controllability of the linearized system using a single control force when the time is sufficiently large. In both cases, we prove the exact controllability of the system in the space L 2 ​ ( 0 , 2 ​ π ) × L 2 ​ ( 0 , 2 ​ π ) × L 2 ​ ( 0 , 2 ​ π ) L^{2}(0,2\pi)\times L^{2}(0,2\pi)\times L^{2}(0,2\pi) . We establish the exact controllability results by proving an observability inequality with the help of an Ingham-type inequality. Moreover, we prove that the system is exactly controllable at any time if the control acts everywhere in the domain in any of the equations. Next, we prove the small time lack of controllability of the concerned system. Further, using a Gramian-based approach demonstrated by Urquiza, we prove the exponential stabilizability of the corresponding closed-loop system with an arbitrary prescribed decay rate using boundary feedback control law.

Let us consider the one-dimensional compressible Navier-Stokes system in the domain

(1.1)

\left.\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\hat{\rho}+\partial_{x}(\hat{\rho}\hat{u})=0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{ in }(0,T)\times(0,2\pi),\\
&\partial_{t}\left({\hat{\rho}\hat{u}}\right)+\partial_{x}\left({\hat{\rho}\hat{u}^{2}}\right)+\partial_{x}p=\partial_{x}\hat{S}\qquad\text{in }(0,T)\times(0,2\pi),\end{aligned}\right\}

1.1
)
1

1.1
)

(1.2)

Here

In this paper, we study the control aspects of the one-dimensional compressible Navier-Stokes system with Maxwell’s law and with periodic boundary conditions

1.2.
Interior controllability

At first, we consider the case when
f_{2}=0=f_{3}
in (

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(t,x)\>{\rm d}x\right)=0,\quad\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}S(t,x)e^{\frac{t}{\kappa}}\>{\rm d}x\right)=0,

\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(T,x)\>{\rm d}x=\int_{0}^{2\pi}u_{0}(x)\>{\rm d}x,\quad\int_{0}^{2\pi}S(T,x)e^{\frac{T}{\kappa}}\>{\rm d}x=\int_{0}^{2\pi}S_{0}(x)\>{\rm d}x.

\dot{L}^{2}\left(0,2\pi\right)=\left\{f\in L^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}):\>f(x+2\pi)=f(x),\text{ for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R},\>\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(x)\,{\rm d}x=0\right\}.

1.4.
Methodology of the proof of main results

The proof of the exact controllability result relies on an observability inequality and the spectral analysis of the linearized operator. The spectrum of the linear operator consists of three sequences of complex eigenvalues whose real parts converge to three distinct finite numbers, and the imaginary parts behave as
n
for
|n|\rightarrow\infty
(see,
Section

2.1
). Therefore the system behaves like a hyperbolic system. Hence the geometric control condition is needed for the controllability of the system using localized control. Moreover, the eigenfunctions of the linearized operator and its adjoint form Riesz bases on
L^{2}(0,2\pi)\times L^{2}(0,2\pi)\times L^{2}(0,2\pi)
. Then using the series representation of the solution of the adjoint problem and a hyperbolic type Ingham inequality, we can prove
Theorem

1.2
. The proof of the Ingham-type inequality relies on the construction of a family biorthogonal to the family of exponentials
\{e^{-{\lambda_{k}^{l}}t},k\in\mathbb{Z}^{*},l=1,2,3\}
, see
Section

7
. The technique we have adapted is inspired from
[
30
]
and
[
6
]
. The entire study relies on the theory of logarithmic integral and complex analysis. One can refer to the book
[
44
]
for more details. Moreover, it is worthwhile to mention that a modification (for example
[
11
, Proposition 3.1]
or
[
1
, Proposition 8.1]
) to the general hyperbolic Ingham inequality (see
[
24
]
,
[
31
, Theorem 4.1]
) is not enough to prove the required Ingham-type inequality for our case.

The controllability result (
Theorem

1.5
) is established using a direct approach: constructing the control explicitly to bring the system’s solution at rest at any given finite time
T
. Since the eigenfunctions (or the generalized eigenfunctions) of the linearized operator form a Riesz Basis, the system (
1.4
) with control acting everywhere in the domain can be projected onto each finite dimensional eigenspaces for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. For any given time
T>0
,
we can achieve null controllability of this finite-dimensional system using the ‘Hautus lemma’. Furthermore, constructing the control using the finite-dimensional controllability operator can be achieved alongside a uniform estimate that is independent of
n
(see
3.6
). Next, summing up these finite dimensional controls obtained for each projected system, we construct a control for the whole system and we show that the control brings the solution of the whole system to rest at time
T>0
. This approach closely resembles that utilized in
[
12
]
.

1.5.
Stabilizability

In this paper, we also study the boundary feedback stabilization of linearized compressible Navier-Stokes system with Maxwell’s law (
1.8
) with the control acting only in the any boundary of density (
1.9
), velocity (
1.10
) and stress (
1.11
). More precisely, we construct a feedback control law which forces the solution of (

)-(
1.9
) to decay exponentially towards the origin with any order.

In our case, we prove the exponential stabilization result by an explicit feedback law following a method demonstrated by J.M. Urquiza
[
38
]
which relies on the Gramian approach and the Riccati equations, see
[
40
]
,
[
26
]
for more details. This method addresses the exponential stabilization issue for an exactly controllable system with an operator that generates an infinitesimal group of continuous operator. This approach has been successfully utilized to study rapid exponential stabilization of KdV equation
[
8
]
, Boussinesq system of KdV-KdV type in
[
7
]
, linearized compressible Navier-Stokes equation in the case of creeping flow in
[
9
]
. Our work regarding exponential stabilization is inspired from
[
8
]
,
[
7
]
and
[
9
]
. Recently, classical moment method has been extensively utilized to establish the exact controllability and stabilizability of some dispersive system, see
[
41
]
,
[
42
]
. In these works, utilizing the group structure of the corresponding system, the authors conclude the stabilizability from the controllability in some periodic Sobolev spaces.

Let us state the main stabilization result for the closed-loop system (
1.8
)-(
1.9
).

Theorem 1.10
.

The control system (
1.8
)-(
1.9
) is completely stabilizable in
\dot{L}^{2}(0,2\pi)\times\dot{L}^{2}(0,2\pi)\times\dot{L}^{2}(0,2\pi)
by boundary feedback control
q\in L^{2}\left(0,\infty\right)
for the density. That is, for any
\nu>0
, there exists a continuous linear map
\Pi:L^{2}(0,2\pi)\times L^{2}(0,2\pi)\times L^{2}(0,2\pi)\to\mathbb{C}
such that for any initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0},S_{0})\in\dot{L}^{2}(0,2\pi)\times\dot{L}^{2}(0,2\pi)\times\dot{L}^{2}(0,2\pi)
, the solution
(\rho,u,S)
of system (
1.8
)-(
1.9
) with

satisfies the following

\displaystyle\left\|\rho(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}(0,2\pi)}+\left\|u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}(0,2\pi)}+\left\|S(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}(0,2\pi)}

\displaystyle\leqslant Ce^{-\nu t}\bigg[\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,2\pi)}+\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,2\pi)}+\left\|S_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,2\pi)}\bigg],

for all

For any

.

The operator

Proof.

We rewrite
\mathcal{A}:=\mathcal{A}_{1}+\mathcal{A}_{2},
with

\mathcal{A}_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}-u_{s}\frac{d}{dx}\hskip 2.84526pt&-\rho_{s}\frac{d}{dx}\hskip 2.84526pt&0\vskip 5.69054pt\\

0&0&0\\
0&0&-\frac{1}{\kappa}\end{bmatrix}.

Note that

u\\
S\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{\mathcal{{Z}}}\leqslant\frac{1}{{\kappa}}\left\|\begin{pmatrix}\rho\\
u\\
S\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{\mathcal{{Z}}}\quad\mbox{ for all }\begin{pmatrix}\rho\\

6.

Let us recall the control system (
1.8

1.8

q(t)
,

or
p(t)
of the form
\Pi\left(\rho(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot),S(t,\cdot)\right)
such that the solution of the closed loop system (

[

which is the key argument of this section.

6.1.
Urquiza’s method

Let us consider an abstract control system

(6.1)

\begin{cases}\dot{y}(t)=\mathcal{A}y(t)+\mathcal{B}q(t),\quad t\in(0,T),\\
y(0)=y_{0},\end{cases}

where
y(t)\in\mathcal{H},y_{0}\in\mathcal{H},q\in L^{2}(0,T)
,
\mathcal{B}
is an unbounded operator from
\mathbb{C}
to
\mathcal{H}
.
\mathcal{A}:D(\mathcal{A})\subset\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is an unbounded operator and
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
is dense in
\mathcal{H}.
To employ the method of Urquiza, one needs to take the following assumptions on the operator
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
:

(H1)

\mathcal{A}
is the infinitesimal generator of a strongly continuous group
\{e^{t\mathcal{A}}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on
\mathcal{H}
.

(H2)

\mathcal{B}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{*})^{\prime}
is linear and continuous.

(H3)

Regularity property.
For all
T>0
there exists
C(T)>0
such that

\int_{0}^{T}\left|\mathcal{B}^{*}e^{-t\mathcal{A}^{*}}y_{T}\right|^{2}\leqslant C\left\|y_{T}\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}},\quad\forall\,y_{T}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{*}).

(H4)

Controllability property.
There are two constants
T>0
and
c(T)>0
such that

\int_{0}^{T}\left|\mathcal{B}^{*}e^{-t\mathcal{A}^{*}}y_{T}\right|^{2}\geq c\left\|y_{T}\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}},\quad\forall\,y_{T}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{*}).

These hypotheses lead to the the following stabilization result. Its proof relies on algebraic Riccati equation associated with the linear quadratic regulator problem
[
19
]
. Let us introduce the growth bound of the semigroup
\{e^{\mathcal{A}t}\}_{t\geq 0}
of continuous linear operator as follows:

g(\mathcal{A})=\inf_{t>0}\frac{1}{t}\log\left\|e^{t\mathcal{A}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}.

Theorem 6.1
.

[Urquiza
[
38
]
, Theorem
2.1
]
Consider operators
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
under assumptions (H1)-(H4). For any
\omega>\max\left(g(-\mathcal{A}),0\right)
, we have

(i)

The symmetric positive operator
\Lambda_{\omega}
defined by

\left<{\Lambda_{\omega}x},{z}\right>_{\mathcal{H}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left<{\mathcal{B}^{*}e^{-\tau(\mathcal{A}+\omega I)^{*}}x},{\mathcal{B}^{*}e^{-\tau(\mathcal{A}+\omega I)^{*}}z}\right>_{\mathbb{C}}d\tau,\,\forall\,x,z\in\mathcal{H}

is coercive and is an isomorphism on
\mathcal{H}
.

(ii)

Let
\Pi_{\omega}:=-\mathcal{B}^{*}\Lambda_{\omega}^{-1}
. The operator
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\Pi_{\omega}
with
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\Pi_{\omega})=\Lambda_{\omega}(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{*}))
is the infinitesimal generator of a strongly continuous semigroup on
\mathcal{H}
.

(iii)

The closed-loop system (
6.1
) with
q=\Pi_{\omega}(y)
is exponentially stable that is,

\left\|e^{t(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}\Pi_{\omega})}x\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant Ce^{\left(-2\omega+g(-\mathcal{A})\right)t}\left\|x\right\|_{\mathcal{H}},\forall x\in\mathcal{H},

where
C
is a positive constant.

We utilize
Theorem

6.1
to prove the complete exponential stabilization of the linearized compressible Navier-Stokes equation with Maxwell’s law (
1.8
)-(
1.9
) with boundary feedback law.

\sigma^{2}(t,0)=\sigma^{2}(t,2\pi),v^{2}(t,0)=v^{2}(t,2\pi),\>\tilde{S}^{2}(t,0)=\tilde{S}^{2}(t,2\pi),&\mbox{ in }(0,T),\\
\left(\sigma^{2}(T,x),v^{2}(T,x),\tilde{S}^{2}(T,x)\right)=\mathbb{T}_{-T}\left(\sigma^{2}_{0}(x),v^{2}_{0}(x),\tilde{S}^{2}_{0}(x)\right)&x\in(0,2\pi).\end{cases}

Let us define the operator
\Lambda_{\omega}:\mathcal{Z}_{m,m}\to\mathcal{Z}_{m,m}
satisfying the following

(6.4)

\left<{\Lambda_{\omega}U^{1}_{0}},{U^{2}_{0}}\right>_{\mathcal{Z}_{m,m}}=a_{\omega}(U^{1}_{0},U^{2}_{0}),\,U^{1}_{0},U^{2}_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}_{m,m}.

Next we see that

\displaystyle a_{w}(U^{1}_{0},U^{2}_{0})=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2\omega t}\left(bu_{s}{\sigma^{1}(t,2\pi)}+b\rho_{s}{v^{1}(t,2\pi)}\right)\,\,\overline{\left(bu_{s}{\sigma^{2}(t,2\pi)}+b\rho_{s}{v^{2}(t,2\pi)}\right)}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2\omega t}{\mathcal{B}^{*}_{\rho}}U^{1}(t)\,\,\overline{{\mathcal{B}^{*}_{\rho}}U^{2}(t)}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2\omega t}\left({\mathcal{B}^{*}_{\rho}}\mathbb{T}^{*}_{T-t}\mathbb{T}^{*}_{-T}U^{1}_{0}\right)\overline{\left({\mathcal{B}^{*}_{\rho}}\mathbb{T}^{*}_{T-t}\mathbb{T}^{*}_{-T}U^{2}_{0}\right)}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2\omega t}\left({\mathcal{B}^{*}_{\rho}}\mathbb{T}^{*}_{-t}U^{1}_{0}\right)\overline{\left({\mathcal{B}^{*}_{\rho}}\mathbb{T}^{*}_{-t}U^{2}_{0}\right)}\,{\rm d}t.

Therefore from (
6.4
), we have

(6.5)

\left<{\Lambda_{\omega}U^{1}_{0}},{U^{2}_{0}}\right>_{\mathcal{Z}_{m,m}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2\omega t}\left<{{\mathcal{B}^{*}_{\rho}}\mathbb{T}^{*}_{-t}U^{1}_{0}},{\,{\mathcal{B}^{*}_{\rho}}\mathbb{T}^{*}_{-t}U^{2}_{0}}\right>_{\mathbb{C}}\,{\rm d}t.

Thanks to Theorem
6.1
, the operator
\Lambda_{\omega}
defined by (

) is coercive and isomorphism. Finally, let us define the operator
{\Pi}_{\omega}:\mathcal{Z}_{m,m}\to\mathbb{C}
by

\displaystyle{\Pi}_{\omega}(\mathbf{z})=-\left(bu_{s}{\sigma^{1}_{0}(2\pi)}+b\rho_{s}{v^{1}_{0}(2\pi)}\right),

where
U^{1}_{0}=(\sigma^{1}_{0},v^{1}_{0},\tilde{S}^{1}_{0})
is the solution of the following Lax-Milgram problem

a_{\omega}(U^{1}_{0},U^{2}_{0})=\left<{\mathbf{z}},{U^{2}_{0}}\right>,\,\forall\,U^{2}_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}_{m,m}.

Hence we obtain
\left<{\Lambda_{\omega}U^{1}_{0}},{U^{2}_{0}}\right>_{\mathcal{Z}_{m,m}}=\left<{\mathbf{z}},{U^{2}_{0}}\right>,\forall\,U^{2}_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}_{m,m}.
This gives
\Lambda_{\omega}U^{1}_{0}=\mathbf{z}.
It follows that
U^{1}_{0}=\Lambda_{\omega}^{-1}\mathbf{z}.
Thus we have
{\Pi}_{\omega}=-{\mathcal{B}}^{*}\Lambda_{\omega}^{-1}.

Thanks to Theorem
6.1
, rapid exponential stabilization for the system (

\displaystyle\leqslant Ce^{-\nu t}\bigg[\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,2\pi)}+\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,2\pi)}+\left\|S_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,2\pi)}\bigg].

A similar process will give the complete stabilization for the control systems (

7.
Construction of the biorthogonal family and Ingham-type inequality

This section is devoted to the proof of a suitable Ingham-type inequality which essentially helps to derive the required observability inequality. The proof of the Ingham-type inequality relies on the construction of a biorthogonal family of
\{e^{-{\lambda_{k}^{l}}t}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{*},l\in\{1,2,3\}}
. All the constants used in this section while finding the relevant estimates are generic, which may vary from line to line.

Let us assume (

H

). We recall the expressions of the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{A}
in the following manner.

(7.1)

\left.\begin{aligned} \lambda_{n}^{1}=&-\omega_{1}+i\beta_{1}n+O\left(\frac{1}{|n|}\right),&n\in\mathbbm{Z}^{*},\\
\lambda_{n}^{2}=&-\omega_{2}+i\beta_{2}n+O\left(\frac{1}{|n|}\right),&n\in\mathbbm{Z}^{*},\\
\lambda_{n}^{3}=&-\omega_{3}+i\beta_{3}n+O\left(\frac{1}{|n|}\right)&n\in\mathbbm{Z}^{*}.\end{aligned}\right\}

We further denote the following notation:

\Sigma=\{(n,j):n\in\mathbb{Z}^{*},1\leqslant j\leqslant 3\}.

Lemma 7.1

(Gap properties of the spectrum)
.

For any
(n,j),(k,l)\in\Sigma
, there exists
\hat{C}>0
depending on
\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3},\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}
, such that

(7.2)

\left|\lambda_{n}^{j}-\lambda_{k}^{l}\right|\geq\hat{C}.

Proof.

Note that
\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}
are distinct and
\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}
are also distinct. Thanks to the asymptotic behavior of the eigenvalues (
7.1
), we have a large
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for
(n,j),(k,j)\in\Sigma,\text{ with }n\neq k
and
|n|,|k|>N
,

\left|\lambda_{n}^{j}-\lambda_{k}^{j}\right|\geq\left|\Im({\lambda_{n}^{j}-\lambda_{k}^{j}})\right|\geq C\left|\beta_{j}(n-k)\right|\geq C|\beta_{j}|.

Analogously,
for
(n,j),(k,l)\in\Sigma,j\neq l
,
|n|,|k|>N
, we have

\left|\lambda_{n}^{j}-\lambda_{k}^{l}\right|\geq\left|\Re({\lambda_{n}^{j}-\lambda_{k}^{l}})\right|\geq C\left|\omega_{j}-\omega_{l}\right|.

As the eigenvalues are simple, we also find the gap condition (
7.2
) for finite number of the lower frequencies.
∎

Let us first define the exponential type and sine type functions.

Definition 7.2

(Entire functions of exponential type)
.

An entire function

is said to be of

A
if there exist positive constants

such that

\displaystyle|f(z)|\leqslant Be^{A|z|},\,z\in\mathbb{C},

and of
exponential type at most
A
, if for any
\epsilon>0
, there exits
B_{\epsilon}>0
such that

\displaystyle|f(z)|\leqslant B_{\epsilon}\,e^{(A+\epsilon)|z|},\,z\in\mathbb{C}.

Definition 7.3
.

(Sine type function)
An entire function
f
of exponential type
\pi
is said to be of sine type if

•

the zeros of
f(z)
, say
\mu_{k}
satisfy the gap condition, i.e., there exists
\delta>0
such that
|\mu_{k}-\mu_{l}|>\delta
for
k\neq l
, and

•

there exist positive constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
and
C_{3}
such that

C_{1}e^{\pi|y|}\leqslant|f(x+iy)|\leqslant C_{2}e^{\pi|y|},\,\forall\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}\text{ with }|y|\geq C_{3}.

The following proposition states some important properties of sine-type functions (see Remark and Lemma 2, p.164 of
[
29
]
and Lemma 2, p.172 of
[
44
]
for details):

Proposition 7.4
.

Let

be a sine type function, and let

with

•

for any

K_{\epsilon}e^{\pi|y|}\leqslant|f(x+iy)|\leqslant\tilde{K}_{\epsilon}e^{\pi|y|},\text{ if dist$\left(x+iy,\{\mu_{k}\}\right)>\epsilon$},

•

there exist some constants

such that

K_{1}<|f^{\prime}(\mu_{k})|<K_{2},\quad\forall k\in\mathcal{I}.

The main goal of this section is to find a class of entire functions
\mathcal{G}=\{\Psi_{k}^{j}\}_{(k,j)\in\Sigma}
with the following properties:

1.

The family
\mathcal{G}
contains entire functions of exponential type
\frac{T}{2}

\displaystyle\left|\Psi_{k}^{j}(z)\right|\leqslant Ce^{\frac{T}{2}\left|z\right|},\quad\forall z\in\mathbb{C},\,\,(k,j)\in\Sigma.

All the members of

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\Psi_{k}^{j}(x)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x<\infty.

Let us now state the celebrated Paley-Wiener theorem, from which we can conclude about the desired biorthogonal family using the family

Theorem 7.5

(

)
.

[

Let

\displaystyle f(z)=\int_{-A}^{A}e^{izt}\phi(t)\,{\rm d}t,\,z\in\mathbb{C}.

Thus if we have the existence of such family

Clearly,

respectively, for

Also, by Plancharel’s Theorem, we have

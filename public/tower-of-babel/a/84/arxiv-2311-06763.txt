L p L^{p} -boundedness of wave operators for fourth order Schrödinger operators with zero resonances on ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3}
Haruya Mizutani, Zijun Wan, Xiaohua Yao

Abstract
. Let H = Δ 2 + V H=\Delta^{2}+V be the fourth-order Schrödinger operator on ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} with a real-valued fast-decaying potential V V . 1.
Introduction

1.1.
Backgrounds

We consider the fourth-order Schrödinger operator

H=\Delta^{2}+V(x)

on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with a real-valued potentials
V(x)
satisfying
|V(x)|\lesssim{\langle}x{\rangle}^{-\mu}
with a specific
\mu>0
and
{\langle}x{\rangle}=\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}
. Under this condition,
H
is self-adjoint on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with domain
H^{4}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, the
L^{2}
-Sobolev space of order
4
, and its spectrum consists of discrete eigenvalues and the essential spectrum
[0,\infty)
(with possible zero eigenvalue). Throughout the paper we always assume that
H
has no positive eigenvalue.

If
\mu>1

[
23
]
) that the
wave operators

\displaystyle W_{\pm}=W_{\pm}(H,\Delta^{2}):=\slim_{t\to\pm\infty}e^{itH}e^{-it\Delta^{2}}

(
1.1)

exist as partial isometries on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and are asymptotically complete, namely the range of
W_{\pm}
coincides with the absolutely continuous spectral subspace
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{ac}}\nolimits}(H)
of
H
. In particular,
W_{\pm}
satisfy the identities

W_{\pm}W_{\pm}^{*}=P_{\mathop{\mathrm{ac}}\nolimits}(H)
,
W_{\pm}^{*}W_{\pm}=I

and exhibit
the intertwining property

f(H)W_{\pm}=W_{\pm}f(\Delta^{2})
for any Borel measurable function

on
\mathbb{R}
,
where

denotes the projection onto
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{ac}}\nolimits}(H)
. These formulas, in particular, imply

\displaystyle f(H)P_{\mathop{\mathrm{ac}}\nolimits}(H)=W_{\pm}f(\Delta^{2})W_{\pm}^{*}.

(
1.2)

We refer to
[
26
]
for a detailed description on the mathematical scattering theory.

In this paper we are interested in the boundedness of the wave operators
W_{\pm}
and
W_{\pm}^{*}
on

for

. One of our motivations to study the

(​​

)

-

from the same estimates for the free operator

\displaystyle\|f(H)P_{\mathop{\mathrm{ac}}\nolimits}(H)\|_{L^{p}\to L^{q}}\leq\|W_{\pm}\|_{L^{q}\to L^{q}}\ \|f(\Delta^{2})\|_{L^{p}\to L^{q}}\|W_{\pm}^{*}\|_{L^{p}\to L^{p}}.

(
1.3)

If
\mu>9
and zero is neither an eigenvalue nor a resonance of

(see Definition

[

established for the first time that

are bounded on

for

.
In the endpoint cases

and

become unbounded on

and

Furthermore, the weak

were also established in the regular case if

\displaystyle\big|\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3};\ |W_{\pm}f(x)|\geq\lambda\}\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|f(x)|dx,\quad\lambda>0.

(

As is known, the range of values

Our results in the present paper are divided into three cases according to the types of the zero resonances. For the first-kind resonance, we show that

are bounded on

for all

are bounded on

for all

, but not for any

are bounded on

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}x_{i}x_{j}x_{k}V(x)\phi(x)dx=0

for
3\leq p\leq\infty
.

We here stress that, the proofs of these results significantly rely on the thorough analysis of the asymptotic expansions of the resolvent of

[

and Goldberg and Green
[

]

1.2.
The main results and zero resonances

In order to present our main results, we first introduce the definition of zero resonance by Erdoğan, Green, and Toprak
[
9
]
.
Recall that if there is a nontrivial solution
\phi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
satisfying
\Delta^{2}\phi+V\phi=0
in the distribution sense, then zero is an eigenvalue of
H
. In general, there possibly exists a nontrivial resonant solution
\phi(x)
in
L^{2}_{-s}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
with some
s>0
but
\phi\notin L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
such that
H\phi(x)=0
. This may lead to challenges in achieving dispersive estimates or establishing
L^{p}
-boundedness for the wave operator of
H
.

Define
L^{2}_{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):=\langle\cdot\rangle^{s}L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
W_{-\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):=\bigcap_{s>\sigma}L^{2}_{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for
\sigma\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
W_{-\sigma}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
increases as
\sigma
increases and
L^{2}_{-\sigma}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\subset W_{-\sigma}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. In particular,
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\subsetneq W_{-\frac{1}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\subsetneq W_{-\frac{3}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.

Definition 1.1
.

The zero resonance of
H=\Delta^{2}+V
can be classified into the following four types:

(i)

Zero is the first kind resonance of
H
if there exists a
0\neq\phi\in W_{-{3\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
but no

0\neq\phi\in W_{-{1\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that
H\phi=0
holds in the distributional sense;

(ii)

Zero is the second kind resonance of
H
if there exists a
0\neq\phi\in W_{-{1\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
but no

0\neq\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that
H\phi=0
holds in the distributional sense;

(iiii)

Zero is the third kind resonance of
H
if
H
has a zero eigenvalue, namely there exists a
0\neq\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that
H\phi=0
holds in the distributional sense;

(iv)

Zero is a regular point of
H
if
H
has neither zero eigenvalue nor zero resonance.

An equivalent characterization of zero resonances of
H
will be given by Definition
2.4
in Section
2.3
. We also recall the notions of
s
-wave and
p
-wave resonances.
We say that zero is a
s
-wave resonance if there exists
\phi\in W_{-{3\over 2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\setminus W_{-{\frac{1}{2}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
such that
H\phi=0
, and a
p
-wave resonance if there exists
\phi\in W_{-{1\over 2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\setminus L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
such that
H\phi=0
. Using these notions,

•

Zero is the first kind resonance of
H
if there is only a
s
-wave zero resonance;

•

Zero is the second kind resonance of
H
if there is a
p
-wave zero resonance, but no zero eigenvalue. In this case, there may or not may be a
s
-wave resonance;

Remark 1.2
.

Some examples of zero resonance or eigenvalue are constructed as follows:

(i) (
The
s
-wave and
p
-wave resonances
)
If
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is positive and
\phi(x)=c>0
for
|x|>1
, then
H\phi(x)=0
when the potential
V(x)
is defined as
V(x)=-(\Delta^{2}\phi)/\phi(x)
. It can be readily verified that
V\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\phi\in W_{-\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\setminus W_{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Consequently, zero is a resonance of the operator
H
, even in the presence of a compactly supported smooth potential.

Moreover, if we consider a positive smooth function
\phi(x)
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfying
\phi(x)=|x|^{-1}
for
|x|>1
, then we still find
V\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that
H\phi=0
and
\phi\in W_{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\setminus L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

(ii) (
The zero eigenvalue
)
Unlike the case of zero resonance, the problem of zero eigenvalues for the operator

is more subtle. For example, consider
\phi(x)=(1+x^{2})^{-s}
with

and define
V(x)
as

. In this setup, it can be observed that

as

, and
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
while satisfying
H\phi=0
. This demonstrates that zero is an eigenvalue of
H
in the presence of such slowly decaying potential.

We emphasize that these zero resonances are closely linked to the asymptotic expansions of the resolvent

of the operator

near zero energy. More specifically, the solutions of the Helmholtz equation
Hf=0
(called zero resonant states) related to zero resonances can be characterized by the kernel subspaces of certain special bounded operators

,
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. These solutions are manifested in the asymptotic expansion through the projection operators

for

onto the kernel
\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}\nolimits(T_{j})
, respectively (see Section
2.3
below for the details).
Moreover, it is worth noting that these asymptotic expansions play a crucial role in proving the
L^{p}
-boundedness of
W_{\pm}(H,\Delta^{2})
, especially in the lower energy part. This will become clear through the stationary formula
(​​

, which will be presented later in Section
3

Remark 1.5

We emphasize that the absence of embedded positive eigenvalues is a fundamental requirement for establishing

1.3.
Further related results

For the classical Schrödinger operator
H=-\Delta+V(x)
, since the seminal work by Yajima in
[
28
]
, there has been a significant body of works focused on the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the wave operators
W_{\pm}
. This research has gained great importance due to its applications in linear and nonlinear dispersive equations. In particular, in the one-dimensional space (
d=1
), the wave operators
W_{\pm}
are bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
for
1<p<\infty
in both regular and zero resonance cases. However, they are generally unbounded for
p=1
and
p=\infty
(see, for example,
[
13
,
4
,
27
]
).
In the regular case, for dimension
d=2
, the wave operators
W_{\pm}
are bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
1<p<\infty
, but the results at the endpoints are yet unknown (see
[
30
,
20
]
). For dimensions
d\geq 3
, the wave operators
W_{\pm}
are bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
in the regular case (see, for example,
[
2
,
29
,
28
]
). However, the presence of threshold resonances narrows down the range of values for
p
, depending on the dimension
d
and asymptotic behaviors of zero resonant states and zero eigenfunctions (as discussed in
[
5
,
12
,
15
,
16
,
21
,
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
,
35
]
).

For the higher-order Schrödinger operator
H=(-\Delta)^{m}+V(x)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
m>1
, many progresses have been made in recent years. The first result in this direction is due to Goldberg–Green
[
17
]
for the case
(m,d)=(2,3)
, where the
L^{p}
-boundedness of wave operators was proved for
1<p<\infty
if the zero energy is a regular point. Additionally, for the case of
(m,d)=(2,1)
, Mizutani–Wan–Yao
[
24
]
have obtained the
L^{p}
-boundedness for all
1<p<\infty
in the all kinds of resonances and counterexamples at the endpoint
p=1,\infty
, as well as weak-boundedness in the framework of
L^{1,\infty}
,
{\mathcal{H}}^{1}
and
{\mathop{\mathrm{BMO}}}
. For
d>2m\geq 4
, Erdoğan–Green
[
6
,
7
]
proved the
L^{p}
-boundedness for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
if the zero energy is a regular point and the potential
V(x)
is sufficiently smooth. Furthermore, for the case
d>4m-1
, Erdoğan–Goldberg–Green
[
8
]
provides examples of compactly supported non-smooth potential
V(x)
for which the wave operators are not bounded on
L^{p}
if
2d/(d-4m+1)<p\leq\infty
. More recently, the case
d=2m=4
was considered by Galtbayar–Yajima
[
14
]
where the
L^{p}
-boundedness was proved for
1<p<p_{0}
with suitable
p_{0}
depending on the type of the singularity at the zero energy.

It can be observed from these works that the behavior of wave operators are roughly classified into three cases:
d<2m
,
d=2m
and
d>2m
. When
d<2m
, as observed by
[
17
]
, the resolvent has a singularity at the zero energy even in the free case and singular integrals similar to Hilbert transform are appeared in the stationary representation of the low energy part of wave operators even in the regular case. It thus can be expect that the wave operators are generically not bounded on
L^{p}
for
p=1,\infty
in this case (see e.g. Mizutani–Wan–Yao
[
25
]
). On the other hand, when
d>2m
, the singularity at the zero energy of the resolvent is relatively mild, but the high energy part becomes more complicated than the case
d<2m
since the resolvent does not decay (or even can grow in higher space dimensions) in the high energy limit. The case
d=2m
is critical in the sense that it has these difficulties in the low and high energy parts of the wave operators.
In this paper, our focus is to further establish the
L^{p}
-bounds of wave operators that exhibit all types of zero energy singularities for the case
(m,d)=(2,3)
.

7.1.
The proof of Lemma

Here we prove Lemma

by establishing the following four propositions. Recalling that
\widetilde{\chi}
was defined in Section

, we set

m_{1}(\xi,\eta)=\frac{\widetilde{\chi}_{<4a}(\xi)\widetilde{\chi}_{<a}(\eta)}{|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}},\quad m_{2}(\xi,\eta)=\delta_{\xi}\otimes\frac{\widetilde{\chi}_{<a}(\eta)}{|\eta|},\quad m_{3}(\xi,\eta)=\frac{\widetilde{\chi}_{<4a}(\xi)\widetilde{\chi}_{<a}(\eta)}{|\xi|+|\eta|}.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let

L_{j}(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}e^{i\xi x-i\eta y}m_{j}(\xi,\eta)d\xi d\eta,\ j=1,2,3.

Then
|L_{1}(x,y)|\lesssim\langle|x|+|y|\rangle^{-4},

|L_{2}(x,y)|\lesssim\langle y\rangle^{-2}

and
|L_{3}(x,y)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-1}\langle y\rangle^{-1}\langle|x|+|y|\rangle^{-3}.

Proof.

(i)
L_{1}(x,y)
can be written as

\begin{split}L_{1}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i\xi x-s|\xi|^{2}}\widetilde{\chi}_{<4a}(\xi)d\xi\Big)\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i\eta y-s|\eta|^{2}}\widetilde{\chi}_{<a}(\eta)d\eta\Big)ds.\end{split}

It is not hard to see that the functions inside parentheses above are convolutions of Gauss-Weierstrass kernels with Schwartz functions

{\mathcal{F}}\widetilde{\chi}_{<a}
and
{\mathcal{F}}\widetilde{\chi}_{<4a}
, respectively.

Hence, for any
N>0
,

\begin{split}\left|L_{1}(x,y)\right|&\lesssim\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}({\mathcal{F}}\widetilde{\chi}_{<4a})(x-w)({\mathcal{F}}\widetilde{\chi}_{<a})(y-z)\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s^{3}}e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}+|z|^{2}}{4s}}ds\Big)dwdz\right|\\
&\lesssim\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\frac{({\mathcal{F}}\widetilde{\chi}_{<4a})(x-w)({\mathcal{F}}\widetilde{\chi}_{<a})(y-z)}{(|w|^{2}+|z|^{2})^{2}}dwdz\bigg|\\
&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}{\langle}x-w{\rangle}^{-N}{\langle}y-z{\rangle}^{-N}(|w|^{2}+|z|^{2})^{-2}dwdz\\
&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}{\langle}(x-w,y-z){\rangle}^{-N}|(w,z)|^{-4}dwdz\lesssim{\langle}|x|+|y|{\rangle}^{-4}.\end{split}

(ii) For
L_{2}(x,y)
, similarly we have

\begin{split}\big|L_{2}(x,y)\big|&=\bigg|\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i\eta y-s|\eta|}\widetilde{\chi}_{<a}(\eta)d\eta\Big)ds\bigg|\\
&\lesssim\bigg|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{s}{(s^{2}+|z|^{2})^{2}}\mathcal{F}\big(\widetilde{\chi}_{<a}\big)(y-z)dzds\bigg|\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|z|^{-2}{\langle}y-z{\rangle}^{-N}dz\lesssim{\langle}y{\rangle}^{-2}.\end{split}

(iii)
L_{3}(x,y)
can be written as

\begin{split}L_{3}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i\xi x-s|\xi|}\widetilde{\chi}_{<4a}(\xi)d\xi\Big)\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i\eta y-s|\eta|}\widetilde{\chi}_{<a}(\eta)d\eta\Big)ds.\end{split}

(
7.1)

By computing
{\mathcal{F}}\big(e^{-s|\cdot|}\big)(w)\cdot{\mathcal{F}}\big(e^{-s|\cdot|}\big)(z)
, we can see that
L_{3}(x,y)
is dominated by

\begin{split}&\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\mathcal{F}\big(\widetilde{\chi}_{<4a}\big)(x-w)\mathcal{F}\big(\widetilde{\chi}_{<a}\big)(y-z)\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{s^{2}}{\big(s^{4}+(|w|^{2}+|z|^{2})s^{2}+|w|^{2}|z|^{2}\big)^{2}}ds\bigg)dwdz\bigg|.\end{split}

Let
\rho=(|w|^{2}+|z|^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}s
and
\gamma=|w||z|(|w|^{2}+|z|^{2})^{-1}
. Then

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}&\frac{s^{2}}{\big(s^{4}+(|w|^{2}+|z|^{2})s^{2}+|w|^{2}|z|^{2}\big)^{2}}ds=\frac{1}{(|w|^{2}+|z|^{2})^{\frac{5}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{2}}{(\rho^{4}+\rho^{2}+\gamma^{2})^{2}}d\rho\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{(|w|^{2}+|z|^{2})^{\frac{5}{2}}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\gamma}\frac{\rho^{2}}{\gamma^{4}}d\rho+\int_{\gamma}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{2}}{\rho^{4}}d\rho\Big)\lesssim\frac{1}{\gamma(|w|^{2}+|z|^{2})^{\frac{5}{2}}}\lesssim\frac{1}{|w||z|(|w|+|z|)^{3}}.\end{split}

Hence choosing a enough large
N
, we obtain

\begin{split}\big|L_{3}(x,y)\big|\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\frac{1}{\langle x-w\rangle^{N}\langle y-z\rangle^{N}|w||z|(|w|+|z|)^{3}}dwdz.\end{split}

(
7.2)

To estimate the integral in
(​​

7.2
)

\begin{split}&\Sigma_{1}=\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\ |\ |w|\leq 1/2\},\quad\Sigma_{2}=\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\ |\ |w|>1/2\},\\
&\Sigma_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\ |\ |z|\leq 1/2\},\quad\Sigma_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\ |\ |z|>1/2\}.\end{split}

Then one has

\begin{split}\big|L_{3}(x,y)\big|\lesssim\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\int_{\Sigma_{i}\times\Sigma_{j}^{{}^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{\langle x-w\rangle^{N}\langle y-z\rangle^{N}|w||z|(|w|+|z|)^{3}}dwdz=:\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}L_{3}^{ij}(x,y).\end{split}

Thus, it is enough to show that each of

.

For

\begin{split}L_{3}^{11}(x,y)&=\int_{\Sigma_{1}\times\Sigma_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{\langle x-w\rangle^{N}\langle y-z\rangle^{N}|w||z|(|w|+|z|)^{3}}dwdz\\

&\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle x\rangle^{N}\langle y\rangle^{N}}\int_{|z|\leq\frac{1}{2}}\int_{|w|\leq\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{|w|^{\frac{5}{2}}|z|^{\frac{5}{2}}}dwdz\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle x\rangle^{N}\langle y\rangle^{N}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle x\rangle\langle y\rangle{\langle}|x|+|y|{\rangle}^{3}},\end{split}

7.1

Proposition 7.2

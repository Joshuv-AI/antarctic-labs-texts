1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper
A
is a commutative noetherian ring,
\mathfrak{a}
is an ideal of
A
,
m,n
are non-negative integer numbers and
M
is an
A
-module, unless otherwise stated. Grothendieck
[
4
]
, expos
\acute{e}
13, 1.2 conjectured that if
M
is a finitely generated
A
-module, then
\Hom_{A}(A/\mathfrak{a},H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M))
is finitely generated, where
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M)
is the
i
-th local cohomology of
M
with respect to the ideal
\mathfrak{a}
.

The concept of cofiniteness of modules was defined for the first time by Hartshorne
[
5
]
, giving a negative answer to the Grothendieck’s conjecture and later was studied by the numerous authors
[
1
,
3
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
.

An
A
-module
M
is said to be
\mathfrak{a}
-
cofinite
if
\Supp M\subseteq V(\mathfrak{a})
and
\Ext_{A}^{i}(A/\mathfrak{a},M)
is finitely generated for all integers
i\geq 0
.
In
[
11
]
, the authors introduced a criterion for the cofiniteness of modules. In a roughly speaking, the criterion estimates how much a module is close to be cofinite. We recall that an
A
-module
M
satisfies the condition P
{}_{n}(\mathfrak{a})
if the following implication holds:

P
{}_{n}(\mathfrak{a}):\hskip 8.5359pt

If
\Ext_{A}^{i}(A/\mathfrak{a},M)
is
finite for all
i\leq n
and
\Supp(M)\subseteq V(\mathfrak{a})
,
then
M
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite.

We denotes by
\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathfrak{a})
, the class of all modules satisfying the condition P
{}_{n}(\mathfrak{a})
.

We start this paper by describing and getting some explanations of the class
\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathfrak{a})
, especially in the case where
n\leq 2
. We give several examples and results at the beginning of the paper. But, our main aim of this paper is to study the cofiniteness of local cohomology modules when they belong to
\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathfrak{a})
without any condition on the ideal
\mathfrak{a}
or on the ring
A
. The first result is devoted for the case
n=1
as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

\rm{(\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{s1})}

If
\Ext_{A}^{i}(A/\mathfrak{a},M)
is finitely generated for all
i\leq m+1
and
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M)\in\mathcal{S}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})
for all
i\leq m
, then
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite for all
i\leq m
.

In the case where
\dim A/\mathfrak{a}=1
, Melkersson
[
10
]
showed that
\mathcal{S}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})=\mathrm{Mod}\text{-}A
and so the theorem implies that
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite for all
i
whenever
\Ext^{i}(A/\mathfrak{a},M)
is finitely generated for all
i\leq n+1
where
n=\dim M
.

Moreover, we have the following result for the class
\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathfrak{a})
where
n\geq 2
.

Theorem 1.2
.

\rm{(\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{tt})}

Assume that
m
is a non-negative integer such that
\Ext^{i}(A/\mathfrak{a},M)
is finitely generated for all
i\leq m+n
and
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{s}(M)\in\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathfrak{a})
for all
s\leq m
. If
\Ext_{A}^{i}(A/\mathfrak{a},H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{t+s-i}(M))
is finitely generated for all
1\leq t\leq n-1
,
i\leq t-1
and
s\leq m
, then
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{s}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite for all
s\leq m
.

As an application of this theorem, let
\Ext_{A}^{i}(A/\mathfrak{a},M)
be finitely generated for all
i\leq m+2
and let
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M)\in\mathcal{S}_{2}(\mathfrak{a})
for all
i\leq m
. We show that if
\Hom_{A}(A/\mathfrak{a},H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M))
is finitely generated for all
i\leq m+1
, then
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite for all
i\leq m
.
In the case where
\dim A/\mathfrak{a}=2
where
A
is a local ring, Bahmanpour et al.
[
1
]
showed that
\mathcal{S}_{2}(\mathfrak{a})=\mathrm{Mod}\text{-}A
and so this application generalizes
[
1
, Theorem 3.7]
and
[
11
, Theorem 3.7]
. For the case of
\dim A/\mathfrak{a}=3
, assume that
\depth(\Ann M,A/\mathfrak{a})>0
and
\Ext_{A}^{i}(A/\mathfrak{a},M)
is finitely generated for all
i\leq 2
. Then we show that
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite if
\Hom_{A}(A/\mathfrak{a},H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M))
is finitely generated for
i=0,1
.

We give a result about those modules which their local cohomology modules are nonzero only in two consecutive numbers. To be more precise, assume that
t
is a non-negative integer such that
\Ext^{i}_{A}(A/\mathfrak{a},M)
is finitely generated for all
i\leq n+t+1
and
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M)=0
for all
i\neq t,t+1
. Then we show that
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{t+1}(M)\in\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathfrak{a})
if and only if
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{t}(M)\in\mathcal{S}_{n+2}(\mathfrak{a})
.

One of the substantial results in the local cohomology theory and cofiniteness is the change of ring principle. We show that this result holds for
\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathfrak{a})
as well. Let
B
be a finitely generated
A
-algebra and let
M
be a
B
-module. Then we show that
M\in\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathfrak{a})
if and only if
M\in\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathfrak{a}B).

We end this paper by the following theorem which is a generalization of
[
11
, Theorem 2.3]
:

Theorem 1.3
.

\rm{(\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{quot})}

Let
A
be a ring of dimension
d
and
M\in\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathfrak{a})
for any ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of dimension
\leq d-1
(i.e

\mathfrak{a}
of

.

2.
The main results

We start this section by defining a class of
A
-modules which has an essential role in this paper. For the basic properties of local cohomology modules, we refer the readers to
[
2
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
n
be a non-negative integer
and let
M
be an
A
-module. We say that
M
satisfies the
condition P
{}_{n}(\mathfrak{a})
if the following implication holds:

P
{}_{n}(\mathfrak{a}):\hskip 8.5359pt

If
\Ext_{A}^{i}(A/\mathfrak{a},M)
is
finitely generated for all
i\leq n
and
\Supp(M)\subseteq V(\mathfrak{a})
,

then
M
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite.

We define a class of
A
-modules as follows

\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathfrak{a})=\{M\in\mathrm{Mod}\text{-}A|\hskip 2.84544ptM\hskip 2.84544pt\textnormal{satisfies the condition}\hskip 2.84544ptP_{n}(\mathfrak{a})\}.

We observe that
\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathfrak{a})\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})\subseteq\dots
. We also say that
A

satisfies the condition
P
{}_{n}(\mathfrak{a})
if
\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathfrak{a})=\mathrm{Mod}\text{-}A
where
\mathrm{Mod}\text{-}A
denotes the category of
A
-modules.

In the rest of this section, we assume that
\mathfrak{a}
is an ideal of
A
,
n
is a non-negative integer and
M
is an
A
-module, unless otherwise stated. In order to describe the class
\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathfrak{a})
, we give several examples. The first example shows that the top local cohomology modules lie in
\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathfrak{a})
.

Example 2.2
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{a}
is an arbitrary ideal of
A
and
M
is an
A
-module of dimension
d
where
\dim M
means the dimension of
\Supp M
. Then
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{d}(M)
is in
\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathfrak{a})
. To be more precise, if
\Hom_{A}(A/\mathfrak{a},H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{d}(M))
is a finitely generated
A
-module, then it follows from
[
11
, Theorem 3.11]
that
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{d}(M)
is artinian and so, since
\Hom_{A}(A/\mathfrak{a},H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{d}(M))
has finite length, according to
[
9
, Proposition 4.1]
, the module
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{d}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite.

The following example specifies some modules in
\mathcal{S}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})
.

Example 2.3
.

Given an arbitrary ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
A
, by virtue of
[
1
, Lemma 2.2]
,
M\in\mathcal{S}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})
for all modules
M
with
\dim M\leq 1
. Especially, if
\dim A/\mathfrak{a}=1
, then it follows from
[
10
, Theorem 2.3]
that
\mathcal{S}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})=\mathrm{Mod}\text{-}A
. Furthermore, if
\dim A=2
, then it follows from
[
11
, Corollary 2.4]
that
\mathcal{S}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})=\mathrm{Mod}\text{-}A
for any ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
A
.

For the class
\mathcal{S}_{2}(\mathfrak{a})
, we have the following example.

Example 2.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of a local ring
A
with
\dim A/\mathfrak{a}=2
. It follows from
[
1
, Theorem 3.5]
that
\mathcal{S}_{2}(\mathfrak{a})=\mathrm{Mod}\text{-}A
. Furthermore, if
A
is a local ring with
\dim A=3
, then it follows from
[
11
, Corollary 2.5]
that
\mathcal{S}_{2}(\mathfrak{a})=\mathrm{Mod}\text{-}A
for any ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
A
.

In the above example we may have
\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathfrak{a})\neq\mathrm{Mod}\text{-}A
or
\mathcal{S}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})\neq\mathrm{Mod}\text{-}A
.

Example 2.5
.

The Hartshorne’s example
[
5
]
shows that if
\mathfrak{a}
is an ideal of
A
with
\dim A/\mathfrak{a}=2
, then we may have both
\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathfrak{a})\neq\mathrm{Mod}\text{-}A
and
\mathcal{S}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})\neq\mathrm{Mod}\text{-}A
. More precisely, assume that
A=k[[x,y,u,v]]
where
x,y,u,v
are variables and
k
is a field. let
\mathfrak{p}=(x,u)
and
M=A/(xy-uv)
. Then
H_{\mathfrak{p}}^{1}(M)\notin\mathcal{S}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})
and
H_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}(M)\notin\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathfrak{a})
. To be more precise,
H_{\mathfrak{p}}^{i}(A)=0
for all
i\neq 2
as
\depth(\mathfrak{p},A)=2
and since
\depth(\mathfrak{p},M)=1
, we have
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(M)=0
; and hence we have an exact sequence of modules

0\longrightarrow H_{\mathfrak{p}}^{1}(M)\longrightarrow H_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}(A)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle xy-uv.}}{{\longrightarrow}}H_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}(A)\longrightarrow H_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}(M)\longrightarrow 0
. By
[
5
]
, the module
H_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}(M)
is not
\mathfrak{p}
-cofinite and it follows from
[
7
, Proposition 2.5]
that
H_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}(A)
is
\mathfrak{p}
-cofinite, and hence
H_{\mathfrak{p}}^{1}(M)
is not
\mathfrak{p}
-cofinite. We observe that
\Ext^{i}_{A}(A/\mathfrak{p},H_{\mathfrak{p}}^{1}(M))
is finitely generated for
i=0,1
so that
H_{\mathfrak{p}}^{1}(M)\notin\mathcal{S}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})
. Moreover,
\Hom_{A}(A/\mathfrak{p},H_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}(M))
is finitely generated so that
H_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}(M)\notin\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathfrak{a})
.

For the case
\dim A/\mathfrak{a}=3
, we have the following result.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
A
be a local ring with
\dim A/\mathfrak{a}=3
,
\depth(\Ann M,A/\mathfrak{a})>0
and let
\Ext_{A}^{i}(A/\mathfrak{a},M)
is finitely generated for all
i\leq 2
. If
\Hom_{A}(A/\mathfrak{a},H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M))
is finitely generated for
i=0,1
, then
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite.

Proof.

There exists an element
x\in\Ann M
such that
x
is an
A/\mathfrak{a}
-sequence; and hence
\dim\frac{A}{xA+\mathfrak{a}}=2
. It follows from
[
3
, Proposition 1]
that
\Ext_{A}^{i}(A/xA+\mathfrak{a},M)
is finitely generated for all
i\leq 2
. On the other hand, using
[
3
, Proposition 2]
, for each
i\geq 0
, the module
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite if and only if
H_{xA+\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M)
is
xA+\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite. Set
\mathfrak{b}=xA+\mathfrak{a}
and
\overline{M}=M/\varGamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
. Then there is an exact sequence of modules
0\to\overline{M}\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow 0
such that
E
is an injective
A
-module with
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(E)=0
and so
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{b}}(E)=0
as
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}
. By the assumption,
\Hom_{A}(A/\mathfrak{a},\varGamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\cong\Hom_{A}(A/\mathfrak{a},H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{1}(M))
is finitely generated and since
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}
and
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{b}}(N)\subseteq\varGamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
, the module
\Hom_{A}(A/\mathfrak{b},\varGamma_{\mathfrak{b}}(N))
is finitely generated. Since
x\in\Ann M
, we have
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)=\varGamma_{\mathfrak{b}}(M)
; and hence
\Hom_{A}(A/{\mathfrak{b}},\varGamma_{\mathfrak{b}}(M))
is finitely generated. Therefore, the isomorphisms
H_{\mathfrak{b}}^{1}(M)\cong H_{\mathfrak{b}}^{1}(\overline{M})\cong\varGamma_{\mathfrak{b}}(N)
imply that
\Hom_{A}(A/\mathfrak{b},H_{\mathfrak{b}}^{1}(M))\cong\Hom_{A}(A/\mathfrak{b},\varGamma_{\mathfrak{b}}(N))
is finitely generated. Now, it follows from
[
11
, Theorem 3.7]
that
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{b}}(M)
is
\mathfrak{b}
-cofinite so that the first argument deduces that
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite.
∎

The following result establishes a relation between the classes
\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathfrak{a})
and
\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})
where
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
are the minimal prime ideals of
\mathfrak{a}
for
1\leq i\leq t
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{p}_{t}
be the minimal prime ideals of
\mathfrak{a}
and let
\Supp(M)\subseteq V(\mathfrak{p}_{1}+\dots+\mathfrak{p}_{t})
. If
M\in\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})
for each
1\leq i\leq t
, then
M\in\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathfrak{a})
.

Proof.

Clearly
\Supp M\subseteq V(\mathfrak{p}_{i})\subseteq V(\mathfrak{a})
. Now assume that
\Ext_{A}^{j}(A/\mathfrak{a},M)
is finitely generated for all
1\leq j\leq n
. It follows from
[
3
, Proposition 1]
that
\Ext_{A}^{j}(A/\mathfrak{p}_{i},M)
is finitely generated for all
1\leq j\leq n
and all
1\leq i\leq t
; and hence using the assumption,
\Ext_{A}^{j}(A/\mathfrak{p}_{i},M)
is finitely generated for all
j\geq 0
and all
1\leq i\leq t
. Then it follows from
[
3
, Corollary 1]
that
\Ext_{A}^{j}(A/\mathfrak{a},M)
is finitely generated for all
j\geq 0
.
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
x\in\mathfrak{a}
and
\Supp M\subseteq V(\mathfrak{a})
such that
(0:_{M}x),M/xM\in\mathcal{S}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})
. Then
M\in\mathcal{S}_{2}(\mathfrak{a})
.

Proof.

Assume that
\Ext_{A}^{i}(A/\mathfrak{a},M)
is finitely generated for all
i\leq 2
. Applying the functor
\Hom_{A}(A/\mathfrak{a},-)
to the exact sequences of modules
0\longrightarrow(0:_{M}x)\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow xM\longrightarrow 0
and
0\longrightarrow xM\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow M/xM\longrightarrow 0
, it is straightforward to see that

\Ext_{A}^{i}(A/\mathfrak{a},(0:_{M}x))
is finitely generated for
i=0,1
and since
(0:_{M}x)\in\mathcal{S}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})
, we conclude that
(0:_{M}x)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite. But this implies that
\Ext_{A}^{i}(A/\mathfrak{a},M/xM)
is finitely generated for
i=0,1
and since
M/xM\in\mathcal{S}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})
, we conclude that

M/xM
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite. It now follows from
[
9
, Corollary 3.4]
that
M
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite.
∎

For the local cohomology modules of a finitely generated
A
-module of dimension 3 we have the following result.

Proposition 2.9
.

If
M
is a finitely generated
A
-module of dimension
3
such that
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{2}(M)\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathfrak{a})
, then
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{1}(M)\in\mathcal{S}_{2}(\mathfrak{a})
.

Proof.

Assume that
\Ext^{i}(A/\mathfrak{a},H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{1}(M))
is finitely generated for
i\leq 2
. We may assume that
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)=0
and so
\mathfrak{a}
contains a non-zerodivisor
x
of
M
. Application the functor
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(-)
to the exact sequence
0\longrightarrow M\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x.}}{{\longrightarrow}}M\longrightarrow M/xM\longrightarrow 0
gives the following exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\varGamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M/xM)\longrightarrow H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{1}(M)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x.}}{{\longrightarrow}}H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{1}(M)\longrightarrow H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{1}(M/xM)\longrightarrow H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{2}(M)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x.}}{{\longrightarrow}}\dots.

Since
\Ext^{i}(A/\mathfrak{a},H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{1}(M))
is finitely generated for
i\leq 2
, it is straightforward to see that
\Hom_{A}(A/\mathfrak{a},H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{2}(M))
is finitely generated and so the assumption implies that
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{2}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite. On the other hand
\dim M/xM=2
and so it follows from
[
9
, Proposition 5.1]
that
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{2}(M/xM)
and
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{3}(M)
are
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite and by virtue of
[
7
, Proposition 2.5]
, the module
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{1}(M/xM)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite. Thus, it is straightforward to show that
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{1}(M)/xH_{\mathfrak{a}}^{1}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite and hence it follows from
[
9
, Corollary 3.4]
that
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{1}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite.
∎

The following result is the first main theorem about cofiniteness of local cohomology modules when they lie in
\mathcal{S}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})
.

Theorem 2.10
.

If
\Ext_{A}^{i}(A/\mathfrak{a},M)
is finitely generated for all
i\leq m+1
and
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M)\in\mathcal{S}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})
for all
i\leq m
, then
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite for all
i\leq m
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
m
. If
m=0
, then the isomorphism
\Hom_{A}(A/\mathfrak{a},\varGamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\cong\Hom_{A}(A/\mathfrak{a},M)
and the exact sequence
0\longrightarrow\Ext_{A}^{1}(A/\mathfrak{a},\varGamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\longrightarrow\Ext_{A}^{1}(A/\mathfrak{a},M)
imply that
\Ext_{A}^{i}(A/\mathfrak{a},\varGamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))
is finite for
i\leq 1
and since
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\in\mathcal{S}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})
, we deduce that
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite. Now, suppose that
m>0
and the result has been proved for all values
<m
. Considering
\overline{M}=M/\varGamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
, there is an exact sequence of modules
0\longrightarrow\overline{M}\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow 0
such that
E
is injective and
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(E)=0
. The case
m=0
implies that
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite so that
\Ext_{A}^{i}(A/\mathfrak{a},\overline{M})
is finitely generated for all
i\leq m+1
. Thus the isomorphisms
\Ext^{i}_{A}(A/\mathfrak{a},N)\cong\Ext_{A}^{i+1}(A/\mathfrak{a},\overline{M})
for all
i\geq 0
implies that
\Ext^{i}_{A}(A/\mathfrak{a},N)
is finitely generated for all
i\leq m
; furthermore
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(N)\cong H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i+1}(M)\in\mathcal{S}_{1}(\mathfrak{a})
for all
i\leq m-1
. Now, the induction hypothesis implies that
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(N)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite for all
i\leq m-1
and the isomorphism
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(N)\cong H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i+1}(M)
for all
i\geq 0
, forces that
H_{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite for all
i\leq m
.
∎

We now extend the above theorem for the class
\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathfrak{a})
where
n\geq 2
.

Theorem 2.11
.

Assume that

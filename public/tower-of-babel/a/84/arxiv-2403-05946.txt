Shear viscosity of quark-gluon plasma at finite temperature and chemical potential and QCD phase transitions
Wei-bo He, Guo-yun Shao, Chong-long Xie, Ren-xin Xu

Abstract
We explore the shear viscosity of quark-gluon plasma (QGP) in the full QCD phase diagram within the framework of kinetic theory with the relaxation time approximation based on the 2 ↔ 2 2\leftrightarrow 2 elastic scatterings of quark quasiparticles. The temperature and chemical potential dependent masses of particles, including u , d , s u,d,s quarks, their antiparticles, and exchanged mesons, are calculated in the Polyakov-loop extended Nambu–Jona Lasinio (PNJL) model. The results indicate that, at small chemical potential, the value of η / s \eta/s has a minimum near the Mott dissociation of mesons and increases rapidly in the lower-temperature side of the chiral crossover phase transition. At large chemical potential (high density), η / s \eta/s in the QGP phase is dominated by the temperature, and the value of η / s \eta/s is greatly enhanced at lower temperature. At intermediate temperature and chemical potential near the QCD phase transition, the situation is relatively complicated. The behavior of η / s \eta/s is influenced by the competition between temperature, density effect, and QCD phase transition.

An important issue that deserves further exploration is the viscosity of QGP at finite temperature and density as well as its relation with the QCD phase transition.
In this work, we will elaborate on the shear viscosity of QGP in the full QCD phase diagram in the framework of three-flavor PNJL quark model. The calculation will be performed within the kinetic theory with the relaxation time approximation based on the

II.2
Shear viscosity in kinetic theroy with the relaxation time approximation

We shall calculate the transport coefficients in the PNJL model based on the kinetic theory with the relaxation time approximation. The quarks, antiquarks and mesons are taken as quasiparticle in the elastic scatterings. It has been found that the transport coefficients derived in kinetic theory with the quasiparticle approximation are close to the one-loop diagram calculations
based on Kubo relations. In this study, we will focus on the properties of shear viscosity at different temperature and baryon density.

III
Numerical results and discussion

In this section, we present the numerical results, including the Mott dissociation of mesons, the cross section, the flavor dependent relaxation time and the shear viscosity to entropy density ratio in the full QCD diagram. In particular, we will discuss the dependence of the relaxation processes and the transport coefficient on temperature and chemical potential as well as the QCD phase transition.

III.1
Mott dissociation of mesons

The dissociation of a meson involves its physical mass and constitute mass in the QGP medium. When the physical mass of a bound meson equals the constituent one, the meson becomes unstable and undergoes a Mott dissociation (Mott phase transition)
Blaschke et al. 2017
. The corresponding temperature is referred to as the Mott phase transition temperature,
T_{\text{Mott}}
. Above the
T_{\text{Mott}}
, mesons will dissociate into quark-antiquark pairs. From the onset of Mott phase transition the meson decay width becomes non-zero and increases with rising temperature.

III.2
Shear viscosity

We present in Fig.

When the temperature is much higher than that of Mott phase transition, the cross sections decrease since the
\pi

Fig.

IV
summary

In this work, we explored the shear viscosity of QGP in the full QCD phase diagram within the framework of kinetic theory with the relaxation time approximation. The flavor dependent relaxation time is derived according to the
2\leftrightarrow 2
elastic scattering between quasiparticles including the
u,d,s
quarks and their antiparticles. The temperature and chemical potential dependent masses of quasiparticles are calculated in the PNJL model. The masses of exchanged mesons are derived with the random phase approximation.

The numerical results show that, for small chemical potential, the relaxation time for each kind of quark and its antiparticle has a minimum near the Mott dissociations of mesons in the high-temperature side. The relaxation time increases rapidly below the chiral crossover phase transition line. Correspondingly, from high temperature to lower one the value of
\eta/s
has a concave behavior and increase rapidly in the lower-temperature side of the crossover phase transition. The minimum value of
\eta/s
at
\mu_{B}=0
is about 0.1, close to the KSS limit and the value extracted from the experimental data.

At large chemical potential (high density), the behavior of
\eta/s
in the QGP phase is mainly dominated by temperature. The value of
\eta/s
of dense QGP is greatly enhanced with the decline of temperature.
\eta/s
is about 1 when approaching to the critical region from the high-temperature side. The situation becomes relatively complicated for the first-order phase transition. The value of
\eta/s
is influenced by the competition between temperature, density effect and QCD phase transition. The evolving behaviors of
\eta/s
in the QCD phase diagram can be referenced by hydrodynamics to simulate the QCD phase transition.

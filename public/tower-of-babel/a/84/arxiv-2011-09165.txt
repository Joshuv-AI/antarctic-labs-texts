On eigenvalue distributions of large auto-covariance matrices
Jianfeng Yao label=e2]jeffyao@hku.hk, Wangjun Yuanlabel=e3]ywangjun@connect.hku.hk

Abstract
In this article, we establish a limiting distribution for eigenvalues of a class of auto-covariance matrices. The same distribution has been found in the literature for a regularized version of these auto-covariance matrices. The original non-regularized auto-covariance matrices are non invertible which introduces supplementary difficulties for the study of their eigenvalues through Girko’s Hermitization scheme. The key result in this paper is a new polynomial lower bound for the least singular value of the resolvent matrices associated to a rank-defective quadratic function of a random matrix with independent and identically distributed entries. Another improvement in the paper is that the lag of the auto-covariance matrices can grow to infinity with the matrix dimension.

Introduction

Most of matrix ensembles studied in random matrix theory are either Hermitian or unitary. General random matrices without such symmetry or invariance are much less studied as their eigenvalues can lay everywhere in the complex plane. Typically eigenvalues of non-Hermitian matrices are much more unstable than the Hermitian ones, and need new mathematical tools for their study. These tools have taken long time to emerge as illustrated by the history of the circular law. The law states that the empirical spectral distribution of a
n\times n
random matrix with i.i.d entries of mean 0 and variance
1/n
converges to the uniform distribution on the unit disk of the plane. When the common distribution is complex Gaussian and as early as in 1964,
Ginibre, 1964
established the circular law in expectation (although the abstract of the paper ended with a quite confusing statement that “the limit of the eigenvalue density as
n\to\infty
is constant over the whole complex plane”). The real Gaussian equivalent of Ginibre’s result was later established by
Edelman, 1997
. The general non-Gaussian case was first tackled by
Girko, 1984
who coined the name of circular law and more importantly, introduced a powerful analytic tool known as Girko’s Hermitization which is followed in all the subsequent papers. His own result remains however controversial as few key steps in his argument, were deemed not fully justified at the time
(
Tao, 2012
, Section 2.8)
.
Bai, 1997
provided a rigorous proof of the circular law assuming a few additional conditions on the moments and density functions of the entries. These non necessary conditions are afterwards removed in several subsequent papers before reaching the final circular law with minimal conditions established in
Tao et al., 2010
. We refer to
Bordenave and Chafaï, 2012
for a more detailed description on these different episodes of the circular law.

One immediately notices that such multi-decade long effort was only about the simplest non-Hermitian matrix filled with i.i.d. entries. Recent progress is made with a more involved matrix ensemble known as
structured random matrices
of the form
A_{n}\circ X^{(n)}+B_{n}
where the random matrix
X^{(n)}
has i.i.d. entries as in the circular law and
(A_{n})
and
(B_{n})
are two sequences of deterministic matrices (the
\circ
denotes the Hadamard product), see
Cook, 2018
. This model includes the class of random matrices with profile
Cook et al., 2018
with
B_{n}\equiv 0
and
A_{n}(i,j)=\sigma(i/n,j/n)
for
(i,j)\in[n]^{2}
and a scalar profile function
\sigma:(0,1)^{2}\to(0,\infty)
, and the class of band matrices with
B_{n}\equiv 0
and
A_{n}(i,j)=0
if
|i-j|>k
for some given bandwidth
k\geq 1
. The limiting distribution of random matrices with profiles is obtained in
Cook et al., 2018
where a key ingredient is a polynomial lower bounds for the least singular value of the associated resolvent matrices established in
Cook, 2018
.

In this paper we study a particular random matrix of the following form. Let
N=N(n)
and
1\leq k=k(n)<n
be sequences of positive integers varying with
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. For each
n
, consider a rectangular
N\times n
random matrix
X^{(n)}=(X_{ij}^{(n)})_{1\leq i\leq N,1\leq j\leq n}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times n}
with i.i.d. complex-valued entries with mean 0 and variance
1/n
. The matrix of interest is

\displaystyle Y^{(n)}=X^{(n)}A^{(n)}(X^{(n)})^{*},

(1.1)

where
(A^{(n)})
is the sequence of deterministic matrices with entries
A_{ij}^{(n)}=\delta_{i=j+k}
, i.e.
A^{(n)}
is of the form

\displaystyle A^{(n)}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&0\\
I_{n-k}&0\end{matrix}\right),

(1.2)

The non-Hermitian matrix
Y^{(n)}
originates from high-dimensional time series analysis. Write
X^{(n)}
in function of its
n
column vectors of dimension
N
as in
X^{(n)}=(\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots\mathbf{x}_{n})
. Clearly,

\displaystyle Y^{(n)}=\mathbf{x}_{k+1}\mathbf{x}_{1}^{*}+\cdots+\mathbf{x}_{n}\mathbf{x}_{n-k}^{*}.

In this form the matrix
Y^{(n)}
is seen as the so-called
lag-
k
auto-covariance matrix
of the time series
(\mathbf{x}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq n}
in the space
\mathbb{C}^{N}
and observed at time
1\leq j\leq n
. The case of
k=1
would be of special interest and we denote it as
Y^{(n)}_{1}
. The spectral property of the matrix has a fundamental role for the analysis of the series. For example it helps to test the hypothesis whether the series is a
white noise
, that is the series is indeed an i.i.d. sequence. The limiting distribution of the singular values of
Y_{1}^{(n)}
has been found in
Li et al., 2015
;
Wang and Yao, 2016
; it has been applied to high-dimensional statistics in
Li et al., 2017
;
Li et al., 2019
.

To fix the discussions, throughout the paper the dimension parameters are taken to satisfy the following asymptotic scheme:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{N}{n}=\gamma_{0}\in(0,\infty),\quad\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{k}{n}=\gamma_{1}\in[0,1).

(1.3)

Very few is known on the eigenvalue distribution of the matrix
Y^{(n)}
. Simulation and plots are given in the book
Bose and Bhattacharjee, 2018
for (see Figure 8.1 there).
In a recent paper
Bose and Hachem, 2020
, the authors consider a variant of
Y^{(n)}_{1}
, namely

Z^{(n)}=\mathbf{x}_{2}\mathbf{x}_{1}^{*}+\cdots+\mathbf{x}_{n}\mathbf{x}_{n-1}^{*}+\mathbf{x}_{1}\mathbf{x}_{n}^{*}=Y^{(n)}_{1}+\mathbf{x}_{1}\mathbf{x}_{n}^{*},

(1.4)

and established that the empirical spectral distribution of
Z^{(n)}
converges to a deterministic limiting distribution
\mu^{(\gamma_{0})}
in probability (this distribution will be detailed later).
Therefore the two matrices
Y^{(n)}_{1}
and
Z^{(n)}
differ only by the rank-one matrix
\mathbf{x}_{1}\mathbf{x}_{n}^{*}
. However due to the already mentioned high spectral instability, rank-one perturbations can preserve or destroy the spectrum of the original matrix depending on their nature.
In other words, the existence of the LSD
\mu^{(\gamma_{0})}
for
Z^{(n)}
does not imply anything a priori on the asymptotic properties of the lag-1 auto-covariance matrix
Y^{(n)}_{1}
.

Actually the present paper aims at establishing the LSD for the general lag-
k
auto-covariance matrix
Y^{(n)}
under reasonable conditions.
Note that while in
Bose and Hachem, 2020
, the matrix
Z^{(n)}
corresponds
to the case
k=1
, we allow
k
growing with
n
in this paper.
Technically, by mimicking the methodology introduced in the development of the circular law, the main technical challenge here is to establish a polynomial lower bounds for the least singular value of the resolvent matrix
Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}
for almost all
z\in\mathbb{C}
.
Consider for a moment the method employed in
Bose and Hachem, 2020
for the establishment of the LSD
\mu^{(\gamma_{0})}
for the matrix
Z^{(n)}
.
Note that this matrix can be rewritten as

\displaystyle Z^{(n)}=X^{(n)}J^{(n)}(X^{(n)})^{*},

with the permutation matrix

\displaystyle J^{(n)}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\
I_{n-1}&0\end{matrix}\right).

In a setting where the entries of
X^{(n)}
have a (common) density and noting that
J^{(n)}
is of full rank, the matrix
Z^{(n)}
is of full rank almost surely.
This is a main ingredient for the method in
Bose and Hachem, 2020
to establish a polynomial lower bound for the least singular value of the corresponding resolvent
Z^{(n)}-zI_{N}
(
z\in\mathbb{C}
).
(Note that in the reference such polynomial lower bound is established for general degree 2 monomials of the form
X^{(n)}C^{(n)}(X^{(n)})^{*}
where
C^{(n)}
is asymptotically non degenerated).
This method is broken in our case of
Y^{(n)}
since the inner matrix
A^{(n)}
in
Y^{(n)}
is nilpotent and of rank
n-k
. We thus introduce a specially designed non-degenerated approximation
H(z)\in\mathbb{C}^{(N+n-k)\times(N+n-k)}
to the resolvent with a smaller least singular value than the resolvent
Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}
. A careful analysis leads to a manageable polynomial bound for the least singular value of
H(z)
which is thus easily transferred to the resolvent
Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}
. This construction of a lower bound for the resolvent is indeed the main technical innovation of the paper. It is developed in Section
3
. Note that in the case of the circular law or the structured matrices of
Cook, 2018
, the matrix is linear in its independent entries. In contrary, the matrix
Y^{(n)}
as well as the matrix
Z^{(n)}
is a more involved quadratic function of these independent entries.
Note that complex Gaussian valued auto-covariance matrices were also considered in
Nowak and Tarnowski, 2017
.

The rest of the paper is as follows. Section
2
recalls a few preliminaries and useful results from the literature.
Section
3
presents the main result of the paper, that is, a polynomial bound on the least singular value of the resolvent
Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}
.
Applying this bound leads to the LSD for the matrix
Y^{(n)}
in Section
4
.
The two appendices collect a few useful but standard lemmas from linear algebra and probability theory.

2.1
Compressible vectors and incompressible vectors

For
\theta,\rho\in(0,1)
, we define the set of compressible vectors

\displaystyle\mathrm{Comp}(\theta,\rho)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\cap\bigcup_{I\subseteq[n],|I|=\theta n}B(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{I},\rho)

\displaystyle=\{u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:\exists\ J\in[n],|J|=\theta n,\exists\ v\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\ s.t.\ v_{[n]\setminus J}=0\ \mathrm{and}\ \|u-v\|\leq\rho\},

and the set of incompressible vectors

\displaystyle\mathrm{Incomp}(\theta,\rho)=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\setminus\mathrm{Comp}(\theta,\rho).

The following lemma is the structure of the set of Incompressible vectors, which could be found in
(
Rudelson and Vershynin, 2008
, Lemma 3.4)
or
(
Cook, 2018
, Lemma 2.1)

Lemma 2.1
.

(
Rudelson and Vershynin, 2008
, Lemma 3.4)

For
\theta,\rho\in(0,1)
, for any

, the set

\displaystyle J=\left\{i\in[n]:\dfrac{\rho}{\sqrt{n}}\leq|u_{i}|\leq\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{\theta n}}\right\}

has cardinal number
|J|\geq 3\theta n/4
.

Lemma

Lemma 2.2
.

(

For

\displaystyle J^{\prime}=\left\{i\in[n]:\dfrac{\rho}{\sqrt{n}}\leq|u_{i}|\leq\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{\theta n}},|\tilde{u}_{i}|\leq\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{\theta n}}\right\},

Proof.

Denote

\displaystyle J^{\prime\prime}=\left\{i\in[n]:|\tilde{u}_{i}|\leq\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{\theta n}}\right\},

Small ball probability

The following definition is the small ball probability, which could be found in
Tao and Vu, 2008
.

Definition 2.1
.

(
Tao and Vu, 2008
, Definition 3.1)

Let
Z=(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n})

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}Z_{j},r\right)=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}Z_{j}\in B(z,r)\right).

The following lemma states that the small ball probability is monotone with respect to the dimension.

Lemma 2.6
.

(
Rudelson and Vershynin, 2008
, Lemma 2.1)

For any
r\geq 0
and any index set
I\subseteq[n]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\left(\sum_{j\in[n]}Z_{j},r\right)\leq\mathcal{S}\left(\sum_{j\in I}Z_{j},r\right).

The following lemma is the Berry-Esseen theorem for small ball probability, which could be found in
Bordenave and Chafaï, 2012
.

Lemma 2.7
.

(
Bordenave and Chafaï, 2012
, Lemma A.6)

Suppose that the independent complex random variables
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}
are centered with finite third moments. Then there exists a constant
c^{\prime}>0
, such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}Z_{j},r\right)\leq\dfrac{c^{\prime}r}{\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[|Z_{j}|^{2}]}}+\dfrac{c^{\prime}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[|Z_{j}|^{3}]}{\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[|Z_{j}|^{2}]\right)^{3/2}}.

The following lemma estimate the probability that quadratic form is bounded, which is introduced in
Bose and Hachem, 2020

Lemma 2.8
.

(

3
Least singular value estimation

In this section, denote
C
be a large positive constant and
c
be a small positive constant that may vary in different place and may depend on
z
. The dimension
n
is very large and is fixed. We may omit it in the superscript without ambiguity. Moreover, by (
1.3
), we may assume that
N/n\in(\gamma_{0}/2,3\gamma_{0}/2)
and
k<c_{0}^{\prime}n
for a positive constant
c_{0}^{\prime}<1
.

We impose the following conditions on the random variable
X_{11}^{(n)}
.

(C1)

For
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, the complex random variables
\{X_{ij}^{(n)}:i\in[N],j\in[n]\}
are i.i.d. with mean zero and variance
1/n
. Besides, there exists a positive constant
m_{4}
, such that
n^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|X_{11}^{(n)}\right|^{4}\right]\leq m_{4}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

(C2)

c_{0}=1-\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\left|n\mathbb{E}\left[\left(X_{11}^{(n)}\right)^{2}\right]\right|>0
.

Assumption (C1) is the standard moment conditions on the matrix entries.
Assumption (C2) means that the complex random variable
X_{11}^{(n)}
cannot be supported on a line passing by the origin. This assumption facilitates the least singular value estimation using projection arguments.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that the conditions (C1) and (C2) hold. Then there exists a positive constant
C
that depends on
z
and
C_{0}
, such that for all
n
large,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(s_{N}(XAX^{*}-zI_{N})\leq n^{-37/22},\|X\|\leq C_{0}\right)\leq Cn^{-1/22},

(3.1)

for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
.

Outline of the proof of Theorem
3.1
:  We use the inverse formula for blocking matrices to find an invertible matrix
H\in\mathbb{C}^{(N+n-k)\times(N+n-k)}
, such that
s_{N+n-k}(H)\leq s_{N}(XAX^{*}-zI_{N})
. To estimate the least singular value of
H
, we notice that
s_{N+n-k}(H)=\inf_{w\in\mathbb{S}^{N+n-k-1}}\|Hw\|
. Then we may estimate the infimum over compressible vectors by using an
\epsilon
-net argument in Section
3.1
, and incompressible vectors by using Lemma
2.3
in Section
3.2
(the case
2k+1\leq n
) and Section
3.3
(the case
2k+1>n
), respectively. In Section
3.2
, due to the structure of
H
, we estimate the distance
\mathrm{dist}(H_{[N+n-k],l},H_{-l})
for
N+k+1\leq l\leq N+n-k
in Section
3.2.1
, for
N+1\leq l\leq N+k
in Section
3.2.2
and for
1\leq l\leq N
in Section
3.2.3
, respectively. In Section
3.3
, we estimate the distance
\mathrm{dist}(H_{[N+n-k],l},H_{-l})
for
N+1\leq l\leq N+n-k
in Section
3.3.1
and for
1\leq l\leq N
in Section
3.3.2
, respectively.

To start the proof, we can assume that
X_{11}
has density by a perturbation argument (see
Bose and Hachem, 2020
).
We fix arbitrary
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
. All constants in the proof may depend on
z
.
Denote
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
then we have

\displaystyle XAX^{*}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})A\left(\begin{matrix}X_{1}^{*}\\
\vdots\\
X_{n}^{*}\end{matrix}\right)=(X_{k+1},\ldots,X_{n},0,\ldots,0)\left(\begin{matrix}X_{1}^{*}\\
\vdots\\
X_{n}^{*}\end{matrix}\right)=(X_{k+1},\ldots,X_{n})\left(\begin{matrix}X_{1}^{*}\\
\vdots\\
X_{n-k}^{*}\end{matrix}\right).

Denote

\displaystyle H^{\prime}=\left(\begin{matrix}zI_{N}&(X_{k+1},\ldots,X_{n})\\
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-k})^{*}&I_{n-k}\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathbb{C}^{(N+n-k)\times(N+n-k)}.

Set
H=H^{\prime}
if

\displaystyle H=\left(\begin{matrix}zI_{N}&(X_{n-k+1},\ldots,X_{n},X_{k+1},\ldots,X_{n-k})\\
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-k})^{*}&\left(e_{n-2k+1}^{(n-k)},\ldots,e_{n-k}^{(n-k)},e_{1}^{(n-k)},\ldots,e_{n-2k}^{(n-k)}\right)\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathbb{C}^{(N+n-k)\times(N+n-k)},

if

.

Next, we show that

is invertible with probability

, then it is a polynomial of the entries of

, which implies that

is a non-zero polynomial of the entries of

has zero Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}
. Since the entries of
X
have density,
f(X)\not=0
almost surely. By lemma
A.1
, we have

\displaystyle\left(H^{\prime}\right)^{-1}=\left(\begin{matrix}\left(zI_{N}-XAX^{*}\right)^{-1}&*\\
*&*\end{matrix}\right)

Thus, by Lemma
A.3
,

\displaystyle s_{N}\left(XAX^{*}-zI_{N}\right)=\dfrac{1}{s_{1}\left(\left(XAX^{*}-zI_{N}\right)^{-1}\right)}\geq\dfrac{1}{s_{1}\left(\left(H^{\prime}\right)^{-1}\right)}=s_{N+n-k}\left(H^{\prime}\right)

(3.2)

Note that one can obtain
H
from
H^{\prime}
by permuting the columns. Thus, the sets of singular values of
H
and
H^{\prime}
are exactly the same. Hence, by (
3.2
), it is enough to show

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(s_{N+n-k}(H)\leq n^{-37/22},\|X\|\leq C_{0}\right)\leq Cn^{-1/22},

(3.3)

for all
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
.

Note that for any
\theta,\rho\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle s_{N+n-k}(H)=\inf_{w\in\mathbb{S}^{N+n-k-1}}\|Hw\|=\inf_{w\in\mathrm{Comp}(\theta,\rho)}\|Hw\|\wedge\inf_{w\in\mathrm{Incomp}(\theta,\rho)}\|Hw\|.

Then the conclusion of the theorem will follow from the following two key estimates:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{w\in\mathrm{Comp}(\theta,\rho)}\|Hw\|\leq c,\|X\|\leq C_{0}\right)\leq\exp(-cn),

(3.4)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\inf_{w\in\mathrm{Incomp}(\theta,\rho)}\|Hw\|\leq n^{-37/22},\|X\|\leq C_{0}\right)\leq Cn^{-1/22},

(3.5)

separably for a pair of special
\theta
and
\rho

These estimates are established in next subsections, respectively.

3.1
Estimate (
3.4
) for compressible vectors

We derive the proof of (
3.4
) for the case
2k+1\leq n
first.

For a deterministic vector
w=(u^{\intercal},v^{\intercal})^{\intercal}\in\mathbb{S}^{N+n-k-1}
, where
u\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
v\in\mathbb{C}^{n-k}
, for
0\leq t\leq c
, by Lemma
B.2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\|Hw\|\leq t,\|u\|>1/2\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-k})^{*}u+\left(e_{n-2k+1}^{(n-k)},\ldots,e_{n-k}^{(n-k)},e_{1}^{(n-k)},\ldots,e_{n-2k}^{(n-k)}\right)v\right\|\leq t,\|u\|>1/2\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-k})^{*}\dfrac{u}{\|u\|}+\dfrac{1}{\|u\|}\left(v_{k+1},\ldots,v_{n-k},v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\right)^{\intercal}\right\|\leq 2t,\|u\|>1/2\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{dist}\left((X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-k})^{*}\dfrac{u}{\|u\|},\mathrm{Span}\left\{\left(v_{k+1},\ldots,v_{n-k},v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\right)^{\intercal}\right\}\right)\leq 2t\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp(-cn).

(3.6)

Similarly, by Lemma
B.2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\|Hw\|\leq t,\|v\|>1/2\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|zu+(X_{n-k+1},\ldots,X_{n},X_{k+1},\ldots,X_{n-k})v\right\|\leq t,\|v\|>1/2\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|\dfrac{z}{\|v\|}u+(X_{n-k+1},\ldots,X_{n},X_{k+1},\ldots,X_{n-k})\dfrac{v}{\|v\|}\right\|\leq 2t,\|v\|>1/2\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{dist}\left((X_{n-k+1},\ldots,X_{n},X_{k+1},\ldots,X_{n-k})\dfrac{v}{\|v\|},\mathrm{Span}\left\{u\right\}\right)\leq 2t\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\exp(-cn).

(3.7)

Thus, by (
3.1
) and (
3.1
), for
0<t<c
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\|Hw\|\leq t\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\|Hw\|\leq t,\|u\|>1/2\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\|Hw\|\leq t,\|v\|>1/2\right)\leq\exp(-cn).

(3.8)

Note that on the event
\{\|X\|\leq C_{0}\}
,

\displaystyle\|H\|=

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\begin{matrix}zI_{N}&(X_{n-k+1},\ldots,X_{n},X_{k+1},\ldots,X_{n-k})\\
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-k})^{*}&\left(e_{n-2k+1}^{(n-k)},\ldots,e_{n-k}^{(n-k)},e_{1}^{(n-k)},\ldots,e_{n-2k}^{(n-k)}\right)\end{matrix}\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\begin{matrix}zI_{N}&0\\
0&\left(e_{n-2k+1}^{(n-k)},\ldots,e_{n-k}^{(n-k)},e_{1}^{(n-k)},\ldots,e_{n-2k}^{(n-k)}\right)\end{matrix}\right)\right\|

\displaystyle+\left\|\left(\begin{matrix}0&(X_{n-k+1},\ldots,X_{n},X_{k+1},\ldots,X_{n-k})\\
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-k})^{*}&0\end{matrix}\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|z|+1+C_{0}.

(3.9)

Thus, we choose
r_{H}
to be a small number satisfying

\displaystyle r_{H}<\dfrac{s_{0}}{4(|z|+1+C_{0})},

then by (
3.8
), we can choose
s_{0}<c
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\exists w^{\prime}\in B(w,2r_{H}):\|Hw^{\prime}\|\leq s_{0}/2,\|X\|\leq C_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\|Hw\|\leq s_{0},\|X\|\leq C_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-cn),

Note that for small
\theta>0
that will be determined in the sequel, and
I\subseteq[N+n-k]
with
|I|=\theta(N+n-k)
, by Lemma
2.5
, the set of unit vector in
\mathbb{S}^{N+n-k-1}
supported in
I
has a

-net of cardinality bounded by
(3/r_{H})^{2|I|}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\exists w^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Comp}(\theta,r_{H}):\|Hw^{\prime}\|\leq s_{0}/2,\|X\|\leq C_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{I\subseteq[N+n-k],|I|=\theta(N+n-k)}\mathbb{P}\left(\exists w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}\cap B(\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}_{I},r_{H}):\|Hw^{\prime}\|\leq s_{0}/2,\|X\|\leq C_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\binom{N+n-k}{\theta(N+n-k)}\left(\dfrac{3}{r_{H}}\right)^{2\theta(N+n-k)}\mathbb{P}\left(\exists w^{\prime}\in B(w,2r_{H}):\|Hw^{\prime}\|\leq s_{0}/2,\|X\|\leq C_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\binom{N+n-k}{\theta(N+n-k)}\left(\dfrac{3}{r_{H}}\right)^{2\theta(N+n-k)}\exp(-cn).

By the Stirling formula,

\displaystyle\binom{N+n-k}{\theta(N+n-k)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(N+n-k)\cdots(N+n-k-\theta(N+n-k)+1)}{(\theta(N+n-k))!}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{(N+n-k)^{\theta(N+n-k)}}{(\theta(N+n-k))!}

\displaystyle\sim\dfrac{(N+n-k)^{\theta(N+n-k)}e^{\theta(N+n-k)}}{\sqrt{2\pi\theta(N+n-k)}(\theta(N+n-k))^{\theta(N+n-k)}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{e^{\theta(N+n-k)}}{\sqrt{2\pi\theta(N+n-k)}\theta^{\theta(N+n-k)}}.

Hence, when
n
large,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\exists w^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Comp}(\theta,r_{H}):\|Hw^{\prime}\|\leq s_{0}/2,\|X\|\leq C_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{e}{\theta}\right)^{\theta(N+n-k)}\left(\dfrac{3}{r_{H}}\right)^{2\theta(N+n-k)}\exp(-cn)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left(-cn+\theta(N+n-k)\ln\left(\dfrac{9e}{\theta r_{H}^{2}}\right)\right).

Since
\theta\ln((9e)/(\theta r_{H}^{2}))
tends to zero as
\theta
tends to zero, we can choose
\theta=\theta_{0}
, where
\theta_{0}
is sufficiently small, such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\exists w^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Comp}(\theta_{0},r_{H}):\|Hw^{\prime}\|\leq s_{0}/2,\|X\|\leq C_{0}\right)\leq\exp\left(-cn\right),

which lead to (
3.4
).

The proof of (
3.4
) for the case

is similar and is omitted.

4.1
The case

Let

\displaystyle g(x)=\dfrac{x(1-\gamma_{0}+2x)^{2}}{1+x},\quad x\in[0\vee(\gamma_{0}-1),\gamma_{0}].

then
Bose and Hachem, 2020
showed that

is increasing and invertible on its domain. Moreover, under the conditions (C1) and (C2),
Bose and Hachem, 2020
also showed that
\mu_{Z^{(n)}}
of the matrix in (
1.4
) converges to a deterministic rotation invariant probability measure
\mu^{(\gamma_{0})}
in probability. Moreover, the distribution function of the radial component of
\mu^{(\gamma_{0})}
is

\displaystyle\mu^{(\gamma_{0})}\left(B(0,r)\right)=\begin{cases}\gamma_{0}^{-1}g^{-1}(r^{2}),&0\leq r\leq\gamma_{0}^{1/2}(\gamma_{0}+1)^{1/2},\\
1,&r>\gamma_{0}^{1/2}(\gamma_{0}+1)^{1/2},\end{cases}

if
\gamma_{0}\leq 1
, and

\displaystyle\mu^{(\gamma_{0})}\left(B(0,r)\right)=\begin{cases}1-\gamma_{0}^{-1},&0\leq r\leq(\gamma_{0}-1)^{3/2}\gamma_{0}^{-1/2},\\
\gamma_{0}^{-1}g^{-1}(r^{2}),&(\gamma_{0}-1)^{3/2}\gamma_{0}^{-1/2}\leq r\leq\gamma_{0}^{1/2}(\gamma_{0}+1)^{1/2},\\
1,&r>\gamma_{0}^{1/2}(\gamma_{0}+1)^{1/2},\end{cases}

if

. Recall the matrix of interest

in (

).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
k=1
and let
N
satisfy (
1.3
). Assume that the conditions (C1) and(C2) hold. Then
\mu_{Y^{(n)}}
converges weakly to
\mu^{(\gamma_{0})}
in probability.

Proof.

Note that
A^{(n)}-J^{(n)}
is a rank one matrix, so is
Y^{(n)}-Z^{(n)}
. Thus, by Lemma
A.2
, we have

\displaystyle s_{i}\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)\geq s_{i+1}\left(Z^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right),\ \forall i\in[N-1].

Thus, for small
\delta\in(0,1)
, on the event
\left\{s_{N}\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)<\delta\right\}
,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\delta}\ln(\lambda)d\nu_{\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)}(\lambda)\right|=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{s_{i}\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)<\delta}\left|\ln\left(s_{i}\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{N}\left|\ln\left(s_{N}\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle+\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{s_{i}\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)<\delta,i<N}\left|\ln\left(s_{i}\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{N}\left|\ln\left(s_{N}\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle+\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{s_{i}\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)<\delta,i<N}\left|\ln\left(s_{i+1}\left(Z^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{N}\left|\ln\left(s_{N}\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle+\dfrac{1}{N}\sum_{s_{i}\left(Z^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)<\delta}\left|\ln\left(s_{i}\left(Z^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{N}\left|\ln\left(s_{N}\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)\right)\right|+\left|\int_{0}^{\delta}\ln(\lambda)d\nu_{\left(Z^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)}(\lambda)\right|.

Thus, for any
\epsilon>0
, by Theorem
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\int_{0}^{\delta}\ln(\lambda)d\nu_{\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)}(\lambda)\right|>\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\int_{0}^{\delta}\ln(\lambda)d\nu_{\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)}(\lambda)\right|>\epsilon,s_{N}\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)<\delta\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\dfrac{1}{N}\left|\ln\left(s_{N}\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)\right)\right|+\left|\int_{0}^{\delta}\ln(\lambda)d\nu_{\left(Z^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)}(\lambda)\right|>\epsilon,s_{N}\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)<\delta\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\dfrac{1}{N}\left|\ln\left(s_{N}\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)\right)\right|+\left|\int_{0}^{\delta}\ln(\lambda)d\nu_{\left(Z^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)}(\lambda)\right|>\epsilon,\right.

\displaystyle\left.n^{-13/11}<s_{N}\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)<\delta\right)+Cn^{-1/22}+\mathbb{P}\left(\|X\|>C_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\dfrac{13\ln n}{11N}+\left|\int_{0}^{\delta}\ln(\lambda)d\nu_{\left(Z^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)}(\lambda)\right|>\epsilon\right)+Cn^{-1/22}+\mathbb{P}\left(\|X\|>C_{0}\right).

In
(
Bose and Hachem, 2020
, (35))
, for all
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
, for all
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0+}\limsup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\int_{0}^{\delta}\ln(\lambda)d\nu_{\left(Z^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)}(\lambda)\right|>\epsilon\right)=0.

Thus, when choosing

large, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0+}\limsup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\int_{0}^{\delta}\ln(\lambda)d\nu_{\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)}(\lambda)\right|>\epsilon\right)=0,

where we obtain the uniform integrability of the logarithm with respect to the
\nu_{\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)}
in probability, for all
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
.

Next, we consider the Hermitian matrix

\displaystyle\Sigma_{A}(z)=\left(\begin{matrix}0&Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\\
\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)^{*}&0\end{matrix}\right).

and

\displaystyle\Sigma_{J}(z)=\left(\begin{matrix}0&Z^{(n)}-zI_{N}\\
\left(Z^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)^{*}&0\end{matrix}\right).

Then by
Bose and Hachem, 2020

\nu_{z}
, such that
\nu_{\left(Z^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)}
converges weakly to
\nu_{z}
almost surely. Note that the set of eigenvalues of

\displaystyle\left\{\lambda_{i}(\Sigma_{J}):i\in[2N]\right\}=\left\{\pm s_{i}\left(Z^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right):i\in[N]\right\},

we have

\displaystyle\mu_{\Sigma_{J}}(x)=\dfrac{\nu_{\left(Z^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)}(x)+\nu_{\left(Z^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)}(-x)}{2}.

Thus, if we denote by
\check{\nu}_{z}
the symmetrization of
\nu_{z}
, which is the probability measure defined by
\check{\nu}_{z}(E)=\dfrac{\nu_{z}(E)+\nu_{z}(-E)}{2}
for all Borel set

, then
\mu_{\Sigma_{J}(z)}
converges weakly to
\check{\nu}_{z}
almost surely for almost all

. Moreover, since

has rank one,
\Sigma_{A}(z)-\Sigma_{J}(z)
is a rank two matrix. By the Stability of ESD laws with respect to small rank perturbations (
(
Tao, 2012
, Exercise 2.4.4)
), we can deduce the weakly convergence of
\mu_{\Sigma_{A}(z)}
towards
\check{\nu}_{z}
almost surely for almost all
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Hence, we obtain the weakly convergence of
\nu_{\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)}
towards
\nu_{z}
.

Therefore, by Lemma
2.10
,
\mu_{Y^{(n)}}
converges weakly to some probability measure

in probability for almost all

, and the limit measure
\mu^{\prime}
is satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu^{\prime}}(z)=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\ln(\lambda)d\nu_{z}(\lambda).

Since the singular value empirical distributions of

and

converge to the same limit

, we have

for almost all

. Then by Lemma

, we have

.

The general case

Therefore, by
(
Guionnet et al., 2011
, Lemma 15)
and (

), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mu_{\Sigma_{Y^{(n)}}}((-t,t))\leq Ct\left(1+t^{-\alpha}n^{-\beta}\right).

(4.25)

Note that
2\mu_{\Sigma_{Y^{(n)}}}(\cdot)=\nu_{Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}}(\cdot)+\nu_{Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}}(-\cdot)
, by a standard argument, we can deduce the following uniform integrability of the logarithm function

\displaystyle\lim_{K\to+\infty}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\int_{|\ln\lambda|>K}|\ln\lambda|\nu_{Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}}(d\lambda)\right|>\epsilon\right)=0,\forall\epsilon>0.

Step 2.

By a standard concentration argument (see
(
Bose and Hachem, 2020
, Proposition 26)
) one can assume that the entries of

are complex Gaussian. For test function

, for

, we have

\displaystyle\int f(x)d\nu_{Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}}(x)

\displaystyle=\int f(\sqrt{x})d\mu_{\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)\left(Y^{(n)}-zI_{N}\right)^{*}}(x)

\displaystyle=\int f(\sqrt{x})d\mu_{Y^{(n)}\left(Y^{(n)}\right)^{*}-z\left(Y^{(n)}\right)^{*}-\overline{z}Y^{(n)}+|z|^{2}I_{N}}(x)

\displaystyle=\int f(\sqrt{x+|z|^{2}})d\mu_{Y^{(n)}\left(Y^{(n)}\right)^{*}-z\left(Y^{(n)}\right)^{*}-\overline{z}Y^{(n)}}(x).

(4.26)

By
(
Horn and Johnson, 2013
, Theorem 1.3.22)
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\mu_{Y^{(n)}\left(Y^{(n)}\right)^{*}-z\left(Y^{(n)}\right)^{*}-\overline{z}Y^{(n)}}

\displaystyle=\mu_{X^{(n)}A^{(n)}\left(X^{(n)}\right)^{*}X^{(n)}\left(A^{(n)}\right)^{*}\left(X^{(n)}\right)^{*}-zX^{(n)}\left(A^{(n)}\right)^{*}\left(X^{(n)}\right)^{*}-\overline{z}X^{(n)}A^{(n)}\left(X^{(n)}\right)^{*}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{n}{N}\mu_{A^{(n)}\left(X^{(n)}\right)^{*}X^{(n)}\left(A^{(n)}\right)^{*}\left(X^{(n)}\right)^{*}X^{(n)}-z\left(A^{(n)}\right)^{*}\left(X^{(n)}\right)^{*}X^{(n)}-\overline{z}A^{(n)}\left(X^{(n)}\right)^{*}X^{(n)}}+\dfrac{N-n}{N}\delta_{0}.

(4.27)

Denote
\gamma_{0}^{+}=[\gamma_{0}]+1
be the smallest integer that is strictly greater than
\gamma_{0}
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
N<\gamma_{0}^{+}n
. Let
\widehat{X}^{(n)}
be a
(\gamma_{0}^{+}n)\times n
matrices whose entries are complex Gaussian with mean zero and variance
1/n
, such that
\widehat{X}^{(n)}_{[N],[n]}=X^{(n)}
. Then Wishart matrix

can be written as

\displaystyle\left(X^{(n)}\right)^{*}X^{(n)}=\left(\widehat{X}^{(n)}\right)^{*}\left(\begin{matrix}I_{N}&0_{N\times(\gamma_{0}^{+}n-N)}\\
0_{(\gamma_{0}^{+}n-N)\times N}&0_{(\gamma_{0}^{+}n-N)\times(\gamma_{0}^{+}n-N)}\end{matrix}\right)\widehat{X}^{(n)}.

Under the condition (
1.3
), for all polynomial

in
2
non-commutative indeterminates, one has

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{n}\mathrm{Tr}\left[P\left(A^{(n)},\left(A^{(n)}\right)^{*}\right)\right]\to\tau\left[P\left(\textbf{a},\textbf{a}^{*}\right)\right]

for some non-commutative element

in a

-probability space
\left(\mathcal{A},\cdot^{*},\tau,\|\cdot\|\right)
with a faithful trace
\tau
. Besides, the Hermitian matrix

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}I_{N}&0_{N\times(\gamma_{0}^{+}n-N)}\\

x
and
a
are free, such that for any polynomial

3
non-commutative indeterminates,

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{n}\mathrm{Tr}\left[P\left(\left(X^{(n)}\right)^{*}X^{(n)},A^{(n)},\left(A^{(n)}\right)^{*}\right)\right]\to\tau\left[P\left(\textbf{x},\textbf{a},\textbf{a}^{*}\right)\right],\ n\to\infty,

(4.28)

almost surely. Consequently, the eigenvalue empirical measure of

converges almost surely. In particular, for

\displaystyle\mu_{A^{(n)}\left(X^{(n)}\right)^{*}X^{(n)}\left(A^{(n)}\right)^{*}\left(X^{(n)}\right)^{*}X^{(n)}-z\left(A^{(n)}\right)^{*}\left(X^{(n)}\right)^{*}X^{(n)}-\overline{z}A^{(n)}\left(X^{(n)}\right)^{*}X^{(n)}}.

The NNLO gluon beam function for jet-veto resummation
Guido Bell, Kevin Brune, Goutam Das, Ding Yu Shao, and Marcel Wald

Abstract
We compute the gluon beam function for jet-veto resummation to next-to-next-to-leading order (NNLO) in the strong-coupling expansion. Our calculation is based on an automated framework that was previously used for the computation of the respective quark beam function, and which we significantly extended for the present calculation. In particular, the perturbative matching kernels are directly calculated in momentum space, without the need to perform an additional Mellin transform. We present results for both gluon and quark-initiated processes, which we cross-checked with an independent semi-analytical method that exploits the similarity of the beam functions to the more familiar case of transverse-momentum resummation. Our computation is relevant for jet-veto resummations at NNLL ′ accuracy.

Introduction

Many experimental analyses at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC) apply jet vetoes to enhance the signal sensitivity. In these analyses, the jet veto is usually imposed as a cut on the transverse momenta
p_{T}^{\rm veto}
of the reconstructed jets. If this scale is much smaller than the typical hard scale
Q
of the process, the QCD radiation gets constrained to be either soft or collinear to the beam directions, which induces large Sudakov-type corrections of the form
\alpha_{S}^{n}\ln^{2n}(p_{T}^{\rm veto}/Q)
. These logarithms challenge the convergence of the perturbative expansion, and they need to be resummed to all orders to obtain reliable predictions.

The resummation of jet-veto logarithms has received considerable attention in recent years. While the formalism was originally developed for Higgs-boson and Drell-Yan production
Banfi:2012yh
;
Becher:2012qa
;
Tackmann:2012bt
;
Banfi:2012jm
;
Becher:2013xia
;
Stewart:2013faa
;
Banfi:2015pju
;
Monni:2019yyr
, it was later applied to processes with other electroweak final states
Shao:2013uba
;
Li:2014ria
;
Jaiswal:2014yba
;
Becher:2014aya
;
Wang:2015mvz
;
Dawson:2016ysj
;
Campbell:2023cha
;
Gavardi:2023aco
and beyond-the-standard-model signatures
Tackmann:2016jyb
;
Ebert:2016idf
;
Fuks:2017vtl
;
Arpino:2019fmo
. In most of these analyses, the jet-veto logarithms were resummed to next-to-next-to-leading logarithmic (NNLL) accuracy, but the theoretical predictions can be further improved using methods from effective field theory. In Soft-Collinear Effective Theory (SCET)
Bauer:2000yr
;
Bauer:2001yt
;
Beneke:2002ph
the effects from the relevant scales
p_{T}^{\rm veto}\ll Q
are systematically disentangled (factorised), and the logarithmic corrections are resummed using renormalisation-group (RG) techniques. The case of a
p_{T}^{\rm veto}
veto is special in the sense that it contains rapidity logarithms, which cannot be resummed with conventional RG methods. One therefore resorts to collinear-anomaly
Becher:2010tm
;
Becher:2011pf
or rapidity RG techniques
Chiu:2012ir
for these SCET-2 type observables.

The starting point of the SCET analyses is a factorisation theorem, which at leading power in
p_{T}^{\rm veto}/Q\ll 1
can be written in the schematic form

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}^{2}\sigma(p_{T}^{\rm veto})}{{\rm d}Q^{2}{\rm d}Y}=\sum_{i,j}\,H_{ij}(Q,\mu)\,\mathcal{B}_{i/h_{1}}(x_{1},p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)\,\mathcal{B}_{j/h_{2}}(x_{2},p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)\,\mathcal{S}_{ij}(p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)\,,

(1)

where the sum runs over all partonic channels,
Q
is the invariant mass and
Y
the rapidity of the colourless final state with
x_{1,2}=(Q/\sqrt{s})\,e^{\pm Y}
. Here the purely virtual corrections to the Born process are contained in the hard functions
H_{ij}
, whereas the beam functions
\mathcal{B}_{i/h}
and the soft functions
\mathcal{S}_{ij}
describe the effects from collinear and soft radiation, respectively. The factorisation theorem furthermore assumes that the jet radius
R
satisfies
\lambda\ll R\ll\ln(1/\lambda)
with
\lambda=p_{T}^{\rm veto}/Q

Becher:2012qa
.

While the hard functions
H_{ij}(Q,\mu)
are process-dependent, the soft functions
\mathcal{S}_{ij}(p_{T}^{\rm veto}\!\!,\mu)
trivially depend on the process via the colour representations of the partons
i,j
. They are currently known to next-to-next-to-leading order (NNLO) accuracy
Stewart:2013faa
;
Bell:2020yzz
;
Abreu:2022sdc
. The beam functions
\mathcal{B}_{i/h}(x,p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)
, on the other hand, are universal and depend on the
parton
i
within the hadron
h
that carries the longitudinal momentum fraction
x
after emitting collinear radiation that passes the jet-veto constraint. They are by themselves non-perturbative objects, but as long as the jet-veto scale satisfies
p_{T}^{\rm veto}\gg\Lambda_{\rm QCD}
, they can be matched onto the standard parton distribution functions as we will review below. The corresponding matching kernels can then be computed perturbatively, and they were determined to NLO in
Becher:2012qa
;
Shao:2013uba
. A subset of the current authors extended this calculation to NNLO for quark-initiated processes
Bell:2022nrj
, whereas the full set of matching kernels was determined afterwards in
Abreu:2022zgo
. These results were subsequently used to perform NNLL
′
resummations in
Campbell:2023cha
;
Gavardi:2023aco
.

The purpose of this work is twofold. First, we will complete the NNLO calculation initiated in

We will also study the endpoint behaviour of the matching kernels in some detail since these are often divergent and therefore hard to control numerically. In addition we for the first time present results for the gluon beam function in Mellin space. Second, we stress that the method we use for the calculation of the jet-veto kernels is generic, and it can be applied to a much broader class of observables. It actually follows a strategy that was successfully applied for the calculation of soft functions within the

We view this extension as a major improvement of our automated approach, and we anticipate many further results of this novel framework in the future. We, in fact, already recalculated the matching kernels for transverse-momentum resummation and the event-shape variable N-jettiness in this setup

Bell:2022tmi
;
Wald:thesis

2
Theoretical framework

To set up the notation, we introduce two light-like vectors
n^{\mu}
and
\bar{n}^{\mu}
that obey
n^{2}=\bar{n}^{2}=0
and
n\cdot\bar{n}=2
. Any four-vector
k^{\mu}
can then be decomposed according to its projections
k^{-}=\bar{n}\cdot k
,
k^{+}=n\cdot k
, and a transverse component
k^{\perp}_{\mu}
that satisfies
n\cdot k^{\perp}=\bar{n}\cdot k^{\perp}=0
. In this notation, the gluon beam function for jet-veto resummation is defined as

\displaystyle{\cal B}_{g/h}(x,p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)=

\displaystyle-(xP^{-})\sum_{X}\,\delta\Big((1-x)P^{-}-\sum_{i}k_{i}^{-}\Big)\,\widehat{\mathcal{M}}(p_{T}^{\rm veto};\{k_{i}\})

\displaystyle\times\bra{h(P)}\mathcal{A}_{n\perp}^{c,\mu}(0)\ket{X}\bra{X}\mathcal{A}_{n\perp,\mu}^{c}(0)\ket{h(P)},

(2)

where
\mathcal{A}_{n\perp}^{c,\mu}(x)=1/g_{s}\,W_{n}^{\dagger}(x)\big[iD^{c,\mu}_{\perp}\,W_{n}(x)\big]
is the
n
-collinear gluon field operator,
W_{n}
a collinear Wilson line, and the sum over
X
represents the phase space of the final-state partons with momenta
\{k_{i}\}
. At the tree level, when there is no collinear emission, this is simply the vacuum state, and at NNLO it denotes the phase space of up to two massless partons. The hadronic state with collinear momentum
P^{\mu}=P^{-}n^{\mu}/2
is furthermore indicated by
\ket{h(P)}
, whereas the jet-veto constraint is imposed by the measurement function
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}(p_{T}^{\rm veto};\{k_{i}\})
, which we will specify in Sec.
3
below.

As a matrix element of hadronic states, the beam function is not directly accessible in perturbation theory. But as long as the scale that constrains the collinear emissions satisfies
p_{T}^{\rm veto}\gg\Lambda_{\rm QCD}
, it can be matched onto the usual parton distribution functions
f_{i/h}(x,\mu)
according to

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{i/h}(x,p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\int_{x}^{1}\frac{{\rm d}z}{z}\,{\cal I}_{i\leftarrow k}(z,p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)~f_{k/h}\Big(\frac{x}{z},\mu\Big)\,,

(3)

which holds at leading power in
\Lambda_{\rm QCD}/p_{T}^{\rm veto}
.
Our goal consists in computing the matching kernels
{\cal I}_{i\leftarrow k}(z,p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)
for gluon-initiated processes (
i=g
) to NNLO in QCD. However, since our previous results for the quark channels (
i=q
) were provided only in Mellin space
Bell:2022nrj
, we will for completeness also determine those kernels directly in momentum space in this work. The matching calculation can, in fact, be most easily performed using partonic on-shell states, since the parton distribution functions then evaluate to
f_{i/j}(x,\mu)=\delta_{ij}\,\delta(1-x)
to all orders in perturbation theory when dimensional regularisation is used to regularise both ultraviolet (UV) and infrared (IR) divergences. The partonic calculation therefore directly yields the matching kernels in this case.

As stated in the introduction, the jet veto is usually imposed on the transverse momenta of the reconstructed jets. It is well known, however, that transverse-momentum dependent observables belong to a special class of observables that suffer from rapidity divergences that are not captured by the dimensional regulator
\epsilon=(4-d)/2
. This follows from the fact that the relevant soft and collinear modes have the same virtuality in the effective theory, which is also known as SCET-2. To regularise these rapidity divergences, we use the analytic phase-space regulator from
Becher:2011dz
, which is introduced for each emission with momentum
k_{i}^{\mu}
via

\displaystyle\int{\rm d}^{d}k_{i}\;\left(\frac{\nu}{k_{i}^{-}+k_{i}^{+}}\right)^{\alpha}\;\delta(k_{i}^{2})\,\theta(k_{i}^{0})\,,

(4)

where
\alpha
is the rapidity regulator and
\nu
the associated rapidity scale. The same prescription is also implemented for the soft integrals in
SoftSERVE
, but for collinear emissions with
k_{i}^{-}\gg k_{i}^{+}
it can be further simplified at leading power. The very fact that the rapidity regulator in (
4
) respects the
n
-
\bar{n}
symmetry of the process, ensures that the beam functions for collinear and anti-collinear emissions are equivalent to all orders in perturbation theory.

The matching kernels defined in (
3
) thus depend on the scheme that is used to regularise the rapidity divergences and, in particular, the rapidity scale
\nu
. To obtain a result that is scheme independent, we follow the collinear-anomaly approach
Becher:2010tm
;
Becher:2011pf
, which states that the product of the soft and collinear functions can be refactorised as

\displaystyle\Big[{\cal I}_{g\leftarrow i}(z_{1},p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu,\nu)\;{\cal I}_{g\leftarrow j}(z_{2},p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu,\nu)\;{\cal S}_{gg}(p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu,\nu)\Big]_{Q}

\displaystyle\qquad=\left(\frac{Q}{p_{T}^{\rm veto}}\right)^{-2F_{gg}(p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)}\;I_{g\leftarrow i}(z_{1},p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)\;I_{g\leftarrow j}(z_{2},p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)\,,

(5)

where the matching kernels
I_{i\leftarrow j}(z,p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)
on the right-hand side of this equation are independent of the rapidity scale
\nu
. The dependence on the hard scale
Q
is furthermore resummed through the collinear-anomaly exponent
F_{gg}(p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)
, which satisfies the RG equation

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\ln\mu}\;F_{gg}(p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)=2\,\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{A}(\alpha_{s})

(6)

with the cusp anomalous dimension in the adjoint representation
\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{A}(\alpha_{s})
. Up to two-loop order, its solution is given by

\displaystyle F_{gg}(p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)=\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)\left\{2\Gamma_{0}^{A}L+d_{1}^{A}\right\}+\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)^{2}\left\{2\beta_{0}\Gamma_{0}^{A}L^{2}+2\left(\Gamma_{1}^{A}+\beta_{0}d_{1}^{A}\right)L+d_{2}^{A}\right\}\,,

(7)

where
L=\ln(\mu/\ptv)
, and
\Gamma_{i}^{A}
and
\beta_{i}
are the expansion coefficients of the cusp anomalous dimension and the
\beta
-function, respectively, that are given explicitly to the required order in App.
A
. As the anomaly exponent describes the physics from the overlap of the soft and collinear regions, the non-logarithmic terms
d_{i}^{A}
in (
7
) are constrained by Casimir scaling, and they are therefore related to the corresponding quantities for the quark channels, which were given in our previous work
Bell:2022nrj
. We also note that the anomaly exponent renormalises additively,
F_{gg}^{\rm bare}=F_{gg}+Z^{F}_{gg}
, and the corresponding
\,\overline{\!{\text{MS}}}
counterterm
Z^{F}_{gg}
obeys a similar RG equation as in (
6
), whose two-loop solution reads

\displaystyle Z^{F}_{gg}(p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)=\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)\left\{\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{A}}{\epsilon}\right\}+\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)^{2}\left\{-\frac{\beta_{0}\Gamma_{0}^{A}}{2\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{\Gamma_{1}^{A}}{2\epsilon}\right\}\,.

(8)

The remaining ingredients in the collinear-anomaly relation (
5
) are the refactorised matching kernels
I_{i\leftarrow j}(z,p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)
, which are independent of the rapidity regularisation scheme. Their scale dependence is determined by the RG equation

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\ln\mu}\;I_{i\leftarrow j}(z,p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)

\displaystyle=2\left[\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{R_{i}}(\alpha_{s})\,L-\gamma^{i}(\alpha_{s})\right]I_{i\leftarrow j}(z,p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)

\displaystyle\quad-2\sum_{k}\int_{z}^{1}\frac{{\rm d}z^{\prime}}{z^{\prime}}\,I_{i\leftarrow k}(z^{\prime},p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)~P_{k\leftarrow j}\Big(\frac{z}{z^{\prime}},\alpha_{s}\Big)\,,

(9)

where
\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{R_{i}}(\alpha_{s})
is the cusp anomalous dimension in the representation of the parton
i
,
\gamma^{i}(\alpha_{s})
is the collinear anomalous dimension for quarks
(i=q)
or gluons
(i=g)
, and
P_{k\leftarrow j}(z,\alpha_{s})
are the DGLAP splitting functions. The renormalisation of the refactorised matching kernels is, in fact, slightly more complicated, and it is convenient to introduce two types of counterterms that subtract the UV divergences of the beam function (
Z_{i}^{B}
) and the IR divergences associated with the matching onto the parton distribution functions (
Z_{k\leftarrow j}^{f}
) for them
Bell:2022nrj
. Specifically, we write

\displaystyle{I}_{i\leftarrow j}(z,p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)=Z_{i}^{B}(p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)\,\sum_{k}\int_{z}^{1}\frac{{\rm d}z^{\prime}}{z^{\prime}}\,I_{i\leftarrow k}^{\rm bare}(z^{\prime},p_{T}^{\rm veto})~Z_{k\leftarrow j}^{f}\Big(\frac{z}{z^{\prime}},\mu\Big)\,,

(10)

where the UV counterterm obeys the RG equation

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\ln\mu}\;Z_{i}^{B}(p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)=

\displaystyle 2\left[\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{R_{i}}(\alpha_{s})\,L-\gamma^{i}(\alpha_{s})\right]Z_{i}^{B}(p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)\,.

(11)

Up to two loops, its solution reads

\displaystyle Z_{i}^{B}(p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)=1+\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)\left\{-\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{i}}{2\epsilon^{2}}-\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{i}L-\gamma_{0}^{i}}{\epsilon}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)^{2}\bigg\{\frac{(\Gamma_{0}^{i})^{2}}{8\epsilon^{4}}+\left(\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{i}}{2}L-\frac{\gamma_{0}^{i}}{2}+\frac{3\beta_{0}}{8}\right)\frac{\Gamma_{0}^{i}}{\epsilon^{3}}+\bigg(\frac{(\Gamma_{0}^{i})^{2}}{2}L^{2}-\Gamma_{0}^{i}\Big(\gamma_{0}^{i}-\frac{\beta_{0}}{2}\Big)L

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-\frac{\Gamma_{1}^{i}}{8}+\frac{(\gamma_{0}^{i})^{2}}{2}-\frac{\beta_{0}\gamma_{0}^{i}}{2}\bigg)\,\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}-\frac{\Gamma_{1}^{i}L-\gamma_{1}^{i}}{2\epsilon}\bigg\}\,,

(12)

and the specific values for the coefficients of the anomalous dimensions are also summarised in App.
A
. The IR counterterm, on the other hand, satisfies the RG equation

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\ln\mu}\;Z_{k\leftarrow j}^{f}(z,\mu)=

\displaystyle-2\sum_{l}\int_{z}^{1}\frac{{\rm d}z^{\prime}}{z^{\prime}}\,Z_{k\leftarrow l}^{f}(z^{\prime},\mu)~P_{l\leftarrow j}\Big(\frac{z}{z^{\prime}},\alpha_{s}\Big)\,,

(13)

whose two-loop solution is given by

\displaystyle Z_{k\leftarrow j}^{f}(z,\mu)=\delta_{kj}\,\delta(1-z)+\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)\left\{P_{k\leftarrow j}^{(0)}(z)\,\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)^{2}\bigg\{-P_{k\leftarrow j}^{(0)}(z)\,\frac{\beta_{0}}{2\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}\Big(P_{k\leftarrow l}^{(0)}\otimes P_{l\leftarrow j}^{(0)}\Big)(z)+P_{k\leftarrow j}^{(1)}(z)\,\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\bigg\}\,,

(14)

where the coefficients of the DGLAP splitting functions
{P}_{k\leftarrow j}^{(i)}(z)
are defined in App.
A
. We furthermore introduced a short-hand notation for the convolutions

\displaystyle\Big(P_{k\leftarrow l}^{(0)}\otimes P_{l\leftarrow j}^{(0)}\Big)(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l}\int_{z}^{1}\frac{{\rm d}z^{\prime}}{z^{\prime}}\,P_{k\leftarrow l}^{(0)}(z^{\prime})~P_{l\leftarrow j}^{(0)}\Big(\frac{z}{z^{\prime}}\Big)

(15)

that appear frequently in the calculation. Note that this notation implies a sum over intermediate partonic channels with index
l
. For completeness, we give the explicit expressions for these convolutions also in App.
A
.

Finally, we state the solution of the RG equation (
2
) for the renormalised matching kernels. Up to NNLO, it takes the form

\displaystyle I_{i\leftarrow j}(z,p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu)

(16)

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}\,\delta(1-z)+\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)\bigg\{\Big(\Gamma_{0}^{i}\,L^{2}-2\gamma_{0}^{i}\,L\Big)\delta_{ij}\,\delta(1-z)-2L\,P_{i\leftarrow j}^{(0)}(z)+I_{i\leftarrow j}^{(1)}(z)\bigg\}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{4\pi}\right)^{2}\bigg\{\bigg(\frac{(\Gamma_{0}^{i})^{2}}{2}L^{4}-2\Gamma_{0}^{i}\left(\!\gamma_{0}^{i}-\frac{\beta_{0}}{3}\right)L^{3}+\big(\Gamma_{1}^{i}+2(\gamma_{0}^{i})^{2}-2\beta_{0}\gamma_{0}^{i}\big)L^{2}-2\gamma_{1}^{i}L\bigg)\delta_{ij}\,\delta(1-z)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad-2\Big(\Gamma_{0}^{i}L^{3}+\big(\beta_{0}-2\gamma_{0}^{i}\big)L^{2}\Big)P_{i\leftarrow j}^{(0)}(z)+\Big(\Gamma_{0}^{i}L^{2}-2(\gamma_{0}^{i}-\beta_{0})L\Big)\,I_{i\leftarrow j}^{(1)}(z)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+2L^{2}\Big(P_{i\leftarrow k}^{(0)}\otimes P_{k\leftarrow j}^{(0)}\Big)(z)-2L\Big(I_{i\leftarrow k}^{(1)}\otimes P_{k\leftarrow j}^{(0)}\Big)(z)-2L\,P_{i\leftarrow j}^{(1)}(z)+I_{i\leftarrow j}^{(2)}(z)\bigg\}.

As the logarithmic terms in this expression are already known, we will focus on the non-logarithmic terms
I_{i\leftarrow j}^{(m)}(z)
in later sections.

3
Computational aspects

As stated in the introduction, we followed two different approaches to compute the renormalised matching kernels for jet-veto resummation. The first approach is based on an automated numerical framework that can be used for a generic class of observables, while the second approach is a semi-analytical method that exploits the similarity of the jet-veto beam functions to the ones relevant for transverse-momentum resummation. We will now describe each of these methods in turn.

3.1
Numerical approach

In the first approach, we start from the definition of the beam function (
2
) with the measurement function replaced by the ansatz

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\tau;\{k_{i}\})=\exp\big(-\tau\, \omega(\lbrace k_{i} \rbrace)\,\big)\,,

(17)

where
\tau
is a Laplace variable of dimension 1/mass, and the function
\omega(\{k_{i}\})
specifies a generic observable. Following
Bell:2022tmi
;
Bell:2022nrj
, we find it convenient to work in Laplace space, since both the calculation of the bare beam function and its renormalisation simplify in this case. We note that for the specific jet-veto beam function defined in (
2
), the measurement function is
not
of the form (
17
), but it is instead given by
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}(p_{T}^{\rm veto};\{k_{i}\})=\theta\big(p_{T}^{\rm veto}-\omega(\{k_{i}\})\,\big)
. We then follow the procedure described in
Bell:2020yzz
to convert this into the form (
17
) by taking a Laplace transform, and we extract the results in the original
p_{T}^{\rm veto}
space by inverting the Laplace transformation at the very end of the calculation.

To factorise the implicit divergences of the collinear matrix elements, we use universal phase-space parametrisations as in
Bell:2022tmi
;
Bell:2022nrj
. Particularly, for a single emission with momentum
k^{\mu}
, we use the magnitude of its transverse momentum relative to the beam axis, which is set by
P^{\mu}\propto n^{\mu}
, and we allow for a non-trivial azimuthal dependence of the observable,

\displaystyle k_{T}=|\vec{k}^{\perp}|\,,\qquad\qquad t_{k}=\frac{1-\cos\theta_{k}}{2}\,,

(18)

where
\theta_{k}
is the angle between
\vec{k}^{\perp}
and a reference vector
\vec{v}^{\perp}
that could be imposed by the observable. The remaining momentum components are then fixed by the on-shell condition and the explicit delta function in (
2
), yielding
k^{+}=k_{T}^{2}/k^{-}
and
k^{-}=\bar{z}P^{-}
with
\bar{z}=1-z
. This leads to the following measurement function for a single emission,

\displaystyle\omega_{1}(k)=k_{T}\,.

(19)

For two emissions, we proceed similarly and parametrise the momenta
k^{\mu}
and
l^{\mu}
of the massless final-state partons in the form
2
2

2

Note that we use a slightly different notation here compared to our setup that was used for the calculation of the Mellin-space kernels
Bell:2022nrj
.

\displaystyle a=\frac{k^{-}l_{T}}{l^{-}k_{T}},\qquad b=\frac{k_{T}}{l_{T}},\qquad\bar{z}=\frac{k^{-}+l^{-}}{P^{-}},\qquad q_{T}=\sqrt{(k^{-}+l^{-})(k^{+}+l^{+})}\,,

(20)

where again
k_{T}=|\vec{k}^{\perp}|
,
l_{T}=|\vec{l}^{\perp}|
, and we in addition now have three non-trivial angles in the transverse plane, with
\theta_{k}
and
\theta_{l}
referring to the external vector
\vec{v}^{\perp}
as before, and
\theta_{kl}
being the angle between
\vec{k}^{\perp}
and
\vec{l}^{\perp}
. We then rewrite these angles in terms of variables
t_{k}
,
t_{l}
, and
t_{kl}
that are defined on the unit interval, similar to (
18
).

In terms of these variables, the two-emission measurement function for jet-veto resummation takes the form

\displaystyle\omega_{2}(k,l)=\frac{q_{T}\,\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{(1+ab)(a+b)}}\,\bigg\{\theta(\Delta-R)\;\max(1,b)+\theta(R-\Delta)\;\sqrt{(1-b)^{2}+4b(1-t_{kl})}\bigg\}\,,

(21)

where
R
is the jet radius and
\Delta=\sqrt{\ln^{2}a+\arccos^{2}(1-2t_{kl})}
is the distance measure of the jet algorithm. The twofold structure in (
21
) arises as follows: If the distance between the two emissions is smaller than
R
, the emissions are clustered together and the veto is imposed on the recombined pseudo-particle. If, on the other hand, this distance is larger than
R
, the veto constrains both of the reconstructed jets. Similar to our previous work on the quark beam function
Bell:2022nrj
, we consider the general class of
k_{T}
-type jet algorithms in this work, for which the expression in (
21
) turns out to be independent of the specific clustering prescription (
k_{T}
, Cambridge/Aachen, anti-
k_{T}
, …)
Becher:2012qa
.

Having defined the phase-space parametrisations and the structure of the measurement function, the partonic beam functions can now be computed. The required collinear matrix elements are related to the spin-averaged
d
-dimensional time-like splitting functions
Ritzmann:2014mka
. Specifically, we used the expressions from
Kosower:1999rx
;
Bern:1999ry
;
Sborlini:2013jba
for the real-virtual contributions, and the ones from
Campbell:1997hg
;
Catani:1998nv
for the double real-emission part, and we applied standard crossing relations to convert these to time-like kinematics
Wald:thesis
. The main task then consists in factorising the implicit phase-space divergences, which allows one to perform the expansion in the two regulators
\alpha
and
\epsilon
directly on the integrand level. While this can easily be achieved in the one-emission case, this is only true in certain cases for the double real emissions in the parametrisation (
20
). We therefore employed a number of further techniques that involve sector-decomposition steps and non-linear transformations to factorise all divergences for this contribution (details can be found in
Wald:thesis
).

In comparison to our previous work on the quark beam function
Bell:2022nrj
, our novel framework is capable of computing the beam functions directly in momentum space. In particular, within the parameterisations (
18
) and (
20
) it is possible to factorise the divergence associated with the longitudinal momentum fraction
z
. We may then introduce the corresponding distributions via

\displaystyle(1-z)^{-1-m\alpha}=-\frac{\delta(1-z)}{m\alpha}+\left[\frac{1}{1-z}\right]_{+}-m\alpha\left[\frac{\ln(1-z)}{1-z}\right]_{+}+\,\ldots

(22)

After expanding the integrand first in the rapidity regulator
\alpha
and subsequently in the dimensional regulator
\epsilon
, the coefficients in this double expansion can be integrated numerically. The final expressions for the matching kernels then consist of distributions with numerical coefficients and a non-trivial
z
-dependent ‘grid’ contribution that we sample for different values of
z
. To perform these steps, we have implemented our formalism in the publicly available program
pySecDec

Borowka:2017idc
, and we use the
Vegas
routine of the
Cuba
library
Hahn:2004fe
for the numerical integrations. By following this procedure, one obtains the regulator-dependent matching kernels on the left-hand side of the refactorisation condition (
5
). In the last step, we then combine these expressions with the corresponding NNLO soft function that is provided by the
SoftSERVE
distribution to extract the
I_{i\leftarrow j}^{(m)}(z)
part of the refactorised and renormalised matching kernels that are defined in (
16
).

3.2
Semi-analytical approach

In the second approach, we use the fact that the beam functions for jet-veto resummation are related to the ones for transverse-momentum resummation. As the latter are known analytically at the considered NNLO, one focuses on the difference

\displaystyle\Delta{\cal I}_{i\leftarrow j}(z,p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu,\nu)={\cal I}_{i\leftarrow j}(z,p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu,\nu)-{\cal I}_{i\leftarrow j}^{\text{ref}}(z,p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu,\nu)\,,

(23)

where the first term refers to the jet-veto matching kernels that appear on the left-hand side of the refactorisation condition (
5
), and the second term indicates the corresponding kernels for a so-called reference observable. Following
Becher:2013xia
, we choose for the latter the integrated
q_{T}
-spectrum,

\displaystyle\sigma^{\text{ref}}(p_{T}^{\rm veto})=\int_{0}^{p_{T}^{\rm veto}}\!\!\!\!{\rm d}q_{T}\;\frac{{\rm d}\sigma}{{\rm d}q_{T}}\,,

(24)

and we provide details on the derivation of the corresponding refactorised matching kernels
I_{i\leftarrow j}^{\text{ref},(2)}(z)
in App.
B
.

At NLO the difference in (
23
) vanishes by construction, and at NNLO it only receives contributions from diagrams with double real emissions. The measurement function capturing these contributions can be expressed as

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{\Delta}(p_{T}^{\rm veto};\{k,l\})=\theta(\Delta-R)\left[\theta\big(p_{T}^{\rm veto}-|\vec{k}^{\perp}|\big)\theta\big(p_{T}^{\rm veto}-|\vec{l}^{\perp}|\big)-\theta\big(p_{T}^{\rm veto}-|\vec{k}^{\perp}+\vec{l}^{\perp}|\big)\right],

(25)

where the angular separation
\Delta
between the two emissions is quantified as before.

As has been further argued in
Becher:2013xia
, there is a price to pay when working with the difference (
23
), i.e. one has to explicitly compute mixing terms that originate from different sectors of the effective theory. Notably, these contributions are independent of the jet radius
R
and they emerge solely within the uncorrelated contribution to the amplitude. To manage these contributions effectively, we decompose the double-emission amplitude in the collinear region
\mathcal{A}_{c}^{(2)}
into its uncorrelated and correlated components,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{c}^{(2)}(k,l,{P})=\mathcal{A}_{c}^{\text{uncor},\,(2)}(k,l,{P})+\mathcal{A}_{c}^{\text{cor},\,(2)}(k,l,{P})\,,

(26)

wherein the uncorrelated term is constructed from the product of one-emission collinear and soft amplitudes,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{c}^{\text{uncor},\,(2)}(k,l,{P})=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}_{c}^{(1)}(k,{P})\mathcal{A}_{s}^{(1)}(l)+(k\leftrightarrow l)\,.

(27)

Therefore only specific colour structures receive mixing contributions.
The starting point for the evaluation of (
23
) is the representation

\displaystyle{\Delta{\cal I}_{i\leftarrow j}^{(2)}(z,p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu,\nu)}

\displaystyle=\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}l}{(2\pi)^{3}}\;\delta(k^{2})\theta(k^{0})\,\delta(l^{2})\theta(l^{0})\,\delta\Big(k^{-}+l^{-}-(1-z)P^{-}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\mathcal{A}_{c}^{(2)}(k,l,{P})\;\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{\Delta}(p_{T}^{\rm veto};\{k,l\})\,\left(\frac{\nu}{k^{-}}\right)^{\alpha}\left(\frac{\nu}{l^{-}}\right)^{\alpha}\,,

(28)

where the rapidity regulator
\alpha
has been factored in. As the divergences in the dimensional regulator
\epsilon
are absent for the measurement (
25
), one can immediately set
d=4
in the calculation. To evaluate this contribution we employ the following phase-space parametrisation
Becher:2013xia
,

\displaystyle\Delta y=y_{k}-y_{l},\qquad\Delta\phi=\phi_{k}-\phi_{l},\qquad p_{T}=k_{T}+l_{T},\qquad{\xi}=\frac{k_{T}}{p_{T}}\,.

(29)

In the computation of the correlated term, we then utilize integration techniques as delineated in
Becher:2013xia
. Initially, we determine the asymptotic behaviour in the limit
R\rightarrow 0
by expanding the integrands around this limit. Moreover, the integral includes non-singular terms, which can be represented as a power series in
R^{2}
. To proceed, we first subtract the leading singularities as
\Delta\phi
and
\Delta y
approach zero, and we evaluate the coefficients of the power series in
R^{2}
numerically.

The evaluation of the uncorrelated contribution necessitates consideration of the mixing terms as discussed above. For this part, we rewrite the theta function
\theta(\Delta-R)
in (
25
) as
1-\theta(R-\Delta)
, which bifurcates the analysis into
R
-independent and
R
-dependent terms. The
R
-dependent terms are free of mixing contributions, and their computation parallels the treatment of the correlated part. Conversely, for the
R
-independent terms one starts from the measurement function

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{\Delta}^{\text{R-indep.}}(p_{T}^{\rm veto};\{k,l\})=\theta\big(p_{T}^{\rm veto}-|\vec{k}^{\perp}|\big)\theta\big(p_{T}^{\rm veto}-|\vec{l}^{\perp}|\big)-\theta\big(p_{T}^{\rm veto}-|\vec{k}^{\perp}+\vec{l}^{\perp}|\big)\,,

(30)

and the mixed collinear/soft contribution can in this case be calculated from

\displaystyle{\Delta{\cal I}_{i\leftarrow j}^{(2,cs)}(z,p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu,\nu)}=\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}l}{(2\pi)^{3}}\;\delta(k^{2})\theta(k^{0})\,\delta(l^{2})\theta(l^{0})\,\delta\Big(k^{-}-(1-z)P^{-}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}_{c}^{(1)}(k,{P})\,\mathcal{A}_{s}^{(1)}(l)\;\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{\text{R-indep.}}_{\Delta}(p_{T}^{\rm veto};\{k,l\})\,\left(\frac{\nu}{k^{-}}\right)^{\alpha}\left(\frac{\nu}{l^{-}+l^{+}}\right)^{\alpha}+(k\leftrightarrow l)\,,

(31)

where we stress that the rapidity regulator is retained in its original form (
4
) in the soft region. Due to the
n
-
\bar{n}
symmetry of the process, there is no need to evaluate the mixing between anti-collinear and soft contributions explicitly, and for the collinear/anti-collinear mixing one finds a scaleless integral that vanishes in the applied regularisation prescription. Finally, for the double soft emissions one has

\displaystyle\Delta{\cal S}_{ii^{\prime}}^{(2)}(p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}l}{(2\pi)^{3}}\;\delta(k^{2})\theta(k^{0})\,\delta(l^{2})\theta(l^{0})\,\left(\frac{\nu}{k^{-}+k^{+}}\right)^{\alpha}\left(\frac{\nu}{l^{-}+l^{+}}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\quad\times\mathcal{A}_{s}^{(2)}(k,l)\;\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{\text{R-indep.}}_{\Delta}(p_{T}^{\rm veto};\{k,l\})\,,

(32)

where
\mathcal{A}_{s}^{(2)}
denotes the soft double-emission amplitude, whose explicit expression can be found e.g. in
Becher:2012qc
.

Focusing on concreteness on the diagonal gluon channel, the various contributions can be put together and the final correction to the refactorised matching kernel in (
16
) can be obtained from

\displaystyle\Delta I_{g\leftarrow g}^{(2)}(z)

\displaystyle=\Delta{\cal I}_{g\leftarrow g}^{(2)}(z,p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu,\nu)+\Delta{\cal I}_{g\leftarrow g}^{(2,cs)}(z,p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\,\Delta{\cal S}_{gg}^{(2)}(p_{T}^{\rm veto},\mu,\nu)\,\delta(1-z)-\Delta d_{2}^{A}(R)\,\ln\frac{p_{T}^{\rm veto}}{Q}\,\delta(1-z)\,,

(33)

where the last term subtracts the contribution from the two-loop anomaly exponent, which we decompose as

\Delta d_{2}^{A}(R)=\Delta d_{2}^{C_{A}^{2}}(R)\,C_{A}^{2}+\Delta d_{2}^{n_{f}}(R)\,C_{A}T_{F}n_{f}
. Explicitly, we find

\displaystyle\Delta d_{2}^{C_{A}^{2}}(R)

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{524}{9}+\frac{16\pi^{2}}{3}+\frac{176}{3}\ln 2\right)\ln R+\frac{3220}{27}-\frac{44\pi^{2}}{9}-\frac{560}{9}\ln 2-\frac{176}{3}\ln^{2}2-48\zeta_{3}

\displaystyle\quad+17.9R^{2}-1.28R^{4}-0.0169R^{6}+0.000402R^{8}-2.62\cdot 10^{-5}R^{10}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta d_{2}^{n_{f}}(R)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{184}{9}-\frac{64}{3}\ln 2\right)\ln R-\frac{1256}{27}+\frac{16\pi^{2}}{9}+\frac{256}{9}\ln 2+\frac{64}{3}\ln^{2}2

\displaystyle\quad-0.706R^{2}+0.0142R^{4}-0.00217R^{6}+0.000135R^{8}-1.19\cdot 10^{-5}R^{10}\,,

(34)

which agrees with previous calculations
Becher:2013xia

. The extraction is less involved for the off-diagonal channels, and in each case, we can write the final expression for the refactorised matching kernels in the form

where the coefficient of the plus-distribution is given by the anomaly exponent in the representation

We conclude this section by noting that our semi-analytical approach is similar in spirit to the method that was used in
Abreu:2022zgo

First and foremost, our calculation uses a different rapidity regulator, which is purely analytic, and therefore all zero-bin subtractions are scaleless and vanish in our setup. Moreover, we use slightly different techniques for computing the uncorrelated-emission contribution, as well as a different reference observable than
Abreu:2022zgo

we show our results for the individual coefficients in this decomposition in Fig.

The Mellin-space kernels are particularly useful when the resummation is performed in Mellin space, as was done e.g. for joint threshold and transverse-momentum resummation in

5
Conclusion

We computed the beam-function matching kernels for jet-veto resummation to NNLO in QCD. Building on our earlier study of the respective quark beam function
Bell:2022nrj
, we provide the full set of matching kernels in this work. To this end, we applied two different computational methods, and we found perfect agreement between the two approaches. Our final results are displayed in Fig.
2
and Fig.
7
, and they are also provided in electronic form as supplementary material to this paper.

The jet-veto matching kernels were previously determined to NNLO in
Abreu:2022zgo
. We performed a detailed comparison to these results, paying special attention to the endpoint regions
z\to 0
and
z\to 1
, and found very good agreement for all kernels. This represents the first confirmation of the results in
Abreu:2022zgo
. In addition, we for the first time computed the NNLO matching kernels for the gluon channels in Mellin space.

Whereas our semi-analytical method is specific to the jet-veto observable, our numerical approach is generic, and it can therefore be applied to a much broader class of observables. In contrast to our previous study
Bell:2022nrj
, in which we determined the matching kernels in Mellin space to avoid distribution-valued expressions, we have extended our formalism in this work such that it becomes capable of calculating the matching kernels directly in momentum space. We view this extension as a major improvement of our framework, and we anticipate many further applications in the future. In the long term, we plan to provide a public code for the computation of NNLO beam functions in the spirit of the
SoftSERVE
distribution.

Appendix B
Details on the reference observable

Within the approach described in Sec.
3.2
, the jet-veto matching kernels are computed starting from the difference (
23
) with respect to a reference observable, for which we use the integrated
q_{T}
-spectrum according to (
24
). One then needs to add the matching kernels for this reference observable to the final expressions for the refactorised matching kernels given in (
35
).

The matching kernels for the reference observable can easily be derived from available results for transverse-momentum resummation. As the latter are usually given in position-space, one needs to Fourier-transform these expressions and to integrate them up to the
p_{T}^{\rm veto}
cut. Specifically, logarithms in the transverse-momentum framework then translate into
p_{T}^{\rm veto}
logarithms using the relation

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{p_{T}^{\rm veto}}\!\!\!\!{\rm d}q_{T}\;2q_{T}\;\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}x_{T}\;x_{T}\int_{0}^{2\pi}{\rm d}\varphi\;\;e^{iq_{T}x_{T}\cos\varphi}\;\ln^{n}\Big(\frac{x_{T}^{2}\mu^{2}}{4e^{-2\gamma_{E}}}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad=p_{T}^{\rm veto}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}x_{T}\;J_{1}\big(x_{T}\,p_{T}^{\rm veto}\big)\;\ln^{n}\Big(\frac{x_{T}^{2}\mu^{2}}{4e^{-2\gamma_{E}}}\Big)\,,

(58)

where
J_{1}(x)
is a Bessel function. For
n=0,1,2
there is thus a one-to-one correspondence between the two types of logarithms, whereas for
n=3,4
they differ by certain
\zeta_{3}
terms. This implies that the refactorised matching kernels for the reference observable are not precisely equal to the ones from transverse-momentum resummation for the diagonal channels. Particularly, we find

\displaystyle I_{i\leftarrow j}^{\text{ref},(2)}(z)=I_{i\leftarrow j}^{q_{T},(2)}(z)-\delta_{ij}\,\zeta_{3}\,\Gamma_{0}^{i}\bigg\{2P_{i\leftarrow j}^{(0)}(z)+\Big(d_{1}^{i}+2\gamma_{0}^{i}-\frac{2}{3}\beta_{0}\Big)\,\delta(1-z)\bigg\}\,,

(59)

where
I_{i\leftarrow j}^{q_{T},(2)}(z)
are the matching kernels for transverse-momentum resummation, for which we use the explicit expressions provided in
Gehrmann:2014yya
(setting the RG logarithms therein to zero).

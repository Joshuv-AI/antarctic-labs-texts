Shadowing, Hyers–Ulam stability and hyperbolicity for nonautonomous linear delay differential equations
Lucas Backes, Davor Dragičević, Mihály Pituk

Abstract
. It is known that hyperbolic nonautonomous linear delay differential equations in a finite dimensional space are Hyers–Ulam stable and hence shadowable. The converse result is available only in the special case of autonomous and periodic linear delay differential equations with a simple spectrum. In this paper, we prove the converse and hence the equivalence of all three notions in the title for a general class of nonautonomous linear delay differential equations with uniformly bounded coefficients. The importance of the boundedness assumption is shown by an example.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are interested in the relationship between the three notions in the title for a general class of nonautonomous linear delay differential equations in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The shadowing property and the closely related concept of Hyers–Ulam stability of a dynamical system requires that in a neighborhood of an approximate solution there exists a true solution. The nonautonomous counterpart of hyperbolicity is the notion of an exponential dichotomy. Roughly speaking, a linear delay equation admits an exponential dichotomy if, at each time
t
, the phase space can be decomposed into the direct sum two closed subspaces, called the stable and unstable subspace at
t
,
respectively,
such that along the stable subspaces the associated evolution family is exponentially contractive and along the unstable subspaces it is exponentially expanding. For the precise notions in the context of delay equations, see Definitions
1
–
3
in Sec.
2
.

Recently, it has been shown that hyperbolic nonautonomous linear delay equations are Hyers–Ulam stable and shadowable
[
2
]
, while the converse result has been proved only for autonomous and periodic equations under additional spectral conditions
[
4
]
. It should be emphasized that delay differential equations form a special case of infinite dimensional systems and for infinite dimensional systems, even in the simplest case of autonomous linear dynamics, the shadowing property alone does not imply hyperbolicity
[
7
, Theorem B and Remark 14]
. It is therefore a nontrivial question whether the shadowing property implies hyperbolicity for nonautonomous linear delay differential equations. In Sec.
2
, we give an affirmative answer to this question for a general class of linear delay equations with uniformly bounded coefficients. As we shall see in the proof, this follows from the eventual compactness of the solution operator and from the fact that shadowable linear delay differential equations have the so-called Perron property. The Perron property guarantees that the corresponding nonhomogeneous equation has at least one bounded solution for every bounded nonhomogeneity. For other variants of the Perron property and their consequences for the stability of delay differential equations, see
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
10
]
and the references therein. The Perron property, combined with the compactness of the solution operator, will be used to show that the stable subspaces are closed and have constant finite codimension. This implication is a consequence of a result due to Schäffer
[
17
]
about regular covariant subspaces corresponding to nonautonomous difference equations with compact coefficient operators in a Banach space. The importance of Schäffer’s result has been also recognized and used by Pecelli
[
15
]
in his study of admissibility of various pairs of function spaces for delay equations although he has not considered the case when the input and output spaces consist of bounded and continuous functions
which is needed for our purposes.

It follows from the above arguments that the
stable subspaces of
a shadowable linear delay equation are complemented and their complements, the corresponding unstable sets, have constant finite dimension. Solutions starting from the unstable sets can be continued backwards. At this point it is worth mentioning that Kurzweil
[
14
]
obtained a similar qualitative result. In
[
14
, Theorems 4.1 and 4.2]
, he proved that for nonautonomous linear delay differential equations with integrally bounded coefficients the space of those solutions which are (exponentially) bounded as
t\to-\infty
is finite dimensional and he provided estimates for its dimension.

After establishing the direct sum decomposition of the phase space into stable and unstable subspaces, we will use the Perron property again and a technique from the admissibility theory (
[
9
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
15
]
) to show the required exponential estimates of the exponential dichotomy. For a detailed exposition of the admissibility theory, see
[
3
]
and references therein.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
2
, we introduce the definitions and we formulate our main results. A brief comparison with existing results and the importance of the boundedness assumption are discussed as well. The proof of the main theorem is presented in Sec.
3
.

2.
Main result

Given
r\geq 0
, let
C=C([-r,0],\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the Banach space of all continuous maps
\phi\colon[-r,0]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
equipped with the supremum norm,
\|\phi\|:=\sup_{\theta\in[-r,0]}|\phi(\theta)|
for
\phi\in C
, where
|\cdot|
is any norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. As usual, the symbol
\mathcal{L}(C,\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the space of bounded linear operators from
C
into
\mathbb{R}^{d}
equipped with the operator norm.

Consider the nonautonomous linear delay differential equation

x^{\prime}(t)=L(t)x_{t},

(1)

where
L\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathcal{L}(C,\mathbb{R}^{d})
is continuous and
x_{t}\in C
is defined by
x_{t}(\theta)=x(t+\theta)
for
\theta\in[-r,0]
. Given
s\geq 0
, by a
solution of (
1
) on
[s,\infty)
, we mean a continuous function
x\colon[s-r,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
which is differentiable on
[s,\infty)
and satisfies (
1
) for all
t\geq s
. (By the derivative at
t=s
, we mean the right-hand derivative.) It is well-known (see
[
11
, Chap. 6]
) that, for every
s\geq 0
and
\phi\in C
, Eq. (
1
) has a unique solution
x
on
[s,\infty)
with initial value
x_{s}=\phi
. For
t\geq s\geq 0
, the
solution operator

T(t,s)\colon C\to C
is defined by

T(t,s)\phi=x_{t}
, where
x
is the unique solution of (
1
)
with
x_{s}=\phi
.

In the following definitions, we recall the notions of shadowing, Hyers–Ulam stability and exponential dichotomy for Eq. (
1
).

Definition 1
.

We say that Eq. (
1
) is
shadowable
if for each
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
with the following property: for each continuous function
y\colon[-r,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
which is continuously differentiable on
[0,\infty)
and satisfies

\sup_{t\geq 0}|y^{\prime}(t)-L(t)y_{t}|\leq\delta,

there exists a solution

x
of (
1
) on
[0,\infty)
such that

\sup_{t\geq 0}\|x_{t}-y_{t}\|\leq\varepsilon.

Definition 2
.

We say that Eq. (
1
) is
Hyers–Ulam stable
if there exists
\kappa>0
such that for each continuous function
y\colon[-r,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
which is continuously differentiable on
[0,\infty)
and satisfies

\sup_{t\geq 0}|y^{\prime}(t)-L(t)y_{t}|\leq\delta\qquad\text{for some $\delta>0$},

there exists a solution

x
of (
1
) on
[0,\infty)
such that

\sup_{t\geq 0}\|x_{t}-y_{t}\|\leq\kappa\delta.

Definition 3
.

We say that Eq. (
1
) admits an
exponential dichotomy
if there exist a family of projections
(P(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
C
and constants
D,\lambda>0
with the following properties:

•

for
t\geq s\geq 0
,

P(t)T(t,s)=T(t,s)P(s),

(2)

and
T(t,s)\rvert_{\Ker P(s)}\colon\Ker P(s)\to\Ker P(t)
is onto and invertible;

•

for
t\geq s\geq 0
,

\|T(t,s)P(s)\|\leq De^{-\lambda(t-s)};

(3)

•

for
0\leq t\leq s
,

\|T(t,s)Q(s)\|\leq De^{-\lambda(s-t)},

(4)

where
Q(s)=\text{\rm Id}-P(s)
for
s\geq 0
and
T(t,s):=\left(T(s,t)\rvert_{\Ker P(t)}\right)^{-1}
for
0\leq t\leq s
.

It follows immediately from the definitions that if Eq. (
1
) is Hyers–Ulam stable, then it is shadowable. The following result is a corollary of a more general theorem on weighted shadowing from
[
2
]
.

Theorem 1
.

[
2
, Theorem 2.3 (
\gamma=0
,
f\equiv 0
)]

If Eq. (
1
) has an exponential dichotomy, then it is Hyers–Ulam stable and hence shadowable.

The purpose of this paper is to show that if the linear operators
L(t)\colon C\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
t\geq 0
,
are uniformly bounded, then the converse of Theorem
1
is also true. The importance of the uniform boundedness of the coefficients will be illustrated by an example. Our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that

M:=\sup_{t\geq 0}\|L(t)\|<\infty.

(5)

If Eq. (
1
) is shadowable, then it has an exponential dichotomy.

The proof of Theorem
2
will be given in Sec.
3
.

As a corollary of Theorems
1
and
2
, we obtain that under condition (
5
) all three notions in the title are equivalent for Eq. (
1
).

Theorem 3
.

If (
5
) holds, then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

Eq. (
1
) is Hyers–Ulam stable;

(ii)

Eq. (
1
) is shadowable;

(iii)

Eq. (
1
) admits an exponential dichotomy.

Remark 1
.

Theorem
3
is a significant improvement of the recent results by Barreira and Valls
[
4
]
, where the equivalence of Hyers–Ulam stability and the existence of an exponential dichotomy
has been proved only in the rather special case of autonomous and periodic linear delay differential equations under the additional assumption that their spectrum is “simple” (see
[
4
, Theorems 4.3 and 4.7]
).

Remark 2
.

The boundedness assumption (
5
) in Theorems
2
and
3
cannot be omitted. This can be shown by the following example taken from
[
9
]
.
Let
v\colon[0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be a positive, continuously differentiable function satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}v(s)\,ds\leq v(t),\qquad t\geq 0,

(6)

\displaystyle v(t)\to\infty,\qquad t\to\infty,

(7)

and

\frac{v(n-\alpha_{n})}{v(n)}>n,\qquad n\in\mathbb{N},

(8)

where
\alpha=(\alpha_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of positive numbers such that
\alpha_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. For an explicitly given
v
and
\alpha
with the above
properties, see
[
9
, p. 131]
. Consider the scalar ordinary differential equation

x^{\prime}(t)=a(t)x(t),

(9)

where

a(t)=-\frac{v^{\prime}(t)}{v(t)},\qquad t\geq 0.

Eq. (
9
) is a special case of (
1
) when
r=0
,
d=1
and
L(t)\phi=a(t)\phi(0)
for
t\geq 0
and
\phi\in C
. The solutions of Eq. (
9
) have the form

x(t)=\frac{v(s)}{v(t)}x(s),\qquad t,s\in[0,\infty).

(10)

Hence

[T(t,s)\phi](0)=\frac{v(s)}{v(t)}\phi(0),\qquad t\geq s\geq 0,\quad\phi\in C,

which implies that

\|T(t,s)\|=\frac{v(s)}{v(t)},\qquad t\geq s\geq 0.

(11)

From this and (
6
), we find for
t\geq 0
,

\int_{0}^{t}\|T(t,s)\|\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}\frac{v(s)}{v(t)}\,ds\leq 1.

This shows that the assumptions of
[
2
, Theorem 2.2]
are satisfied with
f\equiv 0
,
w\equiv 1
,
P(t)\equiv\text{\rm Id}
,
Q(t)\equiv 0
,
M=1
and
c=0
. By the application of
[
2
, Theorem 2.2]
, we conclude that Eq. (
9
) is Hyers–Ulam stable and hence shadowable. We will show that Eq. (
9
) has no exponential dichotomy. Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that Eq. (
9
) has an exponential dichotomy. It follows from the definition that if Eq. (
1
) has an exponential dichotomy and all solutions of (
1
) are bounded, then
Q(s)=0
and hence
P(s)=\text{\rm Id}
for all
s\geq 0
, which implies that the exponential dichotomy is an exponential contraction, i.e

\|T(t,s)\|\leq De^{-\lambda(t-s)},\qquad t\geq s\geq 0.

(12)

Indeed, if
Q(s)\phi\neq 0
for some
s\geq 0
and
\phi\in C
, then (
4
) implies that the norm of the solution
x_{t}=T(t,s)\phi
of Eq. (
1
) tends to infinity exponentially as
t\to\infty
, which contradicts the boundedness of
x
.
Since Eq. (
9
) has an exponential dichotomy and (
7
) and (
10
) imply that all solutions of (
9
) are bounded, we have that (
12
) holds.
From (
11
) and (
12
), we obtain for
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\frac{v(n-\alpha_{n})}{v(n)}=\|T(n,n-\alpha_{n})\|\leq De^{-\lambda\alpha_{n}}.

From this, letting
n\to\infty
and taking into account that
\alpha_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, we conclude that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{v(n-\alpha_{n})}{v(n)}\leq D.

On the other hand, (
8
) implies that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{v(n-\alpha_{n})}{v(n)}=\infty
, which yields a contradiction.
Thus, Eq. (
9
) is Hyers–Ulam stable and shadowable, but it has no exponentially dichotomy.

3.
Proof of the main theorem

In this section, we a give a proof of Theorem
2
.
The key arguments are the following:

•

The shadowing property of Eq. (
1
) implies the Perron type property. Namely, the nonhomogeneous equation associated with Eq. (
1
) has at least one bounded solution for every bounded and continuous nonhomogeneity.

•

The Perron property, combined with the eventual compactness of the solution operator and Schäffer’s result about regular covariant sequences corresponding to compact linear operators in a Banach space, implies that the stable subspace of Eq. (
1
) is closed and has finite codimension.

•

The fact that the stable subspace is complemented yields a direct sum decomposition of the phase space into stable and unstable subspaces at each time instant in a standard manner.

•

The required exponential estimates along the stable and unstable directions are obtained by appropriate adaptation of a technique from the admissibility theory of ordinary differential equations to delay equations.

Now we present the details.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Suppose that Eq. (
1
) is shadowable. We will show that Eq. (
1
) admits an exponential dichotomy. For better transparency,
we split the proof into a series of auxiliary results.

Claim 1
.

Eq. (
1
) has the following
Perron type property
:
for each bounded and continuous function
z\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
there exists a bounded and continuous function
x\colon[-r,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
which is differentiable on
[0,\infty)
and satisfies

x^{\prime}(t)=L(t)x_{t}+z(t),\qquad t\geq 0.

(13)

Proof of Claim
1
.

Let
z\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an arbitrary bounded and continuous function. If
z(t)=0
identically for
t\geq 0
, then (
13
) is trivially satisfied with
x(t)=0
for
t\geq-r
. Now suppose that
z(t)\neq 0
for some
t\geq 0
so that
\|z\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{t\geq 0}|z(t)|>0
.
Choose a constant
\delta>0
corresponding to the choice of
\varepsilon=1
in Definition
1
. Take an arbitrary solution
y\colon[-r,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
of the nonhomogeneous equation

y^{\prime}(t)=L(t)y_{t}+\frac{\delta}{\|z\|_{\infty}}z(t),\qquad t\geq 0.

(The unique solution
y
with initial value
y_{0}=0
is sufficient for our purposes.) Since

\sup_{t\geq 0}|y^{\prime}(t)-L(t)y_{t}|\leq\delta,

according to Definition
1
, there exists a solution
\tilde{x}

of Eq. (
1
) on
[0,\infty)
such that

\sup_{t\geq-r}|\tilde{x}(t)-y(t)|=\sup_{t\geq 0}\|\tilde{x}_{t}-y_{t}\|\leq 1.

Define a continuous
x\colon[-r,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

x(t):=\frac{\|z\|_{\infty}}{\delta}(\,y(t)-\tilde{x}(t)\,),\qquad t\geq-r.

It can be easily verified that
x
satisfies (
13
) and

\sup_{t\geq-r}|x(t)|\leq\frac{\|z\|_{\infty}}{\delta}<\infty.

∎

For each
s\geq 0
, define

\mathcal{S}(s)=\bigl\{\phi\in C:\sup_{t\geq s}\|T(t,s)\phi\|<\infty\bigr\}.

Clearly,
\mathcal{S}(s)
is a subspace of
C
which will be called the
stable subspace of Eq. (
1
) at time
s\geq 0
.

Claim 2
.

For each
t\geq s\geq 0
, we have that

[\,T(t,s)\,]^{-1}(\mathcal{S}(t))=\mathcal{S}(s).

Proof of Claim
2
.

Let
t\geq s\geq 0
. If
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(s)
, then,

\sup_{\tau\geq t}\|T(\tau,t)T(t,s)\phi\|=\sup_{\tau\geq t}\|T(\tau,s)\phi\|\leq\sup_{\tau\geq s}\|T(\tau,s)\phi\|<\infty.

This shows that
T(t,s)\phi\in\mathcal{S}(t)
and hence
\phi\in[\,T(t,s)\,]^{-1}(\mathcal{S}(t))
.

Now suppose that
\phi\in[\,T(t,s)\,]^{-1}(\mathcal{S}(t))
. Then,
T(t,s)\phi\in\mathcal{S}(t)
, which implies that

\sup_{\tau\geq t}\|T(\tau,s)\phi\|=\sup_{\tau\geq t}\|T(\tau,t)T(t,s)\phi\|<\infty.

(14)

Let
x
denote the solution of Eq. (
1
) with
x_{s}=\phi
. It is well-known (see, e.g.,
[
11
, Sec. 2.2, Claim 2.1, p. 38]
) that the solution segment
x_{\tau}=T(\tau,s)\phi
is a continuous function of
\tau\in[s,\infty)
and hence it is bounded on the compact interval
[s,t]
. Therefore,
\sup_{s\leq\tau\leq t}\|T(\tau,s)\phi\|<\infty
, which, together with (
14
), implies that

\sup_{\tau\geq s}\|T(\tau,s)\phi\|<\infty
. Thus,
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(s)
.
∎

Claim 3
.

For
t\geq s\geq 0
, we have the algebraic sum decomposition

C=T(t,s)C+\mathcal{S}(t).

(15)

Proof of Claim
3
.

It is sufficient to prove the claim for
s=0
. Indeed, assuming that the desired conclusion holds for
s=0
, we now take an arbitrary
s>0
. Then, for every
t\geq s
and
\phi\in C
, there exist
\phi_{1}\in C
and
\phi_{2}\in\mathcal{S}(t)
such that
\phi=T(t,0)\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}
. Hence,

\phi=T(t,0)\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}=T(t,s)T(s,0)\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}\in T(t,s)C+\mathcal{S}(t).

Thus, (
15
) holds.
Therefore, from now on, we suppose that
s=0
. Since
T(0,0)=\text{\rm Id}
, for every
\phi\in C
, we have

\phi=T(0,0)\phi+0\in T(0,0)C+\mathcal{S}(0).

Thus, (
15
) holds for
t=s=0
. Now suppose that
t>0
and let

\phi\in C
be arbitrary. Choose a continuous function
\psi\colon[t-r,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with compact support contained in
[t,\infty)
such that
\int_{t}^{\infty}\psi(\tau)\,d\tau=1
. Let
u
denote the unique solution of Eq. (
1
) on
[t,\infty)
with initial value
u_{t}=\phi
. Set

\bar{\psi}(\tau):=\int_{\tau}^{\infty}\psi(\tau)\,d\tau\qquad\text{ for $\tau\geq t-r$}.

Finally, define
v\colon[t-r,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
z\colon[-r,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

v(\tau)=\bar{\psi}(\tau)u(\tau)\qquad\text{for $\tau\geq t-r$}

and

z(\tau)=\begin{cases}-\psi(\tau)u(\tau)+\bar{\psi}(\tau)L(\tau)u_{\tau}-L(\tau)(\bar{\psi}_{\tau}u_{\tau})&\quad\text{for $\tau\geq t$},\\
0&\quad\text{for $\tau\in[-r,t)$},\end{cases}

respectively.
Note that
z
is continuous on
[-r,\infty)
. The continuity of
z
at
t
follows from the relations
\psi(t)=0
,
\bar{\psi}(t)=1
,
\bar{\psi}_{t}\equiv 1
and
\bar{\psi}_{t}u_{t}=u_{t}
, which imply that
z(t)=0
. Since
\psi
has compact support, we have that
\psi(\tau)=\bar{\psi}(\tau)=0
for all large
\tau
. Hence,

\sup_{\tau\geq t-r}|v(\tau)|<\infty\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sup_{\tau\geq-r}|z(\tau)|<\infty.

By Claim
1
, there exists a continuous function
x\colon[-r,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
which is differentiable on
[0,\infty)
such that
\sup_{\tau\geq-r}|x(\tau)|<\infty
and (
13
) holds. It is straightforward to show that

v^{\prime}(\tau)=L(\tau)v_{\tau}+z(\tau),\qquad\tau\geq t.

Hence,
x-v
is a solution of Eq. (
1
) on
[t,\infty)
and thus

x_{\tau}-v_{\tau}=T(\tau,t)(x_{t}-v_{t})=T(\tau,t)(x_{t}-\phi),\qquad\tau\geq t.

From this and the boundedness of
x
and
v
, we conclude that
x_{t}-\phi\in\mathcal{S}(t)
. On the other hand, since
z\equiv 0
on
[0,t]
, we have that
x_{t}=T(t,0)x_{0}
. This implies that

\phi=x_{t}+(\phi-x_{t})=T(t,0)x_{0}+(\phi-x_{t})\in T(t,0)C+\mathcal{S}(t).

Since
\phi\in C
was arbitrary, we conclude that (
15
) holds for
s=0
.
∎

Claim 4
.

For each
s\geq 0
,
\mathcal{S}(s)
is the image of a Banach space under the action of a bounded linear operator.

Proof of Claim
4
.

Fix
s\geq 0
and
let
\mathcal{B}
denote the Banach space of all bounded and continuous functions
x\colon[s-r,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}

\|x\|_{\mathcal{B}}:=\sup_{t\geq s-r}|x(t)|<\infty,\qquad x\in\mathcal{B}.

Let
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
denote the set of all

which are solutions of (

) on
[s,\infty)
. We will show that
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{B}
. Let
(x^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
such that
x^{k}\to y
in
\mathcal{B}
for some

. Then,
x^{k}\to y
pointwise on
[s-r,\infty)

and
x^{k}_{s}\to y_{s}
in

as

. From the last limit relation, it follows by the continuous dependence of the solutions on the initial data (see

, Sec. 6.1, Corollary 1.1, p. 143]

locally uniformly on

as

, where

is the unique solution of Eq. (

) on

with initial value

. It follows from the uniqueness of the limit of

that

identically on

and hence

.
This shows that
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{B}
and hence it is a Banach space. Now define
\Phi\colon\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\to C
by
\Phi(x)=x_{s}
for
x\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
. Clearly,
\Phi
is a bounded linear operator with

\|\Phi\|\leq 1
and
\Phi(\mathcal{B}^{\prime})=\mathcal{S}(s)
.
∎

Claim 5
.

The stable subspace
\mathcal{S}(0)
of Eq. (
1
) is closed and has finite codimension in
C
.

As noted above, the proof of Claim
5
will be based on Schäffer’s result about closed covariant sequences associated with compact linear operators in a Banach space
[
17
]
. Before we formulate Schäffer’s result, let us recall the following notions.

Let
X
be a Banach space.
A subspace
S
of
X
is called
subcomplete in
X
if there exist a Banach space
Z
and a bounded linear operator
\Phi\colon Z\to X
such that
\Phi(Z)=S
.

Let
A\colon\mathbb{N}_{0}\to\mathcal{L}(X)
be an operator-valued map, where
\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\mathcal{L}(X)
denote the set of nonnegative integers and the space of bounded linear operators in

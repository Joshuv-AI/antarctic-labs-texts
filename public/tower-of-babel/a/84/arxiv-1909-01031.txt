Precise high moment asymptotics for parabolic Anderson model with log-correlated Gaussian field
Yangyang Lyu [8pt] a a School of Mathematics, Jilin University, Changchun 130012, China

Abstract




Abstract
In this paper, we consider the continuous parabolic Anderson model (PAM) driven by a time-independent log-correlated Gaussian field (LGF).
We obtain an asymptotic result of

\mathbb{E}\exp\Bigg\{\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{j,k=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(r))drds\Bigg\}\qquad(N\rightarrow\infty)

which is composed of the independent Brownian motions
\{B_{j}(s)\}
and the function
\gamma
approximating to a logarithmic potential at
0
, such as the covariances of massive free field and Bessel field. Based on the asymptotic result, we get the precise high moment asymptotics for Feynman-Kac formula of the PAM with LGF.

Keywords
Precise high moment asymptotics; Large deviation; Log-correlated Gaussian field; Massive free field; Bessel field

1
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the continuous parabolic Anderson model (PAM)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\triangle u(t,x)+u(t,x)V(x),\qquad(t,x)\in\mathrm{R}_{+}\times\mathrm{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\qquad x\in\mathrm{R}^{d},\end{array}\right.

with the log-correlated Gaussian field
V(x)
on
\mathrm{R}^{d}
,
which is given by a centered Gaussian family
\{\langle V,\varphi\rangle;\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{R}^{d})\}
with covariance

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\mathbb{E}[\langle V,\phi\rangle\langle V,\psi\rangle]=\int_{\mathrm{R}^{d}\times\mathrm{R}^{d}}\phi(x)\psi(y)k(x,y)dxdy\qquad\forall\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{R}^{d}).

(4)

Here,
\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{R}^{d})
is Schwartz space,
k(x,y)
is a positive definite kernel on
\mathrm{R}^{d}\times\mathrm{R}^{d}
and

\displaystyle k(x,y)=\log_{+}\frac{T}{|x-y|}+g(x,y),

(5)

where
\log_{+}x=(\log x)\vee 0
,
\log
is the natural logarithm, the constant
T>0
is called correlation length and
g(x,y)
is a bounded function on
\mathrm{R}^{d}\times\mathrm{R}^{d}
. For the details of LGF, see e.g.
[
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
.
Throughout the paper, we assume that the initial value
u_{0}(x)
satisfies

\displaystyle 0<\inf\limits_{x\in\mathrm{R}^{d}}u_{0}(x)\leq\sup\limits_{x\in\mathrm{R}^{d}}u_{0}(x)<+\infty.

(6)

Because Stratonovich integral is closer to “physical integral” than Skorokhod integral, we interpret the
uV
in (
1
) in the Stratonovich’s sense. For the details of Stratonovich and Skorokhod integral, see e.g.
[
11
]
.

There are many achievements for the continuous PAM with other random fields, such as Poisson potential, fractional noise, etc. (see e.g.
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
12
,
13
]
).
In view of the fruitful developments of LGF in quantum field theory, Wolfgang König proposed the study of the PAM with LGF in his book
[
14
]
. As far as we know, there are few results for the high moment asymptotics of solutions to the model.

The precise high moment asymptotic problem for the solution
u(t,x)
is to find a suitable rate
\sigma(N)
about positive integer
N
such that the quantity

\frac{1}{\sigma(N)}\log\mathbb{E}u^{N}(t,x)

converges to a nonzero constant as
N\rightarrow\infty
.
To some extent, it is meaningful to study the problem in physics.
One hand, in
[
9
,
10
]
, the precise high moment asymptotics is a key step in the process of computing spatial asymptotics. As explained in
[
8
]
, spatial asymptotics is one of the problems of “physical intermittency”
which is applied to describing the absence of diffusion of waves in a disordered medium in physics.
On the other hand, based on the precise high moment asymptotic result, the precise asymptotics of tail probability of
\log u(t,x)
can be obtained by nonnegative large deviation technique in
[
15
]
. Here, the
\log u(t,x)
represents the free energy of directed Brownian polymer in a continuous random environment.
The high moment asymptotic problem has attracted a lot of attention.
There have been many results for precise high moment asymptotics
when the covariances of driven Gaussian fields are homogeneous in time and space, or locally integrable in time and bounded in space, see
[
1
,
9
,
10
,
13
,
15
,
16
]
as references.

By the following (
10
) and the same method as
[
11
]
, it’s trivial to prove that if the Feynman-Kac formula of equation (
1
) exists then it is a mild solution in the Stratonovich’s sense. However, according to
[
11
,
12
]
, the uniqueness of the mild and weak solutions of PAM can’t be proved in the Stratonovich’s sense now.
Thus, this paper is only concerned with the precise high moment asymptotics of the Feynman-Kac formula of equation (
1
). We remark that,
in the Young’s sense, it can be proved that the Feynman-Kac formula is the unique mild solution for PAM with the Gaussian fields in
[
11
,
25
]
. The similar result might be also extended to the PAM with LGF, which will be our future work.

According to
[
12
]
, the Feynman-Kac formula for the solution to equation (
1
) is

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\mathbb{E}^{B}\left[\exp\left\{\int_{0}^{t}V(B^{x}(s))ds\right\}u_{0}(B^{x}(t))\right],

(7)

where
B^{x}
is a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion independent of
V
and starting at
x\in\mathrm{R}^{d}
and
\mathbb{E}^{B}
is the expectation with respect to the standard Brownian motion
B
.
For all
\varepsilon>0
, let
p_{\varepsilon}(x):=(2\pi\varepsilon)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\exp\{-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}|x|^{2}\}
and
V_{\varepsilon}(x):=\langle V(\cdot),p_{\varepsilon}(x-\cdot)\rangle
, then for all
t>0
, the time integral in (
7
) is defined as the
L^{2}
-limit

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}V(B(s))ds:=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{0}^{t}V_{\varepsilon}(B(s))ds-\mbox{ $L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})$}.

(8)

It’s easy to get that (
8
) is well defined by (
5
).
Conditioning on the Brownian motion, the
\int_{0}^{t}V(B(s))ds
is a centered Gaussian process with the conditional covariance
\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}k(B(s),B(r))dsdr
. Furthermore, by reference to
[
12
]
, the Feynman-Kac moment representation of (
7
) is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}u^{N}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\exp\Bigg\{\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{j,k=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}k(B_{j}^{x}(s),B_{k}^{x}(r))drds\Bigg\}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{N}u_{0}(B_{j}^{x}(t))\Bigg],

(9)

where
\{B_{j}^{x};j=1,\cdots,N\}
is a family of
d
-dimensional independent B.M. starting at
x
. By reference to
[
12
]
, the existence of (
7
) and (
9
) is provided by the exponential integrability of
\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}k(B(s),B(r))dsdr
.
In fact, by (
5
), there exists some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
C>0
such that

\displaystyle|k(x,y)|\leq|x-y|^{-\alpha}+C,\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

(10)

\displaystyle\mbox{then\qquad\qquad}\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}k(B(s),B(r))dsdr\right\}\leq e^{Ct^{2}}\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}|B(s)-B(r)|^{-\alpha}dsdr\right\}.\qquad\qquad

(11)

Hence, the exponential integrability of
\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}k(B(s),B(r))dsdr
is transformed into that of the right-hand side in (
11
).
To explain the exponential integrability of
\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}|B(s)-B(r)|^{-\alpha}dsdr
and prove the following Proposition
2.1
, we need to introduce a general framework.
Let
\gamma
be the Fourier transform of the tempered measure
\mu
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathrm{R}^{d})
, i.e.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\langle\gamma,\phi\rangle=\int_{\mathrm{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}(\phi)(\xi)\mu(d\xi),\qquad\qquad\forall\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{R}^{d}).

(12)

Here,
\mathcal{F}(\phi)(\xi):=\int_{\mathrm{R}^{d}}e^{i\xi\cdot x}\phi(x)dx
is the Fourier transform of
\phi
on
\mathrm{R}^{d}
.
For all
\varepsilon>0
, let
\mu_{\varepsilon}(d\xi):=\exp\left\{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|\xi|^{2}\right\}\mu(d\xi)
and
\gamma_{\varepsilon}(x):=\int_{\mathrm{R}^{d}}e^{i\xi\cdot x}\mu_{\varepsilon}(d\xi)
, then we define

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(B(s)-B(r))dsdr:=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma_{\varepsilon}(B(s)-B(r))dsdr-\mbox{ $L^{1}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})$}.

(13)

By (
13
) and Proposition 4.4 in
[
25
]
, it can be obtained that the Dalang’s condition

\int_{\mathrm{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)<+\infty
is equivalent to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(B(s)-B(r))dsdr\right\}<+\infty.

(14)

Becasue the Riesz potential
|\cdot|^{-\alpha}
in (
11
) satisfies the Dalang’s condition,
\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}k(B(s),B(r))dsdr
is exponentially integrable. Hence, (
7
) and (
9
) are well defined. Based on it, we obtain
the following main result about the precise high moment asymptotics for the Feynman-Kac formula (
7
) of the PAM with LGF.

Theorem 1.1
.

For the Feynman-Kac formula (
7
), it has

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N\lambda_{N}}\log\mathbb{E}u^{N}(t,x)=\frac{t^{2}}{2}.

(15)

Here,
\lambda_{N}
is a function about positive integer
N
, which satisfies
\lambda_{N}>e
and
\frac{\log\lambda_{N}}{\lambda_{N}}=\frac{2}{N}
when
N
is enough large.

Remark 1.1
.

There is usually no explicit solution for the equation
\frac{\log\lambda_{N}}{\lambda_{N}}=\frac{2}{N}
.
When
N
is enough large, the equation has two implicit solutions, and the
\lambda_{N}
is unique if we assume
\lambda_{N}>e
. Moreover, the
\lambda_{N}
is a regularly varying function about
N
and satisfies
\frac{1}{\lambda_{N}}=o(\frac{1}{N})
as
N\rightarrow\infty
.

2

2.1

3.1
. In Section
3

3.1

2
A general limit in the time-independent case

To prove Proposition
3.1
, we need the following Proposition
2.1
which is a general result for positive definite function in the time-independent case. In view of the time-space case, i.e.
\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma_{0}(s-r)\gamma(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(r))dsdr
, we call
\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(r))dsdr
and
\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq N}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(s))ds
time-independent case (
\gamma_{0}\equiv 1
) and time-white case (
\gamma_{0}=\delta_{0}
, i.e. Dirac delta function at
0
), respectively. Before Proposition
2.1
, the time-white case has been considered in
[
1
]
. When the
\mu
is finite in Proposition
2.1
, our method is partly inspired by
[
1
]
and we use the semigroup inequality in
[
2
]
. When the
\mu
is unbounded, we can’t transform the time-independent case into the time-white case because the
\sum_{1\leq j\neq k\leq N}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(s))ds
may be not exponentially integrable.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that the
\gamma
in (
12
) satisfies the Dalang’s condition, then for any sequence
\{t_{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying
t_{N}\rightarrow+\infty
as
N\rightarrow\infty
, it holds that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{Nt_{N}}\log\mathbb{E}\exp\Bigg\{\frac{1}{2Nt_{N}}\sum\limits_{j,k=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t_{N}}\int_{0}^{t_{N}}\gamma(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(r))dsdr\Bigg\}

(16)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sup\limits_{g\in\mathcal{F}_{d}}\Bigg\{\int_{\mathrm{R}^{d}}\Bigg|\int_{\mathrm{R}^{d}}e^{i\xi\cdot x}g^{2}(x)dx\Bigg|^{2}\mu(d\xi)-\int_{\mathrm{R}^{d}}|\nabla g(x)|^{2}dx\Bigg\}:=\mathcal{E}(\gamma)

(17)

and the variation
\mathcal{E}(\gamma)<+\infty
, where we define the set of functions

\mathcal{F}_{d}:=\left\{g\in W^{1,2}(\mathrm{R}^{d});\|g\|_{2}=1\right\}
.

To prove Proposition
2.1
, we need the following two Lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
t_{1},t_{2}>0
and positive integer
m
, it holds that

\displaystyle~\mathbb{E}\exp\Bigg\{\frac{1}{t_{1}+t_{2}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t_{1}+t_{2}}\int_{0}^{t_{1}+t_{2}}\gamma(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(r))dsdr\Bigg\}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle~\leq\mathbb{E}\exp\Bigg\{\frac{1}{t_{1}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\gamma(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(r))dsdr\Bigg\}\cdot\mathbb{E}\exp\Bigg\{\frac{1}{t_{2}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t_{2}}\int_{0}^{t_{2}}\gamma(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(r))dsdr\Bigg\}.

(18)

proof:

By the approximation in (
13
) and Jensen’s inequality, the following inequality is almost surely established.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t_{1}+t_{2}}\int_{\mathrm{R}^{d}}\Bigg|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t_{1}+t_{2}}e^{i\xi\cdot B_{j}(s)}ds\Bigg|^{2}\mu(d\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{t_{1}}\int_{\mathrm{R}^{d}}\Bigg|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}e^{i\xi\cdot B_{j}(s)}ds\Bigg|^{2}\mu(d\xi)+\frac{1}{t_{2}}\int_{\mathrm{R}^{d}}\Bigg|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{1}+t_{2}}e^{i\xi\cdot B_{j}(s)}ds\Bigg|^{2}\mu(d\xi).

(19)

Moreover, by (
19
) and strong Markov property, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\exp\Bigg\{\frac{1}{t_{1}+t_{2}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t_{1}+t_{2}}\int_{0}^{t_{1}+t_{2}}\gamma(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(r))dsdr\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\exp\Bigg\{\frac{1}{t_{1}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\gamma(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(r))dsdr\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\cdot\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\exp\Bigg\{\frac{1}{t_{2}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{m}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{1}+t_{2}}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{1}+t_{2}}\gamma(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(r))dsdr\Bigg\}\Bigg|\mathfrak{F}_{t_{1}}\Bigg]\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\exp\Bigg\{\frac{1}{t_{1}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\gamma(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(r))dsdr\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\cdot\Bigg(\sup\limits_{\tilde{b}}\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{b}}\exp\Bigg\{\frac{1}{t_{2}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t_{2}}\int_{0}^{t_{2}}\gamma(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(r))dsdr\Bigg\}\Bigg),

(20)

where
\tilde{b}=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{m})\in(R^{d})^{m}
and
\mathfrak{F}_{t_{1}}
is the
\sigma
-algebra up to
t_{1}
.
We claim that for any
t>0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\tilde{b}}\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{b}}\exp\Bigg\{\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j,k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(r))dsdr\Bigg\}\leq\mathbb{E}\exp\Bigg\{\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j,k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(r))dsdr\Bigg\}.

(21)

Indeed, by Taylor expansion and Bochner’s representation, for all integer
n\geq 1
and
\tilde{b}\in(R^{d})^{m}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{b}}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j,k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(r))dsdr\Bigg]^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t^{n}}\int_{\mathrm{R}^{dn}}\int_{[0,t]^{2n}}\sum_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{n}=1\atop k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}\prod\limits_{l=1}^{n}\mathrm{e}^{i\xi_{l}\cdot(b_{k_{l}}-b_{j_{l}})}\cdot\mathrm{e}^{i\xi_{l}\cdot(B_{k_{l}}(s_{l})-B_{j_{l}}(r_{l}))}\prod\limits_{l=1}^{n}ds_{l}dr_{l}\mu(d\xi_{l})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{t^{n}}\int_{\mathrm{R}^{dn}}\int_{[0,t]^{2n}}\sum_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{n}=1\atop k_{1},\cdots,k_{n}=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}\mathrm{e}^{i\sum\limits_{l=1}^{n}\xi_{l}\cdot(B_{k_{l}}(s_{l})-B_{j_{l}}(r_{l}))}\prod\limits_{l=1}^{n}ds_{l}dr_{l}\mu(d\xi_{l})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j,k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(r))dsdr\Bigg]^{n}.

(22)

Then, by (
20
) and (
21
), the proof is completed.

The following Lemma is a direct result of Lemma
2.1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any positive integer
m
,
n
and
t>0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\exp\Bigg\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j,k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{nt}\int_{0}^{nt}\gamma(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(r))dsdr\Bigg\}\leq\Bigg(\mathbb{E}\exp\Bigg\{\sum_{j,k=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(r))dsdr\Bigg\}\Bigg)^{n}.

(23)

The proof of Proposition
2.1
:

Step 1.
We prove (
17
) in the case that
\mu
is finite.
Firstly, we prove the lower bound of (
17
). When
\mu
is finite, by Bochner’s representation, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\exp\Bigg\{\frac{1}{2Nt_{N}}\sum\limits_{j,k=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t_{N}}\int_{0}^{t_{N}}\gamma(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(r))dsdr\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\exp\Bigg\{\frac{Nt_{N}}{2}\int_{\mathrm{R}^{d}}\Bigg|\frac{1}{Nt_{N}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t_{N}}\mathrm{e}^{i\xi\cdot B_{k}(s)}ds\Bigg|^{2}\mu(d\xi)\Bigg\}.

(24)

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a subspace of complex-value Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathrm{R}^{d},\mu)
satisfying
f(-x)=\overline{f(x)}
and
\langle f,h\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}:=\int_{\mathrm{R}^{d}}f(\xi)\overline{h(\xi)}\mu(d\xi)
for all
f,h\in\mathcal{H}
. Then, by (
24
),
\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\geq 2\langle f,h\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}-\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}
for all
f,h\in\mathcal{H}
and Theorem 4.1.6 in
[
2
]
, we have

\displaystyle\liminf\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{Nt_{N}}\log\mathbb{E}\exp\Bigg\{\frac{1}{2Nt_{N}}\sum\limits_{j,k=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t_{N}}\int_{0}^{t_{N}}\gamma(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(r))dsdr\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\geq\sup\limits_{f\in\mathcal{H}}\left\{\liminf\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t_{N}}\log\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\int_{0}^{t_{N}}f^{*}(B(s))ds\right\}-\frac{1}{2}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right\},

(25)

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{f\in\mathcal{H}}\sup\limits_{g\in\mathcal{F}_{d}}\left\{\int_{\mathrm{R}^{d}}f^{*}(x)g^{2}(x)dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathrm{R}^{d}}|\nabla g(x)|^{2}dx-\frac{1}{2}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{g\in\mathcal{F}_{d}}\sup\limits_{f\in\mathcal{H}}\left\{\langle f,\mathcal{F}(g^{2})\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}-\frac{1}{2}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathrm{R}^{d}}|\nabla g(x)|^{2}dx\right\}=\mathcal{E}(\gamma),

(26)

where
f^{*}(x)=\int_{\mathrm{R}^{d}}e^{i\xi\cdot x}f(-\xi)\mu(d\xi)
and the last equality is due to
\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\sup\limits_{f\in\mathcal{H}}\{2\langle f,h\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}-\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\}
.

Secondly, we prove the upper bound of (
17
) when
\mu
is finite. We first assume that
t_{N}/t
is always an integer for all positive integer
N
and fixed
t>0
.
By Lemma
2.2
and Bochner’s representation, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Nt_{N}}\log\mathbb{E}\exp\Bigg\{\frac{1}{2Nt_{N}}\sum\limits_{j,k=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t_{N}}\int_{0}^{t_{N}}\gamma(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(r))dsdr\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{Nt}\log\mathbb{E}\exp\Bigg\{\frac{Nt}{2}\int_{\mathrm{R}^{d}}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{i\xi\cdot B_{k}(s)}ds\right|^{2}\mu(d\xi)\Bigg\}.

(27)

For all positive integer
1\leq k\leq N
, define
X_{k}:=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{i\xi\cdot B_{k}(s)}ds
and
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}X_{k}
. Let
\left\{X,X_{k}\right\}_{1\leq k\leq N}
be a sequence of i.i.d
\mathcal{H}
-valued random variables. Apparently, the
X
satisfies the exponential moment condition:

\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\theta\left\|X\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}\right\}<\infty
for all
\theta>0.

Let Cramér function and its Legendre transform be

\Lambda(y):=\log\mathbb{E}\exp\left\{\left\langle y,X\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right\}\quad\forall y\in\mathcal{H}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Lambda^{*}(x):=\sup\limits_{y\in\mathcal{H}}\left\{\langle y,x\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}-\Lambda(y)\right\}\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{H},

respectively.
By Donsker-Varadhan’s large deviation principle (LDP) in a separable Banach space (Theorem 5.3,
[
3
]
), the distributions
\Big\{\mathbb{P}\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}X_{k}\in\cdot\Big)\Big\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfy LDP in
\mathcal{H}
with the rate function
\Lambda^{*}(x)
. By
\|X_{k}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq\mu(\mathrm{R}^{d})
for all
1\leq k\leq N
and Varadhan integral Lemma (theorem 4.3.1,
[
4
]
), we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{E}\exp\Bigg\{\frac{Nt}{2}\int_{\mathrm{R}^{d}}\Bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{i\xi\cdot B_{k}(s)}ds\Bigg|^{2}\mu(d\xi)\Bigg\}=\sup\limits_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\left\{\frac{t}{2}\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}-\Lambda^{*}(h)\right\}.

(28)

Here, we claim that for all

\displaystyle\leq\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon_{1}\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{2p}\sup\limits_{g\in\mathcal{F}_{d}}\Bigg\{(1+\varepsilon)\varepsilon_{1}p\int_{\mathrm{R}^{d}\times\mathrm{R}^{d}}(|x-y|^{-\alpha}+C)g^{2}(x)g^{2}(y)dxdy-\int_{\mathrm{R}^{d}}|\nabla g(x)|^{2}dx\Bigg\}=0.

For the error term (

Indeed,

Moreover, by the same computations as (
52
)-(

Second, we prove the lower bound. For any parameter

\displaystyle\cdot\Bigg[\mathbb{E}\exp\Bigg\{\frac{-1}{2(p-1)\sigma_{N}^{2}}\sum\limits_{j,k=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t_{N}}\int_{0}^{t_{N}}(\gamma(1-\gamma_{\delta}))(\sigma_{N}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(B_{j}(s)-B_{k}(r)))dsdr\Bigg\}\Bigg]^{(1-p)}.

On Weakly Distinguishing Graph Polynomials
Johann A. Makowsky, Vsevolod Rakita

Abstract
A univariate graph polynomial P ⁡ ( G , X ) P(G;X) is weakly distinguishing if for almost all finite graphs G G there is a finite graph H H with P ⁡ ( G , X ) = P ⁡ ( H , X ) P(G;X)=P(H;X) . We show that the clique polynomial and the independence polynomial are weakly distinguishing. Furthermore, we show that generating functions of induced subgraphs with property C C are weakly distinguishing provided that C C is of bounded degeneracy or treewidth. The same holds for the harmonious chromatic polynomial.

The Degree Polynomial

Definition 4
.

For a graph

of order

, denote by

.
Define the Degree polynomial of

. Thus, we get

\beta_{Deg}(n)\leq(n+1)^{n-1}\leq(n+1)^{n}

Now we are ready to prove theorem

:

Theorem
1
: Almost all graphs

have a

-mate.

Proof.

Let

be a graph with
|V(G)|=n
.
We now evaluate:

\\
\\
\begin{aligned} \lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{U_{Deg}(n)}{|\mathcal{G}(n)|}\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{\beta_{Deg}(n)}{|\mathcal{G}(n)|}\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{n^{n}}{|\mathcal{G}(n)|}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{(n+1)^{n}n!}{2^{n(n-1)/2}}\\
\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{(n+1)^{n}\cdot(n+1)^{n}}{2^{n(n-1)/2}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{(n+1)^{2n}}{2^{n(n-1)/2}}=0\end{aligned}\\
\\

∎

4
A General Method for Proving Graph Polynomials are Weakly Distinguishing

We wish to apply the same idea used in proving the degree polynomial is weakly distinguishing
to a large class of graph polynomials. We start with some lemmas.
First, we show that if a graph polynomial
P
is weakly distinguishing on a
large subset of
\mathcal{G}
, it is weakly distinguishing:

Lemma 6
.

Let
P
be a graph polynomial and
\mathcal{C}
a family of graphs such that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}|\mathcal{C}(n)|/|\mathcal{G}(n)|=1
.
If
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}|U_{P}(n)\cap\mathcal{C}|/|\mathcal{G}(n)|=0
then
P
is weakly distinguishing.

Proof.

\dfrac{|U_{P}(n)|}{|\mathcal{G}(n)|}=\dfrac{|U_{P}(n)\cap\mathcal{C}|+|U_{P}(n)\cap(\mathcal{G}(n)-\mathcal{C})|}{|\mathcal{G}(n)|}=\dfrac{|U_{P}(n)\cap\mathcal{C}|}{|\mathcal{G}(n)|}+\dfrac{|U_{P}(n)\cap(\mathcal{G}(n)-\mathcal{C})|}{|\mathcal{G}(n)|}

When taking the limit, note that the left term in the sum converges to 0 by assumption, so it remains to evaluate:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{|U_{P}(n)\cap(\mathcal{G}-\mathcal{C})|}{|\mathcal{G}(n)|}\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{|\mathcal{G}(n)-\mathcal{C}|}{|\mathcal{G}(n)|}=0

∎

Lemma 7
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. If
f(n)\leq(\log n)^{O(1)}
, then asymptotically

{n\choose f(n)}^{f(n)}\leq(\dfrac{n}{f(n)})^{f(n)+nf(n)}\dfrac{1}{(2\pi)^{f(n)/2}\cdot n^{f(n)/2}}

Proof.

By applying the Stirling approximation
k!=\sqrt{2\pi k}(\frac{k}{e})^{k}
we evaluate:

\\
\begin{aligned} {n\choose f(n)}^{f(n)}=(\dfrac{n!}{f(n)!(n-f(n))!})^{f(n)}\\
\\
\approx(\dfrac{\sqrt{2\pi n}(n/e)^{n}}{\sqrt{2\pi f(n)}(f(n)/e)^{f(n)}\sqrt{2\pi(n-f(n))}((n-f(n))/e)^{(n-f(n))}})^{f(n)}\\
\\
=(\dfrac{n^{n}}{(f(n))^{f(n)}(n-f(n)^{n-f(n)}}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{2\pi f(n)(n-f(n)}})^{f(n)}\\
\\
\leq(\dfrac{n}{2\pi f(n)\cdot f(n)})^{f(n)/2}\cdot\dfrac{n^{nf(n)}}{f(n)^{nf(n)}}\end{aligned}\\
\\

where the inequality is due to
f(n)\leq n-f(n)
for a sufficiently large
n
.

\\
\\
\begin{aligned} (\dfrac{n}{2\pi f(n)\cdot f(n)})^{f(n)/2}\cdot\dfrac{n^{nf(n)}}{f(n)^{nf(n)}}=(\dfrac{n}{f(n)})^{f(n)+nf(n)}\dfrac{1}{(2\pi)^{f(n)/2}\cdot n^{f(n)/2}}\end{aligned}\\
\\

∎

Our main tool for proving graph polynomials are weakly distinguishing is theorem
3
, which provides a sufficient condition for a polynomial

to be weakly distinguishing. This condition is given in terms of independence and clique functions (see definition
1
). We will prove theorem

using the following theorems:

Theorem 8

(Frieze
[
7
]
)
.

For a graph
G
, denote by
\alpha(G)
the size of the largest independent set of vertices in
G
.
Then for almost all graphs of order
n

\alpha(G)\approx 4\log\frac{n}{2}

Theorem 9

(Erdös and Bollobás
[
2
]
)
.

For almost all graphs

of order
n

\omega(G)\approx\frac{2}{\log 2}\cdot\log n

We are now ready to prove theorem

:

Theorem
3
:
Let
Q
be a graph property that has an independence or a clique function

f
that satisfies that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

f(n)\geq n/a
for some fixed
a\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

is weakly distinguishing.

Proof.

Assume

is an independence function.
Set
\epsilon=1/10
and let
\mathcal{C}=\{G:\alpha(G)\leq 4\log\frac{n}{2}+\epsilon\}
.
By theorem

, almost all graphs are in

.
Note that if
G\in\mathcal{C}
, and

is an induced subgraph of

with

,
then there is an independent set of size
\frac{|V(H)|}{a}
in

and so

This implies that

with

for all

\beta_{P_{Q},\mathcal{C}}(n)\leq{n\choose 4\log\frac{|V(G)|}{2}+\epsilon}^{4\log\frac{|V(G)|}{2}+\epsilon}

hence by lemmas

If

∎

Applications of the Method

5.1
The Clique and Independence Polynomials

Definition 5
.

Let
G

, denote

The clique polynomial of

Note that this is a graph polynomial, and that the sum in the definition is finite.
The clique number of

, denoted

, is the degree of the clique polynomial

).

Definition 6
.

Let

, denote

.
The independence polynomial of

is defined to be
Ind(G,x)=1+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}s_{i}(G)x^{i}
.
Note that this is a graph polynomial, and that the sum in the definition is finite.
The independence number of

, denoted

, is the degree of the independence polynomial
(i.e. this is the size of the largest independent set in

).

Theorem

is now a direct corollary of theorem

:

2
: Almost all graphs
G
have an

Proof.

For the independence polynomial, note that

, were

is the property consisting of edgeless graphs. Note that the identity function on

is an independence function for

, and clearly it satisfies the condition in theorem

for

. Hence the independence polynomial is weakly distinguishing.

Similarly, for the clique polynomial note that

is the property of complete graphs. Note that the identity function on

is a clique function for

5.2
Generating Functions

Theorem
3
can be applied to many graph classes to produce weakly distinguishing graph polynomials.
Of particular interest are
k
-degenerate classes and amongst them classes of bounded treewidth.

For a graph
G
, and

denote by

N_{G}(v)
the closed neighbourhood of

in

, i.e.

N_{G}(v)=\{v\}\cup\{u\in V(G):\{v,u\}\in E(G)\}

Definition 7
.

For

, a graph

is said to be

has a vertex of degree at most

.

The following propositions
9.1
,

and lemma

are well known results about degenerate graphs and treewidth. For completeness, we include their proofs:

Proposition 9.1
.

A graph
G=(V,E)
is
k

\{v_{1},v_{2},...,v_{n}\}=V
such that for all
1\leq i\leq n
the degree of
v_{i}

in the subgraph of

induced by
V-\{v_{1},v_{2},...,v_{i-1}\}
is at most
k
.

Proof.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a
k

v\in V
with degree at most
k
. Denote this vertex by
v_{1}
.
Define
v_{i}
inductively: from the definition, the subgraph induced by
V-\{v_{1},...,v_{i-1}\}
has a vertex with degree at most
k
.
Define
v_{i}
to be this vertex. Clearly, the enumeration
\{v_{1},...,v_{n}\}=V
has the desired property.

Conversely, let
\{v_{1},...,v_{n}\}
an enumeration as in the theorem, and let
H
be a subgraph of
G
induced by
U\subseteq V
.
Denote
u=v_{i}
the vertex in
H
who’s index in the enumeration is the smallest. Note that the degree of

in

is at most
k
, and
H
is a subgraph of

, hence the degree of

in

is at most

.
So

is

∎

Lemma 10
.

A graph with treewidth at most
k
has a vertex with degree at most
k
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a graph, and
(T,X)
a tree decomposition of
G
with width

.
Note that
T
has a leaf, and there is a vertex
v
in the bag corresponding to this

Thus every neighbour of

in

is in the same bag. But the bag is of size at most

, so

is of degree at most

.
∎

Proposition 10.1
.

A graph

with treewidth at most

is

Proof.

Let
H
be an induced subgraph of

. If

, then

has a vertex of degree at most

by the previous lemma.
If

is a proper subgraph, note that

has treewidth at most

, so

has a vertex of degree at most

. So

∎

The following proposition shows that a
k
degenerate graph has a large independent set:

Proposition 10.2
.

Every
k
degenerate graph
G
has an independent set of size
{\left\lceil\frac{|V|}{k+1}\right\rceil}
.

Proof.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a
k
degenerate graph, and
\{v_{1},...,v_{n}\}
be an enumeration as in proposition
9.1
. Let
I_{0}=\emptyset
, and
H_{0}=G
. We construct an independent set inductively as follows. There exists
1\leq l\leq n
and an increasing sequence
i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{l}
in
\{1,2,...,n\}
with
i_{1}=1
such that for all
1\leq j\leq l

\displaystyle I_{j}=I_{j-1}\cup\{v_{i_{j}}\}

\displaystyle H_{j}=G[V(H_{j-1})-N_{G}(v_{j})]

with
I_{l}
an independent set in
G
and
l\geq{\left\lceil\frac{n}{k+1}\right\rceil}
.

Indeed,
I_{1}=\{v_{1}\}
and
H_{1}=G[V(G)-N_{G}(v_{1})]
. Clearly,
I_{1}
is an independent set, and note that
|V(H_{1})|\geq n-(k+1)
and no vertex in
H_{1}
is a neighbour of the vertex in
I_{1}
. Now, given an independent set
I_{j}
and an induced subgraph
H_{j}
of
G
such that
|V(H_{j})|\geq n-j(k+1)
and no vertex of
H_{j}
is a neighbour of a vertex in

, select

with minimal index. Now

is an independent set, no vertex of

is a neighbour of a vertex in

, and since

,

|V(H_{j+1})|\geq|V(H_{j})|-(k+1)\geq n-(j+1)(k+1)
.

The induction stops when no more vertices can be selected, i.e. when
V(H_{l})=\emptyset
. From the induction, we have that
0=|V(H_{l})|\geq n-l(k+1)
and hence
l\geq{\left\lceil\frac{n}{k+1}\right\rceil}
as required.
∎

Combining this proposition with theorem

Corollary 10.2

, and let

Theorem
4
:
Almost all graphs

-mate.

Proof.

Let

be the class of graphs

there is a vertex

Note that any harmonious colouring of a graph

the evaluation

G
at
\lambda
is
\chi_{harm}(G,\lambda)=\lambda(\lambda-1)(\lambda-2)...(\lambda-n+1)
.
Since this is true for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
, by interpolation it is true for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
and so every two graphs in
\mathcal{C}
of the same order have the same harmonious polynomial.
Hence, there is an
n_{0}
such that all graphs in
\mathcal{C}
of order greater than
n_{0}
have an
\chi_{harm}
-mate.
Thus the harmonious polynomial is weakly distinguishing.
∎

This result can be easily generalised.

Definition 9
.

For a fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
and a graph
G
,
we say that a proper colouring of
G
with
\lambda
colours
f:V(G)\rightarrow[\lambda]
is
k
-harmonious
if for every
S\subseteq[\lambda]
such that
|S|=k
,
S
appears as the colour set of a clique in the graph at most once,
i.e. if
\{v_{1},v_{2},...,v_{k}\},\{u_{1},...,u_{k}\}\subseteq V(G)
induce complete graphs of size
k
and

f(\{v_{1},v_{2},...,v_{k}\})=f(\{u_{1},...,u_{k}\})
, then
\{v_{1},v_{2},...,v_{k}\}=\{u_{1},...,u_{k}\}
.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
define
h_{k}(G,\lambda)=|\{f:V(G)\rightarrow[\lambda]:f
is proper and
k
-harmonious
\}|
.

h_{k}(G,\lambda)
is a polynomial in
\lambda
(again, by
[
12
]
).
We will prove that
h_{k}
is weakly distinguishing.

We start with a lemma:

Lemma 11
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the class of graphs
G
with the property that for every two vertices
v,u\in V(G)

there are vertices
w_{1},...,w_{k}\in V(G)
such that
\{u,w_{1},...,w_{k}\}
and
\{v,w_{1},...,w_{k}\}
induce a complete graph.
Then almost all graphs are in
\mathcal{C}
.

For convenience, we restate and prove the lemma in probabilistic language:

Lemma 12
.

Fix
p\in(0,1)
and let
G\in\mathcal{G}(n,p)
(i.e.
G
is a graph with
n

vertices and every edge is in the graph with probability
p
, independently of the others).
Then
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(G\in\mathcal{C})=1

Proof.

For a graph
G
, denote by
Y_{k}(G)
the number of
k
cliques in
G
.
For fixed
u,v\in V(G)
, denote by
G_{u,v}
the subgraph of
G
induced by the common neighbours of
v
and
u
.
Note that
\mathbb{E}(|V(G_{u,v})|)=(n-2)p^{2}
, so from the multiplicative Chernoff bound,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[|V(G_{u,v})|\leq\frac{1}{9}(n-2)p^{2}]\leq\left(\frac{e^{-9/10}}{(1/9)^{1/9}}\right)^{(n-2)p^{2}}<e^{-\frac{1}{200}(n-2)p^{2}}

Hence, from the union bound

\mathbb{P}[\exists u,v\in V(G)s.t.|V(G_{u,v})|\leq\frac{1}{9}(n-2)p^{2}]\leq{n\choose 2}e^{-\frac{1}{200}(n-2)p^{2}}

Denote this number
r_{p}(n)
, and note that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}r_{p}(n)=0
.

Next, assume that
\forall u,v\in V(G)
,
|V(G_{u,v})|>\frac{1}{9}(n-2)p^{2}
. Fix
u,v\in V(G)
. Then
G_{u,v}
is a random graph with more than
\frac{1}{9}(n-2)p^{2}
vertices, and hence
\mathbb{P}[Y_{k}(G_{u,v})<1]\leq\mathbb{P}[Y_{k}(G^{\prime})<1]
were
G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}(\frac{1}{9}(n-2)p^{2},p)
.
From theorem 2 in
[
1
]
, we have:

\mathbb{P}[Y_{k}(G^{\prime})<1]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[Y_{k}(G^{\prime})\leq\frac{9}{10}\left(\frac{1}{9}(n-2)p^{2}\right)^{3/2}\right]\leq\exp\left[-(1/100+\alpha\sqrt{\frac{1}{9}(n-2)p^{2}})\right]

for some constant
\alpha>0
. Hence, if we denote
\mathcal{A}=\{G:\exists u,v\in V(G)s.t.|V(G_{u,v})|\leq\frac{1}{9}(n-2)p^{2}\}
, from the union bound we have:

\mathbb{P}\left[\exists u,v\in V(G)s.t.Y_{k}(G_{u,v})<1|G\not\in\mathcal{A}\right]\leq{n\choose 2}\exp\left[-(1/100+\alpha\sqrt{\frac{1}{9}(n-2)p^{2}})\right]

Denote the right side of this inequality by
r^{\prime}(n)
, and note that
r^{\prime}(n)\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

To conclude, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[G\not\in\mathcal{C}]=\mathbb{P}[G\in\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{C}^{c}]+\mathbb{P}[G\in\mathcal{A}^{c}\cap\mathcal{C}^{c}]\leq

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}[G\in\mathcal{A}]+\mathbb{P}[G\in\mathcal{A}^{c}]\mathbb{P}[G\in\mathcal{C}^{c}|G\in\mathcal{A}^{c}]=r_{p}(n)+(1-r_{p}(n))r^{\prime}_{p}(n)

and both these terms tend to 0 as
n
tends to infinity.
∎

We can now prove:

Theorem 13
.

For a fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
h_{k}
is weakly distinguishing.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the same as in the lemma. Similarly to the previous theorem, note that if
G\in\mathcal{C}
,
then for
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
any
k
-harmonious colouring
f:V(G)\rightarrow[\lambda]
of
G
must assign
a different colour to every vertex (otherwise, if
v,u\in V(G)
are such that

f(v)=f(u)
, and
w_{1},...,w_{k}\in V(G)
are such that
\{u,w_{1},...,w_{k}\}
and
\{v,w_{1},...,w_{k}\}

induce a complete graph in
G
, then
f(\{u,w_{1},...,w_{k}\})=f(\{v,w_{1},...,w_{k}\})
),
and thus
h_{k}(G,\lambda)=\lambda(\lambda-1)(\lambda-2)...(\lambda-n+1)
.
By the same reasoning as in the proof of theorem
4
,
h_{k}
is weakly distinguishing.
∎

7
Conclusion

We have shown for many graph properties
Q
that the polynomials
P_{Q}
are weakly distinguishing, including the well studied clique and independence polynomials. We have also shown that the harmonious and k-harmonious polynomials are weakly distinguishing.

Our results relied on the fact that the number of polynomials that can be the
P_{Q}
polynomial of a graph is small, and on the fact that almost all graphs have properties that imply their harmonious and k-harmonious polynomials are trivial. This does not seem to be the case for the Tutte and the chromatic polynomials, so the original question of whether they, as well as the characteristic and matching
polynomials, are weakly distinguishing remains open.

Q
such that the fraction of
P_{Q}
unique graphs is strictly positive.

P

G,H
two graphs, and write
G\sim_{P}H
if
P(G)=P(H)
. What can be said about the sizes of the equivalence classes of
\sim_{P}
?

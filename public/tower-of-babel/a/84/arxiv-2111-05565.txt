Abstract
We find the best possible constant C C in the inequality ‖ φ ‖ L r ⩽ C ​ ‖ φ ‖ L p p r ​ ‖ φ ‖ BMO 1 − p r \|\varphi\|_{L^{r}}^{\phantom{\frac{p}{r}}}\leqslant C\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{p}{r}}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{BMO}}^{1-\frac{p}{r}} for all possible values of parameters p p and r r such that 1 ⩽ p < r < + ∞ 1\leqslant p<r<+\infty . We employ the Bellman function technique to solve this problem. The Bellman function of three variables corresponding to this problem has a rather complicated structure, however, we managed to provide the explicit formulas for this function. First, we solve the problem on an interval and then transfer our results to the circle and the line. We also obtain explicit estimates in multi-dimensional cases. 2010 MSC subject classification : 42B35, 60G45. Keywords : bounded mean oscillation, Bellman function, interpolation.

Lebesgue spaces and

Simple properties of the optimization problem

The domain of the functions

Proposition 2.2

The set

\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{3}=\Big\{{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\colon{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Omega^{2}_{\varepsilon},\;x_{3}\in\big[\boldsymbol{B}^{-}_{p;\varepsilon}(x_{1},x_{2}),\boldsymbol{B}^{+}_{p;\varepsilon}(x_{1},x_{2})\big]}\Big\}

there is a test function

. The proof of Proposition

(Proposition 2.2 there).

In what follows by the skeleton of

we mean the set of points
\{(t,t^{2},|t|^{p})\colon t\in\mathbb{R}\}

A function

/

We collect standard facts concerning Bellman functions of such kind.

Proposition 2.3
.

1)

The functions
\boldsymbol{B}^{\pm}_{p,r}
satisfy the boundary conditions on the skeleton
of
\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{3}
:

\boldsymbol{B}^{\pm}_{p,r}(t,t^{2},|t|^{p})=|t|^{r},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.18)

2)

The function
\boldsymbol{B}_{p,r}^{+}
is locally concave and the
function
\boldsymbol{B}_{p,r}^{-}
is locally convex on
\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{3}
.

3)

The function
\boldsymbol{B}_{p,r}^{+}
is the pointwise minimal among all locally concave

\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{3}

that satisfy the boundary condition (

). The

\boldsymbol{B}_{p,r}^{-}
is the pointwise maximal among all locally convex
on
\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{3}
functions
B
that satisfy the boundary condition (

).

Proposition

is proved in
[
9
]
for the case of concave functions
(see Proposition 2.3 there). The proof of the other case is literally the same.

In view of Proposition

, to find the Bellman function
\boldsymbol{B}^{\pm}_{p,r}

-smooth

(ii)

the function

H(u,0)=W_{+}(u-\varepsilon),\qquad H\Big(u,\tfrac{1}{2}\big((u+\varepsilon)^{p}-(u-\varepsilon)^{p}\big)-\varepsilon m_{p}(u-\varepsilon)\Big)=H_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}(u).

We see that
\sign h=\sign(2-p)
, because the point
W_{+}(v)
is on the lower boundary of the domain
if
p<2
and on the upper boundary if
p>2
.

Since
B
has to be linear along the extremal
[U(v),H(u,h)]
, we write

B(x)=v^{r}+K_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}(u,h)(x_{1}-v),\qquad x\in[U(v),H(u,h)],

(4.7)

where
K_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}=K_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}(u,h)
is the unknown slope of this linear function. Now, we find the slope
K_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}

using the property of
\grad B
to be constant along the extremal. First, we have to calculate
the partial derivatives of the variables
u
and
h
.

The function
u=u(x)
is defined by (
4.3
),
v=u-\varepsilon=x_{1}-\Delta_{-}
, whence

\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}=\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{2}}=\frac{1}{2(x_{1}-u)};\qquad\quad\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{3}}=\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{3}}=0.

(4.8)

The function
h=h(x)
is determined by the fact that the points
H(u,h)
,
x
, and
U(v)
lie
on the same straight line, i. e.,

\frac{x_{1}-v}{\varepsilon}=\frac{x_{3}-v^{p}}{\varepsilon m_{p}(v)+h}.

(4.9)

This yields

h=\varepsilon\Big(\frac{x_{3}-v^{p}}{x_{1}-v}-m_{p}(v)\Big),

(4.10)

and

x_{3}=v^{p}+\Big(\frac{h}{\varepsilon}+m_{p}(v)\Big)(x_{1}-v)=(u-\varepsilon)^{p}+\Big(\frac{h}{\varepsilon}+m_{p}(u-\varepsilon)\Big)\Delta_{-}.

(4.11)

Since
v
depends only on
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
, we have

\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{3}}=\frac{\varepsilon}{x_{1}-v};\qquad\quad\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{2}}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\eqref{m-diff}}}\frac{h+\varepsilon m_{p}^{\prime}(v)(u-x_{1})}{x_{1}-v}\cdot\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}.

(4.12)

Differentiating the function
B
with respect to
x_{3}
, we obtain

\frac{\partial B}{\partial x_{3}}=\frac{\partial K_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}}{\partial h}\cdot(x_{1}-v)\cdot\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{3}}=\varepsilon\frac{\partial K_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}}{\partial h}.

(4.13)

The expression for the derivative of
B
with respect to
x_{2}
is more complicated:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial B}{\partial x_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(x_{1}-u)}\cdot\Big[rv^{r-1}+\frac{\partial K_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}}{\partial u}\cdot(x_{1}-v)-K_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}+\frac{\partial K_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}}{\partial h}\cdot\big(h+\varepsilon m_{p}^{\prime}(v)(u-x_{1})\big)\Big]

(4.14)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(x_{1}-u)}\cdot\Big[rv^{r-1}+\varepsilon\frac{\partial K_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}}{\partial u}-K_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}+h\frac{\partial K_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}}{\partial h}\Big]+\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{\partial K_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}}{\partial u}-\varepsilon m_{p}^{\prime}(v)\frac{\partial K_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}}{\partial h}\Big).

Since the derivative of the function
B
with respect to
x_{2}
has to be constant on the
extremal line with the fixed parameters
(u,h)
, we conclude that

rv^{r-1}+\varepsilon\frac{\partial K_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}}{\partial u}-K_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}+h\frac{\partial K_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}}{\partial h}=0

(4.15)

and

\frac{\partial B}{\partial x_{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{\partial K_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}}{\partial u}-\varepsilon m_{p}^{\prime}(v)\frac{\partial K_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}}{\partial h}\Big).

(4.16)

From the formula for the function
B
at the point
W_{+}(v)
, we get the boundary
value
K_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}(u,0)=m_{r}(v)
. The general solution of the differential equation (
4.15
) with this

K_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}(u,h)=e^{\frac{u}{\varepsilon}}\Psi_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}(e^{-\frac{u}{\varepsilon}}h)+m_{r}(v),

(4.17)

where
\Psi_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}
is an arbitrary sufficiently smooth function with
\Psi_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}(0)=0
. We remind the reader that in this subsection we set
v=u-\varepsilon
.

We find
\Psi_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}
from the boundary value at the point
H_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}(u)
. Recall that
H_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}(u)
coincides with
H(u,h)
for

h=\tfrac{1}{2}\big((u+\varepsilon)^{p}-(u-\varepsilon)^{p}\big)-\varepsilon m_{p}(u-\varepsilon).

Since we assume the segment
[U(u-\varepsilon),U(u+\varepsilon)]
to be an extremal line,
B

has to be linear on it, i. e.,
B=\tfrac{1}{2}\big((u-\varepsilon)^{r}+(u+\varepsilon)^{r}\big)
at the midpoint
of this segment. As a result, we come to the equation

B\big(H_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}(u)\big)=(u-\varepsilon)^{r}+K_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}\Big(u,\tfrac{1}{2}\big((u+\varepsilon)^{p}-(u-\varepsilon)^{p}\big)-\varepsilon m_{p}(v)\Big)\cdot\varepsilon=\tfrac{1}{2}\big((u+\varepsilon)^{r}+(u-\varepsilon)^{r}\big),

K_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}\Big(u,\tfrac{1}{2}\big((u+\varepsilon)^{p}-(u-\varepsilon)^{p}\big)-\varepsilon m_{p}(v)\Big)=\frac{(u+\varepsilon)^{r}-(u-\varepsilon)^{r}}{2\varepsilon},

(4.18)

or

\Psi_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}\Big(e^{-\frac{u}{\varepsilon}}\big[\tfrac{1}{2}\big((u+\varepsilon)^{p}-(u-\varepsilon)^{p}\big)-\varepsilon m_{p}(u-\varepsilon)\big]\Big)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}e^{-\frac{u}{\varepsilon}}\big[\tfrac{1}{2}\big((u+\varepsilon)^{r}-(u-\varepsilon)^{r}\big)-\varepsilon m_{r}(u-\varepsilon)\big].

(4.19)

Let us introduce a function

by the formula

w_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}(\xi;s,\varepsilon)=e^{-\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon}}\big[\tfrac{1}{2}\big((\xi+\varepsilon)^{s}-(\xi-\varepsilon)^{s}\big)-\varepsilon m_{s}(\xi-\varepsilon)\big],\qquad\xi\geqslant\varepsilon.

(4.20)

The symbols
s
and
\varepsilon
are considered here as fixed parameters, we will sometimes omit them when it does not lead to misunderstanding. Then we can rewrite
relation (
4.19
) in terms of
w_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}
:

\Psi_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}\big(w_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}(u;p,\varepsilon)\big)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}w_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}(u;r,\varepsilon\big).

In Lemma
4.1
below we will prove that the function

is monotone, and thereby,
the inverse function is correctly defined.

This representation suggests the following change of parametrization for the extremals of the fan
F_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}(u)
.
Till now, they were parametrized by
h
. Let us introduce a new parameter

:

(4.22)

i. e.,

When the variable

is running from

covers the domain of

, and we can write down the following expression for

:

\Psi_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}(\;\cdot\;)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}w_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}^{\phantom{1}}\big(w_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}^{-1}(\;\cdot\;;p,\varepsilon);r,\varepsilon\big).

(4.24)

Rewriting (
4.11
) in terms of

x_{3}=v^{p}+\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}e^{\frac{u}{\varepsilon}}w_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}(\xi;p,\varepsilon)+m_{p}(v)\Big)(x_{1}-v).

(4.25)

Taking into account (
4.21
), we rewrite (

) as follows:

B(x)=v^{r}+\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}e^{\frac{u}{\varepsilon}}w_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}(\xi;r,\varepsilon)+m_{r}(v)\Big)(x_{1}-v).

(4.26)

At the end of this subsection, we collect some formulas for the derivatives of the function

.
We will use them in various proofs. For brevity, we omit the argument

Recall that

Now, using relations (

), we rewrite formula (

) as follows

Differentiating (

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}B}{\partial x_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{2}}e^{-\frac{u}{\varepsilon}}(h+\varepsilon^{2}m_{p}^{\prime})\Psi_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{2}}\Big(e^{\frac{u}{\varepsilon}}\Psi_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}-(h+\varepsilon^{3}m_{p}^{\prime\prime})\Psi_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}^{\prime}+he^{-\frac{u}{\varepsilon}}(h+\varepsilon^{2}m_{p}^{\prime})\Psi_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}^{\prime\prime}+\varepsilon^{2}m_{r}^{\prime\prime}\Big)

Differentiating (

(4.31)

Collecting (

4.2

\Xi_{{}_{\mathrm{R}+}}

The construction of a Bellman candidate in

the fan

.

Since we consider right tangents, now we have

, and we choose the smaller root

4.3

(4.33)

(compare with (
4.4
)). The tangent lines forming the fan

have the common endpoint

,

. The second endpoint of the extremal passing through

will be

. Now

is determined by the third coordinates of this endpoint

(compare with (

)).
This second endpoint runs throw the segment
[W_{-}(v),H_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}(u)]
of the line

of the parabolic part of the boundary, where

(4.35)

or in coordinates

(4.36)

This means that in the fan
F_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}(u)
the variable

runs from

till

\varepsilon k_{p}(v)-\tfrac{1}{2}\big((u+\varepsilon)^{p}-(u-\varepsilon)^{p}\big)
for

and till
\varepsilon k_{p}(v)-2\varepsilon uv^{p-2}

We see that

is on the upper boundary of the domain

and on the lower boundary if

.

Since
B
has to be linear along the extremal
[U(v),H(u,h)]
, we write

B(x)=v^{r}+K_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}(u,h)(x_{1}-v),\qquad x\in[U(v),H(u,h)],

(4.37)

In the same way as we got (
4.9
), we obtain:

\frac{x_{1}-v}{-\varepsilon}=\frac{x_{3}-v^{p}}{-\varepsilon k_{p}(v)+h}.

(4.38)

This yields

h=\varepsilon\Big(k_{p}(v)-\frac{x_{3}-v^{p}}{x_{1}-v}\Big)

(4.39)

or

x_{3}=v^{p}-\Big(\frac{h}{\varepsilon}-k_{p}(v)\Big)(x_{1}-v)=(u+\varepsilon)^{p}+\Big(\frac{h}{\varepsilon}-k_{p}(u+\varepsilon)\Big)\Delta_{-}.

(4.40)

We collect the expressions for the derivatives of
u
and
h
:

\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{2}}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}}=\frac{1}{2(x_{1}-u)},\qquad\qquad\qquad\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{3}}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{3}}=0,

(4.41)

\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{2}}\;{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\eqref{m-diff}}}}\;\frac{h+\varepsilon(x_{1}-u)k_{p}^{\prime}(v)}{x_{1}-v}\cdot\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{2}},\qquad\frac{\partial h}{\partial x_{3}}=-\frac{\varepsilon}{x_{1}-v}.

(4.42)

Similarly to (
4.13
) and (
4.14
), we have

\frac{\partial B}{\partial x_{3}}=-\varepsilon\frac{\partial K_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}}{\partial h},

(4.43)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial B}{\partial x_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(x_{1}-u)}\cdot\Big[rv^{r-1}+\frac{\partial K_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}}{\partial u}\cdot(x_{1}-v)-K_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}+\frac{\partial K_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}}{\partial h}\cdot\big(h-\varepsilon k_{p}^{\prime}(v)(u-x_{1})\big)\Big]

(4.44)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(x_{1}-u)}\cdot\Big[rv^{r-1}-\varepsilon\frac{\partial K_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}}{\partial u}-K_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}+h\frac{\partial K_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}}{\partial h}\Big]+\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{\partial K_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}}{\partial u}+\varepsilon k_{p}^{\prime}(v)\frac{\partial K_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}}{\partial h}\Big).

)):

rv^{r-1}-\varepsilon\frac{\partial K_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}}{\partial u}-K_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}+h\frac{\partial K_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}}{\partial h}=0.

As before, we know the value

4.17

, we have

Taking into account (

As at the end of the preceding subsection, we collect here some formulas for the derivatives of the function

Special case:

We will prove that the Bellman function

), and (

)). This happens because all the chords of the form

Comments on multidimensional case

The proof of Theorem
1.7
follows the reasoning from
[
7
]
. Following the arguments of Lemma 5.2 there it is possible to prove the following assertion. If
G
is a non-negative locally concave function on
\Omega_{\varepsilon}^{3}
and
\phi\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\|\phi\|_{\scriptscriptstyle K}<\varepsilon
, then

G\Big(\phi_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t),\big(\phi^{2}\big)_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t),\big(|\phi|^{p}\big)_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t)\Big)\geqslant\big(G(\phi,\phi^{2},|\phi|^{p})\big)_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t),\qquad y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad t>0.

Fix a point
(y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
and use this inequality for the function
G=\boldsymbol{B}_{p,r;\varepsilon}^{+}
and for
\phi=\varphi-\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t)
. Then we obtain

\boldsymbol{B}_{p,r;\varepsilon}^{+}\Big(0,\big((\varphi-\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t))^{2}\big)_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t),\big(|\varphi-\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t)|^{p}\big)_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t)\Big)\geqslant\big(|\varphi-\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t)|^{r}\big)_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t).

(12.1)

Now, we use the definitions of the Bellman function
\boldsymbol{B}_{p,r;\varepsilon}^{+}
and the constant
C(p,r)
in the corresponding inequality. We estimate the left-hand side of (
12.1
):

\boldsymbol{B}_{p,r;\varepsilon}^{+}\Big(0,\big((\varphi-\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t))^{2}\big)_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t),\big(|\varphi-\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t)|^{p}\big)_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t)\Big)\leqslant C(p,r)^{r}\varepsilon^{r-p}\big(|\varphi-\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t)|^{p}\big)_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t).

Therefore, we obtain

\varepsilon^{r-p}C(p,r)^{r}\big(|\varphi-\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t)|^{p}\big)_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t)\geqslant\big(|\varphi-\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t)|^{r}\big)_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t).

Rewrite the last inequality in the form

\varepsilon^{r-p}C(p,r)^{r}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\varphi(\tilde{y})-\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t)|^{p}K_{t}(y-\tilde{y})d\tilde{y}\geqslant\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\varphi(\tilde{y})-\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t)|^{r}K_{t}(y-\tilde{y})d\tilde{y}.

(12.2)

At this moment we recall that
K_{t}
coincides either with
P
or
H
. For each of these kernels there exists the function

Q(t)=\begin{cases}\frac{\pi^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{d+1}{2})}t^{d},&K=P,\\
(4\pi t)^{\frac{d}{2}},&K=H,\end{cases}

such that
Q(t)K_{t}(y)\to 1
for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
when
t\to+\infty
. Moreover, this convergence is monotone and uniform on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We also note that since the function
\varphi
lies in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have
\varphi_{\scriptscriptstyle K}(y,t)\to 0
for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
when
t\to+\infty
. Multiplying both sides of (
12.2
) by
Q(t)
and passing
t\to+\infty
, we finally get

\varepsilon^{r-p}C(p,r)^{r}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\varphi(\tilde{y})|^{p}d\tilde{y}\geqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\varphi(\tilde{y})|^{r}d\tilde{y}.

(12.3)

Theorem
1.7
is proved.

\frac{w_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}(\xi;s,\varepsilon)+e^{-\frac{u}{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{3}m_{s}^{\prime\prime}(v)}{w_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}^{\prime}(\xi;s,\varepsilon)}=\frac{w_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}(\xi;s,\varepsilon)+e^{-\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{3}m_{s}^{\prime\prime}(\xi-\varepsilon)}{w_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}^{\prime}(\xi;s,\varepsilon)}+\frac{e^{-\frac{u}{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{3}m_{s}^{\prime\prime}(v)-e^{-\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{3}m_{s}^{\prime\prime}(\xi-\varepsilon)}{w_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}^{\prime}(\xi;s,\varepsilon)}

(B.11)

and prove that both of them are decreasing.

We begin with a simpler second function rewriting the expression in the numerator.
By (
2.13
) we have

e^{-\frac{u}{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{3}m_{s}^{\prime\prime}(v)-e^{-\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{3}m_{s}^{\prime\prime}(\xi-\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^{2}s(s-1)(s-2)\int\limits_{u}^{\xi}e^{-\frac{t}{\varepsilon}}(t-\varepsilon)^{s-3}dt\,,

and due to (
4.66
) and (
4.65
) the question is reduced to verification that the function

s\mapsto\frac{\int\limits_{u}^{\xi}e^{-\frac{t}{\varepsilon}}(t-\varepsilon)^{s-3}dt}{\int\limits_{-\varepsilon\ }^{\;\varepsilon}(\varepsilon^{2}-\lambda^{2})(\lambda+\xi)^{s-3}d\lambda}

(B.12)

is decreasing. Indeed, for
\varepsilon<t<\xi
and
\lambda>-\varepsilon
we have

\frac{\lambda+\xi}{t-\varepsilon}>1
, and therefore, the function

s\mapsto\Big(\frac{\lambda+\xi}{t-\varepsilon}\Big)^{s-3}

is increasing. After integration of this family of increasing functions with positive weight we get
the following increasing function:

s\mapsto\int\limits_{-\varepsilon\ }^{\;\varepsilon}(\varepsilon^{2}-\lambda^{2})\Big(\frac{\lambda+\xi}{t-\varepsilon}\Big)^{s-3}d\lambda\,,

or the following decreasing function:

s\mapsto\frac{(t-\varepsilon)^{s-3}}{\int\limits_{-\varepsilon\ }^{\;\varepsilon}(\varepsilon^{2}-\lambda^{2})(\lambda+\xi)^{s-3}d\lambda}\,.

(B.13)

We integrate once more family of functions (
B.13
) with the positive weight
e^{-\frac{t}{\varepsilon}}

and finally obtain the decreasing function (
B.12
).

Now, we consider the first summand in (
B.11
). Again, we start with simplifying the expression in
the numerator. Using twice (
2.15
), we get

\varepsilon^{2}m_{s}^{\prime\prime}(v)=\varepsilon\big(m_{s}(v)-sv^{s-1}\big)^{\prime}=m_{s}(v)-sv^{s-1}-\varepsilon s(s-1)v^{s-2}.

Then, we use the definition (
4.20
) of
w_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}
:

\displaystyle w_{{}_{\mathrm{L}}}(\xi;

\displaystyle s,\varepsilon)+e^{-\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{3}m_{s}^{\prime\prime}(\xi-\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon}}\Big(\frac{(\xi+\varepsilon)^{s}-(\xi-\varepsilon)^{s}}{2}-\varepsilon m_{s}(\xi-\varepsilon)\Big)+\varepsilon e^{-\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon}}\Big(m_{s}(\xi-\varepsilon)-s(\xi-\varepsilon)^{s-1}-\varepsilon s(s-1)(\xi-\varepsilon)^{s-2}\Big)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon}}\Big(\frac{(\xi+\varepsilon)^{s}-(\xi-\varepsilon)^{s}}{2}-\varepsilon s(\xi-\varepsilon)^{s-1}-\varepsilon^{2}s(s-1)(\xi-\varepsilon)^{s-2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}s(s-1)(s-2)e^{-\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon}}\int\limits_{-\varepsilon\ }^{\;\varepsilon}(\varepsilon-\mu)^{2}(\mu+\xi)^{s-3}d\mu\,.

Therefore, we need to check that the function

s\mapsto\frac{\int\limits_{-\varepsilon\ }^{\;\varepsilon}(\varepsilon-\mu)^{2}(\mu+\xi)^{s-3}d\mu}{\int\limits_{-\varepsilon\ }^{\;\varepsilon}(\varepsilon^{2}-\lambda^{2})(\lambda+\xi)^{s-3}d\lambda}

(B.14)

is decreasing. After differentiating this function, we get in numerator the following expression:

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\varepsilon\ }^{\;\varepsilon}(\varepsilon-\mu)^{2}

\displaystyle(\mu+\xi)^{s-3}\log(\mu+\xi)\,d\mu\int\limits_{-\varepsilon\ }^{\;\varepsilon}(\varepsilon^{2}-\lambda^{2})(\lambda+\xi)^{s-3}d\lambda

\displaystyle-\int\limits_{-\varepsilon\ }^{\;\varepsilon}(\varepsilon-\mu)^{2}(\mu+\xi)^{s-3}d\mu\int\limits_{-\varepsilon\ }^{\;\varepsilon}(\varepsilon^{2}-\lambda^{2})(\lambda+\xi)^{s-3}\log(\lambda+\xi)\,d\lambda

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\!-\varepsilon\ \ }^{\;\varepsilon}\!\!\!\int\limits_{\!-\varepsilon\ }^{\;\varepsilon}(\varepsilon-\mu)^{2}(\varepsilon^{2}-\lambda^{2})(\mu+\xi)^{s-3}(\lambda+\xi)^{s-3}\big(\log(\mu+\xi)-\log(\lambda+\xi)\big)\,d\mu\,d\lambda\,.

Now, we symmetrize this expression (interchanging
\mu
and
\lambda
):

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\!\!\int\limits_{\!-\varepsilon\ \ }^{\;\varepsilon}\!\!\!\int\limits_{\!-\varepsilon\ }^{\;\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\big[(\varepsilon-\mu)^{2}(\varepsilon^{2}-\lambda^{2})-(\varepsilon-\lambda)^{2}(\varepsilon^{2}-\mu^{2})\big](\mu+\xi)^{s-3}(\lambda+\xi)^{s-3}\big(\log(\mu+\xi)-\log(\lambda+\xi)\big)\,d\mu\,d\lambda

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\!\!\int\limits_{\!-\varepsilon\ \ }^{\;\varepsilon}\!\!\!\int\limits_{\!-\varepsilon\ }^{\;\varepsilon}(\lambda-\mu)(\varepsilon-\mu)(\varepsilon-\lambda)(\mu+\xi)^{s-3}(\lambda+\xi)^{s-3}\big(\log(\mu+\xi)-\log(\lambda+\xi)\big)\,d\mu\,d\lambda\,.

Since
\log
is an increasing function, we have

(\lambda-\mu)\big(\log(\mu+\xi)-\log(\lambda+\xi)\big)<0\,.

This means that the derivative we have calculated is negative and the function (
B.14
)
decreases. This completes the proof of the lemma.

\square

Lemma B.3
.

B_{x_{2}x_{2}}B_{x_{3}x_{3}}-B_{x_{2}x_{3}}^{2}\geqslant 0
on
\Xi_{{}_{\mathrm{R}+}}
.

Proof.

u<x_{1}<v=u+\varepsilon

\sign\Psi_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}^{\prime\prime}=-\sign(r-2)(r-p)
(see (

\sign(r-2)(r-p)\times\sign\Big(e^{-\frac{u}{\varepsilon}}\Psi_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}(e^{\frac{u}{\varepsilon}}h)-\big(h-\varepsilon^{3}k_{p}^{\prime\prime}(v)\big)\Psi_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}^{\prime}(e^{\frac{u}{\varepsilon}}h)+\varepsilon^{2}k_{r}^{\prime\prime}(v)\Big).

\sign\Big(e^{-\frac{u}{\varepsilon}}\Psi_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}(e^{\frac{u}{\varepsilon}}h)-\big(h-\varepsilon^{3}k_{p}^{\prime\prime}(v)\big)\Psi_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}^{\prime}(e^{\frac{u}{\varepsilon}}h)+\varepsilon^{2}k_{r}^{\prime\prime}(v)\Big)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{?}}\sign(r-p)(r-2)\,.

because

by Lemma
4.2
. In other words, we need to prove that
the function

decreases for all

.

We split this function into the sum of two

\frac{w_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\xi;s,\varepsilon)-e^{\frac{u}{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{3}k_{s}^{\prime\prime}(v)}{w_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}^{\prime}(\xi;s,\varepsilon)}=\frac{w_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}(\xi;s,\varepsilon)-e^{\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{3}k_{s}^{\prime\prime}(\xi+\varepsilon)}{w_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}^{\prime}(\xi;s,\varepsilon)}+\frac{e^{\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{3}k_{s}^{\prime\prime}(\xi+\varepsilon)-e^{\frac{u}{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon^{3}k_{s}^{\prime\prime}(v)}{w_{{}_{\mathrm{R}}}^{\prime}(\xi;s,\varepsilon)}

and prove that both of them are decreasing.

\xi\geqslant\varepsilon

), and (
4.65

to verification that the function

Lemma B.4
.

B_{x_{2}x_{2}}B_{x_{3}x_{3}}-B_{x_{2}x_{3}}^{2}\geqslant 0
on
\Xi_{{}_{\mathrm{ch}+}}
.

Proof.

Using (
A.9
), we take a half sum of expressions (
A.15
) and (
A.24
) to obtain:

B_{x_{2}}=D(r)-D(p)B_{x_{3}}\,,

(B.22)

where

D(s)=D(s;a,b)=\frac{s}{2}\cdot\frac{b^{s-1}-a^{s-1}}{b-a}\,.

Using this representation, we express the derivatives of
B
with respect to
x_{2}
via derivatives with
respect to
x_{3}
:

\displaystyle B_{x_{2}x_{3}}

\displaystyle=D_{x_{3}}(r)-D_{x_{3}}(p)B_{x_{3}}-D(p)B_{x_{3}x_{3}};

\displaystyle B_{x_{2}x_{2}}

\displaystyle=D_{x_{2}}(r)-D_{x_{2}}(p)B_{x_{3}}-D(p)B_{x_{2}x_{3}}

\displaystyle=\big[D_{x_{2}}(r)-D(p)D_{x_{3}}(r)\big]-\big[D_{x_{2}}(p)-D(p)D_{x_{3}}(p)\big]B_{x_{3}}+D(p)^{2}B_{x_{3}x_{3}}\,.

Using these formulas, after some simplifications, we rewrite the minor under consideration in the following form:

B_{x_{2}x_{2}}B_{x_{3}x_{3}}-B_{x_{2}x_{3}}^{2}=\big[E(r)-E(p)B_{x_{3}}\big]B_{x_{3}x_{3}}-\big[D_{x_{3}}(r)-D_{x_{3}}(p)B_{x_{3}}\big]^{2}\,,

(B.23)

where
E(s)=D_{x_{2}}(s)+D(p)D_{x_{3}}(s)
.

Now, we plan to find some integral representations for each factor in (
B.23
), from where it will be clear that
this expression is positive. We already have an integral representation for
B_{x_{3}x_{3}}
, this is
formula (
B.8
). Let us show that the factor
E(r)-E(p)B_{x_{3}}
has almost the same representation,
the difference consists in the factor in front of the integral.

We start with an integral representation for
D
:

D(s)=\frac{s(s-1)}{2(b-a)}\int\limits_{a}^{b}\lambda^{s-2}d\lambda\,.

(B.24)

Whence

\displaystyle D_{a}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{s(s-1)(s-2)}{2(b-a)^{2}}\int\limits_{a}^{b}(b-\lambda)\lambda^{s-3}d\lambda\,;

\displaystyle D_{b}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{s(s-1)(s-2)}{2(b-a)^{2}}\int\limits_{a}^{b}(\lambda-a)\lambda^{s-3}d\lambda\,,

and

D_{x_{i}}(s)=\frac{s(s-1)(s-2)}{2(b-a)^{2}}\int\limits_{a}^{b}\big[(b-\lambda)a_{x_{i}}+(\lambda-a)b_{x_{i}}\big]\lambda^{s-3}d\lambda\,.

(B.25)

We use this expression to get a representation for
E
:

\displaystyle E(s)

\displaystyle=D_{x_{2}}(s)+D(p)D_{x_{3}}(s)

(B.26)

\displaystyle=\frac{s(s-1)(s-2)}{2(b-a)^{2}}\int\limits_{a}^{b}\big[(b-\lambda)(a_{x_{2}}+D(p)a_{x_{3}})+(\lambda-a)(b_{x_{2}}+D(p)b_{x_{3}})\big]\lambda^{s-2}d\lambda\,.

We have formula (
B.6
) for
a_{x_{3}}
and
b_{x_{3}}
:

(b-x_{1})a_{x_{3}}=(x_{1}-a)b_{x_{3}}=\frac{(b-a)^{2}}{A(p)}\,,

(B.27)

and formulas (
A.23
) and (
A.14
) for
a_{x_{2}}
and
b_{x_{2}}
:

(b-x_{1})a_{x_{2}}=\frac{b^{p}-a^{p}-pb^{p-1}(b-a)}{A(p)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(x_{1}-a)b_{x_{2}}=-\frac{b^{p}-a^{p}-pa^{p-1}(b-a)}{A(p)}\,.

If we plug these expressions into the parts of (
B.26
), we get

\displaystyle a_{x_{2}}+D(p)a_{x_{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b-x_{1}}\Big[\frac{b^{p}-a^{p}-pb^{p-1}(b-a)}{A(p)}+\frac{p}{2}\cdot\frac{b^{p-1}-a^{p-1}}{b-a}\cdot\frac{(b-a)^{2}}{A(p)}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2A(p)(b-x_{1})}\big[2b^{p}-2a^{p}-p(b^{p-1}+a^{p-1})(b-a)\big]=-\frac{1}{2(b-x_{1})}\,,

and similarly

b_{x_{2}}+D(p)b_{x_{3}}=\frac{1}{2(x_{1}-a)}\,.

As a result, we can rewrite (
B.26
) as follows

\displaystyle E(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{s(s-1)(s-2)}{4(b-a)^{2}}\int\limits_{a}^{b}\Big[\frac{\lambda-a}{x_{1}-a}-\frac{b-\lambda}{b-x_{1}}\Big]\lambda^{s-3}d\lambda

(B.28)

\displaystyle=\frac{s(s-1)(s-2)}{4(b-a)(x_{1}-a)(b-x_{1})}\int\limits_{a}^{b}(\lambda-x_{1})\lambda^{s-3}d\lambda=\frac{A(p)}{4(b-a)^{4}}A_{x_{3}}(s)\,.

In the last equality we use representation (
B.7
).

Finally, we calculate the first factor in (
B.23
):

\displaystyle E(r)-E(p)B_{x_{3}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\eqref{230603}}}\frac{E(r)A(p)-E(p)A(r)}{A(p)}=\frac{1}{4(b-a)^{4}}\big(A_{x_{3}}(r)A(p)-A_{x_{3}}(p)A(r)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{A(p)^{2}}{4(b-a)^{4}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}\Big(\frac{A(r)}{A(p)}\Big)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\eqref{230603}}}\frac{A(p)^{2}}{4(b-a)^{4}}B_{x_{3}x_{3}}\,.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\big[E(r)

\displaystyle-E(p)B_{x_{3}}\big]B_{x_{3}x_{3}}=\Big(\frac{A(p)}{2(b-a)^{2}}B_{x_{3}x_{3}}\Big)^{2}

(B.29)

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\frac{r(r-1)(r-2)p(p-1)(p-2)(b-a)}{4(x_{1}-a)(b-x_{1})A(p)^{2}}\times

\displaystyle\times\int\limits_{a}^{b}\!\!\int\limits_{a}^{b}\big[\lambda\mu-ab+(a+b-\lambda-\mu)x_{1}\big](\lambda-\mu)(\lambda^{r-p}-\mu^{r-p})\lambda^{p-3}\mu^{p-3}d\lambda\,d\mu\Bigg)^{2}.

Now, we find an integral representation for another term in (
B.23
):

\displaystyle D_{x_{3}}

\displaystyle(r)-D_{x_{3}}(p)B_{x_{3}}=\frac{D_{x_{3}}(r)A(p)-D_{x_{3}}(p)A(r)}{A(p)}

(B.30)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\eqref{270801},\eqref{030902}}}\frac{r(r-1)(r-2)p(p-1)(p-2)(b-a)}{2A(p)(b-a)^{2}}\times

\displaystyle\times\int\limits_{a}^{b}\!\!\int\limits_{a}^{b}\big[(\lambda-a)(\mu-a)b_{x_{3}}-(b-\lambda)(b-\mu)a_{x_{3}}\big](\lambda-\mu)\lambda^{r-3}\mu^{p-3}d\lambda\,d\mu

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\eqref{030904}}}\frac{r(r-1)(r-2)p(p-1)(p-2)}{4(x_{1}-a)(b-x_{1})A(p)^{2}}\times

\displaystyle\times\int\limits_{a}^{b}\!\!\int\limits_{a}^{b}\big[(\lambda-a)(\mu-a)(b-x_{1})-(b-\lambda)(b-\mu)(x_{1}-a)\big](\lambda-\mu)(\lambda^{r-p}-\mu^{r-p})\lambda^{p-3}\mu^{p-3}d\lambda\,d\mu\,,\hskip-20.0pt

where we, as usual, used symmetrization to get the last formula in the chain.

It remains to compare the different parts of expression in (
B.29
) and (
B.30
). We see that

(b-a)\big[\lambda\mu-ab+(a+b-\lambda-\mu)x_{1}\big]\geqslant\big|(\lambda-a)(\mu-a)(b-x_{1})-(b-\lambda)(b-\mu)(x_{1}-a)\big|

for
\lambda,\mu,x_{1}\in[a,b]
.
Indeed, on the left-hand side we have a linear function in
x_{1}
and we have a convex piecewise linear function
on the right-hand side. The values of both functions coincide at the endpoints of the interval
a\leqslant x_{1}\leqslant b
.
Therefore, inside the interval the left function is strictly larger than the right one. Since the remaining
terms are the same and they do not change its sign inside the interval, we have the required inequalities for
the squares of integrals.

\square

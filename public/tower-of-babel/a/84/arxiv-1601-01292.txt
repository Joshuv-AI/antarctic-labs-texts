Relative Reproducing Kernels in Vector-Valued Hilbert and Banach Spaces
Ali Ebadian 1 , ∗ Saeed Hashemi Sababe 2

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to the study of vector valued reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces. We focus on reproducing kernels in vector-valued reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces. In particular we extend reproducing kernels to relative reproducing kernels and prove some theorems in this subject.

1.
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to establish the notion of vector-valued relative reproducing kernels in Hilbert and Banach spaces. In learning theory, reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHS) and reproducing kernel Banach spaces (RKBS) are important tools for designing learning algorithms. The basic object is a Hilbert or Banach space of functions
f
from a set
X
into a normed vector space
\mathcal{Y}
with the property that, for any
x\in X,|f(x)|\leq C_{x}|f|
for a positive constant
C_{x}
independent of

Given a set
X
and a normed vector space
\mathcal{Y}
, a map
K:X\times X\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{Y})
is called a
\mathcal{Y}
-reproducing kernel if

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\langle K(x_{i},x_{j})y_{j},y_{i}\rangle\geq 0

(1.1)

or any
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
in
X
,
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}
in
\mathcal{Y}
and
n\geq 1
. Given
x\in X
,
K_{x}:\mathcal{Y}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}(X,\mathcal{Y})
denotes the linear operator whose action on a vector
y\in\mathcal{Y}
is the function
K_{xy}\in\mathcal{F}(X,\mathcal{Y})
defined by

(K_{xy})(t)=K(t,x)y\qquad t\in X.

(1.2)

Given a
\mathcal{Y}
-reproducing kernel
K
, there is a unique Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{K}\subset\mathcal{F}(X,\mathcal{Y})
satisfying

\displaystyle K_{x}\in L(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{H}_{K})\qquad x\in X

(1.3)

\displaystyle f(x)=K_{x}^{∗}f\qquad x\in X,f\in\mathcal{H}_{K},

(1.4)

where
K_{x}^{*}:\mathcal{H}_{K}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
is the adjoint of
K_{x}
. The space
\mathcal{H}_{K}
is called the reproducing kernel Hilbert space associated with
K
, the corresponding scalar product and norm are denoted by
\langle.,.\rangle_{K}
and
\|.\|_{K}
,
respectively. As a consequence of
(\ref{3})
, we have that

\displaystyle K(x,t)=K_{x}^{∗}K_{t}\qquad x,t\in X

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{K}=\overline{span}\{K_{xy}|x\in X,y\in Y\}.

(1.6)

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space of functions defined on the set
X
. We say that it is a relative reproducing kernel Hilbert space if there exists a function
M_{x,y}
from
X\times X
into
\mathcal{H}
such that

F(x)-F(y)=\langle F,M_{x,y},\mathcal{H}\rangle\qquad\forall x,y\in X,\forall F\in\mathcal{H}.

(1.7)

A semi-inner product on a Banach space

V
is a function from
V\times V
to
\mathbb{C}
, denoted by
[.,.]_{V}
, such that for all
u,v,w\in V
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}

(1)

(linearity )
[\alpha f+\beta g,h]_{V}=\alpha[f,h]_{V}+\beta[g,h]_{V}
;

(2)

(positivity)
[f,f]_{V}>0
for
f\neq 0
;

(3)

(conjugate homogeneity)
[f,\alpha g]_{V}=\alpha[f,g]_{V}
;

(4)

(Cauchy-Schwartz inequality)
|[f,g]_{V}|\leq[f,f]_{V}^{1/2}[g,g]_{V}^{1/2}
.

A semi-inner product
[.,.]_{V}
on
V
is said to be compatible if

[f,f]_{V}^{1/2}=\|f\|_{V}\qquad\forall f\in V,

(1.8)

where
\|.\|_{V}
denotes the norm on
V
. Every Banach space has a compatible semi-inner product.
Let
[.,.]_{V}
be a compatible semi-inner product on
V
. Then one sees by the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality
that for each
f\in\mathcal{B}
, the linear functional
f^{*}
on
V
defined by

f^{*}(g):=[g,f]_{V},\qquad g\in V

(1.9)

is bounded on
V
. In other words,
f^{*}
lies in the dual space
\mathcal{B}^{∗}
of
\mathcal{B}
. Moreover, we have

\|f^{*}\|_{V^{*}}=\|f\|_{V}

(1.10)

Introduce the duality mapping
\mathcal{I}_{V}
from
V
to
V^{∗}
by setting

\mathcal{I}_{V}(f):=f^{∗},\qquad f\in V.

(1.11)

Let
\Lambda
be a Banach space. A space
\mathcal{B}
is called a Banach space of
\Lambda
-valued functions on
X
if it consists of certain functions from
X
to
\Lambda
and the norm on
\mathcal{B}
is compatible with point evaluations in the sense that

\|f\|_{B}=0\Longleftrightarrow f(x)=0\quad\forall x\in X.

(1.12)

We call
\mathcal{B}
a
\Lambda
-valued RKBS on
X
if both
\mathcal{B}
and
\Lambda
are uniform and
\mathcal{B}
is a Banach space of functions from
X
to
\Lambda
such that for every
x\in X
, the point evaluation
\delta_{x}:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\lambda
defined by

\delta_{x}(f):=f(x),\qquad f\in\mathcal{B},

(1.13)

is continuous from
\mathcal{B}
to
\Lambda
.

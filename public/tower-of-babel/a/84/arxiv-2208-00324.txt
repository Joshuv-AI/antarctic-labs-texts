A general family of Plotkin-optimal two-weight codes over ℤ 4 \mathbb{Z}_{4}
Hopein Christofen Tang and Djoko Suprijanto

Abstract
We obtained all possible parameters of Plotkin-optimal two-Lee weight projective codes over ℤ 4 , \mathbb{Z}_{4}, together with their weight distributions. We show the existence of codes with these parameters as well as their weight distributions by constructing an infinite family of two-weight codes. Previously known codes constructed by Shi et al. ( Des Codes Cryptogr. 88 (12): 2493-2505, 2020) can be derived as a special case of our results. We also prove that the Gray image of any Plotkin-optimal two-Lee weight projective codes over ℤ 4 \mathbb{Z}_{4} has the same parameters and weight distribution as some two-weight binary projective codes of type SU1 in the sense of Calderbank and Kantor ( Bull. Lond. Math. Soc. 18 :97-122, 1986).



Keywords:
Plotkin-optimal codes, projective codes, two-weight codes.

Mathematics Subject Classification:
94B05, 05E30.

1
Introduction

A code
C\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
over an alphabet
{\mathbb{F}}
is called a constant weight code (or one-weight code) if every nonzero codeword has the same weight. Well-known examples of such codes over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
are simplex codes, which are the duals of Hamming codes (see, e.g.,
[
6
]
). Constant weight codes over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
have been extensively studied (see
[
9
]
for a detailed account). One important result regarding constant weight linear codes over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
is that for every positive integer
k
, there exists a unique (up to equivalence) one-weight code of dimension
k
such that any two columns in its generator matrix are linearly independent. A similar phenomenon was proved by Carlet
[
3
]
for constant weight linear codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}.

A two-weight code
C\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
over an alphabet
{\mathbb{F}}
is a code with exactly two nonzero different weights. Two-weight codes over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
have been investigated since a long time because of their connection to other combinatorial objects such as strongly regular graphs, difference sets and also finite geometries
[
1
,
2
,
4
]
. Recently, there were also some progress in the study of two-weight codes over finite rings, such as
\mathbb{Z}_{4}

[
13
,
14
,
15
]
,
\mathbb{Z}_{2^{m}}

[
8
,
12
,
11
]
,
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}

[
11
]
, and
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}+u{\mathbb{F}}_{p}

[
10
]
, with
u^{2}=u
, where
p
is an odd prime. Interestingly, two-weight codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{m}}
for some prime
p
also have some connections to strongly regular graphs
[
12
]
and finite geometries
[
11
]
.

Constructing (optimal) codes is one of the main problems in coding theory. The construction of one-weight codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
has been provided in
[
13
]
, while several constructions of two-weight codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
have been found by several authors
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
, and recently by
[
15
]
. All two-weight codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
constructed in
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
are Plotkin-optimal and projective (see Section 2 for the definition). The purpose of this paper is to find a complete list of possible parameters and weight distributions of Plotkin-optimal two-weight projective codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Section 2, we provide some definitions related to linear codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}.
Several useful identities related to linear codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
are derived in Section 3. In Section 4, we study possible parameters and weight distributions of Plotkin-optimal two-weight projective codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
and their Gray images. In Section 5, we first consider a family of free two-weight codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
. After that, we use these free codes to construct an infinite family of two-weight codes. The paper is ended by concluding remarks.
We refer the readers to
[
6
,
9
]
for undefined terms in coding theory.

2
Preliminaries

A
code

C
of length
n
over the ring
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
is a nonempty subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n}.
If the code is also a submodule of
\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n},

then we say that the code is
linear.
The linear code is called
free
if it is a free submodule of
\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n}.

A matrix
G\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k\times n}
is called a
generator matrix
of a linear code
C
of length
n
over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
if the rows of
G
generate
C
and no proper subset of the rows of
G
generates
C
.

Two codes are said to be
equivalent
if one can be obtained from the other by permuting the coordinates and (if necessary) changing the signs of certain coordinates. Codes differing by only a permutation of coordinates are called
permutation-equivalent.
It is well-known (see
[
5
]
) that any linear code over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
is permutation-equivalent to the linear code
C
with generator matrix
G
of the form

G=\begin{pmatrix}I_{k_{1}}&A&B_{1}+2B_{2}\\
0&2I_{k_{2}}&2D\end{pmatrix},

(1)

where
A,B_{1},B_{2},
and
D
are
(0,1)
-matrices. Moreover, the code
C
is a free linear code if and only if
k_{2}=0.
The generator matrix of a linear code
C
over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
is called in a
standard form
if it has the form as given in the Equation (
1
).

The
Lee weight
of
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{4},
denoted by
w_{L}(x),
is defined by
w_{L}(0)=0,

w_{L}(1)=1,

w_{L}(2)=2,
and
w_{L}(3)=1.
The Lee weight of a vector
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n}
is defined as

w_{L}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{L}(x_{i})
. For a given positive integer
\beta,
a linear code
C
over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
is called a
\beta
-weight code
if

|\{w_{L}(\mathbf{c})\;\colon\;\mathbf{0}\neq\mathbf{c}\in C\}|=\beta,

where
\mathbf{0}
denotes the all-zero vector.

For
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n},
the
Lee distance
between
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y},
denoted by
d_{L}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}),
is defined by

d_{L}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=w_{L}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}).
The
minimum Lee distance
of a linear code
C\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n}
is defined by

d_{L}=d_{L}(C):=\mathrm{min}\{d_{L}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}):~\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in C,\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}\}.

Throughout the paper, what we mean by weight and distance are Lee weight and Lee distance, respectively. It is clear that the minimum Lee distance of a linear code
C
is exactly the same as the minimum Lee weight, namely

d_{L}(C)=\mathrm{min}\{w_{L}(\mathbf{x}):~\mathbf{0}\neq\mathbf{x}\in C\}.

We write the parameters of a linear code
C

over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
as
[n,4^{k_{1}}2^{k_{2}},d_{L}],
where
n
is a length of
C,

|C|=4^{k_{1}}2^{k_{2}},
and
d_{L}=d_{L}(C).
Following
Hammons et al.
[
5
]
(cf.
[
17
]
), we say that
C
is of type
4^{k_{1}}2^{k_{2}}.
In
[
5
]
, the
Gray map

\phi:\mathbb{Z}_{4}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{2}
is defined as
\phi(0)=(0,0),\phi(1)=(0,1),\phi(2)=(1,1)
, and
\phi(3)=(1,0)
. The Gray map
\phi
can be extended naturally to
\Phi:\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{2n}
.
\Phi
is a distance-preserving map (isometry) from
(\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n},d_{L})
to
({\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{2n},d_{H})
, where
d_{H}
denotes the Hamming distance.

In 1968, Wyner and Graham
[
18
]
proved an upper bound for the minimum Lee distance of codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
, as follows (see also
[
16
, Theorem 3.4]
for a simpler proof).

Theorem 2.1

(Plotkin-type Lee distance bound)
.

If
C
is a linear code of length
n
over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
with parameters
[n,4^{k_{1}}2^{k_{2}},d_{L}]
, then

d_{L}\leq\frac{|C|}{|C|-1}n.

(2)

A linear code
C
whose minimum Lee distance
d_{L}(C)
is an integer nearest to the upper bound of the Plotkin-type Lee distance bound (
2
) as given in Theorem
2.1
is called
Plotkin-optimal.
In other words, a linear code
C
is Plotkin-optimal if
\displaystyle d_{L}(C)=\left\lfloor\frac{|C|}{|C|-1}n\right\rfloor.

For
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}),~\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n},
we define the
Euclidean inner product
of
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
as

\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}.
The
Dual
of
C,
denoted by
C^{\perp},
is defined as

C^{\perp}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n}:~\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}=0,\text{ for all }\mathbf{y}\in C\}.

A
projective code

C
of length
n
over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
is a linear code such that the minimum Lee distance of its dual,
d_{L}(C^{\perp}),
is at least three.

3
Some useful identities

In this section, we derive several identities related to linear codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}.
We will use these identities to obtain parameters and weight distributions of Plotkin-optimal two-weight projective codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
.

Let
G\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{(k_{1}+k_{2})\times n}
be a generator matrix of a linear code
C
over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}.
Following Kløve
[
7
]
, for any
\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k_{1}+k_{2}},
we define the
multiplicity
of
\mathbf{c},
denoted by
\mu(\mathbf{c}),
as the number of occurrences of
\mathbf{c}
as a column vector in
G.
Observe that for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}
with
k:=k_{1}+k_{2}
, we have

w_{L}\left(\mathbf{x}G\right)=\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}}\mu(\mathbf{c})w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c}).

It is also clear that

\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}}\mu(\mathbf{c})=n.

It is known that for any nonzero codeword
\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}
, the sum
\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}}w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c})
is constant, and depends only on
k.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
16
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

Let
k
be a positive integer. If
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{k})\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}
is a nonzero vector, then

\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}}w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c})=4^{k}.

Moreover, we have a constant sum of Lee weights of codewords in the linear code as follows.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
16
, Lemma 3.2]
,
[
16
, Lemma 3.3]
)
.

Let
C
be a linear code over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
with parameters
[n,4^{k_{1}}2^{k_{2}},d_{L}]
. If
G
is a generator matrix of
C
and
\mu(\mathbf{0})=0
, then

\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k_{1}+k_{2}}}w_{L}(\mathbf{x}G)=4^{k_{1}+k_{2}}n\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in C}w_{L}(\mathbf{c})=|C|n.

Now, consider the quotient group
\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}/(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}
for a positive integer
k.
For any
\mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}/(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k},
it is clear that
|\mathbf{S}|=|(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}|=2^{k}
. Moreover, for any
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{S}
, we have
2\mathbf{x}=2\mathbf{y}.
We can prove the identity stronger than the one in Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
by proving that
\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{S}}w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c})
and
\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{S}}w_{L}(\mathbf{x}G)
are constant for any
\mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}/(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}
in certain conditions. We first consider the case when
\mathbf{S}=(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}.
For this purpose, we recall the property of one-weight codes below.

Theorem 3.3

(
[
13
, Theorem 4.5]
,
[
16
, Theorem 4.2]
)
.

For any nonnegative integer
k_{1},k_{2}
with
2k_{1}+k_{2}\geq 1,
let
G^{(k_{1},k_{2})}
denote the generator matrix of a linear code
C^{(k_{1},k_{2})}
whose columns consist of all possible nonzero vectors in
\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k_{1}}\times(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k_{2}}.
Then
C^{(k_{1},k_{2})}
is a one-weight linear code over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
with parameters
[n,4^{k_{1}}2^{k_{2}},d_{L}],
where
n=4^{k_{1}}2^{k_{2}}-1,
and
d_{L}=4^{k_{1}}2^{k_{2}}.

By using the above theorem, we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
k
be a positive integer. If
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{k})\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}
, then

\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}}w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }\mathbf{c}\in(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k},\\
2^{k}&\text{if }\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}-(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}.\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

Since it is clear for
\mathbf{c}\in(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k},
we prove the case
\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}-(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}.
By Theorem
3.3
, we know that the code
C^{(0,k)}
has parameters
[2^{k}-1,4^{0}2^{k},2^{k}]
and also a one-weight code having weight
2^{k}.
Because
\mathbf{c}G^{(0,k)}
is a nonzero codeword in
C^{(0,k)},
we have

2^{k}=w_{L}(\mathbf{c}G^{(0,k)})=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}}\mu(\mathbf{x})w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c})=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}}w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c}).\qed

A constant summation of the Lee weight along any nonzero element in
\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}/(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}
is given in the following. Throughout the rest of this paper, the notation
[a,b]_{\mathbb{Z}}
denotes the set
\{x\in\mathbb{Z}\;\colon\;x\geq a\text{ and }x\leq b\}
, where
a,b
are integers.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
k
be a positive integer and let
\mathbf{S}
be a nonzero element of
\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}/(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}.
If
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{k})\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}-(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}
, then

\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{S}}w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c})=2^{k}.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{S}.
We consider two cases.

Case 1
: If
w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c})=1,
then for any
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{S}
we have
2\mathbf{y}\cdot\mathbf{c}=2\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c}=2
, which implies
w_{L}(\mathbf{y}\cdot\mathbf{c})=1.
Hence,
\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{S}}w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c})=|\mathbf{S}|=2^{k}.

Case 2
: If
w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c})\in\{0,2\},
take
j\in[1,k]_{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
c_{j}\in\{1,3\}.
Without loss of generality, assume
w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c})=0.
For
i\in[1,k]_{\mathbb{Z}}
, define a vector
\mathbf{e}_{i}=(e_{i1},e_{i2},\dots e_{ik})\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}
as

e_{ij}:=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }j=i,\\
0,&\text{if }j\neq i.\end{cases}

Now, define a map
f:\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}
by
f(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{x}+2\mathbf{e}_{i}.
It is easy to check that
f
is a bijection from
A:=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{S}\,\colon\,w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c})=0\}
to
B:=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{S}\,\colon\,w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c})=2\}.
For any
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{S}
, we have
2\mathbf{y}\cdot\mathbf{c}=2\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c}=0,
so
w_{L}(\mathbf{y}\cdot\mathbf{c})\in\{0,2\}
. Hence,
A\cup B=\textbf{S}
and

|A|=|B|=\frac{\mathbf{|S|}}{2}=2^{k-1}.

We conclude that

\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{S}}w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c})=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in A}w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c})+\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in B}w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c})=2^{k}.\qed

From the constant summation properties as given in Lemma
3.4
and Lemma
3.5
, we obtain two new identities similar to Lemma
3.2
.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
C
be a linear code over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
with parameters
[n,4^{k_{1}}2^{k_{2}},d_{L}]
and a generator matrix
G.
If
\mu(\mathbf{c})=0,
for all
\mathbf{c}\in(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k_{1}+k_{2}},
then

\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k_{1}+k_{2}}-\{\mathbf{0}\}}w_{L}(\mathbf{x}G)=2^{k_{1}+k_{2}}n.

(3)

Proof.

Let
k:=k_{1}+k_{2}
. By Lemma
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}}w_{L}(\mathbf{x}G)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}}\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}}\mu(\mathbf{c})w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c})=\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}}\mu(\mathbf{c})\left(\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}}w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c})\right)=2^{k}n.

The result follows as
w_{L}(\mathbf{0}G)=0.

∎

Similarly, we can prove the following using Lemma
3.5
.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
C
be a linear code over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
with parameters
[n,4^{k_{1}}2^{k_{2}},d_{L}]
and a generator matrix
G
. If
\mu(\mathbf{c})=0,
for all
\mathbf{c}\in(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k_{1}+k_{2}},
and
\mathbf{S}
is a nonzero element in
\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k_{1}+k_{2}}/(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k_{1}+k_{2}}
, then

\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{S}}w_{L}(\mathbf{x}G)=2^{k_{1}+k_{2}}n.

(4)

Equations (
3
) and (
4
) will be very useful to prove several results in next section.

4
Two-weight projective codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}

In
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
, Shi and his coauthors considered two-weight projective codes. In this section, we provide all possible parameters and weight distributions of Plotkin-optimal two-weight projective codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}.
As consequences, we show that the results obtained before in
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
are just special cases of our results.

First, let us recall the necessary and sufficient existence conditions for projective codes.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
15
, Lemma 4.8]
)
.

Let
C
be a linear code over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
with type
4^{k_{1}}2^{k_{2}}
of length
n
and a generator matrix
G
. Then
C
is projective if and only if the following two conditions hold:

(1)

every column of
G
contains 1 or 3 (i.e.
\mu(\mathbf{c})=0,
for all
\mathbf{c}\in(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k_{1}+k_{2}}
);

(2)

any two columns of G are not multiple of each other by
\pm 1
(i.e.
\mu(\mathbf{c})+\mu(3\mathbf{c})\leq 1,
for all
\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k_{1}}\times(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k_{2}},
given
G
in a standard form).

By using Lemma
4.1
, we can easily obtain an upper bound for the length of projective codes.

Proposition 4.2
.

If
C
is a projective code of length
n
over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
with type
4^{k_{1}}2^{k_{2}}
, then
n\leq 2^{2k_{1}+k_{2}-1}-2^{k_{1}+k_{2}-1}
.

Proof.

Let
k:=k_{1}+k_{2}
. From Lemma
4.1
, it is clear that

\displaystyle 2n=2\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}}\mu(\mathbf{c})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}}\mu(\mathbf{c})+\mu(3\mathbf{c})\leq\left|\left(\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k_{1}}\times(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k_{2}}\right)-(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}\right|=2^{2k_{1}+k_{2}}-2^{k_{1}+k_{2}}.

Here, we consider the multiplicity using the generator matrix in a standard form.
∎

We knew that Plotkin-optimal projective codes always satisfy
d_{L}=n.

From the above proposition, it is clear that
n<|C|-1
. Thus, a projective code
C
of length
n
is Plotkin-optimal if and only if

d_{L}(C)=\left\lfloor\frac{|C|}{|C|-1}n\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor n+\frac{n}{|C|-1}\right\rfloor=n.

As a consequence, Plotkin-optimal projective codes always contain a codeword of Lee weight
n.
Hence, for two-weight codes, we can obtain the other weight by using the result in
[
13
]
below.

Proposition 4.3

(
[
13
, Proposition 5.1 (12)]
)
.

Let
C
be any linear code of length
n
over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
. If
C
is a two-Lee weight projective code with nonzero weights
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
, then

n(2n+1)-(w_{1}+w_{2})2n+2w_{1}w_{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{|C|}\right)=0.

(5)

From Proposition
4.3
, we conclude that the other weight of Plotkin-optimal projective codes must be
|C|/2.
The relation between two-weight projective codes and Plotkin-optimal codes is summarized in the following corollary.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
C
be any linear code of length
n
over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
. If
C
is a two-Lee weight projective code with nonzero weights
w_{1}<w_{2}
, then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

C
is Plotkin-optimal;

(2)

w_{1}=n
;

(3)

\displaystyle w_{2}=\frac{|C|}{2}
.

Notice that
w_{1}=n
and
w_{2}=\frac{|C|}{2}
satisfy Equation (
5
) for all positive integers
n<\frac{|C|}{2}.
In this case, we could not determine the nonexistence of Plotkin-optimal two-weight projective codes of certain length from that equation. Fortunately, by using additional equations, namely Equations (
3
) and (
4
), we obtain the nonexistence results for many values of
n
as follows (note that Corollary
3.6
and
3.7
hold for any projective codes by Lemma
4.1
).

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
C
be a two-Lee weight projective code of length
n
over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
with a generator matrix
G.
If
C
is Plotkin-optimal, then
w_{L}(\mathbf{x}G)=n,
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k_{1}+k_{2}}-(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k_{1}+k_{2}}.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{S}
be any nonzero element of

. From Lemma

2^{k_{1}+k_{2}}n=\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathbf{S}}w_{L}(\mathbf{c}G)\geq|\mathbf{S}|d_{L}=2^{k_{1}+k_{2}}n.

The equality occurs when
w_{L}(\mathbf{c}G)=n,
for all
\mathbf{c}\in\mathbf{S}.
Since
\mathbf{S}
is arbitrary,
w_{L}(\mathbf{x}G)=n,
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k_{1}+k_{2}}-(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k_{1}+k_{2}}.

∎

Let

denote the number of codewords in

having the Lee weight equal to

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
C
be a Plotkin-optimal two-weight projective code with type
4^{k_{1}}2^{k_{2}}
of length
n
over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
. Then
n=d_{L}=2^{2k_{1}+k_{2}-1}-2^{2k_{1}+k_{2}-t-1},
for some integer
1\leq t\leq k_{1}
. Moreover, the nonzero weights of
C
are
w_{1}=2^{2k_{1}+k_{2}-1}-2^{2k_{1}+k_{2}-t-1}
and
w_{2}=2^{2k_{1}+k_{2}-1}
, with
A_{w_{1}}=2^{2k_{1}+k_{2}}-2^{t}
and
A_{w_{2}}=2^{t}-1
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a generator matrix of
C.
Let
w_{1}<w_{2}
be the two nonzero weights of
C.
By Corollary
4.4
, we have
w_{1}=n
and
w_{2}=\frac{|C|}{2}.

Let
s
be the number of vectors
\mathbf{x}\in(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}
such that
w_{L}(\mathbf{x}G)=\frac{|C|}{2}.
Since
C
is projective,
C
satisfies Corollary
3.6
:

\displaystyle 2^{k_{1}+k_{2}}n

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k_{1}+k_{2}}-\{\mathbf{0}\}}w_{L}(\mathbf{c}G)=s\frac{|C|}{2}+(2^{k_{1}+k_{2}}-1-s)n.

From the above equation, we have
n=\frac{s}{2(s+1)}|C|.
It implies that
s|C|
is divisible by
s+1,
and hence
s+1
divides
|C|=2^{2k_{1}+k_{2}}.
It forces
s+1=2^{t}
for some

By Proposition
4.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle 2^{2k_{1}+k_{2}-1}-2^{k_{1}+k_{2}-1}\geq n

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{t}-1}{2^{t+1}}2^{2k_{1}+k_{2}}=2^{2k_{1}+k_{2}-1}-2^{2k_{1}+k_{2}-t-1},

which implies
1\leq t\leq k_{1}.
We conclude that

n=2^{2k_{1}+k_{2}-1}-2^{2k_{1}+k_{2}-t-1}=w_{1}\text{ and }w_{2}=\frac{|C|}{2}=2^{2k_{1}+k_{2}-1}

for some integer
t
with
1\leq t\leq k_{1}.
Now, to find the values of

and

we know from Corollary

, that
w_{L}(\mathbf{x}G)=n,
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k_{1}+k_{2}}-(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k_{1}+k_{2}}.
Therefore,
A_{w_{1}}=|C|-1-s=2^{2k_{1}+k_{2}}-2^{t}
and
A_{w_{2}}=s=2^{t}-1.

∎

5.1
A family of two weight codes with
k_{2}=0

Let
k
be a positive integer. Following Shi et al.
[
15
]
, we define a matrix
Y_{k}
as follows: for

, we define
Y_{k}:=\begin{bmatrix}1\end{bmatrix};
and for

we define

Y_{k}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c|c|c| c@{}}Y_{k-1}&Y_{k-1}&Y_{k-1}&Y_{k-1}&B_{k-1}\\
\hline\cr 0\;0\dots 0&1\;1\dots 1&2\;2\dots 2&3\;3\dots 3&1\;1\dots 1\end{array}\right],

where
B_{k-1}
is a
(k-1)\times 2^{k-1}
matrix whose columns are all possible vectors in
(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k-1}
. From the definition, it is easy to check that the linear code generated by

is projective for any positive integer
k
(Lemma
4.1
). In other words,
\mu(\mathbf{c})=0,
for all
\mathbf{c}\in(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}
and
\mu(\mathbf{c})+\mu(3\mathbf{c})\leq 1,
for all
\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}.
Now, we reprove
[
15
, Proposition 3.1]
and
[
15
, Corollary 3.2]
with a slightly different (and shorter) proof.

Proposition 5.1

(
[
15
, Proposition 3.1]
,
[
15
, Corollary 3.2]
)
.

If
C
is the code generated by
Y_{k}
, then
C
is a free two-weight code of length

n=2^{k-1}(2^{k}-1)
, with nonzero Lee weights
w_{1}=2^{k-1}(2^{k}-1)
and
w_{2}=2^{2k-1},
also
A_{w_{1}}=4^{k}-2^{k}
and
A_{w_{2}}=2^{k}-1.
Moreover,

w_{L}(\mathbf{x}Y_{k})=\begin{cases}2^{2k-1},&\text{if }\mathbf{x}\in(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}-\{\mathbf{0}\},\\
2^{k-1}(2^{k}-1),&\text{if }\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}-(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}.\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

It is easy to check that
n=2^{k-1}(2^{k}-1),k_{1}=k
, and
k_{2}=0
. From the proof of Proposition
4.2
, we conclude that
\mu(\mathbf{c})+\mu(3\mathbf{c})=1
for all
\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}
. Now, for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}-\{\mathbf{0}\}
we have

\displaystyle w_{L}(\mathbf{x}Y_{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(2\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}}\mu(\mathbf{c})w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}}\mu(\mathbf{c})w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c})+\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}}\mu(3\mathbf{c})w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot 3\mathbf{c})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}}(\mu(\mathbf{c})+\mu(3\mathbf{c}))w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}}w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c})-\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}}w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c})\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2^{2k-1},&\text{if }\mathbf{x}\in(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}-\{\mathbf{0}\},\\
2^{k-1}(2^{k}-1),&\text{if }\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}-(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}.\\
\end{cases}

The last equality is obtained from Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.4
. It is clear that
A_{w_{1}}=|\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}-(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}|=4^{k}-2^{k}
and
A_{w_{2}}=|(2\mathbb{Z}_{4})^{k}-\{\mathbf{0}\}|=2^{k}-1
.
∎

Remark 2

n

5.2
An infinite family of two-weight codes

We begin with the following construction.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
C
be a two-weight linear code of parameters
[n,4^{k_{1}}2^{k_{2}},d_{L}]
with a generator matrix
G
and nonzero weights
w_{1}<w_{2}
. Let
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
be two linear codes generated by the matrices

G_{1}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c|c|c@{}}G&G&G&G\\
\hline\cr 0\;0\dots 0&1\;1\dots 1&2\;2\dots 2&3\;3\dots 3\end{array}\;\right]

(6)

and

G_{2}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c@{}}G&G\\
\hline\cr 0\;0\dots 0&2\;2\dots 2\end{array}\;\right],

(7)

respectively. Then the following five statements hold:

(1)

C
is projective if and only if
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are projective;

(2)

A_{4n}(C_{1})=3|C|+\delta_{n,w_{1}}A_{n}(C);\text{ and }A_{4w}(C_{1})=A_{w}(C),
for any integer
w\in\{w_{1},w_{2}\}-\{n\};

(3)

A_{2n}(C_{2})=|C|+\delta_{n,w_{1}}A_{n}(C);\text{ and }A_{2w}(C_{2})=A_{w}(C),
for any integer
w\in\{w_{1},w_{2}\}-\{n\};

(4)

C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are two-weight projective codes if and only if
C
is Plotkin-optimal and projective;

(5)

If
C
is Plotkin-optimal, then
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
is also Plotkin-optimal.

Proof.

(1)

Straightforward from Lemma
4.1
.

(2)

For any
\mathbf{c}^{\ast}\in C_{1}
, there exists
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k+1})\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k+1}(k:=k_{1}+k_{2})
such that
\mathbf{c}^{\ast}=\mathbf{x}G_{1}
. For any
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k+1})\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k+1}
, let
\mathbf{y}^{\prime}
denote the vector
(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k})\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}
. Notice that

w_{L}(\mathbf{c}^{\ast})=w_{L}\left(\mathbf{x}G_{1}\right)=\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k+1}}\mu(\mathbf{c})w_{L}(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{c})=\sum_{\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}}\mu(\mathbf{c}^{\prime})\sum_{c_{k+1}=0}^{3}w_{L}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\cdot\mathbf{c}^{\prime}+x_{k+1}c_{k+1}).

Note that
\mu(\mathbf{c})
denotes the multiplicity of
\mathbf{c}
in
G_{1}
for
\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k+1}
and
\mu(\mathbf{c}^{\prime})
denotes the multiplicity of
\mathbf{c}^{\prime}
in
G
for
\mathbf{c}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{k}
. We obtain the following:

•

If
x_{k+1}=0
, then
w_{L}(\mathbf{c}^{\ast})=4w_{L}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}G)
where
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}G\in C
.

•

If
x_{k+1}\in\{1,2,3\}
, then

w_{L}(\mathbf{c}^{\ast})=4n
.

In this case,
A_{4n}(C_{1})=3|C|+\delta_{n,w_{1}}A_{n}(C)\text{ and }A_{4w}(C_{1})=A_{w}(C)
for any integer
w\in\{w_{1},w_{2}\}-\{n\}.

(3)

Similar to (2).

(4)

If
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are two-weight projective codes, then
C
is projective from (1). Suppose that
C
is not Plotkin-optimal. Then
d_{L}(C)=w_{1}<n
. If
w_{2}\leq n
, then from Lemma
3.2
we have

|C|n=\sum_{\mathbf{c}\in C}w_{L}(\mathbf{c})<(|C|-1)n,

which is impossible. Thus,
w_{2}>n
. From (2) and (3), we have
A_{4w}>0
and
A_{2w}>0
for
w\in\{w_{1},n,w_{2}\}
. Hence,
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are not two-weight codes, a contradiction. Therefore,
C
must be Plotkin-optimal.

If
C
is Plotkin-optimal and projective, then from (1),
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are projective. Moreover, by Corollary
4.4
, we have
w_{1}=n
and
w_{2}=\frac{|C|}{2}
. From (2) and (3), we conclude that the nonzero weights of
C_{1}
must be
4n
,
2|C|
and the nonzero weights of
C_{2}
must be
2n
,
|C|
. Therefore,
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are two-weight codes.

(5)

Straightforward from (2), (3), (4), and Corollary
4.4
. ∎

2k_{1}+k_{2}\geq 2
. Then for any integer
t
with
1\leq t\leq k_{1},
there exists a two-weight projective code with parameters
[n,4^{k_{1}}2^{k_{2}},d_{L}]
where
n=d_{L}=2^{2k_{1}+k_{2}-1}-2^{2k_{1}+k_{2}-t-1}
. Moreover, the nonzero weights of the code are
w_{1}=2^{2k_{1}+k_{2}-1}-2^{2k_{1}+k_{2}-t-1}
and
w_{2}=2^{2k_{1}+k_{2}-1}
, with
A_{w_{1}}=2^{2k_{1}+k_{2}}-2^{t}
and
A_{w_{2}}=2^{t}-1
.

Proof.

By applying Theorem
5.3
with
a=k_{1}-t
,
b=k_{2}
on the linear code
C
generated by
Y_{t}
(the parameters of this code are given in Proposition
5.1
), we get the desired result.
∎

The Theorem
5.5
above proves the existence of Plotkin-optimal two-weight projective codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
with the same parameters and weight distribution as the codes in Theorem
4.6
for any
1\leq t\leq k_{1}
.

Remark 4
.

If
C
is a linear code over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
with a generator matrix
G
whose columns consist of all distinct nonzero vectors

(c_{1},\dots,c_{t},c_{t+1},\dots,c_{k_{1}},c_{k_{1}+1},\dots,c_{k_{1}+k_{2}})^{T},

where
(c_{1},\dots,c_{t})^{T}
is a column of
Y_{t}
,
c_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4},
for
i\in[t+1,k_{1}]_{\mathbb{Z}}
, and
c_{i}\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{4},
for
i\in[k_{1}+1,k_{1}+k_{2}]_{\mathbb{Z}}
, then we can completely determine the linearity of
\Phi(C)
. It is easy to check that
\Phi(C)
is not linear for
1\leq t<k_{1}-1
from
[
5
, Theorem 5]
. The linearity of
\Phi(C)
for
t=k_{1}-1
and
t=k_{1}
have been completely determined in
[
15
, Theorem 5.8]
and
[
15
, Theorem 5.6]
, respectively.

6
Concluding remarks

We constructed a general family of two-weight projective codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}.
Our result generalized the previous results, mainly from
[
15
]
. We also obtained all possible parameters and weight distributions of Plotkin-optimal two-Lee weight projective codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
. However, the number of non-equivalent codes for some fixed parameters and weight distribution is still unknown, which is interesting to investigate further.

Although the two-weight projective codes obtained here are all Plotkin-optimal, it is not the case that all two-weight projective codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
are Plotkin-optimal. As an example, consider the linear code
C
with generator matrix
G
as follows:

G=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0&3&3&3&2&0&0&3&1&2&3&3\\
0&1&0&2&1&3&0&1&3&2&0&1&1&1\\
0&0&1&2&2&2&3&1&1&1&1&2&1&3\end{bmatrix}.

C
is a linear code with parameters
[14,4^{3}2^{0},12].
The code
C
is a two-weight code with nonzero weights
w_{1}=12,~w_{2}=16
and weight distribution
A_{w_{1}}=28,~A_{w_{2}}=35.
It can be checked that
d_{L}(C^{\perp})=3,
and hence
C
is a projective code. However,
d_{L}(C)=12<\left\lfloor\frac{4^{3}}{4^{3}-1}14\right\rfloor=14,
and hence
C
is not Plotkin-optimal. Moreover, the Gray image of this code has the same parameters and distribution as some two-weight binary linear codes of type SU2 (see Figure 1a and Figure 2a in
[
2
]
). So far, we have found a family of non-Plotkin-optimal two-weight projective codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
of this type. It is interesting to find all possible parameters and weight distributions of two-weight linear projective codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}
of this type and we are now working on that direction.

Adiabatic elimination for composite open quantum systems: reduced model formulation and numerical simulations
Francois-Marie Le Régent, Pierre Rouchon

Abstract
A numerical method is proposed for simulation of composite open quantum systems. It is based on Lindblad master equations and adiabatic elimination. Each subsystem is assumed to converge exponentially towards a stationary subspace, slightly impacted by some decoherence channels and weakly coupled to the other subsystems. This numerical method is based on a perturbation analysis with an asymptotic expansion. It exploits the formulation of the slow dynamics with reduced dimension. It relies on the invariant operators of the local and nominal dissipative dynamics attached to each subsystem. Second-order expansion can be computed only with local numerical calculations. It avoids computations on the tensor-product Hilbert space attached to the full system. This numerical method is particularly well suited for autonomous quantum error correction schemes. Simulations of such reduced models agree with complete full model simulations for typical gates acting on one and two cat-qubits (Z, ZZ and CNOT) when the mean photon number of each cat-qubit is less than 8. For larger mean photon numbers and gates with three cat-qubits (ZZZ and CCNOT), full model simulations are almost impossible whereas reduced model simulations remain accessible. In particular, they capture both the dominant phase-flip error-rate and the very small bit-flip error-rate with its exponential suppression versus the mean photon number.

To reduce errors hence resource overheads, bosonic encodings have emerged, taking advantage of the infinitely large Hilbert space of harmonic oscillators for intrinsic autonomous error correction

However, with such infinite systems, capturing the physics of gates and error processes becomes challenging. Classical numerical simulations require taking into account many states of the Hilbert space to model their dynamics

sellem2023gkp

In addition, simulations of composite systems with more than two modes are often intractable, as the dimension of the total Hilbert space is exponential in the number of modes, each mode description requiring an Hilbert-space of large dimension

The computational requirements even quickly surpass the capabilities of classical computers when considering only two bosonic qubits, and simulating gates involving three bosonic qubits with high precision becomes unfeasible.
Model reduction techniques have thus been developed and can use a more suitable basis of the Hilbert space to describe the physical systems via a subsystem decomposition
AmazonPRXQ2022
;
pantaleoni2023zak
;
schlegel2023coherentstate

Other methods, such as adiabatic elimination, are used to analyze the dynamics of open and dissipative quantum systems under a deterministic Lindblad master equation. Adiabatic elimination corresponds to a perturbation technique known in dynamical and control system theory as singular perturbations for slow/fast systems.
It is related to the Tikhonov approximation theorem (see, e.g.,
VerhulstBook2005
;
kokotovic-book-1
) and its coordinate-free formulation due to Fenichel
Fenichel79
with the
notion of invariant slow manifold of a dynamical system having two time-scales
dynamics: the fast and exponentially converging ones and the slow ones of
reduced dimension.
Adiabatic elimination produces low
dimensional dynamical models via the derivation of the slow differential equation governing the
evolution on the invariant slow manifold
BrionJPA07
;
ZanarC2014PRL
;
AzouitCDC15
;
AzouitQST2017
;
BurgarthQ2019
;
TokiedaIFAC23
.

In this context, we propose here an original numerical method based on adiabatic elimination to simulate on a classical computer, quantum master equations modeling composite systems having fast and local dissipation with weak coupling between the sub-systems and slow decoherence. These calculations are simplified by exploiting the invariant operators attached the fast dynamics.
The resulting reduced model of the
slow evolution yields an
efficient numerical method for classical simulations of composite slow/fast
systems having a too large Hilbert space for brute-force numerical integration
of the original slow/fast master equations.
In particular, we show how to perform classical simulations involving three bosonic qubits with high precision.

Such low-dimensional reduced models are particularly well suited for numerical simulation of
autonomous quantum error correction schemes developed for bosonic codes.
In particular, for cat-qubit systems, around 50 to 100 photons per cat-qubit are required for simulating experimental setups, corresponding to a mean photon number of 10 to 15. Two-qubit quantum process tomography
Smithey1993
;
Chuang1997a
is manageable via standard simulation methods for a small mean photon number but becomes infeasible when it exceeds 10. In the case of a three-qubit gate with a truncation of 100, standard simulations are impossible as they require storing density matrices of dimension
100^{6}
and quantum process tomography would present even greater challenges.
For such cat-qubit systems, several numerical simulations based on formal adiabatic calculations and their numerical implementations are presented. They succeed in capturing both the macroscopic phase-flip errors associated with finite gate time and photon losses (the dominant error process for harmonic oscillators), and also the exponentially small bit-flip errors known to be much harder to estimate
GuillaudMirrahimiPRX2019
;
AmazonPRXQ2022
.
This method enables reduced computations with low-dimensional density operator for the global system state (
2^{6}
for three-qubit gate).

In Section
II
, we recall for quantum master differential equations the formalism of stationary states and invariant operators, and detail the formal adiabatic calculations up to the second-order of the continuous-time slow dynamics. These formal calculations are then exploited numerically to simulate the resulting second-order slow model for a Z-gate on a single cat-qubit. Comparison with numerical simulations of the full slow/fast model are given.
In Section
III
, we then extend these second-order calculations to a composite system of locally stabilized subsystems. We show how their numerical implementations can be done with only local computations on the Hilbert space of each subsystem. This avoids computations on the full Hilbert space of the complete system.
For the composite system made of two (resp. three) cat-qubits, numerical simulations of a ZZ (resp. ZZZ) gate are presented with an emphasis on the different error rates.
In Section
IV
, we adapt this simulation method to composite systems for which one of the subsystem is not stabilized.
For two (resp. three) cat-qubits, numerical simulations provide the error probabilities of a CNOT (resp. CCNOT) gate where the target qubit is not stabilized during the gate.
Sections in appendix are mainly devoted to high-order adiabatic calculations, additional simulation results, discrete-time formulations with Kraus maps and the derived time-discretization schemes underlying the numerical simulations.

II.1
Invariant manifold and slow dynamics approximation

The calculations of this sub-section are very similar to section 2 and 3 of
FMRPR-CDC23
.

Consider the time-varying density operator
\rho_{t}
on underlying Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}
obeying to the following dynamics

\centering\frac{d}{dt}\rho_{t}=\mathcal{L}_{0}(\rho_{t})+\epsilon\mathcal{L}_{1}(\rho_{t})\@add@centering

(1)

with two Gorini–Kossakowski–Sudarshan–Lindblad (GKSL) linear superoperators
\mathcal{L}_{0}
and
\mathcal{L}_{1}
where
\epsilon
is a small positive parameter. For
\sigma=0,1
one has

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}_{\sigma}(\rho)=\\
&-i[\widehat{H}_{\sigma},\rho]+\sum_{\nu}\widehat{L}_{\sigma,\nu}\rho\widehat{L}_{\sigma,\nu}^{\dagger}-\tfrac{1}{2}\Big(\widehat{L}_{\sigma,\nu}^{\dagger}\widehat{L}_{\sigma,\nu}\rho+\rho\widehat{L}_{\sigma,\nu}^{\dagger}\widehat{L}_{\sigma,\nu}\Big)\end{split}

(2)

with
\widehat{H}_{\sigma}
Hermitian operator and
\widehat{L}_{\sigma,\nu}
any operator not necessarily Hermitian.

Assume that for
\epsilon=0
and any initial condition
\rho_{0}
, the solution of (
1
)
converges exponentially towards a steady-state depending a priori on
\rho_{0}
. This means that we have a quantum channel
\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}
defined by

\lim_{t\mapsto+\infty}e^{t\mathcal{L}_{0}}(\rho_{0})\triangleq\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}(\rho_{0}).

(3)

The range of
\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}
is denoted by
\mathcal{D}_{0}
, the set of steady-states corresponding to the kernel of
\mathcal{L}_{0}
, a vector subspace of Hermitian operators. Denote by
\bar{d}
the dimension of
\mathcal{D}_{0}
and consider an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{D}_{0}
made of
\bar{d}
Hermitian operators
\widehat{S}_{1}
, …,
\widehat{S}_{\bar{d}}
such that
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{S}_{d}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}\right)=\delta_{d,d^{\prime}}
.
To each
\widehat{S}_{d}
is associated an invariant operator

\widehat{J}_{d}=\lim_{t\mapsto+\infty}e^{t\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}}(\widehat{S}_{d})

being a steady-state of the adjoint dynamics (according to the Frobenius Hermitian product)
\frac{d}{dt}\widehat{J}=\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}(\widehat{J})
where
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}
is the adjoint of
\mathcal{L}_{0}
(see, e.g.,
AlberJ2014PRA
). For any solution
\rho_{t}
of (
1
) with
\epsilon=0
,
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d}\rho_{t}\right)
is constant. This gives the following expression for
\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}
:

\lim_{t\mapsto+\infty}\rho_{t}=\sum_{d=1}^{\bar{d}}\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d}\rho_{0}\right)\widehat{S}_{d}\triangleq\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}(\rho_{0}).

(4)

Moreover,
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}\right)=\delta_{d,d^{\prime}}
since for any
t>0

\begin{split}&\rm{Tr}\left(e^{t\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}}(\widehat{S}_{d})~\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}\right)=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{S}_{d}~e^{t\mathcal{L}_{0}}(\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}})\right)\\
&=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{S}_{d}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}\right)=\delta_{d,d^{\prime}}\end{split}

(5)

using the fact that
e^{t\mathcal{L}_{0}}(\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}})=\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}
.

For
\epsilon>0
and small, Eq. (
1
) also admits a
\bar{d}

dimensional linear subspace denoted by
\mathcal{D}_{\epsilon}
invariant and close to

\mathcal{D}_{0}
(see
kato-book-66
for a mathematical justification in finite dimension). Thus,
the set of
\bar{d}
real variables

x_{1}=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{1}\rho\right),\ldots,x_{\bar{d}}=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{\bar{d}}\rho\right)

can be chosen to be local coordinates on
\mathcal{D}_{\epsilon}
:
any density operators
\rho\in\mathcal{D}_{\epsilon}
reads

\rho=\sum_{d=1}^{\bar{d}}x_{d}\widehat{S}_{d}(\epsilon)
with the perturbed basis
\widehat{S}_{1}(\epsilon)
, …
\widehat{S}_{\bar{d}}(\epsilon)
and
\bar{d}
real numbers
x_{d}
.

Invariance of
\mathcal{D}_{\epsilon}
with respect to (
1
) means that, if at some time
t
, the solution
\rho_{t}
of the
perturbed system (
1
) belongs to
\mathcal{D}_{\epsilon}
, it remains on

\mathcal{D}_{\epsilon}
at any time:
\frac{d}{dt}\rho_{t}=(\mathcal{L}_{0}+\epsilon\mathcal{L}_{1})(\rho_{t})

with
\rho_{t}=\sum_{d=1}^{\bar{d}}x_{d}(t)\widehat{S}_{d}(\epsilon)
. For any

(x_{1}(t),\ldots,x_{\bar{d}}(t))\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{d}}
, this invariance
property reads

\sum_{d=1}^{\bar{d}}\frac{dx_{d}}{dt}~\widehat{S}_{d}(\epsilon)=\left(\mathcal{L}_{0}+\epsilon\mathcal{L}_{1}\right)\left(\sum_{d=1}^{\bar{d}}x_{d}\widehat{S}_{d}(\epsilon)\right).

(6)

Thus, for any
d\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\}
,
\frac{dx_{d}}{dt}
depends linearly on
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\bar{d}})
, i.e.

\frac{d}{dt}x_{d}=\sum_{d^{\prime}}F_{d,d^{\prime}}(\epsilon)x_{d^{\prime}}.

(7)

The invariance condition reads now,

\begin{split}\forall(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\bar{d}})\in\mathbb{R}^{\bar{d}},~\sum_{d,d^{\prime}}x_{d^{\prime}}F_{d,d^{\prime}}(\epsilon)\widehat{S}_{d}(\epsilon)\\
\equiv\sum_{d}x_{d}(\mathcal{L}_{0}+\epsilon\mathcal{L}_{1})(\widehat{S}_{d}(\epsilon))\end{split}

(8)

which is equivalent to

\begin{split}\forall d\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\},\quad\sum_{d^{\prime}=1}^{\bar{d}}F_{d^{\prime},d}(\epsilon)\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}(\epsilon)=(\mathcal{L}_{0}+\epsilon\mathcal{L}_{1})(\widehat{S}_{d}(\epsilon)).\end{split}

(9)

With the asymptotic expansion

\begin{split}F_{d,d^{\prime}}(\epsilon)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\epsilon^{n}F_{d,d^{\prime}}^{(n)},\quad\widehat{S}_{d}(\epsilon)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\epsilon^{n}\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n)}\end{split}

(10)

one can compute recursively
F_{d,d^{\prime}}^{(n)}
and
\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n)}
from
F_{d,d^{\prime}}^{(m)}
and
\widehat{S}_{d}^{(m)}
with
m<n
. The recurrence relationship is based on the identification of terms with same orders versus
\epsilon
in the following equations

\begin{split}&\forall d\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\},\quad\sum_{d^{\prime}=1}^{\bar{d}}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}\epsilon^{n}F_{d^{\prime},d}^{(n)}\right)\left(\sum_{n^{\prime}\geq 0}\epsilon^{n^{\prime}}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}^{(n^{\prime})}\right)\\
&=\left(\mathcal{L}_{0}+\epsilon\mathcal{L}_{1}\right)\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}\epsilon^{n}\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n)}\right).\end{split}

(11)

The zero-order condition is satisfied with
F^{(0)}_{d,d^{\prime}}=0
and
\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)}=\widehat{S}_{d}
.
First-order condition reads

\begin{split}\forall d\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\},\quad\sum_{d^{\prime\prime}=1}^{\bar{d}}F_{d^{\prime\prime},d}^{(1)}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime\prime}}^{(0)}=\mathcal{L}_{0}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)})+\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)}).\end{split}

(12)

Left multiplication by operator
\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}
and taking the trace yields

F_{d^{\prime},d}^{(1)}=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}{{}^{(0)}})\right)

(13)

since
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime\prime}}^{(0)}\right)=\delta_{d^{\prime},d^{\prime\prime}}
and
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\mathcal{L}_{0}(\widehat{W})\right)=0
for any operator
\widehat{W}
because
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}})=0
.
Thus,
\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)}
is a solution
\widehat{X}
of the following equation:

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{0}(\widehat{X})&=\sum_{d^{\prime}}\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}^{(0)}_{d})\right)\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}-\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)})\\
&=\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}\big(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)})\big)-\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)})\end{split}

(14)

where the quantum channel
\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}
is defined in (
3
). Following

, the general solution
\widehat{X}
is given by the absolutely converging integral,

\begin{split}\widehat{X}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{s\mathcal{L}_{0}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)})-\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}\big(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)})\big)\right)~ds+\widehat{W}\end{split}

(15)

where
\widehat{W}
belongs to
\mathcal{D}_{0}
the kernel of
\mathcal{L}_{0}
. We consider the solution with
\widehat{W}=0
and thus

\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{s\mathcal{L}_{0}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)})-\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}\big(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)})\big)\right)~ds

(16)

where for all
d^{\prime}
,
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)}\right)=0
.
The superoperator
\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}
defined for any operator
\widehat{W}
by

\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(\widehat{W})=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{s\mathcal{L}_{0}}\left(\widehat{W}-\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}\big(\widehat{W}\big)\right)~ds

(17)

provides thus the unique solution
\widehat{X}=\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(\widehat{W})
of

\mathcal{L}_{0}(\widehat{X})=\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}(\widehat{W})-\widehat{W}

such that for all
d
,
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d}\widehat{X}\right)=0
. To summarize, the first-order terms in
\epsilon
are

F_{d^{\prime},d}^{(1)}=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}{{}^{(0)}})\right)\text{ and }\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)}=\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}\big(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d})\big).

(18)

Second-order conditions are

\begin{split}&\forall d\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\},\\
&\sum_{d^{\prime\prime}=1}^{\bar{d}}F_{d^{\prime\prime},d}^{(1)}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime\prime}}^{(1)}+F_{d^{\prime\prime},d}^{(2)}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime\prime}}^{(0)}=\mathcal{L}_{0}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(2)})+\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)}).\end{split}

(19)

Left multiplication by operator
\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}
and taking the trace yields:

F_{d^{\prime},d}^{(2)}=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)})\right)=\rm{Tr}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1}^{*}(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}})~\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)}\right).

(20)

Computations similar to the ones performed for the first-order conditions yield

\widehat{S}_{d}^{(2)}=\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)})-\sum_{d^{\prime\prime}=1}^{\bar{d}}F_{d^{\prime\prime},d}^{(1)}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime\prime}}^{(1)}\right).

(21)

Higher order formulae are given in appendix

.

II.2
Z-gate simulations for a single cat-qubit

For a cat-qubit system
MirrahimiCatComp2014
;
LeghtTPKVPSNSHRFSMD2015S
;
GuillaudMirrahimiPRX2019
, the quantum state
\rho
is attached to a harmonic oscillator. It is confined through an engineered
two-photon driven dissipation process to have its range close to a two-dimensional subspace spanned by two coherent wave functions
\left|\pm\alpha\right\rangle
of opposite complex amplitudes
\pm\alpha
. This means that the support of
\rho
remains close to the sub-Hilbert space of dimension
2
spanned by the orthonormal wave functions (the Schrödinger cat states)

\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{\pm}\right\rangle:=\mathcal{N}_{\pm}(\left|\alpha\right\rangle\pm\left|-\alpha\right\rangle),

(22)

where

\displaystyle\left|0\right\rangle_{C}

\displaystyle=(\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{+}\right\rangle+\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{-}\right\rangle)/\sqrt{2}=\left|\alpha\right\rangle+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2|\alpha|^{2}})

(23)

\displaystyle\left|1\right\rangle_{C}

\displaystyle=(\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{+}\right\rangle-\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{-}\right\rangle)/\sqrt{2}=\left|-\alpha\right\rangle+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2|\alpha|^{2}}).

(24)

The engineered two-photon driven dissipation process can be effectively modeled by as single Lindblad term of the form

It stabilizes exponentially the cat-qubit subspace corresponding then to

Its real dimension is

\displaystyle\widehat{S}_{1}=(\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{+}\right\rangle\left\langle\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{+}\right|+\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{-}\right\rangle\left\langle\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{-}\right|)/\sqrt{2},

(26)

\displaystyle\widehat{S}_{2}=(\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{+}\right\rangle\left\langle\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{+}\right|-\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{-}\right\rangle\left\langle\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{-}\right|)/\sqrt{2},

\displaystyle\widehat{S}_{3}=(i\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{+}\right\rangle\left\langle\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{-}\right|-i\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{-}\right\rangle\left\langle\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{+}\right|)/\sqrt{2},

III.1
Second-order approximation with only local computations

Take a bipartite system made of sub-systems
A
and
B
with Hilbert spaces

\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
. The system Hilbert space is
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
.
Assume that the unperturbed dynamics
\mathcal{L}_{0}
in (
1
) admit the following structure:

\mathcal{L}_{0}=\mathcal{L}_{A,0}+\mathcal{L}_{B,0}

(32)

where
\mathcal{D}_{A,0}
and
\mathcal{D}_{B,0}
are the
steady-state subspaces of operators on
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
of local Lindblad
superoperators
\mathcal{L}_{A,0}
and
\mathcal{L}_{B,0}
. These local nominal dynamics
stabilize the subspaces of dimensions
\bar{d}_{A}
and
\bar{d}_{B}
, having

(\widehat{S}_{A,d_{A}})_{1\leq d_{A}\leq\bar{d}_{A}}
and
(\widehat{S}_{B,d_{B}})_{1\leq d_{B}\leq\bar{d}_{B}}
as orthonormal basis of Hermitian operators. Thus, all Hermitian operators in
\mathcal{D}_{0}
, the kernel of
\mathcal{L}_{A}+\mathcal{L}_{B}
, read

\sum_{d_{A},d_{B}}x_{d_{A},d_{B}}\widehat{S}_{A,d_{A}}\otimes\widehat{S}_{B,d_{B}}

(33)

where
x_{d_{A},d_{B}}
are arbitrary real numbers.

We assume that
\mathcal{L}_{A,0}

and
\mathcal{L}_{B,0}
ensure exponential convergence towards
\mathcal{D}_{A,0}
and

\mathcal{D}_{B,0}
: for any operators
\rho
on
\mathcal{H}
,

\begin{split}&\lim_{t\mapsto+\infty}e^{t(\mathcal{L}_{A,0}+\mathcal{L}_{B,0})}(\rho)=\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}(\rho)\\
&=\sum_{d_{A},d_{B}}\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{A,d_{A}}\otimes\widehat{J}_{B,d_{B}}~\rho\right)\widehat{S}_{A,d_{A}}\otimes\widehat{S}_{B,d_{B}}\end{split}

(34)

where
\widehat{J}_{A,d_{A}}
and
\widehat{J}_{B,d_{B}}
are local invariant operators

\begin{split}&\widehat{J}_{A,d_{A}}=\lim_{t\mapsto+\infty}e^{t\mathcal{L}_{A,0}^{*}}(\widehat{S}_{A,d_{A}}),\\
&\widehat{J}_{B,d_{B}}=\lim_{t\mapsto+\infty}e^{t\mathcal{L}_{B,0}^{*}}(\widehat{S}_{B,d_{B}}).\end{split}

(35)

Assume that
\widehat{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{1,\nu}
defining the super operator

\mathcal{L}_{1}
in (
1
) only involve finite sums of tensor products of
operators on
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
. This means that for any
\widehat{X}_{A}
and
\widehat{X}_{B}

local operators on
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
,

\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{X}_{A}\otimes\widehat{X}_{B})=\sum_{\nu=1}^{\bar{\nu}}\widehat{L}_{A,\nu}\widehat{X}_{A}\widehat{R}_{A,\nu}\otimes\widehat{L}_{B,\nu}\widehat{X}_{B}\widehat{R}_{B,\nu}

(36)

where
\bar{\nu}
is a positive integer, where
\widehat{L}_{A,\nu}
,
\widehat{R}_{A,\nu}
are operators on
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and where
\widehat{L}_{B,\nu}
,
\widehat{R}_{B,\nu}
are operators on
\mathcal{H}_{B}
.

The operator
\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}
appearing in (
13
) corresponds here to

\widehat{J}_{A,d^{\prime}_{A}}\otimes\widehat{J}_{B,d^{\prime}_{B}}
with
d^{\prime}=(d^{\prime}_{A},d^{\prime}_{B})
. Similarly,
\widehat{S}_{d}

reads here
\widehat{S}_{A,d_{A}}\otimes\widehat{S}_{B,d_{B}}
with
d=(d_{A},d_{B})
.
With (
36
) one obtains

F^{(1)}_{(d^{\prime}_{A},d^{\prime}_{B}),(d_{A},d_{B})}=\sum_{\nu=1}^{\bar{\nu}}\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{A,d^{\prime}_{A}}\widehat{L}_{A,\nu}\widehat{S}_{A,d_{A}}\widehat{R}_{A,\nu}\right)\ldots\\
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{B,d^{\prime}_{B}}\widehat{L}_{B,\nu}\widehat{S}_{B,d_{B}}\widehat{R}_{B,\nu}\right).

(37)

For
X=A,B
, consider the local operators

\widehat{J}_{X,d^{\prime}_{X},\nu}=\widehat{R}_{X,\nu}\widehat{J}_{X,d^{\prime}_{X}}\widehat{L}_{X,\nu},\quad\widehat{S}_{X,d_{X},\nu}=\widehat{L}_{X,\nu}\widehat{S}_{X,d_{X}}\widehat{R}_{X,\nu}.

(38)

Then

F^{(1)}_{(d^{\prime}_{A},d^{\prime}_{B}),(d_{A},d_{B})}\\
=\sum_{\nu=1}^{\bar{\nu}}\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{A,d^{\prime}_{A}}\widehat{S}_{A,d_{A},\nu}\right)\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{B,d^{\prime}_{B}}\widehat{S}_{B,d_{B},\nu}\right)\\
=\sum_{\nu=1}^{\bar{\nu}}\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{S}_{A,d_{A}}\widehat{J}_{A,d^{\prime}_{A},\nu}\right)\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{S}_{B,d_{B}}\widehat{J}_{B,d^{\prime}_{B},\nu}\right).

(39)

This gives the first-order approximation of the reduced dynamics for which the coordinate vector
x=(x_{d^{\prime}_{A},d^{\prime}_{B}})_{d^{\prime}_{A},d^{\prime}_{B}}
evolves according to:

\frac{d}{dt}x_{d^{\prime}_{A},d^{\prime}_{B}}=\sum_{d_{A},d_{B}}\epsilon F^{(1)}_{(d^{\prime}_{A},d^{\prime}_{B}),(d_{A},d_{B})}x_{d_{A},d_{B}}.

(40)

Take the second-order correction
F^{(2)}
given by the general formula (
20
).
We have

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{*}(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}})&=\sum_{\nu^{\prime}=1}^{\bar{\nu}}\widehat{J}_{A,d^{\prime}_{A},\nu^{\prime}}\otimes\widehat{J}_{B,d^{\prime}_{B},\nu^{\prime}},\\
\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d})&=\sum_{\nu=1}^{\bar{\nu}}\widehat{S}_{A,d_{A},\nu}\otimes\widehat{S}_{B,d_{B},\nu}\end{split}

(41)

where
d^{\prime}=(d^{\prime}_{A},d^{\prime}_{B})
and
d=(d_{A},d_{B})
.
By linearity of
\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}_{1}^{*}(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}})\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}\big(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d})\big)\\
&=\sum_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}=1}^{\bar{\nu}}\Big(\widehat{J}_{A,d^{\prime}_{A},\nu^{\prime}}\otimes\widehat{J}_{B,d^{\prime}_{B},\nu^{\prime}}\Big)\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}\Big(\widehat{S}_{A,d_{A},\nu}\otimes\widehat{S}_{B,d_{B},\nu}\Big).\end{split}

(42)

Combining
e^{s\mathcal{L}_{A,0}+s\mathcal{L}_{B,0}}=e^{s\mathcal{L}_{A,0}}\otimes e^{s\mathcal{L}_{B,0}}
with (
17
) and (
34
) gives

\begin{split}\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}\Big(\widehat{S}_{A,d_{A},\nu}\otimes\widehat{S}_{B,d_{B},\nu}\Big)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\Bigg(&e^{s\mathcal{L}_{A,0}}\big(\widehat{S}_{A,d_{A},\nu}\big)\otimes e^{s\mathcal{L}_{B,0}}\big(\widehat{S}_{B,d_{B},\nu}\big)\\
&-\sum_{d^{\prime\prime}_{A},d^{\prime\prime}_{B}}\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{A,d^{\prime\prime}_{A}}\widehat{S}_{A,d_{A},\nu}\right)\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{B,d^{\prime\prime}_{B}}\widehat{S}_{B,d_{B},\nu}\right)\widehat{S}_{A,d^{\prime\prime}_{A}}\otimes\widehat{S}_{B,d^{\prime\prime}_{B}}\Bigg)~ds\\
\end{split}

(43)

Here, we are only interested in the trace of the product with
\widehat{J}_{A,d^{\prime}_{A},\nu^{\prime}}\otimes\widehat{J}_{B,d^{\prime}_{B},\nu^{\prime}}
:

\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{A,d^{\prime}_{A},\nu^{\prime}}\otimes\widehat{J}_{B,d^{\prime}_{B},\nu^{\prime}}~\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}\Big(\widehat{S}_{A,d_{A},\nu}\otimes\widehat{S}_{B,d_{B},\nu}\Big)\right)\\
={{\int}}_{0}^{+\infty}\Bigg(\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{A,d^{\prime}_{A},\nu^{\prime}}~e^{s\mathcal{L}_{A,0}}\big(\widehat{S}_{A,d_{A},\nu}\big)\right)\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{B,d^{\prime}_{B},\nu^{\prime}}~e^{s\mathcal{L}_{B,0}}\big(\widehat{S}_{B,d_{B},\nu}\big)\right)\ldots\\
\ldots-\sum_{d^{\prime\prime}_{A},d^{\prime\prime}_{B}}\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{A,d^{\prime\prime}_{A}}\widehat{S}_{A,d_{A},\nu}\right)\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{B,d^{\prime\prime}_{B}}\widehat{S}_{B,d_{B},\nu}\right)\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{A,d^{\prime}_{A},\nu^{\prime}}~\widehat{S}_{A,d^{\prime\prime}_{A}}\right)\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{B,d^{\prime}_{B},\nu^{\prime}}~\widehat{S}_{B,d^{\prime\prime}_{B}}\right)\Bigg)~ds\\
={{\int}}_{0}^{+\infty}\Bigg(\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{A,d^{\prime}_{A},\nu^{\prime}}~e^{s\mathcal{L}_{A,0}}\big(\widehat{S}_{A,d_{A},\nu}\big)\right)\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{B,d^{\prime}_{B},\nu^{\prime}}~e^{s\mathcal{L}_{B,0}}\big(\widehat{S}_{B,d_{B},\nu}\big)\right)\ldots\\
\ldots-G_{A,d^{\prime}_{A},d_{A},\nu,\nu^{\prime}}G_{B,d^{\prime}_{B},d_{B},\nu,\nu^{\prime}}\Bigg)~ds,

where for
X=A,B

\begin{split}&G_{X,d^{\prime}_{X},d_{X},\nu,\nu^{\prime}}\\
&=\sum_{d^{\prime\prime}_{X}}\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{X,d^{\prime}_{X}}~\widehat{S}_{X,d^{\prime\prime}_{X},\nu^{\prime}}\right)\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{X,d^{\prime\prime}_{X}}\widehat{S}_{X,d_{X},\nu}\right)\end{split}

and using identities like
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{X,d^{\prime}_{X}}~\widehat{S}_{X,d^{\prime\prime}_{X},\nu^{\prime}}\right)\equiv\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{S}_{X,d^{\prime\prime}_{X}}~\widehat{J}_{X,d^{\prime}_{X},\nu^{\prime}}\right)
.

To conclude, one gets each entry of
F^{(2)}
with only local numerical
computations on
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\mathcal{H}_{B}
:

F^{(2)}_{(d^{\prime}_{A},d^{\prime}_{B}),(d_{A},d_{B})}={{\sum}}_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}=1}^{\bar{\nu}}{\int}_{0}^{+\infty}\Bigg(\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{A,d^{\prime}_{A},\nu^{\prime}}~e^{s\mathcal{L}_{A,0}}\big(\widehat{S}_{A,d_{A},\nu}\big)\right)\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{B,d^{\prime}_{B},\nu^{\prime}}~e^{s\mathcal{L}_{B,0}}\big(\widehat{S}_{B,d_{B},\nu}\big)\right)\ldots\\
\ldots-G_{A,d^{\prime}_{A},d_{A},\nu,\nu^{\prime}}G_{B,d^{\prime}_{B},d_{B},\nu,\nu^{\prime}}\Bigg)~ds.

(44)

The equivalent of Eqs. (
39
) and (
44
) for a discrete time setting are given in appendix
D.2

III.2
ZZ gate

A ZZ-gate corresponds to a unitary transformation changing
\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{\pm}\right\rangle\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{\pm}\right\rangle
to
\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{\mp}\right\rangle\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{\mp}\right\rangle
(parity change).
As for the Z-gate implementation, it can be approximately engineered via the propagator of time duration
T>0
associated to the Hamiltonian
\widehat{H}_{1}=\epsilon_{ZZ}\left(\widehat{a}\widehat{b}^{\dagger}+\widehat{a}^{\dagger}\widehat{b}\right)
where

\widehat{a}
(resp.
\widehat{b}
) is the photon annihilation operator on sub-system
A
(resp.
B
) and where
\epsilon_{ZZ}=\frac{\pi}{4\alpha^{2}T}
has to be much smaller than
\kappa_{2}
.
The superoperators
\mathcal{L}_{0}
and
\mathcal{L}_{1}
corresponding here to Eq. (
1
) are thus

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\kappa_{2}\left[D_{\widehat{a}^{2}-\alpha^{2}}+D_{\widehat{b}^{2}-\alpha^{2}}\right](\rho),

(45)

\displaystyle\epsilon\mathcal{L}_{1}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\kappa_{1}\left[D_{\widehat{a}}+D_{\widehat{b}}\right](\rho)-i\tfrac{\pi}{4\alpha^{2}T}\left[\left(\widehat{a}\widehat{b}^{\dagger}+\widehat{a}^{\dagger}\widehat{b}\right),\rho\right]

where
\kappa_{1}/\kappa_{2}
and
T\kappa_{2}
are much smaller than
1
.

\epsilon F^{(1)}_{(d_{A}^{\prime},d_{B}^{\prime}),(d_{A},d_{B})}
and
\epsilon^{2}F^{(2)}_{(d_{A}^{\prime},d_{B}^{\prime}),(d_{A},d_{B})}
defined in (
39
) and (
44
) are computed using discrete-time formulae and provide, up
to third-order terms, the generator of the continuous-time reduced dynamics of Eq. (
30
) with the coordinate-vector
x

x=\left(x_{(d_{A},d_{B})}=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d_{A}}\otimes\widehat{J}_{d_{B}}\rho\right)\right)_{d_{A},d_{B}=1,\ldots,4}.

The parameters of the numerical simulations of figures
6
are

\begin{split}&\kappa_{2}\delta t=\frac{1}{1000},\quad\kappa_{1}=\frac{\kappa_{2}}{100},\\
&\epsilon_{ZZ}=\frac{\pi}{4\alpha^{2}T}=\frac{\kappa_{2}}{20}\text{ with }1\leq\alpha^{2}\leq 16\end{split}

where photon-number truncation
N
is equal to
100
for the reduced-model and to
40
for the full-model.

As for the Z gate, the reduced model error propagator
E_{\text{\tiny red }}=G_{\text{\tiny ideal }}^{-1}G_{\text{\tiny red }}
and the full model error propagator
E_{\text{\tiny full }}=G_{\text{\tiny ideal }}^{-1}G_{\text{\tiny full }}
are close to identity matrix
I_{16}
and characterize the errors. These channels can also be decomposed according to the basis
(\widehat{S}_{A,1},\ldots,\widehat{S}_{A,4})\otimes(\widehat{S}_{B,1},\ldots,\widehat{S}_{B,4})
.
This means that for
E=E_{\text{\tiny red }},E_{\text{\tiny full}}
, the following identity

\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{16},\quad\sum_{d_{A},d_{B},d_{A}^{\prime},d_{B}^{\prime}=1}^{4}E_{(d_{A},d_{B}),(d_{A}^{\prime},d_{B}^{\prime})}x_{(d_{A}^{\prime},d_{B}^{\prime})}\widehat{S}_{A,d_{A}}\widehat{S}_{B,d_{B}}\\
=\sum_{m_{A},m_{B},n_{A},n_{B}=1}^{4}\chi^{E}_{(m_{A},m_{B}),(n_{A},n_{B})}\widehat{S}_{A,m_{A}}\widehat{S}_{B,m_{B}}\left(\sum_{d_{A},d_{B}=1}^{4}x_{(d_{A},d_{B})}\widehat{S}_{A,d_{A}}\widehat{S}_{B,d_{B}}\right)\widehat{S}_{A,n_{A}}\widehat{S}_{B,n_{B}}

(46)

uniquely defines the
16\times 16
,
\chi^{E}
matrix characterizing the errors (close to
\chi^{I_{16}}
having a single non-zero entry
\chi^{I_{16}}_{1,1}=1
).
An illustration of
\chi^{E_{\text{\tiny red }}}
and
\chi^{E_{\text{\tiny full }}}
is given in appendix
E
, figure
12
for
\alpha=2
.

\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{\pm}\right\rangle\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{\pm}\right\rangle\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{\pm}\right\rangle

\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{\mp}\right\rangle\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{\mp}\right\rangle\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{\mp}\right\rangle

V
Concluding remarks

We have introduced a new numerical method for simulating open quantum systems
composed of several subsystems, exponentially stabilized towards
stationnary subspaces.
This numerical method is based on a perturbation
analysis with an original asymptotic expansion exploiting the reduced model
formulation of the dynamics,
relying on the invariant operators of the local and nominal dissipative
dynamics of the subsystems.
The derivation was shown up to a second-order
expansion which can be computed with only local calculations for each subsystem.
We have applied this method on several cat-qubit gates (Z, ZZ, ZZZ, CNOT and CCNOT) and shown that the dominant phase-flip error rates and the exponentially small bit-flip error rates are well described by such reduced-order models and simulations up to 16 photons in each cat-qubit.
Furthermore, this approach, which has provided significant space savings, can be used to an even larger number of bosonic qubits.

The two-photon dissipation of the cat-qubit encoding comes from a more complex master equation involving a buffer mode coupled to the memory cavity via a two-photon exchange Hamiltonian,
MirrahimiCatComp2014
. Similar analysis can thus be built with such composite cavity-buffer description for each cat-qubit.

The derivations shown here can be further applied to other similar composite systems with dominant local stabilization used in autonomous quantum error correction schemes, such as squeezed cat-qubits
Schlegel2022
;
Xu2022
or grid-states
GirvinPRL20
;
Sellem2022
.

This numerical method exploiting strong local dissipation with weak coupling and decoherence in many-body systems
has been presented in the context of continuous-time processes and
could also be useful for time-discrete processes, see App.
D

such as those appearing in quantum error correction schemes, as the repetition code
Guillaud2021

or the surface code
FowlerMariantoniMartinisEtAl2012
.

A.1
Expansion order exceeding 2

Take
n\geq 2
and assume that we have computed all the terms
F_{d^{\prime},d}^{(r)}

and
\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}^{(r)}
of order
r<n
with
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\widehat{S}_{d}^{(r)}\right)=0
for
all
d
and
d^{\prime}
. Invariance condition of order
n
reads

\begin{split}&\forall d\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\},\\
&\sum_{d^{\prime\prime}=1}^{\bar{d}}\sum_{r=1}^{n}F_{d^{\prime\prime},d}^{(r)}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime\prime}}^{(n-r)}=\mathcal{L}_{0}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n)})+\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n-1)}).\end{split}

Left multiplication by operator
\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}
and
taking the trace yields

F_{d^{\prime},d}^{(n)}=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n-1)})\right)=\rm{Tr}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1}^{*}(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}})~\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n-1)})\right).

(52)

For
\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n)}
we take the solution of

\mathcal{L}_{0}(\widehat{X})=\sum_{d^{\prime\prime}=1}^{\bar{d}}\sum_{r=1}^{n}F_{d^{\prime\prime},d}^{(r)}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime\prime}}^{(n-r)}-\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n-1)})

such that, for all
d^{\prime}
,
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\widehat{X}\right)=0
:

\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n)}=\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n-1)})-\sum_{d^{\prime\prime}=1}^{\bar{d}}\sum_{r=1}^{n}F_{d^{\prime\prime},d}^{(r)}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime\prime}}^{(n-r)}\right)\\
=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{s\mathcal{L}_{0}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n-1)})-\sum_{d^{\prime\prime}=1}^{\bar{d}}\sum_{r=1}^{n}F_{d^{\prime\prime},d}^{(r)}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime\prime}}^{(n-r)}\right)~ds.

(53)

Since
\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}(\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime\prime}}^{n-r})=0
for any
r\in\{1,n-1\}
and
\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}\big(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n-1)})\big)=\sum_{d^{\prime\prime}=1}^{\bar{d}}F_{d^{\prime\prime},d}^{(n)}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime\prime}}^{(0)}
, the above integral is absolutely convergent.

With such asymptotic expansion, we get an order
n
approximation of the
dynamics on the invariant slow-manifold
\mathcal{D}_{\epsilon}
, a reduced dynamical
model of (
1
) based on the following
\bar{d}
dimensional linear
system:

\frac{d}{dt}x(t)=\left(\sum_{r=1}^{n}\epsilon^{r}F^{(r)}\right)x(t)

(54)

where
\rho_{t}=\sum_{d=1}^{\bar{d}}x_{d}(t)\left(\sum_{r=0}^{n}\epsilon^{r}\widehat{S}_{d}^{(r)}\right)
satisfies (
1
) up to
\epsilon^{n+1}
terms. Here
F^{(r)}
is the matrix of real entries
F^{(r)}_{d,d^{\prime}}
. Since
x_{d}=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d}\rho_{t}\right)
, the dynamical system (
54
) is an approximation of order
n
for the reduced model slow dynamics of the nominal invariant operators
\widehat{J}_{d}
:
Up-to
\epsilon^{n+1}

Appendix B
Second-order approximation with slow time dependency

Here we only derive the second-order approximation with slow time dependency.
We are looking for solutions of the perturbed system

\frac{d}{dt}\rho_{t}=\mathcal{L}_{0}(\rho_{t})+\epsilon\mathcal{L}_{1}(\epsilon t,\rho_{t})

(55)

based on the following asymptotic expansion:

\rho_{t}=\sum_{d=1}^{d_{0}}x_{d,t}\left(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)}+\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)}\right)

where

X_{t+1}=\left(F^{(0)}+F^{(1)}+F^{(2)}\right)X_{t}

with
F^{(0)}=I
,
X_{t}=\left(x_{1,t},\ldots,x_{d_{0},t}\right)^{T}
.
We assume that the GKSL superoperator
\mathcal{L}_{1}
in (
1
) depends
slowly on time, i.e., that the operators
\widehat{H}_{1}
and
\widehat{L}_{1,\nu}
are smooth
functions of
\epsilon t
:

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}_{1}(\epsilon t,\rho)=-i[\widehat{H}_{1}(\epsilon t),\rho]+\sum_{\nu}\widehat{L}_{1,\nu}(\epsilon t)\rho\widehat{L}_{1,\nu}^{\dagger}(\epsilon t)\\
&-\tfrac{1}{2}\Big(\widehat{L}_{1,\nu}^{\dagger}(\epsilon t)\widehat{L}_{1,\nu}(\epsilon t)\rho+\rho\widehat{L}_{1,\nu}^{\dagger}(\epsilon t)\widehat{L}_{1,\nu}(\epsilon t)\Big).\end{split}

Then for
each
n
,
F^{(n)}_{d^{\prime},d^{\prime}}
and
\widehat{S}^{(n)}_{d}
depend also on
\epsilon t
. Thus, the invariance condition (
6
) becomes

\sum_{d=1}^{\bar{d}}\left(\frac{dx_{d}}{dt}~\widehat{S}_{d}(\epsilon)+x_{d}\frac{d}{dt}\widehat{S}_{d}(\epsilon)\right)=\left(\mathcal{L}_{0}+\epsilon\mathcal{L}_{1}\right)\left(\sum_{d=1}^{\bar{d}}x_{d}\widehat{S}_{d}(\epsilon)\right)

where
F_{d,d^{\prime}}(\epsilon t,\epsilon)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\epsilon^{n}F_{d,d^{\prime}}^{(n)}(\epsilon t)
and
\widehat{S}_{d}(\epsilon t,\epsilon)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\epsilon^{n}\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n)}(\epsilon t)
. One has to
identify terms with same orders versus
\epsilon
in

\forall d\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\},\\
\sum_{n\geq 0}\epsilon^{n}\frac{d}{dt}\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n)}+{{\sum}}_{d^{\prime}=1}^{\bar{d}}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}\epsilon^{n}F_{d^{\prime},d}^{(n)}\right)\left(\sum_{n^{\prime}\geq 0}\epsilon^{n^{\prime}}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}^{(n^{\prime})}\right)\\
=\left(\mathcal{L}_{0}+\epsilon\mathcal{L}_{1}\right)\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}\epsilon^{n}\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n)}\right),

(56)

using the fact that, for each
n
,
\frac{d}{dt}\widehat{S}^{(n)}(\epsilon t)
is of order
\epsilon
. .

The zero-order condition is satisfied with
F^{(0)}_{d,d^{\prime}}=0
and

\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)}=\widehat{S}_{d}
. First-order condition remains unchanged and yields as
in (
13
) and (
16
) to

F_{d^{\prime},d}^{(1)}(\epsilon t)=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\mathcal{L}_{1}(\epsilon t,\widehat{S}_{d})\right)

with

\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)}(\epsilon t)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{s\mathcal{L}_{0}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\epsilon t,\widehat{S}_{d})-\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}\big(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\epsilon t,\widehat{S}_{d})\big)\right)~ds

(57)

where

\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)}(\epsilon t)\right)=0
and thus
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}~\frac{d}{dt}\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)}(\epsilon t)\right)=0
, for all
d^{\prime}
and
t
.
The second-order
condition is:

\forall d\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\},\\
\frac{d}{d(\epsilon t)}\widehat{S}^{(1)}_{d}(\epsilon t)+\sum_{d^{\prime\prime}=1}^{\bar{d}}\left(F_{d^{\prime\prime},d}^{(1)}(\epsilon t)\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime\prime}}^{(1)}(\epsilon t)+F_{d^{\prime\prime},d}^{(2)}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime\prime}}\right)\\
=\mathcal{L}_{0}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(2)})+\mathcal{L}_{1}(\epsilon t,\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)}(\epsilon t)).

(58)

Multiplying by
\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}
and tacking the
trace show that the second-order correction formula is identical to the one for
time-invariant
\mathcal{L}_{1}
. To summarize, we have either for time-invariant or
slowly time-varying
\mathcal{L}_{1}
, the following second-order approximation formula
for the dynamics of
x
:

\forall d^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\},\quad\frac{d}{dt}x_{d^{\prime}}=\sum_{d=1}^{\bar{d}}\left(\epsilon F_{d^{\prime},d}^{(1)}(\epsilon t)+\epsilon^{2}F_{d^{\prime},d}^{(2)}(\epsilon t)\right)x_{d}

(59)

with

F_{d^{\prime},d}^{(1)}(\epsilon t)=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\mathcal{L}_{1}(\epsilon t,\widehat{S}_{d})\right),\\
F^{(2)}_{d^{\prime},d}(\epsilon t)=\rm{Tr}\left(\mathcal{L}_{1}^{*}(\epsilon t,\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}})~\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}\Big(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\epsilon t,\widehat{S}_{d})\Big)\right)

(60)

where

We propose here an adapted numerical scheme to convert the continuous-time

1
) into a discrete-time dynamic (
65

Take a time-step
\delta t>0
very small compared to evolution time-constant
of (

identical, up to

\rho_{t+\delta t}=\mathcal{K}_{0}(\rho_{t})\\

where
\mathcal{K}_{0}
admits the following Kraus structure:

\begin{split}&\mathcal{K}_{0}(\rho)=\widehat{U}_{0}\left(\mathbf{\widehat{M}}_{0}~\widehat{U}_{0}\rho\widehat{U}_{0}^{\dagger}~\mathbf{\widehat{M}}_{0}^{\dagger}\right.\\

with

\begin{split}\widehat{U}_{0}=e^{-i\delta t\widehat{H}_{0}/2},\quad\mathbf{\widehat{M}}_{0}=\widehat{M}_{0}\widehat{W}_{0}^{-1/2},\quad\mathbf{\widehat{L}}_{0,\nu}=\widehat{L}_{0,\nu}~\widehat{W}_{0}^{-1/2}\end{split}

where

\begin{split}&\widehat{M}_{0}=I-\sum_{\nu}\tfrac{\delta t}{2}\widehat{L}_{0,\nu}^{\dagger}\widehat{L}_{0,\nu},\\
&\widehat{W}_{0}=\widehat{M}_{0}^{\dagger}\widehat{M}_{0}+\delta t\sum_{\nu}\widehat{L}_{0,\nu}^{\dagger}\widehat{L}_{0,\nu}.\end{split}

Take as perturbation
\mathcal{K}_{1}
the simplest approximation:

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}_{1}(\rho)=\delta t\mathcal{L}_{1}(\rho).\end{split}

(64)

Adiabatic elimination in discrete-time

D.1
Single system

When
t
is an integer, (
1
) is replaced by

\rho_{t+1}=\mathcal{K}_{0}(\rho_{t})+\epsilon\mathcal{K}_{1}(\rho_{t})

(65)

where
\mathcal{K}_{0}
is a quantum channel stabilizing the subspace
\mathcal{D}_{0}
spanned by the orthonormal basis
\widehat{S}_{d}

\widehat{J}_{d}=\lim_{t\mapsto+\infty}(\mathcal{K}_{0}^{*})^{t}(\widehat{S}_{d})

(\mathcal{K}_{0}^{*})^{t}

\mathcal{K}_{0}^{*}

For any

\begin{split}\lim_{t\mapsto+\infty}(\mathcal{K}_{0})^{t}(\rho_{0})=\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}(\rho_{0})=\sum_{d}\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d}\rho_{0}\right)\widehat{S}_{d}.\end{split}

(66)

Invariance condition (

\begin{split}\sum_{d=1}^{\bar{d}}x_{d}(t+1)~\widehat{S}_{d}(\epsilon)=\left(\mathcal{K}_{0}+\epsilon\mathcal{K}_{1}\right)\left(\sum_{d=1}^{\bar{d}}x_{d}(t)\widehat{S}_{d}(\epsilon)\right)\end{split}

(67)

\widehat{S}_{d}(\epsilon)

\begin{split}&\forall d\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\},\\

&=\left(\mathcal{K}_{0}+\epsilon\mathcal{K}_{1}\right)\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}\epsilon^{n}\widehat{S}_{d}^{(n)}\right).\end{split}

The zero-order term is satisfied with
F^{(0)}_{d,d^{\prime}}=\delta_{d,d^{\prime}}

\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)}=\widehat{S}_{d}

\begin{split}&\forall d\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\},\\
&\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)}+\sum_{d^{\prime\prime}=1}^{\bar{d}}F_{d^{\prime\prime},d}^{(1)}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime\prime}}^{(0)}=\mathcal{K}_{0}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)})+\mathcal{K}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)}).\end{split}

Left multiplication by operator
\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}
and taking the trace yields

F_{d^{\prime},d}^{(1)}=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\mathcal{K}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d})\right)

(68)

since
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime\prime}}^{(0)}\right)=\delta_{d^{\prime},d^{\prime\prime}}
and
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\mathcal{K}_{0}(\widehat{W})\right)=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\widehat{W}\right)
for any operator
\widehat{W}
because
\mathcal{K}_{0}^{*}(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}})=\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}
.
Thus,
\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)}
is a solution
\widehat{X}
of the following equation:

\widehat{X}=\mathcal{K}_{0}(\widehat{X})+\mathcal{K}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)})-\sum_{d^{\prime\prime}=1}^{\bar{d}}F_{d^{\prime\prime},d}^{(1)}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime\prime}}^{(0)}

Since the quantum channel
\mathcal{K}_{0}
is a contraction with a rate assumed to be strictly less than 1, the following solution is chosen,

\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)}=\sum_{s\geq 0}(\mathcal{K}_{0})^{s}\left(\mathcal{K}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)})-\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}\big(\mathcal{K}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)})\big)\right),

based on this absolutely converging series and satisfying

\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)}\right)=0
for all
d^{\prime}
.
This defines the superoperator

\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}(\widehat{W})=\sum_{s=0}^{+\infty}(\mathcal{K}_{0})^{s}\big(\widehat{W}-\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{0}(\widehat{W})\big)

where
(\mathcal{K}_{0})^{0}
stands for identity.

Take
n\geq 2
and assume that we have computed all the terms

\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime}}^{(r)}

\forall d\in\{1,\ldots,\bar{d}\},\\

Left multiplication by operator
\widehat{J}_{d^{\prime}}
and
taking the trace yields

For

Appendix F
Leakage computation

F.1
Single-mode leakage

The equation (
16
) allows to perform a first-order computation of the leakage, defined as the population outside the code space.
If we define
\widehat{I}_{c}
to be the projector on the code space of our system, then the population of the state
\rho_{t}
at a given time
t
inside the code space is
\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{I}_{c}\rho_{t}\right)
.
In the context of cat-qubit, the code space projector is defined by

\widehat{I}_{c}=(\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{+}\right\rangle\!\left\langle\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{+}\right|+\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{-}\right\rangle\left\langle\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{-}\right|)\sim\sqrt{2}\widehat{S}_{1}.

So for any state written at first-order

\rho_{t}=\sum_{d=1}^{d_{0}}x_{d,t}\left(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)}+\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)}\right)
,
the leakage
l
is given by:

l(t)=1-\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{I}_{c}\rho_{t}\right)=1-\sum_{d=1}^{d_{0}}x_{d,t}c_{d}

(77)

where

c_{d}=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{I}_{c}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(0)}+\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)})\right)=\sqrt{2}\delta_{1,d}+\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{I}_{c}~\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}\big(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d})\big)\right).

(78)

F.2
Composite-system leakage

In the case of a composite system, we can still compute the leakage at first-order, using the generalization of equation (
16
).
For
(d_{A},d_{B})
, we define
c_{d_{A},d_{B}}
as:

c_{d_{A},d_{B}}=\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{I}_{c,A}\widehat{I}_{c,B}\widehat{S}_{d_{A},d_{B}}(\epsilon)\right)

where
\widehat{S}_{d_{A},d_{B}}(\epsilon)=\widehat{S}_{d_{A},d_{B}}^{(0)}+\widehat{S}_{d_{A},d_{B}}^{(1)}
with
\widehat{S}_{d_{A},d_{B}}^{(0)}=\widehat{S}_{A,d_{A}}^{(0)}\widehat{S}_{B,d_{B}}^{(0)}
and

\widehat{S}_{d_{A},d_{B}}^{(1)}=\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}\big(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d_{A},d_{B}}^{(0)})\big)=\sum_{\nu=1}^{\bar{\nu}}\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}\Big(\widehat{S}_{A,d_{A},\nu}\otimes\widehat{S}_{B,d_{B},\nu}\Big).

So we find that
c_{d_{A},d_{B}}=2\delta_{1,d_{A}}\delta_{1,d_{B}}+\sum_{\nu=1}^{\bar{\nu}}\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{I}_{c,A}\widehat{I}_{c,B}\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}\Big(\widehat{S}_{A,d_{A},\nu}\otimes\widehat{S}_{B,d_{B},\nu}\Big)\right)
.

And finally, the leakage
l
of a state
\rho_{t}=\sum_{d_{A},d_{B}}x_{d_{A},d_{B},t}\widehat{S}_{d_{A},d_{B}}
is given by:

l(t)=1-\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{I}_{c}\rho_{t}\right)=1-\sum_{d_{A},d_{B}=1}^{d_{0}}x_{d_{A},d_{B},t}c_{d_{A},d_{B}}

(79)

However, second-order leakage can be obtained numerically via the following relation

\rm{Tr}\left(\widehat{I}_{c}\widehat{S}_{d}^{(2)}\right)=\rm{Tr}\left(\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}^{\star}\left(\widehat{I}_{c}\right)\left(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\widehat{S}_{d}^{(1)})-\sum_{d^{\prime\prime}=1}^{\bar{d}}F_{d^{\prime\prime},d}^{(1)}\widehat{S}_{d^{\prime\prime}}^{(1)}\right)\right)

with only local computations.

Appendix G
Analytic error models

In this section, we briefly recall the formalism of the Shifted Fock Basis (SFB) introduced in
[
19
]
in the context of cat-qubits.
We use the decomposition of the cat-qubit into a two-Level System (TLS) and a gauge to derive analytical formulas of phase-flip errors for the ZZZ gate.

The basis is defined as the displacement along the
+\alpha
and
-\alpha
directions of the Fock states
\left|n\right\rangle
:

\left|\pm\right\rangle_{L}\otimes\left|n\right\rangle_{g}:=\mathcal{N}_{\pm}\left[\hat{D}(\alpha)\pm(-1)^{n}\hat{D}(-\alpha)\right]\left|\hat{n}=n\right\rangle.

We can equivalently think about it as a separation of the full Hilbert space as a direct sum between the even an odd parity spaces or, after relabelling, as a tensor product structure of a logical two level system, a qubit encoding the logical state of the cat mode, and a gauge mode
\widehat{g}
of another oscillator:

\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}_{L}^{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{g}.

For example, using this basis, the Schrödinger cat states
\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{\pm}\right\rangle
are given by:

\left|\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{\pm}\right\rangle=\left|\pm\right\rangle_{L}\otimes\left|0\right\rangle_{g}.

We will use the following approximation of the annihilation operator, valid for large cat-qubits:

\hat{a}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle|\alpha|^{2}\gg d}}{{\longrightarrow}}\hat{Z}\otimes(\widehat{g}_{a}+\alpha).

(80)

This decomposition of the annihilation operator of the full mode as a
\hat{Z}
operator acting on a qubit tensored with a gauge mode
\widehat{g}_{a}
is well suited in the pertubative regime where the cat-qubit can be excited to its first excited state, but will quickly decay back to its ground state because of the engineered two-photon dissipation.

Indeed, the operator of the dissipation mechanism
\widehat{a}^{2}-|\alpha|^{2}
is more intuitive than the annihilation operator because it corresponds to
2\alpha\widehat{I}\otimes\widehat{g}
, i.e. just to cool down the gauge mode
\widehat{g}
to vacuum.

In the following, we will use these correspondence in order to compute analytical expressions of the
Z
errors of the Z and ZZ gates used in Sec.
II.2
and Sec.
III.2
, and explicitly derive the analytical expressions of the
Z
errors of the ZZZ gate involving three cat-qubits used in Sec.
III.3
.

G.1
Z and ZZ gates

The analytical expressions of the
Z
errors of the Z gate were derived using the SFB in
[
19
]
by adiabatically eliminating the gauge which decays to the ground state manifold with the two-photon dissipation and induces phase flips via the coupling Hamiltonian:
p_{Z}=|\alpha|^{2}\kappa_{1}T+\frac{\epsilon_{Z}^{2}T}{|\alpha|^{2}\kappa_{2}}
.
For a ZZ gate, the gauges of both modes are adiabatically eliminated independently.
The two-photon dissipators with a decay rate
\kappa=4|\alpha|^{2}\kappa_{2}
and the coupling Hamiltonian of rate
g=\epsilon_{ZZ}
simplify into a single dissipator with a rate
2\cdot 4g^{2}/\kappa=2\epsilon_{ZZ}^{2}/\kappa_{2}
causing a
ZZ
errors with probability
p_{Z_{a}Z_{b}}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{8\alpha^{4}\kappa_{2}T}=\frac{\pi\epsilon_{ZZ}}{2\alpha^{2}\kappa_{2}}
while the
Z_{a}
and
Z_{b}
errors are pure photon loss errors
p_{Z_{a}}=p_{Z_{b}}=\alpha^{2}\kappa_{1}T=\frac{\pi\kappa_{1}}{4\epsilon_{ZZ}}
.
To obtain the total value of
p_{Z_{a}Z_{b}}
, one has to add the errors due to single photon losses on the two qubits
p_{Z_{a}}p_{Z_{b}}
.

G.2
ZZZ gate

We first recall the full master equation then write its expression in the SFB
before performing an adiabatic elimination of the three gauges to derive the
ZZZ
error rate.
As detailed in
III.3
, the master equation of this tripartite systems made of three cat-qubits with
annihilation operators
\widehat{a}
,
\widehat{b}
and
\widehat{c}
and gauges
\widehat{g}_{a}
,
\widehat{g}_{a}
and
\widehat{g}_{c}
is composed of the stabilization
\mathcal{L}_{0}
and perturbations
\epsilon\mathcal{L}_{1}
that can be split between errors and gate dynamics:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}(\rho)=\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{a}^{2}-\alpha^{2}}(\rho)+\kappa_{2}\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{b}^{2}-\alpha^{2}}(\rho)+\kappa_{2}\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{c}^{2}-\alpha^{2}}(\rho),

\displaystyle\epsilon\mathcal{L}_{1}(\rho)=\kappa_{1}\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{a}}(\rho)+\kappa_{1}\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{b}}(\rho)+\kappa_{1}\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{c}}(\rho)-i\left[\widehat{H}_{1},\rho\right]

where
\widehat{H}_{1}=\epsilon_{ZZZ}\left(\widehat{a}\widehat{b}\widehat{c}^{\dagger}+\widehat{a}^{\dagger}\widehat{b}^{\dagger}\widehat{c}\right)
is
applied for a gate-time
T=\frac{\pi}{4|\alpha|^{3}\epsilon_{ZZZ}}
.

In the SFB, the dissipation writes:

\widehat{a}^{2}-\alpha^{2}=\widehat{g}_{a}^{2}+2\alpha\widehat{g}_{a}\sim 2\alpha\widehat{g}_{a}

and so the stabilization
\mathcal{L}_{0}
becomes
4|\alpha|^{2}\kappa_{2}(\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{g}_{a}}+\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{g}_{b}}+\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{g}_{c}})(\rho)
.
The one photon loss becomes:

\kappa_{1}|\alpha|^{2}\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{Z}_{a}}(\rho)
.

The gate dynamics
\widehat{H}_{1}
becomes

2|\alpha|^{3}\epsilon_{ZZZ}\widehat{Z}_{a}\widehat{Z}_{b}\widehat{Z}_{c}\\
+\epsilon_{ZZZ}|\alpha|^{2}\widehat{Z}_{a}\widehat{Z}_{b}\widehat{Z}_{c}\otimes\left(\widehat{g}_{a}+\widehat{g}_{a}^{\dagger}+\widehat{g}_{b}+\widehat{g}_{b}^{\dagger}+\widehat{g}_{c}+\widehat{g}_{c}^{\dagger}\right).

The first term of the gate dynamics produces the desired rotation. It comes with excitations on the gauges, each with a coupling strength
g=\epsilon_{ZZZ}|\alpha|^{2}
, inflicting a
ZZZ
error on the cat-qubits. This excitation decays back to the code space (i.e. ground state of the gauges) with a decay rate
\kappa=4|\alpha|^{2}\kappa_{2}
due to
\mathcal{L}_{0}
.
In the regime
\kappa\gg g
, the gauges remain mainly on their ground states and thus can be adiabatically eliminated, by adding an effective
ZZZ
error rate on the qubits with a rate
3\times 4g^{2}/\kappa
, the factor 3 coming from the three gauges indistinctively.
The effective master equation of the effective system
\rho
therefore becomes:

\frac{d}{dt}\rho=\kappa_{1}|\alpha|^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{Z}_{a}}+\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{Z}_{b}}+\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{Z}_{c}})(\rho(t))\\
+\frac{3\epsilon_{ZZZ}|\alpha|^{2}}{\kappa_{2}}\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{Z}_{a}\widehat{Z}_{b}\widehat{Z}_{c}}(\rho(t))\\
-i\left[2|\alpha|^{3}\epsilon_{ZZZ}\widehat{Z}_{a}\widehat{Z}_{b}\widehat{Z}_{c},\rho(t)\right]

The effective Hamiltonian term describes the gate dynamics. We perform a rotation around the ZZZ axis of the qubits with an angle
\theta=4|\alpha|^{3}\epsilon_{ZZZ}T
.
The first terms due to one photon losses induces
Z
errors on the three cat-qubits:
p_{Z_{a}}=p_{Z_{b}}=p_{Z_{c}}=|\alpha|^{2}\kappa_{1}T
.
The
Z_{a}Z_{b}Z_{c}
errors due to the middle term is given by:
3\frac{\epsilon_{ZZZ}^{2}|\alpha|^{2}}{\kappa_{2}}T=\frac{3\pi\epsilon_{ZZZ}}{4\alpha\kappa_{2}}
for a
\pi
rotation, to which one has to add the errors due to single photon losses on the three qubits
p_{Z_{a}}p_{Z_{b}}p_{Z_{c}}
to obtain the total value of
p_{Z_{a}Z_{b}Z_{c}}
.

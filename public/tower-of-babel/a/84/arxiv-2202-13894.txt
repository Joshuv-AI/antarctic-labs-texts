Spherical cap discrepancy of perturbed lattices under the Lambert projection
Damir Ferizović

Abstract
Given any full rank lattice Λ \Lambda and a natural number N N , we regard the point set Λ / N ∩ ( 0 , 1 ) 2 \Lambda/N\cap(0,1)^{2} under the Lambert map to the unit sphere, and show that its spherical cap discrepancy is at most of order N N , with leading coefficient given explicitly and depending on Λ \Lambda only. The proof is established using a lemma that bounds the amount of intersections of certain curves with fundamental domains that tile ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} , and even allows for local perturbations of Λ \Lambda without affecting the bound, proving to be stable for numerical applications. A special case yields the smallest constant for the leading term of the cap discrepancy for deterministic algorithms up to date.

Introduction and main result

The problem of distributing
N
-many points
P_{N}=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{N}\}
uniformly on a sphere is well known and has applications in numerical integration, approximation, cartography and the applied sciences, see
[
28
]
,
[
24
]
and
[
13
]
for some reviews which span over a century of development.

One distinguishes between point sequences and point sets: in the first case
P_{N}
consists of the initial
N
elements of a sequence, and in the latter case
P_{N}
usually can only be constructed for infinitely many
N
, with no relation from one admissible
N
to the next.

There are a few notions to quantify how well distributed
P_{N}
is: one example is the notion of energy, which is the sum of the
N(N-1)
-many evaluations of pair-interactions under a given lower semi-continuous potential
F
, where for
F(x,y)=\|x-y\|^{-s}
one speaks of the Riesz
s
-energy and minimizers of this potential have been intensively investigated, see for instance the comprehensive monograph by Borodachov, Hardin and Saff
[
12
]
.
Another notion is that of seperation distance, i.e. the minimum of the pairwise distances of distinct elements among
P_{N}
.
Our main result uses the notion of spherical cap discrepancy.

Definition 1
.

Let
P_{N}=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{N}\}\subset\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
The spherical cap discrepancy is

\mathcal{D}(P_{N})=\sup_{w\in\mathbb{S}^{2}}\sup_{-1\leq t\leq 1}\Bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\chi_{C(w,t)}(p_{j})-\sigma(C(w,t))\Bigg|.

Here
\mathbb{S}^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the unit two-sphere, i.e. the set of all unit vectors with norm induced by the standard dot product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. A spherical cap is the set

C(w,t)=\big\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\ |\ \langle w,x\rangle\geq t\big\},

with center
w\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
and height
t\in(-1,1)
. The boundary of a set
S
is denoted by
\partial S
, thus

\partial C(w,t)=\big\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{2}\ |\ \langle w,x\rangle=t\big\},

and
\sigma
is the normalized surface measure of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, such that
\sigma\big(C(w,t)\big)=\frac{1}{2}(1-t)
. The characteristic function of a set
A
is
\chi_{A}
, i.e.
\chi_{A}(x)=1
iff
x\in A
, and
0
else.

A reason why discrepancy is useful comes from the following relation

\Bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}f(p_{j})-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}f\ \mathrm{d}\sigma\Bigg|\leq\hat{\mathcal{D}}(P_{N})\mathcal{V}(f),

where
\hat{\mathcal{D}}
is a notion of discrepancy (not restricted to spherical cap) and
\mathcal{V}(f)
is a constant depending on the function
f
, see
[
21
]
,
[
15
]
. Such inequalities often bear the name Koksma–Hlawka in honour of the discoverers.

This paper deals exclusively with point sets on the two-sphere, which are derived as perturbations of any full rank lattice under a specific equal area map and thus adding to the list of known constructions with good distribution properties – an excerpt of which is found below for the readers convenience, where we state the origin and place of investigation to the best of our knowledge.

We will prove a bound on the spherical cap discrepancy of our point constructions which is of the same order as the best results obtained so far for deterministic point sets found in the literature. An open and hard problem is to improve these bounds to the order obtained in a fundamental result due to Beck in
[
5
,
6

The construction of Beck is probabilistic and an algorithm to generate random point sets with discrepancy matching this upper bound with high probability was obtained for instance in
[

]
.

Bounds for deterministic point sets are usually hard to derive and were first achieved by Lubotzky, Phillips and Sarnak in
[

Aistleitner, Brauchart, and Dick showed in

\mathrm{GL(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

Definition 2
.

A lattice
\Lambda^{Q}
is a set

Let

We note that the Lambert map

\partial I^{2}
and maps two sides to the poles. This identification has an effect on the discrepancy, and to describe it we define a parametrization
\rho:[0,4]\rightarrow\partial I^{2}
via
f(x):=\max\big(\min(x,1)+\min(2-x,0),0\big):

x\mapsto\rho(x)\ =\ f(x)e_{1}+f(4-x)e_{2}.

(1)

Before we state our main result, we need to introduce three terms, which are bounded above by constants depending on

1)

Let

be the amount of points in

2)

Set

C_{L}^{Q}=\sup_{w\in\mathbb{S}^{2}}\sup_{-1\leq t\leq 1}\mathrm{length}\Big(Q^{-1}\big(L^{-1}\big(\partial C(w,t)\big)\big)\Big).

3)

For
S\subset[0,1]
and
f
as in (
1

R(S)=\{x\in[0,4]\ |\ f(4-x)\in S\}
and

Ad(S,K)=\big\{p\in\Lambda^{Q}\ |\ T_{K}(p)^{o}\cap\rho(R(S))\neq\emptyset\big\},

the collection of points

such that the interior of

intersects
\partial I^{2}
at
\rho(R(S))
. The supremum of the marginal discrepancy around
\partial I^{2}
then is

M^{Q}(K)=\sup_{0\leq a<b\leq 1}\frac{K}{|\det(Q)|}\ \bigg|\sum_{p\in Ad([a,b],K)}\Big(\frac{\chi_{P^{Q}(K)}(z^{p}_{K})}{N^{Q}(K)}-\lambda\big(T_{K}(p)\cap I^{2}\big)\Big)\bigg|.

The term

bounds the discrepancy that arises around

Theorem 1

(Main Result)
.

Let

and

This follows from Theorem

The Lambert map

By
\mathbb{S}^{2}_{p}
we denote the punctured unit sphere, i.e.
\mathbb{S}^{2}

Definition 3
.

The Lambert map
L
is a well known area preserving map, i.e. for open sets
U\subset I^{2}
and the Lebesgue measure
\lambda
, we have
\lambda(U)=\sigma(L(U))
, where

\begin{array}[]{rcl}L:I^{2}&\rightarrow&\mathbb{S}^{2}_{p}\\
&&\\
(x,y)&\mapsto&\hskip-5.69046pt\left(\hskip-5.69046pt\begin{array}[]{l}2\sqrt{y-y^{2}}\cos(2\pi x)\\
2\sqrt{y-y^{2}}\sin(2\pi x)\\
1-2y\end{array}\hskip-5.69046pt\right),\end{array}

and the inverse is given in terms of the standard parametrization
1
1

1

Here
0\leq\phi<2\pi
and
0<\theta<\pi
.
of
\mathbb{S}^{2}

\begin{array}[]{rcl}L^{-1}:\mathbb{S}^{2}_{p}&\rightarrow&\hskip-5.69046ptI^{2}\\
&&\\
\left(\hskip-5.69046pt\begin{array}[]{l}\cos(\phi)\sin(\theta)\\
\sin(\phi)\sin(\theta)\\
\cos(\theta)\end{array}\hskip-5.69046pt\right)&\mapsto&\hskip-5.69046pt\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\hskip-5.69046pt\begin{array}[]{c}\phi\\
\pi(1-\cos(\theta))\end{array}\hskip-5.69046pt\right).\end{array}

(2)

The map
L
clearly extends to all of

Lemma 2
.

C_{L}^{\mathds{1}}\ \leq\ 3
.

Proof.

Note that the map
L^{-1}
projects points from the sphere, parallel to the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\{0\}
, to the enveloping cylinder (or rather cylindrical surface). The cylinder is then ”cut”, ”rolled” out flat and scaled to a square of area 1. In order to bound the length of the curve
L^{-1}(\partial C\cap\mathbb{S}^{2}_{p})
, it will be enough to work with the projection on the enveloping cylinder, where we make following observation that we consider obviously true:

Let
z_{1}(w,t)<1,\ z_{2}(w,t)>-1
denote the maximal and minimal
z
-value of a spherical cap
C(w,t)
in cylindrical coordinates. If
1>z_{1}(w^{\prime},t^{\prime})\geq z_{1}(w,t)
and
-1<z_{2}(w^{\prime},t^{\prime})\leq z_{2}(w,t)
, then the length of
\partial C(w^{\prime},t^{\prime})
is bigger or equal the one of
\partial C(w,t)
, and the length of
\partial C(w,t)
is bounded by the length of any cap
C
with the same maximal/minimal
z
-value that contains exactly one of the poles of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. This stays true under the aforementioned scaling to the unit square.

Thus in order to maximize the length we let
z_{1},z_{2}
approach
\pm 1
and choose
w
so that exactly one of the poles is contained in the cap, i.e. regard
C(w,0)
with

w=\big(\sin\big(\tfrac{\pi}{2}-\epsilon\big),0,\cos\big(\tfrac{\pi}{2}-\epsilon\big)\big)^{t}

and
\epsilon>0
small.
A parametrization for
\partial C(w,0)
is then given by

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\varphi_{\pm}:[\epsilon,\pi-\epsilon]&\rightarrow&[0,2\pi]\\
&&\\
\theta&\mapsto&\pm\arccos\big(-\cot(\theta)\cot\big(\frac{\pi}{2}-\epsilon\big)\big);\end{array}

such that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\gamma_{+}:[\epsilon,\pi-\epsilon]&\rightarrow&\partial C(w,0)\\
&&\\
\theta&\mapsto&\hskip-8.5359pt\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\cos\big(\varphi_{+}(\theta)\big)\sin(\theta)\\
\sin\big(\varphi_{+}(\theta)\big)\sin(\theta)\\
\cos(\theta)\end{array}\hskip-5.69046pt\right)\end{array}

defines a curve that runs from
z_{1}
to
z_{2}
along
\partial C(w,0)
, and
\gamma_{-}
defines the other half.
Since
L^{-1}\circ\gamma_{+}
and
L^{-1}\circ\gamma_{-}
have the same length, it is enough to consider just one of these curves, bound its length and multiply by 2: thus we regard the curve

\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\hskip-5.69046pt\begin{array}[]{c}\arccos\big(-\cot(\theta)\cot\big(\frac{\pi}{2}-\epsilon\big)\big)\\
\pi(1-\cos(\theta))\end{array}\hskip-5.69046pt\right),

where we already multiplied by a factor of
2
in equation (
2
). Thus we have to bound

\mathrm{length}=\int_{\epsilon}^{\pi-\epsilon}\sqrt{\frac{1}{1-\cot(\theta)^{2}\cot\big(\frac{\pi}{2}-\epsilon\big)^{2}}\frac{\cot\big(\frac{\pi}{2}-\epsilon\big)^{2}}{\pi^{2}\sin(\theta)^{4}}+\sin(\theta)^{2}}\ \mathrm{d}\theta.

We can simplify the expression in the square root, first with the angle sum formula for the cosine (with
\epsilon<\theta<\pi-\epsilon
) to obtain

\frac{-1}{\cos\big(\frac{\pi}{2}-\epsilon-\theta\big)\cos\big(\frac{\pi}{2}-\epsilon+\theta\big)}\frac{\cos\big(\frac{\pi}{2}-\epsilon\big)^{2}}{\pi^{2}\sin(\theta)^{2}}+\sin(\theta)^{2},

and then with
\cos\big(\epsilon-\frac{\pi}{2}\big)=\sin(\epsilon)
and a symmetry argument to get

\mathrm{length}=2\int_{\epsilon}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\sin(\epsilon+\theta)\sin(\theta-\epsilon)}\frac{\sin(\epsilon)^{2}}{\pi^{2}\sin(\theta)^{2}}+\sin(\theta)^{2}}\ \mathrm{d}\theta.

Let
\sinc(x)=\frac{\sin(x)}{x}
, then for any choice of
a\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
, we have

\sinc(a)\ x\leq\sin(x)\leq x,\mbox{ for }x\in[0,a];

which we use for small
\epsilon
and
0<2\epsilon<a<\frac{\pi}{2}
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{length}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\sinc(a)^{2}\pi}\int_{\epsilon}^{a-\epsilon}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\theta^{2}-\epsilon^{2}}\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{\theta^{2}}}\ \mathrm{d}\theta+\frac{\epsilon\cdot\pi^{2}}{4\sqrt{a(a-2\epsilon)^{3}}}+2\int_{\epsilon}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sin(\theta)\ \mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sinc(a)^{2}\pi}\int_{1}^{a/\epsilon-1}\sqrt{\frac{1}{x^{2}-1}}\frac{1}{x}\ \mathrm{d}x+O(\epsilon)+2\overset{\lim\epsilon\rightarrow 0}{=}\frac{2}{\sinc(a)^{2}\pi}\frac{\pi}{2}+2,

where we used that the length increases with decreasing
\epsilon
, and that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\arctan\big(\sqrt{x^{2}-1}\big)=\sqrt{\frac{1}{x^{2}-1}}\frac{1}{x}.

The inequality above is thus true for any
0<a
, and hence also for
\sinc(0)=1
, which proves the claim.

2.2

In this section we will introduce the notion of

Definition 4
.

A continuous curve
\beta:[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
(
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,
a<b
) is
n
-convex for
n\in\mathbb{N}

\beta\big([t_{j},t_{j+1}]\big)
is a convex curve for each

A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
with
\beta\big([t_{j},t_{j+1}]\big)\subset\partial A_{j}
.

A circle is
1
-convex, as is a straight line segment. Finite spirals (in length and winding number) are
n
-convex. It seems that every (finite) connected part of the boundary of a pseudo-convex set, as introduced in
[
1
]

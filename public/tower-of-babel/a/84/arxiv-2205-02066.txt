Biembeddings of Archdeacon type: their full automorphism group and their number
Simone Costa

Abstract
. Archdeacon, in his seminal paper [ 1 ] , defined the concept of Heffter array in order to provide explicit constructions of ℤ v \mathbb{Z}_{v} -regular biembeddings of complete graphs K v K_{v} into orientable surfaces. Then, our main goal is to study the full automorphism groups of these embeddings: here we are able to prove, using a probabilistic approach, that, almost always, this group is exactly ℤ v \mathbb{Z}_{v} . As an application of this result, given a positive integer t ≢ 0 ( mod 4 ) t\not\equiv 0\pmod{4} , we prove that there are, for infinitely many pairs of v v and k k , at least ( 1 − o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) ​ ( v − t 2 ) ! ϕ ⁡ ( v ) (1-o(1))\frac{(\frac{v-t}{2})!}{\phi(v)} non-isomorphic biembeddings of K v t × t K_{\frac{v}{t}\times t} whose face lengths are multiples of k k . Here ϕ ⁡ ( ⋅ ) \phi(\cdot) denotes the Euler’s totient function. Moreover, in case t = 1 t=1 and v v is a prime, almost all these embeddings define faces that are all of the same length k ​ v kv , i.e. we have a more than exponential number of non-isomorphic k ​ v kv -gonal biembeddings of K v K_{v} .

1.
Introduction

An
m\times n
partially filled (p.f., for short) array on a set
\Omega
is an
m\times n
matrix whose elements belong to
\Omega

and where some cells can be empty. In 2015, Archdeacon (see
[
1
]
), introduced a class of p.f. arrays which has been extensively studied:
the
Heffter arrays
.

Definition 1.1
.

A
Heffter array

\mathrm{H}(m,n;h,k)
is an
m\times n
p. f. array with entries in
\mathbb{Z}_{2nk+1}
such that:

(a)

each row contains
h
filled cells and each column contains
k
filled cells,

(b)

for every
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{2nk+1}\setminus\{0\}
, either
x
or
-x
appears in the array,

(c)

the elements in every row and column sum to
0
(in
\mathbb{Z}_{2nk+1}
).

These arrays have been introduced because of their vast variety of applications and links to other problems and concepts such as orthogonal cycle decompositions (see
[
5
,
6
]
) and
2
-colorable embeddings (briefly
biembeddings
) see, for instance,
[
1
,
8
,
11
,
16
]
. The existence problem of this combinatorial structure has been also deeply investigated: we refer to
[
2
,
3
,
7
,
17
]
for the known results in this direction.
This paper will focus mainly on the connection between p.f. arrays and embeddings. To explain this link, we first recall some basic definitions, see
[
28
,
29
]
.

Definition 1.2
.

Given a graph
\Gamma
and a surface
\Sigma
, an
embedding
of
\Gamma
in
\Sigma
is a continuous injective mapping
\psi:\Gamma\rightarrow\Sigma
, where
\Gamma
is viewed with the usual topology as
1
-dimensional simplicial complex.

The connected components of
\Sigma\setminus\psi(\Gamma)
are said
\psi
-
faces
. Also, with abuse of notation, we say that a circuit
F
of
\Gamma
is a face (induced by the embedding
\psi
) if
\psi(F)
is the boundary of a
\psi
-face. Then, if each
\psi
-face is homeomorphic to an open disc, the embedding
\psi
is called
cellular
.
In this context, we say that two embeddings
\psi:\Gamma\rightarrow\Sigma
and
\psi^{\prime}:\Gamma^{\prime}\rightarrow\Sigma^{\prime}
are
isomorphic
if and only if there is a graph isomorphism
\sigma:\Gamma\rightarrow\Gamma^{\prime}
such that
\sigma(F)
is a
\psi^{\prime}
-face if and only if
F
is a
\psi
-face.

Archdeacon, in his seminal paper
[
1
]
, showed that, if some additional technical conditions are satisfied, Heffter arrays provide explicit constructions of
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
-regular biembeddings of complete graphs
K_{v}
into orientable surfaces.
Following
[
10
,
14
]
and
[
15
]
the embeddings defined, using this construction, via partially filled arrays, will be denoted as
embeddings of Archdeacon type
or, more simply,
Archdeacon embeddings
. Indeed, this kind of embedding can be considered also for more general arrays than the Heffter’s. In
[
12
]
, the authors introduced the concept of
relative
Heffter array and, in
[
13
]
, it was proved that it can be used, with essentially the same construction of
[
1
]
, to embed the complete multipartite graph with
v/t
parts each of size
t
, denoted by
K_{\frac{v}{t}\times t}
, into orientable surfaces. We also refer to
[
30
]
, and to the references therein, for the known results on the existence problem of relative Heffter arrays. More recently, in
[
10
]
the authors introduced a variation of the Heffter arrays, denoted by
non-zero sum Heffter
arrays and showed that, also in this case, that embedding is well defined. In this paper, we first provide a generalization of both the (relative)
Heffter
and the
non-zero sum Heffter
arrays, and then we define the Archdeacon embedding in this more general context.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
v=2nk+t
be a positive integer,
where
t
divides
2nk
, and
let
J
be the subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
of order
t
.
A
quasi
-
Heffter array
A
over
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
relative to
J
, denoted by
\mathrm{Q}\mathrm{H}_{t}(m,n;h,k)
, is an
m\times n
p.f. array with elements in
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
such that:

(
\rm{a_{1}})

each row contains
h
filled cells and each column contains
k
filled cells,

(
\rm{b_{1}})

the multiset
[\pm x\mid x\in A]
contains each element of
\mathbb{Z}_{v}\setminus J
exactly once.

If, moreover, also the following property holds, then
A
is said to be a
non-zero sum

Heffter array
A
over
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
relative to
J
, and it is denoted by
\mathrm{N}\mathrm{H}_{t}(m,n;h,k)
.

(
\rm{c_{1}})

the sum of the elements in every row and column is different from
0
(in
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
).

Also, as done with the Heffter arrays and the non-zero sum Heffter arrays, a square quasi-Heffter array will be simply denoted by
\mathrm{Q}\mathrm{H}_{t}(n;k)
or, if
t=1
, by
\mathrm{Q}\mathrm{H}(n;k)
. Note that, following
[
14
]
, we could further generalize this concept to the case where each element of
\mathbb{Z}_{v}\setminus J
is covered exactly
\lambda
times in the multiset
[\pm x\mid x\in A]
(where
A
denotes the partially filled array). However, since when
\lambda>1
the definition of the Archdeacon embedding is more complicated and then it is untrivial even to characterize when two arrays provide the same embedding (see Remark 2.17 of
[
15
]
), here we will focus only on the case
\lambda=1
and we will keep the notation as simple as possible.

A formal definition of the Archdeacon embedding, starting from suitable quasi-Heffter arrays, will be given in Section
2
. Then, we will study the full automorphism group of these kinds of embeddings. As remarked in
[
1
]
(see also
[
13
,
14
]
), the embeddings of Archdeacon type are
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
-regular where, if we start from an
\mathrm{H}_{t}(m,n;h,k)
,
v=2kn+t
. In this paper, we will first show that, if we construct the embedding starting from a
\mathrm{Q}\mathrm{H}_{t}(m,n;h,k)
or from an
\mathrm{N}\mathrm{H}_{t}(m,n;h,k)
, almost always its full automorphism group is exactly
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
. These two theorems will be proved in Section 3 of this paper. Then, in Section 4, we will show that, given a positive integer
t\not\equiv{0}\pmod{4}
, there are, for infinitely many pairs of
v
and
k
, at least
(1-o(1))(\frac{v-t}{2})!
different biembeddings of
K_{\frac{v}{t}\times t}
whose face lengths are multiples of
k
and whose automorphisms group is exactly
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
. Finally, in the last section, we will apply these results to show that we also have (again given a positive integer
t\not\equiv{0}\pmod{4}
and for infinitely many pairs of
v
and
k
), at least
(1-o(1))\frac{(\frac{v-t}{2})!}{\phi(v)}
non-isomorphic embeddings of
K_{\frac{v}{t}\times t}
whose face lengths are multiples of
k
. Here
\phi(\cdot)
denotes the Euler’s totient function. Moreover, if we start from quasi-Heffter arrays of type
\mathrm{Q}\mathrm{H}(n;k)
and
v=2nk+1
is a prime, almost all these embeddings define only faces of length
kv
(where
k
is the second parameter of the quasi-Heffter array), i.e. we have a more than an exponential number of non-isomorphic
kv
-gonal biembeddings of
K_{v}
.
This lower-bound is interesting also from the graph-theoretical point of view. Indeed the number of non-isomorphic embeddings of complete graphs whose faces are of a given length is a well-studied problem (see, for instance,
[
19
,
21
,
22
,
26
,
31
]
) and in several situations, only exponential bounds are known (see
[
4
,
20
,
24
,
25
]
).

2.
The Archdeacon Embedding

Following
[
18
,
23
,
32
]
, we provide an equivalent, but purely combinatorial, definition of graph-embedding into a surface.
Here, we denote by
D(\Gamma)
the set of all the oriented edges of the graph
\Gamma
and, given a vertex
x
of
\Gamma
, by
N(\Gamma,x)
the neighborhood of
x
in
\Gamma
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a connected multigraph. A
combinatorial embedding
of
\Gamma
(into an orientable surface) is a pair
\Pi=(\Gamma,\rho)
where
\rho:D(\Gamma)\rightarrow D(\Gamma)
satisfies the following properties:

(a)

for any
y\in N(\Gamma,x)
, there exists
y^{\prime}\in N(\Gamma,x)
such that
\rho(x,y)=(x,y^{\prime})
,

(b)

we define
\rho_{x}
as the permutation of
N(\Gamma,x)
such that, given
y\in N(\Gamma,x)
,
\rho(x,y)=(x,\rho_{x}(y))
. Then the permutation
\rho_{x}
is a cycle of order
|N(\Gamma,x)|
.

If properties
(a)
and
(b)
hold, the map
\rho
is said to be a
rotation
of
\Gamma
.

Then, as reported in
[
18
]
, a combinatorial embedding
\Pi=(\Gamma,\rho)
is equivalent to a cellular embedding
\psi
of
\Gamma
into an orientable surface
\Sigma
(see also
[
1
]
, Theorem 3.1).

Example 2.2
.

Figure 1.
A biembedding of
K_{7}
into the torus. This picture is taken from
[
32
]
.

As explained in
[
29
]
, the rotation
\rho
associated to the cellular embedding of Figure
1
can be obtained through the following steps.

1)

Given
x\in\{1,2,3,4,5,6,7\}
, we define the map
\rho_{x}
by looking at the neighbourg of
x
in a clockwise order.
For example, given
x=3
we have that
\rho_{3}
is the cyclic permutation of
\{1,2,4,5,6,7\}
given by
(4,1,7,2,5,6)
.

2)

Due to property
(b)
of the definition of combinatorial embedding we have that
\rho((x,y))=(x,\rho_{x}(y))
.

Clearly, these steps are enough to determine the map
\rho
. However, in this case, we can also choose just one
x\in\{1,2,3,4,5,6,7\}
, define, as above, the map
\rho
on the oriented edges that start from
x
and then define the map
\rho
for the other ones as follows.

3)

We note that every oriented edge of
K_{7}
can be written in the form
(x+z,y+z)
where the sum is performed modulo
7
. Here we set
\rho((z+x,z+y))=(z+x,z+\rho_{x}(y))
.

If we do so, we can easily check that the expression we obtain for
\rho
is independent from the initial vertex
x
.

Now we show that the Archdeacon embedding can be defined also starting from quasi-Heffter arrays. We first introduce some notation.
The rows and the columns of an
m\times n
array
A
are denoted by
R_{1},\ldots,R_{m}
and by
C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}
, respectively. Also we denote by
\mathcal{E}(A)
,
\mathcal{E}(R_{i})
,
\mathcal{E}(C_{j})
the list of the elements of the filled cells of
A
, of the
i
-th row and of the
j
-th column, respectively. Given an
m\times n
p.f. array
A
, by
\omega_{R_{i}}
and
\omega_{C_{j}}
we denote an ordering of
\mathcal{E}(R_{i})
and
\mathcal{E}(C_{j})
, respectively and we define by
\omega_{r}=\omega_{R_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ\omega_{R_{m}}
the ordering for the rows and by
\omega_{c}=\omega_{C_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ\omega_{C_{n}}
the ordering for the columns.

Definition 2.3
.

Given a quasi-Heffter array
A
, the orderings
\omega_{r}
and
\omega_{c}
are said to be
compatible
if
\omega_{c}\circ\omega_{r}
is a cycle of order
|\mathcal{E}(A)|
.

Now we are ready to adapt the definition of the Archdeacon embedding, see
[
1
,
13
]
, to the case of quasi-Heffter arrays.

Definition 2.4

(Archdeacon embedding)
.

Let
A
be an
\mathrm{Q}\mathrm{H}_{t}(m,n;h,k)
that admits two compatible orderings
\omega_{r}
and
\omega_{c}
. We consider the permutation
\rho_{0}
on

\pm\mathcal{E}(A)=\mathbb{Z}_{2nk+t}\setminus\frac{2nk+t}{t}\mathbb{Z}_{2nk+t}
, where
\frac{2nk+t}{t}\mathbb{Z}_{2nk+t}

denotes the subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{2nk+t}
of order
t
, so defined

(2.1)

\displaystyle\rho_{0}(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\omega_{r}(a)\mbox{ if }a\in\mathcal{E}(A),\\
\omega_{c}(-a)\mbox{ if }a\in-\mathcal{E}(A).\\
\end{cases}

Now, we define a map
\rho
on the set of the oriented edges of this graph as follows

(2.2)

\displaystyle\rho((x,x+a))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x,x+\rho_{0}(a)).

Remark 2.5
.

Note that in Definitions
2.3
and
2.4
we can interchange the roles of
\omega_{r}
and
\omega_{c}
. This operation corresponds to transposing the array
A
and hence it does not change the class of maps here considered.

Reasoning as in
[
10
]
and in
[
13
]
(see also Proposition 5.6 of
[
27
]
), we can prove that, since the orderings
\omega_{r}
and
\omega_{c}
are compatible, the map
\rho
is a rotation of
K_{\frac{2nk+t}{t}\times t}
and we get the following Theorem.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
A
be a
\mathrm{Q}\mathrm{H}_{t}(m,n;h,k)
that admits two compatible orderings
\omega_{r}
and
\omega_{c}
. Then there exists a cellular biembedding
\psi
of
K_{\frac{2nk+t}{t}\times t}
, such that every edge is on a face whose boundary length is a multiple of
h
and on a face whose boundary length is a multiple of
k
.

Moreover, setting
v=2nk+t
,
\psi
is
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
-regular.

We are also interested in describing the faces (and the face lengths) induced by the Archdeacon embedding under the condition of Theorem
2.6
.

At this purpose, we take a p.f. array
A
that is a
\mathrm{Q}\mathrm{H}_{t}(m,n;h,k)
and that admits two compatible orderings
\omega_{r}
and
\omega_{c}
. We consider the oriented edge
(x,x+a)
with
a\in\mathcal{E}(A)
, and let
{C}
be the column of
A
containing
a
.
We denote by
\lambda_{c}
the minimum positive integer such that
\sum_{i=0}^{\lambda_{c}|\mathcal{E}({C})|-1}\omega_{c}^{i}(a)=0
in
\mathbb{Z}_{2nk+t}
.
Due to Theorem 6.4 of
[
10
]
(see also
[
27
]
)
(x,x+a)
belongs to the face
F_{1}
whose boundary is

(2.3)

\left(x,x+a,x+a+\omega_{c}(a),\ldots,x+\sum_{i=0}^{\lambda_{c}|\mathcal{E}({C})|-2}\omega_{c}^{i}(a)\right).

We also note that, denoted by
\Sigma C
the sum of the elements of the column
C
, then
\lambda_{c}
is the minimum positive integer such that
\lambda_{c}(\Sigma C)=0
in
\mathbb{Z}_{2nk+t}
and
F_{1}
has length
k\lambda_{c}
. Moreover, this face covers exactly
\lambda_{c}
edges of type
(y,y+a)
for some
y\in\mathbb{Z}_{2nk+t}
. Since the total number of such edges is
2nk+t
, the column
C
induces
(2nk+t)/\lambda_{c}
different faces.

Let us now consider the oriented edge
(x,x+a)
with
a\not\in\mathcal{E}(A)
and let
{R}
be the row containing
-a
.
In this case, we denote by
\lambda_{r}
the minimum positive integer such that
\sum_{i=0}^{\lambda_{r}|\mathcal{E}({R})|-1}\omega_{r}^{i}(-a)=0
in
\mathbb{Z}_{2nk+t}
and hence
a=\sum_{i=1}^{\lambda_{r}|\mathcal{E}({R})|-1}\omega_{r}^{i}(-a)
. Then
(x,x+a)
belongs to the face
F_{2}
whose boundary is

(2.4)

\left(x,x+\sum_{i=1}^{\lambda_{r}|\mathcal{E}({R})|-1}\omega_{r}^{-i}(-a),x+\sum_{i=1}^{\lambda_{r}|\mathcal{E}({R})|-2}\omega_{r}^{-i}(-a),\dots,x+\omega_{r}^{-1}(-a)\right).

Here we have that, denoted by
\Sigma R
the sum of the elements of the row
R
, then
\lambda_{r}
is the minimum positive integer such that
\lambda_{r}(\Sigma R)=0
in
\mathbb{Z}_{2nk+t}
and
F_{2}
has length
h\lambda_{r}
. Moreover, reasoning as for the columns, we have that the row
R
induces
(2nk+t)/\lambda_{r}
different faces.

To describe the spectrum of the face lengths, we need to introduce the following notation. Given a multiset
M
, we denote by
M^{\alpha}
the multiset in which each element of
M
appears
\alpha
times.
From the above discussion, it follows that (see also
[
27
]
).

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
A
be a
\mathrm{Q}\mathrm{H}_{t}(m,n;h,k)
that admits two compatible orderings
\omega_{r}
and
\omega_{c}
. Then, set
v=2nk+t
, there exists a
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
-regular, cellular biembedding
\psi
of
K_{\frac{v}{t}\times t}
whose face lengths define the following multiset

\bigcup_{C\mbox{ is a column of }A}\left[k\lambda_{c}\right]^{v/\lambda_{c}}\cup\bigcup_{R\mbox{ is a row of }A}\left[h\lambda_{r}\right]^{v/\lambda_{r}}.

3.
On the Automorphism group

This section aims to study the full automorphism group of an embedding of Archdeacon type.
We first recall that also the notions of embedding isomorphism and automorphism can be defined purely combinatorially as follows (see Korzhik and Voss
[
24
]
page 61).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\Pi:=(\Gamma,\rho)
and
\Pi^{\prime}:=(\Gamma^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
be two combinatorial embeddings of, respectively,
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
. We say that
\Pi
is
isomorphic
to
\Pi^{\prime}
if there exists a graph isomorphism
\sigma:\Gamma\rightarrow\Gamma^{\prime}
such that, for any
(x,y)\in D(\Gamma)
, we have either

(3.1)

\sigma\circ\rho(x,y)=\rho^{\prime}\circ\sigma(x,y)

or

(3.2)

\sigma\circ\rho(x,y)=(\rho^{\prime})^{-1}\circ\sigma(x,y).

We also say, with abuse of notation, that
\sigma
is an
embedding isomorphism
between
\Pi
and
\Pi^{\prime}
.
Moreover, if equation (
3.1
) holds,
\sigma
is said to be an
orientation preserving isomorphism
while,
if (
3.2
) holds,
\sigma
is said to be an
orientation reversing isomorphism
.

Example 3.2
.

We consider again the embedding
(K_{7},\rho)
represented by Figure
1
. Due to the step
(3)
of its definition (see Example
2.2
),
\rho
satisfies the following property

(3.3)

\rho((z+x,z+y))=(z+x,z+\rho_{x}(y)).

Denoted by
\tau_{z}:\mathbb{Z}_{7}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{7}
the map
\tau_{z}(x):=z+x
, property
3.3
can be written as

\tau_{z}\circ\rho(x,y)=\rho\circ\tau_{z}(x,y).

This means that, for any
z\in\mathbb{Z}_{7}
, the map
\tau_{z}
is an orientation preserving automorphism of
(K_{7},\rho)
.

Here, using the notations of
[
15
]
, given an embedding
\Pi
, we denote by
Aut(\Pi)
the group of all automorphisms of
\Pi
and by
Aut^{+}(\Pi)
the group of the orientation preserving automorphisms. Similarly, we denote by
Aut_{0}(\Pi)
the subgroup of
Aut(\Pi)
of the automorphisms that fix
0
and by
Aut_{0}^{+}(\Pi)
the group of the orientation preserving automorphisms that fix
0
. We remark that, since an orientable surface admits exactly two orientations,
Aut^{+}(\Pi)
(resp.
Aut_{0}^{+}(\Pi)
) is a normal subgroup of
Aut(\Pi)
(resp.
Aut_{0}(\Pi)
) whose index is either
1
or
2
.
Also, as done in Example
3.2
, we denote by
\tau_{g}
the translation by
g
, i.e. the map
V(\Gamma)=\mathbb{Z}_{v}\rightarrow V(\Gamma)=\mathbb{Z}_{v}
such that
\tau_{g}(x)=x+g
.
Then, when we consider a
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
-regular embedding
\Pi
of
\Gamma
, we identify the vertex set of
\Gamma
with
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
and we assume that the translation action is regular.
Applying this convention, we have that
\tau_{g}\in Aut(\Pi)
for any
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{v}
. Moreover, in the case of the Archdeacon embedding, recalling equation (
2.2
), the translations also belong to
Aut^{+}(\Pi)
.

Now we want to characterize some properties of the elements of
Aut_{0}
. First, we deal with the elements of
Aut_{0}^{+}
: the following proposition was already implicity proved in the proof of Proposition 3.3 of
[
15
]
but, for sake of completeness, we write also here its proof.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\Pi
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
-regular embedding of
K_{m\times t}
(where
m=v/t
) and let us assume that all the translations belong to
Aut^{+}(\Pi)
.
Then, given
\sigma\in Aut_{0}^{+}(\Pi)
, the following condition holds

\sigma|_{N(K_{m\times t},0)}=\rho_{0}^{\ell}\mbox{ for some }1\leq\ell\leq(m-1)t-1.

Moreover, given
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in Aut_{0}^{+}(\Pi)
such that
\sigma_{1}|_{N(K_{m\times t},0)}=\sigma_{2}|_{N(K_{m\times t},0)}
, then
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}
.

Proof.

Because of the definition,
\sigma\in Aut_{0}^{+}(\Pi)
implies that, for any
x\in N(K_{m\times t},0)

\sigma\circ\rho(0,x)=\rho\circ\sigma(0,x).

Recalling that
\rho(0,x)=(0,\rho_{0}(x))
for a suitable map
\rho_{0}:N(K_{m\times t},0)\rightarrow N(K_{m\times t},0)
, we have that

(3.4)

(0,\sigma\circ\rho_{0}(x))=\sigma\circ\rho(0,x)=\rho\circ\sigma(0,x)=(0,\rho_{0}\circ\sigma(x)).

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\Pi
and
\Pi^{\prime}
be two
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
-regular embeddings of
K_{m\times t}
that are isomorphic through the isomorphism
\sigma
. Given
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{v}
we set
\phi_{\sigma,g}:=\sigma\circ(\tau_{g})^{-1}\circ(\sigma^{-1})\circ\tau_{\sigma(g)}
.

Then
\phi_{\sigma,g}=id
if and only if the following diagram commutes
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{g}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{\sigma_{1}(g)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}

In particular, identified
V(K_{m\times t})
with
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
, we have that
\phi_{\sigma,g}=id
for any
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{v}
if and only if
\sigma
is a group automorphism of
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
.

Proof.

Because of the definition we have that
\phi_{\sigma,g}=\sigma\circ(\tau_{g})^{-1}\circ(\sigma^{-1})\circ\tau_{\sigma(g)}
. Therefore
\phi_{\sigma,g}=id
is equivalent to

\tau_{g}\circ\sigma^{-1}=\sigma^{-1}\circ\tau_{\sigma(g)},

and to

(5.1)

\sigma\circ\tau_{g}=\tau_{\sigma(g)}\circ\sigma.

i.e. the diagram commutes.

Equation
5.1
can be also written as

(5.2)

\sigma(x+g)=(\sigma(x)+\sigma(g)).

Note that, if equation
5.2
holds for any
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{v}
, it means that
\sigma
is a group automorphism of
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
.
∎

In order to state a refinement of Proposition
5.1
, we recall that the number of automorphisms of
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
is
\phi(v)
, where
\phi(\cdot)
is the Euler’s totient function.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{\Pi_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathcal{A}\}
be a family of
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
-regular distinct embeddings of
K_{m\times t}
where
v=mt
. Then, if
\Pi_{\alpha}
is isomorphic to
\Pi_{0}
for any
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
and if
Aut_{0}(\Pi_{0})=\{id\}
, we have that

|\mathcal{F}|\leq 2\phi(v).

Proof.

We can assume
\Pi_{0}\in\mathcal{F}
and let us denote by
\sigma_{\alpha}
an isomorphism between
\Pi_{\alpha}
and
\Pi_{0}
that fixes
0
. Note that this isomorphism exists since
\mathcal{F}
is a family of
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
-regular embeddings.

Now we prove that, for any
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
and any
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{v}
,
\phi_{\sigma_{\alpha},g}
is an element of
Aut_{0}(\Pi_{0})
. Since
\phi_{\sigma_{\alpha},g}
is a composition of isomorphisms, it is an embedding isomorphism. It is also easy to see that
\phi_{\sigma_{\alpha},g}:\Pi_{0}\rightarrow\Pi_{0}
and hence it is an element of
Aut(\Pi_{0})
. Because of definition, we have that

\phi_{\sigma_{\alpha},g}(0)=\sigma_{\alpha}\circ(\tau_{g})^{-1}\circ(\sigma_{\alpha}^{-1})\circ\tau_{\sigma_{\alpha}(g)}(0)=

\sigma_{\alpha}\circ(\tau_{g})^{-1}\circ(\sigma_{\alpha}^{-1})(\sigma_{\alpha}(g))=\sigma_{\alpha}\circ(\tau_{g})^{-1}(g)=\sigma_{\alpha}(0)=0.

This means that
\phi_{\sigma_{\alpha},g}
is an element of
Aut_{0}(\Pi_{0})
. On the other hand, we know that
Aut_{0}(\Pi_{0})=\{id\}
and hence
\phi_{\sigma_{\alpha},g}=id
for any
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{v}
. Due to Lemma
5.4
, this implies that
\sigma_{\alpha}
is a group automorphism of
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
.

Let now assume, by contradiction, that
|\mathcal{F}|>2\phi(v)
. Since the number of automorphisms of
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
is
\phi(v)
and each
\sigma_{\alpha}
is an automorphism of
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
, due to the pigeonhole principle, there exists
\Pi_{1},\Pi_{2}
and
\Pi_{3}
that are isomorphic to
\Pi_{0}
through the same map
\bar{\sigma}:\mathbb{Z}_{v}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{v}
. Hence we would have that the identity is an isomorphism both from
\Pi_{1}=(K_{m\times t},\rho_{1})
to
\Pi_{2}=(K_{m\times t},\rho_{2})
and from
\Pi_{1}=(K_{m\times t},\rho_{1})
to
\Pi_{3}=(K_{m\times t},\rho_{3})
. It follows from Definition
2.1
that
\rho_{2},\rho_{3}\in\{\rho_{1},\rho_{1}^{-1}\}
. But this means that either
\Pi_{1}=\Pi_{2}
or
\Pi_{1}=\Pi_{3}
or
\Pi_{2}=\Pi_{3}
. In each of these cases we would obtain that the elements of
\mathcal{F}
are not all distinct that contradicts the hypotheses.
∎

A radial scalar product for Kerr quasinormal modes
Lionel T. London

Abstract
A scalar product for quasinormal mode solutions to Teukolsky’s homogeneous radial equation is presented. Evaluation of this scalar product can be performed either by direct integration, or by evaluation of a confluent hypergeometric functions. For direct integration, it is explicitly shown that the quasinormal modes’ radial functions are regular on a family of physically bounded complex paths. The related scalar product will be useful for better understanding analytic solutions to Teukolsky’s radial equation, particularly the quasi-normal modes, their potential spatial completeness, and whether the quasi-normal mode overtone excitations may be estimated by spectral decomposition rather than fitting. With that motivation, the scalar product is applied to confluent Heun polynomials where it is used to derive their peculiar orthogonality and eigenvalue properties. A potentially new relationship is derived between the confluent Heun polynomials’ scalar products and eigenvalues. Using these results, it is shown for the first time that Teukolsky’s radial equation (and perhaps similar confluent Heun equations) are, in principle, exactly tri-diagonalizable. To this end, “ canonical ” confluent Heun polynomials are conjectured.

While

The second perspective is that

It is left as an exercise for the reader to show that one can actually enforce a strictly positive and real valued weight function,

While

Further, there do not appear to be significant gains in using

V
Evaluation of scalar products

The scalar product developed in the previous section has been copied below,

\displaystyle{\langle{\text{a}}\,|\,{\text{b}}\rangle}\;=\;\int_{0}^{1}\,\text{a}(\xi)\,\text{b}(\xi)\;\;\xi^{\text{B}_{0}}(1-\xi)^{\text{B}_{1}}e^{\frac{\text{B}_{2}}{1-\xi}}\;d\xi\;.

(33)

One of the simplest ways to evaluate Eq. (
33
) is direct integration (i.e. explicit numerical integration via discretization of the domain).
However, divergence of the weight function at spatial infinity or the BH horizon means that naive integration along real values of
\xi
is bound to fail.
So other means must be considered.

In Sec.
V.1
, we make use of coordinate dependence to develop a method to
directly integrate
Eq. (
33
).
In Sec.
V.2
, we note that Eq. (
33
) coincides with the integral representation of the Tricomi confluent hypergeometric function, also known as the Kummer function of the second kind
Arfken et al. 2013
;
Chen and Dai 2010
.
That fact, along with the well known
analytic continuation
of e.g. the Gamma function to complex inputs, is used to analytically evaluate Eq. (
33
).
This section concludes with a brief comparison of the pros and cons of direct integration and analytic continuation.

V.1
Direct integration

An extremely useful lesson from calculus is that, given an integral, we may change the coordinate representation of its integrand without changing the value of the integral.
So while the kernel of Eq. (
33
) is coordinate
dependent
, its value,
{\langle{\text{a}}\,|\,{\text{b}}\rangle}
, is coordinate
independent
.
This concept leads us to seek a new coordinate,
z
, such that the kernel of Eq. (
33
) does not diverge.
Henceforth, we will use
\xi(z)
to refer to a complex valued integration path, and
\xi
to refer to the quantity defined by Eq. (
10
).

The following definition of
\xi(z)
has the desired effect of augmenting singular behavior at the horizon and spatial infinity,

\displaystyle\xi(z)\;=\;\frac{{x_{2}}z^{{x_{0}}}}{({x_{2}}+1)z^{{x_{0}}}-1}\;.

(34)

In Eq. (
34
),
x_{0}
and
x_{2}
are coordinate parameters that modify
\text{B}_{0}
and
\text{B}_{2}
, respectively.
We will consider
\text{Re}(x0)>0
, such that
\xi(0)=0
.
The dependence of Eq. (
34

x_{2}
is motivated by the complex radial coordinate used in Ref.
Mano et al. 1996

It may also be developed by placing the Laguerre polynomial equation into a
\xi
-like compactified coordinate, and then studying that system’s weight function.
The dependence of Eq. (
34

x_{0}
is motivated by the observation that
z^{x_{0}}
is the simplest apparent coordinate modification that impacts singular behavior arising from
\text{B}_{0}
.

The effect of this coordinate choice is found by applying Eq. (
34
) to the scalar product’s integrand,

\displaystyle{\langle{\text{a}}\,|\,{\text{b}}\rangle}=\int_{0}^{1}\text{a}(\xi(z))\,\text{b}(\xi(z))\;\left[\text{W}(\xi(z))\;\partial_{z}\xi(z)\right]\;dz\;.

(35)

In Eq. (
35

d\xi=\partial_{z}\xi(z)\,dz
to write the integral explicitly in
z
.
According to Eq. (
34
),
\xi(z=0)=0
, and
\xi(z=1)=1
; thus, in Eq. (
35

Within Eq. (
34
), the quantity in square brackets is of particular practical use.
It is an
effective weight function
,

\displaystyle\text{W}_{\text{eff.}}(z)\;

\displaystyle=\;\text{W}(\xi(z))\;\partial_{z}\xi(z)

(36a)

\displaystyle=\;e^{\frac{\text{B}_{2}\text{x}_{2}}{z^{\text{x}_{0}}-1}}\;z^{\text{x}_{0}(\text{B}_{0}+1)-1}\;\left(1-z^{\text{x}_{0}}\right)^{\text{B}_{1}}\;

(36b)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\;\left(1-(\text{x}_{2}+1)z^{\text{x}_{0}}\right)^{-\text{B}_{0}-\text{B}_{1}-2}\;.

While Eq. (
34

\text{W}(\xi)

36

x_{0}
and
x_{2}
that prevent the effective weight function from diverging at the domain boundaries.
In essence, we have two independent conditions: (i)
\text{W}_{\text{eff.}}(0)=0
, and (ii)
\text{W}_{\text{eff.}}(1)=0
.
We also have two unknowns:
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
.
Thus,
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
may be determined (up to positive constants) by careful analysis of
\text{W}_{\text{eff.}}
.

This analysis is somewhat tedious, but tractable.
First, one should focus on the factor of

within
\text{W}_{\text{eff.}}

If
x_{0}
is chosen appropriately, then the real part of
\text{x}_{0}(\text{B}_{0}+1)-1

\text{K}_{0}
, thereby allowing
\text{W}_{\text{eff.}}
to go to zero as
z
goes to zero.
Since,
\text{B}_{0}
is complex valued,
x_{0}
must be also.
These ideas result in the following constraints,

\displaystyle\text{K}_{0}

\displaystyle=-1+\left[1+\text{Re}(\text{B}_{0})\right]\text{Re}(x_{0})-\text{Im}(\text{B}_{0})\text{Im}(x_{0}),

(37a)

\displaystyle\text{K}_{0}

\displaystyle>0\;.

(37b)

If we assume that
\text{Re}(x_{0})
only depends on
\text{Re}(\text{B}_{0})
, then then the result for
x_{0}
follows,

\displaystyle\text{x}_{0}\;

\displaystyle=\;\text{K}_{0}\,\frac{\text{sgn}(\mathrm{Re}(\text{B}_{0})+1)}{\mathrm{Re}(\text{B}_{0})+1}

(38)

\displaystyle\quad\;+\;i\,\frac{-1\,+\,\text{K}_{0}\,[\text{sgn}(\;\mathrm{Re}(\text{B}_{0})+1\;)-1]}{\mathrm{Im}(\text{B}_{0})}\;.

Note that, in Eq. (

\text{Re}(\text{x}_{0})>0

Given,
x_{0}
, one may then determine
x_{2}
.
Returning to
\text{W}_{\text{eff.}}

\text{W}_{\text{eff.}}
is assured to be regular in that limit.
To that end, one should consider
e^{\frac{\text{B}_{2}\text{x}_{2}}{z^{\text{x}_{0}}-1}}
, and seek

such that

\displaystyle\lim_{z\rightarrow 1}\frac{\text{B}_{2}\text{x}_{2}}{z^{\text{x}_{0}}-1}\;

\displaystyle\sim\;-\frac{\text{K}_{2}}{1-z}\;,

(39a)

\displaystyle\text{K}_{2}\;

\displaystyle>0\;.

(39b)

To evaluate the left-hand-side of Eq. (

Upgrading the asymptotic equivalence in Eq. (

\displaystyle\text{x}_{2}\;

\displaystyle=\;\text{K}_{2}\;\text{x}_{0}\,/\,\text{B}_{2}\;.

(40)

Note that, since

\displaystyle S(\text{K}_{0},\text{K}_{2},z)\;

\displaystyle=\;|1/2-\text{mean}_{z}(|\xi(z)|)|

The method described in the previous section uses coordinate freedom to extend the domain upon which Eq. (

;
Ronveaux and Arscott 1995

It happens that there is a more direct, and ultimately more robust, route to evaluating Eq. (

) with analytic continuation.

This alternative route begins with the observation that if

Abramowitz and Stegun 1964

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\,

\displaystyle\xi^{\text{B}_{0}}\,(1-\xi)^{\text{B}_{1}}\,e^{\frac{\text{B}_{2}}{1-\xi}}\;d\xi

(43)

\displaystyle=\;e^{\text{B}_{2}}\,\Gamma(\text{B}_{0}+1)\,U(1+\text{B}_{0},-\text{B}_{1},-\text{B}_{2})\;.

In Eq. (

),
\Gamma(x)
is the usual Euler-Bernoulli Gamma function, and

is the Tricomi confluent hypergeometric function,

\displaystyle U(\text{x},\text{y},\text{z})\;=\;

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(1-\text{y})}{\Gamma(\text{x}-\text{y}+1)}M(\text{x},\text{y},\text{z})

(44)

The Gamma functions is constructed to extend the factorial function to the complex numbers.
The confluent hypergeometric function is a product of Gamma functions and a power series in

This parade of special functions has the following use:
Every
\text{a}(\xi)
and
\text{b}(\xi)
of interest to Eq. (

\displaystyle\text{a}(\xi)\;=\;\sum_{j=0}\,\text{a}_{j}\,\xi^{j}\quad\text{ and }\quad\text{b}(\xi)\;=\;\sum_{k=0}\,\text{b}_{k}\,\xi^{k}\;.

\displaystyle{\langle{\text{a}}\,|\,{\text{b}}\rangle}\;=\;\sum_{j,k}\;\text{a}_{j}\,\text{b}_{k}\,\left<\xi^{j+k}\right>\;.

In Eq. (

The result is that the scalar product of any two smooth functions may be equated with a sum over confluent hypergeometric terms,

\displaystyle{\langle{\text{a}}\,|\,{\text{b}}\rangle}\;

\displaystyle=\;e^{\text{B}_{2}}\,\sum_{j,k}\;\text{a}_{j}\,\text{b}_{k}\,\Gamma(\text{B}_{0}+j+k+1)\,

(48)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\times\,U(1+\text{B}_{0}+j+k,-\text{B}_{1},-\text{B}_{2})\;.

\displaystyle\left<\xi^{p}\right>\;\leftarrow\;\left<\xi^{p}\right>/\left<\xi^{1}\right>\;.

VI
Limitations of direct integration and analytic continuation

Direct integration, analytic continuation, and their application to monomial moments and confluent Heun polynomials are all subject to various caveats and limitations.
Here, these facets are reviewed.
At times, technical details are omitted for brevity, but particular attention is given to practical workarounds.
While analytic continuation and direct integration are found to be complementary, analytic continuation is determined to be preferred.

VI.1
Direct Integration: disallowed BH spins, and the zero frequency limit

The effective weight function introduced in Sec.
V.1
was

\displaystyle\text{W}_{\text{eff.}}(z)\;

\displaystyle=\;e^{\frac{\text{B}_{2}\text{x}_{2}}{z^{\text{x}_{0}}-1}}\;z^{\text{x}_{0}(\text{B}_{0}+1)-1}\;\left(1-z^{\text{x}_{0}}\right)^{\text{B}_{1}}\;

(50)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\;\left(1-(\text{x}_{2}+1)z^{\text{x}_{0}}\right)^{-\text{B}_{0}-\text{B}_{1}-2}\;.

In Sec.
V.1
, a direct integration approach was introduced to evaluate scalar product using
\text{W}_{\text{eff.}}(z)
.
This approach has two limiting features: (

) for a given value of

, there are values of the BH spin,

In Sec.

VI.3
Direct Integration vs. Analytic continuation

In practice, analytic continuation has the following relative attributes that may make it preferable in most circumstances.
Since analytic continuation does not require the determination of optimal coordinate parameters (see Sec.

In addition, analytic continuation requires monomial moments to compute scalar products, ans do does not require the storage of arrays (discretizations of

Thus, while direct integration may serve as a valuable alternative, particularly in cases where the Gamma function cannot be evaluated, analytic continuation is expected to be structurally simpler and computationally more efficient.

All forthcoming numerical results will make use of analytic continuation with

package
mpmath development team 2023
.

Confluent Heun polynomials

The differential operator that results from applying QNM boundary conditions to Teukolsky’s radial equation was

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}{{}_{\xi}}\;

\displaystyle=\;(\text{C}_{0}+\text{C}_{1}(1-\xi))

VII.2.1
Construction

Let
\mathbf{y}_{pk}(\xi)

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{pk}(\xi)\;=\;\sum_{q=0}^{p}\,\text{a}_{pkq}\,\xi^{q}\;.

We will soon see that the label,

The polynomial coefficients,

One then asserts that the coefficients of each power in

To simplify our notation, we will henceforth drop two indices,

\displaystyle\text{a}_{q}\;\leftarrow\;\text{a}_{pkq}\;.

\displaystyle\text{a}_{q-1}\,\alpha_{q}\;+\;\text{a}_{q}\,\beta_{q}\;+\;\text{a}_{q+1}\,\gamma_{q}\;=\;\lambda_{pk}\,\text{a}_{q}\;,

\displaystyle\alpha_{q}\;

\displaystyle=\;-(q-1)(\text{C}_{4}+q-2)-\mu_{p}

(77a)

\displaystyle\beta_{q}\;

\displaystyle=\;q\,(\text{C}_{3}+2(\text{C}_{4}+q-1))

(77b)

\displaystyle\gamma_{q}\;

\displaystyle=\;-(q+1)(\text{C}_{2}+\text{C}_{3}+\text{C}_{4}+q)\;.

\displaystyle\text{a}_{0}\,\beta_{0}\;+\;\text{a}_{1}\,\gamma_{0}\;=\;\lambda_{pk}\,\text{a}_{0}\;,

VIII
Implications for analytic solutions to Teukolsky’s radial equation

We previously set out to apply two concepts to Teukolsky’s radial problem.
When stated in the context of the radial equation, these two concepts are:

(
i
)

That a scalar product may be developed from the structure of the radial equation (i.e.
(Q1)
).

(
ii
)

That special functions may be developed from the structure of the radial equation, and that these functions
may
be used to exactly represent analytic solutions (i.e.
(Q2)
).

Previous sections show that concept (
i
) is indeed applicable to the radial equation, thus affirmatively answering
(Q1)
.
A radial scalar product was developed in Sec.
IV
.
Methods for evaluation of the scalar product were developed in Sec.
V
.
Connections between the scalar product and concept (
ii
) were discussed in Sec.
VII
.
In particular, the scalar products connection with problem specific special functions (i.e. the confluent Heun polynomials) was detailed in Sec.
VII.2
.
Here, we take a first step towards applying confluent Heun polynomials to the representation of analytic solutions to Teukolsky’s radial equation for QNMs.
A non-classical feature of the confluent Heun polynomials means that, for now,
only a preliminary and conceptual step
is taken.

Recall that Teukolsky’s radial equation is a confluent Heun equation of the form,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}{{}_{\xi}}\;

\displaystyle=\;[\text{C}_{0}+\text{C}_{1}(1-\xi)]+\mathcal{D}_{\xi}\;.

(101)

In Eq. (
101
), the transformed radial operator,
\mathcal{L}{{}_{\xi}}
, is simply rewritten such the differential part is explicitly identified as
\mathcal{D}_{\xi}
(see Eq.
72
).
In Sec.
VII.2
, it was found that
\mathcal{D}_{\xi}
generates confluent Heun polynomials,
y_{pk}(\xi)
, such that each
y_{pk}(\xi)
satisfies a two parameter eigenvalue problem,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}{{}_{\xi}}\;|{{y}_{pk}}\rangle\;

\displaystyle=\;(\lambda_{pk}\;+\;\mu_{p}\,\xi)\;|{{y}_{pk}}\rangle\;,

(102)

where, for a given polynomial order
p
, there are
p+1
solutions,
y_{pk}
.
This situation differs starkly from that of classical (e.g. Jacobi) polynomials, for which every
single
polynomial order maps to a
single
polynomial solution.
In other words, for each polynomial order, classical polynomials have a
simplex
of solutions, while the confluent Heun polynomials have a
multiplex
.

The existence of a multiplex for each polynomial order prompts a nontrivial conceptual tension:
The Jacobi polynomials’ recursion relationships play a key role in the angular problem (see Appx.
B
); however, there cannot exist similar recursion relationships between the confluent Heun polynomials,
y_{pk}
.
This is because such recursion relationships require an orthogonal polynomial sequence, wherein each polynomial has a unique order (i.e. is a simplex)
Chihara 2011
.
Further, since each confluent Heun polynomial multiplex is composed of polynomials of like order, the collection of all such multiplexes would appear to contain
too many polynomials
, meaning that the they allow for multiple equivalent representations (i.e. decompositions) of radial functions.

This does not, however, preclude the possibility that there are special combinations of confluent Heun polynomials allow for unique representations, and classical recursion relationships.
While this possibility is perhaps too open ended to be fully pursued here, we may
assume
(somewhat adventurously) that such polynomials exist, and then contemplate implications for Teukolsky’s radial equation:

Suppose that solutions to the radial equation,
|{f}\rangle
, can be exactly represented in terms of orthonormal polynomials,
u_{n}
,

\displaystyle|{f}\rangle\;=\;\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}a_{p}\,|{u_{p}}\rangle\;,

(103a)

where it is additionally supposed that

\displaystyle|{u_{p}}\rangle\;

\displaystyle=\;\sum_{k=0}^{p}\,\text{c}_{pk}\,|{{y}_{pk}}\rangle\;,

(103b)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{I}}\;

\displaystyle=\;\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\,|{u_{p}}\rangle\langle{u_{p}}|\;,

(103c)

and

\displaystyle\xi|{u_{p}}\rangle=\sigma_{p0}|{u_{p-1}}\rangle+\sigma_{p1}|{u_{p}}\rangle+\sigma_{p2}|{u_{p+1}}\rangle\;.

(103d)

In Eqs. (
103a
-
103d
), despite not being classical,
u_{n}
are constructed to have the essential properties of classical polynomials, namely completeness, orthonormality and three-term recursion
Chihara 2011
;
Chen and Dai 2010
.
In this sense, we may think of
u_{n}
as having
canonical
properties.
This motivates their being referred to as
canonical confluent Heun polynomials
.

Further, in Eqs. (
103a
-
103d
),
a_{p}
,
c_{pk}
,
\sigma_{p0}
,
\sigma_{p1}
and
\sigma_{p2}
are assumed to be generally non-zero complex valued constants.
Henceforth,
\sigma_{pj}
will refer to complex valued constants that are assumed to be known if
u_{p}
are known.
In Eq. (
103c
),
{\mathbb{I}}
is the identity operator for the space of all confluent Heun functions of the fractional radial coordinate,
\xi
.
Equation (
103c
) requires that the expansion coefficients present in Eq. (
103a
) are
a_{p}={\langle{u_{p}}\,|\,{f}\rangle}
.
The three-term-recursion relation given by Eq. (
103d
) is a universal feature of all (simplexical) orthogonal polynomial sequences, and it is a direct consequence of Eq. (
103c
) (i.e. completeness) along with the fact that
{\langle{u_{j}}\,|\,{\xi|u_{k}}\rangle}={\langle{\xi u_{j}}\,|\,{u_{k}}\rangle}={\langle{u_{j}}\,|\,{\xi u_{k}}\rangle}

Chihara 2011
.

Naively, if each order
p
polynomial multiplex is a
p+1
dimensional orthonormal basis, then it should be capable of exactly representing any order
p
polynomial on the same domain
Axler 2015
.
Thus it may be possible to construct
u_{p}
such that Eqs. (
103b
-
103d
) hold.

In effect, Eq. (
103
) fully defines a solution ansatz.
This solution ansatz may be used to investigate whether the scalar product and confluent Heun polynomials imply that Teukolsky’s radial equation is, like the angular equation, exactly tridiagonalizable.
To this end, we may attempt to represent the radial equation in the basis defined by
\{u_{p}\}_{p=0}^{\infty}
.

In bra-ket notation, the transformed radial equation is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}{{}_{\xi}}\,|{f}\rangle\;

\displaystyle=\;A\,|{f}\rangle\;.

(104)

Applying Eq. (
103c
) such that
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}
is represented as a matrix,
\hat{L}
, and
|{f}\rangle
a vector,
\vec{f}
, gives

\displaystyle\sum_{p,p^{\prime}}\,|{u_{p^{\prime}}}\rangle

\displaystyle\langle{u_{p^{\prime}}}|\,\mathcal{L}{{}_{\xi}}\,|{u_{p}}\rangle{\langle{u_{p}}\,|\,{f}\rangle}\;\;

(105a)

\displaystyle=\;A\,\sum_{p}\,|{u_{p}}\rangle{\langle{u_{p}}\,|\,{f}\rangle}\;,

\displaystyle\hat{L}\,\vec{f}\;

\displaystyle=\;A\,\vec{f}\;.

(105b)

In Eq. (
105b
), the elements of
\hat{L}
are
\langle{u_{p^{\prime}}}|\,\mathcal{L}{{}_{\xi}}\,|{u_{p}}\rangle
, and the elements of
\vec{f}
are
{\langle{u_{p}}\,|\,{f}\rangle}
.
Henceforth,

will label rows, and

To compute the matrix elements,
\langle{u_{p^{\prime}}}|\,\mathcal{L}{{}_{\xi}}\,|{u_{p}}\rangle

Rewriting

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}{{}_{\xi}}\,|{u_{p}}\rangle\;=\;(\text{C}_{0}+\text{C}_{1}[1-\xi])\,|{u_{p}}\rangle+\mathcal{D}_{\xi}\,|{u_{p}}\rangle\;.

(106)

In Eq. (
106

\mathcal{D}_{\xi}\,|{u_{p}}\rangle
can be rewritten using the definition of
u_{p}
in terms of confluent Heun polynomials (Eq.
103b
), and the two parameter eigenvalue relationship (Eq.
102
).
Doing so yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\xi}\,|{u_{p}}\rangle\;

\displaystyle=\;\sum_{k=0}^{p}\,\text{c}_{pk}\,\mathcal{D}_{\xi}\,|{{y}_{pk}}\rangle

(107a)

\displaystyle=\;\sum_{k=0}^{p}\,\text{c}_{pk}\,(\lambda_{pk}\,+\,\mu_{p}\,\xi)\,|{{y}_{pk}}\rangle\;,

(107b)

\displaystyle=\;|{v_{p}}\rangle\,+\,\mu_{p}\,\xi\,|{u_{p}}\rangle\;.

(107c)

In going from Eq. (
107b
) to Eq. (
107c
), the term dependent on the confluent Heun eigenvalues,
\lambda_{pk}
, has been used to define a new polynomial,

\displaystyle|{v_{p}}\rangle\;=\;\sum_{k=0}^{p}\,\text{c}_{pk}\,\lambda_{pk}\,|{{y}_{pk}}\rangle\;,

(108)

and it has been noticed that the remaining term in Eq. (
107b
) is simply proportional to
|{u_{n}}\rangle
.

It follows from Eq. (
107c
) that
\mathcal{L}{{}_{\xi}}\,|{u_{p}}\rangle
may be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}{{}_{\xi}}|{u_{p}}\rangle=(\text{C}_{0}+\text{C}_{1})|{u_{p}}\rangle+|{v_{p}}\rangle+(\mu_{p}-\text{C}_{1})\xi|{u_{p}}\rangle\;,

(109)

and that the matrix element,
\langle{u_{p^{\prime}}}|\mathcal{L}{{}_{\xi}}|{u_{p}}\rangle
, is

\displaystyle\langle{u_{p^{\prime}}}|\mathcal{L}{{}_{\xi}}|{u_{p}}\rangle\;=\;

\displaystyle(\text{C}_{0}+\text{C}_{1}){\langle{u_{p^{\prime}}}\,|\,{u_{p}}\rangle}

(110a)

\displaystyle+(\mu_{p}-\text{C}_{1})\langle{u_{p^{\prime}}}|\xi|{u_{p}}\rangle

(110b)

\displaystyle+{\langle{u_{p^{\prime}}}\,|\,{v_{p}}\rangle}\;.

(110c)

Since
|{u_{p}}\rangle
are orthonormal (i.e.
{\langle{u_{p^{\prime}}}\,|\,{u_{p}}\rangle}=\delta_{p^{\prime}p}
), Eq. (
110a
) only contributes to diagonal matrix elements.
Since
|{u_{p}}\rangle
possess a three term recursion relation given by Eq. (
103d
), Eq. (
110b
) will only contribute when
|p^{\prime}-p|\leq 1
.

It happens that
{\langle{u_{p^{\prime}}}\,|\,{v_{p}}\rangle}
is not only non-zero when
|p^{\prime}-p|\leq 1
, but also restricted to the upper-triangle (i.e.
p\geq p^{\prime}
).
This follows from the fact that
\mathcal{D}_{\xi}
is self-adjoint w.r.t. the scalar product, meaning that

\displaystyle{\langle{u_{p^{\prime}}}\,|\,{\mathcal{D}_{\xi}\,u_{p}}\rangle}\;=\;{\langle{\mathcal{D}_{\xi}\,u_{p^{\prime}}}\,|\,{u_{p}}\rangle}\;.

(111)

Using Eq. (
107c
) to expand both sides of Eq. (
111
) yields,

\displaystyle{\langle{u_{p^{\prime}}}\,|\,{v_{p}}\rangle}-{\langle{v_{p^{\prime}}}\,|\,{u_{p}}\rangle}\;=\;(\mu_{p}-\mu_{p^{\prime}}){\langle{u_{p^{\prime}}}\,|\,{\xi|u_{p}}\rangle}\;.

(112)

Equation (
112
) may be understood thusly:
Since
u_{p^{\prime}}
is a member of a complete orthonormal sequence, its scalar product with any order
p
polynomial will be non-zero if
p\geq p^{\prime}
and zero otherwise
Chihara 2011
.
In other words, any order
p
polynomial may be expressed as a linear combination of
u_{p^{\prime}}
, where
p^{\prime}\leq p
is required since the order of interest is
p
.
Thus
{\langle{u_{p^{\prime}}}\,|\,{v_{p}}\rangle}
and
{\langle{v_{p^{\prime}}}\,|\,{u_{p}}\rangle}

only
have non-zero values in the respective upper (
p\geq p^{\prime}
) and lower (
p\leq p^{\prime}
) triangle of
\hat{L}
.
Further,
\xi\,u_{p}
is a linear combination of
\{u_{p-1},u_{p},u_{p+1}\}
due to Eq. (
103d
), so the matrix whose elements are
{\langle{u_{p^{\prime}}}\,|\,{\xi|u_{p}}\rangle}
will be tridiagonal.
Together, these points mean that the only way that Eq. (
112
) can hold, i.e. the only way that the upper and lower triangular structure of
{\langle{u_{p^{\prime}}}\,|\,{v_{p}}\rangle}
and
{\langle{v_{p^{\prime}}}\,|\,{u_{p}}\rangle}
can be equal to the tridiagonal structure of
{\langle{u_{p^{\prime}}}\,|\,{\xi|u_{p}}\rangle}
, is if
\{{\langle{u_{p^{\prime}}}\,|\,{v_{p}}\rangle}\}_{p^{\prime}p}
is upper-triangular
and
tridiagonal.
This means that
|{v_{p}}\rangle
must be a linear combination of
u_{p-1}
and
u_{p}
,

\displaystyle|{v_{p}}\rangle=\sigma_{p3}|{u_{p-1}}\rangle+\sigma_{p4}|{u_{p}}\rangle\;,

(113)

where
\sigma_{p3}={\langle{u_{p-1}}\,|\,{v_{p}}\rangle}
and
\sigma_{p4}={\langle{u_{p}}\,|\,{v_{p}}\rangle}
are complex valued constants which are determined by
c_{pk}
(i.e.
u_{p}
) and the confluent Heun eigenvalues
\lambda_{pk}
.

Together, Eq. (
103d
), Eq. (
110
) and Eq. (
113
) allow
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}|{u_{p}}\rangle
to be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\xi}|{u_{p}}\rangle\;=\;\sigma_{p5}\,|{u_{p-1}}\rangle+\sigma_{p6}\,|{u_{p}}\rangle+\sigma_{p7}\,|{u_{p+1}}\rangle\;,

(114)

where

\displaystyle\sigma_{p5}\;

\displaystyle=\;(\mu_{p}-\text{C}_{1})\sigma_{p0}+\sigma_{p3}\;,

(115a)

\displaystyle\sigma_{p6}\;

\displaystyle=\;(\mu_{p}-\text{C}_{1})\sigma_{p1}+\sigma_{p4}+(\text{C}_{0}+\text{C}_{1})\;,

(115b)

\displaystyle\sigma_{p7}\;

\displaystyle=\;(\mu_{p}-\text{C}_{1})\sigma_{p2}\;.

(115c)

The implication of Eqs. (
114
-
115
) is that, if the canonical polynomials
u_{n}
can be determined, then Teukolsky’s radial operator is exactly tridiagonalizable.

IX
Discussion

The primary results of this article are
(
i
) the introduction of a radial scalar product for QNMs (Sec.
IV
),
(
ii
) the development of QNM specific confluent Heun polynomials that may be defined for arbitrary frequency parameter (Sec.
VII.2
), and
(
iii
) the demonstration that, in principle, the scalar product and proposed canonical confluent Heun polynomials enable the exact tridiagonalization of Teukolsky’s radial equation for QNMs (Sec.
VIII
).

It is also of note that the scalar product may be evaluated by direct integration with a complex valued radius, or by more standard analytic extension using confluent hypergeometric functions (See Sec.
VII.1
).
While analytic extension was found to be the more practical route, the ability to construct a complex radial coordinate,
\xi(z)
, for evaluation of the scalar product also implies (via Eq. (
30
)) that
the QNMs themselves are spatially bounded on
\xi(z)
.
Since
\xi(z)
must be constructed to coincide with Sturm-Liouville boundary conditions, a QNM on
\xi(z)
would have physically relevant values at both the event horizon and spatial infinity.

Regarding the canonical polynomials, a clear next step is to investigate their computation.
Like the Gram-Schmidt polynomials, which are unique once certain processing choices are made, one might expect that there are multiple constructions of the canonical polynomials.
This a topic of ongoing research that is near conclusion
London and Gurevich 2023a
.

Similarly, the tridiagonalization of Teukolsky’s radial equation directly implies that its solutions (for fixed frequency parameter) are orthogonal.
This would be directly analogous to the orthogonality of the spheroidal harmonics at fixed frequency (i.e. fixed “oblateness”)
London 2023
.
It would also be compatible with the conclusions of complementary studies of linear perturbations of Kerr that have encountered a kind of QNM orthogonality
Green et al. 2023
.

An adjacent implication is that the radial functions are complete (i.e. isomorphic to canonical confluent Heun polynomials).
If the radial functions are both orthogonal and complete for given value of the frequency parameter
\tilde{\omega}
, then this would strongly imply the existence of a biorthogonal dual for the radial solutions determined by the QNM frequencies.
Again, this would be directly analogous to the QNM’s spheroidal harmonics, where each spheroidal harmonic requires input from a different QNM frequency
London 2023
.
A consequence would be that the QNMs are “
quasi-complete
”, i.e. complete in the radial and angular domains, but not complete (specifically under- or over-complete) in the time domain.
Investigation of these implications is also a topic of ongoing work that is near conclusion
London and Gurevich 2023b
.

Lastly, these ideas imply a potentially useful direction of research for e.g. BBH post-merger signals: rather than attempting to fit various QNMs’ within numerical data, it may be possible to
directly project
out individual QNMs’ time series using orthogonality.
In the context of ongoing efforts in Numerical Relativity and signal modeling for future gravitational wave detectors, this may be a fruitful direction for future research.

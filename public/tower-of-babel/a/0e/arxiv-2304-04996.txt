Aging during Phase Separation in Long-Range Ising Model
Soumik Ghosh, Subir K. Das

Abstract
The kinetics of domain growth and aging in conserved order parameter systems, in the presence of short-range interaction, is widely studied. Due to technical difficulties and lack of resources, regarding computation, the dynamics is still not well established in the cases where long-range interactions are involved. Here we present related results from the Monte Carlo simulations of the two-dimensional long-range Ising model (LRIM). Random initial configurations, for 50 : 50 50:50 compositions of up and down spins, mimicking high temperature equilibrium states, have been quenched to temperatures inside the coexistence curve. Our analysis of the simulation data, for such a protocol, shows interesting dependence of the aging exponent, λ \lambda , on σ \sigma , the parameter, within the Hamiltonian, that controls the range of interaction. To complement these results, we also discuss simulation outcomes for the growth exponent. The obtained values of λ \lambda are compared with a well-known result for the lower bounds. For this purpose we have extracted interesting properties of the evolving structure.

Here

The Ising model Hamiltonian can be written in the general form

;

For nonconserved order-parameter dynamics, that is relevant for ordering, say, in magnetic systems, it is predicted

\alpha=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{1+\sigma},&\mbox{for}\ \sigma<1\\
\frac{1}{2},&\mbox{when}\ \sigma>1.\par\end{cases}

(6)

Similar predictions exist for kinetics of phase separation, say, in a binary (A + B) mixture, with conserved order-parameter, as well

;

\alpha=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2+\sigma},&\mbox{for}\ \sigma<1\\
\frac{1}{3},&\mbox{when}\ \sigma>1.\par\end{cases}

(7)

There exist certain logarithmic behaviour

For the long-range interaction, there has been a surge of interest in recent times

II
Model and Method

We have considered spins on periodic square lattices of size
L\times L
. Unless mentioned otherwise, the results are presented for
\L=256
, the unit being the lattice constant. Conserved order-parameter dynamics requires that the total number of

(or particles of type

-1
(or particles of type
B
) spins remain constant throughout the evolution of the system. To ensure this, Kawasaki spin-exchange dynamics is used in our Monte Carlo simulations

Landau2005
. For this purpose, energy change is needed to be calculated. With the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
4
), and the coupling term mentioned in Eq. (
5
), this calculation is computationally demanding

. To minimize the cost of computation, a generalized
Horita2017
Ewald summation

;
Horita2017
technique is used. We have carried out the calculations with our (in-house)
parallel
codes, written with OpenMP and MPI, for even faster computation. Time in our simulations is measured in units of Monte Carlo steps (MCS), one MCS being equivalent to
L^{2}
trials. For each
\sigma
value, random initial configurations, with equal concentrations of up and down spins, are quenched to a temperature that is
0.6
times the corresponding critical temperature
Horita2017
. At a finite temperature there exists noise in the structure. This was removed, via a majority spin rule
Nalina2019
, for the calculation of lengths. The latter quantity was obtained from the domain-size distribution function. All results are presented after averaging over
80
initial configurations. For each value of
\sigma
, total run length was
t=8\times 10^{4}
MCS.

IV
Summary

In conclusion, we have studied the kinetics of phase separation
Bray2002
in the long-range Ising model
Bray1993
. We have presented results on aging phenomena obtained via Monte Carlo simulations
Landau2005
in space dimension
2
, for several values of the interaction range parameter
\sigma

\sigma
, the aging exponent
\lambda

We have compared the values of
\lambda
with the lower bounds predicted in Ref.
Yeung1996

\lambda
quite accurately. In a previous study

\lambda
for the nearest-neighbour Ising model in

was estimated to be approximately
3.6
. For rather high values of
\sigma
, we have checked that our estimates are consistent with this. However, we are uncertain whether such a crossover occurs at
\sigma=1
, which is the boundary for the crossover of the growth exponent, between short-range and long-range classes.

We have also discussed results on domain growth. Our results on this quantity are consistent with the theoretical predictions
Fisher1972
and very recently published simulation reports by Müller et al.
Muller2022
.

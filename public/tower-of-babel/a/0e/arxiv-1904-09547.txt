measure-theoretic equicontinuity and rigidity
Fangzhou Cai

Abstract
. Let ( X , T ) (X,T) be a topological dynamical system and μ \mu be a invariant measure, we show that ( X , ℬ , μ , T ) (X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T) is rigid if and only if there exists some subsequence A A of ℕ \mathbb{N} such that ( X , T ) (X,T) is μ \mu - A A -equicontinuous if and only if there exists some IP-set A A such that ( X , T ) (X,T) is μ \mu - A A -equicontinuous. We show that if there exists some subsequence A A of ℕ \mathbb{N} with positive upper density such that ( X , T ) (X,T) is μ \mu - A A -mean-equicontinuous, then ( X , ℬ , μ , T ) (X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T) is rigid. We also give results with respect to a function.



1.
introduction

Throughout this paper, a topological dynamical system(t.d.s. for short) is a pair
(X,T)
, where
X
is an non-empty compact metric space with a metric
d
and
T
is a homeomorphism from
X
to itself. For a t.d.s.
(X,T)
, denote by
M(X,T)
the set of
T
-invariant regular Borel probability measures on
X
. It is well known that there exists some
\mu\in M(X,T)
, thus
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
can be viewed as a measure preserving system(m.p.s. for short), where
\mathcal{B}
is the Borel
\sigma
-algebra.

A t.d.s.
(X,T)
is called
equicontinuous
if
\{T^{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is uniformly equicontinuous. For a t.d.s., equicontinuity represents predictability, one may see that the dynamical behaviour of such a system is very “rigid”. For example, it is well known that a transitive t.d.s.
(X,T)
is equicontinuous if and only if it is topologically conjugate to a minimal rotation on a compact abelian metric group, if and only if it has topological discrete spectrum(see
[
18
]
).

The analogous concept of equicontinuity for a m.p.s. was also introduced. While studying cellular automata(a subclass of t.d.s.), Gilman
[
7
,
8
]
introduced a notion of
\mu
-equicontinuity. Later Huang
et\ al

[
11
]
introduced a different definition of
\mu
-equicontinuity(which under some conditions are equivalent
[
4
]
) and showed that
\mu
-equicontinuous systems have discrete spectrum. García-Ramos
[
5
]
introduced a weakening of
\mu
-equicontinuity called
\mu
-mean-equicontinuity and showed that if a m.p.s.
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
is an ergodic system, then it has discrete spectrum if and only if it is
\mu
-mean-equicontinuous. Recently, Huang
et\ al

[
10
]
proved that for a general m.p.s.
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
, it has discrete spectrum if and only if it is
\mu
-mean-equicontinuous.

The notion of rigidity in ergodic theory was first
introduced by Furstenberg and Weiss in
[
3
]
. A m.p.s.
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
is called
rigid
if there exists a subsequence
\{t_{k}\}
of
\mathbb{N}
such that
T^{t_{k}}f\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L^{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}f
for all
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
. So rigidity is a spectral property. A function
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
is called
rigid
if there exists a subsequence
\{t_{k}\}
of
\mathbb{N}
such that
T^{t_{k}}f\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L^{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}f
. Note that the sequence
\{t_{k}\}
may depend on
f
. It is clear that if
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
is rigid, then every function in
L^{2}(\mu)
is rigid. An interesting result showed that the converse is true (see
[
2
, Corollary 2.6]
).

The notion of uniformly rigidity was introduced by
Glasner and Maon in
[
9
]
as a topological analogue of
rigidity in ergodic theory. A t.d.s.
(X,T)
is
uniformly rigid
if there exists a subsequence
\{t_{k}\}
of
\mathbb{N}
such that
T^{t_{k}}\rightarrow id
uniformly on
X
, where
id
is the identity mapping.

The concept of rigidity is close to equicontinuity.
In
[
13
]
, the authors proved the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

[
13
, Lemma 4.1]

Let
(X,T)
be a t.d.s.. If
(X,T)
is uniformly rigid, then there exists an IP-set
A
such that
(X,T)
is
A
-equicontinuous. If in addition
(X,T)
is an E-system, the converse holds.

In fact, in this paper we prove that:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(X,T)
be a t.d.s.. Then
(X,T)
is uniformly rigid if and only if there exists an IP-set
A
such that
(X,T)
is
A
-equicontinuous, if and only if there exists a subsequence
A
of
\mathbb{N}
such that
(X,T)
is
A
-equicontinuous.

It is natural to consider the corresponding relation between measure-theoretic rigidity and measure-theoretic equicontinuity. Following this idea, in our paper we prove that:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(X,T)
be a t.d.s. and
\mu\in M(X,T)
. Then
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
is rigid if and only if there exists some subsequence
A
of
\mathbb{N}
such that
(X,T)
is
\mu
-
A
-equicontinuous if and only if there exists some IP-set
A
such that
(X,T)
is
\mu
-
A
-equicontinuous.

Inspired by the work in
[
10
]
, we also show that:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(X,T)
be a t.d.s. and
\mu\in M(X,T)
. If there exists a subsequence
A
of
\mathbb{N}
with
\bar{D}(A)>0
such that
(X,T)
is
\mu
-
A
-mean-equicontinuous, then
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
is rigid.

Since rigidity implies zero entropy, we have following corollary:

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
(X,T)
be a t.d.s.. If there exists a subsequence
A
of
\mathbb{N}
with
\bar{D}(A)>0
such that
(X,T)
is
A
-mean-equicontinuous, then the topological entropy of
(X,T)
is zero.

2.
preliminaries

In this section we recall some notions and aspects of dynamical systems.

2.1.
Subsets of non-negative integers

In the article, integers and natural numbers are denoted by

\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{N}
respectively.
Let
F
be a set, denote by
|F|
the number of elements of
F
. Let
A
be a subset of
\mathbb{N}
. Define the
lower density

\underline{D}(A)
and the
upper density

\bar{D}(A)
of
A
by:

\underline{D}(A)=\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|A\cap[0,n-1]|}{n},

\bar{D}(A)=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|A\cap[0,n-1]|}{n}.

If
\underline{D}(A)=\bar{D}(A)
, denote by
D(A)=\underline{D}(A)=\bar{D}(A)
the
density
of
A
.

Let
\{b_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset\mathbb{N}
. Define

FS\{b_{i}\}=\{b_{i_{1}}+b_{i_{2}}+\cdots+b_{i_{k}}:i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}\}.

We say
F\subset\mathbb{N}
is an
IP
-set
if there exists a subset
\{b_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
of
\mathbb{N}
such that
F=FS\{b_{i}\}
.

2.2.
Discrete spectrum in measurable dynamics

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
be a m.p.s., one can define the m.p.s.
(X\times X,\mathcal{B}\times\mathcal{B},\mu\times\mu,T)
by
T(x,y)=(Tx,Ty)
for
(x,y)\in X\times X
. One can define the Koopman operator
U_{T}
as an isometry of
L^{p}(\mu)
into
L^{p}(\mu)
by

U_{T}f=f\circ T
, where
1\leq p<\infty
. We say a function
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
is
almost periodic
if
\{U_{T}^{n}f:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is pre-compact in
L^{2}(\mu)
, that is the closure of
\{U_{T}^{n}f:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is compact in
L^{2}(\mu)
. We say that
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
has
discrete spectrum
if for all
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
,
f
is almost periodic.

2.3.
Measure-theoretic equicontinuity

Let
(X,T)
be a t.d.s. and
\mu\in M(X,T)
, let
K
be a subset of
X
and
A=\{a_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a subsequence of
\mathbb{N}
.

We say that
K
is
A
-equicontinuous
if for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

x,y\in K,d(x,y)<\delta\Rightarrow d(T^{a_{n}}x,T^{a_{n}}y)<\epsilon,\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

We say
(X,T)
is
A
-equicontinuous
if
X
is
A
-equicontinuous.

We say
(X,T)
is
\mu
-
A
-equicontinuous
if for any
\tau>0
, there exists a compact subset
K
of
X
with
\mu(K)>1-\tau
such that
K
is
A
-equicontinuous.

By the regularity of
\mu
, the compactness of
K
in the definition above can be ignored. It is clear that the definition of
\mu
-
A
-equicontinuity is independent on the choice of metric
d
.

2.4.
Sequence entropy for a measure

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
be a m.p.s. and
S=\{s_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a subsequence of
\mathbb{N}
. Suppose
\xi
and
\eta
are two finite measurable partitions of
X
. Define
\xi\vee\eta=\{A\cap B:A\in\xi,B\in\eta\}
. It is easy to see
\xi\vee\eta
is also a finite partition of
X
. The
entropy of
\xi
, written
H_{\mu}(\xi)
, is defined by

H_{\mu}(\xi)=-\sum_{A\in\xi}\mu(A)\log\mu(A),

and the
entropy of
\xi
given
\eta
, written
H_{\mu}(\xi|\eta)
, is defined by

H_{\mu}(\xi|\eta)=H_{\mu}(\xi\vee\eta)-H_{\mu}(\eta)=-\sum_{A\in\xi}\sum_{B\in\eta}\mu(A\cap B)\log\frac{\mu(A\cap B)}{\mu(B)}.

The
sequence entropy of
\xi
with respect to
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
along
S
is defined by

h_{\mu}^{S}(T,\xi)=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{\mu}(\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}T^{-s_{i}}\xi).

And the
sequence entropy of
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
along
S
is

h_{\mu}^{S}(T)=\sup_{\alpha}h_{\mu}^{S}(T,\alpha),

where supremum is taken over all finite measurable partitions. When
S=\mathbb{N}
, we simply write
h_{\mu}(T)
and it is called the
entropy of
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
.

3.
measure-theoretic equicontinuity and rigidity

In this section, we study the relation between measure-theoretic equicontinuity and rigidity. We prove that
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
is rigid if and only if there exists some subsequence
A
of
\mathbb{N}
such that
(X,T)
is
\mu
-
A
-equicontinuous. In the rest we give an IP-version of our result.

3.1.
Measure-theoretic equicontinuity, rigidity and sequence entropy

In this subsection, we prove that
\mu
-
A
-equicontinuity implies rigidity via sequence entropy.

First we give a characterization of measure-theoretic rigidity. It is worth pointing out the following theorem:

Theorem 3.1
.

[
12
, Theorem 3.10]

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
be a m.p.s.. Then
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
is rigid if and only if there exists some IP-set
F
such that
h_{\mu}^{A}(T)=0
for any subsequence
A
of
F
.

Lemma 3.2
.

[
12
, Lemma 3.3]

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
be a m.p.s. and
B\in\mathcal{B}
. Let
A
be a subsequence of
\mathbb{N}
. Then
{\{U_{T}^{n}1_{B}:n\in A\}}
is pre-compact in
L^{2}(\mu)
if and only if

h_{\mu}^{S}(T,\{B,B^{c}\})=0
for any subsequence
S
of
A
.

Now we can give a characterization of rigidity:

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
be a m.p.s.. Then
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
is rigid if and only if there exists some subsequence
A
of
\mathbb{N}
such that
h_{\mu}^{S}(T)=0
for any subsequence
S
of
A
, if and only if
{\{U_{T}^{n}f:n\in A\}}
is pre-compact in
L^{2}(\mu)
for all
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
.

Proof.

We only need to prove that: If there exists some subsequence
A
of
\mathbb{N}
such that
h_{\mu}^{S}(T)=0
for any subsequence
S
of
A
, then
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
is rigid.
By Lemma
3.2
, we have that for any
B\in\mathcal{B}
,
\overline{\{U_{T}^{n}1_{B}:n\in A\}}
is compact in
L^{2}(\mu)
. Let
\{f_{j}:j\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a dense subset of
L^{2}(\mu)
, then for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
\overline{\{U_{T}^{n}f_{j}:n\in A\}}
is compact in
L^{2}(\mu)
. Hence

\prod_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{\{U_{T}^{n}f_{j}:n\in A\}}\text{ is compact in }(L^{2}(\mu))^{\mathbb{N}}.

It follows that

\{(U_{T}^{n}f_{1},\cdots,U_{T}^{n}f_{j},\cdots):n\in A\}\subset\prod_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{\{U_{T}^{n}f_{j}:n\in A\}}

is totally bounded.
For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist
n_{1},\ldots,n_{N}\in A
such that

\{(U_{T}^{n}f_{1},\cdots,U_{T}^{n}f_{j},\cdots):n\in A\}\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}B((U_{T}^{n_{i}}f_{1},\cdots,U_{T}^{n_{i}}f_{j},\cdots),\frac{1}{k}).

Without loss of generality, we assume
B((U_{T}^{n_{1}}f_{1},\cdots,U_{T}^{n_{1}}f_{j},\cdots),\frac{1}{k})
contains infinite many elements of the left set. Hence there are infinite
n\in A
such that

\begin{split}d((U_{T}^{n}f_{1},\cdots,U_{T}^{n}f_{j},\cdots),(U_{T}^{n_{1}}f_{1},\cdots,U_{T}^{n_{1}}f_{j},\cdots))&=\\
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\|U_{T}^{n}f_{j}-U_{T}^{n_{1}}f_{j}\|_{L^{2}}}{2^{j}}&=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\|U_{T}^{n-n_{1}}f_{j}-f_{j}\|_{L^{2}}}{2^{j}}<\frac{1}{k}.\end{split}

Take
t_{k}=n-n_{1}
, since
n
can be sufficiently large, we can choose
\{t_{k}\}
increasing. It follows that

\|U_{T}^{t_{k}}f_{j}-f_{j}\|_{L^{2}}<\frac{2^{j}}{k}
for all
j,k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Hence

U_{T}^{t_{k}}f_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L^{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}f_{j}\text{ as }k\rightarrow\infty\text{ for each }f_{j}.

It follows that

U_{T}^{t_{k}}f\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L^{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}f
for each
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
.
∎

Now we study the relation between measure-theoretic equicontinuity and measure-theoretic sequence entropy.

Let
A=\{a_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a subsequence of
\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathcal{U}
be a finite open cover of
X
and
K
be a subset of
X
. Denote by
N(\mathcal{U}|K)
the minimum among the cardinalities of the subset of
\mathcal{U}
which covers
K
, and set
C_{A}(\mathcal{U}|K)=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}N(\bigvee\limits_{i=1}^{n}T^{-a_{i}}\mathcal{U}|K)
, where
\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}=\{U\cap V:U\in\mathcal{U},V\in\mathcal{V}\}
for two finite open covers
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}
of
X
. We recall that for two finite covers
\alpha,\beta
of
X
,
\alpha\preceq\beta
means that for each
B\in\beta
, there exists
A\in\alpha
such that
B\subset A.

The following lemma is useful(see
[
14
, Lemma 2.3]
):

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\alpha=\{A_{1},A_{2}\ldots,A_{k}\}
be a Borel partition of
X
and
\epsilon>0
, then there exists a finite open cover
\mathcal{U}
with
k
elements such that for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
and any Borel partition
\beta
satisfying
T^{-j}\mathcal{U}\preceq\beta
,
H_{\mu}(T^{-j}\alpha|\beta)\leq\epsilon
.

Now we prove that
\mu
-
A
-equicontinuity implies rigidity. The idea is from
[
11
, Proposition 5.4]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
(X,T)
be a t.d.s. and
\mu\in M(X,T)
. If there exists a subsequence
A
of
\mathbb{N}
such that
(X,T)
is
\mu
-
A
-equicontinuous, then
h_{\mu}^{S}(T)=0
for any subsequence
S
of
A
, hence

(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
is rigid.

Proof.

Claim
: For any
\tau>0
, there exists a compact subset
K
of
X
with
\mu(K)>1-\tau
such that for any finite open cover
\mathcal{U}
,
C_{A}(\mathcal{U}|K)<\infty
.

Proof of Claim: Given
\tau>0
, since
(X,T)
is
\mu
-
A
-equicontinuous, there exists a compact
A
-equicontinuous subset
K
of
X
with
\mu(K)>1-\tau
. For any finite open cover
\mathcal{U}
, let
\epsilon>0
be a Lebesgue number of
\mathcal{U}
. Since
K
is
A
-equicontinuous, there exists
\delta>0
such that

x,y\in K,d(x,y)<\delta\Rightarrow d(T^{a}x,T^{a}y)<\epsilon,\ \forall a\in A.

As
K
is compact, there exist

x_{1},\ldots,x_{L}\in K
such that

K=\bigcup_{i=1}^{L}(B(x_{i},\frac{\delta}{2})\cap K).

For any
a\in A
and
1\leq i\leq L
,
we have that

diam(T^{a}(B(x_{i},\frac{\delta}{2})\cap K))\leq\epsilon.

Hence there exists
U_{a,i}\in\mathcal{U}
such that

T^{a}(B(x_{i},\frac{\delta}{2})\cap K)\subset U_{a,i}.

Thus

B(x_{i},\frac{\delta}{2})\cap K\subset\bigcap_{a\in A}T^{-a}U_{a,i},\ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq L.

This implies
C_{A}(\mathcal{U}|K)\leq L<\infty
. The proof of Claim is complete.

Now we prove
h_{\mu}^{S}(T)=0
for any subsequence
S
of
A
.
Assume the contrary, there exists a subsequence
S
of
A
such that
h_{\mu}^{S}(T)>0
, then there exists a Borel partition
\alpha
of
X
and
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that
h_{\mu}^{S}(T,\alpha)>\epsilon_{0}
.
By Lemma
3.4
, there exists a finite open cover
\mathcal{U}
of
X
such that for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
and any Borel partition
\beta
satisfying
T^{-j}\mathcal{U}\preceq\beta
,
H_{\mu}(T^{-j}\alpha|\beta)\leq\epsilon_{0}
.

For any
\tau>0
, by Claim, there exists a compact set
K
with
\mu(K)>1-\tau
and
C_{A}(\mathcal{U}|K)<\infty
, set
C_{A}(\mathcal{U}|K)=L
. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we pick out two sub-collections
\mathcal{E}^{1}_{n},\mathcal{E}^{2}_{n}
of
\bigvee\limits_{i=1}^{n}T^{-s_{i}}\mathcal{U}
such that
\mathcal{E}^{1}_{n}
is a cover of
K
with
N(\bigvee\limits_{i=1}^{n}T^{-s_{i}}\mathcal{U}|K)
elements, and
\mathcal{E}^{2}_{n}
is a cover of

X
with
N(\bigvee\limits_{i=1}^{n}T^{-s_{i}}\mathcal{U})
elements. Enumerate them by

\mathcal{E}^{1}_{n}=\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m(n,1)},\mathcal{E}^{2}_{n}=\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m(n,2)}.

Set

\beta_{1}=\{K\cap(U_{i}-\bigcup_{k=1}^{i-1}U_{k}):i=1,\ldots,m(n,1)\},

\beta_{2}=\{K^{c}\cap(V_{i}-\bigcup_{k=1}^{i-1}V_{k}):i=1,\ldots,m(n,2)\}.

Then
\beta_{1}
is a partition of
K
with
|\beta_{1}|\leq N(\bigvee\limits_{i=1}^{n}T^{-s_{i}}\mathcal{U}|K)
and

\beta_{2}
is a partition of
K^{c}
with
|\beta_{2}|\leq N(\bigvee\limits_{i=1}^{n}T^{-s_{i}}\mathcal{U})
. Put
\beta=\beta_{1}\cup\beta_{2}
, then
\beta
is a partition of
X
and
\beta\succeq\bigvee\limits_{i=1}^{n}T^{-s_{i}}\mathcal{U}
. Hence we have

\begin{split}H_{\mu}(\beta)&=-\sum_{A\in\beta_{1}}\mu(A)\text{log}\mu(A)-\sum_{B\in\beta_{2}}\mu(B)\text{log}\mu(B)\\
&\leq(\mu(K)\text{log}|\beta_{1}|-\mu(K)\text{log}\mu(K))+(\mu(K^{c})\text{log}|\beta_{2}|-\mu(K^{c})\text{log}\mu(K^{c}))\\
&\leq\mu(K)\text{log}N(\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}T^{-s_{i}}\mathcal{U}|K)+\mu(K^{c})\text{log}N(\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}T^{-s_{i}}\mathcal{U})+\text{log}2\\
&\leq\mu(K)\text{log}L+n\mu(K^{c})\text{log}N(\mathcal{U})+\text{log}2.\end{split}

On the other hand, since
\beta\succeq\bigvee\limits_{i=1}^{n}T^{-s_{i}}\mathcal{U}
, by the construction of
\mathcal{U}
,

H_{\mu}(T^{-s_{i}}\alpha|\beta)\leq\epsilon_{0},\ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq n.

It follows that

\begin{split}&H_{\mu}(\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}T^{-s_{i}}\alpha)\leq H_{\mu}((\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}T^{-s_{i}}\alpha)\vee\beta)\leq H_{\mu}(\beta)+H_{\mu}(\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}T^{-s_{i}}\alpha|\beta)\\
&\leq\mu(K)\text{log}L+n\mu(K^{c})\text{log}N(\mathcal{U})+\text{log}2+\sum_{i=1}^{n}H_{\mu}(T^{-s_{i}}\alpha|\beta)\\
&\leq\mu(K)\text{log}L+n\mu(K^{c})\text{log}N(\mathcal{U})+\text{log}2+n\epsilon_{0}.\end{split}

Hence

h_{\mu}^{S}(T,\alpha)=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{\mu}(\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}T^{-s_{i}}\alpha)\leq\mu(K^{c})\text{log}N(\mathcal{U})+\epsilon_{0}\leq\tau\text{log}N(\mathcal{U})+\epsilon_{0}.

Let
\tau\rightarrow 0,
we have
h_{\mu}^{S}(T,\alpha)\leq\epsilon_{0}
, it is a contradiction. Hence
h_{\mu}^{S}(T)=0
for any subsequence
S
of
A
. By Theorem
3.3
,

In this subsection, we prove that if

First we give a useful characterization of measure-theoretic equicontinuity:

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
(X,T)
be a t.d.s. and
\mu\in M(X,T)
. Let
A=\{a_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a subsequence of
\mathbb{N}
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

(X,T)
is
\mu
-
A
-equicontinuous.

(2)

For any
\tau>0
and
\epsilon>0
, there exist a compact subset
K
of
X
with
\mu(K)>1-\tau
and
\delta>0
such that

x,y\in K,d(x,y)<\delta\Rightarrow d(T^{a_{n}}x,T^{a_{n}}y)<\epsilon,\ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2) is obvious.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1): Given
\tau>0
, for any

, by (2) there exist a compact set

with

and

for all

Let
K=\bigcap\limits_{l=1}^{\infty}K_{l}
, then
\mu(K)>1-\tau
and
K
is compact. It is easy to see that
K
is
A
-equicontinuous, hence
(X,T)
is
\mu
-
A
-equicontinuous.
∎

Now we prove that rigidity implies

-
A
-equicontinuity. Note that the following theorem is stronger.

\mu
-
A
-equicontinuous.

Proof.

It is similar to Theorem

.
∎

Combining Theorem

.

Let

be a t.d.s. and

. Then

Now we prove that for a function

,
f
-
\mu
-
A
-equicontinuity implies rigidity.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
(X,T)
be a t.d.s.,
\mu\in M(X,T)
and
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
. If there exists a subsequence
A=\{a_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
of
\mathbb{N}
such that
f
is
\mu
-
A
-equicontinuous, then
\{U_{T}^{a_{n}}f:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is pre-compact in
L^{2}(\mu)
, hence
f
is rigid.

Proof.

Assume that
\{U_{T}^{a_{n}}f:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is not pre-compact in
L^{2}(\mu)
, then
there exists
\epsilon>0
and a subsequence
\{n_{i}\}
of
\mathbb{N}
such that

||U^{a_{n_{i}}}_{T}f-U^{a_{n_{j}}}_{T}f||^{2}_{L^{2}}>9\epsilon^{2}+4\epsilon,\ \forall i\neq j\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
, there exists
\eta>0
such that

\mu(E)<\eta\Rightarrow\int_{E}|f|^{2}d\mu<\epsilon,\ \forall E\subset X.

Claim
: For any subsequence
\{s_{i}\}
of
\mathbb{N}
, we have

\mu(\{x:|f(T^{s_{i}}x)|\rightarrow+\infty\})=0.

Proof of Claim: Let
M=\{x:|f(T^{s_{i}}x)|\rightarrow+\infty\}
. It is easy to see that
M
is measurable. By Fatou’s lemma

\int_{M}\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}|f(T^{s_{i}}x)|d\mu(x)\leq\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}\int_{M}|f(T^{s_{i}}x)|d\mu(x).

Note that

\int_{M}|f(T^{s_{i}}x)|d\mu(x)\leq\int|f(T^{s_{i}}x)|d\mu(x)=\int|f|d\mu<+\infty,

we have
+\infty\cdot\mu(M)<+\infty
, hence
\mu(M)=0
. The proof of Claim is complete.

Since
f
is
\mu
-
A
-equicontinuous, there exists a
f
-
A
-equicontinuous set
K_{0}
in
X
with
\mu(K_{0})>1-\eta
, hence there exists
\delta>0
such that

x,y\in K_{0},d(x,y)<\delta\Rightarrow|f(T^{a_{i}}x)-f(T^{a_{i}}y)|<\epsilon,\ \forall i\in\mathbb{N}.

By the compactness of
X
, there exist

K_{0}=X_{1}\cup X_{2}\cup\cdots\cup X_{m_{0}},

where
diamX_{i}<\delta,\ 1\leq i\leq m_{0}
. We assume
\mu(X_{i})>0,\ 1\leq i\leq m;\ \mu(X_{i})=0,\ m+1\leq i\leq m_{0}
.
Let
K=X_{1}\cup X_{2}\cup\cdots\cup X_{m}
, then
K\subset K_{0}
and
\mu(K)=\mu(K_{0})>1-\eta
. Since
\mu(X_{1})>0
and

\mu(\{x:|f(T^{a_{n_{i}}}x)|\rightarrow+\infty\})=0,

we can find
y_{1}\in X_{1}
and
|f(T^{a_{n_{i}}}y_{1})|
not convergent to
+\infty
, hence there exists a subsequence
\{a_{n_{i}}^{1}\}\subset\{a_{n_{i}}\}
such that
\{f(T^{a_{n_{i}}^{1}}y_{1})\}
is a Cauchy sequence. Note that
\mu(X_{2})>0
and

\mu(\{x:|f(T^{a_{n_{i}}^{1}}x)|\rightarrow+\infty\})=0,

we can find
y_{2}\in X_{2}
and a subsequence
\{a_{n_{i}}^{2}\}\subset\{a_{n_{i}}^{1}\}
such that
\{f(T^{a_{n_{i}}^{2}}y_{2})\}
is a Cauchy sequence.
Working inductively, we can find
y_{i}\in X_{i},1\leq i\leq m
and a subsequence
\{s_{i}\}\subset\{a_{n_{i}}\}
such that
\{f(T^{s_{i}}y)\}
is a Cauchy sequence for all
y\in\{y_{1},y_{2}\ldots,y_{m}\}
. Hence there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\forall i,j>N,\ |f(T^{s_{i}}y)-f(T^{s_{j}}y)|<\epsilon,\ \forall y\in\{y_{1},y_{2}\ldots,y_{m}\}.

Choose
i_{0}\neq j_{0}>N
, now we estimate
||U_{T}^{s_{i_{0}}}f-U_{T}^{s_{j_{0}}}f||_{L^{2}}^{2}
.

||U_{T}^{s_{i_{0}}}f-U_{T}^{s_{j_{0}}}f||_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{K}|f\circ T^{s_{i_{0}}}-f\circ T^{s_{j_{0}}}|^{2}d\mu+\int_{K^{c}}|f\circ T^{s_{i_{0}}}-f\circ T^{s_{j_{0}}}|^{2}d\mu.

We first estimate
\int_{K}|f\circ T^{s_{i_{0}}}-f\circ T^{s_{j_{0}}}|^{2}d\mu
. If
x\in K
, then there exists
y\in\{y_{1},y_{2}\ldots,y_{m}\}
such that
d(x,y)<\delta
. Since
x,y\in K_{0}
, we have

|f(T^{a_{i}}x)-f(T^{a_{i}}y)|<\epsilon,\ \forall i\in\mathbb{N},

hence

|f(T^{s_{i}}x)-f(T^{s_{i}}y)|<\epsilon,\ \forall i\in\mathbb{N}.

Since

|f(T^{s_{i_{0}}}y)-f(T^{s_{j_{0}}}y)|<\epsilon,

it follows that

\begin{split}|f(T^{s_{i_{0}}}x)-f(T^{s_{j_{0}}}x)|&\leq|f(T^{s_{i_{0}}}x)-f(T^{s_{i_{0}}}y)|+|f(T^{s_{j_{0}}}x)-f(T^{s_{j_{0}}}y)|\\
&+|f(T^{s_{i_{0}}}y)-f(T^{s_{j_{0}}}y)|<3\epsilon.\end{split}

Hence
\int_{K}|f\circ T^{s_{i_{0}}}-f\circ T^{s_{j_{0}}}|^{2}d\mu<9\epsilon^{2}
.

Now we estimate

\int_{K^{c}}|f\circ T^{s_{i_{0}}}-f\circ T^{s_{j_{0}}}|^{2}d\mu
.

\begin{split}\int_{K^{c}}|f\circ T^{s_{i_{0}}}-f\circ T^{s_{j_{0}}}|^{2}d\mu&\leq\int_{K^{c}}2(|f\circ T^{s_{i_{0}}}|^{2}+|f\circ T^{s_{j_{0}}}|^{2})d\mu\\
&=2\int_{T^{s_{i_{0}}}K^{c}}|f|^{2}d\mu+2\int_{T^{s_{j_{0}}}K^{c}}|f|^{2}d\mu.\end{split}

Since

\mu(T^{s_{i_{0}}}K^{c})=\mu(T^{s_{j_{0}}}K^{c})=\mu(K^{c})<\eta,

the right side
<4\epsilon
. Hence

\int_{K^{c}}|f\circ T^{s_{i_{0}}}-f\circ T^{s_{j_{0}}}|^{2}d\mu<4\epsilon.

It follows that

||U_{T}^{s_{i_{0}}}f-U_{T}^{s_{j_{0}}}f||_{L^{2}}^{2}<9\epsilon^{2}+4\epsilon.

Note that
\{s_{i}\}\subset\{a_{n_{i}}\}
and

||U^{a_{n_{i}}}_{T}f-U^{a_{n_{j}}}_{T}f||_{L^{2}}>9\epsilon^{2}+4\epsilon,\ \forall i\neq j\in\mathbb{N},

it is a contradiction. So
\{U_{T}^{a_{n}}f:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is pre-compact in
L^{2}(\mu)
.

Now we proof
f
is rigid. Since
\{U_{T}^{a_{n}}f:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is pre-compact in
L^{2}(\mu)
, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\{U_{T}^{a_{n}}f:n\in\mathbb{N}\}\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}B(U_{T}^{a_{i}}f,\frac{1}{k}).

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
1\leq i\leq N
such that

||U_{T}^{a_{n}-a_{i}}f-f||_{L^{2}}=||U_{T}^{a_{n}}f-U_{T}^{a_{i}}f||_{L^{2}}<\frac{1}{k}.

Let
n_{k}=a_{n}-a_{i}
, since
a_{n}\rightarrow+\infty
we can choose
\{n_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
increasing, hence we have
||U_{T}^{n_{k}}f-f||_{L^{2}}<\frac{1}{k},\ U_{T}^{n_{k}}f\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L^{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}f
.
∎

The next theorem shows that for a function
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
, rigidity implies
f
-
\mu
-
A
-equicontinuity.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
(X,T)
be a t.d.s.,
\mu\in M(X,T)
and

. If there exists a subsequence

of
\mathbb{N}

U_{T}^{a_{n}}f\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.e.}}{{\longrightarrow}}f

is
\mu
-
A

Proof.

\forall\tau>0,\forall\epsilon>0
, let

A_{N}=\{x:|U_{T}^{a_{n}}f(x)-f(x)|<\frac{\epsilon}{3},\ \forall n>N\}.

It is easy to see that
\{A_{N}\}
is increasing and

\{x:U_{T}^{a_{n}}f(x)\rightarrow f(x)\}\subset\bigcup_{N=1}^{\infty}A_{N}.

Hence
\mu(\bigcup\limits_{N=1}^{\infty}A_{N})=1
so there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mu(A_{N})>1-\dfrac{\tau}{2}
. Since
f,U_{T}^{a_{1}}f,\ldots,U_{T}^{a_{N}}f\in L^{2}(\mu)
, by Lusin’s theorem, there exists a compact subset
K_{0}
of
X
with
\mu(K_{0})>1-\dfrac{\tau}{2}
such that

K_{0}
. Then there exists
\delta>0
such that

x,y\in K_{0},d(x,y)<\delta\Rightarrow|f(x)-f(y)|<\dfrac{\epsilon}{3},|U_{T}^{a_{n}}f(x)-U_{T}^{a_{n}}f(y)|<\epsilon,\ 1\leq n\leq N.

Choose a compact set
K\subset A_{N}\cap K_{0}
with
\mu(K)>1-\tau
. For any
x,y\in K
and
d(x,y)<\delta
, if
1\leq n\leq N
, then
|U_{T}^{a_{n}}f(x)-U_{T}^{a_{n}}f(y)|<\epsilon
; if
n>N
, then

\begin{split}|U_{T}^{a_{n}}f(x)-U_{T}^{a_{n}}f(y)|\leq|U_{T}^{a_{n}}f(x)-f(x&)|+|U_{T}^{a_{n}}f(y)-f(y)|+|f(x)-f(y)|\\
<\frac{\epsilon}{3}+\frac{\epsilon}{3}+\frac{\epsilon}{3}=\epsilon.\end{split}

Hence
|U_{T}^{a_{n}}f(x)-U_{T}^{a_{n}}f(y)|<\epsilon
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By Proposition
3.9
,
f
is
\mu
-
A
-equicontinuous.
∎

Let

U^{a_{s}}_{T}d(x_{i},x_{j}),&(x,y)\in X_{i}\times X_{j}\ 1\leq i,j\leq N\hbox{.}\end{array}\right.

For any

|d_{s}(x,y)-U^{a_{s}}_{T}d(x,y)|=|U^{a_{s}}_{T}d(x,y)-U^{a_{s}}_{T}d(x_{i},x_{j})|\leq U^{a_{s}}_{T}d(x,x_{i})+U^{a_{s}}_{T}d(x_{j},y).

Sum

\begin{split}\sum_{s=1}^{n_{0}}|d_{s}(x,y)-U^{a_{s}}_{T}d(x,y)|&\leq\sum_{s=1}^{n_{0}}U^{a_{s}}_{T}d(x,x_{i})+\sum_{s=1}^{n_{0}}U^{a_{s}}_{T}d(x_{j},y)\\

Hence

\sum_{s=1}^{n_{0}}|d_{s}(x,y)-U^{a_{s}}_{T}d(x,y)|\leq 2n_{0}\epsilon

since

, we have

Note that

\begin{split}\int_{X_{0}\times X\cup X\times X_{0}}\sum_{s=1}^{n_{0}}|d_{s}-U^{a_{s}}_{T}d|&=\int_{X_{0}\times X\cup X\times X_{0}}\sum_{s=1}^{n_{0}}|U^{a_{s}}_{T}d|\\

we have

\sum_{s=1}^{n_{0}}||d_{s}-U^{a_{s}}_{T}d||_{L^{1}}\leq 4n_{0}\epsilon.

It follows that

|\{1\leq s\leq n_{0}:||d_{s}-U^{a_{s}}_{T}d||_{L^{1}}\leq 8M\epsilon\}|\geq n_{0}-[\frac{n_{0}}{2M}].

Note that

\begin{split}|A\cap[1,n_{0}]|=|\{s\in\mathbb{N}:a_{s}\leq n_{0}\}|&=\\
|\{1\leq s\leq n_{0}:a_{s}\leq n_{0}\}|&>\frac{n_{0}}{M}\geq 2[\frac{n_{0}}{2M}],\end{split}

S
. Divide

\{b_{ij}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq N}
must be in one of the

with
|S_{0}|\geq[\frac{[\frac{n_{0}}{2M}]}{C}]\geq\frac{n_{0}}{4MC}

It follows that for any

\begin{split}||U^{a_{s}}_{T}d-U^{a_{s^{\prime}}}_{T}d||_{L^{1}}&\leq||d_{s}-U^{a_{s}}_{T}d||_{L^{1}}+||d_{s^{\prime}}-U^{a_{s^{\prime}}}_{T}d||_{L^{1}}+||d_{s}-d_{s^{\prime}}||_{L^{1}}\\
&\leq 8M\epsilon+8M\epsilon+\epsilon\leq 17M\epsilon.\end{split}

Enumerate

Consider the set

∎

In order to prove Theorem
4.9
, we need the following theorem. The idea is from
[
10
, Theorem 4.4]
.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
(X,T)
be a t.d.s.
\mu\in M(X,T)
and
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
. If there exists a subsequence
A=\{a_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
of
\mathbb{N}
such that
U_{T}^{a_{n}}d_{f}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.e.}}{{\longrightarrow}}d_{f}
, then
f
is
\mu
-
A
-equicontinuous.

Proof.

Given
\tau>0
and
\epsilon>0
. Since
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
, there exists a compact set
K_{0}
with
\mu(K_{0})>1-\dfrac{\tau}{4}
such that
f
is uniformly continuous on
K_{0}
. Then there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that for any
x,y\in K_{0}
and
d(x,y)<\delta_{0}
, we have
d_{f}(x,y)<\dfrac{\epsilon}{8}
. By the compactness of
X
, there exist
K_{0}=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{C}X_{i}
, where
diamX_{i}<\delta_{0},1\leq i\leq C,\ C\in\mathbb{N}
. Without loss of generality, assume
X_{i}\neq\emptyset
and choose
x_{i}\in X_{i},\ 1\leq i\leq C.
It is easy to see that

K_{0}\subset\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{C}B_{d_{f}}(x_{i},\dfrac{\epsilon}{8}).

It follows that

\mu(\bigcup_{i=1}^{C}B_{d_{f}}(x_{i},\dfrac{\epsilon}{8}))>1-\dfrac{\tau}{4}.

For any
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let

A_{N}=\{(x,y):|U_{T}^{a_{n}}d_{f}(x,y)-d_{f}(x,y)|<\dfrac{\epsilon}{4},\ \forall n>N\}.

It is easy to see that

\{(x,y):U_{T}^{a_{n}}d_{f}(x,y)\rightarrow d_{f}(x,y)\}\subset\bigcup_{N=1}^{\infty}A_{N}.

Note that
\{A_{N}\}
is increasing, so there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mu\times\mu(A_{N})>1-(\dfrac{\tau}{4C})^{2}
. By Fubini’s theorem there exists a subset
D
of
X
with
\mu(D)>1-\dfrac{\tau}{4C}
such that
\forall x\in D,\mu((A_{N})_{x})>1-\dfrac{\tau}{4C},
where
(A_{N})_{x}=\{y\in X:(x,y)\in A_{N}\}
.

Note that

\mu(\bigcup_{i=1}^{C}(B_{d_{f}}(x_{i},\dfrac{\epsilon}{8})\cap D))>1-\dfrac{\tau}{2},

without loss of generality, assume
B_{d_{f}}(x_{i},\dfrac{\epsilon}{8})\cap D\neq\emptyset
and choose

y_{i}\in B_{d_{f}}(x_{i},\dfrac{\epsilon}{8})\cap D,1\leq i\leq C.

Then

\mu((A_{N})_{y_{i}})>1-\dfrac{\tau}{4C},1\leq i\leq C.

Since
B_{d_{f}}(x_{i},\dfrac{\epsilon}{8})\subset B_{d_{f}}(y_{i},\dfrac{\epsilon}{4})
, we have

\mu(\bigcup_{i=1}^{C}B_{d_{f}}(y_{i},\dfrac{\epsilon}{4}))\geq\mu(D\cap\bigcup_{i=1}^{C}B_{d_{f}}(y_{i},\dfrac{\epsilon}{4}))>1-\dfrac{\tau}{2}.

Since
U_{T}^{a_{1}}f,\ldots,U_{T}^{a_{N}}f\in L^{2}(\mu)
, there exists a compact set
K_{1}
with
\mu(K_{1})>1-\dfrac{\tau}{4}
such that
U_{T}^{a_{1}}f,\ldots,U_{T}^{a_{N}}f
are uniformly continuous on
K_{1}
. Then there exists
\delta_{1}>0
such that for any
x,y\in K_{1}
and
d(x,y)<\delta_{1}
, we have

U_{T}^{a_{n}}d_{f}(x,y)<\epsilon,1\leq n\leq N.

Note that

\mu(K_{1}\cap\bigcup_{i=1}^{C}B_{d_{f}}(y_{i},\dfrac{\epsilon}{4})\cap\bigcap_{i=1}^{C}(A_{N})_{y_{i}})>1-\tau,

by the regularity of
\mu
, we can find pairwise disjoint compact sets

E_{i}\subset B_{d_{f}}(y_{i},\dfrac{\epsilon}{4}),1\leq i\leq C

such that

\bigcup_{i=1}^{C}E_{i}\subset K_{1}\cap\bigcup_{i=1}^{C}B_{d_{f}}(y_{i},\dfrac{\epsilon}{4})\cap\bigcap_{i=1}^{C}(A_{N})_{y_{i}}

and
\mu(\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{C}E_{i})>1-\tau.
Let
K=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{C}E_{i}
, then
K
is compact and
\mu(K)>1-\tau
. Choose
\delta
with

0<\delta<\min\{\delta_{1},d(E_{i},E_{j}),1\leq i\neq j\leq C\}.

For any
x,y\in K
and
d(x,y)<\delta
, if
1\leq n\leq N
, since
x,y\in K_{1}
,
U_{T}^{a_{n}}d_{f}(x,y)<\epsilon
; if
n>N
, since
d(x,y)<\delta
, there exists
1\leq i_{0}\leq C
such that
x,y\in E_{i_{0}}
. Then

U_{T}^{a_{n}}d_{f}(x,y)\leq U_{T}^{a_{n}}d_{f}(y_{i_{0}},x)+U_{T}^{a_{n}}d_{f}(y_{i_{0}},y).

Note that
x,y\in(A_{N})_{y_{i_{0}}}
, we have
(y_{i_{0}},x),(y_{i_{0}},y)\in A_{N}
, hence

U_{T}^{a_{n}}d_{f}(y_{i_{0}},x)<d_{f}(y_{i_{0}},x)+\dfrac{\epsilon}{4}.

Since
E_{i_{0}}\subset B_{d_{f}}(y_{i_{0}},\dfrac{\epsilon}{4}),
we have
d_{f}(y_{i_{0}},x)<\dfrac{\epsilon}{4},
it follows that
U_{T}^{a_{n}}d_{f}(y_{i_{0}},x)<\dfrac{\epsilon}{2}
. Similarly
U_{T}^{a_{n}}d_{f}(y_{i_{0}},y)<\dfrac{\epsilon}{2}
, so
U_{T}^{a_{n}}d_{f}(x,y)<\epsilon
. By Proposition
3.9
,
f
is
\mu
-
A
-equicontinuous.
∎

Now we prove Theorem
4.9
:

proof of Theorem

.

Replacing
U_{T}^{a_{n}}d
by
U_{T}^{a_{n}}d_{f}
, the proof of Theorem
4.5
still works, so we have
{\{U_{T}^{a_{n}}d_{f}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}}
is pre-compact in

. Then there exists a subsequence
S=\{s_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
of
\mathbb{N}
such that

. By
Theorem
4.10
,
f
is
\mu
-
S
-equicontinuous. By Theorem
3.10
,
f
is rigid.
∎

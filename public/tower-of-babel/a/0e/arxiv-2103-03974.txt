Paul Balmer, Ivo Dell’Ambrogio

Abstract
. We show that the bicategory of finite groupoids and right-free permutation bimodules is a quotient of the bicategory of Mackey 2-motives introduced in [ BD20 ] , obtained by modding out the so-called cohomological relations. This categorifies Yoshida’s Theorem for ordinary cohomological Mackey functors, and provides a direct connection between Mackey 2-motives and the usual blocks of representation theory.

1.
Introduction

We consider Mackey 2-functors in the sense of
[
BD20
]
and develop three themes.
The first two are general. They consist of a decategorification result and a new simpler description of the Mackey 2-motives of
[
BD20
]
. The third theme is the more specific purpose of this paper, the study of
cohomological
Mackey 2-functors.
Let us now explain in some detail the interplay of these three topics.

*\ *\ *

A
Mackey 2-functor

\mathcal{M}
consists of additive categories
\mathcal{M}(G)
depending on finite groupoids
G
and whose variance in
G
is reminiscent of ordinary Mackey functors
[
Web00
]
. This categorification, recalled in
Section

2
, involves restriction functors
u^{*}\colon\mathcal{M}(G)\to\mathcal{M}(K)
for every morphism
u\colon K\to G
and
two-sided
adjoints
i_{!}=i_{*}\colon\mathcal{M}(H)\to\mathcal{M}(G)
to
i^{*}
for every faithful
i\colon H\mathop{\rightarrowtail}G
. The commutation of
i_{!}
and
u^{*}
is governed by a
2-Mackey formula
(
2.4
). As discussed at length in
[
BD20
, Ch. 4]
, this type of structure has been used for a long time in a variety of settings.
For instance, in representation theory, the category
\mathcal{M}(G)=\Modcat(\Bbbk G)
of
\Bbbk G
-modules is an example of a Mackey 2-functor. In topology, the equivariant stable homotopy category
\mathcal{M}(G)=\SH(G)
of genuine
G
-spectra is another one.

Recall
[
Web00
, § 7]
that an ordinary Mackey (1-) functor
M
is
cohomological
if, for every subgroup
H\leq G
, the composite
I_{H}^{G}\,R^{G}_{H}\colon M(G)\to M(G)
of the restriction homomorphism
R^{G}_{H}\colon M(G)\to M(H)
with the induction (transfer) homomorphism
I_{H}^{G}\colon M(H)\to M(G)
is equal to multiplication by the index
[G\!:\!H]
on the abelian group
M(G)
.
It might be tempting to define a cohomological Mackey 2-functor
\mathcal{M}
as one for which the composite of
i^{*}\colon\mathcal{M}(G)\to\mathcal{M}(H)
with
i_{!}\colon\mathcal{M}(H)\to\mathcal{M}(G)
is some form of multiplication by
[G\!:\!H]
on
\mathcal{M}(G)
.
This is not a good idea, however, if only because of a lack of interesting examples.
We propose here a definition that shifts the cohomological relation to the level of 2-cells, as follows:

1.1 Definition
.

A Mackey 2-functor
\mathcal{M}
is
cohomological
if, whenever
i\colon H\mathop{\rightarrowtail}G
is the inclusion of a subgroup
H
in a finite group
G
, the composite

\textstyle{\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{M}(G)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{}^{r\!}\eta}
\textstyle{\Ind^{G}_{H}\Res^{G}_{H}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{}^{\ell}\varepsilon}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{M}(G)}}

equals multiplication by the index
[G\!:\!H]
, where
{}^{r\!}\eta
is the unit of the right adjunction
\Res^{G}_{H}=i^{*}\dashv i_{*}=\Ind_{H}^{G}
and
{}^{\ell}\varepsilon
the counit of the left one
\Ind_{H}^{G}=i_{!}\dashv i^{*}=\Res_{H}^{G}
.

The Mackey 2-functors arising in representation theory are often cohomological, like the above
\mathcal{M}(G)=\Modcat(\Bbbk G)
or the derived version
\mathcal{M}(G)=\Der(\Modcat(\Bbbk G))
. We present further examples of a more geometric nature in
Section

5
.

The reader should be warned that the standard decategorification of a Mackey 2-functor via the Grothendieck group
K_{0}
, as in
[
BD20
, § 2.5]
, does
not
necessarily turn cohomological Mackey 2-functors into cohomological Mackey 1-functors (see Remarks
5.8
and
5.9
).
To restore such a connection we propose in
Section

3
a different type of decategorification, that works as follows:

1.2 Theorem

(Hom-Decategorification)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\colon\mathsf{gpd}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathsf{ADD}
be a Mackey 2-functor. Let
G
be a finite group and let
X,Y\in\mathcal{M}(G)
be two objects. Then there is an ordinary Mackey functor
M=M_{\mathcal{M},G,X,Y}
for
G
whose value on every subgroup
H\leq G
is given by the abelian group

M(H)=\Hom_{\mathcal{\mathcal{M}}(H)}(\Res^{G}_{H}X\,,\,\Res^{G}_{H}Y).

This is
3.7
specialized to
3.8
.
We emphasize that this result works without the label ‘cohomological’ and is thus of interest in the generality of
[
BD20
]
.
When
\mathcal{M}
is moreover cohomological then all its Hom-decategorifications are cohomological in the classical sense (
5.6
).

Several examples of Hom-decategorification are discussed in
Section

5
.
For instance, group cohomology (the ur-example of a cohomological Mackey functor) is a Hom-decategorification of the derived category Mackey 2-functor
G\mapsto\Der(\Bbbk G)
, itself a prime example of a cohomological Mackey 2-functor.

We also prove in
Section

5
a descent result for cohomological Mackey 2-functors; see
5.10
.

*\ *\ *

Let us now turn our attention to Mackey motives.
Fix a commutative ring
\Bbbk
of coefficients, for instance
\Bbbk=\mathbb{Z}
. Suppose from now on that all our Mackey 2-functors take values in idempotent-complete
\Bbbk
-linear additive categories
\mathcal{M}(G)
and
\Bbbk
-linear functors
u^{*}
. In
[
BD20
]
, we constructed a
\Bbbk
-linear bicategory
\Motk
(there denoted ‘
\Bbbk\textrm{\sf S}\widehat{\textrm{\sf pan}}
’) of
Mackey 2-motives
, together with a canonical contravariant embedding of the 2-category of finite groupoids

(1.3)

\mot\colon\mathsf{gpd}^{\mathrm{op}}\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\Motk.

It enjoys the following universal property: Every Mackey 2-functor
\mathcal{M}
factors as
\mathcal{M}\cong\widehat{\mathcal{M}}\circ\mot
for a unique
\Bbbk
-linear pseudo-functor
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
on
\Motk
.
The original construction of the bicategory
\Motk
is pretty involved, with spans of 1-cells
and
spans of 2-cells. (See
4.1
.) We prove in
Section

4
that there is a simpler description of
\Motk
. Again, this holds beyond the cohomological world.

1.4 Theorem

(Mackey 2-motives via bisets)
.

General
\Bbbk
-linear Mackey 2-motives are modeled by the block-completion
(\Bbbk\mathsf{bi}\widehat{\mathsf{set}}{}^{\mathsf{rf}})^{\flat}
of the bicategory
\Bbbk\mathsf{bi}\widehat{\mathsf{set}}{}^{\mathsf{rf}}
whose objects are finite groupoids, 1-morphisms are right-free finite bisets between them, and 2-morphisms are
\Bbbk
-linear combinations of spans of equivariant maps.

Block-completion
(-)^{\flat}
is the 2-categorical analogue of idempotent-completion, a standard feature of motivic constructions. It simply adds formal summands, both at the 0- and 1-level, in order to split idempotent 2-cells; see
2.2
.

1.4
categorifies the usual equivalence between Webb’s
inflation functors

[
Web93
]
and Bouc’s
right-free biset functors

[
Bou10
]
. See
4.28
.

It is natural to look for an analogous motivic construction with
cohomological
Mackey 2-functors.
Since these are just Mackey 2-functors satisfying some additional relations at the level of 2-cells, one can obtain the corresponding bicategory of
cohomological Mackey 2-motives

\CohMotk
by formally modding out in
\Motk
the relevant 2-cells.
Thus formulated,
\CohMotk
remains rather mysterious and one of our main goals is to give a simple computable description.
It comes as a categorification and generalization of Yoshida’s Theorem
[
Yos83
]
; see
6.17
.

1.5 Theorem

(Cohomological Mackey 2-motives)
.

Cohomological
\Bbbk
-linear Mackey 2-motives are modeled by the block-completion
(\perm^{\mathsf{rf}}_{\Bbbk})^{\flat}
of the bicategory
\perm^{\mathsf{rf}}_{\Bbbk}
whose objects are finite groupoids, 1-morphisms are right-free permutation bimodules, and 2-morphisms are equivariant
\Bbbk
-linear maps (see
2.13
). In other words, there is a canonical pseudo-functor
\cohmot\colon\mathsf{gpd}^{\mathrm{op}}\to(\perm^{\mathsf{rf}}_{\Bbbk})^{\flat}=:\CohMotk
sending a groupoid to itself and a functor
u\colon H\to G
to the
G,H
-bimodule
\Bbbk[G(u-,-)]\colon

H^{\mathrm{op}}\times G\to\Modcat(\Bbbk)
, such that every
\Bbbk
-linear cohomological Mackey 2-functor
\mathcal{M}
factors uniquely through this pseudo-functor up to isomorphism.

\textstyle{\mathsf{gpd}^{\mathrm{op}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cohmot}
\scriptstyle{\mathcal{M}}
\textstyle{\mathsf{ADD}^{\mathsf{ic}}_{\Bbbk}}
\textstyle{\CohMotk\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\exists!\,\widehat{\mathcal{M}}}

A proof and more details on these constructions can be found in
Section

6
. See in particular the 2-categorical universal property of
\CohMotk
in
6.20
.

In the second part of the paper, from

2.
Recollections

We fix a commutative ring
\Bbbk
throughout the article.

2.1 Terminology
.

We use the language of bicategories, 2-categories (
i.e.
strict bicategories), pseudo-functors etc., in a standard way as recalled in
[
BD20
, App. A]
.
For simplicity, a
\Bbbk
-linear
category means an
additive
category enriched over
\Bbbk
-modules.
Similarly, a
\Bbbk
-linear
bicategory
\mathcal{B}
means one in which all Hom categories
\mathcal{B}(X,Y)
and all horizontal composition functors are
\Bbbk
-linear, and which admits finite direct sums of objects.
A pseudo-functor
\mathcal{F}
is
\Bbbk
-linear
if each functor
\mathcal{F}_{X,Y}
is
\Bbbk
-linear – thus automatically preserves directs sums.
We call a contravariant pseudo-functor
additive
if it turns coproducts of objects into products, as in (Mack
1
) below.

2.2 Recollection
.

An additive category is
idempotent-complete
if every idempotent endomorphism
e=e^{2}
on an object
X
has an image, so that
X\cong\mathrm{Im}(e)\oplus\mathrm{Im}(1-e)
identifying
e
with the matrix
\mathrm{diag}(1,0)
.
Recall from
[
BD20
, § A.7]
that a
\Bbbk
-linear bicategory
\mathcal{B}
is
block-complete
if its Hom categories are idempotent-complete and if every decomposition of an identity 2-cell
\mathrm{id}_{\mathrm{Id}_{X}}
in orthogonal idempotent 2-cells induces a direct sum decomposition of the object
X
; so idempotent 2-cells split 1-cells and objects in directs sums.
Every
\Bbbk
-linear bicategory
\mathcal{B}
admits a
block-completion

\mathcal{B}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}^{\flat}
, the universal
\Bbbk
-linear pseudo-functor into a block-complete bicategory
\mathcal{B}^{\flat}
.
The precise construction of
\mathcal{B}^{\flat}
is not difficult but requires some work. It can be found in
[
BD20
, A.7.22]
. Suffice it to say that it is a categorification of Karoubi’s classical idea to replace objects
X
by pairs
(X,e)
where
e=e^{2}\colon X\to X
is an idempotent on
X
, suitably adapted to the 2-categorical setting,
i.e.
applied to both 0-cells (objects) and to 1-cells (as objects of the
\Hom
-categories).

2.3 Definition
.

We recall that a
(
\Bbbk
-linear
)
Mackey 2-functor
is the data of a 2-functor
\mathcal{M}\colon\mathsf{gpd}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathsf{ADD}_{\Bbbk}
from the 2-category of finite groupoids, functors and natural transformations to the 2-category of (possibly large)
\Bbbk
-linear additive categories, additive functors and natural transformations.
It inverts the direction of 1-cells, so that we have a
restriction
functor
u^{*}=\mathcal{M}(u)\colon\mathcal{M}(G)\to\mathcal{M}(H)
for every morphism (functor) of groupoids
u\colon H\to G
, and we have a natural isomorphism
\alpha^{*}\colon u^{*}\Rightarrow v^{*}
for every natural isomorphism
u\Rightarrow v
. This is subject to four axioms:

(Mack 1)

Additivity:

\mathcal{M}(G_{1}\sqcup G_{2})\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathcal{M}(G_{1})\times\mathcal{M}(G_{2})
for all
G_{1},G_{2}\in\mathsf{gpd}
.

(Mack 2)

Adjoints:
For every faithful morphism
i\colon H\mathop{\rightarrowtail}G
, the restriction functor
i^{*}\colon\mathcal{M}(G)\to\mathcal{M}(H)
admits a left adjoint
i_{!}
and a right adjoint
i_{*}
.

(Mack 3)

Mackey formulas:
For every

(a.k.a. pseudo-pullback, homotopy pullback; see
[
BD20

By the Rectification Theorem

We make use of two closely related bicategories: that of

2.8 Recollection

(Bisets)
.

Let
G
and
H
be finite groupoids.
By a
(
finite
)

G,H
-biset

S={}_{G}S_{H}
we mean a functor
S\colon H^{\mathrm{op}}\times G\to\mathrm{set}
to the category of finite sets.
We will often write

g\cdot s=S(\mathrm{id},g)(s)\quad\textrm{ and }\quad s\cdot h=S(h,\mathrm{id})(s)

to denote the ‘left action’ of a morphism
g\in G(x,x^{\prime})
and the ‘right action’ of a morphism
h\in H(y^{\prime},y)
on an element
s\in S(y,x)
.
We denote by
\mathsf{biset}
the bicategory with finite groupoids as objects, all
G,H
-bisets
{}_{G}S_{H}
as 1-cells
H\to G
, and all equivariant maps (
i.e.
natural transformations)
\alpha\colon S\Rightarrow T
as 2-cells.

The horizontal composition of bisets is provided by the tensor product of functors (a.k.a. set-theoretic coends). Concretely, the value at
(z,x)\in K^{\mathrm{op}}\times G
of a composite biset
({}_{G}T_{H})\circ({}_{H}S_{K})=T\times_{H}S
is the following coequalizer of sets:

(2.9)

\big(T\times_{H}S\big)(z,x)=\mathop{\mathrm{coeq}}\left(\coprod_{y^{\prime}\to y\atop\in\,\Mor H}T(y,x)\times S(z,y^{\prime})\rightrightarrows\!\coprod_{y\atop\in\,\Obj H}T(y,x)\times S(z,y)\right)\kern-6.99997pt

Even more concretely, an element of
(T\times_{H}S)(z,x)
is the equivalence class of a pair
(t,s)\in T(y,x)\times S(z,y)
for some
y\in\Obj H
, and two pairs
(t,s)
and
(t^{\prime},s^{\prime})
are equivalent if and only if there exists a morphism
h\in H(y,y^{\prime})
such that
(t,h\cdot s)=(t^{\prime}\cdot h,s^{\prime})
.
We will write
[t,s]
for such a class, or sometimes
[t,s]_{y}
or “
[t,s]
at
y
” if we need to keep track of the object
y\in\Obj H
.
The actions of
G
and
K
on
T\times_{H}S
are the evident
g\cdot[t,s]=[g\cdot t,s]
and
[t,s]\cdot k=[t,s\cdot k]
.

The identity biset of
G
is the Hom-functor
\mathrm{Id}_{G}=G(-,-)\colon G^{{\mathrm{op}}}\times G\to\mathrm{set}
. (By defining bisets and bimodules (below) as functors on
H^{\mathrm{op}}\times G
, rather than the perhaps more common
G\times H^{\mathrm{op}}
, we think of them as ‘generalized Hom-functors’.)

A
G,H
-biset
{}_{G}S_{H}
is
right-free
if the right action of
H
is free in the usual sense:
s\cdot h=s\Rightarrow h=\mathrm{id}
, for any
(y,x)\in H^{\mathrm{op}}\times G
,
s\in S(y,x)
and
h\in H(y^{\prime},y)
.
It is a straightforward exercise to see that the tensor product
T\times_{H}S
of two right-free bisets is again right-free.
Thus right-free bisets form a 2-full sub-bicategory of
\mathsf{biset}
which we denote
\mathsf{biset}^{\mathsf{rf}}
.

.

For finite groups, seen as one-object groupoids, the notions of bisets and their composition in

agree with those of Bouc

Bou10
]
. The full subcategory of groups in the 1-truncation
\tau_{1}(\mathsf{biset})
is the ordinary category of bisets of

, and
ditto
for right-free bisets. (
Cf.

[
Del22a
]
.)

2.11 Recollection

(Bimodules)
.

Let
G
and
H
be finite groupoids.
A
G,H
-bimodule
is a functor
M\colon H^{\mathrm{op}}\times G\to\Modcat(\Bbbk)
.
We denote by
\Bimod_{\Bbbk}
the bicategory with finite groupoids as objects,
G,H
-bimodules as 1-cells
H\to G
, and equivariant maps as 2-cells.
The horizontal composition of bimodules is given by the usual tensor product,
i.e.

\Bbbk
-linearly enriched coends (as in (
2.9
) but taking the coequalizer in
\Modcat(\Bbbk)
)

({}_{G}M_{H})\circ({}_{H}N_{K})=M\otimes_{\Bbbk H}N,

that we simply denote
M\otimes_{H}N
.
A
(
finite
)
permutation
G,H
-bimodule
is a
G,H
-bimodule which admits a finite
G,H
-invariant basis: There exist finite sets
S(y,x)
for all
(y,x)\in H^{\mathrm{op}}\times G
which are collectively stable under the
G
- and
H
-actions and such that each

is a basis of the (free)
\Bbbk
-module

.

Permutation bimodules are closed under tensor products, hence form a sub-bicategory
\biperm_{\Bbbk}
of
\Bimod_{\Bbbk}

-modules are simply the essential image of
G
-sets inside
\Bbbk G
-modules, under
\Bbbk
-linearization. Extended to bicategories, this takes the following form:

2.12 Proposition

(Linearization)
.

There is a well-defined and canonical pseudo-functor
\Bbbk[-]\colon\mathsf{biset}\to\biperm_{\Bbbk}
mapping a groupoid
G
to itself, a
G,H
-biset
U
to the
G,H
-bimodule
\Bbbk[U]
defined by taking the free
\Bbbk
-module termwise:
(\Bbbk[U])(y,x)=\Bbbk[U(y,x)]
, and extending equivariant maps
\Bbbk
-linearly.

Proof.

This is a well-known phenomenon with groups and it extends to finite groupoids without a wrinkle. For horizontal functoriality, we use the canonical isomorphism
\Bbbk[U\times_{H}V]\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\to}\limits^{\sim}}\Bbbk[U]\otimes_{H}\Bbbk[V]
for every
G,H
-biset
U
and
H,K
-biset
V
, given on basis elements by
[u,v]\mapsto u\otimes v
. Details are left to the reader.
∎

2.13 Definition
.

We denote by
\perm^{\mathsf{rf}}_{\Bbbk}
the 2-full sub-bicategory of
\biperm_{\Bbbk}
with the same objects (finite groupoids) and whose 1-cells are
right-free
permutation bimodules, that is, those which belong to the essential image of the above linearization
\Bbbk[-]
restricted to right-free bisets. In other words, linearization restricts to a canonical pseudo-functor
\Bbbk[-]\colon\mathsf{biset}^{\mathsf{rf}}\to\perm^{\mathsf{rf}}_{\Bbbk}
.
Note that, for
H
a group, a 1-cell
{}_{G}M_{H}
in

is right-free in this sense iff it is free as a right
\Bbbk H
-module.

3.
Hom-decategorification

In this section we show that any Mackey 2-functor provided with a coherent choice of a pair of objects in each of its value categories gives rise to an ordinary Mackey functor of Hom-groups.
This is a sort of ‘decategorification’ procedure, distinct from the more usual
K_{0}
-style decategorification.

For future reference, we work here under more general hypotheses:

3.1 Hypotheses
.

In this section,
(\mathbb{G};\mathbb{J})
denotes a

as in
[
Del22a
, §3]

i.e.
an essentially small extensive (2,1)-category with sufficiently many Mackey squares and coproducts with respect to a 2-subcategory

closed under them and containing all equivalences. The examples to keep in mind are

, used in this section; but

does not necessarily consist of groupoids nor

of faithful 1-morphisms.
The definition of a Mackey 2-functor still makes immediate sense for a general spannable pair
(\mathbb{G};\mathbb{J})
.

3.2 Definition

(Mackey 1-functors)
.

5.
Cohomological Mackey 2-functors

Recall from
1.1
that a (rectified) Mackey 2-functor is
cohomological
if
{}^{\ell}\varepsilon\,{}^{r\!}\eta=[G\!:\!H]\,\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{M}(G)}
for every subgroup inclusion
i\colon H\mathop{\rightarrowtail}G
.
In this section, we provide some familiar examples as well as the first applications of our definition.

5.1 Example

(Representation theory)
.

There are Mackey 2-functors whose value
\mathcal{M}(G)
at a group
G
is either the category of linear representations
\Modcat(\Bbbk G)
, or its derived category
\Der(\Bbbk G)
, or its stable module category
\Stab(\Bbbk G)
(for the third example, one limits the domain to
\mathbb{G}=\mathsf{gpd}{}^{\mathsf{f}}
; see
[
BD20
, Ch. 4.1-2]
for details).
An easy computation with the usual adjunctions shows they are all cohomological.

5.2 Example

(Permutation modules)
.

The full subcategories
\permcat_{\Bbbk}(G)\subseteq\Modcat(\Bbbk G)
of finitely generated permutation
\Bbbk G
-modules form a Mackey sub-2-functor, since inductions and restrictions preserve permutation modules. It is still cohomological.
The same holds for the subcategory
\permcat_{\Bbbk}(G)^{\natural}\subseteq\Modcat(\Bbbk G)
of direct summands of permutation modules.
(In characteristic
p
, these are known as
p
-permutation
modules or
trivial source
modules. But the above makes sense for any ring
\Bbbk
.)

5.3 Example

(Represented 2-functors)
.

Once we develop our motivic theory, we will see that every cohomological Mackey 2-motive represents a cohomological Mackey 2-functor (
6.21
).
The trivial motive
(1,\mathrm{id})
represents the Mackey 2-functor
\permcat_{\Bbbk}(G)^{\natural}
of
5.2
.

5.4 Example

(Equivariant objects)
.

All

-local examples of Mackey 2-functors of equivariant objects from
[
BD20
, Ch. 4.4]
are cohomological, by an easy computation using the concrete adjunctions provided there. (Here as before, ‘
G
-local’ means defined over
{\mathsf{gpd}{}^{\mathsf{f}}_{\!{}^{\scriptscriptstyle/}\!G}}\cong G\textrm{-}\mathrm{set}
rather than
\mathsf{gpd}
;
cf.

3.4
.) These include many geometric examples, such as equivariant coherent sheaves on a noetherian
G
-scheme.

5.5 Example

(Cohomological Mackey functors)
.

There is a cohomological Mackey 2-functor whose value

\mathcal{M}(G)=\CoMack_{\Bbbk}(G)
is
the category of (ordinary) cohomological Mackey functors for

.
To see this, we can apply the construction

BD20

is cohomological then so is
\mathcal{M}

Taking

the (pointwise dual of) the Mackey 2-functor

Let

5.9 Remark

Following up on the previous remark, suppose that

In particular, this means that the categories

After applying

This applies to the representation ring (

5.10 Theorem

(

-local separable monadicity)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a cohomological Mackey 2-functor, and let

be a finite group such that
\mathcal{M}(G)
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-linear category for a prime number

(

is

Since

As

6.

We now turn to

Here we use the results of

Since cohomological Mackey 2-functors are Mackey 2-functors that send some special 2-cells to zero, there is a tautological approach to the bicategory of cohomological Mackey 2-motives: It is the quotient

\qquad{}^{\ell}\varepsilon_{i}\circ{}^{r\!}\eta_{i}-[G\!:\!H]\cdot\mathrm{id}\qquad\in\End_{\Motk}(\mathrm{Id}_{G})

for every inclusion

of a subgroup

in a group

. But this definition is rather sprawling: We need to consider the closure of the above class of 2-cells inside
\Motk
under composition and
\Bbbk
-linear combination inside each Hom category; plus we need to take into account horizontal composition with arbitrary 2-cells, including whiskering. So the tautological definition is unwieldy.

Our

as
(\perm^{\mathsf{rf}}_{\Bbbk})^{\flat}
, the bicategory obtained by block-completing the bicategory of right-free permutation bimodules
\biperm_{\Bbbk}^{\mathsf{rf}}
(

In view of

, it is more convenient to use the model of
\Motk
via bisets, as described in
Section

4
. To do so, we need to translate the 2-cell (
6.1
) under the equivalence of
4.9
.
In view of
4.24
, the image of
{}^{\ell}\varepsilon_{i}\circ{}^{r\!}\eta_{i}-[G\!:\!H]\cdot\mathrm{id}
in the category
\Bbbk\mathsf{bi}\widehat{\mathsf{set}}{}^{\mathsf{rf}}(G,G)
is simply the following linear combination of spans of equivariant maps between right-free

-bisets (with
\mu
induced by multiplication):

(6.2)

\big[G\overset{\mu\,}{\Leftarrow}G\times_{H}G\overset{\mu}{\Rightarrow}G\big]-[G\!:\!H]\cdot\mathrm{id}_{\mathrm{Id}_{G}}.

For simplicity, we call this the

corresponding to

.

Let

be two groups. We can view

-bisets

\mathcal{P}
of the span
\big[\Gamma/K\overset{\pr}{\Leftarrow}\Gamma/(K\cap{}^{\gamma\!}L)\overset{\cdot\gamma\,\,\;}{\Rightarrow}\Gamma/L\big]
is by definition
(\cdot\gamma)_{\star}\pr^{\star}\colon\Bbbk[U]\to\Bbbk[V]
, that is, it maps a generator
[x]\in U=\Gamma/K
to the sum
\sum_{[y]\in\pr^{-1}([x])}[y\gamma]
over its preimages
[y]\in\Gamma/(K\cap{}^{\gamma}L)
, which are precisely those
[y]
such that
[y]=[x]
in
\Gamma/K
.
∎

6.17 Remark

(Yoshida’s Theorem; see
[
Web00
, §7]
)
.

Fix a finite group
G
.
Consider the functor
\mathcal{P}_{1,G}\colon\Bbbk\mathsf{bi}\widehat{\mathsf{set}}{}^{\mathsf{rf}}(1,G)\to\perm^{\mathsf{rf}}_{\Bbbk}(1,G)
, the component functor of our pseudo-functor
\mathcal{P}
at the pair
(1,G)
.
By
4.27
, its source 1-category is

\mathrm{Sp}_{\Bbbk}(G):=\Bbbk\widehat{(G\textrm{-}\mathrm{set})},

the
\Bbbk
-linear span category of finite left
G
-sets,
i.e.
the classical Burnside category for
G
.
Its target is just
\permcat_{\Bbbk}(G)
, the category of finitely generated left permutation
\Bbbk G
-modules. Thus
\mathcal{P}_{1,P}
identifies with ‘Yoshida’s functor’

Y_{G}\colon\mathrm{Sp}_{\Bbbk}(G)\to\permcat_{\Bbbk}(G)

sending a left
G
-set
X
to
\Bbbk[X]
and a span of
G
-maps to the associated sum-over-fibers
\Bbbk G
-linear map.
As we know, this functor is
\Bbbk
-linear, essentially surjective and full, and Yoshida’s Theorem says that an ordinary Mackey functor for

(
i.e.
an additive functor
\mathrm{Sp}_{\Bbbk}(G)\to\Modcat(\Bbbk)
) is cohomological if and only if it factors through
Y_{G}
. Equivalently, this says that the kernel of
Y_{G}
is generated as a
\Bbbk
-linear categorical ideal by the differences

I^{K}_{L}R^{K}_{L}-[K:L]\cdot\mathrm{id}_{K/L}
for all
L\leq K\leq G
.
Thus we can view the results of this section as a categorification of Yoshida’s Theorem.

We now derive from
6.13
the 2-universal property for
\perm^{\mathsf{rf}}_{\Bbbk}
and thus a proof of
1.5
.
The moment has also come to define our model for the bicategory of cohomological Mackey 2-motives.

6.18 Definition
.

We define the

6.19 Definition

Recall the definition of a
cohomological Mackey 2-functor
(

).

\biCoMackk\subset\biMackk\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\bicCoMackk\subset\bicMackk

6.20 Theorem

(Universal property)
.

The pseudo-functor
\cohmot\colon\mathsf{gpd}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\perm^{\mathsf{rf}}_{\Bbbk}
induces by precomposition biequivalences of 2-categories

\PsFun_{\Bbbk}(\perm^{\mathsf{rf}}_{\Bbbk},\mathsf{ADD}_{\Bbbk})\overset{\sim}{\to}\biCoMackk\quad\textrm{and}\quad\PsFun_{\Bbbk}(\CohMotk,\mathsf{ADD}^{\mathsf{ic}}_{\Bbbk})\overset{\sim}{\to}\bicCoMackk

where
\PsFun_{\Bbbk}
denotes 2-categories of
\Bbbk
-linear (hence additive) pseudo-functors, pseudo-natural transformations and modifications. In particular, every idempotent-complete
\Bbbk
-linear cohomological Mackey 2-functor factors uniquely up to isomorphism through
\CohMotk
as claimed in
1.5
.

Proof.

By the universal property of Mackey 2-motives (see
4.7
), the canonical embedding
\mot\colon\mathsf{gpd}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\Bbbk\mathsf{bi}\widehat{\mathsf{set}}{}^{\mathsf{rf}}
induces a biequivalence

\PsFun_{\Bbbk}(\Bbbk\mathsf{bi}\widehat{\mathsf{set}}{}^{\mathsf{rf}},\mathsf{ADD}_{\Bbbk})\overset{\sim}{\to}\biMack_{\Bbbk}.

This restricts to a biequivalence between, on the left,
\Bbbk
-linear pseudo-functors
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
annihilating the cohomological 2-cells (
6.1
),
and, on the right, (rectified) Mackey 2-functors
\mathcal{M}
which are cohomological. Combined with the (evident) biequivalences arising from the 2-universal property of the quotient
\Bbbk\mathsf{bi}\widehat{\mathsf{set}}{}^{\mathsf{rf}}\twoheadrightarrow\Bbbk\mathsf{bi}\widehat{\mathsf{set}}{}^{\mathsf{rf}}/\biker(\mathcal{P})
and from the biequivalence
\overline{\mathcal{P}}\colon\Bbbk\mathsf{bi}\widehat{\mathsf{set}}{}^{\mathsf{rf}}/\biker(\mathcal{P})\overset{\sim}{\to}\perm^{\mathsf{rf}}_{\Bbbk}
of
6.13

Since

is block-complete, the second claimed biequivalence follows readily from the first one by the universal property
[
BD20
, Thm. A.7.23]
of the block-completion

∎

7.
Motivic decompositions

In this section, we take a closer look at the bicategory of
\Bbbk
-linear cohomological Mackey 2-motives
\CohMotk:=(\perm^{\mathsf{rf}}_{\Bbbk})^{\flat}
of
6.18
, in order to compare cohomological and general motives.

Let us start with a few words about motivic decompositions.
Any decomposition of the Mackey 2-motive
\mot(G)\simeq X_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus X_{n}
in
\Motk
can be realized for
any
Mackey 2-functor
\mathcal{M}
as a decomposition of the additive category
\mathcal{M}(G)
:

\mathcal{M}(G)\simeq\widehat{\mathcal{M}}(X_{1})\oplus\ldots\oplus\widehat{\mathcal{M}}(X_{n})

where
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
is the pseudo-functor extending
\mathcal{M}
to Mackey 2-motives. See after (
1.3
).
Motivic decompositions are universal, in that they only depend on the group
G
and not on the particular Mackey 2-functor
\mathcal{M}
.
See
[
BD20
, §7.4-5]
for details.

7.1 Remark
.

In
[
BD20
, Ch. 7.4]
, we found an explicit isomorphism of
\Bbbk
-algebras between the motivic algebra of 2-cells
\End_{\Bbbk\textrm{\sf S}\widehat{\textrm{\sf pan}}{}^{\mathsf{rf}}}(\mathrm{Id}_{G})
of a group
G
and the socalled
crossed Burnside algebra

\mathrm{B}^{\mathrm{c}}_{\Bbbk}(G)
first studied by Yoshida
[
Yos97
]
. Concretely,
\mathrm{B}^{\mathrm{c}}_{\Bbbk}(G)
is a finite free
\Bbbk
-module generated by the set of
G
-conjugacy classes
[H,a]_{G}
of pairs
(H,a)
with
H\leq G
a subgroup and
a\in\mathrm{C}_{G}(H)
an element of the centralizer of
H
, equipped with the (commutative!) multiplication induced by the formula

[K,b]_{G}\cdot[H,a]_{G}=\sum_{[g]\in K\backslash G/H}[K\cap{}^{g}H,bgag^{-1}]_{G}.

Thus every general Mackey 2-motive is equivalent to a direct sum of pairs
(G,e)
with
G
a finite group and
e=e^{2}\in\mathrm{B}^{\mathrm{c}}_{\Bbbk}(G)
an idempotent.

An analogous discussion can be done for
cohomological
motivic decompositions of
\cohmot(G)
in
\CohMotk
. To understand the analogue of the crossed Burnside algebra in the cohomological setting, we need to understand
\End_{\perm^{\mathsf{rf}}_{\Bbbk}}(\mathrm{Id}_{G})
.

7.2 Remark
.

For a finite group
G
, the 2-cell endomorphism
\Bbbk
-algebra in
\perm^{\mathsf{rf}}_{\Bbbk}
can be easily identified with the center of the group algebra
\Bbbk G
:

\End_{\perm^{\mathsf{rf}}_{\Bbbk}}(\mathrm{Id}_{G})\cong\mathrm{Z}(\Bbbk G).

Indeed, if
\varphi
is an equivariant endomorphisms of the bimodule
\mathrm{Id}_{G}={}_{G}\Bbbk G_{G}
, the image
\varphi(1_{G})
determines
\varphi
and belongs to the center, since
g\varphi(1_{G})=\varphi(g)=\varphi(1_{G})g
for all
g\in G
. Conversely, it is clear that any element of the center may serve as
\varphi(1_{G})
.
Hence, by the definition of the block-completion, every cohomological Mackey 2-motive is equivalent to a direct sum of pairs
(G,f)
with
G
a group and
f=f^{2}\in\Bbbk G
a central idempotent.
It is indecomposable if and only if
f
is primitive.

7.3 Remark
.

The decomposition of a Mackey 2-motive into a direct sum of indecomposable ones, both in
\Motk
(as in
7.1
) and in
\CohMotk
(as in
7.2
), is unique up to permutation and equivalence of the factors; see
[
Del22b
, Cor. 7.9]
.

Following the above pattern, we now obtain:

7.4 Theorem
.

If
\mathcal{M}
is any cohomological Mackey 2-functor, its value category at each finite group
G
admits a canonical decomposition into direct factors

\mathcal{M}(G)\cong\bigoplus_{b}\mathcal{M}(G;b)

indexed by the blocks (primitive central idempotents)
b
of the group algebra
\Bbbk G
.

Proof.

Just apply the reasoning of
[
BD20
, § 7.5]
to the motivic decompositions of
7.2
, as explained above.
∎

7.6 Remark
.

Note that
\rho_{G}([H,1]_{G})=[G\!:\!H]\in\Bbbk\subset\Bbbk G
(of course!).
Recall that the ordinary
Burnside ring

\mathrm{B}_{\Bbbk}(G)
identifies with the subalgebra of
\mathrm{B}^{\mathrm{c}}_{\Bbbk}(G)
generated by such basis elements, hence is sent to
\Bbbk
by
\rho_{G}
.
It follows that, on
cohomological
Mackey 2-functors, the idempotents of
\mathrm{B}_{\Bbbk}(G)
do not produce any interesting factor.
This is wrong for
non
-cohomological Mackey 2-functors,
e.g.
for equivariant stable homotopy theory (see
[
BD20
, Ex. 4.3.8]
and
[
GM95
, App. A]
).

Very recently, Oda, Takegahara and Yoshida
[
OTY22
]
have proved explicit refinements of
7.5
for some important cases of the base ring
\Bbbk
, by describing the primitive idempotents of
\mathrm{B}^{\mathrm{c}}_{\Bbbk}(G)
and their behavior under
\rho_{G}
in terms of the structure of
G
. Perhaps not surprisingly, this problem appears to be subtle even for
\Bbbk
a well-behaved local ring, where there are connections with character theory;
cf.

[
Bou03
]
.
On our part, we can offer the following general lifting result:

7.7 Corollary
.

Assume the commutative ring
\Bbbk
is a complete local noetherian ring, for instance, a field.
In this case, the pseudo-functor

\mathcal{P}\colon\Motk\to\CohMotk

is essentially surjective on objects and 1-cells. In particular, it is genuinely a quotient pseudo-functor of
\Bbbk
-linear bicategories – not just up to retracts.

Proof.

By
6.18
and
6.13
, we already know that each object or 1-cell of the block-completion
\CohMotk=(\perm^{\mathsf{rf}}_{\Bbbk})^{\flat}
is a retract of an object or 1-cell in the image of
\mathcal{P}
.
Hence it suffices to show that arbitrary idempotents can be lifted along the algebra morphisms

\mathcal{P}\colon\End_{\Bbbk\textrm{\sf S}\widehat{\textrm{\sf pan}}{}^{\mathsf{rf}}}(S)\to\End_{\perm^{\mathsf{rf}}_{\Bbbk}}(\mathcal{P}(S))

for all 1-cells
S
of
\Bbbk\textrm{\sf S}\widehat{\textrm{\sf pan}}{}^{\mathsf{rf}}\cong\Bbbk\mathsf{bi}\widehat{\mathsf{set}}{}^{\mathsf{rf}}
.
By construction, the latter are surjective (by
6.13
) morphisms of (noncommutative) finite dimensional
\Bbbk
-algebras. For
\Bbbk
as above, the result now follows from the general lifting theorem
[
Lin18
, Thm. 4.7.1]
.
∎

Abstract
We describe a measurement of charge-parity ( C ​ P C\!P ) violation asymmetries in B 0 → η ​ K S 0 ′ B^{0}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}} decays using Belle II data. We consider η → ′ η ( → γ γ ) π π + − \mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\rightarrow\eta(\rightarrow\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}})\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}} and η → ′ ρ ( → π π + ) − γ \mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\rightarrow\rho(\rightarrow\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}})\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}} decays. The data were collected at the SuperKEKB asymmetric-energy e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} collider between the years 2019 and 2022, and contain ( 387 ± 6 ) × 10 6 (387\pm 6)\times 10^{6} bottom-antibottom meson pairs. We reconstruct 829 ± 35 829\pm 35 signal decays and extract the C ​ P C\!P violating parameters from a fit to the distribution of the proper-decay-time difference between the two B B mesons. The measured direct and mixing-induced C ​ P C\!P asymmetries are C η ​ K S 0 ′ = − 0.19 ± 0.08 ± 0.03 C_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}}=-0.19\pm 0.08\pm 0.03 and S η ​ K S 0 ′ = + 0.67 ± 0.10 ± 0.03 S_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}}=+0.67\pm 0.10\pm 0.03 , respectively, where the first uncertainties are statistical and the second are systematic. These results are in agreement with current world averages and standard model predictions.



I
Introduction

In the standard model (SM), the only source of charge-parity (
C\!P
) violation is an irreducible phase in the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) quark-mixing matrix
Cabibbo 1963
;
Kobayashi and Maskawa 1973
.
This phase is measured with high precision in tree-dominated

b\rightarrow{c\bar{c}s}
decays
Adachi et al. 2012
;
Aubert et al. 2009a
;
Aaij et al. 2015
,
e.g.
,

B^{0}\rightarrow\mathit{{J\mspace{-2mu}/\mspace{-2mu}\psi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}K^{0}
.
In contrast,
b\rightarrow{s}q\bar{q}
decays, with
q
indicating
u
,
d
, or
s
quark, are dominated by loop amplitudes, in which additional sources of
C\!P
violation from physics beyond the SM could be involved
Altmannshofer et al. 2019
;
Ed. A. J. Bevan, B. Golob, Th. Mannel, S. Prell, and
B. D. Yabsley 2014
.
A comparison between
C\!P
asymmetries measured precisely in
b\rightarrow{s}q\bar{q}
and
b\rightarrow{c\bar{c}s}
transitions can thus probe non-SM physics.

The
B^{0}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}
decay is of particular interest
due to its relatively large branching fraction
and limited contribution from tree amplitudes compared to other
b\rightarrow{s}q\bar{q}
decays.
The deviation of the mixing-induced
C\!P
asymmetry (
S_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}}
) from
\sin 2\phi_{1}
is expected to be
0.01\pm 0.01

Beneke 2005
, in the SM, where
\phi_{1}\equiv\arg{(-V_{cd}V^{*}_{cb}/V_{td}V^{*}_{tb})}
is an angle of the CKM unitarity triangle
and
V_{ij}
are the CKM matrix elements.
The direct
C\!P
asymmetry (
C_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}}
) is predicted to be zero
pdg
.

Measurements of
C_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}}
and
S_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}}
have been reported by the Belle
Šantelj et al. 2014
and
BaBar
Aubert et al. 2009b
experiments, yielding the current world averages

C_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}}=-0.05\pm 0.04
and
S_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}}=0.63\pm 0.06

Amhis et al. 2023
.
These are the most precise measurements of
C\!P
asymmetries with

b\rightarrow sq\bar{q}
transitions.
However, improved measurements are needed to match the precision of
the theoretical prediction of possible deviations from the SM
Beneke 2005
.
The sensitivity of experiments operating at hadron colliders, such as LHCb, is
limited by the challenge of the reconstruction of neutral final-state particles.

At an
e^{+}e^{-}
flavor-factory,
B^{0}\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}
pairs are produced via the process
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Upsilon(4\mathrm{S})\rightarrow B^{0}\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}
.
We consider the case when one neutral
B
meson (
{B}_{\text{tag}}
) decays into a flavor-specific final state at time
t_{\text{tag}}
, and
the other
B
(
{B}_{\textsl{CP}}
) decays into a
C\!P
eigenstate at time
t_{\textsl{CP}}
.
As the two neutral
B
mesons remain in a quantum-entangled state until one of them decays,
the flavor of
{B}_{\textsl{CP}}
is opposite to that of
{B}_{\text{tag}}
at
t_{\text{tag}}
.
We define
q_{\mathrm{tag}}
as the flavor of
{B}_{\text{tag}}
at
t_{\text{tag}}
, with
q_{\mathrm{tag}}
taking the value
+1\;(-1)
for
B^{0}
(
\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}
).
The decay rate for the
{B}_{\textsl{CP}}
can be given by

\mathcal{P}(\Delta{t},q_{\mathrm{tag}})=\frac{e^{-|\Delta{t}|/\tau_{B^{0}}}}{4\tau_{B^{0}}}\left[1+q_{\mathrm{tag}}\mathcal{A}_{C\!P}(\Delta{t})\right],

(1)

where
\Delta{t}=t_{\textsl{CP}}-t_{\text{tag}}
is the proper-time difference between the
{B}_{\textsl{CP}}
and
{B}_{\text{tag}}
decays,

\tau_{B^{0}}
is the
B^{0}
lifetime, and
\mathcal{A}_{C\!P}
is the time-dependent
C\!P
asymmetry, defined as

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}&{}_{C\!P}(\Delta{t})=\frac{\Gamma(\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}})-\Gamma(B^{0}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}})}{\Gamma(\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}})+\Gamma(B^{0}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}})}\\
&=S_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}}\sin(\Delta{m_{d}}\Delta{t})-C_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}}\cos(\Delta{m_{d}}\Delta{t}),\end{split}

(2)

where
\Delta{m_{d}}
is the mass difference between the two neutral
B
-meson mass eigenstates.

This paper reports a measurement of
C\!P
asymmetries

C_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}}
and
S_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}}

the SuperKEKB asymmetric-energy
e^{+}e^{-}
collider
Akai, Furukawa, and Koiso 2018
,
operating at the
\Upsilon(4\mathrm{S})
resonance.
The total integrated luminosity is
362\pm\pm 2
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
, which corresponds to
387\pm\pm 6\text{\times}{10}^{6}

B\!\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
pairs.

We reconstruct the signal decay (
{B}_{\textsl{CP}}
) by combining the
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}\rightarrow\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}}
candidate with the
\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}
meson reconstructed in two channels,

\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\rightarrow\eta(\rightarrow\gamma\gamma)\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}}
and
\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\rightarrow\rho(\rightarrow\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}})\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
.
We also explore the channel
\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\rightarrow\eta(\rightarrow\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\pi^{0})\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}}

The
{B}_{\text{tag}}
flavor is determined with a flavor tagging algorithm

.
The values of
C_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}}

II
Belle II detector and simulation

Belle II
Abe et al. 2010
is a particle physics experiment operating at the SuperKEKB collider
in Tsukuba, Japan.
Several subsystems, cylindrically arranged around the interaction point, enable reconstruction of heavy flavor particles
and
\tau
leptons produced in energy-asymmetric
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions.
The innermost part of the detector comprises a two-layer silicon pixel
detector (PXD), surrounded by a four-layer double-sided silicon microstrip
detector (SVD). Together, they provide information about the charged particle trajectories (tracks)
and
B
decay positions (vertices). The momenta and charge of charged particles are reconstructed
with a 56-layer central drift chamber (CDC), which is the main tracking subsystem.
Only one sixth of the second PXD layer
is installed for the data analyzed in this paper.

Charged particle identification (PID)
is accomplished by a time-of-propagation counter and an aerogel ring-imaging Cherenkov
counter, located in the barrel and forward-endcap regions, respectively.
The CDC provides additional PID information through the measurement of specific ionization.
An electromagnetic calorimeter (ECL), made of CsI(Tl) crystals, is used for precise
determination of the photon energy and angular coordinates as well as for electron
identification.
The tracking, PID, and ECL subsystems are surrounded by a superconducting solenoid,
providing an axial magnetic field of
1.5\,\text{T}
.
A
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{L}}
and muon identification system is located outside of the magnet and consists of flux-return iron plates interspersed
with resistive plate chambers and plastic scintillators.
The central axis of the solenoid defines the
z
axis of the laboratory frame, pointing approximately in the direction of the electron beam,
with respect to which the polar angle
\theta
is defined.

The analysis strategy is tested and optimized on Monte Carlo simulated event samples before being applied to the data.
Simulation is also used to determine the signal efficiency and the fit model.
Quark-antiquark pairs from
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions are generated using

KKMC

Jadach, Ward, and Wa̧s 2000
with
Pythia8

Sjöstrand et al. 2015
, while hadron
decays are simulated with
EvtGen

Lange 2001
. The detector response and

K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}
decays are simulated using
Geant4

Agostinelli et al. 2003
.
Both data and simulated samples are processed using the Belle II analysis software framework
Kuhr et al. 2019
;
Belle II
Collaboration 2022
.

III
Event reconstruction and selection

The reconstruction of signal candidates starts by reconstructing the
B^{0}
decay products,

\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}[\rightarrow\eta(\rightarrow{\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}})\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}}]
,
\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}[\rightarrow\rho(\rightarrow\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}})\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}]
, and
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}\rightarrow\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}}
.

Charged particles, assumed to be pions, are reconstructed using the tracking algorithm described in Ref.
Bertacchi et al. 2021
,
with measurement points from tracking subdetectors (PXD, SVD, and CDC).
Pions are required to be within the CDC angular acceptance (
17^{\circ}<\theta<150^{\circ}
)
and to have a distance of closest approach from the interaction
point less than 2.0 cm along the
z
axis and less than 0.5 cm in the transverse
plane, in order to reduce contamination from tracks not produced in the collision.
Furthermore, at least one of the two charged pions from
\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}
or
\rho
,
which are used to reconstruct the
{B}_{\textsl{CP}}
decay vertex, is required to have at least one PXD measurement point.

The photons are reconstructed from ECL energy deposits not associated to any track.
They are required to be in the CDC angular acceptance and have energy deposit in more than one ECL crystal.

The
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}
candidates are reconstructed from two oppositely charged pions
coming from the same vertex and required to
have a momentum direction compatible with the direction defined by the
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}
and
{B}_{\textsl{CP}}
decay
vertices (
\cos\alpha>0.99
, where
\alpha
is the angle between the two directions) and have an invariant mass
0.49<m(\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}})<0.51{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
.

For the first decay subchannel, the
\eta\rightarrow\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}

candidates are reconstructed from two photons with energies greater than
150\,\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}
and an invariant mass in the range

0.505<m(\gamma\gamma)<0.580{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
. The candidate
\eta
and two oppositely charged
pions are combined to form an
\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}
, which is retained if its mass satisfies
0.945<m(\eta\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}})<0.970{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
.
The
\eta
mass is constrained to its known value
Workman et al. 2022
to reconstruct the
\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}
candidate.

For
\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\rightarrow\rho\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
subchannel, we require two charged pions to first form a
\rho
candidate.
For this subchannel, the pions are required to satisfy a PID requirement,
computed using all PID capable detectors, which has a
\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\pm}}}
identification efficiency of about 90%,
and a
\mathit{{K}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\pm}}}
mis-identification probability of about 10%.
The pion tracks are also required to have at least 20 measurement points
in the CDC, which is sufficient to provide high efficiency and a low rate of misreconstructed tracks.
The tighter selection for the pions in the second subchannel helps to reduce the larger background as well as the number of misreconstructed signal candidates.
A less restrictive requirement is applied on the dipion mass, due to the broad width of the
\rho
resonance:

0.51<m(\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}})<1.0{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
.
The lower bound of the invariant mass criterion avoids contamination from
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}
decays.
An
\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}
candidate is formed combining a
\rho
and a photon candidate with energy greater than 250
\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}
.
We require the invariant mass to be in the range
0.92<m(\rho\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}})<0.98{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
.
This subchannel has broader
\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}
mass resolution due to lack of constraint of
\rho
mass.

The invariant mass criteria for the
\eta
,
\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}
, and
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}
candidates correspond to approximately
\pm 1.7\sigma_{m}
intervals, where
\sigma_{m}
is the Gaussian mass resolution of each decay mode.

The
\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}
and
K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}
candidates are combined to form a
B^{0}
candidate.
The
B^{0}
vertex is determined by
fitting the entire decay chain with the
TreeFitter
algorithm
Krohn et al. 2020
;
Hulsbergen 2005
,
constraining the mass of all intermediate mesons to their known values
Workman et al. 2022
, except for
the
\rho
, due to its large width, and requiring the fit to converge.
The momenta of all particles are updated after this vertex fit.
The
B^{0}
vertex is constrained to point back,
along the direction of the reconstructed
B^{0}
momentum,
to the interaction region,
calibrated with
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
.

For each
B
candidate, the beam-energy constrained mass

M_{\text{bc}}=\sqrt{E^{*2}_{\text{beam}}/c^{4}-p^{*2}_{B}/c^{2}}
and energy difference
\Delta{E}=E^{*}_{B}-E^{*}_{\text{beam}}
are calculated,
where
(E,p)^{*}_{B}
is the four-momentum of the
B
candidate and
E^{*}_{\text{beam}}
is the beam energy,
both calculated in the center-of-mass frame.
For correctly reconstructed signal events,
M_{\text{bc}}
peaks at the
B^{0}
invariant mass and
\Delta{E}
at zero.
The requirements
M_{\text{bc}}>5.2\penalty\ {\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
and
|\Delta{E}|<0.2\penalty\ \mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
are applied.

In data, the average
B
candidate multiplicity for events with at least one reconstructed candidate
is about 1.4 for
B^{0}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}[\rightarrow\eta(\rightarrow\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}})\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}}]K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}
and 1.8 for
B^{0}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}[\rightarrow\rho\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}]K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}
.
The difference is due to the presence of an intermediate, narrow resonance (
\eta
) in the first subchannel.
If multiple
B
candidates are present in an event, the one with the smallest
B^{0}
vertex
\chi^{2}
value is
retained. In the simulation, this criterion selects the correct candidate in more than
99\%
of the cases when a true one is reconstructed.

The reconstruction efficiencies, determined using simulation, are
28.3\%
and
19.2\%
for
B^{0}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}[\rightarrow\eta(\rightarrow\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}})\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}}]K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}
and
B^{0}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}[\rightarrow\rho\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}]K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}
, respectively.
The lower efficiency for the second subchannel is mostly due to PID requirements applied to the two pions from the
\rho
decay in order to suppress background.

The control channel
B^{+}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\mathit{{K}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}
uses an
\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}
reconstructed as described above and a charged kaon candidate
with
\theta<136^{\circ}
,
excluding candidates
in the backward part of the detector, where the background is higher. The PID selection for the
\mathit{{K}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}
candidate
has an efficiency of about
90\%
for kaons, and a misidentification probability for pions of about
5\%
.
In the control channel, the
\mathit{{K}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}
is not used for the
B^{+}
vertex determination in order to have a vertex resolution similar
to that of the signal channel.

The
{B}_{\text{tag}}
candidate is reconstructed using all the charged particles that are not associated to
{B}_{\textsl{CP}}
, having
measurement points both in the SVD and CDC, and a momentum greater than 50
{\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c}
.
The RAVE algorithm is used to reconstruct the
{B}_{\text{tag}}
vertex
Waltenberger 2011
.
This algorithm downweights tracks with large contributions to the vertex
\chi^{2}
, which are likely to originate from decays of secondary long-lived charm hadrons.
The decay position of
{B}_{\text{tag}}
is
determined by constraining its direction, as determined from its decay vertex and the interaction point, to be collinear
with its momentum vector, reconstructed from the
{B}_{\textsl{CP}}
and momenta of the colliding beams
Dey and Soffer 2020
.

The proper-decay-time difference
\Delta{t}
between the two
B
mesons is determined from the positions of their
reconstructed vertices along the Lorentz boost axis,

\Delta{t}=\Delta{z}/\beta\gamma\gamma^{*}{c}
, where
\beta\gamma=0.287
is the boost of the
\Upsilon(4\mathrm{S})
with respect to the laboratory frame,
and
\gamma^{*}=1.002
is the Lorentz factor of the
B
meson in the center-of-mass frame.
We reject poorly reconstructed events requiring
|\Delta{t}|<8\penalty\ \textrm{ps}
and
require the per-event uncertainty
\sigma_{\Delta{t}}
to be less than
2\penalty\ \textrm{ps}
.

The main source of background is random combinations of tracks and photons that arise from continuum
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow q\bar{q}
(
q=u,d,s,c
) events.
A boosted-decision-tree (BDT) classifier
Keck 2017
is trained using 26
event-shape variables to separate jet-like continuum events from more spherical
B\!\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
topologies.
The variables, in order of decreasing discriminating power, are the cosine of the angle
between the
{B}_{\textsl{CP}}
and
{B}_{\text{tag}}
thrust axes
Brandt et al. 1964
, the cosine of the angle between the
{B}_{\textsl{CP}}
thrust axis and the
z
axis,
the ratio of the second to the zeroth Fox–Wolfram moments
Fox and Wolfram 1978
,
the modified Fox–Wolfram moments
Lee et al. 2003
,
the
{B}_{\text{tag}}
thrust magnitude, and the CLEO cones
Asner et al. 1996
.
Variables that exhibit correlations greater than 10% with those used for the
C\!P
asymmetry measurement (
M_{\text{bc}}
,
\Delta{E}
,
\Delta{t}
, and
\sigma_{\Delta{t}}
) are excluded.
The BDT training is performed using simulated signal events and data in the sidebands (
M_{\text{bc}}<5.27{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}

and
\Delta{E}<-0.07
or
>0.05\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
), which are dominated by continuum background.
As a consistency check, we repeat the training with the off-resonance data collected 60
\mathrm{\,Me\kern-1.00006ptV}
below the

\Upsilon(4\mathrm{S})
resonance, for an integrated luminosity of
42\penalty\ \mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
, and find the results to be in agreement with those obtained with the nominal training.
A high-efficiency selection on the BDT output
C_{\rm BDT}
is applied, retaining about 95% of the signal while suppressing 60% of the continuum background.

IV
Time-dependent
C\!P
asymmetry fit

The parameters
C_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}}
and
S_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}}
are extracted with an extended unbinned
maximum-likelihood fit using the
M_{\text{bc}}
,
\Delta{E}
,
C_{\rm BDT}
,
\Delta{t}
, and tag-flavor
q_{\mathrm{tag}}
observables,
plus
\sigma_{\Delta{t}}
as a conditional observable for
\Delta{t}
resolution function.
The first three observables provide discrimination between signal and continuum background.
The
\Delta{t}
and
q_{\mathrm{tag}}
ones provide access to time-dependent
C\!P
asymmetry.
Four different sample components are considered: signal;
self-cross-feed (SxF), where a signal decay is
misreconstructed, mostly due to wrong
\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
associations; background from
continuum; and background from
B\!\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
.
The SxF sample amounts to about 5% of the signal while
B\!\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
amounts to about 1% of the continuum.

The fit is performed in two steps.
In the first step the shapes and yields of all sample components are reliably determined in a time-independent fit,
using
M_{\text{bc}}
,
\Delta{E}
, and
C_{\rm BDT}
distributions in the region
M_{\text{bc}}>5.2{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
and
|\Delta{E}|<0.2\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
,
which in turn simplifies the time-dependent fit of the second step.
In the first step most parameters of the signal and all those of the continuum models are allowed to vary, as well as the yields of signal and continuum.
The SxF shape is fixed from simulated events, as well as its normalization relative to the signal component.
The shape and yields of the
B\!\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
component are fixed from simulation, as their contribution is too small to be determined from data.

The second step of the fit uses
\Delta{t}
and
q_{\mathrm{tag}}
in addition to the three observables utilized in the first step,
and is performed only in the signal region, defined by
M_{\text{bc}}>5.27{\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV\!/}c^{2}}
and
-0.07<\Delta{E}<0.05\mathrm{\,Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}
, where approximately
98\%
of signal is present.
In this region the SxF amounts to about 3% of signal.
In this step, the shapes for
M_{\text{bc}}
,
\Delta{E}
, and
C_{\rm BDT}
as well as the yields of all components are fixed from the previous one,
so the only free parameters are
C_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}}
and
S_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}}
.

The
{B}_{\text{tag}}
flavor is determined using the flavor tagging algorithm described in Ref.
Abudinén et al. 2022
,
which uses the properties of particles not associated with the
{B}_{\textsl{CP}}
.
The algorithm provides the flavor
q_{\mathrm{tag}}
and the tagging quality
r=(1-2w)
, where
w
is the mistagging probability.
The range of
r
varies from
r=0
(no flavor information can be obtained) to
r=1
(corresponding to unambiguous flavour determination).

Both fit steps are performed simultaneously in seven subsets of data (bins) selected according to tagging quality
r
,
with boundaries set at 0, 0.1, 0.25, 0.45, 0.6, 0.725, 0.875, and 1, to gain statistical sensitivity from events with different wrong-tag fractions.
In the first step, the signal and continuum yields of each bin are varied independently.

Each observable is modeled independently and hence the total probability density function (PDF)
is the product of the four independent PDFs,
as shown in Eq.
3
and
4
:

\begin{split}\text{Model}(M_{\text{bc}},\Delta{E},&C_{\rm BDT},\Delta{t})=\\
&\mathcal{F}(M_{\text{bc}},\Delta{E},C_{\rm BDT})\cdot\mathcal{P}(\Delta{t}),\\
\end{split}

(3)

with:

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}(M_{\text{bc}},&\Delta{E},C_{\rm BDT})=\\
&\text{pdf}(M_{\text{bc}})\cdot\text{pdf}(\Delta{E})\cdot\text{pdf}(C_{\rm BDT}),\\
\end{split}

(4)

where
\mathcal{F}
represents the time-independent PDF, and pdf is the PDF used to model each variable as described below. The function
\mathcal{P}
represents the time-dependent PDF, also described below.

Correlations among observables are considered as a source of systematic uncertainty.
The largest linear correlation is between
M_{\text{bc}}
and
\Delta{E}
(10% for
B^{0}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}[\rightarrow\eta(\rightarrow\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}})\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}}]K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}
and 20% for
B^{0}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}[\rightarrow\rho\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}]K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}
, respectively),
due to the presence of photons in the final state. Linear correlations between other observables are smaller than 5%.

The
M_{\text{bc}}
distribution is modeled with a sum of two Gaussian functions with a common mean for signal,
a Crystal Ball function
Gaiser 1982
;
Skwarnicki 1986
;
Oreglia et al. 1982
for SxF,
an ARGUS function
Albrecht et al. 1990
for continuum, and an ARGUS plus a Gaussian function for
B\!\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
.
In the case of
\Delta{E}
, two Gaussian functions are used for signal and SxF, with the addition of a linear function for
B^{0}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}[\rightarrow\rho\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}]K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}
.
For continuum, we use an exponential for
B^{0}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}(\rightarrow\eta(\rightarrow\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}})\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}})K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}\penalty\
and the sum of an exponential and a wide Gaussian function for
B^{0}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}[\rightarrow\rho\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}]K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}
.
For
B\!\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
, we use an exponential plus a Gaussian function.
For
C_{\rm BDT}
, the sum of an asymmetric and a regular Gaussian function is used for signal, SxF, and
B\!\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
, and two Gaussian functions for continuum.

In the first step of the fit, for signal PDF we fix from simulation the
sigma of the wider Gaussian functions for
M_{\text{bc}}
and
\Delta{E}
, whose fractions
are about
2\%
and
10\%
of the total PDF for
M_{\text{bc}}
and
\Delta{E}
, respectively. For
C_{\rm BDT}
, we fix the mean
and sigma of the wider regular Gaussian function (fraction about

20\%
). The evaluation of the systematic uncertainties associated with fixed parameters is described in Sec.
V
.

The
\Delta{t}
model for the signal and SxF components is derived from Eq.
1
.
Taking into account the probability of assigning the wrong flavor,
w
, its difference between
B^{0}
and
\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}
,
\Delta{w}
,
and the tagging efficiency asymmetry for
B^{0}
and
\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}
,
a_{\epsilon}^{\rm tag}
, Eq.
1
becomes

\begin{split}\mathcal{P}(&\Delta{t},q_{\mathrm{tag}})=\frac{e^{-|\Delta{t}|/\tau_{B^{0}}}}{4\tau_{B^{0}}}\Biggl\{1-q_{\mathrm{tag}}\Delta{w}+q_{\mathrm{tag}}a_{\epsilon}^{\rm tag}(1-2w)+\\
&\left[q_{\mathrm{tag}}(1-2w)+a_{\epsilon}^{\rm tag}(1-q_{\mathrm{tag}}\Delta{w})\right]\left[S_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}}\sin(\Delta{m_{d}}\Delta{t})-C_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}}\cos(\Delta{m_{d}}\Delta{t})\right]\Biggr\}.\\
\end{split}

(5)

The effect of detector resolution on
\Delta{t}
changes Eq.
5
to

\mathcal{P}_{\rm exp}(\Delta{t},q_{\mathrm{tag}})=\int\mathcal{P}(\Delta{t^{\prime}},q_{\mathrm{tag}})\mathcal{R}(\Delta{t}-\Delta{t^{\prime}}|\sigma_{\Delta{t}})d\Delta{t^{\prime}},

(6)

where
\mathcal{R}
is the resolution function for
\Delta{t}
conditional on
\sigma_{\Delta{t}}
.
The
\mathcal{R}
function has been determined in data using
B^{0}\rightarrow D^{(*)-}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}
decays and it is described in details in Ref.
Abudinén et al. 2023
.
Like the resolution function, also the flavor tagging parameters
w
,
\Delta{w}
, and
a_{\epsilon}^{\rm tag}
are extracted from data using flavor-specific
B^{0}\rightarrow D^{(*)-}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}
decays.
In simulation all these parameters from
B^{0}\rightarrow D^{(*)-}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}
are compatible with our expectations based on signal.

The
\Delta{t}
distribution for the continuum component is modeled with three Gaussian functions and
is determined using the data sidebands described earlier.
The
\Delta{t}
distribution of the
B\!\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
background is also modeled with the sum of three Gaussian functions and a component with an effective lifetime, that accounts for the sizable
B
lifetime, convolved with the same three Gaussian functions. Its parameters are determined from simulation.

The complete PDF used for the likelihood fit is the following:

\begin{split}\text{pdf}\biggl(M_{\text{bc}},\Delta{E}&,C_{\rm BDT},\Delta{t},q_{tag};C_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}},S_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}}\biggr)=\\
Y_{\text{sig}}\biggl[&\bigl\{\mathcal{F}_{\text{sig}}(M_{\text{bc}},\Delta{E},C_{\rm BDT})+f_{\text{SxF}}\mathcal{F}_{\text{SxF}}(M_{\text{bc}},\Delta{E},C_{\rm BDT})\bigr\}\cdot\mathcal{P}_{\text{exp}}(\Delta{t},q_{\text{tag}};C_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}},S_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}})\biggr]+\\
Y_{\text{cont}}\biggl[&\mathcal{F}_{\text{cont}}(M_{\text{bc}},\Delta{E},C_{\rm BDT})\mathcal{P}_{\text{cont}}(\Delta{t})+f_{B\!\kern 1.47495pt\overline{\kern-1.47495ptB}{}}\mathcal{F}_{B\!\kern 1.47495pt\overline{\kern-1.47495ptB}{}}(M_{\text{bc}},\Delta{E},C_{\rm BDT})\mathcal{P}_{B\!\kern 1.47495pt\overline{\kern-1.47495ptB}{}}(\Delta{t})\biggr],\\
\end{split}

(7)

where
Y_{\text{sig/cont}}
is the yield of the corresponding component,
f_{\text{SxF}}
is the fraction of SxF with respect to signal, and
f_{B\!\kern 1.47495pt\overline{\kern-1.47495ptB}{}}
is the fraction of
B\!\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
with respect to continuum.
Combined PDFs for time-independent observables are represented by
\mathcal{F}_{\text{x}}
(Eq.
4
) for each component x, and the time-dependent one by
\mathcal{P}_{\text{x}}
(Eq.
6
).

using a simultaneous fit to both
\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}

.

The fit is further validated measuring the
C\!P
asymmetry on the charged

with a simultaneous fit to the two
\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}

The same flavor tagger used for the neutral channels is used also for the control ones.
In the signal region we find
1345\pm 39

with purities (which is the ratio of signal events over the total number of events in the signal region)

, respectively.

VI
Summary

A measurement of
C\!P
asymmetries in
B\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}
decays
is conducted using
e^{+}e^{-}
collision data collected in 2019–-2022 by the Belle II experiment at the SuperKEKB collider.
We find
829\pm 35
signal decays in a sample of
387\pm\pm 6\text{\times}{10}^{6}

B\!\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
events and measure the
C\!P
asymmetries to be

\begin{split}C_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}}&=-0.19\pm 0.08\pm 0.03,\\
S_{\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}\!K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}}&=+0.67\pm 0.10\pm 0.03,\\
\end{split}

(8)

where the first uncertainties are statistical and the second systematic.
This measurement is based on the subchannels
B^{0}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}[\rightarrow\eta(\rightarrow\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}})\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}}]K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}
and
B^{0}\rightarrow\mathit{{\eta}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{\prime}}}[\rightarrow\rho\mathit{{\gamma}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}]K^{0}_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle\textsl{S}}
.

This is the first measurement of
C\!P
violation in this channel at Belle II.
The results are in agreement with the current world averages,
and have sensitivities close to those of Belle
Šantelj et al. 2014
and BaBar
Aubert et al. 2009b
, despite the smaller data size.

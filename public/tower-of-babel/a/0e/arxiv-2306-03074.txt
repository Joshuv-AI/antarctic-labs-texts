A General Perspective on Objectives of Reinforcement Learning


Abstract
In this lecture, we present a general perspective on reinforcement learning (RL) objectives, where we show three versions of objectives. The first version is the standard definition of objective in RL literature. Then we extend the standard definition to the λ \lambda -return version, which unifies the standard definition of objective. Finally, we propose a general objective that unifies the previous two versions. The last version provides a high level to understand of RL’s objective, where it shows a fundamental formulation that connects some widely used RL techniques (e.g., TD ( λ ) (\lambda) and GAE), and this objective can be potentially applied to extensive RL algorithms.

Introduction

Although reinforcement learning (RL) is widely applied to extensive fields, there is stills lack a work that establishes the objective of starting from RL from the Markov decision process, which is very unfriendly to beginners.
To fill the gap in this view, in this lecture, we provide a self-contained, teachable technical introduction to the objectives of RL, where each section tackles a particular line of work from the transition probability matrix over the Markov decision process, reward, Bellman equation, discounted state distribution, and objectives.

Concretely, this lecture provides three equivalent versions of objectives.
The first version is presented in Theorem
2.3
, where it shows the objective as the expectation with respect to the random variable
(s,a,s^{{}^{\prime}})
.
Theorem
2.3
illustrates all the random factors in the Markov decision process (MDP), and we refer to it as the
standard objective
of MDP.
Furthermore, Theorem
3.3
extends and unifies the objective that appears in Theorem
2.3
.
Theorem
3.3
is traceable to TD
(\lambda)

(
Sutton 1984
;
Sutton 1988
)
, and we present it as the expectation with respect to the random variable the state
s
, where the state
s
follows the
\lambda
-version of discounted state distribution.
Finally, we present a general objective that unifies the previous two versions (see Theorem
4.1
), which provides a high level to understand of RL’s objective, where it shows a fundamental formulation that connects some widely used RL techniques (e.g., TD
(\lambda)
and GAE), and this objective can be potentially applied to extensive RL algorithms.
For example,
Yang et al. 2022
apply the main technique of Theorem
4.1
to obtain the surrogate function with respect to GAE
(
Schulman et al. 2016
)
.
Although GAE has been widely used in RL, it lacks a theoretical analysis of the related algorithms.
Theorem
4.1
provides a possible way to establish GAE and empirical results by rigorous analysis.
To clarify this view, we present a surrogate function with respect to GAE, see Section
4.3
, where it provides a theoretical fundament for policy optimization with GAE.

An MDP is described as a tuple

•

•

•

•

•

The probability and reward satisfy Markov property, i.e.,
\mathbb{P}(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s,a)

A stationary Markov policy

,

We use

2.2
Multi-Step State Transition Probability Matrix

We are interested in the state distribution induced by a policy.
Recall the following visitation sequence
\tau=\{s_{t},a_{t},r_{t+1}\}_{t\geq 0}

{\pi}

\mathbb{P}_{\pi}(s_{t}=s|s_{0})

Particularly, we notice if

\mathbb{P}_{\pi}(s_{t}=s|s_{0})=0

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\pi}(s_{t}=s|s_{0})=0,~~t=0~\text{and}~s\neq s_{0}.

(3)

In this lecture, to simplify the expressions, we also use
\mathbb{P}^{(t)}_{{\pi}}
to denote the notation
\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{t}=s^{{}^{\prime}}|s_{0}=s)

s_{0}\sim\rho_{0}(\cdot)

\mathbb{P}^{(t)}_{{\pi}}(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s)=:\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{t}=s^{{}^{\prime}}|s_{0}=s).

Furthermore, we use
\mathbf{P}^{(t)}_{\pi}
to denote the

{\pi}

\mathbf{P}^{(t)}_{\pi}[s,s^{{}^{\prime}}]=\mathbb{P}^{(t)}_{{\pi}}(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s).

In order to express the above stochastic process more vividly, we introduce a chain (induced by
\pi
) as follows,

\displaystyle s_{0}\overset{a_{0}\sim{\pi}(\cdot|s_{0})}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}\{r_{1},s_{1}\}\overset{a_{1}\sim{\pi}(\cdot|s_{1})}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}\{r_{2},s_{2}\}\overset{a_{2}\sim{\pi}(\cdot|s_{2})}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 28.45274pt}}\{r_{3},s_{3}\}\cdots.

(4)

Particularly, for the chain (
4

, the following equity holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{t}=s|s_{0})=

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{t}=s|s_{t-1}=s^{{}^{\prime}})\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{t-1}=s^{{}^{\prime}}|s_{0}),

(5)

we will show it later.
Due to the Markov property, we know

\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{t}=s|s_{t-1}=s^{{}^{\prime}})=\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s|s^{{}^{\prime}}),

then, we rewrite (
5
) as follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{t}=s|s_{0})=

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s|s^{{}^{\prime}})\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{t-1}=s^{{}^{\prime}}|s_{0}),

(6)

which can be rewritten as the following concise formulation,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}^{(t)}(s|s_{0})=

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s|s^{{}^{\prime}})\mathbb{P}^{(t-1)}_{{\pi}}(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s_{0}).

(7)

Eq.(
7

, which illustrates the relationship between single-step state transition probability and multi-step state transition probability.

Theorem 2.1

(Chapman-Kolmogorov Equation)
.

Let
\mathbb{P}^{(t)}_{{\pi}}(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s)
be the probability of transition from state
s
to state
s^{{}^{\prime}}
after
t

additional steps by executing a stationary Markovian policy
\pi
, and its corresponding
t
-step transition matrix is
\mathbf{P}^{(t)}_{\pi}
.
Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}^{(t)}(s|s_{0})=

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s|s^{{}^{\prime}})\mathbb{P}^{(t-1)}_{{\pi}}(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s_{0}).

(8)

Furthermore, we know

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{{\pi}}^{(t)}=\mathbf{P}_{{\pi}}^{t}.

(9)

Proof.
We only need to show the result (
5
), we give a simple derivation of (
5
) follows
Weng 2018
,

•

For the case
t=0
,

\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{t}=s|s_{0})=\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{0}=s|s_{0})=1,

which is a trivial fact.

•

For the case
t=1
, we know

\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{1}=s|s_{0})=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}{\pi}(a|s_{0})\mathbb{P}(s|s_{0},a)=:\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi(\cdot|s_{0})}[\mathbb{P}(s|s_{0},a)],

which is reduced to single state transition probability by executing
{\pi}
, and it is same with the result of (
5
). In fact, since the chain (
4
) starts from the initial state
s_{0}
, then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{1}=s|s_{0}=s^{{}^{\prime}})\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{0}=s^{{}^{\prime}}|s_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}-\{s_{0}\}}\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{1}=s|s_{0}=s^{{}^{\prime}})\underbrace{\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{t-1}=s^{{}^{\prime}}|s_{0})}_{=0,~\text{if}~t=1;\tau~\text{starts~from}~s_{0}}+\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{1}=s|s_{0})\underbrace{\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{t-1}=s_{0}|s_{0})}_{=1;~\text{if}~t=1}.

•

For the general case time
t
,
we can first travel from
s_{0}
to a middle point
s^{{}^{\prime}}
(any state can be a middle point), after

steps, and then go to the final state

during the last step. In this way, we are able to update the visitation probability recursively as (
5

2.3
Discounted State Distribution

Let
d_{{\pi}}^{s_{0}}(s)
denote the normalized discounted weighting of the future state
s
encountered starting at
s_{0}
by executing
{\pi}
,

\displaystyle d_{{\pi}}^{s_{0}}(s)=(1-\gamma)\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{t}=s|s_{0}).

(10)

Furthermore, since
s_{0}\sim\rho_{0}(\cdot)
, we define

\displaystyle d_{{\pi}}^{\rho_{0}}(s)=\mathbb{E}_{s_{0}\sim\rho_{0}(\cdot)}[d_{{\pi}}^{s_{0}}(s)]=\sum_{s_{0}\in\mathcal{S}}\rho_{0}(s_{0})d^{s_{0}}_{{\pi}}(s)=\int_{s_{0}\in\mathcal{S}}\rho_{0}(s_{0})d^{s_{0}}_{{\pi}}(s)\text{d}s_{0}

(11)

as the discounted state visitation distribution over the initial distribution
\rho_{0}(\cdot)
.

We use
\mathbf{d}_{{\pi}}^{\rho_{0}}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}
to collect all the normalized discounted state distributions, and its components are:

\mathbf{d}_{{\pi}}^{\rho_{0}}[s]=d_{{\pi}}^{\rho_{0}}(s),~~s\in\mathcal{S}.

Recall
\mathbf{P}_{\pi}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|\times|\mathcal{S}|}
denotes the one-step state transition matrix by executing
\pi
, and
we use
\bm{\rho}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}
denotes the initial state distribution vector, and their components are:

\mathbf{P}_{\pi}[s,s^{\prime}]=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a|s)\mathbb{P}(s^{\prime}|s,a),~~\bm{\rho}_{0}[s]=\rho_{0}(s).

Then, we rewrite
\mathbf{d}_{{\pi}}^{\rho_{0}}
as a matrix version as follows,

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}_{{\pi}}^{\rho_{0}}=(1-\gamma)\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}(\gamma\mathbf{P}_{\pi})^{t}\bm{\rho}_{0}=(1-\gamma)(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}_{\pi})^{-1}\bm{\rho}_{0}.

(12)

2.5
Value Function

The
state value function
of
\pi
is defined as

\displaystyle V_{\pi}(s)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}r_{t+1}\Big|s_{0}=s\right],

(16)

where
\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\cdot|\cdot]
denotes a conditional expectation on actions which are selected by
\pi
.
Its
state-action value function
is

\displaystyle Q_{\pi}(s,a)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}r_{t+1}\Big|s_{0}=s,a_{0}=a\right],

(17)

and advantage function is

\displaystyle A_{\pi}(s,a)=Q_{\pi}(s,a)-V_{\pi}(s).

(18)

2.6
Bellman Equation

Bellman equation illustrates the relationship between the states’ values and actions, which plays a central role in MDP theory and reinforcement learning.

Theorem 2.2

(Bellman Equation)
.

The state value function
V_{\pi}(s)
and state-action value function
Q_{\pi}(s,a)
satisfy the following equation:

\displaystyle V_{\pi}(s)=

\displaystyle R_{\pi}(s)+\gamma\sum_{s^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}_{\pi}(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s)V_{\pi}(s^{{}^{\prime}}),

(19)

\displaystyle Q_{\pi}(s,a)=

\displaystyle R(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\sum_{a^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbb{P}(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s,a)\pi(a^{{}^{\prime}}|s^{{}^{\prime}})Q_{\pi}(s^{{}^{\prime}},a^{{}^{\prime}}).

(20)

Proof.

First, we notice

\displaystyle G_{t}

\displaystyle=:r_{t+1}+\gamma r_{t+2}+\gamma^{2}r_{t+3}+\cdots

\displaystyle=r_{t+1}+\gamma G_{t+1}.

Then we rewrite the state value function as follows

\displaystyle V_{\pi}(s)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}r_{t+1}\Big|s_{0}=s\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[r_{1}+\gamma G_{1}|s_{0}=s\right].

For the first term, we know

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[r_{1}|s_{0}=s]=\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi(\cdot|s),s^{{}^{\prime}}\sim\mathbb{P}(\cdot|s,a)}[r(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s,a)]=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{s^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\pi(a|s)\mathbb{P}(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s,a)r(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s,a).

(21)

For the second term, we know

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[G_{1}|s_{0}=s]=

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}_{\pi}(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s)\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[G_{1}|s_{0}=s,s_{1}=s^{{}^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}_{\pi}(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s)\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[G_{1}|s_{1}=s^{{}^{\prime}}\right]

(22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}_{\pi}(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s)V_{\pi}(s^{{}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{s^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\pi(a|s)\mathbb{P}(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s,a)V_{\pi}(s^{{}^{\prime}}),

(23)

where Eq.(
22
) holds due to the conditional independence as follows,

\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[G_{1}|s_{1}=s^{{}^{\prime}}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[G_{1}|s_{1}=s^{{}^{\prime}},s_{0}=s\right].

Such conditional independence property is due to the memoryless Markov property that the future behavior totally depends on the current state.

Then, combining (
21
) and (
22
), we obtain the
Bellman equaiton
as follows,

\displaystyle V_{\pi}(s)=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{s^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\pi(a|s)\mathbb{P}(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s,a)r(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s,a)}_{\text{mean~of~current~rewards}}+\underbrace{\gamma\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{s^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\pi(a|s)\mathbb{P}(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s,a)V_{\pi}(s^{{}^{\prime}})}_{\text{mean~of~future~rewards}}

(24)

\displaystyle\overset{(\ref{reward-wrt-policy})}{=}

\displaystyle R_{\pi}(s)+\gamma\sum_{s^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}_{\pi}(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s)V_{\pi}(s^{{}^{\prime}}).

(25)

Similarly, we know the state-action value function version of the Bellman equation.

\Box

Finally, we use
\mathbf{v}_{\pi}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}
to collect all the state value functions, and each entry of
\mathbf{v}_{\pi}
is defined as

\mathbf{v}_{\pi}[s]=V_{{\pi}}(s),

then we rewrite the Bellman equation as the following matrix version:

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{\pi}=\mathbf{r}_{\pi}+\gamma\mathbf{P}_{\pi}\mathbf{v}_{\pi}=(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}_{\pi})^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{\pi}.

(26)

2.7
Objective of Reinforcement Learning

Recall
\tau=\{s_{t},a_{t},r_{t+1}\}_{t\geq 0}\sim{\pi}
, according to
\tau
,
we define the expected return
J(\pi|s_{0})
by

\displaystyle J(\pi|s_{0})=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim{\pi}}[R(\tau)]=V_{\pi}(s_{0}),

(27)

where
R(\tau)=\sum_{t\geq 0}\gamma^{t}r_{t+1}
, and the notation
J(\pi|s_{0})
is “conditional” on
s_{0}
is to emphasize the trajectory
\tau
starting from
s_{0}
.
Let

\displaystyle J({\pi})=\mathbb{E}_{s_{0}\sim\rho_{0}(\cdot)}[J(\pi|s_{0})]=\mathbb{E}_{s_{0}\sim\rho_{0}(\cdot)}[V_{\pi}(s_{0})]=\sum_{s_{0}\in\mathcal{S}}\rho_{0}(s_{0})V_{\pi}(s_{0}).

(28)

The goal of reinforcement learning is to search the optimal policy
\pi_{\star}
satisfies

\displaystyle\pi_{\star}=\arg\max_{\pi}J(\pi).

(29)

To see the objective (
28
) clearly, we use rewrite
J(\pi)
with respect to
\mathbf{d}_{{\pi}}^{\rho_{0}}
.

Theorem 2.3
.

The objective
J(\pi)
shares the following versions

\displaystyle J({\pi})=

\displaystyle\sum_{s_{0}\in\mathcal{S}}\rho_{0}(s_{0})V_{\pi}(s_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\sum_{s_{0}\in\mathcal{S}}\rho_{0}(s_{0})\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}d^{s_{0}}_{\pi}(s)R_{{\pi}}(s)

(30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\pi}^{\rho_{0}}(\cdot),a\sim\pi(\cdot|s),s^{{}^{\prime}}\sim\mathbb{P}(\cdot|s,a)}\left[r(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s,a)\right].

(31)

Furthermore, the matrix version is

\displaystyle J(\pi)=\bm{\rho}_{0}^{\top}\mathbf{v}_{\pi}=\bm{\rho}_{0}^{\top}(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}_{\pi})^{-1}\mathbf{r}_{\pi}=\dfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\left\langle\mathbf{d}_{\pi}^{\rho_{0}},\mathbf{r}_{\pi}\right\rangle.

(32)

Proof.

Recall the Bellman equation, we obtain

\displaystyle V_{{\pi}}(s_{0})=

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}{\pi}(a|s_{0})R(s_{0},a)+\gamma\sum_{s^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{1}=s^{{}^{\prime}}|s_{0})V_{{\pi}}(s^{{}^{\prime}}),

(33)

and we unroll the expression of (
33
) repeatedly, then we have

\displaystyle V_{{\pi}}(s_{0})=

\displaystyle R_{{\pi}}(s_{0})+\gamma\sum_{s^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{1}=s^{{}^{\prime}}|s_{0})\underbrace{\left(R_{\pi}(s^{{}^{\prime}})+\gamma\sum_{s^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{2}=s^{{}^{\prime\prime}}|s_{1}=s^{{}^{\prime}})V_{{\pi}}(s^{{}^{\prime\prime}})\right)}_{=V_{{\pi}}(s^{{}^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{{\pi}}(s_{0})+\gamma\sum_{s^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{1}=s^{{}^{\prime}}|s_{0})R_{\pi}(s^{{}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+\gamma^{2}\sum_{s^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\underbrace{\left(\sum_{s^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{1}=s^{{}^{\prime}}|s_{0})\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{2}=s^{{}^{\prime\prime}}|s_{1}=s^{{}^{\prime}})\right)}_{=\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{2}=s^{{}^{\prime\prime}}|s_{0})}V_{{\pi}}(s^{{}^{\prime\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{{\pi}}(s_{0})+\gamma\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{1}=s|s_{0})R_{{\pi}}(s)+\gamma^{2}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{2}=s|s_{0})V_{{\pi}}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{{\pi}}(s_{0})+\gamma\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}_{{\pi}}(s_{1}=s|s_{0})R_{{\pi}}(s)

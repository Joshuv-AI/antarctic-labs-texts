Quantitative estimates for singularity for conjugate equations driven by linear fractional transformations
Kazuki Okamura

Abstract
. We consider the conjugate equation driven by two families of finite maps on the unit interval satisfying a compatibility condition. This framework contains de Rham’s functional equations. We give sufficient conditions for singularity of the solution with quantitative estimates in the case that the equation is driven by non-affine maps, in particular, linear fractional transformations.



1.
Introduction

Let
N\geq 2
and let
\mathcal{I}_{N}:=\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\}
.
Let

\{f_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}
and
\{g_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}

N

We consider the following conjugate equation for
\varphi:[0,1]\to[0,1]
:

g_{i}\circ\varphi=\varphi\circ f_{i},\ i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}.

(1.1)

See Section 2 below for the definition of the compatibility condition.
An alternative expression is given by

\varphi(x)=\begin{cases}g_{0}(\varphi(f_{0}^{-1}(x)))&0\leq x\leq f_{0}(1)\\
\vdots&\\
g_{N-1}(\varphi(f_{N-1}^{-1}(x)))&f_{N-1}(0)\leq x\leq 1\end{cases}.

(1.2)

This is known as de Rham’s functional equation
[
7
,
8
]
.
This functional equation is generally fractal in nature, and solutions of (
1.1
) can be singular functions.

Assume that
N=2
,
0<p<1
,
f_{0}(x)=x/2
,
f_{1}(x)=(x+1)/2
,
g_{0}(x)=px
and
g_{1}(x)=(1-p)x+p
.
Then, (

\varphi(x)=\begin{cases}p\varphi(2x)&0\leq x\leq 1/2\\
(1-p)\varphi(2x-1)+p&1/2\leq x\leq 1\end{cases}.

There exists a unique continuous strictly increasing solution
\varphi_{p}
for this equation,
and
\varphi_{p}
is singular if

and
\varphi_{p}(x)=x
if

.

\varphi_{p}
is called the Lebesgue singular function.
This function is also a solution of the functional equation by Matkowski and Wesołowski
[

,

,

]

Minkowski’s question-mark function
[

It can also be expressed in the form

\varphi(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\varphi(\frac{x}{1-x})&0\leq x\leq 1/2\\
\frac{1}{2}\varphi\left(2-\frac{1}{x}\right)+\frac{1}{2}&1/2\leq x\leq 1\end{cases}.

This function has appeared in various areas of mathematics such as number theory and dynamical systems.
The real analytic properties of this function have been considered (

The main purpose of this paper is to investigate some properties of the solution of (

2.1.
Existence and uniqueness

We first discuss the existence and uniqueness of the solution of the conjugate equation (
1.1
).

We say that a family of maps
\{h_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}
on
[0,1]
is a
compatible system
or satisfies a
compatibility condition
if
they are strictly increasing continuous functions such that
h_{0}(0)=0
,
h_{i-1}(1)=h_{i}(0)
,
1\leq i\leq N-1
,
h_{N-1}(1)=1
, and, a compatible system
\{h_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}
is a
D-system
on
[0,1]

if

\mathcal{D}_{h}:=\{h_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ h_{i_{n}}(j):i_{1},\cdots i_{n}\in\mathcal{I}_{N},j\in\{0,1\}\}

is dense in
[0,1]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\{h_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}
be a D-system on
[0,1]
.
Then, for every
x\in[0,1]
, there exists an infinite sequence
(i_{n})_{n}
of
\mathcal{I}_{N}
such that

x=\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ h_{i_{n}}(0)=\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ h_{i_{n}}(1).

(2.1)

If
x\in\mathcal{D}_{h}\setminus\{0,1\}
, then, there exist two such infinite sequences.
Otherwise, there exists exactly one such infinite sequence.

Proof.

We first show that for every infinite sequence
(i_{n})_{n}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}|h_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ h_{i_{n}}(0)-h_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ h_{i_{n}}(1)|=0.

(2.2)

Assume this fails.
By the definition of
\{h_{i}\}_{i}
,

\left(h_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ h_{i_{n}}(0)\right)_{n}
is increasing and
\left(h_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ h_{i_{n}}(1)\right)_{n}
is decreasing.
Since
\mathcal{D}_{h}
is dense in
[0,1]
,
there exists
k_{1},\cdots,k_{N}
and
j\in\{0,1\}
such that

h_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ h_{i_{N}}(0)\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ h_{i_{n}}(0)<h_{k_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ h_{k_{N}}(j)

<\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ h_{i_{n}}(1)\leq h_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ h_{i_{N}}(1).

This cannot occur.
Hence (
2.2
) holds.

Case 1. Assume
x\in\mathcal{D}_{h}\setminus\{0,1\}
.
Assume that
x=h_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ h_{i_{n}}(0)
.
Since
h_{0}(0)=0
and
x>0
, we can assume that
i_{n}\geq 1
.
Let
i_{k}:=0
for every
k\geq n+1
.
Then, (
2.1
) holds.
By replacing
i_{n}
with
i_{n}-1
and let
i_{k}:=N-1
for every
k\geq n+1
,
then, (
2.1
) holds.
Assume that
x=h_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ h_{i_{n}}(1)
.
Since
h_{N-1}(1)=1
and
x<1
, we can assume that
i_{n}\leq N-2
.
Let
i_{k}:=N-1
for every
k\geq n+1
.
Then, (
2.1
) holds.
By replacing
i_{n}
with
i_{n}+1
and let
i_{k}:=0
for every
k\geq n+1
,
then, (
2.1
) holds.

Case 2. Assume
x\in\{0,1\}
.
Assume
x=0
.
Since each
h_{i}
is strictly increasing,
there is no other choice than
i_{k}=0
for every
k\geq 1
for (
2.1
).
Assume
x=1
.
Then, there is no other choice than
i_{k}=N-1
for every
k\geq 1
for (
2.1
).

Case 3. Assume
x\notin\mathcal{D}_{h}
.
Then, for each
n\geq 1
, there exists a unique finite sequence
(i_{n,1},\cdots,i_{n,n})
such that

h_{i_{n,1}}\circ\cdots\circ h_{i_{n,n}}(0)<x<h_{i_{n,1}}\circ\cdots\circ h_{i_{n,n}}(1).

Then,
i_{n-1,k}=i_{n,k}
for every
k\leq n-1
.
Let
i_{k}:=i_{n,k}
.
By (
2.2
), (
2.1
) holds.
If (
2.1
) holds, then, for every
N\geq 1
,

h_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ h_{i_{N}}(0)\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ h_{i_{n}}(0)=x=\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ h_{i_{n}}(1)\leq h_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ h_{i_{N}}(1).

Hence,

h_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ h_{i_{N}}(0)<x<h_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ h_{i_{N}}(1).

Hence the uniqueness holds.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}
and
\{g_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}
are D-systems on
[0,1]
.
Then, there exists a unique continuous strictly increasing solution of (

).

Hereafter, we let
\mu_{\varphi}
be the continuous probability measure on
[0,1]
whose distribution function is
\varphi
.
This is the central object considered in this paper.

Proof.

We first see that

\varphi\left(f_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{i_{n}}(j)\right)=g_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ g_{i_{n}}(j),\ i_{1},\cdots,i_{n}\in\mathcal{I}_{N},\ j\in\{0,1\},

(2.3)

by induction in
n
.
Hence,
\varphi(\mathcal{D}_{f})=\mathcal{D}_{g}
.

Step 1. We define
\varphi(x)
for

.
By the assumption, there exists an infinite sequence
(i_{n})_{n}
of
\mathcal{I}_{N}
such that

x\in(f_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{i_{n}}(0),f_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{i_{n}}(1))
.
Since
\left[g_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ g_{i_{n}}(0),g_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ g_{i_{n}}(1)\right]
is decreasing with respect to

,

\bigcap_{n\geq 1}\left[g_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ g_{i_{n}}(0),g_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ g_{i_{n}}(1)\right]=\left[\lim_{n\to\infty}g_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ g_{i_{n}}(0),\lim_{n\to\infty}g_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ g_{i_{n}}(1)\right].

By the density of
\mathcal{D}_{g}
in
[0,1]
, we can apply (
2.2
), and obtain that

\lim_{n\to\infty}g_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ g_{i_{n}}(0)=\lim_{n\to\infty}g_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ g_{i_{n}}(1).

(2.4)

We let
\varphi(x):=\lim_{n\to\infty}g_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ g_{i_{n}}(0)
.

Step 2. We show
\varphi
is strictly increasing on
[0,1]
.
By (
2.3
),
\varphi
is strictly increasing on
\mathcal{D}_{f}
.
Let
x<y
.
By (
2.4
), for some

f_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{i_{n}}(0)\leq x\leq f_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{i_{n}}(1)<f_{j_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{j_{n}}(0)\leq y\leq f_{j_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{j_{n}}(1).

By this and Step 1,

\varphi(x)\leq g_{i_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ g_{i_{n}}(1)<g_{j_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ g_{j_{n}}(0)\leq\varphi(y).

Step 3. We show that
\varphi
is continuous on
[0,1]
.
Assume that there is a discontinuous point

of

.
Then,

b_{0}:=\lim_{x\to x_{0}+0}\varphi(x)>\lim_{x\to x_{0}-0}\varphi(x)=:a_{0}.

By Step 2,
\varphi([0,1])\cap(a_{0},b_{0})=\emptyset

\mathcal{D}_{g}\cap(a_{0},b_{0})=\emptyset

This contradicts the density of

in

.
The same argument is applied to the case that

2.2.
Weak contraction

Hereafter, we assume more additional conditions for the D-systems
\{f_{i}\}_{i}
and
\{g_{i}\}_{i}
.
We say that a function
h:[0,1]\to[0,1]
is a
contraction
on
[0,1]
if

\textup{Lip}(h):=\sup_{x\neq y}\left|\frac{h(x)-h(y)}{x-y}\right|<1.

\textup{Lip}(h)
is called the Lipschitz constant of
h
.
We say that a function
h:[0,1]\to[0,1]
is a
weak contraction
on
[0,1]
if

\phi_{h}(t):=\lim_{s\to t+0}\sup\left\{|h(x)-h(y)|:|x-y|\leq s\right\}<t,\ \ t>0.

The following is well-known.
See
[
5
,
14
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
h
be a weak contraction.
Then,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\phi_{h}^{n}(t)=0
for every
t>0
and

|h(x)-h(y)|\leq\phi_{h}(|x-y|)
for
x,y\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

Let

\omega_{h}(s):=\sup\left\{|h(x)-h(y)|:|x-y|\leq s\right\}.

Since this function is increasing,

\phi_{h}
is right-continuous and increasing on
[0,1]
.
Hence,

|h(x)-h(y)|\leq\lim_{s\to|x-y|+0}\phi_{h}(s)=\phi_{h}(|x-y|)
.

By the assumption, for every
t>0
,
(\phi_{h}^{n}(t))_{n}
is strictly decreasing.
There exists the limit
\phi^{\infty}_{h}(t):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\phi_{h}^{n}(t)
.
By the right-continuity of
\phi_{h}
,

\phi_{h}\left(\phi^{\infty}_{h}(t)\right)=\phi^{\infty}_{h}(t)
.
Hence,
\phi^{\infty}_{h}(t)=0
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\{h_{i}\}_{i}
be a compatible system on
[0,1]
such that each
h_{i}
is a weak contraction on
[0,1]
.
Then,
\{h_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}
is a D-system.

We say that a compatible system
\{h_{i}\}_{i}
on
[0,1]
is an
(weakly) contractive system
on
[0,1]
if each
h_{i}
is an (weak) contraction on
[0,1]
.
Every weakly contractive system is a D-system.

Proof.

Let
\phi:=\max_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}\phi_{h_{i}}
.
Then,
\phi
is right-continuous

N
is finite would be important.

[0,1]
,
\phi(t)<t
for every

,
and
\max_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}|h_{i}(x)-h_{i}(y)|\leq\phi(|x-y|)
,

.
Then, we see that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\phi^{n}(t)=0
for every

Hence,

2.3.
Linear fractional transforms

Definition 2.7
.

We say that a family of functions
\{h_{i}\}_{i}
is an
LF system
on
[0,1]
if

h_{i}(x):=\dfrac{a_{i}x+b_{i}}{c_{i}x+d_{i}},x\in[0,1]
, for each
i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}
,
where
a_{i},b_{i},c_{i},d_{i},i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}
, are real numbers such that

(1) (well-defined)
c_{i}+d_{i}>0
,
d_{i}>0
,
i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}
.

(2) (strictly increasing, weak contraction)

0<a_{i}d_{i}-b_{i}c_{i}\leq\min\{d_{i}^{2},(c_{i}+d_{i})^{2}\},\ i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}.

(3) (compatibility)
b_{0}=0
,

\dfrac{a_{i}+b_{i}}{c_{i}+d_{i}}=\dfrac{b_{i+1}}{d_{i+1}}
,
i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}
, where we let
\dfrac{b_{N}}{d_{N}}:=1
.

For condition (2), we may consider a stronger condition that

(2’)
0<a_{i}d_{i}-b_{i}c_{i}<\min\{d_{i}^{2},(d_{i}+c_{i})^{2}\},\ i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}
.

Since
h_{i}
is invariant if we multiply a positive constant by each of
a_{i},b_{i},c_{i}
and
d_{i}
,
we may assume that
d_{i}=1
, or,
a_{i}d_{i}-b_{i}c_{i}=1
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Every LF system is a weakly contractive system.
If (2’) holds instead of (2), then, it is a contractive system.

Proof.

Let
\{h_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}}
be an LF-system.
We assume that
d_{i}=1
for every
i
.
By Lemma
2.6
, it suffices to show that each
h_{i}
is a weak contraction.
We first remark that

|h_{i}(x)-h_{i}(y)|=\frac{a_{i}-b_{i}c_{i}}{(c_{i}x+1)(c_{i}y+1)}|x-y|.

We consider three cases: (i)
c_{i}>0
, (ii)
c_{i}<0
, and (iii)
c_{i}=0
.

(i) Assume
c_{i}>0
.
Then,
a_{i}-b_{i}c_{i}\leq 1
and

|h_{i}(x)-h_{i}(y)|\leq\frac{a_{i}-b_{i}c_{i}}{c_{i}|x-y|+1}|x-y|\leq\frac{|x-y|}{c_{i}|x-y|+1}.

Hence,

\omega_{h_{i}}(s)\leq\frac{s}{c_{i}s+1},\ \ s\geq 0.

By this and
c_{i}>0
,

\phi_{h_{i}}(t)\leq\frac{t}{c_{i}t+1}<t,\ t>0.

(ii) Assume
c_{i}<0
.
Then,
a_{i}-b_{i}c_{i}\leq(c_{i}+1)^{2}
and

|h_{i}(x)-h_{i}(y)|\leq\frac{a_{i}-b_{i}c_{i}}{(c_{i}+1)(c_{i}(1-|x-y|)+1)}|x-y|\leq\frac{(1+c_{i})|x-y|}{-c_{i}|x-y|+1+c_{i}}.

Hence,

\omega_{h_{i}}(s)\leq\frac{(1+c_{i})s}{-c_{i}s+1+c_{i}},\ \ s\geq 0.

By this and
c_{i}<0
,

\phi_{h_{i}}(t)\leq\frac{(1+c_{i})t}{-c_{i}t+1+c_{i}}<t,\ t>0.

(iii) Assume
c_{i}=0
.
Then,
0<a_{i}\leq 1
and
|h_{i}(x)-h_{i}(y)|=a_{i}|x-y|
.
By the assumption for
a_{i},b_{i},c_{i}
,
h_{j}(1)>h_{j}(0)
for every
j
.
Hence, by
N\geq 2
,
a_{i}=h_{i}(1)-h_{i}(0)<1
.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

The technique used above is related to a characterization of Rakotch contractions.
See
[
20

Let
(\mathcal{I}_{N})^{*}:=\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(\mathcal{I}_{N})^{n}
.
We denote the length of
\sigma\in(\mathcal{I}_{N})^{*}
by
|\sigma|
.
For an LF system
\{h_{i}\}_{i}
and
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{n})\in(\mathcal{I}_{N})^{*}
,
we let

h_{\sigma}(x):=h_{\sigma_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ h_{\sigma_{n}}(x),\ x\in[0,1].

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\{h_{i}\}_{i}
be an LF system.
Then, there exists
c\in(0,1)
such that for every
\sigma\in(\mathcal{I}_{N})^{*}
,

h_{\sigma}(1)-h_{\sigma}(0)\geq c^{|\sigma|}.

Proof.

Let
A_{i}:=\begin{pmatrix}a_{i}&b_{i}\\
c_{i}&d_{i}\end{pmatrix}
,
i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}
.
We assume that
a_{i}d_{i}-b_{i}c_{i}=1
for each
i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}
.
Let
(i_{n})_{n}\in(\mathcal{I}_{N})^{\mathbb{N}}
.
Let

\begin{pmatrix}p_{n}&q_{n}\\
r_{n}&s_{n}\end{pmatrix}=A_{i_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{n}},\ n\geq 1.

Let
C_{1}:=\max\{|a_{i}|,|b_{i}|,|c_{i}|,|d_{i}|:i\in\mathcal{I}_{N}\}
.
Then, for every
n
,

|r_{n+1}|+|s_{n+1}|\leq C_{1}(|r_{n}|+|s_{n}|)
.
Hence,

|r_{n}|+|s_{n}|\leq 2C_{1}^{n}
for every
n
.

Assume that
\sigma=(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})
.
Then,
|\sigma|=n
and

Example 4.2
.

.

Y=[-1/6,0]
.
We see that

G_{0}(y)=\frac{y+1}{y+7},G_{1}(y)=\frac{35(y+1)}{(6y+7)(y+7)},\textup{ and }G_{2}(y)=\frac{1}{6y+7}.

{\bf G}(\textup{Fix}(H_{0}))=\left(\frac{1}{8},\frac{245}{344},\frac{7}{43}\right),{\bf G}(\textup{Fix}(H_{1}))=\left(\frac{1}{7},\frac{5}{7},\frac{1}{7}\right),\textup{ and }{\bf G}(\textup{Fix}(H_{2}))=\left(\frac{5}{41},\frac{175}{246},\frac{1}{6}\right).

Let

\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{3}}:=\left\{(p_{0},p_{1},p_{2})\in\overline{\mathcal{P}_{3}}\ \middle|\ p_{0},p_{2}\in\left[\frac{7}{16},\frac{1}{2}\right]\right\}.

We now show that

s_{3}\left({\bf G}(y)|{\bf q}\right)\leq-0.74,\ y\in[-1/6,0],\ {\bf q}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{3}}.

(4.3)

For
q>0

for

4.3

By Proposition
3.6
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log R_{n}({\bf q};z)}{n}\leq-0.74,\ \textup{ $\nu_{0}$-a.s.$z$, \ ${\bf q}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{3}}$.}

Example 4.3
.

Let
N=4
.
Let

g_{0}(x)=\frac{x}{3},\ g_{1}(x)=\frac{x+2}{6},\ g_{2}(x)=\frac{x+3}{6},\textup{ and }g_{3}(x)=\frac{x+2}{3}.

Then,
\alpha=\beta=0

Y=\{0\}
.
We see that

G_{0}(0)=\frac{1}{3},\ G_{1}(0)=\frac{1}{6},\ G_{2}(0)=\frac{1}{6},\textup{ and }G_{3}(0)=\frac{1}{3}.

Let

\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{4}}:=\left\{(p_{0},p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})\in\overline{\mathcal{P}_{4}}\ \middle|\ p_{0},p_{3}\in\left[0,\frac{1}{8}\right]\right\}.

Then, for

,

s_{4}\left({\bf G}(0)|{\bf q}\right)=\log 3+\frac{1}{3}\log 2+\frac{1}{6}(2\log q_{0}+\log q_{1}+\log q_{2}+2\log q_{3}).

Since
\sum_{i=0}^{3}q_{i}=1
,

\log q_{1}+\log q_{2}\leq 2\log((1-q_{0}-q_{3})/2)

for
x,y\in[0,1/8]
.
Then,

\max_{x,y\in[0,1/8]}f(x,y)=f\left(\frac{1}{8},\frac{1}{8}\right)=\log 3-8\log 2.

Therefore,

s_{4}\left({\bf G}(0)|{\bf q}\right)\leq\frac{4}{3}\log\frac{3}{4}\leq-0.38,\ {\bf q}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{4}}.

By Proposition
3.6
,
it holds that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log R_{n}({\bf q};z)}{n}\leq-0.38,\ \textup{ $\nu_{0}$-a.s.$z$, \ ${\bf q}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}_{4}}$.}

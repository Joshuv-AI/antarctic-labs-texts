Asymptotic expansions for blocks estimators: PoT framework
Zaoli Chen, Rafał Kulik

Abstract
We consider disjoint and sliding blocks estimators of cluster indices for multivariate, regularly varying time series in the Peak-over-Threshold framework. We aim to provide a complete description of the limiting behaviour of these estimators. This is achieved by a precise expansion for the difference between the sliding and the disjoint blocks statistics. The rates in the expansion stem from internal clusters and boundary clusters . To obtain these rates we need to extend the existing results on vague convergence of cluster measures. We reveal dichotomous behaviour between small blocks and large blocks scenario.



1
Introduction

Consider
a stationary, regularly varying
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued time series
\boldsymbol{X}=\{\boldsymbol{X}_{j},j\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. We are interested in estimating cluster indices that describe its extremal behaviour. Informally speaking, a cluster is a triangular array
(\boldsymbol{X}_{1}/u_{n},\ldots,\boldsymbol{X}_{r_{n}}/u_{n})
with
r_{n},u_{n}\to\infty
that converges in distribution in a certain sense. Cluster indices are obtained by applying the appropriate functional
H
to the cluster.
The functionals are defined on
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\mathbb{Z}}
and are such that their values do not depend on coordinates whose entries are small.
More precisely, denote
\boldsymbol{X}_{i,j}=(\boldsymbol{X}_{i},\ldots,\boldsymbol{X}_{j})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{(j-i+1)}
.
Then, we identify
H(\boldsymbol{X}_{i,j})
with

H(({\boldsymbol{0}},\boldsymbol{X}_{i,j},{\boldsymbol{0}}))
, where
{\boldsymbol{0}}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\mathbb{Z}}
is the zero sequence. Such a functional
H
will be called a
cluster functional
.

Let
w_{n}:=\mathbb{P}(\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n})
.
Given a cluster functional
H
on
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\mathbb{Z}}
, we want to estimate

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{*}(H):=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{*}_{n,r_{n}}(H):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[H(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}})]}{r_{n}w_{n}}\;.

(1.1)

To guarantee existence of the limit we require additional anticlustering assumptions on
\boldsymbol{X}
. The particular cluster quantity of interest is the (candidate) extremal index obtained with
H(\boldsymbol{x})=\mathbbm{1}{\left\{\boldsymbol{x}^{\ast}>1\right\}}
,
\boldsymbol{x}=\{\boldsymbol{x}_{j},j\in\mathbb{Z}\}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\mathbb{Z}}
.
See Chapter 6 in
[
13
]
and
Section

2.8
below.

Several methods of estimation of the limit
{\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{*}(H)
in (
1.1
) may be
employed. The natural one is to consider a
statistics based on disjoint blocks of size
r_{n}
,

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}^{*}_{n,r_{n}}(H):=\frac{1}{nw_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{n}}H(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{(i-1)r_{n}+1,ir_{n}})\;,

(1.2)

where
m_{n}=[n/r_{n}]
.
Although some special cases were considered (e.g. the extremal index in
[
12
]
and
[
16
]
), the general theory was developed in the seminal paper
[
11
]
.
The appropriately scaled and centered statistics is asymptotically normal with the limiting variance given by
{\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{*}(H^{2})
.
A summary of the theory for the disjoint blocks statistics and the corresponding data based estimators (where the threshold
u_{n}
is replaced with the appropriate intermediate order statistics) can be found in
[
13
, Chapter 10]
.
Some recent developments in the context of
\ell^{p}
-blocks can be found in
[
5
]
.

Alternatively, we can consider the sliding blocks statistics

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}}^{*}_{n,r_{n}}(H):=\frac{1}{nr_{n}w_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n-r_{n}+1}H\left(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{i,i+r_{n}-1}\right)\;.

(1.3)

This and the corresponding data based estimators have been studied for some specific functionals
H
, however there was no unified theory available. Recently,
[
10
]
used the framework of
[
11
]
and showed that the limiting variance of the sliding blocks estimator never exceeds that of the disjoint blocks one. In case of the extremal index, both variances are equal. In
[
7
]
, building upon
[
13
, Chapter 10]
, it has been proven that in case of regularly varying time series the asymptotic behaviour of sliding and disjoint blocks estimators is the same. We note in passing that the same holds for so-called runs estimators, which can be viewed as a special case of the sliding blocks estimators. See
[
8
]
.

We note that all the discussion above is valid in the Peak-over-Threshold (PoT) framework. On the basic technical level, the ”PoT framework” refers to the assumption
\lim_{n\to\infty}r_{n}w_{n}=0
; see

R

(

r
n

,

u
n

)

below. To the contrary, in the block maxima framework it has been observed that the asymptotic variance for sliding blocks estimators is strictly smaller as compared to disjoint blocks. We refer to
[
3
]
and a review in
[
4
]
.

The goal of the paper.

We aim to provide a mathematical explanation for the aforementioned phenomena observed in the PoT framework. This is achieved by a precise expansion for the difference between the sliding and the disjoint blocks statistics. The rate in the expansion will stem from
internal clusters
and
boundary clusters
.

1.1
Probabilistic tools for asymptotic expansions

The techniques we use in the paper stem from Chapters 6 and 10 in
[
13
]
.

Internal clusters.

Intuitively, an internal cluster occurs if there is a large value in a single block, but there are no large values in adjacent blocks.
The starting point of our analysis is thus vague convergence of clusters.

Proposition

2.8.1
(see also Theorem 6.2.5 in
[
13
]
) establishes such convergence, where the class of test functions consists of bounded functionals that vanish around zero. The rate of convergence is

r_{n}w_{n}
, which is proportional to the probability of an occurrence of a large value in a single block.

In the present context we face a challenge. Starting with a functional
H
, the internal cluster statistics involves another functional
\widetilde{H}_{\mathcal{IC}}
, which is induced from
H
. For example, if
H(\boldsymbol{x})=\mathbbm{1}{\left\{\boldsymbol{x}^{*}>1\right\}}
, then
\widetilde{H}_{\mathcal{IC}}
equals a cluster length (subtracted by one), the distance between the location of the last and the first large value. In fact, we shall note that the cluster length functional plays a key role in this paper. Note also that the cluster length is unbounded. Hence, at the first step of our analysis we need to extend vague convergence of clusters to unbounded functionals. Under the appropriate uniform integrability conditions we can recover
Proposition

2.8.1
- cluster functionals still have the rate
r_{n}w_{n}
. The uniform integrability condition holds for tight cluster functionals (such as the cluster length) as long as a new anticlustering condition is valid. The latter in turn is related to
small blocks
. On the other hand,
in case of
large blocks
or some unbounded non-tight functionals (such as locations of large values),
Proposition

2.8.1
is no longer valid and cluster functionals grow at a different rate.

Boundary clusters.

When proving central limit theorem (CLT) for (disjoint) blocks estimators, mixing conditions allow to treat the consecutive blocks as if they were independent. In such the case (referred below to as ”piecewise stationarity”) the chance of large values in two consecutive blocks is proportional to
r_{n}^{2}w_{n}^{2}
. It turns out that this CLT-based heuristic fails in the context of the asymptotic expansion.

Indeed, in case of
small blocks
the rate for the event ”large values in two consecutive blocks” is proportional to
w_{n}
. Intuitively, large values occur at the end of one block and at the beginning of the next block. Bounded cluster functionals grow then at the same rate
w_{n}
.
On the other hand, in case of
large blocks
, the blocks behave as if they were independent and the cluster functionals have a different rate.

Internal vs. boundary clusters.

Both types of clusters have a different asymptotic behaviour. Also, the notion of
small
and
large
blocks is different for both types of clusters.

Asymptotic expansions.

In case of small blocks the rate in the asymptotic expansion stems from both internal and boundary clusters. As a consequence, after the appropriate scaling, the difference between the disjoint and the sliding blocks statistics is of the rate
O_{P}(1/r_{n})
.
On the other hand, in case of large blocks, the difference is of the rate
O_{P}(r_{n}w_{n})
.

Weak dependence assumptions.

Most of the results in the paper are valid just under the appropriate anticlustering condition and only some of them involve mixing. This is the situation of the small blocks scenario.

The large blocks scenario is presented in case of
\ell
-dependence. We can re-write our results in an expense of cumbersome mixing assumptions. We decided to focus on a very simple dependence structure that is sufficient to understand the difference between the small and the large blocks scenario, as well as between internal and boundary clusters. All phenomena that appear in the paper can be illustrated by a simple, 1-dependent time series!

Also, we obtain an expansion for a piecewise stationary time series. In the small blocks scenario, the result is quantitatively different as compared to the weakly dependent case.

Conclusion for the asymptotic behaviour of blocks estimators.

In the PoT framework, disjoint and sliding blocks estimators have the same asymptotic behaviour in either weakly dependent or piecewise stationary situation: the limiting variance in the Central Limit Theorem is the same. This is in contrast to the block maxima framework.

1.3
What is missing?

We provide rather complete theory is the small blocks scenario. The behavior in the large
blocks scenario is illustrated under much simpler
dependence assumptions. However, from the
statistical perspective,
large blocks are much less relevant in practice.

Also, we do not consider here functionals that do not vanish around zero. For example functionals that lead to the large deviation index (see e.g.
[
14
]
). These functionals may be large due to a cumulation of small values. This is usually prevented by imposing a ”negligibility of small values” condition, but it is still not suitable for techniques and the framework of the paper.

Furthermore,
we do not formulate here nor prove the central limit theorems for
two types cluster. We refer to
[
6
]
for the appropriate results related to the internal clusters.

2.2
Tail process

Let
\boldsymbol{X}=\{\boldsymbol{X}_{j},j\in\mathbb{Z}\}
be a stationary, regularly varying time series with values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and tail index
\alpha
. In particular,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}|>tx)}{\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}|>x)}=t^{-\alpha}

for all

Then, there exists a sequence
\boldsymbol{Y}=\{\boldsymbol{Y}_{j},j\in\mathbb{Z}\}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(x^{-1}(\boldsymbol{X}_{i},\dots,\boldsymbol{X}_{j})\in\cdot\mid|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}|>x)\mbox{ converges weakly to }\mathbb{P}((\boldsymbol{Y}_{i},\dots,\boldsymbol{Y}_{j})\in\cdot)

as

.
We call
\boldsymbol{Y}
the tail process.
See
[
2
]
. Equivalently, viewing
\boldsymbol{X}
and
\boldsymbol{Y}
as random elements with values in
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\mathbb{Z}}
, we have for every bounded or
non-negative functional
H
on
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{\mathbb{Z}}
, continuous with respect to the product topology,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[H(x^{-1}\boldsymbol{X})\mathbbm{1}{\left\{\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>x\right\}}]}{\mathbb{P}(\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>x)}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[H(\boldsymbol{Y})]\;.

(2.1)

The random variable
|\boldsymbol{Y}_{0}|
has the Pareto distribution with index
\alpha
and hence
\left|\boldsymbol{Y}_{0}\right|>1
.
The spectral tail process
\boldsymbol{\Theta}=\{\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j},j\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is defined as
\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}=\boldsymbol{Y}_{j}/|\boldsymbol{Y}_{0}|
.

\displaystyle T^{(i+1)}(\boldsymbol{x},\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\inf\{j>T^{(i)}(\boldsymbol{x},\epsilon):|\boldsymbol{x}_{j}|>\epsilon\}\;,\ \ i\geqslant 1\;,

(2.2c)

\displaystyle\Delta T^{(i)}(\boldsymbol{x},\epsilon)

2.9
Dependence assumptions

Let

We will consider two temporal dependence schemes.

•

Stationary, weakly dependent:

\{\boldsymbol{X}_{j},j\in\mathbb{Z}\}

\displaystyle\alpha_{j}=O(\exp(-Cj))\;,\ \ \lim_{n\to\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{C\epsilon}{2+\epsilon}r_{n}\right)/(r_{n}w_{n})^{\epsilon/(2+\epsilon)}=0\;.

(2.9)

In most of the results in the paper we can replace the above rate with a polynomial decay. We keep the exponential bound for clarity.

•

Piecewise stationary:
For a sample of size

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{X}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{X}_{n})=((\boldsymbol{X}_{1}^{(1)},\ldots,\boldsymbol{X}_{r_{n}}^{(1)}),(\boldsymbol{X}_{1}^{(2)},\ldots,\boldsymbol{X}_{r_{n}}^{(2)}),\ldots,(\boldsymbol{X}_{1}^{(m_{n})},\ldots,\boldsymbol{X}_{r_{n}}^{(m_{n})}))

The latter assumption will serve for some illustration purposes only. We will show that this is not the case when the asymptotic expansion is considered.

For a future use, we recall the following mixing inequality. Let

be the sigma field generated by

be such that

\displaystyle|\mathrm{Cov}(U,V)|\leqslant 8\alpha_{i}^{1/r}\|U\|_{p}\|V\|_{q}\;.

(2.10)

\displaystyle I_{j}=\{(j-1)r_{n}+1,\ldots,jr_{n}\}\;,\ \ j=1,\ldots,m_{n}\;.

Likewise, let

\displaystyle{\rm{SB}}_{j}:={\rm{SB}}_{j}(H):=\sum_{i\in I_{j}}H\left(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{i,i+r_{n}-1}\right)\;.

We will distinguish between two kinds of clusters, with distinct properties.
We shall call the first kind an

4.3
Large blocks scenario

For a simple treatment of the large blocks scenario, we consider the functional
H(\boldsymbol{x})=\mathbbm{1}{\left\{\boldsymbol{x}^{*}>1\right\}}
and a very special case of a time series. The result can be extended to a mixing case with a cumbersome mixing rates. Furthermore, unbounded functionals can be considered.

However, we decided to keep a very simple formulation of the large blocks scenario to illustrate a quantitative difference as compared to the small blocks scenario. From a statistical point of view, one can argue that large blocks are not relevant.

To proceed, consider
an MMA(
1
) sequence
\boldsymbol{X}=\{X_{j},j\in\mathbb{Z}\}
defined by

\displaystyle X_{j}=c_{0}\xi_{j}\vee c_{1}\xi_{j+1}\;,

where
c_{0},c_{1}
are non-negative numbers and
\xi=\{\xi_{j},j\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is a sequence of i.i.d. non-negative, regularly varying random variables with the tail index
\alpha
. Non-negativity here is assumed for simplicity, to illustrate easily the main messages from the paper.
Then,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(X_{0}>x)}{\mathbb{P}(\xi_{0}>x)}=c_{0}^{\alpha}+c_{1}^{\alpha}\;,\ \ \lim_{x\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(X_{1}>x\mid X_{0}>x)=\frac{(c_{0}\wedge c_{1})^{\alpha}}{c_{0}^{\alpha}+c_{1}^{\alpha}}\;.

(4.6)

The tail process is
Y_{1}=\mathrm{b}(c_{1}/c_{0})Y_{0}
,
Y_{-1}=(1-\mathrm{b})(c_{0}/c_{1})Y_{0}
, where
\mathrm{b}
is a Bernoulli random variables with the mean
1/(1+(c_{1}/c_{0})^{\alpha})
, and
Y_{0}
is a standard Pareto random variable with the tail index
\alpha
. Furthermore,
Y_{j}=0
if
|j|\geqslant 2
. Hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(Y_{1}>1)=\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{b}=1)\mathbb{P}((c_{1}/c_{0})Y_{0}>1)=\frac{c_{0}^{\alpha}}{c_{0}^{\alpha}+c_{1}^{\alpha}}\left(\left(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{0}}\right)^{\alpha}\wedge 1\right)=\frac{(c_{0}\wedge c_{1})^{\alpha}}{c_{0}^{\alpha}+c_{1}^{\alpha}}\;,

(This can be also derived from (
4.6
), since
\mathbb{P}(Y_{1}>1)=\lim_{x\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(X_{1}>x\mid X_{0}>x)
.)
The candidate extremal index is then

\displaystyle{\vartheta}=\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{1,\infty}^{*}\leqslant 1)=\mathbb{P}(Y_{1}\leqslant 1)=1-\mathbb{P}(Y_{1}>1)=1-\frac{(c_{0}\wedge c_{1})^{\alpha}}{c_{0}^{\alpha}+c_{1}^{\alpha}}=\frac{(c_{0}\vee c_{1})^{\alpha}}{c_{0}^{\alpha}+c_{1}^{\alpha}}\;.

The next result shows that the rates in the asymptotic expansion for blocks statistics are different as compared to the small blocks scenario. Also, similarly to the piecewise stationary case, only the internal clusters contribute. In other words, in the large blocks scenario, the blocks behave as if they were independent.

Theorem 4.3.1
.

Assume that
\boldsymbol{X}
is the MMA(1) process and
H(\boldsymbol{x})=\mathbbm{1}{\left\{\boldsymbol{x}^{*}>1\right\}}
.
Assume
r_{n}^{3}w_{n}\to\infty
.
Then

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}^{*}_{n,r_{n}}(H)-{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}}^{*}_{n,r_{n}}(H)=\frac{1}{nr_{n}w_{n}}\mathcal{IC}(H)+o_{P}(r_{n}w_{n})+O_{P}\left(\frac{r_{n}^{2}w_{n}}{n}\right)\;,

with

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{IC}(H)]}{nr_{n}^{2}w_{n}^{2}}=\frac{1}{6}{\vartheta}^{2}\;.

Proof.

Corollary

6.2.1
gives the rate for the internal clusters statistics,
Corollary

6.2.2
yields that the internal clusters statistics is negligible, while the second part of
Lemma

6.4.1
(applied with
\gamma=0
) gives the rate for the remainder.
∎

In particular,

\displaystyle\left[{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\nu}}}^{*}_{n,r_{n}}(H)-{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}}^{*}_{n,r_{n}}(H)\right]=O_{P}(r_{n}w_{n})\;

and this rate cannot be improved in general.

5.1
Consequences of the anticlustering conditions

5.1.1

First and last jump decompositions

Recall the notation (
3.7
) for the exceedance times.
Set
t(i)=t_{1}{(i)}
,
i=1,\ldots,N_{1}
.
Note that for

,

\displaystyle\{t(1)=i\}=\{\boldsymbol{X}_{1,i-1}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{i}\right|>u_{n}\}\;,\ \ \{t{(N_{1})}=i\}=\{\boldsymbol{X}_{i+1,r_{n}}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{i}\right|>u_{n}\}\;,

with the convention
\boldsymbol{X}_{1,0}^{\ast}\equiv 0

and
\boldsymbol{X}_{r_{n}+1,r_{n}}^{\ast}\equiv 0
. These types of decompositions will play a crucial role.

5.1.2

Conditional convergence of clusters

As a consequence of
Proposition

2.7.1
, for
any bounded functional
H
on
\ell_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
that is almost
surely continuous with respect to the distribution of the tail process
\boldsymbol{Y}

and
any

,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[H(u_{n}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{X}_{-[r_{n}s]-j_{1},-i_{1}},\boldsymbol{X}_{i_{2},j_{2}+[r_{n}s]}))\mid\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[H(\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-i_{1}},\boldsymbol{Y}_{i_{2},\infty})\right]\;.

(5.1)

This type of convergence will be referred to as the
conditional convergence of clusters
.

5.1.3

Vague convergence of clusters

In view of the anticlustering condition

⁢
A
C

(

r
n

,

u
n

)

, the following argument will be used repeatedly (cf.
Proposition

2.8.1
and the proof of Theorem 6.2.5 in
[
13
]
). Let
G
be a bounded cluster functional such that
G(\boldsymbol{x})=0
whenever
\boldsymbol{x}^{\ast}\leqslant 1
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{r_{n}}\mathbb{E}\left[G(\boldsymbol{X}_{1-i,r_{n}-i}/u_{n})\mathbbm{1}{\left\{\boldsymbol{X}_{1-i,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n}\right\}}\mid\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n}\right]=\int_{0}^{1}g_{n}(s)\mathrm{d}s

(5.2)

with

g_{n}(s)=\mathbb{E}\left[G(\boldsymbol{X}_{1-[r_{n}s],r_{n}-[r_{n}s]}/u_{n})\mathbbm{1}{\left\{\boldsymbol{X}_{1-[r_{n}s],-1}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n}\right\}}\mid\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n}\right]

converging to
g(s):=\mathbb{E}[G(\boldsymbol{Y})\mathbbm{1}{\left\{\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant 1\right\}}]
for each

. By the dominated convergence the limit of the expression in (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[G(\boldsymbol{Y})\mathbbm{1}{\left\{\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant 1\right\}}]={\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{*}(G)\;.

In the similar spirit, since

converges to

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{r_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{r_{n}}\left(\frac{i}{r_{n}}\right)^{\gamma}\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{X}_{1-i,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}s^{\gamma}g_{n}(s)\mathrm{d}s=\frac{1}{\gamma+1}\;.

(5.3)

We will refer to this type of convergence as the
vague convergence of clusters
.

5.3
Extensions of vague convergence: boundary clusters

Section

5.3.1

Summary of the results

•

Proposition

5.3.1
and
Corollary

5.3.2
give the rates of convergence for

in the small blocks scenario. The rate is
w_{n}

for
\mathbb{P}(A_{1}\cap A_{2})
. That is, a finite number of

•

Propositions

and
5.3.5
extend the previous results to unbounded functionals with a particular focus on cluster length. We note that the small blocks condition for the finite
\gamma

.

•

(with

•

To control

; cf. the first part of

Remark

2.2
.

For
\widetilde{J}(H,r_{n};\ell)
, we note that

\displaystyle\widetilde{J}(H,r_{n};\ell)\leqslant

\displaystyle\|H\|\sum_{i=\ell}^{2r_{n}-1}i\mathbb{P}\left(|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}|>u_{n},|\boldsymbol{X}_{i}|>u_{n}\right)

and hence due to the condition
\mathcal{S}^{(1)}(r_{n},u_{n})
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\widetilde{J}(H,r_{n};\ell)}{w_{n}}=0\;.

∎

Proof of
Lemma

5.3.8
.

We proceed in a slightly different way as compared to
Proposition

5.3.1
.
We use the last jump decomposition in the first block, then the first jump decomposition in the second block, and combine this with stationarity to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(A_{1}\cap A_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{r_{n}}\sum_{j_{2}=r_{n}+1}^{2r_{n}}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n},\boldsymbol{X}_{1,j_{2}-j_{1}-1}^{*}\leqslant u_{n},\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{j_{2}-j_{1}}\right|>u_{n}\right)\;.

(5.9)

\displaystyle\leqslant w_{n}\sum_{i=1}^{2r_{n}-1}\left(i\wedge(2r_{n}-i)\right)\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{n}=i\mid|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}|>u_{n}\right)\;,

where
\tau_{n}
is the
random variable

\displaystyle\tau_{n}:=\inf\{t\geqslant 1:|\boldsymbol{X}_{t}|>u_{n}\}\;.

Fix an integer
\ell\geqslant 1
(we can assume that
\ell\leqslant r_{n}
) and split

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{1}\cap A_{2})

\displaystyle\leqslant w_{n}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}i\cdot\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{n}=i\mid|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}|>u_{n}\right)+w_{n}\sum_{i=\ell+1}^{r_{n}}i\cdot\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{n}=i\mid|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}|>u_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+w_{n}\sum_{i=r_{n}+1}^{2r_{n}}(2r_{n}-i)\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{n}=i\mid|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}|>u_{n}\right)=:J_{1}(\ell)+J_{2}(\ell,r_{n})+J_{3}(r_{n})\;.

For each finite
i\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{n}=i\mid|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}|>u_{n}\right)=\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{Y}_{1,i-1}^{*}\leqslant 1,|\boldsymbol{Y}_{i}|>1)\;

and hence
J_{1}(\ell)=O(w_{n})
.

In case of
\ell
-dependence we have immediately

\displaystyle J_{2}(\ell,r_{n})+J_{3}(r_{n})\leqslant w_{n}\left\{\sum_{i=\ell+1}^{r_{n}}i+\sum_{i=r_{n}+1}^{2r_{n}}(2r_{n}-i)\right\}\mathbb{P}(\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{i}\right|>u_{n}\mid\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n})=O(r_{n}^{2}w_{n}^{2})\;

and the latter rate dominates
w_{n}
in the large blocks scenario.
∎

Proof of
Proposition

5.3.3
.

We mimic the proof of
Proposition

5.3.1
. Recall the term
J(H,r_{n};\ell)
in (
5.3.5
). On the event
\{t_{2}{(N_{2})}-t_{1}{(1)}>\ell/2\}\subseteq\{\mathcal{L}_{1,2}>\ell/2\}
, we have
J(r_{n};\ell/2)\equiv 0
.

Since
H\in\mathcal{H}(\gamma)
, we bound
|H|(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}})\leqslant C_{H}(t_{N_{1}}-t_{1})^{\gamma}
. Assume without loss of generality that
C_{H}=1
.

Recall again
\widetilde{J}(H,r_{n};\ell)
defined in (
5.3.5
).
It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|H|(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,2r_{n}})\mathbbm{1}{\left\{|H|(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,2r_{n}})>\ell/2\right\}}\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{1}\cap A_{2}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{L}_{1,2}^{\gamma}\mathbbm{1}{\left\{\mathcal{L}_{1,2}>(\ell/2)^{\gamma/2}\right\}}\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{1}\cap A_{2}\right\}}\right]\leqslant\widetilde{J}(\mathcal{L}^{\gamma},r_{n};(\ell/2)^{\gamma/2})\;.

We have

\displaystyle\widetilde{J}(H,r_{n};(\ell/2)^{\gamma/2})\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{i=\ell}^{2r_{n}-1}i^{\gamma+1}\mathbb{P}\left(|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}|>u_{n},|\boldsymbol{X}_{i}|>u_{n}\right)

and hence due to the condition
\mathcal{S}^{(\gamma+1)}(r_{n},u_{n})
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\widetilde{J}(H,r_{n};(\ell/2)^{\gamma/2})}{w_{n}}=0\;.

The proof of the uniform integrability is finished.
∎

Proof of
Corollary

5.3.6
.

We will only show (
5.6b
).
We assume that
H
is bounded. The unbounded case follows from
Proposition

5.3.4
.

For a bounded case, we proceed in a slightly
different manner as compared to
Proposition

5.3.1
.
Fix an integer
\ell>0
. For any
n
such that
r_{n}>\ell
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[H\left(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}}\right)\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{1}\cap A_{2}\right\}}\right]=\sum_{i_{1}=r_{n}+1-\ell}^{r_{n}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\mathbbm{1}{\left\{t_{1}(1)=i_{1},\mathcal{L}_{1,2}\geqslant r_{n}+2-i_{1}\right\}}H(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{i_{1},r_{n}})\bigg]

\displaystyle+\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{r_{n}-\ell}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\mathbbm{1}{\left\{t_{1}(1)=i_{1},\mathcal{L}_{1,2}\geqslant r_{n}+2-i_{1}\right\}}H(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{i_{1},r_{n}})\bigg]=:J(r_{n};\ell)+\widetilde{J}(r_{n};\ell)\;.

We have

\displaystyle J(r_{n};\ell)

\displaystyle=w_{n}\sum_{i_{1}=r_{n}+1-\ell}^{r_{n}}\mathbb{E}[\mathbbm{1}{\left\{\boldsymbol{X}_{1-i_{1},-1}^{\ast}\leqslant u_{n},\mathcal{L}_{1,2}\geqslant r_{n}+2-i_{1}\right\}}H(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{0,r_{n}-i_{1}})\mid\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n}]

\displaystyle=w_{n}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathbb{E}[\mathbbm{1}{\left\{\boldsymbol{X}^{\ast}_{1-(r_{n}+i-\ell),-1}\leqslant u_{n},\mathcal{L}_{1,2}\geqslant 2-i+\ell\right\}}H(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{0,\ell-i})\mid\left|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}\right|>u_{n}]\;.

Since
H
is bounded, the conditional convergence of clusters argument yields

\displaystyle\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{J(r_{n};\ell)}{w_{n}}=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathbb{E}[\mathbbm{1}{\left\{\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant 1,\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{Y})\geqslant 2-i+\ell\right\}}H(\boldsymbol{Y}_{0,\ell-i})]

\displaystyle=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\sum_{j=2}^{\ell+1}\mathbb{E}[\mathbbm{1}{\left\{\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant 1,\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{Y})\geqslant j\right\}}H(\boldsymbol{Y}_{0,j-2})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[\mathbbm{1}{\left\{\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant 1,\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{Y})\geqslant j\right\}}H(\boldsymbol{Y}_{0,j-2})]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}{\left\{\boldsymbol{Y}_{-\infty,-1}^{\ast}\leqslant 1\right\}}\sum_{j=2}^{\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{Y})}H(\boldsymbol{Y}_{0,j-2})\right]={\vartheta}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=2}^{\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{Z})}H(\boldsymbol{Z}_{0,j-2})\right]\;.

Next,

\displaystyle\widetilde{J}(r_{n};\ell)\leqslant\|H\|\sum_{i=\ell}^{2r_{n}}i\cdot\mathbb{P}(|\boldsymbol{X}_{0}|>u_{n},|\boldsymbol{X}_{i}|>u_{n})

and the term vanishes on account of the anticlustering condition.
∎

Proof of
Lemma

5.3.9
.

As in the proof of
Proposition

5.3.1
, we proceed with the last jump decomposition in the first block, followed by the first jump decomposition in the second block. We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{1}\cap A_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{r_{n}}\sum_{j_{2}=r_{n}+1}^{2r_{n}}\mathbb{P}(t_{1}(1)=j_{1},t_{2}(N_{2})=j_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{r_{n}}\sum_{j_{2}=r_{n}+1}^{2r_{n}}\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{X}_{1,j_{1}-1}^{*}\leq u_{n},X_{j_{1}}>u_{n},X_{j_{2}}>u_{n},\boldsymbol{X}_{j_{2}+1,2r_{n}}^{*}\leq u_{n})\;.

By 1-dependence we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(A_{1}\cap A_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{r_{n}}\sum_{j_{2}=r_{n}+1}^{2r_{n}}\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{X}_{1,j_{1}-1}^{*}\leq u_{n},X_{j_{1}}>u_{n})\mathbb{P}(X_{j_{2}}>u_{n},\boldsymbol{X}_{j_{2}+1,2r_{n}}^{*}\leq u_{n})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-w_{n}^{2}\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{X}_{1-r_{n},-1}^{*}\leq u_{n}\mid X_{0}>u_{n})\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}-1}^{*}\leq u_{n}\mid X_{0}>u_{n})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}-1}^{*}\leq u_{n},X_{r_{n}}>u_{n},X_{r_{n}+1}>u_{n},\boldsymbol{X}_{r_{n}+2,2r_{n}}^{*}\leq u_{n})

\displaystyle=:J_{1}(r_{n})-J_{2}(r_{n})+J_{3}(r_{n})\;.

(5.10)

The vague convergence of clusters gives
J_{1}(r_{n})\sim{\vartheta}^{2}r_{n}^{2}w_{n}^{2}
.
Next, the conditional convergence of clusters gives
J_{2}(r_{n})\sim{\vartheta}^{2}w_{n}^{2}
as

. Finally,

\displaystyle J_{3}(r_{n})\sim\mathbb{P}(c_{1}\xi_{r_{n}}\leqslant u_{n},c_{0}\xi_{r_{n}}\vee c_{1}\xi_{r_{n}+1}>u_{n},c_{0}\xi_{r_{n}+1}\vee c_{1}\xi_{r_{n}+2}>u_{n},c_{0}\xi_{r_{n}+2}\leqslant u_{n})

\displaystyle\sim\mathbb{P}((c_{0}\wedge c_{1})\xi_{r_{n}+1}>u_{n})\sim w_{n}\mathbb{P}(Y_{1}>1)\;.

(5.11)

In summary, the term
J_{2}(r_{n})
does not contribute, while the terms
J_{1}(r_{n})
and
J_{3}(r_{n})
dominate in the large and the small blocks scenario, respectively.
∎

Proof of
Lemma

5.3.10
.

We use the notation from (
5.3.5
) to get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(t_{2}{(N_{2})}-t_{1}{(1)}\right)^{\gamma}\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{1}^{c}\cap A_{2}^{c}\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{r_{n}}\sum_{j_{2}=r_{n}+1}^{2r_{n}}(j_{2}-j_{1})^{\gamma}\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{X}_{1,j_{1}-1}^{*}\leqslant u_{n},X_{j_{1}}>u_{n},X_{j_{2}}>u_{n},\boldsymbol{X}_{j_{2}+1,2r_{n}}^{*}\leqslant u_{n})

\displaystyle=w_{n}^{2}\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{r_{n}}\sum_{j_{2}=r_{n}+2}^{2r_{n}}(j_{2}-j_{1})^{\gamma}\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{X}_{1-j_{1},-1}^{*}\leqslant u_{n}\mid X_{0}>u_{n})\mathbb{P}(\boldsymbol{X}_{1,2r_{n}-j_{1}}^{*}\leqslant u_{n}\mid X_{0}>u_{n})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-J_{2}(r_{n})+J_{3}(r_{n})=:w_{n}^{2}J_{0}(r_{n})-J_{2}(r_{n})+J_{3}(r_{n})\;.

For

we have as

\displaystyle w_{n}^{2}J_{0}(r_{n})\sim{\vartheta}^{2}w_{n}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2r_{n}}j^{\gamma}[(2r_{n}-j)\wedge j]\sim{\vartheta}^{2}w_{n}^{2}r_{n}^{\gamma+2}\left[\int_{0}^{1}s^{\gamma+1}\mathrm{d}s+\int_{1}^{2}s^{\gamma}(2-s)\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\sim\frac{2^{\gamma+2}-1}{(\gamma+1)(\gamma+2)}{\vartheta}^{2}w_{n}^{2}r_{n}^{\gamma+2}\;.

Combining this with (

, we conclude the proof.
∎

Proof of
Lemma

5.3.11
.

The expectation of interest is

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{r_{n}}\sum_{j_{2}=r_{n}+j_{1}}^{2r_{n}}(j_{2}-j_{1}-r_{n})\mathbb{P}(X_{j_{1}}>u_{n},\boldsymbol{X}_{j_{1}+1,\ldots,j_{2}-1}^{*}\leqslant u_{n},X_{j_{2}}>u_{n})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(c_{0}\xi_{0}\vee c_{1}\xi_{1}>u_{n},c_{0}\xi_{1}\leqslant u_{n})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\times\mathbb{P}(c_{1}\xi_{0}\leqslant u_{n},c_{0}\xi_{0}\vee c_{1}\xi_{1}>u_{n})\sum_{i=r_{n}+1}^{2r_{n}-i}(i-r_{n})(2r_{n}-i)\mathbb{P}(D_{2,i-1})

Proofs II - Limit theorems for internal and boundary clusters statistics

In this section we apply the theory established in

to particular functionals that appear in the context of the asymptotic expansion of blocks statistics. As a result, we obtain convergence in probability for

6.1
Moments of clusters - small blocks

6.1.1

Internal Clusters

Recall the notation (
3.14
) for
\mathcal{IC}_{j}(H)
.
We analyse the moments of
\mathcal{IC}_{1}
.
Since one block is involved and
\widetilde{H}_{\mathcal{IC}}
is tight under the conditional law, the scaling is

Corollary 6.1.1
.

Assume that
\mathcal{S}^{(p(\gamma+1))}(r_{n},u_{n})
holds. For any

we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{IC}_{1}(H)|^{p}]}{r_{n}w_{n}}={\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{*}(|\widetilde{H}_{\mathcal{IC}}|^{p})\;.

Corollary 6.1.2
.

Assume that
\mathcal{S}^{(2(\gamma+1))}(r_{n},u_{n})
holds. For any
H\in H(\gamma)
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Var}(\mathcal{IC}_{1}(H))}{r_{n}w_{n}}={\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{*}(\widetilde{H}_{\mathcal{IC}}^{2})\;.

Proof of
Corollaries

6.1.1
and
6.1.2
.

The functional
\widetilde{H}_{\mathcal{IC}}
is clearly continuous with respect to the law of
\boldsymbol{Y}
. We also note that

|\widetilde{H}_{\mathcal{IC}}(\mathbb{X}_{1})|\leqslant 3C_{H}\mathcal{L}^{\gamma+1}(\mathbb{X}_{1})
yielding

\displaystyle|\mathcal{IC}_{1}(H)|\leqslant\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{1}\right\}}|\widetilde{H}_{\mathcal{IC}}(\mathbb{X}_{1})|\leqslant 3C_{H}\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{1}\right\}}\mathcal{L}^{\gamma+1}(\mathbb{X}_{1})\;.

Thus, the first conclusion follows from
Lemma

5.2.1
along with
Remark

5.1
.

With
p=1
,
Corollary

6.1.1
gives
(\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{IC}_{1}(H)])^{2}=O(r_{n}^{2}w_{n}^{2})=o(r_{n}w_{n})
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{r_{n}w_{n}}\mathrm{Var}(\mathcal{IC}_{1}(H))=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{r_{n}w_{n}}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{IC}_{1}^{2}(H)]

and the term converges to the desired limit.
∎

6.1.2

Recall the functionals
\widetilde{H}_{\mathcal{BC}}
and
\widetilde{H}_{\mathcal{BC},p}
defined in (

)-(

Recall (

is a function of the block

only. Hence, the independence between blocks gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[{\rm{SB}}_{j-1}\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{j-1}^{c}\cap A_{j}\cap A_{j+1}\cap A_{j+2}^{c}\right\}}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[{\rm{SB}}_{j-1}\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{j}\right\}}]\mathbb{P}(A_{j-1})\mathbb{P}(A_{j+1}^{c})\mathbb{P}(A_{j+2})

\displaystyle\leqslant r_{n}\mathbb{E}[|H|(u_{n}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}_{1,r_{n}})\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{1}\right\}}]\mathbb{P}^{2}(A_{1}^{c})\mathbb{P}(A_{1})\;.

Hence,
Lemma

5.2.1
gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[{\rm{SB}}_{j-1}\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{j-1}^{c}\cap A_{j}\cap A_{j+1}\cap A_{j+2}^{c}\right\}}]

\displaystyle=O(r_{n}r_{n}w_{n}r_{n}w_{n})\;.

Likewise,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[{\rm{SB}}_{j+1}\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{j-1}^{c}\cap A_{j}\cap A_{j+1}\cap A_{j+2}^{c}\right\}}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[{\rm{SB}}_{j+1}\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{j+1}\right\}}]\mathbb{P}(A_{j-1}^{c})\mathbb{P}(A_{j})\mathbb{P}(A_{j+2}^{c})=O(r_{n}^{3}w_{n}^{2})\;.

The term

was bounded in the preceding corollary.
∎

6.3.1

Internal Clusters Statistics

Recall that the statistics is defined as

; cf.
(
3.13
) and (
3.14
).
Recall also the functional
\widetilde{H}_{\mathcal{IC}}
defined in (
2.4a
).
The first result is an immediate consequence of
Corollary

6.1.1
.

Corollary 6.3.1
.

Assume that
\mathcal{S}^{(\gamma+1)}(r_{n},u_{n})
holds. For any
H\in\mathcal{H}(\gamma)
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{IC}(H)]}{nw_{n}}={\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{*}(\widetilde{H}_{\mathcal{IC}})\;.

The next result shows that the internal clusters statistics behaves as if the summands were independent.

Proposition 6.3.2
.

Assume that
\boldsymbol{X}
is stationary and mixing with the rates (
2.9
).
Assume that
\mathcal{S}^{(2\gamma+2+\delta)}(r_{n},u_{n})
holds for some
\delta>0
. For any
H\in\mathcal{H}(\gamma)
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Var}(\mathcal{IC}(H))}{nw_{n}}={\boldsymbol{\nu}}^{*}(\widetilde{H}_{\mathcal{IC}}^{2})\;.

Proof.

It suffices to show that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=2}^{m_{n}-1}\mathrm{Cov}(\mathcal{IC}_{1},\mathcal{IC}_{j})\right|=o\left(\mathrm{Var}(\mathcal{IC}_{1})\right)=o(r_{n}w_{n})\;.

We divide the proof into the following four steps.

(\textup{i})
: Since
\mathcal{IC}_{j}
is based on the indicator
\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{j-1}^{c}\cap A_{j}\cap A_{j+1}^{c}\right\}}
, for
j=2
we have by
Corollary

6.1.1

\left|\mathrm{Cov}(\mathcal{IC}_{1},\mathcal{IC}_{2})\right|=(\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{IC}_{1}])^{2}=O(r_{n}^{2}w_{n}^{2})=o(r_{n}w_{n})\;.

For this we need
\mathcal{S}^{(\gamma+1)}(r_{n},u_{n})
.

(\textup{ii})
: Let
j=3,4
.
Take
p=2+\epsilon
for any
\epsilon>0
such that

(\gamma+1)(2+\epsilon)\leqslant 2\gamma+2+\delta\;.

It follows from
Corollary

6.1.1
and the mixing inequality (
2.10
),

\displaystyle|\mathrm{Cov}(\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{1}\right\}}\widetilde{H}_{\mathcal{IC}}(\mathbb{X}_{1}),\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{j}\right\}}\widetilde{H}_{\mathcal{IC}}(\mathbb{X}_{j})|\leqslant\alpha^{1/r}_{(j-2)r_{n}}\|\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{1}\right\}}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{\gamma+1}\|_{p}^{2}\leqslant

\displaystyle(r_{n}w_{n})^{2/(2+\epsilon)}\alpha^{\epsilon/(2+\epsilon)}_{(j-2)r_{n}}\;.

It is
o(r_{n}w_{n})
by applying (
2.9
). For this we need
\mathcal{S}^{(2\gamma+2+\delta)}(r_{n},u_{n})
.

Next,

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{IC}_{1}(H)-\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{1}\right\}}\widetilde{H}_{\mathcal{IC}}(\mathbb{X}_{1})]|\leqslant\mathbb{E}[\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{1}\right\}}\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{2}^{c}\right\}}|\widetilde{H}_{\mathcal{IC}}|(\mathbb{X}_{1})]=O(w_{n})=o(r_{n}w_{n})

by (
5.6b
).
Hence, together,
\mathrm{Cov}(\mathcal{IC}_{1}(H),\mathcal{IC}_{j}(H))=o(r_{n}w_{n})
.

(\textup{iii})
: In this step, we will show that for
M>4
,

\lim_{M\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\;\sum_{j=M}^{m_{n}-1}\mathrm{Cov}(\mathcal{IC}_{1},\mathcal{IC}_{j})=o(r_{n}w_{n})\;.

We apply the covariance inequality under mixing to get

\displaystyle|\mathrm{Cov}(\mathcal{IC}_{1},\mathcal{IC}_{j})|\leqslant\mathrm{cst}\;\alpha^{1/r}_{(j-4)r_{n}}\|\mathcal{IC}_{1}\|_{p}^{2}\leqslant\mathrm{cst}\;\;\alpha^{1/r}_{(j-4)r_{n}}\|\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{1}\right\}}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{\gamma+1}\|_{p}^{2}\;.

Take
p=2+\epsilon
for any
\epsilon>0
such that

(\gamma+1)(2+\epsilon)\leqslant 2\gamma+2+\delta\;.

It follows from
Corollary

6.1.1

\displaystyle|\mathrm{Cov}(\mathcal{IC}_{1},\mathcal{IC}_{j})|\leqslant\alpha^{1/r}_{(j-4)r_{n}}\|\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{1}\right\}}\mathcal{L}_{1}^{\gamma+1}\|_{p}^{2}\leqslant

\displaystyle(r_{n}w_{n})^{2/(2+\epsilon)}\alpha^{\epsilon/(2+\epsilon)}_{(j-4)r_{n}}\;.

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=M}^{m_{n}-1}|\mathrm{Cov}(\mathcal{IC}_{1},\mathcal{IC}_{j})|\leqslant(r_{n}w_{n})^{2/(2+\epsilon)}\sum_{j=M}^{m_{n}-1}\alpha^{\epsilon/(2+\epsilon)}_{(j-4)r_{n}}

and the result follows by applying (
2.9
).
∎

The difference between the sliding and the disjoint blocks statistics can be decomposed as follows:

\ell

{\rm{SB}}_{j}

5.2.1

Assume that

•

Assume that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{R}^{\rm IC}(H)+\mathcal{R}^{\rm BC}(H)|]=O(r_{n}^{\gamma+3}w_{n}^{2})\;.

.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{R}^{\rm NC}(H)|]\leq\mathrm{cst}\ m_{n}r_{n}^{\gamma+1}r_{n}^{-(2\gamma+3)}w_{n}\;.

Explanation for (

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=2}^{m_{n}-1}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N_{j}-1}\Delta t_{j}{(i)}\left\{H(\mathbb{X}_{j}{(1:i)}+H(\mathbb{X}_{j}{(i+1:N_{j})})-H(\mathbb{X}_{j})\right\}\right]\mathbbm{1}{\left\{A_{j-1}^{c}\cap A_{j}\cap A_{j+1}^{c}\right\}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=2}^{m_{n}-1}\mathcal{IC}_{j}(H)\;.

).

We analyse
{\rm{SB}}_{j-1}(H)
on

. The index

(j-1)
th block:

(I-a)

At the beginning of the

If

.

(I-b)

In the middle of the

If

(I-c)

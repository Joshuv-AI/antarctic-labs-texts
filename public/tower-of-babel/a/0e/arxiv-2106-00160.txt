M. A. Fontelos, J. López-Ríos

Abstract
. We study the problem of controlling the free surface for a two dimensional solid container in the context of the gravity waves and the sloshing problem. By using conformal maps and the Dirichlet–Neumann operator, the problem is formulated as a second order evolutionary equation on the free surface involving a self-adjoint operator. We present then the appropriate Sobolev spaces where having solutions for the system and study the exact controllability through an observability inequality for the adjoint problem.

1.
Introduction

Finding and controlling the frequencies for the oscillations of the free
boundary in the context of gravity waves and studying related spectral
problems is a classical, well known problem in the literature
[
11
,
14
,
15
,
31
]
. Roughly speaking, controlling a system consist not only in testing that
its behavior is satisfactory, but also in putting things in order to
guarantee that it behaves as desired. In mathematical terms, controlling the

state

y
, ruled by the
state equation

A(y)=f(v),

where
v
is the
control
, consists in finding
v\in U_{ad}
,

the set of admissible controls
, such that the solution to the
equation gets close to a desired prescribed state,
\bar{y}
.

In this paper, we study controllability of a Partial Differential Equation
(PDE), in the context of controlling the oscillations of a liquid free
surface in a two-dimensional bounded container and the so-called sloshing
problem. We formulate the geometrical problem in terms of an
integrodifferential equation by using the Hilbert transform, then we
establish the appropriate Sobolev spaces to study existence of solutions for
the eigenvalue problem and, finally, we set up an observability inequality
for the homogeneous adjoint problem. The sloshing problem adduces to an
important difference with respect to the classical water-waves formulation:
the presence of vertical walls and the contact with the free surface.
Inspired in the developments for the classical wave equation, we introduce
analytical tools to prove that it is possible to control the oscillations of
the free surface, by injecting fluid on the rigid side walls.

The main strengths of our method lie in the use of the Hilbert transform to
formulate the problem as an evolutionary equation involving a self-adjoint
operator. This is known as the boundary integral method and has proved to be
very fruitful in the study of water waves problems (see
[
13
]
and references therein, for instance). Moreover,
the use of Tchebyshev polynomials provides an explicit orthogonal basis
which allows to study, analytically, the associated eigenvalue problem.
Then, an observability inequality arises naturally.

Generally speaking, the water-waves problem for an ideal liquid consists of
describing the motion of a layer of incompressible, inviscid fluid,
delimited below by a solid bottom, and above by a free surface under the
influence of gravity. In mathematical terms, if
\boldsymbol{u}(x,y)
is the
fluid velocity and
\varphi
is the velocity potential such that
\boldsymbol{u}=\nabla\varphi
, by the conservation laws
[
19
]

(1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta\varphi=0,&&\Omega_{t},\\
&\eta_{t}+\eta_{x}\varphi_{x}=\varphi_{y},&&y=\eta,\\
&\varphi_{t}+\frac{1}{2}(\varphi_{x}^{2}+\varphi_{y}^{2})+g\eta=0,&&y=\eta,\\
&\partial_{n}\varphi=0,&&y=b,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\eta
and
b
are the free boundary and bottom parametrization,
respectively,
g
is the acceleration due to gravity and
\Omega_{t}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:b(x)<y<\eta(t,x)\}
. We put ourselves in the general situation
described in (
1
). However, we consider the case of a bounded domain
and the sloshing problem of describing the contact line between the free
surface and the solid walls
[
14
]
. Two
conditions are customary, the pinned–end boundary condition
where the contact line is always pinned to the solid surface, as considered
in
[
7
,
16
]
, and the free–end condition where the contact angle between the fluid–air
interface and the side walls is fixed and the contact line is allowed to
move
[
24
]
. We will consider both and will
impose conditions on the Cauchy problem to deal with it, accordingly.

Controlling the surface by different methods is of practical interest in
oceanography, controllability and inverse problems theory. We
mention, for example, the work by Reid and Russell
[
26
]

where the authors dealt with the linear conservation laws and the
null-controllability in infinite time of the free surface, by a source
control, in a two dimensional domain with flat side walls. Also, the work by
Reid,
[
25
]
, where the capillary version and the control in
finite time is considered. Concerning nonlinear water-waves, there is the
recent work by Alazard
[
3
]
, for a two
dimensional rectangular domain, where the stabilization through an external
pressure acting on a small part of the free surface is studied. Also
[
4
]
, where the author studied the boundary observability
problem in a three dimensional rectangular domain; namely, an estimate for
the energy of the system in terms of the surface velocity at the contact
line with a vertical wall. Finally, in
[
5
]
, Alazard et
al. addressed the local exact controllability of the two dimensional full
water-waves system, by controlling a localized portion of the free surface,
through the external pressure. On the literature concerning the generation
of waves by wave-makers, controllability and stability properties in the
water-waves context, we refer to
[
22
,
27
,
28
]
. From the
optimal control point of view, we mention
[
23
]
,
where the authors designed the ‘best’ moving solid bottom generating a
prescribed wave under the context of a BBM-type equation. We mention also the possibility of studying the inverse problem of detecting the source where jets originate, denoted as
J
, by measuring the free surface as in
[
20
]
.

When we talk about the controllability by fluid injection, for instance, we
mean the condition

\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial n}=J,

for a given function
J=J(t,x)
, with
n
being the outward normal vector.
This boundary condition lead to think of a boundary control of the gravity
waves problem; nevertheless, we will restate the problem as an
integrodifferential equation on the free surface and the boundary condition
becomes a source term. Then, we may use the classical approach of interior
controllability by means of the adjoint problem and the observability
inequality
[
21
,
30
]
.

By addressing this problem, we give an answer to a practical
question raised in
[
9
]
and numerically studied in

[
15
]
; namely, the problem of controlling undesirable
splashing appearing in a cooper converter when air is injected into the
molten matte. In
[
15
]
, the authors studied the problem
by using triangular finite elements to mesh a half-ball bounded domain, on a
damped linear gravity waves model. Our approach allows to
consider any general simply connected two dimensional domain, through a
conformal mapping into the lower half-plane. Moreover, if
f
represents
such a conformal mapping, the geometry is characterized explicitly by the
term
1/|f^{\prime}|
appearing as a factor in the evolution problem (see (
47
) below). On this matter, see
[
14
]
, where
oscillations are numerically computed for bottoms with rectangular even distributions.

Following the methods introduced in
[
14
]
, after
linearizing (
1
), the problem is restated through a conformal map into
the lower half–plane. We link the normal derivative to the specific
conformal map and rewrite the problem as a second order evolution equation
on the interval
[-1,1]
, where the Hilbert transform is involved. In
[
14
]
, we used this approach to propose an efficient
computational method to find the sloshing frequencies on general 2d domains.
We mention
[
18
]
where the capillary version of the
problem is developed, under the context of the oscillations in a nozzle of
an inkjet printer. In these works, two possibilities for the contact line
were considered: the ‘pinned-end edge condition’, where the contact line is
always pinned to the solid surface, and the ‘free-end condition’ where the
contact angle between the fluid–air interface and the side walls is fixed
and the contact line is allowed to move, with contact angle
\pi/2
.

With the present work, we have completed the numerical analysis started in

[
14
]
. Namely, we establish a Sobolev frame where the
Cauchy problem is well-posed and study the eigenvalue associated problem. We
also prove an observability inequality for the adjoint problem and explore
the possibility of finding explicit controls taking the oscillations at the
free surface to zero.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows: In section
2
we formulate the general equations to be considered, the linearization approach and corresponding formulation on the half-plane by the conformal mapping. In section
3
we use the Hilbert transform to state a second order evolutionary
PDE modeling the dynamics of the fluid interface on the bounded domain
[-1,1]
. In section
4
we make use of the Tchebyshev polynomials to study the stationary adjoint problem in suitable Sobolev spaces. In section
5
we
prove an observability inequality for the adjoint problem. Finally, in section
6
we establish the controllability of the problem and explore the
possibility of determining possible control functions, explicitly.

2.
Formulation

Let us consider a two dimensional container, filled with water, bounded from
above by a free surface. In this context, the motion is governed by the
incompressible Euler equations with zero surface tension:

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\rho\left[\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\right]

\displaystyle=-\nabla p-ge_{2},

with
-ge_{2}
being the constant acceleration of gravity,
g>0
, and
e_{2}

the unit upward vector in the vertical direction,
\rho
is the (constant)
density of the fluid and
p
is the pressure inside. We use the classical
notation
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
z=x+iy
for complex numbers.

By considering the potential function
\varphi
of
\mathbf{u}
, so that
\mathbf{u}=\nabla\varphi
:

(2)

\displaystyle\Delta\varphi

\displaystyle=0,

(3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\frac{1}{\rho}p+gy

\displaystyle=\text{const}.

We complement system above with the nonzero Neumann condition at the solid
walls,
\mathbf{u}\cdot n=J(t,x)
, and a kinematic condition on the free
boundary. In terms of
\varphi
:

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial n}

\displaystyle=J(t,x),\quad\text{at the solid
boundary }

(5)

\displaystyle\eta_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial n},\quad|x|<1.

2.1.
Linearized equations

Next, we are going to linearize Euler’s system around the zero state. This will allow us to obtain a single, explicit, evolutionary conservation law on the free surface modeling the fluid dynamics of the free surface side bounded by vertical solid walls. This equation must be complemented with boundary conditions at the contact line, as explained in section
3
.

Even if we consider the reference domain
\Omega=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{D}:y^{\prime}<0\}
, other domains can be considered though. Let

\eta(t,x)=\epsilon\zeta(t,x),

and

J(t,x)=\epsilon j(t,x).

Then if

\varphi=const.+\epsilon\phi,

conditions (
2
)-(
4
) in terms of
\phi
, at the first order
for
\epsilon<<1
, become (after re-scaling to make

)

(6)

\displaystyle\Delta\phi

\displaystyle=0,

(7)

\displaystyle\phi_{t}+\zeta

\displaystyle=0,\quad\text{at }y=0,\ |x|\leq 1,

(8)

\displaystyle\zeta_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n},\quad\text{at }y=0,\ |x|\leq 1,

(9)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}

\displaystyle=j(t,x),\quad\text{on the solid walls.}

System above is complemented with initial conditions
\phi_{0}
,
\zeta_{0}
such that the following mass conservation is satisfied:

(10)

\int_{-1}^{1}\zeta_{0}(x)dx=0.

Therefore, from (

(11)

\phi_{tt}+\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}=0.

We remark that the motion of the fluid interface may also be affected by

2.2.
Conformal transplants

As is explained in
[
6
,
14
]
, let
\Psi

be a real-valued function written as

\Psi:(x,y)\to\Psi(x,y)=\Psi(z)

be defined in a domain

. Also, let
\widetilde{\Psi}
be defined in
\widetilde{D}
as follows: for any

\widetilde{\Psi}(\omega):=\Psi(f^{[-1]}(\omega))=\Psi(x(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}),y(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})).

Then the following relation between the normal derivatives holds:

(13)

\frac{\partial\widetilde{\Psi}}{\partial\widetilde{n}}=\left|\frac{dz}{d\omega}\right|\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial n}=\frac{1}{|f^{\prime}(z)|}\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial n}.

2.3.
Reformulation as an integrodifferential PDE

In the particular case of the half-cylinder geometry,
\Omega

conformal map

\omega=f(z)=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}\left(z+\frac{1}{z}\right)},

into the half plane
\Omega^{\prime}:=\{(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\mathbb{\ R}^{2}:y^{\prime}<0\}

From (

(14)

\displaystyle\Delta\widetilde{\phi}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\text{for}\ y^{\prime}<0,

(15)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}_{tt}+|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})|\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}}{\partial\widetilde{n}}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\text{at }y^{\prime}=0,\ |x^{\prime}|\leq 1,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}}{\partial\widetilde{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\widetilde{j}(t,x^{\prime})}{|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})|},\quad\text{at }y^{\prime}=0,\ |x^{\prime}|>1,

By relation (

By taking the Fourier transform in (

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\Phi}(t,k,0)\widehat{\frac{-\sqrt{2}y^{\prime}}{\sqrt{\pi}(x^{\prime 2}+y^{\prime 2})}}.

By taking inverse Fourier transform

Since we want to establish properties on the normal derivative,

\displaystyle=\widehat{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}x^{\prime}}}\widetilde{\Phi}_{x^{\prime}}(t,k,0).

Then, taking inverse Fourier transform

(19)

\left.\widetilde{\phi}_{y^{\prime}}\right|_{y^{\prime}=0}=\frac{1}{\pi}P.V.\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}_{x^{\prime}}(t,\xi,0)}{x^{\prime}-\xi}d\xi=H\left(\left.\widetilde{\phi}_{x^{\prime}}\right|_{y^{\prime}=0}\right).

2.4.
The Dirichlet-Neumann operator

Let us consider in this section the basic situation when
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}=0
, on the solid walls of the domain
\Omega
. The value
of
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}
at the fluid interface may be viewed
as the result of an operator (the so-called Dirichlet-Neumann operator)
acting on the function
\phi
restricted to the interface. We are going to
deduce a few simple consequences obtained from the properties of this
operator. Keeping in mind the equation (
8
), we can easily deduce the
following mass conservation relation:

(21)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{-1}^{1}\zeta dx=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}dx=\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot\left(\nabla\phi\right)=0.

Notice then that, in the mapped coordinates, by (
13
)

(22)

0=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}dx=\int_{-1}^{1}\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}}{\partial\widetilde{n}}\frac{dx^{\prime}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}}{\partial\widetilde{n}}dx^{\prime},

and hence, from (
21
) and (
13
)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{-1}^{1}\widetilde{\zeta}\frac{dx^{\prime}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}=0.

Next, since
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}=0
at the solid boundaries of

\Omega
, we have

\int_{-1}^{1}\psi\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}dx=\int_{\partial\Omega}\psi\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}dx

for any harmonic function
\psi
also vanishing at the solid boundaries of
\Omega
, and therefore by Green’s identity we deduce

\int_{\partial\Omega}\psi\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}dx-\int_{\partial\Omega}\phi\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial n}dx=\int_{\Omega}(\psi\Delta\phi-\phi\Delta\psi)=0,

which implies

\int_{\partial\Omega}\psi\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}dx=\int_{\partial\Omega}\phi\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial n}dx,

or, equivalently,

\int_{-1}^{1}\widetilde{\psi}\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}}{\partial\widetilde{n}}dx^{\prime}=\int_{-1}^{1}\widetilde{\phi}\frac{\partial\widetilde{\psi}}{\partial\widetilde{n}}dx^{\prime},

showing that the Dirichlet-Neumann operator, mapping
\widetilde{\phi}
at
the free surface into
\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}}{\partial\widetilde{n}}
, is formally selfadjoint. The details on the
appropriate functional space where the operator is self-adjoint will be
given in section
4
.

Finally, from (
7
), (
21
), and the mass
conservation property (
10
):

(23)

\int_{-1}^{1}\phi_{t}dx=-\int_{-1}^{1}\zeta dx=0,

which implies

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{-1}^{1}\phi(t,x)dx=0.

Therefore, by choosing
\phi(0,x)
such that
\int_{-1}^{1}\phi(0,x)dx=0

(this can always be achieved by adding a suitable constant to a given
\phi
), we have

\int_{-1}^{1}\phi(t,x)dx=0,

implying

(24)

\int_{-1}^{1}\widetilde{\phi}(t,x^{\prime})\frac{dx^{\prime}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}=0.

3.
An associated Cauchy problem

As in the last section, let
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}=0
on the
solid walls of the domain
\Omega
. Then, from the integrodifferential
formulation (
20
), we obtain

(25)

\left.\widetilde{\phi}_{x^{\prime}}\right|_{y^{\prime}=0}=-\frac{1}{\pi}P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}_{y^{\prime}}(t,\xi,0)}{x^{\prime}-\xi}d\xi.

We observe two properties of
\widetilde{\phi}_{x^{\prime}}
. Firstly, by making use of the identity (see section 4.3 of

\frac{1}{\pi}P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}\frac{dx^{\prime}}{x^{\prime}-\xi}=0,\ \text{for }\xi\in\left(-1,1\right),

(27)

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}_{x^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}\left(P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}_{y^{\prime}}(\xi)}{x^{\prime}-\xi}d\xi\right)dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\widetilde{\phi}_{y^{\prime}}(\xi)\left(P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}(x^{\prime}-\xi)}dx^{\prime}\right)d\xi

\displaystyle=0.

Secondly, by the mass conservation
\int_{-1}^{1}\widetilde{\phi}_{y^{\prime}}(\xi)d\xi=0
(see (
22
)), and using (see (
51
), for

\frac{1}{\pi}P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{x^{\prime}}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}\frac{dx^{\prime}}{x^{\prime}-\xi}=1,\ \text{for }\xi\in(-1,1),

we also have

(28)

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{x^{\prime}\widetilde{\phi}_{x^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{x^{\prime}}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}\left(P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}_{y^{\prime}}(\xi)}{x^{\prime}-\xi}d\xi\right)dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\widetilde{\phi}_{y^{\prime}}(\xi)\left(P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{x^{\prime}}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}(x^{\prime}-\xi)}dx^{\prime}\right)d\xi

\displaystyle=-\int_{-1}^{1}\widetilde{\phi}_{y^{\prime}}(\xi)d\xi=0.

By using the inverse transform instead, from (
25

(29)

\left.\widetilde{\phi}_{y^{\prime}}\right|_{y^{\prime}=0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}\frac{1}{\pi}P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{1-\xi^{2}}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}_{x^{\prime}}(t,\xi,0)}{x^{\prime}-\xi}d\xi+\frac{C}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}},

]

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\widetilde{\phi}_{y^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}

Expression (

, while expression (

\widetilde{\phi}=\chi_{x^{\prime}}

so that

(note that integrability at

We have then

\chi_{y^{\prime}},&-1<x^{\prime}\leq 1,\\
0,&x^{\prime}>1.\end{cases}

Since

\chi_{x^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})=-\frac{1}{\pi}P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\chi_{y^{\prime}}(\xi)}{x^{\prime}-\xi}d\xi.

Therefore, inverting Hilbert transform

(36)

\chi_{y^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})=\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}\frac{1}{\pi}P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\xi^{2}}}\frac{\chi_{x^{\prime}}(\xi)}{x^{\prime}-\xi}d\xi,

and taking

\widetilde{\phi}_{y^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})=\partial_{x^{\prime}}\left(\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}\frac{1}{\pi}P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\xi^{2}}}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}(\xi)}{x^{\prime}-\xi}d\xi\right)

\widetilde{\phi}_{tt}=-\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|\partial_{x^{\prime}}\left(\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}\frac{1}{\pi}P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}(t,\xi)}{\sqrt{1-\xi^{2}}(x^{\prime}-\xi)}d\xi\right).

The equation (
38
) may be rewritten as an identical equation for
\widetilde{\zeta}

. We remark again that the

(see Appendix

).

We need to complement (

) with suitable initial and boundary

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}(0,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\phi}_{0}(x^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}_{t}(0,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\phi}_{1}(x^{\prime}),

where, by equation (

\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}_{1}(x^{\prime})}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}dx^{\prime}={\color[rgb]{0,0,1}-}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\zeta}(x^{\prime})}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}dx^{\prime}=0.

Moreover, from (

In the case when

and the initial data decomposed accordingly

\displaystyle S_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\widetilde{\phi}_{0}(x)+\widetilde{\phi}_{0}(-x)}{2},\quad N_{0}(x)=\frac{\widetilde{\phi}_{0}(x)-\widetilde{\phi}_{0}(-x)}{2},

\displaystyle S_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\widetilde{\phi}_{1}(x)+\widetilde{\phi}_{1}(-x)}{2},\quad N_{1}(x)=\frac{\widetilde{\phi}_{1}(x)-\widetilde{\phi}_{1}(-x)}{2}.

Then, one can consider the evolution problem for symmetric and antisymmetric

We discuss now on boundary conditions for (

In the case of free-end boundary conditions one imposes

\widetilde{\phi}_{x^{\prime}}(t,\pm 1)=0\ \ \text{(free-end boundary

We discuss now the case with fluid injection, i.e. with the condition (

\ \int\frac{\widetilde{j}(t,x^{\prime})}{|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})|}dx^{\prime}=0

Inverting as in (

\displaystyle\left.\widetilde{\phi}_{y^{\prime}}\right|_{y^{\prime}=0}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x^{\prime}}\left(\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}\frac{1}{\pi}P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\xi^{2}}}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}(t,\xi,0)}{x^{\prime}-\xi}d\xi\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\sqrt{1-\xi^{2}}}{(x^{\prime}-\xi)}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-1,1]}\frac{\widetilde{j}(t,z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|(\xi-z)}dzd\xi,

\widetilde{\phi}_{tt}=-\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|\partial_{x^{\prime}}\left(\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}\frac{1}{\pi}P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}(t,\xi)}{\sqrt{1-\xi^{2}}(x^{\prime}-\xi)}d\xi\right)+\widetilde{h}(t,x^{\prime}),

\widetilde{h}(t,x^{\prime})=\frac{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\sqrt{1-\xi^{2}}}{(x^{\prime}-\xi)}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-1,1]}\frac{\widetilde{j}(t,z)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|(\xi-z)}dzd\xi.

Notice that

implies expression (

\chi_{x^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})=-\frac{1}{\pi}P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\chi_{y^{\prime}}(\xi)}{x^{\prime}-\xi}d\xi-\frac{1}{\pi}P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-1,1]}\frac{J(\xi)}{x^{\prime}-\xi}d\xi

, inverting the

) in the first term at

) and using finally (

).

4.
Spectrum of the sloshing problem

From now on, from equation (
45
) and to summarize
the computations from the preceding sections, we are concerned with the
following Initial Value Problem,

(47)

\begin{cases}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}_{tt}}{|f^{\prime}|}+\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\phi}=\widetilde{h}(t,x^{\prime}),\quad&(t,x^{\prime})\in(0,\infty)\times(-1,1),\\
\widetilde{\phi}(0,x^{\prime})=\widetilde{\phi}_{0}(x^{\prime}),&x^{\prime}\in(-1,1),\\
\widetilde{\phi}_{t}(0,x^{\prime})=\widetilde{\phi}_{1}(x^{\prime}),&x^{\prime}\in(-1,1),\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{A}
is the integral, non-local operator

\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\phi}\equiv\partial_{x^{\prime}}\left(\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}\frac{1}{\pi}P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}(\xi)}{\sqrt{1-\xi^{2}}(x^{\prime}-\xi)}d\xi\right).

Following
[
21
]
, to study the interior controllability problem (
47
), we need to consider the homogeneous (backward in time)
adjoint version in
(0,T)
as that given in (
38
). For that purpose, let us
consider first the eigenvalue problem

(48)

\lambda\frac{\widetilde{\phi}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}=\partial_{x^{\prime}}\left(\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}P.V.\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}(\xi)}{\sqrt{1-\xi^{2}}(x^{\prime}-\xi)}d\xi\right).

Let
T_{n}(x)
,
U_{n}(x)
, with
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, be the Tchebyshev polynomials of the first and second kind respectively. They are
defined as the polynomial solutions of the equations (see

[
1
]
for details)

\displaystyle T_{n}(\cos\theta)

\displaystyle=\cos(n\theta),

\displaystyle U_{n}(\cos\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin((n+1)\theta)}{\sin\theta}.

They satisfy the following orthogonality relations in
L^{2}(-1,1)
with the
corresponding inner products
\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{fg}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}dx
,
\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{-1}^{1}fg\sqrt{1-x^{2}}dx
:

(49)

\int_{-1}^{1}T_{n}(x)T_{m}(x)\frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}=\begin{cases}0,\ \text{if }n\neq m,\\
\pi\ \text{if }n=m=0,\\
\frac{\pi}{2}\ \text{if }n=m\neq 0,\end{cases}

(50)

\int_{-1}^{1}U_{n}(x)U_{m}(x)\sqrt{1-x^{2}}dx=\begin{cases}0,\ \text{if }n\neq m,\\
\frac{\pi}{2}\ \text{if }n=m.\end{cases}

Moreover, for
n\geq 1
, we have relations

(51)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{T_{n}(\xi)}{\sqrt{1-\xi^{2}}(x^{\prime}-\xi)}d\xi

\displaystyle=-U_{n-1}(x^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx^{\prime}}\left(\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}U_{n-1}(x^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=-r\frac{T_{n}(x^{\prime})}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}.

By writing

(52)

\widetilde{\phi}(\xi)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}T_{n}(\xi),

and using the identities above, we find

(53)

\partial_{x^{\prime}}\left(\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}\frac{1}{\pi}P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}(\xi)}{\sqrt{1-\xi^{2}}(x^{\prime}-\xi)}d\xi\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}na_{n}\frac{T_{n}(x^{\prime})}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}.

We use now the fact that
\int_{-1}^{1}\widetilde{\phi}(x^{\prime})\frac{dx^{\prime}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}=0
(see (
24
)), to deduce

a_{0}\int_{-1}^{1}T_{0}(x^{\prime})\frac{dx^{\prime}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\int_{-1}^{1}T_{n}(x^{\prime})\frac{dx^{\prime}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}=0.

So that

a_{0}=-\frac{1}{\int_{-1}^{1}T_{0}(x^{\prime})\frac{dx^{\prime}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\int_{-1}^{1}T_{n}(x^{\prime})\frac{dx^{\prime}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|},

and conclude the estimate

a_{0}^{2}\leq\frac{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-1}^{1}T_{n}(x^{\prime})\frac{dx^{\prime}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}\right)^{2}}{\left(\int_{-1}^{1}T_{0}(x^{\prime})\frac{dx^{\prime}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}\right)^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{2}.

Now, let
c_{n}
be such that

\frac{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}{|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})|}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{n}T_{n}(x^{\prime}).

Then, by the orthogonality of the Tchebyshev polynomials,

c_{0}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{T_{0}(x^{\prime})}{|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})|}dx^{\prime},\quad c_{n}=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{T_{n}(x^{\prime})}{|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})|}dx^{\prime}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1-x^{\prime 2}}{|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})|^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}dx^{\prime}=\sum_{n,m=0}^{\infty}c_{n}c_{m}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{T_{n}T_{m}}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}dx^{\prime}=\pi c_{0}^{2}+\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}^{2},

namely

\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\int_{-1}^{1}T_{0}(x^{\prime})\frac{dx^{\prime}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}\right)^{2}+\frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-1}^{1}T_{n}(x^{\prime})\frac{dx^{\prime}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}\right)^{2}=\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}\frac{dx^{\prime}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|^{2}}<\infty,

which implies

(54)

a_{0}^{2}\leq C\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{2}.

Let

(55)

\widetilde{\psi}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}T_{n}(x^{\prime}).

We consider now the scalar product

(\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\phi},\widetilde{\psi})_{L^{2}}=\int_{-1}^{1}\widetilde{\psi}\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}}{\partial\widetilde{n}}dx^{\prime},

and find

(56)

(\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\phi},\widetilde{\psi})_{L^{2}}=\int_{-1}^{1}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{m}T_{m}(x^{\prime})\right)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}na_{n}\frac{T_{n}(x^{\prime})}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}\right)dx^{\prime}=\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}na_{n}b_{n},

so that,
(\widetilde{\psi},\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\phi})_{L^{2}}=(\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\psi},\widetilde{\phi})_{L^{2}}
, implying the selfadjoint
character of the operator
\mathcal{A}
, a fact already shown in a previous
section.

We study the problem

(57)

\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\phi}=u,

where
u\in L_{w}^{2}
with
w=\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}
and

L_{w}^{2}\equiv\left\{u:\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}\left|u\right|^{2}dx^{\prime}<\infty\right\}.

Since
\left\{T_{n}(x^{\prime}){/\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
form an orthogonal base for
L_{w}^{2}
, given
u\in L_{w}^{2}

we can expand

u=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}u_{n}\frac{T_{n}(x^{\prime})}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}},

and hence, assuming

(58)

u_{0}=\int_{-1}^{1}u(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}=0,

write the following weak version of (
57
)

(59)

(\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\phi},\widetilde{\psi})_{L^{2}}=(u,\widetilde{\psi})_{L^{2}},

to be satisfied for any
\widetilde{\psi}
in

(60)

H_{w^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\equiv\left\{\widetilde{\psi}:\left\|\widetilde{\psi}\right\|_{H_{w^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}=\left\|\widetilde{\psi}\right\|_{L_{w^{-1}}^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\left(\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{T_{n}(x^{\prime})}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}\widetilde{\psi}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}\right)^{2}<\infty\right\}

such that, in addition,

(61)

\int_{-1}^{1}\widetilde{\psi}(x^{\prime})\frac{dx^{\prime}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}=0.

By using the preceding functional framework, we can state a result on
existence of weak solutions for problem (
57
).

Lemma 2
.

Let
u\in L^{2}_{w}
satisfying (
58
). Then, there exists a unique
weak solution
\widetilde{\phi}\in H_{w^{-1}}^{1/2}
for problem (
57
).

Proof.

We can write the equation (
59
) in the form

(62)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}na_{n}b_{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}u_{n}b_{n},

making it clear that
(\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\phi},\widetilde{\psi})_{L^{2}}

defines a continuous bilinear form on
H_{w^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Indeed,

Remark 1
.

By using the orthogonality property (
49
) on (
55
) we have, for
n\geq 1
,

\sqrt{n}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{T_{n}(x^{\prime})}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}\widetilde{\psi}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}=\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{n}b_{n},

or, equivalently,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\left(\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{T_{n}(x^{\prime})}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}\widetilde{\psi}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}\right)^{2}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|b_{n}|^{2}.

Notice, from this last equality and (
56
), that
H_{w^{-1}}^{1/2}
can be written as

H_{w^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\equiv\left\{\widetilde{\psi}:\left\|\widetilde{\psi}\right\|_{H_{w^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}=\left\|\widetilde{\psi}\right\|_{L_{w^{-1}}^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\pi}{2}(\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\psi},\widetilde{\psi})_{L^{2}}<\infty\right\}.

Thus from (
56
) and the last remark, we have

\displaystyle|(\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\phi},\widetilde{\psi})_{L^{2}}|

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}\left|\sum_{n\geq 1}na_{n}b_{n}\right|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}n|a_{n}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}n|b_{n}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\pi}\|\widetilde{\phi}\|_{H_{w^{-1}}^{1/2}}\|\widetilde{\psi}\|_{H_{w^{-1}}^{1/2}}.

(\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\phi},\widetilde{\psi})_{L^{2}}
is also coercive
(the
L_{w^{-1}}^{2}
part of the norm of
\widetilde{\phi}
is trivially
bounded by
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}na_{n}^{2}
, except for
a_{0}^{2}
that is
also bounded by (
54
)):

Remark 2
.

By using the orthogonality property (
49
) on (
52
),

\|\widetilde{\phi}\|_{L_{w^{-1}}^{2}}^{2}=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{|\widetilde{\phi}|^{2}}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}dx^{\prime}=\pi a_{0}^{2}+\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{2}.

Moreover,

a_{n}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{T_{n}(x^{\prime})}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}\widetilde{\phi}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime},\quad&n=0,\\
\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{T_{n}(x^{\prime})}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}\widetilde{\phi}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime},&n\geq 1.\end{cases}

Then, from the last remark and (
54
), coerciveness holds as follows:

(63)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\phi},\widetilde{\phi})_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{n\geq 1}n|a_{n}|^{2}\geq C\left[\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{2}+\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}na_{n}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq C\left[\pi a_{0}^{2}+\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}na_{n}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=C\left[\|\widetilde{\phi}\|_{L_{w^{-1}}^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\pi}{2}(\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\phi},\widetilde{\phi})\right]

\displaystyle=C\|\widetilde{\phi}\|_{H_{w^{-1}}^{1/2}}^{2},

for a constant
C>0
.

Finally,
u\in L_{w}^{2}
defines a linear continuous functional on
H_{w^{-1}}^{1/2}
:

\displaystyle\left|\int_{-1}^{1}u\widetilde{\phi}dx^{\prime}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt[4]{1-x^{\prime 2}}|u|\frac{\widetilde{\phi}}{\sqrt[4]{1-x^{\prime 2}}}dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L_{w}^{2}}\|\widetilde{\phi}\|_{L_{w^{-1}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L_{w}^{2}}\|\widetilde{\phi}\|_{H_{w^{-1}}^{1/2}}.

Hence, by Lax-Milgram’s theorem, there exists a unique weak solution
\widetilde{\phi}
to (
57
) that belongs to
H_{w^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
∎

If we consider now
u=\frac{v}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}
then the problem

\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\phi}=\frac{v}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}

where
v
is such that

(64)

\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}\frac{v^{2}(x^{\prime})}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|^{2}}dx^{\prime}<\infty

has also a unique solution
\widetilde{\phi}\in H_{w^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Note that (
64
) is satisfied if
v\in L_{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|^{-1}}^{2}
provided there exists a
constant
C
such that

(65)

\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|\geq C\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}},\quad x^{\prime}\in[-1,1],

because

\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}\frac{v^{2}(x^{\prime})}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|^{2}}dx^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{C^{2}}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{v^{2}(x^{\prime})}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}dx^{\prime},

or

\left\|u\right\|_{L_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}}^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{C}\left\|u\right\|_{L_{w^{-1}}^{2}}^{2}.

We define now the operator
\mathcal{T}
such that
\widetilde{\phi}=\mathcal{T}v
. If we view
\mathcal{T}
as an operator from
L_{\left|f^{\prime}\right|^{-1}}^{2}
to
L_{\left|f^{\prime}\right|^{-1}}^{2}
, since
H_{w^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\subset L_{w^{-1}}^{2}\subset L_{\left|f^{\prime}\right|^{-1}}^{2}
, we can see that
\mathcal{T}

is a selfadjoint operator (due to the selfadjoint character of
\mathcal{A}
) and also a compact operator (due to the compact embedding
H_{w^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\subset L_{w^{-1}}^{2}
(see Appendix
B
) and the
continuous inclusion
L_{w^{-1}}^{2}\subset L_{\left|f^{\prime}\right|^{-1}}^{2}
) . Moreover,
\ker(\mathcal{T})=\left\{0\right\}
and

(\mathcal{T}v,v)_{L_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}}^{2}}=(\widetilde{\phi},|f^{\prime}|\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\phi})_{L_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}}^{2}}=(\widetilde{\phi},\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\phi})_{L^{2}}\geq 0,

for any
v\in L_{\left|f^{\prime}\right|^{-1}}^{2}
. Therefore, by
the spectral decomposition theorem,
L_{\left|f^{\prime}\right|^{-1}}^{2}
admits a Hilbert basis
\left\{e_{n}\right\}
formed by
eigenvectors of
\mathcal{T}
, with eigenvalues
\mu_{n}
such that
\mu_{n}>0
and
\mu_{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. We have then
\mathcal{T}e_{n}=\mu_{n}e_{n}
,
e_{n}\in H_{w^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
, and thus

(66)

{(\mathcal{A}e_{n},\widetilde{\psi})_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{\mu_{n}}(\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{T}e_{n}),\widetilde{\psi})_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{\mu_{n}}\left(\frac{e_{n}}{|f^{\prime}|},\widetilde{\psi}\right)_{L^{2}},}\quad\text{for any }\widetilde{\psi}\in H_{w^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This implies
e_{n}
is a weak solution to the eigenvalue problem (
48
) with
\lambda=\lambda_{n}=\mu_{n}^{-1}
. The base can be made
orthonormal, i.e.

\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{e_{i}e_{j}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}dx^{\prime}=\delta_{ij}.

Let us remark that condition (
65
) is satisfied provided the angles
\alpha_{1,2}
between the fluid interface and the solid container satisfy

\alpha_{1,2}\geq\frac{\pi}{2},

since
|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})|
has a singularity weaker (i.e. with
larger exponent) than
|x\pm 1|^{1/2}
otherwise
[
6
]
.

We summarize the results above in the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain such that the interior angles between the free
liquid interface and the solid are greater or equal than
\pi/2
, so that (
65
) is fulfilled. There exist a Hilbert basis

\{e_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
of
L_{\left|f^{\prime}\right|^{-1}}^{2}

and a sequence
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
of real numbers with
\lambda_{n}>0

\forall n
and
\lambda_{n}\rightarrow+\infty
such that

e_{n}\in H_{w^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}},

\mathcal{A}e_{n}=\lambda_{n}\frac{e_{n}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}.

We will show next that the eigenfunctions
e_{n}
possess
further regularity. From (
52
)-(
53
), if
e_{n}(x^{\prime})=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}T_{m}(x^{\prime})
, by using (
49
) we have

\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}\left|\mathcal{A}e_{n}\right|^{2}dx^{\prime}=\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{2}a_{m}^{2}.

Therefore, by using condition (
65
),

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}\left|\mathcal{A}e_{n}\right|^{2}dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{n}^{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}\frac{\left|e_{n}\right|^{2}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|^{2}}dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\left|e_{n}\right|^{2}}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}dx^{\prime}=C_{n}\|e_{n}\|_{L_{w^{-1}}^{2}}^{2},

we have that
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{2}a_{m}^{2}
is bounded by
\|e_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{w^{-1}}}^{2}
. On the other hand, since
\frac{dT_{m}(x^{\prime})}{dx^{\prime}}=mU_{m-1}(x^{\prime})
, from the orthogonality property (
50
) we deduce

\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}\left|\frac{de_{n}}{dx^{\prime}}\right|^{2}dx^{\prime}=\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{2}a_{m}^{2},

which is bounded by
\|e_{n}\|_{L_{w^{-1}}^{2}}^{2}
. Hence,
e_{n}\in H_{w^{-1}}^{1}
implying, by Sobolev embeddings, that
e_{n}(x^{\prime})
is a continuous function in
(-1,1)
. Moreover, it is bounded at
x=\pm 1
since:

e_{n}(x^{\prime})-e_{n}(-1)=\int_{-1}^{x^{\prime}}e_{n}^{\prime}(x)dx\leq\left(\int_{-1}^{x^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{-1}^{x^{\prime}}\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\left|e_{n}^{\prime}(x)\right|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

So that

\sup_{-1\leq x^{\prime}\leq-1+\delta}\frac{\left|e_{n}(x^{\prime})-e_{n}(-1)\right|^{2}}{\left|1+x^{\prime}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq C\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\left|e_{n}^{\prime}\right|^{2}dx,

and hence

\left|e_{n}(x^{\prime})-e_{n}(-1)\right|\leq C\left|1+x^{\prime}\right|^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Identical estimate may be obtained in the neighborhood of
x^{\prime}=1
.

Note that no condition has been imposed on the eigenfunctions at
x^{\prime}=\pm 1
.
The eigenfunctions correspond to free-end boundary conditions. We can also
consider the pinned-end boundary condition by writing the same weak
formulation (
59
) but assuming that
\widetilde{\psi}
belongs to
the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(-1,1)
in the topology defined by the
H_{w^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
norm and satisfying (
61
). We denote such
space as
H_{w^{-1},0}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. The same arguments as for the
free-end case lead to the existence of a complete set of eigenfunctions as
in the Theorem above.

As a final remark, notice that for bounded symmetric
\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|
, one has
L_{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|^{-1}}^{2}\subset L^{2}
and hence vectors in
L_{\left|f^{\prime}\right|^{-1}}^{2}
may be expanded in
trigonometric basis of
L^{2}
. Using the base
\left\{\sin(n\pi x^{\prime})\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
one can approximate antisymmetric
eigenfunctions (solutions to (
48
)) with pinned contact lines while
the set
\left\{\cos\left(\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\pi x^{\prime}\right)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
(with the extra mass conservation condition also
imposed) allows to approximate symmetric eigenfunctions. Likewise, the sets
\left\{\sin\left(\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\pi x^{\prime}\right)\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
and
\left\{\cos(n\pi x^{\prime})\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
allow to find antisymmetric and symmetric
eigenfunctions with free-end condition. This approach was followed in
[
14
]
, in order to compute eigenvalues and eigenfunctions
numerically for various domains.

5.
Observability and energy estimates

We will consider next the control problem consisting of finding
\widetilde{h}(t,x^{\prime})
in equation (
45
) such that for given initial
data the solution vanishes at some time
T
. As it is customary in
controllability theory, we need to consider first the homogeneous adjoint
problem and obtain observability estimates.

The analysis presented above allows us to consider the solution in
(0,T)

of the following homogeneous adjoint problem (see (
38
))

(67)

\frac{\widetilde{\phi}_{tt}}{\left|f^{\prime}\right|}+\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\phi}=0,

with suitable initial conditions
\widetilde{\phi}(0)=\widetilde{\phi}_{0}
,
\widetilde{\phi}^{\prime}(0)=\widetilde{\phi}_{1}
, whose solution we
can write as

(68)

\widetilde{\phi}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(A_{n}\cos(\theta_{n}t)+B_{n}\sin(\theta_{n}t)\right)e_{n},

where
\theta_{n}=\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}
and

\widetilde{\phi}_{0}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}e_{n},\quad\widetilde{\phi}_{1}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\theta_{n}B_{n}e_{n},

with

A_{n}=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}_{0}e_{n}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}dx^{\prime}=(\widetilde{\phi}_{0},e_{n})_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}},\quad B_{n}=\frac{1}{\theta_{n}}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}_{1}e_{n}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}dx^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\theta_{n}}(\widetilde{\phi}_{1},e_{n})_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}}.

Hence
\|\widetilde{\phi}\|_{L^{2}(L_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}}^{2})}^{2}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}^{2}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}dx^{\prime}dt
can be
bounded from below by

(69)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{n}\left(A_{n}^{2}\cos^{2}(\theta_{n}t)+B_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}(\theta_{n}t)+2A_{n}B_{n}\sin(\theta_{n}t)\cos(\theta_{n}t)\right)dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\left[\frac{A_{n}^{2}}{2}\left(T+\frac{\sin(2\theta_{n}T)}{2\theta_{n}}\right)+\frac{B_{n}^{2}}{2}\left(T-\frac{\sin(2\theta_{n}T)}{2\theta_{n}}\right)+A_{n}B_{n}\frac{1-\cos(2\theta_{n}T)}{2\theta_{n}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{n}\left[\frac{A_{n}^{2}}{2}\left(T+\frac{\sin(2\theta_{n}T)}{2\theta_{n}}\right)+\frac{B_{n}^{2}}{2}\left(T-\frac{\sin(2\theta_{n}T)}{2\theta_{n}}\right)-\frac{A_{n}^{2}+B_{n}^{2}}{2}\frac{1-\cos(2\theta_{n}T)}{2\theta_{n}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{T}{2}\sum_{n}\left[A_{n}^{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{2\theta_{n}T}+\frac{\sin(2\theta_{n}T)}{2\theta_{n}T}+\frac{\cos(2\theta_{n}T)}{2\theta_{n}T}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\phantom{\ =}\left.+B_{n}^{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{2\theta_{n}T}-\frac{\sin(2\theta_{n}T)}{2\theta_{n}T}+\frac{\cos(2\theta_{n}T)}{2\theta_{n}T}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq C\frac{T}{2}\sum_{n}\left[A_{n}^{2}+B_{n}^{2}\right],

for some positive
C>0
and
T>2.42/2\min(\theta_{n})=2.42/2\theta_{1}
.

From the orthogonality of the Hilbert basis
\{e_{n}\}
,

(70)

\|\widetilde{\phi}_{0}\|_{L_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}}^{2}}^{2}=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}_{0}^{2}}{|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})|}dx^{\prime}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}^{2}.

Moreover, from (
66
), the decomposition given in (
68
), and (
67
) we conclude
\lambda_{n}=\theta_{n}^{2}
and thus

(71)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}B_{n}^{2}=\sum_{n}\left(\frac{1}{\theta_{n}}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}_{1}e_{n}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}dx^{\prime}\right)^{2}=\sum_{n}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\left(\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}_{1}e_{n}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}dx^{\prime}\right)^{2}.

Let us define, accordingly, the space

\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\left\{\widetilde{\phi}:\sum_{n}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n}}\left(\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}e_{n}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}dx^{\prime}\right)^{2}<\infty\right\},

and its dual space

\widetilde{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\left\{\widetilde{\phi}:\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}\left(\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}e_{n}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}dx^{\prime}\right)^{2}<\infty\right\}.

Note that, by (
56
) and definition of
\lambda_{n}
and
e_{n}
,

{\ \left\|\widetilde{\phi}\right\|_{\widetilde{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}=\sum\left(\int\frac{\widetilde{\phi}\lambda_{n}e_{n}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x)\right|}\right)\left(\int\frac{\widetilde{\phi}e_{n}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x)\right|}\right)=\int\widetilde{\phi}\mathcal{\ A}\widetilde{\phi}=\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{n\geq 1}na_{n}^{2}\sim\left\|\widetilde{\phi}\right\|_{H_{w^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}.}

From (
69
)–(
71
), we conclude then for some
C>0
and
T

sufficiently large,

C^{-1}T\left(\left\|\widetilde{\phi}_{1}\right\|_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+\left\|\widetilde{\phi}_{0}\right\|_{L_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}}^{2}}^{2}\right)\geq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}^{2}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}dx^{\prime}dt\geq CT\left(\left\|\widetilde{\phi}_{1}\right\|_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+\left\|\widetilde{\phi}_{0}\right\|_{L_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}}^{2}}^{2}\right).

This proves the following theorem:

Theorem 2
.

The equation (
67
) is observable in time
T>T_{0}
for some
T_{0}

sufficiently large. That is, there exists a positive constant
C>0
such
that for
T
sufficiently large

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}^{2}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}dx^{\prime}dt\geq CT\left(\left\|\widetilde{\phi}_{1}\right\|_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+\left\|\widetilde{\phi}_{0}\right\|_{L_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}}^{2}}^{2}\right).

We consider next the nonhomogeneous problem (as in (
45
))

(72)

\frac{\widetilde{\psi}_{tt}}{\left|f^{\prime}\right|}+\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\psi}=\frac{\widetilde{h}}{|f^{\prime}|}.

It will be convenient the following energy estimate:

(73)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\psi}_{t}^{2}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}dx^{\prime}+\int_{-1}^{1}\widetilde{\psi}_{t}\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\psi}dx^{\prime}=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{h}\widetilde{\psi}_{t}}{|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})|}dx^{\prime}.

If we let

\widetilde{\psi}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}e_{n},

then

\displaystyle c_{n}=\int_{-1}^{1}

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{\psi}e_{n}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}dx^{\prime},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\psi}_{t}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n,t}e_{n}\quad\implies\quad

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\psi_{t}^{2}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}dx^{\prime}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n,t}^{2}.

Noticing that

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\widetilde{\psi}_{t}\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\psi}dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\left(\sum_{n}c_{n,t}e_{n}\right)\left(\sum_{m}\lambda_{m}c_{m}\frac{e_{m}}{|f^{\prime}|}\right)dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}c_{n,t}c_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}c_{n}^{2},

(
73
) is equivalent to the following inequality

\frac{dE}{dt}\leq\|\widetilde{h}\|_{L_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}}^{2}}E^{\frac{1}{2}},

for the energy defined as

E=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n}c_{n,t}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}c_{n}^{2}.

Hence, the natural initial data for which the problem is well-posed is
\left(\widetilde{\psi}_{0},\widetilde{\psi}_{1}\right)\in\widetilde{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\times L_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}}^{2}
and, the energy is bounded
provided
\int_{0}^{T}\|\widetilde{h}\|_{L_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}}^{2}}^{2}dt<\infty
, i.e.
\widetilde{h}\in L^{2}(0,T;L_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}}^{2})
. Namely,
we have the following:

Theorem 3
.

Given
f
a conformal mapping that satisfies condition (
65
), for any
\widetilde{h}\in L^{2}(0,T;L_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}}^{2})
and
\left(\widetilde{\psi}_{0},\widetilde{\psi}_{1}\right)\in\widetilde{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\times L_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}}^{2}
equation (
72
) has a
unique weak solution

(\widetilde{\psi},\widetilde{\psi}^{\prime})\in C([0,T];H_{w^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\times L_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}}^{2}).

6.
Controllability

Once we have studied in the previous sections the forward evolution equation
and the backward homogeneous system, given by (
72
) and (
67
)
respectively, we are in position of comparing them to get the
controllability condition on the system.

That is, if we assume the control drives the initial data of system (
72
) to zero, by multiplying the source term in (
72
) by the
solution to the adjoint problem (
67
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{h}\widetilde{\phi}}{|f^{\prime}|}dx^{\prime}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-1}^{1}\left[\frac{\widetilde{\psi}_{tt}}{|f^{\prime}|}+\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\psi}\right]\widetilde{\phi}dx^{\prime}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\psi}_{t}\widetilde{\phi}|_{0}^{T}}{|f^{\prime}|}dx^{\prime}-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\psi}_{t}\widetilde{\phi}_{t}}{|f^{\prime}|}dx^{\prime}dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-1}^{1}\widetilde{\psi}\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\phi}dx^{\prime}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\psi}_{t}\widetilde{\phi}|_{0}^{T}}{|f^{\prime}|}dx^{\prime}-\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\psi}\widetilde{\phi}_{t}|_{0}^{T}}{|f^{\prime}|}dx^{\prime}+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-1}^{1}\widetilde{\psi}\left[\frac{\widetilde{\phi}_{tt}}{|f^{\prime}|}+\mathcal{A}\widetilde{\phi}\right]dx^{\prime}dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\psi}_{1}\widetilde{\phi}_{0}}{|f^{\prime}|}dx^{\prime}+\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\psi}_{0}\widetilde{\phi}_{1}}{|f^{\prime}|}dx^{\prime},

where
(\widetilde{\psi}_{0},\widetilde{\psi}_{1})=(\widetilde{\psi}(0),\widetilde{\psi}_{t}(0))\in\widetilde{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\times L_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}}^{2}
. So that
\widetilde{h}
can be chosen as the
minimizer of the functional

J[\widetilde{\phi}_{0},\widetilde{\phi}_{1}]=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}^{2}}{|f^{\prime}|}dx^{\prime}dt+\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\psi}_{1}\widetilde{\phi}_{0}}{|f^{\prime}|}dx^{\prime}-\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\psi}_{0}\widetilde{\phi}_{1}}{|f^{\prime}|}dx^{\prime}.

Notice that

\left|\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\psi}_{1}\widetilde{\phi}_{0}}{|f^{\prime}|}dx^{\prime}-\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\psi}_{0}\widetilde{\phi}_{1}}{|f^{\prime}|}dx^{\prime}\right|\leq\|\widetilde{\phi}_{0}\|_{L_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}}^{2}}\|\widetilde{\psi}_{1}\|_{L_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}}^{2}}+\|\widetilde{\phi}_{1}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\|\widetilde{\psi}_{0}\|_{\widetilde{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

As it is customary in controllability theory, coerciveness of the functional
J
is guaranteed if the adjoint problem is observable in time
T
, that is:

(74)

\int_{0}^{T}dt\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}^{2}}{|f^{\prime}|}dx^{\prime}\geq C\left(\|\widetilde{\phi}(0)\|_{L_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}}^{2}}^{2}+\|\widetilde{\phi}_{t}(0)\|_{\widetilde{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\right),

a fact that was proved in the previous section. Hence, we have proved the
following controllability theorem:

Theorem 4
.

The system (
72
) is exactly controllable in time
T
. That
is, for any initial data
\left(\widetilde{\psi}_{0},\widetilde{\psi}_{1}\right)\in\widetilde{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\times L_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}}^{2}
,
there exist
\widetilde{h}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}})
and
T>0

such that

\|\widetilde{\psi}\|_{H_{w^{-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}}=0,\quad\text{for }t>T.

The fact that the control
\widetilde{h}\in L^{2}((0,T);L_{|f^{\prime}|^{-1}}^{2})
implies that one can write

\widetilde{h}(t,x^{\prime})=\sum_{n}\widetilde{h}_{n}(t)e_{n},

with

\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{n}|\widetilde{h}_{n}(t)|^{2}dt<\infty.

We are going to discuss next on how to approach the control function
\widetilde{h}
, defined at the fluid interface by means of a function
j

defined at the solid boundaries. This function
j
will represent a fluid
injection at certain points
\left\{x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime},...,x_{N}^{\prime}\right\}
of the solid boundary with a flow rate
\left\{j_{1}(t),j_{2}(t),...,j_{N}(t)\right\}
and under the mass
conservation condition

\sum_{i=1}^{N}j_{i}(t)=0.

More precisely,

(75)

\frac{\partial\widetilde{\phi}}{\partial\widetilde{n}}=\sum_{i}j_{i}(t)\delta(x^{\prime}-x_{i}^{\prime}).

We replace the expression for
\widetilde{j}(t,z,0)
in (
46
) by the
right hand side of (
75
) at the solid side walls and obtain

\displaystyle\frac{|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})|}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}

\displaystyle P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\sqrt{1-\xi^{2}}}{(x^{\prime}-\xi)}\int_{\mathbb{\ R}\setminus[-1,1]}\frac{\widetilde{j}(t,z,0)}{|f^{\prime}(z)|(\xi-z)}dzd\xi

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\frac{j_{i}(t)}{|f^{\prime}(x_{i}^{\prime})|}\frac{|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})|}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\sqrt{1-\xi^{2}}}{(x^{\prime}-\xi)(\xi-x_{i}^{\prime})}d\xi

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\frac{j_{i}(t)}{|f^{\prime}(x_{i}^{\prime})|}\frac{|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})|}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}\frac{1}{x^{\prime}-x_{i}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{1-\xi^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{x^{\prime}-\xi}+\frac{1}{\xi-x_{i}^{\prime}}\right]d\xi

(76)

\displaystyle=\frac{|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})|}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}\sum_{i}j_{i}(t)\frac{G(x_{i}^{\prime})-G(x)}{\pi^{2}|f^{\prime}(x_{i}^{\prime})|}\frac{1}{x^{\prime}-x_{i}^{\prime}},

with

G(x_{i}^{\prime})=P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\sqrt{1-\xi^{2}}}{(\xi-x_{i}^{\prime})}d\xi.

Hence, the challenge is to approximate the control function
\widetilde{h}

by (
6
). One possibility is to approximate the first
N-1
modes
so that

\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{h}e_{i}}{\left|f^{\prime}(x^{\prime})\right|}dx^{\prime}=h_{i}(t),\quad i=1,...,N-1,

or, denoting
m_{ij}=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}|f^{\prime}(x_{j}^{\prime})|}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{G(x_{j}^{\prime})-G(x)}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}(x^{\prime}-x_{j}^{\prime})}e_{i}dx^{\prime}
and including the mass conservation
condition, solving the system

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}m_{ij}j_{j}(t)

\displaystyle=h_{i}(t),\quad i=1,...,N-1,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}j_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=0.

The fact that
h_{i}(t)\in L^{2}\left(0,T\right)
implies
j_{i}(t)\in L^{2}\left(0,T\right)
.

Let us summarize what we have achieved so far in connection
with the injection of fluid problem and the control of splashing appearing
in a cooper converter
[
9
,
15
]
. First,
the main relation between the inner source
\widetilde{h}
, and the
injection of fluids
\widetilde{j}
at the solid boundary of the half-plane,
is given by (
46
). Second, by the computations above, we found the
source
\widetilde{h}
given in (
75
), which corresponds to the fluid
injection of jets
\widetilde{j}
on a finite number of points. Third, once
\widetilde{j}
is known on the half-plane, we can recover
j
, on the
cylindrical container, from (
13
). Of course this procedure is
directly connected to the controllability, through Theorem
4
.

Finally, it is important to highlight that, although we used the case of the
cylinder as a reference, all the computations are valid for any simply
connected domain through the conformal mapping term
|f^{\prime}|
, which
appears as a factor on the main operator for the Cauchy problem.

As far as the three-dimensional case is concerned, the conformal mapping
strategy cannot be replicated directly. While it is true that the integral
equations can be formulated in an equivalent frame in the 3d case (see
[
18
]
for more details).

Appendix A
Equivalent expressions of the operator
\mathcal{A}

We discuss in this appendix the relation between different expressions of
the operator
\mathcal{A}
based on expansions in Tchebyshev polynomials. We
remind that the Tchebyshev polynomials
\left\{T_{n}(x^{\prime})\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
form an orthogonal basis in
L_{(1-x^{\prime 2})^{-1/2}}^{2}(-1,1)
and the Tchebyshev polynomials
\left\{U_{n}(x^{\prime})\right\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
form an orthogonal basis in
L_{(1-x^{\prime 2})^{1/2}}^{2}(-1,1)
. Remind that tangential and normal
derivatives of
\widetilde{\phi}
in the interval
(-1,1)
are related by

(77)

\widetilde{\phi}_{x^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})=-\frac{1}{\pi}P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\widetilde{\phi}_{y^{\prime}}(\xi)}{x^{\prime}-\xi}d\xi.

Let

(78)

\widetilde{\phi}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}T_{n},

and observe the following well-known identities

(79)

\displaystyle\frac{dT_{n}}{dx^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=nU_{n-1},

(80)

\displaystyle-U_{n-1}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{T_{n}(\xi)}{\sqrt{1-\xi^{2}}(x^{\prime}-\xi)}d\xi,

(81)

\displaystyle T_{n}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}P.V.\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\sqrt{1-\xi^{2}}U_{n-1}(\xi)}{(x^{\prime}-\xi)}d\xi,

(82)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx^{\prime}}\left(\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}U_{r-1}(x^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=-r\frac{T_{r}(x^{\prime})}{\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}}.

Since, by (
79
),

(83)

\widetilde{\phi}_{x^{\prime}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}na_{n}U_{n-1}(x^{\prime}),

we have

\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{1-x^{\prime 2}}|\widetilde{\phi}_{x^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})|^{2}dx^{\prime}=\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{2}a_{n}^{2}.

By writing

Since the spaces

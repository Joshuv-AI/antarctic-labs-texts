Approximative Atomic Systems for operators in Banach spaces
Shah Jahan

Abstract
. K K -frames and atomic systems for an operator K K in Hilbert spaces were introduced by Gavruta [ 17 ] and further studied by Xio, Zhu and Gavruta [ 35 ] . In this paper, we have introduced the notion of an approximative atomic system for an operator K K in Banach spaces and obtained interesting results. A complete characterization of family of approximative local atoms of subspace of Banach space has been obtained. Also, a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of an approximative atomic system for an operator K K is given. Finally, explicit methods are given for the construction of an approximative atomic systems for an operator K K from a given Bessel sequence and approximative 𝒳 d \mathcal{X}_{d} -Bessel sequence.

1.
Introduction

A sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is called a frame for the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H},
if there exist positive constants
A,~B>0
such that

\displaystyle A\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\langle f,f_{n}\rangle|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}},~~~\mbox{for all}~~f\in\mathcal{H}

(1.1)

The positive constants
A
and
B
are called the lower and upper frame bounds of the frame, respectively. The inequality in (
1.1
) is called the frame inequality of the frame. The frame is called a tight frame if
A=B
and is called normalized tight frame if
A=B=1.
If
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a frame for
\mathcal{H}
then the following operators are associated with it.

(a)

Pre-frame operator
T:l^{2}(\mathbb{N})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
is defined as
T\{c_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{n}f_{n},~~\{c_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in l^{2}(\mathbb{N}).

(b)

Analysis operator
T^{*}:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow l^{2}(\mathbb{N}),T^{*}f=\{\langle f,f_{k}\rangle\}_{k=1}^{\infty}~~~f\in\mathcal{H}.

(c)

Frame operator
S=TT^{*}=:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H},~~Sf=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\langle f,f_{k}\rangle f_{k},~~f\in\mathcal{H}.
The frame operator
S
is bounded, linear and invertible on
\mathcal{H}
. Thus, a frame for
\mathcal{H}
allows each vector in
\mathcal{H}
to be written as a linear combination of the elements in the frame, but the linear independence between the elements is not required; i.e for each vector
f\in\mathcal{H}
we have,

\displaystyle f=SS^{-1}f=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\langle f,f_{k}\rangle f_{k}.

SEQUENTIAL CONVERGENCE ON THE SPACE OF BOREL MEASURES
LIANGANG MA

Abstract
We study equivalent descriptions of the vague, weak, setwise and total-variation (TV) convergence of sequences of Borel measures on metrizable and non-metrizable topological spaces in this work. On metrizable spaces, we give some equivalent conditions on the vague convergence of sequences of measures following Kallenberg, and some equivalent conditions on the TV convergence of sequences of measures following Feinberg-Kasyanov-Zgurovsky. There is usually some hierarchy structure on the equivalent descriptions of convergence in different modes, but not always. On non-metrizable spaces, we give examples to show that these conditions are seldom enough to guarantee any convergence of sequences of measures. There are some remarks on the attainability of the TV distance and more modes of sequential convergence at the end of the work.

The vague, weak, setwise and TV convergence of sequences of measures in
\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)

In this section we introduce the four kind of sequential convergence on the space of Borel measures
\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)
. These notions differ from one another in strength, such that one can expect better properties on the sequence of measures or their limit measures under stronger convergent mode, at the cost of more difficulty in guaranteeing the stronger convergence. One can subtly choose the appropriate level of convergence mode, this is sometimes crucial in dealing with its problems. There are basically two points of views to describe these notions of convergence of sequences of measures. One is from their behaviours on measurable sets in
X
, another is from integrations of functions with respect to them. There are some equivalent descriptions of these notions from both point of views, however, the difficulty on verifying these descriptions may be of big difference in various applications, at least technically. This is one of the reason for us to provide more (equivalent, necessary or sufficient) conditions on these convergence. We decide to take the second point of view to define these notions of convergence, that is, we are going to define them by the asymptotic behaviours of integrations of functions of certain regularity with respect to the sequences of measures in
\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)
.

For a set
A\subset X
, let
\overline{A},A^{o},A^{c},\partial A
be its closure, interior, complement and boundary respectively. A continuous function
f:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is said to
vanish at infinity
if

f^{-1}\big((-\infty,-a]\cup[a,\infty)\big)

is always compact for any
a>0

[
Fol
, p132]
in
X
. The
support
of a function
f:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined to be

S(f):=\overline{f^{-1}\big((-\infty,0)\cup(0,\infty)\big)}
.

The following families of functions are highlighted in our work.

•

C(X)=\{f:f\mbox{ is a continuous function from }X\mbox{ to }\mathbb{R}\}.

•

C_{0}(X)=\{f:f\mbox{ is a continuous function from }X\mbox{ to }\mathbb{R}\mbox{ vanishing at infinity}\}
.

•

C_{c}(X)=\{f:f\mbox{ is a continuous function from }X\mbox{ to }\mathbb{R}\mbox{ with compact support}\}
.

•

C_{b}(X)=\{f:f\mbox{ is a bounded continuous function from }X\mbox{ to }\mathbb{R}\}
.

•

M_{b}(X)=\{f:f\mbox{ is a bounded measurable function from }X\mbox{ to }\mathbb{R}\}
.

•

M_{\gamma}(X)=\{f:f\mbox{ is a measurable function from }X\mbox{ to }[-\gamma,\gamma]\}
for
\gamma\geq 0
.

The following relationships between these families of functions are obvious.

M_{\gamma}(X)\subset M_{b}(X)
and
C_{c}(X)\subset C_{0}(X)\subset C_{b}(X)\subset M_{b}(X)
.

For integrations on a topological space
X
with a Borel measure, see
[
Li
]
or
[
Tay
, Chapter 3]
.

Definition 2.1
.

For a sequence of measures
\{\nu_{n}\in\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, we say
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges
vaguely
to
\nu\in\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)
, denoted by
\nu_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v}}{{\rightarrow}}\nu
, if

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}f(x)d\nu_{n}=\int_{X}f(x)d\nu

for any
f\in C_{c}(X)
.

Denote by
\nu_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v}}{{\nrightarrow}}\nu
as
n\rightarrow\infty
if
\{\nu_{n}\in\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
does not converge vaguely to
\nu\in\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)
.

\nu\in\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)

A metric space is said to be a
Heine-Borel
space if any closed bounded subset in it is compact
[

]
. There are lots of typical spaces in the Heine-Borel family, for example, the Euclidean spaces, or any
\sigma
-compact and locally compact metric space. For a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)

\mu

\mu(\partial A)=0

\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)

Theorem 2.3
.

For a sequence of measures
\{\nu_{n}\in\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
\nu\in\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)
on a separable and complete Heine-Borel space
X
, the following conditions are equivalent.

1.

\nu_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v}}{{\rightarrow}}\nu
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

2.

For any compact set
A\subset X
,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(A)\leq\nu(A)
,

while for any bounded open set
B\subset X
,

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(B)\geq\nu(B)
.

3.

For any bounded closed set
A\subset X
,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(A)\leq\nu(A)
,

while for any bounded open set
B\subset X
,

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(B)\geq\nu(B)
.

4.

For any bounded set
A\in\mathscr{B}
,

\nu_{n}(A^{o})\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(A)\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(A)\leq\nu(\overline{A})
.

5.

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(A)=\nu(A)
for any bounded
\nu
-continuity set
A\in\mathscr{B}
.

6.

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}fd\nu_{n}=\int_{X}fd\nu
for any continuous function
f
with bounded support.

7.

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}fd\nu_{n}=\int_{X}fd\nu
for any Hölder continuous function
f\in C_{c}(X)
.

8.

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}fd\nu_{n}=\int_{X}fd\nu
for any uniformly continuous function
f\in C_{c}(X)
.

9.

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}fd\nu_{n}=\int_{X}fd\nu
for any function
f\in M_{b}(X)
with bounded support and

\nu(\{x\in X:f\mbox{ is discontinuous at }x\})=0
.

10.

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}fd\nu_{n}=\int_{X}fd\nu
for any non-negative (or non-positive) valued function
f\in C_{c}(X)
.

A stronger notion of sequential convergence of measures than vague convergence is the
weak convergence
as following.

Definition 2.6
.

For a sequence of measures
\{\nu_{n}\in\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, we say
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges
weakly
to
\nu\in\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)
, denoted by
\nu_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\rightarrow}}\nu
, if

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}f(x)d\nu_{n}=\int_{X}f(x)d\nu

for any
f\in C_{b}(X)
.

There are lots of equivalent descriptions on the weak convergence of sequences of measures whose collection is called the
Portemanteau Theorem
, see for example
[
Bil1
, Theorem 2.1]
,
[

[

Although the weak convergence of sequences of measures is a powerful tool, there are some situations under which some important properties are not guaranteed under this mode of convergence, for example, the Vitali-Hahn-Saks Theorem (refer to
[

of sequences of measures, which appears as a stronger mode of convergence than the weak convergence as following.

Definition 2.7
.

For a sequence of measures
\{\nu_{n}\in\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, we say
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges
setwisely
to
\nu\in\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)
, denoted by
\nu_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\rightarrow}}\nu
, if

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}f(x)d\nu_{n}=\int_{X}f(x)d\nu

for any
f\in M_{b}(X)
.

Since simple functions are dense in the space of bounded measurable functions,
\nu_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\rightarrow}}\nu
is equivalent to say that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(A)=\nu(A)

for any
A\in\mathscr{B}
. Feinberg, Kasyanov and Zgurovsky gave some equivalent conditions on the setwise convergence of sequences of measures in
\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)
with a metric ambient space

as following
[

, Theorem 2.3]
.

Theorem 2.8

(Feinberg-Kasyanov-Zgurovsky)
.

For a sequence of measures
\{\nu_{n}\in\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
\nu\in\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)
on a metric space
X
, the following conditions are equivalent to each other:

1.

\nu_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\rightarrow}}\nu
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

2.

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(B)=\nu(B)
for any open set
B\subset X
.

3.

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(A)=\nu(A)
for any closed set
A\subset X
.

The strongest notion of convergence of sequences of measures considered in our work is the

Definition 2.11
.

For a sequence of measures
\{\nu_{n}\in\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, we say
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges
in total variation

\nu\in\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)

\nu_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle TV}}{{\rightarrow}}\nu

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{f\in M_{1}(X)}|\int_{X}f(x)d\nu_{n}-\int_{X}f(x)d\nu|=0

\|\mu-\nu\|_{TV}:=2\sup_{A\in\mathscr{B}}|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|=\sup_{f\in M_{1}(X)}|\int_{X}f(x)d\mu-\int_{X}f(x)d\nu|

(2.1)

for any two probability measures
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)
. We also give some equivalent conditions on the TV convergence of sequences of measures in Theorem

Description of the vague convergence of sequences of measures in
\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
2.5

2.1
and Remark
2.2
. Example
3.1
shows that the one in Remark
2.2
may be strictly stronger than the one in Definition
2.1
for vaguely sequential convergence in
\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)
. For a measure
\nu\in\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)
and
A\in\mathscr{B}
, let
\nu|_{A}
be the restriction of
\nu
on

.

Example 3.1
.

Let
X=\{1,2,\dots\}=\mathbb{N}
be endowed with the discrete topology and the corresponding Borel
\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{B}
. Let
\nu_{c}
be the counting measure on
(X,\mathscr{B})
. Let
\nu_{n}:=\nu_{c}|_{\{n,n+1,\dots\}}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
on
(X,\mathscr{B})
. Let
\nu
be the null measure on
(X,\mathscr{B})
.

Since a subset of
X
is compact if and only if it is finite, for any function
f\in C_{c}(X)
, it is identically zero outside a finite subset. Therefore,

\int_{X}fd\nu_{n}=\sum_{m\geq n}f(m)\rightarrow 0=\int_{X}fd\nu

as
n\rightarrow\infty.

Now consider the continuous function
g(n)=\frac{1}{n}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it is in
C_{0}(X)
, while

\int_{X}gd\nu_{n}=\sum_{m\geq n}\frac{1}{m}

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, which does not converge to
0=\int_{X}gd\nu.

Even if one restricts the consideration on the probability space
\mathcal{M}(X)
, the two versions of vague convergence may differ from each other if the ambient space
X
is pathological, see Theorem
2.10
. However, the two notions are equivalent to each other on
\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)
with a
\sigma
-compact and locally compact Hausdorff (LCH) space
X
.

Proposition 3.2
.

In case
X
is a
\sigma
-compact LCH space, let
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\nu\}\subset\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)
. If

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}f(x)d\nu_{n}=\int_{X}f(x)d\nu

for any
f\in C_{c}(X)
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}f(x)d\nu_{n}=\int_{X}f(x)d\nu

(3.1)

for any
f\in C_{0}(X)
.

Proof.

If the ambient space
X
is
\sigma
-compact and LCH, according to
[
Tao
, Section 1.10]
, for any
f\in C_{0}(X)
, we can find
f_{c}\in C_{c}(X)
, such that

\|f-f_{c}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon

for any
\epsilon>0
. Since
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}f_{c}d\nu_{n}=\int_{X}f_{c}d\nu
, there exists
N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
large enough, such that

|\int_{X}f_{c}d\nu_{n}-\int_{X}f_{c}d\nu|<\epsilon

for any
\epsilon>0
. So

\begin{array}[]{ll}&|\int_{X}fd\nu_{n}-\int_{X}fd\nu|\\
\leq&|\int_{X}f_{c}d\nu_{n}-\int_{X}f_{c}d\nu|+\int_{X}|f-f_{c}|(d\nu_{n}+d\nu)\\
\leq&(2+3\nu(X))\epsilon\end{array}

for
n
large enough. This implies (
3.1
) since
\nu(X)<\infty
.

∎

From now on we go towards the proof of Theorem
2.5
. To do this we need the following preceding results. For two subsets
A,B
in a metric space
X
with metric
\rho
, let

\rho(A,B)=\inf_{x\in A,y\in B}\rho(x,y)

be their distance in
X
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\nu\}\subset\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)
with
X
being Heine-Borel with metric
\rho
. If
\nu_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v}}{{\rightarrow}}\nu
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, then

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(A)\leq\nu(A)

(3.2)

for any bounded closed set
A\subset X
, while

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(B)\geq\nu(B)

(3.3)

for any bounded open set
B\subset X
.

Proof.

Let
A\subset X
be an arbitrarily bounded closed set. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the following function,

f_{n,A}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&x\in A,\\
1-n\rho(x,A)&0<\rho(x,A)<\frac{1}{n},\\
0&\rho(x,A)\geq\frac{1}{n}.\end{array}\right.

(3.4)

Note that
f_{n,A}
has bounded support, and so compact support since
X
is Heine-Borel for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(A)\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}f_{n,A}d\nu_{n}=\int_{X}f_{n,A}d\nu

as
\nu_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v}}{{\rightarrow}}\nu
. Since the sequence
\{\int_{X}f_{n,A}d\nu\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
decreases to
\nu(A)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, we get (
3.2
).

Now let
B\subset X
be an arbitrary bounded open set. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the following function,

g_{n,B}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&x\in B\mbox{ and }\rho(x,\partial B)\geq\frac{1}{n},\\
n\rho(x,\partial B)&x\in B\mbox{ and }\rho(x,\partial B)<\frac{1}{n},\\
0&x\notin B\end{array}\right.

(3.5)

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough.
g_{n,B}
has bounded support and so compact support since
X
is Heine-Borel for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(B)\geq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}g_{n,B}d\nu_{n}=\int_{X}g_{n,B}d\nu

as
\nu_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v}}{{\rightarrow}}\nu
. Since the sequence
\{\int_{X}g_{n,B}d\nu\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
increases to
\nu(B)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, we get (
3.3
).

∎

Remark 3.4
.

Note that if we assume

The following result interprets some limit behaviours of sequences of measures on bounded continuity sets in words of limit behaviours of sequences of those measures on bounded measurable sets in a metric space.

Lemma 3.5

(Kallenberg)
.

Let
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\nu\}\subset\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)
for a metric space
(X,\rho)
. Then

\nu_{n}(A^{o})\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(A)\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(A)\leq\nu(\overline{A})

(3.6)

for any bounded set
A\in\mathscr{B}
if and only if

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(A)=\nu(A)

(3.7)

for any bounded
\nu
-continuity set
A\in\mathscr{B}
.

Proof.

If (
3.6
) holds on any bounded set
A\in\mathscr{B}
, then (
3.7
) holds on any bounded
\nu
-continuity sets obviously. In the following we show the converse. Suppose (
3.7
) holds for any bounded
\nu
-continuity set

is a
\nu
-continuity set, this induces

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(A)\geq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(A^{o})=\nu(A^{o}).

(3.8)

Now we justify the third inequality in (
3.6
), which is enough to finish the proof. We do this by reduction to absurdity. Suppose
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(A)>\nu(\overline{A})
for some bounded set

. Consider the following collection of bounded open sets in

,

\big\{A_{\epsilon}:=\{x\in X:\rho(x,A)<\epsilon\}\big\}_{0<\epsilon<\infty}
.

It is obvious that

\limsup_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}A_{\epsilon}=\overline{A}
,

which forces

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\nu(A_{\epsilon})=\nu(\overline{A})

(3.9)

since
\nu
is finite. Now choose a decreasing sequence of positive real numbers
\{\epsilon_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
with
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\epsilon_{k}=0
, such that

are all

-continuity set for any

(in fact there can be at most countably many

such that

is not an

\nu(A_{\epsilon_{k}})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(A_{\epsilon_{k}})\geq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(A)>\nu(\overline{A})

for any

. This gives

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\nu(A_{\epsilon_{k}})=\lim_{\epsilon_{k}\rightarrow 0}\nu(A_{\epsilon_{k}})>\nu(\overline{A}),

(3.10)

which contradicts (

).
∎

Description of the setwise convergence of sequences of measures in
\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)

In this section we focus on the equivalent descriptions of setwise convergence of sequences of bounded Borel measures with the ambient space
X
being a general topological space. We first give an example to show that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(A)=\nu(A)

for any closed (or open) bounded set
A\subset X
is not enough to guarantee its setwise convergence in
\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)
. Then we give an example that the sequence of integrations of an unbounded measurable function with respect to a setwisely convergent sequence of measures in
\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)
diverge, followed by a partially convergent result. At last we prove Theorem
2.10
and indicate some of its applications. Let
\mathfrak{L}_{d}
be the
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Example 4.1
.

Let
X=[1,\infty)
endowed with the Euclidean metric. Consider the following sequence of Borel measures
on
(X,\mathscr{B})
,

\nu_{n}(A)=\int_{[1,n]\cap A}\frac{1}{x^{4}}dx+\mathfrak{L}_{1}|_{[n,n+1]}(A)

for any
A\in\mathscr{B}
, in which
\mathfrak{L}_{1}|_{[n,n+1]}
is the restriction of
\mathfrak{L}_{1}
on
[n,n+1]
. Let

\nu(A)=\int_{X\cap A}\frac{1}{x^{4}}dx

for any
A\in\mathscr{B}
.

In Example
4.1

X
is a separable and complete Heine-Borel space. One can check easily that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(A)=\nu(A)

for any closed (or open) bounded set
A\in\mathscr{B}
(in fact
\nu_{n}(A)=\nu(A)
for any
n
large enough). However,
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
does not converge setwisely to
\nu
since

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(X)=\nu(X)+1
.

Example 4.2
.

Let
X=[1,\infty)
endowed with the Euclidean metric. Consider the following sequence of Borel measures
on
(X,\mathscr{B})
,

\nu_{n}(A)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\int_{[1,n]\cap A}\frac{1}{x^{4}}dx+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\mathfrak{L}_{1}|_{[n,n+1]}(A)&n\mbox{ is odd},\\
\int_{[1,n]\cap A}\frac{1}{x^{4}}dx+\frac{2}{n^{2}}\mathfrak{L}_{1}|_{[n,n+1]}(A)&n\mbox{ is even }\end{array}\right.

for any
A\in\mathscr{B}
. Let

\nu(A)=\int_{X\cap A}\frac{1}{x^{4}}dx

for any
A\in\mathscr{B}
.

One can check easily that in Example
4.2
that
\nu_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\rightarrow}}\nu
as
n\rightarrow\infty
(in fact the convergence is even TV). Let
f(x)=x^{2}
on
X
. Now consider the integrations of the function
f
with respect to
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\nu
. One can check that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}fd\nu_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{4}{3}&n\mbox{ is odd},\\
\frac{7}{3}&n\mbox{ is even},\end{array}\right.

while
\int_{X}fd\nu=\frac{1}{3}
.

Remark 4.3
.

The measures in Example
4.1
and
4.2
can be made into probability ones with continuous or even smooth density with respect to
\mathfrak{L}_{1}
on
[1,\infty)
.

This example indicates the complication of integral behaviours on families of unbounded measurable test functions with respect to setwisely convergent sequences of finite measures. However, in some special cases one can expect the convergence of integrals of test functions with respect to setwisely convergent sequences of measures. For a function
f:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
on a topological space
X
, let

f^{+}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f(x)&\mbox{ for }f(x)\geq 0,\\
0&\mbox{ for }f(x)<0\end{array}\right.

and

f^{-}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\mbox{ for }f(x)>0,\\
-f(x)&\mbox{ for }f(x)\leq 0\end{array}\right.

be its
positive part
and
negative part
respectively. So
f(x)=f^{+}(x)-f^{-}(x)
on
X
. We say
f
is
integrable
with respect to a Borel measure
\nu
on
X
if at least one of the integrals
\int_{X}f^{+}d\nu
and
\int_{X}f^{-}d\nu
is finite.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
X
be a topological space and
\nu_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\rightarrow}}\nu
as
n\rightarrow\infty
in
\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)
. For an unbounded function
f:X\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, if
\int_{X}fd\nu=\infty
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}fd\nu_{n}=\infty.

(4.1)

Proof.

Let
A_{k}=\{x\in X:0\leq f(x)<k\}
. Since
\int_{X}fd\nu=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{A_{k}}fd\nu=\infty
, for any
M>0
, there exists some
k_{M}\in\mathbb{N}
large enough, such that

\int_{A_{k_{M}}}fd\nu>M
.

Now consider the truncated bounded measurable function

1_{A_{k_{M}}}f(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f(x)&\mbox{ for }f(x)<k_{M},\\
0&\mbox{ for }f(x)\geq k_{M}\end{array}\right.

on
X
, in which
1_{A_{k_{M}}}
is the characteristic function of
A_{k_{M}}
. Since
\nu_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\rightarrow}}\nu
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}fd\nu_{n}\geq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}1_{A_{k_{M}}}fd\nu_{n}=\int_{X}1_{A_{k_{M}}}fd\nu\geq\int_{A_{k_{M}}}fd\nu>M
.

This justifies (
4.1
).
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Proposition
4.4
does not apply to unbounded integrable functions
f:X\rightarrow[0,\infty)
whose integration is finite with respect to
\nu
, as one can construct a counter example based on Example
4.2
. This again indicates the complication of integral behaviours on families of unbounded measurable test functions with respect to setwisely convergent sequences of measures.

The rest of the section is devoted to descriptions of setwise convergence of sequences of measures in
\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)
with non-metrizable ambient space
X
. Let
\delta_{x}
be the Dirac measure at the point

. We first give a proof of Theorem
2.10
.

Proof of Theorem
2.10
:

Proof.

We only prove the case on existence of closed sets with the desiring properties, the open case is similar to the closed case.

According to the assumption on the topology of
X
, let
\{A_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be an infinite sequence of non-empty and pairwise disjoint closed sets in
X
such that

X\setminus A\neq\emptyset
,

in which
A=\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}\{A_{i}\}
.
Now choose a sequence of points
\{a_{i}\in A_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
a_{*}\in X\setminus A
. Define a sequence of probability measures
\{\nu_{n}\in\mathcal{M}(X)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
as following on

\nu_{n}=\cfrac{n-1}{n}\delta_{a_{n}}+\cfrac{1}{n}\delta_{a_{*}}
.

Let
\nu=\delta_{a_{*}}
. We claim that the sequence of measures
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and

satisfy both the conditions
(II),(III)
in Theorem
2.8
. To see this, for any proper closed subset

\{n_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, such that

are all closed subset of

, which contradicts the assumption that every proper closed set contains at most finitely many disjoint non-empty closed subsets. Now we can see that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(F)=0
.

This forces

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(G)=1
.

for any non-empty open set
G\subset X
, which justifies the claim. However, it is easy to see that

\nu_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\nrightarrow}}\nu

as
n\rightarrow\infty
since

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(A)=1>\nu(A)=0
.

∎

Considering Theorem
2.8
, we can deduce the following result in virtue of Theorem

. One can clearly see the impact of the topology of the ambient space

Corollary 4.6
.

If a topological space

Description of the TV convergence of sequences of measures in
\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)

In this section we prove Theorem
2.12
, followed by a similar discussion on the descriptions of TV sequential convergence of measures in
\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)
with the ambient space being non-metrizable. To prove Theorem
2.12
, we need several preliminary results on the equivalent description of TV distance between two measures in
\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For two finite measures
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)
with the ambient space
X
being metrizable, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\sup_{A\in\mathscr{B}}|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|\\
=&\sup_{A\mbox{ is closed and bounded}}|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|\\
=&\sup_{B\mbox{ is open and bounded}}|\mu(B)-\nu(B)|.\end{array}

Proof.

According to
[
FKZ1
, Theorem 2.5 (i)(ii)]
, to justify these equalities, we only need to show

\sup_{A\mbox{ is closed}}|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|=\sup_{A\mbox{ is closed and bounded}}|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|

(5.1)

and

\sup_{B\mbox{ is open}}|\mu(B)-\nu(B)|=\sup_{B\mbox{ is open and bounded}}|\mu(B)-\nu(B)|.

(5.2)

In the following we only show (
5.1
), as the proof of (
5.2
) follows a similar way. Choose an arbitrary point
x_{0}\in X
, consider the sequence of closed balls
\{\overline{B(x_{0},n)}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
centred at
x_{0}
of radius
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{B(x_{0},n)}=X
, for any small
\epsilon>0
, there exists
n_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
large enough, such that

\mu(\overline{B(x_{0},n)})>\mu(X)-\epsilon\mbox{ and }\nu(\overline{B(x_{0},n)})>\nu(X)-\epsilon.

(5.3)

Now let

\sup_{A\mbox{ is closed}}|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|:=s
.

Then for any small
\epsilon>0
, there exists a closed set
A\subset X
such that

0\leq s-|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|<\epsilon
.

Let
A_{\epsilon}=A\cap\overline{B(x_{0},n)}
. It is a bounded and closed set. Since

\mu(A)-\nu(A)=\mu(A_{\epsilon})-\nu(A_{\epsilon})+\mu(A\setminus A_{\epsilon})-\nu(A\setminus A_{\epsilon})
,

then

\begin{array}[]{ll}&|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|-|\mu(A_{\epsilon})-\nu(A_{\epsilon})|\\
\leq&|\mu(A\setminus A_{\epsilon})-\nu(A\setminus A_{\epsilon})|\\
\leq&\max\{\mu(A\setminus A_{\epsilon}),\nu(A\setminus A_{\epsilon})\}\\
\leq&\max\{\mu(X\setminus\overline{B(x_{0},n)}),\nu(X\setminus\overline{B(x_{0},n)})\}\\
\leq&\epsilon.\end{array}

The last inequality is due to (
5.3
). Then we have

0\leq s-|\mu(A_{\epsilon})-\nu(A_{\epsilon})|=s-|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|+|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|-|\mu(A_{\epsilon})-\nu(A_{\epsilon})|<2\epsilon,

which is enough to imply (
5.1
).
∎

Remark 5.2
.

In case
X
is a
\sigma
-compact metric space, there is an increasing sequence of compact subsets
\{K_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
whose union is
X
. Then

\sup_{A\mbox{ is compact}}|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|=\sup_{A\mbox{ is closed}}|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|.

(5.4)

This can be seen by repeating the proof of Lemma
5.1
with
\overline{B(x_{0},n)}
replaced by
K_{n}
therein.
A_{\epsilon}
is now a compact set since it is a closed subset of a compact set.

Lemma 5.3
.

For any non-negative
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\sup_{f\in M_{\gamma}(X)}|\int_{X}f(x)d\mu-\int_{X}f(x)d\nu|=\gamma\sup_{f\in M_{1}(X)}|\int_{X}f(x)d\mu-\int_{X}f(x)d\nu|

for any
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)
with
X
being a topological space.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we assume
\gamma>0
. We only show

\sup_{f\in M_{\gamma}(X)}\Big|\int_{X}f(x)d\mu-\int_{X}f(x)d\nu\Big|\leq\gamma\sup_{f\in M_{1}(X)}\Big|\int_{X}f(x)d\mu-\int_{X}f(x)d\nu\Big|

(5.5)

in the following, the inverse inequality follows a similar way. For any small
\epsilon>0
, there exists
f_{\epsilon}\in M_{\gamma}(X)
such that

0\leq\sup_{f\in M_{\gamma}(X)}\Big|\int_{X}f(x)d\mu-\int_{X}f(x)d\nu\Big|-\Big|\int_{X}f_{\epsilon}(x)d\mu-\int_{X}f_{\epsilon}(x)d\nu\Big|<\epsilon.

(5.6)

Since
\cfrac{1}{\gamma}f_{\epsilon}\in M_{1}(X)
, we have

\gamma\sup_{f\in M_{1}(X)}\Big|\int_{X}f(x)d\mu-\int_{X}f(x)d\nu\Big|\geq\Big|\int_{X}f_{\epsilon}(x)d\mu-\int_{X}f_{\epsilon}(x)d\nu\Big|.

(5.7)

Now combing (
5.6
) and (
5.7
) together, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\sup_{f\in M_{\gamma}(X)}|\int_{X}f(x)d\mu-\int_{X}f(x)d\nu|-\gamma\sup_{f\in M_{1}(X)}|\int_{X}f(x)d\mu-\int_{X}f(x)d\nu|\\
\leq&\sup_{f\in M_{\gamma}(X)}|\int_{X}f(x)d\mu-\int_{X}f(x)d\nu|-|\int_{X}f_{\epsilon}(x)d\mu-\int_{X}f_{\epsilon}(x)d\nu|\\
<&\epsilon.\end{array}

(5.8)

Then we get (
5.5
) by letting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
in (
5.8
).
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
X
be a metric space endowed with the metric
\rho
. For any non-negative
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\sup_{f\in M_{\gamma}(X)}|\int_{X}f(x)d\mu-\int_{X}f(x)d\nu|\\
=&\sup_{f\mbox{ has bounded support in }C(X)\cap M_{\gamma}(X)}|\int_{X}f(x)d\mu-\int_{X}f(x)d\nu|\end{array}

for any
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)
.

Proof.

Considering
[
FKZ1
, Theorem 2.5 (iv)]
and Lemma
5.3
, we only need to justify

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\sup_{f\in C(X)\cap M_{\gamma}(X)}|\int_{X}f(x)d\mu-\int_{X}f(x)d\nu|\\
\leq&\sup_{f\mbox{ has bounded support in }C(X)\cap M_{\gamma}(X)}|\int_{X}f(x)d\mu-\int_{X}f(x)d\nu|\end{array}

(5.9)

for any non-negative
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)
. To see this, for any small
\epsilon>0
, there exists some
f_{\epsilon}\in C(X)\cap M_{\gamma}(X)
, such that

0\leq\sup_{f\in C(X)\cap M_{\gamma}(X)}\Big|\int_{X}f(x)d\mu-\int_{X}f(x)d\nu\Big|-\Big|\int_{X}f_{\epsilon}(x)d\mu-\int_{X}f_{\epsilon}(x)d\nu\Big|<\epsilon.

(5.10)

Choose an arbitrary
x_{0}\in X
, consider the sequence of open balls
\{B(x_{0},n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
centred at
x_{0}
of radius
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
n_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
be large enough such that

\mu(B(x_{0},n_{\epsilon}))>\mu(X)-\epsilon\mbox{ and }\nu(B(x_{0},n_{\epsilon}))>\nu(X)-\epsilon.

(5.11)

The function

f_{1,B(x_{0},n_{\epsilon})}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&x\in B(x_{0},n_{\epsilon}),\\
1-\rho(x,B(x_{0},n_{\epsilon}))&0<\rho(x,B(x_{0},n_{\epsilon}))<1,\\
0&\rho(x,B(x_{0},n_{\epsilon}))\geq 1.\end{array}\right.

is continuous with bounded support. So
f_{1,B(x_{0},n_{\epsilon})}f_{\epsilon}\in C(X)\cap M_{\gamma}(X)
also has bounded support. Moreover, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}&|\int_{X}f_{1,B(x_{0},n_{\epsilon})}f_{\epsilon}d\mu-\int_{X}f_{1,B(x_{0},n_{\epsilon})}f_{\epsilon}d\nu|\\
\geq&|\int_{X}f_{\epsilon}(x)d\mu-\int_{X}f_{\epsilon}(x)d\nu|-|\int_{X}(f_{1,B(x_{0},n_{\epsilon})}-1)f_{\epsilon}d\mu-\int_{X}(f_{1,B(x_{0},n_{\epsilon})}-1)f_{\epsilon}d\nu|\\
\geq&|\int_{X}f_{\epsilon}(x)d\mu-\int_{X}f_{\epsilon}(x)d\nu|-\gamma\mu\big(X\setminus B(x_{0},n_{\epsilon})\big)-a\nu\big(X\setminus B(x_{0},n_{\epsilon})\big)\\
\geq&|\int_{X}f_{\epsilon}(x)d\mu-\int_{X}f_{\epsilon}(x)d\nu|-2\gamma\epsilon\\
\geq&\sup_{f\in C(X)\cap M_{\gamma}(X)}|\int_{X}f(x)d\mu-\int_{X}f(x)d\nu|-(1+2\gamma)\epsilon.\end{array}

(5.12)

The third inequality is due to (
5.11
), while the last one is due to (
5.10
). Now let
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
in (
5.12
), we get (
5.9
).

∎

Attainability of the TV metric between two measures and further sequential convergence in
\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)

Comparing Theorem
2.5
, Example
4.1
and Theorem
2.12
, we can see that there is some fracture between descriptions of these successive modes of convergence. Due to the Hahn decomposition, the total-variation distance of two finite measures can be attained on some bounded measurable function or on some Borel measurable set in (
2.1
). Although Theorem
2.12
reduces the difficulty on checking TV convergence of sequences of measures in
\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)
in some cases, the total-variation metric between two measures may never be attained.

Example 6.1
.

Let
X=(0,1)
. Let

\mu=\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{L}_{1}|_{(0,1)}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\frac{2}{3}}

and

\nu=\mathfrak{L}_{1}|_{(0,\frac{1}{3})}+\mathfrak{L}_{1}|_{(\frac{2}{3},1)}+\frac{1}{3}\delta_{\frac{1}{3}}

be two Borel probabilities on
X
.

One can check that

\|\mu-\nu\|_{TV}=\frac{2}{3}

in Example
6.1
. However, there does not exist any open (or closed) set
B
such that

2|\mu(B)-\nu(B)|=\frac{2}{3}
,

or any continuous function
f
such that

|\int_{X}fd\mu-\int_{X}fd\nu|=\frac{2}{3}
.

One should also be careful that although there are many equivalent ways to formulate the total-variation metric between two finite measures on metric spaces (
[
FKZ1
, Theorem 2.5]
, Lemma
5.1
, Lemma
5.4
), the attainability of the total-variation metric between two measures may vary depending on situations. For example, there are examples of unbounded metric spaces
X
with finite Borel measures
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{\hat{M}}(X)
such that

\|\mu-\nu\|_{TV}=2|\mu(B)-\nu(B)|

(6.1)

for some open (or closed)
B\subset X
, while there does not exist any bounded open (or closed)
B\subset X
satisfying (
6.1
), or any continuous function
f
such that

|\int_{X}fd\mu-\int_{X}fd\nu|=\|\mu-\nu\|_{TV}
.

It is possible that any of the four kinds of sequential convergence is still not subtle enough in its applications to real problems. In this case one can define the
F
-convergence and
S
-convergence for
sequences of measures in
\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)
.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
F
be a family of functions from
X
to
\mathbb{R}
. For a sequence of measures
\{\nu_{n}\in\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, we say
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

F
-converges to
\nu\in\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)
, denoted by
\nu_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle F}}{{\rightarrow}}\nu
, if

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}f(x)d\nu_{n}=\int_{X}f(x)d\nu

for any
f\in F
.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
S\subset\mathscr{B}
be a family of sets. For a sequence of measures
\{\nu_{n}\in\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, we say
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

S
-converges to
\nu\in\mathcal{\tilde{M}}(X)
, denoted by
\nu_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle S}}{{\rightarrow}}\nu
, if

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{n}(A)=\nu(A)

for any
A\in S
.

Topologies induced from delicate choices of
F
or
S
may result in solutions of ones’ problems instantly.

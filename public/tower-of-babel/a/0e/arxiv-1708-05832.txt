A posteriori error bounds for fully-discrete h ​ p hp -discontinuous Galerkin timestepping methods for parabolic problems
Emmanuil H. Georgoulis, Omar Lakkis and Thomas P. Wihler

Abstract
. We consider fully discrete time-space approximations of abstract linear parabolic partial differential equations (PDEs) consisting of an h ​ p hp -version discontinuous Galerkin (DG) time stepping scheme in conjunction with standard (conforming) Galerkin discretizations in space. We derive abstract computable a posteriori error bounds resulting, for instance, in concrete bounds in L ∞ ⁡ ( I ; L 2 ⁡ ( Ω ) ) \operatorname{L}_{\infty}(I;\operatorname{L}_{2}(\varOmega)) - and L 2 ⁡ ( I ; H 1 ⁡ ( Ω ) ) \operatorname{L}_{2}(I;\operatorname{H}^{1}(\varOmega)) -type norms when I I is the temporal and Ω \varOmega the spatial domain for the PDE. We base our methodology for the analysis on a novel space-time reconstruction approach. Our approach is flexible as it works for any type of elliptic error estimator and leaves their choice to the user. It also exhibits mesh-change estimators in a clear and concise way. We also show how our approach allows the derivation of such bounds in the H 1 ⁡ ( I ; H − 1 ⁡ ( Ω ) ) \operatorname{H}^{1}(I;\operatorname{H}^{-1}(\varOmega)) norm.

1.
Introduction

Adaptive numerical methods have been shown to
provide accurate and efficient
numerical treatment of evolution PDEs
thanks to their properties for localized mesh resolution especially in the
context of moving fronts, interfaces, singularities, or layers (both
boundary and interior). Such numerical methods predominantly admit
spatial discretizations of variational type, e.g., finite element
methods (FEMs), which allow for general,
possibly unstructured, dynamic mesh modification. FEMs are also
ideally suited for deriving mathematically rigorous a posteriori bounds, owing to their variational nature.

Some of the classical works on adaptive finite
element methods for parabolic problems

[
EJ91
,
EJ95a
,
EJ95b
,
EJ95c
,
EJL98
]
are based on discontinuous Galerkin
(DG) time stepping combined with FEM in space, and proving
a posteriori bounds in various norms using duality techniques. The key
motivation in using DG in time, which is also of variational type, is
that it naturally allows for spatially-local-time stepping, i.e.
different time step sizes in different parts of the spatial domain

[
Jam78
,
EJT85
,
EJ91
,
MB97
]
. This classical, but as of yet
undeveloped in full, concept of local adaptivity in both space and
time has the potential of delivering substantial computational savings
and even complexity reduction.

In addition to the ability of Galerkin time marching schemes to
employ locally different time step sizes, their variational
character also allows for arbitrary variations in the local
approximation orders. They can therefore be cast naturally into the
framework of
hp
-approximation schemes. In the context of
parabolic PDEs,
hp
-version time marching methods can be
used, for instance, to resolve an initial layer in the (otherwise
smooth) solution at high algebraic or even exponential rates of
convergence, see, e.g., the
works
[
SS00
,
SS01
,
WGSS01
]
on linear
parabolic PDEs, and also
[
MSW05
,
MSW06
]
which employ a
combination of
hp
-version time stepping with suitable wavelet
spatial discretizations to yield a log-linear complexity algorithm
for nonlocal evolution processes involving pseudo-differential
operators. Additionally, we note the numerical analysis of
high-dimensional parabolic problems using sparse grids in space;
see
[
vPS04
]
.

More recent results on rigorous a posteriori bounds for parabolic
problems have focused on extending the paradigm of the reliable and
efficient a posteriori error analysis of elliptic problems to the
parabolic case
[
Pic98
,
Ver98
,
Ver03
]
. Such works typically involve basic
low-order time stepping schemes combined with various types of FEM in
space. A posteriori error bounds for DG time-stepping methods have
also appeared in the last few years; we point
to
[
MN06a
,
SW10
,
KMW18
]
which are based on the

reconstruction
technique, to
[
ESV17
,
ESV19
]
which employ
an equilibrated flux approach, or to
[
GSKZ19
]
which presents a
provably convergent adaptive algorithm for a residual-type a
posteriori estimator.

In this paper, we present a posteriori error bounds for an
hp
-version DG-in-time and conforming Galerkin
discretization in space method for
both

\operatorname{L}_{\infty}(I;\operatorname{L}_{2})
- and
\operatorname{L}_{2}(I;\operatorname{H}^{1})
-norm errors
separately, allowing for what appears to be optimal order in each
case. The key idea is the use of suitable reconstruction frameworks
to derive a perturbed PDE for the reconstructed error of the numerical method;
a posteriori error bounds are then deduced using PDE stability properties,
cf.
[
MN03
,
MN06a
,
LM06a
,
AMN06
]
. Our approach is based
on new space and space-time reconstructions which are built
on the combination of respective ideas for DG-time stepping methods

[
MN06a
,
SW10
]
and elliptic reconstruction
[
MN03
,
LM06a
]
to the fully-discrete setting. To that end, the key
challenge of constructing a globally time-continuous reconstruction in
the presence of mesh modification between time-steps is addressed by
first reconstructing onto the solution space with respect to the
spatial variables via a novel elliptic reconstruction
definition, given in
(
3.14
), which is a
modification of the one in
[
LM06a
]
. In particular, the new proposed
elliptic reconstruction takes into account the effect of
mesh-change
.

Our results are closely related, however, with important departures,
to those of
[
ESV17
,
ESV19
,
GSKZ19
]
. In particular, the new reconstructions defined below allow for the derivation
of a posteriori upper error bounds for each of the following norms

\operatorname{L}_{2}(I;\mathscr{X})
(Theorem
4.4
) and

\operatorname{H}^{1}(I;{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})
(§
6
)
separately; the Hilbert space
\mathscr{X}
is the domain of a
self-adjoint uniformly elliptic operator
\mathscr{A}
(see §
2

for details). A key attribute
of our approach is the flexibility in incorporating any a posteriori elliptic error estimators available, as the reconstruction-type approach, allows to separate the challenges in the a posteriori error estimation of elliptic and time-evolution errors. To facilitate a wide range of applications, we will present the theory within a Gelfand-triple-type abstract setting allowing, for instance, both second- and fourth-order spatial operators. This generality comes at the possible expense of different, yet quantitatively analogous, computable constants in the resulting a posteriori error estimators compared to the bounds in
[
ESV17
,
ESV19
,
GSKZ19
]
. For instance, when the
equilibrated flux elliptic error estimators from
[
BPS09
]

are used (with
\mathscr{X}=\operatorname{H}^{1}_{0}(\varOmega)
and
\mathscr{H}=\operatorname{L}_{2}(\varOmega)
), we
recover similar estimators to the upper bounds derived in

[
ESV17
,
ESV19
]
. Importantly, however, the work
[
ESV17
]

shows that these are also lower bounds for the “joint-norm” of

\operatorname{H}^{1}(I;{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})\cap\operatorname{L}_{2}(I;\mathscr{X})
, and the article

[
ESV19
]
does the same for the
\operatorname{L}_{2}(I;\mathscr{X})
under the
condition
h^{2}<c\tau
, relating the mesh-size
h
with the time-step

\tau
for some constant
c>0
. Also, in the present work, we are
not concerned with the interesting question of convergence of
adaptive algorithms as in
[
GSKZ19
]
. Crucially, however, the novel space-time reconstruction, allows for the proof of an a posteriori error bound for the

\operatorname{L}_{\infty}(I;\mathscr{H})
-norm, which appears to be of optimal order; this result, to the best of our reading, is not captured in
[
ESV17
,
ESV19
,
GSKZ19
]
.

2.1.
Abstract setting

Throughout this work, Bochner spaces will be used. To that end, given
an interval
J\subset\mathbb{R}
, and a real Hilbert
space
\mathscr{Z}
with inner product
\left({\cdot,\cdot}\right)_{\mathscr{Z}}
and induced
norm
\|{\cdot}\|_{\mathscr{Z}}
, we define

(2.1)

\|{u}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{p}(J;\mathscr{Z})}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\left(\int_{J}\|{u(t)}\|^{p}_{\mathscr{Z}}\operatorname{d}\!t\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}},&1\leq p<\infty,\\
\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{t\in J}\|{u(t)}\|_{\mathscr{Z}},&p=\infty.\end{cases}

We write
\operatorname{L}_{p}(J;\mathscr{Z})
to signify the space of measurable
functions
u:J\to\mathscr{Z}
such that the corresponding norm is
bounded. Note that
\operatorname{L}_{2}(J;\mathscr{Z})
is a Hilbert space with inner
product and induced norm given by

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left({u,v}\right)_{\operatorname{L}_{2}(J;\mathscr{Z})}=\int_{J}(u(t),v(t))_{\mathscr{Z}}\operatorname{d}\!t,

and
\|{u}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}(J;\mathscr{Z})}:=\left({u,u}\right)_{\operatorname{L}_{2}(J;\mathscr{Z})}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}
,
respectively. We also let
\operatorname{H}^{1}(J;\mathscr{Z})
be the Sobolev space of
all functions in
\operatorname{L}_{2}(J;\mathscr{Z})
whose (weak temporal) derivative is
bounded in
\operatorname{L}_{2}(J;\mathscr{Z})
, with the norm

(2.3)

\|{u}\|_{\operatorname{H}^{1}(J;\mathscr{Z})}={\left({\int_{J}\left[\|{u(t)}\|_{\mathscr{Z}}^{2}+\|{u^{\prime}(t)}\|_{\mathscr{Z}}^{2}\right]\operatorname{d}\!t}\right)}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}.

Finally, the space
\operatorname{C}^{0}(\overline{J};V)
consists of all
functions that are continuous on
\overline{J}
, the closure of
J
,
with values in

\mathscr{Z}
, endowed with the standard maximum norm

(2.4)

\|{u}\|_{\operatorname{C}^{0}(\overline{J};\mathscr{Z})}=\max_{t\in\overline{J}}\|{u(t)}\|_{\mathscr{Z}}.

We consider henceforth two (real) Hilbert spaces
\mathscr{X}
and

\mathscr{H}
forming a Gelfand triple

(2.5)

\mathscr{X}{\,\hookrightarrow\,}\mathscr{H}{\,\hookrightarrow\,}{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime},

where
{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}
denotes the dual of
\mathscr{X}
. The duality
pairing
\langle\cdot\,|\,\cdot\rangle
of
{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}
and
\mathscr{X}
can be
seen as a continuous extension of the inner
product
\left({\cdot,\cdot}\right)_{\mathscr{H}}
. In particular,
identifying
{\mathscr{H}}^{\prime}\simeq\mathscr{H}
, for
u\in\mathscr{H}

and
v\in\mathscr{X}
, there holds

(2.6)

\langle u\,|\,v\rangle=\left({u,v}\right)_{\mathscr{H}};

see, e.g.,
[
Rou13
, §7.2]
for details.

Moreover, let

(2.7)

\mathscr{A}:\mathscr{X}\to{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}

be a
self-adjoint linear elliptic operator

continuous and coercive
in the sense that there exist constants

\beta\geq\alpha>0

such that

(2.8)

\begin{split}\langle\mathscr{A}v\,|\,w\rangle&\leq\beta\|{v}\|_{\mathscr{X}}\|{w}\|_{\mathscr{X}}\text{ for each }v,w\in\mathscr{X},\\
\langle\mathscr{A}v\,|\,v\rangle&\geq\alpha\|{v}\|_{\mathscr{X}}^{2}\text{ for each }v\in\mathscr{X}.\end{split}

Given an initial value
u_{0}\in\mathscr{H}
, a final time
T>0
,
denoting henceforth the time interval

(2.9)

I:=\left(0,T\right],

and given a source function
f\in\operatorname{L}_{2}(I;{{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}})
,
we are interested in a
Galerkin-type numerical approximation of the function

(2.10)

u\in\operatorname{H}^{1}(I;{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})\cap\operatorname{L}_{2}(I;\mathscr{X}),

which solves uniquely the linear parabolic initial value problem

(2.11)

{u}^{\prime}+\mathscr{A}u=f\and u(0)=u_{0}.

Incidentally, due to the continuous embedding

(2.12)

\operatorname{H}^{1}(I;{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})\cap\operatorname{L}_{2}(I;\mathscr{X}){\,\hookrightarrow\,}\operatorname{C}^{0}(\overline{I};\mathscr{H}),

it follows that
u
belongs to
\operatorname{C}^{0}(\overline{I};\mathscr{H})

[
Rou13
, e.g., Lemma 7.3]
and the initial condition
in (
2.11
) makes sense.

2.2.
Example (concrete elliptic operators)

A commonly encountered situation which can be cast in the above
framework is the classical linear diffusion equation, i.e.
\mathscr{A}v=-\nabla\cdot[\bm{{A}}\nabla v]
, where, for a given open, connected, and
bounded domain
\varOmega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=1,2
or
3
, we consider a given
symmetric matrix-valued function
\bm{{A}}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
,

\bm{{A}}\in\operatorname{L}_{\infty}(\Omega)^{d\times d}
, satisfying

(2.13)

{\bm{v}}^{\intercal}\bm{{A}}(\bm{x})\bm{v}\geq\alpha\left|\bm{v}\right|^{2}\text{ for each }\bm{x}\in\Omega,\text{ for each }\bm{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

for some constant
\alpha>0
. Here, we choose, e.g.,

\mathscr{H}:=\operatorname{L}_{2}(\varOmega)
,
\mathscr{X}:={\operatorname{H}^{1}_{0}(\varOmega)}
, and

{{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}}:=\operatorname{H}^{-1}(\varOmega)
to be the typical function spaces in the
context of second-order linear elliptic PDEs with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition

.

Another possible choice, e.g., is
\mathscr{H}:=\operatorname{L}_{2}(\varOmega)
,
\mathscr{X}:=\operatorname{H}^{2}_{0}(\varOmega)
, and

{{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}}:=\operatorname{H}^{-2}(\varOmega)

3.4.
Proposition (time-reconstruction error identities)

Consider any (real) Hilbert space
\mathscr{W}
, and

\left.W\right|_{{I_{n}}}\in\mathbb{P}^{{r_{n}}}({I_{n}};\mathscr{W})
,

(3.9)

\|{W-\widehat{W}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({I_{n}};\mathscr{W})}=C_{{{\tau_{n}},{r_{n}}}}\|\llbracket W\rrbracket_{n-1}\|_{\mathscr{W}},

where

(3.10)

C_{{{\tau},{r}}}:=\left(\frac{{\tau}({r}+1)}{(2{r}+1)(2{r}+3)}\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}},

and

(3.11)

\|{W-\widehat{W}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{\infty}({I_{n}};\mathscr{W})}=\|\llbracket W\rrbracket_{n-1}\|_{\mathscr{W}}.

Proof.

The identity (

[
MN06a
, Lemma 2.2]
,
and extended to this exact form in
[

, Theorem 2]
accounting
for the dependence on the polynomial degree explicitly. The second

3.11
) follows directly by combining the explicit representation

[

.
∎

Owing to

3.6
) originally defined
in
[

]
and

\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}

3.6.
Elliptic reconstruction

Let
\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}\subset\mathscr{X}
be a generic conforming Galerkin
space. Then, given the elliptic operator
\mathscr{A}
from (
2.7
),
we define the
discrete elliptic operator

{\mathsf{A}}_{\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}}:\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}\to\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}
, for each
{\mathsf{w}}\in\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}
, as
{\mathsf{A}}_{\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}}{\mathsf{w}}\in\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}
such that

(3.12)

\left({{\mathsf{A}}_{\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}}{\mathsf{w}},{\mathsf{v}}}\right)_{\mathscr{H}}=\langle\mathscr{A}{\mathsf{w}}\,|\,{\mathsf{v}}\rangle\text{ for each }{\mathsf{v}}\in\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}.

From the ellipticity of
\mathscr{A}
, it follows that
{\mathsf{A}}_{\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}}:\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}\to\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}
is
invertible.
Note that the discrete elliptic operator’s domain may be extended
from
\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}
to all of
\mathscr{X}
; indeed, this may be convenient in some cases where
we are ready to give up its invertibility. If
\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}
is one of
\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}
s, for
some
n={0},\dotsc,{N}
, we denote
{\mathsf{A}}_{\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}}

by
{\mathsf{A}}_{n}
.

To optimize on the structure of the mesh-change indicator
below, we use a nonstandard elliptic reconstruction on each time
interval
I_{n}
. To that end, for each
t\in I_{n}
, we define the

elliptic reconstruction

\widetilde{U}(t)\in\mathscr{X}
, by

(3.13)

\langle\mathscr{A}\widetilde{U}(t)\,|\,v\rangle=\left({{\mathsf{A}}_{n}{{\mathsf{U}}}(t)+{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n}\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}(t)-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}(t),v}\right)_{\mathscr{H}}\quad\text{ for each }v\in\mathscr{X},

where
\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}
is the time-reconstruction of the fully discrete solution
{{\mathsf{U}}}
from (
2.24
) and (
2.25
).
It follows that
\widetilde{U}\in\mathscr{Y}
and may be written implicitly, for
any
n={1},\dotsc,{N}
, as the solution of the
t
-dependent
elliptic problem

(3.14)

\mathscr{A}\widetilde{U}(t)={\mathsf{A}}_{n}{{\mathsf{U}}}(t)+{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n}\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}(t)-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}(t)\quad\text{ for }t\in I_{n}.

The initial value of
\widetilde{U}
is given by

(3.15)

\widetilde{U}(0)={\widetilde{U}}({t_{0}^{-}}):={{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{0}u({t_{0}^{-}})={{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{0}u_{0},

with
u_{0}\in\mathscr{H}
from (
2.11
).

Upon restricting the test functions in (
3.13
) to
\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}
, we obtain

(3.16)

\langle\mathscr{A}{\widetilde{U}(t)}\,|\,{\mathsf{v}}\rangle=\left({{\mathsf{A}}_{n}{{\mathsf{U}}}(t),{\mathsf{v}}}\right)_{\mathscr{H}}=\langle\mathscr{A}{{{\mathsf{U}}}(t)}\,|\,{\mathsf{v}}\rangle\quad\text{ for each }{\mathsf{v}}\in\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n},

cf. (
3.12
). Evidently this identity implies

(3.17)

\int_{{I_{n}}}\langle\mathscr{A}{\widetilde{U}}\,|\,{{\mathsf{V}}}\rangle\operatorname{d}\!t=\int_{{I_{n}}}\left({{\mathsf{A}}_{n}{{\mathsf{U}}},{{\mathsf{V}}}}\right)_{\mathscr{H}}\operatorname{d}\!t,

for each
{{\mathsf{V}}}\in\mathbb{P}^{{r_{n}}}({I_{n}};\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n})
,
n={1},\dotsc,{N}
.

3.7.
Assumption (elliptic a posteriori error estimates)

For given
g\in\mathscr{H}
, consider the abstract elliptic problem of finding
w\in\mathscr{X}
such that
\mathscr{A}w=g
. Moreover, let
\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}\subseteq\mathscr{X}
be a generic
\mathscr{X}
-conforming Galerkin space, and let
{\mathsf{w}}\in\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}

be
w
’s Galerkin approximation in
\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}
, defined implicitly as
the solution of
{\mathsf{A}}_{\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}}{\mathsf{w}}={{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}}g
. Then some
a posteriori error bound
holds, viz.,

(3.18)

\|{w-{\mathsf{w}}}\|_{\mathscr{Z}}\leq\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{Z},\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}}[{\mathsf{w}},g],

with a suitable
a posteriori error estimator

\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{Z},\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}}
, which
we assume to be available for
\mathscr{Z}
representing any of the spaces

\mathscr{X},\mathscr{H}
or
{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}
.
Recalling (
3.14
), assumption
(
3.18
) allows, for instance, to get a posteriori error
control of the
elliptic reconstruction error
in the
\mathscr{Z}
-norm,
i.e.

(3.19)

\|\widetilde{U}-{{\mathsf{U}}}\|_{\mathscr{Z}}\leq\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{Z},\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}}[{{\mathsf{U}}},{\mathsf{A}}_{n}{{\mathsf{U}}}+{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n}\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}]\quad\text{on }I_{n},

for the selection of spaces
\mathscr{Z}=\mathscr{X},\mathscr{H},\text{or
}{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}
. Details on such a posteriori error estimates can be
found, e.g., in
[
AO00
,
Bra07
,
BS07
,
DM99
]
. It is worth mentioning
that
{\mathsf{w}}
does not need to belong to
\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}

for (
3.18
) to hold; instead, it is usually enough that

\left({w-{\mathsf{w}}}\right)
is
\mathscr{A}
-orthogonal to
\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}
in order to derive
elliptic a posteriori error estimates.

3.8.
Pointwise form

For
n={1},\dotsc,{N}
, we denote by

(3.20)

{{\mathsf{\Pi}}}_{n}:\,\operatorname{L}_{2}({I_{n}};\mathscr{X}^{\prime})\to\mathbb{P}^{{r_{n}}}({I_{n}};\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}),\qquad f\mapsto{{\mathsf{\Pi}}}_{n}f,

the time-local fully discrete
\operatorname{L}_{2}({I_{n}};\mathscr{H})
-orthogonal
projection defined by

(3.21)

\int_{{I_{n}}}\left({{{\mathsf{\Pi}}}_{n}f,{{\mathsf{V}}}}\right)_{\mathscr{H}}\operatorname{d}\!t=\int_{{I_{n}}}\langle f\,|\,{{\mathsf{V}}}\rangle\operatorname{d}\!t\ \text{ for each }{{\mathsf{V}}}\in\mathbb{P}^{{r_{n}}}({I_{n}};\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}),

and its time-global counterpart

(3.22)

\begin{array}[]{rrcl}{{{\mathsf{\Pi}}}}:&{\operatorname{L}_{2}(I;\mathscr{X}^{\prime})}&\to&{\mathbb{Y}}\\
&{f}&\mapsto&{{{\mathsf{\Pi}}}f:\left.{{\mathsf{\Pi}}}f\right|_{I_{n}}={{\mathsf{\Pi}}}_{n}f\text{ for each }n=1,\dots,N\text{\ .}}\end{array}\quad

Let
{{\mathsf{V}}}\in\mathbb{Y}
, and note that using
Definition
3.3

and identity (
3.17
),
the fully discrete local DG formulation (
2.25
) transforms into

(3.23)

\int_{{I_{n}}}\left({{\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}}^{\prime}+{\mathsf{A}}_{n}{{\mathsf{U}}}-f,{{\mathsf{V}}}}\right)_{\mathscr{H}}\operatorname{d}\!t=0,\qquad n=1,\ldots,N.

Thus, for each
n={1},\dotsc,{N}
, we have

(3.24)

{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n}{\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}}^{\prime}(t)+{\mathsf{A}}_{n}{{\mathsf{U}}}(t)-{{\mathsf{\Pi}}}_{n}f(t)=0\quad\text{ for each }t\in{I_{n}},

noting that
{{\mathsf{\Pi}}}_{n}{\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}}^{\prime}={{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n}{\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}}^{\prime}

from Fubini’s Theorem. Hence, from the elliptic
reconstruction (
3.13
), we deduce the pointwise form

(3.25)

{\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}}^{\prime}(t)+\mathscr{A}\widetilde{U}(t)={{\mathsf{\Pi}}}_{n}f(t)\ \text{ $\ $for each }t\in{I_{n}}.

3.9.
Remark

Upon noting the trivial identity for
t\in I_{n}
,

(3.26)

\mathscr{A}\widetilde{U}(t)={{\mathsf{\Pi}}}_{n}f(t)-{\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}}^{\prime}(t),

we observe that the above definition of the elliptic reconstruction constitutes a high order extension of the zero-th order version from
[

, Definition 6.1]
.

4.
\operatorname{L}_{2}(I;\mathscr{X})
-norm a posteriori error analysis

To highlight the potential generality of the reconstruction
approach, we first embark on proving a posteriori error bounds in
the
\operatorname{L}_{2}(I;\mathscr{X})
-norm. As we will see below, the resulting
bounds are qualitatively closely related to the bounds from

[
ESV17
,
ESV19
,
GSKZ19
]
when
\mathscr{X}=\operatorname{H}^{1}(\varOmega)
. A key attribute of the approach
presented below is that the resulting a posteriori bounds are
flexible with respect to the choice of respective elliptic bounds,
such as residual, recovery, or flux-reconstruction ones.

4.1.
Errors

Introduce the following
errors

(4.1)

\widehat{\sigma}:=\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}-{{\mathsf{U}}}\text{ and }\widetilde{\sigma}:=\widetilde{U}-{{\mathsf{U}}}

and the corresponding
remainder error

(4.2)

\widehat{e}:=u-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}\ \and\ \widetilde{e}:=u-\widetilde{U},

whereby the
full error
can be decomposed as

(4.3)

e:=u-{{\mathsf{U}}}=\widehat{e}+\widehat{\sigma}=\widetilde{\sigma}+\widetilde{e}.

Recalling that our ultimate goal is to find a posteriori estimates
for
e
, and noting that such estimates for
\widehat{\sigma}
and

\widetilde{\sigma}
are provided by Proposition

3.4
and Assumption
3.7
, respectively, it
will suffice to estimate
\widehat{e}
, or
\widetilde{e}
, in terms of

\widehat{\sigma}
or
\widetilde{\sigma}
.

4.2.
Lemma (time reconstruction error bounds)

For
n={1},\dotsc,{N}
, we have the explicitly computable bounds

(4.4)

\|{\widehat{\sigma}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}^{2}=\sum_{m=1}^{n}C_{{{\tau_{m}},{r_{m}}}}^{2}\|\llbracket{{\mathsf{U}}}\rrbracket_{m-1}\|_{\mathscr{X}}^{2},

and

(4.5)

\|{\widehat{\sigma}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{\infty}(0,t_{n};\mathscr{H})}^{2}=\max_{m=1,\dots,n}\|\llbracket{{\mathsf{U}}}\rrbracket_{m-1}\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}.

Proof.

The proof follows immediately from Proposition
3.4
.
∎

4.3.
Lemma (space reconstruction error bounds)

Assume the availability of elliptic a posteriori error bounds as per Assumption
3.7
. Then, for
n={1},\dotsc,{N}
, we have the explicitly computable bound

(4.6)

\|{\widetilde{\sigma}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}^{2}\leq\sum_{m=1}^{n}\int_{I_{m}}\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{X},\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}}[{{\mathsf{U}}},{\mathsf{A}}_{n}{{\mathsf{U}}}+{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n}\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}]^{2}\operatorname{d}\!t.

∎

We can now prove the first main result of this work.

4.4.
Theorem (
\operatorname{L}_{2}(I;\mathscr{X})
-norm a posteriori error bound)

Let Assumption
3.7

hold. Then, for
n=1,\dots,N
,
and

(4.7)

\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\right)^{-1}\leq\vartheta^{2}\leq 1+\frac{\alpha}{\beta},

we have the following a posteriori error bound

(4.8)

\begin{split}\sqrt{\alpha}\|{e}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}\leq&\ \eta^{\rm in}+c_{\alpha,\beta}\vartheta^{-1}{\eta_{n}^{\textup{osc}}}+c_{\alpha,\beta}\vartheta\beta\left(\sum_{m=1}^{n}C_{{{\tau_{m}},{r_{m}}}}^{2}\|\llbracket{{\mathsf{U}}}\rrbracket_{m-1}\|_{\mathscr{X}}^{2}\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\\
&+\left(\sqrt{\alpha}+c_{\alpha,\beta}\vartheta\beta\right)\left(\sum_{m=1}^{n}\int_{I_{m}}\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{X},\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}}[{{\mathsf{U}}},{\mathsf{A}}_{n}{{\mathsf{U}}}+{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n}\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}]^{2}\operatorname{d}\!t)\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}},\end{split}

where
c_{\alpha,\beta}:=\sqrt{\alpha^{-1}+\beta^{-1}}
,
with the constants
\alpha,\beta
from (
2.8
), and
{\eta_{n}^{\textup{osc}}}:=\|{{{\mathsf{\Pi}}}f-f}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})},
and
\eta^{\rm in}:=\|{u_{0}-{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{0}u_{0}}\|_{\mathscr{H}}
to signify the data oscillation and initial error indicators, respectively.

Proof.

From
(
3.25
) and the PDE
(
2.11
), on each
I_{n}
, we have

(4.9)

{\widehat{e}\,}^{\prime}+\mathscr{A}\widetilde{e}={u}^{\prime}+\mathscr{A}u-{\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}}^{\prime}-\mathscr{A}\widetilde{U}=f-{{\mathsf{\Pi}}}_{n}f.

Testing (
4.9
) with

\widehat{e}
, we deduce

(4.10)

\begin{split}\left({{\widehat{e}\,}^{\prime},\widehat{e}}\right)_{\mathscr{H}}+\langle\mathscr{A}\widetilde{e}\,|\,\widetilde{e}\,\rangle=&\ \langle f-{{\mathsf{\Pi}}}_{n}f\,|\,\widehat{e}\,\rangle+\langle\mathscr{A}\widetilde{e}\,|\,\widehat{\sigma}-\widetilde{\sigma}\,\rangle.\end{split}

Note that the term
{{\mathsf{\Pi}}}_{n}f-f
occurring in
the above estimator is computable
signifying the so-called
oscillation error
.
Thanks to the continuity of
\widehat{e}
, we can integrate the last identity on
(0,t_{n})
, for some
n=1,\dots,N
, and through standard arguments, we obtain

(4.11)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\|{\widehat{e}(t_{n})}\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}+\alpha\|{\widetilde{e}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}^{2}&\leq\ \frac{1}{2}\|{\widehat{e}(0)}\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}+\beta\|{\widetilde{e}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}\|{\widehat{\sigma}-\widetilde{\sigma}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}\\
&\quad+{\eta_{n}^{\textup{osc}}}\big(\|{\widetilde{e}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}+\|{\widehat{\sigma}-\widetilde{\sigma}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}\big),\end{split}

using the continuity and coercivity of
\mathscr{A}
, cf. (
2.8
).
For any
\vartheta>0

observe the identity

(4.12)

\begin{split}\beta&\|{\widetilde{e}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}\|{\widehat{\sigma}-\widetilde{\sigma}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}+{\eta_{n}^{\textup{osc}}}\big(\|{\widetilde{e}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}+\|{\widehat{\sigma}-\widetilde{\sigma}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}\big)\\
&=\frac{\alpha}{2}\|{\widetilde{e}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha^{-1}+\beta^{-1}\right)\left(\vartheta\beta\|{\widehat{\sigma}-\widetilde{\sigma}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}+\vartheta^{-1}{\eta_{n}^{\textup{osc}}}\right)^{2}\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\left(\beta\|{\widehat{\sigma}-\widetilde{\sigma}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}-\alpha\|{\widetilde{e}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}+{\eta_{n}^{\textup{osc}}}\right)^{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha^{-1}-{\vartheta^{2}}\left(\alpha^{-1}+\beta^{-1}\right)\right)\beta^{2}\|{\widehat{\sigma}-\widetilde{\sigma}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}^{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\left(\alpha^{-1}-{\vartheta^{-2}}\left(\alpha^{-1}+\beta^{-1}\right)\right)({\eta_{n}^{\textup{osc}}})^{2}.\end{split}

Notice that the last two terms are non-positive for
\vartheta
as in (
4.7
).
Then, from (
4.11
), we infer

(4.13)

\begin{split}\alpha\|{\widetilde{e}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}^{2}\leq\|{\widehat{e}(0)}\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}+\left(\alpha^{-1}+\beta^{-1}\right)\left(\vartheta\beta\|{\widehat{\sigma}-\widetilde{\sigma}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}+\vartheta^{-1}{\eta_{n}^{\textup{osc}}}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

Taking square root and using the triangle inequality,

\|{e}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}\leq\|{\widetilde{e}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}+\|{\widetilde{\sigma}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}
,
we deduce

(4.14)

\begin{split}\sqrt{\alpha}\|{e}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}\leq&\ \|{\widehat{e}(0)}\|_{\mathscr{H}}+c_{\alpha,\beta}\vartheta\beta\|{\widehat{\sigma}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}\\
&+\left(\sqrt{\alpha}+c_{\alpha,\beta}\vartheta\beta\right)\|{\widetilde{\sigma}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};\mathscr{X})}+c_{\alpha,\beta}\vartheta^{-1}{\eta_{n}^{\textup{osc}}}.\end{split}

Invoking now Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
, and noting that
\|{\widehat{e}(0)}\|_{\mathscr{H}}=\|{u_{0}-{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{0}u_{0}}\|_{\mathscr{H}}
, the result already follows.
∎

4.5.
Remark

For the particular case
\mathscr{A}=-\Delta
,
\mathscr{X}=\operatorname{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
\mathscr{H}=\operatorname{L}_{2}(\Omega)
, cf. Example
2.2
, note that the constants in (

) can be chosen to be

4.6.
Remark (mesh change error via elliptic reconstruction)

The elliptic reconstruction
(
3.13
) features a
mesh-change type term. This is an important departure from the (standard) elliptic
reconstruction proposed in

[
LM06b
]
; cf. also
[
CGM14
, Definition 6.1]
. For instance,
if
\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{X},\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}}
from (
3.18
) is the standard
residual energy norm a posteriori error estimator, the element
residual includes the expression

{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n}\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}
, which is
effectively a mesh-change term. Indeed, from
(
3.6
) we have on
I_{n}
:

(4.15)

\begin{split}{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n}\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}=&\ {{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n}\big({{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}+\chi_{n}(\llbracket{{\mathsf{U}}}\rrbracket_{n-1})}\big)-\big({{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}+\chi_{n}(\llbracket{{\mathsf{U}}}\rrbracket_{n-1})}\big)\\
=&\ \chi_{n}({{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n}\llbracket{{\mathsf{U}}}\rrbracket_{n-1}-\llbracket{{\mathsf{U}}}\rrbracket_{n-1})=\chi_{n}({{\mathsf{U}}}(t_{n-1}^{-})-{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n}{{\mathsf{U}}}(t_{n-1}^{-})),\end{split}

from the commutativity of the spatial projection
{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n}
and
operations on the time variable. Let now

\varkappa_{n}:I_{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be the
polynomial of degree
r_{n}
representing the time lifting
\chi_{n}
from (
3.2
),
i.e. such that

(4.16)

\chi_{n}(\llbracket W\rrbracket_{n-1})(t)=\varkappa_{n}(t)\llbracket W\rrbracket_{n-1},

for all
t\in I_{n}
and
W\in\mathscr{Y}
; we refer to
[
SW10
, Lemmas 6
& 7]
or
[
HW18
, Remark 1]
for an explicit formula for

\varkappa_{n}
. Then, we can
conclude

(4.17)

\begin{split}{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n}\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}(t)-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}(t)=&\ \varkappa_{n}(t)\big({{\mathsf{U}}}(t_{n-1}^{-})-{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n}{{\mathsf{U}}}(t_{n-1}^{-})\big),\qquad t\in I_{n},\end{split}

i.e. the arbitrary order DG-analogue to the classical mesh-change
indicator. We note that the representation
(
4.17
) is particularly relevant in
implementation as it can be used to efficiently realize the space
reconstruction error bounds in Lemma
4.3
. Finally, observing that
\varkappa_{n}
takes its maximum at
t_{n-1}
, which follows from
[
SW10
, Lemma 6]
, and recalling (
3.2
), it is possible to compute the maximum value of

\varkappa_{n}
on
\overline{I}_{n}
:

(4.18)

\|{\varkappa_{n}}\|^{2}_{\operatorname{L}_{\infty}(I_{n})}=|\varkappa_{n}(t_{n-1})|^{2}=\int_{I_{n}}\varkappa_{n}(t)^{2}\operatorname{d}\!t.

Hence

(4.19)

\|{\varkappa_{n}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{\infty}(I_{n})}=\|{\varkappa_{n}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}(I_{n})}=\frac{r_{n}+1}{\sqrt{\tau_{n}}},

see
[
SW10
, Proposition 2]
.

4.7.
Remark (reconstruction vs direct approach)

As noted by one referee, it is possible to spare the use of
elliptic reconstruction in the proof of the

\operatorname{L}_{2}(I;\mathscr{X})
-norm a posteriori bound, for particular cases
of operators
\mathscr{A}
and of Gelfand triples (
2.5
),
as discussed in
[
ESV17
,
ESV19
,
GSKZ19
]
for the specific
case
\mathscr{A}=-\Delta
,
\mathscr{X}=\operatorname{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and

\mathscr{H}=\operatorname{L}_{2}(\Omega)
;
cf. Example
2.2
. As a
result, different a posteriori error estimators arise with,
possibly, slightly different constants multiplying common terms in
the estimator. Elliptic reconstruction, nonetheless, offers the
ability to use various elliptic estimators from the literature in
the bound: this feature may become important for multiscale
operators
\mathscr{A}
, e.g., singular perturbations. In general,
elliptic reconstruction allows for, crucial in some cases,
flexibility in the handling for more complicated spatial operators

\mathscr{A}
, e.g., nonlinear or singularly perturbed operators

[
CGM14
,
GM14
,
CGS20a
]
.

5.
\operatorname{L}_{\infty}(I;\mathscr{H})
-norm a posteriori error analysis

We continue by proving an a posteriori error bound in the
\operatorname{L}_{\infty}(I;\mathscr{H})
-norm, which appears to be of higher order than the
\operatorname{L}_{2}(I;\mathscr{X})
-norm one presented above. A key difference with respect to the above proof of the
\operatorname{L}_{2}(I;\mathscr{X})
-norm bound is the use of a combined space-time reconstruction defined below.

5.1.
Error-residual relation

Set
w:=\widehat{\widetilde{U}}
, i.e. the time reconstruction of the elliptic reconstruction, noting that Definition
3.3
is still valid for functions on
\mathscr{X}
in space.
Now, introducing the
time error

(5.1)

\rho:=w-u,

which is continuous on
\overline{I}=[0,T]
, and subtracting the PDE (
2.11
) from (
3.25
), we have:

(5.2)

(\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}-u)^{\prime}+\mathscr{A}(\widetilde{U}-u)={{\mathsf{\Pi}}}f-f,

our aim being to deduce an evolution equation for
\rho
. To this end, we have

(5.3)

\begin{split}\rho^{\prime}+\mathscr{A}\rho=&\ {{\mathsf{\Pi}}}f-f+(w-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}})^{\prime}+\mathscr{A}(w-\widetilde{U})\\
=&\ {{\mathsf{\Pi}}}f-f+(\widehat{\widetilde{U}-{{\mathsf{U}}}})^{\prime}+\mathscr{A}(w-\widetilde{U})=:\xi,\end{split}

from the linearity of the time reconstruction.

We define
{\mathcal{{T}}_{n}}\wedge{\mathcal{{T}}_{n-1}}
to be the finest common coarsening of the two meshes, with the associated largest common finite element subspace given by
\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}^{\ominus}=\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}\cap\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n-1}
, and a meshsize
h^{\ominus}_{n}=h_{n}\vee h_{n-1}
.

5.2.
Lemma (time reconstruction error bound)

Let
t\in I_{n}
,
n=0,1\dots,N
. Then, we have the abstract estimate

(5.4)

\|{\mathscr{A}(w-\widetilde{U})}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({I_{n}};{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})}\leq C_{{{\tau_{n}},{r_{n}}}}\eta_{n,1}^{\rm time},

with

(5.5)

\eta_{n,1}^{\rm time}:=\|\llbracket{{\mathsf{\Pi}}}f-{\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}}^{\prime}\rrbracket_{n-1}\|_{{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}}.

Assume further that, for every
v\in\mathscr{X}
, there exists a
{\mathsf{v}}\in\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}^{\ominus}
, such that

(5.6)

\|(h^{\ominus}_{n})^{-s}(v-{\mathsf{v}})\|_{\mathscr{H}}\leq C_{\rm ap}\|v\|_{\mathscr{X}},\quad\text{and}\quad\|{\mathsf{v}}\|_{\mathscr{X}}\leq C_{\rm stab}\|v\|_{\mathscr{X}},

for some
s>0
, and for generic constants
C_{\rm ap},C_{\rm stab}>0
, independent of
v
and of
h^{\ominus}_{n}
. Then, we also have the bound

(5.7)

\|{\mathscr{A}(w-\widetilde{U})}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({I_{n}};{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})}\leq C_{{{\tau_{n}},{r_{n}}}}\eta_{n,2}^{\rm time},

with

(5.8)

\eta_{n,2}^{\rm time}:=C_{\rm ap}\|(h^{\ominus}_{n})^{s}\llbracket{{\mathsf{\Pi}}}f-{\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}}^{\prime}\rrbracket_{n-1}\|_{\mathscr{H}}+C_{\rm stab}\|\llbracket{{\mathsf{U}}}\rrbracket_{n-1}\|_{\mathscr{X}}.

Therefore, setting
\eta_{n}^{\rm time}:=\min\{\eta_{n,1}^{\rm time},\eta_{n,2}^{\rm time}\}
, we have the combined estimate

(5.9)

\|{\mathscr{A}(w-\widetilde{U})}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({I_{n}};{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})}\leq C_{{{\tau_{n}},{r_{n}}}}\eta_{n}^{\rm time}.

Proof.

Recalling (
3.6
) and using the fact
that the elliptic operator
\mathscr{A}
is time independent, we
immediately observe that

(5.10)

\mathscr{A}w=\widehat{\mathscr{A}\widetilde{U}}.

Therefore, by Proposition
3.4
and of Remark
3.9
, for
n={1},\dotsc,{N-1}
, we conclude that

(5.11)

\displaystyle C_{{{\tau_{n}},{r_{n}}}}^{-1}\|{\mathscr{A}(w-\widetilde{U})}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({I_{n}};{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\|{\llbracket\mathscr{A}\widetilde{U}\rrbracket_{n-1}}\|_{{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}}=\|{\llbracket{{\mathsf{\Pi}}}f-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}\rrbracket_{n-1}}\|_{{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}},

which is (
5.4
). Furthermore,
exploit the orthogonality identity

(5.12)

\langle\llbracket\mathscr{A}\widetilde{U}\rrbracket_{n-1}\,|\,{\mathsf{v}}\rangle=\left({\llbracket{\mathsf{A}}_{n}{{\mathsf{U}}}\rrbracket_{n-1},{\mathsf{v}}}\right)_{\mathscr{H}}=\langle\llbracket\mathscr{A}{{\mathsf{U}}}\rrbracket_{n-1}\,|\,{\mathsf{v}}\rangle\quad\text{for all }{\mathsf{v}}\in\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}^{\ominus},

which follows from the definition of
\widetilde{U}
and the properties of
{{\mathsf{\Pi}}}
. From the latter, together with Remark
3.9
, and the temporal independence of
\mathscr{A}
, we have

(5.13)

\displaystyle\langle\llbracket\mathscr{A}\widetilde{U}\rrbracket_{n-1}\,|\,v\rangle=

\displaystyle(\llbracket{{\mathsf{\Pi}}}f-{\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}}^{\prime}\rrbracket_{n-1},v-{\mathsf{v}})_{\mathscr{H}}+\langle\mathscr{A}\llbracket{{\mathsf{U}}}\rrbracket_{n-1}\,|\,{\mathsf{v}}\rangle\quad\text{for all }{\mathsf{v}}\in\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}^{\ominus}.

From (
5.6
) and the continuity of
\mathscr{A}
, therefore, we deduce

(5.14)

\displaystyle\langle\llbracket\mathscr{A}\widetilde{U}\rrbracket_{n-1}\,|\,v\rangle\leq

\displaystyle\big(C_{\rm ap}\|(h^{\ominus}_{n})^{s}\llbracket{{\mathsf{\Pi}}}f-{\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}}^{\prime}\rrbracket_{n-1}\|_{\mathscr{H}}+C_{\rm stab}\|\llbracket{{\mathsf{U}}}\rrbracket_{n-1}\|_{\mathscr{X}}\big)\|v\|_{\mathscr{X}},

and the result already follows.
∎

5.3.
Remark (Practical upper bound for
\eta_{n,1}^{\rm time}
)

In standard settings, e.g., in the canonical example of the heat equation, cf. Example
2.2
, for which (
5.6
) holds, we can use
\eta_{n,2}^{\rm time}
for
\eta_{n}^{\rm time}
; this is particularly pertinent when the dimension of
\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}^{\ominus}
is comparable to those of
\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}
and
\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n-1}
. In cases, however, in which (
5.6
) may not be effective, or even known, we may be forced to revert to (
5.4
); see
[
CGS20b
]
for two such settings, where one involves virtual element methods, and the other incorporates moving mesh methods. The dual norm in
\eta_{n,1}^{\rm time}
typically requires a global inversion to be evaluated, in the absence of a natural Galerkin orthogonality property like (
5.12
) above.

Although solving an elliptic problem to determine/estimate the time estimator
\eta_{n,1}^{\rm time}
may appear to be computationally demanding, such global solves may be typically performed in a lower dimensional space than
\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}
itself, through an a posteriori error controlled fashion, as we now demonstrate. Let
\varphi\in\mathscr{X}
be the solution to the problem
\mathscr{A}\varphi=w
(with
w:=\llbracket{{\mathsf{\Pi}}}f-{\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}}^{\prime}\rrbracket_{n-1}
for instance).
Let also an approximation
\Phi\in\tilde{\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}}_{n}
to
\varphi
, for some Galerkin space
\tilde{\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}}_{n}
, be given by

(5.15)

\langle\mathscr{A}\Phi\,|\,{\mathsf{v}}\rangle=\langle w\,|\,{\mathsf{v}}\rangle\quad\text{ for all }{\mathsf{v}}\in\tilde{\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}}_{n}.

Now, the continuity of
\mathscr{A}
implies

\|w\|_{{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}}\leq\beta\|\varphi\|_{\mathscr{X}}.

In addition, from coercivity, continuity and the Galerkin orthogonality
\langle\mathscr{A}(\varphi-\Phi)\,|\,\Phi\rangle=0
, we have, respectively,

(5.16)

\alpha\|\varphi\|_{\mathscr{X}}^{2}\leq\langle\mathscr{A}\varphi\,|\,\varphi\rangle=\langle\mathscr{A}(\varphi-\Phi)\,|\,\varphi-\Phi\rangle+\langle\mathscr{A}\Phi\,|\,\Phi\rangle\leq\beta\big(\|\varphi-\Phi\|_{\mathscr{X}}^{2}+\|\Phi\|_{\mathscr{X}}^{2}\big),

using also the self-adjointness of
\mathscr{A}
. The above, together with the assumed availability of an a posteriori error indicator in the energy norm give the computable bound

(5.17)

\|w\|_{{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}}^{2}\leq\frac{\beta^{3}}{\alpha}\big(\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{X},\tilde{\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}}_{n}}^{2}[\Phi,w]+\|\Phi\|_{\mathscr{X}}^{2}\big),

or, for the specific case
w=\llbracket{{\mathsf{\Pi}}}f-{\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}}^{\prime}\rrbracket_{n-1}
,

(5.18)

\eta_{n,1}^{\rm time}\leq\sqrt{\nicefrac{{\beta^{3}}}{{\alpha}}}\big(\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{X},\tilde{\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}}_{n}}^{2}[\Phi,\llbracket{{\mathsf{\Pi}}}f-{\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}}^{\prime}\rrbracket_{n-1}]+\|\Phi\|_{\mathscr{X}}^{2}\big)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}.

5.4.
Lemma (space reconstruction error bound)

For
t\in{I_{n}}
,
n={1},\dotsc,{N}
, we have the bound

(5.19)

\|{(\widehat{\widetilde{U}-{{\mathsf{U}}}})^{\prime}(t)}\|_{{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}}\leq{\eta_{n}^{\textup{space}}},

where

(5.20)

\begin{split}{\eta_{n}^{\textup{space}}}(t):=&\ \mathscr{E}_{{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime},\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}}[{{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}(t),({\mathsf{A}}_{n}{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}+{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n}\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime\prime}-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime\prime})(t)}]\\
&+|\varkappa_{n}(t)|\mathscr{E}_{{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime},\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}}[{{\mathsf{U}}}(t_{n-1}^{+}),({\mathsf{A}}_{n}{{\mathsf{U}}}+{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n}\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime})(t_{n-1}^{+})]\\
&+|\varkappa_{n}(t)|\mathscr{E}_{{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime},\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n-1}}[{{\mathsf{U}}}(t_{n-1}^{-}),({\mathsf{A}}_{n-1}{{\mathsf{U}}}+{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n-1}\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime})(t_{n-1}^{-})],\end{split}

with
\varkappa_{n}:I_{n}\to\mathbb{R}
from
(
4.16
) in Remark
4.6
, and
the estimator is explicitly computable, via Assumption

3.7
.

Proof.

Recalling (
3.6
), on
I_{n}
we have

(5.21)

(\widehat{\widetilde{U}-{{\mathsf{U}}}})^{\prime}={\widetilde{U}}^{\prime}+\chi_{n}(\llbracket\widetilde{U}\rrbracket_{n-1})-{{{\mathsf{U}}}}^{\prime}-\chi_{n}\left({\llbracket{{\mathsf{U}}}\rrbracket_{n-1}}\right).

Hence, with the notation of Remark
4.6
, it follows that

(5.22)

\|{(\widehat{\widetilde{U}-{{\mathsf{U}}}})^{\prime}(t)}\|_{{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}}\leq\|{\widetilde{U}^{\prime}-{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}}\|_{{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}}+|\varkappa_{n}(t)|\|{\llbracket\widetilde{U}-{{\mathsf{U}}}\rrbracket_{n-1}}\|_{{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}},\quad t\in I_{n}.

Differentiating (
3.16
) with respect to
t\in I_{n}
,
and recalling that the elliptic operator
\mathscr{A}
is
t
-independent, we deduce the Galerkin orthogonality relation

(5.23)

\langle\mathscr{A}{\widetilde{U}}^{\prime}-\mathscr{A}{{{\mathsf{U}}}}^{\prime}\,|\,{\mathsf{v}}\rangle=0\text{ for each }{\mathsf{v}}\in\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}.

We conclude that
{{{\mathsf{U}}}}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}
is the Galerkin approximation of
\widetilde{U}^{\prime}
, where

(5.24)

\mathscr{A}\widetilde{U}^{\prime}={\mathsf{A}}_{n}{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}+{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n}\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime\prime}-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime\prime}\quad\text{ for }t\in I_{n},

by differentiating
(
3.14
) with respect to
t
.
Furthermore, from the definition of the elliptic reconstruction (
3.14
), we have, respectively,

(5.25)

\begin{split}\mathscr{A}\widetilde{U}(t_{n-1}^{+})=&\ {\mathsf{A}}_{n}{{\mathsf{U}}}(t_{n-1}^{+})+{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n}\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}(t_{n-1}^{+})-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}(t_{n-1}^{+}),\\
\mathscr{A}\widetilde{U}(t_{n-1}^{-})=&\ {\mathsf{A}}_{n-1}{{\mathsf{U}}}(t_{n-1}^{-})+{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n-1}\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}(t_{n-1}^{-})-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}(t_{n-1}^{-}),\end{split}

implying, therefore,

(5.26)

\begin{split}\langle\mathscr{A}\widetilde{U}(t_{n-1}^{+})-\mathscr{A}{{\mathsf{U}}}(t_{n-1}^{+})\,|\,{\mathsf{v}}\rangle=&\ 0\quad\text{ for each }{\mathsf{v}}\in\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n},\\
\langle\mathscr{A}\widetilde{U}(t_{n-1}^{-})-\mathscr{A}{{\mathsf{U}}}(t_{n-1}^{-})\,|\,{\mathsf{v}}\rangle=&\ 0\quad\text{ for each }{\mathsf{v}}\in\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n-1}.\end{split}

This means that
{{\mathsf{U}}}(t_{n-1}^{+})\in\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}
and
{{\mathsf{U}}}(t_{n-1}^{-})\in\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n-1}
are the Galerkin approximations of the first and second problem in (
5.25
), respectively.

Now, (
5.24
) together with Assumption

3.7
, yield

(5.27)

\|{\widetilde{U}^{\prime}-{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}}\|_{{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}}\leq\mathscr{E}_{{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime},\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}}[{{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime},{\mathsf{A}}_{n}{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}+{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n}\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime\prime}-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime\prime}}]\quad\text{on }I_{n}.

Similarly, noting that

(5.28)

\begin{split}\llbracket\widetilde{U}-{{\mathsf{U}}}\rrbracket_{n-1}=(\widetilde{U}-{{\mathsf{U}}})(t_{n-1}^{+})-(\widetilde{U}-{{\mathsf{U}}})(t_{n-1}^{-}),\end{split}

and combining (
5.25
) with Assumption

3.7
, leads to

(5.29)

\begin{split}\|{\llbracket\widetilde{U}-{{\mathsf{U}}}\rrbracket_{n-1}}\|_{{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime}}\leq&\ \mathscr{E}_{{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime},\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n}}[{{\mathsf{U}}}(t_{n-1}^{+}),({\mathsf{A}}_{n}{{\mathsf{U}}}+{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n}\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime})(t_{n-1}^{+})]\\
&+\mathscr{E}_{{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime},\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{n-1}}[{{{\mathsf{U}}}(t_{n-1}^{-}),({{\mathsf{A}}_{n-1}{{\mathsf{U}}}+{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{n-1}\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}})(t_{n-1}^{-})}].\end{split}

The result already follows by inserting the above estimates into (
5.22
).
∎

We are now ready to present the second main result of this work.

5.5.
Theorem (
\operatorname{L}_{\infty}(I;\mathscr{H})
-norm a posteriori bound)

With the notation of §
2
and under
Assumption
3.7
, along
with the assumptions of Lemmata
5.2
and

5.4
, for each
n={1},\dotsc,{N}
, we
have the bound

(5.30)

\displaystyle\|{u-{{\mathsf{U}}}}\|

\displaystyle{}_{\operatorname{L}_{\infty}(0,t_{n};\mathscr{H})}

\displaystyle\leq\eta^{\rm in}+(2\alpha)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}{\eta_{n}^{\textup{osc}}}+\max_{j=1,\ldots,n}\|{\llbracket{{\mathsf{U}}}\rrbracket_{j-1}}\|_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle+\Big({\frac{1}{2\alpha}\sum_{m=1}^{n}C_{{{\tau_{m}},{r_{m}}}}^{2}({\eta_{m}^{\textup{time}}})^{2}}\Big)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}+\Big({\frac{1}{2\alpha}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\int_{I_{m}}({\eta_{m}^{\textup{space}}})^{2}\operatorname{d}\!t}\Big)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}

\displaystyle+3\max_{j=1,\ldots,n}\sup_{I_{j}}\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{H},\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{j}}[{{{\mathsf{U}}},{\mathsf{A}}_{j}{{\mathsf{U}}}+{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{j}\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}}],

with
{\eta_{n}^{\textup{osc}}}
and
\eta^{\rm in}
as in Theorem
4.4
.

Proof.

We start from (
5.3
), which upon testing
with
\rho
and integrating with respect to
t
between
(0,t)
,
along with the coercivity of
\mathscr{A}
, (
2.8
), gives

(5.31)

\frac{1}{2}\|{{\rho}(t)}\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}+\alpha\|{\rho}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t};\mathscr{X})}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|{{\rho}(0)}\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\xi\,|\,\rho\rangle\operatorname{d}\!s,

and, thus,

(5.32)

\|{{\rho}(t)}\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\leq\|{{\rho}(0)}\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\|{\xi}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t};{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})}^{2},

via standard arguments.

Now fix
n\in\{1,\dots,N\}
and choose
t^{\star}\in[0,t_{n}]
such that

\|{{\rho}(t^{\star})}\|_{\mathscr{H}}=\|{\rho}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{\infty}(0,t_{n};\mathscr{H})}.

Then, letting
t=t^{\star}
in (
5.32
), and taking the square root, yields

(5.33)

\|{\rho}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{\infty}(0,t_{n};\mathscr{H})}\leq\|{{\rho}(0)}\|_{\mathscr{H}}+(2\alpha)^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|{\xi}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})}.

It remains to bound the last term on the right-hand side of
(
5.33
). To that end, applying (
3.9
), and involving Lemmata
5.2

and
5.4
, results in

(5.34)

\begin{split}\|{\xi}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})}\leq&\ {\eta_{n}^{\textup{osc}}}+\|{\mathscr{A}(w-\widetilde{U})}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})}+\|{(\widehat{\widetilde{U}-{{\mathsf{U}}}})^{\prime}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({0,t_{n}};{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})}\\
\leq&\ {\eta_{n}^{\textup{osc}}}+\!\Big(\sum_{m=1}^{n}C_{{{\tau_{m}},{r_{m}}}}^{2}({\eta_{m}^{\textup{time}}})^{2}\Big)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\!\!+\!\Big(\sum_{m=1}^{n}\int_{I_{m}}({\eta_{m}^{\textup{space}}})^{2}\operatorname{d}\!t\Big)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\!.\end{split}

The triangle inequality now gives

(5.35)

\begin{split}\|{u-{{\mathsf{U}}}}\|&{}_{\operatorname{L}_{\infty}(0,t_{n};\mathscr{H})}\\
&\leq\|{{\rho}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{\infty}(0,t_{n};\mathscr{H})}+\|{w-\widetilde{U}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{\infty}(0,t_{n};\mathscr{H})}+\|{\widetilde{U}-{{\mathsf{U}}}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{\infty}(0,t_{n};\mathscr{H})}.\end{split}

To estimate the second and third term on the right-hand side
of (
5.35
), we apply (
3.11
), and the
triangle inequality, to obtain

(5.36)

\displaystyle\|{w-\widetilde{U}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{\infty}(0,t_{n};\mathscr{H})}

\displaystyle=\max_{j=1,\dots,n}\|{\llbracket\widetilde{U}\rrbracket_{j-1}}\|_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{j=1,\dots,n}\left(\|{\llbracket\widetilde{U}-{{\mathsf{U}}}\rrbracket_{j-1}}\|_{\mathscr{H}}+\|{\llbracket{{\mathsf{U}}}\rrbracket_{j-1}}\|_{\mathscr{H}}\right).

Also, with the aid of (
3.19
),

(5.37)

\|{\widetilde{U}-{{\mathsf{U}}}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{\infty}(0,t_{n};\mathscr{H})}\leq{\max_{j=1,\ldots,n}\sup_{t\in I_{j}}\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{H},\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{j}}[{{\mathsf{U}}},{\mathsf{A}}_{j}{{\mathsf{U}}}+{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{j}\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}]}.

Combining the last two estimates, we conclude

(5.38)

\begin{split}&\|{w-\widetilde{U}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{\infty}(0,t_{n};\mathscr{H})}+\|{\widetilde{U}-{{\mathsf{U}}}}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{\infty}(0,t_{n};\mathscr{H})}\\
\leq&\ {\max_{j=1,\ldots,n}\Big(3\sup_{t\in I_{j}}\mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{H},\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{j}}[{{\mathsf{U}}},{\mathsf{A}}_{j}{{\mathsf{U}}}+{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{j}\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}]}+\|{\llbracket{{\mathsf{U}}}\rrbracket_{j-1}}\|_{\mathscr{H}}\Big).\end{split}

Combining the above completes the argument.
∎

5.6.
Remark (alternative ‘short-time’ a posteriori error estimator)

Starting from the alternative estimate

(5.39)

\int_{0}^{t}\langle\xi\,|\,\rho\rangle\operatorname{d}\!s\leq\|{\xi}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{1}(0,t_{n};\mathscr{H})}\|{\rho}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{\infty}(0,t_{n};\mathscr{H})},

) with

(5.41)

\begin{split}\|{\xi}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{1}(0,t_{n};\mathscr{H})}\leq&\ \|{{{\mathsf{\Pi}}}f-f}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{1}(0,t_{n};\mathscr{H})}+\|{\mathscr{A}(w-\widetilde{U})}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{1}(0,t_{n};\mathscr{H})}\\

5.10
), and applying Proposition
3.4
and

3.9
, we have, respectively,

(5.42)

\displaystyle\|{\mathscr{A}(w-\widetilde{U})}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{1}(0,t_{n};\mathscr{H})}^{2}

5.4

\|{(\widehat{\widetilde{U}-{{\mathsf{U}}}})^{\prime}(t)}\|_{\mathscr{H}}\leq{\eta_{m}^{\textup{space}}}^{\star}(t),

, where

(5.44)

\begin{split}{\eta_{m}^{\textup{space}}}^{\star}(t):=&\ \mathscr{E}_{\mathscr{H},\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}{}_{m}}[{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}(t),({\mathsf{A}}_{m}{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime}+{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{m}\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime\prime}-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}^{\prime\prime})(t)]\\

Combining the above estimates, we arrive at

(5.45)

\begin{split}\|{\rho}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{\infty}(0,t_{n};\mathscr{H})}&\leq\sqrt{2}\|{u_{0}-{{\mathsf{\pi}}}_{0}u_{0}}\|_{\mathscr{H}}+2\|{{{\mathsf{\Pi}}}f-f}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{1}(0,t_{n};\mathscr{H})}\\

this, in conjunction with (
5.38
) now yields

.

6.
\operatorname{H}^{1}(\mathscr{I};{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})
-type a posteriori error estimates

We conclude this work by briefly arguing on how our
techniques allow to derive
\operatorname{H}^{1}(\mathscr{I};{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})
-type
a posteriori error estimates. To this end, for any
\mathscr{I}
-piecewise
sufficiently smooth function
z
, we define the broken (semi-)norm

(6.1)

|\!|\!|z|\!|\!|_{\operatorname{H}^{1}(\mathscr{I};{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})}:=\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|{{z}^{\prime}+\chi_{n}\left({\llbracket z\rrbracket_{n-1}}\right)}\|^{2}_{\operatorname{L}_{2}(I_{n};{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})}\Big)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}},

with
\mathscr{I}
signifying the time partition of
I=(0,T]
from (
2.17
). Then, recalling
\rho
from (
5.1
), the triangle inequality yields

(6.2)

|\!|\!|u-{{\mathsf{U}}}|\!|\!|_{\operatorname{H}^{1}(\mathscr{I};{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})}\leq\left|\rho\right|_{\operatorname{H}^{1}(I;{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})}+|\!|\!|w-{{\mathsf{U}}}|\!|\!|_{\operatorname{H}^{1}(\mathscr{I};{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})},

upon noting the continuity of
\rho
with respect to the time
variable. To control the first term on the right-hand side
of (
6.2
),
we start from (
5.3
), and notice that

(6.3)

\left|\rho\right|_{\operatorname{H}^{1}(I;{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})}=\|{\xi-\mathscr{A}\rho}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}(I;{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})}\leq\|{\xi}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}(I;{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})}+\beta\|{\rho}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}(I;\mathscr{X})},

with
\beta
from (
2.8
).
Furthermore, based on (
5.31
) with
t=T
, standard arguments give

(6.4)

\alpha\|{\rho}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}({I};\mathscr{X})}^{2}\leq\|{{\rho}(0)}\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}+\alpha^{-1}\|{\xi}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}(I;{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})}^{2}.

Combining the last two bounds, we arrive at

(6.5)

\left|\rho\right|_{\operatorname{H}^{1}(\mathscr{I};{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})}^{2}\leq\frac{2\beta^{2}}{\alpha}\|{{\rho}(0)}\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}+2\Big(1+\frac{\beta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\Big)\|{\xi}\|_{\operatorname{L}_{2}(I;{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})}^{2}.

The first term on the right-hand side
of (
6.5
) is
bounded trivially by
\eta^{\rm in}
(defined in Theorem
4.4
),
while for the second we use (
5.34
).
To estimate the second term on the right-hand side
of (
6.2
),
we consider the splitting

(6.6)

|\!|\!|w-{{\mathsf{U}}}|\!|\!|_{\operatorname{H}^{1}(\mathscr{I};{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})}\leq|\!|\!|w-\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}|\!|\!|_{\operatorname{H}^{1}(\mathscr{I};{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})}+|\!|\!|\widehat{{\mathsf{U}}}-{{\mathsf{U}}}|\!|\!|_{\operatorname{H}^{1}(\mathscr{I};{\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})}.

The second term on the right-hand side of the last estimate is a

using Lemma
5.4
, through the linearity of the time
reconstruction.

7.
Conclusions

In this article we have presented a posteriori error estimates for fully discrete
hp
-DG-in-time and general Galerkin-in-space discretizations of abstract linear parabolic PDE. Our approach is based on a novel combination of temporal and elliptic reconstructions, the latter allowing for arbitrary elliptic error spatial estimators. Our main results include computable
\operatorname{L}_{\infty}(\mathscr{H})
- and

\operatorname{L}_{2}(\mathscr{X})
-a posteriori error estimates; some remarks concerning
\operatorname{H}^{1}({\mathscr{X}}^{\prime})
-norm error estimation are given as well. Finally, we note that a series of numerical experiments showcasing the optimality of the a posteriori error estimators derived above are given in

Cohomology and Deformation theory of crossed homomorphisms on Leibniz algebras
Yizheng Li, Dingguo Wang 1 \mbox{Dingguo\ Wang}^{1}

Abstract
. In this paper, we construct a differential graded Lie algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are given by crossed homomorphisms on Leibniz algebras. This allows us to define cohomology for a crossed homomorphism. Finally, we study linear deformations, formal deformations and extendibility of finite order deformations of a crossed homomorphism in terms of the cohomology theory.

Introduction

In 1960, Baxter
[
3
]
introduced the notion of Rota-Baxter operators on associative algebras in his study of fluctuation theory in probability. Rota-Baxter operators have been found many applications, including in Connes-Kreimer’s algebraic approach
to the renormalization in perturbative quantum field theory
[
4
]
.
For more details on the Rota-Baxter operator, see
[
9
]
.

The notion of crossed homomorphisms of Lie algebras was first introduced by Lue
[
12
]
in the study of non-abelian extensions of Lie algebras. A crossed homomorphism is nothing but a differential
operator of weight 1. The authors showed that the category of weak representations
(resp. admissible representations) of Lie-Rinehart algebras (resp. Leibniz pairs) is a left module
category over the monoidal category of representations of Lie algebras using crossed homomorphisms
[
13
]
. Recently, the author considerd crossed homomorphisms between associative algebras
[
6
]
.

The concept of Leibniz algebras was introduced by Loday
[
10
,
11
]
in the study of the algebraic
K
-theory. Relative Rota-Baxter
operators on Leibniz algebras were studied in
[
15
]
, which is the main ingredient in the study of the twisting theory and the bialgebra theory for Leibniz algebras
[
14
]
. Recently, the author has considered weighted relative Rota-Baxter operators on Leibniz algebras in
[
7
]
. Our aim in this paper is to consider crossed homomorphisms between Leibniz algebras using Leibniz representation introduced by
[
7
]
, More precisely, we construct a differential graded Lie algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements
are given by crossed homomorphisms on Leibniz algebras. This allows us to define cohomology
for a crossed homomorphism. Finally, we study linear deformations, formal deformations and extendibility of finite order deformations of a crossed homomorphism in terms of the cohomology theory.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 1, we recall some basic definitions about Leibniz algebras and their cohomology. In Section 2, we consider crossed homomorphisms between Leibniz algebras. In Section 3, we construct a differential graded Lie algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements
are given by crossed homomorphisms on Leibniz algebras and define cohomology
for a crossed homomorphism. In Section 4, we study linear deformations, formal deformations and extendibility of finite order deformations of a crossed homomorphism in terms of the cohomology theory.

Throughout this paper, let
{\bf k}
be a field of characteristic
0
. Except specially stated, vector spaces are
{\bf k}
-vector spaces and all tensor products are taken over
{\bf k}
.

1.
Leibniz algebras, representations and their cohomology theory

We start with the background of Leibniz algebras and their cohomology that we refer the reader to
[
5
,
11
,
7
]
for more details.

Definition 1.1
.

A Leibniz algebra is a vector space
{\mathfrak{g}}
together with a bilinear operation (called bracket)
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}}:{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
satisfying

\displaystyle[x,[y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},z]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[y,[x,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}},~\text{ for }x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

A Leibniz algebra as above may be denoted by the pair
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot])
or simply by
{\mathfrak{g}}
when no confusion arises. A Leibniz algebra whose bilinear bracket is skewsymmetric is nothing but a Lie algebra. Thus, Leibniz algebras are the non-skewsymmetric analogue of Lie algebras.

Definition 1.2
.

A representation of a Leibniz algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
consists of a triple
(V,\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
in which
V
is a vector space and
\rho^{L},\rho^{R}:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow gl(V)
are linear maps satisfying for
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\rho^{L}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})=\rho^{L}(x)\rho^{L}(y)-\rho^{L}(y)\rho^{L}(x),\\
\rho^{R}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})=\rho^{L}(x)\rho^{R}(y)-\rho^{R}(y)\rho^{L}(x),\\
\rho^{R}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})=\rho^{L}(x)\rho^{R}(y)+\rho^{R}(y)\rho^{R}(x).\end{cases}

It follows that any Leibniz algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
is a representation of itself with

\displaystyle\rho^{L}(x)=L_{x}=[x,~]_{\mathfrak{g}}~~\text{ and }~~\rho^{R}(x)=R_{x}=[~,x]_{\mathfrak{g}},\text{ for }x\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

Here
L_{x}
and
R_{x}
denotes the left and right multiplications by
x
, respectively. This is called the regular representation.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Leibniz algebra and
(V,\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
be a representation of it. The cohomology of the Leibniz algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
with coefficients in
V
is the
cohomology of the cochain complex
\{C^{\ast}({\mathfrak{g}},V),\delta\}
, where
C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)=\mathrm{Hom}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\otimes n},V)
for
n\geq 0
, and the
coboundary operator
\delta:C^{n}({\mathfrak{g}},V)\rightarrow C^{n+1}({\mathfrak{g}},V)
given by

\displaystyle(\delta f)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{i=1}(-1)^{i+1}\rho^{L}(x_{i})f(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{i},\ldots,x_{n+1})+(-1)^{n+1}\rho^{R}(x_{n+1})f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{i}f(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{i},\ldots,x_{j-1},[x_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{n+1}),

for
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. The corresponding cohomology groups are denoted by
H^{\ast}({\mathfrak{g}},V).
This cohomology has been first appeared in
[
5
]
and rediscover by Loday and Pirashvili
[
11
]
. This cohomology is also the Loday-Pirashvili cohomology.

Definition 1.3
.

(
[
2
,
8
]
) The graded vector space
C^{\ast}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})

equipped with Balavoine bracket

\displaystyle\llbracket P,Q\rrbracket:=P\overline{\diamond}Q-(-1)^{pq}Q\overline{\diamond}P~~~~~~~~~\forall P\in C^{p+1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),Q\in C^{q+1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})

is a graded Lie algebra, where
P\overline{\diamond}Q\in C^{p+q+1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
is defined by

\displaystyle P\overline{\diamond}Q=\sum_{k=1}^{p+1}(-1)^{(k-1)q}P\diamond_{\mathrm{{\bf k}}}Q

and
\diamond_{\mathrm{{\bf k}}}
is defined by

\displaystyle P\diamond_{\mathrm{{\bf k}}}Q(x_{1},\cdots,x_{p+q+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}_{(k-1,q)}}(-1)^{\sigma}P(x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(k-1)},Q(x_{\sigma(k)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(k+q-1)},x_{k+q}),x_{k+q+1},\cdots,x_{p+q+1}),

for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{p+q+1}\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Moreover,
\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a Leibniz bracket if and only if
\llbracket\mu_{\mathfrak{g}},\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}\rrbracket=0
, i. e.
\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}
is a Maurer

-Cartan element of the graded Lie algebra
(C^{\ast}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}),\llbracket-,-\rrbracket)
.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
({\mathfrak{h}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
be two Leibniz algebras. We say that
{\mathfrak{h}}
is a Leibniz
{\mathfrak{g}}
-representation if there are bilinear
maps
\rho^{L},\rho^{R}:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow
End
({\mathfrak{h}})
that make
({\mathfrak{h}},\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
into a representation of the Leibniz algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}

satisfying additionally

\displaystyle(La)\qquad\rho^{L}(x)[h,k]_{\mathfrak{h}}=[\rho^{L}(x)h,k]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[h,\rho^{L}(x)k]_{\mathfrak{h}},

\displaystyle(Lb)\qquad[h,\rho^{R}(x)k]_{\mathfrak{h}}=\rho^{R}(x)[h,k]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[k,\rho^{R}(x)h]_{\mathfrak{h}},

\displaystyle(Lc)\qquad[h,\rho^{L}(x)k]_{\mathfrak{h}}=[\rho^{R}(x)h,k]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\rho^{L}(x)[h,k]_{\mathfrak{h}},

for
h,k\in{\mathfrak{h}},x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Note that, for any Leibniz algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
, the regular representation is a Leibniz
{\mathfrak{g}}
-representation.

2.
Crossed homomorphisms on Leibniz algebras

In this section, we study crossed homomorphisms between Leibniz algebras.

Definition 2.1
.

[
7
]
Let
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
({\mathfrak{h}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
be Leibniz algebras and
({\mathfrak{h}},\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
be a Leibniz
{\mathfrak{g}}
-representation. If a linear map
H:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}
is said to be a crossed homomorphism from
{\mathfrak{g}}
to
{\mathfrak{h}}
such that the following equation

(1)

\displaystyle H([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})=\rho^{L}(x)H(y)+\rho^{R}(y)H(x)+[H(x),H(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}

holds for any
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Remark 2.2
.

A crossed homomorphism from
{\mathfrak{g}}
to
{\mathfrak{g}}
with respect to the regular representation is also called
a differential operator of weight 1.

Example 2.3
.

If the action
\rho^{L},\rho^{R}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
on
{\mathfrak{h}}
is zero, then any crossed homomorphism from
{\mathfrak{g}}
to
{\mathfrak{h}}
is
nothing but a Leibniz algebra homomorphism.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
H,H^{\prime}:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}
be two crossed homomorphisms from
{\mathfrak{g}}
to
{\mathfrak{h}}
.
A morphism from
H
to
H^{\prime}
consists of two Leibniz algebra morphisms
\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}
and

\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}:{\mathfrak{h}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}
satisfying
\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}\circ H=H^{\prime}\circ\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}
,
\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(\rho^{L}(x)h)=\rho^{L}(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(h)
and
\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(\rho^{R}(x)h)=\rho^{R}(\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}(x))\phi_{\mathfrak{h}}(h)
, for all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
and
h\in{\mathfrak{h}}
.

One can construct a new Leibniz
{\mathfrak{g}}
-representation.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
H:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}
be a crossed homomorphism. Define maps
\rho_{H}^{L},\rho_{H}^{R}:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow
End
({\mathfrak{h}})
by

\displaystyle\rho^{L}_{H}(x)h:=\rho^{L}(x)h+[H(x),h]_{\mathfrak{h}},

(2)

\displaystyle\rho^{R}_{H}(x)h:=\rho^{R}(x)h+[h,H(x)]_{\mathfrak{h}},

for
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
and
h\in{\mathfrak{h}}
. Then
({\mathfrak{h}},\rho^{L}_{H},\rho^{R}_{H})
is a Leibniz
{\mathfrak{g}}
-representation.

Proof.

First we prove that
\rho_{H}^{L},\rho_{H}^{R}
satisfy the conditions L(a)-L(c) as follows.

For any
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
and
h,k\in{\mathfrak{h}}
, we have

\displaystyle(La^{\prime})\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle[\rho^{L}_{H}(x)h,k]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[h,\rho^{L}_{H}(x)k]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\rho^{L}(x)h,k]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[[H(x),h]_{\mathfrak{h}},k]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[h,\rho^{L}(x)k]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[h,[H(x),k]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{L}(x)[h,k]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[H(x),[h,k]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{L}_{H}(x)[h,k]_{\mathfrak{h}}.

Similar to prove that

\displaystyle(Lb^{\prime})\qquad\qquad[h,\rho_{H}^{R}(x)k]_{\mathfrak{h}}=\rho_{H}^{R}(x)[h,k]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[k,\rho_{H}^{R}(x)h]_{\mathfrak{h}}.

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle(Lc^{\prime})\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle[\rho_{H}^{R}(x)h,k]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\rho_{H}^{L}(x)[h,k]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\rho^{R}(x)h,k]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[[h,H(x)]_{\mathfrak{h}},k]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\rho^{L}(x)[h,k]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[H(x),[h,k]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[h,\rho^{L}(x)k]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[h,[H(x),k]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[h,\rho^{L}_{H}(x)k]_{\mathfrak{h}}.

Next we prove that
({\mathfrak{h}},\rho_{H}^{L},\rho_{H}^{R})
is a representation over
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
.

For any
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
and
h\in{\mathfrak{h}}
, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{H}^{L}(x)\rho_{H}^{L}(y)h-\rho_{H}^{L}(y)\rho_{H}^{L}(x)h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{H}^{L}(x)(\rho^{L}(y)h+[H(y),h]_{\mathfrak{h}})-\rho_{H}^{L}(y)(\rho^{L}(x)h+[H(x),h]_{\mathfrak{h}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{L}(x)\rho^{L}(y)h+[H(x),\rho^{L}(y)h]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\rho^{L}(x)[H(y),h]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[H(x),[H(y),h]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle-\rho^{L}(y)\rho^{L}(x)h-[H(y),\rho^{L}(x)h]_{\mathfrak{h}}-\rho^{L}(y)[H(x),h]_{\mathfrak{h}}-[H(y),[H(x),h]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(La,Lc)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\rho^{L}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})h+[\rho^{L}(y)H(x),h]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[\rho^{R}(x)H(y),h]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[H(x),H(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}},h]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{L}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})h+[H([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}),h]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{L}_{H}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})h.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{H}^{R}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})=\rho_{H}^{L}(x)\rho_{H}^{R}(y)-\rho_{H}^{R}(y)\rho_{H}^{L}(x).

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{H}^{R}(y)\rho_{H}^{L}(x)h+\rho_{H}^{R}(y)\rho_{H}^{R}(x)h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{H}^{R}(y)(\rho^{L}(x)h+[H(x),h]_{\mathfrak{h}})+\rho_{H}^{R}(y)(\rho^{R}(x)h+[h,H(x)]_{\mathfrak{h}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{R}(y)\rho^{L}(x)h+[\rho^{L}(x)h,H(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\rho^{R}_{H}(y)[H(x),h]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle+\rho^{R}(y)\rho^{R}(x)h+[\rho^{R}(x)h,H(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\rho^{R}_{H}(y)[h,H(x)]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(La,Lb^{\prime},Lc)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle 0.

Hence,
({\mathfrak{h}},\rho^{L}_{H},\rho^{R}_{H})
is a Leibniz
{\mathfrak{g}}
-representation.
∎

Since
({\mathfrak{h}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a Leibniz
{\mathfrak{g}}
-representation, there is a semi-direct product Leibniz algebra
structure on
{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}
given by

\displaystyle[(x,h),(y,k)]=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\rho^{L}(x)k+\rho^{R}(y)h+[h,k]_{\mathfrak{h}},

for
(x,h),(y,k)\in{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}
. We denote this semi-direct product algebra by
{\mathfrak{g}}\ltimes{\mathfrak{h}}
.
Moreover, it follows from Lemma
2.5
that the direct sum
{\mathfrak{g}}\ltimes{\mathfrak{h}}
carries another
semi-direct product Leibniz algebra structure given by

\displaystyle[(x,h),(y,k)]_{H}=[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\rho^{L}_{H}(x)k+\rho^{R}_{H}(y)h+[h,k]_{\mathfrak{h}}.

We denote this semi-direct product algebra by
{\mathfrak{g}}\ltimes_{H}{\mathfrak{h}}
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
({\mathfrak{h}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
be Leibniz algebras with
respect to the Leibniz
{\mathfrak{g}}
-representation
({\mathfrak{h}},\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
and
H:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}
be a linear map.

(Ca) Suppose that
({\mathfrak{h}},\rho^{L}_{H},\rho^{R}_{H})
is a Leibniz
{\mathfrak{g}}
-representation given by (2). Then the linear map
\hat{H}:{\mathfrak{g}}\ltimes_{H}{\mathfrak{h}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}\ltimes{\mathfrak{h}}
defined by

\displaystyle\hat{H}(x,h)=(x,H(x)+h),\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}},h\in{\mathfrak{h}},

is a Leibniz algebra isomorphism if and only if
H
is a crossed homomorphism from
{\mathfrak{g}}
to
{\mathfrak{h}}
with
respect to the Leibniz
{\mathfrak{g}}
-representation
({\mathfrak{h}},\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
.

(Cb)
H
is a crossed homomorphism from
{\mathfrak{g}}
to
{\mathfrak{h}}
with
respect to the Leibniz
{\mathfrak{g}}
-representation
({\mathfrak{h}},\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
if and only if the
map
\iota_{H}:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}\ltimes_{H}{\mathfrak{h}}
defined by

\displaystyle\iota_{H}(x)=(x,H(x)),\forall x\in{\mathfrak{g}}

is a Leibniz algebra homomorphism.

Proof.

(Ca) Clearly
\hat{H}
is an invertible linear map. For all
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}},h,k\in{\mathfrak{h}}
, we have

\displaystyle[\hat{H}(x,h),\hat{H}(y,k)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(x,H(x)+h),(y,H(y)+k)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},\rho^{L}(x)(H(y)+k)+\rho^{R}(y)(H(x)+h)+[H(x)+h,H(y)+k]_{\mathfrak{h}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},\rho^{L}(x)k+\rho^{R}(y)h+[H(x),k]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[h,H(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[h,k]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle+[H(x),H(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\rho^{L}(x)H(y)+\rho^{R}(y)H(x))

\displaystyle\hat{H}[(x,h),(y,k)]_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},H([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\rho_{H}^{L}(x)k+\rho_{H}^{R}(y)h+[h,k]_{\mathfrak{h}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},H([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})+\rho^{L}(x)k+[H(x),k]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\rho^{R}(y)h+[h,H(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle+[h,k]_{\mathfrak{h}}).

Thus
[\hat{H}(x,h),\hat{H}(y,k)]=\hat{H}[(x,h),(y,k)]_{H}
if and only if (
1
) holds for
H
, which is equivalent
to that
H
is a crossed homomorphism from from
{\mathfrak{g}}
to
{\mathfrak{h}}
with
respect to the Leibniz
{\mathfrak{g}}
-representation
({\mathfrak{h}},\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
.

(Cb) follows from the proof of (Ca) by taking
h=k=0
.
∎

3.
Cohomology theory of crossed homomorphisms on Leibniz algebras

In this section, we consider a differential graded Lie algebra (dgLa) whose Maurer-Cartan elements are given by crossed homomorphisms on Leibniz algebras. This characterizations of
a crossed homomorphism allow us to define cohomology for a crossed homomorphism.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
({\mathfrak{h}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
be two Leibniz algebras and
({\mathfrak{h}},\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
be a Leibniz
{\mathfrak{g}}
-representation. We denote the Leibniz products on
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{h}}
respectively by
\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}
. Consider the semidirect product

{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}
. Note that
\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}
is a Maurer-Cartan element in the graded Lie algebra
\oplus_{n}\mathrm{Hom}(({\mathfrak{g}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}})^{\otimes n},{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}})
. Therefore, we can define a differential
d_{\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}}=\llbracket\mu_{\mathfrak{h}},~\rrbracket
and the derived bracket on the graded space
\oplus_{n}\mathrm{Hom}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\otimes n},{\mathfrak{h}})
by

\displaystyle\widehat{\llbracket f,g\rrbracket}:=(-1)^{m}\llbracket\llbracket\mu_{\mathfrak{h}},f\rrbracket,g\rrbracket,

for any
f\in\mathrm{Hom}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\otimes m},{\mathfrak{h}})
and
g\in\mathrm{Hom}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\otimes n},{\mathfrak{h}})
.

Moreover, we know that
\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}+\rho^{L}+\rho^{R}
is a Maurer-Cartan element in the graded Lie algebra
\oplus_{n}\mathrm{Hom}(({\mathfrak{g}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}})^{\otimes n},{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}})
from
[
14
]
, Thus it induces a differential
d_{\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}+\rho^{L}+\rho^{R}}=\llbracket\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}+\rho^{L}+\rho^{R},~\rrbracket
, and the graded space
\oplus_{n}\mathrm{Hom}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\otimes n},{\mathfrak{h}})
is closed under the differential
d=d_{\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}+\rho^{L}+\rho^{R}}
and is given by

\displaystyle(df)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n+1}\sum^{n}_{i=1}(-1)^{i+1}\rho^{L}(x_{i})f(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{i},\ldots,x_{n+1})+\rho^{R}(x_{n+1})f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{i}f(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{i},\ldots,x_{j-1},[x_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{n+1}).

Finally, we have
\llbracket\mu_{\mathfrak{g}}+\rho^{L}+\rho^{R},\mu_{\mathfrak{h}}\rrbracket
=0. Hence,
(\oplus_{n}\mathrm{Hom}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\otimes n},{\mathfrak{h}}),\widehat{\llbracket~,~\rrbracket},d)
is a dgLa.

Proposition 3.1
.

A linear map
H:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}
is a crossed homomorphism from
{\mathfrak{g}}
to
{\mathfrak{h}}
if
and only if
H\in C^{1}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})
is a Maurer-Cartan element in the dgLa
(\oplus_{n}\mathrm{Hom}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\otimes n},{\mathfrak{h}}),\widehat{\llbracket~,~\rrbracket},d)
.

Proof.

For any linear map
H:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}
and
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, we have

\displaystyle(dH+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\llbracket H,H\rrbracket})(x,y)=\rho^{L}(x)H(y)+\rho^{R}(y)H(x)-H([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})+[H(x),H(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}.

Hence
H
is a crossed homomorphism if and only if
H
is a Maurer-Cartan element.
∎

It follows from the above proposition that a crossed homomorphism
H
induces a differential
d_{H}=d+\widehat{\llbracket H,~\rrbracket}

on the graded Lie algebra
(\oplus_{n}\mathrm{Hom}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\otimes n},{\mathfrak{h}}),\widehat{\llbracket~,~\rrbracket})
. Define
C^{n}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})=\mathrm{Hom}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\otimes n},{\mathfrak{h}})

and
C^{\ast}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})=\oplus_{n}C^{n}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})
. Thus a
crossed homomorphism induces a dgLa
(C^{\ast}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}),\widehat{\llbracket~,~\rrbracket},d_{H})
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
({\mathfrak{h}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
be two Leibniz algebras and
({\mathfrak{h}},\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
be a Leibniz
{\mathfrak{g}}
-representation. Suppose
H
is a crossed homomorphism. Then for any linear map
H^{\prime}:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}
, the sum
H+H^{\prime}
is also a crossed homomorphism if and only if
H^{\prime}
is a Maurer-Cartan element in the dgLa
(C^{\ast}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}),\widehat{\llbracket~,~\rrbracket},d_{H})
.

Proof.

\displaystyle(d(H+H^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\llbracket H+H^{\prime},H+H^{\prime}\rrbracket})

\displaystyle=dH+dH^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}(\widehat{\llbracket H,H\rrbracket}+\widehat{\llbracket H,H^{\prime}\rrbracket}+\widehat{\llbracket H^{\prime},H\rrbracket}+\widehat{\llbracket H^{\prime},H^{\prime}\rrbracket})

\displaystyle=dH^{\prime}+\widehat{\llbracket H,H^{\prime}\rrbracket}+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\llbracket H^{\prime},H^{\prime}\rrbracket}

\displaystyle=d_{H}(H^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{\llbracket H^{\prime},H^{\prime}\rrbracket}.

And the proof is finished.
∎

The cohomology of the cochain complex
(C^{\ast}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}),d_{H})
is called the cohomology of the crossed homomorphism
H
, if
\mathcal{Z}_{H}^{k}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})=\{f\in C^{k}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})|d_{H}(f)=0\}
is the space of
k
-cocycles and
\mathcal{B}^{k}_{H}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})=\{d_{H}(f)\in C^{k}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})|f\in C^{k-1}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})\}
is the space of
k
-coboundaries then
\mathcal{B}^{k}_{H}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})\subset\mathcal{Z}^{k}_{H}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})
, for
k\geq 0
. The corresponding cohomology groups

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{k}_{H}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})=\frac{\mathcal{Z}^{k}_{H}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})}{\mathcal{B}^{k}_{H}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})},k\geq 0.

We denote the corresponding cohomology groups simply by
\mathcal{H}^{\ast}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})
.

First recall from Lemma (
3
) that a crossed homomorphism
H
induces a Leibniz
{\mathfrak{g}}
-representation given by

\displaystyle\rho^{L}_{H}(x)h:=\rho^{L}(x)h+[H(x),h]_{\mathfrak{h}},~~~~~~~~\rho^{R}_{H}(x)h:=\rho^{R}(x)h+[h,H(x)]_{\mathfrak{h}}.

The corresponding cochain groups are given by
C^{n}_{\mathrm{Leib}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})=\mathrm{Hom}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\otimes n},{\mathfrak{h}})
, for
n\geq 0
, and the coboundary operator
\delta_{\mathrm{Leib}}:C^{n}_{\mathrm{Leib}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})\rightarrow C^{n+1}_{\mathrm{Leib}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})
is given by

\displaystyle(\delta_{\mathrm{Leib}}f)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{i=1}(-1)^{i+1}\rho^{L}(x_{i})f(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{i},\ldots,x_{n+1})+[H(x_{i}),f(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{i},\ldots,x_{n+1})]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+1}\rho^{R}(x_{n+1})f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})+(-1)^{n+1}[f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),H(x)]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{i}f(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{i},\ldots,x_{j-1},[x_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{n+1}),

for
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}\in{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

The coboundary operators
d_{H}
and
\delta_{\mathrm{Leib}}
are related by

\displaystyle d_{H}(f)=(-1)^{n-1}\delta_{\mathrm{Leib}}(f),\forall f\in C^{n}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}).

Proof.

For any
f\in C^{n}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})
, we have

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-1}(d_{H}(f))(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n-1}(df+\widehat{\llbracket H,f\rrbracket})(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{i=1}(-1)^{i+1}\rho^{L}(x_{i})f(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{i},\ldots,x_{n+1})+(-1)^{n+1}\rho^{R}(x_{n+1})f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{i}f(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{i},\ldots,x_{j-1},[x_{i},x_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}},x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\{[H(x_{i}),f(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{i},\ldots,x_{n+1})]_{\mathfrak{h}}+(-1)^{n+1}[f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),H(x)]_{\mathfrak{h}}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{Leib}}(f).

And the proof is finished.
∎

4.
Deformations of crossed homomorphisms on Leibniz algebras

In this section, we study deformations of a crossed homomorphism on Leibniz algebras.

4.1.
Linear deformations

Let
H:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}
be a crossed homomorphism. A linear deformation of
H
consists
of a sum
H_{t}=H+tH_{1}
such that
H_{t}

is a crossed homomorphism, for all values
of
t
. In such a case, we say that
H_{1}
generates a one-parameter linear deformation
of
H
.
Thus, if
H_{1}
generates a linear deformation of
H
then
H_{t}=H+tH_{1}

satisfies

\displaystyle H_{t}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})=\rho^{L}(x)H_{t}(y)+\rho^{L}(y)H_{t}(x)+[H_{t}(x),H_{t}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}},\forall x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

That is

(3)

\displaystyle H_{1}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})=\rho^{L}(x)H_{1}(y)+\rho^{L}(y)H_{1}(x)+[H_{1}(x),H(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[H(x),H_{1}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}},

(4)

\displaystyle[H_{1}(x),H_{1}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}=0.

It follows from (3) that
H_{1}
is a 1-cocycle in the cohomology of the crossed homomorphism
H
.

Definition 4.1
.

Two linear deformations
H_{t}=H+tH_{1}
and
H^{\prime}_{t}=H+tH^{\prime}_{1}

are said to be equivalent if there exists
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
such that

\displaystyle\phi_{t}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{g}}+tL_{x},\quad\psi_{t}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{h}}+t\rho^{L}(x)

is a homomorphism from
H_{t}
to
H^{\prime}_{t}
.

Thus, if
H_{t}
and
H^{\prime}_{t}
are equivalent linear deformations, then the following conditions hold:

\displaystyle(i)~~[\phi_{t}(y),\phi_{t}(z)]_{\mathfrak{g}}=\phi_{t}([y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}),~~~[\psi_{t}(h),\psi_{t}(k)]_{\mathfrak{h}}=\psi_{t}([h,k]_{\mathfrak{h}}),

\displaystyle(ii)~~\phi_{t}(\rho^{L}(y)h)=\rho^{L}(\phi_{t}(y))\psi_{t}(h),

\displaystyle(iii)~~\phi_{t}(\rho^{R}(y)h)=\rho^{R}(\phi_{t}(y))\psi_{t}(h),

\displaystyle(iv)~~H^{\prime}_{t}\circ\phi_{t}(y)=\psi_{t}\circ H_{t}(y),

for all
y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
and
h,k\in{\mathfrak{h}}
.

Note that (i) is equivalent

(5)

\displaystyle[[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},[x,z]_{\mathfrak{g}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=0,~~~~~[\rho^{L}(x)h,\rho^{L}(x)k]_{\mathfrak{h}}=0.

Further, (ii)and (iii) imply that

(6)

\displaystyle\rho^{L}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})\rho^{L}(x)=0,

(7)

\displaystyle\rho^{R}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})\rho^{L}(x)=0,~~~~\forall y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

Finally, the condition (iv) is equivalent to

(8)

\displaystyle H_{1}(y)-H^{\prime}_{1}(y)=\rho^{R}(y)H(x)+[H(x),H(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}},

(9)

\displaystyle\rho^{L}(x)H_{1}(y)=H_{1}^{\prime}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}).

It follows from (8) that
H_{1}(y)-H^{\prime}_{1}(y)=\delta_{\mathrm{Leib}}(-H(x))y.
Hence, we obtain the following

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
H_{t}=H+tH_{1}
be a linear deformation of
H
. Then the linear term

H_{1}
is a 1-cocycle in the cohomology of
H
. Its cohomology class depends only on the
equivalence class of
H_{t}
.

Definition 4.3
.

A linear deformation
H_{t}
of a crossed homomorphism
H
is said to
be trivial if
H_{t}
is equivalent to
H^{\prime}_{t}=H
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
H
be a crossed homomorphism from
{\mathfrak{g}}
to
{\mathfrak{h}}
. An element

x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
is called a Nijenhuis element associated to
H
if
x
satisfies (5), (6), (7) and

\displaystyle[x,\rho^{R}(y)H(x)+[H(x),H(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=0,~~~~\forall y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

Denote by
Nij(H)
the set of Nijenhuis elements associated to
H
. Then we have

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
H
be a crossed homomorphism from
{\mathfrak{g}}
to
{\mathfrak{h}}
.. Then for any
x\in Nij(H)
,
the sum
H_{t}=H+tH_{1}
with
H_{1}=-\delta_{\mathrm{Leib}}(H(x))
is a trivial deformation of
H
.

4.2.
Formal deformations

Let
H:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}
be a crossed homomorphism. Let
\mathbf{k}[[t]]
be the ring of power series in one variable
t
. For any
\mathbf{k}
-linear space
{\mathfrak{g}}
,
let
{\mathfrak{g}}[[t]]
denotes the vector space of formal power series in
t
with coefficients from
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then
{\mathfrak{g}}[[t]]
is Leibniz
algebra structure over
\mathbf{K}[[t]]
. If
{\mathfrak{h}}
is a Leibniz algebra which is also a Leibniz
{\mathfrak{g}}
-representation, then
{\mathfrak{h}}[[t]]
is a Leibniz algebra over
\mathbf{k}[[t]]
and a Leibniz
{\mathfrak{g}}[[t]]
-representation.

Definition 4.6
.

A formal one-parameter deformation of a crossed homomorphism

H:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}
consists of a formal sum

(10)

\displaystyle H_{t}=H_{0}+tH_{1}+t^{2}H_{2}+\cdots\in\mathrm{Hom}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})[[t]]

with
H_{0}=H
such that
H_{t}:{\mathfrak{g}}[[t]]\rightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}[[t]]
is a crossed homomorphism from

{\mathfrak{g}}[[t]]
to
{\mathfrak{h}}[[t]]
.

Note that (10) is equivalent to: for each
n\geq 0

\displaystyle H_{n}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})=\rho^{L}(x)H_{n}(y)+\rho^{R}(y)H_{n}(x)+\sum_{i+j=n}[H_{i}(x),H_{j}(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}.

That is

\displaystyle d_{H}(H_{n})=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j=n,i,j\geq 1}\widehat{\llbracket H_{i},H_{j}\rrbracket},n\geq 0.

The identity holds for
n=0
as
H
is a crossed homomorphism. For
n=1
, we get

d_{H}(H_{1})=0
. Hence
H_{1}
is a 1-cocycle in the cohomology complex of
H
. This is
called the infinitesimal of the deformation
H_{t}
.

Definition 4.7
.

Two o formal deformations
H_{t}
and
H^{\prime}_{t}
of a crossed homomorphism
H

are said to be equivalent if there exists
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
such that

\displaystyle\phi_{t}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{g}}+tL_{x},\quad\psi_{t}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{h}}+t\rho^{L}(x)

is a homomorphism from
H_{t}
to
H^{\prime}_{t}
.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
H_{t}
be a formal deformation of a crossed homomorphism
H
. Then
the linear term
H_{1}
is a 1-cocycle in the cohomology of
H
. (It is called the infinitesimal
of the deformation.) Moreover, the corresponding cohomology class depends only on the
equivalence class of
H_{t}
.

Definition 4.9
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
({\mathfrak{h}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
be two Leibniz algebras and
({\mathfrak{h}},\rho^{L},\rho^{R})
a Leibniz
{\mathfrak{g}}
-representation. A crossed homomorphism
H:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}
is said to be rigid if any
formal deformation
H_{t}
of
H
is equivalent to
H^{\prime}_{t}=H
.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
H
be a crossed homomorphism. If
\mathcal{Z}^{1}_{H}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})=\delta_{\mathrm{Leib}}(H(Nij(H)))
, then
H
is rigid.

Proof.

Let
H_{t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}t^{i}H_{i}
be any formal deformation of
H
. By Proposition 4.8, we deduce
H_{1}\in\mathcal{Z}^{1}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})
. By the assumption

\mathcal{Z}^{1}_{H}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})=\delta_{\mathrm{Leib}}(H(Nij(H)))
, we obtain
H_{1}=-\delta_{\mathrm{Leib}}(H(x))
for some
x\in Nij(H)
. Then setting

\phi_{t}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{g}}+tL_{x},\quad\psi_{t}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{h}}+t\rho^{L}(x)
, we get a formal deformation
\overline{H}_{t}:=\psi_{t}\circ H_{t}\phi_{t}^{-1}

Thus,
H_{t}
is equivalent to
\overline{H}_{t}
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\overline{H}_{t}(y))(\mathrm{mod}~t^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\rho^{L}(x))\circ(H+tH_{1})(y-t[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})(\mathrm{mod}~t^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{g}}+t\rho^{L}(x))(H(y)+tH_{1}(y)-tH([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}))(\mathrm{mod}~t^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H(y)+t(\rho^{L}(x)H(y)-H([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+H_{1}(y)).

Since

\displaystyle H_{1}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\delta_{\mathrm{Leib}}(H(x))(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho^{R}(y)H(x)+[H(x),H(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\rho^{L}(x)H(y)-H([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}).

The coefficient of
t
in the expression of
\overline{H}_{t}

is zero. Then by repeating this argument, one get the equivalence between

H_{t}
and
H
. Hence the proof.
∎

4.3.
Extensions of finite order deformations

In this subsection, we introduce a cohomology class associated to any order
N
deformation of a crossed homomorphism, and show that an order
n
deformation is extensible if and only if this cohomology class is trivial. Thus, we call this cohomology class the obstruction class of the order
N
deformation being extensible.

Let
H:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}
be a crossed homomorphism. Consider the space
{\mathfrak{g}}[[t]]/(t^{N+1})

which inherits a Leibniz algebra structure over
\mathbf{k}[[t]]/(t^{N+1})
, similarly,
{\mathfrak{h}}[[t]]/(t^{N+1})
is a Leibniz algebra and a Leibniz
{\mathfrak{g}}[[t]]/(t^{N+1})
-representation.

Definition 4.11
.

A deformation of
H
of order
N
consists of a finite sum
H_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{N}t^{i}H_{i}
with
H_{0}=H
such that
H_{t}
is a crossed homomorphism from
{\mathfrak{g}}[[t]]/(t^{N+1})
to
{\mathfrak{h}}[[t]]/(t^{N+1})
.

Definition 4.12
.

If there exists a linear map
H_{N+1}:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}
such that
\hat{H}_{t}=H_{t}+t^{N+1}H_{N+1}
is a deformation of order
N+1
, we say that
H_{t}
is extensible.

Thus, if a deformation
H_{t}
of order
N
is extensible then one more deformation
equation need to be satisfied, namely,

\displaystyle d_{H}(H_{N+1})=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j=N+1,i,j\geq 1}\widehat{\llbracket H_{i},H_{j}\rrbracket},n\geq 0.

for some
H_{N+1}
. Note that the right hand side of the above equation does not
involve
H_{N+1}
, we denote it by
Ob_{H_{t}}
. Obviously
Ob_{H_{t}}

is a 2-cochain in the cohomology of
H
, we have the following.

Proposition 4.13
.

The
2
-cochain
Ob_{H_{t}}
is a 2-cocycle, that is,
d_{H}(Ob_{H_{t}})=0
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle d_{H}(Ob_{H_{t}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j=N+1,i,j\geq 1}(d\widehat{\llbracket H_{i},H_{j}\rrbracket}+\widehat{\llbracket H,\widehat{\llbracket H_{i},H_{j}\rrbracket}\rrbracket})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j=N+1,i,j\geq 1}(\widehat{\llbracket d(H_{i}),H_{j}\rrbracket}-\widehat{\llbracket H_{i},d(H_{j})\rrbracket}+\widehat{\llbracket\widehat{\llbracket H,H_{i}\rrbracket},H_{j}\rrbracket}-\widehat{\llbracket H_{i},\widehat{\llbracket H,H_{j}\rrbracket}\rrbracket})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j=N+1,i,j\geq 1}(\widehat{\llbracket d_{H}(H_{i}),H_{j}\rrbracket}-\widehat{\llbracket H_{i},d_{H}(H_{j})\rrbracket})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i_{1}+i_{2}+j=N+1,i_{1},i_{2},j\geq 1}\widehat{\llbracket\widehat{\llbracket H_{i_{1}},H_{i_{2}}\rrbracket},H_{j}\rrbracket}-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i+j_{1}+j_{2}=N+1,i_{1},i_{2},j\geq 1}\widehat{\llbracket H_{i},\widehat{\llbracket H_{j_{1}},H_{j_{2}}\rrbracket}\rrbracket}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i+j+k=N+1,i,j,k\geq 1}\widehat{\llbracket\widehat{\llbracket H_{i},H_{j}\rrbracket},H_{k}\rrbracket}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

∎

The cohomology class
[Ob_{H_{t}}]\in\mathcal{H}_{H}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})
is called the
obstruction class
to
extend the deformation
H_{t}
. we have the
following.

Theorem 4.14
.

A finite order deformation
H_{t}
of a crossed homomorphism
H
extends
to a deformation of next order if and only if the obstruction class
[Ob_{H_{t}}]\in\mathcal{H}_{H}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})

vanishes.

Corollary 4.15
.

If
\mathcal{H}_{H}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})=0
, then any finite order deformation of
H
extends to a
deformation of next order.

Theorem 4.16
.

If
\mathcal{H}_{H}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})=0
, then every 1-cocycle in the cohomology of
H
is the
infinitesimal of some formal deformation of
H
.

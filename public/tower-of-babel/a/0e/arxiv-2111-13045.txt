On the stability and null-controllability of an infinite system of linear differential equations Thanks: MR would like to thank The FWF for supporting his research through the Lise Meitner Fellowship FWF M-2816. The authors also thank the referees for useful comments which improved the exposition.
Abdulla Azamov, Gafurjan Ibragimov, Khudoyor Mamayusupov, Marks Ruziboev

Abstract
. In this work, the null controllability problem for a linear system in ℓ 2 \ell^{2} is considered, where the matrix of a linear operator describing the system is an infinite matrix with λ ∈ ℝ \lambda\in\mathbb{R} on the main diagonal and 1s above it. We show that the system is asymptotically stable if and only if λ ≤ − 1 \lambda\leq-1 , which shows the fine difference between the finite and the infinite-dimensional systems. When λ ≤ − 1 \lambda\leq-1 we also show that the system is null controllable in large. We also show a dependence of the stability on the norm i.e. the same system considered in ℓ ∞ \ell^{\infty} is not asymptotically stable if λ = − 1 \lambda=-1 .

Often it is useful to study finite dimensional approximations of the infinite system, such an approach is taken in

In the above works the authors obtain infinite linear ODEs, where the right hand has a diagonal form. The proofs suggest that similar results maybe proven for linear systems with block diagonal form under certain mild assumptions.

In fact, as it is shown in

In this work we consider a simple infinite linear controllable system in
\ell^{2}

\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
on the main diagonal. Therefore, any finite dimensional approximation of the system is asymptotically stable whenever
\lambda<0

\lambda\leq-1
and when
\lambda>-1
solutions in certain directions grow exponentially fast. This shows fine difference between finite dimensional and infinite systems. Another main feature of this notes is that using Gramian operators, we give explicit form of control functions that stabilize the system.

Let
\ell^{2}=\{\y=(y_{1},y_{2},\dots)\mid y_{n}\in\mathbb{R},\sum_{n\geq 1}y_{n}^{2}<\infty\}
. We consider
\ell^{2}
with it’s natural norm:
\|\y\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{n\geq 1}y_{n}^{2}
, which turns it into a Hilbert space.

Given an infinite system of ODEs:

(1)

\dot{y}_{n}=\lambda y_{n}+y_{n+1},y_{n}(0)=y_{n,0},

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is a fixed number and
\y_{0}=\{y_{n,0}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\ell^{2}
.
We can rewrite the system in an operator form

(2)

\dot{\y}=A\y,\y(0)=\y_{0},

where
\y_{0}=\{y_{n,0}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
A:\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}
is a linear operator defined by

A\y=\{\lambda y_{n}+y_{n+1}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}.

This is an example of an ODE in a Banach space, which is a well studied topic (see for example
[
13
,
12
]
), here we study the stability and control problems. In particular, we construct controls function explicitly.

Observe that
A
is a bounded linear operator, in fact we have

\displaystyle\|A\y\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{n\geq 1}(\lambda y_{n}+y_{n+1})^{2}\leq(1+|\lambda|)^{2}\|\y\|_{2}^{2}.

Hence,
\|A\|=\sup_{{\|\y\|}_{2}=1}\|A\y\|_{2}\leq 1+|\lambda|
.

Now, it is standard to define
e^{tA}
as

e^{tA}:=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{t^{n}A^{n}}{n!},

which is bounded on
\ell^{2}
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Further,
e^{tA}
admits all the properties of analogues operator for matrices. In particular,
e^{tA}
defines a group of operators.
The solution of (
2

\y(t)=e^{tA}\y_{0}.

We also consider the Cauchy problem for non-homogeneous equation

(3)

\dot{\y}=A\y+\f,\quad\y(0)=\y_{0},

for
\textbf{f}:\mathbb{R}\to\ell^{2}
,
\f\in L^{2}([0,T],\ell^{2})
, i.e.
\|\f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{0}^{T}\|\f(t)\|^{2}_{2}dt<+\infty
1
1

1

We note that this norm coincides with
\|\f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\int_{0}^{T}|f_{n}(t)|^{2}dt
thanks to Beppo-Levi’s theorem.
.

A function
\y:[0,T]\to\ell^{2}
defined as

\y(t)=e^{tA}\y_{0}+e^{tA}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-sA}\f(s)ds

is called a mild solution of (
3

\y\in C([0,T],\ell^{2})
. Here the integration is understood componentwise.
For completeness we start with the following.

Proposition 1.1
.

For every
\f\in L^{2}([0,T],\ell^{2})
and
\y_{0}\in\ell^{2}
we have
\y\in C([0,T],\ell^{2})
.

The next result is about stability. In this simple setting we can characterize the system completely. We have the following.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
\y(t)
be the solution of (
1
) with an initial condition
\y_{0}\in\ell^{2}
.
System (

) is asymptotically stable if and only if
\lambda\leq-1
. Moreover for every
\y_{0}\in\ell^{2}
and for every

holds
\|e^{tA}\y_{0}\|_{2}\leq e^{(1+\lambda)t}\|\y_{0}\|_{2}
.

Let
\rho>0
be fixed. A control function
\textbf{f}:\mathbb{R}\to\ell^{2}
is called admissible if

\|\f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{0}^{T}\|\f(t)\|^{2}_{2}dt\leq\rho^{2}.

We say that the system (
3

null-controllable
from
\y_{0}\in\ell^{2}
an admissible control
\textbf{f}:\mathbb{R}\to\ell^{2}
and

such that the solution of (

We say that the system (

We say that the system (

The main result of this notes is the following

Theorem 1.1
.

(i)

The system (

\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

(ii)

If
\lambda\leq-1
, then system (
3

(iii)

If
\lambda<-1
the systems can be transferred from an initial point
\y_{0}\in\ell^{2}
into the origin for time

, where

is a constant independent of

.

Notice that we didn’t aim to state the results in the most general form. Also, in Proposition

for

somewhat unusual. But apparently it is due to the structure of

2.
Asymptotic stability

We start this section with the proof of Proposition
1.1
.

Proof of Proposition
1.1
.

For any
T\in\mathbb{R}
,
t_{0},t\in[0,T]
and
y_{0}\in\ell^{2}
we have

(4)

\|e^{tA}-e^{t_{0}A}\|_{2}\leq\|e^{t_{0}A}\|_{2}\cdot\|e^{(t-t_{0})A}-\id\|_{2}\leq|t-t_{0}|\cdot\|A\|_{2}\cdot e^{2T\|A\|_{2}},

where in the last inequality we have used the definition of
e^{tA}
and
\|e^{tA}\|_{2}\leq e^{|t|\cdot\|A\|_{2}}
. For any
\f\in L^{2}([0,T],\ell^{2})
and
0\leq t_{0}\leq t\leq T
we have

(5)

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-sA}\f(s)ds\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|e^{-sA}\|_{2}\cdot\|\f(s)\|_{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{2s\cdot\|A\|_{2}}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|\f(s)\|_{2}^{2}ds\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq|t-t_{0}|^{1/2}e^{t\cdot\|A\|_{2}}\|\f\|_{L^{2}},

where in the second inequality the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality is used.
We have

(6)

\displaystyle\|\y(t)-\y(t_{0})\|_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\|e^{tA}-e^{t_{0}A}\|_{2}\cdot\|\y_{0}\|_{2}

\displaystyle+\|e^{tA}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-sA}\f(s)ds-e^{t_{0}A}\int_{0}^{t_{0}}e^{-sA}\f(s)ds\|_{2}.

The first term on the right hand side of the inequality tends to
0
when
t\to t_{0}
by (
4
). We will show that the second term also tends to
0
as
t
approaches
t_{0}
. Without loss of generality, assume
t>t_{0}
then the second term of (
6
) is bounded by

\|e^{tA}\|_{2}\cdot\|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-sA}\f(s)ds\|_{2}+\|e^{tA}-e^{t_{0}A}\|_{2}\cdot\|\int_{0}^{t_{0}}e^{-sA}\f(s)ds\|_{2},

which tends to
0
by (
5
). The proof is finished.
∎

2.1.
Stability of the system

Here we give necessary and sufficient condition for the asymptotic stability of the system in
\ell^{2}
. Recall that the system (
1
) is called globally asymptotically stable if
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\y(t)=0
for the solution
\y(t)
of (
1
) with any initial condition
\y_{0}\in\ell^{2}
.

Proof of Proposition
1.2
.

We write
A=\lambda\id+E
, where

\id
is the identity map, and
E:\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}
is the shift map, i.e.
[Ef]_{i}=f_{i+1}
. Then we have
e^{tA}=e^{\lambda t}e^{tE}
. Now, we obtain item (i) directly:

\|\y(t)\|_{2}\leq\|e^{tA}\|\cdot\|\y_{0}\|_{2}=e^{\lambda t}\|e^{tE}\|\cdot\|\y_{0}\|_{2}\leq e^{(\lambda+1)t}\cdot\|\y_{0}\|_{2}.

If
\lambda<-1
the latter inequality implies
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\y(t)=0
.

If
\lambda=-1
then the above argument doesn’t imply the desired conclusion. Thus we proceed as follows. Observe that

(7)

e^{tE}=\begin{pmatrix}1&t&\frac{t^{2}}{2!}&\dots&\frac{t^{k}}{k!}&\dots\\
0&1&t&\dots&\frac{t^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}&\dots\\
0&0&1&\dots&\frac{t^{k-2}}{(k-2)!}&\dots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}.

Then for any
\z\in\ell^{2}
with
\|\z\|_{2}=1
and for the solution
\y(\cdot)
started from
\y_{0}=(y_{10},y_{20},\dots)\in\ell^{2}
we obtain

(8)

\langle\y(t),\z\rangle=e^{-t}\langle\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}E^{j}\y_{0},\z\rangle=e^{-t}\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\langle E^{j}\y_{0},\z\rangle.

From the definition of
E
we have
\|E^{j}\y_{0}\|_{2}\leq\|\y_{0}\|_{2}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\|E^{j}\y_{0}\|_{2}\to 0
as
j\to\infty
. Thus for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
N=N(\y_{0})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
\|E^{j}\y_{0}\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon/2
for all
j\geq N
. Fixing such an
N
and using
|\langle E^{j}\y_{0},\z\rangle|\leq\|E^{j}\y_{0}\|_{2}\leq\|\y_{0}\|_{2}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
from (
8
) we obtain

(9)

\displaystyle|\langle\y(t),\z\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq e^{-t}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\|E^{j}\y_{0}\|_{2}+e^{-t}\sum_{j=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\|E^{j}\y_{0}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\y_{0}\|_{2}e^{-t}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}e^{-t}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\leq\|\y_{0}\|_{2}C_{N}e^{-t/2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Notice that the choice of
N
and hence
C_{N}
is independent of
t
. Therefore, (
9
) implies that there exists
t(\varepsilon)>0
such that
\|\y_{0}\|_{2}C_{N}e^{-t/2}\leq\varepsilon/2
for all
t\geq t(\varepsilon)
. Finally, taking
\z=\y(t)/\|\y(t)\|_{2}
in (
9
) results to

\|\y(t)\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon\text{ for all }t\geq t(\varepsilon).

This finishes the proof of item (ii).

Now we show item (iii). Suppose that
\lambda>-1
.
Since for
\lambda>0
the system (
1
) is not stable, it suffices to consider the case
-1<\lambda\leq 0
.
Let
\theta\in(0,1)
and
\Theta=(1,\theta,\theta^{2},\theta^{3},\dots)
. Obviously,
\Theta\in\ell^{2}
and
e^{tE}\Theta=e^{t\theta}\Theta
. Since

-1<\lambda\leq 0
if we let
\theta=-\lambda+\frac{|1+\lambda|}{2}\in(0,1)
then as
t\to+\infty
one gets

(10)

\|e^{tA}\Theta\|_{2}=e^{t\lambda}\|e^{tE}\Theta\|_{2}=e^{t|1+\lambda|/2}\|\Theta\|_{2}\to+\infty.

This implies that if
\lambda>-1
then (
1
) is not stable.
This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 1
.

Consider the system
\dot{\y}=A\y
,
\y(0)=\y_{0}\in\ell^{\infty}
, where

\ell^{\infty}=\{\y=(y_{1},y_{2},\dots)\mid\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}|y_{n}|<\infty\}.

Then
\textbf{e}=(1,1,1\dots)\in\ell^{\infty}

e^{tE}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
e^{t}
. Thus
0

3.
Null-controllability

Here we show that system (
3
) is null controllable.

We start with a standard lemma from operator theory, which will be useful below.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
L:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
be a self adjoint operator defined on a Hilbert space
(\mathcal{H},\|\cdot\|)
. Assume that
there exists
\kappa>0
such that
\|Lx\|\geq\kappa\|x\|
for all
x\in L
. Then
L
is invertible and
\|L^{-1}\|\leq\kappa^{-1}
.

To prove controllability we use Gramian operators and prove an observability inequality. For
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
define

W(\tau)=\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-sA}\cdot e^{-sA^{\ast}}ds,

where and
A^{\ast}
is the adjoint of
A
in
\ell^{2}
. The following lemma is the main technical tool

Lemma 3.2
.

For every
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
the operator
W(\tau)
is bounded, self adjoint, positive definite and invertible. Moreover, there exists
\kappa>0
such that
\|W(\tau)\y\|_{2}\geq\kappa\|\y\|_{2}
for any
\y\in\ell^{2}
.

Proof.

One can easily verify that
E^{\ast}\f=(0,\f)=(0,f_{1},f_{2},\dots)
. Then
e^{tA}e^{tA^{\ast}}=e^{2t\lambda}e^{tE}\cdot e^{tE^{\ast}}
. Further,

(11)

\displaystyle|\langle W(\tau)\y,\z\rangle|\leq\|\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-2t\lambda}e^{-tE}\cdot e^{-tE^{\ast}}\y dt\|_{2}\cdot\|\z\|_{2}\leq

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{2t(1-\lambda)}dt\cdot\|\y\|_{2}\cdot\|\z\|_{2}\leq M(\tau)\cdot\|\y\|_{2}\cdot\|\z\|_{2},

where the constant
M(\tau)
depends only on
\tau
.

Let
e_{ij}(t)
,
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
, denote an element of
e^{tE}\cdot e^{tE^{\ast}}
. For
\y,\z\in\ell^{2}
we have

(12)

\langle W(\tau)\y,\z\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-2t\lambda}e_{ij}(-t)y_{j}z_{i}dt.

By (
11
) the right hand side of (
12
) is absolutely convergent. Thus,

\langle W(\tau)\y,\z\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-2t\lambda}e_{ij}(-t)y_{j}z_{i}dt=\langle\y,W(\tau)\z\rangle.

This implies that
W(\tau)
is self adjoint for every
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
.

Notice that
e^{tE^{\ast}}
is just the transpose of
e^{tE}
. Therefore, by (
7
) for
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
we have

e_{ij}(t)=\sum_{m=|i-j|}^{\infty}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\cdot\frac{t^{m-|i-j|}}{(m-|i-j|)!},

which implies that both of the series

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e_{ij}(-t)y_{j}z_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e_{ij}(-t)y_{j}z_{i}

converge uniformly in
[0,\tau]
, hence

(13)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\langle W(\tau)\y,\z\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-2t\lambda}e_{ij}(-t)y_{j}z_{i}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{-2t\lambda}e_{ij}(-t)y_{j}z_{i}dt=\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-2t\lambda}\langle e^{-tE^{\ast}}\y,e^{-tE^{\ast}}\z\rangle dt.

The above equation immediately implies that
\langle W(\tau)\y,\y\rangle>0
for every
\y\neq 0
i.e.
W(\tau)
is positive definite. In (
13
) we have showed that we can take integration out of the scalar product
\langle W(\tau)\y,\z\rangle
. We will use this property several times below.

For every
\varepsilon\in[0,\tau]

\langle W(\tau)\y,\y\rangle\geq\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}e^{-2t\lambda}\langle e^{-tE^{\ast}}\y,e^{-tE^{\ast}}\y\rangle dt.

Now we look at the operator
e^{-tE}\cdot e^{-tE^{\ast}}
. Note that
EE^{\ast}=\id
, we have

(14)

e^{-tE}\cdot e^{-tE^{\ast}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{2n}}{(n!)^{2}}\id+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{(-t)^{n+m}}{n!m!}(E^{m-n}+{(E^{\ast})}^{m-n}).

It follows that for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
and
t\in(0,\varepsilon)
we have

e^{-tE}\cdot e^{-tE^{\ast}}=\id-t(E+E^{\ast})+o(t),

where
o(t)
is a linear operator whose
\ell^{2}
norm is
o(t)
in the usual sense. Finally,

(15)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}e^{-2t\lambda}\langle e^{-tE}\cdot e^{-tE^{\ast}}\y,\y\rangle dt=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}e^{-2t\lambda}\langle(\id-t(E+E^{\ast})+o(t))y,y\rangle dt

\displaystyle>(1-3\varepsilon)\|y\|_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}e^{-2t\lambda}dt=\frac{1-3\varepsilon}{-2\lambda}(e^{-2\lambda\varepsilon}-1)\|y\|_{2}^{2},

where we used
\langle E\y,\y\rangle\leq\|y\|_{2}^{2}
. This proves

(16)

\|W(\tau)\y\|_{2}\geq\kappa\|\y\|_{2},\text{ with }\kappa^{2}=\frac{1-3\varepsilon}{-2\lambda}(e^{-2\lambda\varepsilon}-1)>0.

Thus Lemma
3.1
is applicable and implies that
W(\tau)
is invertible for every
\tau>0
and
W^{-1}(\tau):\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}
is a bounded linear operator with the norm
\|W(\tau)^{-1}\|\leq\kappa^{-1}
, where
\kappa
is independent of
\tau
.
∎

To prove item (ii) we consider cases

\lambda=-1

Global null-controllability for

Since

Consequently, for any
\y\in\ell^{2}
holds

\langle W(\tau)\y,\y\rangle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\|e^{-tA^{\ast}}\y\|^{2}_{2}dt\geq\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-2t(1+\lambda)}\|\y\|^{2}_{2}dt=\|\y\|^{2}_{2}\cdot\frac{e^{-2(1+\lambda)\tau}-1}{-2(1+\lambda)}.

Recalling
\|W^{-1}(\tau)\|\leq\kappa^{-1}
and letting
\z(\tau)=W(\tau)^{-1}\y_{0}\in\ell^{2}
by the above inequality we have

\kappa^{-1}\|\y_{0}\|^{2}_{2}\geq\langle\y_{0},W^{-1}(\tau)\y_{0}\rangle=\langle W(\tau)\z(\tau),\z(\tau)\rangle\geq\|\z(\tau)\|^{2}_{2}\cdot\frac{e^{-2(1+\lambda)\tau}-1}{-2(1+\lambda)}.

Hence,

(20)

\|\z(\tau)\|_{2}\leq\left(\frac{-2(1+\lambda)}{\kappa(e^{-2(1+\lambda)\tau}-1)}\right)^{1/2}\|\y_{0}\|_{2}.

Since
\lambda<-1
the right hand side of the above inequality converges to
0
exponentially fast as
\tau\to+\infty
and so does
\|\z(\tau)\|_{2}
. Thus, by (
19
) there exists
\tau_{0}
such that

\int_{0}^{\tau}\|\f_{0}(t)\|^{2}_{2}dt\leq\rho^{2}\quad\text{for all}\quad\tau>\tau_{0}.

This finishes the proof of global controllability for
\lambda<-1
.

Global null-controllability for
\lambda=-1
.

This case needs a slightly different argument. Recall that that in this case the system is locally null controllable i.e. the control function defined in (
17
) remains admissible in the neighbourhood of the origin: if
\|\y_{0}\|_{2}\leq\rho\sqrt{\kappa}
, where
\kappa
is the constant defined in (
16
), we set

(21)

\f_{1}(t)=-e^{-tA^{\ast}}\cdot W^{-1}(1)\y_{0}\quad\text{for every}\quad\y_{0}\in\ell^{2}.

Then by (
19
) we get

\int_{0}^{\tau}\|\f_{1}(t)\|^{2}_{2}dt\leq\|\y_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\cdot\|W^{-1}(1)\|\leq\rho^{2},

and

\y(1)=e^{A}\y_{0}+e^{A}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-sA}\f(s)ds=0.

Further, by stability of the system (
1
) for any
\y_{0}\in\ell^{2}
there exists
\tau_{0}=\tau(\kappa,\rho,\y_{0})
such that
\|e^{tA}\y_{0}\|_{2}\leq\rho\sqrt{\kappa}
for any
t\geq\tau_{0}
. Therefore, we set

(22)

\f_{0}(t)=\begin{cases}0,\text{ if }t\leq\tau_{0},\\
\f_{1}(t),\tau_{0}\leq t\leq\tau_{0}+1.\end{cases}

One can easily check that
\f_{0}
is admissible and
\y(\tau_{0}+1)=0
for the corresponding solution of (
3
), which finishes the proof. This finishes the proof of item (ii)

Observe that to prove the item (iii) it is sufficient to obtain estimates on
\tau
satisfying

\|\y_{0}\|_{2}\cdot\|\z(\tau)\|_{2}\leq\rho^{2},

where
z(\tau)
is given by (
20
), which is equivalent to

\left(\frac{-2(1+\lambda)}{\kappa(e^{-2(1+\lambda)\tau}-1)}\right)^{1/2}\|\y_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\rho^{2},

which is satisfied if

\tau\geq\frac{\|\y_{0}\|^{4}_{2}}{\kappa\rho^{4}}\geq\frac{1}{2|\lambda+1|}\log\left(1+\frac{2|\lambda+1|}{\kappa}\frac{\|\y_{0}\|^{4}_{2}}{\rho^{4}}\right).

This completes the proof of the Theorem.
∎

4.
Discussion of the results and further questions

In this paper we addressed an infinite system of linear ODEs with a special operator
A=\lambda\id+E
on the right hand side. We obtained stability and controllability of the system when
\lambda\leq-1
. Initially, the main motivation for this choice was to construct an example whose finite dimensional projections having qualitatively different behavior than the system itself. In the proofs we used Gramian operators, which raised a natural question whether or not the constructed control functions are optimal, since in the finite dimensional setting this method is known to produce optimal control. In the setting of the current paper, when
\lambda<-1
we expect to obtain optimal control. But we were unable to find an analogue of a general result in the spirit of (for example,
[
18
, Propostion 2.]
), in the infinite dimensional setting; when
\lambda=-1
we don’t control the system until it gets closer to the origin. Therefore, we don’t expect to obtain optimal control. Notice that, in the proofs we used the special form of
A
.
It would be interesting to obtain similar results for more general system

(23)

\dot{\y}=A\y+B\f,\quad\y(0)=\y_{0},

where
A:\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}
is a bounded operator, and
B:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}
is an operator from (possible finite dimensional) subspace
\mathcal{L}
of
\ell^{2}
. The proofs suggest that if
B
is identity and the spectrum of
A
lies on the left hand side of the imaginary axes, then (
23
) is globally asymptotically stable. Invertibility of the Gramians seems also to work since it is a perturbative argument. But for the global null controllability, one needs different estimates to the inverses of the Gramians, or another approach is needed. However, for general

B
the situation is unclear, it would be nice to obtain a similar conditions
to the classical Kalman (See for example,
[
11
, Theorem 1.16]
) or
an analog of Fattorini-Hautus but in both situations, it isn’t clear what should be the exact conditions. Since for Kalman condition injectivity of an operator isn’t sufficient for invertibility, and for Fattorini-Hautus usually one assumes countable spectrum with certain properties (see for example
[
5
]
and references therein).

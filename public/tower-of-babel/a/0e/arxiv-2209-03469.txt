Nonequilibrium magnons from hot electrons in antiferromagnetic systems
Marion M. S. Barbeau, Mikhail Titov, Mikhail I. Katsnelson, Alireza Qaiumzadeh

Abstract
We describe a nonthermal magnon activation mechanism in antiferromagnetic (AFM) systems via locally equilibrated spin-unpolarized hot electrons excited by an ultrafast intense laser pulse. We employ a quantum kinetic equation that takes into account a direct electron-magnon scattering channel in either bulk AFM metal or at the interface of the AFM/normal-metal heterostructure. The mechanism is responsible for the nonequilibrium population of AFM magnon modes on a subnanosecond timescale, which are formed shortly after quasithermalization of hot electrons by Coulomb interactions. Nonequilibrium magnon populations can be additionally manipulated by applying an external magnetic field. Our work paves the way toward spin dynamics control in AFM systems via the ultrafast manipulation of out-of-equilibrium magnon excitations.



The discovery of ultrafast spin control by subpicosecond laser pulses promises new opportunities for data storage and processing
Stanciu et al. 2007
;
Kirilyuk et al. 2010
;
Lambert et al. 2014
. At the same time ultrafast magnetization dynamics involve nonequilibrium phenomena that lack comprehensive
ab initio
or model description. The field has no shortage of experimental observations: ultrafast demagnetization and magnetic switching as well as nonequilibrium magnon population excitation.

The observed phenomena have obtained qualitative interpretations based on the mechanisms of laser-induced heating
Beaurepaire et al. 1996
;
Ostler et al. 2012
, magnetic exchange engineering
Mikhaylovskiy et al. 2015
;
Mentink 2017
;
Losada et al. 2019
, light-induced fields
Kimel et al. 2007
;
Qaiumzadeh et al. 2013
;
Qaiumzadeh and Titov 2016
, etc.

Ultrafast photophysical phenomena are often successfully described within the phenomenological multitemperature model that assumes thermalized distributions of electron, magnon, phonon and other subsystems
Kirilyuk et al. 2010
. Many experiments with conducting magnets assume initial excitation of nonthermal hot electrons and their energy transfer into thermalized spin and lattice degrees of freedom
Matsubara et al. 2015
;
Shim et al. 2020
. Such energy transfer may, however, prompt nonequlibrium magnon excitations on transient timescales of electron-magnon interactions
Tveten et al. 2015
;
Brener et al. 2017
;
Beens et al. 2022
. In particular, magnons in half-metal ferromagnets may be nonthermally excited by laser-induced hot electrons via nonquasiparticle (incoherent) states
Katsnelson et al. 2008
;
Irkhin 2015
;
Brener et al. 2017
;
Fujiwara et al. 2018
.
In half-metal ferromagnets, the electrons are almost fully spin polarized; hence, magnon excitations are facilitated by some virtual scattering processes
Brener et al. 2017
.

In this Letter, we consider antiferromagnetic (AFM) systems, where electron subbands are spin degenerate.
We show how nonequilibrium magnons are nonthermally activated via a direct magnon-electron scattering channel. We assume that the strongly excited electron subsystem, by an ultrafast laser pulse, is initially quasithermalized at some hot temperature.

Despite the vast literature on different aspects of electron-magnon interactions and the diversity of correlated materials with AFM ordering, there exists only a handful of studies of AFM magnon excitation by electrons
Yu et al. 2022
;
Cheng and Zhang 2020
.

Typical resonance frequency in AFM systems is in the terahertz range. This is in contrast to ferromagnetic (FM) systems where it can hardly exceed a few gigahertz. This property has been put forward as the basis for ultrafast AFM memory and computational devices
Marti et al. 2014
. Unlike FM systems, the AFM materials are typically characterized by two circularly polarized magnon modes with opposite polarizations
Rezende et al. 2019
. Energy transfer from hot electrons may induce a nonequilibrium population of these modes on transient time-scales. We argue that such nonequilibrium magnon kinetics is generic for AFM metals
Siddiqui et al. 2020
;
Qaiumzadeh et al. 2018
and AFM-insulator/normal-metal hetero-structures.

We apply the model of Eqs. (

In order to construct a kinetic equation for magnon densities, we employ a perturbation formalism that was originally developed by Fröhlich and Taylor
Fröhlich and Taylor 1964
in the context of electron-phonon interaction.

We use the Heisenberg picture to introduce time-dependent magnon densities,

N_{\mathbf{q}}^{\alpha}(t)=\left\langle\alpha^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{q}}(t)\alpha^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{q}}(t)\right\rangle,\quad N_{\mathbf{q}}^{\beta}(t)=\left\langle\beta^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{q}}(t)\beta^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{q}}(t)\right\rangle,

(5)

and interband magnon transition probabilities,

P_{\mathbf{q}}^{\alpha\beta}(t)=\left\langle\alpha^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{q}}(t)\beta^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{q}}(t)\right\rangle,\quad P_{\mathbf{q}}^{\beta\alpha}(t)=\left\langle\beta^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{q}}(t)\alpha^{\phantom{\dagger}}_{\mathbf{q}}(t)\right\rangle,

(6)

where the angular brackets represent the averaging over the canonical ensemble at an initial moment of time. Our goal is to derive a kinetic equation on these quantities to model their time evolution.

Using nonequilibrium field theory
SM
, we can cast these equations in the following form:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}N^{\gamma}_{\mathbf{q}}(t)=

\displaystyle\left[1-\big({e^{\frac{\omega^{\gamma}_{\mathbf{q}}}{k_{\text{B}}T}}-1}\big)N_{\mathbf{q}}^{\gamma}(t)\right]\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{q}}^{\gamma\gamma}(t),

(7a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}P_{\mathbf{q}}^{\alpha\beta}(t)=

\displaystyle P_{\mathbf{q}}^{\alpha\beta}(t)\mathcal{I}^{\alpha\beta}_{\mathbf{q}}(t)-(1+P_{\mathbf{q}}^{\alpha\beta}(t))\mathcal{I}^{\beta\alpha}_{\mathbf{q}}(t),

(7b)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}P_{\mathbf{q}}^{\beta\alpha}(t)=

\displaystyle(1+P_{\mathbf{q}}^{\beta\alpha}(t))\mathcal{I}^{\alpha\beta}_{\mathbf{q}}(t)-P_{\mathbf{q}}^{\beta\alpha}(t)\mathcal{I}^{\beta\alpha}_{\mathbf{q}}(t),

(7c)

where
T
is the effective temperature of hot electrons,
k_{\text{B}}
is the Boltzmann constant,
\mathcal{I}^{\gamma{\gamma}}_{\mathbf{q}}
and
\mathcal{I}^{\gamma\bar{\gamma}}_{\mathbf{q}}
represent intra- and interband collision integrals,
\gamma=\{\alpha,\beta\}
, and
\bar{\gamma}\neq\gamma
.
Intraband collision integrals are defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\alpha\alpha}_{\mathbf{q}}=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi SI_{\textrm{sd}}^{2}}{N}\sum_{m,n,\mathbf{k}}|\mathcal{V}^{\alpha\uparrow}_{mn}|^{2}\big[1-f(\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}})\big]f(\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}}+\omega^{\alpha}_{\mathbf{q}})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\mathcal{A}^{n}_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}\downarrow}(t,\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}}+\omega^{\alpha}_{\mathbf{q}}),

(8a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\mathbf{q}}^{\beta\beta}=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi SI_{\textrm{sd}}^{2}}{N}\sum_{m,n,\mathbf{k}}|\mathcal{V}^{\beta\downarrow}_{mn}|^{2}\big[1-f(\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}})\big]f(\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}}+\omega^{\beta}_{\mathbf{q}})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\mathcal{A}^{n}_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}\uparrow}(t,\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}}+\omega^{\beta}_{\mathbf{q}}),

(8b)

while the interband collision integrals
\mathcal{I}^{\gamma\bar{\gamma}}_{\mathbf{q}}(t)
are given by,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\alpha\beta}_{\mathbf{q}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi SI_{\rm sd}^{2}}{N}\sum\limits_{m,n,\mathbf{k}}|\mathcal{V}^{\alpha\uparrow}_{mn}|^{2}\Big[f(\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}}+\omega^{\beta}_{\mathbf{q}})\mathcal{A}^{n}_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}\downarrow}(t,\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}}+\omega^{\beta}_{\mathbf{q}})

\displaystyle-f(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}-\omega^{\beta}_{\mathbf{q}})\mathcal{A}^{m}_{\mathbf{k}\uparrow}(t,\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}-\omega^{\beta}_{\mathbf{q}})\Big],

(9a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\beta\alpha}_{\mathbf{q}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi SI_{\rm sd}^{2}}{N}\sum\limits_{m,n,\mathbf{k}}|\mathcal{V}^{\beta\downarrow}_{mn}|^{2}\Big[f(\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}-\omega^{\alpha}_{\mathbf{q}})\mathcal{A}^{m}_{\mathbf{k}\downarrow}(t,\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}-\omega^{\alpha}_{\mathbf{q}})

\displaystyle-f(\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}}+\omega^{\alpha}_{\mathbf{q}})\mathcal{A}^{n}_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}\uparrow}(t,\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}}+\omega^{\alpha}_{\mathbf{q}})\Big],

(9b)

where

\mathcal{A}^{m}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}(t,\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\Gamma^{\sigma}_{m\mathbf{k}}(t,\varepsilon)/2}{(\varepsilon_{m\mathbf{k}}-\varepsilon)^{2}+\big(\Gamma^{\sigma}_{m\mathbf{k}}(t,\varepsilon)/2\big)^{2}},

(10)

is the electron spectral function. Here, the inverse quasiparticle lifetime
\Gamma^{\sigma}_{m\mathbf{k}}(t,\varepsilon)
is defined by the electron-magnon scattering in the adiabatic approximation. Its dependence on the evolution time

originates in the time dependence of magnon density.

The scattering rate

In summary, we developed quantum Boltzmann equations for nonthermal AFM magnon densities in the presence of hot electrons. We showed that one may activate and control nonequilibrium AFM magnon populations with an external magnetic field. We discussed the qualitative importance of electron scattering on magnon evolution and compared our results to magnon quasi-classic approximation. Considering electron-magnon scattering rates beyond quasi-classical approximation, one suppresses the effects of temperature but enhances those of magnetic field.
These results show that nonthermal magnon distribution can be activated by hot electrons in AFM systems.
Our results pave the way for the emerging field of AFM spintronics, AFM magnon condensation

THE COHOMOLOGICAL DIMENSIONS OF THE TERMS OF THE JOHNSON FILTRATION
Daniel Minahan

Abstract
. We prove that the k k th term of the Johnson filtration of a closed, orientable surface of genus g ≥ 2 g\geq 2 has cohomological dimension 2 ​ g − 3 2g-3 for all k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 and g ≥ 2 g\geq 2 . This answers a question of Farb and Bestvina–Bux–Margalit.



1.
Introduction

Let
S_{g}
be a closed orientable surface of genus
g
. The mapping class group
\Mod(S_{g})
is the group of isotopy classes of orientation preserving diffeomorphisms of
S_{g}
. Let
\pi_{1}^{(k)}(S_{g})
denote the
k
th term of the lower central series of
\pi_{1}(S_{g})
, where
\pi_{1}^{(0)}(S_{g})=\pi_{1}(S_{g})
. The
k
th
term of the Johnson filtration of
\Mod(S_{g})
is the kernel of the map

\Mod(S_{g})\rightarrow\Out(\pi_{1}(S_{g})/\pi_{1}^{(k)}(S_{g}))

and is denoted
\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g})
. The first term,
\Mod_{(1)}(S_{g})
, is called the
Torelli group
and is denoted
\mathcal{I}_{g}
. The second term,
\Mod_{(2)}(S_{g})
, is called the
Johnson kernel
and is denoted
\mathcal{K}_{g}
.

The
cohomological dimension
of a group
G
, denoted
\cd(G)
, is the supremum over all
n
such that there is a
G
–module
M
with
H^{n}(G;M)\neq 0
. The simplest lower bound for
\cd(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g}))
is given by the maximal
n
such that
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is a subgroup of
\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g})
. The largest known free abelian subgroup of
\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g})
for
k\geq 3
is of rank
g-1

[
Far06
]
. We have the following theorem, which answers a question of Farb
[
Far06
, Problem 5.9]
and Bestvina–Bux–Margalit
[
BBM10
, Question 1.8]
.

Theorem A
.

Let
g\geq 2
and
k\geq 3
. The cohomological dimension of
\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g})
is
2g-3
.

If
G
has torsion, the
virtual cohomological dimension
of
G
is
\cd(\Gamma)
for any
\Gamma\subseteq G
finite index and torsion free. The quantity
\vcd(G)
is indpendent of the choice of
\Gamma
by a theorem of Serre
[
Bro94
, pg 190]
. The following is the known values for
\cd
and
\vcd
of the terms of the Johnson filtration:

•

\vcd(\Mod(S_{g}))=4g-5
by the work of Harer
[
Har86
]
,

•

\cd(\mathcal{I}_{g})=3g-5
by Bestvina–Bux–Margalit
[
BBM10
, Theorem A]
, and

•

\cd(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g}))=2g-3
for
k\geq 2
.

The third result in the case that
k=2
is also due to Bestvina–Bux–Margalit
[
BBM10
, Theorem B]
.

Outline of the proof of Theorem
A
. Cohomological dimension is
monotonic
, in the sense that
H\subseteq G
implies
\cd(H)\leq\cd(G)
. Our Theorem
A
is a corollary of monotonicity of cohomological dimension, Bestvina–Bux–Margalit’s theorem that
\cd(\mathcal{K}_{g})=2g-3
as above
[
BBM10
, Theorem B]
, and the following theorem.

Theorem B
.

Let
g\geq 2
and
k\geq 3
. The group
H_{2g-3}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g});\mathbb{Z})
is infinitely generated.

Theorem
B
is a partial answer to the following question (see, e.g., Margalit
[
Mar19
, Question 5.6]
).

Question 1
.

For which choices of natural numbers
g,k,j
and commutative ring
R
is the
R
–module
H_{j}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g});R)
finitely generated?

Prior partial answers to Question
1
. Mess showed that
\mathcal{I}_{2}
is an infinitely generated free group
[
Mes92
]
, which answers Question
1
for all
j,k
and
R
when
g=2
. Johnson showed that
\mathcal{I}_{g}
is finitely generated for
g\geq 3
, which answers Question
1
for
j=k=1
and any
R
in the case that
g\geq 3

[
Joh85
]
. Church–Ershov–Putman
[
CEP21
, Theorem C]
showed that for
g\geq 2k-1
,
\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g})
is finitely generated, which answers the question for
j=1
and any
R
when
g\geq 2k-1
. In contrast, Bestvina–Bux–Margalit showed that
H_{3g-5}(\mathcal{I}_{g};\mathbb{Z})
is infinitely generated for all
g

[
BBM10
, Theorem C]
. Gaifullin showed that
H_{j}(\mathcal{I}_{g};\mathbb{Z})
is infinitely generated for
2g-3\leq j\leq 3g-5

[
Gai19a
]
, and also showed that
H_{2g-3}(\mathcal{K}_{g};\mathbb{Z})
is infinitely generated
[
Gai19b
]
.

The strategy of the proof of Theorem
B
. Our proof of Theorem
B
follows the same strategy of Bestvina–Bux–Margalit
[
BBM10
]
. Gaifullin also used a variant of this approach to prove that
H_{2g-3}(\mathcal{K}_{g};\mathbb{Z})
is infinitely generated
[
Gai19b
]
. Bestvina–Bux–Margalit
[
BBM10
]
define a complex
\mathcal{B}(S_{g})
called the complex of cycles. They consider the action of
\mathcal{K}_{g}
on
\mathcal{B}(S_{g})
and the associated equivariant homology spectral sequence
\mathbb{E}_{p,q}^{r}
(see, e.g.,
[
Bro94
, Section VII.7]
). The strategy is to apply the following inductive process for any
k\geq 3
:

(1)

Mess proved that
H_{1}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{2});\mathbb{Z})
is infinitely generated
[
Mes92
]
.

(2)

We will prove that
H_{2(g-1)-3}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1});\mathbb{Z})
infinitely generated implies that

H_{2g-3}(\Stab_{c}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g}));\mathbb{Z})

is infinitely generated for
c\subseteq S_{g}
a nonseparating simple closed curve.

(3)

Bestvina–Bux–Margalit
[
BBM10
, Proposition 5.2]
show that, for any
k\geq 2
, there is an inclusion

\mathbb{E}_{0,2g-3}^{1}\hookrightarrow H_{2g-3}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g});\mathbb{Z}).

By construction, the complex
\mathcal{B}(S_{g})
contains a vertex represented by a nonseparating simple closed curve
c\subseteq S_{g}
. By examining the spectral sequence
\mathbb{E}_{p,q}^{r}
, we can show that there is a sequence of inclusions

H_{2g-3}(\Stab_{c}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g}));\mathbb{Z})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{E}_{0,2g-3}^{1}\hookrightarrow H_{2g-3}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g});\mathbb{Z}).

By Step (2),
H_{2g-3}(\Stab_{c}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g}));\mathbb{Z})
is infinitely generated. Since the composition of injections is an injection,
H_{2g-3}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g});\mathbb{Z})
is infinitely generated as well.

The outline of the paper
. In Section
2
, we will construct a variant of the Birman exact sequence for the Johnson filtration. In Section
3
, we will prove in Proposition
3.1
that for certain vertices
c\in\mathcal{B}(S_{g})
, if Theorem
B
holds for
g^{\prime}<g
and
k\geq 3
, then
H_{2g-3}(\Stab_{v}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g});\mathbb{Z}))
is infinitely generated for every
k\geq 3
. To do this, we will apply the Leray–Serre spectral sequence to the Birman exact sequence constructed Section
2
. In Section
4
, we prove Theorem
B
by the inductive process described above. We then use Theorem
B
to prove Theorem
A
.

2.
Birman exact sequences for the Johnson filtration

Our goal in this section is to prove Proposition
2.1
below, which gives a variant of the Birman exact sequence for the Johnson filtration and verifies that this variant satisfies certain properties.

Curves on a surface
. For the remainder of this paper, a curve
c
on a surface
S
is an isotopy class of essential, simple, closed curves.

Partitioned surfaces
. Let
S=S_{g}^{b}
be an orientable compact surface of genus
g
with
b
boundary components. Putman
[
Put07
]
defined the notion of a
partitioned surface

\Sigma=(S,P)
, which is a surface
S
equipped with a partition
P
of
\pi_{0}(\partial S)
. Suppose that
S\subseteq S_{h}
, and this embedding has the property that for each connected component
S^{\prime}\in\pi_{0}(S_{h}\setminus\Int(S))
, there is some
p\in P
such that
\partial S^{\prime}=p
. Additionally, suppose that no connected component of
S_{h}\setminus\Int(S)
is a disk or annulus. The Torelli group
\mathcal{I}(\Sigma)
, alternatively notated as
\mathcal{I}(S,P)
, is the subgroup of
\Mod(S)
generated by all elements
\varphi\in\mathcal{I}_{h}
such that
\varphi
is supported on
S\subseteq S_{h}
. Putman
[
Put07
, Theorem 1.1]
showed that
\mathcal{I}(S,P)
is well–defined, i.e., independent of the choice of embedding
S\subseteq S_{h}
. Later, Church
[
Chu14
, Theorem 4.6]
showed that the Johnson filtration of
\Sigma
, denoted
\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma)
or
\Mod_{(k)}(S,P)
, is also independent of the choice of embedding
S\subseteq S_{h}
. Now, let
\Sigma=(S,P)
be a partitioned surface, and let
\Sigma^{\prime}=(S^{\prime},P^{\prime})
be another partitioned surface given by capping off one boundary component
b\in S
with a disk and forgetting the corresponding boundary component from the partition
P
. Let
\UT S^{\prime}
denote the unit tangent bundle of
S^{\prime}
, let
*\in S^{\prime}
be a basepoint, and let
\widetilde{*}\in\UT S^{\prime}
be a lift of
*
to
\UT S^{\prime}
. Putman
[
Put07
, Theorem 1.2]
showed that the Birman exact sequence
[
FM12
]

1\rightarrow\pi_{1}(\UT S^{\prime},\widetilde{*})\rightarrow\Mod(S)\rightarrow\Mod(S^{\prime})\rightarrow 1

restricts to a short exact sequence

1\rightarrow\Gamma\rightarrow\mathcal{I}(\Sigma)\rightarrow\mathcal{I}(\Sigma^{\prime})\rightarrow 1.

The subgroup
\Gamma\subseteq\pi_{1}(\UT S^{\prime},*)
is constructed as follows. If
\varphi:H\rightarrow K
is a homomorphism of groups, the
graph of
\varphi
, denoted
\Gamma(\varphi)
, is the subgroup of
H\oplus K
generated by elements of the form
(h,\varphi(h))
. There is a split short exact sequence

1\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\pi_{1}(\UT S^{\prime},*^{\prime})\rightarrow\pi(S^{\prime},*)\rightarrow 1,

and the group
\Gamma
in Putman’s Birman exact sequence for the Torelli group is
\Gamma(\kappa)
for some function
\kappa:F_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
, where
F_{1}\subseteq\pi_{1}(S^{\prime},*)
. We will extend the Birman exact sequence for the Torelli group to
\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma)
in the following special case.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
g\geq 3
. Let
\Sigma_{1}=(S_{g-1}^{1},\pi_{0}(\partial S_{g-1}^{1}))
and
\Sigma_{2}=(S_{g-1}^{2},\pi_{0}(\partial S_{g-1}^{2}))
. Choose a boundary component
p\subseteq\partial S_{g-1}^{2}
, and let
\rho_{1}:\mathcal{I}(\Sigma_{2})\rightarrow\mathcal{I}(\Sigma_{1})
be the map in Putman’s Birman exact sequence for the Torelli group. Choose a basepoint
*\in S_{g-1}^{1}
and a lift of this basepoint to
\widetilde{*}\in\UT S_{g-1}^{1}
. Let
\kappa:\pi_{1}(S_{g-1}^{1},*)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
be a map such that there is a subgroup
F_{1}\subseteq\pi_{1}(S_{g-1}^{1},*)
with
\Gamma(\kappa|_{F_{1}})\subseteq\pi_{1}(\UT S_{g-1}^{1},\widetilde{*})
equal to
\ker(\rho_{1})
. For any
k\geq 3
, there a subgroup
F_{k}\subseteq\left[\pi_{1}(S_{g-1}^{1},*),\pi_{1}(S_{g-1}^{1},*)\right]
such that there is a short exact sequence

1\rightarrow\Gamma(\kappa|_{F_{k}})\rightarrow\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{2})\xrightarrow{\rho_{k}}\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{1})\rightarrow 1,

given by restricting the Birman exact sequence. Furthermore, for any
k\geq 3
, the group
F_{k}
has the following properties:

(1)

the quotient group
F_{k}/F_{k+1}
is nontrivial, finitely generated and free abelian,

(2)

the induced action of
\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1,1})
on
F_{k}/F_{k+1}
is trivial, and

(3)

the quotient group
\pi_{1}(S_{g-1}^{1},*)/F_{k}
is solvable and non–cyclic.

There are several parts to the proof of Proposition
2.1
. We show in Lemma
2.2
that the map
\rho_{k}
given by restricting
\rho
to
\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{2})
has
\im(\rho_{k})=\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{1})
. We then use the higher Johnson homomorphisms of Morita to show that the group
F_{k}
defined in the statement Proposition
2.1
has properties (1)–(3) given in Proposition
2.1
. The relationship between
F_{k}
and the higher Johnson homomorphisms is described in Lemma
2.3
. Properties (1) and (2) are verified in Lemma
2.4
, and property (3) is verified in the proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
g\geq 3
and
k\geq 2
. Let
\Sigma_{1}=(S_{g-1}^{1},\pi_{0}(\partial S_{g-1}^{1}))
and
\Sigma_{2}=(S_{g-1}^{2},\pi_{0}(\partial S_{g-1}^{2}))
. Choose a boundary component
p\subseteq\partial S_{g-1}^{2}
. Let
\rho:\Mod(S_{g-1}^{2})\rightarrow\Mod(S_{g-1}^{1})
be the map in the Birman exact sequence given by capping off
p
with a disk. Let
\rho_{k}
denote the restriction of
\Mod(S_{g-1}^{2})
to
\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{2})
. Then
\im(\rho_{k})=\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{1})\subseteq\Mod(S_{g-1}^{1})
.

Proof.

Let
\iota:S_{g-1}^{1}\rightarrow S_{g-1}^{2}
be an embedding. Let
\iota_{*}:\Mod(S_{g-1}^{1})\rightarrow\Mod(S_{g-1}^{2})
denote the map given by extending
\varphi\in\Mod(S_{g-1}^{1})
by the identity map on
S_{g-1}^{2}\ssearrow\iota(S_{g-1}^{1})
. The map
\iota_{*}
splits
\rho
from the definition of the Birman exact sequence (see, e.g.
[
FM12
, Section 4.2]
). Furthermore, Church’s work on the Johnson filtration
[
Chu14
, Theorem 4.6]
says that
\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{1})=\iota_{*}^{-1}(\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{2}))
. Since
\iota_{*}
splits
\rho
, we therefore have
\im(\rho_{k})=\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{2})
.
∎

We now study the properties of the groups
F_{k}=\ker(\rho_{k})
.

Higher Johnson homomorphisms
. Let
h\geq g
. For each
j\geq 1
, there are maps
\tau_{j,h}:\Mod_{(j)}(S_{h})\rightarrow A_{j,h}
called the
higher Johnson homomorphisms
, such that:

•

the group
A_{j,h}
is finitely generated and free abelian group, and

•

\ker(\tau_{j,h})=\Mod_{(j+1)}(S_{h})
.

For
j=1
these maps were constructed by Johnson
[
Joh80
]
, and for
j\geq 2
these were constructed by Morita
[
Mor93

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

By Lemma

. By Lemma

\pi_{1}(S_{g-1}^{1},*)/F_{k}

\pi_{1}(S_{g-1}^{1},*)/F_{k}
is non–cyclic follows the fact that
\pi_{1}(S_{g-1}^{1},*)/F_{1}\cong H_{1}(S_{g-1}^{1};\mathbb{Z})

\pi_{1}(S_{g-1}^{1},*)/F_{k}\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}(S_{g-1}^{1},*)/F_{1})\cong H_{1}(S_{g-1}^{1};\mathbb{Z})
, so
\pi_{1}(S_{g-1}^{1},*)/F_{k}
cannot be cyclic.
∎

3.
The homology of curve stabilizers in the Johnson filtration

The goal of this section is to prove Proposition
3.1
below. This i part s the main part of the inductive step of the proof of Theorem
B
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
g\geq 3
and
k\geq 3
. Suppose that
H_{2g-5}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1});\mathbb{Z})
is infinitely generated. Let
c\subseteq S_{g}
be a nonseparating simple closed curve. Then
H_{2g-3}(\Stab_{c}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g}));\mathbb{Z})
is an infinitely generated abelian group.

The proof of Proposition
3.1
requires three ingredients:

(1)

Lemma
3.2
, a general fact about the homology of a group extension,

(2)

Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.4
, which are general facts about the homology of infinite covers of surfaces, and

(3)

Lemma
3.5
, which says that
H_{2g-5}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1});\mathbb{Z})
infinitely generated implies

H_{2g-4}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1,1});\mathbb{Z})
infinitely generated.

Lemma
3.5
follows by applying Lemma
3.2
, Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.4
to a variant of the Birman exact sequence given by Akin
[
Aki17
]
. Proposition
3.1
will follow by applying the same argument structure to the short exact sequence constructed in Section
2
.

Homology of group extensions
. If
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is a symplectic form on a free abelian group
V
, we say that
v\in V
is degenerate if
\langle v,\cdot\rangle:V\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is the zero map. We say that a form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
V
is degenerate if some element of
V
is degenerate, and otherwise that
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is nondegenerate. We will say that
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
is a
degenerate subspace
if every
v\in V^{\prime}
is degenerate, and denote by
V^{\degen}
the subspace spanned by all degenerate elements of
V
. The following lemma is a generalization of a result of Bestvina–Bux–Margalit
[
BBM10
, Lemma 7.7]
, and is ingredient one in the proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
F
be a non–cyclic free group and let

1\rightarrow F\rightarrow G\rightarrow Q\rightarrow 1

be a short exact sequence of groups. Equip
H_{1}(F;\mathbb{Z})
with the
Q
–action induced by the conjugation action of
G
on
F
. Let
V\subseteq H_{1}(F;\mathbb{Z})
be a
Q
–invariant subgroup such that
H_{1}(F;\mathbb{Z})/V
is torsion free. Suppose that:

(1)

H_{1}(F;\mathbb{Z})
admits a nondegenerate
Q
–invariant symplectic form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
,

(2)

the restriction of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
to
V
is nondegenerate,

(3)

the quotient
H_{1}(F;\mathbb{Z})/V
is a trivial
Q
–module, and

(4)

H_{\cd(Q)}(Q;\mathbb{Z})
is infinitely generated.

Then
H_{\cd(Q)+1}(G;\mathbb{Z})
is infinitely generated.

Proof.

We begin with the following claim.

Claim
. There is an isomorphism
H_{\cd(Q)+1}(G;\mathbb{Z})\cong H_{\cd(Q)}(Q;H_{1}(F;\mathbb{Z}))
.

Proof of claim
. The cohomological dimension of
F
is 1 since
F
is free. Consider the Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence
\mathbb{E}_{\cd(Q),1}^{2}

[
Bro94
, Section VII.6]
corresponding to the short exact sequence

1\rightarrow F\rightarrow G\rightarrow Q\rightarrow 1.

This is the spectral sequence with
\mathbb{E}_{p,q}^{2}=H_{p}(Q;H_{q}(F;\mathbb{Z}))
that converges to
H_{p+q}(G;\mathbb{Z})
. We have
\mathbb{E}_{p,q}^{r}=0
for all
p+q>\cd(Q)+1
and
r\geq 2
, since either
p>\cd(Q)
or
q>\cd(F)=1
. Thus there are no non–trivial differentials into or out of
\mathbb{E}_{\cd(Q),1}^{2}
and therefore
\mathbb{E}_{\cd(Q),1}^{2}\cong\mathbb{E}_{\cd(Q),1}^{\infty}
. By the definition of the Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence,
\mathbb{E}_{\cd(Q),1}^{2}=H_{\cd(Q)}(Q;H_{1}(F;\mathbb{Z}))
, so the claim holds.

By the claim it suffices to show that
H_{\cd(Q)}(Q;H_{1}(F;\mathbb{Z}))
is infinitely generated. By hypothesis (2), the subspace
V^{\perp}\subseteq H_{1}(F;\mathbb{Z})
is isomorphic to
H_{1}(F;\mathbb{Z})/V
as a
Q
–module. But the form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is
Q
–invariant by hypothesis (1), so
V\oplus V^{\perp}
is a decomposition of
H_{1}(F;\mathbb{Z})
into
Q
–modules. Therefore the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow V\rightarrow H_{1}(F;\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H_{1}(F;\mathbb{Z})/V\rightarrow 0

splits as a sequence of
Q
–modules, and thus
H_{\cd(Q)}(Q;H_{1}(F;\mathbb{Z}))
splits as a direct sum

H_{\cd(Q)}(Q;V)\oplus H_{\cd(Q)}(Q;H_{1}(F;\mathbb{Z})/V).

By hypothesis (3), the action of
Q
on
H_{1}(F;\mathbb{Z})/V
is trivial. Therefore there is an isomorphism

H_{\cd(Q)}(Q;H_{1}(F;\mathbb{Z})/V)\cong H_{\cd(Q)}(Q;\mathbb{Z})\otimes H_{1}(F;\mathbb{Z})/V.

Since
H_{\cd(Q)}(Q;\mathbb{Z})
is infinitely generated by hypothesis,
H_{\cd(Q)+1}(G;\mathbb{Z})
contains an infinitely generated free abelian subgroup and thus is infinitely generated as well.
∎

Homology of covers
. We now discuss the second ingredient of the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
S=S_{g}^{b}
with
b\in\{0,1\}
. Let
\Gamma\trianglelefteq\pi_{1}(S)
be an infinite index normal subgroup contained in
\pi_{1}^{(1)}(S)
. Suppose that
\pi_{1}(S)/\Gamma
is solvable and non–cyclic. Let
S_{\Gamma}
be the cover of
S
corresponding to
\Gamma
. Suppose that, if
\delta
is a loop surrounding a boundary component of
S
, then
\delta\not\in\Gamma
. Then the algebraic intersection form on
H_{1}(S_{\Gamma};\mathbb{Z})
is non–degenerate.

Proof.

We use the fact that solvable, non–cyclic groups are one–ended (see, e.g., Löh
[
L1̈7
, Theorem 8.2.14]
). Choose a basepoint
*_{\Gamma}\in S_{\Gamma}
and a finite set of generators
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\pi_{1}(S)
. Let
G\subseteq S_{\Gamma}
be an embedding of the Cayley graph of
\pi_{1}(S)/\Gamma
containing
*_{\Gamma}
such that each edge of
G
is a lift of a loop
\gamma\in\mathcal{F}
. Since
S
is compact, basic covering space theory tells us that
S_{\Gamma}
is quasi–isometric to
G
. Therefore
S_{\Gamma}
has one end, so if
\delta\subseteq\pi_{1}(S)
is a separating simple closed curve, at least one connected component of
S\ssearrow\delta
has compact closure in
S
. But since no loop surrounding a boundary component is contained in
\Gamma
, the surface
S_{\Gamma}
has no compact boundary components. Therefore
\delta
is the boundary of a compact subsurface of
S_{\Gamma}
given by the closure in
S_{\Gamma}
of the finite–type connected component of
S_{\Gamma}\setminus\delta
. In particular, this implies that any class in
H_{1}(S_{\Gamma};\mathbb{Z})
represented by a separating curve is the trivial class, so any nonzero class
v\in H_{1}(S_{\Gamma};\mathbb{Z})
is a multiple of a class represented by a nonseparating curve
a
. Since
a
is nonseparating, there is a curve
b\subseteq S_{\Gamma}
with
\left|b\cap a\right|=1
. Therefore the class
v
has nonzero algebraic intersection with the class
[b]
, so the algebraic intersection form on
H_{1}(S_{\Gamma};\mathbb{Z})
is nondegenerate, as desired.
∎

We need one more fact about the homology of covers.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
S=S_{g}^{b}
with
b\in\{0,1\}
. Let
\Gamma\trianglelefteq\pi_{1}(S)
be an infinite index normal subgroup such that
\pi_{1}(S)/\Gamma
is solvable and non–cyclic. The subgroup of
H_{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})
spanned by the
\pi_{1}(S)
–orbit of any nonzero
v\in H_{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})
is infinitely generated.

Proof.

Let
S_{\Gamma}\rightarrow S
be the regular cover corresponding to the subgroup
\Gamma\subseteq\pi_{1}(S)
. Since
\Gamma
has infinite index in
\pi_{1}(S_{g})
, the cover
S_{\Gamma}\rightarrow S
is infinite-sheeted. By way of contradiction, suppose that there is a nonzero
v\in H_{1}(S_{\Gamma};\mathbb{Z})
such that the span of the orbit
\pi_{1}(S)v
is finitely generated. Since
\pi_{1}(S)v
is finitely generated if and only if the span of
\pi_{1}(S)(mv)
is finitely generated for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, we may assume that
v
is primitive.

Let
\delta\subseteq S_{\Gamma}
be a simple closed curve representing
v
. Such a
\delta
exists since
v
is primitive. By Lemma
3.3
,
H_{1}(S_{\Gamma};\mathbb{Z})
is nondegenerate, so there is another simple closed curve
\varepsilon\subseteq S_{\Gamma}
such that
\left|\delta\cap\varepsilon\right|=1
. Let
T\subseteq S_{\Gamma}
be an embedded copy of
S_{1}^{1}
that contains
\delta
and
\varepsilon
. Since
T
is compact and the action of
\pi_{1}(S_{g})/\Gamma
on
S_{\Gamma}
is properly discontinuous, there are infinitely many disjoint
\pi_{1}(S_{g})/\Gamma
–translates of
T
. Hence there are infinitely many elements in the orbit
(\pi_{1}(S)/\Gamma)\delta
that are all supported on disjoint embedded copies
S_{1}^{1}\subseteq S_{\Gamma}
. Therefore there are infinitely many classes in the orbit
\pi_{1}(S)/\Gamma[v]
that are pairwise orthogonal in
H_{1}(S_{\Gamma};\mathbb{Z})
, so the lemma holds.
∎

We are almost ready to prove Proposition
3.1
. Let
S_{g,n}
denote an orientable surface of genus
g
with
n
punctures. If
n=1
and
*\in S_{g,1}
is a basepoint, we define
\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1,1})
to be the subgroup of
\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1,1})
given by the kernel of the natural map

\Mod(S_{g-1,1})\rightarrow\Out(\pi_{1}(S_{g-1,1},*)/\pi_{1}^{(k)}(S_{g-1,1}^{*})).

Akin
[
Aki17
]
proved that there is a short exact sequence

1\rightarrow\pi_{1}^{(k-1)}(S_{g-1})\rightarrow\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1,1})\rightarrow\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1})\rightarrow 1.

which we call the
Akin–Birman exact sequence
. We will apply Lemma
3.2
to Akin’s sequence to prove the following.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
g\geq 3
,
k\geq 2
. Suppose that
H_{2g-5}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1});\mathbb{Z})
is infinitely generated. Then
H_{2g-4}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1,1};\mathbb{Z}))
is infinitely generated.

Proof.

We will show that the Akin–Birman exact sequence satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma
3.2
. Let
V\subseteq H_{1}(\pi_{1}^{(k-1)}(S_{g-1});\mathbb{Z})
be the image of the map

H_{1}(\pi_{1}^{(k)}(S_{g-1});\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H_{1}(\pi_{1}^{(k-1)}(S_{g-1});\mathbb{Z}).

Since
\pi_{1}^{(k)}(S_{g-1})
is a characteristic subgroup, the subgroup
V
is
\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1})
–invariant. We will verify each hypothesis of Lemma
3.2
in turn.

(1)

H_{1}(\pi_{1}^{(k-1)}(S_{g-1});\mathbb{Z})
admits a nondegenerate
\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1})
–invariant symplectic form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.

Let
\Gamma=\pi_{1}^{(k-1)}(S_{g-1})
. Let
S_{\Gamma}
denote the cover of
S_{g-1}
with
\pi_{1}(S_{\Gamma})\cong\Gamma
. There is a canonical isomorphism
H_{1}(S_{\Gamma};\mathbb{Z})\cong H_{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})
induced by the canonical map
\Gamma\rightarrow H_{1}(S_{\Gamma};\mathbb{Z})
. The symplectic form on
H_{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})
will be the pushforward of the algebraic intersection form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
H_{1}(S_{\Gamma};\mathbb{Z})
under this isomorphism. We will show that the
\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1})
–action on
H_{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})
given by the
\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g,1})
–conjugation on
\Gamma
respects this symplectic form.

Choose a basepoint
b\in S_{g-1}
and a lift of the basepoint
b
to
b_{\Gamma}\in S_{\Gamma}
. The Birman exact sequence coupled with the Dehn–Nielsen–Baer theorem gives an identification

\Mod(S_{g-1,1})\cong\Aut(\pi_{1}(S_{g})).

The subgroup
\Gamma
is characteristic, so there is a map

\Aut(\pi_{1}(S_{g-1}))\rightarrow\Aut(\Gamma).

By composing with the map
\Aut(\Gamma)\rightarrow\Out(\Gamma)
, there is a natural map

\Aut(\pi_{1}(S_{g-1}))\rightarrow\Out(\Gamma).

Since
\Gamma
is characteristic, the choice of basepoints
b
and
b_{\Gamma}
coupled with the lifting criterion for covering spaces yields a map

\Mod(S_{g-1,1})\rightarrow\Mod(S_{\Gamma}).

Hence the algebraic intersection form on
H_{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})
is a
\Mod(S_{g-1,1})
–invariant symplectic form, and by restriction
\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1,1})
–invariant. By the Akin–Birman exact sequence
[
Aki17
]
, the map

\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1,1})\rightarrow\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1})

is surjective. Hence for any
\varphi\in\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1})
, there is a lift
\overline{\varphi}\in\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1,1})
. But if
\widehat{\varphi}
is another such lift, the Akin–Birman exact sequence says that
\overline{\varphi}^{-1}\widehat{\varphi}\in\pi_{1}^{(k)}(S_{g-1})=\Gamma
. Therefore the composition
\overline{\varphi}^{-1}\widehat{\varphi}
acts trivially on
H_{1}(S_{\Gamma};\mathbb{Z})
since
H_{1}(S_{\Gamma};\mathbb{Z})\cong H_{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})
, and so
\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1})
acts in a well–defined way on
H_{1}(S_{\Gamma};\mathbb{Z})
and respects the nondegenerate form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
H_{1}(S_{\Gamma};\mathbb{Z})
. Thus the
\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1})
–action on
H_{1}(\Gamma;\mathbb{Z})
respects the pushforward of the
H_{1}(S_{\Gamma};\mathbb{Z})
–intersection form. The nondegeneracy of the form follows from Lemma
3.3
, so hypothesis (1) holds.

(2)

The restriction of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
to
V
is nondegenerate

Suppose by way of contradiction that there is a non–zero
v\in V
such that the linear map
\langle v,\cdot\rangle:H_{1}(\pi_{1}^{(k-1)}(S_{g-1});\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is the zero map. By Lemma
3.4
, the
\pi_{1}(S_{g-1})
–orbit of
v
in
H_{1}(\pi_{1}^{(k-1)}(S_{g-1});\mathbb{Z})
is infinitely generated. Since
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is
\pi_{1}(S_{g-1,1})
–invariant, any
\gamma v
is degenerate for
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(S_{g-1,1})
. Now, choose
w\in H_{1}(\pi_{1}^{(k-1)}(S_{g-1});\mathbb{Z})
such that
\langle v,w\rangle=1
. By Lemma
3.4
, the span of the
\pi_{1}(S_{g-1})
orbit of
w
is infinitely generated. But
\langle\gamma w,\gamma v\rangle\neq 0
for any
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(S_{g-1,1})
. Since
\gamma v
is degenerate in
V
for any
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(S_{g-1,1})
, it must be the case that
\Span(\{\gamma w:\gamma\in\pi_{1}(S_{g-1})\})
embeds into
H_{1}(\pi_{1}^{(k-1)}(S_{g-1};\mathbb{Z}))/V
. But this latter space is finitely generated, which is a contradiction.

(3)

The quotient
H_{1}(\pi_{1}^{(k-1)};\mathbb{Z})/V
is a trivial
\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1})
–module.

Since
\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1})
acts trivially on
\pi_{1}(S_{g-1})/\pi_{1}^{(k)}(S_{g-1})
by definition,
\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1})
acts trivially on
H_{1}(\pi_{1}^{(k-1)}(S_{g-1});\mathbb{Z})/V
as well.

(4)

H_{\cd(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1}))}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1});\mathbb{Z})
is infinitely generated.

By hypothesis,
H_{2g-5}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1});\mathbb{Z})
is infinitely generated. Bestvina–Bux–Margalit
[
BBM10
]
showed that
\cd(\Mod_{(2)}(S_{g-1}))=2(g-1)-3=2g-5
. Hence

\cd(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1})\leq\cd(\Mod_{(2)}(S_{g-1}))\leq 2g-5.

Therefore if
H_{2g-5}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1};\mathbb{Z}))\neq 0
, we have
\cd(\Mod_{(k)})=2g-5
, so hypothesis (4) of Lemma
3.2
holds.

Hence the hypotheses of Lemma
3.2
hold for the Akin–Birman sequence, so
H_{2g-4}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1,1});\mathbb{Z})
is infinitely generated.
∎

We are now almost ready to prove the main result of the section. Before doing so, we will need one more auxiliary lemma which relates the slight variations of
\Mod_{(k)}
that we have used throughout the paper.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
g\geq 3
and
k\geq 3
. Let
\Sigma_{1}=(S_{g-1}^{1},\{\{b_{0}\}\})
and
\Sigma_{2}=(S_{g-1}^{2},\{\{b_{0},b_{1}\}\})
. Then the following hold:

(1)

\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1,1})\cong\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{1})
, and

(2)

if
c\subseteq S_{g}
is a nonseparating simple closed curve
\Stab_{c}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g}))=\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{2})
.

Proof.

We prove each of these in turn.

Part (1)
. Church
[
Chu14
, Definition 4.1]
defines
\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{1})
to be the kernel of the action of
\Mod(S_{g-1}^{1})
on the group
\pi_{1}(S_{g-1}^{1},*)/\pi_{1}^{(k)}(S_{g-1}^{1},*)
, where
*
is a basepoint contained in
\partial S_{g-1}^{1}
. Therefore there is a natural map

\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{1})\rightarrow\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1,1})

that is surjective and has kernel contained in
\langle T_{\delta}\rangle
, where
T_{\delta}
is the twist around the boundary component of
S_{g-1}^{1}
. But no power of
T_{\delta}
is contained in
\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{1})
for
k\geq 3
, so the map
\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{1})\rightarrow\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1,1})
is an isomorphism.

Part (2)
. Let
f\in\Mod(S_{g-1}^{2})
denote the bounding pair map corresponding to the bounding pair consisting of the two boundary components of
S_{g-1}^{2}
. A result of Birman–Lubotzky–McCarthy
[
BLM83
, Lemma 2.1]
says that there is a short exact sequence

1\rightarrow\langle f\rangle\rightarrow\Mod(S_{g-1}^{2})\rightarrow\Stab_{c}(\Mod(S_{g}))\rightarrow 1.

The latter map
\Mod(S_{g-1}^{2})\rightarrow\Stab_{c}(\Mod(S_{g}))
is induced by the embedding
S_{g-1}^{2}\rightarrow S_{g}
such that the complement of the image of the embedding contains
c
. In particular, the work of Church
[
Chu14
, Theorem 4.6]
says that this short exact sequence restricts to a short exact sequence

1\rightarrow F\rightarrow\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{2})\rightarrow\Stab_{c}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g}))\rightarrow 1

for
F\subseteq\langle f\rangle
. But then no power of
f
is contained in
\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{2})
for
k\geq 3
, so
F=\{1\}
and hence
\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{2})\cong\Stab_{c}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g}))
.
∎

Recall that our goal is to show that
H_{2g-5}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1};\mathbb{Z}))
is infinitely generated, then the group
H_{2g-3}(\Stab_{c}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g}));\mathbb{Z}))
is infinitely generated as well, for
c
a non–separating simple closed curve.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Since
k\geq 3
, Lemma
3.6
says that we have isomorphisms
\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{1})\cong\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1,1})
and
\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{2})\cong\Stab_{c}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g}))
. Hence, it suffices to show that if the group
H_{2g-4}(\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{1});\mathbb{Z})
is infinitely generated, then
H_{2g-3}(\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{2});\mathbb{Z})
infinitely generated. We will apply Lemma
3.2
to the short exact sequence from Proposition
2.1
. We take
V\subseteq H_{1}(F_{k};\mathbb{Z})
to be the image of
F_{k+1}
in
H_{1}(F_{k};\mathbb{Z})
. We verify each hypothesis for Lemma
3.2
in turn.

(1)

H_{1}(F_{k};\mathbb{Z})
admits a nondegenerate
\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{1})
–invariant symplectic form

This form is the algebraic intersection form on the cover of
S_{F_{k}}\rightarrow S_{g-1}^{1}
corresponding to
F_{k}
. The group
\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{1})
acts on
H_{1}(S_{F_{k}};\mathbb{Z})
as a consequence of Lemma
2.5
. This action respects the algebraic intersection form on
H_{1}(S_{F_{k}};\mathbb{Z})
, since the action of
\varphi\in\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{1})
on
H_{1}(S_{F_{k}};\mathbb{Z})
is the pushforward of a homeomorphism
S_{F_{k}}\rightarrow S_{F_{k}}
. The group
\pi_{1}(S_{g-1}^{1})/F_{k}
is solvable and non–cyclic by property (3) of Lemma
2.1
. Furthermore, if
\delta
is the loop around the boundary component of
S_{g-1}^{1}
, then
\delta\not\in F_{k}
for
k\geq 3
, since the push along
\delta
induces a Dehn twist along a separating curve. Therefore the algebraic intersection form on
H_{1}(F_{k};\mathbb{Z})
is non–degenerate by Lemma
3.3
.

(2)

The restriction of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
to
V
is nondegenerate

The form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is non–degenerate when restricted to
V=\im(F_{k+1}\rightarrow H_{1}(F_{k};\mathbb{Z}))
. In particular, Proposition
2.1
says that
H_{1}(F_{k};\mathbb{Z})/V
is nontrivial, finitely generated, and free abelian. Then Lemma
3.4
of says that the
\pi_{1}(S_{g-1}^{1})
orbit of any nontrivial element in
H_{1}(F_{k};\mathbb{Z})
spans an infinitely generated subgroup. The argument is complete by the same argument as step (2) of Lemma
3.5
.

(3)

The quotient
H_{1}(F_{k};\mathbb{Z})/V
is a trivial
\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{2})
–module

This is property (2) of
F_{k}
from Proposition
2.1
.

(4)

The group
H_{\cd(\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{1}))}(\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{1});\mathbb{Z})
is infinitely generated.

By monotonicity and the fact that
\cd(\Mod_{(2)}(S_{g-1}))=2g-3

[
BBM10
, Theorem B]
, we have
\cd(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1})\leq 2g-3
. By applying the sub–additivity of cohomological dimension on short exact sequences
[
Bro94
, Section VIII.2]
to the Akin–Birman exact sequence, we have
\cd(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1,1}))\leq 2g-4
. Since
H_{2g-4}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1,1});\mathbb{Z})
is non–zero by Lemma
3.5
, we must have

\cd(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1,1}))=2g-4.

Then Lemma
3.5
says that
H_{2g-4}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g-1,1}))
is infinitely generated, so the hypothesis is satisfied.

Therefore
H_{2g-3}(\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{2});\mathbb{Z})
is infinite dimensional by Lemma
3.2
. Since
\Mod_{(k)}(\Sigma_{2})
is isomorphic to
\Stab_{c}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g}))
by Lemma
3.6
, the proof is complete.
∎

B
. We begin by briefly discussing the complex of cycles,
\mathcal{B}(S_{g})
, a cell complex defined by Bestvina–Bux–Margalit
[
BBM10
, Section 2]
. The following properties of
\mathcal{B}(S_{g})
are the relevant properties for the purposes of this paper:

(a)

\mathcal{B}(S_{g})
is contractible
[
BBM10
, Theorem E]
,

(b)

there is a vertex
v\in\mathcal{B}(S_{g})
represented by a nonseparating simple closed curve
c\subseteq S_{g}
, and

(c)

\mathcal{B}(S_{g})
admits a rotation–free
\mathcal{I}_{g}
–action
[
Iva91
, Corollary 1.8]
.

As discussed in the introduction, we will adopt Bestvina–Bux–Margalit’s strategy for their proof that
H_{3g-5}(\mathcal{I}_{g};\mathbb{Z})
is infinitely generated
[
BBM10
, Section 8]
. Specifically, we will consider the action of
\mathcal{I}_{g}
on the complex
\mathcal{B}(S_{g})
. We will apply the equivariant homology spectral sequence
[
Bro94
, Section VII.7]
. We will use Proposition
3.1
and the second property of
\mathcal{B}(S_{g})
above to show that the entry
\mathbb{E}_{0,2g-3}^{\infty}
is infinitely generated. We begin with the following result.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
S=S_{g}
with
g\geq 3
, and let
k\geq 3
. Let
c\subseteq S_{g}
be a nonseparating simple closed curve. The pushforward map

H_{2g-3}(\Stab_{c}\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g});\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H_{2g-3}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g});\mathbb{Z})

is an injection.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{E}_{*,*}^{*}
be the equivariant homology spectral sequence given by the action of
\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g})
on
\mathcal{B}(S_{g})
as discussed in the introduction. For each
0\leq k\leq 2g-3
, let
\Sigma_{k}
be a set of representatives in
\mathcal{B}(S_{g})
of the
k
–cells in the quotient
\mathcal{B}(S_{g})/\mathcal{I}_{g}
. By property
(c)
of
\mathcal{B}(S_{g})
,
\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g})
acts on
\mathcal{B}(S_{g})
without rotations. Hence there is a decomposition

\mathbb{E}_{p,q}^{1}\cong\bigoplus_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{p}}H_{q}(\Stab_{\sigma}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g})).

By property
(b)
of
\mathcal{B}(S_{g})
, there is a vertex
v
of
\mathcal{B}(S_{g})
represented by a simple closed curve
c
, there is an injection

H_{2g-3}(\Stab_{c}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g}));\mathbb{Z})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{E}_{0,2g-3}^{1}.

Bestvina–Bux–Margalit prove that for any
j
–cell
\sigma\subseteq\mathcal{B}(S)

[
BBM10
, Proposition 6.2]
, we have

\cd(\Stab_{\sigma}\Mod_{(2)}(S_{g}))\leq 2g-3-j.

Since
H\subseteq G
implies
\cd(H)\subseteq\cd(G)

[
Bro94
, Section VIII.2]
, we have

\cd(\Stab_{\sigma}\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g}))\leq 2g-3-j.

for any
j
–cell
\sigma\subseteq\mathcal{B}(S)
. Hence the non–zero entries of the first page of the spectral sequence
\mathbb{E}_{p,q}^{r}
are as in Figure

We are now ready to prove Theorem
B
.

Proof of Theorem
B
.

Fix a
k\geq 3
. We will prove the theorem by induction on
g
.

Base case:
g=2
. As mentioned in the introduction, Mess showed that
\Mod_{(1)}(S_{2})
is an infinitely generated free group
[
Mes92
]
. Since the
k
th term of the lower central series
\Mod_{(1)}^{(k)}(S_{2})
is contained in
\Mod_{(k)}(S_{2})
for every
k\geq 2
,
\Mod_{(k)}(S_{2})
is an infinitely generated free group as well. Hence
H_{1}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{2});\mathbb{Z})
is infinitely generated.

Inductive step
. Suppose that the theorem holds for all
2\leq g^{\prime}<g
. Let
c
be a nonseparating simple closed curve on
S_{g}
. By Proposition
3.1
and the inductive hypothesis,
H_{2g-3}(\Stab_{c}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g}));\mathbb{Z})
is infinitely generated. Then by Lemma
4.1
,
H_{2g-3}(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g});\mathbb{Z}))
is infinitely generated as well, so the proof is complete.
∎

We now prove the main result of the paper.

Proof of Theorem
A
.

By Theorem
B
and the universal coefficient theorem,

\cd(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g}))\geq 2g-3.

Bestvina–Bux–Margalit showed that
\cd(\Mod_{(2)}(S_{g}))=2g-3

[
BBM10
, Theorem B]
. By the monotonicity of cohomological dimension, we have

\cd(\Mod_{(2)}(S_{g}))\geq\cd(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g}))

for
k\geq 2
. Hence the equality
\cd(\Mod_{(k)}(S_{g}))=2g-3
holds.
∎

Activity estimation via distributed measurements in an orientation sensitive neural fields model of the visual cortex Thanks: This work has been partially supported by the ANR-20-CE48-0003.
Adel Malik Annabi, Jean-Baptiste Pomet, Dario Prandi, Ludovic Sacchelli

Abstract
This paper investigates the online estimation of neural activity within the primary visual cortex (V1) in the framework of observability theory. We focus on a low-dimensional neural fields modeling hypercolumnar activity to describe activity in V1. We utilize the average cortical activity over V1 as measurement. Our contributions include detailing the model’s observability singularities and developing a hybrid high-gain observer that achieves, under specific excitation conditions, practical convergence while maintaining asymptotic convergence in cases of biological relevance. The study emphasizes the intrinsic link between the model’s non-linear nature and its observability. We also present numerical experiments highlighting the different properties of the observer.

To achieve our state estimation objective,

From the standpoint of observability, the considered model introduces distinct challenges as a non-linear system.

We propose an hybrid observer design which accounts for these difficulties.
This approach allows for practical state estimation in regions where observability is hindered, and guarantees tunable exponential asymptotic convergence for cases of biological relevance.

The application to neural fields model of observability theory is quite recent. Up to our knowledge, the only study in this direction is

In that paper, the authors consider two interconnected cortical zones, one of which is thoroughly measured. Their observer design is then based on the contractivity of the dynamics of the un-measured zone and a persistence of excitation condition.

Statement of the results

For

Proof of Proposition

From (

The observability mapping

In this section we focus on the observability of system (

5.1
Observability mapping extension and differential observability

From the symmetry of the system, we want to study

We then have the following.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume

Proof.

Let
t\geq 0
be such that

The map

Using the expression (

I_{1:2}^{\top}\end{bmatrix}

5.2
The pseudo-inverse

The goal of this section is to construct a pseudo-inverse of the observability mapping
T_{t}
, that is, to construct a Lipschitz map
\mathfrak{T}_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
\mathfrak{T}_{t}\circ T_{t}
is the identity on (most of)
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. This map
\mathfrak{T}_{t}
is a fundamental building block of our observer.
Under the assumptions of Theorem
5.1
, it is not difficult to provide an inverse of the map
T_{t}
since, letting
\Phi(X):=(X_{0},X_{1}X_{2},X_{1}X_{3})
for
X\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
, it holds

\Phi\circ S^{-1}_{t}\circ T_{t}=\operatorname{Id}\quad\text{on }\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\mathcal{Z}.

(72)

The above cannot be directly used to obtain a Lipschitz inverse since, as can be seen in the proof of Theorem
5.1
, the Lipschitz constant of
S_{t}^{-1}
blows up when
\rho
or
v_{0}
are small.
However,
S_{t}^{-1}
is Lipschitz on
S_{t}(\mathcal{X}_{\delta,\eta})
, which suggests defining an inverse via (
72
) by inserting a projection on
S_{t}(\mathcal{X}_{\delta,\eta})
in the definition. However, due to the complicated structure of
S_{t}(\mathcal{X}_{\delta,\eta})
, this is unpractical, and thus we resort to the following definition for the pseudo-inverse
\mathfrak{T}_{t}
:

\mathfrak{T}_{t}=\Phi\circ\Sat\circ S_{t}^{-1}\circ\Pi_{t}.

(73)

Here,
\Pi_{t}
is a projection depending on the output
y(t)=v_{0}(t)
and
\Sat
is an appropriate cut-off function, bounding the non-linear part in the observer and thus insuring that trajectories do not explode in finite time.

Namely, observe that
a necessary condition for
z
to belong to
S_{t}(\mathcal{X}_{\delta,\eta})
is that

|z_{0}|>\delta,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\begin{cases}J_{0}\Gamma_{0}^{0}(z_{0},R)\leq z_{1}+z_{0}-I_{0}\leq J_{0}\Gamma_{0}^{0}(z_{0},\eta)&\text{ if $z_{0}>0$,}\\
J_{0}\Gamma_{0}^{0}(z_{0},\eta)\leq z_{1}+z_{0}-I_{0}\leq J_{0}\Gamma_{0}^{0}(z_{0},R)&\text{ if $z_{0}<0$.}\end{cases}

(74)

Hence,
\Pi_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
is defined as the projection on the above set. That is,

\displaystyle\Pi_{t}(z)_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\min\{R,\max\{\delta,z_{0}\}\}&y(t)\geq 0,\\
\max\{-R,\min\{-\delta,z_{0}\}\}&y(t)<0,\end{cases}

(75)

\displaystyle\Pi_{t}(z)_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}g_{\eta,R}(\Pi_{t}(z)_{0},z_{1}),\qquad y(t)\geq 0,\\
g_{R,\eta}(\Pi_{t}(z)_{0},z_{1}),\qquad y(t)<0,\end{cases}

(76)

\displaystyle\Pi_{t}(z)_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z_{2},

(77)

\displaystyle\Pi_{t}(z)_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z_{3}.

(78)

Here, we let the auxiliary function
g
to be defined by

g_{\rho_{1},\rho_{2}}(z_{0},z_{1})=\begin{cases}J_{0}\Gamma_{0}^{0}(z_{0},\rho_{1})-z_{0}+I_{0}&\qquad\text{ if }z_{1}>J_{0}\Gamma_{0}^{0}(z_{0},\rho_{1})-z_{0}+I_{0},\\
J_{0}\Gamma_{0}^{0}(z_{0},\rho_{2})-z_{0}+I_{0}&\qquad\text{ if }z_{1}<J_{0}\Gamma_{0}^{0}(z_{0},\rho_{2})-z_{0}+I_{0},\\
z_{1}&\qquad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(79)

It is clear that
\Pi_{t}
is a Lipschitz function with Lipschitz constant uniformly bounded w.r.t. time.

The image of the projection
\Pi_{t}
fails to be in
S_{t}(\mathcal{X}_{\delta,\eta})
due to not respecting the
R
upper bound in its last two components.
Although we can still inverse
S_{t}^{-1}
on the image of
\Pi_{t}
,
in order to avoid solutions of the observer (
30
) from blowing up in finite time, we add an additional cut-off.
Let
p:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
be a smooth function such that
p(x)=1
if
|x|\leq R-1
,
p(x)=0
if
|x|\geq R
(recall
R
has been assumed to be arbitrarily large, and in particular larger than 3). Then

\Sat(X)=(X_{0},X_{1},p(|X_{2:3}|)X_{2:3}).

(80)

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
3.10
.

Proof of Theorem
3.10
.

For each
t\geq 0
, the function
\mathfrak{T}_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
introduced in Equation (
73
) is well defined. Since
\Pi_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{4})=S_{t}(\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{4},\ |v_{0}|>\delta,\eta<\rho<R,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\})
where the inverse
S_{t}^{-1}
is well defined and
C^{\infty}
due to Theorem
5.1
, it is a composition of continuous functions.
The image of the map
\mathfrak{T}_{t}
is

\mathfrak{T}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{4})=\Phi\circ\Sat\circ S_{t}^{-1}\circ\Pi_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{4})=\Phi(\mathcal{X}_{{\delta},{\eta}})\subset\{\delta\leq|v_{0}|\leq R\}\times B_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(0,R^{2}).

(81)

Moreover
\mathfrak{T}_{t}
is globally Lipschitz continuous. Indeed,
it is a composition of locally Lipschitz functions. The map
S_{t}^{-1}\circ\Pi_{t}
is not globally Lipschitz continous however (due to the last two components).
By construction of the cut-off function, we have

\lVert\partial_{z}\Sat\circ S_{t}^{-1}\rVert_{\Pi_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}=\lVert(\partial_{X}\Sat)(\partial_{z}S_{t}^{-1})\rVert_{S_{t}(\mathcal{X}_{{\delta},{\eta}})}\leq\lVert\partial_{X}\Sat\rVert\ \lVert\partial_{z}S_{t}^{-1}\rVert_{S_{t}(\mathcal{X}_{{\delta},{\eta}})}.

(82)

Since
\Phi
is smooth and thus locally Lipschitz continuous, and
\Sat\circ S_{t}^{-1}\circ\Pi_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{4})\subset\mathcal{X}_{{\delta},{\eta}}
, we have by construction
that

is a globally Lipschitz continuous map for every time

. We also have that
\Pi_{t}
being globally Lpischitz continous uniformly with respect to time. Hence, as a consequence of (

, Lipschitz constant for

exhibits uniform boundedness with respect to time.

We now provide an argument for (
28

for some

such that

has the same sign as the input

and

. If

(83)

By continuity, the above inequality extends to

We need the following Lemma, insuring that the non-linear part of the observer is globally Lipschitz.

Lemma 6.1
.

Under Assumption
3.7
, for every
t\geq 0
the map
(\mathcal{L}^{4}h)_{t}\circ\mathfrak{T}_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}

is Lipschitz continuous uniformly with respect to time. Here, we let
(\mathcal{L}^{4}h)_{t}:=(\mathcal{L}^{4}h)(\cdot,t)
.

Proof.

Observe that
(\mathcal{L}^{4}h)_{t}
is well-defined for
t\geq 0
, since
I\in C^{3}([0,+\infty),\mathbb{R}^{3})
by Assumption
3.7
.
Moreover,
(\mathcal{L}^{4}h)_{t}
is locally Lipschitz, uniformly with respect to time. Indeed, being the composition of smooth functions,
(\mathcal{L}^{4}h)_{t}
is locally Lipschitz (actually smooth) for any
t\geq 0
. The uniformity with respect to time follows by observing that the time dependent terms in the Jacobian matrix
\partial_{v}(\mathcal{L}^{4}h)_{t}
are linear combinations of
I_{i}^{(j)}
,
i\in\{0,1,2\}
and

, which are bounded in time by Assumption
3.7
.

Observe that by Theorem
3.10
the map
\mathfrak{T}_{t}
is globally Lipschitz, uniformly with respect to time, and has bounded image.
Therefore,
\mathcal{L}^{4}h\circ\mathfrak{T}_{t}
is also globally Lipschitz continuous uniformly with respect to time.
∎

We now prove the well definiteness of the observer (
30
).

Proof of Proposition
3.11
.

Thanks to Lemma
6.1
the first equation of (

.
By Proposition

∎

Remark 6.2

Let

.

]
,

]

.

Proof of Theorem
3.12
.

As
0<\delta<\delta^{*}
and
0<\eta<R
, we proved in Proposition
3.11
that, under these assumptions, solutions of (
30
) are well defined globally.

We start by proving Point (
i
).
We suppose at first that the output verifies
y(t_{0})\geq\delta
. From Proposition
3.2
it follows that
y(t)\geq\delta
for every time
t\geq 0

Since we are assuming
y(t)\geq\delta
, by Theorem
3.10
and by the fact that
z=T_{t}(v)
and
v_{0}(t)=y(t)
, we have

|v-\hat{v}|\leq|v-\mathfrak{T}_{t}(z)|+|\mathfrak{T}_{t}(z)-\mathfrak{T}_{t}(\hat{z})|\leq\eta\chi(v)+M_{0}|z-\hat{z}|,

(85)

where
M_{0}>0
is a uniform bound of the Lipschitz constant of
\mathfrak{T}_{t}
, which depends on
\eta
and
\delta
.
We are thus left to estimate
|z-\hat{z}|
. We define
e:=\hat{z}-z
and consider for any
l>0
the following Lyapounov function candidate

\|e\|_{l}^{2}:=e^{\top}P_{l}e,

(86)

where
P_{l}
is the matrix with elements
P_{l}(i,j):=\frac{(-1)^{i+j}}{l^{i+j-1}}\cdot\frac{(i+j-2)!}{(i-1)!(j-1)!}
, for
i,j\in\{1,2,3,4\}
.
From Lemma
6.1
, we can set
L>0
to be an upper bound for the Lipschitz constant of
(\mathcal{L}^{4}h)_{t}
on the compact set
\mathcal{U}:=\{(u_{0},u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\delta\leq|u_{0}|\leq R\ \text{and}\ \eta\leq|u_{1:2}|\leq R^{2}\}
, which can be chosen to be uniform with respect to time
t\geq 0
.

Then, since
y=Cz
, it holds

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d\|e\|_{l}^{2}}{dt}&=e^{\top}P_{l}(A-P_{l}^{-1}C^{\top}C)e+e^{\top}P_{l}W\left((\mathcal{L}^{4}h)\bigl(\mathfrak{T}_{t}(\hat{z})\bigr)-(\mathcal{L}^{4}h)(v)\right),\\
&=-\frac{l}{2}\|e\|_{l}^{2}+e^{\top}P_{l}W\left((\mathcal{L}^{4}h)\bigl(\mathfrak{T}_{t}(\hat{z})\bigr)-(\mathcal{L}^{4}h)(v)\right),\\
&\leq-\frac{l}{2}\|e\|_{l}^{2}+\sqrt{\frac{\nu_{\max}}{\nu_{\min}}}LM_{0}\|e\|_{l}^{2}+\chi(v)Ll^{-7/2}{\eta}\|e\|_{l}.\end{split}

(87)

Here, on the second line we used the fact that
P_{l}A+A^{\top}P_{l}-C^{\top}C=-lP_{l}
. The last line follows by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
85
), thanks to the estimates

\|W\|_{l}\leq l^{-7/2}\sqrt{\nu_{\max}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad l^{-7}\nu_{\min}\operatorname{Id}_{4}\leq P_{l}\leq l^{-1}\nu_{\max}\operatorname{Id}_{4}.

(88)

Here,
\nu_{\max}
and
\nu_{\min}
are respectively the max and the min eigenvalue of
P_{1}
.

We denote
l^{*}:=2\sqrt{\frac{\nu_{\max}}{\nu_{\min}}}LM_{0}
, and
M:=M_{0}\frac{\max\{1,\sqrt{\nu_{\max}}\}}{\sqrt{\nu_{\min}}}
. Dividing both sides of (
87
) by
\|e\|_{l}
, we can apply a linear Grönwall’s inequality to the variation of
\|e\|_{l}
. Since
\|e\|_{l}\geq\nu_{\min}l^{-7}|\hat{z}-z|
and we have (
85
), we obtain the following estimation in the immersed domain, for
t\in[t_{0},t_{f}]
,

|v(t)-\hat{v}(t)|\leq\eta\chi(v(t))+Me^{-(l-l^{*})t}\biggl(l^{3}|\hat{z}(t_{0})-z(t_{0})|e^{(l-l^{*})t_{0}}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{(l-l^{*})s}L\eta\chi(v(s))\,ds\biggr).

(89)

Using that
z(t)=T_{t}(v(t))
for all times
t\geq t_{0}
yields the first part of the point.

We now deal with the error increase during the switch.
We recall that
t_{1}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:|y(t)|<\delta\}=\inf\mathcal{S}
and
t_{2}=\sup\mathcal{S}
. We suppose now that
0<t_{1}<\infty
.
We recall that by Proposition
3.2
, it holds
t_{2}\leq t_{1}+t_{\delta}
, and
y(t)\geq\delta
for all times
t\geq t_{2}
.
If a switch occurs, the initial condition at
t_{1}
for the second dynamics in (
30
) is
\hat{v}(t_{1}^{+})=\mathfrak{T}_{t}(\hat{z}(t_{1}^{-}))
. The solution
\hat{v}
is therefore well defined in the interval
[t_{1},t_{2}]
and continuous, since
f
is uniformly Lipschitz and
y(\cdot)
is strictly increasing.

Let
L_{1}>0
be the Lipschitz constant of
T_{t}
on the compact set
\mathcal{U}
, which is uniform with respect to time thanks to Assumption
3.7
(see,
e.g.
, proof of Lemma
6.1
).
Then, since
\hat{z}(t_{2}^{-})=T_{t_{2}}(\hat{v}(t_{2}^{-}))
and
z(t_{2})=T_{t_{2}}(v(t_{2}))
, we have

|\hat{z}(t_{2}^{-})-z(t_{2}^{-})|\leq L_{1}|\hat{v}(t_{2}^{-})-v(t_{2}^{-})|.

(90)

Letting
L_{2}>0
be the Lipschitz constant of
f(\cdot,t)
on the compact
\mathcal{U}
, which is uniform with respect to time (see,
e.g.
, Proposition
2.4
), we have

|\hat{v}(t_{2}^{-})-v(t_{2}^{-})|\leq e^{(t_{2}-t_{1})L_{2}}|\hat{v}(t_{1})-v(t_{1})|\leq e^{t_{\delta}L_{2}}|\hat{v}(t_{1})-v(t_{1})|.

(91)

We have therefore characterised a bound of the error for all times
t\geq 0
using both relations (
89
) and (
91
).

Let us establish Point (
ii
) of the theorem.
Under the assumptions of the statement,
\chi(v(t))=0
for all times
t\geq 0
.
Recalling that
\mathcal{S}=[t_{1},t_{2}]
, we will distinguish two cases:
t_{1}=0
or
t_{1}>0
.
In the first case, by the second part of Point (
i
), for any
t\leq t_{2}
we have

|\hat{v}(t)-v(t)|\leq e^{L_{2}t_{\delta}}|\hat{v}(0)-v(0)|\leq e^{\left(L_{2}+(l-l^{*})\right)t_{\delta}}e^{-(l-l^{*})t}|\hat{v}(0)-v(0)|.

(92)

Here we used that
e^{-(l-l^{*})t_{1}}\leq e^{-(l-l^{*})t}e^{(l-l^{*})t_{\delta}}
as
t\leq t_{2}
. Letting now
t\geq t_{2}
and using the first part of Point (

) with

we get

|\hat{v}(t)-v(t)|\leq Ml^{3}e^{\left(L_{2}+(l-l^{*})\right)t_{\delta}}e^{-(l-l^{*})t}|\hat{v}(0)-v(0)|.

(93)

Hence, the statement stands proved in this case, with
k=Ml^{3}e^{\left(L_{2}+(l-l^{*})\right)t_{\delta}}
.
The argument for the case
t_{1}>0
is similar, except that one has to start by applying the first part of Point (
i
) to
t\in[0,t_{1}]
, then plug this estimate in the error during the switching time for

, and finally reapply the first part of Point (
i
) to obtain the estimate for
t\geq t_{2}
.

We finally provide an argument for Point (
iii
). We start by observing that, since
v(0)\in B_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(0,R)
and
\hat{v}
is initialized in the compact set

, it holds
|\hat{z}(0)-z(0)|\leq L_{1}(\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal{K})+R)
. Then, one proceeds exactly as for the previous point except that instead of having

.
This leads to the following estimates:

e^{-(l-l^{*})t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(l-l^{*})s}\chi(v(s))\,ds\leq\frac{1}{l-l^{*}}\quad\text{and}\quad e^{-(l-l^{*})t}\int_{t_{2}}^{t}e^{(l-l^{*})s}\chi(v(s))\,ds\leq\frac{1}{l-l^{*}}

(94)

We assume
\delta
to be sufficiently small, to be fixed later, so that
t_{\delta}<t^{*}
.
In the case where the first switching time
t_{1}
is positive, letting
C>0
be a constant independent of
\delta
,
\eta
, and

one then obtains that for any

\frac{1}{C}|\hat{v}(t)-v(t)|\leq\eta\left(1+Ml^{3}e^{L_{2}t_{\delta}}e^{-(l-l^{*})t^{*}}\left(1+\frac{M}{l-l^{*}}\right)+\frac{M}{l-l^{*}}\right)+M^{2}l^{6}e^{(L_{2}+(l-l^{*}))t_{\delta}}e^{-(l-l^{*})t}.

(95)

Here, one has to be careful due to the fact that

depends on
\eta
, and in particular

as

.
One starts by fixing
\eta<\varepsilon/(4C)
. Then, with
\eta
fixed, one chooses

sufficiently large so that

M^{2}l^{6}e^{-(l-l^{*})t}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4C}.

(96)

Assuming that

, this also implies that

|\hat{v}(t)-v(t)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\left(2+{l^{-3}}e^{L_{2}t_{\delta}}\frac{\varepsilon}{2C}\right)+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}e^{(L_{2}+(l-l^{*}))t_{\delta}}.

Then, assuming

|\hat{v}(t)-v(t)|\leq{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{e^{(L_{2}+(l-l^{*}))t_{\delta}}}{2}\right).

(98)

Finally, since

(99)

Here,

It is then easy to see that the choice of

Appendix A
Appendix: Modelisation adjustments

Some authors favor a neural field model for activity instead of post-membrane potential,
e.g.
,
[
4
,
8
]
. The model is similar to (
1
):

\tau\partial_{t}A(x,t)=\ -A(x,t)\ +\ \sigma\bigl(J\cdot A(\cdot,t)\ +I_{\text{ext}}(t)\bigr).

(100)

Supposing
I_{\text{ext}}
smooth enough, we can easily go from (
100
) to (
1
) by considering the change of variable:
V=J\cdot A+I_{\text{ext}},
and replacing
I_{\text{ext}}
with
I_{\text{ext}}+\dot{I}_{\text{ext}}
. We favor (
1
) because it appears easier to study from a mathematical point of view.
If our measurement is the averaged neural activity
a_{0}=\int_{\Omega}A(u)du
, the change of variable
V=J\cdot A+I_{\text{ext}}
leads to
\int_{\Omega}V(u)du=\langle A,J\cdot 1\rangle+\langle I_{\text{ext}},1\rangle=J_{0}a_{0}+I_{0}.

Therefore knowing the parameters
J_{0}
and
I_{0}
, we can retrieve the mean membrane potential
v_{0}
based on measurement not done in voltage but in spark per second (activity).

We also have some freedom in changing the shape of the nonlinearity. Some model choose sigmoidal functions
\sigma_{+}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
that are strictly positive, strictly increasing (i.e.,
\sigma_{+}^{\prime}>0
), convex-concave (i.e.,
x\sigma_{+}^{\prime\prime}(x)\leq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
), and such that

\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\to-\infty\end{subarray}}\sigma_{+}(x)=0,\quad\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\to+\infty\end{subarray}}\sigma_{+}(x)=1,\quad\sigma_{+}^{\prime}>0,\quad\max_{\mathbb{R}}\sigma_{+}^{\prime}=\sigma_{+}^{\prime}(0).

In this case there exist two parameters
s_{1}>0
and
s_{2}>0
such that

\sigma_{+}=s_{1}\sigma+s_{2}
where
\sigma
satisfies Assumption
2.1
. Therefore, it suffices to replace
I_{0}
by
I_{0}+s_{2}
and
J_{i}
by
J_{i}s_{1}
for
i\in\{0,1\}
.

On the other hand, if there is a threshold in the sigmoid function, then there exists a constant
h_{0}
such that
\sigma(\cdot)
is replaced by
\sigma(\cdot-h_{0})
. Hence, we can always go back to our main equation by taking the change of variable from
v_{0}
to
v_{0}-h_{0}
. Replacing then
I_{0}
with
I_{0}+h_{0}
maintains the performed analysis.

Interpolating Boundary Conditions on A ​ d ​ S 2 AdS_{2}
Anthonny F. Canazas Garay, Diego H. Correa, Alberto Faraggi, Guillermo A. Silva

Abstract
We consider two instances of boundary conditions for massless scalars on A ​ d ​ S 2 AdS_{2} that interpolate between the Dirichlet and Neumann cases while preserving scale invariance. Assessing invariance under the full S ​ L ​ ( 2 , ℝ ) SL(2;\mathds{R}) conformal group is not immediate given their non-local nature. To further clarify this issue, we compute holographically 2- and 4-point correlation functions using the aforementioned boundary conditions and study their transformation properties. Concretely, motivated by the dual description of some multi-parametric families of Wilson loops in ABJM theory, we look at the excitations of an open string around an A ​ d ​ S 2 ⊂ A ​ d ​ S 4 × ℂ ​ ℙ 3 AdS_{2}\subset AdS_{4}\times\mathbb{CP}^{3} worldsheet, thus obtaining correlators of operators inserted along a 1 1 -dimensional defect in 𝒩 = 6 {\cal N}=6 super Chern-Simons-matter theory at strong coupling. Of the two types of boundary conditions analyzed, only one leads to the expected functional structure for conformal primaries; the other exhibits covariance under translations and rescalings but not under special conformal transformations.

One way of testing if a set of boundary conditions is compatible with a given symmetry is by computing correlation functions holographically. As it turns out, the 2-point functions that result from sourcing the combination (

2.1
Variational Principle and AdS/CFT

Let us start by reviewing the role that boundary terms and the variational principle play in the calculation of correlation functions in AdS/CFT

\displaystyle S_{0}

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}x\sqrt{g}\,\partial_{\mu}\bar{\phi}\partial^{\mu}\phi\,.

Setting

\displaystyle\phi(z,\tau)

\displaystyle=\alpha(\tau)+z\beta(\tau)+\mathcal{O}(z^{2})\,.

Assuming regularity in the bulk (

\displaystyle\delta S_{0}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau\sqrt{h}\left(\partial_{n}\bar{\phi}\delta\phi+\partial_{n}\phi\delta\bar{\phi}\right)\Big|_{z=\epsilon}

\displaystyle\langle\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{D}(\tau)\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad S_{D}

\displaystyle\equiv S_{0}\,.

(8)

Alternatively, Neumann boundary conditions require the addition of the boundary term

\displaystyle S^{N}_{\textrm{bdry}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau\sqrt{h}\left(\phi\partial_{n}\bar{\phi}+\bar{\phi}\partial_{n}\phi\right)\Big|_{z=\epsilon}

\displaystyle\delta\left(S_{0}+S^{N}_{\textrm{bdry}}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau\left(\bar{\alpha}(\tau)\delta\beta(\tau)+\alpha(\tau)\delta\bar{\beta}(\tau)\right)\,.

\displaystyle\langle\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{N}(\tau)\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv-\frac{\delta S_{N}[J]}{\delta J_{N}(\tau)}

\displaystyle=-\bar{\alpha}(\tau)\,,

\displaystyle\qquad S_{N}

\displaystyle\equiv S_{0}+S^{N}_{\textrm{bdry}}\,.

(11)

The possibility of imposing either boundary condition is due to the fact that a massless scalar field in

2.2
Local and Non-local Boundary Conditions

Generically, Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions are compatible with the isometries of
AdS_{2}
, leading to correlation functions that exhibit 1d conformal symmetry. Moreover, when appropriately combined with fermionic fields, they also respect supersymmetry
Sakai:1984vm
. In this paper we explore boundary conditions that interpolate between the Dirichlet and Neumann cases while preserving scale invariance, and wonder whether they are consistent with the full conformal group.

To achieve our goal we need a well-posed variational problem in which the boundary source
J(\tau)
combines the two fall-off functions
\alpha(\tau)
and
\beta(\tau)
in a suitable way. Since these fields have different mass dimensions, directly adding them necessarily breaks scale invariance
Witten:2001ua
;
Hartman_2008

and interpreted as a Renormalization Group flow in the dual CFT
1

Correa_2020

\beta(\tau)
with the derivative of
\alpha(\tau)
or, more conveniently (for reasons that will become clear below),
\alpha(\tau)
with an integral of
\beta(\tau)

\displaystyle J(\tau)

3
Correlation Functions at strong coupling

We now move on to study the tree-level correlation functions that result from applying the AdS/CFT dictionary to the interpolating boundary conditions introduced in the previous section. To this purpose we consider the dynamics arising from the fluctuations of a type IIA open string around an
AdS_{2}\subset AdS_{4}\times\mathds{CP}^{3}
classical worldsheet. The bosonic spectrum includes two transverse fluctuations in
AdS_{4}
with
m^{2}=2
and six fluctuations along
\mathbb{CP}^{3}
with
m^{2}=0
. We identify the scalar field discussed in previous section with a complex combination of these massless excitations, which can be put in correspondence with certain components of the displacement (super) multiplet along the Wilson loop. The expansion of the Nambu-Goto action to fourth order in the effective string tension was performed in
Bianchi_2020
. Interestingly, the quartic interactions involve derivatives of the field. This forces us to revisit the variational problem and modify the definition of the source and vev. Performing a first principles derivation of the
4
-point function (relegated to appendix
C

Before we proceed with the calculation of holographic correlation functions, let us explain how the Dirichlet and Neumann results can be recovered as limiting cases of our proposed interpolating boundary conditions. From (
12
) and (
18
) we immediately see that

\displaystyle J_{D}(\tau)

\displaystyle=J(\tau)\Big|_{\chi=0}\,,

\displaystyle\qquad\langle\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{D}(\tau)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\bar{\mathcal{O}}(\tau)\rangle\Big|_{\chi=0}\,,

(29)

so the
\chi=0

On the other end of the interpolation, however, neither the source nor the vev directly reduce to their Neumann counterparts. Instead, the relation involves a derivative/Hilbert transform, namely,

\displaystyle J_{N}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{>{\displaystyle}r>{\displaystyle}l}\partial_{\tau}J(\tau)\Big|_{\chi=\frac{\pi}{2}}&\quad\textrm{L}\\
\\
-\partial_{\tau}\hat{J}(\tau)\Big|_{\chi=\frac{\pi}{2}}&\quad\textrm{NL}\end{array}\right.\,,

\displaystyle\qquad\partial_{\tau}\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{N}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{>{\displaystyle}r>{\displaystyle}l}-\bar{\mathcal{O}}(\tau)\Big|_{\chi=\frac{\pi}{2}}&\quad\textrm{L}\\

\hat{\bar{\mathcal{O}}}(\tau)\Big|_{\chi=\frac{\pi}{2}}&\quad\textrm{NL}\end{array}\right.\,.

These relations are crucial for correctly interpreting the

limit of the interpolating boundary conditions: it will yield correlation functions that are related to those in the Neumann case by taking the Hilbert transform and/or (NL/L) a derivative with respect to each of the insertion points. In particular, we expect that

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{O}(\tau_{1})\bar{\mathcal{O}}(\tau_{2})\rangle\Big|_{\chi=\frac{\pi}{2}}

\displaystyle=\langle\partial_{\tau_{1}}\mathcal{O}_{N}(\tau_{1})\overline{\partial_{\tau_{2}}\mathcal{O}_{N}(\tau_{2})}\rangle\left\{\begin{array}[]{>{\displaystyle}r>{\displaystyle}l}1&\quad\textrm{L}\\
\\
-1&\quad\textrm{NL}\end{array}\right.\,,

where we have used
\bar{\hat{f}}(\tau)=-\hat{\bar{f}}(\tau)
and that successive Hilbert transforms of
f(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})
over
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
give back
f(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})
. We will confirm this expectation below.

3.1
2
-point function

The boundary-to-bulk propagator
K(z,\tau;\tau^{\prime})
for a massless scalar field is defined such that the regular solution to the equation of motion is expressed as

\displaystyle\phi(z,\tau)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau^{\prime}K(z,\tau;\tau^{\prime})J(\tau^{\prime})\,,

\displaystyle\qquad\Box K(z,\tau;\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=0\,,

(44)

where
J(\tau)
is the boundary data that is fixed in the variational problem. In the case of Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions they read

\displaystyle K_{D}(z,\tau;\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{z}{z^{2}+(\tau-\tau^{\prime})^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\qquad K_{N}(z,\tau;\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\left(z^{2}+(\tau-\tau^{\prime})^{2}\right)\,,

(45)

and satisfy

\displaystyle K_{D}(z,\tau;\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle\underset{z\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\,,

\displaystyle\qquad\partial_{z}K_{N}(z,\tau;\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle\underset{z\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\,,

(46)

as appropriate for
J_{D}(\tau)=\alpha(\tau)
and
J_{N}(\tau)=\beta(\tau)
, respectively. For the case at hand, in order to comply with the boundary condition (
12
), the propagator must behave as

\displaystyle\cos\chi\,K(z,\tau;\tau^{\prime})+\sin\chi\,\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau^{\prime\prime}\partial_{z}K(z,\tau^{\prime\prime};\tau^{\prime})g(\tau-\tau^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle\underset{z\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\,.

(47)

We readily find

\displaystyle K(z,\tau;\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{>{\displaystyle}c>{\displaystyle}l}\cos\chi\,K_{D}(z,\tau;\tau^{\prime})+\sin\chi\,\partial_{\tau}K_{N}(z,\tau;\tau^{\prime})&\quad\textrm{L}\\
\\
\frac{K_{D}(z,\tau;\tau^{\prime})}{\cos\chi+\sin\chi}&\quad\textrm{NL}\end{array}\right.\,.

It is easy to check that

\displaystyle\cos\chi\,K(z,\tau;\tau^{\prime})+\sin\chi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau^{\prime\prime}\partial_{z}K(z,\tau^{\prime\prime};\tau^{\prime})g(\tau-\tau^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=K_{D}(z,\tau;\tau^{\prime})\,,

(51)

thus verifying (
47
) for both L and NL boundary conditions.

From (
8
) and (
11
) we find that the
2
-point functions for Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions are given by

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{O}_{D}(\tau_{1})\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{D}(\tau_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1}{(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})^{2}}\,,\qquad\langle\mathcal{O}_{N}(\tau_{1})\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{N}(\tau_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\ln\left(|\tau_{2}-\tau_{1}|\right)\,.

(52)

In the Neumann case, the dual (
\Delta_{{\cal O}_{N}}=0
) operator does not transform as a proper primary. As explained above, it is its derivative that is well-behaved, that is,

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{\tau_{1}}\mathcal{O}_{N}(\tau_{1})\overline{\partial_{\tau_{2}}\mathcal{O}_{N}(\tau_{2})}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\tau_{1}}\partial_{\tau_{2}}\langle\mathcal{O}_{N}(\tau_{1})\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{N}(\tau_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1}{(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})^{2}}\,,

(53)

where we have used that
\overline{\partial_{\tau}\mathcal{O}_{N}(\tau)}=-\partial_{\tau}\bar{\mathcal{O}}_{N}(\tau)
. Similarly, according to (
18
), the
2
-point functions for the interpolating boundary conditions are

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{O}(\tau_{1})\bar{\mathcal{O}}(\tau_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1}{(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})^{2}}\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{>{\displaystyle}c>{\displaystyle}l}1&\qquad\textrm{L}\\
\\
\frac{\cos\chi-\sin\chi}{\cos\chi+\sin\chi}&\qquad\textrm{NL}\end{array}\right.\,.

As advertised, these expressions correctly reproduce the Dirichlet and Neumann limits. In the version discussed in
Correa_2020
, the L case exhibited a contact term that is now absent from the
2
-point function. This slight difference is explained by the presence of the term
\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}\bar{J}(\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau}J(\tau)
in the boundary action (
16
). The same term is also responsible for yielding a vev that appropriately interpolates between
\beta(\tau)
(Dirichlet) and
\partial_{\tau}\alpha(\tau)
(Neumann).

3.2
4
-point function

The computation of
4
-point functions depends on the precise form of the quartic interactions. In the following we consider the Nambu-Goto action expanded around an open
AdS_{2}
worldsheet ending on a straight line at the
AdS_{4}\times\mathbb{CP}^{3}
boundary. The fluctuations along the
\mathbb{CP}^{3}
directions are massless, and we focus on a single complex combination of these. To fourth order in
\delta X\sim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{4}}\phi
, the Nambu-Goto action in static gauge becomes
Bianchi_2020

\displaystyle S_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}x\sqrt{g}\left[|\partial\phi|^{2}-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\lambda}}\left(|\partial\phi|^{2}|\phi|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\partial\phi|^{4}\right)\right]\,,

(57)

where
g_{\mu\nu}
is the
AdS_{2}
worldsheet metric (
4
),
\lambda=2T^{2}
is the ABJM ’t Hooft coupling and
T
is the effective string tension.

A crucial aspect of the action (
57
) is that the quartic interactions involve derivatives of the field. This forces us to revisit the variational problem. Indeed, we now have

\displaystyle\delta S_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau\left(\bar{\eta}(\tau)\delta\alpha(\tau)+\eta(\tau)\delta\bar{\alpha}(\tau)\right)\,,

(58)

with

\displaystyle\eta(\tau)

\displaystyle\equiv\beta(\tau)\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\lambda}}|\alpha(\tau)|^{2}\right)\,.

(59)

As with
S_{0}
, this action is appropriate for the Dirichlet problem in which
\alpha(\tau)
is fixed. The natural analogue of Neumann boundary conditions corresponds to fixing
\eta(\tau)
via

\displaystyle S^{N}_{\textrm{bdry}}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau\left(\alpha(\tau)\bar{\eta}(\tau)+\bar{\alpha}(\tau)\eta(\tau)\right)\,.

(60)

In order to impose our proposal of interpolating boundary conditions we add to
S_{\lambda}
the term

\displaystyle S_{\textrm{bdry}}

\displaystyle=-\cos\chi\sin\chi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau^{\prime}\left(\bar{\eta}(\tau)\eta(\tau^{\prime})-\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}\bar{\alpha}(\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau}\alpha(\tau)\right)g(\tau-\tau^{\prime})

(61)

\displaystyle\hskip 11.0pt+\sin^{2}\chi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau\left(\bar{\alpha}(\tau)\eta(\tau)+\alpha(\tau)\bar{\eta}(\tau)\right)

(62)

\displaystyle=-\tan\chi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau^{\prime}\left(\bar{\eta}(\tau)\eta(\tau^{\prime})-\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}\bar{J}(\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau}J(\tau)\right)g(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\,,

(63)

with
g(\tau)
as before and

\displaystyle J(\tau)

\displaystyle\equiv\cos\chi\,\alpha(\tau)+\sin\chi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau^{\prime}\eta(\tau^{\prime})g(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\,.

(64)

The
1
-point function then reads

\displaystyle\langle\bar{\mathcal{O}}(\tau)\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv\cos\chi\,\bar{\eta}(\tau)+\sin\chi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau^{\prime}\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}\bar{\alpha}(\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau}g(\tau-\tau^{\prime})

(65)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\cos\chi}\left(\bar{\eta}(\tau)+\sin\chi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau^{\prime}\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}\bar{J}(\tau^{\prime})\partial_{\tau}g(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\right)\,.

(66)

We see that the derivative nature of the quartic potential not only alters the expression for the vev but also requires us to modify the definition of the source (c.f. (
12
), (
18
), (
14
)). Fortunately, the modification is quite simple, we just need to replace
\beta(\tau)\to\eta(\tau)
in every expression. It is important to mention that the boundary condition (
64
) is now non-linear in the field and depends on the coupling
\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.

In Appendix
C
we show that, in spite of the above modifications to the source and vev, the Witten diagram prescription for computing the
4
-point correlation function still works in the usual way: (i) replace each field in the quartic vertex (
57

satisfying the appropriate boundary conditions and (ii) symmetrize the insertion points. The expression arising from the connected diagram depicted in figure

\sqrt{\frac{2}{\lambda}}\left(|\partial\phi|^{2}|\phi|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\partial\phi|^{4}\right)
\mathcal{O}(\tau_{1})
\bar{\mathcal{O}}(\tau_{2})
\mathcal{O}(\tau_{3})
\bar{\mathcal{O}}(\tau_{4})

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{2}}\langle\mathcal{O}(\tau_{1})\bar{\mathcal{O}}(\tau_{2})\mathcal{O}(\tau_{3})\bar{\mathcal{O}}(\tau_{4})\rangle

\displaystyle=T(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3},\tau_{4})+(\tau_{1}\leftrightarrow\tau_{3})+(\tau_{2}\leftrightarrow\tau_{4})+(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\leftrightarrow\tau_{3},\tau_{4})\,,

(67)

\displaystyle=U(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3},\tau_{4})+V(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\tau_{3},\tau_{4})\,,

(69)

4
Discussion

Motivated by the existence of a one-parameter family of supersymmetric Wilson Loops in ABJM theory,
in this paper we studied two examples of interpolating scale invariant boundary conditions for interacting massless scalar fields in
AdS_{2}
.
In order to test if they preserve full conformal symmetry, we computed 4-point correlation functions holographically. Quartic interaction terms were derived from the expansion of the Nambu-Goto action around an open string worldsheet in
AdS_{4}\times\mathbb{CP}^{3}
. These terms involve derivatives which induce additional boundary terms in the action. We verified that a first-principles derivation of the 4-point function coincides with the Witten diagrams prescription.

For scalar fields satisfying the L boundary condition, previously discussed in
Correa_2020
, the expression for the 2-point correlation function (
3.1
) is conformally covariant. However, the holographic 4-point function shown in (
73
) cannot be interpreted as that of primary operators in a CFT
1
. This failure of conformal covariance is reminiscent of the one found for the pure Neumann case, studied in
Beccaria:2019dws
in the context of ordinary Wilson Loops in
{\cal N}=4
super Yang-Mills. In that reference the anomalous factor disappeared after integrating over the position in
S^{5}
around which the Nambu-Goto action was expanded. No such integration appears to be justified in the ABJM case.

On the contrary, in view of the results (
3.1
) and (
74
), massless scalars satisfying NL boundary conditions are dual representations of
\Delta=1
primaries in the defect CFT
1
. The values
\chi=\pm\frac{\pi}{4}
of the interpolating parameter are special: for
\chi=-\frac{\pi}{4}
the combination we source in (
2.2
) is identically zero, whereas for
\chi=\frac{\pi}{4}
the vev of the dual operator in the presence of non-zero sources vanishes. Notice that these two statements, which are a consequence of (
28
), are only valid at leading order in
\lambda^{-1/2}
. The fact that the 2-point correlator vanishes for
\chi=\frac{\pi}{4}
and becomes negative beyond this point seems puzzling, as it would imply a violation of unitarity. We claim, however, that in the range
\frac{\pi}{4}<\chi\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
the LHS of (
12
) should be interpreted as the Hilbert transform of the source and not as the source itself. The physical result is then obtained by transforming back with respect to
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
, a procedure that yields a positive 2-point function. This interpretation becomes evident at the endpoint
\chi=\pi/2
, as argued at the begginning of section
3
.

The reasons why the 4-point function (
74
) is consistent with conformal covariance but (
73
) is not are not clear to us at the moment. One difference between the L and NL boundary conditions, perhaps relevant to understand this issue, is that the former break invariance under parity transformation
\tau\to-\tau
whereas the latter do not.

In conclusion, out of the two interpolating boundary conditions we have studied, only the NL ones lead to conformally covariant 4-point correlation functions of primary operators. The failure of conformal covariance for the L-boundary conditions case might appear surprising, as the interpolating parameter
\chi
is dimensionless and the correlation functions are consistent with scale invariance.
What are the implications of this for the problem of identifying the dual description of the supersymmetric family of interpolating Wilson Loops in ABJM theory
Ouyang:2015bmy
;
Ouyang:2015iza
? In principle, this does not necessarily imply that L-boundary condition should be rejected or that the NL-boundary condition should be preferred. The field theory arguments mentioned in the introduction suggest that the interpolating parameter in the family of Wilson loops should be regarded as an exactly marginal deformation
5
5

5

This interpretation applies when perturbative computations are done at framing
f=1
. Recently, the same problem was considered with a regularization scheme that uses framing
f=0

Castiglioni:2022yes
, in which case the interpolation was interpreted as an RG flow. An interesting open question is what is in correspondence to framing in the dual string theory description.
. However, scale invariance does not imply full conformal symmetry. In order to make a more assertive proposal for the dual representation of the interpolating Wilson loops it would be necessary to understand the conformal transformation properties in the field theory description. For example, it would be interesting to study perturbatively 4-point correlations functions of insertions along those Wilson loops and appraise their conformal covariance in the field theory side. It was shown in
Correa_2020
that the L-boundary condition describes configurations preserving 4 real supersymmetries, matching the number of supersymmetries of the interpolating Wilson loops. The consistency of supersymmetry transformations with the NL-boundary condition is another interesting problem that remains to be explored.

Another more speculative possibility is that, instead of being broken, conformal covariance of the L-boundary condition could be realized in a more intricate way. The anomalous terms in (
73
), although inappropriate for a 4-point correlation function of primary operators of scale dimension
\Delta=1
, would appear in 4-point functions involving operators of scale dimensions
\Delta=3/2
and
\Delta=1/2
. The appearance of fractional scale dimension for bosonic excitations could point towards derivatives of fractional order, as the ones defined in
Khodaee:2017tbk
. These describe fields in CFT
1
that transform under non-local representations of
sl(2)
. Therefore, it could be interesting to explore the possibility of relating our L-boundary condition to excitations in those more generic representations.

Finally, in a general context not related to Wilson loops, one could wonder whether the interpolating boundary conditions presented here can be generalized to scalar fields in
AdS_{d+1}
. Indeed, consider a massive field with
\Delta_{+}-\Delta_{-}=1
; any other masses for which
\Delta_{+}-\Delta_{-}
equals a larger integer lie outside the BF window. For the L-choice of boundary conditions, the
\tau
-derivative could be generalized to a directional derivative on the
d
-dimensional boundary. This, of course, would break rotational invariance as well as the
d
-dimensional conformal invariance. In order to generalize the NL-boundary condition, we could use the following non-local first order differential operator:
6
6

6

This definition follows from the Fourier transform of the Laplace operator.

\sqrt{\nabla^{2}}f(x)\equiv-\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac{d+1}{2})}{\pi^{\tfrac{d+1}{2}}}\int d^{d}x^{\prime}\frac{f(x^{\prime})}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{d+1}}\,.

(78)

Since
\sqrt{\partial_{\tau}^{2}}f(\tau)=\partial_{\tau}\hat{f}(\tau)
for
d=1
, a natural generalization of the NL boundary condition could be

\partial_{\tau}\hat{J}(\tau)=\cos\chi\,\partial_{\tau}\hat{\alpha}(\tau)-\sin\chi\,\beta(\tau)\qquad\mapsto\qquad\sqrt{\nabla^{2}}J(x)=\cos\chi\,\sqrt{\nabla^{2}}\alpha(x)-\sin\chi\,\beta(x)\,.

(79)

It would be interesting to further analyze these higher-dimensional generalizations and the correlation functions that can be obtained from them.

\displaystyle\hat{f}(\tau)

(81)

f(\tau)

Appendix B
Bulk-to-bulk propagators

In AdS/CFT the computation of tree-level correlation functions involves solving non-linear equations of motion, which in turn requires knowing both the bulk-to-boundary and bulk-to-bulk propagators. For a massless scalar field the latter is defined by

\displaystyle\Box_{x}G(x;x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\delta(x;x^{\prime})\,.

(83)

For the
AdS_{2}
metric in conformal gauge (
4
), the Dirichlet and Neumann propagators coincide with those in flat space,

\displaystyle G_{D/N}(z,\tau;z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(\ln\left((z-z^{\prime})^{2}+(\tau-\tau^{\prime})^{2}\right)\mp\ln\left((z+z^{\prime})^{2}+(\tau-\tau^{\prime})^{2}\right)\right)\,,

(84)

and satisfy

\displaystyle G_{D}(z,\tau;z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})\Big|_{z=0}

\displaystyle=0\,,

\displaystyle\qquad\partial_{z}G_{N}(z,\tau;z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})\Big|_{z=0}

\displaystyle=0\,.

(85)

Moreover, they are related to the corresponding
AdS_{2}
bulk-to-boundary propagators (
45
) by

\displaystyle K_{D}(z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime};\tau)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{z}G_{D}(z,\tau;z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})\Big|_{z=0}\,,

\displaystyle\qquad K_{N}(z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime};\tau)

\displaystyle=G_{N}(z,\tau;z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})\Big|_{z=0}\,.

(86)

This follows directly from Green’s third identity

\displaystyle\phi(z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau\left(\phi(z,\tau)\partial_{z}G(z,\tau;z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})-\partial_{z}\phi(z,\tau)G(z,\tau;z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})\right)\Big|_{z=0}\,,

(87)

valid for any harmonic function
\phi
on the upper half-plane.

In order to identify the correct boundary conditions for
G(x;x^{\prime})
in the interpolating case we re-write Green’s identity as

\displaystyle\phi(z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau\left[\left(\phi(z,\tau)+\tan\chi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau^{\prime\prime}\partial_{z}\phi(z,\tau^{\prime\prime})g(\tau-\tau^{\prime\prime})\right)\partial_{z}G(z,\tau;z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})\right.

(88)

\displaystyle\hskip 11.0pt\left.-\partial_{z}\phi(z,\tau)\left(G(z,\tau;z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})+\tan\chi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau^{\prime\prime}\partial_{z}G(z,\tau^{\prime\prime};z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})g(-\tau+\tau^{\prime\prime})\right)\right]\Bigg|_{z=0}\,,

(89)

where we have swapped the order of integration in the last term. Recalling that

\displaystyle\Box\phi

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau\,\partial_{z}\phi(z,\tau)\Big|_{z=0}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(90)

we see that a sufficient condition for the second line in (
88
) to vanish is

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\left(\cos\chi\,G(z,\tau;z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})+\sin\chi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tau^{\prime\prime}\partial_{z}G(z,\tau^{\prime\prime};z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})g(-\tau+\tau^{\prime\prime})\right)\Bigg|_{z=0}

\displaystyle=0\,.

(91)

The field can then be reconstructed from the boundary data using the bulk-to-boundary propagator

\displaystyle K(z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime};\tau)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\cos\chi}\partial_{z}G(z,\tau;z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})\Big|_{z=0}\,.

(92)

The bulk-to-bulk Green’s functions with boundary conditions (
91
) can be found with the help of the relations

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}G_{D/N}(z,\tau;z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\partial_{z^{\prime}}\hat{G}_{N/D}(z,\tau;z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})\,,\hskip 15.0pt\partial_{z^{\prime}}G_{D/N}(z,\tau;z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\tau^{\prime}}\hat{G}_{N/D}(z,\tau;z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})\,,

(93)

valid for
z>z^{\prime}
, where the Hilbert transforms of the Dirichlet and Neumann propagators read

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{D/N}(z,\tau;z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\tau-\tau^{\prime}}{|z-z^{\prime}|}\right)\mp\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\tau-\tau^{\prime}}{z+z^{\prime}}\right)\right)\,.

(94)

Notice that

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{D}(z,\tau;z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})-\hat{G}_{N}(z,\tau;z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\tau-\tau^{\prime}}{z+z^{\prime}}\right)

(95)

is a harmonic function but

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{D}(z,\tau;z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})+\hat{G}_{N}(z,\tau;z^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\tau-\tau^{\prime}}{|z-z^{\prime}|}\right)

(96)

is not. We find that

\displaystyle G(x;x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{>{\displaystyle}c>{\displaystyle}l}c^{2}_{\chi}\,G_{D}(x;x^{\prime})+s^{2}_{\chi}\,G_{N}(x;x^{\prime})-c_{\chi}s_{\chi}\big(\hat{G}_{D}(x;x^{\prime})-\hat{G}_{N}(x;x^{\prime})\big)&\quad\textrm{L}\\
\\
\frac{c_{\chi}\,G_{D}(x;x^{\prime})+s_{\chi}\,G_{N}(x;x^{\prime})}{c_{\chi}+s_{\chi}}&\quad\textrm{NL}\end{array}\right.\,,

where we have abbreviated
c_{\chi}=\cos\chi
and
s_{\chi}=\sin\chi
. Satisfyingly, we find from (
92
) that (
B
) correctly reproduces (
3.1
).

Appendix C
First Principles Derivation of Witten Diagrams

According to the AdS/CFT dictionary, at tree-level, the
4
-point function in the dual field theory is given by

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{O}(\tau_{1})\bar{\mathcal{O}}(\tau_{2})\mathcal{O}(\tau_{3})\bar{\mathcal{O}}(\tau_{4})\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{\delta^{4}S[J]}{\delta\bar{J}(\tau_{1})\delta J(\tau_{2})\delta\bar{J}(\tau_{3})\delta J(\tau_{4})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\cos\chi}\frac{\delta^{3}\bar{\eta}(\tau_{4})}{\delta\bar{J}(\tau_{1})\delta J(\tau_{2})\delta\bar{J}(\tau_{3})}\,,

(100)

where we have used (
65
) to identify the first derivative of the on-shell action. Notice that the contact term that renders the
2
-point function conformally covariant is linear in
J(\tau)
and therefore does not affect higher order correlators.

To find the vev as a function of the source we need to solve the non-linear equation of motion derived from the quartic action (

) with interpolating boundary conditions (
64
).
We do this perturbatively in the coupling
\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=\phi_{0}+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\lambda}}\phi_{1}+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{-1})\,.

We find that

\displaystyle\Box\phi_{0}

\displaystyle=0\,,

\displaystyle\qquad\Box\phi_{1}

\displaystyle=J_{\textrm{bulk}}\,,

(102)

where

\displaystyle J_{\textrm{bulk}}

\displaystyle=-\phi_{0}|\partial\phi_{0}|^{2}+\nabla_{\mu}\left(\partial^{\mu}\phi_{0}|\phi_{0}|^{2}\right)+\nabla_{\mu}\left(\partial^{\mu}\phi_{0}|\partial\phi_{0}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\bar{\phi}_{0}(\partial\phi_{0})^{2}+\nabla_{\mu}\left(\partial^{\mu}\phi_{0}|\partial\phi_{0}|^{2}\right)\,.

(103)

The asymptotic coefficients
\alpha(\tau)
and
\beta(\tau)
as well as the function

\displaystyle\qquad\eta

\displaystyle=\eta_{0}+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\lambda}}\eta_{1}+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{-1})\,,

Motivic real topological Hochschild spectrum
Doosung Park

Abstract
. We define real topological Hochschild homology of separated log schemes with involutions. We show that real topological Hochschild homology is ( ℙ n , ℙ n − 1 ) (\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathbb{P}^{n-1}) -invariant, which leads to the definition of the motivic real topological Hochschild spectrum living in a certain ℤ / 2 {\mathbb{Z}/2} -equivariant logarithmic motivic category. We explore properties of real topological Hochschild homology that can be deduced from the logarithmic motivic homotopy theory. We also define the motivic real topological cyclic spectrum.

1.
Introduction

Topological Hochschild homology
\mathrm{THH}
and its cousin
\mathrm{TC}
have a deep connection with algebraic
K
-theory via the cyclotomic trace of Bökstedt-Hsiang-Madsen
[
8
]
.
The Dundas-Goodwillie-McCarthy theorem
[
11
]
provided a computational tool for algebraic
K
-theory.
It was also discovered that
\mathrm{THH}
contains arithmetic data.
Bhatt, Morrow, and Scholze
[
3
]
studied filtrations on the
S^{1}
-homotopy fixed point spectrum
\mathrm{TC}^{-}
of
\mathrm{THH}
,
whose graded pieces are closely related to the prismatic cohomology of Bhatt-Scholze according to
[
4
, §13]
.

Hesselholt and Madsen
[
15
]
defined real algebraic
K
-theory,
which refines both algebraic and hermitian
K
-theories in a uniform manner via equivariant homotopy theory.
They also defined real topological Hochschild homology
\mathrm{THR}
of rings.
For further developments on
\mathrm{THR}
,
we refer to
[
20
]
,
[
10
]
, and
[
27
]
.
A forthcoming work of Harpaz, Nikolaus, and Shah will show a real refinement of the Dundas-Goodwillie-McCarthy theorem,
which would justify the real trace method as a computational tool for real or hermitian
K
-theory.

One feature of
\mathrm{THH}
different from algebraic
K
-theory is that
\mathrm{THH}
is not
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant even for regular schemes,
i.e.,
the induced map
\mathrm{THH}(X)\to\mathrm{THH}(X\times\mathbb{A}^{1})
is not an equivalence of spectra for every nonempty scheme
X
.
The definition of
\mathrm{THH}
of schemes is due to Geisser and Hesselholt
[
13
]
.
Hence the motivic methods in
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy theory initiated by Morel and Voevodsky
[
23
]
are not directly applicable to
\mathrm{THH}
.

Non
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariance of
\mathrm{THH}
is related to the fact that the sequence of spectra

\mathrm{THH}(Z)\xrightarrow{i_{*}}\mathrm{THH}(X)\xrightarrow{j^{*}}\mathrm{THH}(X-Z)

is not a fiber sequence when
i\colon Z\to X
is a closed immersion of regular schemes and
j\colon X-Z\to X
is its open complement.
On the other hand,
the localization sequence in algebraic
K
-theory can be read as the fiber sequence of spectra

\mathrm{K}(Z)\xrightarrow{i_{*}}\mathrm{K}(X)\xrightarrow{j^{*}}\mathrm{K}(X-Z).

Hesselholt-Madsen
[
16
]
and Rognes-Sagave-Schlichtkrull
[
30
]
studied localization sequences for
\mathrm{THH}
in the logarithmic setting.

The author’s joint work with Binda and Østvær
[
7
]
introduced the
\infty
-category of logarithmic motivic spectra
\mathrm{logSH}(S)
for fs log schemes
S
,
which aims to incorporate various non
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant cohomology theories into the motivic framework using logarithmic geometry.
We refer to Ogus’s book
[
25
]
for logarithmic geometry.
For a closed immersion of schemes
Z\to X
,
let
(X,Z)
denote the log scheme with the underlying scheme
X
and with the compactifying log structure associated with the open immersion
X-Z\to X
.
In the construction of
\mathrm{logSH}(S)
when
S
is a scheme (with the trivial log structure),
the interval
\mathbb{A}^{1}
is replaced with the set of log schemes
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathbb{P}^{n-1})
for all integers
n\geq 1
,
and the Nisnevich topology is replaced with the strict Nisnevich topology in
[
6
]
.
To construct a motivic spectrum in
\mathrm{logSH}(S)
that represents an existing cohomology theory of schemes,
one can take the following two steps:

Step 1.

Extend the cohomology theory to fs log schemes.

Step 2.

Show that the extension is
(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathbb{P}^{n-1})
-invariant for all integers
n\geq 1
and satisfies strict Nisnevich descent.

Rognes
[
29
]
defines
\mathrm{THH}
of log rings.
According to
[
7
, §8]
,
it is possible to define
\mathrm{THH}
and
\mathrm{TC}
of log schemes based on Rognes’ definition and to construct a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-spectrum
\mathbf{THH}
and
\mathbf{TC}
(written as
\mathbf{logTHH}
and
\mathbf{logTC}
in loc. cit.) representing
\mathrm{THH}
and
\mathrm{TC}
.
The author’s joint work with Binda, Lundemo, and Østvær
[
5
, §8]
constructed a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-spectrum representing Hochschild homology.

In this article,
we employ this strategy for
\mathrm{THR}
.
We define
\mathrm{THR}
of log rings with involutions,
where an involution means an automorphism
\sigma
such that
\sigma\circ\sigma=\mathrm{id}
.
With the aid of the author’s joint work with Hornbostel

We also consider real topological cyclic homology,

2.
Dihedral replete bar constructions

Rognes
[
29
, Definition 3.16]
defined the replete bar construction of a commutative monoid,
which is a key input to define topological Hochschild homology of log rings.
In this section,
we discuss a dihedral refinement of this construction.

We refer to
[
18
, Example 4.1.3]
for a review of real simplicial sets and dihedral sets,
which are defined using crossed simplicial groups of Fiedorowicz-Loday
[
12
]
.
A
real simplicial set

X
is a simplicial set equipped with involutions
w_{q}\colon X_{q}\to X_{q}
(i.e., an automorphism such that
w_{q}\circ w_{q}=\mathrm{id}
) for all simplicial degrees
q
satisfying
the relations

d_{i}w_{q}=w_{q-1}d_{q-i},\text{ }s_{i}w_{q}=w_{q+1}s_{q-i}

for
0\leq i\leq q
.
Note that a real simplicial set is different from a simplicial set with involution, i.e., a simplicial object in the category of sets with involution.
A
dihedral simplicial set

X
is a real simplicial set equipped with automorphisms
t_{q}\colon X_{q}\to X_{q}
for all simplicial degrees
q
satisfying certain relations.

Let
\oplus
denote the coproduct in the category of commutative monoids.
For commutative monoids
P
and
Q
,
the coproduct
P\oplus Q
is naturally isomorphic to the product
P\times Q
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Delta_{\sigma}^{1}
be the real simplicial set whose underlying simplicial set is
\Delta^{1}
and whose involution
w\colon(\Delta^{1})_{q}\to(\Delta^{1})_{q}
in simplicial degree
q
sends the
q
-simplex
a_{0}\cdots a_{q}
to
(1-a_{0})\cdots(1-a_{q})
.
For a commutative monoid
P
with involution
w
,
let
P\otimes\Delta_{\sigma}^{1}
be the real simplicial set whose underlying simplicial set is the tensor product
P\otimes\Delta^{1}
defined by the formula

(P\otimes\Delta^{1})_{q}:=\bigoplus_{i\in(\Delta^{1})_{q}}P

and whose involution is given by
(x_{0},\ldots,x_{q+1})\to(w(x_{q+1}),\ldots,w(x_{0}))
in simplicial degree
q
,
where the indices
0,1,\ldots,q+1
correspond to the
q
-simplicies
0\cdots 00,0\cdots 01,\ldots,1\cdots 11
in
\Delta^{1}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
P
be a commutative monoid with involution.
Then the map of real simplicial sets

P\otimes\Delta_{\sigma}^{1}\to P

given by
(x_{0},\ldots,x_{q})\mapsto x_{0}+\cdots+x_{q}
in simplicial degree
q
is a
{\mathbb{Z}/2}
-weak equivalence.

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{sd}_{\sigma}
be the Segal subdivision functor
[
33
]
.
We have an isomorphism of simplicial sets with involutions

\mathrm{sd}_{\sigma}(\Delta_{\sigma}^{1})\simeq\Delta^{1}\amalg_{\{1\}}\Delta^{1}

where the involution on the right-hand side switches the factors.
This induces an isomorphism of simplicial sets with involutions

\mathrm{sd}_{\sigma}(P\otimes\Delta_{\sigma}^{1})\simeq(P\otimes\Delta^{1})\oplus_{P}(P\otimes\Delta^{1}),

where the involution on the right-hand side is obtained by the formula
(x,y)\mapsto(w(y),w(x))
with
w
in Definition
2.1
.
Since
\Delta^{1}
is contractible,
the map
P\to P\otimes\Delta^{1}
induced by
\{1\}\to\Delta^{1}
is a homotopy equivalence.
This yields a
{\mathbb{Z}/2}
-homotopy equivalence

P\xrightarrow{\simeq}(P\otimes\Delta^{1})\oplus_{P}(P\otimes\Delta^{1}),

which implies the claim.
∎

from the category of commutative rings with involutions to
\mathrm{NAlg}_{\mathbb{Z}/2}
preserves filtered colimits since the Eilenberg-MacLane functor preserves filtered colimits,

and

preserve colimits, and

is the coproduct.
It follows that the functor
(A,P)\mapsto\mathrm{THR}(A,P)
from the category of commutative rings with involutions to
\mathrm{NAlg}_{\mathbb{Z}/2}
preserves filtered colimits.
The forgetful functor
\mathrm{NAlg}_{\mathbb{Z}/2}\to\mathrm{Sp}_{\mathbb{Z}/2}
preserves filtered colimits as observed in
[
18
, §1]
,
which finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.2

P_{J}:=\bigcap_{j\in J}P_{j}
.
By convention,
we have

P_{\emptyset}:=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
There is a canonical equivalence

\mathrm{THR}(U_{I})\simeq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{S}[P_{[n]-I}])&\text{if $0\in I$},\\
\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{S}[P_{[n]-I}],P_{[n]-I}\cap F_{0})&\text{if $0\notin I$}.\end{array}\right.

Each
P_{J}
is isomorphic to products of finite copies of
\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{N}
.
The computations of
\mathrm{B}^{\mathrm{di}}\mathbb{N}
and
\mathrm{B}^{\mathrm{di}}\mathbb{Z}
in
[

, (4.12), Propositions 4.2.11, 4.2.12]
and the fact that
\mathrm{B}^{\mathrm{di}}
preserves finite products
[
18
, Proposition 4.2.4]
yield a natural equivalence of
{\mathbb{Z}/2}
-spectra

\mathrm{THR}(U_{I})\simeq\mathbb{S}[\Phi(I)]

with

\displaystyle\Phi(I):=\coprod_{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\Phi(I;(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})),

\displaystyle\Phi(I;(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Phi_{1}(I;x_{1})\times\cdots\times\Phi_{n}(I;x_{n})&\text{if $(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in P_{[n]-I}$},\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\Phi_{i}(I;x_{i}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}*&\text{if $x_{i}=0$ and $i\in I$},\\
S^{\sigma}&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Since there is a natural decomposition

\mathrm{tcofib}(\Phi)\simeq\coprod_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\mathrm{tcofib}(\Phi(-;x)),

it suffices to show
\mathrm{tcofib}(\Phi(-;x))\simeq 0
for every
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

If
x\neq(0,\ldots,0)
,
then there exists
i\in[n]
such that
x\notin P_{i}
.
The induced map
\Phi(I;x)\to\Phi(I\cup\{i\};x)

is a
{\mathbb{Z}/2}
-homeomorphism whenever
i\notin I
.
Together with the categorical result
[
7
, Proposition A.6.5]
,
we have
\mathrm{tcofib}(\Phi(-;x))\simeq 0
.

If
x=(0,\ldots,0)
,
then the induced map
\Phi(I;x)\to\Phi(I\cup\{0\};x)
is a
{\mathbb{Z}/2}
-homeomorphism whenever
0\notin I
.
We have
\mathrm{tcofib}(\Phi(-;x))\simeq 0
similarly.
∎

On short Edgeworth expansions for weighted sums of random vectors
S. A. Ayvazyan

Abstract
The "typical" asymptotic behavior of the weighted sums of independent, identically distibuted random vectors in k k -dimensional space is considered. It is shown that under finitnes of fifth absolute moment of an individual term the rate of convergence by Edgeworth correction in the multivariate central limit theorem is of order O ⁡ ( 1 / n 3 / 2 ) O(1/n^{3/2}) . This extends the one-dimensional Bobkov(2020) result.



Ключевые слова:

Edgeworth correction, standart Gaussian random vector, multivariate central limit theorem.

1
Introduction and Main Result

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n}
be independent, identically distributed random vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
with finite third absolute moment
\beta_{3}=\mathbb{E}\|X_{1}\|^{3}<\infty
, zero mean
\mathbb{E}X_{1}={0}
and unit covariance matrix
\mathrm{Cov}(X_{1})=I
.
Let
\phi(x)
be the density function of standard Gaussian random vector in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
with zero mean and unit covariance matrix.
Denote by
\mathfrak{B}
, the class of all Borel convex sets in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.
Sazonov
[
3
]
obtained the following error bound of approximation for distribution of the normalized sum of random vectors by the standard multivariate normal law:

\sup\limits_{B\in\mathfrak{B}}\left|\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\in B\right)-\int_{B}\phi(x)dx)\right|\leqslant C(k)\frac{\beta_{3}}{\sqrt{n}},

(1.1)

where
C(k)
depends on dimension
k
only.

The bound (
1.1
) is optimal one in general. Moreover, the rate
O(1/\sqrt{n})
can not be improved under higher order moment assumptions. This is easy to show in one-dimensional case
k=1
taking
X
such that

\mathbb{P}(X_{1}=1)=\mathbb{P}(X_{1}=-1)=1/2.

However, the situation is different when we consider a weighted sum

\theta_{1}X_{1}+\dots+\theta_{n}X_{n},

where
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta_{j}^{2}=1
. If we are interested in
the typical behavior of these sums for most of
\theta
in the sense of the normalized Lebesgue measure
\lambda_{n-1}
on the unit sphere

S^{n-1}=\{(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}):\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta_{j}^{2}=1\},

then we have to refer to a recent remarkable result due to Klartag and Sodin
[
4
]
. In
[
4
]
they have showed that in one-dimensional case
k=1
for any
\rho:1>\rho>0,
there exists a set
\mathbb{Q}\subseteq S^{n-1}:\lambda_{n-1}(\mathbb{Q})>1-\rho,
and a constant
C(\rho)
depending on
\rho
only such that for any
\theta=(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n})\in\mathbb{Q}
one has

\sup\limits_{a,b\in\mathbb{R},a<b}\left|\mathbb{P}\left(a\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{i}X_{i}\leqslant b\right)-\int_{a}^{b}\phi(x)dx\right|\leqslant C(\rho)\frac{\beta_{4}}{n},

(1.2)

where
\beta_{4}=\mathbb{E}|X_{1}|^{4}
and
C(\rho)\leqslant C\log^{2}(\frac{1}{\rho})
with some absolute constant
C
. It is clear that
C(\rho)\to\infty
as
\rho
tends to
0
. And the case of equal weights, that is when
\theta_{i}=1/\sqrt{n}
for all
i=1,\dots,n
is the worst case in the sense of closeness of distribution function of weighted sum to the standard normal distribution function. The result was extended to multidimensional case in
[
5
]
.

For independent, identically distributed random variables Bobkov
[
6
]
refined the rates of approximation for distributions of weighted sums (
1.2
) up to order
O(n^{-3/2})
by using the Edgeworth correction of the fourth order provided
\beta_{5}=\mathbb{E}|X_{1}|^{5}<\infty
.
The following corrected normal "distribution"  function was introduced:

G(x)=\Phi(x)-\frac{\beta_{4}-3}{8n}(x^{3}-3x)\phi(x),

where
\Phi(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\phi(y)dy
. Using
\mathbb{E}_{\theta}
to denote the integral over the normalized Lebesgue measure on the unit sphere
S^{n-1}
, Bobkov proved, that if the third moment
\mathbb{E}X_{1}^{3}=0
, then the rate of convergence by
G(x)
can be improved

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\sup_{x}\Big|P\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{i}X_{i}\leqslant x\Big)-G(x)\Big|\leqslant C\frac{\beta_{5}}{n^{3/2}},

(1.3)

where
C
is a universal constant.

In multidimensional case for a given positive integer vector
\alpha
we define
\alpha
-moment of random vector
X_{1}
as
\mu_{\alpha}=\mathbb{E}X_{1}^{\alpha}
and determine density
g(x)
of corrected normal "distribution":

\phi(x)+\frac{3}{n}\phi(x)\Big(-\frac{1}{24}\Big[(\mu_{4,0,..,0}-3)(3-6x_{1}^{2}+x_{1}^{4})+\dots+(\mu_{0,0,..,4}-3)(3-6x_{k}^{2}+x_{k}^{4})\Big]

-\frac{1}{6}\Big[\mu_{3,1,..,0}(x_{1}^{3}x_{2}-3x_{1}x_{2})+\dots+\mu_{0,..,1,3}(x_{k}^{3}x_{k-1}-3x_{k}x_{k-1})\Big]

-\frac{1}{4}\Big[(\mu_{2,2,..,0}-1)(1-x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2}+x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2})+\dots+(\mu_{0,..,2,2}-1)(1-x_{k}^{2}-x_{k-1}^{2}+x_{k-1}^{2}x_{k}^{2})\Big]

-\frac{1}{2}\Big[\mu_{2,1,1,..,0}(x_{1}^{2}x_{2}x_{3}-x_{2}x_{3})+\dots+\mu_{0,..,1,1,2}(x_{k}^{2}x_{k-1}x_{k-2}-x_{k-1}x_{k-2})\Big]

-\Big[\mu_{1,1,1,1,..,0}(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{4})+\dots+\mu_{0,..,1,1,1,1}(x_{k-3}x_{k-2}x_{k-1}x_{k})\Big]\Big).

This work examines the rate of convergence of a weighted sum of independent, identically distributed random vectors to a multivariate standard normal vector by Edgeworth correction. The main achievement of the work is the extension of Bobkov’s result (
1.3
) to the multidimensional case using the signed measure
G(B)=\int_{B}g(x)dx
, presented in the following theorem:

Theorem 1

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n}
be independent, identically distributed random vectors of dimension
k
with zero mean
\mathbb{E}X_{1}=\overline{0}
, unit covariance matrix
\mathrm{Cov}(X_{1})=I
and finite fifth absolute moments
\beta_{5}=\mathbb{E}\|X_{1}\|^{5}
. Denote by
\mathfrak{B}
the class of all convex Borel sets. If for any positive integer vector
\alpha
with
|\alpha|=3
holds
\mathbb{E}X_{1}^{\alpha}=0
, then one has

\mathbb{E}_{\theta}\sup_{B\in\mathfrak{B}}\Big|P\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{i}X_{i}\in B\Big)-G(B)\Big|\leqslant C(k)\frac{\beta_{5}}{n^{3/2}},

(1.4)

where
C(k)
is a universal constant depending only on the dimension
k.

Notation and auxiliary results

Notation

In the following the weighting coefficients
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{n}
belong to the unit sphere
S^{n-1}=\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{n}:\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta_{j}^{2}=1\}.
For a given random vector
Z
in
R^{k}
, a positive integer vector
\alpha
and a positive integer
s

\alpha
-moment as
\mu_{\alpha}(Z)=\mathbb{E}Z^{\alpha}
and the absolute moment of order
s

\rho_{s}(Z)=\mathbb{E}\|Z\|^{s}
.
Consider a sequence of independent, not necessarily identically distributed, random vectors
X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n}
with zero mean and unit covariance matrix. And introduce the following truncated random vectors
Y_{j}
and
Z_{j}
as

Y_{j}=X_{j}\mathbb{I}(\|\theta_{j}X_{j}\|\leqslant 1),\;\;Z_{j}=Y_{j}-\mathbb{E}Y_{j},\;j=1\dots n,

(2.1)

where
\mathbb{I}(A)
is the indicator function of event
A
.
Truncated random vectors have an absolute moment of any order, however, their moments may not coincide with the moments of
X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n}
. Therefore, we introduce

A_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta_{j}\mathbb{E}Y_{j},\;\;D=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta_{j}^{2}\mathrm{Cov}(Z_{j}).

(2.2)

Also below we denote for
j=1\dots n
,
|\alpha|\geqslant 1
and
s\geqslant 1

\mu_{\alpha,j}=\mu_{\alpha}(X_{j}),\;\;\rho_{s,j}=\rho_{s}(X_{j}),

(2.3)

\tilde{\mu}_{\alpha,j}=\mu_{\alpha}(Z_{j}),\;\;\tilde{\rho}_{s,j}=\rho_{s}(Z_{j}),

(2.4)

\rho_{s}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\rho_{s}(\theta_{i}X_{i}),\;\;\beta_{s}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\rho_{s}(X_{i}),

(2.5)

\tilde{\rho}_{s}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\rho_{s}(\theta_{j}Z_{j}),\;\;\eta_{s}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\rho_{s}(Q\theta_{j}Z_{j}),

(2.6)

where matrix
Q^{2}=D^{-1}
.

To derive the result (
1.4
), the author is using the technique of proving the multivariate central limit theorem from the monograph by Bhattacharya and Ranga Rao
[
7
]
, as well as the further development of methods and approaches proposed by Klartagam, Sodin
[
4
]
and Bobkov
[
6
]
. The proof relies on the use of characteristic functions. This method is based on the Fourier transform and its extension, the Fourier-Stieltjes transform. The characteristic function of a random vector
Z
is defined as

\varphi(t)=\mathbb{E}\exp(i\langle t,Z\rangle),

where
i
is a unit imaginary number
Next, we define the logarithm of a non-zero complex number
z=r\exp(i\xi)
as

\log(z)=\log(r)+i\xi,\;r>0,\;\;\xi\in(-\pi,\pi],

therefore, we always take the so-called main branch of the logarithm. Let
Z
have an absolute moment of order
m
, then in the neighborhood of zero the logarithm of the characteristic function expands into a Taylor series

\log(\varphi(t))=\sum_{1\leqslant|\nu|\leqslant m}\kappa_{\nu}(Z)\frac{(it)^{\nu}}{\nu!}+o(\|t\|^{m}),

(2.7)

where
\kappa_{\nu}(Z)
is the cumulant of the random vector
Z
of order
\nu
. The following formal identity allows us to express cumulants in terms of
corresponding moments by equating the coefficients of
t^{\nu}
on both sides (see Chapter 2, Section 6 in
[
7
]
)

\sum_{\nu\geqslant 1}\kappa_{\nu}(Z)\frac{(it)^{\nu}}{\nu!}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{s+1}\frac{1}{s}\Big(\sum_{\nu\geqslant 1}\mu_{\nu}(Z)\frac{(it)^{\nu}}{\nu!}\Big)^{s}.

(2.8)

From the identity (
2.8
) and the inequality
|\mu_{\nu}(Z)|\leqslant\rho_{|\nu|}(Z)
, it follows that for the absolute value of the cumulants one has

|\kappa_{\nu}(Z)|\leqslant c(\nu)\rho_{|\nu|}(Z),

(2.9)

where
c(\nu)
is a constant that depends only on the vector
\nu
. Let us introduce the following notation for
j=1\dots n,

|\nu|>1

\tilde{\kappa}_{\nu,j}=\kappa_{\nu}(Z_{j}),\;\;\kappa_{\nu,j}=\kappa_{\nu}(X_{j}),

\tilde{\kappa}_{\nu}=\kappa_{\nu}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta_{j}Z_{j}\Big),\;\;\kappa_{\nu}=\kappa_{\nu}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta_{j}X_{j}\Big).

(2.10)

Since we are considering independent random variables, the characteristic function of the sum
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta_{j}Z_{j}
is calculated as

\hat{F}_{Z}(t)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\varphi_{j}(\theta_{j}t),

(2.11)

where

\varphi_{j}(t)=\mathbb{E}\exp(i\langle t,Z_{j}\rangle),\;j=1,\dots,n.

(2.12)

If we take
Z=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta_{j}Z_{j}
, then from (
2.7
) and (
2.11
) an important property of the cumulants of the sum of random vectors follows (similarly, if
Z=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta_{j}X_{j}
)

\tilde{\kappa}_{\nu}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta_{j}^{|\nu|}\tilde{\kappa}_{\nu,j}\;\;\Big(\kappa_{\nu}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta_{j}^{|\nu|}\kappa_{\nu,j}\Big).

(2.13)

We need to introduce a multivariate normal distribution
\Phi_{a,V}
with mean
a
and covariance matrix
V
, whose density is given by

\phi_{a,V}(x)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{k}{2}}(\mathrm{det}V)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\langle x-a,V^{-1}(x-a)\rangle\Big),

(2.14)

where
a\in R^{k}
and
V
is a symmetric positive definite matrix
k\times k
. If
a=\overline{0}
and
V=I
, then by
\Phi(x)
and
\phi(x)
we mean
\Phi_{\overline{0},I}(x)
and
\phi_{\overline{0},I}(x)
, respectively. The characteristic function of the normal distribution is known to be equal to

\hat{\phi}_{a,V}(t)=\exp\Big(i\langle t,a\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\langle t,Vt\rangle\Big).

(2.15)

For improving the rate of convergence in (
1.1
) the distribution
of the normal law should be corrected in some smooth way.
In order to do this, we use the expansion of the characteristic function in terms of cumulants and to recover the density through the inverse Fourier transform. For a random vector
Z
, we introduce the polynomials
\hat{P}_{r}(t:\{\kappa_{\nu}(Z)\})\;,r\geqslant 0,
from the formal expression

1+\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}\hat{P}_{r}(t:\{\kappa_{\nu}(Z)\})u^{r}=\exp\Big(\sum_{|\nu|=3}^{\infty}\frac{\kappa_{\nu}(Z)}{\nu!}u^{|\nu|-2}\Big),

(2.16)

If we assume that
\mathbb{E}Z=\overline{0}
and
\mathrm{Cov}(Z)=V
, then, as shown in Lemma 7.2,
[
7
]
the function
\hat{P}_{r}(it:\{\kappa_{\nu}(Z)\})\hat{\phi}_{\overline{0},V}(t)

Partitioned difference families and harmonious linear spaces
Marco Buratti, Dieter Jungnickel

Abstract
We say that a linear space is harmonious if it is resolvable and admits an automorphism group acting sharply transitively on the points and transitively on the parallel classes. Generalizing old results by the first author et al. we present some difference methods to construct harmonious linear spaces. We prove, in particular, that for any finite non-singleton subset K K of ℤ + \mathbb{Z}^{+} there are infinitely many values of v v for which there exists a partitioned difference family that is the base parallel class of a harmonious linear space with v v points whose block sizes are precisely the elements of K K .

1
Introduction

We recall that a
linear space
S
(2,K,v)
is a pair
(V,{\cal B})
where
V
is a set of
v
“points” and
\cal B
is
a set of subsets of
V
called
blocks
with sizes belonging to
K
and the property that
any 2-subset of
V
is contained in exactly one block. A S
(2,K,v)
is
mandatory
if the set of block sizes is precisely
K
.
Throughout this paper, speaking of a S
(2,K,v)
it will be tacitly understood that it is mandatory.
When
K
is a singleton
\{k\}
one speaks of a

Steiner 2-design
and writes S
(2,k,v)
(see, e.g.,
[
2
]
).

A
resolution
of a linear space is a partition
\cal R
of its block set into classes (
parallel classes
)
each of which is, in its turn, a partition of the point set. A linear space is
resolvable
if it admits at
least one resolution. Speaking of a
resolved
linear space one means a triple
(V,{\cal B},{\cal R})

where
(V,{\cal B})
is a linear space and
\cal R
is a specific resolution of it.
An automorphism group of such a resolved linear space is a subgroup of
Sym(V)

leaving
\cal R
(and consequently
\cal B
) invariant.

The construction of a resolvable Steiner 2-design is not easy in general. Suffice it to say that for any fixed
k\geq 5
the set of values of
v
for which
there is a resolvable S
(2,k,v)
is still unknown.
Anyway it was proved in
[
26
]
(see also
[
2
]
, p. 801)
that a resolvable S
(2,k,v)
certainly exists if
v
is admissible – which means
v\equiv k
(mod
k(k-1)
) – and “sufficiently large”.
As far as we are aware the analogous result for general linear spaces is still lacking.

We propose the following definitions.

Definition 1.1
.

A
harmonious
linear space S
(2,K,v)
, briefly a HS
(2,K,v)
, is a resolved S
(2,K,v)
with an automorphism group
acting sharply transitively on the points and transitively on the parallel classes.

An
almost harmonious
linear space S
(2,K,v)
, briefly an AHS
(2,K,v)
, is a resolved S
(2,K,v)
with an automorphism group
acting sharply transitively on all but one point and transitively on the parallel classes.

Example 1.2
.

Here is the resolution
{\cal R}=\{{\cal P}_{0},{\cal P}_{1},{\cal P}_{2},{\cal P}_{3}\}
of a HS
(2,\{2,3\},8)
with point set
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
:

{\cal P}_{0}=\bigl\{\{0,4\},\ \{1,6,7\},\ \{5,2,3\}\bigl\};

{\cal P}_{1}=\bigl\{\{1,5\},\ \{2,7,0\},\ \{6,3,4\}\bigl\};

{\cal P}_{2}=\bigl\{\{2,6\},\ \{3,0,1\},\ \{7,4,5\}\bigl\};

{\cal P}_{3}=\bigl\{\{3,7\},\ \{4,1,2\},\ \{0,5,6\}\bigl\}.

Indeed it is readily seen that we have
{\cal P}_{i}+1={\cal P}_{i+1\ (mod\ 4)}
, hence
the group
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}_{8}
of translations modulo 8 acts sharply transitively on
\mathbb{Z}_{8}
and
transitively on
\cal R
.

If in the above example we add an extra point, say
\infty
, to the blocks of size 2 we get an AHS
(2,3,9)
.

It is easy to see that a Steiner 2-design cannot be harmonious. On the other hand, several classes of almost
harmonious Steiner 2-designs have been constructed in
[
11
,
13
]
.
In particular, it was proved in
[
11
]
that for any integer
k
there are infinitely many values of
v

for which there exists an almost harmonious S
(2,k,v)
.

In this paper we give some constructions for harmonious linear spaces and, as main result, we prove
that for any finite non-singleton set
K
of positive integers, there are infinitely many values of
v
for which there
exists a HS
(2,K,v)
.

The starter parallel class of each of our harmonious linear spaces is a

partitioned difference family
, that is a partition of a group in blocks
whose lists of differences cover every non-identity element of the group a constant number of times.
Thus, as a consequence of our main result, for any finite non-singleton
set
K
of positive integers there are infinitely many values of
v
for which there exists
a partitioned difference family in a group of order
v
with set of block sizes equal to
K
.

2
Use of difference families

The
list of differences
of a multisubset
B=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{k}\}
of an additive group
G

is the multiset
\Delta B=\{b_{i}-b_{j}\ |\ 1\leq i,j\leq k;i\neq j\}
.
More generally,
if
\cal F
is a collection of multisubsets of
G
, its list of differences is the multiset union
of the lists of differences of all its members:

\Delta{\cal F}:=\bigcup_{B\in{\cal F}}\Delta B
.
The multiset union of all the members of
{\cal F}
will be called the

flatten
of
\cal F
.

If
\Delta{\cal F}
is
\lambda
times
G\setminus H
with
H

a subgroup of
G
, then
{\cal F}
is a
difference family in
G
relative to
H
with index
\lambda
.
In this case one briefly says that
\cal F
is a
(G,H,K,\lambda)
-DF where
K
denotes the multiset of all sizes of the members of
\cal F
.

If
\Delta{\cal F}
is
\lambda
times all of
G
(zero included) then
{\cal F}
is a
(G,K,\lambda)

strong difference family
(SDF) in
G
and we briefly say that it is a
(G,K,\lambda)
-SDF with
K
defined as above.
Note that the index of a SDF is necessarily even.

The members of a DF or SDF are called
blocks
.
It is evident that every block of a difference family of index 1 is a set, not a multiset. In contrast at least one block of a SDF has some repeated elements.
A DF or SDF whose blocks have all the same size
k
is said to be
uniform
and one usually writes

(G,H,k,\lambda)
-DF or
(G,k,\lambda)
-SDF, respectively.

Speaking of a
(G,K,\lambda)
-DF (without the adjective “strong”) one usually means a

(G,H,K,\lambda)
-DF with
H=\{0\}
. A difference family with only one block is called a
difference set

[
22
]
.
A
(G,K,\lambda)
-DF whose blocks partition
G

(or, equivalently, whose flatten is
G
) is said to be
partitioned
(PDF).

Partitioned difference families have been introduced by Ding and Ying
[
19
]
in view of
their useful applications to
constant composition codes
.
The equivalent notion of a
zero difference balanced function
(ZDBF) was introduced a few years
later by Ding
[
18
]
. As pointed out in
[
7
,
8
]
, this double terminology unfortunately caused some
confusion. Indeed several authors presented some seemingly new ZDBFs which,
as a matter of fact, could be seen as PDFs known since a long time (see also
[
5
]
).

The following result is very well known.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of order
u
of a group
G
of order
v
, and let
\cal F
be a
(G,H,K^{\prime},1)
-DF.
Then

\{B+g\ |\ B\in{\cal F};g\in G\}\ \cup\ \{\mbox{right cosets of $H$ in $G$}\}

is the block set of a S
(2,K,v)
where
K
is the underlying set of
K^{\prime}\ \cup\ \{u\}
.

In
[
13
]
, a
(G,H,k,1)
-DF has been called
resolvable
if its flatten is a complete system
of representatives for the non-trivial left cosets of
H
in
G
.
Here, we extend this notion also to non-uniform DF’s.

Definition 2.2
.

A
(G,H,K,1)
-DF is
resolvable
if its flatten is a complete system of representatives
for the non-trivial left cosets of
H
in
G
.

Throughout the paper we agree to use the following notation.

Notation 2.3
.

Multisets will be often denoted in “exponential notation”. Hence, writing
K=\{k_{1}^{\mu_{1}},...,k_{t}^{\mu_{t}}\}
we mean
the multiset with underlying set
\{k_{1},...,k_{t}\}
where each
k_{i}
has multiplicity
\mu_{i}
. The exponent
\mu_{i}
will be omitted
when it is equal to
1
. If
n
is a positive integer and
K
is a multiset, then
\underline{n}K
denotes
the multiset union of
n
copies of
K
. Thus, given
K
as above, we have
\underline{n}K=\{k_{1}^{\mu_{1}n},...,k_{t}^{\mu_{t}n}\}
.
The multiset consisting of the zero element of an additive group repeated a certain number
n
of times, that is

\underline{n}\{0\}=\{0^{n}\}
, will be denoted by
O_{n}
.

The proof of the “only if” part of Theorem 1 in
[
13
]
can be easily adapted to obtain the following.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of order
u
of an additive group
G
of order
v
,
and let
\cal F
be a resolvable
(G,H,K^{\prime},1)
-DF. Then
{\cal P}=\{B+h\ |\ B\in{\cal F},h\in H\}\ \cup\ \{H\}

is a
(G,\underline{u}K^{\prime}\ \cup\ \{u\},u)
-PDF which is the base parallel class of a HS
(2,K,v)
where
K

is the underlying set of
K^{\prime}\cup\{u\}
.

Proof.

We have
\Delta{\cal F}=G\setminus H
by definition of a
(G,H,K^{\prime},1)
-DF.
Of course, for any
B\in{\cal F}
and any
h\in H
we have
\Delta(B+h)=\Delta B
.
Hence the list of differences of
\{B+h\ |\ B\in{\cal F},h\in H\}
is
|H|
times
G\setminus H
:

\Delta\{B+h\ |\ B\in{\cal F},h\in H\}=\underline{u}(G\setminus H).

Also, it is evident that
\Delta H=\underline{u}(H\setminus\{0\})
. The last two equalities give

\Delta{\cal P}=\underline{u}(G\setminus\{0\})
, i.e.,
{\cal P}
is a DF in
G
of index
u
.

The fact that
\cal F
is resolvable implies that the flatten of
\{B+h\ |\ B\in{\cal F},h\in H\}

is precisely
G\setminus H
and then the flatten of
\cal P
is precisely
G
. We conclude that
{\cal P}
is
a
(G,\underline{u}K^{\prime}\ \cup\ \{u\},u)
-PDF.

Let
G_{\cal P}
be the stabilizer of
\cal P
under the natural right action of
G
.
It is obvious that
H\subset G_{\cal P}
.
Let us prove the reverse inclusion. Assume that
g\in G_{\cal P}\setminus H
. In this case

H+g\in{\cal P}
(since
H
is a member of
\cal P
) but
H+g\neq H
. Thus we have
H+g=B+h
for a suitable pair
(B,h)\in{\cal F}\times H
.
It follows that
\Delta(H+g)=\Delta(B+h)
, hence
\Delta H=\Delta B
. Considering that
\Delta B
is disjoint with

H
and that every element of
\Delta H
is in
H
, we infer that
H=\{0\}
, that
is the case where
\Delta H
is empty. So
\cal P
is a PDF of index 1.
Let
B=\{b,b+g,\dots\}
be the block of
\cal F
such that
g\in\Delta B
.
We have
B+g\in{\cal P}
since
g\in G_{\cal P}
and we see that
b+g\in B\cap(B+g)
so that

B+g=B
since the blocks of
\cal P
are pairwise disjoint.
Hence
g
stabilizes
B
which means that
B
is a union of right cosets of

\langle g\rangle
in
G
. It follows that
\Delta B
contains
\Delta\langle g\rangle
which is
o(g)
times all
the elements of
\langle g\rangle\setminus\{0\}
. This contradicts the fact that
\cal P
has index 1.
We conclude that
G_{\cal P}=H
.
Hence, if
S
is a complete system of representatives for the right cosets of
H
in
G
,
the
G
-orbit of
\cal P
is
\{{\cal P}+s\ |\ s\in S\}
. Now note that
\bigcup_{s\in S}({\cal P}+s)

is precisely the block set of the S
(2,K,v)
generated by
\cal F
(see Lemma
2.1
). Also, given
that
\cal P
is partitioned, it is clear that
{\cal P}+s
is a parallel class of this S
(2,K,v)
for every
s\in S

and then
\{{\cal P}+s\ |\ s\in S\}
is a resolution of it.
The assertion follows.
∎

3
Harmonious strong difference families

Uniform strong difference families have been implicitly used in the literature for many years
and they have been formally introduced for the first time in
[
4
]
. Since then, they have
been explicitly used in many papers
[
3
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
14
,
16
,
17
,
20
,
23
,
28
]
. Non-uniform SDFs have been used in
[
6
]

for the construction of the so-called
Hadamard
partitioned difference families also considered in
[
24
]
.

The following definition is crucial in this paper.

Definition 3.1
.

A
(G,K,\lambda)
-SDF is
harmonious
if its block sizes sum up to
\lambda

(that is, if its flatten has size
\lambda
).

A
(G,k,\lambda)
-SDF with
n
blocks has flatten of size
kn
and we have
\lambda|G|=k(k-1)n
. We deduce that a uniform
(G,k,\lambda)
-SDF
is harmonious if and only if
|G|=k-1
.

Example 3.2
.

If
G
is any additive group of order
k-1
, then
\Sigma=\{O_{k},(G\cup\{0\})^{k}\}

is a harmonious
(G,k,k^{2}+k)
-SDF.

Example 3.3
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}_{9}
. Then

\{0^{3},1\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\{0^{3},1,2^{2},3,4,5,6^{2},7\}

are the blocks of a harmonious
(G,\{4,12\},16)
-SDF. Also,

O_{3}\ \cup\ \{1,4,7\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad O_{3}\ \cup\ G

are the blocks of a harmonious
(G,\{6,12\},18)
-SDF.

Example 3.4
.

Let
G=\{0,1,a,b\}
be one of the two groups of order
4
.
Then

\{O_{3},\ \{0^{2},1,a,b\},\ \{1^{2},a^{2},b^{2}\}\}

is a
harmonious
(G,\{3,5,6\},14)
-SDF and

\{O_{3},\ \{1,a,b\},\ \{0^{3},1,a,b\}^{2}\}

is a harmonious
(G,\{3^{2},6^{2}\},18)
-SDF.

Example 3.5
.

Let
G
be any group of order
k
.
Then
\Sigma=\{O_{k}^{2},\ G^{2k-4},\ \underline{2}G\}
is a harmonious
(G,\{k^{2k-2},2k\},2k^{2})
-SDF.
For instance,

\{\{0,0,0\},\ \{0,0,0\},\ \{0,1,2\},\ \{0,1,2\},\ \{0,0,1,1,2,2\}\}

is a harmonious
(\mathbb{Z}_{3},\{3^{4},6\},18)
-SDF.

In what follows,
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
will denote the finite field of order
q
and its multiplicative
group, respectively.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
\Sigma
be a
(G,K,\lambda)
-SDF and let
q\equiv 1
(mod
\lambda
) be a prime power.
Lift each
B=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{k}\}\in\Sigma
to a subset
\ell(B)=\{(b_{1},c_{1}),\dots,(b_{k},c_{k})\}

of
G\times\mathbb{F}_{q}
and set
{\cal L}=\{\ell(B)\ |\ B\in\Sigma\}
.
By definition of a SDF the list of differences of
\cal L
is of the form

\Delta{\cal L}=\bigcup_{g\in G}\{g\}\times\Delta_{g}

(3.1)

where each
\Delta_{g}
is a list of
\lambda
elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Thus it makes sense to ask whether there exists a suitable
{q-1\over\lambda}
-subset
S
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that

S\cdot\Delta_{g}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\quad\forall\ g\in G.

(3.2)

If the answer is in the affirmative, we say that
S
is
a
good companion
for
\cal L
and call
\cal L
a
good lifting
of
\Sigma
.

Assume that
\Sigma
is harmonious and that
\cal L
is a good lifting of
\Sigma
.
By definition of a harmonious SDF, both the flatten of
\cal L
and its projection
\pi({\cal L})
on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
have size
\lambda
.
Thus it may happen that there is a good companion
S
for
\cal L
satisfying the identity

S\cdot\pi({\cal L})=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}.

(3.3)

In this case we say that
S
is a
perfect companion
for
\cal L
and call
\cal L
a
perfect lifting
of
\Sigma
.

If, for instance, each
\Delta_{g}
and
\pi({\cal L})
are complete systems
of representatives for the cosets of the subgroup
S
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
of index
\lambda
, then a
perfect companion for
\cal L
is just
S
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
G
be a group of order
g
, let
q=\lambda n+1
be a prime power, and let

\Sigma
be a
(G,K,\lambda)
-SDF admitting a good lifting
\cal L
to
G\times\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Then there exists a
(G\times\mathbb{F}_{q},G\times\{0\},\underline{n}K,1)
-DF which is resolvable
if
\Sigma
is harmonious and
\cal L
is perfect.

Proof.

By definition of a good lifting there is a suitable good companion
S
for
\cal L
for which
(
3.1
) and (
3.2
) hold.
For each block
B=\{(b_{1},c_{1}),\dots,(b_{k},c_{k})\}
of
{\cal L}
, and for each
s\in S
,
set
B_{s}=\{(b_{1},sc_{1}),\dots,(b_{k},sc_{k})\}
. Then set
{\cal F}=\{B_{s}\ |\ B\in{\cal L};s\in S\}
. Note
that
\Delta B_{s}
is obtainable from
\Delta B
by multiplying all its elements by
s
. Thus (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) give

\Delta{\cal F}=\bigcup_{g\in G}\{g\}\times(S\cdot\Delta_{g})=\bigcup_{g\in G}\{g\}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}=(G\times\mathbb{F}_{q})\setminus(G\times\{0\})

which means that
\cal F
is a
(G\times\mathbb{F}_{q},G\times\{0\},\underline{n}K,1)
-DF.

Now assume that
\Sigma
is harmonious,
\cal L
is perfect and
S
is a perfect companion for
\cal L
.
In this case the flatten of the difference family
\cal F
constructed above is

S\cdot\pi({\cal L})=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
because (
3.3
) holds.
This means that the flatten of
{\cal F}
is a complete system of representatives for the non-trivial cosets of

G\times\{0\}
in
G\times\mathbb{F}_{q}
, i.e., that
\cal F
is resolvable.
∎

The above theorem was essentially applied in
[
12
]
to construct some infinite series of non-uniform and non-resolvable
relative difference families starting from
(\mathbb{Z}_{g},K,\lambda)
-SDFs with
\lambda=2
or 4 and flatten of size
2g
.
Applying the theorem with the use of the harmonious SDFs of Example
3.2
one can obtain
uniform and resolvable DF’s. This was done in
[
13
]
implicitly (at that time the notion of a SDF had not yet been
introduced) and explicitly in
[
11
]
.

Now note that Theorem
3.7
and Theorem
2.4
immediately give the following.

Corollary 3.8
.

Assume that there exists a harmonious
(G,K^{\prime},\lambda)
-SDF admitting a perfect lifting to
G\times\mathbb{F}_{q}

with
G
of order
g
and
q=\lambda n+1
.
Then there exists a
(G\times\mathbb{F}_{q},\underline{gn}K^{\prime}\ \cup\ \{g\},g)
-PDF which is the base parallel class of a HS
(2,K,gq)
with

K
the underlying set of
K^{\prime}\ \cup\ \{g\}
.

Example 3.9
.

It is straightforward to check that
\Sigma=\{\{0,0\},\ \{0,0\},\ \{0,0,1,1\}\}
is a
(\mathbb{Z}_{2},\{2,2,4\},8)
-SDF.
We try to obtain a perfect lifting of
\Sigma
to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{F}_{17}
of the form

{\cal L}=\bigl\{\{(0,a),(0,-a)\},\ \{(0,b),(0,c)\},\ \{(0,-b),(0,d),(1,-c),(1,-d)\}\bigl\}.

The list of differences of
\cal L
is

\Delta{\cal L}=(\{0\}\times\Delta_{0})\ \cup\ (\{1\}\times\Delta_{1})

with
\Delta_{0}=\{1,-1\}\cdot\Delta^{\prime}_{0}
and
\Delta_{1}=\{1,-1\}\cdot\Delta^{\prime}_{1}

where
\Delta^{\prime}_{0}=\{2a,b-c,b+d,c-d\}
and
\Delta^{\prime}_{1}=\{2d,b-c,b-d,c+d\}
.
Also, the projection of the flatten of
\cal L
on
\mathbb{F}_{17}
is
\pi({\cal L})=\{1,-1\}\cdot\pi^{\prime}
with
\pi^{\prime}=\{a,b,c,d\}
.

Let
C
be the subgroup of
\mathbb{F}_{17}^{*}
of index
4
, that is
C=\{1,4,13,16\}
and assume that

\Delta^{\prime}_{0}
,
\Delta^{\prime}_{1}
,
\pi^{\prime}
are complete systems of
representatives for the cosets of
C
in
\mathbb{F}_{17}^{*}

(3.4)

Thus we are assuming that
C\cdot\Delta^{\prime}_{0}=C\cdot\Delta^{\prime}_{1}=C\cdot\pi^{\prime}=\mathbb{F}_{17}^{*}
.
Observing that
C=S\cdot\{1,-1\}
with
S=\{1,4\}
,
we see that
S
is a perfect companion for
\cal L
, i.e., conditions (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) hold.
Indeed we have

S\cdot\Delta_{i}=S\cdot\{1,-1\}\cdot\Delta^{\prime}_{i}=C\cdot\Delta^{\prime}_{i}=\mathbb{F}_{17}^{*}\quad\mbox{for $i=1,2$};

S\cdot\pi({\cal L})=S\cdot\{1,-1\}\cdot\pi^{\prime}=C\cdot\pi^{\prime}=\mathbb{F}_{17}^{*}

Looking at the cosets of
C
in
\mathbb{F}_{17}^{*}
,

C=\{1,4,13,16\},\quad 2C=\{2,8,9,15\},\quad 3C=\{3,12,5,14\},\quad 6C=\{6,7,10,11\},

it is readily seen that
(2,3,6,4)
is a quadruple satisfying (
3.4
).
Thus

4
Composition constructions

It is worth observing that any perfect lifting of a harmonious SDF automatically gives an
infinite series of harmonious linear spaces. Indeed the following result, in the same spirit of Theorem 1 in
[
27
]
, holds.

Proposition 4.1
.

If a harmonious SDF in
G
admits a perfect lifting to
G\times\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then it also
admits a perfect lifting to
G\times\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
for every integer
k
.

Proof.

Assume that
\Sigma
is a harmonious SDF in
G
admitting a perfect lifting

{\cal L}
to
G\times\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Thus, using the same notation as in the previous section, we have

\Delta{\cal L}=\bigcup_{g\in G}\{g\}\times\Delta_{g},\quad S\cdot\Delta_{g}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\ \ \forall g\in G,\quad{\rm and}\quad S\cdot\pi({\cal L})=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}

where
S
is a perfect companion for
\cal L
.

Take a complete system
T
of representatives for the cosets of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}^{*}
so that we have
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\cdot T=\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}^{*}
.
Then, for every block
L\in\cal L
and any
t\in T
, let
L_{t}
be the subset of
G\times\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}^{*}

obtainable from
L
by multiplying the second coordinates of all its elements by
t
. Now set

{\cal L}^{\prime}=\{L_{t}\ |\ L\in{\cal L},t\in T\}
. Clearly,
{\cal L}^{\prime}
is a lifting of
\Sigma
to
G\times\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}
.
Also, we have
\Delta{\cal L}^{\prime}=\bigcup_{g\in G}\{g\}\times\Delta^{\prime}_{g}
with
\Delta^{\prime}_{g}=\Delta_{g}\cdot T
for every
g\in G
,
and
\pi({\cal L}^{\prime})=\pi({\cal L})\cdot T
. Thus we have:

S\cdot\Delta^{\prime}_{g}=S\cdot\Delta_{g}\cdot T=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\cdot T=\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}^{*}\ \ \forall g\in G\quad{\rm and}\quad S\cdot\pi({\cal L}^{\prime})=S\cdot\pi({\cal L})\cdot T=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\cdot T=\mathbb{F}_{q^{k}}^{*}

which means that
S
is a perfect companion for
{\cal L}^{\prime}
, i.e.,
{\cal L}^{\prime}
is perfect.
∎

Applying Proposition
4.1
together with Example
3.9
we get a HS
(2,\{2,4\},2\cdot 17^{k})
for every
k
.

Now we show a composition construction making use of
difference matrices
(see, e.g.,
[
2
]
or
[
15
]
).
A
(k\times v)
-matrix with entries in an additive group
G
of order
v
such that the difference of any two distinct rows
is a permutation of
G
is said to be a
(G,k,1)
difference matrix (DM for short). It is called
homogeneous
if each row is also a permutation of
G
.
Adapting the construction
[
21
]
for ordinary difference families by the second author we have the following.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
X
,
Y
be two groups and let
Z=X\times Y
. If there exist a resolvable
(G\times X,G\times\{0\},K_{1},1)
-DF,
a resolvable
(G\times Y,G\times\{0\},K_{2},1)
-DF and a homogeneous
(Y,\max(K_{1}),1)
-DM, then there exists
a resolvable
(G\times Z,G\times\{0\},K,1)
-DF with
K=\underline{|Y|}K_{1}\ \cup\ K_{2}
.

Proof.

For each block
B=\{(g_{1},x_{1}),\dots,(g_{k},x_{k})\}

of a resolvable
(G\times X,G\times\{0\},K_{1},1)
-DF, say
{\cal F}_{X}
, and for each column

(m_{1c},\dots,m_{kc})^{T}
of a homogeneous
(Y,\max(K_{1}),1)
-DM, say
M=(m_{rc})
,
set

B\circ M^{c}=\{(g_{1},x_{1},m_{1c}),\dots,(g_{k},x_{k},m_{kc})\}.

Also, for each block
B=\{(g_{1},y_{1}),\dots,(g_{k},y_{k})\}
of a resolvable
(G\times Y,G\times\{0\},K_{2},1)
-DF,
say
{\cal F}_{Y}
, set

\overline{B}=\{(g_{1},0,y_{1}),\dots,(g_{k},0,y_{k})\}
.
It is easy to check that

\{B\circ M^{c}\ |\ B\in{\cal F}_{X};1\leq c\leq|Y|\}\ \cup\ \{\overline{B}\ |\ B\in{\cal F}_{Y}\}

is a resolvable
(G\times Z,K,1)
-DF.
∎

In view of Theorem
2.4
,
with the same hypotheses as in Theorem

, we see that
there also exists a
(G\times Z,\underline{|Z|}K\ \cup\ \{|Z|\},|Z|)
-PDF
and hence a HS
(2,K^{\prime},v)
with

the underlying set of

.

5
Cyclotomy

Here we show how to use cyclotomy for getting a perfect lifting of a harmonious SDF.
Given a prime power
q\equiv 1
(mod
e
), the subgroup of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
of index
e
– that is the group
of non-zero
e
-th powers of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
– will be denoted by
C^{e}
.
If
r
is a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then the set of cosets of
C^{e}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
is
\{r^{i}C^{e}\ |\ 0\leq i\leq e-1\}
.
As usual, the coset
r^{i}C^{e}
(called
the
i
-th cyclotomic class of order
e
) will be denoted by
C_{i}^{e}
.
It is evident that an
e
-subset
S
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
is a complete system of representatives for the cosets of
C^{e}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
if and only
if there is an ordering
(s_{0},s_{1},\dots,s_{e-1})
of its elements such that
s_{i}\in C_{i}^{e}
for each
i
.

From now on, given two positive integers
e
,
n
, we set

U=\sum_{h=1}^{n}{n\choose h}(e-1)^{h}(h-1)\quad{\rm and}\quad Q(e,n)={1\over 4}(U+\sqrt{U^{2}+4ne^{n-1}})^{2}.

We will need the following result deriving from the theorem of Weil on multiplicative character sums (see Theorem 2.2 in
[
10
]
).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
q\equiv 1

(
mod
e)
be a prime power, let
\{c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{n-1}\}
be a
n
-subset of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
let
(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n-1})
be a
n
-tuple of elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{e}
.
Then the set

X=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\ :\ x-c_{j}\in C_{\alpha_{j}}^{e}\ \mbox{for }0\leq j\leq n-1\}

is non-empty if
q>Q(e,n)
.

In other words, whenever a prime power
q\equiv 1
(mod
e
)
is greater than
Q(e,n)
, the existence of an element
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
satisfying
n
cyclotomic conditions of the
form
x-c_{j}\in C^{e}_{\alpha_{j}}
is guaranteed provided that the
c_{j}
’s are pairwise distinct.

The following theorem is a variation of Theorem 7.2 in
[
9
]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Every harmonious
(G,K,\lambda)
-SDF admits a perfect lifting to
G\times\mathbb{F}_{q}

for all prime powers
q\equiv\lambda+1

(
mod
2\lambda)
greater than
Q(\lambda,\max(K))
.

Proof.

Let
\Sigma=\{B_{1},\dots,B_{\sigma}\}
be a harmonious
(G,K,\lambda)
-SDF with
|B_{h}|=k_{h}
and

B_{h}=\{b_{h,1},\dots,b_{h,k_{h}}\}
for
1\leq h\leq\sigma
.

Let
S
be the set of all pairs
(h,i)
with
h\in\{1,\dots,\sigma\}
and
i\in\{1,\dots,k_{h}\}
.
This set has size
k_{1}+k_{2}+\dots+k_{\sigma}=\lambda
since
\Sigma
is harmonious, hence we may choose a
bijection
\phi:S\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\lambda}
.

Let
G_{0}=\{g\in G\ |\ 2g=0\}
be the set of all involutions of
G
together with
0
.
Obviously,
G\setminus G_{0}
can be partitioned into pairs
\{g,-g\}
of opposite and distinct elements of
G
.
Thus we can write
G=G_{0}\ \cup\ G^{+}\ \cup\ G^{-}
where
G^{+}
is a complete set of representatives for the
above pairs and
G^{-}=-G^{+}
.

Let
T
be the set of all triples
(h,i,j)
with
h\in\{1,\dots,\sigma\}
and
i
,
j

distinct elements of
\{1,\dots,k_{h}\}
. Then consider the bijection

{}^{{}^{\overline{\ \ \ }}}:(h,i,j)\in T\longrightarrow\overline{(h,i,j)}=(h,j,i)\in T

which is clearly involutory and without fixed elements.

For every
g\in G
, let
T_{g}
be the set of triples
(h,i,j)
of
T
such that
b_{h,i}-b_{h,j}=g
.
By definition of a
(G,K,\lambda)
-SDF,
\{T_{g}\ |\ g\in G\}
is a partition of
T
into subsets of size
\lambda
.
We have:

(h,i,j)\in T_{g}\Longleftrightarrow b_{h,i}-b_{h,j}=g\Longleftrightarrow b_{h,j}-b_{h,i}=-g\Longleftrightarrow(h,j,i)\in T_{-g}

hence

\overline{T_{g}}=T_{-g}\quad\forall g\in G

(5.1)

For any
g\in G^{+}
, let us choose an arbitrary bijection
\psi_{g}:T_{g}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\lambda}
.
In view of (
5.1
) and recalling that the index of a SDF is always even, it makes sense to consider the map

\psi_{-g}:(h,i,j)\in T_{-g}\longrightarrow\psi_{g}(h,j,i)+{\lambda\over 2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\lambda}

(5.2)

The fact that
\psi_{g}
is a bijection implies that
\psi_{-g}
is a bijection as well.

Given
g\in G_{0}
, we have
g=-g
and hence
\overline{T}_{g}=T_{g}
in view of (
5.1
).
Thus we can write
T_{g}=T^{\prime}_{g}\ \cup\ \overline{T^{\prime}}_{g}
for a suitable
{\lambda\over 2}
-subset

T^{\prime}_{g}
of
T_{g}
. Let
\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}_{\lambda}
be a system of representatives for the
cosets of
\{0,{\lambda\over 2}\}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{\lambda}
and let us choose any bijection

\psi^{\prime}_{g}:T^{\prime}_{g}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}_{\lambda}
. Then consider the map

\overline{\psi^{\prime}}_{g}:(h,i,j)\in\overline{T^{\prime}}_{g}\longrightarrow\psi^{\prime}_{g}(h,j,i)+{\lambda\over 2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\lambda}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{\prime}_{\lambda}

(5.3)

Also here, the fact that
\psi^{\prime}_{g}
is a bijection assures that
\overline{\psi^{\prime}}_{g}
is a bijection too.
The map
\psi_{g}:T_{g}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\lambda}

defined by

\psi_{g}(h,i,j)=\begin{cases}\psi^{\prime}_{g}(h,i,j)&{\rm if}\ (h,i,j)\in T^{\prime}_{g}\cr\overline{\psi^{\prime}}_{g}(h,i,j)&{\rm if}\ (h,i,j)\in\overline{T^{\prime}}_{g}\end{cases}

is clearly a bijection as well.

Finally, let
\psi:T\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\lambda}
be the map whose restriction to
T_{g}
coincides with
\psi_{g}
for every
g\in G
.
In view of (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) this map has the following property:

\psi(h,j,i)=\psi(h,i,j)+{\lambda\over 2}\quad\forall(h,j,i)\in T

(5.4)

Now let
q
be a prime power as in the statement and let us lift each
B_{h}
to a subset

\ell(B_{h})=\{(b_{h,1},c_{h,1}),\dots,(b_{h,k_{h}},c_{h,k_{h}})\}
of
G\times\mathbb{F}_{q}

by taking the first element
c_{h,1}
arbitrarily in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and then by taking the other elements

c_{h,2}
,
c_{h,3}
, …,
c_{h,k_{h}}
iteratively, one by one, according to the rule that
once that
c_{h,i-1}
has been chosen, we pick
c_{h,i}
arbitrarily in the set

X_{h,i}=\{x\in C^{\lambda}_{\phi(h,i)}\ :\ x-c_{h,j}\in C^{\lambda}_{\psi(h,i,j)}\quad{\rm for}\ 1\leq j\leq i-1\}

where
\phi
is the bijection from
S
to
\mathbb{Z}_{\lambda}
chosen at the beginning of this proof.
It is convenient to rewrite
X_{h,i}
as

X_{h,i}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\ :x-0\in C^{\lambda}_{\phi(h,i)}\ {\rm and}\ x-c_{h,j}\in C^{\lambda}_{\psi(h,i,j)}\quad{\rm for}\ 1\leq j\leq i-1\}.

The above choice of
c_{h,i}
can be actually done since
X_{h,i}
is not empty by Lemma
5.1
.
Indeed, as shown below,
\{0,c_{h,1},...,c_{h,i-1}\}
is a set and
q>Q(\lambda,i)
:

•

if
\{0,c_{h,1},...,c_{h,i-1}\}
had repeated elements we would have
either
0=c_{h,j}
with
1\leq j\leq i-1
contradicting that

c_{h,j}
had been picked in
X_{h,j}\subset C^{\lambda}_{\phi(h,j)}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, or

0=c_{h,j_{1}}-c_{h,j_{2}}
with
1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}\leq i-1
contradicting that

c_{h,j_{2}}-c_{h,j_{1}}\in C^{\lambda}_{\psi(h,j_{2},j_{1})}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
since
c_{h,j_{2}}
had been picked in
X_{h,j_{2}}
;

•

we have
\max(K)\geq k_{h}\geq i
and then
q>Q(\lambda,\max(K))>Q(\lambda,i)
since
the function
Q(\lambda,n)
defined before Theorem
5.1
is clearly increasing in the variable
n
.

Now we show that the lifting
{\cal L}=\{\ell(B_{1}),\dots,\ell(B_{\sigma})\}
of
\Sigma
to
G\times\mathbb{F}_{q}
is perfect.
First observe that we have

c_{h_{,}i}-c_{h,j}\in C^{\lambda}_{\psi(h,i,j)}\quad\forall(h,i,j)\in T

(5.5)

This is clear if
i>j
considering the rule used for selecting the
c_{h,i}
’s.
If
i<j
, for the same reason, we have
c_{h_{,}j}-c_{h,i}\in C^{\lambda}_{\psi(h,j,i)}
and then, by (
5.4
), we can write

c_{h_{,}j}-c_{h,i}\in C^{\lambda}_{\psi(h,i,j)+\lambda/2}

(5.6)

The hypothesis
q\equiv\lambda+1
(mod
2\lambda
) implies that

-1\in C^{\lambda}_{\lambda/2}
. Thus, multiplying (
5.6
) by
-1
we get (
5.5
) again.

Now we have

\bigcup_{h=1}^{\sigma}\Delta\ell(B_{h})=\bigcup_{g\in G}\{g\}\times\Delta_{g}

with
\Delta_{g}=\{c_{h,i}-c_{h,j}\ |\ (h,i,j)\in T_{g}\}
. Thus, considering (
5.5
) and
the fact that
\psi
is bijective on each
T_{g}
, we see that
\Delta_{g}
is a
complete system of representatives for the cosets of
C^{\lambda}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}

whichever is
g\in G
. Hence we have
C^{\lambda}\cdot\Delta_{g}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
for every
g\in G
, i.e.,

C^{\lambda}
is a good companion for
\cal L
.
Finally, the projection of the flatten of
\cal L
on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is

\pi({\cal L})=\{c_{h,i}\ |\ (h,i)\in S\}
and then, recalling that
c_{h,i}\in C^{\lambda}_{\phi(h,i)}

for every
(h,i)\in S
and that
\phi:S\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\lambda}
is bijective, we can
say that
\pi({\cal L})
is also a complete system of representatives for the cosets of
C^{\lambda}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, i.e.,
C^{\lambda}\cdot\pi({\cal L})=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
.
We conclude that
C^{\lambda}
is a perfect companion for
\cal L
.
∎

Putting together Lemma
5.2
and Corollary
3.8
we obtain the following.

Theorem 5.3
.

If there exists a harmonious
(G,K^{\prime},\lambda)
-SDF, then there exists a HS
(2,K,gq)
, with
g=|G|
and
K
the underlying set
of
K^{\prime}\ \cup\ \{g\}
, for every prime power
q\equiv\lambda+1

(
mod
2\lambda)
greater than
Q(\lambda,\max(K^{\prime}))

Theorem 6.2

7
Main result

It is quite evident that the union of some harmonious SDFs in the same group
G
is still
a harmonious SDF whose index is the sum of their indices.

Lemma 7.1
.

If there exist harmonious
(G,K_{i},\lambda_{i})
-SDFs for
1\leq i\leq n
, then their union is
a harmonious
(G,K,\lambda)
-SDF with
K=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}K_{i}
and
\lambda=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}
.

Lemma 7.2
.

For any group
G
of order
k
and any integer
h>k+1
, there exists a harmonious

(G,\{k^{h(h-k-1)},h^{k}\},hk(h-k))
-SDF.

Proof.

Consider the multisets
X
,
Y
,
Z
on
G
defined as follows

X=O_{k};\quad Y=G;\quad Z=O_{h-k}\ \cup\ G.

Trivially, we have
\Delta X=O_{k(k-1)}
and
\Delta Y=\underline{k}(G\setminus\{0\})
since the
set of all elements of any group
G
is a difference set in
G
of index
|G|
.
Also, rewriting
Z
as
O_{h-k+1}\ \cup\ (G\setminus\{0\})
we
see that

\Delta Z=\Delta O_{h-k+1}\ \cup\ \Delta(G\setminus\{0\})\ \cup\ [(G\setminus\{0\})-O_{h-k+1}]\ \cup[O_{h-k+1}-(G\setminus\{0\})].

We have

\Delta O_{h-k+1}=O_{(h-k+1)(h-k)}
and
\Delta(G\setminus\{0\})=\underline{k-2}(G\setminus\{0\})

since the set of all non-zero elements of any group
G
is a difference set in
G
of index
|G|-2
.
Finally
(G\setminus\{0\})-O_{h-k+1}=O_{h-k+1}-(G\setminus\{0\})=\underline{h-k+1}(G\setminus\{0\})
. Hence we can write

\Delta Z=O_{(h-k+1)(h-k)}\ \cup\ \underline{2h-k}(G\setminus\{0\}).

Now consider the family
\Sigma=\{X^{h-k},Y^{h^{2}-hk-2h+k},Z^{k}\}
. The above informations about
\Delta X
,
\Delta Y
and
\Delta Z

allow us to say that
\Delta\Sigma=O_{\mu_{0}}\ \cup\ \mu_{1}(G\setminus\{0\})
with

\mu_{0}=(h-k)k(k-1)+k(h-k+1)(h-k)=hk(h-k);

\mu_{1}=(h^{2}-hk-2h+k)k+k(2h-k)=hk(h-k).

This means that
\Sigma
is a SDF of index
\lambda=hk(h-k)
. Its blocks have sizes
k
and
h
. Those of size
k
are the

h-k
copies of
X
and the
h^{2}-hk-2h+k
copies of
Y
for a total of
(h-k)+(h^{2}-hk-2h+k)=h(h-k-1)
blocks of size
k
;
those of size
h
are the
k
copies of
Z
.
Thus the flatten of
\Sigma
has size equal to

h(h-k-1)k+hk=hk(h-k)=\lambda.

Thus
\Sigma
is harmonious and the assertion follows.
∎

We are now ready to proof our main theorem.

Theorem 7.3
.

For any finite non-singleton subset
K
of
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, there are infinitely many values of
v

for which there exists a harmonious
S(2,K,v)
.

Proof.

Write
K=\{k_{0},k_{1},\dots,k_{t}\}
with
k_{0}<k_{1}<\dots<k_{t}
, and let
G
be any group of order
k_{0}
.
Distinguish two cases according to whether
k_{1}>k_{0}+1

or
k_{1}=k_{0}+1
.

1st case:
k_{1}>k_{0}+1
.

For
i=1,...,t
, using Lemma
7.2
we can construct a harmonious
(G,\{k_{0}^{\alpha_{i}},k_{i}^{\beta_{i}}\},\lambda_{i})
-SDF,
say
\Sigma_{i}
, for suitable positive integers
\alpha_{i}
,
\beta_{i}
,
\lambda_{i}
.
By Lemma
7.1
, the union
\Sigma
of the
\Sigma_{i}
s is a harmonious

(G,K^{\prime},\lambda)
-SDF with
K^{\prime}=\{k_{0}^{\alpha},k_{1}^{\beta_{1}},...,k_{t}^{\beta_{t}}\}
where
\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\alpha_{i}

and
\lambda=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\lambda_{i}
. Thus, by Theorem
5.3
, there exists a HS
(2,K,v)
with

v=k_{0}q
and
q
any prime power congruent to
\lambda+1
(mod
2\lambda
) and greater than
Q(\lambda,k_{t})
. The assertion then follows considering that there are infinitely
many primes
q\equiv\lambda+1
(mod
2\lambda
) by Dirichlet’s theorem on arithmetic progressions.

2nd case:
k_{1}=k_{0}+1

It is enough to proceed as in the 1st case by replacing
\Sigma_{1}
with a harmonious
(G,k_{1},\lambda_{1})
-SDF
(which exists in view of Example
3.2
).
∎

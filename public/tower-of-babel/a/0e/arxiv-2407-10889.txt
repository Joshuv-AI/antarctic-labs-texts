Why Mg 2 IrH 6 is predicted to be a high temperature superconductor, but Ca 2 IrH 6 is not
Xiaoyu Wang, Warren Pickett, Micheal Hutcheon, Rohit Prasankumar, Eva Zurek

Abstract
The X 2 ​ MH 6 \text{X}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{MH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}} family, consisting of an electropositive cation X and a main group metal M octahedrally coordinated by hydrogen, has been predicted to hold promise for high-temperature conventional superconductivity. Herein, we analyze the electronic structure of two members of this family, Mg 2 ​ IrH 6 \text{Mg}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{IrH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}} and Ca 2 ​ IrH 6 \text{Ca}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{IrH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}} , showing why the former may possess superconducting properties rivaling those of the cuprates, whereas the latter does not. Within Mg 2 ​ IrH 6 \text{Mg}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{IrH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}} the vibrations of the IrH 6 4 – \text{IrH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{4\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}} anions are key for the superconducting mechanism, and they induce coupling in the e g ∗ e_{g}^{*} set, which are antibonding between the H 1 s s and the Ir d x 2 − y 2 d_{x^{2}-y^{2}} or d z 2 d_{z^{2}} orbitals. Because calcium possesses low-lying d-orbitals, e g ∗ → e_{g}^{*}\rightarrow Ca d d back-donation is preferred, quenching the superconductivity. Our analysis explains why high critical temperatures were only predicted for second or third row X metal atoms, and may hold implications for superconductivity in other systems where the antibonding anionic states are filled.

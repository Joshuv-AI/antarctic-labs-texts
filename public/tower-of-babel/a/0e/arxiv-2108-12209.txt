Exponential clustering of bipartite quantum entanglement at arbitrary temperatures
Tomotaka Kuwahara 1,2 and Keiji Saito 3

Abstract
Many inexplicable phenomena in low-temperature many-body physics are a result of macroscopic quantum effects. Such macroscopic quantumness is often evaluated via long-range entanglement, that is, entanglement in the macroscopic length scale. Long-range entanglement is employed to characterize novel quantum phases and serves as a critical resource for quantum computation. However, the conditions under which long-range entanglement is stable, even at room temperatures, remain unclear. In this regard, this study demonstrated the unstable nature of bi-partite long-range entanglement at arbitrary temperatures, which exponentially decays with distance. The proposed theorem is a no-go theorem pertaining to the existence of long-range entanglement. The obtained results were consistent with existing observations, indicating that long-range entanglement at non-zero temperatures can exist in topologically ordered phases, where tripartite correlations are dominant. The derivation in this study introduced a quantum correlation defined by the convex roof of the standard correlation function. Further, an exponential clustering theorem for generic quantum many-body systems under such a quantum correlation at arbitrary temperatures was established, which yielded the primary result by relating quantum correlation with quantum entanglement. Moreover, a simple application of analytical techniques was demonstrated by deriving a general limit on the Wigner-Yanase-Dyson skew and quantum Fisher information; this is expected to attract significant attention in the field of quantum metrology. Notably, this study reveals the novel general aspects of low-temperature quantum physics and clarifies the characterization of long-range entanglement.



I
Introduction

I.1
Background

In quantum many-body physics, macroscopic quantum effects such as superconductivity, Bose-Einstein condensation, quantum spin liquid, and quantum topological order are critical features of exotic quantum phenomena.
In these phenomena, the length scale of the quantum effect is comparable to that in the real world.
However, the clarification of such macroscopic quantum effects remains a crucial problem in modern physics, and various methods for characterizing quantumness in the macroscopic length scale have been proposed
Yang 1962
;
Leggett 1980
;
Leggett 2002
;
Nimmrichter and Hornberger 2013
.
In particular, over the last two decades, quantum entanglement has become a representative measure for the quantumness
Horodecki et al. 2009
;
Fröwis et al. 2018
.
Several studies have investigated the entanglement
behaviors in quantum many-body systems from various perspectives
Osborne and Nielsen 2002
;
Arnesen et al. 2001
;
Vidal et al. 2003
;
Verstraete et al. 2004
;
Amico et al. 2008
;
Latorre and Riera 2009
;
Li and Haldane 2008
;
Calabrese and Cardy 2009
;
Eisert et al. 2010
;
Laflorencie 2016
.
These advances in quantum entanglement have significantly contributed toward improving our understanding and establishing efficient classical and quantum algorithms to simulate quantum many-body systems
Vidal 2007
;
Verstraete et al. 2008
;
Schollwöck 2011
;
Osborne 2012
;
Gharibian et al. 2015
.

This study aimed to elaborate the observation and prove that (more than) tripartite entanglement is required for long-range entanglement.
The quantum entanglement between two systems is shown to decay exponentially with distance at arbitrary non-zero temperatures, in any quantum Gibbs state with short-range interacting Hamiltonians.

A critical question regarding many-body quantum entanglement is whether entanglement can exist in the macroscopic length scale. Such entanglement is often referred to as long-range entanglement, which plays a crucial role not only in characterizing quantum phases
Chen et al. 2010
;
Wen 2017
but also in realizing quantum computing
Raussendorf et al. 2005
;
Nayak et al. 2008
;
Kim 2013
.
It can be inferred that temperature plays an essential role in this context.
Moreover, owing to the fragility of quantumness, thermal noise destroys the entanglement, making the length scale of the entanglement short range.
Indeed, at a sufficiently high temperature where the possibility of thermal phase transition is eliminated, the quantum thermal state can be classified as the trivial phase
Kuwahara et al. 2020
(i.e., generated by the finite-depth quantum circuit
Chen et al. 2010
).
By contrast, at zero temperature, various quantum systems are known to exhibit long-range entanglement
Campos Venuti et al. 2006
;
Gottesman and Hastings 2010
;
Vitagliano et al. 2010
;
Gualdi et al. 2011
;
Sahling et al. 2015
.
However, at non-zero but low temperatures, where thermal phase transition can occur, the effect of temperature on the entanglement remains highly unclear.
In this case, the effect of thermal noise is sufficiently suppressed, and it is possible to observe long-range entanglement in this temperature regime.
Consequently, in such quantum systems, quantum phases protected by the topological order can exhibit long-range entanglement.
It has been shown that, in the 4D toric code model
Kitaev 2003
, long-range entanglement can occur even at room temperatures
Alicki et al. 2010
;
Hastings 2011
(see also
Eldar 2019
;
Anshu and Nirkhe 2020
).

The purpose of this study was to identify the limitations associated with the structure of long-range entanglement at arbitrary non-zero temperatures.
In the known example involving long-range entanglement, the protection afforded by the topological order plays an essential role.
Moreover, the topological order is inherently a tripartite correlation
Kitaev and Preskill 2006
;
Levin and Wen 2006
;
Kato et al. 2016
.
However, these findings pose the following fundamental question:
“Can the long-range entanglement at non-zero temperatures only exist as (more than) tripartite correlations, or equivalently, does bi-partite entanglement necessarily decay to zero at long distances under arbitrary temperatures?”

We conjecture that the answer to this question is yes (Fig.
1
). The possibility of this conjecture being true can provide crucial information related to identifying the essence of long-range entanglement in the quantum phases at non-zero temperatures, which can further serve a guideline in the search for candidate systems suitable for quantum devices.
The conjecture is trivially true for arbitrary commuting Hamiltonians

Thus, as long as the commuting Hamiltonian is considered, the conjecture does not contradict the observations.

Thus far, rigorous and general studies on low-temperature phases remain scarce.
At low temperatures, in contrast to high-temperature phases, the structures of quantum many-body systems are considerably influenced by the system details.
Therefore, analyses of the low-temperature properties are often considered as computationally hard problems
Barahona 1982
;
Kempe et al. 2006
.
In such situations, all the long-range quantum effects are not strictly prohibited (e.g., off-diagonal long-range order
Yang 1962
),
with only a fraction of them being forbidden at low temperatures.
In the latter example, the thermal area law is known as a representative characterization of the low-temperature phases of many-body systems, which is universally true at arbitrary temperatures

;
Wolf et al. 2008
;
Kuwahara et al. 2021

It states that the entanglement between two adjacent subsystems can reach the maximum of the size of their boundaries.
In other words, the area law implies that entanglement should be localized around the boundary and thus indirectly supports the argument presented.

(1)

Set up and Preliminaries

Consider a quantum system on a
D
-dimensional lattice with

On each of the sites, the Hilbert space with dimension
d_{0}

Let
\Lambda

In addition, a complementary subset of

For an arbitrary subset
X\subseteq\Lambda

\mathcal{D}_{X}
is defined as the dimension of the Hilbert space on

\mathcal{D}_{X}=d_{0}^{|X|}
.
Finally,
X\cup Y
is denoted as
XY

For arbitrary subsets
X,Y\subseteq\Lambda
,

is defined as the shortest path length on the graph connecting
X
and
Y
; in other words, if
X\cap Y\neq\emptyset
,
d_{X,Y}=0
.
However, when
X
comprises only one element (e.g.,
X=\{i\}
), the distance

is denoted as

for simplicity.
In addition, the surface subset of
X
is denoted as
\partial X:=\{i\in X|d_{i,X^{\rm c}}=1\}

For a subset
X\subseteq\Lambda
, the extended subset
X[r]
is defined as

\displaystyle X[r]:=\{i\in\Lambda|d_{X,i}\leq r\},

(6)

where
X[0]=X
, and

is an arbitrary positive number (i.e.,
r\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
).
Based on the notation, for

, the subset

is concluded to be a ball region with radius

A geometric parameter
\gamma
is introduced, which is determined based on the lattice structure alone.
Further,
\gamma\geq 1
is defined as a constant of

that satisfies the following inequalities:

\displaystyle\max_{i\in\Lambda}\left(|\partial i[r]|\right)\leq\gamma r^{D-1},\quad\max_{i\in\Lambda}\left(|i[r]|\right)\leq\gamma r^{D},

(7)

where

II.1
Hamiltonian and quantum Gibbs state

Throughout the study, generic Hamiltonians with few-body interactions are considered.
Here, the Hamiltonian is expressed in the following
k
-local form:

\displaystyle H=\sum_{|Z|\leq k}h_{Z},\quad\max_{i\in\Lambda}\sum_{Z:Z\ni i}\|h_{Z}\|\leq g,

(8)

where each of the interaction terms
\{h_{Z}\}_{|Z|\leq k}
acts on the spins on
Z\subset\Lambda
.
For an arbitrary subset
L\subset\Lambda
, the subset Hamiltonian, which includes interactions in a subset
L
, is denoted as
H_{L}
:

\displaystyle H_{L}=\sum_{Z:Z\subset L}h_{Z}.

(9)

To characterize the interaction strength of the Hamiltonian, the following assumption is imposed:

\displaystyle\max_{\{i,j\}\subset\Lambda}\sum_{Z\supset\{i,j\}}\|h_{Z}\|\leq J(d_{i,j}),

(10)

where
J(x)
is a function that monotonically decreases with
x\geq 0
.
Here, the short-range interaction is primarily considered, where the decay of the function
J(x)
is faster than exponential decay; in other words,

\displaystyle J(x)\leq g_{0}e^{-\mu_{0}x}\quad(\textrm{short-range interaction})

(11)

with
g_{0}=\mathcal{O}(1)
and
\mu_{0}=\mathcal{O}(1)
.
The results can be generalized to a broader class of interactions, as discussed in Appendix
B
.

Using the Hamiltonian, the quantum Gibbs state can be defined as follows:

\displaystyle\rho_{\beta}=\frac{e^{-\beta H}}{Z_{\beta}},\quad Z_{\beta}={\rm tr}(e^{-\beta H}),

(12)

where
\beta
is the inverse temperature.
Throughout the paper, by appropriately choosing the energy origin,
Z_{\beta}=1
is enforced, that is,

\displaystyle\rho_{\beta}=e^{-\beta H}.

(13)

However, when considering a reduced density matrix on a region
L
(
L\subset\Lambda
), it is denoted as
\rho_{\beta,L}
:

\displaystyle\rho_{\beta,L}:={\rm tr}_{L^{\rm c}}(\rho_{\beta}),

(14)

where
{\rm tr}_{L^{\rm c}}
implies the partial trace for the Hilbert space on the subset
L^{\rm c}
.

\displaystyle O_{X}(t,Y):=\frac{1}{{\rm tr}_{Y^{\rm c}}(\hat{1})}{\rm tr}_{Y^{\rm c}}\left[O_{X}(t)\right]\otimes\hat{1}_{Y^{\rm c}},

where

II.3
Quantum entanglement

Here, the basic definition of quantum entanglement
Horodecki et al. 2009
;
Plenio and Virmani 2014
is presented.
First,
{\rm SEP}(A:B)
is defined as a set of separable quantum states on the subset
AB
.
For an arbitrary quantum state
\rho
,
the reduced density matrix
\rho_{AB}
satisfies
\rho_{AB}\in{\rm SEP}(A:B)
if and only if the following decomposition exists:

\displaystyle\rho_{AB}=\sum_{s}p_{s}\rho_{s,A}\otimes\rho_{s,B}.

(19)

When
\rho_{AB}
is a pure state,
\rho_{AB}\in{\rm SEP}(A:B)
implies that
\rho_{AB}
is given by the product state.
Further, a quantum state
\rho_{AB}
is defined to be entangled if and only if
\rho_{AB}\notin{\rm SEP}(A:B)
.

In quantifying the entanglement, the relative entanglement
Vedral et al. 1997
;
Vedral and Plenio 1998
;
Vedral 2002
can be adopted as follows:

\displaystyle E_{R}^{\mathcal{X}}(\rho_{AB}):=\inf_{\sigma_{AB}\in\mathcal{X}}S(\rho_{AB}||\sigma_{AB}),

(20)

where
\mathcal{X}
is the arbitrary class of quantum states (focus of this study) and

S(\rho_{AB}||\sigma_{AB})
is the relative entropy:

\displaystyle S(\rho_{AB}||\sigma_{AB})

\displaystyle:={\rm tr}\left[\rho_{AB}\log(\rho_{AB})\right]-{\rm tr}\left[\rho_{AB}\log(\sigma_{AB})\right].

(21)

In particular, on choosing
\mathcal{X}={\rm SEP}(A:B)
, the following is denoted

\displaystyle E_{R}(\rho_{AB}):=\inf_{\sigma_{AB}\in{\rm SEP}(A:B)}S(\rho_{AB}||\sigma_{AB})

(22)

for simplicity.

The relative entanglement
E_{R}(\rho_{AB})
is also related to the closeness of the target state to the zero-entangled state.
Pinsker’s inequality entails

\displaystyle\|\rho_{AB}-\sigma_{AB}\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{2S(\rho_{AB}||\sigma_{AB})}

(23)

for an arbitrary
\sigma_{AB}
. Hence, definition (
22
) immediately yields

\displaystyle\delta_{\rho_{AB}}

\displaystyle:=\inf_{\sigma_{AB}\in{\rm SEP}(A:B)}\left\|\rho_{AB}-\sigma_{AB}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2E_{R}(\rho_{AB})}.

(24)

The quantity
\delta_{\rho_{AB}}
yields meaningful upper bounds for various entanglement measures.
Using the continuity of the information measures
Alicki and Fannes 2004
;
Terhal et al. 2002
,
most of the entanglement measures are upper-bounded by

II.4
Clustering theorem at high-temperatures: Known results

This section reviews an established clustering theorem that holds above a threshold temperature, which is usually determined by the convergence of the cluster expansion.
In high-temperature regimes, clustering of the entanglement can be immediately derived by combining Pinsker’s inequality and the exponential decay of the mutual information (Corollary
4
below).

For an arbitrary quantum state
\rho
, the standard correlation function
{\rm C}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B})
between observables
O_{A}
and
O_{B}
can be defined as

\displaystyle{\rm C}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B}):={\rm tr}(\rho O_{A}O_{B})-{\rm tr}(\rho O_{A})\cdot{\rm tr}(\rho O_{B}).

(25)

As a stronger concept of the bi-partite correlation, the mutual information
{\mathcal{I}}_{\rho}(A:B)
between two subsystems

and

can be defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\rho}(A:B):=S_{\rho}(A)+S_{\rho}(B)-S_{\rho}(AB),

where

is the von Neumann entropy for the reduced density matrix on subset

being the reduced density matrix on

Previous studies

\beta\lesssim\log(n)
(see also
*2
*2

*2

In general, the results in Refs.

Moreover, the correlation length should be at least as large as

In the temperature regimes

For the relative entanglement (

Therefore, in high-temperature regimes,

*3
*3

*3

Consequently, this study focused on the low-temperature regimes, where thermal phase transitions can occur and the clustering of bi-partite correlations may no longer be satisfied.

III
Quantum correlation

Before discussing the entanglement clustering theorem, the quantum correlation function, defined as a convex roof of the standard correlation function
{\rm C}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B})
in Eq. (
25
), must be considered.
Quantum correlation is a natural quantum analog of the standard correlation function and has a significant relationship with quantum entanglement (Sec.
III.2
).
Quantum correlation is introduced for two primary reasons:

1.

The clustering theorem for quantum correlation can be proved in a completely general manner (Theorem
10
).

2.

The clustering of quantum correlation is also utilized to prove the entanglement clustering theorems
(Corollary
11
and Theorem
12
)

III.1
Definition

For an arbitrary many-body quantum state
\rho
,
the quantum correlation for observables
O_{A}
and
O_{B}
can be defined by the convex roof of the standard correlation function (
25
), that is,
{\rm C}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B})
[
={\rm tr}(\rho O_{A}O_{B})-{\rm tr}(\rho O_{A})\cdot{\rm tr}(\rho O_{B})
]:

\displaystyle{\rm QC}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B}):=\inf_{\{p_{s},\rho_{s}\}}\sum_{s}p_{s}|{\rm C}_{\rho_{s}}(O_{A},O_{B})|,

(33)

where minimization is performed for all possible decompositions of
\rho
such that
\rho=\sum_{s}p_{s}\rho_{s}
with
p_{s}>0
, and
\rho_{s}
is a quantum state.
Herein, the mixed convex roof was adopted instead of the pure convex roof, for which decomposed states
\{\rho_{s}\}
are restricted to the pure state; in other words,
\rho_{s}=|\phi_{s}\rangle\langle\phi_{s}|
for
\forall s
.
This is because using it ensures inequality (
37
) in Lemma
5
.
For example, the mixed convex roof has been considered in Refs.
Synak-Radtke and Horodecki 2006
;
Yang et al. 2005
;
Paz-Silva and Reina 2009
;
Yang et al. 2009
.

Subsequently, the definition immediately implies

\displaystyle{\rm QC}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B})=\bigl|{\rm C}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B})\bigr|

(34)

when
\rho
is given by the pure state.

The quantum correlations for a density matrix
\rho
may be different from those for a reduced density matrix
\rho_{L}
(
L\subset\Lambda
), that is,
{\rm QC}_{\rho_{L}}(O_{A},O_{B})\neq{\rm QC}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B})
[Ineq. (
37
)].
For example, consider the case wherein
\rho
is given by the Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) state, as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(|0_{\Lambda}\rangle+|1_{\Lambda}\rangle)(\langle 0_{\Lambda}|+\langle 1_{\Lambda}|),

(35)

where
|0_{\Lambda}\rangle
(
|1_{\Lambda}\rangle
) is the product state of
|0_{i}\rangle
(
|1_{i}\rangle
) states (
i\in\Lambda
).
Then, the quantum state
\rho
has a non-zero quantum correlation, based on Eq. (
34
),
while the reduced density matrices in arbitrary subsystems
L\subset\Lambda
are given by a mixed state of
|0_{L}\rangle
and
|1_{L}\rangle
, each of which exhibits no correlations.
Hence, no quantum correlations exist in the reduced density matrix of the GHZ state.

As the basic properties of
{\rm QC}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B})
, the following lemma is proven:

Lemma 5
.

Let
O_{A}
and
O_{B}
be arbitrary operators supported on
A
and
B
, respectively.
Subsequently, the following inequalities are obtained:

\displaystyle{\rm QC}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B})\leq|{\rm C}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B})|,

(36)

and

\displaystyle{\rm QC}_{\rho_{L}}(O_{A},O_{B})\leq{\rm QC}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B}),

(37)

where
A\subseteq L
and
B\subseteq L
.
The second inequality is consistent with the example of the GHZ state (
35
).

In addition, the quantum correlation satisfies the following continuity bound.
For arbitrary two quantum states
\rho
and
\sigma
, the difference between
{\rm QC}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B})
and
{\rm QC}_{\sigma}(O_{A},O_{B})
is upper-bounded as

\displaystyle|{\rm QC}_{\sigma}(O_{A},O_{B})-{\rm QC}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B})|\leq 7\sqrt{2}\epsilon^{1/2},

(38)

where
\|O_{A}\|=\|O_{B}\|=1
and
\epsilon=\|\sigma-\rho\|_{1}
are set.

Proof.
The proof of inequality (
36
) is obtained by choosing the decomposition as
\rho=p_{1}\rho_{1}
with
p_{1}=1
and
\rho_{1}=\rho
in definition (
33
).
Regarding the second inequality, the decomposition
\{p_{s},\rho_{s}\}
is considered such that

\displaystyle\sum_{s}p_{s}|{\rm C}_{\rho_{s}}(O_{A},O_{B})|={\rm QC}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B}).

(39)

For the reduced density matrix
\rho_{L}
, the decomposition using
\{p_{s},\rho_{s}\}
is chosen as

\displaystyle\rho_{L}=\sum_{s}p_{s}\rho_{s,L},\quad\rho_{s,L}={\rm tr}_{L^{\rm c}}(\rho_{s}).

(40)

Subsequently,
|{\rm C}_{\rho_{s}}(O_{A},O_{B})|=|{\rm C}_{\rho_{s,L}}(O_{A},O_{B})|
is obtained, and hence, inequality (
37
) is derived as

\displaystyle{\rm QC}_{\rho_{L}}(O_{A},O_{B})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s}p_{s}|{\rm C}_{\rho_{s,L}}(O_{A},O_{B})|

\displaystyle={\rm QC}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B}).

(41)

Finally, the inequality (
38
) is proven via the application of the method in Ref.
(
Synak-Radtke and Horodecki 2006
, Proposition 5)
.
For the standard correlation
{\rm C}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B})
, straightforward calculations yield

\displaystyle|{\rm C}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B})|\leq 1,

(42)

and

\displaystyle\left|{\rm C}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B})-{\rm C}_{\sigma}(O_{A},O_{B})\right|\leq 3\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1},

(43)

where
\|O_{A}\|=\|O_{B}\|=1
.
Hence, we can choose parameters
K
and
M
in Ref.
(
Synak-Radtke and Horodecki 2006
, Ineqs. (29) and (30))
as
K=3/\log(d_{X})
and
M=1/\log(d_{X})
, where
d_{X}
is the total Hilbert space dimension for
\rho
, that is,
d_{X}=\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}
according to this study’s notations.
Thus, inequality (
38
) can be obtained from Ref.
(
Synak-Radtke and Horodecki 2006
, Ineqs. (31), (51) and Proposition 5)
.
This completes the proof.

\square

III.2
Condition for zero quantum correlation

As a trivial statement, we first prove the following lemma:

Lemma 6
.

For a quantum state
\rho_{AB}
supported on
A\cup B
, the quantum correlation
{\rm QC}_{\rho_{AB}}(O_{A},O_{B})
is equal to zero for arbitrary operators
O_{A}
and
O_{B}
if
\rho_{AB}
is not entangled between the subsystems
A
and
B
(i.e.,
\rho_{AB}\in{\rm SEP}(A:B)
):

\displaystyle\textrm{$\rho_{AB}\in{\rm SEP}(A:B)$}\longrightarrow\textrm{${\rm QC}_{\rho_{AB}}(O_{A},O_{B})=0$ }

(44)

for arbitrary pairs of
O_{A},O_{B}
.
Considering the contraposition of statement (
44
), it can be concluded that

{\rm QC}_{\rho_{AB}}(O_{A},O_{B})\neq 0
for a pair of
O_{A},O_{B}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\rho_{AB}\notin{\rm SEP}(A:B).

(45)

Proof.

Considering definition (
19
) for
{\rm SEP}(A:B)
, there exists a decomposition of

\displaystyle\rho_{AB}=\sum_{s}p_{s}\rho_{s,A}\otimes\rho_{s,B}

(46)

when the quantum state
\rho_{AB}
is not entangled.
For such a decomposition, the state
\rho_{AB}
exhibits no quantum correlations for operators
O_{A}
or
O_{B}
:

\displaystyle{\rm QC}_{\rho_{AB}}(O_{A},O_{B})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s}p_{s}|{\rm C}_{\rho_{s,A}\otimes\rho_{s,B}}(O_{A},O_{B})|

\displaystyle=0.

(47)

This completes the proof.
\square

Thus, zero entanglement has been proven to be a sufficient condition for the zero-quantum correlation, as in Eq. (
44
).
However, this leads to the immediate question of whether the converse is also true, that is,

{\rm QC}_{\rho_{AB}}(O_{A},O_{B})=0
for arbitrary pairs of
O_{A},O_{B}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\ ?\ }\textrm{$\rho_{AB}\in{\rm SEP}(A:B)$ }.

(48)

To address this question, the following conjecture is proposed:

Conjecture 7
.

Statement (
III.2
) is true.
In other words, the zero-quantum correlation for arbitrary pairs of
O_{A},O_{B}
is necessary and sufficient for zero entanglement.

The reason for considering the conjecture to be true is that the following relationship exists for the standard correlation function:

{\rm C}_{\rho_{AB}}(O_{A},O_{B})=0
for arbitrary pairs of
O_{A},O_{B}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\textrm{$\rho_{AB}$ is a product state}.

(49)

Hence, it is natural to expect that the quantum version of the above relationship is true as well.
Regarding the above conjecture, at the very least, the following statement can be proven:

Lemma 8
.

If
{\rm QC}_{\rho_{AB}}(O_{A},O_{B})=0
for arbitrary pairs of
O_{A},O_{B}
, the Peres-Horodecki separability criterion
Peres 1996
;
Horodecki et al. 1996
, i.e., the positive partial transpose (PPT) condition, is satisfied.
Thus, the operator
\rho_{AB}^{T_{A}}
has no negative eigenvalues, where
T_{A}
is the partial transpose with respect to the Hilbert space on the subset
A
.

Proof.

The statement is immediately followed by Proposition
9
below.
The condition that
{\rm QC}_{\rho_{AB}}(O_{A},O_{B})=0
for arbitrary pairs of
O_{A},O_{B}
implies
\epsilon=0
in (
51
).
Hence, by applying
\epsilon=0
to inequality (
9
),
\rho_{AB}\in{\rm PPT}
is obtained, where
{\rm PPT}
is a set of states such that the PPT condition is satisfied [Eq. (
50
) below].
This completes the proof.
\square

The above lemma shows that conjecture
7
rigorously holds for a certain class of quantum systems, such as
2\times 2
,
2\times 3
quantum systems
Horodecki et al. 1996
;
Horodecki et al. 2003
.
Thus, any attempt to prove/disprove the conjecture in general cases must consider the existence of the bound entanglement
Horodecki et al. 1998
;
Horodecki et al. 1999
.
A possible route to proving conjecture
7
relies on the entanglement witness
Lewenstein and Sanpera 1998
;
Lewenstein et al. 2001
;
Doherty et al. 2004
;
Brandão 2005
.
However, appropriately reducing the calculations of the witnesses to those of quantum correlations is a challenging task.
As shown in the proofs of Proposition
9
and Lemma
25
below, the calculation of the partial transpose can be related to the quantum correlations.

III.3
Positive partial transpose (PPT) relative entanglement

Finally, in this section, quantum correlation is related to the PPT relative entanglement.
As shown in Lemma
8
, quantum correlation is proven to be strongly related with the PPT condition.
Consequently, using this property, quantum correlations can be related to the following PPT relative entanglement
Audenaert et al. 2001
;
Audenaert et al. 2002
;
Miranowicz and Ishizaka 2008
;
Girard et al. 2014
:

\displaystyle E_{R}^{{\rm PPT}}(\rho_{AB}):=\inf_{\sigma_{AB}\in{\rm PPT}}S(\rho_{AB}||\sigma_{AB}),

(50)

where
\mathcal{X}={\rm PPT}
is used in Eq. (
20
) with
{\rm PPT}
as a set of the quantum states
\sigma_{AB}
that satisfy the PPT condition, that is,
\sigma_{AB}^{T_{A}}\succeq 0
for
\sigma_{AB}\in{\rm PPT}
.
Because the
{\rm PPT}
set includes the separable set
{\rm SEP}
(
{\rm PPT}\supseteq{\rm SEP}
),

E_{R}^{{\rm PPT}}(\rho_{AB})
is smaller than or equal to
E_{R}(\rho_{AB})
, except for special cases.
As shown in Ref.
Vedral and Plenio 1998
, the PPT relative entanglement satisfies all basic conditions for the entanglement measure (i.e., the four conditions in Ref.
Plenio and Virmani 2014
).
In addition, it provides an upper bound for Rains’ bound
Rains 2001
;
Wang and Duan 2017
, which is strongly related to the distillable entanglement
Rains 2001
;
Audenaert et al. 2001
.

As shown in the following proposition, the quantum correlation (
33
) provides an upper bound for the PPT relative entanglement (see Appendix
E
for the proof):

Proposition 9
.

Let
\rho_{AB}
be an arbitrary quantum state such that

\displaystyle{\rm QC}_{\rho_{AB}}(O_{A},O_{B})\leq\epsilon\|O_{A}\|\cdot\|O_{B}\|

(51)

for two arbitrary operators
O_{A}
and
O_{B}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle E_{R}^{{\rm PPT}}(\rho_{AB})\leq 4\mathcal{D}_{AB}\bar{\delta}\log(1/\bar{\delta})\leq 4\mathcal{D}_{AB}\bar{\delta}^{1/2},

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}:=4\epsilon\min(\mathcal{D}_{A},\mathcal{D}_{B}),

(52)

where the second inequality is trivially derived from
x\log(1/x)\leq x^{1-1/e}\leq x^{1/2}
for
0\leq x\leq 1
.
Recall that
\mathcal{D}_{AB}
is the Hilbert space dimension in the region
AB
.

Based on the proposition, if there are no quantum correlations, that is, if
\epsilon=0
in (
51
), it can be ensured that
E_{R}^{{\rm PPT}}(\rho_{AB})=0
, which also yields Lemma
8
.
Consequently, the clustering theorem for the quantum correlation can be associated with that for quantum entanglement.
In the following section, the generic quantum Gibbs states are presented to satisfy the exponential clustering for quantum correlations at arbitrary temperatures, thereby indicating that the entanglement clustering theorem also holds.

IV
Exponential clustering for quantum correlations

In this section, the main theorems of this study on the exponential clustering of the quantum correlations as well as quantum entanglement are presented.
The theorems capture the universal structures of generic quantum Gibbs states at arbitrary temperatures.

First, consider the following theorem on quantum correlation (Appendix

IV.1
Exponential entanglement clustering

The combination of Proposition

Corollary 11

and

Further, using the quantum belief propagation technique

;

Let

Herein, another type of quantum correlation

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\rho}^{(\alpha)}(K):={\rm tr}(\rho K^{2})-{\rm tr}\left(\rho^{1-\alpha}K\rho^{\alpha}K\right)

(58)

for

, where
K
is an arbitrary operator.
The WYD skew information is considered as a measure of the non-commutability between

K

However, as a representative application, it is utilized in formulating the Heisenberg uncertainty relation for mixed states

;
Luo 2005
;
Yanagi 2010
;
Li and Fei 2018

More recently, the WYD skew information has garnered attention in the context of the quantum coherence theory

In Refs.

\displaystyle\bar{Q}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B}):=\int_{0}^{1}Q_{\rho}^{(\alpha)}(O_{A},O_{B})d\alpha

\displaystyle={\rm tr}(\rho O_{A}O_{B})-\int_{0}^{1}{\rm tr}\left(\rho^{1-\alpha}O_{A}\rho^{\alpha}O_{B}\right)d\alpha

\displaystyle Q_{\rho}^{(\alpha)}(O_{A},O_{B}):={\rm tr}(\rho O_{A}O_{B})-{\rm tr}\left(\rho^{1-\alpha}O_{A}\rho^{\alpha}O_{B}\right).

(60)

The quantity

is reduced to the standard correlation function
{\rm C}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B})
when
\rho
is a pure state.

The authors in Refs.

This problem can be resolved through the following theorem (see Appendix
C
for the proof):

Theorem 13
.

The quantum correlation (
60

0\leq\alpha\leq 1
as follows:

\displaystyle Q_{\rho_{\beta}}^{(\alpha)}(O_{A},O_{B})\leq C^{\prime}_{\beta}\min(|\partial A|,|\partial B|)e^{-R/\xi^{\prime}_{\beta}},

(61)

where
C^{\prime}_{\beta}
and
\xi^{\prime}_{\beta}
are characterized solely by the parameters in the Lieb-Robinson bound (
15
) as follows:

\displaystyle C^{\prime}_{\beta}=\frac{12+2C}{\pi}+\frac{4C}{v\beta},\quad\xi_{\beta}^{{}^{\prime}-1}=\frac{\mu}{2+(v\beta)/\pi}.

(62)

It is evident that the same upper bound trivially holds for
\bar{Q}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B})
in Eq. (
V

As shown in Appendix
C

Hastings 2004a

Thus, using Theorem
13
,
a general upper bound for the WYD skew information (see Appendix
C.2
for the proof) can be obtained:

Corollary 14
.

Let
K
be an operator expressed as

\displaystyle K=\sum_{i\in\Lambda}O_{i}\quad(\|O_{i}\|\leq 1).

(63)

Then, the WYD skew information
\mathcal{I}_{\rho_{\beta}}^{(\alpha)}(K)
(

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\rho_{\beta}}^{(\alpha)}(K)

\displaystyle:={\rm tr}(\rho_{\beta}K^{2})-{\rm tr}\left(\rho_{\beta}^{1-\alpha}K\rho_{\beta}^{\alpha}K\right)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{\beta}^{\prime}\xi_{\beta}^{\prime D}n=\mathcal{O}(\beta^{D})n,

V.1
Quantum Fisher information

As a relevant quantity, the quantum Fisher information
\mathcal{F}_{\rho}(K)
, which is defined as follows
Fröwis and Dür 2012

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\rho}(K)=\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}\frac{2(\lambda_{s}-\lambda_{s^{\prime}})^{2}}{\lambda_{s}+\lambda_{s^{\prime}}}|\langle\lambda_{s}|K|\lambda_{s^{\prime}}\rangle|^{2},

(65)

where
K=\sum_{i\in\Lambda}O_{i}
and
\rho=\sum_{s}\lambda_{s}|\lambda_{s}\rangle\langle\lambda_{s}|
(
\lambda_{s}>0
).
Here,
\lambda_{s}
and
|\lambda_{s}\rangle
are defined by the spectral decomposition
\rho=\sum_{s}\lambda_{s}|\lambda_{s}\rangle\langle\lambda_{s}|
.
When considering the quantum Gibbs states (i.e.,
\rho_{\beta}
),
\lambda_{s}=e^{-\beta E_{s}}
and
|\lambda_{s}\rangle=|E_{s}\rangle
are obtained,
where
|E_{s}\rangle
is the eigenstate of the Hamiltonian with the corresponding eigenenergy
E_{s}
. Subsequently, the quantum Fisher information is expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\rho_{\beta}}(K)=\sum_{s,s^{\prime}=1}^{\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}}\frac{2(e^{-\beta E_{s}}-e^{-\beta E_{s^{\prime}}})^{2}}{e^{-\beta E_{s}}+e^{-\beta E_{s^{\prime}}}}|\langle E_{s}|K|E_{s^{\prime}}\rangle|^{2},

where
\mathcal{D}_{\Lambda}
is the dimension of the total Hilbert space.

The quantum Fisher information was introduced in the field of quantum metrology
Braunstein and Caves 1994
;
Braunstein et al. 1996
;
Tóth and Apellaniz 2014
;
Yadin et al. 2021
.
As per the definition (

characterizes the sensitivity of the quantum state
\rho
to the unitary transformation

. Specifically, the uncertainty in estimating the parameter
\theta
is lower-bounded by the quantum Cramér–Rao bound
Braunstein and Caves 1994
;
Braunstein et al. 1996

\displaystyle\Delta\theta\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{m\mathcal{F}_{\rho}(K)}},

(66)

is the number of independent measurements on

Thus, with an increase in the quantum Fisher information, the required number of measurements decreases.
In the context of the entanglement theory, this is also regarded among the representative measures for macroscopic quantum entanglement

;
Fröwis and Dür 2012
;
Yadin and Vedral 2015
;
Hauke et al. 2016
;
Hyllus et al. 2012
;
Fröwis et al. 2018
.
In recent studies, the quantum Fisher information has garnered attention in the development of quantum technologies
(see Refs.
Fröwis et al. 2018
;
Liu et al. 2019
;
Meyer 2021
for recent reviews).

The quantum Fisher information is associated with the WYD skew information through the inequality
(\mathcal{F}_{\rho_{\beta}}(K)/4)\leq 2\mathcal{I}_{\rho_{\beta}}^{(\alpha=1/2)}(K)
, which was proven in Ref.
(
Luo 2004
, Theorem 2)
(

according to this study’s notation.
).
Hence, based on inequality (

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\rho_{\beta}}(K)\leq 8\tilde{C}_{\beta}^{\prime}\xi_{\beta}^{\prime D}n,

(67)

where

Further, in Appendix

To discuss macroscopic entanglement using the quantum Fisher information, the scaling exponent,

Further discussion

VI.1
Macroscopic quantum effect v.s. quantum entanglement

The entanglement properties have been discussed in the finite-temperature Gibbs state.
This section shows that, in general, the observations on the entanglement properties for the finite-temperature mixed state are considerably different from those for pure states. Nevertheless, the typical unusual wave function at low temperatures in condensed matter physics is worth discussing, such as Bardeen-Cooper-Schrieffer states in a superconductor, which exhibit off-diagonal long-range orders (ODLRO
Yang 1962
). In Refs.
Vedral 2004
;
Vedral 2008
, Vedral discussed
\eta
-pairing states, which are eigenstates in the Hubbard, and similar models to explain high-temperature superconductivity. It was argued that such states have a vanishing entanglement between two sites as the distance diverges, whereas the classical correlations remain finite even in the thermodynamic limit. In addition, maximally mixed states with
\eta
-paring states also exhibit this property. Consequently, this observation suggests that ODLRO is not directly associated with the quantum entanglement discussed in this study. The quantum entanglement properties in the finite-temperature Gibbs state have not been analytically scrutinized under a general framework thus far. However, recent large-scale numerical computations involving two-dimensional transverse field Ising models revealed that entanglement measured via the Rényi negativity is short-ranged, even at finite critical temperatures
Lu and Grover 2020a
;
Wu et al. 2020
.
This observation is consistent with the general statement declared.

VI.2
Relation to the quantum Markov property

In this subsection, a brief derivation of the relation between the clustering of quantum entanglement and the approximate quantum Markov property is presented.

For this purpose, the squashed entanglement
Christandl and Winter 2004
;
Brandão et al. 2011
;
Li and Winter 2014
, defined using the conditional mutual information
{\mathcal{I}}_{\rho_{ABE}}(A:B|E)

for tripartite quantum systems, is considered:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{\rho_{ABE}}(A:B|E):=

\displaystyle S_{\rho_{ABE}}(AE)+S_{\rho_{ABE}}(BE)

\displaystyle-S_{\rho_{ABE}}(ABE)-S_{\rho_{ABE}}(E).

(68)

Recall that
S_{\rho_{ABE}}(L)
is the von Neumann entropy for the reduced density matrix on the subset
L\subseteq ABE
.
Thus, the squashed entanglement is defined as follows:

\displaystyle E_{\rm sq}(\rho_{AB})

\displaystyle:=\inf_{E}\left\{\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{I}}_{\rho_{ABE}}(A:B|E)\biggl|{\rm tr}_{E}(\rho_{ABE})=\rho_{AB}\right\},

(69)

where
\inf_{E}
is considered over all extensions of
\rho_{AB}
, such that
{\rm tr}_{E}(\rho_{ABE})=\rho_{AB}
.
In contrast to the PPT relative entanglement (
50
),
squashed entanglement is equal to zero if and only if the quantum state is not entangled
Brandão et al. 2011
.

In addition, squashed entanglement is strongly related with the quantum Markov property, which implies the following equation for the arbitrary tripartition of total systems (
\Lambda=A\sqcup C\sqcup B
):

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{\rho}(A:B|C)=0\quad\textrm{for}\quad d_{A,B}\geq r_{0},

(70)

where
r_{0}
is a constant of
\mathcal{O}(1)
.
When the Hamiltonian is short-range and commuting, the above Markov property strictly holds for quantum Gibbs states at arbitrary temperatures
Brown and Poulin 2012
;
Jouneghani et al. 2014
.
Further, the quantum Markov property has a useful operational meaning
Fawzi and Renner 2015
, and it is crucial to preparing the quantum Gibbs states on a quantum computer
Kastoryano and Brandão 2016
;
Brandão and Kastoryano 2019
;
Kato and Brandão 2019
;
Kuwahara et al. 2020
.
Thus, for non-commuting Hamiltonians with short-range interactions, it is conjectured that, in general, the quantum Markov property holds in an approximate sense:

Conjecture 15

(Quantum Markov conjecture)
.

For arbitrary quantum Gibbs states, the conditional mutual information
{\mathcal{I}}_{\rho_{\beta}}(A:B|E)
(
\Lambda=A\sqcup E\sqcup B
) exponentially decays with the distance between
A
and
B
:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{\rho_{\beta}}(A:B|E)\leq{\rm poly}(|A|,|B|)e^{-d_{A,B}/\xi_{\beta}}

(71)

with
\xi_{\beta}={\rm poly}(\beta)
.

If the inequality (
71
) holds, the exponential clustering for the squashed entanglement is obtained as

\displaystyle E_{\rm sq}(\rho_{\beta,AB})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{I}}_{\rho_{\beta}}(A:B|E)

\displaystyle\leq{\rm poly}(|A|,|B|)e^{-d_{A,B}/\xi_{\beta}},

(72)

where
E=\Lambda\setminus(AB)
and
\rho_{ABE}=\rho_{\beta}
are considered in Eq. (
VI.2
).
Thus far, the above conjecture has been proven only in high-temperature regimes, where thermal phase transition cannot occur, that is,

\beta\lesssim\log(n)
in 1D cases
Kato and Brandão 2019
, and
\beta<\beta_{c}
(
\beta_{c}=\mathcal{O}(1)
) in high-dimensional cases
Kuwahara et al. 2020
.
Moreover, in these temperature regimes, regarding entanglement, considerably stronger statements than (
VI.2
) (i.e., Corollary
4
) have already been derived.

Finally, it is shown that inequality (
VI.2
) cannot be used to prove the exponential clustering of other quantum entanglement measures [e.g., the relative entanglement (
22
) or the entanglement of formation (
79
)] in general.

To upper-bound the other entanglement measures, it is necessary to upper-bound the quantity
\delta_{\rho_{AB}}
, which is defined in Eq. (
II.3
) as
\delta_{\rho_{AB}}:=\inf_{\sigma_{AB}\in{\rm SEP}(A:B)}\left\|\rho_{AB}-\sigma_{AB}\right\|_{1}.

This characterizes the distance between the quantum
\rho_{AB}
and non-entangled states.
The squashed entanglement yields the following upper bound for
\delta_{\rho_{AB}}

Brandão et al. 2011
;
Li and Winter 2014
:

\displaystyle\delta_{\rho_{AB}}\leq 42\sqrt{\mathcal{D}_{AB}E_{\rm sq}(\rho_{AB})},

(73)

where
\mathcal{D}_{AB}
is the dimension of the Hilbert space of
AB
.
If
E_{\rm sq}(\rho_{AB})\ll 1/\mathcal{D}_{AB}
, it can be ensured that
\delta_{\rho_{AB}}
is sufficiently small.
However,
\mathcal{D}_{AB}
is exponentially large, with a size of
|AB|
.
Hence, regardless of the quantum Markov conjecture
15
being proven,
the distance
\delta_{\rho_{\beta,AB}}
for the quantum Gibbs state may still be considerably large when subsets
A
or
B
are as large as the system size
n
.
Thus, a problem similar to that in inequality (
9
) of Proposition
9
is encountered.
Therefore, the clustering problem of bi-partite entanglement cannot be generalized to other entanglement measures by simply clarifying the quantum Markov property.

VI.3
General upper bound on the quantum correlation

Here, it is shown that the entanglement formation
Bennett et al. 1996
;
Wootters 1998
is a simple upper bound for the quantum correlation
{\rm QC}_{\rho_{AB}}(O_{A},O_{B})
.
The relation between the entanglement of formation and the quantum correlation
{\rm QC}_{\rho_{AB}}(O_{A},O_{B})
is derived from that between mutual information
{\mathcal{I}}_{\rho_{AB}}(A:B)
and standard correlation function
{\rm C}_{\rho_{AB}}(O_{A},O_{B})
.
The entanglement of formation is defined as follows:

\displaystyle E_{F}(\rho_{AB})

\displaystyle:=\inf_{\{p_{s},|\psi_{s,AB}\rangle\}}\sum_{s}\frac{p_{s}}{2}{\mathcal{I}}_{|\psi_{s,AB}\rangle}(A:B)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\{p_{s},|\psi_{s,AB}\rangle\}}\sum_{s}p_{s}S_{|\psi_{s,AB}\rangle}(A),

(74)

where
{\mathcal{I}}_{|\psi_{s,AB}\rangle}(A:B)
and
S_{|\psi_{s,AB}\rangle}(A)
are the mutual information and the von Neumann entropy for the reduced density matrix on the subset
A
, respectively. Furthermore,
\inf_{\{p_{s},|\psi_{s,AB}\rangle\}}
is considered for arbitrary decomposition
\rho=\sum_{s}p_{s}|\psi_{s,AB}\rangle\langle\psi_{s,AB}|
with
p_{s}>0
.
In addition,
{\mathcal{I}}_{\rho_{s}}(A:B)=2S_{\rho_{s,AB}}(A)
when
\rho_{s}
is a pure state.

The mutual information
{\mathcal{I}}_{|\psi_{s,AB}\rangle}(A:B)
captures the entire correlations between two subsystems
Wolf et al. 2008
.
Hence, it is quite plausible that the entanglement of formation provides an upper bound for quantum correlations.
Indeed, the following lemma connects the quantum correlation
{\rm QC}_{\rho_{AB}}(O_{A},O_{B})
and the entanglement of formation:

Lemma 16
.

For arbitrary operators
O_{A}
and
O_{B}
, the quantum correlation
{\rm QC}_{\rho_{AB}}(O_{A},O_{B})
is upper-bounded using the entanglement of formation
E_{F}(\rho_{AB})
, as follows:

\displaystyle{\rm QC}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B})\leq 2\|O_{A}\|\cdot\|O_{B}\|\sqrt{E_{F}(\rho_{AB})}.

(75)

Proof.

First, we note that

\displaystyle\sum_{s}p_{s}|{\rm C}_{\rho_{s,AB}}(O_{A},O_{B})|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\sum_{s}p_{s}|{\rm C}_{\rho_{s,AB}}(O_{A},O_{B})|\right)^{2},

(76)

which yields

\displaystyle\inf_{\{p_{s},\rho_{s,AB}\}}\sum_{s}p_{s}|{\rm C}_{\rho_{s,AB}}(O_{A},O_{B})|^{2}\geq[{\rm QC}_{\rho_{AB}}(O_{A},O_{B})]^{2}.

(77)

Hence, the aim is to provide an upper-bound for the LHS in the above inequality.

Second, the classical squashed (c-squashed) entanglement
Yang et al. 2009
, which is obtained from the mixed convex roof of mutual information, is considered
*5
*5

*5

The c-squashed entanglement
E_{\rm sq}^{c}(\rho_{AB})
is obtained from the infimum of

under the restriction of classical extension.
The classical extension of
\rho_{AB}
is defined by the form of
\rho_{ABE}=\sum_{s}p_{s}\rho_{AB,s}\otimes|\phi_{s}\rangle\langle\phi_{s}|
, where
\rho_{AB}=\sum_{s}p_{s}\rho_{AB,s}
is an arbitrary decomposition of
\rho_{AB}
, and
\{|\phi_{s}\rangle\}
is a certain orthonormal basis on
E
.
For the classical extension, the conditional mutual information (
68

Thus, based on the definition, the c-squashed entanglement
E_{\rm sq}^{c}(\rho_{AB})
is larger than or equal to the original squashed entanglement (
VI.2
).
Further, as reported in Ref.
(
Brandão 2008
, Sec. 6.5.2)
, in general,

is obtained.

\displaystyle E_{\rm sq}^{c}(\rho_{AB}):=\inf_{\{p_{s},\rho_{s,AB}\}}\sum_{s}\frac{p_{s}}{2}\mathcal{I}_{\rho_{s,AB}}(A:B),

where
\inf_{\{p_{s},\rho_{s,AB}\}}
is considered for all possible decompositions of
\rho_{AB}

\rho_{AB}=\sum_{s}p_{s}\rho_{s,AB}

The difference between

and
E_{F}(\rho_{AB})
is
whether the decomposed quantum states of
\rho
are restricted to a pure state

*6

*6

The difference between
E_{\rm sq}^{c}(\rho_{AB})
and

can be significantly large.
For example, the quantity
E_{\rm sq}^{c}(\rho_{AB})
is trivially upper-bounded using the mutual information as
\mathcal{I}_{\rho_{AB}}(A:B)/2

By contrast, as indicated in Ref.
Berta et al. 2018

.
Trivially, the entanglement of formation

\displaystyle E_{F}(\rho_{AB})

\displaystyle\geq E_{\rm sq}^{c}(\rho_{AB}).

(79)

Finally,

is compared with the LHS in (

For this purpose, the following inequality reported in Ref.

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\rho_{AB}}(A:B)\geq\frac{|C_{\rho_{AB}}(O_{A},O_{B})|^{2}}{2\|O_{A}\|^{2}\cdot\|O_{B}\|^{2}}.

\displaystyle E_{\rm sq}^{c}(\rho_{AB})

VI.4
Optimality of the obtained bounds

Herein, the optimality of the correlation length
\xi_{\beta}
or
\xi^{\prime}_{\beta}
in Theorems
10
and
13
is discussed.
The
\beta
-dependence of the correlation length
\xi_{\beta}
(i.e.,
\xi_{\beta}\propto\beta
) is shown to be qualitatively optimal, which cannot be improved in general. This point is ensured by the correspondence of the inverse temperatures and spectral gap, as follows:

\displaystyle\beta\longleftrightarrow 1/\Delta

(82)

with
\Delta
being the spectral gap between the ground and first excited states.
Consequently, the correlation length of
\mathcal{O}(\Delta^{-1})
in the gapped ground states
Hastings 2004b
;
Hastings and Koma 2006
;
Nachtergaele and Sims 2006
implies the correlation length of
\mathcal{O}(\beta)
in the thermal states.

To elaborate, first, the following inequality for the number of energy eigenstates in an arbitrary energy shell
(E-1,E]

Hastings 2007b
;
Masanes 2009
;
Kuwahara and Saito 2020a
is assumed:

\displaystyle{\cal N}_{E,1}

\displaystyle\leq n^{cE},

(83)

where
{\cal N}_{E,1}
is the number of eigenstates within the energy shell of
(E-1,E]
, and
c
is a constant of
\mathcal{O}(1)
.
Furthermore, the energy origin is set such that the ground state’s energy is equal to zero.
Here, the above condition is satisfied in various types of quantum many-body systems
Hastings 2007b
.

Thus, under condition (
83
), the quantum Gibbs states
\rho_{\beta}
are close to the ground state
\rho_{\infty}
in the sense that

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{\beta}-\rho_{\infty}\right\|_{1}\leq{\rm const.}\times\frac{e^{-(\beta-c\log(n))\Delta}}{\beta-c\log(n)}.

(84)

Therefore, the properties of the thermal states and the ground state are approximately the same for
\beta\approx\log(n)/\Delta
, as follows:

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{\beta}-\rho_{\infty}\right\|_{1}=1/{\rm poly}(n).

(85)

When the ground state is non-degenerate and gapped, the correlation function
{\rm C}_{\rho_{\infty}}(O_{A},O_{B})
is expressed as
Hastings and Koma 2006
;
Nachtergaele and Sims 2006

\displaystyle{\rm C}_{\rho_{\infty}}(O_{A},O_{B})={\rm QC}_{\rho_{\infty}}(O_{A},O_{B})={\rm const.}\times e^{-\mathcal{O}(\Delta)R},

(86)

where Eq. (
34
) is used for the pure state in the first equation.
Subsequently, using the continuity bound (
38
)

\displaystyle{\rm QC}_{\rho_{\beta}}(O_{A},O_{B})={\rm C}_{\rho_{\infty}}(O_{A},O_{B})-1/{\rm poly}(n)

\displaystyle={\rm const.}\times e^{-\mathcal{O}(\Delta)R}-1/{\rm poly}(n)

\displaystyle={\rm const.}\times e^{-\mathcal{O}(R)/(\beta/\log(n))}-1/{\rm poly}(n),

(87)

where the second equation results from the fact that
\beta\approx\log(n)/\Delta
implies
\Delta\approx\log(n)/\beta
.
Thus, the quantum correlation starts to decay for
R\gtrsim\beta/\log(n)
; hence, the correlation length is proportional to
\beta
at sufficiently low temperatures.

By contrast, for the WYD skew and quantum Fisher information, there is scope for improvement in the present
\beta
-dependences, which have been assigned inequalities (
14
) and (

), respectively.
In the ground states, the WYD skew and quantum Fisher information reduce to the variance of the operator.
For an arbitrary operator
K
expressed in Eq. (

), the variance

is upper-bounded by

;
Kuwahara et al. 2017

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\rho_{\infty}}^{(\alpha)}(K)=(\Delta K)^{2}\leq{\rm const.}\times\Delta^{-1}n.

(88)

The above inequality holds in infinite dimensional systems and long-range interacting systems; hence, the (
\beta
,
\Delta
) correspondence (

) indicates an improvement in the current upper bounds as

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\rho_{\beta}}^{(\alpha)}(K)\leq\mathcal{O}(\beta n)\quad{\rm and}\quad\mathcal{F}_{\rho_{\beta}}(K)\leq\mathcal{O}(\beta n),

(89)

which affords better bounds in dimensions greater than

(
D\geq 2
).

VI.5
Beyond quantum Gibbs states

Throughout the discussion, the equilibrium situation is considered at a finite temperature. However, when considering a non-equilibrium density matrix, the entanglement properties exhibit different properties in general
Galve et al. 2010
.
Consequently, a natural question arises as to whether the current results hold for more general quantum states.
Based on definition (
33
) of the quantum correlation, concavity is satisfied, that is,

\displaystyle{\rm QC}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B})\leq p_{1}{\rm QC}_{\rho_{1}}(O_{A},O_{B})+p_{2}{\rm QC}_{\rho_{2}}(O_{A},O_{B})

for an arbitrary decomposition of
\rho=p_{1}\rho_{1}+p_{2}\rho_{2}
(
p_{1}>0
,
p_{2}>0
).
Hence, considering a quantum state in the form of

\displaystyle\rho=\int_{0}^{\infty}a(z)e^{-zH}dz

(90)

with
a(z)
being a non-negative function, Theorem
10
can be applied to the state
\rho
.
Subsequently, the state
\rho
has a finite quantum correlation length, while the entanglement clustering is also satisfied.
A similar discussion can be also applied to the WYD skew information
\mathcal{I}_{\rho}^{(\alpha)}(K)
and the quantum Fisher information
\mathcal{F}_{\rho}(K)
owing to their concavities
*7
*7

*7

The concavity of the WYD skew information has been proven through the celebrated Wigner-Yanase-Dyson-Lieb theorem
Lieb 1973
;
Hansen 2006
.
The concavity of the quantum Fisher information can be easily proven from the convex roof expression (
299
), which has been proven in
Tóth and Petz 2013
;
Yu 2013
.
.
Herein, if the state
\rho
includes extremely low-temperature states, for example,
\int_{\beta_{0}}^{\infty}a(z){\rm tr}(e^{-zH})\approx 1
with
\beta_{0}\approx\mathcal{O}(n)
,
the state
\rho
is similar to low-temperature Gibbs states; consequently, the quantum correlation length may become large.

As an important class of quantum states, the following density matrix is considered to be characterized by a monotonically decreasing function
F(x)
:

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{F(H)}{{\rm tr}\left[F(H)\right]},

(91)

where
F(x)\geq 0
.
This class of the quantum state is referred to as the passive state
Pusz and Woronowicz 1978
;
Lenard 1978
and plays a crucial role in quantum thermodynamics
Perarnau-Llobet et al. 2015
;
Skrzypczyk et al. 2015
;
Sparaciari et al. 2017
;
Uzdin and Rahav 2018
.
Moreover, the quantum Gibbs state trivially corresponds to the case
F(x)=e^{-\beta x}
.
Based on the Bernstein-Widder theorem
Bernstein 1929
;
Widder 1931
;
Qi and Agarwal 2019
, the passive state (
91
) can be represented in the form of Eq. (
90
) if and only if the function
F(x)
is completely monotonic, as follows:

\displaystyle(-1)^{m}\frac{d^{m}}{dx^{m}}F(x)\geq 0

(92)

for arbitrary
m\geq 0
.
Therefore, for every passive state with condition (
92
), structural restrictions similar to that for the quantum Gibbs state must be imposed
*8
*8

*8

Passive states usually possess different properties from those in Gibbs states.
For example, regarding the Markov property in classical systems, only the Gibbs states satisfy the property from the Hammersley-Clifford theorem
Hammersley and Clifford 1971
, whereas the passive states do not.
.

VII
Summary and Future works

This study primarily addressed the conjecture of the exponential clustering of bipartite entanglement,
which revealed the fundamental aspect of long-range entanglement.
The entanglement was accessed via the introduction of a novel concept, referred to as the quantum correlation
{\rm QC}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B})
,
which is defined by the convex roof of the standard bipartite correlation function, as in Eq. (
33
).
Consequently, as a fundamental theorem, the exponential clustering of the quantum correlation was derived, which holds at arbitrary temperatures, even at the critical point of thermal phase transition.
Based on its definition and exploiting the fact that it uses the convex roof, quantum correlation exhibits properties similar to those of entanglement.
Subsequently, several basic statements in Sec.
III
were derived, including the relationship between the quantum correlation and the PPT relative entanglement (Proposition
9
).
Further, based on the clustering theorem for the quantum correlation, entanglement clustering theorems (Corollary
11
and Theorem
12
) for PPT relative entanglement (
2
) were presented.
Moreover, using similar analytical techniques, the exponential clustering of another type of quantum correlation based on the WYD skew information (Theorem
13
) was derived, which yielded the fundamental limitations of the WYD skew and quantum Fisher information (Corollary
14
). Consequently, these serve as representative measures for quantum coherence and macroscopic entanglement.

Furthermore, this study expressed simple and general no-go theorems on the existence of long-range entanglement.
On the other hand, there is still room for improvement of the present analytical techniques, and hence the obtained results may be further strengthened.
Based on the results obtained, the strongest form of the bi-partite entanglement clustering may be expressed as follows:

[The strongest conjecture]

\displaystyle E_{R}(\rho_{\beta,AB})\leq{\rm poly}(|A|,|B|)e^{-R/\tilde{\xi}_{\beta}}

(93)

for an arbitrary choice of
A
and
B
such that
d_{A,B}=R
,
where
\tilde{\xi}_{\beta}={\rm poly}(\beta)
and
{\rm poly}(x)
denote a finite degree polynomial.
As shown in Sec.
II.3
, from the continuity bounds, inequality (
VII
) yields the same upper bound for other entanglement measures.
However, the main theorems presented in this paper did not arrived at this form of entanglement clustering, and further investigations are required to refine the current results.

In conclusion, this study unveiled a fundamental limit on the characteristic length scale, such that certain types of quantum effects can exist.
Moreover, the present results do not depend on system details and hold at arbitrary temperatures.
The understanding of the universal structural constraints in low-temperature physics, which must be satisfied for every quantum many-body system, still remains limited.
Consequently, identifying these constraints is a critical task for understanding the complicated quantum many-body phases as well as developing efficient algorithms for quantum many-body simulations.
This study is expected to introduce a novel approach to address this profound problem.

Finally, the following topics are mentioned as specific open questions:

•

First, deriving a clustering theorem for the relative entanglement instead of the PPT relative entanglement.
This may be addressed by resolving conjecture
7
.
Subsequently, Proposition
9
can be improved; in other words, under the condition of (almost) zero quantum correlations [i.e., Ineq. (
51
)], a similar inequality to (
9
) may hold for the relative entanglement
E_{R}(\rho_{AB})
instead of
E_{R}^{{\rm PPT}}(\rho_{AB})
.
This improvement immediately yields the entanglement clustering for other popular measures, such as the entanglement of formations [see also the discussion after Ineq. (
II.3
)].

•

As a related problem, the
(|A|,|B|)
dependence in Corollary
11
may be improved under dimensions greater than one.
In the present form, the independence is in the exponential form, and hence, a meaningful bound for the case of
|A|
and
|B|
being as large as the system size cannot be obtained.
To improve this, as has been discussed after Corollary
11
, considering the operator correlations
{\rm QC}_{\rho}(A,B)
alone is not sufficient.
Instead, the complete information between the two subsystems must be considered.
However, at the current stage, the problem may be challenging as it should include an analogous difficulty to the data hiding problem in the context of the area law conjecture at zero temperature
Hastings 2007b
;
Brandão and Horodecki 2013
;
Brandão and Horodecki 2015
.

•

Third, identifying the class of quantum coherence measures
Baumgratz et al. 2014
, which are always short range at non-zero temperatures, remains an intriguing problem.
In this study, it was shown that bi-partite entanglement cannot exist at long distances; however, as has been demonstrated in Sec.
VI.1
, macroscopic quantum effects do not necessarily imply long-distance entanglement.
For example, quantum discord, a well-known measure for quantum correlation
Ollivier and Zurek 2001
;
Luo 2008
, only decays algebraically at thermal critical points
Malpetti and Roscilde 2016
.
Thus, the current results can still be expanded to include other coherence measures.

•

Finally, the question remains as to whether entanglement clustering can be applied to more practical problems such as the efficient simulation of quantum Gibbs states.
The clustering of entanglement imposes a strong constraint on the structure of quantum Gibbs states. Hence, it is likely that the property can be utilized to reduce computational complexity.

To prove the statements, the following steps are adopted.
In the first and second lemmas (Lemmas

Thereafter, in the third, fourth, and fifth lemmas (Lemmas
19

20

In the first step, the general upper bound for the quantum correlation is proven, as follows:

Lemma 17

For an arbitrary operator

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\|\left[\left(\rho^{-1/2}\mathcal{L}_{O_{A}}\rho^{1/2}\right),\left(\rho^{1/2}\mathcal{L}_{O_{B}}\rho^{-1/2}\right)\right]\right\|.

(141)

140

O_{A}
and
O_{B}

\rho^{-1/2}\mathcal{L}_{O_{A}}\rho^{1/2}

\rho^{1/2}\mathcal{L}_{O_{B}}\rho^{-1/2}

\{|\psi_{m}\rangle\}

\{|\phi_{m}\rangle\}

O_{A}
and
O_{B}

For an arbitrary operator

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{m}|O|\phi_{m}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}\frac{U_{m,s^{\prime}}U^{\ast}_{m,s}}{p_{m}}\sqrt{\lambda_{s}\lambda_{s^{\prime}}}\langle\lambda_{s}|O|\lambda_{s^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}\frac{U_{m,s^{\prime}}U^{\ast}_{m,s}}{p_{m}}\frac{\lambda_{s}+\lambda_{s}^{\prime}}{2}\langle\lambda_{s}|\mathcal{L}_{O}|\lambda_{s^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}\frac{U_{m,s^{\prime}}U^{\ast}_{m,s}}{p_{m}}\frac{1}{2}\langle\lambda_{s}|\{\rho,\mathcal{L}_{O}\}|\lambda_{s^{\prime}}\rangle

where definition (

) is used for
\mathcal{L}_{O}
from the second to third equations.

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{O}:=\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}\frac{2\sqrt{\lambda_{s}\lambda_{s^{\prime}}}}{\lambda_{s}+\lambda_{s^{\prime}}}\langle\lambda_{s}|O|\lambda_{s^{\prime}}\rangle|\lambda_{s}\rangle\langle\lambda_{s^{\prime}}|.

(161)

Herein,
\{|\psi_{m}\rangle\}
are chosen as the simultaneous eigenstates of
\mathcal{L}_{O_{A}}
and
\mathcal{L}_{O_{B}}
.

[\mathcal{L}_{O_{A}},\mathcal{L}_{O_{B}}]=0

),

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{m}|O_{A}|\phi_{m}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2p_{m}}\langle\psi_{m}|\{\rho,\mathcal{L}_{O_{A}}\}|\psi_{m}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{1,m}}{p_{m}}\langle\psi_{m}|\rho|\psi_{m}\rangle=\alpha_{1,m}

(162)

and
\langle\phi_{m}|O_{B}|\phi_{m}\rangle=\alpha_{2,m}
,
where
\alpha_{1,m}
and
\alpha_{2,m}
are defined as the
m
th eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}_{O_{A}}
and
\mathcal{L}_{O_{B}}
, respectively.
We, therefore, obtain

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{m}|O_{A}|\phi_{m}\rangle\langle\phi_{m}|O_{B}|\phi_{m}\rangle=\alpha_{1,m}\alpha_{2,m}

(163)

for an arbitrary

We next consider
\langle\phi_{m}|O_{A}O_{B}|\phi_{m}\rangle
.
Then, from Eq. (
D.2
),

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{m}|O_{A}O_{B}|\phi_{m}\rangle=\frac{1}{2p_{m}}\langle\psi_{m}|\{\rho,\mathcal{L}_{O_{A}O_{B}}\}|\psi_{m}\rangle.

(164)

Further, based on the equation, if

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{m}|O|\psi_{m^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}\frac{U_{m^{\prime},s^{\prime}}U^{\ast}_{m,s}}{\sqrt{p_{m}}}\sqrt{\lambda_{s}}\langle\lambda_{s}|O|\lambda_{s^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}\frac{U_{m^{\prime},s^{\prime}}U^{\ast}_{m,s}}{\sqrt{p_{m}}}\sqrt{\lambda_{s}\lambda_{s^{\prime}}}\langle\lambda_{s}|O\rho^{-1/2}|\lambda_{s^{\prime}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}\frac{U_{m^{\prime},s^{\prime}}U^{\ast}_{m,s}}{\sqrt{p_{m}}}\frac{\lambda_{s}+\lambda_{s}^{\prime}}{2}\langle\lambda_{s}|\mathcal{L}_{O\rho^{-1/2}}|\lambda_{s^{\prime}}\rangle

where

is used
from definition (
161

(166)

where

\mathcal{L}_{O_{B}}|\psi_{m}\rangle=\alpha_{2,m}|\psi_{m}\rangle
, the above equation can be reduced to

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{m}|O_{A}O_{B}|\phi_{m}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4p_{m}}\langle\psi_{m}|\left(\rho\alpha_{1,m}\alpha_{2,m}+\alpha_{1,m}\rho\alpha_{2,m}+\alpha_{1,m}\alpha_{2,m}\rho+\rho\mathcal{L}_{O_{A}}\rho^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{O_{B}}\rho\right)|\psi_{m}\rangle

(168)

where
\langle\psi_{m}|\rho|\psi_{m}\rangle=p_{m}
.

To obtain this, consider

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{m}|\rho\mathcal{L}_{O_{A}}\rho^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{O_{B}}\rho|\psi_{m}\rangle

\displaystyle\left|\langle\phi_{m}|O_{A}O_{B}|\phi_{m}\rangle-\alpha_{1,m}\alpha_{2,m}\right|\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\|\left[\left(\rho^{-1/2}\mathcal{L}_{O_{A}}\rho^{1/2}\right),\left(\rho^{1/2}\mathcal{L}_{O_{B}}\rho^{-1/2}\right)\right]\right\|.

(171)

Therefore, by combining the above inequality and Eq. (
163
)
with (
159

17

\square

The PPT relative entanglement in Eq. (

Herein, consider entanglement negativity, which is given by

Appendix H
Quantum Fisher information matrix

Here, the definition (
33
) for the quantum correlation
{\rm QC}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B})
proposed is compared
with the quantum Fisher information matrix.
First, it should be noted that the quantum Fisher information can be defined in the form of the convex roof of the variance.
If
\rho
is a pure state, the quantum Fisher information
\mathcal{F}_{\rho}(K)
simply reduces to the variance of
K
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\rho}(K)=4\left(\langle\psi|K^{2}|\psi\rangle-\langle\psi|K|\psi\rangle^{2}\right),

(298)

where
\rho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
.
For the general state
\rho
, the quantum Fisher information is known to be equal to the convex roof of the variance
Tóth and Petz 2013
;
Yu 2013
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\rho}(K)=4\inf_{\{p_{s},|\psi_{s}\rangle\}}\sum_{s}p_{s}\left(\langle\psi_{s}|K^{2}|\psi_{s}\rangle-\langle\psi_{s}|K|\psi_{s}\rangle^{2}\right),

(299)

where minimization is considered for all possible decompositions of
\rho
, such that
\rho=\sum_{s}p_{s}|\psi_{s}\rangle\langle\psi_{s}|
with
p_{s}>0
.
Thus, the quantum Fisher information shows a certain similarity to the quantum correlation
{\rm QC}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B})
.

To view this similarity in more detail, consider the following quantum Fisher information matrix
Liu et al. 2019
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\rho}(O_{i},O_{j})=\sum_{s,s^{\prime}}\frac{2(\lambda_{s}-\lambda_{s^{\prime}})^{2}}{\lambda_{s}+\lambda_{s^{\prime}}}\langle\lambda_{s}|O_{i}|\lambda_{s^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\lambda_{s^{\prime}}|O_{j}|\lambda_{s}\rangle.

(300)

Herein,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\rho}(K)=\sum_{i,j}\mathcal{F}_{\rho}(O_{i},O_{j}).

(301)

The quantum Fisher information matrix has been used in the multiparameter quantum estimation theory
Helstrom 1969
;
Liu et al. 2019
;
Gessner et al. 2018
;
Fiderer et al. 2021
.
Then, the question remains as to whether it can be associated with the convex roof of certain observables in the analogy of Eq. (
299
).

The partial answer to this question is yes. The quantum Fisher information matrix is relevant to the following quantity
{\rm QC}^{\ast}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B})
, which is weaker than (
33
):

\displaystyle{\rm QC}^{\ast}_{\rho}(O_{A},O_{B}):=\inf_{\{p_{s},\rho_{s}\}}\left|\sum_{s}p_{s}{\rm C}_{\rho_{s}}(O_{A},O_{B})\right|,

(302)

which is the minimization of the absolute value of the average correlation.

17
:

Lemma 28
.

O_{A}
and
O_{B}

302

(304)

has been defined in Eq. (
139

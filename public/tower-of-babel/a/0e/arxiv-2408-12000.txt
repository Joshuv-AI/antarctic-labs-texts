Gevrey Regularity for a Fluid-Structure Interaction Model Thanks: Author 1 partially supported by NSF Grant DMS-1948942
George Avalos, Dylan McKnight, Sara McKnight

Abstract
. A result of Gevrey regularity is ascertained for a semigroup which models a fluid-structure interaction problem. In this model, the fluid evolves in a piecewise smooth or convex geometry 𝒪 \mathcal{O} . On a portion of the boundary, a fourth order plate equation is coupled with the fluid through pressure and matching velocities. The key to obtaining the conclusion of Gevrey regularity is an appropriate estimation of the resolvent of the associated C 0 C_{0} -semigroup operator. Moreover, a numerical scheme and example is provided which empirically demonstrates smoothing of the fluid-structure semigroup.



Keywords: Fluid-structure interaction Stokes equations Kirchoff plate Gevrey class semigroups

1.
Introduction

During the course of communication between the authors of
[
3
]
and the late Igor Chueshov, the latter posed the conjecture that the
C_{0}
-semigroup
\{e^{\mathcal{A}t}\}_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}
– formulated below – which models the fluid-structure interaction (FSI) (
1
) below, besides manifesting exponential decay – see
[

]
– is actually analytic in an appropriate sector of the complex plane. Generally, couplings of hyperbolic and parabolic dynamics which evolve on distinct geometries and interact strictly accross a boundary interface are not at all analytic or analytic-like; see
[
4
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
(and references therein).

However, unlike the FSI systems in said references, the PDE components in FSI (
1
) below do
not
share the same dimensionality: in particular, in (
1
), the three dimensional incompressible flow “immerses” the two dimensional plate dynamics. Accordingly, it would seem reasonable to attempt to acertain the Gevrey regularity of the associated FSI semigroup. Recall that a strongly continuous semigroup
S(t)
on a Banach space
B
is of Gevrey class
\delta
(where
\delta>1)
if
S(t)
is infinitely differentiable on
(t_{0},\infty),
for some
t_{0}>0,
and for every compact
K\in(t_{0},\infty)
and for any
\theta>0,
there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|S^{(n)}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(B)}\leq C\theta^{n}(n!)^{\delta},

for all
k\in K
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
(see
[
23
]
, p. 143). Roughly speaking, Gevrey regularity for a strongly continuous semigroup is a state which lies between differentiability and analyticity.

Our main result of Gevrey regularity, in Theorem
3
, is similar to that obtained in
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
, which generally deal with 2D-1D FSI with moving boundary interface. Therein, the necessary Gevrey (resolvent) estimates for their fixed domain models are obtained for two dimensional fluids which are coupled to one dimensional beams, on specified geometrical configurations. On such special geometries, the authors in
[
6
]
are able to clearly characterize the FSI generator resolvent, and subsequently estimate it.

In contrast, the present work deals with 3D-2D FSI on rough, convex domains – see Assumption 1 below. Moreover, here there is no clear representation and estimation of the associated FSI resolvent operator (there cannot be, for arbitrary domains). Instead, our short proof uses in an essential way the underlying dissipation which arises from the fluid PDE component of the FSI dynamics.

2.1.
The Underlying Abstract Framework

On this geometry
\{\mathcal{O},\Omega\},
we will consider the following fluid-structure interaction (FSI):

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}w_{tt}+\mbox{\AA}w=p|_{\Omega}\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T)\\
w=\frac{\partial w}{\partial n}=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\\

\mbox{div}(u)=0\text{ in }\mathcal{O}\times(0,T)\\

[w(0),w_{t}(0),u(0)]=[w_{1}^{0},w_{2}^{0},u^{0}]\in\mathcal{H},\end{cases}

We start by writing the biharmonic operator with clamped boundary conditions:

\mbox{\AA}:=\Delta^{2}
,

D(\mbox{\AA})=H^{4}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
,

As defined,
\mbox{\AA}:D(\mbox{\AA})\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is a positive definite, self-adjoint operator, and so
\mbox{\AA}^{s}
is well-defined for all

. Note that in making this conclusion, we are implicitly using the higher regularity results of
[
10
]
(see theorem 2, p. 563 therein) and the geometric Assumption 1 to conclude that
Å

onto

Moreover, we define the following Robin maps

\tilde{R}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{-1/2}(S),H^{1}(\mathcal{O}))
:

\displaystyle R(g)=f\mbox{ iff }\begin{cases}\Delta f=0\mbox{ in }\mathcal{O}\\
\frac{\partial f}{\partial n}+f=g\mbox{ on }\Omega\\
\frac{\partial f}{\partial n}=0\mbox{ on }S\end{cases}

\displaystyle\tilde{R}(g)=f\mbox{ iff }\begin{cases}\Delta f=0\mbox{ in }\mathcal{O}\\
\frac{\partial f}{\partial n}+f=0\mbox{ on }\Omega\\
\frac{\partial f}{\partial n}=g\mbox{ on }S\end{cases}

Subsequently, we set

(2)

\displaystyle G_{1}(w_{1})

\displaystyle=R(\mbox{\AA}w_{1}),

(3)

\displaystyle G_{2}(u)

\displaystyle=R(\Delta u^{(3)}|_{\Omega})+\tilde{R}(\Delta u\cdot n|_{S}).

The associated finite energy space
\mathcal{H}
is given by:

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}w_{1}\\
w_{2}\\
u\end{bmatrix}\in[H_{0}^{2}(\Omega)\cap\hat{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\hat{L}^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{H}_{f}:u\cdot n|_{S}=0,~u=\begin{bmatrix}0\\

w_{2}\end{bmatrix}\text{ on }\Omega\right\}.

Here,

is

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{f}:=\{u\in L^{2}(\mathcal{O}):\mbox{div}(u)=0,~u\cdot n|_{S}=0\},

and

\hat{L}^{2}(\Omega)=\left\{g\in L^{2}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}g\,d\Omega=0\right\}.

Throughout, we will use the following notation for inner products and norms:

(5)

\displaystyle\|w\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},~\langle w,\tilde{w}\rangle_{\Omega}=(w,\tilde{w})_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(6)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{O}}

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})},~~(u,\tilde{u})_{\mathcal{O}}=(u,\tilde{u})_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}.

Given the characterization

]

\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\langle\Phi,\tilde{\Phi}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\left\langle\begin{bmatrix}w_{1}\\
w_{2}\\
u\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{w}_{1}\\
\tilde{w}_{2}\\
\tilde{u}\end{bmatrix}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\left\langle\mbox{\AA}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{1},\mbox{\AA}^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{w}_{1}\right\rangle_{\Omega}+\langle w_{2},\tilde{w}_{2}\rangle_{\Omega}+(u,\tilde{u})_{\mathcal{O}}.

Following the procedure laid out in

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta p=0\text{ in }\mathcal{O}\\
\frac{\partial p}{\partial n}=\Delta u\cdot n|_{\Omega}\text{ on }S\\

Given that

.

(see Theorem 1.1 of

3.
Gevrey Class

By Gevrey class, we mean the following estimate on the resolvent of
\mathcal{A}
along some vertical line in the right half complex plane.

Theorem 2

(Theorem 4 of
[
23
]
)
.

Let
S(t)
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup generated by
\mathcal{A}
satisfying
\|S(t)\|\leq Me^{\omega t}
. Suppose that for some
\mu\geq\omega
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
,

(11)

\displaystyle\limsup_{|\beta|\to\infty}|\beta|^{\alpha}\|R(\mu+i\beta;\mathcal{A})\|=C<\infty.

then
S(t)
is of Gevrey class
\delta
for
t>0
for each
\delta>\alpha^{-1}
.

For
C_{0}-
semigroups of contractions, one has that
M=1
and
\omega=0
. Hence, we will take
\mu=0
(c.f. Theorem 1.1 of
[
14
]
). Intuitively, one may think of Gevrey class as an intermediary between differentiability and analyticity.

The main result to be proved here is as follows:

Theorem 3
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

(12)

\|R(i\beta;\mathcal{A})\|_{op}\leq\frac{C}{|\beta|^{\alpha}},

for all
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
which satisfies
|\beta|\geq\beta_{0}>0
(
\beta_{0}
fixed). Accordingly, the FSI semigroup
\{e^{\mathcal{A}t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is of Gevrey class
\delta=2+\rho
, for
t>0,
where
\rho>0
is arbitrarily small.

4.
Gevrey Class Estimates of the Generator

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Given
\mathcal{A}:D(\mathcal{A})\subset\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
of (
9
) - (
10
), we consider the resolvent equation

(13)

\displaystyle(i\beta I-\mathcal{A})\Phi=\Phi^{*}.

where dependent variable
\Phi=[w_{1},w_{2},u]^{T}\in D(\mathcal{A})
and data
\Phi^{*}=[w_{1}^{*},w_{2}^{*},u^{*}]^{T}\in\mathcal{H}.
Also, fixed (“frequency domain”) parameter
\beta
satisfies
|\beta|>\beta_{0},
where
\beta_{0}\geq 1.

In view of (
12
), we must obtain a suitable estimate for the solution variable
\Phi.
Taking the inner product of both sides of (
13
) with respect to
\Phi,
we have

(14)

((i\beta I-\mathcal{A})\Phi,\Phi)_{\mathcal{H}}=(\Phi^{*},\Phi)_{\mathcal{H}}.

Now, using the definition of
\mathcal{A}
in (
9
), we have

\displaystyle(-\mathcal{A}\Phi,\Phi)_{\mathcal{H}}=

\displaystyle-\langle\mbox{\AA}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{2},\mbox{\AA}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{1}\rangle_{\Omega}+\langle\mbox{\AA}w_{1},w_{2}\rangle_{\Omega}-\langle p|_{\Omega},w_{2}\rangle_{\Omega}-(\Delta u,u)_{\mathcal{O}}+(\nabla p,u)_{\mathcal{O}}.

Here, the pressure variable
p
is given viz.

p=G_{1}(w_{1})+G_{2}(u),

as in (
8
) (and (
2
)-(
3
)).

Integrating by parts and using the matching velocity boundary condition
u|_{\Omega}=[0,0,w_{2}]
(as well as
u|_{\partial\mathcal{O}\setminus\Omega}=0),
we have

\displaystyle-(\mathcal{A}\Phi,\Phi)_{\mathcal{H}}=2i\im\left\langle\mbox{\AA}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{1},\mbox{\AA}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{2}\right\rangle_{\Omega}-\|\nabla u\|_{\mathcal{O}}^{2}.

Applying this relation to the left hand side of (
14
), we have now

(15)

\displaystyle i\beta\|\Phi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}+2i\im\langle\mbox{\AA}w_{1},w_{2}\rangle_{\Omega}+\|\nabla u\|_{\mathcal{O}}^{2}=\langle\Phi^{*},\Phi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

Taking real and imaginary parts of (
15
) and applying the triangle inequality gives the following inequalities:

(16)

\displaystyle\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|\beta|}|\langle\Phi^{*},\Phi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}|+\frac{2}{|\beta|}|\langle\mbox{\AA}w_{1},w_{2}\rangle_{\Omega}|,

(17)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{\mathcal{O}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|\langle\Phi^{*},\Phi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}|.

The main task is to bound the second term on the right hand side of (
16
). At this point, we recall the characterization of the domains of fractional powers of
\mbox{\AA}:D(\mbox{\AA})\subset L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
in
[
18
]
. Namely,

(18)

D(\mbox{\AA}^{\alpha})=\begin{cases}H^{4\alpha}(\Omega),&\text{ for }0\leq\alpha<\frac{1}{8}\\
H_{0}^{4\alpha}(\Omega),&\text{ for }\alpha\in\left(\frac{1}{8},\frac{3}{8}\right)\cup\left(\frac{3}{8},\frac{1}{2}\right]\\
H^{4\alpha}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{2}(\Omega),&\text{ for }\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq 1.\end{cases}

Recalling the definition of the FSI generator domain
D(\mathcal{A})
from (
10
), we then transfer
\mbox{\AA}^{\frac{1}{8}-\varepsilon}
from
w_{1}
onto
w_{2}
, where
\varepsilon>0
is small, and interpolate.

(19)

\displaystyle\frac{2}{|\beta|}|\langle\mbox{\AA}w_{1},w_{2}\rangle_{\Omega}|

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{|\beta|}|\langle\mbox{\AA}^{\frac{7}{8}+\varepsilon}w_{1},\mbox{\AA}^{\frac{1}{8}-\varepsilon}w_{2}\rangle_{\Omega}|

(20)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{|\beta|}\|\mbox{\AA}^{\frac{7}{8}+\varepsilon}w_{1}\|_{\Omega}\|\mbox{\AA}^{\frac{1}{8}-\varepsilon}w_{2}\|_{\Omega}

(21)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{|\beta|}\|\mbox{\AA}w_{1}\|_{\Omega}^{\frac{3}{4}+2\varepsilon}\|\mbox{\AA}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{1}\|_{\Omega}^{\frac{1}{4}-2\varepsilon}\|\mbox{\AA}^{\frac{1}{8}-\varepsilon}w_{2}\|_{\Omega}.

Next, we note that
D(\mbox{\AA}^{\frac{1}{8}-\varepsilon})=H^{\frac{1}{2}-4\varepsilon}(\Omega)
from (
18
). Applying this characterization to the last term of inequality (
21
) and invoking the Sobolev Trace Theorem gives

\displaystyle\frac{2}{|\beta|}\|\mbox{\AA}w_{1}\|_{\Omega}^{\frac{3}{4}+2\varepsilon}\|\mbox{\AA}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{1}\|_{\Omega}^{\frac{1}{4}-2\varepsilon}\|\mbox{\AA}^{\frac{1}{8}-\varepsilon}w_{2}\|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|\beta|}\|\mbox{\AA}w_{1}\|_{\Omega}^{\frac{3}{4}+2\varepsilon}\|\mbox{\AA}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{1}\|_{\Omega}^{\frac{1}{4}-2\varepsilon}\|w_{2}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}-4\varepsilon}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|\beta|}\|\mbox{\AA}w_{1}\|_{\Omega}^{\frac{3}{4}+2\varepsilon}\|\mbox{\AA}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{1}\|_{\Omega}^{\frac{1}{4}-2\varepsilon}\|w_{2}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|\beta|}\|\mbox{\AA}w_{1}\|_{\Omega}^{\frac{3}{4}+2\varepsilon}\|\mbox{\AA}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{1}\|_{\Omega}^{\frac{1}{4}-2\varepsilon}\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{O})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|\beta|}\|\mbox{\AA}w_{1}\|_{\Omega}^{\frac{3}{4}+2\varepsilon}\|\mbox{\AA}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{1}\|_{\Omega}^{\frac{1}{4}-2\varepsilon}\|\nabla u\|_{\mathcal{O}}.

In obtaining this estimate, we have also invoked the matching of velocities on
\Omega
and Poincare’s inequality. We next turn our attention to the two terms involving
w_{1}
. Majorizing by the measurements
\|\mathcal{A}\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}
and
\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}
, then applying (
13
) and (
17
) gives

\displaystyle\frac{C}{|\beta|}\|\mbox{\AA}w_{1}\|_{\Omega}^{\frac{3}{4}+2\varepsilon}\|\mbox{\AA}^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{1}\|_{\Omega}^{\frac{1}{4}-2\varepsilon}\|\nabla u\|_{\mathcal{O}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|\beta|}\|\mathcal{A}\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{3}{4}+2\varepsilon}\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{4}-2\varepsilon}\|\nabla u\|_{\mathcal{O}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|\beta|}\|i\beta\Phi-\Phi^{*}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{3}{4}+2\varepsilon}\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{4}-2\varepsilon}\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Phi^{*}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|\beta|}\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{4}-2\varepsilon}\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\Phi^{*}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(|\beta|^{\frac{3}{4}+2\varepsilon}\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{3}{4}+2\varepsilon}+\|\Phi^{*}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{3}{4}+2\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{|\beta|}(|\beta|^{\frac{3}{4}+2\varepsilon}\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\Phi^{*}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{3}{4}-2\varepsilon}\|\Phi^{*}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{5}{4}+2\varepsilon}).

Finally, take
\varepsilon_{1}>0
. We apply Young’s inequality with
\varepsilon_{1}
(Appendix B.2.c of
[
17
]
) to the two terms on the right hand side to get

(22)

\displaystyle\frac{C}{|\beta|}|\beta|^{\frac{3}{4}+2\varepsilon}\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\Phi^{*}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{|\beta|^{\frac{1}{4}-2\varepsilon}}\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\Phi^{*}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\varepsilon_{1}\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\frac{C(\varepsilon_{1})}{|\beta|^{1-8\varepsilon}}\|\Phi^{*}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}},

(23)

\displaystyle\frac{C}{|\beta|}\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{3}{4}-2\varepsilon}\|\Phi^{*}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{5}{4}+2\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon_{1}\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\frac{C(\varepsilon_{1})}{|\beta|^{\frac{8}{5+8\varepsilon}}}\|\Phi^{*}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

Next, note that
1-8\varepsilon<\frac{2}{\frac{5}{4}+2\varepsilon}
, so
|\beta|^{1-8\varepsilon}<|\beta|^{\frac{2}{\frac{5}{4}+2\varepsilon}}
. Applying this to (
23
) combining with (
22
), and rescaling
\varepsilon>0
gives the final estimate

(24)

\displaystyle\frac{2}{|\beta|}|\langle\mbox{\AA}w_{1},w_{2}\rangle_{\Omega}|\leq C\varepsilon_{1}\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\frac{C(\varepsilon_{1})}{|\beta|^{1-8\varepsilon}}\|\Phi^{*}\|^{2}.

Returning to (
16
), we apply Young’s inequality with
\varepsilon_{1}
to the first term of the right hand side, and (
24
) to the second term of the right hand side to obtain

(25)

\displaystyle\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{1}\|\Phi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}+\frac{C(\varepsilon_{1})}{|\beta|^{2}}\|\Phi^{*}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}+C\varepsilon_{1}\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\frac{C(\varepsilon_{1})}{|\beta|^{1-8\varepsilon}}\|\Phi^{*}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

(26)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon_{1}\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\frac{C(\varepsilon_{1})}{|\beta|^{1-8\varepsilon}}\|\Phi^{*}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}.

Take
\varepsilon_{1}
sufficiently small so that
1-C\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,1)
. Rearranging and taking the square root yields

(27)

\displaystyle\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\frac{C(\varepsilon_{1})}{(1-C\varepsilon_{1})|\beta|^{\frac{1}{2}-4\varepsilon}}\|\Phi^{*}\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

That is, we infer the resolvent estimate

(28)

\displaystyle\|R(i\beta;\mathcal{A})\|_{op}\leq\frac{C(\varepsilon_{1})}{(1-C\varepsilon_{1})|\beta|^{\frac{1}{2}-4\varepsilon}}.

To conclude: The FSI semigroup S(t) = exp(tA) is of Gevrey class
\delta=\frac{2}{1-8\varepsilon},
or with
\rho=\frac{16\varepsilon}{1-8\varepsilon}
where
\varepsilon
is arbitrarily small,
\delta=2+\rho,
and
t\geq 0.
∎∎

One should note that this result arises in absence of any damping on the plate. For
\eta\in(0,1]
one may ask how the presence of a damping term on the plate affects the overall regularity of the system. This is encapsulated by the following result.

Corollary 4
.

In the fluid structure system (
1
), replace the undamped plate equation with the damped plate equation

\displaystyle w_{tt}+\mbox{\AA}w+\mbox{\AA}^{\frac{\eta}{2}}w_{t}=p|_{\Omega}.

The generator of this
C_{0}
-semigroup of contractions,
\mathcal{A}_{\eta}
, is such that
D(\mathcal{A}_{\eta})=D(\mathcal{A})
for all
\eta\in(0,1]
, and in addition, the semigroup is Gevrey class
\delta\geq\frac{1}{\eta}
for
t\geq 0
. In particular, for
\eta=1
, the semigroup is analytic.

5.
Numerical Results

With Theorem
3
in hand, one would expect to see empirical evidence of “infinite speed of propagation” in given numerical simulations. That is, rough appropriate initial data is “smoothed out” as time evolves. Below, we supply a numerical scheme and example which reinforces this assertion. The issue of convergence of the finite dimensional system to the fully infinite dimensional system (
1
) will be addressed in a future work. Numerical results which suggest this convergence are supplied in
[
21
]
.

We take
\mathcal{O}=(0,1)^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\Omega=\{(x,1):x\in(0,1)\}
and
S=\partial\mathcal{O}\setminus\Omega
. Note that this geometry satisfies geometric assumption (G.2). With respect to this geometry
\{\mathcal{O},\Omega\},
the FSI system becomes

(29)

\begin{cases}w_{tt}+\frac{d^{4}w}{dx^{4}}=p|_{\Omega}\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,\infty)\\
w=w_{x}=0\text{ on }x=0\text{ and }x=1,t>0\\
u_{t}-\Delta u+\nabla p=0\text{ in }\mathcal{O}\times(0,\infty)\\
\text{div}(u)=0\text{ in }\mathcal{O}\times(0,\infty)\\
u=0\text{ on }S,u=[0,0,w_{t}]\text{ on }\Omega\\
[w(0),w_{t}(0),u(0)]=[w_{1}^{0},w_{2}^{0},u^{0}]\in\mathcal{H}.\end{cases}

To deal with geometries satisfying geometric assumption (G.1) via the finite element method (FEM), one could in principle appeal to isoparametric elements (see
[
5
]
). Here, take
\mathbb{T}
to be a triangulation of the geometry
\mathcal{O}
, which consists of
L_{f}
triangular elements with
M
nodes. Relative to the triangulation
\mathbb{T}
, we invoke
\mathbb{P}_{2}/\mathbb{P}_{1}
Taylor-Hood conforming bases, with respect to the fluid and pressure solution components of the FSI (see p. 195 of
[
5
]
). We thusly define
\{\phi_{1},\phi_{2},...,\phi_{M}\}
and
\{\psi_{1},\psi_{2},...,\psi_{M_{p}}\}
to be the fluid and pressure bases, respectively. This basis is appropriately conforming.

As the space of finite energy for the plate component is
H_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
, we impose an appropriate
H^{2}
-conforming basis. In one spatial dimension, one can appeal to standard Hermite elements. In particular we employ the following quintic Hermite element as our standard element:

x_{i_{1}^{\ell}}
\theta_{5}
\theta_{1}
x_{i_{2}^{\ell}}
\theta_{6}

\theta_{3}
x_{i_{4}^{\ell}}
\theta_{4}
\partial_{x}w:
w:

The above equidistant standard element is appropriate for the computations at hand, yielding mass and plate stiffness matrices which are sufficiently conditioned
[
24
]
. We thus define the Hermite basis
\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2},...,\theta_{DOF_{s}}\}
. Denoting by
\tilde{M}
and
L_{s}
the number of plate nodes and elements respectively, one finds by counting that
DOF_{s}=\tilde{M}+\frac{\tilde{M}+2}{3}
. In our plate basis, the global node indices
\{1,...,\tilde{M}\}
correspond to the Lagrange interpolation points, and the global node indices
\{\tilde{M}+1,...,DOF_{s}\}
correspond to the points where we additionally enforce Hermite interpolation. In extending these dynamics to a 3d-2d coupled system, one would need to employ Argyris elements for the plate, as the usual Hermite elements are not
H^{2}
conforming in dimensions higher than 1 (see p. 255 of
[
24
]
).

We these bases in hand, we define the following matrices:

(30)

\displaystyle\text{(Fluid Mass Matrix)}~M_{f}

\displaystyle:=\begin{bmatrix}(\phi_{1},\phi_{1})_{\mathcal{O}}&\ldots&(\phi_{1},\phi_{M})_{\mathcal{O}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
(\phi_{M},\phi_{1})_{\mathcal{O}}&\ldots&(\phi_{M},\phi_{M})_{\mathcal{O}}\end{bmatrix}

(31)

\displaystyle\text{(Fluid Stiffness Matrix)}~K_{f}

\displaystyle:=\begin{bmatrix}(\nabla\phi_{1},\nabla\phi_{1})_{\mathcal{O}}&\ldots&(\nabla\phi_{1},\nabla\phi_{M})_{\mathcal{O}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
(\nabla\phi_{M},\nabla\phi_{1})_{\mathcal{O}}&\ldots&(\nabla\phi_{M},\nabla\phi_{M})_{\mathcal{O}}\end{bmatrix}

(32)

\displaystyle\text{(Divergence Component 1)}~B_{x}

\displaystyle:=\begin{bmatrix}(\partial_{x}\phi_{1},\psi_{1})_{\mathcal{O}}&\ldots&(\partial_{x}\phi_{1},\psi_{M_{p}})_{\mathcal{O}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
(\partial_{x}\phi_{M},\psi_{1})_{\mathcal{O}}&\ldots&(\partial_{x}\phi_{M},\psi_{M_{p}})_{\mathcal{O}}\end{bmatrix}

(33)

\displaystyle\text{(Divergence Component 2)}~B_{y}

\displaystyle:=\begin{bmatrix}(\partial_{y}\phi_{1},\psi_{1})_{\mathcal{O}}&\ldots&(\partial_{y}\phi_{1},\psi_{M_{p}})_{\mathcal{O}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
(\partial_{y}\phi_{M},\psi_{1})_{\mathcal{O}}&\ldots&(\partial_{y}\phi_{M},\psi_{M_{p}})_{\mathcal{O}}\end{bmatrix}

(34)

\displaystyle\text{(Plate Mass Matrix)}~M_{s}

\displaystyle:=\begin{bmatrix}\langle\theta_{1},\theta_{1}\rangle_{\Omega}&\ldots&\langle\theta_{1},\theta_{\tilde{M}}\rangle_{\Omega}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\langle\theta_{\tilde{M}},\theta_{1}\rangle_{\Omega}&\ldots&\langle\theta_{\tilde{M}},\theta_{\tilde{M}}\rangle_{\Omega}\end{bmatrix}

(35)

\displaystyle\text{(Plate ``S'' Matrix)}~S

\displaystyle:=\begin{bmatrix}\langle\theta_{1}^{\prime\prime},\theta_{1}^{\prime\prime}\rangle_{\Omega}&\ldots&\langle\theta_{1}^{\prime\prime},\theta_{\tilde{M}}^{\prime\prime}\rangle_{\Omega}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\langle\theta_{\tilde{M}}^{\prime\prime},\theta_{1}^{\prime\prime}\rangle_{\Omega}&\ldots&\langle\theta_{\tilde{M}}^{\prime\prime},\theta_{\tilde{M}}^{\prime\prime}\rangle_{\Omega}\end{bmatrix}

From
[
3
]
, recall that the pressure
p
has two distinct components, a mean free part, and a constant part. We denote this decomposition as

(36)

\displaystyle p(t)=q(t)+\tilde{c},

where
q\in\hat{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})
and
\tilde{c}\in\mathbb{R}
. We now denote
\{[u_{1},u_{2},w_{1},w_{2}],p\}
to be the (finite dimensional) approximation to (infinite dimensional) FSI solution
\{[\vec{u},w,w_{t}],p\}
of (
1
), which solves the approximating semi-discretized system of ordinary differential equations:

(37)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}M_{f}u_{1}^{\prime}+K_{f}u_{1}+B_{x}q=F_{1f},\\
M_{f}u_{2}^{\prime}+K_{f}u_{2}+B_{y}q=F_{2f},\\
B_{x}^{T}u_{1}+B_{y}^{T}u_{2}=0,\\
M_{s}w_{2}^{\prime}+Sw_{1}-\tilde{C}E_{s}^{T}=F_{s},\\
M_{s}w_{1}^{\prime}=M_{s}w_{2},\\
E_{s}w_{2}=0.\end{cases}

That is, in the above system we take
u_{1}
to mean the solution (c.f. FSI (
29
))
u_{1I}\in V_{M}=\spanset\{\phi_{1},\phi_{2},...,\phi_{M}\}\subset H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{O})
and likewise for the other solution variables. The quantity
\tilde{C}=[\langle\tilde{c},\theta_{1}\rangle_{\Omega},...,\langle\tilde{c},\theta_{DOF_{s}}\rangle_{\Omega}]^{T}
in
37
is the Babuska-Brezzi pressure which arises from the constraint that
w_{2}
is mean free as

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}w_{2}~d\Omega=\int_{\mathcal{O}}\mbox{div}([u_{1},u_{2}])~d\mathcal{O}=0.

The matrices (
30
)-(
35
) handle the spatial discretization aspect of the system. For temporal discretization, we specify a time step
\Delta t
and step using Backward Euler:

(38)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}M_{f}\alpha^{N+1}_{1}+\Delta tK\alpha_{1}^{N+1}+\Delta tB_{x}\beta^{N+1}=M_{f}\alpha_{1}^{N}+\Delta tF_{1f}^{N+1},\\
M_{f}\alpha^{N+1}_{2}+\Delta tK\alpha_{1}^{N+2}+\Delta tB_{y}\beta^{N+1}=M_{f}\alpha_{2}^{N}+\Delta tF_{2f}^{N+1},\\
B_{x}^{T}\alpha_{1}^{N+1}+B_{y}^{T}\alpha_{2}^{N+1}=0,\\
M_{s}\omega_{2}^{N+1}+\Delta tS\omega_{1}^{N+1}-\Delta tE_{s}^{T}\tilde{c}^{N+1}=M_{s}\omega_{2}^{N}+\Delta tF_{s}^{N+1},\\
\omega_{1}^{N+1}-\Delta t\omega_{2}^{N+1}=\omega_{1}^{N},\\
E_{s}\omega_{1}^{N+1}=0.\end{cases}

To capture the essential boundary conditions of (
1
), we have that the degrees of freedom satisfy

(39)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\alpha|_{S}=0,\\
\alpha|_{\Omega}=\omega_{2},\\
\omega_{1}^{\text{Lag}}|_{\partial\Omega}=\omega_{2}^{\text{Lag}}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\\
\omega_{1}^{\text{Herm}}|_{\partial\Omega}=\omega_{2}^{\text{Herm}}|_{\partial\Omega}=0.\end{cases}

We require additionally that each of the boundary conditions (
39
) is satisfied at each time step
N+1
. In particular, to retain the interesting dynamics between the fluid and plate, we require

(40)

\displaystyle u_{2,I}^{N+1}(N_{i})=w^{N+1}_{2,I}(N_{i})

for each node
N_{i}
on the fluid boundary
\Omega
. Note that this constitutes a variational crime – see
[
22
]
– in that the
y
component of fluid velocity only equals the plate velocity at the nodes, not on the entire boundary
\Omega
. For fine meshes, such an approximation is sufficient (p. 186 of
[
5
]
).

Theorem

[

]
,

]

1

[

. This is the subject of an upcoming work.

For now, the authors note that in contrast to thermoelastic plates, where all three dynamics evolve in the interior, the plate portion of the fluid structure system (

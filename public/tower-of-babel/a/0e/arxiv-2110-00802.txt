Introduction

One version of Beilinson-Bernstein localization theory for a semisimple algebraic group
G
can be stated as follows
(
[
BK
]
,
[
BMR
]
,
[
BN
]
).
Let
B
be a Borel subgroup and
N
its unipotent radical. Denote by
\tilde{X}=G/N
the affine flag variety
and
D_{\mathrm{mon}}(\tilde{X})
the category of monodromic
D
-modules on
\tilde{X}
. Functors of global sections and localization
between categories of
U\mathfrak{g}
-modules and monodromic
D
-modules form a Frobenius adjunction, in the sense that they are
adjoint to each other from both sides up to twist by the dualizing sheaf
\omega_{\tilde{X}}
.

The composition of localization and global sections functors give both a monad and a comonad
W
on
D_{\mathrm{mon}}(\tilde{X})
, and
the Barr-Beck theorem identifies the derived category
D(U\mathfrak{g}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})
with the categories of
W
-modules and
W
-comodules
in
D(D_{\mathrm{mon}}(\tilde{X}))
. In particular for a regular weight
\lambda
the specialization of the monad
W_{\lambda}
can be
seen to be the group algebra of the Weyl group of
G
, its action identifies monodromic
D
-modules with the monodromies on
the
W
-orbit of
\lambda
. This recovers the usual equivalence of the twisted
D
-modules with regular dominant weight
and representations of
\mathfrak{g}
with corresponding central character.

Moreover, identifying the category of
G
-equivariant endofunctors on
D_{\mathrm{mon}}(\tilde{X})
with the
D
-modules on the double
quotient
N\backslash G/N
the Weyl monad
W
corresponds to the differential operators on
N\backslash G/N
. The orbit
structure of the double quotient is used to obtain information regarding the monad
W
itself.

In case of the super-groups we no longer have the Frobenius adjunction, however we still can define one of the adjoints
(left of right depending on the setting) after replacing the derived categories of representations and monodromic
D
-modules
with suitable Ind-completions. The main result of this paper is the ind-monadicity of the right localization functor, which
allows us to identify the ind-completion of the bounded derived category of
U\mathfrak{g}
-modules with the ind-completion of

W
-modules in
D_{\mathrm{mon}}(\tilde{X})
(
2.3.6
). We consider here only Lie super-algebras of type
A
, however we expect the theorem to
hold in other types as well, since the main point of the proof is the reduction to the classical group underlying the super-group.

In order to identify the Weyl monad with the differential operators on the double quotient, we believe we need to work with
filtered
D
-modules or even better Hodge modules. We will investigate this question elsewhere.

0.0.1
Outline of the paper..

We start by recalling the definition and basic properties of coderived categories (
[
Po
]
), as well as a Koszul duality
theorem where these categories arise. Next investigate relation between
D
-modules and
\Omega
-modules on super-manifolds
and establish equivalences between appropriate derived categories analogous to those shown by Positselski. In particular
we show that the derived category of
D
-modules is equivalent to the coderived category of
\Omega
-modules (
1.3.3
)
and the equivalence of coderived categories of filtered
D
-modules and filtered
\Omega
-modules (
1.4.1
).

We finish the section with discussion of equivariant
D
-modules, and illustrate why we believe that filtration is necessary
to obtain the sound theory of
D
-modules on the quotient stacks.

In the second section we show an equivalence between coderived categories of
U\mathfrak{g}
-modules and modules over the cochain algebra

C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
for a Lie super-algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Then we define localization functors both on the
D
-module and
\Omega
-module sides
and show that they are compatible with these equivalences. Finally we define the notion of ind-monadicity of a functor and show
that the right localization functor in monodromic
D
-modules is ind-monadic.

In conclusion we illustrate the main theorem in the case of
\mathfrak{sl}(1,1)
where we give an explicit description of the Weyl monad
as an algebra in monodromic
D
-bimodules.

1.1
Notations and setting

Throughout this paper we work over an algebraically closed field
k

1.1.1
Category
\mathrm{sVect}
..

By a super vector space we mean a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector vector space
V=\bigoplus_{i}V_{s=i}
, where
V_{s=i}
or simply
V_{i}
,
when there is no ambiguity what grading is being used, is the component of super-degree
i
. We will write
V\!\left<n\right>
for the
super vector space with the
n
-shifted grading, i.e.

V\!\left<n\right>_{i}=V_{i+n}.

By
\mathrm{sVect}
we denote the category of super vector spaces and
k
-linear maps of degree
0
. The degree of an element
v\in V

will be denoted by
|v|_{s}
or simply
|v|
, when the implied grading is clear.

We have the standard symmetric monoidal structure on
\mathrm{sVect}
:

(V\mathop{\otimes}\limits W)_{n}=\bigoplus_{i+j=n}V_{i}\mathop{\otimes}\limits W_{j},

with the symmetry map
V\mathop{\otimes}\limits W\to W\mathop{\otimes}\limits V
given by the Koszul sign rule. Namely, for
v\in V
and
w\in W

v\mathop{\otimes}\limits w=(-1)^{|v|_{s}|w|_{s}}w\mathop{\otimes}\limits v.

1.1.2
Category
\mathrm{dgsVect}
..

Let
V^{\bullet}
be a complex of super vector spaces, with cohomological grading, i.e. with differentials
d\colon V^{i}\to V^{i+1}
.
The cohomological degree of an element
v\in V^{\bullet}
will be denoted by
\bar{v}
. As customary, the cohomological shift
will be denoted by

V[n]^{i}=V^{n+i}.

We will write
\mathrm{dgsVect}
for the dg-category of complexes of super vector spaces. We have the standard symmetric monoidal structure

(V\mathop{\otimes}\limits W)^{n}=\bigoplus_{i+j=n}V^{i}\mathop{\otimes}\limits W^{j},

and the symmetry map is given by the Koszul sign with respect to the total grading:

v\mathop{\otimes}\limits w=(-1)^{(\bar{v}+|v|_{s})(\bar{w}+|w|_{s})}w\mathop{\otimes}\limits v.

1.1.3
Filtered complexes and Rees construction..

Consider a complex
V^{\bullet}
equipped with an increasing cocomplete filtration by subcomplexes
F_{p}V^{\bullet}
. Rees construction
is the canonical functor from the quasi-abelian category of filtered complexes to its abelian envelop. Explicitly, it is given
by the graded object

\mathrm{Rees}(V^{\bullet},F)=\bigoplus_{p}F_{p}V^{\bullet}.

The degree of
v\in\mathrm{Rees}(V^{\bullet},F)
coming from the Rees construction will be denoted by
|v|_{h}
. We can think of

\mathrm{Rees}(V^{\bullet},F)
as a
k[h]
-module with multiplication by
h
given by inclusion
F_{p}V\hookrightarrow F_{p+1}V
.
Here
h
is a formal variable of degrees

\bar{h}=|h|_{s}=0,\quad|h|_{h}=1.

The subscript
h
in the notation for the degree, refers both to the formal variable
h
as well as to the Hodge filtration that
we will be concerned with. The essential image of the Rees functor is formed by flat
k[h]
-modules, and because of the
cocompleteness assumption it is fully faithful. Indeed, given a flat
k[h]
-module
M
we can recover
V^{\bullet}
as
V^{\bullet}=\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\mathrm{lim}$}\raisebox{-9.28778pt}{\makebox[13.88893pt][s]{\rightarrowfill}}}M_{i}
.

Let
V
be a filtered complex, then we will denote the complex with shifted filtration (or Tate twist) by
V(n)
:

F_{p}V(n)=F_{p+n}V.

For a filtered algebra
A
in
\mathrm{dgsVect}
the Rees construction
\mathrm{Rees}(A,F)
is a flat central
k[h]
-algebra. If
M
is a
filtered
A
-module with a compatible filtration, i.e.
F_{p}A\mathop{\otimes}\limits F_{q}M\to F_{p+q}M
, then
\mathrm{Rees}(M,F)
is a

\mathrm{Rees}(A,F)
-module.

1.2
Enhancements of the Derived Category

Let
A
be an algebra in
\mathrm{dgsVect}
. We will denote by
\mathrm{Htp}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})
the homotopy category, i.e. the localization of
A
-modules
with respect to homotopy equivalences, and by
\mathrm{D}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})
the unbounded derived category, i.e. the localization of
\mathrm{Htp}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})

with respect to quasi-isomorphisms, or equivalently the quotient of
\mathrm{Htp}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})
by the thick subcategory of acyclic objects

\mathrm{Acyc}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})
. We will now recall the definitions of coderived, contraderived and absolutely derived
categories.

1.2.1
Absolutely acyclic, coacyclic and contra-acyclic modules..

The category of absolutely acyclic
A
-modules is the minimal subcategory of
A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}
, containing total complexes of short exact
sequences of
A
-modules and closed under shifts, cones and retracts. In particular it contains total complexes of all finite
exact sequences. We will denote this category as
\mathrm{AbsAc}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})
.

The category of coacyclic modules is the minimal subcategory of
A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}
containing absolutely acyclic modules and closed under
all operations listed above and under taking filtered colimits. It will be denoted by
\mathrm{CoAc}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})
.

Similarly, the contra-acyclic modules is the minimal subcategory containing absolutely acyclic modules and closed under shifts,
cones, retracts and filtered limits. It will be denoted by
\mathrm{CntAc}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})
.

We will say that a morphism of
A
-modules and absolutely acyclic, coacyclic or contra-acyclic if its cone belongs to the
corresponding subcategory.

1.2.2
Absolute derived, coderived and contraderived categories..

The absolute derived, coderived and contraderived categories are defined as quotients of the homotopy category
by the respective subcategories of acyclics.

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{abs}}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Htp}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})/\mathrm{AbsAc}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{co}}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Htp}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})/\mathrm{CoAc}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{cnt}}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Htp}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})/\mathrm{CntAc}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})

From the definitions it is clear we have the following diagram of localizations:

\textstyle{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{abs}}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

For an algebra

we will denote

The full subcategory of

.

a)

Let

\textstyle{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{abs}}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{fd}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Let us also recall the compact generation of the coderived category.

Proposition 1.2.4
.

Let

\#\!A

1.2.5
Singularity category..

Recall that the unbounded derived category
\mathrm{D}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})
is compactly generated by the perfect complexes
\mathrm{Perf}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})
.
When
A
is concentrated in cohomological degree
0
, the singularity category of

is defined as the quotient

\mathrm{Sing}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})=\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{fg}})/\mathrm{Perf}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}).

In other words, in this case the difference between derived and coderived categories is the
\mathrm{Ind}\textrm{-}
completion of the singularity
category.

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{gr}\textrm{-}\mathrm{co}}(\tilde{D}_{A}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})^{h+}

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{co}}(\tilde{\Omega}_{A}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})^{h+},

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}(\tilde{D}_{A}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})^{h+}

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{D}(\tilde{\Omega}_{A}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})^{h+}.

Proof:
Since
\tilde{D}_{A}
is concentrated in positive
h
-degrees and
\Omega_{A}
in negative
h
-degrees (and therefore
\tilde{\Omega}_{A}
stabilizes
in positive degrees),
the functors in question restrict to the subcategories of modules with bounded below
h
-grading. Moreover, restrictions
on grading imply that
\mathrm{Hom}(\tilde{\Omega}_{A},N)\simeq\widetilde{Sp}\mathop{\otimes}\limits N
, so that both functors preserve filtered colimits,
and send coacyclic objects to coacyclic.

First equivalence.

Let
M\in\tilde{\Omega}_{A}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}^{h+}
, then
M
has a bounded from below increasing filtration by
\tilde{\Omega}_{A}
-submodules. If every
associated quotient
\mathrm{gr}_{i}M
is coacyclic as an
A=\mathrm{gr}_{0}\tilde{\Omega}_{A}
-module, then
M
is coacyclic
\tilde{\Omega}_{A}
-module.
So the classes of coacyclic and graded-coacyclic module coincide and
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{gr}\textrm{-}\mathrm{co}}(\tilde{\Omega}_{A}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})^{h+}\simeq\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{co}}(\tilde{\Omega}_{A}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})^{h+}
.

Let
N
be a graded-coacyclic object in
\tilde{D}_{A}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}^{h+}
and show that
\widetilde{Sp}\mathop{\otimes}\limits N
is coacyclic. It has an increasing
filtration by
\tilde{\Omega}_{A}
-submodules induced by the tautological filtration of
\widetilde{Sp}
. The associated graded for this filtration
are
A
-modules
\widetilde{Sp}^{n}\mathop{\otimes}\limits N
and we must show that they are coacyclic. Indeed,
since
N
is graded-coacyclic
\tilde{D}_{A}
-module, it has an increasing filtration by

A
-submodules with coacyclic quotients and hence it is also coacyclic as an
A
-module.

In order to show that the cone of the unit
\tilde{M}\to\widetilde{Sp}\mathop{\otimes}\limits\tilde{D}\mathop{\otimes}\limits\tilde{M}
is coacyclic, use the filtration
induced by the tautological filtration on
\tilde{M}
. It is an increasing filtration bounded from below by assumption on
\tilde{M}
and
therefore in order to show that the cone coacyclic it is enough to show coacyclicity of the associated graded module. But since
\tilde{\Omega}^{\geqslant 1}

acts trivially on
\mathrm{gr}M
, this follows from lemma
1.3.2
.

It remains to show that the counit map
\tilde{D}\mathop{\otimes}\limits\widetilde{Sp}\mathop{\otimes}\limits N\to N
is graded-coacyclic, in other words that
the map between associated quotients
\mathrm{gr}_{i}\left(\tilde{D}\mathop{\otimes}\limits\widetilde{Sp}\mathop{\otimes}\limits N\right)\to\mathrm{gr}_{i}N
is coacyclic in
A
-modules.
For convenience, denote the module on the left by
L_{i}
. It has another filtration by subcomplexes of
A
-modules

G_{\bullet}L_{i}
induced by the tautological increasing filtration on
\tilde{D}\mathop{\otimes}\limits\widetilde{Sp}
. The associated graded for this
filtration is

\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}^{G}L_{i}=\bigoplus_{p+q=i}\mathrm{gr}_{p}(\tilde{D}\mathop{\otimes}\limits\widetilde{Sp})\mathop{\otimes}\limits\mathrm{gr}_{q}N.

Now, the zero-term
\mathrm{gr}^{G}_{0}L_{i}\simeq\mathrm{gr}_{i}N
, while higher terms are finite exact complexes of
A
-modules. This establishes
that the counit map is graded-coacyclic.

Second equivalence.

To show that the first equivalence descends to the derived categories we just need to show that the adjoint functors preserve
acyclic objects.

Let
N
be an acyclic object in
\tilde{D}_{A}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}^{h+}
, and show that
\widetilde{Sp}\mathop{\otimes}\limits N
is an acyclic
\tilde{\Omega}_{A}
-module.
To do that it is enough to consider increasing bounded from below filtration on
\widetilde{Sp}\mathop{\otimes}\limits N
induced by the filtration
of
\widetilde{Sp}
. The associated quotients are acyclic, since
N
is an acyclic
A
-module, therefore using spectral sequence
argument we find that
\widetilde{Sp}\mathop{\otimes}\limits N
is acyclic.

Now let
M
be an acyclic object in
\tilde{\Omega}_{A}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}^{h+}
, and show that
\tilde{D}\mathop{\otimes}\limits M
is acyclic. Consider filtration
induced by the filtration of
M
, its associated quotients are acyclic, since
\mathrm{gr}_{i}M
are acyclic. Since it is a bounded increasing
filtration we conclude as before that
\tilde{D}\mathop{\otimes}\limits M
is also acyclic.

\square

Remark 1.4.4
.

In the classical case the algebra
A=\mathcal{O}_{X}
has finite cohomological dimension, so that classes of acyclic and coacyclic objects in
A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}

coincide. Therefore,
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{co}}(\tilde{\Omega}_{X}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})^{h+}\simeq\mathrm{D}(\tilde{\Omega}_{X}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})^{h+}
and

\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{gr}\textrm{-}\mathrm{co}}(\tilde{D}_{X}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})^{h+}\simeq\mathrm{D}(\tilde{D}_{X}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})^{h+}
.

Assume for a moment that the algebra of functions
A
on a smooth super-manifold is concentrated in negative
s
-grading, in which
case
\Omega_{A}
is also negatively
s
-graded, and
s
-grading of
D_{A}
is bounded from below (

has only odd negative generators).
We would like to consider full subcategories
\mathrm{Htp}(\tilde{D}_{A}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})^{s+}
,
\mathrm{Htp}(\tilde{\Omega}_{A}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})^{s+}

of the corresponding homotopy categories formed by objects with

-grading bounded from below, and their localizations.
The pair of adjoint functors
\mathrm{Hom}(\tilde{\Omega}_{A},-)
and
\tilde{D}_{A}\mathop{\otimes}\limits-
restrict to these subcategories and we have the following statement.

Proposition 1.4.5
.

Let
A
be the ring of functions on a smooth super-manifold concentrated in negative
s
-degrees, then the following localizations are equivalences

\textstyle{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{co}}(\tilde{\Omega}_{X}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})^{h+,s+}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{\mathrm{D}(\tilde{\Omega}_{X}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})^{h+,s+},}

It is enough to show that the classes of graded-coacyclic and acyclic modules coincide in each case. Let

be an acyclic

1.5
Equivariant
D
- and
\Omega
-modules

One of the advantages of working on the
\Omega
-module side is that the analogs of notions of weak and strong equivariance
of
D
-modules are defined in a uniform fashion. Let
G
be an affine
\mathbb{Z}
-super-group, then the algebra of functions
\mathcal{O}_{G}
is a commutative Hopf algebra.
The comultiplication
\Delta
can be extended from
\mathcal{O}_{G}
to the de Rham complex
\Omega_{G}=\mathcal{O}_{G}\mathop{\otimes}\limits\mathrm{Sym}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}[-1])

by setting
\Delta(x)=x\mathop{\otimes}\limits 1+1\mathop{\otimes}\limits x
for any
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. This makes
\Omega_{G}
into a commutative Hopf algebra
in
\mathrm{dgsVect}
.

An affine
\mathbb{Z}
-super-manifold
X
with the left action of
G
is given by an algebra
\mathcal{O}_{X}
in the category of left
\mathcal{O}_{G}
-comodules
relative to the monoidal structure as described in section
1.2.10
. The coaction of
\mathcal{O}_{G}
on
\mathcal{O}_{X}
induces
a coaction map
a\colon\Omega_{X}\to\Omega_{G}\mathop{\otimes}\limits\Omega_{X}
. The latter is determined by its restriction to
\Omega_{X}^{1}
,
and corresponds to the derivation
d\circ a\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{G}\mathop{\otimes}\limits\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\Omega_{G}\mathop{\otimes}\limits\Omega_{X}
. Furthermore,
restricting with respect to the map
\Omega_{G}\to\mathcal{O}_{G}
, we obtain the coaction
\Omega_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{G}\mathop{\otimes}\limits\Omega_{X}
. In other
words
\Omega_{X}
is an algebra is
\Omega_{G}
-comodules and
\mathcal{O}_{G}
-comodules.

Definition 1.5.1
.

a)

A weakly
G
-equivariant
\Omega_{X}
-module is an object of
(\Omega_{X},\mathcal{O}_{G})\textrm{-}\mathrm{MC}
.

b)

A strongly
G
-equivariant
\Omega_{X}
-module is an object of
(\Omega_{X},\Omega_{G})\textrm{-}\mathrm{MC}
.

The relative version of Koszul duality provides equivalences

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{co}}((\Omega_{X},\mathcal{O}_{G})\textrm{-}\mathrm{MC})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{co}}(\overline{\mathrm{Cob}}_{\Omega_{X}}(\mathcal{O}_{G}\mathop{\otimes}\limits\Omega_{X})),

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{co}}((\Omega_{X},\Omega_{G})\textrm{-}\mathrm{MC})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{co}}(\overline{\mathrm{Cob}}_{\Omega_{X}}(\Omega_{G}\mathop{\otimes}\limits\Omega_{X})).

Similarly, we define weakly and strongly
G
-equivariant filtered
\Omega_{X}
-modules.

1.5.2
.

The main reason we want to work with the filtered
D
-modules instead of unfiltered is that they have the “expected” behavior.
In particular, let
X
and a smooth super-manifold and
G
super-group acting on
X
. Since
D
-modules, as well as
\Omega
-modules
according to equivalence
1.3.3
, don’t detect the odd part of either
X
or
G
, the
action of the odd part of the super-group is ignored. For our purposes this is not acceptable, since this action plays
an important role in Beilinson-Bernstein localization theory (see example in section
2.4
).

This problem doesn’t arise in the classical case because passing to
D
-modules doesn’t lose any information about the
action of the group. Indeed, let
X
and
Y
be two smooth manifolds, denote
X_{\mathrm{dR}}
the de-Rham stack of
X
, i.e., the quotient
of
X
by the action of the formal completion of the diagonal
\widehat{\Delta}\subset X\times X
. It is easy to see that if

f\colon Y_{\mathrm{dR}}\to X_{\mathrm{dR}}
has a lift
\tilde{f}\colon Y\to X
, then such a lift is unique.

1.5.3
Symplectic quotient..

Let
(X,E)
be a smooth
\mathbb{Z}
-super-manifold and
G
a
\mathbb{Z}
-super-group, acting on it. The cotangent bundle on
(X,E)
as
a super-manifold is isomorphic to
(T^{*}X,E\oplus E^{*})
, and the induced action of
G
on
T^{*}(X,E)
preserves the natural
symplectic form. Denote by
\mu\colon T^{*}(X,E)\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
the moment map for this action.

The (derived) symplectic quotient of
T^{*}(X,E)
by the action of
G
is defined as

\left(T^{*}(X,E)\ \mathop{\times}\limits^{h}_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}}\ \{0\}\right)\bigg/G,

where
\mathop{\times}\limits^{h}_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}}
denotes the homotopy fiber product over
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
with respect to the moment map.

Denote by
K(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}})=\mathrm{Sym}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}[1])\mathop{\otimes}\limits\mathrm{Sym}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
the Koszul complex resolution of the skyscraper sheaf at
0\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
.
On each open affine piece
U
of
(X,E)
the homotopy fiber product can be expressed as
\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\nolimits K(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{O}_{T^{*}U})
.

In the case when
X
is a
G
-torsor over a super-manifold
Y
the symplectic quotient
T^{*}X/\!/G
is quasi-isomorphic to the cotangent
bundle
T^{*}Y
.

Example 1.5.4
.

We will illustrate connection between the strongly equivariant
\Omega
-modules and symplectic quotients with the following example.
Consider the odd affine line
G=\mathbb{A}^{0,1}
with the group operation given by addition. We have
\mathcal{O}_{G}=k[\varepsilon]
, with the degree

|\varepsilon|_{s}=1
and comultiplication
\Delta(\varepsilon)=1\mathop{\otimes}\limits\varepsilon+\varepsilon\mathop{\otimes}\limits 1
, and algebra
\Omega_{G}=k[\varepsilon,d\varepsilon]

with coproduct extended from
\mathcal{O}_{G}
by setting

.

Let us describe strongly
\mathbb{A}^{0,1}
-equivariant
\Omega

\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\nolimits k

\Omega
-modules on the quotient

B\mathbb{A}^{0,1}

\Omega_{G}
is conilpotent we have

\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{co}}(\overline{\mathrm{Cob}(\Omega_{G})}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})\simeq\mathrm{D}(\overline{\mathrm{Cob}(\Omega_{G})}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}).

Now, since derived categories preserve quasi-isomorphism we may replace the cobar complex with the quasi-isomorphic algebra

B=k[u,du]
, where
\bar{u}=1
and
|u|_{s}=1
. The latter, after shear of grading, can be thought of as the de Rham complex
of affine line
\mathbb{A}^{1}=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\nolimits k[x]

\mathbb{G}_{m}
on
\mathbb{A}^{1}

Since

\mathrm{D}(B\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})
and hence the category of strongly

\Omega
-modules

\mathrm{dgsVect}

-modules the group

Now we turn to the case of filtered
\Omega
-modules. As before the filtered coalgebra
\tilde{\Omega}_{G}
is conilpotent, so that
the derived and coderived categories over the reduced cobar complex coincide, and we can pass to the derived category of

\tilde{B}
-modules, where
\tilde{B}=\mathrm{Rees}(B,F)
for the one-step filtration
F_{-1}B=k[u]du\subset F_{0}B=B
.

Let us restrict ourselves to the full subcategories formed by object with bounded from below
h
-grading, in which case according
to proposition
1.4.3
we have an equivalence of derived categories of filtered

-modules and filtered
\Omega
-modules.
One can see that
\tilde{\Omega}_{G}
is quasi-isomorphic to

\bar{v}=1
,

and

Now, the derived category
\mathrm{D}(\tilde{B}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})^{h+}
can be shown to be equivalent to
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{co}}(\tilde{B}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})^{h+}
. After change of

\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant
\tilde{\Omega}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}
-modules,
or equivalently with the equivariant derived category

2
Representations of Lie super-algebras

A
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie super-algebra is an object
\mathfrak{g}\in\mathrm{sVect}
equipped with a bracket operation
[,]\colon\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
,
where the exterior product is understood with respect to the monoidal structure in
\mathrm{sVect}
. The bracket satisfies the Jacobi
relation, i.e., for any
f,g,h\in\mathfrak{g}
we have

[[f,g],h]=[f,[g,h]]-(-1)^{|f|_{s}|g|_{s}}[g,[f,h]].

We will only consider finite dimensional Lie super-algebras, in the sense that the total dimension
\sum_{i}\mathrm{dim}\ (\mathfrak{g}_{s=i})
is finite.
Denote by
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{ev}}\subset\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{odd}}\subset\mathfrak{g}
the subspaces of even and odd
s
-degrees respectively.

The universal enveloping algebra
U\mathfrak{g}
is the quotient of the tensor power
T^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}
by the commutation relations

fg-(-1)^{|f|_{s}|g|_{s}}gf=[f,g]
. For any
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M
we can form two complexes, the chain complex

C_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},M)=\bigoplus_{n}\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\mathfrak{g}[1])\mathop{\otimes}\limits M,

and the cochain complex

C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},M)=\bigoplus_{n}\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}[-1])\mathop{\otimes}\limits M,

with the differentials induced by the bracket in
\mathfrak{g}
and the action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
M
. We will write
C_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
and
C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})

for the chain and cochain complexes of the trivial representation of
\mathfrak{g}
. It is easy to see that
C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
is an algebra
and
C_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
is a coalgebra in
\mathrm{dgsVect}
.

2.0.1
Lie super-algebra
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
..

Let
V=\bigoplus_{i}V_{s=i}
be an object of
\mathrm{sVect}
, we will write
d_{i}=\mathrm{dim}\ V_{s=i}
and
d=\sum_{i}d_{i}
.
The algebra of
k
-endomorphisms of
V
(not necessarily preserving
s
-grading)
will denoted by
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
. It is a super-vector space with components

\mathfrak{gl}(V)_{s=n}\ =\ \bigoplus_{i}\ \mathrm{Hom}(V_{s=i},V_{s=i+n}).

The
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie super-algebra structure on
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is given by the commutator bracket
[f,g]=fg-(-1)^{|f|_{s}|g|_{s}}gf
.

Choosing an ordered basis
\{e_{1},\ldots e_{d}\}
of
V
(we do not assume compatibility of this order with the ordering of components

V_{s=i}
), we can identify
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
with the space of
d\times d
matrices, such that an entry in position
(i,j)
is of degree

|e_{i}|_{s}-|e_{j}|_{s}
. The subalgebra of upper-triangular matrices with respect to this basis is a Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
,
and its nilpotent radical consists of upper-triangular matrices with zeroes on the diagonal.

The Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{sl}(V)\subset\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is formed by endomorphisms
f\in\mathfrak{gl}(V)
with zero super-trace

\mathrm{str}f=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\mathrm{tr}(f|_{V_{s=i}}).

2.1
Derived and coderived categories of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules

Let us discuss relation between derived and coderived categories of
\mathfrak{g}
-modules,
C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules and
C_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
-comodules.
This can be considered a generalization of duality described in section
1.2.8
, where we were working with

\mathfrak{g}
-modules for the odd one-dimensional Lie super-algebra
\mathfrak{g}=k^{0,1}
with trivial bracket. In the following propositions

\mathfrak{g}
is a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie super-algebra.

Proposition 2.1.1
.

The pair of adjoint functors
C_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},-)\colon\mathfrak{g}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}\to C_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})\textrm{-}\mathrm{Comod}
and
U\mathfrak{g}\mathop{\otimes}\limits-\colon C_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})\textrm{-}\mathrm{Comod}\to\mathfrak{g}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}

induce equivalence

\mathrm{D}(U\mathfrak{g}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})\simeq\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{co}}(C_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})\textrm{-}\mathrm{Comod}).

Proof:
Clearly coalgebra
C_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
is conilpotent, therefore from the general Koszul duality
1.2.7
we find that
the coderived category of
C_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
-comodules is equivalent to the derived category of
\overline{\mathrm{Cob}(C_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}))}
-modules.
It is straightforward to check that this cobar algebra is quasi-isomorphic to the universal enveloping algebra
U\mathfrak{g}
. Since
derived categories respect quasi-isomorphisms, the equivalence descends to
\mathrm{D}(U\mathfrak{g}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})
.

\square

Proposition 2.1.2
.

We have an equivalence of categories

\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{co}}(U\mathfrak{g}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})\simeq\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{co}}(C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}).

Proof:
We proceed as indicated in the remark
1.3.4
(a) by separating even and odd parts of
\mathfrak{g}
, and then dualizing first
the odd part and then the even. Since
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{odd}}
is a
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{ev}}
-module with respect to the adjoint action,

\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{odd}}^{*}
is equipped with the coadjoint action
\mathrm{ad}^{*}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{ev}}
and we may form intermediate algebra

B=U\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{ev}}\mathop{\otimes}\limits\mathrm{Sym}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{odd}}^{*}[-1]).

The product is given by the obvious product on the tensor components and the commutation relation
[x,y]=\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{x}(y)
,
for any
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{ev}}
and
y\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{odd}}^{*}
, and put the differential to be
0
. Consider also an element
c\in B^{2}

defined as the composition

\textstyle{c\colon k\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{ev}^{*}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{ev}}[1]\mathop{\otimes}\limits\mathfrak{g}^{*}_{\mathrm{ev}}[-1]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{g}[1]\mathop{\otimes}\limits\mathfrak{g}^{*}[-1]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{1\mathop{\otimes}\limits d}

\scriptstyle{1\mathop{\otimes}\limits d}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{g}[1]\mathop{\otimes}\limits\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}[-1])\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{1\mathop{\otimes}\limits\mathrm{pr}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{g}[1]\mathop{\otimes}\limits\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}_{\mathrm{odd}}[-1]).}

Here
d
denotes the differential in the cochain complex
C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
, and
\mathrm{pr}
is the projection to the odd component.
This is a central element. Indeed, from the definition it is immediate that
c
is
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{ev}}
-invariant, and therefore it commutes with
the first factor
U\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{ev}}
of
B
. Using Jacobi identity one can show that it also commutes with the second factor
\mathrm{Sym}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}^{*}_{\mathrm{odd}}[-1])
.
In other words
(B,d=0,c)
is a curved dg-algebra, i.e.
d^{2}(x)=[c,x]
for any
x\in B
.

A curved
B
-module is a
\#B
-module
M
equipped with the differential
d
satisfying the usual identities except that

d^{2}(m)=cm
for any
m\in M
. Denote by
B\textrm{-}\mathrm{CMod}
the category of curved modules over the curved dg-algebra
(B,d,c)
, and morphisms
commuting with the differential.

We have the following pair of adjunctions

2.2

The forgetful functor

Similarly, on the

Remark 2.2.4

a)

The lemma holds for filtered modules as well and we define localization functors

b)

c)

Similarly, functor

In the classical case, however, functor

On the

-module side the coderived categories are equivalent to the derived categories and the left localization

2.3
Monadicity of localization

Consider two dg-categories
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
and a pair of adjoint functors
(L,R)
between them, specifically

and

. The composition

is a monad, and we will write

for the category of
A
-modules
in
\mathcal{C}
, i.e. collections
(M\in\mathcal{C},a_{1}\colon A(M)\to M,a_{2}\colon A^{2}(M)\to M,\ldots)

satisfying higher associativity conditions and compatibility with the monad structure of

.

The right adjoint
R
induces the comparison functor
K
.

\textstyle{\mathcal{D}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{R}
\scriptstyle{K}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}

is called

if the comparison functor is an equivalence.

If
\mathcal{D}
has all colimits then
K
has a left adjoint

. Indeed, consider an
A
-module
(M,a)
, and form
the following simplicial object in
\mathcal{D}
, where the face maps are given by the counit of adjunction

and
the action map

\textstyle{\ldots LRLRL(M)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

2.3.1
Ind-monadicity..

Let
X_{\bullet}
be a simplicial object in
\mathcal{D}
. We say that
X_{\bullet}
is of

(relative to functor

L\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
) if all
X_{i}\simeq L(Q_{i})
for
some compact objects
Q_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
, and all but finite number of homotopy groups
\pi_{i}(X_{\bullet})
vanish. We also say that
an object
Z\in\mathcal{D}
is
compactly generated
(relative to
L\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
) if it is isomorphic to the homotopy colimit of
some simplicial object
X_{\bullet}
of compact type. We will denote
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{cg}}
the full subcategory of compactly generated objects in
\mathcal{D}
.

Definition 2.3.2
.

We say that functor
R
is ind-monadic if the induced comparison functor
\widehat{K}\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathrm{Ind}\textrm{-}(A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{cg}})
is
an equivalence.

Let us give a sufficient condition for a functor to be ind-monadic that is useful in the localization setting.

Proposition 2.3.3
.

Let
(L,R)
be an adjunction between two compactly generated complete dg-categories
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
, such that

a)

functor
R
preserves filtered colimits and is conservative on compacts,

b)

the full subcategory
\mathcal{D}_{c}
of compact objects in
\mathcal{D}
is contained in
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{cg}}
,

c)

if
X_{\bullet}
is a simplicial object with
X_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{c}
, then all
\pi_{i}(X_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{D}_{c}
.

Then
R
is ind-monadic.

Proof:
First of all notice that since
R
preserves filtered colimits the left adjoint
L
sends compact objects in
\mathcal{C}
to compact
objects in
\mathcal{D}
. Now, let
Z=\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\mathrm{holim}$}\raisebox{-9.28778pt}{\makebox[24.44452pt][s]{\rightarrowfill}}}X_{\bullet}
an object in
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{cg}}
and
X_{\bullet}
a simplicial object of compact type.
We have all
X_{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{c}
, so from (c) we have all
\pi_{i}(X)
are compact and since only finite number of them are non-zero
the realization

is also compact. We have shown that
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{cg}}
is contained in
\mathcal{D}_{c}
, and combining this with (b) we conclude
that
\mathcal{D}_{c}=\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{cg}}
.

Next let us show the following fully faithful inclusions

\textstyle{\mathcal{D}_{c}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{K}
\textstyle{A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{J}
\textstyle{\mathcal{D}.}

Indeed, a continuous exact functor

between dg-categories commutes with arbitrary colimits, and therefore, as was mentioned in the beginning of this
section, we have the second inclusion. Furthermore, since

is conservative on compacts it identifies
\mathcal{D}_{c}
with a full subcategory of

-modules.

It remains to show that

identifies
\mathcal{D}_{c}
with
A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{cg}}
. Since

preserves arbitrary colimits, it respects
Postnikov tower construction, hence if

has only finite number of non-zero homotopy groups, so does

.
By definition

is the free

-module generated by

, therefore

sends simplicial objects of compact type in
\mathcal{D}
to
objects of compact type in
A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}
and compactly generated objects in
\mathcal{D}
to

, which establishes one inclusion.

Let
Z=\mathop{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\mathrm{holim}$}\raisebox{-9.28778pt}{\makebox[24.44452pt][s]{\rightarrowfill}}}X_{\bullet}
be a compactly generated object in

A\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}
. Since

, it is enough to show that

is compactly generated object in
\mathcal{D}
.

is a left adjoint to

,
therefore it preserves arbitrary colimits, so it remains to show that

is a simplicial object of compact type in
\mathcal{D}
.
This follows from the fact that

for a compact

.

\square

\mathfrak{b}
, and the bundle
\mathfrak{s}^{T}_{\tilde{X}}
is isomorphic
to
\mathfrak{n}_{X}
with fibers conjugate to subalgebra
\mathfrak{n}
.

We will be interested in properties
of the right monodromic localization functor
R^{D,T}_{\tilde{X}}

Lemma 2.3.5
.

The right localization functor
R^{D,T}_{\tilde{X}}\colon\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{co}}(U\mathfrak{g}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})\to\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{co}}(D^{T}_{\tilde{X}}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})

Proof:
Recall that coderived category of

-modules is the ind-completion of the absolute derived category of finitely generated

U\mathfrak{g}
-modules (

), and since
U\mathfrak{g}
sits in cohomological degree

, the latter is equivalent to the
bounded derived category of finitely generated modules. Consider the following diagram

\textstyle{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(U\mathfrak{g}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{fg}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{R^{D,T}_{\tilde{X}}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(U\mathfrak{g}_{\mathrm{ev}}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathrm{fg}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\mathrm{D}(D_{\tilde{X}}^{T}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

The top arrow, given by the restriction of action from

Denote by

the
Weyl monad
, i.e. the endofunctor on the category of monodromic

-modules obtained as the

W=R^{D,T}_{\tilde{X}}\Gamma\colon\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{co}}(D^{T}_{\tilde{X}}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})\to\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{co}}(D^{T}_{\tilde{X}}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}).

Theorem 2.3.6
.

The right localization functor
R^{D,T}_{\tilde{X}}
is ind-monadic, in other words we have an equivalence

\textstyle{\widehat{K}\colon\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{co}}(U\mathfrak{g}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Ind}\textrm{-}(W\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod}^{\mathrm{cg}}_{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{co}}(D^{T}_{\tilde{X}}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})}).}

Proof:
Let us check conditions of the proposition
2.3.3
. As was mentioned earlier (
2.2.4
(b)) the right localization
functor is continuous, combined with lemma
2.3.5
it establishes condition (a).

To check (c) recall that the coderived category of
U\mathfrak{g}
-modules is compactly generated by finitely generated
U\mathfrak{g}
-modules. Since

U\mathfrak{g}
is Noetherian, a complex of finitely generated modules has finitely generated cohomology groups.

It is easier to verify (b) on the
\Omega
-module side. Again, since

is Noetherian, a finitely generated
C^{\bullet}(g)
-module
has a simplicial resolution of compact type relative to vector spaces. However, from the description of the global sections functor
(lemma

\square

2.4
Example:
\mathfrak{sl}(1,1)

Let
V=k\oplus k\left<-1\right>
and consider
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(1,1)\subset\mathfrak{gl}(V)
, i.e. the Lie subalgebra consisting of elements with
super-trace zero. Fixing an ordered basis of
V
we identify
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
with a Lie algebra of
2\times 2
matrices and
\mathfrak{g}
is the subalgebra
spanned by

e=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\quad f=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\quad h=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

The super-degrees are
|e|_{s}=1
,
|f|_{s}=-1
,
|h|_{s}=0
, and the bracket is given by

[e,f]=h,\quad\quad[h,e]=[h,f]=0.

The universal enveloping algebra
U\mathfrak{g}
is isomorphic to a quotient of the free associative algebra on two generators
e
and
f
by the following relations

U\mathfrak{g}=k\left<e,f\right>/e^{2}=f^{2}=0.

The corresponding group
G=\mathrm{SL}(1,1)
is an affine Lie super-group with the ring of functions
\mathcal{O}_{G}=k[x_{11},x_{12},x_{21},x_{22}]/\mathrm{Ber}=1
,
where

\mathrm{Ber}={x_{11}\over x_{22}}-{x_{12}x_{21}\over x_{22}^{2}},

and coproduct is given by
\Delta(x_{ij})=\sum_{k}x_{ik}\mathop{\otimes}\limits x_{kj}
.

2.4.1
Affine flag variety..

The affine flag variety
\tilde{X}=G/N
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathbb{A}^{0,1}=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\nolimits k[t^{\pm},\varepsilon]
, where coordinates
t
and
\varepsilon

lift to the right
N
-invariant functions
x_{11}
and
x_{21}
respectively. The left action of
G
on
\tilde{X}
induces a map

\mathfrak{g}\to D_{\tilde{X}}
given by

e\mapsto\varepsilon\partial_{t},\quad\quad f\mapsto t\partial_{\varepsilon},\quad\quad h\mapsto t\partial_{t}+\varepsilon\partial_{\varepsilon}.

Both functions
t
and
\varepsilon
are of weight
1
for the action of the diagonal torus
T
, therefore the space of
T
-invariants

D^{T}_{\tilde{X}}
is generated as a subalgebra of
D_{\tilde{X}}
by
\varepsilon t^{-1}
,
t\partial_{t}
and
t\partial_{\varepsilon}
.
Alternatively, using the map
U\mathfrak{g}\to D^{T}_{\tilde{X}}
described above, we can rewrite it as

(2.4.1)

D^{T}_{\tilde{X}}\ \simeq\ k[h]\mathop{\otimes}\limits k\left<\alpha,f\right>/[\alpha,f]=1,

or equivalently,

D^{T}_{\tilde{X}}\ \simeq\ U\mathfrak{g}\left<\alpha\right>\big/e\alpha=\alpha e=0,[f,\alpha]=1,

where
\alpha
corresponds to the element
\varepsilon t^{-1}
.

Passing to the associated graded we find that the ring of functions on the bundle
\mathfrak{b}^{*}_{X}=(T^{*}\tilde{X})/T
is the free commutative algebra

\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{b}^{*}_{X}}=k[\alpha,v_{\alpha},h].

The moment map
\mu^{*}\colon\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{b}^{*}_{X}}
is given by

e\mapsto\alpha h,\quad\quad f\mapsto v_{\alpha},\quad\quad h\mapsto h,

in other words

\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{b}^{*}_{X}}\ \simeq\ \mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{g}^{*}}[\alpha]/e=\alpha h.

Geometrically, the moment map
\mu\colon\mathfrak{b}^{*}_{X}\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is a contraction of the odd affine line fibers
\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}\nolimits k[\alpha]

over the closed subscheme
Z_{h=0}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, and isomorphism on the complement.

2.4.2
Weyl monad..

The second factor in
2.4.1
is an algebra of differential operators on an odd affine line, and as was discussed in the
beginning of section
1.3
the category of
D
-modules on an odd affine line is equivalent to
\mathrm{sVect}
. Therefore, the category
of
D^{T}_{\tilde{X}}
-modules is equivalent to
k[h]
-modules in
\mathrm{sVect}
. The Weyl monad is represented by an algebra object in

D^{T}_{\tilde{X}}
-bimodules, and since in this case the flag manifold is affine, this algebra can be written as

W=\mathrm{End}_{U\mathfrak{g}}(D^{T}_{\tilde{X}}).

The enveloping algebra of
D^{T}_{\tilde{X}}
is equivalent to the tensor product of
k[h_{1},h_{2}]
and the algebra of differential
operators on
\mathbb{A}^{0,2}
, therefore we can write

W=\overline{W}\mathop{\otimes}\limits\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{0,2}},

where
\overline{W}
is some
k[h_{1},h_{2}]
-algebra and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{0,2}}
is the unique indecomposable
D_{\mathbb{A}^{0,2}}
-module.
Moreover, since
h
is central in
D^{T}_{\tilde{X}}
it is also central in
W
, and
\overline{W}
is in fact a
k[h]
-algebra, where
h=h_{1}=h_{2}
.

The standard resolution

\mathrm{Sym}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{n}[1])\mathop{\otimes}\limits U\mathfrak{g}_{X}\to D^{T}_{\tilde{X}}

is periodic and can be written explicitly as

\textstyle{\ldots U\mathfrak{g}^{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\begin{pmatrix}e&0\\
0&e\end{pmatrix}}
\textstyle{U\mathfrak{g}^{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\begin{pmatrix}0&e\\
e&-ef\end{pmatrix}}
\textstyle{U\mathfrak{g}^{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\alpha\end{pmatrix}}
\textstyle{D^{T}_{\tilde{X}}.}

A straightforward calculation shows that

\overline{W}\simeq k[h,u]/hu,\quad\quad\bar{u}=1,\quad|u|_{s}=-1,

where
u
is the variable Koszul dual to
e
.

2.4.3
Localization in weight
h\neq 0
..

The category of representations of
\mathfrak{sl}(1,1)
in non-zero weight
h=\lambda
is semi-simple with a unique indecomposable
V_{\lambda}
up to shift of
super-grading. On the other hand, the quotient
D^{T}_{\tilde{X}}/(h-\lambda)
is isomorphic after rescaling to
D_{X}
, and so the category of
monodromic
D
-modules on
X
in weight
h=\lambda
is equivalent to
D_{X}
-modules. Clearly under this identification
the unique indecomposable representation
V_{\lambda}
localizes as the unique indecomposable
D_{X}
-module
\mathcal{O}_{X}
.

2.4.4
Localization in weight
h=0
..

Let us clarify what happens in weight
0
. The category of representation is no longer semi-simple, but it is generated by the unique
irreducible module, namely the trivial representation. The subcategory of
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{co}}(U\mathfrak{g}\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})
with nilpotent action of
h
is identified
under the Koszul equivalence
2.1.2
with the
\mathrm{D}(C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})\textrm{-}\mathrm{Mod})
considered as a subcategory of the coderived category.

Now compare localizations for the two opposite Borel subalgebras
B^{+}
and
B^{-}
, namely the upper-triangular matrices and lower-triangular matrices.
The respective Weyl monads are represented by algebras
W^{+}=k[h,u]/hu
and
W^{-}=k[h,v]/hv
, where
v
is Koszul dual to
f
.
The singularity categories of
W^{+}
and
W^{-}
modules are generated by
Y^{+}=k[u]
and
Y^{-}=k[v]
respectively. They are the localizations
of the trivial representation on
X^{+}
and
X^{-}
. The subcategories generated
by these objects are equivalent to the derived categories of their endomorphism algebras. We find that

\mathrm{End}_{W^{+}}(Y^{+})=k[u,s]/us\ \simeq\ C^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})\ \simeq\ k[v,t]/vt=\mathrm{End}_{W^{-}}(Y^{-}),

where we identify
s=v
and
u=t
.

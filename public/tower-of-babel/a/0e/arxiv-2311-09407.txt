Singular Lagrangians in the Faddeev-Jackiw Formalism in Classical Mechanics
Carlos Manuel López Arellano † , Jaime Manuel Cabrera ‡ Luis Gerardo Romero Hernández ⊕ and Jorge Paulin Fuentes

Abstract
Classical mechanical systems with internal constraints will be studied using the extended symplectic formalism of Faddeev-Jackiw. Generalized brackets of the theory and equations of motion will be derived. A gauge system will be examined, leading to the associated gauge transformations. The results will be compared with the Dirac-Bergmann algorithm, which has already been reported in the literature. It will be shown that the symplectic approach is simpler and more economical than the Dirac method. Keywords: FaddeevJackiw Formalism, Symplectic Matrix, Constrained Mechanical System, Gauge Symmetries.

I
Introduction

Singular Lagrangians are those that describe singular (non-regular) systems, which exhibit internal constraints that indicate relationships and dependencies among the dynamic variables
Teitelboim
. The study of these systems was independently addressed by Paul Dirac
Dirac
and Peter Bergmann
Ber
;
Ber1
, and currently, the framework is referred to as the Dirac-Bergmann algorithm. This approach involves identifying constraints within a system, resulting in primary, secondary, tertiary, and so forth constraints. The primary objective is to categorize these constraints as first and second class. The latter are instrumental in constructing a new structure referred to as ’Dirac brackets,’ from which we can extract the system’s dynamics. Obtaining these new geometric structures is essential for the quantization of such systems
Teitelboim
;
Sun
.

The significance of singular systems lies in the fact that the fundamental interactions of nature, such as electromagnetism, gravity, and nuclear forces, are described by singular Lagrangians and, most importantly, exhibit gauge symmetries. Gauge theories are particularly intriguing because it is believed that any proposed physical interaction should possess gauge symmetries or variations thereof, often with spontaneously broken symmetries.

An alternative to the Dirac-Bergmann algorithm consists of a geometric approach that utilizes a symplectic structure, the Faddeev-Jackiw method
Faddeev
. This method is presented as a description based on linear Lagrangians, allowing for the direct derivation of generalized brackets (when the system is regular) as elements of a matrix called the Faddeev-Jackiw symplectic matrix.In the original formulation, it did not deal with systems with constraints. The extension of the method was carried out by Barcelos-Neto and Wotzasek
Bar
;
Bar1
, in which the structure of constraints is obtained from the zero modes of the symplectic matrix. The derivation of gauge symmetries in the extended full configuration space was provided by Montani and Wotzasek
Montani
. The equivalence between the Dirac-Bergmann algorithm and the original Faddeev-Jackiw method is discussed in
Garcia
, and in
Liao
, an approach is presented that rigorously ensures the equivalence between both methods for constrained systems.

In the symplectic approach, all constraints have the same status, meaning there is no classification of them; therefore, the count of degrees of freedom is simpler. Relevant information can be obtained through the invertible symplectic matrix, where the elements correspond to the generalized brackets of Faddeev-Jackiw, coinciding with the brackets obtained in the Dirac method
Liao
;
Huang
. Because there is no classification of constraints, the Faddeev-Jackiw formalism turns out to be simpler, with the only difficulty lying in the inversion of the symplectic matrix.

The Faddeev-Jackiw method has proven to be useful in various applications, not only in the study of the electromagnetic field
Huang1
and Yang-Mills theory
Huang2
, but also in topological theories
Bar3
;
Lee
and gravitational models
Escalante
;
Ro
;
Jaime
. Its application in mechanical systems is focused on toy models
Anjali
;
Caro
to exemplify the functioning of the method.

Therefore, in this work, classical mechanical systems of the mass-spring type will be studied. These systems will be described by singular Lagrangians, and thus, the extended Faddeev-Jackiw symplectic formalism
Liao
will be implemented. It is noteworthy that these systems have already been analyzed by David Brown
Brown
;
Brown1
using the Dirac-Bergmann algorithm with the aim of demonstrating that the same bracket structures and equations of motion are obtained. This highlights the equivalence between both methods.

In Section II, a system of two springs coupled to a mass under the influence of gravity is considered. In Section III, a pendulum is studied in which its coupling point is movable through two springs separated by a distance from the origin. Generalized Faddeev-Jackiw brackets and equations of motion will be derived for both systems, both of which exhibit constraints. In Section IV, the Lagrangian proposed in
Brown1
is analyzed, which has the particularity of not only having constraints but also being a system with gauge symmetry. Therefore, gauge transformation rules and generalized brackets will be obtained.

V
Conclusions AND PROSPECTS

Generalized Faddeev-Jackiw brackets have been obtained from the inversion of the symplectic matrix, allowing us to derive the equations of motion. For the system discussed in Section IV, gauge transformation rules are obtained from the null vectors associated with the non-invertible symplectic matrix (
42
). These rules keep both the Lagrangian and the equations of motion invariant. It is noteworthy that the results obtained coincide with those reported in
Brown
;
Brown1
, establishing equivalence with the Dirac-Bergmann algorithm. This shows that the symplectic approach does not require extensive calculations or classifications of constraints; the only difficulty lies in inverting the symplectic matrix.

Therefore, it can be stated that the Faddeev-Jackiw method is a great alternative for handling singular systems, proving to be more effective. Once the bracket structures are obtained, they can be promoted to commutators for quantization
Barbosa

\{\xi_{i},\xi_{j}\}_{FJ}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}[\hat{\xi_{i}},\hat{\xi_{j}}].

;

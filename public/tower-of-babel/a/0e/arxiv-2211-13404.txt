Asymptotic stability and sharp decay rates to the linearly stratified Boussinesq equations in horizontally periodic strip domain
Juhi Jang, Junha Kim

Abstract
We consider an initial boundary value problem of the multi-dimensional Boussinesq equations in the absence of thermal diffusion with velocity damping or velocity diffusion under the stress free boundary condition in horizontally periodic strip domain. We prove the global-in-time existence of classical solutions in high order Sobolev spaces satisfying high order compatibility conditions around the linearly stratified equilibrium, the convergence of the temperature to the asymptotic profile, and sharp decay rates of the velocity field and temperature fluctuation in all intermediate norms based on spectral analysis combined with energy estimates. To the best of our knowledge, our results provide first sharp decay rates for the temperature fluctuation and the vertical velocity to the linearly stratified Boussinesq equations in all intermediate norms.

Introduction

We consider the Boussinesq equations for buoyant fluids

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}v_{t}+\nu(-\Delta)^{\alpha}v+(v\cdot\nabla)v=-\nabla p+\rho e_{d},\\
\rho_{t}+\kappa(-\Delta)^{\beta}\rho+(v\cdot\nabla)\rho=0,\\
\operatorname{div}\,\,v=0,\\
v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),\,\,\rho(x,0)=\rho_{0}(x),\end{array}\right.

In fact, the two-dimensional inviscid Boussinesq equations can be viewed as the three-dimensional axisymmetric Euler equations for swirling flows

\left\{\begin{aligned} &v_{t}+(-\Delta)^{\alpha}v+(v\cdot\nabla)v=-\nabla P+\theta e_{d},\qquad\mathrm{div}\,v=0,\\
&\theta_{t}+(v\cdot\nabla)\theta=-v_{d},\\
&v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),\,\,\theta(x,0)=\theta_{0}(x),\end{aligned}\right.

where the boundary conditions of the velocity field are preserved and

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

with

satisfying

\delta>0
such that if initial data
(v_{0},\,\theta_{0})\in\mathbb{X}^{m}\times X^{m}(\Omega)
with
\operatorname{div}v_{0}=0
,
\int_{\Omega}v_{0}\,\mathrm{d}x=0
, and
\|(v_{0},\theta_{0})\|_{H^{m}}^{2}<\delta^{2}
, then (
1.2
) with
\alpha=1
possesses a unique global classical solution
(v,\,\theta)
satisfying

v\in C([0,\infty);\mathbb{X}^{m}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}([0,\infty);\mathbb{X}^{m+1}(\Omega)),\qquad\theta\in C([0,\infty);X^{m}(\Omega))

with

\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}\|(v,\theta)(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\nabla v(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\nabla_{h}\theta(t)\|_{H^{m-2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq 4\|(v_{0},\theta_{0})\|_{H^{m}}^{2}.

(1.3)

Moreover, there exists a function

\sigma(x_{d}):=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d-1}}\theta_{0}\,\mathrm{d}x_{h}-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left((v\cdot\nabla)\theta+v_{d}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x_{h}

(1.4)

such that

(1+t)^{\frac{m-s}{4}}\|\theta(t)-\sigma(x_{d})\|_{H^{s}}+(1+t)^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{m-s}{4}}||v(t)\|_{H^{s}}+(1+t)^{1+\frac{m-s}{4}}\|v_{d}(t)\|_{H^{s}}\leq C

(1.5)

for any
s\in[0,m]
.

Remark 1.2
.

The assumption
\int_{\Omega}v_{0}\,\mathrm{d}x=0
is essential for the velocity field
v
decaying in

(see Lemma
2.1
).

Remark 1.3
.

Indeed for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

t^{\frac{3}{4}+\frac{m-s}{4}}\|v(t)\|_{H^{s-\epsilon}}\leq C

for any
s\in[0,m+1]
. See Proposition
6.6
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
with
d\geq 2
and let
m\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
m>2+\frac{d}{2}
. Then there exists a constant
\delta>0
such that if initial data
(v_{0},\,\theta_{0})\in\mathbb{X}^{m}\times X^{m}(\Omega)
with
\operatorname{div}v_{0}=0
and
\|(v_{0},\theta_{0})\|_{H^{m}}^{2}<\delta^{2}
, then (
1.2
) with
\alpha=0
possesses a unique global classical solution
(v,\,\theta)
satisfying

v\in C([0,\infty);\mathbb{X}^{m}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}([0,\infty);\mathbb{X}^{m}(\Omega)),\qquad\theta\in C([0,\infty);X^{m}(\Omega))

with

\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}\|(v,\theta)(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\|v(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\nabla_{h}\theta(t)\|_{H^{m-1}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq 4\|(v_{0},\theta_{0})\|_{H^{m}}^{2}.

(1.6)

Moreover, there exists a function

defined by (

(1+t)^{\frac{m-s}{2}}\|\theta(t)-\sigma(x_{d})\|_{H^{s}}+(1+t)^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{m-s}{2}}\|v(t)\|_{H^{s}}+(1+t)^{1+\frac{m-s}{2}}\|v_{d}(t)\|_{H^{s}}\leq C

(1.7)

for any
s\in[0,m]
.

Remark 1.5
.

The decay rates for
\theta
and
v_{d}
in Theorem

To the best of our knowledge, our results provide the first sharp decay rates for the temperature fluctuation and the vertical velocity in all intermediate norms. In particular, they show the enhanced
L^{2}
decay rate for higher order initial data, while
H^{m}
decay rate doesn’t change for both velocity damping and velocity diffusion. This is in contrast to parabolic equations for which higher norms enjoy faster decay rates. The regularity index
m
required in our analysis is higher than the one required for the local existence, but it is still significantly smaller than the ones required in the previous results. Also our results demonstrate that the velocity damping leads to faster decay than the velocity diffusion in the presence of the slip boundary, despite having the Poincaré inequality for the velocity field in hand. This is because of coupling structure between the velocity field and the temperature fluctuation of Boussinesq equations: it causes the temperature to decay much slower than the velocity field and the velocity diffusion weakens the temperature damping in high frequency. Moreover, the method developed in this paper is robust and applicable to the periodic box
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, and to various partially dissipative PDEs including non-resistive MHD and IPM (cf.
[
25
]
).

The main difficulty comes from the non-decaying
\theta
and weak damping in
\nabla_{h}\theta
, which makes the standard energy estimates alone hard to bootstrap the local theory to global theory and to capture precise decay rates. To establish the results, we employ the spectral analysis using the orthonormal basis associated to our domain with the slip boundary together with energy estimates, first to obtain the global existence and then to prove the decay rates by relying on the already established uniform bounds of the solutions. The relaxed condition for
m
comes from estimating the key quantities
\int\|\nabla v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,\mathrm{d}t
and
\int\|\partial_{d}v_{d}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,\mathrm{d}t
which appear in the energy estimates. The previous works on the stability problem of (
1.2
) (
d=2
) were devoted to obtaining the temporal decay estimate for
\|u(t)\|_{H^{4}}
or
\|\partial_{1}\operatorname{curl}v(t)\|_{H^{2}}
, which obviously require stronger condition for
m
(see
[
5
]
and
[
14
]
). Getting decay rates in bounded domains turns out to be more subtle than in the whole space, since
\theta
does not decay, while it decays in the whole space.
To prove the sharp decay rates of
(v,\theta-\sigma)
in
H^{s}
norms for all
s\in[0,m]
in our domain, we adapt Elgindi’s the splitting scheme of the density first used for the linearly stratified IPM equation in
\mathbb{T}^{2}

[
19
]
. In particular, splitting the density into decaying part and non-decay part and using the boundedness of high norms obtained from the global existence part, the decay of low norms can be obtained through optimizing splitting scale of frequency in spirit of
[
19
]
.
We refer to Lemma
3.1
for a clear view of the sharp decay estimates for the linearized system of (
1.2
), and Section
6
for controlling the nonlinear terms in (
1.2
) with the splitting scheme.

The rest of this paper proceeds as follows. In Section
2
, we give some preliminary results used for the paper and introduce key function spaces
X^{m}(\Omega),Y^{m}(\Omega),\mathbb{X}^{m}(\Omega)
and their orthonormal bases. Section

Preliminaries

We first introduce some notations that will be used throughout this paper. Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
be the standard inner product on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
for any
d\geq 2
. We use
\gamma
as a multi-index, and let
v_{h}:=(v_{1},\cdots,v_{d-1})^{T}
,
x_{h}:=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d-1})^{T}
, and
\nabla_{h}:=(\partial_{1},\cdots,\partial_{d-1})^{T}
. For any smooth function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, we use the notation

\bar{f}:=f-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d-1}}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x_{h}.

Next we investigate the average of the solution
(v,\theta)
over time.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(v,\theta)
be a smooth solution to (
1.2
) with
\alpha\in\{0,1\}
. Then, there hold

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d-1}}v_{d}(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x_{h}=0,\qquad x_{d}\in[-1,1]

(2.1)

and

\int_{\Omega}\theta(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\theta_{0}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

(2.2)

for all
t\geq 0
. Moreover, if
\alpha=1
, then

\int_{\Omega}v_{h}(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}v_{h}(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x,

(2.3)

and if
\alpha=0
,

\int_{\Omega}v_{h}(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x=e^{-t}\int_{\Omega}v_{h}(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(2.4)

Proof.

By the divergence-free condition and the boundary condition
v_{d}(x_{h},-1)=0
, we have

0=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d-1}\times[-1,x_{d}]}\nabla_{h}\cdot v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d-1}\times[-1,x_{d}]}\partial_{d}v_{d}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d-1}}v_{d}(x_{h},x_{d})\,\mathrm{d}x_{h}

for all
x_{d}\in[-1,1]
. From the
v_{h}
equation in (
1.2
), we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}x_{h}+\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}(v\cdot\nabla)v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}P\,\mathrm{d}x.

Integration by parts and the boundary condition for
v_{d}
yield

\int_{\Omega}(v\cdot\nabla)v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}P\,\mathrm{d}x=0,

thus,

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}x_{h}+\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta)^{\alpha}v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

This gives (
2.4
) when
\alpha=0
. In the case of
\alpha=1
,
\partial_{d}v_{h}=0
on
\partial\Omega
implies (
2.3
). Similarly, we can obtain (
2.2
) by the use of (
2.1
). This completes the proof.
∎

2.1
Boundary conditions

In the section, we briefly show in both cases
\alpha=0
and
\alpha=1
the high compatibility conditions, whose statement is as follows:
Let
(v,\theta)
be a global-in-time smooth solution to (
1.2
) and suppose that there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\partial_{d}^{2k}\theta_{0}=0
holds on the boundary for all
0\leq k\leq n
. Then, we have

\partial_{d}^{2k}v_{d}=\partial_{d}^{2k-1+2\alpha}v_{h}=\partial_{d}^{2k}\theta=\partial_{d}^{2k-1}P=0

(2.5)

for any
1\leq k\leq n
.

When
d=2
, Castro, Córdoba, and Lear
[
5
]
and Dong
[
14
]
showed (
2.5
) for
\alpha=0
and
\alpha=1
respectively. It is not hard to extend it to the
d\geq 3
case. Here, we only give details for the case
\alpha=1
.

From our boundary conditions, we see that

v_{d}(x)=\theta(x)=0\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\partial_{d}v_{h}(x)=0,\qquad x\in\partial\Omega.

(2.6)

By (
2.6
) and the incompressibility, it holds

\partial_{d}^{2}v_{d}(x)=-\nabla_{h}\cdot\partial_{d}v_{h}(x)=0,\qquad x\in\partial\Omega.

Then from the
v_{d}
equation in (
1.2
), we can see

-\partial_{d}P=\partial_{t}v_{d}-\Delta v_{d}+(v\cdot\nabla)v_{d}-\theta=0

on the boundary. Next, we apply
\partial_{d}
to the
v_{h}
equation in (
1.2
) and have

\partial_{t}\partial_{d}v_{h}-\Delta\partial_{d}v_{h}+\partial_{d}(v\cdot\nabla)v_{h}=-\nabla_{h}\partial_{d}P.

The previous results implies that
\partial_{d}^{3}v_{h}=0
on the boundary. From the
\theta
equation in (
1.2
), we can see

\partial_{t}\partial_{d}^{2}\theta+\partial_{d}^{2}(v\cdot\nabla)\theta=-\partial_{d}^{2}v_{d},

hence,

\partial_{t}\partial_{d}^{2}\theta+\partial_{d}v_{d}\partial_{d}^{2}\theta+(v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h})\partial_{d}^{2}\theta=0,\qquad x\in\partial\Omega.

Consider the flow map
\Phi(t,x)
with
\partial_{t}\Phi(t,x)=(v_{h}(t,\Phi(t,x)),0)
. Then, it holds

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\partial_{d}^{2}\theta(t,\Phi(t,x))+\partial_{d}v_{d}(t,\Phi(t,x))\partial_{d}^{2}\theta(t,\Phi(t,x))=0.

By the use of Grönwall’s inequality, we have

\partial_{d}^{2}\theta(t,\Phi(t,x))=\partial_{d}^{2}\theta_{0}(x)\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{d}v_{d}(\tau,\Phi(\tau,x))\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right).

Thus,
\partial_{d}^{2}\theta_{0}=0
is conserved over time on the boundary, whenever
\partial_{d}v_{d}\in L^{1}_{t}
. Thus, (
2.5
) with
k=1
is obtained. It is clear that

\partial_{d}^{4}v_{d}(x)=-\nabla_{h}\cdot\partial_{d}^{3}v_{h}(x)=0,\qquad x\in\partial\Omega.

Repeating the above processes, we can deduce (
2.5
) for all
1\leq k\leq n
.

2.2
Functional spaces and orthonormal bases

To introduce our solution spaces, we define orthonormal sets
\{b_{q}\}_{q\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{c_{q}\}_{q\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}
by

\displaystyle b_{q}(x_{d})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}qx_{d})&\quad q:\mbox{ even }\\
\displaystyle\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}qx_{d})&\quad q:\mbox{ odd }\end{cases}\qquad\mbox{with }x_{d}\in[-1,1],

\displaystyle c_{q}(x_{d})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\displaystyle-\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}qx_{d})&\quad q:\mbox{ odd }\\
\displaystyle\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}qx_{d})&\quad q:\mbox{ even }\end{cases}\qquad\mbox{with }x_{d}\in[-1,1].

Note that each set is orthonormal basis for
L^{2}([-1,1])
. Let

\mathscr{B}_{n,q}(x):=e^{2\pi in\cdot x_{h}}b_{q}(x_{d}),\qquad(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N},

\mathscr{C}_{n,q}(x):=e^{2\pi n\cdot x_{h}}c_{q}(x_{d}),\qquad(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

Then we have the following relations

\nabla_{h}\mathscr{B}_{n,q}=2\pi in\mathscr{B}_{n,q},\qquad\nabla_{h}\mathscr{C}_{n,q}=2\pi in\mathscr{C}_{n,q},\qquad\partial_{d}\mathscr{B}_{n,q}=\frac{\pi}{2}q\mathscr{C}_{n,q},\qquad\partial_{d}\mathscr{C}_{n,q}=-\frac{\pi}{2}q\mathscr{B}_{n,q}.

Now, we consider the function spaces

\displaystyle X^{m}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=\{f\in H^{m}(\Omega);\partial_{d}^{k}f|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\quad k=0,2,4,\cdots,m^{*}\},

\displaystyle Y^{m}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=\{f\in H^{m}(\Omega);\partial_{d}^{k}f|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\quad k=1,3,5,\cdots,m_{*}\},

where

\displaystyle m^{*}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle m-2,&\quad m:\mbox{ even }\\
\displaystyle m-1,&\quad m:\mbox{ odd }\end{cases}\qquad and\qquad m_{*}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle m-1,&\quad m:\mbox{ even }\\
\displaystyle m-2,&\quad m:\mbox{ odd }.\end{cases}

Then,
\{\mathscr{B}_{n,q}\}_{(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}}
and
\{\mathscr{C}_{n,q}\}_{(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}
become orthonormal bases of
X^{m}(\Omega)
and
Y^{m}(\Omega)
respectively. For the velocity field, we define a
d
-dimensional vector space
\mathbb{X}^{m}(\Omega)
by

\mathbb{X}^{m}(\Omega):=\{v\in H^{m}(\Omega);v=(v_{h},v_{d})\in Y^{m}(\Omega)\times X^{m}(\Omega)\}.

We introduce series expansions of the elements in
X^{m}(\Omega)
and
Y^{m}(\Omega)
. Let

\mathscr{F}_{b}f(n,q):=\int_{\Omega}f(x)\overline{\mathscr{B}_{n,q}(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad\mathscr{F}_{c}f(n,q):=\int_{\Omega}f(x)\overline{\mathscr{C}_{n,q}(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x

for each
(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}
and
(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
respectively. Then for any
f\in X^{m}(\Omega)
and
g\in Y^{m}(\Omega)
, we can write

f(x)=\sum_{(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}}\mathscr{F}_{b}f(n,q)\mathscr{B}_{n,q}(x),\qquad g(x)=\sum_{(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}\mathscr{F}_{c}g(n,q)\mathscr{C}_{n,q}(x).

We refer to
[
5
, Lemma 3.1]
for details.

We give two simple lemmas. The first one implies
fg\in X^{m}
when
f\in X^{m}
and
g\in Y^{m}
, and the second one implies
fg\in Y^{m}
when
f,g\in X^{m}
or
f,g\in Y^{m}
for any given
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m>d/2
. Since the proofs are elementary, we omit them.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
q_{e}
and
q_{o}
be even and odd number respectively. Then, there hold

\displaystyle-\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}q_{e}x_{d})\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}q_{o}x_{d})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}(q_{e}+q_{o})x_{d})-\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}(q_{e}-q_{o})x_{d})\right),

\displaystyle\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}q_{e}x_{d})\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}q^{\prime}_{e}x_{d})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}(q_{e}+q_{e}^{\prime})x_{d})+\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}(q_{e}-q_{e}^{\prime})x_{d})\right),

\displaystyle-\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}q_{o}x_{d})\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}q_{o}^{\prime}x_{d})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}(q^{\prime}_{o}+q_{o})x_{d})+\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}(q_{o}^{\prime}-q_{o})x_{d})\right),

\displaystyle\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}q_{o}x_{d})\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}q_{e}x_{d})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}(q_{o}+q_{e})x_{d})+\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}(q_{o}-q_{e})x_{d})\right).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
q-q^{\prime}
and
q-q^{\prime\prime}
be odd and even number respectively. Then, there hold

\displaystyle-\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}qx_{d})\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}q^{\prime\prime}x_{d})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}(q+q^{\prime\prime})x_{d})-\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}(q-q^{\prime\prime})x_{d})\right),

\displaystyle\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}qx_{d})\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}q^{\prime}x_{d})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}(q+q^{\prime})x_{d})+\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}(q-q^{\prime})x_{d})\right),

\displaystyle-\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}qx_{d})\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}q^{\prime}x_{d})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}(q^{\prime}+q)x_{d})+\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}(q^{\prime}-q)x_{d})\right),

\displaystyle\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}qx_{d})\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}q^{\prime\prime}x_{d})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}(q+q^{\prime\prime})x_{d})+\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}(q-q^{\prime\prime})x_{d})\right).

The next proposition provides convolution estimates similar to the Fourier expansion.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
f,f^{\prime}\in X^{m}
and
g,g^{\prime}\in Y^{m}
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m>\frac{d}{2}
. Then, there hold

\displaystyle\sum_{(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}}|\mathscr{F}_{b}[fg](n,q)|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}}|\mathscr{F}_{b}f(n,q)|\right)\left(\sum_{(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}|\mathscr{F}_{c}g(n,q)|\right),

\displaystyle\sum_{(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}|\mathscr{F}_{c}[ff^{\prime}](n,q)|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}}|\mathscr{F}_{b}f(n,q)|\right)\left(\sum_{(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}}|\mathscr{F}_{b}f^{\prime}(n,q)|\right),

\displaystyle\sum_{(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}|\mathscr{F}_{c}[gg^{\prime}](n,q)|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}|\mathscr{F}_{c}g(n,q)|\right)\left(\sum_{(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}|\mathscr{F}_{c}g^{\prime}(n,q)|\right).

Proof.

We only show the first inequality because the others can be proved similarly. By the series expansions of
f\in X^{m}(\Omega)
and
g\in Y^{m}(\Omega)
, it holds

\displaystyle fg(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}}\mathscr{F}_{b}f(n,q)\mathscr{B}_{n,q}(x)\right)\left(\sum_{(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}\mathscr{F}_{c}g(n,q)\mathscr{C}_{n,q}(x)\right).

By the use of Lemma
2.2
, we can see for each
(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}
that

\displaystyle|\mathscr{F}_{b}[fg](n,q)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n^{\prime}+n^{\prime\prime}=n}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{q^{\prime}+q^{\prime\prime}=q}|\mathscr{F}_{b}f(n^{\prime},q^{\prime})||\mathscr{F}_{c}g(n^{\prime\prime},q^{\prime\prime})|+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{|q^{\prime}-q^{\prime\prime}|=q}|\mathscr{F}_{b}f(n^{\prime},q^{\prime})||\mathscr{F}_{c}g(n^{\prime\prime},q^{\prime\prime})|\right).

This estimate infers

\displaystyle\sum_{(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}}|\mathscr{F}_{b}[fg]|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}}|\mathscr{F}_{b}f|\right)\left(\sum_{(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}|\mathscr{F}_{c}g|\right).

This finishes the proof.
∎

3
Spectral analysis

In this section, we give a different form of (
1.2
) via spectral analysis. Then, we provide temporal decay estimates for the linear operator of (
1.2
). From now on, we use the notations
\tilde{n}:=2\pi n
and
\tilde{q}:=\frac{\pi}{2}q
for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
and
q\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. We define two sets

I:=\{\eta=(\tilde{n},\tilde{q});(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\},\qquad J:=\{\eta=(\tilde{n},\tilde{q});(n,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{N}\}.

We estimate the pressure term frist. From the
v
equation in (
1.2
), we can see

\mathrm{div}\,(v\cdot\nabla)v=-\Delta P+\partial_{d}\theta.

Using the basis
\mathscr{C}_{n,q}(x)=\mathscr{C}_{\eta}(x)
, we have for each
\eta\in I\setminus\{0\}
that

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{c}P(\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\eta|^{2}}\mathscr{F}_{c}[\mathrm{div}\,(v\cdot\nabla)v](\eta)-\frac{1}{|\eta|^{2}}\mathscr{F}_{c}\partial_{d}\theta(\eta).

Since
\nabla_{h}\mathscr{C}_{\eta}=i\tilde{n}\mathscr{C}_{\eta}
and
\partial_{d}\mathscr{C}_{\eta}=-\tilde{q}\mathscr{B}_{\eta}
, we can see

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{c}[\mathrm{div}\,(v\cdot\nabla)v](\eta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}\cdot(v\cdot\nabla)v_{h}(x)\overline{\mathscr{C}_{\eta}(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\partial_{d}(v\cdot\nabla)v_{d}(x)\overline{\mathscr{C}_{\eta}(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=i\tilde{n}\cdot\int_{\Omega}(v\cdot\nabla)v_{h}(x)\overline{\mathscr{C}_{\eta}(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x+\tilde{q}\int_{\Omega}(v\cdot\nabla)v_{d}(x)\overline{\mathscr{B}_{\eta}(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=i\tilde{n}\cdot\mathscr{F}_{c}[(v\cdot\nabla)v_{h}](\eta)+\tilde{q}\mathscr{F}_{b}[(v\cdot\nabla)v_{d}](\eta)

and

\mathscr{F}_{c}\partial_{d}\theta(\eta)=\tilde{q}\mathscr{F}_{b}\theta(\eta).

Thus, we obtain

\nabla P(x)=\left(\sum_{\eta\in I}\mathscr{F}_{c}\nabla_{h}P(\eta)\mathscr{C}_{\eta}(x),\sum_{\eta\in J}\mathscr{F}_{b}\partial_{d}P(\eta)\mathscr{B}_{\eta}(x)\right)^{T},

where

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{c}\nabla_{h}P(\eta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\tilde{n}\otimes\tilde{n}}{|\eta|^{2}}\mathscr{F}_{c}[(v\cdot\nabla)v_{h}](\eta)+i\frac{\tilde{q}\tilde{n}}{|\eta|^{2}}\mathscr{F}_{b}[(v\cdot\nabla)v_{d}](\eta)-i\frac{\tilde{q}\tilde{n}}{|\eta|^{2}}\mathscr{F}_{b}\theta(\eta)

and

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{b}\partial_{d}P(\eta)

\displaystyle=-i\frac{\tilde{q}\tilde{n}}{|\eta|^{2}}\cdot\mathscr{F}_{c}[(v\cdot\nabla)v_{h}](\eta)-\frac{\tilde{q}^{2}}{|\eta|^{2}}\mathscr{F}_{b}[(v\cdot\nabla)v_{d}](\eta)+\frac{\tilde{q}^{2}}{|\eta|^{2}}\mathscr{F}_{b}\theta(\eta).

From these formulas, we have

\begin{gathered}\partial_{t}\mathscr{F}_{c}v_{h}+|\eta|^{2\alpha}\mathscr{F}_{c}v_{h}+\left(I-\frac{\tilde{n}\otimes\tilde{n}}{|\eta|^{2}}\right)\mathscr{F}_{c}[(v\cdot\nabla)v_{h}]+i\frac{\tilde{q}\tilde{n}}{|\eta|^{2}}\mathscr{F}_{b}[(v\cdot\nabla)v_{d}]-i\frac{\tilde{q}\tilde{n}}{|\eta|^{2}}\mathscr{F}_{b}\theta=0\end{gathered}

(3.1)

for
\eta\in I\setminus\{0\}
, and

\begin{gathered}\partial_{t}\mathscr{F}_{b}v_{d}+|\eta|^{2\alpha}\mathscr{F}_{b}v_{d}+\left(1-\frac{\tilde{q}^{2}}{|\eta|^{2}}\right)\mathscr{F}_{b}[(v\cdot\nabla)v_{d}]-i\frac{\tilde{q}\tilde{n}}{|\eta|^{2}}\cdot\mathscr{F}_{c}[(v\cdot\nabla)v_{h}]-\frac{|\tilde{n}|^{2}}{|\eta|^{2}}\mathscr{F}_{b}\theta=0,\end{gathered}

(3.2)

\begin{gathered}\partial_{t}\mathscr{F}_{b}\theta+\mathscr{F}_{b}[(v\cdot\nabla)\theta]+\mathscr{F}_{b}v_{d}=0\end{gathered}

(3.3)

for
\eta\in J
. Due to the linear structure of (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we can observe a partially dissipative nature by writing the two equaitons at once with
\mathbb{u}:=(v_{d},\theta)^{T}
.

Let us define an operator
\mathscr{F}:(L^{2})^{d-1}\times L^{2}\to\mathbb{C}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{C}
by
\mathscr{F}:=(\mathscr{F}_{c},\mathscr{F}_{b})
. Then, it follows

\partial_{t}\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbb{u}+M\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbb{u}+\langle\mathbb{P}\,\mathscr{F}(v\cdot\nabla)v,e_{d}\rangle e_{1}+\mathscr{F}_{b}[(v\cdot\nabla)\theta]e_{2}=0,

(3.4)

where

\mathbb{P}:=I-\frac{1}{|\eta|^{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\tilde{n}\otimes\tilde{n}&-i\tilde{q}\tilde{n}\\
\hline\cr i\tilde{q}\tilde{n}&\tilde{q}^{2}\end{array}\right),\qquad M:=\begin{pmatrix}|\eta|^{2\alpha}&-\frac{|\tilde{n}|^{2}}{|\eta|^{2}}\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

For simplicity, we use the notation

N(v,\theta):=\langle\mathbb{P}\,\mathscr{F}(v\cdot\nabla)v,e_{d}\rangle e_{1}+\mathscr{F}_{b}[(v\cdot\nabla)\theta]e_{2}.

Since the characteristic equation of
M^{T}
is given by

\operatorname{det}\big(M^{T}-\lambda I\big)=\lambda^{2}-|\eta|^{2\alpha}\lambda+\frac{|\tilde{n}|^{2}}{|\eta|^{2}},

the two pair of eigenvalue and eigenvector
(\lambda_{\pm}(\eta),\overline{\mathbb{a}_{\pm}(\eta)})
satisfy

\lambda_{\pm}(\eta)=\frac{|\eta|^{2\alpha}\pm\sqrt{|\eta|^{4\alpha}-{4|\tilde{n}|^{2}}/{|\eta|^{2}}}}{2},\qquad\overline{\mathbb{a}_{\pm}(\eta)}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{\pm}\\
-\frac{|\tilde{n}|^{2}}{|\eta|^{2}}\end{pmatrix},

where
M^{T}\overline{\mathbb{a}_{\pm}(\eta)}=\lambda(\eta)_{\pm}\overline{\mathbb{a}_{\pm}(\eta)}
holds. We note that there is no pair
\eta\in J
satisfying
|\eta|^{4\alpha}-{4|\tilde{n}|^{2}}/{|\eta|^{2}}=0
. Since

A:=(\overline{\mathbb{a}_{+}}\,\,\overline{\mathbb{a}_{-}})\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad B:=\frac{1}{\lambda_{+}-\lambda_{-}}\begin{pmatrix}1&\frac{|\eta|^{2}}{|\tilde{n}|^{2}}\lambda_{-}\\
-1&-\frac{|\eta|^{2}}{|\tilde{n}|^{2}}\lambda_{+}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{b}_{+}\\
\mathbb{b}_{-}\end{pmatrix}

satisfy
BA=I
, it follows by Duhamel’s principle

\langle\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbb{u}(t),\mathbb{a}_{\pm}\rangle\mathbb{b}_{\pm}=e^{-\lambda_{\pm}t}\langle\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbb{u}_{0},\mathbb{a}_{\pm}\rangle\mathbb{b}_{\pm}-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{\pm}(t-\tau)}\langle N(v,\theta)(\tau),\mathbb{a}_{\pm}\rangle\mathbb{b}_{\pm}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

(3.5)

However, using this formula directly can be problematic because of the unboundedness of
|\mathbb{b}_{\pm}|
around the set
\{|\eta|^{4\alpha}=4|\tilde{n}|^{2}/|\eta|^{2}\}
. For this reason, we employ

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{b}\theta(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\pm}e^{-\lambda_{j}t}\langle\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbb{u}_{0},\mathbb{a}_{j}\rangle\langle\mathbb{b}_{j},e_{2}\rangle-\sum_{j\in\pm}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{j}(t-\tau)}\langle N(v,\theta)(\tau),\mathbb{a}_{j}\rangle\langle\mathbb{b}_{j},e_{2}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\tau

(3.6)

\displaystyle=(e^{-\lambda_{-}t}-e^{-\lambda_{+}t})\langle\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbb{u}_{0},\mathbb{a}_{-}\rangle\langle\mathbb{b}_{-},e_{2}\rangle+e^{-\lambda_{+}t}\mathscr{F}_{b}\theta_{0}

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}(e^{-\lambda_{-}(t-\tau)}-e^{-\lambda_{+}(t-\tau)})\langle N(v,\theta)(\tau),\mathbb{a}_{-}\rangle\langle\mathbb{b}_{-},e_{2}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{+}(t-\tau)}\mathscr{F}_{b}[(v\cdot\nabla)\theta]\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

which allows us to get rid of the singularity of
\mathbf{b}_{\pm}
. Here, we note some useful calculations when using (
3.6
). From the definition of
\lambda_{\pm}
,
\mathbf{a}_{\pm}
, and
\mathbf{b}_{\pm}
, we have

|e^{-\lambda_{+}(\eta)t}|\leq e^{-|\eta|^{2\alpha}\frac{t}{2}},\qquad|e^{\lambda_{-}(\eta)t}|\leq\begin{cases}e^{-|\eta|^{2\alpha}\frac{t}{2}},&\qquad|\eta|^{2+4\alpha}-4|\tilde{n}|^{2}\leq 0,\\
e^{-\frac{|\tilde{n}|^{2}}{|\eta|^{2+2\alpha}}t},&\qquad|\eta|^{2+4\alpha}-4|\tilde{n}|^{2}\geq 0,\end{cases}

(3.7)

|\mathbb{a}_{-}|^{2}=|\lambda_{-}|^{2}+\frac{|\tilde{n}|^{4}}{|\eta|^{4}},\qquad|\langle\mathbb{b}_{-},e_{2}\rangle|^{2}=\frac{|\eta|^{4}|\lambda_{+}|^{2}}{|\tilde{n}|^{4}|\lambda_{+}-\lambda_{-}|^{2}}.

Thus, it follows

|\mathbb{a}_{-}||\langle\mathbb{b}_{-},e_{2}\rangle|\leq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{C}{|\lambda_{+}-\lambda_{-}|},&\qquad|\eta|^{2+4\alpha}-4|\tilde{n}|^{2}\leq 0,\\
\displaystyle\frac{C|\eta|^{2\alpha}}{|\lambda_{+}-\lambda_{-}|},&\qquad|\eta|^{2+4\alpha}-4|\tilde{n}|^{2}\geq 0.\end{cases}

Let us consider the three sets

\displaystyle D_{1}

\displaystyle:=\{\eta\in J;|\eta|^{4\alpha}-\frac{4|\tilde{n}|^{2}}{|\eta|^{2}}\leq 0\},

\displaystyle D_{2}

\displaystyle:=\{\eta\in J;0\leq|\eta|^{4\alpha}-\frac{4|\tilde{n}|^{2}}{|\eta|^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{4}|\eta|^{4\alpha}\},

\displaystyle D_{3}

\displaystyle=\{\eta\in J;|\eta|^{4\alpha}-\frac{4|\tilde{n}|^{2}}{|\eta|^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{4}|\eta|^{4\alpha}\},

with
J=D_{1}\cup D_{2}\cup D_{3}
. Then, for any
\mathbb{f}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle|(e^{-\lambda_{-}t}-e^{-\lambda_{+}t})\langle\mathbb{f},\mathbb{a}_{-}\rangle\langle\mathbb{b}_{-},e_{2}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-|\eta|^{2\alpha}\frac{t}{4}}|\mathbb{f}|,

\displaystyle\quad\eta\in D_{1},

(3.8)

\displaystyle|(e^{-\lambda_{-}t}-e^{-\lambda_{+}t})\langle\mathbb{f},\mathbb{a}_{-}\rangle\langle\mathbb{b}_{-},e_{2}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-|\eta|^{2\alpha}\frac{t}{4}}|\mathbb{f}|,

\displaystyle\quad\eta\in D_{2},

\displaystyle|(e^{-\lambda_{-}t}-e^{-\lambda_{+}t})\langle\mathbb{f},\mathbb{a}_{-}\rangle\langle\mathbb{b}_{-},e_{2}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\frac{|\tilde{n}|^{2}}{|\eta|^{2+2\alpha}}t}|\mathbb{f}|,

\displaystyle\quad\eta\in D_{3}.

For the first and second inequalities, we apply the mean value theorem so that

|(e^{-\lambda_{-}t}-e^{-\lambda_{+}t})\langle\mathbb{f},\mathbb{a}_{-}\rangle\langle\mathbb{b}_{-},e_{2}\rangle|\leq C\frac{|e^{-\lambda_{-}t}-e^{-\lambda_{+}t}|}{|\lambda_{+}-\lambda_{-}|}|\mathbb{f}|\leq Cte^{-|\eta|^{2\alpha}\frac{t}{2}}|\mathbb{f}|

for any
\eta\in D_{1}
and

|(e^{-\lambda_{-}t}-e^{-\lambda_{+}t})\langle\mathbb{f},\mathbb{a}_{-}\rangle\langle\mathbb{b}_{-},e_{2}\rangle|\leq C|\eta|^{2\alpha}\frac{|e^{-\lambda_{-}t}-e^{-\lambda_{+}t}|}{|\lambda_{+}-\lambda_{-}|}|\mathbb{f}|\leq C|\eta|^{2\alpha}te^{-|\eta|^{2\alpha}\tau}|\mathbb{f}|,\qquad\tau\in(\frac{t}{4},\frac{t}{2})

for any
\eta\in D_{2}
. One can easily obtain the last inequality in (
3.8
) by the use of

.

(3.9)

Lemma 3.1
.

for all

We recall

\theta_{0}\in X^{m}
and
v_{0}\in\mathbb{X}^{m}
. Then there exists a
T>0
such that there exists a unique classical solution
(v,\theta)
to the stratified Boussinesq equations (
1.2
) satisfying

v\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{X}^{m}(\Omega)),\qquad\theta\in L^{\infty}(0,T;X^{m}(\Omega)).

Let
T^{*}\in(0,\infty]
be the maximal time of existence. Moreover, if
T^{*}<\infty
, then it holds

\lim_{t\nearrow T^{*}}\big(\|v(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\big)=\infty.

We will frequently used the following result on the product estimates (see
[
21
]
for the proof).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for any subset
D\subset\{\gamma;|\gamma|=m\}
, there exists a constant
C=C(m)>0
such that

\left\|\sum_{\gamma\in D}\partial^{\gamma}(fg)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{H^{m}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|g\|_{H^{m}}\right)

for all
f,g\in H^{m}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Moreover, if
m>1
, then it holds

\left\|\sum_{\gamma\in D}\partial^{\gamma}(fg)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{m}}\|g\|_{H^{m}}.

Let us use the notations for
k\in\mathbb{N}

E_{k}(t):=\left(\|v(t)\|_{H^{k}}^{2}+\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{k}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

A_{k}(t):=\sum_{|\gamma|=1}^{k}\int_{\Omega}\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}(t)\partial^{\gamma}\theta(t)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Note that Young’s inequality implies

|A_{k}(t)|\leq\frac{1}{2}E_{k}(t)^{2}.

(4.1)

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
with
d\geq 2
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\geq 2+\frac{d}{2}
. Assume that
(v,\theta)
is a smooth global solution to (
1.2
) with
\alpha=1
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\begin{gathered}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(E_{m}(t)^{2}-A_{m-1}(t))+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{h}\theta(t)\|_{H^{m-2}}^{2}\\
\leq C\|\nabla v(t)\|_{H^{m}}\|\nabla_{h}\theta(t)\|_{H^{m-2}}\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{m}}+C\big(\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|v(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\big)\|\nabla v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\end{gathered}

(4.2)

for all
t>0
.

Proof.

From the system (
1.2
), we have

\begin{gathered}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big(\|v(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\big)+\|\nabla v\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\\
\leq-\sum_{1\leq|\gamma|\leq m}\int_{\Omega}\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla)v\partial^{\gamma}v\,\mathrm{d}x-\sum_{1\leq|\gamma|\leq m}\int_{\Omega}\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla)\theta\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{gathered}

We only consider the
|\gamma|=m
case because the others can be treated similarly. It is clear by the divergence-free condition and the boundary condition that

\int_{\Omega}(v\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}v)\partial^{\gamma}v\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}(v\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}\theta)\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x=0,\qquad|\gamma|=m.

Thus, Lemma
4.2
implies

\left|-\sum_{|\gamma|=m}\int_{\Omega}\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla)v\partial^{\gamma}v\,\mathrm{d}x-\sum_{|\gamma|=m-1}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla v\cdot\partial^{\gamma}\nabla\theta)\cdot\partial^{\gamma}\nabla\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}(\|v\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}^{2}).

For estimating the remainder term

\left|\sum_{|\gamma|=m-2}\int_{\Omega}\partial^{\gamma}(\Delta v\cdot\nabla\theta)\partial^{\gamma}\Delta\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|,

we use a simple formula
\Delta v\cdot\nabla\theta=\Delta v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}\theta+\Delta v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta
. We can infer from Hölder’s inequality with Sobolev embeddings that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{|\gamma|=m-2}\int_{\Omega}\partial^{\gamma}(\Delta v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}\theta)\partial^{\gamma}\Delta\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\Delta v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{H^{m-2}}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{H^{m}}\|\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{H^{m-2}}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}+\|\Delta v\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{H^{m-2}}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}.

Since
m\geq 2+d/2
implies

\|\Delta v\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\|\nabla v\|_{H^{m}},

it follows

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{|\gamma|=m-2}\int_{\Omega}\partial^{\gamma}(\Delta v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}\theta)\partial^{\gamma}\Delta\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{H^{m}}\|\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{H^{m-2}}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}+C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}^{2}.

Otherwise, we have

\begin{gathered}\left|\sum_{|\gamma|=m-2}\int_{\Omega}\partial^{\gamma}(\Delta v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta)\partial^{\gamma}\Delta\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\\
\leq\left|\sum_{|\gamma|=m-3}\int_{\Omega}\partial^{\gamma}\nabla_{h}(\Delta v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta)\cdot\partial^{\gamma}\Delta\nabla_{h}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|+\left|\int_{\Omega}\partial_{d}^{m-2}(\partial_{d}^{2}v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta)\partial_{d}^{m}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|.\end{gathered}

(4.3)

Here, we need to estimate carefully with the boundary conditions. The first integral on the right-hand side is bounded by

\left|\sum_{|\gamma|=m-3}\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial^{\gamma}\nabla_{h}(\Delta v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta)\cdot\partial_{d}\partial^{\gamma}\nabla_{h}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x_{h}\right|+\left|\sum_{|\gamma|=m-2}\int_{\Omega}\partial^{\gamma}\nabla_{h}(\Delta v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta)\cdot\partial^{\gamma}\nabla_{h}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|.

Since
v_{d}\in X^{m+1}(\Omega)
and
\theta\in X^{m}(\Omega)
for a.e.
t>0
, the boundary term vanishes. Lemma
4.2
implies

\left|\sum_{|\gamma|=m-2}\int_{\Omega}\partial^{\gamma}\nabla_{h}(\Delta v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta)\cdot\partial^{\gamma}\nabla_{h}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{H^{m}}\|\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{H^{m-2}}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}.

On the other hand, we write the second integral in (
4.3
) as

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\partial_{d}^{m-2}(\partial_{d}^{2}v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta)\partial_{d}^{m}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\left|\int_{\Omega}\partial_{d}^{m-2}\nabla_{h}\cdot(\partial_{d}v_{h}\partial_{d}\theta)\partial_{d}^{m}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|+\left|\int_{\Omega}\partial_{d}^{m-2}(\partial_{d}v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}\partial_{d}\theta)\partial_{d}^{m}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|.

It can be shown by Hölder’s inequalities and Sobolev embeddings that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\partial_{d}^{m-2}(\partial_{d}v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}\partial_{d}\theta)\partial_{d}^{m}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq(\|\partial_{d}^{m-3}(\partial_{d}^{2}v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}\partial_{d}\theta)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{d}v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}\partial_{d}^{m-1}\theta\|_{L^{2}})\|\partial_{d}^{m}\theta\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{H^{m}}\|\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{H^{m-2}}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}+C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}^{2}.

Since
\partial_{d}v_{h}\partial_{d}\theta\in X^{m-1}
and
\theta\in X^{m}
, it holds

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\partial_{d}^{m-2}\nabla_{h}\cdot(\partial_{d}v_{h}\partial_{d}\theta)\partial_{d}^{m}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{\eta\in J}\tilde{q}^{m-2}i\tilde{n}\cdot\mathscr{F}_{b}(\partial_{d}v_{h}\partial_{d}\theta)(\eta)\overline{\tilde{q}^{m}\mathscr{F}_{b}\theta(\eta)}\right|

(4.4)

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{\eta\in J}\tilde{q}^{m-1}\mathscr{F}_{b}(\partial_{d}v_{h}\partial_{d}\theta)(\eta)\cdot\overline{i\tilde{n}\tilde{q}^{m-1}\mathscr{F}_{b}\theta(\eta)}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\Omega}\partial_{d}^{m-1}(\partial_{d}v_{h}\partial_{d}\theta)\cdot\nabla_{h}\partial_{d}^{m-1}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\partial_{d}^{m-1}(\partial_{d}v_{h}\partial_{d}\theta)\cdot\nabla_{h}\partial_{d}^{m-1}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\hphantom{\qquad\qquad}\leq\left|\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{d}v_{h}\partial_{d}^{m}\theta)\cdot\nabla_{h}\partial_{d}^{m-1}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|+\left|\int_{\Omega}\partial_{d}^{m-2}(\partial_{d}^{2}v_{h}\partial_{d}\theta)\cdot\nabla_{h}\partial_{d}^{m-1}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\hphantom{\qquad\qquad}=\left|\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{d}v_{h}\partial_{d}^{m}\theta)\cdot\nabla_{h}\partial_{d}^{m-1}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|+\left|\int_{\Omega}\partial_{d}^{m-1}(\partial_{d}^{2}v_{h}\partial_{d}\theta)\cdot\nabla_{h}\partial_{d}^{m-2}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\hphantom{\qquad\qquad}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+C\|\nabla v\|_{H^{m}}\|\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{H^{m-2}}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}.

Combining the above estimates, we have

\begin{gathered}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big(\|v(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\big)+\|\nabla v\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\\
\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}(\|v\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}^{2})+C\|\nabla v\|_{H^{m}}\|\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{H^{m-2}}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}.\end{gathered}

(4.5)

Now we claim that

\frac{3}{2}\|\nabla v\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{H^{m-2}}^{2}-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}A_{m-1}(t)-CE_{m}(t)\|\nabla v\|_{H^{m-2}}^{2}.

(4.6)

We recall the
v_{d}
equation in (
1.2
)

\partial_{t}v_{d}-\Delta v_{d}+\langle\mathbb{P}\,(v\cdot\nabla)v,e_{d}\rangle=\langle\mathbb{P}\,\theta e_{d},e_{d}\rangle.

(4.7)

We first take
-\Delta
on the both sides of (
4.7
). Since the definition of
\mathbb{P}
implies

-\Delta\langle\mathbb{P}\,(v\cdot\nabla)v,e_{d}\rangle=-\Delta(v\cdot\nabla)v_{d}+\partial_{d}\nabla\cdot(v\cdot\nabla)v

and

-\Delta\langle\mathbb{P}\,\theta e_{d},e_{d}\rangle=-\Delta_{h}\theta,

it follows

\partial_{t}(-\Delta)v_{d}+(-\Delta)^{2}v_{d}-\Delta(v\cdot\nabla)v_{d}+\partial_{d}\nabla\cdot(v\cdot\nabla)v=-\Delta_{h}\theta.

Then, we have for
|\gamma|=m-2
that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\nabla\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\nabla(-\Delta)\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla)v_{d}\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla)v\partial_{d}\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{h}\partial^{\gamma}\theta|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

On the other hand, we have from the
\theta
equation in (
1.2
)

\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\nabla\partial^{\gamma}\theta\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla)\theta\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

Adding these two equalities gives

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\nabla(-\Delta)\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla)v\partial_{d}\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\big(\nabla\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla)v_{d}\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}\theta+\nabla\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla)\theta\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{h}\partial^{\gamma}\theta|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

We have by Lemma
4.2
and the divergence-free condition that

\bigg|-\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla)v\partial_{d}\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg|\leq C\|v\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}.

From
\partial_{d}v_{d}=-\nabla_{h}\cdot v_{h}
and the cancellation property, we deduce

\left|\int_{\Omega}\nabla(-\Delta)\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\|\Delta\partial^{\gamma}\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial^{\gamma}\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta\partial^{\gamma}\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\partial^{\gamma}\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

and

\bigg|\int_{\Omega}\big(\nabla\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla)v_{d}\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}\theta+\nabla\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla)\theta\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg|\leq C\|v\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}

respectively. The above estimates yields

\displaystyle\sum_{|\gamma|=m-2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v\|_{\dot{H}^{m}}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{d}\|_{\dot{H}^{m-2}}^{2}+C\|v\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-2}}^{2}.

Similarly, we can repeat the above procedure for the lower order derivatives. Then, (
4.6
) is obtained.

We multiply (
4.5
) by
2

\begin{gathered}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big(\|v(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\big)+2\|\nabla v\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\\
\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}(\|v\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}^{2})+C\|\nabla v\|_{H^{m}}\|\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{H^{m-2}}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}},\end{gathered}

and recall (
4.6
)

-\frac{3}{2}\|\nabla v\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{H^{m-2}}^{2}-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}A_{m-1}(t)\leq CE_{m}(t)\|\nabla v\|_{H^{m-2}}^{2}.

Adding these two inequality, we obtain (
4.2
). This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
with
d\geq 2
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m>1+\frac{d}{2}
. Assume that
(v,\theta)
is a smooth global solution to (
1.2
) with
\alpha=0
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\begin{gathered}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(E_{m}(t)^{2}-A_{m}(t))+\frac{1}{2}\|v(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{h}\theta(t)\|_{H^{m-1}}^{2}\\
\leq C\|v(t)\|_{H^{m}}\|\nabla_{h}\theta(t)\|_{H^{m-1}}\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{m}}+C\big(\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|v(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\big)\|\partial_{d}v_{d}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\end{gathered}

(4.8)

for all
t>0
.

Proof.

From (
1.2
), we can have

\begin{gathered}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big(\|v(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\big)+\|v\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\\
\leq-\sum_{1\leq|\gamma|\leq m}\int_{\Omega}\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla)v\partial^{\gamma}v\,\mathrm{d}x-\sum_{1\leq|\gamma|\leq m}\int_{\Omega}\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla)\theta\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x.\end{gathered}

We only estimate
|\gamma|=m
case because the others can be treated similarly. The first integral on the right-hand side can be estimated by lemma
4.2
and the divergence-free condition that

\left|\int_{\Omega}\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla)v\partial^{\gamma}v\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq C\|v\|_{H^{m}}^{3}.

To estimate the remainder term, we consider the case
\partial^{\gamma}\neq\partial_{d}^{m}
first. As estimating the first one, we can see

\left|\int_{\Omega}\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla)\theta\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq C\|v\|_{H^{m}}\|R_{h}\theta\|_{H^{m}}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}.

In the case of
\partial^{\gamma}=\partial_{d}^{m}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{d}^{m}(v\cdot\nabla)\theta\partial_{d}^{m}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{d}^{m-1}(\partial_{d}v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}\theta)\partial_{d}^{m}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\partial_{d}^{m-1}(\partial_{d}v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta)\partial_{d}^{m}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\|v\|_{H^{m}}\|R_{h}\theta\|_{H^{m}}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}+C\|\partial_{d}v_{d}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}\partial_{d}^{m-2}(\partial_{d}^{2}v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta)\partial_{d}^{m}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x.

We note that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{d}^{m-2}(\partial_{d}^{2}v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta)\partial_{d}^{m}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{d}^{m-2}\nabla_{h}\cdot(\partial_{d}v_{h}\partial_{d}\theta)\partial_{d}^{m}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\partial_{d}^{m-2}(\partial_{d}v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}\partial_{d}\theta)\partial_{d}^{m}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{d}^{m-2}\nabla_{h}\cdot(\partial_{d}v_{h}\partial_{d}\theta)\partial_{d}^{m}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x+C\|v\|_{H^{m}}\|R_{h}\theta\|_{H^{m}}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}.

By (
4.4
), we can deduce

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\partial_{d}^{m-2}\nabla_{h}\cdot(\partial_{d}v_{h}\partial_{d}\theta)\partial_{d}^{m}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq C\|v\|_{H^{m}}\|R_{h}\theta\|_{H^{m}}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}},

thus,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\partial_{d}^{m-2}(\partial_{d}^{2}v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta)\partial_{d}^{m}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq C\|v\|_{H^{m}}\|R_{h}\theta\|_{H^{m}}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}.

Collecting the above estimates, we have

\begin{gathered}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big(\|v(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\big)+\|v\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{m}}^{3}+C\|v\|_{H^{m}}\|R_{h}\theta\|_{H^{m}}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}+C\|\partial_{d}v_{d}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}^{2}.\end{gathered}

(4.9)

As estimating (
4.6
), we can show

\frac{3}{2}\|v\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{H^{m-1}}^{2}-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}A_{m}(t)-CE_{m}(t)\|v\|_{H^{m}}^{2}.

(4.10)

We only consider the highest derivative case. We can see from the
v_{d}
equation in (
1.2
)

\partial_{t}(-\Delta)v_{d}+(-\Delta)v_{d}-\Delta(v\cdot\nabla v_{d})+\partial_{d}\nabla\cdot(v\cdot\nabla)v=-\Delta_{h}\theta.

Thus, we have for
|\gamma|=m-1
that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\nabla\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla v_{d})\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla)v\partial_{d}\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{h}\partial^{\gamma}\theta|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

From the
\theta
equation in (
1.2
), it follows

\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\nabla\partial^{\gamma}\theta\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla\theta)\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

Combining the two above gives

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla)v\partial_{d}\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\big(\nabla\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla v_{d})\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}\theta+\nabla\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla\theta)\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{h}\partial^{\gamma}\theta|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

The divergence-free condition and Lemma
4.2
imply

\bigg|\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla)v\partial_{d}\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg|\leq C\|v\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}.

We also have with the divergence-free condition that

\left|\int_{\Omega}\nabla\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\|\partial^{\gamma}\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial^{\gamma}\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\partial^{\gamma}\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\partial^{\gamma}\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

The cancellation property yields

\bigg|\int_{\Omega}\big(\nabla\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla v_{d})\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}\theta+\nabla\partial^{\gamma}(v\cdot\nabla\theta)\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg|\leq C\|v\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}.

Therefore, we deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{|\gamma|=m-1}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\partial^{\gamma}v_{d}\cdot\nabla\partial^{\gamma}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{3}{2}\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{m}}^{2}+C\|v\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-1}}^{2},

which implies (
4.10
).

Multiplying (
4.9
) by 2 and adding (
4.10
) gives (
4.8
). This completes the proof.
∎

and

5.1
Proof of Theorem

:Global-in-time existence part

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
with
d\geq 2
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
m>1+\frac{d}{2}
. Assume that
(v,\theta)
be a smooth global solution to (

\alpha=1
. Then there exists a constant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\bar{\theta}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}v_{d}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\leq-\frac{1}{4}(\|\Lambda^{\alpha}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle+C\|v\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{-2\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|v\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{m}}\|\bar{\theta}\|_{L^{2}}-\int_{\Omega}v_{d}\partial_{d}\widetilde{\theta}\cdot\bar{\theta}\,\mathrm{d}x.

To estimate the integral on the right-hand side, we note

\displaystyle\left|-\int_{\Omega}v_{d}\partial_{d}\widetilde{\theta}\cdot\bar{\theta}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\left|-\int_{\Omega}v_{d}\partial_{d}\widetilde{\theta}\cdot-\Delta_{h}(-\Delta)^{-1}\bar{\theta}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|+\left|-\int_{\Omega}v_{d}\partial_{d}\widetilde{\theta}\cdot-\partial_{d}^{2}(-\Delta)^{-1}\bar{\theta}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|.

We consider

also used here. Hence, we can deduce

\displaystyle\left|-\int_{\Omega}v_{d}\partial_{d}\widetilde{\theta}\cdot\bar{\theta}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq C\|\Lambda^{\alpha}v\|_{L^{2}}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}\|\Lambda^{-\alpha}R_{h}\theta\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\bar{\theta}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}v_{d}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right)

it holds

\begin{gathered}-\frac{1}{8}(\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\leq-\frac{1}{8M}\left(\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\bar{\theta}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}v_{d}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\\

, we deduce

\displaystyle\left|-\int_{\Omega}(v\cdot\nabla)\theta(R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}\theta-\frac{1}{2}R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}v_{d})\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}(\|v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}+\|R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}})+C(\|v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\|\partial_{d}\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Thus, by
W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{m-\alpha}(\Omega)
, (
6.1
), and Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\|\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}v_{d}R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-4\alpha}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\leq-(\frac{1}{4}-C\|\theta\|_{H^{m}})(\|R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+C\|v\|_{L^{2}}(\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{L^{\infty}})(\|v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}+\|R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}})

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{8}(\|R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+C\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{m}}^{2}+\|R_{h}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-\alpha}}^{2}).

Let
M\geq 1
which will be specified later. Since

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta\in J}\left(\frac{1}{M}-\frac{|\tilde{n}|^{2}}{|\eta|^{2(1+\alpha)}}\right)|\mathscr{F}R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}\theta(\eta)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{M}\sum_{\frac{|\tilde{n}|^{2}}{|\eta|^{2(1+\alpha)}}\leq\frac{1}{M},|\tilde{n}|\neq 0}|\mathscr{F}R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}\theta(\eta)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{M^{1+\frac{m}{1+\alpha}}}\|R_{h}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-\alpha}}^{2},

together with

\left|-\int_{\Omega}v_{d}R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-4\alpha}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\|\Lambda^{-2\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}\|R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(6.7)

we can have as estimating (
6.4
) that

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8}\left(\|R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{16M}\left(\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}v_{d}R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-4\alpha}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right)+\frac{1}{8M^{1+\frac{m}{1+\alpha}}}\|R_{h}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-\alpha}}^{2}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\|\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}v_{d}R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-4\alpha}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{16M}\left(\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}v_{d}R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-4\alpha}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{8M^{1+\frac{m}{1+\alpha}}}\|R_{h}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-\alpha}}^{2}+C\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{m}}^{2}+\|R_{h}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-\alpha}}^{2}).

We take
M=1+\frac{t}{8(1+\frac{m}{1+\alpha})}
. Then, we can have with (
6.6
) that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left((1+\frac{t}{8(1+\frac{m}{1+\alpha})})^{1+\frac{m}{1+\alpha}}\left(\|\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}v_{d}R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-4\alpha}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{\tau\in[0,t]}(1+\frac{\tau}{8(1+\frac{m}{1+\alpha})})^{1+\frac{m}{1+\alpha}}\left(\|\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}\theta(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{m}}^{2}+\|R_{h}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-\alpha}}^{2})

\displaystyle+C(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{m}}^{2}+\|R_{h}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-\alpha}}^{2}).

We integrate it over time and use (
6.7
) with

\int_{0}^{\infty}(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{m}}^{2}+\|R_{h}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-\alpha}}^{2})\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C.

Then, for

f(t):=\sup_{\tau\in[0,t]}(1+\frac{\tau}{8(1+\frac{m}{1+\alpha})})^{1+\frac{m}{1+\alpha}}\left(\|\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}\theta(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right),

it holds

\displaystyle f(t)\leq C+\int_{0}^{t}f(\tau)(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{m}}^{2}+\|R_{h}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-\alpha}}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{d}\|_{L^{\infty}})\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Applying Grönwall’s ineqaulity, we obtain

\sup_{\tau\in[0,t]}(1+\frac{\tau}{8(1+\frac{m}{1+\alpha})})^{1+\frac{m}{1+\alpha}}\left(\|\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}\theta(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq C.

With (
6.6
), we deduce (
6.5
). This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
with
d\geq 2
and
\alpha\in\{0,1\}
. Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m>2+\frac{d}{2}+\alpha
and
(v,\theta)
be a smooth global solution to (
1.2
) with (
1.3
) or (
1.6
). Suppose that (
6.1
) be satisfied. Then, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|v_{d}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}\theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-(2+\frac{m}{1+\alpha})}.

(6.8)

Proof.

Recalling the definition of
{\bf b}_{\pm}
, we can verify that

\mathscr{F}_{b}v_{d}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{+}}\frac{|\tilde{n}|^{2}}{|\eta|^{2}}\mathscr{F}_{b}\theta+\frac{|\tilde{n}|^{2}}{|\eta|^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{-}}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{+}}\right)\langle\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{a}_{+}\rangle\langle\mathbf{b}_{+},e_{2}\rangle,\qquad\eta\in J.

We note that

\frac{1}{|\lambda_{+}|}\leq\frac{|\eta|}{|\tilde{n}|}\leq\frac{2}{|\eta|^{2\alpha}},\qquad\eta\in D_{1}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\frac{1}{|\lambda_{+}|}\leq\frac{2}{|\eta|^{2\alpha}},\qquad\eta\not\in D_{1}.

Together with

\frac{|\tilde{n}|^{2}}{|\eta|^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{-}}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{+}}\right)\langle\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{a}_{+}\rangle\langle\mathbf{b}_{+},e_{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{+}}\langle\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{a}_{+}\rangle,

we have

\|v_{d}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}\theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}+C\left(\sum_{\eta\in J}\frac{1}{|\eta|^{4\alpha}}|\langle\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{a}_{+}\rangle|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(6.9)

We show

\left(\sum_{\eta\in J}\frac{1}{|\eta|^{4\alpha}}|\langle\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{a}_{+}\rangle|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq(1+t)^{-(1+\frac{m}{2(1+\alpha)})}(C+C\delta\sup_{\tau\in[0,t]}(1+\tau)^{1+\frac{m}{2(1+\alpha)}}\|v_{d}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}),

(6.10)

where
\delta
is a small constant in (
6.1
). Then by taking
\delta
small enough, we obtain

\sup_{\tau\in[0,t]}(1+\tau)^{1+\frac{m}{2(1+\alpha)}}\|v_{d}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C+C\sup_{\tau\in[0,t]}(1+\tau)^{1+\frac{m}{2(1+\alpha)}}\|R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}\theta(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}.

(6.11)

We recall (
3.5
) and have

\langle\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbb{u}(t),\mathbb{a}_{+}\rangle=e^{-\lambda_{+}t}\langle\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbb{u}_{0},\mathbb{a}_{+}\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{+}(t-\tau)}\langle N(v,\theta)(\tau),\mathbb{a}_{+}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Since
|e^{-\lambda_{+}t}|\leq e^{-|\eta|^{2\alpha}\frac{t}{2}}
for
\eta\in J
, it follows by the Minkowski inequality

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\eta\in J}\frac{1}{|\eta|^{4\alpha}}|\langle\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{a}_{+}\rangle|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{\eta\in J}\frac{1}{|\eta|^{4\alpha}}e^{-|\eta|^{2\alpha}t}|\langle\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbf{u}_{0},\mathbf{a}_{+}\rangle|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sum_{\eta\in J}\frac{1}{|\eta|^{4\alpha}}e^{-|\eta|^{2\alpha}(t-\tau)}|\langle N(v,\theta)(\tau),\mathbb{a}_{+}\rangle|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

From the simple fact
|\mathbf{a}_{+}|^{2}=|\lambda_{+}|^{2}+\frac{|\tilde{n}|^{4}}{|\eta|^{4}}\leq C|\eta|^{4\alpha}
with (
6.1
), we have

\left(\sum_{\eta\in J}\frac{1}{|\eta|^{4\alpha}}e^{-|\eta|^{2\alpha}t}|\langle\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbf{u}_{0},\mathbf{a}_{+}\rangle|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq Ce^{-t}\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-(1+\frac{m}{2(1+\alpha)})}.

We note that

|\langle N(v,\theta)(\tau),\mathbb{a}_{+}\rangle|\leq|\mathscr{F}(v\cdot\nabla)v||\lambda_{+}|+|\mathscr{F}_{b}(v\cdot\nabla)\theta|\frac{|\tilde{n}|^{2}}{|\eta|^{2}}.

(6.12)

Thus, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sum_{\eta\in J}\frac{1}{|\eta|^{4\alpha}}e^{-|\eta|^{2}(t-\tau)}|\langle N(v,\theta)(\tau),\mathbb{a}_{+}\rangle|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sum_{\eta\in J}e^{-|\eta|^{2}(t-\tau)}|\mathscr{F}(v\cdot\nabla)v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sum_{\eta\in J}e^{-|\eta|^{2}(t-\tau)}\frac{1}{|\eta|^{4\alpha}}|\mathscr{F}_{b}(v\cdot\nabla)\theta|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)}(\|(v\cdot\nabla)v(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h})\bar{\theta}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}+\|v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta(\tau)\|_{L^{2}})\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

We have used

(v\cdot\nabla)\theta=(v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h})\bar{\theta}+v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta

in the last inequality. We note by
H^{m-2-\alpha}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}

\|(v\cdot\nabla)v\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|v\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{1+\frac{1+\alpha}{m}}\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{m}}^{1-\frac{1+\alpha}{m}},

\|(v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h})\bar{\theta}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|v\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla_{h}\bar{\theta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}}\|\bar{\theta}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1+\alpha}{m}}\|\bar{\theta}\|_{\dot{H}^{m}}^{1-\frac{1+\alpha}{m}},

and

\|v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{d}\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}.

Combining (
6.5
), (
6.2
) and our assumptions, we can see

\displaystyle(1+\tau)^{1+\frac{m}{2(1+\alpha)}}(\|(v\cdot\nabla)v(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h})\bar{\theta}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}+\|v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta\|_{L^{2}})

\displaystyle\leq C+C\delta\sup_{\tau\in[0,t]}(1+\tau)^{1+\frac{m}{2(1+\alpha)}}\|v_{d}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}},

where
\delta
is a small constant in (
6.1
). Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\tau)}(\|(v\cdot\nabla)v(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h})\bar{\theta}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}+\|v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta\|_{L^{2}})\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-(1+\frac{m}{2(1+\alpha)})}(C+C\delta\sup_{\tau\in[0,t]}(1+\tau)^{1+\frac{m}{2(1+\alpha)}}\|v_{d}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}).

Collecting the above estimates, we obtain (
6.10
) and (
6.11
).

Now, we show

\|R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}\theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-(1+\frac{m}{2(1+\alpha)})}.

(6.13)

Since we have from (
3.2
) and (
3.3
),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sum_{\eta\in J}|\mathscr{F}R_{h}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}v_{d}(\eta)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|(v\cdot\nabla)v\|_{L^{2}}\|R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-4\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}+\sum_{\eta\in J}\frac{|\tilde{n}|^{4}}{|\eta|^{4(1+\alpha)}}\mathscr{F}_{b}\theta(\eta)\mathscr{F}_{b}v_{d}(\eta)

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sum_{\eta\in J}|\mathscr{F}R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}\theta(\eta)|^{2}\leq-\int_{\Omega}(v\cdot\nabla)\theta R_{h}^{4}\Lambda^{-4\alpha}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x-\sum_{\eta\in J}\frac{|\tilde{n}|^{4}}{|\eta|^{4(1+\alpha)}}\mathscr{F}_{b}\theta(\eta)\mathscr{F}_{b}v_{d}(\eta)

respectively, it holds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq-\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|(v\cdot\nabla)v\|_{L^{2}}\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}-\int_{\Omega}(v\cdot\nabla)\theta R_{h}^{4}\Lambda^{-4\alpha}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x.

We can infer from (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) that

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}v_{d}R_{h}^{4}\Lambda^{-6\alpha}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}v_{d}R_{h}^{4}\Lambda^{-6\alpha}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\theta R_{h}^{4}\Lambda^{-6\alpha}v_{d}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\|(v\cdot\nabla)v\|_{L^{2}}\|R_{h}^{4}\Lambda^{-6\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{\Omega}(v\cdot\nabla)\theta R_{h}^{4}\Lambda^{-6\alpha}v_{d}\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\|R_{h}^{3}\Lambda^{-3\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{3}{2}\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Combining the above, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}v_{d}R_{h}^{4}\Lambda^{-6\alpha}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\leq-\frac{1}{4}(\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|R_{h}^{3}\Lambda^{-3\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle+\|v\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}(\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}+\|R_{h}^{3}\Lambda^{-3\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}})-\int_{\Omega}(v\cdot\nabla)\theta(R_{h}^{4}\Lambda^{-4\alpha}\theta-\frac{1}{2}R_{h}^{4}\Lambda^{-6\alpha}v_{d})\,\mathrm{d}x.

We estimate the last integral with
(v\cdot\nabla)\theta=(v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h})\theta+v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta
. Hölder’s inequality implies

\displaystyle\left|-\int_{\Omega}(v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h})\theta(R_{h}^{4}\Lambda^{-4\alpha}\theta-\frac{1}{2}R_{h}^{4}\Lambda^{-6\alpha}v_{d})\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{L^{p}}(\|R_{h}^{4}\Lambda^{-6\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{q}}+\|R_{h}^{3}\Lambda^{-4\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{q}}),

where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{2}
. We take
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{d}
. Then for
\epsilon\in(0,1)
with
m>2+\frac{d}{2}+\alpha+2\epsilon
, we can see

\|\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|R_{h}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{1+\frac{d}{2}+\epsilon}}\leq C\|R_{h}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-1-\epsilon}},\qquad\alpha=0,

and

\|\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|R_{h}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{d}{2}+\epsilon}}\leq C\|R_{h}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-3-\epsilon}},\qquad\alpha=1,

together with
\|R_{h}^{4}\Lambda^{-6\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{q}}+\|R_{h}^{3}\Lambda^{-4\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{q}}\leq C\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|R_{h}^{3}\Lambda^{-3\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|-\int_{\Omega}(v_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h})\theta(R_{h}^{4}\Lambda^{-4\alpha}\theta-\frac{1}{2}R_{h}^{4}\Lambda^{-6\alpha}v_{d})\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}}\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-1-\alpha-\epsilon}}(\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}+\|R_{h}^{3}\Lambda^{-3\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}).

On the other hand, it holds

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta(R_{h}^{4}\Lambda^{-4\alpha}\theta-\frac{1}{2}R_{h}^{4}\Lambda^{-6\alpha}v_{d})\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{h}(v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta)\cdot\nabla_{h}(-\Delta)^{-1}(R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-4\alpha}\theta-\frac{1}{2}R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-6\alpha}v_{d})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}(\nabla_{h}v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta)\cdot\nabla_{h}(-\Delta)^{-1}(R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-4\alpha}\theta-\frac{1}{2}R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-6\alpha}v_{d})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}(v_{d}\partial_{d}\nabla_{h}\theta)\cdot\nabla_{h}(-\Delta)^{-1}(R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-4\alpha}\theta-\frac{1}{2}R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-6\alpha}v_{d})\,\mathrm{d}x.

The second integral on the right-hand side is bounded by

\displaystyle\left|-\int_{\Omega}(v_{d}\partial_{d}\nabla_{h}\theta)\cdot\nabla_{h}(-\Delta)^{-1}(R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-4\alpha}\theta-\frac{1}{2}R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-6\alpha}v_{d})\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{d}\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}(\|R_{h}^{3}\Lambda^{-3\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}+\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}).

We note

\displaystyle\left|-\int_{\Omega}(\nabla_{h}v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta)\cdot\nabla_{h}(-\Delta)^{-1}(R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-4\alpha}\theta-\frac{1}{2}R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-6\alpha}v_{d})\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\Omega}(\nabla_{h}\Delta(-\Delta)^{-1}v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta)\cdot\nabla_{h}(-\Delta)^{-1}(R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-4\alpha}\theta-\frac{1}{2}R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-6\alpha}v_{d})\,\mathrm{d}x\right|.

When
\alpha=0
, with the integration by parts, it holds

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}(\nabla_{h}\Delta(-\Delta)^{-1}v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta)\cdot\nabla_{h}(-\Delta)^{-1}(R_{h}^{2}\theta-\frac{1}{2}R_{h}^{2}v_{d})\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|R_{h}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}(\|\partial_{d}\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{d}\theta\|_{L^{\infty}})(\|R_{h}^{3}\theta\|_{L^{2}}+\|R_{h}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}})

\displaystyle\leq C\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}(\|R_{h}^{3}\Lambda^{-3\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

For
\alpha=1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}(\nabla_{h}\Delta(-\Delta)^{-1}v_{d}\partial_{d}\theta)\cdot\nabla_{h}(-\Delta)^{-1}(R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-4}\theta-\frac{1}{2}R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-6}v_{d})\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-1}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}(\|\Delta\partial_{d}\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla\partial_{d}\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{d}\theta\|_{L^{\infty}})(\|R_{h}^{3}\Lambda^{-3}\theta\|_{L^{2}}+\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-1}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}})

\displaystyle\leq C\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}(\|R_{h}^{3}\Lambda^{-3\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|-\int_{\Omega}(v\cdot\nabla)\theta(R_{h}^{4}\Lambda^{-4\alpha}\theta-\frac{1}{2}R_{h}^{4}\Lambda^{-6\alpha}v_{d})\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}}\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-1-\alpha-\epsilon}}(\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}+\|R_{h}^{3}\Lambda^{-3\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}})

\displaystyle+C\|v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{d}\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}(\|R_{h}^{3}\Lambda^{-3\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}+\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}})+C\|\theta\|_{H^{m}}(\|R_{h}^{3}\Lambda^{-3\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

With Young’s inequality and (
6.1
) we can have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}v_{d}R_{h}^{4}\Lambda^{-6\alpha}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\leq-(\frac{1}{4}-C\|\theta\|_{H^{m}})(\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|R_{h}^{3}\Lambda^{-3\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle+C\|v\|_{L^{2}}(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{m-1-\alpha-\epsilon}}+\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-1-\alpha-\epsilon}})(\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}+\|R_{h}^{3}\Lambda^{-3\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}})

\displaystyle+C\|v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{d}\nabla_{h}\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}(\|R_{h}^{3}\Lambda^{-3\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}+\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}})

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{8}(\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|R_{h}^{3}\Lambda^{-3\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+C\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{m-1-\alpha-\epsilon}}^{2}+\|R_{h}\Lambda^{-\alpha}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-1-\alpha-\epsilon}}^{2})

\displaystyle+C\|v_{d}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|R_{h}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-\alpha}}^{2}.

Let
M\geq 1
which will be specified later. Since

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\|R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-2\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|R_{h}^{3}\Lambda^{-3\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{8}\left(\|R_{h}v_{d}\|_{\dot{H}^{m-1}}^{2}+\|R_{h}^{3}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-3}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+C(\|R_{h}v\|_{\dot{H}^{m-1}}^{2}+\|R_{h}^{2}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-2}}^{2}+\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{m-2}}^{2})(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{m}}^{2}+\|R_{h}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-1}}^{2}).

We have used Young’s inequality and (
6.1
) in the last inequality. We consider

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\|R_{h}^{2}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-2}}^{2}-\|R_{h}^{3}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-3}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{M^{2}}\|R_{h}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-1}}^{2}

and

\left|\int_{\Omega}v_{d}(-\Delta)^{m-3}R_{h}^{4}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\|R_{h}v_{d}\|_{\dot{H}^{m-3}}\|R_{h}^{3}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-3}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|R_{h}v_{d}\|_{\dot{H}^{m-1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|R_{h}^{3}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-3}}^{2},

(6.27)

it holds

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8}\left(\|R_{h}v_{d}\|_{\dot{H}^{m-1}}^{2}+\|R_{h}^{3}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-3}}^{2}\right)\leq-\frac{1}{8M}\left(\|R_{h}v_{d}\|_{\dot{H}^{m-1}}^{2}+\|R_{h}^{2}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-2}}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{16M}\int_{\Omega}v_{d}(-\Delta)^{m-3}R_{h}^{4}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{8M^{2}}\|R_{h}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-1}}^{2}-\frac{1}{16M}\int_{\Omega}v_{d}(-\Delta)^{m-3}R_{h}^{4}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{16M}\left(\|R_{h}v_{d}\|_{\dot{H}^{m-2}}^{2}+\|R_{h}^{2}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-2}}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}v_{d}(-\Delta)^{m-3}R_{h}^{4}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right)+\frac{1}{8M^{2}}\|R_{h}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-1}}^{2}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{16M}\left(\|R_{h}v_{d}\|_{\dot{H}^{m-2}}^{2}+\|R_{h}^{2}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-2}}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}v_{d}(-\Delta)^{m-3}R_{h}^{4}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x\right)+\frac{1}{8M^{2}}\|R_{h}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle+C(\|R_{h}v\|_{\dot{H}^{m-1}}^{2}+\|R_{h}^{2}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-2}}^{2}+\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{m-2}}^{2})(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{m}}^{2}+\|R_{h}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-1}}^{2}).

\displaystyle+C(1+\frac{t}{16})^{2}(\|R_{h}v\|_{\dot{H}^{m-1}}^{2}+\|R_{h}^{2}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-2}}^{2}+\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{m-2}}^{2})(\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{m}}^{2}+\|R_{h}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-1}}^{2}).

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left((1+\frac{t}{16})^{2}(\|R_{h}v_{d}\|_{\dot{H}^{m-2}}^{2}+\|R_{h}^{2}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{m-2}}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}v_{d}(-\Delta)^{m-3}R_{h}^{4}\theta\,\mathrm{d}x)\right)

Now, we prove that

\|v_{d}\|_{\dot{H}^{m}}\leq C(1+t)^{-1}.

\|v_{d}(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{m}}\leq C\|R_{h}^{2}\Lambda^{-2}\theta(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{m}}+C\left(\sum_{\eta\in J}|\eta|^{2(m-2)}|\langle\mathscr{F}\mathbf{u},\mathbf{a}_{+}\rangle|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

\langle\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbb{u}(t),\mathbb{a}_{+}\rangle=e^{-\lambda_{+}t}\langle\mathscr{F}\mathbb{u}_{0},\mathbb{a}_{+}\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda_{+}(t-\tau)}\langle N(v,\theta)(\tau),\mathbb{a}_{+}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

6.28

7
Sharpness of decay rates

In this section, we prove that the decay rates in Theorem
1.1
and
1.4
are sharp in the following sense. We recall the linearized system of (
3.4
):

\partial_{t}\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbb{u}+M\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbb{u}=0,\qquad M:=\begin{pmatrix}|\eta|^{2\alpha}&-\frac{|\tilde{n}|^{2}}{|\eta|^{2}}\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},

(7.1)

where
\mathbf{u}=(v_{d},\theta)^{T}
. The eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the linear operator is previously given by

\lambda_{\pm}(\eta)=\frac{|\eta|^{2\alpha}\pm\sqrt{|\eta|^{4\alpha}-{4|\tilde{n}|^{2}}/{|\eta|^{2}}}}{2},\qquad\overline{\mathbb{a}_{\pm}(\eta)}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{\pm}\\
-\frac{|\tilde{n}|^{2}}{|\eta|^{2}}\end{pmatrix},

and it holds

\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbf{u}=\sum_{j=\pm}\langle\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbb{u}(t),\mathbb{a}_{j}\rangle\mathbb{b}_{j}=\sum_{j=\pm}e^{-\lambda_{j}t}\langle\mathscr{F}\mathbb{u}_{0},\mathbb{a}_{j}\rangle\mathbb{b}_{j},

(7.2)

where

\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{b}_{+}\\
\mathbb{b}_{-}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{+}-\lambda_{-}}\begin{pmatrix}1&\frac{|\eta|^{2}}{|\tilde{n}|^{2}}\lambda_{-}\\
-1&-\frac{|\eta|^{2}}{|\tilde{n}|^{2}}\lambda_{+}\end{pmatrix}.

Note that If we consider
\mathbf{u}_{0}
such that
\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbf{u}_{0}=0
for
\eta\not\in D_{3}
, then
|\mathbf{a}_{\pm}||\mathbf{b}_{\pm}|\leq C
for some
C>0
not depending on
\eta
. Now, we are ready to provide the sharpness of the decay rates.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}

Let
\epsilon>0

\mathbf{u}_{0}:=(0,\sum_{\eta\in J}\mathscr{F}_{b}\theta_{0}(\eta)\mathscr{B}_{\eta}(x))^{T},

where
\mathscr{F}_{b}\theta_{0}(\eta):=|q|^{-(m+\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon)}

A:=D_{3}\cap\{|n|=1\}

7.3

7.2

\|\bar{\theta}\|_{H^{s}}\geq\|e^{-\lambda_{-}t}\langle\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbb{u}_{0},\mathbb{a}_{-}\rangle\langle\mathbb{b}_{-},e_{2}\rangle\|_{H^{s}}-\|e^{-\lambda_{+}t}\langle\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbb{u}_{0},\mathbb{a}_{+}\rangle\langle\mathbb{b}_{+},e_{2}\rangle\|_{H^{s}}.

\|v_{d}\|_{H^{s}}\geq\|e^{-\lambda_{-}t}\langle\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbb{u}_{0},\mathbb{a}_{-}\rangle\langle\mathbb{b}_{-},e_{1}\rangle\|_{H^{s}}-Ce^{-\frac{t}{2}}.

Note that

|e^{-\lambda_{-}t}\langle\mathscr{F}_{b}\mathbb{u}_{0},\mathbb{a}_{-}\rangle\langle\mathbb{b}_{-},e_{1}\rangle|=\frac{|\tilde{n}|^{2}}{|\eta|^{2(1+\alpha)}}|e^{-\lambda_{-}t}\mathscr{F}_{b}\theta|\geq\frac{|\tilde{n}|^{2}}{|\eta|^{2(1+\alpha)}}e^{-\frac{|\tilde{n}|^{2}}{|\eta|^{2(1+\alpha)}}\frac{t}{2}}|q|^{-(m+\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon)}.

Using that
\frac{|\tilde{n}|^{2}}{|\eta|^{2(1+\alpha)}}\geq\frac{C}{|q|^{2(1+\alpha)}}
for all
\eta\in A
, repeating the above procedures, we obtain (
7.4
). This completes the proof.

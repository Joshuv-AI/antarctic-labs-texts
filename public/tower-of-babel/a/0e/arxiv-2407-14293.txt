The multiplicative structure of the K-theoretical McKay correspondence for the Hilbert scheme of points in the complex plane
Jakub Koncki, Magdalena Zielenkiewicz

Abstract
. We consider the K-theory of the Hilbert scheme of points in the complex plane, which under McKay correspondence is isomorphic to the space of symmetric functions Λ n \Lambda^{n} . We prove a formula conjectured by Boissière for the endomorphism of Λ n \Lambda^{n} induced by multiplication by the classes of the Adams powers of the tautological bundle. We describe the structure constants for the multiplication on Λ n \Lambda^{n} induced by the tensor product in K-theory.



1.
Introduction

Hilbert schemes, introduced in the 60’s, quickly became standard objects in algebraic geometry. In this paper, we focus on the Hilbert scheme of
n
points in the complex plane
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}:=\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}})
. It is a smooth quasiprojective variety parametrizing zero-dimensional subschemes of
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
of length
n
. The McKay correspondence forms a bridge between the geometry of
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
and the combinatorics of symmetric functions of degree
n
.

The permutation group
\operatorname{S}_{n}
of
n
elements acts on the vector space
(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{n}
by permuting the
\mathbb{C}^{2}
factors. This action preserves the standard symplectic form on
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
. The quotient space is a symplectic singularity in the sense of
[
Kal09
,
Bea00
]
and the Hilbert scheme
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
is its symplectic resolution. The McKay correspondence predicts a relation between the geometry of
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
and the
\operatorname{S}_{n}
-equivariant geometry of
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
.
Various instances of the McKay correspondence are known. It was studied as an isomorphism of cohomology or K-theory groups e.g.
[
Kal02
,
IN00
]
, as an equality of elliptic classes
[
BL05
,
Wae08
]
or as an equivalence of derived categories
[
BKR01
]
. The K-theoretical correspondence was used by Haiman to prove several combinatorial conjectures
[
Hai01
,
Hai02
]
. The study of the McKay correspondence (often for different groups and resolutions of singularities) is an active area of research, e.g.
[
Ohm08
,
Kru18
,
MW21
,
DBW18
]
.

We focus on the K-theoretical correspondence for the Hilbert scheme
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}})
. It provides us with an isomorphism

\Theta:\Lambda^{n}\simeq\KTh_{\operatorname{S}_{n}}(\mathbb{C}^{2n})\to\KTh(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}(\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}))\,,

where
\Lambda^{n}
is the space of symmetric functions of degree
n
. The codomain of this isomorphism has a product structure given by the tensor product. It induces interesting operations on the space
\Lambda^{n}
. We are interested in two natural questions:

(1)

For a given element
F\in\KTh(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})
, find a compact formula for the map
\mathcal{E}_{F}:\Lambda^{n}\to\Lambda^{n}
such that

\Theta(\mathcal{E}_{F}(x))=\Theta(x)\otimes F\,,

i.e. the diagram below commutes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Lambda^{n}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\KTh(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Lambda^{n}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\KTh(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Theta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-\otimes F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}_{F}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Theta}

(2)

Describe the product structure on
\Lambda^{n}
induced from
\KTh(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})
.

The Hilbert scheme
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
comes with a distinguished vector bundle
B_{n}
called the tautological vector bundle. Kirwan surjectivity type results imply that the cohomology
\operatorname{H}^{*}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})
is generated as a ring by the graded parts of the Chern character
\operatorname{ch}^{k}(B_{n})

[
ES93
]
and the K-theory is generated by the Adams powers
\psi^{k}B_{n}

[
MN18
]
.
Therefore,
it is enough to solve problem
1
for
F=\psi^{k}B_{n}
. Characteristic classes of the tautological bundles and their relation with the McKay correspondence can also be considered for varieties other than the affine plane, e.g.
[
BNW07
,
Sca09
,
Kru14
]
.

A cohomological version of problem
1
was solved in
[
Leh99
]
. It was used in
[
LS01
]
to describe the multiplicative structure on
\Lambda^{n}
induced from
\operatorname{H}^{*}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})
.
In K-theory, the formula for the map
\mathcal{E}_{B_{n}}
was given in
[
Boi06b
]

\mathcal{E}_{B_{n}}=\operatorname{Coeff}\left(t^{0};\sum_{r\geq 1}rp_{r}t^{r}\cdot\exp\left(\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{r}}t^{-r}\right)\right)\,.

Moreover, the formula for
F=\psi^{k}{B_{n}}
was conjectured.

Theorem

(
[
Boi06b
, Conjecture 8.1]
)
.

\mathcal{E}_{\psi^{k}{B_{n}}}=k^{-1}\cdot\operatorname{Coeff}\left(t^{0};\sum_{r\geq 1}rp_{r}t^{r}\cdot\exp\left(k\cdot\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{r}}t^{-r}\right)\right)\,.

Our main result is Theorem
5.14
, where we prove an equivalent version of the above formula. Both the results of
[
Boi06b
]
and our proof rely on the study of the torus action.

The standard action of the two dimensional complex torus
\mathbb{T}=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
on the affine plane induces an action on the Hilbert scheme
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
. This action was first used to compute the Betti numbers of the Hilbert scheme in
[
ES87
]
and its use is a standard method since then, e.g.
[
CE15
,
Smi20
,
KZ23
,
SV13
]
.
The fixed point set
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}^{\mathbb{T}}
is finite, which allows for a combinatorial approach to the equivariant cohomology and K-theory using localization theorems
[
Tho92
,
AB84
]
. Roughly speaking, any class in K-theory is determined by local contributions coming from the fixed point set. These contributions are governed by the combinatorics of the tangent weights. Therefore, many cohomological statements can be reduced to combinatorial problems. Unfortunately, they are usually complicated. This is the case for the computation of the map
\mathcal{E}_{F}
.

Our idea is to simplify the problem using a reduction to a well-chosen subtorus
\mathbb{T}_{1}\subset\mathbb{T}
. For a general
\mathbb{T}_{1}
, the fixed point
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}^{\mathbb{T}_{1}}
is finite, yet the description of the tangent weights for
\mathbb{T}_{1}
is no easier than for
\mathbb{T}
. On the other hand, the combinatorics of the tangent weights visibly simplifies when the fixed point set
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}^{\mathbb{T}_{1}}
is bigger than
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}^{\mathbb{T}}
. Yet, in such case one needs to consider the geometry of the fixed point set which may be complicated, see
[
Eva04
]
.
We choose
{\mathbb{T}_{q}}\subset\mathbb{T}
to be a coordinate subtorus. The fixed point set
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}^{{\mathbb{T}_{q}}}
is much bigger than
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}^{\mathbb{T}}
, therefore the tangent weights become much easier. Moreover,
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}^{{\mathbb{T}_{q}}}
is a disjoint union of affine spaces, thus it has trivial geometry (from the cohomological point of view). As expected, after restriction to the subtorus
{\mathbb{T}_{q}}
, problem
1
simplifies.

Our proof is purely combinatorial but it is based on the geometric intuition presented above. Let
q
and
t
be the coordinate characters of
\mathbb{T}
. The restriction to
{\mathbb{T}_{q}}
corresponds to the substitution
t=1
in the combinatorial formulas. After this restriction
the adjoint operator
\mathcal{E}^{*}_{\psi_{k}B}

satisfies the Leibniz rule, which allows for an effective calculation.

In the last section, we study problem
2
. We consider the multiplication
\odot:\Lambda^{n}\times\Lambda^{n}\to\Lambda^{n}
induced by the tensor product in the K-theory ring
\KTh(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})
. This product is determined by its structure constants
c^{\mu}_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}\in\mathbb{Q}
in the power sum basis, i.e.

p_{\lambda_{1}}\odot p_{\lambda_{2}}=\sum_{\mu\vdash n}c^{\mu}_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}p_{\mu}\,.

In Theorem
7.3
, we obtain a formula for the constants
c^{\mu}_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}
as the coefficients of a certain power series. Our method of proof is again the study of the action of the one-dimensional torus
{\mathbb{T}_{q}}
.

The structure of this paper is as follows. Sections
2
–
5
provide the necessary background. In Section
5
we state equivalent formulations of
[
Boi06b
, Conjecture 8.1]
in Theorem
5.14
and Proposition
5.15
. Section
6
contains the proof of the main theorem, Theorem
5.14
. In Section
7
we study the product
\odot
on
\Lambda^{n}
. In Appendix
A
we include a proof of the equivariant K-theoretic McKay correspondence. It is a well-known result, but we were unable to find a reference.

2.
K-theory

The standard reference for equivariant K-theory is
[
CG10
]
. Let
\mathbb{T}
be an algebraic torus and
X
a
\mathbb{T}
-variety. The equivariant K-theory
\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X)
is the
\KTh
-group of the additive category of
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant vector bundles on
X
. If
X
is smooth, it is isomorphic to the
\KTh
-group of the category of equivariant coherent sheaves on
X
. All our varieties are quasiprojective. Moreover, we consider only K-theory with rational coefficients, i.e.

\KTh(X):=\KTh(X,\mathbb{Q})=\KTh(X,\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{Q}\,,\qquad\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X):=\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X,\mathbb{Q})=\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X,\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{Q}\,.

For an algebraic torus
\mathbb{T}
, let
S_{\mathbb{T}}\subset\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{pt})
be the multiplicative system of all nonzero elements. The localised K-theory of a point is
S^{-1}_{\mathbb{T}}\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{pt})
. For any
\mathbb{T}
-variety
X
, the ring
\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X)
is a
\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{pt})
-algebra. We denote the localised K-theory of
X
by

S^{-1}_{\mathbb{T}}\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X):=\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X)\otimes_{\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{pt})}S^{-1}_{\mathbb{T}}\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{pt})\,.

Analogously, for a finite group
G
and a
\mathbb{T}\times G
-variety
X
we have

S^{-1}_{\mathbb{T}}\KTh_{\mathbb{T}\times G}(X):=\KTh_{\mathbb{T}\times G}(X)\otimes_{\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{pt})}S^{-1}_{\mathbb{T}}\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{pt})\,.

Theorem 2.1

(Localisation Theorem
[
Tho92
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{T}
be an algebraic torus and
X
a
\mathbb{T}
-variety. The inclusion
i:X^{\mathbb{T}}\hookrightarrow X
induces an isomorphism of rings

i^{*}:S_{\mathbb{T}}^{-1}\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X)\simeq S_{\mathbb{T}}^{-1}\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X^{\mathbb{T}})\,.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\mathbb{T}
be an algebraic torus and
X
be a
\mathbb{T}
-variety.

(1)

Suppose that the fixed point set
X^{\mathbb{T}}
is finite. Then the fundamental classes of the fixed points form a basis of the localised K-theory
S_{\mathbb{T}}^{-1}\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X)
over
S_{\mathbb{T}}^{-1}\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{pt})
.

(2)

More generally, suppose that the fixed point set is a disjoint union of affine spaces. Then the fundamental classes of the components of the fixed point set form a basis of the localised K-theory
S_{\mathbb{T}}^{-1}\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X)
over
S_{\mathbb{T}}^{-1}\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{pt})
.

For a
\mathbb{T}
-variety
X
, the K-theory
\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X)
is a
\lambda
-ring with
\lambda
-operations induced by taking exterior powers, see
[
FL85
]
for
\lambda
-rings. The theory of
\lambda
-rings provides us with Adams operations

\psi^{m}:\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X)\to\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X)\,,

for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
. They were first introduced in the non-equivariant setting in
[
Ada62
]
.
Let us recall some basic properties of the Adams operations.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\mathbb{T}
be an algebraic torus and
X
be a
\mathbb{T}
-variety.

(1)

The Adams operation
\psi^{m}:\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X)\to\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(X)
is a ring homomorphism.

(2)

For an equivariant line bundle
\mathcal{L}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{\mathbb{T}}(X)
we have

\psi^{m}(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{L}^{\otimes m}\,.

(3)

The Adams operations are natural transformations with respect to pullbacks, i.e. for any
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant map
f:Y\to X
we have

f^{*}\circ\psi^{m}=\psi^{m}\circ f^{*}\,.

3.
Combinatorics

3.1.
Partitions

A partition is a non-increasing sequence of positive integers, i.e.

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})\,,\qquad\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{k}>0\,.

We say that
s(\lambda):=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}
is the sum of the partition
\lambda
or that
\lambda
is a partition of
s(\lambda)
, and write
\lambda\vdash s(\lambda)
. We call
l(\lambda):=k
the length of the partition
\lambda
. A partition can be presented in an alternative way
\lambda=(1^{a_{1}(\lambda)},2^{a_{2}(\lambda)},\dots)
where

a_{j}(\lambda):=|\{i\in\mathbb{N}|\lambda_{i}=j\}|\,.

In further considerations we use the numbers

(1)

z_{\lambda}=\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(a_{i}(\lambda)!\cdot i^{a_{i}(\lambda)}\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}(\lambda)!\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{l(\lambda)}\lambda_{i}\,.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition of
n
and
\mu
a partition of
m
.
The partition
\lambda\cdot\mu
of
m+n
is defined by

a_{i}(\lambda\cdot\mu)=a_{i}(\lambda)+a_{i}(\mu)\,.

Example 3.2
.

For
\lambda=(5,2,1)
and
\mu=(3,2,1)
we obtain

\lambda\cdot\mu=(5,3,2,2,1,1)
.

3.2.
Symmetric functions

We recall some properties of symmetric functions. For a full exposition and proofs see
[
Mac15
]
.
Let
\operatorname{S}_{n}
denote the permutation group of
n
elements. Let
\Lambda^{n}
be the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space of symmetric functions of degree
n
and
\Lambda
be the ring of symmetric functions, i.e.

\Lambda^{n}:=\lim\limits_{\leftarrow}\{W\in\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]^{\operatorname{S}_{m}}|W\text{ is homogeneous, }\deg W=n\}\,,\qquad\Lambda=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Lambda^{n}\,.

Denote by
\Lambda_{q}^{n},\Lambda_{q,t}^{n},\Lambda_{q},\Lambda_{q,t}
the spaces of symmetric functions with additional parameters

\Lambda_{q}^{n}=\Lambda^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}(q)\,,\qquad\Lambda_{q,t}^{n}=\Lambda^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}(q,t)\,,\qquad\Lambda_{q}=\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}(q)\,,\qquad\Lambda_{q,t}=\Lambda\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}(q,t)\,.

The space
\Lambda^{n}
has several standard bases indexed by partitions of
n
, such as: the power sum functions
p_{\lambda}
, the full symmetric functions
h_{\lambda}
and the Schur functions
s_{\lambda}
.

Definition 3.3
.

•

The involution
\omega:\Lambda^{n}\to\Lambda^{n}
is defined by
\omega(p_{\lambda})=(-1)^{l(\lambda)}p_{\lambda}
. We extend it
\mathbb{Q}(q,t)-
linearly to an involution
\omega:\Lambda_{q,t}^{n}\to\Lambda_{q,t}^{n}
.

•

There is an inner product
\langle-,-\rangle
on the space
\Lambda^{n}
defined by one of the equivalent conditions:

\langle p_{\lambda},p_{\mu}\rangle=\delta_{\lambda,\mu}z_{\lambda}\,,\qquad\langle s_{\lambda},s_{\mu}\rangle=\delta_{\lambda,\mu}\,.

We extend it to a
\mathbb{Q}(q,t)
-bilinear form on
\Lambda^{n}_{q,t}
.

Remark 3.4
.

In the literature there are two conventions concerning the involution
\omega
. One either defines
\omega(p_{\lambda})=(-1)^{l(\lambda)}p_{\lambda}
(e.g.
[
Boi06b
]
), or
\omega(p_{\lambda})=(-1)^{n+l(\lambda)}p_{\lambda}
(e.g.
[
Mac15
,
Hai03
]
). As we prove a conjecture originating from
[
Boi06b
]
, we use the first convention to be consistent with the notation therein. This convention has a nice interpretation in terms of plethystic substitutions, see Example
3.9
(1).

The ring of symmetric functions is a polynomial ring generated by the power sums
\{p_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, i.e.

(2)

\Lambda=\mathbb{Q}[p_{1},p_{2},\dots]\,.

This allows us to define derivations
\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{n}}:\Lambda\to\Lambda
in the standard way.

Definition 3.5
.

For a partition
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{l})
, the map
\partial_{\mu}:\Lambda\to\Lambda
is defined by

\partial_{\mu}=\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\mu_{1}}}\circ\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\mu_{2}}}\circ\dots\circ\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{\mu_{l}}}\,.

This map may be extended
\mathbb{Q}(q,t)
-linearly to a map
\partial_{\mu}:\Lambda_{q,t}\to\Lambda_{q,t}
.

The following widely known fact can be easily checked using the power sum basis.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
f
and
g
be symmetric functions.

(1)

Let
m
be a natural number. Multiplication by
\frac{p_{m}}{m}
and differentiation
\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{m}}
are adjoint, i.e.

\left\langle\frac{f\cdot p_{m}}{m},g\right\rangle=\left\langle f,\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{m}}g\right\rangle\,.

(2)

Let
\lambda
be a partition. Multiplication by
\frac{p_{\lambda}}{\prod_{i=1}^{l(\lambda)}\lambda_{i}}
and differentiation
\partial_{\lambda}
are adjoint, i.e.

\left\langle\frac{f\cdot p_{\lambda}}{\prod_{i=1}^{l(\lambda)}\lambda_{i}},g\right\rangle=\left\langle f,\partial_{\lambda}g\right\rangle\,.

3.3.
Plethystic substitution

The plethystic substitution describes the
\lambda
-ring structure on the ring of symmetric functions, see e.g.
[
GH96
]
for a detailed description. Thanks to the presentation (
2
) of
\Lambda
, in order to define a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra map
\varphi:\Lambda^{n}\to\Lambda^{n}
it is enough to specify the values
\varphi(p_{n})
.

Definition 3.7
.

For an element
A\in\Lambda
, the plethystic substitution
f\to f[A]
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra morphism
-[A]:\Lambda\to\Lambda
such that

p_{k}[A]=A(x_{1}^{k},x_{2}^{k},\dots)\,.

For
A\in\Lambda_{q,t}
, the plethystic substitution is a
\mathbb{Q}(q,t)
-algebra morphism
-[A]:\Lambda_{q,t}\to\Lambda_{q,t}
such that
p_{k}[A]=A(x_{1}^{k},x_{2}^{k},\dots,q^{k},t^{k})\,.

In plethystic notation we write
X
for the sum of all variables:
X=x_{1}+x_{2}+\dots

Definition 3.8
.

Let
n
be a natural number and
\lambda
a partition. We write

[n]_{q}:=\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}\in\mathbb{Q}(q)\,,\qquad[{\lambda}]_{q}:=\prod_{i=1}^{l({\lambda})}[{\lambda}_{i}]_{q}\in\mathbb{Q}(q)\,.

Example 3.9
.

Let
f,g\in\Lambda_{q}
be symmetric functions.

(1)

We have
f[X]=f
and
f[-X]=\omega(f)
.

(2)

Let
A(q)\in\mathbb{Q}(q)
be a non-zero rational function. The substitution
f\to f[A(q)\cdot X]
is a
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-linear isomorphism of
\Lambda^{n}_{q}
with inverse
f\to f[\frac{X}{A(q)}]
.

(3)

Let
A(q)\in\mathbb{Q}(q)
be a rational function. The substitution
f\to f[A(q)\cdot X]
is self-adjoint, i.e.

\langle f,g[A(q)\cdot X]\rangle=\langle f[A(q)\cdot X],g\rangle

(4)

For any partition
\lambda
, we have

\langle p_{\lambda},f[X(q-1)]\rangle=\langle p_{\lambda}[X(q-1)],f\rangle=(q-1)^{l(\lambda)}[{\lambda}]_{q}\cdot\langle p_{\lambda},f\rangle\,.

3.4.
Macdonald Polynomials

Macdonald polynomials form an important basis of the space
\Lambda_{q,t}
, see
[
Mac15
, Chapter 6]
. To a partition
\lambda
of
n
we associate a symmetric function
\tilde{H}_{\lambda}(q,t)\in\Lambda_{q,t}^{n}
called the modified Macdonald polynomial, see

[
Hai01
, Proposition 2.1.1]

for a definition. The functions
\tilde{H}_{\lambda}
are in fact polynomials in
q,t
. It was proved in
[
Hai01
, Theorem 2]
that their coefficients in the Schur basis are polynomials with non-negative integer coefficients, i.e.

\tilde{H}_{\mu}(q,t)=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\tilde{K}_{\lambda\mu}(q,t)s_{\lambda}\in\Lambda_{q,t}^{n}\,,\qquad\tilde{K}_{\lambda\mu}(q,t)\in\mathbb{N}[q,t]\,.

Several restrictions of these polynomials are known, see e.g.
[
Hai03
, Section 3.5.3]
. For us the restriction to
t=1
will be important.

Proposition 3.10

(
[
Mac15
, p. 364, Example 7]
, see also

[
Hai03
, Proposition 3.5.8]
for this formulation)
.

We have

\tilde{H}_{\lambda}(q,1)=c_{\lambda}(q)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{l(\lambda)}h_{\lambda_{i}}\left[\frac{X}{1-q}\right]\,,

where
c_{\lambda}(q)=\prod_{i=1}^{l(\lambda)}\prod_{j=1}^{\lambda_{i}}(1-q^{j})\in\mathbb{Q}(q)
.

Definition 3.11
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition of
n
. We use the notation

v_{\lambda}:=h_{\lambda}\left[\frac{X}{1-q}\right]\in\Lambda_{q}^{n}\,.

Proposition 3.12
.

(1)

Let
\lambda
and
\mu
be partitions. We have

v_{\lambda\cdot\mu}=v_{\lambda}\cdot v_{\mu}\,.

(2)

The set
\{v_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\vdash n}
is a
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-basis of
\Lambda^{n}_{q}
.

Proof.

The proposition follows from analogous properties of the polynomials
h_{\lambda}
. We have

v_{\lambda\cdot\mu}=h_{\lambda\cdot\mu}\left[\frac{X}{1-q}\right]=(h_{\lambda}\cdot h_{\mu})\left[\frac{X}{1-q}\right]=h_{\lambda}\left[\frac{X}{1-q}\right]\cdot h_{\mu}\left[\frac{X}{1-q}\right]=v_{\lambda}\cdot v_{\mu}\,.

For the second point, note that the set
\{h_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\vdash n}
is a basis of
\Lambda^{n}_{q}
. The plethystic substitution
f\to f[\frac{X}{1-q}]
is a
\mathbb{Q}(q)
-linear automorphism of
\Lambda^{n}_{q}
, cf. Example
3.9
(2).
Therefore the set
\{v_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\vdash n}
is also a basis.
∎

3.5.
Frobenius characteristic map

The conjugacy classes of the elements of the permutation group
\operatorname{S}_{n}
are indexed by the partitions of
n
. Therefore, the representation ring
\KTh_{\operatorname{S}_{n}}(\operatorname{pt})
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space with a basis corresponding to partitions. The space
\Lambda^{n}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space of the same dimension. There is a standard choice of isomorphism between these spaces called the Frobenius characteristic map
\operatorname{Fr}\colon\KTh_{\operatorname{S}_{n}}(\operatorname{pt})\to\Lambda^{n}
, see e.g.
[
Sta99
, Section 7.18]
. It is defined by

\operatorname{Fr}([V])=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{S}_{n}}\chi_{V}(\sigma)p_{\lambda(\sigma)}\,,

where
\chi_{V}

4.
Hilbert Scheme

Let
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}:=\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2})
be the Hilbert scheme of
n
points in the complex plane
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. We recall some standard facts about
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
, for reference see
[
Nak99
]
.
The Hilbert scheme
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
parametrises zero-dimensional subschemes of
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
of length
n
. It is a smooth irreducible quasi-projective variety of dimension
2n
,
[
Fog68
]
. Set-theoretically,
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
can be identified with the set of ideals
I\lhd\mathbb{C}[x,y]
such that the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
\mathbb{C}[x,y]/I
has dimension
n
, i.e.,

\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}:=\{I\lhd\mathbb{C}[x,y]|\,\dim\mathbb{C}[x,y]/I=n\}\,.

Let
\mathbb{T}=(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{2}
be the algebraic torus. The standard
\mathbb{T}
–action on
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}

induces an action of
\mathbb{T}
on the Hilbert scheme
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
. The fixed points of this action are monomial ideals contained in
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
. They correspond bijectively to partitions of
n
. To a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{l})
we associate an ideal

\langle x^{\lambda_{1}}\,,x^{\lambda_{2}}y\,,\dots\,,x^{\lambda_{l}}y^{l-1}\,,y^{l}\rangle\in\operatorname{Hilb}_{{s(\lambda)}}^{\mathbb{T}}\,.

We do not distinguish in the notation between the partition
\lambda
and the associated fixed point.
Denote by
q
and
t
the coordinate characters of the torus
\mathbb{T}
. We consider the one-dimensional coordinate subtorus
{\mathbb{T}_{q}}\subset\mathbb{T}
acting on the first coordinate.
Then

\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{pt})=\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm 1},t^{\pm 1}]\,,\qquad\KTh_{\mathbb{T}_{q}}(\operatorname{pt})=\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm 1}]\,.

The restriction map
\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{pt})\to\KTh_{\mathbb{T}_{q}}(\operatorname{pt})
is given by substitution
t=1
.

The fixed point set
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}^{\mathbb{T}_{q}}
is a disjoint union of affine spaces,
[
Eva04
, Section 1.2]
. Corollary
2.2
gives the following description of the localised K-theory of
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

(1)

The fundamental classes of the
\mathbb{T}
-fixed points form a basis of the localised
\KTh
-theory
S^{-1}_{\mathbb{T}}\KTh_{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})
.

(2)

The fundamental classes of the components of the fixed point set
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}^{\mathbb{T}_{q}}
form a basis of the localised
\KTh
-theory
S^{-1}_{\mathbb{T}_{q}}\KTh_{\mathbb{T}_{q}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
F
be a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant vector bundle on the Hilbert scheme
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
and
\lambda
a partition of
n
. We write

F_{\lambda}\in\KTh^{\mathbb{T}}(\operatorname{pt})\simeq\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm 1},t^{\pm 1}]\,,

for the class of the restriction of
F
to the fixed point
\lambda
.
We write

F^{q}_{\lambda}\in\KTh^{{\mathbb{T}_{q}}}(\operatorname{pt})\simeq\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm 1}]

for the restriction to
{\mathbb{T}_{q}}
-equivariant K-theory, i.e.
F^{q}_{\lambda}=(F_{\lambda})_{t=1}
.

The Hilbert scheme
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
comes with a universal flat family

\mathcal{F}_{n}\subset\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}\times\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}\,,

whose fibre over a subscheme
Z\in\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
is
Z
, treated as a subscheme in
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
. The push-forward of the sheaf of regular functions on
\mathcal{F}_{n}
along the projection
\pi:\mathcal{F}_{n}\to\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
defines (a sheaf of sections of) a rank
n
vector bundle on
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
,

B_{n}=\pi_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}\in\operatorname{Vect}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})\,.

It is called the
tautological vector bundle
on
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
. We are interested in the Adams powers of the tautological bundle, due to the following instance of Kirwan surjectivity.

Theorem 4.3

(
[
MN18
, Corollary 1.4]
)
.

The classes of the bundles
\psi^{k}B_{n}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
generate
\KTh(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})
as a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra.

The tautological bundle
B_{n}
admits a natural
\mathbb{T}
-linearisation. Its fibre over a fixed point
I\in\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}^{\mathbb{T}}
is isomorphic to the
n
-dimensional vector space
\mathbb{C}[x,y]/I
as a
\mathbb{T}
-representation. This allows for an explicit computation of the restrictions
(\psi^{k}B_{n})_{\lambda}
.

Example 4.4
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{l})
be a partition of
n
. We have

(B_{n})_{\lambda}=\sum^{l}_{i=1}\sum_{j=1}^{\lambda_{i}}t^{i-1}q^{j-1}\,.

More generally, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

(\psi^{k}B_{n})_{\lambda}=\sum^{l}_{i=1}\sum_{j=1}^{\lambda_{i}}(t^{i-1}q^{j-1})^{k}\,,\qquad(\psi^{k}B_{n})^{q}_{\lambda}=\sum^{l}_{i=1}\sum_{j=1}^{\lambda_{i}}(q^{j-1})^{k}.

The last formula in the above example implies the following fact.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition of
n
,
\mu
be a partition of
m
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
be a natural number. We have

(\psi^{k}B_{n+m})^{q}_{\lambda\cdot\mu}=(\psi^{k}B_{n})^{q}_{\lambda}+(\psi^{k}B_{m})^{q}_{\mu}\,.

Remark 4.6
.

The above fact is false if we restrict to
q=1
instead of
t=1
. In this case, we have

(\psi^{k}B_{n+m})^{t}_{(\lambda^{\perp}\cdot\mu^{\perp})^{\perp}}=(\psi^{k}B_{n})^{t}_{\lambda}+(\psi^{k}B_{m})^{t}_{\mu}\,,

where
\perp
denotes the transpose partition.

.

Let
F
be a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant vector bundle on the Hilbert scheme
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}

The operators

Theorem 5.9

(
[
Boi06b
, Theorem 4.1 and its proof]
)
.

Let

be a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant vector bundle over
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
. We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{q,t}_{F}=\omega(\nabla^{q,t}_{F})^{*}\omega\,,

where the dual is taken with respect to the standard scalar product.

Remark 5.10
.

There is a clear intuition behind the above theorem. The fundamental classes of the fixed points basis
[\lambda]
form a basis of K-theory, cf. Proposition
4.1
(1). It is an eigenbasis of the map
-\otimes F
. Therefore

\Theta^{-1}([\lambda])
is an eigenbasis of the operator
\mathcal{E}_{F}^{q,t}
.
The essence of the theorem is a description of the element
\Theta^{-1}([\lambda])
in terms of Macdonald polynomials.

Theorem
5.9
and Proposition
5.6
imply that the map
\nabla_{F}^{q,t}
has well-defined restrictions to
\Lambda^{n}_{q}
and
\Lambda^{n}
.

Definition 5.11
.

Let
F
be a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant vector bundle over
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
. We define operators
\nabla_{F}^{q}:\Lambda^{n}_{q}\to\Lambda_{q}^{n}
and
\nabla_{F}:\Lambda^{n}\to\Lambda^{n}
such that

\nabla^{q}_{F}:=(\omega\mathcal{E}^{q}_{F}\omega)^{*}\,,\qquad\nabla_{F}:=(\omega\mathcal{E}_{F}\omega)^{*}\,.

Corollary 5.12
.

Let
F
be a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant vector bundle over
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
and
x\in\Lambda^{n}_{q,t}
.

(1)

Suppose that
x_{|t=1}\in\Lambda^{n}_{q}
is defined. Then
\nabla_{F}^{q,t}(x)_{|t=1}
is also defined. Moreover,

\nabla_{F}^{q}(x_{|t=1})=\nabla_{F}^{q,t}(x)_{|t=1}\,.

(2)

Suppose that
x_{|t=1,q=1}\in\Lambda^{n}
is defined. Then
\nabla_{F}^{q,t}(x)_{|t=1,q=1}
is also defined. Moreover,

\nabla_{F}(x_{|t=1,q=1})=\nabla_{F}^{q,t}(x)_{|t=1,q=1}\,.

Proof.

Theorem
5.9
and Definition
5.11
imply that

\nabla^{q,t}_{F}(x)=(\omega\mathcal{E}^{q,t}_{F}\omega)^{*}(x)=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\frac{\langle x,\omega\mathcal{E}^{q,t}_{F}\omega(p_{\lambda})\rangle}{z_{\lambda}}p_{\lambda}\,,\qquad\nabla^{q}_{F}(x_{|t=1})=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\frac{\langle x_{|t=1},\omega\mathcal{E}^{q}_{F}\omega(p_{\lambda})\rangle}{z_{\lambda}}p_{\lambda}\,.

The restriction
x_{t=1}
is well defined, thus for any partition
\lambda\vdash n
we have

\langle x,\omega\mathcal{E}^{q,t}_{F}\omega(p_{\lambda})\rangle_{|t=1}=\langle x_{|t=1},(\omega\mathcal{E}^{q,t}_{F}\omega(p_{\lambda}))_{|t=1}\rangle=\langle x_{|t=1},\omega\mathcal{E}^{q}_{F}\omega(p_{\lambda})\rangle\,.

We used Proposition
5.6
for
f=\omega(p_{\lambda})
. This proves the first point.

The proof of the second point is analogous.
∎

The
\KTh
-theory of the Hilbert scheme is generated as a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra by the Adams powers of the tautological bundle, cf. Theorem
4.3
. Therefore in order to describe the map
\mathcal{E}_{F}
for every
F
it is enough to describe it for
F=\psi^{k}B\,,k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 5.13
.

We write
\nabla_{k}^{q,t}
(or
\nabla_{k}^{q}
,
\nabla_{k}
) for
\nabla^{q,t}_{\psi^{k}B}
(or
\nabla_{\psi^{k}B}^{q}
,
\nabla_{\psi^{k}B}
respectively). In this definition we intentionally omit the number of points in the Hilbert scheme.

The operator
\nabla_{1}^{q,t}
can be expressed using plethystic substitution. The Macdonald polynomial
\tilde{H}_{\lambda}(q,t)
is an eigenvector of the operator
D:\Lambda^{n}_{q,t}\to\Lambda^{n}_{q,t}
defined by

(4)

\displaystyle D(f)=\operatorname{Coeff}\left(z^{0};f\left[X+\frac{z}{(1-q)(1-t)}\right]\cdot\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{-z^{k}p_{k}}{k}\right)\right)

with eigenvalue
1-(1-q)(1-t)B_{\lambda}
. Therefore,
\nabla_{1}^{q,t}=\frac{1-D}{(1-q)(1-t)}
.

5.3.
The main result

We are now ready to state the main theorem, which gives a closed formula for the multiplication induced by the tensoring with the
k
’th Adams power of the tautological bundle.

Theorem 5.14

(cf.
[

.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be a natural number. We have

\mathcal{E}_{\psi^{k}B}=\sum_{\lambda}\frac{k^{l(\lambda)-1}s(\lambda)}{\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}a_{i}(\lambda)!}p_{s(\lambda)}\partial_{\lambda}\,.

For
k=1
the above theorem is
[
Boi06b
, Theorem 5.1]
.
Using
\omega\mathcal{E}_{F}\omega=\nabla_{F}^{*}
(Definition
5.11
) and
\omega(p_{\lambda})=(-1)^{l(\lambda)}p_{\lambda}
(Definition
3.3
), the above theorem can be reformulated in terms of the operator
\nabla_{k}^{*}
, as follows.

Proposition 5.15
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be a natural number. We have

(5)

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}^{*}=\sum_{\lambda}\frac{(-k)^{l(\lambda)-1}s(\lambda)}{\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}a_{i}(\lambda)!}p_{s(\lambda)}\partial_{\lambda}\,.

The next section is devoted to the proof of the above result.

Remark 5.16
.

Our proof is purely combinatorial, yet it has a geometric motivation. It is based on the substitution
t=1
, which corresponds to the restriction to
{\mathbb{T}_{q}}
-equivariant
\KTh
-theory. The fixed point set
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}^{\mathbb{T}_{q}}
is still relatively simple. Proposition
4.1
(2) provides an eigenbasis of the operator
-\otimes F
acting on
S^{-1}_{\mathbb{T}_{q}}\KTh_{\mathbb{T}_{q}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})
. On the other hand, the fixed point set
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}^{\mathbb{T}_{q}}
is much bigger than
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}^{\mathbb{T}}
. This implies that the combinatorics of the tangent weights simplifies after the restriction to
{\mathbb{T}_{q}}
.
Therefore, the formulas for
\mathcal{E}^{q}_{F}
and
\nabla_{F}^{q}
should also simplify.

Remark 5.17
.

The main step of our proof is showing that the maps
\nabla_{k}
and
\nabla_{k}^{q}
satisfy the Leibniz rule in Proposition
6.2
. For the non-equivariant map
\nabla_{k}
this can be predicted from Formula (
5
). Multiplication by
\frac{p_{m}}{m}
and differentiation
\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{m}}
are adjoint. Therefore, we expect that every summand of
\nabla_{k}
contains a single differentiation.

Remark 5.18
.

The
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant map
\nabla_{k}^{q,t}
does not satisfies the Leibniz rule even for
p_{1}^{2}
.
This confirms that restriction to the subtorus
{\mathbb{T}_{q}}
simplifies the problem, not only the proofs.

Remark 5.19
.

In
[
Boi06b
]
, Theorem
5.14
for
k=1
is stated in the equivalent version

\mathcal{E}_{B}=\operatorname{Coeff}\left(t^{0};\sum_{r\geq 1}rp_{r}t^{r}\cdot\exp\left(\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{r}}t^{-r}\right)\right)\,.

It is possible to write the result for an arbitrary
k
in this form

\mathcal{E}_{\psi^{k}B}=k^{-1}\cdot\operatorname{Coeff}\left(t^{0};\sum_{r\geq 1}rp_{r}t^{r}\cdot\exp\left(k\cdot\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{r}}t^{-r}\right)\right)\,.

Remark 5.20
.

Contrary to the cohomological case, the formula for
\mathcal{E}_{\psi^{k}B}
does not follow directly from the formula for
\mathcal{E}_{B}
, as the K-theory is not graded. It is tempting to use the Chern character to transfer the results from cohomology to K-theory, yet the relation between the Chern character and the McKay correspondence is surprisingly complicated,
[
Boi06a
, Theorem 1.1]
.

6.
Restriction to the one-dimensional torus

In this section we study the restriction of the operators
\nabla^{q,t}_{F}
to
t=1
. It turns out that the restricted operator is much simpler than the original one. For
F=\psi^{k}B
it satisfies the Leibniz rule (Proposition
6.2
), which allows us to obtain a closed formula for the operators
\nabla_{k}
(Proposition
6.8
). We use Proposition
3.6
to compute the dual
\nabla^{*}_{k}
. Recall that we defined
v_{\lambda}=h_{\lambda}\left[\frac{X}{1-q}\right]
(Definition
3.11
).

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition. The element
v_{\lambda}
is an eigenvector of
\nabla^{q}_{F}
with eigenvalue
F^{q}_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

Let
c_{\lambda}(q)\in\mathbb{Q}(q)
be the constant from Proposition
3.10
. Then

\nabla^{q}_{F}(c_{\lambda}(q)v_{\lambda})=\nabla^{q}_{F}(\tilde{H}_{\lambda}(q,1))=\nabla^{q,t}_{F}(\tilde{H}_{\lambda}(q,t))_{|t=1}=(F_{\lambda}\cdot\tilde{H}_{\lambda}(q,t))_{|t=1}=F^{q}_{\lambda}\cdot c_{\lambda}(q)v_{\lambda}\,.

Thus,
c_{\lambda}(q)v_{\lambda}
is an eigenvector of
\nabla^{q}_{F}
with eigenvalue
F^{q}_{\lambda}
. It follows that
v_{\lambda}
is also an eigenvector with the same eigenvalue.
∎

The multiplicative behaviour of the basis
v_{\lambda}
implies that the operators
\nabla_{k}^{q}
satisfy the Leibniz rule.

Proposition 6.2
.

Fix a positive integer
k
. The operator
\nabla_{k}^{q}
satisfies the Leibniz rule with regard to the multiplication in
\Lambda_{q}
, i.e. for
f,g\in\Lambda_{q}
, we have

\nabla_{k}^{q}(fg)=\nabla_{k}^{q}(f)\cdot g+f\cdot\nabla_{k}^{q}(g)\,.

Proof.

Let
F=\psi^{k}B
. It is enough to check the equation on the basis elements
v_{\lambda}
. We have

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}^{q}(v_{\lambda}\cdot v_{\mu})=\nabla_{k}^{q}(v_{\lambda\cdot\mu})=F^{q}_{\lambda\cdot\mu}v_{\lambda\cdot\mu}=

\displaystyle(F^{q}_{\lambda}+F^{q}_{\mu})\cdot v_{\lambda,\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{q}_{\lambda}v_{\lambda}\cdot v_{\mu}+v_{\lambda}\cdot F^{q}_{\mu}v_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}^{q}(v_{\lambda})\cdot v_{\mu}+v_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla_{k}^{q}(v_{\mu})\,.

The first and the fourth equalities follow from Proposition
3.12
, the second and the fifth from Proposition
6.1
and the third from Corollary
4.5
.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{l})
be a partition and
k\in\mathbb{N}
a natural number.
Denote by
\widehat{\lambda}_{i}
the partition
\lambda
without the
i
-th part. We have

\nabla^{q}_{k}(p_{\lambda})=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\nabla^{q}_{k}(p_{\lambda_{i}})p_{\widehat{\lambda}_{i}}\,.

The above corollary implies that in order to provide a formula for the operator
\nabla^{q}_{k}
it is enough to compute
\nabla^{q}_{k}(p_{n})
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We start with the case
k=1
.

Proposition 6.4
.

We have

\nabla^{q}_{1}(p_{n})=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}(-1)^{l({\lambda})-1}\cdot\frac{1-q^{n}}{1-q}\cdot\frac{n}{z_{\lambda}}\cdot p_{\lambda}\,,\qquad\nabla_{1}(p_{n})=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}(-1)^{l({\lambda})-1}\cdot\frac{n^{2}}{z_{\lambda}}\cdot p_{\lambda}\,.

Proof.

Let
D
be the operator defined by Equation (
4
). By definition

\displaystyle D(p_{n})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Coeff}\left(z^{0};\left(p_{n}+\frac{(1-q^{n})(1-t^{n})}{z^{n}}\right)\cdot\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{-z^{k}p_{k}}{k}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=p_{n}\operatorname{Coeff}\left(z^{0};\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{-z^{k}p_{k}}{k}\right)\right)+(1-q^{n})(1-t^{n})\operatorname{Coeff}\left(z^{n};\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{-z^{k}p_{k}}{k}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=p_{n}+(1-q^{n})(1-t^{n})\cdot\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\frac{(-1)^{l(\lambda)}}{z_{\lambda}}p_{\lambda}

We have
\nabla_{1}^{q,t}=\frac{1-D}{(1-q)(1-t)}
, therefore

\nabla_{1}^{q,t}(p_{n})=\frac{(1-q^{n})(1-t^{n})}{(1-q)(1-t)}\cdot\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\frac{(-1)^{l({\lambda})-1}}{z_{\lambda}}p_{\lambda}\,.

To obtain the result, we compute the limit at
t\to 1
and then at
q\to 1
.
∎

It turns out that the coefficients of
\nabla^{q}_{1}(p_{n})
determine the operators
\nabla^{q}_{k}
for an arbitrary
k\in\mathbb{N}
, as the next two propositions show.

Proposition 6.5
.

There exist polynomials
C_{\lambda}(q)\in\mathbb{Q}[q]
such that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\nabla^{q}_{k}(p_{n})=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\frac{(1-q^{n})\cdot C_{\lambda}(q^{k})}{\prod_{i=1}^{l(\lambda)}(1-q^{\lambda_{i}})}p_{\lambda}\,.

Proof.

There exist rational numbers
a_{\mu},b_{\mu\lambda}
such that

p_{n}=\sum_{\mu\vdash n}a_{\mu}h_{\mu}\,,\qquad h_{\mu}=\sum_{\mu\vdash n}b_{\mu\lambda}p_{\lambda}\,.

Applying the plethystic substitution
f\to f[\frac{X}{1-q}]
, we get

\frac{p_{n}}{1-q^{n}}=\sum_{\mu\vdash n}a_{\mu}v_{\mu}\,.

It follows that

\nabla_{k}(p_{n})=(1-q^{n})\sum_{\mu\vdash n}a_{\mu}\nabla_{k}(v_{\mu})=(1-q^{n})\sum_{\mu\vdash n}a_{\mu}B^{q}_{\mu}(q^{k})v_{\mu}=(1-q^{n})\sum_{\mu\vdash n}\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\frac{a_{\mu}b_{\mu\lambda}B^{q}_{\mu}(q^{k})}{\prod_{i=1}^{l(\lambda)}(1-q^{\lambda_{i}})}p_{\lambda}\,.

Here we used
(\psi^{k}B)^{q}_{\mu}(q)=B^{q}_{\mu}(q^{k})
. To obtain the result we set

C_{\lambda}(q)=\sum_{\mu\vdash n}a_{\mu}b_{\mu\lambda}B^{q}_{\mu}(q)\,.\qed

Corollary 6.6
.

The polynomial
C_{\lambda}(q)
is given by the formula

C_{\lambda}(q)=(-1)^{l({\lambda})-1}\cdot\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{l(\lambda)}(1-q^{\lambda_{i}})}{(1-q)}\cdot\frac{n}{z_{\lambda}}\,.

Proof.

We compute the coefficient of
p_{\lambda}
in
\nabla^{q}_{1}(p_{n})
using Propositions
6.4
and
6.5
. Comparing the results we obtain

\frac{(1-q^{n})\cdot C_{\lambda}(q)}{\prod_{i=1}^{l(\lambda)}(1-q^{\lambda_{i}})}=(-1)^{l({\lambda})-1}\cdot\frac{1-q^{n}}{(1-q)}\cdot\frac{n}{z_{\lambda}}\,.

This implies the statement.
∎

Corollary 6.7
.

We have

\nabla^{q}_{k}(p_{n})=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}(-1)^{l({\lambda})-1}\cdot\frac{1-q^{n}}{1-q^{k}}\cdot\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l(\lambda)}\frac{1-q^{k\lambda_{i}}}{1-q^{\lambda_{i}}}\right)\cdot\frac{n}{z_{\lambda}}\cdot p_{\lambda}\,.

Moreover,

\nabla_{k}(p_{n})=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}(-1)^{l({\lambda})-1}\cdot\frac{n^{2}\cdot k^{l(\lambda)-1}}{z_{\lambda}}\cdot p_{\lambda}\,.

Proof.

The first part is a direct consequence of Corollary
6.6
and Proposition
6.5
. The second is obtained by computing the limit at
q\to 1
.
∎

Proposition 6.8
.

We have

(6)

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}=\sum_{\lambda}\frac{(-k)^{l(\lambda)-1}s(\lambda)^{2}}{z_{\lambda}}\cdot p_{\lambda}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{s(\lambda)}}\,.

Proof.

Both sides of Equation (
6
) are endomorphisms of
\Lambda
which satisfy the Leibniz rule. Therefore, it is enough to check this equality evaluated on the ring generators of
\Lambda
. Corollary
6.7
imply that both sides have the same value on the elements
p_{n}
.
∎

As a consequence we obtain a proof of Proposition
5.15
, which in turn is a reformulation of the main result, Theorem
5.14
, i.e. for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\nabla_{k}^{*}=\sum_{\lambda}\frac{(-k)^{l(\lambda)-1}s(\lambda)}{\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}a_{i}(\lambda)!}p_{s(\lambda)}\partial_{\lambda}\,.

Proof of Proposition
5.15
.

By Proposition
3.6
(2), for any partition
\lambda
we have

\left(\frac{s(\lambda)^{2}}{z_{\lambda}}\cdot p_{\lambda}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{s(\lambda)}}\right)^{*}=\frac{s(\lambda)^{2}}{z_{\lambda}}\cdot\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{l(\lambda)}\lambda_{i}}{s(\lambda)}\cdot p_{s(\lambda)}\partial_{\lambda}=\frac{s(\lambda)}{\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}a_{i}(\lambda)!}\cdot p_{s(\lambda)}\partial_{\lambda}\,,

here we treat
p_{\lambda}
as a multiplication map
\Lambda\to\Lambda
. Multiply the above expression by
(-k)^{l(\lambda)-1}
and sum over all partitions
\lambda
. Using Proposition
6.8
, the left hand side is equal to
\nabla_{k}^{*}
. The right hand side gives the desired formula.
∎

Remark 6.9
.

An analogous reasoning may be repeated for the operator
\nabla^{q}_{k}
. We obtain formulas

\displaystyle\nabla^{q}_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda}\frac{(-1)^{l(\lambda)-1}s(\lambda)}{z_{\lambda}}\cdot\frac{1-q^{s(\lambda)}}{1-q^{k}}\cdot\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l(\lambda)}\frac{1-q^{k\lambda_{i}}}{1-q^{\lambda_{i}}}\right)\cdot p_{\lambda}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{s(\lambda)}}\,,

\displaystyle(\nabla^{q}_{k})^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda}\frac{(-1)^{l(\lambda)-1}}{\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}a_{i}(\lambda)!}\cdot\frac{1-q^{s(\lambda)}}{1-q^{k}}\cdot\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l(\lambda)}\frac{1-q^{k\lambda_{i}}}{1-q^{\lambda_{i}}}\right)\cdot p_{s(\lambda)}\partial_{\lambda}\,.

7.
Multiplicative structure

The tensor product in K-theory of the
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}
induces a product on the space of symmetric functions. In this section we provide an explicit formula for this product in the power sum basis.

Definition 7.1
.

The multiplication
\odot:\Lambda^{n}\times\Lambda^{n}\to\Lambda^{n}
is defined as the map induced by the tensor product in
\KTh(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})
, i.e.

f\odot g=\Theta^{-1}(\Theta(f)\otimes\Theta(g))\,.

The multiplication
\odot_{q}:\Lambda_{q}^{n}\times\Lambda_{q}^{n}\to\Lambda_{q}^{n}
is defined as the map induced by the tensor product in
S_{\mathbb{T}_{q}}^{-1}\KTh^{\mathbb{T}_{q}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})
, i.e.

f\odot_{q}g=\Theta_{q}^{-1}(\Theta_{q}(f)\otimes\Theta_{q}(g))\,.

Definition 7.2
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
and
\mu
be partitions of
n
. The structure constants
c^{\mu}_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}\in\mathbb{Q}
and the equivariant structure constants
c^{\mu}_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}(q)\in\mathbb{Q}(q)
are defined by

p_{\lambda_{1}}\odot p_{\lambda_{2}}=\sum_{\mu\vdash n}c^{\mu}_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}p_{\mu}\,,\qquad p_{\lambda_{1}}\odot_{q}p_{\lambda_{2}}=\sum_{\mu\vdash n}c^{\mu}_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}(q)p_{\mu}\,.

The product
\odot
is fully determined by its structure constants. We give a formula for the constant
c^{\mu}_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}
as a coefficient of a certain power series. First we introduce some notation.
For a power series
W(q)\in\mathbb{Q}[[q-1]]
and a natural number
m
, we write

\operatorname{Coeff}_{q-1}(m;W(q))

for the coefficient of
W(q)
at
(q-1)^{m}
. We use the function

W_{\lambda}(q)=(q-1)^{n}\left\langle s_{n},h_{\lambda}\left[\frac{X}{q-1}\right]\right\rangle\,.

An easy computation (cf. Example
3.9
(4)) shows that

W_{\lambda}(q)=\sum_{\nu\vdash n}(q-1)^{n-l(\nu)}\frac{\left\langle p_{\nu},h_{\lambda}\right\rangle}{z_{\nu}}\cdot[{\nu}]_{q}^{-1}\,.

The inner product
\langle p_{(1)^{n}},h_{\lambda}\rangle
is non-zero for any
\lambda
, thus
W_{\lambda}(q)
has a non-zero restriction at
q=1
.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
and
\mu
be partitions of
n
. Then

c^{\mu}_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}=\operatorname{Coeff}_{q-1}\left({l(\lambda_{1})+l(\lambda_{2})-n-l(\mu);\frac{[{\mu}]_{q}}{z_{\mu}[{\lambda_{1}}]_{q}[{\lambda_{2}}]_{q}}\cdot\sum_{\nu\vdash n}\frac{\langle p_{\lambda_{1}},h_{\nu}\rangle\langle p_{\lambda_{2}},h_{\nu}\rangle\langle p_{\mu},m_{\nu}\rangle}{W_{\nu}(q)}}\right)

Corollary 7.4
.

Suppose that
l(\lambda_{1})+l(\lambda_{2})-n-l(\mu)
is negative, then
c^{\mu}_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}=0
.

Proof.

The power series in Theorem
7.3
has a well-defined restriction at
q=1
.
∎

Remark 7.5
.

The above corollary agrees with
[
Boi06a
, Theorem 1.1]
. The condition that
l(\lambda_{1})+l(\lambda_{2})-n-l(\mu)
is negative is equivalent to

(n-l(\lambda_{1}))+(n-l(\lambda_{2}))>n-l(\mu)\,.

By
[
Boi06a
, Theorem 1.1]
, for every partition
\lambda
the class
\operatorname{ch}(\Theta(p_{\lambda}))
has non-zero graded components in
\operatorname{H}^{2(n-l(\lambda))}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})
and is equal to zero in smaller gradations. Corollary
7.4
may be deduced from this theorem and the fact that the cup product in cohomology preserves gradation.

The rest of this section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
7.3
. First, we study the
{\mathbb{T}_{q}}
-equivariant case. The tensor product in
S^{-1}_{\mathbb{T}_{q}}\KTh_{{\mathbb{T}_{q}}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})
has an easy presentation in the fixed point basis, cf. Proposition
4.1
(2). For a partition
\lambda
, let
[X_{\lambda}]
be the class of the corresponding component of the fixed point set
\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}}^{\mathbb{T}_{q}}
. Then

(7)

\displaystyle[X_{\lambda}]\otimes[X_{\mu}]=\delta_{\lambda,\mu}a_{\lambda}[X_{\lambda}]\,,

for some rational functions
a_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{Q}(q)
. This observation allows us to describe the product
\odot_{q}
in Propositions
7.8
and
7.9
. Next, we study the connection between the products
\odot_{q}
and
\odot
in Proposition
7.10
. To obtain a formula for
\odot
we need to change the basis.

Lemma 7.6
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional vector space equipped with a non-degenerate bilinear form
\langle-,-\rangle
. Let
A
be an endomorphism of
V
. Suppose that
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}
is an eigenbasis for
A
with eigenvalues
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}
. Then
\alpha^{*}_{1},\dots,\alpha^{*}_{k}
is an eigenbasis for the adjoint map
A^{*}
with the same eigenvalues.

Proof.

It is enough to show that for any
i,j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
we have

\langle A^{*}\alpha^{*}_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle=\langle a_{i}\alpha^{*}_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle
. This is straightforward, as

\langle A^{*}\alpha^{*}_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle=\langle\alpha^{*}_{i},A\alpha_{j}\rangle=a_{j}\cdot\langle\alpha^{*}_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle=a_{j}\delta_{ij}=a_{i}\delta_{ij}=\langle a_{i}\alpha^{*}_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle\,.

∎

Lemma 7.7
.

The set
\{m_{\lambda}[X(q-1)]\}_{\lambda\vdash n}
is an eigenbasis of the map
\mathcal{E}^{q}_{B_{n}}
.

Proof.

The set
\{h_{\lambda}[\frac{X}{1-q}]\}_{\lambda\vdash n}
is an eigenbasis of
\nabla^{q}_{1}
by Proposition
6.1
. Therefore,

\left\{\omega\left(h_{\lambda}\left[\frac{X}{1-q}\right]\right)\right\}_{\lambda\vdash n}=\left\{h_{\lambda}\left[\frac{X}{q-1}\right]\right\}_{\lambda\vdash n}

is an eigenbasis of
\omega\nabla^{q}_{1}\omega
. We have that
(\mathcal{E}^{q}_{B})^{*}=\omega\nabla^{q}_{1}\omega
, so by Lemma
7.6
it is enough to prove that the bases
\{h_{\lambda}[\frac{X}{q-1}]\}_{\lambda\vdash n}
and
\{m_{\lambda}[X(q-1)]\}_{\lambda\vdash n}
are mutually dual. This is the case, as

\left\langle h_{\lambda}\left[\frac{X}{q-1}\right],m_{\mu}[X(q-1)]\right\rangle=\left\langle h_{\lambda},(m_{\mu}[X(q-1)])\left[\frac{X}{q-1}\right]\right\rangle=\left\langle h_{\lambda},m_{\mu}\right\rangle=\delta_{\lambda\mu}\,.

The first equality follows from Example
3.9
(3),
the second from Example
3.9
(2).
∎

Proposition 7.8
.

Let
\lambda
and
\mu
be partitions of
n
. We have

m_{\lambda}[X(q-1)]\odot_{q}m_{\mu}[X(q-1)]=\delta_{\lambda\mu}b_{\lambda}m_{\mu}[X(q-1)]

for some rational function
b_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{Q}(q)
.

Proof.

The fundamental classes
[X_{\lambda}]\in\KTh^{\mathbb{T}_{q}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})
form an eigenbasis of the map
-\otimes B_{n}
.
Therefore, the elements
\Theta^{-1}[X_{\lambda}]
form an eigenbasis of
\mathcal{E}^{q}_{B_{n}}
. The eigenvalues of
\mathcal{E}^{q}_{B_{n}}
are pairwise different, therefore Lemma
7.7
implies that

\Theta^{-1}[X_{\lambda}]=d_{\lambda}\cdot m_{\lambda}[X(q-1)]

for some nonzero
d_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{Q}(q)
. The proposition now follows from Equation (
7
).
∎

Proposition 7.9
.

For arbitrary symmetric functions
f,g\in\Lambda_{q}^{n}
, we have

f\odot_{q}g=\sum_{\mu\vdash n}p_{\mu}\cdot\frac{(q-1)^{n}}{z_{\mu}}\cdot\sum_{\nu\vdash n}\frac{\langle f,h_{\nu}[\frac{X}{q-1}]\rangle\langle g,h_{\nu}[\frac{X}{q-1}]\rangle\langle p_{\mu},m_{\nu}[X(q-1)]\rangle}{W_{\nu}(q)}\,.

Proof.

Proposition
7.8
implies that

(8)

\displaystyle f\odot_{q}g=\sum_{\nu\vdash n}\left\langle f,h_{\nu}\left[\frac{X}{q-1}\right]\right\rangle\left\langle g,h_{\nu}\left[\frac{X}{q-1}\right]\right\rangle b_{\nu}\cdot m_{\nu}[X(q-1)]\,

for some
b_{\nu}\in\mathbb{Q}(q)
.
It is a standard fact that
\Theta_{q}(s_{n})
is the trivial bundle.
Therefore
s_{n}
is the neutral element for
\odot_{q}
. For
g=s_{n}
and
f=m_{\lambda}[X(q-1)]
, Equation (
8
) gives

m_{\lambda}[X(q-1)]=\sum_{\nu\vdash n}\delta_{\lambda\nu}\left\langle s_{n},h_{\nu}\left[\frac{X}{q-1}\right]\right\rangle b_{\nu}\cdot m_{\nu}[X(q-1)]\,.

It follows that

b_{\lambda}=\left\langle s_{n};h_{\lambda}\left[\frac{X}{q-1}\right]\right\rangle^{-1}=\frac{(q-1)^{n}}{W_{\lambda}(q)}\,.

Moreover,
m_{\nu}[X(q-1)]=\sum_{\mu\vdash n}\langle p_{\mu},m_{\nu}[X(q-1)]\rangle\frac{p_{\mu}}{z_{\mu}}\,.
After substituting these two equations into (
8
) we obtain the desired formula.
∎

Proposition 7.10
.

Suppose that the symmetric functions
f,g\in\Lambda_{q}^{n}
have well-defined restrictions at
q=1
. Then
f\odot_{q}g
also has a well-defined restriction at
q=1
. Moreover,

(f\odot_{q}g)_{|q=1}=f_{|q=1}\odot g_{|q=1}\,.

Proof.

An analogous property is satisfied by the products in
S_{\mathbb{T}_{q}}^{-1}\KTh^{\mathbb{T}_{q}}(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})
and
\KTh(\operatorname{Hilb}_{{n}})
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
7.3
.

Proposition
7.9
and Example
3.9
(4)
imply that the formula for the equivariant structure constants is the following:

\displaystyle c^{\mu}_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}}(q)

\displaystyle=(q-1)^{n+l(\mu)-l(\lambda_{1})-l(\lambda_{2})}\frac{[{\mu}]_{q}}{z_{\mu}[{\lambda_{1}}]_{q}[{\lambda_{2}}]_{q}}\cdot\sum_{\nu\vdash n}\frac{\langle p_{\lambda_{1}},h_{\nu}\rangle\langle p_{\lambda_{2}},h_{\nu}\rangle\langle p_{\mu},m_{\nu}\rangle}{W_{\nu}(q)}.

By Proposition
7.9
the above expression has a well-defined restriction at
q=1
.
∎

Appendix A
Equivariant McKay correspondence

Definition A.1
.

Let
k
be a field and
V
be a
k
-vector space of finite dimension. The restriction

Lemma A.3
.

Let
k
be a field, let
V
and
W
be
k
-vector spaces of finite dimension, and let
\psi:V\to W
be a
k
-linear map. Let
\psi_{q}:V\otimes_{k}k(q)\to W\otimes_{k}k(q)
be a
k(q)
-linear map which restricts to
\psi
. Suppose that
\psi
is an isomorphism. Then
\psi_{q}
is also isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
be a basis of
V
. Then
v_{1}\otimes 1,\dots,v_{k}\otimes 1
is a
k(q)
-basis of
V\otimes k(q)
. It is enough to prove that the vectors
\psi_{q}(v_{1}\otimes 1),\dots,\psi_{q}(v_{k}\otimes 1)
are
k(q)
-linearly independent. Suppose otherwise that there exist rational functions
a_{k}(q)\in k(q)
such that

(9)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{k}(q)\psi_{q}(v_{i}\otimes 1)=0\,.

Without loss of generality we can assume that all
a_{k}(q)
are non-zero. Let
\operatorname{val}_{q-1}
be the valuation associated with
(q-1)
and

t=\min\{\operatorname{val}_{q-1}(a_{1}(q)),\dots,\operatorname{val}_{q-1}(a_{k}(q))\}\,.

Multiplying Equation (
9
) by
(q-1)^{-t}
and applying
\operatorname{res}_{q=1}
yields a non-trivial
k
-linear relation between elements
\operatorname{res}_{q=1}(\psi_{q}(v_{i}\otimes 1))
. The map
\psi_{q}
restricts to
\psi
, therefore

\operatorname{res}_{q=1}(\psi_{q}(v_{i}\otimes 1))=\psi(v_{i})\,.

This contradicts that
\psi
is an isomorphism.
∎

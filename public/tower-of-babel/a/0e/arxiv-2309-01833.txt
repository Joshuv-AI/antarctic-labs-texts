The eigenvalue spectrum of a large real antisymmetric random matrix with non-zero mean
Andrei Katsevich a,b , Pavel Meshcheriakov a,b

Abstract
We study the eigenvalue spectrum of a large real antisymmetric random matrix J i ​ j J_{ij} . Using a fermionic approach and replica trick, we obtain a semicircular spectrum of eigenvalues when the mean value of each matrix element is zero, and in the case of a non-zero mean, we show that there is a set of critical finite mean values above which eigenvalues arise that are split off from the semicircular continuum of eigenvalues. The result converged with numerical simulations.



I
Introduction

For even
N
consider a purely imaginary antisymmetric
N\times N
matrix
iJ_{ij}=-iJ_{ji}.
Its eigenvalues are decomposed naturally into pairs
\pm\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
with
i=1,2,\dots,N/2
and
\lambda_{i}>0
. We define the density of eigenvalues as

\nu(\lambda)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N/2}\delta(\lambda-\lambda_{i})+\delta(\lambda+\lambda_{i})\,.

(1)

We would like to find
\nu(\lambda)
in the large
N
limit for the random antisymmetric matrix
J_{ij}
whose elements are taken from Gaussian ensemble with fixed mean
J_{0}/N
and variance
\sigma^{2}=J^{2}/N
(
J_{0}
and
J
are of the order of unity), so the probability density function reads

p(J_{ij})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{(J_{ij}-J_{0}/N)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\,.

(2)

The eigenvalue probability density function for some ensembles of Gaussian random matrices with zero mean
J_{0}=0
is a Wigner semicircle. This result was obtained in classical works on various matrix ensembles. See, for example, the book
Mehta 2004
. Several examples include Gaussian orthogonal (GOE), unitary (GUE), and symplectic (GSE) ensembles.

For these ensembles, it was showed that the eigenvalue probability density function of the matrix ensemble approaches a Wigner semicircle as the size of the matrices increases to infinity. This is a fundamental result in random matrix theory, demonstrating the universality of the Wigner semicircle.

\rho_{0}(\lambda)\equiv\braket{\nu(\lambda)}_{J_{ij}:J_{0}=0}=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{\pi J}\sqrt{1-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4J^{2}}}\,,&\quad&|\lambda|<2J\,;\\
&0\,,&\quad&|\lambda|>2J\,.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(3)

In the case of antisymmetric matrices Wigner semicircle was obtained in
Mehta and Rosenzweig 1968
. Our goal is to generalize this result to arbitrary
J_{0}\neq 0
.

A similar problem in the case of real symmetric random matrices
\bm{M}
with mean
\frac{M_{0}}{N}
was discussed in
Edwards and Jones 1976
;
Jones et al. 1978
. Using replica trick, it was shown that if the mean value of random matrix elements is greater than a critical one the distribution of the eigenvalues consists of the Wigner semicircle plus a separate single state:

\rho_{M_{0}}(\lambda)\equiv\braket{\nu(\lambda)}_{M_{ij}:M_{0}\neq 0}=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\rho_{0}(\lambda)+\frac{1}{N}\delta\left(\lambda-M_{0}-\frac{J^{2}}{M_{0}}\right)\,,&\quad&|M_{0}|>J\,;\\
&\rho_{0}(\lambda)\,,&\quad&|M_{0}|<J\,.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(4)

An algorithm for applying the replica trick:

1.

Rewrite delta-functions in
\nu(\lambda)
as derivatives of logarithmic functions using Sokhotski–Plemelj formula.

2.

Rewrite logarithmic function in
\nu(\lambda)
using limit:

\log x=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow 0}\frac{x^{n}-1}{n}\,.

(5)

3.

Swap the limit
n\rightarrow 0
with the limit
N\rightarrow\infty
. Treat
n
as an integer during calculating the Gaussian integrals.

4.

Take a limit
n\rightarrow 0
after evaluating the integrals.

Physically, the replica trick introduces
n
replicated copies of the original disordered system that are coupled together. It uses the non-commutativity of limits
n\rightarrow 0
and
N\rightarrow\infty
. Taking the limit
n\rightarrow 0
at the end serves to analytically continue the results for integer
n
to non-integer values. The coupling between the replicated systems enables averaging over the disorder in the original system.

This problem also arises when one considers the
\text{SYK}_{2}
model with
\frac{N(N-1)}{2}
couplings
Klebanov and Tarnopolsky
:

H_{SYK}=i\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\psi_{i}\psi_{j},

(6)

where
\psi_{i},i=1,...,N
are Majorana fermions with anticommutation rules
\{\psi_{i},\psi_{j}\}=\delta_{ij}
and the random couplings
J_{ij}=-J_{ji}
are drawn from Gaussian distribution with non-zero mean
J_{0}/N
and variance
\sigma^{2}=J^{2}/N
:

\braket{J_{ij}}=\frac{J_{0}}{N},\quad\braket{(J_{ij}-J_{0}/N)^{2}}=\frac{J^{2}}{N}\,.

(7)

As in the case of a random matrix,
\text{SYK}_{2}
is likely to have similar properties.

II
The technique

In this section we give a general scheme of calculations. The presented formulas can be applied to an arbitrary matrix
\bm{H}
, so here we do not restrict ourselves to the case of antisymmetric ones. We can write
\nu(\lambda)
as

\nu(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\pi N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\textrm{Im}\frac{1}{\lambda-i\epsilon-\lambda_{i}}\,,

(8)

where we used Sokhotski–Plemelj formula

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{x-i\epsilon}=i\pi\delta(x)+\mathcal{P}\Big(\frac{1}{x}\Big)\,.

(9)

On the other hand we have

\textrm{det}(\bm{I}\lambda-\bm{H})=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(\lambda-\lambda_{i})\,.

(10)

Therefore, we obtain

\nu(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\pi N}\textrm{Im}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\ln\textrm{det}(\bm{I}(\lambda-i\epsilon)-\bm{H})

(11)

and using replicas

\nu(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\pi N}\text{Im}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\lim_{n\to 0}\frac{1}{n}\{\det(\bm{I}\lambda_{\epsilon}-\bm{H})^{n}-1\}\,,

(12)

where
\lambda_{\epsilon}\equiv\lambda-i\epsilon
. The determinant of a matrix may be represented as an integral over the Grassmann numbers (fermionic approach):

\textrm{det}(\bm{I}\lambda_{\epsilon}-\bm{H})=\int\prod_{i=1}^{N}d\bar{c}_{i}dc_{i}\exp\left(-\sum\limits_{i,j}\bar{c}_{i}(I_{ij}\lambda_{\epsilon}-H_{ij})c_{j}\right)\,,

(13)

where
\{\bar{c}_{i},c_{j}\}=\{\bar{c}_{i},\bar{c}_{j}\}=\{c_{i},c_{j}\}=0
. Thus, we obtain the basic working formula:

\nu(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\pi N}\textrm{Im}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\lim_{n\to 0}\frac{1}{n}\left\{\int\prod_{i;\alpha}d\bar{c}^{\alpha}_{i}dc^{\alpha}_{i}\exp\left(-\sum\limits_{i,j;\alpha}\bar{c}^{\alpha}_{i}(I_{ij}\lambda_{\epsilon}-H_{ij})c^{\alpha}_{j}\right)-1\right\}\,.

(14)

III
Spectrum of the matrix with zero mean

In this section we elaborate on the case of antisymmetric matrices with zero mean. This is important because the case of non-zero mean will refer to the case of zero mean. Now we consider an antisymmetric matrix in which each element has zero mean with the probability density function:

p(J_{ij})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{J_{ij}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\,.

(15)

The average density of eigenvalues:

\rho_{0}(\lambda)=\braket{\nu(\lambda)}_{J_{ij}:J_{0}=0}=\int\nu\left(\lambda;\{J_{ij}:J_{0}=0\}\right)\prod\limits_{i<j}\;p(J_{ij})\;dJ_{ij}\,.

(16)

Using (
14
) and (
16
), we obtain

\rho_{0}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\pi N}\textrm{Im}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{n}\Biggl\{\int\left(\prod\limits_{i;\alpha}d\bar{c}_{i}^{\alpha}dc_{i}^{\alpha}\right)\exp\left(-\lambda_{\epsilon}\sum\limits_{i;\alpha}\bar{c}_{i}^{\alpha}c_{i}^{\alpha}\right)\times\\
\times\left(\prod\limits_{i<j}\frac{dJ_{ij}}{2\pi\sigma^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{J_{ij}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\right)\exp\left(i\sum\limits_{i,j;\alpha}\bar{c}_{i}^{\alpha}J_{ij}c^{\alpha}_{j}\right)-1\Biggl\}\,.

(17)

Let it be
P_{ij}=\sum\limits_{\alpha}\bar{c}^{\alpha}_{i}c^{\alpha}_{j}
is antihermitian tensor (
P_{ij}=-\bar{P}_{ji}
) and
P^{A}_{ij}=\sum\limits_{\alpha}\frac{\bar{c}^{\alpha}_{i}c^{\alpha}_{j}-\bar{c}^{\alpha}_{j}c^{\alpha}_{i}}{2}
is its antisymmetric part. Extract and carry out the Gaussian integrals over
J_{ij}
:

I_{1}=\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^{2})^{\frac{N(N-1)}{2}}}\int\left(\prod\limits_{i<j}dJ_{ij}\right)\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i,j}iP_{ij}J_{ij}-\sum\limits_{i<j}\frac{J_{ij}^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)=\exp\left(-\sigma^{2}\sum\limits_{i,j}(P^{A}_{ij})^{2}\right)\,.

(18)

Substituting (
18
) into (
17
), we obtain

\rho_{0}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\pi N}\textrm{Im}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{n}\Biggl\{\int\left(\prod\limits_{i;\alpha}d\bar{c}_{i}^{\alpha}dc_{i}^{\alpha}\right)\exp\left(-\lambda_{\epsilon}\sum\limits_{i}P_{ii}-\sigma^{2}\sum\limits_{i,j}(P^{A}_{ij})^{2}\right)-1\Biggl\}\,.

(19)

Let us define the antihermitian tensor
T^{\alpha\beta}=\sum\limits_{i}\bar{c}_{i}^{\alpha}c_{i}^{\beta}
(
T^{\alpha\beta}=-\bar{T}^{\beta\alpha}
) with symmetric and antisymmetric parts
T_{S}^{\alpha\beta}=\sum\limits_{i}\frac{\bar{c}_{i}^{\alpha}c_{i}^{\beta}+\bar{c}_{i}^{\beta}c_{i}^{\alpha}}{2}
,
T_{A}^{\alpha\beta}=\sum\limits_{i}\frac{\bar{c}_{i}^{\alpha}c_{i}^{\beta}-\bar{c}_{i}^{\beta}c_{i}^{\alpha}}{2}
and antisymmetric tensor
K^{\alpha\beta}=\sum\limits_{i}c_{i}^{\alpha}c_{i}^{\beta}
(
K^{\alpha\beta}=-K^{\beta\alpha}
). We rewrite the tensor expressions associated with
\bm{P},\bm{P}^{A}
in terms of
\bm{T},\bm{K}
in (
19
). Thus, we obtain

\rho_{0}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\pi N}\textrm{Im}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{n}\Biggl\{\int\left(\prod\limits_{i;\alpha}d\bar{c}_{i}^{\alpha}dc_{i}^{\alpha}\right)\exp\left(-\lambda_{\epsilon}\sum\limits_{\alpha}T^{\alpha\alpha}-\frac{J^{2}}{2N}\sum\limits_{\alpha,\beta}\left(T^{\alpha\beta}T^{\beta\alpha}+\bar{K}^{\alpha\beta}K^{\beta\alpha}\right)\right)-1\Biggl\}\,.

(20)

For convenience, we introduce

I_{2}=\int\left(\prod\limits_{i;\alpha}d\bar{c}_{i}^{\alpha}dc_{i}^{\alpha}\right)\exp\left(-\lambda_{\epsilon}\sum\limits_{\alpha}T^{\alpha\alpha}-\frac{J^{2}}{2N}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\left(T^{\alpha\beta}T^{\beta\alpha}+\bar{K}^{\alpha\beta}K^{\beta\alpha}\right)\right)\,.

(21)

The next step is to apply the Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation to the tensors
\bm{T},\bm{K}
, which allows us to replace quadratic expressions with tensors with linear ones by introducing additional integration variables. For
\bm{T}
we have

\exp\left(-\frac{J^{2}}{2N}\sum\limits_{\alpha,\beta}T^{\alpha\beta}T^{\beta\alpha}\right)=\exp\left(-\frac{J^{2}}{2N}\text{tr}\;\bm{T}^{2}\right)=\exp\left(-\frac{J^{2}}{2N}\text{tr}\;\bm{T}_{S}^{2}-\frac{J^{2}}{2N}\text{tr}\;\bm{T}_{A}^{2}\right)=\\
=2^{-\frac{n}{2}}\left(\frac{N\lambda^{2}}{\pi J^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n^{2}}{2}}\int[d\bm{R}_{S}][d\bm{R}_{A}]\exp\left(-\frac{N\lambda^{2}}{2J^{2}}\text{\;tr\;}\bm{R}_{S}^{2}+\frac{N\lambda^{2}}{2J^{2}}\text{\;tr\;}\bm{R}_{A}^{2}-i\lambda\text{\;tr\;}\bm{T}_{S}\bm{R}_{S}+\lambda\text{\;tr\;}\bm{T}_{A}\bm{R}_{A}\right)\,,

(22)

where
\bm{R}_{S}
is a real symmetric
\bm{R}_{S}^{T}=\bm{R}_{S}
and
\bm{R}_{A}
is a real antisymmetric
\bm{R}_{A}^{T}=-\bm{R}_{A}
matrices.

[d\bm{R}_{S}]=\prod\limits_{\alpha\leq\beta}dR_{S}^{\alpha\beta},\quad[d\bm{R}_{A}]=\prod\limits_{\alpha<\beta}dR_{A}^{\alpha\beta}\,.

(23)

For the tensor
\bm{K}
we have:

\exp\left(-\frac{J^{2}}{2N}\sum\limits_{\alpha,\beta}\bar{K}^{\alpha\beta}K^{\beta\alpha}\right)=\exp\left(-\frac{J^{2}}{2N}\text{tr\;}\bar{\bm{K}}\bm{K}\right)=\\
=2^{n(n-1)}\left(\frac{N\lambda^{2}}{\pi J^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\int[d\bm{M}]\exp\left(\frac{2N\lambda^{2}}{J^{2}}\text{\;tr\;}\bar{\bm{M}}\bm{M}+\lambda\text{\;tr\;}\bm{K}\bm{M}+\lambda\text{\;tr\;}\bar{\bm{K}}\bar{\bm{M}}\right)\,,

(24)

where
\bm{M}
is an antisymmetric complex matrix and
[d\bm{M}]=\prod\limits_{i<j}d^{2}M_{ij}
. Substitute (
22
) and (
24
) in (
21
) and obtain

I_{2}=2^{n(n-\frac{3}{2})}\left(\frac{N\lambda^{2}}{\pi J^{2}}\right)^{n(n-\frac{1}{2})}\int\left(\prod\limits_{i;\alpha}d\bar{c}_{i}^{\alpha}dc_{i}^{\alpha}\right)[d\bm{R}_{S}][d\bm{R}_{A}][d\bm{M}]\exp\left(-\lambda_{\epsilon}\text{tr}\;\bm{T}-\frac{N\lambda^{2}}{2J^{2}}\text{tr\;}\bm{R}_{S}^{2}+\frac{N\lambda^{2}}{2J^{2}}\text{tr\;}\bm{R}_{A}^{2}-\right.\\
\left.-i\lambda\text{\;tr\;}\bm{T}_{S}\bm{R}_{S}+\lambda\text{\;tr\;}\bm{T}_{A}\bm{R}_{A}+\frac{2N\lambda^{2}}{J^{2}}\text{tr}\;\bar{\bm{M}}\bm{M}+\lambda\text{\;tr\;}\bm{K}\bm{M}+\lambda\text{\;tr\;}\bar{\bm{K}}\bar{\bm{M}}\right)\,.

(25)

Let us introduce the vector of
2n
parameters
\bm{\theta}_{i}=\{c^{1}_{i},...,c^{n}_{i},\bar{c}^{1}_{i},...,\bar{c}^{n}_{i}\}
with the measure
d\bm{\theta}_{i}=\prod\limits_{\alpha}d\bar{c}_{i}^{\alpha}dc_{i}^{\alpha}
and we represent the linear tensor part in a more compact form

-\lambda_{\epsilon}\text{tr}\;\bm{T}-i\lambda\text{\;tr\;}\bm{T}_{S}\bm{R}_{S}+\lambda\text{\;tr\;}\bm{T}_{A}\bm{R}_{A}+\lambda\;\text{tr}\;\bm{K}\bm{M}+\lambda\text{\;tr\;}\bar{\bm{K}}\bar{\bm{M}}=-\sum_{i}\frac{1}{2}\bm{\theta}^{T}_{i}\left(\lambda_{\epsilon}\bm{\Omega}+i\lambda\bm{\sigma}_{1}\bm{L}\right)\bm{\theta}_{i}\,,

(26)

where
\bm{R}=\frac{\bm{R}_{S}+i\bm{R}_{A}}{2}
is the Hermitian matrix,
\bm{\sigma}_{1}
is the Pauli matrix and also

\bm{\Omega}=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{0}&-\bm{I}_{n}\\
\ \bm{I}_{n}&\bm{0}\end{pmatrix},\quad\bm{L}=2\begin{pmatrix}\bm{R}^{T}&-i\bar{\bm{M}}\\
-i\bm{M}&-\bm{R}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(27)

It should be noted that the matrix
\bm{L}
has the following properties

\bm{L}=\bm{L}^{\dagger},\quad\bm{\sigma}_{1}\bm{L}\bm{\sigma}_{1}=-\bm{L}^{T}\,.

(28)

In terms of the Cartan classification of Hamiltonians, we work with class
D
with
P^{2}=1
symmetry (see appendix
B
). This class was considered, for example, in
Ivanov 2002
using a supersymmetric approach.

The next step is to perform integration over the Grassmannian variables in (
25
). To do this, we use the formula for the Berezin integral:

\int d\bm{\theta}_{i}\exp{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\bm{\theta}^{T}_{i}\left(\lambda_{\epsilon}\bm{\Omega}+i\lambda\bm{\sigma}_{1}\bm{L}\right)\bm{\theta}_{i}\right)}=\text{Pf}(\lambda_{\epsilon}\bm{\Omega}+i\lambda\bm{\sigma}_{1}\bm{L})\,.

(29)

We obtain

I_{2}=c_{n}\int[d\bm{R}][d\bm{M}]\exp\left(-\frac{2N\lambda^{2}}{J^{2}}\text{tr}\left(\bm{R}^{2}-\bar{\bm{M}}\bm{M}\right)+\frac{N}{2}\log\det\left(\lambda_{\epsilon}\bm{\Omega}+i\lambda\bm{\sigma}_{1}\bm{L}\right)\right),

(30)

where
c_{n}=2^{n(n-\frac{3}{2})}\left(\frac{N\lambda^{2}}{\pi J^{2}}\right)^{n(n-\frac{1}{2})}
. We keep in mind that

\text{tr}\;\bm{L}^{2}=8\text{tr}\;(\bm{R}^{2}-\bar{\bm{M}}\bm{M}),\quad\bm{\Omega}^{2}=-\bm{I},\quad\bm{\Omega}\bm{\sigma}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}-\bm{I}_{n}&0\\
0&\bm{I}_{n}\end{pmatrix}=-\bm{\sigma}_{3}\,.

(31)

Therefore, we can rewrite (
30
) through one matrix
\bm{L}
over which the integration is performed

I_{2}=c_{n}\int[d\bm{L}]\exp\left(-N\text{tr}\left(\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4J^{2}}\bm{L}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\lambda_{\epsilon}\bm{I}+i\lambda\bm{\sigma}_{3}\bm{L}\right)\right)\right)\,.

(32)

By varying the action, the equation of the saddle-point manifold is

\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2J^{2}}\bm{L}_{0}-\frac{1}{2}i\lambda\bm{\sigma}_{3}(\lambda_{\epsilon}\bm{I}+i\lambda\bm{\sigma}_{3}\bm{L}_{0})^{-1}=0\,,

(33)

and it is solved by

\bm{L}_{0}=\text{diag}(x_{1},...,x_{n},x_{n+1},...,x_{2n})\,,

(34)

where
x_{i}
are saddle points

\begin{cases}x^{\pm}_{i}=\frac{i\lambda_{\epsilon}\pm\sqrt{4J^{2}-\lambda_{\epsilon}^{2}}}{2\lambda},\qquad i=\{1,...,n\}\,;\\
x^{\pm}_{j}=-\frac{i\lambda_{\epsilon}\pm\sqrt{4J^{2}-\lambda_{\epsilon}^{2}}}{2\lambda},\quad j=\{n+1,...,2n\}\,.\end{cases}

(35)

Let us introduce
g_{\pm}(x)
as

g_{\pm}(x)=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4J^{2}}x^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\lambda_{\epsilon}\pm ix\lambda\right)\,.

(36)

By deforming the integration contour onto the saddle-point manifold, the integral (
32
) reduces to

I_{2}\simeq c_{n}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{x_{i}\in\{x_{i}^{+},x_{i}^{-}\}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx_{i}e^{-Ng_{+}(x_{i})}\right)\prod\limits_{j=n+1}^{2n}\left(\sum_{x_{j}\in\{x_{j}^{+},x_{j}^{-}\}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx_{j}e^{-Ng_{-}(x_{j})}\right)\,.

(37)

Considering
g_{+}(x)=g_{-}(-x)\equiv g(x)
, we get

I_{2}\simeq c_{n}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{x_{i}\in\{x_{i}^{+},x_{i}^{-}\}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx_{i}e^{-Ng_{+}(x_{i})}\right)^{2}\,.

(38)

Without loss of generality, let it be
\lambda>0
. We know that
\rho_{0}(\lambda)=\rho_{0}(-\lambda)
due to the matrix antisymmetry.

x^{\pm}
are the saddle points of

. We choose only
x^{-}
. Red lines means branch cut.

We investigate 2 cases:

•

\lambda<2J
.

Value of
g(x)
in
x_{i}^{\pm}
:

g(x^{\pm}_{i})=\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\lambda_{\epsilon}^{2}}{8J^{2}}\left(-1\pm i\sqrt{\frac{4J^{2}}{\lambda_{\epsilon}^{2}}-1}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{\lambda_{\epsilon}}{2}\left(1\pm i\sqrt{\frac{4J^{2}}{\lambda_{\epsilon}^{2}}-1}\right)\right)\,.

(39)

In the limit
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
:

g^{\prime\prime}(x^{\pm}_{i})=\frac{\lambda^{4}}{4J^{4}}\left(\frac{4J^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}-1\pm i\sqrt{\frac{4J^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}-1}\right)\,,

(40)

|g^{\prime\prime}(x^{\pm}_{i})|=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2J^{3}}\sqrt{4J^{2}-\lambda^{2}},\quad\arg g^{\prime\prime}(x^{\pm}_{i})=\pm\arctan\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{4J^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}-1}}\right)\,.

(41)

For all
i
there are two variants:
x_{i}=x^{-}_{i}
or
x_{i}=x^{+}_{i}
. Therefore, we have a
n
-dimensional complex saddle-point manifold. We choose all
x_{i}=x^{-}_{i}\equiv x^{-}
to
\rho_{0}(\lambda)>0
because contributions of all other variants are suppressed (see fig.
1
). Similar reasoning was made in
Kamenev and Mézard 1999
for GUE, which consists of Hermitian matrices.

Since
N\gg 1
, we use the complex steepest descent method:

\rho_{0}(\lambda)\simeq\frac{1}{\pi N}\text{Im}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\log\left(\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{N|g^{\prime\prime}(x^{-})|}}e^{-Ng(x^{-})-i\frac{\arg g^{\prime\prime}(x^{-})}{2}}\right)^{2}\,.

(42)

As the matrix size
N
grows to infinity,
|g^{\prime\prime}(x^{-}_{i})|
and
\arg g^{\prime\prime}(x^{-}_{i})
remain approximately constant, scaling as
\mathcal{O}(1)
for each
i
. We derive

\rho_{0}(\lambda)\simeq\frac{1}{\pi}\text{Im}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}(-2g(x^{-}))=\frac{1}{\pi J}\sqrt{1-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4J^{2}}}\,.

(43)

•

\lambda>2J
.

Both saddle points are purely imaginaries:

x^{\pm}_{i}=\frac{i}{2}\left(1\pm\sqrt{1-\frac{4J^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}}\right)\,.

(44)

We have
g(x^{\pm}_{i})\in\mathbb{R}
, consequently
\rho_{0}(\lambda)=0
.

Combining the results of both cases and remembering that
\lambda
can be negative, we get

\rho_{0}(\lambda)=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{\pi J}\sqrt{1-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4J^{2}}},&\quad&|\lambda|<2J\,;\\
&0,&\quad&|\lambda|>2J\,.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(45)

This is the Wigner semicircle (see fig.
2
). We reproduce this well known result for the
D
class using a replica approach with fermions.

IV
Spectrum of the antisymmetric matrix with non-zero mean

In this section we consider antisymmetric matrices with elements having non-zero mean
\frac{J_{0}}{N}
with the probability density function:

p(J_{ij})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}e^{-\frac{(J_{ij}-J_{0}/N)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}\,.

(46)

The average density of eigenvalues:

\rho_{J_{0}}(\lambda)=\braket{\nu(\lambda)}_{J_{ij}:J_{0}\neq 0}=\int\nu\left(\lambda;\{J_{ij}:J_{0}\neq 0\}\right)\prod\limits_{i<j}\;p(J_{ij})\;dJ_{ij}\,.

(47)

Similarly to (
17
) we have

\rho_{J_{0}}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\pi N}\textrm{Im}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{n}\Biggl\{\int\left(\prod\limits_{i;\alpha}d\bar{c}_{i}^{\alpha}dc_{i}^{\alpha}\right)\exp\left(-\sum\limits_{i,j;\alpha}\bar{c}_{i}^{\alpha}(\lambda_{\epsilon}I_{ij}-iJ_{ij})c^{\alpha}_{j}\right)\times\\
\times\left(\prod\limits_{i<j}\frac{dJ_{ij}}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{(J_{ij}-\frac{J_{0}}{N})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right)\right)-1\Biggl\}\,.

(48)

Now we are reducing the problem with a non-zero mean to a zero-mean case. For this purpose, add and subtract term
A_{ij}\frac{J_{0}}{N}
to
J_{ij}
:

J_{ij}=\underbrace{J_{ij}-A_{ij}\frac{J_{0}}{N}}_{\text{zero-mean matrix}}+A_{ij}\frac{J_{0}}{N}\,,

(49)

where
A_{ij}
is a constant antisymmetric matrix (see appendix
A
). Using the result of (
20
), we get

\rho_{J_{0}}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\pi N}\textrm{Im}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\lim_{n\to 0}\frac{1}{n}\Biggl\{\int\left(\prod\limits_{i;\alpha}d\bar{c}_{i}^{\alpha}dc_{i}^{\alpha}\right)\exp\bigg(-\sum\limits_{i,j;\alpha}\bar{c}^{\alpha}_{i}\left(\bm{I}\lambda_{\epsilon}-i\bm{A}\frac{J_{0}}{N}\right)_{ij}c^{\alpha}_{j}-\\
-\frac{J^{2}}{2N}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\left(T^{\alpha\beta}T^{\beta\alpha}+\bar{K}^{\alpha\beta}K^{\beta\alpha}\right)\bigg)-1\Biggl\}\,.

(50)

As in the case of the zero mean for the integral in (
50
), we perform the Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation and obtain an analogous formula to (
25
):

\rho_{J_{0}}(\lambda)=\rho_{0}(\lambda)+\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{k}\delta(\lambda-\mu_{k}),\quad k\in\left\{j:f(\mu_{j})=0\right\}

Let us simplify the value of the

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &2J^{2}N<2JJ_{0}\lambda_{k},&\quad&\lambda>2J,\;\lambda_{k}>0\,;\\
&2J^{2}N>-2JJ_{0}\lambda_{k},&\quad&\lambda<-2J,\;\lambda_{k}<0\,.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(64)

We will remain from (

k

(65)

•

\lambda<2J
.

In this case we only have a regular term.

In the limit

\rho_{J_{0}}(\lambda)=\rho_{0}(\lambda)\,.

Considering the spectrum is even and combining the results of both cases, we get

Thus there are a set of critical values

When the value of

\rho_{J_{0}}(\lambda)\equiv\braket{\nu(\lambda)}_{J_{ij}:J_{0}\neq 0}=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\rho_{0}(\lambda),&\quad&J_{0}<J^{crit}_{0,1}\,;\\
&\rho_{0}(\lambda)+\frac{1}{N}(\delta(\lambda-\mu_{1})+\delta(\lambda+\mu_{1})),&\quad&J^{crit}_{0,1}<J_{0}<J^{crit}_{0,2}\,;\\
&\ldots\\
&\rho_{0}(\lambda)+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N/2}\delta(\lambda-\mu_{k})+\delta\left(\lambda+\mu_{k}\right),&\quad&J^{crit}_{0,\frac{N}{2}}<J_{0}\,.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

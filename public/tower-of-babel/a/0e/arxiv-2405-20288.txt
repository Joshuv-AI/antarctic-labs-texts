A family of cyclic quartic monogenic polynomials
P. Voutier

Abstract
. We produce an explicit family of totally real cyclic quartic polynomials that are monogenic in many cases, and conjecturally generate distinct monogenic quartic fields infinitely often.



1.
Introduction

Recall that a monic polynomial
f(X)\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
is called a
monogenic polynomial

if it is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\left\{1,\theta,\theta^{2},\ldots,\theta^{\deg(f)-1}\right\}

is an integral basis for the ring of integers,
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, of
K=\mathbb{Q}(\theta)
,
where
\theta
is a root of
f(X)
. Similarly, a number field is called a
monogenic
field
if there exists such a power basis for its ring of integers,
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.

Recently, Harrington and Jones
[
6
]
produced families of quartic polynomials that
are monogenic infinitely often for each of the non-cyclic Galois groups that are
possible for quartic polynomials.

To the best of this author’s knowledge, the following are all the known quartic
monogenic fields with
C_{4}
as their Galois group.

Gras
[
4
]
showed that there are only two non-real monogenic cyclic quartic
fields
\mathbb{Q}\left(\zeta_{5}\right)
and

\mathbb{Q}\left(\zeta_{16}-\zeta_{16}^{-1}\right)
,
where
\zeta_{n}
is a primitive
n
-th root of unity. The minimal polynomials of
generators of a power basis for these fields are
X^{4}+X^{3}+X^{2}+X+1

and
X^{4}+4X^{2}+2
, respectively.

Gras
[
4
]
also found
12
real cyclic quartic fields that are monogenic –
see Table 1 in
[
4
]
.

In this note, we produce an analogue in the case of totally real cyclic quartic
polynomials of the results of Harrington and Jones.

Theorem 1
.

For any integer
z
, let

(1)

\displaystyle f_{z}(X)=

\displaystyle X^{4}-zX^{3}-\frac{3z^{6}-16z^{4}+37z^{2}-32}{8}X^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{2z^{9}-19z^{7}+72z^{5}-135z^{3}+96z}{16}X

\displaystyle+\frac{-3z^{12}+40z^{10}-214z^{8}+576z^{6}-719z^{4}+64z^{2}+512}{256}.

If
z\not\equiv 2\pmod{4}
, then
f_{z}(X)\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
,
irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\Gal\left(f_{z}(X)\right)=C_{4}
.

In addition, if
z^{2}-2
is squarefree and if
\left(z^{4}-4z^{2}+8\right)/4

is squarefree when
z
is even and if
z^{4}-4z^{2}+8
is squarefree when
z
is
odd, then
\mathbb{Q}(\vartheta)
is a monogenic field, with

\left\{1,\vartheta,\vartheta^{2},\vartheta^{3}\right\}
as a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
for
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\vartheta)}
,
where
\vartheta
is a root of
f_{z}(X)
.

Remark
.

(1) If
|z|\geq 2
, then all the roots of
f_{z}(X)
are real (see the proof
of Lemma
8
for
|z|\geq 3
and then check
z=\pm 2
.)
If
z=\pm 1
, we obtain
\mathbb{Q}\left(\zeta_{5}\right)
.
If
z=0
, we obtain
\mathbb{Q}\left(\zeta_{16}-\zeta_{16}^{-1}\right)
.

(2) The square-free conditions on the values of both
z^{2}-2
and
\left(z^{4}-4z^{2}+8\right)/4

are required. For example, in the following cases,
f_{z}(X)
does not yield a
monogenic field:

z=4
(here
z^{2}-2=14
, but
\left(z^{4}-4z^{2}+8\right)/4=50
),

z=88
(here
z^{2}-2=7742
is divisible by
7^{2}
),

z=96
(here
\left(z^{4}-4z^{2}+8\right)/4=21224450
is divisible by
5^{2}
).

There are also examples where both are not squarefree, such as for

z=284
, where
z^{2}-2=80654
is divisible by
7^{2}
and

\left(z^{4}-4z^{2}+8\right)/4=1626266930
is divisible by
29^{2}
.

Corollary 2
.

Assuming the
abc
conjecture, there are infinitely many distinct monogenic
cyclic quartic fields.

This is an immediate consequence of Theorem
1
above and Theorem 1 in

[
3
]
.

In fact, the
abc
conjecture may be more than we require to show there are
infinitely many such fields. What is actually required is that the two polynomials
in
z
in Theorem
1
are both squarefree for infinitely many
z\not\equiv 2\pmod{4}
.

For polynomials that are products of irreducible polynomials of degree at most

3
, this has been known for some time (see
[
2
]
), but is an open
problem for polynomials with larger degree irreducible factors (see also
[
1
]
).

in the

.

2.
Construction of our polynomials

Our family of polynomials is found by using a result of Gras
[
4
]
, so we
begin by recalling some relevant terminology and notation there.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a cyclic extension of degree
4
, let
G=\langle\sigma\rangle=\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q})
, and let
k
be the quadratic subfield of
K
. Let
f
be the
conductor of
K
. Since
G
is an abelian group, the Kronecker-Weber theorem states
that
K
is a subfield of a
cyclotomic field
\mathbb{Q}\left(\zeta_{n}\right)
, where
\zeta_{n}
denotes a
primitive
n
-th root of unity.
If
n
is the smallest integer for which this holds, the conductor of

K
is
n
if
K
is fixed by complex conjugation.
If
m
is the conductor of
k
,
then the conductor of
K
is of the form
mg
, where
g\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
.

Let
\chi
be one of the two Dirichlet characters associated to
K
.
We have
\chi(-1)=\pm 1
and the field
K
is real if and only if
\chi(-1)=+1
.

From the bottom of page 1 of
[
4
]
, we have
K=\mathbb{Q}(\psi)
, where

\psi=\sqrt{\frac{\chi(-1)g\sqrt{m}\left(a+\sqrt{m}\right)}{2}},

for some
a
and
b
with
b
even satisfying
m=a^{2}+b^{2}
, recalling
g
and

m
from above. As on line 2 of page 2 of
[
4
]
, we put

(2)

\vartheta=\frac{t+z\sqrt{m}+2x\psi+2y\sigma(\psi)}{4},

where
x
,
y
and
z
will be as in Lemma
3
below and
t
is selected
according to equation (0) on page 2 of
[
4
]
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{(i) if $m$ is odd}&:t\equiv z\bmod{2},\dfrac{t+z}{2}\equiv gx\bmod{2}\text{ and }\dfrac{t-z}{2}\equiv gy\bmod{2},\\
\text{(ii) if $m$ is even}&:t\equiv 0\bmod{4}\text{ and }z\equiv 0\bmod{2}.\end{array}\right.

This ensures that
\vartheta
is an algebraic integer.

We now quote here Théorème 1 of
[
4
]
translated.

Lemma 3

(Gras
[
4
]
)
.

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a cyclic extension of degree
4
and conductor
f=mg
, where

m=a^{2}+b^{2}
, with
b\equiv 0\pmod{2}
, is the conductor of the quadratic

K
. The ring

of integers of

has a
\mathbb{Z}

if and only if there exist

such that:

(3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}b\left(x^{2}-y^{2}\right)+2axy=\pm 2\\

Proof.

This is Théorème 1 of
[
4
]
.
∎

We are interested in real fields,
K
, here, so we only consider
\chi(-1)=1
.

We searched for quartic cyclic monogenic polynomials using Gras’ condition.

Using PARI/GP
[
9
]
, for all triples,
(a,b,x)
, satisfying

1\leq a\leq 500
,
2\leq b\leq 20,000
(with
b
even) and
1\leq x\leq 200
,
we found the values of
y
such that the first equation in (
3
) holds.

For such solutions of that first equation, we did search over all
g

satisfying
1\leq g\leq 10^{6}
such that the second equation in (
3
)
has an integer solution,
z
.

For integer solutions,
(a,b,g,m,x,y,z)
, of the system of equations in
(
3

satisfying the conditions above required so that

\vartheta
is an algebraic integer, then determined
\vartheta
and its algebraic
conjugates in order to find its minimal polynomial.

We then calculated and compared the discriminant of the associated number field
with the discriminant of the minimal polynomial of
\vartheta
.

Among the examples found, we found examples satisfying the following pattern.

Lemma 4
.

Let
z
be any integer and put
\chi(-1)=1
. Then

(4)

\displaystyle a=z^{2}-2,

\displaystyle b=2,

\displaystyle g=a,

\displaystyle m=a^{2}+b^{2}=z^{4}-4z^{2}+8,

\displaystyle t=z,

\displaystyle x=1,

\displaystyle y=0,

satisfy (
3
) in Lemma
3
.

This requires just the following simple verification:

b\left(x^{2}-y^{2}\right)+2axy=2
and

\displaystyle m\left[z^{2}-\chi(-1)g\left(x^{2}+y^{2}\right)\right]^{2}-4g^{2}=\left(z^{4}-4z^{2}+8\right)\left[z^{2}-\left(z^{2}-2\right)\right]^{2}-4\left(z^{2}-2\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(4z^{4}-16z^{2}+32\right)-\left(4z^{4}-16z^{2}+16\right)=16.

∎

.

Let
a
,
b
,

,

,

\displaystyle=\sqrt{g\sqrt{m}(\sqrt{m}+a)/2},\quad

\displaystyle\psi^{(2)}

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{g\sqrt{m}(\sqrt{m}+a)/2},

\displaystyle\psi^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{-g\sqrt{m}(-\sqrt{m}+a)/2}\hskip 8.53581pt\text{ and }

3.
Further Lemmas

Lemma 6
.

Suppose
z
is an integer.
f_{z}(X)\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
if and only if

z\not\equiv 2\pmod{4}
.

Proof.

We need only consider the coefficients of
X^{2}
and
X
in
f_{z}(X)
, as the
other coefficients are integers. If
z\equiv z_{1}\pmod{4}
, then the numerator
of the
X^{2}
coefficient is
z_{1}^{2}\left(5z_{1}^{4}+3\right)\bmod{8}
,
while its denominator is
8
. From
z_{1}^{2}\equiv 0,1,4\pmod{8}
, we see that
the numerator is divisible by
8
if and only if

z\not\equiv 2\pmod{4}
.

Similarly, we see that the coefficient of
X
is an integer if and only if

z\not\equiv 2\pmod{4}
.
∎

3.1.
Irreducibility

We now show that
f_{z}(X)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
.
When
z\equiv 0\pmod{4}
, we can use Eisenstein’s criterion with
p=2
,
while for
z\equiv\pm 1\pmod{5}
, we can use Eisenstein’s criterion with
p=5
.
Similar results hold for
p=13
,
29
,…. However, we are not able to treat
all
z
in this way. E.g, for
z=13
and
17
, we were not able to find a prime,

p
, such that Eisenstein’s criterion applies. Hence we proceed here by rather
brute force techniques.

Lemma 7
.

For all integers,
z\not\equiv 2\pmod{4}
,
f_{z}(X)
has no quadratic factors.

Proof.

We prove this by contradiction. By Gauss’ Lemma, we may assume that if
f_{z}(X)

has two quadratic factors, then they are in
\mathbb{Z}[X]
and monic. So we suppose that we can
write

\displaystyle f_{z}(X)

\displaystyle=\left(X^{2}+a_{1}X+a_{0}\right)\left(X^{2}+b_{1}X+b_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=X^{4}+\left(a_{1}+b_{1}\right)X^{3}+\left(a_{0}+a_{1}b_{1}+b_{0}\right)X^{2}+\left(a_{0}b_{1}+a_{1}b_{0}\right)X+a_{0}b_{0},

where
a_{0},a_{1},b_{0},b_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
. We will iteratively solve for these
four coefficients (of
X^{3}
,
X^{2}
,
X
and the constant coefficient) and
find that they cannot all be integers.

We start with the
X^{3}
coefficient. We must have
-z=a_{1}+b_{1}
, so
b_{1}=-a_{1}-z
.
Substituting this back into our hypothetical factorisation of
f_{z}(X)
above
and comparing the
X^{2}
coefficients, we obtain

b_{0}=-\frac{3z^{6}+37z^{2}}{8}+2z^{4}+a_{1}z+a_{1}^{2}-a_{0}.

Substituting this back into the factorisation and comparing the
X
coefficient,
we obtain

a_{0}=\frac{2z^{9}-19z^{7}-6a_{1}z^{6}+72z^{5}+32a_{1}z^{4}-135z^{3}-74a_{1}z^{2}+\left(16a_{1}^{2}+96\right)z+64a_{1}+16a_{1}^{3}}{16\left(2a_{1}+z\right)}.

Substituting this back into the factorisation and comparing the constant coefficients,
we find that

\displaystyle\left(4a_{1}^{2}+4za_{1}-z^{6}+4z^{4}-7z^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left(16a_{1}^{4}+32za_{1}^{3}+\left(-8z^{6}+48z^{4}-104z^{2}+128\right)a_{1}^{2}+\left(-8z^{7}+48z^{5}-120z^{3}+128z\right)a_{1}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\left.+z^{12}-12z^{10}+62z^{8}-180z^{6}+305z^{4}-288z^{2}+128\right)

must be zero.

The first factor is zero when

a_{1}=\frac{-1\pm\sqrt{z^{4}-4z^{2}+8}}{2}.

The second factor is zero when

a_{1}=-\frac{z}{2}\pm\frac{\sqrt{\left(z^{2}-2\right)\left(z^{4}-4z^{2}+8\right)\pm\left(z^{2}-2\right)\sqrt{z^{4}-4z^{2}+8}}}{2},

for any of the four choices of the two
\pm
.

In both cases
z^{4}-4z^{2}+8=\left(z^{2}-2\right)+4=z_{1}^{2}
must have
integer solutions. The only squares whose difference is
4
are
0
and
4
.
So here this means
z^{2}-2=0
. But this has no integer solution. Hence an
factorisation of
f_{z}(X)
into two quadratic polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}[X]
is not
possible.

See the
quadratic_factorisation()
procedure in
maple\my-poly-checks.txt

in the github repository for the Maple code to check this.
∎

For the possibility of a linear factor, we use a different approach of bracketting
the roots of
f_{z}(X)
to show they cannot be integers.

Lemma 8
.

For all integers,
z\not\equiv 2\pmod{4}
,
f_{z}(X)
has no linear factors.

Proof.

By Gauss’ Lemma, we need only show that
f_{z}(X)
has no integer roots.

We can bound the four roots of
f_{z}(X)
as follows for
|z|\geq 3
:

\displaystyle-\frac{z^{3}}{4}+\frac{z}{4}-\frac{1}{z^{3}}-\frac{2}{z^{5}}-\frac{2}{z^{7}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle r_{1}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle-\frac{z^{3}}{4}+\frac{z}{4}-\frac{1}{z^{3}}-\frac{2}{z^{5}},

\displaystyle-\frac{z^{3}}{4}+\frac{5z}{4}-\frac{1}{z}-\frac{1}{z^{3}}-\frac{1}{z^{5}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle r_{2}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle-\frac{z^{3}}{4}+\frac{5z}{4}-\frac{1}{z}-\frac{1}{z^{3}},

\displaystyle-\frac{z^{3}}{4}+\frac{5z}{4}-\frac{1}{z}+\frac{1}{z^{5}}+\frac{3}{z^{7}}+\frac{6}{z^{9}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle r_{3}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle-\frac{z^{3}}{4}+\frac{5z}{4}-\frac{1}{z}+\frac{1}{z^{5}}+\frac{3}{z^{7}}+\frac{6}{z^{9}}+\frac{7}{z^{11}},

\displaystyle\frac{3z^{3}}{4}-\frac{7z}{4}+\frac{2}{z}+\frac{2}{z^{3}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle r_{4}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{3z^{3}}{4}-\frac{7z}{4}+\frac{2}{z}+\frac{2}{z^{3}}+\frac{2}{z^{5}}.

We establish these bounds by substituting the upper and lower bounds for each
root in
f_{z}(X)
to get an expression for the value of the polynomial, in terms
of
z
, at the bounds and then showing that these values have opposite signs for

z
sufficiently large. We need the additional terms for
r_{3}
, as we need to
separate it from
r_{2}
, which is very close to
r_{3}
, as well as having
values of
f_{z}(X)
at the upper and lower bound for
r_{3}
having opposite
signs.

Using
r_{2}
as an example, we find that

f_{z}\left(-\frac{z^{3}}{4}+\frac{5z}{4}-\frac{1}{z}-\frac{1}{z^{3}}\right)=\frac{-3z^{12}-z^{10}+8z^{8}+12z^{6}+10z^{4}+4z^{2}+1}{z^{20}}.

For
|z|\geq 2
, the quantity on the right-hand side is negative (the numerator
of the right-hand side, a polynomial, has only two real roots at
\pm 1.4694266\ldots
).

Also

f_{z}\left(-\frac{z^{3}}{4}+\frac{5z}{4}-\frac{1}{z}-\frac{1}{z^{3}}-\frac{1}{z^{5}}\right)=\frac{z^{8}-3z^{6}+2z^{4}+4z^{2}+1}{z^{12}}.

For all real
z
, the right-hand side is positive (here the polynomial forming
the numerator of the right-hand side has no real roots). Hence for
|z|\geq 2
,
the bounds for
r_{2}
hold.

Next we need to ensure that
-z^{3}/4+5z/4-1/4<r_{2}<-z^{3}/4+5z/4+1/4
. This
holds when
|z|\geq 5
.

We have
f_{z}\left(-z^{3}/4+5z/4\right)=2-z^{2}\neq 0
for
|z|\geq 2
.
Hence
r_{2}
is never an integer.

Lastly, we check
f_{z}(X)
for
|z|\geq 4
and find that they are all
irreducible.

We proceed in the same way for the other three roots of
f_{z}(X)
. The above
calculations were done using Maple. See the
root_location_check()
procedure
in
maple\my-poly-checks.txt
in the github repository for the Maple code
to check this.

(

(5)

(2)

C_{2}\times C_{2}
: if and only if

\mathbb{Q}

(3)

D_{4}

has exactly one root

A_{4}

Lemma 11
.

If
f_{z}(X)\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
, then
\Gal\left(f_{z}(X)\right)=C_{4}
.

Proof.

We apply Lemma
10
.

We are only interested here in case
1
. Using Maple, it can be shown
that for our polynomials
f_{z}(X)
, we can write
512r(X)
as

8X-\left(32-23z^{2}+8z^{4}-z^{6}\right)
times

\displaystyle 64X^{2}+\left(16z^{6}-64z^{4}+112z^{2}\right)X

\displaystyle+z^{12}-8z^{10}+14z^{8}+72z^{6}-399z^{4}+768z^{2}-512.

So here
s=\left(32-23z^{2}+8z^{4}-z^{6}\right)/8
.

The discriminant of the quadratic factor is

4096\left(z^{2}-2\right)^{2}\left(z^{4}-4z^{2}+8\right).

We can write
z^{4}-4z^{2}+8=\left(z^{2}-2\right)^{2}+4
. The only squares
whose difference is
4
are
0
and
4
. But
z^{2}-2=0
has no integer solutions.
Hence the discriminant of the quadratic factor is never a square and
r(X)
has
no other rational roots.

Again, using Maple, we find that

16\disc\left(X^{2}-sX+d\right)=(z-1)^{2}(z-2)^{2}(z+1)^{2}(z+2)^{2}\left(z^{4}-4z^{2}+8\right)

and

\disc\left(X^{2}+aX+(b-s)\right)=z^{2}\left(z^{4}-4z^{2}+8\right).

The discriminants of both factors of
g(X)
are rational squares times
z^{4}-4z^{2}+8
.
So
g(X)
splits in
L
.

Hence,
f_{z}(X)
has
C_{4}
as its Galois group over
\mathbb{Q}
.

3.3.
Conductor of
K

We start with a result due to Spearman and Williams
[
10
]
that we will
use to determine the conductor.

Suppose that

Decomposition of Approximately Monotone and Convex Sequences
Angshuman R. Goswami

Abstract
. In this paper, we primarily deal with approximately monotone and convex sequences. We start by showing that any sequence can be expressed as the difference between two nondecreasing sequences. One of these two monotone sequences act as the majorant of the original sequence, while the other possesses non-negativity. Another result establishes that an approximately monotone(increasing) sequence can be closely approximated by a non-decreasing sequence. A similar assertion can be made for approximately convex sequence. A sequence ⟨ u n ⟩ n = 0 ∞ \big<u_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty} is said to be approximately convex (or ε \varepsilon -convex) if the following inequality holds under the mentioned assumptions u i − u i − 1 ≤ u j − u j − 1 + ε where i , j ∈ ℕ with i < j . u_{i}-u_{i-1}\leq u_{j}-u_{j-1}+\varepsilon\quad\quad\mbox{ where}\quad\quad i,j\in\mathbb{N}\quad\mbox{with}\quad i<j. We proved that an approximately convex sequence can be written as the algebraic difference of two specific types of sequences. The initial sequence contains sequential convexity property, while the other sequence possesses the Lipschitz property. Moreover, we introduce an operator 𝒯 \mathscr{T} , that termed as a twisting operator. In a compact interval I ( ⊆ ℝ ) I(\subseteq\mathbb{R}) ; we characterized the convex function with this newly introduced operator. Besides various sequence decomposition results and study related to 𝒯 \mathscr{T} -operator on convex function; a characterization regarding non-negative sequential convexity, a fractional inequality, implication of 𝒯 \mathscr{T} operator on various types of functions, relationship between a convex function and a convex sequence are also included. Motivation, previous research in this direction, various applications and linkage with some other branches of mathematics are discussed in the introduction section.

1.
On monotone and convex sequences

Throughout this section the symbols
\mathbb{N}
,
\,\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}_{+}
will denote the sets of natural, real and non-negative real numbers respectively.
A sequence
\big<u_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
is said to be convex if it satisfies the following discrete functional inequality

(2)

\displaystyle 2u_{n}\leq u_{n-1}+u_{n+1}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

In other words,
\big<u_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
is said to be sequentially convex, if the sequence
\big<u_{n}-u_{n-1}\big>_{n=1}^{\infty}
possesses monotonicity.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
\big<u_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a non-negative sequence such that
u_{n}\leq\sqrt{u_{n-1}u_{n+1}}
holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the sequence is convex.

Proof.

Since
\big<u_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
is non-negative; we can conclude
\Big(\sqrt{u_{n+1}}-\sqrt{u_{n}}\Big)^{2}\geq 0.
By expanding this expression and utilizing the condition on
\big<u_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
, we obtain the following inequality

\displaystyle 2u_{n}\leq 2\sqrt{u_{n-1}u_{n+1}}\leq u_{n-1}+u_{n+1}.

This yields that
\big<u_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
is convex and completes the proof.
∎

Besides, the above proposition also shows that geometric sequences possess convexity. The next proposition gives a characterization to convex sequence under some minimal assumptions.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
\big<u_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a decreasing, convex sequence with positive terms if and only if the reciprocal sequence
\big<v_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
defined by
v_{n}=\dfrac{1}{u_{n}}
is positive, non-decreasing and concave.

Proof.

The non-negativity and monotonicity (increasingness)of the sequence
\big<v_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
is self-explanatory.
The convexity of the sequence
\big<u_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
yields (
2
). Utilizing convexity, decreasingness and non-negativity of the sequence
\big<u_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
, we can obtain the following inequality

\displaystyle(u_{n}-u_{n-1})u_{n+1}\leq(u_{n+1}-u_{n})u_{n-1}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Dividing both sides of the above inequality by
(u_{n-1}u_{n}u_{n+1})
, and rearranging the terms we get

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{u_{n-1}}+\dfrac{1}{u_{n+1}}\leq\dfrac{2}{u_{n}}.

This shows that the sequence
\big<v_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
is concave.
The reverse implication can be obtained in a similar way.
∎

The next proposition shows that any sequence can be represented as the difference of two non-deceasing sequences.

Proposition 1.3
.

Any sequence
\big<u_{i}\big>_{i=0}^{\infty}
can be expressed as the difference of two non-decreasing sequences
\big<v_{i}\big>_{i=0}^{\infty}
and
\big<w_{i}\big>_{i=0}^{\infty}
of
\big<u_{i}\big>_{i=0}^{\infty}
. Where the sequence
\big<v_{i}\big>_{i=0}^{\infty}
also act as a majorant of
\big<u_{i}\big>_{i=0}^{\infty}
and the second sequence
\big<w_{i}\big>_{i=0}^{\infty}
is non-negative.

Proof.

Let
\big<u_{i}\big>_{i=0}^{\infty}
be any arbitrary sequence. We can define two sequences
\big<v_{i}\big>_{i=0}^{\infty}
and
\big<w_{i}\big>_{i=0}^{\infty}
as follows

\displaystyle v_{i}:=\begin{cases}u_{0},\quad\mbox{if}\quad i=0\\
u_{0}+\overset{n}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}|u{{}_{i}}-u_{i-1}|\quad n\in\mathbb{N}\end{cases}\quad\quad\quad\mbox{and}\quad\quad\quad w_{i}=v_{i}-u_{i}.

We can express
u_{i}=v_{i}-w_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.
By definition, It can be easily observed that

\displaystyle v_{i}\geq u_{i}\quad\mbox{and}\quad w_{i}\geq 0\quad\mbox{for all}\quad i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

Therefore, only remains to show the monotonicity of these two newly defined sequences.

It can be easily observed that
u_{0}\leq u_{1}
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
to be arbitrary. Then we can compute

\displaystyle v_{n+1}=u_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}|u_{i}-u_{i-1}|=u_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}|u_{i}-u_{i-1}|+|u_{n+1}-u_{n}|\geq v_{n}.

This establishes the monotonicity of the sequence
\big<v_{i}\big>_{i=0}^{\infty}
. We can see that

(3)

\displaystyle w_{1}-w_{0}=(v_{1}-u_{1})-(v_{0}-u_{0})=v_{1}-u_{1}=u_{0}+|u_{1}-u_{0}|-u_{1}\geq 0.

Also, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have the inequality

\displaystyle v_{n+1}=u_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}|u_{i}-u_{i-1}|=u_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}|u_{i}-u_{i-1}|+|u_{n+1}-u_{n}|\geq v_{n}+u_{n+1}-u_{n}.

It shows
w_{n+1}\geq w_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This together with (
3
) proves the monotonicity of the sequence
\big<w_{i}\big>_{i=0}^{\infty}
and completes the proof.
∎

A sequence
\big<u_{i}\big>_{i=0}^{\infty}
is said to be
\varepsilon
-monotone, if for any
u_{m}\in\big<u_{i}\big>_{i=0}^{\infty}
; the inequality

(4)

\displaystyle u_{m}\leq u_{n}+\varepsilon\quad\mbox{for all}\quad{m<n}\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

holds. In the next theorem, we are going to show that a
\varepsilon
-monotone sequence can be approximated by an ordinary monotone(increasing) sequence. The theorem extensively uses the decomposition result of
\varepsilon
-monotone function; which was introduced by Páles in his paper
[
88
]

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\big<u_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a
\varepsilon
-monotone sequence. Then there exists a continuous, almost everywhere differentiable
\varepsilon
-monotone function
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
f(n)=u_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.
Additionally, there exists a monotone sequence

\big<v_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
, such that

(5)

\displaystyle|u_{n}-v_{n}|\leq\dfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\quad\mbox{holds for all}\quad n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

Proof.

To prove this theorem, We construct a function
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle f(x):=tu_{n-1}+(1-t)u_{n}\quad\mbox{;}\,\,x\in[{n-1},n]\,\,\mbox{satisfying}\,\,x=t(n-1)+(1-t)n\quad(t\in[0,1]).

From above, the continuity of the function
f
is clearly visible. Besides it is evident that the constructed function is differentiable everywhere except the points at
\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Substituting
t=0
; at the definition of
f
, we obtain
f(n)=u_{n}
for all
\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.
Now to show
f
is
\varepsilon
-monotone; let
x,y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
x<y
. It leads to the following

\displaystyle x\in[{n_{1}-1},{n}]\quad\mbox{and}\quad y\in[{n_{2}-1},{n_{2}}],\quad\mbox{where}\quad n_{1}\leq n_{2}\quad\quad(n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}).

Based on it
f(x)
and
f(y)
will satisfy the following two inequalities

\displaystyle\min\Big\{u_{n_{1}-1},u_{n_{2}}\Big\}\leq f(x)\leq\max\Big\{u_{n_{1}-1},u_{n_{1}}\Big\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\min\Big\{u_{n_{2}-1},u_{n_{2}}\Big\}\leq f(y)\leq\max\Big\{u_{n_{2}-1},u_{n_{2}}\Big\}

By, using
\varepsilon
-monotonicity; from the two inequalities above, we can compute

\displaystyle f(x)-f(y)\leq\max\Big\{u_{n_{1}-1},u_{n_{1}}\Big\}-\min\Big\{u_{n_{2}-1},u_{n_{2}}\Big\}\leq\varepsilon.

This yields that
f
is
\varepsilon
-monotone and completes the first part of the theorem.

To prove the second part, we will utilize one of the results of
\varepsilon
-monotonicity that appeared in the paper
[
14
]
. The statement of the theorem can also be formulated as follows

If a function
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
is
\varepsilon
-monotone then there exists a monotone(increasing) function
g:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
that satisfies the inequality

\displaystyle|f(x)-g(x)|\leq\dfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad x\in I.

Now choosing the elements of the sequence
\big<v_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
as
v_{n}=g(n)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and using the above mentioned result, we can obtain the inequality (
5
). Besides, monotonicity of the function
g:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
ensures that the sequence
\big<v_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
is non-decreasing. This completes the proof and establishes the result.
∎

In the upcoming result, we will show the relationship of the Cauchy sequence with
\varepsilon
-monotonicity. A sequence
\big<u_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
is said to be Cauchy sequence if for any given
\delta\geq 0
there exists
r\in\mathbb{N}
; that satisfies the following inequality under the mentioned conditions

(6)

\displaystyle|u_{m}-u_{n}|\leq\delta\quad\mbox{for all}\quad m,n\geq r\quad(m,n,r\in\mathbb{N}).

Proposition 1.5
.

Every Cauchy sequence is approximately monotone.

Proof.

To prove the proposition, we assume
\big<u_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a Cauchy sequence such that for a fixed
\delta\geq 0
, it satisfies (
6
). We consider

(7)

\displaystyle\gamma=\max\Big\{\max|u_{p}-u_{q}|,\delta\Big\}\quad p,q\leq r\quad(p,q,r\in\mathbb{N}).

We take
\varepsilon=\gamma+\delta
and will show that
\big<u_{i}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
is
\varepsilon
-monotone. For that we have to consider three cases. At first, we assume
m,n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
with

m<n\leq r
. Using (
7
), we can compute the following

\displaystyle u_{m}-u_{n}\leq\max_{p,q\leq r}|u_{p}-u_{q}|\leq\gamma<\varepsilon.

For
r\leq m<n,
utilizing (
6
) we can proceed as follows

\displaystyle u_{m}-u_{n}\leq|u_{m}-u_{n}|\leq\delta\leq\varepsilon.

Finally for
m<r<n
, utilizing triangular inequality, we obtain the following

\displaystyle u_{m}-u_{n}\leq|u_{p}-u_{r}|+|u_{r}-u_{n}|\leq\gamma+\delta=\varepsilon.

The analogous inequalities under these three situations establish that
\big<u_{i}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
possesses
\varepsilon
-monotonicity.
∎

The above result leaves us with the question, how effectively we can approximate a Cauchy sequence with a non-decreasing sequence. The next proposition shows the close relationship of a Cauchy sequence with a usual monotonic sequence.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
\big<u_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{R}
. Then there exists a monotone(increasing) sequence
\big<v_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
satisfying
\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}u_{n}=\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}v_{n}

Proof.

Since
\big<u_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{R}
; it converges to a finite value. Without losing the generality, we assume that there exists
l\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}u_{n}=l
. Now we define a sequence
\big<u_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
as follows

\displaystyle v_{n}=\inf_{n\leq k}v_{k}\qquad\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})

By definition; it is evident that
v_{n}\leq u_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Also for arbitrary
n_{1}
and
n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
with
n_{1}\leq n_{2}
; we can compute the inequality below

\displaystyle v_{n_{1}}=\inf_{n_{1}\leq k}v_{k}\leq\inf_{n_{2}\leq k}v_{k}=v_{n_{2}}.

This yields the monotonicity of the sequence
\big<v_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
. Now, we only need to show that
\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}v_{n}=l
.

Since
\big<u_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
converges to
l
; it also indicates that
l
is both limit superior and limit inferior for this sequence. By using the definition of limit inferior, we obtain the following

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}v_{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big(\inf_{n\leq k}u_{k}\Big)=l\qquad\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}).

This completes the proof.
∎

Before proceeding to the next theorem, we must establish the following lemma.

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be arbitrary. Then for any
a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
and
b_{1},\cdots,b_{n}\in]0,\infty[
; the following inequality holds

(8)

\displaystyle\min\bigg\{\dfrac{a_{1}}{b_{1}},\cdots,\dfrac{a_{n}}{b_{n}}\bigg\}\leq\dfrac{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}}{b_{1}+\cdots+b_{n}}\leq\max\bigg\{\dfrac{a_{1}}{b_{1}},\cdots,\dfrac{a_{n}}{b_{n}}\bigg\}.

Proof.

We will prove the theorem using the principle of mathematical induction. For
n=1
, there is nothing to prove. For
n=2
; without loss of generality; for
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and
b_{1},b_{2}\in]0,\infty[
, we assume that
\dfrac{a_{1}}{b_{1}}\leq\dfrac{a_{2}}{b_{2}}
. We can also express this in terms of two inequalities as follows

\displaystyle a_{1}(b_{1}+b_{2})\leq(a_{1}+a_{2})b_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad(a_{1}+a_{2})b_{2}\leq(b_{1}+b_{2})a_{2}.

The two inequalities above together yield the following

(9)

\displaystyle\dfrac{a_{1}}{b_{1}}\leq\dfrac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{b_{1}+b_{2}}\leq\dfrac{a_{2}}{b_{2}}

and validates the result for
n=2.

Now we assume that the statement is true for any fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
. That is (
8
) holds true. Now, to establish the statement, we need to validate it for
n+1\in\mathbb{N}
.

We assume
a_{1},\cdots,a_{n},a_{n+1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
b_{1},\cdots,b_{n},b_{n+1}\in]0,\infty[
. First utilizing (
9
) and then (
8
), we can compute the following inequality

\displaystyle\dfrac{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}+a_{n+1}}{b_{1}+\cdots+b_{n}+b_{n+1}}\leq\max\bigg\{\dfrac{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}}{b_{1}+\cdots+b_{n}},\dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_{n+1}}\bigg\}\leq\max\bigg\{\dfrac{a_{1}}{b_{1}},\cdots,\dfrac{a_{n}}{b_{n}},\dfrac{a_{n+1}}{b_{n+1}}\bigg\}.

In a similar way, we can show the inequality below

\displaystyle\min\bigg\{\dfrac{a_{1}}{b_{1}},\cdots,\dfrac{a_{n}}{b_{n}},\dfrac{a_{n+1}}{b_{n+1}}\bigg\}\leq\min\bigg\{\dfrac{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}}{b_{1}+\cdots+b_{n}},\dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_{n+1}}\bigg\}\leq\dfrac{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}+a_{n+1}}{b_{1}+\cdots+b_{n}+b_{n+1}}.

Combining the above two inequalities, we will have
our desired result.
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

The origin
\Gamma
-function is directly linked to the study of factorial sequences. It states that a continuous function
\Gamma:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is obtainable from the sequence
\big<n!\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
that possesses all the factorial properties. Similarly, the upcoming theorem shows that any discrete graph of a convex sequence can be extendable to a convex function. In other words, for every convex sequence
\big<u_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
there exists atleast one continuous convex function
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
that precisely approximates the sequential points.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
\big<u_{n}\big>_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a convex sequence. There there exists a continuous almost everywhere differentiable convex function
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
f(n)=u_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

Conversely, if
f
is convex in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
; then the sequence
<f(n)>_{n=0}^{\infty}
is convex in
\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.

Proof.

To prove the statement, we define a function
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
as follows

(10)

\displaystyle f(x):=tu_{n}+(1-t)u_{n+1}\quad\mbox{where}\quad x:=tn+(1-t)(n+1)\quad(t\in[0,1]\,\mbox{ and }\,n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}).

From the construction, it is clearly visible that
f
is continuous in

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}^{2}(f)=\mathscr{T}(\mathscr{T}(f))=T_{\mathscr{T}_{f}}=\mathscr{I}\quad\mbox{that is}\quad\mathscr{T}^{2}(f)(x)=x\quad\quad(x\in I).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let

To prove the statement, we assume

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}_{f}(y)=f(a+b-y)\leq f(a+b-x)=\mathscr{T}_{f}(x).

The above inequality establishes decreasingness of

and completes the proof.

Mutual-visibility and general position in double graphs and in Mycielskians
Dhanya Roy, Sandi Klavžar, Aparna Lakshmanan S

Abstract
The general position problem in graphs is to find the maximum number of vertices that can be selected such that no three vertices lie on a common shortest path. The mutual-visibility problem in graphs is to find the maximum number of vertices that can be selected such that every pair of vertices in the collection has a shortest path between them with no vertex from the collection as an internal vertex. In this paper, the general position problem and the mutual-visibility problem is investigated in double graphs and in Mycielskian graphs. Sharp general bounds are proved, in particular involving the total mutual-visibility number and the outer mutual-visibility number of base graphs. Several exact values are also determined, in particular the mutual-visibility number of the double graphs and of the Mycielskian of cycles.



Keywords
: general position, mutual-visibility, double graph, Mycielskian graph, outer mutual-visibility, total mutual-visibility

In discrete geometry, a shortest path between two points is unique while in graphs there can be more than one shortest path between two vertices. This fact, as well as the computational concept of visibility between robots bring the mutual-visibility problem in graphs into picture. This problem was introduced by Di Stefano

In

Preliminaries

If
G
is a connected graph,
S\subseteq V(G)
, and
{\cal P}=\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{t}\}
a partition of
S
, then
{\cal P}
is
distance-constant
if for any
i,j\in[t]
,
i\neq j
, the distance
d_{G}(x,y)
, where
x\in S_{i}
and
y\in S_{j}
, is independent of the selection of
x
and
y
. This distance is then also the distance
d_{G}(S_{i},S_{j})
between
S_{i}
and
S_{j}
. A distance-constant partition
{\cal P}
is
in-transitive
if
d_{G}(S_{i},S_{k})\neq d_{G}(S_{i},S_{j})+d_{G}(S_{j},S_{k})
holds for
i,j,k\in[p]
. The following characterization of general position sets will be used either implicitly or explicitly in the rest of the paper. By
G[S]
we denote the subgraph of
G
induced by
S\subseteq V(G)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
1
, Theorem 3.1]

Let
G
be a connected graph. Then
S\subseteq V(G)
is a general position set if and only if the components of
G[S]
are complete subgraphs, the vertices of which form an in-transitive, distance-constant partition of
S
.

Let
G
be a graph. Vertices
x
and
y
of
G
are
false twins
if
N_{G}(x)=N_{G}(y)
. (Note that false twins are not adjacent.) Further,
x
and
y
are
true twins
if
N_{G}[x]=N_{G}[y]
, where
N_{G}[x]
denotes the closed neighborhood of the vertex
x
in
G
. In
[
17
]
, relations between true twins, the general position number, and strong resolving graphs were investigated. The following easy but useful general properties of twins hold.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a graph and
u,v\in V(G)
.

(i) If
u
,
v
are false twins, and
S
is a general position (resp. mutual-visibility) set of
G
such that
S\cap\{u,v\}=\{u\}
, then
(S\setminus\{u\})\cup\{v\}
is also a general position (resp. mutual-visibility) set of
G
.

(ii) If
u
,
v
are true twins, and
S
is a general position set of
G
such that
u\in S
, then
S\cup\{v\}
is also a general position set of
G
.

Proof.

(i) Since
d_{G}(u,x)=d_{G}(v,x)
for each
x\in V(G)\setminus\{u,v\}
, Theorem
2.1
yields that
(S\setminus\{u\})\cup\{v\}
is a general position set of
G
. Moreover, two vertices are
S
-visible if and only they are
(S\setminus\{u\})\cup\{v\}
-visible, hence
(S\setminus\{u\})\cup\{v\}
is a mutual-visibility set provided that
S
is a mutual-visibility set.

(ii) Using the fact that
d_{G}(u,x)=d_{G}(v,x)
for each
x\in V(G)\setminus\{u,v\}
, Theorem
2.1
again can be used to deduce that
S\cup\{v\}
is a general position set. Indeed, if
v\in S
, there is nothing to prove. Let now
v\notin S
and let
Q
be the complete subgraph cotaining
u
from the partition of
S
corresponding to Theorem
2.1
. Then
Q\cup\{v\}
is also complete, therefore Theorem
2.1
implies that
S\cup\{v\}
is a general position.
∎

Note that Lemma
2.2
(ii) does not hold if general position sets are replaced by mutual-visibility sets. For example, consider the complete graph
K_{4}
minus an edge with the vertex set
\{a,b,c,d\}
, where
a
and
b
are the non-adjacent pair. Then
S=\{a,b,c\}
is a mutual-visibility set,
c
and
d
are true twins, but we cannot add
d
to
S
without affecting the mutual-visibility.

3
Mutual-visibility in double graphs

In this section we consider mutual-visibility in double graphs. For this task recall that if
G
is a graph, then
V(D(G))=V(G)\cup V(G^{\prime})
and that for each pair
u\in V(G)
and
u^{\prime}\in V(G^{\prime})
we have
N_{D(G)}(u)=N_{D(G)}(u^{\prime})
.

If
G
is a graph, then, clearly,
\mu(G)=n(G)
if and only if
G
is a complete graph, the same conclusion holds for the total mutual-visibility
[
20
]
. (Here and later,
n(G)
denotes the order of
G
.) For each vertex
u\in V(D(G))
, clearly,
N_{D(G)}[u]
is a mutual-visibility set of
D(G)
. Thus
\mu(D(G))\geq 2\Delta(G)+1
. Hence for the double graphs of graphs with a universal vertex, we have the following observation:

Observation 3.1
.

If
G
is a graph with
n(G)\geq 2
and with a universal vertex, then
\mu(D(G))=2n(G)-1
.

Theorem 3.2
.

If
G
is not a complete graph, then
\mu(D(G))\geq n(G)+\mathop{\mu_{\mathrm{t}}}(G)
. Moreover, the bound is sharp.

Proof.

Consider an arbitrary
\mathop{\mu_{\mathrm{t}}}
-set
S
of
G
. We claim that
X=V(G^{\prime})\cup S
is a mutual-visibility set of
D(G)
. Since
S
is a total mutual-visibility set of
G
, for any two vertices in
G
, there exists a shortest path whose internal vertices are in
V(G)\setminus S
. Hence any two vertices
u,v\in V(G^{\prime})\cup S
, where
v\neq u^{\prime}
, there exists a shortest
u,v
-path whose internal vertices are in
V(G)\setminus S
. Moreover, since
N_{G}[u]\subseteq S
if and only if
G
is complete, for each
u\in S
there exists a vertex
v\in N_{G}(u)
such that
v\in V(G)\setminus S
. Hence the path
u-v-u^{\prime}
demonstrates that
u
and
u^{\prime}
are also
X
-visible whenever
u,u^{\prime}\in X
. Thus
V(G^{\prime})\cup S
is indeed a mutual-visibility set of
D(G)
which proves that
\mu(D(G))\geq n+\mathop{\mu_{\mathrm{t}}}(G)
.

To demonstrate the sharpness, consider the path graph
P_{n}
,
n\geq 3
, with the vertices
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
. Let
S
be an arbitrary mutual-visibility set of
D(P_{n})
. If we would have indices
i<j<k
, such that
\{u_{i},u_{i}^{\prime},u_{j},u_{j}^{\prime},u_{k},u_{k}^{\prime}\}\in S
, then
u_{i}
and
u_{k}
would not be
S
-visible. Therefore, for at most two indices
i\in[n]
we have
|S\cap\{u_{i},u_{i}^{\prime}\}|=2
which in turn implies that
\mu(D(P_{n}))\leq n+2
. Since
\mathop{\mu_{\mathrm{t}}}(P_{n})=2
, the above proved bound yields
\mu(D(P_{n}))\geq n+2
, hence the bound is sharp.
∎

In the seminal paper on the mutual-visibility
[
9
]
it was proved that the mutual-visibility problem is NP-complete, while in
[
5
]
the same conclusion was obtained for each of the problems from the variety of mutual-visibility problems including the total mutual-visibility problem. Theorem
3.2
could indicate that the mutual-visibility problem is difficult also when restricted to double graphs.

The next result yields another family for which the bound of Theorem
4.2
is sharp.

Theorem 3.3
.

If
n\geq 7
, then
\mu(D(C_{n}))=n
.

Proof.

Let
V(D(C_{n}))=V\cup V^{\prime}
, where
V=V(C_{n})
and
V^{\prime}=\{u^{\prime}:\ u\in V\}
. Let
S
be a
\mu
-set of
D(C_{n})
such that it contains as many vertices of
V^{\prime}
as possible. This choice of
S
, together with Lemma
2.2
(i), implies that if
u\in V\cap S
, then
u^{\prime}
also belongs to
S
.

If
V^{\prime}\subseteq S
, then no vertex from
V
can be present in
S
, because if
v\in S
, then the two neighbors of
v^{\prime}
in
V^{\prime}
are not
S
-visible. Therefore, in this case
|S|=n
. By the same argument we also get that if
|S|>n
, then
|S\cap V|\geq 2
. We may hence assume in the rest that not all vertices from
V^{\prime}
are in
S
. We now distinguish two cases.

Assume first that
S
contains at least three vertices from
V
, say
u,v,w\in V\cap S
. Then, by the maximality assumption,
u^{\prime}
,
v^{\prime}
and
w^{\prime}
are also in
S
. Now, if
x^{\prime}
belongs to
S
, where
x\neq u,v,w
, then at least one of the shortest paths in
V^{\prime}
from
x^{\prime}
to
u^{\prime}
,
v^{\prime}
or
w^{\prime}
must contain at least one vertex among
u^{\prime}
,
v^{\prime}
and
w^{\prime}
as an internal vertex. We may assume without loss of generality that a shortest
x^{\prime},u^{\prime}
-path contains
v^{\prime}
as an internal vertex. (It could be that also the other
x^{\prime},u^{\prime}
-path in
D(C_{n})[V^{\prime}]
is shortest. Then it contains
w
as an internal vertex, and the argument is parallel.) Since
v
is also in
S
, the vertices
x^{\prime}
and
u^{\prime}
are not
S
-visible. Therefore,
|S\cap V^{\prime}|=3
so that
|S|=6
, a contradiction with Theorem
3.2
which asserts that
|S|\geq n\geq 7
.

Assume second that
S\cap V=\{u,v\}
. Using the maximality assumption again,
u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\in S
. There is nothing to prove if
|S\cap V^{\prime}|\leq n-2
, hence assume that
|S\cap V^{\prime}|=n-1
. Let
w^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
be the vertex not in
S
. If
w
is not adjacent to both
u
and
v
, then we may assume without loss of generality that the two neigbors of
u^{\prime}
are in
S
, but then they are not
S
-visible. Similarly, if
w
is in
C_{n}
adjacent to both
u
and
v
, and
z
is the other neighbor of
u
, then
z^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime}
are not
S
-visible.

As none of the cases above is possible we can conclude that
\mu(D(C_{n}))\leq n
and so
\mu(D(C_{n}))=n
.
∎

The proof of Theorem
3.3
asserts that for any
n\geq 4
, we have
\mu(D(C_{n}))\leq\max\{6,\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor+4,n\}
. Hence
\mu(D(C_{4}))\leq 6
,
\mu(D(C_{5}))\leq 6
and
\mu(D(C_{6}))\leq 7
. Also,
\{v_{1}^{\prime},v_{2}^{\prime},v_{3}^{\prime},v_{4}^{\prime},v_{1},v_{2}\}
is a mutual-visibility set of
D(C_{4})
,
\{v_{2}^{\prime},v_{3}^{\prime},v_{4}^{\prime},v_{5}^{\prime},v_{2},v_{5}\}
is a mutual-visibility set of
D(C_{5})
and
\{v_{2}^{\prime},v_{3}^{\prime},v_{4}^{\prime},v_{5}^{\prime},v_{6}^{\prime},v_{2},v_{6}\}
is a mutual-visibility set of
D(C_{6})
. Therefore
\mu(D(C_{4}))=\mu(D(C_{5}))=6
and
\mu(D(C_{6}))=7
.

While the lower bound of Theorem
3.2
is sharp, it can, on the other hand, be arbitrarily bad, that is, the difference
\mu(D(G))-(n(G)+\mathop{\mu_{\mathrm{t}}}(G))
can be arbitrarily large. For example, consider the balloon graph
G_{k}
,
k\geq 2
, constructed from the disjoint union of
k
copies of
C_{5}
and a vertex which is adjacent to exactly one vertex of each of the
k
copies of
C_{5}
. Then we have:

Proposition 3.4
.

If
k\geq 2
, then
\mu(D(G_{k}))-(n(G_{k})+\mathop{\mu_{\mathrm{t}}}(G_{k}))\geq k-1
.

Proof.

Clearly,
n(G_{k})=5k+1
. Using the characterization
[
27
, Theorem 8]
of graphs
G
with
\mathop{\mu_{\mathrm{t}}}(G)=0
(or by verifying it directly), we can deduce that
\mathop{\mu_{\mathrm{t}}}(G_{k})=0
. Further, we can use Fig.
1
to find out that
\mu(D(G_{k}))\geq 6k
.

A mutual-visibility set in the double graph of a balloon graph.

Hence we have

\mu(D(G_{k}))-(n(G_{k})+\mathop{\mu_{\mathrm{t}}}(G_{k}))\geq 6k-((5k+1)+0)=k-1\,,

and we are done.
∎

4
General position in double graphs

In this section we consider the general position number of double graphs. We will use the convention that if
S\subseteq V(G)\subset V(D(G))
, then
S^{\prime}=\{u^{\prime}\in V(G^{\prime}):\ u\in S\}
.

We first state a simple but useful lemma which easily follows from the fact that if
u\in V(G)\subset V(D(G))
is not an isolated vertex, then
d_{D(G)}(u,u^{\prime})=2
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
G
be a graph,
uv\in E(G)
, and let
S
be a general position set of
D(G)
. If
|S\cap\{u,v,u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\}|\geq 2
, then
|S\cap\{u,v,u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\}|=2
.

Note that Lemma
4.1
in particular implies that if
S
is a general position set of
D(G)
such that
u,u^{\prime}\in S
, then both
u
and
u^{\prime}
are non-adjacent to all other vertices in
S
.

Theorem 4.2
.

If
G
is a graph, then
\mathop{\mathrm{gp}}(G)\leq\mathop{\mathrm{gp}}(D(G))\leq 2\mathop{\mathrm{gp}}(G)
and the bounds are sharp. Moreover,
\mathop{\mathrm{gp}}(D(G))=2\mathop{\mathrm{gp}}(G)
if and only if the
gp
-sets of
D(G)
are of the form
X\cup X^{\prime}
, where
X
is an independent
gp
-set of
G
.

Proof.

If
S
is a gp-set of
G
, then
S\subset V(D(G))
is a general position set of
D(G)
. Hence
\mathop{\mathrm{gp}}(G)\leq\mathop{\mathrm{gp}}(D(G))
. Let now
S
be a gp-set of
D(G)
. Since
G
and
G^{\prime}
are isometric subgraphs of
D(G)
, we infer that
S\cap V(G)
is a general position set of
G
and
S\cap V(G^{\prime})
is a general position set of
G^{\prime}
. Hence

\mathop{\mathrm{gp}}(D(G))=|S|=|S\cap V(G)|+|S\cap V(G^{\prime})|\leq\mathop{\mathrm{gp}}(G)+\mathop{\mathrm{gp}}(G^{\prime})=2\mathop{\mathrm{gp}}(G)\,,

establishing the upper bound.

To see that the lower bound is sharp, note that
\mathop{\mathrm{gp}}(D(K_{n}))=n
holds for all
n\geq 2
by Lemma
4.1
.

Assume now that
\mathop{\mathrm{gp}}(D(G))=2\mathop{\mathrm{gp}}(G)
and consider an arbitrary gp-set
S
of
D(G)
. As we already observed,
S\cap V(G)
is a general position set of
G
and
S\cap V(G^{\prime})
is a general position set of
G^{\prime}
, therefore
S\cap V(G)
is a gp-set of
G
and
S\cap V(G^{\prime})
a gp-set of
G^{\prime}
. If
(S\cap V(G))^{\prime}\neq S\cap V(G^{\prime})
, then we may assume without loss of generality that there exists a vertex
u\in S
such that
u^{\prime}\notin S
. But then an application of Lemma
2.2
yields a general position set in
G^{\prime}
larger that
\mathop{\mathrm{gp}}(G^{\prime})=\mathop{\mathrm{gp}}(G)
, a contradiction. Hence
S=(S\cap V(G))\cup(S\cap V(G))^{\prime}
. Moreover, by Lemma
4.1
,
S\cap V(G)
must be an independent set and we are done.
∎

There are many graphs admitting independent gp-sets which in turn explicitly demonstrate that the upper bound of Theorem
3.2
is sharp. This is in particular the case for paths
P_{n}
,
n\geq 3
, and for cycles
C_{n}
,
n\geq 6
. Hence by Theorem
4.2
we get
\mathop{\mathrm{gp}}(D(P_{n}))=4
,
n\geq 3
, and
\mathop{\mathrm{gp}}(D(C_{n}))=6
,
n\geq 6
. More on independent general position sets can be found in
[
25
]
.

Another family of graphs for which the lower bound in Theorem
4.2
is sharp are the edge deleted complete graphs
K_{n}^{-}
,
n\geq 5
, that is,
K_{n}^{-}
is the graph obtained from
K_{n}
by deleting one of its edges. Note first that since
D(K_{n}^{-})
contain a clique of order
n-1
we have
\mathop{\mathrm{gp}}(D(K_{n}^{-}))\geq n-1
. Let
u
and
v
be the non-adjacent pair of vertices in
K_{n}^{-}
and let
S
be a general position set of
D(K_{n}^{-})
. If
w,w^{\prime}\in S
, where
w\neq u,v
, then
S=\{w,w^{\prime}\}
. Assume hence that for each
w\neq u,v
, the set
S
contains at most one vertex among
w
and
w^{\prime}
. If
|S|\geq n
, then we must have that
|S\cap\{u,u^{\prime},v,v^{\prime}\}|\geq 2
. However, as soon as this is fulfilled we can infer that in each possible case we have
S\subseteq\{u,v,u^{\prime},v^{\prime}\}
. We conclude that

Mutual-visibility in Mycielskian graphs

The general position number of Mycielskian graphs was investigated in

26

We find the exact value of the mutual-visibility number of Mycielskian graph of paths, cycles and graphs with universal vertices. Bounds of mutual-visibility number of Mycielskian graph of graphs having diameter at most three in terms of outer mutual-visibility and mutual-visibility of the graph are also presented.

.

|S|\leq n+\mu(G)+1
. Assume second that
S\cap V(G)
is not a mutual-visibility set of
G
. Then there exists
u,v\in S\cap V(G)
such that
u
and
v
are not mutually visible in
G
but are mutually visible in
M(G)
. Denoting by
I_{G}[u,v]
the set of all vertices that lie on shortest
u,v
-paths in
G
, we then have
x\in I_{G}[u,v]
such that
x\in S
but
x^{\prime}\notin S
. If
(S\setminus\{x\})\cap V(G)
is a mutual-visibility set of
G
then
|S\cap V(G)|\leq\mu(G)+1
. Hence,
|S|\leq n+\mu(G)+1
. If
(S\setminus\{x\})\cap V(G)
is not a mutual-visibility set of
G
then proceed as above, that is, at each step we detect a vertex
y^{\prime}\in V(G^{\prime})\setminus S
corresponding to a vertex
y\in S
. Therefore,
\mu(M(G))\leq n+\mu(G)+1
.

Assume now that
\mu(M(G))=n+\mu(G)+1
and suppose by way of contradiction that
S
be a
\mu
-set of
M(G)
such that
v^{*}\notin S
. Then
|S\cap V(G)|=\mu(G)+k
, for some
k\geq 1
. It follows that
|S\cap V(G^{\prime})|=n(G)-k+1
. Using a parallel argument as above, we are now going to show that
|S\cap V(G^{\prime})|\leq n(G)-k
, which becomes a contradiction. Since
|S\cap V(G)|
is not a mutual-visibility set of
G
, there exists
u,v\in S\cap V(G)
such that
u
and
v
are not mutually visible in
G
but are mutually visible in
M(G)
. Then there exists
x\in I_{G}[u,v]
such that
x\in S
but
x^{\prime}\notin S
. If
k\neq 1
then
(S\cap V(G))\setminus\{x\}
is still not a mutual-visibility set of
G
and hence the above process can be repeated. Thus there exists distinct vertices
x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{k}^{\prime}
that are not in
S\cap V(G^{\prime})
and hence
|S\cap V(G^{\prime})|\leq n(G)-k
.
∎

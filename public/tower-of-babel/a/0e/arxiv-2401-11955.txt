Introduction

There is a well-developed theory of approximation by weighted polynomials
w(z)^{n}p_{n}(z)
in one complex variable where
w:K\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a
nonnegative weight function on a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}
(or a closed set
K
with additional hypotheses on
w
). This theory is highly developed for certain subsets of the real line where a major issue is in finding the support
S_{w}
of the weighted equilibrium measure and its relation to uniform approximation of continuous functions, c.f.,
[
15
]
Chapter VI. Pritsker and Varga
[
13
]
considered the case of a “holomorphic weight”
W(z)
in the sense that they studied approximation by weighted holomorphic polynomials
W(z)^{n}p_{n}(z)
. Precisely, let
G
be an open set in
\mathbb{C}
and
W
be a non-vanishing holomorphic function in
G
.

Definition 1.1
.

The pair
(G,W)
is said to have the
approximation property
if any
f
holomorphic in
G
can be locally uniformly approximated in
G
by weighted holomorphic polynomials; i.e., for any
K
compact in
G
, there exist a sequence
\{P_{n}\}
of polynomials
P_{n}\in\mathbb{C}[z]
with
\mbox{{\rm deg} }P_{n}\leq n
(we write
P_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
) such that

\|f-W^{n}P_{n}\|_{K}:=\sup_{z\in K}|f(z)-W(z)^{n}P_{n}(z)|\to 0.

As pointed out in
[
13
]
, for
W\equiv 1
,
G
should be a finite or countable disjoint union of simply connected domains, and thus they only considered such
G
. Let
Q:=-\log|W|
. We state the main result of
[
13
]
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
13
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

The pair
(G,W)
has the approximation property if and only if there exists a probability measure
\mu
supported on
\partial G
and a real constant
F
such that

U^{\mu}(z)+Q(z)=F,\quad z\in G.

Here, for a measure
\nu
,

U^{\nu}(z):=\int\log\frac{1}{|z-\zeta|}d\nu(\zeta)

is the logarithmic potential of
\nu
.
If
W
is continuous on
\overline{G}
, the measure
\mu
in Theorem
1.2
is the solution
\mu^{\overline{G},Q}
of the weighted equilibrium problem on
\overline{G}
with weight
Q
(see section 2). Thus the approximation property implies that
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits\mu^{\overline{G},Q}\subset\partial G
.

Many interesting classes of examples are given in their paper. For example, if
W(z)=z
, for a disk
G=D(a,r)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-a|<r\}
centered at a point
a
on the positive real axis, the pair
(G,W)
has the approximation property if and only if
r<a/3
.

In this paper, we further develop the theory of weighted holomorphic polynomial approximation. Our first main goal, in the next section, is to give a type of quantitative version of Theorem
1.2
in the spirit of Bernstein-Walsh (Theorem
2.4
). For
W
entire,
K\subset\mathbb{C}
compact,
R<0
, and
Q:=-\log|W|
, define

K_{R}=\{z\in\mathbb{C},~U^{\mu^{K,Q}}(z)+Q(z)-F_{K,Q}>R\}

(see section
2
for this (standard) notation in weighted potential theory). For
f
continuous on
K
, define, for
n=1,2,...
,

d_{n}^{W}(f,K):=\inf\{\|f-W^{n}p_{n}\|_{K}:p_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}\}.

Theorem
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}
be a compact subset of a domain
G
with
\mathbb{C}\setminus K
connected such that
(G,W)
is an approximation pair.
Let
f
be a function continuous in
K
, and let
R<0
.
Then

\limsup_{n}d_{n}^{W}(f,K)^{1/n}\leq e^{R}.

(1.1)

if and only if
f
is the restriction to
K
of a function holomorphic in the connected component of
K_{R}
that contains
K
.

The technique of proof of the “if” direction follows that of
[
13
]
: we use polynomial interpolation of
f/W^{n}
at
n-
th order weighted Fekete points
of
K
and the Hermite remainder formula. For the “only if” direction, the standard proof considering a telescoping series
p_{0}+\sum_{n}(W^{n}p_{n}-W^{n-1}p_{n-1})

where
\{W^{n}p_{n}\}
are asymptotically optimal approximators on
K
to
f
requires some care as the difference
W^{n}p_{n}-W^{n-1}p_{n-1}
is not a weighted holomorphic polynomial. A special case of this result was stated as Theorem 3.4 in
[
12
]
(see the next paragraph). There is a slight gap in their proof of the “only if” direction (in applying their Corollary 4.2) but
their result remains valid using our proof.

The article
[
12
]
considered the case where
W(z)=e^{-z}
and
G
is the “inside” of the Szegő curve

S:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|ze^{1-z}|=1\ \hbox{and}\ |z|\leq 1\}.

Their starting point was work of Szegő
[
19
]
on the asymptotic zero distribution of the normalized
partial sums
\{s_{n}(nz):=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(nz)^{k}/k!\}
of the exponential function. They derived an asymptotic formula for the weighted normalized
partial sums
\{e^{-nz}s_{n}(nz)\}
which led them to a special case of an approximation pair
(G,W)=(G,e^{-z})
. In section 3 we replace
e^{-z}
by
1/(1+z)
and we obtain analogues of the results in
[
12
]
for
G
being the inside of the right loop of the lemniscate

\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z(z+1)|=1/4\}.

There is a third paper of Pritsker of interest. In
[
11
]
, he works on a compact set
E
with
W\in A(E)
, the algebra of continuous functions on
E
which
are holomorphic on the interior
E^{o}
of
E
. Defining
A(E,W)
to be the uniform closure of sequences of weighted holomorphic polynomials
\{W^{n}p_{n}\}
on
E
, in an attempt to determine when a weighted Mergelyan property holds, namely,
A(E,W)=A(E)
he introduces the intermediate algebra
[W(z),zW(z)]
generated by
W(z)
and
zW(z)
; i.e., the uniform closure of bivariate holomorphic polynomials in

W(z)
and
zW(z)
on
E
. Thus

A(E,W)\subset[W(z),zW(z)]\subset A(E).

We make two important observations:

1.

[W(z),zW(z)]
consists of all uniform limits on
E
of
linear combinations
of weighted polynomials; and

2.

[W(z),zW(z)]
contains the constants while this is not necessarily the case for
A(E,W)
.

With respect to this second item, we mention that the proof of the “only if” direction of Theorem
1.2
only uses the hypothesis that the constant function
1
(or any single non-vanishing holomorphic function in
G
) can be locally uniformly approximated by weighted holomorphic polynomials
\{W^{n}p_{n}\}
on
G
. We should mention that Pritsker and Varga also studied the problem of local uniform
rational
approximation of functions holomorphic in
G
by weighted rational functions; see
[
14
]
.

Using these univariate results as motivation, we begin a study of the multivariate situation in section 4. Tom Bloom and his students M. Branker and J. Callaghan
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
proved multivariate analogues of weighted polynomial approximation of continuous functions on certain sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n},\ n\geq 2
for specific symmetric weights using pluripotential theory. At first glance, weighted holomorphic polynomial approximation in several complex variables appears unnatural. However, in considering multivariate generalizations of the algebras [
W(z),zW(z)]
and
A(E)
, something can be said.

Given a bounded, pseudoconvex domain
G\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we say
G
has the
Mergelyan property
if each function in the algebra
A(\overline{G})
of continuous functions on
\overline{G}
which are holomorphic on
G
can be uniformly approximated on
\overline{G}
by functions that are holomorphic on a neighborhood of
\overline{G}
. In particular, this holds if
A(\overline{G})
coincides with the uniform algebra
P(\overline{G})
; i.e., uniform limits of holomorphic polynomials on
\overline{G}
. Necessary and sufficient conditions so that
G
has the Mergelyan property
are not known. Following
[
20
]
, we state a result giving sufficient conditions on a finite family of functions in
A(\overline{G})
to be a set of generators of
A(\overline{G})
for special
G
(Theorem
4.1
). As a corollary, for certain weights
W
on
G\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
we will have an analogue of “
[W(z),zW(z)]=A(\overline{G})
” but not necessarily “
A(\overline{G},W)=[W(z),zW(z)]
”. In section 5 we give some explicit examples of generators of
A(\overline{G})
.

2
Some results in one variable

Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}
be closed and let
w
be a nonnegative, upper semicontinuous function with

\{z\in K:w(z)>0\}
nonpolar. If
K
is unbounded, we require
|z|w(z)\to 0\ \hbox{as}\ |z|\to\infty,\ z\in K.
Let
Q:=-\log w
. For a probability measure
\tau
supported in
K
(we write
\tau\in\mathcal{M}(K)
), the logarithmic energy is
I(\tau):=\int_{K}\int_{K}\log\frac{1}{|z-t|}d\tau(t)d\tau(z)
and we define the weighted energy

I^{w}(\tau):=\int_{K}\int_{K}\log\frac{1}{|z-t|w(z)w(t)}d\tau(t)d\tau(z)=I(\tau)+2\int_{K}Qd\tau.

Then there exists a unique
\mu^{K,Q}\in\mathcal{M}(K)
with

I^{w}(\mu^{K,Q})=\inf_{\tau\in\mathcal{M}(K)}I^{w}(\tau)=:V_{w}.

Let
S_{w}
denote the support of
\mu^{K,Q}
.
Setting

F_{K,Q}:=I^{w}(\mu^{K,Q})-\int_{K}Qd\mu^{K,Q}=V_{w}-\int_{K}Qd\mu^{K,Q},

the measure
\mu^{K,Q}
is characterized by the Frostman inequalities,

\displaystyle U^{\mu^{K,Q}}(z)+Q(z)

\displaystyle\geq F_{K,Q},\qquad\text{q.e.}~z\in K,

(2.1)

\displaystyle U^{\mu^{K,Q}}(z)+Q(z)

\displaystyle\leq F_{K,Q},\qquad z\in S_{w}

(2.2)

where q.e. means except for a set of capacity zero. Moreover, we have

V_{K,Q}^{*}=-U^{\mu^{K,Q}}+F_{K,Q}

where

V_{K,Q}(z)=\sup\{\frac{1}{degp}\log|p(z)|:p\in\cup_{n}\mathcal{P}_{n},\ ||w^{degp}p||_{K}\leq 1\}

and
V_{K,Q}^{*}(z):=\limsup_{\zeta\to z}V_{K,Q}(\zeta)
. We write
V_{K}
if
Q\equiv 0
.

We say
K
is regular if
V_{K}=V_{K}^{*}
; i.e.,
V_{K}
is continuous. If
K
is regular and
Q
is continuous, then
V_{K,Q}
is continuous. For simplicity, we
will assume our compact sets are regular. For a sequence of weighted monic Fekete polynomials
F_{n}
with respect to
K,Q
, i.e.,
F_{n}(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-t_{j}^{(n)})
where
t_{1}^{(n)},...,t_{n}^{(n)}\in K
attain the supremum in

\sup_{z_{1},...,z_{n}\in K}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|z_{i}-z_{j}|w(z_{i})w(z_{j}),

it is known that

\lim_{n}\|w^{n}F_{n}\|^{1/n}_{K}=e^{-F_{K,Q}}\ \hbox{and}\ \frac{1}{n}\log|F_{n}(z)|\to-U^{\mu^{K,Q}}(z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus K

(2.3)

where this latter convergence is locally uniform (
[
15
]
p. 150). Furthermore, for
P_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
we have a weighted Bernstein-Walsh inequality (
[
15
]
p. 153) :

|P_{n}(z)|\leq\|w^{n}P_{n}\|_{S_{w}}e^{n(-U^{\mu^{K,Q}}(z)+F_{K,Q})},\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}.

(2.4)

Let
G
be an open set in
\mathbb{C}
and let
W
be a non-vanishing holomorphic function in
G
. In this setting,
w:=|W|
and
Q:=-\log w=-\log|W|
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(G,W)
be a pair with the approximation property, and let
K
be a regular compact subset of
G
. Then

U^{\mu^{K,Q}}(z)+Q(z)=F_{K,Q},\quad z\in K,

(2.5)

and

U^{\mu^{K,Q}}(z)+Q(z)<F_{K,Q},\quad z\in\overline{G}\setminus K.

(2.6)

Proof.

For the first part, we have

Q(z)\geq V_{K,Q}(z),~z\in K,\quad\text{ and }\quad V_{K,Q}(z)\geq V_{\overline{G},Q}(z)=Q(z),~z\in G,

hence, for
z\in K
,

V_{K,Q}(z)=Q(z),\quad\text{or }\quad U^{\mu^{K,Q}}(z)+Q(z)=F_{K,Q}.

For the second part, we consider the function
U^{\mu^{\overline{G},Q}}-U^{\mu^{K,Q}}
which is superharmonic outside of
K
and vanishes at infinity. Note
U^{\mu^{K,Q}}
restricted to
\partial K
is continuous and hence it is continuous on
\partial K
as a function in
G
. We have

U^{\mu^{\overline{G},Q}}(z)-U^{\mu^{K,Q}}(z)=[F_{\overline{G},Q}-Q(z)]-[F_{K,Q}-Q(z)]=F_{\overline{G},Q}-F_{K,Q},\quad z\in\partial K,

hence, by the minimum principle for superharmonic functions applied on the complement of
K
,

U^{\mu^{\overline{G},Q}}(z)-U^{\mu^{K,Q}}(z)\geq F_{\overline{G},Q}-F_{K,Q},\quad z\in\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus K.

If equality would hold somewhere in
\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus K
, then the function
U^{\mu^{\overline{G},Q}}-U^{\mu^{K,Q}}
would be constant, hence equal to
0
. As this is not the case (e.g.,
\mu^{\overline{G},Q}
is supported on
\partial G
),

U^{\mu^{\overline{G},Q}}(z)-U^{\mu^{K,Q}}(z)>F_{\overline{G},Q}-F_{K,Q},\quad z\in\overline{\mathbb{C}}\setminus K.

On
\overline{G}\setminus K
, we get

-Q(z)-U^{\mu^{K,Q}}(z)>-F_{K,Q},\qquad z\in\overline{G}\setminus K,

or

U^{\mu^{K,Q}}(z)+Q(z)<F_{K,Q},\qquad z\in\overline{G}\setminus K.

∎

Remark 2.2
.

From Lemma
2.1
follows that if
(G,W)
is an approximation pair then, for any open subset
G^{\prime}\subset G
,
(G^{\prime},W)
is also an approximation pair (since, by the above lemma, Theorem
1.2
applies).

We will need the following result (cf.,
[
1
]
, Lemma 7.3).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
K
be a compact set and
Q_{j}
a sequence of weights on
K
with
Q_{j}\uparrow Q
. Then

\lim_{j}V_{K,Q_{j}}(z)=V_{K,Q}(z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}.

If
K
is regular and
Q_{j}
and
Q
are continuous on
K
, then the convergence is locally uniform.

As in the introduction, for
f
continuous on
K
, define

d_{n}^{W}(f,K):=\inf\{\|f-W^{n}p_{n}\|_{K}:p_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{n}\},

and for
R<0
, we define

K_{R}=\{z\in\mathbb{C},~U^{\mu^{K,Q}}(z)+Q(z)-F_{K,Q}>R\},

which always contains
K
.
The boundary of
K_{R}
is denoted by

E_{R}=\{z\in\mathbb{C},~U^{\mu^{K,Q}}(z)+Q(z)-F_{K,Q}=R\}.

(2.7)

In the next theorem, we assume that the weight
W
is an entire function. This implies, in particular, that the function
zW(z)
is unbounded in
\mathbb{C}
, and thus
U^{\mu^{K,Q}}(z)+Q(z)
is unbounded below (note
U^{\mu^{K,Q}}(z)
behaves like
-\log|z|
near infinity), so that, for any
R<0
, the level set
E_{R}
is not empty.
Also, without loss of generality, we normalize
W
so that
|W|\leq 1
on
K
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}
be a compact subset of a domain
G
with
\mathbb{C}\setminus K
connected such that
(G,W)
is an approximation pair.
Let
f
be a function continuous in
K
, and let
R<0
.
Then

\limsup_{n}d_{n}^{W}(f,K)^{1/n}\leq e^{R}.

(2.8)

if and only if
f
is the restriction to
K
of a function holomorphic in the connected component of
K_{R}
that contains
K
.

Proof.

Denote by
K_{R}^{(K)}
the component of
K_{R}
that contains
K
, and by
E_{R}^{(K)}
its boundary.
Assume
f
is holomorphic in
K_{R}^{(K)}
. We consider the interpolants
P_{n}(z)
of
f(z)/W(z)^{n}
at the weighted Fekete points on
K
.
For
F_{n}
the
n-
th order monic Fekete polynomial associated to
K,Q
, for
z\in K
, and some
R^{\prime}
with
R<R^{\prime}<0
, we have, by Hermite’s remainder formula,

f(z)-W(z)^{n}P_{n}(z)=\frac{W(z)^{n}}{2i\pi}\int_{E_{R^{\prime}}^{(K)}}\frac{F_{n}(z)}{F_{n}(\xi)}\frac{f(\xi)}{W(\xi)^{n}}\frac{d\xi}{\xi-z}.

From the asymptotics of
W^{n}F_{n}
in (
2.3
), for
z\in K
and
\xi\in E_{R^{\prime}}^{(K)}
, we have

\frac{|W(z)^{n}F_{n}(z)|}{|W(\xi)^{n}F_{n}(\xi)|}\asymp\exp[n\bigl(-F_{K,Q}-(-F_{K,Q}-R^{\prime})\bigr)]=e^{nR^{\prime}}.

Since
|f|
is bounded on
K
and
|\xi-z|
is bounded below for
z\in K
and
\xi\in E_{R^{\prime}}^{(K)}
,

\limsup_{n}||f-W^{n}P_{n}||_{K}^{1/n}\leq e^{R^{\prime}}.

Letting
R^{\prime}
decrease to
R
gives (
2.8
).

Conversely, assume (
2.8
) holds true, that is, there exists a sequence of polynomials
p_{n}
such that

\limsup_{n}\|f-W^{n}p_{n}\|_{K}^{1/n}\leq e^{R}.

We choose any
R<R_{1}<0
and show that
f
is holomorphic in
K_{R_{1}}^{(K)}
, which is sufficient.
We adapt the usual proof of the indirect implication in the Bernstein-Walsh theorem. Thus we want to show that the series

p_{0}+\sum_{n}(W^{n}p_{n}-W^{n-1}p_{n-1})

converges uniformly in
K_{R_{1}}
. For given
\epsilon>0
and
\rho<0
, let
q_{\epsilon n}
be a sequence of polynomials of degree
O(\epsilon n)
such that

\limsup_{n}\|W-q_{\epsilon n}\|_{K_{R_{1}}}^{1/n}\leq e^{\rho}.

(2.9)

The usual Bernstein-Walsh theorem shows that such a sequence exists (recall that
W
is assumed to be an entire function).
We have

\|W^{n}p_{n}-W^{n-1}p_{n-1}\|_{K_{R_{1}}}\leq\|W^{n-1}(q_{\epsilon n}p_{n}-p_{n-1})\|_{K_{R_{1}}}+\|(W-q_{\epsilon n})W^{n-1}p_{n}\|_{K_{R_{1}}}.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
R_{1}
is such that
W
does not vanish on
E_{R_{1}}
.
Thus, we may multiply the upper bound by
W
to get

\|W^{n}(q_{\epsilon n}p_{n}-p_{n-1})\|_{K_{R_{1}}}+\|(W-q_{\epsilon n})W^{n}p_{n}\|_{K_{R_{1}}}.

Because of (
2.4
) and (
2.9
), the second norm decreases geometrically to zero as soon as
\rho<R_{1}
.
We next consider the first norm. Since
|W|\leq 1
on
K
, we have
Q\geq 0
on
K
. We define the weights

Q_{\epsilon}=(1+\epsilon)^{-1}Q\uparrow Q\quad\text{as}\quad\epsilon\downarrow 0.

By Lemma
2.3
, for any
R_{2}<R_{1}
, there exists
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small so that

U^{\mu^{Q_{\epsilon},K}}(z)+Q_{\epsilon}(z)-F_{K,Q_{\epsilon}}\geq R_{2},\quad z\in K_{R_{1}}.

(2.10)

Since
q_{\epsilon n}p_{n}-p_{n-1}
is of degree
n+O(\epsilon n)\sim(1+\epsilon)n
and

W^{n}(q_{\epsilon n}p_{n}-p_{n-1})=(W^{1/(1+\epsilon)})^{(1+\epsilon)n}(q_{\epsilon n}p_{n}-p_{n-1}),

we get by using the weighted Bernstein-Walsh inequality (
2.4
) with weight
W^{1/(1+\epsilon)}
, along with (
2.10
), that

\displaystyle\|W^{n}(q_{\epsilon n}p_{n}-p_{n-1})\|_{K_{R_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-(1+\epsilon)nR_{2}}\|W^{n}(q_{\epsilon n}p_{n}-p_{n-1})\|_{K}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-(1+\epsilon)nR_{2}}\left(\|W^{n+1}p_{n}-W^{n}p_{n-1}\|_{K}+\|W^{n}(q_{n}-W)p_{n}\|_{K}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\|W\|_{K}e^{n(-(1+\epsilon)R_{2}+R)}+2\|f\|_{K}e^{n(-(1+\epsilon)R_{2}+\rho)}.

Hence, we see that three inequalities need to be satisfied, namely,

R<(1+\epsilon)R_{2},\qquad\rho<(1+\epsilon)R_{2},\qquad\rho<R_{1}.

The first one can be satisfied by taking
R_{2}
sufficiently close to
R_{1}
and
\epsilon
small. The second one can also be satisfied because
\rho
can be chosen as any large negative number (since
W
is an entire function). Finally, the second inequality implies the third one since
(1+\epsilon)R_{2}<R_{1}
.
∎

Remark 2.5

Let

3
An analogue of the Szegő curve and a related approximation pair

In this section, we state some properties for an analogue of the classical Szegő curve
[

, where we replace the exponential function

with the rational function

. As we will see in Theorem
3.2
, this special curve, denoted by
\mathcal{L}_{+}
, consists of the right loop of the lemniscate of degree 2,

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{+}\cup\mathcal{L}_{-}=\{z\in\mathbb{C},~|z(z+1)|=1/4\},

where
\mathcal{L}_{+}
(resp.
\mathcal{L}_{-}
) denotes the loop around 0 (resp. around
-1
), see Fig.

be the bounded domain with boundary
\mathcal{L}_{+}
.

The lemniscate
4|z(z+1)|=1

We prove that the pair
(G,W)
with
W(z)=1+z

form a
maximal
approximation pair, see 4) of Theorem
3.2
. For that, we will consider the weighted equilibrium measure
\mu^{\overline{G},Q}
with
Q(z)=-\log|1+z|
. Note that, for
z\in\mathcal{L}_{+}
,

\log\frac{1}{|z|}+Q(z)=\log 4\quad\iff\quad U^{\widehat{\delta}_{0}}(z)+Q(z)=\log 4,

(3.1)

where
\widehat{\delta}_{0}
is the balayage of
\delta_{0}
onto
\mathcal{L}_{+}
. Hence, by the minimum principle for superharmonic functions,
U^{\widehat{\delta}_{0}}(z)+Q(z)\geq\log 4
,

, which implies that
\mu^{\overline{G},Q}=\widehat{\delta}_{0}
and
F_{\overline{G},Q}=\log 4
. Moreover, (

) implies that
U^{\widehat{\delta}_{0}}
is continuous as a function restricted to the support of
\widehat{\delta}_{0}
, hence is continuous everywhere. Thus, by applying the maximum principle to the function
U^{\widehat{\delta}_{0}}+Q
, harmonic in
G
, continuous in
\overline{G}
, we get that

U^{\widehat{\delta}_{0}}(z)+Q(z)=\log 4,\qquad z\in\overline{G}.

(3.2)

The following lemma is elementary but will be useful in the proof of our theorem. Since we have not found a reference in the literature, we give a proof.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f
be a function holomorphic in the unit disk
D:=\{z:|z|<1\}
. Then Taylor’s formula with integral remainder holds true in
D
, namely, for any
z\in D
,

f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{f^{(k)}(0)}{k!}z^{k}+\frac{1}{n!}\int_{\gamma}f^{(n+1)}(t)(z-t)^{n}dt,

(3.3)

where
\gamma
is any path from 0 to
z
in
D
.

Proof.

First we note that (
3.3
) holds true with
\gamma=[0,z]
. Indeed, it suffices to apply the usual Taylor’s formula to the function of a real variable
s\in[0,1]\mapsto f(sz)
and to make the change of variable
t=sz
, see also
[
21
, §7.1 p.125]
. Now, the segment
[0,z]
can be deformed to any contour from
0
to
z
in
D
and the formula still holds since each function
t\mapsto f^{(n+1)}(t)(z-t)^{n}
is holomorphic. ∎

Finally, for a compact set
K
, and a function
f
defined on
K
, we define the essential sup of
f
on
K
,

\|f\|_{K}^{*}=\inf\{L:~|f(z)|\leq L~\text{ q.e.\ on }K\}.

Let
s_{n}((1+z)^{-n})
denote the

(1+z)^{-n}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

The following hold:

1) As

(3.4)

2) Let
\widetilde{s}_{n}((1+z)^{-n})
be the constant multiple of
s_{n}((1+z)^{-n})
normalized so that its leading coefficient is 1.
Then, the sequence of monic polynomials
\widetilde{s}_{n}((1+z)^{-n})
is asymptotically extremal for
\overline{G}
(cf.,
[
8

) and the weight

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\|(1+z)^{n}\widetilde{s}_{n}((1+z)^{-n}\right)\|_{\overline{G}}^{*})^{1/n}=\exp(-F_{\overline{G},Q})=\frac{1}{4}.

(3.5)

3) Let

\nu_{n}\to\mu^{\overline{G},Q},\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty.

4) The pair

Theorem 3.4
.

1) Let

, continuous in

. Then, for any compact subset

of
\overline{G}\setminus\{-1/2\}

,

, such that

(3.8)

2) Let
f
be a function holomorphic in
G
, continuous in
\overline{G}
with
f(-1/2)=0
. Then
there exists a sequence of polynomials
\{P_{n}\}
,
\mbox{{\rm deg} }P_{n}\leq n
, such that

\|f(z)-(1+z)^{n}P_{n}(z)\|_{\overline{G}}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

1) We may assume
K
has connected complement. It follows from (

) that the constant function 1 can be approximated as in (

Hence, all monomials

satisfying the assumptions in 1).

4
Several complex variables

In the general setting of a uniform algebra
\mathcal{U}
on a compact Hausdorff space
X
, given a collection of elements

f_{1},...,f_{k}\in\mathcal{U}
, let
[f_{1},...,f_{k}|X]
denote the smallest closed subalgebra of
\mathcal{U}
containing the constants and
f_{1},...,f_{k}
. If
[f_{1},...,f_{k}|X]=\mathcal{U}
, we say that

f_{1},...,f_{k}
are a set of generators for
\mathcal{U}
. Thus, in the previous univariate setting, given
(G,W)
with
W\in A(\overline{G})
and
W\not=0
on
\overline{G}
, with this notation we have

[W,zW]=[W,zW|\overline{G}].

For
G\subset\mathbb{C}
such that
\overline{G}
has connected complement,
A(\overline{G})=P(\overline{G})
by Mergelyan’s theorem. Thus the question of whether
[W,zW|\overline{G}]=A(\overline{G})
reduces to the question of whether
W,zW
are generators of
P(\overline{G})
, and sufficient conditions are given in
[
11
, Proposition 2.5]
. We use this as motivation for a multivariate analogue.

Given
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
compact,

\widehat{K}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|p(z)|\leq||p||_{K}\ \hbox{for all holomorphic polynomials}\ p\}

is the polynomial hull of
K
. If
\widehat{K}=K
we say
K
is polynomially convex. The algebra
P(K)
consists of uniform limits of holomorphic polynomials restricted to
K
. We begin by stating a generalization of Theorem 19.1 of
[
20
]
(c.f., also
[
17
]
). The proof is essentially the same; for the reader’s convenience, we include a sketch.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
G\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded, pseudoconvex domain with the property that
\overline{G}
is polynomially convex and

A(\overline{G})=P(\overline{G})
(in particular,
G
has the Mergelyan property). Let
f_{1},...,f_{k}
(
k\geq n
) be holomorphic in a neighborhood of
\overline{G}
satisfy the following three properties:

1.

f_{1},...,f_{k}
separate points in
\overline{G}
;

2.

the matrix
\bigl[\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial z_{j}}\bigr]
has rank
n
at each point of
\overline{G}
;

3.

for
F:=(f_{1},...,f_{k})
the map from
\overline{G}
to
\mathbb{C}^{k}
, the set
K:=F(\overline{G})
is polynomially convex.

Then the functions
f_{1},...,f_{k}
restricted to
\overline{G}
generate
A(\overline{G})
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we take
k=n
. Since
\overline{G}
is bounded the

are bounded there so we may assume
|f_{j}|\leq 1/2
on
\overline{G}
. For

are holomorphic in
G_{\epsilon^{\prime}}
. From 1., there exists
\epsilon^{\prime\prime}>0

is one-to-one on

. Then from 2., we can fix
\epsilon\leq\min[\epsilon^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime\prime}]
so that
\bigl[\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial z_{j}}\bigr]
has rank
n
at each point of
G_{\epsilon}
and hence for each

,
F
is a biholomorphic mapping on a neighborhood of
z^{o}
in
G_{\epsilon}
.

Given

Q_{a}
such that

Q_{a}(F(a))>\max_{\overline{G}}|Q_{a}(F)|.

Define
g_{a}:=Q_{a}(F)
. Note that
g_{a}\in[f_{1},...,f_{n}|\overline{G}]
and we have
|g_{a}(a)|>||g_{a}||_{\overline{G}}
. By rescaling, we can assume
|g_{a}(a)|>1>||g_{a}||_{\overline{G}}
and then
by replacing
g_{a}
by a power

we can assume

|g_{a}(a)|>2\ \hbox{and}\ ||g_{a}||_{\overline{G}}<1/2.

Thus there exists a neighborhood
N_{a}
of

in
G_{\epsilon}
on which
|g_{a}|>2
. We do this construction for each
a\in\overline{G}_{3\epsilon/4}\setminus G_{\epsilon/2}
; the corresponding neighborhoods
N_{a}
cover this compact set. Take a finite subcover
N_{a_{1}},...,N_{a_{s}}
and consider the corresponding functions

g_{a_{1}},...,g_{a_{s}}\in[f_{1},...,f_{n}|\overline{G}]
.

Define the mapping
\Phi:G_{3\epsilon/4}\to\mathbb{C}^{n+s}
via

\Phi(z):=(F(z),g_{a_{1}}(z),...,g_{a_{s}}(z))=(f_{1}(z),...,f_{n}(z),g_{a_{1}}(z),...,g_{a_{s}}(z)).

Since
F
is one-to-one on
G_{3\epsilon/4}
, so is
\Phi
. Let
\Delta
be the open unit polydisk in
\mathbb{C}^{n+s}
and define the non-empty set

V:=\Phi(G_{3\epsilon/4})\cap\Delta.

Following, mutatis mutandis, the argument on p. 133 of
[
20
]
, it follows that
V
is an analytic subvariety of
\Delta
. (To see this, we first show that
V
is relatively closed in
\Delta
. Take a sequence
\{p_{n}\}\subset V
with
p_{n}\to p\in\Delta
. We claim
p_{n}=\Phi(z^{(n)})
where
z^{(n)}\in G_{\epsilon/2}
. For if
z^{(n)}\in G_{3\epsilon/4}\setminus G_{\epsilon/2}
then
z^{(n)}\in N_{a_{j}}
for some
j\in\{1,...,s\}
. But then
|g_{a_{j}}(z^{(n)})|>2
contradicting
\Phi(z^{(n)})\in\Delta
. Thus
z^{(n)}\in G_{\epsilon/2}
and hence there exists a limit point
z
of
\{z^{(n)}\}
with
z\in\overline{G}_{\epsilon/2}
. Without loss of generality,
z^{(n)}\to z
and hence
\Phi(z^{(n)})\to\Phi(z)
; thus
p=\Phi(z)
and
p\in V
. It remains to show
V
is locally defined by holomorphic functions. For
p\in V
, we have
p=\Phi(z^{o})
for some
z^{o}\in G_{3\epsilon/4}
. Since
F
is biholomorphic in a neighborhood of
z^{o}
, each coordinate function
z_{\alpha}
is a holomorphic function of
f_{1},...,f_{n}
and hence each component of
\Phi
is a holomorphic function of
f_{1},...,f_{n}
in a neighborhood of
z^{o}
. Thus
V
is defined locally near
p
by holomorphic functions.)

Now for each
j=1,...,n
define the function
Z_{j}
on
V
as

Z_{j}(p):=z_{j}(\Phi^{-1}(p)).

These functions are holomorphic on
V
. A standard extension result allows us to find
H_{j}
holomorphic in
\Delta
with
H_{j}=Z_{j}
on
V
(e.g., the Proposition on p. 132
[
20
]
). Now

\Phi(\overline{G})\subset\Phi(G_{3\epsilon/4})
and
\Phi(\overline{G})
is compact. Since each
|f_{k}|,|g_{a_{l}}|\leq 1/2
on
\overline{G}
,
\Phi(\overline{G})\subset\Delta
and

\Phi(\overline{G})
is compact in
V
. Thus if we fix
j\in\{1,...,n\}
, the Taylor series at the origin of
H_{j}
in
\Delta
converges uniformly on
\Phi(\overline{G})
. Taking
partial sums and returning to
\overline{G}
, we get a sequence of polynomials in the functions
f_{1},...,f_{n},g_{a_{1}},...,g_{a_{s}}
which converge uniformly on
\overline{G}
to

H_{j}(\Phi)
. But on
V
,
H_{j}=Z_{j}
so

H_{j}(\Phi)=Z_{j}(\Phi)=z_{j},\ j=1,...,n\ \hbox{on}\ \overline{G}.

As observed earlier, each
g_{a_{k}}\in[f_{1},...,f_{n}|\overline{G}]
so we conclude that for
j=1,...,n
,

z_{j}\in[f_{1},...,f_{n}|\overline{G}].

Thus
P(\overline{G})\subset[f_{1},...,f_{n}|\overline{G}]
. Since
[f_{1},...,f_{n}|\overline{G}]\subset A(\overline{G})
and
A(\overline{G})=P(\overline{G})
, we are done.

∎

Note that one simply strengthens the first two of the necessary conditions (1) - (3) on page 131 of
[
20
]
to require more regularity of
f_{1},...,f_{k}
at the boundary. Indeed, if one is interested solely in the algebra
P(\overline{G})
, for general bounded

\overline{G}
is polynomially convex, the proof above is valid to show
[f_{1},...,f_{k}|\overline{G}]=P(\overline{G})
(note by the Oka-Weil theorem,
f
holomorphic in a neighborhood of
\overline{G}
implies
f\in P(\overline{G})
).

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
G\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded domain with the property that
\overline{G}
is polynomially convex. Let
f_{1},...,f_{k}
(
k\geq n
) be holomorphic in a neighborhood of
\overline{G}
satisfy the following three properties:

1.

f_{1},...,f_{k}
separate points in
\overline{G}
;

2.

the matrix
\bigl[\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial z_{j}}\bigr]
has rank
n
at each point of
\overline{G}
;

3.

for
F:=(f_{1},...,f_{k})
,
K:=F(\overline{G})
is polynomially convex (as a subset of
\mathbb{C}^{k}
).

Then the functions
f_{1},...,f_{k}
restricted to
\overline{G}
generate
P(\overline{G})
.

In particular, one could apply this to polynomials
f_{1},...,f_{k}
.

Remark 4.3
.

Domains
G\subset\mathbb{C}^{n},\ n>1
for which
A(\overline{G})=P(\overline{G})
include bounded, strictly pseudoconvex domains with smooth boundary

G\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}

are also polynomially convex (
[

, p. 402). If we begin with such a domain and
f_{1},...,f_{k}
holomorphic in a neighborhood
U
of
\overline{G}
satisfying condition 3. of Corollary
4.2
and conditions 1. and 2. on the neighborhood
U
, then for sufficiently small

perturbations
\overline{G}^{\prime}\subset U
of
\overline{G}
we can insure that
\overline{G}^{\prime}
and
K^{\prime}:=F(\overline{G}^{\prime})
are polynomially convex. Hence the same
f_{1},...,f_{k}
generate
P(\overline{G}^{\prime})
.

Product domains
G=G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{n}
where
G_{i}\subset\mathbb{C}
is bounded with
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{G}_{i}
connected (so that
A(\overline{G}_{i})=P(\overline{G}_{i})
) also satisfy

,

.

Thus, working in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with coordinates
(z_{1},...,z_{n})
, given
W=W(z_{1},...,z_{n})
holomorphic on
\overline{G}
, it seems natural to consider the algebra

[W,z_{j_{1}}W,...,z_{j_{n-1}}W|\overline{G}]

for
n-1
of the coordinate functions
z_{j_{1}},...,z_{j_{n-1}}
. This algebra contains certain linear combinations of weighted polynomials
W^{n}p_{n}(z_{1},...,z_{n})
.
For example, in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, consider the algebra generated by
W
and
z_{1}W
. Given a polynomial
Q\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},z_{2}]
, say of degree

, so that

Q(z_{1},z_{2})=\sum_{j,k\geq 0,\ j+k\leq m}a_{jk}z_{1}^{j}z_{2}^{k},

we have

Q(W,z_{1}W)=\sum_{j,k\geq 0,\ j+k\leq m}a_{jk}W^{j}(z_{1}W)^{k}=\sum_{j,k\geq 0,\ j+k\leq m}a_{jk}W^{j+k}z_{1}^{k}.

However, although
[W,z_{j_{1}}W,...,z_{j_{n-1}}W|\overline{G}]\subset A(\overline{G})
, unlike the univariate case, we do not necessarily have

A(\overline{G},W)\subset[W,z_{j_{1}}W,...,z_{j_{n-1}}W|\overline{G}].

(4.1)

Indeed, if
W=W(z_{j_{1}},...,z_{j_{n-1}})
and
p_{n}
depends on the missing variable, clearly
W^{n}p_{n}\not\in[W,z_{j_{1}}W,...,z_{j_{n-1}}W|\overline{G}]
. In
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, for example,

W(z_{1})^{n}z_{2}^{n}\not\in[W,z_{1}W|\overline{G}]
as

is independent of

. Of course these examples are quite special; and, in the case where we do have the inclusion (

In the next section, we apply Theorem

.

5
Examples in
\mathbb{C}^{2}

The biggest obstacle to overcome in order to apply Theorem
4.1
to conclude the equality
[W,z_{j_{1}}W,...,z_{j_{n-1}}W|\overline{G}]=A(\overline{G})
is
3.:
K:=F(\overline{G})
is polynomially convex. In this section, we give some explicit examples where this can be verified; for simplicity, we work in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

We begin with some preliminaries on polynomial convexity. Note that the function
F
constructed in Theorem
4.1
was a biholomorphic map on a neighborhood of
\overline{G}
. Unfortunately, the image of a polynomially convex set (such as
\overline{G}
in the theorem) under a biholomorphic map need not be polynomially convex; Wermer himself constructed an example which can be found in Example 1 on pp. 135-136 of
[
20
]
. For the reader’s convenience, we list a few known facts/results on polynomial convexity.

1.

(
[
18
]
, Theorem 1.6.24) Let
F:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a proper holomorphic map. For
X\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
compact, we have
\widehat{X}=X
if and only if
\widehat{F^{-1}(X)}=F^{-1}(X)
, and
P(X)=C(X)
if and only if
P(F^{-1}(X))=C(F^{-1}(X))
.

2.

(
[
18
]
, Theorem 1.3.11) For
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
compact,
\widehat{K}
coincides with

\widehat{K}_{psh}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:u(z)\leq sup_{K}u,\ \hbox{all}\ u\ \hbox{plurisubharmonic in}\ \mathbb{C}^{n}\}.

(For the Wermer example, he constructs a biholomorphic image of a polynomially convex set (an ellipsoid) in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
under the polynomial mapping

F(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}):=(z_{1},z_{1}z_{2}+z_{3},z_{1}z_{2}^{2}-z_{2}+2z_{2}z_{3}).

Here
\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits JF=0
if
z_{3}=1/2
, and one sees easily that
F(0,z_{2},1/2)=(0,1/2,0)
; hence
F:\mathbb{C}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}^{3}
is not proper.)

Working in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, in order to obtain the equality
[W,z_{1}W]=A(\overline{G})
for
G
satisfying
\overline{G}
is polynomially convex and

A(\overline{G})=P(\overline{G})
, we seek
W
on
\overline{G}
such that
F=(f_{1},f_{2})=(W,z_{1}W)
satisfy 1.-3. of Theorem
4.1
. A simple example of a holomorphic
W
is the choice
W(z_{1},z_{2})=z_{2}
. Thus we consider
F=(f_{1},f_{2})=(z_{2},z_{1}z_{2})
. It is easy to check that as long as
\overline{G}\cap\{(z_{1},0):z_{1}\in\mathbb{C}\}=\emptyset
, conditions 1. and 2. of Theorem
4.1
hold.
Note that
F(z_{1},z_{2})=(z_{2},z_{1}z_{2})
is not proper as a map from
\mathbb{C}^{2}
to
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. For example, points of the form
(z_{1},0)
with
|z_{1}|\to\infty
have as image
F(z_{1},0)=(0,0)
(e.g., the pre-image of
(0,0)
is not compact). Thus we cannot appeal to the first result above on polynomial convexity to conclude
F(\overline{G})
is polynomially convex.

Example 5.1
.

A bidisk
G
satisfies
\overline{G}
is polynomially convex and

A(\overline{G})=P(\overline{G})
(cf.,
[
5
]
for this second point). Thus we consider

G:=\{(z_{1},z_{2}):|z_{1}-a_{1}|<r_{1},\ |z_{2}-a_{2}|<r_{2}\}\ \hbox{with}\ r_{2}<|a_{2}|

so that
\overline{G}\cap\{(z_{1},0):z_{1}\in\mathbb{C}\}=\emptyset
. We show that
K:=F(\overline{G})
is polynomially convex. Note

K=\{(w_{1},w_{2})=(z_{2},z_{1}z_{2}):|z_{1}-a_{1}|\leq r_{1},\ |z_{2}-a_{2}|\leq r_{2}\}

=\{(w_{1},w_{2}):|\frac{w_{2}}{w_{1}}-a_{1}|\leq r_{1},\ |w_{1}-a_{2}|\leq r_{2}\}.

Fix
(w_{1}^{o},w_{2}^{o})\not\in K
. If
|w_{1}^{o}-a_{2}|>r_{2}
, taking
Q(w_{1},w_{2})=w_{1}-a_{2}
shows
(w_{1}^{o},w_{2}^{o})\not\in\widehat{K}
.
If
|w_{1}^{o}-a_{2}|\leq r_{2}
but
|\frac{w_{2}^{o}}{w_{1}^{o}}-a_{1}|=(1+\delta)r_{1}
with
\delta>0
, we show that by taking
\epsilon=\epsilon(K,\delta,w_{2}^{o})>0

sufficiently small, and choosing a univariate polynomial
p(w_{1})
with

|p(w_{1})-1/w_{1}|<\epsilon\ \hbox{for}\ |w_{1}-a_{2}|\leq r_{2},

setting
Q(w_{1},w_{2})=w_{2}p(w_{1})-a_{1}
shows
(w_{1}^{o},w_{2}^{o})\not\in\widehat{K}
. To see this, first of all, for
(w_{1},w_{2})\in K
, since
|w_{1}|\leq|a_{2}|+r_{2}
and
|w_{2}|\leq|w_{1}|(|a_{1}|+r_{1})
,

|w_{2}|\leq|w_{1}|(|a_{1}|+r_{1})\leq(|a_{2}|+r_{2})(|a_{1}|+r_{1})

and hence

||Q||_{K}=||w_{2}p(w_{1})-a_{1}||_{K}=||w_{2}[p(w_{1})-1/w_{1}]+w_{2}/w_{1}-a_{1}||_{K}

\leq||w_{2}[p(w_{1})-1/w_{1}]||_{K}+||w_{2}/w_{1}-a_{1}||_{K}\leq(|a_{2}|+r_{2})(|a_{1}|+r_{1})\epsilon+r_{1}.

On the other hand,

|Q(w_{1}^{o},w_{2}^{o})|=|w_{2}^{o}p(w_{1}^{o})-a_{1}|=|w_{2}^{o}[p(w_{1}^{o})-1/w_{1}^{o}]+w_{2}^{o}/w_{1}^{o}-a_{1}|

\geq|w_{2}^{o}/w_{1}^{o}-a_{1}|-|w_{2}^{o}||p(w_{1}^{o})-1/w_{1}^{o}|>(1+\delta)r_{1}-|w_{2}^{o}|\epsilon.

Thus, given
(w_{1}^{o},w_{2}^{0})
and hence
\delta
, if we take
\epsilon>0
satisfying

\epsilon<\frac{\delta r_{1}}{|w_{2}^{o}|+(|a_{2}|+r_{2})(|a_{1}|+r_{1})},

then
|Q(w_{1}^{o},w_{2}^{o})|>||Q||_{K}
.

This shows that
[W,z_{1}W]=[z_{2},z_{1}z_{2}]=A(\overline{G})
. What can we say about
A(\overline{G},W)
? Let
f(z_{1})
be holomorphic in a neighborhood of
the closed disk
\{z_{1}:|z_{1}-a_{1}|\leq r_{1}\}
with
f_{1}\not\equiv 0
. Recall we are assuming
|a_{2}|>r_{2}
; for simplicity, suppose
a_{2}>0
. If
r_{2}>a_{2}/3
, we claim
that
\widetilde{f}(z_{1},z_{2}):=f(z_{1})\not\in A(\overline{G},W)
. For suppose
\widetilde{f}(z_{1},z_{2}):=f(z_{1})\in A(\overline{G},W)
so that there exist a sequence of
bivariate polynomials
p_{n}(z_{1},z_{2})
with
W^{n}p_{n}=z_{2}^{n}p_{n}(z_{1},z_{2})\to\widetilde{f}(z_{1},z_{2})
on
\overline{G}
. In particular, for each
fixed
z_{1}^{o}
with
|z_{1}^{o}-a_{1}|\leq r_{1}
,

z_{2}^{n}p_{n}(z_{1}^{o},z_{2})\to f(z_{1}^{o})\ \hbox{on}\ |z_{2}-a_{2}|\leq r_{2}.

But
\{z_{2}^{n}p_{n}(z_{1}^{o},z_{2})\}
are weighted holomorphic univariate polynomials, and by
[
13
]
, if
r_{2}>a_{2}/3
we arrive at a
contradiction provided
f(z_{1}^{o})\not=0
.

Example 5.2
.

We suspect that the Euclidean ball

G:=\{(z_{1},z_{2}):|z_{1}-a_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}-a_{2}|^{2}<r_{2}^{2}\}\ \hbox{with}\ r_{2}<|a_{2}|

should also work with the same mapping
F(z_{1},z_{2})=(z_{2},z_{1}z_{2})
. We were not able to show directly that
K:=F(\overline{G})
is polynomially convex. However, we can consider a
slightly different domain

\widetilde{G}:=\{(z_{1},z_{2}):|z_{1}z_{2}-a_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}-a_{2}|^{2}<r_{2}^{2}\}\ \hbox{with}\ r_{2}<|a_{2}|.

A direct calculation of the complex Hessian
H(\rho)
of the smooth plurisubharmonic defining function

\rho(z)=|z_{1}z_{2}-a_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}-a_{2}|^{2}-r_{2}^{2}

shows that
\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits H(\rho)=|z_{2}|^{2}
so that, since
\overline{\widetilde{G}}\cap\{(z_{1},0):z_{1}\in\mathbb{C}\}=\emptyset
,
\widetilde{G}
is strictly
pseudoconvex with smooth boundary and hence satisfies
A(\overline{\widetilde{G}})=P(\overline{\widetilde{G}})
. Moreover, since
\rho
is continuous and plurisubharmonic on all of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
with

\overline{\widetilde{G}}=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:\rho(z_{1},z_{2})\leq 0\}
,
\overline{\widetilde{G}}
is polynomially convex (the polynomial hull coincides with the hull with respect to plurisubharmonic functions on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
). With
F(z_{1},z_{2})=(z_{2},z_{1}z_{2})
, the set
\widetilde{K}:=F(\overline{\widetilde{G}})
becomes a Euclidean ball:

\widetilde{K}=\{(w_{1},w_{2}):|w_{1}-a_{1}|^{2}+|w_{1}-a_{2}|^{2}\leq r_{2}^{2}\}.

Hence
[W,z_{1}W]=[z_{2},z_{1}z_{2}]=A(\overline{\widetilde{G}})
.

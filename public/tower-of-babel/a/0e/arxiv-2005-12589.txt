Abstract
. We establish weighted L p L^{p} -Fourier-extension estimates for O ⁡ ( N − k ) × O ⁡ ( k ) O(N-k)\times O(k) -invariant functions defined on the unit sphere 𝕊 N − 1 \mathbb{S}^{N-1} , allowing for exponents p p below the Stein-Tomas critical exponent 2 ​ ( N + 1 ) N − 1 \frac{2(N+1)}{N-1} . Moreover, in the more general setting of an arbitrary closed subgroup G ⊂ O ⁡ ( N ) G\subset O(N) and G G -invariant functions, we study the implications of weighted Fourier-extension estimates with regard to boundedness and nonvanishing properties of the corresponding weighted Helmholtz resolvent operator. Finally, we use these properties to derive new existence results for G G -invariant solutions to the nonlinear Helmholtz equation − Δ ​ u − u = Q ⁡ ( x ) ​ | u | p − 2 ​ u , u ∈ W 2 , p ​ ( ℝ N ) , -\Delta u-u=Q(x)|u|^{p-2}u,\quad u\in W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}), where Q Q is a nonnegative bounded and G G -invariant weight function.



1.
Introduction

Starting with the pioneering work of Stein (cf.
[
11
]
), Tomas
[
15
]
and Strichartz
[
13
]
, Fourier restriction and extension estimates have been receiving extensive attention due to their various applications, especially to partial differential equations. For an overview on classical results and recent progress, we refer the reader to e.g.
[
5
,
12
,
14
]
. In its classical form, the famous Fourier extension theorem of Stein and Tomas (see e.g.
[
12
, §8: Corollary 5.4]
) states that
the inverse Fourier transform
\check{F}_{\sigma}
of
F\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{N-1})
, given by

\check{F}_{\sigma}(x)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}e^{i\omega\cdot x}F(\omega)\penalty\ d\sigma(\omega)

belongs to
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
N\geq 2
if
q\geq\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1}
, and that

(1.1)

\|\check{F}_{\sigma}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\left\|F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{N-1})}

with a constant
C>0
depending only on
q
and
N
. Here
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
denotes the
(N-1)-
dimensional sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
d\sigma
the induced Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
. Due to the Knapp example given by a characteristic function of a small spherical cap in
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, this range of exponents is known to be sharp for arbitrary functions, see e.g
[
14
, Chapter 4]
. On the other hand, it is a natural question whether the range of exponents can be improved both by considering weighted
L^{q}
-norms and by restricting to functions having additional symmetries. A well known and classical observation in this context
yields that case (
1.1
) holds for
q>\frac{2N}{N-1}
and
radial
(and thus constant) functions
F\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{N-1})
, see e.g.
[
12
, §8: Proposition 5.1]
.

In the present paper, we analyze this question for more general symmetries with respect to closed subgroups of
O(N)
.

For this we introduce the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
q\geq 1
, let
G\subset O(N)
be closed subgroup, and let
Q:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{C}
be a measurable function. We call
(G,q,Q)
an admissible extension triple if there exists a constant
C>0
with

(1.2)

\|Q\check{F}_{\sigma}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\left\|F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{N-1})}\qquad\text{for every $G$-invariant function $F\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{N-1})$.}

Here and in the following, a function
F\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{N-1})
is called
G
-invariant if
F(A\theta)=F(\theta)
for every
\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
,
A\in G
. By the remarks above,
(\{\id\},q,1)
is an admissible extension triple if
q\geq\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1}
and

(O(N),q,1)
is an admissible extension triple if
q>\frac{2N}{N-1}
. As a further specific example, we mention the subgroup
O(N-1)\cong O(N-1)\times\{\id_{\mathbb{R}}\}\subset O(N)
which corresponds to axial symmetry with respect to a fixed axis in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Since a characteristic function of a small spherical cap in
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
– as considered in Knapp’s example mentioned above – is axially symmetric, the range for
q
with
(O(N-1),q,1)
being an admissible extension triple cannot be extended beyond the value
\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1}
.

If, on the other hand, we consider weight functions
Q\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for suitable
s<\infty
, then the range of exponents giving rise to admissible extension triples can be readily extended by applying Hölder’s inequality to the LHS of (
1.2
). In particular, this yields that
(\{\id\},q,Q)
is an
admissible extension triple if
Q\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some
s\in[1,\infty)
and
q\geq\max\bigl\{\frac{2s(N+1)}{2(N+1)+s(N-1)},1\bigr\}
. Moreover,
(O(N),q,Q)
is an admissible extension triple if
Q\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some
s\in[1,\infty)
and
q\geq\max\bigl\{\frac{2sN}{2N+s(N-1)},1\}
.

In the present paper, we are interested in weight functions
Q\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where Hölder’s inequality does not yield an extended range of admissible exponents. The main aims of the paper are the following. First, we wish to detect a class of admissible extension triples corresponding to nontrivial subgroups of
O(N)
and corresponding to functions
Q\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
which are not
s
-integrable for any
s<\infty
. Second, starting from a range of admissible extension triples
(G,q,Q)
, we wish to derive selfdual
(L^{p^{\prime}},L^{p})
-estimates for the restriction of mappings of the form

f\mapsto{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}f:=Q{\mathcal{R}}(Qf)

to
G
-invariant functions in the Schwartz space
{\mathcal{S}}
of rapidly decreasing functions in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Here
{\mathcal{R}}
denotes the standard Helmholtz resolvent defined by
{\mathcal{R}}f=\Phi\ast f
, where

(1.3)

\Phi(x):=\frac{\text{i}}{4}(2\pi|x|)^{\frac{2-N}{2}}H^{(1)}_{\frac{N-2}{2}}(|x|),\quad\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\},

is the fundamental solution of the Helmholtz operator associated with Sommerfeld’s
outgoing radiation condition
\partial_{r}u(x)-\text{i}u(x)=o(|x|^{\frac{1-N}{2}})
, as
|x|\to\infty
.
Here
H^{(1)}_{\frac{N-2}{2}}
denotes the Hankel function of the first kind of order
\frac{N-2}{2}
. Moreover, we wish to derive corresponding nonvanishing results in the spirit of
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
. Finally, we wish to deduce existence results for real-valued
G
-invariant solutions of nonlinear Helmholtz equations of the form

(1.4)

-\Delta u-u=Q(x)|u|^{p-2}u,\quad u\in W^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

With regard to our first aim, we focus our attention to the subgroups

(1.5)

G_{k}:=O(N-k)\times O(k)\;\subset\;O(N)\qquad\text{for $k=1,\dots,N-1$.}

Moreover, we consider weight functions of the form
Q_{\alpha}=\mathds{1}_{L_{\alpha}}
for the set

(1.6)

L_{\alpha}:=\{x=(x^{(N-k)},x^{(k)})\in\mathbb{R}^{N-k}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}:|x^{(N-k)}|\leq a|x^{(k)}|^{-\alpha}\},

where
a>0
is an arbitrary fixed number and
\alpha>0
. Since
|L_{\alpha}|=\infty
, we have
Q_{\alpha}\not\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for any
\alpha>0
,
s<\infty
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N\geq 3
,
k\in\{1,\dots,N-1\}
, let
\alpha>0
, and let
Q_{\alpha}=\mathds{1}_{L_{\alpha}}
with
L_{\alpha}
given in (
1.6
). Moreover, suppose that

(1.7)

\alpha>\frac{1}{N-1}\quad\text{if $k=1$,}\qquad\qquad\qquad\alpha<N-1\quad\text{if $k=N-1$},

and let

(1.8)

\lambda_{N,k,\alpha}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{2(N-1)-\frac{2}{\alpha}}{N-2},&&\qquad\text{if $k=1$;}\\
&\max\left\{\frac{2(N-k)-\frac{2k}{\alpha}}{N-k-1},\frac{2k-2\alpha(N-k)}{k-1}\right\}&&\qquad\text{if $2\leq k\leq N-2$;}\\
&\frac{2(N-1)-2\alpha}{N-2},&&\qquad\text{if $k=N-1$.}\end{aligned}\right.

Then
(G_{k},q,Q_{\alpha})
is an admissible extension triple for every
q>\lambda_{N,k,\alpha}
.

We note that, in Theorem
2.2
below, we shall in fact prove a generalization of this result for characteristic functions of sets of the form
L_{\alpha,\beta}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|x^{(N-k)}|\leq a\max\{|x^{(k)}|^{-\alpha},|x^{(k)}|^{-\beta}\}\}
with
\alpha\geq\beta>0
. Regarding Theorem
1.1
, we note in particular that
\lambda_{N,k,\alpha}=0
for
\alpha=\frac{k}{N-k}
, so
(G_{k},q,Q_{\alpha})
is an admissible extension triple for every
q\geq 1
in this case if also (
1.7
) is satisfied. More generally, the latter property holds if
\alpha\in(\frac{k+1}{2(N-k)},\frac{2k}{N-k+1})
, since then we have
\lambda_{N,k,\alpha}<1
. Comparing with the classical Stein-Tomas exponent, we have
\lambda_{N,k,\alpha}<\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1}
if

\displaystyle k\leq\frac{N-1}{2},

\displaystyle\alpha\in\bigl(\frac{N+1-2k}{(N-k)(N-1)},\infty\bigr)\qquad\text{or}

\displaystyle\frac{N-1}{2}<k<\frac{N+1}{2},

\displaystyle\alpha\in\bigl(\frac{N+1-2k}{(N-k)(N-1)},\frac{k(N-1)}{2k-(N-1)}\bigr)\qquad\text{or}

\displaystyle k\geq\frac{N+1}{2},

\displaystyle\alpha\in\bigl(0,\frac{k(N-1)}{2k-(N-1)}\bigr).

q
for
N=6
and
k=2

The main part of the proof of this Theorem consists in a detailed asymptotic study of one-dimensional integrals which arise after integrating along the orbits of
G_{k}
. Here, the well-known bound

|\check{d\sigma_{k}}(x)|\leq C(1+|x|)^{\frac{1-k}{2}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{k}

for the Fourier transform of the standard measure
d\sigma_{k}
on
\mathbb{S}^{k-1}
will play a key role (see e.g.
[
12
, §8: Theorem 3.1]
).

We also remark that, if
(G,q,Q)
is an admissible extension triple and
Q^{\prime}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{C}
is a measurable function with
|Q^{\prime}|\leq|Q|
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then, by definition,
(G,q,Q^{\prime})
is also an admissible extension triple. Consequently, the statement of Theorem
1.1
extends to functions
Q\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
|Q|\leq c\mathds{1}_{L_{\alpha}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
for some
c>0
.

Next we state our main result on
(L^{p^{\prime}},L^{p})
-Helmholtz resolvent estimates for
G
-invariant functions. Here and in the following, for
r\in[1,\infty]
, we let
L^{r}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
denotes the closed subspace of
G
-invariant functions in
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
N\geq 3
, let
G\subset O(N)
be a closed subgroup, let
Q\in L^{\infty}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and let
q\in[1,\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1}]
be such that
(G,q,Q)
is an admissible extension triple. Then for every
p\in\bigl(\frac{2N}{N-1}\frac{2q}{q+2}\,,\,\frac{2N}{N-2}\bigr]
there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(1.9)

\left\|{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}(f)\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\qquad\text{for every $f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{G}$.}

Here and in the following,
{\mathcal{S}}_{G}\subset{\mathcal{S}}
denotes the subspace of
G
-invariant functions in the Schwartz space
{\mathcal{S}}
.

Our proof of Theorem
1.2
is based on the strategy used in
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
, see also
[
3
]
. We recall that a selfdual estimate of the form (
1.9
) has been proved in
[
8
]
for the Helmholtz resolvent
{\mathcal{R}}
in place of
{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}
in the range of exponents
p\in[\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1},\frac{2N}{N-2}\bigr]
, while corresponding non-selfdual estimates were obtained in
[
7
]
. Clearly, these already available
(L^{r},L^{s})
-estimates for
{\mathcal{R}}
extend, by approximation, to the weighted resolvent
{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}
in the case where
Q\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Theorem
1.2
complements
the selfdual estimate in
[
8
]
, for
{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}
and
G
-invariant functions, in the case where
(G,q,Q)
is an admissible extension triple for some
q<\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1}
, which is equivalent to the inequality
\frac{2N}{N-1}\frac{2q}{q+2}<\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1}
. In fact, we will prove a non-selfdual generalization of Theorem
1.2
in Theorem
3.1
below.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
, it follows,
by density, that the weighted resolvent
{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}
extends to a bounded linear operator
L^{p^{\prime}}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{p}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. In our next result we state that, under the same assumptions, a nonvanishing property in the spirit of
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
holds.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
N\geq 3
, let
G\subset O(N)
be a closed subgroup, let
Q\in L^{\infty}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and let
q\in\bigl[1,\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1}\bigr]
be
such that
(G,q,Q)
is an admissible extension triple. Moreover, let
p\in\bigl(\frac{2N}{N-1}\frac{2q}{q+2},\frac{2N}{N-2}\bigr]
.
Then for every bounded sequence
(v_{n})_{n}\subset L^{p^{\prime}}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying
\Bigl|\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v_{n}{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}(v_{n})\penalty\ dx\Bigr|>0
, there exist – after passing to a subsequence – numbers
R,\zeta>0
and a sequence of points
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
with

\int\limits_{B_{{R}(x_{n})}}|Qv_{n}(x)|^{p^{\prime}}\penalty\ dx\geq\zeta,\quad\text{ for all }n.

In the special (non-symmetric) case
G=\{\id\}
,
Q\equiv 1
,
q=\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1}
, this theorem reduces to
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
. Here we note that
\frac{2N}{N-1}\frac{2q}{q+2}=\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1}
if
q=\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1}
. The general strategy of the proof of Theorem
1.3
is inspired by
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
. However, additional difficulties, related to the fact that the multiplication with
Q\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
does not map
{\mathcal{S}}
into itself, lead to a somewhat more involved argument.

Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
are useful in the study of real-valued
G
-invariant solutions of the nonlinear Helmholtz equation (
1.4
)
with a real-valued weight function
Q\in L^{\infty}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where
G\subset O(N)
is a given closed subgroup. In the following, we focus on
dual bound state solutions
, which arise
as
solutions
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of the integral equation
u=R\bigl(Q|u|^{p-2}u\bigr)
, where
R
is the real part of the resolvent operator
{\mathcal{R}}
, see Section
5
for details.
Our first main result in this context is the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
N\geq 3
, let
G\subset O(N)
be a closed subgroup, and let
Q\in L^{\infty}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a real-valued nonnegative function with
Q\not\equiv 0
and with the property that

(1.10)

\|Q\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(x))}\to 0\qquad\text{as $|x|\to\infty$ for some $R>0$.}

Moreover, let
q\in\bigl[1,\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1}\bigr]
, and let
p\in\bigl(\max\bigl\{\frac{2N}{N-1}\frac{2q}{q+2},2\bigr\}\,,\,\frac{2N}{N-2}\bigr)
be such that
(G,q,Q^{\frac{1}{p}})
is an admissible extension triple. Then (
1.4
) admits a nontrivial
G
-invariant dual bound state solution.

We recall that, by the Stein-Tomas inequality,
(G,q,Q^{\prime})
is an admissible extension triple for
q=\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1}
and every closed subgroup
G\subset O(N)
and every
Q^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Recalling moreover that
\frac{2N}{N-1}\frac{2q}{q+2}=\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1}
in this case, we readily deduce the following corollary of Theorem
1.4
.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
N\geq 3
, let
G\subset O(N)
be a closed subgroup, and let
Q\in L^{\infty}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a nonnegative function satisfying
Q\not\equiv 0
and (
1.10
). Then (
1.4
) admits a nontrivial
G
-invariant dual bound state solution for every
p\in(\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1},\frac{2N}{N-2}).

This corollary applies in particular in the non-symmetric case
G=\{\id\}
, but it requires the asymptotic condition (
1.10
). On the other hand, in the case of special symmetries corresponding to the subgroups
G_{k}
defined in (
1.5
), we may drop assumption (
1.10
), as the following result shows.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
N\geq 4
, let
k\in\{2,\dots,N-2\}
, and let
Q\in L^{\infty}_{G_{k}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a nonnegative function with
Q\not\equiv 0
. Then (
1.4
) admits a nontrivial
G_{k}
-invariant dual bound state solution for every
p\in(\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1},\frac{2N}{N-2})
.

Finally, we point out that assumption (
1.10
) holds in particular for functions
Q\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying
|Q|\leq c\mathds{1}_{L_{\alpha}}
for some
c,\alpha>0
, where
L_{\alpha}
is given in (
1.6
). Using this fact, the following corollary can be deduced from Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
N\geq 3
, let
k\in\{1,\dots,N-1\}
, and let
\alpha>0
. Moreover, let
Q\in L^{\infty}_{G_{k}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a nonnegative function with
Q\not\equiv 0
and satisfying
|Q|\leq c\mathds{1}_{L_{\alpha}}
for some
c>0
with
L_{\alpha}
given in (
1.6
).
Then (
1.4
) admits a nontrivial
G_{k}
-invariant dual bound state solution for all
p\in(\mu_{N,k,\alpha},\frac{2N}{N-2})
if one of the following holds:

(i)

k=1
and

(1.11)

\mu_{N,1,\alpha}:=\text{\footnotesize$\left\{\begin{aligned} &\text{\normalsize$2$},&&\hskip 17.00024pt\hskip 17.00024pt\frac{1}{N-1}\text{\normalsize$\,<\,\alpha\leq$}\>\frac{N+1}{3(N-1)},\\
&\frac{4N(\alpha(N-1)-1)}{(N-1)(2\alpha N-3\alpha-1)},&&\hskip 17.00024pt\hskip 17.00024pt\text{\normalsize$\alpha\,>$}\>\frac{N+1}{3(N-1)}.\end{aligned}\right.$}

(ii)

k=N-1
and

(1.12)

\mu_{N,N-1,\alpha}:=\text{\footnotesize$\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{4N(N-1-\alpha)}{(N-1)(2N-\alpha-3)},&&\hskip 17.00024pt\hskip 17.00024pt\text{\normalsize$0\,<\alpha\leq\>$}\frac{3(N-1)}{N+1},\\
&\text{\normalsize$2$},&&\hskip 17.00024pt\hskip 17.00024pt\frac{3(N-1)}{N+1}\text{\normalsize$\,<\,\alpha\,<$}\>N-1.\end{aligned}\right.$}

(iii)

2\leq k\leq N-2
and

(1.13)

\mu_{N,k,\alpha}:=\text{\footnotesize$\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{4N(k-\alpha(N-k))}{(N-1)(2k-1-\alpha(N-k))},&&\hskip 17.00024pt\hskip 17.00024pt\text{\normalsize$\alpha\leq\>$}\frac{N+2k-1}{(N+1)(N-k)},\\
&\text{\normalsize$2$},&&\hskip 17.00024pt\hskip 17.00024pt\frac{N+2k-1}{(N+1)(N-k)}\text{\normalsize$\,<\,\alpha\leq$}\>\frac{(N+1)k}{N-1+2(N-k)},\\
&\frac{4N(\alpha(N-k)-k)}{(N-1)(2\alpha(N-k)-\alpha-k)},&&\hskip 17.00024pt\hskip 17.00024pt\text{\normalsize$\alpha\,>$}\>\frac{(N+1)k}{N-1+2(N-k)}.\end{aligned}\right.$}

We point out that, in contrast to Corollary
1.5
and Theorem
1.6
, Corollary

allows to consider exponents

.

To put our existence results for (
1.4
) into perspective, we recall some previous results. In
[
7
]
, the existence of small complex solutions has been proved via the use of contraction mappings in dimensions
N=3,4
,
p=3
and
Q=\pm 1
. A variant of this technique is developed in
[

]
, where continua of small real-valued solutions of (

) are detected for a larger class of nonlinearities. The dual variational approach to (
1.4
) was introduced in
[
3
]
, where the existence of nontrivial dual bound state solutions was proved for
p\in\left(\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1},\frac{2N}{N-2}\right)
and for nonnegative weight functions
Q\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}
which are either
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
-periodic or satisfy the uniform decay assumptions
Q(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
. Under additional restrictions on
Q
, this approach was extended to the Sobolev critical case
p=\frac{2N}{N-2}
in
[
4
]
. Moreover, a dual approach in Orlicz spaces was developed in
[
2
]
to treat more general nonlinearities in (
1.4
). The defocusing case
Q\leq 0
in (
1.4
) and radial solutions are considered in
[
10
]
. We are not aware of any previous work where symmetries different from radial symmetry are used to extend the range of admissible exponents to values below the Stein-Tomas exponent
\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1}
and to overcome lack of compactness issues in the context of (
1.4
). .

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

-invariant functions, where

.
Moreover, we set

and
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
for the boundary of

. The constant

represents the volume of the unit ball

in

2.
Fourier extension estimates for
G_{k}
-invariant functions

We recall that, for a function
F\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{N-1})
, we define the (inverse) Fourier transform of
Fd\sigma

\check{F}_{\sigma}(x)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}e^{i\omega\cdot x}F(\omega)\penalty\ d\sigma(\omega).

For
F\equiv 1
we use the notation

\check{d\sigma}_{N}(x)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}e^{i\omega\cdot x}\penalty\ d\sigma(\omega)

and will often omit the dimensional index if no confusion is possible. We point out that this function satisfies the key uniform
bound

(2.1)

|\check{d\sigma}_{N}(x)|\leq C(1+|x|)^{\frac{1-N}{2}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

with a constant
C=C(N)>0
, see e.g.
[
12
, §8: Theorem 3.1]
.

For
k\in\{1,\dots,N-1\}
, we consider the closed subgroup
G_{k}=O(N-k)\times O(k)\subset O(N)
. We first derive a useful expression for
\check{F}_{\sigma}
in the case where
F\in{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{S}^{N-1})
is
G_{k}
-invariant. Note that in this case
F
only depends on one variable
r\in[0,1]
via the function

(2.2)

h_{F}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R},\qquad h_{F}(r):=F(r\eta,\sqrt{1-r^{2}}\mu)\qquad\text{for $\eta\in\mathbb{S}^{N-k-1}$, $\mu\in\mathbb{S}^{k-1}$.}

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
k\in\{1,\dots,N-1\}
and
F\in{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{S}^{N-1})
be
G_{k}
-invariant. Then we have

(2.3)

\check{F}_{\sigma}(x)=(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}\frac{k\alpha_{k}}{N\alpha_{N}}\int\limits^{1}_{0}r^{N-k-1}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{k-2}{2}}h_{F}(r)\check{d\sigma}_{N-k}(rx^{(N-k)})\check{d\sigma}_{k}(\sqrt{1-r^{2}}x^{(k)})\penalty\ dr

with
h_{F}
given in (
2.2
). Moreover,

|\check{F}_{\sigma}(x)|\leq(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}\frac{k\alpha_{k}}{N\alpha_{N}}\frac{\left\|F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{N-1})}}{\sqrt{|\mathbb{S}^{N-k-1}||\mathbb{S}^{k-1}|}}\left(\int\limits^{1}_{0}r^{N-k-1}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{k-2}{2}}|\check{d\sigma}_{N-k}(rx^{(N-k)})|^{2}\penalty\ |\check{d\sigma}_{k}(\sqrt{1-r^{2}}x^{(k)})|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Proof.

By using slice integration (see e.g.
[
1
, A.5]
) we have

\displaystyle\check{F}_{\sigma}(x)=(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}e^{ix\cdot\omega}F(\omega)\penalty\ d\sigma(\omega)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}\frac{k\alpha_{k}}{N\alpha_{N}}\int\limits_{B^{(N-k)}}(1-|y|^{2})^{\frac{k-2}{2}}e^{ix^{(N-k)}y}\int\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{k-1}}e^{ix^{(k)}\sqrt{1-|y|^{2}}\mu}F\left(y,\sqrt{1-|y|^{2}}\mu\right)\penalty\ d\sigma_{k}(\mu)\penalty\ d_{N-k}(y)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}\frac{k\alpha_{k}}{N\alpha_{N}}\int\limits^{1}_{0}r^{N-k-1}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{N-k-1}}e^{ix^{(N-k)}r\eta}\int\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{k-1}}e^{ix^{(k)}\sqrt{1-r^{2}}\mu}F\left(r\eta,\sqrt{1-r^{2}}\mu\right)\penalty\ d\sigma_{k}(\mu)\penalty\ d\sigma_{N-k}(\eta)\penalty\ dr

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}\frac{k\alpha_{k}}{N\alpha_{N}}\int\limits^{1}_{0}r^{N-k-1}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{k-2}{2}}h_{F}(r)\check{d\sigma}_{N-k}(rx^{(N-k)})\check{d\sigma}_{k}(\sqrt{1-r^{2}}x^{(k)})\penalty\ dr

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
h_{F}
given in (
2.2
), as claimed in (
2.3
). In particular, we get

\displaystyle|\check{F}_{\sigma}(x)|\leq(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}\frac{k\alpha_{k}}{N\alpha_{N}}\int\limits^{1}_{0}r^{N-k-1}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{k-2}{2}}|h_{F}(r)|\penalty\ |\check{d\sigma}_{N-k}(rx^{(N-k)})|\penalty\ |\check{d\sigma}_{k}(\sqrt{1-r^{2}}x^{(k)})|\penalty\ dr.

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}\frac{k\alpha_{k}}{N\alpha_{N}}\left(\int\limits^{1}_{0}r^{N-k-1}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{k-2}{2}}|h_{F}(r)|^{2}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\times

\displaystyle\;\qquad\left(\int\limits^{1}_{0}r^{N-k-1}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{k-2}{2}}|\check{d\sigma}_{N-k}(rx^{(N-k)})|^{2}\penalty\ |\check{d\sigma}_{k}(\sqrt{1-r^{2}}x^{(k)})|^{2}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}\frac{k\alpha_{k}}{N\alpha_{N}}\frac{\left\|F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{N-1})}}{\sqrt{|\mathbb{S}^{N-k-1}||\mathbb{S}^{k-1}|}}\left(\int\limits^{1}_{0}r^{N-k-1}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{k-2}{2}}|\check{d\sigma}_{N-k}(rx^{(N-k)})|^{2}\penalty\ |\check{d\sigma}_{k}(\sqrt{1-r^{2}}x^{(k)})|^{2}dr\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

∎

For
\alpha\geq\beta>0
and fixed
a>0
, we now consider the subset

(2.4)

L_{\alpha,\beta}:=\{x=(x^{(N-k)},x^{(k)}):|x^{(N-k)}|\leq a\max\left\{|x^{(k)}|^{-\alpha},|x^{(k)}|^{-\beta}\right\}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}.

We shall prove the following generalization of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
N\geq 3.
Suppose we have
\alpha\geq\beta>0
,
k\in\{1,\dots,N-1\}
,
Q=\mathds{1}_{L_{\alpha,\beta}}
with
L_{\alpha,\beta}
given as in (
1.6
), and

(2.5)

\lambda_{N,k,\alpha,\beta}:=\max\left\{\frac{2k-2\beta(N-k)}{k-1},\frac{2(N-k)-\frac{2k}{\alpha}}{N-k-1}\right\}.

Suppose furthermore that
q\geq 1
and
k\in\{1,\dots,N-1\}
satisfy

(2.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &k=1,&&\qquad\beta>\frac{1}{N-1}&&\text{ and }\penalty\ q>\frac{2(N-1)-\frac{2}{\alpha}}{N-2}\text{ or}\\
&2\leq k\leq N-2,&&\qquad q>\lambda_{N,k,\alpha,\beta}&&\text{or}\\
&k=N-1,&&\qquad\alpha<N-1,&&\text{and }q>\frac{2(N-1)-2\beta}{N-2}.\end{aligned}\right.

Then there exists a constant
C=C(N,k,\alpha,\beta,a)
with the property that

(2.7)

\left\|Q\check{F}_{\sigma}\right\|_{q}\leq C\left\|F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{N-1})}\qquad\text{for every $G_{k}$-invariant function $F\in{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{S}^{N-1})$.}

Proof.

We shall prove
(
2.7
) under the additional assumption

(2.8)

q<4,

noting that for any
\alpha\geq\beta>0
and
k\in\{1,\dots,N-1\}
, the set of values
q
satisfying (
2.6
) and (
2.8
) is non-empty. Moreover, by interpolating with the trivial estimate

\left\|Q\check{F}_{\sigma}\right\|_{\infty}\leq(2\pi)^{-N}\sqrt{|\mathbb{S}^{N-1}|}\left\|F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{N-1})},

we can remove the extra assumption (
2.8
) a posteriori.

In the following, the letter
C
stands for positive and possibly different constants depending only on
N,k
,
\alpha,\beta
and
a
. Let
F\in{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{S}^{N-1})
be a
G_{k}
-invariant function. Without loss of generality we assume that
\|F\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{N-1})}=1
. Using Lemma
2.1
, we can write

\displaystyle\left\|Q\check{F}_{\sigma}\right\|_{q}^{q}=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|[Q\check{F}_{\sigma}](x)|^{q}\penalty\ dx=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(x)|\check{F}_{\sigma}(x)|^{q}\penalty\ dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(x)\int\limits^{1}_{0}r^{(N-k-1)\frac{q}{2}}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{k-2}{2}\frac{q}{2}}|\check{d\sigma}_{N-k}(rx^{(N-k)})|^{q}|\check{d\sigma}_{k}(\sqrt{1-r^{2}}x^{(k)})|^{q}\penalty\ dr\penalty\ dx

\displaystyle=C\int\limits^{1}_{0}r^{(N-k-1)\frac{q}{2}}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{k-2}{2}\frac{q}{2}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q(x)|\check{d\sigma}_{N-k}(rx^{(N-k)})|^{q}|\check{d\sigma}_{k}(\sqrt{1-r^{2}}x^{(k)})|^{q}\penalty\ dx\penalty\ dr

(2.9)

\displaystyle=C\int\limits^{1}_{0}r^{(N-k-1)\frac{q}{2}-(N-k)}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{q}{4}(k-2)-\frac{k}{2}}H_{k}(r)dr,

where we have used that
|Q|^{q}=Q
due to the special choice of
Q
as a characteristic function. In the last line we have set

H_{k}(r):=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q\left(\frac{x^{(N-k)}}{r},\frac{x^{(k)}}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\right)|\check{d\sigma}_{N-k}(x^{(N-k)})|^{q}\penalty\ |\check{d\sigma}_{k}(x^{(k)})|^{q}\penalty\ dx.

Using the definition of
Q
and the estimate (
2.1
), we deduce that

\displaystyle H_{k}(r)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}

\displaystyle|\check{d\sigma}_{k}(x^{(k)})|^{q}\!\!\!\!\int\limits_{|x^{(N-k)}|\,\leq\,ar\max\bigl\{\left(\frac{|x^{(k)}|}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\right)^{-\alpha},\left(\frac{|x^{(k)}|}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\right)^{-\beta}\bigr\}}|\check{d\sigma}_{N-k}(x^{(N-k)})|^{q}\penalty\ dx^{(N-k)}\penalty\ dx^{(k)}

\displaystyle\leq C\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}

\displaystyle(1+|x^{(k)}|)^{q\frac{1-k}{2}}\!\!\!\!\int\limits_{|x^{(N-k)}|\,\leq\,ar\max\bigl\{\left(\frac{|x^{(k)}|}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\right)^{-\alpha},\left(\frac{|x^{(k)}|}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\right)^{-\beta}\bigr\}}(1+|x^{(N-k)}|)^{q\frac{1-(N-k)}{2}}\penalty\ dx^{(N-k)}\penalty\ dx^{(k)}

\displaystyle=C\int\limits^{\infty}_{0}

\displaystyle s^{k-1}(1+s)^{-\frac{q}{2}(k-1)}\int\limits^{ar\max\bigl\{\bigl(\frac{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}{s}\bigr)^{\alpha},\bigl(\frac{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}{s}\bigr)^{\beta}\bigr\}}_{0}t^{N-k-1}(1+t)^{-\frac{q}{2}(N-k-1)}\penalty\ dt\penalty\ ds

\displaystyle=C

\displaystyle\int\limits^{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}_{0}s^{k-1}(1+s)^{-\frac{q}{2}(k-1)}\int\limits^{ar(1-r^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}s^{-\alpha}}_{0}t^{N-k-1}(1+t)^{-\frac{q}{2}(N-k-1)}\penalty\ dt\penalty\ ds

(2.10)

\displaystyle+\;C\int\limits^{\infty}_{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}s^{k-1}(1+s)^{-\frac{q}{2}(k-1)}\int\limits^{ar(1-r^{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}s^{-\beta}}_{0}t^{N-k-1}(1+t)^{-\frac{q}{2}(N-k-1)}\penalty\ dt\penalty\ ds,

where the last equality follows since
\alpha\geq\beta>0
by assumption and therefore

\max\bigl\{\left(\frac{s}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\right)^{-\alpha},\left(\frac{s}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}\right)^{-\beta}\bigr\}=\begin{cases}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}s^{-\alpha}\qquad&0<s<\sqrt{1-r^{2}};\\
(1-r^{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}s^{-\beta},\qquad&s\geq\sqrt{1-r^{2}}.\end{cases}

Combining (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), we can estimate

(2.11)

\left\|Q\check{F}_{\sigma}\right\|_{q}^{q}\leq C\Bigl(I^{(1)}_{k}+I^{(2)}_{k}\Bigr)

with

(2.12)

I^{(i)}_{k}:=\int\limits^{1}_{0}r^{(N-k-1)\frac{q}{2}-(N-k)}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{q}{4}(k-2)-\frac{k}{2}}H^{(i)}_{k}(r)\penalty\ dr\qquad\text{for $i=1,2$}

and

\displaystyle H^{(1)}_{k}(r)

\displaystyle:=\int\limits^{\infty}_{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}s^{k-1}(1+s)^{-\frac{q}{2}(k-1)}\int\limits^{ar(1-r^{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}s^{-\beta}}_{0}t^{N-k-1}(1+t)^{-\frac{q}{2}(N-k-1)}\penalty\ dt\penalty\ ds,

\displaystyle H^{(2)}_{k}(r)

\displaystyle:=\int\limits^{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}_{0}s^{k-1}(1+s)^{-\frac{q}{2}(k-1)}\int\limits^{ar(1-r^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}s^{-\alpha}}_{0}t^{N-k-1}(1+t)^{-\frac{q}{2}(N-k-1)}\penalty\ dt\penalty\ ds.

We first estimate
I^{1}_{k}
, and we note that

\displaystyle H_{k}^{(1)}(r)

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits^{\infty}_{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}s^{(1-\frac{q}{2})(k-1)}\int\limits^{ar(1-r^{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}s^{-\beta}}_{0}t^{N-k-1}\penalty\ dt\penalty\ ds

(2.13)

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{N-k}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}(N-k)}\int\limits^{\infty}_{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}s^{(1-\frac{q}{2})(k-1)-\beta(N-k)}\penalty\ ds\leq Cr^{N-k}(1-r^{2})^{(1-\frac{q}{2})\frac{k-1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}

for

. Here we used in the last step that

(1-\frac{q}{2})(k-1)-\beta(N-k)<-1
since by (
2.6
) we have

\beta>\frac{1}{N-1}\quad\text{in case $k=1$}\qquad\text{and}\qquad q>\frac{2k-2\beta(N-k)}{k-1}\quad\text{if $2\leq k\leq N-1$.}

Combining (
2.12
) and (

), we conclude that

I^{(1)}_{k}\leq C\int\limits^{1}_{0}r^{(N-k-1)\frac{q}{2}}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{q}{4}(k-2)-\frac{k}{2}+(1-\frac{q}{2})\frac{k-1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}dr=C\int\limits^{1}_{0}r^{(N-k-1)\frac{q}{2}}(1-r^{2})^{-\frac{q}{4}}dr,

where the integral on the RHS is finite due to (

). As a consequence,
I^{(1)}_{k}\leq C_{1}
with a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(N,k,\beta,a)>0
.

Next we consider
I^{(2)}_{k}
, and we distinguish the following cases.

Case 1:

q(N-k-1)<2(N-k)
.

In this case we estimate as follows:

\displaystyle H^{(2)}_{k}(r)

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits^{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}_{0}s^{k-1}\int\limits^{ar(1-r^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}s^{-\alpha}}_{0}t^{N-k-1-\frac{q}{2}(N-k-1)}\penalty\ dt\penalty\ ds

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{N-k-\frac{q}{2}(N-k-1)}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}(N-k)-\frac{\alpha}{2}\frac{q}{2}(N-k-1)}\int\limits^{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}_{0}s^{k-1-\alpha(N-k)+\alpha\frac{q}{2}(N-k-1)}\penalty\ ds

\displaystyle=Cr^{N-k-\frac{q}{2}(N-k-1)}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}\qquad\text{for $r\in(0,1)$.}

Here we used in the last step that
k-1-\alpha(N-k)+\alpha\frac{q}{2}(N-k-1)>-1
since by (
2.6
) we have

q>\frac{2(N-k)-\frac{2k}{\alpha}}{N-k-1}\quad\text{in case $k<N-1$}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\alpha<N-1\quad\text{if $k=N-1$.}

We thus conclude that

(2.14)

I^{(2)}_{k}=\int\limits^{1}_{0}r^{(N-k-1)\frac{q}{2}-(N-k)}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{q}{4}(k-2)-\frac{k}{2}}H^{(2)}_{k}(r)\penalty\ dr\leq C\int\limits^{1}_{0}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{q}{4}(k-2)}\penalty\ dr<\infty.

We now consider

Case 2:

q(N-k-1)\geq 2(N-k)

In this case we choose
\delta\in\bigl(\,\frac{q}{2}(N-k-1)-(N-k)\>,\>\min\bigl\{\frac{q}{2}(N-k-1),\frac{q}{2}(N-k-1)+\frac{k}{\alpha}-(N-k)\bigr\}\,\bigr)

\displaystyle H^{(2)}_{k}(r)\leq\int\limits^{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}_{0}s^{k-1}\int\limits^{ar(1-r^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}s^{-\alpha}}_{0}t^{N-k-1}(1+t)^{\delta-\frac{q}{2}(N-k-1)}\penalty\ dt\penalty\ ds

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits^{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}_{0}s^{k-1}\int\limits^{ar(1-r^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}s^{-\alpha}}_{0}t^{N-k-1+\delta-\frac{q}{2}(N-k-1)}\penalty\ dt\penalty\ ds

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{N-k+\delta-\frac{q}{2}(N-k-1)}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}(N-k+\delta)-\frac{\alpha}{2}\frac{q}{2}(N-k-1)}\int\limits^{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}_{0}s^{k-1-\alpha(N-k+\delta)+\alpha\frac{q}{2}(N-k-1)}\penalty\ ds

\displaystyle=Cr^{N-k+\delta-\frac{q}{2}(N-k-1)}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{k}{2}}\qquad\text{for $r\in(0,1)$.}

We thus conclude that

(2.15)

I^{(2)}_{k}=\int\limits^{1}_{0}r^{(N-k-1)\frac{q}{2}-(N-k)}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{q}{4}(k-2)-\frac{k}{2}}H^{(2)}_{k}(r)\penalty\ dr\leq C\int\limits^{1}_{0}r^{\delta}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{q}{4}(k-2)}\penalty\ dr<\infty.

Combining (
2.14
) and (
2.15
), we conclude that

I^{(2)}_{k}\leq C_{2}
with a constant
C_{2}=C_{2}(N,k,\alpha,a)>0

Going back to (

), we deduce that

\left\|Q\check{F}_{\sigma}\right\|_{q}^{q}\leq C(C_{1}+C_{2}),

where the constant on the RHS only depends on
N,k,\alpha,\beta
and
a
. The proof is thus finished.
∎

Moreover, we have the following duality property.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that a closed subgroup
G\subset O(N)
,
q\geq 1
, and

are given with the property that
(G,q,Q)
is an admissible extension pair in the sense of Definition

. Then there exists a constant
C>0
with

\bigl\|\widehat{Qf}\big|_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\bigr\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{N-1})}\leq C\|f\|_{q^{\prime}}\qquad\text{for every $f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{G}$.}

In particular, this holds if
G=G_{k}
and

and

satisfy the assumptions of Theorem

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\|F\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{N-1})}^{2}=\int\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\widehat{Qf}\overline{F}\penalty\ d\sigma=(2\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-ix\theta}f(x)Q(x)\penalty\ dx\penalty\ \overline{F(\theta)}\penalty\ d\sigma(\theta)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x)\overline{\overline{Q(x)}\int\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}e^{ix\theta}F(\theta)\penalty\ d\sigma(\theta)}\penalty\ dx\leq\|f\|_{q^{\prime}}\|Q\check{F_{\sigma}}\|_{q}\leq C\|f\|_{q^{\prime}}\|F\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{N-1})}

and therefore

, as claimed.

3.
Resolvent estimates for
G
-invariant functions

For
N\geq 3
, the radial outgoing fundamental solution of the Helmholtz equation
-\Delta u-u=\delta_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
is given by

(3.1)

\Phi(x):=\frac{\text{i}}{4}(2\pi|x|)^{\frac{2-N}{2}}H^{(1)}_{\frac{N-2}{2}}(|x|),\quad\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\},

where
H^{(1)}_{\frac{N-2}{2}}
denotes the Hankel function of the first kind of order
\frac{N-2}{2}
.
For a function
f\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
the convolution
u:=\Phi\ast f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a solution
of the inhomogeneous Helmholtz equation
-\Delta u-u=f
which satisfies the Sommerfeld
outgoing radiation condition
\partial_{r}u(x)-\text{i}u(x)=o(|x|^{\frac{1-N}{2}})
, as
|x|\to\infty
.
Moreover, it is known (see
[
6
]
) that, in the sense of tempered distributions, the Fourier transform of
\Phi

is given by

(3.2)

\widehat{\Phi}(\xi)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}-(1+\text{i}0)}:=(2\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}-(1+\text{i}\varepsilon)}.

As a consequence of a classical estimate of Kenig, Ruiz and Sogge (see
[
8
, Theorem 2.3]
), the mapping
f\mapsto\Phi\ast f
for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
extends as a continuous linear operator

{\mathcal{R}}:L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

for
\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1}\leq p\leq\frac{2N}{N-2}
. Moreover, non-selfdual
(L^{r},L^{p^{\prime}})
-estimates for
{\mathcal{R}}
were established by Gutiérrez in
[
7
, Theorem 6]
. The aim of this section is to establish a similar estimate for the operator
{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}
defined by
{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}(f):=f\mapsto Q[\Phi\ast(Qf)]
, where
G
is a closed subgroup of
O(N)
and
Q\in L^{\infty}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a weight function. The main result of this section is the following generalization of Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
N\geq 3
, let
G\subset O(N)
be a closed subgroup, let
Q\in L^{\infty}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and let
q\in\bigl[1,\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1}\bigr]
be such that
(G,q,Q)
is an admissible extension triple. Moreover, let
p,r\in(1,\infty)
satisfy

(3.3)

\frac{N-2}{N}\>\leq\>\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{r}\><\>\frac{q+2}{2q}\,\frac{N-1}{N}.

Suppose moreover that

(3.4)

\max\left\{\frac{1}{r},\frac{1}{p}\right\}<\frac{N-1}{2N}\qquad\text{if $q\geq 2$,}

and that

(3.5)

\frac{2q}{(N-1)(2-q)}\frac{1}{p}-\frac{(N-1)q-2(N-3)}{2N(2-q)}<\frac{1}{r}<\frac{(N-1)(2-q)}{2q}\frac{1}{p}+\frac{(N-1)[(N-1)q-2(N-3)]}{4qN}

if
q<2
. Then there exists
C>0
such that

(3.6)

\left\|{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}f\right\|_{r}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\qquad\text{for all functions $f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$.}

Remark 3.2
.

(i) In the special case
r=p
, the assumptions of Theorem

Hence Theorem

(ii) The assumption

considered in

.

(iii) Geometrically, the conditions (

), (

to be contained in the trapezoid in

with part of the boundary being excluded due to the fact that some of these inequalities are strict. In the case

In order to prove Theorem

, we adapt the strategy of

. Throughout the remainder of this section, we fix a closed subgroup
G\subset O(N)
and
Q\in L^{\infty}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. We first note the following lemma which is a basic consequence of complex interpolation.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
1\leq q<\infty
and let
\rho\in{\mathcal{S}}
be a radial function. Suppose furthermore that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|Q[\rho*(Qu)]\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq CD\left\|u\right\|_{q^{\prime}}

and

(3.8)

\displaystyle\|Q[\rho*(Qu)]\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq CD^{1-N}\left\|u\right\|_{1}

for all
u\in{\mathcal{S}}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

with constants
C,D>0
. Suppose furthermore that
p,r\in(1,\infty)
satisfy

(3.9)

\frac{d_{q,1}}{p}\leq\frac{1}{r}\leq\min\left\{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p},\frac{d_{q,2}}{p}\right\},

where

(3.10)

d_{q,1}=\min\left\{\frac{q}{2},\frac{2}{q}\right\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad d_{q,2}=\max\left\{\frac{q}{2},\frac{2}{q}\right\}.

Then we have

(3.11)

\left\|Q\bigl[\rho*(Qu)\bigr]\right\|_{r}\leq CD^{A_{r,p,q}}\left\|u\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\qquad\text{for all $u\in{\mathcal{S}}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$,}

with

(3.12)

A_{r,p,q}:=\frac{2qN}{q+2}\Bigl(\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{p}\Bigr)-(N-1).

Proof.

Since
\rho\in{\mathcal{S}}
is radial, the convolution with
\rho
maps
G
-invariant functions to
G
-invariant functions. Moreover, by assumption we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}vQ\bigl[\rho*(Qu)\bigr]dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}uQ\bigl[\rho*(Qv)\bigr]dx\leq\|u\|_{2}\bigl\|Q\bigl[\rho*(Qv)\bigr]\bigr\|_{2}\leq CD\|u\|_{2}\left\|v\right\|_{q^{\prime}}

for all
u,v\in{\mathcal{S}}_{G}
. By duality, we therefore have

(3.13)

\left\|Q\bigl[\rho*(Qu)\bigr]\right\|_{q}\leq CD\left\|u\right\|_{2}\qquad\text{for all $u\in{\mathcal{S}}_{G}$.}

Note that, by (
3.9
), the point
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{r})
is contained in the closed symmetric triangle in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
spanned by the points
(\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{2})
,
(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{q})
and
(0,0)
. Hence we can write

(3.14)

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\lambda}{q}+\frac{\mu}{2},\quad\frac{1}{r}=\frac{\lambda}{2}+\frac{\mu}{q}\quad\qquad\text{with}\quad\qquad\lambda,\mu\geq 0,\quad\lambda+\mu\leq 1.

With
\gamma:=1-(\lambda+\mu)
, we thus have

\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=\frac{\lambda}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{\mu}{2}+\frac{\gamma}{1},\qquad\frac{1}{r}=\frac{\lambda}{2}+\frac{\mu}{q}+\frac{\gamma}{\infty},

so complex interpolation of the inequalities (
3.7
), (
3.8
) and (
3.13
) gives

(3.15)

\left\|Q\bigl[\rho*(Qu)\bigr]\right\|_{r}\leq CD^{\lambda+\mu-\gamma(N-1)}\left\|u\right\|_{p^{\prime}}=CD^{1-\gamma N}\left\|u\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\qquad\text{for all $u\in\mathcal{S}_{G}$.}

Solving (
3.14
) yields

\lambda+\mu=\frac{2q}{q+2}\Bigl(\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{p}\Bigr)
and therefore
\gamma=1-\frac{2q}{q+2}\Bigl(\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{p}\Bigr).
Hence (
3.11
) follows from (
3.15
).
∎

Next we decompose the fundamental solution
\Phi
as in
[
7
]
and
[
3

. For this we fix
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\widehat{\psi}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}
is radial,
0\leq\widehat{\psi}\leq 1
and
\widehat{\psi}=1
for
\left||\xi|-1\right|\leq\frac{1}{6}
,
\widehat{\psi}(\xi)=0
for
\left||\xi|-1\right|\geq\frac{1}{4}.
We then write

\Phi=\Phi_{1}+\Phi_{2}\qquad\text{with}\quad\Phi_{1}=\psi\ast\Phi,\quad\Phi_{2}=\Phi-\Phi_{1}.

Accordingly, we write

{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}={\mathcal{R}}^{1}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}+{\mathcal{R}}^{2}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}\qquad\text{with}\quad{\mathcal{R}}^{i}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}(f):=Q[\Phi_{i}\ast(Qf)],\quad i=1,2.

As shown in
[
3
, Section 3]
, we have

(3.16)

|\Phi_{1}(x)|\leq C(1+|x|)^{\frac{1-N}{2}}\qquad\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}

and

(3.17)

|\Phi_{2}(x)|\leq C\min\{|x|^{2-N},|x|^{-N}\}\qquad\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}.$}

In particular, by the Hardy Littlewood Sobolev inequality, we have the following.

Lemma 3.4
.

For every pair of numbers
p,r\in(1,\infty)
satisfying

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{r}\geq\frac{N-2}{N},

the convolution operator
f\mapsto\Phi_{2}*f
defines a bounded linear map from
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Consequently, the operator
{\mathcal{R}}^{2}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}
also defines a bounded linear map from
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
in this case.

Next we turn to the operator
{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}^{1}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
q\in\bigl[1,\infty)
be given such that

(G,q,Q)
is an admissible extension triple. Moreover, let
p,r\in(1,\infty)
satisfy

(3.18)

\frac{d_{q,1}}{p}\leq\frac{1}{r}\leq\frac{d_{q,2}}{p}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{r}<\frac{q+2}{2q}\,\frac{N-1}{N}

with
d_{q,1},d_{q,2}
defined in (
3.10
). Then there exists
C>0
such that

\left\|{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}^{1}f\right\|_{r}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\qquad\text{for all functions $f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$.}

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be such that
\widehat{\varphi}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is radial,
0\leq\widehat{\varphi}\leq 1
with
\widehat{\varphi}\equiv 1
for
\left||\xi|-1\right|\leq\frac{1}{4}
and
\widehat{\varphi}\equiv 0
for
\left||\xi|-1\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}
. By construction of
\Phi_{1}
, we then have
\widehat{\Phi_{1}}=\widehat{\Phi_{1}}\widehat{\varphi}
, which means that
\Phi_{1}=(2\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\Phi_{1}*\varphi

and therefore

{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}^{1}f=Q[\Phi_{1}\ast(Qf)]=(2\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}Q[\Phi_{1}\ast\varphi\ast Qf]\qquad\text{for $f\in{\mathcal{S}}$.}

Choose
\eta\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
radial with
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
\eta(x)=1
for
|x|\leq 1
,
\eta(x)=0
for
|x|\geq 2
. Moreover we define

\psi_{j}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
by
\psi_{0}=\eta
and
\psi_{j}(x)=\eta(2^{-j}x)-\eta(2^{-(j-1)}x)
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Then we have the dyadic composition

\Phi_{1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Phi_{1}^{j}\qquad\text{with}\quad\Phi_{1}^{j}=\psi_{j}\Phi_{1}

Using (
3.16
), we find that

\left\|\Phi_{1}^{j}\right\|_{\infty}\leq C2^{-\frac{j(N-1)}{2}},\quad\text{ for all }j,

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
j
. Using that
\widehat{\Phi_{1}^{j}}
is radial, we get, with Plancherel’s theorem and Lemma
2.3
,

\displaystyle\left\|\bigl(\Phi_{1}^{j}\ast\varphi\bigr)\ast(Qf)\right\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=C\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\widehat{\Phi_{1}^{j}}(\xi)\,\widehat{\varphi}\,\widehat{Qf}(\xi)|^{2}\penalty\ d\xi\leq C\int\limits^{\frac{7}{4}}_{\frac{1}{4}}r^{N-1}|\widehat{\Phi_{1}^{j}}(r)|^{2}\int\limits_{\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}|\widehat{Qf}(r\omega)|^{2}\penalty\ d\sigma(\omega)\penalty\ dr

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Phi_{1}^{j}\|^{2}_{2}\left\|Qf\right\|^{2}_{q^{\prime}}\leq C2^{j}\left\|Qf\right\|^{2}_{q^{\prime}}\qquad\text{for all $f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{G}$,}

where the constant does not depend on
j
. Consequently, we thus have

\left\|Q\bigl(\Phi_{1}^{j}\ast\varphi\bigr)\ast(Qf)\right\|_{2}\leq C2^{\frac{j}{2}}\left\|Qf\right\|_{q^{\prime}}\qquad\text{for all $f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{G}$.}

Moreover, we have

\|\Phi_{1}^{j}\ast\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq\|\Phi_{1}^{j}\|_{\infty}\|\varphi\|_{1}\leq C2^{-\frac{j(N-1)}{2}},\quad\text{ for all }j,

which implies that

\left\|Q\bigl(\Phi_{1}^{j}\ast\varphi\bigr)\ast(Qf)\right\|_{\infty}\leq C2^{-\frac{j(N-1)}{2}}\|Qf\|_{1}\leq C2^{-\frac{j(N-1)}{2}}\|f\|_{1}\qquad\text{for all $f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{G}$.}

Since the assumption (
3.18
) implies (
3.9
), we may apply Lemma
3.3
to the radial kernel
\Phi_{1}^{j}\ast\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and deduce that

(3.19)

\left\|Q\bigl(\Phi_{1}^{j}\ast\varphi\bigr)\ast(Qf)\right\|_{r}\leq C2^{\frac{j}{2}A_{r,p,q}}\left\|f\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\qquad\text{for all $f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{G}$}

with
A_{r,p,q}
given in (
3.12
). By assumption (
3.18
), we have
A_{r,p,q}<0
. Since, as remarked above,
\Phi_{1}=(2\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\Phi_{1}\ast\varphi
,
we deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}^{1}f\right\|_{r}

\displaystyle=\left\|Q\Phi_{1}\ast(Qf)\right\|_{r}=(2\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\left\|Q(\Phi_{1}*\varphi)\ast(Qf)\right\|_{r}

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left\|Q(\Phi_{1}^{j}*\varphi)\ast(Qf)\right\|_{r}\leq C_{0}\|f\|_{p^{\prime}}\qquad\text{for all $f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{G}$}

with
C_{0}=C(2\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{\frac{j}{2}A_{r,p,q}}<\infty
. The proof is finished.
∎

We may now complete the

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
M
denote the set of points

. By combining Lemma

Hence the closure of
M
contains the points
(\frac{N-1}{qN},\frac{N-1}{2N})
,
(\frac{N-1}{2N},\frac{N-1}{qN})
and therefore also the line segment between these points. Moreover, by

,
M
also contains the open line segment between the points

\frac{N-2}{N}\>\leq\>\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{r}\><\>\frac{q+2}{2q}\,\frac{N-1}{N},

4.
Nonvanishing for
G
-invariant functions

Our next aim is to deduce a nonvanishing theorem for the operator
{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}
and
G
-invariant functions, where again

G\subset O(N)
is a closed subgroup and
Q\in L^{\infty}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a given weight function. We restate Theorem
1.3
for the reader’s convenience.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
N\geq 3
, let
G\subset O(N)
be a closed subgroup, let
Q\in L^{\infty}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and let
q\in\bigl[1,\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1}\bigr]
be
such that
(G,q,Q)
is an admissible extension triple. Moreover, let
p\in\bigl(\frac{2N}{N-1}\frac{2q}{q+2},\frac{2N}{N-2}\bigr]
.
Then for every bounded sequence
(v_{n})_{n}\subset L^{p^{\prime}}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying
\Bigl|\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v_{n}{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}(v_{n})\penalty\ dx\Bigr|>0
, there exist – after passing to a subsequence – numbers
R,\zeta>0
and a sequence of points
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
with

(4.1)

\int\limits_{B_{{R}}(x_{n})}|Qv_{n}(x)|^{p^{\prime}}\penalty\ dx\geq\zeta,\quad\text{ for all }n.

The remainder of this section is devoted to the proof of this theorem. For this we fix
N\geq 3
and
q\in\bigl[1,\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1}\bigr]
such that
(G,q,Q)
is an admissible extension triple. Moreover, we
keep using the notation of the previous section, so we write
\Phi=\Phi_{1}+\Phi_{2}
and
{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}={\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}^{1}+{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}^{2}
.
We need to analyse the operators
{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}^{1}
and
{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}^{2}
separately. We start by proving the following variant of Proposition
3.5
for the operator
{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}^{1}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be such that
\widehat{\varphi}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is radial,
0\leq\widehat{\varphi}\leq 1
with
\widehat{\varphi}\equiv 1
for
\left||\xi|-1\right|\leq\frac{1}{4}
and
\widehat{\varphi}\equiv 0
for
\left||\xi|-1\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

Moreover, let
\eta\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be radial with
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
\eta(x)=1
for
|x|\leq 1
,
\eta(x)=0
for
|x|\geq 2
, and let
\eta_{k}(x)=\eta(2^{-k}x)
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Finally, let
p\in\bigl(\frac{2N}{N-1}\frac{2q}{q+2},\infty)
and
A_{p,q}:=\frac{N}{p}\frac{4q}{q+2}+1-N<0
.
Then there exists
C>0
such that for
k\geq 1
we have

\left\|Q\Bigl([(1-\eta_{k})\Phi_{1}]\ast\varphi\ast(Qf)\Bigr)\right\|_{p}\leq C\frac{2^{\frac{k+1}{2}A_{p,q}}}{1-2^{\frac{A_{p,q}}{2}}}\left\|f\right\|_{p^{\prime}}

for all functions
f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{G}
.

Proof.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Using the given function
\eta
, we let
\Phi_{1}^{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
be defined as in the proof of Proposition
3.5
. The proof of this proposition yields, in particular, inequality (
3.19
) with
r=p
, which writes as

\left\|Q\bigl[\bigl(\Phi_{1}^{j}\ast\varphi\bigr)\ast(Qf)\bigr]\right\|_{p}\leq C2^{\frac{j}{2}A_{p,q}}\left\|f\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\qquad\text{for $j\in\mathbb{N}$ and all functions $f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{G}$}

with
A_{p,q}:=A{p,p,q}=\frac{N}{p}\frac{4q}{q+2}+1-N
, cf. (
3.12
). Moreover, by construction, we have the dyadic decomposition

(1-\eta_{k})\Phi_{1}=\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=k+1}\Phi_{1}^{j}

and therefore

\displaystyle\left\|Q\Bigl([(1-\eta_{k})\Phi_{1}]\ast\varphi\ast(Qf)\Bigr)\right\|_{p}\leq\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=k+1}\left\|Q\bigl[\bigl(\Phi_{1}^{j}\ast\varphi\bigr)\ast(Qf)\bigr]\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{p^{\prime}}\sum\limits^{\infty}_{j=k+1}2^{\frac{j}{2}A_{p,q}}

\displaystyle=C\frac{2^{\frac{k+1}{2}A_{p,q}}}{1-2^{\frac{A_{p,q}}{2}}}\left\|f\right\|_{p^{\prime}},

for all functions
f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{G}
, as claimed.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
p>\frac{2N}{N-1}\frac{2q}{q+2}
and suppose that
(v_{n})_{n}\subset\mathcal{S}_{G}
is an
L^{p^{\prime}}
-bounded sequence with

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\>\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{B_{\rho}(y)}|Qv_{n}|^{p^{\prime}}\penalty\ dx=0,\quad\text{ for all }\rho>0.

Then

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Qv_{n}[\Phi_{1}\ast(Qv_{n})]\penalty\ dx\to 0,\qquad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Proof.

Let, as in the assumptions of Proposition
4.2
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be such that
\widehat{\varphi}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is radial,
0\leq\widehat{\varphi}\leq 1
with
\widehat{\varphi}\equiv 1
for
\left||\xi|-1\right|\leq\frac{1}{4}
and
\widehat{\varphi}\equiv 0
for
\left||\xi|-1\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}
. Moreover, let
w_{n}=\varphi\ast(Qv_{n})
. Then we have

(4.2)

\|w_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq\|\varphi\|_{1}\|Qv_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq\|\varphi\|_{1}\|Q\|_{\infty}\|v_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
by Young’s inequality, so
(w_{n})_{n}
is also a bounded sequence in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
by assumption. Since
\widehat{\Phi_{1}}=\widehat{\Phi_{1}}\,\widehat{\varphi}
, we have
(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}\Phi_{1}=\Phi_{1}\ast\varphi
. Therefore, with
\eta_{k}
defined as in Proposition
4.2
, we can write

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Qv_{n}[\Phi_{1}\ast(Qv_{n})]\penalty\ dx

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Qv_{n}[\Phi_{1}\ast\varphi\ast(Qv_{n})]\penalty\ dx

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Qv_{n}[\eta_{k}\Phi_{1}\ast\varphi\ast(Qv_{n})]\penalty\ dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Qv_{n}[(1-\eta_{k})\Phi_{1}\ast\varphi\ast(Qv_{n})]\penalty\ dx,

for every
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
, where

\Bigl|\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Qv_{n}[(1-\eta_{k})\Phi_{1}\ast\varphi\ast(Qv_{n})]\penalty\ dx\Bigr|\leq\|v_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}\left\|Q\Bigl([(1-\eta_{k})\Phi_{1}]\ast\varphi\ast(Qv_{n})\Bigr)\right\|_{p}\leq C\frac{2^{\frac{k+1}{2}A_{p,q}}}{1-2^{\frac{A_{p,q}}{2}}}\left\|v_{n}\right\|_{p^{\prime}}^{2}

by Hölder’s inequality and Proposition
4.2
. Since
A_{p,q}<0
, it follows that

(4.3)

\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Bigl|\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Qv_{n}[(1-\eta_{k})\Phi_{1}\ast\varphi\ast(Qv_{n})]\penalty\ dx\Bigr|\to 0,\qquad\text{as }k\to\infty.

For fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we now choose
R=2^{k+1}
, which implies that
\eta_{k}\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}
.
Decomposing
\mathbb{R}^{N}
into disjoint
N
-cubes
\{Z_{l}\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
of side
length
R
, and considering for each
l
the
N-
cube
Z^{\prime}_{l}
with the same center as
Z_{l}
but with side length
3R
, we find

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Qv_{n}[\bigl(\eta_{k}\Phi_{1}\bigr)\ast\varphi\ast(Qv_{n})]\penalty\ dx\Bigr|=\Bigl|\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Qv_{n}[\bigl(\eta_{k}\Phi_{1}\bigr)\ast w_{n}]\penalty\ dx\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits^{\infty}_{l=1}\int\limits_{Z_{l}}\Bigl(\int\limits_{|x-y|<R}|\Phi_{1}(x-y)|\penalty\ |Qv_{n}(x)|\penalty\ |w_{n}(y)|\penalty\ dx\Bigr)\penalty\ dy

\displaystyle\leq\|\Phi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\sum\limits^{\infty}_{l=1}\int\limits_{Z^{\prime}_{l}}|Qv_{n}|\penalty\ dx\int\limits_{Z^{\prime}_{l}}|w_{n}|\penalty\ dx

\displaystyle\leq\|\Phi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\Bigl[\sum\limits^{\infty}_{l=1}\Bigl(\int\limits_{Z^{\prime}_{l}}|w_{n}|\penalty\ dx\Bigr)^{p^{\prime}}\Bigr]^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\Bigl[\sum\limits^{\infty}_{l=1}\Bigl(\int\limits_{Z^{\prime}_{l}}|Qv_{n}|\penalty\ dx\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Phi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}(3R)^{\frac{2N}{p}}\Bigl[\sum\limits^{\infty}_{l=1}\int\limits_{Z^{\prime}_{l}}|w_{n}|^{p^{\prime}}\penalty\ dx\Bigr]^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\Bigl[\sum\limits^{\infty}_{l=1}\Bigl(\int\limits_{Z^{\prime}_{l}}|Qv_{n}|^{p^{\prime}}\penalty\ dx\Bigr)^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}\Bigr]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Phi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}(3R)^{\frac{2N}{p}}3^{\frac{N}{p^{\prime}}}\|w_{n}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\Bigl[\sup\limits_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\int\limits_{Z^{\prime}_{l}}|Qv_{n}|^{p^{\prime}}\penalty\ dx\Bigr]^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}-1}\Bigl[\sum\limits^{\infty}_{l=1}\int\limits_{Z^{\prime}_{l}}|Qv_{n}|^{p^{\prime}}\penalty\ dx\Bigr]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Phi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}(3R)^{\frac{2N}{p}}3^{N}\|w_{n}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\Bigl[\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{B_{3R\sqrt{N}}(y)}|Qv_{n}|^{p^{\prime}}\penalty\ dx\Bigr]^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}-1}\|Qv_{n}\|^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}_{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Phi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|Q\|_{\infty}^{1+\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\|\varphi\|_{1}(3R)^{\frac{2N}{p}}3^{N}\|v_{n}\|^{1+\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}_{p^{\prime}}\Bigl[\>\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{B_{3R\sqrt{N}}(y)}|Qv_{n}|^{p^{\prime}}\penalty\ dx\Bigr]^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}-1},

where we used (
4.2
) in the last step. By assumption, it now follows that

(4.4)

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Qv_{n}[\bigl(\eta_{k}\Phi_{1}\bigr)\ast\varphi\ast(Qv_{n})]\penalty\ dx\to 0\qquad\text{as $n\to\infty$ for every $k\in\mathbb{N}$.}

The claim now follows by combining (
4.3
) and (
4.4
).
∎

Regarding
\Phi_{2}
we make use of the following variant of
[
4
, Theorem 2.5]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
2<p\leq\frac{2N}{N-2}
and suppose that
(v_{n})_{n}
is a bounded sequence in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{B_{\rho}(y)}|v_{n}|^{p^{\prime}}\penalty\ dx=0,\qquad\text{ for all }\rho>0.

Then

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v_{n}[\Phi_{2}\ast v_{n}]\penalty\ dx\to 0\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Proof.

The claim follows from
[
4
, Theorem 2.5]
in the case where
v_{n}\in\mathcal{S}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
If
(v_{n})_{n}
is an arbitrary bounded sequence in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we first recall that, by Lemma
3.4
, there exists a constant
C>0
with

\|\Phi_{2}\ast v\|_{p}\leq C\|v\|_{p^{\prime}}\qquad\text{for every $v\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$.}

Moreover we choose, by density,
\tilde{v}_{n}\in{\mathcal{S}}
with

\|v_{n}-\tilde{v}_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq\frac{1}{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The assumption then implies that also

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{B_{\rho}(y)}|\tilde{v}_{n}|^{p^{\prime}}\penalty\ dx=0,\qquad\text{ for all }\rho>0

and therefore

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\tilde{v}_{n}[\Phi_{2}\ast\tilde{v}_{n}]\penalty\ dx\to 0,\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty

by
[
4
, Theorem 2.5]
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[v_{n}(\Phi_{2}\ast v_{n})-\tilde{v}_{n}(\Phi_{2}\ast\tilde{v}_{n})]\,dx\Bigr|=\Bigl|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(v_{n}-\tilde{v}_{n})\Phi_{2}\ast(v_{n}+\tilde{v}_{n})\,dx\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq C\|v_{n}-\tilde{v}_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}\|v_{n}+\tilde{v}_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq\frac{C(1+\frac{1}{n})\|v_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}}{n}\to 0\qquad\text{as $n\to\infty$}

and thus also

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v_{n}[\Phi_{2}\ast v_{n}]\penalty\ dx\to 0\quad\text{ as }n\to\infty,

as claimed.
∎

We are now in position to finish the proof of Theorem
4.1
:

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let
(v_{n})_{n}\subset L^{p^{\prime}}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a bounded sequence, and suppose by contradiction that (
4.1
) does not hold. Then we have

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{B_{\rho}(y)}|Qv_{n}|^{p^{\prime}}\penalty\ dx=0,\qquad\text{ for all }\rho>0.

By density, we may choose
\tilde{v}_{n}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{G}
with

\|v_{n}-\tilde{v}_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq\frac{1}{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, which implies that
\|Qv_{n}-Q\tilde{v}_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}\leq\frac{\|Q\|_{\infty}}{n}
for all
n
and therefore also

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int\limits_{B_{\rho}(y)}|Q\tilde{v}_{n}|^{p^{\prime}}\penalty\ dx=0,\qquad\text{ for all }\rho>0.

Combining Lemma
4.3
(applied to
\tilde{v}_{n}
) and Lemma
4.4
(applied to
Q\tilde{v}_{n}
), we then deduce that

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\tilde{v}_{n}{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}\tilde{v}_{n}dx=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q\tilde{v}_{n}[\Phi_{1}\ast(Q\tilde{v}_{n})]\penalty\ dx+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Q\tilde{v}_{n}[\Phi_{2}\ast(Q\tilde{v}_{n})]\penalty\ dx\rightarrow 0\qquad\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Moreover, by Theorem
1.2
we have

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}[v_{n}{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}v_{n}-\tilde{v}_{n}{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}\tilde{v}_{n}]\,dx\Bigr|=\Bigl|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(v_{n}-\tilde{v}_{n}){\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}(v_{n}+\tilde{v}_{n})\,dx\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{n}-\tilde{v}_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}\|{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}(v_{n}+\tilde{v}_{n})\|_{p}\leq C\|v_{n}-\tilde{v}_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}\|v_{n}+\tilde{v}_{n}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\to 0\qquad\text{as $n\to\infty$.}

Consequently, we also have that
\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v_{n}{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}v_{n}dx\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, contrary to the assumption. The claim thus follows.
∎

5.
Dual variational framework and
G-
invariant solutions

Let
G\subset O(N)
be a fixed closed subgroup, and let
Q\in L^{\infty}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a nonnegative fixed weight function with
Q\not\equiv 0
. We now focus our attention to the equation

(5.1)

-\Delta u-u=Q(x)|u|^{p-2}u,\qquad u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

To prove the existence of nontrivial real-valued solutions of (
5.1
), we will use the dual variational approach introduced in
[
3
]
and consider the operator
K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}
formally defined as
K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}f=Q^{\frac{1}{p}}R(Q^{\frac{1}{p}}f)
, where

R
denotes the real part of the Helmholtz resolvent operator
{\mathcal{R}}
, i.e.,
Rg=\bigl({\rm Re\,}\Phi\bigr)\ast g
with the fundamental solution
\Phi
defined in (
1.3
).

To analyze the mapping properties of
K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}
and to set up a variational framework, we assume, as in Theorem
1.4
, that
q\in\bigl[1,\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1}\bigr]
and
p\in\bigl(\max\{\frac{2N}{N-1}\frac{2q}{q+2},2\},\frac{2N}{N-2}\bigr)
are fixed such that
(G,q,Q^{\frac{1}{p}})
is an admissible extension triple.
The following is an immediate consequence of Theorem
3.1
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\tilde{p},r\in(1,\infty)
satisfy (
3.3
) with
\tilde{p}
in place of
p
, and suppose moreover that (
3.4
) holds with
\tilde{p}
in place of
p
if
q\geq 2
, and that (
3.5
) holds with
\tilde{p}
in place of
p
if
q<2
.

Then the operator
K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}
is bounded as a map
L_{G}^{\tilde{p}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L_{G}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

We note that Lemma
5.1
applies in particular in the case
r=\tilde{p}=p
, so

K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}
is a bounded operator
L_{G}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L_{G}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

We also note the following immediate corollary of Lemma
5.1
.

Corollary 5.2
.

There exist
\sigma_{1}<p<\sigma_{2}
with the property that
K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}
is bounded as a map
L_{G}^{\sigma_{i}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L_{G}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and as a map

L_{G}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L_{G}^{\sigma_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
i=1,2
.

Next we note the following variant of
[
3
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Lemma 5.3
.

The operator
K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}:L_{G}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L_{G}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is locally compact, i.e., the operators

K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}\mathds{1}_{B}:L_{G}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L_{G}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathds{1}_{B}K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}:L_{G}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L_{G}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

are compact for every bounded and measurable set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Proof.

Let
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be bounded and measurable, and fix
s\in\bigl[\frac{2(N+1)}{N-1},\frac{2N}{N-2}\bigr)
with
s\geq p
, i.e.,
s^{\prime}\leq p^{\prime}
. By
[
3
, Lemma 4.1]
, the operator
\mathds{1}_{B}K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}:L^{s^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is compact. By duality, the operator

K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}\mathds{1}_{B}:L^{s^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is therefore also compact.

Next, let
(v_{n})_{n}\subset L_{G}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a sequence with
v_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
in
L_{G}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then we have

w_{n}:=\mathds{1}_{B}v_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
in
L_{G}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and thus also in
L_{G}^{s^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, since
B
has finite measure.
By the compactness property mentioned above, it follows that
K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}\mathds{1}_{B}v_{n}=K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}\mathds{1}_{B}w_{n}\to 0
strongly in
L_{G}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Moreover, it follows from Corollary
5.2
that the sequence of functions
K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}\mathds{1}_{B}v_{n}=K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}w_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
is bounded in
L_{G}^{\sigma_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some
\sigma_{1}<p
. Since
\sigma_{1}<p\leq s
, it thus follows by interpolation that there exists
\theta\in(0,1]
with

\|K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}\mathds{1}_{B}v_{n}\|_{p}\leq\|K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}\mathds{1}_{B}v_{n}\|^{1-\theta}_{\sigma_{1}}\|K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}\mathds{1}_{B}v_{n}\|_{s}^{\theta}\to 0\qquad\text{as $n\to\infty$.}

Hence the operator
K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}\mathds{1}_{B}:L_{G}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L_{G}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is compact, and by duality it follows that also

\mathds{1}_{B}K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}:L_{G}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L_{G}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a compact operator.
∎

As in
[
3
]
, we now introduce the (dual) energy functional

J:L_{G}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to\mathbb{R},\qquad J(v)=\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|^{p^{\prime}}\penalty\ dx-\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v[K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}v](x)\penalty\ dx.

Then
J
is of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
with

J^{\prime}(v)w=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|v|^{p^{\prime}-2}v-K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}v)w\penalty\ dx\qquad\text{for $v,w\in L_{G}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$.}

Moreover, we have

Lemma 5.4
.

If
v\in L_{G}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a critical point of
J
, then
u=RQ^{\frac{1}{p}}v
is a real-valued solution of
(
5.1
) of class
W^{2,q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{1,s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
q\geq p
,
s\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Let
w\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and let
w_{G}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be defined by

w_{G}=\int\limits_{G}w\circ Ad\mu(A),\qquad\text{i.e.,}\qquad w_{G}(x)=\int\limits_{G}w(Ax)d\mu(A)\quad\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$,}

where
\mu
is the Haar-measure of
G
. Since
v
is
G
-invariant, it follows that

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|v|^{p^{\prime}-2}v-K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}v)w\penalty\ dx=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|v|^{p^{\prime}-2}v-K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}v)[w\circ A]\penalty\ dx\qquad\text{for all $A\in G$}

and therefore

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|v|^{p^{\prime}-2}v-K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}v)w\penalty\ dx=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|v|^{p^{\prime}-2}v-K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}v)w_{G}\penalty\ dx=J^{\prime}(v)w_{G}=0

Consequently, we have
|v|^{p^{\prime}-2}v=K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}v
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, which implies that
u=RQ^{\frac{1}{p}}v
satisfies the equation

u=RQ|u|^{p-2}u\qquad\text{in $L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$.}

The claim now follows by
[
3
, Lemma 4.3]
.
∎

Next we note that the functional
J
has a mountain pass geometry. More precisely, we have:

Lemma 5.5
.

(i)

There exists
\delta>0
and
0<\rho<1
such that
J(v)\geq\delta>0
for all
v\in L^{p^{\prime}}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\|v\|_{p^{\prime}}=\rho
.

(ii)

There is
v_{0}\in L^{p^{\prime}}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\|v_{0}\|_{p^{\prime}}>1
and
J(v_{0})<0
.

(iii)

Every Palais-Smale sequence for
J
is bounded in
L^{p^{\prime}}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

(iv)

There exists a Palais-Smale sequence for
J
at the mountain pass level

(5.2)

d:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\max_{t\in[0,1]}J(\gamma(t))\>>\>0,

where
\Gamma=\{\gamma\in C([0,1],L^{p^{\prime}}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\>:\>\gamma(0)=0,\,\|\gamma(1)\|_{p^{\prime}}>\rho,\,J(\gamma(1))<0\}
.

Proof.

Since
p>2
, the parts (i)-(iii) are proved in
[
3
, Lemma 4.2]
for
G=\{id\}
, and the proof remains the same for general closed subgroups
G\subset O(N)
.
Moreover, the positivity of the mountain pass level
c
defined in (
5.2
) is a direct consequence of (i) and (ii), which also shows that the set
\Gamma
is nonempty. Finally, the proof of the existence of a Palais-Smale sequence for
J
at level
d
is exactly the same as the proof of
[
3
, Lemma 6.1]
. Here we note that periodicity of
Q
was assumed in
[
3
, Section 6]
, but this property is not used in Lemma 6.1.
∎

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
(v_{n})_{n}\subset L^{p^{\prime}}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a Palais-Smale sequence of
J
with
c:=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}J(v_{n})>0
. Moreover, suppose that one of the following conditions hold:

(A1)

For some
R>0
, we have
\lim\limits_{|x|\to\infty}\|Q\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(x))}=0
.

(A2)

For every
R>0
we have
\lim\limits_{|x|\to\infty}N_{G}(x,R)=\infty
, where, for
R>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
,
N_{G}(x,R)
denotes the maximal number of elements of a subset
H\subset G
with
B_{R}(Ax)\cap B_{R}(A^{\prime}x)=\varnothing
for
A,A^{\prime}\in H
.

Then, after passing to a subsequence, we have

v_{n}\rightharpoonup v\qquad\text{in $L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$,}

where
v\in L^{p^{\prime}}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}
is a critical point of
J
.

Proof.

We first note that
(v_{n})_{n}
is bounded by Lemma
5.5
. Consequently, since
L^{p^{\prime}}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is reflexive, there exists
v\in L^{p^{\prime}}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

(5.3)

v_{n}\rightharpoonup v\qquad\text{in $L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$.}

Moreover,

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v_{n}K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}v_{n}\,dx=\frac{2p^{\prime}}{2-p^{\prime}}\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\left(J(v_{n})-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}J^{\prime}(v_{n})v_{n}\right)=\frac{2p^{\prime}}{2-p^{\prime}}c>0

by assumption, which implies that

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\Bigl|\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v_{n}{\mathcal{R}}_{\text{\tiny$Q^{\frac{1}{p}}$}}v_{n}\penalty\ dx\Bigr|>0.

Since moreover
(G,q,Q^{\frac{1}{p}})
is an admissible extension triple by assumption, Theorem
1.3
applies and yields
\delta,R>0
and a sequence of points
(x_{n})_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that, after passing to a subsequence,

(5.4)

\int\limits_{B_{R}(x_{n})}|Q^{\frac{1}{p}}v_{n}|^{p^{\prime}}\penalty\ dx\geq\delta>0,\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

We claim that
(x_{n})_{n}
has to be bounded. To see this, we argue by contradiction and assume that, after passing to a subsequence again,
|x_{n}|\to\infty
.
We distinguish two cases.

Case 1: (A1) holds.

In this case we put
\varphi_{n}:=Q^{p-1}v_{n}\mathds{1}_{B_{R}(x_{n})}
, and we note that
(\varphi_{n})_{n}
is a bounded sequence in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Moreover, we have

(5.5)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B_{R}(x_{n})}|Q^{\frac{1}{p}}v_{n}|^{p^{\prime}}\penalty\ dx

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v_{n}|^{p^{\prime}-2}v_{n}\varphi_{n}dx=J^{\prime}(v_{n})\varphi_{n}+\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v_{n}K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}\varphi_{n}dx

\displaystyle\leq o(1)\left\|\varphi_{n}\right\|_{p}+\Bigl|\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v_{n}K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}\varphi_{n}\penalty\ dx\Bigl|=o(1)+\|v_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}\|K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}\varphi_{n}\|_{p}

as
n\to\infty
. By Corollary
5.2
, there exists
\sigma>p^{\prime}
and
C>0
with the property that

\|K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}\varphi_{n}\|_{p}\leq C\|\varphi_{n}\|_{\sigma^{\prime}}\qquad\text{for $n\in\mathbb{N}$,}

whereas, since
\sigma^{\prime}<p^{\prime}
and by Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{n}\|_{\sigma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\|Q^{p-1}v_{n}\|_{L^{\sigma^{\prime}}(B_{R}(x_{n}))}\leq\Bigl(\int_{B_{R}(x_{n})}|Q|^{\frac{p^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}(p-1)}{p^{\prime}-\sigma^{\prime}}}dx\Bigr)^{\frac{p^{\prime}-\sigma^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}}\|v_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\|Q\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(x_{n}))}\Bigr)^{\frac{p^{\prime}-\sigma^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}}\|Q\|_{\infty}^{\bigl(\frac{p^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}(p-1)}{p^{\prime}-\sigma^{\prime}}-1\bigr)\frac{p^{\prime}-\sigma^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}}\|v_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}

Since
\|Q\|_{L^{1}(B_{R}(x_{n}))}\to 0
by (
1.10
), it thus follows that
\|\varphi_{n}\|_{\sigma^{\prime}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
Here we note that, by an easy covering argument, (
1.10
) holds for every
R>0
if it holds for one
R>0
. Going back to (
5.5
), we thus deduce that

\int\limits_{B_{R}(x_{n})}|Q^{\frac{1}{p}}v_{n}|^{p^{\prime}}\penalty\ dx\to 0\qquad\text{as $n\to\infty$,}

which contradicts (
5.4
).

Case 2: (A2) holds.

In this case it follows from (
5.4
) and the fact that
v_{n}
and
Q
are
G
-invariant that

\|Q^{\frac{1}{p}}v_{n}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}\geq N_{G}(x_{n},R)\int\limits_{B_{R}(x_{n})}|Q^{\frac{1}{p}}v_{n}|^{p^{\prime}}\penalty\ dx\geq N_{G}(x_{n},R)\delta\to\infty

as
n\to\infty
, which contradicts the boundedness of the sequence
(v_{n})_{n}

in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Since in both cases we have reached a contradiction, we conclude that
(x_{n})_{n}
is bounded. Therefore, making
R
larger if necessary, we can assume that (
5.4
) holds with
x_{n}=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Now for any fixed
G
-invariant function
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, any

and
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle\left|\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|v_{n}|^{p^{\prime}-2}v_{n}-|v_{m}|^{p^{\prime}-2}v_{m}\right)\varphi\penalty\ dx\right|

\displaystyle=\left|J^{\prime}(v_{n})\varphi-J^{\prime}(v_{m})\varphi+\int\limits_{B_{r}}\varphi K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}(v_{n}-v)\penalty\ dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|J^{\prime}(v_{n})-J^{\prime}(v_{m})\right\|\left\|\varphi\right\|_{p^{\prime}}+\left\|\mathds{1}_{B_{r}}K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}(v_{n}-v_{m})\right\|_{p}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{p^{\prime}}.

So by assumption and the local compactness of
K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}
, as stated in Lemma
5.3
, we get that
(|v_{n}|^{p^{\prime}-2}v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{p}(B_{R})
. Consequently,
|v_{n}|^{p^{\prime}-2}v_{n}\to\tilde{v}
strongly in
L^{p}(B_{R})
for some
\tilde{v}\in L^{p}(B_{R})
, and passing to a subsequence also pointwisely almost everywhere on
B_{R}
. This clearly implies that
v_{n}\to|\tilde{v}|^{p-2}\tilde{v}
almost everywhere on
B_{r}
. Now (
5.3
) and the uniqueness of the weak limit gives
\tilde{v}=|v|^{p^{\prime}-2}v
and

0<\delta\leq\int\limits_{B_{R}}|Q^{\frac{1}{p}}v_{n}(x)|^{p^{\prime}}\penalty\ dx\to\int\limits_{B_{R}}|Q^{\frac{1}{p}}v|^{p^{\prime}}\penalty\ dx,\quad\text{as }n\to\infty

which implies that
v\neq 0
.

For every
G
-invariant function
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}
, we now have

\displaystyle J^{\prime}(v)\varphi

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|^{p^{\prime}-2}v\varphi\penalty\ dx-\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}(v)\penalty\ dx

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\left[\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v_{n}|^{p^{\prime}-2}v_{n}\varphi\penalty\ dx-\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\varphi K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}(v_{n})\penalty\ dx\right]

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}J^{\prime}(v_{n})\varphi=0

using the local strong convergence of
|v_{n}|^{p^{\prime}-2}v_{n}
and the continuity of linear operator
K_{\text{\tiny$Q$}}:L^{p^{\prime}}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{p}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. By density, it now follows that
J^{\prime}(v)w=0
for every
w\in L^{p^{\prime}}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, i.e.,
v\in L^{p^{\prime}}_{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}
is a critical point of
J
.
∎

G
-invariant dual ground state solutions as stated in the introduction.

Proof of Theorem

.

As above, it follows by the classical Stein-Tomas estimate that

\lim\limits_{|x|\to\infty}N_{G}(x,R)=\infty\qquad\text{for every $R>0$,}

Proof of Corollary

.

We first note that

satisfies the asymptotic condition (

|L_{\alpha}\cap B_{R}(x)|\to 0\qquad\text{as $|x|\to\infty$ for every $R>0$.}

To see the latter, it suffices to consider a sequence

Next, we first consider the case

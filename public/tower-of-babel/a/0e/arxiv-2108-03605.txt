Can multipartite entanglement be characterized by two-point connected correlation functions ?
Luca Lepori, Andrea Trombettoni, Domenico Giuliano, Johannes Kombe, Jorge Yago Malo, Andrew J. Daley, Augusto Smerzi, Maria Luisa Chiofalo

Abstract
Résumé We discuss under which conditions multipartite entanglement in mixed quantum states can be characterized only in terms of two-point connected correlation functions, as it is the case for pure states. In turn, the latter correlations are defined via a suitable combination of (disconnected) one- and two-point correlation functions. In contrast to the case of pure states, conditions to be satisfied turn out to be rather severe. However, we were able to identify some interesting cases, as when the point-independence is valid of the one-point correlations in each possible decomposition of the density matrix, or when the operators that enter in the correlations are (semi-)positive/negative defined.

Multipartite Entanglement

II.1
Separability and producibility

For a
d

N

;

The usual separability corresponds to

When

is said to host
genuine
ME

For mixed states,

-producibility in

\displaystyle\rho=\sum_{\tilde{\lambda}}p_{\tilde{\lambda}}\,|\tilde{\lambda}\rangle\langle\tilde{\lambda}|\,,

(1)

where

If

In general, the

Moreover, the producibility of

III.1
The general problem

The producible decompositions

The first term,

\displaystyle\bar{V}_{1}[\rho,\hat{O}]_{N}=4\,\sum_{x,y}\mathrm{Tr}\big[\rho\,\hat{o}(x)\hat{o}(y)\big]=4\,\mathrm{Tr}\big[\rho\,\hat{O}^{2}]

(the first term in Eq. (

are meant to be expressed in a generic basis

This expression does not depend explicitly on

III.4
Comments on the space independence of the one-point correlations

Here, having in mind the point
ii)
of Section
III.2
, we discuss under which conditions the independence of the one-point correlations on the point itself can
hold in every decomposition.

At first, we consider, in a given decomposition
|\lambda\rangle
, the matrix elements

\langle\lambda|\hat{o}(x)|\lambda\rangle
and
\langle\lambda|\hat{o}(y)|\lambda\rangle
,
y\neq x
, and we assume the stronger condition of translational invariance.
Note that we do not a priori require that these conditions hold in every decomposition. We can interpolate between the two matrix elements above, writing :

\displaystyle\langle\lambda|\hat{o}(y)|\lambda\rangle=\langle\lambda|\hat{T}_{y-x}\,\hat{o}(x)\,\hat{T}^{-1}_{y-x}|\lambda\rangle\,,

where
\hat{T}_{y-x}=e^{i\,\hat{P}\,(y-x)}
is a unitary translation operator, generated by the momentum operator

. Here

is a multiple number of the lattice steps, possibly even in different primary directions for regular lattices.
If
|\lambda\rangle
in translationally invariant, then
\hat{T}_{y-x}\,|\lambda\rangle=e^{i\,k\,(y-x)}|\lambda\rangle

being the momentum quantum number.
The space-dependent phases cancel out in the matrix element
\langle\lambda|\hat{o}(y)|\lambda\rangle

\displaystyle\langle\lambda|\hat{o}(y)|\lambda\rangle=\langle\lambda|\hat{o}(x)|\lambda\rangle\,,

(25)

matrix elements
\langle\lambda^{\prime}|\hat{o}(y)|\lambda\rangle

|\lambda^{\prime}\rangle\neq|\lambda\rangle

Consider now a second decomposition
|\eta\rangle

as in Eq. (
16

\displaystyle\langle\eta|\hat{o}(y)|\eta\rangle=\sum_{\lambda\,,\,\lambda^{\prime}}\frac{\sqrt{p_{\lambda}p_{\lambda^{\prime}}}}{p_{\eta}}\,[U^{*}]_{\lambda^{\prime}\,\eta}U_{\eta\,\lambda}\,\langle\lambda^{\prime}|\hat{o}(y)|\lambda\rangle\,.

(26)

Importantly, the involved unitary matrix

is not space dependent, as well as the numerical factors
\sqrt{p_{\eta}}
and
\sqrt{p_{\lambda}}

Therefore, the only difference between the elements
\langle\eta|\hat{o}(y)|\eta\rangle
and
\langle\eta|\hat{o}(x)|\eta\rangle
can come from the phases

e^{i\,k^{\prime}\,(y-x)}
,
not canceling out in the off-diagonal matrix elements
\langle\lambda^{\prime}|\hat{o}(y)|\lambda\rangle
,
|\lambda^{\prime}\rangle\neq|\lambda\rangle
.

We conclude that, if the states of the decomposition
|\lambda\rangle
are such that

the same property holds for another related decomposition

A required, but not sufficient, condition, is clearly that, in the same condition, also
\langle\lambda^{\prime}|\hat{O}|\lambda\rangle=0

Due to Eq. (
26

without the strongest assumption of translational invariance (still provided the vanishing of the off-diagonal matrix elements, assumed
x
-dependent).

Various examples are plausible with the feature described above.
Consider for instance a density matrix with an orthogonal decomposition (as a spectral one)

,
such that its elements are eigenstates of

V
Conclusions

We discussed some physical and mathematical conditions that make it possible to bound multipartite entanglement for
mixed quantum states, via
one- and two-point correlation functions.

Our analysis holds for discrete systems,
but it could be extended – although not straightforwardly – to continuous systems

;
gessner2017_2
. Further investigation
is required to identify other physically interesting cases, where our approach can be useful.

SM 1 : QFI and ME for mixed states

In this Section, we recall some elements of the general theory of the quantum Fisher information (QFI)

a central tool in estimation theory
pezze2014

This is defined via the fidelity between a pure quantum state

\theta

\mathscr{F}(\theta,\mathrm{d}\theta)=|\langle\psi(\theta)|\psi(\theta+\mathrm{d}\theta)\rangle|^{2}\approx 1-F(\theta){(\mathrm{d}\theta)^{2}}/{8}

Among the possible transformations relating

and

on discrete systems, like numerable sets of qubits or lattices (in the following labelled by
x
and

\hat{O}=\sum_{x}\hat{o}(x)
:

|\psi(\theta+\mathrm{d}\theta)\rangle=e^{i\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\hat{0}}|\psi(\theta)\rangle

For
d

-component discrete systems, the QFI for pure states equals the variance
V[|\psi\rangle,\hat{O}]_{N}
in Eq. (3)
of the main text, and it can be cast in terms of two-point
connected
correlations (the
\theta

\langle\psi|\hat{o}(x)\hat{o}(y)|\psi\rangle_{\mathrm{c}}\equiv\langle\psi|\hat{o}(x)\hat{o}(y)|\psi\rangle-\langle\psi|\hat{o}(x)|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\hat{o}(y)|\psi\rangle\,,

(32)

as

\displaystyle F[|\psi\rangle,\hat{O}]_{N}=V[|\psi\rangle,\hat{O}]_{N}=4\sum_{x,y}\langle\psi|\hat{o}(x)\hat{o}(y)|\psi\rangle_{\mathrm{c}}\,.

(33)

For two mixed states, described by density matrices
\rho
and

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}(\rho,\sigma)=\Big(\mathrm{Tr}\,\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}\,\sigma\sqrt{\rho}}\Big)^{2}\,\,.

(34)

The last expression is considered applied to two infinitesimally-separated density matrices

\displaystyle\rho(\theta)=\sum_{\lambda}p_{\lambda}\,\rho_{\lambda}(\theta)\,,\quad\rho_{\lambda}(\theta)=|\lambda(\theta)\rangle\langle\lambda(\theta)|\,,

(35)

and

The resulting QFI,

\displaystyle F[\rho_{D},\hat{O}]_{N}=2\,\sum_{x,y}\sum_{\lambda,\nu}\,\frac{\big(p_{\lambda}-p_{\nu}\big)^{2}}{p_{\lambda}+p_{\nu}}\,\langle\lambda|\hat{o}(x)|\nu\rangle\langle\nu|\hat{o}(y)|\lambda\rangle\,,

SM 3 : change of decomposition in Eq. (16) of the main text

In this appendix, we illustrate the derivation of the general law for a change of decomposition of a density matrix
\rho

Let us consider a generic

N

The density matrix

) :

\displaystyle\rho=U\,\rho_{D}\,U^{-1},\quad\quad\quad\rho_{D}=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N})\,,

(54)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\rho=U\,\rho_{D}\,U^{-1},\quad\quad\quad\rho_{D}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\,\lambda_{i}\,|e_{i}\rangle\langle e_{i}|\,,

(55)

with

forming an orthonormal

\rho

If we assume at the beginning that the set
\{|v_{i}\rangle\}
was also orthonormal, then
\{|e_{i}\rangle\}
and
\{|v_{i}\rangle\}
can be considered connected by the unitary matrix

Finally, we assumed, without any loss of generality, that
\lambda_{i}>0,\,\,\forall\,i
, strictly holds, exceptions being also discussed below.

From Eq. (
54
) and due to the linearity of

, we also have :

\displaystyle\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\,\lambda_{i}\,|\tilde{\psi}_{i}\rangle\langle\tilde{\psi}_{i}|\,,\quad\quad\quad|\tilde{\psi}_{i}\rangle=U|e_{i}\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,U_{ij}\,|e_{j}\rangle\,,

(56)

where
\langle e_{j}|U|e_{i}\rangle\equiv U_{ij}
.
Therefore, the set
\{|\tilde{\psi}_{i}\rangle\}
is a new decomposition for

Nevertheless,

via the states

An example of this situation is the following : consider the density matrix

and
|\downarrow\rangle

\displaystyle\rho=\Big(\begin{array}[]{cc}1/2&1/4\\
1/4&1/2\end{array}\Big)

Referring to Eq. (

\{p_{i}\}
, can be obtained, rescaling the transformation above by

and
\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}

\displaystyle|\psi_{i}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_{i}}}\,\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,U_{ij}\,\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\,|e_{j}\rangle\,,\quad\mathrm{leading\,\,to}\quad\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\,p_{i}\,|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|\,,

\rho

\{|\tilde{\psi}_{i}\rangle\}

If we did not assume

, for
M<N
eigenvalues, all the argument above can be repeated in a reduced
(N-M)

Now, starting from the relation

changing between the spectral decomposition

\{|\psi_{i}\rangle\}

\{|\psi_{i}\rangle\}

\{|\eta_{i}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}}\,\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,V_{ij}\,\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\,|e_{j}\rangle\}

)
. Inverting the second term of Eq. (

\displaystyle|e_{i}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}}\,\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,[U^{-1}]_{ij}\,\sqrt{p_{j}}\,|\psi_{j}\rangle\,.

Therefore, we can also write :

\displaystyle|\eta_{i}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}}\,\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}\,V_{ij}\,[U^{-1}]_{jk}\,\sqrt{p_{k}}\,|\psi_{k}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_{i}}}\,\sum_{k=1}^{N}\,M_{ik}\,\sqrt{p_{k}}\,|\psi_{k}\rangle\,,

SM 4 : on Eq. (22)
on the main text and on the cluster-decomposition theorem for pure quantum states

In this Section, we provide additional details on the role of Eq. (22)
of the main text,

\displaystyle\langle\lambda|\hat{o}(x)\hat{o}(y)|\lambda\rangle\to\langle\lambda|\hat{o}(x)|\lambda\rangle\langle\lambda|\hat{o}(y)|\lambda\rangle\quad\mathrm{as}\quad|x-y|\to\infty\,,

(63)

for a pure state
|\lambda\rangle
on a lattice and for the specific operator
\hat{o}(x)
, as well as that of the related stronger property, called
cluster decomposition theorem
. In particular, the latter theorem states that Eq. (
63
) holds for every local operator, and it encodes the locality principle
hastings2004
;
sims2006
;
weinberg1
for Hamiltonian systems. The same theorem can be valid at least for all the physical systems where the area law for the entanglement entropy is not violated more than logarithmically, like in systems with volume-law violation. Eq. (
63
) is equivalent to the limit
\langle\lambda|\hat{o}(x)\hat{o}(y)|\lambda\rangle_{\mathrm{c}}\to 0
as
|x-y|\to\infty
.

We assume at first that the pure state
|\lambda\rangle
is not degenerate, for instance in energy.
Eq. (
63
) can be understood starting from
the following decomposition of the two-points connected correlations :

\displaystyle\langle\lambda|\hat{o}(x)\hat{o}(y)|\lambda\rangle_{\mathrm{c}}=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}\,\big(\langle\lambda|\hat{o}(x)|\lambda^{\prime}\rangle\langle\lambda^{\prime}|\hat{o}(y)|\lambda\rangle\big)-\langle\lambda|\hat{o}(x)|\lambda\rangle\langle\lambda|\hat{o}(y)|\lambda\rangle=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\neq\lambda}\,\langle\lambda|\hat{o}(x)|\lambda^{\prime}\rangle\langle\lambda^{\prime}|\hat{o}(y)|\lambda\rangle\,,

(64)

where
|\lambda\rangle
and
|\lambda\rangle^{\prime}
form again a orthonormal basis.
In Eq. (
64
),
\langle\lambda^{\prime}|\hat{o}(x)|\lambda\rangle\to 0

(
\langle\lambda^{\prime}|\hat{o}(y)|\lambda\rangle\to 0
)
if
\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}
, then
\langle\lambda|\lambda^{\prime}\rangle=0
,
and if
x
(
y
, or both) is (are) located on the (infinite) boundary of the system, as consequence of locality.
The condition
\langle\lambda^{\prime}|\hat{o}(x)|\lambda\rangle\to 0
is violated by highly-entangled states
|\lambda\rangle
,

|\lambda^{\prime}\rangle
, e. g. cat-states.
More general, Eq. (
63
) implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\neq\lambda}\,a_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\,\langle\lambda|\hat{o}(x)|\lambda^{\prime}\rangle\langle\lambda^{\prime}|\hat{o}(y)|\lambda\rangle\to 0\quad\mathrm{if}\quad|x-y|\to\infty\,,

(65)

at least if
|a_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}|<\infty
,
a generally fulfilled condition in physical systems.

The latter argument suggests that Eq. (
63
) and the cluster-decomposition theorem, often
referred to non-degenerate ground-state(s) of local Hamiltonians, must hold also for excited states, although they are more entangled in general (for instance fulfilling a volume law for the Von Neumann entropy, see e. g.
eisert2010
;
alba2009
;
sierra2011
). Indeed, Eq. (
65
) means that a local operator
\hat{o}(x)
applied on (one point of) the infinite boundary of the states
|\lambda\rangle
or
|\lambda^{\prime}\rangle
(no matter their energies), cannot change the same states in a way to induce a nonvanishing overlap with other states of the same orthogonal basis.

Expressed in a alternative manner, Eq. (
63
) and the cluster decomposition theorem must be valid for all the states of the considered systems at least if
c<\infty
, and therefore if some producibility holds. Otherwise, the entanglement between the infinite boundary and a point in the bulk, or between two points of the boundary, would immediately set
c\to\infty
. More in detail, if
N\sim L^{d}
, then in the latter case,
c\sim L^{\delta}
, with
\delta\geq d-1
,
d-1
being the dimension of the boundary, then also
c\sim N^{\gamma}
,
\gamma\geq{(d-1)}/{d}
. The bound for
\delta
can be further improved by noticing that, if Eq. (
63
) fails and translational invariance is assumed (at least asymptotically), then :

\displaystyle\langle\psi|\hat{o}(x)\hat{o}(y)|\psi\rangle_{\mathrm{c}}\sim|x-y|^{\beta}\,\quad\quad\mathrm{for}\,\quad\quad|x-y|\to\infty\,,\quad\quad\beta\geq 0\,.

(66)

Merging Eq. (
66
) and the scaling property
f[|\psi\rangle,\hat{O}]_{N}\sim c\sim N^{\frac{d+2{\beta}}{d}}
(see more details in
hauke2016
for critical lattice systems and in
pezze2017

for one-dimensional gapped lattices), we obtain
\delta=d+2\beta\geq d
.
Therefore, the failure of Eq. (
63
) and implies the reach of genuine multipartite entanglement. Thus, no producibility of any kind is allowed.
The same result can be also inferred directly from the bound in Eq. (
39
). For this purpose, it is crucial that, in the original demonstration of the bound
PezzePRL2009
;
pezze2014
, Eq. (
63
) and has not been used.
For the same reason, we point out that Eq. (
63
)
turns out to be strictly required to prove the bound in SM 2,
even though in those proofs no explicit mention to it is made.
Finally, the validity of Eq. (
63
) and of the cluster decomposition theorem does not require translational invariance, avoiding any prescription on the limit operations in Eqs. (
65
) and (
66
).

Above, we claimed that Eq. (
63
) and the cluster-decomposition can hold if the ground state is unique. Instead, if the ground-state is degenerate, the same properties do not hold in a generic basis for the ground-states. However, at least in the presence of a finite number of degenerate states, the same properties can be recovered, provided one chooses the combination of them properly. This fact can be illustrated via a paradigmatic example, i.e. the ferromagnetic quantum Ising model in a transverse field, governed by the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{IS}}=h\,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{\sigma}_{i}^{(x)}+J\,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\,\hat{\sigma}_{i}^{(z)}\hat{\sigma}_{i+1}^{(z)}\,,\quad\quad J<0\,.

(67)

It is known that, if
|J|>h
, the ground state in the thermodynamic limit is doubly degenerate, in the states
|\uparrow,\dots,\uparrow\rangle\equiv|\uparrow\rangle
and
|\downarrow,\dots,\downarrow\rangle\equiv|\downarrow\rangle
, orthonormal to each other. However, at finite volume, these states are mixed, forming the almost degenerate states
|\pm\rangle=\frac{|\uparrow\rangle\pm|\downarrow\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}
, exactly degenerate in the thermodynamic limit only.
For the latter states, Eq. (
63
) is not fulfilled, exactly since they result from the mixing of
|\uparrow\rangle
and
|\downarrow\rangle
. Indeed :

\displaystyle\mathrm{lim}_{|x-y|\to\infty}\langle\pm|\hat{\sigma}^{(z)}(x)\hat{\sigma}^{(z)}(y)|\pm\rangle\to\frac{1}{2}\quad\mathrm{but}\quad\langle\pm|\hat{\sigma}^{(z)}(x)|\pm\rangle\langle\pm|\hat{\sigma}^{(z)}(y)|\pm\rangle=0\,.

(68)

However, it is fulfilled for the states
|\uparrow\rangle
and
|\downarrow\rangle
. This means that, adopting the basis formed by
|\uparrow\rangle
,
|\downarrow\rangle
, and by the excited states above them, Eq. (
63
) can be recovered, and locality made explicit. This fact parallels the possibility, described in the main text, to hidden producibility in the presence of degeneracies. In a sense, in the presence of a finite degenerate ground state, Eq. (
63
) still holds, up to global unitary transformations, as that linking the states
|\pm\rangle
and
|\uparrow\rangle
and
|\downarrow\rangle
in the example above. In Eq. (
65
), they transform the states
|\lambda\rangle
and
|\lambda\rangle^{\prime}
but, critically, not the operators
\hat{o}(x)
and
\hat{o}(y)
, set at the beginning to act on them. Therefore, the value of the sums in the same equation can change. Clearly, the bounds after Eq. (
66
) still hold ; in particular not-validity of Eq. (
63
) in any basis implies the simultaneous absence of any sort of producibility.

In real experiments, the states corresponding to
|\uparrow\rangle
and
|\downarrow\rangle
are generically selected by the fluctuations (also classical) or by perturbations suitably added, as well as in simulations : for instance, in the example above, by an additional term
h^{\prime}\,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{\sigma}_{i}^{(z)}
,
h^{\prime}\to 0
, customary e.g. in DMRG calculations.

The discussion above does not cover the important cases of continuous degeneracies, as for spontaneously broken continuous symmetries and for genuine topologically ordered matter
wen2012
. Moreover, it is not clear whether a volume-law dependence for the Von Neumann entropy is enough to guarantee the violation of Eq. (
30
) and of cluster decomposition. These issues are beyond the scope of the present work.

SM 5 : derivation of Eq. (21)
of the main text

Starting from Eq. (6)
of the main text,

\displaystyle\bar{V}[\rho,\hat{O}]_{N}\equiv 4\,\sum_{\tilde{\lambda}}p_{\tilde{\lambda}}\,\Big(\sum_{x,y}\,\langle\tilde{\lambda}|\hat{o}(x)\hat{o}(y)|\tilde{\lambda}\rangle_{\mathrm{c}}\Big)\,,

(69)

in this Section, we obtain Eq. (21) again of the main text.
This step is strictly required to operatively evaluate

, since in general the producible basis
|\tilde{\lambda}\rangle
, fulfilling Eq. (1)
of the main text, is not known. For this purpose, it is useful

transformation.
Setting this transformation as

\displaystyle|\tilde{\lambda}\rangle=\sum_{n}\,a_{\tilde{\lambda},n}\,|n\rangle\,,\quad\quad\sum_{n}\,|a_{\tilde{\lambda},n}|^{2}\neq 1\,,

(70)

where

\displaystyle\sum_{\tilde{\lambda}}p_{\tilde{\lambda}}\,\sum_{x,y}\,\langle\tilde{\lambda}|\hat{o}(x)\hat{o}(y)|\tilde{\lambda}\rangle\to\sum_{x,y}\sum_{\tilde{\lambda}}p_{\tilde{\lambda}}\sum_{n,m}\,a^{*}_{\tilde{\lambda},m}a_{\tilde{\lambda},n}\,\langle m|\hat{o}(x)\hat{o}(y)|n\rangle\equiv\sum_{x,y}\sum_{n,m}\,p_{n,m}\,\langle m|\hat{o}(x)\hat{o}(y)|n\rangle\,.

Exploiting again the orthonormality of the

\displaystyle\sum_{x,y}\mathrm{Tr}\big[\rho\,\hat{o}(x)\hat{o}(y)\big]\,,

\displaystyle\rho=\sum_{\tilde{\lambda}}p_{\tilde{\lambda}}\sum_{n,m}\,a_{\tilde{\lambda},n}a^{*}_{\tilde{\lambda},m}\,|n\rangle\langle m|\equiv\sum_{n,m}\,p_{n,m}\,|n\rangle\langle m|

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho=\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho_{P}=\sum_{\tilde{\lambda}}p_{\tilde{\lambda}}\sum_{n}\,|a_{\tilde{\lambda},n}|^{2}=1\,.

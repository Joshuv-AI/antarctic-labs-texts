An Interpolation of Metrics and Spaces of Metrics
Yoshito Ishiki

Abstract
. As a generalization of Hausdorff’s extension theorem of metrics, we prove an interpolation theorem of a family of metrics defined on closed subsets of metrizable spaces. As an application, we investigate typicality of subsets of moduli spaces of metrics. We observe that various sets of all metrics with properties appearing in metric geometry are dense intersections of countable open subsets in spaces of metrics on metrizable spaces. For instance, our study is applicable to the set of all non-doubling metrics and the set of all non-uniformly disconnected metrics.

1.
Intoroduction

1.1.
Backgrounds

In 1930,
Felix
Hausdorff

[
11
]

proved the extension theorem stating that for every metrizable space

X
,
for every closed subset

A

of

X

and for every
metric

d

on

A

generating the same topology of

A
,
there exists
a metric

D

on

X

such that

D
generates the same topology of

X
and

D|_{A^{2}}=d

(see Theorem

2.1
).
Motivated by this result, the author thought that Hausdorff’s theorem suggested the study of spaces of metrics and tried to investigate their topological properties.

For a metrizable space

X
,
we denote by

\mathrm{Met}(X)

the set of all metrics on

X

generating
the same topology of

X
.
We define a function

\mathcal{D}_{X}:\mathrm{Met}(X)\times\mathrm{Met}(X)\to[0,\infty]

by

\mathcal{D}_{X}(d,e)=\sup_{(x,y)\in X^{2}}|d(x,y)-e(x,y)|.

The function

\mathcal{D}_{X}

is a metric on

\mathrm{Met}(X)

valued in

[0,\infty]
.
Throughout this paper,
we consider that

\mathrm{Met}(X)

is always equipped with the topology generated by

\mathcal{D}_{X}
.
To investigate
topological properties of

\mathrm{Met}(X)
,
we
first generalize the Hausdorff extension theorem to an interpolation theorem of metrics with an approximation by

\mathcal{D}_{X}

(Theorem

1.1
or

3.4
).
As applications of it,
for a
certain
property

\mathcal{P}
on metric spaces,
we prove that
the set set

\{\,d\in\mathrm{Met}(X)\mid\text{$(X,d)$ satisfies $\mathcal{P}$}\,\}

is generic in

\mathrm{Met}(X)

(see Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
).
This result
can be considered as an analogue of
Banach’s famous result, which states
that
in the continuous function space on
[0,1]
,
a generic function is nowhere differentiable.

Since a metric on

X

determines the geometry on

X
,
studying the moduli space

\mathrm{Met}(X)

of metrics is equivalent to investigating the moduli of geometries that can be expanded on the space

X
.

1.2.
Main results

1.2.1.
Interpolation of metrics

A family

\{S_{i}\}_{i\in I}

of subsets of a topological space

X

is said to be

discrete
if for every

x\in X

there exists a neighborhood of

x
intersecting at most single member of

\{S_{i}\}_{i\in I}
.

Our first main results is the following interpolation theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

X

be a metrizable space, and let

\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}

be a discrete family of closed subsets of

X
.
Then for every metric

d\in\mathrm{Met}(X)
,
and for every
family

\{e_{i}\}_{i\in I}

of metrics with

e_{i}\in\mathrm{Met}(A_{i})
,
there exists a metric

m\in\mathrm{Met}(X)

satisfying the following:

(1)

for every

i\in I

we have

m|_{A_{i}^{2}}=e_{i}
;

(2)

\mathcal{D}_{X}(m,d)=\sup_{i\in I}\mathcal{D}_{A_{i}}(e_{A_{i}},d|_{A_{i}^{2}})
.

Moreover, if

X

is completely metrizable,
and if each

e_{i}\in\mathrm{Met}(A_{i})

is a complete metric, then we can choose

m\in\mathrm{Met}(X)

as a complete one.

Remark 1.1
.

For every metrizable space

X
,
and for every closed subset

A

of

X
,
Nguyen Van Khue and Nguyen To Nhu

[
16
]

constructed
a Lipschitz metric extensor from

(\mathrm{Met}(A),\mathcal{D}_{A})

into

(\mathrm{Met}(X),\mathcal{D}_{X})
,
and a monotone continuous
metric extensor from

\mathrm{Met}(A)

into

\mathrm{Met}(X)
;
moreover,
if

X

is completely metrizable,
then each of these metric extensors maps any complete metric in

\mathrm{Met}(A)

into a complete metric in

\mathrm{Met}(X)
.
To obtain
such metric extensors, they used the Dugundji extension theorem concerning locally convex topological linear spaces.

A central idea of the proof of Theorem
1.1

is a correspondence between a metric on a metrizable space and a topological embedding from a metrizable space into
a Banach space.
A metric

d

on a metrizable space

X

induces a topological embedding
from

X

into a Banach space such as
the Kuratowski embedding
(see Theorem
2.6
).
Conversely, a topological embedding

F

from a metrizable space

X

into a Banach space

V

with norm

\|\cdot\|_{V}

induces a metric

m\in\mathrm{Met}(X)

on

X

defined by

m(x,y)=\|F(x)-F(y)\|_{V}
.
In the proof of Theorem

1.1
,
we utilize this correspondence to translate
the statement of Theorem

1.1
into an approximation problem on
topological embeddings into a Banach space.
We then resolve such a problem by using the Michael continuous selection theorem
(see Theorem
2.2
),
and by using a similar method to
Kuratowski

[
17
]

(see also

[
12
]

and

[
1
]
)
of converting a continuous function
into a topological embedding by extending a codomain.

1.2.2.
Moduli spaces of metrics

Theorem
1.1
enables
us to investigate dense

G_{\delta}

subsets in the topology of the space

(\mathrm{Met}(X),\mathcal{D}_{X})

for a metrizable space

X
.
To describe our second result precisely,
we define a class of geometric properties that unifies various properties appearing
in metric geometry. Due to this purpose,
the definition may seem somewhat complicated at first glance.

Let

\mathcal{P}^{*}(\mathbb{N})

be the set of all non-empty subsets of

\mathbb{N}
.
For a topological space

T
,
we denote by

\mathcal{F}(T)

the set of all closed subsets of

T
.
For a subset

W\in\mathcal{P}^{*}(\mathbb{N})
,
and for a set

S
,
we denote by

\seq(W,S)

the set of all finite injective sequences

\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}

in

S
with

n\in W
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let

Q

be an at most countable set,

P

a topological space.
Let

F:Q\to\mathcal{F}(P)

and

G:Q\to\mathcal{P}^{*}(\mathbb{N})

be maps.
Let

Z

be a set.
Let

\phi

be a correspondence
assigning a pair

(q,X)
of

q\in Q

and a metrizable space

X

to a map

\phi^{q,X}:\seq(G(q),X)\times Z\times\mathrm{Met}(X)\to P
.
We say that
a sextuple

(Q,P,F,G,Z,\phi)

is
a
transmissible paremeter

if
for every metrizable space

X
,
for every
q\in Q
,
and
for every

z\in Z

the following are satisfied:

(TP1)

for every

a\in\seq(G(q),X)
,
and for every

z\in Z
,
the map

\phi^{q,X}(a,z):\mathrm{Met}(X)\to P

defined by

\phi^{q,X}(a,z)(d)=\phi^{q,X}(a,z,d)

is continuous;

(TP2)

for every

d\in\mathrm{Met}(X)
,
if
S
is a subset of
X
and

a\in\seq(G(q),S)
,
then we have

\phi^{q,X}(a,z,d)=\phi^{q,S}(a,z,d|_{S^{2}})
.

The diameter

\phi^{q,X}(a,z,X)=\delta_{d}(\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n})
is a typical example
of a map appearing in
the condition
(TP1)
.

We introduce a property determined by a transmissible parameter.

Definition 1.2
.

Let

\mathfrak{G}=(Q,P,F,G,Z,\phi)

be a transmissible parameter.
We say that a metric space

(X,d)

satisfies
the

\mathfrak{G}
-transmissible property

if
there exists

q\in Q

such that
for every

a\in\seq(G(q),X)

and for every

z\in Z

we have

\phi^{q,X}(a,z,d)\in F(q)
.
We say that

(X,d)

satisfies the

anti-
\mathfrak{G}
-transmissible property

if

(X,d)

satisfies the negation of the

\mathfrak{G}
-transmissible property;
namely,
for every

q\in Q

there exist

a\in\seq(G(q),X)

and

z\in Z

with

\phi^{q,X}(a,z,d)\in X\setminus F(q)
.
A property on metric spaces is a

transmissible property
(resp.
anti-transmissible property
)
if it is equivalent to a

\mathfrak{G}
-transmissible property
(resp. anti-
\mathfrak{G}
-transmissible property)
for some
transmissible parameter

\mathfrak{G}
.

The class of transmissible properties contains various properties appeared in metric geometry.

Example 1.1
.

The following properties on metric spaces are transmissible properties
(see Section
4
).

(1)

the doubling property;

(2)

the uniform disconnectedness;

(3)

satisfying the ultratriangle inequality;

(4)

satisfying the Ptolemy inequality;

(5)

the Gromov

\cycl_{m}(0)
condition;

(6)

the Gromov hyperbolicity.

To state our second result, we need a more additional condition for transmissible properties.

Definition 1.3
.

Let

\mathfrak{G}=(Q,P,F,G,Z,\phi)

be a transmissible parameter.
We say that

\mathfrak{G}

is

singular

if for each

q\in Q

and for every

\epsilon\in(0,\infty)

there exist

z\in Z

and a finite metrizable space

(L,d_{L})

such that

(1)

\delta_{d_{L}}(L)\leq\epsilon
,
where

\delta_{d_{L}}(L)

stands for the diameter of
L
;

(2)

\card(L)\in G(q)
,
where

\card

stands for the cardinality;

(3)

\phi^{q,L}(L,z,d_{L})\in X\setminus F(q)
.

As we will see in
Lemma
4.5
,
the singularity of a transmissible parameter

\mathfrak{G}
is
equivalent to the existence of
a special countable metric subspace that
satisfies the anti-
\mathfrak{G}
-transmissible property.

Note that not all transmissible parameters are singular;
especially,
the Gromov hyperbolicity
does not have
a singular transmissible parameter (see Proposition
4.22
).

Due to Theorem

1.1
,
we obtain the second main result on dense

G_{\delta}

subsets in spaces of metrics:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

\mathfrak{G}

be a singular transmissible parameter.
For every non-discrete metrizable space
X
,
the set of all

d\in\mathrm{Met}(X)

for which

(X,d)

satisfies the
anti-
\mathfrak{G}
-transmissible property
is dense

G_{\delta}
in
\mathrm{Met}(X)
.

Remark 1.2
.

Theorem

1.2

holds true for the
space

\cm(X)

of all complete metrics in

\mathrm{Met}(X)

(see Theorems
4.7
).

We can apply Theorem

1.2

to the properties

(1)
–
(5)

mentioned in Example
1.1
.
Therefore we conclude
that
the set of all metrics not satisfying these properties
are dense

G_{\delta}

in spaces of metrics
(see
Theorem
4.12

and Corollary
4.17
).
We also conclude that the set of all metrics with rich pseudo-cones is
dense

G_{\delta}

in spaces of metrics
(see Theorem
4.15
).

Our third result is based on the fact that for
every
second-countable locally compact space

X
,
the space

\mathrm{Met}(X)

is Baire
(see Lemma

5.1
).
For a property

P

on metric spaces,
we say that a metric space

(X,d)

satisfies the

local
P

if
every non-empty open metric subspace of

X

satisfies the property

P
.

As a local version of Theorem

1.2
,
we obtain the following:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

X

be a second-countable,
locally compact locally non-discrete space.
Then for every singular transmissible parameter

\mathfrak{G}
,
the set of all metrics

d\in\mathrm{Met}(X)

for which

(X,d)

satisfies
the local
anti-
\mathfrak{G}
-transmissible property
is dense

G_{\delta}

in

\mathrm{Met}(X)
.

Recall
that
all second-countable locally compact spaces are metrizable,
which is a consequence of
the Urysohn metrization theorem.

2.1.
The Hausdorff extension theorem

The following celebrated theorem was first proven by Hausdorff

[
11
]

(cf.
[
12
]
,

[
3
]
,

[
2
]
,
and

[
21

The latter part on complete

Bacon

[

Theorem 2.1
.

Let

X
be a metrizable space,
and let

A

be a closed subset of

X
.
Then for every

d\in\mathrm{Met}(A)

there exists

D\in\mathrm{Met}(X)

with

D|_{A^{2}}=d
.
Moreover,
if

X
is completely metrizable,
and if

d\in\mathrm{Met}(A)

is a complete metric on

A
,
then we can choose

D\in\mathrm{Met}(X)

as a complete metric on

X
.

2.2.
The Michael continuous selection theorem

Let

V

be a Banach space.
We denote by

\conv(V)

the set of all non-empty closed convex subsets of

V
.
For a topological space

X

we say that a map

\phi\colon X\to\conv(V)

is

lower semi-continuous

if for every open subset

O

of

V

the set

\{\,x\in X\mid\phi(x)\cap O\neq\emptyset\,\}

is open in
X
.

The following theorem is known as one of the Michael continuous selection theorems proven in

[
19
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let

X

be a paracompact space,
and

A

a closed subsets of

X
,

V
a Banach space,
and

\phi\colon X\to\conv(V)

be a lower semi-continuous map.
If a continuous map

f\colon A\to B

satisfies

f(x)\in\phi(x)

for all

x\in A
,
then there exists a continuous map

F\colon X\to V
with

F|_{A}=f

such that
for every

x\in X

we have

F(x)\in\phi(x)
.

By using linear structure,
we have the following:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
V
be a Banach space,
and

x,y\in V
.
Then for every

r\in(0,\infty)

we have

\mathcal{H}(B(x,r),B(y,r))=\|x-y\|_{V}
,
where

\mathcal{H}

is the Hausdorff distance induced from the norm

\|\cdot\|_{V}

of

V
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let

X

be a topological space,

V

be a Banach space,
and let

H\colon X\to V

be a continuous map and

r\in(0,\infty)
.
Then a map

\phi\colon X\to\conv(V)

defined by

\phi(x)=B(H(x),r)

is lower semi-continuous.

Proof.

For every open subset

O

of

V
,
and for every point

a\in X

with

\phi(a)\cap O\neq\emptyset
,
choose

u\in\phi(a)\cap O

and

l\in(0,\infty)

with

U(u,l)\subset O
.
By Proposition
2.3
,
we can take

\delta\in(0,\infty)

such that for every

x\in U(a,\delta)

we have

\mathcal{H}(\phi(x),\phi(a))=\|H(x)-H(a)\|_{V}<l
.
Then we have

\phi(x)\cap U(u,l)\neq\emptyset
,
and hence

\phi(x)\cap O\neq\emptyset
.
Therefore the set

\{\,x\in X\mid\phi(x)\cap O\neq\emptyset\,\}

is open in
X
.

2.3.
The Kuratowski embedding theorem

For a metric space

(X,d)
,
we denote by

C_{b}(X)

the Banach space of all bounded continuous functions on

X

equipped with the supremum norm.
For

x\in X
,
we denote by

d_{x}

the function from

X

to

\mathbb{R}

defined by

d_{x}(p)=d(x,p)
.
The following theorem,
which states that every metric space
is isometrically embeddable into Banach spaces,
is known as the Kuratowski embedding theorem.
The proof can be seen in, for example,

[
8
, Theorem 4.3.14]
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let

(X,d)

be a metric space.
Take

o\in X
.
Then
the map

K:X\to C_{b}(X)

defined by

K(x)=d_{x}-d_{o}

is an isometric embedding.
Moreover,
if

(X,d)

is bounded,
the map

L\colon X\to C_{b}(X)

defined by

L(x)=d_{x}

is an isometric embedding.

2.4.
Baire spaces

A topological space

X
is said to be

Baire
if the intersections of
countable dense open subsets of

X

are dense in

X
.

The following is
known as the Baire category theorem.

Theorem 2.7
.

Every completely metrizable space is a Baire space.

Since

G_{\delta}

subset of completely metrizable space is
completely metrizable
(see, e.g.
[
23
, Theorem 24.12]
),
we obtain the following:

Lemma 2.8
.

Every

G_{\delta}

subset of a completely metrizable space is a Baire space.

3.
An interpolation Theorem of metrics

In this section, we prove Theorem

1.1

3.1.
Amalgamation lemmas

Let

X

and

Y

be sets,
and let

\tau\colon X\to Y

be a bijection.
For a metric

d

on

Y
,
we denote by

\tau^{*}d

the metric on

X

defined by

(\tau^{*}d)(x,y)=d(\tau(x),\tau(y))
.
In this case,
the map

\tau

is an isometry between

(X,\tau^{*}d)

and

(Y,d)
.

The following proposition can be considered as a specific case
of the realization of
the Gromov–Hausdorff distance of two metric spaces.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

X

be a metrizable space.
For

r\in(0,\infty)
,
let

d,e\in\mathrm{Met}(X)

with

\mathcal{D}_{X}(d,e)\leq r
.
Put

X_{0}=X
,
and
let

X_{1}

be a set with

\card(X_{1})=\card(X_{0})

and

X_{0}\cap X_{1}=\emptyset
.
Take
a bijection

\tau:X_{0}\to X_{1}
.
Then there exists a metric

h\in\mathrm{Met}(X_{0}\sqcup X_{1})

such that

(1)

h|_{(X_{0})^{2}}=d
;

(2)

h|_{(X_{1})^{2}}=(\tau^{-1})^{*}e
;

(3)

for every

x\in X_{0}

we have

h(x,\tau(x))=r/2
.

Proof.

We define a symmetric function

h:(X_{0}\sqcup X_{1})^{2}\to[0,\infty)

by

\displaystyle h(x,y)=\begin{cases}d(x,y)&\text{if $x,y\in X_{0}$;}\\
e(x,y)&\text{if $x,y\in X_{1}$;}\\
\inf_{a\in X_{0}}(d(x,a)+r/2+e(\tau(a),y))&\text{if $(x,y)\in X_{0}\times X_{1}$. }\end{cases}

Then
h
is a metric as desired.
For more details, we refer the readers to
[
5
, Theorem 7.3.25]
.

The following proposition
is known as an amalgamation of metrics.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let

(X,d_{X})

and

(Y,d_{Y})

be metric spaces,
and let

Z=X\cap Y
.
If

Z\neq\emptyset

and

d_{X}|_{Z^{2}}=d_{Y}|_{Z^{2}}
,
then there exists a metric

h
on

X\cup Y

such that

(1)

h|_{X^{2}}=d_{X}
;

(2)

h|_{Y^{2}}=d_{Y}
.

Proof.

We define a symmetric function

h:(X\cup Y)^{2}\to[0,\infty)

by

\displaystyle h(x,y)=\begin{cases}d_{X}(x,y)&\text{if $x,y\in X$;}\\
d_{Y}(x,y)&\text{if $x,y\in Y$;}\\
\inf_{z\in Z}(d_{X}(x,z)+d_{Y}(z,y))&\text{if $(x,y)\in X\times Y$. }\end{cases}

Since

d_{X}|_{Z^{2}}=d_{Y}|_{Z^{2}}
,
the function

h

is well-defined, and it becomes a desired metric.
The detailed proof can be seen in, for example,

[
4
]

(cf.
[
22
]
and
[
7
]
).

For a mutually disjoint family

\{T_{i}\}_{i\in I}

we consider that the space

\coprod_{i\in I}T_{i}

Proposition 3.3
.

Let

X

be a metrizable space,
and

\{A_{i}\}_{i\in I}

be a discrete family of closed subsets of

X
.
Take

d\in\mathrm{Met}(X)
,
and let

\{e_{i}\}_{i\in I}

be a family of metrics with

e_{i}\in\mathrm{Met}(A_{i})
.
Put

\eta=\sup_{i\in I}\mathcal{D}_{A_{i}}(e_{A_{i}},d|_{(A_{i})^{2}})

and assume that

\eta<\infty
.
Let

\{B_{i}\}_{i\in I}

be a mutually disjoint family of sets
such that for all

i\in I

we have

X\cap B_{i}=\emptyset
.
Take

\tau\colon\coprod_{i\in I}A_{i}\to\coprod_{i\in I}B_{i}

be a bijection such that
for each

i\in I

the map

\tau_{i}=\tau|_{A_{i}}

is a bijection between

A_{i}

and

B_{i}
.
Then
there exists a metric

h

on

X\sqcup\coprod_{i\in I}B_{i}

such that

(1)

for every

i\in I

we have

h|_{(B_{i})^{2}}=(\tau_{i}^{-1})^{*}e_{i}
;

(2)

h|_{X^{2}}=d
;

(3)

for every

x\in\coprod_{i\in I}A_{i}

we have
h(x,\tau(x))=\eta/2
.

Proof.

for every

i\in I
,
Proposition
3.1

Proof of Theorem

To show Theorem

Theorem 3.4
.

Let

be a metrizable space,
and let

Then

,

(2)

Proof.

Put

.
If

\eta=\infty
,
then
the theorem follows
from
the Hausdorff extension theorem

2.1
.

We may assume

.
Let

B

be a set such that

and

,
and
let

be a bijection.
Put

.
By Proposition

3.3
,
we find a metric

on

such that

(H1)

;

(H2)

;

(H3)

for every

We can take an isometric embedding

from

into a Banach space

(Y,\|\cdot\|_{Y})

(see e.g., the Kuratowski embedding theorem

).
Define a map

\phi(x)=B(H(x),\eta/2)
.
By Corollary

2.4
,
the map

is lower semi-continuous.
We define a map

by

.
Then

is continuous.
By the property

of

for every

.
Due to the Stone theorem

the space

is paracompact.
Thus

2.2

to the map

,
and hence
we obtain a continuous map

such that

and for every

we have

.
Note that

means that

\|F(x)-H(x)\|_{Y}\leq\eta/2.

Define a map

.
Note that

.
We consider that the product Banach space

is equipped with

Define a map

By the Kuratowski embedding theorem

the map

Therefore

is a topological embedding.
We also define a map

Then,

,
the map

from

to

Since for every

and

\|l_{x}\|_{C_{b}(X)}\leq\eta/2
,
we obtain

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|E(x)-K(x)\|=\max\{\|F(x)-H(x)\|_{Y},\|l_{x}\|_{C_{b}(X)}\}\leq\eta/2.

Define a function

m:X^{2}\to[0,\infty)

by

m(x,y)=\|E(x)-E(y)\|
,
then

is a metric on

.
Since

is a topological embedding,
we see that

.
For every

,
we have

and

thus we obtain

and hence

.
Moreover,
we have

\eta\leq\mathcal{D}_{X}(m,d)
.
We also obtain the opposite inequality

\mathcal{D}_{X}(m,d)\leq\eta
;
indeed,
the inequality
(
3.1
)
shows that

\displaystyle|m(x,y)-d(x,y)|=\Bigl|\|E(x)-E(y)\|-\|K(x)-K(y)\|\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\|E(y)-K(y)\|+\|E(x)-K(x)\|\leq\eta/2+\eta/2=\eta.

Therefore we conclude that

\mathcal{D}_{X}(m,d)=\eta
.
This completes the proof of the former part of Theorem

.

By the latter part of the Hausdorff theorem
2.1
,
we can choose

as a complete metric.
Then

become a complete metric.
This shows the latter part of Theorem

.
∎

.

X

.

By the condition

.

Let

By the virtue of Lemma

Corollary 4.2

Let

For

,

and for

,
we denote by

We also denote by

the set of all

d\in\mathrm{Met}(X)

such that

(X,d)

satisfies the
anti-
\mathfrak{G}
-transmissible property.

.

Let
X
be a metrizable space,

\mathfrak{G}=(Q,P,F,G,Z,\phi)

Then
for every

q\in Q
,

,
and

Fix

.

Let

be a metrizable space,

is

in

.

We say that a topological space is an

Lemma 4.5

-metric space

Proof.

Let

4.2.
The doubling property and the uniform disconnectedness

For a metic space

(X,d)

and for a subset

A

of

X
,
we set

\alpha_{d}(A)=\inf\{\,d(x,y)\mid\text{$x,y\in A$ and $x\neq y$}\,\}.

A metric space

(X,d)

is said to be

doubling

if there exist

C\in(0,\infty)

and

\lambda\in(0,\infty)

such that for every finite subset

A

of

X

we have

\card(A)\leq C\left(\frac{\delta_{d}(A)}{\alpha_{d}(A)}\right)^{\lambda}.

Note that

(X,d)

is doubling if and only if

(X,d)

has finite Assouad dimension
(see e.g.,

[
13
, Section 10]
).

By the definitions of the topology of

\mathrm{Met}(X)
,

\alpha_{d}

and

\delta_{d}
,
we obtain:

Lemma 4.8
.

Let

X

be a metrizable space.
Fix a finite subset

A

of

X
.
Then maps

\alpha_{*,A},\delta_{*,A}\colon\mathrm{Met}(X)\to\mathbb{R}

defined by

\alpha_{*,A}(d)=\alpha_{d}(A)

and

\delta_{*,A}(d)=\delta_{d}(A)

is continuous.

Proposition 4.9
.

The doubling property on metric spaces is a transmissible property with a singular transmissible parameter.

Proof.

Define a map

F_{D}\colon(\mathbb{Q}_{>0})^{2}\to\mathcal{F}((\mathbb{R}_{>0})^{2})

by

F_{D}((q_{1},q_{2}))=\{\,(x,y)\in(\mathbb{R}_{>0})^{2}\mid x\leq q_{1}\cdot y^{q_{2}}\,\},

and define a constant map

G_{D}\colon(\mathbb{Q}_{>0})^{2}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})^{*}

by

G_{D}(q)=[2,\infty)
.
Put

Z_{D}=\{1\}
.
For each metrizable space

X
,
and for each

q\in(\mathbb{Q}_{>0})^{2}
,
define a map

\phi_{D}^{q,X}\colon\seq(G(q),X)\times Z_{D}\times\mathrm{Met}(X)\to(\mathbb{R}_{>0})^{2}

by

\phi_{D}^{q,X}(\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N},1,d)=\left(N,\frac{\delta_{d}(\{\,a_{i}\mid i\in\{1,\dots,N\}\,\})}{\alpha_{d}(\{\,a_{i}\mid i\in\{1,\dots,N\}\,\})}\right).

Let

\mathfrak{DB}=((\mathbb{Q}_{>0})^{2},(\mathbb{R}_{>0})^{2},F_{D},G_{D},\{1\},\phi_{D})
.
Then

\mathfrak{DB}

satisfies
the condition

(TP2)

in Definition

1.1
.
By the Lemma

4.8
,
we see that

\mathfrak{DB}

satisfies the condition

(TP1)
.
Hence

\mathfrak{DB}

is a transmissible parameter.
The

\mathfrak{DB}
-transmissible property
is equivalent to
the doubling property.
We next prove that

\mathfrak{DB}

is singular.
For

q=(q_{1},q_{2})\in(\mathbb{Q}_{>0})^{2}

and for

\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
,
we denote by

(R_{q},d_{q})

a finite metric space with

\card(R_{q})>q_{1}+1

and

d_{q}(x,y)=\epsilon

whenever
x\neq y
.
Then

\delta_{d_{q}}(R_{q})=\epsilon
,
and

\phi^{q,R_{q}}(R_{q},1,d_{q})=(\card(R_{q}),1)\not\in F_{D}(q).

This implies the proposition.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Let

\mathbb{Z}

be the set of all integers with discrete topology,
and let

h\in\mathrm{Met}(\mathbb{Z})
be
the relative metric on

\mathbb{Z}

induced from the Euclidean metric on

\mathbb{R}
.
Then

h

has a neighborhood

U

in

\mathrm{Met}(X)

such that for every

d\in U

the space

(\mathbb{Z},d)

is doubling.

A metric space

(X,d)

is said to be

uniformly disconnected

if there exists

\delta\in(0,1)

such that if a finite sequence

\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}

in

X

satisfies

\max_{i}d(z_{i},z_{i+1})<\delta d(z_{1},z_{N})
,
then we have

N=1
.
Namely, the inequality
N>1

implies
\max_{i}d(z_{i},z_{i+1})\geq\delta d(z_{1},z_{N})
.
Note that a metric space is uniformly disconnected
if and only if
it is bi-Lipschitz to an ultrametric space
(see e.g.,
[
18
, Lemma 5.1.10]
).

By the definition of the topology of

\mathrm{Met}(X)
,
we obtain:

Lemma 4.10
.

Let

X

be a metrizable space.
Fix two points

a
,
b

in

X
.
Then a map

f\colon\mathrm{Met}(X)\to\mathbb{R}

defined by

f(d)=d(a,b)

is
continuous.

Proposition 4.11
.

The uniform disconnectedness on metic spaces
is a transmissible property with a singular parameter.

Proof.

Define a map

F_{UD}\colon\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1)\to\mathcal{F}((\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})^{2})

by

For each metrizable space

,
and for each

Let

be a metric space.
Let

\mathscr{G}
is countable.

\pc(X,d)

.

The rich pseudo-cones property on metric spaces is

Proof.

Let

Lemma 5.1

We define a metric

\mathcal{E}

\mathcal{E}(f,g)=\min\left\{1,\sup_{(x,y)\in X^{2}}|f(x,y)-g(x,y)|\right\}.

Note that
the metric

\mathcal{E}|_{\mathrm{Met}(X)^{2}}

on

\mathrm{Met}(X)

generates the same topology as

\mathcal{D}_{X}

on

\mathrm{Met}(X)
,
which coincides with the uniform topology.

Note that the space

(C(X^{2}),\mathcal{E})

is completely metrizable.
By Lemma

2.8
,
to prove the lemma,
it suffices to show that

\mathrm{Met}(X)

is

G_{\delta}

in

(C(X^{2}),\mathcal{E})
.
We denote by

\mathrm{PM}

(1)

for every

x\in X

f(x,x)=0
;

(2)

for all

x,y\in X

f(x,y)=f(y,x)

and

f(x,y)\geq 0
;

(3)

for every triple

x,y,z\in X

we have
f(x,y)\leq f(x,z)+f(z,y)
.

Namely,

\mathrm{PM}

is the set of all
continuous pseudo-metrics on

X
.
Note that

\mathrm{PM}

is
a closed subset in the metric space

(C(X^{2}),\mathcal{E})
.
Since all closed subsets
of a metric space are

G_{\delta}

in the whole space,
the set

\mathrm{PM}

is

G_{\delta}

in

(C(X^{2}),\mathcal{E})
.

Using the assumption that

X

is second-countable and locally compact,
now we take a sequence

\{B(n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

of compact subsets of

X^{2}

with

\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}B(n)=X^{2}\setminus\Delta_{X}
,
where

\Delta_{X}

X^{2}
,

X

\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}K(n)=X
,
where

\nai
”

For every

n\in\mathbb{N}
,
let

\mathrm{L}_{n}

be the set of all

f\in C(X^{2})

there exists

c\in(0,\infty)

such that
for each

s>n

we have

\inf\{\,f(x,y)\mid x\in K(n),\ y\in X\setminus K(s)\,\}>c.

For each

n\in\mathbb{N}
,
let

\mathrm{E}_{n}

be
the set of all

f\in C(X^{2})

such that
for each

(x,y)\in B(n)

0<f(x,y)
.
Note that each

\mathrm{L}_{n}

and each

\mathrm{E}_{n}

are open subsets
of

(C(X^{2}),\mathcal{E})
.

We next prove that

,
take

.

\displaystyle<d(K(n),X\setminus\nai(K(n+1)))\leq d(K(n),X\setminus K(n+1))

.
Hence we obtain

\mathrm{Met}(X)\subset\mathrm{PM}\cap\left(\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{L}_{n}\right)\cap\left(\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{E}_{n}\right).

Next we take

d\in\mathrm{PM}\cap\left(\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{L}_{n}\right)\cap\left(\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{E}_{n}\right)
.
Since

d\in\mathrm{PM}\cap\left(\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{E}_{n}\right)
,
the function

d

is continuous on

X^{2}

and it is a metric on

X
.
Fix

e\in\mathrm{Met}(X)
.
We show that
the metric

d

is topologically equivalent to

e
.
Since

d

is continuous on

X^{2}
,
the metric

d

generates a weaker topology than that of

(X,e)
.
Namely,
if

\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}=a

in

(X,e)
,
then

\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}=a

in

(X,d)
.
Assume next that

\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}=a

in

(X,d)
.
Our purpose is
to prove
that

\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}\to a

in

(X,e)
.
Since

\{K(n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

is a covering of

X
,
there exists

s\in\mathbb{N}

such that

a\in K(s)
.
For the sake of contradiction,
suppose that
there exist infinitely many

i\in\mathbb{N}

with

(X\setminus K(i))\cap\{\,x_{n}\mid{n\in\mathbb{N}}\,\}\neq\emptyset
.
Then we have

\liminf_{i\to\infty}d(K(s),X\setminus K(i))\leq\lim_{j\to\infty}d(a,x_{j})=0.

This contradicts the fact that

d\in\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{L}_{n}
.
Hence there exists
a sufficiently large number

m\in\mathbb{N}

such that
the sequence

\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

is contained in

K(m)
.
We may assume that

s\leq m
, which implies that

a\in K(m)
.
By the compactness of

K(m)
and by
the fact that

(X,d)

is Hausdorff,
the map

1_{K(m)}\colon(K(m),e)\to(K(m),d)

becomes a homeomorphism.
Thus
the restricted metrics

e|_{K(m)^{2}}
and

d|_{K(m)^{2}}

generate the same topology on

K(m)
.
Since

\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}=a
in
(X,d)

and since

\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

is
contained in

K(m)
,
we see that

\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}=a

in

(X,e)
.

As a result,
the metric

d

generates the same topology as

e
,
and hence

\mathrm{Met}(X)\supset\mathrm{PM}\cap\left(\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{L}_{n}\right)\cap\left(\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{E}_{n}\right).

Therefore we conclude that

\mathrm{Met}(X)

is a

G_{\delta}

subset of

(C(X^{2}),\mathcal{E})
.
∎

1.3
.

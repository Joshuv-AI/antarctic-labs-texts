Abstract
Locally repairable codes(LRCs) play important roles in distributed storage systems(DSS). LRCs with small locality have their own advantages since fewer available symbols are needed in the recovery of erased symbols. In this paper, we prove an upper bound on the dimension of LRCs with minimum distance d ≥ 7 d\geq 7 . An upper bound on the length of almost optimal LRCs with d = 7 d=7 , r = 2 r=2 at q 2 + q + 3 q^{2}+q+3 is proved. Then based on the t t -spread structure, we give an algorithm to construct almost optimal LRCs with d = 7 d=7 , r = 2 r=2 and length n ≥ 3 ​ ⌈ 2 ​ q 3 ⌉ n\geq 3\lceil\frac{\sqrt{2}q}{3}\rceil when q ≥ 4 q\geq 4 , whose dimension attains the aforementioned upper bound. Index Terms -Locally repairable codes, distributed storage systems, t t -spreads.

Introduction

To meet the growing needs for efficient and reliable cloud storage systems and big data storage systems, distributed storage systems have been widely used. In distributed storage systems, a data is partitioned and stored in different
data storage devices. Inevitably, data loss may occur. Erasure codes are introduced to prevent data loss, such as locally repairable codes.
The notions of
the locality of code symbols
and the formal definition of

[
1

[
2

Let

An

\mathcal{C}

and

LRCs with parameters satisfying

•

Now, we give the formal definition of locally repairable codes.

Definition 1

A
q
-ary
[n,k,d]
linear code
\mathcal{C}
is said to be a locally repairable code with
locality
r
if for any
i\in[n]
, there exists a set
R_{i}\subseteq[n]\backslash\{i\}

such that
|R_{i}|\leq r
and for any codeword
(c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{n})\in\mathcal{C}

c_{i}=\sum_{j\in R_{i}}{k_{j}c_{j}},

where

are fixed elements.
R_{i}\cup\{i\}
is called a local repair group.
We denote its parameters by
(n,k,d,r)_{q}
.

If some local repair groups form a partition of
[n]
,
and each of them has cardinality
(r+1)
, then we say that
\mathcal{C}
has disjoint local
repair

An LRC has disjoint local repair groups if and only if it has an equivalent parity-check matrix of the following form:

\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc|cccc|c|cccc}1&1&\dots&1&0&0&\dots&0&\dots&0&0&\dots&0\\
0&0&\dots&0&1&1&\dots&1&\dots&0&0&\dots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&0&0&0&\dots&0&\dots&1&1&\dots&1\\
\hline\cr\bm{u}_{1}^{(1)}&\dots&\bm{u}_{r}^{(1)}&\bm{0}&\bm{u}_{1}^{(2)}&\dots&\bm{u}_{r}^{(2)}&\bm{0}&\dots&\bm{u}_{1}^{(L)}&\dots&\bm{u}_{r}^{(L)}&\bm{0}\\
\end{array}\right]

Lemma 1

Let

]

Let

be positive integers and

,
then there is
no
(n,k,d,r)_{q}
LRC
satisfying

and

.

Remark 1

Due to this

. However, in this paper, we call it an almost optimal LRC since its minimum distance differs one from the

We review a recent result in

(

Let C be an optimal

3.1
An upper bound on the dimension of LRCs with disjoint local repair groups and

By analysing the parity-check matrix, we have the following upper bound on the dimension of LRCs with minimum distance

Lemma 3

Let

\mathcal{C}

Let

be the

-th column of the above matrix,

We have the following claim.

Claim 1
:Let
S_{1}=\{(i,j,t)|i\textless j\in[r+1]\backslash\{1\},t=1\}
,
S_{2}=\{(i,j,t)|i\textless j\in[r+1],t\in[L]\backslash\{1\}\}

\{\Span\{\bm{u}_{1,2}^{(1)},\bm{u}_{i,j}^{(t)}\}|(i,j,t)\in S_{1}\cup S_{2}\}

and these subspaces pairwisely intersect in the

To prove Claim 1, we need to show that for any

\bm{u}_{i_{1},j_{1}}^{(t_{1})},\bm{u}_{i_{2},j_{2}}^{(t_{2})},\bm{u}_{1,2}^{(1)}

Assume that

, where

, then we have

k_{1}(\bm{h}_{j_{1}}^{(t_{1})}-\bm{h}_{i_{1}}^{(t_{1})})+k_{2}(\bm{h}_{j_{2}}^{(t_{2})}-\bm{h}_{i_{2}}^{(t_{2})})+k_{3}(\bm{h}_{2}^{(1)}-\bm{h}_{1}^{(1)})=\bm{0}.

If
k_{3}\neq 0
, then the coefficient of
\bm{h}_{1}^{(1)}
in above equation is non-zero. Note that any six columns of

are linear independent. This leads to a contradiction.

If

,

\bm{h}_{j_{1}}^{(t_{1})}

\bm{h}_{i_{1}}^{(t_{1})}
in above equation is non-zero, which is a contradiction.

If
k_{3}=0
,
k_{2}\neq 0
, then the coefficient of
\bm{h}_{j_{2}}^{(t_{2})}
or
\bm{h}_{i_{2}}^{(t_{2})}
in above equation is non-zero, which is a contradiction.

Thus,

,
\bm{u}_{i_{1},j_{1}}^{(t_{1})},\bm{u}_{i_{2},j_{2}}^{(t_{2})},\bm{u}_{1,2}^{(1)}
are linear independent.

By Claim 1, there exist
\big(L\frac{r(r+1)}{2}-r\big)
distinct two-dimensional subspaces containing
\Span\{\bm{u}_{1,2}^{(1)}\}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{u}
. Note that there are totally
\frac{q^{u}-q}{q^{2}-q}

\Span\{\bm{u}_{1,2}^{(1)}\}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{u}
. We have

\frac{q^{u}-q}{q^{2}-q}\geq L\frac{r(r+1)}{2}-r,

(4)

q^{u}\geq q+(q-1)q(\frac{r}{2}n-r),

k\leq\frac{rn}{r+1}-\log_{q}\big(q+(q-1)q(\frac{r}{2}n-r)\big).

Since

\frac{rn}{r+1}
are positive integers, we have

k\leq\frac{rn}{r+1}-\lceil\log_{q}\big(q+(q-1)q(\frac{r}{2}n-r)\big)\rceil.

[

In this paper, we focus on LRCs with minimum distance

We prove an upper bound on the code length of almost optimal LRCs with

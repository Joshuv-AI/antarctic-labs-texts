Abstract




I
Explanation of the agreement between the generalized Kirchhoff’s law of thermal radiation and the scattering model

In the Generalized Kirchhoff’s law of thermal radiation, absorptivity describes the rate of excited electrons by a light source and is defined as
\alpha=1-\rho-\tau
, where
\rho
and
\tau
are the reflection and transmission coefficients, respectively. These electrons recombine radiatively or non-radiatively (defined by
QE
). The photoluminescence rate normalized by the pump,
PL_{n}
, and absorptivity are related as follows:

PL_{n}=\alpha\times QE

Following Eq. (8) from
Miller et al. 2017
, we generalize the scattering coefficient
|s_{p}|^{2}
as “everything that is exiting the material” in normalized units (including reflection, transmission, and
PL_{n}
):

|s_{p}|^{2}=\rho+\tau+\alpha\times QE=1-\alpha+\alpha\times QE

(S.1)

Following Eq. (9) from
Miller et al. 2017
, for the absorptivity of the system and Eq. (12) from
Miller et al. 2017
, substituting absorptivity and emissivity according to Kirchhoff’s law in equilibrium, we get:

\epsilon_{M}{}_{p}=\alpha_{M}{}_{p}=1-|s_{p}|^{2}

Using (
S.1
), this results in:

\epsilon_{M}{}_{p}=1-(1-\alpha+\alpha\times QE)=\alpha\times(1-QE)

In addition, Eq. (10) from
Miller et al. 2017
defines the linear superposition between scattered light and thermal emission. We show in our model the same linear superposition between PL (at 0K) and thermal emission.

II
Boltzmann distribution of excited electron populations between two energy levels

The electron population in the three-level systems is described by Eq. (12a) and (12b) in the paper. For convenience, we present these equations here as well:

\displaystyle\frac{dn_{2}}{dt}=

\displaystyle(n_{1}-n_{2})B_{r12}n_{ph12}-n_{2}\gamma_{r12}+(n_{1}-n_{2})B_{nr12}n_{pn12}-n_{2}\gamma_{nr12}+

(S.2a)

\displaystyle(n_{3}-n_{2})B_{nr23}n_{pn23}+n_{3}\gamma_{nr23}

\displaystyle\frac{dn_{3}}{dt}=

\displaystyle(n_{1}-n_{3})B_{r13}n_{ph13}-n_{3}\gamma_{r13}+(n_{1}-n_{3})B_{nr13}n_{pn13}-n_{3}\gamma_{nr13}-

(S.2b)

\displaystyle(n_{3}-n_{2})B_{nr23}n_{pn23}-n_{3}\gamma_{nr23}

The last two terms in both equations are the non-radiative interactions between
n_{2}
and
n_{3}
.
\gamma_{nr23}
is the spontaneous non-radiative rate and
B_{nr23}=\gamma_{nr23}/DoS_{pn}
is the stimulated non-radiative rate. The electronic levels interact with the phonon field, given by the equilibrium distribution
n_{pn23}=DoS_{pn}\left[exp\left(\frac{E_{23}}{k_{B}T}\right)-1\right]^{-1}
. Thus, we can rewrite these two last terms from Eq. (
S.2a
) as:

\displaystyle(n_{3}-n_{2})B_{nr23}n_{pn23}-n_{3}\gamma_{nr23}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma_{nr23}\left(\left[exp\left(\frac{E_{23}}{k_{B}T}\right)-1\right]^{-1}\left[n_{3}-n_{2}\right]+n_{3}\right)

(S.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n_{3}\gamma_{nr23}\left(\left[exp\left(\frac{E_{23}}{k_{B}T}\right)-1\right]^{-1}\left[1-\frac{n_{2}}{n_{3}}\right]+1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n_{3}\gamma_{nr23}\left(1-\frac{\frac{n_{2}}{n_{3}}-1}{exp\left(\frac{E_{23}}{k_{B}T}\right)-1}\right)

For the case of dominant
\gamma_{nr23}
, at steady-state where
\frac{dn_{2}}{dt}=\frac{dn_{3}}{dt}=0
, the equality can be satisfied when these terms approach zero, leading to a Boltzmann distribution:

\frac{n_{2}}{n_{3}}\approx exp\left(\frac{E_{23}}{k_{B}T}\right)

(S.4)

III
Analytical derivation of the generalized Kirchhoff law
\epsilon=\alpha\times(1-QE)
for a two-level system

In Eq. (4), (5a) and (5b) in the paper, we derived the theoretical formalism for the populations of excited electrons and photons within a cavity with a two-level system. This formalism enables the calculation of the rate of photon emission from the cavity. We subsequently demonstrated that this emission consists of two mechanisms: quantum and thermal. For convenience, we have rewritten the equations and will now proceed to prove the primary equationn.

The rate of photons leaving the cavity:

n_{ph12}\Gamma_{out}=\Gamma_{out}\frac{n_{pump}\Gamma_{p}+NB_{nr12}n_{pn12}\frac{\gamma_{r}}{\gamma_{r}+\gamma_{nr}}}{\Gamma_{out}+NB_{r12}\left(1-\frac{\gamma_{r}}{\gamma_{r}+\gamma_{nr}}\right)}

(S.5)

Where:

L_{quantum}=\Gamma_{out}\frac{n_{pump}\Gamma_{p}}{\Gamma_{out}+NB_{r12}\left(1-\frac{\gamma_{r}}{\gamma_{r}+\gamma_{nr}}\right)}

(S.6a)

L_{thermal}=\Gamma_{out}\frac{NB_{nr12}n_{pn12}\frac{\gamma_{r}}{\gamma_{r}+\gamma_{nr}}}{\Gamma_{out}+NB_{r12}\left(1-\frac{\gamma_{r}}{\gamma_{r}+\gamma_{nr}}\right)}

(S.6b)

We refer to the ratio between the emitted (or reflected) and incoming photons into the cavity as the external quantum efficiency (EQE):

EQE\equiv\frac{n_{ph12}\Gamma_{out}}{n_{pump}\Gamma_{p}}=\Gamma_{out}\frac{1+\frac{NB_{nr12}n_{pn12}X}{n_{pump}\Gamma_{p}}}{\Gamma_{out}+NB_{r12}(1-X)}

(S.7)

Where
X=\frac{\gamma_{r}}{\gamma_{r}+\gamma_{nr}}
.

For
any

\gamma_{r}
, we can examine the case of
QE\rightarrow 0
where
\gamma_{nr}\gg\gamma_{r}
and Eq. (
S.7
) is reduced to:

EQE_{(QE\rightarrow 0)}=\frac{1}{1+\frac{NB_{r12}}{\Gamma_{out}}}

(S.8)

EQE can be positive while
QE\rightarrow 0
when the non-absorbed pump is reflected off the cavity. In this case, absorptivity is:

\alpha=1-EQE_{(QE\rightarrow 0)}=\frac{1}{1+\frac{\Gamma_{out}}{NB_{r12}}}

(S.9)

which is independent of
\gamma_{nr}
. At 0K, phonons are not thermally excited (
n_{pn12}\rightarrow 0
) and Eq. (
S.7
) becomes:

EQE_{(T\rightarrow 0)}=\frac{\Gamma_{out}}{\Gamma_{out}+NB_{r12}(1-X)}

(S.10)

The quantum efficiency is defined only at 0K, where the thermal emission vanishes, and it equals to the
EQE_{(T\rightarrow 0)}
minus the photons reflected off the cavity, normalized to the pump absorbed photons. Therefore:

\displaystyle QE_{(T\rightarrow 0)}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{n_{ph12}\Gamma_{out}-(\alpha-1)n_{pump}\Gamma_{p}}{\alpha\cdot n_{pump}\Gamma_{p}}

(S.11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{EQE_{(T\rightarrow 0)}}{\alpha}-\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{X}{1+\frac{NB_{r12}}{\Gamma_{out}}(1-X)}

In order to relate the EQE and the absorption coefficient,
\alpha
, we simplified Eq. (
S.11
):

\alpha(1-QE)=\frac{NB_{r12}(1-X)}{\Gamma_{out}+NB_{r12}(1-X)}

(S.12)

The rate of photons leaving the cavity when the pump is off is the thermal emission, Eq. (
S.6b
). After substituting
n_{pn}
it can rewritten as:

n_{ph12}\Gamma_{out}=L_{thermal}=\Gamma_{out}f(T)\frac{NB_{nr12}DoS_{pn}X}{\Gamma_{out}+NB_{r12}(1-X)}

(S.13)

Considering the relations between the stimulated and spontaneous rates, we get:

B_{nr12}=B_{r12}\frac{1-X}{X}\cdot\frac{DoS_{ph}}{DoS_{pn}}

(S.14)

Substituting back into Eq. (
S.13
) yields:

L_{thermal}=\Gamma_{out}DoS_{ph}\cdot f(T)\frac{NB_{r12}(1-X)}{\Gamma_{out}+NB_{r12}(1-X)}

(S.15)

Using Eq. (
S.12
), the thermal emission becomes:

L_{thermal}=\Gamma_{out}\alpha\left(1-QE\right)DoS_{ph}\cdot f(T)

(S.16)

Comparing Eq. (
S.16
) with Plank’s law of thermal radiation:

L_{BB}=\epsilon\cdot DoS_{ph}\cdot f(T)

, results in the prime equation:

\epsilon=\alpha\left(1-QE\right)

(S.17)

In this model, we assumed that the fundamental material parameters

\gamma_{r},\gamma_{nr},DoS_{ph},DoS_{pn}

are temperature independent. Therefore, the emissivity,
\epsilon
, quantum efficiency and stimulated rates

B_{r12},B_{nr12}

are also temperature independent.

V
Normalization of the measured thermal spectrum

The experiment is performed in a free-space system, depicted in the paper. All samples are similarly aligned relative to the fiber. The numerical aperture and the distance at which the fiber is placed are such that the edges have no direct optical path to the fiber. Since Er:YAG has non-unity absorption at the measured spectra, we also account for the volume emission confined by the sample,
presented in FIG.
S.2
.

Figure S.2
:
Sample solid angle and emitting area.

Therefore, we normalize the measured thermal emission power with the solid angle of the fiber and measured emitting volume.

I_{emitted}[W]=I_{measured}[W]\cdot\frac{V_{sample}}{V_{cone}}\cdot\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{fiber}}

(S.18)

Then
I_{emitted}
is divided by the sample area and the
\pi
solid angle to obtain the radiance:

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{I_{emitted}}{\pi A_{sample}}

(S.19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{I_{measured}}{\pi A_{sample}}\cdot\frac{V_{sample}}{V_{cone}}\cdot\frac{4\pi}{\Omega_{fiber}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{I_{measured}\cdot L}{\Omega_{fiber}\cdot V_{cone}}\left[\frac{W}{sr\cdot m^{2}}\right]

Here
V_{sample}=a\cdot L^{2}
and
A_{sample}=4aL
(upper and lower faces are reflecting), where
a
is the sample’s height and
L
is the sample’s length and width. In this case, the emissivity
\epsilon=\frac{R}{B.B}
where
B.B
denotes the black-body emission.

VII
Emissivity independent of temperature

The generalized Kirchhoff’s law
\epsilon=\alpha\times(1-QE)
is correct for any wavelength direction and
QE
.
For simplicity in the paper, we develop the temperature evolution of the emission for
\gamma_{r}
and
\gamma_{nr}
to be temperature independent.
We show that this assumption is true for the experiment with the Er:YAG samples.
Fig.
S.5
depicts the emissivity values of all measured samples. These values are the spectral sum of the emissivity
\sum_{\lambda}\epsilon(\lambda)
, obtained as the ratio of the thermal and black-body emissions at the range of 1450-1650 nm. We can see that the emissivity is, to a good approximation, constant, and therefore, independent of temperature.

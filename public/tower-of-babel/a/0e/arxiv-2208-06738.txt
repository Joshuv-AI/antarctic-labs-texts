Proofs of main results

Corollary 1
.

Let
\mathbf{\Sigma},\,\mathbf{H},\,\tilde{\mathbf{\Sigma}}
be as defined in
M1
-
M3
, then iff
m\leq n
,
\tilde{\mathbf{\Sigma}}
can be expressed as the covariance matrix of a CAR model
(\mathbf{I}-\tilde{\mathbf{C}})^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf{M}}
for a unique pair of
m\times m
matrices
\tilde{\mathbf{C}}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{M}}
.

Proof.

To prove the sufficiency part, since Theorem 2.1 states that the statement is true for any positive-definite covariance matrix, we only need to prove that
\tilde{\mathbf{\Sigma}}
is in fact positive definite. This can be achieved by simply noting that:
\tilde{\mathbf{\Sigma}}=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{H}^{T}=\mathbf{H}^{T}\mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{H}
and both
\mathbf{\Sigma}
and
\mathbf{H}
are of full rank, so by applying (3) in
Theorem 14.2.9
by
harville2008matrix
we obtain the positive definiteness of
\tilde{\mathbf{\Sigma}}
.

To prove necessity, we observe that when
m>n

rank(\tilde{\mathbf{\Sigma}})=n
making it rank deficient, thus we conclude that it cannot be also positive definite.
∎

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathbf{H}
be a matrix as defined in
M1
-
M3
.
\mathbf{H}
has a left inverse
\mathbf{L}
iff
m\geq n
, and this matrix is unique iff
m=n
.

Proof.

In the case
m=n
it is obvious that
\mathbf{L}
exists, given that
\mathbf{H}
is of full rank by
M3
, and in fact coincides with
\mathbf{H}^{-1}
guaranteeing uniqueness.
Since an
m\times n
matrix has a left inverse iff it is of full column rank, it follows that when
m<n
,
\mathbf{H}
will not have any such inverse.

Moving on to the
m>n
case, following Theorem 9.2.7 in
harville2008matrix
: for a generic
m\times n
matrix
\mathbf{H}
and any of its generalised inverses
\mathbf{G}
, an
n\times m
matrix
\mathbf{G}^{*}
is also a generalised inverse of
\mathbf{H}
iff

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}^{*}=\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{Z}-\mathbf{G}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{G},

(1)

for some
n\times m
matrix
\mathbf{Z}
. We can verify that in our case this is true for
any

\mathbf{Z}
. If we take
\mathbf{G}=\left(\mathbf{H}^{T}\mathbf{H}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{H}^{T}
as a generalised inverse: first, we note that
\mathbf{G}
is evidently a left inverse, then we can see that so is
\mathbf{G}^{*}
, in fact for any
\mathbf{Z}
we have:

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}^{*}\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{G}\mathbf{H}+\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{H}+\mathbf{G}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{I}_{n}+\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{H}-\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{I}_{n},

(2)

thus proving non uniqueness of
\mathbf{H}
’s left inverse.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Any left inverse
\mathbf{L}
as in Lemma
1
will satisfy the same constraint
M2-1
as
\mathbf{H}
, i.e the columns adding up to the
\mathbf{1}
vector

Proof.

\mathbf{H}\mathbf{1}_{n}=\mathbf{1}_{m}\,\Leftrightarrow\,\mathbf{L}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{1}_{n}=\mathbf{L}\mathbf{1}_{m}\,\Leftrightarrow\,\mathbf{1}_{n}=\mathbf{L}\mathbf{1}_{m}
.
∎

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathbf{\Sigma},\,\mathbf{H},\,\tilde{\mathbf{\Sigma}}
be as defined in
M1
-
M3
, then given
\tilde{\mathbf{\Sigma}}
and
\mathbf{H}
,
\mathbf{\Sigma}
is uniquely defined iff
m\geq n
.

Proof.

In the case
m\geq n
it is evident that there is only one
\mathbf{\Sigma}
for a given
\mathbf{H}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{\Sigma}}
. This can be proved by contradiction because, if we assume that there was not, it would mean that there existed a covariance matrix
\mathbf{\Sigma}^{*}\neq\mathbf{\Sigma}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}\mathbf{\Sigma}^{*}\mathbf{H}^{T}=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{H}^{T},

(3)

but, by Lemma
1
, we have that a left inverse exists thus leading to
\mathbf{\Sigma}^{*}=\mathbf{\Sigma}
which contradicts the initial assumption.

For
m<n
it suffices to look at a simple case
m=2
and
n=3
case. Assuming

\mathbf{H}=\begin{pmatrix}0.5&0&0.5\\
0&1&0\end{pmatrix}
, then in order to find a
3\times 3
covariance matrix
\mathbf{\Sigma}
for a fixed
2\times 2

\tilde{\mathbf{\Sigma}}
, we would need to solve a 4 equation linear systems with 9 unknowns, which is clearly underdetermined, thus making
\mathbf{\Sigma}
not uniquely defined.
∎

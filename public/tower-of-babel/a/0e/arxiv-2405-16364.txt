On a multi-dimensional transport equation with nonlocal velocity and fractional dissipation Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author
Wanwan Zhang 1

Abstract
. This paper aims to investigate a multi-dimensional transport equation with nonlocal velocity and fractional dissipation. The balance between the nonlinearity and dissipation gives rise to three different cases, namely the subcritical, critical and supercritical ranges. We study those three cases and obtain a set of results containing local well-posedness, global smoothness, eventual regularity and finite-time blowup of smooth solutions.

35Q35; 35B35; 76D05

1.
Introduction and main results

In this paper, we consider a multi-dimensional transport equation with nonlocal velocity and fractional dissipation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\theta+u\cdot\nabla\theta+\kappa\Lambda^{\gamma}\theta=0,~(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
&u=\nabla\Lambda^{-2+2\alpha}\theta,\\
&\theta(x,0)=\theta_{0}(x),~x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{aligned}\ \right.

(1.1)

where
n\geq 2
,
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
,
\gamma\in(0,2)
and
\kappa>0
. Here
\theta:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
is the unknown function, and the fractional Laplacian
\Lambda^{s}=(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}
with
s\in\mathbb{R}
is defined by the Fourier transform:

\displaystyle\widehat{\Lambda^{s}f}(\xi)=(2\pi|\xi|)^{s}\widehat{f}(\xi).

The non-locality of velocity
u
is due to the singular integral representation of
\eqref{1.1}_{2}
(see, e.g.,
[
39
]
):

\displaystyle u(x,t)=C_{n,\alpha}P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{n+2\alpha}}\theta(y,t)dy,

(1.2)

where

\displaystyle C_{n,\alpha}=-\frac{2^{2\alpha-1}\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+\alpha)}{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}.

The one-dimensional analogous model of (
1.1
) has been extensively studied.
When
n=1
and
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
, (
1.1
) reduces to the well-known Córdoba-Córdoba-Fontelos model

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\theta-H(\theta)\theta_{x}+\kappa\Lambda^{\gamma}\theta=0,

(1.3)

where
H\theta
is the Hilbert transform of
\theta
. The inviscid case, i.e., (
1.3
) with
\kappa=0
, was first proposed by Córdoba, Córdoba and Fontelos in
[
15
]
as a one-dimensional analogue of the two-dimensional surface quasi-geostrophic equation (SQG), which is an incompressible model (see e.g.,
[
9
,
37
]
).
We will not review here the known results for SQG and related model. One can refer to
[
5
,
7
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
17
,
18
,
23
,
24
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
42
]
and the references therein for more details and the recent progress.
Here we briefly summarize the progress related to the CCF model.
Regarding the inviscid CCF model, Córdoba et al.
[
15
]
first obtained an ingenious nonlinear weighed inequality
for the Hilbert transform with the help of Meillin transform and complex analysis, and proved
smooth solutions must blow up in finite time for a generic family of even initial data (see also
[
16
]
for another proof of the blowup for the initial data not necessarily even).
This blow-up phenomena was later proved by Silvestre and Vicol in
[
38
]
via four essentially different methods.
Based on completely real-variable arguments, Li and Rodrigo
[
36
]
recently provided a short proof of the nonlinear inequality first proved by Córdoba et al. in
[
15
]
, and obtained several new weighted inequalities for the Hilbert transform and various nonlinear versions, which can be applied to show the finite-time blow-up of smooth solutions to (
1.1
) with
n=1
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\kappa=0
.
For the dissipative case, the authors in
[
15
]
also obtained the global well-posedness of (
1.3
) for the positive
H^{2}
initial data in the subcritical case
\gamma\in(1,2)
, as well as in the case
\gamma=1
with small initial data. Later in
[
20
]
, by adapting the method of continuity first used in
[
30
]
, Dong established the global regularity of solutions to (
1.3
) in the critical case
\gamma=1
for arbitrary initial data in appropriate critical Sobolev space. In
[
34
]
, Li and Rodrigo first proved the finite-time blowup of smooth solutions to (
1.3
) in the supercritical case
\gamma\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
(see also
[
29
,
36
,
38
]
for different proofs for this blow-up result).
For the remaining range
\gamma\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
, whether smooth solutions to (
1.3
) may blow up in finite time is currently still open. In
[
38
]
, the authors conjectured that solutions obtained as vanishing viscosity approximations could be bounded in
C^{\frac{1}{2}}
for all
t>0
, which would possibly yield Hölder regularization effect and then solve the problem of global regularity of solutions to (
1.3
) with
\gamma\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
.
Recently, for each smooth nonnegative
\theta_{0}
, Ferreira and Moitinho
[
25
]
obtained the existence of global classical solutions to (
1.3
) for
\gamma\in(\gamma_{1},1)
with
\gamma_{1}
depending on the
H^{\frac{3}{2}}
-norm of
\theta_{0}
.
Ferreira and Moitinho
[
26
]
also studied the interpolation
\alpha
-CCF model, i.e., (
1.1
) with
n=1
,
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
\gamma\in(0,2)
, and obtained a set of results including local existence, global smoothness, eventual regularity and blow-up of solutions.

We continue to review the existing results on the multi-dimensional (
1.1
). The inviscid case of (
1.1
) was understood well.
The local well-posedness of (
1.1
) with
\kappa=0
in the Sobolev space
H^{s}
for appropriate
s>0
was established by Chae in
[
6
]
.
For
\kappa=0
and
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
in (
1.1
), Balodis and Córdoba
[
3
]
presented a weighted nonlinear inequality for the Riesz transform by using Meillin transform and spherical harmonic expansion, and obtained the blow-up of smooth solution for any nonnegative, not-identically zero initial data.
When
n=2
, such finite-time singularity formation result was also proved for a similar model in
[
22
]
independently.
For the general dimension
n\geq 2
and the full range
\alpha\in(0,1)
, Dong later in
[
21
]
was able to obtain the blow-up of smooth solutions to (
1.1
) with
\kappa=0
for any smooth, radially symmetric and nonnegative
\theta_{0}
with compact support and its positive maximum attained at the origin. Recently, motivated by
[
38
]
, Jiu and Zhang
[
27
]
proved the finite-time singularity of solutions to (
1.1
) with
\kappa=0
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
n\geq 2
for smooth initial data
\theta_{0}
with
\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\theta_{0}(x)>0
via the De Giorgi iteration technique.
This iteration strategy in
[
38
]
was later adapted by Alonso-Orán and Martínez
[
1
]
to the proof of finite-time blow-up for non-local active scalar equations (
1.1
) with
\kappa=0
on compact Riemannian manifolds.
For the two-dimensional and fractionally dissipative case , Li and Rodrigo
[
35
]
proved the finite-time blow-up of radial smooth solutions to (
1.1
) for
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{4},1)
and
\gamma\in(0,\alpha)
.
Very recently, Li, Liu and Zhang
[
33
]
studied a related nonlocal transport model with the power type of damping, which reads as

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\theta+u\cdot\nabla\theta+\kappa|\theta|^{\nu-1}\theta=0,~(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
&u=\nabla\Lambda^{-2+2\alpha}\theta,\\
&\theta(x,0)=\theta_{0}(x),~x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\end{aligned}\ \right.

(1.4)

where
n\geq 1
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
and
\nu>0
. In
[
33
]
, by some change of time variable to implement the iteration technique originated from
[
38
]
, the authors showed that the damping term can not avoid the singularity formation in finite time, and that for particular
\theta_{0}
depending on the size of its
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
-norm, the solutions to (
1.4
) must blow up in finite time, independent of the value of
\kappa
.

In the present paper, we investigate the multi-dimensional nonlocal transport (
1.1
) with fractional
dissipation. Due to scaling property, if
\theta(x,t)
solves (
1.1
) with the initial datum
\theta_{0}
, then
for any
\lambda>0
,

\displaystyle\theta_{\lambda}(x,t)\triangleq\lambda^{\gamma-2\alpha}\theta(\lambda x,\lambda^{\gamma}t)

so does with the initial datum
\lambda^{\gamma-2\alpha}\theta_{0}(\lambda x)
. Therefore, the initial value problem (
1.1
) admits three basic cases: subcritical
\gamma\in(2\alpha,2)
, critical
\gamma=2\alpha
and supercritical
\gamma\in(0,2\alpha)
.
For the critical and subcritical dissipation, our main result on (
1.1
) can be stated as

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
\gamma\in[2\alpha,2)
.
For every nonnegative initial datum
\theta_{0}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists a unique global-in-time smooth solutions to (
1.1
).

In the case of supercritical dissipation with
\gamma
close to
2\alpha
, we have the next type of global regularity result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
\gamma\in(\alpha,2\alpha)
.
Let
R>0
be arbitrary. For every nonnegative initial datum
\theta_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
with
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
such that

\displaystyle\|\theta_{0}\|^{\frac{n+4\alpha-2\gamma}{2s}}_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\|\theta_{0}\|^{1-\frac{n+4\alpha-2\gamma}{2s}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq R,

(1.5)

there exists
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{1}(R)\in(\alpha,2\alpha)
such that for each
\gamma\in[\gamma_{1},2\alpha)
the initial problem (
1.1
) admits a unique global
H^{s}
-solution.

For the case of supercritical dissipation with appropriately small
\gamma
, we obtain the finite time blowup result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\gamma\in(0,\alpha)
.
For any radial initial datum
\theta_{0}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that for some constant
A>0
depending only on
n
,
\alpha
and
\gamma
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\theta_{0}(0)-\theta_{0}(x)}{|x|^{n}}e^{-|x|}dx\geq A(1+\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}),

the corresponding smooth solution
\theta
to (
1.1
) must blow up in finite time.

Remark 1.1
.

We make some comments on Theorems
1.1
-
1.3
. Firstly, Dong claimed without proofs in
[
21
]
that one can modify the arguments of nonlocal moduli of continuity firstly appeared in
[
30
]
to prove Theorem
1.1
.
One can refer to
[
30
]
for the construction of the modulus of continuity for the case
\alpha=\frac{1}{2},\gamma=1
, and refer to
[
26
]
for
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
\gamma\in[2\alpha,2)
. For the transparent way in which the dissipation dominates the nonlinear term,
we show Theorem
1.1
by utilizing the nonlinear maximum principle firstly presented in
[
11
]
. Secondly, the author also claimed without proofs in
[
21
]
that, for
\gamma\in(0,\alpha)
and
\delta\in(0,2\alpha-2\gamma)
, there exists a constant
N
depending only on
n,\alpha,\gamma
and
\delta
, such that if

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\theta_{0}(0)-\theta_{0}(x)}{|x|^{n+\delta}}dx\geq N(1+\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}),

then the solution to (
1.1
) with the radially symmetric
\theta_{0}
blows up in finite time.
We revisit this problem of the finite time blowup of smooth solutions to (
1.1
) with
\gamma\in(0,\alpha)
and obtain Theorem
1.3
by some different arguments.
Finally, the 1-d analogous conclusions of Theorem
1.2
for
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
and
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
were established in
[
25
]
and
[
26
]
, respectively.

Now we explain our approaches in some details. Considering all basic cases of
\gamma
, that is, for
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
\gamma\in(0,2)
, we firstly study the local well-posedness of solutions to (
1.1
) and after provide a Beale-Kato-Majda blow-up criterion and control on the existence time for initial datum
\theta_{0}
in
H^{s}
with
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
(see Propositions
3.1
-
3.2
and Corollary
3.4
). In the critical and subcritical cases we show the global existence of smooth solutions via an approach based on the nonlinear maximum principle inspired by
[
11
]
. For the supercritical case, with the help of a conditional regularity result (see Lemma
5.1
), we prove an eventual regularization result, i.e., Proposition
5.2
, and then conclude the global existence of solutions when
\gamma
is sufficiently close to
2\alpha
for nonnegative initial datum.
Finally, for the supercritical case
\gamma\in(0,\alpha)
, we establish a new weighted inequality involving the dissipation along with the use of a nonlinear weighted inequality from
[
41
]
to show the finite time blowup result (see Lemmas
6.1
and
6.2
).

The outline of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some definitions and basic facts needed later.
Section 3 is devoted to the local well-posedness, the Beale-Kato-Majda criterion, and a suitable control on the existence time for the model (
1.1
).
In Section 4, we prove Theorem
1.1
.
The proof of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
is given in Sections
5
and
6
, respectively.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we will present some notations and basic facts needed later.

2.1.
Notations

For
p\in[1,\infty]
, we denote
L^{p}(\Omega)
the standard
L^{p}
-space and its norm by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
.
For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we use
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
to denote the homogeneous Sobolev space of
s
order,
whose endowed norm is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}=\|\Lambda^{s}(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}
. Here the fractional Laplacian
\Lambda^{s}=(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}
with
s\in\mathbb{R}
is defined by the Fourier transform (see e.g.,
[
2
]
). For
s\in(0,2)
, another equivalent definition is given as follows: for
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
,

\displaystyle(\Lambda^{s}f)(x)=C_{n,s}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\frac{f(x)-f(x+y)}{|y-2\pi k|^{n+s}}dy=C_{n,s}P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(x)-f(x+y)}{|y|^{n+s}}dy,

(2.1)

where
C_{n,s}
is a normalization constant (see, e.g.,
[
19
]
).
In the above identity, and throughout this paper, we abuse notation and still denote by
f
the periodic extension of
f
to the whole space.
Moreover, for
\beta\in(0,1)
, we recall the Hölder space
C^{\beta}(\Omega)
whose norm is given by

\|f\|_{C^{\beta}(\Omega)}=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+[f]_{C^{\beta}(\Omega)}
, where

[\cdot]_{C^{\beta}(\Omega)}
means the semi-norm

\displaystyle[f]_{C^{\beta}(\Omega)}=\sup_{x,y\in\Omega,x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}.

For convenience of the notation, the
L^{p}(\Omega)
-norm of a function
f
is always abbreviated as
\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}}
and the
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
-norm as
\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}
. The positive part of
\ln a
with
a>0
is given by

\displaystyle\ln^{+}a=\begin{cases}\ln a,&\mbox{{\rm for} $a\geq 1,$ }\\
0,&\mbox{{\rm for} $0<a<1.$ }\\
\end{cases}

It is clear that, for any
a>0
,

\displaystyle 1+\ln^{+}a\leq 2\ln(a+e).

(2.2)

Throughout this paper, we will denote by
\chi
a smooth radially nondecreasing cut-off function that vanishes for
|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}
, is equal to 1 for
|x|\geq 1
, and its gradient satisfies
\|\nabla\chi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2
. We will use
C
to denote a positive constant, whose value may change from line to line, and write
C_{n,\alpha}
or
C(n,\alpha)
to emphasize the dependence of a constant on
n
and
\alpha
.

2.2.
Some basic facts

In the following, we present some basic facts needed later.
We begin with a classical commutator estimate for fractional Laplacian (see, e.g.,
[
28
]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
s>0
. Let
p,p_{1},p_{4}\in(1,\infty)
,
p_{2},p_{3}\in(1,\infty]
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{p_{3}}+\frac{1}{p_{4}}.

For
f,g\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
or
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there exists a constant
C=C_{n,s,p,p_{1},p_{3}}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{s}(fg)-f\Lambda^{s}g\|_{L^{p}}\leq C(\|\Lambda^{s}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p_{3}}}\|\|\Lambda^{s-1}g\|_{L^{p_{4}}}).

For the fractional operator
\Lambda^{\gamma}
, we next recall a pointwise identity, which follows from the arguments in

[
14
]
(see also
[
8
]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,2)
and
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Then, for all
x\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
,

\displaystyle f(x)\Lambda^{\gamma}f(x)=\frac{1}{2}\Lambda^{\gamma}(f^{2})(x)+\frac{1}{2}D_{\gamma}(f)(x),

where the operator
D_{\gamma}
is defined by

\displaystyle D_{\gamma}(f)(x)=c_{n,\gamma}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(x+y)|^{2}}{|y-2\pi k|^{n+\gamma}}dy=c_{n,\gamma}P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(x)-f(x+y)|^{2}}{|y|^{n+\gamma}}dy.

(2.3)

We proceed to present the
L^{\infty}
and
C^{\beta}
nonlinear lower bounds for the operator
D_{\gamma}
, whose proof can be found in
[
11
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,2)
and
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle D_{\gamma}(\nabla f)(x)\geq\frac{|\nabla f(x)|^{2+\gamma}}{c_{n,\gamma}\|f\|^{\gamma}_{L^{\infty}}}.

In addition, for any
\beta\in(0,1)
and for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle D_{\gamma}(\nabla f)(x)\geq C\frac{|\nabla f(x)|^{2+\frac{\gamma}{1-\beta}}}{\|f\|^{\frac{\gamma}{1-\beta}}_{C^{\beta}}}.

We end this subsection with an auxiliary inequality needed later.

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
\rho>0
, we have

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{\rho}\frac{e^{-r}}{r}dr\leq 2\ln\Big(e+\frac{1}{\rho}\Big).

Proof
.
For
\rho>1
,

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{\rho}\frac{e^{-r}}{r}dr\leq\int^{\infty}_{1}\frac{e^{-r}}{r}dr<1.

For the remaining value
\rho\in(0,1]
, by integration by parts, we derive that

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{\rho}\frac{e^{-r}}{r}dr=\frac{\ln(\frac{1}{\rho})}{e^{\rho}}+\int^{\infty}_{\rho}\frac{\ln r}{e^{r}}dr\leq\ln\Big(\frac{1}{\rho}\Big)+\int^{\infty}_{1}\frac{\ln r}{e^{r}}dr\leq\ln\Big(\frac{1}{\rho}\Big)+1.

Therefore, it follows from (
2.2
) that, for any
\rho>0
,

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{\rho}\frac{e^{-r}}{r}dr\leq\ln^{+}\Big(\frac{1}{\rho}\Big)+1\leq 2\ln\Big(e+\frac{1}{\rho}\Big),

which concludes the proof of Lemma
2.4
.

2.3.
Some properties of solutions

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\gamma\in(0,2)
and
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{n}
or
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
If
\theta
is a smooth solution to (
1.1
) on
[0,T]
for nonnegative
\theta_{0}\in H^{s}(\Omega)
with
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
, then

\displaystyle 0\leq\theta(x,t)\leq\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},~for~all~(x,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T],

and

\displaystyle\|\theta(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},~for~all~t\in[0,T].

(2.4)

Proof
. Define

\displaystyle m(t)=\min_{x\in\Omega}\theta(x,t),~M(t)=\max_{x\in\Omega}\theta(x,t).

Following completely analogous arguments as Lemma 3.2 in
[
27
]
, we can show that
m(t)
is non-decreasing, and that
M(t)
is non-increasing on
[0,T]
. Thus, for all
(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\times[0,T]
,

\displaystyle 0\leq\min_{x\in\Omega}\theta_{0}(x)\leq m(t)\leq\theta(x,t)\leq M(t)\leq\max_{x\in\Omega}\theta_{0}(x).

(2.5)

Multiplying (
1.1
) by
\theta
and integrating by parts lead to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\kappa\|\Lambda^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\theta(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\theta^{2}{\rm div}udx.

(2.6)

By the parity, (
2.1
) and (
2.5
), we note that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\theta^{2}{\rm div}udx&=-\int_{\Omega}\theta^{2}\Lambda^{2\alpha}\theta dx=-C_{n,\alpha}\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\theta^{2}(x,t)\frac{\theta(x,t)-\theta(y,t)}{|x-y|^{n+2\alpha}}dxdy\\
&=-\frac{C_{n,\alpha}}{2}\iint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\theta(x,t)+\theta(y,t))(\theta(x,t)-\theta(y,t))^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+2\alpha}}dxdy\leq 0,\end{split}

which along with (
2.6
) yields that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq 0.

By direct integration, it immediately gives (
2.4
). We complete the proof of Lemma
2.5
.

At the end of this section, we show that the radial symmetry of initial data is preserved by solutions to (
1.1
).

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\gamma\in(0,2)
.
If
\theta
is a smooth local solution to (
1.1
) with radially symmetric
\theta_{0}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
\theta(x,t)
is radial for all time
t>0
.

Proof
. Let
\mathbf{O}
be any orthogonal matrix. By the uniqueness of solutions to (
1.1
) (c.f. Proposition
3.1
below) and the radial property of
\theta_{0}
, it suffices to show that
\theta_{\mathbf{O}}(x,t):=\theta(\mathbf{O}x,t)
is also a solution to (
1.1
) with the initial data
\theta_{0}(\mathbf{O}x)
. Indeed, following some computations in
[
41
]
, we have

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}\theta_{\mathbf{O}})(x,t)=(\partial_{t}\theta)(\mathbf{O}x,t),~(\nabla_{x}\theta_{\mathbf{O}})(x,t)=\mathbf{O}^{T}(\nabla_{x}\theta)(\mathbf{O}x,t),

and

\displaystyle u_{\mathbf{O}}(x,t)=\nabla\Lambda^{-2+2\alpha}\theta_{\mathbf{O}}=\mathbf{O}^{-1}u(\mathbf{O}x,t).

By (
2.1
) and a change of variables, we also have

\displaystyle(\Lambda^{\gamma}\theta_{\mathbf{O}})(x,t)=C_{n,\gamma}P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\theta(\mathbf{O}x,t)-\theta(\mathbf{O}y,t)}{|x-y|^{n+\gamma}}dy

\displaystyle\quad=C_{n,\gamma}P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\theta(\mathbf{O}x,t)-\theta(z,t)}{|Ox-z|^{n+\gamma}}dz=\Lambda^{\gamma}\theta(\mathbf{O}x,t).

Finally, we derive that

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}\theta_{\mathbf{O}}+u_{\mathbf{O}}\cdot\nabla\theta_{\mathbf{O}}+\Lambda^{\gamma}\theta_{\mathbf{O}})(x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}\theta)(\mathbf{O}x,t)+\mathbf{O}^{-1}u(\lambda x,t)\cdot\mathbf{O}^{T}(\nabla_{x}\theta)(\mathbf{O}x,t)+\Lambda^{\gamma}\theta(\mathbf{O}x,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}\theta+u\cdot\nabla_{x}\theta+\Lambda^{\gamma}\theta)(\mathbf{O}x,t)=0.

The proof of Lemma
2.6
is then finished.

3.
Local well-posedness and Beale-Kato-Majda criterion

In this section, we study the local existence of solutions to (
1.1
). Also, we obtain a regularity criterion and a control on the existence time.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
,
\gamma\in(0,2)
and
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{n}
or
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For each
\theta_{0}\in H^{s}(\Omega)
with
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
, there exists
T=T(\|\theta_{0}\|_{H^{s}})>0
and a unique solution to (
1.1
) such that

\displaystyle\theta\in L^{\infty}([0,T);H^{s}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}([0,T);H^{s+\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\Omega)).

(3.1)

Proof
. We derive the local energy estimate.
By (
2.6
) and Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\kappa\|\Lambda^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\theta(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\theta(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\|{\rm div}u\|_{L^{\infty}}.

(3.2)

Applying
\Lambda^{s}
on
\eqref{1.1}_{1}
, multiplying by
\Lambda^{s}\theta
, integrating by parts and utilizing Hölder’s inequality, we can arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\Lambda^{s}\theta(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\kappa\|\Lambda^{s+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\theta(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

(3.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\Lambda^{s}\theta\Big[\Lambda^{s}(u\cdot\nabla\theta)-u\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{s}\theta\Big]dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}{\rm div}u(\Lambda^{s}\theta)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{s}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{s}(u\cdot\nabla\theta)-u\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{s}\theta\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\|{\rm div}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s}\theta\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

By virtue of Lemma
2.1
, the commutator in (
3.3
) can be bounded as

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{s}(u\cdot\nabla\theta)-u\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{s}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(\|\Lambda^{s}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\Lambda^{s-1}\nabla\theta\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}).

(3.4)

For
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
,
L^{2}
-boundedness of the Riesz transform and the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality lead us to following estimates

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{s}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\Lambda^{s-1+2\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\theta\|^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{s}}_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{s}\theta\|^{1-\frac{1-2\alpha}{s}}_{L^{2}},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|\theta\|^{1-\frac{n+2}{2s}}_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{s}\theta\|^{\frac{n+2}{2s}}_{L^{2}},

(3.6)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}&=\|\Lambda^{2\alpha}(\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathcal{R})\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\leq C\|(\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathcal{R})\theta\|^{1-\frac{n+4\alpha}{2s}}_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{s}(\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathcal{R})\theta\|^{\frac{n+4\alpha}{2s}}_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\|\theta\|^{1-\frac{n+4\alpha}{2s}}_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{s}\theta\|^{\frac{n+4\alpha}{2s}}_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

(3.7)

Substituting estimates (
3.4
)-(
3.7
) into (
3.3
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\Lambda^{s}\theta(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\kappa\|\Lambda^{s+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\theta(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\theta(t)\|^{1-\frac{n+4\alpha}{2s}}_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{s}\theta(t)\|^{2+\frac{n+4\alpha}{2s}}_{L^{2}}.

(3.8)

Finally, inserting (
3.7
) into (
3.2
), and adding with (
3.8
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta(t)\|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\kappa\|\Lambda^{s+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\theta(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\theta(t)\|^{3}_{H^{s}}.

With the help of this energy estimate, we then use the standard technique (see, e.g.,
[
40
]
) to obtain the local existence of solutions to (
1.1
).

We proceed to prove the uniqueness of solutions. Suppose that
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
are solutions to (
1.1
) with the initial data
\theta_{0}\in H^{s}(\Omega)
in the class (
3.1
).
Thus
\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}
satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})+(u_{1}-u_{2})\cdot\nabla\theta_{1}+u_{2}\cdot\nabla(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})+\kappa\Lambda^{\gamma}(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})=0.

(3.9)

Multiplying (
3.9
) by
\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}
, integrating over
\mathbb{T}^{n}
and integrating by parts, we can arrive at

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta_{1}(t)-\theta_{2}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\kappa\|\theta_{1}(t)-\theta_{2}(t)\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\\
=&\underbrace{-\int_{\Omega}((u_{1}-u_{2})\cdot\nabla\theta_{1})(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})dx}_{I_{1}}+\frac{1}{2}\underbrace{\int_{\Omega}(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})^{2}\Lambda^{2\alpha}\theta_{2}dx}_{I_{2}}.\end{split}

By Hölder’s inequality and the
L^{2}
-boundedness of Riesz’s transform, we can derive that

\displaystyle\begin{split}|I_{1}|&\leq\|\Lambda^{-(1-2\alpha)}\mathcal{R}(\theta_{1}-\theta_{2})\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2+4\alpha}}}\|\nabla\theta_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{1-2\alpha}}}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\|\theta_{1}\|_{H^{s}}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}},\end{split}

where in the last inequality we used the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{-(1-2\alpha)}f\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2+4\alpha}}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}~{\rm~for}~\alpha\in\Big(0,\frac{1}{2}\Big)

and the embedding inequality
\|\nabla\theta_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{n}{1-2\alpha}}}\leq C\|\theta_{1}\|_{H^{s}}
for
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
.

Similarly, we also have

\displaystyle|I_{2}|\leq\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{2\alpha}\theta_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|\theta_{2}\|_{H^{s}}\|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta_{1}(t)-\theta_{2}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C[\|\theta_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T);H^{s})}+\|\theta_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T);H^{s})}]\|\theta_{1}(t)-\theta_{2}(t)\|_{L^{2}},

which along with Gronwall’s inequality yields the uniqueness of solutions.
The proof of Proposition
3.1
is now completed.

Next, we prove the Beale-Kato-Majda blow-up criterion for (
1.1
), which can be stated as

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
\gamma\in(0,2)
. Let
\theta
be the solution to (
1.1
) constructed as in Proposition
3.1
for nonnegative
\theta_{0}\in H^{s}
with
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
.
Assume
T_{\ast}
is the first time such that
\theta
cannot be continued in the class (
3.1
) to
T=T_{\ast}
. Then

\displaystyle\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow T_{\ast}}\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{s}}=\infty~{\rm if~and~only~if}~\int_{0}^{T_{\ast}}\|\nabla\theta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}dt=\infty.

To prove Proposition
3.2
, we need the
L^{\infty}
estimate of the gradient of the velocity, whose proof will be postponed into Appendix A.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
,
\theta\in H^{s}
with
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
, and
u
be defined via
\theta
by (
1.2
). Then

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\begin{cases}C\Big[1+(1+\ln^{+}\|\theta\|_{H^{s}})\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{2}}\Big],&\mbox{{\rm for} $\alpha=\frac{1}{2},$ }\\
C(1+\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{2}}),&\mbox{{\rm for} $\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})$. }\\
\end{cases}

(3.10)

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.
To show the necessity, we will assume
\int_{0}^{T_{\ast}}\|\nabla\theta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}dt\triangleq M_{0}<\infty,

and then show that there exists some
C_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{k}}\leq C_{0},~t<T_{\ast},

(3.11)

which is a contradiction with our assumption.
Indeed, by (
3.2
)-(
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{s}}\leq C(\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{s}}.

(3.12)

In view of Lemma
3.3
, we may need the estimate of
\nabla\theta
in
L^{2}
.
Applying
\nabla
on
\eqref{1.1}_{1}
and taking its dot product by
\nabla\theta
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla)|\nabla\theta|^{2}+\kappa\nabla\theta\cdot\Lambda^{\gamma}\nabla\theta+\nabla u:\nabla\theta\cdot\nabla\theta=0,

(3.13)

which follows from direct integration, integration by parts and (
2.4
) that,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla\theta\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\kappa\|\Lambda^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\nabla\theta\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\rm div}u|\nabla\theta|^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla u:\nabla\theta\cdot\nabla\theta dx

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|\Lambda^{2\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\leq\begin{cases}C\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\theta\|^{2}_{L^{2}},&\mbox{for $\alpha=\frac{1}{2},$ }\\
C\|\theta_{0}\|^{1-2\alpha}_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\theta\|^{1+2\alpha}_{L^{2}},&\mbox{for $\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}).$ }\\
\end{cases}

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{2}}\leq\begin{cases}C\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{2}},&\mbox{for $\alpha=\frac{1}{2},$ }\\
C\|\theta_{0}\|^{1-2\alpha}_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\theta\|^{2\alpha}_{L^{2}},&\mbox{for $\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}).$ }\\
\end{cases}

Solving this differential inequality directly yields that, for any
t\in(0,T_{\ast})
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\nabla\theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}&\leq\begin{cases}\|\nabla\theta_{0}\|_{L^{2}}e^{C\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\theta(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}d\tau},&\mbox{for $\alpha=\frac{1}{2},$ }\\
\Big[\|\nabla\theta_{0}\|^{1-2\alpha}_{L^{2}}+C_{\alpha,\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{2}}}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\theta(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}d\tau\Big]^{\frac{1}{1-2\alpha}},&\mbox{for $\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}).$ }\\
\end{cases}\\
&\leq\begin{cases}\|\nabla\theta_{0}\|_{L^{2}}e^{CM_{0}},&\mbox{for $\alpha=\frac{1}{2},$ }\\
\Big(\|\nabla\theta_{0}\|^{1-2\alpha}_{L^{2}}+CM_{0}\Big)^{\frac{1}{1-2\alpha}},&\mbox{for $\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}).$ }\\
\end{cases}\end{split}

By virtue of this bound for
\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{2}}
, Lemma
3.3
and (
2.2
), we can get

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\begin{cases}C\Big[1+\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}\log(e+\|\theta\|_{H^{s}})\Big],&\mbox{for $\alpha=\frac{1}{2},$ }\\
C(1+\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}),&\mbox{for $\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}).$ }\\
\end{cases}

(3.14)

Here
C
denotes a constant depending on
M_{0}
and
T_{\ast}
.
Substituting (
3.14
) into (
3.12
), we can derive that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{s}}\leq\begin{cases}C\Big[1+\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}\log(e+\|\theta\|_{H^{s}})\Big]\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{s}},&\mbox{for $\alpha=\frac{1}{2},$ }\\
C(1+\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{s}},&\mbox{for $\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})$, }\\
\end{cases}

It follows from Gronwall’s inequality that, for
t\in(0,T_{\ast})
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{s}}&\leq\begin{cases}e^{[\log(e+\|\theta_{0}\|_{H^{s}})+C\int_{0}^{t}(1+\|\nabla\theta(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}})d\tau]e^{C\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\theta(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}d\tau}},&\mbox{for $\alpha=\frac{1}{2},$ }\\
\|\theta_{0}\|_{H^{s}}e^{C\int_{0}^{t}(1+\|\nabla\theta(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}})d\tau},&\mbox{for $\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}),$ }\\
\end{cases}\\
&\leq\begin{cases}e^{[\log(e+\|\theta_{0}\|_{H^{s}})+C(T_{\ast}+M_{0})]e^{CM_{0}}},&\mbox{for $\alpha=\frac{1}{2},$ }\\
\|\theta_{0}\|_{H^{s}}e^{C(T_{\ast}+M_{0})},&\mbox{for $\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}),$ }\\
\end{cases}\end{split}

which gives (
3.11
).
The sufficient part follows easily by Sobolev’s inequality: for
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T_{\ast}}\|\nabla\theta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}dt\leq CT_{\ast}\sup_{t\in[0,T_{\ast}]}\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{s}}.

The proof of Proposition
3.2
is now finished.

We conclude this section by providing a lower bound for the existence time.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
\gamma\in(0,2)
. For every nonnegative
\theta_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
with
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
, consider the unique local-in-time solution

\displaystyle\theta\in L^{\infty}([0,T);H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))\cap L^{2}([0,T);H^{s+\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))

to (
1.1
) originating from
\theta_{0}
provided by Proposition
3.1
. Then

\displaystyle T\geq\frac{1}{C\|\theta_{0}\|^{1-\frac{n+4\alpha}{2s}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\|\theta_{0}\|^{\frac{n+4\alpha}{2s}}_{\dot{H}^{s}(T^{n})}},

where
C
is the constant appearing in (
3.8
).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, let
[0,T)
be the maximal time interval.
By (
3.8
) and (
2.4
) in Lemma
2.5
, we derive that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\leq C\|\theta_{0}\|^{1-\frac{n+4\alpha}{2s}}_{L^{2}}\|\theta(t)\|^{1+\frac{n+4\alpha}{2s}}_{\dot{H}^{s}}.

Solving this differential inequality directly yields that

\displaystyle\|\theta(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\leq\frac{\|\theta_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}}{\Big[1-C\Big(\frac{n}{2s}+\frac{2\alpha}{s}\Big)\|\theta_{0}\|^{1-\frac{n+4\alpha}{2s}}_{L^{2}}\|\theta_{0}\|^{\frac{n+4\alpha}{2s}}_{\dot{H}^{s}}t\Big]^{\frac{2s}{n+4\alpha}}},

which along with (
2.4
) and
s>\frac{n}{2}+1\geq\frac{n}{2}+2\alpha
implies that
\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{s}}
cannot blow up until

\displaystyle T_{0}\triangleq\frac{1}{C\|\theta_{0}\|^{1-\frac{n+4\alpha}{2s}}_{L^{2}}\|\theta_{0}\|^{\frac{n+4\alpha}{2s}}_{\dot{H}^{s}}}<\frac{1}{C\Big(\frac{n}{2s}+\frac{2\alpha}{s}\Big)\|\theta_{0}\|^{1-\frac{n+4\alpha}{2s}}_{L^{2}}\|\theta_{0}\|^{\frac{n+4\alpha}{2s}}_{\dot{H}^{s}}}.

This fact shows that
T\geq T_{0}
. The proof of Corollary
3.4
is then completed.

4.
Global regularity for the subcritical and critical dissipation

In this section, we will prove Theorem
1.1
, which is on the global regularity of the solutions to (
1.1
) with critical and subcritical dissipation.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Proposition
3.2
,
it is sufficient to obtain a control of
\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}
uniformly in time.
For this purpose, by (
3.13
), Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
, we can derive that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla+\Lambda^{\gamma})|\nabla\theta|^{2}+c_{1}\frac{|\nabla\theta|^{2+\gamma}}{\|\theta_{0}\|^{\gamma}_{L^{\infty}}}+\frac{1}{4}D_{\gamma}(\nabla\theta)\leq|\nabla u||\nabla\theta|^{2}.

(4.1)

Here, for simplicity, we consider
\kappa=1
.
We proceed to estimate the term
\nabla u
in (
4.1
), which will be divided into two different cases, i.e., (
1.1
) with sub-critical and critical dissipation.

Subcritical dissipation
:
2\alpha<\gamma<2
.
Similar to (
1.2
), we split
\nabla u
into two pieces:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\nabla u(x,t)&=\nabla\Lambda^{-2+2\alpha}(\nabla\theta)(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{n+2\alpha}}(\nabla\theta(y,t)-\nabla\theta(x,t))dy\\
&=\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big[1-\chi\Big(\frac{x-y}{\rho}\Big)\Big]F(x,y,t)dy}_{J_{1}}+\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi\Big(\frac{x-y}{\rho}\Big)F(x,y,t)dy}_{J_{2}},\end{split}

where
\rho=\rho(x,t)>0
will be chosen later and

\displaystyle F(x,y,t)=\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{n+2\alpha}}(\nabla\theta(y,t)-\nabla\theta(x,t))

(4.2)

Also, the constant depending on
n
and
\alpha
before the integral has been omitted.

By Hölder’s inequality,
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and (
2.3
),
J_{1}
can be bounded by

\displaystyle\begin{split}|J_{1}|&\leq\int\limits_{|x-y|\leq\rho}\frac{|\nabla\theta(y,t)-\nabla\theta(x,t)|}{|x-y|^{n-1+2\alpha}}dy\\
&\leq\Big[\int\limits_{|y|\leq\rho}\frac{dy}{|y|^{n-2+4\alpha-\gamma}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big[\int\limits_{|x-y|\leq\rho}\frac{|\nabla\theta(y,t)-\nabla\theta(x,t)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{n+\gamma}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq c_{2}(n,\alpha,\gamma)\rho^{1-2\alpha+\frac{\gamma}{2}}(D_{\gamma}(\nabla\theta)(x))^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

(4.3)

By integration by parts, Hölder’s inequality, the choice of cut-off function
\chi
and Lemma
2.5
, we can derive that

\displaystyle\begin{split}|J_{2}|&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big[\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{|\nabla\chi(\frac{x-y}{\rho})|}{|x-y|^{n-1+2\alpha}}+\frac{|\chi(\frac{x-y}{\rho})|}{|x-y|^{n+2\alpha}}\Big]|\theta(y,t)-\theta(x,t)|dy\\
&\leq 2\|\theta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big[\int\limits_{\frac{\rho}{2}<|z|\leq\rho}\frac{2dz}{\rho|z|^{n-1+2\alpha}}+\int\limits_{|z|>\frac{\rho}{2}}\frac{dz}{|z|^{n+2\alpha}}\Big]\leq c_{3}(n,\alpha)\frac{\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\rho^{2\alpha}}.\end{split}

(4.4)

Gathering up (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) gives that

\displaystyle|\nabla u(x,t)|\leq c_{2}\rho^{1-2\alpha+\frac{\gamma}{2}}(D_{\gamma}(\nabla\theta)(x))^{\frac{1}{2}}+c_{3}\frac{\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\rho^{2\alpha}},

which follows form Young’s inequality and (
4.1
) that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla+\Lambda^{\gamma})|\nabla\theta|^{2}+c_{1}\frac{|\nabla\theta|^{2+\gamma}}{\|\theta_{0}\|^{\gamma}_{L^{\infty}}}\leq c^{2}_{2}\rho^{2-4\alpha+\gamma}|\nabla\theta|^{4}+c_{3}\frac{\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\rho^{2\alpha}}|\nabla\theta|^{2}.

In this inequality, we explicitly choose
\rho>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{c_{1}}{2}\frac{|\nabla\theta|^{2+\gamma}}{\|\theta_{0}\|^{\gamma}_{L^{\infty}}}=c^{2}_{2}\rho^{2-4\alpha+\gamma}|\nabla\theta|^{4},~{\rm i.e.,}~\rho=\Big[\frac{c_{1}}{2c^{2}_{2}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2-4\alpha+\gamma}}\frac{|\nabla\theta|^{\frac{\gamma-2}{2-4\alpha+\gamma}}}{\|\theta_{0}\|^{\frac{\gamma}{2-4\alpha+\gamma}}_{L^{\infty}}}

to yield that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla+\Lambda^{\gamma})|\nabla\theta|^{2}\leq c_{3}\Big[\frac{2c^{2}_{2}}{c_{1}}\Big]^{\frac{2\alpha}{2-4\alpha+\gamma}}\|\theta_{0}\|^{1+\frac{2\alpha\gamma}{2-4\alpha+\gamma}}_{L^{\infty}}|\nabla\theta|^{\frac{2(1-\alpha)(2+\gamma)}{2-4\alpha+\gamma}}-\frac{c_{1}}{2}\frac{|\nabla\theta|^{2+\gamma}}{\|\theta_{0}\|^{\gamma}_{L^{\infty}}},

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{c_{1}|\nabla\theta|^{\frac{2(1-\alpha)(2+\gamma)}{2-4\alpha+\gamma}}}{2\|\theta_{0}\|^{\gamma}_{L^{\infty}}}\Big[\frac{2c_{3}}{c_{1}}\Big(\frac{2c^{2}_{2}}{c_{1}}\Big)^{\frac{2\alpha}{2-4\alpha+\gamma}}\|\theta_{0}\|^{\frac{(\gamma+2)(\gamma+1-2\alpha)}{2-4\alpha+\gamma}}_{L^{\infty}}-|\nabla\theta|^{\frac{(2+\gamma)(\gamma-2\alpha)}{2-4\alpha+\gamma}}\Big].

(4.5)

For further arguments, we set

\displaystyle c_{4}=\Big[\frac{2c_{3}}{c_{1}}\Big]^{\frac{2-4\alpha+\gamma}{(2+\gamma)(\gamma-2\alpha)}}\Big[\frac{2c^{2}_{2}}{c_{1}}\Big]^{\frac{2\alpha}{(2+\gamma)(\gamma-2\alpha)}},

and introduce a nondecreasing
C^{2}
convex function
\phi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
satisfying

\displaystyle\phi(r)=\begin{cases}0,&\mbox{{\rm for} $0\leq r\leq\max\Big\{\|\nabla\theta_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}},c^{2}_{4}\|\theta_{0}\|^{2+\frac{2}{\gamma-2\alpha}}_{L^{\infty}}\Big\},$ }\\
{\rm positive},&\mbox{{\rm otherwise}. }\\
\end{cases}

By virtue of the convexity of
\phi
, we see that (see e.g.,
[
8
]
)

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(|\nabla\theta|^{2})\Lambda^{\gamma}(|\nabla\theta|^{2})\geq\Lambda^{\gamma}(\phi(|\nabla\theta|^{2})).

Thus, we may multiply (

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla+\Lambda^{\gamma})\phi(|\nabla\theta|^{2})\\
\leq&\frac{c_{1}\phi^{\prime}(|\nabla\theta|^{2})|\nabla\theta|^{\frac{2(1-\alpha)(2+\gamma)}{2-4\alpha+\gamma}}}{\|\theta_{0}\|^{\gamma}_{L^{\infty}}}\Big[\frac{2c_{4}}{c_{1}}\Big[\frac{2c_{3}}{c_{1}}\Big]^{\frac{2\alpha}{2-4\alpha+\gamma}}\|\theta_{0}\|^{\frac{(\gamma+2)(\gamma+1-2\alpha)}{2-4\alpha+\gamma}}_{L^{\infty}}-|\nabla\theta|^{\frac{(2+\gamma)(\gamma-2\alpha)}{2-4\alpha+\gamma}}\Big]\leq 0,\end{split}

which follows from
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
\gamma>2\alpha
that

\phi(|\nabla\theta|^{2})=0
a.e.. Indeed, we can show that

\displaystyle M(t)=:\displaystyle\max_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{n}}\phi(|\nabla\theta|^{2})(x,t)=\phi(|\nabla\theta|^{2})(x_{t},t)

is almost differentiable in time. Also, we can derive that

\displaystyle M^{\prime}(t)\leq-C_{n,\gamma}P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\phi(|\nabla\theta|^{2})(x_{t},t)-\phi(|\nabla\theta|^{2})(y,t)}{|x_{t}-y|^{n+\gamma}}dy\leq 0,

which implies that

\displaystyle 0\leq M(t)\leq M(0)=\max\phi(|\nabla\theta_{0}|^{2})(x)=0.

This along with the choice of

yields that, for a.e.
x
and all

,

\displaystyle|\nabla\theta(x,t)|\leq\max\Big\{\|\nabla\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},c_{4}\|\theta_{0}\|^{1+\frac{1}{\gamma-2\alpha}}_{L^{\infty}}\Big\},

which is the desired estimate. Next we turn to the proof of critical part of Theorem

.

Critical dissipation
:
\gamma=2\alpha
.
Following
[
11
]
, the proof is split in two steps. The first step shows that a solution to (
1.1
) having “only small shocks” is regular, while
the second step shows that if the initial data has only small shocks, then so does the solution for all later time.
To be precise, we begin with the definition of Only Small Shocks.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\delta>0
and
t>0
. We say
\theta(x,t)
has the
OSS_{\delta}
property, if there exists an
L>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\{(x,y):|x-y|<L\}}|\theta(x,t)-\theta(y,t)|\leq\delta.

Moreover, for
T>0
, we say
\theta(x,t)
has the uniform
OSS_{\delta}
property on
[0,T]
, if
there exists an
L>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\{(x,y,t):|x-y|<L,0\leq t\leq T\}}|\theta(x,t)-\theta(y,t)|\leq\delta.

(4.6)

The proof will be divided into a sequence of lemmas. The first states that the uniform
OSS_{\delta}
property implies regularity of the solution:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
\gamma=2\alpha
. For nonnegative initial data
\theta_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
, there exists a
\delta=\delta(\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}})>0
such that if
\theta
is a solution to (
1.1
) with the uniform
OSS_{\delta}
property on
[0,T]
,
then it is a smooth solution on
[0,T]
. Moreover

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\nabla\theta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(L,\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|\nabla\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}).

Proof
. By hypothesis, there exists an
L>0
such that (
4.6
) holds, where
\delta>0
will be chosen later. By (
1.2
) and (
4.2
), we split the gradient of the velocity into three parts as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\nabla u(x)&=\underbrace{\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big[1-\chi\Big(\frac{x-y}{\rho}\Big)\Big]F(x,y)dy}_{K_{1}}+\underbrace{\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi\Big(\frac{x-y}{L}\Big)F(x,y)dy}_{K_{3}}\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\underbrace{\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big[\chi\Big(\frac{x-y}{\rho}\Big)-\chi\Big(\frac{x-y}{L}\Big)\Big]F(x,y)dy}_{K_{2}},\end{split}

where
\rho=\rho(x,t)\leq L
will be determined later.
Similar to (
4.3
),
K_{1}
can be bounded by

\displaystyle|K_{1}|\leq c_{2}\rho^{1-2\alpha+\frac{\gamma}{2}}(D_{\gamma}(\nabla\theta)(x))^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.7)

Integrating by parts on
K_{2}
and utilizing Hölder’s inequality,
\rho\leq L
, (
4.6
) and Lemma
2.5
to yield that

\displaystyle\begin{split}|K_{2}|&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big|\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla\chi\Big(\frac{x-y}{\rho}\Big)-\frac{1}{L}\nabla\chi\Big(\frac{x-y}{L}\Big)\Big|\frac{|\theta(y,t)-\theta(x,t)|}{|x-y|^{n-1+2\alpha}}dy\\
&\ \ \ +\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big|\chi\Big(\frac{x-y}{\rho}\Big)-\chi\Big(\frac{x-y}{L}\Big)\Big|\frac{|\theta(y,t)-\theta(x,t)|}{|x-y|^{n+2\alpha}}dy\\

&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\int\limits_{\frac{\rho}{2}<|x-y|<L}\frac{|\theta(y,t)-\theta(x,t)|}{|x-y|^{n+2\alpha}}dy\\

(4.8)

Similar to (
4.4
), we can deduce that

\displaystyle|K_{3}|\leq c_{3}\frac{\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{L^{2\alpha}},

which along with (
4.7
) and (
4.8

\displaystyle|\nabla u(x,t)|\leq c_{2}\rho^{1-2\alpha+\frac{\gamma}{2}}(D_{\gamma}(\nabla\theta)(x))^{\frac{1}{2}}+c_{4}\Big[\frac{\delta}{\rho^{2\alpha}}+\frac{\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{L^{2\alpha}}\Big].

Inserting this estimate into (

) and utilizing Young’s inequality yields that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla+\Lambda^{2\alpha})|\nabla\theta|^{2}+c_{1}\frac{|\nabla\theta|^{2+\gamma}}{\|\theta_{0}\|^{\gamma}_{L^{\infty}}}\leq c^{2}_{2}\rho^{2-4\alpha+\gamma}|\nabla\theta|^{4}+c_{4}\Big[\frac{\delta}{\rho^{2\alpha}}+\frac{\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{L^{2\alpha}}\Big]|\nabla\theta|^{2}.

In this differential inequality, we choose
\rho\in(0,L]
such that
c^{2}_{2}\rho^{2-4\alpha+\gamma}|\nabla\theta|^{4}\leq\frac{c_{1}}{4}\frac{|\nabla\theta|^{2+\gamma}}{\|\theta_{0}\|^{\gamma}_{L^{\infty}}}
, that is,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho&=\min\Big\{\Big(\frac{c_{1}}{4c^{2}_{2}}\frac{|\nabla\theta|^{\gamma-2}}{\|\theta_{0}\|^{\gamma}_{L^{\infty}}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2-4\alpha+\gamma}},L\Big\}\\
&=\Big(\frac{c_{1}}{4c^{2}_{2}}\frac{|\nabla\theta|^{\gamma-2}}{\|\theta_{0}\|^{\gamma}_{L^{\infty}}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2-4\alpha+\gamma}},~{\rm whenever}~|\nabla\theta|\geq\frac{1}{L}\Big[\frac{c_{1}}{4c^{2}_{2}\|\theta_{0}\|^{\gamma}_{L^{\infty}}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2-2\alpha}},\end{split}

and then choose
\delta>0
such that

, that is,

\displaystyle\delta=\frac{c_{1}}{4c_{4}}\Big[\frac{c_{1}}{4c^{2}_{2}}\Big]^{\frac{2\alpha}{2-4\alpha+\gamma}}\frac{|\nabla\theta|^{\frac{(\gamma+2)(\gamma-2\alpha)}{2-4\alpha+\gamma}}}{\|\theta_{0}\|^{\frac{\gamma(\gamma-2\alpha+2)}{2-4\alpha+\gamma}}_{L^{\infty}}}=\frac{\frac{c_{1}}{4c_{4}}\Big[\frac{c_{1}}{4c^{2}_{2}}\Big]^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}}{\|\theta_{0}\|^{\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}_{L^{\infty}}}.

Then, when

, we can obtain that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla+\Lambda^{\gamma})|\nabla\theta|^{2}&\leq c_{4}\frac{\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{L^{2\alpha}}|\nabla\theta|^{2}-\frac{c_{1}}{2}\frac{|\nabla\theta|^{2+\gamma}}{\|\theta_{0}\|^{\gamma}_{L^{\infty}}}\\
&=\frac{c_{1}|\nabla\theta|^{2}}{2\|\theta_{0}\|^{\gamma}_{L^{\infty}}}\Big[\frac{2c_{4}\|\theta_{0}\|^{1+\gamma}_{L^{\infty}}}{c_{1}L^{2\alpha}}-|\nabla\theta|^{\gamma}\Big],\end{split}

which immediately implies that

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla+\Lambda^{\gamma})|\nabla\theta(x,t)|^{2}\leq 0,~{\rm whenever}~|\nabla\theta(x,t)|\geq C_{\ast}

where

\displaystyle C_{\ast}=\max\Big\{\frac{1}{L}\Big[\frac{2c_{4}}{c_{1}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\|\theta_{0}\|^{1+\frac{1}{\gamma}}_{L^{\infty}},\frac{1}{L}\Big[\frac{c_{1}}{4c^{2}_{2}\|\theta_{0}\|^{2\alpha}_{L^{\infty}}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2-2\alpha}}\Big\}.

Conducting the similar arguments as in the subcritical dissipation, we can derive that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\nabla\theta(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(L,\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|\nabla\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}),

which concludes the proof of Lemma

Proof
. By (
2.3
) and Lemma

, we derive that

\displaystyle D_{\gamma}(\delta_{h}\theta)(x)\geq c_{n,\gamma}P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\delta_{h}\theta(x)-\delta_{h}\theta(x+y))^{2}}{|y|^{n+\gamma}}\chi\Big(\frac{y}{\rho}\Big)dy

\displaystyle c_{n,\gamma}|\delta_{h}\theta(x)|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\chi(\frac{y}{\rho})}{|y|^{n+\gamma}}dy-2c_{n,\gamma}|\delta_{h}\theta(x)|\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\delta_{h}\theta(x+y)}{|y|^{n+\gamma}}\chi\Big(\frac{y}{\rho}\Big)dy\Big|

L
, then
\|\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times B_{L}(0))}<\frac{\sigma^{2}}{16}
,
and so, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
|h|<L
and
t\leq T
, (
4.15
) holds.
Therefore,
|\delta_{h}\theta(x,t)|\leq\sigma
for all
x,t\leq T
and
|h|\leq\min\{\Psi^{-1}(4\ln 2),L\}
.
We then complete the proof of Lemma
4.2
.

Combining Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
concludes the proof of the critical part of Theorem
1.1
.

5.
Smoothness for the supercritical case with
\gamma
close to
2\alpha

In this section, we show Theorem
1.2
.
We begin with a conditional regularity result of (
1.1
) with
\gamma\in(0,2\alpha)
, which shows that the control of the Hölder seminorm of the solution is sufficient to obtain smoothness.
It is obtained by following the steps of Constantin and Vicol
[
11
]
for the two-dimensional quasi-geostrophic equation without the use of Besov-space technique, which is utilized in
[
12
]
for this conditional regularity result.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
,
\gamma\in(0,2\alpha)
and
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{n}
or
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose that
\theta
is a solution of (
1.1
) in
C([0,T),H^{s}(\Omega))
for nonnegative initial data
\theta_{0}\in H^{s}(\Omega)
with
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
.
If
\theta\in L^{\infty}((t_{0},t_{1});C^{\beta}(\Omega))
with
0<t_{0}<t_{1}\leq T
and
\beta\in(2\alpha-\gamma,2\alpha)
,
then
\theta\in C^{\infty}(\Omega\times(t_{0},t_{1}])
.

Proof
.
We only consider the case of whole space for simplicity and clarity.
It is sufficient to prove that
\theta\in L^{\infty}((t_{0},t_{1}];W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. This is sub-critical information which may be used to bootstrap and show that
\theta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(t_{0},t_{1}])
.

To this end, by (
3.13
), Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
, we can derive that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla+\Lambda^{\gamma})|\nabla\theta|^{2}+\mathfrak{c}_{1}\frac{|\nabla\theta|^{2+\frac{\gamma}{1-\beta}}}{\|\theta\|^{\frac{\gamma}{1-\beta}}_{C^{\beta}}}+\frac{1}{4}D_{\gamma}(\nabla\theta)\leq|\nabla u||\nabla\theta|^{2}.

(5.1)

To bound
|\nabla u(x)|
, by (
1.2
) and (
4.2
), we split it into two pieces, according to an undetermined
\varrho>0
.

\displaystyle\nabla u(x,t)=\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big[1-\chi\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varrho}\Big)\Big]F(x,y)dy}_{\mathcal{J}_{1}}+\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varrho}\Big)F(x,y)dy}_{\mathcal{J}_{2}}.

Similar to (
4.3
), the inner piece
\mathcal{J}_{1}
can be bounded as

\displaystyle|\mathcal{J}_{1}|\leq\mathfrak{c}_{2}\varrho^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1-2\alpha}(D_{\gamma}(\nabla\theta)(x))^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For the outer piece, integrating by parts, we bound
\mathcal{J}_{2}
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\mathcal{J}_{2}|&=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla_{y}\Big[\chi\Big(\frac{x-y}{\rho}\Big)\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{n+2\alpha}}\Big](\theta(y,t)-\theta(x,t))dy\Big|\\
&\leq\|\theta\|_{C^{\beta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big|\nabla_{y}\Big[\chi\Big(\frac{x-y}{\rho}\Big)\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{n+2\alpha}}\Big]\Big||x-y|^{\beta}dy\\
&\lesssim\|\theta\|_{C^{\beta}}\int_{|z|\geq\frac{\varrho}{2}}\frac{dz}{|z|^{n+2\alpha-\beta}}\leq\mathfrak{c}_{3}\frac{\|\theta\|_{C^{\beta}}}{\varrho^{2\alpha-\beta}}.\end{split}

Therefore, we obtain that

\displaystyle|\nabla u(x)|\leq\mathfrak{c}_{2}\varrho^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1-2\alpha}(D_{\gamma}(\nabla\theta)(x))^{\frac{1}{2}}+\mathfrak{c}_{3}\frac{\|\theta\|_{C^{\beta}}}{\varrho^{2\alpha-\beta}}.

Substituting this estimate into (
5.1
) and utilizing Young’s inequality yield that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla+\Lambda^{\gamma})|\nabla\theta|^{2}+\mathfrak{c}_{1}\frac{|\nabla\theta(x)|^{2+\frac{\gamma}{1-\beta}}}{\|\theta\|^{\frac{\gamma}{1-\beta}}_{C^{\beta}}}\leq\mathfrak{c}^{2}_{2}\varrho^{\gamma+2-4\alpha}|\nabla\theta|^{4}+\mathfrak{c}_{3}\frac{\|\theta\|_{C^{\beta}}}{\varrho^{2\alpha-\beta}}|\nabla\theta|^{2}.

In this equality, we take
\varrho>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}^{2}_{2}\varrho^{\gamma+2-4\alpha}|\nabla\theta|^{4}=\mathfrak{c}_{3}\frac{\|\theta\|_{C^{\beta}}}{\varrho^{2\alpha-\beta}}|\nabla\theta|^{2},~{\rm i.e.,}~\varrho=\Big(\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{3}\|\theta\|_{C^{\beta}}}{\mathfrak{c}^{2}_{2}|\nabla\theta|^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\gamma+2-2\alpha-\beta}}

to imply that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla+\Lambda^{\gamma})|\nabla\theta|^{2}\leq\mathfrak{c}_{4}\|\theta\|^{\frac{\gamma+2-4\alpha}{\gamma+2-2\alpha-\beta}}_{C^{\beta}}|\nabla\theta|^{\frac{2(\gamma+2-2\beta)}{\gamma+2-2\alpha-\beta}}-\mathfrak{c}_{1}\frac{|\nabla\theta|^{2+\frac{\gamma}{1-\beta}}}{\|\theta\|^{\frac{\gamma}{1-\beta}}_{C^{\beta}}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{1}|\nabla\theta|^{\frac{2(\gamma+2-2\beta)}{\gamma+2-2\alpha-\beta}}}{\mathfrak{M}^{\frac{\gamma}{1-\beta}}}\Big[\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{4}}{\mathfrak{c}_{1}}\mathfrak{M}^{\frac{\gamma}{1-\beta}+\frac{\gamma+2-4\alpha}{\gamma+2-2\alpha-\beta}}-|\nabla\theta|^{\frac{\gamma}{1-\beta}+\frac{2(\beta-2\alpha)}{\gamma+2-2\alpha-\beta}}\Big],

where
\mathfrak{M}:=\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}C^{\beta}_{x}}
.
Note that, for
\beta>2\alpha-\gamma
,

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{1-\beta}+\frac{2(\beta-2\alpha)}{\gamma+2-2\alpha-\beta}>\frac{\gamma}{1-(2\alpha-\gamma)}+\frac{2(2\alpha-\gamma-2\alpha)}{\gamma+2-2\alpha-(2\alpha-\gamma)}=0.

Thus, we derive that

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla+\Lambda^{\gamma})|\nabla\theta(x,t)|^{2}\leq 0,

provided

\displaystyle|\nabla\theta(x,t)|\geq\Big[\frac{\mathfrak{c}_{4}}{\mathfrak{c}_{1}}\mathfrak{M}^{\frac{\gamma}{1-\beta}+\frac{\gamma+2-4\alpha}{\gamma+2-2\alpha-\beta}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{\frac{\gamma}{1-\beta}+\frac{2(\beta-2\alpha)}{\gamma+2-2\alpha-\beta}}}.

Then the maximum of
|\nabla\theta|
can not exceed a certain constant which depends on
\mathfrak{M}
, showing that

\nabla\theta\in L^{\infty}((t_{0},t_{1});L^{\infty})
. We then complete the proof of Lemma
5.1
.

Based on the conditional regularity result, we establish an eventual regularity result for (
1.1
) with the supercritical dissipation.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
,
\gamma\in(0,2\alpha)
and nonnegative
\theta_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
with
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
.
Consider
\beta\in(2\alpha-\gamma,2\alpha)
and define

\displaystyle T^{\ast}_{\alpha}:=C\beta^{\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha-\gamma}}\|\theta_{0}\|^{\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha-\gamma}}_{L^{\infty}},

(5.2)

where
C>0
is a constant independent of
\beta
and
\theta_{0}
.
If
\theta\in C([0,T),H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
is a solution to (
1.1
) and
T^{\ast}_{\alpha}<T<+\infty
, then
\theta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times(T^{\ast}_{\alpha},T])
.

In view of Lemma
5.1
, it is reduced to show that
\theta\in L^{\infty}((T^{\ast}_{\alpha},T);C^{\beta}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
.
For this purpose, we consider

\displaystyle\vartheta(x,t;h)\triangleq\frac{\delta_{h}\theta(x,t)}{(\eta^{2}(t)+|h|^{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}},

(5.3)

where
\eta:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is a bounded decreasing differentiable function determined later. In order to control the
C^{\beta}
-seminorm of
\theta
, it is sufficient to estimate
\|\vartheta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}
when
\eta(t)=0
.
By Lemma
2.2
and (
4.11
), a standard computation yields that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\alpha,\gamma}\vartheta^{2}+\frac{D_{\gamma}(\delta_{h}\theta)}{(\eta^{2}(t)+|h|^{2})^{\beta}}=-\frac{2\beta\eta^{\prime}(t)\eta(t)}{\eta^{2}(t)+|h|^{2}}\vartheta^{2}-\frac{2\beta\delta_{h}u\cdot h}{\eta^{2}(t)+|h|^{2}}\vartheta^{2}.

(5.4)

Next we bound below the second term in the left-hand side of (
5.4
).

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
,
0<\gamma_{0}\leq\gamma<2\alpha
and
\beta\in(2\alpha-\gamma,2\alpha)
.
Then, for
x,h\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\frac{D_{\gamma}(\delta_{h}\theta)(x)}{(\eta^{2}(t)+|h|^{2})^{\beta}}\geq\frac{1}{\mathfrak{c}_{0}|h|^{\gamma}}\Big[\frac{|\vartheta(x,t;h)|}{\|\vartheta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}}\Big]^{\frac{\gamma}{1-\beta}}|\vartheta(x,t;h)|^{2}.

Proof
. From the proof of Lemma
4.3
, we can see that, for
\rho\geq 8|h|
,

\displaystyle D_{\gamma}(\delta_{h}\theta)(x)-c_{n,\gamma}\frac{|\delta_{h}\theta(x)|^{2}}{\rho^{\gamma}}\geq-2c_{n,\gamma}|\delta_{h}\theta(x)|\Big|\int_{|y|>\frac{\rho}{4}}\delta_{y}\theta(x)\delta_{-h}\Big(\frac{\chi(\frac{y}{\rho})}{|y|^{n+\gamma}}\Big)dy\Big|

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\geq-2c_{n,\gamma}|\delta_{h}\theta(x)|\cdot C_{n,\gamma}|h|\int_{|y|>\frac{\rho}{4}}\frac{|\delta_{y}\theta(x)|}{|y|^{n+\gamma+1}}dy

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\geq-2c_{n,\gamma}|\delta_{h}\theta(x)|\cdot C_{n,\gamma}|h|\|\vartheta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}\int_{|y|>\frac{\rho}{4}}\frac{(\eta^{2}(t)+|y|^{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}}{|y|^{n+\gamma+1}}dy

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\geq-2c_{n,\gamma}|\delta_{h}\theta(x)|\cdot\underbrace{C_{n,\gamma}\frac{|h|\|\vartheta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}}{\rho^{\gamma}}\Big[\frac{\eta^{\beta}(t)}{\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho^{1-\beta}}\Big]}_{\mathcal{K}}.

(5.5)

Now choose
\rho>0
given by

\displaystyle\rho=\Big[\frac{8C_{n,\gamma}\|\vartheta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}}{|\vartheta(x,t;h)|}\Big]^{\frac{1}{1-\beta}}|h|.

Since we can assume
C_{n,\gamma}\geq 1
and
|\vartheta(x,t;h)|\leq\|\vartheta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}
,
\rho\geq 8^{\frac{1}{1-\beta}}|h|\geq 8|h|
.
By the choice of
\rho
and the inequality
a^{\frac{1}{1-\beta}}\leq a
for all
a\leq 1
,
we can bound
\mathcal{K}
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\mathcal{K}|&\leq\frac{1}{\rho^{\gamma}}\Big[\frac{C_{n,\gamma}\eta^{\beta}(t)\|\vartheta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}}{(8C_{n,\gamma})^{\frac{1}{1-\beta}}}\Big(\frac{|\vartheta(x,t;h)|}{\|\vartheta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{1-\beta}}+\frac{|\vartheta(x,t;h)||h|^{\beta}}{8}\Big]\\
&\leq\frac{\eta^{\beta}(t)+|h|^{\beta}}{8\rho^{\gamma}}|\vartheta(x,t;h)|\leq\frac{2^{1-\frac{\beta}{2}}(\eta^{2}(t)+|h|^{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}}{8\rho^{\gamma}}|\vartheta(x,t;h)|\\
&=\frac{|\delta_{h}\theta(x,t)|}{2^{2+\frac{\beta}{2}}\rho^{\gamma}}\leq\frac{|\delta_{h}\theta(x,t)|}{4\rho^{\gamma}}.\end{split}

Substituting this estimate of
\mathcal{K}
into (
5
)
and utilizing the choice of
\rho
, we arrive at

\displaystyle D_{\gamma}(\delta_{h}\theta)(x)\geq c_{n,\gamma}\frac{|\delta_{h}\theta(x)|^{2}}{2\rho^{\gamma}}=\frac{c_{n,\gamma}}{2(8C_{n,\gamma})^{\frac{\gamma}{1-\beta}}|h|^{\gamma}}\Big[\frac{|\vartheta(x,t;h)|}{\|\vartheta(x,t;\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}}\Big]^{\frac{\gamma}{1-\beta}}|\delta_{h}\theta(x)|^{2},

which along with (
5.3
) concludes the proof of Lemma
5.3
.

We proceed to derive the differential equation that
\eta
should solve to control the first term in the right-hand side of (
5.4
).

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
\gamma_{0}>0
,
\gamma\in[\gamma_{0},1)
and
\beta\in(1-\gamma,1)
.
If
\eta(t)
solves

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\eta(t)=-\frac{1}{16\mathfrak{c}_{0}\beta}\eta^{1-\gamma}(t),

(5.6)

then

\displaystyle-\frac{2\beta\eta^{\prime}\eta}{\eta^{2}+|h|^{2}}\vartheta^{2}\leq\frac{\vartheta^{2}}{8\mathfrak{c}_{0}|h|^{\gamma}}~{\rm for~all}~x,h\in\mathbb{T}^{n},

where
\mathfrak{c}_{0}
is the constant appearing in Lemma
5.3
.

Proof
.
If
\eta
solves (
5.6
), then

\displaystyle-\frac{2\beta\eta^{\prime}\eta}{\eta^{2}+|h|^{2}}\vartheta^{2}=\frac{\eta^{2-\gamma}(t)}{8\mathfrak{c}_{0}(\eta^{2}+|h|^{2})}\vartheta^{2}\leq\frac{\vartheta^{2}}{8\mathfrak{c}_{0}(\eta^{2}+|h|^{2})^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\leq\frac{\vartheta^{2}}{8\mathfrak{c}_{0}|h|^{\gamma}},

which completes the proof of Lemma
5.4
.

Following
[
18
]
, we now consider the second term in the right-hand side of (
5.4
) to derive a suitable upper bound in terms of the dissipation.
We begin with the estimate of
\delta_{h}u
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
,
0<\gamma_{0}\leq\gamma<2\alpha
and
\beta\in(2\alpha-\gamma,2\alpha)
. If
\rho\geq 4|h|
, then we have, for all
x,h\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
,

\displaystyle|\delta_{h}u(x)|\leq C\Big[\rho^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1-2\alpha}(D_{\gamma}(\delta_{h}\theta)(x))^{\frac{1}{2}}+|h|\|\vartheta\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}\Big(\frac{\eta^{\beta}(t)}{\rho^{2\alpha}}+\frac{1}{\rho^{2\alpha-\beta}}\Big)\Big].

Proof
.
By (
4.10
), we divide
\delta_{h}u(x)
into two parts

\displaystyle\delta_{h}u(x)=\underbrace{\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big[1-\chi(\frac{y}{\rho})\Big]\frac{y[\delta_{h}\theta(x-y)-\delta_{h}\theta(x)]}{|y|^{n+2\alpha}}dy}_{\mathcal{K}_{1}}+\underbrace{\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi(\frac{y}{\rho})\frac{y[\delta_{h}\theta(x-y)-\delta_{h}\theta(x)]}{|y|^{n+2\alpha}}dy}_{\mathcal{K}_{2}}.

Similar to (
4.3
), we can estimate
\mathcal{K}_{1}
as

\displaystyle|\mathcal{K}_{1}|\leq C\rho^{\frac{\gamma}{2}+1-2\alpha}(D_{\gamma}(\delta_{h}\theta)(x))^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(5.7)

By the mean value theorem, we can derive that, for
\rho\geq 8|h|
,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{K}_{2}|\leq\int_{|y|>\frac{\rho}{4}}\Big|\delta_{-h}\Big(\chi\Big(\frac{y}{\rho}\Big)\frac{y}{|y|^{n+2\alpha}}\Big)\Big||\delta_{-y}\theta(x)|dy

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{n,\alpha}\int_{|y|>\frac{\rho}{4}}\frac{|h|}{|y|^{n+2\alpha}}|\delta_{-y}\theta(x)|dy

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{n,\alpha}|h|\|\vartheta\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}\int_{|y|>\frac{\rho}{4}}\frac{(\eta^{2}(t)+|y|^{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}}{|y|^{n+2\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\qquad=C_{n,\alpha}|h|\|\vartheta\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}\Big[\frac{\eta^{\beta}(t)}{\rho^{2\alpha}}+\frac{1}{\rho^{2\alpha-\beta}}\Big],

which along with (
5.7
) concludes the proof of Lemma
5.5
.

In view of Lemma
5.5
, we are able to compare the nonlinear term in (
5.4
) with the lower bound on the dissipation term given in Lemma
5.3
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
,
0<\gamma_{0}\leq\gamma<2\alpha
and
\beta\in(2\alpha-\gamma,2\alpha)
. Suppose that

\displaystyle\|\vartheta\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}\leq\frac{4\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\eta^{\beta}_{0}}.

There exists a constant
\mathfrak{c}=\mathfrak{c}(\gamma_{0})\geq 1
such that if we choose
\eta_{0}=(\mathfrak{c}\beta\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}})^{\frac{1}{2\alpha-\gamma}}
,
then, for every
x,h\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
with
|h|\leq\eta_{0}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{2\beta|h||\delta_{h}u|}{\eta^{2}+|h|^{2}}\vartheta^{2}\leq\frac{D_{\gamma}(\delta_{h}\theta)(x)}{2(\eta^{2}+|h|^{2})^{\beta}}+\frac{\vartheta^{2}}{8\mathfrak{c}_{0}|h|^{\gamma}}

where
\mathfrak{c}_{0}
is the constant appearing in Lemma
5.3
.

Proof
.
For
|h|\leq\eta_{0}
, letting
\rho=4(\eta^{2}+|h|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq 4|h|

in Lemma
5.5
and utilizing Young’s inequality lead us to

\displaystyle\frac{2\beta|h||\delta_{h}u|}{\eta^{2}+|h|^{2}}\vartheta^{2}\leq\frac{D_{\gamma}(\delta_{h}\theta)(x)}{2(\eta^{2}+|h|^{2})^{\beta}}+\frac{C\beta|h|^{2}}{\eta^{2}+|h|^{2}}\Big[\frac{\beta\rho^{\gamma+2-4\alpha}\vartheta^{2}}{(\eta^{2}+|h|^{2})^{1-\beta}}+\|\vartheta\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}\Big(\frac{\eta^{\beta}}{\rho^{2\alpha}}+\frac{1}{\rho^{2\alpha-\beta}}\Big)\Big]\vartheta^{2},

which along with the assumption of
\|\vartheta\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}
, the monotonicity of
\eta
and
2\alpha-\gamma<\beta
implies

\displaystyle\frac{2\beta|h||\delta_{h}u|}{\eta^{2}+|h|^{2}}\vartheta^{2}-\frac{D_{\gamma}(\delta_{h}\theta)(x)}{2(\eta^{2}+|h|^{2})^{\beta}}\leq C\beta\Big[\frac{\beta\rho^{\gamma+2-4\alpha}\|\vartheta\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}}{(\eta^{2}+|h|^{2})^{1-\beta}}+\frac{\|\vartheta\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}}{\rho^{2\alpha-\beta}}\Big]\vartheta^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq C\beta\|\vartheta\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big[\beta\|\vartheta\|_{L^{\infty}}\frac{(\eta^{2}+|h|^{2})^{\gamma+\beta-2\alpha}}{(\eta^{2}+|h|^{2})^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}}+\frac{(\eta^{2}+|h|^{2})^{\frac{\beta+\gamma}{2}-\alpha}}{(\eta^{2}+|h|^{2})^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\Big]\vartheta^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq C\frac{4\beta\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\eta^{\beta}_{0}}\Big[\frac{4\beta\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}(2\eta^{2}_{0})^{\gamma+\beta-2\alpha}}{\eta^{\beta}_{0}|h|^{\gamma}}+\frac{(2\eta^{2}_{0})^{\frac{\gamma+\beta}{2}-\alpha}}{|h|^{\gamma}}\Big]\vartheta^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq C_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\Big[\frac{\beta^{2}\|\theta_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}}{\eta^{2(2\alpha-\gamma)}_{0}}+\frac{\beta\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\eta^{2\alpha-\gamma}_{0}}\Big]\frac{\vartheta^{2}}{|h|^{\gamma}}\leq\frac{\vartheta^{2}}{8\mathfrak{c}_{0}|h|^{\gamma}},

provided we choose
\eta_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\eta_{0}=(16\bar{C}\mathfrak{c}_{0}\beta\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}})^{\frac{1}{2\alpha-\gamma}},

for some constant
\bar{C}=\max\{C,\frac{1}{\mathfrak{c}_{0}}\}
. We then complete the proof of Lemma
5.6
.

With the help of Lemmas
5.3
,
5.4
and
5.6
, we are ready to conclude the proof of Proposition
5.2
.

Proof of Proposition
5.2
.
Given
\eta_{0}
as in Lemma
5.6
and define

\displaystyle t_{\star}=\sup\{t\in[0,T):\|\vartheta(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}<M,{\rm~for~all}~\tau\in[0,t]\},

where
M=:\frac{4\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\eta^{\beta}_{0}}
.
By (
5.3
), we can see that

\displaystyle\|\vartheta(0)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}\leq\frac{2\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\eta^{\beta}_{0}}=\frac{M}{2},

which follows from the continuity of
\|\vartheta\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}
in time that
t_{\star}>0
is well-defined.

We claim that
t_{\star}=T
. Assume, contrary to our claim, that
t_{\star}<T
.
By the continuity of
\vartheta
in time and the definition of
t_{\star}
,
we have
\|\vartheta(t_{\star})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}=M
. By Lemmas
5.3
,
5.4
and
5.6
, (
5.4
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{L_{\alpha,\gamma}}\vartheta^{2}+\frac{1}{4\mathfrak{c}_{0}|h|^{\gamma}}\Big[\Big[\frac{\vartheta(x,t;h)}{\|\vartheta\|_{L^{\infty}}}\Big]^{\frac{\gamma}{1-\beta}}-1\Big]\vartheta^{2}+\frac{1}{4\mathfrak{c}_{0}|h|^{\gamma}}\Big[\frac{\vartheta(x,t;h)}{\|\vartheta\|_{L^{\infty}}}\Big]^{\frac{\gamma}{1-\beta}}\vartheta^{2}\leq 0,

(5.8)

for all
x,h\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
with
|h|\leq\eta_{0}
and
t\in(0,t_{\star}]
.
Since
\|\vartheta(t_{\star})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}=M
, we can choose a sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\vartheta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}\geq\frac{2M}{3}~{\rm~for~all}~t\in[t_{\star}-\varepsilon,t_{\star}).

(5.9)

For
t\in[t_{\star}-\varepsilon,t_{\star})
, the function
(x,h)\mapsto\vartheta^{2}(x,t;h)
has a maximum value which is reached at some point
(x_{t},h_{t})\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\vartheta^{2}(x_{t},t,h_{t})=\nabla_{h}\vartheta^{2}(x_{t},t,h_{t})=0,~\Lambda^{\gamma}\vartheta^{2}(x_{t},t,h_{t})\geq 0,

which follows from the definition of
\mathcal{L_{\alpha,\gamma}}
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L_{\alpha,\gamma}}\vartheta^{2}(x_{t},t,h_{t})\geq(\partial_{t}\vartheta^{2})(x_{t},t,h_{t}).

(5.10)

We also have
|h_{t}|\leq\eta_{0}
.
Indeed, for every
|h|>\eta_{0}
, it holds that, for any
(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\times[0,T)
,

\displaystyle|\vartheta(x,t;h)|\leq\frac{2\|\theta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}}{|h|^{\beta}}\leq\frac{2\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\eta_{0}^{\beta}}=\frac{M}{2},

which along with (
5.9
) implies
|h_{t}|\leq\eta_{0}
. Reading (
5.8
) at
(x_{t},t,h_{t})
and using
\frac{|\vartheta(x_{t},t,h_{t})|}{\|\vartheta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}}=1
lead us to

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\vartheta^{2}(x_{t},t,h_{t})+\frac{\vartheta^{2}(x_{t},t,h_{t})}{4\mathfrak{c}_{0}|h_{t}|^{\gamma}}\leq 0,

which together with (
5.10
) gives

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}\vartheta^{2})(x_{t},t,h_{t})\leq\mathcal{L}\vartheta^{2}(x_{t},t,h_{t})+\frac{\vartheta^{2}(x_{t},t,h_{t})}{4\mathfrak{c}_{0}|h_{t}|^{\gamma}}\leq 0.

Following an same argument as the Appendix B in
[
10
]
, we can show that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\vartheta(t)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}\leq(\partial_{t}\vartheta^{2})(x_{t},t,h_{t})\leq 0,{\rm~for~all}~t\in[t_{\star}-\varepsilon,t_{\star}).

Then, for all
t\in[t_{\star}-\varepsilon,t_{\star})
,
\|\vartheta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}\leq\|\vartheta(t_{\star}-\varepsilon)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}.

Letting
t\rightarrow t_{\star}
and utilizing
\|\vartheta(t_{\star}-\varepsilon)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}<M
thus implies that

\|\vartheta(t_{\star})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}<M
,
which is a contradiction with the definition of
t_{\star}
. Then
t_{\star}=T
and
\vartheta\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times(0,T))
.
Note that the solution of (
5.6
) is

\displaystyle\eta(t)=\begin{cases}(\eta^{\gamma}_{0}-\frac{\gamma t}{16\mathfrak{c}_{0}\beta})^{\frac{1}{\gamma}},&\mbox{if $0\leq t\leq T^{\ast}_{\alpha},$ }\\
0,&\mbox{if $T^{\ast}_{\alpha}<t<T,$ }\\
\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle T^{\ast}_{\alpha}=\frac{16\mathfrak{c}_{0}\beta\eta^{\gamma}_{0}}{\gamma}=\frac{16\mathfrak{c}_{0}\mathfrak{c}^{\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha-\gamma}}}{\gamma}\beta^{\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha-\gamma}}\|\theta_{0}\|^{\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha-\gamma}}_{L^{\infty}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle[\theta(t)]_{C^{\beta}}=\|\vartheta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,h}}\leq M=\frac{4\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\eta^{\beta}_{0}},{\rm~for~all}~t\in(T^{\ast}_{\alpha},T),

which finishes the proof of Proposition
5.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.
Given nonnegative
\theta_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
with
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
.
In view of Proposition
3.1
, there exists a unique local solution to (
1.1
)
in the class (
3.1
).
By Corollary
3.4
, the local existence
T
satisfies

\displaystyle T_{1}:=\frac{1}{C_{1}\|\theta_{0}\|^{1-\frac{n+4\alpha}{2s}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\|\Lambda^{s}\theta_{0}\|^{\frac{n+4\alpha}{2s}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}}\leq T,

(5.11)

where
C_{1}
is a positive constant independent of
\gamma
,
\beta
and
\theta_{0}
.

Next we will choose
\gamma
sufficiently close to
2\alpha
such that
T^{\ast}_{\alpha}\leq T_{1}
, where
T^{\ast}_{\alpha}
is as in (
5.2
). By Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality,
T^{\ast}_{\alpha}
can be bounded as

\displaystyle\begin{split}T^{\ast}_{\alpha}&\leq C\beta^{\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha-\gamma}}\Big[C_{2}\|\theta_{0}\|^{\frac{n}{2s}}_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\|\theta_{0}\|^{1-\frac{n}{2s}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\Big]^{\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha-\gamma}}\\
&\leq C_{3}\beta^{\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha-\gamma}}\|\theta_{0}\|^{\frac{n\gamma}{2s(2\alpha-\gamma)}}_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\|\theta_{0}\|^{\frac{\gamma(2s-n)}{2s(2\alpha-\gamma)}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})},\end{split}

(5.12)

where
C_{2}>0
is independent of
\gamma
,
\beta
and
\theta_{0}
,
C_{3}=CC^{\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha-\gamma}}_{2}
and
C
is a constant that can be bounded from below and above for all
\gamma\in[\gamma_{0},1)
.
By (
5.11
), (
5.12
) and (
1.5
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{T^{\ast}_{\alpha}}{T_{1}}\leq C_{1}C_{3}\beta^{\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha-\gamma}}\Big[\|\theta_{0}\|^{\frac{n+4\alpha-2\gamma}{2s}}_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\|\theta_{0}\|^{1-\frac{n+4\alpha-2\gamma}{2s}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\Big]^{\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha-\gamma}}\leq C_{1}C_{3}\beta^{\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha-\gamma}}R^{\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha-\gamma}}.

In order to obtain
T^{\ast}_{\alpha}\leq T_{1}
, we need to show
C_{1}C_{3}\beta^{\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha-\gamma}}R^{\frac{2\alpha}{2\alpha-\gamma}}\leq 1
,
which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\beta\leq R^{-1}\Big(\frac{1}{C_{1}C_{3}}\Big)^{\frac{2\alpha-\gamma}{2\alpha}}.

(5.13)

To do this, we choose
\beta=\min\{1-2\gamma+2\alpha,\alpha\}
. By (
5.13
) it follows that there exists
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{1}(R)\in[\gamma_{0},2\alpha)

such that
T^{\ast}_{\alpha}\leq T_{1}
for all
\gamma\in[\gamma_{1},2\alpha)
.
To finish, let
T_{max}
be the maximal existence time for the solution to (
1.1
) in the class. For the moment, suppose that
T_{max}<\infty
, then

\theta\in C([0,T_{max});H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
with
T^{\ast}_{\alpha}<T_{1}\leq T_{max}
.
By Proposition
5.2
,
\theta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times(T^{\ast}_{\alpha},T_{max}])
and
\theta(T_{max})\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Thus, by Proposition
3.1
, we can extend
\theta
in the class
(
3.1
) beyond
T_{max}
. This leads to a contradiction with the maximality of
T_{max}
. Consequently,
T_{max}=\infty
and
\theta
is a global
H^{s}
-solution.

6.
Finite-time blow-up for the supercritical case
\gamma<\alpha

In this section, we will prove the finite-time blow-up of radial smooth solutions to (
1.1
) with
\gamma\in(0,\alpha)
.
We start by recalling a weighted inequality involving the nonlinear term in (
1.1
), whose proof can be found in
[
41
]
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a radially symmetric Schwartz function. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\Lambda^{-2+2\alpha}\nabla f(x)\cdot\nabla f(x)}{|x|^{n}}e^{-|x|}dx\geq C^{\prime}_{n,\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(f(0)-f(x))^{2}}{|x|^{n+2\alpha}}dx-C^{\prime\prime}_{n,\alpha}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}},

where
C^{\prime}_{n,\alpha}
and
C^{\prime\prime}_{n,\alpha}
are constants depending only on
n
and
\gamma
.

In order to handle the dissipative term, we establish a type of weighted inequality below, whose one-dimensional version was proved by Li and Rodrigo in
[
36
]
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
.
Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a Schwartz function.
Then

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\Lambda^{\gamma}f(0)-\Lambda^{\gamma}f(x)}{|x|^{n}}e^{-|x|}dx\Big|\leq C_{n,\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|f(0)-f(x)|}{|x|^{n+\gamma}}\ln\Big(e+\frac{1}{|x|}\Big)dx,

where
C_{n,\gamma}
is a constant depending only on
n
and
\gamma
.

Proof.

By (
2.1
), a change of variables and the use of parity, we rewrite
\Lambda^{\gamma}f(x)
as

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\gamma}f(x)=\frac{1}{2}C_{n,\gamma}P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{2f(x)-f(x+y)-f(x-y)}{|y|^{n+\gamma}}dy,

which along with

\displaystyle(\Lambda^{\gamma}f)(0)=C_{n,\gamma}P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(0)-f(y)}{|y|^{n+\gamma}}dy,

implies that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{2}{C_{n,\gamma}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\Lambda^{\gamma}f(0)-\Lambda^{\gamma}f(x)}{|x|^{n}}e^{-|x|}dx\\
&=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{2(f(0)-f(x))+f(x+y)+f(x-y)-2f(y)}{|x|^{n}|y|^{n+\gamma}}e^{-|x|}dxdy\\
&=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\underbrace{\frac{-2g(x)+g(x+y)+g(x-y)-2g(y)}{|x|^{n}|y|^{n+\gamma}}e^{-|x|}}_{H(x,y)}dxdy\\
&=\Big(\underbrace{\iint_{\frac{|x|}{e}\leq|y|\leq e|x|}}_{X_{1}}+\underbrace{\iint_{|y|\geq e|x|}}_{X_{2}}+\underbrace{\iint_{|y|\leq\frac{|x|}{e}}}_{X_{3}}\Big)H(x,y)dxdy,\end{split}

where
g(x)=f(x)-f(0)
.

We estimate
X_{1}
,
X_{2}
and
X_{3}
separately. First, the term
X_{1}
can be estimated as

\displaystyle\begin{split}|X_{1}|&\leq\iint\limits_{\frac{|x|}{e}\leq|y|\leq e|x|}\frac{2|g(x)|}{|x|^{n}|y|^{n+\gamma}}dxdy+e^{n+\gamma}\iint\limits_{\frac{|x|}{e}\leq|y|\leq e|x|}\frac{|g(x+y)|}{|x|^{2n+\gamma}}dxdy\\
&\ +e^{n+\gamma}\iint\limits_{\frac{|x|}{e}\leq|y|\leq e|x|}\frac{|g(x-y)|}{|x|^{2n+\gamma}}dxdy+\iint\limits_{\frac{|x|}{e}\leq|y|\leq e|x|}\frac{2|g(y)|}{|x|^{n}|y|^{n+\gamma}}dxdy\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{2|g(x)|}{|x|^{n}}\Big[\int_{\frac{|x|}{e}\leq|y|\leq e|x|}\frac{dy}{|y|^{n+\gamma}}\Big]dx+2e^{n+\gamma}\iint\limits_{\frac{|x|}{e}\leq|x-z|\leq e|x|}\frac{|g(z)|}{|x|^{2n+\gamma}}dxdz\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{2|g(y)|}{|y|^{n+\gamma}}\Big[\int_{\frac{|y|}{e}\leq|x|\leq e|y|}\frac{dx}{|x|^{n}}\Big]dy\\
&\leq C^{\prime}_{n,\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|g(x)|}{|x|^{n+\gamma}}dx+2e^{n+\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|g(z)|\Big[\int\limits_{|x|\geq\frac{|z|}{e+1}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{2n+\gamma}}\Big]dz\\
&\leq C^{\prime\prime}_{n,\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|g(x)|}{|x|^{n+\gamma}}dx,\end{split}

(6.1)

where in above identity we used a change of variables.
Secondly, by a change of variables, we rewrite
X_{2}
as

\displaystyle X_{2}=\underbrace{\iint\limits_{|y|\geq e|x|}\frac{-2g(x)e^{-|x|}}{|x|^{n}|y|^{n+\gamma}}dxdy}_{X_{21}}+\underbrace{\iint\limits_{|y-x|\geq e|x|}\frac{2g(y)e^{-|x|}dxdy}{|x|^{n}|y-x|^{n+\gamma}}-\iint\limits_{|y|\geq e|x|}\frac{2g(y)e^{-|x|}dxdy}{|x|^{n}|y|^{n+\gamma}}}_{X_{22}}.

The estimate of
X_{21}
is straightforward:

\displaystyle|X_{21}|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{2|g(x)|}{|x|^{n}}\Big[\int\limits_{|y|\geq e|x|}\frac{dy}{|y|^{n+\gamma}}\Big]dx=C^{\prime}_{n,\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|g(x)|}{|x|^{n+\gamma}}dx.

By an elementary formula

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{A}}h_{1}dz-\int_{\mathbf{B}}h_{2}dz=\int_{\mathbf{A}\cap\mathbf{B}}(h_{1}-h_{2})dz+\int_{\mathbf{A}\cap\mathbf{B}^{c}}h_{1}dz-\int_{\mathbf{B}\cap\mathbf{A}^{c}}h_{2}dz

(6.2)

we express the term
X_{22}
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}X_{22}&=\underbrace{\iint_{\mathbf{A_{1}}\cap\mathbf{B_{1}}}\frac{2g(y)e^{-|x|}}{|x|^{n}}\Big[\frac{1}{|y-x|^{n+\gamma}}-\frac{1}{|y|^{n+\gamma}}\Big]dxdy}_{X_{221}}\\
&\ \ \ +\underbrace{\iint_{\mathbf{A}_{1}\cap\mathbf{B}^{c}_{1}}\frac{2g(y)e^{-|x|}dxdy}{|x|^{n}|y-x|^{n+\gamma}}}_{X_{222}}-\underbrace{\iint_{\mathbf{B}_{1}\cap\mathbf{A}^{c}_{1}}\frac{2g(y)e^{-|x|}dxdy}{|x|^{n}|y|^{n+\gamma}}}_{X_{223}}.\end{split}

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{1}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}:|y-x|\geq e|x|\},~\mathbf{B}_{1}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}:|y|\geq e|x|\}.

By the mean-value theorem, we estimate the term
X_{221}
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}|X_{221}|&\leq\iint_{\mathbf{A}_{1}\cap\mathbf{B}_{1}}\frac{2|g(y)|}{|x|^{n}}\Big|\frac{1}{|y-x|^{n+\gamma}}-\frac{1}{|y|^{n+\gamma}}\Big|dxdy\\
&\leq(n+\gamma)\Big(\frac{e}{e-1}\Big)^{n+\gamma+1}\iint\limits_{|y|\geq e|x|}\frac{|g(y)|dxdy}{|x|^{n-1}|y|^{n+\gamma+1}}\\
&=C_{n,\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|g(y)|}{|y|^{n+\gamma+1}}\Big[\int_{|x|\leq\frac{|y|}{e}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{n-1}}\Big]dy\\
&\leq C^{\prime}_{n,\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|g(y)|}{|y|^{n+\gamma}}dy.\end{split}

The estimates of
X_{222}
and
X_{223}
are standard:

\displaystyle\begin{split}|X_{222}|+|X_{223}|&\leq\iint\limits_{(e-1)|x|\leq|y|\leq e|x|}\frac{2|g(y)|dxdy}{|x|^{n}|y|^{n+\gamma}}+\iint\limits_{e|x|\leq|y|\leq(e+1)|x|}\frac{2|g(y)|dxdy}{|x|^{n}|y|^{n+\gamma}}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{2|g(y)|}{|y|^{n+\gamma}}\Big[\int_{\frac{|y|}{e+1}\leq|x|\leq\frac{|y|}{e-1}}\frac{dx}{|x|^{n}}\Big]dy\leq C_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|g(y)|}{|y|^{n+\gamma}}dy.\end{split}

Thus, we obtain that

\displaystyle|X_{2}|\leq C^{\prime}_{n,\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|g(x)|}{|x|^{n+\gamma}}dx.

(6.3)

Similarly, by a change of variables,
X_{3}
can be bounded by

\displaystyle\begin{split}|X_{3}|&\leq\underbrace{\Big|\iint\limits_{|y|\leq\frac{|x-y|}{e}}\frac{g(x)e^{-|x-y|}}{|x-y|^{n}|y|^{n+\gamma}}dxdy-\iint\limits_{|y|\leq\frac{|x|}{e}}\frac{g(x)e^{-|x|}}{|x|^{n}|y|^{n+\gamma}}dxdy\Big|}_{X_{31}}\\
&\ +\underbrace{\Big|\iint\limits_{|y|\leq\frac{|x+y|}{e}}\frac{g(x)e^{-|x+y|}dxdy}{|x+y|^{n}|y|^{n+\gamma}}-\iint\limits_{|y|\leq\frac{|x|}{e}}\frac{g(x)e^{-|x|}dxdy}{|x|^{n}|y|^{n+\gamma}}\Big|}_{X_{32}}+\underbrace{\iint\limits_{|y|\leq\frac{|x|}{e}}\frac{2|g(y)|dxdy}{|x|^{n}|y|^{n+\gamma}e^{|x|}}}_{X_{33}}.\end{split}

By the identity (
6.2
) and the mean value theorem, we can derive that

\displaystyle\begin{split}X_{31}&\leq\iint_{\mathbf{A_{2}}\cap\mathbf{B_{2}}}\frac{|g(x)|}{|y|^{n+\gamma}}\Big|\frac{e^{-|x-y|}}{|x-y|^{n}}-\frac{e^{-|x|}}{|x|^{n}}\Big|dxdy\\
&\ \ \ +\iint_{\mathbf{A_{2}}\cap\mathbf{B}^{c}_{2}}\frac{|g(x)|e^{-|x-y|}}{|x-y|^{n}|y|^{n+\gamma}}dxdy+\iint_{\mathbf{A^{c}_{2}}\cap\mathbf{B}_{2}}\frac{|g(x)|e^{-|x|}}{|x|^{n}|y|^{n+\gamma}}dxdy\\
&\leq C_{n}\iint_{\mathbf{A_{2}}\cap\mathbf{B_{2}}}\frac{|g(x)|}{|y|^{n-1+\gamma}}e^{-(1-\frac{1}{e})|x|}\Big[\frac{1}{|x|^{n}}+\frac{1}{|x|^{n+1}}\Big]dxdy\\
&\ \ \ +\iint\limits_{\mathbf{A_{2}}\cap\mathbf{B}^{c}_{2}}\frac{|g(x)|}{|x|^{n}|y|^{n+\gamma}}dxdy+\iint\limits_{\mathbf{A^{c}_{2}}\cap\mathbf{B}_{2}}\frac{|g(x)|}{|x|^{n}|y|^{n+\gamma}}dxdy\\
&\leq C_{n}\iint_{\mathbf{A_{2}}\cap\mathbf{B_{2}}}\frac{|g(x)|dxdy}{|x|^{n+1}|y|^{n-1+\gamma}}+2\iint_{|y|\geq\frac{e-1}{e^{2}}|x|}\frac{|g(x)|}{|x|^{n}|y|^{n+\gamma}}dxdy\\
&\leq C_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|g(x)|}{|x|^{n+1}}\int_{|y|\leq\frac{|x|}{e}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{n-1+\gamma}}dx+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|g(x)|}{|x|^{n}}\int_{|y|\geq\frac{e-1}{e^{2}}|x|}\frac{dy}{|y|^{n+\gamma}}dx\\
&\leq C_{n,\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|g(x)|}{|x|^{n+\gamma}}dx,\end{split}

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{2}=\Big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}:|y|\leq\frac{|x-y|}{e}\Big\},~\mathbf{B}_{2}=\Big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}:|y|\leq\frac{|x|}{e}\Big\}.

Completely similar to the estimate of
X_{31}
,
X_{32}
can be bounded by

\displaystyle X_{32}\leq C_{n,\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|g(x)|}{|x|^{n+\gamma}}dx.

Finally, by direct computation and Lemma
2.4
, we can obtain that

\displaystyle X_{33}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|g(y)|}{|y|^{n+\gamma}}\Big[\int^{\infty}_{|y|}\frac{e^{-r}}{r}dr\Big]dy\leq C_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|g(y)|}{|y|^{n+\gamma}}\ln\Big(e+\frac{1}{|y|}\Big)dy.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle|X_{3}|\leq C_{n,\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|g(y)|}{|y|^{n+\gamma}}\ln\Big(e+\frac{1}{|y|}\Big)dy,

which along with (
6.1
) and (
6.3
) then complete the proof of Lemma
6.2
.

With the help of Lemmas
6.1
and
6.2
, we are ready to prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.
Taking into account the quantity

\displaystyle J(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\theta(0,t)-\theta(x,t)}{|x|^{n}}e^{-|x|}dx

and computing
J^{\prime}(t)
from
\eqref{1.1}_{1}
, we arrive at the differential identity

\displaystyle J^{\prime}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\Lambda^{-2+2\alpha}\nabla\theta\cdot\nabla\theta}{|x|^{n}}e^{-|x|}dx-\kappa\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\Lambda^{\gamma}\theta(0,t)-\Lambda^{\gamma}\theta(x,t)}{|x|^{n}}e^{-|x|}dx,

where we used the fact that the velocity vanishes at the origin, that is, by Lemma
2.6
,

\displaystyle u(0,t)=C_{n,\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\nabla\theta(y,t)}{|y|^{n-2+2\alpha}}dy=C_{n,\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{y\theta^{\prime}(|y|,t)}{|y|^{n-1+2\alpha}}dy\equiv 0.

It follows from Lemmas
6.1
,
2.5
and
6.2
that

\displaystyle\begin{split}J^{\prime}(t)&\geq C_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(\theta(0,t)-\theta(x,t))^{2}}{|x|^{n+2\alpha}}dx-C_{2}\|\theta_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\ \ \ -\kappa C_{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\theta(0,t)-\theta(x,t)}{|x|^{n+\gamma}}\ln\Big(e+\frac{1}{|x|}\Big)dx.\end{split}

(6.4)

By Hölder’s inequality, we derive that

\displaystyle\begin{split}|J(t)|&\leq\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\theta(0,t)-\theta(x,t)|^{2}}{|x|^{n+2\alpha}}dx\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{e^{-2|x|}}{|x|^{n-2\alpha}}dx\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq C_{4}(n,\alpha)\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\theta(0,t)-\theta(x,t)|^{2}}{|x|^{n+2\alpha}}dx\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\theta(0,t)-\theta(x,t)|^{2}}{|x|^{n+2\alpha}}dx\geq\frac{(J(t))^{2}}{C^{2}_{4}}.

(6.5)

Furthermore, by Hölder’s inequality, Lemma
2.5
and the restriction
\gamma\in(0,\alpha)
, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\theta(0,t)-\theta(x,t)}{|x|^{n+\gamma}}\ln\Big(e+\frac{1}{|x|}\Big)dx\Big|

(6.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big[\int_{|x|\leq 1}\frac{e^{|x|}\ln^{2}(e+\frac{1}{|x|})}{|x|^{n+2(\gamma-\alpha)}}dx\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}(Q(t))^{\frac{1}{2}}+2\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{|x|>1}\frac{\ln(e+\frac{1}{|x|})}{|x|^{n+\gamma}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{5}(n,\alpha,\gamma)(Q(t))^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{6}(n,\gamma)\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},

where

\displaystyle Q(t)=\int_{|x|\leq 1}\frac{|\theta(0,t)-\theta(x,t)|^{2}}{|x|^{n+2\alpha}}e^{-|x|}dx.

Substituting (
6.6
) and (
6.5
) into (
6.4
) and utilizing the inequality
a\tau-b\sqrt{\tau}\geq-\frac{b^{2}}{4a^{2}}
for
a,b>0
,
\tau\geq 0
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\begin{split}J^{\prime}(t)&\geq\frac{C_{1}}{2C^{2}_{4}}(J(t))^{2}-C_{2}\|\theta_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}-\kappa C_{3}C_{6}\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\frac{C_{1}}{2}Q(t)-\kappa C_{3}C_{5}(Q(t))^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\geq\frac{C_{1}}{2C^{2}_{4}}(J(t))^{2}-C_{2}\|\theta_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}-\kappa C_{3}C_{6}\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}-\frac{\kappa^{2}C^{2}_{3}C^{2}_{5}}{C^{2}_{1}}.\end{split}

which implies that

\displaystyle J^{\prime}(t)\geq C_{7}(n,\alpha)(J(t))^{2}-C_{8}(\kappa,n,\alpha,\gamma)(1+\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}})^{2}.

(6.7)

Now, choosing
A>\sqrt{\frac{C_{8}}{C_{7}}}
and considering
J(0)\geq A(1+\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}})
, (
6.7
) implies
J^{\prime}(0)>0
and that
J
blows up in finite time. Since

\displaystyle\begin{split}|J(t)|&\leq\|\nabla\theta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{|x|\leq 1}\frac{dx}{|x|^{n-1}}+2\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{|x|>1}\frac{e^{-|x|}}{|x|^{n}}dx\\
&\leq C(n)(\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}),\end{split}

we conclude that
\|\nabla\theta(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}
must blow up in finite time. The proof of Theorem
1.3
is finished.

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma
3.3

In this appendix, we give the proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.
By the integral representation of the Riesz potential
[
39
]
, we have

\displaystyle u(x,t)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}-1+\alpha)}{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}2^{2-2\alpha}\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\nabla\theta(y)}{|x-y|^{n-2+2\alpha}}dy.

(A.1)

Following
[
4
]
, we introduce a cut-off function
\zeta_{\rho}(x)
satisfying

\displaystyle\zeta_{\rho}(x)=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{{\rm for} $|x|<\rho,$ }\\
0,&\mbox{{\rm for} $|x|>2\rho,$ }\\
\end{cases}~{\rm and}~\nabla\zeta_{\rho}(x)\leq C/\rho.

Here
\rho\leq 1
is a radius to be chosen suitably small later.
By introducing a factor
\zeta_{\rho}(x-y)+[1-\zeta_{\rho}(x-y)]
under the integral sign in (
A.1
), we can compute
\nabla u(x)
as

\displaystyle\nabla u(x)=\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\zeta_{\rho}(x-y)}{|x-y|^{n-2+2\alpha}}\nabla^{2}\theta(y)dy}_{\nabla u^{(1)}(x)}+\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla_{x}\Big[\frac{1-\zeta_{\rho}(x-y)}{|x-y|^{n-2+2\alpha}}\Big]\nabla\theta(y)dy}_{\nabla u^{(2)}(x)}.

(A.2)

For
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
s\neq\frac{n}{2}+2
, by Hölder’s inequality and the Sobolev embedding, we can estimate
\nabla u^{(1)}(x)
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\nabla u^{(1)}(x)|&\leq\begin{cases}\|\nabla^{2}\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}\int\limits_{|z|<2\rho}\frac{dz}{|z|^{n-2+2\alpha}},&\mbox{for $s>\frac{n}{2}+2,$ }\\
\Big[\int\limits_{|z|<2\rho}\frac{dz}{|z|^{\frac{2n(n-2+2\alpha)}{n+2(s-2)}}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{s-2}{n}}\|\nabla^{2}\theta\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2(s-2)}}},&\mbox{for $s\in(\frac{n}{2}+1,\frac{n}{2}+2),$ }\\
\end{cases}\\
&\leq\begin{cases}C_{n,\alpha}\rho^{2-2\alpha}\|\theta\|_{H^{s}},&\mbox{for $s>\frac{n}{2}+2,$ }\\
C_{n,\alpha,s}\rho^{s-\frac{n}{2}-2\alpha}\|\theta\|_{H^{s}},&\mbox{for $s\in(\frac{n}{2}+1,\frac{n}{2}+2).$ }\\
\end{cases}\end{split}

(A.3)

For the remaining case
s=\frac{n}{2}+2
and
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
, by Hölder’s inequality and the embedding
H^{\frac{n}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for any
q\in[2,\infty)
, we can derive that

\displaystyle|\nabla u^{(1)}(x)|\lesssim\Big[\int_{|z|<2\rho}\frac{dz}{|z|^{\frac{(n+1)(n-1)}{n}}}\Big]^{\frac{n}{n+1}}\|\nabla^{2}\theta\|_{L^{n+1}}\leq C_{n}\rho^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\|\theta\|_{H^{\frac{n}{2}+2}}.

(A.4)

Similarly, for
s=\frac{n}{2}+2
and
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, the term
\nabla u^{(1)}(x)
can be estimated as

\displaystyle|\nabla u^{(1)}(x)|\leq\Big[\int_{|z|<2\rho}\frac{dz}{|z|^{\frac{n(n-2+2\alpha)}{n-1}}}\Big]^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\|\nabla^{2}\theta\|_{L^{n}}\leq C_{n,\alpha}\rho^{1-2\alpha}\|\theta\|_{H^{\frac{n}{2}+2}}.

(A.5)

Letting
\varepsilon>0
depending only on
s,n,\alpha
given by, for
n\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\varepsilon=\begin{cases}2-2\alpha,&\mbox{for $s>\frac{n}{2}+2$ and $\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]$, }\\
s-\frac{n}{2}-2\alpha,&\mbox{for $s\in(\frac{n}{2}+1,\frac{n}{2}+2)$ and $\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]$, }\\
\frac{1}{n+1},&\mbox{for $s=\frac{n}{2}+2$ and $\alpha=\frac{1}{2}$,}\\
1-2\alpha,&\mbox{for $s=\frac{n}{2}+2$ and $\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})$, }\\
\end{cases}

and gathering up (
A.3
)-(
A.5
) yield that, for
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
with
n\geq 2
,

\displaystyle|\nabla u^{(1)}(x)|\leq C\rho^{\varepsilon}\|\theta\|_{H^{s}}.

(A.6)

We proceed to split
\nabla u^{(2)}(x)
into two parts as

\displaystyle\nabla u^{(2)}(x)=\Big[\underbrace{\int_{\rho\leq|x-y|\leq 1}}_{\nabla u^{(21)}(x)}+\underbrace{\int_{|x-y|>1}}_{\nabla u^{(22)}(x)}\Big]\nabla_{x}\Big[\frac{1-\zeta_{\rho}(x-y)}{|x-y|^{n-2+2\alpha}}\Big]\nabla\theta(y)dy.

(A.7)

For the term
\nabla u^{(21)}(x)
, it can be bounded as, for
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
\rho\in(0,1]
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\nabla u^{(21)}(x)|&\leq C\Big[\int^{1}_{\rho}\frac{r^{n-1}dr}{r^{n-1+2\alpha}}+\rho^{-1}\int^{2\rho}_{\rho}\frac{r^{n-1}dr}{r^{n-2+2\alpha}}\Big]\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\leq\begin{cases}C(-\log\rho+1)\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}},&\mbox{for $\alpha=\frac{1}{2},$ }\\
C\Big[\frac{1}{1-2\alpha}+\frac{2^{2-2\alpha}-1}{2-2\alpha}\Big]\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}},&\mbox{for $\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}).$ }\\
\end{cases}\end{split}

(A.8)

When
\rho\leq\frac{1}{2}
, by the choice of
\zeta
and Hölder inequality, we have, for
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
,

\displaystyle|\nabla u^{(22)}(x)|\lesssim\Big[\int_{|z|>1}\frac{dz}{|z|^{2(n-1+2\alpha)}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{n,\alpha}\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{2}}.

(A.9)

For
\rho\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
, the term
\nabla u^{(22)}(x)
can be estimated as

\displaystyle|\nabla u^{(22)}(x)|=\Big|\Big[\int_{1<|x-y|\leq 2\rho}+\int_{|x-y|>2\rho}\Big]\nabla_{x}\Big[\frac{1-\zeta_{\rho}(x-y)}{|x-y|^{n-2+2\alpha}}\Big]\nabla^{\bot}\theta(y)dy\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big[\int^{2\rho}_{1}\frac{r^{n-1}dr}{r^{n-1+2\alpha}}+\frac{1}{\rho}\int^{2\rho}_{1}\frac{r^{n-1}dr}{r^{n-2+2\alpha}}\Big]+\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{2}}\Big[\int^{\infty}_{2\rho}\frac{r^{n-1}dr}{r^{2(n-1+2\alpha)}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,\alpha}(\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{2}}),

which along with (
A.9
) yields that for any
\rho\in(0,1]
,

\displaystyle|\nabla u^{(22)}(x)|\leq C_{n,\alpha}(\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{2}}).

It follows from (
A.8
) and (
A.7
) that, for any
\rho\in(0,1]
,

\displaystyle|\nabla u^{(2)}(x)|\leq\begin{cases}C(-\log\rho+1)\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{2}},&\mbox{for $\alpha=\frac{1}{2},$ }\\
C(\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{2}}),&\mbox{for $\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}).$ }\\
\end{cases}

Combining this estimate and (
A.6
) implies that, for
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
and any
\rho\in(0,1]
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\begin{cases}C\rho^{\varepsilon}\|\theta\|_{H^{s}}+C(-\log\rho+1)\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{2}},&\mbox{for $\alpha=\frac{1}{2},$ }\\
C\rho^{\varepsilon}\|\theta\|_{H^{s}}+C(\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{2}}),&\mbox{for $\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2}).$ }\\
\end{cases}

(A.10)

If
\|\theta\|_{H^{s}}\leq 1
, we take
\rho=1
; otherwise we choose
\rho

such that
\rho^{\varepsilon}\|\theta\|_{H^{s}}=1
, namely,
\rho=\|\theta\|^{-\frac{1}{\epsilon}}_{H^{s}}
,
and (
A.10
) becomes (
3.10
). In either case (
3.10
) holds.

With only minor changes, the same proof applies to periodic fluid
flow. We then complete the proof of Lemma
3.3
.

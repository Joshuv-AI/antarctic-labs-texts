Bethe Algebra using Pure Spinors Preprint: APS/123-QED
Simon Ekhammar, Dmytro Volin

Abstract
We explore a 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 r {\mathfrak{gl}}_{r} -covariant parameterisation of Bethe algebra appearing in 𝔰 ​ 𝔬 2 ​ r {\mathfrak{so}}_{2r} integrable models, demonstrate its geometric origin from a fused flag, and use it to compute the spectrum of periodic rational spin chains, for various choices of the rank r r and Drinfeld polynomials.



I
Introduction

Conserved charges of integrable models form a commutative algebra known as the Bethe algebra. In this paper, we propose an efficient way to parameterise it for models with
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
symmetry. Our motivation is two-fold. From a practical perspective,
{\mathfrak{so}}_{\mathsf{n}}
systems emerged in AdS/CFT integrability offering an elementary toolkit to study holography, in terms of fishnets and fishchains
(
Gürdoğan and Kazakov 2016
;
*Gromov:2019bsj
)
. Understanding of the Bethe algebra is required for solving thermodynamic Bethe Ansatz (TBA) equations and performing separation of variables
(
Balog and Hegedus 2005
;
*Basso:2019xay
;
*Derkachov:2019tzo
;
*Cavaglia:2021mft
)
. On the other hand, from a conceptual point of view, the Bethe algebra is surprisingly universal. For generic values of the spectral parameter, it features the same relations that appear in numerous studies: integrable models derived by quantum inverse scattering method (with rational, trigonometric, elliptic cases included); TBA (both relativistic and AdS/CFT-type systems); a variety of differential equations (leading to ODE/IM and similar correspondences); supersymmetric gauge theories (by virtue of Bethe/gauge correspondence); enumerative geometry (where the Bethe algebra is a quantum cohomology ring)
(
[Seee.g.thefollowingworksandreferencestherein:][]Kuniba:2010ir
;
*Masoero:2015rcz
;
*Nekrasov:2013xda
;
*Maulik:2012wi
)
. Another name for our study is parameterisation of a finite-difference oper,
e.g.
of the one in
Frenkel et al. 2020
. Whereas universality holds for any Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
,
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
is the simplest possible case after
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{r+1}
. The
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{r+1}
case, although studied in great detail, is liable to simplifications shading the nature of relations we would like to explore.
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
is hence an important stepping stone towards the general case we aim to address in future works.

In
Ekhammar et al. 2020
building on techniques of
Sun 2012
;
Masoero et al. 2016
, H. Shu and the authors described the Bethe algebra for any simply-laced
{\mathfrak{g}}
in terms of an extended Q-system. This approach offers full covariance: if an integrable model is based on the quantum deformation of the current or the loop algebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
then Baxter Q-functions generating the Bethe algebra are Plücker coordinates of the fused flag corresponding to the Langlands dual
{}^{L}\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
. A physical counterpart is a recently proposed realisation of Q-operators by magnetically charged ’t Hooft lines
Costello et al. 2021
.

The covariant description has an unfortunate downside: too many Q-functions are involved. Indeed, the number of Q-functions of the extended Q-system grows exponentially with
r
, whereas the number of functionally independent Q-functions is equal to the rank of the algebra.

In the
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{r}
case (of rank
r-1
), this issue is elegantly handled with ‘single-indexed’ Q-functions
{\mathcal{Q}}_{a}
,
a=1,\ldots,r
, which are projective coordinates
[{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}:\ldots:{\mathcal{Q}}_{r}]
of
\mathbb{P}^{r-1}
. They naturally transform under
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{r}
-action (
\mathsf{PGL}_{r}
to be precise, but we mostly work on the Lie algebra level). The other Q-functions, which are Plücker coordinates of Grassmannians
\mathsf{Gr}(r,k)
, are conveniently computed by discrete Wronskian determinants
{\mathcal{Q}}_{a_{1}\ldots a_{k}}=W({\mathcal{Q}}_{a_{1}},\ldots,{\mathcal{Q}}_{a_{k}})

(
Krichever et al. 1997
;
Tsuboi 2010
)
. For a reader familiar with nested Bethe Ansatz, we recall that Q-functions in the case of spin chains have the structure
{\mathcal{Q}}_{a_{1}\ldots a_{k}}=\sigma_{k}\,q_{a_{1}\ldots a_{k}}
, where
q_{a_{1}\ldots a_{k}}
are polynomials. A rather standard notation choice is to identify
q_{1\ldots k}
for
k=1,\ldots,r-1
as the polynomials whose zeros satisfy nested Bethe equations. We can then choose
{\mathcal{Q}}_{12\ldots k}
to generate the Bethe algebra but this is probably the least covariant way to proceed, and this is not what we are aiming for. We want to work with single-indexed
{\mathcal{Q}}_{a}
as generators, and this is practically feasible because a replacement of nested Bethe equations exists: Wronskian Bethe equations
W({\mathcal{Q}}_{1},{\mathcal{Q}}_{2},\ldots,{\mathcal{Q}}_{r})=1
are used directly for fixing
{\mathcal{Q}}_{a}
.

For
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
models, candidates for analogs of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{a}
appeared in the work
Ferrando et al. 2021
by G. Ferrando, R. Frassek, and V. Kazakov who considered Q-system on the Weyl orbit (we use the terminology of
Ekhammar et al. 2020
). Overall, this work offered a variety of relations that transform naturally under the action of the Weyl group of
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
and moreover are
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{r}
-covariant, where
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{r}\subset{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
corresponds to the standard embedding
\mathsf{U}(r)\subset{\mathsf{SO}}(2r)
(we do not need to specify real forms and hence select
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{r}
for notation). In particular, equation (7.2) there was suggested as a substitute for Wronskian Bethe equations, it featured
r+1
Q-functions, their equivalents in our work shall be
\Psi_{a}
and
\Psi_{\emptyset}
, where
\Psi_{a}
form a vector
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{r}
-multiplet and
\Psi_{\emptyset}
is a singlet. However, unlike in the
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{r}
case, (7.2) is not sufficient by itself. As we shall see, extra relations involving an anti-symmetric tensor
\Psi_{ab}
are required.

In this paper we explain how the
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{r}
-covariant expressions of
Ferrando et al. 2021
are related to the geometric
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
-covariant description in
Ekhammar et al. 2020
. We investigate this interplay and complete (7.2) to a system of equations on a relatively small number of Q-functions to effectively encode the whole Bethe algebra.

Our study starts from the observation made in
Ekhammar et al. 2020
: Because Q-functions are Plücker coordinates, those of them who define maximal isotropic subspaces are components of pure spinors. Then one can benefit from Cartan parameterisation of pure spinors which uses only
1+r+{r\choose 2}
functions, we shall denote them respectively as

\displaystyle{\Psi_{\emptyset}}\equiv 1/\Phi\,,\quad\Psi_{a}\equiv{\bf P}_{a}/\Phi\,,\quad\Psi_{ab}\equiv\mu_{ab}/\Phi\,.

(1)

Because of the fusion relations, only
r
of them are independent. If one chooses
{\bf P}_{a}
as the independent ones, they would be naturally the ‘single-indexed’ Q-functions of a
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{r}
Q-system (and not of
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{r}
because they alone do not form a set of projective coordinates). Owing to other fused flag relations, all the members of the extended Q-system are just rational combinations of
{\Psi_{\emptyset}},\Psi_{a},\Psi_{ab}
and so this triple contains all, or almost all, non-trivial information about the Bethe algebra. A suitable approach to work with the triple depends on the question to study, we give two examples in Sections
IV
and
V
.

The paper is organised as follows: in Section
II
we summarise the relevant findings of
Ekhammar et al. 2020
about the extended
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
Q-system, with a slight update of notations. Section
III
describes
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{r}
parameterisation of the Q-system and builds up to relations (
34
) between
{\Psi_{\emptyset}},\Psi_{a},\Psi_{ab}
which play the role of Wronskian Bethe equations and is a way to concisely describe Bethe Algebra. From these relations we then derive all the features of the extended Q-system and use this techniques to prove that, starting from generic enough
r
Q-functions as an input, one can always construct a consistent extended Q-system in the unique up to the symmetry way. Section
IV
studies a
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{r}
-invariant approach to compute transfer matrices in terms of
\Phi,{\bf P}_{a}
only but eventually resolves that adding
\mu_{ab}
, while optional, is a useful simplification. Section
V
is a proof of concept that the developed formalism works in practice: we explicitly and efficiently compute the spectrum of rational spin chains, the results are available in the ancillary
Mathematica
notebook. These results offer rich experimental evidence that equations (
34
), plus kinematic constraints in the non-basic cases, provide a rigorous description of the Bethe Algebra in the same sense as
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{r}
Wronskian Bethe equations do, the precise mathematical conjecture is in Section
V.4
. The results of the work are summarised in section Conclusions. The first appendix explores action of Weyl group on Q-functions and provides further comparison with the results of
Ferrando et al. 2021
. Finally, the second appendix contains several technical proofs.

The

runs over all components of the representation. With an appropriate choice
Ekhammar et al. 2020

in

Q_{(a)}=\sum_{\mathfrak{i}}Q_{(a)}^{\mathfrak{i}}{\mathfrak{e}}_{\mathfrak{i}}^{(a)}

Until Section
V

u
need only to be defined and single-valued in a certain domain, their analyticity and even continuity may be in principle waived. What matters is existence of a free action of the ‘shift’ group

on the domain of definition. For a function
F
, the notation
F^{[k]}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}

F^{\pm}\equiv F^{[\pm 1]}
.

In Section
V
, we study an explicit physical example of a rational spin chain when the analytic properties of Q-functions will play a role. For this example, the shift group lives up to its name:
F^{[k]}(u)=F(u+\frac{{\mathsf{i}}}{2}k)
. Other typical case is a trigonometric system with the multiplicative shift
F^{[k]}(z)=F(q^{k/2}z)
(we will not consider it). The additive implementation of the shift explains the mnemonics of our abbreviations: The shift operator
D=e^{\frac{{\mathsf{i}}}{2}\partial_{u}}
acts as
D^{k}\,F=F^{[k]}
, the fused power is defined by
f^{[n]_{D}}=\prod\limits^{n}_{k=1}f^{[n+1-2k]}
, and the finite-difference Wronskian determinant is
W(F_{1},F_{2},\dots,F_{k})=|F^{[k+1-2a]}_{b}|_{\begin{subarray}{c}a=1\dots k\\
b=1\dots k\end{subarray}}
, where
|M_{ab}|
is the determinant of a matrix, the range of indices
a,b
will be omitted when there is no risk of confusion.

The function
Q_{(a)}(u)
valued in
L(\omega_{a})
can be viewed locally as a section in an associated
\mathsf{SO}({2r})
-bundle (or, more accurately,
\mathsf{Pin}(2r)
-bundle). We can then introduce a connection and gauge the definition of the shift
Q_{(a)}^{[k]}
, it would be a finite-difference analog of passing from ordinary to covariant derivative,
cf.
Kazakov et al. 2016
for a similar discussion in the
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{r}
case. This way of thinking links our results to those about opers, as is explained in
Ekhammar et al. 2020
, and has its advantages. However, one can always locally gauge away the connection as long as the function class of Q-functions is unconstrained, and we assume this gauge choice throughout the paper.

Probably the most famous relations satisfied by Q-functions are the so-called QQ-relations
Mukhin and Varchenko 2005
,
Masoero et al. 2016
. If to include their transforms under action of Weyl group elements
{\mathsf{w}}
—precise definitions are in
Ekhammar et al. 2020
and Appendix
A
—they read

\displaystyle W({Q}_{(a)}^{{\mathsf{w}}(1)},{Q}_{(a)}^{{\mathsf{w}}(2)})=\pm\prod_{b\sim a}{Q}_{(b)}^{{\mathsf{w}}(1)}\,,

(2)

where
b\sim a
indicates nodes on the Dynkin diagram adjacent to
a
, and the sign
\pm
depends on
{\mathsf{w}}
.

The other relations of the extended Q-system, at least in general position situation, ultimately follow from (
2
) but this feature is not trivial to demonstrate, its full combinatorial proof is one of the results of this work. For the moment we shall list them as an additional requirement. Some of them first time appeared in
Sun 2012
but their complete package is due to
Ferrando et al. 2021
,
Ekhammar et al. 2020
. These relations have an intriguing geometrical meaning. For instance, they imply that components of
Q_{(a)}
are projective Plücker coordinates
[Q_{(a)}^{1}:Q_{(a)}^{2}:\ldots]
of the partial flag manifold
G/P_{a}
, where
P_{a}
is the corresponding maximal parabolic subgroup. More generally, they tell us that all Q-functions together parameterise a fused flag
Ekhammar et al. 2020
. The very fact that all these relations can be simultaneously satisfied is remarkable.

To list down the relations, it is useful to introduce a more intuitive labelling for functions
Q_{(a)}^{\mathfrak{i}}
. Note that the vector representation of
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
is associated to the first node of the Dynkin diagram and the spinor representations with the last two nodes. Hence the following notation becomes natural:

\displaystyle Q_{(1)}^{i}=V^{i}\,,

\displaystyle Q_{(r-1)}=\Gamma^{-}\Psi\,,

\displaystyle Q_{(r)}=\Gamma^{+}\Psi\,.

(3)

Here
\Psi
is a
2^{r}
-dimensional Dirac spinor and
\Gamma^{+},\Gamma^{-}
are projection-matrices onto
2^{r-1}
-dimensional Weyl spinors. An
{\mathfrak{so}}(2r)
vector has
2r
components, to label them it is convenient to use
i,j,\ldots\in\{1,2,\dots,r,-r,\dots,-2,-1\}
, while
a,b,\ldots\in\{1,2,\dots,r\}
shall be always positive. To further precise conventions, we choose the anti-diagonal metric
g_{ij}
and
\Gamma
-matrices forming the Clifford algebra:

\displaystyle g_{ij}=\delta_{i+j,0}\,,\quad\{\Gamma_{i},\Gamma_{j}\}=g_{ij}\,.

(4)

Also, we pick the charge-conjugation matrix
C
as

\displaystyle C=(-1)^{\frac{r(r-1)}{2}}(\Gamma_{1}+\Gamma_{-1})\dots(\Gamma_{r}+\Gamma_{-r})\,.

(5)

Using the metric we have the identification

V^{-a}=V_{a}\,.

(6)

Apart from Q-functions that are components for the vector and the spinor representations, there are
r-3
other collections of Q-functions corresponding to anti-symmetric tensor representations. These Q-functions can be equated to Wronskians of
V^{i}
: Introduce a multi-index
I=i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{a}
, and let
|I|=a
be its cardinality, then, for
Q_{(a\leq r-2)}^{\mathfrak{i}\equiv I}\equiv V^{I}
,

V^{I}=W(V^{i_{1}},\dots V^{i_{a}})\,.

(7)

For
|I|=r-1,r
,
i.e.
for tensors outside of the Dynkin diagram, (
7
) shall be the definition of
V^{I}
.

The above identification (
7
) is analogous to the one in the
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{r}
Q-system

, however

\left(V^{i}\right)^{[m]}V_{i}^{[-m]}=0\,,\quad m\in\{-r+2,\dots,r-2\}\,;

(8)

In particular, the vectors are null

from spinors:

\displaystyle{\Psi}^{[-r+1+|I|]}C\Gamma^{I}\Gamma^{\pm}{\Psi}^{[r-1-|I|]}={V}^{I}\,,\quad|I|<r\,,

\displaystyle{\Psi}^{-}C\Gamma^{I}{\Psi}^{+}={V}^{I}\,,\quad|I|=r\,.

(9b)

Spinors also satisfy relations analogous to (

\Psi^{[-m]}C\Gamma^{I}\Gamma^{\pm}\Psi^{[m]}=0\,,\quad|I|\leq r-2\,,\quad m\in\{-r+2+|I|,\dots,r-2-|I|\}\,.

(10)

In particular
\Psi
is a pure spinor, that is, it satisfies

\Psi C\Gamma^{I}\Gamma^{\pm}\Psi=0\,,\quad|I|\leq r-2\,.

(11)

Relations of type (

Finally, there is also a non-vanishing inner product between vectors and between spinors when the appropriate shift of the spectral parameter is included:

\displaystyle\left({V}^{I}\right)^{[r-1]}{V}_{I}^{[-r+1]}=(-1)^{|I|(r+1)}\,,

(12a)

\displaystyle{\Psi}^{[-r+1]}C\Gamma^{\pm}{\Psi}^{[r-1]}=1\,.

(12b)

The values of shifts

is the Coxeter number of
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}

\frac{1}{|I|!}

that are defined to feature only positive entries.

Finally, to avoid misinterpretations, we spell out how the above-encountered expressions of type
\Psi_{1}C\Gamma^{I}\Psi_{2}
are decoded for the sake of explicit computations:
\Psi_{1}
is transposed to be a row-vector (boldly, without doing extra complex conjugation or other involution), and then the standard row-times-column multiplication is performed to get a number which depends on
I

III
Pure spinor parameterisation

III.1
Cartan decomposition

Consider the
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{r}
subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
corresponding to removing the
r
-th node of the Dynkin diagram (but keeping the whole Cartan subalgebra) and decompose spinor representations into the irreps w.r.t.
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{r}
. This gives rise to what is known as
\mathsf{U}(r)
or Cartan decomposition. In it, the spinor representation of the
(r-1)
-th node is a direct sum of the exterior forms of odd rank, and the spinor representation of the
r
-th node is a direct sum of the exterior forms of even rank. We shall denote these forms collectively as
\Psi_{(k)}
,
k=0,\dots,r
, the components of the
k
-form
\Psi_{(k)}
are
\Psi_{a_{1}\dots,a_{k}}
and we denote the basis
\theta^{A}
for
A=a_{1}\ldots a_{k}
. We think about
\theta^{a}
as Grassmann variables and Dirac spinor is treated as a super-function:

\Psi=\sum^{r}_{k=0}\Psi_{(k)}\,,\quad{\rm where}\quad\Psi_{(k)}=\Psi_{A}\theta^{A}\,,\quad|A|=k\,.

(13)

Furthermore we will raise and lower indices using Levi-Civita symbol so that

\Psi_{A}=\Psi^{B}\epsilon_{BA}\,.

The off-diagonal metric in (
4
) implies that the
\Gamma
-matrices satisfy the fermionic oscillator algebra. It can be realised on the
\theta
-basis as

\displaystyle\Gamma^{a}\,\theta^{B}=\theta^{a}\theta^{B}\,,\quad\Gamma_{a}\,\theta^{B}=\partial_{a}\theta^{B}\,,

(14a)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\pm}\,\theta^{B}=\frac{1\pm(-1)^{|B|}}{2}\theta^{B}\,,

(14b)

\displaystyle C\,\theta^{A}=(-1)^{\frac{(r-|A|)(r-|A|-1)}{2}}\,*\theta^{A}\,,

(14c)

where
\bar{A}
is the complement of
A
and
*\theta^{A}\equiv\epsilon^{A\bar{A}}\theta^{\bar{A}}
is Hodge conjugation.

As
\mathfrak{so}_{2r}
is realised by commutator of
\Gamma
-matrices, action (
14a
) suggests the decomposition
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}=\Lambda^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{r})\oplus\mathfrak{u}(r)\oplus\Lambda^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{*r})
, where the three terms are spanned, respectively, by
\theta^{a}\theta^{b}
,
\theta^{a}\partial_{b}-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{a}{}_{b}
,
\partial_{a}\partial_{b}
. Additionally, one should think about
\theta^{a}\partial_{b}
with
a>b
and
\partial_{a}\partial_{b}
as raising operators,
\theta^{a}\partial_{a}-\frac{1}{2}
for
a=1,\ldots,r
as Cartan subalgebra, and the rest as lowering operators. It follows that the highest-weight components of the Q-vectors are

{\Psi_{\emptyset}}=Q_{(r)}^{1}\,,\quad\Psi_{r}=Q_{(r-1)}^{1}\,.

(15)

In the introduced parameterisation,
\Psi_{1}C\Gamma^{I}\Psi_{2}
(where
\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2}
are column-vectors) gets the meaning of the top component of the form
\Psi_{1}\wedge*\,C\Gamma^{I}\Psi_{2}
(where
\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2}
are super-functions).

We shall now use the introduced exterior algebra notation to solve explicitly the pure spinor conditions. To this end, one computes

\displaystyle 0=\epsilon_{AB}\left(\Psi\,C\Gamma^{A}\Gamma^{\pm}\Psi\right)\theta^{B}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{{r-|A|}\choose 2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\ \rm{even/odd}\end{subarray}}^{r-|A|}(-1)^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}\Psi_{(r-|A|-k)}\wedge\Psi_{(k)}\,,

\displaystyle r-|A|

\displaystyle\geq 2\,,

(16a)

\displaystyle 0=\epsilon_{AB}\left(\Psi\,C\Gamma^{Ac}{}\Gamma_{c}\Gamma^{\pm}\Psi\right)\theta^{B}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{{r-|A|}\choose 2}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\ \rm{even/odd}\end{subarray}}^{r-|A|}(-1)^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}k\,\Psi_{(r-|A|-k)}\wedge\Psi_{(k)}\,,

\displaystyle r-|A|

\displaystyle\geq 4\,.

(16b)

Here in the second line we use a non-antisymmetrised expression
\Gamma^{Ac}\Gamma_{c}
which is acceptable since its difference with
\Gamma^{Ac}{}_{c}
is
\frac{1}{2}\Gamma^{A}
for which the first line can be used. It is easy to solve (
16
) explicitly for all
\Psi_{(k)}
with
k\geq 3
in terms of
\Psi_{(k)}
for
k=0,1,2
. We illustrate the first two steps. Taking even
k
and
r-|A|=3
in (
16a
), one gets
\Psi_{(3)}\Psi_{\emptyset}-\Psi_{(1)}\wedge\Psi_{(2)}=0
, while
r-|A|=4
leads to
\Psi_{(2)}\wedge\Psi_{(2)}-2\,\Psi_{\emptyset}\Psi_{(4)}=0
. For
r-|A|\geq 4
it is sufficient to use only (
16b
). The full solution is

\displaystyle\boxed{\Psi_{(2n)}=\frac{1}{n!}\frac{\Psi_{(2)}\wedge\Psi_{(2)}\wedge\dots\wedge\Psi_{(2)}}{{\Psi_{\emptyset}}^{n-1}}\,,\quad\Psi_{(2n-1)}=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\frac{\Psi_{(1)}\wedge\Psi_{(2)}\wedge\dots\wedge\Psi_{(2)}}{{\Psi_{\emptyset}}^{n-1}}}\,.

(17)

This is a well-known property of pure spinors that can be found already in the book of Cartan
Cartan 1966
. Its meaning becomes transparent after we rewrite it as follows:

\displaystyle Q_{(r)}=\sum_{n=0}^{[r/2]}\Psi_{(2n)}=e^{\frac{\Psi_{(2)}}{\Psi_{\emptyset}}}\,\Psi_{\emptyset}\,,\quad Q_{(r-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{[(r-1)/2]}\Psi_{(2n+1)}=e^{\frac{\Psi_{(2)}}{\Psi_{\emptyset}}}\,\Psi_{(1)}\,.

(18)

Notice that
\frac{\Psi_{(2)}}{\Psi_{\emptyset}}\in\Lambda^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{r})
and so its exponent can be viewed as a group element. Hence, since
\Psi_{(1)}\in e^{\mathfrak{u}(r)}\theta^{r}
, (
18
) states that Weyl spinors
Q_{(r)},Q_{(r-1)}
are on the group orbit of the corresponding highest-weight vectors. Being on such an orbit is the universal definition of a pure vector for any choice of a Lie group and its irrep. Furthermore, a group element acting on the two highest-weight vectors of
L(\omega_{r-1})
and
L(\omega_{r})
to produce
Q_{(r)}
,
Q_{(r-1)}
can be chosen to be the same one, this is just saying differently that two pure Weyl spinors are combined into one pure Dirac spinor
\Psi
.

This interpretation allows us to also argue why all relations (
11
) are satisfied even though we only used their subset to derive (
17
). Indeed, the two highest-weight vectors obviously satisfy (
11
) and then, by covariance, any spinors on the group orbit of the highest-weight vectors will satisfy (
11
) as well. A direct combinatorial derivation of (
11
) from (
17
) shall be given later as part of Section
III.4
.

III.2
Tensors from spinors

We concluded in the previous subsection that all components of spinor Q-functions can be expressed through
\Psi_{\emptyset},\Psi_{a},\Psi_{ab}
.
We shall now derive that all tensor Q-functions
V^{I}
can be expressed through
\Psi_{\emptyset},\Psi_{a},\Psi_{ab}
as well. This time, in contrast to pure spinor relations (
11
), we will use equations that are non-local in spectral parameter. In other words, we need the fact that the extended Q-system is not just a flag but a fused flag.

A typical fused combination we shall exploit is

\begin{split}\epsilon_{AB}\left(\Psi^{[-m]}C\Gamma^{A}\Gamma^{\pm}\Psi^{[m]}\right)\theta^{B}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
r-|A|-k\ {\rm even/odd}\end{subarray}}^{r-|A|}(-1)^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}\Psi^{[m]}_{(r-|A|-k)}\wedge\Psi^{[-m]}_{(k)}\,.\end{split}

(19)

A particularly important case is to consider a compatibility condition following from (
9
) and to rewrite it using (

2
) to become

\displaystyle W(q_{(a)}^{{\mathsf{w}}(1)},q_{(a)}^{{\mathsf{w}}(2)})\propto P_{a}\prod_{b\sim a}q_{(b)}^{{\mathsf{w}}(1)}\,.

(59)

One can choose
{\mathsf{w}}={\rm Id}
and apply the standard argument to get nested Bethe equations

\displaystyle\frac{q_{(a)}^{1[+2]}}{q_{(a)}^{1[-2]}}\prod_{b\sim a}\frac{q_{(b)}^{1[-1]}}{q_{(b)}^{1[+1]}}=-\frac{P_{a}^{+}}{P_{a}^{-}}\quad\quad\text{at zeros of }q_{(a)}^{1}\,.

(60)

Comparing to nested Bethe equations from the literature
Reshetikhin 1983
;
Ogievetsky and Wiegmann 1986
,
we see that the chosen analytic Bethe Ansatz is supposed to describe the spectrum of rational spin chains in finite-dimensional representations of Yangian
\mathcal{Y}({\mathfrak{so}}_{2r})
, and with periodic boundary conditions used in the construction of the Bethe algebra. In particular, it should describe a spin chain of length
L
with sites in the vector representation of
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
if

P_{1}=\prod_{l=1}^{L}(u-\theta_{l})\,,\quad P_{a\neq 1}=1\,.

(61)

Numbers
\theta_{l}
are known as inhomogeneities. The homogeneous case
\theta_{l}=0
corresponds to a chain with nearest-neighbour interactions.

Every finite-dimensional representation of
\mathcal{Y}({\mathfrak{so}}_{2r})
has the highest weight state dubbed the ferromagnetic vacuum which is uniquely characterised by the collection of Drinfeld polynomials. From (
60
), one identifies that
P_{a}
,
a=1,\ldots,r
, are Drinfeld polynomials
Kirillov and Reshetikhin 1990
.

For generic values of parameters, (
59
) for
{\mathsf{w}}={\rm Id}
follows from the nested Bethe equations and then using arguments of the reproduction procedure in
Mukhin and Varchenko 2005
we conclude that all Q-functions on the Weyl orbit satisfy the analytic Bethe Ansatz; finally, with help of
e.g.
(
7
) we show that all Q-functions of the extended Q-system satisfy the analytic Bethe Ansatz. Hence the analytic Bethe Ansatz is equivalent to the nested Bethe equations, however in generic position.

We included the discussion of nested Bethe equations because it is a useful connection point with other works. However, we have no goal to rely on them, instead we shall use Wronskian Bethe equations.

Physical vs Langlands-dual
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
.

Before going into the computational hurdles, we need to further discuss the setting of the problem. There are
two different

{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
algebras involved. One
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
algebra is the physical symmetry. It is a subalgebra of
\mathcal{Y}({\mathfrak{so}}_{2r})
which commutes with the Bethe algebra and in particular with the spin chain Hamiltonian. As a module under this
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
action, the Hilbert space decomposes as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus\limits_{\lambda}d_{\lambda}\,L(\lambda)\,,

(62)

where
L(\lambda)
are
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
irreps with the highest weight
\lambda
and
d_{\lambda}
is the multiplicity of their occurrence. The multiplicity of the ferromagnetic vacuum multiplet is
d_{\lambda_{\rm max}}=1
; the weight of the ferromagnetic vacuum is encoded in the degrees of Drinfeld polynomials:

\displaystyle\lambda_{\rm max}=\sum_{a=1}^{r}\omega_{a}\,{\rm deg}\,P_{a}\,.

(63)

The other
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
algebra is the one which we worked with in all the previous sections. We remind that it acts on
Q_{(a)}
transforming them as vectors in the
a
-th fundamental representation
L(\omega_{a})
and this action is the symmetry of the equations of the extended Q-system. In the decomposition
Q_{(a)}=\sum_{\mathfrak{i}}Q_{(a)}^{\mathfrak{i}}{\mathfrak{e}}_{\mathfrak{i}}^{(a)}
, vectors
{\mathfrak{e}}_{\mathfrak{i}}^{(a)}
are weight basis vectors of
L(\omega_{a})
of weight
\gamma_{(a)}^{\mathfrak{i}}
, and it is the convention that
\gamma_{(a)}^{1}\equiv\omega_{a}
is the highest weight. The following rules for computing
\gamma_{(a)}^{\mathfrak{i}}
apply

\displaystyle V^{\pm a}\to\gamma_{(1)}^{\pm a}=\pm\varepsilon_{a}\,,\quad\Psi_{a}\to\gamma_{(r-1)}^{r-a+1}=\omega_{r}-\varepsilon_{a}\,,\quad\Psi_{\emptyset}\to\gamma_{(r)}^{1}=\omega_{r}\,.

(64)

More generally, the weight of
{\mathfrak{e}}_{\mathfrak{i}}^{(a)}
associated to
\Psi_{A}
is
\frac{1}{2}\big(\sum\limits_{a\notin A}\varepsilon_{a}-\sum\limits_{a\in A}\varepsilon_{a}\big)
, and to
V^{I}
is
\sum\limits_{a\in I}\varepsilon_{a}-\sum\limits_{-a\in I}\varepsilon_{a}
. Here we used the standard orthonormal basis
\varepsilon_{a}
,
(\varepsilon_{a},\varepsilon_{b})=\delta_{ab}
, of the weight space. The fundamental weights are expanded as
\omega_{a}=\sum\limits_{b=1}^{a}\varepsilon_{b}
if
a<r-1
,
\omega_{r}=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{a=1}^{r}\varepsilon_{a}
and
\omega_{r-1}=\omega_{r}-\varepsilon_{r}
in this basis.

Each physical multiplet
L(\lambda)
is expected to be represented by a solution of the extended Q-system and hence we expect
d_{\lambda}
solutions representing multiplets of weight
\lambda
. There is one solution describing the ferromagnetic vacuum multiplet, it has the simplest possible highest-weight components of Q-functions:
q_{(a)}^{1}=1
. Other multiplets—excitations around the vacuum—have non-trivial polynomials
q_{(a)}^{1}
whose degrees are known as magnon numbers. Overall, degrees of Baxter polynomials for a multiplet
L(\lambda)
are decided as follows

\displaystyle{\rm deg}\,q_{(a)}^{\mathfrak{i}}=(\omega_{a},\lambda_{\rm max}+\rho)-(\gamma_{(a)}^{\mathfrak{i}},\lambda+\rho)\,,

(65)

where
\rho=\sum\limits_{a}\omega_{a}=\sum\limits_{a=1}^{r-1}(r-a)\varepsilon_{a}
is the Weyl vector.

In the ‘scalar products’
(\omega_{a},\lambda_{\rm max}+\rho)
and
(\gamma_{(a)}^{\mathfrak{i}},\lambda+\rho)
, vectors
\lambda_{\rm max},\lambda,\rho
are the ones from the weight space of
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
which is the physical symmetry, while
\omega_{a},\gamma_{(a),\mathfrak{i}}
are from the weight space of
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
which is the Q-system symmetry. Taking scalar products of vectors from different spaces is of course nonsense and we use this notation only because it produces correct numbers within the chosen computational prescriptions. A conceptually accurate interpretation of (
65
) is that the ‘scalar product’ is in fact the pairing between dual vector spaces, and the two
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
algebras are Langlands duals of one another.

V.2
Implementation via Wronskian Bethe equations

We shall search for solutions of the extended Q-system by first focusing on the triple of spinor Q-functions. For them, the analytic Bethe Ansatz reads
\Psi_{\emptyset}\propto\sigma_{r}\times\psi_{0}
,
\Psi_{a}\propto\sigma_{r-1}\times\psi_{a}
,
\Psi_{ab}\propto\sigma_{r}\times\psi_{ab}
. Let
M_{a}
be the degree of
\psi_{a}
and
M_{0}
the degree of
\psi_{0}
. Parameterise these polynomials as

\displaystyle\psi_{a}=u^{M_{a}}+\sum^{M_{a}-1}_{l=0}c_{a,l}\,u^{l}\,,\quad\psi_{0}=u^{M_{0}}+\sum^{M_{0}-1}_{l=0}c_{0,l}\,u^{l}\,.

(66)

Our goal shall be to derive equations on
c_{a,l}
for
a=0,\ldots,r
.

The Q-system is covariant under action of
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
. Generally, one can introduce a
u
-dependent holomorphic connection making this covariance into a gauge symmetry. As was mentioned in the introduction, we work in a gauge where the connection is trivial or more accurately where the parallel transport from
u
to
u+{\mathsf{i}}
is trivial. Assuming this, we need to fix the residual gauge freedom. Ansatz (
57
) allows for only global
u
-independent
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
transformations, and only transformations from the Borel subalgebra remain once (
65
) is imposed. Symmetry transformations from Cartan subalgebra can rescale
e.g.
\Psi_{a}
by arbitrary constants, but this is hidden in the
\propto
sign of (
57
). We are left with the nilpotent subalgebra only, and we partially fix this freedom by setting

\displaystyle c_{a,M_{b}}=0\,,\quad a<b\leq r\,.

(67a)

It is finally the time to consider Wronskian Bethe equations (
34
). Start by rewriting (
34b
) in terms of polynomial functions

\begin{split}W(\psi_{ab},\psi_{0})\propto S_{ab}\,,\quad S_{ab}=\frac{P_{r}}{P_{r-1}}W(\psi_{a},\psi_{b})\,,\end{split}

(68)

and then formally invert this relation to find
\psi_{ab}
:

\psi_{ab}\propto\psi_{0}\frac{1}{D-D^{-1}}\frac{S_{ab}}{\psi_{0}^{+}\psi_{0}^{-}}\,,

(69)

where the overall normalisations are fixed from the requirement that
\psi_{ab}
are monic.
Expand r.h.s. around
u=\infty
, to this end one can use
D=e^{\frac{{\mathsf{i}}}{2}\partial_{u}}
and so

\frac{1}{D-D^{-1}}=-\frac{{\mathsf{i}}}{\partial_{u}}-\frac{{\mathsf{i}}}{24}\partial_{u}-\frac{7{\mathsf{i}}}{5760}\partial^{3}_{u}+\dots\,.

(70)

Since
\psi_{ab}
has to be polynomial, the obtained Laurent series in (
69
) must terminate and we can discard terms of order
\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{u})
, for the same reason we can set
\frac{1}{\partial_{u}}\frac{1}{u}=0
, for other powers
\frac{1}{\partial_{u}}
is the usual integration
\frac{1}{\partial_{u}}u^{n}=\frac{1}{n+1}u^{n+1}
. The integration constant can be chosen arbitrarily because it is yet unused piece of the global
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
symmetry: we can subtract any multiple of
\psi_{0}
from
\psi_{ab}
. We fix this symmetry by setting the coefficient in
\psi_{ab}
in front of
u^{M_{0}}
to zero. All symmetries are fixed after this step.

Now we plug the obtained
\psi_{ab}
back into (
68
), this gives non-trivial equations for the remaining
c_{a,l}
. We combine them with (
34a
)

W(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{r})\propto\psi_{0}^{[r-2]_{D}}\prod_{a=1}^{r-1}P^{[a]_{D}}_{a}\,.

(71)

Equations (
71
) and (
68
) is an explicit implementation of the analytic Bethe Ansatz applied to Wronskian Bethe equations (
34
). Let us assume we have solved them,
i.e.
found
c_{a,l}
and hence
\Psi_{\emptyset},\Psi_{a},\Psi_{ab}
. Does it mean that we solved the extended Q-system as well? Indeed, it is possible to compute other Q-functions from
\Psi_{\emptyset},\Psi_{a},\Psi_{ab}
and, by Theorem
2
, they will satisfy the extended Q-system relations. But this argument is not sufficient. We need also to verify whether all the Q-functions computed in this way conform with analytic Bethe Ansatz (
57
), otherwise the solution of Wronskian Bethe equations should be deemed nonphysical.

One can readily notice that (
71
) and (
68
) cannot reflect all details of a sophisticated enough spin chain because Drinfeld polynomials
P_{a}
, which could be in principle
r
independent functions, appear only through combinations
P_{r}/P_{r-1}
and
\prod\limits_{a=1}^{r-1}P^{[a]_{D}}_{a}
. We mark off important cases when these combinations nonetheless provide the sufficient information—when
P_{r}=1
for
r\neq 1,a-1,a
and
P_{r-1}
and
P_{r}
are co-prime. Such cases shall be referred to as basic spin chains. They include for instance chains where every node is in the vector representation, or in a Weyl spinor representation of the same chirality.

For non-basic spin chains, we need further detailing. To this end we notice that tensor Q-functions
V^{A}
computed via (
25
) do not automatically satisfy the analytic Bethe Ansatz in consequence of Wronskian Bethe equations. Indeed, with the Ansatz
V^{A}\propto\sigma_{|A|}v^{A}
, we get

v^{A}\propto\frac{\epsilon^{Aa^{\prime}_{1}\ldots a^{\prime}_{k}}W(\psi_{a^{\prime}_{1}},\ldots,\psi_{a^{\prime}_{k}})}{\psi_{0}^{[k-2]_{D}}\prod\limits_{b=r-k+1}^{r-1}P_{b}^{[b+k-r]_{D}}}\,.

For
v^{A}
to be polynomials for
1\leq|A|\leq r-2
, the following requirement should be
additionally imposed:

\displaystyle{\rm Remainder}\left(\frac{W(\psi_{a_{1}},\ldots,\psi_{a_{k}})}{\psi_{0}^{[k-2]_{D}}\prod\limits_{b=r-k+1}^{r-1}P_{b}^{[b+k-r]_{D}}}\right)=0\,\quad\quad\text{for $2\leq k\leq r-1\,.$}

(72)

Practical interpretation is the following: if we impose only Wronskian Bethe equations (
71
) and (
68
), this would correspond to considering an effective basic spin chain with
P_{r}^{\rm eff}/P_{r-1}^{\rm eff}=P_{r}/P_{r-1}
and
P_{1}^{\rm eff}=(P_{r-1}^{\rm eff})^{-[r-1]_{D}}\prod\limits_{a=1}^{r-1}P^{[a]_{D}}_{a}
. We shall get a finite number of solutions but this number is excessive compared to the dimension of Hilbert space if the true spin chain is not basic. For description of the latter one needs to filter the obtained solutions and keep only those that satisfy (
72
); we refer to (
72
) as ‘kinematic constraints’ for this reason. They are written as an overdetermined system for all possible choices of
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}
but using them in this way turns out to be time-efficient in explicit computations.

We are now ready to discuss analytic properties of all Q-functions in consequence of (
68
), (
71
), and (
72
).

Lemma 4
.

In consequence of (
68
) only, and in generic position,
W(\psi_{a_{1}},\ldots,\psi_{a_{k}})
is divisible by
\psi_{0}^{[k-2]_{D}}
for
k\leq r
.

Proof.

For
k=3
:
W(\psi_{a},\psi_{b},\psi_{c})=\frac{W(W(\psi_{a},\psi_{b}),W(\psi_{a},\psi_{c}))}{\psi_{a}}=\left(\frac{P_{r-1}}{P_{r}}\right)^{[2]_{D}}\frac{1}{\psi_{a}}W(\psi_{0},\psi_{ab},\psi_{ac})\psi_{0}
from where divisibility by
\psi_{0}
follows assuming
\psi_{0}
and
P_{r}^{[2]_{D}}\psi_{a}
do not have common zeros. For
k>3
, we use
W(\psi_{a_{1}},\ldots,\psi_{a_{k}})=\frac{W(W(\psi_{a_{1}},\ldots,\psi_{a_{k-2}},\psi_{a_{k-1}}),W(\psi_{a_{1}},\ldots,\psi_{a_{k-2}},\psi_{a_{k}}))}{W(\psi_{a_{1}},\ldots,\psi_{a_{k-2}})}
and confirm divisiblity by
\psi_{0}^{[k-2]_{D}}
using induction in
k
.
∎

This was a technical observation demonstrating that
\psi_{0}^{[k-2]_{D}}
can be, under the assumptions of the lemma, pulled out from the remainder in (
72
) and hence (
72
) in the case of basic spin chains offers no additional information compared to Wronskian Bethe equations.

Lemma 5

(Sufficient condition for polynomiality of
q_{(a)}^{\mathfrak{i}}
)
.

Assuming Wronskian Bethe equations (
68
), (
71
) together with kinematic constraints (
72
), all the Q-functions of the extended Q-system computed from
\Psi_{\emptyset},\Psi_{a},\Psi_{ab}
satisfy analytic Bethe Ansatz (
57
) if
\psi_{0}
does not contain zeros separated by
{\mathsf{i}}
.

The proof is in Appendix
B
.

It is natural to assume that zeros of
\psi_{0}
non-trivially depend on the input data—zeros of Drinfeld polynomials a.k.a. inhomogeneities—and thus we can get zeros of
\psi_{0}
not differ by
{\mathsf{i}}
by changing inhomogeneities a bit. Likewise it is assumed that genericity assumptions used in the proof of Lemma
4
can be achieved by modifying inhomogeneities as well. Although we have a solid experimental evidence for this assumption we cannot offer a systematic rigorous proof.

With this assumption on inhomogeneities, we conclude that analytic Bethe Ansatz follows from (
68
), (
71
), (
72
) for generic values of parameters and in particular, the proposed equations can be considered as an alternative equivalent to nested Bethe equations. The observations we made are fully analogous to the ones between Wronskian Bethe equations and nested Bethe equations for
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
chains
Mukhin et al. 2013
;
Chernyak et al. 2022
. Moreover, the analogy includes the role of kinematic constraints: The focus of
Mukhin et al. 2013
;
Chernyak et al. 2022
was on a spin chain with nodes in vector representations which is an example of a basic spin chain. If one wants to explore more complicated examples, a supplement with kinematic constraints similar to (
72
) is needed for
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
case as well, such constraints were for instance a part of the AdS/CFT spectrum computation
Marboe and Volin 2018
.

The proposed approach always gave the expected number of solutions

V.4
Completeness and faithfulness conjecture

In the conducted experiments, not only generic position values of inhomogeneities were tested meaning we considered situations where physically valid solutions contained Q-functions with coinciding Bethe roots or roots separated by
{\mathsf{i}}
. In fact, a homogeneous chain was often not a generic position either. Although we could not have probably tested all possibilities, the achieved success in all trials is a good indication that the ‘generic position’ clause can be waived in Wronskian Bethe equation describing the spectrum statements and hence we conjecture that the developed formalism can be used to formulate the rigorous completeness theorems. This is also supported by previous experience with
\mathfrak{gl}_{r}
chains
Mukhin et al. 2013
;
Chernyak et al. 2022
, the results of these papers invited us to investigate the completeness features at the first place.

The completeness question consists of two parts. The one, which
Chernyak et al. 2022
refers to as faithfulness, is to prove that the Q-system is indeed isomorphic to the Bethe algebra generated by transfer matrices. More accurately, the isomorphism to establish is between Wronskian Bethe algebra—the polynomial ring
\mathcal{W}=\mathbb{C}[c_{a,l}]/\langle\text{Wronskian Bethe equations}\rangle
computed at weight
\lambda
—and the Bethe algebra restricted to the highest-weight subspace of weight
\lambda
. An important argument for this assesment that would prove surjectivity of the map from
\mathcal{W}
to the Bethe algebra and facilitate proving injectivity is to construct Q-functions as eigenvalues of Baxter Q-operators. For
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
chains, this construction and in particular the derivation of the analytic Bethe Ansatz conditions is not yet fully accomplished, but an important progress was made in
Frassek 2020
. Combining these results with combinatorics of Theorem
2
seems to be a sufficient toolkit to finalise the Q-operator question, at least for basic spin chains, but it is yet to be done.

We did not do experimental checks on the level of Q-operators. However, where it was technically possible, we computed the eigenvalues of transfer matrices as operators acting on the spin chain constructed explicitly from Lax operators and confirmed them to match with T-functions computed via (
43
).

The second part is the actual completeness statement. Within the established isomorphism according to the faithfulness property, it can be equivalently formulated either as the fact that the Bethe algebra contains all possible simultaneously commuting operators,
i.e.
it is a maximal commutative algebra, or that the algebraic number of solutions,
i.e.
the dimension of the Wronskian Bethe algebra
\mathcal{W}
as a vector space over
\mathbb{C}
, is
d_{\lambda}
. Our main argument in favour of completeness is reproducing numbers
d_{\lambda}
in various experiments and we notice that even generic position points are worthwhile of checking as, to our knowledge, completeness of nested Bethe equations was not proven for the
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
case even under generic position assumptions.

We explain now more accurately how the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
of spin chain is constructed, from here it follows what values of
d_{\lambda}
we are checking to reproduce on experiments. The
a
-th fundamental representation of Yangian is, by definition, the irrep with
P_{b}=(u-\theta)^{\delta_{ba}}
,
b=1,\ldots,r
. We denote it by
W_{a}(\theta)
.
In contrast to the
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{r}
case, these fundamental representations can be
reducible
as representations of
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
. The description of
W_{a}(\theta)
as an
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
-module reads

\displaystyle W_{a}(\theta)=L(\omega_{a})\oplus L(\omega_{a-2})\oplus\dots\,,\quad a=1,2,\dots,r-2\,,

(74a)

\displaystyle W_{{r-1}}(\theta)=L(\omega_{r-1})\,,\quad W_{r}(\theta)=L(\omega_{r})\,,

(74b)

where the sum in (
74a
) terminates either by
L(\omega_{1})
for odd
a
or by the trivial representation
L(0)
for even
a
.

By taking tensor products of the fundamental representations, one builds a spin chain of length
L
, with Hilbert space

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\bigotimes\limits_{\ell=1}^{L}W_{a_{\ell}}(\theta_{\ell})\,.

(75)

Its highest-weight vector is characterised by
P_{a}=\prod\limits_{\ell=1}^{L}(u-\theta_{\ell})^{\delta_{aa_{\ell}}}
. Typically
\mathcal{H}
is an irreducible Yangian representation. It can become reducible for specific values of
\theta_{\ell}
in which case the order in which the tensor product (
75
) is taken is important. Solving Wronskian Bethe equations produces
d_{\lambda}
for
\mathcal{H}
as in (
75
) regardless of whether it is reducible or not. So far it is an experimental fact which however was expected. Indeed, the isomorphism between Wronskian and Bethe algebras was proven in
Mukhin et al. 2013
for
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{r}
case under the assumption of cyclicity of Yangian representation, cyclicity in the case of reducible representations was achieved by choosing an order in product (
75
). These observations explain the logic behind the conjecture below.

Conjecture
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
(
75
) be a cyclic representation of
\mathcal{Y}({\mathfrak{so}}_{2r})
with the highest-weight vector being a cyclic vector. Define the Bethe algebra as the commutative subalgebra of
{\rm End}(\mathcal{H})
generated by the row-to-row transfer matrices (in all finite-dimensional representations in the auxiliary space) with periodic boundary conditions, and let
\mathfrak{B}
be the Bethe algebra restricted to the highest-weight subspace (w.r.t.
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
action) of
d_{\lambda}\nu_{\lambda}\subset\mathcal{H}
(
62
). Then
\mathfrak{B}
is a maximal commutative subalgebra of this subspace endomorphisms (completeness), and variables
c_{a,l}
,
a=0,\ldots,r
, that satisfy conditions (
68
),(
71
),(
72
) and the gauge-fixing prescriptions for
\psi_{a},\psi_{ab}
form an algebra
\mathcal{W}
(Wronskian Bethe algebra) isomorphic to
\mathfrak{B}
(faithfulness). In particular, the algebraic number of solutions to (
68
),(
71
),(
72
) with the prescribed gauge fixing is
d_{\lambda}
.

Our general expectation is that the requirement for all
Q_{(a)}^{\mathfrak{i}}
to satisfy the analytic Bethe anastz is the necessary and sufficient condition for faithfullness.
This requirement is not part of the above conjecture because we expect it to be a consequence. As was discussed in Section
V.2
, it would be a consequence for generic values of parameters and it was a consequence for all attempted experiments as well. A weaker and safer version of the conjecture would be to include this requirement. From a practical perspective, checking it starting from the solution of Wronskian Bethe equations demands a small fraction of the computation time comparted to solving of Wronskian Bethe equations themselves.

In the case when
\mathcal{H}
is reducible, and if one wants to select the solutions corresponding to the quotient-representation
\mathcal{H}/U
, where
U
is an invariant subspace, one needs further constraints on
c_{a,l}
. Consider for instance a Kirillov-Reshetikhin module
W_{1,m}(\theta)
that is the
irrep
with
P_{a}=(u-\theta)^{\delta_{1,a}[m]_{D}}
, and build a spin chain by taking tensor products
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\bigotimes_{\ell=1}^{L}W_{1,m}(\theta_{\ell})
. It is isomorphic to the quotient-representation inside spin chain (
75
) of length
m\,L
which is based on the same Drinfeld polynomials as
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
but reducible. To select the Bethe algebra on the quotient one imposes the extra requirement: the polynomials
\frac{T_{1,s}}{\sigma_{1}^{[r-1+s]}\sigma_{1}^{[-r+1-s]}}
are divisible by
(p^{[r-2]}p^{[-r+2]})^{[m-s]_{D}}
,
s<m
, where
p=\prod\limits_{\ell=1}^{L}(u-\theta_{\ell})
.

Kinematic constraints (
72
) serve a similar purpose. We can construct any spin chain as a quotient from a specially designed basic spin chain, and applying the kinematic constraints corresponds to performing this quotient. In particular, the
a
’th fundamental representation of Yangian with
P_{a}=(u-\theta)
,
1<a\leq r-2
, can be achieved as a quotient-representation in the vector spin chain of length
a
described by
P_{1}=(u-\theta)^{[a]_{D}}
, this feature can be spotted in the structure of r.h.s. of (
71
). As for spinor representations, consider as an illustration the simplest case when
P_{r-1}
and
P_{r}
are not co-prime:
P_{r}=P_{r-1}=u-\theta
. This corresponds to an irreducible Yangian representation of dimension
2^{2r-2}
which decomposes as an
\mathfrak{so}_{2r}
-module into the sum
\Lambda^{r-1}(\mathbb{C}^{2r})\oplus\Lambda^{r-3}(\mathbb{C}^{2r})\oplus\ldots
. This Yangian representation can be built as a quotient-representation in the vector spin chain of length
r-1
described by
P_{1}=(u-\theta)^{[r-1]_{D}}
.

VI
Conclusions

We put into practice the extended Q-system introduced in
Ferrando et al. 2021
and
Ekhammar et al. 2020
. To this end, we compromise between maintaining a covariant description and keeping a manageable number of Q-functions: the full
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
covariance is partially broken to
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{r}
subalgebra. Whereas this breakdown and an equivalent parameterisation is already present in
Ferrando et al. 2021
, we make additionally a geometric connection to the fused pure spinor point of view of
Ekhammar et al. 2020
. This allows us to single out a system of equations (
34
) that contains all the essential information and is useful for explicit computations.

Across the paper we encountered three classes of functions:
V_{A},{\bf P}_{A},\Psi_{A}
transforming covariantly under
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{r}
action. Their contra-variant counterparts are
V^{A},{\bf P}^{A},\Psi^{A}
. Let us summarise basic relationships between them. To focus on the conceptual features, we shall suppress shifts of the spectral parameter in
\mu_{AB}
and set
\Phi=1
(while the bulk of the article contains full expressions):

\displaystyle{\bf P}^{A}=\epsilon^{AB}{\bf P}_{B}\,,\quad\Psi^{A}=\epsilon^{AB}\Psi_{B}\,,\quad V^{A}=\mu^{AB}V_{B}\,\quad{\bf P}^{a}=V^{a}\,,\quad{\bf P}_{a}=\Psi_{a}.

(76)

While
{\bf P}^{A}=V^{A}
in the suggested simplifications, we emphasise that
{{{\bf P}}}_{A}\neq\Psi_{A}
for
|A|>1
. These features reflect the following fact:
{{{\bf P}}}^{A},{{{\bf P}}}_{B}
form
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{r}
Q-system (
23
), so do
V^{A},V_{B}
and in fact
V_{A}{}^{B}=g_{AI}V^{IB}
is a piece of
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{2r}
Q-system, while, in contrast,
\Psi^{A},\Psi_{B}
form pure spinor Q-system (
35
).

Equations (
34
) are formulated for the triple
\Psi_{\emptyset},\Psi_{a},\Psi_{ab}
which plays the fundamental role for this article. All other Q-functions can be computed from the triple through (
17
) and (
33
) and we proved that all the relations of the extended Q-system follow from (
34
). This in turn implies that an extended Q-system can always be constructed from
Q_{(a)}^{1}
and is unique up to the symmetry. The derivation of the extended Q-system was not easy to demonstrate previously: In
Ferrando et al. 2021
, some of its relations were conjectured while derived for low-rank cases only; In
Ekhammar et al. 2020
, the relations were obtained using an analytic and non-combinatorial argument via ODE/IM. We now offer their combinatorial derivation for an arbitrary rank.

We demonstrated that, in general position,
\Psi_{\emptyset},\Psi_{a},\Psi_{ab}
and
Q_{(a)}^{1}
can be derived from one another and, owing to the fact that zeros of
Q_{(a)}^{1}
satisfy nested Bethe equations, we confirm that our approach is in principle consistent with these equations in general position. We however circumvented the passage to the nested Bethe equations: Section
V
demonstrates how to use directly Wronskian Bethe equations to find spectrum of rational spin chains in a variety of different representations. The proposed approach is more efficient, by a large margin, than solving nested Bethe equations, at least when the goal is to find all solutions instead of focusing on a single one. Furthermore, the offered equations define de-facto a coordinate ring of an algebraic variety, and we conjecture based on rich numerical evidence that this ring is isomorphic to the Bethe algebra which should be maximally commutative, notably the conjecture does not involve a general position assumption. Using nested Bethe equations towards the same type of statement is impractical if even possible. These observations are in full parallel with the ones for Wronskian Bethe equations of
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{r}
spin chains in
Mukhin et al. 2013
;
Chernyak et al. 2022
.

Having additionally in mind potential applications to
e.g.
TBA equations, we investigated in Section
IV
how T-functions
T_{a,s}
are decomposed into
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{r}
-invariants. We got closed although rather bulky formulae for all symmetric powers of miniscule representations (
a=1,r-1,r
); many expressions were known previously, in particular in
Ferrando et al. 2021
, but expressions involving fermionic representations (
a=r-1,r
) with
s>1
are new. Arguments from representation theory, notably sign-free decompositions (
52
) and (
56
), suggest that the obtained expressions are as compact as they can possibly be. In this respect, more elegant and explicitly
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
-invariant expressions (
43
) seem to be more practical even though they use more Q-functions, especially because computing all the Q-functions is streamlined by (
17
) and (
33
).

Appendix A
Implementing Weyl group action

Let
U
be an invertible linear transformation from the Clifford algebra acting on spinors and
{\mathcal{O}}
an invertible linear transformation acting on vectors. The transformation
\Psi\mapsto U\,\Psi\,,V\mapsto{\mathcal{O}}\,V
is a symmetry of the Q-system if it preserves inner products (
12
) and if
U
and
{\mathcal{O}}
are mutually compatible. To preserve the inner product means that
{\mathcal{O}}\in\mathsf{O}(2r)
while
U
must satisfy
U^{T}CU=C
. Compatibility requires that
U^{-1}\Gamma^{i}U={\mathcal{O}}^{i}{}_{j}\Gamma^{j}
. The two constraints on
U
imply that
U
is an element of
\mathsf{Pin}(2r)
and hence we can consider
\mathsf{Pin}(2r)
as our global symmetry group.

Weyl group
{\mathsf{W}}
is the symmetry group acting on the space of weights, it preserves the set of weights for any finite-dimensional representation of
{\mathfrak{so}}_{2r}
. If
{\mathsf{w}}\in{\mathsf{W}}
, we call
\hat{\mathsf{w}}\in\mathsf{Pin}(2r)
a representative of
{\mathsf{w}}
if, in any given representation
L(\omega)
the following holds:

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{w}}\cdot{\mathfrak{e}}_{\gamma}=\#\,{\mathfrak{e}}_{{\mathsf{w}}\cdot\gamma}\,,

(77)

where
{\mathfrak{e}}_{\gamma}\in L(\omega)
is a basis vector of weight
\gamma
and
\#
is a numerical constant that can depend on
{\mathsf{w}}
and
\gamma
. Except in special cases, it is impossible to set all such constants to identity or to make
\hat{\mathsf{w}}
a homomorphism from
{\mathsf{W}}
to
\mathsf{Pin}(2r)
. Nevertheless, since
\hat{\mathsf{w}}\in\mathsf{Pin}(2r)
,
\hat{\mathsf{w}}
is obviously a symmetry transformation of the extended Q-system and therefore, allowing some freedom of speech, we say the Weyl group is the symmetry of the extended Q-system as well. This Weyl symmetry is another important organisational principle of the extended Q-system, note for instance its role in the original QQ-relations (
2
). To work with it, it is pertinent to fix
\#
. There are prescriptions with simple choices of
\#
, and the goal of this appendix is to explicitly write them down. It is enough to do it for minuscule representations, the rest follows easily.

One choice for fixing
\#
was proposed in
Ekhammar et al. 2020
using general Lie algebra formalism: For
{\mathsf{s}}_{a}
being the elementary Weyl reflection, its representative is picked as
\hat{\mathsf{s}}_{a}=e^{e_{a}}e^{-f_{a}}e^{e_{a}}
where
e_{a}
and
f_{a}
are Chevalley generators. For the spinor representation we take, cf. Section
III.1
,
e_{a}=\theta^{a+1}\partial_{a}\,,f_{a}=\theta^{a}\partial_{a+1}
for
a<r
and
e_{r}=\partial_{r}\partial_{r-1}\,,f_{r}=\theta^{r-1}\theta^{r}
. Then, when acting on basis vectors of fermionic representations, the explicit action computes to

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{s}}_{a}\cdot\theta^{A,a}

\displaystyle=\theta^{A,a+1}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{s}}_{a}\cdot\theta^{A,a+1}

\displaystyle=-\theta^{A,a}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{s}}_{r}\cdot\theta^{B}

\displaystyle=\theta^{B,r-1,r}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{s}}_{r}\cdot\theta^{B,r-1,r}

\displaystyle=-\theta^{B}\,,

(78a)

and the consistent transformation for basis vectors for vector representation is

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{s}}_{a}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{\pm a}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{e}_{\pm(a+1)}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{s}}_{a}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{\pm(a+1)}

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{e}_{\pm a}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{s}}_{r}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{\pm(r-1)}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{e}_{\mp r}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{s}}_{r}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{\pm r}

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{e}_{\mp(r-1)}\,,

(78b)

In these formulae,
a=1,2,\ldots,r-1
,
A
does not contain
a
and
a+1
and
B
does not contain
r
and
r-1
; only nontrivial transformations were written down.

It is useful to tabulate more transformations than just the elementary Weyl reflections. We will here describe explicitly permutations of any two indices dubbed
{\mathsf{w}}_{ab}
and sign swaps dubbed
{\mathsf{w}}_{a}
. Their action on the orthonormal basis of the weight space is

\displaystyle{\mathsf{w}}_{ab}\cdot\varepsilon_{a}=\varepsilon_{b}\,,\quad{\mathsf{w}}_{ab}\cdot\varepsilon_{b}=\varepsilon_{a}\,,\quad{\mathsf{w}}_{ab}\cdot\varepsilon_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{c}\,,\ c\neq a,b\,,

(79)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{w}}_{a}\cdot\varepsilon_{a}=-\varepsilon_{a}\,,\quad\quad\quad\quad{\mathsf{w}}_{a}\cdot\varepsilon_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{b}\,,\ b\neq a\,.

(80)

Reflections
{\mathsf{w}}_{a}
are not elements of Weyl group, their products
{\mathsf{w}}_{a}{\mathsf{w}}_{b}
are. Nevertheless,
{\mathsf{w}}_{a}
are symmetries for weights in all vector representations and for Dirac spinor, so considering them is still useful.

Note that
{\mathsf{s}}_{a}={\mathsf{w}}_{a,a+1}
for
a<r
and
{\mathsf{s}}_{r}={\mathsf{w}}_{r-1,r}{\mathsf{w}}_{r-1}{\mathsf{w}}_{r}
. In
Ekhammar et al. 2020
, defining
\hat{\mathsf{w}}
for
{\mathsf{w}}
other than
{\mathsf{s}}_{a}
was based on representing
{\mathsf{w}}
as a minimal length product of
{\mathsf{s}}_{a}
. Given specifics of
\mathfrak{so}_{2r}
, it is more suitable to use a different convention for
\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{ab}
and
\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{a}
which symmetrically treats indices, this means that sign prescriptions in
\#
shall be not always the same as implied by the recipe of
Ekhammar et al. 2020
.

For permutations, one can pick
\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{ab}=\Gamma^{b}\Gamma_{a}-\Gamma^{a}\Gamma_{b}+\Gamma^{b}\Gamma^{a}\Gamma_{a}\Gamma_{b}+\Gamma_{a}\Gamma_{b}\Gamma^{b}\Gamma^{a}
. On the level of components this translates into

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{ab}:\Psi_{Aab}\mapsto\Psi_{Aab}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{ab}:\Psi_{Aa}\mapsto-\Psi_{Ab}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{ab}:\Psi_{Ab}\mapsto\Psi_{Aa}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{ab}:\Psi_{A}\mapsto\Psi_{A}\,,

(81a)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{ab}:V^{\pm a}\mapsto-V^{\pm b}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{ab}:V^{\pm b}\mapsto V^{\pm a}\,.

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{ab}:V^{i}\rightarrow V^{i}\,,

(81b)

where
A
does not contain
a,-a
and
i\neq\pm a,b
.

For sign flips, a good choice is
\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{a}=(-1)^{N}(\Gamma_{a}-\Gamma^{a})
where
(-1)^{N}\theta^{A}=(-1)^{|A|}\theta^{A}
and explicitly in Gamma-matrices
(-1)^{N}=2^{r}\,\Gamma_{1}{}^{1}\dots\Gamma_{r}{}^{r}
. The action on components is

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{a}:\Psi_{A}\mapsto\Psi_{Aa}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{a}:\Psi_{Aa}\mapsto\Psi_{A}\,.

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{a}:V^{a}\mapsto V_{a}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{a}:V_{a}\mapsto V^{a}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{a}:V^{i}\mapsto V^{i}\,,

(82)

where
A
does not contain
a
and
i\neq\pm a
.

We note that clearly the action on
V^{i}
is a reflection which is in the disconnected component from identity of
\mathsf{O}(2r)
. Another remark is that
(-1)^{N}=2^{r}\,\Gamma_{1}{}^{1}\dots\Gamma_{r}{}^{r}
is recognisable as the chiral gamma-matrix
\Gamma^{5}
, however in the chosen normalisation it is not an element of
\mathsf{Pin}(2r)
, the correctly normalised option is
\Gamma^{5}=e^{-\frac{i\pi}{2}r}(-1)^{N}
; under the map
\mathsf{Pin}(2r)\to\mathsf{O}(2r)
,
\Gamma^{5}
and
-\Gamma^{5}
have their image in the connected component
\mathsf{SO}(2r)
as
-{\rm Id}
which is nothing but the space inversion
V^{i}\mapsto-V^{i}
.

Prescription (
81a
) is compatible with (
78
) for what concerns the fundamental reflections
{\mathsf{s}}_{a}
, however this comes at price that
\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{ba}=\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{ab}^{-1}\neq\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{ab}
. If not to insist on (
78
), a more symmetric prescription is possible with
\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{ab}=\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{ba}={\mathsf{i}}(\Gamma^{a}\Gamma_{b}+\Gamma^{b}\Gamma_{a}+\Gamma^{a}\Gamma^{b}\Gamma_{a}\Gamma_{b}+\Gamma_{a}\Gamma_{b}\Gamma^{b}\Gamma^{a})
which explicitly translates in the action on components as

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{ab}:\Psi_{Aab}\mapsto-{\mathsf{i}}\,\Psi_{Aab}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{ab}:\Psi_{Aa}\mapsto{\mathsf{i}}\,\Psi_{Ab}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{ab}:\Psi_{Ab}\mapsto{\mathsf{i}}\,\Psi_{Aa}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{ab}:\Psi_{A}\mapsto{\mathsf{i}}\,\Psi_{A}\,,

(83a)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{ab}:V^{\pm a}\mapsto V^{\pm b}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{ab}:V^{\pm b}\mapsto V^{\pm a}\,.

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{w}}_{ab}:V^{i}\rightarrow V^{i}\,.

(83b)

For what concerns vectors, all
\#
factors in this prescription are set to
1
, so (
83
) and (
82
) realise an embedding of
{\mathsf{W}}
as subgroup of
\mathsf{SO}(2r)
, and
{\mathsf{W}}
adjoined with
{\mathsf{w}}_{a}
is embedded in
\mathsf{O}(2r)
.

The discussed Weyl symmetry properties allow for further comparison with results of
Ferrando et al. 2021
. Most of the relations there enjoy both
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{r}
covariance and covariance with respect to Weyl group action. This is expected as
Ferrando et al. 2021
works with a Q-system on the Weyl orbit. In hindsight, as we have typically the same covariance structures, many of our expressions have a counterpart in
Ferrando et al. 2021
, note however that some of the relations in
Ferrando et al. 2021
were not derived but conjectured for arbitrary rank, for instance an equivalent of fused orthogonality (

). To relate formulae between the two papers, we note that spinor Q-functions are denoted in

as
S_{\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}\}}
, and the relation to
\Psi_{A}
is the following:
\Psi_{A}=S_{\{A^{\prime}\bar{A}\}}
, where
A^{\prime}
means a multi-index with all entries having swapped signs, and
{\bar{A}}
means the multi-index with entries from the complementary set. Q-functions for the tensor nodes of Dynkin diagram are identified as
V^{I}\ ({\rm here})=Q_{I}\ ({\rm in}\ \text{\cite[cite]{\@@bibref{Authors Phrase1YearPhrase2}{Ferrando:2020vzk}{\@@citephrase{(}}{\@@citephrase{)}}}})
. The offered identifications might need an adjustment in normalisations:
Ferrando et al. 2021
tends to take twist variables (
\tau_{a,{\rm in\ [8]}}=x_{a,{\rm here}}
) and source terms of Bethe equations outside of Q-functions whereas we pack them all inside, in particular through the dressing factors, see Section 4.3 of
Ekhammar et al. 2020
.

Among the most notable identifications with
Ferrando et al. 2021
, we mention that (5.10) there is the same as our (
25
) when
I
in
Q_{I}
has only positive entries. A particular case

) present in

The key difference with
Ferrando et al. 2021

\Psi_{A}
are coordinates of pure spinors. This observation, originally from

\Psi_{\emptyset},\Psi_{a},\Psi_{ab}

symmetry of the formalism.

Technical details

Proof of Lemma

Due to rescaling invariance of equations and condition
\Psi_{\emptyset}\neq 0

\Psi_{\emptyset}=1

We need to prove that
\Psi_{(k)}

\Psi_{(k)}

\Psi_{(k^{\prime})}

By the nature of equation (

|Aab|=k=r
, so
\Psi_{(k)}
is the top form and
\Psi_{Aab}=*\Psi_{(k)}\,\epsilon_{Aab}

\displaystyle W(*\Psi_{(k)},*({\bf a}\wedge{\bf b}\wedge\Psi_{(k-2)}))=W(*({\bf a}\wedge\Psi_{(k-1)}),*({\bf b}\wedge\Psi_{(k-1)}))

(84)

that should hold for arbitrary vectors
{\bf a},{\bf b}

\displaystyle*({\bf v}_{1}\wedge{\bf v}_{2}\ldots\wedge{\bf v}_{k})\,{\bf a}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}*({\bf v}_{1}\wedge{\bf v}_{2}\ldots\wedge\underset{i{\rm-th\ position}}{{\bf a}}\wedge\ldots\wedge{\bf v}_{k})\,{\bf v}_{i}\,.

(85)

Details of the computation depends on the parity of

Even case,
k=2n+2
.
First, derive the important technical result:

\displaystyle\Psi_{(2n+2)}^{+}-\Psi_{(2n+2)}^{-}=(\Psi_{(2)}^{+}-\Psi_{(2)}^{-})\wedge\Psi_{(2n)}^{\pm}=\Psi_{(1)}^{+}\wedge\Psi_{(1)}^{-}\wedge\Psi_{(2n)}^{\pm}.

(86)

Derivation is done by induction in

\Psi_{(2)}^{+}-\Psi_{(2)}^{-}=\Psi_{(1)}^{+}\wedge\Psi_{(1)}^{-}

\Psi_{(1)}^{\pm}\wedge(\Psi_{(1)}^{+}\wedge\Psi_{(1)}^{-})=0

\Psi_{(1)}^{\pm}\wedge\Psi_{(2n)}^{+}=\Psi_{(1)}^{\pm}\wedge\Psi_{(2n)}^{-}
because
\Psi_{(2n)}=\frac{1}{n!}\Psi_{(2)}^{n}
, this explains the possibility to choose an arbitrary shift sign in (

For

\displaystyle\Psi_{(2n+2)}^{+}=\frac{1}{n+1}\Psi_{(2)}^{+}\wedge\Psi_{(2n)}^{+}=\frac{1}{n+1}\Psi_{(2)}^{+}\wedge\Psi_{(2n)}^{-}+\frac{1}{n+1}(\Psi_{(2)}^{+}-\Psi_{(2)}^{-})\wedge\Psi_{(2n-2)}^{\pm}\wedge{\Psi_{(2)}^{\pm}}

\displaystyle=\Psi_{(2n+2)}^{-}+\frac{1}{n+1}(\Psi_{(2)}^{+}-\Psi_{(2)}^{-})\wedge\Psi_{(2n)}^{-}+\frac{n}{n+1}(\Psi_{(2)}^{+}-\Psi_{(2)}^{-})\wedge\Psi_{(2n)}^{\pm}=\Psi_{(2n+2)}^{-}+(\Psi_{(2)}^{+}-\Psi_{(2)}^{-})\wedge\Psi_{(2n)}^{\pm}\,.

We use (
86
) to rewrite the l.h.s. of (

)

\displaystyle W(*\Psi_{(2n+2)},*({\bf a}\wedge{\bf b}\wedge\Psi_{(2n)}))=*(\Psi_{(1)}^{+}\wedge\Psi_{(1)}^{-}\wedge\Psi_{(2n)}^{\pm})\,*\!({\bf a}\wedge{\bf b}\wedge\Psi_{(2n)}^{-})-*\Psi_{(2n+2)}^{-}\,*\!({\bf a}\wedge{\bf b}\wedge\Psi_{(1)}^{+}\wedge\Psi_{(1)}^{-}\wedge\Psi_{(2n-2)}^{\pm})\,,

(87)

and in what follows we pick the negative shift
\Psi^{-}
in both places where
\Psi^{\pm}
appears.

Consider now
\Psi_{ab}^{-}
as components of an anti-symmetric matrix. Anti-symmetric matrices of even dimension have eigenvalues coming in pairs, where in each pair the two eigenvalues sum up to zero. Denote hence the eigenvalues by
\lambda_{\pm\alpha}\equiv\pm\lambda_{\alpha}
and the corresponding eigenvectors as
\Upsilon_{\pm\alpha}
. Note that two eigenvectors
\Upsilon_{\alpha}
and
\Upsilon_{\beta}
are orthogonal (with naive metric) if
\alpha+\beta\neq 0
. With these remarks, it is easy to conclude that, with an appropriate normalisation of the eigenvectors, one can represent
\Psi_{(2)}^{-}=\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{n+1}\Upsilon_{\alpha}\wedge\Upsilon_{-\alpha}
. From here

\displaystyle\Psi_{(2n+2)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\gamma}\Upsilon_{\gamma}\wedge\Upsilon_{-\gamma}\,,

\displaystyle\Psi_{(2n)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}\prod_{\gamma\neq\alpha}\Upsilon_{\gamma}\wedge\Upsilon_{-\gamma}\,,

\displaystyle\Psi_{(2n-2)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha<\beta}\prod_{\gamma\neq\alpha,\beta}\Upsilon_{\gamma}\wedge\Upsilon_{-\gamma}\,.

We substitute these expressions into (
87
) and employ Plücker relations: for vector
{\bf a}
in the first term and for vector
{\bf b}
in the second term. All the unwanted terms (those coming from the exchange of
{\bf a}
or
{\bf b}
with
\Upsilon_{\pm\gamma}
) shall cancel and what remains is precisely the r.h.s. of (
84
), if we use
\Psi_{(2n+1)}^{+}=\Psi_{(1)}^{+}\wedge\Psi_{(2n)}^{\pm}
and
\Psi_{(2n+1)}^{-}=\Psi_{(1)}^{-}\wedge\Psi_{(2n)}^{\pm}
.

Odd case,
k=2n+1
.
Using the above-established properties, we rewrite both sides of (
84
) as follows

\displaystyle W(*\Psi_{(2n+1)},*({\bf a}\wedge{\bf b}\wedge\Psi_{(2n-1)}))

\displaystyle=*(\Psi_{(1)}^{+}\wedge\Psi_{(2n)}^{-})\,*\!({\bf a}\wedge{\bf b}\wedge\Psi_{(1)}^{-}\wedge\Psi_{(2n-2)}^{-})-*(\Psi_{(1)}^{-}\wedge\Psi_{(2n)}^{-})\,*\!({\bf a}\wedge{\bf b}\wedge\Psi_{(1)}^{+}\wedge\Psi_{(2n-2)}^{-})\,,

(88a)

\displaystyle W(*({\bf a}\wedge\Psi_{(2n)}),*({\bf b}\wedge\Psi_{(2n)}))

\displaystyle=*({\bf a}\wedge\Psi_{(1)}^{+}\wedge\Psi_{(1)}^{-}\wedge\Psi_{(2n-2)}^{-})\,*\!({\bf b}\wedge\Psi_{(2n)}^{-})-*({\bf a}\wedge\Psi_{(2n)}^{-})\,*\!({\bf b}\wedge\Psi_{(1)}^{+}\wedge\Psi_{(1)}^{-}\wedge\Psi_{(2n-2)}^{-})\,.

(88b)

In odd
(2n+1)
-dimensions, a two-form can be represented as
\Psi_{(2)}^{-}=\sum\limits_{\alpha=1}^{n}\Upsilon_{\alpha}\wedge\Upsilon_{-\alpha}
, here one eigenvector of
\Psi_{ab}^{-}
does not participate in the sum, the one with zero eigenvalue which is orthogonal to all other eigenvectors. Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{(2n)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\gamma}\Upsilon_{\gamma}\wedge\Upsilon_{-\gamma}\,,

\displaystyle\Psi_{(2n-2)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha}\prod_{\gamma\neq\alpha}\Upsilon_{\gamma}\wedge\Upsilon_{-\gamma}\,.

We substitute these expressions to both lines of (
88
) and then demonstrate that these lines are equal by employing Plücker identity in the first line, with vector
{\bf a}
for the first summand and vector
{\bf b}
for the second. This accomplishes the proof that Ansatz (
17
) solves (
35
).

To demonstrate that (
17
) is the unique solution, assume the opposite: let there exists
\Psi_{(k)}^{\prime}
that is different from
\Psi_{(k)}
and that also solves (
35
). Consider the smallest
k
for which such feature holds. Construct
\delta\Psi_{(k)}=\Psi_{(k)}^{\prime}-\Psi_{(k)}
. Since
k\geq 3
we can parameterise the components of
\delta\Psi_{(k)}
as
\delta\Psi_{Aabc}
. As both
\Psi_{(k)}^{\prime}
and
\Psi_{(k)}
solve (
35
), it must be that
W(\delta\Psi_{Aabc},\Psi_{Aa})=0
and so
\delta\Psi_{Aabc}=h_{bc}\Psi_{Aa}
, where
h_{bc}^{+}=h_{bc}^{-}
. On the other hand,
\delta\Psi_{Aabc}
is anti-symmetric in its indices and so it must hold
\delta\Psi_{Aabc}=h_{ca}\Psi_{Ab}
. Then
0=W(\delta\Psi_{Aabc},\Psi_{Aa})=h_{ca}^{\pm}\,W(\Psi_{Ab},\Psi_{Aa})
and we arrive at contradiction with the assumption of the lemma.
∎

Derivation of (
9b
) for the proof of Theorem
2
.

Recall that by definition
V^{i_{1}\dots i_{r}}=W(V^{i_{1}},\dots,V^{i_{r}})
, our aim is to establish the identification
V^{i_{1}\dots i_{r}}=\Psi^{-}C\Gamma^{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}}\Psi^{+}
. The calculation will be divided into 3 cases: only positive indices, one negative index, and two or more negative indices.

We start with the case when all indices are positive, then, using the definition of
\Gamma
and
C
, we find
\Psi^{-}C\Gamma^{1\dots r}\Psi^{+}=\Psi_{\emptyset}^{[2]_{D}}
. It is also true that
V^{1\dots r}=\Psi_{\emptyset}^{[2]_{D}}
as follows from
V^{1\dots r}V^{1\dots r-2}=W(V^{12\dots r-1},V^{1\dots,r-2,r})
and
V^{a_{1}\dots a_{r-1}}=\epsilon^{a_{1}\dots a_{r-1}a_{r}}\frac{{{{\bf P}}}_{a_{r}}}{\Phi^{2}}
. We conclude that the highest-weight components agree.

Next, let one superscript in
V^{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}}
be negative, for convenience we will write
V^{Aa}{}_{b}
with
|A|=r-2
. The reason to introduce the extra index
a
is the following
\Gamma
-matrix identity:
\Gamma^{Aa}{}_{b}=\Gamma^{Aa}\Gamma_{b}-\frac{1}{2}\Gamma^{[A}\delta^{a]}_{b}
where
[\,\,]
denotes anti-symmetrisation. Using this we find

\begin{split}\Psi^{-}C\Gamma^{Aa}{}_{b}\Psi^{+}&=\mu^{+}_{bc}V^{Aac}+{{{\bf P}}}^{+}_{b}\Psi^{-}C\Gamma^{Aa}\Psi^{+}-V^{[A}\delta^{a]}_{b}=\mu_{bc}^{+}V^{Aac}+\Psi^{[2]_{D}}_{\emptyset}\epsilon^{Aac}{{{\bf P}}}^{+}_{b}{{{\bf P}}}^{-}_{c}-V^{[A}\delta^{a]}_{b}\,.\end{split}

(89)

Notice that
\Psi_{\emptyset}^{[2]_{D}}\epsilon^{Aac}{{{\bf P}}}_{bc}=V^{B}\epsilon^{Aac}\epsilon_{Bbc}=V^{[A}\delta^{a]}_{b}
so that we have

\Psi^{-}C\Gamma^{Aa}{}_{b}\Psi^{+}=\mu_{bc}^{+}V^{Aac}+\Psi_{\emptyset}^{[2]_{D}}\epsilon^{Aac}{{{\bf P}}}_{c}^{+}{{{\bf P}}}_{b}^{-}\,.

(90)

From the definition of
V^{Aa}{}_{b}
as a Wronskian of vectors
V^{i}
it follows that

V^{Aa}{}_{b}=\mu^{+}_{bc}V^{Aac}-(V^{Aa})^{+}{{{\bf P}}}_{bc}(V^{c})^{[-r+1]}=\mu^{+}_{bc}V^{Aac}+\Psi^{[2]_{D}}_{\emptyset}\epsilon^{Aac}{{{\bf P}}}_{c}^{+}{{{\bf P}}}^{-}_{b}=\Psi^{-}C\Gamma^{Aa}{}_{b}\Psi^{+}\,.

(91)

This completes the case
V^{Aa}{}_{b}
.

Finally we consider the case when two or more indices are lowered, the notation
V^{Aa}{}_{Bbc}
with
|A|+|B|=r-3
will be used. We proceed just as before and start by finding an identity for
\Gamma
-matrices that allows us to raise indices. Multiplying (
26
) from the left with additional differential operators and reordering leads to

\displaystyle\Gamma_{Bbc}\Psi=(\mu_{Bbc,C}\Gamma^{C}+\mu_{[Bb,|D|}{{{\bf P}}}_{c]}\Gamma^{D}\Gamma^{+}-\mu_{[B,|E|}\mu_{bc]}\Gamma^{E})\Psi+\dots\,,

(92)

where all suppressed terms contain
\Gamma
-matrices with less than
|B|
indices. In the above equation, indices surrounded by
|\,\,|
are not to be anti-symmetrized with the others enclosed by
[\,\,]
. Using this as well as
\Gamma^{Aa}{}_{Bbc}=\Gamma^{Aa}\Gamma_{Bbc}-\frac{1}{2}\Gamma^{[A}{}_{[cB}\delta^{a]}_{b]}+\dots
leads to

\Psi^{-}C\Gamma^{Aa}{}_{Bbc}\Psi^{+}=\mu^{+}_{Bbc,C}V^{AaC}+\mu^{+}_{[Bb,|D|}{{{\bf P}}}^{+}_{c]}\Psi^{-}C\Gamma^{AaD}\Psi^{+}-V^{Aa}{}_{[B}\mu^{+}_{bc]}-V^{[A}{}_{[cB}\delta^{a]}_{b]}\,.

(93)

All terms previously omitted in (
92
) have now dropped out due to the projection relations. The second term can be simplified according to
\mu^{+}_{[Bb,|D|}{{{\bf P}}}^{+}_{c]}\Psi^{-}C\Gamma^{AaD}\Psi^{+}=\frac{\Psi^{+}_{\emptyset}}{\Psi^{-}_{\emptyset}}\mu^{+}_{[Bb,|D|}{{{\bf P}}}_{c]}V^{AaD}_{-}=\Psi_{\emptyset}^{[2]_{D}}\epsilon^{AaDd}\mu^{+}_{[Bb,|D|}{{{\bf P}}}^{+}_{c]}{{{\bf P}}}^{-}_{d}
. Notice that

\Psi_{\emptyset}^{[2]_{D}}\epsilon^{AaDd}\mu^{+}_{[Bb,|D|}{{{\bf P}}}_{c]d}=\epsilon^{AaDd}\mu^{+}_{[Bb,|D}\epsilon_{F|c]d}V^{F}=V^{Aa}{}_{[B}\mu^{+}_{bc]}+V^{[A}{}_{[cB}\delta^{a]}_{b]}\,.

(94)

So that in the end one can write

\Psi^{-}C\Gamma^{Aa}{}_{Bbc}\Psi^{+}=\mu^{+}_{Bbc,C}V^{AaC}+\Psi_{\emptyset}^{[2]_{D}}\epsilon^{AaDd}\mu^{+}_{[Bb,|D|}{{{\bf P}}}^{+}_{c]}{{{\bf P}}}^{-}_{d}\,.

(95)

From the definition of
V^{Aa}{}_{Bbc}
as Wronskian determinant of vectors, one can calculate

\begin{split}V^{Aa}{}_{Bbc}&=\mu^{+}_{Bbc,C}V^{AaC}-(V^{Aa}{}_{[Bb})^{+}{{{\bf P}}}_{c]d}(V^{d})^{[-r+1]}=\mu^{+}_{Bbc,C}V^{AaC}+\epsilon^{AaDd}\Psi_{\emptyset}^{[2]_{D}}\mu^{+}_{[Bb,|D|}{{{\bf P}}}^{-}_{c]}{{{\bf P}}}_{d}^{+}\end{split}

(96)

showing that indeed
\Psi^{-}C\Gamma^{Aa}{}_{Bbc}\Psi^{+}=V^{Aa}{}_{Bbc}
.

∎

Proof of Lemma
5
.

By rescaling invariance, we can replace
\Psi_{(k)}
with
\psi_{(k)}
in (
17
) and so all
\psi_{A}
with
|A|>2
are rational combinatinos of polynomials
\psi_{0},\psi_{a},\psi_{ab}
, with only
\psi_{0}
in the denominators. Hence the only potential way to violate analytic Bethe Ansatz for fermionic Q-functions is to get a pole in
\psi_{(k)}
located at a zero of
\psi_{0}
.

For forms of odd rank, one has
\psi_{(2n+1)}^{+}\propto\psi_{(1)}^{+}\wedge\left(\left(\frac{\psi_{(2)}}{\psi_{0}}\right)^{n}\right)^{\pm}
and therefore if
\psi_{(2n+1)}^{+}
has a pole at a point
u^{*}
, it must be that both
\psi_{0}^{+}
and
\psi_{0}^{-}
vanish at this point or in other words
\psi_{0}
has zeros at
u^{*}+{\mathsf{i}}/2
and
u^{*}-{\mathsf{i}}/2
.

For forms of even rank, consider (
86
) and restore
\Psi_{\emptyset}
in it. Rewriting it for polynomial functions, one gets

\displaystyle\psi_{(2n+2)}^{+}\psi_{0}^{-}-\psi_{(2n+2)}^{-}\psi_{0}^{+}\propto\psi_{(1)}^{+}\wedge\psi_{(1)}^{-}\wedge\left(\left(\frac{\psi_{(2)}}{\psi_{0}}\right)^{n}\right)^{\pm}\,.

(97)

If
\psi_{(2n+2)}^{+}
has a pole but
\psi_{(2n+2)}^{+}\psi_{0}^{-}
does not this means that
\psi_{0}^{+}
and
\psi_{0}^{-}
have zeros at the same point. The same applies to the second term on the l.h.s. of the above relation. If both terms on the l.h.s. have a pole at the same point, we again come to the same conclusion about zeros of
\psi_{0}^{+}
and
\psi_{0}^{-}
. Finally, if at a given point only one term on the l.h.s. has a pole then the r.h.s. has a pole as well and we come back to the argument used for
\psi_{(2n+1)}^{+}
.

For vector Q-functions, the analytic Bethe Ansatz reads
V^{I}\propto\sigma_{|I|}v^{I}
, where
v^{I}
are polynomials. For the case of positive indices only, the polynomiality of
v^{A}
is ensured by (
72
). For arbitrary
I
, we use
v_{A}{}^{B}=\mu_{AA^{\prime}}^{[m]}v^{A^{\prime}B}
. Notice now that
\mu_{ab}=\frac{\Psi_{ab}}{\Psi_{\emptyset}}=\frac{\psi_{ab}}{\psi_{0}}
is actually a rational function of the spectral parameter with only possible poles at zeros of
\psi_{0}
, and notice that there are at least two different admissible values of
m
separated by
1
for
|A|+B|\leq r-2
which covers all the cases of tensor
Q_{(a)}^{\mathfrak{i}}
on Dynkin diagram. By varying
m
, we get again to the conclusion that poles in
v^{I}
—the only way to violate analytic Bethe Ansatz in the setting of the lemma—would imply existence of zeros in
\psi_{0}
separated by
{\mathsf{i}}
.
∎

The eighth moment of Dirichlet L L -functions II
Vorrapan Chandee, Xiannan Li, Kaisa Matomäki, Maksym Radziwiłł

Abstract
. We prove an asymptotic formula for the eighth moment of Dirichlet L L -functions averaged over primitive characters χ \chi modulo q q , over all moduli q ≤ Q q\leq Q and with a short average on the critical line. Previously the same result was shown conditionally on the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis by the first two authors.

11M06

1.
Introduction

Moments of
L
-functions have attracted a great deal of attention. Not only do they have numerous applications, but they have also their own intrinsic interest. The first moments studied were naturally those of the Riemann zeta function, which are averages of the form

I_{k}(T):=\int_{0}^{T}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2k}dt.

An asymptotic formula for
I_{k}(T)
was proven for
k=1
by Hardy and Littlewood and for
k=2
by Ingham (see e.g.
[
12
, Chapter VII]
). Despite considerable effort, such an asymptotic formula is still not known for any other value of
k
.

The situation for other
L
-functions is very similar; asymptotics are only available for small values of
k
, and often only when averaged over a suitable family. In case of Dirichlet
L
-functions, Conrey, Iwaniec and Soundararajan
[
4
]
have proven an asymptotic formula for the sixth moment with an averaging over characters
\chi\textup{ (mod $q$)}
, over all moduli
q\leq Q
and with a short average on the critical line. The first two authors
[
1
]
have shown a similar asymptotic formula for the eighth moment, conditionally on the GRH. The aim of this paper is to provide an unconditional proof of this eighth moment result.

\chi\textup{ (mod $q$)}
be a primitive even

2.
A sketch of the proof

In this section we provide a sketch of the proof where we ignore various technicalities such as complicated smooth weights, the inclusion-exclusion within the orthogonality over primitive characters and a number of coprimality conditions and common divisors.

Roughly speaking, after applying the approximate functional equation, we need to understand sums of the form

(3)

Q\sum_{q}\Psi\left(\frac{q}{Q}\right)\sum_{m\leq Q^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq Q^{2}\\
m\equiv n\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\frac{\tau_{4}(m)\tau_{4}(n)}{\sqrt{mn}},

where
\tau_{4}(n)=\sum_{n=n_{1}n_{2}n_{2}n_{4}}1.
Here we were able to make the restriction
m,n\leq Q^{2}
instead of just
mn\leq Q^{4}
thanks to the
t
-average in the theorem.

The diagonal contribution
m=n
in (
3
) is fairly easy to understand, and in this sketch we shall concentrate on the non-diagonal contribution. Let
\varepsilon_{0}>0
be small but fixed. Following
[
1
]
the sums over
m
and
n
in (
3
) can be truncated to

m,n\leq N:=\frac{Q^{2}}{\exp((\log Q)^{\varepsilon_{0}})}

using the multiplicative large sieve. Still following
[
1
]
(and
[
4
]
) we apply in the most critical range the complementary divisor trick. That is we write
m-n=hq
in (
3
) and replace the congruence condition modulo
q
with a congruence condition modulo
h
. Note that
h\ll N/q
is smaller than
Q
, so we have a reduction in the arithmetic conductor.

After switching to the complementary divisor, we express the congruence condition modulo
h
using characters modulo
h
, so that roughly we want to study

(4)

Q\sum_{h\leq 2N/Q}\frac{1}{\phi(h)}\sum_{\chi\textup{(mod $h$)}}\sum_{m,n}\frac{\tau_{4}(m)\tau_{4}(n)\chi(m)\overline{\chi(n)}}{\sqrt{mn}}\Psi\left(\frac{|m-n|}{hQ}\right)\Psi_{1}\left(\frac{m}{N}\right)\Psi_{1}\left(\frac{n}{N}\right),

where
\Psi_{1}
is smooth and supported on
[0,1]
.

The principal characters give a main term contribution. In this sketch we concentrate on the non-principal characters. The smooth factor

h(m,n):=\Psi\left(\frac{|m-n|}{hQ}\right)\Psi_{1}\left(\frac{m}{N}\right)\Psi_{1}\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)

restricts
m
and
n
to being within distance
2hQ
from each other. Morally, the short interval type condition
|m-n|\ll 2hQ
introduces an archimedean conductor of size
T=\frac{N}{hQ}
. The hybrid conductor is then
hT\asymp\frac{N}{Q}
, and this is still smaller than the original conductor
Q
. It is important that the sums of length
N\asymp\frac{Q^{2}}{\exp((\log Q)^{\varepsilon_{0}})}
are long compared to the hybrid conductor. In particular, applying Fourier analysis to such a sum with
\tau_{4}(n)
as the coefficient produces dual sums of length
\frac{(N/Q)^{4}}{N}\asymp\frac{N^{3}}{Q^{4}}\ll\frac{Q^{2}}{\exp(3(\log Q)^{\varepsilon_{0}})}
, and this is shorter than the length of the original sum
N=\frac{Q^{2}}{\exp((\log Q)^{\varepsilon_{0}})}
. Actually for technical convenience we use the approximate functional equation rather than the functional equation, and this gives us sums of length
\left(\frac{N}{Q}\right)^{2}\asymp\frac{Q^{2}}{\exp(2(\log Q)^{\varepsilon_{0}})}
, which still suffices. This is the main motivation for the more technical arguments that follow.

To be more precise, we will reduce our problem to that of bounding a mean square of a corresponding Dirichlet series, in the spirit of arithmetic problems on almost all short intervals (see e.g.
[
5
, Lemma 9.3]
).
Indeed, one can show that the Mellin transform

(5)

\widetilde{h}(s_{1},s_{2})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}h(x,y)x^{s_{1}}y^{s_{2}}\frac{dy}{y}\frac{dx}{x}

converges for
\textup{Re }s_{i}>0
and satisfies, for
\textup{Re }s_{i}\in(0,100)
,

(6)

\widetilde{h}(s_{1},s_{2})\ll\frac{1}{\textup{Re }s_{1}\cdot\textup{Re }s_{2}}\left(\frac{N}{hQ}\right)^{k-1}\frac{1}{\max\{|s_{1}|,|s_{2}|\}^{k}|s_{1}+s_{2}|^{l}}N^{\textup{Re }s_{1}+\textup{Re }s_{2}}

for any integers
k\geq 1
and
l\geq 0
. In showing this, one can assume
|s_{1}|\geq|s_{2}|
, and in this case (
6
) follows by applying in (
5
) first partial integration
k
times with respect to
x
, then substituting
y=xz
and finally applying partial integration
l
times with respect to
x
. A similar argument with our more complicated weight function can be found from the proof of Lemma
6.3
.

Now by the Mellin inversion, the non-principal characters contribute to (
4
)

Q\sum_{h\leq 2N/Q}\frac{1}{\phi(h)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\textup{(mod $h$)}\\
\chi\neq\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}\int_{(1/2+\varepsilon)}\int_{(1/2+\varepsilon)}L(1/2+s_{1},\chi)^{4}L(1/2+s_{2},\overline{\chi})^{4}\widetilde{h}(s_{1},s_{2})ds_{1}ds_{2}.

Moving the contours close to
\textup{Re }s_{i}=0
, plugging in the bound (
6
) for
\widetilde{h}(s_{1},s_{2})
, and using the inequality
xy\leq|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}
, this can be seen to be essentially

\ll Q\sum_{h\leq 2N/Q}\frac{1}{\phi(h)}\sum_{\chi\textup{(mod $h$)}}\frac{hQ}{N}\int_{-N/(hQ)}^{N/(hQ)}\left|L(1/2+it,\chi)\right|^{8}dt.

Hence our task more-or-less reduces to showing that, for any
H\leq N/Q
and
C\geq 1
, we have

(7)

\frac{Q^{2}}{N}\sum_{h\sim H}\sum_{\chi\textup{(mod $h$)}}\int_{-T}^{T}\left|L(1/2+it,\chi)\right|^{8}dt\ll\frac{Q^{2}}{(\log Q)^{C}}\quad\text{for $T=N/(HQ)$}.

Here and later the notation
h\sim H
in summations means that
H<h\leq 2H
. Since
HT=N/Q
, the approximate functional equation morally allows us to approximate
|L(1/2+it,\chi)|^{4}
by
|\sum_{n\ll(N/Q)^{2}}\chi(n)\tau_{4}(n)n^{-1/2-it}|
(see Proposition
3.2
below for a rigorous argument) and hence by the large sieve (Lemma
3.1
below) the left hand side of (
7
) can be shown to be

\ll\frac{Q^{2}}{N}\left(H^{2}T+\left(\frac{N}{Q}\right)^{2}\right)(\log N)^{O(1)}\ll\left(QH+N\right)(\log N)^{O(1)}.

We recall that
H\leq N/Q
and
N=\frac{Q^{2}}{\exp((\log Q)^{\varepsilon_{0}})},
so the above is
O(Q^{2}/(\log Q)^{C})
for any
C\geq 1
.

The current paper has a lot in common with
[
1
]
and we freely borrow results from there, so the reader may want to have
[
1
]
at hand. However, if the reader is ready to take those results granted or to work them out, the current paper can be read alone.

Throughout the paper,
\varepsilon
denotes a small positive real
number. Furthermore
\varepsilon_{0}
and
\Delta_{0}
will be fixed positive constants that are chosen later.

3.
Large sieve and upper bounds for moments

Let us first recall the hybrid large sieve (see e.g.
[
9
, Theorem 5.1]
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
T,Q\geq 1
. For any complex coefficients
a_{n}
with
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|<\infty
, one has

\sum_{q\leq Q}\,\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi\ ({\rm mod}\ q)}\frac{q}{\phi(q)}\int_{-T}^{T}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\chi(n)n^{it}\right|^{2}\ll\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(Q^{2}T+n)|a_{n}|^{2}.

In particular, for any
N\geq 1
and any complex coefficients
a_{n}
, one has

\sum_{q\leq Q}\,\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi\ ({\rm mod}\ q)}\frac{q}{\phi(q)}\int_{-T}^{T}\left|\sum_{n\leq N}a_{n}\chi(n)n^{it}\right|^{2}\ll(Q^{2}T+N)\sum_{n\leq N}|a_{n}|^{2}.

The following proposition gives an upper bound for the eight moment of Dirichlet
L
-functions averaged over
\chi,q
and
t
. It will be used in bounding the off-diagonal terms in Section
6.3
.

Proposition 3.2
.

For
Q,T\geq 3
, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{q\leq Q}\sum_{\chi\ ({\rm mod}\ q)}\int_{0}^{T}\left|L\left(\frac{1}{2}+c+it,\chi\right)\right|^{8}dt\ll Q^{2}T^{2}(\log(QT))^{16},

whenever
0\leq c\leq 1/100
.

Remark 3.3
.

In
[
1
]
it was shown that conditionally on GRH, one has

(8)

\sum_{\chi\ ({\rm mod}\ q)}\int_{0}^{T}\left|L\left(\frac{1}{2}+c+it,\chi\right)\right|^{8}dt\ll qT(\log(qT))^{16+\varepsilon}.

The unconditional Proposition
3.2
suffices for us since we will afford to lose a factor
T
due to the decay in
|s_{1}+s_{2}|
coming from Lemma
6.3
below. On the other hand the fact that we need an additional average over
q
in Proposition
3.2
compared to (
8
) will cause us some minor technical difficulties (see in particular Remark
6.5
).

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

The contribution from
q=1
is acceptable by known moment bound for the Riemann zeta function (see Lemma
3.5
below). Moreover, the part with bounded
t
can be easily dealt with using the approximate functional equation and the large sieve (see
[
7
, Theorem 7.34]
, the proof works just as well with
16
in place of
17
).

Hence, by dyadic splitting, it suffices to prove, for
Q\geq 3/2
and
T\geq 3
,

(9)

\sum_{q\sim Q}\sum_{\chi\ ({\rm mod}\ q)}\int_{T}^{2T}\left|L\left(\frac{1}{2}+c+it,\chi\right)\right|^{8}dt\ll Q^{2}T^{2}(\log(QT))^{16}.

As usual, we first reduce to primitive characters; we claim that (
9
) follows once we have shown that

(10)

\sum_{q\sim Q}\ \sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi\ ({\rm mod}\ q)}\int_{T}^{2T}\left|L\left(\frac{1}{2}+c+it,\chi\right)\right|^{8}dt\ll Q^{2}T^{2}(\log(QT))^{16}.

Indeed, the left hand side of (
9
) is

(11)

\begin{split}&\leq\sum_{r\leq Q}\sum_{q_{1}\sim\frac{Q}{r}}\ \sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi_{1}\ ({\rm mod}\ q_{1})}\int_{T}^{2T}\left|L\left(\frac{1}{2}+c+it,\chi_{1}\right)\right|^{8}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|r\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{1/2}}\right)^{8}\>dt\end{split}

Applying (
10
), this is at most

Q^{2}T^{2}(\log(QT))^{16}\sum_{r\leq Q}\frac{1}{r^{2}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|r\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{1/2}}\right)^{8}\ll Q^{2}T^{2}(\log(QT))^{16}

as claimed.
Hence it suffices to prove (
10
). For
\chi
a primitive character mod
q
, by the approximate functional equation we have morally that

\left|L\left(\frac{1}{2}+it,\chi\right)^{4}\right|\ll\left|\sum_{n\ll(TQ)^{2}}\frac{\tau_{4}(n)\chi(n)}{n^{1/2+it}}\right|+\left|\sum_{n\ll(TQ)^{2}}\frac{\tau_{4}(n)\overline{\chi}(n)}{n^{1/2-it}}\right|.

If this was true, the claim (
10
) (for
c=0
) would follow from the hybrid large sieve (Lemma
3.1
). However, there is a technical issue that the Dirichlet polynomials in the approximation depend mildly on
t
and
q
. To proceed rigorously, we use a method of Ramachandra
[
8
]
.

Let us concentrate on the sum in (
10
) over even characters, the odd characters being handled similarly. By the functional equation (
1
), we have

L(\tfrac{1}{2}+s,\chi)^{4}=F(\tfrac{1}{2}+s)L(\tfrac{1}{2}-s,\overline{\chi})^{4}

with

F(\tfrac{1}{2}+s):=\varepsilon_{\chi}^{4}\Big(\frac{\pi}{q}\Big)^{4s}\frac{\Gamma\Big(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{s}{2}\Big)^{4}}{\Gamma\Big(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{s}{2}\Big)^{4}},

where
|\varepsilon_{\chi}|=1
. For further convenience, let us record here Stirling’s formula which gives, for
|\textup{Arg }(z)|<\pi-\varepsilon
,

(12)

\Gamma(z)=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{z}}\left(\frac{z}{e}\right)^{z}\left(1+O(1/z)\right)\ll\frac{|z|^{\textup{Re }z-1/2}}{e^{\textup{Re }z}}\exp(-\textup{Im }z\cdot\textup{Arg }(z)).

In particular, for
\textup{Re }s\in[-1,1/3]
and
|\textup{Im }s|\geq 10
, we have

(13)

\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{s}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{s}{2})}\asymp\frac{\exp(\textup{Re }(-\frac{1}{4}-\frac{s}{2})\log|\frac{1}{4}-\frac{s}{2}|-\textrm{Im}(\frac{-s}{2})\textup{Arg }(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{s}{2}))}{\exp(\textup{Re }(-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{s}{2})\log|\frac{1}{4}+\frac{s}{2}|-\textrm{Im}(\frac{s}{2})\textup{Arg }(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{s}{2}))}

and

\log\left|\frac{1}{4}\pm\frac{s}{2}\right|=\log(|s|+1)+O(1)\quad\text{and}\quad\textup{Arg }\left(\frac{1}{4}\pm\frac{s}{2}\right)=\textup{Arg }\left(\pm\frac{s}{2}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{|s|+1}\right).

Noticing that the left hand side of (
13
) stays bounded when
\textup{Re }s\in[-1,1/3]
and
|\textup{Im }s|<10
, we obtain that, for any
s
with
\textup{Re }s\in[-1,1/3]
,

(14)

\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{s}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{s}{2})}\ll\exp(-\textup{Re }(s)\log(|s|+1))\asymp(|s|+1)^{-\textup{Re }s}.

The starting point in the proof of (
10
) is the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
0\leq c\leq 1/100
, let
\chi
be an even primitive Dirichlet character of modulus
q\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

(15)

\begin{split}L(\tfrac{1}{2}+c+it,\chi)^{4}&=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\tau_{4}(n)\chi(n)e^{-n/X}}{n^{1/2+c+it}}+F(\tfrac{1}{2}+c+it)\sum_{n\leq X}\frac{\tau_{4}(n)\overline{\chi}(n)}{n^{1/2-c-it}}\\
&-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(-3/4)}F(\tfrac{1}{2}+c+it+w)\Big(\sum_{n>X}\frac{\tau_{4}(n)\overline{\chi}(n)}{n^{1/2-w-c-it}}\Big)\Gamma(w)X^{w}dw\\
&-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(1/4)}F(\tfrac{1}{2}+c+it+w)\Big(\sum_{n\leq X}\frac{\tau_{4}(n)\overline{\chi}(n)}{n^{1/2-c-w-it}}\Big)\Gamma(w)X^{w}dw\\
&-\mathbf{1}_{q=1}\operatorname*{Res}_{w=1/2-c-it}\Gamma(w)X^{w}\zeta^{4}\left(\frac{1}{2}+c+it+w\right),\end{split}

where
\mathbf{1}_{q=1}
is
1
if
q=1
, and
0
otherwise.

Proof.

This follows as
[
8
, Theorem 2]
: Notice first that by Mellin inversion (since
\Gamma(s)
is the Mellin transform of the function
e^{-x}
)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\tau_{4}(n)\chi(n)e^{-n/X}}{n^{1/2+c+it}}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(2)}L(\tfrac{1}{2}+c+it+w,\chi)^{4}\Gamma(w)X^{w}dw.

We shift the integral to the line
\textup{Re }w=-\tfrac{3}{4}
, collecting a residue from the pole at
w=0
and in case
q=1
also from a pole at
w=1/2-c-it
. Applying also the functional equation to the integral on the line
\textup{Re }w=-\tfrac{3}{4}
, we see that

\begin{split}&\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\tau_{4}(n)\chi(n)e^{-n/X}}{n^{1/2+c+it}}=L(\tfrac{1}{2}+c+it,\chi)^{4}+\mathbf{1}_{q=1}\operatorname*{Res}_{w=1/2-c-it}\Gamma(w)X^{w}\zeta^{4}\left(\frac{1}{2}+c+it+w\right)\\
&+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(-3/4)}F(\tfrac{1}{2}+c+it+w,\chi)L(\tfrac{1}{2}-c-it-w,\overline{\chi})^{4}\Gamma(w)X^{w}dw.\end{split}

Writing on the last line

L(\tfrac{1}{2}-c-it-w,\overline{\chi})^{4}=\sum_{n\leq X}\frac{\tau_{4}(n)\overline{\chi}(n)}{n^{1/2-c-it-w}}+\sum_{n>X}\frac{\tau_{4}(n)\overline{\chi}(n)}{n^{1/2-c-it-w}},

the claim of the lemma reduces to the claim that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(-3/4)}F(\tfrac{1}{2}+c+it+w,\chi)\sum_{n\leq X}\frac{\tau_{4}(n)\overline{\chi}(n)}{n^{1/2-c-it-w}}\Gamma(w)X^{w}dw\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(1/4)}F(\tfrac{1}{2}+c+it+w,\chi)\sum_{n\leq X}\frac{\tau_{4}(n)\overline{\chi}(n)}{n^{1/2-c-it-w}}\Gamma(w)X^{w}dw\\
&\qquad\qquad-F(\tfrac{1}{2}+c+it,\chi)\sum_{n\leq X}\frac{\tau_{4}(n)\overline{\chi}(n)}{n^{1/2-c-it}}.\end{split}

But this is immediate from shifting the integration line, picking up a residue from a pole at
w=0
.
∎

Let us now return to (
10
) for even characters. Recall
Q\geq 3/2
and
T\geq 3
. We take
X=(QT)^{2}
and apply Lemma
3.4
, writing (
15
) as
L(\tfrac{1}{2}+c+it,\chi)^{4}=(J_{1}+J_{2}-J_{3}-J_{4})(c,t,\chi)
(the fifth term in (

) always vanishes as

, so we do not need to include it). Then it suffices to show that

\sum_{q\sim Q}\ \sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\int_{T}^{2T}|J_{k}(c,t,\chi)|^{2}dt\ll(QT)^{2}(\log(QT))^{16}

for
k=1,2,3,4
. First, by the large sieve (Lemma
3.1
), we have

\begin{split}&\sum_{q\sim Q}\ \sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\int_{T}^{2T}|J_{1}(c,t,\chi)|^{2}dt\ll\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(Q^{2}T+n)\frac{\tau_{4}(n)^{2}e^{-2n/(Q^{2}T^{2})}}{n^{1+2c}}\\
&\ll Q^{2}T^{2}\sum_{n\leq Q^{2}T^{2}}\frac{\tau_{4}(n)^{2}}{n}+\sum_{n>Q^{2}T^{2}}\tau_{4}(n)^{2}e^{-2n/(Q^{2}T^{2})}\ll Q^{2}T^{2}(\log(QT))^{16}.\end{split}

Furthermore, noting that, by (
14
),
|F(\tfrac{1}{2}+c+it)|\ll(q(1+|t|))^{-4c}
, the large sieve (Lemma
3.1
) implies that

\begin{split}\sum_{q\sim Q}\ \sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\int_{T}^{2T}|J_{2}(c,t,\chi)|^{2}dt\ll(QT)^{2-8c}\sum_{n\leq Q^{2}T^{2}}\frac{\tau_{4}(n)^{2}}{n^{1-2c}}\ll Q^{2}T^{2}(\log(QT))^{16}.\end{split}

To deal with the remaining two cases we notice that, by (
14
) and (
12
), we have, for
\textup{Re }w\in\{-3/4,1/4\}
,

|F(\tfrac{1}{2}+it+c+w)|\ll q^{-4\textup{Re }w-4c}\cdot(1+|t+\textup{Im }w|))^{-4\textup{Re }w-4c}\quad\text{and}\quad|\Gamma(w)|\ll e^{-|\textup{Im }w|}.

Splitting into cases according to whether
|\textup{Im }w|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}|t|
or not, we see that

|F(\tfrac{1}{2}+c+it+w)\Gamma(w)(QT)^{2w}|\ll(QT)^{-2\textup{Re }w-4c}e^{-\tfrac{1}{2}|\textup{Im }w|}.

Hence by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, noting the rapid decay in
\textup{Im }w
, and using the large sieve (
3.1
) we have

\begin{split}&\sum_{q\sim Q}\ \sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\int_{T}^{2T}|J_{3}(c,t,\chi)|^{2}dt\\
&\ll(QT)^{3-8c}\sum_{q\sim Q}\ \sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\int_{T}^{2T}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|\sum_{n>Q^{2}T^{2}}\frac{\tau_{4}(n)\overline{\chi}(n)}{n^{5/4-c-it-iu}}\right|e^{-u/2}du\right)^{2}dt\\
&\ll(QT)^{3-8c}\max_{u}\sum_{q\sim Q}\ \sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\sum_{n>Q^{2}T^{2}}\frac{\tau_{4}(n)\overline{\chi}(n)}{n^{5/4-c-it-iu}}\right|^{2}dt\\
&\ll(QT)^{3-8c}\sum_{n>Q^{2}T^{2}}(Q^{2}T+n)\frac{\tau_{4}(n)^{2}}{n^{5/2-2c}}\ll(QT)^{2}(\log(QT))^{16}.\end{split}

Similarly

\begin{split}&\sum_{q\sim Q}\ \sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\int_{T}^{2T}|J_{4}(c,t,\chi)|^{2}dt\\
&\ll(QT)^{-1-8c}\max_{u}\sum_{q\sim Q}\ \sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\pmod{q}\end{subarray}}\int_{T}^{2T}\left|\sum_{n\leq Q^{2}T^{2}}\frac{\tau_{4}(n)\overline{\chi}(n)}{n^{1/4-c-it-iu}}\right|^{2}dt\\
&\ll(QT)^{-1-8c}(Q^{2}T+Q^{2}T^{2})\sum_{n\leq Q^{2}T^{2}}\frac{\tau_{4}(n)^{2}}{n^{1/2-2c}}\ll(QT)^{2}(\log(QT))^{16}.\end{split}

∎

In case of the Riemann zeta function, a better upper bound is available, and it will be helpful in evaluating the main terms:

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
T\geq 3
. Then

(16)

\int_{-T}^{T}|\zeta(1/2+c+it)|^{8}dt\ll T^{3/2}(\log T)^{21/2}

for any
c\geq 0
.

Proof.

Let us first consider the case
c=0
. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\int_{-T}^{T}|\zeta(1/2+it)|^{8}dt\ll\left(\int_{-T}^{T}|\zeta(1/2+it)|^{4}dt\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{-T}^{T}|\zeta(1/2+it)|^{12}dt\right)^{1/2}

Applying upper bounds for the fourth and twelfth power moments of the Riemann zeta function (see e.g.
[
12
, formula (7.6.3)]
for the fourth moment and see
[
6
]
for the twelfth moment), this is

\ll\left(T(\log T)^{4}\right)^{1/2}\cdot\left(T^{2}(\log T)^{17}\right)^{1/2},

and the claim follows in case
c=0
. In case
c\geq 1
the left hand side of (
16
) is trivially bounded by
O(T)
. In the remaining case
c\in(0,1)
the claim follows from a convexity argument (see
[
12
, Section 7.8]
).
∎

Let us here also record orthogonality relations for characters. There and later when
\pm
appears only on one side of an equation, both options are summed.

Lemma 3.6
.

If
m,n
are integers with
(mn,q)=1
then

\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi\textup{ (mod $q$)}}\chi(m)\overline{\chi}(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=dr\\
r|(m-n)\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\phi(r),

and

\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{\chi\textup{ (mod $q$)}}\chi(m)\overline{\chi}(n)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=dr\\
r|(m\pm n)\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\phi(r).

Proof.

The first claim follows from the orthogonality of all characters and Möbius inversion, while the second claim follows from the first by detecting even characters with
\frac{1+\chi(-1)}{2}
.
∎

4.
The approximate functional equation and truncation

Writing

G(s,t):=\Gamma^{4}\left(\tfrac{s}{2}+\tfrac{it}{2}\right)\Gamma^{4}\left(\tfrac{s}{2}-\tfrac{it}{2}\right)

we would like to find an approximation to

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\Lambda(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\chi)|^{8}dt=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}G\left(\tfrac{1}{2},t\right)L^{4}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it,\chi\right)L^{4}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}-it,\overline{\chi}\right)dt.

Note that, for
\textup{Re }(s)>1,
we have

L^{4}\left(s+it,\chi\right)L^{4}\left(s-it,\overline{\chi}\right)=\sum_{m,n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\tau_{4}(m)\tau_{4}(n)}{m^{s}n^{s}}\chi(m)\overline{\chi}(n)\left(\frac{n}{m}\right)^{it}.

For stating an approximate functional equation for the eighth moment, we need the weight function
V\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by

(17)

V(\xi,\eta;\mu):=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\eta}{\xi}\right)^{it}W\left(\frac{\xi\eta\pi^{4}}{\mu^{4}},t\right)\>dt

with
W\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by

(18)

W(x,t):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(1)}G(1/2+s,t)x^{-s}\frac{ds}{s}.

For the short proof of the following proposition, see
[
1
, Lemma 2.1]
(but note that the definition of
P(\chi,t)
there is missing a factor
(n/m)^{it}
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\chi
be an even primitive character
\pmod{q}
. Then

(19)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\Lambda(1/2+iy,\chi)|^{8}dy=2\sum_{m,n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\tau_{4}(m)\tau_{4}(n)}{\sqrt{mn}}\chi(m)\overline{\chi}(n)V(m,n;q).

The integration in
y
gives rise to
V(m,n;q)
on the right hand side of (
19
) which makes the essential support of
m
and
n
sums more restricted; from the following lemma, we see that the main contribution comes from when
m,n
are both at most
q^{2+\varepsilon}.

Lemma 4.2
.

The weight function
W(x,t)
defined in (
18
) is a smooth function of
x\in(0,\infty)
. Furthermore the function
V(\xi,\eta;\mu)
defined in (
17
) satisfies, for any
\xi,\eta,\mu>0
and any non-negative integers
\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3}
,

(20)

\frac{d^{\nu_{1}}d^{\nu_{2}}d^{\nu_{3}}}{d\xi^{\nu_{1}}d\eta^{\nu_{2}}d\mu^{\nu_{3}}}V(\xi,\eta;\mu)\ll_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3}}\exp\left(-\left(\frac{\max(\xi,\eta)^{2}}{\mu^{4}}\right)^{1/4}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{\xi^{\nu_{1}}\eta^{\nu_{2}}\mu^{\nu_{3}}}.

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as
[
4
, Proof of Lemma 1]
but for completeness we prove (
20
) here. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\eta\geq\xi
. By definition, for any
c>0
,

V(\xi,\eta;\mu)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\eta}{\xi}\right)^{it}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}G(1/2+s,t)\left(\frac{\xi\eta\pi^{4}}{\mu^{4}}\right)^{-s}\frac{ds}{s}\>dt.

Substituting
it=z
, we obtain

V(\xi,\eta;\mu)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-i\infty}^{i\infty}\left(\frac{\eta}{\xi}\right)^{z}\int_{(c)}G(1/2+s,-iz)\left(\frac{\xi\eta\pi^{4}}{\mu^{4}}\right)^{-s}\frac{ds}{s}\>dz.

We move the
z
-integration to the line
\textup{Re }z=-c
. Writing
z=-c+it
and
s=c+iu
, we obtain

V(\xi,\eta;\mu)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\eta}{\xi}\right)^{-c+it}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}G(1/2+c+iu,t-ic)\left(\frac{\xi\eta\pi^{4}}{\mu^{4}}\right)^{-c-iu}\frac{du}{c+iu}\>dt.

Taking derivatives, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{d^{\nu_{1}}d^{\nu_{2}}d^{\nu_{3}}}{d\xi^{\nu_{1}}d\eta^{\nu_{2}}d\mu^{\nu_{3}}}V(\xi,\eta;\mu)

\displaystyle\ll_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3}}\frac{1}{\xi^{\nu_{1}}\eta^{\nu_{2}}\mu^{\nu_{3}}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|G(1/2+c+iu,t-ic)|\left(\pi\frac{\eta^{1/2}}{\mu}\right)^{-4c}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot|t+u|^{\nu_{1}}|2c+i(u-t)|^{\nu_{2}}|c+iu|^{\nu_{3}}\frac{du}{|c|+|u|}\>dt.

When
\eta^{1/2}/\mu\leq 10
we take
c=2
and the result follows immediately. Otherwise we choose
c=\eta^{1/2}/\mu>10
. Then, by Stirling’s formula (
12
),

(21)

\begin{split}&|G(1/2+c+iu,t+ic)|\left(\pi\frac{\eta^{1/2}}{\mu}\right)^{-4c}=\left|\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{4}+i\frac{u+t}{2}\right)^{4}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{4}+c+i\frac{u-t}{2}\right)^{4}\right|(\pi c)^{-4c}\\
&\ll\exp(-|u+t|)\frac{\left|\frac{1}{4}+c+i\frac{u-t}{2}\right|^{4c}}{(e\pi c)^{4c}}\exp\left(-2|u-t|\arctan\left(\frac{|u-t|}{2c+1/2}\right)\right)\end{split}

If
|u-t|\leq 2c
, then (
21
) is

\ll\exp(-|u+t|)\frac{(2c)^{4c}}{(e\pi c)^{4c}}\ll\exp(-|u+t|-4c)\ll\exp(-|u+t|-|u-t|-2c).

On the other hand if
|u-t|>2c
, then (
21
) is

\begin{split}&\ll\exp(-|u+t|)\frac{\left|u-t\right|^{4c}}{(e\pi c)^{4c}}\exp\left(-2|u-t|\cdot\frac{3}{4}\right)\\
&\ll\exp(-|u+t|-|u-t|)\exp\left(-c\left(4\log(e\pi)+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{|u-t|}{c}-4\log\frac{|u-t|}{c}\right)\right)\\
&\ll\exp(-|u+t|-|u-t|-2c).\end{split}

Hence in any case

\frac{d^{\nu_{1}}d^{\nu_{2}}d^{\nu_{3}}}{d\xi^{\nu_{1}}d\eta^{\nu_{2}}d\mu^{\nu_{3}}}V(\xi,\eta;\mu)\ll_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3}}\frac{e^{-c}}{\xi^{\nu_{1}}\eta^{\nu_{2}}\mu^{\nu_{3}}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}(|u+t|+|u-t|))du\>dt

and the claim follows.
∎

Let us make some initial reductions to the left hand side of our claim (
2
). First, by Lemma
4.1

\sum_{q}\Psi\left(\frac{q}{Q}\right)\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{\chi\textup{ (mod $q$)}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|\Lambda\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+iy,\chi\right)\right|^{8}\>dy=2\Delta(\Psi,Q),

where

\Delta(\Psi,Q)=\sum_{q}\Psi\left(\frac{q}{Q}\right)\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{\chi\textup{ (mod $q$)}}\sum_{m,n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\tau_{4}(m)\tau_{4}(n)}{\sqrt{mn}}\chi(m)\overline{\chi}(n)V(m,n;q).

The next step is to slightly truncate the sums over
m
and
n
in
\Delta(\Psi,Q)
. This truncation will allow us to apply the complementary divisor trick to reduce the conductor in Section
6
. For the truncation, let
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and define

(22)

Q_{0}:=\exp((\log Q)^{\varepsilon_{0}})

and

\widetilde{\Delta}(\Psi,Q)=\sum_{q}\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{\chi\textup{ (mod $q$)}}\Psi\left(\frac{q}{Q}\right)\sum_{m,n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\tau_{4}(m)\tau_{4}(n)}{\sqrt{mn}}\chi(m)\overline{\chi}(n)V\left(m,n;\frac{q}{Q_{0}}\right).

Note that we expect (and will later show) that
\Delta(\Psi,Q)\asymp Q^{2}(\log Q)^{16}.
In this section we recall from
[
1
]
that
\widetilde{\Delta}(\Psi,Q)
is a sufficiently close approximation to
\Delta(\Psi,Q)
. This type of procedure has previously appeared in the contexts of moments also in other situations (see for instance the works of Soundararajan
[
10
]
and Soundararajan and Young
[
11
]
).

Proposition 4.3
.

With the above notation

{\Delta}(\Psi,Q)-\widetilde{\Delta}\left(\Psi,Q\right)\ll_{\alpha}Q^{2}(\log Q)^{15+\varepsilon_{0}}.

Proof.

This follows as
[
1
, Proof of Proposition 3.1]
. The key ingredient in the proof is the large sieve. In
[
1
]
one has
(\log Q)^{\alpha}
in place of
Q_{0}
but the same proof works by replacing every occurrence of
(\log Q)^{\alpha}
by
Q_{0}
— our choice of
Q_{0}
is sufficiently small since in the critical case of
[
1
, Proof of Proposition 3.1]
where
Q^{2}/Q_{0}^{2}\leq M,N\leq Q^{2}
the parameters
M
and
N
are each summed over
\ll\log Q_{0}\ll(\log Q)^{\varepsilon_{0}}
dyadic intervals.
∎

5.
Splitting
\widetilde{\Delta}\left(\Psi,Q\right)

Now, by Proposition
4.3
and orthogonality of characters (see Lemma
3.6
), it is sufficient to consider

\widetilde{\Delta}\left(\Psi,Q\right)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m,n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\tau_{4}(m)\tau_{4}(n)}{\sqrt{mn}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d,r\\
(dr,mn)=1\\
r|m\pm n\end{subarray}}\phi(r)\mu(d)\Psi\left(\frac{dr}{Q}\right)V\left(m,n,\frac{dr}{Q_{0}}\right).

Let
D=(\log Q)^{\Delta_{0}}
for some
\Delta_{0}
to be determined later and split

\widetilde{\Delta}\left(\Psi,Q\right)=\mathcal{D}(\Psi,Q)+\mathcal{S}_{1}(\Psi,Q)+\mathcal{O}(\Psi,Q),

where the diagonal term
\mathcal{D}(\Psi,Q)
consists of the terms with
m=n
, the term
\mathcal{S}_{1}(\Psi,Q)
consists of the remaining terms with
d>D
and
\mathcal{O}(\Psi,Q)
consists of the remaining terms with
d\leq D
.

The first two terms
\mathcal{D}(\Psi,Q)
and
\mathcal{S}_{1}(\Psi,Q)
were handled in
[
1
, Propositions 4.1 and 5.1]
that gave the following asymptotic formulas.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
\widetilde{\Psi}
be the Mellin transform of
\Psi,
which is defined by

(23)

\widetilde{\Psi}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\Psi(u)u^{s}\frac{du}{u}.

Then

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}(\Psi,Q)&=2^{16}Q^{2}\frac{(\log Q)^{16}}{16!}\widetilde{\Psi}(2)\frac{\mathcal{A}}{2}\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{\mathcal{B}_{p}}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{2}}-\frac{1}{p^{3}}\right)\right)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}G(1/2,t)dt\\
&\qquad\qquad+O_{\varepsilon}(Q^{2}(\log Q)^{15+\varepsilon}),\end{split}

where

\mathcal{B}_{p}=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{\tau_{4}^{2}(p^{r})}{p^{r}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{A}=\prod_{p}\mathcal{B}_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{16}.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. There exists an absolute constant
C
such that

\mathcal{S}_{1}(\Psi,Q)=\mathcal{MS}_{1}(\Psi,Q)+O_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{Q^{2}(\log Q)^{C}}{D^{1-\varepsilon}}\right),

where

\mathcal{MS}_{1}(\Psi,Q):=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n=1\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}\frac{\tau_{4}(m)\tau_{4}(n)}{\sqrt{mn}}\sum_{(q,mn)=1}\Psi\left(\frac{q}{Q}\right)\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}dr=q\\
d\leq D\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\right)V\left(m,n;\frac{q}{Q_{0}}\right).

In
[
1
]
the remaining term
\mathcal{O}(\Psi,Q)
was treated by the complementary divisor trick. In doing this, the first step is to replace the arithmetic factor
\phi(r)
by a smooth function which can be done by writing
\phi(r)=\sum_{al=r}\mu(a)l
so that

\mathcal{O}(\Psi,Q)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n=1\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\tau_{4}(m)\tau_{4}(n)}{\sqrt{mn}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq D,a,l\\
(dal,mn)=1\\
al|m\pm n\end{subarray}}\mu(a)\mu(d)l\Psi\left(\frac{adl}{Q}\right)V\left(m,n,\frac{adl}{Q_{0}}\right).

It turns out that we can only use the complementary divisor trick when
a
is not too large (this is ultimately due to Proposition
3.2
involving a
q
-average, as explained in Remark
6.5
below). Accordingly we write

\begin{split}\mathcal{O}(\Psi,Q)&=\mathcal{S}_{2}(\Psi,Q)+\mathcal{G}(\Psi,Q),\end{split}

where
\mathcal{S}_{2}(\Psi,Q)
consists of the terms with
a>A:=\exp((\log Q)^{\varepsilon_{0}/2})

and
\mathcal{G}(\Psi,Q)
consists of the terms with
a\leq A
.

To handle
\mathcal{S}_{2}(\Psi,Q)
, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. One has

\mathcal{S}_{2}(\Psi,Q)=\mathcal{MS}_{2}(\Psi,Q)+O_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{Q^{2}\exp(2(\log Q)^{\varepsilon/3})}{A^{\varepsilon/3}}\right),

where

\mathcal{MS}_{2}(\Psi,Q):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n=1\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}\frac{\tau_{4}(m)\tau_{4}(n)}{\sqrt{mn}}\sum_{(q,mn)=1}\Psi\left(\frac{q}{Q}\right)\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}adl=q\\
a>A,d\leq D\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(a)\mu(d)l}{\phi(al)}\right)V\left(m,n;\frac{q}{Q_{0}}\right).

Proof.

The proof mostly follows the proof of
[
1
, Proposition 5.1]
(i.e. proof of Lemma
5.2
).
Recall that

\mathcal{S}_{2}(\Psi,Q)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n=1\\
m\neq n\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\tau_{4}(m)\tau_{4}(n)}{\sqrt{mn}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a>A,d\leq D,l\\
(dal,mn)=1\\
al|m\pm n\end{subarray}}\mu(a)\mu(d)l\Psi\left(\frac{adl}{Q}\right)V\left(m,n,\frac{adl}{Q_{0}}\right).

We express the condition
al|m\pm n
using the even characters
\chi\pmod{al}.
Hence

\mathcal{S}_{2}(\Psi,Q)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a>A\\
d\leq D,l\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(a)\mu(d)l}{\phi(al)}\Psi\left(\frac{adl}{Q}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\pmod{al}\\
\chi(-1)=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n=1\\
m\neq n\\
(mn,d)=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(m)\overline{\chi}(n)\tau_{4}(m)\tau_{4}(n)}{\sqrt{mn}}V\left(m,n,\frac{adl}{Q_{0}}\right).

The principal character
\chi=\chi_{0}
gives the claimed main term, so we can concentrate on the contribution of the non-principal characters.

Reintroducing the terms with
m=n
leads to (here our treatment is simpler than that in
[
1
, Proof of Proposition 5.1]
) an error at most

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\tau_{4}^{2}(n)}{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a>A\\
d\leq D,l\\
(dal,n)=1\end{subarray}}l\Psi\left(\frac{adl}{Q}\right)\left|V\left(n,n,\frac{adl}{Q_{0}}\right)\right|.

Recalling the support of
\Psi
and Lemma
4.2
this is

\sum_{n\leq Q^{3}}\frac{\tau_{4}^{2}(n)}{n}\sum_{A<a\leq 2Q}\sum_{d\leq D}\sum_{l\leq\frac{2Q}{ad}}l+O\left(\frac{1}{Q}\right)\ll\frac{Q^{2}(\log Q)^{16}}{A}.

Hence it suffices to bound the sum

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\prime}(\Psi,Q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a>A\\
d\leq D,l\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(a)\mu(d)l}{\phi(al)}\Psi\left(\frac{adl}{Q}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\pmod{al}\\
\chi(-1)=1\\
\chi\neq\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n=1\\
(mn,d)=1\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(m)\overline{\chi}(n)\tau_{4}(m)\tau_{4}(n)}{\sqrt{mn}}V\left(m,n,\frac{adl}{Q_{0}}\right)

Arguing similarly to the proof of Proposition 5.1 in
[
1
]
but with
al
in place of
r
, with not deducing to primitive characters, and with an additional factor
\mu(a)l/\phi(al)\ll\log Q/\phi(a)
, we get that

(24)

\begin{split}\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\prime}(\Psi,Q)&\ll_{\varepsilon}(\log Q)\sum_{d\leq D}d^{\varepsilon}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp(-|t_{1}|-|t_{2}|)\sum_{a>A}\frac{1}{\phi(a)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\leq\frac{2Q}{ad}\end{subarray}}1\\
&\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\ \textrm{mod}\ al\\
\chi(-1)=1\\
\chi\neq\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}\left\{\left|L\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{1}{\log Q}+it_{1},\chi\right)\right|^{8}+\left|L\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{1}{\log Q}+it_{2},\overline{\chi}\right)\right|^{8}\right\}\>dt_{1}\>dt_{2}.\end{split}

Now, writing

,

\sum_{a>A}\frac{1}{\phi(a)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\leq 2Q/(ad)\end{subarray}}1\leq\sum_{r\leq 2Q/d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\mid r\\
a>A\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\phi(a)}.

Here

(25)

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\mid r\\
a>A\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\phi(a)}&\ll\frac{\log Q}{A^{\varepsilon/3}}\sum_{a\mid r}\frac{1}{a^{1-\varepsilon/3}}\ll\frac{\log Q}{A^{\varepsilon/3}}\prod_{p\mid r}\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{1-\varepsilon/3}}\right)\\

Using this and Proposition
3.2
in (
24
), we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}^{\prime}(\Psi,Q)

\displaystyle\hskip 108.405pt\cdot\sum_{r\sim R}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\textrm{mod}\ r\\

Lemma 6.2

Lemma 6.3

Then the functions
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{\pm}_{2}(s_{1},s_{2};u)
are analytic in the region

\mathcal{W}^{\pm}(x,y;u)=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\int_{(c_{1})}\int_{(c_{2})}\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{\pm}_{2}(s_{1},s_{2};u)x^{-s_{1}}y^{-s_{2}}\>ds_{2}\>ds_{1},

]
(and are easy to prove). In the proof of (

. The proof is based on partial integration, and for this we first study the derivatives of

To simplify the notation, we write in this proof

for unspecified functions

By Lemma

\frac{d}{dx}V(x,y;u|x\pm y|)=\frac{1}{x}V_{1}(x,y;u|x\pm y|)+u\cdot\frac{1}{u|x\pm y|}V_{2}(x,y;u|x\pm y|),

so that in the region

k

|\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{\pm}_{2}(s_{1},s_{2};u)|\ll\frac{1}{|s_{1}|^{k}}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+ux)^{k}W^{\pm}_{1}(x,y;u)x^{s_{1}}y^{s_{2}}\frac{dx}{x}\frac{dy}{y}\right|,

Now we substitute

|\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{\pm}_{2}(s_{1},s_{2};u)|\ll\frac{1}{|s_{1}|^{k}}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+uw)^{k}W^{\pm}_{1}(w,wz;u)w^{s_{1}+s_{2}}z^{s_{2}}\frac{dw}{w}\frac{dz}{z}\right|.

Next we perform partial integration with respect to

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dw}V(w,wz,uw|1\pm z|)&=\frac{1}{w}V_{5}(w,wz,uw|1\pm z|)+z\cdot\frac{1}{wz}V_{6}(w,wz,uw|1\pm z|)\\
&\qquad+u|1\pm z|\frac{1}{uw|1\pm z|}V_{7}(w,wz,uw|1\pm z|)\\
&=\frac{1}{w}V_{8}(w,wz,uw|1\pm z|).\end{split}

Note also that

\frac{d}{dw}(1+uw)^{k}=\frac{1}{w}(1+uw)^{k}\cdot k\frac{uw}{1+uw}.

Using similar bounds for higher order derivatives and considering other terms as well, we see that

\frac{d^{l}}{dw^{l}}\left((1+uw)^{k}W^{\pm}_{1}(w,wz;u)\right)=\frac{1}{w^{l}}(1+uw)^{k}W^{\pm}_{2}(w,wz;u).

Hence, by applying partial integration
l
times to the right hand side of (
45
), we obtain

\begin{split}|\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}^{\pm}_{2}(s_{1},s_{2};u)|&\ll\frac{1}{|s_{1}|^{k}|s_{1}+s_{2}|^{l}}\Bigl|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}w^{s_{1}+s_{2}}z^{s_{2}}(1+uw)^{k}W^{\pm}_{3}(w,wz;u)\frac{dz}{z}\frac{dw}{w}\Bigr|\end{split}

Substituting back
w=x
and
z=y/x
, we see that the above integral is

(46)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s_{1}}y^{s_{2}}(1+ux)^{k}W^{\pm}_{3}(x,y;u)\frac{dy}{y}\frac{dx}{x}.

Recall that
k\geq 1
and
\textup{Re }s_{i}>0
and that
W^{\pm}_{3}(x,y;u)
is supported on
u|x\pm y|\in[1,2]
and satisfies
W^{\pm}_{3}(x,y;u)\ll\exp\left(-2c\max\{x,y\}^{1/4}\right)
for some
c>0
.

In the part of (
46
) with
x>10/u
, we have
y\asymp x
and
y
is restricted to an interval of length
1/u
(depending on
x
). Hence the contribution of
x>10/u
to (
46
) is bounded by

\begin{split}&u^{k-1}\int_{10/u}^{\infty}x^{\textup{Re }s_{1}+\textup{Re }s_{2}}x^{k}\exp(-2cx^{1/4})\frac{dx}{x^{2}}\\
&\ll u^{k-1}\frac{1}{\textup{Re }s_{1}+\textup{Re }s_{2}+k-1}\left(\frac{1}{u^{\textup{Re }s_{1}+\textup{Re }s_{2}+k-1}}+1\right)\exp(-2cu^{-1/4})\\
&\ll u^{k-1}\frac{1}{\textup{Re }s_{1}\cdot\textup{Re }s_{2}}\exp(-cu^{-1/4}).\end{split}

In the part of (
46
) with
x\leq 10/u
, we have
y\leq 12/u
and
\max\{x,y\}\geq 1/(2u)
(since
|x\pm y|\in[1/u,2/u]
), so the contribution of this part is bounded by

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{10/u}\int_{0}^{12/u}\mathbf{1}_{\max\{x,y\}\geq 1/(2u)}x^{\textup{Re }s_{1}}y^{\textup{Re }s_{2}}\left|W^{\pm}_{3}(x,y;u)\right|\frac{dx}{x}\frac{dy}{y}\\
&\ll\frac{1}{\textup{Re }s_{1}}\frac{1}{\textup{Re }s_{2}}u^{-\textup{Re }s_{1}-\textup{Re }s_{2}}\exp(-2cu^{-1/4})\ll\frac{1}{\textup{Re }s_{1}}\frac{1}{\textup{Re }s_{2}}\exp(-cu^{-1/4}).\end{split}

∎

Lemma 7.1

Let

&\cdot\frac{\zeta(1-z)\mathcal{F}(-z,g,MN)}{\zeta(1+z)\phi(gMN,1+z)}\left(\frac{Q}{gQ_{0}^{2}}\right)^{-z}\>dz+O\left(\frac{Q^{2}}{(\log Q)^{C}}\right),\end{split}

where

p\nmid MN\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{1+s}}-\frac{1}{p-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s}}\right)\right).

(h,MN)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\phi(abh)}\mathcal{W}^{\pm}\left(\frac{gMQ_{0}^{2}}{Q^{2}},\frac{gNQ_{0}^{2}}{Q^{2}};\frac{dQ}{ghQ_{0}^{2}}\right),

Proposition 7.2

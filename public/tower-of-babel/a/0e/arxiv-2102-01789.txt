1.
Notation and terminology

Throughout the paper we work in the following framework and with the following notation and terminology.

(S,+)
is a commutative semigroup,
K
is a field of characteristic different from
2
,
G
is a
2
-cancellative abelian group and
H
is an abelian group uniquely divisible by
2
.

(i)

A mapping
\sigma:S\to S
is called involution if

\sigma(\sigma(x))=x\;\;\text{and}\;\;\sigma(x+y)=\sigma(x)+\sigma(y),\;\;x,y\in S.

(ii)

We say that
\chi:S^{2}\to K
is a multiplication function if

\chi(x+y,z+w)=\chi(x,z)\chi(y,w),\;\;\;x,y,z,w\in S.

(iii)

A function
A:S^{2}\to G
is called additive if

A(x+y,z+w)=A(x,z)+A(y,w),\;\;\;x,y,z,w\in S.

(iv)

We say that
B:S^{2}\times S^{2}\to H
is a biadditive function if

B(u+v,w)=B(u,w)+B(v,w),\;\;u,v,w\in S^{2},

and

B(u,v+w)=B(u,v)+B(u,w),\;\;\;u,v,w\in S^{2}.

2.
Introduction

The functional equation

(2.1)

\displaystyle f(x+y)+f(x-y)=2f(x)f(y),\;\;x,y\in\mathbb{R}

is known as the scalar d’Alembert functional equation. It has a long history going back to d’Alembert
[
8
]
.
This functional equation has an obvious extension to groups, and even to semigroups with an involution, and a satisfactory theory of its solutions exists as described in
[
16
, Chapter 9]
(also, see
[
2
,
9
,
10
]
for details and references).

In 1989, Aczél and Dhombres
[
2
]
proved that a mapping
Q:X\to Y
satisfies the quadratic functional equation

(2.2)

Q(x+y)+Q(x-y)=2Q(x)+2Q(y)

if and only if there exists a symmetric bi-additive mapping
b:X^{2}\to Y
such that
Q(x)=b(x,x)
, where

b(x,y):=\frac{1}{4}[Q(x+y)+Q(x-y)],\;\;x,y\in X,

where
X
and
Y
are two vector spaces. Later, many different quadratic functional equations were solved by numerous authors; for example, see
[
7
,
12
,
13
]
.

Let
X
and
Y
be real vector spaces. For a given involution
\sigma:X\to X,
the functional equation

(2.3)

g(x+y)+g(x+\sigma(y))=2g(x)+2g(y),

is called the quadratic functional equation with involution. According to
[
15
, Corollary 8]
, a function
g:X\to Y
is a solution of (
2.3
) if and only if there exist an additive function
a:X\to Y,
and a bi-additive symmetric function
b:X^{2}\to Y
such that
a(\sigma(x))=a(x)
,
b(\sigma(x),y)=-b(x,y)
and
g(x)=a(x)+b(x,x)
for all
x,y\in X.

In 2010, Sinopoulos
[
14
]
determined the general solution of the following functional equations:

(2.4)

\displaystyle g(x+y)+g(x+\sigma(y))=2g(x)g(y),\;\;x,y\in S,

(2.5)

\displaystyle g(x+y)+g(x+\sigma(y))=2g(x),\;\;x,y\in S,

(2.6)

\displaystyle g(x+y)+g(x+\sigma(y))=2g(x)+2g(y),\;\;x,y\in S,

where
(S,+)
is a commutative semi-group and
\sigma:S\to S
is an involution.

The equation (
2.5
), in the case where
S
is an abelian group
divisible by
2
and
\sigma(x)=-x
, is equivalent to the Jensen equation

(2.7)

\displaystyle J\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)=\frac{J(x)+J(y)}{2},

and is easily reduced to the Cauchy equation (see
[
3
]
).

The equation (
2.6
), again with
\sigma(x)=-x
, plays a fundamental role in the
characterization of inner product spaces
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
11
]
.

The aim of this paper is to solve the following functional equations:

(2.8)

\displaystyle f(x+y,z+w)+f(x+\sigma(y),z+\tau(w))=2f(x,z)f(y,w)

(2.9)

\displaystyle f(x+y,z+w)+f(x+\sigma(y),z+\tau(w))=2f(x,z)

(2.10)

\displaystyle f(x+y,z+w)+f(x+\sigma(y),z+\tau(w))=2f(x,z)+2f(y,w)

for all
x,y,z,w\in S,
where
(S,+)
is a commutative semi-group and
\sigma,\;\tau
are two involutions.

The functional equation

(2.11)

\displaystyle f(x+y,z+w)=f(x,z)f(y,w),\;\;x,y,z,w\in S

corresponds to
\sigma(x)=\tau(x)=x
in (
2.8
), and the functional equation

(2.12)

\displaystyle f(x+y,z+w)=f(x,z)+f(y,w),\;\;x,y,z,w\in S

corresponds to
\sigma(x)=\tau(x)=x
in (
2.10
). In 2007, Bae and Park
[
5
]
introduced the functional equation:

(2.13)

\displaystyle f(x+y,z+w)+f(x-y,z-w)=2f(x,z)+2f(y,w).

The functional equation (
2.13
) corresponds to
\sigma(x)=\tau(x)=-x
in (
2.10
), where
S
is an Abelian group. When
X=Y=\mathbb{R},
the function
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by
f(x,y)=ax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2}
is a solution of (
2.13
) where
a,b
and
c
are fixed real numbers. It is clear that when
(z,w)=(x,y)
and
\sigma=\tau
in (
2.8
), (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), we get the functional equations (
2.4
), (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), where
g(x):=f(x,x)
for all
x\in S
. In addition, if
S
is an Abelian group,
\sigma(x)=-x
and
z=w=0
in (
2.8
), (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), we find the functional equations
(
2.1
), (
2.7
) and (
2.2
) with
f(x):=f(x,0)
for all
x\in S.

General solution of Eq. (
2.8
)

Theorem 1
.

Let
(S,+)
be a commutative semigroup,
K
a field of
characteristic different from
2
, and let
\sigma,\tau:S\to S
be involutions. Then, the general solution
f:S^{2}\to K
of the d’Alembert type equation:

(3.1)

\displaystyle f(x+y,z+w)+f(x+\sigma(y),z+\tau(w))=2f(x,z)f(y,w),\quad x,y,z,w\in S,

is

(3.2)

\displaystyle f(x,y)=\frac{\chi(x,y)+\chi(\sigma(x),\tau(y))}{2},\quad x,y\in S,

where
\chi:S^{2}\to K
is an arbitrary multiplicative function (i.e.,
\chi(x+y,z+w)=\chi(x,z)\chi(y,w)
for
x,y,z,w\in S
).

Proof.

Replacing
(y,w)
by
(\sigma(y),\tau(w))
in (
4.10
), we get

(3.3)

\displaystyle f(x+y,z+w)+f(x+\sigma(y),z+\tau(w))=2f(x,z)f(\sigma(y),\tau(w))

for all
x,y,z,w\in S
. From (
4.10
) and (
3.3
), we obtain

(3.4)

\displaystyle f(\sigma(y),\tau(w))=f(y,w),\;\;\;y,w\in S,

and hence

(3.5)

\displaystyle f(\sigma(x)+y,\tau(z)+w)=f(x+\sigma(y),z+\tau(w)),\;\;\;x,y,z,w\in S.

We have two cases:
f(x+\sigma(y),z+\tau(w))=f(x+y,z+w)
and
f(x+\sigma(y),z+\tau(w))\neq f(x+y,z+w).

Case 1:
If
f(x+\sigma(y),z+\tau(w))=f(x+y,z+w),
then by (
4.10
), we get

(3.6)

\displaystyle f(x+y,z+w)=f(x,z)f(y,w),\;\;\;x,y,z,w\in S,

i.e.,

f(u+v)=f(u)f(v)

with
u=(x,z),

v=(y,w)
and
u+v=(x+y,z+w).
So,
f
is a multiplicative function and can be written in the form (
4.11
):
f(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}(f(x,y)+f(\sigma(x),\tau(y))
.

Case 2:
If
f(x+\sigma(y),z+\tau(w))\neq f(x+y,z+w),
then there exist
x_{0},y_{0},z_{0},w_{0}\in S
such that

(3.7)

\displaystyle f(x_{0}+y_{0},z_{0}+w_{0})-f(x_{0}+\sigma(y_{0}),z_{0}+\tau(w_{0}))\neq 0.

In this case, we define the function
F:S^{2}\to K
by

F(x,z)=f(x+y_{0},z+w_{0})-f(x+\sigma(y_{0}),z+\tau(w_{0})),\;\;x,z\in S.

By (
3.7
), we have
F(x_{0},z_{0})\neq 0
and from (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we get

(3.8)

\displaystyle F(\sigma(x),\tau(z))=-F(x,z)\;\;\text{and}\;\;F(x+\sigma(y),z+\tau(w))=-F(\sigma(x)+y,\tau(z)+w)

for all
x,y,z,w\in S
. Also

F(x+y,z+w)=f(x+y+y_{0},z+w+w_{0})-f(x+y+\sigma(y_{0}),z+w+\tau(w_{0}))

\displaystyle F(x+\sigma(y),z+\tau(w))=f(x+\sigma(y)

\displaystyle+y_{0},z+\tau(w)+w_{0})

\displaystyle-f(x+\sigma(y)+\sigma(y_{0}),z+\tau(w)+\tau(w_{0}))

for all
x,y,z,w\in S
. Adding these equations and using (
3.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle F(x+y,z+w)+

\displaystyle F(x+\sigma(y),z+\tau(w))

\displaystyle=2f(y,w)[f(x+y_{0},z+w_{0})-f(x+\sigma(y_{0}),z+\tau(w_{0}))]

for all
x,y,z,w\in S
. Hence

(3.9)

\displaystyle F(x+y,z+w)+F(x+\sigma(y),z+\tau(w))=2f(y,w)F(x,z),\;\;x,y,z,w\in S.

Interchanging
x,y
and
z,w
in (
3.9
), we get

F(x+y,z+w)+F(y+\sigma(x),w+\tau(z))=2f(x,z)F(y,w),\;\;x,y,z,w\in S,

and by (
3.8
), we have

(3.10)

\displaystyle F(x+y,z+w)-F(x+\sigma(y),z+\tau(w))=2f(x,z)F(y,w),\;\;x,y,z,w\in S.

Adding (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), we find

(3.11)

\displaystyle F(x+y,z+w)=F(x,z)f(y,w)+F(y,w)f(x,z),\;\;x,y,z,w\in S.

i.e.,

F(u+v)=F(u)f(v)+F(v)f(u),\;\;\text{with}\;\;u=(x,z),\;\;v=(y,w).

It follows from this equation that
F
has the form

(3.12)

\displaystyle F(u)=\frac{\chi_{1}(u)+\chi_{2}(u)}{2},\;\;\text{with}\;\;u=(x,z),\;\;x,z\in S,

where the functions
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}:S^{2}\to K
are multiplicative (see
[
2
,
6
,
17
]
).

Replacing
(x,z)
by
(x+t,z+s)
in (
3.11
), we obtain

\displaystyle F(x+t+y,z+s+w)

\displaystyle=F(x+t,z+s)f(y,w)+F(y,w)f(x+t,z+s)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{by}\eqref{dalbtype10}}{=}[F(x,z)f(t,s)+F(t,s)f(x,z)]f(y,w)

(3.13)

\displaystyle\quad+F(y,w)f(x+t,z+s)

for all
x,y,z,w,s,t\in S.
Replacing
(y,w)
by
(t+y,s+w)
in (
3.11
), we get

\displaystyle F(x+t+y,z+s+w)

\displaystyle=F(x,z)f(t+y,s+w)+F(t+y,s+w)f(x,z)

\displaystyle\overset{\text{by}\eqref{dalbtype10}}{=}F(x,z)f(t+y,s+w)

(3.14)

\displaystyle\quad+[F(y,w)f(t,s)+F(t,s)f(y,w)]f(x,z)

for all
x,y,z,w,s,t\in S.
It follows from (
3
) and (

) that

F(y,w)[f(x+t,z+s)-f(x,z)f(t,s)]=F(x,z)[f(t+y,s+w)-f(t,s)f(y,w)]

for all
x,y,z,w,s,t\in S.

Setting
(y,w)=(x_{0},z_{0})
and
h(t,s)=F(x_{0},z_{0})^{-1}[f(x_{0}+t,z_{0}+s)-f(x_{0},z_{0})f(t,s)]
. We have

(3.15)

\displaystyle f(x+t,z+s)-f(t,s)f(x,z)=F(x,z)h(t,s),\;\;x,z,t,s\in S

and

f(x+t,z+s)-f(t,s)f(x,z)=F(t,s)h(x,z),\;\;x,z,t,s\in S.

So,
F(x,z)h(t,s)=F(t,s)h(x,z),\;\;x,z,t,s\in S.
Setting
x=x_{0}
and
z=z_{0}
, we have

h(t,s)=F(x_{0},z_{0})^{-1}F(t,s)h(x_{0},z_{0}).

Since
K
is quadratically closed there exists an
\alpha\in K
such that

\alpha^{2}=F(x_{0},z_{0})^{-1}h(x_{0},z_{0}).

Hence the equation (
3.15
), with
(y,w)
in place of
(t,s)
, becomes

(3.16)

\displaystyle f(x+y,z+w)=f(y,w)f(x,z)+\alpha^{2}F(x,z)F(y,w),\;\;x,z,y,w\in S.

Multiplying (
3.11
) by
\alpha
and adding to and subtracting it from (
3.16
), we obtain respectively

f(x+y,z+w)+\alpha F(x+y,z+w)=[f(y,w)+\alpha F(y,w)][f(x,z)+\alpha F(x,z)]

So the functions
\chi_{1}:=f+\alpha F
and
\chi_{2}:=f-\alpha F
are multiplicative, and by addition
we get (
3.12
).

Now in view of (
3.4
) and (

) we have

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(\sigma(x),\tau(z))

\displaystyle=f(\sigma(x),\tau(z))+\alpha F(\sigma(x),\tau(z))

\displaystyle f(x,z)-\alpha F(x,z)=\chi_{2}(x,z),\;\;x,z\in S.

The converse implication follows by simple calculations. This completes the proof of the theorem.

Theorem 4
.

Let
(S,+)
be a commutative semigroup,
G
a 2-cancellative
abelian group, and let
\sigma,\tau:S\to S
be involutions. Then, the general solution
f:S^{2}\to G
of the Jensen type equation:

(4.1)

\displaystyle f(x+y,z+w)+f(x+\sigma(y),z+\tau(w))=2f(x,z),\quad x,y,z,w\in S,

(4.2)

\displaystyle f(x,y)=a(x,y)+c,\quad\quad x,y\in S,

where
c\in G
is an arbitrary constant and

a(x+y,z+w)=a(x,z)+a(y,w)
for

a(\sigma(x),\tau(y))=-a(x,y)

x,y\in S
.

Replacing
(y,w)
by
(y+\sigma(y),w+\tau(w))
in (
4.1
), we obtain

(4.3)

\displaystyle f(x+y+\sigma(y),z+w+\tau(w))=f(x,z),\quad x,y,z,w\in S.

Putting
(x,z)=(x+u,z+v)
in (
4.1
), we get

(4.4)

\displaystyle f(x+u+y,z+v+w)+f(x+u+\sigma(y),z+v+\tau(w))=2f(x+u,z+v)

for all
x,y,z,u,v,w\in S
. Interchanging
u,y
and
v,w
in (
4.4
), we find

(4.5)

\displaystyle f(x+u+y,z+v+w)+f(x+y+\sigma(u),z+w+\tau(v))=2f(x+y,z+w)

for all
x,y,z,u,v,w\in S
. Adding the last two equations and using (
4.1
), we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle f(x+u+y,z+v+w)+f(x,z)=f(x+u,z+v)+f(x+y,z+w)

for all
x,y,z,u,v,w\in S
. Replacing
(u,v)
by
(\sigma(x),\tau(z))
in (
4.6
), we get

(4.7)

\displaystyle f(x+\sigma(x)+y,z+\tau(z)+w)+f(x,z)=

\displaystyle f(x+\sigma(x),z+\tau(z))

\displaystyle+f(x+y,z+w)

for all
x,y,z,,w\in S
. From (
4.7
) and (
4.3
), we obtain

(4.8)

\displaystyle f(y,w)+f(x,z)=f(x+\sigma(x),z+\tau(z))+f(x+y,z+w)

for all
x,y,z,w\in S
. Interchanging
x,y
and
z,w
in (

), we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle f(x,z)+f(y,w)=f(y+\sigma(y),w+\tau(w))+f(x+y,z+w)

for all

Then

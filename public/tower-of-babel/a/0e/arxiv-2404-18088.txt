On self-dual completely regular codes with covering radius ρ ≤ 3 \rho\leq 3
J. Borges, V. A. Zinoviev

Abstract
We give a complete classification of self-dual completely regular codes with covering radius ρ ≤ 3 \rho\leq 3 . For ρ = 1 \rho=1 the results are almost trivial. For ρ = 2 \rho=2 , by using properties of the more general class of uniformly packed codes in the wide sense, we show that there are two sporadic such codes, of length 8 8 , and an infinite family, of length 4 4 , apart from the direct sum of two self-dual completely regular codes with ρ = 1 \rho=1 , each one. For ρ = 3 \rho=3 , in some cases, we use similar techniques to the ones used for ρ = 2 \rho=2 . However, for some other cases we use different methods, namely, the Pless power moments which allow to us to discard several possibilities. We show that there are only two self-dual completely regular codes with ρ = 3 \rho=3 and d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 , which are both ternary: the extended ternary Golay code and the direct sum of three ternary Hamming codes of length 4. Therefore, any self-dual completely regular code with d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 and ρ = 3 \rho=3 is ternary and has length 12. We provide the intersection arrays for all such codes.

Introduction

Denote by
\mathbb{F}^{n}_{q}
the
n
-dimensional vector space over the finite field of order
q
, where
q
is a prime power. The
(Hamming) distance
between two vectors
{\bf v},{\bf u}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, denoted by
d({\bf v},{\bf u})
, is the number of coordinates in which they differ. The
(Hamming) weight
of a vector
{\bf v}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, denoted by
\mbox{\rm wt}({\bf v})
, is the number of nonzero coordinates of
{\bf v}
.

A
q
-ary
code

C
of length
n
is a subset
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. The elements of
C
are called
codewords
. The
minimum distance

d
of
C
is the minimum distance between any pair of codewords. The
minimum weight

w
of
C
is the minimum weight of any nonzero codeword. A
linear code
with parameters
[n,k,d]_{q}
is a
q
-ary code of length
n
with minimum distance
d
, such that it is a
k
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. For linear codes, the minimum distance and the minimum weight coincide,
d=w
. A
t
-
weight
code is a code where the nonzero codewords have
t
different weights (
t\geq 1
). A linear code of length
n
is said to be
antipodal
if there is some codeword of weight
n
.

The
packing radius
of a code
C
is

e=\lfloor(d-1)/2\rfloor
. Given any vector
{\bf v}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, its
distance to the code
C
is
d({\bf v},C)=\min_{{\bf x}\in C}\{d({\bf v},{\bf x})\}
and the
covering radius
of the code
C
is

\rho=\max_{{\bf v}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}}\{d({\bf v},C)\}
. Note that
e\leq\rho
. If
e=\rho
, then
C
is a
perfect code
.
It is well known that any nontrivial (with more than two codewords) perfect code has
e\leq 3

[
19
,
20
]
. For
e=1
, linear perfect codes are called Hamming codes which exist for lengths
n=(q^{m}-1)/(q-1)
(
m\geq 2
), dimension
k=n-m
and minimum distance
d=3
. For
e=2
, the only nontrivial perfect code is the ternary Golay code of length 11.

Given two vectors
{\bf v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
and
{\bf u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})
, their inner product is

{\bf v}\cdot{\bf u}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}u_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}.

For a linear code
C
, its
dual code
is
C^{\perp}=\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\mid{\bf x}\cdot{\bf v}=0,\;\forall{\bf v}\in C\}
. The code
C
is
self-dual
if
C=C^{\perp}
. In this case,
C
and
C^{\perp}
have the same dimension
n/2
, hence
n
must be even.

Denote by
{\mathbf{0}}
the all-zero vector. The
support
of a vector
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is the set of nonzero coordinate positions of
{\bf x}
,
\mbox{\rm supp}({\bf x})=\{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\mid x_{i}\neq 0\}
. Say that a vector
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}

covers
a vector
{\bf y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
if
x_{i}=y_{i}
, for all
i=1,\ldots,n
such that
y_{i}\neq 0
.

For a given code
C
of length
n
and covering radius
\rho
,
define

C(i)~=~\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}:\;d({\bf x},C)=i\},\;\;i=0,1,\ldots,\rho.

The sets
C(0)=C,C(1),\ldots,C(\rho)
are called the
subconstituents
of
C
.

Say that two vectors
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
are
neighbors
if

d({\bf x},{\bf y})=1
.

Definition 1
(
[
14
]
)

A code
C
of length

n
and covering radius
\rho
is completely regular (shortly
CR), if for all
l\geq 0
every vector
{\bf x}\in C(l)
has the same
number
c_{l}
of neighbors in
C(l-1)
and the same number
b_{l}
of
neighbors in
C(l+1)
. Define
a_{l}=(q-1){\cdot}n-b_{l}-c_{l}
and
set
c_{0}=b_{\rho}=0
. The parameters
a_{l}
,
b_{l}
and

c_{l}
(
0\leq l\leq\rho
) are called intersection numbers
and the sequence
\{b_{0},\ldots,b_{\rho-1};c_{1},\ldots,c_{\rho}\}

is called the intersection array (shortly
\operatorname{IA}
) of
C
.

For any
{\bf v}\in\mathbb{F}^{n}_{q}
and any
t\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
, define
B_{{\bf v},t}=|\{x\in C\mid d(v,x)=t\}|
. Then,
C
is CR if
B_{{\bf v},t}
depends only on
t
and
d({\bf v},C)

[
8
]
. The equivalence with Definition
1
can be seen in
[
14
]
.

Existence, construction and classification of completely regular codes, in general, are open hard problems (see
[
6
,
7
,
12
,
14
]
) of algebraic and combinatorial coding theory.

All linear completely regular codes with covering radius

\rho=1
are known
[
3
]
. The next case, i.e.
completely regular codes with
\rho=2
, was solved for the special
case when the dual codes are antipodal
[
3
,
5
]
. In the present paper, we classify all self-dual completely regular codes with covering radius
\rho\leq 3
.

In Section
2
, we see some definitions and results that we use later. In Section
3
, we show that for
\rho=1
we only have some trivial codes with length and minimum distance
n=d=2
, and the ternary Hamming code of length 4. For
\rho=2
, we prove that the only possible parameters for a self-dual completely regular code are:
[8,4,4]_{2}
,
[8,4,3]_{3}
, and
[4,2,3]_{q}
for any
q=2^{r}
with
r>1
, apart from the direct sum of two self-dual
[2,1,2]_{q}
codes. In Section
4
, we prove that for
\rho=3
the only possibilities are: the direct sum of three self-dual
[2,1,2]_{q}
codes, a
[12,6,6]_{3}
code and a
[12,6,3]_{3}
code. We identify all such codes and show that, indeed, they are self-dual and completely regular. Moreover, all such codes are antipodal except when they are direct sums of other codes. Finally, in Section
5
, we summarize the results and briefly discuss the case
\rho>3
.

[

]

A code

2.2
Self-dual two-weight and three-weight codes

We start with three general an easy results on self-dual codes.

Lemma 6

Let
C
be a
q
-ary self-dual code.

(i)

If

(ii)

If

Proof.
If
C
is self-dual, then
{\bf x}\cdot{\bf x}=0
, for any codeword
{\bf x}\in C
. Therefore (i) is trivial. For (ii), note that for any ternary vector
{\bf z}\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{n}
,
{\bf z}\cdot{\bf z}\equiv\mbox{\rm wt}({\bf z})\pmod{3}
.

\Box

The next well-known property shows which is the only self-dual perfect code.

Lemma 7

The only self-dual perfect code is the ternary Hamming
[4,2,3]_{3}
code.

Proof.
For any self-dual
[n,k,d]_{q}
code, we have that
n=2k
.
The only perfect codes with minimum distance
d>3
are the ternary Golay
[11,6,5]_{3}
code, the binary Golay
[23,12,7]_{2}
code and binary repetition codes
[n,1,n]_{2}
codes of odd length. Since the length of these codes is odd, no one can be self-dual.

For the case of a self-dual perfect code with
d=3
, hence for a self-dual Hamming
[n,n-m,3]_{q}
code,
n=2(n-m)
and thus
n=2m
implying

\frac{q^{m}-1}{q-1}=2m.

The only solution is
q=3
and
m=2
. Therefore,
n=4
and
k=2
.

\Box

Lemma 8

If
C
is a self-dual code, then
|\mbox{\rm supp}({\bf x})\cap\mbox{\rm supp}({\bf y})|\neq 1
, for any
{\bf x},{\bf y}\in C
.

Proof.
Otherwise
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
would not be orthogonal vectors.

\Box

Now, we show the nonexistence of a particular self-dual code.

Lemma 9

There is no self-dual
[6,3,4]_{4}
code.

Proof.
Let
C
be a
[6,3,4]_{4}
code and consider a generator matrix for
C
:

G=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}I_{3}&P\\
\end{array}\right),

where
I_{3}
is the
3\times 3
identity matrix and
P
is a
3\times 3
matrix with nonzero entries, since
C
has minimum weight
4
.
If
C
is self-dual then any row of
G
must be self-orthogonal, implying that for any row
abc
of
P
, we have
1+a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}=0
. Thus,
(a+b+c)^{2}=1
and
a+b+c=1
. In
\mathbb{F}_{4}
and since
P
has no zero entries, this means that
abc\in\{1xx,x1x,xx1\}
, where
x\neq 0
. If
abc=111
, then it is not orthogonal to any other row

a^{\prime}b^{\prime}c^{\prime}\in\{1xx,x1x,xx1\}
, where
x\notin\{0,1\}
. So, the three rows contain exactly one
1
. Hence, two rows of
P
have the same value for
x
, say
\alpha
. But such two rows must agree in one position (with
1
), since the distance must be two. Therefore, they are not orthogonal and hence the corresponding rows of
G
are also non-orthogonal.

\Box

For a code
C
, let
A_{w}=|C_{w}|
. Thus,
\{A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}\}
is the weight distribution of
C

[

, as well as the McWilliams identities, relate the weight distribution of

the first three equations are:

\displaystyle A_{w_{1}}+A_{w_{2}}+A_{w_{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{k}-1

(6)

\displaystyle w_{1}A_{w_{1}}+w_{2}A_{w_{2}}+w_{3}A_{w_{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{k-1}2k(q-1)

(7)

\displaystyle w_{1}^{2}A_{w_{1}}+w_{2}^{2}A_{w_{2}}+w_{3}^{2}A_{w_{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{k-2}\left[2k(q-1)(2k(q-1)+1)\right].

(8)

As a consequence of these equations, we have the following result.

Lemma 10

If

-weight

(w_{3}-w_{1})A_{w_{1}}+(w_{3}-w_{2})A_{w_{2}}+w_{3}=q^{k-1}[(w_{3}-2k)q+2k].

Obviously, both hand sides must be positive. Thus, we obtain
q(2k-w_{3})<2k
.

\Box

3
Self-dual completely regular codes with
\rho\leq 2

Let
C
be a self-dual CR
[n,k,d]_{q}
code with covering radius
1\leq\rho\leq 2
.
In this section we give a full classification of such codes. Note that
n=2k
(since
C
is self-dual) and
C
is a 1-weight code (or equidistant code) or a 2-weight code (because
s=\rho
by Lemma
5
). Since
e\leq\rho
, we have that
1\leq d\leq 6
. But for
d\geq 5
,
e=\rho
and
C
would be a perfect 2-error-correcting code, that is,
C
would be a ternary Golay
[11,6,5]_{3}
code which obviously is not self-dual (the extended ternary Golay code is self-dual, but with covering radius 3). Clearly, for
d=1
there is no self-dual code. Therefore,
C
must be a
[2k,k,d]_{q}
code with one weight

or with two weights
w_{1}=d\in\{2,3,4\}
and
w_{2}
, where

.

Now we study separately the cases
d=2
,
d=3
and
d=4
.

3.1
The case
d=2

This is a very simple case. If
C
is a self-dual CR
[n,k,2]_{q}
code, then by Lemmas
2
and
8
,
C
is the direct sum of codes of length 2. If
C_{i}
is one such code, then
C_{i}
has generator matrix
G_{i}=(1\;\;\alpha)
, where
1+\alpha^{2}=0
. Indeed, such a code is CR with covering radius 1 (and
p_{1,1}=2
). Therefore we have the following characterization.

Proposition 3

If
C
is a self-dual CR
[n,k,2]_{q}
code, then
C
is a direct sum
C=C_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus C_{j}
, where
C_{i}
is a
[2,1,2]_{q}
code (
1\leq i\leq j
) and
q
is such that
-1
is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The covering radius of
C
is
\rho=j
and its intersection array is:

\operatorname{IA}=\{2j(q-1),2(j-1)(q-1),\ldots,2(q-1);2,4,\ldots,2j\}.

Proof.
Straightforward from Lemma
3
, taking into account that each
C_{i}
has covering radius 1 and intersection array
\{2(q-1);2\}
.

\Box

3.2
The case
d=3

Recall that for any code
C
, the set of codewords of weight
w
is denoted by
C_{w}
.

Lemma 11

If
C
is a
2
-weight
[n,k,3]_{q}
code such that
|\mbox{\rm supp}({\bf x})\cap\mbox{\rm supp}({\bf y})|\in\{0,3\}
for all
{\bf x},{\bf y}\in C_{3}
, then
C
is not CR.

Proof.
Let
{\bf x},{\bf y}\in C_{3}
such that
|\mbox{\rm supp}({\bf x})\cap\mbox{\rm supp}({\bf y})|=0
, by the assumption and Lemma
2
such vectors must exist. Then,
C
has weights 3 and 6. Any other codeword
{\bf z}\in C_{3}
will have
\mbox{\rm supp}({\bf z})=\mbox{\rm supp}({\bf x})
or
\mbox{\rm supp}({\bf z})=\mbox{\rm supp}({\bf y})
, otherwise
C
would have more than two weights.

Without loss of generality, assume that
{\bf x}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},0,0,0)
and
{\bf y}=(0,0,0,y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})
. Now, the vector
{\bf v}=(x_{1},v_{2},0,0,0,0)
, where
v_{2}\neq x_{2}
is clearly at distance 2 to
C
and, since
d({\bf v},{\bf x})=d({\bf v},{\mathbf{0}})=2
, we have
B_{{\bf v},2}=2
. Indeed, a vector
{\bf x}^{\prime}=(x_{1},v_{2},x_{3}^{\prime},0,0,0)
cannot be a codeword because
d({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\leq 2
. Now take
{\bf u}=(x_{1},0,0,y_{1},0,0)
. Clearly,
d({\bf u},C)=2
but
B_{{\bf u},2}=1
. Therefore,
C
is not CR.

\Box

Proposition 4

If
C
is a self-dual CR
[n,k,3]_{q}
code with covering radius
\rho=2
, then
n=4
or
n=8
.

Proof.
By Lemmas
2
and
11
, there exist codewords
{\bf x},{\bf y}\in C_{3}
, such that
|\mbox{\rm supp}({\bf x})\cap\mbox{\rm supp}({\bf y})|=2
and thus
|\mbox{\rm supp}({\bf x})\cup\mbox{\rm supp}({\bf y})|=4
. Now, if
{\bf z}\in C_{3}
has
\mbox{\rm supp}({\bf z})\cap(\mbox{\rm supp}({\bf x})\cup\mbox{\rm supp}({\bf y}))\neq\emptyset
, we claim that
\mbox{\rm supp}({\bf z})\subset(\mbox{\rm supp}({\bf x})\cup\mbox{\rm supp}({\bf y}))
. Otherwise, without loss of generality assume that
{\bf x}=(1,x_{2},x_{3},0,\ldots,0)
and
{\bf y}=(1,y_{2},0,y_{3},0,\ldots,0)
. By (i) of Lemma
11
, we can assume that
{\bf z}=(z_{1},z_{2},0,0,z_{3},0,\ldots,0)
. Now, since
q>2
, by Lemma
6
, we can take a multiple of
{\bf z}
, say
{\bf z}^{\prime}=(z^{\prime}_{1},z^{\prime}_{2},0,0,z^{\prime}_{3},0,\ldots,0)
, such that
z^{\prime}_{2}=x_{2}-y_{2}
. Hence,
\mbox{\rm wt}({\bf x}-{\bf y}-{\bf z}^{\prime})=4
. But we can take another multiple, say
{\bf z}^{\prime\prime}
, such that
z^{\prime\prime}_{2}\neq x_{2}-y_{2}
. In this case,
\mbox{\rm wt}({\bf x}-{\bf y}-{\bf z}^{\prime\prime})=5
. So,
C
has more than two nonzero weights, leading to a contradiction.

As a consequence, we have that
C_{3}
induces a partition of the set of coordinates in
4
-subsets, implying that
n
is a multiple of
4
. But for
n>8
, clearly
C
would have more than two nonzero weights. Therefore
n=4
or
n=8
.

\Box

The case
d=4

Proposition 5

If
C
is a self-dual CR

, then

.

Define the function

,

. Clearly, for a fixed

. Also, fixing

. Again,

. For all these possible values (

,

), we have computationally checked that the only natural values of

are

\Box

We start with a very restrictive condition.

Lemma 12

Let

[2k,k,4]_{q}

\rho=3
. If

.

Proof.
Let
{\bf v}=(1,\alpha,0,\ldots,0)
be a
2
-weight vector. Clearly,

and
{\mathbf{0}}
is a codeword at distance

from

. assume that
p_{2,2}>2
and let
{\bf x},{\bf y}\in C_{4}
be codewords such that

. Then,
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}

. It holds that

.

Now we establish the nonexistence of self-dual CR quaternary codes of length

Proposition 8

For

is a quaternary

Consider the 2-weight vector

Corollary 6

, apart from the direct sums of copies of a self-dual CR code with
\rho=1
, it seems that there are no other possibilities. However, the techniques used here become of high complexity for
\rho>3
.

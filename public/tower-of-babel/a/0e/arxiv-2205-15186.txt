Double-Cover-Based Analysis of the Bethe Permanent of Non-negative Matrices
Kit Shing NG, Pascal O. Vontobel

Abstract
The permanent of a non-negative matrix appears naturally in many information processing scenarios. Because of the intractability of the permanent beyond small matrices, various approximation techniques have been developed in the past. In this paper, we study the Bethe approximation of the permanent and add to the body of literature showing that this approximation is very well behaved in many respects. Our main technical tool are topological double covers of the normal factor graph whose partition function equals the permanent of interest, along with a transformation of these double covers.

In this paper we focus on the particularly important special case when

\mathbf{A}

[

\displaystyle\perm_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathbf{A})

\displaystyle\perm_{\mathrm{B},M}(\mathbf{A})

\mathbf{A}

\displaystyle\underbrace{\frac{\perm(\mathbf{A})}{\perm_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathbf{A})}}_{\text{➀}}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{\perm(\mathbf{A})}{\perm_{\mathrm{B},2}(\mathbf{A})}}_{\text{➁}}\cdot\underbrace{\frac{\perm_{\mathrm{B},2}(\mathbf{A})}{\perm_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathbf{A})}}_{\text{➂}}\ .

(4)

Numerically computing the ratios in (

We assume that the reader is familiar with the basics of factor
graphs
[
21
,
22
,
23
]
, in
particular with a variant of factor graphs called normal factor graphs (NFGs).
Recall that an NFG is a graph consisting of vertices (called function nodes)
and edges, where so-called local functions are associated with vertices and
where variables are associated with edges. The local functions are such that
their arguments are only variables associated with edges incident on the
corresponding factor node. The global function is then defined to be the
product of the local functions, and the partition function is defined to be
the sum of the global function over all possible assignments to the variables
associated with the edges.

Let
\mathbf{A}=(a_{i,j})
be an arbitrary non-negative matrix of size

n\times n
. There are various ways of defining an NFG such that its
partition function equals
\perm(\mathbf{A})
. In this paper, we use the NFG

\mathsf{N}(\mathbf{A})
in Fig.
2
(left), which is the
same as in
[
2
]
:

•

The NFG
\mathsf{N}(\mathbf{A})
is based on a complete bipartite graph with
two times
n
vertices.

•

For every
i,j\in[n]
, let the variable associated with the
edge connecting
f_{\mathrm{L},i}
with
f_{\mathrm{R},j}
be called

x_{i,j}
and take value in the set
\mathcal{X}\triangleq\{0,1\}
.

•

For every
i\in[n]
, let

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!f_{\mathrm{L},i}(x_{i,1},\ldots,x_{i,n})

\displaystyle\triangleq\begin{cases}\!\sqrt{a_{i,j}}&\!\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}\text{$\exists j\in[n]$ s.t.
$x_{i,j}=1$;}\\
\text{$x_{i,j^{\prime}}=0$,
$\forall j^{\prime}\in[n]\setminus\{j\}$}\end{array}\\[14.22636pt]
0&\text{(otherwise)}\end{cases}

For every
j\in[n]
, let

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!f_{\mathrm{R},j}(x_{1,j},\ldots,x_{n,j})

\displaystyle\triangleq\begin{cases}\!\sqrt{a_{i,j}}&\!\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}\text{$\exists i\in[n]$ s.t.
$x_{i,j}=1$;}\\
\text{$x_{i^{\prime},j}=0$,
$\forall i^{\prime}\in[n]\setminus\{i\}$}\end{array}\\[14.22636pt]
0&\text{(otherwise)}\end{cases}

•

The global function is defined to be

\displaystyle g(x_{1,1},x_{1,2},\ldots,x_{n,n})

\displaystyle\triangleq\left(\prod_{i\in[n]}f_{\mathrm{L},i}(x_{i,1},\ldots,x_{i,n})\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 7.11317pt\cdot\left(\prod_{j\in[n]}f_{\mathrm{R},j}(x_{1,j},\ldots,x_{n,j})\right).

•

The partition function (or partition sum) is defined to be

\displaystyle Z(\mathsf{N})

\displaystyle\triangleq\sum_{x_{1,1},x_{1,2},\ldots,x_{n,n}}g(x_{1,1},x_{1,2},\ldots,x_{n,n}).

One can verify the following:

•

g(x_{1,1},x_{1,2},\ldots,x_{n,n})=\prod_{i\in[n]}a_{i,\sigma(i)}

if there exists a permutation
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
such that for all

i,j\in[n]
either
x_{i,j}=1
if
j=\sigma(i)
or
x_{i,j}=0

otherwise.

•

g(x_{1,1},x_{1,2},\ldots,x_{n,n})=0
if there exists no such
permutation
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
.

•

Z(\mathsf{N})=\perm(\mathbf{A})
.

For NFGs whose local functions take on non-negative values, the

V
Double-Cover-Based Analysis: I.I.D. Matrix

In this section we consider matrices satisfying the following assumption.

Assumption 1
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a random matrix of size
n\times n
whose entries are
i.i.d. according to some distribution with support over the non-negative
reals, first moment
\mu_{1}
, second moment
\mu_{2}
, and, consequently,
variance
\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}^{2}
.

Theorem 2
.

Given Assumption
1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\perm(\mathbf{A})^{2}\right]}}{\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\perm_{\mathrm{B},2}(\mathbf{A})^{2}\right]}}\sim\sqrt[4]{\frac{\pi n}{\mathrm{e}}}.

(17)

Interestingly, although
\mathbb{E}\left[\perm_{\mathrm{B},2}(\mathbf{A})^{2}\right]
and

\mathbb{E}\left[\perm(\mathbf{A})^{2}\right]
both
depend
on the chosen distribution
(see the calculations below), the right-hand side
of (

) is
independent

of this distribution!

Proof.

A sketch of the proof of this theorem is given below. For all the details of
the proof, see Appendix
D
.
∎

Let us sketch the proof of Theorem
2
. We start by
evaluating
\mathbb{E}\left[\perm_{\mathrm{B},2}(\mathbf{A})^{2}\right]
. From
Proposition
2
we obtain the following
corollary.

Corollary 2

(of Proposition
2
)
.

Given Assumption
1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\perm_{\mathrm{B},2}(\mathbf{A})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=n!\cdot\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}}\mu_{2}^{c_{1}(\sigma)}\cdot\left(\prod_{\ell\geq 2}\mu_{1}^{2\cdot\ell\cdot c_{\ell}(\sigma)}\right)\cdot 2^{-c(\sigma)}

where for
\ell\geq 1
we define
c_{\ell}(\sigma)
to be the number of
cycles of length
\ell
in the cycle notation expression of the
permutation
\sigma
. Note that
\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\ell\cdot c_{\ell}=n

and
\sum_{\ell\geq 2}c_{\ell}(\sigma)=c(\sigma)
.

Proof.

From Proposition
2
it follows that

\displaystyle\hskip-1.42271pt\mathbb{E}\left[\perm_{\mathrm{B},2}(\mathbf{A})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}\!\!\!\!\!\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\prod_{i_{1}\in[n]}\!\!a_{i_{1},\sigma_{1}(i_{1})}\right)\!\cdot\!\left(\prod_{i_{2}\in[n]}\!\!a_{i_{2},\sigma_{2}(i_{2})}\right)\right]\!\!\cdot\!2^{-c(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}\mu_{2}^{c_{1}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})}\cdot\left(\prod_{\ell\geq 2}\mu_{1}^{2\cdot\ell\cdot c_{\ell}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})}\right)\cdot 2^{-c(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})}

\displaystyle=n!\cdot\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}}\mu_{2}^{c_{1}(\sigma)}\cdot\left(\prod_{\ell\geq 2}\mu_{1}^{2\cdot\ell\cdot c_{\ell}(\sigma)}\right)\cdot 2^{-c(\sigma)},

where for
\ell\geq 1
we define
c_{\ell}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})
to be the number
of cycles of length
\ell
in the cycle notation expression of the
permutation
\sigma_{1}\circ\sigma_{2}^{-1}
.
∎

Using results for the cycle index of the symmetric group, one can analyze the
expression

\displaystyle Z_{n}

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}\mu_{2}^{c_{1}(\sigma)}\cdot\left(\prod_{\ell\geq 2}\mu_{1}^{2\cdot\ell\cdot c_{\ell}(\sigma)}\right)\cdot 2^{-c(\sigma)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_{n}|}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}\mu_{2}^{c_{1}(\sigma)}\cdot\left(\prod_{\ell\geq 2}\mu_{1}^{2\cdot\ell\cdot c_{\ell}(\sigma)}\right)\cdot 2^{-c(\sigma)},

for positive integers
n
. In order to evaluate
Z_{n}
, one can use

\displaystyle Z_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\cdot B_{n}\left(0!\cdot\mu_{2},1!\cdot\frac{1}{2}(\mu_{1}^{2})^{2},\dots,(n\!-\!1)!\cdot\frac{1}{2}(\mu_{1}^{2})^{n}\right)\!,

where
B_{n}
denotes the
n
-th complete exponential Bell polynomial. Then,
carefully bounding the above expression, one obtains

\displaystyle Z_{n}

\displaystyle\sim\mu_{1}^{2n}\cdot\mathrm{e}^{(\mu_{2}/\mu_{1}^{2})-1}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{e}}{\pi n}},

The combination of the above results yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\perm_{\mathrm{B},2}(\mathbf{A})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\sim(n!)^{2}\cdot\mu_{1}^{2n}\cdot\mathrm{e}^{(\mu_{2}/\mu_{1}^{2})-1}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{e}}{\pi n}}.

Turning our attention now to
\mathbb{E}\left[\perm(\mathbf{A})^{2}\right]
, calculations
similar to the above calculations yield

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\perm(\mathbf{A})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=n!\cdot\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}}\mu_{2}^{c_{1}(\sigma)}\cdot\left(\prod_{\ell\geq 2}(\mu_{1}^{2})^{\ell c_{\ell}(\sigma)}\right)

\displaystyle=\Psi_{n}(\mu_{2},\mu_{1}^{2},1)

\displaystyle\sim(n!)^{2}\cdot\mu_{1}^{2n}\cdot\mathrm{e}^{(\mu_{2}/\mu_{1}^{2})-1}.

Finally, combining the above results, we
get (
17
).

\displaystyle(1+t)^{\alpha}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{\alpha}{k}t^{k},

\alpha\in\mathbb{R}

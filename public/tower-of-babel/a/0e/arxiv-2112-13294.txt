On a class of weighted anisotropic p p -Laplace equation with singular nonlinearity
Prashanta Garain

Abstract
. We consider a class of singular weighted anisotropic p p -Laplace equations. We provide sufficient condition on the weight function that may vanish or blow up near the origin to ensure the existence of at least one weak solution in the purely singular case and at least two different weak solutions in the purturbed singular case.

35A01, 35D30, 35J92, 35J75

1.
Introduction

In this article, we establish existence of weak solutions for the following class of weighted anisotropic singular problems

(1.1)

-F_{p,w}u=g(x,u)\text{ in }\Omega,\quad u>0\text{ in }\Omega,\quad u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

where
1<p<\infty
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a bounded smooth domain with
N\geq 2
. Here

F_{p,w}u:=\text{div}(w(x)F(\nabla u)^{p-1}\nabla_{\xi}F(\nabla u))

is the weighted anisotropic
p
-Laplace operator, where
F:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to[0,\infty)
is the Finsler-Minkowski norm, that is

(H0)

F(x)\geq 0
, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

(H1)

F(x)=0
, if and only if
x=0
.

(H2)

F(tx)=|t|H(x)
, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

(H3)

F\in C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}\right)
.

(H4)

the Hessian matrix
\nabla_{\xi}^{2}\big(\frac{F^{2}}{2}\big)(x)
is positive definite for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
.

Here
\nabla_{\xi}
denotes the gradient operator with respect to the
\xi
variable. The weight function
w
belong to a class of
p
-admissible weights
W_{p}^{s}
defined in Section
2
. By singularity, we refer to the blow up property of the nonlinearity
g
in the right hand side of (
1.1
). We discuss existence of at least one weak solution of the problem (
1.1
) for the purely singular nonlinearity
g
of the form
(g_{1})
given by

[formula]

g(x,u)=\lambda h(u)u^{-\gamma},\quad

where
\lambda>0,\gamma\in(0,1)
and

(h_{1})

h:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous nondecreasing function such that
h(0)>0
and

(h_{2})

(1.2)

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{h(t)}{t^{\gamma}}=\infty,\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{h(t)}{t^{\gamma+p-1}}=0.

Further, we study multiplicity result of the equation (
1.1
) for the purturbed singular nonlinearity
g
of the form
(g_{2})
given by

[formula]

g(x,u)=\lambda u^{-\gamma}+u^{q},\quad

for a certain range of
\lambda,q>0
, when
\gamma\in(0,1)
.

To give some more insight on the equation (
1.1
), let us discuss few examples of
F
.

Examples:
Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

(i)

For
t>1
, we define

(1.3)

F_{t}(x):=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|x_{i}|^{t}\Big)^{\frac{1}{t}}.

(ii)

For
\lambda,\mu>0
, we define

(1.4)

F_{\lambda,\mu}(x):=\sqrt{\lambda\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}^{4}}+\mu\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}^{2}}.

Then, it follows that the functions
F_{t},F_{\lambda,\mu}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to[0,\infty)
given by (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) satisfies all the hypothesis from (H0)-(H4), see Mezei-Vas
[
40
]
.

Remark 1.1
.

For
i=1,2
, if
\lambda_{i},\mu_{i}
are positive real numbers such that
\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\mu_{1}}\neq\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\mu_{2}}
, then
F_{\lambda_{1},\mu_{1}}
and

given by (

) defines two non-isometric norms in

.

Remark 1.2
.

If

is given by (
1.3
), then we have

(1.5)

F_{p,w}u=\begin{cases}\Delta_{p,w}u:=\text{div}(w(x)|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u),\,(weighted\,$p$\text{-Laplacian})\,\text{if }t=2,\,1<p<\infty,\\
S_{p,w}u=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\Big(|u_{i}|^{p-2}u_{i}\Big),\,(weighted\,pseudo\,$p$\text{-Laplacian})\,\text{if }t=p\in(1,\infty),\end{cases}

where

, for

.

Therefore, it is clear from the above examples that the equation (

) covers a wide range of weighted singular equations. In particular, equation (

) extends the weighted

-Laplace equation

-\Delta_{p,w}u=g(x,u)\text{ in }\Omega,\quad u>0\text{ in }\Omega,\quad u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

Preliminaries

Throughout the rest of the article, we assume
1<p<\infty
, unless otherwise mentioned.

In order to define the notion of weak solutions for the problem (
1.1
), first we present some known facts about weighted Sobolev spaces, refer to
[
34
]
for more details.

Let
1<p<\infty
. We say that
w
is a
p
-admissible weight
W_{p}
, if
w\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
0<w<\infty
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and satisfies the following assumptions:

(i)

for any ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, there exists a positive constant
C_{\mu}
such that
\mu(2B)\leq C_{\mu}\mu(B)
, where
\mu(E)=\int_{E}w\,dx
for a measurable subset
E
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
d\mu(x)=w(x)\,dx
.

(ii)

If
D
is an open set and
\phi\in C^{\infty}(D)
is a sequence of functions such that
\int_{D}|\phi_{i}|^{p}\,d\mu\to 0
and
\int_{D}|\nabla\phi_{i}-v|^{p}\,d\mu\to 0
as
i\to\infty
, where
v
is a vector valued measurable function in
L^{p}(D,w)
, then
v=0
.

(iii)

There exists constants
\kappa>1
and
C_{1}>0
such that

(2.1)

\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}|\phi|^{\kappa p}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{\kappa p}}\leq C_{1}r\left(\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}|\nabla\phi|^{p}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

whenever
B=B(x_{0},r)
is a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B)
.

(iv)

There exists a positive constant
C_{2}
such that

(2.2)

\int_{B}|\phi-\phi_{B}|^{p}\,d\mu\leq C_{2}r^{p}\int_{B}|\nabla\phi|^{p}\,d\mu,

whenever
B=B(x_{0},r)
is a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\phi\in C^{\infty}(B)
is bounded. Here

\phi_{B}=\frac{1}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}\phi\,d\mu.

Examples:
The class of Muckenhoupt weights
A_{p}
are
p
-admissible, see
[
34
, Theorem 15.21]
. In particular, if
c\leq w\leq d
for some positive constants

for any

Definition 2.1
.

(Weighted Spaces)
Let
1<p<\infty
and
w\in W_{p}
. Then the weighted Lebesgue space
L^{p}(\Omega,w)
is the class of measurable functions
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that the norm of
u
given by

(2.3)

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,w)}=\Big(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}w(x)\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

The weighted Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
is the class of measurable functions
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(2.4)

\|u\|_{1,p,w}=\Big(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}w(x)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}w(x)\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

If
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega^{\prime},w)
for every
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
, then we say that
u\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega,w)
. The weighted Sobolev space with zero boundary value is defined as

W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega,w)=\overline{\big(C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega),\|\cdot\|_{1,p,w}\big)}.

By the Poincaré inequality from
[
34
]
and Remark
2.13
below, the norm defined by (
2.4
) on the space
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
is equivalent to the norm given by

(2.5)

\|u\|_{W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)}=\Big(\int_{\Omega}F(\nabla u)^{p}w\,dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Moreover, the space
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega,w)
is a separable and uniformly convex Banach space, see
[
34
]
.

Next, we state an embedding result for a sublcass of
W_{p}
, which is important in our context. To this end, let

(2.6)

I:=\Big[\frac{1}{p-1},\infty\Big)\cap\Big(\frac{N}{p},\infty\Big)

and consider the following subclass of
W_{p}
given by

(2.7)

W_{p}^{s}:=\Big\{w\in W_{p}:w^{-s}\in L^{1}(\Omega)\,\,\text{for some}\,\,s\in I\Big\}.

Then we observe that

w(x)=|x|^{\alpha}\in W_{p}^{s},\text{ for }-N<\alpha<\min\Big\{\frac{N}{s},N(p-1)\Big\}.

We refer the interested reader to
[
20
]
for more applications of such class of weight functions. Following the lines of the proof of
[
26
, Theorem
2.6
]
based on
[
20
]
the following embedding result holds.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
w\in W_{p}^{s}
for some
s\in I
. Then the following continuous inclusion maps hold

W^{1,p}(\Omega,w)\hookrightarrow W^{1,p_{s}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\begin{cases}L^{t}(\Omega),&\text{for }p_{s}\leq t\leq p_{s}^{*},\text{ if }1\leq p_{s}<N,\\
L^{t}(\Omega),&\text{ for }1\leq t<\infty,\text{ if }p_{s}=N,\\
C(\overline{\Omega}),&\text{ if }p_{s}>N,\end{cases}

where
p_{s}=\frac{ps}{s+1}\in[1,p)
.
Moreover, the second embedding above is compact except for
t=p_{s}^{*}=\frac{Np_{s}}{N-p_{s}}
, if
1\leq p_{s}<N
and the same result holds for the space
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
.

Remark 2.3
.

We note that if
0<c\leq w\leq d
for some constants

, then
W^{1,p}(\Omega,w)=W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and by the Sobolev embedding, Lemma
2.2
holds by replacing

and
p_{s}^{*}
with

and

respectively.

Now we are ready to define the notion of weak solutions for the problem (

Definition 2.4
.

Let

Auxiliary results:

Remark 2.13
.

Since all norms in

are equivalent, there exists positive constants

C_{1}|x|\leq F(x)\leq C_{2}|x|,\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

For some positive constants

Lemma 2.14
.

The following relations hold:

(2.9)

F(x)^{p-1}\nabla_{\xi}F(x)\cdot x=F(x)^{p}\geq c_{1}|x|^{p},\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

(2.10)

\big|F(x)^{p-1}\nabla_{\xi}F(x)\big|\leq c_{2}|x|^{p-1},\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\text{ and}

(2.11)

F(tx)^{p-1}\nabla_{\xi}F(tx)=|t|^{p-2}tF(x)^{p-1}\nabla_{\xi}F(x),\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\text{ and }t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}.

Also, we have the following anisotropic algebraic inequality from
[

.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let

. Then, for every

\begin{split}\langle F(x)^{p-1}\nabla_{\xi}F(x)-F(y)^{p-1}\nabla_{\xi}F(y),x-y\rangle&\geq cF(x-y)^{p}.\end{split}

Next, we state the algebraic inequality from Peral

For any

C\frac{|a-b|^{2}}{(|a|+|b|)^{2-p}},\text{ if }1<p<2.\end{cases}

We have the following result from

Lemma 2.17

Let

\left|I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(u_{k})-\frac{1}{q+1}I_{\lambda,\epsilon}^{\prime}(u_{k})u_{k}\right|\leq C+o(\|u_{k}\|),

for some positive constant
C
(independent of
k
). Since
p>1
, combining (
4.11
) and (
4.12
), our claim follows.

Step
2
.
We claim that upto a subsequence,
u_{k}\to u_{0}
strongly in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
as
k\to\infty
.

By Step
1
, since
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
, due to the reflexivity of
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
, there exists
u_{0}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
such that upto a subsequence,
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
weakly in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
as
k\to\infty
. Again, by (
4.9
), we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\mathcal{\int}_{\Omega}w(x)F(\nabla u_{k})^{p-1}\nabla_{\xi}F(\nabla u_{k})\nabla u_{0}\,dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+}+\epsilon)^{-\gamma}u_{0}\penalty\ dx-\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+})^{q}u_{0}\penalty\ dx\right)=0

and

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\mathcal{\int}_{\Omega}w(x)F(\nabla u_{k})^{p-1}\nabla_{\xi}F(\nabla u_{k})\nabla u_{k}\,dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+}+\epsilon)^{-\gamma}u_{k}\penalty\ dx-\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+})^{q}u_{k}\penalty\ dx\right)=0,

which gives

(4.13)

\begin{split}&\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}w(x)\big\{F(\nabla u_{k})^{p-1}\nabla_{\xi}{F}(\nabla u_{k})-{F}(\nabla u_{0})^{p-1}\nabla_{\xi}{F}(\nabla u_{0})\big\}\nabla(u_{k}-u_{0})\,dx\\
&=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left(\lambda\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+}+\epsilon)^{-\gamma}u_{k}\penalty\ dx+\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+})^{q}u_{k}\penalty\ dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+}+\epsilon)^{-\gamma}u_{0}\penalty\ dx-\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+})^{q}u_{0}\penalty\ dx\right)\\
&\quad-\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\int_{\Omega}w(x){F}(\nabla u_{0})^{p-1}\nabla_{\xi}{F}(\nabla u_{0})\nabla u_{k}\penalty\ dx-\int_{\Omega}w(x){F}(\nabla u_{0})^{p}\penalty\ dx\right).\end{split}

Since
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
weakly in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
as
k\to\infty
, we get

(4.14)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\int_{\Omega}w(x){F}(\nabla u_{0})^{p-1}\nabla_{\xi}{F}(\nabla u_{0})\nabla u_{k}\penalty\ dx-\int_{\Omega}w(x){F}(\nabla u_{0})^{p}\penalty\ dx\right)=0.

On the otherhand, since

\displaystyle\left|(u_{k}^{+}+\epsilon)^{-\gamma}u_{0}\right|\leq\epsilon^{-\gamma}u_{0}\text{ and }\int_{\Omega}\left|\epsilon^{-\gamma}u_{0}\right|dx\leq\epsilon^{-\gamma}\int_{\Omega}|u_{0}|\penalty\ dx<+\infty,

by the Lebesgue Dominated convergence theorem, it follows that

(4.15)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+}+\epsilon)^{-\gamma}u_{0}\penalty\ dx=\int_{\Omega}(u_{0}^{+}+\epsilon)^{-\gamma}u_{0}\penalty\ dx.

Since
u_{k}\to u_{0}
pointwise almost everywhere in
\Omega
and for any measurable subset
E
of
\Omega
,

\begin{split}\int_{E}|(u_{k}^{+}+\epsilon)^{-\gamma}u_{k}|\penalty\ dx&\leq\int_{E}\epsilon^{-\gamma}|u_{k}|\penalty\ dx\leq\|\epsilon^{-\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|u_{k}\|_{L^{l}(\Omega)}|E|^{\frac{l-1}{l}}\leq C(\epsilon)|E|^{\frac{l-1}{l}},\end{split}

using Vitali’s convergence theorem, we have

(4.16)

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\lambda\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+}+\epsilon)^{-\gamma}u_{k}\penalty\ dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}(u_{0}^{+}+\epsilon)^{-\gamma}u_{0}\penalty\ dx.

Since
q+1<l
, we have

\int_{E}|(u_{k}^{+})^{q}u_{0}|\penalty\ dx\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{l}(\Omega)}\left(\int_{E}(u_{k}^{+})^{ql^{{}^{\prime}}}\penalty\ dx\right)^{\frac{1}{l{{}^{\prime}}}}\leq C_{3}|E|^{\alpha}

and

\int_{E}|(u_{k}^{+})^{q}u_{k}|\penalty\ dx\leq\|u_{k}\|_{L^{l}(\Omega)}\left(\int_{E}(u_{k}^{+})^{ql{{}^{\prime}}}\penalty\ dx\right)^{\frac{1}{l{{}^{\prime}}}}\leq C_{4}|E|^{\beta}

for some positive constants
C_{3},C_{4},\alpha
and
\beta
. Again using Vitali’s convergence theorem, we get

(4.17)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+})^{q}u_{0}\penalty\ dx=\int_{\Omega}(u_{0}^{+})^{q}u_{0}\penalty\ dx,

and

(4.18)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}^{+})^{q}u_{k}\penalty\ dx=\int_{\Omega}(u_{0}^{+})^{q}u_{0}\penalty\ dx.

Using (
4.14
), (
4.15
), (
4.16
), (
4.17
) and (
4.18
) in (
4.13
), we obtain

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}w(x)\big({F}(\nabla u_{k})^{p-1}\nabla_{\xi}{F}(\nabla u_{k})-{F}(\nabla u_{0})^{p-1}\nabla_{\xi}{F}(\nabla u_{0})\big)\nabla(u_{k}-u_{0})\,dx=0.

Since
2\leq p<\infty
, using Lemma
2.15
, we obtain
u_{k}\to u_{0}
strongly in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
as
k\to\infty
which proves our claim.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Let
2\leq p<\infty
. Then by Lemma
4.1
, Lemma
4.2
and the Mountain Pass Lemma, for every
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda)
, there exists
\zeta_{\epsilon}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
such that
I_{\lambda,\epsilon}^{\prime}(\zeta_{\epsilon})=0
and

I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(\zeta_{\epsilon})=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\max_{t\in[0,1]}I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(\gamma(t))\geq\rho>0,

where

\Gamma=\big\{\gamma\in C([0,1],W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)):\gamma(0)=0,\gamma(1)=Te_{1}\big\}.

Moreover, as a consequence of Lemma
4.1
, since for every
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda)
we have
\inf\limits_{\|v\|\leq R}I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(v)<0
, by the weak lower semicontinuity of

, there exists a nonzero

such that
\|\nu_{\epsilon}\|\leq R
and

(4.19)

\inf\limits_{\|v\|\leq R}I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(v)=I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(\nu_{\epsilon})<0<\rho\leq I_{\lambda,\epsilon}(\zeta_{\epsilon}).

Thus,
\zeta_{\epsilon}
and
\nu_{\epsilon}
are two different non trivial critical points of
I_{\lambda,\epsilon}
, provided
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda)
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
2\leq p<\infty
, then the critical points
\zeta_{\epsilon}
and
\nu_{\epsilon}
of

are nonnegative in
\Omega.

Proof.

Let
u=\zeta_{\epsilon}
or
\nu_{\epsilon}
. Therefore, since the integrand

\lambda(u^{+}+\epsilon)^{-\gamma}+(u^{+})^{q}

is nonnegative in

, testing (

) with

, we get

This proves that

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
2\leq p<\infty
. Then there exists a constant
\Theta>0
(independent of
\epsilon
) such that
\|v_{\epsilon}\|\leq\Theta
, where
v_{\epsilon}=\zeta_{\epsilon}
or
\nu_{\epsilon}
.

Proof.

We notice that the result trivially holds if
v_{\epsilon}=\nu_{\epsilon}
. Thus, it is enough to deal with the case when
v_{\epsilon}=\zeta_{\epsilon}
. Recalling the terms from Lemma
4.1
and Remark
4.3

then

Therefore

(4.20)

\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}w(x){F}(\nabla\zeta_{\epsilon})^{p}\,dx-{\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\frac{(\zeta_{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{1-\gamma}-\epsilon^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}\penalty\ dx-\frac{1}{q+1}\int_{\Omega}\zeta_{\epsilon}^{q+1}\penalty\ dx\leq A.

Choosing

(4.21)

-\frac{1}{q+1}\int_{\Omega}w(x){F}(\nabla\zeta_{\epsilon})^{p}\,dx+\frac{\lambda}{q+1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\zeta_{\epsilon}}{(\zeta_{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{\gamma}}\,dx+\frac{1}{q+1}\int_{\Omega}\zeta_{\epsilon}^{q+1}\,dx=0.

Adding (
4.20
) and (
4.21
) we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q+1}\right)\int_{\Omega}w(x){F}(\nabla\zeta_{\epsilon})^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq{\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\frac{(\zeta_{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{1-\gamma}-\epsilon^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}\penalty\ dx-\frac{\lambda}{q+1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\zeta_{\epsilon}}{(\zeta_{\epsilon}+\epsilon)^{\gamma}}\,dx+A

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}{\zeta_{\epsilon}}^{1-\gamma}+A\leq C\|\zeta_{\epsilon}\|^{1-\gamma}+A,

for some positive constant
C
being independent of
\epsilon
, where we have used Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
2.2
. Thus, since
q+1>p
, the sequence
\{\zeta_{\epsilon}\}
is uniformly bounded in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
with respect to
\epsilon
. This completes the proof.
∎

.

{\lambda}{(t+\epsilon)^{-\gamma}}+t^{q}\geq{\lambda}{(t+1)^{-\gamma}}+t^{q}\geq\text{min}\left\{1,\frac{\lambda}{2}\right\}:=C>0,\text{ say}.

\int_{\Omega}w(x)\big\{{F}(\nabla\xi)^{p-1}\nabla_{\xi}{F}(\nabla\xi)-{F}(\nabla v_{\epsilon})^{p-1}\nabla_{\xi}{F}(\nabla v_{\epsilon})\big\}\nabla(\xi-v_{\epsilon})^{+}\,dx\leq 0.

, by Lemma
2.15
, we have
v_{\epsilon}\geq\xi
in
\Omega

v_{\epsilon}\geq c(\omega)>0,\text{ for every }\omega\Subset\Omega.

Step

Weighted anisotropic eigenvalue problem:
In this section, we consider an weighted anisotropic eigenvalue problem, which is crucial to obtain our main results above. Related eigenvalue problem in the absence of weight functions has been considered in
[
9
,
39
]
. Eigenvalue problems for the weighted
p
-Laplace equation is studied in
[
20
]
. We study the following weighted ansiotropic eigenvalue problem,

(5.1)

-{F}_{p,w}u=\lambda a(x)|u|^{p-2}u\text{ in }\Omega,\quad u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

where
1<p<\infty
,
w\in W_{p}^{s}
for some
s\in I
and
a
is a nonnegative measurable function in
\Omega
. Further, we assume that
a\in L^{\frac{q}{q-p}}(\Omega)
for some
p<q<p_{s}^{*}
where
p_{s}^{*}=\frac{Np_{s}}{N-p_{s}}
if
1\leq p_{s}<N
and
p_{s}^{*}=\infty
if
p_{s}\geq N
. If
q=p
, we assume
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Moreover, let
|x\in\Omega:a(x)>0|>0
. Below, we prove the result in case of
q>p
. In case of
q=p
, the proof is analogous.

Definition 5.1
.

We say that
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is an eigenvalue of the problem (
5.1
), if there exists
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)\setminus\{0\}
such that for every
\phi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
, we have

(5.2)

\int_{\Omega}w(x){F}(\nabla u)^{p-1}\nabla_{\xi}{F}(\nabla u)\nabla\phi\,dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{p-1}u\phi\,dx.

We define
u
to be an eigenfunction of (
5.1
) corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda
.

In this section our main result reads as follows:

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
2\leq p<\infty
. Then there exists the least one eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}>0
and at least one corresponding eigenfunction
e_{1}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
which is nonnegative in
\Omega
such that for every
\omega\Subset\Omega
, there exists a positive constant
c(\omega)
such that
e_{1}\geq c(\omega)>0
in
\omega
.

Proof.

Existence and nonnegativity:
We claim that

\lambda_{1}:=\inf\left\{\int_{\Omega}w(x){F}(\nabla u)^{p}\,dx:\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u|^{p}\,dx=1\right\}

is the least eigenvalue of the problem (
5.1
). It is clear that
\lambda_{1}\geq 0
. Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
be a minimizing sequence for
\lambda_{1}
, which gives

(5.3)

\int_{\Omega}a(x)|u_{n}|^{p}\,dx=1\text{ and }\int_{\Omega}w(x){F}(\nabla u_{n})^{p}\,dx=\lambda_{1}+\delta_{n},

where
\delta_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Therefore,
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
and so by the reflexivity of
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
and Lemma
2.2
, upto a subsequence
u_{n}\rightharpoonup e_{1}
weakly in
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)
and strongly in
L^{q}(\Omega)
. Now proceeding along the lines of the proof of
[
20
, Lemma
3.1
]
, we obtain the existence of a nonnegative eigenfunction
e_{1}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega,w)\setminus\{0\}
such that
\int_{\Omega}a(x)|e_{1}|^{p}\,dx=1
and

(5.4)

\lambda_{1}=\int_{\Omega}w(x){F}(\nabla e_{1})^{p}\,dx

is the eigenvalue of the problem (
5.1
).

Uniform positivity:
Since
e_{1}\neq 0
, we have
\lambda_{1}>0
and therefore noting Lemma
2.14
and Lemma
2.15
, applying
[
34
, Thoerem
3.59
]
, for every
\omega\Subset\Omega
, there exists a positive constant
c(\omega)
such that
e_{1}\geq c(\omega)>0
in
\omega
. This also gives
e_{1}>0
in
\Omega
.

Boundedness:
Let
k>0
and define by
u_{k}(x)=\min\{e_{1}(x),k\}
. For
m\geq 0
, choosing
\phi=u_{k}^{mp+1}
as a test function in (
5.2
) we obtain

(5.5)

\int_{\Omega}w(x){F}(\nabla e_{1})^{p-1}\nabla_{\xi}{F}(\nabla e_{1})\nabla u_{k}^{mp+1}\,dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}a(x)e_{1}^{p-1}u_{k}^{mp+1}\,dx.

By Lemma
2.14
, for some positive constant
c_{1}
, we observe that

\int_{\Omega}w(x){F}(\nabla e_{1})^{p-1}\nabla_{\xi}{F}(\nabla e_{1})\nabla u_{k}^{mp+1}\,dx\geq c_{1}(mp+1)\int_{\Omega}w(x)u_{k}^{mp}|\nabla u_{k}|^{p}\,dx.

Now using the embedding result in Lemma
2.2
and proceeding with the same proof of
[
20
, Lemma
3.2
]
, we obtain
e_{1}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
∎

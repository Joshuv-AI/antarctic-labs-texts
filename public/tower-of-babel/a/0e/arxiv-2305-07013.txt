Computing Unique Information for Poisson and Multinomial Systems
Chaitanya Goswami, Amanda Merkley, and Pulkit Grover

Abstract
Bivariate Partial Information Decomposition (PID) describes how the mutual information between a random variable M M and two random variables Y Y and Z Z is decomposed into unique, redundant, and synergistic terms. Recently, PID has shown promise as an emerging tool to understand biological systems and biases in machine learning. However, computing PID is a challenging problem as it typically involves optimizing over distributions. In this work, we study the problem of computing PID in two systems: the Poisson system inspired by the “ideal Poisson channel” and the multinomial system inspired by multinomial thinning, for a scalar M M . We provide sufficient conditions for both systems under which closed-form expressions for many operationally-motivated PID can be obtained, thereby allowing us to easily compute PID for these systems. Our proof consists of showing that one of the unique information terms is zero, which allows the remaining unique, redundant, and synergistic terms to be easily computed using only the marginal and the joint mutual information.

1-\left[\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}}p_{j}\right]\cdot\left[\sum_{j=1}^{k_{2}-1}q_{j}\right],&\ i=k_{2}.\end{cases}

(19)

To obtain the UI result for the multinomial system, we show that we can construct a valid Markov distribution that lies in
\Delta_{P}
. Hence, one of the UI terms is zero.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose
M,Y,
and
Z
are as defined in the multinomial system described above. Let
I_{Y}\mkern 1.2mu{=}\mkern 1.2mu[s_{y}]
and

be the set of class indices of
Y
and

\displaystyle\min_{i\in I_{Z}}p_{z_{i}}\geq\min_{i\in I_{Y}}p_{y_{i}},

\Delta_{P}

Proof.

We define
\tilde{Q}(M,Y,Z)\mkern 1.2mu{=}\mkern 1.2muP(M)P(Y|M)\tilde{Q}(Z|Y)
with
\tilde{Q}(Z|Y)\mkern 1.2mu{=}\mkern 1.2mu\text{Multinomial}\left(\mathbf{z};\sum_{i\in I_{Y}^{-}}y_{i},\mathbf{p_{z}}^{*}\right)
. Here,
I_{Y}^{-}\mkern 1.2mu{=}\mkern 1.2muI_{Y}\backslash\argmin_{i\in I_{Y}}p_{y_{i}}
is the set of class indices of

\displaystyle\mathbf{p_{z}}^{*}\mkern 1.2mu{=}\mkern 1.2mu\begin{bmatrix}\frac{p_{z_{1}}}{\sum_{i\in I_{Y}^{-}}p_{y_{i}}}&\ldots&\frac{p_{z_{s_{z}-1}}}{\sum_{i\in I_{Y}^{-}}p_{y_{i}}}&1-\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{s_{z}-1}p_{z_{i}}}{\sum_{i\in I_{Y}^{-}}p_{y_{i}}}\end{bmatrix}^{T}.

\displaystyle\min_{i\in I_{Z}}p_{z_{i}}\geq\min_{i\in I_{Y}}p_{y_{i}}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\sum_{i\in I_{Y}^{-}}p_{y_{i}}\geq\sum_{i\in I_{Z}^{-}}p_{z_{i}}\geq p_{z_{i}},\;\forall i\in I_{Z}

(23)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow 1\geq\frac{p_{z_{i}}}{\sum_{i\in I_{Y}^{-}}p_{y_{i}}}\geq 0,\ \forall i\in I_{Z},

(24)

where the last inequality is due to

V
Discussion and Limitations

We study the bivariate PID for the Poisson system and the multinomial system. We provide sufficient conditions for both systems under which one of the UI terms in their respective PID is zero, thereby facilitating the computation and estimation of the remaining PID terms. Our results can alternatively be interpreted as sufficient conditions for checking the stochastic degradeness between two Poisson channels (defined in Sec.
III-A
) and two multinomial channels (defined in Sec.
IV
).

In this work, our results are restricted to
scalar

M
for both systems. Future work would address the extension to a vector
M
, as was done for the Gaussian case in
[
12
]
. There also exist alternative definitions of multivariate Poisson distributions
[
41
]
, which can be considered for constructing the Poisson system. The multivariate Poisson distribution used in this work only allows for positive covariance, limiting its modelling capabilities.

Notably, given that there exist three distinct systems (Poisson, multinomial, and Gaussian) for scalar
M
in which one of the UI terms is zero, an interesting future direction is discovering the commonality between these systems to obtain a more general characterization of systems in which UI can be reduced to zero.

Let
\mathbf{K}\sim\text{Poisson}(d,d^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\Lambda})

\mathbf{K}

d

\mathbf{K}=A\mathbf{K}_{g}

85

84

89

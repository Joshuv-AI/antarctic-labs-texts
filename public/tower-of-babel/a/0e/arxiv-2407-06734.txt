Variation of the one-dimensional centered maximal operator on simple functions with gaps between pieces
Paul Hagelstein, Dariusz Kosz, Krzysztof Stempak

Abstract
. Let M M denote the centered Hardy–Littlewood operator on ℝ \mathbb{R} . 1.
Introduction

Regularity properties of maximal operators have been investigated in numerous papers and various frameworks. Kinnunen
[
10
]
initiated the study
of boundedness of the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator on the Sobolev spaces
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
p\in(1,\infty)
. Subsequently,
[
10
]
was complemented
by Kinnunen and Lindqvist
[
11
]
to the setting of open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. See also Tanaka
[
17
]
for the case
p=1
and
n=1
.
Starting from the Euclidean setting and the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operators, both centered and uncentered, the study then encompassed their variants
such as fractional maximal operators or maximal operators of convolution type; see
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
, and references therein.
Also discrete analogues of these operators were considered, and related questions in both settings have been recently studied; see
[
15
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
8
]
.

It was an important observation of Aldaz and Pérez Lázaro
[
1
]
that maximal operators can actually improve the regularity of involved
functions rather than simply preserve it. They also pointed out the role to be played by the variation when measuring the regularity.
In particular, it was proved in
[
1
]
that if
f\in{\rm BV}(\mathbb{R})
, then for the uncentered Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator
\widetilde{M}

the function
\widetilde{M}f
is absolutely continuous on
\mathbb{R}
and
{\rm Var}(\widetilde{M}f)\leq{\rm Var}(f)
. For the centered operator
M
, on the other hand, Kurka
[
12
]
proved

{\rm Var}(Mf)\leq C{\rm Var}(f),\qquad f\in{\rm BV}(\mathbb{R}),

with some, quite large,
C>0
. It was conjectured that also in this case
C=1
is enough, see e.g.
[
12
, Section 1]
. In the discrete case,
i.e., for
f\in{\rm BV}(\mathbb{Z})
and the discrete counterpart of
M
,
the same was conjectured, see
[
3
, Question B]
. It is worth recalling the common truth that the uncentered maximal operator has better
regularity behavior than its centered counterpart. Furthermore, the proofs of analogous results are usually much subtler in the centered setting.

It seems that both conjectures, although reasonable and expected to be true, do not take the full advantage of how the limits
f(\pm\infty)
or
F(\pm\infty)

for
f\in{\rm BV}(\mathbb{R})
or
F\in{\rm BV}(\mathbb{Z})
, respectively, determine the limits
Mf(\pm\infty)
or
MF(\pm\infty)
; see Section
3
. We thus propose the following strengthening, which would complement analogous inequalities for other maximal operators; see Remark
4.1
.

Conjecture
\Delta
.

Suppose
f\in{\rm BV}(\mathbb{R})
. Then

(1.1)

{\rm Var}(Mf)\leq{\rm Var}(f)-\frac{1}{2}\big||f(\infty)|-|f(-\infty)|\big|.

Our first main result, Theorem
1.1
, supports this conjecture. We mention that there is no need to formulate separately the discrete
counterpart of Conjecture

Δ

, since by our second main result, Theorem
2.2
, inequality (
1.1
) and its discrete analogue are equivalent. Clearly, verification of either version of
the conjecture, continuous or discrete, reduces to the consideration of nonnegative functions. We shall prove that a certain further reduction to nonnegative simple functions is available; see Proposition
2.3
. Finally, we show that the constants
1

and
\frac{1}{2}
in (
1.1
) are optimal; see Section
4
.

Let
\mathbb{N}\coloneqq\{1,2,\dots\}
. Let
\mathbb{K}
be either
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{Z}
. For
g\colon\mathbb{K}\to\mathbb{C}
and any nonempty
E\subseteq\mathbb{K}
define

{\rm Var}_{E}(g)\coloneqq\sup_{\text{monotone }\phi\colon\mathbb{Z}\to E}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|g\big(\phi(m+1)\big)-g\big(\phi(m)\big)\big|,

which we call the
variation of

g

over

E
.
We write
g\in{\rm BV}(\mathbb{K})
when the
total variation

{\rm Var}(g)\coloneqq{\rm Var}_{\mathbb{K}}(g)
is finite. For each such
g
the limits

g(\pm\infty)\coloneqq\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}g(x)
exist and, when
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}
the one-sided limits
g(x^{\pm})\coloneqq\lim_{y\to x^{\pm}}g(y)

exist as well.

Recall the definition of the centered Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator
M
. We set

Mg(x)\coloneqq\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{2r}\int_{x-r}^{x+r}|g|\quad\text{and}\quad MG(n)\coloneqq\sup_{m\geq 0}\frac{1}{2m+1}\sum_{y=n-m}^{n+m}|G(y)|

for any locally integrable
g\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, and for any
G\colon\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Of course, if
g\in{\rm BV}(\mathbb{R})
, then
g
is bounded, and hence so is
Mg
. The same is true for
MG
when
G\in{\rm BV}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Our first main result says that (
1.1
) holds for a certain special class of functions.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
-\infty<A<B<+\infty
,
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
,
K\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\{\alpha_{k}\}_{1}^{K}\subset\mathbb{C}
such that
\alpha_{k}\alpha_{k+1}=0
,
1\leq k\leq K-1
, be given.
For a system
-\infty<A=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{K}=B<+\infty

define

f(x)\coloneqq a{\mathds{1}_{{(-\infty,A)}}}(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_{k}{\mathds{1}_{{[t_{k-1},t_{k})}}}(x)+b{\mathds{1}_{{[B,\infty)}}}(x)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

(1.2)

{\rm Var}(Mf)\leq{\rm Var}(f)-\frac{1}{2}\big||a|-|b|\big|.

Similarly, replacing the system of real numbers
\{t_{k}\}_{0}^{K}
by a system of integers
-\infty<A=n_{0}<n_{1}<\dots<n_{K}=B<+\infty
, and assuming
\alpha_{k}\alpha_{k+1}=0
,
1\leq k\leq K-1
, define

F(n)\coloneqq a{\mathds{1}_{{(-\infty,A)}}}(n)+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_{k}{\mathds{1}_{{[n_{k-1},n_{k})}}}(n)+b{\mathds{1}_{{[B,\infty)}}}(n)

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then

(1.3)

{\rm Var}(MF)\leq{\rm Var}(F)-\frac{1}{2}\big||a|-|b|\big|.

Theorem
1.1
implies (
1.2
) or (
1.3
) for all indicator functions of bounded variation,
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\{0,1\}
or
F\colon\mathbb{Z}\to\{0,1\}
, respectively, thus strengthening

[
4
, Theorems 1.1 and 1.3]
.
On the other hand, Theorem
1.1
does not recover
[
4
, Theorem 1.2]
, which asserts that
{\rm Var}(Mf)\leq{\rm Var}(f)
holds for every
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
satisfying
f(x)=0
or
f(x)=Mf(x)
for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Notice the
separation condition

\alpha_{k}\alpha_{k+1}=0
encoded in the systems

\{t_{k}\}_{0}^{K}
and
\{n_{k}\}_{0}^{K}
.
In fact, our techniques allow us to skip this condition at the expense of using
|\alpha_{k}|+|\alpha_{k+1}|
, not
|\alpha_{k+1}-\alpha_{k}|
, on the right-hand side of (
1.2
) or (
1.3
). For example, if
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Z}
and
a=b=0
, then (
1.3
) would take the form
{\rm Var}(MF)\leq 2(|\alpha_{1}|+\dots+|\alpha_{K}|)
. Building on this, one can recover
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
, which asserts that
{\rm Var}(MF)\leq 2\|F\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})}
holds for all
F\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Moreover, by combining Theorem
1.1
with
[
16
, Subsection 5.3]
, we obtain a variant of (
1.2
) with
M
replaced by
M^{\theta}
for any
\theta>0
, where
M^{\theta}
is the nontangential maximal operator studied in
[
16
]
.

Lastly, our methods differ from
[
4
]
and align more closely with
[
14
]
. In particular, the approach in Lemma
3.2
resembles
[
14
, Section 2]
, though it gains additional efficiency through Lemma
3.1
.

Throughout the paper,
I
with possible subscripts and/or superscripts affixed will denote an open bounded subinterval of
\mathbb{R}
. Then
c(I),l(I),r(I)
will be the center and the left/right endpoint of
I
, respectively.
As already mentioned,
{\rm Var}
with no subscripts,
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{Z}
, will denote the total variation; it will be clear from the context to which situation this symbol refers to. Appearance of a subscript will indicate that the variation is related to a subset represented by this subscript.

2.
Transference

The main goal of this section is to prove a transference result, Theorem
2.2
, which seems to be of independent interest. We begin with a simple result that exhibits a substantial improvement of regularity of
M
. Namely, given
g\in{\rm BV}(\mathbb{R})
, not only does
Mg
have one-sided limits at every
x\in\mathbb{R}
but it is one-sided continuous as well. Of course, the first claim follows, since
Mg\in{\rm BV}(\mathbb{R})
, cf.
[
12
]
, but we decided to provide a short proof of this fact to make the whole argument self-contained.

Lemma 2.1
.

For a generic function
g\in{\rm BV}(\mathbb{R})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
the limits
Mg(x^{\pm})
exist and we have

(2.1)

Mg(x)=\min\{Mg(x^{-}),Mg(x^{+})\}.

Proof.

We can assume
g\geq 0
. Regarding the existence of the limits, fix
\varepsilon>0
and take
\delta>0
such that
|g(y)-g(x^{-})|<\varepsilon
for
y\in(x-2\delta,x)
and
|g(y)-g(x^{+})|<\varepsilon
for
y\in(x,x+2\delta)
. Let

\tilde{g}(y)
be
g(x^{-})
,
g(x^{+})
, or
g(y)
, if
y
belongs to
(x-2\delta,x)
,
[x,x+2\delta)
, or
\mathbb{R}\setminus(x-2\delta,x+2\delta)
, respectively. Then
|Mg(y)-M\tilde{g}(y)|\leq\varepsilon
for
y\in\mathbb{R}
. Note that
M\tilde{g}=\max\{M_{<\delta}\tilde{g},M_{\geq\delta}\tilde{g}\}
, where
M_{<\delta}
and
M_{\geq\delta}
allow only the centered intervals
I
with
|I|<2\delta
and
|I|\geq 2\delta
, respectively. If
y\in(x,x+\delta)
, then

M_{<\delta}\tilde{g}(y)=\max\big\{g(x^{+}),(2\delta)^{-1}\big((\delta-y+x)g(x^{-})+(\delta+y-x)g(x^{+})\big)\big\},

whereas if
x<y<z<x+\delta
, then

|M_{\geq\delta}\tilde{g}(z)-M_{\geq\delta}\tilde{g}(y)|\leq(2\delta)^{-1}(z-y){\rm Var}(g),

since for
r\geq\delta
we have

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1}{2r}\int_{z-r}^{z+r}\tilde{g}-\frac{1}{2r}\int_{y-r}^{y+r}\tilde{g}\Big|=\int_{y+r}^{z+r}\frac{|\tilde{g}(t)-\tilde{g}(t-2r)|}{2r}\,dt\leq\frac{(z-y){\rm Var}(g)}{2r}\leq\frac{(z-y){\rm Var}(g)}{2\delta}.

Thus,
M\tilde{g}(x^{+})
exists and so
\limsup_{t\to x^{+}}Mg(t)-\liminf_{t\to x^{+}}Mg(t)\leq 2\varepsilon
. Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain that
Mg(x^{+})
exists as well. Analogously we verify that
Mg(x^{-})
exists.

Regarding (
2.1
), we have
Mg(x)\leq\min\{Mg(x^{-}),Mg(x^{+})\}
by lower semicontinuity of
Mg
. To prove the opposite inequality, it suffices to consider the case

\min\{Mg(x^{-}),Mg(x^{+})\}>\frac{g(x^{-})+g(x^{+})}{2}
because

Mg(x)\geq\frac{g(x^{-})+g(x^{+})}{2}.

Assume
g(x^{-})\leq g(x^{+})
(the case
g(x^{-})\geq g(x^{+})
is symmetric). Now

Mg(x^{-})>\max\{g(x^{-}),\frac{g(x^{-})+g(x^{+})}{2}\}
and so
Mg(y)=M_{\geq\delta}g(y)
for some
\delta>0
and all
y\in(x-\delta,x)
. Since, as before,

|M_{\geq\delta}g(x)-M_{\geq\delta}g(y)|\leq(2\delta)^{-1}(x-y){\rm Var}(g)
for all such
y
,
the proof is complete.
∎

Now, for
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Z}
, and
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
, define

{\rm BV}_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{K})\coloneqq\{g\in{\rm BV}(\mathbb{K}):g(-\infty)=a,\,g(+\infty)=b\}.

By
C(\mathbb{K})
we denote the smallest value
C\in(0,\infty]
such that

{\rm Var}(Mg)\leq C{\rm Var}(g),\qquad g\in{\rm BV}(\mathbb{K}),

and by
c_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{K})
we denote the largest value
c\in[0,\infty)
such that

{\rm Var}(Mg)\leq C(\mathbb{K}){\rm Var}(g)-c,\qquad g\in{\rm BV}_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{K}),

with the convention
c_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{K})=0
if
C(\mathbb{K})=\infty
.

The transference result that follows complements
[
4
, Proposition 1.4]
. We include the potential case
C(\mathbb{K})=\infty
to emphasize that the proof works independently of whether
C(\mathbb{K})
is finite or not. Nonetheless, in
[
12
]
it was shown that
C(\mathbb{R})<\infty
, thus Theorem
2.2
, even in the weaker form with the additional assumption
C(\mathbb{K})<\infty
, immediately gives
C(\mathbb{Z})<\infty
.

Theorem 2.2
.

We have
C(\mathbb{Z})=C(\mathbb{R})
and
c_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{Z})=c_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{R})
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

We first show
C(\mathbb{Z})\leq C(\mathbb{R})
and
c_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{Z})\geq c_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{R})
by using a natural extension procedure, cf.
[
4
, Lemma 4.1]
.
If
C(\mathbb{R})=\infty
, then
c_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{R})=0
and there is nothing to show. Assume therefore that
C(\mathbb{R})<\infty
.
Given
G\in{\rm BV}_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{Z})
, consider

g\coloneqq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}G(n){\mathds{1}_{{[n-1/2,n+1/2)}}}.

Then
{\rm Var}(g)={\rm Var}(G)
and so
g\in{\rm BV}_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{R})
. Note that
Mg(n)=MG(n)
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, since, for
m\in\mathbb{N}
, one of the averages of
|g|
over the centered intervals
I

of lengths
|I|=2m\pm 1
dominates those with
|I|\in[2m-1,2m+1]
. Thus,
{\rm Var}(MG)={\rm Var}_{\mathbb{Z}}(Mg)\leq{\rm Var}(Mg)
, and the claimed inequalities hold.

In the opposite direction, we claim that
C(\mathbb{Z})\geq C(\mathbb{R})
and
c_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{Z})\leq c_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{R})
, and use a convenient variant of a sampling procedure.
Obviously, we can assume
C(\mathbb{Z})<\infty
.
Given
g\in{\rm BV}_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{R})
and
\varepsilon>0
, in view of (
2.1
), there is
N_{*}=N_{*}(\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for
E_{N_{*}}\coloneqq 2^{-N_{*}}\mathbb{Z}\cap[-2^{N_{*}},2^{N_{*}}]
we have

(2.2)

{\rm Var}_{E_{N_{*}}}(Mg)\geq{\rm Var}(Mg)-\varepsilon\quad\text{or}\quad{\rm Var}_{E_{N_{*}}}(Mg)\geq\varepsilon^{-1},

depending on whether
{\rm Var}(Mg)
is finite or not, respectively. For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let
M_{N}
be the variant of
M
allowing only the centered intervals
I
with
|I|\in(2\mathbb{N}-1)/2^{N}
. We have
\lim_{N\to\infty}M_{N}g=Mg
pointwise, since for
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
\delta>0
there are
0<\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}<\infty
such that
\frac{1}{2\rho}\int_{x-\rho}^{x+\rho}|g|\geq Mg-\delta
for all
\rho\in[\rho_{1},\rho_{2}]
. Thus, there exists
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

{\rm Var}_{E_{N_{*}}}(M_{N}g)\geq{\rm Var}_{E_{N_{*}}}(Mg)-\varepsilon

for all
N\geq N_{0}
.
Consider
\tilde{G}_{N}(n)\coloneqq 2^{N}\int_{(n-1/2)/2^{N}}^{(n+1/2)/2^{N}}|g|
and
G_{N}(n)\coloneqq 2^{N}\int_{(n-1/2)/2^{N}}^{(n+1/2)/2^{N}}g
. Then

(2.3)

\max\{{\rm Var}(\tilde{G}_{N}),{\rm Var}(G_{N})\}\leq\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}{\rm Var}_{(\mathbb{Z}+t)/2^{N}}(g)\,{\rm d}t\leq{\rm Var}(g),

so that
\tilde{G}_{N}\in{\rm BV}_{|a|}^{|b|}(\mathbb{Z})
and
G_{N}\in{\rm BV}_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{Z})
. Also,
MG_{N}(n)\leq M\tilde{G}_{N}(n)=M_{N}g(n/2^{N})
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Now, we show that
MG_{N}(n)\geq M\tilde{G}_{N}(n)-\varepsilon/2^{2N_{*}+4}
for all
n\in E_{N_{*}}^{N}\coloneqq\{2^{N}Q:Q\in E_{N_{*}}\}\subset\mathbb{N}
, provided that
N\geq\max\{N_{*},N_{0}\}
is large enough.
Given
Q\in E_{N_{*}}
, we have

|I_{Q}|^{-1}\int_{I_{Q}}|g|\geq M\tilde{G}_{N}(2^{N}Q)-\varepsilon/2^{2N_{*}+5}

for some
I_{Q}
centered at
Q
. For each
N
large enough, let
I_{Q}^{N}
be the longest interval centered at
Q
, contained in
I_{Q}
, with
|I_{Q}^{N}|\in(2\mathbb{N}-1)/2^{N}
. Denoting
g_{N}\coloneqq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}G_{N}(n){\mathds{1}_{{[(n-1/2)/2^{N},(n+1/2)/2^{N})}}}
, we see that
\lim_{N\to\infty}|I_{Q}^{N}|^{-1}\int_{I_{Q}^{N}}|g_{N}|=|I_{Q}|^{-1}\int_{I_{Q}}|g|
by the dominated convergence theorem on
I_{Q}
, because
g
is continuous almost everywhere and bounded.
Since
MG_{N}(2^{N}Q)\geq|I_{Q}^{N}|^{-1}\int_{I_{Q}^{N}}|g_{N}|
and
E_{N_{*}}
is finite,
MG_{N}(n)\geq M\tilde{G}_{N}(n)-\varepsilon/2^{2N_{*}+4}
for some
N\geq\max\{N_{*},N_{0}\}
, as desired. Thus,

(2.4)

{\rm Var}_{E_{N_{*}}}(Mg)-\varepsilon\leq{\rm Var}_{E_{N_{*}}}(M_{N}g)={\rm Var}_{E_{N_{*}}^{N}}(M\tilde{G}_{N})\leq{\rm Var}_{E_{N_{*}}^{N}}(MG_{N})+\varepsilon\leq{\rm Var}(MG_{N})+\varepsilon.

Assuming
{\rm Var}(Mg)=\infty
gives

\varepsilon^{-1}\leq{\rm Var}_{E_{N_{*}}}(Mg)\leq{\rm Var}(MG_{N})+2\varepsilon\leq C(\mathbb{Z}){\rm Var}(g)-c_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{Z})+2\varepsilon,

which contradicts our assumption
C(\mathbb{Z})<\infty
. Consequently,
{\rm Var}(Mg)<\infty
(this includes the case
C(\mathbb{R})<\infty
). Applying successively
the first part of (
2.2
), (
2.4
), the definitions of
C(\mathbb{Z})
and
c_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{Z})
in the context of
G_{N}\in{\rm BV}_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{Z})
, and (
2.3
) for
G_{N}
, and then letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we verify the claim.
∎

Later on, we shall refer to the two methods from the proof of Theorem
2.2
as to the
extension procedure
and the
sampling procedure
, respectively.

Below we show that proving Conjecture

Δ

reduces to consideration of nonnegative simple functions.
We call
G\in{\rm BV}(\mathbb{Z})

simple
, abusing
slightly the common terminology, if there exist
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
G(-n)=a
and
G(n)=b
when
n>N
. Similarly,
g\in{\rm BV}(\mathbb{R})
is
simple
if
G(-x)=a
and
G(x)=b
when
x>N
, and
g
is constant on the intervals
[n-\frac{1}{2},n+\frac{1}{2})
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Conjecture

Δ

is true if (
1.1
) is satisfied by nonnegative simple functions.

Proof.

It is convenient to prove a version of the above for the discrete case,
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Z}
, and then apply Theorem
2.2
and the extension procedure to obtain the result for
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
.

Given nonnegative
G\in{\rm BV}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\varepsilon>0
, we have
{\rm Var}_{[-N_{*},N_{*}]\cap\mathbb{Z}}(MG)\geq{\rm Var}(MG)-\varepsilon
for some
N_{*}=N_{*}(\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
.
Note that
G\in{\rm BV}_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{Z})
for some
a,b\in[0,\infty)
. We choose
N=N(\varepsilon,N_{*})\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|G(-n)-a|<\varepsilon/(5N_{*})
and
|G(n)-b|<\varepsilon/(5N_{*})

if
n>N
.
Let
G_{N}(n)
be equal to
a
,
G(n)
, or
b
, for
n<N
,
n\in[-N,N]
, or
n>N
, respectively. Then

{\rm Var}_{[-N_{*},N_{*}]\cap\mathbb{Z}}(MG_{N})\geq{\rm Var}_{[-N_{*},N_{*}]\cap\mathbb{Z}}(MG)-\varepsilon\geq{\rm Var}(MG)-2\varepsilon,

because
|MG_{N}(n)-MG(n)|\leq\varepsilon/(5N_{*})
. Since
{\rm Var}(G_{N})\leq{\rm Var}(G)
, we are done.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

For a generic function
g\in{\rm BV}(\mathbb{K})
one has

Mg\geq\frac{|g(-\infty)|+|g(\infty)|}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad Mg(\pm\infty)=\max\Big\{|g(\pm\infty)|,\frac{|g(-\infty)|+|g(\infty)|}{2}\Big\}.

We say that
Mg
has a
local maximum
at
x\in\mathbb{K}
if there exist
y^{\prime},y^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{K}
such that
y^{\prime}<x<y^{\prime\prime}
and

(3.1)

\displaystyle\max\{Mg(y^{\prime}),Mg(y^{\prime\prime})\}<\sup\{Mg(y):y\in\mathbb{K}\cap[y^{\prime},y^{\prime\prime}]\}=Mg(x);

a
local minimum
of
Mg
at
x
is defined analogously.

We are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
. By the extension procedure it suffices to deal with

\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
.
Indeed, given
F\in{\rm BV}_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{Z})
as in Theorem
1.1
with the corresponding system
\{n_{k}\}_{0}^{K}
, define
f\in{\rm BV}_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{R})
with the
corresponding system
\{t_{k}\}_{0}^{K}
,
t_{k}=n_{k}-\frac{1}{2}
, by
f\coloneqq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}F(n){\mathds{1}_{{[n-1/2,n+1/2)}}}
. By using the separation condition
\alpha_{k}\alpha_{k+1}=0
for
F
, we verify that
f
has the form as in Theorem
1.1
. Moreover,
{\rm Var}(f)={\rm Var}(F)
and

{\rm Var}(Mf)\geq{\rm Var}(MF)
. Then (
1.3
) for
F
follows from (
1.2
) for
f
.

Until the end of this section we fix
f
as in Theorem
1.1
and denote
I_{k}\coloneqq(t_{k-1},t_{k})
,
k=1,\ldots,K
, the interiors of the corresponding intervals.
Without any loss of generality we may consider only nonnegative
f
. Hence, from now on, we assume
a,b\geq 0
and
\alpha_{k}\geq 0
,
k=1,\ldots,K
. Clearly,
Mf
may not have local maxima at all. However, if
Mf
has a local maximum at
x
, then the following two cases may occur:

(1)

Mf
is constant on some interval
J\ni x
, i.e.,
Mf
has a local maximum at each point of
J
;

(2)

there is no
J
with this property, i.e.,
x
is
isolated
.

In the first case, considering
J
as the longest interval with the declared property, we shall choose exactly one element
x_{*}\in J
as a
representative
. In addition every isolated point
x
from the second case, if any, will also be called a representative. It follows from Lemma
3.1
that the number of representatives
is finite and does not exceed
K^{2}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
Mf
have a local maximum at
x
. Then there exists
x_{*}
(either equal to
x
or such that
Mf
is constant on the closed interval connecting
x
and
x_{*}
) with the property that
Mf(x_{*})
is attained for some interval
I_{x_{*}}
which is centered at
x_{*}
and such that
l(I_{x_{*}})=l(I_{k^{\prime}})
and
r(I_{x_{*}})=r(I_{k^{\prime\prime}})
hold for some
1\leq k^{\prime}\leq k^{\prime\prime}\leq K
satisfying
Mf(x_{*})\leq\min\{\alpha_{k^{\prime}},\alpha_{k^{\prime\prime}}\}
.

Proof.

Take any sequence
\{\rho_{n}\}\subset(0,\infty)
such that
Mf(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2\rho_{n}}\int_{x-\rho_{n}}^{x+\rho_{n}}f
. Clearly, if
\{\rho_{n}\}
has
a subsequence diverging to
\infty
, then
Mf(y)\geq Mf(x)
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus,
\{\rho_{n}\}
is bounded.

Assume
\{\rho_{n}\}
has a subsequence converging to
0
. Then
Mf(x)=\frac{f(x^{-})+f(x^{+})}{2}
and so
f(x^{-})=f(x^{+})=Mf(x)
by
Mf(x)\geq Mf(x^{\pm})\geq f(x^{\pm})
, where the first inequality holds because
Mf
has a local maximum at
x
. If
x\leq A
, then
Mf(x)=f(x^{-})=a=\frac{f(y^{-})+f(y^{+})}{2}\leq Mf(y)
for all
y<x
, contradicting (
3.1
). Similarly,
x\geq B
is impossible. Thus,
x\in I_{k}
and
Mf(x)=\alpha_{k}
for some
k
. We can replace
x
by
c(I_{k})
, since
Mf(y)\geq Mf(x)
for all
y\in I_{k}
so that
Mf
is constant
on
I_{k}
because
Mf
has a local maximum at
x
. Taking
x_{*}=c(I_{k})
and
I_{x_{*}}=I_{k}
, we obtain the claimed property.

If
\{\rho_{n}\}
is separated from
0
, then it contains a subsequence converging to a positive number. Consequently,
Mf(x)=\frac{1}{|I_{x}|}\int_{I_{x}}f
for some
I_{x}
centered at
x
. Note that
f\big(l(I_{x})^{+}\big)\geq Mf(x)
, since otherwise we could show
Mf(y)>Mf(x)
for some
\delta>0
and all
y\in(x,x+\delta)
by taking
I_{y}
with
r(I_{y})=r(I_{x})
. In particular,
l(I_{x})\geq A
because otherwise we would have
Mf(y)\geq Mf(x)
for all
y\leq x
, again by taking
I_{y}
with
r(I_{y})=r(I_{x})
.
Thus,
l(I_{x})\in\big[l(I_{k^{\prime}}),r(I_{k^{\prime}})\big)
for some
k^{\prime}
such that
\alpha_{k^{\prime}}\geq Mf(x)
. Similarly,
f\big(r(I_{x})^{-}\big)\geq Mf(x)
and
r(I_{x})\in\big(l(I_{k^{\prime\prime}}),r(I_{k^{\prime\prime}})\big]
for some
k^{\prime\prime}\geq k^{\prime}
such that
\alpha_{k^{\prime\prime}}\geq Mf(x)
. Let
x_{*}=\frac{l(I_{k^{\prime}})+r(I_{k^{\prime\prime}})}{2}
. If
x=x_{*}
, then
Mf(x)
is attained for
\big(l(I_{k^{\prime}}),r(I_{k^{\prime\prime}})\big)
. If not, then we can show
Mf(y)\geq Mf(x)
for all
y
in the closed interval connecting
x
and
x_{*}
by taking
x_{\theta}=(1-\theta)x+\theta x_{*}
and
I_{x}\subseteq I_{x_{\theta}}\subseteq\big(l(I_{k^{\prime}}),r(I_{k^{\prime\prime}})\big)
with
l(I_{x_{\theta}})=(1-\theta)l(I_{x})+\theta l(I_{k^{\prime}})
and
r(I_{x_{\theta}})=(1-\theta)r(I_{x})+\theta r(I_{k^{\prime\prime}})
for all
\theta\in[0,1]
. Since
Mf
has a local maximum at
x
, it is constant on this closed interval and so
Mf(x_{*})
is attained for
\big(l(I_{k^{\prime}}),r(I_{k^{\prime\prime}})\big)
, as desired.

From the reasoning above, it follows that
Mf(x_{*})\leq\min\{\alpha_{k^{\prime}},\alpha_{k^{\prime\prime}}\}
in each case.
∎

From now on, we take each representative
x
to be of the form
\frac{l(I_{k^{\prime}})+r(I_{k^{\prime\prime}})}{2}
for

1\leq k^{\prime}\leq k^{\prime\prime}\leq K
such that
Mf(x)=\frac{1}{|I_{x}|}\int_{I_{x}}f
, where
I_{x}=\big(l(I_{k^{\prime}}),r(I_{k^{\prime\prime}})\big)
.
Furthermore, if
Mf
has local maxima and there are
N\geq 1
representatives, say
x_{1}<x_{2}<\dots<x_{N}
, then for
N\geq 2
and each
2\leq n\leq N
,
we choose
y_{n}\in(x_{n-1},x_{n})
, where
Mf
has a local minimum. Then

(3.2)

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(Mf)={\rm Var}_{(-\infty,x_{1}]}(Mf)+\Big(\sum_{n=2}^{N}Mf(x_{n-1})-2Mf(y_{n})+Mf(x_{n})\Big)+{\rm Var}_{[x_{N},\infty)}(Mf).

Let
T_{I}(x)\coloneqq\frac{|I|}{|J_{I}(x)|}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, where
J_{I}(x)
is the smallest interval
J\supseteq I
with
c(J)=x
.
The next result says that the variation of
Mf
is controlled locally by the variations of
T_{I_{k}}
,
1\leq k\leq K
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
Mf
has
N\geq 1
representatives
x_{1}<\dots<x_{N}
.
Then

\displaystyle Mf(x_{n-1})-Mf(y_{n})\leq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_{k}{\rm Var}_{[x_{n-1},y_{n}]}(T_{I_{k}})\quad\text{and}\quad Mf(x_{n})-Mf(y_{n})\leq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_{k}{\rm Var}_{[y_{n},x_{n}]}(T_{I_{k}})

for all
2\leq n\leq N
and
x_{1}<y_{2}<x_{2}<\dots<x_{N-1}<y_{N}<x_{N}
. Similarly,

\displaystyle Mf(x_{N})\leq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_{k}{\rm Var}_{[x_{N},\infty)}(T_{I_{k}})\quad\text{and}\quad Mf(x_{1})\leq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_{k}{\rm Var}_{(-\infty,x_{1}]}(T_{I_{k}}).

If
\alpha_{1}\leq a
, then the first summand in each of the four sums above may be omitted.

Proof.

We shall estimate
Mf(x_{n})-Mf(y_{n})
and for
Mf(x_{n-1})-Mf(y_{n})
the proof is analogous.
Given
x_{n}
, we take
I_{x_{n}}
as in the conclusions of Lemma
3.1
with accompanying
1\leq k^{\prime}\leq k^{\prime\prime}\leq K
.
Then, for
I_{y_{n}}
centered at
y_{n}
and such that
r(I_{y_{n}})=r(I_{x_{n}})
, we have
I_{x_{n}}\subseteq I_{y_{n}}
and so

Mf(x_{n})-Mf(y_{n})\leq\frac{1}{|I_{x_{n}}|}\int_{I_{x_{n}}}f-\frac{1}{|I_{y_{n}}|}\int_{I_{y_{n}}}f\leq\bigg(\frac{1}{|I_{x_{n}}|}-\frac{1}{|I_{y_{n}}|}\bigg)\int_{I_{x_{n}}}f=\sum_{k=k^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime\prime}}\bigg(\frac{\alpha_{k}|I_{k}|}{|I_{x_{n}}|}-\frac{\alpha_{k}|I_{k}|}{|I_{y_{n}}|}\bigg).

Note that if
\alpha_{1}\leq a
and
k^{\prime}=1
, then
\alpha_{1}\geq Mf(x_{n})
by Lemma
3.1
, contradicting (
3.1
). Indeed, we can show
Mf(y)\geq Mf(x_{n})
for all

y<x_{n}
by taking
I_{y}
with
r(I_{y})=r(I_{x_{n}})
. This explains the final sentence of the statement. Next, we notice that the numerical inequality
\frac{1}{\rho_{2}}-\frac{1}{\rho_{2}+\rho_{0}}\leq\frac{1}{\rho_{1}}-\frac{1}{\rho_{1}+\rho_{0}}
holds for
\rho_{0},\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in(0,\infty)
when
\rho_{1}\leq\rho_{2}
. Thus, if either
x_{n}\leq c(I_{k})
or
c(I_{k})\leq y_{n}
, then

\frac{\alpha_{k}|I_{k}|}{|I_{x_{n}}|}-\frac{\alpha_{k}|I_{k}|}{|I_{y_{n}}|}\leq\alpha_{k}\bigg|\frac{|I_{k}|}{|J_{I_{k}}(x_{n})|}-\frac{|I_{k}|}{|J_{I_{k}}(y_{n})|}\bigg|=\alpha_{k}|T_{I_{k}}(x_{n})-T_{I_{k}}(y_{n})|=\alpha_{k}{\rm Var}_{[y_{n},x_{n}]}(T_{I_{k}}).

In the remaining case,
y_{n}<c(I_{k})<x_{n}
, we add and subtract
\frac{\alpha_{k}|I_{k}|}{|I^{\prime}|}
with
I^{\prime}
such that
c(I^{\prime})=c(I_{k})
and
r(I^{\prime})=r(I_{y_{n}})=r(I_{x_{n}})
. This gives

\alpha_{k}\bigg(\frac{|I_{k}|}{|I_{x_{n}}|}-\frac{|I_{k}|}{|I^{\prime}|}+\frac{|I_{k}|}{|I^{\prime}|}-\frac{|I_{k}|}{|I_{y_{n}}|}\bigg)\leq\alpha_{k}\bigg(\,\bigg|\frac{|I_{k}|}{|J_{I_{k}}(x_{n})|}-\frac{|I_{k}|}{|J_{I_{k}}(c(I_{k}))|}\bigg|+\bigg|\frac{|I_{k}|}{|J_{I_{k}}(c(I_{k}))|}-\frac{|I_{k}|}{|J_{I_{k}}(y_{n})|}\bigg|\,\bigg)

and the last quantity does not exceed

\alpha_{k}\big(2T_{I_{k}}(c(I_{k}))-T_{I_{k}}(x_{n})-T_{I_{k}}(y_{n})\big)=\alpha_{k}{\rm Var}_{[y_{n},x_{n}]}(T_{I_{k}})
.

Similarly, taking
y_{1}\to-\infty
and
I_{y_{1}}
centered at
y
and such that
r(I_{y})=r(I_{x_{1}})
, we obtain

Mf(x_{1})=\lim_{y_{1}\to-\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{|I_{x_{1}}|}-\frac{1}{|I_{y_{1}}|}\bigg)\int_{I_{x_{1}}}f\leq\lim_{y_{1}\to-\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_{k}{\rm Var}_{[y_{1},x_{1}]}(T_{I_{k}})=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_{k}{\rm Var}_{(-\infty,x_{1}]}(T_{I_{k}})

with the first summand omitted if
\alpha_{1}\leq a
. As before, for
Mf(x_{N})
the proof is analogous.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

For simplicity, we assume
a\geq b
. If
Mf
has no local maxima, then

{\rm Var}(Mf)=\frac{a-b}{2}=\big(f(+\infty)-f(-\infty)\big)-\frac{a-b}{2}\leq{\rm Var}(f)-\frac{a-b}{2}.

Assume therefore that
N\geq 1
and
x_{1}<\dots<x_{N}
are all representatives. Since
a\geq b
, we obtain
Mf(x)\geq Mf(\infty)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and so
Mf
decreases on
[x_{N},\infty)
. Therefore, Lemma
3.2
gives

{\rm Var}_{[x_{N},\infty)}(Mf)=Mf(x_{N})-\frac{a+b}{2}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_{k}{\rm Var}_{[x_{N},\infty)}(T_{I_{k}})-\frac{a+b}{2}

with the first summand omitted if
\alpha_{1}\leq a
.
If
Mf
is nondecreasing on
(-\infty,x_{1}]
, then similarly

{\rm Var}_{(-\infty,x_{1}]}(Mf)=Mf(x_{1})-a\leq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_{k}{\rm Var}_{(-\infty,x_{1}]}(T_{I_{k}})-a.

In this case, combining (
3.2
) with the two estimates above and Lemma
3.2
gives (
1.2
), since

{\rm Var}(Mf)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_{k}{\rm Var}(T_{I_{k}})-a-\frac{a+b}{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}2\alpha_{k}-a-b-\frac{a-b}{2}\leq{\rm Var}(f)-\frac{a-b}{2}.

If

Mf
is not nondecreasing on
(-\infty,x_{1}]
, then

Mf
has a local minimum at
y_{1}\in[A,x_{1})
such that

{\rm Var}_{(-\infty,x_{1}]}(Mf)=a-2Mf(y_{1})+Mf(x_{1})\leq\min\Big\{Mf(x_{1})-b,a-\frac{a+b}{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_{k}{\rm Var}_{[y_{1},x_{1}]}(T_{I_{k}})\Big\}.

The above inequality, which splits into two parts, follows from
(a+b)/2\leq Mf
(first part) and the estimate for
Mf(x_{1})
from Lemma
3.2
with the additional nonnegative term
2Mf(y_{1})-(a+b)/2
added (second part). Again the first summand is not used if
\alpha_{1}\leq a
.
Now, if
\alpha_{1}\leq a
, then choosing the second term from the above minimum and applying (
3.2
) and Lemma
3.2
, we obtain

{\rm Var}(Mf)\leq\sum_{k=2}^{K}2\alpha_{k}-b=(a-\alpha_{1})+\Big(\alpha_{1}+\sum_{k=2}^{K-1}2\alpha_{k}+\alpha_{K}\Big)+(\alpha_{K}-b)-a\leq{\rm Var}(f)-a

by the separation condition
\alpha_{k}\alpha_{k+1}=0
. Finally, if
\alpha_{1}>a
, then we choose the first term from the relevant minimum. Now
x_{1}>r(I_{1})
, since otherwise we would have

Mf(y_{1})>a
by taking
I_{y_{1}}
with
r(I_{y_{1}})=r(I_{1})
. Also,
Mf(x_{1})<\alpha_{1}
cannot occur because
Mf(r(I_{1}))\geq\alpha_{1}>Mf(-\infty)
, while
x_{1}
is the leftmost representative. Similarly,
Mf(x_{1})=\alpha_{1}
would imply

Mf(y)=\alpha_{1}
for all
y\in[c(I_{1}),x_{1}]
due to
x_{1}
being the leftmost representative, leading to the contradiction
Mf(y_{1})>a
. Thus,
l(I_{x_{1}})=l(I_{k^{\prime}})
for some
k^{\prime}
with

\alpha_{k^{\prime}}>\alpha_{1}>a
and
c(I_{k^{\prime}})\leq x_{1}
by Lemma
3.1
. We then obtain

{\rm Var}(Mf)\leq\alpha_{k^{\prime}}-b+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_{k}{\rm Var}_{[x_{1},\infty)}(T_{I_{k}})-\frac{a+b}{2}\leq(a-\alpha_{1})+{\rm Var}(f)-\frac{a+b}{2}<{\rm Var}(f)-\frac{a-b}{2},

since by
x_{1}\geq c(I_{k^{\prime}})>c(I_{1})
the sum above does not exceed
2(\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{K})-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{k^{\prime}}
.
∎

4.
Further comments

Here we collect several thoughts that complement our main results. We verify optimality of the constants in Conjecture

Δ

, discuss variants of
(
1.2
) and (
1.3
) for other operators, comment on the special case of indicator functions, and point out limitations of our proof method.

First, we note that the constants
1
and
\frac{1}{2}
in (
1.1
) are optimal. Indeed, for
a,b\in[0,\infty)
with
a\geq b
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, take
g_{N}\in{\rm BV}_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{R})
defined by

g_{N}\coloneqq a{\mathds{1}_{{(-\infty,-1)}}}+N{\mathds{1}_{{[-1,1)}}}+b{\mathds{1}_{{[1,\infty)}}}.

Then for
N\geq a
, a simple calculation shows
{\rm Var}(g_{N})=2N-a-b
and
{\rm Var}(Mg_{N})=2N-a-\frac{a+b}{2}
. Letting
N\to\infty
, we obtain
C(\mathbb{R})\geq 1
and
c_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{R})\leq\frac{a-b}{2}
.

Next, we show that certain variants of Conjecture

Δ

hold for other maximal operators. Set

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{M}g(x)\coloneqq\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{r}\int_{x}^{x+r}|g|\quad\text{and}\quad\overrightarrow{M}G(n)\coloneqq\sup_{m\geq 0}\frac{1}{m+1}\sum_{y=n}^{n+m}|G(y)|,

and, similarly,

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}g(x)\coloneqq\sup_{r_{1},r_{2}>0}\frac{1}{r_{1}+r_{2}}\int_{x-r_{1}}^{x+r_{2}}|g|\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{M}G(n)\coloneqq\sup_{m_{1},m_{2}\geq 0}\frac{1}{m_{1}+m_{2}+1}\sum_{y=n-m_{1}}^{n+m_{2}}|G(y)|.

We call
\overrightarrow{M}
and
\widetilde{M}
the one-sided and uncentered maximal operators, respectively. When
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Z}
, one can instead consider a variant of
\widetilde{M}
allowing only

m_{1},m_{2}\geq 0
with
m_{1}+m_{2}+1\in 2\mathbb{N}-1
so that the corresponding intervals are balls in
\mathbb{Z}
.
This does not change the conclusion below.

Since the sampling procedure works in the context of
\overrightarrow{M}
or
\widetilde{M}
with only minor adjustments, for all
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
the associated constants
\overrightarrow{C}(\mathbb{R}),\overrightarrow{c_{a}^{b}}(\mathbb{R}),\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{R}),\widetilde{c_{a}^{b}}(\mathbb{R})
are not worse than their discrete counterparts. Furthermore, when
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Z}
and
a,b\in[0,\infty)
, it is natural to conjecture that
\overrightarrow{C}(\mathbb{Z})=\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{Z})=1
,
\overrightarrow{c_{a}^{b}}(\mathbb{Z})=\max\{0,b-a\}
, and
\widetilde{c_{a}^{b}}(\mathbb{Z})=|b-a|
. Indeed, a suitable variant of (
1.3
) follows because for each nonnegative
G\in{\rm BV}_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{Z})
one has
\overrightarrow{M}G\geq\max\{G,b\}
and
\widetilde{M}G\geq\max\{G,a,b\}
, while the equality
\overrightarrow{M}G(n)=G(n)
or
\widetilde{M}G(n)=G(n)
holds whenever
\overrightarrow{M}G
or
\widetilde{M}G
has a local maximum
at
n
. Considering
G_{N}\in{\rm BV}_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{Z})
given by
G_{N}\coloneqq a{\mathds{1}_{{-\mathbb{N}}}}+N{\mathds{1}_{{\{0\}}}}+b{\mathds{1}_{{\mathbb{N}}}}
and
g_{N}\in{\rm BV}_{a}^{b}(\mathbb{R})
given by
g_{N}\coloneqq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}G_{N}(n){\mathds{1}_{{[n-1/2,n+1/2)}}}
, we verify that the postulated constants are optimal.

Remark 4.1
.

For all
a,b\in[0,\infty)
we have

(1)

\overrightarrow{C}(\mathbb{R})=\overrightarrow{C}(\mathbb{Z})=1

and
\overrightarrow{c_{a}^{b}}(\mathbb{R})=\overrightarrow{c_{a}^{b}}(\mathbb{Z})=\max\{0,b-a\}
,

(2)

\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{R})=\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{Z})=1

and
\widetilde{c_{a}^{b}}(\mathbb{R})=\widetilde{c_{a}^{b}}(\mathbb{Z})=|b-a|
.

In the case of indicator functions our proof of (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) is simpler. Indeed, let
f\in{\rm BV}(\mathbb{R})
be as in Theorem
1.1
with

a,b,\alpha_{k}\in\{0,1\}
. Now, if
Mf
has a local maximum at
x
, then either
f(x)=0
or
x
can be adjusted to
x_{*}=c(I_{k})
for some
k
.
Moreover, if
Mf
has a local minimum at
y
, then
f(y)=0
. Thus, we can replace
T_{I_{k}}
by
M{\mathds{1}_{{I_{k}}}}
, since both functions coincide on

\{c(I_{k})\}\cup(\mathbb{R}\setminus I_{k})
. In particular, if
Mf
is monotone on the closed interval
J
with endpoints
x,y
as before, then

{\rm Var}_{J}(Mf)=Mf(x)-Mf(y)\leq\frac{1}{|I_{x}|}\int_{I_{x}}|f|-\frac{1}{|I_{y}|}\int_{I_{y}}|f|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\alpha_{k}{\rm Var}_{J}(M{\mathds{1}_{{I_{k}}}}).

Using this and relevant estimates at
\pm\infty
yields (
1.2
), and (
1.3
) follows
by the extension procedure.

Finally, notice that the method presented in the proof of Theorem
1.1
has some natural limitations. Indeed, during the proof we always choose
I_{y}
to be the smallest interval centered at
y
which contains
I_{x}
. This choice is sometimes very far from optimal. To illustrate this obstacle, let us take
g\in{\rm BV}_{0}^{0}(\mathbb{R})
given by
g\coloneqq{\mathds{1}_{{[-2,-1)\cup[1,2)}}}+C{\mathds{1}_{{[-2,2)}}}
with
C=10^{6}
, say. Then
Mg
has local maxima at
-\frac{3}{2},0,\frac{3}{2}
with the related values being
C+1,C+\frac{1}{2},C+1
, and local minima at
-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}
with the related values being
C+\frac{1}{3},C+\frac{1}{3}
. Thus,
{\rm Var}(Mg)=2C+4-\frac{1}{3}\leq 2C+4={\rm Var}(g)
, as desired. However, for
x=0
we have
I_{x}=(-2,2)
so that for
y=\frac{1}{2}
we should take
I_{y}=(-2,3)
in Theorem
1.1
. Unfortunately, the difference between the corresponding averages is much larger than
Mg(0)-Mg(\frac{1}{2})
, therefore
{\rm Var}(Mg)\leq{\rm Var}(g)
cannot be verified this way.

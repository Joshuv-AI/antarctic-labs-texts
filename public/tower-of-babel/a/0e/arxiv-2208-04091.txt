Abstract
In this work we set up the generating function of the ultimate time survival probability φ ⁡ ( u + 1 ) \varphi(u+1) , where φ ⁡ ( u ) = ℙ ⁡ ( sup n ⩾ 1 ∑ i = 1 n ( X i − κ ) < u ) \varphi(u)=\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{n\geqslant 1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(X_{i}-\kappa\right)<u\right) and u ∈ ℕ 0 , κ ∈ ℕ u\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\,\kappa\in\mathbb{N} , and the random walk { ∑ i = 1 n X i , n ∈ ℕ } \left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i},\,n\in\mathbb{N}\right\} consists of independent and identically distributed random variables X i X_{i} , which are non-negative and integer valued. We also give expressions of φ ⁡ ( u ) \varphi(u) via the roots of certain polynomials. Based on the proven theoretical statements, we give several examples on φ ⁡ ( u ) \varphi(u) and its generating function expressions, when random variables X i X_{i} admit Bernoulli, Geometric and some other distributions.

Introduction and preliminaries

The study of sum of independent and identically distributed random variables (r.vs.)
\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
is hardily avoidable in probability theory and related fields. This sequence of sums
\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i},\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
is called
the random walk
. Let us define the stochastic process

\displaystyle W(n):=u+\kappa n-\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i},\,n\in{\mathbb{N}},

(1)

where
u\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}
and random variables
X_{i},\,i\in\mathbb{N}
are independent, identically distributed, non-negative and integer valued. The defined process (
1
) is called
the generalized premium discrete time risk model
, we abbreviate this naming by
GPDTRM
. Such type of processes appear in insurance mathematics arguing that they describe insurers wealth in time moments
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, where
u
means initial surplus (also called capital or reserve),
\kappa
denotes premium rate (earnings per unit of time), i.e.
(n+1)\kappa-n\kappa=\kappa
, and the random walk
\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i},\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
represents expenses caused by random size claims. Then, it is curious to know whether initial surplus and gained premiums are sufficient to cover an incurred random expenses. More precisely, one aims to know whether
W(n)>0
for all
n\in\{1,\,2,\,\ldots,\,T\}
when
T
is some fixed natural number or
T\to\infty
. The positivity of
W(n)
is of course associated to likelihood. For the
GPDTRM
given in (
1
) we define
the finite time survival probability

\varphi(u,T):=\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{n=1}^{T}\left\{W(n)>0\right\}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{1\leqslant n\leqslant T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(X_{i}-\kappa\right)<u\right),\,T\in{\mathbb{N}}

and
the ultimate time survival probability

\displaystyle\varphi(u):=\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\{W(n)>0\right\}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{n\geqslant 1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(X_{i}-\kappa\right)<u\right).

(2)

Both
\varphi(u,T)
and
\varphi(u)
are nothing but distribution functions of the provided integer valued sequence of sums of random variables; these functions are left-continuous, non-decreasing and step functions if we allow
u\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Also,
\varphi(\infty)=1
if
\mathbb{E}{X}<\kappa
, see the next Section
2
.

Calculation of
\varphi(u,T)
is simple. If
X_{i},\,i\in{\mathbb{N}}
are independent copies of random variable (r.v.)
X
and
x_{i}:=\mathbb{P}(X=i),\,i\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, then

\varphi(u,\,1)=\mathbb{P}(X\leqslant u+\kappa-1),\,\varphi(u,\,T)=\sum_{i=1}^{u+\kappa-1}\varphi(u+\kappa-i,\,T-1)x_{i},\,T\geqslant 2.

see, for instance,
[
11
, Theorem 1]
.

Let’s turn to the ultimate time survival probability
\varphi(u)
. The law of total probability and rearrangements in (
2
) imply

\displaystyle\varphi(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{u+\kappa}x_{u+\kappa-i}\varphi(i),

(3)

see
[
11
, page 3]
.

By setting
u=0
in (
3
), we get

\displaystyle\varphi(0)=x_{\kappa-1}\varphi(1)+x_{\kappa-2}\varphi(2)+\ldots+x_{0}\varphi(\kappa),

(4)

what means that aiming to calculate
\varphi(\kappa)
when
x_{0}>0
, we must know know the initial ones
\varphi(0),\,\varphi(1),\,\ldots,\,\varphi(\kappa-1)
. Equally, requirement to know
\varphi(0),\,\varphi(1),\,\ldots,\,\varphi(\kappa-1)
remains actual calculating
\varphi(u)
for
u=\kappa,\,\kappa+1\,\ldots
by recurrence (
3
). The needed quantity of these initial values is
X
distribution dependent as some of
x_{0},\,x_{1},\,\ldots,\,x_{\kappa-1}
may vanish, c.f. (
4
) when
\mathbb{P}(X>j)=1
for some
j\geqslant 0
. The paper
[
11
]
deals with finding the mentioned initial values and it is shown there that they can be found calculating limits of a certain recurrent sequences. For instance, if
\kappa=2
and
x_{0}>0
, then it follows by (
4
) that

\varphi(0)=x_{1}\varphi(1)+x_{0}\varphi(2),

where (see
[
9
, page 2 and 3]
)

\displaystyle\varphi(0)=\varphi(\infty)\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\gamma_{n+1}-\gamma_{n}}{\begin{vmatrix}\beta_{n}&\gamma_{n}\\
\beta_{n+1}&\gamma_{n+1}\end{vmatrix}},\,\varphi(1)=\varphi(\infty)\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\beta_{n}-\beta_{n+1}}{\begin{vmatrix}\beta_{n}&\gamma_{n}\\
\beta_{n+1}&\gamma_{n+1}\end{vmatrix}},

(5)

when
|\cdot|
is determinant,

\displaystyle\beta_{0}=1,\,\beta_{1}=0,\,\beta_{n}=\frac{1}{x_{0}}\left(\beta_{n-2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{n-i}\beta_{i}\right),\text{ for }n\geqslant 2,

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}=0,\,\gamma_{1}=1,\,\gamma_{n}=\frac{1}{x_{0}}\left(\gamma_{n-2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{n-i}\gamma_{i}\right),\text{ for }n\geqslant 2,

and
\varphi(\infty)=1
if
\mathbb{E}{X}<2
.

Calculating the limits in (

never vanishes, in paper

it was proved their connection to the solutions of

Several auxiliary notations and the net profit condition

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}:=\sup_{n\geqslant 1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\kappa)\right)^{+},

where
x^{+}=\max\{0,x\}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
is the positive part function and r.vs.
X_{i}
and
\kappa\in{\mathbb{N}}
are the same as in the model (
1
). Let us denote the local probabilities of r.v.
\mathcal{M}
by

\displaystyle\pi_{i}:=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{M}=i),\,i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Then, the ultimate time survival probability definition (
2
) implies that

\displaystyle\varphi(u+1)=\sum_{i=0}^{u}\pi_{i}=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{M}\leqslant u)\text{ for all }u\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(6)

In general, the r.v.
\mathcal{M}
can be extended, i.e.
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{M}=\infty)>0
, however the condition
\mathbb{E}{X}<\kappa

ensures
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{M}<\infty)=1
. This is true due to

\lim_{u\to\infty}\varphi(u)=1\text{ if }\mathbb{E}{X}<\kappa,

see
[
11
, Lemma 1]
. The condition
\mathbb{E}{X}<\kappa
is called
the net profit condition
and it is crucial because the survival is impossible, i.e.
\varphi(u)=0
for all
u\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, if
\mathbb{E}{X}\geqslant\kappa
, except few trivial cases when
\mathbb{P}(X=\kappa)=1
, see
[
11
, Theorem 9]
.
Intuitively, it is clear that long term survival by model (
1
) is impossible if the threatening claim amount
X
on average is equal or greater to the collected premium
\kappa
per unit of time.

For
s\in\mathbb{C}
, let us denote the generating function of
\varphi(1),\,\varphi(2),\,\ldots,

\Xi(s):=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\varphi(i+1)s^{i},\,|s|<1

and the probability generating functions of r.vs.
X
and
\mathcal{M}

G_{X}(s):=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x_{i}s^{i},\,G_{\mathcal{M}}(s):=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\pi_{i}s^{i},\,|s|\leqslant 1.

Then,
\Xi(s)
and
G_{\mathcal{M}}(s)
, for
|s|<1
, satisfy the relation

\displaystyle\Xi(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\varphi(i+1)s^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{i}\pi_{j}s^{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\pi_{j}\sum_{i=j}^{\infty}s^{i}=\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\pi_{j}s^{j}}{1-s}=\frac{G_{\mathcal{M}}(s)}{1-s}.

(7)

In many examples, the radius of convergence of
G_{X}(s)
or
G_{\mathcal{M}}(s)
is larger than one. See
[
9
, Lemma 8]
for more properties of probability generating function in
|s|\leqslant 1
.

Lemma 7

Let

\alpha=-1
is the root of

iff

, see

and description

Example 9
.

Suppose the random claim amount
X
is Bernoulli distributed, i.e.
1-\mathbb{P}(X=0)=p=\mathbb{P}(X=1),\,0<p<1
. We find the ultimate time survival probability generating function
\Xi(s)
and calculate
\varphi(u),\,u\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

In view of the first part of Corollary
5
and recurrence (
3
), it is trivial that
\Xi(s)=1/(1-s),\,|s|<1
and
\varphi(0)=x_{0}\varphi(1)=1-p
,
\varphi(u)=1
,
u\in\mathbb{N}
. In other words, the ultimate time survival is guaranteed if initial surplus
u\in\mathbb{N}
and maximal claim size is one in the model
u+n-\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
.

Example 10

Suppose the random claim amount
X

p\in(0,1)
, i.e.
\mathbb{P}(X=k)=p(1-p)^{k}

\kappa=2

\Xi(s)

\varphi(0)
and
\varphi(1)

\mathbb{E}{X}<2

We start with an observation on the net profit condition

\mathbb{E}{X}=\frac{1-p}{p}<2\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\frac{1}{3}<p<1.

Then, according to Theorem
1
and description (
i
) in Section
3
,

G_{X}(s)=\frac{p}{1-(1-p)s}=s^{2}\Rightarrow\alpha:=s=\frac{p-\sqrt{4p-3p^{2}}}{2(1-p)}\in(-1,0),

when
1/3<p<1
and, by Corollary
5
with
\kappa=2
and
x_{0}=p>0
,

\Xi(s)=\frac{(3p-1)(p-\sqrt{4p-3p^{2}})}{p(3p-2-\sqrt{4p-3p^{2}})}\cdot\frac{1-(1-p)s}{(1-s)s^{2}+(s^{3}-1)p},\,\frac{1}{3}<p<1.

For
\kappa=2
,
u=0
and
1/3<p<1
the recurrence (
3
) or Theorem
4
yields

\displaystyle\varphi(0)

\displaystyle=x_{1}\varphi(1)+x_{0}\varphi(2)=(1-p)p\,\Xi(0)+p\,\Xi^{\prime}(0)=\frac{2-\mathbb{E}{X}}{1-\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{3p-2+\sqrt{4p-3p^{2}}}{2p},

\displaystyle\varphi(1)

\displaystyle=\Xi(0)=\frac{2-\mathbb{E}{X}}{x_{0}}\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}=\frac{3p-\sqrt{4p-3p^{2}}}{2p^{2}}.

One may check that for
p=101/300

\displaystyle\varphi(0)=\frac{\sqrt{90597}-297}{202}=0.0197691\ldots,

\displaystyle\varphi(1)=\frac{45450-150\sqrt{90597}}{10201}=0.0295066\ldots

,

Example 12

Suppose the random claim amount
X

3x_{0}+2x_{1}+x_{2}&2x_{0}+x_{1}&x_{0}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\pi_{0}\\
\pi_{1}\\
\pi_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\

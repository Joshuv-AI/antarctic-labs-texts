Series and sums involving the floor function


Abstract
Let ( a n ) n ≥ 0 (a_{n})_{n\geq 0} be an arbitrary sequence and ( a ⌊ n / k ⌋ ) n ≥ 0 (a_{\lfloor n/k\rfloor})_{n\geq 0} its dual floor sequence. We study infinite series and finite generalized binomial sums involving ( a ⌊ n / k ⌋ ) n ≥ 0 (a_{\lfloor n/k\rfloor})_{n\geq 0} . As applications we prove a range of new closed form expressions for Fibonacci (Lucas) series and binomial sum identities as particular cases. Keywords: Floor function, power series, generating function, binomial transform, Fibonacci (Lucas) number, gibonacci sequence. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification : Primary 11B39; Secondary 11B37, 40A05.

Motivation

As usual, the Fibonacci numbers
F_{n}
and the Lucas numbers
L_{n}
are defined, for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, through the recurrence relations
F_{n}=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2}
,
n\geq 2
, with initial values
F_{0}=0
,
F_{1}=1
and
L_{n}=L_{n-1}+L_{n-2}
with
L_{0}=2
,
L_{1}=1
.

For negative subscripts we have
F_{-n}=(-1)^{n-1}F_{n}
and
L_{-n}=(-1)^{n}L_{n}
. They possess the explicit formulas (Binet forms)

F_{n}=\frac{\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n}}{\alpha-\beta},\quad L_{n}=\alpha^{n}+\beta^{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

(1)

For more information we refer to Koshy
[
6
]
and Vajda
[
16
]
who have written high quality books dealing with Fibonacci and Lucas numbers.

The paper is motivated by the following series which appeared recently as a problem proposal
[
3
]
:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(\Big\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\Big\rfloor+1\Big)\frac{F_{n}}{2^{n}}=\frac{32}{5}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(\Big\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\Big\rfloor+1\Big)\frac{L_{n}}{2^{n}}=16,

where
\lfloor x\rfloor
denotes the floor function.

Such series allow generalizations in various directions. A gibonacci version of the above series formulas is given by

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(\Big\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\Big\rfloor+1\Big)\frac{G_{n}}{2^{n}}=\frac{8(3a+4b)}{5},

where
G_{n}
denotes the gibonacci number defined by the recurrence
G_{n}=G_{n-1}+G_{n-2}
,
n\geq 2
,
with
G_{0}=a
and
G_{1}=b
(
a
and
b
are arbitrary). Note that
F_{n}
corresponds to the case of
G_{n}
when
a=0
and
b=1
, while
L_{n}
is obtained when
a=2
and
b=1
.

Many properties and applications of the floor and ceiling functions have been previously investigated.
Here we refer to the contributions
[
4
,
5
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
15
]
. Additionally, the analysis of series (both finite and infinite) with these functions has attracted recent attention
[
13
,
14
]
.

In this paper, we consider the following general setting. Let
(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be an arbitrary sequence and
(a_{\lfloor n/k\rfloor})_{n\geq 0}
its dual floor sequence. We study infinite series and finite generalized binomial sums involving
(a_{\lfloor n/k\rfloor})_{n\geq 0}
. As applications we prove a range of new closed form expressions for Fibonacci (Lucas) series and binomial sum identities as particular cases.

2
Some infinite series

The next fundamental lemma will be crucial in the first part of the study.
It will enable us to establish many results for infinite series involving the floor function.

Lemma 1
.

Let
k\geq 1
be an integer and
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
|z|<1
. Let further
(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be an arbitrary sequence with ordinary generating function
F(z)
. Then the ordinary generating function of the sequence

(a_{\lfloor n/k\rfloor})_{n\geq 0}
is

F^{+}(z,k)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}z^{n}=\frac{1-z^{k}}{1-z}F(z^{k}).

(2)

Also, the ordinary generating function of the sequence
((-1)^{n}a_{\lfloor n/k\rfloor})_{n\geq 0}
is

F^{-}(z,k)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}(-1)^{n}z^{n}=\frac{1+(-1)^{k+1}z^{k}}{1+z}F\big((-1)^{k}z^{k}\big).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle F^{+}(z;k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}z^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}z^{kn+j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}z^{j}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{kn}=\frac{1-z^{k}}{1-z}F(z^{k}).

The second identity follows from the first by replacing
z
by
-z
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

For integer
k\geq 1
and
|z|<1
the following expressions are valid:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\Big\rfloor z^{n}=\frac{z^{k}}{(1-z)(1-z^{k})}

(3)

and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\Big\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\Big\rfloor z^{n}=\frac{(-1)^{k}z^{k}}{(1+z)\big(1+(-1)^{k+1}z^{k}\big)}.

(4)

Proof.

Use Lemma
1
with
a_{n}=n
in conjunction with generating function

F(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}nz^{n}=\frac{z}{(1-z)^{2}}.

The second identity follows from the first by replacing
z
with
-z
.
∎

Remark 1
.

Since for
k<0
,
\left\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\right\rfloor=-1-\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{-k}\right\rfloor
,
we can extend Formulas (
3
) and (
4
) to negative integer
k
as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big\lfloor\frac{n+1}{k}\Big\rfloor z^{n}=\frac{z^{k}}{(1-z)(1-z^{k})},

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\Big\lfloor\frac{n+1}{k}\Big\rfloor z^{n}=\frac{(-1)^{k}z^{k}}{(1+z)\big(1+(-1)^{k+1}z^{k}\big)}.

Remark 2
.

Since

\left\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\right\rfloor=\left\lceil\frac{n+1}{k}\right\rceil-1
for
k\geq 1
and

\left\lfloor\frac{n+1}{k}\right\rfloor=\left\lceil\frac{n}{k}\right\rceil-1
for
k\leq-1
,
from (
3
) and (
4
) we have infinite series involving the ceiling function:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big\lceil\frac{n+1}{k}\Big\rceil z^{n}=\frac{1}{(1-z)(1-z^{k})},\qquad k\geq 1,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big\lceil\frac{n}{k}\Big\rceil z^{n}=\frac{1}{(1-z)(1-z^{k})},\qquad k\leq-1,

and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\Big\lceil\frac{n+1}{k}\Big\rceil z^{n}=\frac{1}{(1+z)\big(1-(-1)^{k}z^{k}\big)},\qquad k\geq 1,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\Big\lceil\frac{n}{k}\Big\rceil z^{n}=\frac{1}{(1+z)\big(1-(-1)^{k}z^{k}\big)},\qquad k\leq-1.

Lemma
2
leads immediately to a range of new Fibonacci (Lucas) series evaluations.

Theorem 1
.

For any integers
m
and
s
, an integer
k\geq 1
and any
p>\alpha^{s}
, we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\Big\rfloor\frac{F_{sn+m}}{p^{n+1}}=\frac{p^{k}\big(pF_{sk+m}-(-1)^{s}F_{s(k-1)+m}\big)-(-1)^{sk}\big(pF_{m}-(-1)^{s}F_{m-s}\big)}{\big(p^{2}-pL_{s}+(-1)^{s}\big)\big(p^{2k}-p^{k}L_{sk}+(-1)^{sk}\big)}

and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\Big\rfloor\frac{L_{sn+m}}{p^{n+1}}=\frac{p^{k}\big(pL_{sk+m}-(-1)^{s}L_{s(k-1)+m}\big)-(-1)^{sk}(pL_{m}-(-1)^{s}L_{m-s})}{\big(p^{2}-pL_{s}+(-1)^{s}\big)\big(p^{2k}-p^{k}L_{sk}+(-1)^{sk}\big)}.

Proof.

Inserting
z=\alpha^{s}/p
in (
3
) gives

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\Big\rfloor\frac{\alpha^{sn}}{p^{n}}=\frac{p\alpha^{sk}}{(p-\alpha^{s})(p^{k}-\alpha^{sk})}\quad{\text{or}}\quad\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\Big\rfloor\frac{\alpha^{sn+m}}{p^{n}}=\frac{p\alpha^{sk+m}}{(p-\alpha^{s})(p^{k}-\alpha^{sk})}.

Similarly,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\Big\rfloor\frac{\beta^{sn+m}}{p^{n}}=\frac{p\beta^{sk+m}}{(p-\beta^{s})(p^{k}-\beta^{sk})}.

Now, we combine the results according to the Binet forms (
1
) making use of
\alpha\beta=-1
and
\alpha+\beta=1
.
∎

Lemma 6
.

For an integer

, any integer

Proof.

We proof (

3
The binomial transform of
(a_{\lfloor n/k\rfloor})_{n\geq 0}

We begin with recalling some basic facts. Let
(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be an arbitrary sequence of numbers
with ordinary generating function
F(z)
. Let further
(s_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be the generalized binomial transform of

(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, i.e.,

s_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}b^{n-j}c^{j}a_{j}

with
b
and
c
being nonzero real numbers. Then the ordinary generating function of the sequence
(s_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is given
by
[
1
,
11
]

S(z)=\frac{1}{1-bz}F\left(\frac{cz}{1-bz}\right).

Therefore, the ordinary generating function of the sequence

s_{n}^{*}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}b^{n-j}c^{j}a_{\lfloor j/k\rfloor}

equals

\displaystyle S^{*}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-bz}\frac{1-\left(\frac{cz}{1-bz}\right)^{k}}{1-\frac{cz}{1-bz}}\,F\!\left(\Bigl(\frac{cz}{1-bz}\Bigr)^{k}\right)=\frac{1-\left(\frac{cz}{1-bz}\right)^{k}}{1-(b+c)z}\,F\!\left(\Bigl(\frac{cz}{1-bz}\Bigr)^{k}\right).

(22)

In general, such a function will be a complicated expression. Focusing on the sequence
a_{n}=n
we have

S^{*}(z,k)=\frac{1-\left(\frac{cz}{1-bz}\right)^{k}}{1-(b+c)z}\frac{\left(\frac{cz}{1-bz}\right)^{k}}{\left(1-\left(\frac{cz}{1-bz}\right)^{k}\right)^{2}}=\frac{(cz)^{k}}{(1-(b+c)z)((1-bz)^{k}-(cz)^{k})}.

The complex function
P(z)=(1-bz)^{k}-(cz)^{k}
is a polynomial of degree
k
for
b\neq\pm c
and we have

P(z)=(z-r_{1})(z-r_{2})\cdots(z-r_{k}),

where
r_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
,
i=1,\ldots,k,
are the roots of
P(z)
. The partial fraction decomposition yields

\frac{1}{P(z)}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{z-r_{i}}\prod_{j=1,\\
\,j\neq i}^{k}\frac{1}{r_{j}-r_{i}}.

This shows that (at least theoretically) the function
S^{*}(z,k)
can be expressed as a convolution. But even for small values of
k
this will be a challenging issue. Our first result is therefore concerned with
analyzing the case
k=2
.

Theorem 13
.

For any numbers
b
and
c
with
c\neq 0
and
n\geq 1
, we have

{\sum_{j=1}^{n}}\binom{n}{j}\Big\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\Big\rfloor b^{n-j}c^{j}=\frac{cn}{2}(b+c)^{n-1}-\frac{(b+c)^{n}-(b-c)^{n}}{4}.

(23)

If
b=c
, then this identity becomes

{\sum_{j=1}^{n}}\binom{n}{j}\Big\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\Big\rfloor=2^{n-2}(n-1).

Proof.

If
k=2
we simplify
S^{*}(z,2)
and get

S^{*}(z,2)=\frac{(cz)^{2}}{(1-(b+c)z)(1-2bz+(b^{2}-c^{2})z^{2})}.

Obviously, near zero, there exists the power series

\frac{1}{1-(b+c)z}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(b+c)^{n}z^{n}.

Also, from the partial fraction decomposition

\frac{1}{1-2bz+(b^{2}-c^{2})z^{2}}=\frac{b+c}{2c(1-(b+c)z)}-\frac{b-c}{2c(1-(b-c)z)}

Hence, applying Cauchy’s product formula for power series

or, for

\displaystyle{\sum_{j=1}^{n}}\binom{n}{j}\Big\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\Big\rfloor b^{n-j}c^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}(b+c)^{j}\left((b+c)^{n-1-j}-(b-c)^{n-1-j}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{2}(b+c)^{n-1}(n-1)-\frac{c}{2}(b-c)^{n-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}\left(\frac{b+c}{b-c}\right)^{j}.

The statement now follows from simplifying making use of the geometric series.

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor}\binom{n-r}{2j-s}5^{j}j^{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor}\binom{n-r}{2j-s}5^{j}j^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+10\,(2s+1)(n-r)F_{n-r-1}+4s^{2}L_{n-r}\big),

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor}\binom{n-r}{2j-s+1}5^{j}j^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\,+2\,(2s-1)(n-r)L_{n-r-1}+4(s-1)^{2}F_{n-r}\big).

Proof.

Proof.

Use of

and

Example 6
.

For integers
k\geq 1
and any integer
m
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}\frac{F_{n+m}}{2^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(4^{k}-(-1)^{k})F_{m+2}-2^{k}F_{k+m+2}-(-1)^{m}2^{k}F_{k-m-2}}{4^{k}+2^{k}L_{k}+(-1)^{k}},

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}\frac{L_{n+m}}{2^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(4^{k}-(-1)^{k})L_{m+2}-2^{k}L_{k+m+2}+(-1)^{m}2^{k}L_{k-m-2}}{4^{k}+2^{k}L_{k}+(-1)^{k}},

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}\frac{F_{n+m}}{3^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(9^{k}-(-1)^{k})L_{m+1}-3^{k}L_{k+m+1}-(-1)^{m}3^{k}L_{k-m-1}}{5(9^{k}+3^{k}L_{k}+(-1)^{k})},

For the finite binomial sum

v_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{\lfloor j/k\rfloor}\binom{n}{j}b^{n-j}c^{j}

with
b
and
c
real, the ordinary generating function is given by

\displaystyle S_{v}^{*}(z,k)=\frac{1-\left(\frac{cz}{1-bz}\right)^{k}}{1-(b+c)z}\frac{1}{1+\left(\frac{cz}{1-bz}\right)^{k}}=\frac{1}{1-(b+c)z}\left(1-\frac{2(cz)^{k}}{(1-bz)^{k}+(cz)^{k}}\right).

Now the same analysis as above applies.

Theorem 34
.

For nonzero real numbers
b
and
c
and
n\geq 2
we have

\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{\lfloor j/2\rfloor}\binom{n}{j}b^{n-j}c^{j}=\frac{1}{2}(1-i)(b+ic)^{n}+\frac{1}{2}(1+i)(b-ic)^{n},

(38)

where
i
is the imaginary unit. Equivalently, using the polar form, we get the expression

\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{\lfloor{j/2}\rfloor}\binom{n}{j}b^{n-j}c^{j}=\sgn b\,\sqrt{2(b^{2}+c^{2})^{n}}\cos\left({n\arctan{\frac{c}{b}}-\frac{\pi}{4}}\right).

Proof.

As

\frac{1}{(1-bz)^{2}+(cz)^{2}}=\frac{1}{2c}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\big((b+ic)^{n}(c-ib)+(b-ic)^{n}(c+ib)\big)z^{n},

we have

\displaystyle S_{v}^{*}(z,2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(b+c)^{n}z^{n}-cz^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(b+c)^{n}z^{n}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left((b+ic)^{n}(c-ib)+(b-ic)^{n}(c+ib)\right)z^{n}

\displaystyle=(b+c)^{0}+(b+c)^{1}z

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left((b+c)^{n}-c\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}(b+c)^{j}\left((b+ic)^{n-2-j}(c-ib)+(b-ic)^{n-2-j}(c+ib)\right)\right)\!z^{n}.

This shows that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{\lfloor j/2\rfloor}

\displaystyle\binom{n}{j}b^{n-j}c^{j}

\displaystyle=(b+c)^{n}-c\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}(b+c)^{j}\left((b+ic)^{n-2-j}(c-ib)+(b-ic)^{n-2-j}(c+ib)\right).

The last sum can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}(b+c)^{j}\left((b+ic)^{n-2-j}(c-ib)+(b-ic)^{n-2-j}(c+ib)\right)

\displaystyle\quad=(b+ic)^{n-2}(c-ib)\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}\left(\frac{b+c}{b+ic}\right)^{j}+(b-ic)^{n-2}(c+ib)\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}\left(\frac{b+c}{b-ic}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{c-ib}{c(1-i)}\left((b+c)^{n-1}-(b+ic)^{n-1}\right)+\frac{c+ib}{c(1+i)}\left((b+c)^{n-1}-(b-ic)^{n-1}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{(b+c)^{n}}{c}-\frac{1}{2}(b+ic)^{n-1}\left(1+\frac{b}{c}+i\left(1-\frac{b}{c}\right)\right)-\frac{1}{2}(b-ic)^{n-1}\left(1+\frac{b}{c}-i\left(1-\frac{b}{c}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{(b+c)^{n}}{c}-\frac{b+c}{2c}\left((b+ic)^{n-1}+(b-ic)^{n-1}\right)+i\frac{b-c}{2c}\left((b+ic)^{n-1}-(b-ic)^{n-1}\right).

This gives

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{\lfloor\frac{j}{2}\rfloor}\binom{n}{j}b^{n-j}c^{j}=\frac{b+c}{2}\!\left((b+ic)^{n-1}+(b-ic)^{n-1}\right)\!-i\frac{b-c}{2}\left((b+ic)^{n-1}\!-(b-ic)^{n-1}\right)\!,

and from here

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{\lfloor j/2\rfloor}\binom{n}{j}b^{n-j}c^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{b}{2}(1-i)(b+ic)^{n-1}+\frac{c}{2}(1+i)(b+ic)^{n-1}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{b}{2}(1+i)(b-ic)^{n-1}+\frac{c}{2}(1-i)(b-ic)^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{(b+ic)^{n}}{b^{2}+c^{2}}\left(\frac{b}{2}(1-i)(b-ic)+\frac{c}{2}(1+i)(b-ic)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(b-ic)^{n}}{b^{2}+c^{2}}\left(\frac{b}{2}(1+i)(b+ic)+\frac{c}{2}(1-i)(b+ic)\right)

which is the stated formula.
∎

Remark 7
.

It is worth noting that a very short proof of Theorem

goes as follows. Recall

h_{1}(x;b,c,n)=\sum_{j=0}^{\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor}\binom{n}{2j}b^{n-2j}c^{2j}x^{2j}=\frac{(b+cx)^{n}+(b-cx)^{n}}{2},

h_{2}(x;b,c,n)=\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lceil{n/2}\right\rceil}\binom{n}{2j-1}b^{n-2j+1}c^{2j-1}x^{2j-2}=\frac{(b+cx)^{n}-(b-cx)^{n}}{2x}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{\left\lfloor{j/2}\right\rfloor}\binom{n}{j}b^{n-j}c^{j}

\displaystyle=h_{1}(i;b,c,n)+h_{2}(i;b,c,n)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(1-i)(b+ic)^{n}+\frac{1}{2}(1+i)(b-ic)^{n}.

6
Concluding comments

This paper was about studying infinite series and binomial sums involving a general floor sequence
(a_{\lfloor n/k\rfloor})_{n\geq 0}

which we termed the dual sequence corresponding to
(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
. In the first part we derived some properties of
infinite series involving
(a_{\lfloor n/k\rfloor})_{n\geq 0}
and evaluated these series in closed form at certain Fibonacci (Lucas) arguments. As a byproduct we have also established a apparently new expression for the polylogarithm.

In the second part of the study we have studied finite binomial sums, hereby focusing on three particular classes of sequences, namely,

a_{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}=\lfloor n/k\rfloor,\quad a_{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}=\lfloor n/k\rfloor^{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad a_{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}=(-1)^{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}.

Based on three general theorems (Theorem
13
, Theorem
27
and Theorem
34
) a range of new Fibonacci (Lucas) binomial sums were evaluated exactly. As was pointed out in the main text there are still gaps to fill which we leave for the readers’ personal exploration.

We cannot resist to close the article with another obvious class of sequences.
This class comes from choosing
a_{n}=F_{n}
and
a_{n}=L_{n}
, respectively. Using well-known generating functions

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n}z^{n}=\frac{z}{1-z-z^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}L_{n}z^{n}=\frac{2-z}{1-z-z^{2}},

we get from Lemma
1
for integers
k\geq 1
and
|z|<|\beta|

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}z^{n}=\frac{(1-z^{k})z^{k}}{(1-z)(1-z^{k}-z^{2k})},\qquad\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}L_{\lfloor n/k\rfloor}z^{n}=\frac{(1-z^{k})(2-z^{k})}{(1-z)(1-z^{k}-z^{2k})}.

This produces the next results.

Theorem 39
.

The exact solution of a fragmented Bose-Hubbard model and twisted models
Gilberto N. Santos Filho

Abstract
We present the exact solution of a family of fragmented Bose-Hubbard models and represent the models as graphs with the condensates in the vertices. The models are solved by the algebraic Bethe ansatz method. We show that the models have the same spectrum of a family of twisted models that we get by a local U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) gauge transformation.

Algebraic Bethe ansatz method

In this section we will shortly review the ABA method and present the transfer matrix used to get the solution of the
models
[
31
,
28
]
. We begin with the
gl(2)
-invariant
R
-matrix, depending on the spectral parameter
u
,

R(u)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&0&0&0\\
0&b(u)&c(u)&0\\
0&c(u)&b(u)&0\\
0&0&0&1\end{array}\right),

(1)

with
b(u)=u/(u+\eta)
,
c(u)=\eta/(u+\eta)
and
b(u)+c(u)=1
. Above,

\eta
is an arbitrary parameter, to be chosen later.

It is easy to check that
R(u)
satisfies the Yang-Baxter equation

R_{12}(u-v)R_{13}(u)R_{23}(v)=R_{23}(v)R_{13}(u)R_{12}(u-v),

(2)

where
R_{jk}(u)
denotes the matrix acting non-trivially
on the
j
-th and the
k
-th spaces and as the identity on the remaining
space.

Next we define the monodromy matrix
\hat{T}(u)
,

\hat{T}(u)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\hat{A}(u)&\hat{B}(u)\\
\hat{C}(u)&\hat{D}(u)\end{array}\right),

(3)

such that the Yang-Baxter algebra is satisfied

R_{12}(u-v)\hat{T}_{1}(u)\hat{T}_{2}(v)=\hat{T}_{2}(v)\hat{T}_{1}(u)R_{12}(u-v).

(4)

In what follows we will choose a realization for the monodromy matrix
\pi(\hat{T}(u))=\hat{L}(u)

to obtain solutions of a family of fragmented Bose-Einstein condensates models. In this construction, the Lax operators
\hat{L}(u)
have to satisfy the relation

R_{12}(u-v)\hat{L}_{1}(u)\hat{L}_{2}(v)=\hat{L}_{2}(v)\hat{L}_{1}(u)R_{12}(u-v).

(5)

Then, defining the transfer matrix, as usual, through

\hat{t}(u)=tr\;\pi(\hat{T}(u))=\pi(\hat{A}(u)+\hat{D}(u)),

(6)

it follows from (
4
) that the transfer matrix commutes for
different values of the spectral parameter; i. e.,

[\hat{t}(u),\hat{t}(v)]=0,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\forall\;u,\;v.

(7)

Consequently, the models derived from this transfer matrix will be integrable. Another consequence is that the
coefficients
\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{k}
in the transfer matrix
\hat{t}(u)
,

\hat{t}(u)=\sum_{k}\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{k}u^{k},

(8)

are conserved quantities or simply
c
-numbers, with

[\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{j},\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{k}]=0,\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\forall\;j,\;k.

(9)

If the transfer matrix
\hat{t}(u)
is a polynomial function in
u
, with
k\geq 0
, it is easy to see that,

\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{0}=\hat{t}(0)\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\left.\frac{d^{k}\hat{t}(u)}{du^{k}}\right|_{u=0}.

(10)

For the standard bosonic operators satisfying the Heisenberg-Weyl algebra

[\hat{p}_{i}^{\dagger},\hat{q}_{j}^{\dagger}]=[\hat{p}_{i},\hat{q}_{j}]=0,\qquad[\hat{p}_{i},\hat{q}_{j}^{\dagger}]=\delta_{pq}\delta_{ij}\hat{I},

(11)

[\hat{N}_{pi},\hat{q}_{j}^{\dagger}]=+\hat{p}_{j}^{\dagger}\delta_{pq}\delta_{ij},\qquad[\hat{N}_{pi},\hat{q}_{j}]=-\hat{p}_{j}\delta_{pq}\delta_{ij},

(12)

with
p,q=a\;\mbox{or}\;b
,
i=1,\ldots,n
and
j=1,\ldots,m
, we have the following Lax operators,

\hat{L}^{\Sigma_{a}^{n}}(u)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u\hat{I}+\eta\sum_{j=1}^{n}\hat{N}_{aj}&\sum_{j=1}^{n}t_{aj}\hat{a}_{j}\\
\sum_{j=1}^{n}s_{aj}\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}&\eta^{-1}\zeta_{a}\hat{I}\end{array}\right),

(13)

and

\hat{L}^{\Sigma_{b}^{m}}(u)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u\hat{I}+\eta\sum_{k=1}^{m}\hat{N}_{bk}&\sum_{k=1}^{m}t_{bk}\hat{b}_{k}\\
\sum_{k=1}^{m}s_{bk}\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger}&\eta^{-1}\zeta_{b}\hat{I}\end{array}\right),

(14)

if the conditions,
\zeta_{a}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}s_{aj}t_{aj}
and
\zeta_{b}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}s_{bk}t_{bk}
, are satisfied. The above Lax operators satisfy the equation (
5
).

3
The fragmented model

The Hamiltonian of the BECs fragmented model is,

\displaystyle\hat{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}U_{ajaj}\hat{N}^{2}_{aj}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}U_{bkbk}\hat{N}^{2}_{bk}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1(j\neq l)}^{n}U_{ajal}\hat{N}_{aj}\hat{N}_{al}

(15)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{s=1(k\neq s)}^{m}U_{bkbs}\hat{N}_{bk}\hat{N}_{bs}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}U_{ajbk}\hat{N}_{aj}\hat{N}_{bk}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\epsilon_{aj}-\mu_{aj})\hat{N}_{aj}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}(\epsilon_{bk}-\mu_{bk})\hat{N}_{bk}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}J_{ajbk}(\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{k}+\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j}).

The parameters,
U_{prqs}
, describe the atom-atom
S
-wave scattering, the
\mu_{ps}
parameters are the external potentials and
\epsilon_{ps}
are the energies in the BECs. The parameters
J_{ajbk}
are the tunnelling amplitudes. The operators
N_{ps}
are the number of atoms operators. The labels
p
and
q
stand for the BECs
a
and
b
with
r,s=j,k
,
j=1,\ldots,n
and
k=1,\ldots,m
. We just remark that
U_{pjpk}=U_{pkpj}
. The BECs are coupled by Josephson tunnelling and the total number of atoms,
\hat{N}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\hat{N}_{aj}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\hat{N}_{bk}
, is a conserved quantity,
[\hat{H},\hat{N}]=0
.

For each pair of
aj
and
bk
condensates we have the following currents algebra
[

]

[\mathcal{T}_{ajbk},\mathcal{J}_{ajbk}]=i\mathcal{I}_{ajbk},\quad[\mathcal{I}_{ajbk},\mathcal{T}_{ajbk}]=i\mathcal{J}_{ajbk},\quad[\mathcal{J}_{ajbk},\mathcal{I}_{ajbk}]=i\mathcal{T}_{ajbk},

(16)

with the currents

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{ajbk}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(N_{aj}-N_{bk}),

(17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{ajbk}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2i}(\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{k}-\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j}),

(18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{ajbk}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{k}+\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j}).

(19)

The state space is spanned by the base
\{|n_{a1},\ldots,n_{bm}\rangle\}
and we can write each vector state as

\displaystyle|n_{a1},\ldots,n_{bm}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{n}n_{aj}!\prod_{k=1}^{m}n_{bk}!}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger})^{n_{aj}}\prod_{k=1}^{m}(\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger})^{n_{bk}}|0\rangle,

where
|0\rangle=|0_{a1},\ldots,0_{bm}\rangle
is the vacuum vector state in the Fock space. We can use the states (
) to write the matrix representation of the Hamiltonian (
15
). The dimension of the space of states increase very fast when we increase
N
,

d=\frac{(n+m-1+N)!}{(n+m-1)!N!},

(21)

where
n+m
is the total number of BECs in the system and
N
is the eigenvalues of
\hat{N}
,
N=\sum_{j=1}^{n}n_{aj}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}n_{bk}
. In the case where we have only two BECs
[
37
]
(one
a_{1}
and one
b_{1}
) the dimension is
d=N+1
.

We can write a general state in the space spanned by the vectors (
) as

|\Psi\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{n}n_{aj}!\prod_{k=1}^{m}n_{bk}!}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger})^{n_{aj}}\prod_{k=1}^{m}(\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger})^{n_{bk}}|0\rangle.

(22)

If
N
is even,
\sum_{j=1}^{n}n_{aj}
and
\sum_{k=1}^{m}n_{bk}
have the same parity and the wavefunction (
22
) is even. If
N
is odd,
\sum_{j=1}^{n}n_{aj}
and
\sum_{k=1}^{m}n_{bk}
have different parity and the wavefunction (
22
) is odd
[
81
]
. The kernel of the Hamiltonian (
15
) is the vacuum,

ker(\hat{H})={\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}|0\rangle_{aj}\otimes\bigotimes_{k=1}^{m}|0\rangle_{bk}},

(23)

and the dimension of the Hilbert space is
d+1
.

In the Figs. (

Exact solution of the fragmented model

In this section we present the exact solution of the BEC fragmented model using the Lax operators (
13
) and (

) for models with different number of BECs
a
and

.

Using the co-multiplication property of the Lax operators we can write,

\hat{L}(u)=\hat{L}_{1}^{\Sigma_{a}^{n}}(u+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\omega_{aj})\hat{L}_{2}^{\Sigma_{b}^{m}}(u-\sum_{k=1}^{m}\omega_{bk}).

(31)

Following the monodromy matrix (
3
) we can write the operators,

\displaystyle\pi(\hat{A}(u))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(u\hat{I}+\hat{I}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\omega_{aj}+\eta\sum_{j=1}^{n}\hat{N}_{aj}\right)\left(u\hat{I}-\hat{I}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\omega_{bk}+\eta\sum_{k=1}^{m}\hat{N}_{bk}\right)

(32)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}t_{aj}s_{bk}\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j},

\displaystyle\pi(\hat{B}(u))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(u\hat{I}+\hat{I}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\omega_{aj}+\eta\sum_{j=1}^{n}\hat{N}_{aj}\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}t_{bk}\hat{b}_{k}\right)+\frac{\zeta_{b}}{\eta}\sum_{j=1}^{n}t_{aj}\hat{a}_{j},

(33)

\displaystyle\pi(\hat{C}(u))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}s_{aj}\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}\right)\left(u\hat{I}-\hat{I}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\omega_{bk}+\eta\sum_{k=1}^{m}\hat{N}_{bk}\right)+\frac{\zeta_{a}}{\eta}\sum_{k=1}^{m}s_{bk}\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger},

(34)

\displaystyle\pi(\hat{D}(u))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}s_{aj}t_{bk}\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{k}+\eta^{-2}\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}\hat{I}.

(35)

Taking the trace of the operator (
31
) we get the transfer matrix

\displaystyle\hat{t}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u^{2}\hat{I}+u\hat{I}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\omega_{aj}-\sum_{k=1}^{m}\omega_{bk}\right)+u\eta\hat{N}

(36)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}}{\eta^{2}}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\omega_{aj}\omega_{bk}\right)\hat{I}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\eta\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(\omega_{bk}\hat{N}_{aj}-\omega_{aj}\hat{N}_{bk}\right)+\eta^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\hat{N}_{aj}\hat{N}_{bk}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}(s_{aj}t_{bk}\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{k}+s_{bk}t_{aj}\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j}).

From (
10
) we identify the conserved quantities of the transfer matrix (

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\eta^{-2}\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\omega_{aj}\omega_{bk}\right)\hat{I}

(37)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\eta\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left(\omega_{bk}\hat{N}_{aj}-\omega_{aj}\hat{N}_{bk}\right)+\eta^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\hat{N}_{aj}\hat{N}_{bk}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}(s_{aj}t_{bk}\hat{a}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{k}+s_{bk}t_{aj}\hat{b}_{k}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{j}),

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{I}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\omega_{aj}-\sum_{k=1}^{m}\omega_{bk}\right)+\eta\hat{N},

(38)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{I}.

(39)

We can rewrite the Hamiltonian (
15
) using these conserved quantities

\hat{H}=\xi_{0}\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{0}+\xi_{1}\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{2},

(40)

with the following identification for the parameters

\displaystyle\xi_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\xi_{0}\left(\eta^{-2}\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}-\omega_{ab}^{nm}\right)-\xi_{1}\Delta\omega_{ab}^{2},

(41)

\displaystyle\Delta\omega_{ab}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\omega_{aj}-\sum_{k=1}^{m}\omega_{bk},

(42)

\displaystyle\omega_{ab}^{nm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\omega_{aj}\omega_{bk},

(43)

U_{ajaj}=U_{bkbk}=\eta^{2}\xi_{1},\qquad U_{ajal}=U_{bkbs}=2\eta^{2}\xi_{1},

\hat{H}=\xi_{0}\hat{t}(u)+\xi_{1}\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{1}^{2}-\xi_{0}\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{1}u-(\xi_{0}u^{2}-\xi_{2})\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{2}.

7
Summary

We have solved a family of fragmented Bose-Hubbard models using the multi-states boson Lax operator
[
41
]
. These models can be considered as graphs, with the BECs in the vertices and the edge representing the tunnelling between the BECs. The graphs form the complete bipartite graph
K_{n,m}
. We have showed that in the no interaction limit,
\eta\rightarrow 0
, if the Bethe roots are all real numbers, they are on a
N
-dimensional sphere of radius
R_{N}
. If the Bethe roots are complex numbers, they are on a
2N
-dimensional proper central surface with semiaxes
R_{N}
. For each Bethe roots set we have a set of
N!
permutations of the Bethe roots that satisfy the Bethe ansatz equation. By a local
U(1)
gauge transformation we got twisted models and showed that they have the same spectrum of eigenvalues but with different wavefunctions. The new wavefunction presents a pattern of interference dependent of the gauge transformation parameters and preserve the parity.

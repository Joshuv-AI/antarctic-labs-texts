Regular decomposition of the edge set of graphs with applications
Béla Csaba

Abstract
We introduce a new method for decomposing the edge set of a graph, and use it to replace the Regularity lemma of Szemerédi in some graph embedding problems. An algorithmic version is also given.



1
Introduction

The Szemerédi Regularity lemma is among the most powerful tools in graph theory. The lemma appeared in
[
27
]
in 1978. A weaker version had already been used earlier
by Szemerédi to prove the Erdős-Turán conjecture
[
14
,
26
]
. Since then the lemma and its ramifications have found several applications in graph and hypergraph theory,
number theory, algebra, geometry, and computer science.

Given a simple
1
1

1

We consider only simple graphs in this paper.
graph
G=(V,E)
and a number
\varepsilon\in(0,1),
the lemma asserts that
V

can be partitioned into
k\leq N(\varepsilon)
subsets
V_{1}\cup\ldots\cup V_{k}
and another set
V_{0}=V-(\cup_{i}V_{i})
such that
G[V_{i},V_{j}]
is
\varepsilon
-regular for every
1\leq i\neq j\leq k,

except at most
\varepsilon k^{2}
pairs of indices,
|V_{0}|\leq\varepsilon n
and
|V_{i}|=(n-|V_{0}|)/k
for every
1\leq i\leq k.

In the proof the threshold
N(\varepsilon)
for the number of parts is a tower of twos of height
O(\varepsilon^{-5}).
As Gowers proved in
[
19
]
, this tower-type bound is
unavoidable in general, more
precisely, there are graphs for which
N(\varepsilon)
has to be at least a tower of twos of height
\Omega(\varepsilon^{-1/16}).
Conlon and Fox
[
4
]
further improved the lower bound
to
\Omega(\varepsilon^{-1}).
This shows the major drawback of the Regularity lemma: in order to get meaningful results, one has to work with enormously large graphs, thereby ruling out practical applications of the lemma.
We remark that if the number of edges between vertex sets
A
and
B
is
o(|A|\cdot|B|),
then the
(A,B)
-pair is
\varepsilon
-regular by definition, unless

\varepsilon
is very small compared to
|A|
and
|B|.

Hence, the lemma is useful only when the density of the graph is essentially a positive constant.

In order to avoid these disadvantages, several alternatives for substituting the Regularity lemma have been discovered, e.g., the weak regularity lemma of Frieze and Kannan
[
15
]
, or the cylindrical regularity lemma by Eaton and Rödl
[
1
,
10
,
11
]
. Let us also mention a regularity lemma by Gowers
[
20
]
, and a somewhat similar one by the author
[
7
]
. There are also versions for dense subgraphs of random graphs or
C_{4}
-free graphs
[
6
]
, for graphs with bounded VC-dimension
[
2
,
24
]
or for graphs with low threshold-rank
[
17
]
. This list is long, but still far from being complete. However, none of the above results have the full strength of the original Regularity lemma.

In this paper we introduce a new, iterative graph decomposition method. Our technique allows one to use some of the methods developed in the applications of the Regularity lemma of Szemerédi.
As we will see, this decomposition may be used for solving embedding or packing problems in graphs on
n
vertices that have
\Omega(n^{2}/(\log n)^{1/q})
edges, where
q>2
is a
number depending on the problem itself. Besides, the number of vertices in the graph is allowed to be just “reasonably”
large.

In the decomposition of this paper one does not partition the vertex set of the graph as in the Szemerédi Regularity lemma, rather the edge set.
The basic building blocks of the decomposition are pseudorandom subgraphs, usually super-regular pairs or regular pairs.
The most important difference between this method and the many versions of the Regularity lemma is that the regular pairs of our decomposition are edge-disjoint, but their vertex sets may intersect. These intersections make the use of the edge decomposition method more difficult,
but it still can replace the Regularity lemma in many cases. We will demonstrate this via some examples. Still, we think that one cannot use this technique for so diverse problems as the Regularity lemma.

Let us remark that the present decomposition method is similar to the cylindrical regularity lemma for bipartite graphs by Eaton and Rödl
[
10
,
11
]
and
[
1
]
, although
it is slightly less restrictive.
Our main motivation is the graph functional method of Komlós
[
22
]
. This allows us to get better bounds than that of the cylindrical regularity lemma, which
can be especially important in case the graph has vanishing density.

We also provide an algorithmic version: a decomposition that can be found in polynomial time by a randomized algorithm.
The algorithmic version works for somewhat larger and somewhat denser graphs than the one based on graph functionals, but it is still meaningful for graphs having vanishing densities.

The outline of the paper is as follows. In Section 2 we review the necessary notions, results for bundle decompositions, and state our main theorem. In Section 3 we prove our main theorem via several lemmas.
In Section 4 we apply our method for packing large trees and bounded degree bipartite graphs into a host graph such that only
o(n^{2})
edges remain uncovered. In Section 5 we prove a conditional triangle removal lemma for graphs having relatively few
C_{5}
s.
Finally, in Section 6 we prove an algorithmic version of our main theorem.

We made no attempts to optimize on the constants in the paper,
and will not be concerned with floor signs and divisibility in the proofs. This makes the notation simpler, easier to follow. Throughout the paper
\log x
will denote the
natural logarithm
of
x,
and
exp(f)=e^{f}
for any expression
f.

2.2
The edge-decomposition theorem

We are ready to state our main result:

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
G
be a balanced bipartite graph on
n

d_{G},
and let

and
0<\varepsilon<1/12

Then the

of

can be decomposed as follows:

E(G)
can be written as the edge-disjoint union of the
(\varepsilon,d-\varepsilon)
-super-regular

and another bipartite graph
H_{0}\in\mathcal{B},
where

For

has at least
m=m(\varepsilon,d)

d,

while
H_{0}
has density less than
d.
Furthermore,

m\geq d^{(10/\varepsilon^{2})\log(1/\varepsilon)}n/2

and

K\leq 2d_{G}\cdot d^{-(20/\varepsilon^{2})\log(1/\varepsilon)}/d.

Note that while implicit, the density of

is less than

Proof of Theorem
2.5

We begin with the definition of our graph functional. After showing some of its properties, we will prove the theorem.

3.1
A graph functional for finding super-regular pairs

The method of graph functionals was introduced by Komlós
[
22
]
. The general form of such a graph functional

\Phi
for some graph
H
is as follows:

\Phi(H)=\varphi(d_{H})\cdot v(H),

where (as before)
d_{H}
denotes the density of
H
and
\varphi
is an increasing real function.
We will be interested in the special case
\varphi(x)=x^{r}/2
for
r=r(\varepsilon),
with
0<\varepsilon\ll 1.

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
we
consider the following maximization problem:

\max_{H\subset G,\ H\in\mathcal{B}}\Phi(H)=\max_{H\subset G,\ H\in\mathcal{B}}d_{H}^{r}\cdot v(H).

We will prove that if
d_{G}
is not too
small and
r=r(\varepsilon)
is sufficiently large, then the subgraph
H\subset G
at which
\Phi
attains its maximum is a super-regular pair.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a balanced bipartite graph on
n
vertices with density
d_{G}
and
0<\varepsilon<1/4
be a real number. Set

r=(10/\varepsilon^{2})\log(1/\varepsilon).
If
n>exp(r\log(1/d_{G}))
and
d_{G}>\varepsilon,
then
G
contains an

(\varepsilon,\delta)
-super-regular subgraph
H\in\mathcal{B}

with
\delta\geq d_{G}-\varepsilon,

v(H)\geq d^{r}_{G}n/2
and density
d_{H}\geq d_{G}.

Proposition
3.1
is proved via a few lemmas. For the first one let’s assume that
H=(A,B;E)
is a balanced bipartite graph such that
m=|A|=|B|.
Let
\Psi
be a
graph functional defined on balanced bipartite subgraphs
F
of
H
as follows:

\Psi(F)=d_{F}^{q}v(F),

where
q\in[2/\varepsilon,m]
is an arbitrary real number. Notice,
that here we allow the exponent of the density to be smaller than the
r
in Proposition
3.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that there exists
v\in A
such that
deg(v)\leq(d_{H}-\varepsilon)m.
Then there is a vertex
w\in B
such that
\Psi(H^{\prime})>\Psi(H),
where
H^{\prime}
is
the subgraph of
H
induced by the vertex classes
A-v
and
B-w.

Proof:
First we take an arbitrary vertex
w\in B
such that
deg(w)\leq d_{H}m.
Such a vertex must exist using that
e(H)=d_{H}m^{2}.

Let us compute the density of
H^{\prime}=H[A-v,B-w].
Since
H^{\prime}
has
m-1
vertices in both vertex classes, we have

d_{H^{\prime}}\geq\frac{d_{H}m^{2}-2d_{H}m+\varepsilon m}{(m-1)^{2}}.

Hence,

\frac{\Psi(H^{\prime})}{\Psi(H)}\geq\left(\frac{(d_{H}m^{2}-2d_{H}m+\varepsilon m)m^{2}}{(m-1)^{2}d_{H}m^{2}}\right)^{q}\frac{m-1}{m}.

After simplifying the above expression we get that

\frac{\Psi(H^{\prime})}{\Psi(H)}\geq\left(\frac{m^{2}-2m+\frac{\varepsilon m}{d_{H}}}{(m-1)^{2}}\right)^{q}\frac{m-1}{m}.

We show that the right hand side of the above inequality is larger than 1. This implies that
\Psi(H^{\prime})>\Psi(H),
thereby proving the lemma.
This is equivalent to the following

\left(\frac{m^{2}-2m+\frac{\varepsilon m}{d_{H}}}{(m-1)^{2}}\right)^{q}>1+\frac{1}{m-1}.

Using Bernoulli’s inequality

\left(\frac{m^{2}-2m+\frac{\varepsilon m}{d_{H}}}{(m-1)^{2}}\right)^{q}=\left(\frac{(m-1)^{2}-1+\frac{\varepsilon m}{d_{H}}}{(m-1)^{2}}\right)^{q}\geq 1+q\frac{\frac{\varepsilon m}{d_{H}}-1}{(m-1)^{2}}.

Using that

and that we must have

one can easily verify that the left hand side is larger than 1, proving what was desired.

3.2
Finishing the proof of Theorem

Let
G=(V,E)
be a balanced bipartite graph on
n

d_{G}.
Let
0<\varepsilon<1/4
and
0<d\leq 1
be real numbers. For finding the decomposition of
G

3.1

In the first step we check if
d_{G}\geq d.
If not, we let
H_{0}=G,
and stop. Otherwise let
H_{1}
be a balanced bipartite graph for which the graph functional

\Phi(H)=d_{H}^{r}\cdot v(H)
is maximal. By Proposition
3.1

H_{1}
is an
(\varepsilon,d_{H}-\varepsilon)
-super-regular pair.
Let
G_{1}=(V,E_{1})
be a subgraph of
G
what we obtain by deleting the edges of
H_{1}
from
G,
that is,
E_{1}=E(G)-E(H_{1}).

Next we repeat the above procedure for
G_{1}.
First check if the density of
G_{1}
is at least
d.
If not, we let
H_{0}=G_{1}.
If
G_{1}
is sufficiently large, then
we apply Proposition
3.1
again in order to obtain a new super-regular pair in
G_{1},
which we call
H_{2}.

In general, let us assume that we have found the first
i
super-regular pairs
H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{i}.
Let
G_{i}=(V,E_{i}),
where
E_{i}=E(G_{i-1})-E(H_{i}).

We check if

d_{G_{i}}\geq d.
If not, then we let
H_{0}=G_{i},
and stop. Otherwise we use Proposition
3.1
for finding
H_{i+1}.

Next we consider the bounds for
m
and

The lower bound for
m
is just the lower bound for

in Proposition
3.1
, with

plugged in for the density of the graph.
For the upper bound for

since the
H_{i}
’s are edge-disjoint, each has at least
m
vertices in both parts and density at least
d,
while
e(G)=d_{G}n^{2}/2.
Reordering the inequality and using that
m\geq d^{(10/\varepsilon^{2})\log(1/\varepsilon)}n/2
gives the claimed bound:

K\leq 2d_{G}\cdot d^{-(20/\varepsilon^{2})\log(1/\varepsilon)}/d.

\Box

4
Applying Proposition
3.1
for approximate decompositions

The study of packing of graphs dates back to more than a century, and recently it has received much
attention
[
8
,
23
,
18
]
. The generic
packing question is as follows: Given a “large” host graph
G
and a “small” graph
H,
is it possible to cover the edge set of

G
edge-disjointly by copies of
H
? Equivalently, we sometimes say that the edge set of
G
is decomposed by edge-disjoint copies of
H
. In many cases the single small graph
H
is replaced by a family of graphs.
Perhaps one of the most beautiful questions in the area is the still open Gyárfás–Lehel conjecture from 1978 on the decomposition of the edge set of

K_{n}
into trees having every order between
1
and
n.

In general even an approximate form of these problems, when we may leave a small percentage of the edge set of the host graph uncovered, is very challenging. Below we present an application of Proposition
3.1

for approximately decomposing the edge set of a graph into edge-disjoint trees. Depending on
\varepsilon
and
\delta,
these trees may have linear size and linear maximum degree.

Let
T
be a rooted tree on
t
vertices, its root is denoted by
r.
Given any
x\in V(T)
let
N^{*}(x)
denote the set of its children, and
deg_{T}^{*}(x)=|N^{*}(x)|.
We define the
L_{i}

level sets
of
T
as follows:

L_{1}=\{r\},
and for
i\geq 1
we have that
L_{i+1}=\cup_{x\in L_{i}}N^{*}(x).
Hence, if a vertex
y
lies in
L_{j},
then
y
is at distance
j-1
from
r.
Let us denote the total number of levels by
s.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
T
be as above. Assume that
H(A,B;E)
is an
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-super-regular pair with vertex parts
A
and
B,
such that
m=|A|=|B|\geq 2t,
and
\delta\geq 3\varepsilon.

We further assume that
|L_{i}|\leq\delta m/4
for every
i\geq 1.
Then
T\subset H.

Proof:

Our goal is to find an edge-preserving injection
\varphi:V(T)\longrightarrow A\cup B.
The algorithm we use constructs
\varphi
step-by-step, alternately embedding consecutive levels of
T,

starting at
L_{1}=\{r\}.

At any point in time we denote the uncovered subset of
A
by
A_{u},
and similarly, the uncovered subset of
B
by
B_{u}.
We need to define two more subsets:

A^{\prime}=\{v\in A_{u}:\ deg_{H}(v,B_{u})\geq(\delta-\varepsilon)|B_{u}|\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad B^{\prime}=\{v\in B_{u}:\ deg_{H}(v,A_{u})\geq(\delta-\varepsilon)|A_{u}|\}.

Observe, that
A_{u},B_{u},A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
shrink dynamically as we embed more and more levels. After succesfully embedding, say, level
L_{i}
into
A^{\prime}
we update these
sets as follows:
A_{u}=A_{u}-\varphi(L_{i}),

A^{\prime}=A^{\prime}-\varphi(L_{i}),
and we also have to leave out those vertices of
B^{\prime}
that have degree less than
(\delta-\varepsilon)|A_{u}|
in the newly updated
A_{u},
the set
B_{u}
remains the same. We do the updating analogously when a level is embedded into
B^{\prime}.

The proof of the claim below follows easily from our discussion above and the definition of super-regular pairs, we leave it for the reader.

Claim 4.2
.

In the beginning we have
A_{u}=A^{\prime}=A,

B_{u}=B^{\prime}=B.

Claim 4.3
.

Given the embedding of
L_{i}
into
A^{\prime},
we can embed
L_{i+1}
into
B^{\prime}
greedily, here
1\leq i\leq s-1.
Analogous statement holds in case
L_{i}
is embedded into
B^{\prime}.

Proof:
Assume that
x\in L_{i},
and therefore
v=\varphi(x)\in A^{\prime}.
By the definition of
A^{\prime}
we have that
deg_{G}(v,B_{u})\geq(\delta-\varepsilon)|B_{u}|\geq|L_{i+1}|+\varepsilon m.

Since
|B_{u}|-|B^{\prime}|\leq\varepsilon m
using
\varepsilon
-regularity, this implies that
deg_{G}(v,B^{\prime})\geq|L_{i+1}|.

Hence, for every
x\in L_{i}
we can greedily choose a subset
S_{x}\subset N(v,B^{\prime})
such that
|S_{x}|=deg^{*}_{T}(x),
and
S_{x}\cap S_{x^{\prime}}=\emptyset
for every
x^{\prime}\neq x,

x^{\prime}\in L_{i}.

Therefore we may choose the
\varphi(y)
images for
y\in N^{*}(x)
greedily from
S_{x}.
This extension of
\varphi
clearly satisfies the requirements of the lemma. It is easy to
see that the same procedure works if
L_{i}
is embedded into
B^{\prime},
only the letters
A
and
B
has to be switched.

\Box

We have the following algorithm for finding the embedding
\varphi.
First we embed
L_{1}
into
A^{\prime},
which is possible by Claim
4.2
. Then we repeatedly apply Claim
4.3
, and embed more and more levels, always using the
B^{\prime}
or
A^{\prime}
subsets. Since
m\geq 2t,
we always have sufficiently many vertices so that
|A^{\prime}|,|B^{\prime}|\geq\delta m/2-\varepsilon m.
Hence, this procedure never gets stuck, proving what was desired.

\Box

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
0<\varepsilon<1/4
and
3\varepsilon\leq\delta\leq 1
be real numbers,
m
and
k
be positive integers such that
k\leq\delta m/4.
Set
r=(10/\varepsilon^{2})\log 1/\varepsilon.
Assume that
T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{l}
are rooted trees, each on at

vertices. Denote their total number of edges by

Let

be a balanced bipartite graph on

such that

k

If

then
G
has edge-disjoint copies of
T_{1},\ldots,T_{l}.

Proof:
The theorem follows from Proposition
3.1
and Lemma
4.1
. First, we apply Proposition
3.1
in order to find an
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-super-regular pair
H_{1},

where
H_{1}
has at least

vertices in both of its parts.
Since
H_{1}
and

satisfies the requirements of Lemma
4.1
, we can embed a copy of
T_{1}
into
H_{1}.
We used up precisely
e(T_{1})
edges of

this way, we delete them from

the rest of
H_{1}
is added back to the graph. This procedure is repeated for every

we find a large
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-super-regular pair

by Proposition

having at least
m

vertices in both parts, since the density of what is left from

no matter how many trees were embedded thus far. Then we embed

into

by Lemma

, delete

we used for

is added back to the graph.

\Box

Note that, as we indicated earlier, the maximum degree of a tree can be even linear, if
\varepsilon,\delta
are (small) constants.

The idea of the above almost decomposition, that is, iteratively applying Proposition
3.1
for the vacant part of the host graph after embedding a subgraph, can be used for edge-disjoint packing of other kind of graphs.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices with density
d=\Omega(1).
Assume that
\varepsilon,\delta
are real numbers and

a positive integer such that
0<\varepsilon<\delta^{5D}<\delta\ll d.

Set
r=10\log(1/\varepsilon)/\varepsilon^{2}.
Let

be bipartite graphs with
maximum degree
\Delta(H_{i})\leq D

and assume that
v(H_{i})\leq d^{r}n/2.
If
\sum_{i}e(H_{i})\leq(1-2\delta)e(G),
then

can be packed edge-disjointly into
G.

For the proof we need the Blow-up lemma
[
21
]
of Komlós, Sárközy and Szemerédi.

Theorem 4.6
.

[Blow-up Lemma]
Given a graph
R
of order
r
and positive integers
\delta
and
\Delta

there exists a positive
\varepsilon=(\delta,\Delta,r)
such that the following holds: Let

be
arbitrary positive parameters and let us replace the vertices
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{r}
of

with pairwise
disjoint sets
W_{1},W_{2},\ldots,W_{r}
of sizes
n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}
(blowing up

). We construct two graphs
on the same vertex set
V=\cup_{i}W_{i}
: The first graph
F
is obtained by replacing each edge

v_{i}v_{j}\in E(R)
with the complete bipartite graph between
W_{i}
and
W_{j}.
A sparser graph
G
is
constructed by replacing each edge
v_{i}v_{j}
arbitrarily with an
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-super-regular pair between

W_{i}
and

H
with maximum degree

We are going to use the Blow-up lemma for the case when

contains two vertices (

) and the edge that connects them. For us it is more useful to have a more explicit relation
of

and

than what is given in the above formulation. By carefully reading the proof of the Blow-up lemma in

Proof:
Assume that we have already packed the first

Using the Blow-up lemma one can find a copy of

If not, the remaining density in the host graph

\Box

Theorem

gives an approximate solution of a special case of the Oberwolfach problem (see e.g. in

, where the solution is also given), in which 2-regular spanning

While we only pack subgraphs that are relatively small compared to the host graph (but still can have linear size), our host graph does not have to be the complete graph,
and the subgraphs to be packed can be arbitrary bounded degree bipartite graphs.

5
A conditional triangle removal lemma

The celebrated result of Ruzsa and Szemerédi
[
25
]
states, roughly speaking, that if a graph of order
n
has
o(n^{3})
triangles, then it can be made triangle-free
by deleting
o(n^{2})
edges from it.
Let us give a more precise formulation:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that if
G
has at most
\delta n^{3}
triangles, then
it can be made triangle-free by removing at most
\varepsilon n^{2}
edges.

This very important result has far reaching implications in graph and hypergraph theory, number theory, etc., see for example in
[
5
]
. The currently best bound for
\delta

is a tower of twos of height
O(\log{1/\varepsilon})
by Fox
[
16
]
.

Bollobás and Győri raised the following question in
[
3
]
: How many triangles can a graph
G
on
n
vertices have, if
G
has no cycle of length 5? They proved that the number of triangles in such a graph is at most
(5/4)n^{3/2}+o(n^{3/2}).
Since then several improvements were found on the constant multiplier of
n^{3/2}

[
13
,
12
]
.

In
[
6
]
Conlon et al. considered a conditional removal problem, which relates the above two questions. They proved that, given a graph of order
n
with
o(n^{2})
copies of
C_{4}
’s and

o(n^{5/2})
copies of
C_{5}
’s, it can be made
\{C_{3},C_{5}\}
-free by deleting
o(n^{3/2})
edges. Below we state and prove a somewhat similar theorem in which we have
no condition on the number of
C_{4}
’s of the graph, only for the number of
C_{5}
’s. Since
C_{4}
’s emerge when the number of edges is
\Omega(n^{3/2}),

the bound for the number of edges to be removed is much larger. Still, the
\delta
below is just a single exponential function of a polynomial of
1/\varepsilon,
a huge gain compared
to the
\delta
in the known proofs of the (unconditional) triangle removal lemma.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
G
be a tripartite graph with vertex parts
X,Y
and
Z
such that
|X|=|Y|=|Z|=n.
Let
0<\varepsilon\leq 1/4
and set

K_{0}=exp((20/\varepsilon^{2})\log^{2}(1/\varepsilon))/\varepsilon
and
m=exp(-10\log^{2}(1/\varepsilon)/\varepsilon^{2})n.
If the number of
C_{5}
’s in
G
is at most

\delta n^{5}=\frac{\varepsilon^{6}m^{2}n^{3}}{2K_{0}^{2}},

then
G
can be made triangle-free by deleting at most
4\varepsilon n^{2}
edges.

Proof:
We begin with applying Theorem
2.5
with parameters
\varepsilon
and
d=2\varepsilon
for the bipartite subgraph
G[X,Y].

We obtain the
(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
-super-regular pairs
H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{K},

each having parts on at least
m
vertices, and
H_{0}
with less than
dn^{2}=2\varepsilon n^{2}
edges. Note that
K\leq K_{0}.

Call a
C_{5}=x_{1}y_{1}x_{2}y_{2}z

good
, if
x_{1},x_{2}\in X,

y_{1},y_{2}\in Y,

z\in Z,
its edges are
x_{1}y_{1},y_{1}x_{2},x_{2}y_{2},y_{2}z,zx_{1},
and
x_{1}y_{1},y_{1}x_{2},x_{2}y_{2}
belong to the same super-regular pair of the
decomposition. Observe that if
x^{\prime}y^{\prime}z^{\prime}
is a triangle in
G-H_{0}
such that
x^{\prime}\in X,y^{\prime}\in Y
and
z^{\prime}\in Z,
then the
x^{\prime}y^{\prime}
edge must belong to a bundle.

Simple computation tells that the number of
z\in Z
which appears in more than

\frac{\varepsilon^{5}m^{2}n^{2}}{2K_{0}^{2}}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{\varepsilon^{6}m^{2}n^{2}}{2K_{0}^{2}}

good
C_{5}
’s is less than
\varepsilon n.

Call such vertices of
Z

bad,
the rest of
Z
are the
good
vertices. Delete every edge that joins a bad vertex to any vertex in
X,
this way we deleted
at most
\varepsilon n^{2}
edges.

Let
z\in Z
be any good vertex. Assume that it has at least
\varepsilon n/K
neighbors in both parts of some
H_{i}.
Denote the parts of
H_{i}
by
X_{i}\subset X
and

Y_{i}\subset Y,
and let
m_{i}=|X_{i}|=|Y_{i}|\geq m.
Notice that if
x\in X_{i},
then by the

(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
-super-regularity it has at least
\varepsilon m_{i}
neighbors in
Y,
and similar holds for any
y\in Y_{i}.
Hence, if
x\in X_{i},y\in Y_{i}
and
x,y\in N(x),

then, using
\varepsilon
-regularity, there are at least
\varepsilon^{3}m_{i}^{2}
path of length 3 that connects
x
and
y
in
H_{i}.
Using our assumption that
z
has at least
\varepsilon n/K
neighbors in
X_{i}
and
Y_{i},
there are at least
\frac{\varepsilon^{5}m^{2}n^{2}}{K^{2}}
such good
C_{5}
’s which contain
z
and three edges from
H_{i}.
Since this number
is larger than the upper bound of good
C_{5}
’s which may contain
z,
we conclude that for every good vertex
z\in Z
there is no
H_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq K
) for which
z
has many
neighbors in both parts of it.

Next we repeat the following for every good
z\in Z,
for every
1\leq i\leq K
: if
deg(z,X_{i})\leq deg(z,Y_{i}),
then delete all edges that join
z
to
X_{i},
otherwise delete
all the edges that join
z
to
Y_{i}.
This way we delete at most
n\cdot K\cdot\varepsilon n/K=\varepsilon n^{2}
edges from
G.
Since after these deletions no good
C_{5}
is left, we have removed every triangle.

\Box

We remark that in
[
6
]
it was proved that there exist
n
-vertex graphs with
o(n^{2.442})

C_{5}
’s that cannot be made triangle-free
by deleting
o(n^{3/2})
edges. Note the large gap between the two bounds for conditional removal.

The proof method of Theorem
5.2
can easily be generalized to prove statements of the following type. Let
2\leq k\leq\ell
be integers. If an
n
-vertex,
(2k-1)
-partite graph

’s, then

6
Algorithmic aspects

The algorithmic version of Szemerédi’s Regularity lemma proved to be very useful in many problems in computer science, e.g., by providing good approximation algorithms for several NP-complete questions.
The graph functionals we considered can be used to show the existence of a large super-regular pair, but they are not capable of finding one effectively.
In this section we present a deterministic polynomial time algorithm for finding a large
\varepsilon
-regular subgraph in a
sufficiently dense graph. This
algorithm can then be used for decomposing the edge set of a graph into large
\varepsilon
-regulars pairs, similarly to Theorem
2.5
, albeit the result will be somewhat weaker. Our algorithm is based on the method of
[
1
]
.

Let
M(n)
denote the time needed to multiply two
n\times n
matrices with
0,1
entries over the integers (so
M(n)=O(n^{2.376})
).
For proving an algorithmic version of the Regularity lemma, the authors of
[
1
]
, among other lemmas, used the following
4
4

4

This is Corollary 3.3 in
[
1
]
, slightly rewritten.
:

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
H
be a bipartite graph with equal parts
|A|=|B|=n.
Let
2n^{-1/4}<\varepsilon<1/16.
Then there is a
O(M(n))
time deterministic algorithm that verifies that
H
is
\varepsilon
-regular, or finds
two subsets,
A_{1}\subset A,

B_{1}\subset B,

|A_{1}|,|B_{1}|\geq\varepsilon^{4}n/16,
such that
|d(A,B)-d(A_{1},B_{1})|\geq\varepsilon^{4}.

The algorithm can be parallelized and implemented in
NC^{1}.

We call the sets
A_{1}
and
B_{1}
the
witnesses of
\varepsilon^{4}
-irregularity.
Note that the cardinalities of the witnesses for irregularity could be much smaller than
\varepsilon n.
As it is proved in
[
1
]
, this is unavoidable unless
P=NP.

We need a simple lemma before discussing the decomposition algorithm.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
H
be a bipartite graph with vertex parts
A,B
such that
|A|=|B|=n,
and let
0<\eta<d\leq 1.
Assume that the density of
H
is
d,
and that there exists
A_{1}\subset A,B_{1}\subset B

such that
|A_{1}|,|B_{1}|\geq\eta n,
and
|d-d(A_{1},B_{1})|\geq\eta.
Then in polynomial time we can find
A^{\prime}\subset A,B^{\prime}\subset B
with
|A^{\prime}|=|B^{\prime}|\geq\eta n
such that
d(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\geq d+\eta^{3}.

Proof:
We begin with the case
d(A_{1},B_{1})\leq d-\eta.
Let us assume first, that
|A_{1}|<n.
Define the sets
A_{2}=A-A_{1}
and
B_{2}=B-B_{1}.
Below we show that one of the densities
d(A_{1},B_{2}),

d(A_{2},B_{1})
or
d(A_{2},B_{2})

must be at least
d+\eta^{3}.

Suppose not. Then

e(H)=dn^{2}<(d-\eta)|A_{1}|\cdot|B_{1}|+(d+\eta^{3})(n^{2}-|A_{1}|\cdot|B_{1}|).

This implies that

\eta|A_{1}|\cdot|B_{1}|<\eta^{3}n^{2}-\eta^{3}|A_{1}|\cdot|B_{1}|.

Using that
|A_{1}|,|B_{1}|\geq\eta n,
we arrived at a contradiction. Hence, one of the subgraphs
H[A_{1},B_{2}],

H[A_{2},B_{1}]
or
H[A_{2},B_{2}]
must have density at least
d+\eta^{3}.

Denote the parts of the densest of these subgraphs by
A^{\prime\prime}
and
B^{\prime\prime},
here
A^{\prime\prime}\subset A,\ B^{\prime\prime}\subset B.

Clearly,
|A^{\prime\prime}|,|B^{\prime\prime}|\geq\eta n.
If
|A^{\prime\prime}|=|B^{\prime\prime}|,
then we are done. Assume, that
|A^{\prime\prime}|>|B^{\prime\prime}|.
Order the vertices of
A^{\prime\prime}
in decreasing order according to their degrees in
B^{\prime\prime}.
Keep the first
|B^{\prime\prime}|

vertices, and discard the rest. Call the resulting set
A^{\prime},
and let
B^{\prime}=B^{\prime\prime}.
It is easy to see that
d(A^{\prime\prime},B^{\prime\prime})\leq d(A^{\prime},B^{\prime}),
so we are done.

There is one case left, when
|A_{1}|=n.
Clearly,
|B_{1}|<n,
so
B_{2}=B-B_{1}
is non-empty. Assume that
d(A,B_{2})<d+\eta^{2}.
Then we would have

dn^{2}<(d-\eta)|A|\cdot|B_{1}|+(d+\eta^{2})|A|\cdot|B_{2}|,

which implies that

\eta|B_{1}|\cdot n<\eta^{2}n^{2}-\eta^{2}n|B_{1}|.

We arrived at a contradiction again, since
|B_{1}|\geq\eta n.
By discarding those
|B_{2}|
vertices of
A
that have the smallest degrees into
B_{2}

we get two equal sized subsets
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}=B_{2}
such that
d(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\geq d+\eta.

Let us now assume that
d(A_{1},B_{1})\geq d+\eta.
We are almost done. The only problem is if
|A_{1}|\neq|B_{1}|.
Then we discard vertices from the larger set, similarly to the way it is described above, and we
obtain the pair of sets
A^{\prime},B^{\prime}
such that
d(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\geq d+\eta
and
|A^{\prime}|=|B^{\prime}|\geq\eta n.

It is easy to see that the above
A^{\prime},\ B^{\prime}
subsets can be found in
O(n^{2})
time: we need to compute densities of at most four subgraphs, and order vertices according to their degrees.
Hence, we proved what was desired.

\Box

Using Lemma
6.2
we can easily formulate a polynomial time algorithm that finds a large

1.

Apply Lemma
6.1
. If

2.

If not, by Lemma

, substituting
\varepsilon^{4}/16
for

with
|A^{\prime}|=|B^{\prime}|\geq\varepsilon^{12}|A|/16^{3}

3.

Let
A=A^{\prime},\ B=B^{\prime},
and continue with Step 1.

The above algorithm stops in at most
16^{3}/\varepsilon^{12}
steps, since if the density of a bipartite graph is 1, it must be
\varepsilon
-regular.
With the above we have proved the following.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a balanced bipartite graph on
n
vertices with density
d_{G}
and
0<\varepsilon<1/4
be a real number such that the following is satisfied:

e^{r/4}2n^{-1/4}<\varepsilon\ll d_{G}\leq 1,
where

r=(16^{3}/\varepsilon^{12})\log(16^{3}/\varepsilon^{12}).
Then
G
contains an

\varepsilon
-regular subgraph
H\in\mathcal{B}

with density
\delta\geq d_{G}
and
v(H)\geq e^{-r}n.

Iterating the above procedure as in the proof of Theorem
2.5
we get the following.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
G
be a balanced bipartite graph on
n
vertices with density
d_{G},
and let
0<\delta\leq 1
and
0<\varepsilon<1/4

be real numbers such that the following is satisfied:

e^{r/4}2n^{-1/4}<\varepsilon\ll\delta\leq 1,
where

r=(16^{3}/\varepsilon^{12})\log(16^{3}/\varepsilon^{12}).

Then the
edge set
of
G
can be decomposed as follows:

E(G)
can be written as the edge-disjoint union of the
\varepsilon
-regular balanced
bipartite graphs
F_{1},\ldots,F_{K},
and another balanced bipartite graph
F_{0},
where

K=K(\varepsilon,\delta,d_{G})\leq d_{G}e^{r}/\delta.
For
i\geq 1
each
F_{i}
has at least

m=m(\varepsilon)\geq e^{-r}n
vertices and density at least
\delta,

while
F_{0}
has density less than
\delta.

Note that in Proposition
6.3
we guarentee only
\varepsilon
-regularity, not
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-super-regularity for
some
\delta.
If one needs super-regularity, Claim
2.4
can be used, resulting only a small loss in
the size of the pair. Of course, this remark also
applies for Theorem
6.4
.

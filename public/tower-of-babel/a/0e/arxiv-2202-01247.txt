On the Cubic Shimura lift to PGL ⁡ ( 3 ) \operatorname{PGL}(3) : The Fundamental Lemma Thanks: This work was supported by the NSF, grant numbers DMS-1801497 and DMS-2100206, by a Simons Fellowship in Mathematics, award number 816862 (Friedberg) and by a Simons Collaboration Grant, award number 709678 (Offen).
Solomon Friedberg, Omer Offen

Abstract
. The classical Shimura correspondence lifts automorphic representations on the double cover of SL 2 \operatorname{SL}_{2} to automorphic representations on PGL 2 \operatorname{PGL}_{2} . Here we take key steps towards establishing a relative trace formula that would give a new global Shimura lift, from the triple cover of SL 3 \operatorname{SL}_{3} to PGL 3 \operatorname{PGL}_{3} , and also characterize the image of the lift. The characterization would be through the nonvanishing of a certain global period involving a function in the space of the automorphic minimal representation Θ SO 8 \Theta_{\operatorname{SO}_{8}} for split SO 8 ⁡ ( 𝔸 ) \operatorname{SO}_{8}(\mathbb{A}) , consistent with a 2001 conjecture of Bump, Friedberg and Ginzburg. In this paper, we first analyze a global distribution on PGL 3 ⁡ ( 𝔸 ) \operatorname{PGL}_{3}(\mathbb{A}) involving this period and show that it is a sum of factorizable orbital integrals. The same is true for the Kuznetsov distribution attached to the triple cover of SL 3 ⁡ ( 𝔸 ) \operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{A}) . We then match the corresponding local orbital integrals for the unit elements of the spherical Hecke algebras; that is, we establish the Fundamental Lemma.

1.
Introduction

The classical Shimura correspondence
[
Shi73
]
lifts automorphic representations on the double cover of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}
to automorphic representations on
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}
. Niwa
[
Niw75
]
(working classically) and
Waldspurger
[
Wal80
]
(working adelically) showed that the map may be obtained as a theta lifting. Waldspurger
also showed that the image of the lift is characterized by the nonvanishing of a certain period (the integral of an automorphic form over a cycle). Jacquet
[
Jac87
]
then used his relative trace formula,
which compares distributions on two different groups, one involving the relevant period, to give another proof of these facts.

Let
n\geq 2
and let
G
be a split connected reductive algebraic group over a global field
F
with a full
set of
n
-th roots of unity
\mu_{n}\subset F
. Let
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}_{F}
be the ring of adeles of
F
.
Then one may define an
n
-fold cover
\widetilde{G}^{(n)}(\mathbb{A})
of the adelic points of
G
, and it is natural to ask if there is an analogue of the Shimura map for
\widetilde{G}^{(n)}(\mathbb{A})
.
The local Shimura correspondence was investigated by Savin
[
Sav88
]
, who proved (in generality) an isomorphism of Iwahori Hecke algebras.
Based on this work, for Cartan type A one expects a global functorial lift from genuine cuspidal automorphic representations on
\widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}_{r}^{(n)}(\mathbb{A})
to automorphic
representations on
\operatorname{PGL}_{r}(\mathbb{A})
if
n
divides
r
, and a lift to automorphic representations on
\operatorname{SL}_{r}(\mathbb{A})
if
(n,r)=1
. (See also
Bump, Friedberg and Ginzburg
[
BFG01
]
, Section 1, for a discussion of this.) Such lifts have been studied since the 1980s, but progress in establishing a global Shimura
correspondence has been obtained only in the cases
n=2
or
r=2
.
In this paper we establish the Fundamental Lemma for a relative trace formula
that will give the global Shimura lift in the case
n=r=3
; moreover this project will
characterize the image of the lift by means of a period involving a function in the space of the automorphic minimal representation for
\operatorname{SO}_{8}(\mathbb{A})
, confirming a 2001 conjecture of Bump, Friedberg and Ginzburg.

To put this work on context, recall that
Ginzburg, Rallis and Soudry
[
GRS97a
]
observed that
\operatorname{SL}_{2}
and its 3-fold cover
\widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}^{(3)}
form a dual pair in the 3-fold cover of the exceptional group
G_{2}
, and used this to establish a
lifting of genuine cuspidal automorphic representations on
\widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}_{2}^{(3)}(\mathbb{A})
to automorphic representations on
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A})
and to determine its image.
Let
\text{Sym}^{3}:\operatorname{SL}_{2}\to\operatorname{Sp}_{4}
be the symmetric cube map, and
\Theta_{\operatorname{Sp}_{4}}
be the theta representation on the metaplectic double cover of
\operatorname{Sp}_{4}(\mathbb{A})
. This cover
splits on the image of
\text{Sym}^{3}
. Then they showed that an irreducible
cuspidal automorphic representation
\tau
of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A})
is in the image of the rank-one cubic Shimura map if and only if the period integral

(1)

\int_{\operatorname{SL}_{2}(F)\backslash\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A})}\varphi(g)\theta(\text{Sym}^{3}(g))\,dg

is nonzero for some
\varphi
in the space of
\tau
and some
\theta
in the space of
\Theta_{\operatorname{Sp}_{4}}
. Another proof of these results was given by Mao and Rallis
[
MR99
]
via a relative trace formula.
However, for higher degree covers or higher rank special linear groups there is no similar dual pair.

By comparing orbital integrals, Flicker
[
Fli80
]
succeeded in establishing
a Shimura-type correspondence for the
n
-fold cover of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A})
(there is more than one cover, and he treated a specific one); in this generality there is no known general characterization of the image by periods.
Kazhdan and Patterson
[
KP86
]
and Flicker and Kazhdan
[
FK86
]
studied these orbital integrals for higher rank general linear groups, but were not able to obtain sufficient control to establish a correspondence.
As for double covers, Mao
[
Mao98
]
established
the fundamental lemma for a relative trace formula for the double cover of
\operatorname{GL}_{3}
, and the case of the double cover of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}
has recently been treated by Do
by first establishing the equal characteristic case
[
Do15
]
and then using model theoretic methods to move to characteristic zero
[
Do20
]
.

Here we are concerned with the conjectured Shimura map from the cubic cover of
\operatorname{SL}_{3}
to
\operatorname{PGL}_{3}
.
The question of characterizing its image was considered by
Bump, Friedberg and Ginzburg
[
BFG01
]
.
Let
\Theta_{\operatorname{SO}_{8}}
be the
automorphic minimal representation on the split special orthogonal group
\operatorname{SO}_{8}(\mathbb{A})
. This representation
was constructed by Ginzburg, Rallis and Soudry
[
GRS97b
]
as a multi-residue of a Borel Eisenstein series on
\operatorname{SO}_{8}
. Let
\operatorname{Ad}
denote the adjoint representation

\operatorname{Ad}:\operatorname{PGL}_{3}\to\operatorname{SO}_{8}
(see Subsection
3.1
below).
Supposing that a Shimura lift exists in this case, they then conjectured

Conjecture 1.1

(Bump, Friedberg, Ginzburg)
.

Let
\pi
be an irreducible cuspidal automorphic representation of
\operatorname{PGL}_{3}(\mathbb{A})
. Then
\pi
is in the image of the cubic Shimura correspondence from

\widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}_{3}^{(3)}(\mathbb{A})
if and only if the period

(2)

\int_{\operatorname{PGL}_{3}(F)\backslash\operatorname{PGL}_{3}(\mathbb{A})}\varphi(g)\,\theta(\operatorname{Ad}(g))\,dg

is nonzero for some
\varphi
in the space of
\pi
and some
\theta
in
\Theta_{\operatorname{SO}_{8}}
.

Those authors presented two pieces of evidence for this conjecture, the first from finite fields, and the second by supposing
that
\pi
was not cuspidal but rather an Eisenstein series induced from cuspidal data
\tau
on
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A})
, formally unfolding
the period (
2
) in this case, and showing that the resulting integral is nonvanishing for some choice of data if and only if the period (
1
) for
\tau
is nonvanishing for some choice of data.

The study of periods in the context of Langlands type correspondences is a main theme of contemporary research; see for example
Sakellaridis and Venkatesh
[
SV17
]
. However, the extension to metaplectic covers is not well developed. In particular,
we do not know of an
extension of Conjecture
1.1
to higher rank special linear groups or higher degree covers.

Here we take key steps towards establishing the existence of the global Shimura
correspondence with
n=r=3
and towards characterizing its image as in Conjecture
1.1
above.
Our approach, following Jacquet, Mao and Rallis, and Mao, is to establish
a comparison of relative trace formulas.
For an algebraic group
H
defined over
F
we denote its automorphic quotient space by
[H]=H(F)\backslash H(\mathbb{A})
.
For an affine variety
X
defined over
F
denote by
\mathcal{S}(X(\mathbb{A}))
the space of Schwartz-Bruhat functions on
X(\mathbb{A})
.
The action of
f\in\mathcal{S}(H(\mathbb{A}))
on
L^{2}([H])

obtained by averaging the right regular representation

R(f)\phi(h)=\int_{H(\mathbb{A})}\phi(hy)f(y)\,dy,

is realized by the kernel function

\sum_{\gamma\in G(F)}f(h_{1}^{-1}\gamma h_{2}),\quad h_{1},h_{2}\in[H].

The relative trace formula compares two distributions obtained as
integrals of these kernel functions for different groups, one integral involving the period and a Whittaker character and the other involving solely
Whittaker characters.

Let
G=\operatorname{PGL}(3)
considered as an algebraic group defined over
F
and
N
the standard maximal unipotent subgroup of
G
realized as the
group of upper triangular
3\times 3
unipotent matrices. Fix a non-trivial character
\psi
of
F\backslash\mathbb{A}
.
By abuse of notation, we further denote by
\psi
the generic character
\psi(n)=\psi(n_{1,2}+n_{2,3})
of
[N]
.
For
\Theta\in\Theta_{\operatorname{SO}_{8}}
we consider the distribution
I(\Theta)
on
G(\mathbb{A})
defined by

(3)

I(f,\Theta)=\int_{{[N]}}\int_{[G]}\Big\{\sum_{\gamma\in G(F)}f(g^{-1}\gamma n)\Big\}\Theta(\operatorname{Ad}(g))\psi(n)\ dg\ dn,\ \ \ f\in\mathcal{S}(G(\mathbb{A})).

Let
G^{\prime}(\mathbb{A})
be the adelic
3
-fold metaplectic cover of
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{A})
. It is a central extension of
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{A})

satisfying the exact sequence

1\rightarrow\mu_{3}\rightarrow G^{\prime}(\mathbb{A})\rightarrow\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{A})\rightarrow 1.

(Since
G^{\prime}(\mathbb{A})
is not the adelic points of an algebraic group this is an abuse of notation.)
Denote by
\mathcal{S}(G^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}))
the space of genuine (that is,
\mu_{3}
-equivariant) Schwartz-Bruhat functions on
G^{\prime}(\mathbb{A})
.
There is a splitting of
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(F)
in
G^{\prime}(\mathbb{A})
and we denote by
G^{\prime}(F)
its image.
The group
N(\mathbb{A})
also splits in
G^{\prime}(\mathbb{A})
and we continue to denote by
N(\mathbb{A})
the image of this splitting.
The Kuznetsov trace formula is realized by the distribution on
G^{\prime}(\mathbb{A})
defined by

(4)

J(f^{\prime})=\int_{[N]}\int_{[N]}\Big\{\sum_{\gamma\in G^{\prime}(F)}f^{\prime}(n_{1}\gamma n_{2})\Big\}\psi(n_{1}n_{2})\ dn_{1}\ dn_{2},\ \ \ f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}(G^{\prime}(\mathbb{A})).

Our goal is to study a comparison between the two distributions
I(f,\Theta)
and
J(f^{\prime})
.

The first step is to
geometerize
the distribution
I(\Theta)
, that is, express it as a sum (over some geometric orbits) of distributions that are factorizable.
In most relative trace formulas this step is straightforward: one writes the sums using the Bruhat decomposition or some variant and unfolds to get a sum over double cosets of adelic
orbital integrals, with only
the relevant double cosets contributing. For factorizable test functions, each side is then factorizable.

The obstacle in our case is the automorphic minimal representation that appears in the distribution (
3
). In contrast to the rank one case, this
representation is not directly obtained from the Weil representation, so there is no easy way to express the functions in
\Theta_{\operatorname{SO}_{8}}
as sums that may then be unfolded.
To address this, we make use of the dual pair
(\operatorname{SL}_{2},\operatorname{SO}_{8})
inside
\operatorname{Sp}_{16}
, and realize the automorphic minimal representation
\Theta_{\operatorname{SO}_{8}}
as the
theta lift of the trivial representation from
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{A})

to
\operatorname{SO}_{8}(\mathbb{A})
. This realization is due to Ginzburg, Rallis and Soudry
[
GRS97b
, Theorem 6.9]
; their proof
relies on the work of Kudla and Rallis
[
KR94
]
(or in a classical language over
\mathbb{Q}
, the work of Deitmar and Krieg
[
DK91
]
).
When applied to the groups at hand, these works establish that the suitably regularized theta lift of an Eisenstein series on
\operatorname{SL}_{2}
is an Eisenstein series on
\operatorname{SO}_{8}
;
then taking a residue, one obtains the desired realization of
\Theta_{\operatorname{SO}_{8}}
. Since we are aiming for a factorizable integral, it is perhaps surprising that
it is helpful to introduce an additional integration over an automorphic quotient
[\operatorname{SL}_{2}]
. Nonetheless, it turns out that this
realization allows us to do an unfolding and to express the distribution

I(f,\Theta)
as a sum of factorizable orbital integrals.

An analogous geometric expansion for
J(f^{\prime})
follows directly from the Bruhat decomposition on
G^{\prime}

(and is well-known).
This allows us to establish a
one-to-one correspondence for the relevant orbits of the two distributions. To effect a global comparison,
we must then achieve a comparison of the corresponding local orbital integrals for each family of relevant orbits up to a transfer factor whose product
over all places is
1
.

The main local result of this paper is a matching of these orbital integrals for the unit element of the spherical Hecke algebra,
that is, the Fundamental Lemma for this relative trace formula.
The calculations are rather elaborate, but as in prior work on relative trace formulas that give Shimura correspondences, there is an algebraic fact that is key.
In Jacquet’s work on the Shimura correspondence, this was the computation of a certain Salié sum; this fact was also
key in Iwaniec’s work
[
Iwa87
]
on the same topic
with an eye towards analytic number theoretic applications. For the work of Mao and Rallis, key use was made of an identity relating a
cubic exponential sum to a Kloosterman
sum with a cubic character that is due to Duke and Iwaniec
[
DI93
]
(as these authors remark, the analogous fact at a real
archimedean place is Nicholson’s formula
for the Airy integral!). The proof of that result in turn is based on the Davenport-Hasse relation. Their result applies when the additive
character is of conductor 1, that is, to exponential
sums of the form

\sum_{x\bmod p}\exp\left(2\pi i\frac{ax^{3}+bx}{p}\right)

where
p
in our computation is the cardinality of the residue field. These sums are shown to be equal to certain Kloosterman sums modulo
p
with a cubic character.
Remarkably, we observe that a similar relation is true for exponential sums that involve additive characters of higher conductor (Corollary
7.5
).
For higher conductor, the proof
is based on the method of stationary phase.

In our work the orbital integrals for the big cell frequently reduce to integrals of pairs of Kloosterman integrals with cubic characters.
These integrals appear intractable. However,
using this identity twice to make
them into integrals of pairs of cubic exponential integrals, we are able to effect the desired comparison.
It also sometimes happens that we encounter Kloosterman integrals without a cubic character. In this case for conductor 1 the result of
Duke-Iwaniec is not applicable, but another identity, which
expresses the characteristic function of the local condition

(ab^{-1})^{3}\equiv-1\bmod\mathfrak{p}
in terms of a sum
of three cubic exponential integrals, is used instead (Lemma
7.6
).

We describe the contents of this paper. The first part of this paper gives the geometric expansions of the distributions we have described above.
Our first task is to express the distribution
I(f,\Theta)
as a sum of factorizable orbital integrals.
In Section
2
, we formulate a general unfolding principle and recall the definition of relevant orbits. Then, in Section
3
,
we use this principle and the theta lift to
\operatorname{SO}_{8}
to unfold the distribution
I(f,\Theta)

and to express it
as a sum of factorizable orbital integrals over relevant orbits (Proposition
3.2
). In the following Section
4
, we
work with flag varieties to
determine these relevant orbits explicitly (Theorem
4.2
) and also write the orbital integrals for each relevant orbit in a form amenable for computation.
We also need the geometric expansion for the Kuznetsov distribution
J(f^{\prime})
and this is presented in Section
5
.
This completes the reduction of the comparison of spectral sides of the global distributions to a comparison of a collection of explicitly given geometric local distributions.

The second part of this paper establishes the Fundamental Lemma. We begin with the big cell comparison, which is stated in Theorem
6.1
,
and whose proof occupies the following four sections. In Section
7
, we develop the necessary local ingredients, including the comparison
of cubic exponential and Kloosterman integrals, which is given (as a consequence of prior computations that are closely related to the method
of stationary phase) in Subsection
7.5
. The companion computation of cubic integrals when Duke-Iwaniec does not apply
is in Subsection
7.6
.
The orbital integral
J(a,b)
attached to the big cell is then evaluated in Section
8
. This is the piece of the calculation that
requires computations in the metaplectic group. Specifically, if
\mathcal{O}
denotes the local ring of integers then
the embedding of
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathcal{O})
in the local covering group is given by means of a section
that must be computed in order to work with the unit element in the corresponding Hecke algebra.
In doing so we make use of an algorithm for computing the local two-cocycle (defined by Matsumoto
[
Mat69
]
)
that is given in
Bump and Hoffstein
[
BH87
]
. This leads to the complicated orbital integrals involving pairs of Kloosterman
integrals that we then evaluate. The
orbital integral
I(a,b)
is evaluated in Section
9
. The comparison is then completed in the brief Section
10
.
The final section, Section
11
, matches the remaining orbital integrals with an explicit transfer factor. For the two one-parameter families
of relevant orbits, the
comparison once again requires replacing a Kloosterman integral by a cubic exponential integral, but this time only once.

Part I. Global Theory: The Geometric Expansions

2.
An unfolding principle

Let
F
be a global field,
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}_{F}
the ring of adeles of
F
,

X
an affine
F
-variety and
G
an affine algebraic
F
-group acting on
X
on the right.
We denote the action by
(x,g)\mapsto x^{g}
,
x\in X
,
g\in G
. Let
\delta_{G}
be the modular quasicharacter of
G(\mathbb{A})
and
[G]=G(F)\backslash G(\mathbb{A})
.

For
\xi\in X(F)
we denote by
G_{\xi}
the stabilizer of
\xi
in
G
, an affine algebraic group defined over
F
.
We say that a function
\mathfrak{c}:X(\mathbb{A})\times G(\mathbb{A})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is an
automorphic cocycle
if it satisfies the following two conditions:

•

\mathfrak{c}(\xi,\gamma)=1
,
\xi\in X(F)
,
\gamma\in G(F)
;

•

\mathfrak{c}(\xi,gh)=\mathfrak{c}(\xi,g)\mathfrak{c}(\xi^{g},h)
,
\xi\in X(F)
,
g,\,h\in G(\mathbb{A})
.

This implies that
g\mapsto\mathfrak{c}(\xi,g)
restricts to an automorphic character of
G_{\xi}(\mathbb{A})
for every
\xi\in X(F)
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{c}
is an automorphic cocycle such that for every
\xi\in X(F)

(5)

\int_{[G_{\xi}]}\left|{\mathfrak{c}(\xi,h)}\right|\delta_{G}(h)\delta_{G_{\xi}}(h)^{-1}\ dh<\infty.

Consequently,

(6)

\int_{[G_{\xi}]}\mathfrak{c}(\xi,h)\delta_{G}(h)\delta_{G_{\xi}}(h)^{-1}\ dh=\begin{cases}\operatorname{vol}([G_{\xi}])&(\mathfrak{c}(\xi,\cdot)\delta_{G})|_{G_{\xi}(\mathbb{A})}\equiv\delta_{G_{\xi}}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Remark 2.1
.

Note that if
[G_{\xi}]
is compact for every
\xi\in X(F)
then the assumption (
5
) clearly holds. If
G(\mathbb{A})
and
G_{\xi}(\mathbb{A})
are unimodular and
\mathfrak{c}(\xi,\cdot)|_{G_{\xi}(\mathbb{A})}
is a unitary character then for (
5
) to hold it is enough to assume that
[G_{\xi}]
is of finite volume.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that if
\mathfrak{c}:X(\mathbb{A})\times G(\mathbb{A})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is an automorphic cocycle and
\chi
is an automorphic character of
G(\mathbb{A})
then the function
\mathfrak{c}_{\chi}
defined by
\mathfrak{c}_{\chi}(x,g)=\chi(g)\mathfrak{c}(x,g)
is also an automorphic cocycle.

We say that
\xi\in X(F)
is
relevant
if
\mathfrak{c}(\xi,\cdot)\delta_{G}\delta_{G_{\xi}}^{-1}
is trivial on
G_{\xi}(\mathbb{A})
.
By our assumptions, for
\xi\in X(F)
,
\gamma\in G(F)
and
h\in G_{\xi}(\mathbb{A})
we have
\mathfrak{c}(\xi^{\gamma},\gamma^{-1}h\gamma)=\mathfrak{c}(\xi,h)
. It is also clear that
\delta_{G}(\gamma^{-1}h\gamma)=\delta_{G}(h)
and
\delta_{G_{\xi^{\gamma}}}(\gamma^{-1}h\gamma)=\delta_{G_{\xi}}(h)
. Therefore
\xi
is relevant if and only if
\xi^{\gamma}
is relevant. We denote by
(X/G)
a set of representatives for the
G(F)
-orbits in
X(F)
and by
(X/G)_{\operatorname{rel}}
the subset of relevant elements in
(X/G)
.
We note the following formal unfolding principle. At this generality we ignore convergence issues, hence the term principle. For
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(X(\mathbb{A}))
, formally, we have

(7)

\int_{[G]}\sum_{\xi\in X(F)}\phi(\xi^{g})\mathfrak{c}(\xi,g)\ dg=\sum_{\xi\in(X/G)_{\operatorname{rel}}}\operatorname{vol}([G_{\xi}])\int_{G_{\xi}(\mathbb{A})\backslash G(\mathbb{A})}\phi(\xi^{g})\mathfrak{c}(\xi,g)\ dg.

Indeed, let
(X/G)
be a set of representatives for the
G(F)
-orbits in
X(F)
. Then

\sum_{\xi\in X(F)}\phi(\xi^{g})\mathfrak{c}(\xi,g)=\sum_{\xi\in(X/G)}\sum_{\gamma\in G_{\xi}(F)\backslash G(F)}\phi(\xi^{\gamma g})\mathfrak{c}(\xi^{\gamma},g)=\sum_{\xi\in(X/G)}\sum_{\gamma\in G_{\xi}(F)\backslash G(F)}\phi(\xi^{\gamma g})\mathfrak{c}(\xi,\gamma g).

On the left hand side of (
7
) we exchange the order of integration over
[G]
and summation over
(X/G)
and then unfold
\gamma
with
g
to obtain

\sum_{\xi\in(X/G)}\int_{G_{\xi}(F)\backslash G(\mathbb{A})}\phi(\xi^{g})\mathfrak{c}(\xi,g)\ dg.

Integrating in stages this equals

\sum_{\xi\in(X/G)}\int_{G_{\xi}(\mathbb{A})\backslash G(\mathbb{A})}\phi(\xi^{g})\int_{[G_{\xi}]}\mathfrak{c}(\xi,hg)\delta_{G}(h)\delta_{G_{\xi}}(h)^{-1}\ dh\ dg=\\
\sum_{\xi\in(X/G)}\int_{G_{\xi}(\mathbb{A})\backslash G(\mathbb{A})}\phi(\xi^{g})\mathfrak{c}(\xi,g)\ dg\int_{[G_{\xi}]}\mathfrak{c}(\xi,h)\delta_{G}(h)\delta_{G_{\xi}}(h)^{-1}\ dh.

It follows from (
6
) that this equals the right hand side of (
7
).

3.
The geometrization of the distribution
I(f,\Theta)

Throughout the rest of Part I we suppose that the global field
F
contains a
primitive cube root of unity
\rho
(that is, there exits
\rho\in F
such that
\rho^{2}+\rho+1=0
), and we set

\mu_{3}=\langle\rho\rangle
. Our goal is to geometrize the distribution
I(f,\Theta)
given by (
3
).

3.1.
The Adjoint embedding

For
n\geq 2
let
w_{n}
denote the
n\times n
antidiagonal matrix
w_{n}=(\delta_{i,n+1-j})\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}
.
We realize the Adjoint representation of
\operatorname{PGL}(3)
as the map
\operatorname{PGL}(3)\to\operatorname{GL}({\mathfrak{sl}}(3))
defined by conjugation. Considering
{\mathfrak{sl}}(3)
as a quadratic
space with respect to the bilinear form
<x,y>=\text{Tr}(xy)
, the image lies in the special orthogonal group
\operatorname{SO}({\mathfrak{sl}}(3))
.
With respect to the basis

\{e_{1,3},e_{2,3},e_{1,2},e_{1,1}-e_{2,2},e_{2,2}-e_{3,3},e_{2,1},e_{3,2},e_{3,1}\}

of
{\mathfrak{sl}}(3)
where the
e_{i,j}
are the standard elementary matrices,
\operatorname{SO}({\mathfrak{sl}}(3))
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{SO}(J)
where

J=\begin{pmatrix}&&&w_{3}\\
&2&-1&\\
&-1&2&\\
w_{3}&&&\end{pmatrix}.

Since
F
contains the cube roots of unity, the group
\operatorname{SO}(J)
is split over
F
and may be conjugated to
\operatorname{SO}_{8}:=\operatorname{SO}(w_{8})
; indeed
if
g_{0}=-\tfrac{1}{3}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1-\rho&2+\rho\\
-(1+2\rho)&1+2\rho\end{smallmatrix}\right)
then

{}^{t}g_{0}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&-1\\
-1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)g_{0}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}&1\\
1&\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.
In this way we realize the adjoint map as a homomorphism
\operatorname{Ad}:\operatorname{PGL}(3)\rightarrow\operatorname{SO}_{8}
. This is the map that is used in the period (
2
).

3.3.
On the Weil representation

We realize the group

For a matrix

Lemma 3.1
.

The function

satisfies the cocycle condition

\mathfrak{c}(x,p_{1}p_{2})=\mathfrak{c}(x,p_{1})\mathfrak{c}(x^{p_{1}},p_{2}),\ \ \ x\in\mathbb{A}^{8},\,p_{1},\,p_{2}\in P^{\circ}(\mathbb{A}).

Write

7.
Ingredients needed for the comparison

We develop the ingredients that are needed to establish Theorem

. From now on we assume

.

7.1.
The cubic Hilbert symbol

We begin by recalling the basic properties of the cubic Hilbert symbol. They will be used throughout our computation without further mention.
For
x,\,y,\,z\in F^{*}
we have

•

(y,x)_{3}=\overline{(x,y)_{3}}

•

(xy,z)_{3}=(x,z)_{3}(y,z)_{3}

•

(x,1-x)_{3}=1
,
x\neq 1

•

(x,y)_{3}=1
for all
y\in F^{*}
if and only if
x\in F^{*3}

•

(x,u)_{3}=1
for all
u\in\mathcal{O}^{*}
if and only if
3|{\operatorname{val}}(x)
.

The last property is a consequence of our assumption that the residual characteristic
p>3
.
We further observe that by this assumption and Hensel’s Lemma
1+\mathfrak{p}\subseteq F^{*3}
and consequently

(x+y,z)_{3}=(x,z)_{3}\ \ \ \text{whenever}\ \ \ \left|{y}\right|<\left|{x}\right|.

7.2.
Cubic Gauss sums

For future reference we recall here a formula that is essentially the calculation of the absolute value squared of a cubic Gauss sum. This result is standard and the proof is omitted.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
a\in F
be such that
\left|{a}\right|=q
. Then

\int_{\mathcal{O}^{*}}\int_{\mathcal{O}^{*}}(a,uv^{-1})_{3}\,\psi(a(u+v))\ du\ dv=q^{-1}.

∎

7.3.
Kloosterman integrals

For
y\in F^{*}
and
a,b\in F
consider the integral

\mathcal{K}(y;a,b)=\int_{\mathcal{O}^{*}}(y,u)_{3}\,\psi(au+bu^{-1})\ du.

We provide a formula for
\mathcal{K}(y;a,b)
in terms of Kloosterman sums over the residual field, the function

\delta_{3\mid{\operatorname{val}}(x)}=\begin{cases}1&3\mid{\operatorname{val}}(x)\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases},\ \ \ x\in F^{*}

and the function
\mathfrak{s}
on
F^{*}
defined by

\mathfrak{s}(x)=\sum_{z\in\mathfrak{p}^{\ell-e}/\mathfrak{p}^{\ell}}\psi(xz^{2})

where
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
and
e\in\{0,1\}
are such that
\left|{x}\right|=q^{2\ell-e}
. Note that
\ell=\lfloor\frac{1-{\operatorname{val}}(x)}{2}\rfloor
and the summand
\psi(xz^{2})
is well defined (independent of the class of
z
mod
\mathfrak{p}^{\ell}
). In particular,
\mathfrak{s}(x)=1
if
{\operatorname{val}}(x)
is even.
It is easy to verify the basic property of
\mathfrak{s}
:

(17)

\mathfrak{s}(xy^{2})=\mathfrak{s}(x),\ \ \ x,y\in F^{*}.

In the evaluation below and subsequent formulas, if
x\in F^{*}
is a square then we write
\sqrt{x}
for a square root of
x
in
F
; all formulas will be independent of the choice of square root.

Lemma 7.2
.

We have

\mathcal{K}(y;a,b)=\begin{cases}(1-q^{-1})\delta_{3\mid{\operatorname{val}}(y)}&\max(\left|{a}\right|,\left|{b}\right|)\leq 1\\
q^{-1}\sum_{u\in k_{F}^{*}}(y,u)_{3}\,\psi(au+bu^{-1})&\max(\left|{a}\right|,\left|{b}\right|)=q\\
q^{-\lfloor\frac{1-{\operatorname{val}}(a)}{2}\rfloor}(y,ab^{-1})_{3}\,\sum_{\epsilon=\pm 1}\psi(2\epsilon\sqrt{ab})\mathfrak{s}(\epsilon\sqrt{ab})&\max(\left|{a}\right|,\left|{b}\right|)>q\text{ and }a^{-1}b\in\mathcal{O}^{*2}\\
0&\max(\left|{a}\right|,\left|{b}\right|)>q\text{ and }a^{-1}b\not\in\mathcal{O}^{*2}.\end{cases}

Proof.

If
\max(\left|{a}\right|,\left|{b}\right|)\leq q
then the integrand
(y,u)_{3}\psi(au+bu^{-1})
depends only on
u+\mathfrak{p}
and therefore
\mathcal{K}(y;a,b)=q^{-1}\sum_{u\in k_{F}^{*}}(y,u)_{3}\psi(au+bu^{-1})
. If in addition
a,b\in\mathcal{O}
then
\psi(au+bu^{-1})=1
for all
u\in k_{F}^{*}
. This explains the formula when
\max(\left|{a}\right|,\left|{b}\right|)\leq q
.

Assume now that
\max(\left|{a}\right|,\left|{b}\right|)\geq q^{2}
and let
\ell\geq 1
and
e\in\{0,1\}
be such that
\max(\left|{a}\right|,\left|{b}\right|)=q^{2\ell-e}
. We decompose the integral by writing
u=v(1+z)
with
v\in\mathcal{O}^{*}/(1+\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})
and
z\in\mathfrak{p}^{\ell}
. Since
(1+z)^{-1}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-z)^{i}\in 1-z+\mathfrak{p}^{2\ell}
and
bv^{-1}\mathfrak{p}^{2\ell}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
we conclude that

\mathcal{K}(y;a,b)=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{O}^{*}/(1+\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})}(y,v)_{3}\psi(av+bv^{-1})\int_{\mathfrak{p}^{\ell}}\psi((av-bv^{-1})z)\ dz.

We have

\int_{\mathfrak{p}^{\ell}}\psi((av-bv^{-1})z)\ dz=\begin{cases}q^{-\ell}&av-bv^{-1}\in\mathfrak{p}^{-\ell}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Note that
av-bv^{-1}\in\mathfrak{p}^{-\ell}
if and only if
\varpi^{2\ell-e}(av^{2}-b)\in\mathfrak{p}^{\ell-e}
and by assumption
\ell-e\geq 1
.
By Hensel’s lemma, there exists
v\in\mathcal{O}^{*}
such that
av-bv^{-1}\in\mathfrak{p}^{-\ell}
if and only if
a^{-1}b\in\mathcal{O}^{*2}
and in this case
v
is of this form if and only if
v\in\pm v_{0}+\mathfrak{p}^{\ell-e}
where
v_{0}^{2}=a^{-1}b
.
Note that for
v=\pm v_{0}(1+z)
with
z\in\mathfrak{p}^{\ell-e}
we have

(y,v)_{3}=(y,\pm v_{0})_{3}=(y,v_{0}^{-2})_{3}=(y,ab^{-1})_{3},

av+bv^{-1}\in\pm(av_{0}+bv_{0}^{-1}+(av_{0}-bv_{0}^{-1})z+bv_{0}^{-1}z^{2})+b\mathfrak{p}^{3(\ell-e)}

and by assumption
av_{0}-bv_{0}^{-1}=0
and
b\mathfrak{p}^{3(\ell-e)}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
so that

\psi(av+bv^{-1})=\psi(\pm(av_{0}+bv_{0}^{-1})+bv_{0}^{-1}z^{2}).

Note further that
av_{0}+bv_{0}^{-1}=2av_{0}=2bv_{0}^{-1}
and that
bv_{0}^{-1}
is a square root of

. The lemma readily follows.
∎

We remark that the analysis above of the case

is tantamount to an evaluation of the sum using the method of stationary phase.

7.4.

For
a,b\in F
consider the integrals

\mathcal{C}(a,b)=\int_{\mathcal{O}}\psi(ax+bx^{3})\ dx\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \mathcal{C}_{0}(a,b)=\int_{\mathcal{O}^{*}}\psi(au+bu^{3})\ du.

Lemma 7.3
.

We have

\mathcal{C}(a,b)=\begin{cases}1&\max(\left|{a}\right|,\left|{b}\right|)\leq 1\\
0&\left|{b}\right|<\left|{a}\right|=q\\
q^{-1}\sum_{x\in k_{F}}\psi(ax+bx^{3})&\left|{a}\right|\leq\left|{b}\right|=q\\
0&\max(\left|{b}\right|,q)<\left|{a}\right|\\
q^{-\lfloor\frac{1-{\operatorname{val}}(a)}{2}\rfloor}\sum_{\epsilon=\pm 1}\psi(\epsilon\frac{2}{9}a\sqrt{-3ab^{-1}})\mathfrak{s}(\epsilon\frac{1}{9}a\sqrt{-3ab^{-1}})&\left|{a}\right|=\left|{b}\right|>q\text{ and }ab^{-1}\in\mathcal{O}^{*2}\\
0&\left|{a}\right|=\left|{b}\right|>q\text{ and }ab^{-1}\not\in\mathcal{O}^{*2}\end{cases}

and

\mathcal{C}_{0}(a,b)=\begin{cases}1-q^{-1}&\max(\left|{a}\right|,\left|{b}\right|)\leq 1\\
-q^{-1}&\left|{b}\right|<\left|{a}\right|=q\\
q^{-1}\sum_{u\in k_{F}^{*}}\psi(au+bu^{3})&\left|{a}\right|\leq\left|{b}\right|=q\\
q^{-\lfloor\frac{1-{\operatorname{val}}(a)}{2}\rfloor}\sum_{\epsilon=\pm 1}\psi(\epsilon\frac{2}{9}a\sqrt{-3ab^{-1}})\mathfrak{s}(\epsilon\frac{1}{9}a\sqrt{-3ab^{-1}})&\max(\left|{a}\right|,\left|{b}\right|)>q\text{ and }ab^{-1}\in\mathcal{O}^{*2}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

In particular,

\mathcal{C}(a,b)=\begin{cases}q^{-1}+\mathcal{C}_{0}(a,b)&\max(\left|{a}\right|,\left|{b}\right|)\leq q\\
\mathcal{C}_{0}(a,b)&\max(\left|{b}\right|,q^{2})\leq\left|{a}\right|.\end{cases}

Remark 7.4
.

The lemma excludes the computation of
\mathcal{C}(a,b)
when
\max(\left|{a}\right|,q)<\left|{b}\right|
. In fact, in this case we have

\mathcal{C}(a,b)=\begin{cases}q^{-\ell}\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{p}^{\lfloor\frac{\ell+1-e}{2}\rfloor}/\mathfrak{p}^{\ell}}\psi(ax+bx^{3})&\left|{a}\right|\leq q^{\ell}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

where
\ell\geq 1
and
e\in\{0,1\}
are such that
\left|{b}\right|=q^{2\ell-e}
.
However, we omit the proof for this case since it is never used in our work.

Proof.

The two formulas are straightforward when
\max(\left|{a}\right|,\left|{b}\right|)\leq q
. Assume now that
\max(\left|{b}\right|,q^{2})\leq\left|{a}\right|
and let
\ell\geq 1
and
e\in\{0,1\}
be such that
\left|{a}\right|=q^{2\ell-e}
.
Since
b\mathfrak{p}^{2\ell}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
, for
y\in\mathcal{O}
we have

\int_{y+\mathfrak{p}^{\ell}}\psi(ax+bx^{3})\ dx=\psi(ay+by^{3})\int_{\mathfrak{p}^{\ell}}\psi((a+3by^{2})z)\ dz=\begin{cases}q^{-\ell}\psi(ay+by^{3})&a+3by^{2}\in\mathfrak{p}^{-\ell}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

It follows that

\mathcal{C}(a,b)=q^{-\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{\ell}\\
a+3bx^{2}\in\mathfrak{p}^{-\ell}\end{subarray}}\psi(ax+bx^{3})\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \mathcal{C}_{0}(a,b)=q^{-\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\mathcal{O}^{*}/(1+\mathfrak{p}^{\ell})\\
a+3bu^{2}\in\mathfrak{p}^{-\ell}\end{subarray}}\psi(au+bu^{3}).

Note that for
y\in\mathcal{O}
we have
a+3by^{2}\in\mathfrak{p}^{-\ell}
if and only if
\varpi^{2\ell-e}(a+3by^{2})\in\mathfrak{p}^{\ell-e}
and that by assumption
\mathfrak{p}^{\ell-e}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
while
\varpi^{2\ell-e}a\in\mathcal{O}^{*}
. Consequently, if
y\in\mathcal{O}
is such that
a+3by^{2}\in\mathfrak{p}^{-\ell}
then
|a|=|b|
and
y\in\mathcal{O}^{*}
. It follows that indeed in this case
\mathcal{C}(a,b)=\mathcal{C}_{0}(a,b)
.
Note that
-3=(1+2\rho)^{2}\in\mathcal{O}^{*}
. It further follows from Hensel’s lemma that there exists
y\in\mathcal{O}^{*}
such that
a+3by^{2}\in\mathfrak{p}^{-\ell}
if and only if
ab^{-1}\in\mathcal{O}^{*2}
.
The vanishing of
\mathcal{C}(a,b)
when
\max(\left|{b}\right|,q)<\left|{a}\right|
and when
\left|{a}\right|=\left|{b}\right|>q
and
ab^{-1}\not\in\mathcal{O}^{*2}
follows.

Assume now that
\left|{a}\right|=\left|{b}\right|>q
and
ab^{-1}\in\mathcal{O}^{*2}
and let

v_{0}\in\mathcal{O}^{*}
be such that
v_{0}^{2}=-3ab^{-1}
. Then for
y\in\mathcal{O}
we have
a+3by^{2}\in\mathfrak{p}^{-\ell}
if and only if
y\in\pm\frac{1}{3}v_{0}+\mathfrak{p}^{\ell-e}
.

Since
b\mathfrak{p}^{3(\ell-e)}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
, for
z\in\mathfrak{p}^{\ell-e}
and
x=\pm(\frac{1}{3}v_{0}+z)
we have

ax+bx^{3}\in\pm(\frac{2}{9}av_{0}+bv_{0}z^{2})+\mathcal{O}.

Consequently,

\mathcal{C}(a,b)=q^{-\ell}\sum_{\epsilon=\pm 1}\psi(\epsilon\frac{2}{9}av_{0})\mathfrak{s}(\epsilon bv_{0}).

Since by assumption
\frac{1}{9}ab^{-1}\in F^{*2}
it follows from (
17
) that
\mathfrak{s}(\epsilon bv_{0})=\mathfrak{s}(\epsilon\frac{1}{9}av_{0})
and the lemma follows.
∎

Once again, the proof above may be regarded as a use of the method of stationary phase.

For future reference, for
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
let

\mathcal{C}_{\ell}(a,b)=\int_{{\operatorname{val}}(x)=\ell}\psi(ax+bx^{3})\ dx.

The change of variables
x\mapsto tx
shows that

(18)

\mathcal{C}_{\ell}(a,b)=\left|{t}\right|\mathcal{C}_{\ell-{\operatorname{val}}(t)}(ta,t^{3}b),\ \ \ t\in F^{*}.

As an immediate consequence of Lemma
7.3
we record here that

(19)

\mathcal{C}_{\ell}(a,b)=0\ \ \ \text{whenever}\ \ \ \max(q^{-\ell}\left|{a}\right|,q^{-3\ell}\left|{b}\right|)>q\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \left|{a^{-1}b}\right|\neq q^{2\ell}.

7.5.
The comparison of cubic exponential integrals and Kloosterman integrals

Duke and Iwaniec
[
DI93
]
established the following identity between cubic exponential and Kloosterman sums.
Let
\chi
be an order three character of
k_{F}^{*}
(
\chi
and
\chi^{-1}
are the only such characters) and
\xi
a non-trivial character of
k_{F}
. Then

(20)

\sum_{x\in k_{F}}\xi(x+ax^{3})=\chi(a)^{-1}\sum_{u\in k_{F}^{*}}\chi(u)\xi(u-3^{-3}a^{-1}u^{-1}).

This identity, together with the work above which may be regarded as an application of the method of stationary phase, gives
the following key comparison of cubic exponential and Kloosterman sums.

Corollary 7.5
.

We have

\mathcal{C}(a,-3^{-3}c^{-1}d^{-1}a^{3})=(t,c^{-1}d)_{3}\mathcal{K}(t;c,d)

whenever

•

either
\left|{a}\right|=\left|{c}\right|=\left|{d}\right|=q
and
3\nmid{\operatorname{val}}(t)

•

or
\left|{a}\right|=\left|{c}\right|=\left|{d}\right|>q
.

Proof.

If
\left|{a}\right|=\left|{c}\right|=\left|{d}\right|=q
then

\mathcal{C}(a,-3^{-3}c^{-1}d^{-1}a^{3})=q^{-1}\sum_{x\in k_{F}}\psi(ax-3^{-3}c^{-1}d^{-1}a^{3}x^{3})

while

\mathcal{K}(t;c,d)=q^{-1}\sum_{u\in k_{F}^{*}}(t,u)_{3}\psi(cu+du^{-1}).

If in addition
3\nmid{\operatorname{val}}(t)
then the character
(t,\cdot)_{3}
is not trivial on
\mathcal{O}^{*}
and we apply (
20
) with

\xi(x+\mathfrak{p})=\psi(ax)\ \ \ x\in\mathcal{O}\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \chi(u+\mathfrak{p})=(t,u)_{3},\ \ \ u\in\mathcal{O}^{*}

to obtain

\mathcal{C}(a,-3^{-3}c^{-1}d^{-1}a^{3})=q^{-1}(t,-3^{3}cda^{-2})_{3}\sum_{u\in k_{F}^{*}}(t,u)_{3}\xi(u+cda^{-2}u^{-1}).

Note that

(t,-3^{3}cda^{-2})_{3}(t,a^{-1}c)_{3}=(t,c^{-1}d)_{3}

and therefore the change of variables
u\mapsto a^{-1}cu
proves the desired identity.

If
\left|{a}\right|=\left|{c}\right|=\left|{d}\right|>q
the lemma follows by comparing the formulas in Lemmas
7.2
and
7.3
.
∎

7.6.
An additional computation involving cubic exponential integrals

We present one additional computation involving a cubic exponential integral.
We will use this evaluation when an orbital integral involves Kloosterman integrals without a cubic character – that is, integrals
\mathcal{K}(t;c,d)
with

\left|{c}\right|=\left|{d}\right|=q
but with
3\mid{\operatorname{val}}(t)
–
and so the result of Duke-Iwaniec is not applicable.

Lemma 7.6
.

For
\left|{a}\right|=\left|{b}\right|\leq q^{-3}
we have

3+\left|{a}\right|^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{2}\mathcal{C}_{{\operatorname{val}}(a)-1}(b^{-1}+a^{-1}\rho^{k},-3^{-3}a^{-1}b^{-1})=\begin{cases}q&-(ab^{-1})^{3}\in 1+\mathfrak{p}\\
0&-(ab^{-1})^{3}\not\in 1+\mathfrak{p}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Note that by (
18
) with
t=\varpi^{-1}a
we have

\left|{a}\right|^{-1}\mathcal{C}_{{\operatorname{val}}(a)-1}(b^{-1}+a^{-1}\rho^{k},-3^{-3}a^{-1}b^{-1})=q\,\mathcal{C}_{0}(\varpi^{-1}(ab^{-1}+\rho^{k}),-3^{-3}\varpi^{-3}a^{2}b^{-1})

and by assumption
-3^{-3}\varpi^{-3}a^{2}b^{-1}\in\mathcal{O}
so that by Lemma
7.3
we have

q\,\mathcal{C}_{0}(\varpi^{-1}(ab^{-1}+\rho^{k}),-3^{-3}\varpi^{-3}a^{2}b^{-1})=\begin{cases}q-1&-ab^{-1}\in\rho^{k}+\mathfrak{p}\\
-1&-ab^{-1}\not\in\rho^{k}+\mathfrak{p}.\end{cases}

Since
-ab^{-1}\in\rho^{k}+\mathfrak{p}
for at most one
k\in\{0,1,2\}
and this is the case if and only if
(-ab^{-1})^{3}\in 1+\mathfrak{p}
the lemma follows.
∎

8.
The integral
J(a,b)

In this section we establish the following formula for
J(a,b)
.

Proposition 8.1
.

For
\left|{b}\right|\leq\left|{a}\right|
we have

(a,b)_{3}\,J(a,b)=

\begin{cases}1&\left|{a}\right|=\left|{b}\right|=1\\
2q&\left|{a}\right|=\left|{b}\right|=q^{-1}\\
3\left|{a}\right|^{-1}+\left|{ab}\right|^{-1}\sum_{\ell=\lfloor\frac{{\operatorname{val}}(a)+1}{2}\rfloor}^{{\operatorname{val}}(a)-1}\sum_{k=0}^{2}\mathcal{C}_{\ell}(b^{-1}+\rho^{k}a^{-1},-3^{-3}a^{-1}b^{-1})&\left|{a}\right|=\left|{b}\right|\leq q^{-2}\\
\left|{b}\right|^{-1}\mathcal{C}(b^{-1},-3^{-3}a^{-1}b^{-1})&\left|{b}\right|<\left|{a}\right|=1\\
\left|{ab}\right|^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{2}\mathcal{C}_{\frac{{\operatorname{val}}(a)}{2}}(b^{-1}+\rho^{k}a^{-1},-3^{-3}a^{-1}b^{-1})&\left|{b}\right|<\left|{a}\right|<1\text{ and }2|{\operatorname{val}}(a)\\
0&\left|{b}\right|<\left|{a}\right|<1\text{ and }2\nmid{\operatorname{val}}(a)\\
0&\left|{a}\right|>1.\end{cases}

Furthermore,

(21)

J(b,a)=\overline{J(a,b)},\ \ \ a,\,b\in F^{*}.

8.1.
Fixing coordinates

By definition

J(a,b)=\int_{A[a,b]}\kappa(u_{1}g_{a,b}u_{2})^{-1}\psi(u_{1}u_{2})\ du_{1}\ du_{2}

where

A[a,b]=\{(u_{1},u_{2})\in N\times N:u_{1}g_{a,b}u_{2}\in K\}.

We fix coordinates on
N\times N
as follows.
In our computation we set

u_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}1&x_{i}&z_{i}\\
&1&y_{i}\\
&&1\end{pmatrix}\in N,\ \ \ i=1,2

so that

(22)

u_{1}g_{a,b}u_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}az_{1}&az_{1}x_{2}-a^{-1}bx_{1}&az_{1}z_{2}-a^{-1}bx_{1}y_{2}+b^{-1}\\
ay_{1}&ay_{1}x_{2}-a^{-1}b&ay_{1}z_{2}-a^{-1}by_{2}\\
a&ax_{2}&az_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

We also recall that an element
g=(g_{i,j})\in\operatorname{SL}_{3}(F)
satisfies
g\in K
if and only if
g_{i,j}\in\mathcal{O}
for all
1\leq i,\,j\leq 3
.

8.2.
A functional equation

We observe the following properties on the big Bruhat cell.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
u_{1},u_{2}\in N
,
a,b\in F^{*}
and
g=u_{1}g_{a,b}u_{2}
. Then

•

\sigma(g,g^{-1})=1
.

•

Consequently, if in addition
g\in K
then
\kappa(g^{-1})=\kappa(g)^{-1}=\overline{\kappa(g)}
.

Proof.

The second part is immediate from the first since

1=\kappa(I_{3})=\kappa(gg^{-1})=\kappa(g)\kappa(g^{-1})\sigma(g,g^{-1}).

For the first part note that
g_{a,b}^{-1}=g_{b,a}
and

\sigma(g,g^{-1})=\sigma(g_{a,b},g_{b,a})=1

by the explicit formulas for
\sigma
on monomial matrices in
[
BH87
, §2]
.
∎

The following consequence of the lemma reduces the computation of
J
to the case
\left|{b}\right|\leq\left|{a}\right|
.

Corollary 8.3
.

We have

J(b,a)=\overline{J(a,b)},\ \ \ a,\,b\in F^{*}

Proof.

Since
g_{a,b}^{-1}=g_{b,a}
the map
(u_{1},u_{2})\mapsto(u_{2}^{-1},u_{1}^{-1})
maps
A[a,b]
bijectively to
A[b,a]
. Since
\psi(u_{2}^{-1}u_{1}^{-1})=\overline{\psi(u_{1}u_{2})}
the Corollary is now immediate from Lemma
8.2
.
∎

8.3.
Computation of
\kappa

We provide a formula for
\kappa(g)
for almost all
g
in the intersection of
K
with the big Bruhat cell (outside a measure zero set).
Throughout the computation we freely use coordinates of
g
as in (
22
).

The fact that
g\mapsto(g,\kappa(g))
is a splitting of
K
in
\widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}_{3}(F)
is expressed by the equality

\kappa(g_{1}g_{2})=\kappa(g_{1})\kappa(g_{2})\sigma(g_{1},g_{2}),\ \ \ g_{1},\,g_{2}\in K.

Our computation uses properties of
\kappa
which we recall below and an algorithm of Bump and Hoffstein for computing
\sigma(g_{1},g_{2})
. The algorithm is based on an explicit Bruhat decomposition of
g_{1}
and it will be more convenient to write such a decomposition with factors in
\operatorname{GL}_{3}(F)
rather than in
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(F)
.
For this reason we consider
\widetilde{\operatorname{SL}}_{3}(F)
as a subgroup of the cubic cover
\widetilde{\operatorname{GL}}_{3}(F)
of
\operatorname{GL}_{3}(F)
constructed in Bump and Hoffstein
[
BH87
]
(following Matsumoto). We then freely use the algorithm for the computation of the two-cocycle
\sigma
in
[
BH87
, §2]
. In particular, in terms of the Bruhat decomposition, for
h\in\operatorname{GL}_{3}(F)
we denote by
R(h)
the unique
monomial (i.e., scaled permutation) matrix such that
h\in NR(h)N
. Furthermore, we continue to denote by
\kappa
an extension to a splitting of
\operatorname{GL}_{3}(\mathcal{O})
in
\widetilde{\operatorname{GL}}_{3}(F)
and freely use the following properties of
\kappa
(see
[
KP84
, §1]
):

•

\kappa
is trivial on signed permutation matrices and on upper-triangular matrices in
\operatorname{GL}_{3}(\mathcal{O})
,

•

\kappa(\operatorname{diag}(g,1))=\kappa(\operatorname{diag}(1,g))=\kappa_{2}(g)
,
g\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})
where

\kappa_{2}(g)=\begin{cases}(c,d\det g^{-1})_{3}&0<\left|{c}\right|<1\\
1&\text{otherwise}\end{cases},\ \ \ g=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}).

Lemma 8.4
.

Let

g=\begin{pmatrix}0&a&b\\
u&0&0\\
x&c&d\end{pmatrix}\in K

with
x,\,c\neq 0
. Then

\kappa(g)=(u,x)_{3}\kappa_{2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\begin{cases}(u,x)_{3}&\left|{c}\right|=1\\
(c,d)_{3}(u,xc^{-1})_{3}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Note that
g=ksh
with

k=\begin{pmatrix}1&&\\
&1&\\
&u^{-1}x&1\end{pmatrix},\ \ \ s=\begin{pmatrix}&1&\\
1&&\\
&&1\end{pmatrix},\ \ \ h=\begin{pmatrix}u&0&0\\
0&a&b\\
0&c&d\end{pmatrix}\in K

and therefore
\kappa(g)=\kappa(ks)\kappa(h)\sigma(ks,h)
.
Clearly,
\kappa(h)=\kappa_{2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
\kappa(ks)=\sigma(k,s)
.
We compute that
\sigma(k,s)=1
and
\sigma(ks,h)=(u,x)_{3}
using the algorithm in
[
BH87
, §2]
.
∎

Lemma 8.5
.

Let

g=\begin{pmatrix}a&0&b\\
c&0&d\\
x&u&0\end{pmatrix}\in K

with
x,\,c\neq 0
. Then

\kappa(g)=\kappa_{2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)(c,u^{-1}x)_{3}=\begin{cases}(c,dx)_{3}&\left|{c}\right|<1\\
(c,x)_{3}&\left|{c}\right|=1.\end{cases}

Proof.

Note that
g=hsk
with

h=\begin{pmatrix}a&b&0\\
c&d&0\\
0&0&u\end{pmatrix},\ \ \ s=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&0&1\\
0&1&0\end{pmatrix},\ \ \ k=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
u^{-1}x&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\in K

and therefore
\kappa(g)=\kappa(h)\kappa(sk)\sigma(h,sk)
.
Clearly,
\kappa(h)=\kappa_{2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
\kappa(sk)=\sigma(s,k)
.
We compute that
\sigma(s,k)=1
and
\sigma(h,sk)=(c,u^{-1}x)_{3}
using the algorithm in
[
BH87
, §2]
.
∎

Lemma 8.6
.

Let

g=\begin{pmatrix}a&0&b\\

with

\kappa(g)=(x,c)_{3}(c-u^{-1}xy,u^{-1}x)_{3}\kappa_{2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{a}&{b}\\

Note first that
g=sk

0&1&0\end{pmatrix},\ \ \ k=\begin{pmatrix}a&0&b\\

so that
\kappa(g)=\kappa(s)\kappa(k)\sigma(s,k)

[
BH87

.
In order to compute

8.4.

8.4.1.
The case
\max(\left|{a}\right|,\left|{b}\right|)>1

Lemma 8.8
.

Plugging this into the formula in Lemma

(a,b)_{3}\,\mathcal{J}_{\ell}(a,b)=\left|{a}\right|^{-2}\int_{\left|{y_{2}}\right|=q^{-2\ell}\left|{b}\right|^{-1},\,\left|{1-y_{2}}\right|=\left|{ab^{-1}}\right|}\mathcal{C}(\varpi^{\ell}b^{-1},3^{-3}\varpi^{3\ell}a^{-1}b^{-1}(y_{2}^{-1}-1))\\

Note that if
\left|{b}\right|<\left|{a}\right|<1

2\ell={\operatorname{val}}(a)

The domain of integration over

If either

•

\left|{b}\right|<\left|{a}\right|

\int_{\left|{y_{2}}\right|=q^{-2\ell}\left|{b}\right|^{-1}}\psi(ty_{2}^{-1})\ dy_{2}=\begin{cases}q^{-2\ell}\left|{b}\right|^{-1}(1-q^{-1})&t\in b^{-1}\mathfrak{p}^{2\ell}\\

In this section we provide the following formula for

.

Proposition 9.1
.

For
\left|{b}\right|\leq\left|{a}\right|
we have

I(a,b)=\begin{cases}1&\left|{a}\right|=\left|{b}\right|=1\\
2q&\left|{a}\right|=\left|{b}\right|=q^{-1}\\
3\left|{a}\right|^{-1}+\left|{ab}\right|^{-1}\sum_{\ell=\lfloor\frac{{\operatorname{val}}(a)+1}{2}\rfloor}^{{\operatorname{val}}(a)-1}\sum_{k=0}^{2}\mathcal{C}_{\ell}(b^{-1}-\rho^{k}a^{-1},2a^{-1}b^{-1})&\left|{a}\right|=\left|{b}\right|\leq q^{-2}\\

Furthermore,

(35)

I(b,a)=\overline{I(-a,-b)},\ \ \ a,\,b\in F^{*}.

9.1.
A first simplification and functional equation

In this section we show that
I(a,b)
satisfies a functional equation that allows us to reduce the computation to the case
\left|{b}\right|\leq\left|{a}\right|
.

9.1.1.

We begin by observing some basic properties of the integral
I(a,b)
.

Lemma 9.2
.

We have the following properties of
I(a,b)
.

(1)

The integral
I(a,b)
takes real values, that is,

\overline{I(a,b)}=I(a,b).

(2)

I(a,b)=0
unless
a,\,b\in\mathcal{O}
.

(3)

For
a,\,b\in\mathcal{O}
we have

(36)

I(a,b)=\sum_{j=0}^{\min({\operatorname{val}}(a),{\operatorname{val}}(b))}I(j;a,b)

where

I(j;a,b)=q^{-2j}\int\psi[x+y+2a\rho^{2}xy^{2}-(2\rho bx+2\rho^{2}ay)z]\ ds\ dx\ dy\ dz

and the integral is over
x,y,z,s\in F
such that

x+as,\,y-bs,\,(xb+y\rho^{2}a)s-(xy+z\rho)\in\mathfrak{p}^{-j},\ xb+y\rho^{2}a\in\mathfrak{p}^{j}.

Proof.

Changing order of integration, it follows from the definition (
15
) that

I(a,b)=\int_{\max(\left|{a}\right|,\left|{b}\right|)\leq\left|{t}\right|\leq 1}\left|{t}\right|^{2}\left[\int\psi[x+y+2a(xy-z)y\rho^{2}-2bxz\rho]\ ds\ dx\ dy\ dz\right]\ d^{*}t

where the inner integral is over
x,y,z,s\in F
such that

t(x+as),t(bs-y),t^{-1}(xb+y\rho^{2}a),t[(xb+y\rho^{2}a)s-(xy+z\rho)])\in\mathcal{O}.

The second part immediately follows.
Furthermore, the change of variables

(x,y,s)\mapsto-(x,y,s)

preserves the domain of integration and transforms the integrand into its conjugate. The first part follows.

Assume that
a,b\in\mathcal{O}
. The inner integral over
x,\,y,\,z,\,s
depends only on
\left|{t}\right|
and the third part of the lemma also follows, observing that
I(j;a,b)
is the integral over the part of the domain where
\left|{t}\right|=q^{-j}
.
∎

9.1.2.

In the next lemma, a variable change in
z
simplifies the domain of integration and allows us to integrate over
z
and simplify the expression for
I(j;a,b)
. We then observe that the resulting expression satisfies a symmetry between
a
and
b
.

Lemma 9.3
.

For
a,\,b\in\mathcal{O}
and
0\leq j\leq\min({\operatorname{val}}(a),{\operatorname{val}}(b))
we have

(37)

I(j;a,b)=q^{-j}\int\psi[x+y+2\left(bx^{2}y-axy^{2}+(abxy-b^{2}x^{2}-a^{2}y^{2})s\right)]\ ds\ dx\ dy

where the integral is over
x,y,s\in F
such that

x+as,\,y-bs\in\mathfrak{p}^{-j},\ bx,\,ay\in\mathfrak{p}^{j}.

Consequently

(38)

I(a,b)=I(-b,-a),\ \ \ a,\,b\in F^{*}.

Proof.

After the variable change

z\mapsto z+b\rho^{2}xs+a\rho ys-\rho^{2}xy

we have

I(j;a,b)=q^{-2j}\int\psi[x+y+2a\rho^{2}xy^{2}-(2\rho bx+2\rho^{2}ay)(z+b\rho^{2}xs+a\rho ys-\rho^{2}xy)]\ ds\ dx\ dy\ dz

where the integral is over
x,y,z,s\in F
such that

x+as,\,y-bs,\,z\in\mathfrak{p}^{-j},\ xb+y\rho^{2}a\in\mathfrak{p}^{j}.

After integrating over
z
this becomes

q^{-j}\int\psi[x+y+2a\rho^{2}xy^{2}-(2\rho bx+2\rho^{2}ay)(b\rho^{2}xs+a\rho ys-\rho^{2}xy)]\ ds\ dx\ dy=

q^{-j}\int\psi[x+y+2\left(bx^{2}y-axy^{2}+(abxy-b^{2}x^{2}-a^{2}y^{2})s\right)]\ ds\ dx\ dy

where the integral is now over
x,y,s\in F
such that

x+as,\,y-bs\in\mathfrak{p}^{-j},\ xb+y\rho^{2}a,\,xb+y\rho a\in\mathfrak{p}^{j}

or equivalently

x+as,\,y-bs\in\mathfrak{p}^{-j},\ xb,\,ya\in\mathfrak{p}^{j}.

This completes the proof of (
37
).

Applying (
37
), an interchange between
x
and
y
shows that
I(j;a,b)=I(j;-b,-a)
.
The functional equation (
38
) immediately follows from (
36
) and Lemma
9.2
(
2
).
∎

This reduces the computation of

to the case where
\left|{b}\right|\leq\left|{a}\right|\leq 1
.

9.2.
The case

Our goal in this section is to compute

for

9.2.1.

First, we further simplify the expression for

for the case
\left|{b}\right|\leq\left|{a}\right|\leq 1
. In the following lemma we first apply to (
37
) a variable change
in
s

\left|{b}\right|\leq\left|{a}\right|
simplifies the domain of integration and allows us to integrate over
s
. Motivated by the polynomial decomposition

X^{2}+Y^{2}-XY=(X+\rho Y)(X+\rho^{2}Y)

we follow up with a variable change in

that further simplifies the domain of integration.

Lemma 9.4
.

For
\left|{b}\right|\leq\left|{a}\right|\leq 1
and
0\leq j\leq\min({\operatorname{val}}(a),{\operatorname{val}}(b))
we have

(39)

I(j;a,b)=\left|{a}\right|^{-1}\int\psi[(1-\rho a^{-1}b)x-(1+2\rho)y+2a^{-1}b^{2}x^{3}]\ dx\ dy

where the integral is over

such that

(40)

bx-\rho ay\in a\mathfrak{p}^{-j},\ bx,\,ay,y(bx+ay)\in\mathfrak{p}^{j}.

Proof.

Apply to (
37

to obtain

I(j;a,b)=q^{-j}\int\psi[x+y+2\left(bx^{2}y-axy^{2}+(abxy-b^{2}x^{2}-a^{2}y^{2})(s-a^{-1}x)\right)]\ ds\ dx\ dy

=q^{-j}\int\psi[x+y+2\left(a^{-1}b^{2}x^{3}+(abxy-b^{2}x^{2}-a^{2}y^{2})s\right)]\ ds\ dx\ dy

where the integral is now over
x,y,s\in F
such that

as,\,bs-(a^{-1}bx+y)\in\mathfrak{p}^{-j},\ xb,\,ya\in\mathfrak{p}^{j}

or, under our assumption that
\left|{b}\right|\leq\left|{a}\right|
, equivalently such that

as,\,a^{-1}bx+y\in\mathfrak{p}^{-j},\ xb,\,ya\in\mathfrak{p}^{j}.

Now integrating over

I(j;a,b)=\left|{a}\right|^{-1}\int\psi[x+y+2a^{-1}b^{2}x^{3}]\ dx\ dy

where the integral is now over

9.2.2.

By splitting the domain of integration three ways we express

as a sum of three integrals that we subsequently evaluate separately.

Let
\left|{b}\right|\leq\left|{a}\right|\leq 1
, fix an integer

such that
0\leq j\leq{\operatorname{val}}(a)
and set

m=\min(j,{\operatorname{val}}(a)-j,\lfloor\frac{{\operatorname{val}}(a)-j}{2}\rfloor).

By definition

Note further that
y\in\mathfrak{p}^{-\lfloor\frac{{\operatorname{val}}(a)-j}{2}\rfloor}
if and only if
ay^{2}\in\mathfrak{p}^{j}
. Consequently, we have

(41)

y\in\mathfrak{p}^{-m}\ \ \ \text{if and only if}\ \ \ ay\in a\mathfrak{p}^{-j}\cap\mathfrak{p}^{j}\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ ay^{2}\in\mathfrak{p}^{j}.

It follows that the condition

y\not\in\mathfrak{p}^{-m}\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ ay\in\mathfrak{p}^{j}

is equivalent to the condition

(y\not\in\mathfrak{p}^{-j}\ \ \ \text{or}\ \ \ ay^{2}\not\in\mathfrak{p}^{j})\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ ay\in\mathfrak{p}^{j}.

Consequently, the set of
x,\,y\in F
satisfying (
40

(1)

y\in\mathfrak{p}^{-m}

(2)

y\not\in\mathfrak{p}^{-j}

(3)

y\in\mathfrak{p}^{-j}
and
ay^{2}\not\in\mathfrak{p}^{j}
.

Write

I(j;a,b)=I_{1}(a,b)+I_{2}(a,b)+I_{3}(a,b)

where

is defined by the integral (
39
) over
x,\,y\in F
satisfying (
40
) as well as the condition (
\ell
) above,
\ell=1,2,3
.

For future reference, we further point out that it is immediate from the definitions that

(42)

m\geq 0\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ m=0\ \ \ \text{if and only if}\ \ \ j\in\{0,{\operatorname{val}}(a)-1,{\operatorname{val}}(a)\}.

9.2.3.

In the notation of §
9.2.2
we compute

.

Lemma 9.5
.

We have

I_{1}(a,b)=\begin{cases}\left|{b}\right|^{-1}\mathcal{C}(ab^{-1},2a^{2}b^{-1})&j\in\{0,{\operatorname{val}}(a)\}\\
q\left|{b}\right|^{-1}\mathcal{C}(\varpi^{-1}(\rho^{2}ab^{-1}-1),2\varpi^{-3}a^{2}b^{-1})&1\leq j={\operatorname{val}}(a)-1\\
\left|{ab}\right|^{-1}\mathcal{C}_{j}(b^{-1}-\rho a^{-1},2a^{-1}b^{-1})&\lfloor\frac{{\operatorname{val}}(a)+1}{2}\rfloor\leq j\leq{\operatorname{val}}(a)-2\\
0&1\leq j\leq\lfloor\frac{{\operatorname{val}}(a)-1}{2}\rfloor.\end{cases}

Proof.

It follows from (
41

x\neq 0,\,y\in\mathfrak{p}^{-m},\,bx-\rho ay\in a\mathfrak{p}^{-j},\ bx,\,ay,y(bx+ay)\in\mathfrak{p}^{j}

and

x\neq 0,\,x\in b^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}^{j}\cap a\mathfrak{p}^{-j}),\,y\in\mathfrak{p}^{-m}\cap(bx)^{-1}\mathfrak{p}^{j}

are equivalent. Excluding the measure zero set where
x=0
we conclude that

I_{1}(a,b)=\left|{a}\right|^{-1}\int_{b^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}^{j}\cap a\mathfrak{p}^{-j})}\psi[(1-\rho a^{-1}b)x+2a^{-1}b^{2}x^{3}]\left\{\int_{\mathfrak{p}^{-m}\cap(bx)^{-1}\mathfrak{p}^{j}}\psi[-(1+2\rho)y]\ dy\right\}\ dx.

We have

\int_{\mathfrak{p}^{-m}\cap(bx)^{-1}\mathfrak{p}^{j}}\psi[-(1+2\rho)y]\ dy=\begin{cases}1&m=0\text{ or }\left|{bx}\right|=q^{-j}\\
0&m>0\text{ and }\left|{bx}\right|<q^{-j}.\end{cases}

Note that the set of
x\in b^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}^{j}\cap a\mathfrak{p}^{-j})
such that
\left|{bx}\right|=q^{-j}
is empty unless
\frac{{\operatorname{val}}(a)}{2}\leq j
and in this case it is precisey the set of

\left|{bx}\right|=q^{-j}
.
Taking (

y\in a+\mathcal{O}
. Thus

I_{2,j}(a)=\begin{cases}\psi(a)&j=0\\
0&0<j<n.\end{cases}

If
j>n
then in the domain of integration we have
|1+\rho^{2}a^{-1}y|<1
,
and this implies that
|y|=|a|=q^{n}
which contradicts
|y|\leq q^{2n-j}
. In this case the domain is thus empty.
Finally if
j=n
then
the inequality
|y|\leq q^{n}
implies the second and third inequalities above, and in view of the inequality

\left|{y(1+\rho^{2}a^{-1}y)}\right|\leq 1
we see that the domain of integration is the union of the two
\mathcal{O}
-cosets
y\in\mathcal{O}
and
y\in-\rho a+\mathcal{O}
.
It follows that
I_{2,n}(a)=1+\psi(-\rho a)
. We conclude that the following holds.

Lemma 11.2
.

If
a\in F^{*}
then

I_{2}(a)=\begin{cases}1+\psi(a)+\psi(-\rho a)&\left|{a}\right|>1\\
1&\left|{a}\right|=1\\
0&\left|{a}\right|<1.\end{cases}

∎

11.2.
The J integrals

The non-big cell relevant orbits are given by

(1)

\zeta I_{3},\zeta\in\mu_{3}

(2)

\begin{pmatrix}&a^{-2}\\
aI_{2}&\end{pmatrix},a\in F^{*}

(3)

\begin{pmatrix}&aI_{2}\\
a^{-2}&\end{pmatrix},a\in F^{*}.

For the first family of three orbits, the local integral for the unit element is given by

\int_{N(F)}f_{0}(\zeta n)\psi(n)\ dn,\quad\zeta\in\mu_{3}.

Since

f_{0}(\zeta n)=\begin{cases}1&n\in N\cap K\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

each integral is
1
.

We turn to the second family of orbits. For
i=1,2
, write the
n_{i}
that appears in the integration as

n_{i}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&x_{i}&z_{i}\\
&1&y_{i}\\
&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right).
Since we are modding out by the stabilizer we may take
y_{1}=0
. Multiplying, we see that the local integral is given by

J_{1}(a):=\int_{F^{5}}f_{0}\left(\begin{pmatrix}-ax_{1}&-ax_{1}x_{2}-az_{1}&a^{-2}-a(z_{1}y_{2}+x_{1}z_{2})\\
a&ax_{2}&az_{2}\\
0&a&ay_{2}\end{pmatrix}\right)\psi(-x_{1}+x_{2}+y_{2})\,d(*).

Since
f_{0}
vanishes away from
K\times\mu_{3}
we see that
J_{1}(a)=0
unless
|a|\leq 1
. In addition, in the support of the integrand, the variables of integration must satisfy the inequalities

\left|{x_{1}}\right|,\left|{x_{2}}\right|,\left|{y_{2}}\right|,\left|{z_{2}}\right|,\left|{x_{1}x_{2}+z_{1}}\right|\leq|a|^{-1},\left|{a^{-2}-a(z_{1}y_{2}+x_{1}z_{2})}\right|\leq 1.

If
|a|=1
the domain of integration is
\mathcal{O}^{5}
and it follows directly from the factorization of the argument of
f_{0}
that
\kappa=1
in this domain. Thus the integral is
1
.

Suppose from now on that
|a|<1
. Then in the support of the integrand the diagonal entries must be units.
Thus the domain of integration is given by

(46)

\left|{x_{1}}\right|=\left|{x_{2}}\right|=\left|{y_{2}}\right|=\left|{a}\right|^{-1},\\
z_{1}\in-x_{1}x_{2}+a^{-1}\mathcal{O},z_{2}\in x_{1}^{-1}a^{-3}-x_{1}^{-1}z_{1}y_{2}+\mathcal{O},\left|{a^{-3}+x_{1}x_{2}y_{2}}\right|\leq\left|{a}\right|^{-2}.

Note that the last condition is required since
\left|{z_{2}}\right|\leq\left|{a}\right|^{-1}
.
We have the following evaluation.

Lemma 11.3
.

Suppose that
\left|{a}\right|<1
and that the conditions (
46
) hold. Then

\kappa\left(\begin{pmatrix}-ax_{1}&-ax_{1}x_{2}-az_{1}&a^{-2}-a(z_{1}y_{2}+x_{1}z_{2})\\
a&ax_{2}&az_{2}\\
0&a&ay_{2}\end{pmatrix}\right)=(a,x_{2}y_{2}^{2})_{3}.

Proof.

We describe the proof in brief. Since
ay_{2}\in\mathcal{O}^{*}
, left multiplication by an element of
N\cap K
reduces the computation to the evaluation of
\kappa(g_{1})

where

g_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}-ax_{1}&-ax_{1}x_{2}-y_{2}^{-1}a^{2}+y_{2}^{-1}ax_{1}z_{2}&0\\
a&ax_{2}-ay_{2}^{-1}z_{2}&0\\
0&a&ay_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

We have
y_{2}^{-1}\in\mathcal{O}
. If

g_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}1&&\\
0&1\\
0&-y_{2}^{-1}&1\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{then}\quad g_{1}g_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}-ax_{1}&-ax_{1}x_{2}-y_{2}^{-1}a^{2}+y_{2}^{-1}ax_{1}z_{2}&0\\
a&ax_{2}-ay_{2}^{-1}z_{2}&0\\
0&0&ay_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Since
g_{1}g_{2}
is block diagonal it is easy to evaluate

\kappa(g_{1}g_{2})=(a,a(x_{2}-y_{2}^{-1}z_{2})ay_{2})_{3}=(a,x_{2}y_{2}-z_{2})_{3}=(a,x_{2}y_{2})_{3}.

In the last equality above we have used that
\left|{z_{2}}\right|<\left|{x_{2}y_{2}}\right|
in the domain (
46
). Furthermore,
\kappa(g_{2})=1
. We conclude that the value we require is given by

(a,x_{2}y_{2})_{3}\,\sigma(g_{1},g_{2})^{-1}.

Applying the algorithm of Bump-Hoffstein to compute
\sigma
, one finds that
\sigma(g_{1},g_{2})=(y_{2},a)_{3}
. The result follows.
∎

We arrive at the expression

J_{1}(a)=\int(a,x_{2}^{2}y_{2})_{3}\psi(-x_{1}+x_{2}+y_{2})\,dz_{2}\,dz_{1}\,dx_{1}\,dx_{2}\,dy_{2},

where the integration is over the domain (
46
). We carry out the inner integrations in
z_{2}
and then
z_{1}
to obtain

\left|{a}\right|^{-1}\int(a,x_{2}^{2}y_{2})_{3}\psi(-x_{1}+x_{2}+y_{2})\,dx_{1}\,dx_{2}\,dy_{2},

where the integration is over

\left|{x_{1}}\right|=\left|{x_{2}}\right|=\left|{y_{2}}\right|=\left|{a}\right|^{-1},\left|{a^{-1}+a^{2}x_{1}x_{2}y_{2}}\right|\leq 1.

Now we change
x_{1}\to a^{-1}x_{1}
,
x_{2}\to a^{-1}x_{2}
, and
y_{2}\to a^{-1}y_{2}
to obtain

\left|{a}\right|^{-4}\int(a,x_{2}^{2}y_{2})_{3}\,\psi(a^{-1}(-x_{1}+x_{2}+y_{2}))\,dx_{1}\,dx_{2}\,dy_{2},

where the integration is over

\left|{x_{1}}\right|=\left|{x_{2}}\right|=\left|{y_{2}}\right|=1,\left|{1+x_{1}x_{2}y_{2}}\right|\leq\left|{a}\right|.

Changing
x_{1}\mapsto x_{1}/(x_{2}y_{2})
the last inequality becomes
\left|{1+x_{1}}\right|\leq\left|{a}\right|
. This allows us to do the
x_{1}
integral. We arrive at

J_{1}(a)=\left|{a}\right|^{-3}\int_{\left|{x_{2}}\right|=\left|{y_{2}}\right|=1}(a,x_{2}^{2}y_{2})_{3}\,\psi\left(a^{-1}\left(x_{2}+y_{2}+\frac{1}{x_{2}y_{2}}\right)\right)\,dx_{2}\,dy_{2}.

We now make use of Corollary
7.5
to replace the integral in
x_{2}
with the integral of a cubic (so the parameters
c,d,t
of the
Corollary are given by
c=a^{-1}
,
d=(ay_{2})^{-1}
,
t=a^{2}
; note that
(t,c^{-1}d)_{3}=(a^{2},y_{2})_{3}^{-1}
). We see that

J_{1}(a)=\left|{a}\right|^{-3}\int_{\left|{y_{2}}\right|=1}\mathcal{C}(a^{-1},-3^{-3}y_{2}a^{-1})\psi(a^{-1}y_{2})\,dy_{2}.

Note that in the integrand above, the symbol
(a^{2},y_{2})_{3}
obtained from the Corollary cancels the symbol
(a,y_{2})_{3}
obtained from
\kappa
.

We evaluate the last integral by using the definition of
\mathcal{C}
. Substituting this definition and changing
x\to 3x
, we have

J_{1}(a)=\left|{a}\right|^{-3}\int_{\mathcal{O}^{*}}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\psi(a^{-1}x-3^{-3}y_{2}a^{-1}x^{3}+y_{2}a^{-1})\,dx\,dy_{2}\\
=\left|{a}\right|^{-3}\int_{\mathcal{O}}\psi(3a^{-1}x)\,\int_{\mathcal{O}^{*}}\psi(y_{2}a^{-1}(1-x^{3}))\,dy_{2}\,dx.

Note that

•

\left|{a^{-1}(1-x^{3})}\right|\leq 1
if and only if
x\in\rho^{k}+a\mathcal{O}
,
k=0,1,2

•

\left|{a^{-1}(1-x^{3})}\right|=q
if and only if
x\in\rho^{k}+\varpi^{-1}a\mathcal{O}^{*}
,
k=0,1,2
,

and the inner integral is zero if
\left|{a^{-1}(1-x^{3})}\right|>q
.
If
x\in\rho^{k}+a\mathcal{O}
,
k=0,1,2
, then the inner integral is
1-q^{-1}
, and so this piece contributes

(1-q^{-1})\left|{a}\right|^{-2}\sum_{k=0}^{2}\psi(3\rho^{k}a^{-1}).

If
x\in\rho^{k}+\varpi^{-1}a\mathcal{O}^{*}
for some
k=0,1,2
then the inner integral is
-q^{-1}
. If in addition
\left|{a}\right|<q^{-1}
then the sets
\rho^{k}+\varpi^{-1}a\mathcal{O}^{*}
are disjoint for
k=0,1,2
, so this piece contributes

-q^{-1}\left|{a}\right|^{-3}\sum_{k=0}^{2}\psi(3\rho^{k}a^{-1})\int_{\varpi^{-1}a\mathcal{O}^{*}}\psi(3a^{-1}x)\,dx=q^{-1}\left|{a}\right|^{-2}\sum_{k=0}^{2}\psi(3\rho^{k}a^{-1}).

If
\left|{a}\right|=q^{-1}
then the sets
\rho^{k}+\varpi^{-1}a\mathcal{O}^{*}=\rho^{k}+\mathcal{O}^{*}
,
k=0,1,2
are no longer disjoint, however, their union equals
\mathcal{O}\setminus(\sqcup_{k=0}^{2}(\rho^{k}+\mathfrak{p}))
. Consequently, in this case this piece contributes

-q^{2}\int_{\mathcal{O}\setminus(\sqcup_{k=0}^{2}(\rho^{k}+\mathfrak{p}))}\psi(3a^{-1}x)\ dx=q^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{2}\int_{\rho^{k}+\mathfrak{p}}\psi(3a^{-1}x)\ dx=q\sum_{k=0}^{2}\psi(3\rho^{k}a^{-1}).

We arrive at the following evaluation.

Lemma 11.4
.

The orbital integral for the orbit
\begin{pmatrix}&a^{-2}\\
aI_{2}&\end{pmatrix}
,
a\in F^{*}
, is given by

J_{1}(a)=\begin{cases}\left|{a}\right|^{-2}\sum_{k=0}^{2}\psi(3\rho^{k}a^{-1})&\left|{a}\right|<1\\
1&\left|{a}\right|=1\\
0&\left|{a}\right|>1.\end{cases}

We turn to the evaluation of the orbital integral for the orbit
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}&aI_{2}\\
a^{-2}&\end{smallmatrix}\right)
with
a\in F^{*}.
In this case modding out by the stabilizer we may take
x_{1}=0
.
Then the integral of concern is

J_{2}(a):=\int_{F^{5}}f_{0}\left(\begin{pmatrix}-a^{-2}z_{1}&a-a^{-2}x_{2}z_{1}&ay_{2}-a^{-2}z_{1}z_{2}\\
-a^{-2}y_{1}&-a^{-2}x_{2}y_{1}&a-a^{-2}y_{1}z_{2}\\

Since
f_{0}
is supported in
K
, we see that
J_{2}(a)=0
unless

\left|{x_{2}}\right|,\left|{y_{1}}\right|,\left|{z_{2}}\right|,\left|{z_{1}}\right|,\left|{x_{2}y_{1}}\right|\leq\left|{a}\right|^{2},\left|{a-a^{-2}y_{1}z_{2}}\right|,\left|{a-a^{-2}x_{2}z_{1}}\right|,\left|{ay_{2}-a^{-2}z_{1}z_{2}}\right|\leq 1.

Suppose from now on that

\left|{z_{1}}\right|=\left|{z_{2}}\right|=\left|{a}\right|^{2}

.

(47)

11.3.
Comparison

The comparison is given by the following Theorem

Theorem 11.7
.

The relevant
I
and
J
orbitals integrals match up to transfer factors:

(1)

The orbital integrals attached to the 3 singleton relevant orbits for
I
and for
J
each have value
1
.

(2)

Let
a=3(\rho-\rho^{2})c^{-1}.
Then
I_{1}(a)=\left|{c}\right|^{2}\psi(-3\rho c^{-1})J_{1}(c).

(3)

Let
a=3(\rho-1)c
. Then

I_{2}(a)=\left|{c}\right|^{-2}\psi(3\rho c)J_{2}(c)
.

The Theorem follows at once from the evaluations above. We remark that there are other possible matchings available. First, for all
c\in F^{*}
we have

J_{i}(\rho^{k}c)=J_{i}(c)
for
i=1,2
and
k=1,2
. In addition, we have the following matchings.

(1)

If
a=3(\rho-1)c
then
I_{1}(a)=\left|{c}\right|^{-2}\psi(3c)J_{2}(c)
.

(2)

If
a=3(\rho^{2}-1)c^{-1}
then
I_{2}(a)=\left|{c}\right|^{2}\psi(-3c^{-1})J_{1}(c)
.

This completes the proof of the Fundamental Lemma.

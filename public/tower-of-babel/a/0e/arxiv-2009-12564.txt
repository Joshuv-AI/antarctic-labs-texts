Limit theorems for continuous-state branching processes with immigration
Clément Foucart & Chunhua Ma & Linglong Yuan

Abstract
A continuous-state branching process with immigration, whose branching mechanism is Ψ \Psi and immigration mechanism Φ \varPhi , CBI ( Ψ , Φ ) (\Psi,\varPhi) for short, may have two different asymptotic regimes depending on whether ∫ 0 Φ ⁡ ( u ) | Ψ ⁡ ( u ) | ​ 𝑑 u < ∞ \int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u<\infty or ∫ 0 Φ ⁡ ( u ) | Ψ ⁡ ( u ) | ​ 𝑑 u = ∞ \int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u=\infty . When ∫ 0 Φ ⁡ ( u ) | Ψ ⁡ ( u ) | ​ 𝑑 u < ∞ \int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u<\infty , CBIs either have a limit distribution or a growth rate dictated by the branching dynamics. When ∫ 0 Φ ⁡ ( u ) | Ψ ⁡ ( u ) | ​ 𝑑 u = ∞ \int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u=\infty , immigration overwhelms branching dynamics. Asymptotics in this case are studied via a non-linear time-dependent renormalization in law. Three regimes of weak convergence are exhibited. Processes with critical branching mechanisms subject to a regular variation assumption are studied. This article proves and extends results stated by M. Pinsky in Limit theorems for continuous state branching processes with immigration [Bull. Amer. Math. Soc. 78 (1972)].



Key words.

Continuous-state branching processes, Immigration, Grey’s martingale, Limit distribution, Non-linear renormalization, Regularly varying functions.

primary 60J80; secondary 60F05; 60F15

Introduction

Continuous-state branching processes with immigration (CBI) have been defined by Kawazu and Watanabe
[
23
]
. They are scaling limits of Galton-Watson Markov chains with immigration, see e.g.
[
23
, Theorem 2.2]
. Recent years have seen renewed interest in this class of Markov processes. They appear for instance as strong solutions of some stochastic differential equations with jumps, see Dawson and Li
[
9
]
, and from a more applied point of view, form an important subclass of the so-called affine processes, which are known in the financial mathematics setting for modelling interest rates, see
[
11
]
. We mention for instance the works in this direction of Jiao et al.
[
21
]
and Barczy et al.
[
2
]
where certain CBI processes are studied from a statistical point of view.

The asymptotic behaviors of Galton-Watson processes with immigration have been extensively studied since the seventies. We refer to the works of Cohn
[
8
]
, Heathcote
[
19
]
, Heyde
[
20
]
, Pakes
[
31
]
and Seneta
[
34
]
and to their references. Transience and recurrence of CBIs have been characterized by Duhalde et al.
[
10
]
. Fine properties of the stationary distributions of CBI processes, when they exist, have also been recently established in Chazal et al.
[
7
]
and Keller-Ressel and Mijatovic
[
24
]
. In the case where no stationary distribution exists, less attention has been paid to the limit theorems of CBI processes. It will certainly not be surprising that the results found in the seventies for Galton-Watson processes with immigration, have counterparts in the continuous-state and continuous-time framework. A year after Kawazu and Watanabe’s founding work, Pinsky thus published a short note
[
32
]
without proof, on the limits of CBIs. We believe it is of interest to write down some details and resume in this article the study of limit theorems for CBIs initiated by Pinsky.

We start by proving an almost-sure convergence for CBI processes by adapting Grey’s approach
[
17
]
to the framework with immigration (Theorem
2
). We then provide a general non-linear renormalization in law (Theorem
6
). To the best of our knowledge this latter renormalization does not appear in the literature about Galton-Watson processes with immigration. We explain now our main results. Denote respectively by
\Psi
and
\varPhi
the branching and immigration mechanisms, we will recall their definitions in the next section. In the case of supercritical branching, we show the existence of two distinct almost-sure asymptotic regimes according to the convergence/divergence of the integral
\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u
.
When this integral converges, i.e
\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u<\infty
, the branching dynamics takes precedence over immigration and directs the divergence of the process towards infinity. More precisely, under the classical
L\ln L
moment assumption (also called Kesten-Stigum condition) over the branching Lévy measure, the CBI process grows at the same exponential rate as the pure branching process.
On the other hand, when it diverges, i.e
\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u=\infty
, immigration is so substantial that the branching, although supercritical, is somehow overtaken. So that, typically the process grows faster than the pure branching process on the event of its non-extinction.

A similar dichotomy occurs more generally for non-critical CBI processes when we consider their long-term behaviour in law. Our main contribution is to design a non-linear time-dependent renormalization in law of a non-critical CBI(
\Psi,\varPhi
)-process
(Y_{t},t\geq 0)
satisfying
\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u=\infty
. We shall find, see Theorem
6
, a deterministic function
\lambda\mapsto r_{t}(\lambda)
only depending on
\Psi
and
\varPhi
such that

r_{t}(1/Y_{t})\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{e}_{1}\text{ in law}

(1.1)

where
\mathrm{e}_{1}
is a standard exponential random variable. The latter renormalization is actually equivalent to the following property, see Corollary
1
. Given two independent CBI
(\Psi,\varPhi)
processes
(Y_{t},t\geq 0)
and
(\tilde{Y}_{t},t\geq 0)
such that
\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u=\infty
, one has

\frac{Y_{t}}{\tilde{Y}_{t}}\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\Lambda\text{ in law}

(1.2)

where
\mathbb{P}(\Lambda=0)=\mathbb{P}(\Lambda=\infty)=\frac{1}{2}
. As a consequence of (
1.2
), we shall see that no function
(\eta(t),t\geq 0)
exists such that
\eta(t)Y_{t}
converges in law towards a nondegenerate random variable (i.e whose support is not contained in
\{0,\infty\}
). This has been shown by Cohn
[
8
]
in the setting of discrete time and space. The meaning of the limit in law (
1.1
) will be made more explicit by introducing further assumptions on the rate of divergence of the integral
\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u
, namely on the speed at which
\int_{\varepsilon}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u
goes to
\infty
as
\varepsilon
goes to
0
. In the same vein as Pakes, see
[
31
]
, we design three regimes of divergence: Slow (S), Log (L) and Fast (F). Each corresponds to a specific renormalization and a specific limiting law. The faster the integral diverges, the more the branching dynamics is overtaken by immigration. This is reflected by the different renormalizations occurring in the three regimes. In particular, in the fast case (F) the branching mechanism plays no role in the renormalization. Pinsky’s result
[
32
, Theorem 2]
which corresponds to the subcritical case under condition (S) has now a proof, see Remark
14
–ii), and a misprint in his statement is corrected.

\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}

\overset{p}{\longrightarrow}

.

The paper is organized as follows. First we recall in Section
2
the definition of a CBI process and some of its most fundamental properties. Our main results are stated in Section
3
. We first establish in Section
3.1

2
Preliminaries

We recall hereafter the definition of a CBI process and some of its most fundamental properties. Our main references are Chapter 3 of Li’s book
[
29
]
and Chapter 12 of Kyprianou’s book
[
26
]
. We say that a random variable is nondegenerate if its support is not contained in
\{0,\infty\}
and proper if it is finite almost surely.

Write
\pi
and
\nu
for two
\sigma
-finite nonnegative measures on
(0,\infty)
satisfying respectively
\int^{\infty}_{0}(z\wedge z^{2})\,\pi(\text{\rm\,d}z)<\infty
and
\int^{\infty}_{0}(1\wedge z)\,\nu(\text{\rm\,d}z)<\infty
. Consider a triple
(\sigma,b,\beta)
such that
\sigma\geq 0,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
\beta\geq 0
. Let
\Psi
be the Laplace exponent of a spectrally positive Lévy process with finite mean (here we assume
|\Psi^{\prime}(0+)|<\infty
, so that in particular the CBI process does not explode) and whose characteristic triple is
(b,\sigma,\pi)
. Let
\varPhi
be the Laplace exponent of a subordinator with drift
\beta
and Lévy measure
\nu
. They are specified by the Lévy-Khinchine formula

\displaystyle\Psi(q)

\displaystyle=bq+\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}q^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}(e^{-qu}-1+qu)\pi(\mathrm{d}u),\quad q\geq 0,

So
\Psi
is convex (i.e.,
\Psi^{\prime\prime}(q)\geq 0,\forall q\geq 0
) with
\Psi(0)=0
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\varPhi(q)

\displaystyle=\beta q+\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-e^{-qu})\nu(\mathrm{d}u),\quad q\geq 0,

So
\varPhi
is a concave continuous, strictly increasing function with
\varPhi(0)=0
.

A CBI process with branching and immigration mechanisms
\Psi
and
\varPhi
, is a strong Markov process
(Y_{t},t\geq 0)
taking values in
[0,\infty)
whose transition kernels are characterized by their Laplace transforms. So for
\lambda\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-\lambda Y_{t}}]=\exp\left(-xv_{t}(\lambda)-\int_{0}^{t}\varPhi(v_{s}(\lambda))\mathrm{d}s\right),

(2.3)

where the map
t\mapsto v_{t}(\lambda)
is the solution to the differential equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}v_{t}(\lambda)=-\Psi(v_{t}(\lambda)),\quad v_{0}(\lambda)=\lambda.

(2.4)

Note that
v_{t+s}(\lambda)=v_{t}(v_{s}(\lambda))
from the Markov property.

Existence and unicity of CBI processes have been established in
[
23
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Recently Dawson and Li
[
9
]
, see also
[
22
]
, have shown that any CBI is the strong solution of a certain stochastic differential equation (SDE) with jumps.

Suppose that
(\Omega,\mathscr{F}_{t},\mathbb{P})
is a filtered probability space satisfying the usual hypotheses. Let
\{B_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be an
(\mathscr{F}_{t})
-Brownian motion. Let
N_{0}(\text{\rm\,d}s,\text{\rm\,d}z,\text{\rm\,d}u)
and
N_{1}(\text{\rm\,d}s,\text{\rm\,d}u)
denote two

(\mathscr{F}_{t})
-Poisson random measures on
(0,\infty)^{3}
and
(0,\infty)^{2}
with
intensities
\text{\rm\,d}s\,\pi(\text{\rm\,d}z)\,\text{\rm\,d}u
and
\text{\rm\,d}s\,\nu(\text{\rm\,d}z)
. We assume that the Brownian motion and the Poisson random measures are independent of each other.
Let
\tilde{N}_{0}(\text{\rm\,d}s,\text{\rm\,d}z,\text{\rm\,d}u)
be the corresponding compensated measure of
N_{0}
, namely
\tilde{N}_{0}(\text{\rm\,d}s,\text{\rm\,d}z,\text{\rm\,d}u):=N_{0}(\text{\rm\,d}s,\text{\rm\,d}z,\text{\rm\,d}u)-\text{\rm\,d}s\,\pi(\text{\rm\,d}z)\,\text{\rm\,d}u.
The following SDE

\begin{split}Y_{t}=Y_{0}&+\sigma\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{Y_{s}}\,\text{\rm\,d}B_{s}\\
&+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{Y_{s-}}z\,\tilde{N}_{0}(\text{\rm\,d}s,\text{\rm\,d}z,\text{\rm\,d}u)+\int_{0}^{t}(\beta-bY_{s})\text{\rm\,d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}zN_{1}(\text{\rm\,d}s,\text{\rm\,d}z).\end{split}

(2.5)

admits a unique strong solution whose law is that of a CBI with branching mechanism
\Psi
and immigration mechanism
\varPhi
. When there is no immigration, that is to say
\varPhi\equiv 0
, the drift
\beta
and the Poisson random measure
N_{1}
vanish and the process
(Y_{t},t\geq 0)
solution to (
2.5
) is a continuous-state branching process (CB process for short) with branching mechanism
\Psi
. When
\Psi\equiv 0
, only the immigration part remains and
(Y_{t},t\geq 0)
is a subordinator with Laplace exponent
\varPhi
. Lastly, we recall that a (sub)-critical CB
(\Psi)
process conditioned on the non-extinction is a CBI
(\Psi,\varPhi)
process with
\varPhi=\Psi^{\prime}-\Psi^{\prime}(0+)
, see Lambert
[
27
]
, Li
[
28
, Theorem 4.1]
and Fittipaldi and Fontbona
[
14
]
. From now on, unless explicitely mentioned, we consider processes with immigration, namely
\varPhi(q)>0
for all
q>0
.

Recall the form of the function
\Psi
and notice that
b=\Psi^{\prime}(0+)
. A CBI process is said to be critical, subcritical or supercritical according as
b=0
,
b>0
or
b<0
. Note that
\Psi
has at most two roots. Introduce

\displaystyle\rho=\inf\{z>0,\Psi(z)\geq 0\},\quad\inf\varnothing=\infty.

We see that
\rho=0
if
b\geq 0
and
\rho>0
if
b<0
. In particular
\rho=\infty
if and only if
-\Psi
is the Laplace exponent of a subordinator. By (
2.4
), if
0<\lambda<\rho
(resp.
\lambda>\rho
), then
v_{t}(\lambda)\in[\lambda,\rho]
is increasing (resp.
v_{t}(\lambda)\in[\rho,\lambda]
is decreasing) in
t
. Then (
2.4
) implies

\int_{v_{t}(\lambda)}^{\lambda}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{\Psi(z)}=t,\quad\forall t\in[0,\infty),\quad\forall\lambda\in(0,\infty)/\{\rho\},

(2.6)

Recall
\bar{v}_{t}:=\underset{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}{\lim\uparrow}v_{t}(\lambda)\in[0,\infty]
and set

\bar{v}:=\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\lim\downarrow}\bar{v}_{t}\in[0,\infty].

Grey shows in
[
17
]
that

\bar{v}_{t}<\infty\text{ for all }t>0\text{ if and only if }\int^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}q}{\Psi(q)}<\infty\text{ (Grey's condition)}.

(2.7)

Note that
\rho\leq\bar{v}
, and if
\bar{v}<\infty
then
\bar{v}=\rho
.

Recall (
2.3
). Define the map
r_{t}(\lambda):=\int_{0}^{t}\varPhi(v_{s}(\lambda))\mathrm{d}s
. A simple change of variable gives

r_{t}(\lambda)=\begin{cases}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!&\int_{v_{t}(\lambda)}^{\lambda}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u\text{ if }\Psi\not\equiv 0\\
\ \!\!\!\!\!\!\!&t\varPhi(\lambda)\text{ if }\Psi\equiv 0.\end{cases}

(2.8)

Then (
2.3
) can also be written as

\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-\lambda Y_{t}}]=\exp\left(-xv_{t}(\lambda)-r_{t}(\lambda)\right).

(2.9)

Note also that for any
t\geq 0
and any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
Y_{t}=Y_{t}^{1}+\cdots+Y_{t}^{n}
in law where
((Y_{t}^{i})_{t\geq 0},1\leq i\leq n)
are i.i.d copies of a CBI
(\Psi,\frac{1}{n}\varPhi)
process. So that in particular,
Y_{t}
has an infinite divisible law on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\lambda\mapsto r_{t}(\lambda)
is the Laplace exponent of a subordinator (with no killing term). For any
t\geq 0
, we set
r_{t}(\infty)=\int_{\bar{v}_{t}}^{\infty}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u\in(0,\infty]
, where
\bar{v}_{t}:=\underset{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\uparrow v_{t}(\lambda)
, with the convention that if
\int^{\infty}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u=\infty
then
r_{t}(\infty)=\infty
for all
t>0
. From (
2.8
), we easily check that
r_{t}(\infty)<\infty
as soon as
\int^{\infty}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u<\infty
. Letting
\lambda
tend to
\infty
in (
2.9
) readily entails that
r_{t}(\infty)<\infty
if and only if
\mathbb{P}_{x}(Y_{t}=0)>0
. We refer the reader interested in the zero-set of CBIs to
[
16
]
.

We refer to Section 3.2 of
[
29
]
for proofs of the following technical statements; see also
[
17
]
. We gather in the next lemma analytical results on the map
\lambda\mapsto v_{t}(\lambda)
and its inverse (whenever it exists).

Lemma 1
.

The map
\lambda\mapsto v_{t}(\lambda)
is strictly increasing on
[0,\infty)
. For any
t\geq 0
, let
\lambda\mapsto v_{-t}(\lambda)
be the inverse map of
\lambda\mapsto v_{t}(\lambda)
. This is a strictly increasing function, well-defined on
[0,\bar{v}_{t})
which satisfies for all
s,t\geq 0
and
0\leq\lambda<\bar{v}_{s+t}

v_{-(s+t)}(\lambda)=v_{-s}(v_{-t}(\lambda)).

For
0\leq\lambda<\bar{v}_{t}
, such that
\Psi(\lambda)\neq 0
, by (
2.6
) one has

\int_{\lambda}^{v_{-t}(\lambda)}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{\Psi(z)}=\int_{v_{t}(v_{-t}(\lambda))}^{v_{-t}(\lambda)}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{\Psi(z)}=t.

(2.10)

In particular, in the supercritical case, i.e
b\in(-\infty,0)
, for
\lambda\in(0,\rho)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial v_{-t}(\lambda)}{\partial t}=\Psi(v_{-t}(\lambda)),\quad v_{0}(\lambda)=\lambda.

(2.11)

The map
t\mapsto v_{-t}(\lambda)
is decreasing and by letting
t\rightarrow\infty
in (
2.10
) we see that
v_{-t}(\lambda)\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
.
Moreover,

v_{-(t+u)}(\lambda)/v_{-t}(\lambda)\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}e^{bu}
for any
u\geq 0
.

The following theorem has been announced by Pinsky
[
32
]
and provides some first information on the growth rate. It has been established in the (sub)critical case by Li, see
[
29
, Theorem 3.20, P.66]
and Keller-Ressel and Mijatović see
[
24
, Appendix]
.

Theorem 1

(Pinsky
[
32
]
, Li
[
29
]
)
.

Let
(Y_{t},t\geq 0)
be a CBI process with
|\Psi^{\prime}(0+)|<\infty
. Set
\tau(t)=e^{bt}
if
b<0
and
\tau(t)=1
if
b\geq 0
. The process
(\tau(t)Y_{t},t\geq 0)
converges in law, as
t\rightarrow\infty
, towards a proper random variable if and only if

\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u<\infty.

If
\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u=\infty
, then for all
z\geq 0
,
\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau(t)Y_{t}\leq z)\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
, that is to say
(\tau(t)Y_{t},t\geq 0)
converges to
\infty
in probability.

Our paper aims to enrich the above Theorem
1
by studying almost-sure limits in the supercritical case and finding new results on the growth rates when
\Psi
is non-critical and
\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u=\infty
. It is clear that a supercritical CBI process
(Y_{t},t\geq 0)
is transient i.e.
Y_{t}\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
a.s. The properties of transience and recurrence for subcritical and critical CBI processes have been studied in Duhalde et al.
[
10
]
. It is established in
[
10
, Theorem 3]
that a (sub)critical CBI
(\Psi,\varPhi)
process is recurrent or transient according as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}:=\int_{0}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\Psi(x)}\exp\left(-\int_{x}^{1}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u\right)=\infty\ \mbox{or}\ <\infty.

(2.12)

We see that the integral
\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u
plays a crucial role in this integral test. In particular, it is worth noticing that in the (sub)critical case, the divergence of
\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u
is necessary for the CBI process to be transient but not sufficient.

The notion of regularly varying functions will be used in several places. Recall that a function

is regularly varying at

(resp. at

) with index
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
if for any
\lambda>0

\frac{R(\lambda x)}{R(x)}\rightarrow\lambda^{\theta}\text{ as }x\rightarrow\infty\,(\text{resp. }0).

The function
R
is said to be
slowly varying
if
\theta=0
and if
R
is regularly varying with index
\theta
, then
R
has the form
R(x)=x^{\theta}L(x)
for all
x\geq 0
with

a slowly varying function. We stress that those functions occur naturally in the study as for instance, in the supercritical case, i.e.
b\in(-\infty,0)
, Lemma
1
ensures that the function

is regularly varying at
\infty
with index

.
We refer the reader to Bingham et al.
[
5
]
for a reference on those functions.

3.1
Almost-sure limits

This section deals with the so-called Seneta-Heyde norming for CBI processes. We refer to Seneta
[

]
and Heyde
[
20
]
for the seminal papers in the discrete setting; see also Lambert
[

]

\varPhi\equiv 0
, this study has been done by Grey
[

]
and Bingham
[
6
]
. We refer the reader for instance to the end of Chapter 12 of Kyprianou’s book
[

Recall
\rho\in[0,\infty]
the largest root of
\Psi
, the map
t\mapsto v_{-t}(\lambda)
and its equation (
2.11
).

Theorem 2
.

Let
(Y_{t},t\geq 0)
be a CBI
(\Psi,\varPhi)
with a supercritical branching mechanism
\Psi
i.e
b<0
. Let
0<\lambda<{{\rho}}
. Then,

i)

if
\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u<\infty
then
v_{-t}(\lambda)Y_{t}\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}W^{\lambda}

\mathbb{P}_{x}
-a.s.,
where
W^{\lambda}
is a non-degenerate proper random variable with Laplace exponent

\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[e^{-\theta W^{\lambda}}\right]=\exp\left(-xv_{-\frac{\ln\theta}{b}}(\lambda)+\int_{0}^{v_{-\frac{\ln\theta}{b}}(\lambda)}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u\right),

(3.13)

ii)

if
\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u=\infty
then
v_{-t}(\lambda)Y_{t}\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty

\mathbb{P}_{x}
-a.s.

Remark 3
.

i)

It is worth mentioning that Grey
[
17
, Theorem 2]
found the same almost-sure renormalization
v_{-t}(\lambda)
for the supercritical process without immigration, namely
\varPhi\equiv 0
, on the event of non-extinction. See also Duquesne and Labbé
[
12
, Lemma 2.2]
for the expression of the Laplace transform (
3.13
) with
\varPhi\equiv 0
. As explained in the introduction, Theorem
2
reflects the fact that two regimes occur according to the convergence/divergence of the integral
\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u
. In case i), the branching dictates the growth of the process, in case ii) it is overwhelmed by the immigration. Moreover, when
\int^{\infty}(x\ln x)\pi(\mathrm{d}x)<\infty
, we have
v_{-t}(\lambda)\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\sim}K_{\lambda}e^{bt}
for some constant
K_{\lambda}>0
. So in this case, as mentioned in the introduction, the CBI process grows almost-surely exponentially fast.

ii)

In the non-critical case,
|\Psi(u)|\underset{u\rightarrow 0}{\sim}|b|u
and the condition
\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u<\infty
is equivalent to
\int^{\infty}\ln(u)\nu(\mathrm{d}u)=\infty
where
\nu
is the immigration measure
\nu
, see the forthcoming Remark

Proof.

The proof follows from a simple adaptation of Grey’s martingales, see
[
17

, to the setting of CBIs. Consider
(Y_{t},t\geq 0)
a CBI
(\Psi,\varPhi)
. Recall (

\lambda\in(0,\rho)
. For every
t\geq 0
, set
\kappa_{\lambda}(t):=\exp\left(\int_{\lambda}^{v_{-t}(\lambda)}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u\right)
. We show that the process
(M_{t}^{\lambda},t\geq 0)
defined for any

by
M^{\lambda}_{t}=\kappa_{\lambda}(t)\exp\left(-v_{-t}(\lambda)Y_{t}\right)
is a positive martingale. The random variables

are plainly integrable and for any

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[M_{t+s}^{\lambda}|\mathscr{F}_{t}]

\displaystyle=\kappa_{\lambda}(t+s)\mathbb{E}[\exp(-v_{-(t+s)}(\lambda)Y_{t+s})\lvert Y_{t}]\quad\text{\it(by the Markov property)}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{\lambda}(t+s)\exp\left(-Y_{t}v_{s}(v_{-(t+s)}(\lambda))-\int_{v_{s}(v_{-(t+s)}(\lambda))}^{v_{-(t+s)}(\lambda)}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u\right)

\displaystyle=\kappa_{\lambda}(t+s)\exp\left(\int_{v_{-(t+s)}(\lambda)}^{v_{-t}(\lambda)}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u\right)\exp\big(-Y_{t}v_{-t}(\lambda)\big)

\displaystyle=\kappa_{\lambda}(t)\exp\big(-Y_{t}v_{-t}(\lambda)\big)=M_{t}^{\lambda},

where the second equality follows from (
2.9

v_{s}(v_{-(t+s)}(\lambda))=v_{s}(v_{-s}\circ v_{-t}(\lambda))=v_{-t}(\lambda)
.
In particular, we see that the process

is a positive supermartingale. This entails that the process
(v_{-t}(\lambda)Y_{t},t\geq 0)
converges, as

\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost-surely in
\bar{\mathbb{R}}_{+}{:=[0,\infty]}
. Denote its limit by

. We shall see that it is infinite almost-surely in case ii).

Since
\Psi

\rho>0
, and
\Psi(u)<0
for

. By Lemma

, we know that when

v_{-t}(\lambda)\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
.
For fixed
\theta>0
, we choose
t
large enough such that
v_{-t}(\lambda),\theta v_{-t}(\lambda)\in(0,\rho)
. Recall that by assumption
b\in(-\infty,0)
. Then
it can be established from Equations (

v_{t}(\theta v_{-t}(\lambda))\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}v_{-\frac{\ln\theta}{b}}(\lambda).

(3.14)

Note that the above convergence holds no matter the sign of
-\frac{\ln\theta}{b}
.

By (
2.9
), one has for all

\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-\theta v_{-t}(\lambda)Y_{t}}]=\exp\left(-xv_{t}(\theta v_{-t}(\lambda))-r_{t}(\theta v_{-t}(\lambda))\right)

and by letting

to

) for the Laplace transform of

. It is not hard to see that

as
\int_{0}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{\Psi(z)}=-\infty
and
\int_{\lambda}^{\rho}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{\Psi(z)}=-\infty
. In fact, if
\rho<\infty

\Psi(\rho)=0
; if
\rho=\infty
, then
-\Psi
is the Laplace exponent of a subordinator which implies that
\int^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{\Psi(z)}=-\infty
.
Moreover by (

v_{-\frac{\ln\theta}{b}}(\lambda)\underset{\theta\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}0\text{ and }v_{-\frac{\ln\theta}{b}}(\lambda)\underset{\theta\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\rho.

(3.15)

By using the first convergence in (

), we observe that if

W^{\lambda}<\infty

\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost-surely. Applying the second convergence in (

) and the fact that

\mathbb{P}(W^{\lambda}_{x}=0)=\exp\left(-x\rho+\int_{0}^{\rho}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u\right)=0.

If

as
\Psi(u)<0

\Box

The next theorem sheds some light on what limit theorems can be expected in the case
\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u=\infty
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
(Y_{t},t\geq 0)
be a supercritical CBI
(\Psi,\varPhi)
. Assume
\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u=\infty
.
For any positive deterministic function
(\eta(t),t\geq 0)
, if

(\eta(t)Y_{t},t\geq 0)

0
almost surely or
\infty
almost surely.

Remark 5
.

Theorem

breaks the hope of finding any law of large numbers and can be seen as a starting point for a study through non-linear renormalisations, see the forthcoming Section
3.2
.

Proof.
We shall use the framework of flow of SDEs as Dawson and Li
[
9
]
.
We recall that by replacing in the SDE (
2.5
), the Brownian motion
(B_{t},t\geq 0)
by a white noise

, see
[
9
]
and
[
30
]
for details, one can consider on a same probability space, the SDEs

\begin{split}Y^{(n)}_{t}(x)=x&+\sigma\int_{n}^{n+t}\int_{0}^{Y^{(n)}_{s}(x)}\,M(\text{\rm\,d}s,\text{\rm\,d}u)+\int_{n}^{n+t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{Y^{(n)}_{s-}(x)}z\,\tilde{N}_{0}(\text{\rm\,d}s,\text{\rm\,d}z,\text{\rm\,d}u)\\
&+\int_{n}^{n+t}(\beta-bY^{(n)}_{s}(x))\text{\rm\,d}s+\int_{n}^{n+t}\int_{0}^{\infty}zN_{1}(\text{\rm\,d}s,\text{\rm\,d}z).\end{split}

(3.16)

Those SDEs are known to have pathwise unique solutions. More precisely this provides a sequence of flows of CBI
(\Psi,\varPhi)
processes
\{Y^{(n)}_{t}(x),x\geq 0,t\geq 0,n\geq 0\}
such that for any

and any

is a CB process started from

\Psi
and is independent of
\{Y_{t}^{(n)}(y),t\geq 0\}
. We denote by
(Y_{t}(x),t\geq 0)
the process
(Y_{t}^{(0)}(x),t\geq 0)
, the solution to (
3.16
) for

. Pathwise uniqueness entails that
Y_{t}^{(n)}(Y_{n}(x))=Y_{n+t}(x)
for any
x\geq 0
,

and

almost-surely. Let
X_{t}^{(n)}(x)=Y_{t}^{(n)}(x)-Y_{t}^{(n)}(0)
. Then

\displaystyle Y_{n+t}(0)=X^{(n)}_{t}(Y_{n}(0))+Y^{(n)}_{t}(0).

By Theorem
2
-(ii),
v_{-t}(\lambda)Y^{(n)}_{t}(0)\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
a.s and applying Theorem
2
-(i) to the CB
(\Psi)
process

, see Remark

v_{-t}(\lambda)X^{(n)}_{t}(Y_{n}(0))\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}W^{\lambda}
for some finite random variable

. Hence

\displaystyle\frac{Y_{n+t}(0)}{Y^{(n)}_{t}(0)}=1+\frac{X^{(n)}_{t}(Y_{n}(0))}{Y^{(n)}_{t}(0)}\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}1\quad\mbox{a.s..}

(3.17)

Assume that there exists some
\eta(t)>0
such that
\eta(t)Y_{t}(0)\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}V_{0}
a.s.. Then by (
3.17
),
\eta(n+t)Y^{(n)}_{t}(0)\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}V_{0}
a.s., and for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,
\eta(n+l)Y^{(n)}_{\ell}(0)\underset{\ell\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}V_{0}
a.s.. However by iteration, it is not hard to see that for
\ell\geq 1
,

\displaystyle Y^{(n)}_{\ell}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{\ell-1}^{(n+1)}(Y_{1}^{(n)}(0))+Y_{\ell-1}^{(n+1)}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}X_{\ell-k}^{(n+k)}(Y_{1}^{(n+k-1)}(0)),\quad\big(Y_{0}^{(n+\ell)}(0)=0\big)

where
\{X_{\cdot}^{(k)}(Y_{1}^{(k-1)}(0))\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of independent CB(
\Psi
) processes.

By the above iteration, for fixed

,
\{Y^{(n)}_{\ell}(0),\ell\geq 1\}
is measurable with respect to the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{G}_{n}
generated by the sequence of independent processes
\{X_{t}^{(k)}(Y_{1}^{(k-1)}(0)):t\geq 0\}_{k=n}^{\infty}
. Since for any

,
\eta(n+l)Y^{(n)}_{\ell}(0)\underset{\ell\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}V_{0}
a.s., we immediately have that
V_{0}
is measurable with respect to the tail
\sigma
-algebra generated by the same sequence of independent processes

, i.e.

Kolmogorov’s zero-one law, see e.g.
[

, Theorem 2.5.1]
, asserts that

is a constant or infinite a.s.. Assume that
V_{0}
is a finite positive constant a.s.. Since

has the same distribution as

,
we immediately have that
\eta(t)Y^{(n)}_{t}(0)\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}V_{0}
a.s.. Then
\eta(n+t)/\eta(t)\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}1
, which implies that

for some slowly varying function

at
\infty
. However recalling that

is a regularly varying function with index

, see Lemma

as

goes to

is a slowly varying function at

.
This leads to a contradiction. Thus

is

\Box

3.2
A general limit in law for non-critical CBIs when
\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u=\infty

This section focuses on the study of the long-term behavior of CBI
(\Psi,\varPhi)
processes satisfying the condition

\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u=\infty.

(3.18)

By Theorem
1
, in this case
Y_{t}
converges in law to
\infty
as
t
goes to
\infty
. We give a finer description of the behavior of
Y_{t}
through distributional, rather than almost sure, limit theorems. In this section, we prove the main convergence theorem below which provides a non-linear time-dependent renormalization in law of any non-critical CBI process. The three different regimes of convergence in law mentioned in the Introduction are designed in the forthcoming Section
3.3
.

Recall the definition of
r_{t}(\lambda)
in (
2.8
) and that
r_{t}(\infty)=\infty
if and only if
\mathbb{P}_{x}(Y_{t}=0)=0
for all
t>0
. In the next theorem, we take the convention
1/0=\infty
.

Theorem 6
.

Assume (
3.18
) holds, and

\Psi
is non-critical (
b\neq 0
). Then, for all
x\geq 0
, we have

r_{t}(1/Y_{t}):=\int_{v_{t}(1/Y_{t})}^{1/Y_{t}}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{e}_{1},\text{ as }t\rightarrow\infty\text{ under }\mathbb{P}_{x}

(3.19)

where
\mathrm{e}_{1}
is an exponential random variable with parameter
1
.

Remark 7
.

The law of the limiting distribution in (
3.19
) does not depend on the initial state
x
of the CBI process. It justifies the perception that in the regime (
3.18
), the dynamics is governed on the long-term by the immigration part and not by the branching part.

Proof.

Recall the equations (
2.3
) and (
2.9
).

Step 1: We claim that for fixed
\lambda>0
,
r_{t}(\lambda)\rightarrow\infty
as
t\rightarrow\infty
. In fact,
in the subcritical case,
\Psi(u)>0
for
u>0
. By (
2.6
),
v_{t}(\lambda)\downarrow 0
as
t\to\infty
, for any fixed
\lambda>0
. According to (
3.18
) and the second equality of (
2.8
), we have that
r_{t}(\lambda)\rightarrow\infty
as
t\to\infty.

In the supercritical case,
still by (
2.6
),
v_{t}(\lambda)\rightarrow\rho
as
t\to\infty
for fixed
\lambda>0
. Then
\varPhi(v_{t}(\lambda))\rightarrow\varPhi(\rho)>0
,
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.
Together with the first equality of (
2.8
), we obtain that
r_{t}(\lambda)\rightarrow\infty
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.

Step 2: Recall that
\lambda\mapsto r_{t}(\lambda)
is the Laplace exponent of a subordinator with no killing term. For all
t
,
r_{t}(0)=0
, and
r_{t}
is strictly decreasing in
\lambda
. So we can define
\lambda\mapsto c_{t}(\lambda)
as the inverse of
\lambda\mapsto r_{t}(\lambda)
. Fix
\lambda>0
. By Step 1, for any small
\varepsilon>0
, we can find sufficiently large
t
such that
r_{t}(\varepsilon)>\lambda=r_{t}(c_{t}(\lambda))
, which implies that

c_{t}(\lambda)<\varepsilon
.
Thus

c_{t}(\lambda)\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0,\text{ and }v_{t}(c_{t}(\lambda))\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\rightarrow}0.

(3.20)

The second limit follows from (
3.18
) and the second equality of (
2.8
) (replacing
\lambda
by
c_{t}(\lambda)
).

Step 3:
Notice that we can equivalently show that for any
\lambda\geq 0
and
\theta>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-\theta c_{t}(\lambda)Y_{t}}]=e^{-\lambda}.

(3.21)

In fact, if (
3.21
) holds, then
c_{t}(\lambda)Y_{t}
converges in distribution to a random variable
Z
such that
\mathbb{P}(Z=\infty)=1-\mathbb{P}(Z=0)=1-e^{-\lambda}.

Therefore, for
\lambda>0
,

\mathbb{P}(r_{t}(1/Y_{t})>\lambda)=\mathbb{P}(1/Y_{t}>c_{t}(\lambda))=\mathbb{P}(c_{t}(\lambda)Y_{t}<1)\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}e^{-\lambda},

(3.22)

which implies (
3.19
).

Step 4: Note that

\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-\theta c_{t}(\lambda)Y_{t}}]=\exp{\left(-xv_{t}(\theta c_{t}(\lambda))-r_{t}(\theta c_{t}(\lambda))\right)}

(3.23)

and

\displaystyle r_{t}(\theta c_{t}(\lambda))

\displaystyle=\int_{v_{t}(\theta c_{t}(\lambda))}^{\theta c_{t}(\lambda)}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u

(3.24)

\displaystyle={\int_{c_{t}(\lambda)}^{\theta c_{t}(\lambda)}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u}+\underbrace{\int_{v_{t}(c_{t}(\lambda))}^{c_{t}(\lambda)}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u}_{=r_{t}(c_{t}(\lambda))=\lambda}+{\int_{v_{t}(\theta c_{t}(\lambda))}^{v_{t}(c_{t}(\lambda))}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u.}

So to obtain (
3.21
), it suffices to prove that as
t\rightarrow\infty
,

v_{t}(\theta c_{t}(\lambda))\rightarrow 0

(3.25)

and

\displaystyle\int_{c_{t}(\lambda)}^{\theta{c_{t}(\lambda)}}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u\rightarrow 0,\quad\int_{v_{t}(c_{t}(\lambda))}^{v_{t}(\theta c_{t}(\lambda))}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u\rightarrow 0.

(3.26)

By the monotonicity of
\varPhi
, we have for small enough
y
,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\theta y}^{y}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u\right|\leq\max(\varPhi(y),\varPhi(\theta y))\left|\int_{\theta y}^{y}\frac{1}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u\right|.

(3.27)

On the one hand
\max(\varPhi({y}),\varPhi(\theta y))\underset{y\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}0
as
\varPhi
is continuous and
\varPhi(0)=0
. On the other hand, since
\Psi
is non-critical, there exists some constant
h>0
such that
|\Psi(u)|\geq hu
for
u
close enough to
0
. This entails that

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\theta y}^{y}\frac{1}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u\bigg|\leq\frac{|\ln\theta|}{h},

(3.28)

when
y
is small enough. Then by (
3.27
) and (
3.20
),

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{c_{t}(\lambda)}^{\theta{c_{t}(\lambda)}}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u\bigg|\leq\max(\varPhi(c_{t}(\lambda)),\varPhi(\theta c_{t}(\lambda)))\frac{|\ln\theta|}{h}\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

(3.29)

So the first convergence in (
3.26
) is proved.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{v_{t}(c_{t}(\lambda))}^{v_{t}(\theta c_{t}(\lambda))}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\Psi(u)}\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{v_{t}(c_{t}(\lambda))}^{c_{t}(\lambda)}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\Psi(u)}+\int_{c_{t}(\lambda)}^{\theta c_{t}(\lambda)}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\Psi(u)}+\int_{\theta c_{t}(\lambda)}^{v_{t}(\theta c_{t}(\lambda))}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\Psi(u)}

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle t+\int_{c_{t}(\lambda)}^{\theta c_{t}(\lambda)}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\Psi(u)}-t

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{c_{t}(\lambda)}^{\theta c_{t}(\lambda)}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\Psi(u)},

which entails that
v_{t}(\theta c_{t}(\lambda))\to 0
as
t\to\infty
, since
v_{t}(c_{t}(r))\to 0
by (
3.20
) and
\big|\int_{c_{t}(\lambda)}^{\theta c_{t}(\lambda)}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\Psi(u)}\big|\leq\ln\theta/h
by (
3.28
). So the convergence in (
3.25
) is proved.
Then

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{v_{t}(c_{t}(\lambda))}^{v_{t}(\theta c_{t}(\lambda))}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u\bigg|\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\max\{\varPhi(v_{t}(c_{t}(\lambda))),\varPhi(v_{t}(\theta c_{t}(\lambda)))\}\left|\int_{v_{t}(c_{t}(\lambda))}^{v_{t}(\theta c_{t}(\lambda))}\frac{1}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u\right|

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\max\{\varPhi(v_{t}(c_{t}(\lambda))),\varPhi(v_{t}(\theta c_{t}(\lambda)))\}\left|\int_{c_{t}(\lambda)}^{\theta c_{t}(\lambda)}\frac{1}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u\right|,

which goes to
0
similarly as in (
3.29
). Then the second convergence in (
3.26
) is proved. So both conditions (
3.25
) and (
3.26
) hold true, and we can conclude that (
3.19
) holds true.
\Box

Remark 8
.

Note that the steps 1 and 2 also work for the critical case. But step 4 requires

However the same line of arguments as in this proof will be used in Section

We now provide a corollary leading to a more intuitive probabilistic understanding of Theorem

Corollary 1

Assume (

\varliminf_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\eta(t){Y}_{t}}{\eta(t)\tilde{Y}_{t}}=\frac{{Y}_{t}}{\tilde{Y}_{t}}\in[a/b,b/a]\right)>0,

\Box

(\tilde{Y}_{t}(0),t\geq 0)
. One checks that

\frac{Y_{t}(x)}{\tilde{Y}_{t}(y)}=\frac{Y_{t}(0)\left(1+X_{t}(x)/Y_{t}(0)\right)}{\tilde{Y}_{t}(0)\left(1+\tilde{X}_{t}(y)/\tilde{Y}_{t}(0)\right)}\overset{p}{\sim}\frac{Y_{t}(0)}{\tilde{Y}_{t}(0)}\text{ as }t\rightarrow\infty.

Indeed, on the one hand if
\Psi
is (sub)critical then both
\tilde{X}_{t}(x)
and
X_{t}(y)
converge towards
0
almost-surely, and by Theorem
1
,
Y_{t}(0)
and
\tilde{Y}_{t}(0)
go towards
\infty
in probability. On the other hand if
\Psi
is supercritical then by Theorem
2
, for any
x\geq 0
,

\frac{X_{t}(x)}{Y_{t}(0)}=\frac{v_{-t}(\lambda)X_{t}(x)}{v_{-t}(\lambda)Y_{t}(0)}\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0\text{ a.s.}

3.3
Three different regimes

Since the renormalization in Theorem
6
is non-linear and time-dependent, it is rather intricate at a first sight to deduce from it which explicit growth rates are possible. We design here different regimes for which (
3.18
) holds and the rate can be found explicitly. This establishes and completes
[
32
, Theorem 2]
.

3.3.1
Definition of the regimes and preliminary calculations

Recall
\lambda\mapsto c_{t}(\lambda)
the inverse of
\lambda\mapsto r_{t}(\lambda)=\int_{v_{t}(\lambda)}^{\lambda}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u
. Theorem
6
indicates that
Y_{t}
should grow at the speed of
1/c_{t}(\lambda)
as
t\rightarrow\infty
. However the magnitude of
c_{t}(\lambda)
is rather involved and deserves a careful analysis. We shall simplify (
3.19
) to more straightforward forms by imposing some additional conditions.

To start with, let us fix some constant
\lambda_{0}
such that
\lambda_{0}\in(0,\infty)
in the (sub)critical case and
\lambda_{0}\in(0,\rho)
in the supercritical case. Put

\displaystyle\varphi(\lambda)=\int_{\lambda}^{\lambda_{0}}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{|\Psi(u)|},\quad 0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}.

(3.31)

By assumption
|\Psi^{\prime}(0+)|<\infty
and thus
\varphi(\lambda)\rightarrow\infty
as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
.
The mapping
\varphi:(0,\lambda_{0})\;\rightarrow\;(0,\infty)
is strictly decreasing, and we write
g
for its inverse mapping. It is easy to see that
g
is a strictly decreasing continuous function on
(0,\infty)
, and

\lim_{x\to\infty}g(x)=0,\quad\lim_{x\to 0}g(x)=\lambda_{0}.

(3.32)

By (
2.6
), if
b\geq 0
, then
\Psi\geq 0
and

\displaystyle\varphi(v_{t}(\lambda))

\displaystyle=\int_{v_{t}(\lambda)}^{\lambda}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\Psi(u)}+\int_{\lambda}^{\lambda_{0}}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\Psi(u)}=t+\varphi(\lambda).

(3.33)

Similarly if
b<0
, then
\Psi(u)\leq 0
for
0\leq u\leq\rho
and provided that
v_{t}(\lambda)\in(0,\lambda_{0})

\displaystyle\varphi(v_{t}(\lambda))

\displaystyle=-\int_{v_{t}(\lambda)}^{\lambda}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\Psi(u)}-\int_{\lambda}^{\lambda_{0}}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\Psi(u)}=-t+\varphi(\lambda).

(3.34)

Applying
g
to both sides entails that if
b\geq 0

\displaystyle v_{t}(\lambda)=g(\varphi(\lambda)+t),\quad 0<\lambda<\lambda_{0},\ t>0

(3.35)

and if
b<0
and
v_{t}(\lambda)\in(0,\rho)

\displaystyle v_{t}(\lambda)=g(\varphi(\lambda)-t),\quad 0<\lambda<\lambda_{0},\ t>0.

(3.36)

Then for any
x,y>0
such that
\Psi
never attains zero between
x
and
y
, we obtain by a change of variable that

\int_{x}^{y}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u=\int_{g(\varphi(x))}^{g(\varphi(y))}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u=\int_{\varphi(y)}^{\varphi(x)}\varPhi(g(u))\mathrm{d}u.

(3.37)

Inspired by Pinsky
[
32
, Theorem 2]
,
we introduce the following function to characterize the divergence of the integral in (
3.18
):

H(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{1}{|b|}\int_{e^{-x}}^{1}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{u}\mathrm{d}u,\mbox{ if }b\in(-\infty,0)\cup(0,\infty);\\
\\
\displaystyle\int_{g(x)}^{\lambda_{0}}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u,\mbox{ if }b=0\\
\end{array}\right.,\quad x\geq 0

(3.38)

where
H(0)
takes the value
\lim_{x\to 0}H(x)=0
(using (
3.32
)).
It is not hard to check that the condition (
3.18
) is equivalent to
H(x)\underset{x\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty
.
Based on (
3.31
), a simple calculation shows that

H^{\prime}
is strictly decreasing and
H^{\prime}(x)\rightarrow 0
as
x\rightarrow\infty
.
We introduce now different regimes of speed of divergence of the function
H
at
\infty
.
We refer the reader to Bingham et al.
[
5
]
for a reference on those functions. The following three different modes of convergence to
0
of
H^{\prime}
correspond to different possible modes of divergence of
H
:

(S)

(slow-divergence)

xH^{\prime}(x)\rightarrow 0
as
x\rightarrow\infty
and
H(x)\rightarrow\infty
;

(L)

(log-divergence)
xH^{\prime}(x)\rightarrow a
for some constant
a>0
as
x\rightarrow\infty
;

(F)

(fast-divergence)
xH^{\prime}(x)\rightarrow\infty
as
x\rightarrow\infty
and
H^{\prime}
is regularly varying at
\infty
.

Note that conditions (L) and (F) always entail
H(x)\longrightarrow\infty
as
x
goes to
\infty
, while the limit
xH^{\prime}(x)\rightarrow 0
as
x\rightarrow\infty
in condition (S) does not guarantee it. Since
H^{\prime}(x)\rightarrow 0
as
x\rightarrow\infty
,
Condition (F) can be given in the following equivalent form:

\displaystyle H^{\prime}(x)=x^{-\delta}\frac{1}{L(x)},

(3.39)

where
L
is slowly varying at
\infty
and
0\leq\delta\leq 1
, and if
\delta=0
,
L(x)\rightarrow\infty
as
x\rightarrow\infty
; if
\delta=1
,
L(x)\rightarrow 0
as
x\rightarrow\infty
.

Let
h
be the inverse map of
1/H^{\prime}
.
Under (
3.39
) with
\delta>0
,
t\mapsto h(t)
is regularly varying with index
1/\delta
at
\infty
, i.e.
h(t)\sim t^{1/\delta}L^{*}(t)

for some slowly varying function
L^{*}(t)
. Moreover, since
H^{\prime}(x)\rightarrow 0
as
x\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}h(t)=\infty.

(3.40)

It might be difficult to verify the three conditions. We provide a proposition below
to study the asymptotic behaviors of
H
and
H^{\prime}.
To this purpose, recall the immigration mechanism

\varPhi(q)=\beta q+\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-e^{-qu})\nu(\mathrm{d}u).

As observed by Pinsky
[
32
, P. 244]
, for a non-critical CBI process, a faster rate of divergence in (
3.18
) implies heavier tails of the Lévy measure
\nu(\mathrm{d}u)
. The following result specifies this idea.

Proposition 1
.

Assume that
b\neq 0
and

H(x)\rightarrow\infty
as
x\rightarrow\infty
. Denote by
\bar{\nu}
the tail of
\nu
: for all
u\geq 0
,
\bar{\nu}(u)=\nu([u,\infty))
. Then

\displaystyle H(x)\sim\frac{1}{|b|}\int_{1}^{e^{x}}\frac{\bar{\nu}(u)}{u}\mathrm{d}u,\quad\text{ as }x\rightarrow\infty.

Moreover
H^{\prime}(x)=\varPhi(e^{-x})/|b|
for any
x\geq 0
and if
u\mapsto\bar{\nu}(u)
is slowly varying at
\infty
then

\displaystyle H^{\prime}(x)\sim\bar{\nu}(e^{x})/|b|,\quad\text{ as }x\rightarrow\infty.

Remark 10
.

The tail
u\mapsto\bar{\nu}(u)
is slowly varying at
\infty
if and only if
\varPhi
is slowly varying at
0
, see
[
5
, Theorem 8/1/6, P.333]

Proof.
Note that
\varPhi(u)/u=\beta+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-ut}\bar{\nu}(t)\mathrm{d}t
.
A simple calculation shows that for
z\in(0,1)

\displaystyle\int_{z}^{1}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{u}\mathrm{d}u\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\beta(1-z)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\bar{\nu}(u)}{u}(e^{-zu}-e^{-u})\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\beta(1-z)+\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}+\int_{1}^{\infty}\bigg)\frac{\bar{\nu}(u)}{u}(e^{-zu}-e^{-u})\mathrm{d}u.

Since
\int_{0}^{1}u\nu(\mathrm{d}u)<\infty
,

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\bar{\nu}(u)}{u}e^{-u}\mathrm{d}u<\infty
and
\int_{0+}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{u}\mathrm{d}u=\infty
, we have that as
z\rightarrow 0
,

\displaystyle\int_{z}^{1}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{u}\mathrm{d}u\sim\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\bar{\nu}(u)}{u}e^{-zu}\mathrm{d}u,

(3.41)

which implies
\int_{1}^{x}\frac{\bar{\nu}(u)}{u}\mathrm{d}u\rightarrow\infty
as
x\rightarrow\infty
.
It is not hard to see that
\int_{1}^{x}\frac{\bar{\nu}(u)}{u}\mathrm{d}u
is also slowly varying at
\infty
. It follows from Tauberian theorem (see e.g. Bertoin
[
3
, P.10]
)
that

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{x}\frac{\bar{\nu}(u)}{u}\mathrm{d}u\sim\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\bar{\nu}(u)}{u}e^{-u/x}\mathrm{d}u,\quad x\rightarrow\infty.

(3.42)

The first result follows from (
3.41
) and (
3.42
). For the second result, note that
H^{\prime}(x)=\varPhi(e^{-x})/|b|
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that the parameter
\beta
in
\varPhi
equals zero. Applying the Tauberian theorem entails that if
\bar{\nu}(x)\sim\ell(x)
for some slowly varying function
\ell
at
\infty
, then
\frac{\varPhi(u)}{u}\sim\frac{1}{u}\ell(1/u)
as
u
goes
0
. Hence we have

H^{\prime}(x)\sim\bar{\nu}(e^{x})/|b|,\quad x\rightarrow\infty.

\Box

Remark 11
.

By letting
z
to
0
in (
3.41
), we see that
\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(x)}{x}\mathrm{d}x=\infty
if and only if
\int^{\infty}\frac{\bar{\nu}(u)}{u}\mathrm{d}u=\infty
, the latter is equivalent to
\int^{\infty}_{1}\ln x\ \nu(\mathrm{d}x)=\infty
.

Proposition
1
allows us to reformulate the three regimes, in the non-critical case, in terms of the tail of the immigration measure
\nu
when the latter has a slowly varying tail.

(S)

(slow-divergence)

\bar{\nu}(x)\ln x\rightarrow 0
as
x\rightarrow\infty
and
\int^{\infty}_{1}\frac{\bar{\nu}(x)}{x}=\infty
;

(L)

(log-divergence)
\bar{\nu}(x)\ln x\rightarrow c
for some constant

as
x\rightarrow\infty
;

(F)

(fast-divergence)
\bar{\nu}(x)\ln x\rightarrow\infty
as
x\rightarrow\infty
.

The constant
c
in regime (L) matches with
a|b|
where

. We give below some examples of explicit immigration measures
\nu
for which the three different regimes may occur in the non-critical cases.

Example 1
.

Let
b\in(-\infty,0)\cup(0,\infty)
.

1.

If

\bar{\nu}(x)\sim\frac{1}{\ln x\ln\ln x}
as
x\rightarrow\infty
,
then
H(x)\sim(\ln\ln x)/|b|
and
H^{\prime}(x)\sim 1/(|b|x\ln x)
. Condition (S) is satisfied. This example corresponds to Example 3 in
[
10
, Example 3]
of null-recurrent CBI.

2.

If

\bar{\nu}(x)\sim c/{\ln x}

for some constant
c>0
, as
x\rightarrow\infty
,
then
H^{\prime}(x)\sim c/(|b|x)
. Condition (L) is satisfied.

3.

If

\bar{\nu}(x)\sim\frac{\ln\ln x}{(\ln x)^{\delta}},\quad(0<\delta\leq 1)
as
x\rightarrow\infty
,
then
H^{\prime}(x)\sim(x^{-\delta}\ln x)/|b|
. If as
x\rightarrow\infty
,
\bar{\nu}(x)\sim 1/(\ln\ln x)
, then
H^{\prime}(x)\sim 1/(|b|\ln x)
. Both cases satisfy Condition (F).

Remark 12
.

Recall the integral test
\mathcal{E}<\infty
or
\mathcal{E}=\infty
for transience and recurrence of (sub)critical CBIs given in (
2.12
). Plainly, by a change of variable,
\mathcal{E}=\int^{\infty}e^{-H(x)}\mathrm{d}x
. If (F) holds, or (L) is satisfied with
a>1
, then
\mathcal{E}<\infty
and the process is transient. In the case (S), or (L) with

,
\mathcal{E}=\infty
and the process is null-recurrent.

We state now a side result on the growth rate of a subordinator whose Laplace exponent is slowly varying at
0
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
(I_{t},t\geq 0)
be a subordinator with Laplace exponent
\varPhi
. Assume that
\varPhi
is slowly varying at
0
, then

t\varPhi(1/I_{t})\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{e}_{1}\text{ as }t\to\infty.

Proof.
This is reminiscent to the steps 1 and 2 in the proof of Theorem
6
. Observe that the stated convergence holds if and only if for all
\lambda>0

\mathbb{P}(t\varPhi(1/I_{t})>\lambda)=\mathbb{P}(I_{t}<1/\varPhi^{-1}(\lambda/t))=\mathbb{P}(\varPhi^{-1}(\lambda/t)I_{t}<1)\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}e^{-\lambda}.

The latter will occur if for any
\theta>0
,
\mathbb{E}[e^{-\theta\varPhi^{-1}(\lambda/t)I_{t}}]\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}e^{-\lambda}
.
Since
\varPhi
is slowly varying at
0
and
\varPhi^{-1}(\lambda/t)\longrightarrow 0
as
t\to\infty
, we have

\frac{t}{\lambda}\varPhi(\theta\varPhi^{-1}(\lambda/t))=\frac{\varPhi(\theta\varPhi^{-1}(\lambda/t))}{\varPhi(\varPhi^{-1}(\lambda/t))}\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}1.

Therefore

\mathbb{E}[e^{-\theta\varPhi^{-1}(\lambda/t)I_{t}}]=e^{-t\varPhi(\theta\varPhi^{-1}(\lambda/t))}\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}e^{-\lambda}

which finishes the proof.
\Box

(ii) By Condition (L),

(ii).

(iii) The mean value theorem for integrals shows that there is some

between

By Condition (F) and (

Applying (

By changing variable and applying a mean value theorem to the right-hand side of (

\displaystyle\int_{v_{t}(1/Y_{t})}^{1/Y_{t}}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u\!\!\!

3.4
On the critical case

The study of the critical case, i.e.
b=\Psi^{\prime}(0+)=0
, is more involved as
v_{t}(\lambda)
may have different asymptotics as
t\rightarrow\infty
and
\lambda\rightarrow 0
according to the behavior of
\Psi
near
0
. We make the following assumption on the Lévy measure
\pi
in the branching mechanism:

Suppose that
\pi
satisfies

\displaystyle\bar{\pi}(u)\underset{u\rightarrow\infty}{\sim}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(-\alpha)}u^{-1-\alpha}\ell(u),

(3.56)

where
\bar{\pi}(u)=\pi(u,\infty)
for
u>0
,
0<\alpha<1
and
\ell
is slowly varying at
\infty
. By
[
5
, Theorem 8.1.6]
, the above assumption is equivalent to

\displaystyle\Psi(\lambda)\underset{\lambda\rightarrow 0}{\sim}\lambda^{1+\alpha}\ell(1/\lambda).

(3.57)

Recall that
\varphi
is defined by (
3.31
) and
g
is inverse function of
\varphi
. It follows from Karamata’s theorem (
[
33
, P.17 and P.23]
)
that

\displaystyle\varphi(\lambda)\sim\frac{\lambda^{-\alpha}}{\alpha\ell(1/\lambda)}\quad{and}\quad g(1/\lambda)\sim\lambda^{1/\alpha}\ell^{*}(1/\lambda),\quad\text{ as }\lambda\rightarrow 0,

(3.58)

where
\ell^{*}
is slowly varying at
\infty
. We denote by
\varPhi^{-1}
the inverse function of
\varPhi
.

Theorem 15
.

Assume that
b=0
and
(\ref{regular varying levy measure})
holds.

(i)

If Condition (S) holds, let
m(x)
be defined as in (
3.43
). Then

\displaystyle\frac{m(Y_{t})}{m(1/g(t))}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}V\text{ as }t\to\infty,

where
V\text{ is uniformly distributed on }[0,1].

(ii)
If Condition (L) holds, then

g(t)Y_{t}\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}V_{L}\text{ as }t\to\infty,

where
\mathbb{E}[e^{-\lambda V_{L}}]=(1+\lambda^{\alpha})^{-a},\forall\lambda\geq 0.

(iii)
If Condition (F) holds with
\delta>0
in (
3.39
), then

\varrho_{t}Y_{t}\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}V_{F}\text{ as }t\to\infty

(3.59)

where
\mathbb{E}[e^{-\theta V_{F}}]=\exp(-\theta^{\delta\alpha}),\text{ for all }\theta\geq 0
,
with
\varrho_{t}=\varPhi^{-1}(1/t)=t^{-1/(\delta\alpha)}\bar{\ell}(t)\text{ as }t\to\infty
for some slowly varying function
\bar{\ell}
at
\infty
.

If Condition (F) holds with
\delta=0
in (
3.39
), then
t\varPhi(1/Y_{t})\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{e}_{1}\text{ as }t\to\infty
.

Remark 16
.

When
\delta\in(0,1]
, the convergence (
3.59
) is equivalent to the following
t\varPhi(1/Y_{t})\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}V_{F}^{-\delta\alpha}
as
t
goes to
\infty
. Indeed for any
\lambda>0
, the property of regular variation of
\varrho_{t}
implies that

\displaystyle\;\;\mathbb{P}(t\varPhi(1/Y_{t})\geq\lambda)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(\varPhi(1/Y_{t})\geq\lambda/t)=\mathbb{P}(1/Y_{t}\geq\varrho_{t/\lambda})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(\varrho_{t/\lambda}Y_{t}\leq 1)\sim\mathbb{P}(\lambda^{1/(\delta\alpha)}\varrho_{t}Y_{t}\leq 1)\underset{t\to\infty}{\to}\mathbb{P}(V_{F}\leq\lambda^{-1/(\delta\alpha)})=\mathbb{P}((V_{F})^{-\delta\alpha}\geq\lambda).

Since the random variable
V_{F}
has a stable law,
(V_{F})^{-\delta\alpha}
is not a standard exponential random variable. Therefore, unlike in the non-critical cases, see Theorem
13
-(iii) for which

t\varPhi(1/Y_{t})\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{e}_{1}

holds true when condition (F) holds, no matter what value
\delta\in[0,1]
takes, in the critical case, the convergence to
\mathrm{e}_{1}
is only true for
\delta=0.

Proof.
We start with some observations. By (
3.35
) and (
3.37
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{v_{t}(\lambda)}^{\lambda}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u=\int^{t+\varphi(\lambda)}_{\varphi(\lambda)}\varPhi(g(u))\mathrm{d}u,\quad H(x)=\int^{x}_{\varphi(\lambda_{0})}\varPhi(g(u))\mathrm{d}u.

(3.60)

Then
H^{\prime}(x)=\varPhi(g(x)).
Note that we will use
H^{\prime}(x)
or
\varPhi(g(x))
in different contexts.

It was mentioned in Remark
8
that the steps 1 and 2 in the proof of Theorem
6
still hold for the critical case. That is to say; recalling
r_{t}(\lambda)
defined by (
2.8
) and
\lambda\mapsto c_{t}(\lambda)
its inverse; we have for any fixed
\lambda>0
,
c_{t}(\lambda){\rightarrow}0
and
v_{t}(c_{t}(\lambda)){\rightarrow}0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore
\varphi(c_{t}(\lambda))\to\infty\text{ as }t\to\infty
.

(i) By (
3.60
) with
\lambda
replaced by
c_{t}(\lambda)
, applying the mean value theorem, there exists
\hat{\theta}_{t}
between
\varphi(c_{t}(\lambda))
and

\displaystyle\lambda=\int_{v_{t}(c_{t}(\lambda))}^{c_{t}(\lambda)}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u=\int^{t+\varphi(c_{t}(\lambda))}_{\varphi(c_{t}(\lambda))}\varPhi(g(u))\mathrm{d}u=\hat{\theta}_{t}H^{\prime}(\hat{\theta}_{t}){\ln}\Big(\frac{t+\varphi(c_{t}(\lambda))}{\varphi(c_{t}(\lambda))}\Big).

(3.61)

Together with Condition (S), we have that
\varphi(c_{t}(\lambda))/t\rightarrow 0\text{ as }t\rightarrow\infty.
Then still by the mean value theorem and Condition (S), we obtain

\int^{v_{t}(\theta c_{t}(\lambda))}_{v_{t}(c_{t}(\lambda))}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u=\int^{t+\varphi(c_{t}(\lambda))}_{t+\varphi(\theta c_{t}(\lambda))}\varPhi\circ g(u)\mathrm{d}u=o(1)\ln\Big(\frac{t+\varphi(c_{t}(\lambda))}{t+\varphi(\theta c_{t}(\theta\lambda))}\Big),

(3.62)

which goes to
0
as
\varphi
is regularly varying with index
-\alpha
by (
3.58

\int_{\theta c_{t}(\lambda)}^{c_{t}(\lambda)}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u\rightarrow 0

\displaystyle\int_{v_{t}(1/Y_{t})}^{1/Y_{t}}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u=\int^{t+\varphi(1/Y_{t})}_{\varphi(1/Y_{t})}\varPhi(g(u))\mathrm{d}u\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{e}_{1}\text{ as }t\rightarrow\infty.

(3.63)

Applying the same transformation as in (
3.62

3.63

\varphi(1/Y_{t})/t\overset{p}{\rightarrow}0\text{ as }t\rightarrow\infty

(ii) By (

Remark 17
.

Recall that in the critical case when
\varPhi=\Psi^{\prime}
, the CBI
(\Psi,\varPhi)
process has the same law as the CB(
\Psi
) processes conditioned on the non-extinction. Moreover, we readily check that
\int_{0}\frac{\Psi^{\prime}(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u=\infty
. It follows that
H^{\prime}(x)=\Psi^{\prime}(g(x))
for all
x
.

We apply now Theorem
1
and Theorem
15
to the case of stable branching and immigration mechanisms for which explicit calculations can be done.

Corollary 2

(Stable case)
.

Assume
\Psi(q)=dq^{\alpha+1}
for
d>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
\varPhi(q)=d^{\prime}q^{\beta}
for
d^{\prime}>0
and
\beta\in(0,1]
. Then,

i)

if
\beta/\alpha>1
then
Y_{t}\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}Y_{\infty}\text{ as }t\rightarrow\infty
where
Y_{\infty}
has Laplace transform
\mathbb{E}[e^{-\lambda Y_{\infty}}]=e^{-\frac{\lambda^{\beta-\alpha}}{\beta-\alpha}}
for any
\lambda\geq 0
,

ii)

if
\beta/\alpha=1
then
t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}Y_{t}\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}(\alpha d)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}V_{L}\text{ as }t\rightarrow\infty
where
V_{L}
has Laplace transform
\mathbb{E}[e^{-\lambda V_{L}}]=\frac{1}{(1+\lambda^{\alpha})^{\frac{d^{\prime}}{\alpha d}}}
for any
\lambda\geq 0
,

iii)

if
\beta/\alpha<1
then
t^{-\frac{1}{\beta}}Y_{t}\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}(d^{\prime})^{1/\beta}V_{F}\text{ as }t\rightarrow\infty,
where
V_{F}
has Laplace transform
\mathbb{E}[e^{-\lambda V_{F}}]=e^{-\lambda^{\beta}}
for any
\lambda\geq 0
.

Remark 18
.

When
\beta=\alpha
, the specific case
d^{\prime}=(\alpha+1)d
which corresponds to
\varPhi=\Psi^{\prime}
, has been studied by Kyprianou and Pardo
[
25
, Lemma 3]
with other techniques.

Proof.

First notice that
\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{\Psi(u)}\mathrm{d}u=\infty
iff
\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\leq 1
. Statement i) is a direct consequence of Theorem
1
. Let
\lambda_{0}=1
, we compute
\varphi(x)=\frac{1}{\alpha d}\left(\frac{1}{x^{\alpha}}-1\right)
and
g(x)=\left(\frac{1}{\alpha dx+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
for
x>0
. Moreover

as

goes to
\infty
. If
\beta/\alpha=1
then Condition (L) is fulfilled for

and we deduce statement ii) from Theorem
15
. If
\beta/\alpha<1
then Condition (F) and (
3.39
) are fulfilled with
\delta=\frac{\beta}{\alpha}
. Theorem
15
also applies.

We have focused our study on continuous-state branching processes with immigration whose branching Lévy measure has finite mean, i.e.
\Psi^{\prime}(0+)=b\in\mathbb{R}
or equivalently
\int^{\infty}_{1}z\pi(\mathrm{d}z)<\infty
. The proofs of Theorem
6
and Corollary
1
however do not make use of this assumption. They hold then also true in the case of a non-explosive supercritical CBI
(\Psi,\varPhi)
with
\Psi^{\prime}(0+)=-\infty
. A similar dichotomy occurs in the long-term behavior whether
\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u<\infty
or
\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u=\infty
when
b=-\infty
. In the first case, some almost-sure non-linear renormalizations can be found, see
[
18
]
and Foucart and Ma
[
15
]
for the case without immigration. In the case
\int_{0}\frac{\varPhi(u)}{|\Psi(u)|}\mathrm{d}u=\infty
, similar regimes than those found in Section
3.3
exist, see
[
1
]
for the case of discrete state-space processes.

Homomorphic Sensing of Subspace Arrangements
Liangzu Peng, Manolis C. Tsakiris

Abstract
Homomorphic sensing is a recent algebraic-geometric framework that studies the unique recovery of points in a linear subspace from their images under a given collection of linear maps. It has been successful in interpreting such a recovery in the case of permutations composed by coordinate projections, an important instance in applications known as unlabeled sensing, which models data that are out of order and have missing values. In this paper, we provide tighter and simpler conditions that guarantee the unique recovery for the single-subspace case, extend the result to the case of a subspace arrangement, and show that the unique recovery in a single subspace is locally stable under noise. We specialize our results to several examples of homomorphic sensing such as real phase retrieval and unlabeled sensing. In so doing, in a unified way, we obtain conditions that guarantee the unique recovery for those examples, typically known via diverse techniques in the literature, as well as novel conditions for sparse and unsigned versions of unlabeled sensing. Similarly, our noise result also implies that the unique recovery in unlabeled sensing is locally stable.

1.2
Examples of homomorphic sensing and related work

We build some insight and illustrate the significance of Definition
1
by way of some examples.

We begin with a very simple linear algebra example, where
\mathcal{T}=\{\tau_{A}\}
consists of the linear transformation
\tau_{A}:\mathbb{H}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{m}

m\times n

\hsp(\mathbb{H}^{n},\{\tau_{A}\})
holds if and only if
\tau_{A}
is injective, that is, if and only if

m\geq n

\hsp(\mathcal{X},\{\tau_{A}\})
holds if and only if no two vectors in
\mathcal{X}
differ by a nonzero nullvector of

Our next example is compressed sensing. Denoting by
\overline{\mathcal{K}_{\mathscr{I}}}

-sparse vectors of

-sparse vectors under an

A more interesting example is

1.3
Existing homomorphic sensing theory

Prior work on the abstract theory of homomorphic sensing has only considered Definition
1
for
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{V}
a linear subspace of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
of dimension
d
. Even though this formulation is linear algebraic, its nature is inherently algebraic-geometric, because whenever
\tau_{1}(v_{1})=\tau_{2}(v_{2})
, we have that
v_{1},v_{2}
satisfy the quadratic relation
\tau_{1}(v_{1})\wedge\tau_{2}(v_{2})=0
, with
\wedge
being the exterior product. This was the main insight of
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
leading to the following results. With linear maps
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}:\mathbb{H}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{m}
let
\overline{\tau}_{1},\overline{\tau}_{2}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{m}
be their complexifications and let
T_{1},T_{2}
be their matrix representations with respect to the standard basis. Let
\rho
be a linear projection onto the image
\im(\tau_{2})
of
\tau_{2}
with matrix representation
P
and complexification
\overline{\rho}
. With
w
a vector of variables, the
2\times 2
minors of the
m\times 2
matrix
[PT_{1}w\,\,\,T_{2}w]
are polynomials in entries of
w
, so their vanishing locus in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a complex algebraic variety, say
\mathcal{Y}_{\rho\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}
. Removing from
\mathcal{Y}_{\rho\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}
the union of linear subspaces
\mathcal{Z}_{\rho\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}:=\ker(\overline{\rho}\overline{\tau}_{1}-\overline{\tau}_{2})\cup\ker(\overline{\rho}\overline{\tau}_{1})\cup\ker(\overline{\tau}_{2})
gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\rho\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}=\mathcal{Y}_{\rho\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}\backslash\mathcal{Z}_{\rho\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}

which is an open set in the Zariski topology of
\mathcal{Y}_{\rho\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}
, also called quasi-variety. The following was proved in
[
1
,
2
]
: If for any
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\mathcal{T}
it holds that
\rank(\tau_{1}):=\rank(T_{1})\geq 2d
and
\rank(\tau_{2})\geq 2d
, and if there exists a linear projection
\rho
onto
\im(\tau_{2})
such that
d\leq\codim(\mathcal{U}_{\rho\tau_{1},\tau_{2}})
, then a generic subspace
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
of dimension
d
satisfies
\hsp(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{T})
; here generic is meant in the sense that
\hsp(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{T})
is true for every
\mathcal{V}
in a dense open set of the Grassmannian
\Gr_{\mathbb{H}}(d,n)
. In the same work, the dimension of the quasi-variety
\mathcal{U}_{\rho\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}
was calculated for the case of unlabeled sensing, leading to the same sufficient conditions as in
[
19
]
and
[
20
]
, mentioned above. Moreover, an
almost everywhere

\hsp(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{T})
type of result was proved: for a generic
\mathcal{V}
, as long as no two maps in
\mathcal{T}
are multiples of each other and
\rank(\tau)\geq d+1
for every
\tau\in\mathcal{T}
, then
\hsp(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T})
holds true, where
\mathcal{U}
is a Zariski dense open set of
\mathcal{V}
. This is a much easier result to obtain than the one asserting the homomorphic sensing property on the entire space
\mathcal{V}
.

2.2
Homomorphic sensing of a subspace arrangement

We extend Theorem
1
from a single subspace
\mathcal{V}
to a subspace arrangement
\mathcal{A}=(\mathcal{V}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{V}_{\ell})
, the latter being an ordered set of subspaces
\mathcal{V}_{i},\,i\in[\ell]:=\{1,\dots,\ell\}
of
\mathbb{H}^{n}

as the dimension configuration of
\mathcal{A}
. Thus, by a

\mathcal{A}
with dimension configuration

we mean a non-empty Zariski open subset of the product

of subsets of

. Each

gives rise to a subspace

2.3

For any (column) vector

define
\langle u,w\rangle:=u^{H}w

\|w\|_{2}:=\sqrt{\langle w,w\rangle}=\sqrt{w^{H}w}

We consider the homomorphic sensing problem in the presence of additive noise
\epsilon\in\mathbb{H}^{m}

v^{*}\in\mathcal{V}
and
\tau^{*}\in\mathcal{T}
set
y=\tau^{*}(v^{*})
and
\overline{y}=y+\epsilon
. We are interested in the optimization problem

\displaystyle(\hat{\tau},\hat{v})\in\mathop{\rm argmin}_{v\in\mathcal{V},\tau\in\mathcal{T}}\|\overline{y}-\tau(v)\|_{2}.

(3)

What can we say about the optimal solution
\hat{v}
? Under what conditions is
\hat{v}
close to
v^{*}
?

For a non-trivial subspace
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathbb{H}^{m}
and a non-zero
u\in\mathbb{H}^{m}

An algebraic variety is a subset of

\tau_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{j+1})=\tau_{2}(\mathcal{F}_{j+1})=\mathcal{G}_{j+1}
.

Proof 1

i) and ii) are directly by definition and so is
\mathcal{R}_{j+1}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j+1}\subset\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j}
. This latter implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{j+2}=\tau_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{j+1}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j+1})\cap\tau_{2}(\mathcal{R}_{j+1}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j+1})\subset\tau_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j})\cap\tau_{2}(\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j})=\mathcal{G}_{j+1}.

We now show that
\tau_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{j+1})=\mathcal{G}_{j+1}
. From
\mathcal{R}_{j+1}\subset\tau_{1}^{-1}(\mathcal{G}_{j+1})
we have
\tau_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{j+1})\subset\tau_{1}(\tau_{1}^{-1}(\mathcal{G}_{j+1}))\subset\mathcal{G}_{j+1}
. For the reverse direction
\mathcal{G}_{j+1}\subset\tau_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{j+1})
let
z\in\mathcal{G}_{j+1}=\tau_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j})\cap\tau_{2}(\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j})
. In particular
z\in\tau_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j})
so there is some
w\in\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j}
with
\tau_{1}(w)=z
. Then
w\in\tau_{1}^{-1}(z)\cap\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j}
. But
\tau_{1}^{-1}(z)\subset\tau_{1}^{-1}(\mathcal{G}_{j+1})
and so
w\in\tau_{1}^{-1}(\mathcal{G}_{j+1})\cap\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j}=\mathcal{R}_{j+1}
. Hence
z\in\tau_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{j+1})
. A similar derivation gives
\tau_{2}(\mathcal{F}_{j+1})=\mathcal{G}_{j+1}
. ∎

Lemma
6
gives two subspace chains

\cdots\subset\mathcal{R}_{j+1}\subset\mathcal{R}_{j}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{R}_{0}
and
\cdots\subset\mathcal{F}_{j+1}\subset\mathcal{F}_{j}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{F}_{0}
. These stabilize at a common subspace:

Lemma 7

There is a non-negative integer
\alpha
such that
\mathcal{R}_{j}=\mathcal{F}_{j}
for every
j\geq\alpha
and
\tau_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})=\tau_{2}(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})=\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
.

Proof 2

Since the subspaces
\mathcal{R}_{0}
and
\mathcal{F}_{0}
are of finite dimension
m
, both chains stabilize, that is, there exist non-negative integers
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
such that for any
j_{1}\geq\alpha_{1}
and
j_{2}\geq\alpha_{2}
we have
\mathcal{R}_{j_{1}}=\mathcal{R}_{j_{1}+1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{j_{2}}=\mathcal{F}_{j_{2}+1}
. Let
\alpha:=\max\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\}
. We then have
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{R}_{\alpha+1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{F}_{\alpha+1}
. Lemma
6
and the definition of
\alpha
give
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha+1}\subset\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}\subset\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{R}_{\alpha+1}
. This implies
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}
. Similarly we get
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}
. It follows that
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}
. The equality
\tau_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})=\tau_{2}(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})=\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
is immediate from Lemma
6
. ∎

The strategy of the proof is to make use of the
\mathcal{R}
chain ascending from
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}\subset\mathcal{R}_{\alpha-1}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\alpha-1}\subset\mathcal{R}_{\alpha-1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{R}_{1}\subset\mathcal{R}_{0}\cap\mathcal{F}_{0}\subset\mathcal{R}_{0}=\mathbb{C}^{n}

(6)

in the following fashion. First we show that there is always a
j_{0}
, such that either there is a subspace
\mathcal{W}_{j_{0}}\subset\mathcal{R}_{j_{0}}
of dimension
[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j_{0}})-(n-d)]
with
\dim(\tau_{1}(\mathcal{W}_{j_{0}})+\tau_{2}(\mathcal{W}_{j_{0}}))=2\dim(\mathcal{W}_{j_{0}})
or there is a subspace
\mathcal{Z}_{j_{0}}\subset\mathcal{R}_{j_{0}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j_{0}}
of dimension
[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j_{0}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j_{0}})-(n-d)]
with
\dim(\tau_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{j_{0}})+\tau_{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{j_{0}}))=2\dim(\mathcal{Z}_{j_{0}})
. Then we describe devices to pass either from
\mathcal{W}_{j_{0}}
to
\mathcal{Z}_{j_{0}-1}
or from
\mathcal{Z}_{j_{0}}
to
\mathcal{W}_{j_{0}-1}
, all the while preserving the properties i)
\dim(\tau_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{{j_{0}}-1})+\tau_{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{{j_{0}}-1}))=2\dim(\mathcal{Z}_{{j_{0}}-1})
and
\dim(\mathcal{Z}_{j_{0}-1})=[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j_{0}-1}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j_{0}-1})-(n-d)]
or ii)
\dim(\tau_{1}(\mathcal{W}_{{j_{0}}-1})+\tau_{2}(\mathcal{W}_{{j_{0}}-1}))=2\dim(\mathcal{W}_{{j_{0}}-1})
and
\dim(\mathcal{W}_{j_{0}-1})=[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j_{0}-1})-(n-d)]
. Then inductively

will satisfy the statement of the proposition since
\mathcal{R}_{0}=\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Below, we distinguish between three cases, in two out of which the existence of a

is proved while the third one proves the existence of a

. The next lemma handles the case where

can be taken to be

.

Lemma 8
(
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
-Initialization)

In addition to the hypotheses of Proposition
3
, suppose
\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})>n-d
. Then there is a subspace
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
of
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
of dimension
[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})-(n-d)]
such that
\dim(\tau_{1}(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha})+\tau_{2}(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}))=2\dim(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha})
.

Proof 3

By Lemma
7
and the rank-plus-nullity theorem we have

\displaystyle\dim(\ker(\tau_{1}|_{\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}}))=\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})-\dim(\mathcal{G}_{\alpha})=\dim(\ker(\tau_{2}|_{\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}}))

Moreover, by hypothesis
\rank(\tau_{1}|_{\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}})\geq\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})-(n-2d)>d
. Hence

(n-d)+\dim(\mathcal{G}_{\alpha})-\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})=\rank(\tau_{1}|_{\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}})-d>0

Since
\dim(\mathcal{U}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}^{\cl})=\dim(\mathcal{U}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}})\leq n-d
and
\dim(\mathscr{E}_{(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}),1})\leq n-d
as well, Lemma
2
gives a subspace
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
of dimension
\dim(\mathcal{G}_{\alpha})
, such that
\mathcal{H}\cap\ker(\tau_{1}|_{\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}})=\mathcal{H}\cap\ker(\tau_{2}|_{\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}})=0
and

\displaystyle\dim(\mathcal{U}^{\cl}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}\cap\mathcal{H})\leq(n-d)+\dim(\mathcal{G}_{\alpha})-\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})

(7)

\displaystyle\dim(\mathscr{E}_{(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}),1}\cap\mathcal{H})\leq(n-d)+\dim(\mathcal{G}_{\alpha})-\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})

(8)

Since
\tau_{1}(\mathcal{H})=\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}=\tau_{2}(\mathcal{H})
we have that
\tau_{\mathcal{H}}:=(\tau_{1}|_{\mathcal{H}})^{-1}\tau_{2}|_{\mathcal{H}}
is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{H}
. We are going to get our subspace
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
by applying Lemma
4
with ambient space
\mathcal{H}
,
\mathbb{C}
-linear map
\tau_{\mathcal{H}}
and subspace dimension
[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})-(n-d)]
, this number being positive by hypothesis. There are two things we need to check, the first being that
\dim(\mathcal{H})\geq 2[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})-(n-d)]
. Indeed, this is true because

\displaystyle\dim(\mathcal{H})\geq 2[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})-(n-d)]

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow 2n-2d\geq\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})+\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})-\dim(\mathcal{G}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow 2n-2d\geq\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})+\dim(\ker(\tau_{1}|_{\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}}))

\displaystyle\Leftarrow 2n-2d\geq\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})+\dim(\ker(\tau_{1}))

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(\rank(\tau_{1})-2d)+(n-\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}))\geq 0

and the last inequality is true by hypothesis. The second thing that we need to check is that for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle\dim(\mathscr{E}_{\tau_{\mathcal{H}},\lambda})\leq\dim(\mathcal{H})-[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})-(n-d)]

When
\lambda=0
, we have
\mathscr{E}_{\tau_{\mathcal{H}},0}=\ker(\tau_{\mathcal{H}})=0
, because
\tau_{\mathcal{H}}
is an isomorphism. When
\lambda\neq 0
,

v\in\mathscr{E}_{\tau_{\mathcal{H}},\lambda}
is equivalent to
\tau_{1}|_{\mathcal{H}}(v)=\lambda\tau_{2}|_{\mathcal{H}}(v)
or equivalently
v\in(\mathcal{U}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}\cap\mathcal{H})\cup(\mathscr{E}_{(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}),1}\cap\mathcal{H})
. This shows that
\mathscr{E}_{\tau_{\mathcal{H}},\lambda}
lives in
(\mathcal{U}_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}}^{\cl}\cap\mathcal{H})\cup(\mathscr{E}_{(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}),1}\cap\mathcal{H})
and as per (
7
) and (
8
) that latter set has dimension at most
\dim(\mathcal{H})-[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})-(n-d)]
.

Now Lemma
4
gives a subspace
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}
of
\mathcal{H}
of dimension
[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})-(n-d)]
such that
\dim(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}+\tau_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}))=2\dim(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha})
. Since
\tau_{1}|_{\mathcal{H}}
is an isomorphism from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}+\tau_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha})
is a subspace of
\mathcal{H}
, we have that
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}+\tau_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha})\cong\tau_{1}|_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}+\tau_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}))=\tau_{1}(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha})+\tau_{2}(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha})
. That is,
\dim(\tau_{1}(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha})+\tau_{2}(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha}))=2\dim(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha})
. ∎

If
\alpha=0
, then Proposition
3
is proved by Lemma
8
, so we assume
\alpha>0
for the sequel. If
\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})\leq n-d
, and since
\dim(\mathcal{R}_{0})=n
, then necessarily one of the two following cases must occur in the chain (
6
). Either there is a
\beta
with
\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\beta}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\beta})\leq n-d<\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\beta})
or there is a
\gamma
with
\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\gamma+1})\leq n-d<\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\gamma})
. The next two lemmas show how to choose
\mathcal{W}_{\beta}\subset\mathcal{R}_{\beta}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{\gamma}\subset\mathcal{R}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}
, respectively.

Lemma 9
(
\mathcal{W}_{\beta}
-Initialization)

In addition to the hypotheses of Proposition
3
, suppose
\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\beta}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\beta})\leq n-d<\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\beta})
for some non-negative integer
\beta
. Then there exists a subspace
\mathcal{W}_{\beta}
of
\mathcal{R}_{\beta}
of dimension
[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\beta})-(n-d)]
such that
\dim(\tau_{1}(\mathcal{W}_{\beta})+\tau_{2}(\mathcal{W}_{\beta}))=2\dim(\mathcal{W}_{\beta})
.

Proof 4

We have

\displaystyle[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\beta})-(n-d)]+\dim(\ker(\tau_{1}))=\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\beta})+(d-\rank(\tau_{1}))\leq\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\beta})

and a similar inequality for
\tau_{2}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\beta})-(n-d)]+\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\beta}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\beta})\leq\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\beta})+[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\beta}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\beta})-(n-d)]\leq\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\beta}).

Consequently, by Lemma
2
there exists a subspace
\mathcal{W}_{\beta}
of
\mathcal{R}_{\beta}
of dimension
[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\beta})-(n-d)]

\ker(\tau_{1}),\ker(\tau_{2})
and
\mathcal{R}_{\beta}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\beta}
. Clearly
\beta>0
and Lemma
6
gives
\tau_{1}(\mathcal{W}_{\beta})\subset\tau_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\beta})=\mathcal{G}_{\beta}
. Recalling definition (
5
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\beta}\cap\tau_{2}^{-1}(\tau_{1}(\mathcal{W}_{\beta}))

\displaystyle\subset\mathcal{W}_{\beta}\cap\tau_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{G}_{\beta})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}_{\beta}\cap\tau_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{G}_{\beta})\cap\mathcal{R}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}_{\beta}\cap\tau_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{G}_{\beta})\cap\tau_{1}^{-1}(\mathcal{G}_{\beta})\cap\mathcal{R}_{\beta-1}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\beta-1}

In short

, and it follows that
\tau_{2}(\mathcal{W}_{\beta})\cap\tau_{1}(\mathcal{W}_{\beta})=0
. Recalling that
\mathcal{W}_{\beta}\cap\ker(\tau_{1})=0
and
\mathcal{W}_{\beta}\cap\ker(\tau_{2})=0
, we conclude that
\dim(\tau_{1}(\mathcal{W}_{\beta})+\tau_{2}(\mathcal{W}_{\beta}))=2\dim(\mathcal{W}_{\beta})
. ∎

Lemma 10
(
\mathcal{Z}_{\gamma}
-Initialization)

In addition to the hypotheses of Proposition
3
, suppose that

\gamma
. Then there exists a subspace

\dim(\tau_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{\gamma})+\tau_{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{\gamma}))=2\dim(\mathcal{Z}_{\gamma})
.

Proof 5

We have

\displaystyle[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\gamma})-(n-d)]+\dim(\ker(\tau_{1}))=\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\gamma})+(d-\rank(\tau_{1}))\leq\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\gamma})

and a similar inequality for
\tau_{2}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\gamma})-(n-d)]+\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\gamma+1})=\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\gamma})+[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\gamma+1})-(n-d)]\leq\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}).

Thus Lemma
2
implies the existence of a subspace
\mathcal{Z}_{\gamma}
of
\mathcal{R}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}
of dimension
[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\gamma})-(n-d)]
which does not intersect

and
\mathcal{R}_{\gamma+1}
. This gives
\dim(\tau_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{\gamma}))=\dim(\tau_{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{\gamma}))=\dim(\mathcal{Z}_{\gamma})

\tau_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{\gamma})\cap\tau_{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{\gamma})=0

\tau_{1}(v_{1})=\tau_{2}(v_{2})
for some

. Then

\displaystyle\tau_{1}(v_{1})\in\tau_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\gamma})\cap\tau_{2}(\mathcal{R}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\gamma})=:\mathcal{G}_{\gamma+1}.

This implies

and so

\displaystyle v_{1}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\gamma}\cap\tau_{1}^{-1}(\mathcal{G}_{\gamma+1})=\mathcal{Z}_{\gamma}\cap\tau_{1}^{-1}(\mathcal{G}_{\gamma+1})\cap\mathcal{R}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}=\mathcal{Z}_{\gamma}\cap\mathcal{R}_{\gamma+1}=0.

Thus
v_{1}=0
and we have proved
\tau_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{\gamma})\cap\tau_{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{\gamma})=0
. ∎

Lemma 11
(
\mathcal{W}_{j}
-Extension)

In addition to the hypotheses of Proposition
3
, suppose for some
j
that
\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j})>n-d
and that there exists a subspace
\mathcal{Z}_{j}
of
\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j}
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{Z}_{j}):\ \dim(\mathcal{Z}_{j})=[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j})-(n-d)]\text{\ \ and \ }\dim(\tau_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{j})+\tau_{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{j}))=2\dim(\mathcal{Z}_{j}).

Then there exists a subspace
\mathcal{W}_{j}
of
\mathcal{R}_{j}
satisfying

and

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}(\mathcal{W}_{j}):\ \dim(\mathcal{W}_{j})=[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j})-(n-d)]\text{\ \ and \ }\dim(\tau_{1}(\mathcal{W}_{j})+\tau_{2}(\mathcal{W}_{j}))=2\dim(\mathcal{W}_{j}).

Proof 6

If
\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j}=\mathcal{R}_{j}
, then we are done by letting
\mathcal{W}_{j}=\mathcal{Z}_{j}
. In what follows we assume
\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j})>\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j})
, in particular

. The subspace
\tau_{1}^{-1}(\tau_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{j})+\tau_{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{j}))
has dimension at most
(n-\rank(\tau_{1}))+2[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j})-(n-d)]
. Hence

\displaystyle[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j})-\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j})]+\dim(\tau_{1}^{-1}(\tau_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{j})+\tau_{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{j})))

\displaystyle\leq\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j})+[2d-\rank(\tau_{1})]+[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j})-n]

\displaystyle\leq\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j}).

By hypothesis it is also true that
[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j})-\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j})]+\dim(\ker(\tau_{1}))\leq\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j})
and similarly for
\tau_{2}
. Hence, by Lemma
2
there is a subspace
\mathcal{W}_{j}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{R}_{j}
of dimension
[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j})-\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j})]
, which does not intersect the subspaces
\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j},\,\tau_{1}^{-1}(\tau_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{j})+\tau_{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{j})),\,\ker(\tau_{1}),\,\ker(\tau_{2})
. In particular
\tau_{1}(\mathcal{W}_{j}^{\prime})\cap[\tau_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{j})+\tau_{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{j})]=0
. This together with the hypothesis gives
\dim(\tau_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{j})+\tau_{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{j})+\tau_{1}(\mathcal{W}_{j}^{\prime}))=2[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j})-(n-d)]+[\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j})-\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j})]

\displaystyle\dim(\tau_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{j}+\mathcal{W}_{j}^{\prime})+\tau_{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{j}))=\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j})+\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j})-2(n-d).

(9)

\mathcal{Z}_{j}\subset\mathcal{R}_{j}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j}

\tau_{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{j})\subset\tau_{2}(\mathcal{F}_{j})=\mathcal{G}_{j}
. With
\tau_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{j}+\mathcal{W}_{j}^{\prime})\subset\tau_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{j})=\mathcal{G}_{j}

), we obtain that
\tau_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{j}+\mathcal{W}_{j}^{\prime})+\tau_{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{j})
is a subspace of
\mathcal{G}_{j}
, and consequently

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{j}^{\prime}\cap\tau_{2}^{-1}(\tau_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{j}+\mathcal{W}_{j}^{\prime})+\tau_{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{j}))

\displaystyle\subset\mathcal{W}_{j}^{\prime}\cap\tau_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{G}_{j})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}_{j}^{\prime}\cap\tau_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{G}_{j})\cap\mathcal{R}_{j}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}_{j}^{\prime}\cap\tau_{2}^{-1}(\mathcal{G}_{j})\cap\tau_{1}^{-1}(\mathcal{G}_{j})\cap\mathcal{R}_{j-1}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j-1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}_{j}^{\prime}\cap\tau_{1}^{-1}(\mathcal{G}_{j})\cap\mathcal{F}_{j}

\displaystyle\subset\mathcal{W}_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}_{j}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{F}_{j}\cap\mathcal{R}_{j}=0.

In short, we have
\mathcal{W}_{j}^{\prime}\cap\tau_{2}^{-1}(\tau_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{j}+\mathcal{W}_{j}^{\prime})+\tau_{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{j}))=0
and so
\tau_{2}(\mathcal{W}_{j}^{\prime})\cap[\tau_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{j}+\mathcal{W}_{j}^{\prime})+\tau_{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{j})]=0
. Recalling (

), it follows that

is of dimension

, that is,

\displaystyle\dim(\tau_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{j}+\mathcal{W}_{j}^{\prime})+\tau_{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{j}+\mathcal{W}_{j}^{\prime}))=2\dim(\mathcal{R}_{j})-2(n-d).

By letting

\overline{\mathscr{U}}\subset\overline{\F}_{\mathbb{R}}(d_{0},d,n)
, and the above arguments applied to
\overline{\phi}
show that
\overline{\phi}(\overline{\mathscr{U}})
contains a non-empty open subscheme
\overline{\mathscr{V}}
of
\overline{\Gr}_{\mathbb{R}}(d,n)
. Now
\overline{\Gr}_{\mathbb{R}}(d,n)
is locally isomorphic to the affine space
\mathbb{A}^{d(n-d)}=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{R}[Z])
, where
Z
is an
d\times(n-d)
matrix of variables
z_{ij}
and
\mathbb{R}[Z]
is the polynomial ring in the
z_{ij}
’s with coefficients over
\mathbb{R}
. So let
\overline{\mathscr{V}^{\prime}}
be an open subscheme of
\overline{\Gr}_{\mathbb{R}}(d,n)
isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{d(n-d)}
. Then
\overline{\mathscr{V}^{\prime\prime}}=\overline{\mathscr{V}^{\prime}}\cap\overline{\mathscr{V}}
is also open in
\overline{\Gr}_{\mathbb{R}}(d,n)

and in fact non-empty because
\overline{\Gr}_{\mathbb{R}}(d,n)
is irreducible. Under the isomorphism
\overline{\mathscr{V}^{\prime}}\cong\mathbb{A}^{d(n-d)}
we view
\overline{\mathscr{V}^{\prime\prime}}
as a non-empty open subscheme of
\mathbb{A}^{d(n-d)}
. Now
\overline{\mathcal{V}^{\prime\prime}}
can be written as
\bigcup_{p}\operatorname{Spec}(k[Z])_{p}
, with
p\in k[Z]
and
(k[Z])_{p}
the localization of
k[Z]
at the multiplicatively closed set
\{1,p,p^{2},\dots\}
. Since
\overline{\mathscr{V}^{\prime\prime}}
is non-empty, not all
p
’s are zero. Hence there is some non-zero
p
for which
\overline{\mathscr{V}^{\prime\prime\prime}}=\operatorname{Spec}(k[Z])_{p}
is a non-empty open subscheme of
\mathbb{A}^{d(n-d)}
. Let
\mathscr{U}^{\prime}
be the open set of points in
\mathbb{R}^{d(n-d)}
which are not roots of
p
. Since
\mathbb{R}
is infinite,
\mathscr{U}^{\prime}
is non-empty. Finally,
\mathscr{U}^{\prime}
lies in the image of
\phi
. ∎

5.2
Proof of Lemma
3

We assume familiarity with basic dimension theory in commutative algebra and algebraic geometry, for example see
[
42
]
and
[
44
]
respectively. Let
\mathfrak{R}:=\mathbb{C}[w_{1},\dots,w_{n}]
be a polynomial ring associated with
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and let
J
and
I
be the vanishing ideals of
\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{Y}
, respectively. Since
\mathcal{Z}\neq\{0\}
we have that
J
is properly contained in the ideal
(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
generated by the
w_{i}
’s. Let
\mathcal{U}
=
\mathcal{Y}\backslash\mathcal{Z}
. Then the vanishing ideal of the closure
\mathcal{U}^{\cl}
of
\mathcal{U}
is
\mathfrak{a}:=I:J^{\infty}
, where
\mathfrak{a}
is the saturation of
I
with respect to
J
. Hence we have
\dim(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{a})=\dim(\mathcal{U}^{\cl})=\dim(\mathcal{U})>n-d
. Since
I,J
are homogeneous so is
\mathfrak{a}
. Then for
n-d
generic linear forms
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{n-d}
of
\mathfrak{R}
we have

\displaystyle\dim(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{a}+(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{n-d}))=\dim(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{a})-(n-d)>0.

(11)

Geometrically, this means that the generic linear subspace
\mathcal{V}
defined as the common vanishing locus of the linear forms
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{n-d}
intersects
\mathcal{U}^{\cl}
at positive dimension, that is
\mathcal{V}\cap\mathcal{U}^{\cl}\supsetneq\{0\}
.
If
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}^{\cl}
we are done, so assume that
\mathcal{X}:=\mathcal{U}^{\cl}\backslash\mathcal{U}
is not empty. Since
\mathcal{U}
is open in
\mathcal{Y}
, we have that
\mathcal{X}
is closed in
\mathcal{U}^{\cl}
. Suppose that
\dim(\mathcal{X})=\dim(\mathcal{U}^{\cl})
. Let
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
be a maximal irreducible closed subset in
\mathcal{X}
. Then necessarily
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
is an irreducible component of
\mathcal{U}^{\cl}
. But
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{U}=\varnothing
, which contradicts the fact that
\mathcal{U}^{\cl}
is the smallest closed set that contain
\mathcal{U}
. We conclude that
\dim(\mathcal{X})<\dim(\mathcal{U}^{\cl})
. With
\mathfrak{b}
the vanishing ideal of
\mathcal{X}
, we have
\dim(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{a})>\dim(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{b})
. Let us show that
\mathfrak{b}
is homogeneous. For any
z\in\mathcal{X}\subset\mathcal{U}^{\cl}\subset\mathcal{Y}
, we have
\lambda z\in\mathcal{U}^{\cl}
for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
. Assume for the sake of contradiction that
\lambda^{\prime}z\in\mathcal{U}
for some
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}
. Note that
\lambda^{\prime}
can not be zero because
0\in\mathcal{Z}
. But
z\notin\mathcal{U}
implies
z\in\mathcal{Z}
and so
\lambda^{\prime}z\in\mathcal{Z}
, a contradiction. Taking again quotient by
n-d
generic linear forms we have

\displaystyle\dim(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{b}+(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{n-d}))=\max\{\dim(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{b})-(n-d),0\}.

(12)

Combining (
11
), (
12
) with
\dim(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{a})>\dim(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{b})
we get

\displaystyle\dim(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{a}+(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{n-d}))>\dim(\mathfrak{R}/\mathfrak{b}+(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{n-d})).

This implies
\dim(\mathcal{U}^{\cl}\cap\mathcal{V})>\dim(\mathcal{X}\cap\mathcal{V})
for a generic
\mathcal{V}\in\Gr_{\mathbb{C}}(d,n)
. Thus
\mathcal{V}
necessarily intersects
\mathcal{U}
. ∎

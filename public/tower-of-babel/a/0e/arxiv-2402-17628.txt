Geometry and Transcendence of the Hexponential
Scott Schmieding, Christopher-Lloyd Simon

Abstract
The modular group PSL 2 ⁡ ( ℤ ) \PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) acts on the upper-half plane ℍ ​ ℙ \mathbb{HP} with quotient the modular orbifold, uniformized by the 𝔧 \mathfrak{j} -function 𝔧 : ℍ ​ ℙ → ℂ \mathfrak{j}\colon\mathbb{HP}\to\mathbb{C} . We first show that second derived subgroup PSL 2 ⁡ ( ℤ ) ′′ \PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{\prime\prime} corresponds to a ℤ 2 ⋊ ℤ / 6 \mathbb{Z}^{2}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/6 Galois cover of the modular orbifold by a hexpunctured plane, uniformized by the hexponential map hexp : ℍ ​ ℙ → ℂ ∖ ( ω 0 ​ ℤ ​ [ j ] ) \hexp\colon\mathbb{HP}\to\mathbb{C}\setminus(\omega_{0}\mathbb{Z}[j]) , which is a primitive of C ​ η 4 C\eta^{4} where ω 0 ∈ i ​ ℝ \omega_{0}\in i\mathbb{R} and C ∈ ℝ C\in\mathbb{R} are explicit constants and η \eta is the Dedekind eta function. We describe the values of the cusp-compactification ∂ hexp : ℚ ​ ℙ 1 → ω 0 ​ ℤ ​ [ j ] \partial\hexp\colon\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\omega_{0}\mathbb{Z}[j] . After defining the radial-compactification Shexp : ℛ → ℝ / ( 2 ​ π ​ ℤ ) \Shexp\colon\mathscr{R}\to\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z}) , we construct a simple section InSh : ℝ / ( 2 ​ π ​ ℤ ) → 𝒮 mod PSL 2 ⁡ ( ℤ ) ′ \InSh\colon\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})\to\mathscr{S}\bmod{\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})^{\prime}} where 𝒮 ⊂ ℝ ​ ℙ 1 \mathscr{S}\subset\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1} is a set of numbers whose continued fraction expansions arise from Sturmian sequences, which contains the set ℳ \mathscr{M} of Markov quadratic irrationals as those numbers arising from periodic Sturmian sequences. We will show that the values of InSh \InSh are either Markov quadratic irrationals or transcendental. Finally we provide a continued fraction expansion for hexp \hexp , and discuss its monodromy.

Introduction

The paper unfolds the plan of the introduction.

0.1
The modular orbifold and euclidean continued fractions

The modular group
\Gamma=\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
acts by conformal transformations of the hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{HP}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\Im(z)>0\}
with boundary
\partial\mathbb{HP}=\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1}
, as the stabiliser of its ideal triangulation
\triangle
with vertices
\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}^{1}
and edges
\left(\frac{a}{c},\frac{b}{d}\right)
such that
ad-bc=1
. Let

\textstyle S=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\quad T=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\qquad L=T^{-1}S=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}\quad R=TS^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

The modular orbifold
\mathbb{M}=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{HP}
has genus zero, a cusp associated to the fixed point
\infty\in\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}^{1}\subset\partial\mathbb{HP}
of
R
, as well as two conical singularities of order
2
and
3
associated to the fixed points
i,j\in\mathbb{HP}
of
S
and
T
.
Thus
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(\mathbb{M})
is the free amalgam of its subgroups
\mathbb{Z}/2
and
\mathbb{Z}/3
generated by
S
and
T
.

The elements of
\Gamma
stabilising
[0,+\infty]\subset\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1}
form the euclidean monoid
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})
, which is freely generated by the parabolic matrices
R
and
L
.
The subset of sequences in
\{L,R\}^{\mathbb{N}}
which do not end with an infinite string of
L
are in bijective correspondence with the continued fraction expansions of positive numbers in
(0,+\infty]\subset\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1}
. The following result can be traced back to the works of Gauss
[
Gau07
]
and Galois
[
Gal29
]
.

Proposition 0.1

(Geodesics in
\mathbb{M}
)
.

A geodesic in
\mathbb{M}
has a lift
(\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+})\subset\mathbb{HP}
whose endpoints satisfy
-1<\alpha_{-}<0
and
1<\alpha_{+}<\infty
.
It intersects
\triangle
along a sequence of triangles whose
\{L,R\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
-encoding is obtained from the continued fraction expansions of
\alpha_{+}
and
-1/\alpha_{-}
by concatenating the transpose of the latter with the former.

The closed geodesics in
\mathbb{M}
correspond to the periodic sequences, hence to the
\Gamma
-orbits of pairs
(\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+})
of Galois conjugate quadratic irrationals.

0.2
The modular torus and Sturmian numbers

The derived subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}
of
\Gamma
, which is freely generated by
X=LR
and
Y=RL
, corresponds to a Galois cover of
\mathbb{M}
by a punctured torus
\mathbb{M}^{\prime}
with group
\Gamma/\Gamma^{\prime}=\mathbb{Z}/6
.
The simple geodesics of
\mathbb{M}^{\prime}
were described by Cohn
[
Coh71
]
and Series
[
Ser85a
]
.

Proposition 0.2

(Simple geodesics in
\mathbb{M}^{\prime}
)
.

Fix
-1/\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
and consider the
L\&R
-sequence obtained by concatenating their (transposed) continued fractions.

The geodesic
(\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+})\subset\mathbb{HP}
projects to a geodesic in
\mathbb{M}^{\prime}
which is simple if and only if the
L\&R
can be paired to form an
X\&Y
-sequence which is Sturmian.

The simple closed geodesics in
\mathbb{M}^{\prime}
correspond combinatorially to the periodic Sturmian
X\&Y
-sequences, and arithmetically to the Markov quadratic irrationals.

The conformal structure on
\mathbb{M}^{\prime}
is given by a punctured elliptic curve isomorphic over
\mathbb{R}
to the affine cubic
y^{2}=(x-\omega_{0}j^{0})(x-\omega_{0}j^{1})(x-\omega_{0}j^{2})
with abelian differential
dx/y=-dx/\sqrt{x^{3}-\rvert\omega_{0}\rvert^{3}}
having fundamental period
\omega_{0}=\frac{2\pi^{1/2}}{i3^{1/4}}
.

This abelian differential on
\mathbb{M}^{\prime}
lifts on the universal cover
\mathbb{HP}
to
C\eta^{4}(\tau)d\tau
for some
C\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
where
\eta
is the Dedekind eta function, whose expansion in
q=e^{i2\pi\tau}
:

\eta(\tau)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\chi(n)q^{\tfrac{1}{24}n^{2}}\quad\mathrm{yields}\quad\eta^{4}(\tau)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\psi(n)q^{\tfrac{1}{24}n}

where
\chi
is the unique primitive character
\bmod{12}
(that is the Jacobi symbol
\bmod{12}
, equal to
\chi(12n\pm 1)=1
,
\chi(12n\pm 5)=-1
and
\chi(n)=0
if
2\mid n
or
3\mid n
), and where
\psi(n)
is the sum of
\chi(abcd)
over
\{(a,b,c,d)\in\mathbb{N}^{4}\mid a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}+d^{2}=n\}
.

0.3
The hexpunctured plane and its cusp compactifications

The second derived subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\subset\Gamma^{\prime}
corresponds to the universal abelian cover
\mathbb{M}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathbb{M}^{\prime}
of the punctured torus by a lattice-punctured plane with Galois group
\Gamma^{\prime}/\Gamma^{\prime\prime}=\mathbb{Z}_{X}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{Y}
.
The action of the Galois group
\Gamma/\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
on the corresponding cover
\mathbb{M}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathbb{M}
was described in the second author’s thesis
[
Sim22
, Section 3.2]
.

Proposition 0.3

(Hexagonal lattice)
.

We have a semi-direct decomposition

\Gamma/\Gamma^{\prime\prime}=\Gamma^{\prime}/\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\rtimes\Gamma/\Gamma^{\prime}

where the action of the quotient
\Gamma/\Gamma^{\prime}=\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/3
by outer-automophisms of the kernel
\Gamma^{\prime}/\Gamma^{\prime\prime}=\mathbb{Z}_{X}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{Y}
is given by
\AD_{S}=D_{S^{2}}=D_{-\mathfrak{1}}
and
\AD_{T}=D_{T^{2}}
since we have
SXS^{-1}=X^{-1}
,
SYS^{-1}=Y^{-1}
and
TXT^{-1}=Y^{-1}
,
TYT^{-1}=-XY^{-1}
.

This represents
\Gamma/\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
as the affine isometry group of the oriented hexagonal lattice
H_{1}(\Gamma^{\prime};\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}_{X}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{Y}
with
angle{(X,Y)}=\frac{2\pi}{6}
, where the translation action of
\Gamma^{\prime}/\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
is by
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-translation while the outer-automorphism action of
\Gamma/\Gamma^{\prime}
is by
\mathbb{Z}/6
-rotation.

The integration of abelian differential on
\mathbb{M}^{\prime}
identifies its universal abelian cover
\mathbb{M}^{\prime\prime}
with
H_{1}(\mathbb{M}^{\prime};\mathbb{R})\setminus H_{1}(\mathbb{M}^{\prime};\mathbb{Z})
, hence with the hexpunctured plane
\mathbb{C}\setminus\omega_{0}\mathbb{Z}[j]
.

The map
\hexp\colon\mathbb{HP}\to\mathbb{M}^{\prime\prime}
in the coordinates
\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\Im(z)>0\}\to\mathbb{C}\setminus\omega_{0}\mathbb{Z}[j]
is therefore the primitive of
C\eta(z)^{4}dz
, with expansion:

\hexp(\tau)=\int_{\infty}^{\tau}C\eta^{4}(z)dz=\frac{12C}{i\pi}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\psi(n)}{n}\cdot q^{\tfrac{1}{24}n}

We deduce the value of
C=\frac{2^{10/3}}{3^{3/4}}\frac{\pi^{5/2}}{\Upgamma(1/3)^{3}}
from the relation
\omega_{0}=\int_{\infty}^{0}C\eta^{4}(\tau)d\tau
.

The main results in this paper concern arithmetic and geometric properties of the uniformizing map
\hexp\colon\mathbb{HP}\to\mathbb{M}^{\prime\prime}
, and its compactifications.

We first describe the values of the cusp compactification
\partial\hexp\colon\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\omega_{0}\mathbb{Z}[j]
.

Theorem 0.4

(Values of
\partial\hexp
)
.

Consider
a/c\in\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}^{1}
with
\gcd(a,b)=1
.

There exists
A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma^{\prime}
, and we may consider its class
A\equiv X^{m}Y^{n}\mod{\Gamma^{\prime\prime}}
.
The value
\partial\hexp(\frac{a}{c})
of the improper integral and conditionally convergent series:

\partial\hexp(\tfrac{a}{c})=\int_{\infty}^{\frac{a}{c}}C\eta^{4}(z)dz=\tfrac{12C}{i\pi}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\tfrac{\psi(n)}{n}\cdot\exp\left(\tfrac{i\pi}{12}\tfrac{a}{c}n\right)\quad\text{where}\quad C=\tfrac{2^{10/3}}{3^{3/4}}\tfrac{\pi^{5/2}}{\Upgamma(1/3)^{3}}

\text{is equal to}\quad\partial\hexp(\tfrac{a}{c})=\lvert\omega_{0}\rvert\left(m\exp(-\tfrac{i\pi}{6})+n\exp(+\tfrac{i\pi}{6})\right)\quad\text{where}\quad\lvert\omega_{0}\rvert=\tfrac{2\pi^{1/2}}{3^{1/4}}.

The function
\partial\hexp\colon\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{C}
is a modular symbol for the group
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
. Modular symbols were introduced by Manin in
[
Man72
]
for the groups
\Gamma_{0}(N)
, and have since been the source of various generalisations and extensive studies (see
[
MM02
,
Man09
]
).

Remark 0.5

(Rational correlations)
.

The conditional convergence of the series
\partial\hexp(r)
at rationals
r\in\mathbb{Q}
implies a strong correlation between
n\mapsto\psi(n)
and periodic rotations of the circle
n\mapsto n+r\bmod{1}
.
These correlations factor through the cosets
r\in\Gamma^{\prime\prime}\backslash\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and their values are organised according to the lattice
\omega_{0}\mathbb{Z}[j]
.

This may be equivalent to some kind of "Dirichlet equidistribution theorem" for the arithmetic progressions represented by a
\chi
-twisted version of the quadratic form
a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}+d^{2}
or the divisor function
\sum_{d\mid n}d
.

0.4
Transcendent arguments of the hexponential map

We then define the ray compactification
\Shexp\colon\mathscr{R}\to\mathbb{S}H_{1}(\mathbb{M}^{\prime};\mathbb{R})
.
Let
\mathscr{R}
be the set of numbers
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1}
such that as
\tau\in\mathbb{HP}
diverges to
\alpha\in\partial\mathbb{HP}
, the modulus
\lvert\hexp(\tau)\rvert\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
diverges while the argument
\arg\hexp(\tau)\in\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})
converges.
Thus
\alpha\in\mathscr{R}
when the geodesic
(i,\alpha)\subset\mathbb{HP}
maps under
\hexp
to a geodesic of
\mathbb{M}^{\prime\prime}
which escapes towards a definite direction in
\mathbb{S}H_{1}(\mathbb{M}^{\prime};\mathbb{R})
: that is
\Shexp(\alpha)
.

Remark 0.6

(Singularities along the boundary)
.

We have
\mathscr{R}\subset\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
.
If
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1}
is neither rational nor quadratic then
\hexp(\tau)
diverges as
\tau\in\mathbb{HP}
diverges to
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Suppose
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a fixed point of
A\in\Gamma
: if
A^{6}\notin\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
then
\alpha\in\mathscr{R}
, but if
A^{6}\in\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
then
\alpha\notin\mathscr{R}
yet
\lim_{\alpha}\hexp(\tau)=\partial\hexp(r)
for some
r\in\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

The subset
\mathscr{R}\subset\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1}
is
\Gamma^{\prime}
-invariant,
\Gamma^{\prime}/\Gamma
equivariant, and dense.

The map
\Shexp\colon\mathscr{R}\to\mathbb{S}H_{1}(\mathbb{M}^{\prime},\mathbb{R})
is uniformly continuous and surjective.
It sends sends uncountably many distinct
\Gamma^{\prime}
-orbits in
\mathscr{R}
to the same slope.

The surjective maps
\Shexp\colon\mathscr{R}\to\mathbb{S}H_{1}(\mathbb{M}^{\prime};\mathbb{R})
and
\partial\hexp\colon\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}^{1}\to H_{1}(\mathbb{M}^{\prime};\mathbb{Z})
have disjoint domains. Together, they define (for obvious topologies) a continuous compactification of
\hexp\colon\mathbb{HP}\to\mathbb{M}^{\prime\prime}
, equivariant under the left actions of
\Gamma
and
\Gamma/\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
.
The translation action of
\Gamma^{\prime}
on
\mathbb{S}H_{1}(\mathbb{M}^{\prime};\mathbb{R})
is trivial, so we have an equivariant map
\Shexp\colon\mathscr{R}\bmod{\Gamma^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{S}H_{1}(\mathbb{M}^{\prime};\mathbb{R})
under the left actions of
\Gamma
and
\Gamma/\Gamma^{\prime}
.

Finally, we define a simple section
\InSh\colon\mathbb{S}H_{1}(\mathbb{M}^{\prime};\mathbb{R})\to\mathscr{S}\bmod{\Gamma^{\prime}}
of
\Shexp
which is
\Gamma/\Gamma^{\prime}
-equivariant and injective. A slope
(x,y)\in\mathbb{S}H_{1}(\mathbb{M}^{\prime};\mathbb{R})
corresponds to a complete simple geodesic
\alpha\subset\mathbb{M}^{\prime}
: it lifts in
\mathbb{HP}
to the
\Gamma^{\prime}
-orbit of a geodesic
(\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+})
and we let
\InSh(x,y)=\Gamma^{\prime}\cdot\alpha_{+}
.

We denote by
\mathscr{S}
the set of
Sturmian
numbers
-1/\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}
obtained in this way (namely whose continued fractions arise from a Sturmian
X^{\pm 1}\&Y^{\pm 1}
-sequence by replacing
X=LR
and
Y=RL
).
In contains the subset
\mathscr{M}\subset\mathscr{S}
of
Markov
numbers as those arising from rational slopes
(x,y)\in\mathbb{S}H_{1}(\mathbb{M}^{\prime};\mathbb{Z})
, namely those with periodic
X^{\pm 1}\&Y^{\pm 1}
sequences (coinciding with the Markov quadratic irrationals).

Remark 0.7

(Cantor set)
.

The set
\mathscr{S}\subset\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1}
is compact, with a dense countably infinite set of isolated points whose complement is a Cantor set. By
[
Pc65
]
such properties describe a unique space up to homeomorphism.
It has Hausdorff dimension zero.
The map
\Shexp\colon\mathscr{S}\to\mathbb{S}H_{1}(\mathbb{M}^{\prime};\mathbb{R})
is continuous and surjective.

Question 0.8

(Sturmian correlations)
.

We observe that the sequence
\psi(n)/n
bears very strong (lattice-like) correlations with rational numbers (bounded complexity), and rather strong (Cantor-like) correlations with Sturmian numbers (complexity
l+1
), and some correlations with the numbers in
\mathscr{R}
.

We expect that one may characterise the subset of Sturmian numbers
\mathscr{S}\subset\mathscr{R}
by quantifying how fast
\arg\hexp(\theta)
converges to
\Shexp(\alpha)
.
We wonder if one can provide any arithmetic or diophantine interpretation of the numbers in
\mathscr{R}
.

Theorem 0.9

(Transcendence of the Inshection map)
.

For
(x,y)\in\mathbb{S}H_{1}(\mathbb{M};\mathbb{R})

•

(x,y)\in\mathbb{S}H_{1}(\mathbb{M}^{\prime};\mathbb{Z})

1.2
Euclidean monoid and continued fraction expansions

The monoid
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{N})\subset\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is freely generated by the transvections
L
and
R
, and
it identifies with its image
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})\subset\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
which we call the euclidean monoid.

In
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, the conjugacy classes of torsion elements are those of
\mathfrak{1},S,T,T^{-1}
, whereas the conjugacy class of an infinite order element intersects the euclidean monoid
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})
along the cyclic permutations of a unique
L\&R
-word.
Hence the hyperbolic geodesics of
\mathbb{M}
are indexed by cyclic words in
L\&R
containing both letters.

Observe that for
A\in\SL_{2}
, its transpose
A^{\dagger}
and inverse
A^{-1}
are conjugate by
S
. Hence the transposition of cyclic words in
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})
corresponds to inversion of the associated conjugacy classes in
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Every positive real number
x
admits a Euclidean continued fraction expansion:

\lfloor\,{n_{0},n_{1},\dots}\,\rfloor=n_{0}+\frac{1}{n_{1}+\dots}\quad\mathrm{with}\quad n_{j}\in\mathbb{N}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\forall j>0,\;n_{j}>0.

Such an expansion is infinite if and only if
x
is irrational, in which case it is unique. A rational
x
has two expansions
x=\lfloor\,{n_{0},\dots,n_{k}}\,\rfloor
: one for which
n_{k}=1
, the other for which
n_{k}>1
, and exactly one of these has even length
k+1
.

To represent negative real numbers we apply the involution
x\mapsto S(x)=-1/x
.
This corresponds to the partition
\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1}=[0,\infty)\sqcup\,S\cdot[0,\infty)
.
Thus every
x\in\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}^{1}
admits exactly one representation of the form
x=\lfloor\,{n_{0},\dots}\,\rfloor
or

4.1
The Inshection map and Sturmian numbers

We defined a surjective map
\Shexp\colon\mathscr{R}\bmod{\Gamma^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{S}H_{1}(\mathbb{M}^{\prime};\mathbb{R})
where
\mathscr{R}\subset\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{P}^{1}

\Gamma^{\prime}

\mathscr{R}
to the same slope in
\mathbb{S}H_{1}(\mathbb{M}^{\prime};\mathbb{R})

Let us define a section

\Gamma/\Gamma^{\prime}
-equivariant, injective and whose image will have “index at most
2
” in the set
\mathscr{S}

A slope
(x,y)\in\mathbb{S}H_{1}(\mathbb{M}^{\prime};\mathbb{R})

\mathbb{HP}
to the
\Gamma^{\prime}

\mathbb{M}^{\prime\prime}

The

\Gamma^{\prime}/\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
-orbit if and only if they differ by a shift.
Replacing

,

Let us remark that in

the set of
Sturmian

\alpha_{+},-1/\alpha_{-}

Remark 4.1

4.2
Diophantine approximation of Sturmian numbers

For a real
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, its Lagrange constant
\Lag(\alpha)
is the supremum of
L\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that there are infinitely many
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{N}^{*}
with
\lvert\alpha-p/q\rvert<1/(Lq^{2})
, namely:

\textstyle\tfrac{1}{\Lag(\alpha)}=\liminf_{q}({q^{2}\lvert\alpha-p/q\rvert})

It may be expressed in terms of the continued fraction expansion
\alpha=\lfloor\,{a_{0},a_{1},\dots}\,\rfloor
:

\textstyle\Lag(\alpha)=\limsup_{n}({\lfloor\,{0,a_{n-1},\dots,a_{0}}\,\rfloor+\lfloor\,{a_{n},a_{n+1},\dots}\,\rfloor})

In particular
\Lag(\alpha)=0\iff\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
whereas
\Lag(\alpha)=+\infty\iff\limsup_{n}(a_{n})=+\infty
.
Moreover, for
\alpha\notin\mathbb{Q}
we have
\Lag(\alpha)\geq\limsup_{n}\left(\lfloor\,{0,1,\dots,1}\,\rfloor+\lfloor\,{1,1,\dots}\,\rfloor\right)=\sqrt{5}
.

In
\mathbb{M}
, the horoball neighborhood
\mathbb{B}(h)
of the cusp
\infty
at height
h\geq 1
is the image of the projection
\{\Im(z)>h\}\subset\mathbb{HP}
, which has area
1/h
.
We will also consider its lift in
\mathbb{M}^{\prime}
which has area
6/h
, as the cover
\mathbb{M}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{M}
restricts to a degree
6
cover of punctured discs between these cusp neighborhoods.

The geodesic
(\infty,\alpha)\in\mathbb{HP}
projects to a geodesic in
\mathbb{M}^{\prime}
whose penetrations into
\mathbb{B}(h)
are indexed by the
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\lfloor\,{0,a_{n-1},\dots,a_{0}}\,\rfloor+\lfloor\,{a_{n},a_{n+1},\dots}\,\rfloor\right)>h
.

For distinct
\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}\in\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we define the Markov-Cohn constant
\Cohn(\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+})
as the supremum of
2h\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
\mathbb{B}(h)
is disjoint from the geodesic
(\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+})\subset\mathbb{M}
.

When
-1<\alpha_{-}\leq 0
and
1\leq\alpha_{+}<\infty
have continued fraction expansions
-1/\alpha_{-}=\lfloor\,{a_{-1},a_{-2},\dots}\,\rfloor
and
\alpha_{+}=\lfloor\,{a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\dots}\,\rfloor
, the geodesic
(\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+})\subset\mathbb{HP}
intersects
\triangle
according to the sequence of
a_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
It projects to a geodesic in
\mathbb{M}
which penetrates
\mathbb{B}(h)
each time
\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\lfloor\,{0,a_{n-1},a_{n-2},\dots}\,\rfloor+\lfloor\,{a_{n},a_{n+1},\dots}\,\rfloor\right)>h
, thus

\textstyle\Cohn(\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+})=\sup_{n}(\lfloor\,{0,a_{n-1},a_{n-2},\dots}\,\rfloor+\lfloor\,{a_{n},a_{n+1},\dots}\,\rfloor)

[

]
building on work of Cohn
[

]
.

(Spectrum of Sturmian numbers)

For distinct

Transcendence of the Inshection and hexponential

We define the subset
\mathscr{M}\subset\mathscr{S}
of
Markov
numbers as those arising from rational slopes

[

]

Theorem 4.4

(Transcendence of the Inshection map)
.

For
(x,y)\in\mathbb{S}H_{1}(\mathbb{M};\mathbb{R})
,

•

if
(x,y)\in\mathbb{S}H_{1}(\mathbb{M}^{\prime};\mathbb{Z})
is rational then
\InSh(x,y)\subset\mathscr{M}
are quadratic

•

if
(x,y)\notin\mathbb{S}H_{1}(\mathbb{M}^{\prime};\mathbb{Z})
is irrational then
\InSh(x,y)\subset\mathscr{S}\setminus\mathscr{M}
are transcendental

Proof.

If
(x,y)\in\mathbb{S}H_{1}(\mathbb{M}^{\prime};\mathbb{Z})
is rational then every number in
\InSh(x,y)
has a continued fraction expansion which is eventually periodic, so it is quadratic.
If
(x,y)\notin\mathbb{S}H_{1}(\mathbb{M}^{\prime};\mathbb{Z})
is irrational, then every number in
\InSh(x,y)
has a continued fraction expansion which is an eventually aperiodic Sturmian sequence over
\{1,2\}
, so it is transcendental by
[
ADQZ01
, Proposition 3 and Theorem 7]
.

The strategy in
[
ADQZ01
]
consists of showing that a non-Markov Sturmian number is very well approximated by Markov numbers, well enough to satisfy the hypotheses of a theorem of Schmidt
[
Sch67
]
, which can be stated as follows. The height of a quadratic irrational
\xi
with minimal polynomial
ax^{2}+bx+c\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
is defined by
H(\xi)=\gcd(\lvert a\rvert,\lvert b\rvert,\lvert c\rvert)
.
A number
s\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
which is not quadratic must be transcendental, if for some number
\beta>3
there exists infinitely many quadratic irrationals
\xi_{k}
such that
\lvert\xi-\xi_{k}\rvert<H(\xi_{k})^{-\beta}
.
∎

\int_{\infty}^{\tau}\eta^{4}(z)dz=\tfrac{3}{i2\pi}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda\right)^{1/3}\cdot{}_{2}F_{1}(1/3,2/3,4/3;\lambda)

Appendix A
Simple geodesics in other Riemann surfaces

A.1
Arithmetical and dynamical simplicity of geodesics

Let us announce a conjecture, part of which we will prove in a forthcoming paper.

Conjecture A.1

(Arithmetic-Dynamic simplicity)
.

Consider a lattice
F\subset\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
with invariant trace field
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}(\{\disc(\gamma)\mid\gamma\in F\})
and assume that the entries of
F
belong to
\mathbb{K}
(this may be achieved by a conjugacy in
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
). Let
S=F\backslash\mathbb{HP}
.

Fix a complete simple geodesic
\alpha\subset S
and choose any lift
(\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+})\subset\mathbb{HP}
where
\alpha_{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{1}
are its endpoint.
We conjecture that the following trichotomy holds:

1
:

If
\alpha
is forward asymptotic to a cusp, then
\alpha_{+}
belongs to
\mathbb{K}
.

2
:

If
\alpha
is forward asymptotic to a closed geodesic, then
\alpha_{+}
is quadratic over
\mathbb{K}
.

\infty
:

Otherwise,
\alpha_{+}
is transcendental over
\mathbb{K}
.

Of course, this would imply the same result exchanging
\alpha_{+}
for
\alpha_{-}
and forwards asymptotic with backwards asymptotic.

Note that the statement does not depend on the choice of the lift, since all the lifts differ by elements in
F\subset\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{K})
.

The points
1
and
2
are easy to prove, the heart of the conjecture lies in
\infty
.

Remark A.2

(Heuristics on arithmetic-dynamic complexity)
.

This theorem and the ideas involved in the proof lead to the following heuristic principle.

Objects with low dynamical and arithmetic complexity follow a dichotomy: they present either very algebraic features or very transcendent features.

Such dichotomy results appear as a leitmotiv in transcendental function theory.
One expects that a power series with bounded dynamical complexity (say on the dimension of the vector spaces generated by its derivatives) and bounded arithmetic complexity (say on the growth of the algebraic heights of its coefficients) either defines an algebraic function and its values at algebraic numbers are all algebraic or else defines a very transcendent function in the sense that its values at almost all algebraic numbers are transcendent.

Striking examples are the Siegel-Shidlovskii theorem improved by Beukers in
[
Beu06
]
or the Shneider-Lang theorem which is presented in
[
Lan71
, (3.1)]
.

This leitmotiv will appear in the subsections.

Now suppose that

Let us comment on some approaches which have been proposed, and relate them to this work.

Appendix B
On the Fourier coefficients of the hexponential

Let us collect some well known facts about the sequence

.
Our main reference are
[
Lig75
,
Ser85b
]
and
[
LMF24
]

B.1
Modularity and growth of the coefficients

The
\psi(n)
correspond to the Fourier coefficients of the weight
2
modular form
\eta^{4}(6\tau)
, which is
[
LMF24
,
Modular Form 32.2.a.a
]
, the unique normalised Hecke cusp form for the group
\Gamma_{0}(36)=\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma\mid c\equiv 0\bmod{36}\}
.
It has an associated Dirichlet series
L(\psi,s)=\sum_{1}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{n^{s}}\psi(n)
.
Let us recall the following from
[
Lig75
]
.

Théorème B (2.2.3)

The Dirichlet series
L(\psi,s)
attached to the group
\Gamma_{0}(36)
coincides with the
L
-series attached to the elliptic modular curve
X_{0}(36)
(with Weierstrass form
y^{2}=x^{3}-1
, having complex-multiplication by
\mathbb{Z}[j]
).

Théorème C (2.3.2)

The function
\Xi(s)=\left(\tfrac{6}{2\pi}\right)^{s}\Upgamma(s)L(\psi,s)
admits an analytic continuation to an entire function which is bounded in the vertical strip and satisfies the functional equation
\Xi(s)=\Xi(2-s)
.

Théorème F (6.3.3)

The analytic continuation of the function
L(\psi,s)
converges at
s=1
to
a constant divided by the square of the order of its group of rational points
X_{0}(36)(\mathbb{Q})
, which is finite by Théorème E.

In particular the sequence
\psi(n)
satisfies the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture
[
Del71
]
saying that
\lvert\psi(p)\rvert\leq 2\sqrt{p}
for all prime
p\in\mathbb{N}
.

B.2
L-series and Euler product

The ring of integers
\mathbb{Z}[j]
of the quadratic field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})
has Euclidean algorithms, in particular it is a principal ideal domain, hence a unique factorisation domain.

The ramification pattern of a prime
p\in\mathbb{Z}
in
\mathbb{Z}[j]
is given by its residue
p\bmod{3}
as follows.
The prime
3
is ramified as
3=-(1+j)^{2}
.
When
p=-1\bmod{3}
it is inert, namely it remains prime in
\mathbb{Z}[j]
, and has norm
p^{2}
.
When
p=+1\bmod{3}
it splits in
\mathbb{Z}[j]
as a product
p=\mathfrak{p}\bar{\mathfrak{p}}
of conjugate primes with
\Norm(\mathfrak{p})=p
.

The
Hecke Grossencharacter

\mathfrak{c}
of discriminant
-3
and conductor
2\sqrt{-3}
is a character on the multiplicative group of fractional ideals of
\mathbb{Q}(j)
coprime to the conductor
2\sqrt{-3}
defined as follows.
For an ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathbb{Z}[j]
coprime to
2\sqrt{-3}
, we have
\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{a})=\alpha
where
\alpha
is the unique generator of
\mathfrak{a}
such that
\alpha=1\bmod{2\sqrt{-3}}
.
In particular for a prime
p\in\mathbb{Z}
coprime to
6
we have
\mathfrak{c}(p)=\pm 1
according to whether
p=\pm 1\bmod{3}
.

We have the
L
-series representation where the product is over all primes
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathbb{Z}[j]
coprime to the conductor
2\sqrt{-3}
:

L(\psi,s)=\sum_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\psi(n)}{n^{s}}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\frac{1}{1-\mathfrak{c}(p)\Norm(p)^{s}}

One may rewrite this as the product over all primes
p\in\mathbb{Z}
coprime to
6
:

L(\psi,s)=\sum_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\psi(n)}{n^{s}}=\prod_{p}L_{p}(\psi,s)

where the local factors are given by
L_{p}(\psi,s)=(1+p\cdot p^{-2s})^{-1}
if
p\neq 2
is inert and
L_{p}(\psi,s)=(1-(\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{p})+\mathfrak{c}(\bar{\mathfrak{p}}))p^{-s}+p\cdot p^{-2s})^{-1}
if
p=\mathfrak{p}\bar{\mathfrak{p}}
is split.
Hence the multiplicative function
\psi\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{Z}
is determined on powers of primes
p^{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
by:

-

If
p\in\{2,3\}
then
\psi(p^{n})=0
.

-

If
p\neq 2
is inert then
\psi(p^{n})\in\{0,1\}
is equal to the parity
n\bmod{2}
.

-

If
p=\mathfrak{p}\bar{\mathfrak{p}}
splits then
\psi(p^{n})=\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{p})^{n}+\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{p})^{n-1}\mathfrak{c}(\bar{\mathfrak{p}})+\dots+\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{p})\mathfrak{c}(\bar{\mathfrak{p}})^{n-1}+\mathfrak{c}(\bar{\mathfrak{p}})^{n}

B.3

The
[
Ser85b

For

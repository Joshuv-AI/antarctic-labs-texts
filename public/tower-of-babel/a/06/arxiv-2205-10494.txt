Weak anisotropic Hardy inequality: essential self-adjointness of drift-diffusion operators on domains in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , revisited
Gheorghe Nenciu, Irina Nenciu

Abstract
. We consider the problem of essential self-adjointness of the drift-diffusion operator H = − 1 ρ ∇ ⋅ ρ 𝔻 ∇ + V H=-\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla\cdot\rho\mathbb{D}\nabla+V on domains Ω ⊂ ℝ d \Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d} with 𝒞 2 {\mathcal{C}}^{2} -boundary ∂ Ω \partial\Omega and for large classes of coefficients ρ , 𝔻 \rho,\;{\mathbb{D}} and V V . We give criteria showing how the behavior as x → ∂ Ω x\rightarrow\partial\Omega of these coefficients balances to ensure essential self-adjointness of H H . On the way we prove a weak anisotropic Hardy inequality which is of independent interest.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we revisit the problem of essential self-adjointness for a drift-diffusion operator on domains in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
More precisely, we consider the operator

(1.1)

H=-\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla\cdot\rho\mathbb{D}\nabla+V

on a domain
\Omega\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, where
\rho:\Omega\rightarrow(0,\infty)
,

{\mathbb{D}}:\Omega\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}
is a strictly positive definite matrix valued
function, and
V:\Omega\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
.

The self-adjointness problem for second order elliptic partial differential operators
on domains in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(or more general on Riemannian manifolds) has a long and ramified history.
In the 1-dimensional case, the problem is well understood due to the powerful tools of ordinary differential equations theory,
especially Weyl limit point/circle theory
[
26
]
,
[
30
]
. The multi-dimensional case has been also much studied
and we send the reader to
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
26
]
for references, heuristics, and a detailed account.
For more recent developments see
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
25
]
,

[
27
]
,
[
28
]
.

Assuming enough regularity of the coefficients,
H
is symmetric in
L^{2}\big(\Omega;\rho\,dx\big)

on
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
and as it is well known (see e.g.
[
11
]
) the essential self-adjointness
of
H
is dictated only by their behavior near the boundary,
\partial\Omega
of
\Omega
.
Hence the problem boils down to finding conditions on the behavior of its coefficients as
x\rightarrow\partial\Omega

ensuring essential self-adjointness of
H
.
The case which is well understood is when
{\mathbb{D}}
is strongly degenerate, i.e. it decays sufficiently quickly as

x\rightarrow\partial\Omega
so that
\Omega
endowed with the distance

(1.2)

ds^{2}=\sum_{j,k=1}^{d}{\mathbb{D}}(x)^{-1}_{j,k}dx_{j}dx_{k}

is a complete metric space (e.g. in the case when
\|{\mathbb{D}}(x)\|\leqslant\const.\delta(x)^{2}

where
\delta(x)=\dist\big(x,\partial\Omega\big)
). One particular result in this case, which follows from the general theory in

[
9
]
, is that
H_{0}
is essentially self-adjoint irrespective of the geometry of
\Omega
and the behavior of
\rho
.
The situation when
{\mathbb{D}}
is not strongly degenerate is much more involved and after more than five decades of research (see e.g.
the line of research following the famous paper of Wienholtz
[
32
]
,
[
33
]
,
[
34
]
, the Cordes approach
[
12
]
and the
Brusentev theory
[
10
]
) the picture is far from being complete. More recently, for the
particular case of Schrödinger operator,
H=-\Delta+V
on bounded, simply connected domains with smooth boundary we refined
the previously known results on the behavior
of
V(x)
as
x\rightarrow\partial\Omega
ensuring essential self-adjointness
[
23
]
. The key point of the method in
[
23
]

is that essential self-adjointness is ensured by lower bounds of

\big(\varphi,H\varphi\big),\;\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{0}
:

(1.3)

\big(\varphi,\big(-\Delta+V\big)\varphi\big)\geqslant\int_{\Omega}\big(L(x)+V(x)\big)\big|\varphi(x)\big|^{2}dx

via Agmon-type exponential estimates for weak solutions of partial differential equations
[
1
]
.
The heuristics behind is that if the ”barrier”
L(x)+V(x)\rightarrow\infty

sufficiently fast as
x\rightarrow\partial\Omega
the quantum particle cannot reach
\partial\Omega
,
hence no boundary conditions are needed to specify the dynamics. It turns out that
the method in
[
23
]
works as well in more general settings and in particular, very recently,
criteria for essential self-adjointness of some classes of drift-diffusion operators were obtained
in
[
24
]
and (by merging the approaches in
[
24
]
,
[
27
]
) in
[
28
]
.

From the above discussion it is clear that the main ingredient which is needed in this approach is a (as ”optimal” as possible) lower
bound (aka Hardy inequality) for the general drift-diffusion operator
H_{0}=-\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla\cdot\rho\mathbb{D}\nabla

(1.4)

\big(\varphi,H_{0}\varphi\big)\geqslant\int_{\Omega}L(x)\big|\varphi(x)\big|^{2}\rho(x)dx.

If
\rho=1,\;{\mathbb{D}}=\mathds{1}
then (
1.4
) is provided by the well known weak Hardy inequality (see e.g.
[
8
]
,
[
13
]
)

(1.5)

L(x)=C+\frac{1}{4\delta(x)^{2}},\;C>-\infty,\;\delta(x)=\dist(x,\partial\Omega).

More generally if
{\mathbb{D}}(x)
is isotropic i.e.
{\mathbb{D}}(x)=d(x)\mathds{1}=a(x)\delta(x)^{\beta}\mathds{1}
then
L(x)
is provided by weighted Hardy inequalities (see
[
29
]
and references given there). Weighted Hardy inequalities can be used also for anisotropic
{\mathbb{D}}(x)
by noticing that

(1.6)

\big(\varphi,H_{0}\varphi\big)\geqslant\int_{\Omega}\big|\nabla\varphi(x)\big|^{2}d(x)\rho(x)dx

if
d(x)
satisfies
{\mathbb{D}}(x)\geqslant d(x)\mathds{1}
but (
1.6
) might be very poor if
{\mathbb{D}}(x)
is not in a ”neighborhood” of an isotropic one.

The aim of this paper is to extend the results in
[
24
]
,
[
28
]
to larger classes of
\rho,\;{\mathbb{D}}
and
V
in order to allow the degree of degeneracy of
{\mathbb{D}}
to vary with the point on
\partial\Omega
and to dispense of isotropy like conditions. On the way we prove a weak anisotropic Hardy inequality which might be of independent interest.

The content of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
we spell the setting for Sections

and
4

Weak anisotropic Hardy inequality

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that Assumptions A and Q hold true.
Then there exists
0<\nu_{1}<\frac{\nu_{0}}{2}
such that for all
0<\nu<\nu_{1}

\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)

\displaystyle h_{0}\big[\varphi,\varphi\big]=\int_{\Omega}\overline{\nabla\varphi(x)}\cdot\big({\mathbb{D}}(x)\nabla\varphi(x)\big)\rho(x)dx

Optimizing the ground of a Robin Laplacian: asymptotic behavior
Pavel Exner, Hynek Kovařík

Abstract
. In this note we consider achieving the largest principle eigenvalue of a Robin Laplacian on a bounded domain Ω \Omega by optimizing the Robin parameter function under an integral constraint. The main novelty of our approach lies in establishing a close relation between the problem under consideration and the asymptotic behavior of the Dirichlet heat content of Ω \Omega . By using this relation we deduce a two-term asymptotic expansion of the principle eigenvalue and discuss several applications.



AMS 2000 Mathematics Subject Classification:
49R05, 35P30, 58C40

Keywords:
Robin boundary conditions, eigenvalue asymptotics, heat content

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open bounded connected smooth domain. Given a function
\sigma\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega)
we consider the functional
Q[u,\sigma]
on
H^{1}(\Omega)
defined by

(1.1)

Q[u,\sigma]=\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}\sigma\,|u|^{2}\,d\nu}{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}

if the right hand side is finite and by
Q[u,\sigma]=\pm\infty
otherwise. Here

d\nu
denotes the surface measure on
\partial\Omega
. Let

(1.2)

\lambda(\sigma)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in H^{1}(\Omega)\\
u\neq 0\end{subarray}}Q[u,\sigma].

By standard variational arguments it follows that
\lambda(\sigma)
is the principal eigenvalue of the Laplace operator in
L^{2}(\Omega)
subject to Robin boundary conditions formally given by

\partial_{n}u+\sigma u=0\quad\text{on \ }\partial\Omega,

where
\partial_{n}
denotes the outer normal derivative.
For a given
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
we define

(1.3)

\Sigma_{\mu}:=\big\{\sigma\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega):\ \int_{\partial\Omega}\sigma=\mu\big\}.

The main object of our interest is the quantity

(1.4)

\Lambda_{\mu}=\sup_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\mu}}\lambda(\sigma),

and its asymptotic behavior for
\mu\to-\infty
.

The problem of analyzing the asymptotic behavior of
\lambda(\sigma)
for a
\sigma

constant
on
\partial\Omega
and negatively diverging has attracted a huge interest in the literature.
Particular attention has been paid to the link between the geometry of
\partial\Omega
and the coefficients of the associated asymptotic expansion.
In
[
21
]
it was proved that for
C^{1}
domains one has
\lambda(\sigma)=-\sigma^{2}+o(\sigma^{2})
.
A two-term expansion

(1.5)

\lambda(\sigma)=-\sigma^{2}+\sigma(N-1)H_{\max}+o(\sigma)\qquad\sigma\to-\infty,

with
H_{\max}
denoting the maximal mean curvature of
\partial\Omega
,
was obtained, under additional regularity assumptions on
\Omega
, first for
N=2
in
[
22
,
11
]
, and later in
[
25
,
26
]
in any dimension.
Under further geometric hypotheses on
\Omega
, higher terms in the expansion (
1.5
) were found in
[
8
,
14
,
26
]
. Analogous problem on non-smooth domains, in particular on domains with corners and cusps, were studied as well, cf.
[
20
,
5
,
15
,
16
,
19
,
27
,
24
]
. Various estimates on
\lambda(\sigma)
were obtained in
[
6
,
17
,
13
]
. Let us also mention that a modified version of (
1.5
) for the
p-
Laplace operator with Robin boundary conditions was established in
[
18
]
, see also
[
23
]
.

The question that we address here is different: What happens when
\lambda(\sigma)
with a constant
\sigma
is replaced by (
1.4
), in other words when we maximize
\lambda(\sigma)
under
the natural constraint (
1.3
)? Our main result, Theorem
2.5
, provides a complete answer to this question and shows that (
1.4
) has a unique maximizer for any
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, we derive an explicit
expression for
\Lambda_{\mu}
and for the (unique) optimizing
\sigma_{\mu}
in terms of the Green’s function of the Dirichlet Laplacian on
\Omega
, see equations (
2.2
) and (
2.14
).
This is achieved by
a suitable modification of the construction previously used only for
\mu>0
in
[
17
]
, see also
[
7
,
9
]
.

Furthermore, we deduce from Theorem
2.5
that, as
\mu\to-\infty
,

(1.6)

\Lambda_{\mu}=-\frac{\mu^{2}}{|\partial\Omega|^{2}}+\mu\ \frac{N-1}{|\partial\Omega|^{2}}\,\int_{\partial\Omega}H\,d\nu+\mathcal{O}(1)

where
H
denotes the mean curvature of the boundary, see Corollary
2.7
.

Let us put our result into perspective and compare it with the results obtained previously for constant
\sigma
cited above. Note that the first term on the right hand side of (
1.6
) is given by the square of the mean value of
\sigma
. This is compatible with the first term on the right hand side of (
1.5
).
On the other hand, the coefficient of the second term in (
1.6
) is proportional to the mean value of
H
, whereas in (
1.5
) we have the maximum of
H
. The reason for this difference lies in the different behavior of the associated minimizers. While the minimizer of (
1.2
) with constant
\sigma
concentrates, as
\sigma\to-\infty
,
near the set at which the mean curvature attains its maximum, see
[
18
, Thm. 6.5]
, the minimizer of (
1.2
) with
\sigma=\sigma_{\mu}
is
constant on
\partial\Omega
. The latter fact is the
starting point of the proof of our main result. Indeed, it turns out that a sufficient condition for
\sigma
to be an optimizer in (
1.4
) is that the corresponding minimizer of (
1.2
) is constant at the boundary, see equation (
2.17
).

We therefore construct a positive solution of the eigenvalue equation in such a way that its restriction to the boundary of
\Omega
is equal to
1
, cf. (
2.15
). In Theorem
2.5
we then show that this solution is the unique minimizer of (
1.2
) associated to the optimizing function
\sigma_{\mu}
, and find an explicit equation for
\Lambda_{\mu}
. By exploiting the connection with the semi-classical behavior of the heat content of
\Omega
we then derive the asymptotical expansion (
1.6
), see Corollary
2.7
. Several examples are discussed in Remark
2.8
. In the closing Section
3
we present some further
related results.

Remark 1.1
.

If
\partial\Omega
is
C^{1}
smooth, then a trace operator is well defined on
H^{1}(\Omega)
. More precisely, we have

(1.7)

\|u\|_{L^{q}(\partial\Omega)}\,\leq\,C\,\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)},\qquad\forall\ q\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\qquad}l}\leq\,2(N-1)/(N-2)&{\rm if\ }\ N>2,\\
<+\infty&{\rm if\ }\ N=2\end{array}\right.

with a compact imbedding.
If
\sigma\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega)
with
p\geq\frac{2(N-1)}{N}
for
N\geq 3
and
p>1
for
N=2
, then the imbedding (
1.7
) ensures that
Q[u,\sigma]
is bounded from for any
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\mu}
and any
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
.

2.
Main results

In this section we assume throughout that
\Omega
is a bounded and connected set in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with a
C^{3}
boundary.

Before we state our main results let us introduce some necessary notation. We denote by
-\Delta^{D}_{\Omega}
the Laplace operator on
\Omega
with homogenous Dirichlet boundary conditions. The volume and the surface measure of
\Omega
are denoted by
|\Omega|
and
|\partial\Omega|
respectively. The surface measure of the
N-
dimensional unit sphere will be denoted by
|\mathbb{S}_{N}|
. Finally, we denote by
B(x,r)
the open ball of radius
r
centered in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

2.1.
The supremum

Let
E_{1}<E_{2}\leq\dots E_{j}\leq\dots
and
\varphi_{j}
be the eigenvalues and normalized eigenfunctions of
-\Delta^{D}_{\Omega}
and recall that
E_{1}>0
.
We need a series of auxiliary results. Let us start by
recalling the existence of a minimizer in (
1.1
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\mu}
and suppose that
\sigma\in L^{p}(\partial\Omega)
with
p\geq\frac{2(N-1)}{N}
for
N\geq 3
and
p>1
for
N=2
.
Then the functional
Q[\sigma,\cdot\,]
admits a positive minimiser
\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
which satisfies

(2.1)

-\Delta\psi=\lambda(\sigma)\,\psi\quad\text{in \ }\Omega,\qquad\partial_{n}\psi+\sigma\,\psi=0\quad\text{on \ }\partial\Omega.

For the proof of Lemma
2.1
we refer to
[
18
, Prop. 6.1]
.

As already mentioned in the introduction, we will construct the minimizer in(
2.1
) relative to the optimizing
\sigma
explicitly, see (
2.15
). To do so, we define,
for any
s\in(0,E_{1})
,

(2.2)

U_{s}=(-\Delta^{D}_{\Omega}-s)^{-1}\,\mathds{1},

where
\mathds{1}
denotes the function identically equal to
1
on
\Omega
. In view of the regularity of
\Omega
we have
U_{s}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
. Later we will show that
U_{s}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, see Lemma
2.4
below. Moreover,
U_{s}>0
in
\Omega
since
(-\Delta^{D}_{\Omega}-s)^{-1}
is positivity preserving.
Together with
U_{s}
we introduce the function
F:(-\infty,E_{1})\to\mathbb{R}
given by

(2.3)

F(s)=s^{2}\int_{\Omega}U_{s}(x)\,dx+s\,|\Omega|.

Next we state a couple of properties of the function
F
will be fundamental for the proof of the main result.

Lemma 2.2
.

F
is differentiable and strictly increasing.

Proof.

Following
[
17
]
we let

g(s)=\int_{\Omega}U_{s}(x)\,dx=(\mathds{1},\,(-\Delta^{D}_{\Omega}-s)^{-1}\mathds{1})_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

so that
F(s)=s^{2}g(s)+s\,|\Omega|
. Since the resolvent
(-\Delta^{D}_{\Omega}-s)^{-1}
is analytic in
s
on
(-\infty,E_{1})
, it follows that
g\in C^{\infty}(-\infty,E_{1})
. Hence
F\in C^{\infty}(-\infty,E_{1})
.
Moreover, by
[
17
, Lem. 3.2]
, we have

g^{\prime}(s)=\|U_{s}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

This implies

(2.4)

F^{\prime}(s)=2sg(s)+s^{2}\|U_{s}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+|\Omega|=\int_{\Omega}(1+sU_{s})^{2}\,dx>0,

as claimed.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

We have

(2.5)

\lim_{s\to-\infty}F(s)=-\infty.

Proof.

Let
e^{t\Delta_{\Omega}^{D}}(x,y),t>0,
denote the integral kernel of the heat semi-group generated
by
-\Delta_{\Omega}^{D}
. Observe that, for any
s\leq 0
,

(2.6)

\int_{\Omega}U_{s}(x)\,dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{st}\,Q_{\Omega}(t)\,dt,

where

(2.7)

Q_{\Omega}(t)=\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}e^{t\Delta_{\Omega}^{D}}(x,y)\,dxdy

is the heat content of
\Omega
corresponding to initial homogeneous temperature
u=1
. By
[
2
, Thm. 6.2]
,

(2.8)

\Big|Q_{\Omega}(t)-|\Omega|+\frac{2|\partial\Omega|}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,t^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big|\leq C\,t\qquad\forall\,t>0,

with a constant
C
independent of
t
.
Inserting this estimate into (
2.6
) and using the identity

\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{z}\ e^{-z}\,dz=\Gamma(3/2)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2},

we get

(2.9)

\int_{\Omega}U_{s}(x)\,dx=-\frac{|\Omega|}{s}+\frac{|\partial\Omega|}{s\sqrt{|s|}}\,+\mathcal{O}(s^{-2})\qquad s\to-\infty.

Now the claim follows from (
2.3
).
∎

Lemma
2.2
implies that for every
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
there exists a unique
s(\mu)
such that

(2.10)

F(s(\mu))=\mu.

As we will prove shortly, it turns out that
s(\mu)
given implicitly by (
2.10
) coincides with
\Lambda_{\mu}
, and that
u_{\mu}
defined in (
2.15
) below is its corresponding eigenfunction.
It remains to show, however, that the latter is positive. To this end we have to ensure that
u_{\mu}
is positive in
\Omega
. This is done in the following

Lemma 2.4
.

For every
s\leq 0
we have

(2.11)

\sup_{x\in\Omega}|s|\,U_{s}(x)<1.

Proof.

Let
s=-\kappa^{2}
. We
extend
(-\Delta^{D}_{\Omega}+\kappa^{2})^{-1}(x,y)
by zero to
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega
, and we deduce from Dirichlet monotonicity, see
[
2
,
12
]
,
that

(2.12)

(-\Delta^{D}_{\Omega}+\kappa^{2})^{-1}(x,y)<(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}+\kappa^{2})^{-1}(x,y)\qquad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Here
\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
denotes the Laplace operator on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The Green function of the latter is given by

(2.13)

(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}+\kappa^{2})^{-1}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\frac{\kappa}{2\pi|x-y|}\right)^{\nu}\,K_{\nu}(\kappa|x-y|),

where
\nu=\frac{N}{2}-1
, and where
K_{\nu}
denotes the modified Bessel function of the second kind, cf.
[
1
, Chap. 9.6]
.
From
[
10
, Eq.10.43.19]
we learn that

\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\nu+1}\,K_{\nu}(t)\,dt=2^{\nu}\,\Gamma(\nu+1)=2^{\frac{N}{2}-1}\,\Gamma(N/2).

Hence for any
\kappa\geq 0
and
x\in\Omega
we have

\displaystyle\kappa^{2}\,U_{\kappa^{2}}(x)

\displaystyle=\kappa^{2}\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta^{D}_{\Omega}+\kappa^{2})^{-1}(x,y)\,dy<\kappa^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}+\kappa^{2})^{-1}(x,y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\kappa^{2}\,\frac{|\mathbb{S}_{N}|}{2\pi}\left(\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}\right)^{\nu}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{N/2}\,K_{\nu}(\kappa r)\,dr=\frac{|\mathbb{S}_{N}|}{(2\pi)^{N/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\nu+1}\,K_{\nu}(t)\,dt=1,

where, in the last step, we have used the identity

|\mathbb{S}_{N}|=\frac{2\,\pi^{N/2}}{\Gamma(N/2)}\,.

∎

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\Omega
be as above. Then (
1.4
) is attained for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and satisfies

(2.14)

\Lambda_{\mu}=\lambda(\sigma_{\mu})=s(\mu),\quad\text{\rm where \ \ }\sigma_{\mu}=-s(\mu)\,\partial_{n}U_{s(\mu)}\big|_{\partial\Omega},

and
s(\mu)
is given by (
2.10
).
Moreover, the optimizer
\sigma_{\mu}
is unique in
\Sigma_{\mu}
.

Proof.

The case
\mu>0
is treated in
[
17
, Thm. 3.3]
. We therefore assume that
\mu\leq 0
.
Let
\sigma_{\mu}
be given as in (
2.14
). From (
2.14
) it follows that
\sigma_{\mu}\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
. Moreover, since
-\Delta U_{s(\mu)}=s(\mu)U_{s(\mu)}+1
, the divergence
theorem in combination with equations (
2.14
) and (
2.10
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}\sigma_{\mu}\,d\nu

\displaystyle=-\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{n}U_{s(\mu)}\,d\nu=-s(\mu)\int_{\Omega}\Delta U_{s(\mu)}\,dx=F(s(\mu))=\mu.

This shows that
\sigma_{\mu}\in\Sigma_{\mu}
. Now let us set

(2.15)

u_{\mu}:=s(\mu)\,U_{s(\mu)}+1,

so that

(2.16)

-\Delta u_{\mu}=s(\mu)\,u_{\mu}\quad\text{in \ }\Omega,\qquad\partial_{n}u_{\mu}+\sigma_{\mu}=0\quad\text{on \ }\partial\Omega.

Moreover, by Lemma
2.4
we have
u_{\mu}>0
in
\Omega
.
We claim that
u_{\mu}
is a minimiser of
Q[\sigma_{\mu},\cdot\,]
. Indeed, by (
2.16
) we have
Q[\sigma_{\mu},u_{\mu}]=s(\mu)
. Assume that
\lambda(\sigma_{\mu})<s(\mu)
. In view of Lemma
2.1
and the regularity of
\sigma_{\mu}
there exists a positive minimiser
\psi
of
Q[\sigma_{\mu},\cdot\,]
which satisfies equation (
2.1
) with
\sigma=\sigma_{\mu}
. Hence
(\psi,u_{\mu})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0
, which is in contradiction with the positivity of
\psi
and
u_{\mu}
. We thus conclude that
\lambda(\sigma_{\mu})=s(\mu)
.

To show that
\sup_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\mu}}\lambda(\sigma)=\lambda(\sigma_{\mu})
we pick an arbitrary
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\mu}
. Using (
1.2
) and the fact that
u_{\mu}=1
on
\partial\Omega

we obtain

(2.17)

\lambda(\sigma)\leq Q[\sigma,u_{\mu}]=Q[\sigma_{\mu},u_{\mu}]=\lambda(\sigma_{\mu}).

To prove the uniqueness of
\sigma_{\mu}
suppose that
\lambda(\sigma)=\lambda(\sigma_{\mu})
for some
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\mu}
. Mimicking the above argument we find

\lambda(\sigma_{\mu})=\lambda(\sigma)\leq Q[\sigma,u_{\mu}]=Q[\sigma_{\mu},u_{\mu}]=\lambda(\sigma_{\mu}).

Hence
u_{\mu}
satisfies the Euler-Lagrange equation (
2.16
) with
\sigma_{\mu}
replaced with
\sigma
, and therefore
\sigma=\sigma_{\mu}
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

Obviously, for
\mu=0
we have
\sigma_{0}=0
and
\Lambda_{0}=0
.

2.2.
Asymptotic behavior of
\Lambda_{\mu}
on smooth domains

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
\partial\Omega
, and let
\Lambda_{\mu}
be given by (
1.4
) . Then

(2.18)

\Lambda_{\mu}=-\frac{\mu^{2}}{|\partial\Omega|^{2}}+\mu\ \frac{N-1}{|\partial\Omega|^{2}}\,\int_{\partial\Omega}H(y)\,d\nu(y)+\mathcal{O}(1)\qquad\mu\to-\infty,

Proof.

In view of the regularity of
\Omega
and
[
3
, Thm. 1.1]
,

(2.19)

\Big|Q_{\Omega}(t)-|\Omega|+\frac{2|\partial\Omega|}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,t^{\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{(N-1)\,t}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}H(y)\,d\nu(y)\,\Big|\leq C\,t^{3/2}\qquad\forall\,t>0.

Combining this with (
2.6
) and (
2.3
) gives

(2.20)

F(s)=-\sqrt{-s}\ |\partial\Omega|+\frac{N-1}{2}\,\int_{\partial\Omega}H(y)\,d\nu(y)+\mathcal{O}(|s|^{-1/2})\qquad s\to-\infty.

Now the claim follows from eqautions (
2.10
) and (
2.14
).
∎

Remark 2.8
.

Let us make a couple of comments regarding Corollary
2.7
.

(1)

Constant
\sigma
. Obviously,
\sigma=\frac{\mu}{|\partial\Omega|}\in\Sigma_{\mu}
. Equation (
2.18
) thus implies, see (
1.4
),

(2.21)

\displaystyle\lambda(\sigma)\leq\Lambda_{\mu}=-\sigma^{2}+\sigma\,\frac{N-1}{|\partial\Omega|}\,\ \int_{\partial\Omega}H(y)\,d\nu(y)+\mathcal{O}(1)\quad\text{as}\ \ \sigma\to-\infty.

This is to be compared with the two-term asymptotic expansion (
1.5
).
Note that

\int_{\partial\Omega}H(y)\,d\nu(y)\leq|\partial\Omega|\,H_{\rm max}.

(2)

A ball
. If
\Omega
is a ball of radius
R
, then
H(s)=H_{\rm max}=R^{-1}
for any
s\in\partial\Omega
.
Moreover, from (
2.14
) we deduce that
\sigma_{\mu}
is constant and therefore

\sigma_{\mu}=\frac{\mu}{|\partial\Omega|}
.
Hence
\lambda(\sigma)=\Lambda_{\mu}
, and inequality (
2.21
) turns into an asymptotic equation
which coincides with (
1.5
). (Notice that the error term in (
1.5
) is of order
\mathcal{O}(1)
if
\Omega
is a ball, cf.
[
14
]
).

(3)

N
-dimensional spherical shell
. Let
\Omega
be a spherical shell with radii
R
and
r
. Corollary
2.7
then gives

(2.22)

\Lambda_{\mu}=-\frac{\mu^{2}}{|\partial\Omega|^{2}}+\mu\ (N-1)\frac{|\mathbb{S}_{N}|}{|\partial\Omega|^{2}}\,\big(R^{N-2}+r^{N-2}\big)+\mathcal{O}(1).

3.
Some related results

3.1.
Asymptotic behavior of
\Lambda_{\mu}
on polygons

As an example of non-smooth domains with corners, we treat two-dimensional
polygons.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
an open, bounded and connected set with polygonal boundary. Denote by
\gamma_{j},j=1,\dots,n,
the internal angles
of
\partial\Omega
, and let

(3.1)

c(\alpha)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{4\sinh((\pi-\alpha)x)}{\sinh(\pi x)\cosh(\alpha x)}\,dx\,.

Then

(3.2)

\Lambda_{\mu}=-\frac{\mu^{2}}{|\partial\Omega|^{2}}+\frac{2\mu}{|\partial\Omega|^{2}}\,\sum_{j=1}^{n}c(\gamma_{j})+\mathcal{O}(1)\qquad\mu\to-\infty.

Note that in this case the second equation in (
2.14
) has to be replaced by

\sigma_{\mu}(y)=-s(\mu)\,\partial_{n}U_{s(\mu)}(y)\qquad\forall\ y\in\partial\Omega\setminus\{V\},

where
V
denotes the set of vertices of
\Omega
. Obviously,
\sigma_{\mu}\in L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)
.

Proof.

By
[
4
, Thm. 1]
,

(3.3)

\Big|Q_{\Omega}(t)-|\Omega|+\frac{2|\partial\Omega|}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,t^{\frac{1}{2}}-t\sum_{j=1}^{n}c(\gamma_{j})\Big|\leq C\,e^{-\frac{\delta}{t}}\qquad\forall\,t>0.

for some
\delta>0
. Equation (
3.2
) thus follows by mimicking the proof of Corollary
2.7
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

When
\sigma
is constant, then Corollary
3.1
implies the asymptotic upper bound

(3.4)

\displaystyle\lambda(\sigma)\leq\Lambda_{\mu}=-\sigma^{2}+\frac{2\sigma}{|\partial\Omega|}\,\sum_{j=1}^{n}c(\gamma_{j})+\mathcal{O}(1)\qquad\sigma\to-\infty.

It is again instructive to confront the right hand side in the above equation with the asymptotic expansion for
\lambda(\sigma)
on polygons.
The latter reads

(3.5)

\displaystyle\lambda(\sigma)=-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\sin^{2}(\gamma/2)}+\mathcal{O}(\sigma),\qquad\gamma:=\min_{j}\gamma_{j}

as
\sigma\to-\infty,
see
[
5
,
15
,
20
]
. Notice that
\sin^{2}(\gamma/2)<1
. Hence contrary to the case of smooth domains, the asymptotic expansion for
\lambda(\sigma)
differs from the upper bound
(
3.4
) already in the leading term.

3.2.
Asymptotic behavior of
\Lambda_{\mu}
for small
\mu

For the sake of completeness we state a two-term asymptotic expansion of
\Lambda_{\mu}
also for
\mu\to 0
. Notice that the result stated below holds for all Lipschitz regular domains.

Let us denote by
E_{1}<E_{2}\leq\cdots E_{j}\leq\cdots
the eigenvalues of
-\Delta_{\Omega}^{D}
, and by
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
the set of the corresponding eigenfunctions normalized to
1
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded connected and set in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with a Lipschitz boundary. Then

(3.6)

\Lambda_{\mu}=\frac{\mu}{|\Omega|}-\frac{\mu^{2}}{|\Omega|^{2}}\,\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\,\frac{\alpha^{2}_{j}}{E_{j}}+\mathcal{O}(\mu^{3})\qquad\mu\to 0,

where

\alpha_{j}=\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{j}(x)\,dx\,.

Proof.

Since
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
form an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\Omega)
, the Parseval identity implies that

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{j}^{2}=|\Omega|.

Hence the sum on the right hand side of (
3.6
) is convergent. By the definition of
U_{s}
we then get

\displaystyle s^{2}\int_{\Omega}U_{s}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=s^{2}\,\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\,\frac{\alpha^{2}_{j}}{E_{j}-s}=s^{2}\,\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\,\frac{\alpha^{2}_{j}}{E_{j}}+\mathcal{O}(|s|^{3})\qquad s\to 0.

Equation (
3.6
) thus follows from (
2.3
), (
2.10
) and (
2.14
).
∎

3.3.
The infimum

It is not difficult to verify that the infinitum in (
1.4
) is not attained. Indeed, we have

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
N\geq 2
and let
\mu<0
. Then
\inf_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\mu}}\lambda(\sigma)=-\infty
.

Proof.

Let
s_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and let
\sigma_{n}\geq 0
be given by

\sigma_{n}(s)=\Big\{\begin{array}[]{l@{\qquad}l}a_{n}&{\rm if\ }\ s\in\ B(s_{0},2^{-n})\cap\partial\Omega,\\
0&{\rm elsewhere\ },\end{array}\Big.

where
a_{n}
is a positive constant chosen so that
\sigma_{n}\in\Sigma_{\mu}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Depending on the dimension we construct a family of test functions
u_{n}
as follows:

u_{n}(x)=\frac{\log\big|\log|x-s_{0}|\big|}{\log(\log n)}\qquad\text{on \ }B(s_{0},n^{-1})\cap\Omega,\qquad u_{n}\equiv 1\quad\text{on \ }\Omega\setminus B(s_{0},n^{-1}),\quad

if
N=2
, and

u_{n}(x)=n^{\beta}\,|x-s_{0}|^{\beta}\qquad\text{on \ }B(s_{0},n^{-1})\cap\Omega,\qquad u_{n}\equiv 1\quad\text{on \ }\Omega\setminus B(s_{0},n^{-1}),

if
N\geq 3
, where
-\frac{1}{2}<\beta<0
.
Then
u_{n}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and a quick calculation shows that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\,dx\,\sim\,n^{2},\qquad\int_{\partial\Omega}\sigma_{n}\,u_{n}^{2}\,d\nu\,\sim\,n^{2\beta}\,2^{-2n\beta},\qquad n\to\infty,\quad N\geq 3,

and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\,dx\to 0,\qquad\int_{\partial\Omega}\sigma_{n}\,u_{n}^{2}\,d\nu\to\infty\qquad n\to\infty,\quad N=2.

Since
u_{n}\to 1
uniformly in
\Omega
, this implies

\lim_{n\to\infty}Q[\sigma_{n},u_{n}]=-\infty.

Hence the claim.
∎

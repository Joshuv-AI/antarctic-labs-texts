On the Smooth Part Functor
Claudius Heyer

Abstract
. Let G G be a compact p p -adic analytic group and k k a field positive characteristic. We prove that for every smooth representation of G G on a k k -vector space V V , every 1-cocycle G → V G\to V is continuous. We deduce that the first derived functor of the smooth part functor vanishes on smooth representations. As a corollary, we obtain that extensions of smooth representations are automatically smooth.

1.
Main Result

Let
k
be a commutative ring of characteristic
m>0
. Given a locally profinite
group
G
, we denote by
\Rep_{k}(G)
the category of representations of

G
on
k
-modules. Let
\Rep_{k}^{\mathrm{sm}}(G)
be the full subcategory of
\Rep_{k}(G)

consisting of the
smooth
representations, that is, those representations

V
of
G
for which the action map
G\times V\to V
is continuous if we endow

V
with the discrete topology.

Assume now that
G
is a compact
p
-adic analytic group. We can view
G
either
as an abstract group or as a topological group with the profinite
topology. Accordingly, there are two cohomology theories associated with
G

taking values
in
\Rep_{k}^{\mathrm{sm}}(G)
: We denote by
H^{q}_{\mathrm{abs}}(G,V)
the
q
-th cohomology group
of
G
when we view
G
as an abstract group. When
G
is viewed as a
topological group we denote by
H^{q}_{\mathrm{cont}}(G,V)
the
q
-th continuous (or
profinite, or Galois) cohomology group.
There is an obvious comparison homomorphism

\varphi^{q}\colon H^{q}_{\mathrm{cont}}(G,V)\longrightarrow H^{q}_{\mathrm{abs}}(G,V),\qquad\text{for $q\geq 0$,}

and it is a general question when this is an isomorphism. When
G
is a pro-
p

group and
V=\mathbb{F}_{p}
, this question was raised and studied in

[
FKRS07
]
.

The reason why we restrict to
p
-adic analytic groups and coefficient rings of
positive characteristic is the following example communicated to me by
E. Bodon:

Example 1
.

Let
k
be a non-archimedean local field, that is, either a finite
extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
or
\mathbb{F}_{p^{f}}((t))
, for some
f\geq 1
. Let
G
be the
underlying abelian group of
k
. Note that either
k
does not have positive
characteristic or
G
is not (topologically) finitely generated. Consider the
two-dimensional
G
-representation
V=k^{2}
given by

G\longrightarrow\GL_{2}(k),\quad x\longmapsto\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

We obtain a short exact sequence
0\to k\to V\to k\to 0
, where the outer terms
are smooth but
V
is not. Equivalently, the corresponding
1
-cocycle

\chi\colon G\to k
,
\chi(x)=x
is not continuous (because
k
carries the
discrete topology whereas
G
does not). Therefore, the map
\varphi^{1}
above
is not surjective.

It is easy to see that
\varphi^{1}
is always injective. Our main result is that

\varphi^{1}
is also surjective. More precisely, we prove:

Theorem 2
.

Let
V\in\Rep_{k}^{\mathrm{sm}}(G)
. Every
1
-cocycle
G\to V
is automatically
continuous. In particular, the comparison map
\varphi^{1}
is an isomorphism.

If
G
is a finitely generated pro-
p
group acting trivially on
V
,
Theorem
2
follows from the general result, due to Serre

[
Ser13
, §4.2, Exercise 6]
, that every group homomorphism
\chi\colon G\to V
is continuous.

2.
Recollection of Powerful pro-
p
Groups

It follows from
[
DDMS99
, 8.1 Theorem]
that a topological group
G
is

p
-adic analytic if and only if
G
contains an open subgroup which is a
finitely generated powerful pro-
p
group. We collect here some properties of
powerful pro-
p
groups following
[
DDMS99
]
.

Recall that a pro-
p
group

G
is called
powerful
if
p
is odd and
G/\overline{G^{p}}
is abelian, or

p=2
and
G/\overline{G^{4}}
is abelian. Here, the bar
denotes topological closure and
G^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, denotes the subgroup of

G
generated by the subset
\left\{g^{n}\,\middle|\,g\in G\right\}
.
The group
G
is called
uniformly powerful
if it is (topologically)
finitely generated, powerful, and torsionfree.

Fix a finitely generated, powerful pro-
p
group
G
. We collect some properties:

(A)

The subgroup
G^{p}=\overline{[G,G]G^{p}}
is open. If
p=2
,
then
G^{4}
is open
[
DDMS99
, Lem. 3.4]
.

(B)

Writing
G=\overline{\langle a_{1},\dotsc,a_{d}\rangle}
, we
have
G=\overline{\langle a_{1}\rangle}\dotsm\overline{\langle a_{d}\rangle}
,
i.e.
,
G

is a product of procyclic subgroups
[
DDMS99
, Prop. 3.7]
.

(C)

Every closed subgroup
H
of
G
is again a finitely
generated pro-
p
group, and the cardinality of a minimal generating
set for
H
is bounded above by the one for
G

[
DDMS99
, Thm. 3.8]
.

(D)

Let
H
be a finitely generated pro-
p
group, and
suppose there exists
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that every open normal subgroup of
H

can be generated by at most
r
elements. Then
H
has a powerful,
characteristic, open subgroup
[
DDMS99
, Thm. 3.10]
.

(E)

G
contains a characteristic, open, uniformly powerful
subgroup
[
DDMS99
, Cor. 4.3 and Thm. 3.13]
.

(F)

If
G
is uniformly powerful and
\{a_{1},\dotsc,a_{d}\}
is
a minimal generating set, then the mapping

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}\longrightarrow G,\quad(\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{d})\longmapsto a_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\dotsm a_{d}^{\lambda_{d}}

is a well-defined homeomorphism (but
not
a group homomorphism)

[
DDMS99
, Thm. 4.9]
.

It follows from properties
(C)
,
(D)
, and

(E)
above that
G
admits a fundamental basis
\Omega(G)
of open
neighborhoods of
1
consisting of uniformly powerful, normal subgroups.

3.
Proof of the Theorem

Recall that we assumed that
G
is a compact
p
-adic analytic group. We first
prove a special case of Theorem
2

Lemma 3
.

Assume that
G
is abelian. Let
V\in\Rep_{k}^{\mathrm{sm}}(G)
. Every
1
-cocycle
G\to V
is automatically continuous.

Proof.

Let
V\in\Rep_{k}^{\mathrm{sm}}(G)
and let
\chi\colon G\to V
be a
1
-cocycle. We argue
that it suffices to find an open pro-
p
subgroup
U\leq G
with
\chi(G)\subseteq V^{U}
. Given such
U
the map
\chi\big|_{U}\colon U\to V^{U}
is a group
homomorphism. Note that
U
is powerful, since it is abelian, and finitely
generated, since
G
is
p
-adic analytic. Using
(A)
, one easily
verifies that the subgroup
U^{p^{2}m}
is open in
U
. From
m.V=\{0\}
it
follows that
\chi
vanishes on
U^{p^{2}m}
. The cocycle condition then
ensures that
\chi
is locally constant and hence continuous.

For each
g\in G
there exists a compact open subgroup
K_{g}\leq G
with

\chi(g)\in V^{K_{g}}
.
As
G
is finitely generated, there exist
a_{1},\dotsc,a_{d}
in
G
generating a
dense subgroup; denote it by
A
. Let
U\in\Omega(G)
be a subgroup contained
in
\bigcap_{i=1}^{d}K_{a_{i}}
. Then
\chi(a_{i})\in V^{U}
for all

i
. Given
g,h\in G
with
\chi(g),\chi(h)\in V^{U}
, we have

\chi(gh^{-1})=\chi(g)-(gh^{-1})\chi(h)\in V^{U},

because
G
normalizes
U
and hence acts on
V^{U}
. In particular,
\chi(A)\subseteq V^{U}
. As noted at the beginning of the proof, we are done once we show

\chi(G)\subseteq V^{U}
.

Fix any
u\in U
. For each
g\in K_{u}
we compute, using that
G
is abelian,

u.\chi(g)=\chi(ug)-\chi(u)=\chi(gu)-\chi(u)=\chi(g)+g.\chi(u)-\chi(u)=\chi(g),

i.e.
,
\chi(K_{u})\subseteq V^{u}
. As
K_{u}.A=G
and
\chi(A)\subseteq V^{U}\subseteq V^{u}
, we conclude
\chi(G)\subseteq V^{u}
. As
u
was arbitrary, we
deduce
\chi(G)\subseteq\bigcap_{u\in U}V^{u}=V^{U}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Let
\chi\colon G\to V
be a cocycle. Write
G=\overline{\langle a_{1},\dotsc,a_{d}\rangle}
such that
d
is minimal. Then property
(F)

implies that the multiplication map induces a homeomorphism

(1)

H_{1}\times\dotsb\times H_{d}\xrightarrow{\cong}G,

where
H_{i}=\overline{\langle a_{i}\rangle}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
i=1,\dotsc,d
. Denote by

W_{i}
the
H_{i}
-representation obtained from
V
via restriction.
Lemma
3
shows that
\chi\big|_{H_{i}}\colon H_{i}\to W_{i}
is
continuous. In particular, there exists an open subgroup
U_{i}
of
H_{i}
with

\chi(U_{i})=\{0\}
. It follows from (
1
) that the subset
U_{1}\dotsm U_{d}
of
G
is an open neighborhood of
1
, hence it contains an open normal
subgroup
U
of
G
. It follows from the cocycle property that
\chi(U)=\{0\}
and further that
\chi\colon G\to V
is locally constant, that is,
continuous.
∎

4.
Application to the Smooth Part Functor

Let
k
be a field of positive characteristic. Fix a
p
-adic analytic group

G
.
1
1

1

Here,
p
need not be the characteristic of
k
.
For example,

G
could be the group of
F
-points of a connected reductive
F
-group, where

F
is a finite field extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

Given any
V\in\Rep_{k}(G)
, the
k
-vector space

V^{\mathrm{sm}}\coloneqq\bigcup_{K\leq G}V^{K}\subseteq V,

where
V^{K}\coloneqq\left\{v\in V\,\middle|\,\text{$xv=v$ for all $x\in K$}\right\}
,
K
runs
through the compact open subgroups of
G
, is the largest smooth
subrepresentation of
G
contained in
V
. This yields a functor

(\,\cdot\,)^{\mathrm{sm}}\colon\Rep_{k}(G)\longrightarrow\Rep_{k}^{\mathrm{sm}}(G).

Given
V\in\Rep_{k}(G)
, we can also write
V^{\mathrm{sm}}=\varinjlim_{K\leq G}V^{K}
,
where
K
runs through the compact open subgroups of
G
. As filtered colimits
are exact, the underlying
k
-vector spaces of the right derived functors of

(\,\cdot\,)^{\mathrm{sm}}
can be computed as

\mathbf{R}^{i}(\,\cdot\,)^{\mathrm{sm}}(V)=\varinjlim_{K\leq G}H_{\mathrm{abs}}^{i}(K,V)=\varinjlim_{K\leq G}\Ext^{i}_{\Rep_{k}(G)}(\ind_{K}^{G}(1),V),\quad\text{for all $i\geq 0$.}

About
\mathbf{R}^{1}(\,\cdot\,)^{\mathrm{sm}}
we have the following result:

Theorem 4
.

Let
V\in\Rep_{k}^{\mathrm{sm}}(G)
. Then
\mathbf{R}^{1}(\,\cdot\,)^{\mathrm{sm}}(V)=\{0\}
.

Proof.

Applying Theorem
2
, we deduce

\mathbf{R}^{1}(\,\cdot\,)^{\mathrm{sm}}(V)=\varinjlim_{K\leq G}H_{\mathrm{abs}}^{1}(K,V)=\varinjlim_{K\leq G}H_{\mathrm{cont}}^{1}(K,V)=\{0\}.\qed

Corollary 5
.

Let
f\colon V\to W
be a surjective morphism in
\Rep_{k}(G)
such that
\Ker(f)

is smooth. Then
f^{\mathrm{sm}}\colon V^{\mathrm{sm}}\to W^{\mathrm{sm}}
is surjective.

Proof.

The short exact sequence
0\to\Ker(f)\to V\to W\to 0
yields an exact
sequence
V^{\mathrm{sm}}\xrightarrow{f^{\mathrm{sm}}}W^{\mathrm{sm}}\to\mathbf{R}^{1}(\,\cdot\,)^{\mathrm{sm}}\bigl(\Ker(f)\bigr)=\{0\}
. Hence,
f^{\mathrm{sm}}
is surjective.
∎

Corollary 6
.

Let
V\in\Rep_{k}(G)
. Then
(V/V^{\mathrm{sm}})^{\mathrm{sm}}=\{0\}
.

Proof.

The quotient map
V\to V/V^{\mathrm{sm}}
induces the zero map
V^{\mathrm{sm}}\to(V/V^{\mathrm{sm}})^{\mathrm{sm}}
which, by Corollary
5
, is surjective.
∎

Corollary 7
.

Let
0\to V_{1}\to V_{2}\to V_{3}\to 0
be a short exact sequence in
\Rep_{k}(G)
. If

V_{1}
and
V_{3}
are smooth, then so is
V_{2}
.

Proof.

By Corollary
5
the induced sequence
0\to V_{1}\to V_{2}^{\mathrm{sm}}\to V_{3}\to 0
is exact, whence
V_{2}=V_{2}^{\mathrm{sm}}
.
∎

We have seen in Example
1
that Corollary
7
may
fail if either
G
does not admit a finitely generated open subgroup or
k
has
characteristic
0
.

Corollary 8
.

One has
\Ext^{1}_{\Rep_{k}^{\mathrm{sm}}(G)}(V,W)=\Ext^{1}_{\Rep_{k}(G)}(V,W)
, for all
V,W\in\Rep_{k}^{\mathrm{sm}}(G)
.

Proof.

This is a restatement of Corollary
7
.
∎

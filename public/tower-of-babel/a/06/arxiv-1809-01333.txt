Abstract
. Let C C be a smooth complex irreducible projective curve of genus g g with general moduli, and let ( L , H 0 ​ ( L ) ) (L,H^{0}(L)) be a generated complete linear series of type ( d , r + 1 ) (d,r+1) over C C . The syzygy bundle, denoted by M L M_{L} , is the kernel of the evaluation map H 0 ​ ( L ) ⊗ 𝒪 C → L H^{0}(L)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}\to L . In this work we have a double purpose. The first one is to give new examples of stable syzygy bundles admitting theta divisor over general curves. We prove that if M L M_{L} is strictly semistable then M L M_{L} admits reducible theta divisor. The second purpose is to study the cohomological semistability of M L M_{L} , and in this direction we show that when L L induces a birational map, the syzygy bundle M L M_{L} is cohomologically semistable, and we obtain precise conditions for the cohomological semistability of M L M_{L} where such conditions agree with the semistability conditions for M L M_{L} .

Introduction

Let
C
be a smooth complex irreducible projective curve of genus

. Denote by

, is an integer number and set

\mathcal{D}_{N}:=\{E\in S\mathcal{U}(r,M)|h^{0}(E\otimes N)>0\}.

(1.1)

It is well known that

For a vector bundle

The Strange duality (see e.g.

]

making the following diagram commutative

\scriptstyle{\theta}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{\mathcal{L}}}
\textstyle{|\mathcal{L}|^{\vee}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

We say that

We recall some properties of theta divisors:

•

If
E
admits theta divisor, then

is semistable: suppose that there is a subbundle

with

, thus by Riemann Roch we have

and this implies that

does not admit theta divisor.

•

If

admits theta divisor.

•

If

admits theta divisor, then

admits theta divisor for any line bundle

2.
syzygy bundles and theta divisors

Let
L\in Pic^{d}(C)
be a globally generated line bundle over a general curve
C
of genus
g
with
h^{0}(L)=r+1
. In (
[
3
]
, Corollary 4.3), the authors proved that the syzygy bundle
M_{L}
is semistable (not stable) if and only if all the following three conditions hold:

(1)

h^{1}(L)=0
.

(2)

d=g+r
and
r
divides
g
.

(3)

There is an effective divisor
Z
with
h^{0}(L(-Z))=r
and
\text{deg}(Z)=1+\frac{g}{r}
.

From now on we will denote by
Z
the effective divisor that satisfy condition (3) above. Furthermore, with the above hyphotesis on
L
and
Z
we will denote
\xi:=K_{C}\otimes L^{\vee}(Z)
.

Remark 2.1
.

The line bundle
\xi
is globally generated: First note that
\text{deg}(\xi)=g-1-r+\frac{g}{r}
and
h^{0}(\xi)=h^{1}(L(-Z))=\frac{g}{r}
. By Riemann-Roch and Serre duality Theorems, we have that for any
p\in C
, the condition
h^{0}(\xi(-p))=h^{0}(\xi)-1
is equivalent to
h^{0}(L(-Z+p))=r
. From the fact that
h^{0}(L(-Z))=r
, then
h^{0}(L(-Z+p))
is equal to
r
or
r+1
. Suppose that
h^{0}(L(-Z+p))=r+1
for some point
p\in C
. Since
C
is a general curve and
L(-Z+p)
is a line bundle of degree
g+r-\frac{g}{r}
with
r+1
sections, we conclude that the corresponding Brill-Noether number
\rho
for
L(-Z+p)
is nonnegative, however
\rho=g-(r+1)\cdot(r-(g+r-\frac{g}{r})+g))=g-(r+1)\cdot\frac{g}{r}<0
, which contradicts that
C
is general. So
h^{0}(L(-Z+p))=r
and
\xi
is globally generated.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
C
be a general curve of genus
g\geq 3
. Under all three conditions above (1)-(3), the syzygy bundle
M_{L(-Z)}
admits theta divisor.

Proof.

Note that by Riemann-Roch Theorem and following Remark 2.1 it is easy to see that
L(-Z)
is generated, then from the evaluation map
H^{0}(L(-Z))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}\to L(-Z)
we have for every line bundle
\mathcal{P}
the following exact sequence:

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M_{L(-Z)}\otimes\mathcal{P}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

The bundle

admits theta divisor if there exists a line bundle
\mathcal{P}
of degree
\text{deg}(\mathcal{P})=g+\frac{g}{r}
such that
h^{0}(M_{L(-Z)}\otimes\mathcal{P})=0
. The condition
h^{0}(M_{L(-Z)}\otimes\mathcal{P})=0
is equivalent to that the multiplication map
\mu_{\mathcal{P},L(-Z)}:H^{0}(\mathcal{P})\otimes H^{0}(L(-Z))\rightarrow H^{0}(L(-Z)\otimes\mathcal{P})
is injective.
Note that the degree
\text{deg}(L(-Z)\otimes\mathcal{P})=2g+r-1\geq 2g
and
h^{0}(L(-Z)\otimes\mathcal{P})=g+r
, so we describe the theta divisor
\Theta_{M_{L(-Z)}}
analyzing the following three cases:

(i). If
h^{0}(\mathcal{P})>\frac{g}{r}+1
we have
h^{0}(\mathcal{P})\cdot h^{0}(L(-Z))>h^{0}(\mathcal{P}\otimes L(-Z)),
thus
\mathcal{P}\in\Theta_{M_{L(-Z)}}.

(ii). Assume that
h^{0}(P)=\frac{g}{r}+1
and that the linear system
|\mathcal{P}|
has a base point. The spaces
H^{0}(\mathcal{P})\otimes H^{0}(L(-Z))

\text{deg}(L(-Z)\otimes\mathcal{P})=2g+r-1\geq 2g
. Hence we have
\mathcal{P}\in\Theta_{M_{L(-Z)}}
.

(iii). Finally assume that
|\mathcal{P}|
is base-point free and

\mathcal{P}\notin\Theta_{M_{L(-Z)}}

On a general curve we can bound the dimension of the space global sections of the line bundle

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a line bundle over a general curve

of genus

. Moreover, if

then
\text{deg}(\mathcal{A})=t\cdot\frac{d}{r}
,

and
t+1=r

Proof.

Suppose that
h^{0}(\mathcal{A})\geq t+2
. Since

is a general curve and
\mathcal{A}
is a line bundle of degree
\text{deg}(\mathcal{A})
with at least

sections, we conclude that the corresponding Brill-Noether number
\rho
for the line bundle
\mathcal{A}
is nonnegative, but

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle g-(t+2)\cdot(t+1-\text{deg}(\mathcal{A})+g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g-(t+2)(t+1)+\text{deg}(\mathcal{A})(t+2)-(t+2)g

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-g-(t+2)(t+1)+t(t+2)(\frac{g+r}{r})-tg

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g-(t+2)-tg(1-\frac{t+2}{r})<0,

which contradicts that
C
is general. Then
h^{0}(\mathcal{A})\leq t+1
and this proves the first statement. Now, suppose that
h^{0}(\mathcal{A})=t+1
. Since
C
is a general curve and
\mathcal{A}
is a line bundle of degree
\text{deg}(\mathcal{A})
with at least
t+1
sections, we conclude that the corresponding Brill-Noether number
\rho
for the line bundle
\mathcal{A}
is nonnegative, but

\displaystyle 0\leq\rho

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle g-(t+1)\cdot(t-\text{deg}(\mathcal{A})+g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-t(t+1)+\text{deg}(\mathcal{A})(t+1)-tg

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle t(t+1)\frac{d}{r}-t(t+1)-tg

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle t(t+1)(\frac{g+r}{r})-t(t+1)-tg

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle gt(\frac{t+1}{r}-1)\leq 0.

So the Brill-Noether number

is equal to zero if and only if
\text{deg}(\mathcal{A})=t\cdot\frac{d}{r}

and

∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Let

be a line bundle with

, inducing a birational morphism over a general curve

is cohomologically semistable.

Proof.

Let

of degree

. Hence
M_{L}
is cohomologically semistable, and which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.9

If

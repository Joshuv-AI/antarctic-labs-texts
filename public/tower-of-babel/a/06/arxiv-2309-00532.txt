Intuitionistic Gödel-Löb logic, à la Simpson: labelled systems and birelational semantics
Anupam Das, Iris van der Giessen, Sonia Marin

Abstract
We derive an intuitionistic version of Gödel-Löb modal logic ( 𝖦𝖫 {\sf GL} ) in the style of Simpson, via proof theoretic techniques. We recover a labelled system, ℓ ​ 𝖨𝖦𝖫 \ell{\sf IGL} , by restricting a non-wellfounded labelled system for 𝖦𝖫 {\sf GL} to have only one formula on the right. The latter is obtained using techniques from cyclic proof theory, sidestepping the barrier that 𝖦𝖫 {\sf GL} ’s usual frame condition (converse well-foundedness) is not first-order definable. While existing intuitionistic versions of 𝖦𝖫 {\sf GL} are typically defined over only the box (and not the diamond), our presentation includes both modalities. Our main result is that ℓ ​ 𝖨𝖦𝖫 \ell{\sf IGL} coincides with a corresponding semantic condition in birelational semantics: the composition of the modal relation and the intuitionistic relation is conversely well-founded. We call the resulting logic 𝖨𝖦𝖫 {\sf IGL} . While the soundness direction is proved using standard ideas, the completeness direction is more complex and necessitates a detour through several intermediate characterisations of 𝖨𝖦𝖫 {\sf IGL} .

Introduction

Gödel-Löb logic
(
{\sf GL}
) originates in the
provability
reading of modal logic:
\Box
is interpreted as “it is provable that”, in an arithmetical theory with suitable coding capacity, inducing its corresponding
provability logic
.
Löb formulated a set of necessary conditions on the provability logic of Peano Arithmetic (
\mathrm{PA}
), giving rise to
{\sf GL}
, extending basic modal logic
\mathsf{K}
by what we now call
Löb’s axiom
:

\Box(\Box A\to A)\to\Box A
.
Somewhat astoundingly
{\sf GL}
turns out to be
complete
for

\mathrm{PA}
’s provability logic, a celebrated result of Solovay
[
40
]
: all that
\mathrm{PA}
can prove about its own provability is already a consequence of a relatively simple (and indeed decidable) propositional modal logic.

Proof theoretically Löb’s axiom represents a form of
induction
.
Indeed, at the level of modal logic semantics,
{\sf GL}
enjoys a correspondence with transitive relational structures that are
terminating
1
1

1

Other authors refer to this property as
Noetherian
or
conversely well-founded
, but we opt for this simpler nomenclature.

[
36
]
.
Duly, in computer science, Löb’s axiom
has inspired several variants of modal type theories that extend simply-typed lambda calculus with some form of
recursion
.
These range, for instance, from the seminal work of Nakano
[
30
]
, to more recent explorations into guarded recursion
[
6
]
and intensional recursion
[
23
]
.
Based in type theories, these developments naturally cast
{\sf GL}
in a
constructive
setting, but
little attention was given
to analysing
the induced intuitionistic modal logics.
Indeed, for this reason,
[
10
]
has proposed a more foundational basis for studying computational interpretations of
{\sf GL}
, by way of a sequent calculus for the logic of the topos of trees.

Returning to the provability reading of modal logic, several constructive variants of
{\sf GL}
have been independently proposed (see
[
26
,
16
]
for overviews).
An important such logic is
{\sf iGL}
(and its variants) which now enjoys a rich mathematical theory, from semantics (e.g.,
[
26
]
) to proof theory (e.g.,
[
17
,
19
]
).
Interestingly, while
{\sf iGL}
is known to be sound for the provability logic of Heyting Arithmetic (
\mathrm{HA}
), the intuitionistic counterpart of
\mathrm{PA}
, it is not complete.
The provability logic of
\mathrm{HA}
has been recently announced by
[
29
]
, currently under review.

One shortfall of all the above mentioned approaches is that they do not allow us to recover a bona fide computational interpretation of
classical

{\sf GL}
along, say, the Gödel-Gentzen negative translation (GG), a standard way of lifting interpretations of intuitionistic logics to their classical counterparts.
Indeed it was recently observed that the ‘
\mathsf{i}
’ (or ‘constructive’) traditions of intuitionistic modal logic are too weak to validate the GG translation
[
12
,
13
]
.

On the other hand modal logic’s relational semantics effectively renders it a fragment of usual first-order predicate logic (FOL), the so-called
standard translation
.
Interpreting this semantics in an intuitionistic meta-theory defines a logic that
does
validate GG, for the same reason that intuitionistic FOL GG-interprets classical FOL.
This is the approach taken (and considerably developed) by Simpson
[
38
]
, building on earlier work of
Fischer Servi
[
15
]
and Plotkin and Stirling
[
33
]
.
The resulting logic
{\sf IK}
(and friends) enjoys a remarkably robust proof theory by way of
labelled systems
, which may be duly seen as a fragment of Gentzen’s systems for intuitionistic FOL by way of the standard translation.

Contribution

In this work we develop an intuitionistic version of
{\sf GL}
, following Simpson’s methodology
[
38
]
.
In particular, while logics such as
{\sf iGL}
are defined over only the
\Box
, our logic is naturally formulated over both the
\Box
and the
\Diamond
.
A key stumbling block to this end, as noticed already in the classical setting by Negri in
[
31
]
, is that
{\sf GL}
’s correspondence to terminating relations cannot seemingly be inlined within a standard labelled system: termination is not even FOL-definable.
To side-step this barrier we draw from a now well-developed proof-theoretic approach to (co)induction:
non-wellfounded proofs
(e.g.
[
32
,
9
,
39
,
3
,
14
,
11
]
).
Such proofs allow infinite branches, and so are (typically) equipped with a
progress condition
that ensures sound reasoning.
Starting from a standard labelled system for transitive relations,
\mathsf{K4}
, we recover a labelled calculus
\ell{\sf GL}
for
{\sf GL}
by allowing non-wellfounded derivations under a typical progress condition.
In fact, our progress condition is precisely the one from Simpson’s
Cyclic Arithmetic

[
39
,
11
]
.
Following (the same) Simpson, we duly recover an intuitionistic version
\ell{\sf IGL}
of
{\sf GL}

syntactically
in a standard way: we restrict
\ell{\sf GL}
to one formula on the right.

At the same time we can recover an intuitionistic version of
{\sf GL}

semantically
by suitably adapting the
birelational semantics
of intuitionistic modal logics, which combine the partial order
\leq
of intuitionistic semantics with the accessibility relation
R
of modal semantics.
Our semantic formulation

\mathscr{B}{\sf IGL}
is duly obtained by reading the termination criterion of classical
{\sf GL}
’s semantics intuitionistically: the composition
\leq;R
must be terminating.
Our main result is that these two characterisations,
\ell{\sf IGL}
and
\mathscr{B}{\sf IGL}
, are indeed equivalent, and we duly dub the resulting logic
{\sf IGL}
.

The soundness direction
is proved via standard techniques from intuitionistic modal logic and non-wellfounded proof theory.
The completeness direction, on the other hand, is more cumbersome.
To this end we exercise an intricate combination of proof theoretic techniques,
necessitating two further (and ultimately equivalent) characterisations of
{\sf IGL}
: semantically
\mathscr{P}{\sf IGL}
, essentially a class of intuitionistic FOL structures, and syntactically
\mathsf{m}\ell{\sf IGL}
, a
multi-succedent
variant of
\ell{\sf IGL}
.
These formulations facilitate a countermodel construction from failed proof search, inspired by Takeuti’s for
{\sf LJ}

[
41
]
.
Due to the non-wellfoundedness of proofs we employ a determinacy principle to organise the construction, a standard technique in non-wellfounded and cyclic proof theory (e.g.
[
32
]
).
For the reduction to
\ell{\sf IGL}
we devise a form of
continuous cut-elimination
, building on more recent ideas in non-wellfounded proof theory, cf.
[
3
,
14
]
.

Other related work

The
proof theory of
{\sf GL}
is (in)famously complex.
The first sequent calculus of
{\sf GL}
was considered in
[
24
]
but its cut-elimination property was only finally settled (positively) in
[
18
]
after several attempts
[
43
,
35
,
28
]
.
Intuitionistic versions of sequent calculi for
{\sf GL}
are developed in
[
17
,
19
]
and provide calculi for
{\sf iGL}
.
Labelled calculi
[
31
]
and
nested calculi

[
34
]
have also been proposed.
However all of these calculi are arguably unsatisfactory:
the modality introduction rules are non-standard (which is atypical for labelled calculi) and, in particular, modal rules may change the polarity of formula occurrences, due to the inductive nature of Löb’s axiom.

It is possible to recover a system for
{\sf GL}
by admitting non-wellfounded proofs in a sequent calculus for
\mathsf{K4}
, as observed by Shamkanov
[
37
]
.
An intuitionistic version of this system has been studied by Iemhoff
[
21
]
.
In these works
both the base calculus and the corresponding correctness criterion are bespoke, rather than ‘recovered’ from established foundations.

Non-wellfounded proofs
originate in the study of modal logics with fixed points, in particular Niwinski and Walukiewicz’s seminal work on the

\mu
-calculus

[
32
]
.
These ideas were later inlined into the proof theory of FOL with certain inductive definitions by Brotherston and Simpson (e.g.
[
9
]
), a source of inspiration for the present work.
As already mentioned, recent extensions of these ideas to
\mathrm{PA}

[
39
,
11
]
and advances on cut-elimination
[
3
,
14
]
are quite relevant to our development.

2
Preliminaries on (classical) modal logic

Throughout this work we work with a set
{\sf Pr}
of
propositional symbols
, written
p,q,
etc., which we simultaneously construe as unary predicate symbols when working in predicate logic.
For the latter we also assume a single binary relation symbol
R
and a countable set
{\sf Var}
of
(individual) variables
, written
x,y,
etc.

2.1
Language and semantics

The formulas of modal logic are generated by the following grammar:

A\quad\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\quad p\in{\sf Pr}\ \mid\ \bot\ \mid\ A\land A\ \mid\ A\lor A\ \mid\ A\to A\ \mid\ \Box A\ \mid\ \Diamond A

As usual we write
\lnot A:=A\to\bot
, and employ standard bracketing conventions.

Modal formulas are interpreted over
relational frames

\mathcal{F}=(W,R^{\mathcal{F}})
formed of a non empty set of
worlds

W
equipped with an
accessibility relation

R^{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq W\times W
.
2
2

2

We parameterise the relations by the frame or models to be able distinguish it from the fixed binary relation symbol
R
.

A
relational model

\mathcal{M}=(W,R^{\mathcal{M}},V)
is a structure where
(W,R^{\mathcal{M}})
is a frame with a
valuation

V:W\to\mathcal{P}({\sf Pr})
.
Let
\mathcal{M}=(W,R^{\mathcal{M}},V)
be a relational model.
For worlds
w\in W
and formulas
A
we define the satisfaction judgement
\mathcal{M},w\vDash A
as follows:

•

\mathcal{M},w\vDash p
if
p\in V(w)
;

•

\mathcal{M},w\nvDash\bot
;

•

\mathcal{M},w\vDash A\land B
if
\mathcal{M},w\vDash A
and
\mathcal{M},w\vDash B
;

•

\mathcal{M},w\vDash A\lor B
if
\mathcal{M},w\vDash A
or
\mathcal{M},w\vDash B
;

•

\mathcal{M},w\vDash A\to B
if
\mathcal{M},w\vDash A
then
\mathcal{M},w\vDash B
;

•

\mathcal{M},w\vDash\Box A
if for all
v
such that
wR^{\mathcal{M}}v
we have
\mathcal{M},v\vDash A
;

•

\mathcal{M},w\vDash\Diamond A
if there exists
v
such that
wR^{\mathcal{M}}v
and
\mathcal{M},v\vDash A
;

If
\mathcal{M},w\vDash A
for all
w\in W
we simply write
\mathcal{M}\vDash A
, and if
(W,R^{\mathcal{M}},V)\vDash A
for all valuations
V
based on frame
\mathcal{F}=(W,R^{\mathcal{M}})
, we simply write
\mathcal{F}\vDash A
.

2.3
Labelled calculi and the standard translation

The relational semantics of modal logic may be viewed as a bona fide fragment of predicate logic.
Recalling the predicate language we fixed at the start of the section,
the
standard translation
is defined as follows: for individual variables
x
and modal formulas
A
we define the predicate formula
x:A
by:

•

x:p
is
p(x)
;

•

x:\bot
is
\bot
;

•

x:A\star B
is
(x:A)\star(x:B)
for
\star\in\{\lor,\land,\to\}
;

•

x:\Diamond A
is
\exists y(xRy\land(y:A))
;

•

x:\Box A
is
\forall y(xRy\to(y:A))
.

This induces a well-behaved proof theory as a fragment of usual first-order predicate systems
[
31
]
, adaptable to many extensions under correspondence theorems such as
\cref
thm:correspondence-k-kfour.

A
(labelled) sequent
is an expression
\mathbf{R},\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
, where
\mathbf{R}
is a set of
relational atoms
, i.e. formulas of form
xRy
, and
\Gamma
and
\Delta
are multisets of
labelled formulas
, i.e. formulas of form
x:A
.
We sometimes refer to the variable
x
in
x:A
as a
label
.
We write
{\sf Var}(S)
for the subset of labels/variables that occur in a given sequent
S
. Similarly so for
{\sf Var}(\mathbf{R})
, etc.

Notationally, we have identified labelled formulas with the standard translation at the beginning of this section.
This is entirely suggestive, as we can now easily distil systems for modal logics of interest by appealing to the sequent calculus
{\sf LK}
for first-order predicate logic.
In this vein, the labelled calculus
\ell\mathsf{K}
for modal logic
\mathsf{K}
is given in

From here, under the aforementioned correspondence between
\mathsf{K4}
and transitive frames, we have a system
\ell\mathsf{K4}

\mathsf{K4}
that extends
\ell\mathsf{K}
by the
transitivity rule

\mathbf{R},xRy,yRz,xRz,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta

\mathbf{R},xRy,yRz,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta

4

4.1
An intuitionistic

Following Gentzen, it is natural to define intuitionistic calculi based on their classical counterparts by restricting sequents to one formula on the RHS.

to
\infty
-proofs with only singleton RHSs.

Definition 14

We write

for the class of
\infty
-proofs of
\ell{\sf IK4}
.

Here we informally describe
the construction of the proof search tree. For a more formal treatment of parts below we also refer to Appendix
D

applications of rule
\mathsf{tr}

In fact, invertibility of all rules except

For instance, we apply the following derivable ‘macro’ rules for

\mathbf{R},xRy,\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta,x:\Diamond A,y:A

\Diamond\text{-}r\;

By furthermore building weakening into the identity, i.e. allowing initial sequents of form

In order to carry out our countermodel construction to show completeness of

Lemma 35
.

Following the proof search strategy described above, each invertible phase constructs a finite subtree that has saturated sequents at its leaves.

Secondly, and perhaps more importantly, we employ a technique from non-wellfounded proof theory to help us organise the countermodel constructed from a failed proof search: we appeal to determinacy of a
proof search game
. To understand the motivation here, a classical countermodel-from-failed-proof search argument proceeds (very roughly) as follows:
(1) assume a formula is not provable;
(2) for each rule instance there must be an unprovable premiss;
(3) continue in this way to construct an (infinite) ‘unprovable’ branch;
(4) extract a countermodel from this branch.
In our setting we will need the branch obtained through the process above to be

\mathscr{P}{\sf IGL}

However the local nature of the process above does not at all guarantee that this will be the case. For this, we rely on

The step case is given by:

Lemma 44

(Productivity)
.

For any
\infty
-proof
P
and bar
B
there is a sequence of cut-reductions
P=P_{0}\leadsto_{\mathsf{r}_{1}}\cdots\leadsto_{\mathsf{r}_{n}}P_{n}
such that
\mathsf{r}_{n}\cdots\mathsf{r}_{1}(B)
has no
d
-cuts beneath it in
P_{n}
.

The argument proceeds in a relatively standard way:
by induction on the number of
d
-cuts in
\lfloor B\rfloor
, with a subinduction on the multiset of the distances of topmost
d
-cuts in
\lfloor B\rfloor
from
B
, where the distance is the length of the shortest path from the cut to
B
.
Applying
\cref
lem:push-cuts-above-bar to higher and higher bars allows us to reduce cut-degrees as required:

Proposition 45

(Degree-reduction)
.

For each
\infty
-proof of
\lor\ell{\sf IK4}
of
x:A
of degree
d
, there is one of degree
<d
.

Importantly here, the preservation of progress in the limit crucially relies on
\cref

Finally by induction on degree we have:

Corollary 46

(Partial cut-elimination)
.

If
\lor\ell{\sf IK4}
has a bounded-degree
\infty
-proof of
x:A
, then it has one containing only labelled formulas.

From here we have our desired converse to
\cref

\cref

\cref

Theorem 47

(Completeness)
.

If
\mathscr{B}{\sf IGL}\vDash A
then
\ell{\sf IGL}\vdash x:A
.

9
Conclusions

We have
recovered several intuitionistic formulations of
{\sf GL}
, both syntactically and semantically, in the tradition of Simpson, on intuitionistic modal logic
[
38
]
, and Simpson, on non-wellfounded proofs
[
39
]
.
We proved the equivalence of all these formulations, cf.
\cref
fig:tour, motivating the following definition:

Definition 48
.

{\sf IGL}
is the modal logic given by any/all of the nodes of
\cref
fig:tour.

Thanks to the methodology we followed,
{\sf IGL}
satisfies Simpson’s
requirements
from
[
38
]
.

{\sf IGL}

also interprets (classical)
{\sf GL}
along the Gödel-Gentzen negative translation.

It would be interesting to examine
{\sf IGL}
as a logic of provability, returning to the origins of
{\sf GL}
.
In particular
{\sf IGL}
(even
{\sf IK}
) has a
normal

\Diamond
, distributing over
\lor
, so let us point out that it is not sound to interpret
\Diamond
as consistency
\lnot\Box\lnot
.
At the same time (effective) model-theoretic readings of the
\Diamond
stumble on the consequence (already of
{\sf IK}
)
\Box(A\to B)\to\Diamond A\to\Diamond B
.
We expect
{\sf IGL}
to rather correspond to the provability logic of some
model
of
\mathrm{HA}
.

To this end it would be pertinent to develop a bona fide
axiomatisation
of
{\sf IGL}
.
As far as we know it is possible that
{\sf IGL}
is simply the extension of
{\sf IK4}
by Löb’s axiom but, similarly to other ‘
\mathsf{I}
’ logics, we expect that further axioms involving
\Diamond
will be necessary.
On a related note, we believe that a full cut-elimination result holds for
\ell{\sf IGL}
, in particular by extending our argument for
\lor\ell{\sf IGL}
.
Crucial here is that no cut-reductions
delete
progress points, since we do not cut on relational atoms (cf. a key
\Box
reduction).
A full development of this is beyond the scope (and allocated space) of this work.

Now we define the proof search strategy. During bottom-up proof search we will always proceed according to the three following phases in order of priority: applications of rule

Now we prove Lemma

Proof of Lemma

By construction of the invertible phase, each leaf is saturated. Let
\mathbf{R},\Gamma\Rightarrow\Delta
be the start sequent of the invertible phase. We argue that the invertible phase terminates. First observe that, bottom-up, the number of labelled formulas in a sequent increase for each rule because we only apply a rule in the invertible phase when the concerned principal formulas is not saturated. To ensure termination, we show that the number of labelled formulas of each sequent in the invertible phase is bounded.

Let
\mathbf{R}^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime}\Rightarrow\Delta^{\prime}
be a sequent in the invertible phase. We first show that the number of different labels in

is bounded. A new label in
\Gamma^{\prime}
can only be created via the rules

and
\Diamond\text{-}l

\Gamma^{\prime}
, but this rule is not part of the invertible phase. Therefore, each new formula in
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a subformula of another formula in
\Gamma^{\prime}
. As there are only finitely many subformulas, and we only apply the rules to non-saturated sequents, one can only apply rules

and

Now for each label

\mathbf{R}^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime}\Rightarrow\Delta^{\prime}
is bounded.
∎

Now we set up the proof of Proposition
36

As mentioned in the main text, a classical countermodel-from-failed-proof search argument proceeds (very roughly) as follows: (1) Assume a formula is not provable, (2) For each rule instance there must be an unprovable premiss, (3) Continue in this way to construct an (infinite) ‘unprovable’ branch, (4) Extract a countermodel from this branch. In our setting we will need the branch obtained through the process above to be

\mathscr{P}{\sf IGL}
. However the local nature of the process above does not at all guarantee that this will be the case.

In the setting of classical
{\sf GL}

allow us to nonetheless carry out a naïve such argument. For the intuitionistic setting, where invertibe and non-invertible rules may interact in a nontrivial way, we must use more powerful machinery.

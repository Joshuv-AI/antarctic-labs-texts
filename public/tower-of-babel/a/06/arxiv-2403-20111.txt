Divisibility of Integer Laurent Polynomials, Homoclinic Points, and Lacunary Independence
Douglas Lind, Klaus Schmidt

Abstract
. Let f f , p p , and q q be Laurent polynomials with integer coefficients in one or several variables, and suppose that f f divides p + q p+q . We establish sufficient conditions to guarantee that f f individually divides p p and q q . These conditions involve a bound on coefficients, a separation between the supports of p p and q q , and, surprisingly, a requirement on the complex variety of f f called atorality satisfied by many but not all polynomials. Our proof involves a related dynamical system and the fundamental dynamical notion of homoclinic point. Without the atorality assumption our methods fail, and it is unknown whether our results hold without this assumption. We use this to establish exponential recurrence of the related dynamical system, and conclude with some remarks and open problems.

1.
Introduction

We begin with some notation and terminology, and then state our main results. In the next section we describe the dynamical context that underlies our proofs.

Let
d\geqslant 1
, and let
R_{d}=\mathbb{Z}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,x_{d}^{\pm 1}]
denote the ring of Laurent polynomials with integer coefficients in
d
commuting variables. We write
f\in R_{d}
as

f=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f_{\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{n}}
, where
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
,
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{d})
,

\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{n}}=x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{d}^{n_{d}}
,
and
f_{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
f_{\mathbf{n}}=0
for all but finitely many
\mathbf{n}
. The units in
R_{d}
are those elements of the form
\pm\,\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{n}}
. An element of
R_{d}
is
irreducible
provided that it is neither a unit nor a product of two non-units in
R_{d}
. We let
\langle f\rangle=fR_{d}
denote the principal ideal in
R_{d}
generated by
f
. The
support
of
f
is defined as
\operatorname{supp}f=\{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\colon f_{\mathbf{n}}\neq 0\}
, and we set
\|f\|_{\infty}=\max_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|f_{\mathbf{n}}|
. The
adjoint

f^{*}
of
f
is the polynomial with coefficients defined by
f^{*}_{\mathbf{n}}=f_{-\mathbf{n}}
. Let
\|\mathbf{n}\|=\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant d}\{|n_{j}|\}
.
For nonempty subsets
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we define their distance to be
\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{T})=\min_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{S},\mathbf{n}\in\mathcal{T}}\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{n}\|
.

Maps
v\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
are denoted by
v=(v_{\mathbf{n}})_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
. We let
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{R})
be the space of all such maps
v
for which
\|v\|_{1}=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|v_{\mathbf{n}}|<\infty
. This space is equipped with the convolution product

(1.1)

(v\cdot w)_{\mathbf{n}}=\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}v_{\mathbf{m}}w_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}}=\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}v_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}}w_{\mathbf{m}}.

If we view each
f=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f_{\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{n}}\in R_{d}
as the element
(f_{\mathbf{n}})_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{Z})\subset\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{R})
, we obtain an embedding
R_{d}\hookrightarrow\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{R})
in which multiplication of Laurent polynomials agrees with the convolution product (
1.1
) in
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{R})
.

In this paper we investigate divisibility of elements of
R_{d}
by a given irreducible element
f\in R_{d}
. Somewhat surprisingly, our results hinge on a property of
f
called
atorality
. Let
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
denote the additive torus, and
\mathbb{S}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon|z|=1\}
be its multiplicative counterpart.

Definition 1.1
.

The
unitary variety
of
f\in R_{d}
is defined to be

\mathsf{U}(f)=\{(t_{1},\dots,t_{d})\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\colon f(e^{2\pi it_{1}},\dots,e^{2\pi it_{d}})=0\}.

We remark that this is an additive version of the more usual definition of unitary variety as the intersection of the complex variety of
f
with
\mathbb{S}^{d}
.

According to the discussion in
[
10
]
*§1, the unitary variety
\mathsf{U}(f)
(or, more accurately, its multiplicative counterpart) is a real semialgebraic set. By the cell decomposition theorem for such sets, it is a finite disjoint union of cells of various dimensions. We can therefore define its dimension
\dim\mathsf{U}(f)
to be the maximal dimension of these cells, and this is known to be the same for all possible cell decompositions. If
\dim\mathsf{U}(f)=d
, then
f
vanishes on an open subset of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, and by holding all variables but one fixed, it is easy to show that
f=0
.

Definition 1.2
.

A Laurent polynomial
f\in R_{d}
is
atoral
if
\operatorname{dim}\mathsf{U}(f)\leqslant d-2
. This includes the possibility that
\mathsf{U}(f)=\varnothing
. If
\dim\mathsf{U}(f)=d-1
then
f
is called
toral
.

These notions were introduced by Agler, Starkus, and McCarthy
[
1
]
in the context of several complex variables.

Examples 1.3
.

(1) Every
f\in R_{d}
with
\mathsf{U}(f)=\varnothing
is atoral. The dynamical consequences of this case were extensively studied in
[
11
]
.

(2) If
f\in R_{1}
, then
f
is atoral if and only if
\mathsf{U}(f)=\varnothing
, i.e.,
f
has no roots of absolute value 1. For instance,
x^{2}-x-1
is atoral, while
x^{4}-x^{3}-x^{2}-x+1
is toral
[
10
]
*Exam. 4.2.

(3) If
f(x,y)=1+x+y\in R_{2}
, then
\mathsf{U}(f)=\{(\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3}),(\frac{2}{3},\frac{1}{3})\}
with dimension 0, and so
f
is atoral.

(4) If
f(x,y)=3+x+y+x^{-1}+y^{-1}\in R_{2}
, then according to
[
10
]
*Exam. 4.2

\mathsf{U}(f)=\bigl\{(s,t)\in\mathbb{T}^{2}:t=\pm\arccos\bigl(\tfrac{3}{2}-\cos 2\pi s\bigr),\ -\tfrac{1}{6}\leqslant s\leqslant\tfrac{1}{6}\bigr\},

a smooth closed curve of dimension 1. Hence
f
is toral.

(5) If
f(x,y,z)=1+x+y+z\in R_{3}
, then
\mathsf{U}(f)
is a union of three 1-dimensional circles
[
10
]
*Exam. 4.6, and so
f
is atoral.

(6) A consequence of
[
10
]
*§2 is that if
f\in R_{d}
is irreducible and if
f^{*}
is not a unit times
f
, then
f
is atoral. Thus it is very easy to create atoral examples.

Being atoral turns out to be equivalent to an invertibility property in
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{R})
established in
[
10
]
*Prop. 2.2 and Rem. 2.7.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
0\neq f\in R_{d}
. Then
f
is atoral if and only if there exists an element
f^{\#}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{R})
such that
f^{\#}\cdot f\in R_{d}\smallsetminus\langle f\rangle
.

The proof consists of using atorality to find an
h\in R_{d}\smallsetminus\langle f\rangle
so that
h/f
is smooth enough on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
to have absolutely convergent Fourier series, and then using the resulting Fourier coefficients to define
f^{\#}
. The element
f^{\#}
is obviously not unique. We shall call any such
f^{\#}
a
quasi-inverse
of
f
.

For every nonempty subset
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and integer
H\geqslant 1
we let

(1.2)

\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{S},H)=\{p\in R_{d}\colon\operatorname{supp}(p)\subseteq\mathcal{S}{\text{ and }}\|p\|_{\infty}\leqslant H\}.

The following result was proved in
[
6
]
*Thm. 7.3.

Theorem 1.5

(Gap Theorem)
.

Let
f\in R_{d}
be irreducible and atoral. For every
H\geqslant 1
there exists a number
M=M(f,H)\geqslant 1
with the following property. Suppose that
\mathcal{S}
,
\mathcal{T}
are nonempty subsets of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{T})\geqslant M
, and that
p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{S},H)
and
q\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{T},H)
. If
f
divides
p+q
, then
f
divides
p
and
f
divides
q
.

This theorem has two obvious corollaries.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
f\in R_{d}
be irreducible and atoral. For every
H\geqslant 1
there exists a number
M=M(f,H)\geqslant 1
with the following property. Suppose that
(\mathcal{S}_{i})_{i\in I}
is a finite or infinite collection of nonempty subsets of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{S}_{i},\mathcal{S}_{j})\geqslant M
whenever
i\neq j
. Let
F
be an arbitrary finite subset of
I
and let
p_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{S}_{i},H)
for every
i\in F
. If
f
divides
\sum_{i\in F}p_{i}
, then
f
divides every
p_{i}
for
i\in F
.

The second corollary is closely related to the notion of lacunary independence
[
12
]
and its dynamical consequences. We discuss this further in §
4
.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
f\in R_{d}
be irreducible and atoral. For every finite nonempty subset
\mathcal{F}\subset R_{d}
there exists a number
M=M(f,\mathcal{F})\geqslant 1
with the following property. For every finite subset
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{n}\|\geqslant M
for
\mathbf{m}\neq\mathbf{n}
in
\mathcal{S}
, and for every choice of
p^{(\mathbf{n})}\in\mathcal{F}
for
\mathbf{n}\in\mathcal{S}
, if
f
divides the sum
\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{n}}p^{(\mathbf{n})}
then
f
divides each
p^{(\mathbf{n})}
for
\mathbf{n}\in\mathcal{F}
.

Roughly speaking, this says that in the quotient ring
R_{d}/\langle f\rangle
, multiplying a fixed finite subset by sufficiently widely spaced monomials results in independent subsets of the quotient ring as an additive group, hence the terminology lacunary independence.

Although these statements about divisibility of polynomials are purely algebraic, they arose in a dynamical context which we discuss in the next section.

2.
Algebraic
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-actions

An
algebraic
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-action
is an action of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
by continuous automorphisms of a compact metrizable abelian group. Our divisibility results are proved using a particular kind of algebraic
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-action called a
principal action
.

Let
\mathbb{T}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
denote the compact abelian group consisting of all
t=(t_{\mathbf{n}})_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
with
t_{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{T}
for every
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Define the
shift action

\sigma
on
\mathbb{T}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
by
(\sigma^{\mathbf{m}}t)_{\mathbf{n}}=t_{\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{m}}
. Every
g=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\in R_{d}
defines a homomorphism
g(\sigma)=\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{\mathbf{n}}\sigma^{\mathbf{n}}
from
\mathbb{T}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
to itself. Fix such a
g
, and consider the closed, shift-invariant subgroup
X_{g}
of
\mathbb{T}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
defined by

(2.1)

X_{g}=\ker g(\sigma)=\Bigl\{t\in\mathbb{T}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\colon\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}t_{\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{n}}g_{\mathbf{n}}\equiv 0\pmod{1}\text{ for all $\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}$}\Bigr\}.

Let
\alpha_{g}
denote the restriction of
\sigma
to
X_{g}
. The dynamical system
(X_{g},\alpha_{g})
is called the
principal algebraic
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-action
corresponding to
g\in R_{d}
. When
g=0
this reduces to
X_{g}=\mathbb{T}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
and
\alpha_{g}=\sigma
.

In order to use analytical ideas, we linearize
(X_{g},\sigma_{g})
as follows. Let
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{R})
denote the space of all
v=(v_{\mathbf{n}})
for which
v_{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\|v\|_{\infty}=\sup_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|v_{\mathbf{n}}|<\infty
. Define the shift action
\overline{\sigma}
on
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{R})
by
(\overline{\sigma}^{\mathbf{m}}v)_{\mathbf{n}}=v_{\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{m}}
. For every
w\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{R})
we can define a bounded shift-commuting linear operator
w(\overline{\sigma})
on
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{R})
by
w(\overline{\sigma})=\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}w_{\mathbf{m}}\overline{\sigma}^{\mathbf{m}}
. Let
w^{*}
denote the adjoint element
w^{*}_{\mathbf{n}}=w_{-\mathbf{n}}
. Then

\bigl(w(\overline{\sigma})v\bigr)_{\mathbf{n}}=\sum_{mzd}w_{\mathbf{m}}(\overline{\sigma}^{\mathbf{m}}v)_{\mathbf{n}}=\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}w_{\mathbf{m}}v_{\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{m}}=(v\cdot w^{*})_{\mathbf{n}}.

Define the surjective homomorphism
\eta\colon\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{T}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
by
\eta(v)_{\mathbf{n}}=v_{\mathbf{n}}\pmod{1}
. If
w\in R_{d}
, then
\eta\circ w(\overline{\sigma})=w(\sigma)\circ\eta
.

An element
t\in X_{g}
is called a
summable homoclinic point
if there exists a
v\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{R})
such that
\eta(v)=t
. Such points provide a powerful way to localize behavior in algebraic
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-actions (see
[
11
]
and
[
10
]
for many applications), and are the essential ingredient to prove the results in §
1
.

3.
Proof of the Gap Theorem

Since the proof of Theorem
1.5
appearing in
[
6
]
*Theorem 7.3 is quite short, but still illustrates the effective use of dynamical ideas in proving algebraic results, we include it here for the reader’s convenience.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Suppose that
f\in R_{d}
is irreducible and atoral. Put
g=f^{*}
, and define the principal algebraic
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-action
(X_{g},\alpha_{g})
as above. By Theorem
1.4
we may fix a quasi-inverse
f^{\#}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{R})
of
f
such that
f^{\#}\cdot f=h\in R_{d}\smallsetminus\langle f\rangle
. Then

0=\eta(h)=\eta(f^{\#}\cdot f)=\eta(g(\overline{\sigma})\cdot f^{\#})=g(\sigma)\eta(f^{\#}),

so that
\eta(f^{\#})\in X_{g}
. If
\eta(f^{\#})
were equal to zero, then
f^{\#}
would be a polynomial, violating Theorem
1.4
. Hence
\eta(f^{\#})
is a nonzero summable homoclinic point in
X_{g}
.

Since
f^{\#}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{R})
, for every
H\geqslant 1
there is an
R=R(f,f^{\#},H)
such that

\sum_{\|\mathbf{n}\|\geqslant R}|f^{\#}_{\mathbf{n}}|<\frac{1}{2H\|f\|_{1}}.

For every nonempty subset
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, let
B_{R}(\mathcal{S})=\{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\colon\operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{n},\mathcal{S})\leqslant R\}
. We put
M=3R
, and show that this separation satisfies the conclusion of the theorem.

Suppose that
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
are nonempty subsets of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{T})\geqslant M=3R
, and let
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}
. Let
p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{S},H)
and
q\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{T},H)
, and put
r=p+q\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{U},H)
. We assume that
f
divides
r
, and will prove that
f
divides both
p
and
q
individually.

Since
f
divides
r
, there is a
k\in R_{d}
such that
r=k\cdot f
. Then

r\cdot f^{\#}=(k\cdot f)\cdot f^{\#}=k\cdot(f\cdot f^{\#})=k\cdot h\in R_{d}.

Furthermore, we claim that

\operatorname{supp}(r\cdot f^{\#})\subset B_{R}(\mathcal{U})=B_{R}(\mathcal{S})\cup B_{R}(\mathcal{T}).

For if
\mathbf{n}\notin B_{R}(\mathcal{U})
then
r_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}}=0
whenever
\|\mathbf{m}\|\leqslant R
. Hence

|(r\cdot f^{\#})_{\mathbf{n}}|=\Bigl|\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}r_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}}f^{\#}_{\mathbf{m}}\Bigr|\leqslant\|r\|_{\infty}\sum_{\|\mathbf{m}\|\geqslant R}|f^{\#}_{\mathbf{m}}|<H\cdot\frac{1}{2H\|f\|_{1}}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}.

Since
r\cdot f^{\#}=k\cdot h\in R_{d}
has integer coefficients, this shows that
\operatorname{supp}(r\cdot f^{\#})\subset B_{R}(\mathcal{U})
.

Now let
u
be the restriction of the integer polynomial
r\cdot f^{\#}
to
B_{R}(\mathcal{S})
, so that
u\in R_{d}
as well. We claim that
\|u-p\cdot f^{\#}\|_{\infty}<\frac{1}{2\|f\|_{1}}
.

Indeed, if
\mathbf{n}\in B_{R}(\mathcal{S})
, then
\operatorname{dist}(\mathbf{n},\mathcal{T})>R
, so that

\Bigl|(u-p\cdot f^{\#})_{\mathbf{n}}\Bigr|=\Bigl|(q\cdot f^{\#})_{\mathbf{n}}\bigr|=\Bigl|\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{T}}q_{\mathbf{m}}f^{\#}_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}}\Bigr|\leqslant\|q\|_{\infty}\sum_{\|\mathbf{k}\|\geqslant R}|f^{\#}_{\mathbf{k}}|<H\cdot\frac{1}{2H\|f\|_{1}}.

On the other hand, if
\mathbf{n}\notin B_{R}(\mathcal{S})
, then

|(u-p\cdot f^{\#})_{\mathbf{n}}|=|(p\cdot f^{\#})_{\mathbf{n}}|=\Bigl|\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{S}}p_{\mathbf{m}}f^{\#}_{\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{m}}\Bigr|\leqslant\|p\|_{\infty}\sum_{\|\mathbf{k}\|\geqslant R}|f^{\#}_{\mathbf{k}}|<H\cdot\frac{1}{2H\|f\|_{1}}.

Observe that both
p\cdot f^{\#}\cdot f=p\cdot h
and
u\cdot f
lie in
R_{d}
. Furthermore,

\|p\cdot f^{\#}\cdot f-u\cdot f\|_{\infty}\leqslant\|p\cdot f^{\#}-u\|_{\infty}\cdot\|f\|_{1}<\frac{1}{2}.

Hence
(p\cdot f^{\#}-u)\cdot f=0
. Thus
p\cdot h=p\cdot f^{\#}\cdot f=u\cdot f
. Since
f
is irreducible and does not divide
h
, it must divide
p
. Then
f
must also divide
q=r-p
.
∎

In order to interpret Corollary
1.7
for our purposes we first consider the trivial (but instructive) case
f=0
. In this case the Pontryagin dual
\widehat{X_{g}}
of the group
X_{g}=\mathbb{T}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
in (
2.1
) is equal to
R_{d}
(cf.
[
15
]
*Example 5.2 (1)): every character (i.e., continuous group homomorphism)
\chi\colon X_{g}\to\mathbb{T}
of
X_{g}
is of the form

(3.1)

t\mapsto\chi_{p}(t)=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}t_{\mathbf{n}}p_{\mathbf{n}}\pmod{1}

for some
p=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}p_{\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{n}}\in R_{d}
. If
p
and
q
are elements of
R_{d}
with disjoint supports, the corresponding characters are independent of each other; in particular, if
p+q=0
, then both
p
and
q
are equal to zero.

Now assume that
f\neq 0
. In this case the Pontryagin dual of the group
X_{g}
in (
2.1
) is equal to
R_{d}/\langle f\rangle
(
[
15
]
*Example 5.2 (2)): every character
\chi\in\widehat{X_{g}}
is again of the form
\chi_{p}\colon X_{g}\to\mathbb{T}
in (
3.1
) for some
p=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}p_{\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{n}}\in R_{d}
, but
\chi_{p}=\chi_{q}
whenever
p-q\in\langle f\rangle
. In particular, if
p\in\langle f\rangle
, then the corresponding character of
X_{g}
is trivial.

Although the notion of “support” no longer has an obvious meaning for characters of
X_{g}
, the map
p\mapsto\eta(p\cdot f^{\#})
maps an entire coset of
p
in
R_{d}/\langle f\rangle
to a single summable homoclinic point of
X_{g}
. By bounding the coefficients of
p
by
H
and using
R=R(f,f^{\#},H)
from the proof, most of the
\ell^{1}
-mass of
p\cdot f^{\#}
is located within
B_{R}(\operatorname{supp}p)
, and we can think of this set as the “approximate support” of
p
. If the supports of two polynomials
p
and
q
are widely separated, then so are their approximate supports, and multiplication of
p\cdot f^{\#}
and
q\cdot f^{\#}
by
f
leads to Laurent polynomials
p^{\prime}=p\cdot f^{\#}\cdot f
and
q^{\prime}=q\cdot f^{\#}\cdot f
with genuinely disjoint supports, in analogy with the trivial case.

4.
Lacunary Independence and Exponential Recurrence

In this section we discuss some dynamical implications of the results in §
1
. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a finite set,
\nu_{0}
be a probability measure on
\mathcal{A}
,
Y=\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
equipped with the product topology, and
\nu=\nu_{0}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
be the product measure on
Y
. Then the
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-shift
\sigma_{Y}
on
Y
preserves
\nu
, and
(Y,\nu,\sigma_{Y})
is called a
Bernoulli
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-shift
.

An algebraic
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-action
\alpha
on
X
automatically preserves the normalized Haar measure
\mu
on
X
. The action
\alpha
is called
Bernoulli
if there is a Bernoulli
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-shift
(Y,\nu,\sigma_{Y})
and a measure-preserving equivariant map
\pi\colon X\to Y
. If
\pi
can be chosen to be continuous off a null set of
X
, then
\alpha
is called
finitarily Bernoulli
. For instance, a hyperbolic toral automorphism generates a
\mathbb{Z}
-action that is finitarily Bernoulli since it has Markov partitions
[
7
]
. However, if the toral automorphism has eigenvalues of modulus 1, then although known to be Bernoulli, it is not known whether it is finitarily Bernoulli. These cases correspond to toral and atoral polynomials in one variable, respectively.

We address the question of whether analogues of this exist for algebraic
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-actions. Our starting point is the fact that if
f\in R_{d}
is irreducible, then
\alpha_{f}
is Bernoulli except for the special cases of so-called generalized cyclotomic polynomials (see
[
15
]
*§23 and
[
14
]
).

For an integer
R\geqslant 1
, let
B_{R}=\{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\|\mathbf{n}\|\leqslant R\}
. For an integer
M\geqslant 1
, let
B_{R,M}=B_{R}\cap M\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Let
|\cdot|
denote cardinality, and observe that

(4.1)

B_{R}\subset B_{R,M}+B_{M},\text{\quad and hence \quad}|B_{R,M}|\geqslant\frac{|B_{R}|}{|B_{M}|}.

As observed in
[
7
]
, the following condition is necessary for an algebraic action to be finitarily Bernoulli.

Definition 4.1
.

An algebraic
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-action
\alpha
on
(X,\mu)
is called
exponentially recurrent
provided that for every proper closed subset
K
of
X
there are constants
c>0
and
0<\theta<1
such that for all
R\geqslant 1
we have that

(4.2)

\mu\Bigl(\bigcap_{\mathbf{n}\in B_{R}}\alpha^{\mathbf{n}}K\Bigr)\leqslant c\,\theta^{|B_{R}|}.

The following shows that this condition is satisfied for some principal actions.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
f\in R_{d}
be irreducible and atoral. Then the principal algebraic
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-action
\alpha_{f}
is exponentially recurrent with respect to the normalized Haar measure
\mu_{f}
on
X_{f}
.

Proof.

Having already established lacunary independence in Corollary
1.7
, this proof follows the path in
[
12
]
*§2 where
d=1
.

Simplify notation by putting
X=X_{f}
,
\alpha=\alpha_{f}
, and
\mu=\mu_{f}
. Let
K
be a proper closed subset of
X
. Then
\mu(K)<1
. Fix
\eta
with
\mu(K)<\eta<1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
to be determined later. There is an open neighborhood
U
of
K
with
\mu(U\smallsetminus K)<\varepsilon
. By Urysohn’s lemma
[
5
]
*Lemma A.27 there is a continuous function
r
on
X
such that
0\leqslant r\leqslant 1
on
X
,
r=1
on
K
, and
r=0
on
X\smallsetminus U
. By the Stone–Weierstrass theorem
[
5
]
*Thm. 2.40, there are a finite subset
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, a positive integer
H
, and an
\mathbb{R}
-linear combination
\phi
of the characters
\{\chi_{p}:p\in\mathcal{F}\coloneqq\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{E},H)\}\subset\widehat{X_{g}}
(defined as in (
1.2
) and (
3.1
)) such that
\|r-\phi\|_{C(X)}<\varepsilon
. Let
\psi=\phi+\varepsilon
. Then
0\leqslant\psi\leqslant 1+\varepsilon
on
X
,
\psi\geqslant 1
on
K
, and
\psi<\varepsilon
on
X\smallsetminus U
. Hence

\int_{X}\psi\,d\mu\leqslant(1+\varepsilon)\mu(K)+(1+\varepsilon)\mu(U\smallsetminus K)+\varepsilon\mu(X\smallsetminus U)<\eta

provided that
\varepsilon
is small enough.

By Corollary
1.7
, there is an
M=M(f,\mathcal{F})
such that for every
R\geqslant 1
the sets
\{\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{n}}\mathcal{F}\colon\mathbf{n}\in B_{R,M}\}
are independent in the sense described there. This independence shows that the constant term in the product
\prod_{\mathbf{n}\in B_{R,M}}\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{n}}\psi
is the product of the constant terms of the factors, which are all equal to
\int_{X}\psi\,d\mu
. Hence using (
4.1
),

\displaystyle\mu\Bigl(\bigcap_{\mathbf{n}\in B_{R}}\alpha^{\mathbf{n}}K\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mu\Bigl(\bigcap_{\mathbf{n}\in B_{R,M}}\alpha^{\mathbf{n}}K\Bigr)\leqslant\int_{X}\Bigl(\prod_{\mathbf{n}\in B_{R,M}}\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{n}}\psi\Bigr)\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\prod_{\mathbf{n}\in B_{R,M}}\int_{X}\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{n}}\psi\,d\mu<\eta^{|B_{R,M}|}\leqslant\eta^{|B_{R}|/|B_{M}|}=\theta^{|B_{R}|},

where
\theta=\eta^{1/|B_{M}|}
satisfies condition (
4.2
) for an appropriate constant
c>0
.
∎

We remark that
[
12
]
proves exponential recurrence when
d=1
even for toral polynomials. For instance, if
f(x)=5x^{2}-6x+5
then both complex roots of
f
have absolute value 1, but there is a different argument using its

5.
Remarks and Questions

The proof of the Gap Theorem
1.5
depends crucially on the assumption that
f
is atoral, thereby providing a nonzero summable homoclinic point in the associated dynamical system. If
f
is toral it is known that no such points exist
[
9
]
*Prop. 2.2, and this method of proof fails from the very beginning. Ironically, for the toral
f
in Example
1.3
(4), the associated dynamical system has uncountably many points whose coordinates tend to 0 at
\infty
, but not fast enough to be summable (these points are provided by the Fourier coefficients of measures supported on the positively curved unitary variety
\mathsf{U}(f)
, see
[
11
]
*Exam. 7.3).

The possible extensions of results depending on atorality to the toral case typically involve delicate — and at present unresolved — Diophantine questions. For instance, the main result in
[
10
]
*Thm. 1.3 on the convergence of the number of periodic components to entropy uses summable homoclinic points. A completely different proof has been recently given in
[
3
]
*App. B using algebraic number theory to give effective rates of convergence, and in earlier work
[
4
]
this has been partially extended to the toral case using intricate Diophantine arguments.

Question 5.1
.

Is the Gap Theorem valid for irreducible toral polynomials in
R_{d}
?

Lacunary independence can be formulated quite generally. Say that a finite collection
\{E_{j}\colon 1\leqslant j\leqslant n\}
of finite subsets of an abelian group is
independent
if whenever
e_{j},e_{j}^{\prime}\in E_{j}
for all
j
and
\sum_{j=1}^{n}e_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}e_{j}^{\prime}
, then
e_{j}=e_{j}^{\prime}
for
1\leqslant j\leqslant n
. If this holds, then the cardinality of the sum of the
E_{j}
is the product of their cardinalities. Call an
R_{d}
-module
A

lacunarily independent
if for every finite subset
F\subset A
there is an
M=M(F,A)
such that if
\mathcal{S}
is a finite subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
\|\mathbf{m}-\mathbf{n}\|\geqslant M
for distinct
\mathbf{m},\ \mathbf{n}\in\mathcal{S}
, then the sets
\{\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{n}}\cdot F\colon\mathbf{n}\in\mathcal{S}\}
are independent.
Corollary
1.7
shows that if
f
is irreducible and atoral, then
R_{d}/\langle f\rangle
is lacunarily independent as an
R_{d}
-module.

Question 5.2
.

Which
R_{d}
-modules are lacunarily independent?

Some conditions beyond those in the Gap Theorem are clearly necessary. For instance, let
\mathfrak{p}
be the prime ideal
\langle 2,1+x+y\rangle
in
R_{2}
. Then in the quotient module
R_{2}/\mathfrak{p}
, the Frobenius squaring map shows that
(1+x+y)^{2^{n}}=1+x^{2^{n}}+y^{2^{n}}
for all
n\geqslant 1
. Hence
1+x+y
divides sums of monomials with unbounded gaps, and so
R_{2}/\mathfrak{p}
violates lacunary independence using the set
F=\{0,1\}
.

The construction of dynamical actions from Laurent polynomials described in §
1
can be carried out for every
R_{d}
-module
A
, resulting in an action
\alpha_{A}
by automorphisms of a compact abelian group
X_{A}
(the Pontryagin dual of
A
) which preserves the normalized Haar measure
\mu_{A}
(see
[
15
]
*Chap. II). The entropy of
\alpha_{A}
with respect to
\mu_{A}
coincides with its topological entropy, and we denote their common value by
h(\alpha_{A})
.

We are indebted to Hanfeng Li for his comments leading to the following consequences of lacunary independence.

Peters
[
13
]
showed how to compute the entropy of an automorphism of a compact abelian group using the dual automorphism of the discrete dual group. Chung and Li
[
2
]
pointed out that this idea extends to
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-actions (and even to actions by countable amenable groups), and the details are provided in
[
8
]
*§13.8.

Now suppose that
A
is a nonzero
R_{d}
-module that is lacunarily independent. Let
F
be a subset of
A
with two elements. The proof of Theorem
4.2
shows that there are
c>0
and
\gamma>0
such that

\biggl|\,\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in B_{R}}\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{n}}\cdot F\,\biggr|\geqslant c\,2^{\gamma\,|B_{R}|}

for all
R\geqslant 1
. By the extension of
[
13
]
mentioned above, we can conclude that
h(\alpha_{A})>0
. Furthermore, every nonzero submodule
B
of
A
is obviously lacunarily independent as well, so that
h(\alpha_{B})>0
. From this and
[
15
]
*Thm. 20.8 it follows that
\alpha_{A}
has completely positive entropy, and hence is Bernoulli
[
15
]
*§23. In particular, if
\mathfrak{a}
is a nonprincipal ideal in
R_{d}
, then
h(\alpha_{R_{d}/{\mathfrak{a}}})=0
by
[
15
]
*Prop. 17.5 and so
\alpha_{R_{d}/{\mathfrak{a}}}
is not lacunarily independent (for instance the case
\langle 2,1+x+y\rangle
discussed above, with a more direct argument).

Although the question of which
R_{d}
-modules correspond to finitarily Bernoulli actions is still open, even for
d=1
, the additional assumption of lacunary independence may provide an approach.

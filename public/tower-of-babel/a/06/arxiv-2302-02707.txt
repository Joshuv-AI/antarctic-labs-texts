Moving Least Squares Approximation using Variably Scaled Discontinuous Weight Function
Mohammad Karimnejad Esfahani , Stefano De Marchi , and Francesco Marchetti

Abstract
Functions with discontinuities appear in many applications such as image reconstruction, signal processing, optimal control problems, interface problems, engineering applications and so on. Accurate approximation and interpolation of these functions are therefore of great importance. In this paper, we design a moving least-squares approach for scattered data approximation that incorporates the discontinuities in the weight functions. The idea is to control the influence of the data sites on the approximant, not only with regards to their distance from the evaluation point, but also with respect to the discontinuity of the underlying function. We also provide an error estimate on a suitable piecewise Sobolev Space. The numerical experiments are in compliance with the convergence rate derived theoretically.

Introduction

In practical applications, over a wide range of studies such as surface reconstruction, numerical solution of differential equations and kernel learning
[

,

]
, one has to solve the problem of reconstructing an unknown function
f:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
sampled at some finite set of data sites
X=\{{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}}\}_{1\leq\mathnormal{i}\leq N}\subset\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with corresponding data values
f_{\mathnormal{i}}=f({\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}}),\;1\leq\mathnormal{i}\leq N
. Since in practice the function values
f_{\mathnormal{i}}
are sampled at scattered points, and not at a uniform grid, Meshless (or meshfree) Methods (MMs) are used as an alternative of numerical mesh-based approaches, such as Finite Elements Method (FEM) and Finite Differences (FD). The idea of MMs could be traced back to
[
4
]
. Afterwards, multivariate MMs existed under many names and were used in different contexts; interested readers are referred to
[
5
]
for an overview over MMs. In a general setting, MMs are designed, at least partly, to avoid the use of an underlying mesh or triangulation. The approximant of
f
at
X
can be expressed in the form

s_{f,X}({\bf x})=\sum_{\mathnormal{i}=1}^{N}\alpha_{\mathnormal{i}}({\bf x})f_{\mathnormal{i}}.

(1)

One might seek a function
s_{f,X}
that interpolates the data, i.e.
s_{f,X}({\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}})=f_{\mathnormal{i}},\;1\leq\mathnormal{i}\leq N
, and in this case

will be the
cardinal functions
. However, one might consider a more generalized framework known as

only approximates the data, i.e.,
s_{f,X}({\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}})\approx f_{\mathnormal{i}}
. The latter case means that we prefer to let the approximant
s_{f,X}
only nearly fits the function values. This is useful, for instance, when the given data contain some noise, or the number of data is too large. The standard approach to deal with such a problem is to compute the Least-Squares (LS) solution, i.e., one minimizes the error (or cost) function

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathnormal{i}=1}^{N}[s_{f,X}({\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}})-f_{\mathnormal{i}}]^{2}.

(2)

A more generalized setting of LS is known as the weighted LS

, in which (
2
) turns to

\sum_{\mathnormal{i}=1}^{N}[s_{f,X}({\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}})-f_{\mathnormal{i}}]^{2}w({\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}}),

(3)

which is ruled by the
weighted

\ell_{2}
inner product. In practice the role of

is to add more flexibility to the LS formulation for data
f_{\mathnormal{i}}
that influence the approximation process, which are supposed, for example, to be affected by some noise. However, these methods are global in the sense that all data sites have influence on the solution at any evaluation point
{\bf x}\in\Omega
. Alternatively, for a fixed evaluation point
{\bf x}
, one can consider only
n
-th closest data sites
{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}},\,\mathnormal{i}=1,\ldots,n

. The
Moving Least-Squares
(MLS) method, which is a

{\bf x}
one needs to solve a

problem, minimizing

\sum_{\mathnormal{i}=1}^{N}[s_{f,X}({\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}})-f_{\mathnormal{i}}]^{2}w({\bf x},{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}})

by choosing the weight functions

few

{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}}
and
{\bf x}
. Consequently, for each evaluation point
{\bf x}
, a small linear system needs to be solved. Also, one can let
w(\cdot,{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}})
be a radial function i.e.,
w({\bf x},{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}})=\varphi(\lVert{\bf x}-{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}}\lVert_{2})

\varphi:[0,\infty)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Doing in this way,
w(\cdot,{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}})
inherits the translation invariance property of radial basis functions. We mention that (
4
) could be generalized as well by letting
w_{\mathnormal{i}}(\cdot)=w(\cdot,{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}})
moves with respect to a
reference
point
{\bf y}
such that
{\bf y}\neq{\bf x}
.

The earliest idea of MLS approximation technique can be traced back to Shepard’s seminal paper
[
6

, in which the author considered the approximation by constants. Later on, the general framework of MLS was introduced by Lancaster and Salkauskas in
[

[
8
]
the author analyzed the connection between MLS and the Backus-Gilbert approach

]
, and showed that the method is effective for derivatives approximations as well. Since then, MLS method showed its effectiveness in different applications
[
10
,
11
]
. The error analysis of MLS approximation has been provided by some authors, mainly based on the work of Levin
[
12
]
. In
[

and
[

the author suggested error bounds that take into account the so-called

[
15
]
, in which the authors provided error estimates in

for the function and its first derivatives in the one dimensional case, then
[
16
]
, where they generalized this approach to the multi-dimensional case. In both these works, the error analysis is based on the
support

[

[
18

The MLS method has rarely been used for approximating piecewise-continuous functions, i.e, functions that possess some discontinuities or jumps. In this case, it would be essential that the approximant takes into account the location of the discontinuities. To this end, in this paper we let the weight function be a
Variably Scaled Discontinuous Kernel

19
]

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

The error analysis shows that the MLS-VSDKs approximation can outperform classical MLS schemes as the discontinuities of the underlying function are assimilated into the weight function. In Section

Preliminaries on MLS and VSKs

2.1
Moving Least Squares (MLS) approximation

In this introduction to MLS, we resume and deepen what outlined in the previous section. The interested readers are also refereed to
[
22
, Chap. 22]
.

Let
\Omega
be a non-empty and bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
X
be the set of
N
distinct data sites (or centers). We consider the target function
f
, and the corresponding function values
f_{\mathnormal{i}}
as defined above. Moreover,
\mathcal{P}^{d}_{\ell}
indicates the space of
d
-variate polynomials of degree at most
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, with basis
\{p_{1},...,p_{Q}\}
and dimension
Q={\ell+d\choose d}
.

Several equivalent formulations exist for the MLS approximation scheme. As the standard formulation, the MLS approximant looks for the best weighted approximation to
f
at the evaluation point
{\bf x}
in
\mathcal{P}^{d}_{\ell}
(or any other linear space of functions
\mathcal{U}
), with respect to the discrete
\ell_{2}
norm induced by the weighted inner product
\langle f,g\rangle_{w_{{\bf x}}}=\sum_{\mathnormal{i}=1}^{N}w({\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}},{\bf x})f({\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}})g({\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}})
. Mathematically speaking, the MLS approximant will be the linear combination of the polynomial basis i.e.,

s_{f,X}({\bf x})=\sum_{\mathnormal{j}=1}^{Q}c_{\mathnormal{j}}({\bf x})p_{\mathnormal{j}}({\bf x}),

(5)

where the coefficients are obtained by locally minimizing the weighted least square error in (
4
), which is equivalent to minimizing
\lVert f-s_{f}\lVert_{w_{{\bf x}}}
. We highlight that the local nature of the approximant is evident from the fact that the coefficient
c_{\mathnormal{j}}({\bf x})
must be computed for each evaluation point
{\bf x}
.

In another formulation of MLS approximation known as the
Backus-Gilbert
approach, one considers the approximant
s_{f,X}({\bf x})
to be a
quasi interpolant
of the form (
1
). In this case, one seeks the values of the basis functions
\alpha_{\mathnormal{i}}({\bf x})
(also known as generating or shape functions) as the minimizers of

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathnormal{i}=1}^{N}\alpha_{\mathnormal{i}}^{2}({\bf x})\frac{1}{w({\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}},{\bf x})}

(6)

subject to the polynomial reproduction constraints

\sum_{\mathnormal{i}=1}^{N}p({\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}})\alpha_{\mathnormal{i}}({\bf x})=p({\bf x}),\hskip 11.74988pt\text{for all}\;p\in\mathcal{P}_{\ell}^{d}.

Such a constrained quadratic minimization problem can be converted to a
system of linear equations by introducing Lagrange multipliers
\bm{\lambda}({\bf x})=[\lambda_{1}({\bf x}),...,\lambda_{Q}({\bf x})]^{T}
. Consequently (e.g see
[
13
, Corollary 4.4]
), the MLS basis function
\alpha_{\mathnormal{i}}
evaluated at
{\bf x}
is given by

\alpha_{\mathnormal{i}}({\bf x})=w({\bf x},{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}})\sum_{k=1}^{Q}\lambda_{k}({\bf x})p_{k}({\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}}),\hskip 11.74988pt1\leq\mathnormal{i}\leq N,

(7)

such that
\lambda_{k}({\bf x})
are the unique solution of

\sum_{k=1}^{Q}\lambda_{k}({\bf x})\sum_{\mathnormal{i}=1}^{N}w({\bf x},{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}})p_{k}({\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}})p_{s}({\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}})=p_{s}({\bf x}),\hskip 11.74988pt1\leq s\leq Q.

(8)

We observe that the weight function
w_{\mathnormal{i}}({\bf x})=w({\bf x},{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}})
controls the influence of the center
{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}}
over the approximant, so it should be
small
when evaluated at a point that is far from
{\bf x}
, that is it should decay to zero fast enough. To this end we may let
w_{\mathnormal{i}}({\bf x})
be positive on a ball centered at
{\bf x}
with radius
r
,
B({\bf x},r)
, and zero outside. For example, a compactly supported radial kernel satisfies such a behaviour. Thus, let
I({\bf x})=\{\mathnormal{i}\in\{1,\ldots,N\},\lVert{\bf x}-{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}}\lVert_{2}\leq r\}
be the family of indices of the centers
X
, for which
w_{\mathnormal{i}}({\bf x})>0
, with
\lvert I\lvert=n\ll N
. Only the centers
{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}}\in I
influence the approximant
s_{f,X}({\bf x})
. Consequently, the matrix representation of (
7
) and (
8
) is

\displaystyle{\bm{\alpha}}({\bf x})

\displaystyle=W({\bf x})P^{T}{\bm{\lambda}}({\bf x}),

\displaystyle{\bm{\lambda}}({\bf x})

\displaystyle=(PW({\bf x})P^{T})^{-1}{\bf p}({\bf x}),

where
{\bm{\alpha}}({\bf x})=[\alpha_{1}({\bf x}),...,\alpha_{n}({\bf x})]^{T}
,
W({\bf x})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is the diagonal matrix carrying the weights
w_{\mathnormal{i}}({\bf x})
on its diagonal,
P\in\mathbb{R}^{Q\times n}
such that its
k
-th row contains
p_{k}
evaluated at data sites in
I({\bf x})
, and
{\bf p}({\bf x})=[p_{1}({\bf x}),...,p_{Q}({\bf x})]^{T}
. More explicitly the basis functions are given by

{\bm{\alpha}}({\bf x})=W({\bf x})P^{T}(PW({\bf x})P^{T})^{-1}{\bf p}({\bf x}).

(9)

Moreover, it turns out that the solution of (
5
) is identical to the solution offered by the Backus-Gilbert approach (see e.g.
[
13
, Chap. 3 & 4]
).

In the MLS literature, it is known that a local polynomial basis shifted to the evaluation point
{\bf x}\in\Omega
leads to a more stable method (see e.g.
[
13
, Chap. 4]
). Accordingly, we let the polynomial basis to be
\{1,(\cdot-{\bf x}),\dots,(\cdot-{\bf x})^{\ell}\}
, meaning that different bases for each evaluation point are employed. In this case, since with standard monomials basis we have
p_{1}\equiv 1
and
p_{k}(0)=0
for
2\leq k\leq Q
, then
{\bf p}({\bf x})=[1,0,...,0]^{T}
.

To ensure the invertibility of
PW({\bf x})P^{T}
in (
9
),
X
needs to be
\mathbb{P}^{d}_{\ell}
-unisolvent. Then as long as
w_{\mathnormal{i}}({\bf x})
is positive,
PW({\bf x})P^{T}
will be a positive definite matrix, and so invertible; more details are available in
[
22
, Chap. 22]
.

Furthermore, thanks to equation (
7
), it is observable that the behaviour of
\alpha_{\mathnormal{i}}({\bf x})
is heavily influenced by the behaviour of the weight functions
w_{\mathnormal{i}}({\bf x})
, in particular it includes continuity and the support of the basis functions
\alpha_{\mathnormal{i}}({\bf x})
. Another significant feature is that the weight functions
w_{\mathnormal{i}}({\bf x})
which are singular at the data sites lead to cardinal basis functions i.e.,
\alpha_{\mathnormal{i}}({\bf x}_{\mathnormal{j}})=\delta_{\mathnormal{i},\mathnormal{j}}\;\mathnormal{i},\mathnormal{j}=1,...,n
, meaning that MLS scheme interpolates the data (for more details see
[
12
, Theorem 3]
).

We also recall the following definitions that we will use for the error analysis.

1.

A set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is said to satisfy an
interior cone condition
if there exists an angle
\Theta\in(0,\pi/2)
and a radius

so that for every
{\bf x}\in\Omega
a unit vector
\xi({\bf x})
exists such that the cone

\Omega
.

2.

A set

with

is called
\mathbb{P}^{d}_{\ell}
-unisolvent if the zero polynomial is the only polynomial from
\mathbb{P}^{d}_{\ell}
that vanishes on
X
.

3.

The
fill distance
is defined as

h_{X,\Omega}=\underset{{\bf x}\in\Omega}{\sup}\underset{1\leq j\leq N}{\min}\lVert{\bf x}-{\bf x}_{j}\lVert_{2}.

4.

The

q_{X}=\frac{1}{2}\underset{i\neq j}{\min}\lVert{\bf x}_{i}-{\bf x}_{j}\lVert.

5.

The set of data sites

is said to be
quasi-uniform
with respect to a constant

Getting back to the MLS scheme, let

be a derivative operator such that
\lvert\bm{\delta}\lvert\leq\ell

2.3
Variably Scaled Discontinuous Kernels (VSDKs)

Variably Scaled Kernels (VSKs) were firstly introduced in
[
20
]
. The basic idea behind them is to map the data sites from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
via a scaling function
\psi:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
and to construct an augmented approximation space in which the data sites are
\{({\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}},\psi({\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}}))\;\mathnormal{i}=1,...,N\}
(see
[
20
, Def. 2.1]
). Though the first goal of doing so was getting a
better
nodes distribution in the augmented dimension, later on in
[
19
]
the authors came up with the idea of also encoding the behaviour of the underlying function
f
inside the scale function
\psi
. Precisely, for the target function
f
that possesses some jumps, the key idea is the following.

Definition 1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\{\Omega_{1},...,\Omega_{n}\}
be a partition of
\Omega
and let
{\bm{\beta}}=(\beta_{1},...,\beta_{n})
be a vector of real distinct values. Moreover, assume that all the jump discontinuities of the underlying function
f
lie on
\bigcup_{\mathnormal{j}=1}^{n}{\partial\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}}}
. The piecewise constant scaling function
\psi_{\mathcal{P},{\bm{\beta}}}
with respect to the partition
\mathcal{P}
and the vector
{\bm{\beta}}
is defined as

\psi_{\mathcal{P},{\bm{\beta}}}({\bf x})|_{\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}}}=\beta_{\mathnormal{j}},\;{\bf x}\in\Omega.

Successively, let
\Phi^{\varepsilon}
be a positive definite radial kernel on
\Omega\times\Omega
that depends on the shape parameter
\varepsilon>0
. A variably scaled discontinuous kernel on
(\Omega\times\mathbb{R})\times(\Omega\times\mathbb{R})
is defined as

\Phi^{\varepsilon}_{\psi}({\bf x},{\bf y})=\Phi^{\varepsilon}\big(\Psi({\bf x}),\Psi({\bf y})\big),\hskip 11.74988pt{\bf x},{\bf y}\in\Omega.

(12)

such that
\Psi({\bf x})=({\bf x},\psi({\bf x}))
.

Moreover, we point out that if
\Phi^{\varepsilon}
is (strictly) positive definite then so is
\Phi^{\varepsilon}_{\psi}
, and if
\Phi^{\varepsilon}
and
\psi
are continuous then so is
\Phi^{\varepsilon}_{\psi}

[
20
, Theorem 2.2]
. Figure

are assimilated into the kernel

.

MLS-VSDKs

Let
f
be a function with some jump discontinuities defined on
\Omega
,
\mathcal{P}
and
\psi_{\mathcal{P},\beta}
as in Definition
1
. We look for the MLS approximant with
variably scaled discontinuous weight function
such that

w_{\psi}({\bf x},{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}})=w(\Psi({\bf x}),\Psi({\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}})).

(13)

Above all, we point out that in this case the diagonal matrix
W({\bf x})
in (
9
) still carries only positive values by assumption, and therefore the equation (
9
) is still solvable meaning that the basis functions
\alpha({\bf x})
uniquely exist. However, with new weight functions, from (
13
) also
\alpha({\bf x})
might be continuous or discontinuous regarding to the given data values
f_{\mathnormal{i}}
. Therefore our basis functions are indeed data-dependent thanks to (
13
). From now on, we call this scheme MLS-VSDK, and we will denote the corresponding approximant as
s_{f,X}^{\psi}
.

Since the basis functions are data dependent, one might expect that the space in which we express the error bound should be data dependent as well. Towards this idea, for
k\in\mathbb{Z},\;0\leq k
, and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we define the
piecewise
Sobolev Spaces

{\cal W}^{k}_{p}(\Omega)=\{f:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\;\text{s.t.}\;f_{\lvert_{\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}}}}\in W_{p}^{k}(\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}}),\hskip 11.74988pt\mathnormal{j}\in\{1,...,n\}\},

where
f_{\lvert_{\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}}}}
denotes the restriction of
f
to
\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}}
, and
W_{p}^{k}(\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}})
denote the Sobolev space on
\Omega_{\mathnormal{i}}
. We endow
{\cal W}^{k}_{p}(\Omega)
with the norm

\lVert f\lVert_{{\cal W}_{p}^{k}(\Omega)}=\sum_{\mathnormal{j}=1}^{n}\lVert f\lVert_{W_{p}^{k}(\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}})}.

(14)

When
k=0
we simply denote
{\cal W}_{p}^{0}(\Omega)
by
{{\cal L}^{p}}(\Omega)
. Moreover, it could be shown that for any partition of
\Omega
the standard Sobolev space
W_{p}^{k}(\Omega)
is contained in
{\cal W}_{p}^{k}(\Omega)
(see
[
21
]
and reference therein). We assume that every set
\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}}\in\mathcal{P}
satisfies Lipschitz boundary conditions which will be essential for our error analysis.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be as in Definition
1
and set the derivative order
\bm{\delta}=0
. Then, by using Theorem (
1
), the error satisfies the inequality

\lVert f-s_{f,X}^{\psi}\lVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}})}\leq C_{\mathnormal{j}}h_{\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}}}^{\ell+1-d(1/p-1/2)_{+}}\lVert f\lVert_{W_{p}^{\ell+1}(\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}})},\hskip 11.74988pt\hskip 11.74988pt\text{for all}\;\;\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}}\in\mathcal{P}

(15)

with
h_{\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}}}
the fill distance with respect to
\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}}
.

Proof.

Recalling Definition
1
we know that the discontinuities of
f
and subsequently
w_{\mathnormal{i}}(\cdot)
are located only at the boundary and not on the domain
\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}}
, meaning that
w_{\mathnormal{i}}(\cdot)
is continuous inside
\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}}
. Furthermore, the basis
\{\alpha_{\mathnormal{i}}({\bf x})\}_{1\leq\mathnormal{i}\leq n}
forms a local polynomial reproduction i.e., there exists a constant
C
such that
\sum_{\mathnormal{i}=1}^{N}\lvert\alpha_{\mathnormal{i}}\lvert\leq C
. Letting
s=1
and
q=2
, by noticing that
W_{q}^{0}(\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}})=L^{q}(\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}})
, then the error bound (
15
) is an immediate consequence of Theorem (
1
).
∎

From the above proposition, it could be understood that
s_{f,X}^{\psi}
behaves similarly to
s_{f,X}
in the domain
\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}}
, where there is no discontinuity. This is in agreement with Definition
1
. Consequently, it is required to extend the error bound (
15
) to the whole domain
\Omega
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
f
,
\mathcal{P}
,
\psi_{\mathcal{P},\beta}
be as before, and the weight functions as in (
13
). Then, for
\ell>\lvert\bm{\delta}\lvert+d/p
(equality also holds for
p=1
), and
f\in{\cal W}_{p}^{\ell+1}(\Omega)
, for the MLS-VSDK approximant
s_{f,X}^{\psi}
the error can be bounded as follows:

\lVert f-s^{\psi}_{f,X}\lVert_{{\cal L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C{h}^{\ell+1-d(1/p-1/2)_{+}}\lVert f\lVert_{{\cal W}_{p}^{\ell+1}(\Omega)}

(16)

Proof.

By Proposition (
1
), we know that (
15
) holds for each
\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}}
. Let
h_{X,\Omega_{\mathnormal{i}}}
and
C_{\mathnormal{i}}
be the fill distance and a constant associated with each
\Omega_{\mathnormal{i}}
, respectively. Then, we have

\sum_{\mathnormal{j}=1}^{n}\lVert f-s_{f,X}^{\psi}\lVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}})}\leq\sum_{\mathnormal{j}=1}^{n}C_{\mathnormal{j}}h_{X,\Omega_{\mathnormal{i}}}^{\ell+1-d(1/p-1/2)_{+}}\lVert f\lVert_{W_{p}^{\ell+1}(\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}})}.

By definition we get
\sum_{\mathnormal{j}=1}^{n}\lVert f-s_{f,X}^{\psi}\lVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}})}=\lVert f-s_{f,X}^{\psi}\lVert_{{\cal L}^{2}(\Omega)}
. Moreover, letting
C=\max\{C_{1},...,C_{n}\}
and
h=max\{h_{X,\Omega_{1}},...,h_{X,\Omega_{n}}\}
then the right hand side can be bounded by

Ch^{\ell+1-d(1/p-1/2)_{+}}\lVert f\lVert_{{\cal W}_{p}^{\ell+1}(\Omega)}.

Putting these together we conclude.
∎

Some remarks are in order.

1.

One might notice that the error bound in (
11
) is indeed local (the basis functions are local by assumption), meaning that if
f
is less smooth in a subregion of
\Omega
, say it possesses only
\ell^{{}^{\prime}}\leq\ell
continuous derivatives there, then the approximant (interpolant) has order
\ell^{{}^{\prime}}+1
in that region and this is the best we can get. On the other hand according to (
16
), thanks to the definition of piecewise Sobolev space, the regularity of the underlying function in the interior of the subdomain
\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}}
matters. In other words, as long as
f
possesses regularity of order
\ell
in subregions, say
\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}}
and
\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}+1}
, the approximant order of
\ell+1
is achievable, regardless of the discontinuities on the boundary of
\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}}
and
\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}+1}
.

2.

Another interesting property of the MLS-VSDK scheme is that it is indeed data dependent. To clarify, for the evaluation point
{\bf x}\in\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}}
take two data sites
{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}},\;{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}+1}\in B({\bf x},r)
with the same distance from
{\bf x}
such that
{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}}\in\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}}
and
{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}+1}\in\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}+1}
. Due to the definition (
12
),
w_{\psi}({\bf x},{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}+1})
decays to zero faster than
w_{\psi}({\bf x},{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}})
i.e., the data sites from the same subregion
\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}}
pay more contribution to the approximant (interpolant)
s_{f,X}^{\psi}
, rather than the one from another subregion
\Omega_{\mathnormal{j}+1}
beyond a discontinuity line
MK: is line correct for high dimension?
. On the other hand in the classical MLS scheme, this does not happen as the weight function gives the same value to both
{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}}
and
{\bf x}_{\mathnormal{i}+1}
.

3.

We highlight that in MLS-VSDK scheme we do not scale polynomials and so the polynomial space
\mathcal{P}^{d}_{\ell}
is not changed. We scale only the weight functions and thus, in case the given function values bear discontinuities, the basis functions
\{\alpha_{\mathnormal{i}}(\cdot)\}_{1\leq\mathnormal{i}\leq n}
are modified.

We end this section by recalling that the MLS approximation convergence order is achievable only in the
stationary setting
, i.e., the shape parameter
\varepsilon
must be scaled with respect to the fill distance. It leads to
peaked basis functions
for densely spaced data and
flat basis function
for coarsely spaced data. In other words, the local support of the weight functions
B({\bf x},r)
, and subsequently basis functions must be tuned with regards to the
h_{X,\Omega}
using the shape parameter
\varepsilon
. Consequently, this holds also in MLS-VSDK scheme, meaning that after scaling
w_{\mathnormal{i}}
we still need to take care of
\varepsilon
. This is different with respect to VS(D)Ks interpolation where
\varepsilon=1
was kept fixed
[
20
,
19
]
.

1.

Matérn

5
Conclusions

To approximate a discontinuous function using scattered data values, we studied a new technique based on the use of discontinuously scaled weight functions, that we called the MLS-VSDK scheme, that is the application of discontinuous scaled weight functions to the MLS. It enabled us to move toward a data-dependent scheme, meaning that MLS-VSDK is able to encode the behavior of the underlying function. We obtained a theoretical Sobolev-type error estimate which justifies why MLS-VSDK can outperform conventional MLS. The numerical experiments confirmed the theoretical convergence rates. Besides, our numerical tests showed that the suggested scheme can reach high accuracy even if the position of the data values are slightly perturbed.

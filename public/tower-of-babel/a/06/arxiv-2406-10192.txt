On Graph Odd Edge-Colorings and Odd Edge-Coverings
Xiao-Chuan Liu, Mirko Petruševski, Xu Yang

Abstract
. An odd k k -edge-coloring of a graph G G is a (not necessarily proper) edge-coloring with at most k k colors such that each non-empty color class induces a graph in which every vertex is of odd degree; similarly, if more than one color per edge is allowed, we speak of an odd k k -edge-covering of G G . In this paper, we fully resolve two major conjectures on odd edge-colorings and odd edge-coverings of graphs, proposed by Petruševski and Škrekovski ( European Journal of Combinatorics, 91:103225, 2021). The first conjecture states that, apart from two particular exceptions which are respectively odd 5 5 - and odd- 6 6 -edge-colorable, for any other loopless and connected graph G G there exists an edge e e such that G \ { e } G\backslash\{e\} is odd 3 3 -edge-colorable. The second conjecture states that any simple graph G G admits an odd 3 3 -edge-covering in which at most one edge receives more than one color. In addition, we strongly confirm the second conjecture by demonstrating that there exists an odd 3 3 -edge-covering in which at most one edge receives two colors and the rest of the edges receive unique colors.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider only finite undirected loopless graphs, which may contain multiple edges unless specified otherwise as simple graphs. Throughout this paper, we employ the graph notation from
[

For a graph

of

to the edges of

which provides an edge decomposition of

Similarly, an

of

is an assignment of colors from
[k]
to the edges of

Equivalently, we can denote an edge-coloring by a mapping
\varphi:E(G)\to[k]
. In the case of an edge-covering, we denote it by a set-valued mapping
\psi:E(G)\to 2^{[k]}\backslash\emptyset
.
If

,

is the set of edges incident with

. Each

with an even (resp. odd) degree

is an
even
(resp.
odd
)
vertex
of

. A graph is
even
(resp.

)
if all its vertices are even (resp. odd).
We say

odd

-edge-colorable

(respectively,
odd
k
-edge-coverable
) if there is an edge-coloring (respectively, an edge-covering)
of

by at most
k
odd subgraphs, which we call an odd

-edge-coloring (respectively, an odd
k
-edge-covering).
In other words, the coloring map or the covering map

\varphi
is required to satisfy that
any non-empty edge set

E_{j}:=\{e\in E(G):e\in\varphi^{-1}(j)\}

\chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)
(respectively,
\text{cov}_{\text{odd}}(G)
) to be the least integer

such that

is odd

k_{0}
-edge-colorable (respectively, odd
k_{0}
-edge-coverable).
Equivalently,
\chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)
(respectively,
\text{cov}_{\text{odd}}(G)
) is the least integer

k_{0}
such that the edge set of
G
admits a decomposition (respectively, cover) comprised of
k_{0}
odd subgraphs.
An obvious necessary and sufficient condition for odd edge-colorability or odd edge-coverability of

is the absence of vertices incident only to loops. Apart from this, the presence of loops
does not influence the existence nor changes the values of the parameters
\chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)
and
\text{cov}_{\text{odd}}(G)
. Therefore, while studying odd edge-colorability and odd edge-coverability, we may without loss of generality restrict ourselves to loopless connected graphs.

The topics of determining
\chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)
and
\text{cov}_{\text{odd}}(G)
for a graph

have been very active in recent years, and we refer to the recent survey paper
[
6
]
for the history, references, and discussions on many related problems, ranging from theoretical problems on graphs and multigraphs to problems involving algorithms.

As an initiating result in this area, Pyber

proved the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(
[

]
) For any

.
If

\chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)\leq 3

It was also observed in
[
8
]
that the upper bound of

in the previous result can be achieved by
the wheel graph

. Lužar, Petruševski, and Škrekovski
[

showed that connected loopless (multi)graphs are odd 5-edge-colorable, with one particular exception that is odd 6-edge-colorable. Before proceeding, we describe the exception(s). Given a loopless graph
G
, a

is the set of parallel edges on a pair of adjacent vertices. In particular, for adjacent vertices

, the
vw
-bouquet

is the intersection

. A

is a loopless graph on three pairwise adjacent vertices. If

are parities of the sizes of its bouquets in non-increasing order, with

(resp.

Noting that the edge set of every odd subgraph of a Shannon triangle is fully contained in a single bouquet, it can be verified that

\chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(S_{p,q,r})=p+q+r=\text{cov}_{\text{odd}}(S_{p,q,r})
. In particular,
\chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(S_{2,2,2})=6
and
\chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(S_{2,2,1})=5
. In 2017, Petruševski
[
4

proved that the above two families of graphs are the only exceptions -

-edge-colorable. Furthermore, Petruševski
[
4
]
also proved that when

, a color class can be reduced to a size of at most 2. In the end of the paper, the author raised a natural question regarding the existence of an edge-analogue of the second part of Theorem
1.1

is odd
4
-edge-chromatic, is it always possible to delete a particular edge (instead of a vertex), such that the resulting graph is odd
3
-edge-colorable?
Additionally, the following conjecture was formally raised later in
[
6
]
.

Conjecture 1.2
.

(
Conjecture 2.14 of
[
6
]
)
For any connected loopless graph which is not a Shannon triangle of type

or type
(2,2,1)
,

or there exists a special edge

-edge-colorable.

A considerable effort towards the above conjecture was already made in
[
4
]
. Note that an equivalent way to express the conjecture is that, assuming
\chi_{\text{odd}}^{\prime}(G)=4
, there exists an odd

4
-edge-coloring in which the color

Another related result, presented in
[
7

, provided a partial answer by
confirming the conclusion
for the special class of subdivisions of odd graphs.
Now we are ready to state the first result of this paper, which fully resolves the above conjecture positively.

Theorem 1.3
.

Any connected loopless graph
G
that is not a Shannon triangle of type
(2,2,2)
or
(2,2,1)
is either odd
3
-edge-colorable or admits an odd

-edge-coloring with color set

Now we move to graph coverability. Pyber also inquired in
[
8
]

whether three colors are sufficient to give an odd edge-covering for any simple graph

[
2
]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

(
[
2
]
)
For any connected simple graph
G
,
\text{cov}_{\text{odd}}(G)\leq 3
.

This result was later generalized by Petruševski and Škrekovski
[
5
]
to multigraphs, by characterizing which multigraphs are odd
3
-edge-coverable. In addition, as a connection between

and
\text{cov}_{\text{odd}}(G)
, as well as a tool for obtaining more structural information about edge colors and edges, Petruševski and Škrekovski
[

]
defined the overlapping function. In more detail,
for a

-edge-covering

,
let

be the sum of sizes of all images under

that are not singletons. The following are some examples of the values of the function

:

\text{ovl}(\phi)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ if }\phi\text{ is an edge-coloring.}\\
2,&\text{ if }\phi\text{ has exactly one edge receiving two colors, with others receiving one color.}\\
3,&\text{ if }\phi\text{ has exactly one edge receiving three colors, with others receiving one color. }\\
4,&\text{ if }\phi\text{ has exactly two edges receiving two colors each, with others receiving one color.}\end{cases}

Given a graph
G
with
\text{cov}_{\text{odd}}(G)\leq 3
,
define

(1.1)

\min\text{ovl}(G)=\min_{\phi}\text{ovl}(\phi),

where the minimum is taken over all odd 3-edge-coverings

. Immediately from the above definitions,

is odd 3-edge-colorable, then

.
As the main result in
[

, it was proved that except for a list of multigraphs with exactly

Conjecture 1.5

(

)
There is no simple graph

Our second result of this paper strongly confirms the above conjecture.

Theorem 1.6

For any simple connected graph

.

is odd. Throughout the proof, let

(respectively,

is even and

is odd. We provide several structural constraints for the supposed minimal counterexample

Claim 1.

of

of

, then assign to

and

under

(such a color exists by the assumption

\diamond

Next, we show that there are no cut vertices in

is a block graph.

Arguing by contradiction, let

First, we show

this intersection is again a forest and therefore has a leaf vertex in

The assertion follows.

By the previous claims,

contains a cycle

Let

.
Recall that

. We will prove that

admits an odd 3-edge-coloring under which a common color appears at both endvertices of

.

Assign the color
3
to

and take an odd
2

\hat{F}
with color set
\{1,2\}
.
Consider any vertex
w\in(N_{G}^{\prime\prime}(v)-V(P))

. If the edge set

has received

Contrarily, if the edge set

The remaining uncolored edges are the edges of

Case

If both colors

Case

In either case, we can obtain an odd

∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.6

This section is devoted to a proof of Theorem
1.6
.
Our proof, conducted by contradiction, considers a possible minimal counterexample and employs the technique of induction.
The main inductive step is presented in the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
G
be a simple connected graph with
\min\text{ovl}(G)\geq 3
, and let

be an even vertex of

(a)

If

of

(b)

G-v
containing a unique cut vertex

Note that

(a)
By Lemma

, we may assume that all connected components of

are of even order. There are two cases to consider.

Case 1.

For every connected component

of

, the degree

is odd.

In this case, since

is even, there are an even number of components of

. In the graph

in

.
Assign the color
3
to

\hat{H}_{1}

2

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

With all the preparatory work completed as above, we proceed to prove the theorem by contradiction.
To do this, we select a simple connected graph
G
as our example, satisfying
\min\text{ovl}(G)\geq 3
and having the minimum number of vertices.
If
G
contains at most
3
vertices, then
it contains at most
3
edges, and clearly
\min\text{ovl}(G)=0
, which is absurd.
Furthermore, since

v(G)=n
is odd (as otherwise
\min\text{ovl}(G)=0
by Theorem
1.1
), we have
v(G)\geq 5
. By comparing parities, there exists at least one even vertex
v
.
If
v
is a cut vertex, we can apply Proposition
5.1
(a) to obtain an example
G^{\prime}
with
\min\text{ovl}(G^{\prime})\geq 3
with
|G^{\prime}|\leq|G|-2
, which contradicts our choice of
G
.
If
G-v
is connected, then we apply Proposition
5.1
(b) to obtain an example
G^{\prime}
with
\min\text{ovl}(G^{\prime})\geq 3
with
|G^{\prime}|\leq|G|-2
, which again contradicts our choice of
G
.
∎

6.
Concluding remarks

For a pair
(G,e)
consisting of a loopless graph
G
and an edge
e\in E(G)
, let
G+e^{\prime}
denote the spannning supergraph of
G
, obtained by doubling the edge
e
; in other words, we enlarge
G
with a new edge
e^{\prime}
which is parallel to
e
. For example, by doubling any edge of
K_{3}
it becomes the reduced Shannon triangle
S_{2,1,1}
. An equivalent formulation of Theorem
1.3
is the following.

Every loopless connected graph that is not
S_{2,2,2}
or
S_{2,2,1}
is either odd
3
-edge-colorable or there exists an edge
e\in E(G)
such that the graph
G+e^{\prime}
admits an odd
3
-edge-coloring in which
e
and
e^{\prime}
receive the same color.

Similarly, an equivalent formulation of Theorem
1.6
is the following.

Every simple connected graph is either odd
3
-edge-colorable, or there exists an edge
e\in E(G)
such that the graph
G+e^{\prime}
admits an odd
3
-edge-coloring in which
e
and
e^{\prime}
receive different colors.

Consequently, every simple connected graph
G
with
\chi_{\text{odd}}(G)=4
contains edges
e
and
f
(not necessarily distinct) such that:

(1)

the graph
G+e^{\prime}
admits an odd
3
-edge-coloring under which
e
and
e^{\prime}
are colored the same;

(2)

the graph
G+f^{\prime}
admits an odd
3
-edge-coloring under which
f
and
f^{\prime}
are colored differently.

Figure
5
depicts the wheel

, a simple connected graph with odd chromatic index

. Any of its four spokes satisfies both
(1)
and
(2)
. One wonders whether every simple connected graph

with odd chromatic index
4
contains an edge

for which both
(1)
and
(2)
hold. We are tempted to end this article with the following.

with

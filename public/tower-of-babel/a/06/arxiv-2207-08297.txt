A moving lemma for cohomology with support
Stefan Schreieder

Abstract




Abstract.
For a natural class of cohomology theories with support (including étale or pro-étale cohomology with suitable coefficients), we prove a moving lemma for cohomology classes with support on smooth quasi-projective
k
-varieties that admit a smooth projective compactification (
e.g.

\operatorname{char}k=0
).
This has the following consequences for such
k
-varieties and cohomology theories: a local and global generalization of the effacement theorem of Quillen, Bloch–Ogus, and Gabber, a finite-level version of the Gersten conjecture in characteristic zero, and a generalization of the injectivity property and the codimension
1
purity theorem for étale cohomology.
Our results imply that the new invariants from the author’s 2023 Compositio paper are motivic.

Keywords.
Algebraic cycles, Chow groups, unramified cohomology

1. Introduction

On a smooth quasi-projective variety
X
, Chow’s moving lemma, see
[
Cho56
]
, allows one to move an algebraic cycle modulo rational equivalence into good position with respect to a closed subset
S\subset X
.

The essential content of the Gersten conjecture for smooth varieties, proven for K-theory by Quillen, see
[
Qui73
]
, and for étale cohomology by Bloch–Ogus, see
[
BO74
]
, and Gabber, see
[
Gab94
,
CTHK97
]
, is an effacement theorem, which is equivalent to a similar moving lemma for classes with support in the particular case where
X
is affine and
S
is a finite set of points. For instance, in the case of étale cohomology, the effacement theorem is equivalent to saying that for any smooth affine
k
-variety
X
, any finite set
S\subset X
, and any class
\alpha\in H^{i}_{Z}(X)
whose support
Z\subset X
is nowhere dense, there is a class
\alpha^{\prime}\in H^{i}_{Z^{\prime}}(X)
with
S\cap Z^{\prime}=\emptyset
such that
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
have the same image in
H^{i}_{W}(X)
for some closed
W\subset X
with
Z,Z^{\prime}\subset W
and
\dim W=\dim Z+1
. This is a fundamental result in algebraic geometry. We refer to the surveys
[
CTHK97
,
Mo16
]
for more details, applications, references, and historical remarks.

The above analogy leads naturally to the question whether the effacement theorems of Quillen, Bloch–Ogus, and Gabber are special instances of a more general moving lemma which allows one to move a class with support
Z\subset X
to one with support
Z^{\prime}
such that
Z^{\prime}
is in good position with respect to an arbitrary given closed subset
S\subset X
. This paper answers that question positively for a natural class of cohomology theories on smooth quasi-projective varieties in characteristic zero (or more generally, with a smooth projective compactification). In the classical case where
X
is affine and
\dim S=0
, this yields a new proof of the Gersten conjecture for étale cohomology in characteristic zero. Our proof yields a stronger conclusion than what was known before, as it is well behaved with respect to localization and hence gives rise to new Gersten-type resolutions on finite levels. The general case where
\dim S>0
is new and has several applications that go beyond the original Gersten conjecture.

1.1. Main result

We fix a field
k
and a twisted cohomology theory with support
(X,Z)\mapsto H^{\ast}_{Z}(X,n)
for smooth equi-dimensional algebraic
k
-schemes
X
with
Z\subset X
closed. We assume that some natural axioms, as outlined in Section
3
below, are satisfied. Concrete examples include étale or pro-étale cohomology with suitable coefficients; see Proposition
3.2
.

To simplify notation, we write
H^{\ast}(X,n):=H^{\ast}_{X}(X,n)
and
H^{\ast}_{Z}(U,n):=H^{\ast}_{Z\cap U}(U,n)
for
U\subset X
open. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1

(Moving lemma)
.

Let
X
be a smooth equi-dimensional
k
-scheme that admits a smooth projective compactification. Let
S,Z\subset X
be closed subsets with
\dim Z<\dim X
. Then there are closed subsets
Z^{\prime}\subset W\subset X
with
Z\subset W
,
\dim Z^{\prime}=\dim Z
, and
\dim W=\dim Z+1
such that
Z^{\prime}
and
W\setminus Z
meet
S
properly and for any
\alpha\in H^{\ast}_{Z}(X,n)
, there is a class
\alpha^{\prime}\in H_{Z^{\prime}}^{\ast}(X,n)
such that
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
have the same image in
H^{\ast}_{W}(X,n)
.

In Theorem
5.1
below, we prove a stronger, but more technical, version of Theorem
1.1
, where
S\subset X
is replaced by a morphism
f\colon S\to X
and the behaviour under localization on
X
is discussed.

The main idea of our proof is to reduce the problem to a version of Chow’s moving lemma due to Levine. More precisely, we construct an action of correspondences on the cohomology class in question and note that the diagonal acts as the identity. Moving the diagonal via Chow’s moving lemma will then move our class. This is particularly clear in the case where
X
is smooth projective, but technical difficulties appear in the (important) case where
X
is only an open subset of a smooth projective scheme.

Even if
X
in Theorem
1.1
is affine, our proof is global and makes essential use of a smooth projective compactification. We explain several applications in the following two subsections.

1.2. Effacement theorems and a finite-level version of the Gersten conjecture

By the long exact sequence of triples, the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
is equivalent to saying that the natural map
H^{\ast}_{Z}(X,n)\to H^{\ast}_{W}(X\setminus Z^{\prime},n)
is zero. If
\dim S+\dim Z<\dim X
, then the condition that
Z^{\prime}
meets
S
properly simply means that
X\setminus Z^{\prime}
is a neighbourhood of
S
. We thus get the following.

Corollary 1.2

(Global effacement)
.

Let
X
be a smooth equi-dimensional
k
-scheme that admits a smooth projective compactification. Let
S,Z\subset X
be closed subsets with
\dim S+\dim Z<\dim X
. Then there exist a neighbourhood
U\subset X
of
S
and a closed subset
W\subset X
with
Z\subset W
and
\dim W=\dim Z+1
such that the following composition is zero:

H^{\ast}_{Z}(X,n)\longrightarrow H^{\ast}_{W}(X,n)\longrightarrow H^{\ast}_{W}(U,n).

The case
\dim S=0
implies formally by specialization that the result holds for any finite set of (possibly non-closed) points
S\subset X
and any nowhere dense closed subset
Z\subset X
. This is the aforementioned effacement theorem of Bloch–Ogus and Gabber in our context;
cf.

[
CTHK97
, Theorems 2.2.1]
. A similar effacement theorem for K-theory had previously been proven by Quillen; see
[
Qui73
, p. 125, Theorem 5.11]
.

Now let
X_{S}
be the (Zariski) localization of
X
along
S\subset X
,
i.e.
the pro-scheme given by the system of all open neighbourhoods
U\subset X
of
S
in
X
. One defines

\displaystyle H^{\ast}_{Z}(X_{S},n):=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\
S\subset U\subset X\end{subarray}}H^{\ast}_{Z}(U,n)\quad\text{and}\quad H^{\ast}(X_{S}\setminus Z,n):=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\
S\subset U\subset X\end{subarray}}H^{\ast}(U\setminus Z,n).

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth equi-dimensional
k
-scheme that admits a smooth projective compactification. Let
S,Z\subset X
be a closed subsets with
\dim S+\dim Z<\dim X
. Then
H^{\ast}_{Z}(X_{S},n)\to H^{\ast}(X_{S},n)
is zero, and the long exact sequence of triples induces for all
i
a short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow H^{i}(X_{S},n)\longrightarrow H^{i}(X_{S}\setminus Z,n)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{i+1}_{Z}(X_{S},n)\longrightarrow 0.

An interesting special case is when
S=Z
with
\dim Z<\frac{1}{2}\dim X
, where we get an algebro-geometric analogue of the following fact from differential topology: if
N
is a tubular neighbourhood of a submanifold
A
of a real manifold
M
of real codimension
c
, then the natural map
H^{i}_{A}(N,\mathbb Z)\to H^{i}(N,\mathbb Z)
identifies with the map
H^{i-c}(A,\mathbb Z)\to H^{i}(A,\mathbb Z)
given by cup product with the Euler class of the normal bundle of
A
in
M
. Hence the map in question is zero if the Euler class is zero.
Corollary
1.3
is an analogue of that result which also applies to singular subvarieties
Z\subset X
and to the (comparatively coarse) Zariski localization
X_{S}
; the triviality of the Euler class is replaced by the condition
\dim Z<\frac{1}{2}\dim X
.

Contrary to Chow’s moving lemma, the subset
Z^{\prime}
in Theorem
1.1
cannot be chosen to be well behaved with respect to localization: if we shrink
X
, then
Z^{\prime}
typically has to be enlarged; see Remark
5.2
. Surprisingly, the closed subset
W
in Theorem
1.1
is much better behaved. This yields the following.

Corollary 1.4

(Local effacement)
.

Let
X
be a smooth equi-dimensional
k
-scheme which admits a smooth projective compactification. Let
Z\subset X
be a nowhere dense closed subset. Let
S\subset X
be either closed with
\dim S<\operatorname{codim}Z-1
or a finite set of points. Then there is a closed subset
W\subset X
with
Z\subset W
and
\dim W=\dim Z+1
such that the natural map
H^{\ast}_{Z}(X_{S},n)\to H^{\ast}_{W}(X_{S},n)
is zero.

Corollary
1.4
is new even in the classical case where
S
is a finite set of points. In this case,
X_{S}=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{X,S})

is the spectrum of the semi-local ring
\mathcal{O}_{X,S}
, and it was known before that the above vanishing result holds if one passes to the direct limit over all such closed subsets
W
; see
[
BO74
, Theorem 4.2.3]
. The fact that a single subscheme suffices is new (and may be somewhat surprising). In fact, Colliot-Thélène–Hoobler–Kahn write in
[
CTHK97
, Remark 2.2.8]
the following concerning this issue:

“
We would like to point out that
(
contrary to the definition of effaceability
)
the statement in Theorem 2.2.7 is not local: the proof by no means implies that the map of Theorem 2.2.7 (2) remains
0
when U is replaced by a smaller open set.
[…]
This shows the subtlety of the situation and probably why Gersten’s conjecture is so difficult for general regular local rings of dimension
\geq 2
.
”

As a consequence, we obtain the following finite-level version of the Gersten conjecture for the étale cohomology of varieties over fields of characteristic zero.

Corollary 1.5

(The Gersten conjecture on finite levels)
.

Let
X
be a smooth affine variety over a field
k
. Assume
X
admits a smooth projective compactification
(
e.g.
\operatorname{char}k=0)
. Let
x\in X^{(c)}
with localization
X_{x}=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{X,x})
. Let
Z_{c}=\{x\}\subset Z_{c-1}\subset\dots\subset Z_{1}\subset Z_{0}=X_{x}
be a chain of closed subsets of
X_{x}
of increasing dimensions. Up to replacing the given chain
\{Z_{j}\}_{j}
by one that is finer
(
i.e. by a chain
\{Z_{j}^{\prime}\}
as above with
Z_{j}\subset Z_{j}^{\prime}
for all
j)
, the following complex is exact for all
i
:

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow H^{i}(X_{x},n)\longrightarrow H_{\mathrm{BM}}^{i}(X_{x}\setminus Z_{1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial}}{{\longrightarrow}}H_{\mathrm{BM}}^{i-1}(Z_{1}\setminus Z_{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial}}{{\longrightarrow}}H_{\mathrm{BM}}^{1}(Z_{i-1}\setminus Z_{i})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial}}{{\longrightarrow}}H_{\mathrm{BM}}^{0}(Z_{i}\setminus Z_{i+1})\longrightarrow 0,

where

Z_{j}=\emptyset
for
j>c
,

H^{i-j}_{\mathrm{BM}}(Z_{j}\setminus Z_{j+1}):=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\
x\in U\subset X\end{subarray}}H^{i+j}_{\bar{Z}_{j}\setminus\bar{Z}_{j+1}}(U\setminus\bar{Z}_{j+1},n+j),

and
\bar{Z}_{j}\subset X
denotes the closure of
Z_{j}
in
X
. If moreover
T\subset X_{x}
is closed and each closed subset
Z_{j}\subset X_{x}
of the given chain
\{Z_{j}\}
meets
T\setminus\{x\}
dimensionally transversely, then the above chosen refinement
\{Z_{j}^{\prime}\}
still satisfies this transversality condition with respect to
T\setminus\{x\}
.

If
H^{\ast}_{Z}(X,n)=H^{\ast}_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\mu_{\ell^{r}}^{\otimes n})
is étale cohomology with support and coefficients in
\mu_{\ell^{r}}^{\otimes n}
for some prime
\ell
invertible in
k
, then
H^{i-j}_{\mathrm{BM}}(Z_{j}\setminus Z_{j+1})
as defined above coincides up to a shift with Borel–Moore homology of
Z_{j}\setminus Z_{j+1}
(
cf.

[
BO74
, Section 1]
), whence the notation. If moreover
Z_{j}\setminus Z_{j+1}
is regular and equi-dimensional, then
H^{i-j}_{\mathrm{BM}}(Z_{j}\setminus Z_{j+1})=H^{i-j}(Z_{j}\setminus Z_{j+1},\mu_{\ell^{r}}^{\otimes n+j})
agrees by Gabber’s purity theorem, see
[
Fuj02
]
, with ordinary étale cohomology of
Z_{j}\setminus Z_{j+1}
. The latter holds in particular in the limit where we run over all chains
\{Z_{j}\}_{j}
as above.

Corollary
1.5
says that we can compute the cohomology of the localization
X_{x}=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{X,x})
in terms of arbitrarily fine stratifications of
X_{x}
. The original Gersten conjecture for étale cohomology, proven in
[
BO74
]
, asserts this only in the limit: the above complex is exact if we pass to the direct limit over all chains
\{Z_{j}\}_{j}
. The fact that exactness happens already on finite levels as well as the possibility of requiring transversality conditions with respect to a closed subset
T\subset X_{x}
are new.

1.3. Codimension j+1 purity and new motivic invariants

For a subset
S\subset X
, we denote by
F_{j}^{S}X
the pro-scheme given by the inverse system of all open neighbourhoods
U\subset X
of
S
with
\operatorname{codim}_{X}(X\setminus U)>j
. We further set
F_{j}X:=F_{j}^{\emptyset}X
. As before, the cohomology
H^{\ast}(F_{j}^{S}X,n)
is defined as direct limit over
H^{\ast}(U,n)
, where
U
runs through the given inverse system. Intuitively, one obtains
F_{j}^{S}X
by successively removing from
X
all closed subsets
Z\subset X
with
\operatorname{codim}_{X}Z\geq j+1
that are disjoint from
S
. For instance, if
X
is irreducible,
F_{0}X\simeq\operatorname{Spec}(k(X))
, and if
S
is a finite set,
F_{0}^{S}X\simeq\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{X,S})
.

Important consequences of
[
BO74
]
, highlighted for instance in
[
CT95
, Section 3.8]
, are the injectivity and codimension
1
purity property for étale cohomology; see
[
CT95
, Theorems 3.8.1 and 3.8.2]
. Theorem
1.1
implies the following generalization.

Corollary 1.6

(Injectivity and codimension j+1 purity)
.

Let
X
be a smooth equi-dimensional
k
-scheme that admits a smooth projective compactification, and let
S\subset X
be closed.

(1)

The restriction map
H^{\ast}(F_{j}^{S}X,n)\to H^{\ast}(F_{j}X,n)
is injective for
j\geq\dim S
.

(2)

A class in
H^{\ast}(F_{j}X,n)
that lifts to
F_{j+1}X
also lifts to
F_{j+1}^{S}X
for
j\geq\dim S-1
.

The
\dim S=0
case of Corollary
1.6
implies by specialization that the results hold for
j\geq 0
and any finite set of points
S\subset X
. Hence, the case
j=\dim S=0
of Corollary
1.6
corresponds to the injectivity property and codimension
1
purity theorem in étale cohomology; see
[
CT95
, Theorems 3.8.1 and 3.8.2]
. We note that our purity result is new even for
j=0
: we get that an unramified class on the generic point of
X
lifts to an open neighbourhood of any given closed curve in
X
, while this was previously only known for points in
X
.

As pointed out by one of the referees: it is natural to wonder if Corollary
1.6
has an analogue for
G
-torsors,
i.e.
for the functor
H^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(-,G)
for suitable group schemes
G
.

Following
[
Sch23
]
, the refined unramified cohomology associated to the given twisted cohomology theory is given by

H^{\ast}_{j,\mathrm{nr}}(X,n):=\operatorname{im}(H^{\ast}(F_{j+1}X,n)\to H^{\ast}(F_{j}X,n)).

In other words, an element
[\alpha]\in H^{\ast}_{j,\mathrm{nr}}(X,n)
is represented by a class
\alpha\in H^{\ast}(U,n)
on some open
U\subset X
whose complement has codimension
j+2
, and any two such representatives yield the same element in
H^{\ast}_{j,\mathrm{nr}}(X,n)
if they coincide on some open subset
V\subset X
whose complement has codimension
j+1
.

This is a common generalization of traditional unramified cohomology and Kato homology; see
[
Sch23
, Section 1.3]
. The latter are known to be motivic as a consequence of the Gersten conjecture; see
[
BO74
]
. The generalization of the Gersten conjecture proven in this paper implies that refined unramified cohomology
H^{i}_{j,\mathrm{nr}}(X,n)
is motivic for all
i
and
j
.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
X
and
Y
be smooth projective equi-dimensional schemes over a field
k
with
d_{X}=\dim X
. For all
c,i,j\geq 0
, there is a natural bi-additive pairing

\operatorname{CH}^{c}(X\times Y)\times H^{i}_{j,\mathrm{nr}}(X,n)\longrightarrow H^{i+2c-2d_{X}}_{j+c-d_{X},\mathrm{nr}}(Y,n+c-d_{X}),\quad([\Gamma],[\alpha])\longmapsto[\Gamma]_{\ast}([\alpha])

which is functorial with respect to the composition of correspondences.

Corollary
1.7
establishes in particular the existence of functorial pullbacks
f^{\ast}\colon H^{i}_{j,\mathrm{nr}}(Y,n)\to H^{i}_{j,\mathrm{nr}}(X,n)
along morphisms
f\colon X\to Y
between smooth projective varieties;
cf.
Corollary
6.9
. The existence of pullbacks is non-trivial already in the case where
Y=\mathbb P^{n}
and
X
is the blow-up in a smooth subvariety. The main problem is that a class on some open subset
U\subset Y
can of course be pulled back to a class on
f^{-1}(U)
, but if
f
is not flat, then the complement of
f^{-1}(U)
in
X
may have the wrong codimension. The key ingredient that allows one to overcome this issue is the codimension
j
purity property proven in Corollary
1.6
, which allows one to represent unramified classes by classes on particular open subsets
U
where this issue does not occur.

Despite the simple definition, the refined unramified cohomology groups of a variety
X
are rather subtle invariants that interpolate between cohomology and cycle theory of
X
; see
[
Sch23
,
Sch24
]
. For instance,
H_{0,\mathrm{nr}}^{i}(X,n)\simeq H_{\mathrm{nr}}^{i}(X,n)
is classical unramified cohomology, while

H^{i}_{j,\mathrm{nr}}(X,n)\simeq H^{i}(X,n)\quad\text{for }\lceil i/2\rceil\leq j;

see Lemma
6.11
below or
[
Sch23
, Corollary 5.10]
.

If
\lceil i/2\rceil>j
, then the refined unramified cohomology groups differ in general from ordinary cohomology. In this (interesting) range we show that refined unramified cohomology satisfies the following basic properties that generalize the fact that traditional unramified cohomology is a stable birational invariant by
[
CTO89
]
. To state our result, recall the decreasing filtration
F^{\ast}
on
H^{i}_{j,\mathrm{nr}}(X,n)
, given by
F^{m}H^{i}_{j,\mathrm{nr}}(X,n):=\operatorname{im}(H^{i}(F_{m}X,n)\to H^{i}(F_{j}X,n))
for
m\geq j+1
.

Corollary 1.8
.

Let
X
and
Y
be smooth projective equi-dimensional
k
-schemes, and let
i,j,n,m\geq 0
. Then the following properties hold true:

(1)

There is a natural isomorphism

\sumop\displaylimits_{l}f_{l}:\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{l=0}^{\min(j,n)}H^{i-2l}_{j-l,\mathrm{nr}}(Y,m-l)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox to0.0pt{\vss\hbox{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}\vskip-1.5pt}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{i}_{j,\mathrm{nr}}(Y\times\mathbb P^{n}_{k},m),

where
f_{l}
is the composition of the pullback along the projection
Y\times\mathbb P^{n-l}_{k}\to Y
followed by the pushforward along the inclusion
Y\times\mathbb P^{n-l}_{k}\to Y\times\mathbb P^{n}_{k}
induced by some linear embedding
\mathbb P^{n-l}_{k}\subset\mathbb P^{n}_{k}
.

(2)

If
f\colon X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox to0.0pt{\vss\hbox{$\scriptstyle\sim$}\vskip-2.0pt}}}{{\dashrightarrow}}Y
is a birational map that is an isomorphism in codimension
c
, then
f
induces for any
j\leq c
an isomorphism
f^{\ast}\colon H^{i}_{j,\mathrm{nr}}(Y,n)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox to0.0pt{\vss\hbox{$\scriptstyle\sim$}\vskip-2.0pt}}}{{\to}}H^{i}_{j,\mathrm{nr}}(X,n)
.

(3)

The isomorphisms in (
1
) and (
2
) respect the decreasing filtration
F^{\ast}
on both sides.

The formula stated in item (
1
) implies for instance

H^{i}_{j,\mathrm{nr}}(\mathbb P^{n}_{k},m)\simeq\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{l=0}^{\min(j,n)}H^{i-2l}(\operatorname{Spec}(k),m-l).

This is, thanks to Corollary
1.7
, a consequence of the motivic decomposition of
\mathbb P^{n}_{k}
, while a direct computation of
H^{i}_{j,\mathrm{nr}}(\mathbb P^{n}_{k},m)
appears to be a rather difficult task because
H^{i}(F_{j}\mathbb P^{n}_{k},m)
is typically a huge group.

Item (
3
) is new even for
j=0
, where it shows that the unramified cohomology groups from
[
CTO89
]
, that are known to be stable birational invariants, carry a filtration that is still a stable birational invariant.

2. Preliminaries

2.1. Conventions

An algebraic scheme is a separated scheme of finite type over a field; it is smooth if it is smooth over the ground field. A variety is an integral algebraic scheme. A closed subset of a scheme is implicitly identified with the corresponding reduced closed subscheme. A morphism
f\colon X\to Y
of Noetherian schemes is of pure relative dimension
d
if for each
x\in X
, we have
d=\dim_{x}(X)-\dim_{f(x)}(Y)
.

If
X
is an algebraic scheme and
Z\subset X
is an irreducible subset, then the codimension
\operatorname{codim}_{X}(Z)
of
Z
in
X
is the dimension of the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{X,\eta_{Z}}
, where
\eta_{Z}\in Z
denotes the generic point of
Z
. If
Z
is not necessarily irreducible, then the local codimension
\operatorname{codim}_{X,z}(Z)
of
Z
in
X
at a point
z\in Z
is given by

\operatorname{codim}_{X,z}(Z):=\inf_{z\in Z^{\prime}\subset Z}\dim\mathcal{O}_{X,\eta_{Z^{\prime}}}

where the infimum (which is in fact a minimum because
X
is of finite type over a field) ranges over all irreducible components
Z^{\prime}
of
Z
that contain the point
z
. The (global) codimension of
Z
in
X
is the minimum of the local codimensions at points
z\in Z
:

\operatorname{codim}_{X}(Z)=\inf_{z\in Z}\operatorname{codim}_{X,z}(Z).

This agrees with the definition that can for instance be found in
[
GW10
, Definition 5.28]
. (We warn the reader that there are places in the literature where different definitions are used;
e.g.
one could replace the infimum by the supremum in the above definitions; see
e.g.
comments to
[
Sta24
,
Tag 02I0
]
. However, the above definition is the one that works for our purposes.)

For an algebraic scheme
X
, we denote by
X_{(j)}
the set of points of dimension
j
. If
X
is equi-dimensional, then we will also write
X^{(j)}:=X_{(d_{X}-j)}
, where
d_{X}=\dim X
. The free abelian group generated by the closures of points
X^{(j)}
is denoted by
Z^{j}(X)
.
The Chow group
\operatorname{CH}^{j}(X):=\operatorname{CH}_{d_{X}-j}(X)
is the quotient of
Z^{j}(X)
modulo rational equivalence; see
[
Ful98
]
. In this paper we use the above convention only in the case where
X
is pure-dimensional, so that points in
X^{(j)}
have local codimension
j
in
X

The support of a cycle

2.2. Chow’s moving lemma

We will need the following version of Chow’s moving lemma due to Levine; see

and
[

Theorem 2.1
.

Let

be a smooth projective equi-dimensional scheme over a field

. Let

be a locally equi-dimensional algebraic
k
-scheme with a morphism

. Then the following hold:

(1)

can be represented by a cycle
\Gamma
such that each point of

has local codimension

on

(
i.e. the expected codimension
)
.

(2)

Let
\Gamma\in Z^{c}(X)
be such that
f^{-1}(\operatorname{supp}(\Gamma))
has local codimension
c
at each point. If
\Gamma\sim_{\operatorname{rat}}0
, then there is a closed subscheme

of codimension

such that

•

f^{-1}(W)
has, locally at each point, codimension
c-1
on

•

\operatorname{supp}\Gamma\subset W

\Gamma

Occasionally, we will use Theorem
2.1
in conjunction with the following simple result.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f\colon S\to X
be a morphism between locally equi-dimensional algebraic
k
-schemes. Let

be closed such that

is locally on
S
of codimension at least

. Then

.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the result for each irreducible component of

is irreducible. Towards a contradiction, assume that

, or equivalently,

. Let

The purpose of this appendix is to prove Proposition
3.2
from Section

.
We will see that item (

) of Proposition

) and (

A.1. Pro-étale and continuous étale cohomology

The pro-étale site
X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}
of a scheme
X
is formed by weakly étale maps of schemes
U\to X
; see
[
BS15
, Definition 4.1.1 and Remark 4.1.2]
.
Since any étale map is also weakly étale, there is a natural map of topoi
\nu\colon\operatorname{Shv}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}})\to\operatorname{Shv}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}})
; see
[
BS15
, Section 5]
.
In particular, we get functors
\nu^{\ast}\colon\operatorname{Ab}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}})\to\operatorname{Ab}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}})
and
\nu_{\ast}\colon\operatorname{Ab}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}})\to\operatorname{Ab}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}})
on the corresponding categories of sheaves of abelian groups.
Here,
\nu^{\ast}
is exact and right adjoint to the left exact functor
\nu_{\ast}
; see
[
Sta24
,
Tag 00X9
]
.
Since
\nu^{\ast}
is (left) exact,
\nu_{\ast}
maps injective objects to injective objects.

For a closed subset
Z\subset X
, we have the functor

\Gamma_{Z}\colon\operatorname{Ab}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}})\to\operatorname{Ab},

given by taking global sections with support on
Z
.
For a bounded complex
K
of sheaves of abelian groups on
X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}}
, we let

(A.1)

\displaystyle H^{i}_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},K):=\operatorname{R}^{i}\Gamma_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},K).

In the special case where
K
is a sheaf
F
concentrated in degree zero, we also write
H^{i}_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},F):=\operatorname{R}^{i}\Gamma_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},F)
.
(Note that by slight abuse of notation, we do not distinguish between the hypercohomology of a complex as in (
A.1
) and the cohomology of a sheaf.)

Lemma A.1

(
cf.

[
BS15
]
)
.

Let
X
be a scheme, and let
(F_{r})
be an inductive system of sheaves on the small étale site
X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}
and with surjective transition maps.
Let
Z\subset X
be a closed subset.
Then there is a canonical identification

H^{i}_{Z,\mathrm{cont}}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},(F_{r}))\simeq H^{i}_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longleftarrow\\
r\end{subarray}}\nu^{\ast}F_{r}),

where the left-hand side denotes Jannsen’s continuous étale cohomology groups with support; see
[
Jan88
]
.

Proof.

This follows by the same argument as in
[
BS15
, Proposition 5.6.2]
; we give the details for the reader’s convenience.
By definition,
H^{i}_{Z,\mathrm{cont}}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},(F_{r}))
, resp.
H^{i}_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\lim\nu^{\ast}F_{r})
, is the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
cohomology of the complex

\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\operatorname{R}\lim(F_{r}))\in D(\operatorname{Ab}),\quad\text{resp. }\ \operatorname{R}\Gamma_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\lim\nu^{\ast}F_{r})\in D(\operatorname{Ab}).

We claim that
\Gamma_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},-)\colon\operatorname{Ab}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}})\to\operatorname{Ab}
takes injectives to injectives.
To prove this, note that
\Gamma_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},-)
is right adjoint to the exact functor
A\mapsto\iota_{\ast}\underline{A}_{Z}
, where
\iota\colon Z\hookrightarrow X
denotes the inclusion and
\underline{A}_{Z}
denotes the locally constant sheaf with values
A
on
Z
.
The adjointness property thus implies that for any injective sheaf
\mathcal{I}\in\operatorname{Ab}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}})
, the functor
\operatorname{Hom}(-,\Gamma_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\mathcal{I}))\colon\operatorname{Ab}\to\operatorname{Ab}
is exact.
Hence,
\Gamma_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\mathcal{I})\in\operatorname{Ab}
is an injective object, as we want.

Similarly, for any abelian category
\mathcal{B}
, the functor
\lim\colon\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb N}\to\mathcal{B}
maps injectives to injectives, see
[
Jan88
, Remark 1.17(a)]
, where
\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb N}
denotes the category of inverse systems of objects in
\mathcal{B}
.

Since
\lim
and
\Gamma_{Z}
commute, we conclude from Grothendieck’s theorem on composed functors that

\operatorname{R}\lim\circ\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{Z}=\operatorname{R}(\lim\circ\Gamma_{Z})=\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{Z}\circ\operatorname{R}\lim

as functors
\operatorname{Ab}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}})^{\mathbb N}\to\operatorname{Ab}
.
The same argument shows that the above identity holds when the objects are viewed as functors
\operatorname{Ab}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}})^{\mathbb N}\to\operatorname{Ab}
.
Hence,

(A.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\operatorname{R}\lim(F_{r}))\simeq\operatorname{R}\lim\circ\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},F_{r})

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{R}\lim\circ\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\nu^{\ast}F_{r})

\displaystyle\simeq\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\operatorname{R}\lim(\nu^{\ast}F_{r})),

where the second isomorphism uses the fact that
\nu_{\ast}\colon\operatorname{Ab}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}})\to\operatorname{Ab}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}})
maps injectives to injectives and
\operatorname{id}\to\operatorname{R}\nu_{\ast}\nu^{\ast}
is an equivalence on bounded below complexes (see
[
BS15
, Proposition 5.2.6(2)]
), while the last equality uses
\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{Z}\circ\operatorname{R}\lim\simeq\operatorname{R}\lim\circ\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{Z}
.
By
[
BS15
, Propositions 3.2.3 and 4.2.8]
, the topos
\operatorname{Shv}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}})
is replete,
and so
[
BS15
, Proposition 3.1.10]
implies

(A.3)

\displaystyle\operatorname{R}\lim(\nu^{\ast}F_{r})\simeq\lim(\nu^{\ast}F_{r}),

where we use that the transition maps in
(F_{r})
are surjective.
Hence, the above isomorphism simplifies to

\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\operatorname{R}\lim(F_{r}))\simeq\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\lim(\nu^{\ast}F_{r}))
, which proves the proposition.
∎

A.5. Pullbacks and proper pushforwards

Lemma A.6
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a morphism between algebraic schemes, and let
Z_{Y}\subset Y
and
Z_{X}\subset X
be closed subsets.

(1)

If
f^{-1}(Z_{Y})\subset Z_{X}
, then there are functorial pullback maps

(A.9)

\displaystyle f^{\ast}\colon H^{i}_{Z_{Y}}(Y,n)\longrightarrow H^{i}_{Z_{X}}(X,n).

(2)

Assume that
X
and
Y
are smooth and equi-dimensional.
If
f
is proper of relative codimension
c
and
f(Z_{X})\subset Z_{Y}
, then there are functorial
pushforward maps

(A.10)

\displaystyle f_{\ast}\colon H^{i-2c}_{Z_{X}}(X,n-c)\longrightarrow H^{i}_{Z_{Y}}(Y,n).

Proof.

The pullback maps are induced by the natural map of functors
\Gamma_{Z_{Y}}(Y_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},-)\to\Gamma_{Z_{X}}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},f^{\ast}_{\mathrm{comp}}(-))
on
\operatorname{Mod}(Y_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell})
.
The functoriality is clear from this description.
This proves item (
1
).

Let
\pi_{X}\colon X\to\operatorname{Spec}(k)
and
\pi_{Y}\colon Y\to\operatorname{Spec}(k)
denote the structure morphisms, and let
i\colon Z_{Y}\to Y
and
i^{\prime}\colon Z_{X}\to X
denote the respective closed immersions.
Let further
d_{X}:=\dim X
and
d_{Y}:=\dim Y
, so that
c=d_{Y}-d_{X}
.
Applying Lemmas
A.2
and
A.3
, we get canonical isomorphisms of functors

(A.11)

\displaystyle(i^{\prime})^{!}(\pi_{X})_{\mathrm{comp}}^{\ast}(n)\simeq(i^{\prime})^{!}\pi_{X}^{!}(n-d_{X})[-2d_{X}]\simeq(f|_{Z_{X}})^{!}i^{!}\pi_{Y}^{!}(n-d_{X})[-2d_{X}]\simeq(f|_{Z_{X}})^{!}i^{!}(\pi_{Y})^{\ast}_{\mathrm{comp}}(n+c)[2c],

where

is the restriction of
f
.
The trace map in (
A.8
) thus induces by (
A.6
) pushforward maps as claimed in item (

).
The functoriality in
f
follows from the functoriality of the trace map.
This concludes the proof of the lemma.

Remark A.7
.

Let
X
be a smooth equi-dimensional algebraic scheme, and let
\iota_{Z^{\prime}}:Z^{\prime}\hookrightarrow X
and
\iota_{Z}\colon Z\to Z^{\prime}
be closed subschemes.
By (
A.6
), we have
H^{i}_{Z^{\prime}}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},-)=H^{i}(Z^{\prime}_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\iota_{Z^{\prime}}^{!}-)
and

H^{i}_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},-)=H^{i}(Z_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\iota_{Z}^{!}\iota_{Z^{\prime}}^{!}-)=H^{i}(Z^{\prime}_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\operatorname{R}{\iota_{Z}}_{\ast}\iota_{Z}^{!}\iota_{Z^{\prime}}^{!}-).

Hence,
\operatorname{Tr}_{\iota_{Z}}
induces a pushforward map

(\iota_{Z})_{\ast}\colon H^{i}_{Z}(X,n)\longrightarrow H_{Z^{\prime}}^{i}(X,n),

which agrees with the respective pushforward map along the identity in (
A.10

By the construction of the adjunction map
\operatorname{Tr}_{\iota_{Z}}\colon\operatorname{R}{\iota_{Z}}_{\ast}{\iota_{Z}}^{!}\to\operatorname{id}
, we find that
(\iota_{Z})_{\ast}
agrees with the map induced by the natural map
\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},-)\to\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},-)
of functors, induced by the inclusion
\Gamma_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},-)\hookrightarrow\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},-)
.

A.6. Comparison to étale cohomology and excision

Since
K\in D_{\mathrm{cons}}((\operatorname{Spec}(k))_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell})
is constructible,
K\otimes_{\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}}\mathbb Z/\ell^{r}=\nu^{\ast}K_{r}
for a constructible complex
K_{r}\in D_{\mathrm{cons}}((\operatorname{Spec}(k))_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\mathbb Z/\ell^{r})
; see
[
BS15
, Definition 6.5.1]
.
Moreover,
K\simeq\operatorname{R}\lim\nu^{\ast}K_{r}
by completeness.
We then have the following;
cf.

[
Jan88
, Proposition 1.6]
.

Lemma A.9
.

Let
\pi_{X}\colon X\to\operatorname{Spec}(k)
be an algebraic
k
-scheme with a closed subset
Z\subset X
.
Then there is a canonical short exact sequence

(A.12)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\operatorname{R}^{1}\lim H^{i-1}_{Z}\left(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\pi_{X}^{\ast}K_{r}\otimes_{\mathbb Z/\ell^{r}}\mu_{\ell^{r}}^{\otimes n}\right)\longrightarrow H^{i}_{Z}(X,n)\longrightarrow\lim H^{i}_{Z}\left(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\pi_{X}^{\ast}K_{r}\otimes_{\mathbb Z/\ell^{r}}\mu_{\ell^{r}}^{\otimes n}\right)\longrightarrow 0,

where
\lim
denotes the inverse limit functor along
r
.
Moreover, this sequence is compatible with respect to pullbacks (
A.9
) and pushforwards (
A.10
) on the individual pieces.

Proof.

Recall that
\operatorname{R}\lim
and
\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{Z}
commute with each other; see the proof of Lemma
A.1
.
Since
K\simeq\operatorname{R}\lim\nu^{\ast}K_{r}
,
H^{i}_{Z}(X,n)
is the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
cohomology of the complex

\operatorname{R}\lim\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{Z}\left(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\pi_{X}^{\ast}\nu^{\ast}K_{r}\otimes\mu_{\ell^{r}}^{\otimes n}\right)\simeq\operatorname{R}\lim\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{Z}\left(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\nu^{\ast}\pi_{X}^{\ast}K_{r}\otimes\mu_{\ell^{r}}^{\otimes n}\right),

where we use that
\nu^{\ast}
and
\pi_{X}^{\ast}
commute.
Since
\nu_{\ast}\colon\operatorname{Ab}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}})\to\operatorname{Ab}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}})
maps injectives to injectives and
\operatorname{id}\to\operatorname{R}\nu_{\ast}\nu^{\ast}
is an equivalence on bounded below complexes (see
[
BS15
, Proposition 5.2.6(2)]
), the above complex identifies with

\operatorname{R}\lim\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{Z}\left(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\pi_{X}^{\ast}K_{r}\otimes\mu_{\ell^{r}}^{\otimes n}\right).

The lemma then follows from the composed functor spectral sequence, where we note that
\operatorname{R}\lim
has cohomological dimension at most
1
on
\operatorname{Ab}
.
The compatibility with respect to pullbacks is obvious; the compatibility with respect to pushforwards follows from
[
Sch23
, Lemma 6.2]
.
∎

Lemma A.10

(Excision)
.

Let
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be an étale morphism between algebraic schemes.
Let
Z\subset X
and
Z^{\prime}\subset X^{\prime}
be closed subschemes with
f(Z^{\prime})=Z
such that
f|_{Z^{\prime}}\colon Z^{\prime}\to Z
is an isomorphism and
f(X^{\prime}\setminus Z^{\prime})\subset X\setminus Z
.
Then

f^{\ast}\colon H^{i}_{Z}(X,n)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox to0.0pt{\vss\hbox{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}\vskip-1.5pt}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{i}_{Z^{\prime}}(X^{\prime},n).

Proof.

Since
K
is bounded (see
[
BS15
, Lemma 6.5.3]
), induction on its length together with the local-to-global spectral sequence reduces us to the case where
K
is a single sheaf.
In this case, the statement is proven for étale cohomology in
[
Mil80
, Proposition III.1.27]
; the case of pro-étale cohomology follows from this by Lemma
A.9
.
∎

Lemma A.11

and

be smooth algebraic

A.8. Long exact sequence of triples and semi-purity

Lemma A.13

(Long exact sequence of triples)
.

Let
X
be an algebraic scheme, and let

be closed subsets.
Then there is a canonical long exact sequence

\cdots\longrightarrow H^{i}_{Z}(X,n)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota_{\ast}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{i}_{W}(X,n)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{restr}}H^{i}_{W\setminus Z}(X\setminus Z,n)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{i+1}_{Z}(X,n)\longrightarrow\cdots

such that for any morphism
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
of algebraic schemes, with closed subsets
Z^{\prime}\subset W^{\prime}\subset X^{\prime}

(1)

If

and
f^{-1}(W)\subset W^{\prime}
, then pullback along
f

(
as in (
A.9
)
)
induces a commutative ladder between the long exact sequence of the triple
(X^{\prime},W^{\prime},Z^{\prime})
and that of
(X,W,Z)
.

(2)

If
X^{\prime}
and
X
are smooth,
f
is proper of pure relative codimension
c
, and
f(Z^{\prime})=Z
and
f(W^{\prime})=W

f

(
as in (
A.10
)
)
induces a commutative ladder between the long exact sequence of the triple
(X^{\prime},W^{\prime},Z^{\prime})

(X,W,Z)

If
j
is an open immersion, then

(see
[

, Remark 6.5.10]

, and

By
[

, there is an exact triangle

\operatorname{R}(i^{W}_{Z})_{\ast}(i^{W}_{Z})^{!}\longrightarrow\operatorname{id}\longrightarrow\operatorname{R}(j_{W\setminus Z})_{\ast}(j_{W\setminus Z})^{\ast}.

Precomposing with
\operatorname{R}(i_{W})_{\ast}
and postcomposing with
(i_{W})^{!}

\operatorname{R}(i_{W})_{\ast}\operatorname{R}(i^{W}_{Z})_{\ast}(i^{W}_{Z})^{!}(i_{W})^{!}\longrightarrow\operatorname{R}(i_{W})_{\ast}(i_{W})^{!}\longrightarrow\operatorname{R}(i_{W})_{\ast}\operatorname{R}(j_{W\setminus Z})_{\ast}(j_{W\setminus Z})^{\ast}(i_{W})^{!}.

We apply

and use

and (

If

is an open immersion, then

(see

[

denotes the restriction of

.
This implies that the last term in the above triangle identifies with
\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{W\setminus Z}((X\setminus Z)_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},j_{X\setminus Z}^{\ast}(-))

The long exact sequence for the triple

follows immediately from this.
By Lemma
A.9
, the compatibility properties with respect to pullbacks and pushforwards reduce to the analogous results for étale cohomology, which are well known; see
e.g.

[
CTHK97

.

be a closed subset.
Assume that the complex
K\in D_{\mathrm{cons}}((\operatorname{Spec}(k))_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell})
is concentrated in non-negative degrees.
Then

H^{i}_{Z}(X,n)=0\quad\text{for all $i<2\operatorname{codim}_{X}Z$.}

Proof.

By (
A.6
), we have

H^{i}_{Z}(X,n)\simeq H^{i}(Z_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},i^{!}(\pi_{\mathrm{comp}}^{\ast}K\otimes_{\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}}\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}(n))),

where
\pi\colon X\to\operatorname{Spec}(k)
is the structure morphism.

First assume that
Z
is smooth.
By étale excision (see Lemma
A.10
), we may assume that
Z
is equi-dimensional of pure codimension
c
in
X
.
By Lemma
A.5
, we have
i^{\ast}_{\mathrm{comp}}(-c)[-2c]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox to0.0pt{\vss\hbox{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}\vskip-1.5pt}}}{{\to}}i^{!}
, and so

H^{i}_{Z}(X,n)\simeq H^{i-2c}(Z_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},(\pi\circ i)_{\mathrm{comp}}^{\ast}K\otimes_{\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}}\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}(n-c)).

Since
K
is concentrated in non-negative degrees, the above hyper-cohomology group vanishes for
i-2c<0
.
This concludes the case where
Z
is smooth.

For the general case, note that by topological invariance (see Lemma
A.8
), we may assume that
k
is perfect.
Hence,
Z
contains an open subset that is smooth over
k
.
Using this and the fact that we know the result for
Z
smooth, we can prove the claim with the help of Lemma
A.13
by induction on
\dim Z
.
This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma A.15
.

Let
X
be a smooth algebraic scheme, and let
i:Z\hookrightarrow X
be a closed subset.
Let further

be a closed subset with complement

such that

has codimension at least

in

.

is concentrated in non-negative degrees,
then the restriction map

) reduces us to the case where

.
By semi-purity (see Lemma

),

for all

.
∎

A.9. Cup products

The pro-étale topos
\operatorname{Shv}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}})
of a scheme
X
is locally weakly contractible (see
[
BS15
, Definition 3.2.1 and Proposition 4.2.8]
), which means in particular that it is locally coherent.
From now on we assume that
X
is an algebraic scheme, and so local coherence implies that
X
is coherent.
By
[
SGA4.2
, Proposition VI.9.0]
, it follows that
\operatorname{Shv}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}})
has enough points.
Hence, any
F\in\operatorname{Mod}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell})
admits a Godement resolution.
More generally, for any bounded below complex
K\in D^{+}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell})
, there is a natural quasi-isomorphism
K\to\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}(K)
, given by the simple complex of the Godement double complex associated to
K
.
The sheaves in the complex
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}(K)
are
\Gamma_{Z}
-acyclic, and so

\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},K)=\Gamma_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}(K))
.

For any
F,G\in\operatorname{Mod}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell})
and closed subschemes
Z,W\subset X
, we have a canonical map

(A.18)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{Z}(X,F)\otimes_{\mathbb Z_{\ell}}\Gamma_{W}(X,G)\longrightarrow\Gamma_{Z\cap W}(X,F\otimes_{\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}}G),

given by the tensor product of sections.
This induces a map

(A.19)

\displaystyle\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},M)\otimes_{\mathbb Z_{\ell}}^{\mathbb L}\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{W}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},L)\longrightarrow\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{Z\cap W}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},M\otimes^{\mathbb L}_{\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}}L)

in the derived category for any constructible complexes
M,L\in D_{\mathrm{cons}}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell})
.
Using the above-mentioned Godement resolutions, (
A.19
) may be described as follows.
Recall that constructible complexes are bounded (see
[
BS15
, Lemma 6.5.3]
) and that there are enough
\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}
-flat objects in
\operatorname{Mod}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell})
(the latter is a general fact that holds for any ringed site; see
[
Sta24
,
Tag 05NI
]
).
We may therefore choose a quasi-isomorphism
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}(M)\to M
, where
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}(M)
is a complex of
\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}
-flat sheaves; see
[
Sta24
,
Tag 05T7
]
.
Then the Godement resolution
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}(M)\to\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}(M))
is still a complex of flat
\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}
-modules.
Hence,
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}(M))\otimes_{\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}}\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}(L)
represents the derived tensor product
M\otimes^{\mathbb L}_{\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}}L
.
Applying the Godement resolution once again, we get a quasi-isomorphism

\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}(M))\otimes_{\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}}\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}(L)\longrightarrow\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}(M))\otimes_{\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}}\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}(L)).

The cup product (
A.19
) may then explicitly be described by the natural map

\Gamma_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}(M)))\otimes_{\mathbb Z_{\ell}}\Gamma_{W}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}(L))\longrightarrow\Gamma_{Z\cap W}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}(M))\otimes_{\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}}\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}(L))).

In the following arguments, the above explicit description of (
A.19
) will be used throughout.

We denote from now on by

(A.20)

\displaystyle H^{i}_{Z}(X,\mathbb Z_{\ell}(n)):=\operatorname{R}^{i}\Gamma_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\pi_{X}^{\ast}\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}(n))\simeq H^{i}_{Z,\mathrm{cont}}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},(\mu_{\ell^{r}}^{\otimes n})_{r})

twisted
\ell
-adic pro-étale cohomology with support, which coincides with continuous
\ell
-adic étale cohomology with support by Lemma
A.1
.
As before we write
H^{i}_{Z}(X,n):=H^{i}_{Z}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\pi_{\mathrm{comp}}^{\ast}K\otimes_{\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}}\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}(n))
.

Lemma A.16

(Cup products)
.

Let
X
be an algebraic
k
-scheme, and let
Z,W\subset X
be closed subsets.
Then there are cup product maps

H^{i}_{Z}(X,\mathbb Z_{\ell}(n))\otimes_{\mathbb Z_{\ell}}H^{j}_{W}(X,m)\longrightarrow H^{i+j}_{Z\cap W}(X,n+m),\quad\alpha\otimes\beta\longmapsto\alpha\cup\beta

Proof.

We apply (
A.19
) to
M=\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}(m)
and
L=K\otimes_{\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}}\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}(n)
.
The lemma thus follows by taking cohomology because

\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}(n)\otimes^{\mathbb L}_{\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}}(\pi_{\mathrm{comp}}^{\ast}K\otimes_{\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}}\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}(m))\simeq\pi_{\mathrm{comp}}^{\ast}K\otimes_{\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}}\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}(n+m)

since
\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}(n)
is a locally free
\mathbb Z_{\ell}
-module (of rank 1).
∎

Lemma A.17

Lemma A.18
.

Let

Since

not an exact triangle.
However, the natural restriction maps yield a map from the exact triangle

\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{Z_{Y}}(X,M\otimes_{\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}}^{\mathbb L}L)\longrightarrow\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{W_{Y}}(X,M\otimes_{\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}}^{\mathbb L}L)\longrightarrow\operatorname{R}\Gamma_{W_{Y}\setminus Z_{Y}}(X\setminus Z_{Y},M\otimes_{\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}}^{\mathbb L}L)

and

) yields isomorphisms in cohomology by Lemma

because

and

.
(This shows in fact that (

) is isomorphic to an exact triangle, hence is an exact triangle itself.)
∎

B.2. Transcendental Abel–Jacobi maps are motivic

Let
X
be a smooth equi-dimensional scheme over a perfect field
k
, and let
\ell
be a prime invertible in
k
.
We let
H^{i}(X,\mathbb Q_{\ell}(n)):=H^{i}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\widehat{\mathbb}Z_{\ell}(n))\otimes_{\mathbb Z_{\ell}}\mathbb Q_{\ell}
and
H^{i}(X,\mathbb Q_{\ell}/\mathbb Z_{\ell}(n))=\operatorname{colim}_{r}H^{i}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\nu^{\ast}\mu_{\ell^{r}}^{\otimes n})
, where we note that
H^{i}(X_{\mathrm{pro\acute{e}t}},\nu^{\ast}\mu_{\ell^{r}}^{\otimes n})\simeq H^{i}(X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}},\mu_{\ell^{r}}^{\otimes n})
;
cf.
Lemma
A.1
.

Recall the
\ell
-adic cycle group
A_{0}^{i}(X)_{\mathbb Z_{\ell}}
from (
B.1
).
We denote by
A_{0}^{i}(X)[\ell^{\infty}]
the torsion subgroup of
A_{0}^{i}(X)_{\mathbb Z_{\ell}}
.
By
[
Sch23
, Section 7.5]
, there is a transcendental Abel–Jacobi map on torsion cycles

(B.7)

\lambda_{\mathrm{tr}}^{i}\colon A_{0}^{i}(X)[\ell^{\infty}]\longrightarrow H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Q_{\ell}/\mathbb Z_{\ell}(i))/N^{i-1}H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Q_{\ell}(i)),

where
N^{\ast}
denotes Grothendieck’s coniveau filtration on cohomology;
i.e.

\alpha\in H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Q_{\ell}(i))
lies in
N^{j}H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Q_{\ell}(i))
if
\alpha
vanishes on the complement of a closed codimension
j
set of
X
.
Correspondences between smooth projective equi-dimensional
k
-schemes act on both sides of (
B.7
), and the main result in this section is that these actions are compatible with the map
\lambda_{tr}^{i}
.

Corollary B.3
.

Let
X
and
Y
be smooth projective equi-dimensional schemes over a perfect field
k
, let
d_{X}:=\dim(X)
, and let
\ell
be a prime that is invertible in
k
.
Let
[\Gamma]\in\operatorname{CH}^{c}(X\times Y)
be a correspondence.
Then the following diagram is commutative:

\textstyle{A_{0}^{i}(X)[\ell^{\infty}]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\lambda_{tr}^{i}}
\scriptstyle{[\Gamma]_{\ast}}
\textstyle{\frac{H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Q_{\ell}/\mathbb Z_{\ell}(i))}{N^{i-1}H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Q_{\ell}(i))}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{[\Gamma]_{\ast}}
\textstyle{A_{0}^{i+c-d_{X}}(Y)[\ell^{\infty}]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\lambda_{tr}^{i+c-d_{X}}}
\textstyle{\frac{H^{2i+2c-2d_{X}-1}(Y,\mathbb Q_{\ell}/\mathbb Z_{\ell}(i+c-d_{X}))}{N^{i+c-d_{X}-1}H^{2i+2c-2d_{X}-1}(Y,\mathbb Q_{\ell}(i+c-d_{X}))}.}

Proof.

By
[
Sch23
, Lemma 7.15]
, the map
\lambda_{tr}^{i}
can be described as follows.
Let
[z]\in A_{0}^{i}(X)[\ell^{\infty}]
, and let
[\alpha]\in H^{2i-1}_{i-2,\mathrm{nr}}(X,\mathbb Z_{\ell}(i))
be a class that represents
[z]
via the isomorphism in (
B.2
).
This means that whenever
[\alpha]
can be represented by a class
\alpha\in H^{i}(U,\mathbb Z_{\ell}(i))
on some open subset
U\subset X
whose complement
R=X\setminus U
has codimension
2i
, the residue

\partial\alpha\in H^{2i}_{R}(X,\mathbb Z_{\ell}(i))\simeq\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{x\in R_{(d_{X}-i)}}\mathbb Z_{\ell}[x]

is a cycle
z
that represents the class
[z]
.
Since
[z]
is torsion, there is some

such that
\ell^{r}[z]=0\in A^{i}(X)_{\mathbb Z_{\ell}}
.
This implies that
\ell^{r}[\alpha]
lifts to a class
[\beta]\in H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Z_{\ell}(i))
.
Then,
[\beta/\ell^{r}]
gives rise to a class in
H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Q_{\ell}(i))
and hence in
H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Q_{\ell}/\mathbb Z_{\ell}(i))
, and we have

\lambda_{tr}^{i}([z])=[\beta/\ell^{r}]\in H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Q_{\ell}/\mathbb Z_{\ell}(i))/N^{i-1}H^{2i-1}(X,\mathbb Q_{\ell}(i)).

By Proposition
B.1
, the cycle
[\Gamma]_{\ast}[z]
corresponds via (
B.2
) to the class

[\Gamma]_{\ast}[\alpha]\in H^{2i+2c+2d_{X}-1}_{i+c-d_{X}-2,\mathrm{nr}}(X,\mathbb Z_{\ell}(i+c+d_{X})).

Applying the moving lemma (Theorem
2.1
) to
\Gamma
or Corollary
6.5
(
2
) to
[\alpha]
, we can assume that there is a representative
\alpha\in H^{i}(U,\mathbb Z_{\ell}(i))
of
[\alpha]
as above such that
R\times Y
meets
W:=\operatorname{supp}\Gamma
in codimension at least
i+c
.
We then let
S=q(W\cap(R\times Y))
and

and find by Corollary
6.8
(
1
) that
[\Gamma]_{\ast}[\alpha]
is represented by

\Gamma(W)_{\ast}(\alpha)\in H^{2i+2c-2d_{X}-1}(V,\mathbb Z_{\ell}(i+c-d_{X}))

from Lemma
4.1
.
By Corollary
6.8
(
2
), we find that
\ell^{r}\cdot\Gamma(W)_{\ast}(\alpha)=\Gamma(W)_{\ast}(\ell^{r}\cdot\alpha)
extends to the class
\Gamma(W)_{\ast}(\beta)\in H^{2i+2c-2d_{X}-1}(Y,\mathbb Z_{\ell}(i+c-d_{X}))
.
Hence, by the description of
\lambda_{tr}^{i}
given above, we find that

\lambda_{tr}^{i}([\Gamma]_{\ast}[z])=[\Gamma(W)_{\ast}\beta/\ell^{r}]\in\frac{H^{2i+2c-2d_{X}-1}(Y,\mathbb Q_{\ell}/\mathbb Z_{\ell}(i+c-d_{X}))}{N^{i+c-d_{X}-1}H^{2i+2c-2d_{X}-1}(X,\mathbb Q_{\ell}(i+c-d_{X}))}.

with the image of

.
This shows that the diagram in question is commutative, which concludes the proof of the corollary.
∎

Heffter Spaces
Marco Buratti, Anita Pasotti

Abstract
. The notion of a Heffter array, which received much attention in the last decade, is equivalent to a pair of orthogonal Heffter systems. In this paper we study the existence problem of a set of r r mutually orthogonal Heffter systems for any r r . Such a set is equivalent to a resolvable partial linear space of degree r r whose parallel classes are Heffter systems: this is a new combinatorial design that we call a Heffter space . We present a series of direct constructions of Heffter spaces with odd block size and arbitrarily large degree r r obtained with the crucial use of finite fields. Among the applications we establish, in particular, that if q = 2 ​ k ​ w + 1 q=2kw+1 is a prime power with k ​ w kw odd and k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 , then there are at least ⌈ w 4 ​ k 4 ⌉ \lceil{w\over 4k^{4}}\rceil mutually orthogonal k k -cycle systems of order q q .

Introduction

We introduce
Heffter spaces
, a new combinatorial design generalizing the well-known notion of a
Heffter array
.
Whereas a Heffter array gives a pair of orthogonal Heffter systems, more generally a
Heffter space of degree
r

a set of
r

We first need to recall some basic definitions and give some new ones.

The
patterned starter
of a finite group

\{g,-g\}
with

.
Any complete system of representatives for the patterned starter of

is said to be a

of
G

Definition 1.1
.

Let
V
be a half-set of an abelian group
G
of order
2v+1\geq 7
. A
(v,k)

Heffter system

on
V
is a partition
\mathcal{P}
of
V
into zero-sum parts, called
blocks
, of size
k
. The size
v
of

will be called

of
\mathcal{P}
. By saying that a Heffter system is
over a group
G
we mean that it is on an unspecified
half-set of
G
.

If
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2v+1}
, in the classic literature one usually speaks of a
\mathrm{D}(2v+1,k)
, see
[
4
]
.
We have changed the notation in order to be consistent with the one about Heffter spaces that we will introduce later on.
It could be helpful to keep in mind that if
\mathcal{P}
is a Heffter system on
V
, then
V
is certainly zero-sum since it is
partitioned by its blocks which are, by definition, zero-sum.

Definition 1.2
.

Throughout the paper, speaking of a
simple

k

is an

Speaking of a

In particular, a

It has been conjectured that the blocks of

The existence of a simple

Indeed, as it will be recalled in detail in the last section, such a decomposition is equivalent to a

Let us open a parenthesis to recall some definitions from classic design theory
[
7
,
27
]
.

A
partial linear space
(PLS) is a pair
(V,{\mathcal{B}})
where
V
is a set of
points
and
\mathcal{B}

is a set of non-empty subsets (
blocks
or
lines
) of
V
with the property that any 2-subset of
V
is contained in at most one block.
Two distinct points are said to be
collinear
if there is a block containing them.

A PLS where every two distinct points are contained in
exactly
one block is said to be a
linear space
.
In particular, a linear space with
v
points in which every block has size
k
is a
Steiner
2
-design
S
(2,k,v)
.

The
degree
of a point of a PLS is the number of blocks containing that point. A PLS has degree
r
if all its points have the same degree
r
.
A PLS with
v
points, constant block size
k
and degree
r
has necessarily
b={vr\over k}
blocks and it is said to be a
configuration
.
It is often referred to as a
(v_{r},b_{k})

configuration
but here we prefer to write
(v,k;r)
rather than
(v_{r},b_{k})
in order to be consistent with
some other notation that will be used later. A configuration is said to be
symmetric
when the block size
k
is equal to the degree
r

(and consequently the numbers of points and blocks are equal).

A
parallel class
of a PLS is a set of blocks partitioning the point set.
A PLS is said to be
resolvable
if there exists a partition of the block set (
resolution
) into parallel classes.
Clearly, every resolvable PLS has degree equal to the number of its parallel classes.
By a
resolved
PLS we mean a resolvable PLS together with a
specific
resolution of it.
In particular, given an integer
v
and a multiset of integers
\{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\}
, we will write
(v,\{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\})
-RPLS to denote a
resolved PLS whose resolution consists of
r
parallel classes
{\mathcal{P}}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{P}}_{r}
where all the blocks of
\mathcal{P}_{i}
have size
k_{i}

for
1\leq i\leq r
.

A resolvable configuration where the number of points is the square of the block size is said to be a
net
.
A net with block size
k
and degree
r
is often referred to as a
(r,k)
-
net
but here rather than to adopt this notation we prefer to speak of a
(k^{2},k;r)
net.

There is an extensive literature on resolvable linear spaces, especially on resolvable Steiner 2-designs. We point out, in particular,
that the existence of a resolvable S
(2,k,v)

For some literature on resolvable configurations we refer to

Let us return to “the world of Heffter”.
It is quite evident that a H
(m,n;h,k)
gives rise to a resolvable PLS of degree 2 whose parallel classes are the
set of rows and the set of columns, respectively. This observation suggests to introduce the following new concept.

Definition 1.8
.

A
Heffter space
over an abelian group
G

is a resolved PLS whose parallel classes are Heffter systems on a half-set of
G
.
Speaking of a
(v,\{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\})
Heffter space we will mean a Heffter space with
r
parallel classes

{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
, …,
{\mathcal{P}}_{r}
where
{\mathcal{P}}_{i}
is a
(v,k_{i})
Heffter system
for
1\leq i\leq r
. If
k_{i}=k
for each
i
, it is clearly a
(v,k;r)
configuration so that
we will speak of a
(v,k;r)

Heffter configuration
.
In the more special case that
k_{i}=k
for each
i
and
v=k^{2}
, it is a net so that we
will speak of a
(k^{2},k;r)

Heffter net
.
A Heffter space over
G
will be said
cyclic
or
elementary abelian
if the group
G
has the respective property.

Note that a
(v,\{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\})
Heffter space is equivalent to a
(v,\{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\})
-RPLS
whose points can be bijectively labeled with the elements of a half-set of an abelian group
in such a way that the sum of the labels in each block is zero. Such a labeling will be called a
Heffter labeling
.

According to Definition
1.8
, a
(v,k)
Heffter system is nothing but a
(v,k;1)
Heffter space or, if you want,
a
(v,k;1)
Heffter configuration. Also, a H
(m,n;h,k)
is essentially the same as a
(nk,\{h,k\})
Heffter space.
In particular, a H
(n;k)
is essentially the same as a
(kn,k;2)
Heffter configuration.

The aim of this paper is to find sets of mutually orthogonal Heffter systems as large as possible.
Heffter spaces are the crucial tool to investigate this problem in view of the following.

Proposition 1.9
.

A
(v,\{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\})
-MOHS is equivalent to a
(v,\{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\})
Heffter space.
Thus, in particular, a
(v,k;r)
-MOHS is equivalent to a
(v,k;r)
Heffter configuration.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{R}}=\{{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
, …,
{\mathcal{P}}_{r}\}
be a
(v,\{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\})
-MOHS. Thus there is a half-set
V
of an abelian group of order
2v+1

such that
{\mathcal{P}}_{i}
is a
(v,k_{i})
Heffter system on
V
for
1\leq i\leq r
.
Consider the triple
{\mathcal{S}}=(V,{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{R}})
where
{\mathcal{B}}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}{\mathcal{P}}_{i}
.
Given two distinct members
B
,
B^{\prime}
of
{\mathcal{B}}
, we have
B\in{\mathcal{P}}_{i}
and
B^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{j}
for suitable indices
i
and
j
.
For
i=j
,
B
and
B^{\prime}
are disjoint since they are distinct blocks of the same Heffter system whereas for
i\neq j
we have
|B\cap B^{\prime}|\leq 1
since

{\mathcal{P}}_{i}
and
{\mathcal{P}}_{j}
are orthogonal. This means that
{\mathcal{S}}
is a PLS.
Also, by the definition of a Heffter system, each
{\mathcal{P}}_{i}
is a partition of
V
so that
{\mathcal{R}}
is a resolution of
{\mathcal{S}}
.
We conclude that
{\mathcal{S}}
is a
(v,\{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\})
Heffter space.

Conversely, let
{\mathcal{S}}=(V,{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{R}})
be a
(v,\{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\})
Heffter space with

{\mathcal{R}}=\{{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
, …,
{\mathcal{P}}_{r}\}
so that,
by definition,
\mathcal{P}_{i}
is a
(v,k_{i})
Heffter system for each
i
. Any two distinct blocks
of
{\mathcal{S}}
have at most one common point since
{\mathcal{S}}
is a PLS.
Hence, in particular, for
i\neq j
, any block of
{\mathcal{P}}_{i}
shares at most
one point with each block of
{\mathcal{P}}_{j}
, i.e.,
{\mathcal{P}}_{i}
and
{\mathcal{P}}_{j}
are orthogonal. Thus
{\mathcal{R}}
is a
(v,\{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\})
-MOHS.
∎

Let us determine a trivial necessary condition for the existence of a
(v,\{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\})
-MOHS.

Proposition 1.10
.

If there exists a
(v,\{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\})
-MOHS, then
k_{1}+{\dots}+k_{r}-r\leq v-1
and equality holds if and only if
the associated Heffter space is linear.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{R}}=\{{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
, …,
{\mathcal{P}}_{r}\}
be a
(v,\{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\})
-MOHS and let

{\mathcal{S}}=(V,{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{R}})
be its associated Heffter space.
Take any point
x\in V
and, for
1\leq i\leq r
, let
B_{i}
be the only block of
{\mathcal{P}}_{i}
containing
x
.
The set of points which are collinear with
x
is
\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}(B_{i}\setminus\{x\})
which has size
\sum_{i=1}^{r}(k_{i}-1)=k_{1}+{\dots}+k_{r}-r
.
The first part of the statement follows since, obviously, the number of points collinear with
x
is at most
v-1
.
The second part also follows since to say that
\mathcal{S}
is linear is equivalent to
say that every point
x\in V
is collinear with any other point of
V
, i.e., that the number of points collinear with
x
is
precisely
v-1
.
∎

Applying the above in the case that
k_{i}=k
for each
i
we get the following.

Corollary 1.11
.

If there exists a
(v,k;r)
-MOHS, then
r(k-1)\leq v-1
and the equality holds if and only if
the associated Heffter space is a S
(2,k,v)
.

This immediately gives an upper bound on the maximum number of mutually orthogonal
(v,k)
Heffter systems.

Corollary 1.12
.

The maximum number of mutually orthogonal
(v,k)
Heffter systems cannot exceed
\bigl\lfloor{v-1\over k-1}\bigl\rfloor.

Reaching the upper bound
\bigl\lfloor{v-1\over k-1}\bigl\rfloor
seems to us a really exceptional occurrence. For instance, when
v-1
is
divisible by
k-1
this bound is precisely
{v-1\over k-1}
and by Corollary
1.11

the Heffter space associated with a
(v,k;{v-1\over k-1})
-MOHS would be a Heffter S
(2,k,v)
.
Yet, we are tempted to conjecture that a Heffter
linear
space cannot exist. Indeed it should satisfy some necessary conditions that
we will establish in the next section and that appear to us extremely demanding.

Thus it is natural to say that a
(v,\{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\})
Heffter space
{\mathcal{S}}=(V,{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{R}})

is more interesting the closer it is to a linear space.
A good parameter to measure this “distance” is the density
{|E|\over{|V|\choose 2}}
of the collinear graph of

\mathcal{S}
, that is the graph
Col({\mathcal{S}})=(V,E)

with vertex set
V
where two vertices are adjacent if and only if they are collinear.
By the proof of Proposition
1.10
,
Col({\mathcal{S}})
is regular of degree
k_{1}+...+k_{r}-r
,
hence its density is equal to
{k_{1}+...+k_{r}-r\over v-1}
.
It is maximum and equal to 1 precisely when
Col({\mathcal{S}})
is complete and
{\mathcal{S}}
is linear.
In case of a small density
Col({\mathcal{S}})
is
sparse
and
{\mathcal{S}}
is far from being linear.

Speaking of the density of a Heffter space
\mathcal{S}
or its correspondent set of MOHSs we will mean
the density of the collinear graph
Col(\mathcal{S})
.

The construction for Heffter spaces of degree greater than 2 appears to us difficult even without the request of a
high density. In this paper we present some constructions for Heffter spaces with odd block sizes and an arbitrarily large degree
with the crucial help of finite fields. In most of the constructions we take advantage of the elementary fact that the set
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box}

of non-zero-squares of a field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of order
q\equiv 3
(mod 4) is a half-set of its additive group which, with an abuse of notation, will be also denoted by
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

For now, getting Heffter spaces with even block sizes appears to us much more difficult. Some constructions will be presented
in a future paper
[
18
]
where we will use a completely different approach which is not based on finite fields.

Our main results on MOHSs derive from the direct constructions for Heffter spaces obtained throughout the paper.
They can be summarized as follows.

A construction for Heffter spaces obtained in Section 3 using the notion of a
partial partition of a group
allows us to state
the following.

Theorem 1.13
.

If
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}
are mutually coprime
odd integers such that
2k_{1}\cdot{\dots}\cdot k_{r}+1
is a prime power
q
, then there exists a
({q-1\over 2},\{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\})
-MOHS.

The above gives, for any integer
r
, infinite values of
v
for which there exists a set of
r
mutually orthogonal Heffter systems
of order
v
. On the other hand the block sizes of these systems are pairwise distinct whereas, especially for the applications,
it would be better to have the same result with a constant block size. This result, which is one of the strongest in this paper,
is a consequence of a construction obtained in Section 4 using
difference packings
and cyclotomy.

Theorem 1.14
.

There exists a
({q-1\over 2},k;r)
-MOHS for every triple
(q,k,r)
such that
k\geq 3
is odd and
q\equiv 2k+1

(
mod
4k)
is a prime power greater
than
8k^{5}\lceil{r\over k}\rceil
.

We might be quite satisfied of this result but the density of these sets of MOHSs is very small since it is less than
{1\over 2k^{4}}
.
On the other hand, some computer results indicate that our constructions succeed for values of
q
much less than the bound
obtained in the theorem and the density of the related sets of MOHSs is often greater than
{1\over 2}
.

It is clear (also from Theorem
1.7
) that

is the smallest admissible

for which it is possible to have a
(v,k;r)
Heffter configuration

. In the extremal case of

we have a Heffter net.
In Section 5 we will give a method to construct some
(k^{2},k;4)
Heffter nets when
k
is odd and
2k^{2}+1
is a prime power.
Using this method we have established that there exists a cyclic
(k^{2},k;4)
Heffter net for
k=9
, 21 and 27 with the help of a computer.
It is reasonable to believe that this method succeeds for any admissible
k>3
.

We have also constructed a
(121,11;9)
Heffter net over
\mathbb{F}_{3^{5}}
which deserves a special attention considering that it is the
densest Heffter configuration that we have found (its density is equal to
{3\over 4}
). Thus we have the following.

Theorem 1.15
.

There exists a
(k^{2},k;4)
-MOHS for
k=9,21,27
and a
(121,11;9)
-MOHS.

First, we put a little bit of order in the literature concerning

Indeed, while according to some authors two S

on orthogonal

Finally, as major application of our research on the Heffter spaces,
we prove the following.

Theorem 1.16
.

If

is a prime power with
kw
odd and
k\geq 3
, then
there are at least
\lceil{w\over 4k^{4}}\rceil
mutually orthogonal
k
-cycle systems of order
q
.

2.
Heffter linear spaces: do they exist?

It is natural to ask whether there exists a Heffter space which is linear, in particular a Heffter S
(2,k,v)
.

To understand how hard this question is, it is convenient to make a small digression on the theory
of
additive designs
founded in
[
23
]
and then developed in various papers such as
[
14
,
15
,
24
,
36
]
.

A
t
-
(v,k,\lambda)
design
{\mathcal{D}}=(V,{\mathcal{B}})
, in particular a S
(2,k,v)
, is said to be
G
-additive
if there exists a
G
-additive labeling
of
\mathcal{D}
, i.e., an injective map

f
from
V
to an abelian group
G
such that
\sum_{x\in B}f(x)=0

for every block
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
.

The definition can be extended, in the obvious way, to any combinatorial design.
For instance, in
[
13
]

additive graph decompositions
will be considered.

Now note that the definition of a Heffter space can be equivalently formulated saying that it is
a
G
-additive
(v,\{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\})
-RPLS satisfying the “half-set condition” that
the labels fill a half-set of
G
.

The last request becomes very demanding if we want a linear space.
Indeed we have to consider that the construction of additive Steiner 2-designs appears
to be very difficult, even without any other additional request (see
[
14
,
15
]
).
A Heffter Steiner 2-design besides being additive has to be resolvable
and has to satisfy the half-set condition.

In the next proposition we give a necessary condition showing, as a corollary, that the answer to our question is certainly
negative for cyclic Heffter spaces.

Proposition 2.1
.

If there exists a Heffter linear space of degree
r
over a group
G
,
then
r-1
is a multiple of the order of any element
g\in G
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a Heffter linear space as in the statement and let
V
be its point set so that we have
G=\{0\}\ \cup\ V\ \cup\ -V
.
It is enough to prove that
o(g)
divides
r-1
only for
g\in V
. Indeed the assertion is obvious for
g=0
and the orders of the elements of
-V
coincide with
the orders of their opposite which are in
V
.
So take
g\in V
and let
B_{1}
, …,
B_{r}
be the blocks of
{\mathcal{S}}
passing through
g
.
Given that each
B_{i}
is zero-sum, we have that the elements of
B_{i}\setminus\{g\}

sum up to
-g
for
1\leq i\leq r
. The fact that
{\mathcal{S}}
is linear implies that
\{B_{i}\setminus\{g\}\ :\ 1\leq i\leq r\}
is a partition of
V\setminus\{g\}

so that the sum of all the elements of
V
is equal to
r(-g)+g=(1-r)g
. On the other hand, as observed shortly after
Definition
1.1
, the half-set
V
is zero-sum. Thus the identity
(r-1)g=0
holds in
G
, i.e.,
o(g)
divides
r-1
.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

A cyclic Heffter space cannot be linear.

Proof.

Let
r
be the degree of a Heffter linear space over
\mathbb{Z}_{2v+1}
.
By Proposition
2.1
,
r-1
should be a multiple of the order of 1 that is
2v+1
. Thus the degree of a point would
be greater than the number
v
of the points. This is obviously absurd.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

A necessary condition for the existence of a Heffter linear space of order
v
and degree
r
is that every prime
divisor of
2v+1
is also a divisor of
r-1
.

Proof.

Assume that there exists a Heffter linear space of order
v
and degree
r
over a group
G

and let
p
be a prime factor of
2v+1
.
By the theorem of Cauchy, the group
G
has an element of order
p
and then, by Proposition
2.1
,

p
is a divisor of
r-1
.
∎

It is clear from the previous corollaries that the condition of Proposition
2.1
is very strict. Yet, we are going to see that there
are values of
v
and
k
for which this condition does not forbid a Heffter S
(2,k,v)
.
In the next proposition, given a natural number
n
,
we denote by
R_{n}
the ring which is the direct product of all the fields whose orders are the
maximal prime power factors of
n
so that, for instance,
R_{45}
means
\mathbb{F}_{9}\times\mathbb{F}_{5}
.
Also, we denote by
rad(n)
the
radical
of
n
, that is the product of all distinct prime divisors of
n
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
u\geq 7
be an odd integer with
u\not\equiv 3

(
mod
9)
.
Then Proposition
2.1
does not exclude the existence of a Heffter S
(2,k,v)
over
R_{u^{n}}

with
k={u-1\over 2}
,
v={u^{n}-1\over 2}
and
n\equiv 1

(
mod
\omega)
where
\omega
is the multiplicative order of
3

(
mod
u-3)

or
(
mod
{u-3\over 3})
according to whether
3
does not divide or divides
u-3
, respectively.

Proof.

Let
v
and
k
be two integers as in the statement. It is obvious that
v\equiv 0
(mod
k
). Also, by definition of
\omega
, it is easy to check that

v-1\equiv 0
(mod
k-1
).
Thus the trivial necessary conditions for the existence of a resolvable S
(2,k,v)
are satisfied.
The degree of such a putative S
(2,k,v)
is the so-called
replication number

r={v-1\over k-1}
so that we have

r-1=u\cdot{u^{n-1}-1\over u-3}
. It follows that
u
divides
r-1
since one can see that
{u^{n-1}-1\over u-3}

is an integer by the definition of
\omega
again. Also note that
2v+1=u^{n}
and that the orders of the elements
of
R_{u^{n}}
are the divisors of rad
(u)
. Indeed, if
u=p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdot{\dots}\cdot p_{t}^{\alpha_{t}}
is
the prime power factorization of
u
, we have rad
(u)=p_{1}\cdot{\dots}\cdot p_{t}
and

R_{u^{n}}=\mathbb{F}_{p_{1}^{(\alpha_{1}n)}}\times{\dots}\times\mathbb{F}_{p_{t}^{(\alpha_{t}n)}}
. The order of an
element
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{t})\in R_{u^{n}}
is clearly equal to the product of all the primes
p_{i}
with
x_{i}\neq 0
.
Thus the order of any
x\in R_{u^{n}}
is a divisor of
rad(u)
. It follows that it divides
u
and then
r-1
.
∎

The previous proposition applied with
u=7
does not exclude that there exists a Heffter S
(2,3,{7^{n}-1\over 2})
with
n
odd.
On the other hand, according to Theorem 3.7 in
[
23
]
, the only additive S
(2,3,v)
are the point-line designs associated with a projective geometry
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
and the point-line designs associated with an affine geometry over
\mathbb{F}_{3}
. In the first case
v
is a Mersenne number,
in the second case
v
is a power of 3. Thus, for a putative Heffter S
(2,3,{7^{n}-1\over 2})
we should have
{7^{n}-1\over 2}=2^{m}-1
or

{7^{n}-1\over 2}=3^{m}
for some
m
. This would mean that either
7^{n}+1=2^{m+1}
or
{7^{n}-1\over 7-1}=3^{m-1}
. But the first identity contradicts
the well-celebrated proof of Catalan’s conjecture in
[
45
]
that 8 and 9 are the only two consecutive perfect powers of natural numbers.
The second identity is also not possible in view of the known results on the famous
Nagell-Ljunggren equation

{x^{n}-1\over x-1}=y^{m}

(see
[
8
]
).

Applying Proposition
2.4
with
u=9
we deduce the possible existence of an S
(2,4,{9^{n}-1\over 2})
over
\mathbb{F}_{9^{n}}
.
The point-line design associated with the projective geometry PG
(2n-1,3)
is actually a resolvable Steiner 2-design with
these parameters. It is also
\mathbb{F}_{9^{n}}
-additive in view of Theorem 5.1 in
[
15
]
. But unfortunately, the

\mathbb{F}_{9^{n}}
-additive labeling produced by the proof of this theorem is not a Heffter labeling since it does not satisfy the half-set condition.
Indeed the labels fill the set of squares of
\mathbb{F}_{9^{n}}
which is not a half-set of
\mathbb{F}_{9^{n}}
.

Applying Proposition
2.4
with
u=11
we deduce the possible existence of an S
(2,5,{11^{n}-1\over 2})
over
\mathbb{F}_{11^{n}}

with
n
odd. For instance, we cannot exclude that there exists a Heffter S
(2,5,665)
over
\mathbb{F}_{11^{3}}
.

3.
Heffter spaces via partial partitions of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box}

A set
\mathcal{S}
of subgroups of a group
G
covering the whole
G
and intersecting each other trivially is said to be
a partition of
G

and gives rise to a resolvable linear space with special properties
[
1
]
. In particular, when all the subgroups
have the same order
s
and
G
has order
s^{2}
it is called a
partial congruence partition
of
G
and it gives rise to a

translation net
(see, e.g.,
[
6
,
40
]
).
Here we speak of a
partial partition of a group
G
(without the term “congruence”) to mean a set
\mathcal{S}
of subgroups of
G

intersecting each other trivially but not necessarily covering the whole
G
.
The following result is probably folklore but we prove it anyway for convenience of the reader.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}=\{S_{1},\dots,S_{r}\}
be a partial partition of a group
G
of order
v
, let
k_{i}
be the order of
S_{i}
, and let
\mathcal{P}_{i}
be
the set of all right cosets of
S_{i}
in
G
for
1\leq i\leq r
. Then
\mathcal{P}_{1}
, …,
\mathcal{P}_{r}
are the parallel classes
of a
(v,\{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\})
-RPLS.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the union of all the
\mathcal{P}_{i}
’s.
If two distinct elements
g,h
of
G
are contained in a right coset of a subgroup
S_{i}
,
we necessarily have
gh^{-1}\in S_{i}
. On the other hand, by definition of a partial partition of
G
the element
gh^{-1}
belongs
to at most one subgroup of
{\mathcal{S}}
. We conclude that
\{g,h\}
is contained in at most one member of
\mathcal{B}
, i.e.,

(G,{\mathcal{B}})
is a partial linear space. Each
{\mathcal{P}}_{i}
is obviously a partition of
G
for each
i

so that
\{\mathcal{P}_{1},...,\mathcal{P}_{r}\}
is a resolution of
(G,{\mathcal{B}})
and the assertion follows.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
q=2v+1
be a prime power with
v
the product of
r
mutually coprime odd integers
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}
.
Then there exists a
(v,\{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\})
Heffter space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Proof.

It is clear that
q\equiv 3
(mod 4) so that
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box}
is a half-set of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
For
1\leq i\leq r
, let
S_{i}
be the group of
k_{i}
-th roots of unity of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Given that the
k_{i}
’s are mutually coprime, the product
S_{1}\cdot{\dots}\cdot S_{r}
is the subgroup of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
of order
k_{1}\cdot{\dots}\cdot k_{r}={q-1\over 2}
,
hence
S_{1}\cdot{\dots}\cdot S_{r}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box}
. Also,
\{S_{1},\dots,S_{r}\}
is clearly a partial partition of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box}
.
It follows, by Proposition
3.1
, that if
{\mathcal{P}}_{i}
is the set of cosets of
S_{i}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box}
, then

\mathcal{P}_{1}
, …,
\mathcal{P}_{r}
are the parallel classes of a
(v,\{k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\})
-RPLS with point set
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box}
.
Now note that each block of this RPLS is zero-sum since any coset of a non-trivial multiplicative subgroup of a finite field
is zero-sum (see, e.g., Fact 1 in
[
14
]
). It follows that every block of
\mathcal{P}_{i}
is zero-sum, hence

\mathcal{P}_{i}
is a
(v,k_{i})
Heffter system on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box}
.
The assertion follows.
∎

Now it is clear that Theorem
1.13
is an immediate consequence of Theorem
3.2
and Proposition
1.9
.

As an example, we can apply Theorem
3.2
with
r=3
and
(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})=(3,5,7)
since
2k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}+1=211
is a prime.
We obtain in this way a
(105,\{3,5,7\})
Heffter space.

Note that Corollary 3.2 in
[
11
]
is essentially Theorem
3.2
applied in the case
r=2
.

Let us show that Theorem
3.2
allows us to obtain Heffter spaces of arbitrarily large order.

Theorem 3.3
.

There exists a Heffter space of degree
r
for any positive integer
r
.

Proof.

In view of Theorem
3.2
,
it is enough to show that for any given
r
there exists an
r
-tuple
(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})
of mutually coprime odd integers such that
2k_{1}\cdot{\dots}\cdot k_{r}+1
is a prime.
Take
r-1
mutually coprime odd integers
k_{1}
, …,
k_{r-1}
(for instance the first
r-1
odd primes) and set
m=2k_{1}\cdot{\dots}\cdot k_{r-1}
.
It is obvious that we have
\gcd(m^{2},m+1)=1
and hence, by the theorem of Dirichlet, the arithmetic progression

\{m^{2}n+(m+1)\ |\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
has infinitely many primes. Take one of these primes
p
so that we can write
p=m^{2}n+(m+1)=m(mn+1)+1

for a suitable
n
, and set
k_{r}=mn+1
. Note that
k_{r}
is coprime with
m
and hence coprime with each
k_{i}

with
i\in\{1,\dots,r-1\}
. Also note that
k_{r}
is odd since
m
is even and that we have

p=2k_{1}\cdot{\dots}\cdot k_{r-1}k_{r}+1
.
∎

A stronger version of the above result will be obtained in the next section by using
difference methods
.

4.
Heffter spaces via difference packings

Given a subset
B
of an additive (resp. multiplicative) group
G
, the
list of differences
of
B
is the
multiset
\Delta B
consisting of all possible differences
x-y
(resp. ratios
xy^{-1}
) with
(x,y)
an ordered pair of distinct elements of
B
.
More generally, one defines the list of differences of a collection
{\mathcal{F}}=\{B_{1},\dots,B_{n}\}
of subsets of
G
as the multiset

\Delta{\mathcal{F}}:=\Delta B_{1}\ \cup\ \dots\ \cup\ \Delta B_{n}
.
The collection
\mathcal{F}
is said to be a
difference packing
in
G
if
\Delta{\mathcal{F}}
does not have repeated elements.
It is a
difference family of index
1
if
\Delta\mathcal{F}
is precisely the set of all non-identity elements of
G
(see e.g.
[
7
,
27
]
).
Speaking of a
(G,\{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\})
difference packing we mean a difference packing
\{B_{1},\dots,B_{n}\}
in
G
with
|B_{i}|=k_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
.
We will write
(G,k;n)
instead of
(G,\{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\})
when
k_{i}=k
for
1\leq i\leq n
.
Note, in particular, that a
(\mathbb{Z}_{v},k;n)
difference packing can also be seen as a
(v,k,1)

optical orthogonal code
.
The unique member of a
(\mathbb{Z}_{v},k;1)
difference packing is said to be a
(v,k)

modular Golomb ruler
(MGR).
A
(v,k)
-MGR is said to be
resolvable
if
k
is a divisor of
v
and its elements are pairwise distinct modulo
k
, i.e., they
form a complete set of residues (mod
k
) (see
[
20
]
).

Now recall that the
development
of a subset
B
of a group
G
is the collection
devB
consisting of all possible translates of
B
in
G
, that is

devB=\{B\star g\ |\ g\in G\}
where
\star
is the additive or multiplicative operation of
G
.
More generally, the development of a collection
{\mathcal{F}}=\{B_{1},\dots,B_{n}\}
of subsets of
G
is the multiset union
devB_{1}\ \cup\ \dots\ \cup\ devB_{n}
.
It is very well known that the pair
(G,dev{\mathcal{F}})
is a partial linear space (resp. a linear space) if and only if
{\mathcal{F}}
is a difference packing
(resp. a difference family of index
1
).
In particular, if
B
is a
(v,k)
-MGR, then
(\mathbb{Z}_{v},devB)
is a symmetric
(v,k;k)
-configuration which is resolvable if and only if
B
is resolvable as well (see Theorem 4.3 in
[
20
]
).

Considering that our aim is to construct Heffter spaces, we are interested in resolvable partial linear spaces generated by a difference packing.
A complete classification of them is beyond the scope of this paper.
We limit ourselves to consider a special class of them.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
q=2v+1
be a prime power with
v
odd, let
\mathcal{F}=\{B_{1},\dots,B_{n}\}
be a set of subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box}
, and let
\phi
be an isomorphism
between
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
. We say that
\mathcal{F}
is a
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box},\{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\})
Heffter difference packing
if the following conditions hold:

(H
1
)

\phi({\mathcal{F}})
is a
({v},\{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\})
difference packing;

(H
2
)

\phi(B_{i})
is a resolvable
(v,k_{i})
-MGR for
1\leq i\leq n
;

(H
3
)

each
B_{i}
is zero-sum in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

The only member of a
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box},\{k\})
Heffter difference packing will be called a
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box},k)
Heffter ruler
.

Remark 4.2
.

It is convenient to observe that
\mathcal{F}
is a
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box},k;n)
Heffter difference packing if and only if
it is a
n
-set of
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box},k)
Heffter rulers whose lists of differences are pairwise disjoint.

The above terminology is justified by the following.

Theorem 4.3
.

If
q=2v+1
is a prime power with
v
odd, and there exists a
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box},\{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\})
Heffter difference packing, then there exists
a
(v,\{k_{1}^{k_{1}},\dots,k_{n}^{k_{n}}\})
Heffter space. In the additional hypothesis that the difference packing is simple,
the resulting Heffter space is simple too.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}=\{B_{1},\dots,B_{n}\}
be a
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box},\{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\})
Heffter difference packing.

Given any pair
(x,y)
of elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box}
we have
\phi(xy^{-1})=\phi(x)-\phi(y)
since
\phi
is a group isomorphism.
Thus we have

\phi(\Delta B)=\Delta\phi(B)
for every subset
B
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box}
and then we can write

\phi(\Delta B_{1}\ \cup\ \dots\ \cup\ \Delta B_{n})=\phi(\Delta B_{1})\ \cup\ \dots\ \cup\ \phi(\Delta B_{n})=\Delta\phi(B_{1})\ \cup\ \dots\ \cup\ \Delta\phi(B_{n}).

Thus, given that
\phi
is bijective and that condition (H
1
) says that
\Delta\phi(B_{1})\ \cup\ \dots\ \cup\ \Delta\phi(B_{n})

does not have repeated elements, we can say that
\Delta B_{1}\ \cup\ \dots\ \cup\ \Delta B_{n}
is also free of repetitions.
This means that
\mathcal{F}
is a full-fledged
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box},\{k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\})
difference packing so that

(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box},dev{\mathcal{F}})
is a PLS whose point set
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box}
is a half-set of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
since
q\equiv 3
(mod 4)
by assumption.

For
1\leq i\leq n
, condition (H
2
) says that
k_{i}
divides
{v}
and that the elements of
\phi(B_{i})
form a complete set of residues (mod
k_{i}
).
Equivalently, they form a complete system of representatives for the cosets of the subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
of index
k_{i}
, that is
k_{i}\mathbb{Z}_{v}
.
For
i=1,\dots,n
, let
S_{i}
be the subgroup of index
k_{i}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box}
, that is the group of non-zero
k_{i}
th powers of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Given that
\phi
is a group isomorphism, we have that
B_{i}
is a complete system of representatives for the cosets of
S_{i}

in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box}
for
1\leq i\leq n
.
It follows that

{\mathcal{P}}_{i}:=\{B_{i}s\ |\ s\in S_{i}\}
is a partition of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box}
, i.e.,
a parallel class of
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box},dev{\mathcal{F}})
.
Take a complete system
T_{i}
of representatives for the cosets of
S_{i}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box}
so that we have
\{st\ |\ s\in S_{i};t\in T_{i}\}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box}
.
We can take, for instance,
T_{i}=\{g^{j}\ |\ 0\leq j\leq k_{i}-1\}
with
g
a generator of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box}
.
Since
{\mathcal{P}}_{i}
is a parallel class,
{\mathcal{P}}_{i}t:=\{B_{i}st\ |\ s\in S_{i}\}
is a parallel class as well for every
t\in T_{i}
.
Thus we see that
{\mathcal{R}}_{i}:=\{{\mathcal{P}}_{i}t\ |\ t\in T_{i}\}
is a partition of
devB_{i}
into
k_{i}
parallel classes.
We conclude that
{\mathcal{R}}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}{\mathcal{R}}_{i}
is a resolution of
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box},dev{\mathcal{F}})
,
hence
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box},dev{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{R}})
is a
(v,\{k_{1}^{k_{1}},\dots,k_{n}^{k_{n}}\})
-RPLS.
Finally, any block
B
of
dev\mathcal{F}
is of the form
xB_{i}
for a suitable
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box}
and a suitable
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.
The elements of
B
sum up to
xs
where
s
is the sum of the elements of
B_{i}
.
Thus
B
is zero-sum since
s=0
by condition (H
3
) and then every member of
{\mathcal{R}}
is a Heffter system.

Finally note that if
\mathcal{F}
is simple, then every member of
dev\mathcal{F}

Corollary 4.4
.

A
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box},k;n)
Heffter difference packing gives rise to a
({q-1\over 2},k;kn)
Heffter configuration
which is simple if the difference packing is simple as well. Also, this configuration can be extended to a
({q-1\over 2},\{k^{kn},{q-1\over 2k}\})
Heffter space.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}=\{B_{1},\dots,B_{n}\}
be a
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box},k;n)
Heffter difference packing.
Applying Theorem
4.3
we immediately get a
(v,k;kn)
Heffter space
\mathcal{S}=(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box},dev{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{R}})

that is a
({q-1\over 2},k;kn)
Heffter configuration which is simple if
\mathcal{F}
is simple as well.
Recall that the
k
elements of any member of
\mathcal{F}
are in pairwise distinct cosets of the subgroup

S
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box}
of index
k

k

dev\mathcal{F}

S
.
This means that any two distinct elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box}

S

\mathcal{S}

{\mathcal{C}}

5.
Some constructions for Heffter nets over a finite field

In this section we aim to construct some
(k^{2},k;r)
Heffter nets over a finite field.
Thus
2k^{2}+1
should be a prime power
q
and the point set should be a half-set of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
In this case the method of differences considered in the previous section is totally impotent.
Indeed the application of this method would require a Heffter
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\Box},k)
ruler and hence,
by definition, a resolvable
(k^{2},k)
-MGR. This would be a
k
-subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{k^{2}}
whose list of
differences is
\mathbb{Z}_{k^{2}}\setminus k\mathbb{Z}_{k^{2}}
, i.e., a cyclic
(k,k,k,1)

relative difference set

whose non-existence has been known for a long time (see Theorem 6.2 in
[
35
]
).

5.1.
Constructing
(9n^{2},3n;4)
Heffter nets

We first consider the case when
q=2k^{2}+1
with
k
divisible by 3, say
k=3n
, so that
q=18n^{2}+1
. The following theorem
gives a method to construct a
(9n^{2},3n;4)
Heffter net over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
q=18n^{2}+1
a prime power and
n
odd. In the proof
we start from a standard
(9n^{2},3n;4)
Heffter net
\mathcal{N}
with
point set
\mathbb{Z}_{3n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3n}
and then we show that under the hypothesis of the theorem there exists a Heffter labeling of
\mathcal{N}

with labels in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
q=18n^{2}+1
be a prime power with
n
odd, let
x
be a
(3n)
-th primitive root of unity of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
and let
Y=(y_{0},y_{1},\dots,y_{3n-1})
be a zero-sum complete system of representatives for the cosets
of
C^{3n}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
such that both the sums

\displaystyle\sigma=\sum_{i=0}^{3n-1}x^{i}y_{i}
and
\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}=\sum_{i=0}^{3n-1}x^{-i}y_{i}
are null.
Then there exists a
(9n^{2},3n;4)
Heffter net.

Proof.

Consider the following four partitions of
\mathbb{Z}_{3n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3n}
into subsets of size
3n
:

{\mathcal{P}}_{1}=\bigl\{\mathbb{Z}_{3n}\times\{j\}\ |\ j\in\mathbb{Z}_{3n}\bigl\};

{\mathcal{P}}_{2}=\bigl\{\{(0,j),(1,j+1),(2,j+2),\dots,(3n-1,j+3n-1)\}\ |\ j\in\mathbb{Z}_{3n}\bigl\};

{\mathcal{P}}_{3}=\bigl\{\{(0,j),(1,j-1),(2,j-2),\dots,(3n-1,j-3n+1)\}\ |\ j\in\mathbb{Z}_{3n}\bigl\};

{\mathcal{P}}_{4}=\bigl\{\{i\}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3n}\ |\ i\in\mathbb{Z}_{3n}\bigl\}.

It is readily seen that that the triple
{\mathcal{N}}=(V,{\mathcal{B}},{\mathcal{R}})
with

V=\mathbb{Z}_{3n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3n},\quad\quad{\mathcal{B}}=\displaystyle\bigcup_{i=1}^{4}{\mathcal{P}}_{i},\quad\quad{\rm and}\quad\quad{\mathcal{R}}=\{{\mathcal{P}}_{1},{\mathcal{P}}_{2},{\mathcal{P}}_{3},{\mathcal{P}}_{4}\}

is a
(9n^{2},3n;4)
net. Thus, to prove the assertion it is enough to show that
\mathcal{N}
admits a Heffter labeling.

Let
X=\langle x\rangle
be the group of
(3n)
-th roots of unity of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Considering that
n
is odd,
\{1,-1\}\cdot X
is the group
of
(6n)
-th roots of unity. Note that this group is the same as the group
C^{3n}
of
(3n)
-th powers of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}

so that we can write

\{1,-1\}\cdot X=C^{3n}\quad\quad{\rm and}\quad\quad C^{3n}\cdot Y=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}.

Thus, setting
V=X\cdot Y
, we see that we have
\{1,-1\}\cdot V=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, i.e.,
V

is a half-set of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Now consider the map

f:(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}_{3n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3n}\longrightarrow x^{i}y_{j}\in V

which is clearly bijective;
x^{i_{1}}y_{j_{1}}=x^{i_{2}}y_{j_{2}}
implies
y_{j_{1}}y_{j_{2}}^{-1}=x^{i_{2}-i_{1}}\in C^{3n}

so that
j_{1}=j_{2}
by definition of
Y
and then
i_{1}=i_{2}
.
Let us prove that
f
is a Heffter labeling, namely that
f(B)
is zero-sum for every block
B
of the net considered above.

If
B\in{\mathcal{P}}_{1}
, then
B=\{(i,j)\ |\ 0\leq i\leq 3n-1\}
for some
j
, hence we have

\sum_{P\in B}f(P)=\sum_{i=0}^{3n-1}x^{i}y_{j}=y_{j}\sum_{i=0}^{3n-1}x^{i}=0

since
X
is zero-sum. Indeed, as recalled earlier, any non-trivial subgroup
of the multiplicative group of a finite field is zero-sum (see, e.g., Fact 1 in
[
14
]
).

If
B\in{\mathcal{P}}_{2}
, then
B=\{(i,j+i)\ |\ 0\leq i\leq 3n-1\}
for some
j
, hence we have

\sum_{P\in B}f(P)=\sum_{i=0}^{3n-1}x^{i}y_{j+i}=x^{-j}\sum_{i=0}^{3n-1}x^{j+i}y_{j+i}=x^{-j}\sigma=0

since
\sigma
is null by assumption.

If
B\in{\mathcal{P}}_{3}
, then
B=\{(i,j-i)\ |\ 0\leq i\leq 3n-1\}
for some
j
, hence we have

\sum_{P\in B}f(P)=\sum_{i=0}^{3n-1}x^{i}y_{j-i}=x^{j}\sum_{i=0}^{3n-1}x^{i-j}y_{j-i}=x^{j}\sigma^{\prime}=0

since
\sigma^{\prime}
is null by assumption.

Finally, if
B\in{\mathcal{P}}_{4}
, then
B=\{(i,j)\ |\ 0\leq j\leq 3n-1\}
for some
i
, hence we have

\sum_{P\in B}f(P)=\sum_{j=0}^{3n-1}x^{i}y_{j}=x^{i}\sum_{i=0}^{3n-1}y_{j}=0

since
Y
is zero-sum by assumption.
The assertion follows.
∎

, …,
\{x_{k-1},x_{0}\}
.

A
k
-cycle system of order
2v+1
is a set
\mathcal{C}
of
k
-cycles whose edges partition the edge set of
K_{2v+1}
, the complete graph on
2v+1
vertices.
Such a system is said to be
G
-regular
if the vertex set of
K_{2v+1}
is an additive group
G
and we have

C+g\in{\mathcal{C}}
for every pair
(C,g)\in{\mathcal{C}}\times G
. In other words if, up to isomorphism,
G
is an

automorphism group
of
\mathcal{C}
.

A 3-cycle system is usually called a
Steiner triple system
and one usually denotes it by STS
(2v+1)
if its order is
2v+1
.
It is evident that it is equivalent to a S
(2,3,2v+1)
, this is why in this case the vertices of
K_{2v+1}
are usually called
points
and the
3-cycles are called
blocks
or
triples
.
The literature on Steiner triple systems is huge and we refer to
[
29
]
for a comprehensive textbook on the topic.

The existence problem for a
k
-cycle system of order
2v+1
has been completely solved in
[
2
]
for
k
odd and in
[
50
]

for
k
even. See also
[
10
]
for an alternative purely algebraic proof for the odd case.

Cycle systems are special
graph decompositions
. Given a subgraph
\Gamma
of a graph
K
, a
\Gamma
-decomposition of
K
is a set
\mathcal{D}

of graphs isomorphic to
\Gamma
whose edges partition
E(K)
. Thus a
k
-cycle system of order
2v+1
is a
\Gamma
-decomposition of
K_{2v+1}
where
\Gamma

is the
k
-cycle. In general, two graph decompositions
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
of the same graph
K
are said to be
orthogonal
if any graph of
\mathcal{D}

has at most one edge in common with any graph of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
(see, e.g.,
[
25
]
)
and a multitude of authors actually adhered to this definition.
Yet, concerning the orthogonality of
k
-cycle systems, we think it is appropriate to distinguish the case
k=3
from the case
k>3

in order to put some order in the literature.

6.1.
Orthogonal Steiner triple systems

According to the general definition of orthogonal graph decompositions, two Steiner triple systems should be said orthogonal
precisely when they do not have any triple in common, i.e., they are disjoint. So it was done, for instance, in the recent papers
[
21
,
42
]
.
On the other hand this is in contrast
with an extensive literature on
orthogonal Steiner triple systems
as introduced in
[
47
]
.
In this literature, two
Steiner triple systems

{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}

are defined to be orthogonal if, besides being disjoint, satisfy the following demanding condition:

(8)

\{u,v,a\},\{x,y,a\}\in{\mathcal{C}}\quad{\rm and}\quad\{u,v,w\},\{x,y,z\}\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}\Longrightarrow w\neq z.

Here, just in order to avoid confusion, we say that two STSs are orthogonal when they are disjoint (hence orthogonal in the usual sense
used for graph decompositions), and that they are
super-orthogonal

when they are orthogonal in the sense of
[
47
]
.
We prefer, however, to re-elaborate condition (
8
) still in terms of orthogonality in the general sense as follows.
Observe that if
{\mathcal{C}}
is a STS
(v)
with point set
V
and
x
is one of its points, then the set
N(x)
of all pairs
\{y,z\}
such that
\{x,y,z\}
is a triple of
\mathcal{C}

is a near 1-factor of
K_{v}
. Also, the set
{\mathcal{N}}=\{N(x)\ |\ x\in V\}
is a near 1-factorization of
K_{v}

that we call
the near 1-factorization associated with
\mathcal{C}
.
It is then clear that the definition of super-orthogonality can be equivalently reformulated as follows.

Definition 6.1
.

Two Steiner triple systems are super-orthogonal when they do not have any triple in common and their associated near 1-factorizations
are orthogonal.

In view of the well celebrated results about the existence of
large sets of Steiner triple systems

[
43
,
44
,
52
]

we can say that the maximum number of mutually orthogonal STS
(v)
is
v-2
whichever is
v
.

Concerning the maximum number
\omega(v)
of mutually super-orthogonal STS
(v)
we have the following:

•

\omega(v)=1
for
v=3,9
(see
[
46
]
);

•

\omega(v)=2
for
v=7,13,15
(see
[
37
]
);

•

\omega(v)\geq 2
for all admissible
v\geq 19
(see
[
28
]
);

•

\omega(v)\geq 3
for all admissible
v\geq 19
with 24 possible exceptions (see
[
32
]
).

•

\omega(v)>n
for
v
a prime power sufficiently large with respect to
n
(see
[
39
]
).

6.2.
Orthogonal

For

, according to the general definition of orthogonal graph decompositions,
two

,
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
of the same order

are orthogonal if
any cycle

shares at most one edge with any cycle

Apart from special instances of the pair

\mu(k,2v+1)

For now, it is known that we have

in the following cases:

and
kn\equiv 0
or 3 (mod 4) (see

•

k=5
and
3\leq n\leq 100
(see
[

]
);

•

k\equiv 0
(mod 4) (see
[

]
);

•

k\equiv 3
(mod 4) and

(mod 4) (see
[

•

k\equiv 3

We also have:

•

\mu(k,v)\geq 2
for
4\leq k\leq 9
and any admissible

A greater lower bound has been established for
k

:

•

\mu(k,2kn+1)\geq\begin{cases}4n&{\rm if}\ k=4\cr{n\over 4k-2}-1&{\rm if}\ k\equiv 0\ {\rm(mod\ 4)},\ k>4\cr{n\over 24k-18}-1&{\rm if}\ k\equiv 2\ {\rm(mod\ 4)}\end{cases}

Here, exploiting the results of the previous sections, we will be able to prove the existence of
an arbitrarily large set of mutually orthogonal
k
-cycle systems for any odd
k
.
For this we need again some difference methods.

The
list of differences
of a graph
\Gamma
with vertices in an additive group
G
is the multiset

\Delta\Gamma=\bigcup_{e\in E(\Gamma)}\Delta e

where
\Delta e
is the list of differences of the
edge
e
as defined in Section 4: if
e=\{x,y\}
, then
\Delta e=\{x-y,y-x\}
.
In other words,
\Delta\Gamma
is the list of all possible differences between two adjacent vertices of
\Gamma
.

Note, in particular, that a
k
-cycle
C=(c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{k-1})
with vertices in a group
G
has list of differences

\Delta C=\{c_{i+1}-c_{i},\ c_{i}-c_{i+1}\ |\ 0\leq i\leq k-1\}
where it is understood that
c_{k}=c_{0}
.

The following basic fact is just a special consequence of a more general result concerning

G
-regular graph decompositions
obtainable via difference methods (see, e.g., Theorem 2.1 in
[
17
]
).

Proposition 6.2
.

If
G
is an additive group of order
2kn+1
, then the existence of a
G
-regular
k
-cycle system is equivalent to a
n
-set
{\mathcal{F}}=\{C_{1},\dots,C_{n}\}

of
k
-cycles with vertices in
G
such that
\Delta C_{1}\ \cup\ \dots\ \cup\ \Delta C_{n}=G\setminus\{0\}
.

A set
\mathcal{F}
as in the statement of Proposition
6.2
is said to be a set of
base cycles
for the
related
G
-regular
k
-cycle system
\mathcal{C}
since we have
{\mathcal{C}}=\{C_{i}+g\ |\ 1\leq i\leq n;g\in G\}
.

Recall that
B=\{b_{0},b_{1},\dots,b_{k-1}\}
is a simple subset of
G
(see Definition
1.2
) if the associated sequence of partial
sums
C=(c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{k-1})=(b_{0},b_{0}+b_{1},b_{0}+b_{1}+b_{2},...)
does not have repeated elements. In this case
C
may be interpreted as a
k
-cycle and
noting that
c_{i}-c_{i-1}=b_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k-1
we can write

\Delta C=\pm\{b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{k-1},c_{k-1}-b_{0}\}.

Thus, considering that
c_{k-1}
is the sum of all the elements of
B
, when
B
is zero-sum we have
\Delta C=B\ \cup\ -B
.

Hence, given that the blocks of a Heffter system are all zero-sum, if
{\mathcal{P}}=\{B_{1},\dots,B_{n}\}
is a simple
(v,k)

Heffter system on a half-set
V
of
G
, then we have

\Delta C_{1}\ \cup\ \dots\ \cup\ \Delta C_{n}=(B_{1}\ \cup\ \dots\ \cup\ B_{n})\ \cup\ -(B_{1}\ \cup\ \dots\ \cup\ B_{n})

where
C_{i}
is the “partial sum cycle” associated with
B_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
.
At this point, recalling that the blocks of
\mathcal{P}
partition
V
and that
V
is a half-set of
G
, we see that

\Delta C_{1}\ \cup\ \dots\ \cup\ \Delta C_{n}=V\ \cup\ -V=G\setminus\{0\}.

Thus
\{C_{1},\dots,C_{n}\}
is a set of base cycles for a
G
-regular
k
-cycle system in view of Proposition
6.2
.

So we have the following.

Proposition 6.3
.

If
{\mathcal{P}}
is a simple
(v,k)
Heffter system over
G
, then the partial-sum cycles associated with the blocks
of
\mathcal{P}
form a set of base cycles for a
G
-regular
k
-cycle system.

One could show that the converse is also true when
G
has order
2kn+1
for some
n
: in this case any
G
-regular
k
-cycle system
is derived from a
(v,k)
Heffter system over
G
. This is why we can say that a
(v,k)
Heffter system
over
\mathbb{Z}_{2v+1}
exists: as recalled in the introduction,
the existence of a
\mathbb{Z}_{2kn+1}
-regular
k
-cycle system has been established for all possible pairs
(v,k)
(see
[
12
]
and
[
9
]
).

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
and
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
be the sets of base cycles arising, via Proposition
6.3
, from two orthogonal
simple Heffter systems
{\mathcal{P}}
and
{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}
, respectively. Also, let
{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
be
the respective
k
-cycle systems arising from them.
Assume that there is a cycle of
\mathcal{C}
, say
C+g
with
(C,g)\in{\mathcal{F}}\times G
, sharing
two edges
e_{1}
,
e_{2}
with a cycle of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
, say
C^{\prime}+h
with
(C^{\prime},h)\in{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}\times G
.
Then
\Delta e_{1}\ \cup\ \Delta e_{2}
is contained both in
\Delta(C+g)=\Delta C
and in

\Delta(C^{\prime}+h)=\Delta C^{\prime}
. Now recall that we have
\Delta C=B\ \cup\ -B
where
B
is the block of
{\mathcal{P}}

from which
{\mathcal{C}}
derives and, analogously,
\Delta C^{\prime}=B^{\prime}\ \cup\ -B^{\prime}
where
B^{\prime}
is the block of
{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}

from which
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
derives. Thus we have
\Delta e_{1}\ \cup\ \Delta e_{2}\subset(B\ \cap\ B^{\prime})\ \cup\ -(B\ \cap\ B^{\prime})
.
Then two of the four elements of
\Delta e_{1}\ \cup\ \Delta e_{2}
necessarily are in
B\ \cap\ B^{\prime}
contradicting that
any block of
{\mathcal{P}}
intersects any block of
{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}
in at most one element.

We conclude that
{\mathcal{C}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
are orthogonal. Thus we can state the following.

Proposition 6.4
.

If there exists a MOHS
(v,k;r)
whose members are all simple, then
\mu(k,2v+1)\geq r
.

Taking into account Proposition
6.4

.

We have:

For (i), it enough to use the simple

7.
Conclusions

We have established that there is an equivalence between a set of
r
mutually orthogonal Heffter systems and
a resolvable partial linear space of degree
r
that we have called a Heffter space.

We have determined various constructions for Heffter spaces of block size odd allowing us to claim
that for any
r>0
and any odd
k\geq 3
, there are infinitely many values of
v
for which there exists
a set of
r
mutually orthogonal Heffter systems of order
v
and block size
k
.

At this moment, reaching the analogous result for
k
even appears to us quite difficult.
Anyway we are preparing a new paper
[
18
]
with several constructions for Heffter spaces
with block size even and (an unfortunately) small degree
r
.

Several other open problems arise from this research.
In our opinion, the most intriguing is the one presented in Section 2.

Open problem.
Does there exist a Heffter linear space?

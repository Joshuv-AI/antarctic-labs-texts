Falsifiable analog model predictions of W W mass in CDF II and ATLAS
R. N. C. Pfeifer

Abstract
The CDF II measurement of W W boson mass is at significant ( 6.9 ​ σ 6.9\,\sigma ) tension with the Standard Model, and moderate ( 4.0 ​ σ 4.0\,\sigma ) tension with recent results from ATLAS. Any attempt to interpret this as a signature of new physics requires high-precision, robust, falsifiable predictions. The Classical Analogue to the Standard Model In pseudo-Riemannian spacetime (CASMIR) is an analogue model predicting W W and Z Z boson masses of 80.3587 ​ ( 22 ) ​ GeV / c 2 80.3587(22)~\mathrm{G}\mathrm{eV}/c^{2} and 91.1877 ​ ( 35 ) ​ GeV / c 2 91.1877(35)~\mathrm{G}\mathrm{eV}/c^{2} respectively. During baryon collisions satisfying s < 3.09 ​ TeV \sqrt{s}<3.09~\mathrm{T}\mathrm{eV} , CASMIR predicts a color-mediated enhancement of W W and Z Z boson masses, becoming 80.4340 ​ ( 22 ) ​ GeV / c 2 80.4340(22)~\mathrm{G}\mathrm{eV}/c^{2} and 91.1922 ​ ( 35 ) ​ GeV / c 2 91.1922(35)~\mathrm{G}\mathrm{eV}/c^{2} respectively. The unenhanced masses are consistent with ATLAS data collected at s = 7 ​ TeV \sqrt{s}=7~\mathrm{T}\mathrm{eV} , and the enhanced masses are consistent with CDF II data collected at s = 1.96 ​ TeV \sqrt{s}=1.96~\mathrm{T}\mathrm{eV} . According to CASMIR, operation of the LHC at centre-of-mass energies small compared with 3.09 ​ TeV 3.09~\mathrm{T}\mathrm{eV} but large enough for W W boson formation should permit ATLAS to replicate the result from CDF II.

2.1
Weak boson masses

A key discrepancy between CASMIR and the Standard Model is the prediction in CASMIR of paired eigenvalues for the
W
and
Z
boson masses, with the components of each doublet differing by less than
90~\mathrm{M}\mathrm{eV}/c^{2}
. No suggestion of such a doublet has been seen in any single accelerator dataset, though it is only recently that the weak boson masses have been measured to sufficient precision to permit discrimination of such closely-spaced values. It is also noted that in CASMIR the upper eigenvalues of each doublet are associated with the presence of a color charge, which carries implications for the environments in which these eigenvalues may be observed.

In Sec.
3
these properties of CASMIR are assessed for consistency with existing datasets, in particular LEP, ATLAS, and CDF II. On the basis of the available information, and with the exception of the
W
boson mass measurement in CDF II, existing datasets are not provably able either to detect or to exclude the upper
W
and
Z
boson mass eigenvalues predicted by CASMIR.

Prospects for additional near-term tests of CASMIR are discussed, including prospects for
reproduction of the CDF II result in ATLAS.

2.2
Muon
g-2

As a consequence of differences in the electroweak sector, when the muon gyromagnetic anomaly is measured at the muon “magic momentum”
[

, CASMIR predicts a higher value for the muon gyromagnetic anomaly than the Standard Model. While a full-precision re-evaluation of the muon gyromagnetic anomaly using CASMIR is beyond the scope of the present paper, the natures of these changes to the electroweak sector make it straightforward to determine bounds on the result of such a calculation (see Sec.
4

116592071(46)\times 10^{-11}
shows significantly reduced tension with experimental results
[
9

with only a small increase in uncertainty, and this reduction in tension exceeds that which can be attributed to expansion of the confidence interval alone. Consequently CASMIR predicts the muon gyromagnetic anomaly more accurately at the muon magic momentum than the Standard Model, and hence—despite this discrepancy with the Standard Model—measurements of muon
g-2
do not constrain the applicability of CASMIR.

2.3
Preferred rest frame and finite cutoff

An unusual property of CASMIR is the existence of a pseudovacuum state with a preferred rest frame. This pseudovacuum is associated with an energy scale
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}\approx 6.18~\mathrm{T}\mathrm{eV}
, and is responsible for the CASMIR analogue of quantum fluctuations in energy and momentum
[
17
,
1
]

\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}
implies a similarly finite UV cutoff of

\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}\approx 3.1~\mathrm{T}\mathrm{eV}
in the isotropy frame of the cosmic microwave background (CMB).

Energy scales in CASMIR correspond to the inverse of autocorrelation scales in the pseudovacuum. Since a field excitation of wavelength
\lambda

corresponds to an excitation energy

As a consequence, processes whose dominant terms involve energy or momentum fluctuations that are large compared with

\mathrm{O}\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}\right)
in the rest frame of the CMB.

I am unaware of any practical proposals to test the ability of quantum fluctuations to exceed
3.1~\mathrm{T}\mathrm{eV}

Thus, although the existence of a preferred rest frame in CASMIR represents a significant theoretical break from the Standard Model, as yet it provides no prospects for experimental discrimination between CASMIR and the Standard Model.

2.6
Neutrino mass and oscillations

Neutrinos in CASMIR are massless and Weyl, with equilibration between generations arising not as a consequence of neutrino oscillation, but through nonconservation of particle generation at
W
boson vertices (Ref.
[
1
]
, Sec.
). Consequently there exists a discrepancy in the area of neutrino physics between CASMIR and the Standard Model with massive neutrinos and a seesaw mechanism. Direct detection of neutrino inertial mass would place limitations on the application of CASMIR, as would a lower bound on the length scale over which neutrino oscillations take place. Neither of these events have occurred. Likewise, CASMIR does not support neutrinoless double beta decay, and neutrinoless double beta decay has also not been observed.
As yet,
neutrino physics does
not place bounds on the regime of congruency between CASMIR, experiment, and the Standard Model.

2.8
Summary of limits

The discrepancies between CASMIR and the Standard Model which are likely to impact upon present experimental results are those which arise from the existence of additional weak sector vector boson mass eigenstates in CASMIR. These additional eigenstates correspond to colored versions of the
W
and
Z
bosons, having masses within a hundred
\mathrm{M}\mathrm{eV}/c^{2}
of their colorless counterparts.
Incorporating the colored mass eigenstates into the calculation of
W
boson mass yields a good match for the result from CDF II, and existence of these additional mass eigenstates also favorably impacts calculation of the muon gyromagnetic anomaly.

In light of these discrepancies, Sec.
3
addresses why current evaluation of the CDF II dataset is predicted by CASMIR to be sensitive to these novel species while ATLAS is not, and provides a prescription for detection of the higher-mass eigenstates in ATLAS.
Section
4
then adjusts the calculation of the muon gyromagnetic anomaly to include the CASMIR colored weak boson mass eigenstates. The CASMIR result is consistent with experiment with tension well below
1\,\sigma
, demonstrating that results from the Muon
g-2
experiment do not constrain the application of CASMIR as an analogue to the Standard Model in the relevant energy regimes.

3.2.1
Particles in CASMIR

CASMIR is a heirarchical effective field theory model in which all species observed in the Standard Model are constructed from nine charged preons, and these preons themselves emerge as an effective low-energy description of the gradients of scalar fields on a higher-dimensional manifold. Although the microscopic origin of the preons is specific to CASMIR, once they are constructed these fields display the familiar structure of a sequence of effective descriptions valid over a descending ladder of energy scales.
4
4

4

There are two principal energy thresholds in CASMIR, namely:

\mathcal{E}_{\psi}

\mathcal{E}_{0}

This sort of multiscale description is ubiquitous throughout particle physics—for example, hadrons are (quasi)particles composed from collections of quarks, atomic nuclei are composed in turn from hadrons, and yet other quasiparticles such as phonons may emerge from the collective behaviors of atoms

3.2.3
Environments supporting chromatic bosons in CASMIR

With the mass of the chromatic
W
boson anticipated to be only a little higher than that of the achromatic
W
boson, access to both chromatic and achromatic
W
boson mass eigenstates may be anticipated in high-energy environments such as the
1.96~\mathrm{T}\mathrm{eV}
collisions of the CDF II experiment. However, the chromatic
W
boson is a colored particle whereas the total color of a
p\overline{p}
collision vanishes. In an environment containing few color charges a chromatic
W
boson is tightly bound to its color-neutralising counterparts, and gluon exchange distributes energy and momentum among these colored species over the preon confinement timescale
t_{\psi}
. These few-particle confinement interactions do not in general cancel one another out, and if they occur on a similar or shorter timeframe than pseudovacuum scattering events, they disrupt the construction of massive propagators and cause confined colored bosons to behave as if massless.

In contrast, in a sufficiently homogeneous and anisotropic color-rich environment with vanishing net particle color due to a balanced sum over color charges, confinement is weakened and a colored boson acquires asymptotic freedom. Provided the extent of the environment is large compared with
t_{\psi}
, a chromatic
W
boson may propagate freely through the colored environment while engaging in multiple sequential mass-generating interactions with the CASMIR pseudovacuum. Thus the chromatic
W
bosons acquire mass only in color-rich environments with lifetimes long compared with
t_{\psi}
.
Regarding
t_{\psi}
and the associated energy scale
\mathcal{E}_{\psi}
:

•

Since the quark color charges are residuals of the preon color charges, it follows that
\mathcal{E}_{\psi}
is no smaller than the strong force scale in CASMIR.

•

In CASMIR, quasiparticles arise as perturbations on a collection of scalar fields whose inflection points have a characteristic space–time separation
t_{\Omega}
in the CASMIR isotropy frame. As a consequence the energy scale of spontaneous particle emissions is bounded from above by
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}
in this frame, and the mean time between spontaneous emission events is bounded from below by
t_{\Omega}
.
It thus follows that
\mathcal{E}_{\psi}
in this frame is no larger than
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}
.

•

There exists no mechanism in the Standard Model or CASMIR to fix the strong scale below
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}
, nor experimental evidence to suggest this. To the extent that CASMIR reproduces the behaviors of the Standard Model (or in case of discrepancy, reproduces experimentally observed behaviors) below
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}
, it may be assumed that the preon binding scale in CASMIR corresponds to the upper energy scale at which spontaneous gluon exchange may take place, yielding

\mathcal{E}_{\psi}=\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}\qquad t_{\psi}=t_{\Omega}=h\cdot\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}^{-1}.

(35)

Color-rich environments with lifetimes long compared with
t_{\psi}
are thus favored by energy scales small compared with
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}\approx 6.18~\mathrm{T}\mathrm{eV}
.

Next recall that all fermion species in CASMIR are composed from three colored preons, and all bosons are constructed from co-propagating preon/antipreon pairs.
[
23
]
Binding in the fermions is mediated by preon exchange, in particular the exchange of preon/antipreon pairs with nonvanishing color but vanishing electroweak charge, which form a representation of
\mathrm{SU}(3)_{C}
and act as effective gluons.

Keeping the above in mind, it is now necessary to identify those environments which may sustain virtual chromatic weak vector bosons, and those which may sustain real chromatic weak vector bosons. To begin with virtual bosons, recall that in CASMIR, virtual particles borrow energy from the pseudovacuum. The typical energy imparted by a single pseudovacuum interaction is on order
\mathcal{E}_{0}

3.5
Cross-sections and branching ratios

In addition to having effects on the measured values of
W
and
Z
boson mass, the differences between the electroweak sectors of CASMIR and the Standard Model also affect the massive weak vector boson cross-sections and branching ratios. This Section evaluates the magnitudes of these effects.

3.5.1
CASMIR corrections to Standard Model calculations

In hadron colliders such as Tevatron and the LHC, the primary mode of
W
and
Z
boson production is described by the Drell-Yan process
[
36
,
37
]
shown at tree level in Fig.
3
.

Figure 3:
Drell-Yan process for formation of a massive weak vector boson
V\in\{W^{\pm},Z\}
from the collision of two partons (lower vertex) and decay into a fermion pair (upper vertex).

Higher-order corrections to this process are well-understood, with the most important perturbative corrections arising from Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD). These have been evaluated to next-to-next-to-next-to-leading order (N
3
LO) in Refs.
[
38
,
39
,
40
]
, with electroweak (EW) corrections and mixed QCD-EW corrections being evaluated in Refs.
[
41
,
42
,
43
,
44
]
.
As a result the Standard Model values of the associated cross-sections are known to a precision of around 2%, with uncertainty dominated by the Parton Distribution Function (PDF). Similarly, measurements of these cross-sections in ATLAS and CMS are also capable of reaching precisions of a few parts in
10^{2}

[
45
,
46
,
47
]
, permitting testing of these predictions in the LHC.

Theoretical evaluation of these parameters is quite involved and is generally automated through use of specialist software, e.g. Ref.
[
48
]
. Fortuitously, on comparing CASMIR with the Standard Model the corrections to cross-sections and branching ratios are not large, and to determine the equivalent parameters in CASMIR it suffices to consider corrections to the tree-level process.

The relevant differences between the electroweak sectors of CASMIR and the Standard Model are as follows:

1.

In CASMIR there exist eight additional
W
-like and eight additional
Z
-like bosons, carrying color as well as electroweak charges.

2.

The strength of the weak interaction is a derived quantity in CASMIR, differing slightly from its measured value.

3.

The weak boson masses and tau mass in CASMIR are derived quantities also, differing slightly from their measured values.

However, at tree level the Fermi constant may be written

G_{F}=\frac{\pi\alpha}{\sqrt{2}m_{W}^{2}\left(1-\tfrac{m_{W}^{2}}{m_{Z}^{2}}\right)}\left[1+\mathrm{O}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)\right],

(60)

and since the fine structure constant
\alpha
is an input parameter for both CASMIR and the Standard Model, it follows that the corrections arising from item
2
above may be considered collectively with those arising from item
3
.

3.5.2
Consequences for cross sections and decay rates

Consider now the tree-level contribution to the inclusive scattering cross-section
\sigma^{\mathrm{tot}}_{V}
for production of a massive vector boson
V\in\{W^{\pm},Z\}
, associated with the lower vertex of Fig.
3
.
The value of
\sigma^{\mathrm{tot}}_{V}
is affected by (i) changes in the number of boson species having a weak sector representation consistent with boson
V
in the Standard Model (i.e. inclusion of colored weak vector boson species in CASMIR), (ii) changes to the interaction strength associated with the production of these bosons compared with production of boson
V
in the Standard Model, and (iii) changes to the masses of these bosons compared with boson
V
in the Standard Model. It is therefore necessary to consider all three enumerated differences between the electroweak sectors of CASMIR and the Standard Model.

First, consider item
1
in CDF. Recognize that as described in Sec.
3.3.1
, during CDF Run II the generation of weak bosons took place within a color-rich foreground which was long-lived compared with the timescale of the CASMIR mass interaction. As a consequence, in CASMIR:

•

Parton/parton interactions are capable of yielding any of the nine choices of
C
-sector representation for boson
V
.

•

Preon-scale interactions may transfer color between the boson and the color-rich environment of the interior of the colliding hadrons.

•

The unbroken
\mathrm{GL}(3,\mathbb{R})
symmetry of the CASMIR color sector ensures that any boson has a
\frac{1}{9}
chance of carrying the neutral color charge
\lambda_{9}\propto\mathbb{I}_{3}
when attempting to escape confinement.

•

For any such boson which escapes confinement, factorization of
\mathrm{GL}(3,\mathbb{R})_{C}
[implicitly
\mathrm{GL}(3,\mathbb{R})_{C}\otimes\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{C})
] into
\mathrm{GL}(1,\mathbb{R})_{C}\otimes\mathrm{SU}(3)_{C}
[again, implicitly
{}\otimes\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{C})
] as per Appendix
of Ref.
[
22
]

implies that the process of creating this boson [which carries a noninteracting charge on
\mathrm{GL}(1,\mathbb{R})_{C}
] is in turn necessarily factorizable into independent processes on the
A
and
C
sectors, with the
C
sector being overall trivial. This factorizability holds for the emission process as a whole, even if it does not necessarily hold vertex-by-vertex within the domain of the color-rich foreground.

•

Consequently, regardless of the actual
C
-sector representation associated with a
V
-type boson at the time of its initial production, if that boson escapes confinement then the net emission vertex may be rewritten as an equivalent pure
A
-sector process, and the tree-level vertex for this process is identical to that for the colorless
V
boson (having
C
-sector representation
\lambda_{9}
), up to the effects of any differences in the mass vertex coefficients (which are addressed separately—items
2
-
3
in the enumerated list above).

Putting this all together yields nine times as many events generating
V
-type bosons within the hadron collision environment, all of identical weight, of which only
\frac{1}{9}
actually result in
V
boson emission beyond the confinement scale.

Second, the Fermi constant receives a correction, and as mentioned immediately following Eq. (
60
) this may be re-expressed in terms of corrections to the weak boson masses.
Contributions arise from corrections to both the
W
and the
Z
boson masses, though in practice
those arising from corrections to the
Z
boson mass are negligible.

Finally, evaluate the magnitude of corrections arising from the boson and tau masses as per item
3
. For CDF II, factors of
\left(m_{V}^{\mathrm{SM}}\right)^{2}
in the Standard Model calculation may be replaced by
\left(m_{V,\mathrm{r.m.s.}}^{\mathrm{CASMIR}}\right)^{2}
resulting in a multiplicative correction which, to leading order, has the form

\left\{1+\mathrm{O}\left[\frac{\left(m_{V,\mathrm{r.m.s.}}^{\mathrm{CASMIR}}\right)^{2}-\left(m_{V}^{\mathrm{SM}}\right)^{2}}{\left(m_{V}^{\mathrm{SM}}\right)^{2}}\right]\right\}.

(61)

The discrepancies between the CASMIR
Z
boson masses and their Standard Model counterparts are small [see Table
A
of
A
, and Eq. (
59
)], and with
G_{F}
being a derived quantity in CASMIR, corrections dependent on the
W
mass enter all processes at tree level through Eq. (
60
), even for
V=Z
. It therefore suffices to replace
V\rightarrow W
in Eq. (
61
).
Corrections due to differences in the tau mass are also small, and contributions to
\sigma^{\mathrm{tot}}_{V}
are of sub-leading order so may be neglected.

Next consider corrections to massive weak vector boson emission in ATLAS. Provided the centre of mass frame of the collider is sufficiently close to the isotropy frame of CASMIR, the higher energy scale reduces the lifetime of the color-rich foreground to below the timescale of the CASMIR mass interaction. This eliminates color transfer between the color-rich foreground and the weak vector boson over timescales long compared with the mass interaction scale (see Sec.
3.2.3
).
Regarding item
1
, because the color-sector representations of the bosons are fixed over mass-interaction timescales, the only bosons participating in mass interactions are those which escape confinement carrying representation
\lambda_{9}
. However, the resulting numerical factors of
9
and
\frac{1}{9}
remain unchanged. Regarding items
2
-
3
, this fixing of the associated color-sector representations prevents averaging of mass vertices such that
m_{V,\mathrm{r.m.s.}}^{\mathrm{CASMIR}}
is replaced in Eq. (
61
) by
m_{V}^{\mathrm{CASMIR}}
:

\left\{1+\mathrm{O}\left[\frac{\left(m_{V}^{\mathrm{CASMIR}}\right)^{2}-\left(m_{V}^{\mathrm{SM}}\right)^{2}}{\left(m_{V}^{\mathrm{SM}}\right)^{2}}\right]\right\}.

(62)

A similar discussion applies to the evaluation of decay constants
\Gamma(V\rightarrow\ldots)
associated with particular choices of species at the upper vertex of Fig.
3
.
Arguments equivalent to those presented for the cross-section similarly yield multiplicative correction factors having the forms of Eq. (
61
) for CDF II and Eq. (
62
) for ATLAS with
V\in\{W^{\pm},Z\}
.
The very small difference in tau mass between CASMIR and the Standard Model does in principle yield a correction to the some tree-level decay constants, but this is not numerically relevant at current precisions.

To evaluate the significance of the above corrections, it is convenient to rewrite the leading-order corrections of Eqs. (
61
)–(
62
) in a more general form

\left[1+\Theta_{\sigma\Gamma}(s)\Xi_{W}(s)\right]

(63)

where

\Xi_{W}(s)
is a process-independent function of energy satisfying

\Xi_{W}(s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{\left(m_{W,\mathrm{r.m.s.}}^{\mathrm{CASMIR}}\right)^{2}-\left(m_{W}^{\mathrm{SM}}\right)^{2}}{\left(m_{W}^{\mathrm{SM}}\right)^{2}}~~\textrm{for $m_{W}c^{2}\ll\sqrt{s}\ll 3.09~\mathrm{T}\mathrm{eV}$}\\
\frac{\left(m_{W}^{\mathrm{CASMIR}}\right)^{2}-\left(m_{W}^{\mathrm{SM}}\right)^{2}}{\left(m_{W}^{\mathrm{SM}}\right)^{2}}~~\textrm{for $\sqrt{s}\gg 3.09~\mathrm{T}\mathrm{eV}$}\end{array}\right.

(64)

and
\Theta_{\sigma\Gamma}({s})
is a coefficient of
\mathrm{O}(1)
which is dependent on the energy scale and the physical process being corrected.
Parameter
\Xi_{W}(s)
evaluates to
2 parts in
10^{3}
in CDF II and 4 parts in
10^{4}
in ATLAS.

5
Conclusion

The Classical Analogue to the Standard Model In pseudo-Riemannian spacetime (CASMIR) effectively reproduces many properties of the Standard Model. In addition, it predicts the higher
W
boson mass detected by CDF II with a tension of
0.1\,\sigma
. [Observed:
80.4335(94)~\mathrm{G}\mathrm{eV}/c^{2}
. Predicted:
80.4340(22)~\mathrm{G}\mathrm{eV}/c^{2}
.] In CASMIR this increase in mass arises due to the electroweak vector bosons sometimes being able to carry color charges, with the heavier, colored
W
boson mass vertices being excluded from the ATLAS calculation but contributing in CDF II. This mechanism also alters the value of the muon gyromagnetic anomaly, yielding a value more consistent with experiment (tension reduced from
5.2\,\sigma
to
0.2\,\sigma
).

The utility of an analogue model is determined by its ability to make predictions about experimental results. In addition to the shift in apparent
W
boson mass, which occurs only in hadron collisions where
\sqrt{s}
is small compared with
3.09~\mathrm{T}\mathrm{eV}
, CASMIR also predicts a shift in apparent
Z
boson mass under the same conditions, increasing from
91.1877(35)~\mathrm{G}\mathrm{eV}/c^{2}
to
91.1922(35)~\mathrm{G}\mathrm{eV}/c^{2}
.
Existing experimental data lacks the precision to definitively probe this small (
4.5~\mathrm{M}\mathrm{eV}/c^{2}
) correction.

Current and planned future LHC beam energies are large when compared with the CASMIR chromatic mass vertex suppression threshold of
\sqrt{s}<3.09~\mathrm{T}\mathrm{eV}
, so future measurements at the LHC are anticipated to yield the lower (achromatic) values for both
W
boson and
Z
boson mass.
Nevertheless, it is feasible to probe this mass augmentation effect using existing and near-term forthcoming apparatus. The optimal configuration would be a high-luminosity hadron collider such as the LHC or the proposed HL-LHC upgrade
[
63
]
, operating sufficiently below the chromatic mass vertex suppression threshold of

3.09~\mathrm{T}\mathrm{eV}
. (The former Tevatron accelerator satisfied this condition with
\sqrt{s}=1.96~\mathrm{T}\mathrm{eV}
.)

Appropriate lower-energy runs could be performed during the commissioning process for the HL-LHC.

Finally, if the domain of utility for CASMIR is sufficiently broad then it is possible that observations in astroparticle physics
might support or exclude higher-generation weak bosons at the energies predicted in Table
1
, or that
some dark matter detection program or study of scalar boson interactions at the LHC might suggest existence of a species consistent with Sec.
2.5
.
However, there is no imminent expectation for existing experiments to test these latter predictions.

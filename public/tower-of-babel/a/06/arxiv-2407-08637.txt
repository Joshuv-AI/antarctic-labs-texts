Corners with polynomial side length
Noah Kravitz, Borys Kuca, James Leng

Abstract
. A P P -polynomial corner , for P ∈ ℤ ⁡ [ z ] P\in\mathbb{Z}[z] a polynomial, is a triple of points ( x , y ) , ( x + P ⁡ ( z ) , y ) , ( x , y + P ⁡ ( z ) ) (x,y),\;(x+P(z),y),\;(x,y+P(z)) for x , y , z ∈ ℤ x,y,z\in\mathbb{Z} . In the case where P P has an integer root of multiplicity 1 1 , we show that if A ⊆ [ N ] 2 A\subseteq[N]^{2} does not contain any nontrivial P P -polynomial corners, then | A | ≪ P N 2 ( log ⁡ log ⁡ log ⁡ N ) c |A|\ll_{P}\frac{N^{2}}{(\log\log\log N)^{c}} for some absolute constant c > 0 c>0 . The main ingredients in our proof are a multidimensional quantitative concatenation result from our companion paper [ 16 ] and a novel degree-lowering argument for box norms.



1.
Introduction

In recent years, there has been a surge of interest in obtaining “reasonable” upper bounds for the Polynomial Szemerédi Theorem of Bergelson and Leibman
[
2
]
.
This theorem asserts that if
\mathbf{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{P}_{\ell}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}[z]
are polynomials satisfying some mild local conditions, then every subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
with upper positive Banach density must contain a (nontrivial) occurrence of the polynomial progression

{\textbf{x}},\;{\textbf{x}}+\mathbf{P}_{1}(z),\;\ldots,\;{\textbf{x}}+\mathbf{P}_{\ell}(z).

This result provided a unified proof for several earlier results in arithmetic Ramsey theory over
{\mathbb{Z}}
, including Roth’s pioneering theorem
[
36
]
on sets avoiding
3
-term arithmetic progressions, Szemerédi’s later generalization
[
42
]
to sets avoiding
\ell
-term arithmetic progressions for arbitrary fixed
\ell\geq 3
, and work of Sárközy
[
37
,
38
]
and Furstenberg
[
6
]
(independently) on sets avoiding length-
2
polynomial progressions.

The Polynomial Szemerédi Theorem, despite its useful generality, comes with a drawback: Because the proof uses methods from ergodic theory, the result is ineffective and provides no bounds on sets avoiding polynomial progressions. Roth and Sárközy proved their theorems with fairly good bounds. Szemerédi provided bounds of tower-type which, although strictly-speaking “effective”, are not particularly “reasonable”. The work of Gowers on obtaining improved bounds for Szemerédi’s Theorem
[
7
,
8
]
gave birth to the field of higher-order Fourier analysis, and since then there has been substantial work on obtaining quantitative versions of various instances of the Polynomial Szemerédi Theorem.

Of particular interest is an early result due to Shkredov
[
39
,
40
]
, who obtained reasonable bounds for subsets of
[N]^{2}
avoiding the “corner” configuration

(x,y),\;(x+z,y),\;(x,y+z).

His argument involved a suitable notion of “pseudorandom sets” with which one can run a modification of Roth’s original density-increment strategy. Shkredov’s approach was later simplified by Green
[
9
,
10
]
for corner-free subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{D}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}^{D}
; see also the work of Lacey and McClain
[
20
]
for an improvement in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{D}\times\mathbb{F}_{2}^{D}
. Peluse’s recent work on subsets of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{D}
avoiding the “
L
-shaped configuration”
(x,y),\;(x+z,y),\;(x,y+z),\;(x,y+2z)
is an extension of Shkredov’s approach.

Until recently, Shkredov’s work on corners was the only instance of a “reasonable bound” for a multidimensional configuration over the integers. Our main result is a polynomial extension of Shkredov’s theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
be a polynomial with an integer root of multiplicity
1
. If
A\subseteq[N]^{2}
does not contain a configuration of the form
(x,y),\;(x+P(z),y),\;(x,y+P(z))
with
P(z)\neq 0
, then

|A|\ll_{P}\frac{N^{2}}{(\log\log\log N)^{c}}

for some absolute constant
c>0
.

Theorem
1.1
and the concurrently released work of Peluse, Prendiville and Shao
[
33
]
on the configuration
(x,y),\;(x+z,\;y),\;(x,\;y+z^{2})
provide the first examples of reasonable bounds for multidimensional polynomial (i.e., non-linear) progressions over the integers.

Using a calculation of Shkredov
[

W

A key analytic step in our argument is the following inverse theorem for polynomial corners, which may be of independent interest.

Let

Before we say more about our main theorem, let us briefly survey related results. Previous work has focused on
1
-dimensional polynomial progressions over the integers and both
1
-dimensional and multidimensional polynomial progressions in finite-field settings. One of the many important differences between the integers and finite fields is that for a fixed polynomial
P\in{\mathbb{Z}}[z]
of degree at least
2
, the image of
P
is an equidistributed set of positive density in (a sequence of) finite fields, while in the integers it is a sparse set with local biases; since there are more “opportunities” to find polynomial progressions in finite fields
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, it is generally easier to prove bounds on the sizes of sets avoiding polynomial progressions in finite-field settings, and much more is known here than over the integers.

Early results for
1
-dimensional polynomial progressions in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
(
p
a prime) are due to Bourgain and Chang
[
3
]
, Dong, Li, and Sawin
[
5
]
, and Peluse
[
28
]
. In a major breakthrough, Peluse
[
29
]
proved power-saving upper bounds for subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
avoiding the polynomial progression

x,\;x+P_{1}(z),\;\ldots,\;x+P_{\ell}(z)

for any given linearly independent polynomials
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[z]
with constant coefficients all zero. Her proof introduced the now-well-used technique of degree-lowering for Gowers norms. Peluse’s work has been generalized in several ways. For instance, the second author
[
17
]
proved reasonable upper bounds for subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
avoiding a given progression of the form

(x,\;x+z,\;\dots,\;x+(\ell-1)z,\;x+z^{\ell},\;\dots,\;x+z^{\ell+k-1}).

In
[
23
]
, the third author used a
U^{3}
-version of degree-lowering to obtain reasonable upper bounds for a large family of so-called “complexity-
1
” polynomial configurations. Lastly, Bergelson and Best
[
1
]
generalized Peluse’s result to general finite commutative rings.

Less is known about multidimensional progressions in finite fields. Regarding linear configurations, we have already mentioned results on corners and L-shaped configurations. In the higher-degree case, the second author
[
18
,
19
]
proved a general Szemerédi-type theorem for patterns of the form

{\textbf{x}},\;{\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{1}P_{1}(z),\;\dots,\;{\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}_{\ell}P_{\ell}(z),

where
P_{1},\dots,P_{\ell}
are linearly independent polynomials with constant coefficients all zero and
{\textbf{v}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{v}}_{\ell}
are arbitrary vectors in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{D}
. When
\ell=2
, special cases of this result had been established earlier by Han, Lacey, and Yang
[
13
]
.

Let us now turn to polynomial progressions in
{\mathbb{Z}}
. In a sequence of papers
[
29
,
31
,
32
]
, Peluse and Prendiville adapted Peluse’s original degree-lowering argument for progressions of the form
x,\;x+P_{1}(z),\;\ldots,\;x+P_{m}(z)
where the polynomials
P_{i}
have distinct degrees. Prendiville
[
35
]
also proved reasonable bounds for sets avoiding arithmetic progressions with common difference of the form
z^{d}
for fixed
d
; the homogeneity of the polynomial
z^{d}
allowed him to use a density-increment argument. Peluse, Sah, and Sawhney
[
34
]
, adapting a
U^{3}
-degree-lowering argument developed in
[
23
]
, addressed the case of three-term arithmetic progressions with common difference
z^{2}-1
. They proved a “transference” result relating the number of
3
-term arithmetic progressions to the number of
3
-term arithmetic progressions with common difference
z^{2}-1
.

By a simple projection argument (see the proof of Corollary
1.3
below), Theorem
1.1
contains the setting studied by Peluse, Sah, and Sawhney as a special case. Hence, our main theorem can be understood as a common generalization of Shkredov’s corners theorem and the result of Peluse, Sah, and Sawhney. To prove Theorem
1.1
, we will establish a transference result for corners, in the style of
[
34
]
, and then use Shkredov’s work as a black box. The starting point for our argument is a PET and quantitative concatenation result carried out in our companion paper
[
16
]
. The bulk of the present paper is devoted to obtaining degree-lowering for the polynomial corners configuration. Our degree-lowering argument is the first to use box norms instead of Gowers norms, and its success attests to the robustness of degree-lowering as a tool in arithmetic combinatorics.

1.1.
Future directions

Our results and methods suggest several avenues for future inquiry. The most natural open problem is extending Theorem
1.1
to a larger class of polynomials
P
. A particularly appealing example, recently highlighted by Peluse
[
27
, Problem 3.17]
, is
P(z)=z^{2}
. (Theorem
1.1
fails to cover
P(z)=z^{2}
because
0
, the only integer root, has multiplicity
2>1
.) One strategy, which we attempted initially before turning to transference arguments, is obtaining
U^{1}\times U^{1}
-control for the
P
-polynomial corner counting operator and then running Shkredov’s original density-increment argument for corners. Our degree-lowering argument goes through with
\widetilde{P}
replaced by an arbitrary (non-
W
-tricked) polynomial
P\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
, which gives the norm-control necessary for starting Shkredov’s iterative argument. The pitfall of this approach, however, is that in later stages of the iteration one must deal with subsets of pseudorandom sets rather than subsets of
[N]^{2}
, and this creates complications in both the arguments of this paper and the PET and concatenation results of our companion paper
[
16
]
. It seems possible that one could generalize our arguments to the setting of pseudorandom sets as in
[
39
,
40
]
, but we have not pursued this.

The polynomial progressions investigated in this paper belong to the class of linear patterns (in our case, corners) with polynomial differences. Other works on obtaining bounds for such configurations have proceeded either by modifying arguments for the corresponding linear patterns (as in, e.g.,
[
35
]
) or by using transference results to relate the counts of linear and polynomial patterns (as in the present work and
[
17
,
34
]
). Reasonable bounds are known for
L
-shaped configurations and arithmetic progressions of length
\ell\geq 4
, but their polynomial analogues are currently unknown, so it would be interesting to extend our methods to establish transference results for these two particular configurations. It would also be interesting to use this machinery to obtain more general transference principles, even for cases where there are not currently reasonable bounds for linear configurations.

Proof strategy

We now give an overview of the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Our overarching goal is to relate the number of polynomial corners to the number of ordinary corners; once this is accomplished, we can “transfer” Shkredov’s result (in a standard supersaturation version) from ordinary corners to polynomial corners.

Let
A\subseteq[N]^{2}
be a set of density
\delta

\beta_{1},\;\beta_{d}\neq 0

\Lambda^{\corners}(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}):=\sum_{x,y\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\E_{z\in[N]}f_{0}(x,y)f_{1}(x+z,y),f_{2}(x,y+z).

The number of
P

(N/\beta_{d})^{1/d}\Lambda(1_{A},1_{A},1_{A}),

where
\Lambda

\alpha
for

and shifting

in the expression for

is large. Grouping the exponential with

, we recognize this expression as an instance of the counting operator for the
2
-point configuration
(x,y),\;(x-\widetilde{P}(z),y+\widetilde{P}(z))

38

for
2
-point polynomial configurations, we conclude that

\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1})\cdot[\pm N],\;{\mathbf{e}}_{2}\cdot[\pm N]}^{4}

is large. A further sequence of manipulations in the flavor of stashing let us transfer this “degree-
2
control” from

back to

.

It remains to analyze the phase function

. It turns out that it suffices to understand the case where
f_{2}
is a “
U^{1}\times U^{1}
-obstruction” of the form

for some

, in which case we are dealing with the expression

\displaystyle\sum_{x,y}\E_{z\in[N^{1/d}]}e(a(y)x)f_{1}(x+\widetilde{P}(z),y)b_{1}(y+\widetilde{P}(z))b_{2}(x+y+\widetilde{P}(z)).

Shifting

and
b_{2}
are independent of

x,y
and pigeonholing in

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in[N]}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{z\in[N^{1/d}]}e(-a(y)\widetilde{P}(z))b_{1}(y+\widetilde{P}(z))\mathclose{}\right|

is large. A variant of the PET argument from our companion paper
[
16

lets us control this expression by a Gowers norm of

, and the inverse theorem for the Gowers norms produces a nilsequence

of bounded degree, dimension, and complexity such that

(3)

\sum_{y\in[N]}\mathopen{}\left|\E_{z\in[N^{1/d}]}e(a(y)\widetilde{P}(z))\varphi(y+\widetilde{P}(z))\mathclose{}\right|

We use an iterative process, itself a microcosm of degree-lowering, to show that the nilsequence in (
3

for some polynomial

is linear. Another application of Weyl’s inequality shows that there are many values of

is major-arc, i.e., close to a rational of small height; pigeonholing in these rational shifts finally gives that

Our degree-lowering arguments must be modified in several ways to account for the parameter

More importantly, the miniature PET induction in the lead-up to (

We conclude with a short discussion of the shape of bounds in Theorem

3.1.
Basic notation

We write
{\mathbb{N}},{\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}
for the sets of positive integers, integers, real numbers, and complex numbers (respectively). For reals
a<b
, we set
[a,b]:=\{\left\lceil a\right\rceil,a+1,\ldots,\left\lfloor b\right\rfloor\}
, and we abbreviate
[0,N-1]
as
[N]
and
[-N+1,N-1]
as
[\pm N]
. We remark that our somewhat unusual convention for
[N]
(the norm would be
[N]=[1,N]
) behaves well under passing to arithmetic progressions: For
q|N
, we have the partition
[N]=\bigcup_{j\in[q]}(q\cdot[N/q]+j)
.

We write
{\textbf{x}}=(x_{1},...,x_{D})
for an element of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
(for fixed
D\in{\mathbb{N}}
) and
x
for an element of
{\mathbb{Z}}
. When the context is clear, we often write a sum over
{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
or
{\mathbb{Z}}
as
\sum_{{\textbf{x}}}
or
\sum_{x}
instead of
\sum_{{\textbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}}
or
\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
. We also write
{\underline{h}}=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{s})
for a tuple of
s
integers, and we denote its
\ell^{1}
-norm by
|{\underline{h}}|:=|h_{1}|+\cdots+|h_{s}|
. We will often combine these two pieces of notation; we use
{\underline{h}}{\textbf{v}}
to indicate the tuple
(h_{1}{\textbf{v}},\ldots,h_{s}{\textbf{v}})
.

We denote the indicator function of a set
X
by
1_{X}
. If
X
is finite and nonempty, we let
\E_{x\in X}:=\frac{1}{|X|}\sum_{x\in X}
be the average over
X
.

We call a function
f:X\to{\mathbb{C}}

1
-bounded
if
\sup\limits_{x\in X}|f(x)|\leq 1
.

We use the following standard asymptotic notation. Let
f,g:X\to{\mathbb{C}}
, with
g
taking positive real values. We write
f=O(g)
,
f\ll g
,
g\gg f
, or
g=\Omega(f)
if there exists
C>0
such that
|f(x)|\leq Cg(x)
for all
x\in X
, and we write
f\asymp g
or
f=\Theta(g)
if
f\ll g
and
f\gg g
. If the constant
C
depends on a parameter, we record this dependence with a subscript, e.g.,
f=O_{P}(g)
,
f\ll_{P}g
.

We let
{\mathcal{C}}:{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
denote the complex conjugation operator
{\mathcal{C}}z=\overline{z}
.

3.2.
W
-trick notation

Throughout the paper, we let
w=w(N)>10
be a (fixed) slowly-growing function of
N
; we will later take
w(N)=\exp((\log\log\log\log N)^{C_{P}})
for some
C_{P}>0
. Let

W=W(N):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p<w\\
p\text{ prime}\end{subarray}}p

be the product of the primes up to
w
. Standard results on the growth rate of Chebyshev’s function give
\log W\asymp w
.

It will often be useful to restrict functions to arithmetic progressions, which we capture in the following definition.

Definition 3.1

(
V
-tricked function)
.

For
V\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
, we can parameterize the arithmetic progression
V\mathbb{Z}+r\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
by
\{Vx+r:x\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. If
f
is a function on
\mathbb{Z}
, then its restriction to
V\mathbb{Z}+r
, with this parametrization, is

f_{(r,V)}(x):=f(Vx+r).

Likewise, for
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
f
a function on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the restriction of
f
to
(V\mathbb{Z}+r_{1})\times(V\mathbb{Z}+r_{2})
can be parameterized as

f_{(r_{1},r_{2},V)}(x,y):=f(Vx+r_{1},Vy+r_{2}).

We refer to the operation of passing to such a subprogression as a
V
-trick
, and we say that the resulting functions are
V
-tricked
.

In this paper, we will usually take
V
to be a small (nonnegative integer) power of
W
and
r,r_{1},r_{2}
to be elements of
[V]
. We remark that a composition of two such
V
-tricks is itself a
V
-trick. To see this symbolically for
f:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
, one can calculate that

\displaystyle(f_{(r_{1},V_{1})})_{(r_{2},V_{2})}(x)

\displaystyle=f_{(r_{1},V_{1})}(V_{2}x+r_{2})

\displaystyle=f(V_{1}(V_{2}x+r_{2})+r_{1})

\displaystyle=f_{(V_{1}r_{2}+r_{1},V_{1}V_{2})}(x),

where
V_{1}V_{2}
is a power of
W
if
V_{1},V_{2}
are. The case of functions on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is analogous.

Definition 3.2

(
V
-tricked polynomial)
.

For
V
-tricks of polynomials, we will use slightly different notation that will turn out to be helpful for keeping track of divisibility properties of various coefficients. Let
Q\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
be a polynomial. For
V\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, define the
V
-tricked
polynomial of
Q
to be

Q_{[r,V]}(z):=\frac{Q(Vz+r)-Q(r)}{V}\in\mathbb{Z}[z].

That
Q_{[r,V]}
has integer coefficients can be easily verified using the Binomial Theorem.

As in the case of
V
-tricks for functions, the composition of two
V
-tricks for a polynomial is again a
V
-trick. In particular, we have

\displaystyle(Q_{[r_{1},V_{1}]})_{[r_{2},V_{2}]}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{Q_{[r_{1},V_{1}]}(V_{2}z+r_{2})-Q_{[r_{1},V_{1}]}(r_{2})}{V_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{Q(V_{1}(V_{2}z+r_{2})+r_{1})-Q(r_{1})-Q(V_{1}r_{2}+r_{1})+Q(r_{1})}{V_{1}V_{2}}

\displaystyle=Q_{[V_{1}r_{2}+r_{1},V_{1}V_{2}]}(z).

Note that iterated
V
-tricks (in both the function setting and the polynomial setting) do not in general commute.

3.3.
Van der Corput Inequalities

If
E\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
is finite and nonempty, we define the
Fejér kernel

\displaystyle\mu_{E}({\mathbf{h}}):=\E_{{\mathbf{h}}_{1},{\mathbf{h}}_{2}\in E}1_{{\mathbf{h}}_{1}-{\mathbf{h}}_{2}}({\mathbf{h}}).

Note that
\sum_{\mathbf{h}}\mu_{E}({\mathbf{h}})=1
and that
0\leq\mu_{E}({\mathbf{h}})\leq\frac{1_{E-E}({\mathbf{h}})}{|E|}
pointwise. If
E=[\pm H]
, we set

\displaystyle\mu_{H}(h):=\mu_{[\pm H]}(h)=\E_{h_{1},h_{2}\in[\pm H]}1_{h_{1}-h_{2}}(h)=\frac{1}{2H-1}\mathopen{}\left(1-\frac{|h|}{2H-1}\mathclose{}\right)_{+}.

This definition differs slightly from those in
[
30
,
31
,
32
,
35
]
in that we take the ranges of
h_{1},h_{2}
to be
[\pm H]
rather than
[H]
. Making the range of
h_{1},h_{2}
symmetric around the origin will be convenient for us later.

We will use the following form of the
standard van der Corput inequality. For a more thorough discussion, see
[
16
, Section 3]
(which follows from, e.g.,
[
35
, Lemma 3.2]
).

Lemma 3.3

(The van der Corput inequality)
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1]
, and let
H,N\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
H\leq\frac{\delta^{2}}{4}N
. If
f:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is a
1
-bounded function satisfying

(4)

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|\E_{x\in[N]}f(x)\mathclose{}\right|\geq\delta,

then

\displaystyle\E_{h,h^{\prime}\in[\pm H]}\E_{x\in[N]}f(x+h)\overline{f(x+h^{\prime})}\geq\frac{\delta^{2}}{4}

and

\displaystyle{\sum_{h}\mu_{H}(h)\E_{x\in[N]}f(x)\overline{f(x+h)}}\geq\frac{\delta^{2}}{4}.

3.4.
Box norms

We now briefly review the theory of box norms on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
; we will later work with the groups
{\mathbb{Z}}
and
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
.
Given
{\mathbf{h}},{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
and a finitely supported function
f:{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, we define
the (multiplicative) discrete derivatives

\Delta_{\mathbf{h}}f({\textbf{x}}):=f({\textbf{x}})\overline{f({\textbf{x}}+{\mathbf{h}})}
and
\Delta^{\prime}_{({\mathbf{h}},{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime})}f({\textbf{x}}):=f({\textbf{x}}+{\mathbf{h}})\overline{f({\textbf{x}}+{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime})}
. Similarly, compositions of such discrete derivatives are given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{{\mathbf{h}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{h}}_{s}}f({\textbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\Delta_{{\mathbf{h}}_{1}}\cdots\Delta_{{\mathbf{h}}_{s}}f({\textbf{x}})=\prod_{{\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|{\underline{\epsilon}}|}f({\textbf{x}}+{\underline{\epsilon}}\cdot{\underline{\textbf{h}}}),

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime}_{({\mathbf{h}}_{1},{\mathbf{h}}_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,\;({\mathbf{h}}_{s},{\mathbf{h}}_{s}^{\prime})}f({\textbf{x}})

\displaystyle=\Delta^{\prime}_{({\mathbf{h}}_{1},{\mathbf{h}}_{1}^{\prime})}\cdots\Delta^{\prime}_{({\mathbf{h}}_{s},{\mathbf{h}}_{s}^{\prime})}f({\textbf{x}})=\prod_{{\underline{\epsilon}}\in\{0,1\}^{s}}{\mathcal{C}}^{|{\underline{\epsilon}}|}f({\textbf{x}}+(\underline{1}-{\underline{\epsilon}})\cdot{\underline{\textbf{h}}}+{\underline{\epsilon}}\cdot{\underline{\textbf{h}}}^{\prime}),

where
{\underline{\textbf{h}}}=({\mathbf{h}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{h}}_{s})
and again
{\mathcal{C}}z=\overline{z}
for
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

For finite sets
E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
, we define the
box norm
of
f
along
E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}
to be

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}}:=\mathopen{}\left(\E_{{\mathbf{h}}_{1},{\mathbf{h}}_{1}^{\prime}\in E_{1}}\cdots\E_{{\mathbf{h}}_{s},{\mathbf{h}}_{s}^{\prime}\in E_{s}}\sum_{\textbf{x}}\Delta^{\prime}_{({\mathbf{h}}_{1},{\mathbf{h}}_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,\;({\mathbf{h}}_{s},{\mathbf{h}}_{s}^{\prime})}f({\textbf{x}})\mathclose{}\right)^{1/2^{s}}.

We can equivalently express this as

(5)

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}}=\mathopen{}\left(\sum_{{\textbf{x}},{\mathbf{h}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{h}}_{s}}\mu_{E_{1}}({\mathbf{h}}_{1})\cdots\mu_{E_{s}}({\mathbf{h}}_{s})\Delta_{{\mathbf{h}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{h}}_{s}}f({\textbf{x}})\mathclose{}\right)^{1/2^{s}}

after shifting
{\textbf{x}}\mapsto{\textbf{x}}-{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime}_{1}-\cdots-{\mathbf{h}}_{s}^{\prime}
and removing the extraneous averages over
{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime}_{s}
.
For the sake of completeness, we also define
\left\|f\right\|_{\emptyset}:=\sum_{\textbf{x}}f({\textbf{x}})
(but note that this is not a seminorm). If a set
E
repeats
t
times, we often write
E^{t}
instead of
E,\ldots,E
. We also write
\left\|f\right\|_{U^{s}(E)}:=\left\|f\right\|_{E^{s}}
for the degree-
s

Gowers norm
of
f
along
E
.
Box norms satisfy a number of well known properties, listed in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4

(Properties of box norms,
[
16
, Lemma 3.5]
)
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
, let
d,D,N,s\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
and let
E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
be finite and nonempty sets. Let
f:{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a
1
-bounded function supported on
[N]^{D}
. Then the following properties hold:

(i)

(Inductive formula) For any
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}}^{2^{s}}=\E_{{\mathbf{h}}_{1},{\mathbf{h}}_{1}^{\prime}\in E_{1}}\cdots\E_{{\mathbf{h}}_{i},{\mathbf{h}}_{i}^{\prime}\in E_{i}}\left\|\Delta^{\prime}_{({\mathbf{h}}_{1},{\mathbf{h}}_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,\;({\mathbf{h}}_{i},{\mathbf{h}}_{i}^{\prime})}f\right\|_{E_{i+1},\ldots,E_{s}}^{2^{s-i}}.

(ii)

(Permutation invariance) For any permutation
\sigma
on
\{1,\ldots,s\}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}}=\left\|f\right\|_{E_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,E_{\sigma(s)}}.

(iii)

(Monotonicity) If
f
is
1
-bounded and
|[N]^{D}-E_{s}|\leq CN^{D}
for some
C>0
, then

(6)

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{E_{1},\ldots,E_{s-1}}^{2^{s-1}}\geq\delta N^{D}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\left\|f\right\|_{E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}}^{2^{s}}\geq\delta^{2}N^{D}/C.

(iv)

(Enlarging the sets) If
s\geq 2
and
E^{\prime}_{i}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
is a finite set satisfying
E^{\prime}_{i}\supseteq E_{i}
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
, then

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}}\leq\mathopen{}\left(\frac{|E_{1}^{\prime}|\cdots|E_{s}^{\prime}|}{|E_{1}|\cdots|E_{s}|}\mathclose{}\right)^{1/2^{s-1}}\left\|f\right\|_{E_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,E_{s}^{\prime}}.

(v)

(Trimming the lengths of boxes)
Let

\displaystyle E_{i}={\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{i}\cdot[\pm H_{i}]\quad\textrm{and}\quad E_{i}^{\prime}={\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{i}\cdot[\pm\kappa_{i}H_{i}]

for some
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
,
\kappa_{i}>0
, and
H_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. If
f
is
1
-bounded, then

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}}^{2^{s}}\geq\delta N^{D}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\left\|f\right\|_{E_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,E_{s}^{\prime}}^{2^{s}}\gg_{s}\delta^{2^{s}}\min(1,\delta^{2^{s}}/\kappa)^{2s}N^{D}

for
\kappa=\max_{i}\kappa_{i}.

(vi)

(Passing to sub-arithmetic progressions) Suppose that
f
is
1
-bounded and

\displaystyle E_{i}={\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{i}\cdot[\pm H_{i}]\quad\textrm{and}\quad E_{i}^{\prime}=q_{i}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{i}\cdot[\pm H_{i}/q_{i}]

for some
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
and
q_{i},H_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}
satisfying
q_{i}\leq C\delta^{2^{s}}H_{i}
for all
i
and some constant
C>0
.
Then

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}}^{2^{s}}\geq\delta N^{D}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\left\|f\right\|_{E_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,E_{s}^{\prime}}^{2^{s}}\gg_{s,C}\delta N^{D}.

3.5.
Dual-difference interchange

On several occasions, we apply a standard dual-difference interchange trick which has been a linchpin in all previous degree-lowering arguments. Its proof rests on the following observation.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
D,t\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
C>0
, let
E\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
be finite and nonempty,
and for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
, let
F_{i}({\textbf{x}})=\E_{z_{i}\in Z_{i}}f_{i}({\textbf{x}},z_{i})
be a function from
{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
to
{\mathbb{C}}
supported on a set of size at most
CN^{D}
, where
Z_{i}
is a nonempty finite set and
f_{i}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}\times Z_{i}\to\mathbb{C}
is
1
-bounded.
If

\mathopen{}\left|\sum_{{\textbf{x}},{\mathbf{h}}}\mu_{E}({\mathbf{h}})\Delta_{({\mathbf{h}},0)}\prod_{i=1}^{t}\E_{z_{i}\in Z_{i}}f_{i}({\textbf{x}},z_{i})\mathclose{}\right|\geq\delta N^{D},

then

{\sum_{{\textbf{x}},{\mathbf{h}}}\mu_{E}({\mathbf{h}})\prod_{i=1}^{t}\E_{z_{i}\in Z_{i}}\Delta_{({\mathbf{h}},0)}f_{i}({\textbf{x}},z_{i})}\geq(\delta^{2}/C)N^{D}.

Proof.

Expanding and applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

\displaystyle\delta^{2}N^{2D}

\displaystyle\leq\mathopen{}\left|\sum_{\textbf{x}}\E_{{\mathbf{h}}\in E}\prod_{i=1}^{t}\E_{z_{i}\in Z_{i}}f_{i}({\textbf{x}}+{\mathbf{h}},z_{i})\cdot\E_{{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime}\in E}\prod_{i=1}^{t}\E_{z^{\prime}_{i}\in Z_{i}}f_{i}({\textbf{x}}+{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime},z^{\prime}_{i})\mathclose{}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq CN^{D}\cdot\sum_{\textbf{x}}\E_{z_{1}\in Z_{1},\ldots,z_{t}\in Z_{t}}\Bigl|\E_{{\mathbf{h}}\in E}\prod_{i=1}^{t}f_{i}({\textbf{x}}+{\mathbf{h}},z_{i})\Bigr|^{2}

\displaystyle=CN^{D}\cdot\sum_{{\textbf{x}},{\mathbf{h}}}\mu_{E}({\mathbf{h}})\prod_{i=1}^{t}\E_{z_{i}\in Z_{i}}\Delta_{({\mathbf{h}},0)}f_{i}({\textbf{x}},z_{i}),

as desired.
∎

Iterating Lemma
3.5
, we obtain the following.

Lemma 3.6

(Dual–difference interchange)
.

Let
C>0
, let
D,r,s\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
1\leq r\leq s
,
let
E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}\subseteq[\pm CN^{D}]
be nonempty sets,
and let
F({\textbf{x}})=\E_{z\in Z}f({\textbf{x}},z)
be a function from
{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
to
{\mathbb{C}}
,
where
Z
is a nonempty finite set and
f:{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}\times Z\to\mathbb{C}
is a
1
-bounded function supported on
[\pm CN^{D}]\times Z
. If

\displaystyle\left\|F\right\|_{E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}}^{2^{s}}\geq\delta N^{D},

then

\displaystyle\sum_{{\mathbf{h}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{h}}_{r}}\mu_{E_{1}}({\mathbf{h}}_{1})\cdots\mu_{E_{r}}({\mathbf{h}}_{r})\left\|F^{{\mathbf{h}}_{1},\ldots{\mathbf{h}}_{r}}\right\|^{2^{s-r}}_{E_{r+1},\ldots,E_{s}}\gg_{C,r}\delta^{2^{r}}N^{D},

where

\displaystyle F^{{\mathbf{h}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{h}}_{r}}({\textbf{x}}):=\E_{z\in Z}\Delta_{({\mathbf{h}}_{1},0),\ldots,\;({\mathbf{h}}_{r},0)}f({\textbf{x}},z).

Proof.

Expanding the definition of the box norm, we get that

\displaystyle\sum_{{\mathbf{h}}_{2},\ldots,{\mathbf{h}}_{s}}\mu_{E_{2}}({\mathbf{h}}_{2})\cdots\mu_{E_{s}}({\mathbf{h}}_{s})\cdot\sum_{{\textbf{x}},{\mathbf{h}}_{1}}\mu_{E_{1}}({\mathbf{h}}_{1})\Delta_{({\mathbf{h}}_{1},0),\ldots,\;({\mathbf{h}}_{s},0)}\E_{z\in Z}f({\textbf{x}},z)\geq\delta N^{D}.

We apply Lemma
3.5
for a fixed tuple
({\mathbf{h}}_{2},\ldots,{\mathbf{h}}_{s})\in E_{2}\times E_{s}
with the role of the product over
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
played by the iterated multiplicative derivative
\Delta_{({\mathbf{h}}_{2},0),\ldots,\;({\mathbf{h}}_{s},0)}
. This lets us move the differencing over
{\mathbf{h}}_{1}
inside the expectation, which gives

\displaystyle\sum_{{\mathbf{h}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{h}}_{s}}\mu_{E_{1}}({\mathbf{h}}_{1})\cdots\mu_{E_{s}}({\mathbf{h}}_{s})\cdot\sum_{{\textbf{x}}}\Delta_{({\mathbf{h}}_{2},0),\ldots,\;({\mathbf{h}}_{s},0)}\E_{z\in Z}\Delta_{({\mathbf{h}}_{1},0)}f({\textbf{x}},z)\gg_{C}\delta^{2}N^{D}.

We continue in the same fashion to move the differencings over
{\mathbf{h}}_{2},\ldots,{\mathbf{h}}_{r}
inside the expectation; here we are implicitly using the fact that the functions
\Delta_{({\mathbf{h}}_{1},0),\ldots,\;({\mathbf{h}}_{j},0)}f
for
{\mathbf{h}}_{i}\in E_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq j\leq r
are supported on
[\pm CN^{D}]\times Z
.
∎

3.6.
Fourier analysis

We conclude the preliminaries section with a brief summary of Fourier analysis on
{\mathbb{Z}}
, which we shall use on occasion. For a finitely supported function
f:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
and
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
, we define the
Fourier transform

\hat{f}:{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
via

\displaystyle\hat{f}(\xi):=\sum_{x}f(x)e(\xi x).

The Fourier inversion formula gives
f(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}\hat{f}(\xi)e(-\xi x)\,d\xi
, and the Parseval Identity gives
\left\|f\right\|_{2}=\left\|\hat{f}\right\|_{2}
. Here the
L^{p}
-norm of a function
f:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is defined by
\left\|f\right\|_{p}:=\Bigl(\sum_{x}|f(x)|^{p}\Bigr)^{1/p}
for
1\leq p<\infty
and
\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f(x)
, the
L^{p}
-norm of a (Lebesgue-measurable) function
F:{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is defined by
\left\|F\right\|_{p}:=\Bigl(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}|F(\xi)|^{p}\,d\xi\Bigr)^{1/p}
for
1\leq p<\infty
and
\left\|F\right\|_{\infty}:=\sup\{r\geq 0:\;\mathrm{Leb}(|F|^{-1}(r,\infty))>0\}
.

4.
Degree-lowering

For the remainder of the paper, we fix the integer polynomial
P
together with its degree
d
and the auxiliary
W
-tricked polynomial
\widetilde{P}
defined in (
1
). Recall that for
1
-bounded functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
supported on
[N]^{2}
, we have the counting operator

\Lambda^{W}(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2})=\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}}\E_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}]}f_{0}(x,y)f_{1}(x+\widetilde{P}(z),y)f_{2}(x,y+\widetilde{P}(z)),

where
W_{d}
is the leading coefficient of
\widetilde{P}
. Recall also that
{\mathbf{e}}_{1}:=(1,0)
and
{\mathbf{e}}_{2}:=(0,1)
denote the usual basis vectors in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. As described in the proof outline above, the main work of this paper is showing that
\Lambda^{W}
is “controlled” by appropriate degree-
2
box norms of its arguments.

Theorem 4.1

(Inverse theorem for
\Lambda^{W}
)
.

There is a constant
C=C_{P}>0
such that the following holds. Let
\delta\in(0,1/10)
, and let
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be
1
-bounded functions supported on
[N]^{2}
for some integer
N\geq CW^{C}\exp(\log(1/\delta)^{C})
. If

|\Lambda^{W}(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2})|\geq\delta N^{2},

then
there exists some positive integer power
V=W^{O_{P}(1)}
such that

\displaystyle\|f_{0}\|_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}V\cdot[N/V],\;{\mathbf{e}}_{2}V\cdot[N/V]}^{4}\geq\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{O_{P}(1)})N^{2},

\displaystyle\|f_{1}\|_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}V\cdot[N/V],\;({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1})V\cdot[N/V]}^{4}\geq\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{O_{P}(1)})N^{2},

and

\displaystyle\|f_{2}\|_{{\mathbf{e}}_{2}V\cdot[N/V],\;({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1})V\cdot[N/V]}^{4}\geq\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{O_{P}(1)})N^{2}.

Theorem
4.1
is a
W
-tricked version of Theorem
1.2
from the introduction. While we prove only the former, the latter follows from a simplified version of the proof of Theorem
4.1
in which we take
W=V=1
in all places and replace
\widetilde{P}
with
P
. We remark that while Theorem
4.1
has the same restriction on
P
as Theorem
1.1
, no such restriction is present in the statement of Theorem
1.2
. This is because the fraction-comparison argument Lemma
C.3
, whose proof in the
W
-tricked setting uses the special structure of
{P}
, holds trivially for all non-
W
-tricked polynomials.

To prove Theorem
4.1
, we will use a degree-lowering argument. The starting point for the argument is the following special case of
[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

There exist a constant
C=C_{d}>0
and a positive integer
t=O_{d}(1)
such that for all
\delta\in(0,1/10)
and
1
-bounded functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
supported on
[N]^{2}
for some integer
N\geq CW_{d}\delta^{-C}
, the bound

|\Lambda^{W}(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2})|\geq\delta N^{2}

implies that

\displaystyle\|f_{0}\|_{({\mathbf{e}}_{1}W_{d}\cdot[\pm N/W_{d}])^{t},\;({\mathbf{e}}_{2}W_{d}\cdot[\pm N/W_{d}])^{t}}^{2^{2t}}\gg_{d}\delta^{O_{d}(1)}N^{2}

\displaystyle\|f_{1}\|_{({\mathbf{e}}_{1}W_{d}\cdot[\pm N/W_{d}])^{t},\;(({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1})W_{d}\cdot[\pm N/W_{d}])^{t}}^{2^{2t}}\gg_{d}\delta^{O_{d}(1)}N^{2}

and

\displaystyle\|f_{2}\|_{({\mathbf{e}}_{2}W_{d}\cdot[\pm N/W_{d}])^{t},\;(({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1})W_{d}\cdot[\pm N/W_{d}])^{t}}^{2^{2t}}\gg_{d}\delta^{O_{d}(1)}N^{2}.

Our goal in the next few sections is to reduce
t
to
2
in the box norms appearing in Proposition

. The following proposition captures the main iterative step of this process.

Proposition 4.3

(Degree-lowering)
.

Let
k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
with
\max(k,\ell)\geq 2
. For every
C_{1}>0
there exists
C_{2}=C_{2}(C_{1},P,k,\ell)>0
such that the following holds. Let
V\leq W^{C_{1}}
be a nonnegative integer power of
W
, and suppose that for all
\delta\in(0,1/10)
, all positive integers
N\geq C_{2}W^{C_{2}}\exp(\log(1/\delta)^{C_{2}})
, and all
1
-bounded functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
supported on
[N]^{2}
, the lower bound

|\Lambda^{W}(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2})|\geq\delta N^{2}

implies that

\|f_{0}\|_{({\mathbf{e}}_{1}V\cdot[\pm N/V])^{k},\;({\mathbf{e}}_{2}V\cdot[\pm N/V])^{\ell}}^{2^{k+\ell}}\geq\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{C_{1}})N^{2}.

Then
there exists some positive integer power
\tilde{V}=V^{O_{C_{1},P,k,\ell}(1)}
such that

\|f_{0}\|_{({\mathbf{e}}_{1}\tilde{V}\cdot[N/\tilde{V}])^{k-1},\;({\mathbf{e}}_{2}\tilde{V}\cdot[\pm N/\tilde{V}])^{\ell}}^{2^{k+\ell-1}}\geq\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{O_{C_{1},P,k,\ell}(1)})N^{2}

if
k\geq 2
and

\|f_{0}\|_{({\mathbf{e}}_{1}\tilde{V}\cdot[N/\tilde{V}])^{k},\;({\mathbf{e}}_{2}\tilde{V}\cdot[\pm N/\tilde{V}])^{\ell-1}}^{2^{k+\ell-1}}\geq\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{O_{C_{1},P,k,\ell}(1)})N^{2}

if
\ell\geq 2
.

Before proving Proposition
4.3
, we show how it implies Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
assuming Propositions
4.2
and
4.3
.

By Proposition
4.2
, there exists a positive integer
t=O_{d}(1)
such that

\|f_{0}\|_{({\mathbf{e}}_{1}W_{d}\cdot[\pm N/W_{d}])^{t},\;({\mathbf{e}}_{2}W_{d}\cdot[\pm N/W_{d}])^{t}}^{2^{2t}}\gg_{d}\delta^{O_{d}(1)}N^{2}.

Using Lemma
3.4
(
iv
), we can replace
W_{d}
by
W^{d-1}
at the cost of a factor of
O_{P}(1)
in the lower bound.
We now apply Proposition
4.3

2t-2
times. Since
N
is chosen sufficiently large, we end up with a positive integer power
V=W^{O_{d}(1)}
such that

\|f_{0}\|_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}V\cdot[\pm N/V],\;{\mathbf{e}}_{2}V\cdot[\pm N/V]}^{4}\geq\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{O_{P}(1)})N^{2}.

This gives the claimed control in terms of
f_{0}
.

The control in terms of
f_{1},f_{2}
follows by symmetry. To see this, notice that the affine linear map
(x,y)\mapsto(2N-1-(x+y),y)
sends
[N]^{2}
into
[2N]^{2}
and preserves the set of corners with each given side length, with the caveat that the two points on the horizontal side of each corner have been “exchanged”. More precisely, for each

\widetilde{f}_{i}:[2N]^{2}\to{\mathbb{C}}
by
\widetilde{f}_{i}(x,y):=f_{i}(2N-1-(x+y),y)
. Then the change of variables
x\mapsto 2N-1-x-y-\widetilde{P}(z)
gives the identity

\Lambda^{W}(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2})=\Lambda^{W}(\widetilde{f_{1}},\widetilde{f_{0}},\widetilde{f_{2}}).

The previous paragraph (with
N,\delta
replaced by
2N,\delta/4
) tells us that

\|\widetilde{f}_{1}\|_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}V\cdot[\pm 2N/V],\;{\mathbf{e}}_{2}V\cdot[\pm 2N/V]}^{4}\geq\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{O_{P}(1)})N^{2}.

Using Lemma
3.4
(
v
) to replace
[\pm 2N/V]
by
[\pm N/V]
and unwinding the definition of
\widetilde{f}_{1}
, we conclude that

\|f_{1}\|_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}V\cdot[N/V],\;({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1})V\cdot[N/V]}^{4}\geq\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{O_{P}(1)})N^{2}.\\

The control for
f_{2}
follows from an analogous change of variables.
∎

4.1.
Proof of Proposition
4.3
: preliminary maneuvers

We now begin the proof of Proposition
4.3
. All implicit constants are allowed to depend on
P,k,l
.
The initial assumption

\displaystyle|\Lambda^{W}(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2})|\geq\delta N^{2}

implies that

(7)

\displaystyle\|f_{0}\|_{({\mathbf{e}}_{1}V\cdot[\pm N/V])^{k},\;({\mathbf{e}}_{2}V\cdot[\pm N/V])^{\ell}}^{2^{k+\ell}}\geq\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{O(1)})N^{2}

for all
1
-bounded functions
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{C}}
supported on
[N]^{2}
. Our proof strategy is as follows. Applying several standard maneuvers such as stashing, the dual-difference interchange and an inverse theorem for the
U^{2}\times U^{1}
box norm, we reduce the problem to the study of the auxiliary operator

\Lambda^{\prime}(a,\widetilde{f_{1}},\widetilde{f_{2}}):=\sum_{x,y}\E_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]}e(a(y)x)\widetilde{f_{1}}(x+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z),y)\widetilde{f_{2}}(x,y+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z)).

The phase
e(a(y)x)
is the “main term” that arises from the
U^{2}\times U^{1}
inverse theorem.
Our main technical advancement is a structural result on
\Lambda^{\prime}
: We show that if
\Lambda^{\prime}(a,\widetilde{f_{1}},\widetilde{f_{2}})
is large, then
a
is “approximately constant” in the sense that there are many arithmetic progressions of difference
V^{\prime}=V^{O(1)}
on each of which
a
is constant a positive proportion of the time. Morally speaking, this means that
\Lambda^{\prime}(a,\widetilde{f_{1}},\widetilde{f_{2}})
can be large only when
e(a(y)x)
is actually a
U^{1}\times U^{1}
obstruction. We use this information to obtain the auxiliary norm control

\displaystyle\|f_{1}\|^{4}_{({\mathbf{e}}_{1}VV^{\prime}\cdot[\pm N/\tilde{V}])^{k-1},\;({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1})\tilde{V}\cdot[\pm N/\tilde{V}],\;({\mathbf{e}}_{2}\tilde{V}\cdot[\pm N/\tilde{V}])^{\ell-1}}\geq\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{O(1)})N^{2},

where
\tilde{V}=VV^{\prime}
. We then transfer this norm-control from
f_{1}
to
f_{0}
.

Let us fill in some more details of this strategy. In doing so, we will also highlight several techniques used in various guises throughout the paper.

We define the
dual function

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{0}(f_{1},f_{2})(x,y):=\E_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}]}f_{1}(x+\widetilde{P}(z),y)f_{2}(x,y+\widetilde{P}(z))

with respect to the first argument of
\Lambda^{W}
. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

\displaystyle|\Lambda^{W}(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\left|\sum_{x,y}f_{0}(x,y){\mathcal{D}}_{0}(f_{1},f_{2})(x,y)\mathclose{}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq N^{2}\cdot\sum_{x,y}|{\mathcal{D}}_{0}(f_{1},f_{2})(x,y)|^{2}

\displaystyle=N^{2}\cdot\Lambda^{W}({\mathcal{D}}_{0}(f_{1},f_{2}),\overline{f_{1}},\overline{f_{2}}),

for all
1
-bounded functions
f_{0}
supported on
[N]^{2}
. It follows that if
\Lambda^{W}(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2})\geq\delta N^{2}
, then we also have

\displaystyle\Lambda^{W}({\mathcal{D}}_{0}(f_{1},f_{2}),\overline{f_{1}},\overline{f_{2}})\geq\delta^{2}N^{2}.

This trick whereby we replace an arbitrary function
f_{0}
by the structured function
{\mathcal{D}}_{0}(f_{1},f_{2})
is often called
stashing
, and it will be the bread and butter of our arguments.
In particular, once we know that
\Lambda^{W}
is controlled by some norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|
of its first argument, we see that the largeness of
\Lambda^{W}(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2})
implies not only the largeness of
\left\|f_{0}\right\|
but also the largeness of
\left\|{\mathcal{D}}_{0}(f_{1},f_{2})\right\|
.
In this particular case, stashing and (
7
) together give

(8)

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{D}}_{0}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{({\mathbf{e}}_{1}V\cdot[\pm N/V])^{k},\;({\mathbf{e}}_{2}V\cdot[\pm N/V])^{\ell}}^{2^{k+\ell}}\geq\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{O(1)})N^{2}.

The next step is to apply the dual–difference interchange to (
8
) in order to arrive at an average of
U^{2}\times U^{1}
-box norms to which we can apply an inverse theorem. To begin, applying Lemma
3.6
to (
8
) gives

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\underline{h}}_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k-2},\\
{\underline{h}}_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell-1}\end{subarray}}\mu_{N/V}({\underline{h}}_{1},{\underline{h}}_{2})\|\mathcal{D}_{0}^{{\underline{h}}_{1},{\underline{h}}_{2}}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{({\mathbf{e}}_{1}V\cdot[\pm N/V])^{2},\;{\mathbf{e}}_{2}V\cdot[\pm N/V]}^{8}\geq\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{O(1)})N^{2},

where

\mathcal{D}_{0}^{{\underline{h}}_{1},{\underline{h}}_{2}}(f_{1},f_{2})(x,y):=\E_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}]}\Delta_{{\underline{h}}_{1}V{\mathbf{e}}_{1},{\underline{h}}_{2}V{\mathbf{e}}_{2}}(f_{1}(x+\widetilde{P}(z),y)f_{2}(x,y+\widetilde{P}(z)))

for
{\underline{h}}_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k-2}
and
{\underline{h}}_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell-1}
. (We recall here that
{\underline{h}}_{1}V{\mathbf{e}}_{1}=(h_{11}V{\mathbf{e}}_{1},\ldots,h_{1(k-2)}V{\mathbf{e}}_{1})
, and similarly for
{\underline{h}}_{2}V{\mathbf{e}}_{2}
.)
Since
\mu_{N/V}({\underline{h}}_{1},{\underline{h}}_{2})\ll(N/V)^{-k-\ell+3}
pointwise, the popularity principle provides at least
\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{O(1)})(N/V)^{k+\ell-3}
choices of
({\underline{h}}_{1},{\underline{h}}_{2})
such that

(9)

\|\mathcal{D}_{0}^{{\underline{h}}_{1},{\underline{h}}_{2}}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{({\mathbf{e}}_{1}V\cdot[\pm N/V])^{2},\;{\mathbf{e}}_{2}V\cdot[\pm N/V]}^{8}\geq\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{O(1)})N^{2}.

For the next several steps, fix such a choice of
{\underline{h}}_{1},{\underline{h}}_{2}
. (The reader can now see why all of the main ideas appear in the case
k=2
,
\ell=1
described in the proof sketch.)

The box norm in (
9
) is not yet ripe for an application of an inverse theorem for the
U^{2}\times U^{1}
box norms because of the multiplicative factor
V
in the boxes
{\mathbf{e}}_{1}V\cdot[\pm N/V]
and
{\mathbf{e}}_{2}V\cdot[\pm N/V]
. To bring the box norm into a form amenable to an application of the inverse theorem, we split the ranges of
x,y
into arithmetic progressions of common difference
V
and observe the identity

(10)

\displaystyle\left\|g\right\|_{({\mathbf{e}}_{1}V\cdot[\pm N/V])^{2},\;{\mathbf{e}}_{2}V\cdot[\pm N/V]}^{8}=\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}\in[V]}\left\|g_{(j_{1},j_{2},V)}\right\|_{({\mathbf{e}}_{1}\cdot[\pm N/V])^{2},\;{\mathbf{e}}_{2}\cdot[\pm N/V]}^{8}.

for any compactly supported
g:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
; we recall the
V
-tricked function
g_{(j_{1},j_{2},V)}(x,y)=g(Vx+j_{1},Vy+j_{2})
from Definition

. Notice that after this change of variables, the boxes in the box norm have side length

instead of

.

We will of course apply the identity (
10
) to the functions
\mathcal{D}^{{\underline{h}}_{1},{\underline{h}}_{2}}_{0}(f_{1},f_{2})
.
This maneuver requires us to adapt the range of
z
accordingly: Recalling the definition of the
V
-trick for polynomials, we replace
\widetilde{P}(z)
by
\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z)=\frac{\widetilde{P}(Vz+r)-\widetilde{P}(r)}{V}
.
On changing variables

, we can express the dual function as

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{0}^{{\underline{h}}_{1},{\underline{h}}_{2}}(f_{1},f_{2})(Vx+j_{1},Vy+j_{2})

\displaystyle=\E_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}]}\Delta_{V{\underline{h}}_{1}{\mathbf{e}}_{1},V{\underline{h}}_{2}{\mathbf{e}}_{2}}(f_{1}(Vx+j_{1}+\widetilde{P}(z),Vy+j_{2})f_{2}(Vx+j_{1},Vy+j_{2}+\widetilde{P}(z)))

\displaystyle=\E_{r\in[V]}\E_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]}\Delta_{V{\underline{h}}_{1}{\mathbf{e}}_{1},V{\underline{h}}_{2}{\mathbf{e}}_{2}}(f_{1}(V(x+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z))+j_{1}+\widetilde{P}(r),Vy+j_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times f_{2}(Vx+j_{1},V(y+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z))+j_{2}+\widetilde{P}(r)))

\displaystyle=\E_{r\in[V]}\E_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]}\Delta_{{\underline{h}}_{1}{\mathbf{e}}_{1},{\underline{h}}_{2}{\mathbf{e}}_{2}}(f_{1,j_{1}+\widetilde{P}(r),j_{2}}(x+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z),y)f_{2,j_{1},j_{2}+\widetilde{P}(r)}(x,y+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z))),

where we also set

f_{l,j_{1},j_{2}}(x,y):=(f_{l})_{(j_{1},j_{2},V)}(x,y)=f_{l}(Vx+j_{1},Vy+j_{2})

for brevity. We observe that these

-tricked functions are supported on an interval of length

. In the formula above (and in similar expressions in the future), we assume without loss of generality that
(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}\in{\mathbb{N}}
(as we may since

is large compared to

), so that splitting the range of

into arithmetic progressions incurs no error.

Invoking (
10
) with
g=\mathcal{D}_{0}^{{\underline{h}}_{1},{\underline{h}}_{2}}(f_{1},f_{2})
and applying the triangle inequality to (
9

\E_{j_{1},j_{2},r\in[V]}\left|\!\left|\E_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]}\Delta_{{\underline{h}}_{1}{\mathbf{e}}_{1},{\underline{h}}_{2}{\mathbf{e}}_{2}}f_{1,j_{1}+\widetilde{P}(r),j_{2}}(\cdot+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z),\cdot)\right.\right.\\
\left.\left.\Delta_{{\underline{h}}_{1}{\mathbf{e}}_{1},{\underline{h}}_{2}{\mathbf{e}}_{2}}f_{2,j_{1},j_{2}+\widetilde{P}(r)}(\cdot,\cdot+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z))\vphantom{\E_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]}}\right|\!\right|_{({\mathbf{e}}_{1}\cdot[\pm N/V])^{2},\;{\mathbf{e}}_{2}\cdot[\pm N/V]}^{8}\\
\geq\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{O(1)})(N/V)^{2}.

Since each summand contributes at most

\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{O(1)})V^{3}

\left|\!\left|\E_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]}\Delta_{{\underline{h}}_{1}{\mathbf{e}}_{1},{\underline{h}}_{2}{\mathbf{e}}_{2}}f_{1,j_{1}+\widetilde{P}(r),j_{2}}(\cdot+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z),\cdot)\right.\right.\\
\left.\left.\Delta_{{\underline{h}}_{1}{\mathbf{e}}_{1},{\underline{h}}_{2}{\mathbf{e}}_{2}}f_{2,j_{1},j_{2}+\widetilde{P}(r)}(\cdot,\cdot+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z))\vphantom{\E_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]}}\right|\!\right|_{({\mathbf{e}}_{1}\cdot[\pm N/V])^{2},\;{\mathbf{e}}_{2}\cdot[\pm N/V]}^{8}\\
\geq\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{O(1)})(N/V)^{2}.

For the next several steps, fix such a choice of
j_{1},j_{2},r
.
For each “good” choice of

from the previous step, the

-inverse theorem (Lemma

) tells us that there exist a
1
-bounded function

supported on

and phase functions

(all depending on

\sum_{x,y}\E_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]}\Delta_{{\underline{h}}_{1}{\mathbf{e}}_{1},{\underline{h}}_{2}{\mathbf{e}}_{2}}f_{1,j_{1}+\widetilde{P}(r),j_{2}}(x+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z),y)\\
\Delta_{{\underline{h}}_{1}{\mathbf{e}}_{1},{\underline{h}}_{2}{\mathbf{e}}_{2}}f_{2,j_{1},j_{2}+\widetilde{P}(r)}(x,y+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z))g(x)e(a(y)x+b(y))\geq\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{O(1)})(N/V)^{2}.

Hiding the dependence on

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{1}(x,y):=e(b(y))\Delta_{{\underline{h}}_{1}{\mathbf{e}}_{1},{\underline{h}}_{2}{\mathbf{e}}_{2}}f_{1,j_{1}+\widetilde{P}(r),j_{2}}(x,y)

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{2}(x,y):=g(x)\Delta_{{\underline{h}}_{1}{\mathbf{e}}_{1},{\underline{h}}_{2}{\mathbf{e}}_{2}}f_{2,j_{1},j_{2}+\widetilde{P}(r)}(x,y);

then the previous inequality can be rewritten as

\geq\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{O(1)})(N/V)^{2}.

4.2.
Examining the structure of
a

The main task is showing that the phase function
a
possesses some extra structure; specifically, we will show that
a(y)
is approximately constant much of the time. For future reference, define the counting operator

\Lambda^{\prime}(a,\widetilde{f_{1}},\widetilde{f_{2}}):=\sum_{x,y}\E_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]}e(a(y)x)\widetilde{f_{1}}(x+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z),y)\widetilde{f_{2}}(x,y+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z)).

The key structural result for the function
a
is the following proposition.

Proposition 4.4
.

For every
C_{1}>0
there exists
C_{2}=C_{2}(C_{1},P)>0
such that the following holds. Let
\delta\in(0,1/10)
, let
V\leq W^{C_{1}}
be a nonnegative integer power of
W
, and let
r\in[V]
. Let
\widetilde{f_{1}},\widetilde{f_{2}}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be
1
-bounded functions supported on
[N/V]^{2}
, for some integer
N\geq C_{2}V^{C_{2}}\exp(\log(1/\delta)^{C_{2}})
, and let
a:{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
be a phase function. If

|\Lambda^{\prime}(a,\widetilde{f_{1}},\widetilde{f_{2}})|\geq\delta(N/V)^{2},

then there exist a positive integer power
V^{\prime}=V^{O_{C_{1},P}(1)}

and phases
\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{V^{\prime}-1}\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}

such that for at least
\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{O_{C_{1},P}(1)})V^{\prime}
values of
j\in[V^{\prime}]
, the bound

\|V^{\prime}a_{(j,V^{\prime})}(y)-\alpha_{j}\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\leq\exp(\log(1/\delta)^{O_{C_{1},P}(1)})VV^{\prime}/N

holds for at least
\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{O_{C_{1},P}(1)})N/(VV^{\prime})
choices of
y\in[N/(VV^{\prime})]
.

Unfortunately, this set of
y
’s where the phase can be well-approximated by constants may contribute little to the value of
\Lambda^{\prime}(a,\widetilde{f_{1}},\widetilde{f_{2}})
. To obtain a consequence better suited to our purposes, we bootstrap Proposition
4.4
and produce some such set of
y
’s that does make a significant contribution to
\Lambda^{\prime}(a,\widetilde{f_{1}},\widetilde{f_{2}})
. The exact statement is as follows.

Proposition 4.5
.

For every
C_{1}>0
there exists
C_{2}=C_{2}(C_{1},P)>0
such that the following holds. Let
\delta\in(0,1/10)
, let
V\leq W^{C_{1}}
be a nonnegative integer power of
W
, and let
r\in[V]
. Let
\widetilde{f_{1}},\widetilde{f_{2}}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be
1
-bounded functions supported on
[N/V]^{2}
, for some integer

, and let
a:{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
be any function. If

|\Lambda^{\prime}(a,\widetilde{f_{1}},\widetilde{f_{2}})|\geq\delta(N/V)^{2},

then there exist a positive integer power
V^{\prime}=V^{O_{C_{1},P}(1)}
, an integer

\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{O_{C_{1},P}(1)}))N\leq N^{\prime}\leq N,

and phases
\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{V^{\prime}-1}\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}

such that

\sum_{j\in[V^{\prime}]}\sum_{i\in[N/N^{\prime}]}\sum_{y}\left|\sum_{x\in[N^{\prime}/(VV^{\prime})]}\E_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]}e(\alpha_{y\!\!\!\!\pmod{V^{\prime}}}x)\right.\\
\widetilde{f_{1}}(V^{\prime}x+j+\frac{N^{\prime}}{V}i+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z),y)\left.\widetilde{f_{2}}(V^{\prime}x+j+\frac{N^{\prime}}{V}i,y+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z))\vphantom{\sum_{x}}\right|\\
\geq\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{O_{C_{1},P}(1)})(N/V)^{2}.

Proposition
4.5
essentially says that we can replace
a(y)
by a phase function
y\mapsto\alpha_{y\pmod{V^{\prime}}}
that is constant on arithmetic progressions of difference
V^{\prime}
. The summation over
i\in[N/N^{\prime}]
is a technical annoyance, necessitated by the proof, that will be easily removed in our later application of Proposition
4.5
.

Proof.

Fix
C_{1}>0
. Proposition
4.4
applied with
\delta/2
in place of
\delta
tells us that the inequality

(11)

\displaystyle|\Lambda^{\prime}({a},\widetilde{f_{1}},\widetilde{f_{2}})|\geq(\delta/2)(N/V)^{2}

(for
a,\widetilde{f_{1}},\widetilde{f_{2}}
satisfying the conditions of Proposition
4.4
) implies that there exist
C:=C_{2}=C_{2}(C_{1},P)>0
and
V^{\prime}=V^{O_{P}(1)}
such that, on setting
\tilde{V}=VV^{\prime}
, we obtain a set
{\mathcal{V}}\subset[V^{\prime}]
of size
|{\mathcal{V}}|\geq\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{C})V^{\prime}
, and for each
j^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{V}}
we get a phase
\alpha_{j^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
and a set

of size

satisfying

(12)

\displaystyle\|V^{\prime}a_{({j^{\prime}},V^{\prime})}(y^{\prime})-\alpha_{j^{\prime}}\|_{{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}\leq\exp(\log(1/\delta)^{C})\tilde{V}/N

for all
y^{\prime}\in S_{j^{\prime}}
. (Note that if this conclusion holds for
V^{\prime}
, then it also holds with
V^{\prime}
replaced by a larger power of
V
, so we may assume that
V^{\prime}
is independent of the particular choice of
a,\widetilde{f}_{1},\widetilde{f}_{2}
.) For any constant
C^{\prime}>C
, we can set
N^{\prime}\sim(C^{\prime})^{-1}\exp(-2\log(1/\delta)^{2C^{\prime}})N
and split the
x

V^{\prime}x+j+\frac{N^{\prime}}{V}i\quad\textrm{for}\quad x\in[N^{\prime}/\tilde{V}],\;\;j\in[V^{\prime}],\;\;i\in[N/N^{\prime}].

Then for each
j^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{V}}
and
y^{\prime}\in S_{j^{\prime}}
, we can approximate

(13)

\mathopen{}\left|e\mathopen{}\left(a_{({j^{\prime}},V^{\prime})}(y^{\prime})\mathopen{}\left(V^{\prime}x+j+\frac{N^{\prime}}{V}i\mathclose{}\right)\mathclose{}\right)-e\mathopen{}\left(\alpha_{j^{\prime}}x+a_{({j^{\prime}},V^{\prime})}(y^{\prime})\mathopen{}\left(j+\frac{N^{\prime}}{V}i\mathclose{}\right)\mathclose{}\right)\mathclose{}\right|\\
\leq\exp(\log(1/\delta)^{C})\frac{N^{\prime}}{N}\leq\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{2C^{\prime}})/2.

Define the set

\displaystyle\tilde{S}:=\{y\in[N/V]:\;y=V^{\prime}y^{\prime}+j^{\prime},\;j^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{V}},\;y^{\prime}\in S_{j^{\prime}}\},

which has size
|\tilde{S}|\geq\exp(-2\log(1/\delta)^{C})N/V

13

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{x}\E_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]}e(a(y)x)\widetilde{f_{1}}(x+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z),y)\widetilde{f_{2}}(x,y+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z))\right.

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{j\in[V^{\prime}]}\sum_{i\in[N/N^{\prime}]}\sum_{x\in[N^{\prime}/\tilde{V}]}\E_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]}e\mathopen{}\left(\alpha_{y\!\!\!\!\pmod{V^{\prime}}}x+a(y)\mathopen{}\left(j+\frac{N^{\prime}}{V}i\mathclose{}\right)\mathclose{}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.\widetilde{f_{1}}(V^{\prime}x+j+\frac{N^{\prime}}{V}i+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z),y)\widetilde{f_{2}}(V^{\prime}x+j+\frac{N^{\prime}}{V}i,y+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z))\vphantom{\sum_{x}}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq(\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{2C^{\prime}})/2)N/V

for each

,
and hence

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in\tilde{S}}

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{x}\E_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]}e(a(y)x)\widetilde{f_{1}}(x+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z),y)\widetilde{f_{2}}(x,y+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z))\right.

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{j\in[V^{\prime}]}\sum_{i\in[N/N^{\prime}]}\sum_{x\in[N^{\prime}/\tilde{V}]}\E_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]}e\mathopen{}\left(\alpha_{y\!\!\!\!\pmod{V^{\prime}}}x+a(y)\mathopen{}\left(j+\frac{N^{\prime}}{V}i\mathclose{}\right)\mathclose{}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.\widetilde{f_{1}}(V^{\prime}x+j+\frac{N^{\prime}}{V}i+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z),y)\widetilde{f_{2}}(V^{\prime}x+j+\frac{N^{\prime}}{V}i,y+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z))\vphantom{\sum_{x}}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq(\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{2C^{\prime}})/2)(N/V)^{2}.

The upshot is that if
\Lambda^{\prime}(a,\widetilde{f}_{1},\widetilde{f}_{2})
is large, then we can use the approximation (
12
) provided by Proposition
4.4
to approximate

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\prime}_{\tilde{S}}(a,\widetilde{f}_{1},\widetilde{f}_{2}):=\sum_{y\in\tilde{S}}\mathopen{}\left|\sum_{x}\E_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]}e(a(y)x)\widetilde{f_{1}}(x+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z),y)\widetilde{f_{2}}(x,y+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z))\mathclose{}\right|

by a sum closer to what we are seeking. Indeed, if

(14)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\prime}_{\tilde{S}}(a,\widetilde{f}_{1},\widetilde{f}_{2})\geq\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{2C^{\prime}})(N/V)^{2}

for

as above, then the triangle inequality and the long inequality above imply that

\sum_{y}\sum_{j\in[V^{\prime}]}\sum_{i\in[N/N^{\prime}]}\left|\sum_{x\in[N^{\prime}/\tilde{V}]}\E_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]}e(\alpha_{y\!\!\!\!\pmod{V^{\prime}}}x)\widetilde{f_{1}}(V^{\prime}x+j+\frac{N^{\prime}}{V}i+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z),y)\right.\\
\left.\widetilde{f_{2}}(V^{\prime}x+j+\frac{N^{\prime}}{V}i,y+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z))\vphantom{\sum_{x}}\right|\geq(\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{2C^{\prime}})/2)(N/V)^{2},

which establishes the conclusion of the proposition.

The lower bound (
14
) does not always hold, however. We will show that in this case, we can “throw out” the
y
-values in
\widetilde{S}
and repeat the argument from the previous paragraph to obtain a new set
\widetilde{S}
which is mostly disjoint from the set that we threw out; we can then iterate this procedure, which is guaranteed to terminate after a bounded number of steps because of the disjointness of the sets
\widetilde{S}
. The details are as follows.

We first note that if
S\subseteq[N/\widetilde{V}]
and
a^{\prime}:S\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
is a function such that
V^{\prime}a^{\prime}(y)
assumes each integer multiple of
1/|S|
exactly once as
y
ranges over
S
, then, for any interval
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, we have the bound

(15)

|\{y\in S:V^{\prime}a^{\prime}(y)\in I\}|\leq 1+|S|\cdot|I|\leq 1+(N/\widetilde{V})|I|.

In this case, we will say that
a^{\prime}
is
uniformly distributed
on
S
.

We also note that if
S\subseteq[N/V]
and
a^{\prime\prime}:[N/V]\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
is any function that agrees with
a
on
[N/V]\setminus S
, then

(16)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\prime}(a^{\prime\prime},1_{[N/V]\setminus S}\widetilde{f}_{1},\widetilde{f}_{2})=\Lambda^{\prime}(a,1_{[N/V]\setminus S}\widetilde{f}_{1},\widetilde{f}_{2})

(here we write
(1_{[N/V]\setminus S}\widetilde{f}_{1})(x,y)
for
1_{[N/V]\setminus S}(y)\widetilde{f}_{1}(x,y)
). Indeed, there is no contribution from the values of
y
where
a(y)\neq a^{\prime\prime}(y)
because for such
y
we have
(1_{[N/V]\setminus S}\widetilde{f}_{1})(x+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z),y)=0
.

To begin our iterative procedure, set
\widetilde{S}_{0}=\emptyset
and
a_{0}=a
. For
\ell=1,2,\ldots
, we will construct phases
\alpha_{\ell,j^{\prime}}
(for
j^{\prime}\in[V^{\prime}]
), a set
\widetilde{S}_{\ell}
, and a phase function
a_{\ell}
that agrees with
a
on the complement of
\widetilde{S}_{\leq\ell}:=\cup_{\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell}\widetilde{S}_{\ell^{\prime}}
and is uniformly distributed on the intersection of
\widetilde{S}_{\leq\ell}
with each arithmetic progression of common difference
V^{\prime}
. The
\ell
-th stage of the iteration begins with the inequality

\Lambda^{\prime}(a,1_{[N/V]\setminus\tilde{S}_{\leq\ell-1}}\widetilde{f}_{1},\widetilde{f}_{2})\geq(\delta/2)(N/V)^{2}

(which certainly holds for
\ell=1
). Then, by (
16
), we also have

\Lambda^{\prime}(a_{\ell-1},1_{[N/V]\setminus\tilde{S}_{\leq\ell-1}}\widetilde{f}_{1},\widetilde{f}_{2})\geq(\delta/2)(N/V)^{2}.

Arguing as in the first paragraph of the proof, with
a_{\ell-1}
instead of
a
and
1_{[N/V]\setminus\tilde{S}_{\leq\ell-1}}\widetilde{f}_{1}
instead of
\widetilde{f}_{1}
, we obtain phases
\alpha_{\ell,j^{\prime}}
(for
j^{\prime}\in[V^{\prime}]
) and a set
\widetilde{S}_{\ell}
. If (
14
) holds, then we obtain the conclusion of the proposition and our iterative procedure terminates. If not, then, by induction on
\ell
and the triangle inequality, we have

(17)

\Lambda^{\prime}(a,1_{[N/V]\setminus\widetilde{S}_{\leq\ell}}\widetilde{f}_{1},\widetilde{f}_{2})\geq(\delta-\ell\exp(-\log(1/\delta)^{C^{\prime}}))(N/V)^{2}>(\delta/2)(N/V)^{2}

as long as
\ell<(\delta/2)\exp(\log(1/\delta)^{C^{\prime}})
. Now let
a_{\ell}
be a function that agrees with
a
on
[N/V]\setminus\widetilde{S}_{\leq\ell}
and that is uniformly distributed on the intersection of
\widetilde{S}_{\leq\ell}
with each arithmetic progression of common difference
V^{\prime}
. This completes the
\ell
-th step of the iteration and puts us in a position to perform the
(\ell+1)
-th step.

It remains to analyze the sizes of the sets
\widetilde{S}_{\leq\ell}
and argue that the procedure terminates after a bounded number of steps. By (
15
) applied to each of the sets
S_{\ell,j^{\prime}}
(for
j^{\prime}\in[V^{\prime}]
), we have

|\widetilde{S}_{\ell}\cap\widetilde{S}_{\leq\ell-1}|\leq V^{\prime}(1+2(N/\widetilde{V})\exp(\log(1/\delta)^{C})\widetilde{V}/N)<3V^{\prime}\exp(\log(1/\delta)^{C}).

Hence

\displaystyle|\widetilde{S}_{\leq\ell}|

\displaystyle>|\widetilde{S}_{\leq\ell-1}|+|\widetilde{S}_{\ell}|-3V^{\prime}\exp(\log(1/\delta)^{C})

\displaystyle\geq|\widetilde{S}_{\leq\ell-1}|+\exp(-2\log(1/\delta)^{C})N/V-3V^{\prime}\exp(\log(1/\delta)^{C})

\displaystyle\geq|\widetilde{S}_{\leq\ell-1}|+(1/2)\exp(-2\log(1/\delta)^{C})N/V

(using the assumption that

is large), and by induction we obtain

|\widetilde{S}_{\leq\ell}|>(\ell/2)\exp(-2\log(1/\delta)^{C})N/V.

Since
\widetilde{S}_{\leq\ell}
is a subset of
[N/V]
, we conclude that the iterative procedure must terminate in at most
2\exp(-2\log(1/\delta)^{C})
steps, and, in particular, a suitable choice of

guarantees that the estimate in (

) holds for all
\ell
in the iterative procedure.
∎

\gamma
. By the pigeonhole principle, there exists
j\in[\beta_{1}^{2}W]
such that the set

B:=\{x\in[N]:\beta_{1}^{2}Wx+j\in A\}

has size at least
\gamma N
. Since
\nu(z)\geq d^{-1}
pointwise for
z\in[N]
, a standard Varnavides-style supersaturation version of the result of Kelley and Meka
[
15
]
tells us that

\sum_{x}\mathbb{E}_{z\in[N]}1_{B}(x)1_{B}(x+z)1_{B}(x+2z)\geq d^{-1}\exp(-\log(1/\gamma)^{C^{\prime}})N

for some absolute constant
C^{\prime}>0
.

Consider the set

B^{\prime}:=\{(x,y)\in[2N]^{2}:y-x\in B\},

and note that

N\sum_{x}1_{B}(x)1_{B}(x+z)1_{B}(x+2z)\asymp\sum_{x,y}1_{B^{\prime}}(x,y)1_{B^{\prime}}(x+z,y)1_{B^{\prime}}(x,y+z)

for each
z\in[N]
. Using this correspondence and applying Proposition
7.2
with
\delta:=\exp(-\log(1/\gamma)^{C^{\prime}})/(10d)
, we conclude that

\sum_{x}\mathbb{E}_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}]}1_{B}(x)1_{B}(x+\widetilde{P}(z))1_{B}(x+2\widetilde{P}(z))\gg_{P}\exp(-\log(1/\gamma)^{O_{P}(1)})N

as long as
W\geq\exp\exp(\log(1/\delta)^{\Omega_{P}(1)})
satisfies

\displaystyle N\gg_{P}W^{\Omega_{P}(1)}\geq\exp\exp(\log(1/\delta)^{\Omega_{P}(1)})\geq\exp\exp(\log(1/\gamma)^{\Omega_{P}(1)}).

In particular, the set
B
contains nontrivial configurations
x,\;x+\widetilde{P}(z),\;x+2\widetilde{P}(z)
, and hence the set
A
contains nontrivial configurations
x,\;x+P(z),\;x+2P(z)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Appendix A
Inverse theorems for box norms

We require inverse theorems for several box norms and directional Gowers norms that arise in our arguments. Although these results are more or less standard, we include statements and proofs here for ease of reference.

A.1.
U^{1}
-inverse theorems

Heuristically, a function has large degree-
1
Gowers norm in the direction
v
if it correlates with a function that is periodic in the
v
-direction. Since we are working with norms in which the differencing parameters
h
are constrained to lie in intervals, making this heuristic precise is somewhat delicate. In particular, we need the “length” of the differencing parameter to be sufficiently large relative to the size of the support of the function in question, where “sufficiently large” depends on the quality of the lower bound on the Gowers norm.
This issue does not appear for higher-degree box norms since Lemma
3.4
(
iv
) allows us to manipulate the lengths of boxes at will.

We start with the
U^{1}
-inverse theorem for functions on
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Lemma A.1

(
1
-dimensional
U^{1}
-inverse theorem)
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1]
and
N,N^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
N^{\prime}\geq\delta^{-1/2}N\geq\delta^{-1}
. If
f:{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{C}
is a
1
-bounded function supported on
[N]
, then

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{[\pm N^{\prime}]}^{2}\geq\delta N\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathopen{}\left|\sum_{x}f(x)\mathclose{}\right|\gg\delta^{1/4}N.

Proof.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{[\pm N^{\prime}]}^{2}=\sum_{x,x^{\prime}\in[N]}\mu_{N^{\prime}}(x^{\prime}-x)f(x)\overline{f(x^{\prime})}.

The idea is that since
N^{\prime}
is sufficiently large relative to
N
, we have
\mu_{N^{\prime}}(x-x^{\prime})\approx 1/(2N^{\prime}-1)
for all
x,x^{\prime}\in[N]
. More precisely, since
f
is
1
-bounded and
N^{\prime}\geq\delta^{-1/2}N\geq N
, we can make the crude comparison

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|(2N^{\prime}-1)\left\|f\right\|_{[N^{\prime}]}^{2}-\sum_{x,x^{\prime}}f(x)\overline{f(x^{\prime})}\mathclose{}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x,x^{\prime}\in[N]}\mathopen{}\left|(2N^{\prime}-1)\mu_{N^{\prime}}(x^{\prime}-x)-1\mathclose{}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2N^{\prime}-1}\sum_{x,x^{\prime}\in[N]}|x-x^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N^{2}(N-1)}{2N^{\prime}-1}\leq\delta^{1/2}N^{2}/2.

Hence

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|\sum_{x}f(x)\mathclose{}\right|^{2}=\sum_{x,x^{\prime}}f(x)\overline{f(x^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\geq(2N^{\prime}-1)\delta N-\delta^{1/2}N^{2}/2

\displaystyle\geq(3/2)\delta^{1/2}N-\delta N\gg\delta^{1/2}N^{2}

since
N\geq\delta^{-1/2}
.
∎

Lemma A.2

(
2
-dimensional
U^{1}
-inverse theorem)
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1]
and
N,N^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
N^{\prime}\geq(\delta/2)^{-1/2}N\geq 2\delta^{-1}
, and let
f:{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be a
1
-bounded function supported on
[N]^{2}
. Then

\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}\cdot[\pm N^{\prime}]}\geq\delta N^{2}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{$\sum_{x,y}f(x,y)b(y)\gg\delta^{5/4}N^{2}$ for some $1$-bounded $b:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{C}}$};

\left\|f\right\|_{{\mathbf{e}}_{2}\cdot[\pm N^{\prime}]}^{2}\geq\delta N^{2}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{$\sum_{x,y}f(x,y)b(x)\gg\delta^{5/4}N^{2}$ for some $1$-bounded $b:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{C}}$};

and

\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1})\cdot[\pm N^{\prime}]}\geq\delta N^{2}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{$\sum_{x,y}f(x,y)b(x+y)\gg\delta^{5/4}N^{2}$ for some $1$-bounded $b:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{C}}$}.

Proof.

We begin with the first statement; the second follows by symmetry. For each
y\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, define the function
f_{y}:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
via
f_{y}(x):=f(x,y)
. Then we have the identity

\left\|f\right\|_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}\cdot[\pm N^{\prime}]}^{2}=\sum_{y\in[N]}\left\|f_{y}\right\|_{[\pm N^{\prime}]}^{2}.

The popularity principle supplies at least
\delta N/2
values of
y\in[N]
for which
\left\|f_{y}\right\|_{[\pm N^{\prime}]}^{2}\geq\delta N/2
. Applying Lemma
A.1
for each such
y
and using nonnegativity for the remaining
y
’s, we find that

\sum_{y\in[N]}\mathopen{}\left|\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f(x,y)\mathclose{}\right|\gg\delta N\cdot\delta^{1/4}N.

Adding
1
-bounded fudge factors
b(y)
to remove the absolute values, we obtain

\sum_{x,y}f(x,y)b(y)\gg\delta^{5/4}N^{2},

as desired.

The proof of the third statement goes similarly. This time, we foliate
[N]^{2}
using
1
-dimensional arithmetic progressions with common difference
{\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1}
; each such arithmetic progression intersects
[N]^{2}
in at most
N
elements. Applying the popularity principle and Lemma
A.1
, and then removing the absolute values as in the first statement, we arrive at the desired conclusion.
∎

A.2.
Unnormalized box norms

To prove inverse theorems for higher-degree box norms, it is often easier to start with unnormalized box norms. Given
{\textbf{v}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{v}}_{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
, we define the
unnormalized box norm
of a compactly supported function
f:{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
along
{\textbf{v}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{v}}_{s}
to be

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{v}}_{s}}

\displaystyle:=\mathopen{}\left(\sum_{{\textbf{x}},h_{1},\ldots,h_{s}}\Delta_{{\textbf{v}}_{1}h_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{v}}_{s}h_{s}}f({\textbf{x}})\mathclose{}\right)^{1/2^{s}}.

If
D=1
and
{\textbf{v}}_{1}=\cdots={\textbf{v}}_{s}=1
, we also set
\left\|f\right\|_{U^{s}}:=\left\|f\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{v}}_{s}}
.
The following lemma relates unnormalized and normalized box norms.

Lemma A.3

(Relating normalized and unnormalized box norms)
.

Let
s\geq 2
, let
D,H_{1},\ldots,H_{s}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and let
{\textbf{v}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{v}}_{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}
. For any compactly supported function
f:{\mathbb{Z}}^{D}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{1}\cdot[\pm H_{1}],\ldots,{\textbf{v}}_{s}\cdot[\pm H_{s}]}^{2^{s}}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{s}\frac{1}{2H_{i}-1}\cdot\left\|f\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{v}}_{s}}^{2^{s}}.

Proof.

Using the inductive formula for box norms, the pointwise bound
\mu_{H}(h)\leq 1/(2H-1)
, and nonnegativity, we have

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{1}\cdot[\pm H_{1}],\ldots,{\textbf{v}}_{s}\cdot[\pm H_{s}]}^{2^{s}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{h_{2},\ldots,h_{s}}\mu_{H_{2}}(h_{2})\cdots\mu_{H_{s}}(h_{s})\left\|\Delta_{{\textbf{v}}_{2}h_{2},\ldots,{\textbf{v}}_{s}h_{s}}f\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{1}\cdot[\pm H_{1}]}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=2}^{s}\frac{1}{2H_{i}-1}\cdot\sum_{h_{2},\ldots,h_{s}}\left\|\Delta_{{\textbf{v}}_{2}h_{2},\ldots,{\textbf{v}}_{s}h_{s}}f\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{1}\cdot[\pm H_{1}]}^{2}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=2}^{s}\frac{1}{2H_{i}-1}\cdot\sum_{h_{1}}\mu_{H_{1}}(h_{1})\left\|\Delta_{{\textbf{v}}_{1}h_{1}}f\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{2},\ldots,{\textbf{v}}_{s}}^{2^{s-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=1}^{s}\frac{1}{2H_{i}-1}\cdot\left\|f\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{1},\ldots,{\textbf{v}}_{s}}^{2^{s}};

note that the applications of nonnegativity crucially used the assumption
s\geq 2
.
∎

A.3.
U^{2}
-inverse theorems

The starting point for
U^{2}
-inverse theorems is the following well-known
1
-dimensional result (see, e.g.,
[
30
, Lemma 2.4]
).

Lemma A.4

(
1
-dimensional
U^{2}
-inverse theorem)
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1]
and
H,N\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
\delta N\leq H\leq N
. If
f:{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{C}
is a
1
-bounded function supported on
[N]
, then

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{[\pm H],[\pm H]}^{4}\geq\delta N\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{$\sum_{x}f(x)e(ax+b)\gg\delta^{3/2}N$ for some $a,\;b\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}$}.

Proof.

By Lemma
A.3
and the assumption
H\geq\delta N
, we have
\left\|f\right\|_{U^{2}}^{4}\gg\delta^{3}N^{3}
. Fourier inversion and Plancherel’s Identity give

\left\|f\right\|_{U^{2}}^{4}=\left\|\hat{f}\right\|_{4}^{4}\leq\left\|\hat{f}\right\|_{2}^{2}\cdot\left\|\hat{f}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}=\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}\cdot\left\|\hat{f}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq N\cdot\left\|\hat{f}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}.

Hence
\left\|\hat{f}\right\|_{\infty}\gg\delta^{3/2}N
, which is equivalent to the conclusion of the lemma.
∎

Lemma A.5

(
2
-dimensional
U^{2}
-inverse theorem)
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1]
and
H,N\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
\delta^{-O(1)}\ll\delta N\leq H\leq N
, and let
f:{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be a
1
-bounded function supported on
[N]^{2}
. Then

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{({\mathbf{e}}_{1}\cdot[\pm H])^{2}}^{4}\geq\delta N^{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{x,y}f(x,y)e(a(y)x+b(y))\gg\delta^{3/2}N^{2}

\displaystyle\text{for some $1$-bounded $a,\;b:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}$};

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|^{4}_{({\mathbf{e}}_{2}\cdot[\pm H])^{2}}\geq\delta N^{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{x,y}f(x,y)e(a(x)y+b(x))\gg\delta^{3/2}N^{2}

\displaystyle\text{for some $1$-bounded $a,\;b:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}$}.

This lemma follows from Lemma
A.4
just as Lemma
A.2
follows from Lemma
A.1
.

A.4.
U^{1}\times U^{1}
-inverse theorems

Let
{\textbf{v}}_{1},{\textbf{v}}_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
be vectors such that
\langle{\textbf{v}}_{1},{\textbf{v}}_{2}\rangle_{{\mathbb{Z}}}={\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
. Then the unnormalized box norm along
{\textbf{v}}_{1},{\textbf{v}}_{2}
can be written as

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{1},{\textbf{v}}_{2}}

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\left(\sum_{m,m^{\prime},n,n^{\prime}}f({\textbf{v}}_{1}m+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n)\overline{f({\textbf{v}}_{1}m^{\prime}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n)f({\textbf{v}}_{1}m+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{\prime})}f({\textbf{v}}_{1}m^{\prime}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{\prime})\mathclose{}\right)^{1/4}

and satisfies the following inverse theorem.

Lemma A.6

(Inverse theorem for the unnormalized
U^{1}\times U^{1}
-norm)
.

Let
{\textbf{v}}_{1},{\textbf{v}}_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
be vectors of length
O(1)
satisfying
\langle{\textbf{v}}_{1},{\textbf{v}}_{2}\rangle_{{\mathbb{Z}}}={\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, and let
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. If
f:{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is a
1
-bounded function supported on
[N]^{2}
, then there are
1
-bounded functions
b_{1},b_{2}:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{1},{\textbf{v}}_{2}}^{4}\ll N^{2}\cdot\sum_{m,n}f({\textbf{v}}_{1}m+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n)b_{1}(m)b_{2}(n).

Proof.

The pigeonhole principle gives some
n^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, necessarily of size
O(N)
, such that

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{{\textbf{v}}_{1},{\textbf{v}}_{2}}^{4}\ll N\mathopen{}\left|\sum_{m,m^{\prime},n}f({\textbf{v}}_{1}m+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n)\overline{f({\textbf{v}}_{1}m^{\prime}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n)f({\textbf{v}}_{1}m+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{\prime})}f({\textbf{v}}_{1}m^{\prime}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{\prime})\mathclose{}\right|.

Let
S:=\{m^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\;{\textbf{v}}_{1}m^{\prime}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{\prime}\in[N]^{2}\}
.
Then
|S|\ll N
, and the result follows upon setting

b_{1}(m):=\overline{f({\textbf{v}}_{1}m+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{\prime})}
and letting
b_{2}(n)
be equal to
\E_{m^{\prime}\in S}\overline{f({\textbf{v}}_{1}m^{\prime}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n)}f({\textbf{v}}_{1}m^{\prime}+{\textbf{v}}_{2}n^{\prime})
times a suitable complex number of norm
1
that makes the expression in the absolute value positive.
∎

Combining this lemma with Lemma
A.3
immediately gives the following result for unnormalized box norms.

Lemma A.7

(Inverse theorem for the
U^{1}\times U^{1}
-norm)
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1]
and
H,N\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
\delta N\leq H\leq N
. Let
f:{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be a
1
-bounded function supported on
[N]^{2}
. Then

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}\cdot[\pm H],\;{\mathbf{e}}_{2}\cdot[\pm H]}^{4}\geq\delta N^{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{x,y}f(x,y)b_{1}(x)b_{2}(y)\gg\delta^{3}N^{2}

\displaystyle\text{for some $1$-bounded $b_{1},b_{2}:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{C}}$};

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}\cdot[\pm H],\;({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1})\cdot[\pm H]}^{4}\geq\delta N^{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{x,y}f(x,y)b_{1}(x+y)b_{2}(y)\gg\delta^{3}N^{2}

\displaystyle\text{for some $1$-bounded $b_{1},b_{2}:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{C}}$};

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{{\mathbf{e}}_{2}\cdot[\pm H],\;({\mathbf{e}}_{2}-{\mathbf{e}}_{1})\cdot[\pm H]}^{4}\geq\delta N^{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle\sum_{x,y}f(x,y)b_{1}(x)b_{2}(x+y)\gg\delta^{3}N^{2}

\displaystyle\text{for some $1$-bounded $b_{1},b_{2}:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{C}}$}.

A.5.
U^{2}\times U^{1}
-inverse theorem

Recall that our degree-lowering result Theorem
4.1
is based on replacing “
U^{2}\times U^{1}
-obstructions” with “
U^{1}\times U^{1}
-obstructions”. We are finally ready to give a precise characterization of the former obstructions.

Lemma A.8
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1]
and
H,N\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
\delta^{O(1)}\ll\delta N\leq H\leq N
, and let
f:{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be a
1
-bounded function supported on
[N]^{2}
. Then

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}\cdot[\pm H],\;{\mathbf{e}}_{1}\cdot[\pm H],\;{\mathbf{e}}_{2}\cdot[\pm H]}^{8}\geq\delta N^{2}\Longrightarrow\sum_{x,y}f(x,y)g(x)e(a(y)x+b(y))\gg\delta^{O(1)}N^{2}

\displaystyle\text{for some $1$-bounded $g:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{C}}$ and phase functions $a,b:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}$};

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}\cdot[\pm H],\;{\mathbf{e}}_{2}\cdot[\pm H],\;{\mathbf{e}}_{2}\cdot[\pm H]}^{8}\geq\delta N^{2}\Longrightarrow\sum_{x,y}f(x,y)g(y)e(a(x)y+b(x))\gg\delta^{O(1)}N^{2}

\displaystyle\text{for some $1$-bounded $g:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{C}}$ and phase functions $a,b:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}$}.

Proof.

We only prove the first statement as the other can be proved by swapping the roles of
x
and
y
. The inductive formula for box norms gives

\delta^{8}N^{2}\leq\|f\|_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}\cdot[\pm H],\;{\mathbf{e}}_{1}\cdot[\pm H],\;{\mathbf{e}}_{2}\cdot[\pm H]}^{8}=\sum_{h}\mu_{H}(h)\|\Delta_{h{\mathbf{e}}_{2}}f\|_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}\cdot[\pm H],\;{\mathbf{e}}_{1}\cdot[\pm H]}^{4}.

By Lemma
A.5
, there are phases
\alpha(y,y+h),\;\beta(y,y+h)\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
such that

\sum_{x,y,h}\mu_{H}(h)\Delta_{h{\mathbf{e}}_{2}}f(x,y)e(\alpha(y,y+h)x+\beta(y,y+h))\gg\delta^{O(1)}N^{2}

and the summand is real and nonnegative for each fixed
h,y
. In particular, we have

\sum_{x,y,h}\Delta_{h{\mathbf{e}}_{2}}f(x,y)e(\alpha(y,y+h)x+\beta(y,y+h))\gg\delta^{O(1)}N^{3}.

Replacing the variable
h
with the variable
z:=y+h
, we get

\sum_{x,y,z}f(x,y)\overline{f(x,z)}e(\alpha(y,z)x+\beta(y,z))\gg\delta^{O(1)}N^{3}.

The pigeonhole principle gives an integer
|z_{0}|\ll N
such that

\sum_{x,y}f(x,y)\overline{f(x,z_{0})}e(\alpha(y,z_{0})x+\beta(y,z_{0}))\gg\delta^{O(1)}N^{2}.

The lemma now follows upon setting

g(x):=\overline{f(x,z_{0})},\quad a(y):=\alpha(y,z_{0}),\quad\text{and}\quad b(y):=\beta(y,z_{0}).

∎

Appendix B
Nilsequence definitions

This section contains (mostly standard) definitions pertaining to nilsequences.

Definition B.1

(
(t-1)
-fold commutator)
.

For elements
g_{1},\dots,g_{t}
of a group
G
, we define

[g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{t}]:=[[[g_{1},g_{2}],g_{3}],\dots,g_{t}].

We will call any such commutator a
(t-1)
-fold commutator
of the elements
g_{1},\dots,g_{t}
.

We now introduce the lower central series.

Definition B.2
.

The
lower central series
of a nilpotent Lie group
G
is the sequence of nested subgroups
G=G_{(0)}=G_{(1)}\supseteq G_{(2)}\supseteq G_{(3)}\cdots
where
G_{(i)}:=[G,G_{(i-1)}]
for
i\geq 1
. The
step
of
G
is the smallest integer
s
such that
G_{(s+1)}=\mathrm{Id}_{G}
.

Recall that the
height
of the rational number
a/b
(for
a,b
coprime integers) is
\max(|a|,|b|)
.
We will now define a nilmanifold.

Definition B.3

(Nilmanifold)
.

A
nilmanifold
of degree
k
, step
s
, complexity at most
M
, and dimension
m
consists of the following data:

(i)

an
s
-step connected and simply connected nilpotent real Lie group
G
and a discrete cocompact subgroup
\Gamma
of
G
;

(ii)

a
filtration

G_{\bullet}=(G_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}
of nested subgroups
G=G_{0}=G_{1}\supseteq G_{2}\supseteq\cdots
such that
G_{i}=\{\mathrm{Id}_{G}\}
for all
i>k
and
[G_{i},G_{j}]\subseteq G_{i+j}
for all
i,j
; and

(iii)

a
Mal’cev basis
, i.e., a basis
\{X_{1},\dots,X_{m}\}
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}:=\log(G)
.

We furthermore require the data to satisfy the following conditions:

(i)

For
1\leq i,j\leq m
, we have

[X_{i},X_{j}]=\sum_{l>\max(i,j)}a_{ijl}X_{l}

for some rational numbers
a_{ijl}
of height at most
M
.

(ii)

For
m_{i}:=\text{dim}(G_{i})
, we have
\log(G_{i})=\text{span}\{X_{j}:m-m_{i}<j\leq m\}
.

(iii)

Each element
g\in G
can be expressed uniquely as
\exp(t_{1}X_{1})\exp(t_{2}X_{2})\cdots\exp(t_{m}X_{m})
with
t_{1},\dots,t_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
; we let
\psi:g\mapsto(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})
denote the associated
coordinate map
from
G
to
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

(iv)

\Gamma
consists of precisely the elements
g
for which
\psi(g)\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
.

A Mal’cev basis that satisfies the last three of these conditions is said to be
adapted
to the filtration
G_{\bullet}
.

Note that condition (ii) implies that each
G_{i}
is closed, connected, and simply connected.

The lower central series (as defined above) gives the so-called
standard filtration
on a nilpotent Lie group
G
, and we emphasize that there are filtrations other than the standard filtration. The standard filtration on
G
is “minimal” in the sense that any other filtration
(G_{i})
satisfies
G_{i}\supseteq G_{(i)}
for each
i\in{\mathbb{N}}

[
43
, Exercise 1.6.2]
.

A nilmanifold can be endowed with a metric as follows.

Definition B.4

(Metric on a nilmanifold)
.

Given a nilmanifold
G/\Gamma
with coordinate map
\psi
, we define a metric
d_{G}
on
G
to be the right-invariant metric

d_{G}(x,y):=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\min\{|\psi(x_{i}x_{i-1}^{-1})|,|\psi(x_{i-1}x_{i}^{-1})|\}:x_{0}=x,x_{n}=y\right\}.

Setting
d(x\Gamma,y\Gamma):=\inf\{d(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}):x^{\prime}\Gamma=x\Gamma,y^{\prime}\Gamma=y\Gamma\}
, we obtain a metric on

This metric makes

into a compact metric space and hence can be used to define the Lipschitz parameter of a function on
G/\Gamma
.

Definition B.5

(Lipschitz norm)
.

Let
(X,d_{X})
be a metric space. The
Lipschitz constant
of a function
F\colon X\to\mathbb{C}
is the quantity

\sup_{x\neq y\in X}\frac{|F(x)-F(y)|}{d_{X}(x,y)},

and the
Lipschitz norm
of

is

\|F\|_{\mathrm{Lip}(X)}:=\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}+\sup_{x\neq y\in X}\frac{|F(x)-F(y)|}{d_{X}(x,y)}.

If
\|F\|_{\mathrm{Lip}(X)}\leq M
, then we say that
F
is an
M
-Lipschitz function
.

An important property of Lipschitz functions on a nilmanifold is that they can be approximated by finite linear combinations of vertical characters (as defined below).

We now turn to polynomial sequences.

Definition B.6

(Polynomial sequences)
.

Let

be an

(G_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}

It is known (see, e.g.,
[

forms a group under pointwise multiplication.

Our next definition is a smoothness norm on real polynomials that captures the extent to which their coefficients are close to integers.

Definition B.7

(Smoothness norm)
.

Let

be a polynomial of degree

\left\|p\right\|_{C^{\infty}[T]}:=\max_{1\leq j\leq D}T^{j}\left\|\alpha_{j}\right\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}.

Note that this definition of smoothness is written using the monomial basis, which differs slightly from
[

, Definition 2.2]
. By
[

, Lemma A.10]
, these notions are equivalent up to a multiplicative factor of

Two important special classes of polynomial sequences on

are those whose coefficients are “rational” and those which grow slowly and remain “close” to the identity.

Definition B.8

Given

We say that a polynomial sequence

We are finally ready to define a nilsequence.

Appendix C
Fraction-comparison estimates

In this section, we prove elementary “fraction-comparison estimates” in the style of
[
34
]
(see, e.g., the proof of Proposition 5.6 there). These estimates are useful in the nilsequences part of our argument. While the first two lemmas are straightforward, the third makes use of some important properties of
V
-tricked polynomials. Recall the smoothness norms from Definition
B.7
.

Lemma C.1
.

Let
D,Q,T\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
R\in{\mathbb{R}}[z]
be a polynomial of degree
D
, and let
a,b\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
|a|\leq QT
and
0<|b|\leq Q
nonzero. Then the polynomial
\tilde{R}(z):=R(a+bz)
satisfies

\|b^{D}R\|_{C^{\infty}[T]}\ll_{D}Q^{D-1}\|\tilde{R}\|_{C^{\infty}[T]}.

Proof.

Assume that
\|\tilde{R}\|_{C^{\infty}[T]}\leq M
; we will show that
\|b^{D}R\|_{C^{\infty}[T]}\ll_{D}Q^{D-1}M
. Write
R(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{D}\alpha_{i}z^{i}
. Then

\displaystyle\tilde{R}(z)=R(a+bz)=\sum_{i=0}^{D}\alpha_{i}(a+bz)^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{D}\alpha_{i}\sum_{j=0}^{i}{i\choose j}b^{j}z^{j}a^{i-j}=\sum_{j=0}^{D}b^{j}z^{j}\sum_{i=j}^{D}\alpha_{i}{i\choose j}a^{i-j}.

The assumption
\|\tilde{R}\|_{C^{\infty}[T]}\leq M
means that

(34)

\displaystyle\left\|b^{j}\cdot\sum_{i=j}^{D}\alpha_{i}{i\choose j}a^{i-j}\right\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\leq M/T^{j}

for each
1\leq j\leq D
. In particular the
j=D
instance of (
34
) gives

\left\|b^{D}\alpha_{D}\right\|_{{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}\leq M/T^{D}.

Next, the
j=D-1
instance of (
34
) gives

\left\|b^{D-1}(\alpha_{D-1}+Da\alpha_{D})\right\|_{{\mathbb{R}}//Z}\leq M/T^{D-1};

multiplying the expression in
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}
by
b
and using the triangle inequality (and the assumption on the sizes of
a,b
), we find that

\left\|b^{D}\alpha_{D-1}\right\|_{{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}\leq QM/T^{D-1}+D(QT)(M/T^{D})\ll_{D}QM/T^{D-1}.

Continuing inductively in this manner, we find that

\left\|b^{D}\alpha_{j}\right\|_{{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}\ll_{D}Q^{D-j}M/T^{j}\leq Q^{D-1}M/T^{j}

for each
1\leq j\leq D
, as desired.
∎

For ease of reference we record the following special case of this lemma.

Lemma C.2
.

Let
D,T\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and let
R\in{\mathbb{R}}[z]
be a polynomial of degree
D
. If
|y_{0}|\leq QT
and

\|R(y_{0}+\cdot)\|_{C^{\infty}[T]}\leq Q,

then

\|R\|_{C^{\infty}[T]}\ll_{D}Q^{D}.

Recall that we are interested in the
V
-tricked polynomial

\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z):=\frac{\widetilde{P}(Vz+r)-\widetilde{P}(r)}{V},

where
V=W^{O_{P}(1)}
is a nonnegative integer power of
W
and
r\in[V]
; as mentioned previously,
\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}
has integer coefficients, zero constant term and degree
d\geq 2
.
Let us denote the coefficient of
z^{i}
in
\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z)
by
V_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[W,V,r,\;\beta_{1},\ldots,\;\beta_{d}]
. We record that

V_{d}=\beta_{d}\beta_{1}^{d-2}W^{d-1}V^{d-1}

and that
V_{1}
is equal to
1
plus
W
times some integer-coefficient polynomial in
W,V,r,\;\beta_{1},\ldots,\;\beta_{d}
. Thus, if every prime factor of
\beta_{1}\beta_{d}
also divides
W
(which is the case for
w
larger than an absolute constant depending only on
P
), then
V_{1}
is equivalent to
1
modulo every prime dividing
V_{d}
and we have

\gcd(V_{d},V_{1})=1.

The following lemma tells us that precomposing a polynomial with
\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}
cannot significantly decrease its (properly normalized) smoothness norm.

Lemma C.3
.

Let
D\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For every
C_{1}>0
there exists a constant
C_{2}=C_{2}(C_{1},D,P)>0
such that the following holds. Let
V\leq W^{C_{1}}
be a nonnegative integer power of
W
, let
r\in[V]
, and let
T,Q\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Let
R\in\mathbb{R}[z]
be a polynomial of degree
D
. Assume that every prime factor of
\beta_{1}\beta_{d}
also divides
W
. If

\|R\circ\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}\|_{C^{\infty}[(T/V_{d})^{1/d}]}\leq Q

and
T>C_{2}W^{C_{2}}
,
then

\|R\|_{C^{\infty}[T]}\leq C_{2}Q.

The scales of these smoothness norms come from the estimate
\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}((T/V_{d})^{1/d})\asymp T
.

Proof.

We fix
C_{1}>0
and let all the constants depend on
P
and
D
. The proof proceeds by induction on the degree
D
, where the base case
D=0
holds vacuously.
For the induction step, write
R(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{D}\alpha_{i}z^{i}
. Recall that
\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z)=V_{d}z^{d}+\cdots+V_{1}z
. We compute

\displaystyle R(\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{D}\alpha_{i}(V_{d}z^{d}+\cdots+V_{1}z)^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{D}\alpha_{i}\sum_{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{d}=i}\binom{i}{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}(V_{1}z)^{j_{1}}\cdots(V_{d}z^{d})^{j_{d}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{dD}z^{k}\sum_{i=0}^{D}\alpha_{i}\sum_{{\underline{j}}\in\mathcal{P}(i,k)}\binom{i}{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}V_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots V_{d}^{j_{d}},

where
\mathcal{P}(i,k)
denotes the set of tuples
\underline{j}=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{d})
of nonnegative integers satisfying
j_{1}+\cdots+j_{d}=i
and
j_{1}+2j_{2}+\cdots+dj_{d}=k
. The assumption
\|R\circ\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}\|_{C^{\infty}[(T/V_{d})^{1/d}]}\leq Q

tells us that

(35)

\left\|\sum_{i=0}^{D}\alpha_{i}\sum_{{\underline{j}}\in\mathcal{P}(i,k)}\binom{i}{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}V_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots V_{d}^{j_{d}}\right\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\leq Q(V_{d}/T)^{k/d}

for each
1\leq k\leq dD
. Let
E_{k}
denote the expression inside
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}
in (
35
).

We will show that
\|\alpha_{D}\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\leq Q/T^{D}
.
Since
E_{dD}=\alpha_{D}V_{d}^{D}
, Equation (
35
) for
k=dD
tells us that

\left\|\alpha_{D}V_{d}^{D}\right\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\leq Q(V_{d}/T)^{D},

and hence there is some integer
n
such that

(36)

|\alpha_{D}-n/V_{d}^{D}|\leq Q/T^{D}.

We will aim to show that
n
is divisible by
V_{d}^{D}
. To accomplish this, we will find a second rational approximation for
\alpha_{D}
using a denominator that is coprime to
V_{d}^{D}
. To begin, we have
E_{1}=\alpha_{1}V_{1}
. Next,
E_{2}=\alpha_{2}V_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{1}V_{2}
, and so

V_{1}E_{2}-V_{2}E_{1}=V_{1}^{3}\alpha_{2}.

We continue with
E_{3}=\alpha_{3}V_{1}^{3}+\alpha_{2}(2V_{1}V_{2})+\alpha_{1}V_{3}
and

V_{1}^{2}E_{3}-2V_{2}(V_{1}E_{2}-V_{2}E_{1})-V_{1}V_{3}E_{1}=V_{1}^{5}\alpha_{3}.

Continuing inductively in this manner, we obtain a positive integer
\gamma
and polynomials
s_{1},\ldots,s_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}[V_{1},\ldots,V_{d}]
such that

s_{1}E_{1}+\cdots+s_{d}E_{d}=V_{1}^{\gamma}\alpha_{D}

holds (as an identity in the formal variables
V_{1},\ldots,V_{d},\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{D}
); note that the
s_{i}
’s depend only on the parameters
d,D
. The crucial point for the induction is that
E_{k}
(for
1\leq k\leq D
) equals
\alpha_{k}V_{1}^{k}
plus a linear combination of
\alpha_{k-1},\ldots,\alpha_{1}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}[V_{1},\ldots,V_{d}]
.

Recalling that
V_{i}=O(W^{d-1}V^{d-1})=O(W^{O(1)})

and applying (
35
) for
1\leq k\leq D
, we find that

\left\|V_{1}^{\gamma}\alpha_{D}\right\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}=O(QW^{O(1)}/T^{1/d}).

Thus there is an integer
n^{\prime}
such that

\mathopen{}\left|\alpha_{D}-n^{\prime}/V_{1}^{\gamma}\mathclose{}\right|=O(QW^{O(1)}/T^{1/d}).

Comparing this with (
36
) gives

\mathopen{}\left|n/V_{d}^{D}-n^{\prime}/V_{1}^{\gamma}\mathclose{}\right|=O(QW^{O(1)}/T^{1/d}).

Now we must have

n/V_{d}^{D}=n^{\prime}/V_{1}^{\gamma}

since otherwise we would have
\mathopen{}\left|n/V_{d}^{D}-n^{\prime}/V_{1}^{\gamma}\mathclose{}\right|\geq 1/(V_{d}^{D}V_{1}^{\gamma})
, which (due to the constraint on
T
) is not of size
O(QW^{O(1)}/T^{1/d})
. Since
V_{1},V_{d}
are coprime, we conclude that
n
is divisible by
V_{d}^{D}
, and (
36
) tells us that

\left\|\alpha_{D}\right\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\leq Q/T^{D},

as desired.

We claim that

\left\|\alpha_{D}(\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]})^{D}\right\|_{C^{\infty}[(T/V_{d})^{1/d}]}=O(Q).

Indeed, since each
V_{i}\ll(WV)^{d-1}\ll V_{d}
, we see that the
z^{i}
-coefficient of
(\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]})^{D}
has size
\ll V_{d}^{D}
. Then for each
0\leq i\leq dD
,
the distance from the
z^{i}
-coefficient of
\alpha_{D}(\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]})^{D}
to the nearest integer is at most

\ll(Q/T^{D})\cdot V_{d}^{D}\leq(V_{d}/T)^{i/d}Q;

this establishes the claim. Hence

\left\|(R\circ\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]})-\alpha_{D}(\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]})^{D}\right\|_{C^{\infty}[(T/V_{d})^{1/d}]}=O(Q),

and the lemma follows from the induction hypothesis.
∎

Appendix D
Sárközy-type results

The goal of this section is to make precise the heuristic that

(37)

\displaystyle\sum_{\textbf{x}}\E_{z\in[(N/V_{d})^{1/d}]}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})f_{1}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z))\approx\sum_{{\textbf{x}}}\E_{z\in[N]}f_{0}({\textbf{x}})f_{1}({\textbf{x}}+{\textbf{v}}z),

where
V=W^{O_{P}(1)}
is a nonnegative integer power of
W
,
r\in[V]
(in particular, if
V=1
, then
r=0
and hence
\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}=\widetilde{P}
), and
V_{i}
’s are the coefficients of
\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}
.
We use circle-method arguments to make this heuristic precise. These arguments originated in work of Sárközy
[
37
,
38
]
, and we will more closely follow a method due to Green
[
12
]
.

Throughout this section, define
K:=(N/V_{d})^{1/d}
, so that
|\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z)|\ll_{P}N
for
z\in[K]
.
For
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
, we also let

\displaystyle S(\xi):=\sum_{z\in[K]}e(\xi\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z))

denote the Fourier transform of the (multi)set
\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}([K])
.

D.1.
Circle method preliminaries

The first step towards (
37
) is showing that the counting operator for a
2
-point polynomial progression is always controlled by the major arcs of its arguments. Let us make a few more definitions. Let
\varepsilon>0
be a constant to be determined later. For coprime
0\leq a<q\leq K^{\varepsilon}
, we define the
major arc

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{a,q}:=\left\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}:\;\mathopen{}\left|\xi-\frac{a}{q}\mathclose{}\right|\leq K^{-(d-\varepsilon)}\right\}.

As long as
\varepsilon<1/3
, the intervals
\mathfrak{M}_{a,q}
are disjoint.
So we partition
{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
into the
major arcs

\mathfrak{M}
and the
minor arcs

\mathfrak{m}
, where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}:=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a\leq q\leq K^{\varepsilon},\\
\gcd(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{M}_{a,q}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathfrak{m}:=({\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}})\setminus\mathfrak{M}.

A key input for controlling the minor arcs is the following classical lemma that upper-bounds the even moments of
F
(see, e.g.,
[

).

Lemma D.1

(Crude bound on even moments of

There exists some positive integer
s_{0}(d)>d
such that for all
s\geq s_{0}(d)
and
\eta>0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathopen{}\left|S(\xi)\mathclose{}\right|^{2s}\;d\xi\ll_{\eta}K^{2s-d+\eta}.

The following lemma is a generalization of
[
34
, Lemma B.4]
, and it follows from a nearly identical proof, which we omit.

Lemma D.2

(Minor arc control)
.

Let
s_{0}(d)
be as in Lemma
D.1
, and let
s>s_{0}(d)
be a positive integer. There exists an absolute
C=C(d)\geq 1
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)

and positive integers

, if the coefficients of
\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}
have size at most

, then
we have the pointwise bound

\displaystyle\sup_{\xi\in\mathfrak{m}}|S(\xi)|\leq K^{1-\varepsilon/C}

as well as the

-bound

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{m}}\mathopen{}\left|S(\xi)\mathclose{}\right|^{2s}\ll_{s,\varepsilon}K^{2s-d-\varepsilon/C}.

In order to handle the major arcs, we start with the following classical decomposition result for
F
(see e.g.
[
46

).

Lemma D.3
.

Let
0<\varepsilon<1/3
and
0\leq a<q\leq K^{\varepsilon}
with

coprime.
If
\xi\in\mathfrak{M}_{a,q}
, then

\displaystyle S(\xi)=\E_{u\in[q]}e(a\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(u)/q)\int_{0}^{K}e\mathopen{}\left(\mathopen{}\left(\xi-\frac{a}{q}\mathclose{}\right)\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z)\mathclose{}\right)dz+O(K^{2\varepsilon}).

We recall that the assumption
\varepsilon<1/3
ensures the disjointness of the major arcs.

In what follows, let

S(a,q):=\E_{u\in[q]}e(a\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(u)/q)=\E_{u\in{\mathbb{Z}}/q{\mathbb{Z}}}e(a\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(u)/q)

be the exponential sum appearing in Lemma
D.3
. The following lemma gives bounds on
S(a,q)
for various values of
a,q
. It is inspired by
[
4
, Lemma 5.3]
; the proof is almost identical, so we omit it.

Lemma D.4
.

Let
1\leq a<q
be coprime positive integers.
Then

\displaystyle|S(a,q)|\ll_{\eta,P}q^{\eta-1/d}\quad\textrm{for\; every}\quad\eta>0.

Moreover,
S(a,q)=0
whenever
\gcd(q,W)>1
.

Next, we upper-bound the integral appearing in Lemma
D.3
. It essentially follows from the van der Corput Lemma (e.g.,
[
41
, pp. 332]
) and we omit the proof.

Lemma D.5
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|\int_{0}^{K}e(\xi\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z))\;dz\mathclose{}\right|\ll_{P}1+|\xi|^{-1/d}V_{d}^{-1/d}.

Combining the previous three lemmas, we can finally upper-bound the contribution of the major arcs.

Lemma D.6

(Major arc control)
.

For
s>d
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1/10]
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{M}}\mathopen{}\left|S(\xi)\mathclose{}\right|^{2s}\;d\xi\ll_{P,s}\frac{K^{2s-d}}{V_{d}}

as long as
K\geq V_{d}^{2}
.

We recall that the parameter
\varepsilon
figures implicitly in the definition of the major arcs
\mathfrak{M}
; since the left-hand side of the inequality in the lemma is non-decreasing as
\varepsilon
increases, the important point is that we can take
\varepsilon
bounded away from
0
.

Proof.

We let all constants depend on
P,s
.
Applying Lemma
D.3
, integrating over all of the major arcs, and changing variables
\xi\mapsto\xi+a/q
on each
\mathfrak{M}_{a,q}
, we have

\int_{\mathfrak{M}}\mathopen{}\left|S(\xi)\mathclose{}\right|^{2s}\;d\xi=\Bigl(1_{(a,q)=(0,1)}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<q\leq K^{\varepsilon},\\
\gcd(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}|S(a,q)|^{2s}\Bigr)\cdot\int_{-K^{-d+\varepsilon}}^{K^{-d+\varepsilon}}\mathopen{}\left|\int_{0}^{K}e\mathopen{}\left({\xi}\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z)\mathclose{}\right)\,dz\mathclose{}\right|^{2s}\,d\xi\\
+O\Bigl(1_{(a,q)=(0,1)}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<q\leq K^{\varepsilon},\\
\gcd(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}1\Bigr)(K^{2s-1-d+3\varepsilon}).

We bound the terms in the expression above one by one, starting with the contribution of the sums
S(a,q)
. For every
\eta>0
, we can bound

\displaystyle 1_{(a,q)=(0,1)}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<q\leq K^{\varepsilon},\\
\gcd(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}|S(a,q)|^{2s}\ll_{\eta}1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<q\leq K^{\varepsilon},\\
\gcd(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}q^{\eta-2s/d}\ll_{\eta}1+K^{\varepsilon(\eta+2-2s/d)},

where the first inequality follows from an application of Lemma
D.4
with
2\eta s
in place of
\eta
, and the second bound follows from trivially estimating the number of
a
’s in
1\leq a<q
by
q
and then trivially bounding
q\leq K^{\varepsilon}
.
Importantly, this expression is
O(1)
as long as
s>d
and
\eta<2/d
. Since there are no further restrictions on the choice of
\eta
, we can take any
\eta
in the range above.

To bound the contribution of the integral, we split it over
\xi
at
K^{-d}/V_{d}
, obtaining

\int_{-K^{-d+\varepsilon}}^{K^{-d+\varepsilon}}\mathopen{}\left|\int_{0}^{K}e\mathopen{}\left(\xi\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z)\mathclose{}\right)dz\mathclose{}\right|^{2s}d\xi\\
\leq\int_{-K^{-d}/V_{d}}^{K^{-d}/V_{d}}K^{2s}\,d\xi+2\int_{K^{-d}/V_{d}}^{1}\mathopen{}\left|\int_{0}^{K}e\mathopen{}\left(\xi\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z)\mathclose{}\right)dz\mathclose{}\right|^{2s}\,d\xi.

The first term can be bounded trivially by
K^{2s-d}/V_{d}
. To bound the second term, we apply Lemma
D.5
, which gives

\displaystyle\int_{K^{-d}/V_{d}}^{1}\mathopen{}\left|\int_{0}^{K}e\mathopen{}\left(\xi\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z)\mathclose{}\right)dz\mathclose{}\right|^{2s}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\ll\int_{K^{-d}/V_{d}}^{1}|\xi|^{-2s/d}V_{d}^{-2s/d}d\xi\ll\frac{K^{2s-d}}{V_{d}},

where we use Lemma
D.5
to bound the integrand and the assumption
s>d
to ensure that the exponent is of the right order.

Lastly, the error term is at most

\displaystyle\ll\Bigl(1_{(a,q)=(0,1)}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a<q\leq K^{\varepsilon},\\
\gcd(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}1\Bigr)K^{2s-1-d+3\varepsilon}\ll K^{2s-1-d+5\varepsilon},

which can be bounded by
\ll K^{2s-d}/V_{d}
since
K\geq V_{d}^{2}
and
\varepsilon\leq 1/10
.
∎

We summarize the computations performed in this section in the result below.

Proposition D.7

(Even moment bound)
.

There exists
s_{1}(d)\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
C=C(d)>0
such that if
s\geq s_{1}(d)
and
N\geq V_{d}^{C}
, then

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}|S(\xi)|^{2s}\;d\xi\ll_{P,s}\frac{K^{2s}}{N}.

Proof.

Let
s_{1}(d)=\max(s_{0}(d),d+1)
, where
s_{0}
comes from Lemma
D.2
and
d+1
is the smallest possible
s
allowed by Lemma
D.6
. We fix
s>s_{1}(d)
and let all the implicit constants depend on
P,s
.
We split
{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
into major and minor arcs with
\varepsilon=1/10
.
Then we can use Lemma
D.2
to bound the minor arc contribution as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{m}}|S(\xi)|^{2s}\;d\xi\ll K^{2s-d-\varepsilon/C_{1}}

where

is the constant from Lemma

. Since

, this can be bounded by

as long as

∎

D.2.
Applications to Sárközy-type estimates

We are now ready to show that counting operators for Sárközy-type configurations are controlled by the major arcs of their arguments.

Proposition D.8
.

For every
C_{1}>0
there exists
C_{2}=C_{2}(C_{1},P)>0
such that the following holds. Let
\delta\in(0,1/10)
, let
V\leq W^{C_{1}}
be a nonnegative integer power of
W
, let
r\in[V]
, and let
N\geq C_{2}V_{d}^{C_{2}}\delta^{-C_{2}}
be an integer. Let
f_{0},f_{1}:{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be
1
-bounded functions supported on
[N]
. If

(38)

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|\sum_{x}\E_{z\in[K]}f_{0}(x)f_{1}(x+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z))\mathclose{}\right|\geq\delta N,

then for every
i=0,1
there exist phases
\xi_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
and positive integers
q_{i}\ll_{C_{1},P}\delta^{-O_{C_{1},P}(1)}
such that
\left\|q_{i}\xi_{i}\right\|_{{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}\ll_{C_{1},P}\delta^{-O_{C_{1},P}(1)}/N
and
|\widehat{f_{i}}(\xi_{i})|\gg_{C_{1},P}\delta^{O_{C_{1},P}(1)}N
.

Proof.

We fix
C_{1}>0
and let all constants depend on
P
.
We prove the bound only for
i=0
, since one can then deduce the result for
i=1
by shifting
x\mapsto x-\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z)
and exchanging the roles of
f_{0},f_{1}
. Our first goal is to show that the largeness of (
38
) implies the largeness of some Fourier coefficient of
f_{0}
, and then we use the fraction comparison argument to deduce that this Fourier coefficient must be a major arc.

Define the mutliset
E:=\{\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z):\;z\in[K]\}
, so that our assumption can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|\sum_{x,z}f_{0}(x)f_{1}(x+z)1_{E}(z)\mathclose{}\right|\geq\delta NK.

Note that
F=\widehat{1_{E}}
. Now, Fourier-expanding, extracting the largest Fourier coefficient, and applying the triple Hölder inequality (with exponents
\frac{2s}{s-1},2,2s
) followed by Parseval’s Identity,
we get

\displaystyle\delta NK

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\widehat{f_{0}}\right\|_{\infty}^{1/s}\cdot\int_{{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}|\widehat{f_{0}}(\xi)|^{1-1/s}\cdot|\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi)|\cdot|F(\xi)|\;d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\widehat{f_{0}}\right\|_{\infty}^{1/s}\cdot\left\|f_{0}\right\|^{1-1/s}_{2}\cdot\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{2}\cdot\mathopen{}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}|F(\xi)|^{2s}\;d\xi\mathclose{}\right)^{1/(2s)}

for any
s\geq 2
.
We can trivially bound
\left\|f_{0}\right\|^{1-1/s}_{2}\cdot\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{2}\leq N^{(2s-1)/(2s)}
, and Proposition
D.7
gives

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}|S(\xi)|^{2s}\;d\xi\ll\frac{K^{2s}}{N}

for any
s
sufficiently large in terms of
d
.
Combining all these bounds together, we get the bound
\left\|\widehat{f_{i}}\right\|_{\infty}\gg\delta^{O(1)}N
, i.e. we have shown that the Sárközy configuration is controlled by Fourier analysis.

Now, let
\tilde{f}_{0}=\E_{z\in{K}}\overline{f_{1}}(x+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z))
. Stashing, we can replace
f_{0}
by
\tilde{f}_{0}
in (
38
) at the cost of squaring
\delta
in the lower bound. Together with the just proved Fourier control, this gives us

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|\sum_{x}\E_{z\in[K]}\overline{f_{1}}(x+\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z))e(-\xi x)\mathclose{}\right|\gg\delta^{O(1)}N

for some
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
. Shifting
x\mapsto x-\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z)
, splitting the sums over
x
and
z
, and using
\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{1}\leq N
, we get that

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|\sum_{x}f_{1}(x)e(\xi x)\mathclose{}\right|\gg\delta^{O(1)}N\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathopen{}\left|\E_{z\in[K]}e(-\xi\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}(z))\mathclose{}\right|\gg\delta^{O(1)}.

It remains to show that
\xi
is a major arc. By Weyl’s inequality (e.g.
[
11
, Lemma 4.4]
), the second inequality can only hold if there exists a positive integer
q\ll\delta^{-O(1)}
for which
\left\|q\xi\widetilde{P}_{[r,V]}\right\|_{C^{\infty}[K]}\ll\delta^{-O(1)}
. The result then follows from Lemma
C.3
.
∎

Appendix E
Fourier uniformity of difference of weights

The purpose of this section is to show that the weight

\mathopen{}\left|N^{(d-1)/d}W_{d}^{1/d}\sum_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]}e(\theta\widetilde{P}(Vz+r^{\prime}))\mathclose{}\right|\ll\frac{N}{Vw^{c}}

unless there is some positive integer
q\ll w^{O(c)}
such that

(40)

\displaystyle\left\|q\theta\widetilde{P}(V\cdot+r^{\prime})\right\|_{C^{\infty}[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]}\ll w^{O(c)}.

Let
R(z):=q\theta(Vz+\widetilde{P}(r^{\prime}))
, so that
R\circ\widetilde{P}_{[r^{\prime},V]}(z)=q\theta\widetilde{P}(Vz+r^{\prime})
. Setting
T=N/V
and recalling that
V_{d}=W_{d}V^{d-1}
, we can write the estimate (
40
) as

\displaystyle\left\|R\circ\widetilde{P}_{[r^{\prime},V]}\right\|_{C^{\infty}[(T/V_{d})^{1/d}]}\ll w^{O(c)}.

Lemma
C.3
then gives

\displaystyle\left\|qV\theta\right\|_{{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}\ll\frac{Vw^{O(c)}}{N}.

Hence the
\tilde{\nu}
-contribution is
\ll N/(Vw^{c})
unless there is some positive integer
q\ll w^{O(c)}
such that
\left\|qV\theta\right\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\ll Vw^{O(c)}/N
.

Comparing the outputs of the previous two paragraphs, we see that the conclusion of the proposition holds in the “minor arc” case where there is no
q\ll w^{O(c)}
such that
\left\|qV\theta\right\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\ll Vw^{O(c)}/N
. Henceforth, we assume that we are in the “major arc” case where there is some
q\ll w^{O(c)}
such that
\left\|qV\theta\right\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\leq Vw^{O(c)}/N
. In this case, we write

V\theta=a/q+\theta^{*},

where
a,q
are coprime integers with
1\leq q\ll w^{O(c)}
, and

|\theta^{*}|\ll Vw^{O(c)}/N\leq(W_{d}/N)^{1-1/(4d)}V^{d-1/4}

(say) since
N
bounded from below be a sufficiently large power of
W
that is allowed to depend on
d
.
Since the implicit constant in the term
w^{O(c)}
depends only on
d
, choosing
c
sufficiently small (depending on
d
) guarantees that
q<w
.

First, assume that
q>1
. The
\nu
-contribution is
\ll N/(Vw^{c})
. To handle the
\tilde{\nu}
-contribution, we use Lemma
D.3
with
Q(z)=\widetilde{P}_{[r^{\prime},V]}(z)
,
K=(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}
, and
\varepsilon=1/4
to express

\sum_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]}e(\theta\widetilde{P}(Vz+r^{\prime}))\\
=e(\theta\widetilde{P}(r^{\prime}))\E_{u\in[q]}e((a/q)\widetilde{P}_{[r^{\prime},V]}(u))\int_{0}^{(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}}e(\theta^{*}\widetilde{P}_{[r^{\prime},V]}(x))\,dx\\
+O((N/W_{d})^{1/(2d)}V^{-1/2}).

Since
q
is smaller than
w
, it shares a prime factor with
W
, and so Lemma
D.4
tells us that the average
\E_{u\in[q]}e((a/q)\widetilde{P}_{[r^{\prime},V]}(u))
vanishes.
Hence

N^{(d-1)/d}W_{d}^{1/d}\sum_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]}e(\theta\widetilde{P}(Vz+r^{\prime}))\ll N/(Vw^{c})

(with room to spare) by the assumption that
N
is larger than a suitable power of
V
.

Finally, assume that
q=1
, in which case
a=0
and
\left\|V\theta\right\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\ll Vw^{O(c)}/N
. We will break the sum in the
\nu
-contribution into intervals and show that each one approximately cancels out with a single summand from the
\tilde{\nu}
-contribution. Since

\widetilde{P}_{[r^{\prime},V]}(z)-W_{d}V^{d-1}z^{d}\ll V^{O(1)}z^{d-1}

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}(r^{\prime})-r=Vu\quad\textrm{for some integer }|u|\ll W_{d}V^{d-1},

we have

\mathopen{}\left|\sum_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]}(e(\theta\widetilde{P}(Vz+r^{\prime}))-e(\theta(W_{d}V^{d}z^{d}+r)))\mathclose{}\right|\ll V^{O(1)}.

This lets us simplify the
\tilde{\nu}
-contribution by removing lower-order terms, and it suffices to show that

\left|N^{(d-1)/d}W_{d}^{1/d}e(\theta r)\sum_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]}e(\theta W_{d}V^{d}z^{d})\right.\\
\left.-d^{-1}\sum_{z\in[N/V]}(N/(Vz+r+1))^{(d-1)/d}e(\theta(Vz+r))\right|\ll\frac{N}{Vw^{c}}.

We break the second sum into intervals of the form

I_{z}=[A_{z},B_{z}]:=[W_{d}V^{d-1}z^{d},W_{d}V^{d-1}(z+1)^{d}-1]

with length
|I_{z}|=W_{d}V^{d-1}dz^{d-1}(1+O(z^{-1}))
for
z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]
. For
t\in I_{z}
, we bound

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|\mathopen{}\left(\frac{N}{Vt+r+1}\mathclose{}\right)^{(d-1)/d}-\mathopen{}\left(\frac{N}{VA_{z}}\mathclose{}\right)^{(d-1)/d}\mathclose{}\right|

\displaystyle\ll\mathopen{}\left|\frac{N}{Vt+r+1}-\frac{N}{VA_{z}}\mathclose{}\right|\cdot\mathopen{}\left(\frac{N}{VA_{z}}\mathclose{}\right)^{-1/d}

\displaystyle\leq\mathopen{}\left|\frac{VA_{z}-Vt-r-1}{Vt+r+1}\mathclose{}\right|\cdot\mathopen{}\left(\frac{N}{VA_{z}}\mathclose{}\right)^{(d-1)/d}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{|I_{z}|}{A_{z}}\cdot\mathopen{}\left(\frac{N}{VA_{z}}\mathclose{}\right)^{(d-1)/d}\ll z^{-1}\mathopen{}\left(\frac{N}{VA_{z}}\mathclose{}\right)^{(d-1)/d}

and

(41)

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\left|e(\theta\cdot Vt)-e(\theta\cdot VA_{z})\mathclose{}\right|\leq\left\|V\theta\right\|_{{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}}\cdot|I_{z}|\ll\frac{V^{O(1)}}{N^{1/d}}\ll N^{-1/(2d)}

as long as
N\gg V^{\Omega(1)}
. Summing over
t\in I_{z}
, we obtain the key estimate

\displaystyle d^{-1}\sum_{t\in I_{z}}(N/(Vt+r+1))^{(d-1)/d}e(\theta(Vt+r))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=e(\theta r)d^{-1}\sum_{t\in I_{z}}(N/(VA_{z}))^{(d-1)/d}e(\theta VA_{z})(1+O(N^{-1/2d}+z^{-1}))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=e(\theta r)W_{d}V^{d-1}z^{d-1}(N/(W_{d}V^{d}z^{d}))^{(d-1)/d}e(\theta W_{d}V^{d}z^{d}))(1+O(N^{-1/2d}+z^{-1}))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=e(\theta r)N^{(d-1)/d}W_{d}^{1/d}e(\theta W_{d}V^{d}z^{d})(1+O(N^{-1/2d}+z^{-1})).

The lemma follows by summing over
z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]
and noting that

\mathopen{}\left|\sum_{z\in[(N/W_{d})^{1/d}V^{-1}]}N^{(d-1)/d}W_{d}^{1/d}(N^{-1/2d}+z^{-1})\mathclose{}\right|\ll\frac{N}{Vw^{c}},

again with room to spare since
W
is sufficiently small compared to
N
.
∎

Appendix F
Supersaturation

The purpose of this short section is to prove a supersaturation result for corners. We apply Varnavides’ now-standard trick from
[
45
]
to Shkredov’s result on corners
[
39
]
.

Theorem F.1

(
[
39
]
)
.

There exists a constant
c>0
such that the following holds: If
A\subseteq[N]^{2}
has size
\delta N^{2}
for some
\delta\in(0,1/10)
and
N\geq\exp\exp(\delta^{-c})
, then
A
contains a nontrivial corner.

Proposition F.2
.

There exists a constant
c^{\prime}>0
such that the following holds: If
A\subseteq[N]^{2}
has size
\delta N^{2}
for some
\delta\in(0,1/10)
and
N\geq\exp\exp(\delta^{-c^{\prime}})
, then
A
contains at least
(\exp(-\exp(\delta^{-c^{\prime}})))N^{3}
nontrivial corners.

Proof.

Let
M:=\exp\exp((\delta/2)^{c})
, where
c
is the constant from Theorem
F.1
. For each
1\leq d\leq N/(3M)
and
{\textbf{u}}\in[-d(M-1),N-1]^{2}
, define the set

\lambda(d,{\textbf{u}}):=A\cap({\textbf{u}}+d\cdot[M]^{2}).

For fixed
d
, each element of
A
is contained in exactly
M^{2}
of the sets
\lambda(d,{\textbf{u}})
, so

\sum_{\textbf{u}}|\lambda(d,{\textbf{u}})|=M^{2}|A|=\delta M^{2}N^{2}.

Say that the pair
(d,{\textbf{u}})
is
good
if
|\lambda(d,{\textbf{u}})|\geq(\delta/2)M^{2}
, and let
X(d)
denote the number of
u
’s such that
(d,{\textbf{u}})
is good. We can bound

\delta M^{2}N^{2}=\sum_{\textbf{u}}|\lambda(d,{\textbf{u}})|\leq X(d)M^{2}+(N+d(M-1))^{2}\cdot(\delta/2)M^{2},

and so (for instance)

X(d)\geq\delta N^{2}-(\delta/2)(N+N/3)^{2}=(\delta/9)N^{2}.

Hence the total number of good pairs
(d,{\textbf{u}})
(for all
d
) is at least
(\delta/(27M))N^{3}
. Theorem
F.1
tells us that if
(d,{\textbf{u}})
is good, then
\lambda(d,{\textbf{u}})
contains a nontrivial corner. Finally, each nontrivial corner is contained in at most
M^{3}
such sets
\lambda(d,{\textbf{u}})
(there are at most
M
choice of
d
and then at most
M^{2}
choices of
u
), so we conclude that the number of nontrivial corners in
A
is at least

\frac{\delta}{27M^{4}}N^{3}\geq(\exp(-\exp(\delta^{-c^{\prime}})))N^{3}

with a suitable choice of
c^{\prime}>0
.
∎

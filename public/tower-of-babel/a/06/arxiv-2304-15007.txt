Newton’s second law as limit of variational problems
Edoardo Mainini and Danilo Percivale

Abstract
. We show that the solution of Cauchy problem for the classical ode m ​ 𝐲 ′′ = 𝐟 m\mathbf{y}^{\prime\prime}=\mathbf{f} can be obtained as limit of minimizers of exponentially weighted convex variational integrals. This complements the known results about weighted inertia-energy approach to Lagrangian mechanics and hyperbolic equations.



1.
Introduction and statement of the result

†
†
footnotetext:
​​​​​​​​​

2020 AMS Classification Numbers
: 49J45, 70H30.

Key words and phrases
: Calculus of Variations, Newton’s second law,
Weighted Variational Integrals.

Let
\mathbf{f}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{+};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
,
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
,
{\bf v}_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
,
m>0
. Let us consider the Cauchy problem

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&m{\mathbf{y}^{\prime\prime}}=\mathbf{f},\qquad t>0\\
&\\
&\mathbf{y}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0},\qquad{\mathbf{y}^{\prime}}(0)={\bf v}_{0}\end{array}\right.

governing the motion of a material point of mass
m
subject to the force field
\mathbf{f}
.
Our goal is to show that the solution to (
1.1
) is the limit as
h\to+\infty
of the minimizers of the following functionals defined on trajectories
\mathbf{y}:{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{N}

\displaystyle\frac{m}{2h^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\mathbf{y}^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{-ht}\,dt-\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathbf{f}_{h}(t)\cdot\mathbf{y}(t)e^{-ht}\,dt,\qquad h\in\mathbb{N},

subject to the same initial conditions,
as soon as
(\mathbf{f}_{h})_{h\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
is a sequence such that
\mathbf{f}_{h}\rightharpoonup\mathbf{f}
in
w^{*}-L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{+};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
as
h\to+\infty
.
More precisely, letting

\mathcal{A}:=\left\{{\bf v}\in W^{2,1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{+};{\mathbb{R}}^{N}):\int_{0}^{+\infty}|{\bf v}^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt<+\infty\right\}

and
\mathbf{f}_{h}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
for every
h\in\mathbb{N}
, we may define the rescaled energy functional (see also Lemma
2.3
below)

\mathcal{J}_{h}(\mathbf{u}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&\displaystyle\frac{m}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\mathbf{u}^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{2}\,e^{-t}\,dt-h^{-2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathbf{f}_{h}(h^{-1}t)\cdot\mathbf{u}(t)\,e^{-t}\,dt\quad\text{if}\ \mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{A}\\
&\\
&+\infty\quad\text{otherwise in}\ W^{2,1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{+};{\mathbb{R}}^{N}),\end{array}\right.

and we will prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every
h\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a unique solution
\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{h}
to the problem

min\{\mathcal{J}_{h}(\mathbf{u}):\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{A},\ \mathbf{u}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0},\ \mathbf{u}^{\prime}(0)=h^{-1}{\bf v}_{0}\}.

Moreover, if
\mathbf{f}_{h}\rightharpoonup\mathbf{f}
in
w^{*}-L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{+};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
as
h\to+\infty
, by setting
\overline{\mathbf{y}}_{h}(t):=\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{h}(ht)
we have
\overline{\mathbf{y}}_{h}\rightharpoonup\overline{\mathbf{y}}
in
w^{*}-W^{2,\infty}((0,T);{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
for every
T>0
,
where
\overline{\mathbf{y}}
is the unique solution on
{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
of problem (
1.1
).

A variational approach based on the minimization of
weighted inertia-energy
(WIE) functionals can be used for approximating large classes of initial value problems of the second order.
An example is the nonhomogeneous wave equation

w_{tt}=\Delta w+g\qquad\mbox{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.

Indeed, it has been shown in
[
7
]
that given
g\in L^{2}_{loc}((0,+\infty);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
,
\alpha\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
,
\beta\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, there exists a sequence
(g_{h})_{h\in\mathbb{N}}
converging to
g
in
L^{2}((0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
for every
T>0
such that the following properties hold. First, the WIE functional

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}\left\{\frac{1}{2}|u_{tt}(t,x)|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}h^{-2}|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}-h^{-2}g_{h}(h^{-1}t,x)u(t,x)\right\}\,dt\,dx

has, for every
h\in\mathbb{N}
, a unique minimizer
u_{h}

in the class of functions
u\in L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&\nabla u\in L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}),\qquad u^{\prime\prime}\in L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}),\\
&\\
&\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\!\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}e^{-t}\left\{|u_{tt}|^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\right\}\,dt\,dx<+\infty,\\
&\\
&u(0,x)=\alpha(x),\qquad u_{t}(0,x)=h^{-1}\beta(x).\\
\end{array}\right.

Second,
by setting
w_{h}(t,x):=u_{h}(ht,x)
,
the sequence
(w_{h})_{h\in\mathbb{N}}
converges
weakly in
H^{1}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
for every
T>0
to a function
w
which solves in the sense of distributions in
{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
the initial value problem

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&w_{tt}=\Delta w+g\\
&\\
&w(0,x)=\alpha(x),\qquad w_{t}(0,x)=\beta(x).\end{array}\right.

A similar result holds true for other classes of hyperbolic equations as shown in
[
5
,
TT2
,
LS2
]
. In particular it applies to the nonlinear wave equation
w_{tt}=\Delta w-\tfrac{p}{2}|w|^{p-2}w
,
p\geq 2
, as conjectured by De Giorgi
[
1
]
and first proven in
[
4
]
, see also
[
6
]
.
Let us mention that (the scalar version of) Theorem
1.1
is not a direct consequence of the above result from
[
7
]
, since one should apply the latter to constant-in-space forcing terms
g
and initial data
\alpha,\beta
, and since the approximating sequence
(g_{h})_{h\in\mathbb{N}}
in
[
7
]
is not arbitrary but obtained by means of a specific construction, not allowing for instance for the choice
g_{h}\equiv g
for every
h
.

Concerning the WIE approach for odes, let us mention its application in
[
3
]
for providing
a variational approach to Lagrangian mechanics, by considering an equation of the form

(1.3)

m\mathbf{y}^{\prime\prime}+\nabla U(\mathbf{y})=0,\qquad t>0

for given potential energy
U\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, bounded from below, and
m>0
. The main theorem of
[
3
]
proves indeed that solutions to the initial value problem for (
1.3
) can be approximated by rescaled minimizers, subject to the same initial conditions, of the functionals

\mathcal{G}_{h}({\bf v})=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}\left\{\frac{m}{2}|\mathbf{v}^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{2}+h^{-2}U(\mathbf{v}(t))\right\}\,dt,\qquad h\in\mathbb{N}.

It is worth noticing that, also in this case, Theorem
1.1
is not a consequence of the result from
[
3
]
since
the latter requires that the force field is conservative and independent of
t
.

We have already observed that in the scalar case problem (
1.1
) is a particular case of problem (
1.2
), obtained by taking constant initial data and letting the forcing term depend only on time. Let us also mention
another interpretation of (
1.1
)
from a continuum mechanics point of view. Indeed, Newton’s second law (
1.1
) governs the motion of the center of mass of a body occupying a reference configuration
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
. More in detail, let

\rho
be the mass density of the body and let

\mathbf{u}(t,{\bf x})
be the position of the material point
{\bf x}
at time
t
. If
\mathbb{T}
is the Cauchy stress tensor and
\mathbf{b}
is the body force field acting on
\Omega
, then the equation of motion, see for instance
[
2
]
,
takes the form

(1.4)

\rho\mathbf{u}_{tt}=\mathop{{\rm div}}\nolimits\mathbb{T}+\mathbf{b}\qquad\mbox{ in ${\mathbb{R}}^{+}\times\Omega.$}

Therefore, by integrating in
\Omega
both sides of (
1.4
), we formally get

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\rho\mathbf{u}\,d{\bf x}\right)=\int_{\Omega}\mathop{{\rm div}}\nolimits\mathbb{T}\,d{\bf x}+\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{b}\,d{\bf x}=\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathbb{T}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}+\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{b}\,d{\bf x}=:\mathbf{f}_{\Omega},\qquad t>0,

that is,

m_{\Omega}\,\mathbf{y}^{\prime\prime}=\mathbf{f}_{\Omega},\qquad t>0,

where
\mathbf{f}_{\Omega}=\mathbf{f}_{\Omega}(t)
is the total force acting on the body, accounting for surface and body forces,

m_{\Omega}=\int_{\Omega}\rho({\bf x})\,d{\bf x}

is the mass of the body and

\mathbf{y}(t)=m_{\Omega}^{-1}\int_{\Omega}\rho({\bf x})\,\mathbf{u}(t,{\bf x})\,d{\bf x}

is the position at time t of the center of mass of the body during the motion. Therefore Newton’s second law (
1.1
) can be viewed as the average in space
of the equation of motion (
1.4
).
In this perspective Theorem
1.1
can be seen as a result about the equation of motion in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
in the above average sense.

Let us finally stress that the methods that are described in this paper, here only devoted to the elementary problem (
1.1
), can be extended to nonlinear problems like
\mathbf{y}^{\prime\prime}=\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}G(t,\mathbf{y})
under suitable assumptions on
G
, but also to hyperbolic problems such as (
1.2
) allowing to get further results on these topics. In this perspective, we will develop our analysis in a forthcoming paper.

2.
Existence of minimizers

In this section we provide some preliminary results that we are going to use for proving Theorem
1.1
.
First of all, it is worth noticing that if
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{A}
then
\mathbf{u}\in W^{2,2}((0,T);{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
for every
T>0
hence both
\mathbf{u}(0)
and
\mathbf{u}^{\prime}(0)
are well defined. Moreover, if
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{A}
, by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\left|\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathbf{f}(h^{-1}t)\cdot\mathbf{u}(t)e^{-t}\,dt\right|\leq\|\mathbf{f}\|_{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\mathbf{u}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt\right)^{1/2}

and the integral in the left hand side is finite (see Lemma
2.1
below),
so that
\mathcal{J}_{h}(\mathbf{u})
is well-defined and finite. In fact, we have the following estimates

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{A}
. Then
e^{-t/2}\mathbf{u}\in L^{2}((0,+\infty);{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
,
e^{-t/2}\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\in L^{2}((0,+\infty);{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
and

(2.1)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\mathbf{u}^{\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt\leq 2|\mathbf{u}^{\prime}(0)|^{2}+4\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\mathbf{u}^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt,

(2.2)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\mathbf{u}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt\leq 2|\mathbf{u}(0)|^{2}+8|\mathbf{u}^{\prime}(0)|^{2}+16\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\mathbf{u}^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt.

Proof.

We have
\mathbf{u}\in AC([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\in AC([0,T];{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
for every
T>0
. Therefore
\frac{d}{dt}|\mathbf{u}(t)|^{2}=2\mathbf{u}(t)\cdot\mathbf{u}^{\prime}(t)
and
\frac{d}{dt}|\mathbf{u}^{\prime}(t)|^{2}=2\mathbf{u}^{\prime}(t)\cdot\mathbf{u}^{\prime\prime}(t)
for a.e.
t>0
. Moreover, given
T>0
we integrate by parts and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|\mathbf{u}^{\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt

\displaystyle=\left[-e^{-t}|\mathbf{u}^{\prime}(t)|^{2}\right]_{0}^{T}+2\int_{0}^{T}e^{-t/2}\mathbf{u}^{\prime}(t)\cdot\mathbf{u}^{\prime\prime}(t)e^{-t/2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq|\mathbf{u}^{\prime}(0)|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}|\mathbf{u}^{\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt+2\int_{0}^{T}|\mathbf{u}^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt,

where we have used Young inequality.
By letting
T\to+\infty
we get (
2.1
). The same computation entails

\int_{0}^{T}|\mathbf{u}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt\leq|\mathbf{u}(0)|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}|\mathbf{u}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt+2\int_{0}^{T}|\mathbf{u}^{\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt.

By letting
T\to+\infty
and by taking advantage of (
2.1
) we obtain (
2.2
).

The next lemma proves the first statement of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For every
h\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a unique solution to the problem

(2.3)

\min\{\mathcal{J}_{h}(\mathbf{u}):\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{A},\,\mathbf{u}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0},\mathbf{u}^{\prime}(0)=h^{-1}{\bf v}_{0}\}

Proof.

We first observe that
\mathcal{J}_{h}
is strictly convex and that the minimization set is convex. Therefore if a minimizer exists it is necessarily unique, so we are left to prove existence.
If
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{A}
is such that
\mathbf{u}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0},\ \mathbf{u}^{\prime}(0)=h^{-1}{\bf v}_{0}
, Lemma
2.1
entails

(2.4)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\mathbf{u}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt\leq 2|\mathbf{u}_{0}|^{2}+8h^{-2}|{\bf v}_{0}|^{2}+16\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\mathbf{u}^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt

and

(2.5)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\mathbf{u}^{\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt\leq 2h^{-2}|{\bf v}_{0}|^{2}+4\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\mathbf{u}^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt.

Let
(\mathbf{u}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a minimizing sequence for problem (
2.3
). Since
\mathbf{u}_{0}+h^{-1}t{\bf v}_{0}
is admissible for problem (
2.3
), we have for any large enough
k

\mathcal{J}_{h}(\mathbf{u}_{k})\leq\mathcal{J}_{h}(\mathbf{u}_{0}+h^{-1}t{\bf v}_{0})+1,

whence by (
2.4
), by Young and Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities, and by denoting with
C
various constants only depending on
\|\mathbf{f}_{h}\|_{\infty},h,\mathbf{u}_{0},{\bf v}_{0}
,
m
, we get

(2.6)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt\leq\frac{2}{m}h^{-2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathbf{f}_{h}(h^{-1}t)\cdot\mathbf{u}_{k}(t)e^{-t}\,dt

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{m}h^{-2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathbf{f}_{h}(h^{-1}t)\cdot(\mathbf{u}_{0}+h^{-1}t{\bf v}_{0})e^{-t}\,dt+\frac{2}{m}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{m}\|\mathbf{f}_{h}\|_{\infty}h^{-2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\mathbf{u}_{k}(t)|e^{-t}\,dt+C\leq\frac{2}{m}\|\mathbf{f}_{h}\|_{\infty}h^{-2}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\mathbf{u}_{k}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{32}\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\mathbf{u}_{k}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt+\frac{32}{m^{2}}h^{-4}\|\mathbf{f}_{h}\|^{2}_{\infty}+C\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt+C.

By taking into account of (
2.4
), (
2.5
), (
2.6
) we get that the sequence
(e^{-\frac{t}{2}}\mathbf{u}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is equibounded in

W^{2,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{+};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, so there exists
{\bf v}\in W^{2,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{+};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
such that up to extracting a subsequence there holds
e^{-\frac{t}{2}}\mathbf{u}_{k}\rightharpoonup{\bf v}
in
W^{2,2}({\mathbb{R}}^{+};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, hence
\mathbf{u}_{k}\rightharpoonup\mathbf{u}:=e^{\frac{t}{2}}{\bf v}

in
W^{2,2}((0,T);{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
for every
T>0
and
\mathbf{u}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}
,
\mathbf{u}^{\prime}(0)=h^{-1}{\bf v}_{0}
. Therefore we have for every
T>0

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt\geq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}|\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt\geq\int_{0}^{T}|\mathbf{u}^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt,

hence

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\mathbf{u}^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt=\sup_{T>0}\int_{0}^{T}|\mathbf{u}^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\mathbf{u}_{k}^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{-t}\,dt,

so eventually we find
\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{A}
,
and since

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{+\infty}h^{-2}\mathbf{f}_{h}(h^{-1}t)\cdot\mathbf{u}_{k}\,e^{-t}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}h^{-2}\mathbf{f}_{h}(h^{-1}t)\cdot{\bf v}\,e^{-t/2}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}h^{-2}\mathbf{f}_{h}(h^{-1}t)\cdot\mathbf{u}\,e^{-t}\,dt

we get

\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\mathcal{J}_{h}(\mathbf{u}_{k})\geq\mathcal{J}_{h}(\mathbf{u}).

We conclude that
\mathbf{u}
is solution to (
2.3
).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
h\in\mathbb{N}
. If
\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{h}
is the unique solution to (
2.3
), then
\overline{\mathbf{y}}_{h}(t):=\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{h}(ht)

is the unique minimizer of

\mathcal{F}_{h}(\mathbf{y}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&\displaystyle\frac{m}{2h^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\mathbf{y}^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{2}\,e^{-ht}\,dt-\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathbf{f}_{h}(t)\cdot\mathbf{y}(t)\,e^{-ht}\,dt\quad\text{if}\ \mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{A}_{h}\\
&\\
&+\infty\quad\text{otherwise in}\ W^{2,1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{+};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})\end{array}\right.

over
\mathcal{A}_{h}
, where

\mathcal{A}_{h}:=\left\{\mathbf{y}\in W^{2,1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{+};{\mathbb{R}}^{N}):\ \int_{0}^{+\infty}|\mathbf{y}^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{-ht}\,dt<+\infty,\ \mathbf{y}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0},\ \mathbf{y}^{\prime}(0)={\bf v}_{0}\right\}.

Proof.

Since
\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{h}\in\mathcal{A}
and
\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{h}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}
,
\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{\prime}(0)=h^{-1}{\bf v}_{0}
, we directly see that
\overline{\mathbf{y}}_{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{h}
and that
h^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{y}}_{h})=\mathcal{J}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{h})
. Moreover, if
\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{A}_{h}
, by setting
\mathbf{u}_{h}(t)=\mathbf{y}(h^{-1}t)
we get
\mathbf{u}_{h}\in\mathcal{A},\ \mathbf{u}_{h}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0},\ \mathbf{u}_{h}^{\prime}(0)=h^{-1}{\bf v}_{0}
and
h^{-1}\mathcal{F}_{h}(\mathbf{y})=\mathcal{J}_{h}(\mathbf{u}_{h})
. Therefore
\mathcal{F}_{h}(\overline{\mathbf{y}}_{h})\leq\mathcal{F}_{h}(\mathbf{y})
for every
\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{A}_{h}
and equality holds if and only if
\mathbf{y}=\overline{\mathbf{y}}_{h}
, as claimed.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Given
\overline{\mathbf{y}}_{h}
minimizing
\mathcal{F}_{h}
over
\mathcal{A}_{h}
, here we prove suitable boundedness estimates for the sequence
(\overline{\mathbf{y}}_{h})_{h\in\mathbb{N}}
, which is the main step towards the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
h\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\overline{\mathbf{y}}_{h}
as in
Lemma
2.3
. Then
{\overline{\mathbf{y}}}_{h}^{\prime\prime}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{+};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
and

\|{\overline{\mathbf{y}}}_{h}^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq m^{-1}\sup_{h\in\mathbb{N}}\|\mathbf{f}_{h}\|_{\infty}.

Moreover, the sequence
(\overline{\mathbf{y}}_{h})_{h\in\mathbb{N}}
is equibounded in
W^{2,\infty}((0,T);{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
for every
T>0
.

Proof.

Let
h\in\mathbb{N}
,
{\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$}}\in C_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{+};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
and let
\boldsymbol{\xi}
be the unique solution to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime\prime}=e^{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$}},\qquad t>0,\\
&\\
&\boldsymbol{\xi}(0)=\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime}(0)=0.\\
\end{array}\right.

By setting
{\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}}_{h}(t):=h^{-2}\boldsymbol{\xi}(ht)
we see that
{\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}}_{h}(0)={\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}}^{\prime}_{h}(0)=0
and that

\int_{0}^{+\infty}|{\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}}_{h}^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{2}e^{-ht}\,dt=h^{-1}\int_{0}^{+\infty}|{\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$}}(t)|^{2}e^{t}\,dt,

and the integral in the right hand side is finite since
{\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$}}\in C_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{+};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, thus we get
\overline{\mathbf{y}}_{h}+{\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}}_{h}\in\mathcal{A}_{h}
. The minimality of
\overline{\mathbf{y}}_{h}
entails the validity of the first order relation

(3.1)

mh^{-2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}{\overline{\mathbf{y}}_{h}^{\prime\prime}}(t)\cdot{\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}}_{h}^{\prime\prime}(t)\,e^{-ht}\,dt=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathbf{f}_{h}(t)\cdot{\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}}_{h}(t)e^{-ht}\,dt.

Since
\boldsymbol{\xi}(0)=0
, using integration by parts we have
for every
\nu>0
and every
\tau>0

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}|\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)|e^{-t}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\sqrt{|\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)|^{2}+\nu^{2}}e^{-t}\,dt=[-e^{-t}\sqrt{|\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)|^{2}+\nu^{2}}]_{0}^{\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\frac{\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime}(t)\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)}{\sqrt{|\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)|^{2}+\nu^{2}}}\,e^{-t}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\nu+\int_{0}^{\tau}|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime}(t)|e^{-t}\,dt,

and then by the arbitrariness of
\nu
and
\tau
, and by repeating the same argument taking into account that
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime}(0)=0
, we obtain

\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)|e^{-t}\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime}(t)|e^{-t}\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime\prime}(t)|e^{-t}\,dt.

Therefore,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathbf{f}_{h}(t)\cdot{\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}}_{h}(t)e^{-ht}\,dt\right|=h^{-3}\left|\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathbf{f}_{h}(h^{-1}s)\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}(s)e^{-s}\,ds\right|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq h^{-3}\|\mathbf{f}_{h}\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s}|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime\prime}(s)|\,ds=h^{-3}\|\mathbf{f}_{h}\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{+\infty}|{\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$}}(s)|\,ds.

We recall from Lemma
2.3
that
\overline{\mathbf{y}}_{h}(t)=\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{h}(ht)
, where
\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{h}
is the unique solution to (
2.3
).
Hence, by taking into account that

\displaystyle\displaystyle h^{-2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}{\overline{\mathbf{y}}_{h}^{\prime\prime}(t)}\cdot{\mbox{\boldmath$\psi$}}_{h}^{\prime\prime}(t)\,e^{-ht}\,dt=\int_{0}^{+\infty}{\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{\prime\prime}}(ht)\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime\prime}(ht)\,e^{-ht}\,dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle=h^{-1}\int_{0}^{+\infty}{\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{\prime\prime}(s)}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime\prime}(s)\,e^{-s}\,ds=h^{-1}\int_{0}^{+\infty}{\overline{\mathbf{u}}_{h}^{\prime\prime}(s)}\cdot{\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$}}(s)\,ds,

and by using
(
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we get

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{+\infty}{\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime\prime}_{h}}(s)\cdot{\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$}}(s)\,ds\right|\leq m^{-1}h^{-2}\|\mathbf{f}_{h}\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{+\infty}|{\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$}}|\,ds.

By the arbitrariness of
{\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$}}\in C_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{+};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, and since
C_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{+};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
is dense in
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{+};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, (
3.3
) entails

\|{\overline{\mathbf{u}}^{\prime\prime}_{h}}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{h^{2}m}\|\mathbf{f}_{h}\|_{\infty},

that is,

(3.4)

\|{\overline{\mathbf{y}}^{\prime\prime}_{h}}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{m}\|\mathbf{f}_{h}\|_{\infty}.

Eventually, we have for every
t\in[0,T]

{\overline{\mathbf{y}}^{\prime}_{h}}(t)={\bf v}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}{\overline{\mathbf{y}}^{\prime\prime}_{h}}(s)\,ds\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad{\overline{\mathbf{y}}}_{h}(t)=\mathbf{u}_{0}+t{\bf v}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s){\overline{\mathbf{y}}^{\prime\prime}_{h}}(s)\,ds,

hence (
3.4
) yields

(3.5)

\|{\overline{\mathbf{y}}}_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq|\mathbf{u}_{0}|+T|{\bf v}_{0}|+\frac{T^{2}}{2m}\|\mathbf{f}_{h}\|_{\infty}

and

(3.6)

\|{\overline{\mathbf{y}}^{\prime}_{h}}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq|{\bf v}_{0}|+\frac{T}{m}\|\mathbf{f}_{h}\|_{\infty}.

The estimates (
3.4
), (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) prove the result, since the sequence
(\mathbf{f}_{h})_{h\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{+};{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
For every
h\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\overline{\mathbf{y}}_{h}
be as in Lemma
2.3
. Let
T>0
and let
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
with
\mathop{{\rm spt}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\xi}\subset(0,T)
. Then by setting
{\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$}}_{h}(t):=\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)e^{ht}
and by taking into account the first order minimality condition (
3.1
) we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle\displaystyle-m\int_{0}^{T}{\overline{\mathbf{y}}^{\prime}_{h}}(t)\cdot(h^{-2}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)+2h^{-1}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime\prime}(t)+\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime}(t))\,dt

\displaystyle=-mh^{-2}\int_{0}^{T}{\overline{\mathbf{y}}^{\prime}_{h}}(t)\cdot({\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$}}^{\prime\prime}_{h}(t)\,e^{-ht})^{\prime}\,dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle=mh^{-2}\int_{0}^{T}{\overline{\mathbf{y}}^{\prime\prime}_{h}}(t)\cdot{\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$}}_{h}^{\prime\prime}(t)\,e^{-ht}\,dt=mh^{-2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}{\overline{\mathbf{y}}^{\prime\prime}_{h}}(t)\cdot{\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$}}_{h}^{\prime\prime}(t)\,e^{-ht}\,dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathbf{f}_{h}(t)\cdot{\mbox{\boldmath$\varphi$}}_{h}(t)e^{-ht}\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{f}_{h}(t)\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)\,dt.

By Lemma
3.1
there exists
\overline{\mathbf{y}}\in W^{2,\infty}((0,T);{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
such that, up to subsequences,
\overline{\mathbf{y}}_{h}\rightharpoonup\overline{{\bf x}}
in
w^{*}-W^{2,\infty}((0,T);{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
. Therefore we get
\overline{{\bf x}}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0},\ {\overline{{\bf x}}^{\prime}}(0)={\bf v}_{0}
and by taking into account (
3.7
) and the
w^{*}-L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{+})
convergence of
\mathbf{f}_{h}
to
\mathbf{f}

we obtain in the limit as
h\to+\infty

-m\int_{0}^{T}{\overline{\mathbf{y}}^{\prime}}(t)\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\prime}(t)\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{f}(t)\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}(t)\,dt.

The latter holds for every
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
with
\mathop{{\rm spt}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\xi}\subset(0,T)
, therefore
\overline{{\bf x}}
is the unique solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&m{\mathbf{y}^{\prime\prime}}=\mathbf{f}\\
&\\
&\mathbf{y}(0)=\mathbf{u}_{0},\qquad{\mathbf{y}^{\prime}}(0)={\bf v}_{0}\end{array}\right.

on
[0,T]
, hence the whole sequence
(\overline{\mathbf{y}}_{h})_{h\in\mathbb{N}}
is such that
\overline{\mathbf{y}}_{h}\rightharpoonup\overline{{\bf x}}
in
w^{*}-W^{2,\infty}((0,T);{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
. Since the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) has a unique solution
\overline{\mathbf{y}}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
and since
T
is arbitrary, we conclude that
\overline{\mathbf{y}}_{h}\rightharpoonup\overline{\mathbf{y}}
in
w^{*}-W^{2,\infty}((0,T);{\mathbb{R}}^{N})
as
h\to+\infty
for every
T>0
thus proving the theorem.

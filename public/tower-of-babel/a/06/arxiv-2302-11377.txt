Electroweak precision test of axion-like particles Preprint: KEK–TH–2497
Masashi Aiko, and Motoi Endo

Abstract
We study the contributions of an axion-like particle to the electroweak precision observables. The particle is assumed to couple with the standard model electroweak gauge bosons. We provide the formulae of the contributions valid for any mass of the axion-like particle. It is found that the effects arise not only via the oblique S S and U U parameters but also via radiative corrections to the gauge couplings. Besides, the decay of Z → a ​ γ Z\to a\gamma affects the total width of the Z Z boson. All of those contributions are considered simultaneously in the global fit analysis of the electroweak precision observables. Also, we discuss the recent CDF result of the W W -boson mass measurement. Since the model is tightly constrained by flavor and collider constraints, it is found that the discrepancy from the standard model prediction is solved only when the axion-like particle is heavier than 500 ​ GeV 500\,\mathrm{GeV} and its coupling to di-photon is suppressed.

Introduction

New light pseudoscalar particles are one of the most popular and simple extensions of the Standard Model (SM).
They are often motivated in models with spontaneous violations of global symmetries and are called axion-like particles (ALPs).
Their masses are generated by explicit small violations of the global symmetries, whereas their interactions are characterized by the symmetries.
They affect low-energy observables via the interactions with the SM particles.

In this paper, we revisit contributions to the electroweak precision observables (EWPOs).
The ALPs are assumed to be coupled primarily with the SM
{\rm U(1)}_{Y}
and
{\rm SU(2)}_{L}
gauge bosons, yielding ALP couplings with the photon (
\gamma
) and
Z

W

Then, the EWPOs are affected via vacuum polarizations of
\gamma
,
Z

Such contributions have been studied in Ref.
Bauer:2017ris

The authors argued that they are expressed by the oblique parameters,

Interestingly,

Also, the CDF II collaboration recently reported a new result of the

The result is not consistent with the SM prediction as well as the previous experimental values.
In Ref.
Yuan:2022cpw

It is noticed that the above studies ignored the other ALP contributions,

3
Electroweak precision test (EWPT)

New physics models as well as the SM have been tested by using the EWPOs.
The theoretical predictions are compared with the experimental data by performing global fit analyses.
In particular, the former values are determined within the SM by a set of input observables: the fine-structure constant
\alpha
, the Fermi coupling constant
G_{F}
, and the
Z
mass
m_{Z}
.

The ALP contributions arise in vacuum polarizations due to the ALP interactions with the EW gauge bosons.
Such contributions have been studied in terms of the oblique parameters,
S
,
T
, and
U

Bauer:2017ris
.
It has been known that the ALP generates
S
and
U
, while
T
is absent at least at the one-loop level.
Besides,
U
becomes as large as
S
, in contrast to the case in a wide class of new physics models.
However,
S
and
U
are not enough to represent the ALP contributions.
We introduce
three
additional variables,
\Delta_{Z}
,
\Delta_{W}
and
\Delta\alpha
.
#1
#1

#1

No specific parameters are assigned for these variables in Ref.
Hagiwara:1994pw
.

The parameters
\Delta_{Z}
and
\Delta_{W}
represent radiative corrections to the gauge coupling constants of the
Z
and
W
bosons, respectively.
Also,
\Delta\alpha
is those to the QED coupling.
Although they are negligible compared to
S
and
T
in a wide class of new physics models, this is not the case for the ALP models.
Moreover, the decay of
Z\to a\gamma
alters the
Z
-pole observables, such as the hadronic and total decay widths of the
Z
boson (see Eq. (
11
)).
Since the decay proceeds at the tree level, its impact on EWPOs is likely stronger than those via vacuum polarizations.
We provide their formulae as well as those for
S
,
T
, and
U
in this section.

Since the EW data, except for the recent CDF result of the
W
mass, are consistent with the SM predictions, the ALP contributions should be smaller than the SM ones.
Hence, we retain only the leading contributions from the ALP, which stem from interference terms between the ALP and SM transition amplitudes.
The ALP contributions are estimated up to the one-loop level.
Besides, since we are interested in the observables at the EW scale, we focus on the leading terms in the
m_{f}/m_{W}
expansions, where
m_{f}
and
m_{W}
are the SM fermion and
W
masses, respectively.

.
Similar to the charged currents, the leading contributions from new physics via the vacuum polarizations can be implemented via the effective coupling and weak mixing angles as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\rm NC}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{\bar{e}^{2}(s)}{s}Q_{i}Q_{j}+\frac{\bar{g}_{Z}^{2}(s)}{s-m_{Z}^{2}+is\Gamma_{Z}/m_{Z}}[I_{3}^{i}-Q_{i}\bar{s}^{2}(s)][I_{3}^{j}-Q_{j}\bar{s}^{2}(s)],

(31)

where the first term on the right-hand side has a pole at
s=0
, identified as the photon-exchange contribution, and the second one peaks at
s=m_{Z}^{2}
, corresponding to the
Z
amplitude.
Here,
s=k^{2}
is the squared momentum carried by the photon or
Z
boson.
The effective parameters are defined as

\displaystyle\bar{e}^{2}(k^{2})

\displaystyle=e^{2}\left[1-{\rm Re}\,\Pi_{T,\gamma}^{\gamma\gamma}(k^{2})\right],

(32)

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{Z}^{2}(k^{2})

\displaystyle=g_{Z}^{2}\left[1-{\rm Re}\,\Pi_{T,Z}^{ZZ}(k^{2})\right],

(33)

\displaystyle\bar{s}^{2}(k^{2})

\displaystyle=s_{W}^{2}\left[1+\frac{c_{W}}{s_{W}}{\rm Re}\,\Pi_{T,\gamma}^{Z\gamma}(k^{2})\right].

(34)

Since the ALP couples only with the gauge bosons, there are no contributions from vertex and box corrections.

The fine-structure constant,
\alpha
, is associated with the photon-exchange amplitude in the Thomson limit,
k^{2}\to 0
.
The effective coupling at a scale
k^{2}
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{\alpha}(k^{2})}-\frac{1}{\alpha}

\displaystyle=4\pi\,{\rm Re}\!\left[\Pi_{T,\gamma}^{QQ}(k^{2})-\Pi_{T,\gamma}^{QQ}(0)\right],

(35)

where
\bar{\alpha}(k^{2})=\bar{e}^{2}(k^{2})/4\pi
.
Hence, the leading ALP contribution at
k^{2}=m_{Z}^{2}
gives

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}(m_{Z}^{2})

\displaystyle=\alpha\left\{1-{\rm Re}\left[\Pi_{T,\gamma}^{\gamma\gamma}(m_{Z}^{2})-\Pi_{T,\gamma}^{\gamma\gamma}(0)\right]\right\}\equiv\alpha\left(1+\Delta\alpha\right).

(36)

On the other hand, the measured value of the Fermi coupling constant,
G_{F}
, is related to the effective coupling of the charged current amplitude at
k^{2}\simeq 0
as

\displaystyle G_{F}=\frac{\bar{g}^{2}(0)}{4\sqrt{2}\,m_{W}^{2}}.

(37)

Here, since we focus on radiative corrections from the ALP, vertex and box corrections are ignored.
It is noted that the
W
mass is not the input observable, but its theoretical value is determined from Eq. (
37
).

The effective couplings and weak mixing angle are expressed in terms of
\alpha
,
G_{F}
, and
m_{Z}
with the oblique parameters,
S
,
T
, and
U
, as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{g}_{Z}^{2}(0)}=\frac{1-\alpha T}{4\sqrt{2}\,G_{F}m_{Z}^{2}},

(38)

\displaystyle\bar{s}^{2}(m_{Z}^{2})=\frac{1}{2}-\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-\bar{\alpha}(m_{Z}^{2})\left[\frac{4\pi}{\bar{g}_{Z}^{2}(0)}+\frac{S}{4}\right]},

(39)

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi}{\bar{g}_{W}^{2}(0)}=\frac{\bar{s}^{2}(m_{Z}^{2})}{\bar{\alpha}(m_{Z}^{2})}-\frac{S+U}{4}.

(40)

In a given model, the oblique parameters are evaluated as

\displaystyle S=16\pi\,{\rm Re}\!\left[\Pi_{T,\gamma}^{3Q}(m_{Z}^{2})-\Pi_{T,Z}^{33}(0)\right],

(41)

\displaystyle T=\frac{4\sqrt{2}\,G_{F}}{\alpha}\,{\rm Re}\!\left[\Pi_{T}^{33}(0)-\Pi_{T}^{11}(0)\right],

(42)

\displaystyle U=16\pi\,{\rm Re}\!\left[\Pi_{T,Z}^{33}(0)-\Pi_{T,W}^{11}(0)\right].

(43)

Thus, the leading contributions from new physics are shown as

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{Z}^{2}(0)

\displaystyle=g_{Z}^{2}\left(1+\alpha T\right),

(44)

\displaystyle\bar{s}^{2}(m_{Z}^{2})

\displaystyle=s_{W}^{2}\left[1+\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{c_{W}^{2}-s_{W}^{2}}\left(\Delta\alpha-\alpha T\right)+\frac{\alpha S}{4s_{W}^{2}(c_{W}^{2}-s_{W}^{2})}\right],

(45)

\displaystyle\bar{g}^{2}(0)

\displaystyle=g^{2}\left[1-\frac{s_{W}^{2}\Delta\alpha}{c_{W}^{2}-s_{W}^{2}}-\frac{\alpha S}{2(c_{W}^{2}-s_{W}^{2})}+\frac{c_{W}^{2}\alpha T}{c_{W}^{2}-s_{W}^{2}}+\frac{\alpha U}{4s_{W}^{2}}\right].

(46)

It is noticed that the
Z
- and
W
-pole observables, where the gauge bosons carry
k^{2}=m_{Z}^{2}
and
m_{W}^{2}
respectively, require the effective couplings,
\bar{g}_{Z}^{2}(m_{Z}^{2})
and
\bar{g}^{2}(m_{W}^{2})
.
From Eqs. (
29
) and (
33
), they are related to
\bar{g}_{Z}^{2}(0)
and
\bar{g}^{2}(0)
as

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{Z}^{2}(m_{Z}^{2})

\displaystyle=\bar{g}_{Z}^{2}(0)\left\{1-{\rm Re}\left[\Pi_{T,Z}^{ZZ}(m_{Z}^{2})-\Pi_{T,Z}^{ZZ}(0)\right]\right\}\equiv g_{Z}^{2}(0)\left(1+\Delta_{Z}\right),

(47)

\displaystyle\bar{g}^{2}(m_{W}^{2})

\displaystyle=\bar{g}^{2}(0)\left\{1-{\rm Re}\left[\Pi_{T,W}^{WW}(m_{W}^{2})-\Pi_{T,W}^{WW}(0)\right]\right\}\equiv g^{2}(0)\left(1+\Delta_{W}\right).

(48)

Consequently, the EWPOs are evaluated theoretically in terms of
\Delta_{Z}
,
\Delta_{W}
, and
\Delta\alpha
as well as
S
,
T
, and
U
(see also Sec.
3.3
).

3.2
ALP contributions

In Eq. (
5
), the ALP couples with
\gamma
,
Z
, and
W
.
In the
R_{\xi}
gauge, its contributions to the vacuum polarizations at the one-loop level are derived as

\displaystyle\Pi_{T}^{WW}(k^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{288\pi^{2}}g_{aWW}^{2}F(k^{2};a,W),

(49)

\displaystyle\Pi_{T}^{\gamma\gamma}(k^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{288\pi^{2}}\left[g_{a\gamma\gamma}^{2}F(k^{2};a,\gamma)+g_{aZ\gamma}^{2}F(k^{2};a,Z)\right],

(50)

\displaystyle\Pi_{T}^{Z\gamma}(k^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{288\pi^{2}}\left[g_{a\gamma\gamma}g_{aZ\gamma}F(k^{2};a,\gamma)+g_{aZ\gamma}g_{aZZ}F(k^{2};a,Z)\right],

(51)

\displaystyle\Pi_{T}^{ZZ}(k^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{288\pi^{2}}\left[g_{aZ\gamma}^{2}F(k^{2};a,\gamma)+g_{aZZ}^{2}F(k^{2};a,Z)\right].

(52)

These results are valid for any ALP mass.
The loop function is defined as

\displaystyle F(k^{2};a,V)

\displaystyle=3\left[k^{2}-(m_{a}+m_{V})^{2}\right]\left[k^{2}-(m_{a}-m_{V})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\left[B_{0}(k^{2};m_{a},m_{V})-B_{0}(0;m_{a},m_{V})\right]

\displaystyle\quad-3k^{2}\left[A_{0}(m_{a})+A_{0}(m_{V})+(2m_{a}^{2}+2m_{V}^{2}-k^{2})B_{0}(0;m_{a},m_{V})\right]

\displaystyle\quad+7k^{2}(3m_{a}^{2}+3m_{V}^{2}-k^{2}),

(53)

where
A_{0}(x)
and
B_{0}(k^{2};x,y)
are the Passarino-Veltman functions
Passarino:1978jh
and shown explicitly in Appendix
A
.
It is noticed that the results are independent of the gauge parameters.
Then, the ALP contributions to
S
,
T
, and
U
are given by

\displaystyle\alpha S

\displaystyle=\frac{2c_{W}^{2}s_{W}^{2}}{9\pi^{2}m_{Z}^{2}}\frac{c_{WW}c_{BB}}{f_{a}^{2}}\quantity[F(m_{Z}^{2}; a, \gamma)-F(m_{Z}^{2}; a, Z)],

(54)

\displaystyle\alpha T

\displaystyle=0,

(55)

\displaystyle\alpha U

\displaystyle=\frac{2s_{W}^{4}}{9\pi^{2}m_{Z}^{2}}\frac{c_{WW}^{2}}{f_{a}^{2}}\quantity[F(m_{Z}^{2}; a, \gamma)+\frac{c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}F(m_{Z}^{2}; a, Z)-\frac{1}{s_{W}^{2}c_{W}^{2}}F(m_{W}^{2}; a, W)].

(56)

It is noticed that the ALP does not contribute to
T
.
On the other hand, the contributions to
\Delta\alpha
,
\Delta_{Z}
, and
\Delta_{W}
are given by

\displaystyle\Delta\alpha

\displaystyle=-\frac{g_{a\gamma\gamma}^{2}}{288\pi^{2}}\quantity[\frac{F(m_{Z}^{2};a,\gamma)}{m_{Z}^{2}}-F^{\prime}(0; a,\gamma)]-\frac{g_{aZ\gamma}^{2}}{288\pi^{2}}\quantity[\frac{F(m_{Z}^{2};a,Z)}{m_{Z}^{2}}-F^{\prime}(0; a,Z)],

(57)

\displaystyle\Delta_{Z}

\displaystyle=\frac{g_{aZ\gamma}^{2}}{288\pi^{2}}\quantity[\frac{F(m_{Z}^{2};a,\gamma)}{m_{Z}^{2}}-F^{\prime}(m_{Z}^{2}; a,\gamma)]+\frac{g_{aZZ}^{2}}{288\pi^{2}}\quantity[\frac{F(m_{Z}^{2};a,Z)}{m_{Z}^{2}}-F^{\prime}(m_{Z}^{2}; a,Z)],

(58)

\displaystyle\Delta_{W}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{288\pi^{2}}g_{aWW}^{2}\quantity[\frac{F(m_{W}^{2};a,W)}{m_{W}^{2}}-F^{\prime}(m_{W}^{2};a,W)],

(59)

where
F^{\prime}(k^{2};a,V)
is a derivative of
F(k^{2};a,V)
and expressed explicitly as

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(k^{2};a,V)

\displaystyle=6\left(k^{2}-m_{a}^{2}-m_{V}^{2}\right)\left[B_{0}(k^{2};m_{a},m_{V})-B_{0}(0;m_{a},m_{V})\right]

\displaystyle\quad+3\left[k^{2}-(m_{a}+m_{V})^{2}\right]\left[k^{2}-(m_{a}-m_{V})^{2}\right]B_{0}^{\prime}(k^{2};m_{a},m_{V})

\displaystyle\quad-3\left[A_{0}(m_{a})+A_{0}(m_{V})+(2m_{a}^{2}+2m_{V}^{2}-k^{2})B_{0}(0;m_{a},m_{V})\right]

\displaystyle\quad+7(3m_{a}^{2}+3m_{V}^{2}-2k^{2}).

(60)

The relation between
(c_{BB},c_{WW})
and
(g_{a\gamma\gamma},g_{aZ\gamma},g_{aZZ})
is found in Eqs. (
7
)–(
9
).
It is noticed that
\Delta\alpha
,
\Delta_{Z}
, and
\Delta_{W}
can be as large as
\alpha S
and
\alpha U
, and thus, must not be neglected.

Let us show the above results in the light ALP-mass limit,
m_{a}\ll m_{V}\,(V=Z,W)
.
They are obtained as

\displaystyle\alpha S

\displaystyle=-\frac{2c_{W}^{2}s_{W}^{2}m_{Z}^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\frac{c_{WW}c_{BB}}{f_{a}^{2}}\quantity(\ln{\frac{m_{Z}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}}+1),

(61)

\displaystyle\alpha U

\displaystyle=-\frac{2s_{W}^{4}m_{Z}^{2}}{3\pi^{2}}\frac{c_{WW}^{2}}{f_{a}^{2}}\quantity(\ln{\frac{m_{Z}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}}+\frac{1}{3}+\frac{2c_{W}^{2}}{s_{W}^{2}}\ln{\frac{m_{W}^{2}}{m_{Z}^{2}}}),

(62)

\displaystyle\Delta\alpha

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{Z}^{2}}{96\pi^{2}}\quantity[
g_{a\gamma\gamma}^{2}\qty(\ln{\frac{m_{Z}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}}+\frac{11}{3})
+g_{aZ\gamma}^{2}\qty(\ln{\frac{m_{Z}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}}+\frac{11}{6})],

(63)

\displaystyle\Delta_{Z}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{Z}^{2}}{96\pi^{2}}\quantity(g_{aZ\gamma}^{2}+g_{aZZ}^{2})\quantity(\ln{\frac{m_{Z}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}}+\frac{4}{3}),

(64)

\displaystyle\Delta_{W}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{W}^{2}}{96\pi^{2}}g_{aWW}^{2}\quantity(\ln{\frac{m_{W}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}}+\frac{4}{3}).

(65)

The results for
S
,
T
,
U
, and
\Delta\alpha
are consistent with those in Ref.
Bauer:2017ris
.

Let us comment on the ALP contributions,
U
,
\Delta\alpha
,
\Delta_{Z}
, and
\Delta_{W}
.
In a wide class of new physics models in a high-energy scale, they are suppressed compared to
S
and
T
.
However, this is not the case for the ALP models.
This is because the ALP contributions to the vacuum polarizations vanish at
k^{2}=0
(see Eq. (
53
)).
Then, the leading contribution to
S
arises from the first-order derivative of the vacuum polarization, which is comparable to
U
,
\Delta\alpha
,
\Delta_{Z}
, and
\Delta_{W}
.

3.3
Observables

The ALP contributions to the
Z
-pole observables are evaluated by the last term of the transition amplitude in Eq. (
31
).
It is noticed that this term has the same form as the tree-level one except that the coupling and weak mixing are replaced by the effective ones.
Hence, the ALP contributions are represented in terms of the following effective charges of the
Z
coupling,

\displaystyle g_{V,f}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\rho_{Z}}\left[I_{3}^{f}-2\,Q_{f}\bar{s}^{2}(m_{Z}^{2})\right]\equiv\hat{g}_{V,f}+\Delta g_{V,f},

(66)

\displaystyle g_{A,f}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\rho_{Z}}\,I_{3}^{f}\equiv\hat{g}_{A,f}+\Delta g_{A,f},

(67)

with
\hat{g}_{V,f}=I_{3}^{f}-2\,Q_{f}s_{W}^{2}
and
\hat{g}_{A,f}=I_{3}^{f}
.
Note that the effective parameters should be estimated at
k^{2}=m_{Z}^{2}
.
Here,
\rho_{Z}
is defined as

\displaystyle\rho_{Z}=\bar{g}_{Z}^{2}(m_{Z}^{2})/g_{Z}^{2}=1+\alpha T+\Delta_{Z}.

(68)

Here and hereafter, variables with a hat
(\,\hat{}\,)
,
e.g.
,
\hat{g}_{V,f}
and
\hat{g}_{A,f}
, represent the SM contributions at the tree level, and those with
\Delta
such as
\Delta g_{V,f}
and
\Delta g_{A,f}
denote the ALP corrections.
On the other hand, those with a subscript “SM” show the SM predictions including radiative corrections.

When we evaluate the EWPOs, the ALP corrections are dominated by interference terms between the ALP and SM transition amplitudes.
In particular, it is sufficient for the latter to be evaluated at the tree level when we focus on the leading ALP contributions.
Hence, by expressing the partial decay width for
Z\to f\bar{f}
as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{f}=(\Gamma_{f})_{\rm SM}+\Delta\Gamma_{f},

(69)

the SM contribution at the tree level,
\hat{\Gamma}_{f}
, is given by

\displaystyle\hat{\Gamma}_{f}=N_{C}^{f}\frac{G_{F}m_{Z}^{3}}{6\sqrt{2}\pi}\left[\left(\hat{g}_{V,f}\right)^{2}+\left(\hat{g}_{A,f}\right)^{2}\right],

(70)

and
\Delta\Gamma_{f}
is obtained as

\displaystyle\Delta\Gamma_{f}=N_{C}^{f}\frac{G_{F}m_{Z}^{3}}{3\sqrt{2}\pi}\left[\left(\hat{g}_{V,f}\right)^{2}+\left(\hat{g}_{A,f}\right)^{2}\right]\quantity(\frac{\Delta g_{V, f}}{\hat g_{V, f}}+\frac{\Delta g_{A, f}}{\hat g_{A, f}}).

(71)

The radiative corrections to the SM value will be mentioned in Sec.
3.4
.

When
m_{a}
is smaller than
m_{Z}
, the decay channel
Z\to a\gamma
is open.
Its partial decay width is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{a\gamma}\equiv\Gamma(Z\to a\gamma)=\frac{m_{Z}^{3}}{96\pi}g_{aZ\gamma}^{2}\quantity(1-\frac{m_{a}^{2}}{m_{Z}^{2}})^{3}.

(72)

Then, the hadronic and total decay widths of the
Z
boson are modified as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\rm had}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{u}+\Gamma_{d}+\Gamma_{c}+\Gamma_{s}+\Gamma_{b}\equiv(\Gamma_{\rm had})_{\rm SM}+\Delta\Gamma_{\rm had},

(73)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{Z}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{e}+\Gamma_{\mu}+\Gamma_{\tau}+3\Gamma_{\nu}+\Gamma_{\rm had}+\Gamma_{a\gamma}\equiv(\Gamma_{Z})_{\rm SM}+\Delta\Gamma_{Z},

(74)

where
\Delta\Gamma_{{\rm had},Z}
are the sums of
\Delta\Gamma_{f}
.
It is noticed that
\Gamma_{a\gamma}
appears in
\Gamma_{Z}
and is generated at the tree level with
g_{aZ\gamma}
.
Hence, its impact on the EWPO global fit is likely stronger than those via vacuum polarizations.

The other
Z
-pole observables relevant for the following analysis are represented explicitly as follows.
First of all, the hadronic cross-section is shown as

\displaystyle\sigma_{\rm had}^{0}

\displaystyle=(\sigma_{\rm had}^{0})_{\rm SM}+\frac{12\pi}{m_{Z}^{2}}\frac{\hat{\Gamma}_{e}\hat{\Gamma}_{\rm had}}{\hat{\Gamma}_{Z}^{2}}\quantity(
\frac{\Delta\Gamma_{e}}{\hat\Gamma_{e}}
+\frac{\Delta\Gamma_{\rm had}}{\hat\Gamma_{\rm had}}
-2\frac{\Delta\Gamma_{Z}}{\hat\Gamma_{Z}}
).

(75)

Next, the ratios of the partial decay widths become

\displaystyle R^{0}_{\ell}

\displaystyle=(R^{0}_{\ell})_{\rm SM}+\frac{\hat{\Gamma}_{\rm had}}{\hat{\Gamma}_{\ell}}\quantity(
\frac{\Delta\Gamma_{\rm had}}{\hat\Gamma_{\rm had}}
-\frac{\Delta\Gamma_{\ell}}{\hat\Gamma_{\ell}}
),

(76)

\displaystyle R^{0}_{q}

\displaystyle=(R^{0}_{q})_{\rm SM}+\frac{\hat{\Gamma}_{q}}{\hat{\Gamma}_{\rm had}}\quantity(
\frac{\Delta\Gamma_{q}}{\hat\Gamma_{q}}
-\frac{\Delta\Gamma_{\rm had}}{\hat\Gamma_{\rm had}}
),

(77)

where
\ell
denotes the SM leptons.
Also,
\sin^{2}\theta^{f}_{\rm eff}
is given by

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\theta^{f}_{\rm eff}

\displaystyle=(\sin^{2}\theta^{f}_{\rm eff})_{\rm SM}-\frac{1}{4|Q_{f}|}\frac{\hat{g}_{V,f}}{\hat{g}_{A,f}}\left(\frac{\Delta g_{V,f}}{\hat{g}_{V,f}}-\frac{\Delta g_{A,f}}{\hat{g}_{A,f}}\right).

(78)

The left-right asymmetry is shown as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{f}

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{A}_{f})_{\rm SM}+\mathcal{\hat{A}}_{f}\quantity(1-\frac{\hat g_{V,f}}{\hat g_{A,f}}\mathcal{\hat A}_f)\quantity( \frac{\Delta g_{V,f}}{\hat g_{V,f}} -\frac{\Delta g_{A,f}}{\hat g_{A,f}}).

(79)

Here, the SM contribution at the tree level is

\displaystyle\mathcal{\hat{A}}_{f}=\frac{2\hat{g}_{V,f}/\hat{g}_{A,f}}{1+(\hat{g}_{V,f}/\hat{g}_{A,f})^{2}}.

(80)

Finally, the forward-backward asymmetry is given by

\displaystyle A^{0}_{\rm FB}=(A^{0}_{\rm FB})_{\rm SM}+\frac{3}{4}\mathcal{\hat{A}}_{e}\mathcal{\hat{A}}_{f}\quantity(
\frac{\Delta\mathcal{A}_e}{\mathcal{\hat A}_e}
+\frac{\Delta\mathcal{A}_f}{\mathcal{\hat A}_f}
).

(81)

On the other hand, the mass and decay widths of the
W
boson are also evaluated theoretically.
From Eqs. (
37
) and (
46
), the mass is obtained as

\displaystyle m_{W}^{2}

\displaystyle=(m_{W}^{2})_{\rm SM}+\frac{\alpha c_{W}^{2}m_{Z}^{2}}{c_{W}^{2}-s_{W}^{2}}\left[-s_{W}^{2}\frac{\Delta\alpha}{\alpha}-\frac{S}{2}+c_{W}^{2}T+\frac{c_{W}^{2}-s_{W}^{2}}{4s_{W}^{2}}U\right]

\displaystyle\equiv(m_{W}^{2})_{\rm SM}+\Delta m_{W}^{2}.

(82)

Then, the
W
partial decay width becomes

\displaystyle\Gamma(W\to f_{i}f_{j})

\displaystyle=[(\Gamma_{W})_{ij}]_{\rm SM}+(\hat{\Gamma}_{W})_{ij}\bigg[\frac{3\Delta m_{W}^{2}}{2c_{W}^{2}m_{Z}^{2}}+\Delta_{W}\bigg].

(83)

Here,
\Delta_{W}
arises because the
W
coupling is estimated at
k^{2}=m_{W}^{2}
.
The SM prediction at the tree level is expressed as

\displaystyle(\hat{\Gamma}_{W})_{ij}

\displaystyle=N_{c}^{f}|V_{ij}|^{2}\frac{g^{2}}{48\pi}m_{W}.

(84)

where
V_{ij}
is the CKM matrix when
i,j
are quarks, while it is the identity matrix for leptons.

As a test of the ALP models, we perform a global fit analysis on the EWPOs.
A likelihood function is defined by a multivariate Gaussian,
-2\ln L=(\mathbf{y}-{\bf\it\mu})^{T}V^{-1}(\mathbf{y}-{\bf\it\mu})
, where
\mathbf{y}
is a vector of the measured quantities,
{\bf\it\mu}
is the corresponding theoretical predictions, and
V
is the covariance matrix.

In Table
1
, we summarize the measured values of the SM input and the EWPOs.
The covariance matrices are provided in the references for those listed in the right column.
The other SM input parameters, such as
G_{F}
,
\alpha
, and the masses of the light SM fermions, are fixed to be the observed central values
ParticleDataGroup:2022pth
.

In the table, we show two results of the
W
mass
m_{W}

ParticleDataGroup:2022pth
and a value combined with the recent CDF result, for which we adopt the averaged value in Ref.
deBlas:2022hdk
.
They will be denoted by
m_{W}^{\rm PDG}
and
m_{W}^{\rm CDF}
, respectively.
In the following analysis, we study both cases in the ALP models.
On the other hand, the SM prediction of
m_{W}
has been evaluated at the two-loop level

.

\delta m_{W}=0\pm 4\,\mathrm{MeV}
(cf., Ref.

6
Conclusions and discussion

We have studied the EWPOs in the ALP model.
The ALP is assumed to couple with the SM
{\rm SU(2)}_{L}
and the
{\rm U(1)}_{Y}
gauge bosons.
We have provided the formulae of the ALP contributions to the EWPOs valid for any ALP mass and shown that those to
\Delta\alpha
,
\Delta_{Z}
, and
\Delta_{W}
as well as the oblique parameter
U
can be comparable to
S
(
T=0
in the ALP model).
Furthermore, the decay of
Z\to a\gamma
contributes to the total width of the
Z
-boson decay significantly.

We have analyzed the probability likelihood generated from the ALP contributions by performing the global fit to the EWPOs and compared the results with the flavor and collider constraints.
Because of
Z\to a\gamma
, the ALP contributions to the EWPOs are suppressed as long as the ALP is lighter than the
Z
boson.
Besides, since the experimental constraints are tight, the EWPT regions are widely excluded already if the ALP is not so heavy.
Also, even when the ALP is heavy, the parameter regions are limited as long as the ALP is coupled sizably with a pair of photons.
Therefore, a heavy ALP with interactions satisfying
g_{a\gamma\gamma}\sim 0
is preferred to improve the global fit compared to the SM case.

In the numerical analysis, we have studied both of the cases when the recent CDF result of the
W
mass measurement is included and excluded in the experimental data.
If the PDG value adopted,
i.e.
, the CDF result is neglected in the
W
mass average, the ALP is favored to be heavier than about
160\,\mathrm{GeV}
with satisfying
g_{a\gamma\gamma}\sim 0
.
On the other hand, if the recent CDF result, which is inconsistent with the SM prediction, is included, it has been concluded that the ALP model can solve the tension only for
m_{a}>500\,\mathrm{GeV}
with
g_{a\gamma\gamma}\sim 0
.

Let us comment on contributions from higher-dimensional operators that are not included in the ALP Lagrangian (
1
).
They are likely to be non-negligible when
m_{a}
is comparable to
f_{a}
.
The higher-dimensional operators should depend on a UV theory of the ALP model.
The theory also determines the cutoff scale, which has been set as a model parameter in our analysis.
Since we have set
f_{a}=1\,\mathrm{TeV}
, the results might be altered especially around
m_{a}\sim 1\,\mathrm{TeV}
.

We have argued that the EWPOs are sensitive to the ALP models.
In particular, if the recent CDF result of the
W
mass measurement would be confirmed in future experiments, the model could provide an attractive solution.
Although the model parameters are restricted by the experimental constraints, the model setup for solving the tension has not been explored sufficiently by colliders such as the LHC.
Therefore, further collider studies would be helpful to establish the scenario.

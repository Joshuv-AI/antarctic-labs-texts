On the continuity of topological entropy of certain partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms
Weisheng Wu

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider certain partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms with center of arbitrary dimension and obtain continuity properties of the topological entropy under C 1 C^{1} perturbations. The systems considered have subexponential growth in the center direction and uniform exponential growth along the unstable foliation. Our result applies to partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms which are Lyapunov stable in the center direction. It applies to another important class of systems which do have subexponential growth in the center direction, for which we develop a technique to use exponential mixing property of the systems to get uniform distribution of unstable manifolds. A primary example is the translations on homogenous spaces which may have center of arbitrary dimension and of polynomial orbit growth.



1.
Introduction

Entropy is one of the most important invariants of a dynamical system which expresses the amount of “unpredictability” in the system. One of the central subjects in dynamical systems is to investigate the robust properties of the system under perturbations. Specifically, we are addressing in this paper the problem of the continuity properties of the topological entropy of partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms under
C^{1}
perturbations.

The concept of partial hyperbolicity was proposed independently by Brin-Pesin and Pugh-Shub in 1970s, which is a natural generalization of uniform hyperbolicity. The structural stability of uniformly hyperbolic diffeomorphisms implies the topological entropy is locally constant near such systems in the
C^{1}
topology. For systems beyond uniform hyperbolicity, we first briefly survey some existing results from which we can see the key ingredients in the continuity of topological entropy.

The regularity of diffeomorphisms and the existence of homoclinic tangences play important roles in the upper semicontinuity of the topological entropy:

Let us focus on the topological entropy of
C^{1}
partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms. As one can see from above, most known results are established for partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms with
1
-D or
2
-D center. In
[
46
]
, the authors conjectured the topological entropy is continuous on the space of
C^{1}
partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms with
1
-D center. Indeed, the conjecture is true for all the known examples. In contrast, when center has dimension two, the topological entropy can fail to be continuous; such an example is constructed in
[
23
]
. Then it is natural to investigate the topological entropy for more partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms with higher dimensional center.

Partial hyperbolicity includes the hyperbolic part and the center part. In
[
20
]
, the authors introduced the notion of unstable topological entropy, which is caused by the hyperbolic part of the system. Recently, the unstable metric entropy is studied in
[
20
]
and
[
50
]
, see also the survey
[
47
]
. In this paper, we focus on
C^{1}
partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms with subexponential growth in the center direction. Then only the hyperbolic part contributes to the topological entropy, which can be verified using the theory of unstable entropy. We remark that Ledrappier-Young formula which holds only for
C^{r},r>1
diffeomorphisms, is not directly applied here.

On the other hand, we investigate the continuity of unstable topological entropy in the
C^{1}
topology. The upper semicontinuity turns out to be a general result for all
C^{1}
partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms. The lower semicontinuity is more delicate, and we obtain it under the condition that the unstable manifolds have uniform exponential expansion, which has appeared in
[
46
]
.

As explained above, our main result in this paper states that the topological entropy function is lower semicontinuous at partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms which have subexponential growth in the center direction and uniform exponential growth along the unstable foliation. This result applies to partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms which is topologically transitive and Lyapunov stable in the center direction (hence to those with bounded expansion in the center direction). This class includes time-one maps of frame flows; semisimple translations on homogenous spaces; time-one map of geodesic flows in symmetric spaces of noncompact type, etc. An interesting phenomenon is that Lyapunov stable center together with topological transitivity implies uniform exponential growth along the unstable foliation.

A more difficult situation is when the system does have subexponential growth in center direction (for example, polynomial growth). Our technique is to establish effective density of the unstable foliation under the assumption that the system has an exponential mixing property. The fast equidistribution of the unstable foliation then eliminates the effect caused by subexponential growth in the center direction, which guarantees the uniform exponential growth along the unstable foliation. An important example would be the translations on homogeneous spaces. Let
G
be a connected semisimple Lie group,
\Gamma
a cocompact lattice in
G
, and denote by
T_{g}
the map given by the left translation on
G/\Gamma
by
g\in G
. When
\mathrm{Ad}g
is non-quasiunipotent, the system
(G/\Gamma,g)
is a primary example of partially hyperbolic diffeomorphisms (cf. Theorem D in
[
40
]
). The system may have polynomial growth in the center direction, and indeed it satisfies a version of exponential mixing property (“Condition (EM)” as in
[
28
, Section 2.4.1]
). It is also interesting to consider
C^{1}
perturbations of
T_{g}
as well as the time one map of a smooth time change of a homogenous flow. We do not know if such systems have certain exponential mixing property. At last, similar results are obtained for ergodic toral automorphisms, via effective density of their unstable foliations.

1.1.
Statement of main results

In general, for a compact metric space
(X,d)
, a continuous map
T:X\rightarrow X
and
\forall~n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
we define a new distance
d_{n}
on
X
by

\displaystyle d_{n}:X~\times~X

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle(x,y)

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle d_{n}(x,y)=\max_{0\leq j<n}d(T^{j}(x),T^{j}(y)),

which measures the maximal distance between the two orbits of
x
and
y
up to time
n
.
The new metric space
(X,d_{n})
is obviously homeomorphic to
(X,d)
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, we say that a subset
A\subset X
is an
(n,\epsilon)
-separated set,
if for any distinct points
x,y\in A
,

d_{n}(x,y)>\epsilon
.
The compactness of
X
implies that an
(n,\epsilon)
-separated set is a finite set.
Let

N(T,n,\epsilon):=\max\{\#A\ |A\ \text{ is an}~(n,\epsilon)\text{-separated set in\ }X\}

where
\#A
denotes the cardinality of
A
.
The topological entropy of the map
T
is defined by the following formula:

h_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}(T):=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log N(T,n,\epsilon).

Topological entropy can be defined equivalently by using
(n,\epsilon)
-spanning sets or open covers of
X
.
See Chapter
7
in
[
48
]
or Chapter
3
in
[
27
]
for comprehensive discussions on topological entropy.

Let
M
be a
d
-dimensional smooth, closed Riemannian manifold and let
f:M\to M
be a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism.
f
is called a
partially hyperbolic diffeomorphism
(abbreviated as
PHD
) if there exists a nontrivial
Df
-invariant splitting of the tangent bundle
TM=E_{f}^{s}\oplus E_{f}^{c}\oplus E_{f}^{u}
into so-called stable, center, and unstable distributions, such that all unit vectors
v^{\sigma}\in E_{f}^{\sigma}(x)
(
\sigma=s,c,u
) with
x\in M
satisfy

\|D_{x}fv^{s}\|<\|D_{x}fv^{c}\|<\|D_{x}fv^{u}\|,

and

\|D_{x}f|_{E_{f}^{s}(x)}\|<1,\ \ \ \text{\ and\ \ \ \ }\|D_{x}f^{-1}|_{E_{f}^{u}(x)}\|<1,

for some suitable Riemannian metric on
M
. The stable distribution
E_{f}^{s}
and unstable distribution
E_{f}^{u}
are integrable: there exist so-called stable and unstable foliations
W_{f}^{s}
and
W_{f}^{u}
respectively such that
TW_{f}^{s}=E_{f}^{s}
and
TW_{f}^{u}=E_{f}^{u}
. We say that
f
is
dynamically coherent
if there exist
f
-invariant center-stable and center-unstable foliations
W_{f}^{cs}
and
W_{f}^{cu}
tangent to
E_{f}^{cs}
and
E_{f}^{cu}
respectively; then there exists a center foliation
W_{f}^{c}
tangent to
E_{f}^{c}
by intersecting leaves of
W_{f}^{cs}
and
W_{f}^{cu}
. See for example
[
43
]
for the basic properties of PHDs. We mention that
C^{r}
PHDs form an open subset of
C^{r}(M)
, the space of
C^{r}
diffeomorphims of
M
for any
1\leq r\leq\infty
.

We will recall the definitions of unstable entropy
h^{u}_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}(f)
in Section 2. Our first result below is for general PHDs.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
f:M\to M
be any
C^{1}
partially hyperbolic diffeomorphism. Then

h^{u}_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}:C^{1}(M)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\cup\{0\}

is upper semicontinuous at
f
.

Note that in a recent paper
[
45
]
, the authors have shown that the function of unstable volume growth is upper semicontinuous in the
C^{1}
topology using geometric arguments. And it is already proved in
[
20
]
that unstable topological entropy and unstable volume growth coincide. Our proof of Proposition
1.1
uses unstable metric entropy and its upper semicontinuity. Modulo Proposition
1.1
, the main work in this paper is to study whether unstable topological entropy is lower semicontinuous.

We focus on a
C^{1}
PHD
f:M\to M

with subexponential growth in the center direction
, that is, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C_{\epsilon}>0
such that
\|D_{x}f^{n}|_{E_{f}^{c}(x)}\|\leq C_{\epsilon}e^{n\epsilon}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any
x\in M
(cf.
[
6
]
). As for the unstable direction, we introduce the following terminology in order to simplify the presentation.

Definition 1.2
.

We say that a
C^{1}
PHD
f:M\to M
has
uniform exponential growth along the unstable foliation
if for any
\rho>0
and
\delta>0
small enough, there exists
N(\rho,\delta)\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for any
x\in M
,
f^{N(\rho,\delta)}W^{u}_{f}(x,\delta)
contains at least
e^{N(\rho,\delta)(h-3\rho)}
disjoint sets of the form
\{W^{u}_{f}(z_{i},2\delta):z_{i}\in M\}
, where
h=h_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}^{u}(f)
and
W^{u}_{f}(x,\delta)
denotes the open ball centered at
x
of radius
\delta
with respect to the metric
d^{u}
inherited from the Riemannian structure on
W^{u}(x)
.

Our main result in this paper is the following:

Theorem A
.

Let
f:M\to M
be a
C^{1}
partially hyperbolic diffeomorphism.

(1)

If
f
has uniform exponential growth along the unstable foliation, then the unstable topological entropy function
h^{u}_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}:C^{1}(M)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\cup\{0\}
is continuous at
f
.

(2)

Assume further that
f
has subexponential growth in the center direction, then the topological entropy function
h_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}:C^{1}(M)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\cup\{0\}
is lower semicontinuous at
f
. If in addition
f
is
C^{\infty}
,
h_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}:C^{\infty}(M)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\cup\{0\}
is continuous at
f
.

We apply Theorem A to several important classes of PHDs.

A PHD is called
Lyapunov stable in the center direction (or just Lyapunov stable)
if given
any
\epsilon>0
, one can find
\delta>0
such that for any
C^{1}
-arc
\gamma
tangent to
E^{c}
with length
less than
\delta
, the curves
f^{n}\gamma,n\in\mathbb{N}
all have length less than

(cf.
[
42
]
). When
f
and

are both Lyapunov stable, we say that

is bi-Lyapunov stable.
It is known that if

is Lyapunov stable then
E^{c}\oplus E^{s}
integrates to a unique
f
-invariant foliation

. In particular, if
f
is bi-Lyapunov stable, then

is dynamically coherent (cf. Theorem 7.5 in
[
42
]
).

A
C^{1}
PHD
f:M\to M
is said to have

if there exists

\|Df^{n}(x)|_{E_{f}^{c}(x)}\|<K,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

It is clear that bounded expansion implies both Lyapunov stability and subexponential growth in the center direction.

Theorem B
.

Let
f:M\to M
be a topologically transitive
C^{1}
partially hyperbolic diffeomorphism which is Lyapunov stable in the center direction. Then
h^{u}_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}:C^{1}(M)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\cup\{0\}
is continuous at
f
. If in addition
f
has bounded expansion in the center direction, then the topological entropy function
h_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}:C^{1}(M)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\cup\{0\}
is lower semicontinuous at
f
.

Our technique to prove Theorem B, particularly the uniform exponential growth of

along the unstable foliation, is to use effective density of the unstable foliation of

Consider a volume preserving PHD

, i.e.,

preserves a smooth probability measure

. Denote by

the volume induced by the Riemannian structure on

. For any

.
We say

,

,

(1)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{W_{f}^{u}(x,\delta)}\phi(f^{n}(p))\psi(p)dm_{x}^{u}(p)-\int\phi dm\int_{W_{f}^{u}(x,\delta)}\psi dm_{x}^{u}\right|<C\|\phi\|_{r}\|\psi\|_{r}e^{-n\alpha}

Theorem C
.

Let
f:M\to M
be a
C^{1}
dynamically coherent partially hyperbolic diffeomorphism which has subexponential growth in the center direction and satisfies property (EM). Then the topological entropy function
h_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}:C^{1}(M)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\cup\{0\}

h^{u}_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}:C^{1}(M)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\cup\{0\}

.

An important example of PHDs with property (EM) is the partially hyperbolic translations on compact homogeneous spaces. Let
G
be a connected semisimple Lie group and
\Gamma\subset G
be a cocompact lattice. Choose and fix a norm on the Lie algebra

We study the topological entropy of the dynamical system

. Note that

). The following theorem is a corollary of Theorem C.

Theorem D
.

Let

be a connected semisimple Lie group,
\Gamma
be a cocompact lattice of

h_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}:C^{1}(G/\Gamma)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\cup\{0\}

and
h_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}:C^{\infty}(G/\Gamma)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\cup\{0\}

.

The term “Condition (EM)” is used in

Theorem E
.

Let
T_{A}:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{T}^{d}

We will state Theorem D’, an improvement of Theorem D in Section 5.2, and give a simpler proof. Similarly, Theorem E can also be improved, see Theorem E’ in Section 5.3. Nevertheless, our technique provides a systematic way to treat these problems.

2.
Unstable topological entropy

In this section, we collect some results on unstable topological entropy, and prove Proposition

Let
M
be a
d
-dimensional smooth, closed manifold and let
f:M\to M
be a
C^{1}
PHD. We start to recall the definition of unstable topological entropy given in
[
20
]
.

Denote by

the metric induced by the Riemannian structure

.
Given any

Proof of Proposition

.

For any

Let
n_{k}
be a subsequence such that

(3)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}h^{u}_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}(f_{n})=\lim_{k\to\infty}h^{u}_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}(f_{n_{k}}).

Then a subsequence of
\mu_{n_{k}}
converges to a probability measure
\mu
on
M
. Without loss of generality, we assume
\mu_{n_{k}}\to\mu
.
It is straightforward to verify that
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}_{f}(M)
. By Theorem
2.4
, we have

(4)

\lim_{k\to\infty}h^{u}_{\mu_{n_{k}}}(f_{n_{k}})\leq h^{u}_{\mu}(f).

By Theorem
2.3
,
h^{u}_{\mu}(f)\leq h^{u}_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}(f)
. Thus by (
2
),(
3
) and (

),

\limsup_{n\to\infty}h^{u}_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}(f_{n})\leq h^{u}_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}(f).

The proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

Let us consider a
C^{1}
PHD
f:M\to M
with subexponential growth in the center direction, that is, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C_{\epsilon}>0
such that
\|D_{x}f^{n}|_{E_{f}^{c}(x)}\|\leq C_{\epsilon}e^{n\epsilon}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any
x\in M
. The following lemma is useful for
C^{1}
PHDs.

Lemma 2.5
.

(Cf.
[
23
]
)
Let
f:M\to M
be a
C^{1}
PHD and
\mu
an
f
-invariant and ergodic measure. Then

h_{\mu}(f)\leq\chi^{u}(f)+\sum_{\lambda_{i}^{c}(\mu)>0}m_{i}(\mu)\lambda_{i}^{c}(\mu)

where the sum is taken over all the positive Lypapunov exponents
\lambda_{i}^{c}(\mu)
of
\mu
in the center direction with multiplicity
m_{i}(\mu)
.

Proposition 2.6
.

If a
C^{1}
PHD
f:M\to M
has subexponential growth in the center direction, then

h_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}(f)=h^{u}_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}(f)
.

Proof.

Let
\nu
be any
f
-invariant and ergodic Borel probability measure on
M
. It is clear that the Lyapunov exponents of
\nu
in the center direction are always nonpositive, since
f
has subexponential growth in the center direction. Thus by Lemma
2.5
and the fact

, we have
h_{\nu}(f)\leq h^{u}_{\text{top}}(f)
. Thus by the variational principle, we have

h_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}(f)=\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{e}_{f}(M)}h_{\nu}(f)\leq h^{u}_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}(f).

h^{u}_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}(f)\leq h_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}(f)
is automatic and therefore we finish the proof of the proposition.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

If

is a

-PHD,

, we can also obtain

]
).

Remark 2.8
.

A
C^{1}

has subexponential growth in the center direction if and only if the Lyapunov exponents in the center direction are all nonpositive with respect to any
f
-invariant measure. The “only if” part is clear. For the “if” part, assume that for some
\epsilon>0
, there exists a sequence of points

4.
Lyapunov stable PHDs

Recall that a PHD is called Lyapunov stable (in center direction) if given
any
\epsilon>0
, one can find
\delta>0
such that for any
C^{1}
-arc
\gamma
tangent to
E^{c}
with length
less than
\delta
, the curves
f^{n}\gamma,n\in\mathbb{N}
all have length less than
\epsilon
.
If
f
is Lyapunov stable then
E^{c}\oplus E^{s}
integrates to a unique
f
-invariant foliation
W^{cs}
(cf.
[
42
]
).

A
C^{1}
PHD
f:M\to M
is said to have bounded expansion in the center direction if there exists
K>1
such that

\|Df^{n}(x)|_{E_{f}^{c}(x)}\|<K,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Bounded expansion implies both Lyapunov stability and subexponential growth in the center direction.

4.1.
Proof of Theorem B

We will show that a topologically transitive and Lyapunov stable
C^{1}
PHD
f:M\to M
has uniform exponential growth along unstable manifolds, and hence Theorem A(1) applies. If further,
f
has bounded expansion in the center direction, then

has subexponential growth in center direction and Theorem A(2) applies.

The following property is a version of effective density of the unstable foliation.

Lemma 4.1
.

(Effective density, cf.
[
10
, Lemma 6.2]
)
Let
f:M\to M
be a
C^{1}
PHD which is topologically transitive and Lyapunov stable. Then given any
\delta>0
, there is
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for every
x,y\in M
, there is
0\leq k\leq n
such that

f^{k}(W_{f}^{u}(x,\delta))\cap W_{f}^{cs}(y,\delta)\neq\emptyset.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
f:M\to M
be a
C^{1}
PHD which is topologically transitive and Lyapunov stable. Then

has uniform exponential growth along unstable foliation.

Proof.

Let
h=h_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}^{u}(f)
and
\delta_{0}>0
be small enough to be specified later. Recall the definition of unstable topological entropy and Remark
2.2
.
For any
\rho>0
, we can find

and if

(2)

Now take any

4.2.1.
Time-one maps of frame flows

Let
M
be a closed oriented
n
-dimensional manifold of negative sectional curvature.
Consider the unit tangent bundle
SM=\{(x,v):x\in M,v\in T_{x}M,\|v\|=1\}
, and the frame bundle

\displaystyle FM=\{

\displaystyle(x,v_{0},v_{1},\cdots,v_{n-1}):x\in M,v_{i}\in T_{x}M,

\displaystyle\{v_{0},\cdots,v_{n-1}\}\text{\ is a positively oriented orthonormal frame at\ }x\}.

The geodesic flow

g^{t}(x,v)=(\gamma_{(x,v)}(t),\dot{\gamma}_{(x,v)}(t))

\gamma_{(x,v)}(t)

f^{t}(x,v_{0},v_{1},\cdots,v_{n-1})=(g^{t}(x,v_{0}),\Gamma_{\gamma}^{t}(v_{1}),\cdots,\Gamma_{\gamma}^{t}(v_{n-1}))

•

5.2.1.

Let

(resp.

, where
q
denotes the projection from
G
to
G^{nc}
. According to
[
41

,
G^{nc}
can be decomposed as

G^{nc}=G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{m}

such that
\Gamma\cap G_{i}
are irreducible lattices in
G_{i}(1\leq i\leq m)
.
Write
g=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{m},k)
with
g_{i}\in G_{i}
and
k\in K
. We may assume that
g_{i}
is non-quasiunipotent for
1\leq i\leq j
and
g_{i}
is quasiunipotent for
j<i\leq m
. Then set

G^{\prime}=G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{j},\quad G^{\prime\prime}=G_{j+1}\cdots\times G_{m}\times K.

By assumption
G^{\prime}
is nontrivial and we write
g=(g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime})
with
g^{\prime}\in G^{\prime}
non-quasiunipotent and
g^{\prime\prime}\in G^{\prime\prime}
quasiunipotent.
Denote the projection of
G
to
G^{\prime}
as
p
. Using
[
41
, Theorem 5.22]
again, we obtain that the group
p(\Gamma)
is commensurable with
\prod_{1\leq i\leq j}(\Gamma\cap G_{i})
, hence is a lattice in
G^{\prime}
. Thus we know that
G/\Gamma
is a fiber bundle over
G^{\prime}/p(\Gamma)
with fiber
G^{\prime\prime}/(\Gamma\cap G^{\prime\prime})
.

Firstly, we study the system
(G^{\prime}/p(\Gamma),g^{\prime})
. By definition, there is a finite index subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset p(\Gamma)
such that
\Gamma^{\prime}=\prod_{1\leq i\leq j}\Gamma_{i}
with
\Gamma_{i}
irreducible lattices in
G_{i}
. By Proposition
5.7
, each
(G_{i}/\Gamma_{i},g_{i})
has uniform exponential growth along unstable foliation. Then
(G^{\prime}/\Gamma^{\prime},g^{\prime},d^{\prime})
is a product of systems
(G_{i}/\Gamma_{i},g_{i},d_{i})
where it is more convenient to work with the metrics

d^{\prime}((x_{i}),(y_{i}))=\max_{1\leq i\leq j}d_{i}(x_{i},y_{i}).

Note that
h_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}(g^{\prime})=\sum_{1\leq i\leq j}h_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}(g_{i})
. It is not hard to verify that
G^{\prime}/\Gamma^{\prime}
has uniform exponential growth along unstable foliation by choosing

N(\rho,\delta)=\max_{1\leq i\leq j}N_{i}(\rho,\delta).

Now taking into account that there exists a locally isometric finite covering map
\varphi:G^{\prime}/\Gamma^{\prime}\to G^{\prime}/p(\Gamma)
, we see that
G^{\prime}/p(\Gamma)
has uniform exponential growth along unstable foliation.

Now consider the fiber system
(G/\Gamma,g)
over
(G^{\prime}/p(\Gamma),g^{\prime})
with fiber
G^{\prime\prime}/(\Gamma\cap G^{\prime\prime})
. Note that
h_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}(g)=h_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}(g^{\prime})
and the Lyapunov exponents of
g
along the direction of fibers are zero. Notice that the unstable horospherical subgroup
H^{+}
of
g
is a subgroup of
G^{\prime}
. If
x=h\Gamma=h^{\prime}h^{\prime\prime}\Gamma\in G/\Gamma
with
h^{\prime}\in G^{\prime}
and
h^{\prime\prime}\in G^{\prime\prime}
, then for
B^{+}_{\delta}\subset H^{+}
,

p(B_{\delta}^{+}h\Gamma)=B_{\delta}^{+}p(h\Gamma)=B_{\delta}^{+}h^{\prime}p(\Gamma).

So
p
is in fact a local isometry between
W^{u}_{g}(x)\to W^{u}_{g^{\prime}}(p(x))
. As
p\circ T_{g}=T_{g^{\prime}}\circ p
, it is clear that
(G/\Gamma,g)
has uniform exponential growth along unstable foliation if
(G^{\prime}/p(\Gamma),g^{\prime})
has.
∎

Proof of Theorem D.

If
g
is quasiunipotent, the topological entropy of
T_{g}
is
0
, then there is nothing to prove.

Assume that
g
is non-quasiunipotent. Then by Proposition
5.8
,
(G/\Gamma,g)
has uniform exponential growth along unstable foliation. Moreover, it has subexponential growth in the center direction. Now Theorem D is an immediate corollary of Theorem A(2).
∎

5.2.3.
An improvement of Theorem D

Theorem D’
.

Let
G
be a connected semisimple Lie group,
\Gamma
be a cocompact lattice of
G
, and
g\in G
. Then the topological entropy function
h_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}:C^{1}(G/\Gamma)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\cup\{0\}
is continuous at
T_{g}
.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
and
f
be
C^{1}
-close enough to
T_{g}
which will be specified later. By Ruelle inequality, for any
f
-ergodic measure
\nu
and any
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(13)

\displaystyle h_{\nu}(f)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\lambda_{j}(\nu)>0}\lambda_{j}(\nu)m_{j}(\nu)

\displaystyle\leq\dim E_{f}^{c}\cdot\frac{1}{N}\max_{x\in G/\Gamma}\log\|D_{x}f^{N}|_{E_{f}^{c}(x)}\|+\max_{x\in G/\Gamma}\log\det(D_{x}f|_{E_{f}^{u}(x)}).

Note that
\|D_{x}T_{g}^{N}|_{E_{g}^{c}(x)}\|
is constant in
x\in G/\Gamma
. Choose
N
large enough such that
\frac{1}{N}\log\|D_{x}T_{g}^{N}|_{E_{g}^{c}(x)}\|<\frac{\epsilon}{3\dim E_{g}^{c}}
for any
x\in G/\Gamma
. Now choose
f

C^{1}
-close enough to
T_{g}
such that for any
x\in G/\Gamma
,

(14)

\Big|\log\|D_{x}f^{N}|_{E_{f}^{c}(x)}\|-\log\|D_{x}T_{g}^{N}|_{E_{g}^{c}(x)}\|\Big|<\frac{\epsilon}{3\dim E_{f}^{c}}

and

(15)

\Big|\log\det(D_{x}f|_{E_{f}^{u}(x)})-\log\det(D_{x}T_{g}|_{E_{g}^{u}(x)})\Big|<\frac{\epsilon}{3}.

Note that

(16)

h_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}(T_{g})=\log\det(\mathrm{Ad}g|_{\mathfrak{h}^{+}})=\log\det(D_{x}T_{g}|_{E_{g}^{u}(x)})

for any
x\in G/\Gamma
. Combining (
13
), (
14
), (
15
) and (
16
),
we have
h_{\nu}(f)<h_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}(T_{g})+\epsilon
. Then
h_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}(f)=\sup_{\nu\in{\mathcal{M}}_{f}^{e}(G/\Gamma)}h_{\nu}(f)\leq h_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}(T_{g})+\epsilon
. This proves the upper semicontinuity of
h_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}:C^{1}(G/\Gamma)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\cup\{0\}
at
T_{g}
.

The lower semicontinuity of
h_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}:C^{1}(G/\Gamma)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\cup\{0\}
at
T_{g}
is obtained in Theorem D. We give a simple proof when the system after perturbation is
C^{r},r>1
. For any
\epsilon>0
, choose a
C^{r},r>1
diffeomorphism
f:G/\Gamma\to G/\Gamma
which is
C^{1}
-close enough to
T_{g}
such that for any
x\in G/\Gamma
(
15
) is satisfied. As
f
is
C^{r},r>1
, let
\mu
be a Gibbs u-state for
f
, i.e., an
f
-invariant measure whose conditional measures along unstable leaves are absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on the unstable leaves. The existence of
\mu
is guaranteed by
[
39
]
. Then we have

(17)

\displaystyle h_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}(f)\geq h_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}^{u}(f)\geq h^{u}_{\mu}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G/\Gamma}\log\det(D_{x}f|_{E_{f}^{u}(x)})d\mu(x)\geq\log\det(DT_{g}|_{E_{g}^{u}})-\epsilon/3

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}(T_{g})-\epsilon/3.

This proves the lower semicontinuity of
h_{{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}}:C^{r}(G/\Gamma)\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\cup\{0\}
at
T_{g}
,
r>1
.
∎

5.2.4.
Further questions

Though the most work in this paper is done on the unstable foliation of
T_{g}
, we remark that the center distribution of
T_{g}
corresponding to the subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}^{0}:=\bigoplus_{|\lambda|=1}E_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{g}
, is
C^{\infty}
. Then the center foliation is
C^{\infty}
and hence plaque expansive (cf.
[
19
]
). Thus there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of
T_{g}
in the
C^{1}
topology, such that for each
f\in\mathcal{U}
,

(1)

there exists a homeomorphism
h_{f}:G/\Gamma\to G/\Gamma
, called a leaf conjugacy, satisfying
h_{f}(W^{c}_{g}(x))=W^{c}_{f}(h_{f}(x))
and
h_{f}\circ T_{g}(W^{c}_{g}(x))=f\circ h_{f}(W^{c}_{g}(x))
. Moreover, the leaf conjugacy
h_{f}
is close to the identity map in the
C^{0}
topology.

(2)

f
is dynamically coherent, i.e., there exist
f
-invariant foliations
W^{cs}_{f}
and
W^{cu}_{f}
tangent to
E^{c}_{f}\oplus E^{s}_{f}
and
E^{c}_{f}\oplus E^{u}_{f}
respectively; by intersecting their leaves we then obtain an
f
-invariant foliation
W^{c}_{f}
.

A further question arises about the continuity property of the topological entropy at each
f\in\mathcal{U}
. Clearly, the properties of
f
listed above will be fundamental. However, other properties of
f
such as property (EM) should be developed, for which we have no explicit ideas now.

On the other hand, one can study a smooth time change of a partially hyperbolic homogeneous flow.
Let
(G/\Gamma,g_{t})
be a partially hyperbolic homogeneous flow, i.e.,
g_{t}
is a one-parameter subgroup of
G
for which each element is non-quasiunipotent.
Let
\tau:G/\Gamma\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a smooth positive function. The time-changed flow is defined as

g^{\tau}_{t}(x):=g_{\alpha(x,t)}(x)
,
where
\alpha(x,t)
is determined by

\int_{0}^{\alpha(x,t)}\tau(g_{s}(x))ds=t.

Then the time-one map
g^{\tau}_{1}
is also a
C^{\infty}
PHD whose center, center-stable and center-unstable foliations all coincide with those of
T_{g}
. However, we do not know if
g^{\tau}_{1}
has property (EM).

5.3.
Toral automorphisms

Suppose that a toral automorphism
T_{A}:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{T}^{d}
is induced by a linear map
A:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, set

E_{\lambda}:=\{v\in\mathbb{C}^{d}:(A-\lambda I)^{j}v=0\text{\ for some\ }j\in\mathbb{N}-\{0\}\}.

The so-called stable, center and unstable subspaces
E^{u},E^{c},E^{s}
are the intersections with
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
of

E^{u}_{\mathbb{C}}:=\bigoplus_{|\lambda|>1}E_{\lambda},\quad E^{c}_{\mathbb{C}}:=\bigoplus_{|\lambda|=1}E_{\lambda},\quad E^{s}_{\mathbb{C}}:=\bigoplus_{|\lambda|<1}E_{\lambda}

respectively. Then
\mathbb{R}^{d}=E^{u}\oplus E^{c}\oplus E^{s}
. Denote
E^{sc}=E^{s}\oplus E^{c}
. We can use the supremum norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
(denoted by
\|\cdot\|
for short) on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined by
\|v\|:=\max\{\|v^{u}\|,\|v^{c}\|,\|v^{s}\|\}
where
v=v^{u}+v^{c}+v^{s},v^{*}\in E^{*},*\in\{s,c,u\}
. Denote
B^{*}(\epsilon):=\{v\in E^{*}:\|v\|<\epsilon\},*\in\{s,c,u\}
.

Assume that
T_{A}:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{T}^{d}
is ergodic. It is enough to consider the nontrivial case
E^{u}\neq\emptyset
. The following lemma is well known.

Lemma 5.10
.

(Effective density, see Lemma 1 in
[
33
]
)
Let
\tau>1
. Then for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
large enough,
B^{u}(\tau^{n})
is
(1/n^{d})
-dense in
\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

It then follows form Lemma
5.10
that there exists
N_{1}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for any
n\geq N_{1}
, we have an integer function
M=M_{\delta}(n)
such that for any
x,x_{0}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
,

T_{A}^{M}(x+B^{u}(\delta/2))\cap\Big(x_{0}+B(\frac{\delta}{2n^{d-2}})\Big)\neq\emptyset

and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{M_{\delta}(n)}{n}=0
. Then
T_{A}^{M}(x+B^{u}(\delta))
contains a local unstable leaf
W^{u}_{T_{A}}(y,2\delta)
for some
y\in W_{T_{A}}^{cs}(x_{0},\frac{\delta}{2n^{d-2}})
. We note that

•

d(T_{A}^{n}x,T_{A}^{n}y)\leq\delta
if
y\in W_{T_{A}}^{cs}(x,\frac{\delta}{n^{d-2}})
,

•

W^{cs}
is a
C^{\infty}
foliation,

•

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{M_{\delta}(n)}{n}=0
.

The proof of Proposition
5.4
still works here with minor modifications and thus Theorem E also follows from Theorem A.

We remark that Theorem E can be improved as follows.

Theorem E’
.

Suppose that
T_{A}:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{T}^{d}
is ergodic with
E^{u}\neq\emptyset
. Then
h_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}^{u}:C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\cup\{0\}
is locally constant at
T_{A}
, and
h_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}:C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\cup\{0\}
is continuous at
T_{A}
.

Proof.

The first part of Theorem E’ can be derived from the proof of Theorem 1.1 in
[
23
]
. The authors show that the unstable foliation of
T_{A}
stably carries a unique non-trivial homology and hence obtain the above result, taking into account the fact
\chi_{u}(T_{A})=h_{\mathop{\hbox{\footnotesize\rm top}}}^{u}(T_{A})
by
[
20
]
. The second part of Theorem E’ can be proved similarly as in the proof of Theorem D’ in Section 5.2.3.
∎

Remark 5.11
.

The technique in
[
23
]
is inapplicable to homogeneous translations. Indeed, let
g^{t},t\in{\mathbb{R}}
be a one-parameter subgroup of
G
with
g^{1}=g
, then
T_{g}
is isotopic to the identity map
T_{g^{0}}=T_{e}
where
e
is the identity of the group
G
. So neither the stable foliation nor the unstable foliation of
T_{g}
carries a non-trivial homology.

Concluding remarks

In
[
46
]
, the authors conjectured the topological entropy is continuous on the space of

PHDs with

-D center. They obtain the following list of PHDs at which the topological entropy function is continuous in
C^{1}
topology (except that item (7) is new).

(1)

uniformly hyperbolic diffeomorphisms,

(2)

skew products over uniformly hyperbolic diffeomorphisms, with the fiber being a circle,
and perturbations (
[
3
,
37
,
16
,
17
]
),

(3)

derived from Anosov diffeomorphisms (
[
32
,
17
,
38
]
),

(4)

non-dynamically coherent examples of Hertz-Hertz-Ures (
[
44
]
),

(5)

time-one maps of Anosov flows and perturbations (
[
22
,
21
,
46
]
),

(6)

]

(7)

[

In this paper, we have investigated PHDs with higher dimensional center. Now we can add to the above list the following PHDs at which the topological entropy function

and the unstable topological entropy function

Gelfand–Kirillov dimension and the p p -adic Jacquet–Langlands correspondence
Gabriel Dospinescu, Vytautas Paškūnas and Benjamin Schraen

Abstract
. We bound the Gelfand–Kirillov dimension of unitary Banach space representations of p p -adic reductive groups, whose locally analytic vectors afford an infinitesimal character. We use the bound to study Hecke eigenspaces in completed cohomology of Shimura curves and p p -adic Banach space representations of the group of units of a quaternion algebra over ℚ p \mathbb{Q}_{p} appearing in the p p -adic Jacquet–Langlands correspondence, deducing finiteness results in favourable cases.

The aim of this paragraph is to establish a technical result which is the second key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
6.1
. Only the first theorem below will be needed in the proof of Theorem
6.1
, but we found the other statements interesting in their own right.

We fix throughout this section a finite extension
L
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
. If
G
is a connected reductive group defined over a sub-extension

K
of
L
, split over
L
, with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
(a
K
-Lie algebra) then we let
\mathfrak{g}_{L}:=\mathfrak{g}\otimes_{K}L
.
If

\chi:Z(\mathfrak{g}_{L})\to L
is an
L
-algebra homomorphism and

H
is a compact open subgroup of
G(K)
, then

{\rm Lie}(H)={\rm Lie}(G(K))=\mathfrak{g}
, hence

D_{1/p}(H,L)^{K\text{-}\mathrm{la}}
is an
U(\mathfrak{g}_{L})
-module and we can define

D_{1/p}(H,L)^{K\text{-}\mathrm{la}}_{\chi}=D_{1/p}(H,L)^{K\text{-}\mathrm{la}}\otimes_{Z(\mathfrak{g}_{L}),\chi}L.

There is a natural map
L\llbracket H\rrbracket:=L\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{O}\llbracket H\rrbracket\to D_{1/p}(H,L)^{K\text{-}\mathrm{la}}_{\chi}
(recall that
D_{1/p}(H,L)^{K\text{-}\mathrm{la}}
is a quotient of

D_{1/p}(H,L)
, which contains
D(H,L)
and so also
L\llbracket H\rrbracket
).

The following theorem is a re-interpretation of the main result of
[
4
]
. Compared to loc. cit., we drop the hypothesis that
p
is a very good prime for the group
\mathbb{G}
below,
and we explain how one can adapt the arguments in loc. cit. to avoid this hypothesis.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{G}
is a connected, split semisimple and simply connected group scheme over
\mathcal{O}
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Given a character

\chi:Z(\mathfrak{g}_{L})\to L
, for all

n
large enough the natural map

13

13

The isomorphism being a consequence of Proposition

and the discussion preceding Theorem

.

L\llbracket H_{n}\rrbracket\to D_{1/p}(H_{n},L)^{L\text{-}\mathrm{la}}_{\chi}\simeq\widehat{U(p^{n}\mathfrak{g})}[1/p]_{\chi}

is injective, where
H_{n}=\ker(\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{O})\to\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{O}/p^{n+1}\mathcal{O}))
.

Proof.

Fix a maximal split torus
\mathbb{T}
in

\mathbb{G}
and a pair of opposite Borel subgroups

\mathbb{B}
,
\mathbb{B}^{+}
containing
\mathbb{T}
. If

\lambda:\mathfrak{t}_{L}\to L
is a weight (where

\mathfrak{t}={\rm Lie}(\mathbb{T})
,
\mathfrak{b}={\rm Lie}(\mathbb{B})
, etc.), consider the Verma module
V^{\lambda}=U(\mathfrak{g}_{L})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{b}^{+}_{L}),\lambda}L
, where
the map

is induced by the natural projection

\mathfrak{b}^{+}_{L}\to\mathfrak{t}_{L}
. It is a standard result that

V^{\lambda}
has an infinitesimal character, and it follows from the Harish-Chandra isomorphism that we can find

such that this infinitesimal character is precisely

. Fix such

Consider the completion (an affinoid Verma module in the terminology of

)

of

In order to properly finish the proof, we still have to

in
[
4
]
still holds without the assumption that

is a very good prime for

\mathbb{G}
.
The reader is advised to have a copy of that paper at hand when reading the following argument, since we will freely use the notations introduced there.
In particular
B
and

are

-uniform subgroups of
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{O})
and
\mathbb{B}^{+}(\mathcal{O})
with associated

Without any assumption on

the proof of Theorem 5.4 shows that
\widehat{V^{\lambda}}
is a faithful

RB
(with the notations in loc. cit.). The only point where the fact that

is very good for

is used is in citing Proposition 4.8 of loc. cit.
to deduce that

is faithful as

The proof of that proposition seems to use their deep Theorem 4.6, a

thus

Remark 5.2
.

It is unreasonable to expect such injectivity properties to hold without some assumptions. Indeed, it is easy to see that
for any
n
the natural map

L\llbracket p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rrbracket\to D_{1/p}(p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p},L)/tD_{1/p}(p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p},L)

is not injective, where
T=\delta_{1}-1
and

t=\log(1+T)
(so that
D(\mathbb{Z}_{p},L)
is the ring of
L
-rational
analytic functions of the variable
T
in the open unit disc). For instance,

(1+T)^{p^{n+1}}-1\in L\llbracket p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rrbracket
is divisible by
t
in
D_{1/p}(p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p},L)
.

In the following result we drop the hypothesis that the group is split and simply connected.

Proposition 5.3
.

For any connected semisimple group
G
over

\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and any
L
-algebra homomorphism

\chi:Z(\mathfrak{g}_{L})\to L

there is an open uniform pro-
p
subgroup
H
of
G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
such that the natural map

L\llbracket H\rrbracket\to D_{1/p}(H,L)_{\chi}

is injective.

Proof.

We first reduce to the case when
G
is simply connected.
Note that if
H
is such a subgroup, then
L\llbracket H^{\prime}\rrbracket\to D_{1/p}(H^{\prime},L)_{\chi}
is still injective for any open uniform pro-
p
subgroup

H^{\prime}
of
H
(indeed, it suffices to test that the composition with the natural map

D_{1/p}(H^{\prime},L)_{\chi}\to D_{1/p}(H,L)_{\chi}
is injective, but this is simply the restriction of the natural map

L\llbracket H\rrbracket\to D_{1/p}(H,L)_{\chi}
to
L\llbracket H^{\prime}\rrbracket\subset L\llbracket H\rrbracket
). We deduce immediately from this that if
the result holds for the simply connected cover of
G
, then it also holds for
G
, by projecting the corresponding
H
for the simply connected cover down to
G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.

Next, suppose that
L^{\prime}
is a finite extension of
L
. Then
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{L^{\prime}})=L^{\prime}\otimes_{L}Z(\mathfrak{g}_{L})
, thus
\chi:Z(\mathfrak{g}_{L})\to L
induces an

L^{\prime}
-algebra map
\chi^{\prime}:Z(\mathfrak{g}_{L^{\prime}})\to L^{\prime}
with
\ker(\chi^{\prime})=L^{\prime}\otimes_{L}\ker(\chi)
, so that
D_{1/p}(H,L^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}\simeq L^{\prime}\otimes_{L}D_{1/p}(H,L)_{\chi}
and the map
L^{\prime}\llbracket H\rrbracket\to D_{1/p}(H,L^{\prime})_{\chi^{\prime}}
is the base change from
L
to
L^{\prime}
of the map
L\llbracket H\rrbracket\to D_{1/p}(H,L)_{\chi}
. In particular, one of these maps is injective if and only if the other one is so. We are therefore allowed to replace
L
by a finite extension and so we may assume that
G_{L}:=G\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}L
is split over
L
and
L/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is Galois.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be the canonical split semisimple and simply connected model of
G_{L}
over
\mathcal{O}
and, as above, set

H_{n}=\ker(\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{O})\to\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{O}/p^{n+1}\mathcal{O})).

Then
H_{n}
is an open
L
-uniform pro-
p
subgroup of

\mathbb{G}(\mathcal{O})
, thus also of
\mathbb{G}(L)\simeq G(L)=G_{L}(L)
. By the previous theorem the natural
map

L\llbracket H_{n}\rrbracket\to D_{1/p}(H_{n},L)^{L\text{-}\mathrm{la}}_{\chi}

is injective for
n\gg 0
and since

D_{1/p}(H_{n},L)^{L\text{-}\mathrm{la}}_{\chi}
is a quotient of
D_{1/p}(H_{n},L)_{\chi}
, it follows that the natural map

L\llbracket H_{n}\rrbracket\to D_{1/p}(H_{n},L)_{\chi}
is injective for
n
large enough.
Thus we can find an open uniform pro-
p
subgroup
H^{\prime}
of
G(L)=G_{L}(L)

such that the natural map
L\llbracket H^{\prime}\rrbracket\to D_{1/p}(H^{\prime},L)_{\chi}
is injective.

Set
H^{\prime\prime}=H^{\prime}\cap G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. It is a compact open subgroup of
G(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.
Choose any open uniform pro-
p
subgroup
H
of
H^{\prime\prime}
. We claim that the natural map
L\llbracket H\rrbracket\to D_{1/p}(H,L)_{\chi}
is injective. It suffices to check that the composite
L\llbracket H\rrbracket\to D_{1/p}(H,L)_{\chi}\to D_{1/p}(H^{\prime},L)_{\chi}
is injective, but this map is simply the composite
L\llbracket H\rrbracket\to L\llbracket H^{\prime}\rrbracket\to D_{1/p}(H^{\prime},L)_{\chi}
, and both maps are injective.
∎

6.
A bound for the dimension of
Z(\mathfrak{g})
-finite Banach representations

In this rather long section we prove that the existence of an infinitesimal character on the locally analytic vectors of an admissible Banach representation of a
p
-adic group has a serious impact on its dimension.

6.1.
The main result and some consequences

Let
G
be a locally
K
-analytic group with Lie
algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and let
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{L})

be the center of the universal enveloping algebra

U(\mathfrak{g}_{L})
. Here we consider
\mathfrak{g}
as
a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-Lie algebra. We say
that a locally analytic representation

\Pi
of
G
over
L
is
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{L})
-finite
(resp.
quasi-simple
) if the

Z(\mathfrak{g}_{L})
-annihilator of
\Pi
is of finite codimension in
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{L})

(resp. a maximal ideal of
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{L})
). The term
quasi-simple
is motivated
by the representation theory of real groups.
We say that a Banach representation
\Pi
of
G
is
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{L})
-finite
(resp.
quasi-simple
)
if

\Pi^{\mathrm{la}}
(the space of locally
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-analytic vectors in
\Pi
) is

Z(\mathfrak{g}_{L})
-finite (resp. quasi-simple) as locally analytic representation of
G
. The key result of this chapter is the following (see paragraph
3.4
for the notation
d(\Pi)
and
d^{K}(\Pi)
.)

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
G
be a connected reductive group over
K
,

H
a compact open subgroup of
G(K)
and
\mathcal{N}

the nilpotent cone in the Lie algebra of
G_{\overline{K}}
.

a) If
\Pi
is a
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{L})
-finite
admissible locally
K
-analytic representation of

H
, then

d^{K}(\Pi)\leq\dim\mathcal{N}.

b) If
\Pi
is a
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{L})
-finite
admissible
Banach representation of
H
, then

d^{K}(\Pi^{K\text{-}\mathrm{la}})<\dim\mathcal{N}.

We recall that
\dim\mathcal{N}
is twice the
dimension of the flag variety of
G_{\overline{K}}

(with respect to a Borel subgroup). Part a) was
already observed by Schmidt and Strauch
[
49
, prop
7.3]
, under some assumptions on
p
depending on

G
, as a consequence of deep results of
Ardakov–Wadsley
[
3
]
. We give here a much
easier proof, which also works uniformly with respect
to the prime
p
. We note that the bound in part a) is
optimal. On the other hand, part b) is new, slightly
surprising and requires quite a bit more work than
part a).

Remark 6.2
.

Note that the dimension of the nilpotent cone of

\Res_{K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}G
is
[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\dim\mathcal{N}
. Therefore
Theorem
6.1
b) implies

d(\Pi)=d(\Pi^{\mathrm{la}})<[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\dim\mathcal{N}.

This inequality is far from being optimal. Based on
the archimedean case, it is natural to ask if, for

\Pi
a
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{L})
-finite admissible
Banach representation of
G(K)
, we have

\displaystyle d(\Pi)\leq\frac{1}{2}[K:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\dim\mathcal{N},\quad d^{K}(\Pi^{K\text{-}\mathrm{la}})\leq\frac{1}{2}\dim\mathcal{N}.

Remark 6.3
.

In order to prove Theorem
6.1
we are free to replace
L
by a finite extension, in particular we may assume that
L
is a splitting field for
G
.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let

By the previous Theorem

has infinite length. Then we can construct a

which are

-stable and
such that

such

Let

be a connected reductive group over

, with Lie algebra

be the center of

equivalent to the statement that this character is the infinitesimal character of a

-module. If

.

Let

, where
\mathbf{G}

is an algebraic group over

6.2.
Proof of part a) of Theorem
6.1

The key input is the following result

Proposition 6.9
.

Let
G
be a connected reductive group over
K
, with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}

G_{L}
is split, of rank
r
, and let

H
be an open
K
-uniform pro-
p
subgroup of

be an ideal of

a) There are

b) For any

a)
We claim that

as

-algebras. Let

be the Weyl group of
G_{L}
. The Harish-Chandra isomorphism identifies

with

\mathfrak{t}_{L}
is the Lie algebra of a maximal split torus in

this is a polynomial algebra over the algebraic closure of

and we claim it is already the case over

is a polynomial algebra over

is integral over

Since

p_{1}(t_{1}),\ldots,p_{r}(t_{r})\in J

is regular in

.

b) The

are central in

since they are invariant under the adjoint action of

and so they commute with all Dirac distributions, which span a dense subspace of

is flat over

D_{r_{n}}(H,L)^{K\text{-}\mathrm{la}}
.
We have a decomposition of left

-modules

D_{r_{n}}(H,L)^{K\text{-}\mathrm{la}}=\bigoplus_{h\in H/H^{p^{n}}}A_{n}\delta_{h},

where

and

\mathfrak{h}=\mathcal{O}_{L}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{K}}q^{-1}L_{H}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{L}
-Lie lattice in

\mathfrak{g}_{L}
(see Proposition
2.3
and the discussion following it). By Kostant’s Theorem

is flat (even free) over its centre

, so it suffices to prove that

is flat over
U(\mathfrak{h}[1/p])=U(p^{n}\mathfrak{h})[1/p]

This follows easily from the fact that

is noetherian (cf.
[
7
, Section 3.2.3]
).
∎

For

Lemma 7.1

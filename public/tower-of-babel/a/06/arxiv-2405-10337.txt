Suppression of blow-up in Patlak-Keller-Segel system coupled with linearized Navier-Stokes equations via the 3D Couette flow
Shikun Cui, Lili Wang, Wendong Wang

Abstract
. It is known that finite-time blow-up in the 3D Patlak-Keller-Segel system may occur for arbitrarily small values of the initial mass. It’s interesting whether one can prevent the finite-time blow-up via the stabilizing effect of the moving fluid. Consider the three-dimensional Patlak-Keller-Segel system coupled with the linearized Navier-Stokes equations near the Couette flow ( A ​ y , 0 , 0 ) (\ Ay,0,0\ ) in a finite channel 𝕋 × 𝕀 × 𝕋 \mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I}\times\mathbb{T} with 𝕋 = [ 0 , 2 ​ π ) \mathbb{T}=[0,2\pi) and 𝕀 = [ − 1 , 1 ] \mathbb{I}=[-1,1] , with the non-slip boundary condition, and we show that if the shear flow is sufficiently strong (A is large enough), then the solutions to Patlak-Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes system are global in time as long as the initial cell mass is sufficiently small (for example, M < 4 9 M<\frac{4}{9} ) and A ⁡ ( ‖ u 2 , 0 ​ ( 0 ) ‖ L 2 + ‖ u 3 , 0 ​ ( 0 ) ‖ L 2 ) ≤ C 0 A\left(\|u_{2,0}(0)\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{3,0}(0)\|_{L^{2}}\right)\leq C_{0} , which seems to be the first result of considering the suppression effect of Couette flow in the 3D Patlak-Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes model, and also the first time considering the non-slip boundary condition.

1.
Introduction

Considering the following three-dimensional parabolic-elliptic Patlak-Keller-Segel (PKS) system coupled with the
Navier-Stokes equations in a finite channel
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I}\times\mathbb{T}
with
\mathbb{T}=[0,2\pi)
and
\mathbb{I}=[-1,1]
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\partial_{t}n+v\cdot\nabla n=\triangle n-\nabla\cdot(n\nabla c),\\
\triangle c+n-c=0,\\
\partial_{t}v+v\cdot\nabla v+\nabla P=\triangle v+n\nabla\phi,\quad\nabla\cdot v=0,\\
(n,v)\big|_{t=0}=(n_{\rm in},v_{\rm in}),\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
n
represents the cell density,
c
denotes the chemoattractant density, and
v
denotes the velocity of fluid. In addition,
P
is the pressure and
\phi
is the potential function.

If
v=0
and
\phi=0
, the system (
1.1
) is reduced to the classical 3D parabolic-elliptic Patlak-Keller-Segel system.
If
n=0
and
c=0
, the system (
1.1
) becomes the 3D Navier-Stokes equations.
The Patlak-Keller-Segel system is proposed as a macroscopic model for chemotactic
cell migration,
which was jointly developed by Patlak
[
22
]
, Keller and Segel
[
16
]
.
This system has wide applications in the fields of biology, ecology, and medicine. It is of significant importance in cancer research, simulating bacterial diffusion behavior, and tissue development, among others.

In recent years, extensive mathematical
efforts have been undertaken to detect unbounded solutions of the PKS system.
As long as the dimension of space is higher than one, the solutions of the classical PKS system may blow up in finite time.
The 2D PKS model of parabolic-parabolic has a critical mass of
8\pi
, if the cell mass
M:=||n_{\rm in}||_{L^{1}}
is less than
8\pi
, the solutions of the system are global in time
[
2
]
,
if the cell mass is greater than
8\pi
, the solutions will blow up in finite time
[
23
]
.
Moreover, the 2D parabolic-elliptic Patlak-Keller-Segel system is globally well-posed if and only if the total mass
M\leq 8\pi
by Wei in
[
27
]
.
When the spatial dimension is higher than two, the solutions of the PKS system will blow up for any initial mass, meaning that
no mass threshold for aggregation exists in that case (for example, see
[
12
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
29
]
).
Therefore,

an interesting question is to consider whether the stabilizing effect of the moving fluid can suppress the finite-time blow-up?

Let us first recall some results of shear flows in 2D briefly.

I. The PKS system in 2D.
For the parabolic-elliptic PKS system, Kiselev-Xu suppressed the blow-up by stationary relaxation
enhancing flows or time-dependent Yao-Zlatos near-optimal mixing flows in
\mathbb{T}^{d}

[
17
]
.
Bedrossian-He
[
1
]
also studied the suppression of blow-up by shear flows in

\mathbb{T}^{2}
for the 2D parabolic-elliptic case. He
[
9
]
investigated the suppression of blow-up by a large strictly monotone shear flow for the parabolic-parabolic PKS model in
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
.

II. The PKS-NS system in 2D.
For the coupled PKS-NS system, Zeng-Zhang-Zi considered the 2D PKS-NS system near the Couette flow in
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
, and they
proved that if the Couette flow is sufficiently strong, the solution to the system stays globally regular
[
30
]
.
He considered the blow-up suppression for the parabolic-elliptic PKS-NS system in

\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
with the coupling of buoyancy effects
[
10
]
for a class of small initial data.
Li-Xiang-Xu studied the suppression of blow-up in PKS-NS system via the Poiseuille flow in
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
,
and they showed that if Poiseuille flow is sufficiently strong, the solution is global in
[
18
]
by assuming the smallness of the initial vorticity. Cui-Wang considered Poiseuille flow with the boundary of PKS-NS system and obtained the solutions are global regular without any smallness condition
[
5
]
.

III. The PKS system in 3D.

For the 3D PKS system of parabolic-elliptic case, Bedrossian-He investigated the suppression of blow-up by shear flows in
\mathbb{T}^{3}
and
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
by assuming the initial mass is less than
8\pi
in
[
1
]
.
Feng-Shi-Wang
[
8
]
used the planar helical flows as transport
flow to research the advective Kuramoto-Sivashinsky and
Keller-Segel equations, and they proved that when the amplitude of the flow is large enough, the
L^{2}
norm of the solution is uniformly bounded in time.
Shi-Wang
[
24
]
considered the suppression effect of the flow
(y,y^{2},0)
in
\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
, and Deng-Shi-Wang
[
7
]
proved the Couette flow with a sufficiently large amplitude prevents
the blow-up of solutions in the whole space. Besides, for the stability effect of buoyancy,
Hu-Kiselev-Yao considered the blow-up suppression for the Patlak-Keller-Segel system coupled with a fluid flow that obeys Darcy’s law for incompressible porous media via buoyancy force
[
15
]
.
Hu and Kiselev proved that when the coupling is large enough, the Keller-Segel equations coupled with Stokes-Boussinesq flow is globally well-posed
[
14
]
, see also the recent result by Hu
[
13
]
.

IV. The PKS-NS system in 3D.

For the 3D coupled PKS-NS system, it is still unknown whether the blow-up does not happen provided that the amplitude of some shear flow is sufficiently large. Suppression of blow-up via non-parallel shear flows was obtained in
[
6
]
by Cui-Wang-Wang.
As mentioned by Zeng-Zhang-Zi (Remark 1.3 in
[
30
]

“It is very interesting to investigate the corresponding problems with boundary effects taken into account. ”

Exploring the potential suppression of blow-up when considering the non-slip boundary condition or the 3D Couette flow seems to have not been taken into account in this issue yet. Our main goal is to investigate these issues in this paper.

\displaystyle n(t,x,\pm 1,z)=0,\quad c(t,x,\pm 1,z)=0.

In addition,

\quad u(t,x,\pm 1,z)=0.

(1.4)

Remark 1.1
.

For the three dimensional Navier-Stokes equations, 3D lift-up effect is an important factor leading to its instability. In blow-up suppression, the lift-up effect also brings us difficulties. Here we select the special potential function
\phi=x
, for simplicity.

Inspired by
[
4
]

\omega_{2}=\partial_{z}u_{1}-\partial_{x}u_{3}
and
\triangle u_{2}
, satisfying

\partial_{t}\omega_{2}+Ay\partial_{x}\omega_{2}+A\partial_{z}u_{2}-\triangle\omega_{2}=\partial_{z}n,

and

\partial_{t}\triangle u_{2}+Ay\partial_{x}\triangle u_{2}-\triangle^{2}u_{2}=-\partial_{y}\partial_{x}n.

After the time rescaling

, we get

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\partial_{t}n+y\partial_{x}n-\frac{1}{A}\triangle n=-\frac{1}{A}\nabla\cdot(un)-\frac{1}{A}\nabla\cdot(n\nabla c),\\
\triangle c+n-c=0,\\
\partial_{t}\omega_{2}+y\partial_{x}\omega_{2}-\frac{1}{A}\triangle\omega_{2}=-\partial_{z}u_{2}+\frac{1}{A}\partial_{z}n,\\

\nabla\cdot u=0,\\
\end{array}\right.

Remark 1.2
.

The above results seem to be the first results of considering the suppression effect of the 3D Couette flow in the Patlak-Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes model, and also the first time considering the non-slip boundary. The constant in Theorem
1.2
should not be optimal, and the existence of an optimal threshold value is still unknown. When considering the domain
\mathbb{T}^{3}

[

\mathbb{T}^{2}

[

\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I}\times\mathbb{T}

\displaystyle||n^{p}\nabla c||_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq||n^{p}||_{L^{4}}^{2}||\nabla c||_{L^{4}}^{2}\leq C||n^{p}||_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}||\nabla n^{p}||_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}||\nabla c||_{L^{4}}^{2},

we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}||n^{p}||^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{2(2p-1)}{Ap}||\nabla n^{p}||_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2p-1}{Ap}||\nabla n^{p}||_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C(2p-1)p}{A}||n^{p}||_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}||\nabla n^{p}||_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}||\nabla c||_{L^{4}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{5(2p-1)}{4Ap}||\nabla n^{p}||_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C(2p-1)p^{7}}{A}||n^{p}||_{L^{2}}^{2}||\nabla c||_{L^{4}}^{8}.

Consequently

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}||n^{p}||^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2A}||\nabla n^{p}||_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{Cp^{8}}{A}||n^{p}||_{L^{2}}^{2}||\nabla c||_{L^{4}}^{8}.

(6.1)

Using the Nash inequality again

\displaystyle||n^{p}||_{L^{2}}\leq C||n^{p}||_{L^{1}}^{\frac{2}{5}}||\nabla n^{p}||_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{5}},

we infer from (
6.1
) that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}||n^{p}||^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq-\frac{||n^{p}||_{L^{2}}^{\frac{10}{3}}}{2AC||n^{p}||_{L^{1}}^{\frac{4}{3}}}+\frac{Cp^{8}}{A}||n^{p}||_{L^{2}}^{2}||\nabla c||_{L^{\infty}L^{4}}^{8}.

Applying
Lemma
3.1
,
Lemma
3.2
and
Proposition
5.1
, there holds

\displaystyle||\nabla c||_{L^{\infty}L^{4}}\leq

\displaystyle||\nabla c_{\neq}||_{L^{\infty}L^{4}}+||\nabla c_{0}||_{L^{\infty}L^{4}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|n_{\neq}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+\|n_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\right)\leq C\left(E_{0}+H_{2}\right).

Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}||n^{p}||^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq-\frac{||n^{p}||^{\frac{10}{3}}_{L^{2}}}{2CA||n^{p}||_{L^{1}}^{\frac{4}{3}}}+\frac{Cp^{8}}{A}||n^{p}||^{2}_{L^{2}}(E_{0}^{8}+H_{2}^{8}).

(6.2)

Claim that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}||n^{p}||_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\max\Big\{8C^{3}(E_{0}^{8}+H_{2}^{8})^{\frac{3}{2}}p^{12}\sup_{t\geq 0}||n^{p}||^{2}_{L_{1}},2||n_{\rm in}^{p}||_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big\}.

(6.3)

Otherwise, there must exist
t=\tilde{t}>0
such that

\|n^{p}(\tilde{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\max\{8C^{3}(E_{0}^{8}+H_{2}^{8})^{\frac{3}{2}}p^{12}\|n^{p}(\tilde{t})\|_{L^{1}}^{2},2\|n_{\rm in}^{p}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\},

(6.4)

and

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|n^{p}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)|_{t=\tilde{t}}\geq 0.

(6.5)

According to (
6.2
) and (
6.3
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|n^{p}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)|_{t=\tilde{t}}

(6.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\|n^{p}(\tilde{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left[\frac{\|n^{p}(\tilde{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4}{3}}}{2CA\|n^{p}(\tilde{t})\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{4}{3}}}+\frac{Cp^{8}}{A}(E_{0}^{8}+H_{2}^{8})\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\|n^{p}(\tilde{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left[\frac{\left[8C^{3}(E_{0}^{8}+H_{2}^{8})^{\frac{3}{2}}p^{12}\|n^{p}(\tilde{t})\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\right]^{\frac{2}{3}}}{2CA\|n^{p}(\tilde{t})\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{4}{3}}}-\frac{Cp^{8}}{A}(E_{0}^{8}+H_{2}^{8})\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\|n^{p}(\tilde{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\frac{Cp^{8}(E_{0}^{8}+H_{2}^{8})}{A}<0,

which is a contradiction due to (
6.5
) and (
6.6
). Thus, (
6.3
) is proved.

Next, the Moser-Alikakos iteration is used to determine
E_{1}
. Recall
p=2^{j}
with

and we rewrite (

(6.7)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\Big\{C_{1}\Big(\sup_{t\geq 0}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I}\times\mathbb{T}}|n(t)|^{2^{j}}dxdydz\Big)^{2},2\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I}\times\mathbb{T}}|n_{\rm in}|^{2^{j+1}}dxdydz\Big\},

where

From (

The next lemma will be frequently used in the estimates of
\|n_{(0,0)}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}.

Lemma A.1
.

For given functions
f_{1}(y,z)
and
f_{2}(y,z)
, satisfying
f_{1}(y,z),f_{2}(y,z)\in H^{1}\left(\mathbb{I}\times\mathbb{T}\right)
, there holds

\|(f_{1}f_{2})_{(0,0)}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|f_{2}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{y}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\right).

(A.1)

Especially, if
f_{2}(y,z)\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{I}\times\mathbb{T})
, the result can be simplified as

\|(f_{1}f_{2})_{(0,0)}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|f_{2}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{y}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}},

(A.2)

where
f_{i,(0,0)}=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{T}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f_{i}(y,z)dz,
for
i=1,2.

Proof.

Let
f_{1}=\sum_{k_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{1}^{k_{3}}(y)e^{ik_{3}z}
and

f_{2}=\sum_{k_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{2}^{k_{3}}(y)e^{ik_{3}z},
where

f_{1}^{k_{3}}(y)=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{T}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f_{1}(y,z)e^{-ik_{3}z}dz,\quad f_{2}^{k_{3}}(y)=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{T}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f_{2}(y,z)e^{-ik_{3}z}dz,

then

(f_{1}f_{2})_{(0,0)}=\sum_{k_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{1}^{k_{3}}(y)f_{2}^{-k_{3}}(y).

Notice that

\|f_{2}^{-k_{3}}(y)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\left(\|f_{2}^{-k_{3}}(y)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{y}f_{2}^{-k_{3}}(y)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|f_{2}^{-k_{3}}(y)\|_{L^{2}}\right),

(A.3)

then direct calculations yield that

\displaystyle\|(f_{1}f_{2})_{(0,0)}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}}\|f_{1}^{k_{3}}(y)f_{2}^{-k_{3}}(y)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\sum_{k_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}}\|f_{1}^{k_{3}}(y)\|_{L^{2}}\|f_{2}^{-k_{3}}(y)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{k_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}}\|f_{1}^{k_{3}}(y)\|_{L^{2}}\|f_{2}^{-k_{3}}(y)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{y}f_{2}^{-k_{3}}(y)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{2}}+C\sum_{k_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}}\|f_{1}^{k_{3}}(y)\|_{L^{2}}\|f_{2}^{-k_{3}}(y)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\sum_{k_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}}\|f_{1}^{k_{3}}(y)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{k_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}}\|f_{2}^{k_{3}}(y)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\sum_{k_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\partial_{y}f_{2}^{k_{3}}(y)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle+C\left(\sum_{k_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}}\|f_{1}^{k_{3}}(y)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{k_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}}\|f_{2}^{k_{3}}(y)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{y}f_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\right),

which implies (
A.1
).

Moreover, for
f_{2}\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{I}\times\mathbb{T}),
there is no lower order term in (
A.3
), thus (
A.2
) holds.
The proof is complete.

To estimate
\|n_{(0,\neq)}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}},
we need the following lemma to provide the bound-ness of

Lemma A.2
.

Let
f(y,z)
be a function such that
f_{(0,\neq)}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{I}\times\mathbb{T})
, then there holds

||f_{(0,\neq)}||_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\big\|\nabla f_{(0,\neq)}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\epsilon}\big\|\partial_{z}\nabla f_{(0,\neq)}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{\epsilon},

where
\epsilon\in(0,1].

Proof.

For given

f_{(0,\neq)}=\sum_{k_{3}\neq 0}f_{(0,\neq)}^{k_{3}}(y)e^{ik_{3}z},~~{\rm and}~~f_{(0,\neq)}^{k_{3}}(y)=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{T}|}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f_{(0,\neq)}(y,z)e^{-ik_{3}z}dz,

Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality implies

||f_{(0,\neq)}^{k_{3}}(t,y)||_{L^{\infty}_{y}}\leq C\left(||f_{(0,\neq)}^{k_{3}}(t,\cdot)||_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}||\partial_{y}f_{(0,\neq)}^{k_{3}}(t,\cdot)||_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+||f_{(0,\neq)}^{k_{3}}(t,\cdot)||_{L^{2}}\right),

and we have

\displaystyle||f_{(0,\neq)}||_{L^{\infty}}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{3}\neq 0}||f_{(0,\neq)}^{k_{3}}(t,y)||_{L^{\infty}_{y}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{k_{3}\neq 0}||f_{(0,\neq)}^{k_{3}}(t,\cdot)||_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}||\partial_{y}f_{(0,\neq)}^{k_{3}}(t,\cdot)||_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\sum_{k_{3}\neq 0}||f_{(0,\neq)}^{k_{3}}(t,\cdot)||_{L^{2}}.

Furthermore, by Hölder’s inequality we get

\displaystyle||f_{(0,\neq)}||_{L^{\infty}}\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\sum_{k_{3}\neq 0}|k_{3}|^{1+2\epsilon}||f_{(0,\neq)}^{k_{3}}(t,\cdot)||_{L^{2}}||\partial_{y}f_{(0,\neq)}^{k_{3}}(t,\cdot)||_{L^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{k_{3}\neq 0}\frac{1}{|k_{3}|^{1+2\epsilon}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+C\left(\sum_{k_{3}\neq 0}|k_{3}|^{2\varepsilon}\|k_{3}f_{(0,\neq)}^{k_{3}}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{k_{3}\neq 0}\frac{1}{|k_{3}|^{1+\varepsilon}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\sum_{k_{3}\neq 0}|k_{3}|^{2\epsilon}||k_{3}f_{(0,\neq)}^{k_{3}}(t,\cdot)||^{2}_{L^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}\Big(\sum_{k_{3}\neq 0}|k_{3}|^{2\epsilon}\|\partial_{y}f_{(0,\neq)}^{k_{3}}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle+C\left(\sum_{k_{3}\neq 0}|k_{3}|^{2\varepsilon}\|k_{3}f_{(0,\neq)}^{k_{3}}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\epsilon\in(0,1].

Using Hölder’s inequality again, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{3}\neq 0}|k_{3}|^{2\epsilon}||k_{3}f_{(0,\neq)}^{k_{3}}(t,\cdot)||^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k_{3}\neq 0}\|k_{3}^{2}f_{(0,\neq)}^{k_{3}}(t,\cdot)\|^{2\epsilon}_{L^{2}}\|k_{3}f_{(0,\neq)}^{k_{3}}(t,\cdot)\|^{2(1-\epsilon)}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\big\|\partial_{z}f_{(0,\neq)}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2(1-\epsilon)}\big\|\partial_{z}\nabla f_{(0,\neq)}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2\epsilon}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{3}\neq 0}|k_{3}|^{2\epsilon}||\partial_{y}f_{(0,\neq)}^{k_{3}}(t,\cdot)||^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial_{y}f_{(0,\neq)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2(1-\epsilon)}\big\|\partial_{z}\nabla f_{(0,\neq)}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2\epsilon}.

Thus, we conclude that

\displaystyle||f_{(0,\neq)}||_{L^{\infty}}\leq

\displaystyle C\big\|\partial_{z}f_{(0,\neq)}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}}\|\partial_{y}f_{(0,\neq)}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}}\big\|\partial_{z}\nabla f_{(0,\neq)}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{\epsilon}

\displaystyle+C\|\partial_{z}f_{(0,\neq)}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\varepsilon}\|\partial_{z}\nabla f_{(0,\neq)}\|_{L^{2}}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big\|\nabla f_{(0,\neq)}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\epsilon}\big\|\partial_{z}\nabla f_{(0,\neq)}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{\epsilon},

for
\epsilon\in(0,1].
We complete the proof.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
f(y,z)
be a function such that

|f_{(0,\neq)}|\leq\int_{-1}^{1}|\partial_{y}f_{(0,\neq)}|dy.

Similarly

|f_{(0,\neq)}|\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi}|\partial_{z}f_{(0,\neq)}|dz.

(A.6)

It follows from (

) and (

) that

), there holds

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{I}\times\mathbb{T}}\left|\nabla\left(f_{(0,\neq)}^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\right|dydz\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\frac{9}{4}\|f_{(0,\neq)}\|_{L^{1}}\|\nabla f_{(0,\neq)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

Abstract
. The local h h -polynomial was introduced by Stanley as a fundamental enumerative invariant of a triangulation Δ \Delta of a simplex. This polynomial is known to have nonnegative and symmetric coefficients and is conjectured to be γ \gamma -positive when Δ \Delta is flag. This paper shows that the local h h -polynomial has the stronger property of being real-rooted when Δ \Delta is the barycentric subdivision of an arbitrary triangulation Γ \Gamma of the simplex. An analogous result for edgewise subdivisions is proven. The proofs are based on a new combinatorial formula for the local h h -polynomial of Δ \Delta , which is valid when Δ \Delta is any uniform triangulation of Γ \Gamma . A combinatorial interpretation of the local h h -polynomial of the second barycentric subdivision of the simplex is deduced.

This section includes background from the theory

which will be necessary to understand the following
sections; some excellent expositions are
[
9
,
10
,
27
]
. For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, throughout this
paper we set
[n]:=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
and denote
by
{\mathbb{R}}_{n}[x]

2.1.
Polynomials

A polynomial
f(x)=a_{0}+a_{1}x+\cdots+a_{n}x^{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{n}[x]
is called

\bullet

symmetric
, with center of symmetry
n/2
,
if
a_{i}=a_{n-i}
for all
0\leq i\leq n
,

\bullet

unimodal
, if
a_{0}\leq a_{1}\leq\cdots\leq a_{k}\geq a_{k+1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n}

for some
0\leq k\leq n
,

\bullet

\gamma
-positive
, with center of symmetry

n/2
, if
f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\gamma_{i}x^{i}(1+x)^{n-2i}
for some nonnegative real
numbers
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
,

\bullet

real-rooted
, if every root of
f(x)
is real,
or
f(x)\equiv 0
.

Every real-rooted, symmetric polynomial with
nonnegative coefficients is
\gamma
-positive, every

\gamma
-positive polynomial is symmetric and unimodal
and every real-rooted polynomial with nonnegative
coefficients is unimodal; see
[
10
,
16
]
for
more information on the connections among these
concepts.

A real-rooted polynomial
f(x)
, with roots

\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha_{2}\geq\cdots
, is said to

interlace
a real-rooted polynomial
g(x)
, with
roots
\beta_{1}\geq\beta_{2}\geq\cdots
, if

\cdots\leq\alpha_{2}\leq\beta_{2}\leq\alpha_{1}\leq\beta_{1}.

We then write
f(x)\preceq g(x)
. By convention,
the zero polynomial interlaces and is interlaced by
every real-rooted polynomial and nonzero constant
polynomials strictly interlace all polynomials of
degree at most one. If two or more real-rooted
polynomials with positive leading coefficients
interlace (respectively, are interlaced by) a
real-rooted polynomial
f(x)
, then so does their
sum. We will write
{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(f(x))=x^{n}f(1/x)
for

f(x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{n}[x]
. Then, for real-rooted
polynomials
f(x),g(x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{n}[x]
with
nonnegative coefficients,
f(x)\preceq g(x)\Rightarrow{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(g(x))\preceq{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(f(x))
. We
will use these properties of interlacing
frequently in Sections
4

and
5
; see
[
10
, Section 7.8]

for more information.

A sequence
(f_{0}(x),f_{1}(x),\dots,f_{n}(x))
of
real-rooted polynomials is called

interlacing
if
f_{i}(x)\preceq f_{j}(x)

for all
0\leq i<j\leq n
. The properties of
interlacing sequences in the following lemmas
will be used in Sections
4

and
5
. We recall that, given a
positive integer
r
, the
i
th Veronese

r
-section operator is defined on polynomials
or formal power series by the formula

{\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{i}\left(\,\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}x^{n}\right)=\,\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{i+rn}x^{n}

for
i\in\{0,1,\dots,r-1\}
. We have

(3)

{\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{i}(x^{j}f(x))=\begin{cases}{\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{i-j}(f(x)),&\text{if $i\geq j$}\\
x{\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{r-j+i}(f(x)),&\text{if $i<j$}\end{cases}

for
i,j\in\{0,1,\dots,r-1\}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
30
, Proposition 3.3]
)

Let
f_{0}(x),f_{1}(x),\dots,f_{n}(x)\in{\mathbb{R}}[x]
be
nonzero real-rooted polynomials. If
f_{i-1}(x)\preceq f_{i}(x)
for every
i\in[n]
and
f_{0}(x)\preceq f_{n}(x)
, then
(f_{0}(x),f_{1}(x),\dots,f_{n}(x))
is an interlacing sequence.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f(x)\in{\mathbb{R}}[x]
be a polynomial with
nonnegative coefficients such that

({\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{r-j}(f(x)))_{1\leq j\leq r}:=({\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{r-1}(f(x)),\dots,{\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{1}(f(x)),{\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{0}(f(x)))

is an interlacing sequence of real-rooted
polynomials.

(a)

If
g(x)=x^{m}f(x)
for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then

({\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{r-j}(g(x)))_{1\leq j\leq r}
is also an
interlacing sequence of real-rooted polynomials.

(b)

(cf.
[
32
, Theorem 2.2]
)
If
g(x)=(1+x+x^{2}+\cdots+x^{t})f(x)
for some
t\in\{0,1,\dots,r-1\}
, then
({\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{r-j}(g(x)))_{1\leq j\leq r}
is also an interlacing
sequence of real-rooted polynomials.

Proof.

We only need to prove part (a) and for that, we
may assume that
m=1
. Then, the result is
equivalent to the obvious fact that
({\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{r-2}(f(x)),\dots,{\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{0}(f(x)),x{\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{r-1}(f(x)))
is an interlacing sequence of real-rooted
polynomials.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f_{1}(x),f_{n}(x),\dots,f_{n}(x)\in{\mathbb{R}}[x]
be
polynomials with nonnegative coefficients and let

g_{0}(x),g_{1}(x),\dots,g_{n}(x)
be defined by the
equation

(4)

\begin{pmatrix}g_{0}(x)\\
g_{1}(x)\\
\vdots\\
g_{n}(x)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&\cdots&1\\
x&1&\cdots&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
x&x&\cdots&1\\
x&x&\cdots&x\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}f_{1}(x)\\
f_{2}(x)\\
\vdots\\
f_{n}(x).\end{pmatrix}

If
(f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x),\dots,f_{n}(x))
is an
interlacing sequence of real-rooted polynomials,
then so is the sequence
(g_{0}(x),g_{1}(x),\dots,g_{n}(x))
.

Proof.

This follows by a standard application of

[
10
, Theorem 7.8.5]
. Indeed, one needs to
verify that every
2\times 2
submatrix of the

(n+1)\times n
matrix on the right-hand side of

4
) preserves interlacing
sequences of polynomials with nonnegative
coefficients. This has already been

[

and

[

.
∎

2.2.
Simplicial complexes and
triangulations

We assume familiarity with basic notions about
simplicial complexes and their triangulations

[
9
,
25
,
27
]
. All simplicial
complexes considered
here will be finite. We denote by
|V|
the
cardinality of a finite set
V
and by
2^{V}
the
collection of all subsets of
V
, which is the
abstract simplex with vertex set
V
.

The
h
-polynomial
of an

(n-1)
-dimensional simplicial complex
\Delta

is defined by the formula

(5)

h(\Delta,x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}f_{i-1}(\Delta)x^{i}(1-x)^{n-i},

where
f_{i}(\Delta)
is the number of
i
-dimensional
faces of
\Delta
. The sequence
(h_{0}(\Delta),h_{1}(\Delta),\dots,h_{n}(\Delta))
is the

h
-vector
of
\Delta
.

By the term
triangulation
of a simplicial
complex
\Gamma
we will mean a geometric
triangulation. Thus, a simplicial complex
\Delta

is a triangulation of
\Gamma
if there exists a
geometric realization
L
of
\Delta
which
geometrically subdivides a geometric realization

K
of
\Gamma
. The
carrier
of a face

G\in\Delta
is the smallest face

F\in\Gamma
for which the face of
K

corresponding to
F
contains the face of
L

corresponding to
G
. The
restriction
of

\Delta
to a face
F\in\Gamma
is the
subcomplex of
\Delta
which consists of those
faces whose carrier is contained in
F
. This
complex is a triangulation of the simplex
2^{F}

and is denoted by
\Delta_{F}
. A simplicial
complex
\Delta
is called
flag
if every
minimal nonface of
\Delta
has two elements.

The
local
h
-polynomial
of a
triangulation
\Delta
of a simplex
2^{V}
is
defined by Equation (
1
). By the
principle of inclusion-exclusion,

(6)

h(\Delta,x)\,=\sum_{F\subseteq V}\ell_{F}(\Delta_{F},x).

The polynomial
\ell_{V}(\Delta,x)
is symmetric,
with center of symmetry
|V|/2
, and has
nonnegative coefficients; see
[
26
]

[
27
, Section III.10]
for more information
about this important concept.

Barycentric and edgewise subdivisions
.
Let
\Delta
be an
(n-1)
-dimensional simplicial
complex with vertex set
V(\Delta)
and
r
be a
positive integer. The
barycentric subdivision

of
\Delta
, denoted by
{\rm sd}(\Delta)
, is
defined as the simplicial complex of all chains in
the poset of nonempty faces of
\Delta
. The edgewise
subdivision depends on
r
and a linear ordering of

V(\Delta)
(although its face vector is independent
of the latter). Given such an ordering

v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{m}
, we denote by
V_{r}(\Delta)
the
set of maps
f:V(\Delta)\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}
such that

{\rm supp}(f)\in\Delta
and
f(v_{1})+f(v_{2})+\cdots+f(v_{m})=r
, where
{\rm supp}(f)
is the set of all

v\in V(\Delta)
for which
f(v)\neq 0
. For
f\in V_{r}(\Delta)
, we let
\iota(f):V(\Delta)\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}
be
the map defined by setting
\iota(f)(v_{j})=f(v_{1})+f(v_{2})+\cdots+f(v_{j})
for
j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}
.
The
r
-fold edgewise subdivision
of
\Delta
,
denoted by
{\rm esd}_{r}(\Delta)
, is the simplicial complex
on the vertex set
V_{r}(\Delta)
of which a set

E\subseteq V_{r}(\Delta)
is a face if the following
two conditions are satisfied:

\bullet

\bigcup_{f\in E}\,{\rm supp}(f)\in\Delta
and

\bullet

\iota(f)-\iota(g)\in\{0,1\}^{V(\Delta)}
, or

\iota(g)-\iota(f)\in\{0,1\}^{V(\Delta)}
, for
all
f,g\in E
.

The simplicial complexes
{\rm sd}(\Delta)
and

{\rm esd}_{r}(\Delta)
can be realized as triangulations
of
\Delta
; see
[
4
]

[
9
, Section 9.3]

[
25
, Section 3.2]

and the references given there.

Uniform triangulations
. A triangulation

\Delta
of an
(n-1)
-dimensional simplicial
complex
\Gamma
is called
uniform

[
5
]
if for all
0\leq i\leq j\leq n
and
for every
(j-1)
-dimensional face
F\in\Gamma
,
the number of
(i-1)
-dimensional faces of the
restriction
\Delta_{F}
depends only on
i
and
j
.
Denoting this number by
f(i,j)
, we say that the
triangular array
{\mathcal{F}}=(f(i,j))_{0\leq i\leq j\leq n}
is the
f
-triangle
associated to
\Delta

and that
\Delta
is an
{\mathcal{F}}
-uniform

triangulation of
\Gamma
. Barycentric and
r
-fold
edgewise subdivisions are prototypical examples of
uniform triangulations. Clearly, the restriction of
an
{\mathcal{F}}
-uniform triangulation of
\Gamma
to any
subcomplex of
\Gamma
is an
{\mathcal{F}}
-uniform
triangulation of that subcomplex.

Following
[
5
]
, we denote by
\sigma_{n}

the
(n-1)
-dimensional abstract simplex. One of the
main results of
[
5
]
shows that for
0\leq k\leq m\leq n
, there exist polynomials

p_{{\mathcal{F}},m,k}(x)
which depend only on
{\mathcal{F}}
and

an interlacing sequence of real-rooted polynomials

0\leq k\leq n
. Moreover,
d_{n,k,j}(x)

is interlaced by the Eulerian polynomial
A_{n}(x)

for every
j\in\{0,1,\dots,n-k\}
.

Proof.

The result is trivial for
n\in\{0,1\}
, so
we assume that
n\geq 2
. By
Proposition
4.3
we have

\begin{pmatrix}d_{n,k,0}(x)\\
\vdots\\
d_{n,k,n-k}(x)\\
d_{n,k,n-k+1}(x)\\
\vdots\\
d_{n,k,n}(x)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&\cdots&1&\cdots&1\\
x&1&\cdots&1&\cdots&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
x&x&\cdots&1&\cdots&1\\
x&x&\cdots&1+x&\ddots&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
x&x&\cdots&x&\cdots&1+x\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}d_{n-1,k,0}(x)\\
d_{n-1,k,1}(x)\\
\vdots\\
d_{n-1,k,n-1}(x)\end{pmatrix}

for
k\in\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}
and

\begin{pmatrix}d_{n,k,0}(x)\\
d_{n,k,1}(x)\\
\vdots\\
d_{n,k,n-1}(x)\\
d_{n,k,n}(x)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&\cdots&1\\
x&1&\cdots&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
x&x&\cdots&1\\
x&x&\cdots&x\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}d_{n-1,k-1,0}(x)\\
d_{n-1,k-1,1}(x)\\
\vdots\\
d_{n-1,k-1,n-1}(x)\end{pmatrix}

for
k=n
. In both cases, multiplication on the
left by the given matrix preserves the interlacing
of sequences of real-rooted polynomials with
nonnegative coefficients (see

[
20
, Theorem 2.4]
for a more general
statement, in the former case, and
Lemma
2.3
in the latter) and thus the
first statement follows by induction on
n
.

We now deduce that
A_{n}(x)\preceq d_{n,k,j}(x)

for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and all
0\leq k,j\leq n

with
k+j\leq n
. We first show that the sequence

(d_{n,k}(x))_{0\leq k\leq n}
is interlacing for
every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
(as already mentioned, this also
follows from
[
11
, Theorem 3.6]
). Indeed, by
the first statement and Proposition
4.2

(g) we have

d_{n,k}(x)=d_{n,k,0}(x)\preceq d_{n,k,n}(x)=xd_{n,k-1}(x)

for
1\leq k\leq n
and hence
d_{n,k-1}(x)\preceq d_{n,k}(x)
. Since
d_{n,n}(x)
has symmetric
coefficients, with center of symmetry
n/2
, we
conclude that

\displaystyle A_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=d_{n,0}(x)\preceq d_{n,1}(x)\preceq\cdots\preceq d_{n,n}(x)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{I}}_{n}(d_{n,n}(x))\preceq\cdots\preceq{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(d_{n,1}(x))\preceq{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(d_{n,0}(x))

\displaystyle={\mathcal{I}}_{n}(A_{n}(x))=xA_{n}(x).

Since
A_{n}(x)\preceq xA_{n}(x)
, our claim follows
by an application of Lemma
2.1
. We now
recall from Proposition
3.2
(b) that

d_{n,k,n-k-j}(x)={\mathcal{I}}_{n}(d_{n,k,j}(x))
for all
0\leq j\leq n-k
and conclude that

\displaystyle A_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\preceq d_{n,k}(x)=d_{n,k,0}(x)\preceq d_{n,k,1}(x)\preceq\cdots\preceq d_{n,k,n-k}(x)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{I}}_{n}(d_{n,k,0}(x))={\mathcal{I}}_{n}(d_{n,k}(x))\preceq{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(A_{n}(x))

\displaystyle=xA_{n}(x)

for every
k\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
. Another
application of Lemma
2.1
shows that

A_{n}(x)\preceq d_{n,k,j}(x)
for all
0\leq j\leq n-k
and the proof follows.
∎

Proof of
Theorem
4.1
.

Since
\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k,j}(x)=d_{n,k,j}(x)
for

0\leq j\leq n-k
by Proposition
4.2

(b), the result follows from
Theorem
4.4
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem
3.1
shows that
\ell_{V}({\rm sd}(\Gamma),x)
can be expressed as a nonnegative
linear combination of the polynomials

\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k,j}(x)
with
k,j\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
and

. Since each of these

.

5.
Edgewise subdivisions

This section proves Theorem
1.4
, following
the strategy of the proof of Theorem
1.2
,
given in Section
4
, and using the special
features of the combinatorics of edgewise subdivisions.
Throughout it,
{\mathcal{F}}
stands for the
f
-triangle of
size
n
for the
r
-fold edgewise subdivision for
some fixed integer
r\geq n
.

We let
E_{n,r}(x)=p_{{\mathcal{F}},n,0}(x)=h_{\mathcal{F}}(\sigma_{n},x)
be the
h
-polynomial of the
r
-fold edgewise
subdivision of the
(n-1)
-dimensional simplex. By

[
4
, Equation (8)]
we have

(22)

E_{n,r}(x)={\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{0}\left((1+x+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{n}\right)=\sum_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}_{n,r}}x^{{\rm asc}(w)},

where
{\mathcal{W}}_{n,r}
is the set of words
w:\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}\rightarrow\{0,1,\dots,r-1\}
with
w(0)=0

and
{\rm asc}(w)
is the number of indices
i\in[n-1]

such that
w(i-1)<w(i)
.

Proposition 5.1
.

The following formulas hold for the
r
-fold
edgewise subdivision for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
k,j\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
:

\displaystyle p_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k}(x)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{0}\left(x^{k}(1+x+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{n}\right),

\displaystyle\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k}(x)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{0}\left((1+x+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{n-k}(x+x^{2}+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{k}\right),

\displaystyle\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k,j}(x)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{0}\left(x^{j}(1+x+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{n-k}(x+x^{2}+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{k}\right).

Proof.

The second formula is the special case
j=0
of
the third. To verify the first and third formulas
we apply Equations (
10
), (
11
)
and (
22
) to compute that

\displaystyle p_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k}{k\choose i}(x-1)^{i}p_{{\mathcal{F}},n-i,0}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k}{k\choose i}(x-1)^{i}{\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{0}\left((1+x+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{n-i}\right)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{0}\left(\,\sum_{i=0}^{k}{k\choose i}(x^{r}-1)^{i}(1+x+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{n-i}\right)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{0}\left((1+x+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{n-k}(x+x^{2}+\cdots+x^{r})^{k}\right)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{0}\left(x^{k}(1+x+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{n}\right)

and

\displaystyle\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k,j}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}{k\choose i}p_{{\mathcal{F}},n-i,j}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}{k\choose i}{\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{0}\left(x^{j}(1+x+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{n-i}\right)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{0}\left(\,\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}{k\choose i}x^{j}(1+x+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{n-i}\right)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{0}\left(x^{j}(1+x+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{n-k}(x+x^{2}+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{k}\right)

and the proof follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We follow the steps of the proof of
Theorem
1.2
. By
Proposition
5.1
and
Equation (
3
), and since
r\geq n
, we have

\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k,j}(x)=\begin{cases}{\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{0}(f(x)),&\text{if $j=0$}\\
x{\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{r-j}(f(x)),&\text{if $j\geq 1$}\end{cases}

for
j\in\{0,1,\dots,n-k\}
, where

f(x)=x^{k}(1+x+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{n-k}(1+x+\cdots+x^{r-2})^{k}.

This formula and Lemma
2.2
imply
that
(\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k,j}(x))_{0\leq j\leq n-k}

is an interlacing sequence of real-rooted
polynomials for all
0\leq k\leq n
. In
particular,
\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k}(x))
is real-rooted
for all
0\leq k\leq n
. In view of
Theorem
3.1
, it suffices to show
that
E_{n,r}(x)\preceq\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k,j}(x))

for all
0\leq k,j\leq n
with
k+j\leq n
.

We now claim that the sequence

(\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k}(x))_{0\leq k\leq n}
is
interlacing for all
0\leq k\leq n
as well.
We set

g(x)=(1+x+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{n-k-1}(x+x^{2}+\cdots+x^{r-1})^{k}.

By Proposition
5.1
, we have

\displaystyle\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k}(x)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{0}\left((1+x+\cdots+x^{r-1})g(x)\right)={\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{0}(g(x))+x\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}{\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{i}(g(x))

\displaystyle\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k+1}(x)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{0}\left((x+x^{2}+\cdots+x^{r-1})g(x)\right)=x\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}{\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{i}(g(x)).

Since the sequence
({\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{r-j}(g(x)))_{1\leq j\leq r}
is interlacing by
Lemma
2.2
, we have

{\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{0}(g(x))\preceq x\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}{\mathcal{S}}^{r}_{i}(g(x))

and hence
\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k}(x)\preceq\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k+1}(x)
for every
k\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
. Since
\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,n}(x)=\ell_{V}({\rm esd}_{r}(2^{V}),x)
has symmetric coefficients, with
center of symmetry
n/2
, we conclude that

\displaystyle E_{n,r}(x)

\displaystyle=\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,0}(x)\preceq\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,1}(x)\preceq\cdots\preceq\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,n}(x)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,n}(x))\preceq\cdots\preceq{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,1}(x))\preceq{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,0}(x))

\displaystyle={\mathcal{I}}_{n}(E_{n,r}(x)).

Since
E_{n,r}(x)\preceq{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(E_{n,r}(x))
by

[
5
, Corollary 7.3]
and

[
11
, Theorem 2.7]
, our claim follows by an
application of Lemma
2.1
. We now
recall from Proposition
3.2
(b) that

\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k,n-k-j}(x)={\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k,j}(x))
for all
0\leq j\leq n-k

and conclude that

\displaystyle E_{n,r}(x)

\displaystyle\preceq\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k}(x)=\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k,0}(x)\preceq\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k,1}(x)\preceq\cdots\preceq\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k,n-k}(x)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k,0}(x))={\mathcal{I}}_{n}(\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k}(x))

\displaystyle\preceq{\mathcal{I}}_{n}(E_{n,r}(x))

for every
k\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
. A new
application of Lemma
2.1
shows that

E_{n,r}(x)\preceq\ell_{{\mathcal{F}},n,k,j}(x)
for all

0\leq j\leq n-k
and the proof follows.
∎

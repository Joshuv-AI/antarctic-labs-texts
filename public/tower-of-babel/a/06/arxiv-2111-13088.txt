Suslin’s cancellation conjecture in the smooth case
Jean Fasel

Abstract
We prove that stably isomorphic vector bundles of rank d − 1 d-1 on a smooth affine d d -fold X X over an algebraically closed field k k are indeed isomorphic, provided d ! ∈ k × d!\in k^{\times} . This answers an old conjecture of Suslin.



1
Introduction

Let
k
be a field and let
X
be an affine connected scheme of finite type over
k
. For any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
\mathcal{V}_{n}(X)
be the set of isomorphism classes of vector bundles of rank
n
over
X
pointed by the class of the free bundle of rank
n
. Adding a free factor of rank one induces a map of pointed sets

s_{n}:\mathcal{V}_{n}(X)\to\mathcal{V}_{n+1}(X)

whose properties it is important to understand in order to completely classify vector bundles on
X
. It is straightforward to check that the limit term (or stable term)
\mathcal{V}(X)=\lim_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathcal{V}_{n}(X)
is in bijection with the reduced Grothendieck group
\widetilde{\mathrm{K}}_{0}(X)=\ker\left(\mathrm{rk}:\mathrm{K}_{0}(X)\to{\mathbb{Z}}\right)
which is in principle computable using the techniques of algebraic
K
-theory. Early results in this field of study aimed at comparing the unstable terms
\mathcal{V}_{n}(X)
with the stable one. A famous theorem of Serre states that the map
s_{n}
is onto provided
n
is at least the (Krull) dimension of
X
, while Bass’ cancellation theorem states that
s_{n}
is injective provided
n\geq\mathrm{dim}(X)+1
. Consequently, the set
\mathcal{V}_{n}(X)
is already stable (and thus computable in principle) for such an integer
n
. These two fundamental results are the best possible in general, as well-known and easy examples show. The situation improves however if the base field is supposed to have extra (cohomological) properties. Suslin demonstrated first in
[
Sus77b
]
that the map
s_{n}
has trivial fiber (i.e.
s_{n}^{-1}(\{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{n+1}\})=\{{\mathcal{O}}_{X}^{n}\}
) if
n=\mathrm{dim}(X)
and
k
is algebraically closed, before proving shortly after that in fact
s_{n}
is injective (
[
Sus77a
]
). Slightly later, he managed to show that
s_{n}
has trivial fiber provided
X
is normal and defined over a field
k
of cohomological dimension at most
1
with
n!\in k^{\times}
(
[
Sus82
]
), and Bhatwadekar deduced from his result that
s_{n}
is indeed injective under the more restrictive assumption that
k
is of characteristic
0
and
C_{1}
(
[
Bha03
]
). This string of results raised the question to know if
s_{n}
was also injective for
n<\mathrm{dim}(X)
. In fact, Suslin wrote in
[
Sus80
]
that the correct bound should be
n\geq\frac{\mathrm{dim}(X)+1}{2}
, probably based on analogy with the topological situation. This question was quickly addressed by Mohan Kumar who produced examples in
[
Kum85
]
of smooth rational varieties
X
over algebraically closed fields and non trivial vector bundles of rank
\mathrm{dim}(X)-2\geq 2
in the fiber of
s_{d-2}
, thus proving that the map is not injective in general and leaving only the following question open.

Conjecture 1

(Suslin’s cancellation conjecture)
.

Let
X
be an affine variety of dimension
d\geq 3
over an algebraically closed field
k
. Then, the map

s_{d-1}:\mathcal{V}_{d-1}(X)\to\mathcal{V}_{d}(X)

is injective. In other words, two vector bundles
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
of rank
d-1
are stably isomorphic if and only if they are isomorphic.

Some 30 years after Mohan Kumar’s example, a first general positive result was obtained in
[
Fas11
]
where it was shown that
s_{2}
has trivial fiber provided
X
is a smooth threefold and
k
is of characteristic different from
2
. A bit later, this result was extended in
[
FRS12
]
to all integers
d\geq 3
provided
(d-1)!\in k^{\times}
and
X
is normal if
d\geq 4
(in case
d=3
smoothness is still required). Drawing on experiences from the past, it was expected at that time that the triviality of the fiber of
s_{d-1}
was the main step towards a positive answer to Suslin’s conjecture. However, the techniques developed in
[
Sus77a
]
,
[
Bha03
]
or
[
Kes09
]
don’t seem to be easily adaptable to this more complicated situation, and Suslin’s cancellation conjecture has remained open since then. Over the years, it has become clear that new ideas and technical inputs were needed to settle the question. A promising set of results were recently obtained using the motivic homotopy category
\mathcal{H}(k)
of Morel-Voevodsky (
[
MV99
]
), which allows to import many techniques of algebraic topology in algebraic geometry. Using this extended framework and cohomological computations, a complete classification of vector bundles on smooth affine threefolds over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic different from
2
was obtained in
[
AF14b
]
, settling Suslin’s conjecture in that case as a consequence. Following this train of thought, Du recently adapted techniques from algebraic topology and proved Suslin’s conjecture for oriented bundles in
[
Du22
]
assuming a deep conjecture on the second nontrivial homotopy sheaf of
\mathbb{A}^{d}\smallsetminus 0
, while Syed obtained positive results in
[
Sye22
]
for fourfolds. The main object of this paper is to prove Conjecture
1
in the smooth case under some additional mild hypotheses.

Theorem 2
.

Let
X
be a smooth affine scheme of dimension
d\geq 3
over an algebraically closed field
k
such that
d!\in k^{\times}
. Then, the map

s_{d-1}:\mathcal{V}_{d-1}(X)\to\mathcal{V}_{d}(X)

is injective, i.e. two vector bundles
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
are stably isomorphic if and only if they are isomorphic.

Let us now explain the main ideas of the proof. As hinted above, we use the pointed motivic homotopy category
\mathcal{H}(k)
of Morel-Voevodsky. By results of Morel, Schlichting and Asok-Hoyois-Wendt (
[
Mor12
]
,
[
Sch17
]
and
[
AHW17
]
), we know that the classifying space
\mathrm{BGL}_{n}
classifies rank
n
vector bundles for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, in the sense that there is a functorial bijection of pointed sets

[X_{+},\mathrm{BGL}_{n}]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}\simeq\mathcal{V}_{n}(X),\text{ $X$ smooth affine.}

The morphism
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}
defined by
M\mapsto\mathrm{diag}(M,1)
induces a morphism of spaces
\mathrm{BGL}_{n}\to\mathrm{BGL}_{n+1}
yielding a map of pointed sets

[X_{+},\mathrm{BGL}_{n}]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}\to[X_{+},\mathrm{BGL}_{n+1}]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}

which, under the previous identification, is precisely
s_{n}
. If
X
is smooth affine of dimension
d
over
k=\overline{k}
, it follows from Suslin’s cancellation theorem for rank
d
bundles that
[X_{+},\mathrm{BGL}_{d}]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}\simeq[X_{+},\mathrm{BGL}]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}\simeq\mathcal{V}(X)
, and we are led to consider the morphism of spaces

\mathrm{BGL}_{d-1}\to\mathrm{BGL}.

We may then use the Moore-Postnikov tower of this morphism in
\mathcal{H}(k)
, which provides on the one hand a series of (motivic) obstructions to lift a morphism
X\to\mathrm{BGL}
to a morphism
X\to\mathrm{BGL}_{d-1}
and on the other hand a “count” of the possible liftings. These obstructions are constructed by considering suitable cohomology groups of
X
with coefficients in the (twisted) homotopy sheaves
{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{i}(F_{d-1})
, where
F_{d-1}
is the homotopy fiber of
\mathrm{BGL}_{d-1}\to\mathrm{BGL}
. In theory, this allows to completely settle the problem, but there are however some technicalities involved. First, a given vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
of rank
d-1
on
X
provides a morphism
X\to\mathrm{BGL}_{d-1}
whose composite
X\to\mathrm{BGL}_{d-1}\to\mathrm{BGL}
is not necessarily homotopy trivial: This is the case if and only if the vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
is stably free. This forces to somehow change the base point of
[X_{+},\mathrm{BGL}_{d-1}]
and track the relevant changes in the obstructions provided by the Moore-Postnikov tower. This process is explained in
[
Du22
]
, but we give a streamlined presentation in order to avoid non necessary hypotheses, such as the triviality of the determinant of
\mathcal{E}
. The second problem is a priori much more serious. Out of the two homotopy sheaves of
F_{d-1}
needed in the process, we only know the first one! Using convenient fiber sequences and some cohomological vanishing statements due to the fact that the base field is algebraically closed, we are able to reduce to prove the following theorem, which is of independent interest.

Theorem 3
.

Let
X
be a smooth affine scheme of dimension
d\geq 3
over an algebraically closed field
k
with
d!\in k^{\times}
. Then,

\mathrm{H}^{d}_{\mathrm{Nis}}(X,{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{d}({\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0))=\mathrm{H}^{d}_{\mathrm{Zar}}(X,{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{d}({\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0))=0.

We note here that this vanishing statement was expected in view of the conjecture on the structure of the sheaf
{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{d}({\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0)
stated in
[
AF13
]
. We also note that this result, though very useful in classification problems does
not
imply the conjecture in
loc. cit.
(which was recently addressed in characteristic zero in the preprint
[
ABH24
]
). To prove the above theorem, we first observe that by general results of Morel (
[
Mor12
, Corollary 5.43]
), the cohomology groups computed either in the Zariski topology or in the Nisnevich topology coincide. We then show that
\mathrm{H}^{d}_{\mathrm{Nis}}(X,{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{d}({\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0))
is
d!\cdot(d-1)!
-torsion using famous matrices defined by Suslin in
[
Sus77a
]
, and conclude the proof by showing that it is also
d!\cdot(d-1)!
-divisible, provided
d!\in k^{\times}
. The crucial input in our argument is the fact that the set
[X_{+},\mathbb{A}^{d}\smallsetminus 0]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}
is a group in that situation, and that this group is divisible by
[
FRS12
]
.

The organization of the paper is the following. In Section
2
, we gather the different ingredients needed in the proof of Suslin’s cancellation theorem. This
includes a (quick) review of the theory of fiber sequences in the motivic homotopy category including the Moore-Postnikov tower, some notions of cohomotopy and the representability statements we need. In the next section, we use the Moore-Postnikov tower to prove the conjecture, assuming Theorem
3
. We conclude this paper with Section
4
where it is respectively shown that the group
\mathrm{H}^{d}_{\mathrm{Zar}}(X,{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{d}({\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0))
is divisible and torsion, proving Theorem
3
.

Related works

There is ongoing work of R. Rao, R. Swan and the author to settle Suslin’s conjecture when
X
is
singular
. At the moment, we believe that we know how to prove it over a singular threefold over an algebraically closed field of characteristic not
2
, but we don’t know yet how to extend the result to higher dimensions. We point out that the methods are very different from the ones used in the present article. Details will hopefully appear elsewhere.

2
Preliminaries

Fix a base field
k
, which will always be assumed to be infinite perfect (or even algebraically closed). We will denote by
\mathcal{H}(k)
the motivic homotopy category of
pointed
spaces. The reader may consult
[
AF14b
, §2]
for some preliminaries, but we gather a few fundamental facts for convenience. This category is the homotopy category of the category of pointed spaces, i.e. pointed simplicial presheaves on the category
\mathrm{Sm}_{k}
of smooth (separated)
k
-schemes, endowed with a convenient model structure, expressed in
[
MV99
, §3]
. The terms fibrant, connected, etc. always refer to this model structure. Given two pointed spaces
F
and
G
in
\mathcal{H}(k)
, we usually denote by
[F,G]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}
the set of morphisms in
\mathcal{H}(k)
. There are two types of spheres in
\mathcal{H}(k)
, the simplicial sphere
S^{1}
and
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\smallsetminus 0
, pointed by
1
, which is usually denoted by
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
. Given a pointed object
F
, one may consider its homotopy sheaves
{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{i,j}(F)
, which are the Nisnevich sheaves associated to the presheaf

U\mapsto[S^{i}\wedge({\mathbb{G}}_{m})^{\wedge j}\wedge U_{+},F]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}.

A nice feature about these homotopy sheaves is that
{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{i,j}
can be computed purely in terms of
{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{i}:={\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{i,0}

[
Mor12
, Theorem 6.13]
. Indeed, there is a canonical identification

{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{i,j}(F)={\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{i}(F)_{-j}

where
(-)_{-j}
denotes the
j
-th contraction described in
[
Mor12
, Remark 2.23]
.

2.1
Fiber sequences

Let

(F,f)\xrightarrow{p}(E,e)\xrightarrow{q}(B,b)

(2.1.1)

be a fiber sequence of pointed spaces. We suppose that
B
is fibrant and that
q
is a fibration. For any pointed space
\mathscr{X}
, we obtain a sequence

[\mathscr{X},\Omega(E,e)]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}\to[\mathscr{X},\Omega(B,b)]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}\to[\mathscr{X},F]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}\xrightarrow{p_{*}}[\mathscr{X},E]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}\xrightarrow{q_{*}}[\mathscr{X},B]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}

which is exact in the usual sense. The relevant point in this article is exactness at
[\mathscr{X},F]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}
: If
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\prime}
are such that
p_{*}\alpha=p_{*}\alpha^{\prime}
, then there exists an element
g\in[\mathscr{X},\Omega(B,b)]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}
such that
g\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}
. Besides, the image of

[\mathscr{X},\Omega(E,e)]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}
under the group homomorphism

[\mathscr{X},\Omega(E,e)]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}\to[\mathscr{X},\Omega(B,b)]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}

is the stabilizer of the base point in
[\mathscr{X},F]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}
, or more precisely the stabilizer of the homotopy class of the composite
\mathscr{X}\to\ast\xrightarrow{f}F
. It follows in particular that there is a bijection

(p_{*})^{-1}(\ast)\simeq[\mathscr{X},\Omega(B,b)]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}/\mathrm{Im}([\mathscr{X},\Omega(E,e)]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}),

where the right-hand term is the set of orbits under the action of
\mathrm{Im}([\mathscr{X},\Omega(E,e)]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}})
.
A priori, the above exact sequence doesn’t give any information about the stabilizer of an arbitrary
\alpha\in[\mathscr{X},F]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}
but the situation slightly improves in an important case that we now explain.

Suppose that
(E,e)
is an abelian group object in
\mathcal{H}(k)
. In other words, there exists a map

m:(E,e)\times(E,e)\to(E,e)

satisfying the usual properties, and turning the set

into an abelian group functorially in
\mathscr{X}
. The fiber sequence (
2.1.1
) yields a fiber sequence of simplicial sets (

where
\mathrm{RMap}_{\ast}(-,-)
is the derived (pointed) mapping space. Since the spaces
F,E
and
B
are supposed to be fibrant and
\mathscr{X}
is cofibrant, this space is just the simplicial function object considered for instance in
[
MV99
, §2.1]
.
Let
\gamma\in\mathrm{RMap}_{0}(\mathscr{X},(F,f))
. We may consider
\mathrm{RMap}_{\ast}(\mathscr{X},(F,f))
as pointed by
\gamma
and
\mathrm{RMap}_{\ast}(\mathscr{X},(E,e))
as pointed by
p_{*}(\gamma)
, obtaining a fiber sequence

(\mathrm{RMap}_{\ast}(\mathscr{X},(F,f)),\gamma)\xrightarrow{p_{*}}(\mathrm{RMap}_{\ast}(\mathscr{X},(E,e)),p_{*}(\gamma))\xrightarrow{q_{*}}\mathrm{RMap}_{\ast}(\mathscr{X},(B,b))

and then an exact sequence

2.2
The Moore-Postnikov tower

In this section, we briefly recall the formalism of the Moore-Postnikov tower associated to a fibration (see for instance
[
GJ09
, Chapter VI]
, or
[
AF15
, §6.1]
). The output of this tower is that morphisms between spaces can be computed using cohomology groups of specific homotopy sheaves. To introduce the theorem, recall first that if
\mathbf{A}
is a strictly
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
-invariant sheaf
[
Mor12
, Definition 1.7]
, one can define for any integer
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
the so-called Eilenberg MacLane space
\mathrm{K}(\mathbf{A},n)
, which has the property that

[X_{+},\mathrm{K}(\mathbf{A},n)]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}=\mathrm{H}^{n}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}(X,\mathbf{A}).

If furthermore
\mathbf{G}
is a strictly
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
-invariant sheaf of (abelian) groups which acts linearly on
\mathbf{A}
(the assumption that
\mathbf{G}
is abelian is not necessary, but will be sufficient for our purpose), one may consider instead the
twisted
Eilenberg-MacLane space
\mathrm{K}^{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{A},n)
, which has the property to represent
\mathbf{G}
-equivariant cohomology. This space can concretely be constructed by setting
\mathrm{K}^{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{A},n):=E\mathbf{G}\times^{\mathbf{G}}\mathrm{K}(\mathbf{A},n)
, where
E\mathbf{G}
is a simplicially contractible space endowed with a free action of
\mathbf{G}
. Using this model, we see that the structural map
\mathrm{K}(\mathbf{A},n)\to\ast
yields a map
\pi:\mathrm{K}^{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{A},n)\to\mathrm{B}\mathbf{G}
to the classifying space, while the inclusion of the base point gives a morphism
\mathrm{B}\mathbf{G}\to\mathrm{K}^{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{A},n)
.

Taking an appropriate fibrant model for
\mathrm{B}\mathbf{G}
, a (solid) map
\xi\colon X_{+}\to\mathrm{B}\mathbf{G}
now corresponds to a
\mathbf{G}
-torsor
P
on
X
, allowing to define a twisted version of the strictly
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
-invariant sheaf
\mathbf{A}
as follows: We consider the sheaf
\mathbf{A}(P)
associated to the presheaf

\{u:U\to X\}\mapsto\mathbf{A}(U)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[\mathbf{G}(U)]}{\mathbb{Z}}[(u^{*}P)(U)]

where
{\mathbb{Z}}[\mathbf{G}(U)]
is the group algebra of
\mathbf{G}(U)
, and
{\mathbb{Z}}[(u^{*}P)(U)]
is the free abelian group on the
U
-points of
(u^{*}P)
(i.e. the sections of
u^{*}P\to U
). Note that
\mathbf{A}(P)
is étale locally isomorphic to
{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{n}(F)
(or even Zariski locally if the torsor
P
is Zariski locally trivial), but in general not globally. This twisted sheaf allows to concretely understand the set
[X_{+},\mathrm{K}^{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{A},n)]_{\xi,{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}
of homotopy classes of maps
s\colon X_{+}\to\mathrm{K}^{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{A},n)
which have the property that the composite

X_{+}\xrightarrow{f}\mathrm{K}^{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{A},n)\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathrm{B}\mathbf{G}

is homotopic to
\xi
. Forgetting the base point of
X_{+}
, and looking at the Cartesian square (in which we have taken an appropropriate model of
\mathrm{K}^{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{A},n)
to turn
\pi
into a fibration)

\textstyle{X\times_{\mathrm{B}\mathbf{G}}\mathrm{K}^{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{A},n)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{K}^{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{A},n)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\xi}
\textstyle{\mathrm{B}\mathbf{G},}

we obtain that this is equivalent to understand the set of homotopy classes of sections of the projection

. Using now that
\mathrm{K}^{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{A},n)=E\mathbf{G}\times^{\mathbf{G}}\mathrm{K}(\mathbf{A},n)

is a weak-equivalence.

We note that the Moore-Postnikov factorization is functorial in the following sense. If we are given a commutative diagram

\textstyle{F\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{E\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q}

of spaces with induced morphism

on homotopy fibers, then we obtain a family of maps

which make the relevant diagrams commutative. In our situation, all the spaces

will have the same

In this article, we will have to work intensively with diagrams of the form (

One useful way to think about the Moore-Postnikov factorization is as a tower of objects over

, and see all spaces as simplicial sheaves on this space. With this in mind, we can now prove the following lemma, in which the (derived) mapping spaces are thought of as (derived) mapping spaces of objects over

Let

It is usually quite difficult to identify the action of
\mathbf{G}
on the homotopy sheaves
{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{i}(F)
. Let us however give an example where this action is completely determined following
[
AF15
, §6.2]
. For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we may consider the homomorphism

\mathrm{GL}_{n}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}

mapping a matrix
M
to the matrix
\mathrm{diag}(M,1)
. This induces a morphism
\mathrm{BGL}_{n}\to\mathrm{BGL}_{n+1}
and in turn a fiber sequence (
[
Mor12
, Theorem 8.12]
)

{\mathbb{A}}^{n+1}\smallsetminus 0\to\mathrm{BGL}_{n}\to\mathrm{BGL}_{n+1}

The determinant map
\mathrm{BGL}_{n+1}\to\mathrm{B}{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
induces an isomorphism
{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{1}(\mathrm{BGL}_{n+1})\to{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{1}(\mathrm{B}{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\simeq{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
, and thus an action

{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\times({\mathbb{A}}^{n+1}\smallsetminus 0)\to{\mathbb{A}}^{n+1}\smallsetminus 0

which is given by
(\lambda,(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}))\mapsto(\lambda^{-1}x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})
. This action then yields an action of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
on the homotopy sheaves
{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{i}({\mathbb{A}}^{n+1}\smallsetminus 0)
for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

If
\mathbf{A}
is a strictly
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
-invariant sheaf, and
L
is a line bundle over a smooth scheme
X
, then the twisted sheaf
\mathbf{A}(L)
has in general different cohomology groups than the untwisted sheaf. The next result however says that this is not the case in a specific situation that will be important for us.

Lemma 2.2.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth affine scheme of dimension
d
over an algebraically closed field
k
. Let
\mathbf{A}
be a strictly
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
-invariant sheaf, and let
L
be a line bundle over
X
. Then, we have a canonical isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{d}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}(X,\mathbf{A})\simeq\mathrm{H}^{d}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}(X,\mathbf{A}(L)).

The same statement holds in the Zariski topology.

Proof.

If
d=0
, there is nothing to prove and we then suppose that
d\geq 1
. First, recall that the cohomology groups (either in the Nisnevich or Zariski topology) of a strictly
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
-invariant sheaf
\mathbf{A}
can be computed using the so-called Rost-Schmid complex (
[
Mor12
, §5]
), whose term in degree
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
is of the form

\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(j)}}\mathbf{A}_{-j}(k(x),\wedge^{j}(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2})^{*}).

The same holds for the sheaf
\mathbf{A}(L)
, at the difference that this time the term in degree
j
is of the form

\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(j)}}\mathbf{A}_{-j}(k(x),\wedge^{j}(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2})^{*}\otimes_{k(x)}L(x)).

If
x\in X^{(d)}
, then
k(x)=k
is algebraically closed and if
l_{1},l_{2}
are two generators of
L(x)
, there exists
a\in k^{\times}
such that
a^{2}l_{1}=l_{2}
. On the other hand, the choice of a generator induces an isomorphism

\mathbf{A}_{-d}(k(x),\wedge^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2})^{*})\to\mathbf{A}_{-d}(k(x),\wedge^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2})^{*}\otimes L(x)),

and two generators differing by a square yield the same isomorphism by
[
Mor12
, Lemma 3.49]
. It follows that we obtain a canonical isomorphism

\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(d)}}\mathbf{A}_{-j}(k(x),\wedge^{d}(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2})^{*})\xrightarrow{can}\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(d)}}\mathbf{A}_{-d}(k(x),\wedge^{j}(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2})^{*}\otimes_{k(x)}L(x)).

Suppose first that
X
is a curve, i.e.
d=1
. In that case,
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
is
2
-divisible (since
X
is affine, see
[
Ful98
, Example 1.6.6]
) and it follows that
L
is a square: There exists a line bundle
N
and an isomorphism
N^{\otimes 2}\simeq L
. In that case, the Rost-Schmid complexes of the sheaves
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{A}(L)
are canonically isomorphic by
[
Mor12
, Remark 5.13]
(observe that we don’t need the strictly
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
-invariant sheaf involved to be of the form
\mathbf{A}_{-1}
because we don’t need the transfer maps, as closed points have residue field equal to
k
) and we are done. For
d\geq 2
, we will show that the differential

\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(d-1)}}\mathbf{A}_{-d+1}(k(x),\wedge^{d-1}(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2})^{*})\xrightarrow{\partial_{X}}\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(d)}}\mathbf{A}_{-d}(k(x),\wedge^{j}(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2})^{*})

coincides with its twisted analogue. For that, it suffices to prove that this is the case for the differential

\mathbf{A}_{-d+1}(k(x),\wedge^{d-1}(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2})^{*})\xrightarrow{\partial_{X}}\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(d)}}\mathbf{A}_{-d}(k(x),\wedge^{j}(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2})^{*})

associated to any
x\in X^{(d-1)}
. Let
C
be the normalization of
\overline{\{x\}}
in the field
k(x)
. This is a smooth affine curve and the morphism
f\colon C\to X
is finite. It follows from
[
Mor12
, Corollary 5.30]
that we have a commutative diagram

\textstyle{\mathbf{A}_{-d+1}(k(x),\omega(x))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\partial_{C}}
\textstyle{\bigoplus_{z\in C^{(1)}}\mathbf{A}_{-d}(k(z),(\mathfrak{m}_{z}/\mathfrak{m}_{z}^{2})^{*}\otimes_{k(z)}\omega(z))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sum}
\textstyle{\mathbf{A}_{-d+1}(k(x),\wedge^{d-1}(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2})^{*})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\partial_{X}}
\textstyle{\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(d)}}\mathbf{A}_{-d}(k(x),\wedge^{j}(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2})^{*})}

where
\omega:=\omega_{C/k}\otimes f^{*}\omega_{X/k}^{\vee}
, and
\sum
is just the homomorphism obtained out of the equalities
k(x)=k(z)=k
. The same applies with the twisted sheaf
\mathbf{A}(L)
, and we obtain a commutative diagram

\textstyle{\mathbf{A}_{-d+1}(k(x),\wedge^{d-1}(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2})^{*}\otimes L(x))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\partial^{L}_{X}}
\textstyle{\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(d)}}\mathbf{A}_{-d}(k(x),\wedge^{j}(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2})^{*}\otimes L(x))}
\textstyle{\mathbf{A}_{-d+1}(k(x),\omega(x)\otimes L(x))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\partial^{L}_{C}}
\textstyle{\bigoplus_{z\in C^{(1)}}\mathbf{A}_{-d}(k(z),(\mathfrak{m}_{z}/\mathfrak{m}_{z}^{2})^{*}\otimes_{k(z)}\omega(z)\otimes L(z))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sum}
\textstyle{\mathbf{A}_{-d+1}(k(x),\omega(x))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\partial_{C}}
\textstyle{\bigoplus_{z\in C^{(1)}}\mathbf{A}_{-d}(k(z),(\mathfrak{m}_{z}/\mathfrak{m}_{z}^{2})^{*}\otimes_{k(z)}\omega(z))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sum}
\textstyle{\mathbf{A}_{-d+1}(k(x),\wedge^{d-1}(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2})^{*})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\partial_{X}}
\textstyle{\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(d)}}\mathbf{A}_{-d}(k(x),\wedge^{j}(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2})^{*})}

where the dotted arrows (which are isomorphisms) are obtained using an orientation
N^{\otimes 2}\simeq L
as above for some line bundle
N
on
C
. To conclude, it suffices to observe that the diagram

\textstyle{\bigoplus_{z\in C^{(1)}}\mathbf{A}_{-d}(k(z),(\mathfrak{m}_{z}/\mathfrak{m}_{z}^{2})^{*}\otimes_{k(z)}\omega(z))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sum}
\textstyle{\bigoplus_{z\in C^{(1)}}\mathbf{A}_{-d}(k(z),(\mathfrak{m}_{z}/\mathfrak{m}_{z}^{2})^{*}\otimes_{k(z)}\omega(z)\otimes L(z))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sum}
\textstyle{\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(d)}}\mathbf{A}_{-d}(k(x),\wedge^{j}(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2})^{*})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{can}
\textstyle{\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(d)}}\mathbf{A}_{-d}(k(x),\wedge^{j}(\mathfrak{m}_{x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{2})^{*}\otimes L(x))}

commutes. Indeed, both are obtained using generators of
L
at closed points, and the choice of such generators is irrelevant by the previous discussion.
∎

2.3
Motivic cohomotopy groups

In this paper, we will also make use of the motivic analogue of the notion of cohomotopy groups in algebraic topology. We will mainly build on the following proposition (
[
AF21
, Proposition 1.2.1]
).

Proposition 2.3.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
be an integer and let
(\mathscr{X},x)
be a pointed space which is
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}-(n-1)
-connected. Suppose that
U
is a smooth scheme which is of
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
-cohomological dimension
\leq 2n-2
. Then, for any integer
i\geq 1
, the map

[U_{+},(\mathscr{X},x)]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}\to[U_{+},\Omega_{S^{1}}^{i}\Sigma_{S^{1}}^{i}(\mathscr{X},x)]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}

is a bijection. Consequently,
[U_{+},(\mathscr{X},x)]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}
is endowed with the structure of an abelian group, functorial in both inputs.

Remark 2.3.2
.

In fact, the above result is better understood in the context of
S^{1}
-stabilization. In short, one may consider the homotopy category of
S^{1}
-spectra
\mathrm{SH}^{S^{1}}(k)
, which is a triangulated category (e.g.
[
AF21
, discussion before Proposition 1.2.2]
). There is an adjunction

\Sigma_{S^{1}}^{\infty}:\mathcal{H}(k)\leftrightarrows\mathrm{SH}^{S^{1}}(k):\Omega_{S^{1}}^{\infty}

and the above proposition states that the map

[U_{+},(\mathscr{X},x)]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}\to[\Sigma_{S^{1}}^{\infty}(U_{+}),\Sigma_{S^{1}}^{\infty}(\mathscr{X},x)]_{\mathrm{SH}^{S^{1}}(k)}

is a bijection under the stated hypotheses.

It is in general not easy to prove that a space is suitably highly connected, but we now give an example which will be of central importance in this article. Let
d\geq 3
be an integer, and consider the punctured affine space
\mathbb{A}^{d}\smallsetminus 0
, pointed by
(0,\ldots,0,1)
. It is well known (see e.g.
[
MV99
, Example 2.20]
) that there is an isomorphism in
\mathcal{H}(k)
of the form

\mathbb{A}^{d}\smallsetminus 0\simeq S^{d-1}\wedge({\mathbb{G}}_{m})^{\wedge d}.

We obtain as a consequence of
[
Mor12
, Theorem 6.38]
that
\mathbb{A}^{d}\smallsetminus 0
is
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}-(d-2)
-connected, i.e. that
{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{i}(\mathbb{A}^{d}\smallsetminus 0)=0
for any
i\leq d-2
. Moreover, Morel was able to compute the first nontrivial homotopy sheaf of this space, proving that there is a canonical isomorphism of sheaves of abelian groups

{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{d-1}(\mathbb{A}^{d}\smallsetminus 0)\simeq{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{MW}}_{d}

where the right-hand term is the so-called Milnor-Witt
K
-theory sheaf of weight
d
(explicitly described in
[
Mor12
, §3.2]
). As stated in the preliminaries, this allows to obtain an isomorphism of sheaves of abelian groups

{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{d-1,i}(\mathbb{A}^{d}\smallsetminus 0)\simeq{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{MW}}_{d-i}

(2.3.1)

for any
i\geq 0
, and in particular
{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{d-1,d}(\mathbb{A}^{d}\smallsetminus 0)\simeq{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{MW}}_{0}
. Evaluating at
k
, we get a string of isomorphisms

{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{MW}}_{0}(k)\simeq{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{d-1,d}(\mathbb{A}^{d}\smallsetminus 0)(k)\simeq[\mathbb{A}^{d}\smallsetminus 0,\mathbb{A}^{d}\smallsetminus 0]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}

showing in particular that the right-hand term is the free
{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{MW}}_{0}(k)
-module generated by the identity map.

As discussed in the previous section, there is an action of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
on
{\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0
, and consequently an action on its homotopy sheaves. For
{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{d-1}({\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0)\simeq{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{MW}}_{d}
, this action actually coincides with the “obvious” action of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
induced by the map
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{MW}}_{0}
and the multiplication map on Milnor-Witt
K
-theory (
[
AF15
, §6.2]
).

Now, we note that
\mathbb{A}^{d}\smallsetminus 0
is of
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
-cohomological dimension
d-1
by
[
AF21
, Proposition 1.1.5]
and, since it is
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}-(d-2)
-connected as well by the above discussion, the set
[{\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0,{\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}
is endowed with a group structure by Proposition
2.3.1
. This group structure coincides with the one described above by
[
Ler24
, Lemma 3.4]
.

If
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we can consider the map of pointed spaces

\mu_{n}:{\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0\to{\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0

defined by
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\mapsto(x_{1}^{n},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})
. Since the cohomotopy group structure is functorial in the second input (for suitably connected spaces), the map
\mu_{n}
induces a homomorphism of abelian groups

(\mu_{n})_{*}:[\mathscr{Y},{\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}\to[\mathscr{Y},{\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0]_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}

for any space
\mathscr{Y}
satisfying the assumptions of Proposition
2.3.1
. If
\sqrt{-1}\in k
, it follows from
[
Du22
, Remark 6.11]
and the above discussion that this map is in fact the multiplication by
n
map.

Lemma 2.3.3
.

Let
d\geq 3
be an integer, and let
n,m
be integers such that
2\leq m\leq 2d-4
. For any integer
i\geq 0
and any smooth scheme
X
over a field
k
with
\sqrt{-1}\in k
, the map

\mathrm{H}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{i}(X,{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{m}({\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0))\to\mathrm{H}_{{\operatorname{Nis}}}^{i}(X,{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{m}({\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0))

induced by
\mu_{n}:{\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0\to{\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0
is the multiplication by
n

Proof.

According to the Freudenthal suspension theorem
[
Mor12
, Theorem 6.61]
, the map

{\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0\to\Omega_{S^{1}}\Sigma_{S^{1}}({\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0)

is
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}-(2d-4)
-connected. Consequently, the sheaf
{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{m}({\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0)
is already
S^{1}
-stable for
2\leq m\leq 2d-4
. In other words, it can be computed as the sheaf associated to the presheaf

U\mapsto[\Sigma^{m}_{S^{1}}\Sigma_{S^{1}}^{\infty}(U_{+}),\Sigma_{S^{1}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0)]_{\mathrm{SH}^{S^{1}}(k)}.

As noted above, one may use
[
Du22
, Remark 6.11]
and the fact that
\sqrt{-1}\in k
to see that the map

\Sigma_{S^{1}}^{\infty}(\mu_{n})_{*}:\Sigma_{S^{1}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0)\to\Sigma_{S^{1}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0)

induces the multiplication by
n

[\Sigma^{m}_{S^{1}}\Sigma_{S^{1}}^{\infty}(U_{+}),\Sigma_{S^{1}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0)]_{\mathrm{SH}^{S^{1}}(k)}\to[\Sigma^{m}_{S^{1}}\Sigma_{S^{1}}^{\infty}(U_{+}),\Sigma_{S^{1}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0)]_{\mathrm{SH}^{S^{1}}(k)}

for any smooth
k
-scheme
U
. Consequently, the homomorphism on
{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{m}({\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0)
induced by
\mu_{n}
coincides with the multiplication by
n
homomorphism.
∎

Remark 2.3.4
.

If
\sqrt{-1}\not\in k
, then the map in cohomology induced by
\mu_{n}
should be the multiplication by
n_{\epsilon}
. The same arguments as above most certainly work, showing in particular that
{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{m}({\mathbb{A}}^{d}\smallsetminus 0)
is endowed with a

-module structure, and that the map induced by
\mu_{n}
is the multiplication by
n_{\epsilon}
for this module structure. We will come back to this question in future work.

Remark 2.3.5
.

If
L
is a line bundle over
X

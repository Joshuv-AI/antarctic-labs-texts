A faster algorithm of up persistent Laplacian over non-branching simplicial complexes
Rui Dong

Abstract
In this paper we present an algorithm for computing the matrix representation Δ q , up 𝒦 , ℒ \Delta_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}} of the up persistent Laplacian △ q , up 𝒦 , ℒ \triangle_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}} over a pair of non-branching and orientation-compatible simplicial complexes 𝒦 ↪ ℒ \mathcal{K}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L} , which has quadratic time complexity. Moreover, we show that the matrix representation Δ q , up 𝒦 , ℒ \Delta_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}} can be identified as the Laplacian of a weighted oriented hypergraph, which can be regarded as a higher dimensional generalization of the Kron reduction. Finally, we introduce a Cheeger-type inequality with respect to the minimal eigenvalue λ 𝐦𝐢𝐧 𝒦 , ℒ \lambda_{\mathbf{min}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}} of Δ q , up 𝒦 , ℒ \Delta_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}} .



Leiden University, Leiden, The Netherlands

E-mail:
r.dong@hum.leidenuniv.nl

: topological data analysis, persistent Laplacian, hypergraphs, Cheeger inequality

1
Introduction

Topological Data Analysis (TDA) is a mathematical field that studies topological structures of a dataset via algebraic topology tools.
One main advantage of TDA is that it can extract topological features of a dataset,
e.g.,
connected components,
loops,
voids,
which are difficult to be captured by traditional statistical methods.
A typical approach of TDA is to construct a multiscale family of simplicial complexes (collection of vertices, edges, triangles, etc.) from a dataset,
and then detect the behavior the persistent homology groups over scales.
There are more than one way to represent the persistent homology,
some common methods contain persistence barcodes
[
CZCG04
]
,
persistence diagrams
[
EH10
]
,
persistence landscapes
[
Bub15
]
.
An applicable representation of persistent homology should be stable,
in the sense that a slight perturbation of original dataset should result in only a slight modification in the representation.
For instance,
the persistence diagrams are stable with respect to the bottleneck distance
[
CSEH07
,
EH10
]
.

Although persistent homology has been applied in a lot of different disciplines,
it has its own limitations.
The drawback of persistent homology is that it characterizes only topological features of a dataset,
and it is difficult to extract more tiny geometrical information of a dataset via persistent homology.
Compared with that,
persistent Laplacians can capture not only the topological information of persistent homology via the kernel space but also geometrical information via the spectra.

For a pair of simplicial complexes
\mathcal{K}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}
,
The
q
-th persistent Laplacian
\triangle_{q}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
is defined to be the sum of up persistent Laplacian
\triangle_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
and down persistent Laplacian
\triangle_{q,\mathrm{down}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
.
The persistent Laplacian is first introduced in
[
WNW20
]
,
and studied more thoroughly in
[
MWW22
]
,
and later on generalized to the case of simplicial maps in
[
GMWW23
]
.
The algebraic stability property of persistent Laplacian is also investigated recently in
[
LLW23
]
.
We also refer to
[
WW23
]
for a survey of persistent Laplacian.

With respect to the up persistent Laplacian,
two different algorithms of
\Delta_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
have already been given in
[
MWW22
]
,
both have cubic time complexity,
more specifically,
if the number of
q
-simplices and
(q+1)
-simplices of
\mathcal{L}
are
n_{q}^{\mathcal{L}}
and
n_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}
respectively,
the time complexity of up persistent Laplacian
\Delta_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
can be either
O\left(n_{q}^{\mathcal{L}}(n_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}})^{2}+(n_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}})^{3}\right)
or
O\left((n_{q}^{\mathcal{L}})^{3}\right)
,
due to computing either inverse or Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse of a matrix respectively.
In this paper,
we show that if all the
q
-simplices in
\mathcal{L}
are non-branching and all the
(q+1)
-simplices in
\mathcal{L}
are orientation-compatible,
it is not necessary to compute (pseudo)inverse,
and the computation of
\Delta_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
can have quadratic time complexity.
The other contribution of this paper is that we show
\Delta_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
is equivalent to the Laplacian of a weighted hypergraph,
which can be regarded as a higher dimensional generalization of the Kron reduction.

Preliminaries

In this section we discuss the necessary preliminaries to state the main algorithms and results of this paper.
In Section
2.1
we review the basic definition of simplicial complexes and combinatorial Laplacians.
In Section
2.2
we review the definition of persistent Laplacians over a pair of simplicial complexes
\mathcal{K}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}
as introduced in
[
MWW22
]
.

2.1
Simplicial complexes

An (abstract) simplicial complex can be regarded as a higher-dimensional generalization of a graph,
which can describe higher-dimensional structures of a dataset.
More precisely,
let
V
be an ordered finite set,
a collection
\mathcal{K}\in 2^{V}
with
\emptyset\in\mathcal{K}
is called a simplicial complex if
\sigma_{1}\in\mathcal{K}
and
\sigma_{2}\subset\sigma_{1}
,
then also
\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{K}
.
Each element
\sigma\in\mathcal{K}
is referred to as a simplex,
if
|\sigma|=q+1
,
we say
\sigma
is a
q
-simplex,
and we say
\sigma_{2}
is a
face
of
\sigma_{1}
if
\sigma_{2}\subset\sigma_{1}
.
Let
\sigma=\{v_{0},v_{1},\cdots,v_{q}\}\in\mathcal{K}
be a
q
-simplex,
then the ordered set
[v_{0},v_{1},\cdots,v_{q}]
is called an oriented
q
-simplex,
we can get the opposite orientation if we change the order of all vertices in
\sigma
by an odd permutation.
Let
S_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}
denote the set of all
q
-simplices in
\mathcal{K}
,
and an oriented simplex is denoted as
[\sigma]
.
We pick an order
[\sigma]
for each
\sigma
,
and let
\bar{S}_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}:=\{[\sigma]:\sigma\in S_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}\}
.
We define the
q
-th chain group of
\mathcal{K}
as the vector space over
\mathbb{R}
generated by
\bar{S}_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}
,
and denote it as
C_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}
.
Let
n_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}
be the cardinality of
\bar{S}_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}
.
The boundary operator
\partial_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}:C_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}\to C_{q-1}^{\mathcal{K}}
is defined as

\partial_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}:[v_{0},\cdots,v_{q}]\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{q}(-1)^{i}[v_{0},\cdots,\hat{v}_{i},\cdots,v_{q}]

for each
[\sigma]=[v_{0},\cdots,v_{q}]\in\bar{S}_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}
,
here
\hat{v}_{i}
denotes the omission of
v_{i}
.
The
q
-th homology group of
\mathcal{K}
is defined as
H_{q}(\mathcal{K})\coloneqq\frac{\ker(\partial_{q}^{\mathcal{K}})}{\mathrm{im}(\partial_{q+1}^{\mathcal{K}})}
.
We set a positive weight to each simplex via a positive function
w^{\mathcal{K}}:\mathcal{K}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
and we say
\mathcal{K}
is unweighted if
w^{\mathcal{K}}\equiv 1
.
With the weight function
w^{\mathcal{K}}
we can define an inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{w_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}}
over
C_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}
as:

\langle[\sigma_{1}],[\sigma_{2}]\rangle_{w_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}}\coloneqq\delta_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}\cdot\left(w_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}(\sigma_{1})\right)^{-1},

where
\delta_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}
is equal to
1
if and only if
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}
,
otherwise it is equal to
0
.
We denote by
\left(\partial_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}\right)^{\ast}:C_{q-1}^{\mathcal{K}}\to C_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}
the adjoint of
\partial_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}
with respect to this inner product.
The
q
-th up Laplacian is defined as
\triangle^{\mathcal{K}}_{q,\mathrm{up}}\coloneqq\partial_{q+1}^{\mathcal{K}}\circ\left(\partial_{q+1}^{\mathcal{K}}\right)^{\ast}
,
the
q
-th down Laplacian is defined as

\triangle^{\mathcal{K}}_{q,\mathrm{down}}\coloneqq\left(\partial_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}\right)^{\ast}\circ\partial_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}
,
and the
q
-th combinatorial Laplacian
\triangle_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}:C_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}\to C_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}
is defined as

\triangle_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}\coloneqq\triangle^{\mathcal{K}}_{q,\mathrm{up}}+\triangle_{q,\mathrm{down}}^{\mathcal{K}}.

There is a discrete version of the Hodge theorem,
we state it in the following theorem:

Theorem 2.1
.

[
Eck45
]

For a simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
,

\ker(\triangle_{q}^{\mathcal{K}})\cong H_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}
.

\triangle_{q}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
introduced in
[
MWW22

.
Let
\mathcal{K}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}

Let
C_{q}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}
be the subspace of
C_{q}^{\mathcal{L}}
defined as:

C_{q}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}:=\{c\in C_{q}^{\mathcal{L}}:\partial_{q}^{\mathcal{L}}(c)\in C_{q-1}^{\mathcal{K}}\}\subset C_{q}^{\mathcal{L}},

and let
\partial_{q}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}:=\partial_{q}^{\mathcal{L}}\Big|_{C_{q}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}}

C_{q}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}
.

Definition 2.1
.

[
MWW22

The
q

\triangle_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}\coloneqq\partial_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}\circ\left(\partial_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}\right)^{\ast},

q

\triangle_{q}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}:C_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}\to C_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}

\triangle_{q}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}\coloneqq\partial_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}\circ\left(\partial_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}\right)^{\ast}+\left(\partial_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}\right)^{\ast}\circ\partial_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}.

Recall that the inclusion
\mathcal{K}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}

The
q
-th persistent Betti number
\beta_{q}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
is defined as the rank of

.
The persistent Laplacians can recover persistent Betti numbers,
we restate the result in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
MWW22
, Theorem 2.7]

For each integer
q\geq 0
,
the
q
-th persistent Betti number is equal to the dimension of the kernel of
\Delta_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
,

\beta_{q}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}=\mathrm{nullity}(\triangle_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}})

Remark 2.1
.

We observe that
\beta_{q}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
is independent of the weights over simplices in
\mathcal{L}
,
thus the classical persistent homology can not detect the geometric information with respect to weights,
however,
the weights affect the non-zero eigenvalues of
\triangle_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
.

In this paper we focus only on the up persistent Laplacian
\triangle_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
.
We recall the matrix representation of
\triangle_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
in the following theorem:

Theorem 2.3
.

[
MWW22
, Theorem 3.1]

Assume that
n_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}\coloneqq\dim(C_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}})>0
.
Choose any basis of
C_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}\subset C_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}

\partial_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}

\Delta_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}=B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}\left(Z^{T}(W_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}})^{-1}Z\right)^{-1}\left(B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}\right)^{T}(W_{q}^{\mathcal{K}})^{-1}.

\mathcal{K}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}
be a pair of simplicial complexes.
We first sort all the
q
-simplices
\bar{S}_{q}^{\mathcal{L}}

and label them by

\mathcal{L}

Lemma 2.1
.

\mathcal{K}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}

Let

3
A faster algorithm of up persistent Laplacian

In this section we first give the exact definition of a simplicial complex being non-branching and orientation-compatible.
And then we present an algorithm for computing
\Delta_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
with quadratic time complexity.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a simplicial complex.
We say a
q
-simplex in
\mathcal{L}
is
non-branching
if it is a face of at most two
(q+1)
-simplices,
and we say
S_{q}^{\mathcal{L}}
is
non-branching
in
\mathcal{L}
if the collection of all
q
-simplices
S_{q}^{\mathcal{L}}\subset\mathcal{L}
are
non-branching
.

We observe that if
\mathcal{L}
is a
(q+1)
-dimensional pseudomanifold
[
ST80
]
,
or an
\alpha
-complex
[
EH10
]
,
the collection
S_{q}^{\mathcal{L}}
is automatically non-branching.
More than that,
in the case of cubical complex
[
WCVc12
]
,
since each
q
-cube is the face of at most two
(q+1)
-cubes,
a cubical complex is automatically non-branching as well.

Definition 3.2
.

Suppose
S_{q}^{\mathcal{L}}\subset\mathcal{L}
is non-branching.
We say
S_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}\subset\mathcal{L}
is
orientation-compatible
in
\mathcal{L}
if all the
(q+1)
-simplices in
\mathcal{L}
can be oriented in such a way that if a
q
-simplex
\sigma
is a face of exact two
(q+1)
-simplices,
then it is positive-oriented in one
(q+1)
-simplex and negative-oriented in the other
(q+1)
-simplex.

Definition 3.3
.

Suppose
S_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}\subset\mathcal{L}
is orientation-compatible.
We orient all
(q+1)
-simplices in the way as described in Definition
3.2
and denote the set of oriented
(q+1)
-simplices as
\bar{S}_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}
.
We say a matrix representation
B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}
associated with
\bar{S}_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}
is
oriented
.

Remark 3.1
.

Follow the Definition
3.3
,
if the matrix
B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}
is oriented,
then each row in
B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}
contains at most one
1
and at most one
-1
.

.

Consider the simplicial complex
\mathcal{L}

3.1
Up persistent Laplacian with respect to the regular-form

In this section we will describe how to compute the up persistent Laplacian
\Delta_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
when
D_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}
is of the regular-form.
We first analyse the structure of regular-form matrix in more detail.
Let
D
be a
k\times l
matrix in the regular-form,
without loss of generality,
we assume
D
has no zero columns.
we can regard the transpose matrix
D^{T}
as an incidence matrix associated with a directed graph
G
.
We denote by
G_{1},\cdots,G_{r}
the weakly connected components of
G
,
and
C_{1},\cdots,C_{r}
the corresponding column indices in
D
,
i.e.,

(D(:,C_{i}))^{T}
is the incidence matrix associated with
G_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq r
.
For two column-index sets
C_{i}
,
C_{j}
,
we also denote by
C_{i}\#C_{j}
the concatenation of
C_{i}
and
C_{j}
,
i.e.,

C_{i}\#C_{j}:=(c_{i}^{1},\cdots,c_{i}^{l_{i}},c_{j}^{1},\cdots,c_{j}^{l_{j}})
.
A fundamental result in algebraic graph theory says that
the number of weakly connected components of a graph
G
is equal to the difference between the column number and rank of
D
.
We summarize this result in the following lemma,
the proof can be found in many algebraic graph theory textbooks like
[
GR01
, Theorem 8.3.1]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
D
be a

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
D
be a
k\times l
matrix of the regular-form,
and
\widehat{D}=D(:,C_{1}\#\cdots\#C_{r})
be the column rearrangement of
D
obtained by the algorithm ColumnRearrange_Regular.
We denote by
T_{l}
an
l\times l
upper triangular matrix with all entries above diagonal equal to
1
,
that is,

T_{l}=\begin{bmatrix}1&1&\cdots&1\\
0&\ddots&\cdots&1\\
0&0&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&1\end{bmatrix}.

Then all the non-zero columns of
\widehat{D}T_{l}
are linearly independent.

Proof.

We denote by
G
the directed graph derived from
D
,
and
G_{1},\cdots,G_{r}
the weakly connected components of
G
.
Let
l_{j}
be the cardinality of the column-index set
C_{j}
with
1\leq j\leq r
.
Consider the submatrix
D(:,C_{j})
.
Since each row contains exact one
1
and one
-1
,
the sum of each row is equal to
0
,
that is,
the last column of the product
D(:,C_{j})\,T_{l_{j}}
is a zero column.
Therefore,
the product
D(:,C_{1}\#C_{2}\#\cdots\#C_{r})\,T_{l}
contains
r
zero columns.
According to Lemma
3.1
,
the rank of
D
is equal to
(l-r)
,
hence,
all the non-zero columns of
\widehat{D}T_{l}
are linearly independent.
∎

Complexity analysis

Finding all the edges and vertices via an incidence matrix
D_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}
will take time
O(n_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}\times(n_{q}^{\mathcal{L}}-n_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}))
,
finding all connected components via the vertices and edges of a graph using DFS algorithm will take time
O(n_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}+(n_{q}^{\mathcal{L}}-n_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}))
(
[
Erc18
, Section 8.3.1]
).
To compute the
j
-column
B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}(:,j)
the time complexity is
O(n_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}\times l_{j})
,
hence to compute the matrix
B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}
the time complexity is
O(n_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}\times n_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}})
.
The time complexity of computing each diagonal entry
v_{j}^{-1}
in
V^{-1}
is
O(l_{j})
,
hence the time complexity of computing
V^{-1}
is
O(n_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}})
.
The size of
B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}
is
n_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}\times r
,
with
r\leq n_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}
,
thus the time complexity of computing the product
B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}V^{-1}
is
O(n_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}\times n_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}})
,
since each column in
\left(B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}\right)^{T}
contains at most two non-zero entries,
the time complexity of computing the product
\left(B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}\right)^{T}(W_{q}^{\mathcal{K}})^{-1}
is
O(n_{q}^{\mathcal{K}})
.
Finally,
since both
B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}V^{-1}
and
\left(B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}\right)^{T}(W_{q}^{\mathcal{K}})^{-1}
are sparse matrices,
and each column in
\left(B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}\right)^{T}(W_{q}^{\mathcal{K}})^{-1}
contains at most two non-zero entries,
hence the time complexity of computing the product of
B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}V^{-1}
and
\left(B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}\right)^{T}(W_{q}^{\mathcal{K}})^{-1}
can be
O((n_{q}^{\mathcal{K}})^{2})
.
Therefore,
the time complexity of Algorithm
2
is
O\left(n_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}\times(n_{q}^{\mathcal{L}}-n_{q}^{\mathcal{K}})+n_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}\times n_{q}^{\mathcal{K}}+(n_{q}^{\mathcal{K}})^{2}\right)
.

In this section,

Higher dimensional generalization of the Kron reduction

As is shown in
[
MWW22
, Section 4.3]
,
when
\mathcal{K}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}
are graphs,
the up persistent Laplacian
\Delta_{0,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
can be identified as the Laplacian over a weighted graph
\widetilde{K}
,
which is referred to as the
Kron reduction

[
DB13
]
of
\mathcal{L}
.
Although this is not true in higher dimensional case in general
[
MWW22
, Remark 4.12]
,
we can identify the up persistent Laplacian

\Delta_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
as the Laplacian over a weighted oriented hypergraph under the assumption that
S_{q}^{\mathcal{L}}\subset\mathcal{L}
is non-branching and
S_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}\subset\mathcal{L}
is orientation-compatible.

We take the same assumptions and notations as stated in Theorem
3.2
.
Recall that
C_{j}
is the column-index set of
B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}
which is associated with the vertices of a connected component in the corresponding hypergraph,
and
l_{j}
is the cardinality of
C_{j}
.
Notice that
B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}(:,C_{j})
can be identified as
l_{j}
different
(q+1)
-simplices,
and since
B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}
is an oriented representation,
thus according to Theorem
3.2
,
the column
B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}(:,j)
is equal to the column sum of
B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}(:,C_{j})
.
Therefore,

B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}(:,j)
can be identified as the boundary of a
(q+1)
-dimensional polyhedron combined by the
l_{j}

(q+1)
-simplices.

In this section we let
\mathcal{L}
be unweighted.
Then according to Theorem
3.2
,
we get the up persistent Laplacian

\Delta_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}=B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}V^{-1}\left(B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}\right)^{T},

where
V=\mathrm{diag}(l_{1},\cdots,l_{r})
with
l_{j}
being the cardinality of
C_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq r
.
According to Definition
A.5
in Appendix
A
,
if we identify the
q
-simplices as vertices
V
,
the polyhedra as hyperedges
E
,
then the matrix
B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}
can be identified as the incidence matrix over the oriented hypergraph
H=(V,E)
,
moreover,
if we regard
V^{-1}
as the weights over
E
,
then the up persistent Laplacian
\Delta_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
can be regarded as the Laplacian over the oriented hypergraph
H
.

On the other hand,
we can also take the dual hypergraph as defined in Definition
A.7
into account,
i.e.,
we regard each polyhedron as a vertex,
and each
q
-simplex as a hyperedge.
The Laplacian with respect to the dual hypergraph
\Delta_{\mathrm{dual}}
can then be expressed as

\Delta_{\mathrm{dual}}=V^{-1}(B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}})^{T}B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}.

Since for
1\leq j\leq r
,

B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}(:,j)
corresponds to a boundary of some polyhedron
P_{j}
in
\mathcal{L}
,
the
r
diagonal entries of
(B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}})^{T}B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}
therefore count how many
q
-simplices there are in the boundary of the polyhedra
P_{1},P_{2},\cdots,P_{r}
respectively.
On the other hand,

l_{j}
counts the number of
(q+1)
-simplices that combine
P_{j}
.
Thus
we have the following proposition:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}
be a pair of simplicial complexes,
suppose
S_{q}^{\mathcal{L}}\subset\mathcal{L}
is non-branching and
S_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}\subset\mathcal{L}
is orientation-compatible.
Assume
\mathcal{L}
is unweighted.
Let
B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}
be an oriented representation of the boundary operator
\partial_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}
,
and let
B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}
be the matrix representation of
\partial_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}
.
The up persistent Laplacian

\Delta_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}=B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}\mathrm{diag}(l_{1}^{-1},l_{2}^{-1},\cdots,l_{r}^{-1})(B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}})^{T}

can be regarded as the Laplacian associated with some weighted oriented hypergraph
H
.
And

\Delta_{\mathrm{dual}}:=\mathrm{diag}(l_{1}^{-1},l_{2}^{-1},\cdots,l_{r}^{-1})(B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}})^{T}B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}

can be regarded as the Laplacian associated with the dual of
H
.
Moreover,
let
\{P_{1},P_{2},\cdots,P_{r}\}
be the
(q+1)
-dimensional polyhedra in
\mathcal{L}
corresponding to

B_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{K}}\coloneqq\begin{bmatrix}b_{1}&b_{2}&\cdots&b_{r}\end{bmatrix}
,
let
V(P_{j})
be the number of
(q+1)
-simplices that combine
P_{j}
,
and
C(P_{j})
be the the number of
q
-simplices in the boundary of
P_{j}
.
Then we obtain the following Cheeger-type inequality:

\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}\leq\min_{i\leq r}\frac{C(P_{i})}{V(P_{i})},

where
\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
is the minimal non-zero eigenvalue of
\Delta_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
.

6
Discussion

In this paper we present an algorithm to compute up persistent Laplacian with quadratic time complexity when the set
S_{q}^{\mathcal{L}}
is non-branching and
S_{q+1}^{\mathcal{L}}
is orientation-compatible.
It is of definite interest to think of algorithms with quadratic time complexity in more general cases.
Other than that,
although it implies that the minimal non-zero eigenvalue
\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
with respect to
\Delta_{q,\mathrm{up}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
captures some geometric information,
the stability property of
\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}^{\mathcal{K},\mathcal{L}}
need to be explored in more details.

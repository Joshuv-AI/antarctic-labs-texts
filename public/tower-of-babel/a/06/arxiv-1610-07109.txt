Symplectic Geometric Algorithm for Quaternion Kinematical Differential Equation
Hong-Yan Zhang, Lu-Sha Zhou, Zi-Hao Wang, Long Ma, Yi-Fan Niu

Abstract
Solving quaternion kinematical differential equations is one of the most significant problems in the automation, navigation, aerospace and aeronautics literatures. Most existing approaches for this problem neither preserve the norm of quaternions nor avoid errors accumulated in the sense of long term time. We present symplectic geometric algorithms to deal with the quaternion kinematical differential equation by modeling its time-invariant and time-varying versions with Hamiltonian systems by adopting a three-step strategy. Firstly, a generalized Euler’s formula for the autonomous quaternion kinematical differential equation are proved and used to construct symplectic single-step transition operators via the centered implicit Euler scheme for autonomous Hamiltonian system. Secondly, the symplecitiy, orthogonality and invertibility of the symplectic transition operators are proved rigorously. Finally, the main results obtained are generalized to design symplectic geometric algorithm for the time-varying quaternion kinematical differential equation which is a non-autonomous and nonlinear Hamiltonian system essentially. Our novel algorithms have simple algorithmic structures and low time complexity of computation, which are easy to be implemented with real-time techniques. The correctness and efficiencies of the proposed algorithms are verified and validated via numerical simulations. Keywords : Quaternion kinematical differential equation; Symplectic geometric algorithm; Hamiltonian system; Non-autonomous system

Preliminaries

2.1
Hamiltonian System

W. R. Hamiltonian introduced the canonical differential equations
[
39
,
40
]

\frac{\dif{p_{i}}}{\dif{t}}=-\frac{\partial{H}}{\partial{q_{i}}},\quad\frac{\dif{q_{i}}}{\dif{t}}=\frac{\partial{H}}{\partial{p_{i}}},\quad i=1,2,\cdots,N

for problems of geometrical optics, where
p_{i}
are the generalized momentums,

q_{i}
are the generalized displacements and
H=H(p_{1},\cdots,p_{N},q_{1},\cdots,q_{N})
is the Hamiltonian, viz., the total energy of the system. Let

\bm{p}={[p_{1},\cdots,p_{N}]}^{\textsf{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{{N}\times{1}}
,

\bm{q}={[q_{1},\cdots,q_{N}]}^{\textsf{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{{N}\times{1}}
, and

\bm{z}={[p_{1},\cdots,p_{N},q_{1},\cdots,q_{N}]}^{\textsf{T}}={[{\bm{p}}^{\textsf{T}},{\bm{q}}^{\textsf{T}}]}^{\textsf{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{{2N}\times{1}}
,
then
H=H(\bm{p},\bm{q})=H(\bm{z})
can be specified by
\bm{z}
in the
2N
-dimensional phase space. Since the gradient of
H
is

H_{\bm{z}}={\left[\frac{\partial{H}}{\partial{p_{1}}},\cdots,\frac{\partial{H}}{\partial{p_{N}}},\frac{\partial{H}}{\partial{q_{1}}},\cdots,\frac{\partial{H}}{\partial{q_{N}}}\right]}^{\textsf{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{{2N}\times{1}}.

Then the canonical equation is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{\dif{\bm{z}}}{\dif{t}}=\bm{J}^{-1}\cdot H_{\bm{z}}(\bm{z}),\quad\bm{J}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{O}_{N}&\bm{I}_{N}\\
-\bm{I}_{N}&\bm{O}_{N}\end{bmatrix}

(3)

where
\bm{I}_{N}
is the
N
-by-
N
identical matrix,
\bm{O}_{N}
is the
N
-by-
N
zero matrix and
\bm{J}

is the
2N
-th order standard symplectic matrix
[
41
,
36
]
. Any system which can be described by Eq.(
3
) is called an Hamiltonian system. The canonical equation of Hamiltonian system is invariant under the symplectic transform (phase flow), the evolution of the system is the evolution of symplectic transform, both the symplectic symmetry and the total energy of the system can be preserved simultaneously and automatically
[
39
,
42
,
43
,
44
]
.

2.2
Transition Mapping

The symplectic geometric algorithms are motivated by these fundamental facts. Ruth
[
33
]
and Feng
[
41
]
emphasized two key points in their pioneer works:

(a)

symplectic geometric algorithm is a kind of difference scheme which preserves the symplecitc structure of Hamiltonian system;

(b)

the single-step transition mapping is a symplectic transform (matrix) which preserves the symplectic structure of the difference equation obtained by discritizing the original continuous Eq.(
3
).

When the
initial condition

\left.\bm{z}(t)\right|_{t_{0}}=\bm{z}(t_{0})={[p_{1}(t_{0}),\cdots,p_{N}(t_{0}),q_{1}(t_{0}),\cdots,q_{N}(t_{0})]}^{\textsf{T}}

is given, the symplectic difference scheme (SDS) for Eq.(
3
) can be written by

\bm{z}[k+1]=\bm{G}_{\tau}\bm{z}[k],\quad\bm{z}[0]=\bm{z}(t_{0}),\quad k=0,1,2,\cdots

(4)

where
\tau
is the time step,
\bm{z}[k]=\bm{z}(t_{0}+k\tau)
is the sample value at the discrete time
t_{k}=t_{0}+k\tau
,
and

\bm{G}_{\tau}:\mathbb{R}^{{2N}\times{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{{2N}\times{1}}
,
\bm{z}[k]\mapsto\bm{z}[k+1]

is the transition operator, also named as transition mapping or matrix, from
k
-th step to
(k+1)
-th step such that

{\bm{G}_{\tau}}^{\textsf{T}}\bm{J}\bm{G}_{\tau}=\bm{J}.

(5)

Equivalently, we have

\bm{G}_{\tau}\in\mathrm{Sp}(2N,\mathbb{R})\subset\mathrm{GL}(2N,\mathbb{\mathbb{R}})\subset\mathbb{R}^{{2N}\times{2N}},

in which
\mathrm{Sp}(2N,\mathbb{R})
is the symplectic transform group and
\mathrm{GL}(2N,\mathbb{\mathbb{R}})
is the general linear transform group
[
45
,
46
,
47
]
.
Usually,
\bm{G}_{\tau}
is specified by numerical schemes adopted.

For the general nonlinear and nonseparable Hamiltonian system, a useful symplectic difference scheme, i.e., the
centered Euler implicit scheme
of the second order (CEIS-2)
[
36
]
, described by

\begin{split}\frac{\bm{p}[k+1]-\bm{p}[k]}{\tau}=-H_{\bm{q}}(\bar{\bm{z}}_{k}),\quad\frac{\bm{q}[k+1]-\bm{q}[k]}{\tau}=H_{\bm{p}}(\bar{\bm{z}}_{k}),\end{split}

(6)

in which the midpoint

\bar{\bm{z}}_{k}=\frac{\bm{z}[k+1]+\bm{z}[k]}{2}

(7)

can be used to determine the transition matrix
\bm{G}_{\tau}
.
Let

\displaystyle\bm{B}=\bm{J}^{-1}H_{\bm{zz}}(\bar{\bm{z}}_{k}),\quad\phi(\lambda)=\frac{1+\lambda}{1-\lambda},

(8)

where
H_{\bm{z}\bm{z}}(\cdot)=\left(\frac{\partial^{2}{H(\cdot)}}{\partial{z_{i}}\partial{z_{j}}}\right)_{2N\times 2N}
is the Hessel matrix at the midpoint
\bar{\bm{z}}_{k}
,
and

\phi(\cdot)
is the Cayley transform. Then for small
\tau
such that
\bm{I}-\frac{\tau}{2}\bm{B}
is nonsingular, the
\bm{G}_{\tau}
will be
[
36
]

\begin{split}\bm{G}_{\tau}=\phi\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\bm{B}\right)=\left[\bm{I}+\frac{\tau}{2}\bm{B}\right]\cdot\left[\bm{I}-\frac{\tau}{2}\bm{B}\right]^{-1}=\left[\bm{I}-\frac{\tau}{2}\bm{B}\right]^{-1}\left[\bm{I}+\frac{\tau}{2}\bm{B}\right].\end{split}

(9)

Note that although Eq.(
9
) is an approximate result in the sense of approximate
conservation
[
41
]

for the
general nonlinear Hamiltonian system (Linear Hamiltonian system requires that the Hessel matrix
H_{\bm{zz}}(\cdot)
is symmetric), it could be a precise solution for some special cases.

A-QKDE and Autonomous Hamilton System

When the matrix

\bm{q}={[q_{1},q_{2}]}^{\textsf{T}}={[e_{2},e_{3}]}^{\textsf{T}}

\mathbbm{q}={[{\bm{p}}^{\textsf{T}},{\bm{q}}^{\textsf{T}}]}^{\textsf{T}}\equiv\bm{z}

\frac{\dif{\bm{z}}}{\dif{t}}=\bm{J}^{-1}H_{\bm{z}}(\bm{z})=\frac{1}{2}\bm{A}(\bm{\omega})\bm{z}

\bm{J}^{-1}H_{\bm{z}}(\bm{z})=\frac{1}{2}\bm{A}\bm{z}

Therefore

\begin{split}\bm{A}=2\bm{J}^{-1}H_{\bm{zz}}(\bm{z}),\quad H_{\bm{zz}}(\bm{z})=\frac{1}{2}\bm{J}\bm{A},\quad\bm{B}=\bm{J}^{-1}H_{\bm{zz}}(\bm{z})=\frac{1}{2}\bm{A}\end{split}

(11)

according to

8

\bm{J}\bm{A}\neq\bm{A}\bm{J}
, so the Hessel matrix

.

3.2
Symplectic Transition Mapping

Obviously, the A-QKDE is an autonomous Hamiltonian system and the
symplectic algorithm can be adopted to find its numerical solution. In the following,
we will prove a lemma and an interesting Euler’s formula for constructing the symplectic difference scheme for A-QKDE.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose matrix
\bm{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{{2n}\times{2n}}
is skew-symmetric, i.e.,
{\bm{M}}^{\textsf{T}}=-\bm{M}
and there exists
a positive constant
\gamma
such that
\bm{M}^{2}=-\gamma^{2}\bm{I}
. Let
\phi(\lambda)=\frac{1+\lambda}{1-\lambda}
be the
Caylay transformation and
\hat{\bm{M}}={\gamma}^{-1}\bm{M}
, then for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
the Euler formula

\begin{split}\phi(x\bm{M})=\frac{1}{1+\alpha}[(1-\alpha)\bm{I}+2x\bm{M}]=\cos\theta(x,\gamma)\bm{I}+\sin\theta(x,\gamma)\hat{\bm{M}},\quad\hat{\bm{M}}^{2}=-\bm{I}\end{split}

(12)

holds, in which
\theta=\theta(x,\gamma)=2\cdot\arctan(x\gamma)
and
\alpha=x^{2}\gamma^{2}
. Furthermore,
\phi(x\bm{M})
is an orthogonal matrix.

Proof
: It is trivial that
{\hat{\bm{M}}}^{\textsf{T}}=-\hat{\bm{M}}
and
\hat{\bm{M}}^{2}=-\bm{I}
. For any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we find that

(\bm{I}-x\bm{M})(\bm{I}+x\bm{M})=(1+x^{2}\gamma^{2})\bm{I}
. In consequence

(\bm{I}-x\bm{M})^{-1}=\frac{1}{1+x^{2}\gamma^{2}}(\bm{I}+x\bm{M})
.
Hence the Caylay transformation
\phi(x\bm{M})
can be simplified as following

\begin{split}\phi(x\bm{M})&=(\bm{I}-x\bm{M})^{-1}(\bm{I}+x\bm{M})=\frac{1}{1+\alpha}[(1-\alpha)\bm{I}+2x\bm{M}]\end{split}

(13)

where
\alpha=x^{2}\gamma^{2}
. Put
\tan\frac{\theta}{2}=x\gamma,\alpha=x^{2}\gamma^{2}
,
then by the trigonometric identity and
\bm{M}=\gamma\hat{\bm{M}}
we immediately have

\begin{split}\phi(x\bm{M})=\frac{1}{1+x^{2}\gamma^{2}}[(1-x^{2}\gamma^{2})\bm{I}+2x\gamma\hat{\bm{M}}]&=\cos\theta(x,\gamma)\bm{I}+\sin\theta(x,\gamma)\hat{\bm{M}}.\end{split}

(14)

Moreover,

\displaystyle{[\phi(x\bm{M})]}^{\textsf{T}}\cdot\phi(x\bm{M})

\displaystyle={[\cos\theta(x,\gamma)\bm{I}+\sin\theta(x,\gamma)\hat{\bm{M}}]}^{\textsf{T}}[\cos\theta(x,\gamma)\bm{I}+\sin\theta(x,\gamma)\hat{\bm{M}}]

\displaystyle=\cos^{2}\theta(x,\gamma)\bm{I}-\sin^{2}\theta(x,\gamma)\hat{\bm{M}}^{2}=\bm{I}.

Similarly, we can obtain
\phi(x\bm{M})\cdot{[\phi(x\bm{M})]}^{\textsf{T}}=\bm{I}
. Hence
\phi(x\bm{M})
is orthogonal by definition.

Theorem 2
.

(
Euler’s formula
) For any time step
\tau
and time-invariant vector
\bm{\omega}
, the transition mapping
\bm{G}_{\tau}^{\mathbbm{q}}
for the A-QKDE can be represented by

\begin{split}\bm{G}_{\tau}^{\mathbbm{q}}=\frac{1}{1+\alpha}\left[\left(1-\alpha\right)\bm{I}+\frac{\tau}{2}\bm{A}\right]=\cos\theta(\bm{\omega},\tau)\cdot\bm{I}+\sin\theta(\bm{\omega},\tau)\cdot\hat{\bm{A}},\end{split}

(15)

in which
\alpha=\tau^{2}\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert^{2}/16
,
\theta=2\arctan(\tau\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert/4)
,
\hat{\bm{A}}=\bm{A}/\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert
and
\hat{\bm{A}}^{2}=-\bm{I}
.

Proof
: With the help of Eq.(
9
) and Eg.(
11
), the transition mapping will be

\bm{G}_{\tau}^{\mathbbm{q}}=\phi\left(\frac{\tau}{2}\bm{B}_{\bm{z}}\right)=\phi\left(\frac{\tau}{4}\bm{A}\right)
.
Let
x=\tau/4,\bm{M}=\bm{A},\gamma=\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert
,
then
\alpha=x^{2}\gamma^{2}=\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert^{2}\tau^{2}/16
. Thus the theorem follows from
Lemma
1
directly.

Theorem 3
.

For any
\bm{\omega}\in\mathbb{R}^{{3}\times{1}}
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, the transition mapping
\bm{G}_{\tau}^{\mathbbm{q}}

is an orthogonal transformation and an invertible symplectic transformation with first-order precision, i.e.,

\displaystyle{[\bm{G}_{\tau}^{\mathbbm{q}}]}^{\textsf{T}}\bm{J}\bm{G}_{\tau}^{\mathbbm{q}}=\bm{J}+\mathcal{O}(\tau),\quad[\bm{G}_{\tau}^{\mathbbm{q}}]^{-1}=\bm{G}_{-\tau}^{\mathbbm{q}}={[\bm{G}_{\tau}^{\mathbbm{q}}]}^{\textsf{T}}.

(16)

Proof
: For a constant vector
\bm{\omega}
, we can find the function
\theta=2\arctan(\tau\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert/4)
is an odd function of time step
\tau
. By utilizing
\hat{\bm{A}}^{2}=-\bm{I}
and
{\hat{\bm{A}}}^{\textsf{T}}=-\hat{\bm{A}}
, we immediately obtain

\displaystyle{[\bm{G}_{\tau}^{\mathbbm{q}}]}^{\textsf{T}}

\displaystyle={[\cos\theta(\bm{\omega},\tau)\bm{I}+\sin\theta(\bm{\omega},\tau)\hat{\bm{A}}]}^{\textsf{T}}

\displaystyle=\cos\theta(\bm{\omega},\tau)\bm{I}-\sin\theta(\bm{\omega},\tau)\hat{\bm{A}}=\cos(-\theta(\bm{\omega},\tau))\bm{I}+\sin(-\theta(\bm{\omega},\tau))\hat{\bm{A}}

\displaystyle=\cos\theta(\bm{\omega},-\tau))\bm{I}+\sin\theta(\bm{\omega},-\tau)\hat{\bm{A}}=\bm{G}_{-\tau}^{\mathbbm{q}},

which implies that the transition mapping is invertible. Moreover,
\bm{G}_{\tau}^{\mathbbm{q}}
is orthogonal by
Lemma
1
. In consequence,
{[\bm{G}_{\tau}^{\mathbbm{q}}]}^{\textsf{T}}=\bm{G}_{-\tau}^{\mathbbm{q}}=\left(\bm{G}_{\tau}^{\mathbbm{q}}\right)^{-1}
.
Furthermore, simple algebraic operation implies that

\bm{J}\hat{\bm{A}}-\hat{\bm{A}}\bm{J}=2(\bm{J}+\hat{q}\hat{\bm{A}})\hat{\bm{A}}

because
\hat{\bm{A}}\bm{J}\hat{\bm{A}}=\bm{J}+2\hat{q}\hat{\bm{A}}
and

\hat{\bm{A}}^{-1}=-\hat{\bm{A}}
,
where
\hat{q}=\omega_{2}/\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert
. Therefore,

\displaystyle{[\bm{G}_{\tau}^{\mathbbm{q}}]}^{\textsf{T}}\bm{J}[\bm{G}_{\tau}^{\mathbbm{q}}]=\bm{J}+(\bm{J}+\hat{q}\hat{\bm{A}})\cdot(-2\sin^{2}\theta\bm{I}+2\sin\theta\cos\theta\hat{\bm{A}}).\

Since both
\hat{q}
and
\hat{\bm{A}}
are independent with
\tau
,
\sin\theta=\frac{2\tan\frac{\theta}{2}}{1+\tan^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}}=\frac{2\cdot\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert\tau}{1+\frac{1}{16}\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert^{2}\tau^{2}}
,
\cos\theta=\frac{1-\tan^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}}{1+\tan^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}}=\frac{1+\frac{1}{16}\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert^{2}\tau^{2}}{1+\frac{1}{16}\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert^{2}\tau^{2}}
,
we can deduce that

{[\bm{G}_{\tau}^{\mathbbm{q}}]}^{\textsf{T}}\bm{J}[\bm{G}_{\tau}^{\mathbbm{q}}]=\bm{J}+\mathcal{O}(\tau)
.
Hence
\bm{G}_{\tau}^{\mathbbm{q}}
is a symplectic matrix with first-order precision by definition
[
36
]
.

We remark that the nonlinearity of the QKDE, or equivalently the non-symmetric property of the Hessel matrix
\nabla^{2}H(\mathbbm{q})=H_{\bm{zz}}(\bm{z})
,
brings the non-commutativity of
\bm{J}
and
\hat{\bm{A}}
, i.e.,
\bm{J}\hat{\bm{A}}\neq\hat{\bm{A}}\bm{J}
,
which
lowers the precision of the Euler implicit midpoint formula from the second order to the first order
.

3.3
Comparison with Analytic Solution

Fortunately, the analytic solution (AS) for A-QKDE can be found without difficulty.
In fact for any
t\in[t_{0},t_{f}]
where
t_{0}
and
t_{f}
denote the initial and final time respectively, we have

\mathbbm{q}(t)=\exp{\left(\frac{1}{2}\bm{A}\cdot(t-t_{0})\right)}\cdot\mathbbm{q}(t_{0}),\quad t\in[t_{0},t_{f}].

(17)

Let
x=(t-t_{0})/2
and
\hat{\bm{A}}=\bm{A}/\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert
, then
\bm{A}^{2}=-\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert^{2}\bm{I}
show that

\displaystyle\exp{(\bm{A}x)}

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\frac{(\bm{A}x)^{2k}}{(2k)!}+\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\frac{(\bm{A}x)^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}=\cos(\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert x)\bm{I}+\sin(\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert x)\hat{\bm{A}}

\displaystyle=\cos\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert(t-t_{0})\right)\bm{I}+\sin\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert(t-t_{0})\right)\hat{\bm{A}}.

Thus for
t-t_{0}=\tau
, the AS to the transition mapping for the A-QKDE is

\bm{\bm{G}}^{\mathbbm{q}}_{\tau,\mathrm{AS}}=\bm{I}\cos\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert\tau}{2}+\hat{\bm{A}}\sin\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert\tau}{2},\quad\forall\tau\in\mathbb{R}.

(18)

At the same time, for sufficiently small
\tau
in Eq.(
15
) we have

\begin{split}\bm{\bm{G}}^{\mathbbm{q}}_{\tau}=\bm{I}\cos(2\arctan\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert\tau}{4})+\hat{\bm{A}}\sin(2\arctan\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert\tau}{4})\sim\bm{I}\cos\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert\tau}{2}+\hat{\bm{A}}\sin\frac{\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\right\rVert\tau}{2}\end{split}

(19)

for sufficiently small
x=\left\lVert\bm{\omega}\tau\right\rVert
since

\displaystyle\cos\frac{x}{2}-\cos\left(2\arctan\frac{x}{4}\right)

6
Conclusions

In this paper we proposed the key idea of solving the QKDE with symplectic method: each QKDE can be described by a corresponding Hamiltonian system and the CEIS-2 or T-CEIS-4 can be used to design SGA for QKDE. The Euler’s formula for the symplectic transition mapping plays a key role in designing SAG for A-QKDE with first-order precision and NA-QKDE with second-order precision, which simplifies the original algorithmic structures and guarantees the low time complexity of computation.
The correctness and efficiencies of the SAG presented are verified and demonstrated by comparison with AS and NS. Examples show that our proposed algorithms perform very well since the norms of the quaternions can be preserved and the errors accumulated
can be eliminated.

As part of future work, we will investigate the high-order precision SGA
[
48
]
to QKDE, the robustness of the SGA to QKDE perturbed by noise, the applications of SGA to more general linear time-varying system and its combination with precise-integration method
[
49
]
. Additionally, we will also design second-order precision symplectic-precise integrator to solve the linear quadratic regulator problem and the matrix Ricatti equation since they play an important role in automation.

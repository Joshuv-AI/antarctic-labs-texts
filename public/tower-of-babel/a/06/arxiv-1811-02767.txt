1.
Introduction

Let
B,N_{1}
and
N_{2}
be Riemannian manifolds equipped with metrics
g_{B},g_{1}
and
g_{2}
, respectively, and let
f_{1},f_{2}
be two positive functions on
B
. Then the
bi-warped product

B\times_{f_{1}}N_{1}\times_{f_{2}}N_{2}
is the product manifold
B\times N_{1}\times N_{2}
equipped with the bi-warped product metric

g=g_{B}+f_{1}^{2}g_{1}+f_{2}^{2}g_{2}.

(1.1)

Bi-warped products extend the “ordinary” warped products and they form
an important class of multiply warped products (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
11
,
13
,
28
,
29
,
32
]
).

A submanifold
M
of a Kaehler manifold
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{g},J)
is called a
CR
submanifold

[
3
]
if there exist a holomorphic distribution
\mathfrak{D}^{T}
and a totally real distribution
\mathfrak{D}^{\perp}
on
M
such that
TM=\mathfrak{D}^{T}\oplus\mathfrak{D}^{\perp}
, where
TM
denotes the tangent bundle of
M
.

The study of warped products from submanifold theory points of view was initiated by
the first author in
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
. In particular, he proved in
[
5
]
that there do not exist warped products of the form
N_{\perp}\times_{f}N_{T}
in any Kaehler manifold, where
N_{T}
is a holomorphic submanifold and
N_{\perp}
is a totally real submanifold. On the other hand, he proved that, for any warped product of the form
M=N_{T}\times_{f}N^{n_{1}}_{\perp}
in any Kaehler manifold, the second fundamental form
h
of
M
satisfies

||h||^{2}\geq 2n_{1}||\nabla(\ln f)||^{2},

where
n_{1}=\dim\,N^{n_{1}}_{\perp}
and
\nabla(\ln f)
is the gradient of
\ln f
. He simply called such warped product submanifolds
N_{T}\times_{f}N^{n_{1}}_{\perp}
to be
CR
-warped products
.

In this paper, we investigate an important class of bi-warped product submanifolds in a contact metric manifold
\widetilde{M}
; namely,
CRS
bi-warped products of the form
N_{T}\times_{f_{1}}N^{n_{1}}_{\perp}\times_{f_{2}}N^{n_{2}}_{\theta}
, where
N_{T},N^{n_{1}}_{\perp}
and
N^{n_{2}}_{\theta}
are invariant, anti-invariant and pointwise slant submanifolds of
\widetilde{M}
and
f_{1},f_{2}
are warping functions.
We simply call such a submanifold a
CRS
bi-warped product
.

In this paper, first we prove that there are no proper
CRS
bi-warped products other than contact
CR
-biwarped products in any Sasakian manifold. Then we investigate
CRS
bi-warped products
M=N_{T}\times_{f_{1}}N^{n_{1}}_{\perp}\times_{f_{2}}N^{n_{2}}_{\theta}
in cosymplectic manifolds. The main result of this paper states that, for such a
CRS
bi-warped product in a cosymplectic manifold, its second fundamental form
h
satisfies

\|h\|^{2}\geq 2n_{1}\|\nabla(\ln f_{1})\|^{2}+2n_{2}(1+2\cot^{2}\theta)\|\nabla(\ln f_{2})\|^{2},

where
n_{1}=\dim\,N^{n_{1}}_{\perp},\,n_{2}=\dim\,N^{n_{2}}_{\theta}
and
\theta
is the slant function of
N^{n_{2}}_{\theta}
.
In this paper, we also study the equality case of the inequality. Several applications of our main result are also given. Furthermore, we provide a non-trivial example of submanifold which verify the equality case of the inequality.

2.
Preliminaries

A
(2m+1)
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(\widetilde{M}^{2m+1},g)
is said to be a
contact metric manifold
if it admits a
(1,1)
tensor field
\varphi
on its tangent bundle
T\widetilde{M}^{2m+1}
, a vector field
{\mathfrak{\xi}}
(i.e., the structure vector field) and a
1
-form
\eta
satisfying:

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\varphi^{2}=-Id+\eta\otimes{\mathfrak{\xi}},\,\,\,\eta({\mathfrak{\xi}})=1,\,\,\,\varphi(\xi)=0,\,\,\,\eta\circ\varphi=0,

(2.1)

\displaystyle g(\varphi X,\varphi Y)=g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y),\,\,\,\eta(X)=g(X,{\mathfrak{\xi}}).

for any vector fields
X,Y
on
\widetilde{M}^{2m+1}
, where
\widetilde{\nabla}
denotes the Riemannian connection with respect to the Riemannian metric
g
(see
[
4
]
). An almost contact metric manifold
(\widetilde{M},g,\varphi,\xi,\eta)
is said to be
normal
if the tensor field
N_{\varphi}=[\varphi,\varphi]+2d\eta\otimes\xi
vanishes identically, where
[\varphi,\varphi]
denotes the Nijenhuis tensor of
\varphi
.

A normal contact metric manifold is said to be a
Sasakian manifold
. An almost contact metric manifold is Sasakian if and only if

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}\varphi)Y=g(X,Y){\mathfrak{\xi}}-\eta(Y)X,\,\,\,\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}{\mathfrak{\xi}}=-\varphi X,

(2.2)

for any
X,Y\in\Gamma(T\widetilde{M})
, where
\Gamma(T\widetilde{M})
is the Lie algebra of vector fields on
\widetilde{M}
.
An almost contact metric manifold is called
almost cosymplectic
if
d\eta=0
and
d\varphi=0
according to D. E. Blair in
[
4
]
. In particular, an almost cosymplectic manifold is called
cosymplectic
if it satisfies

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}\varphi=0,\quad\widetilde{\nabla}\xi=0.

(2.3)

Let
M^{n}
be an
n
-dimensional submanifold of an almost contact metric manifold
\widetilde{M}^{2m+1}
such that the structure vector field
{\mathfrak{\xi}}
tangent to
M^{n}
with induced metric
g
. Let
\Gamma(T^{\perp}M^{n})
denote the space of all vector fields normal to
M^{n}
. Then the Gauss and Weingarten formulas are given respectively by (see, e,g.,
[
10
,
12
,
33
]
)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}Y=\nabla_{X}Y+h(X,Y),\quad\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}N=-A_{N}X+\nabla^{\perp}_{X}N,

(2.4)

for
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM^{n})
and
N\in\Gamma(T^{\perp}M^{n})
, where
\nabla
and
\nabla^{\perp}
denote the connections on the tangent and normal bundles of
M^{n}
, respectively, and
A
the shape operator of
M^{n}
. It is well known that the second fundamental form
h
and the shape operator
A
are related by
g(h(X,Y),N)=g(A_{N}X,Y).

Let
x\in M^{n}
and
e_{1},\cdots,e_{n},e_{n+1},\cdots,e_{2m+1}
are orthonormal basis of the tangent space
\widetilde{M}^{2m+1}
such that restricted to
M^{n}
,
e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}
are tangent to
M^{n}
and hence
e_{n+1},\cdots,e_{2m+1}
are normal to
M^{n}
. Let
\{h^{r}_{ij}\}\,(1\leq i,j\leq n;\,n+1\leq r\leq 2m+1)
denote the coefficients of
h
with respect to the local frame field. Then

\displaystyle h_{ij}^{r}=g(h(e_{i},e_{j}),e_{r})=g(A_{e_{r}}e_{i},e_{j}),\,\,\,\|h\|^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}g(h(e_{i},e_{j}),h(e_{i},e_{j})).

(2.5)

The mean curvature vector
H
is defined by
H=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}h(e_{i},e_{i})
. A submanifold
M
is called
totally geodesic
if
h=0
;
totally umbilical
if
h(X,Y)=g(X,Y)H
; and
minimal
if
H=0
.

For submanifolds
M
of an almost contact metric manifold
(\widetilde{M}^{2m+1},g,\varphi,\xi,\eta)
, there are three important classes of submanifolds depending on the action of
\varphi
on
TM
.

(a) A submanifold
M^{2n+1}
tangent to
{\mathfrak{\xi}}
is called
invariant
if
\varphi
preserves each tangent space of
M^{2n+1}
, i.e.,
\varphi(T_{x}M^{2n+1})\subseteq T_{x}M^{2n+1},\forall x\in M^{2n+1}
.

(b) A submanifold
M^{n}
is called
anti-invariant
if
\varphi
maps any tangent space of
M^{n}
into the corresponding normal space, i.e.,
\varphi(T_{x}M^{n})\subset T^{\perp}_{x}M^{n},\forall x\in M^{n}
.

(c) A submanifold
M^{n}
is called
pointwise slant

[
16
,
14
]
if for any nonzero vector
X\in T_{x}M^{n}\,(x\in M)
, the angle
\theta(X)
between
\varphi X
and
T_{x}M^{n}
is independent of the choice of
X\in T_{x}M
. In this case,
\theta
defines a function on
M^{n}
, called the
slant function
. In particular, if the slant function
\theta
is a global constant on
M^{n}
, then
M^{n}
is said to be a
slant submanifold
or a
\theta
-slant submanifold
.

Anti-invariant submanifolds are slant submanifolds with slant function
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
. A pointwise slant submanifold is called
proper
if
0<\theta<\frac{\pi}{2}
. See
[
19
,
27
]
for non-trivial examples of pointwise slant submanifolds.

For any
X\in\Gamma(TM^{n})
and
N\in\Gamma(T^{\perp}M^{n})
, we put

\varphi X=TX+FX,\quad\varphi N=tN+fN,

where
TX
(resp.,
tN
) is the tangential component, and
FX
(resp.,
fN
) is the normal component of
\varphi X
(resp. of
\varphi N
).

We recall the following useful characterization from
[
27
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
M^{n}
be a submanifold of an almost contact metric manifold
\widetilde{M}^{2m+1}
with
\mathfrak{\xi}\in\Gamma(TM^{n})
. Then
M^{n}
is pointwise slant if and only if

\displaystyle T^{2}=\cos^{2}\theta\left(-I+\eta\otimes\mathfrak{\xi}\right),

(2.6)

where
\theta
is the slant function and
I
denotes the identity map on
TM^{n}
.

Following relations are straightforward consequence of (
2.6
)

\displaystyle g(TX,TY)=\cos^{2}\theta[g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y)],

(2.7)

\displaystyle g(FX,FY)=\sin^{2}\theta[g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y)],

(2.8)

for vector fields
X,Y\in\Gamma(M^{n})
.
Also, for pointwise slant submanifolds, we have

\displaystyle tFX=\sin^{2}\theta\left(-X+\eta(X)\mathfrak{\xi}\right),~~~~fFX=-FTX,\quad X\in\Gamma(TM^{n}).

(2.9)

Next, we give the following useful result for later use.

Proposition 2.2
.

Every pointwise slant submanifold
M
of a Sasakian manifold
\widetilde{M}^{2m+1}
is anti-invariant if and only if the structure vector field
\xi
is normal to
M
.

Proof.

From (
2.2
) and (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle A_{\mathfrak{\xi}}X=TX,\quad\nabla^{\perp}_{X}\xi=-FX,

(2.10)

for any
X\in\Gamma(TM)
. Taking the inner product in (
2.10
) with
Y\in\Gamma(TM)
, we get

\displaystyle g(h(X,Y),\mathfrak{\xi})=g(TX,Y).

(2.11)

From (
2.11
), we find
\cos^{2}\theta\;g(X,Y)=0
via polarization identity. Since, the metric
g
is Riemannian, we obtain
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
. The converse is trivial.
∎

3.
Some basic results on
CRS
bi-warped products

Let
M=B\times_{f_{1}}N
with
N=N_{1}\times_{f_{2}}N_{2}
be a bi-warped product submanifold of a Riemannian manifold
\widetilde{M}
. Put
{\mathfrak{D}}=TB
. Then we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{Z}X=\nabla_{X}Z=\sum_{i=1}^{2}X(\ln f_{i})Z^{i}

(3.1)

for any
X\in\Gamma({\mathfrak{D}})
and
Z\in\Gamma(TN)
, where
Z^{i}
denotes the
N_{i}
-component of
Z
, and
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection on
M
(see, e.g.,
[
26
]
).

Remark 3.1
.

It is well known that, for a warped product manifold
M=B\times_{f}F
,
B
is totally geodesic and
F
is totally umbilical in
M
(see, e.g.,
[
5
,
18
]
).

Now, we give the following definition of
CRS
bi-warped product submanifolds.

Definition 3.1
.

A
CRS
bi-warped product
of an almost contact metric manifold
(\widetilde{M}^{2m+1},g,\varphi,\xi,\eta)
is a bi-warped product
M^{n}=N_{T}^{2p+1}\times_{f_{1}}N^{n_{1}}_{\perp}\times_{f_{2}}N^{n_{2}}_{\theta}
in
\widetilde{M}^{2m+1}
, where
N_{T}^{2p+1}
is an invariant submanifold tangent to
\xi
,
N^{n_{1}}_{\perp}
is an anti-invariant submanifold,
N^{n_{2}}_{\theta}
is a pointwise slant submanifold of
\widetilde{M}^{2m+1}
, and
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are two positive smooth functions on
N_{T}^{2p+1}
.

Remark 3.2
.

It follows from Remark
3.1
that
N_{T},N_{T}\times_{f_{1}}N^{n_{1}}_{\perp}
and
N_{T}\times_{f_{2}}N^{n_{2}}_{\theta}
are totally geodesic in the
CRS
bi-warped product
M^{n}=N_{T}\times_{f_{1}}N^{n_{1}}_{\perp}\times_{f_{2}}N^{n_{2}}_{\theta}
.

Remark 3.3
.

A
CRS
bi-warped product is a
contact CR-biwarped product
if it is of the form
M^{n}=N_{T}^{2p+1}\times_{f_{1}}N^{n_{1}}_{\perp}\times_{f_{2}}N^{n_{2}}_{\perp}
, where
N_{T}^{2p+1}
is an invariant submanifold tangent to
\xi
,
N^{n_{1}}_{\perp}
and
N^{n_{2}}_{\perp}
are anti-invariant submanifolds of
\widetilde{M}^{2m+1}
.

Let
{\mathfrak{D}^{T}},\,\,{\mathfrak{D}}^{\perp}
and
{\mathfrak{D}}^{\theta}
denote the tangent bundles of
N_{T},\,\,N_{\perp}
and
N_{\theta}
, respectively. Then the tangent and normal spaces of
M^{n}
are given respectively by

\displaystyle TM^{n}={\mathfrak{D}^{T}}\oplus{\mathfrak{D}}^{\perp}\oplus{\mathfrak{D}}^{\theta},\quad T^{\perp}M^{n}=\varphi{\mathfrak{D}}^{\perp}\oplus F{\mathfrak{D}}^{\theta}\oplus\mu,

(3.2)

where
\mu
is the invariant subbundle of
T^{\perp}M^{n}
. Clearly,
{\rm Span}\{\xi\}\subset{\mathfrak{D}^{T}}
by definition.

The following result provides the non-existence of pointwise semi-slant warped products in Sasakian manifolds.

Proposition 3.1
.

There do not exist
CRS
bi-warped products in a Sasakian manifold other than contact
CR
-biwarped products.

Proof.

Let
M=N_{T}\times_{f_{1}}N_{\perp}\times_{f_{2}}N_{\theta}
be a
CRS
bi-warped product in a Sasakian manifold
\widetilde{M}
.
If
V\in\Gamma({\mathfrak{D}}^{\theta})
, it follows from (
2.1
), (
2.2
), (
2.4
) and (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle\xi(\ln f_{2})V+h(\xi,V)=\nabla_{V}\xi+h(\xi,V)=\widetilde{\nabla}_{V}\xi=-\varphi V=-TV-FV.

(3.3)

By comparing the tangential components of (
3.3
), we find
\xi(\ln f_{2})V=-TV
, which implies
\xi(\ln f_{2})=0
and
TV=0
. Hence, we find
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
. Consequently,
M
is a contact
CR
-biwarped product.
∎

The following result is an immediate consequence of Proposition
3.1
.

Corollary 3.1
.

There do not exist any proper pointwise semi-slant warped product submanifold
M=N_{T}\times_{f}N_{\theta}
in a Sasakian manifold.

Remark 3.4
.

One may also prove that there are no proper bi-warped products of the form
M=N_{T}\times_{f_{1}}N_{\perp}\times_{f_{2}}N_{\theta}
in a Sasakian manifold such that the characteristic vector field
\xi
is tangent to either
N_{\perp}
or to
N_{\theta}
, or
\xi
is normal to
M
.

In the next section, we have found that if we replace the ambient spaces from Sasakian to cosymplectic, then such warped products
N_{T}\times_{f_{1}}N_{\perp}\times_{f_{2}}N_{\theta}
do exist.

4.
CRS
bi-warped products of cosymplectic manifolds

Proposition
3.1
shows that there are no proper
CRS
bi-warped products in any Sasakian manifold. In this section, we study
CSR
bi-warped products in cosymplectic manifolds which is quite different from Sasakian case.

From now on, assume that
M=N_{T}\times_{f_{1}}N_{\perp}\times_{f_{2}}N_{\theta}
is a
CRS
bi-warped product of a cosymplectic manifold
\widetilde{M}
whose structure vector field
\xi
is tangent to
N_{T}
.

Now, we give the following useful lemmas for later use.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
M^{n}=N_{T}\times_{f_{1}}N_{\perp}\times_{f_{2}}N_{\theta}
be a
CRS
bi-warped product in a cosymplectic manifold
\widetilde{M}^{2m+1}
. Then
\xi(\ln f_{1})=\xi(\ln f_{2})=0
and
h(X,\xi)=0
for every
X\in\Gamma(N_{\perp}\times N_{\theta})
.

Proof.

It follows from (
2.4
) and (
2.3
) that

\nabla_{X}\xi+h(X,\xi)=\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}{\xi}=0
for every
X\in\Gamma(N_{\perp}\times N_{\theta})
, which implies
\nabla_{X}\xi=0
and
h(X,\xi)=0
. Now, after combining
\nabla_{X}\xi=0
with (
3.1
), we obtain
\xi(\ln f_{1})=\xi(\ln f_{2})=0
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
M^{n}=N_{T}\times_{f_{1}}N_{\perp}\times_{f_{2}}N_{\theta}
be a
CRS
bi-warped product in a cosymplectic manifold
\widetilde{M}^{2m+1}
. Then we have

(i)

g(h(X,Y),\varphi Z)=0=g(h(X,Y),FV)
;

(ii)

g(h(X,Z),\varphi W)=-\varphi X(\ln f_{1})\,g(Z,W)
;

(iii)

g(h(X,U),FV)=-\varphi X(\ln f_{2})\,g(U,V)-X(\ln f_{2})\,g(U,TV)
.

for
X,Y\in\Gamma({\mathfrak{D}^{T}})
,
Z,W\in\Gamma({\mathfrak{D}}^{\perp})
and
U,V\in\Gamma({\mathfrak{D}}^{\theta})
.

Proof.

For any
X,Y\in\Gamma({\mathfrak{D}^{T}})
and
Z\in\Gamma({\mathfrak{D}}^{\perp})
, we find

\displaystyle g(h(X,Y),\varphi Z)\,

\displaystyle=g(\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}Y,\varphi Z)=-g(\widetilde{\nabla}_{X}\varphi Y,Z)=-g(\nabla_{X}\varphi Y,Z).

Using the fact that
N_{T}
is totally geodesic in
M^{n}
(see Remark
3.1
) with the orthogonality of vector fields, we find
g(h(X,Y),\varphi Z)=0
, which is the first equality of (i). Similarly, we have the second equality of (i).
For (ii), we have

\displaystyle g(h(X,Z),\varphi W)\,

\displaystyle=g(\widetilde{\nabla}_{Z}X,\varphi W)=g((\widetilde{\nabla}_{Z}\varphi)X,W)-g((\widetilde{\nabla}_{Z}\varphi X,W).

Using (
2.3
), Remark
3.2
, and (
3.1
) with the orthogonality of vector fields, we get (ii). Similarly, we obtain (iii). ∎

Replacing
X
by
\varphi X
in Lemma
4.2
(ii) and using (
2.1
) and Lemma
4.1
, we find

\displaystyle g(h(\varphi X,Z),\varphi W)=X(\ln f_{1})g(Z,W).

(4.1)

Similarly, if we replace
X
by
\varphi X
,
U
by
TU
, and
V
by
TV
in Lemma
4.2
(iii), we obtain the following useful relations:

\displaystyle g(h(\varphi X,U),FV)=X(\ln f_{2})\,g(U,V)-\varphi X(\ln f_{2})\,g(U,TV),

(4.2)

\displaystyle g(h(X,TU),FV)=\varphi X(\ln f_{2})\,g(U,TV)-\cos^{2}\theta\,X(\ln f_{2})\,g(U,V),

(4.3)

\displaystyle g(h(X,U),FTV)=\cos^{2}\theta\,X(\ln f_{2})\,g(U,V)-\varphi X(\ln f_{2})\,g(U,TV),

(4.4)

\displaystyle g(h(\varphi X,TU),FV)=-X(\ln f_{2})\,g(U,TV)-\cos^{2}\theta\,\varphi X(\ln f_{2})\,g(U,V),

(4.5)

\displaystyle g(h(\varphi X,U),FTV)=X(\ln f_{2})\,g(U,TV)+\cos^{2}\theta\,\varphi X(\ln f_{2})\,g(U,V),

(4.6)

\displaystyle g(h(X,TU),FTV)=-\cos^{2}\theta\,X(\ln f_{2})\,g(U,TV)-\cos^{2}\theta\,\varphi X(\ln f_{2})\,g(U,V),

(4.7)

\displaystyle g(h(\varphi X,TU),FTV)=\cos^{2}\theta\,X(\ln f_{2})\,g(U,V)-\cos^{2}\theta\,\varphi X(\ln f_{2})\,g(U,TV).

(4.8)

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
M^{n}=N_{T}\times_{f_{1}}N_{\perp}\times_{f_{2}}N_{\theta}
be a
CRS
bi-warped product in a cosymplectic manifold
\widetilde{M}^{2m+1}
. Then

\displaystyle g(h(X,Z),FV)=g(h(X,V),\varphi Z)=0,

(4.9)

for any
X\in\Gamma({\mathfrak{D}^{T}})
,
Z\in\Gamma({\mathfrak{D}}^{\perp})
and
V\in\Gamma({\mathfrak{D}}^{\theta})
.

Proof.

For any
X\in\Gamma({\mathfrak{D}^{T}})
,
Z\in\Gamma({\mathfrak{D}}^{\perp})
and
V\in\Gamma({\mathfrak{D}}^{\theta})
, we have

\displaystyle g(h(X,Z),FV)

\displaystyle=g(\widetilde{\nabla}_{Z}X-\nabla_{Z}X,\varphi V-TV)

\displaystyle=g((\widetilde{\nabla}_{Z}\varphi)X,V)-g(\widetilde{\nabla}_{Z}\varphi X,V)-X(\ln f_{1})\,g(Z,TV).

Then we find the first equality of (
4.9
) from (
2.3
), (
3.1

For the second equality, first we have

\displaystyle\hskip 21.68121pt+\sin t\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{7}}+\sin r\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{8}},

\displaystyle W_{3}=-u\sin w\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}-v\sin w\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}+u\cos w\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}+v\cos w\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{3}},

(7.3)

\displaystyle W_{4}=-u\sin s\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}-v\sin s\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{2}}+u\cos s\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{4}}+v\cos s\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{4}},

\displaystyle W_{5}=-u\sin t\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{5}}-v\sin t\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{5}}+u\cos t\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{7}}+v\cos t\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{7}}-k\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{9}}-k\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{9}},

\displaystyle W_{7}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}.

Then we find

and

\displaystyle\varphi W_{1}=-\cos w\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}-\cos s\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{2}}-\sin w\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{3}}-\sin s\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{4}}-\cos t\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{5}}

\displaystyle\hskip 32.52127pt-\cos r\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{6}}-\sin t\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{7}}-\sin r\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{8}},

\displaystyle\varphi W_{2}=\cos w\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+\cos s\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}+\sin w\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}+\sin s\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{4}}+\cos t\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{5}}

\displaystyle\hskip 32.52127pt+\cos r\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{6}}+\sin t\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{7}}+\sin r\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{8}},

\displaystyle\varphi W_{3}=-v\sin w\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+u\sin w\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}+v\cos w\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}-u\cos w\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{3}},

(7.4)

\displaystyle\varphi W_{4}=-v\sin s\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}+u\sin s\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{2}}+v\cos s\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{4}}-u\cos s\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{4}},

\displaystyle\varphi W_{5}=-v\sin t\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{5}}+u\sin t\,\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{5}}+v\cos t\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{7}}-u\cos t\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{7}}-k\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{9}}+k\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{9}},

Clearly,
{\mathfrak{D}^{T}}=\rm{Span}\{W_{1},W_{2},W_{7}\}
is an invariant distribution. Since both
\varphi W_{3}
and
\varphi W_{4}
are orthogonal to
TM^{7}
,
{\mathfrak{D}}^{\perp}=\rm{Span}\{W_{3},W_{4}\}
is an anti-invariant distribution. Further, it is direct to show that
{\mathfrak{D}^{\theta}}=\rm{Span}\{W_{5},W_{6}\}
is a pointwise slant distribution with slant function
\theta=\arccos(\frac{2k^{2}}{u^{2}+v^{2}+2k^{2}})
.

Obviously,
{\mathfrak{D}^{T}},\,{\mathfrak{D}}^{\perp}
and
{\mathfrak{D}}^{\theta}
are integrable distributions on
M
. If we denote the integral manifolds of
{\mathfrak{D}^{T}},\;{\mathfrak{D}}^{\perp}
and
{\mathfrak{D}^{T}}^{\theta}
by
N_{T},\,N_{\perp}
and
N_{\theta}
, respectively, then the induced metric tensor
g
of
M=N_{T}\times_{f_{1}}N_{\perp}\times_{f_{2}}N_{\theta}
is given by

\displaystyle

\displaystyle g=g_{N_{T}}+f^{2}_{1}g_{N_{\perp}}+f^{2}_{2}g_{N_{\theta}},

(7.5)

\displaystyle g_{N_{T}}=4(du^{2}+dv^{2})+dz^{2},\;\;g_{N_{\perp}}=dw^{2}+ds^{2},\;\;g_{N_{\theta}}=dt^{2}+dr^{2},

\displaystyle f_{1}=\sqrt{u^{2}+v^{2}},\;\;f_{2}=\sqrt{u^{2}+v^{2}+2k^{2}}.

Hence,
M^{7}
is a
CRS
bi-warped product in
{\mathbb{R}}^{19}
which satisfy
\xi f_{1}=\xi f_{2}=0
.

Let
\widetilde{\nabla}
denote the Riemannian connection on
{\mathbb{R}}^{19}
.
Since
h(W_{i},W_{j})
is the normal component of
\widetilde{\nabla}_{W_{i}}W_{j}
, it follows easily from (

) that

\displaystyle h(\mathfrak{D}^{T},\mathfrak{D}^{T})=h(\mathfrak{D}^{\perp},\mathfrak{D}^{\perp})=h(\mathfrak{D}^{\perp},\mathfrak{D}^{\theta})=h(\mathfrak{D}^{\theta},\mathfrak{D}^{\theta})=\{0\},

\displaystyle h(\xi,\mathfrak{D}^{\perp})=h(\xi,\mathfrak{D}^{\theta})=\{0\},\quad\xi=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}.

(7.7)

Furthermore, we find from (
7.3
) and (
7.4
) that

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nabla}_{W_{1}}W_{3}=\frac{u}{u^{2}+v^{2}}W_{3}+\frac{uv}{(u^{2}+v^{2})^{2}}\varphi W_{3},

which implies
0\neq h(W_{1},W_{3})\in\varphi(\mathfrak{D}^{\perp})
. In the same way, we find

\displaystyle h({\mathfrak{D}}^{T},\mathfrak{D}^{\perp})\neq\{0\}\;\;{\rm and}\;\;h(\mathfrak{D}^{T},\mathfrak{D}^{\perp})\in\varphi(\mathfrak{D}^{\perp}).

(7.8)

By computing

for
i=1,2
and

, and by applying (
7.7
), we also have

\displaystyle h({\mathfrak{D}}^{T},\mathfrak{D}^{\theta})\neq\{0\}\;\;{\rm and}\;\;h(\mathfrak{D}^{T},\mathfrak{D}^{\theta})\in F(\mathfrak{D}^{\theta}).

(7.9)

Consequently, Remark
3.1
and (
7.7
)–(
7.9
) imply that
N_{T}
is totally geodesic in
\mathbb{R}^{19}
and
M
is
\mathfrak{D}^{\perp}\oplus\mathfrak{D}^{\theta}
-mixed totally geodesic. Furthermore, it follows from (
7.8
) and (
7.9
) that
M^{7}
is neither
\mathfrak{D}^{T}\oplus\mathfrak{D}^{\perp}
-mixed totally geodesic nor
\mathfrak{D}^{T}\oplus\mathfrak{D}^{\theta}
-mixed totally geodesic in

.

POLYIAMONDS ATTAINING EXTREMAL TOPOLOGICAL PROPERTIES, PART II
Greg Malen, Érika Roldán

Abstract
In Part II of this work we construct crystallized polyiamonds with h h holes for every h ≥ 1 h\geq 1 , that is polyiamonds which use the fewest possible tiles necessary to enclose h h holes. Furthermore, we prove that crystallized polyiamonds satisfy a set of structural conditions, and for every h ≥ 3 h\geq 3 there are multiple distinct crystallized polyiamonds with h h holes.

\begin{split}3(h-1)&=|T_{h-1}|+2-p_{min}\left(|T_{h-1}|+h-1\right)\\[8.0pt]

Concluding Remarks

In this paper we have completely solved the problem of finding
g_{\triangle}(h)
for all
h\geq 1
, the minimum number of triangles needed for constructing a polyiamond with
h
holes. This immediately determines the values of
f_{\triangle}(n)
as well, the maximum number of holes that can be enclosed by
n
tiles. Along with this, we examined several structural conditions of polyiamonds, such as having a dual graph which is a tree, having only holes with an area of 1, and having minimal outer perimeter. We refer to polyiamonds satisfying this trio of conditions as being
efficiently structured
, and have shown here that this is in fact equivalent to the property of being
crystallized
. This, however, is not the case for polyominoes, where being efficiently structured was recently shown to be a stronger condition
[
3
]
.

In a more general setting, we can ask these extremal geometrical and topological questions for higher dimensional simplicial complexes, and also consider the effect of changing the dimension of the ambient space.

1.
Introduction

The Young integral has been introduced in
[
14
]
, where the author defines extension of the Riemann-Stieltjes integral
\int fdg
when neither
f
nor
g
have finite total variations. In particular in
[
14
]
it is shown that, if
f
and
g
are continuous functions such that
f
has finite
p
-variation and
g
has finite
q
-variation, with
p,q>0
and
p^{-1}+q^{-1}>1
, then the Stieltjes integral
\int fdg
is well-defined as a limit of Riemann sums.
This was the starting point of the crucial extension to rough paths integration. Indeed, in
[
12
]
the author proves that it is possible to define the integral
\int fdx
also in the case when
f
has finite
p
-variation and
x
has finite
q
-variation with
p,q>0
and
p^{-1}+q^{-1}<1
. In this case, additional information on the function
x
is needed, which would play the role of iterated integrals for regular paths.

An alternative formulation of the integration over rough paths is provided in
[
5
]
, where the author considers Hölder-like (semi)norms instead of
p
-variation norms. Namely, if
f
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous and
g
is
\eta
-Hölder continuous with
\alpha+\beta>1
then the Young integral is well defined as the unique solution to an algebraic problem. Recently, a more general theory of rough integration, when
\alpha+\beta\leq 1
, has been introduced in
[
4
]
.

Here, we consider only Young integrals and focus on the spatial regularity of solutions to infinite dimensional evolution equations leaving aside the enormous amount of results connected to the rough paths case culminating in the breakthrough on singular SPDEs (see, e.g.,
[
6
]
).
Namely, we consider the non-linear evolution equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}dy(t)=Ay(t)dt+\sigma(y(t))dx(t),&t\in(0,T],\\[2.84526pt]
y(0)=\psi,\end{array}\right.

where
A
is the infinitesimal generator of a semigroup with suitable regularizing properties and
x
is a
\eta
-Hölder continuous function with
\eta>1/2
.
Ordinary differential equations (in finite dimensional spaces) driven by
an irregular path of Hölder regularity greater than 1/2 have been understood in full details since
[
15
]
(see also
[
9
]
). On the other hand, the infinite dimensional case was treated in
[
7
]
and then developed in
[
8
]
and
[
3
]
, see also
[
13
]
for earlier results in the context of
stochastic partial differential equations driven by an infinite dimensional
fractional Brownian motion of Hurst parameter
H>1/2
.

In
[
3
]
, problem (
1
) is formulated in a mild form

\displaystyle y(t)=S(t)y(0)+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-r)(\sigma(y(r)))dx(r),\qquad\;\,t\in[0,T],

where
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
is the analytic semigroup generated by the sectorial operator
A
, and the authors exploit the regularizing properties of
S
to show that, if the initial datum
\psi
is smooth enough (i.e., if it belongs to a suitable domain of the fractional powers
(-A)^{\alpha}
), then equation (
1
) admits a unique mild solution with the same spatial regularity as the initial datum. The key technical point in
[
3
]
is to prove that the convolution

\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)f(s)dx(s)

(1.3)

is well defined if
f
takes values in
D((-A)^{\alpha})
and belongs to a Hölder-type function space. To be more precise, the authors require that
f:[0,T]\rightarrow D((-A)^{\alpha})
satisfies the condition

\displaystyle\sup_{s<t,s,t\in[0,T]}\frac{|f(t)-f(s)-(S(t-s)-I)f(s)|_{D((-A)^{\alpha})}}{(t-s)^{\beta}}<+\infty.

This is one of the main difference with respect to the finite dimensional case, where the condition on the function
f
reads in terms of classical Hölder norms.
Once that convolution (
1.3
) is well-defined, the smoothness of the initial datum
\psi
and suitable estimates on (
1.3
) allow the authors to solve the mild reformulation of equation (
1
) by a fixed point argument in the same Hölder-type function space introduced above.

Our point in the present paper is that if one looks a bit more closely to the trade-off between Hölderianity in time and regularity in space of the convolution (
1.3
) one discovers that an extra regularity in space can be extracted by estimates, see Lemma
2.16
. This allows us to show that the mild solution to equation (
1
), which in our situation is driven by a finite dimensional noise, is more regular than the initial datum (that nevertheless has to enjoy the same regularity assumptions as in
[
3
]
). Namely, it is indeed a classical solution, i.e.,
y(t)\in D(A)
for any
t\in(0,T]
(see Theorem
3.2
), and, as a byproduct, we prove that
y
is also an integral solution to (
1
), i.e., it satisfies the equation

\displaystyle y(t)=\psi+\int_{0}^{t}Ay(s)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(y(s))dx(s),\qquad\;\,t\in[0,T].

It is worth mentioning that, when
A
is an unbounded operator, mild formulation of equation (
1
) is the most suitable to prove existence and uniqueness of a solution since it allows to apply a fixed point argument in spaces of functions with a low degree of smoothness. On the other hand, this formulation is too weak in several applications (for instance Section 6 here), where an integral formulation of the solution helps a lot. Moreover, since our solution is classical, we can also obtain a chain rule; in other words we show that we can differentiate with respect to time regular enough functions of the solution to equation (
1
). Finally, the chain rule turns out to be the right tool to obtain necessary conditions for invariance of half spaces with respect to (
1
) (see
[
2
]
for general invariance results in the case of a finite dimensional equation driven by a rough path and
[
1
]
for the state of the art in the case of classical evolution equations in infinite dimensional spaces).

Summarizing, this paper can been described as a first step towards a systematic study, by the classical tools of semigroup theory, of smoothing properties of the mild solution to (
1
) (as they were introduced in
[
3
]
). We plan to go further in the analysis, first weakening the smoothness assumptions on
\psi
and, then, developing results analogous to those in this paper for equations driven by more irregular noises as in the case of rough paths.

The paper is structured as follows.
In Section
2
, we introduce the function spaces that we use in this paper and we recall some results taken from
[
5
,
8
]
, slightly generalizing some of those results.
In Section
3
, we prove the existence and uniqueness of a mild solution to the non-linear Young equation (
1
) when
\psi\in X_{\alpha}
and
x
is
\eta
-Hölder continuous for some
\eta\in(1/2,1)
and
\alpha+\eta>1
. We show that this solution is classical and estimate the blow-up rate of its
X_{1+\mu}
-norm as
t
tends to
0^{+}
, when
\mu\in[0,\eta+\alpha-1)
. Based on this result, in Section
4
we
prove that the mild solution to (
1
) can be written in an integral form, which is used in Section
5
to prove the chain rule. As a byproduct of the previous results, in Section
6
we prove a necessary condition for the invariance of the halfplane. Finally, in Section
7
we provide an example to illustrate our results.

it holds that

\displaystyle F(t,y(t))-F(s,y(s))=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}F_{t}(u,y(u))du+\int_{s}^{t}\langle F_{x}(u,y(u)),Ay(u)\rangle du

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{s}^{t}\langle F_{x}(u,y(u)),\sigma_{i}(y(u))\rangle dx_{i}(u)

(5.7)

for every
(s,t)\in[0,T]
.

6.
Invariance of convex sets

Let
X
be a Hilbert space with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
,
A:D(A)\subset X\rightarrow X
be a self-adjoint nonpositive closed operator which generates an analytic semigroup of bounded linear operators
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
H
and suppose that the results of the previous section hold true with
X_{\zeta}=D((-A)^{\zeta})
for any
\zeta\geq 0
.

We say that a closed convex set
K\subset X
is invariant for the mild solution
y
to (
3.5
), if for any
\psi\in X_{\alpha}\cap K
, then
y(t)
belongs to
K
for any
t\in[0,T]
. Formula (
5.2
) implies the following result.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let Hypotheses
2.1
,
2.7
,
3.1
be fulfilled with
\eta+\alpha>1
. Let
\varphi\in X_{\varepsilon}
for some
\varepsilon\in[0,1)
,
\psi\in X_{\zeta}
for some
\zeta\in[\alpha,1)
and let
K:=\{x\in X:\langle x,\varphi\rangle\leq 0\}
be invariant for
y
. Then:

(i)

If
\psi\in\partial K
and
\eta\leq\zeta+\varepsilon\leq 1
, then

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}t^{-\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle\varphi,\sigma_{i}(\psi)\rangle(x_{i}(t)-x_{i}(0))\leq 0,

for
\beta\in[\eta,\zeta+\varepsilon)
and

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda>0}\limsup_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}t^{-\beta}\displaystyle\bigg(

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(\lambda+\langle\varphi,y(s)\rangle)_{+}}{\lambda}\langle(-A)^{\varepsilon}\varphi,(-A)^{1-\varepsilon}y(s)\rangle ds

\displaystyle\;+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle\varphi,\sigma_{i}(\psi)\rangle(x_{i}(t)-x_{i}(0))\bigg)\leq 0,

for
\beta\in[\zeta+\varepsilon,1]
.

(ii)

If
y(t_{0})\in\partial K
for some
t_{0}\in[0,T)
and
\zeta+\varepsilon>1
, then

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow t_{0}^{+}}|t-t_{0}|^{-\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle\varphi,\sigma_{i}(y(t_{0}))\rangle(x_{i}(t)-x_{i}(t_{0}))\leq 0,

if
\beta\in[\eta,1)
and

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow t_{0}^{+}}|t-t_{0}|^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle\varphi,\sigma_{i}(y(t_{0}))\rangle(x_{i}(t)-x_{i}(t_{0}))-\langle(-A)^{\varepsilon}\varphi,(-A)^{1-\varepsilon}y(t_{0})\rangle\leq 0,

if
\beta=1
.

Remark 6.2
.

If
t_{0}>0
in
(ii)
then
y(t)\in X_{1+\mu}
for any
\mu\in[0,\eta+\alpha-1)
and
t\in(0,T]
(see Theorem
3.2
). Hence, the condition
\zeta+\varepsilon>1
is automatically satisfied.

Proof of Proposition
6.1
.

For any
\lambda>0
we introduce the function
F_{\lambda}:X\to X
, defined by
F_{\lambda}(x):=(\lambda+\langle\varphi,x\rangle)^{2}_{+}
, for any
x\in X
. As it is easily seen, each function

belongs to
C^{1,1}(X)
and
DF_{\lambda}(x)=2(\lambda+\langle\varphi,x\rangle)_{+}\varphi
for any

. Further, for any
x\in K
it holds that
F_{\lambda}(x)\leq\lambda^{2}
, and
F_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{2}
if and only if
x\in\partial K
. For any
t_{0}\in[0,T)
, if
y(t_{0})\in\partial K
then from (

) it follows that

\displaystyle 0\geq

\displaystyle F_{\lambda}(y(t))-F_{\lambda}(y(t_{0}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{t_{0}}^{t}(\lambda+\langle\varphi,y(s)\rangle)_{+}\langle\varphi,Ay(s)\rangle ds+2\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}(\lambda+\langle\varphi,y(s)\rangle)_{+}\langle\varphi,\sigma_{i}(y(s))\rangle dx_{i}(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{t_{0}}^{t}(\lambda+\langle\varphi,y(s)\rangle)_{+}\langle(-A)^{\varepsilon}\varphi,(-A)^{1-\varepsilon}y(s)\rangle ds

\displaystyle+2\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda\langle\varphi,\sigma_{i}(y(t_{0}))\rangle(x_{i}(t)-x_{i}(t_{0}))+{\mathscr{R}}_{f}(t_{0},t),

(6.1)

for any
t\in[t_{0},T]
and any
\lambda>0
, where in the last equality we have used (
2.4
) with
f(t)=(\lambda+\langle\varphi,y(t)\rangle)_{+}\langle\varphi,\sigma_{i}(y(t))\rangle
for
t\in[0,T]
. We recall that from (
2.5
) it follows that
{\mathscr{R}}_{f}(t_{0},t)=o(|t-t_{0}|)
as

, and from Remark
3.4
(ii)
we have
y\in C((0,T];X_{\mu})
for any
\mu\in[0,\eta+\alpha)
.

Now, we separately consider the cases
(i)
and
(ii)
.

{\bf(i)}
. Fix
\psi\in\partial K\cap X_{\zeta}
with
\eta\leq\zeta+\varepsilon\leq 1
. From (

), with

we get

\displaystyle 0\geq

\displaystyle-2\int_{0}^{t}(\lambda+\langle\varphi,y(s)\rangle)_{+}\langle(-A)^{\varepsilon}\varphi,(-A)^{1-\varepsilon}y(s)\rangle ds

\displaystyle+2\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda\langle\varphi,\sigma_{i}(\psi)\rangle(x_{i}(t)-x_{i}(0))+{\mathscr{R}}_{f}(0,t),

(6.2)

for any

. From (

) with

\displaystyle\left|(\lambda+\langle\varphi,y(s)\rangle)_{+}\langle(-A)^{\varepsilon}\varphi,(-A)^{1-\varepsilon}y(s)\rangle\right|\leq\lambda cs^{\zeta+\varepsilon-1},\quad s\in(0,T].

(6.3)

Let
\beta\in[\eta,1]

and by

{\bf(ii)}

.

. Dividing (

Remark 6.3
.

In
[
2
]
, the problem of the invariance of a general convex set
K
is treated for a ordinary differential equation driven by a rough path. In such a paper, the state space is finite dimensional and no unbounded operators are involved in the equation but both the noise, which is
\theta
-Hölder with
\theta>1/3
, and the set
K
are much more general than here. In the present paper, we just wanted to show how the availability of both a classical solution and a chain rule can be exploited to tackle the problem of the invariance of convex sets, when an unbounded operator
A
is involved.
As a matter of fact, the general problem of the invariance of a convex set
K
with respect to a general infinite dimensional evolution equation driven by a rough trajectory is still unexplored (see
[
1
]
and the references therein for corresponding results in the case of classical evolution equations). It is also worth mentioning that, in
[
2
]
, necessary conditions for the invariance are proved under an assumption specifying the genuine rough behaviour of the path (i.e., requiring that
|x(t)-x(0)|\sim t^{\theta}
as
t
tends to
0
).

7.
An example

In this section, we provide an example to which our results apply.

Let
A
be the realization in
X=C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of the second-order elliptic operator

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}q_{ij}D_{ij}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}b_{j}D_{j}+c,

with coefficients bounded and
\beta
-Hölder continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for some
\beta\in(0,1/2)
, and
\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}q_{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\geq\mu|\xi|^{2}

for every
x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and some positive constant
\mu
. Note that
D(A)=\Big\{u\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap\bigcap_{p<+\infty}W^{2,p}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):{\mathcal{A}}u\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\Big\}
.
For every
\alpha\in(0,2)\setminus\{1/2,1\}
, we take
X_{\alpha}=C^{2\alpha}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
endowed with the classical norm of
C^{2\alpha}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Moreover, we take as
X_{1/2}
the Zygmund space of all bounded functions
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
[g]=\displaystyle\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|g(x)+g(y)-2g(2^{-1}(x+y))|}{|x-y|}<+\infty
endowed with the norm
\|g\|_{X_{1/2}}=\|g\|_{\infty}+[g]
.
It is well known that
A
generates an analytic semigroup on
C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
X_{\alpha}
is the interpolation space of order
\alpha
between
X
and
X_{1}=D(A)
. We refer the reader to e.g.,
[
10
, Chapters 3 and 14]
.
Finally, we fix a function
\hat{\sigma}\in C^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
and note that the function
\sigma:X\to X
, defined by
\sigma(f)=\hat{\sigma}\circ f
satisfies Hypothesis
3.1
is satisfied for every
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
, with

L_{\sigma}^{\alpha}=\|\sigma\|_{{\rm Lip}(\mathbb{R})}
. Since the assumptions of Theorem
3.2
are satisfied, we conclude that, for every
\psi\in C^{\alpha}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(
\alpha\in(0,1)
), there exists a unique classical solution
y
to Problem (
3
).

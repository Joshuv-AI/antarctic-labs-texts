Abstract
. Let p p be a prime, and 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} the field with p p elements. We prove that if G G is a mild pro- p p group with quadratic 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} -cohomology algebra H ∙ ​ ( G , 𝔽 p ) H^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}) , then the algebras H ∙ ​ ( G , 𝔽 p ) H^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}) and gr ​ 𝔽 p ​ [ [ G ] ] \mathrm{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!] — the latter being induced by the quotients of consecutive terms of the p p -Zassenhaus filtration of G G — are both Koszul, and they are quadratically dual to each other. Consequently, if the maximal pro- p p Galois group of a field is mild, then Positselski’s and Weigel’s Koszulity conjectures hold true for such a field.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Bbbk
be a field, and let
A_{\bullet}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}A_{n}
be a
\Bbbk
-algebra endowed with a
\mathbb{N}
-grading.
The algebra
A_{\bullet}
is called a
quadratic algebra
if it is generated by
A_{1}

(namely, every element is a sum of products of elements of
A_{1}
),
and its relations are generated by homogeneous relations of degree 2.
A quadratic algebra
A_{\bullet}
comes endowed with a
quadratic dual algebra

A_{\bullet}^{!}
, defined
as the quadratic algebra whose relations are orthogonal to the relations of
A_{\bullet}
(see Definition
2.1
below,
and
[
28
, § 1.2]
).
For example, symmetric algebras and exterior algebras are quadratic, and they are quadratically dual to each other.

A peculiar class of algebras stands out among quadratic algebras: the class of
Koszul algebras
, introduced by S. Priddy in
[
33
]
.
A quadratic algebra
A_{\bullet}
is Koszul if the minimal graded free
A_{\bullet}
-resolution of
\Bbbk
has only linear maps — see §
2.2
below.
In spite of the kinky definition, Koszul algebras have an exceptionally nice behavior — e.g., the
\Bbbk
-cohomology
algebra of a Koszul algebra
A_{\bullet}
is its quadratic dual
A_{\bullet}^{!}
.
Koszulity is a very restrictive property, still it arises in various areas of mathematics, such as representation
theory, algebraic geometry, combinatorics, noncommutative geometry and topology (see, e.g.,
[
17
]
and
[
28
, Introduction]
).
Hence, Koszul algebras have become an important object of study.

Quadratic algebras and Koszul algebras play a prominent role in Galois theory, too.
Given a field
\mathbb{K}
, for
p
a prime number let
G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
denote the
maximal pro-
p
Galois group
of
\mathbb{K}
.
I.e.,
G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
is the maximal pro-
p
quotient of the absolute Galois group of
\mathbb{K}
.
If
\mathbb{K}
contains a root of 1 of order
p
(and also
\sqrt{-1}
if
p=2
), then the celebrated
Bloch-Kato Conjecture
— which was given an affirmative answer by M. Rost and V. Voevodsky, with a “patch” by Ch. Weibel, cf.
[
39
,
14
,
45
,
46
,
44
]
— implies that the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology algebra

H^{\bullet}(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p),\mathbb{F}_{p})=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}H^{n}(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p),\mathbb{F}_{p})

of the maximal pro-
p
Galois group of
\mathbb{K}
, endowed with the graded-commutative
cup product

H^{s}(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p),\mathbb{F}_{p})\times H^{t}(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p),\mathbb{F}_{p})\overset{\cup}{\longrightarrow}H^{s+t}(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p),\mathbb{F}_{p}),\qquad s,t\geq 0,

is a quadratic
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra.
This result was a great achievement in Galois theory, as it allowed the discovery of new insights on the structure
of maximal pro-
p
Galois groups of fields, whose study is crucial in current research in Galois theory (see, e.g.,
[
4
,
36
,
5
,
35
]
).

Given the peculiarity of Koszul algebras among quadratic algebras, the study of Koszul algebras in the context of Galois cohomology has gained considerable importance, in particular after the works by L. Positselski and A. Vishik (cf.
[
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
).
Their work propose an approach to the Bloch-Kato Conjecture which is alternative to Rost-Voevodsky’s proof of the Norm Residue Theorem (see
[
30
, § 1]
).
In particular, Positselski formulated the following conjecture (cf.
[
31
]
*§ 0.1).

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field containing a root of 1 of order
p
.
Then the algebra
H^{\bullet}(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p),\mathbb{F}_{p})
is Koszul.

Since the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology algebra of the maximal pro-
p
Galois group
G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
of a field
\mathbb{K}
containing a root of 1 of order
p
is quadratic, a further natural question one may formulate is: “what is the quadratic dual of
H^{\bullet}(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p),\mathbb{F}_{p})
like?”
The conjectural description of such quadratic algebra is related to the successive factors of the
p
-Zassenhaus filtration
of
G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
(see Definition
3.1
below).
For a pro-
p
group
G
, let
\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{L}(G)}
denote the restricted universal enveloping algebra of the restricted
Lie algebra
\mathfrak{L}(G)
induced by the
p
-Zassenhaus filtration of
G
.
One has the following conjectures, formulated respectively by the authors in
[
25
]
, and by Th. Weigel in
[
48
]
.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field containing a root of 1 of order
p
.

(i)

The algebra
\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{L}(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p))}
is quadratic, and it is isomorphic to the quadratic dual of
H^{\bullet}(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p),\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

(ii)

The algebra
\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{L}(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p))}
is Koszul.

Note that, by a result of S. Jennings, for a finitely generated pro-
p
group
G
, the algebra
\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{L}(G)}
is
isomorphic to the graded algebra
\mathrm{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
associated to the filtration on the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-group algebra of
G

by successive powers of the augmentation ideal (see Proposition
3.4
).

In
[
31
]
it is shown that Conjecture
1.1
holds true if
\mathbb{K}
is a local field or a global field.
Furthermore, in
[
25
]
and in
[
37
]
, Conjectures
1.1
and
1.2
receive positive answers
in several relevant cases: for example, in case
G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
is a
Demushkin group
(cf.
[
25
]
*Thm. C); or if
G
is finitely generated and it has a single
defining relation
, and
H^{\bullet}(G_{\mathbb{K}}(p),\mathbb{F}_{p})
is quadratic (cf.
[
37
]
*Cor. 1.3).

In this paper, we turn our focus on
mild pro-
p
groups
.
Mild pro-
p
groups — defined by J. Labute in
[
21
]
— are finitely generated pro-
p
groups which admit a presentation in terms of generators and defining relations, where the relations satisfy a condition of being free in a maximal possible way with respect to suitably chosen filtrations, such as the
p
-Zassenhaus filtration.
Mild pro-
p
groups enjoy very nice properties: for example, a mild pro-
p
group
G
has cohomological dimension
2
— i.e.,
H^{n}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})=0
for
n\geq 3
—, and the associated algebra
\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{L}(G)}
may be computed easily.
For this reasons, from a group-theoretic point of view mild pro-
p
groups may be seen as a generalization of Demushkin groups
and one-relator pro-
p
groups.

As underlined in
[
21
, § 1.2]
, mild pro-
p
groups arise as Galois groups
of extensions of number fields with restricted ramification (see also, e.g.,
[
40
,
23
]
) — which are very important objects in algebraic number theory, and before Labute’s work very little was known about the structure of these Galois pro-
p
groups.
Most remarkably, mild pro-
p
groups provide examples of Galois pro-
p
groups of number fields with cohomological dimension 2, a property which is very interesting as it yields a lot of arithmetic consequences — see, e.g.,
[
19
,
16
,
41
,
11
]
.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be a mild pro-
p
group.
If the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology algebra
H^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
is quadratic, then the algebras
H^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})

and
\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{L}(G)}
are Koszul, and moreover one has an isomorphism of quadratic algebras

H^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})^{!}\cong\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{L}(G)}.

As a consequence, Conjectures
1.1
and
1.2
have positive answers
in the case the maximal pro-
p
Galois group
G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
of a field
\mathbb{K}
containing a root of 1 of order
p
is mild.
Moreover, Theorem
1.3
provides a big wealth of examples of finitely generated pro-
p
groups
G

such that the algebras
H^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
and
\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{L}(G)}
are Koszul, and quadratically dual to each other
(see §
5
).
In addition, we prove the following cohomological characterization of mild pro-
p
groups with quadratic cohomology.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated pro-
p
group of cohomological dimension
2
such that the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology algebra
H^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
is Koszul.
Then
G
is a mild pro-
p
group.

This raises the following natural Galois-theoretic question.

Question 1.5
.

Let
p
be a prime, and let
\mathbb{K}
be a field such that:

(i)

\mathbb{K}
contains a root of 1 of order
p
, and also
\sqrt{-1}
if
p=2
;

(ii)

the maximal pro-
p
Galois group
G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
is a finitely generated pro-
p
group;

(iii)

the cohomological dimension of
G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
is 2.

Is
G_{\mathbb{K}}(p)
a mild pro-
p
group?

In order to decide when a given pro-
p
group is mild, there are some criteria which provide sufficient conditions for mildness: for example, Anick’s criterion (see
[
1
]
and
[
21
, Prop. 3.7]
), Labute’s non-singular circuits criterion (see
[
21
, Thm. 1.6]
), and Schmidt’s cup-product criterion (see
[
42
, Thm. 1.1]
and §
5.3
).
Similarly, there are some methods to prove Koszulity of a quadratic algebra (see, e.g.,
[
22
, Ch. 4]
).
We establish the following new criterion to decide when a given quadratic algebra is Koszul, which is inspired by the aforementioned cup-product criterion for mildness.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
A_{\bullet}
be a quadratic algebra over an arbitrary field
\Bbbk
, and suppose that the component
A_{1}
admits a decomposition as direct sum of vector spaces
A_{1}=U\oplus W
such that:

(i)

U\cdot W:=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{\>u\cdot w\>\mid\>u\in U,\>w\in W\>\}=A_{2}
;

(ii)

U\cdot U:=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{F}}\{\>u\cdot u^{\prime}\>\mid\>u,u^{\prime}\in U\>\}=0
.

Then the algebra
A_{\bullet}
is Koszul.

2.
Quadratic algebras and Koszul algebras

Throughout the paper every graded algebra
A_{\bullet}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{n}
is assumed to be
unitary associative over a field
\Bbbk
, non-negatively graded, connected, and of finite-type.
I.e.,
A_{0}=\Bbbk
,
A_{n}=0
for
n<0
and
\dim(A_{n})<\infty
for
n\geq 1
.
From §
3
onward, we will assume
\Bbbk=\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

For a complete account on graded algebras and their cohomology, we direct the reader to
[
28
,
22
,
17
]
, and to
[
25
]
*§ 2.

2.1.
Quadratic algebras and quadratic duals

For a vector space
V
of finite dimension, let
\mathbf{T}_{\bullet}(V)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}V^{\otimes n}
denote
the graded tensor algebra generated by
V
(where
V^{\otimes 0}=\Bbbk
), endowed with the multiplication and the grading
induced by the tensor product.
Also, let
V^{*}=\Hom_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(V,\Bbbk)
denote the dual space of
V
.
Since
\dim(V)<\infty
, we will identify
(V\otimes V)^{*}=V^{*}\otimes V^{*}
.

Definition 2.1
.

(i)

A graded algebra
A_{\bullet}
is said to be
quadratic
if

A_{\bullet}\cong\mathbf{T}_{\bullet}(A_{1})/(\Omega)

for some vector subspace
\Omega\subseteq V\otimes V
, with
(\Omega)\trianglelefteq\mathbf{T}_{\bullet}(A_{1})

the two-sided ideal generated by
\Omega
.
We write
A_{\bullet}=\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}(V,\Omega)
, with
V=A_{1}
.

(ii)

For a quadratic algebra
A_{\bullet}=\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}(V,\Omega)
, let
\Omega^{\perp}\subseteq V^{*}\otimes V^{*}
denote the orthogonal space
of
\Omega
, i.e., identifying
(V\otimes V)^{*}=V^{*}\otimes V^{*}
,

\Omega^{\perp}=\left\{\>\alpha\in(V\otimes V)^{*}\>\mid\>\alpha(w)=0\text{ for all }w\in\Omega\>\right\}.

The
quadratic dual
of
A_{\bullet}
, denoted by
A_{\bullet}^{!}
, is the algebra

\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}(V^{*},\Omega^{\perp})
.

Remark 2.2
.

For a quadratic algebra
A_{\bullet}
one has
(A_{\bullet}^{!})^{!}=A_{\bullet}
(cf.
[
22
]
*§ 3.2.4).

Let
\Bbbk\langle X\rangle
denote the algebra of non-commutative polynomials with coefficients in
\Bbbk
on the set of indeterminates
X=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}\}
.
Then
\Bbbk\langle X\rangle
is a graded algebra, with the grading induced by polynomial degree.
The identification of
X
with a fixed basis of a vector space
V
of dimension
d
yields an isomorphism
of graded algebras
\Bbbk\langle X\rangle\cong\mathbf{T}_{\bullet}(V)
.
Thus, one may identify a quadratic algebra with the quotient of
\Bbbk\langle X\rangle
by an ideal generated by homogeneous (non-commutative) polynomials of degree 2.

Example 2.3
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional vector space.

(a)

The tensor algebra
\mathbf{T}_{\bullet}(V)=\mathbf{T}_{\bullet}(V)/(0)
and the
trivial quadratic algebra

\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}(V,V^{\otimes 2})=\mathbf{T}_{\bullet}(V)/(V^{\otimes 2})

are quadratic, and
\mathbf{T}_{\bullet}(V)^{!}\cong\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}(V,V^{\otimes 2})
.

(b)

The symmetric algebra
\mathbf{S}_{\bullet}(V)
and the exterior algebra
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{\bullet}(V)
are quadratic, as

\begin{split}\mathbf{S}_{\bullet}(V)&=\frac{\mathbf{T}_{\bullet}(V)}{(\>v\otimes w-w\otimes v\>\mid\>v,w\in V\>)},\\
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{\bullet}(V)&=\frac{\mathbf{T}_{\bullet}(V)}{(\>v\otimes v\>\mid\>v\in V\>)};\end{split}

moreover,
\mathbf{S}_{\bullet}(V)^{!}\cong\mathbf{\Lambda}_{\bullet}(V)
.

One may combine two quadratic algebras
A_{\bullet}=\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}(A_{1},\Omega_{A})
and
B_{\bullet}=\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}(B_{1},\Omega_{B})
as follows:

(a)

the
direct sum
of
A_{\bullet}
and
B_{\bullet}
is the quadratic algebra

A_{\bullet}\oplus B_{\bullet}=\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}(A_{1}\oplus B_{1},\Omega_{A}\cup\Omega_{B}\cup A_{1}\otimes B_{1}\cup B_{1}\otimes A_{1});

(b)

the
free product
of
A_{\bullet}
and
B_{\bullet}
is the quadratic algebra

A_{\bullet}\sqcup B_{\bullet}=\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}(A_{1}\oplus B_{1},\Omega_{A}\cup\Omega_{B});

(c)

the
symmetric tensor product
of
A_{\bullet}
and
B_{\bullet}
is the quadratic algebra

A_{\bullet}\otimes B_{\bullet}=\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}(A_{1}\oplus B_{1},\Omega_{A}\cup\Omega_{B}\cup\Omega_{\mathrm{S}}),

where
\Omega_{\mathrm{S}}=\langle a\otimes b-b\otimes a,a\in A_{1},b\in B_{1}\rangle
.

(d)

the
wedge product
of
A_{\bullet}
and
B_{\bullet}
is the quadratic algebra

A_{\bullet}\wedge B_{\bullet}=\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}(A_{1}\oplus B_{1},\Omega_{A}\cup\Omega_{B}\cup\Omega_{\Lambda}),

where
\Omega_{\Lambda}=\langle a\otimes b+b\otimes a,a\in A_{1},b\in B_{1}\rangle
.

One has the isomorphisms of quadratic algebras

(2.1)

\begin{split}&A_{\bullet}^{!}\oplus B_{\bullet}^{!}\cong(A_{\bullet}\sqcup B_{\bullet})^{!}\qquad\text{and}\qquad A_{\bullet}^{!}\sqcup B_{\bullet}^{!}\cong(A_{\bullet}\oplus B_{\bullet})^{!},\\
&A_{\bullet}^{!}\otimes B_{\bullet}^{!}\cong(A_{\bullet}\wedge B_{\bullet})^{!}\qquad\text{and}\qquad A_{\bullet}^{!}\wedge B_{\bullet}^{!}\cong(A_{\bullet}\otimes B_{\bullet})^{!}\end{split}

(cf.
[
28
]
*§ 3.1).

Example 2.4
.

Let
\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
be a finite simplicial graph, with vertices

\mathscr{V}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}\}
and edges
\mathscr{E}
(for the notion of graph we refer to
[
6
]
*§ 1.1), and let
V
be the vector space (over an arbitrary field) with basis
\mathscr{V}
.
The
right-angled Artin algebra

\mathbf{A}_{\bullet}(\Gamma)
and the
exterior Stanley-Reisner algebra

\mathbf{\Lambda}_{\bullet}(\Gamma)

associated to
\Gamma
are the quadratic algebras

\begin{split}\mathbf{A}_{\bullet}(\Gamma)&=\frac{\mathbf{T}_{\bullet}(V)}{(\>v\otimes w-w\otimes v\>\mid\>\{v,w\}\in\mathscr{E}\>)},\\
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{\bullet}(\Gamma)&=\frac{\mathbf{\Lambda}_{\bullet}(V)}{(\>v\otimes w\>\mid\>\{v,w\}\notin\mathscr{E}\>)}.\end{split}

Then
\mathbf{A}_{\bullet}(\Gamma)^{!}\cong\mathbf{\Lambda}_{\bullet}(\Gamma)
(cf.
[
47
]
*§ 4.2.2).
In particular,

(a)

if
\Gamma
is complete (i.e.,
\mathscr{E}=\{\{v,w\}\mid v,w\in\mathscr{V}{,v\neq w}\}

\mathbf{A}_{\bullet}(\Gamma)\cong\mathbf{S}_{\bullet}(V)
and

\mathbf{\Lambda}_{\bullet}(\Gamma)\cong\mathbf{\Lambda}_{\bullet}(V)
;

(b)

if
\mathscr{E}=\varnothing
, then
\mathbf{A}_{\bullet}(\Gamma)\cong\mathbf{T}_{\bullet}(V)

and
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{\bullet}(\Gamma)\cong\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}(V,V^{\otimes 2})

2.2.
Koszul algebras

A quadratic algebra
A_{\bullet}
is said to be
Koszul
if the trivial module
\Bbbk
admits a free resolution

\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{P(2)_{\bullet}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{P(1)_{\bullet}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{P(0)_{\bullet}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbb{F}_{p}}

of graded right
A_{\bullet}
-modules
P(i)_{\bullet}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}P(i)_{n}
, such that, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
P(n)_{n}
is finite-dimensional over
\Bbbk
and generates
P(n)_{\bullet}
as graded
A_{\bullet}
-module (cf.
[
25
]
*§ 2.2).

Koszul algebras have an exceptionally nice behavior in terms of cohomology: indeed, a quadratic algebra
A_{\bullet}
is Koszul if, and only if, the Ext-groups
\mathrm{Ext}_{A_{\bullet}}^{i,j}(\Bbbk,\Bbbk)
are trivial for
i\neq j
(cf.
[
28
]
*Ch. 2, Def. 1).
Moreover, Koszul algebras have the following fundamental property (cf.
[
28
]
*Ch. 2, Def. 1 and Cor. 3.3), which is essentially due to S. Priddy
[
33
]
*§2. (See also

[
2
]
*§2 for a very nice exposition of some classical results.)

Proposition 2.5
.

A quadratic algebra
A_{\bullet}
is Koszul if, and only if, the quadratic dual
A_{\bullet}^{!}
is Koszul.
Moreover, if
A_{\bullet}
is Koszul, then the isomorphism of vector spaces
\mathrm{Ext}_{A_{\bullet}}^{1,1}(\Bbbk,\Bbbk)\cong A_{1}^{*}
yields an isomorphism of quadratic algebras

\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\mathrm{Ext}_{A_{\bullet}}^{i,i}(\Bbbk,\Bbbk)\cong A_{\bullet}^{!}.

Example 2.8
.

Put
\Bbbk=\mathbb{F}_{p}
. For
d\geq 1

X=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}\}
and

(a)

If

be the homogeneous polynomial of degree 2

(b)

]
*§ 4.1).

Moreover, Koszulity is preserved by taking direct sums and free products (cf.
[
28
]
*Ch. 3, Cor. 1.2).

Proposition 2.10
.

If
A_{\bullet}
and
B_{\bullet}
are two Koszul algebras, then also the direct sum
A_{\bullet}\oplus B_{\bullet}
and the free product
A_{\bullet}\sqcup B_{\bullet}
are Koszul.

For a graded algebra
A_{\bullet}
, the
Hilbert series

h_{A_{\bullet}}(z)
is the formal power series

h_{A_{\bullet}}(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\dim(A_{n})z^{n}.

There is a rich interplay between Hilbert series and Koszul property: the following two facts are of key importance for the main results of this paper (cf.
[
28
]
*Ch. 2, Cor. 2.2, and Prop. 2.3).

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
A_{\bullet}={\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}(V,\Omega)}
be a quadratic algebra with
d=\dim(V)
generators and
r=\dim(\Omega)
defining relations.

(i)

If
h_{A_{\bullet}}(z)=(1-dz+rz^{2})^{-1}
, then
A_{\bullet}
is Koszul.

(ii)

If
A_{\bullet}
is Koszul, then one has an equality of formal power series

h_{A_{\bullet}}(z)\cdot h_{A_{\bullet}^{!}}(-z)=1.

3.
Pro-
p
groups and graded algebras

Henceforth, we focus on finitely generated pro-
p
groups.
Thus, every subgroup is tacitly assumed to be closed, and generators are to be considered in the topological sense.
In particular, for a pro-
p
group
G
and
n\geq 1
,
G^{n}
denotes the closed subgroup of
G
generated by
n
th powers,
and, for
H_{1},H_{2}
two subgroups of
G
,
[H_{1},H_{2}]
denotes the closed subgroup of
G
generated by the commutators

g_{1}^{-1}\cdot g_{1}^{g_{2}}=g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}g_{2},\qquad g_{1}\in H_{1},g_{2}\in H_{2}.

3.1.
The Zassenhaus filtration

Definition 3.1
.

The
p
-Zassenhaus filtration

(G_{(n)})_{n\geq 1}
of a pro-
p
group
G
is
defined by

(3.1)

\begin{split}G_{(1)}&=G\\
G_{(n)}&=G_{(\lceil n/p\rceil)}^{p}\cdot\prod_{h+k=n}[G_{(h)},G_{(k)}],\qquad n\geq 2,\end{split}

where
\lceil n/p\rceil
is the least integer
l
such that
lp\geq n
.

The
p
-Zassenhaus filtration of
G
is the fastest descending series starting at
G
satisfying

(3.2)

[G_{(h)},G_{(k)}]\subseteq G_{(h+k)}\quad\text{and}\quad G_{(n)}^{p}\subseteq G_{(np)}

for every
h,k,n\geq 1
.
Moreover, every quotient
G_{(n)}/G_{(n+1)}
is a
p
-elementary abelian group, and thus a vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
In particular, one has
G_{(2)}=G^{p}[G,G]
, and

G_{(3)}=\begin{cases}G^{p}[G,[G,G]]&\text{if }p\neq 2\\
G^{4}[G,G]^{2}[G,[G,G]]&\text{if }p=2.\end{cases}

We set
\mathfrak{L}(G)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}G_{(n)}/G_{(n+1)}
.

Let
S
be a free pro-
p
group with basis
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\}
.
Then one may define the
p
-Zassenhaus filtration of
S
as follows.
Let
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![S]\!]
denote the complete group algebra of
S
— namely,
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![S]\!]=\varprojlim_{N}\mathbb{F}_{p}[S/N]
,
where
N
runs through the open normal subgroups of
S
.
The assignment
x\mapsto x-1
induces an embedding of sets
S\hookrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![S]\!]
.

Given a set of indeterminates
X=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}\}
, let
\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle\!\langle X\rangle\!\rangle
denote the algebra of formal power series in
X
,
and let
I(X)
be the augmentation ideal of
\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle\!\langle X\rangle\!\rangle
, that is, the two-sided ideal
(X_{1},\ldots,X_{d})
.
Then there is an isomorphism of topological algebras

(3.3)

\phi\colon\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![S]\!]\to\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle\!\langle X\rangle\!\rangle\qquad x_{i}\mapsto 1+X_{i}.

The composition of the embedding
S\hookrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![S]\!]
with the isomorphism
\phi
is the
Magnus
embedding

\psi\colon S\hookrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle\!\langle X\rangle\!\rangle
, and one has

S_{(n)}=\{x\in S\mid\psi(x)\in I(X)^{n}\}=\{x\in S\mid\phi(x-1)\in I(X)^{n}\}.

for
n\geq 1
.

Moreover, one has a canonical isomorphism of graded algebras

\mathrm{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle\!\langle X\rangle\!\rangle:=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}I(X)^{n}/I(X)^{n+1}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle X\rangle,

so that for a series
f\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle\!\langle X\rangle\!\rangle
such that
f\in I(X)^{n}\smallsetminus I(X)^{n+1}
, one may consider
the class
f+I(X)^{n+1}
as a homogeneous polynomial of
\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle X\rangle
of degree
n
.
Likewise, one may consider the quotient
S_{(n)}/S_{(n+1)}
as a subspace of the space of homogeneous polynomials
on
X
of degree
n

(cf., e.g.,
[
37
]
*§ 3.1).

Definition 3.2
.

For an element
x\in S_{(n)}\smallsetminus S_{(n+1)}
, the class
\psi(x)+I(X)^{n+1}
, considered as a
homogeneous polynomial of
\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle X\rangle
of degree
n
, is called the
initial form
of
x
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle X\rangle
.

In particular, the initial form of
x_{i}
is
X_{i}
, and the initial form of the commutator
[x_{i},x_{j}]=x_{i}^{-1}x_{j}^{-1}x_{i}x_{j}

is the algebra commutator
[X_{i},X_{j}]=X_{i}X_{j}-X_{j}X_{i}
, for every
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
.

3.2.
Restricted Lie algebras and graded group algebras

A unitary associative
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra
A
may be turned into a Lie algebra by defining the Lie brackets

[a,b]=ab-ba
for all
a,b\in A
.
The notion of restricted Lie algebra was introduced by N. Jacobson (cf.
[
15
]
*§ V.5); we follow the definition given in
[
7
]
*§ 12.1.

Definition 3.3
.

A Lie
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra
\mathfrak{L}
is said to be a
restricted Lie algebra
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
if there exists a unitary associative

\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra
A
and a monomorphism of Lie algebras
\vartheta\colon\mathfrak{L}\to A
(with
A
considered as Lie algebra)
such that
\vartheta(v)^{p}\in\image(\vartheta)
for every
v\in\mathfrak{L}
.
In particular, every unitary associative algebra can be considered as restricted Lie algebra.

The algebra
A
together with the monomorphism
\vartheta
is called a
universal envelope
of
\mathfrak{L}
if it satisfies the
following universal property: for every morphism of restricted Lie algebras
\phi\colon\mathfrak{L}\to B
, where
B
is a unitary
associative
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra considered as a restricted Lie algebra, there exists a unique morphism of unitary associative
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebras

\tilde{\phi}\colon A\to B
such that
\phi=\tilde{\phi}\circ\vartheta
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated pro-
p
group.
By (
3.2
), the graded space
\mathfrak{L}(G)
endowed with the Lie brackets induced by commutators,
and
p
-power map induced by the
p
-power of elements, is a restricted Lie algebra.

Let
I_{G}
denote the
augmentation ideal
of the group
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
associated to
G
.
Namely,
I_{G}
is the two-sided ideal of
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
generated by the elements
g-1
,
g\in G
.
The
graded group
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra

\mathrm{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
associated to
G
is the graded algebra

\mathrm{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}I_{G}^{n}/I_{G}^{n+1},\qquad I_{G}^{0}=\mathbb{F}_{p}[G].

One has the following result by A. Jennings (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
*§ 12.2).

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated pro-
p
group.
Then for
g\in G_{(n)}\smallsetminus G_{(n+1)}
, the assignment

gG_{(n+1)}\longmapsto(g-1)+I_{G}^{n+1}\in I_{G}^{n}/I_{G}^{(n+1)}

induces a monomorphism of restricted Lie algebras
\mathfrak{L}(G)\to\mathrm{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
, so that
\mathrm{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
is the restricted envelope of
\mathfrak{L}(G)
.

Given
X=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}\}
, consider
\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle X\rangle
as a restricted Lie algebra.
The Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{L}(X)
of
\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle X\rangle
generated by
X
is a free restricted Lie algebra,
and
\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle X\rangle
is its universal envelope.
Moreover, for
S
a free pro-
p
group with basis
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\}
, we may identify
\mathfrak{L}(S)
with
\mathfrak{L}(X)
via the isomorphism that carries the initial form of each
x_{i}
to
X_{i}
,
and
\mathrm{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[S]
with
\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle X\rangle
via the graded version of the isomorphism (
3.3
) (cf.
[
13
]
*Rem. 2.3).

Remark 3.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{r}
be a restricted ideal of the free restricted Lie algebra
\mathfrak{L}(X)
— i.e.,
\mathfrak{r}
is an
ideal of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{L}(X)
, considered as subalgebra of
\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle X\rangle
, with the further condition that
f^{p}\in\mathfrak{r}

for every
f\in\mathfrak{r}
—, and set
\mathfrak{L}=\mathfrak{L}(X)/\mathfrak{r}
.
Then the universal envelope
\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{L}}
of
\mathfrak{L}
is isomorphic to the quotient

\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle X\rangle/\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{L}(X)}/\mathcal{R},

where
\mathcal{R}
is the two-sided ideal of
\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{L}(X)}
(identified with
\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle X\rangle
) generated by
\mathfrak{r}

(cf.
[
13
]
*Prop. 2.1).

4.1.
Cohomology of pro-
p

For a pro-

we shall denote the Galois
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology groups simply by
H^{n}(G)
for every

.
Thus
H^{0}(G)=\mathbb{F}_{p}
and, given a minimal presentation (
4.1
), one has the following isomorphisms of vector spaces:

(4.2)

\begin{split}H^{1}(G)&\cong H^{1}(S)\cong(G/G_{(2)})^{*},\\
H^{2}(G)&\cong H^{1}(R)^{G}\cong(R/R^{p}[R,S])^{*}\end{split}

(cf.
[
26
]
*Prop. 3.9.1 and Prop. 3.5.9).
In particular,
\dim(H^{1}(G))
is the minimal number of generators of

, and
\dim(H^{2}(G))
is the number of

, which are therefore invariants of

.
For a pro-
p

, the cohomological dimension of

is the number

\cd(G)=\max\{n\geq 1\mid H^{n}(G)\neq 0\}

(cf.
[
26
]
*Def. 3.3.1).

The
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology of a pro-

\alpha\cup\beta=(-1)^{ij}\beta\cup\alpha

*§ III.9.

The cup product from degree

to degree

in the

Proposition 4.1
.

Let

be a finitely generated pro-

).
The following are equivalent:

(i)

the (bilinear extension of the) cup product
H^{1}(G)^{\otimes 2}\to H^{2}(G)
is surjective;

(ii)

one has the equality
R^{p}[R,S]=R\cap S_{(3)}
.

If (i) and (ii) hold, given any set of defining relations
\{r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}\}\subseteq R
, for every

the initial form of

is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
2
(here we identify
\mathfrak{L}(S)
with
\mathfrak{L}(X)
).

In particular, if a finitely generated pro-
p
group has quadratic
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology,
then the two conditions in Proposition

Remark 4.2
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated pro-

), and suppose that

.
Then

Fix a basis

of

and a set of defining relations
\{r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}\}
of

, and
set

.
After identifying
\mathfrak{L}(S)
with
\mathfrak{L}(X)
, we may identify the quotient

with the space of homogeneous polynomial of

Let

4.2.
Strongly free presentations

The concept of mild group has been introduced by J. Labute in
[
20
]
and D. Anick in
[
1
]
as a group
whose relators are as independent as possible with respect to the lower central series.
Mild pro-
p
groups were subsequently defined for several filtrations
in
[
21
]
, for
p
odd, and in
[
18
]
and
[
11
]
, for
p=2
.
It came as a surprise when J. Labute discovered that these groups have cohomological dimension
2
and some occur naturally
as Galois groups of maximal
p
-extensions with restricted ramification.
Here we present the definition with respect to the
p
-Zassenhaus filtration.

Recall that one may consider the initial form of an element of a finitely generated free pro-
p
group
S
as a homogeneous
polynomial in
\mathfrak{L}(X)\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle X\rangle
.

Definition 4.4
.

(a)

Let
\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{m}
be homogeneous elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle X\rangle
of degrees
s_{i}=\deg\rho_{i}{\ >1}
and let
\mathcal{R}
be the two-sided ideal of
\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle X\rangle
generated by
\{\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{m}\}
.
The sequence
(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{m})
is
strongly free
if the Hilbert series of the quotient algebra

A_{\bullet}=\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle X\rangle/\mathcal{R}
is

(4.3)

h_{A_{\bullet}}(z)=\frac{1}{1-dz+(t^{s_{1}}+\dots+t^{s_{m}})}.

The empty sequence is considered to be strongly free.

(b)

A minimal finite presentation of a finitely generated pro-
p
group (
4.1
),
with defining relations
\{r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}\}
,
is called a
strongly free presentation
(with respect to the
p
-Zassenhaus filtration)
if the sequence
(\bar{r}_{1},\dots,\bar{r}_{m})
of the initial forms of the relations is strongly free.
A finitely presented pro-
p
group that has a strongly free presentation is called a
mild
group
(with respect to the
p
-Zassenhaus filtration).

Mild pro-
p
groups have exceptionally nice behavior both in terms of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology and in terms of the graded group algebra,
as shown by the following (cf.
[
13
]
*Thm 2.12).

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
G
be a mild pro-
p
group, with a strongly free presentation (
4.1
)
and defining relations
\{r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}\}
.
Then

(i)

\cd(G)=2
;

(ii)

one has an isomorphism of graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebras
\mathrm{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle X\rangle/\mathcal{R}
,
where
\mathcal{R}
is the two-sided ideal generated by the initial forms of the defining relations

\{r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}\}
.

As a consequence, the graded components of the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology algebra
H^{\bullet}(G)
of a mild pro-
p
group
G
are completely characterized by (
4.2
).

4.3.
Mild pro-
p
groups and Koszul algebras

Now we can prove our main results.

Theorem 4.6

(Theorem
1.3
)
.

Let
G
be a mild pro-
p
group, with strongly free presentation (
4.1
).
If
H^{\bullet}(G)
is quadratic,then the algebras
H^{\bullet}(G)
and
\mathrm{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
are quadratic dual to each other, and Koszul.

Proof.

Let
d=\dim(H^{1}(G))
and
m=\dim(H^{2}(G))
. By Proposition
4.5
,
\mathrm{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle X\rangle/\mathcal{R}
. By definition of mildness,

h_{\mathrm{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]}(z)=h_{\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle X\rangle/\mathcal{R}}=\frac{1}{1-dz+mz^{2}},

hence, by Proposition
2.11
,
\mathrm{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]
is Koszul. By Proposition
4.3
,
H^{\bullet}(G)\cong(\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle X\rangle/\mathcal{R})^{!}
, so
H^{\bullet}(G)
is Koszul as well by Proposition
2.5
.
∎

The converse of Theorem
4.6
gives a characterization of those mild pro-
p
groups whose
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology algebra is quadratic.

Proposition 4.7

(Proposition
1.4
)
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated pro-
p
group of cohomological dimension
2
such that the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology algebra

is Koszul.
Then

p

Proof.

Let (
4.1
) be a minimal presentation of

, with

.
Also, set

, with

, and let
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{m}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle X\rangle
be the initial forms of the relations

.

Since

is Koszul, in particular it is a quadratic algebra, and by Proposition

the initial forms

are homogeneous polynomials of degree

Moreover, by Proposition

and by Proposition

By Proposition

and by (

Namely, the sequence

is strongly free, and (

5.1.
Testing for the quadraticity of cohomology

In general, thanks to the fact that any mild pro-
p
group
G
has cohomological dimension
2
, verifying the quadraticity of
H^{\bullet}(G)
in Theorem
4.6
amounts at checking only two conditions:

(a)

That each element of (a basis of)
H^{2}(G)
is a linear combination of cup products of elements in
H^{1}(G)
. Since
\dim(H^{2}(G))
equals the number of defining relations, this often reduces to checking that the initial forms of the defining relations are polynomials of degree
2
and, as such, are linearly independent (cf. e.g.
[
3
]
*Prop. 1). Dually, one can check that the defining relations induce cup products of elements of
H^{1}(G)
which are linearly independent in
H^{2}(G)
.

(b)

That, for every
\chi,\psi,\varphi\in H^{1}(G)
, the cup product
\chi\cup\psi\cup\varphi
, which is zero as
\cd(G)=2
, is zero
purely as a consequence of what happens in
H^{2}(G)
. In more formal terms, letting
\pi_{\bullet}:\mathbf{T}_{\bullet}(H^{1}(G))\to H^{\bullet}(G)
be the canonical projection of graded algebras,
H^{3}(G)
is included in the ideal
(\ker(\pi_{2}))
.

Example 5.3
.

As an illustrative example, let
G
be the pro-
p

G=\left\langle\>x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\>\mid\>[x_{1},x_{2}]=[x_{1},x_{3}]=[x_{2},x_{3}][x_{4},x_{5}]=1\>\right\rangle.

In order to verify the mildness of

[
1
]
*Thm. 3.2 (cf. also
[

]
*§ 3).
The initial forms of the defining relations are

(5.1)

\rho_{1}=[X_{1},X_{2}],\qquad\rho_{2}=[X_{1},X_{3}],\qquad\rho_{3}=[X_{2},X_{3}]+[X_{4},X_{5}].

We define the total order
X_{1}\prec X_{2}\prec X_{3}\prec X_{4}\prec X_{5}
and we extend it to the degree-lexicographic total order on the monomials in the indererminates
X=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{5}\}

\begin{array}[]{c}X_{i_{1}}\cdots X_{i_{a}}\prec X_{j_{1}}\cdots X_{j_{b}}\\
\Updownarrow\\
a<b\mbox{ or }(a=b\mbox{ and }\exists k\in\{1,\dots,a\}(X_{i_{1}}=X_{j_{1}},\dots,X_{i_{k-1}}=X_{j_{k-1}},X_{i_{k}}\prec X_{j_{k}})).\end{array}

The
leading monomials
of the initial forms (
5.1
), that is, the monomials with higher degree-lexicographic order,
are respectively
X_{2}X_{1}
,
X_{3}X_{1}
and
X_{5}X_{4}

The sequence of the leading monomials is

(no leading monomial is a submonomial of a different one, and the beginning of a leading monomial is not the same as the ending of another, or the same, monomial, cf.
[
1

*§ 3).

(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\rho_{3})

gives
\mathrm{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}\langle X\rangle/\mathcal{R}

Let

dual to

Then the set of the cup products dually associated to the defining relations is

(noticing that
\chi_{4}\cup\chi_{5}=\chi_{2}\cup\chi_{3}

[
26

*Prop. 3.9.13 (ii) to a null linear combination of

The usual graded-commutativity of the cup product gives

\begin{matrix}\chi_{2}\cup\chi_{1}=-\chi_{1}\cup\chi_{2};&\chi_{3}\cup\chi_{1}=-\chi_{1}\cup\chi_{3};\\
\chi_{3}\cup\chi_{2}=-\chi_{2}\cup\chi_{3};&\chi_{4}\cup\chi_{5}=-\chi_{5}\cup\chi_{4}.\end{matrix}

All the remaining cup products

{\chi_{1}\cup\chi_{2}\cup\chi_{3}=\chi_{1}\cup\chi_{4}\cup\chi_{5}=0\cup\chi_{5}=0.}

It is important to notice that the fact that all triple cup products are zero depends only on the linear structure of

\sum_{i}k(\xi_{i}\cup\psi_{i}\cup\varphi_{i}),\qquad k\in\mathbb{F}_{p},\quad\xi_{i},\psi_{i},\varphi_{i}\in H^{1}(G)

such that, for all

Theorem
4.6
can therefore be applied, granting that

\mathrm{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[\![G]\!]

Proposition 5.8

Let

(i)

(ii)

\chi\cup\psi=0

Then

Theorem 5.9

5.4.

The class of
Koszul algebras of
H
-elementary type
is the class of those Koszul algebras which are constructible starting
from trivial quadratic algebras and the cohomology algebras of Demushkin groups (cf. Example

), and taking wedge products with the 1-generated trivial quadratic algebra
\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}(\mathbb{F}_{p},\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\otimes 2})
and direct sums.
Dually, the class of
Koszul algebras of
G
-elementary type
is the class of those Koszul algebras which are constructible starting
from free algebras and Demushkin algebras (cf. Example
2.8
), and taking symmetric tensor products with the 1-generated free algebra
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1}]
and free products (cf.
[

]
*Def. 5.8).

Remark 5.10
.

The definition of Koszul algebras of

For a quadratic algebra

If

\begin{split}&k(A_{\bullet}\oplus B_{\bullet})=\max\{k(A_{\bullet}),k(B_{\bullet})\},\\

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
G
be a finitely presented pro-
p
group.
The following are equivalent:

(i)

the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology algebra
H^{\bullet}(G)
is a Koszul algebra of
H
-elementary type, and
H^{3}(G)=0
;

(ii)

the graded group algebra
\mathrm{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
is a Koszul algebra of
G
-elementary type, and
(\mathrm{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]^{!})_{3}=0
.

Moreover, if the above conditions hold, then
G
is mild.

Proof.

If condition (i) holds, then
G
is mild by Proposition
4.7
.
Then by Proposition
4.5
one has an isomorphism of quadratic algebras
\mathrm{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]\cong H^{\bullet}(G)^{!}
, and thus
\mathrm{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
is a Koszul algebra of
G
-elementary type.
In particular,
(\mathrm{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]^{!})_{3}\cong H^{3}(G)=0
.

Now suppose condition (ii) holds, and set
\mathrm{gr}\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]=:A_{\bullet}=\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}(A_{1},\Omega)
, with
\Omega
a subspace of
A_{1}^{\otimes 2}
.
Since
A_{\bullet}
is Koszul, also
A_{\bullet}^{!}
is Koszul.
Since
A_{3}^{!}=0
, one has

\begin{split}h_{A_{\bullet}^{!}}(z)&=1+\dim(A_{1}^{!})z+\dim(A_{2}^{!})z^{2}+0\\
&=1+\dim(A_{1})z+\left(\dim(T_{2}(A_{1})-\dim(A_{2})\right)z^{2}\\
&=1+dz+mz^{2}\end{split}

where
d=\dim(G/G_{(2)})=\dim(H^{1}(G))
and
m=\dim(H^{2}(G))=\dim(\Omega)
.
Hence, Proposition
2.11
implies

h_{A_{\bullet}}(z)=\frac{1}{h_{A_{\bullet}^{!}}(-z)}=\frac{1}{1-dz+mz^{2}},

i.e., the relations which generate
\Omega
give rise to a strongly free sequence, and
G
is mild.
Hence
H^{3}(G)=0
by Proposition
4.5
, and
H^{\bullet}(G)\cong A_{\bullet}^{!}
. In particular,
H^{\bullet}(G)
is a Koszul algebra of
H
-elementary type.
∎

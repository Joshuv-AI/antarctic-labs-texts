Hybrid Beamforming for Millimeter Wave Full-Duplex under Limited Receive Dynamic Range
Ian P. Roberts, Jeffrey G. Andrews, Sriram Vishwanath

Abstract
Full-duplex millimeter wave (mmWave) communication has shown increasing promise for self-interference cancellation via hybrid precoding and combining. This paper proposes a novel mmWave MIMO design for configuring the analog and digital beamformers of a full-duplex transceiver. Our design is the first to holistically consider the key practical constraints of analog beamforming codebooks, a minimal number of radio frequency (RF) chains, limited channel knowledge, beam alignment, and a limited receive dynamic range. To prevent self-interference from saturating the receiver of a full-duplex device having limited dynamic range, our design addresses saturation on a per-antenna and per- RF chain basis. Numerical results evaluate our design in a variety of settings and validate the need to prevent receiver-side saturation. These results and the corresponding insights serve as useful design references for practical full-duplex mmWave transceivers.



I
Introduction

The ability for a transceiver to transmit and receive simultaneously in-band introduces an exciting upgrade at the physical layer and in medium access when compared to existing half-duplex schemes such as
time-division duplexing (TDD)
and
frequency-division duplexing (FDD)

[

The gains supplied by full-duplex capability in

By full-duplexing access and backhaul, heterogeneous

Key challenges in
mmWave

The presence of communication, radar, and other incumbents in lightly regulated

I-B

Contributions

We formulate
mmWave

MIMO
expressions capturing practical receive dynamic range limitations per-antenna and per-
RF
chain.
In particular, we motivate this work by the limited dynamic range of
LNAs
placed per-antenna and of
ADCs
placed per-
RF
chain.
Using these formulations, we outline constraints on a
mmWave

MIMO
design to limit the self-interference power inflicted on each antenna and each
RF
chain at the receiver of the full-duplex device.
By doing so, we can ensure a limited receive dynamic range does not severely degrade reception of a desired signal.
We outline conditions where meeting these per-antenna and per-
RF
chain self-interference power constraints are implicitly met, either by one another or other system factors.

We present a hybrid digital/analog beamforming design that enables a
mmWave
transceiver to operate in a full-duplex fashion, serving two devices simultaneously in-band.
Our design aims to achieve a high sum spectral efficiency on the two links while ensuring it does not induce receiver-side saturation on a per-antenna and per-
RF
chain basis.
Adhering to a multitude of additional practical considerations beyond a limited receive dynamic range, our design supports beam alignment schemes and codebook-based analog beamforming, rather than assuming full knowledge of the over-the-air channels and the ability to fine-tune phase shifters and attenuators. Furthermore, we limit the number of
RF
chains to the minimum necessary for multi-stream transmission.
To provide our design with some freedom in the choice of its analog beamformers, we present a methodology for building sets of candidate analog beamformers based on measurements from codebook-based beam alignment.
Finally, our design is not self-interference channel model-dependent, in that it does not exploit any particular structure or model.

We evaluate our design under a variety of settings.
Our numerical results indicate scenarios where our design thrives, offering significant spectral efficiency gains over conventional half-duplex operation.
These results also outline conditions under which per-antenna and per-
RF
chain self-interference power constraints restrict what is possible for
mmWave
full-duplex, providing useful insights to engineers on relationships between system parameters such as transmit power,
RF
isolation,
ADC
resolution, and the self-interference power reaching each antenna and each
RF
chain.
Understanding the degree of self-interference mitigation required at specific points in the receiver can drive full-duplex system analyses, including those that may supplement beamforming-based approaches with analog and/or digital self-interference cancellation
[
18
]
.

III
Problem Formulation

This work is motivated by the fact that a receive chain of a full-duplex device—which practically has a limited dynamic range—is susceptible to saturation due to the overwhelming strength of self-interference
[
21
]
.
To highlight this, we consider two sources of limited dynamic range in this work:
LNAs
and
ADCs
.
Like other amplifiers,
LNAs
begin saturating and introduce significant nonlinearities beyond a certain input power level, meaning only signals below some power threshold see an approximately linear amplifier.
The limited resolution of an
ADC
is a classical example of limited dynamic range.
Since the combination of a desired receive signal and self-interference enters the
ADC
, self-interference can drive up quantization noise and degrade the quality of the desired receive signal.

Consider Fig.
1
, where
LNAs
are placed per-antenna and
ADCs
are placed per-
RF
chain.
To avoid saturating the
LNAs
at the full-duplex device, the self-interference power reaching each antenna must be mitigated to below some threshold.
Similarly, to avoid saturating the
ADCs
, the self-interference reaching each
RF
chain must also be limited.
This work investigates relying solely on beamforming to achieve full-duplex, meaning the design herein must prevent
LNA
and
ADC
saturation.
Note that we are only concerned with preventing saturation at the receiver of the
full-duplex
device
i
—not that of the
half-duplex
device
j
—since the saturation we are considering stems from self-interference.
Also, note that
LNAs
are not always placed strictly per-antenna as we have assumed and that there may exist other motivations for restricting the self-interference power at each antenna and at each
RF
chain.
With this in mind, the design we present is not strictly for
LNAs
and
ADCs
but rather for meeting arbitrary per-antenna and per-
RF
chain self-interference power constraints:
LNAs
and
ADCs
are an important special case.

Using the previously defined system model, we can begin analyzing the signals that reach the
LNAs
and
ADCs
of our full-duplex device.
The symbol vector at the
LNAs
of
i
is

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{LNA}}=\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{des,LNA}}+\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{int,LNA}}+\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{noise,LNA}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{\mathrm{r}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\times 1}

(7)

where the desired term is
\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{des,LNA}}=\sqrt{P_{\mathrm{tx}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}}G_{ki}\mathbf{H}_{ki}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}\mathbf{s}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}
, the self-interference term is

\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{int,LNA}}=\sqrt{P_{\mathrm{tx}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}}G_{ii}\mathbf{H}_{ii}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\mathbf{s}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
, and the noise term is
\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{noise,LNA}}=\mathbf{n}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
.
Following reception, the signal at each antenna passes through its respective
LNA
.
We represent
LNA
operation as
\mathcal{L}\left(\cdot\right)
and model it in the following piece-wise fashion

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(x\right)=\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{lin}}\left(x\right)={G}_{\mathrm{rx}}\cdot x,&\left|x\right|^{2}\leq P_{\mathrm{LNA}}^{\mathrm{max}}\\
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sat}}\left(x\right),&\left|x\right|^{2}>P_{\mathrm{LNA}}^{\mathrm{max}}\end{cases}

(8)

where the per-antenna symbol

undergoes linear amplification

offering a linear amplitude gain of

if its average power (over the symbol period) is below some threshold

.
Otherwise, the

is saturated and

undergoes some nonlinear function

.
It is difficult to characterize

at the symbol level since

For this reason, we make no attempt to characterize

.
We would like to point out that for a properly chosen

—which will likely include appropriate backoffs for the signal distribution and pulse shape—the

will undergo linear amplification and, thus, so will the symbols.
Since the gain of the

For ease of notation, we overload the

,
where
{\mathbf{x}}=[x_{1},x_{2},\dots]^{\mathrm{T}}
.

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{ADC}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\times\mathcal{L}\left(\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{LNA}}\right)\in\mathbb{C}^{L_{\mathrm{r}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\times 1}

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{ADC}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\begin{subarray}{c}\mathrm{lin}\end{subarray}}}{{=}}\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\times\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{LNA}}=\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{des,ADC}}+\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{int,ADC}}+\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{noise,ADC}}

We use

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}\left(\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{ADC}}\right)=\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{des,ADC}}+\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{int,ADC}}+\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{noise,ADC}}+{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathrm{quant}}\in\mathbb{C}^{L_{\mathrm{r}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\times 1}.

With these models and formulations in hand, we begin laying out our contribution, which aims to mitigate self-interference to certain levels per-antenna and per-
RF

IV
Beam Candidate Acquisition

To avoid estimating the over-the-air channel and to address initial access, practical
mmWave
systems employ
beam training
, or
beam alignment
, schemes that aim to identify transmit-receive beam pairs that afford link margin sufficient for communication.
Once promising beams have been identified and assigned, analog beamformers at the link’s transmitter and receiver can be set, which nets an effective channel that can be more straightforwardly estimated and can sustain communication.
Typically, these analog beamformers are configured according to a predetermined codebook of beams, which reduces complexity and offers robustness.

In this section, we describe a
beam candidate acquisition
algorithm used to construct a set of analog beamforming candidates when knowledge of the over-the-air channel (e.g.,
\mathbf{H}_{ij}
,
\mathbf{H}_{ki}
) is not present.
Rather than restricting our hybrid beamforming design to transmit and receive along a single choice of analog beamformers, a set of analog beamforming candidates will be supplied to our design in Section
V

This is motivated by the fact that some transmit-receive beam pairs at the full-duplex device will naturally afford more isolation than other pairs, meaning it may be preferable to use them for full-duplex, even if they were sub-optimal in a half-duplex setting.

V
Hybrid Beamforming Design for mmWave Full-Duplex

In this section, we present a hybrid beamforming design that will enable
mmWave
full-duplex while accounting for per-antenna and per-
RF
chain power constraints at the receiver of a full-duplex device.
The goal of our design is to achieve a high spectral efficiency on both links while ensuring that the power of the self-interference reaching the full-duplex device’s receiver is below some thresholds (which we define shortly).
To do so, our design leverages the analog beamforming candidate sets
\mathcal{T}_{ij}
and
\mathcal{T}_{ki}
found in the previous section to configure the analog beamformers at each device.
We would like to remind the reader that the design that follows holds for general per-antenna and per-
RF
chain power constraints, even though we are considering
LNA
and
ADC
power constraints as particular motivators.

Our design supports spatial multiplexing of multiple streams and importantly does not require more
RF
chains than necessary, where
L_{\mathrm{t}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}=L_{\mathrm{r}}^{{}^{\left(j\right)}}=N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(ij\right)}}
and
L_{\mathrm{t}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}=L_{\mathrm{r}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}=N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(ki\right)}}
.
Furthermore, we support the important case where
N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(ij\right)}}=N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(ki\right)}}
, implying
L_{\mathrm{t}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}=L_{\mathrm{r}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
.
This, along with the fact that we have not assumed anything about
\mathbf{H}_{ii}
, means our design’s formulation does not rely on
completely
avoiding (i.e., zero-forcing) the over-the-air self-interference channel or even the
effective
self-interference channel, unlike several existing designs.
We assume we have channel knowledge of
\mathbf{H}_{ii}
but do not assume knowledge of
\mathbf{H}_{ij}
or
\mathbf{H}_{ki}
, as was mentioned in Section
IV
.
We motivate this assumption by presuming that the self-interference channel can be more reliably estimated given its strength and can be done so possibly through calibration.
We assume large-scale quantities (e.g., transmit powers, large-scale channel gains,
SNRs
) are known.
Furthermore, our design holds for general symbol constellations, depending only on the symbol covariance and not the symbols themselves.

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{ij}=\mathrm{log}_{2}\left|\mathbf{I}+\frac{\mathrm{SNR}_{ij}}{N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(ij\right)}}}\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(j\right)}{*}}\mathbf{H}_{ij}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\mathbf{H}_{ij}^{{*}}\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(j\right)}}(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(j\right)}{*}}\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(j\right)}})^{-1}\right|

\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
,
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(j\right)}}

\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}}\

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{I}_{ij}

(30a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.~}\

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\leq 1

(30b)

Theorem 1
.

Problem (

) can be recast as a convex problem.

Proof.

Noting that problem (
30
) is non-convex in
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
but is convex in the product
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}
, we rewrite problem (
30
) using the substitution
\mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}=\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\succeq\mathbf{0}
as

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\succeq\mathbf{0}}\

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{I}_{ij}

(31a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.~}\

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\leq 1

(31b)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left(\mathbf{H}_{ii}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right)\leq\eta_{\mathrm{LNA}}

(31c)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\mathbf{H}_{ii}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right)\leq\eta_{\mathrm{ADC}}

(31d)

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}=\mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}

(31e)

With this simple restructuring, problem (

[

Once solved,
\mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
can be factored to recover
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
.
∎

Remark 1
.

The solution to problem (

) is unique but the factorization
\mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}=\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}

As such, our problem is a function of the digital precoder covariance and not of the digital precoder itself.
In other words, the solution

Remark 2

Since
\mathbf{X}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}=\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}=\mathbf{0}
is always a solution to problem (

), it is feasible.
Intuitively, this can be attributed to the fact that it is always possible for the transmitter at

to shut off completely to ensure the

and

do not saturate.

Remark 3
.

At least one of the constraints (
31b
)–(
31d
) will be tight (have equality) under the optimal solution to problem (
31
).
Intuitively, this can be attributed to the fact that additional power should be supplied to the digital precoder
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
—which will increase the mutual information
\mathcal{I}_{ij}
—until it exceeds its power budget or self-interference power is too high at an

or
ADC
.

To offer the system more freedom in its design, we now incorporate our sets of candidate beams
\mathcal{T}_{ij}
and
\mathcal{T}_{ki}
when

.
By doing so, the diversity between candidate beams may allow our full-duplex transceiver to better transmit while meeting the constraints.
We capture this freedom in choosing our analog beamformers by wrapping problem (

) with an outer maximization over
\mathcal{T}_{ij}
and
\mathcal{T}_{ki}
, resulting in the following optimization problem.

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left(\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}},\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(j\right)}}\right)\in\mathcal{T}_{ij}\\
\left(\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}},\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right)\in\mathcal{T}_{ki}\end{subarray}}\ \max_{\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}}\ \mathcal{I}_{ij}

(32a)

\displaystyle\ \ \mathrm{s.t.~}\ \left\|\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\leq 1

(32b)

\displaystyle\qquad\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left(\mathbf{H}_{ii}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right)\leq\eta_{\mathrm{LNA}}

(32c)

\displaystyle\qquad\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\mathbf{H}_{ii}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right)\leq\eta_{\mathrm{ADC}}

) exhaustively over all possible candidate combinations in
\mathcal{T}_{ij}
and
\mathcal{T}_{ki}
.
Thus, (

K_{ij}\times K_{ki}
times.

Note that, as evidenced in (

),
\mathcal{I}_{ij}
contains
\mathbf{H}_{ij}
, which we have not assumed explicit knowledge of.
Instead, using
\mathcal{H}_{ij}
from (

for all

).
Furthermore, note that we do not require knowledge of
\mathbf{H}_{ki}
or
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\mathbf{H}_{ki}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}

We do require knowledge of

Notice, however, that perhaps an estimate of

V-C

Constraint Redundancy Conditions

We now pause from our design to more closely examine the interplay between our precoding power constraint, per-antenna self-interference power constraint, and per-
RF
chain self-interference power constraint.
In doing so, we will see that under appropriate conditions the latter two constraints may be inherently met by the precoding power constraint and under other conditions, the per-
RF
chain self-interference power constraint may be inherently met by the per-antenna self-interference power constraint.
Knowledge of these conditions can accelerate solving problem (
32
).

Theorem 2
.

When condition (
33
) holds, the per-antenna constraint (
32c
) is implicitly met by the precoding power constraint (
32b
).

\displaystyle\eta_{\mathrm{LNA}}\geq\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left(\mathbf{H}_{ii}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right)

(33)

Proof.

Since the precoding power constraint (
30b
) is satisfied, we note that

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left(\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right)\leq\left\|\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\leq 1

(34)

which allows us to see that

\displaystyle\eta_{\mathrm{LNA}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left(\mathbf{H}_{ii}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right)\

(35)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left(\mathbf{H}_{ii}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right)\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left(\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right)

(36)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left(\mathbf{H}_{ii}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right)

(37)

where we have used
\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left({\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{B}}\right)\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left({\mathbf{A}}\right)\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left({\mathbf{B}}\right)
.
∎

Theorem 3
.

When condition (
38
) holds, the per-
RF
chain constraint (
32d
) is implicitly met by the precoding power constraint (
32b
).

\displaystyle\eta_{\mathrm{ADC}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\mathbf{H}_{ii}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right)

(38)

Proof.

Using the fact from (
34
) and assuming (
38
) to be true, we can write

\displaystyle\eta_{\mathrm{ADC}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\mathbf{H}_{ii}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right)

(39)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\mathbf{H}_{ii}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right)\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left(\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right)

(40)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\mathbf{H}_{ii}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right)

(41)

∎

Remark 4
.

When (
33
) and (
38
) hold, the (half-duplex) capacity-achieving strategy on the transmit link is the solution to problem (
30
) since the per-antenna and per-
RF
chain constraints vanish, leaving only the precoding power constraint.

Theorem 4
.

When the per-antenna constraint (
32c
) is satisfied and condition (
42
) holds, the per-
RF
chain constraint (
32d
) is satisfied.

\displaystyle\eta_{\mathrm{ADC}}\geq\eta_{\mathrm{LNA}}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\right)

(42)

Proof.

Starting with our assumption that the per-antenna constraint (
32c
) is satisfied, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left(\mathbf{H}_{ii}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\eta_{\mathrm{LNA}}\leq\frac{\eta_{\mathrm{ADC}}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\right)}

which yields

\displaystyle\eta_{\mathrm{ADC}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\right)\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left(\mathbf{H}_{ii}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right)

(44)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{max}}^{2}\left(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\mathbf{H}_{ii}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right)

which indicates directly that (

) is satisfied.
∎

.

When

).

A condition analogous to Theorem

stating that satisfying the per-antenna constraint (

) is not possible.

Remainder of the Design

We now complete our
mmWave

MIMO
design.
Solving (
32
) yields selections for
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
,
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
,
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(j\right)}}
,
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}
, and
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
.
Configuring
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(j\right)}}
,
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}
, and
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
remains, which we execute as follows.
Having the rest of the transmit link configured, the optimal linear baseband combiner at
j
can be designed in a
linear minimum mean square error (LMMSE)
fashion as follows.
Let
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{ij}\triangleq\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(j\right)}{*}}\mathbf{H}_{ij}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\in\mathbb{C}^{L_{\mathrm{r}}^{{}^{\left(j\right)}}\times N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(ij\right)}}}
be the effective transmit channel after solving (
32
).
Note that this can computed from the product of
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(j\right)}{*}}\mathbf{H}_{ij}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
, which is referenced from
\mathcal{H}_{ij}
, and
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
.
Then, the
LMMSE
baseband combiner at
j
can be constructed as

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(j\right)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{P_{\mathrm{tx}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}}G_{ij}}\left(\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{ij}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{ij}^{{*}}+\frac{N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(ij\right)}}}{\mathrm{SNR}_{ij}}\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(j\right)}{*}}\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(j\right)}}\right)^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{ij}.

(46)

Since
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
will not generally diagonalize the effective channel (due to meeting the per-antenna and per-
RF
chain constraints), an
LMMSE
combiner at
j
will aim to reduce inter-stream interference and reject noise.
This concludes configuration of the transmit link, having set
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
,
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
,
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(j\right)}}
, and
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(j\right)}}
.

We now turn our attention to the receive link, where we need to configure
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}
and
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
.
Now that we have made our selections of
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}
and
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
, we begin by estimating the relatively small channel
\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{ki}\triangleq\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\mathbf{H}_{ki}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}\in\mathbb{C}^{L_{\mathrm{r}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\times L_{\mathrm{t}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}}
,
which can be observed digitally and we assume is error-free.
Taking the
singular value decomposition (SVD)
of this effective channel from
k
to
i
and accounting for noise coloring, we get
{\mathbf{U}}_{ki}{\mathbf{\Sigma}}_{ki}{\mathbf{V}}_{ki}^{{*}}=\mathrm{SVD}\left(\left(\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\right)^{-1/2}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{ki}\right)
.
We then build the precoder that maximizes the mutual information offered to the
RF
chains of
i
as

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}=\left[{\mathbf{V}}_{ki}\right]_{:,1:N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(ki\right)}}}\times{\mathbf{P}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}

where
{\mathbf{P}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}
is a diagonal water-filling power allocation matrix
[
23
]
.

Finally, we are left to configure
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
.
Before doing so, however, we notice that
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
and the received symbols it acts on exist in the digital domain, residing after the
ADCs
.
Having knowledge of
\mathbf{H}_{ii}
,
\mathbf{s}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
, and all other beamformers, we can synthesize the received self-interference and subtract it before applying our combiner
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
.
Recall that the symbol vector after the
ADCs
is

\displaystyle{\mathbf{y}}_{\mathrm{dig}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{des,ADC}}+\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{int,ADC}}+\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{noise,ADC}}+{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathrm{quant}}.

(48)

Since
{\mathbf{y}}_{\mathrm{dig}}
is in the digital domain, we can compute
\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{int,ADC}}
perfectly as

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{int,ADC}}=\sqrt{P_{\mathrm{tx}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}}G_{ii}\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\mathbf{H}_{ii}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\mathbf{s}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}

(49)

and can subtract it from
{\mathbf{y}}_{\mathrm{dig}}
before applying our combiner.
Subtracting self-interference from
{\mathbf{y}}_{\mathrm{dig}}
, we get

\displaystyle{\mathbf{y}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}

\displaystyle={\mathbf{y}}_{\mathrm{dig}}-\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{int,ADC}}=\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{des,ADC}}+\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{noise,ADC}}+{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathrm{quant}}.

(50)

We can estimate the symbols intended for
i
from
k
by applying a combiner
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
to
{\mathbf{y}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
as

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{s}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}{\mathbf{y}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}=\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\left(\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{des,ADC}}+\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{noise,ADC}}+{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathrm{quant}}\right)

(51)

which will be corrupted by additive noise and the effects of quantization.
At first glance, it appears that self-interference does not play a role our symbol estimate
\hat{\mathbf{s}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}
.
However, given that our
ADCs
have limited resolution, the power of the quantization error term
{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathrm{quant}}
will increase with increased self-interference power.
Recall that this is precisely the motivation for our per-
RF
chain self-interference power constraint.

As evidenced by (
51
), we can see that the linear combiner
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
acts on a desired signal plus two noise terms.
Before proceeding, let us find the covariance of
{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathrm{quant}}
.
Finding the covariance of the symbols reaching the
ADCs
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{ADC}}\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{ADC}}^{{*}}\right]=

\displaystyle\ \frac{P_{\mathrm{tx}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}G_{ki}^{2}}{N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(ki\right)}}}\cdot\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\mathbf{H}_{ki}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}{*}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}{*}}\mathbf{H}_{ki}^{{*}}\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{P_{\mathrm{tx}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}G_{ii}^{2}}{N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(ij\right)}}}\cdot\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\mathbf{H}_{ii}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\mathbf{H}_{ii}^{{*}}\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}+\sigma_{\mathrm{n}}^{2}\cdot\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}.

Applying (
12
) per-
ADC
allows us to write the covariance matrix of
{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathrm{quant}}
as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{R}}_{\mathrm{quant}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathrm{quant}}{\mathbf{e}}_{\mathrm{quant}}^{{*}}\right]=\frac{8}{12\cdot 2^{2b}}\cdot{\mathbf{I}}\odot\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{ADC}}\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{ADC}}^{{*}}\right]

(52)

where
\odot
denotes the Hadamard (element-wise) product.
The linear design of
\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
that minimizes the
mean square error (MSE)
of
\hat{\mathbf{s}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}
is

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{P_{\mathrm{tx}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}}G_{ki}}\left(\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{ki}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{ki}^{{*}}+\frac{N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(ki\right)}}}{\mathrm{SNR}_{ki}}\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}+\frac{N_{\mathrm{s}}^{{}^{\left(ki\right)}}}{P_{\mathrm{tx}}^{{}^{\left(k\right)}}G_{ki}^{2}}{\mathbf{R}}_{\mathrm{quant}}\right)^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_{ki}.

(53)

Now that our design is complete, we characterize the covariance of self-interference and of noise at the detector, which can be used to evaluate
\mathcal{R}_{ij}
and
\mathcal{R}_{ki}
in (
26
) and (
27
), respectively.
Let
{\mathbf{Q}}_{\mathrm{int}}^{{}^{\left(n\right)}}
—the covariance of the received quantization noise due to self-interference at
i
, normalized to the noise power—be
{\mathbf{Q}}_{\mathrm{int}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\mathrm{n}}^{2}}\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}{*}}{\mathbf{R}}_{\mathrm{quant}}\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(i\right)}}
.
Let
{\mathbf{Q}}_{\mathrm{n}}^{{}^{\left(n\right)}}
—the covariance of the received noise at
n\in\{i,j\}
, normalized to the noise power—be written as
{\mathbf{Q}}_{\mathrm{n}}^{{}^{\left(n\right)}}=\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(n\right)}{*}}\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(n\right)}{*}}\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{RF}}^{{}^{\left(n\right)}}\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{BB}}^{{}^{\left(n\right)}}
.
This concludes our design, which we evaluate in the following section.

VII
Conclusion

We have presented a hybrid beamforming design for
mmWave
full-duplex that holistically accounts for a number of practical considerations including codebook-based analog beamforming and beam alignment, a desirably low number of
RF
chains, and the need to prevent receiver-side saturation at the full-duplex device.
Core to our design is its focus on limiting the self-interference power reaching each antenna and each
RF
chain, to prevent pushing
LNAs
beyond their linear region and avoid flooding a desired signal with quantization noise at the
ADCs
.
Our design utilizes sets of candidate analog beamformers to improve its flexibility in mitigating self-interference while maintaining service and to accommodate codebook-based analog beamforming.
Numerical results have highlighted the costs and limitations associated with preventing receiver-side saturation, which can be used in
mmWave
full-duplex system analyses when choosing components, constructing a
mmWave
full-duplex transceiver, and determining what levels of self-interference mitigation should be aimed for.
Useful future work would explore the integration of full-duplex
mmWave
transceivers into cellular standards, implementation of beamforming-based self-interference mitigation, and characterization of
mmWave
self-interference channels.

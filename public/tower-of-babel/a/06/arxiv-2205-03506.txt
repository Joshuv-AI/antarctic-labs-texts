Matings of cubic polynomials with a fixed critical point. Part II: α \alpha -symmetry of limbs
Thomas Sharland

Abstract
. Following Milnor, define 𝒮 1 \mathcal{S}_{1} to be the space of monic, centred cubic polynomials with a marked fixed critical point. In this article, a sequel to [ 18 ] , we provide a combinatorial sufficient (and conjecturally, necessary) condition (called α \alpha - symmetry ) for the mating of two postcritically finite polynomials in 𝒮 1 \mathcal{S}_{1} to be obstructed. To do this, we study the rotation sets associated to the parameter limbs in the connectedness locus of 𝒮 1 \mathcal{S}_{1} , which allows us to determine when there exist ray classes in the formal mating which contain a closed loop. We give a proof of the necessity of α \alpha -symmetry for a particular subset of postcritically finite maps in 𝒮 1 \mathcal{S}_{1} . Many examples are given to illustrate the results of the paper.

1.
Introduction

In this article we discuss the mating of two cubic polynomials with a fixed critical point. Informally, the mating construction allows us to take two complex polynomials
f
and
g
(along with their filled Julia sets
K(f)
and
K(g)
) and paste them together to construct branched covering of the sphere. If this branched covering (or a suitable modification of it) is (Thurston) equivalent to a rational map, we say that
f
and
g
are mateable.

In the quadratic case, it was shown through work of Rees, Shishikura and Tan
[
21
]
that two postcritically finite polynomials with locally connected Julia sets were mateable if and only if they do not belong to conjugate limbs of the Mandelbrot set. In this case, the obstructions obtained are topological; the space obtained from pasting together the filled Julia sets fails to be a sphere. In other words, two such polynomials are mateable if and only if they are topologically mateable.

However, Shishikura and Tan
[
20
]
showed that there exist cubic polynomials which are topologically mateable, but the resulting branched covering fails to be equivalent to a rational map. This indicates that the degree
3
(and higher) case is more complicated. Restricting attention to
\mathcal{S}_{1}
, the space of monic, centred cubic polynomials with a marked fixed critical point, the present author showed in
[
18
]
that any obstruction to the mating of postcritically finite polynomials in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
is topological, paralleling the quadratic case. However, in contrast to the quadratic case, we will prove the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exist postcritically finite polynomials
F_{a}
,
F_{b}
in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
which do not lie in conjugate limbs of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
but for which
F_{a}
and
F_{b}
are not mateable.

Thus the necessity of the conjugate limb condition does not carry over from quadratic polynomials to the space
\mathcal{S}_{1}
. We provide a sufficient (combinatorial) condition for two postcritically finite polynomials in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
to fail to be mateable (Proposition
3.7
). After this, we show that this condition can be satisfied even when the two polynomials do not belong to conjugate limbs of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
(Theorem
4.2
). We conjecture that the given sufficient condition is also necessary, and give a proof of necessity in a special case (Theorem
4.5
). The paper also contains a discussion of the combinatorics of limbs in the space
\mathcal{S}_{1}
, which is used to provide the combinatorial description mentioned above. Examples are given to illuminate the main results.

2.1.
Rotation sets

In this section we discuss the results we need on rotation sets. Much of the background material is taken from
[
22
]
(see also
[
2
]
). Let
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
be the circle. Any continuous
g\colon\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
lifts, via the canonical projection, to a continuous
G\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
which satisfies
G(x+1)=G(x)+d
for some
d\in\mathbb{Z}
called the degree of
g
. Furthermore, we say
g
is
monotone
if
G
is monotone.

Given a continuous non-decreasing map
G\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
G(x+1)=G(x)+1
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, the limit

\tau(G)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{G^{\circ n}(x)-x}{n}

is independent of
x
, and is called the
translation number
of
G
. The
rotation number

\rho(g)
of a degree
1
monotone map
g\colon\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
is then defined as the residue class of
\tau(G)
modulo
\mathbb{Z}
, where
G
is any lift of
g
.

For
d\geq 2
, we define
m_{d}\colon\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
by

m_{d}(t)=dt\pmod{\mathbb{Z}}.

This paper will focus on
m_{2}
and
m_{3}
, since both play an important role in the dynamics of maps in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
. A non-empty compact set
X\subseteq\mathbb{T}
is called a
rotation set
if
m_{d}(X)=X
and the restriction
m_{d}|_{X}
can be extended to a degree
1
monotone map on
\mathbb{T}
. Since any two such extensions
g
and
h
agree on
X
, it follows from Theorem 1.8 of
[
22
]
that
\rho(g)=\rho(h)
. We denote this common rotation number by
\rho(X)
(or just
\rho
if the set
X
is clear in the context) and call it the
rotation number of

X
.

Given a rotation set
X
for
m_{d}
, we call a component of
\mathbb{T}\setminus X
a
gap
of
X
. A gap is called a
major
gap if its length
\ell
satisfies
\ell\geq 1/d
. The
multiplicity
of a major gap is the integer part of
d\ell
. If a gap is not major, then it is called a
minor
gap. A minor gap maps homeomorphically onto its image (which must also be a gap of
X
) under
m_{d}
. We collect some important consequences of the above.

Proposition 2.1

(Theorem 2.9,
[
22
]
)
.

A rotation set is uniquely determined by its major gaps.

Proposition 2.2

(Corollary 2.16,
[
22
]
)
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{T}
be a non-empty
m_{d}
-invariant set. Then
X
is a rotation set if and only if
\mathbb{T}\setminus X
contains
d-1
disjoint intervals, each of length
1/d
.

We will refer to a periodic orbit under
m_{d}
as a
cycle
. When
d=3
, a rotation set can contain at most two cycles
C
and
C^{\prime}
, each with the same rotation number
\rho

[
22
]
. Furthermore, these cycles are
superlinked
in the sense that each gap of
C
meets
C^{\prime}
, and each gap of
C^{\prime}
meets
C
. If
X
is rotation set under
m_{3}
with
\rho(X)\in\mathbb{Q}
made up of the union of two cycles, we will define the
signature

s(X)
to be vector
(s_{1},s_{2})
, where
s_{1}
is the number of elements of
X
in
[0,1/2)
and
s_{2}
is the number of elements of
X
. The following, a special case of a theorem of Goldberg, gives a characterisation of which signatures can occur when
X
is the union of two cycles under
m_{3}
.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
10
]
)
.

Suppose
0\leq p/q<1
is a reduced fraction and
0\leq s_{1}\leq s_{2}=2q
. Then there exists a rotation set under
m_{3}
with rotation number
p/q
and signature
s(X)=(s_{1},s_{2})
made up of the union of two cycles if and only if
s_{1}
is odd. Furthermore, if this rotation set exists, then it is unique.

We remark that for
\rho=0
, the unique
m_{3}
-rotation set made up of the union of two cycles is
X=\{0,1/2\}
, since
0
and
1/2
are the two fixed points of
m_{3}
.

2.2.
Cubic Polynomials with a fixed critical point

We now discuss some preliminaries on the dynamics of cubic polynomials. Much of the material we need is from
[
14
]
. Many properties of the dynamical and parameter spaces of polynomials in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
were also investigated in
[
17
]
and
[
8
]
. Denote by
\mathcal{S}_{1}
the space of monic, centred cubic polynomials with a marked fixed critical point. Under a holomorphic change of coordinates, any such polynomial is of the form

F_{a}(z)=z^{3}-3a^{2}z+2a^{3}+a.

Under this parameterisation
a
is the (fixed)
marked critical point
and
-a
is the
free critical point
of
F_{a}
. We will call
v_{a}=F_{a}(-a)
the
free critical value
of
F_{a}
. The normal form above provides a conformal isomorphism
\mathcal{S}_{1}\cong\mathbb{C}
via
F_{a}\mapsto a
. The
connectedness locus

\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S}_{1})
is the set of polynomials
F_{a}
for which the Julia set is connected; equivalently, it is the set of polynomials
F_{a}
for which the free critical point
-a
has bounded orbit. For any map
F_{a}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
, we denote by
U_{a}
the Fatou component containing the fixed critical point
a
(thus
U_{a}
is the immediate basin of
a
). The
main hyperbolic component

\mathcal{H}_{0}
is the hyperbolic component in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
which contains
F_{0}
, the function
z\mapsto z^{3}
which has a double critical fixed point.

2.2.1.
Structure of filled Julia sets

For all
F_{a}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
, the Julia set of
F_{a}
is the simple closed curve
\partial U_{a}
and
-a\in U_{a}
. Now suppose
F=F_{a}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S}_{1})\setminus\mathcal{H}_{0}
. Then, by Böttcher’s theorem, there exists a conformal isomorphism
\text{B\"{o}t}=\text{B\"{o}t}_{a}\colon U_{a}\to\mathbb{D}
which satisfies

\text{B\"{o}t}(F(z))=(\text{B\"{o}t}(z))^{2}.

By a theorem of Roesch
[
16
]
(see also
[
8
,
17
]
),
\partial U_{a}
is a simple closed curve. Hence by Carathéodory’s Theorem, there exists a homeomorphic extension of
Böt
to the boundary
\partial U_{a}
. By abuse of notation, we will also use
Böt
for this homeomorphism from
\overline{U}_{a}
to
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Thus for each point
z\in\partial U_{a}
, there exists a unique
t\in\mathbb{T}
such that
z=\text{B\"{o}t}(e^{2\pi it})
. For brevity we will write
\beta(t)
instead of
\text{B\"{o}t}(e^{2\pi it})
and call
t
the internal angle of
z
. The following theorem then describes the structure of the (filled) Julia set of a map in
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S}_{1})\setminus\mathcal{H}_{0}
.

Theorem 2.4

(Theorem 2.1,
[
14
]
)
.

If
F_{a}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S}_{1})
with
-a\notin U_{a}
, then the filled Julia set
K(F_{a})
is equal to the union of the topological disk
\overline{U}_{a}
with a collection of compact connected sets
K_{t}
, where
t
ranges over a countable subset
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{T}
. Furthermore

(i)

The
K_{t}
are pairwise disjoint, and each
K_{t}
intersects
\overline{U}_{a}
in the single boundary point
\beta(t)
.

(ii)

There is a distinguished element
t_{0}\in\Lambda
such that the free critical point
-a
belongs to
K_{t_{0}}
.

(iii)

If
t\in\mathbb{T}
, then
t\in\Lambda
if and only if there exists some
n\geq 0
such that
2^{n}t\equiv t_{0}\pmod{\mathbb{Z}}
.

(iv)

For
t\not\equiv t_{0}\pmod{\mathbb{Z}}
, the map
F_{a}
carries
K_{t}
homeomorphically onto
K_{2t}
. However,
F_{a}
maps
K_{t_{0}}
onto the entire filled Julia set
K(F_{a})
.

We call
K_{t}
a
(dynamical) limb
for the map
F
; the point
\beta(t)
where
K_{t}
meets
U_{a}
is called the
root point
of the limb
K_{t}
. The set
K_{t_{0}}
is called the
critical limb
of
F_{a}
.

Suppose a polynomial
F
has a connected filled Julia set
K(F)
. By Böttcher’s theorem, there exists a conformal isomorphism
\text{B\"{o}t}_{F}\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus K(F)
which conjugates the map
z\mapsto z^{d}
on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}
to the map
F
on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus K(F)
. Furthermore, there is a unique choice of
\text{B\"{o}t}_{F}
which is tangent to the identity at infinity.

Definition 2.5
.

The
external ray
of angle
t
for the polynomial
F
is

R_{F}(t)=\text{B\"{o}t}_{F}(r_{t})

where
r_{t}=\{r\exp(2\pi it):r>1\}\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{D}
is the corresponding radial line.

We will often use the notation
R_{a}(t)
instead of
R_{F_{a}}(t)
for a polynomial
F_{a}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
. If the choice of polynomial is clear, we sometimes drop the subscript altogether. By Carathéodory’s theorem, the filled Julia set
K(F)
(or equivalently, the Julia set
J(F)
) of
F
is locally connected if and only if
\text{B\"{o}t}_{F}
extends continuously to the unit circle. The induced semiconjugacy
\gamma_{F}\colon\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\to J(F)
is called the
Carathéodory loop
. The point
\gamma_{F}(t)\in J(F)
is called the
landing point
of the ray
R_{F}(t)
and a point
z
of the Julia set is called
biaccessible
if it is the landing point of (at least) two external rays. That is, there exists
s\neq t
such that
z=\gamma_{F}(s)=\gamma_{F}(t)
.

2.2.2.
Parameter Limbs

We will also need the notion of limbs in the parameter space. For a map
F_{a}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
, then similarly to the above, there exists a unique conformal isomorphism
Böt
on a neighbourhood of
a
conjugating
F_{a}
to the map
w\mapsto w^{2}
. By Lemma 3.6 in
[
14
]
,
Böt
can be analytically continued to a well-defined holomorphic function on a neighbourhood of the free critical point
-a
. We may then define a holomorphic function
\phi\colon\mathcal{H}_{0}\to\mathbb{D}
by
\phi(a)=\text{B\"{o}t}_{a}(-a)
. This map
\phi
then extends to a continuous map
\phi\colon\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S}_{1})\to\overline{\mathbb{D}}
such that if
F\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S}_{1})\setminus\mathcal{H}_{0}
then
\phi(F)=e^{2\pi it_{0}}
, where
t_{0}\in[0,1)
is the internal argument of the critical limb of
F
.

Let
t_{0}\in(0,1)
. We define the
(parameter) limb

\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{+}
to be the set

\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{+}=\{F_{a}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S}_{1})\mid\phi(F)=e^{2\pi it_{0}}\text{ and }\mathrm{Re}(a)>0\}

and analogously

\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{-}=\{F_{a}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S}_{1})\mid\phi(F)=e^{2\pi it_{0}}\text{ and }\mathrm{Re}(a)<0\}.

We will show that one can distinguish
\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{+}
from
\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{-}
by dynamical means in Lemma
3.1
. For
t_{0}=0
we define
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{+}=\{F_{a}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S}_{1})\mid\phi(F)=e^{2\pi i0}\text{ and }\mathrm{Im}(a)<0\}
and
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{-}=\{F_{a}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S}_{1})\mid\phi(F)=e^{2\pi i0}\text{ and }\mathrm{Im}(a)>0\}
. Note that by the
180^{\circ}
rotational symmetry of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
, we have
F_{a}\in\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{+}
if and only if
F_{-a}\in\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{-}
. The root point of a limb
\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{\pm}
is defined to be the unique point of
\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{\pm}\cap\partial\mathcal{H}_{0}
.

In much of this paper, we will have to deal with the special case
t_{0}=0
separately from other periodic internal arguments. Fortunately, most of the time the case for
t_{0}=0
can be proved by inspection. When such a consideration is made, it will be clearly indicated.

We will also need the notion of parameter rays in the complement of the connectedness locus. They are closely related to the concept of external rays, as the following definition shows.

Definition 2.6
.

We say a map
F_{a}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
belongs to the
parameter ray

\mathcal{R}(\theta)
if the external ray
R_{a}(\theta)
passes through the cocritical point
2a
in the dynamical plane of
F_{a}
.

If
\theta
is
m_{3}
-periodic, then the rays
\mathcal{R}(\theta-1/3)
and
\mathcal{R}(\theta+1/3)
land on a parabolic map. In this case the angles
\theta-1/3
and
\theta+1/3
are called
co-periodic
. The following properties of parameter limbs come from
[
14
]
(Compare also
[
4
]
for a more in-depth discussion). See Figure
1
for some examples of parameter limbs.

.

\mathcal{S}_{1}
. Internal rays of the main hyperbolic component
\mathcal{H}_{0}
are shown, as well as some associated limbs.

Proposition 2.7
.

Suppose
t_{0}
is periodic of period
q
under
m_{2}
. Then there are two external parameter rays
\mathcal{R}(\theta)
and
\mathcal{R}(\theta^{\prime})
, where
\theta=\frac{i}{3(3^{q}-1)}
and
\theta^{\prime}=\frac{i+1}{3(3^{q}-1)}
, which land at the root point of
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{\pm}
. Furthermore, for any map
F_{a}\in\mathcal{L}
, the internal ray of angle
t_{0}+\frac{1}{2}
in
U_{a}
, as well as the external rays
R_{a}(\theta)
and
R_{a}(\theta^{\prime})
land at a common pre-periodic point in
J(F_{a})
.

The above result implies the following.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
\theta=\frac{i}{3(3^{q}-1)}
and
\theta^{\prime}=\frac{i+1}{3(3^{q}-1)}
. If the two parameter angles
\mathcal{R}(\theta)
and
\mathcal{R}(\theta^{\prime})
land at the root point of
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{\pm}
, then
R_{a}(\theta+1/3)
and
R_{a}(\theta^{\prime}-1/3)
land at the root of the critical limb of any map
F_{a}\in\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

Let
F_{a}\in\mathcal{L}
. The landing point
z
of the internal ray of angle
t_{0}+\frac{1}{2}
in
U_{a}
has the same image as the landing point
r
of the internal ray of angle
t_{0}
in
U_{a}
. By definition,
r
is the root point of the critical limb of
F_{a}
. Since by Proposition
2.7

R_{a}(\theta)
and
R_{a}(\theta^{\prime})
land at

, then
R_{a}(3\theta)
and
R_{a}(3\theta^{\prime})
land on

. Thus one of the rays
R_{a}(\theta+1/3)
and
R_{a}(\theta-1/3)
must land on
r
, and similarly one of the rays
R_{a}(\theta^{\prime}+1/3)
and
R_{a}(\theta^{\prime}-1/3)
must land on
r
. But since the critical limb maps onto the whole filled Julia set, the difference between the angles of the rays landing on

must be greater than

. But the only choice of angles from the above options which achieve this are
\theta+1/3
and
\theta^{\prime}-1/3
. Thus
R_{a}(\theta+1/3)
and
R_{a}(\theta^{\prime}-1/3)
land on

.
∎

In the case where
t_{0}
is strictly preperiodic under
m_{2}
, the limb
\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{\pm}
consists of a single function

, which is the landing point of exactly one parameter ray. Furthermore, it follows from Theorem

that for this map

, the root

of the critical limb is the free critical point, and so the biaccessible points on

2.3.
Matings of postcritically finite polynomials

In this section we outline the mating construction. Much of the material here can be found in e.g
[
20
,
21
]
.

2.3.1.
Formal Mating

Let
f
and
g
be monic degree
d
polynomials. We define

\text{\textcopyright}=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\cdot e^{2\pi it}:t\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\},

the complex plane compactified with the circle of directions at infinity. We then continuously extend the two polynomials to the circle at infinity by defining

f(\infty\cdot e^{2\pi it})=\infty\cdot e^{2d\pi it}\quad\text{and}\quad g(\infty\cdot e^{2\pi it})=\infty\cdot e^{2d\pi it}.

Label this extended dynamical plane of
f
(respectively
g
) by
\text{\textcopyright}_{f}
(respectively
\text{\textcopyright}_{g}
). We create a topological
2
-sphere
\Sigma_{f,g}
by gluing the two extended planes together along the circle at infinity:

\Sigma_{f,g}=(\text{\textcopyright}_{f}\sqcup\text{\textcopyright}_{g})/\sim

where
\sim
is the relation which identifies the point
\infty\cdot e^{2\pi it}\in\text{\textcopyright}_{f}
with the point
\infty\cdot e^{-2\pi it}\in\text{\textcopyright}_{g}
. The
formal mating
is then defined to be the branched covering
f\uplus g\colon\Sigma_{f,g}\to\Sigma_{f,g}
such that

\displaystyle f\uplus g|_{\text{\textcopyright}_{f}}\,=

\displaystyle\,f\quad\textrm{and}

\displaystyle f\uplus g|_{\text{\textcopyright}_{g}}\,=

\displaystyle\,g.

2.3.2.
Topological Mating

A related concept to the formal mating is the topological mating. Let
f
and
g
be two monic degree
d
polynomials with locally connected Julia set. First, construct the space
\Sigma_{f,g}
as above, and define the
ray-equivalence relation

\approx
as follows. We denote by
\sim_{f}
the smallest equivalence relation on
\text{\textcopyright}_{f}
such that
x\sim_{f}y
if and only if
x,y\in\overline{R}_{f}(t)
for some
t
; the equivalence relation
\sim_{g}
is defined analogously. Now
\approx
is the smallest equivalence relation on
\Sigma_{f,g}
which is generated by
\sim_{f}
on
\text{\textcopyright}_{f}
and
\sim_{g}
on
\text{\textcopyright}_{g}
. The class
[x]
of a point
x\in\Sigma_{f,g}
will be called a
ray-equivalence class
.

Evidently, there is a surjection
K(f)\sqcup K(g)\to\Sigma_{f,g}/\approx
given by the composition

K(f)\sqcup K(g)\hookrightarrow\text{\textcopyright}_{f}\sqcup\text{\textcopyright}_{g}\to\Sigma_{f,g}\to\Sigma_{f,g}/\approx.

Furthermore, since
x\approx y
implies
(f\uplus g)(x)\approx(f\uplus g)(y)
, we can uniquely define the topological mating as the map
f\perp\!\!\!\perp g
so that

\textstyle{K(f)\sqcup K(g)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f\uplus g}
\textstyle{K(f)\sqcup K(g)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Sigma_{f,g}/\approx\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f\perp\!\!\!\perp g}
\textstyle{\Sigma_{f,g}/\approx}

commutes. Under favourable conditions, the topological space
\Sigma_{f,g}/\approx
is a topological 2-sphere, in which case we say that
f
and
g
are
topologically mateable
. In fact, this occurs if and only if the equivalence relation
\approx
is of Moore-type (see
[
12
]
). That is,
\approx
is a non-trivial closed relation such that each equivalence class is closed and no equivalence class separates the sphere
\Sigma_{f,g}
.

2.3.3.
Thurston’s Theorem

Let
F\colon\Sigma\to\Sigma
be an orientation-preserving branched self-covering of a topological
2
-sphere. We denote by
\Omega_{F}
the critical set of
F
and define

P_{F}=\bigcup_{n>0}F^{\circ n}(\Omega_{F})

to be the postcritical set of
F
. We say that

is postcritically finite if
|P_{F}|<\infty
. We call
F\colon\Sigma\to\Sigma
a
Thurston map
if it is a postcritically finite orientation-preserving branched self-covering of a topological
2
-sphere.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
F\colon\Sigma\to\Sigma
and
\widehat{F}\colon\widehat{\Sigma}\to\widehat{\Sigma}
be Thurston maps. An
equivalence
is given by a pair of orientation-preserving homeomorphisms
(\Phi,\Psi)
from
\Sigma
to
\widehat{\Sigma}
such that

•

\Phi|_{P_{F}}=\Psi|_{P_{F}}

•

The following diagram commutes:

\textstyle{(\Sigma,P_{F})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Psi}
\scriptstyle{F}
\textstyle{(\widehat{\Sigma},P_{\widehat{F}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\widehat{F}}
\textstyle{(\Sigma,P_{F})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi}
\textstyle{(\widehat{\Sigma},P_{\widehat{F}})}

•

\Phi
and
\Psi
are isotopic via a family of homeomorphisms

which is constant on
P_{F}
.

If there exists an equivalence as above, we say that
F
and
\widehat{F}
are
equivalent
. Note that in particular, a postcritically finite rational map
R\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
on the Riemann sphere is a Thurston map. Hence it is natural to ask when a general Thurston map is equivalent to a rational map. In particular, since the formal mating of two postcritically finite polynomials is itself a Thurston map, we can ask if a given formal mating is equivalent to a rational map. This requires the notion of a
Thurston obstruction
, which we define below.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
F
be a Thurston map. A
multicurve
is a collection
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\}
of simple, closed, non-peripheral curves such that each
\gamma_{i}\in\Gamma
is disjoint from each other
\gamma_{j}
and the
\gamma_{i}
are pairwise non-homotopic relative to
P_{F}
. A multicurve is called
F
-
stable
if for all
\gamma_{i}\in\Gamma
, all the non-peripheral components of
F^{-1}(\gamma_{i})
are homotopic relative to
P_{F}
to elements of
\Gamma
. If
\Gamma
is a multicurve (not necessarily
F
-stable), we define the non-negative matrix
F_{\Gamma}=(f_{ij})_{n\times n}
as follows.

f_{ij}=\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in F^{-1}(\gamma_{j}),\gamma^{\prime}\sim\gamma_{i}}\frac{1}{\deg F\colon\gamma^{\prime}\to\gamma_{j}}

where
\deg
denotes the degree of the map and
\gamma^{\prime}\sim\gamma_{i}
denotes that
\gamma^{\prime}
is homotopic to
\gamma_{i}
rel
P_{F}
. By standard results on non-negative matrices (see
[
9
]
), this matrix
(f_{ij})
will have a leading non-negative eigenvalue
\lambda
. We write
\lambda(\Gamma)
for the leading eigenvalue associated to the multicurve
\Gamma
.

The importance of the above is due to the following rigidity theorem. A proof can be found in
[
6
]
or
[
11
]
.

Theorem 2.11

(Thurston)
.

(i)

A Thurston map
F\colon\Sigma\to\Sigma
of degree
d\geq 2
with hyperbolic orbifold is equivalent to a rational map
R\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
if and only if there are no
F
-stable multicurves with
\lambda(\Gamma)\geq 1
.

(ii)

Any Thurston equivalence of rational maps
F
and
\widehat{F}
with hyperbolic orbifolds is represented by a Möbius conjugacy.

An
F
-stable multicurve with
\lambda(\Gamma)\geq 1
is called a
(Thurston) obstruction
. The condition that
F
has a hyperbolic orbifold is a purely combinatorial one; this condition can be checked by inspecting the dynamics on the union of the critical and postcritical sets of
F
. We remark that since the maps in this article have two fixed critical points, the respective orbifolds are guaranteed to be hyperbolic. For further details, see
[
6
]
. Following
[
20
]
, we will say that a Thurston obstruction
\Gamma
is
irreducible
if the associated matrix
F_{\Gamma}
is irreducible. The following result from
[
20
]
(see also
[
21
]
) shows that it suffices to search for irreducible obstructions instead of Thurston obstructions.

Proposition 2.12
.

(
[
20
]
, Lemma 3.5)
A Thurston map
F
with hyperbolic orbifold is not equivalent to a rational map if and only if
F
has an irreducible obstruction.

In this paper, we will mainly be concerned with a special kind of obstruction: Levy cycles.

Definition 2.13
.

A multicurve
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\}
is a
Levy cycle
if for each
i=1,\ldots,n
, the curve
\gamma_{i-1}
(or
\gamma_{n}
if
i=1
) is homotopic to some component
\gamma_{i}^{\prime}
of
F^{-1}(\gamma_{i})
(rel
P_{F}
) and the map
F\colon\gamma_{i}^{\prime}\to\gamma_{i}
is a homeomorphism. Furthermore

•

We say
\Gamma
is a
degenerate
Levy cycle if the connected components of
\Sigma\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{i}
are
D_{1},\ldots,D_{n}
and an additional component
C=\Sigma\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\overline{D}_{i}
, where each
D_{i}
is a disk and moreover for each
i
the preimage
F^{-1}(D_{i+1})
contains a component
D_{i+1}^{\prime}
which is isotopic to
D_{i}
relative to
P_{F}
and is such that
F\colon D_{i+1}^{\prime}\to D_{i+1}
is a homeomorphism.

•

A degenerate Levy cycle is called a
removable
Levy cycle if in addition, for all
k\geq 1
and all
i
, the components of
F^{-k}(D_{i})
are disks.

Note that a Levy cycle is an example of an irreducible obstruction. There is a close relationship between Levy cycles and periodic cycles of ray cycles, as given in the following theorem of Tan.

Proposition 2.14

(
[
20
,
21
]
)
.

Let
F=f\uplus g
. Then every Levy cycle
\Gamma
of
F
corresponds to a unique cycle of ray classes
[x_{0}],[x_{1}],\dotsc,[x_{n-1}]
(called the limit set of
\Gamma
) and finitely connected
X_{i}\subset[x_{i}]
for each
i
such that
F\colon[x_{i}]\to[x_{i+1}]
and
F\colon X_{i}\to X_{i+1}
are homeomorphisms. Moreover,
\Gamma
is isotopic to a tubular neighbourhood of
\bigcup_{i=0}^{n-1}X_{i}
. In particular

(i)

If
\Gamma
is degenerate, then each
X_{i}
is a tree and contains at least two elements of
P_{F}
.

(ii)

If
\Gamma
is not degenerate, each
X_{i}
contains a closed loop.

Conversely, if
[x]
is a periodic ray class which contains a closed loop or at least two postcritical points, then each boundary curve of a tubular neighbourhood of
[x]
generates a Levy cycle.

Corollary 2.15
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-removable degenerate Levy cycle of the mating
F=f\uplus g
of postcritically finite polynomials. Then there exists a ray class under the mating which contains two critical values of
F
.

Proof.

First, note that by the Riemann-Hurwitz formula, if
D
is a disk such that
F^{-1}(D)
is not a disk, then
D
must contain (at least) two critical values. Since
\Gamma
is not removable, there exists a disk component
\Delta
of
\Sigma\setminus\Gamma
and some
k
such that
F^{-k}(\Delta)
contains a non-disk component
C
.

By Proposition
2.14
, there exists some
i
such that
\partial\Delta
is isotopic to a tubular neighbourhood of
X_{i}
which is a subset of a periodic ray class
[x_{i}]
. Thus this tubular neighbourhood (isotopically) contains
\Delta
. We claim that there exists a preimage of
[x_{i}]
which contains two critical values of
F
. If not, then no preimage of
X_{i}\subset[x_{i}]
can contain two critical values of
F
. But by Riemann-Hurwitz, all preimages of a tubular neighbourhood of
X_{i}
would have to be disks. But then all preimages of
\Delta
would also be disks, which is a contradiction. Thus
[x_{i}]
must contain two critical values of
F
.
∎

We remark that the converse of the corollary is not true: it is possible that two critical values belong to the same ray class but the mating is not obstructed, see Example
4.7
. The following theorem from
[
18
]
indicates that all obstructions to matings of pairs of postcritically finite polynomials in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
are topological.

Theorem 2.16
.

Let
f
and
g
be monic postcritically finite polynomials in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
. Then any obstruction to the formal mating
f\uplus g
contains a Levy cycle.

Theorem
2.16
does not hold for general cubic polynomials: in
[
20
]
, the authors constructed a mating of two postcritically finite cubic polynomials which was obstructed, but this obstruction was not a Levy cycle. Both the polynomials had critical points of period
3
.

Theorem
2.16
allows us to immediately prove the following.

Corollary 2.17
.

Let
t_{0}
be strictly preperiodic under
m_{2}
and suppose
F_{a}
is the unique element of the limb
\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{\pm}
. If
F_{b}
is a postcritically finite polynomial in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and the mating
F_{a}\uplus F_{b}
is obstructed, then this obstruction contains a degenerate Levy cycle.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.16
, any obstruction of the mating contains a Levy cycle. By Proposition
2.14
, if the obstruction was non-degenerate Levy cycle, then there would have to be a periodic ray cycle containing a closed loop. But
F_{a}
has no biaccessible periodic points, so this is impossible.
∎

2.3.4.
Degenerate Matings

We now discuss a technique due to Shishikura
[
19
]
(and also
[
20
]
) which allows us to modify a branched covering with removable Levy cycles so that it is no longer obstructed. This allows us to deal with cases where two polynomials are topologically mateable, but the formal mating has a Thurston obstruction.

Let
F
be a Thurston map. We need to set up a partial ordering on the (possibly empty) set
\mathfrak{R}(F)
of isotopy classes of removable Levy cycles for
F
. First, recall from Definition
2.13
that if
\Lambda
is a removable Levy cycle, then the set
\Sigma\setminus\Lambda
consists of
n
disks
D_{1},\dotsc,D_{n}
and an additional component
C
, such that for each
j
, the preimage
F^{-1}(D_{j})
has a component
D_{j}^{\prime}
isotopic to
D_{j-1}
and the map
F\colon D_{j}^{\prime}\to D_{j}
is a homeomorphism. Also, by definition, for each
k
, all components of
F^{-k}(D_{1})
are disks. Define
\mathcal{D}(\Lambda)=\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}D_{j}
.

We now construct our partial order on
\mathfrak{R}(F)
. For two elements
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathfrak{R}
, we say
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}
if there exists representatives
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
of
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
respectively such that
\mathcal{D}(\Lambda_{1})\subset\mathcal{D}(\Lambda_{2})
. Define
\mathfrak{R}^{\prime}
to be the set of
\lambda\in\mathfrak{R}
which are maximal under the partial order and such that for any Levy cycle
\Gamma
, the geometric intersection number
\lambda\cdot\Gamma=0
(see e.g
[
7
, p. 28]
for the definition of geometric intersection number). In the case of matings, we have the following.

Proposition 2.18

(
[
20
]
)
.

Let
F=f\uplus g
be the formal mating of two postcritically finite polynomials. The set
\mathfrak{R}^{\prime}(F)
consists of boundary curves of tubular neighbourhoods of periodic cycles
\{[x_{1}],[x_{2}],\dotsc,[x_{m}]\}
of ray classes such that each
[x_{i}]
contains at least two points of
P_{F}
and no ray class in
\bigcup_{n\geq 0}F^{-n}([x_{i}])
contains a closed loop. Furthermore if
\Sigma/\approx
is homeomorphic to
S^{2}
, then all Levy cycles for
F
are removable.

The above result allows us to define (essential) mateability of two polynomials
f
and
g
in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
. This adapts the definition of mateability given in
[
20
]
(Compare
[
19
]
, where this was called the degenerate mating), which uses the notion of being
weakly equivalent
to a rational map. One can think of this weak equivalence as being obtained by shrinking (simultaneously) all removable Levy cycles of the branched covering
F=f\uplus g
to points. If the original two polynomials were topologically mateable, then this new branched covering will no longer have any Levy cycles.

Definition 2.19
.

We say that two postcritically finite polynomials
f,g\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
are
(essentially) mateable
if all irreducible obstructions for
F=f\uplus g
are removable Levy cycles.

Since the matings of postcritically finite polynomials in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
have hyperbolic orbifolds, the above definition is equivalent to the topological mating of
f
and
g
being Thurston equivalent to a rational map.

3.
Combinatorics of Limbs

In this section, we collect together some combinatorial properties of the parameter limbs defined in Section
2.2.2
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
t_{0}\neq 0
be periodic under
m_{2}
and suppose
F_{a}
belongs to a limb with internal argument
t_{0}
.

(i)

F_{a}\in\mathcal{L}^{+}_{t_{0}}
, if and only if
R_{a}(0)
lands on
\partial U_{a}
and
R_{a}(1/2)
lands on the critical limb of
F_{a}
.

(ii)

F_{a}\in\mathcal{L}^{-}_{t_{0}}
if and only if the ray of angle
R_{a}(1/2)
lands on
\partial U_{a}
and
R_{a}(0)
lands on the critical limb of
F_{a}
.

Proof.

Notice that for any
F_{a}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S}_{1})
there exists a fixed point for
F_{a}
on the boundary of
U_{a}
, and this point must be the landing point of either
R_{a}(0)
or
R_{a}(1/2)
. Thus to prove the first claim, it suffices to show that
R_{a}(1/2)
lands in the critical limb of
F_{a}
whenever
F_{a}\in\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{+}
. In this case, since
t_{0}\neq 0
, if
\theta<\theta^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{R}(\theta)
lands with
\mathcal{R}(\theta^{\prime})
at the root of the parameter limb
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{t_{0}}
then
\{\theta,\theta^{\prime}\}\subset(5/6,1/6)\subset\mathbb{T}
. By Corollary
2.8
, the
R_{a}(\theta+1/3)
and
R_{a}(\theta^{\prime}-1/3)
land on the root of the critical limb of
F_{a}
. But
\theta+1/3\in(1/6,1/2)\subset\mathbb{T}
and
\theta^{\prime}-1/3\in(1/2,5/6)\subset\mathbb{T}
, and so
\theta+1/3<1/2<\theta^{\prime}-1/3
. Hence
R_{a}(1/2)
must land in the critical limb of
F_{a}
. The proof for the second case is similar.
∎

In the special case that
t_{0}=0
, the external rays of angle
0
and
1/2
both land on
\partial U_{a}
at the root point of the critical limb of
F_{a}
. Together with their landing point, these rays separate the critical limb from the rest of Julia set.

Recall that for
F_{a}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
, we denote by
\beta(t)=\text{B\"{o}t}(e^{2\pi it})
the point on
\partial U_{a}
which is the landing point of the internal ray of angle
t
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
t_{0}\in\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
be periodic of period
p
under
m_{2}
and suppose
F_{a}
belongs to the limb
\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{\pm}
. Then the points
\beta(t_{0}),\,\beta(2t_{0}),\,\dotsc,\,\beta(2^{p-1}t_{0})
form a period
p
cycle under
F_{a}
on the boundary of
U_{a}
, and these points are each the landing point of two external rays. This periodic cycle will be called the
\alpha
-
periodic cycle
of
F
. The rotation number of the
\alpha
-periodic cycle is defined to be the rotation number of
t_{0}
under angle doubling.

The terminology is chosen to draw a parallel with the notion of the
\alpha
-periodic point of quadratic polynomials. By Proposition
2.7
, this periodic orbit persists within a limb. That is, if
\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{\pm}
is a limb with periodic internal argument, then for all maps in this limb, the internal and external rays landing on the
\alpha
-periodic cycle have the same angles. See Figure
2
for examples of two maps in the same limb. Thus when we refer to the rotation number of a limb
\mathcal{L}^{\pm}_{t_{0}}
, we mean the rotation number of the associated
\alpha
-periodic orbit. By the above discussion, this is well-defined. An equivalent definition is to call the
\alpha
-periodic cycle the unique periodic cycle of biaccessible points on the boundary of
U_{a}
.

.

\mathcal{L}_{2/3}^{+}
in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
. The rays (drawn by hand) landing on the
\alpha
-periodic cycle for both maps are shown. Note the angles of these rays are the same for both maps. The critical points are indicated by

\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{2/3}^{+})=\{\frac{1}{8},\frac{2}{8},\frac{3}{8},\frac{6}{8}\}
.

We remark that we do not define the notion of an
\alpha
-periodic cycle when the internal argument of the limb is strictly preperiodic. Let
t_{0}
be
m_{2}
-periodic and consider the limb
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{\pm}
. Denote by
\Theta(\mathcal{L})
the set of all arguments of external rays landing on the

-periodic cycle of the maps in

.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
t_{0}
be periodic under
m_{2}
.

(i)

If the orbit
t_{0}
under
m_{2}
has rotation number
\rho
, then
\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{\pm})
is a rotation set under
m_{3}
, with rotation number
\rho
. Furthermore,
\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{\pm})
has exactly two major gaps.

(ii)

If the orbit of
t_{0}
under
m_{2}
is not a rotation set, then
\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{\pm})
is not a rotation set and has exactly one major gap.

Furthermore, if
k
is the period of
t_{0}
under
m_{2}
, then for each
0\leq i\leq k-1
,
\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{\pm})
has a gap of length
\frac{3^{i}}{3^{k}-1}
. In particular, the two angles
\theta
and
\theta^{\prime}
for which the corresponding rays land at the root of the critical limb of

bound a major gap of length
\frac{3^{k-1}}{3^{k}-1}
.

Proof.

The proof follows easily from the fact that (external) rays cannot cross and Proposition
2.2
. For part (ii), the angles of the rays landing at the root of the critical limb bound the unique major gap. The existence of the gaps of the prescribed lengths follows from considering the forward images of the sectors bounded by the rays of angle
\theta
and
\theta^{\prime}
in Proposition
2.7
. Details are left to the reader.
∎

We see that if
\Theta(\mathcal{L})
is a rotation set, then it is made up of the union of two distinct cycles under
m_{3}
, each with the same rotation number as
\Theta(\mathcal{L})
. By Proposition
2.3
, such a set is completely described by its rotation number
p/q
and
s_{1}(X)
, the number of elements of
X
in
[0,1/2)
. Furthermore, since
s_{1}
must be odd, we get the following.

Lemma 3.4

(Compare Corollary 3.15,
[
22
]
)
.

Given a rotation number
\rho=p/q
, there exists exactly
q
rotation sets with rotation number
p/q
under
m_{3}
which are the union of two cycles.

Proof.

The admissible values of
s_{1}
are
1,3,\dotsc 2q-1
, and each is associated to a unique rotation set.
∎

We now show that each of these
q
rotation sets is realised as
\Theta(\mathcal{L})
for some limb
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{1}
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Suppose
A
is a rotation set under
m_{3}
which is the union of two cycles and
\rho(A)=p/q
. Then there exists a unique limb
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{t_{0}}
of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
such that
A=\Theta(\mathcal{L}^{+}_{t_{0}})
and a unique limb
\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}^{\prime}}^{-}
such that
A=\Theta(\mathcal{L}^{-}_{t_{0}^{\prime}})
.

We emphasise that
\Theta(\mathcal{L})
only consists of the angles of the external rays landing on the
\alpha
-periodic cycle for maps in
\mathcal{L}
. As noted above, the set
\Theta(\mathcal{L})
is the union of two distinct cycles
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
under
m_{3}
. If
F_{a}\in\mathcal{L}
, then each member of the
\alpha
-periodic cycle of
\mathcal{L}
is the landing point of two external rays
R_{a}(\theta_{1})
and
R_{a}(\theta_{2})
, where
\theta_{1}\in C_{1}
and
\theta_{2}\in C_{2}
. However, if
A=\Theta(\mathcal{L}^{+}_{t_{0}})=\Theta(\mathcal{L}^{-}_{t_{0}^{\prime}})
for some
t_{0},t_{0}^{\prime}\neq 0
, then the way the angles in
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are paired (in the sense that the associated external rays have a common landing point) must be different. Indeed, by Lemma
3.1
, the ray
R_{a}(1/2)
lands on the critical limb if
F_{a}\in\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}^{\prime}}^{+}
. Thus for
F_{a}\in\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}^{\prime}}^{+}
, if
\theta_{1}=\max(A\cap(0,1/2))
and
\theta_{2}=\min(A\cap(1/2,1))
, then
R_{a}(\theta_{1})
and
R_{a}(\theta_{2})
land together. However, for
F_{a}\in\mathcal{L}^{-}_{t_{0}^{\prime}}
, these rays cannot land together, since then they and their common landing point would separate
R_{a}(1/2)
from
\partial U_{a}
, which contradicts Lemma
3.1
.

Proof of Corollary
3.5
.

Since rotation cycles under
m_{2}
are uniquely determined by their rotation number, we see that there are exactly
q
limbs of the form
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{t_{0}}
for which the internal argument
t_{0}
belongs to the unique rotation set of
m_{2}
that has rotation number

. By Lemma
3.4
, there are
q
candidates for
\Theta(\mathcal{L})
for such a limb. So it is sufficient to prove that if
t_{0}\neq s_{0}
, then
\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{+})\neq\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{s_{0}}^{+})
.

Assume
t_{0}
belongs to a
m_{2}
-rotation set
X
. If
s_{0}
does not belong to an
m_{2}
-rotation set, or belongs to a rotation set with a different rotation number than
X
, then by Proposition
3.3
we must have
\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{+})\neq\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{s_{0}}^{+})
. So suppose
t_{0},s_{0}\in X
but
t_{0}\neq s_{0}
. Then the parameter rays landing at the root of
\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{+}
must be different than those landing at the root of
\mathcal{L}_{s_{0}}^{+}
. Hence, by Corollary
2.8
, the angles of the rays landing on the root of the critical limb of maps in
\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{+}
must be different from the angles of the rays landing on the root of the critical limb of maps in
\mathcal{L}_{s_{0}}^{+}
. But these angles bound a major gap for the rotation sets
\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{+})
and
\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{s_{0}}^{+})
respectively. Since the two rotation sets have different major gaps, it follows from Proposition
2.1
that
\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{+})\neq\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{s_{0}}^{+})
.

The proof for limbs of the form
\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{-}
is similar.
∎

Corollary
3.5
indicates that the dynamics of the limbs in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
which have a rotation number are “combinatorially full”, in the following sense. Given a rotation set

under

, which consists of two periodic cycles, then there exists a limb
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{S}_{1}
for which

. In fact, if one considers the moduli space
\mathcal{S}_{1}/\mathcal{I}
, where
\mathcal{I}
is the canonical involution
z\mapsto-z
(so that
\mathcal{S}_{1}/\mathcal{I}
is made up of affine conjugacy classes of cubic polynomials with a fixed critical point), then there is precisely one limb corresponding to each rotation set
A
of the form given above. This is analogous to the case for the Mandelbrot set; given a rotation set
A
under
m_{2}
, there exists a limb in the Mandelbrot set for which the external rays landing on the associated
\alpha
-fixed point is the set
A
. However, it should be noted that, in contrast to the Mandelbrot set, there are limbs of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
which don’t have an associated rotation number. It would be interesting to study the combinatorics of the sets
\Theta(\mathcal{L})
for these limbs. Petersen and Zakeri have studied the combinatorics of periodic orbits under
m_{d}
in
[
15
]
.

We will use the notation
-\Theta(\mathcal{L})
for the set
\{-\theta\mid\theta\in\Theta(\mathcal{L})\}
.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
be limbs in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
. We will say that
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
are
\alpha
-
symmetric
if
\Theta(\mathcal{L})=-\Theta(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
.

Note that the above definition only makes sense for the case where
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
have periodic internal argument. The importance of the above definition comes from the following result.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
be
\alpha
-symmetric limbs of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
. Then if
F_{a}
,
F_{b}
are postcritically finite polynomials such that
F_{a}\in\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
F_{b}\in\mathcal{L}_{2}
, then
F_{a}
and
F_{b}
are not mateable.

Proof.

Consider the ray class
R
containing the
\alpha
-periodic cycle of
F_{a}
. Since
\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{1})=-\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{2})
,
R
is also contains the
\alpha
-periodic cycle of
F_{b}
, and these points are the only elements of
J(F_{a})\cup J(F_{b})
belonging to
R
. The points of an
\alpha
-periodic cycle are biaccessible, so this ray class may be thought of a (not necessarily connected) finite graph where every vertex has (at least) two edges landing on it. Such a graph must contain a closed loop. Thus the ray equivalence relation separates the sphere, and so by Proposition
2.18
, there exists a non-removable Levy cycle for the mating.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

The notion of
\alpha
-symmetry again has close parallels to the quadratic case. More precisely, given a limb
\mathcal{L}
within the Mandelbrot set, one could define
\Theta(\mathcal{L})
to be the angles of the external rays landing on the
\alpha
-fixed point of maps in
\mathcal{L}
(compare Definition
3.2
and the following discussion). Then the condition that
\Theta(\mathcal{L})=-\Theta(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
is exactly the case when
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
are conjugate limbs of the Mandelbrot set. Thus by the result of Rees, Shishikura and Tan
[
21
]
, the mating of two quadratic postcritically finite polynomials is obstructed if and only if they belong to
\alpha
-symmetric limbs; compare Conjecture
4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a limb with periodic internal argument. In light of Proposition
3.7
, our goal is to now find combinatorial criteria for a limb
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
which guarantee that
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
are
\alpha
-symmetric. Since
\alpha
-symmetry is only defined for limbs with periodic internal argument, we will have to deal with limbs with strictly preperiodic internal arguments in a different manner. However, we remark that Theorem
4.5
answers the mateability question in this case.

Given any limb
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{1}
, we will say that the conjugate limb
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
is the set of maps
F_{a}
such that
F_{\overline{a}}\in\mathcal{L}
. In terms of internal arguments, if
t_{0}\neq 0
then the conjugate limb to
\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{\pm}
is the limb
\mathcal{L}_{-t_{0}}^{\pm}
. For
t_{0}=0
, the limbs
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{+}
and
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{-}
are mutually conjugate. In all cases, if the parameter ray
\mathcal{R}(\theta)
lands on the root of
\mathcal{L}
, then
\mathcal{R}(-\theta)
lands on the root of
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a limb of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
with periodic internal angle. Then
\mathcal{L}
and
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
are
\alpha
-symmetric.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{R}(\theta)
and
\mathcal{R}(\theta^{\prime})
(where
\theta<\theta^{\prime}
) land on the root of
\mathcal{L}
. Then
\mathcal{R}(-\theta)
and
\mathcal{R}(-\theta^{\prime})
land on the root of
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
. Hence by Corollary
2.8
, if
F_{b}\in\overline{\mathcal{L}}
then the rays
R_{b}(-(\theta^{\prime}-1/3))
and
R_{b}(-(\theta+1/3))
land on the root of the critical limb of
F_{b}
. But this means
-(\theta^{\prime}-1/3)
and
-(\theta+1/3)
bound a major gap of
\Theta(\overline{\mathcal{L}})
and in particular
\{-(\theta^{\prime}-1/3),-(\theta+1/3)\}\subseteq\Theta(\overline{\mathcal{L}})
. Taking forward iterates under
m_{3}
, it follows that
\Theta(\overline{\mathcal{L}})=-\Theta(\mathcal{L})
.
∎

In the special case where the internal argument is
0
, we have
\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{0}^{+})=\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{0}^{-})=\{0,1/2\}
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
F_{a}
and
F_{b}
be postcritically finite polynomials that lie in conjugate limbs of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
. Then
F_{a}
and
F_{b}
are not mateable.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
are conjugate limbs with periodic internal argument then
\Theta(\mathcal{L})=-\Theta(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
and so by Proposition
3.7
, the mating of
F_{a}
and
F_{b}
must be obstructed.

Now suppose
t_{0}
is strictly preperiodic and
F_{a}
is the unique element of
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}^{\pm}_{t_{0}}
. Then
F_{a}
is the landing point of a parameter ray
\mathcal{R}(\theta)
, and the dynamical ray
R_{a}(\theta)
lands on the cocritical point
2a\in J(F_{a})
. This implies that the rays
R_{a}(\theta+1/3)
and
R_{a}(\theta-1/3)
land on the free critical point
-a\in J(F_{a})
. Similarly if
F_{b}
is the unique map in
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
, then the rays
R_{b}(-(\theta+1/3))
and
R_{b}(-(\theta-1/3))
land on the free critical point
-b\in J(F_{b})
. But then the ray class under the mating containing the two critical points
-a
and
-b
contains a loop, and so the mating is obstructed.
∎

3.1.
Combinatorial data of a limb

We wish to prove the existence of complementary limbs for limbs whose internal argument has a rotation number. We will also give a combinatorial description of how a limb and a complementary limb are related.

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a limb of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
. Then
\mathcal{L}
has a complementary limb if and only if
\mathcal{L}
has a rotation number.

Proof.

First suppose
\mathcal{L}
does not have a rotation number. By Proposition
3.3
, the set
\Theta(\mathcal{L})
has only one major gap, bounded by the angles
\theta
and
\theta^{\prime}
. In the dynamical plane for a map
F_{a}\in\mathcal{L}
, this major gap is bounded by the external rays
R_{a}(\theta)
and
R_{a}(\theta^{\prime})
landing on the root point of the critical limb of
F_{a}
. Now suppose
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
is a limb with
\Theta(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=-\Theta(\mathcal{L})
. Then, since for any map
F_{b}\in\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
the rays landing at the root of the critical limb have to bound a major gap, we see that these rays must be angles
R_{b}(-\theta)
and
R_{b}(-\theta^{\prime})
. But then we see that
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
must be the conjugate limb to
\mathcal{L}
, and so
\mathcal{L}
has no complementary limb.

Now suppose
\mathcal{L}
has a rotation number. Then the set
\Theta(\mathcal{L})
has two major gaps and thus the set
-\Theta(\mathcal{L})
also has two major gaps and so is a rotation set. But by Corollary
3.5
, there are two limbs satisfying
\Theta(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=-\Theta(\mathcal{L})
. Since only one can be a conjugate limb, we see that
\mathcal{L}
must have a complementary limb.
∎

We provide a combinatorial description of parameter limbs which will allow us to find the complementary limb for any limb which has a rotation number. We first need to discuss the combinatorics of rotation sets under
m_{2}
. Let
t_{0}
belong to the unique rotation set
X_{p/q}
under
m_{2}
with rotation number
\rho=p/q\in\mathbb{Q}-\{0\}
. Write
X_{p/q}
in cyclic order as
\{t_{1},t_{2},\dotsc,t_{q}\}
, so that
0\in(t_{q},t_{1})
. Then there exists a unique
1\leq k\leq q
such that
t_{0}=t_{k}
. We call
k
the (combinatorial) position of
t_{0}
in
X_{p/q}
. We define the
combinatorial data
for the limb
\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{\pm}
to be the triple
(\rho,k,\pm)
.

Example 3.16
.

Consider the
m_{2}
-rotation set
X_{2/5}
with rotation number
\rho=\frac{2}{5}
. Then
X_{2/5}=\{5/31,9/31,10/31,18/31,20/31\}
and the combinatorial position of
18/31
is
4
. Thus the Limb
\mathcal{L}_{18/31}^{+}
has combinatorial data
\left(\frac{2}{5},4,+\right)
.

It is clear that, given a triple
\mathcal{D}=(p/q,k,\pm)
where
1\leq k\leq q
, there exists a unique limb
\mathcal{L}
which realises the combinatorial data
\mathcal{D}
. The combinatorial data above allows us to easily recognise complementary limbs. As an aside, we remark that if a limb has combinatorial data
(p/q,k,\pm)
then the conjugate limb has combinatorial data
(-p/q,q+1-k,\pm)
.

Lemma 3.17
.

Suppose the combinatorial position of
t_{0}
is
k
.

(i)

The number of elements in
\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{+})\cap[0,1/2)
is equal to
2k-1
.

(ii)

The number of elements in
\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{-})\cap[1/2,1)
is equal to
2k-1
.

Proof.

As usual, we only prove the first claim, since the proof of the second is similar. For
t_{0}=0
, the only possible position is
k=1
and the result follows since the only rotation set consisting of two cycles with rotation number
0/1
is
\{0,1/2\}
. Suppose
t_{0}\neq 0
has combinatorial position
k
and let
F_{a}\in\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{+}
. Then by Lemma
3.1
, the angles of the external rays
R_{a}(\theta)
,
R_{a}(\theta^{\prime})
landing on the root of the critical limb of
F_{a}
satisfy
\theta<1/2<\theta^{\prime}
. Since
\beta(0)
is the landing point of the ray
R_{a}(0)
, then for
1\leq i\leq k-1
, the two external rays landing on
\beta(t_{i})
lie in
[0,1/2)
, and for
i>k
, the angles of the external rays landing on
\beta(t_{i})
lie in
[1/2,1)
. The result follows.
∎

Lemma 3.18
.

Suppose
\mathcal{L}
is the limb with combinatorial data
(p/q,k,\pm)
. Then the limb with data
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=(-p/q,k,\mp)
is the complementary limb to
\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

The result clearly holds for
t_{0}
= 0. So suppose
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{+}
has data
(p/q,k,+)
with
p/q\neq 0
. The complementary limb
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
, which we know exists by Proposition
3.15
, must then satisfy
\Theta(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=-\Theta(\mathcal{L})
and so will have rotation number
-p/q
. By Corollary
3.5
, there are two limbs for which this equality holds. One must be the conjugate limb to
\mathcal{L}
, which is the limb
\mathcal{L}_{-t_{0}}^{+}
, and the other must be a limb
\mathcal{L}_{s_{0}}^{-}
for some
s_{0}
.

By Lemma
3.17
, there are
2k-1
elements in
\Theta(\mathcal{L})\cap(0,1/2)
. Thus the complementary limb
\mathcal{L}_{s_{0}}^{-}
must have
2k-1
elements in
\Theta(\mathcal{L}_{s_{0}}^{-})\cap(1/2,1)
. But again by Lemma
3.17
, this means the combinatorial position of
s_{0}
is
k
. Hence the complementary limb has data
(-p/q,k,-)
. The proof for a limb with data
(p/q,k,-)
is analogous.
∎

We give some examples of how we find complementary limbs using the above lemma.

Again we consider the limb
\mathcal{L}_{18/31}^{+}

\left(\frac{2}{5},4,+\right)

4.
Main results

We now are ready to state the main theorem. Recall that in the quadratic case, the culmination of work by Rees, Shishikura and Tan gave the following.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
21
]
)
.

Let
f
and
g
be postcritically finite quadratic polynomials in the Mandelbrot set. Then
f\perp\!\!\!\perp g
is obstructed if and only if
f
and
g
belong to conjugate limbs of the Mandelbrot set.

The work carried out so far means we can easily prove the following, which is a slightly stronger version of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
t_{0}
be
m_{2}
-periodic such that the associated orbit has a rotation number. Let
F_{a}
be a postcritically finite polynomial in the limb
\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{\pm}
. Then there exists a postcritically finite polynomial
F_{b}
which does not belong to the conjugate limb
\mathcal{L}_{-t_{0}}^{\pm}
such that
F_{a}
and
F_{b}
are not mateable.

Proof.

Since
t_{0}
has a rotation number under
m_{2}
, then by Proposition
3.15
, the limb
\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{\pm}
has a complementary limb
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
. Given
F_{a}\in\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{\pm}
, take
F_{b}
to be any postcritically finite map in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
. Then
F_{a}\uplus F_{b}
is obstructed.
∎

The proof of Theorem
1.1
follows immediately. We believe that
\alpha
-symmetry is also necessary, and so conjecture the following.

Conjecture 4.3
.

Let
f,g\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
be postcritically finite polynomials in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
. Then
f
and
g
are not mateable if and only if
f
and
g
belong to conjugate or complementary limbs in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
.

Thus, we conjecture that the mateability question in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
can be completely understood from the combinatorics of the
\alpha
-periodic cycles of the two polynomials, just as the mateability question for quadratic polynomials can be completely understood from the combinatorics of the rays landing on the
\alpha
-fixed points of the polynomials being mated. Numerical experiments, involving looking at various parts of the space of cubic rational maps which have two fixed critical points (for published examples, see
[
1
,
3
]
) provide strong evidence that this is the case.

In the quadratic case, the mating of
f
and
g
is obstructed if and only if the ray classes under the mating of the
\alpha
-fixed points of
f
and
g
coincide. The generalisation for the
\alpha
-periodic cycles does not hold: it is possible for the two
\alpha
-periodic cycles to belong to the same ray class (or a union of ray classes, each of which contains an element of both
\alpha
-periodic cycles), but for this ray class (or union of ray classes) not to contain a loop. In terms of the sets
\Theta(\mathcal{L})
and
\Theta(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
, this means that
\Theta(\mathcal{L})\cap-\Theta(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\neq\varnothing
but
\Theta(\mathcal{L})\neq-\Theta(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
. The reason is that
m_{2}
-rotation sets are uniquely defined by their rotation numbers; this is not the case for
m_{3}
-rotation sets.

.

\Theta(\mathcal{L})\cap-\Theta(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\neq\varnothing
but
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
are not

4.1.
Necessity of
\alpha
-symmetry for preperiodic internal arguments

In the case where the internal argument
t_{0}
of the limb
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{\pm}
is strictly preperiodic, we can prove that if the mating
F_{a}\uplus F_{b}
of postcritically finite
F_{a}\in\mathcal{L}
and
F_{b}\in\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
is obstructed, then
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
are
\alpha
-symmetric. The goal of this section is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
t_{0}
be strictly preperiodic under
m_{2}
and suppose
F_{a}
is the unique element of the limb
\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{\pm}
. If
F_{b}
is a postcritically finite polynomial in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
then
F_{a}
and
F_{b}
are not mateable if and only if
F_{b}
is the unique element of the conjugate limb
\mathcal{L}_{-t_{0}}^{\pm}
.

The following lemma contains important properties of the ray classes in the matings considered in the proof of Theorem
4.5
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
t_{0}
be strictly preperiodic under
m_{2}
and suppose
F_{a}
is the unique element of the limb
\mathcal{L}_{t_{0}}^{\pm}
. Let
F_{b}
be a postcritically finite polynomial in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
such that, in the mating
F_{a}\uplus F_{b}
, the free critical values
v_{a}
and
v_{b}
of
F_{a}
and
F_{b}
respectively belong to the same ray class
[v_{a}]
. Then:

(i)

if
R_{a}(\theta)
is the ray landing on
v_{a}
, then
R_{b}(-\theta)
lands on
v_{b}
.

(ii)

there exists an angle
\eta
such that
R_{a}(\eta)
lands on the free critical point
-a
of
F_{a}
and
R_{b}(-\eta)
lands on the free critical point
-b
of
F_{b}
.

(iii)

v_{b}
is the only point in the ray class
[v_{a}]
which belongs to
J(F_{b})
.

(iv)

the free critical points
-a
and
-b
of
F_{a}
and
F_{b}
respectively belong to the same ray class
[-a]
.

(v)

the ray class
[-a]
containing the two free critical points contains a closed loop if and only if there exist distinct
\eta
,
\eta^{\prime}
such that
R_{a}(\eta)
lands together with
R_{a}(\eta^{\prime})
on
-a
and
R_{b}(-\eta)
lands together with
R_{b}(\eta^{\prime})
on
-b
.

Proof.

(i)

Suppose
R_{F_{b}}(-\theta)
does not land on
v_{b}
. Since
R_{a}(\theta)
is the unique external ray landing on
v_{a}
, it follows that there must be a biaccessible point
z
in
[v_{a}]\cap J(F_{a})
. By assumption, the free critical point
-a
of
F_{a}
is not periodic, and so the ray class
[v_{a}]
maps forward homeomorphically under iterates of the mating. In particular, there is a forward iterate of
z
which is a biaccessible periodic point in
J(F_{a})
. But since
F_{a}
has no biaccessible periodic point, this is a contradiction.

(ii)

Let
R_{a}(\theta)
be the ray landing on
v_{a}
. By part (i),
R_{b}(-\theta)
lands on
v_{b}
. Consider the three preimages of
R_{a}(\theta)
under
F_{a}
. Two of these preimage rays must land on
-a
. Similarly, two of the three preimages of
R_{b}(-\theta)
must land on
-b
. By the pigeonhole principle, there must then exist some
\eta
such that
R_{a}(\eta)
lands on
-a
and
R_{b}(-\eta)
lands on
-b
.

(iii)

To see that
v_{b}
is the only element of the ray class that belongs to
J(F_{b})
, first observe that if there is another element of
J(F_{b})
in the ray class, then there must also be a biaccessible point of
J(F_{a})
in the ray class. Similarly to the above, we can then map the ray class forward and obtain a biaccessible periodic point in
J(F_{a})
. Again this is a contradiction.

(iv)

Suppose that
[-a]
and
[-b]
are distinct ray classes. Both of these ray classes map onto
[v_{a}]
by a degree
2
mapping, and so elements of
[v_{a}]
have at least four preimages under
F_{a}\uplus F_{b}
. But the mating has degree
3
, so this is a contradiction and so the ray classes
[-a]
and
[-b]
are equal.

(v)

If there exist
\theta
and
\theta^{\prime}
such that
R_{a}(\theta)
and
R_{a}(\theta^{\prime})
land on
-a
and
R_{b}(-\theta)
and
R_{b}(\theta^{\prime})
land on
-b
, then (the closure of) these rays form a closed loop within the ray class
[-a]
.

Now suppose that there is only one angle
\eta
such that
R_{a}(\eta)
lands on
-a
and
R_{b}(\eta)
lands on
-b
(there must be at least one such
\eta
by part (ii)). Then we see that both
-b
and
2b
both belong to
[-a]
, and these are the only two elements in
[-a]\cap J(F_{b})
, since
v_{b}
is the only element of
J(F_{b})
in
[v_{a}]
(part (iii)). Furthermore, suppose
z\in J(F_{a})\cap[-a]
is an element distinct from
-a
. We claim
z
is the landing point of only one external ray. For if not, similar reasoning as that used in part (i) would imply that a forward iterate of
z
is a biaccessible periodic point for
F_{a}
, which is impossible.

We now think of the ray class
[-a]
as a connected bipartite graph with parts
A=[-a]\cap J(F_{a})
and
B=[-a]\cap J(F_{b})=\{-b,2b\}
, and such that there is an edge between
x\in A
and
y\in B
if and only if there exists
t
such that
R_{a}(t)
lands on
x
and
R_{b}(-t)
lands on
y
. Suppose the number of vertices in this graph is
n
. Then since
B=\{-b,2b\}
,we see there are
n-2
elements of
A
. Since the graph is bipartite, the number of edges in the graph is equal to the sum of the degrees (in the graph theory sense) of the vertices in
A
. But
-a
has degree
2
, and it was shown above that each of the other
n-3
vertices in
A
has degree
1
. Thus there are
n-1
edges in the graph. Since a connected graph with
n
vertices is a tree if and only it has
n-1
edges, we see that the graph does not contain a closed loop.

∎

Proof of Theorem
4.5
.

If
F_{b}
is the conjugate map to
F_{a}
then the mating is obstructed by Proposition
3.10
.

To prove the converse, we will show that if
F_{b}
is not the conjugate map to
F_{a}
, then any obstruction to the mating
F_{a}\uplus F_{b}
is a removable Levy cycle. First note that by Corollary
2.17
, any obstruction to this mating must be a degenerate Levy cycle, and if this Levy cycle is not removable, it follows from Corollary
2.15
that there must be a ray class which contains both
v_{a}
and
v_{b}
, the free critical values of
F_{a}
and
F_{b}
respectively. Let
\mathcal{R}(\theta)
be the unique parameter ray landing on
F_{a}
. Then
R_{a}(\theta)
is the only ray landing on the cocritical point
2a\in J(F_{a})
. Taking the forward image, we see that
R_{a}(3\theta)
is the only ray which lands on the free critical value
v_{a}
of
F_{a}
and so
R_{a}(\theta-1/3)
and
R_{a}(\theta+1/3)
are the only rays landing on the free critical point
-a
. According to Lemma
4.6
(part (i)), since
v_{a}
and
v_{b}
to belong to the same ray class
[v_{a}]
, then
v_{b}
is the landing point of
R_{b}(-3\theta)
. This means that the cocritical point
2b
of
F_{b}
must be the landing point of one of
R_{b}(-\theta)
,
R_{b}(-(\theta-1/3))
or
R_{b}(-(\theta+1/3))
. Hence, if
v_{a}
and
v_{b}
belong to the same ray class, then
F_{b}
is equal to the landing point of one of
\mathcal{R}(-\theta)
,
\mathcal{R}(-(\theta-1/3))
or
\mathcal{R}(-(\theta+1/3))
. Furthermore, again by Lemma
4.6
(part (iii)), the only element of
J(F_{b})
in
[v_{a}]
is
v_{b}
, and the preimage of the ray class
[v_{a}]
contains a ray class
[-a]
which contains both free critical points (part (iv)).

If
F_{b}
is not the conjugate map to
F_{a}
, then
F_{b}
is not the landing point of
\mathcal{R}(-\theta)
. Thus it remains to show that if
F_{b}
is the landing point of
\mathcal{R}(-(\theta-1/3))
or
\mathcal{R}(-(\theta+1/3))
, then no ray class in the mating
F_{a}\uplus F_{b}
which contains a closed loop. In fact, it suffices to show that this is the case for the ray class
[-a]
which contains both
-a
and
-b
, since all other ray classes map homeomorphically onto their image, and the ray class
[v_{a}]
does not contain a closed loop by Proposition
2.14
. We will assume
F_{b}
is the landing point of
\mathcal{R}(-(\theta-1/3))
; the proof of the other case is similar. We may also assume
F_{b}
is not parabolic, since if
F_{b}
is a parabolic map then
v_{b}
does not belong to the Julia set of
F_{b}
.

Thus
R_{b}(-(\theta-1/3))
lands on the cocritical point
2b
and therefore the rays
R_{b}(-\theta)
and
R_{b}(-(\theta+1/3))
land on the free critical point
-b
. Since
R_{a}(\theta)
lands on
2a
and
R_{a}(\theta-1/3)
and
R_{a}(\theta+1/3)
are the only rays which land on
-a
, we see that there is only the angle
\eta=\theta+1/3
for which
R_{a}(\eta)
lands on
-a
and
R_{b}(-\eta)
lands on
-b
. By Lemma
4.6
(part (v)), the ray class does not contain a closed loop, and so
F_{a}
and
F_{b}
are mateable.
∎

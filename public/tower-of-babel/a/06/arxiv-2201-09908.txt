Non-local scalar fields in static spacetimes via heat kernels
Ivan Kolář

Abstract
We solve the non-local equation − e − ℓ 2 ​ □ ​ □ ​ ϕ = J {-e^{-\ell^{2}\square}\square{\phi}=J} for i) static scalar fields in static spacetimes and ii) time-dependent scalar fields in ultrastatic spacetimes. Corresponding equations are rewritten as non-local Poisson/inhomogeneous Helmholtz equations in compact and non-compact weighted/Riemannian manifolds using static/frequency-domain Green’s functions, which can be computed from the heat kernels in the respective manifolds. With the help of the heat kernel estimates, we derive the static Green’s function estimates and use them to discuss the regularity. We also present several examples of exact and estimated static/frequency-domain Green’s functions.

Introduction

Non-local exponential operators of the type
e^{-\ell^{2}\Box}
appear in physics in various contexts. They arise naturally in effective descriptions of the

Witten 1986

;
Gomis et al. 2001
;
Gomis et al. 2004
;
Kolar and Mazumdar 2020
;
Heredia and Llosa 2021

Since the non-linear non-local field equations of IDG or SFT/PST (in curved backgrounds) are extremely difficult, it seems very natural to first better understand linear non-local scalar field equations of the form

\boxed{-e^{-\ell^{2}\square}\square{\phi}=J}

in fixed but curved background spacetimes. This paper aims to develop methods for solving such an equation and to provide several examples of i) static scalar fields in static spacetimes and ii) time-dependent scalar fields in ultrastatic spacetimes. We show that the static space/time splitting together with the exponential form of the non-local operator enable us to find Green’s functions (and discuss their regularity) via the heat kernels. Furthermore, we try to highlight the importance of mathematical results on the heat kernels and their estimates in compact and non-compact (weighted/Riemannian) manifolds, which are perfectly suited for these non-local problems, but often overlooked by physicists.

This paper is structured as follows:
In Sec.
II
we discuss the static space/time splitting, which provides a link between 4-dimensional static spacetimes and 3-dimensional spaces with an additional density (weight).
In Sec.
III
, this viewpoint is utilized for the decomposition of the non-local exponential operator allowing for the representation through the heat kernels on weighted manifolds. In Sec.
IV
we review some exact formulas for the heat kernels and necessary mathematical results on the heat kernel estimates in non-compact weighted manifolds.
In Sec.
V
we solve the non-local equation for static scalar fields in static spacetimes by means of the exact and estimated static Green’s functions; we study their regularity and present several explicit examples.
In Sec.
VI
we solve the non-local equation for time-dependent scalar fields in ultrastatic spacetimes using exact frequency-domain Green’s functions and we also provide some explicit examples. In Sec.
VII
we conclude the paper with a summary and a discussion of our results.

Static space/time splitting

Wave operator

3

{\phi^{t}(\mathrm{x})=\bar{\phi}(t,\mathrm{x})}

{\bar{\square}:=\bar{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}\cdot\bar{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}}^{\sharp}}

\bar{\square}\bar{\phi}(t,\mathrm{x})=\big({-}w^{-2}\partial_{t}^{2}+\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}\big)\phi^{t}(\mathrm{x})\;,

\displaystyle\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}

\displaystyle:=\mathfrak{w}^{-1}{\mathrm{div}}\big(\mathfrak{w}\,{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}}^{\sharp}\big)=w^{-1}{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}\cdot\big(w\,{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}}^{\sharp}\big)

\displaystyle=\triangle+\big(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\log w\big)\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}^{\sharp}\;,

III
Non-local scalar field theory

The space/time splitting of the wave operator
\bar{\Box}
(and the Fourier transform in time) is especially useful in the study of non-local scalar fields. It allows us to rewrite the field equations in terms of operators
e^{\tau\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}}
, which can then be represented through the heat kernels on weighted manifolds.

III.1
Field equation

Consider a non-local theory of a scalar field
{\bar{\phi}\in F\bar{\mathcal{M}}}
in a fixed spacetime
(\bar{\mathcal{M}},\bar{\boldsymbol{g}})
sourced by
{\bar{J}\in F\bar{\mathcal{M}}}
,

\mathcal{S}[\bar{\phi}]=\int\limits_{\bar{\mathcal{M}}}\bar{\mathfrak{g}}^{1/2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\bar{\phi}e^{-\ell^{2}\bar{\square}}\bar{\square}\bar{\phi}+\bar{J}\bar{\phi}\right)\;,

(9)

where the non-local exponential operator can be understood either via the infinite sum of derivatives or via the spectral resolution
\bar{E}_{\bar{\lambda}}
of
-\bar{\square}
,

e^{\tau\bar{\square}}:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\tau^{k}}{k!}\bar{\square}^{k}:=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\!d\bar{E}_{\bar{\lambda}}\,e^{-\tau\bar{\lambda}}\;,\quad\tau\in\mathbb{R}\;,

(10)

provided that
(\bar{\mathcal{M}},\bar{\boldsymbol{g}})
admits such representations for certain spaces of functions. The parameter
{\ell>0}
is called the
(length) scale of non-locality
. The local theory is recovered in the limit
{\ell\to 0}
. By taking the functional derivative of
\mathcal{S}
with respect to
\bar{\phi}
, we may derive the field equation, which is of the of the non-local inhomogeneous wave equation,

-e^{-\ell^{2}\bar{\square}}\bar{\square}\bar{\phi}=\bar{J}\;.

(11)

Considering a static spacetime, we can rewrite this equation in the spirit of the previous section as

e^{\ell^{2}\left(w^{-2}\partial_{t}^{2}-\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}\right)}\big(w^{-2}\partial_{t}^{2}-\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}\big)\phi^{t}=J^{t}\;.

(12)

In this paper, we will study two special subcases, in which the operators in the argument of the exponential commutes,
{\big[w^{-2}\partial_{t}^{2},\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}\big]\phi^{t}=0}
:

i)

If the scalar field and the spacetime are both static, then (
12
) reduces to the
non-local Poisson’s equation
:

\boxed{-\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}\phi=e^{\ell^{2}\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}}J\;.}

(13)

ii)

If the scalar field is time-dependent but the spacetime is ultrastatic, then we can recast the equation (
12
) to the form of the
non-local inhomogeneous Helmholtz equation
(in frequency domain):

\boxed{-\big(\triangle+\omega^{2}\big)\hat{\phi}^{\omega}=e^{\ell^{2}\omega^{2}}e^{\ell^{2}\triangle}\hat{J}^{\omega}\;.}

(14)

Notice that we assumed that the non-local exponential operator can be inverted without affecting the solutions of the field equation
Barnaby and Kamran 2008a
;
Barnaby and Kamran 2008b
. Because of this the non-local operator with the (weighted) Laplace operator appears on the right-hand side with the positive sign in the exponent. As a consequence, its action can be formulated by means of the heat kernel (below).

It is vital to stress that the non-local exponential operators defined through infinite sums of derivatives, spectral representations, and the heat kernels may differ on certain (non-analytic) functions
Moeller and Zwiebach 2002
. Here, we assume that we work in a subspace of functions where all three definitions are equivalent (and the exponential operator is invertible). Explicit identification of such spaces is rather difficult and goes beyond the scope of this paper (see
Heredia et al. 2021
, for the discussion of this problem in the Minkowski spacetime). We will be quite sloppy in this regard and just write
F\bar{\mathcal{M}}
or
F\mathcal{M}
for such optimal spaces of functions.

III.2
Non-local operator via heat kernel

In curved spaces, it is very efficient to characterize the action of
e^{\tau\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}}
,
{\tau\geq 0}
, by means of the heat kernels. Consider the
heat/diffusion equation
on
{[0,\infty)\times\mathcal{M}}
for an arbitrary initial condition
{\Psi_{0}\in F\mathcal{M}}
,

\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}\Psi_{\tau}(\mathrm{x})=\partial_{\tau}\Psi_{\tau}(\mathrm{x})\;,

(15)

where
{\tau\geq 0}
is an evolution parameter. The solution is often expressed using the
heat kernel

{K_{\tau}\in F(\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M})}

Grigoryan 2009
,

\Psi_{\tau}(\mathrm{x})=:\langle K_{\tau}(\mathrm{x},\cdot),\Psi_{0}\rangle_{\mathfrak{w}}\;,

(16)

where we introduced the weighted inner product of two scalar fields
{f_{1},f_{2}\in F\mathcal{M}}
,

\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle_{\mathfrak{w}}:=\int\limits_{\mathclap{\mathrm{x}\in\mathcal{M}}}\mathfrak{w}(\mathrm{x})f_{1}(\mathrm{x})f_{2}(\mathrm{x})\;.

(17)

Let us point out that the heat kernel is always symmetric,
{K_{\tau}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})=K_{\tau}(\mathrm{y},\mathrm{x})}
, but we will not symmetrize it in many cases for brevity reasons.

Denoting the spectral resolution of the weighted Laplace operator
{-\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}}
by
{E_{\lambda}}
, we may write

e^{\tau\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}}:=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\!dE_{\lambda}\;e^{-\tau\lambda}\;.

(18)

Therefore the action of the non-local exponential operator
e^{\tau\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}}
on an arbitrary scalar field
{\Psi_{0}\in F\mathcal{M}}
can be equivalently characterized by means of the solution
\Psi_{\tau}
, i.e., diffused with parameter
\tau
,

e^{\tau\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}}\Psi_{0}=\Psi_{\tau}\;,

(19)

because the differentiation with respect to the parameter
\tau
,
{\partial_{\tau}e^{-\tau\lambda}=-\lambda e^{-\tau\lambda}}
, exactly reproduces (
15
). Comparing (
19
) with (
16
) we also obtain the distributional formula for the heat kernel

K_{\tau}(\cdot,\mathrm{y})=e^{\tau\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}}\delta_{\mathrm{y}}\;,

(20)

which also implies that
K_{\tau}
satisfies the heat equation in both variables. Here,
\delta_{\mathrm{y}}
is the Dirac delta distribution defined by

\langle\delta_{\mathrm{y}},f\rangle_{\mathfrak{w}}:=f(\mathrm{y})\;,

(21)

where
\langle T,f\rangle_{\mathfrak{w}}
is now understood as an action of the distribution (linear functional)
T
on the test function
f
. This bracket reduces back to the inner product (
17
) for distributions associated with ordinary functions. The set of operators
e^{\tau\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}}
,
{\tau\geq 0}
, is often referred to as the
heat semi-group
due to the identity

e^{\tau_{1}\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}}e^{\tau_{2}\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}}=e^{(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}}\;,

(22)

which translates to the following identity for the heat kernel

K_{\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{z})=\langle K_{\tau_{1}}(\mathrm{x},\cdot)K_{\tau_{2}}(\cdot,\mathrm{z})\rangle_{\mathfrak{w}}\;.

(23)

Let us also mention two important limits of the heat kernel
Grigor'yan 2006
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to 0}\tau\log K_{\tau}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{4}D_{\mathrm{xy}}^{2}\;,

(24)

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\frac{1}{\tau}\log K_{\tau}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{\textrm{min}}\;,

where
\lambda_{\textrm{min}}
denotes the bottom of the spectrum of
{-\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}}
. The former can be made more explicit if
D_{\mathrm{xy}}
is sufficiently small. Then
K_{\tau}
always approaches the heat kernel of the Euclidean 3-space
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\boldsymbol{g}_{\textrm{euc}})
(given by (
33
) below) multiplied by a positive function
{f\in F(\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M})}
,

K_{\tau}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})\sim f(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})K_{\tau}^{\textrm{euc}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})\;,\quad\tau\to 0\;.

(25)

If the manifold is compact, then
-\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}
has pure (non-negative) point spectrum
{\lambda_{k}}
,
{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
(counted with multiplicity). Moreover,
\lambda_{k}
can be sorted so that the sequence grows and
{\lambda_{k}\to\infty}
for
{k\to\infty}
. The corresponding eigenfunctions
{\psi_{k}\in F\mathcal{M}}
,

-\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}\psi_{k}=\lambda_{k}\psi_{k}\;,

(26)

form a complete orthonormal set on
F\mathcal{M}
with the inner product (
17
),

\langle\psi_{j},\psi_{k}\rangle=\delta_{jk}\;,

(27)

meaning that we can expand an arbitrary function
{f\in F\mathcal{M}}
as

f(\mathrm{x})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f_{k}\psi_{k}(\mathrm{x})\;,\quad f_{k}:=\langle f,\psi_{k}\rangle_{\mathfrak{w}}\;.

(28)

Furthermore, the minimum principle implies that the constant function is the only harmonic function on compact manifold. Due to the normalization (
27
) we find

\lambda_{0}=0\;,\quad\psi_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{V_{\mathfrak{w}}^{\mathcal{M}}}}\;,

(29)

where
V_{\mathfrak{w}}^{\mathcal{M}}:=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\mathfrak{w}
denotes the weighted volume of
\mathcal{M}
, which is finite because

and
\boldsymbol{g}
are bounded on
\mathcal{M}
(thanks to continuity). The action of the non-local exponential operator (
18
) reduces to the infinite sum

e^{\tau\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}}f=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{-\tau\lambda_{k}}f_{k}\psi_{k}\;,

(30)

which can be easily translated to a direct relation for the heat kernel if we expand
f
in
{e^{\tau\triangle_{\mathfrak{w}}}f(\mathrm{x})=\langle K_{\tau}(\mathrm{x},\cdot),f\rangle_{\mathfrak{w}}}
by means of (
28
) with (
27
),

K_{\tau}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{-\tau\lambda_{k}}\psi_{k}(\mathrm{x})\psi_{k}(\mathrm{y})\;.

(31)

The sum converges absolutely and uniformly for
{\tau\geq\varepsilon}
by Weierstrass
m
-test with the convergent majorant series
{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}m_{k}(\varepsilon)}
for any
{\varepsilon>0}

Grigoryan 2009
.

IV.2
Li–Yau estimate

It turns out that on a large number of non-compact weighted manifolds
(\mathcal{M},\boldsymbol{g},\mathfrak{w})
, the heat kernel can be globally estimated from both sides by (see
Grigor'yan 2006
and references therein)

K_{\tau}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})\asymp\frac{c}{V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},\sqrt{\tau})}\exp\left(-\tilde{c}\frac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}^{2}}{4\tau}\right)\;.

(39)

This formula is known as the
(weighted) Li–Yau estimate
. (The constant
\tilde{c}_{2}
in the upper bound can be taken arbitrarily close to
1
.) It was first proven for Riemannian manifolds
(\mathcal{M},\boldsymbol{g})
(compact or non-compact) with the
non-negative Ricci curvature
,
{\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{Ric}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\geq 0}
,
{\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\boldsymbol{T}_{0}^{1}\mathcal{M}}
in the seminal work
Li and Yau 1986
, where
{\boldsymbol{Ric}\in\boldsymbol{T}_{(2)}^{0}\mathcal{M}}
is the Ricci tensor. Following
Li and Yau 1986
, this result was extended to many other cases including weighted manifolds
(\mathcal{M},\boldsymbol{g},\mathfrak{w})
.

Such a generalization makes use of two alternative characterizations of weighted manifolds satisfying (
39
). The weighted Li–Yau estimate is, on one hand, fully equivalent to the
Harnack inequality
5
5

5

{\forall\mathcal{B}(\mathrm{x},\rho)}
,
{\forall\Psi_{\tau}>0}
solving the heat equation in the cylinder
{\mathcal{C}:=(0,\rho^{2})\times\mathcal{B}(\mathrm{x},\rho)}
,

\sup_{\mathrm{y}\in\mathcal{C}_{-}}\Psi_{\tau}(\mathrm{y})\leq c\inf_{\mathrm{y}\in\mathcal{C}_{+}}\Psi_{\tau}(\mathrm{y})\;,

where
{\mathcal{C}_{-}:=\big(\tfrac{\rho^{2}}{4},\tfrac{\rho^{2}}{2}\big)\times\mathcal{B}\big(\mathrm{x},\tfrac{\rho}{2}\big)}
and
{\mathcal{C}_{+}:=\big(\tfrac{3\rho^{2}}{4},\rho^{2}\big)\times\mathcal{B}\big(\mathrm{x},\tfrac{\rho}{2}\big)}
.

and, on the other hand, to the
Poincaré inequality
6
6

6

{\exists\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
:
\forall\mathcal{B}(\mathrm{x},\rho)
and
{\forall f\in F\mathcal{B}(\mathrm{x},\rho)}
,

\inf_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{{\mathcal{B}(\mathrm{x},\varepsilon\rho)}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathfrak{w}\,\big(f-s\big)^{2}\leq c\rho^{2}\int_{{\mathcal{B}(\mathrm{x},\rho)}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathfrak{w}\,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}f\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}^{\sharp}f\;.

.
together with the
volume doubling

V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},2\rho)\leq cV_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},\rho)\;.

(40)

The last condition has an important consequence called the

\frac{V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},\rho_{2})}{V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},\rho_{1})}\leq c\left(\frac{\rho_{2}}{\rho_{1}}\right)^{\kappa}\;,\quad\rho_{2}>\rho_{1}>0\;,

(41)

for some
{\kappa>0}
. Assuming that (
39
) holds for a non-compact Riemannian manifold, one can show (based on the equivalent characterizations above) that it holds also for its weighted counterpart whenever the weight function
w
is bounded,

w(\mathrm{x})\simeq 1\;,\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{M}\;.

(42)

In fact, this result can be further generalized to unbounded

{\mathrm{o}\in\mathcal{M}}
, called the
origin
, together with the notation

\displaystyle|\mathrm{x}|:=D_{\mathrm{xo}}\;,

\displaystyle V(\rho)

\displaystyle:=V(\mathrm{o},\rho)\;,

\displaystyle V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\rho)

\displaystyle:=V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{o},\rho)\;,

(43)

\displaystyle S(\rho)

\displaystyle:=S(\mathrm{o},\rho)\;,

\displaystyle S_{\mathfrak{w}}(\rho)

\displaystyle:=S_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{o},\rho)\;.

We say that
(\mathcal{M},\boldsymbol{g})
has

if there exists a constant
{c>1}

to
\mathrm{y}
that is fully contained within the annulus

where we denoted

. Moreover, conditions (

If

and

. Note that the unweighted volume growth is already constrained to

for non-compact Riemannian manifolds
{(\mathcal{M},\boldsymbol{g})}
of non-negative Ricci curvature according to the

(see e.g.

),
7
7

7

The upper bound

corresponds to the Euclidean-like volume growth, which can be achieved, for example, by any conformally deformed Euclidean geometries
{\boldsymbol{g}=\Omega^{2}\boldsymbol{g}_{\textrm{euc}}}

{V^{\mathcal{M}}=\infty}

Carron 2020

c_{1}\rho\leq V(\mathrm{x},\rho)\leq c_{2}\rho^{3}\;,

(46)

for sufficiently large

IV.3
Spherical symmetry

The problem simplifies considerably if we focus on certain
spherically symmetric weighted spaces
(with 3 Killing vectors). We define them as the weighted manifolds
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\boldsymbol{g}_{\mathrm{sym}},\mathfrak{s})
, where

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{g}_{\textrm{sym}}

\displaystyle:=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\rho\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\rho+\varrho^{2}(\rho)(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\vartheta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\vartheta+\sin^{2}\vartheta\,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\varphi\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\varphi)\;,

(47)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}

\displaystyle:=s\,\mathfrak{g}_{\textrm{sym}}^{1/2}=s(\rho)\varrho^{2}(\rho)\sin\vartheta\;d\rho d\vartheta d\varphi\;,

with
\varrho
and

\varrho(\rho)=\rho+O(\rho^{3})\;,\quad s(\rho)=1+O(\rho^{2})\;,\quad\rho\to 0\;.

(48)

Just described weighted manifolds are non-compact and have relatively connected annuli with the origin
{\mathrm{o}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}
set at
{\rho=0}
. The additional conditions (
48

\mathrm{o}
; in particular,
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\boldsymbol{g}_{\mathrm{sym}},\mathfrak{s})

(\mathbb{R}^{3},\boldsymbol{g}_{\textrm{euc}})

8
8

8

From the 4-dimensional viewpoint, these spaces correspond to the spherically symmetric static spacetimes (with 4 Killing vectors) with spatial sections diffeomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
, which approach Minkowski spacetime near the origin.
The coordinate
\rho
has the meaning of the geodesic distance in the radial direction
\boldsymbol{\partial}_{\rho}
from the origin,
{\rho=|\mathrm{x}|}
. The weighted volume and surface of a geodesic ball
\mathcal{B}(\mathrm{o},\rho)
are given by

(49)

Since the Weyl tensor vanishes identically in 3 dimensions, the Riemannian curvature is described purely by the Ricci tensor (and Ricci scalar),

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{Ric}_{\textrm{sym}}

\displaystyle={-}\tfrac{2\varrho^{\prime\prime}}{\varrho}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\rho\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\rho{+}\big[1{-}(\varrho\varrho^{\prime})^{\prime}\big](\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\vartheta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\vartheta{+}\sin^{2}\vartheta\,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\varphi\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\varphi)\;,

(50)

\displaystyle R_{\textrm{sym}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{2}{\varrho^{2}}\left(1-2\varrho\varrho^{\prime\prime}-\varrho^{\prime 2}\right)\;.

Let us study the weighted Li–Yau estimate (
39
) in spherically symmetric weighted spaces. As mentioned above, we first need to satisfy its unweighted version (with
w=1
). Recalling that it is met for spaces with non-negative Ricci curvature (and taking into account (
48
)), we may find the constraints on the first and second derivatives,

0\leq\varrho^{\prime}\leq 1\;,\quad\varrho^{\prime\prime}\leq 0\;,\quad\varrho(0)=\varrho^{\prime\prime}(0)=0\;,\quad\varrho^{\prime}(0)=1\;.

(51)

Consequently, in any space
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\boldsymbol{g}_{\textrm{sym}})
with
\varrho
given by (
51
), we can use the unweighted Li–Yau estimate. Following (
44
) (and considering (
48
)), the heat kernel in the corresponding weighted spaces
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\boldsymbol{g}_{\textrm{sym}},\mathfrak{s})
can be estimated by the weighted Li–Yau estimate, if we choose the weight function
s
such that

\begin{gathered}\int\limits_{c}^{\rho}\frac{d\tilde{\rho}}{\tilde{\rho}}s(\tilde{\rho})V(\tilde{\rho})\simeq s(\rho)V(\rho)\;,\quad\forall\rho>2c>0\;,\\
s(0)=1\;,\quad s^{\prime}(0)=0\;,\end{gathered}

(52)

where
{V(\rho)=4\pi\int_{0}^{\rho}\!d\tilde{\rho}\,\varrho^{2}(\tilde{\rho})}
. The constraints in (
52
) still offer many viable weight functions
s
. As a particular example, we can mention
{s(\rho)=C\rho^{\gamma}/V(\rho)}
for
{\rho>2c}
with
{C,\gamma>0}
with an appropriate extension to
{\rho=0}
.

Great simplification can be achieved if the spherical symmetry is further incorporated into the problem. Specifically, if the source
J
of our non-local equation is proportional to
\delta_{\mathrm{o}}
or if we only need the values of field at the origin
\phi(\mathrm{o})
, then it is sufficient to know just the heat kernel with one point centered at the origin
\mathrm{o}
. In these situations, we can use the heat kernel estimate

K_{\tau}^{\textrm{sym}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{o})\asymp\frac{\tilde{c}}{V_{\mathfrak{s}}(\sqrt{\tau})}\exp\left(-c\frac{|\mathrm{x}|^{2}}{4\tau}\right)\;,

(53)

which correctly approximates the heat kernel whenever
Grigor'yan 2006

V_{\mathfrak{s}}(\rho)\simeq\rho S_{\mathfrak{s}}(\rho)

(54)

holds for large
\rho
. Remark that (
54
) is always satisfied for a bounded range of
\rho
. Furthermore, if also
{V(\rho)\simeq\rho S(\rho)}
for sufficiently large
\rho
then necessarily
{V_{\mathfrak{s}}(\rho)\simeq s(\rho)V(\rho)}
,
{\forall\rho>0}
. Let us stress that (

IV.4
Mean curvature

The spherically symmetric weighted spaces described above can actually provide us with the heat kernel estimates even for spaces that are not spherically symmetric. Consider an arbitrary non-compact weighted manifold
(\mathcal{M},\boldsymbol{g},\mathfrak{w})

(55)

(weighted) mean curvature
of the geodesic 2-sphere

{\mathrm{x}=\mathrm{y}}
is still a consequence of (
25
). On the other hand, the non-local static Green’s function
G(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})
, converges absolutely and uniformly
{\forall\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y}\in\mathcal{M}}
. This follows from the relation to the majorant
m_{k}(\ell^{2})
in the convergence of
{K_{\ell^{2}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})}
, because

\sup_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y}\in\mathcal{M}}\Big|\tfrac{e^{-\ell^{2}\lambda_{k}}}{\lambda_{k}}\psi_{k}(\mathrm{x})\psi_{k}(\mathrm{y})\Big|\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}m_{k}(\ell^{2})\;.

(69)

V.2
Estimates and regularity

Returning to the non-compact case, we can study the regularity of (
62
) using the Li–Yau estimate leading to

G(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})\asymp\int\limits_{\ell^{2}}^{\infty}\!d\tau\,\frac{c}{V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},\sqrt{\tau})}\exp\left(-\tilde{c}\frac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}^{2}}{4\tau}\right)\;,

(70)

which we refer to as the
Gaussian estimate of
G
. As we will see shortly, it is possible to derive an even simpler estimate for the static Green’s function. If
{D_{\mathrm{xy}}\geq\ell}
, then we may write

G(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})\asymp\Bigg[\!\int\limits_{\ell^{2}}^{D_{\mathrm{xy}}^{2}}\!+\!\!\int\limits_{D_{\mathrm{xy}}^{2}}^{\infty}\Bigg]d\tau\frac{ce^{-\tilde{c}\frac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}^{2}}{4\tau}}}{V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},\sqrt{\tau})}\begin{cases}\leq\int\limits_{D_{\mathrm{xy}}^{2}}^{\infty}\!\!d\tau\frac{\frac{1}{3}c_{3}c_{2}+c}{V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},\sqrt{\tau})}\;,\\
\geq\int\limits_{D_{\mathrm{xy}}^{2}}^{\infty}\!\!d\tau\frac{ce^{-\tilde{c}/4}}{V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},\sqrt{\tau})}\;,\end{cases}\!

(71)

The upper bound of the first integral is a consequence of the volume doubling (
40
) and the reverse volume doubling (
41
),

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\ell^{2}}^{\mathclap{D_{\mathrm{xy}}^{2}}}\!d\tau\tfrac{ce^{-\tilde{c}\frac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}^{2}}{4\tau}}}{V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},\sqrt{\tau})}

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\mathclap{D_{\mathrm{xy}}^{2}}}\!d\tau\tfrac{ce^{-\tilde{c}\frac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}^{2}}{4\tau}}}{V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},\sqrt{\tau})}\leq\tfrac{cc_{1}\,D_{\mathrm{xy}}^{2\kappa}}{V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},D_{\mathrm{xy}})}\int\limits_{0}^{\mathclap{D_{\mathrm{xy}}^{2}}}\!d\tau\tfrac{e^{-\tilde{c}\frac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}^{2}}{4\tau}}}{\tau^{\kappa}}

(72)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{c_{2}D_{\mathrm{xy}}^{2}}{V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},D_{\mathrm{xy}})}\leq\tfrac{c_{3}c_{2}D_{\mathrm{xy}}^{2}}{V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},2D_{\mathrm{xy}})}

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{D_{\mathrm{xy}}^{2}}^{\mathclap{4D_{\mathrm{xy}}^{2}}}\!\!d\tau\tfrac{\tfrac{1}{3}c_{3}c_{2}}{V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},\sqrt{\tau})}\leq\int\limits_{D_{\mathrm{xy}}^{2}}^{\infty}\!\!d\tau\tfrac{\tfrac{1}{3}c_{3}c_{2}}{V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},\sqrt{\tau})}\;,

where we denoted
c_{2}:=cc_{1}\operatorname{E}_{2-\kappa}\left(\tilde{c}/4\right)>0
. (Here, the letter
\operatorname{E}
stands for the exponential integral function.) On the other hand, if
{D_{\mathrm{xy}}<\ell}
, then we obtain

G(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})\asymp\int\limits_{\ell^{2}}^{\infty}\!d\tau\frac{ce^{-\tilde{c}\frac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}^{2}}{4\tau}}}{V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},\sqrt{\tau})}\begin{cases}\leq\int\limits_{\ell^{2}}^{\infty}\!d\tau\frac{c}{V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},\sqrt{\tau})}\;,\\
\geq\int\limits_{\ell^{2}}^{\infty}\!d\tau\frac{ce^{-\tilde{c}/4}}{V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},\sqrt{\tau})}\;.\end{cases}

(73)

Putting everything together, we get

G(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})\simeq\int\limits_{\mathclap{\max^{2}\left(\ell,D_{\mathrm{xy}}\right)}}^{\infty}\!\frac{d\tau}{V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},\sqrt{\tau})}\;,

(74)

which generalizes the known formula for the estimate of
G_{\textrm{loc}}
from
Grigor'yan 2006
, where the bottom limit becomes
D_{\mathrm{xy}}^{2}
in the limit
{\ell\to 0}
. We call this expression the
volume estimate of
G
.

Based on (
74
), we can now discuss the regularity of the static Green’s function. We observe that
G
converges whenever the upper limit is finite,

\int\limits^{\infty}\!\frac{d\tau}{V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},\sqrt{\tau})}<\infty\;,

(75)

and the bottom limit is strictly positive

\max\left(\ell,D_{\mathrm{xy}}\right)>0\;,

(76)

because
V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},\sqrt{\tau})
is a positive non-decreasing function of
\tau
that vanishes for
{\tau=0}
. Note that only the condition (
76
) depends on the scale of non-locality, but (
75
) is independent of
\ell
. This reflects the fact that the non-locality introduced through the exponential operator affects only the short-distance behavior, while the long-distance asymptotic behavior far from the source remain the same.

The condition (
76
) is satisfied for
G_{\textrm{loc}}
only if
{\mathrm{x}\neq\mathrm{y}}
. As mentioned above,
G_{\textrm{loc}}
diverges at the coinciding points
{\mathrm{x}=\mathrm{y}}
. Of course, this is not the true for the non-local case where (
76
) is always satisfied since the scale of non-locality is positive,
{\ell>0}
. Indeed, the value
{\ell^{2}}
plays a role of the bottom cutoff in (
74
) (or (
70
)); it effectively regularizes the limit of coinciding points by
\int_{\ell^{2}}^{\infty}\!{d\tau}/{V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},\sqrt{\tau})}<\infty
provided that (
75
) is met.

The condition (
75
) is related to the weighted volume growth. Using the limit comparison test for improper integrals, we can find that it is satisfied, for instance, if
{V_{\mathfrak{w}}(\mathrm{x},\rho)\simeq\rho^{\eta}}
,
{\eta>2}
, for large
\rho
. This is still in accordance with the Li–Yau estimate if we take
{\eta=\alpha+\beta>2}
above.

Let us consider a spherically symmetric weighted space
{(\mathbb{R}^{3},\boldsymbol{g}_{\textrm{sym}},\mathfrak{s})}
with the Dirac delta point source located at the origin, i.e.,
{\delta_{\mathrm{o}}}
. The solution is then described by
G^{\textrm{sym}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{o})
. Following the same steps as before, but starting with (
53
), we can arrive at the Gaussian estimates of
G^{\textrm{sym}}
centered at
\mathrm{o}
,

G^{\textrm{sym}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{o})\asymp\int\limits_{\ell^{2}}^{\infty}\!d\tau\,\frac{\tilde{c}}{V_{\mathfrak{s}}(\sqrt{\tau})}\exp\left(-c\frac{|\mathrm{x}|^{2}}{4\tau}\right)\;,

(77)

and the volume estimates of
G^{\textrm{sym}}
centered at
\mathrm{o}
,

G^{\textrm{sym}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{o})\simeq\int\limits_{\mathclap{\max^{2}\left(\ell,|\mathrm{x}|\right)}}^{\infty}\!\frac{d\tau}{V_{\mathfrak{s}}(\sqrt{\tau})}\;.

(78)

Remark that the latter requires the volume doubling property
{V_{\mathfrak{s}}(2\rho)\leq cV_{\mathfrak{s}}(\rho)}
,
{\forall\rho>0}
, to hold.

The estimates (
77
) and (
78
) can be contrasted with the exact formula for the static Green’s function center at
\mathrm{o}
that is available in the local case,

G_{\textrm{loc}}^{\textrm{sym}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{o})=\int\limits_{|\mathrm{x}|}^{\infty}\frac{d\rho}{S_{\mathfrak{s}}(\rho)}\;.

(79)

We may verify (
79
) as follows:

\displaystyle\langle-\triangle_{\mathfrak{s}}G_{\textrm{loc}}^{\textrm{sym}}(\cdot,\mathrm{o}),f\rangle_{\mathfrak{s}}

\displaystyle=\langle\partial_{\rho}G_{\textrm{loc}}^{\textrm{sym}}(\cdot,\mathrm{o}),\partial_{\rho}f\rangle_{\mathfrak{s}}=-\!\int\limits_{\mathcal{M}}\frac{\mathfrak{s}\partial_{\rho}f}{S_{\mathfrak{s}}}

(80)

\displaystyle=-\!\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\!d\rho\;\partial_{\rho}f=f(0)=\langle\delta_{\mathrm{o}},f\rangle_{\mathfrak{s}}\;.

As before, we still get the convergence condition
{\eta=\alpha+\beta>2}
for the upper limit of (
78
) (or (
77
) with
{\mathrm{x}=\mathrm{o}}
) where
{\varrho\simeq\rho^{(\alpha-1)/2}}
and
{s(\rho)\simeq\rho^{\beta}}
. Calculating the Riemann tensor of the full 4-dimensional Lorentzian manifold
(\mathbb{R}^{4},\bar{\boldsymbol{g}}_{\textrm{sym}})
, we find that it vanishes asymptotically if

s=O(1)\;,\quad\varrho=\rho+O(1)\;,\quad\rho\to\infty\;.

(81)

Consequently, an asymptotic flatness of the static symmetric spacetime is sufficient for the convergence of
{G^{\textrm{sym}}_{\textrm{loc}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{o})}
,
{\mathrm{x}\neq\mathrm{o}}
and
{G^{\textrm{sym}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{o})}
,
{\forall\mathrm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}
.

Finally, let us also mention that the inequalities between the heat kernels (
58
) can be promoted to the inequalities between the corresponding static Green’s functions centered at
\mathrm{o}
,

G^{\textrm{sym}-}(\mathrm{x}^{-},\mathrm{o}^{-})\leq G(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{o})\leq G^{\textrm{sym}+}(\mathrm{x}^{+},\mathrm{o}^{+})\;.

(82)

We refer to them as the
plus–minus estimate
. Recall that
G
can be the Green’s function on an arbitrary weighted manifold with an origin that is missing its cut locus, provided that (
57
) are satisfied. Furthermore, the plus-minus estimate holds true even in the local case.

V.3
Example: Ext. & int. Schwarzschild spacetime

As a simple example, consider a static spacetime composed of the
exterior Schwarzschild
geometry glued to the
interior Schwarzschild
metric
Schwarzschild 1916
. In particular, we choose two constants
{0<\tfrac{9}{8}a<b}

{r=b}
is the matching surface,
{r=a}
is the Schwarzschild radius, and
{r=0}
is the origin
\mathrm{o}

Therefore, the metric for

\displaystyle\bar{\boldsymbol{g}}_{\textrm{sch}}^{\textrm{int}}

\displaystyle=-\left(\tfrac{3}{2}\sqrt{1-\tfrac{a}{b}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-\tfrac{r^{2}a}{b^{3}}}\right)^{2}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}T\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}T+\frac{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}r\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}r}{1-\frac{a}{b^{3}}r^{2}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+r^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\vartheta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\vartheta+\sin^{2}\vartheta\,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\varphi\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\varphi)\;,

\displaystyle=-\big(1-\tfrac{a}{r}\big)\,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}T\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}T+\frac{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}r\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}r}{1-\frac{a}{r}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+r^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\vartheta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\vartheta+\sin^{2}\vartheta\,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\varphi\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\varphi)\;.

Both geometries can be rewritten in the form

\displaystyle\bar{\boldsymbol{g}}_{\textrm{sch}}

\displaystyle=-s^{2}(\rho)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}t\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}t+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\rho\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\rho

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+\varrho^{2}(\rho)(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\vartheta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\vartheta+\sin^{2}\vartheta\,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\varphi\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\varphi)\;,

if we use the transformation of the radial coordinate

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle:=\xi(r^{2}):=\begin{cases}\frac{b^{3/2}}{\sqrt{a}}\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{a}}{b^{3/2}}r\right)\;,&r<b\;,\\

\displaystyle\xi_{0}

\displaystyle:=\tfrac{b^{3/2}}{\sqrt{a}}\sin^{-1}\left(\tfrac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}}\right)-b\sqrt{1-\tfrac{a}{b}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}-a\tanh^{-1}\left(\sqrt{1-\tfrac{a}{b}}\right)\;,

and rescale the temporal coordinate as

t:=T/s_{\infty}\;,\quad s_{\infty}:=1/\left(\tfrac{3}{2}\sqrt{1-\tfrac{a}{b}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\;.

(87)

The constant
\xi_{0}
was fixed from the continuity of the radial coordinate and the constant
s_{\infty}
was chosen so that
{s(0)=1}

\varrho
and
s
are given implicitly in terms of
\xi
,

\displaystyle\varrho^{2}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\xi^{-1}(\rho)\;,

(88)

\displaystyle s\big(\xi(r^{2})\big)

\displaystyle=s_{\infty}\times\begin{cases}\tfrac{3}{2}\sqrt{1-\tfrac{a}{b}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-\tfrac{a}{b^{3}}r^{2}}\;,&r<b\;,\\

Unfortunately,

\left(ar^{2}-\frac{5a^{3}}{8}\right)\tanh^{-1}\left(\sqrt{1-\frac{a}{r}}\right)\\

\displaystyle G^{\textrm{sch}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{o})

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{24\pi}\int\limits_{\mathclap{\max^{2}\left(\ell,|\mathrm{x}|\right)}}^{\infty}\tfrac{d\tau}{s\left(\xi(r^{2})\right)\left[\sqrt{\tau}\xi^{-1}(\sqrt{\tau})-\Xi(\xi^{-1}(\sqrt{\tau}))\right]}

(93)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6\pi}\int\limits_{\mathclap{\sqrt{\xi^{-1}\left(\max\left(\ell,|\mathrm{x}|\right)\right)}}}^{\infty}\!dr\,\frac{r\xi(r^{2})\xi^{\prime}(r^{2})}{s\big(\xi(r^{2})\big)\left[r^{2}\xi(r^{2})-\Xi(r^{2})\right]}\;.

V.4
Example: Ultrastatic universes

Consider spacetimes of the homogeneous isotropic
ultrastatic universes
of zero, negative, and positive spatial curvatures,

\bar{\boldsymbol{g}}_{\textrm{uni}}:=-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}t\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}t+\begin{cases}\boldsymbol{g}_{\textrm{euc}}\;,&\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}^{3}\;,\\

where the 3-dimensional metrics are given by (

, the

\displaystyle:=\int\limits_{\beta^{2}}^{\infty}\!d\tau\,\frac{e^{-\tau\gamma^{2}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4\tau}}}{(4\pi\tau)^{\frac{3}{2}}}=\frac{1}{4\pi\alpha}\Big[\tfrac{1}{2}e^{-\gamma\alpha}\operatorname{erf}\big(\tfrac{\alpha}{2\beta}{-}\gamma\beta\big)

{\ell=0.35\,\textrm{m}}
and
{A=B=1\,\textrm{m}}
for better qualitative comparison. The end point
{D_{\mathrm{xy}}=\pi\,B}
of the purple lines corresponds to longest geodesics between opposite poles on the sphere.

V.5
Example: Anti-de Sitter spacetime

Returning to non-compact manifolds, we would like to demonstrate that owing to (
82
), the result (
97
) (and its special case (
96
)) may actually serve as rather precise estimate for the static Green’s function centered at the origin,

G^{\textrm{hyp}-}(\mathrm{x}^{-},\mathrm{o}^{-})\leq G(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{o})\leq G^{\textrm{hyp}+}(\mathrm{x}^{+},\mathrm{o}^{+})\;.

(100)

Here, the two hyperbolic 3-spaces
{(\mathbb{R}^{2},\boldsymbol{g}_{\textrm{hyp}}^{\pm})}
differ by the constants
{A_{+}>A_{-}>0}
(including the special case
{A_{+}=\infty}
corresponding to the Euclidean 3-space
G^{\textrm{euc}+}
). This estimate is satisfied for an arbitrary weighted manifold
{(\mathcal{M},\boldsymbol{g},\mathfrak{w})}
whose origin does not have a cut locus and which mean curvature
M
satisfies (
57
). Compared to unspecified constants in previous estimates, the constants
A_{\pm}
can be computed directly.

Let us restrict ourselves to the spherically symmetric weighted space
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\boldsymbol{g}_{\mathrm{sym}},\mathfrak{s})
. Then the condition (
57
) takes the form

\tfrac{2}{A_{-}}\coth\big(\tfrac{\rho}{A_{-}}\big)\geq\frac{S_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\prime}(\rho)}{S_{\mathfrak{s}}(\rho)}\geq\tfrac{2}{A_{+}}\coth\big(\tfrac{\rho}{A_{+}}\big)\;.

(101)

Interestingly, this differential inequality can be solved exactly using Grönwall’s lemma
Gronwall 1919
, which implies that

S_{\mathfrak{s}}(\rho_{0})\frac{\sinh^{2}\big(\frac{\rho}{A_{-}}\big)}{\sinh^{2}\big(\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{A_{-}}\big)}\geq S_{\mathfrak{s}}(\rho)\geq S_{\mathfrak{s}}(\rho_{0})\frac{\sinh^{2}\big(\frac{\rho}{A_{+}}\big)}{\sinh^{2}\big(\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{A_{+}}\big)}\;,

(102)

with
{\rho>\rho_{0}>0}
. Taking the limit
{\rho_{0}\to 0}
, we obtain an inequality that holds for all
{\rho>0}
,

4\pi A_{-}^{2}\sinh^{2}\big(\tfrac{\rho}{A_{-}}\big)\geq S_{\mathfrak{s}}(\rho)\geq 4\pi A_{+}^{2}\sinh^{2}\big(\tfrac{\rho}{A_{+}}\big)\;,

(103)

where we used the fact that
{S_{\mathfrak{s}}(\rho_{0})/4\pi\rho_{0}^{2}\to 1}
for
{\rho_{0}\to 0}
as it follows from (
48
). Whenever
{S_{\mathfrak{s}}(\rho)=4\pi s(\rho)\varrho^{2}(\rho)}
satisfies these inequalities, we can use the estimate (
100
).

The previous considerations can be applied, for example, to the
anti-de Sitter spacetime
. If we write it in the global coordinates,

\displaystyle\bar{\boldsymbol{g}}_{\textrm{ads}}

\displaystyle=-\cosh^{2}\big(\tfrac{\rho}{A_{0}}\big)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}t\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}t+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\rho\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\rho

(104)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+A_{0}^{2}\sinh^{2}\big(\tfrac{\rho}{A_{0}}\big)(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\vartheta\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\vartheta+\sin^{2}\vartheta\,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\varphi\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\varphi)\;,

we can immediately see that it can be represented as the weighted hyperbolic 3-space
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\boldsymbol{g}_{\textrm{hyp}},\mathfrak{s})
with the weight function
{s(\rho)=\cosh({\rho}/{A_{0}})}
. The weighted volume and surface of geodesic balls are given by

\displaystyle S_{\mathfrak{s}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=4\pi A_{0}^{2}\sinh^{2}\big(\tfrac{\rho}{A_{0}}\big)\cosh\big(\tfrac{\rho}{A_{0}}\big)\;,

(105)

\displaystyle V_{\mathfrak{s}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi}{3}A_{0}^{3}\sinh^{3}\big(\tfrac{\rho}{A_{0}}\big)\;.

From these two expressions, we can see that the weighted volume and surface do not satisfy the condition (
54
), so we cannot use the estimate (
53
). Instead, let use (
100
) and denote
{Q:=A_{\pm}/A_{0}}
and
{\zeta:=\rho/A_{0}}
. The constants
A_{\pm}
are given by the properties of the function
{\sinh^{2}(\zeta)\cosh(\zeta)-Q^{2}{\sinh^{2}(\zeta/Q)}}
. Since it is purely negative if
{Q<\sqrt{2/5}}
and purely positive if
{Q>2/3}
, we may identify

A_{+}=\frac{2}{3}A_{0}\;,\quad A_{-}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{5}}A_{0}\;.

(106)

As before, for the comparison, we can find an exact expression for the local Green’s function centered at the origin using (
79
). Performing the integral, we arrive at the formula

G^{\textrm{ads}}_{\textrm{loc}}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{o})=\frac{\operatorname{csch}\Big(\frac{|\mathrm{x}|}{A_{0}}\Big)+\operatorname{gd}\Big(\frac{|\mathrm{x}|}{A_{0}}\Big)-\frac{\pi}{2}}{16\pi A_{0}}\;,

(107)

which reduces to the flat case
{1/4\pi|\mathrm{x}|}
for
{A_{0}\to\infty}
corresponding to the Minkowski limit of the anti-de Sitter spacetime. (Here
\operatorname{gd}
denotes the Gudermannian function.) The above results are depicted in Fig.
3
.

). Brown region describes the plus–minus estimate of the non-local static Green’s function (

) with (

). We set
{\ell/A_{0}=1/5}
.

VI
Frequency-domain Green’s functions

Let us proceed to time-dependent scalar fields but in ultrastatic spacetimes. Solutions of non-local equations such as (
11
) with time-dependent sources are significantly more challenging to find and rather rare in the literature (see, e.g.,
Frolov et al. 2015
;
Kolář and Boos 2021
). Moreover, it is well known that the spacetime Green’s function of the non-local wave operator
-e^{-\ell^{2}\bar{\square}}\bar{\square}
does not exists even for the Minkowski spacetime
Buoninfante et al. 2019
;
Heredia et al. 2021
. To overcome this issue we propose moving to the frequency domain and solving the non-local inhomomogeneous Helmholtz equation (
14
),

\boxed{-\big(\triangle+\omega^{2}\big)\hat{\phi}^{\omega}=e^{\ell^{2}\omega^{2}}e^{\ell^{2}\triangle}\hat{J}^{\omega}\;.}

Again, we employ the Green’s function method.

VI.1
Frequency-domain Green’s functions from heat kernels

First, we consider the non-compact manifold. We define the
frequency-domain Green’s function

\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\omega}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})
as a solution of the above non-local equation with the source
{\hat{J}^{\omega}=\delta_{\mathrm{y}}}
and the boundary condition
{\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\omega}\to 0}
for
{D_{\mathrm{xy}}\to\infty}
and
{\forall\omega\in\mathbb{R}}
. With the help of (
20
), we may write

-\big(\triangle+\omega^{2}\big)\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\omega}(\cdot,\mathrm{y})=e^{\ell^{2}\omega^{2}}e^{\ell^{2}\triangle}\delta_{\mathrm{y}}=e^{\ell^{2}\omega^{2}}K_{\ell^{2}}(\cdot,\mathrm{y})\;.

(108)

Since we work in the frequency domain, we allow for the complex solutions in general. However, it is clear that only the real part
\operatorname{Re}\hat{\mathsf{G}}
should depend on the scale of non-locality
\ell
. The imaginary part
\operatorname{Im}\hat{\mathsf{G}}
should be independent of
\ell
because it comprises of a purely homogeneous solution. This (local) imaginary part can be chosen so that the character of the solutions is either retarded or advanced. The solution with an arbitrary source
{\hat{J}^{\omega}\in F{\mathcal{M}}}
for each
{\omega\in\mathbb{R}}
is then given by

\hat{\phi}^{\omega}(\mathrm{x})=\langle\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\omega}(\mathrm{x},\cdot),\hat{J}^{\omega}\rangle\;.

(109)

By analogy with (
61
), we may also introduce the local frequency-domain Green’s function
\hat{\mathsf{G}}_{\textrm{loc}}^{\omega}
,

\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\omega}=e^{\ell^{2}\omega^{2}}e^{\ell^{2}\triangle}\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\omega}_{\textrm{loc}}\;,\quad-\big(\triangle+\omega^{2}\big)\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\omega}_{\textrm{loc}}(\cdot,\mathrm{y})=\delta_{\mathrm{y}}\;.

(110)

At this point, we would like to write an integral representation analogous to (
62
). Unfortunately, the naïve integral of
e^{\tau\omega^{2}}K_{\tau}
could have serious problems with the convergence even in the local case (whenever
{\omega^{2}>\lambda_{\textrm{min}}}
). However, the analyticity in
\omega
provides an interesting method of generating possible candidates for the frequency-domain Green’s functions
\hat{\mathsf{G}}
: First, we replace
{\omega^{2}\to-z^{2}}
in our non-local inhomogeneous Helmholtz equation turning it into the non-local screened Poisson equation. Then, we perform the integral

\int\limits_{\ell^{2}}^{\infty}\!d\tau\,e^{-\tau z^{2}}K_{\tau}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})\;,\quad z>0\;.

(111)

Finally, to get the formula for
\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\omega}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})
, we analytically continue the expression to
{z\to i\omega+0^{+}}
. Of course, the result obtained by this formal procedure must be always checked against definitions of the frequency-domain Green’s functions (
108
) and (
110
). Also, let us remark that we have chosen the sign so that it leads to retarded solutions. (The advanced solutions are obtained by replacement
{\omega\to-\omega}
.) Upon taking the limit
{\omega\to 0}
, we regain the formula for the static Green’s function (
62
).

Let us move on to the compact manifolds. Notice that the field equations impose constraints of vanishing source for frequencies
{\omega^{2}=\lambda_{l}}
, i.e.,
{\hat{J}_{l}^{\pm\sqrt{\lambda_{l}}}=\langle\hat{J}^{\pm\sqrt{\lambda_{l}}},\psi_{l}\rangle=0}
, see
Arfken 1985
. If
{\omega^{2}\neq\lambda_{k}}
,
{\forall k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, then we define the Green’s function with the source
{\hat{J}^{\omega}=\delta_{\mathrm{y}}}
,

\displaystyle-(\triangle+\omega^{2})\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\omega}(\cdot,\mathrm{y})

\displaystyle=e^{\ell^{2}\omega^{2}}e^{\ell^{2}\triangle}\delta_{\mathrm{y}}=e^{\ell^{2}\omega^{2}}K_{\ell^{2}}(\cdot,\mathrm{y})

(112)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}e^{-\ell^{2}(\lambda_{k}-\omega^{2})}\psi_{k}(\cdot)\psi_{k}(\mathrm{y})\;.

However, if
{\exists l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
:
{\omega^{2}=\lambda_{l}}
, then we have to modify the source in the definition of the frequency-domain Green’s function to respect the restrictions described above,
{\hat{J}^{\pm\sqrt{\lambda_{l}}}=\delta_{\mathrm{y}}-\psi_{l}(\cdot)\psi_{l}(\mathrm{y})}
,

\displaystyle-(\triangle+\lambda_{l})\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\pm\sqrt{\lambda_{l}}}(\cdot,\mathrm{y})

\displaystyle=e^{\ell^{2}\lambda_{l}}e^{\ell^{2}\triangle}\big(\delta_{\mathrm{y}}-\psi_{l}(\cdot)\psi_{l}(\mathrm{y})\big)

(113)

\displaystyle=e^{\ell^{2}\lambda_{l}}K_{\ell^{2}}(\cdot,\mathrm{y})-\psi_{l}(\cdot)\psi_{l}(\mathrm{y})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\neq l\end{subarray}}}^{\infty}e^{-\ell^{2}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l})}\psi_{k}(\cdot)\psi_{k}(\mathrm{y})\;.

Here, we used the equations (
20
), (
30
), and (
31
). Also, notice that the indexing set of the sum is a consequence of
{\hat{J}_{l}^{\pm\sqrt{\lambda_{l}}}=0}
. The solution for an arbitrary source
{\hat{J}^{\omega}\in F\mathcal{M}}
,
{\omega\in\mathbb{R}}
, satisfying
{\hat{J}_{l}^{\pm\sqrt{\lambda_{l}}}=0}
, then still reads

\hat{\phi}^{\omega}(\mathrm{x})=\langle\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\omega}(\mathrm{x},\cdot),\hat{J}^{\omega}\rangle\;.

(114)

Also, the relation to the local frequency-domain Green’s function remains unchanged,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\omega}=e^{\ell^{2}\omega^{2}}e^{\ell^{2}\triangle}\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\omega}_{\textrm{loc}}\;,

(115)

\displaystyle-\big(\triangle

\displaystyle+\omega^{2}\big)\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\omega}_{\textrm{loc}}(\cdot,\mathrm{y})=\begin{cases}\delta_{\mathrm{y}}\;,\phantom{{}-\psi_{l}(\cdot)\psi_{l}(\mathrm{y})}\;\;\omega^{2}\neq\lambda_{k}\;,\\
\delta_{\mathrm{y}}-\psi_{l}(\cdot)\psi_{l}(\mathrm{y})\;,\;\;\omega^{2}=\lambda_{l}\;.\end{cases}

Similar to (
68
), we can also derive

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\omega}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\ell^{2}(\lambda_{k}-\omega^{2})}}{\lambda_{k}-\omega^{2}}\psi_{k}(\mathrm{x})\psi_{k}(\mathrm{y})\;,\;\;\omega^{2}\neq\lambda_{k}\;,

(116)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\pm\sqrt{\lambda_{l}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\neq l\end{subarray}}}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\ell^{2}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l})}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}}\psi_{k}(\mathrm{x})\psi_{k}(\mathrm{y})+C_{l}\psi_{l}(\mathrm{x})\psi_{l}(\mathrm{y})\;,

either from the corresponding integrals of
K_{\tau}
or by the spectral expansion of the field equation. Here,
C_{l}
are arbitrary constants that characterize the freedom in the homogeneous part of the solution due to
{-\triangle\psi_{l}=\lambda_{l}\psi_{l}}
. They will enable us not only to prescribe a certain value of the field at a given point but also to achieve a desired character of the solutions (retarded/advanced). Notice that the constants
C_{l}
are absent for
{\omega^{2}\neq\lambda_{k}}
because the equation admits no homogeneous solutions beyond the eigenfunctions. This also means that such a Green’s function cannot produce retarded or advanced solutions. Let us also point out that the case
{\omega^{2}=\lambda_{l}}
nicely reproduces the static Green’s functions (
68
) as a special subcase
{l=0}
in contrast to the frequency-domain Green’s functions with
{\omega^{2}\neq\lambda_{k}}
, which tend to blow up for
{\omega^{2}\to\lambda_{k}}
. Finally, it is not difficult to realize that the two formulas in (
116
) are actually related through the limit (see
Szmytkowski 2007
, for the local case),

\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\pm\sqrt{\lambda_{l}}}\big|_{C_{l}=0}=\lim_{\omega^{2}\to\lambda_{l}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega^{2}}\left[\frac{\omega^{2}-\lambda_{l}}{e^{-\ell^{2}(\lambda_{l}-\omega^{2})}}\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\omega}\right]\;.

(117)

VI.2
Example: Minkowski spacetime

Let us start with the Minkowski spacetime. The frequency-domain Green’s function can be obtained by performing the integral (
111
) with the heat kernel (
33
). It leads to the integral that we previously denoted
H(z)
,
{z>0}
, in (
98
). This function can be analytically extended to all
{z\in\mathbb{C}}
. Evaluating
H(i\omega)
, we get the retarded frequency-domain Green’s functions,

\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\textrm{euc}}=H_{D_{\mathrm{xy}},\ell}(i\omega)\;,\quad\hat{\mathsf{G}}_{\textrm{loc}}^{\textrm{euc}}=H_{D_{\mathrm{xy}},0}(i\omega)\;.

(118)

Of course, we should not just blindly rely on the analytic continuation of a diverging integral. However, the correctness of (
118
) can be easily verified by insertion to the left-hand side of (
108
). By realizing that the exponential and error functions commute with the complex conjugations, we can write real and imaginary parts of (
118
) explicitly as

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\textrm{euc}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\operatorname{Re}\left[e^{i\omega D_{\mathrm{xy}}}\operatorname{erf}\left(\tfrac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}}{2\ell}+i\omega\ell\right)\right]}{4\pi D_{\mathrm{xy}}}\;,

(119)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\textrm{euc}}_{\textrm{loc}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\cos\left(\omega D_{\mathrm{xy}}\right)}{4\pi D_{\mathrm{xy}}}\;,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\textrm{euc}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Im}\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\textrm{euc}}_{\textrm{loc}}=-\frac{\sin\left(\omega D_{\mathrm{xy}}\right)}{4\pi D_{\mathrm{xy}}}\;.

Here, we can clearly see that the imaginary part is independent of the scale of non-locality and bounded
{\forall\omega\in\mathbb{R}}
. By contrast, the real part blows up
{|\omega|\to\infty}
in the non-local case. Because of this the spacetime Green’s function of
-e^{-\ell^{2}\bar{\square}}\bar{\square}
, i.e., the inverse Fourier transform of
\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\omega}/\sqrt{2\pi}
, does not exist in the non-local case. (In the local case, the spacetime Green’s function of
-\bar{\square}
is
{\delta(t-\tilde{t}-D_{\mathrm{xy}})/4\pi D_{\mathrm{xy}}}
.) The graphs of the obtained frequency-domain Green’s functions are shown in Fig.
4
. One can check that the limit
{\omega\to 0}
reproduces the static Green’s functions (
96
).

The frequency-domain Green’s functions allow us to compute retarded solutions for various sources. The simplest time-dependent example, which was studied in
Frolov and Zelnikov 2016b
using a different method, is the point-like monochromatic emitter,
{J^{t}=J_{0}\cos(\Omega t)\delta_{\mathrm{y}}}
. Here,
{J_{0}}
and
\Omega
are two real positive constants. In the frequency domain, this source reads

\hat{J}^{\omega}=\sqrt{\tfrac{\pi}{2}}J_{0}\left[\delta(\omega-\Omega)+\delta(\omega+\Omega)\right]\delta_{\mathrm{y}}\;.

(120)

Employing (
114
), we can write the solution as

\hat{\phi}^{\omega}=\sqrt{\tfrac{\pi}{2}}J_{0}\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\omega}\left[\delta(\omega-\Omega)+\delta(\omega+\Omega)\right]\;.

(121)

In the time domain, thanks to the (anti-)symmetry in
\omega
,
{\operatorname{Re}\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\omega}=\operatorname{Re}\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{-\omega}}
and
{\operatorname{Im}\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\omega}=-\operatorname{Im}\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\omega}}
, it takes the form

\phi^{t}=J_{0}\big[\operatorname{Re}\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\Omega}\cos(\Omega t)-\operatorname{Im}\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\Omega}\sin(\Omega t)\big]\;,

(122)

which reduces to
J_{0}\cos[\Omega(D_{\mathrm{xy}}-t)]/4\pi D_{\mathrm{xy}}
in the local case. In
Frolov and Zelnikov 2016b
, this result was derived through the 4-dimensional Fourier transform leading to the principal value integral in the form of the Hilbert transform. The heat kernel approach presented here is more a direct method. Unlike the 4-dimensional Fourier transform, it can be used also in the curved (ultrastatic) spacetimes.

VI.3
Example: Hyperbolic universe

Finding the frequency-domain Green’s functions in the hyperbolic universe is very similar, since the integral (
111
) with the heat kernel (
35
) results in
H(\sqrt{z^{2}{+}1/A^{2}})
,
{z>0}
. Choosing the principal branch for the square root, we see that
\sqrt{z^{2}{+}1/A^{2}}
has a branch cut at
{\operatorname{Re}{z}=0}
and
{|\operatorname{Im}{z}|>1/A}
. Consequently, we can analytically continue the result to complex values with
{\operatorname{Re}z\geq 0}
and compute
H(\sqrt{(i\omega+0^{+})^{2}+1/A^{2}})
. This leads to the frequency-domain Green’s functions,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\textrm{hyp}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{{D_{\mathrm{xy}}}/{A}}{\sinh\left({D_{\mathrm{xy}}}/{A}\right)}H_{D_{\mathrm{xy}},\ell}\left(I/A\right)\;,

(123)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\textrm{hyp}}_{\textrm{loc}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{{D_{\mathrm{xy}}}/{A}}{\sinh\left({D_{\mathrm{xy}}}/{A}\right)}H_{D_{\mathrm{xy}},0}\left(I/A\right)\;,

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{\big|A^{2}\omega^{2}-1\big|}\times\begin{cases}-i\;,&A\omega<-1\;,\\
+1\;,&A|\omega|\leq 1\;,\\
+i\;,&A\omega>1\;,\end{cases}

which can be verified again by direct insertion into (
108
). If
{A|\omega|\leq 1}
, then (
123
) are real and qualitatively similar to the static Green’s functions, i.e., the red lines in Fig.
2
. The absence of the imaginary part means that these functions describe standing waves. Further, if
{A|\omega|>1}
, then the solutions have retarded character. The real and imaginary parts take the form,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\textrm{hyp}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\operatorname{Re}\left[e^{\frac{ID_{\mathrm{xy}}}{A}}\operatorname{erf}\left(\tfrac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}}{2\ell}+\frac{I\ell}{A}\right)\right]}{4\pi A\sinh\left(\frac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}}{A}\right)}\;,

(124)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\textrm{hyp}}_{\textrm{loc}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\cos\left(\omega\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{A^{2}\omega^{2}}}D_{\mathrm{xy}}\right)}{4\pi A\sinh\left(\frac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}}{A}\right)}\;,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\textrm{hyp}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Im}\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\textrm{hyp}}_{\textrm{loc}}=-\frac{\sin\left(\omega\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{A^{2}\omega^{2}}}D_{\mathrm{xy}}\right)}{4\pi A\sinh\left(\frac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}}{A}\right)}\;.

They have similar properties to the retarded frequency-domain Green’s function in the Minkowski spacetime
\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\textrm{euc}}
(with an additional exponential damping), to which they reduce for
{A\to\infty}
. The real part blows up for
{|\omega|\to\infty}
while the imaginary part remains bounded. These functions are depicted in Fig.
5
.

VI.4
Example: Einstein universe

We close this section with an example of the compact case. Considering the Einstein static spacetime, we can compute the frequency-domain Green’s function by means of (
116
). Recall that
-\triangle
on 3-sphere has a discrete spectrum with eigenvalues being the 3-dimensional spherical harmonics
\psi_{k,j}
satisfying the addition theorem (
38
). We arrive at the following results: If
{B^{2}\omega^{2}\neq k(k+2)}
,
{\forall k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, then

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\textrm{sph}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\frac{\ell^{2}}{B^{2}}\left({k(k+2)}-B^{2}\omega^{2}\right)}}{2\pi^{2}B}\frac{(k+1)^{2}}{{k(k+2)}-B^{2}\omega^{2}}

(125)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\times\tfrac{\sin\left((k+1)\frac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}}{B}\right)}{(k+1)\sin\left(\frac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}}{B}\right)}\;,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\textrm{sph}}_{\textrm{loc}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\operatorname{U}_{\sqrt{B^{2}\omega^{2}+1}-1}\left(-\cos\left(\frac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}}{B}\right)\right)}{4\pi B\sin\left(\pi\sqrt{B^{2}\omega^{2}+1}\right)}\;.

Here, the letter
\operatorname{U}
stands for the Chebyshev polynomial/function of the second kind. The solutions are real and describe standing waves. As we anticipated, they blow up for
{B^{2}\omega^{2}\to k(k+2)}
,
{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.

On the other hand, if
{\exists l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
:
{B^{2}\omega^{2}=l(l+2)}
, then we find

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\textrm{sph}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
k\neq l\end{subarray}}}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\frac{\ell^{2}}{B^{2}}\left(k(k+2)-l(l+2)\right)}}{2\pi^{2}B}\frac{(k+1)^{2}}{k(k+2)-l(l+2)}

(126)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\times\left[\tfrac{\sin\left((k+1)\frac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}}{B}\right)}{(k+1)\sin\left(\frac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}}{B}\right)}-\tfrac{(-1)^{k-l}\sin\left((l+1)\frac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}}{B}\right)}{(l+1)\sin\left(\frac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}}{B}\right)}\right]\;,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\textrm{sph}}_{\textrm{loc}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{\left(\pi-\frac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}}{B}\right)(l+1)\cot\left((l+1)\frac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}}{B}\right)+1}{4\pi^{2}B}\tfrac{\sin\left((l+1)\frac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}}{B}\right)}{(l+1)\sin\left(\frac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}}{B}\right)}\;,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Im}\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\textrm{sph}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Im}\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\textrm{sph}}_{\textrm{loc}}=-\operatorname{sgn}(B\omega)\tfrac{\sin\left((l+1)\frac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}}{B}\right)}{4\pi B\sin\left(\frac{D_{\mathrm{xy}}}{B}\right)}\;,

where we have chosen the real parts of constants
C_{l}
so that the real parts of the frequency-domain Green’s functions vanish for the longest geodesics,
{D_{\mathrm{xy}}=\pi B}
. Furthermore, the imaginary part of
C_{l}
have been set to achieve the retarded character (inspired by a formal replacement
{A\to iB}
in its hyperbolic counterpart (
124
)). Notice that the case
{l=0}
corresponds to the static Green’s function. Also, we recover
\hat{\mathsf{G}}^{\textrm{euc}}_{\textrm{loc}}
when we take the flat limit
{B\to\infty}
upon setting
{l=\sqrt{B^{2}\omega^{2}+1}-1}
. Finally, let us mention that the local expressions in (
125
) and (
126
) are in agreement with the results of
Szmytkowski 2007

VII
Conclusions

In this paper we discussed solutions of the linear scalar field equation modified by the non-local exponential operator
e^{-\ell^{2}\square}
. We discussed two separate cases: i) static scalar field in static spacetimes and ii) time-dependent scalar field in ultrastatic spacetimes. Rewriting the problem from the 3-dimensional viewpoint, we showed that the equation takes the form of non-local Poisson/inhomogeneous Helmholtz equations in compact and non-compact weighted/Riemannian manifolds. In the first case, we found solutions by means of the exact and estimated static Green’s functions, which can be derived from the heat kernels and their estimates. We also studied their regularity. In the second case, we introduced the frequency-domain Green’s functions, can be related to the heat kernels through the analytic continuation. Finally, we demonstrated the general techniques discussed in this paper on several examples (ext. & int. Schwarzschild, ultrastatic universes, anti-de Sitter).

Let us now go through possible extensions of our work and follow-up projects. The static and frequency-domain Green’s functions we obtained here can be directly applied in the study of exact and estimated solutions generated by other (physically motivated) sources and also extended to more interesting static curved spacetimes. In the future works, we would like to elaborate more on the relation between the frequency-domain Green’s function and the heat kernels to understand the analytic continuation better. In its current form, it only serves as a tool for generating possible candidates for the frequency-domain Green’s functions. Another interesting direction of research would be the application of the presented methods to non-linear problems, such as the perturbative treatment of IDG or SFT/PST with non-linear potentials. Finally, we would like to extend our results also to higher-order exponential operators such as
e^{(-\ell^{2}\square)^{N}}
,
{N\in\mathbb{N}}
, perhaps with the help of the recent work
Barvinsky et al. 2019
. As discussed in
Frolov and Zelnikov 2016b
;
Kolář and Boos 2021
, the operators with even powers of
\Box
are expected to remove issues with divergences for high frequencies that we also observed in the frequency-domain Green’s functions.

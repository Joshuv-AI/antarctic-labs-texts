Mokobodzki’s intervals: an approach to Dynkin games when value process is not a semimartingale


Abstract
. We study Dynkin games governed by a nonlinear 𝔼 f \mathbb{E}^{f} -expectation on a finite interval [ 0 , T ] [0,T] , with payoff càdlàg processes L , U L,U of class (D) which are not imposed to satisfy (weak) Mokobodzki’s condition – the existence of a càdlàg semimartingale between the barriers. For that purpose we introduce the notion of Mokobodzki’s stochastic intervals ℳ ⁡ ( θ ) \mathscr{M}(\theta) (roughly speaking, maximal stochastic interval on which Mokobodzki’s condition is satisfied when starting from the stopping time θ \theta ) and the notion of reflected BSDEs without Mokobodzki’s condition (this is a generalization and modification of the notion introduced by Hamadéne and Hassani (2005)). We prove an existence and uniqueness result for RBSDEs with driver f f that is non-increasing with respect to the value variable (no restrictions on the growth) and Lipschitz continuous with respect to the control variable, and with data in L 1 L^{1} spaces. Next, by using RBSDEs, we show numerous results on Dynkin games: existence of the value process, saddle points, and convergence of the penalty scheme. We also show that the game is not played beyond ℳ ⁡ ( θ ) \mathscr{M}(\theta) , when starting from θ \theta .

1.
Introduction

Let
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},\mathbb{P})
be a complete probability space,
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
denote
a Brownian motion on
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},\mathbb{P})
and
\mathbb{F}:=({\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
stand for the augmented filtration generated by

B
, i.e.
{\mathcal{F}}_{t}:=\sigma(B_{s}:s\in[0,t])\vee\mathcal{N}
, where
\mathcal{N}\subset{\mathcal{F}}
is the family of all
\mathbb{P}
-null sets.
We fix a terminal time
T>0
. Let
{\mathcal{T}}
denote the set of all
\mathbb{F}
-stopping times
\tau
such that
\tau\leq T

and for given
\tau\in{\mathcal{T}}
,
{\mathcal{T}}_{\tau}\subset{\mathcal{T}}
consists of
\sigma
satisfying
\sigma\geq\tau
.

1.1.
The concept of Mokobodzki’s intervals

Suppose that
L,U
are càdlàg
\mathbb{F}
-adapted processes of class (D) such that

L_{t}\leq U_{t},\quad t\in[0,T].

In the present paper we introduce the following notions: for given
\tau\in{\mathcal{T}}

we let

(1.1)

\Sigma_{\tau}(L,U):=\left\{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}_{\tau}:\exists\text{ c\`{a}dl\`{a}g semimartingale }X\text{ such that }L_{t}\leq X_{t}\leq U_{t},\,t\in[\tau,\sigma]\right\}.

Next, for any
\tau\in{\mathcal{T}}
, we let

(1.2)

\mathring{\tau}:=\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\{\sigma:\sigma\in\Sigma_{\tau}(L,U)\}.

Notice that the set
\Sigma_{\tau}(L,U)
consists of stopping times
\sigma\geq\tau
such that
Mokobodzki’s condition holds for
L,U
on
[\tau,\sigma]
.
As to the point
\mathring{\tau}
it can be thought of as a threshold time
after which Mokobodzki’s condition for
L,U
, when starting from the time
\tau
, ceases to be satisfied.
We prove that
\mathring{\tau}
is a stopping time.
However, the most compelling question is about the structure of
\mathring{\tau}
and
the behavior of barriers
L,U
at
\mathring{\tau}
. Clearly, we cannot expect, in general, that Mokobodzki’s condition
still holds on
[\tau,\mathring{\tau}]
.

1.2.
Main results I

We prove (see Theorem
6.3
) that there exists a (unique) set
\mathscr{C}_{\tau}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{\tau}
such that

(1.3)

\mathscr{M}(\tau):=[[\tau,\mathring{\tau}[[\,\cup\,[[\mathring{\tau}_{|\mathscr{C}_{\tau}}]]\subset\Omega\times[0,T]

is a maximal stochastic interval on which Mokobodzki’s condition is satisfied, i.e.

•

[[\tau,\sigma]]\subset\mathscr{M}(\tau)
for any
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\tau}(L,U)
;

•

there exists a non-decreasing sequence
(\tau_{k})\subset\Sigma_{\tau}(L,U)
such that

\bigcup_{k\geq 1}[[\tau,\tau_{k}]]=\mathscr{M}(\tau).

We call
\mathscr{M}(\tau)
Mokobodzki’s intervals. For those
\omega\in\Omega
for which
\mathscr{M}(\tau)

is closed, we say that Mokobodzki’s condition holds up to
\mathring{\tau}
.
As to the behavior of barriers
L,U
at
\mathring{\tau}
,
we prove (see Theorem
6.3
), among others, that

•

at the threshold time
\mathring{\tau}
the barriers "have to meet somehow":

L_{\mathring{\tau}-}=U_{\mathring{\tau}-}\,\text{ or }\,L_{\mathring{\tau}}=U_{\mathring{\tau}}\quad\text{on}\quad\{\tau<\mathring{\tau}<T\},

•

if Mokobodzki’s condition does not hold up to time
\mathring{\tau}
, then the distance between the barriers tends to
zero when approaching
\mathring{\tau}
from the left:

\bigcap_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\tau}(L,U)}\{\sigma<\mathring{\tau}\}\subset\{L_{\mathring{\tau}-}=U_{\mathring{\tau}-}\},

•

if the distance between the barriers, when approaching the threshold time
\mathring{\tau}
from the left, is bounded away from zero,
then Mokobodzki’s
condition holds up to
\mathring{\tau}
:

\{L_{\mathring{\tau}-}<U_{\mathring{\tau}-}\}\subset\mathscr{C}_{\tau}.

The above notion also may be applied to any
\mathbb{F}
-adapted càdlàg process
X
by taking the pair
(L,U)=(X,X)
.
Then
\mathring{\tau}
may be thought of as a threshold time
after which
X
, when starting from the time
\tau
, "ceases to be a semimartingale".

1.3.
Main results II: Dynkin games with nonlinear expectation

There is an extensive literature on (non-linear) Dynkin games
but the vast majority of it imposes Mokobodzki’s condition on barriers
L,U
,
i.e. existence of a càdlàg semimartingale
X
between
the barriers.
At this point, the nomenclature for Mokobodzki’s condition should be clarified.
What we call here
Mokobodzki’s condition
is often called in the literature
weak Mokobodzki’s condition
,
and
Mokobodzki’s condition
is frequently understood in the literature as
weak Mokobodzki’s condition

plus some integrability of the finite variation and martingale components
from the Doob–Meyer decomposition of
X
. In the present paper
we do not distinguish between these two concepts and we always understand Mokobodzki’s condition as the existence of
a càdlàg semimartingale between the barriers.
This comment is to alert the reader that
there are many papers that study Dynkin games "without Mokobodzki’s condition"
but in a sense that there is a semimartingale between the barriers anyway
(see e.g.
[
24
,
19
,
22
]
).
The reason why Mokobodzki’s condition is very often
assumed in the literature devoted to Dynkin games
is that it is equivalent to the value function being a semimartingale (see
V^{f}
defined below),
and this in turn allows the use of the very fruitful so-called
martingale method
.

The main motivation behind the introducing of the concept of
Mokobodzki’s intervals is to
show that suitably modified martingale method is also applicable to Dynkin games with barriers that do not satisfy
Mokobodzki’s condition or, equivalently, with the value process
V^{f}
that is not a semimartingale.

Consider
{\mathcal{F}}_{T}
-measurable random variable
\xi
, and
payoff
J(\cdot,\cdot)
that for given stopping times
\tau,\sigma\in\mathcal{T}
admits the form

(1.4)

J(\tau,\sigma)=L_{\tau}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau\leq\sigma,\tau<T\}}+U_{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{\{\sigma<\tau\}}+\xi\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau=\sigma=T\}}.

In the game under consideration player I chooses stopping time
\tau
, player II chooses stopping time
\sigma
,
and
J(\tau,\sigma)
represents the amount paid by player II to player I.
The basic problems of the theory are to study the existence and structure of (dynamical) saddle points, i.e.
families

\{(\tau^{*}_{\theta},\sigma^{*}_{\theta}),\,\theta\in{\mathcal{T}}\}
of stopping times such that for any
\theta\in{\mathcal{T}}
,

\mathbb{E}^{f}_{\theta,\tau\wedge\sigma^{*}_{\theta}}J(\tau,\sigma^{*}_{\theta})\leq\mathbb{E}^{f}_{\theta,\tau^{*}_{\theta}\wedge\sigma^{*}_{\theta}}J(\tau^{*}_{\theta},\sigma^{*}_{\theta})\leq\mathbb{E}^{f}_{\theta,\tau^{*}_{\theta}\wedge\sigma}J(\tau^{*}_{\theta},\sigma),\quad\tau,\sigma\in{\mathcal{T}}_{\theta},

where
\mathbb{E}^{f}
is the nonlinear expectation, and to study the existence, regularity and structure
of the value process
V^{f}
that aggregates, if this is the case, the family
of random variables
V^{f}(\theta):=\overline{V}^{f}(\theta)=\underline{V}^{f}(\theta)
(if the last equation holds we say that the game has value), where

(1.5)

\begin{split}\overline{V}^{f}(\theta)&:=\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}_{\sigma\in{\mathcal{T}}_{\theta}}\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}_{\tau\in{\mathcal{T}}_{\theta}}\mathbb{E}^{f}_{\theta,\tau\wedge\sigma}J(\tau,\sigma),\quad\underline{V}^{f}(\theta):=\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}_{\tau\in{\mathcal{T}}_{\theta}}\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}_{\sigma\in{\mathcal{T}}_{\theta}}\mathbb{E}^{f}_{\theta,\tau\wedge\sigma}J(\tau,\sigma).\end{split}

If there exists process
V^{f}

we say that the value is aggregable.
Recall that, by the definition, for a given progressively measurable function

f:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R},

stopping times
\tau\leq\sigma
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{\sigma}
-measurable random variable
X
,

\mathbb{E}^{f}_{\tau,\sigma}(X):=Y_{\tau},

where
(Y,Z)
is a unique solution to backward SDE

Y_{t}=X+\int_{t}^{\sigma}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})\,ds-\int_{t}^{\sigma}Z_{s},\,dB_{s},\quad t\in[0,\sigma].

Observe that if
f\equiv 0
, then
\mathbb{E}^{f}_{\tau,\sigma}(X)=\mathbb{E}(X|{\mathcal{F}}_{\tau})
.
We note at this point that the additional advantage of the present paper are the very weak assumptions on the data
(this is the benefit of the method used here).
We assume that

•

\xi
is integrable,
L,U
are of class (D),

•

f
is continuous and non-increasing with respect to the
y
-variable,
and Lipschitz continuous with respect to
z
variable,

•

f(\cdot,0,0)
is integrable,
f
admits strictly sub-linear growth with respect to
z
-variable and
no growth restrictions on
y
-variable (strict sub-linearity may be dropped in case
L,U\in\mathcal{S}^{p}(0,T)
for some
p>1
).

Based on the concept of Mokobodzki’s intervals, we extend the martingale method and prove the following results:

(1)

Dynkin game under nonlinear expectation
\mathbb{E}^{f}
has the value, i.e.

V^{f}(\theta):=\overline{V}^{f}(\theta)=\underline{V}^{f}(\theta)

for any
\theta\in{\mathcal{T}}
,
where
\overline{V}^{f}(\theta),\underline{V}^{f}(\theta)
are defined by (
1.5
),

(2)

the family
\{V^{f}(\theta),\,\theta\in{\mathcal{T}}\}
is aggregable and process
(V^{f}_{t})
that aggregates it is a semimartingale on
\mathscr{M}(\theta)
for any
\theta\in\mathcal{T}
,

(3)

under the condition

(1.6)

\Delta L_{t}\geq 0,\quad\Delta U_{t}\leq 0,\quad\quad t\in(0,T]

the pair
(\tau^{*}_{\theta},\sigma^{*}_{\theta})
is a saddle point for the game (
1.5
), where

\tau^{*}_{\theta}:=\inf\{t\geq\theta,\,Y_{t}=L_{t}\}\wedge T;\quad\sigma^{*}_{\theta}:=\inf\{t\geq\theta,\,Y_{t}=U_{t}\}\wedge T,

furthermore
V^{f}
is an
\mathbb{E}^{f}
-submartingale on
[\theta,\tau^{*}_{\theta}]
and an
\mathbb{E}^{f}
-supermartingale
on
[\theta,\sigma^{*}_{\theta}]
.

As a result we show that

\begin{split}V^{f}(\theta)&=\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}_{\theta\leq\sigma\leq\mathring{\theta}}\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}_{\theta\leq\tau\leq\mathring{\theta}}\mathbb{E}^{f}_{\theta,\tau\wedge\sigma}J(\tau,\sigma)=\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}_{\theta\leq\tau\leq\mathring{\theta}}\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}_{\theta\leq\sigma\leq\mathring{\theta}}\mathbb{E}^{f}_{\theta,\tau\wedge\sigma}J(\tau,\sigma)\\
&=\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\theta}(L,U)}\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}_{\tau\in\Sigma_{\theta}(L,U)}\mathbb{E}^{f}_{\theta,\tau\wedge\sigma}J(\tau,\sigma)=\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}_{\tau\in\Sigma_{\theta}(L,U)}\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,inf}}_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{\theta}(L,U)}\mathbb{E}^{f}_{\theta,\tau\wedge\sigma}J(\tau,\sigma).\end{split}

This means that both players, starting the game at
\theta
,
need for the optimal decisions only information about payoff processes
L^{\xi},U^{\xi}
up to the time
\mathring{\theta}
,
where
L^{\xi}=\mathbf{1}_{\{t<T\}}L_{t}+\mathbf{1}_{\{t=T\}}\xi
,
U^{\xi}=\mathbf{1}_{\{t<T\}}U_{t}+\mathbf{1}_{\{t=T\}}\xi
.
Observe that if
\mathring{\theta}<T
, then the players do not need information about
\xi
at all.
By (ii) we have that for any
\theta\in{\mathcal{T}}
the value process
V^{f}
is a semimartingale on

\mathscr{M}(\theta)
, i.e.
for any
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\theta}(L,U)

process
[0,T]\ni t\mapsto V^{f}_{(t\vee\theta)\wedge\sigma}
is a càdlàg semimartingale.
Consequently, by the Doob–Meyer decomposition, for any
\theta\in{\mathcal{T}}
there exist
càdlàg finite variation process
C^{[\theta]}
on
\mathscr{M}(\theta)
, with
C^{[\theta]}_{\theta}=0
,
and progressively measurable process
Z^{[\theta]}
on
\mathscr{M}(\theta)
such that

V^{f}_{t}=V^{f}_{\theta}+C^{[\theta]}_{t}+\int_{\theta}^{t}Z_{s}^{[\theta]}\,dB_{s},\quad t\in\mathscr{M}(\theta).

This enables us to define another dynamical saddle point for the game (
1.5
):
for any
\theta\in{\mathcal{T}}
let

(1.7)

\hat{\tau}_{\theta}:=\inf\{t\in\mathscr{M}(\theta):R^{[\theta],+}_{t}>0\},\quad\hat{\sigma}_{\theta}:=\inf\{t\in\mathscr{M}(\theta):R^{[\theta],-}_{t}>0\},

where

R^{[\theta]}_{t}:=-C^{[\theta]}_{t}-\int_{\theta}^{t}f(s,Y_{s},Z^{[\theta]}_{s})\,ds,\quad t\in\mathscr{M}(\theta).

The culmination of the concepts introduced in the paper is the following result (see Theorem
10.8
):
whenever
(\tau_{\theta},\sigma_{\theta})
is a saddle point for the game (
1.5
)
then

(1.8)

\tau_{\theta}\wedge\sigma_{\theta}\leq\hat{\tau}_{\theta}\wedge\hat{\sigma}_{\theta}

and
V^{f}
is a martingale on
[\theta,\tau_{\theta}\wedge\sigma_{\theta}]
.
The result gives a picture of the idea of the
martingale method

for non-semimartingale Dynkin games (i.e. games in which the value process is not a semimartingale).
It means that the game starting at time
\theta
is played no beyond
the point at which the barriers "cease to satisfy the Mokobodzki condition"
or, in other words, no beyond
the point at which the value process
V^{f}
"ceases to be a semi-martingale".
The game is played out right on the Mokobodzki’s interval
\mathscr{M}(\theta)
.
This is the essence of the extended martingale method. Note, however, that in general
\mathscr{M}(\theta)

is not a closed stochastic interval, so we cannot use the tempting formula: "we may assume without loss of generality that
L,U

satisfy Mokobodzki’s condition" (but it is somehow close to it).

1.4.
Main results III: reflected BSDEs without Mokobodzki’s condition

If one looks at Reflected BSDEs and their connection to Dynkin games
one can see that the theory, in the context of Dynkin games, embraces
the martingale method (it just uses a different language, see e.g. the comments following
[
26
]
, Theorem 9.1).
However, BSDEs theory is a much more convenient tool for studying advanced models in stochastic games and optimal control theory.
In recent years, the need to analyze generalized variants of Dynkin games and related control problems
has become strongly apparent.
Therefore, in the present paper, we shall develop the martingale method by
using the BSDEs framework, which at the same time allows us to study general Dynkin game (
1.5
).

Recall that by the classical definition (see e.g.
[
8
]
)
a solution to RBSDE on
[0,T]
with terminal condition
\xi
, driver
f
and barriers
L,U

is a triple
(Y,Z,R)
of
\mathbb{F}
-progressively measurable processes —
Y,R
are càdlàg and
R
is of finite variation, with
R_{0}=0
—
such that

Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})\,ds+(R_{T}-R_{t})-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}\,dB_{s},\quad\quad L_{t}\leq Y_{t}\leq U_{t},\quad t\in[0,T].

Furthermore, to guarantee uniqueness and connection of
(Y,Z,R)

to Dynkin games process
R
is imposed to satisfy the Skorokhod condition

\int_{0}^{T}(Y_{s-}-L_{s-})\,dR^{+}_{s}=\int_{0}^{T}(U_{s-}-Y_{s-})\,dR^{-}_{s}=0.

We call such solutions
classical
or
semimartingale
or
\mathscr{S}
-solution
.
Observe that by the definition
Y
is a semimartingale and it lies between the barriers.
Thus, without Mokobodzki’s condition on the barriers
L,U
classical definition cannot be applied.
We see that in order to study RBSDEs without Mokobodzki’s condition a new definition of a solution is required.
Let us also make one more remark. Observe that processes
Z,R
are uniquely determined by
process
Y
. Indeed, any càdlàg
\mathbb{F}
-semimartingale
X
admits a unique Doob–Meyer decomposition

X_{t}=X_{0}+V_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}H_{s}\,dB_{s},\quad t\in[0,T]

(we also used here representation property of
\mathbb{F}
), where
H
is a progressively measurable process
and
V
is a càdlàg finite variation process. Letting

\mathscr{V}(X):=V,\quad\mathscr{Z}(X):=H,

we get that
Z=\mathscr{Z}(Y)
and
R=\mathscr{V}(Y+\int_{0}^{\cdot}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})\,ds)
.
Therefore, one might as well say that solely
Y
is a solution to aforementioned RBSDE
and treat processes
Z
,
R
as functions of
Y
.

The notion of RBSDEs without Mokobodzki’s condition has been introduced in
[
19
,
20
]

on the Brownian filtration and the Poisson–Brownian filtration, and
extended to a general filtration in
[
29
]
but for drivers depending only on
y
-variable.
Our goal is to modify and simplify the notion in order to
get in a relatively simple way the existence
result for the mentioned class of RBSDEs and then, as a corollary, to get
a number of results for Dynkin games (
1.5
).
We propose the following definition.

We say that a càdlàg
\mathbb{F}
-adapted process
Y
is a solution to RBSDE on
[0,T]
with terminal condition
\xi
, driver
f
and barriers
L,U

if
Y_{T}=\xi
, and for any
\tau\in{\mathcal{T}}
and
\sigma\in\Sigma_{\tau}(L,U)
,
Y
is a classical
solution to RBSDE on
[\tau,\sigma]
with terminal condition
Y_{\sigma}
, driver
f
and barriers
L,U
.

A similar approach has been applied in
[
19
]
but with a subfamily

\tilde{\Sigma}^{Y}_{\tau}\subset\Sigma_{\tau}(L,U)

that depends on the solution (see
[
19
]
, Proposition 3.6). This makes so formulated definition very cumbersome
in applications, especially
when passing to the limit with a sequence of solutions
(Y^{n})
since then we
do not have a fixed interval
[\tau,\sigma]
(since
\sigma\in\tilde{\Sigma}^{Y^{n}}_{\tau}
)
on which underlying RBSDEs can be treated as the classical one.
In
[
29
]
we modified this notion by introducing
a family
\bar{\Sigma}_{\tau}(L,U)
independent of
Y
(see Appendix
B
), which significantly simplified
the proof techniques based on it, but still we had, in general,

\tilde{\Sigma}^{Y}_{\tau}\subsetneq\bar{\Sigma}_{\tau}(L,U)\subsetneq\Sigma_{\tau}(L,U).

In the present paper, we take in the definition of a solution to RBSDE the entire family
\Sigma_{\tau}(L,U)
, i.e.
we solve RBSDEs locally in the classical way where possible.
This is a natural approach, and, in our opinion,
it is the most clear definition
of a solution to RBSDE without Mokobodzki’s condition
and at the same time very convenient to use.
However, the main impetus for extending the previous definitions
(especially the last one, where
\bar{\Sigma}_{\tau}(L,U)
was, after all, independent of the solution) were
the applications of RBSDEs without Mokobodzki’s condition
to Dynkin games. It appears that in general the saddle point
(\hat{\tau}_{\theta},\hat{\sigma}_{\theta})

defined in (
1.7
) satisfies

\hat{\tau}_{\theta},\hat{\sigma}_{\theta}\in\Sigma_{\theta}(L,U)\quad\text{but}\quad\hat{\tau}_{\theta},\hat{\sigma}_{\theta}\notin\bar{\Sigma}_{\theta}(L,U).

In other words, in the previous definitions, the intervals on which RBSDE was considered
as the classical ones were too short to include all possible optimal strategies.

We prove (see Theorem
7.5
) that under aforementioned assumptions on the data there exists a unique solution
Y

of class (D) to RBSDE on
[0,T]
with terminal condition
\xi
, driver
f
and barriers
L,U
and

Y_{\theta}=V^{f}(\theta),\quad\theta\in{\mathcal{T}}.

Moreover,

(1.9)

Y^{n}_{t}\to Y_{t},\quad t\in[0,T],

where
(Y^{n},Z^{n})
is a unique solution to BSDE (penalty scheme)

Y^{n}_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y^{n}_{s},Z^{n}_{s})\,ds+n\int_{t}^{T}(Y^{n}_{s}-L_{s})^{-}\,ds-n\int_{t}^{T}(Y^{n}_{s}-U_{s})^{+}\,ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z^{n}_{s}\,dB_{s}.

Furthermore, the limit is uniform, more precisely,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|Y^{n}_{t}-Y_{t}|\to 0,\quad P\text{-a.s.}

provided that (
1.6
) holds.
We also provide a comparison result for the solutions of studied RBSDEs (Theorem
7.3
).

5.
Asymptotic Mokobodzki’s condition

As an exception, in the present section

Let us consider the following

(AM)

Definition 5.1

of

\mathbb{F}
-adapted processes is an

-solution to reflected backward stochastic differential equation

Assume that

Proof.

By
[
29
]
, Theorem 3.11, there exists a càdlàg adapted process

6.

Let us introduce the following notion. For any càdlàg
\mathbb{F}
-adapted processes

and

\Sigma_{\tau}(X^{1},X^{2}):=\Sigma_{\tau,T}(X^{1},X^{2})

We shall start with the following result.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\tau,\zeta\in\mathcal{A}
,
\tau\leq\zeta
, and

be of class
(D)

.

By
[
25
]

such that

,
and for any random variable
\hat{\sigma}

we have
\sigma^{*}\leq\hat{\sigma}

\sigma^{*}
is a stopping time.
To do this end, we show that the family
\Sigma_{\tau,\zeta}(L,U)
is closed under pairwise maximization i.e.

\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\Sigma_{\tau,\zeta}(L,U)

\sigma_{1}\vee\sigma_{2}\in\Sigma_{\tau,\zeta}(L,U)

First, observe that without loss of generality

may be assumed to be non-negative.
Indeed, for any semimartingale
X

\Sigma_{\tau,\zeta}(L,U)=\Sigma_{\tau,\zeta}(L+X,U+X).

Thus,
\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\Sigma_{\tau,\zeta}(L,U)=\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}\Sigma_{\tau,\zeta}(L+X,U+X)

On the other hand, since

is of class (D) on
[[\tau,\zeta]]

of class (D) on
[[\tau,\zeta]]

such that

[
11
]
, Theorem 24, page 419).

Assume that

Let

Clearly

Clearly

Observe that

\sigma_{1}\vee\sigma_{2}\in\Sigma_{\tau,\zeta}(L,U)

]

,
such that

are of class (D). If
\mathbb{E}\sup_{\nu\leq t\leq\zeta_{2}}|Y^{1}_{t}-Y^{2}_{t}|^{2}<\infty

\begin{split}&|\mathbb{E}^{f_{1}}_{\nu,\zeta_{1}}(\xi_{1})-\mathbb{E}^{f_{2}}_{\nu,\zeta_{2}}(\xi_{2})|^{2}\leq C\mathbb{E}\Big(\int^{\zeta_{1}}_{\nu}|Y^{1}_{r}-Y^{2}_{r}||f_{1}-f_{2}|(r,Y^{2}_{r},Z^{2}_{r})\,dr\\
&\quad+|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|^{2}+\int^{\zeta_{2}}_{\zeta_{1}}|Y^{1}_{r}-Y^{2}_{r}||f_{2}(r,Y^{2}_{r},Z^{2}_{r})|\,dr|\mathcal{F}_{\nu}\Big),\end{split}

for some

\lambda,\mu,T
.

Proposition A.4
.

Assume that
\mathbb{E}\int^{T}_{0}|f(r,y,0)|\,dr<+\infty
for every
y\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\beta\in{\mathcal{T}}
and let
\{\beta_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\subset{\mathcal{T}}
be a sequence of stopping times such
that
\beta_{n}\searrow\beta
,
n\to\infty
. Assume that
\xi\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{\beta},P)
.
Moreover, let
\{\xi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of random variables such that

\xi_{n}\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{\beta_{n}},P)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\xi_{n}\searrow\xi
,
n\to\infty
.
Then
||\mathbb{E}^{f}_{\cdot,\beta_{n}}(\xi_{n})-\mathbb{E}^{f}_{\cdot,\beta}(\xi)||_{\mathcal{D}^{1}(0,\beta)}\to 0
,
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

Let
(Y,Z)
be a solution to BSDE
{}^{\alpha,\beta_{1}}(\xi,f^{\beta})
such that
Y
is of class (D),
where
f^{\beta}(t,y,z)=f(t,y,z)\mathbf{1}_{t\leq\beta}
, and for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

let
(Y^{n},Z^{n})
be a solution to BSDE
{}^{\alpha,\beta_{1}}(\xi_{n},f^{\beta_{n}})
such that
Y^{n}
is of class (D),
where
f^{\beta_{n}}(t,y,z)=f(t,y,z)\mathbf{1}_{t\leq\beta_{n}}
. The existence of the solutions follows from Theorem
3.5
.
Notice that
Y_{t}=\xi

and
Z_{t}=0
for
t\geq\beta
. Obviously
\mathbb{E}^{f}_{\alpha,\beta}(\xi)=Y_{\alpha}
and

Y^{n}_{\alpha}=\mathbb{E}^{f^{\beta_{n}}}_{\alpha,\beta_{n}}(\xi_{n})
,
\alpha\leq\beta
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
The rest of the proof we divide into two steps.

Step 1.
We assume additionally that
\xi
is bounded i.e.
|\xi|\leq M
for some
M>0
. Then by Theorem
3.6
we have that

\begin{split}&\|Y^{n}-Y\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}(0,\beta_{1})}\leq\mathcal{C}\psi_{3}\Big(\mathbb{E}|\xi_{n}-\xi|+\mathbb{E}\int^{\beta_{n}}_{\beta}|f(r,\xi,0)|\,dr\Big)\\
&\quad\leq\mathcal{C}\psi_{3}\Big(\mathbb{E}|\xi_{n}-\xi|+\mathbb{E}\int^{\beta_{n}}_{\beta}|f(r,-M,0)|+|f(r,M,0)|\,dr\Big).\end{split}

By (H8) and by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem we have the result.

Step 2.
Let us consider
\Pi_{k}(x)=(x\vee(-k))\wedge k
,
k\geq 1
, and let
(Y^{k},Z^{k})
be a solution to
BSDE
{}^{\beta_{1}}(\Pi_{k}(\xi),f^{\beta})
such that
Y^{k}
is of class (D).
The existence of the solutions follows from Theorem
3.5
.
By Theorem
3.6

\|Y^{k}-Y\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}(0,\beta_{1})}\to 0
when
k\to\infty
.
Similarly, let
(Y^{n,k},Z^{n,k})
be a solution to BSDE
{}^{\beta_{1}}(\Pi_{k}(\xi_{n}),f^{\beta_{n}})

such that
Y^{n,k}
is of class (D).
The existence of the solutions follows from Theorem
3.5
.
By
Step 1.

we have
\|Y^{n,k}-Y^{k}\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}(0,\beta_{1})}\to 0
when
n\to\infty
. By Theorem
3.6
,

\begin{split}\|Y^{n,k}-Y^{n}\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}(0,\beta_{1})}\leq\mathcal{C}\psi_{3}\Big(\mathbb{E}|\Pi_{k}(\xi_{n})-\xi_{n}|\Big)\leq\mathcal{C}\psi_{3}\Big(\mathbb{E}(|\xi_{1}|+|\xi|)\mathbf{1}_{\{|\xi_{1}|+|\xi|>k\}}\Big)=:\delta_{k},\end{split}

and by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem
\delta_{k}\to 0
,
k\to\infty
. Therefore,

\|Y^{n}-Y\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}(0,\beta_{1})}\leq\mathcal{C}\big(\delta_{k}+\|Y^{n,k}-Y^{k}\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}(0,\beta_{1})}+\|Y^{k}-Y\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}(0,\beta_{1})}\big).

Consequently,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|Y^{n}-Y\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}(0,\beta_{1})}\leq\mathcal{C}\big(\|Y^{k}-Y\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1}(0,\beta)}+\delta_{k}\big),\quad k\geq 1.

Passing to the limit with
k\to\infty
yields the result.
∎

In

For

Definition B.1

(b)

Abstract
. We describe singularities of height functions on singular surfaces in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} parameterized by smooth map-germs 𝒜 \mathcal{A} -equivalent to one of S k S_{k} , B k B_{k} , C k C_{k} and F 4 F_{4} singularities in terms of extended geometric language via finite succession of blowing-ups. We investigate singularities of dual surfaces of such singular surfaces.

2.
Differential geometry for singular surfaces.

If
S
is a regular surface in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and contains the origin, its tangent plane can be given by the
xy
-plane by using a rotation in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Then
S
is defined as the graph of the equation
z=f(x,y)
for some function
f

and

can be locally expressed as

f(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}(k_{1}x^{2}+k_{2}y^{2})+O(x,y)^{3},

where
k_{1}
and

are the principal curvature at the origin.

Two map-germs
f,g:(\mathbb{R}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{3},0)
are said to be
\mathcal{A}
-equivalent
if
g=\Phi\circ f\circ\varphi^{-1}
for some germs of diffeomorphisms

A map-germ

\mathcal{A}
-simple
if there is a finite number of equivalence classes such that if

is embedded in any family

in a sufficiently small neighbourhood of

, the germ of

at

, where we define

Remark 2.2
.

Assume that a singular surface is parameterized by
g:(\mathbb{R}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{3},0)
given in (
2.1

We can easily show that the tangent line is the
x
-axis and the normal plane is the
yz
-plane, where
(x,y,z)
is the usual Cartesian coordinate system of
\mathbb{R}^{3}

Furthermore, the null vector can be chosen as
\eta=\partial_{v}

xy
-plane and
\pm\partial_{z}
are the principal normal vectors.

We can take
\Gamma=(u,c_{1}u+c_{2}u^{2}+O(u^{3}))
as the family of tangential curves.
The 2-jet of

are given by
(u,(b_{2}+c_{1}^{2})u^{2}/2,a_{2,0}u^{2}/2)

It follows that tangential curves tangent to the

is an inflection (resp. degenerate inflection) point if and only if

a_{2,0}=b_{2}=0

By using the above argument, it is easily shown that the singular point
g(0,0)
is an inflection point if and only if

have at least 3-point contact (inflectional tangent) with the principal plane at
g(0,0)
, and that the inflection point

is degenerate if and only if

It is also a point where one principal curvature has an extremum value along the corresponding line of curvature.

Such a point is called the sub-parabolic point, which was first studied in details by Bruce and Wilkinson
[
5

It is also a point where the lines of curvature have geodesic inflections.

The principal vectors can be also extendible near the singularity.

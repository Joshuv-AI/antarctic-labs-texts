Sparse Recovery with Shuffled Labels: Statistical Limits and Practical Estimators
Hang Zhang, Ping Li

Abstract
1 1 1 Preliminary work appeared in Proceedings of the IEEE International Symposium on Information Theory (ISIT’21). This paper considers the sparse recovery with shuffled labels, i.e., 𝒚 = 𝚷 ♮ ​ 𝐗 ​ 𝜷 ♮ + 𝒘 \bm{y}=\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}\mathbf{X}\bm{\beta}^{\natural}+\bm{w} , where 𝒚 ∈ ℝ n \bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n} , 𝚷 ∈ ℝ n × n \bm{\Pi}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n} , 𝐗 ∈ ℝ n × p \mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p} , 𝜷 ♮ ∈ ℝ p \bm{\beta}^{\natural}\in\mathbb{R}^{p} , 𝒘 ∈ ℝ n \bm{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n} denote the sensing result, the unknown permutation matrix, the design matrix, the sparse signal, and the additive noise, respectively. Our goal is to reconstruct both the permutation matrix 𝚷 ♮ \bm{\Pi}^{\natural} and the sparse signal 𝜷 ♮ \bm{\beta}^{\natural} . We investigate this problem from both the statistical and computational aspects. From the statistical aspect, we first establish the minimax lower bounds on the sample number n n and the signal-to-noise ratio ( 𝖲𝖭𝖱 \mathsf{SNR} ) for the correct recovery of permutation matrix 𝚷 ♮ \bm{\Pi}^{\natural} and the support set supp ​ ( 𝜷 ♮ ) \textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural}) , to be more specific, n ≳ k ​ log ⁡ p n\gtrsim k\log p and log ⁡ 𝖲𝖭𝖱 ≳ log ⁡ n + k ​ log ⁡ p n \log\mathsf{SNR}\gtrsim\log n+\frac{k\log p}{n} . Then, we confirm the tightness of these minimax lower bounds by presenting an exhaustive-search based estimator whose performance matches the lower bounds thereof up to some multiplicative constants. From the computational aspect, we impose a parsimonious assumption on the number of permuted rows and propose a computationally-efficient estimator accordingly. Moreover, we show that our proposed estimator can obtain the ground-truth ( 𝚷 ♮ , supp ​ ( 𝜷 ♮ ) ) (\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural})) under mild conditions. Furthermore, we provide numerical experiments to corroborate our claims. In this study, we focus on the “single measurement” problem, i.e., 𝒚 ∈ ℝ n \bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n} and 𝜷 ♮ ∈ ℝ p \bm{\beta}^{\natural}\in\mathbb{R}^{p} , and require 𝖲𝖭𝖱 \mathsf{SNR} being at least of the order Ω ⁡ ( n c ⋅ ( k ⋅ log ⁡ p / n ) c ) \Omega(n^{c}\cdot(k\cdot\log p/n)^{c}) . A recent work ( Zhang and Li 2023 ) studies the permuted sparse recovery problem with multiple measurements, i.e., 𝒚 ∈ ℝ n × m \bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m} and 𝜷 ♮ ∈ ℝ p × m \bm{\beta}^{\natural}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times m} , with m > 1 m>1 . They exploit the strategy of “borrowing strength“ across different sets of measurements and reduce the 𝖲𝖭𝖱 \mathsf{SNR} requirement for the permutation recovery. They propose a new estimator and develop a series of new techniques (including a novel modification of the “leave-one-out” method), which however do not apply to our problem (with m = 1 m=1 ) in this paper.

Introduction

This paper considers the linear sensing relation with
shuffled labels, which can be formulated as

\displaystyle\bm{y}=\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}\mathbf{X}\bm{\beta}^{\natural}+\bm{w}.\vskip 2.84526pt

Here
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes the sensing result,

\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is the unknown permutation matrix,

\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
is the design (sensing) matrix,
\bm{\beta}^{\natural}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}

represents the signals of interests,
and
\bm{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes the additive noise.
In real life, we have witnessed a broad spectrum of its applications,
which spans from communication to data privacy
to
computer vision to curve registration to natural language processing
(
Pananjady et al. 2018
;
Slawski and Ben-David 2019
;
Unnikrishnan et al. 2015
;
Zhang et al. 2019
)
.
Two prominent examples include
linkage record
, which
is to merge two datasets
containing different pieces of information
about the same objects
into one comprehensive
dataset; and
data de-anonymization
, which infers the hidden labels and
can be viewed as the inverse problem
of data anonymization used for privacy protection.
Apart from the above applications,
other applications include
correspondence estimation between
pose and estimation in
graphics to time-domain sampling in
the presence of clock jitter to multi-target tracking in radar.
For a detailed discussion, we
refer the interested readers to
Pananjady et al. 2018
;
Slawski and Ben-David 2019
;
Unnikrishnan et al. 2015
;
Zhang et al. 2019
;
Slawski et al. 2020
.

In the majority of existing works
(
Pananjady et al. 2018
;
Slawski and Ben-David 2019
;
Unnikrishnan et al. 2015
;
Zhang et al. 2019
;
Slawski et al. 2020
;
Hsu et al. 2017
;
Zhang et al. 2022
;
Zhang and Li 2020
)
,
their focus is usually on the regime of sufficient samples, in other words, the sample number
n
is larger than the signal length
p
(i.e.,
n\geq p
). For a general case where the signal
\beta^{\natural}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}

an arbitrary vector residing within the linear space
\mathbb{R}^{p}
, the requirement
n\geq
seems to be inevitable, even with the perfect correspondence information, namely, the permutation matrix
\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}
. Meanwhile, the sample number can be reduced given some prior
knowledge of the signal
\bm{\beta}^{\natural}
, e.g., we know that
\bm{\beta}^{\natural}
lies within a small subspace, or equivalently,

\bm{\beta}^{\natural}
is with a low inherent dimension.
One typical example is the literature on the “compressed sensing” (or
sparse recovery
)
(
Donoho 2006
;
Candes et al. 2006
;
Candès et al. 2006
)
.
Assuming the signal

\bm{\beta}^{\natural}
is
k
-sparse (with
k
-nonzero entries), it is proved that the required sample number
n
can be reduced to

\Omega(k\log p)
, which is far less than
p
provided
k\ll p
.
For other low-dimensional
structures,
similar results have been obtained under the names

M-estimator with regularizer
s
(
Negahban et al. 2012
)
,

atomic norm
s
(
Chandrasekaran et al. 2012
)
,
random convex optimization
s
(
Amelunxen et al. 2014
)
, etc.

Inspired by these works, we
investigate the shuffled linear sensing problem
with insufficient samples, namely,
n\ll p
, by placing
a parsimonious assumption on the signal
\bm{\beta}^{\natural}
.
Assuming
\bm{\beta}^{\natural}
to be
k
-sparse, we
show that the correspondence information can be restored
with
n=\Omega(k\log p)
.
Notice that this order
is the same as the classical works on compressed sensing/sparse recovery
(
Donoho 2006
;
Candes et al. 2006
;
Candès et al. 2006
)
and is far less than the previously required sample number such that
n=\Omega(p)

(
Pananjady et al. 2018
;
Slawski and Ben-David 2019
;
Hsu et al. 2017
;
Unnikrishnan et al. 2015
)
.

Related work.

The research on unlabeled linear regression has a long history
and can be at least traced back to 70s under the name
“broken sample problems”

(
DeGroot and Goel 1976
;
Goel 1975
;
Bai and Hsing 2005
;
DeGroot and Goel 1980
)
. In recent years, we have witnessed
a renaissance of the study in this area.

(
Unnikrishnan et al. 2015
)
investigate
the permutation recovery under the noiseless setting,
i.e.,
\bm{w}=\bm{0}
;
and establish the necessary condition

n\geq 2p
for the general signal recovery.
In
Pananjady et al. 2018
,
the noisy observation is considered and a thorough analysis of the
maximum likelihood
(ML) estimator is presented.
From the statistical perspective,
it is shown that the ML estimator can reach the
statistical optimality with respect to the
signal-to-noise ratio
(
\mathsf{SNR}\triangleq\|\bm{\beta}^{\natural}\|^{2}_{2}/\sigma^{2}
)
requirement for correct permutation recovery,
to be more specific,
\mathsf{SNR}\asymp\Omega(n^{c})
.
From the computational perspective,

Pananjady et al. 2018
show the ML estimator is NP-hard except for the special case

p=1
.
This computational issue is later tackled by
Hsu et al. 2017
,
where an approximation algorithm for the permutation recovery
is proposed with polynomial complexity.
In
Slawski and Ben-David 2019
, the authors take an alternative path
and impose parsimonious constraints on the number of permuted rows.
By viewing
(\mathbf{I}-\bm{\Pi}^{\natural})\mathbf{X}\bm{\beta}^{\natural}
as sparse outliers,
(
Slawski and Ben-David 2019
)
reconstruct the correspondence information from the viewpoint of de-noising. In this work, we adopt a similar
viewpoint in designing the estimator for practical usage. However, some
modifications are required to handle the insufficient sample problem (i.e.,
n\ll p
). A detailed explanation of our proposed estimator
can be found in Section
5
.

In
Emiya et al. 2014
, they
consider a similar setting as ours, namely, a sparse signal
\bm{\beta}^{\natural}
.
A branch-and-bound scheme is proposed to reconstruct the permutation matrix
\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}
. Potential drawbacks of this work include their high computational cost and the missing performance guarantee.

(
Zhang and Li 2021
)
is the
conference version of this work
and proposes different practical estimators.
While the estimators in
Zhang and Li 2021
are rooted in the literature
about the sign consistency in Lasso, Dantzig estimator, etc
(
Zhao and Yu 2006
;
Wainwright 2009
;
Meinshausen et al. 2009
;
Donoho et al. 2005
;
Rosenbaum et al. 2010
;
Zhang et al. 2017
;
Lounici 2008
;
Zhang et al. 2018
)
,
the estimator in this work is more related
to the study of robustness
(
Nguyen and Tran 2013
;
Dalalyan and Thompson 2019
)
.
Despite the above differences and their
distinct looks, we should mention that
they actually share the same spirit, i.e., the viewpoint of de-noising:

Zhang and Li 2021
performs de-noising
in an implicit way while this work takes an explicit approach. Together with the change brings a noticeable performance improvement, which
is discussed in Remark
3
.

Apart from the above-mentioned articles,
there are other works that are worth mentioning,
e.g.,
(
Tsakiris and Peng 2019
;
Haghighatshoar and Caire 2018
;
Emiya et al. 2014
;
Zhang et al. 2019
;
Slawski et al. 2020
;
Fang and Li 2022
;
Slawski and Sen 2022
)
. Since their connection to
our work are rather loose, we only mention their name without giving
detailed discussion.

Contributions.
Our contributions are summarized as follows:

•

We establish the statistical lower bounds
for the correct recovery of
(\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural}))
.
Different from the previous works, our work
focuses on the situation with insufficient samples,
i.e.,
n\ll p
. Exploiting the signal sparsity,
we manage to reduce the sample number
n
from

\Omega(p)
to
\Omega(k\log p)
, where

k
denotes the sparsity number of the signal
\bm{\beta}^{\natural}
.
As compensation, our required
\mathsf{SNR}
inflates
from
\log\mathsf{SNR}\gtrsim\log n
to

\log\mathsf{SNR}\gtrsim\log n+\frac{k\log p}{n}
, which
turns out to be marginal since
n\gtrsim k\log p
.
Moreover, we show an
exhaustive-search-based estimator can
match the above lower bounds up to some multiplicative constants and thus conclude the tightness of the minimax lower bounds thereof.

•

We propose a computational-friendly estimator for the recovery
of
(\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural}))
.
By imposing a parsimonious assumption
on the number of permuted rows, we view
(\mathbf{I}-\bm{\Pi}^{\natural})\mathbf{X}\bm{\beta}^{\natural}

as a sparse outlier and obtain a rough estimation
\widetilde{\bm{\beta}}
of the
signal
\bm{\beta}^{\natural}
. Then, we reconstruct the missing correspondence based on the estimated value
\widetilde{\bm{\beta}}
.
We prove that the ground-truth
permutation matrix
\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}
can be obtained under mild conditions.
More importantly, we show these conditions almost match the
minimax lower bounds thereof.
Once the permutation matrix
\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}
is given, we restore our
problem to the classical setting of sparse recovery/compressed sensing and detect the support set of
\bm{\beta}^{\natural}
accordingly.

Consider the sensing relation

\displaystyle\bm{y}=\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}\mathbf{X}\bm{\beta}^{\natural}+\bm{w},

(1)

where
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the sensing result,

\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
denotes the
permutation matrix, i.e.,
\sum_{i}\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}_{ij}=\sum_{j}\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}_{ij}=1
,
\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}_{ij}\in\left\{0,1\right\}
,

\mathbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
is the sensing matrix, with

\mathbf{X}_{ij}\in\mathsf{N}(0,1)

k

\|\bm{\beta}^{\natural}\|_{0}\leq k
,
and
\bm{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\mathsf{N}(\bm{0},\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I})
.

Zhang et al. 2019
;
Slawski et al. 2020
;

, our work

n\leq p
. By exploiting the
sparsity of the signals

3
Statistical Lower Bounds

This paper focuses on recovering both the permutation matrix

\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}
and the support set
\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural})
,
which are affected by both the sample number
n
and

\mathsf{SNR}
.
In this section, we will separately discuss
their roles and establish the corresponding statistical lower bounds.

3.1
Lower bound on sample number
n

To free us from the impact of
\mathsf{SNR}
,
we consider the oracle scenario with
\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}
being known
and limit ourselves to the small noise case, i.e.,
\bm{w}\approxeq\bm{0}
.
Then, our problem reduces to the
classical setting of CS
(
Donoho 2006
;
Candes et al. 2006
;
Candès et al. 2006
)
,
where
n\gtrsim k\log p
is required for the Gaussian
\mathbf{X}
(cf. P.
507
, Example
15.18
in
Wainwright 2019
).
This order applies to our case as well since it is hopeless
to recover
\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural})
provided we fail even in the
oracle scenario with known
\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}
.

3.2
Lower bound on
\mathsf{SNR}

This subsection studies the mini-max lower bound
for the
\mathsf{SNR}
. The main result is the following.

Theorem 1
.

We have

\displaystyle\inf_{\widehat{\bm{\Pi}},\widehat{\bm{\beta}}}\sup_{\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\bm{\beta}^{\natural}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X},\bm{w}}\mathbbm{1}\left[(\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural}))\neq(\widehat{\bm{\Pi}},\textup{supp}(\widehat{\bm{\beta}}))\right]\geq\frac{1}{2},

(2)

if
n\log\left(1+\mathsf{SNR}\right)+2\leq\log\left(\left\lvert{\cal P}_{n}\right\rvert{p\choose k}\right)
,
where
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X},\bm{w}}(\cdot)
is taken w.r.t
\mathbf{X}
and

\bm{w}
, and the infimum is over all possible
estimators
\widehat{\bm{\Pi}}
and
\widehat{\bm{\beta}}
.

To better understand Theorem
1
, we
spell out the constants and only focus on the orders. Without
any prior information about
\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}
, we can assume
it to distribute uniformly among the set
{\cal P}_{n}
.
Then, we have
\left\lvert{\cal P}_{n}\right\rvert=\log n!
and can rewrite
the
\mathsf{SNR}
requirement in (
2
) as

\displaystyle\log\left(1+\mathsf{SNR}\right)\lesssim\log n+\frac{k\log\left({p}/{k}\right)}{n}.

(3)

Compared with
Pananjady et al. 2018
which requires

\log(\mathsf{SNR})\asymp\log n
and
n=\Omega(p)
for
correct permutation recovery, our bound
only has a slight increase of

\mathsf{SNR}
requirement in (
3
)
since
n\gtrsim k\log p
.
Such an increase of required signal length
is outweighed by the significant
reduction of sample number, which
is from
\Omega(p)
to
\Omega(k\log p)
.
In addition, we believe that
this theorem can be safely relaxed to
the scenario where
\mathbf{X}_{ij}
is an i.i.d. sub-gaussian random variable
with zero mean and unit variance.

The rigorous proof of Theorem
1
is in
Section
A.1
.
Here we only present an intuitive explanation,
which comes from
coding theory

(
Cover and Thomas 2012
)
. The basic idea is to recast the
problem of recovering

(\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural}))

as a decoding problem
(
Pananjady et al. 2018
;
Zhang et al. 2019
)
.
First, we encode
(\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural}))

into the codeword
\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}\mathbf{X}\bm{\beta}^{\natural}
.
Then, we pass it through the additive Gaussian channel
(
Cover and Thomas 2012
)
and
observe
\bm{y}=\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}\mathbf{X}\bm{\beta}^{\natural}+\bm{w}
.
Our goal is to decode
(\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural}))

from the received signal
\bm{y}
. An illustration is available in
Figure
2
.

Figure 2:
Interpretation of Theorem
1
from the viewpoint of coding theory.

Different from
Pananjady et al. 2018
,
we cannot assume
\bm{\beta}^{\natural}
to be given a prior
as this will lead to
absence of sparsity number in the
\mathsf{SNR}
requirement.
Instead, we
assume
\bm{\beta}^{\natural}
to be a binary vector, namely,
\bm{\beta}^{\natural}\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n}
.
A bonus of this assumption is that

\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural})
contains the same amount of
information as
\bm{\beta}
, in other words,
there is no extra information (e.g., the specific
values of the non-zero entries) required for
encoding
(\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural}))
into

\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}\mathbf{X}\bm{\beta}^{\natural}
.
On one hand, the code rate
\mathsf{Rate}
is computed as

\displaystyle\mathsf{Rate}\triangleq\frac{\log\left({p\choose k}n!\right)}{n}\gtrsim\ \left(1+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2n}}\right)\log n+\ \frac{k\log(p/k)}{n}.\vskip 2.84526pt

Meanwhile, the channel capacity is approximately

\displaystyle\mathsf{Capacity}\triangleq\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{\|\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}\mathbf{X}\bm{\beta}^{\natural}\|_{2}^{2}}{n\sigma^{2}}\right)\approx\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{\|\bm{\beta}^{\natural}\|_{2}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right).\vskip 2.84526pt

According to
Cover and Thomas 2012
, we require
the code rate is no greater than the channel capacity, i.e.,

\mathsf{Rate}<\mathsf{Capacity}
, to ensure correct recovery, which naturally yields the

\mathsf{SNR}
requirement in (
2
).

In addition, we notice that the exact recovery
may be unnecessary in certain
applications.
Following a similar approach, we obtain an
analogous lower bound for the approximate recovery, namely,

\mathsf{d_{H}}(\widehat{\bm{\Pi}},\bm{\Pi}^{\natural})+\mathsf{d_{H}}(\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural}),\textup{supp}(\widehat{\bm{\beta}}))\geq\mathsf{D}
, where
\mathsf{D}\geq 0
is some positive integer. A formal statement is given as follows.

Theorem 2
.

Provided that
n\log(1+\mathsf{SNR})+\log 4\leq\log\zeta
,
we conclude

\displaystyle\inf_{\widehat{\bm{\Pi}},\widehat{\bm{\beta}}}\sup_{\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\bm{\beta}^{\natural}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X},\bm{w}}\mathbbm{1}\left[\mathsf{d_{H}}(\widehat{\bm{\Pi}},\bm{\Pi}^{\natural})+\mathsf{d_{H}}(\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural}),\textup{supp}(\widehat{\bm{\beta}}))\geq\mathsf{D}\right]\geq\frac{1}{2},\vskip 2.84526pt

where
\zeta
is defined as

\displaystyle\zeta

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{p!}{(k!)^{2}[(p-k)!]^{2}}\cdot\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\mathsf{D}}\sum_{j=1}^{(\mathsf{D}-i)\wedge k}\ \frac{1}{(n-i)!(k-j)!(p-k-j)!(j!)^{2}}\right]^{-1}.

(4)

Remark 1
.

Due to the complicated form of
\zeta
in (
4
),
we only calculate one special case, i.e.,
\mathsf{D}=0
, to illustrate its behavior.
Notice that
\mathsf{D}=0
corresponds to the exact recovery,
which restore the setting to Theorem
1
.
Parameter
\zeta
under this case is written as

n!{p\choose k}
, which exhibits
the same order as in Theorem
1
.

In this section, we have established the lower bounds, which remain valid
regardless of the estimators being used.
In the next section,
we will confirm their tightness.

4
The Maximum Likelihood Estimator

We will show that the lower bounds in Section
3
can be matched
with the differences only up to some
multiplicative constants, to be more specific,

(i)
sample number
n
can be picked as

n\asymp k\log p
; and
(ii)

\mathsf{SNR}
can be set as

\log\mathsf{SNR}\asymp\log n+\frac{k\log(ep/k)}{n}
.

4.1
A warm-up example: the noiseless case

First, we study the role of sample number
n
.
To free it from the impact of
\mathsf{SNR}
, we consider the
noiseless case, where
\mathsf{SNR}
is infinite.
Assuming the sparsity number
k
is given in advance,
we will recover
(\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\bm{\beta}^{\natural})

via the

maximal likelihood
(ML) estimator reading as

\displaystyle(\widehat{\bm{\Pi}}_{\mathsf{ML}},\widehat{\bm{\beta}}_{\mathsf{ML}})=\ \textup{argmin}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\Pi}\in{\cal P}_{n}\\
{\left\|\bm{\beta}\right\|}_{0}\leq k\end{subarray}}{\left\|\bm{y}-\bm{\Pi}\mathbf{X}\bm{\beta}\right\|}_{2}.

(5)

Then we have the following theorem:

Theorem 3
.

Consider the noiseless case where
\bm{w}=\bm{0}
.
Given that

n\gtrsim k\log p
, we have

\mathbb{P}((\widehat{\bm{\Pi}}_{\mathsf{ML}},\widehat{\bm{\beta}}_{\mathsf{ML}})\neq(\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},~\bm{\beta}^{\natural}))\lesssim n^{-2}
.

Proof outline.

We divide the analysis of the reconstruction
error
(\widehat{\bm{\Pi}}_{\mathsf{ML}},\widehat{\bm{\beta}}_{\mathsf{ML}})\neq(\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural}))

into three categories:

(i)

\big\{\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}=\widehat{\bm{\Pi}}_{\mathsf{ML}},\ \bm{\beta}^{\natural}\neq\widehat{\bm{\beta}}_{\mathsf{ML}}\big\}
;

(ii)

\big\{\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}\neq\widehat{\bm{\Pi}}_{\mathsf{ML}},\ \bm{\beta}^{\natural}=\widehat{\bm{\beta}}_{\mathsf{ML}}\big\}
; and

(iii)

\big\{\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}\neq\widehat{\bm{\Pi}}_{\mathsf{ML}},\ \bm{\beta}^{\natural}\neq\widehat{\bm{\beta}}_{\mathsf{ML}}\big\}
.
Iterating over all possible pairs

(\bm{\Pi},~\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}))
,
we will show the above
3
types of errors rarely happen.
The detailed proof is deferred to Subsection
B.2
.
∎

We notice that Theorem
3
directly recovers
a sparse signal
\bm{\beta}^{\natural}
, which contains more information
than its support set
\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural})
.
Moreover, we observe that the sample number requirement
in Theorem
3
is well aligned with the
lower bound presented in Subsection
3.1
and
can hence confirm its tightness.

4.2
The noisy case

This subsection investigates the noisy case.
To correctly recover the support set
\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural})
,
we need an additional assumption on non-zero entries’ smallest magnitudes.
Otherwise, even in the classical setting without any permutation,
small sensing noise can lead
to incorrect support set detection.
The formal statement reads as follows.

Theorem 4
.

Provided that

(i)

n\gtrsim k\log p
,

(ii)

\log\mathsf{SNR}\gtrsim\log n+\frac{k}{n}\log(\frac{ep}{k})

(iii)

\min_{i\in\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural})}\nicefrac{{|\bm{\beta}^{\natural}_{i}|^{2}}}{{\sigma^{2}}}\gtrsim 1
,

we have

\mathbb{P}((\widehat{\bm{\Pi}}_{\mathsf{ML}},\textup{supp}(\widehat{\bm{\beta}}_{\mathsf{ML}}))\neq(\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural}))\lesssim n^{-2}+e^{-ck\log p}
.

Compared with the result pertaining to the noiseless case (i.e.,
Theorem
3
), Theorem
4
requires
non-zero entries’ magnitudes to be at least some positive
constants. Apart from this constraint, our bound matches the
minimax lower bound in Theorem
1
up to some
multiplicative constants, specifically,

n\gtrsim k\log p
and
\log\mathsf{SNR}\gtrsim\log n+\nicefrac{{k\log p}}{{n}}
.

Remark 2
.

With a slight modification of the ML estimator,
we can significantly relax
the third assumption in the above theorem, i.e.,
\min_{i\in\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural})}\nicefrac{{|\bm{\beta}^{\natural}_{i}|^{2}}}{{\sigma^{2}}}\gtrsim 1
.
Notice that the correct permutation recovery only requires the first two assumptions in Theorem
4
.
Hence, we can first reconstruct the permutation matrix

\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}
with the ML estimator. Afterwards, we restore the shuffled sparse recovery problem to its classical setting and invoke the
previous works to detect the support set
\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural})
. With
the above modifications, we can improve the assumption
\min_{i\in\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural})}\nicefrac{{|\bm{\beta}^{\natural}_{i}|^{2}}}{{\sigma^{2}}}\gtrsim 1

to
\min_{i\in\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural})}\nicefrac{{|\bm{\beta}^{\natural}_{i}|^{2}}}{{\sigma^{2}}}\gtrsim\nicefrac{{\log p}}{{n}}
.

We would like to emphasize that the ML estimator
only serves to confirm the tightness of Theorem
1
.
It is unpractical due to its high computational cost:
it needs to iterate

(i)
all possible
k
-sparse subsets, which consists of

{p\choose k}
cases; and
(ii)

all possible permutation matrices
\bm{\Pi}
, which consists
n!
cases.
The next section will present a computational-friendly estimator.

5
Practical Estimator

This section proposes a practical estimator to combat the high computational
cost associated with the ML estimator,
which consists of
two stages: permutation recovery and
support set detection.
A formal statement is in
Algorithm
1
.

5.1
Permutation Recovery

We note that the major difficulty in the permutation recovery
stems from the missing value of signal
\bm{\beta}^{\natural}
.
One natural solution is to restore the permutation
with an approximate value of
\bm{\beta}^{\natural}
.
To begin with, we impose a
parsimonious assumption on the number of permuted
rows, or equivalently, only a small proportion of rows are permuted. Then, we
adopt a denoising viewpoint and treat
\left(\mathbf{I}-\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}\right)\mathbf{X}\bm{\beta}^{\natural}
as a sparse outlier
to be removed. Inspired by
Nguyen and Tran 2013
and
Slawski and Ben-David 2019
, we can estimate
the signal
\bm{\beta}^{\natural}
as

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\bm{\Xi}},\widetilde{\bm{\beta}})=\textup{argmin}_{\bm{\Xi},\bm{\beta}}~

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2n}{\left\|\bm{y}-\mathbf{X}\bm{\beta}-\sqrt{n}\cdot\bm{\Xi}\right\|}_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{\bm{\Xi}}{\left\|\bm{\Xi}\right\|}_{1}+\lambda_{\bm{\beta}}{\left\|\bm{\beta}\right\|}_{1}.\vskip 2.84526pt

Afterwards, we reconstruct the
permutation matrix
\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}
via the following

linear assignment problem
(LAP), which
reads as

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\Pi}}=\textup{argmax}_{\bm{\Pi}}\langle\bm{y},~\bm{\Pi}\mathbf{X}\widetilde{\bm{\beta}}\rangle,\vskip 2.84526pt

where
\widehat{\bm{\beta}}
is the solution of
(
6
).
Then, we conclude

Theorem 5
.

We set
\lambda_{\bm{\beta}}
and
\lambda_{\bm{\Xi}}
in
(
6
) as

c_{0}\sigma\sqrt{\nicefrac{{\log p}}{{n}}}

and

c_{1}\sigma\sqrt{\nicefrac{{\log n}}{{n}}}
,
respectively. Assuming that

(i)

n\gtrsim k\log p
,
(ii)

h\lesssim\frac{n}{\log n}
, and
(iii)

\displaystyle\mathsf{SNR}\gtrsim\frac{n^{2(1+\varepsilon)}(n-1)^{2}}{4\pi}\bigg[

\displaystyle\sqrt{\log np}\left(k\sqrt{\frac{\log p}{n}}\vee h\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\right)+2\log(n^{1+\varepsilon}(n-1))\bigg]^{2},\vskip 2.84526pt

we conclude that (
7
) can yield the ground truth with probability
exceeding
1-2n^{-\varepsilon}
, i.e.,

\mathbb{P}(\widehat{\bm{\Pi}}=\bm{\Pi}^{\natural})\geq 1-2n^{-\varepsilon}
.

First, we discuss the
\mathsf{SNR}
requirement.
From the above theorem, we conclude
that
the correct permutation matrix
can be obtained provided that
\log\mathsf{SNR}\gtrsim\log n
, which matches the
mini-max lower bound in
Theorem
1
up to some
multiplicative constant.
Then, we consider the maximum allowed number of permuted rows,
i.e.,
h\lesssim\frac{n}{\log n}
.
Compared with the optimal order

O(n)
, we experience a loss of
logarithmic term. This is consistent with our parsimonious assumption on the number of permuted
rows, i.e.,
h\ll n
.
Moreover, we discuss the minimum
required sample number
n
, which
is of the order
\Omega(k\log p)
.
Notice that this is the same as
the mini-max bound discussed in
Subsection
3.1
.

5.2
Support Set Detection

Once we have the correct permutation matrix
\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}
, we can restore
(
1
) to the classical model and detect the support set

\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural})
with the Lasso estimator, which is written as

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\beta}}_{\mathsf{Lasso}}=\ \textup{argmin}_{\bm{\beta}}~\dfrac{1}{2n}\big\|\widehat{\bm{\Pi}}^{\top}\bm{y}-\mathbf{X}\bm{\beta}\big\|_{2}^{2}+\ \lambda_{\mathsf{Lasso}(n)}{\left\|\bm{\beta}\right\|}_{1},\vskip 2.84526pt

where
\widehat{\bm{\Pi}}
denotes the solution of
(
7
).
Then, we detect the support set
\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural})
by
selecting the entries with the first
k
-largest magnitude.
With the standard results concerning the sign consistency of
Lasso estimator, e.g.,
Lounici 2008
, we can show the
support set can be detected with high probability under the
settings of Theorem
5
.

Corollary 1
.

Under the settings of Theorem
5
,
we pick
\lambda_{\mathsf{Lasso}(n)}
in
(
8
) as

c\sigma\sqrt{{\log p}/{n}}
.
Provided that
\min_{\bm{\beta}^{\natural}_{i}\neq 0}(|\bm{\beta}^{\natural}_{i}|^{2}/{\sigma^{2}})\gtrsim\frac{\log p}{n}
,
we have

\textup{sign}(\mathsf{thres}(\widehat{\bm{\beta}};k))=\textup{sign}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural})
hold with probability
1-o(1)
, where

\mathsf{thres}(\cdot;k)
selects the entries with the first
k
-largest magnitude
and is defined in (
9
).

This corollary suggests that the support set
\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural})
can be detected with high probability. Compared with Theorem
5
, Corollary
1
has one additional
assumption on the smallest magnitude of the non-zero entries in
\bm{\beta}^{\natural}
, e.g.,
\min_{\bm{\beta}^{\natural}_{i}\neq 0}(|\bm{\beta}^{\natural}_{i}|^{2}/{\sigma^{2}})\gtrsim\nicefrac{{\log p}}{{n}}
. Notice that this assumption is quite standard
(
Lounici 2008
;
Zhao and Yu 2006
;
Wainwright 2009
)
in studying the property of sign consistency.

Remark 3
.

In
Zhang and Li 2021
, we need

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{\beta}^{\natural}_{i}\neq 0}|\bm{\beta}^{\natural}_{i}|\gtrsim(1+k\sqrt{\nicefrac{{\log p}}{{n}}})\sqrt{\nicefrac{{\log p}}{{n}}}\cdot\left(\|\bm{\beta}^{\natural}\|_{2}\ \sqrt{h\log n}\vee\sigma\right).\vskip 2.84526pt

Meanwhile, our estimator improves this requirement to

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{\beta}^{\natural}_{i}\neq 0}|\bm{\beta}^{\natural}_{i}|\gtrsim\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log p}{n}}.\vskip 2.84526pt

Compared with
Zhang and Li 2021
, our
estimator has a significant improvement. First, our assumption on
\min_{\bm{\beta}^{\natural}_{i}\neq 0}|\bm{\beta}^{\natural}_{i}|
is free from the
total energy
\|\bm{\beta}^{\natural}\|_{2}
.
Even after we factor out the impact of

\|\bm{\beta}^{\natural}\|_{2}
,
(
Zhang and Li 2021
)

still requires
\min_{\bm{\beta}^{\natural}_{i}\neq 0}|\bm{\beta}^{\natural}_{i}|\gtrsim\frac{\sigma(k\log p)}{n}
while our estimator
reduces the requirement to

\min_{\bm{\beta}^{\natural}_{i}\neq 0}|\bm{\beta}^{\natural}_{i}|\gtrsim\sigma\sqrt{\nicefrac{{\log p}}{{n}}}
.

7
Conclusion

We have studied sparse recovery
with shuffled labels.
First, we establish the statistical lower bounds
for both the sample number
n
and
\mathsf{SNR}
.
For the sample number
n
, by exploiting the sparsity of signals,
we manage to reduce the required sample number from

n\geq 2p
to the order of
\Omega(k\log p)
.
For
\mathsf{SNR}
, we have a marginal increase
from
\log\mathsf{SNR}\gtrsim\log n

to
\log\mathsf{SNR}\gtrsim\log n+\nicefrac{{k}}{{n}}\log(\frac{ep}{k})

Then, we present an exhaustive-search based estimator to confirm the

Afterwards, we propose a practical estimator
and show they can yield the correct

under mild
conditions.
Simulations confirm our theorems and suggest
that large sparsity number and Hamming distance
require more samples and stronger signal energy for
correct reconstruction
of

.

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}({\cal E},\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural})~|~\widehat{\bm{\Pi}},\textup{supp}(\widehat{\bm{\beta}}),\bm{y},\mathbf{X})\leq\log 2+\mathsf{H}(\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural}))-\mathbb{P}({\cal E}=0)\log\zeta,\vskip 2.84526pt

where

•

Step III:

Combining the above two steps together, we upper-bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left({\cal E}=0\right)\leq~

\displaystyle\ \dfrac{\log 2+\textup{I}\left(\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural});\bm{y},\mathbf{X}\right)}{\log\zeta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\small{\raisebox{0.6pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.8pt}{1}}}}}}{{=}}\dfrac{\log 2+\textup{I}\left(\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural});\bm{y}~|~\mathbf{X}\right)}{\log\zeta},\vskip 2.84526pt

where
\raisebox{-.8pt}{1}⃝

and

Invoking Lemma

Then we present the computational details.

Step I.

We expand

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}({\cal E},\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural})~|~\widehat{\bm{\Pi}},\textup{supp}(\widehat{\bm{\beta}}),\bm{y},\mathbf{X})

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}(\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural})~|~\widehat{\bm{\Pi}},\textup{supp}(\widehat{\bm{\beta}}),\bm{y},\mathbf{X})+\mathsf{H}({\cal E}~|~\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural}),\widehat{\bm{\Pi}},\textup{supp}(\widehat{\bm{\beta}}),\bm{y},\mathbf{X})

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}(\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural})~|~\bm{y},\mathbf{X}),\vskip 2.84526pt

where in the last equation we have used that
\raisebox{-.8pt}{1}⃝

{\cal E}
is deterministic

\raisebox{-.8pt}{2}⃝

and

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}({\cal E}|\widehat{\bm{\Pi}},\textup{supp}(\widehat{\bm{\beta}}),\bm{y},\mathbf{X})+\mathsf{H}(\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural})|{\cal E},\widehat{\bm{\Pi}},\textup{supp}(\widehat{\bm{\beta}}),\bm{y},\mathbf{X})

Lemma 1
.

Assume that
\bm{\beta}^{\natural}\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{p}
.
we have

\displaystyle\textup{I}\left(\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural});\bm{y}|\mathbf{X}\right)\leq\ \dfrac{n}{2}\log\left(1+\mathsf{SNR}\right),\vskip 2.84526pt

where
\textup{I}(\cdot;\cdot)
denotes the mutual information.

Proof.

Denote
\mathsf{h}(\cdot)
as the differential entropy. We have

\displaystyle\textup{I}\big(\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural});\bm{y}~|~\mathbf{X}\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\small{\raisebox{0.6pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.8pt}{1}}}}}}{{=}}~

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X},\bm{w},\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural})}\left[\mathsf{h}\left(\bm{y}|\mathbf{X}=\bm{x}\right)-\mathsf{h}\left(\bm{y}|\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural}),\mathbf{X}=\bm{x}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\small{\raisebox{0.6pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.8pt}{2}}}}}}{{\leq}}~

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}}\dfrac{1}{2}\log\det\left(\mathbb{E}_{\bm{w},\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}~|~\mathbf{X}=\bm{x}}\bm{y}\bm{y}^{\top}\right)\ -\dfrac{n}{2}\log\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\small{\raisebox{0.6pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.8pt}{3}}}}}}{{\leq}}~

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\log\det\hskip-0.72229pt\bigg[\hskip-0.72229pt\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X},\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}}(\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{n\times n}+\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}\mathbf{X}\bm{\beta}^{\natural}\ \bm{\beta}^{\natural\top}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\bm{\Pi}^{\natural\top})\bigg]-\dfrac{n}{2}\log\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\small{\raisebox{0.6pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.8pt}{4}}}}}}{{=}}~

\displaystyle\dfrac{n}{2}\log\left(\sigma^{2}+\|\bm{\beta}^{\natural}\|_{2}^{2}\right)-\dfrac{n}{2}\log\sigma^{2}=\frac{n}{2}\log(1+\mathsf{SNR}),\vskip 2.84526pt

where
\raisebox{-.8pt}{1}⃝
is because of the definition of conditional
mutual information;

\raisebox{-.8pt}{2}⃝
is due to the property
(
Cover and Thomas 2012
, Theorem 8.6.5)

\displaystyle\mathsf{h}(\mathbf{Z})\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\log\det\textup{Cov}(\mathbf{Z})\leq\ \dfrac{1}{2}\log\det\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{Z}^{\top}\right),\vskip 2.84526pt

for a random variable
\mathbf{Z}
with finite covariance matrix
\textup{Cov}(\mathbf{Z})
,
and
\mathsf{h}(\bm{y}|\bm{\Pi}^{\natural},\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural}),\mathbf{X}=\bm{x})=\mathsf{h}(\bm{w})
as
\bm{\beta}^{\natural}
’s
information is fully encoded in
\textup{supp}(\bm{\beta}^{\natural})
;
in
\raisebox{-.8pt}{3}⃝
we use the
concavity of
\log\det(\cdot)
, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}\log\det(\cdot)\leq\log\det\mathbb{E}(\cdot)
;
and in
\raisebox{-.8pt}{4}⃝
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X},\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}}\left(\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}\mathbf{X}\bm{\beta}^{\natural}\ \bm{\beta}^{\natural\top}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\bm{\Pi}^{\natural\top}\right)=\ \|\bm{\beta}^{\natural}\|^{2}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{n\times n}.\vskip 2.84526pt

Lemma 5
.

Denote
h
as the Hamming distance between

\mathbf{I}
and
\bm{\Pi}
, i.e.,
h\triangleq\mathsf{d_{H}}(\mathbf{I},\bm{\Pi})
.
Assume
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a random
vector satisfying
\bm{x}\sim\mathsf{N}\left(\bm{0},\mathbf{I}_{n\times n}\right)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left({\left\|\left(\mathbf{I}-\bm{\Pi}\right)\bm{x}\right\|}_{2}^{2}\leq\vartheta\right)\leq\ 6\exp\left[\frac{h}{10}\left(\log\left(\frac{\vartheta}{2h}\right)-\frac{\vartheta}{2h}+1\right)\right],\vskip 2.84526pt

for
\vartheta\leq 2h
.

Proof.

Adopting the similar proof tricks as in
Pananjady et al. 2018
, we separately consider
the two cases where
h=2
and
h\geq 3
.

(Case I)
h=2
.
We assume the first two rows are switched w.l.o.g.
Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left({\left\|\left(\mathbf{I}-\bm{\Pi}\right)\bm{x}\right\|}_{2}^{2}\leq\vartheta\right)=~

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{P}\big[\left(x_{1}-x_{2}\right)^{2}\leq\nicefrac{{\vartheta}}{{2}}\big]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\small{\raisebox{0.6pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.8pt}{1}}}}}}{{\leq}}\exp\left(-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}\left(\nicefrac{{\vartheta}}{{4}}-\log\left(\nicefrac{{\vartheta}}{{4}}\right)-1\right)\right),\vskip 2.84526pt

where in
\raisebox{-.8pt}{1}⃝
we use the tail bounds for the
\chi^{2}
random variable

(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}/2
with freedom
1
.

(Case II)
h\geq 3
.

We decompose the non-zero rows
of
\left(\bm{\Pi}-\bm{\Pi}^{\natural}\right)
into three disjoint sets

{\cal I}_{\ell}

(1\leq\ell\leq 3)
such that

(i)
the cardinality of each set
{\cal I}_{\ell}
is lower bounded by

\lfloor h/3\rfloor
, i.e.,
\left\lvert{\cal I}_{\ell}\right\rvert=h_{\ell}\geq\lfloor h/3\rfloor
;
and
(ii)
we have
j
and
\pi(j)
reside within
different sets for an arbitrary index
j
, where
\pi(\cdot)
denotes the permutation map pertaining
to
\bm{\Pi}
.

Define

,
which is a
\chi^{2}
random variable with freedom

.
Then, we can decompose

{\left\|(\mathbf{I}-\bm{\Pi})\bm{x}\right\|}_{2}^{2}=2(Z_{1}+Z_{2}+Z_{3})

and obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left({\left\|\left(\mathbf{I}-\bm{\Pi}\right)\bm{x}\right\|}_{2}^{2}\leq\vartheta\right)\leq~

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{\ell}\leq\nicefrac{{h_{\ell}\vartheta}}{{(2h)}}\right)\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}\ \exp\left(-\frac{h_{\ell}}{2}\left(\frac{\vartheta}{2h}-\log\left(\frac{\vartheta}{2h}\right)-1\right)\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\small{\raisebox{0.6pt}{\textcircled{\raisebox{-.8pt}{2}}}}}}{{\leq}}~

\displaystyle 6\exp\left(-\frac{h}{10}\left(\frac{\vartheta}{2h}-\log\left(\frac{\vartheta}{2h}\right)-1\right)\right),\vskip 2.84526pt

where in
\raisebox{-.8pt}{2}⃝
we use the relation
h_{i}\geq\lfloor h/3\rfloor
.
The proof is completed by summarizing the above two cases.

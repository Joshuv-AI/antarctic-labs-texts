A procdh topology
Shane Kelly, Shuji Saito

Abstract
In this article we propose a definition of a procdh topos. We show that it encodes procdh excision, has bounded homotopy dimension and therefore is hypercomplete and admits a conservative family of fibre functors. We also describe the local rings. As an application, we show that nonconnective K K -theory is the procdh sheafification of connective K K -theory, and that the motivic cohomology recently proposed by Elmanto and Morrow is the procdh sheafification of Voevodsky’s motivic cohomology.

Definition 7.1

Recall that the

\dim_{v}(X)=\sup\left\{\dim R\ \middle|\ \begin{array}[]{c}\exists\ x\in X^{\mathrm{gen}};R\textrm{ is a valuation ring of }k(x)\\
\exists\ \Spec(R)\to X\textrm{ compatible with }R\subseteq k(x)\end{array}\right\}.

The following basic properties of valuative dimension follow directly from well-known facts about valuation rings.

We have

-scheme of finite type.

9
Procdh motivic complex

In this section, we address the following conjecture.

Conjecture 9.1

(Beilinson,
[
Beĭ87
]
, cf. also
[
EM23
, Introduction]
)
.

For any reasonable scheme
X
, there is a natural spectral sequence:

E^{p,q}_{2}=H^{p-q}_{\mathcal{M}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(-q))\Rightarrow K_{-p-q}(X)

(40)

where
K_{*}(X)
is the non-connective algebraic
K
-theory of
X
, and

H^{i}_{\mathcal{M}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(n))
is the
motivic cohomology
of
X
yet to be defined.

When
X
is smooth over a field
k

Theorem 9.2

(
[
FS02
]
and
[
Lev08
]
)
.

Let
\mathrm{Sm}_{k}
denote the category of smooth schemes separated of finite type over
a field
k
.
There exists a complete multiplicative decreasing
\mathbb{N}
-indexed filtration
\biggl\{Fil_{\operatorname{mot}}^{n}K(X)\biggl\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
on
K(X)
and identifications of spectra

\operatorname{gr}_{Fil_{\operatorname{mot}}}^{n}K(X)\simeq\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{sm}}(X)[2n]

(41)

functorial in
X\in\mathrm{Sm}_{k}
.

Above,
\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{sm}}(X)[2n]
is a chain complex of abelian groups regarded as a spectrum via the Eilenberg-Maclane functor
D(\mathbb{Z})\to\mathrm{Spt}
. As a complex of abelian groups, it can be defined as

\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{sm}}(X)=\underline{C}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{tr}(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\wedge n}))(X)[-n]\quad\text{for }X\in\mathrm{Sm}_{k},

(42)

where
\underline{C}_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{tr}(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\wedge q}))[-q]
is Voevodsky’s
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant motivic complex defined in
[
SV00

. This is strictly functorial in
\mathrm{Sm}_{k}
in the sense that it defines a functor between the 1-category
\mathrm{Sm}_{k}
and the 1-category of chain complexes of abelian groups. Scheme-wise this is shown to be quasi-isomorphic to Bloch’s cycle complex,
[
Blo86
]
, in
[
Voe02
, Cor.2]
. Of course, another approach is to just take Eq.(
41
) as the definition for
\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{sm}}(X)[2n]
.

In what follows, we write
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Q}
or
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and continue to let
\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{qcqs}}_{\mathbb{F}}
be the category of qcqs schemes over
\mathbb{F}
and
\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{noe}}_{\mathbb{F}}
be its full subcategory of noetherian schemes.
Recently, Elmanto-Morrow
[

extended Theorem

to all

by using instead of
\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{sm}}
a new motivic complex

\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{EM}}\in\mathrm{PSh}(\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{qcqs}}_{\mathbb{F}},D(\mathbb{Z}))).

They construct
\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{EM}}
by modifying the cdh sheafification
\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\mathrm{cdh}}
of the left Kan extension of
\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{sm}}
along
\mathrm{Sm}_{\mathbb{F}}\to\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{qcqs}}_{\mathbb{F}}
by using Hodge-completed derived de Rham complexes in case
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Q}
and syntomic complexes in case
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
The construction is motivated by trace methods in algebraic
K
-theory using the cyclotomic trace map
{\operatorname{tr}}
from (
39

The purpose of this section is to give a different approach to Conjecture

by using our procdh topology.

Definition 9.3
.

For integers

procdh-local motivic complex

\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\mathrm{procdh}}:=a_{\mathrm{procdh}}L^{\operatorname{sm}}\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{sm}}\in\mathrm{Shv}_{\mathrm{procdh}}(\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{qcqs}}_{\mathbb{F}},D\mathbb{Z})),

as the procdh sheafification of the left Kan extension
L^{\operatorname{sm}}\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{sm}}
of
\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{sm}}
along
\mathrm{Sm}_{\mathbb{F}}\to\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{qcqs}}_{\mathbb{F}}

For

Remark 9.6

By definition, Eq.(

Now, it is a natural question if two constructions

Theorem 9.7
.

Assume given
\mathcal{Z}(n)\in\mathrm{PSh}(\mathrm{Sch}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\mathrm{qcqs}},D(\mathbb{Z})))
and consider the following conditions.

(a)

There is a natural comparison map

\psi:\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{sm}}\to\mathcal{Z}(n)_{|\mathrm{Sm}_{\mathbb{F}}}

in
\mathrm{PSh}(\mathrm{Sm}_{\mathbb{F}},D(\mathbb{Z})))
, whose induced map
\phi:L^{\operatorname{sm}}\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{sm}}\to\mathcal{Z}(n)
has the properties that
\phi(R)
is an equivalence for all procdh local rings
R
with
\length Q(R)
and
\dim R
finite.

(b)

\mathcal{Z}(n)
is finitary. That is,

\mathcal{Z}(n)(\lim_{\lambda}P_{λ})=\underset{\lambda}{\operatorname{colim}}\mathcal{Z}(n)(P_{λ})

for any cofiltered system
(P_{λ})_{λ\in\Lambda}
in
\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{qcqs}}_{\mathbb{F}}
with affine transition morphisms.

(c)

\mathcal{Z}(n)
is a procdh sheaf on Noetherian schemes. That is,

\mathcal{Z}(n)\in\mathrm{Shv}_{\mathrm{procdh}}(\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{noe}}_{\mathbb{F}},D(\mathbb{Z}))).

If (a), (b), and (c) are satisfied then,
\phi
induces an equivalence
\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\mathrm{procdh}}(X)\simeq\mathcal{Z}(n)(X)
for any
X\in\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{noe}}_{\mathbb{F}}
.

Proof.

For procdh sheaves of chain complexes
\mathcal{E}\in\mathrm{Shv}_{\mathrm{procdh}}(\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{noe}}_{\mathbb{F}},D(\mathbb{Z}))
, we have a descent spectral sequence

E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}_{\mathrm{procdh}}(X,\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{procdh}}^{q}\mathcal{E})\Rightarrow H^{p+q}\mathcal{E}(X),

which converges by finiteness of cohomological dimension, Cor.
7.11
. Here
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{procdh}}^{q}\mathcal{E}
is the procdh sheafification of the presheaf of abelian groups
X\mapsto π_{-q}\mathcal{E}(X)
. So since
\mathcal{Z}(n)
and
\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\mathrm{procdh}}
are procdh sheaves, Assumption (c), it suffices to show that the morphism

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{procdh}}^{q}\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\mathrm{procdh}}\to\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{procdh}}^{q}\mathcal{Z}(n)

of procdh cohomology sheaves of abelian groups is an isomorphism on Noetherian schemes. Since the 1-topos
\mathrm{Shv}_{\mathrm{procdh}}(\mathrm{Sch}_{X})
has enough points, Thm.
5.2
,
and cohomology commutes with filtered colimits,
and both
\mathcal{Z}(n)
and
L^{\operatorname{sm}}\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{sm}}
are finitary, Assumption (b),
it suffices to show that for all procdh local rings
\Spec(R)\to X
with
R
as in Assumption (a), the comparison

H^{q}L^{\operatorname{sm}}\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{sm}}(R)\to H^{q}\mathcal{Z}(n)(R)

is an isomorphism for all
q\in\mathbb{Z}
.
This is precisely Assumption (a).
∎

Theorem 9.8

(Elmanto-Morrow)
.

The presheaf
\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{EM}}
satisfies the conditions (a), (b), (c) of Theorem
9.7
.

Proof.

Condition (b), i.e., finitary-ness, is
[
EM23
, Th.4.10(5), Th.4.24(4)]
and
Condition (c), i.e., procdh descent, is
[
EM23
, Th.8.2]
.

We prove (a). Existence of the morphism
\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{sm}}\to\mathcal{Z}(n)_{|\mathrm{Sm}_{\mathbb{F}}}
is contained in
[
EM23
, Th.1.1(9)]
, although we don’t need the full force of their theorem since we are only asking for existence, not the stronger fact that the morphism is an equivalence. Now we want to show that the induced map
L^{\operatorname{sm}}\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{sm}}\to\mathcal{Z}(n)
is an equivalence on procdh local rings
R
with
\length Q(R)
and
\dim R
finite.

By
[
EM23
, Th.7.7]
, it suffices to prove
\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{EM}}(R)
is supported in cohomological degrees
\leq n
for any procdh local ring
R
.
By
[
EM23
, Th.4.10(2), Th.4.24.(2)]
, there are fiber sequences (cf. Eq.(
30
))

\operatorname{Nil}\widehat{L\Omega}^{\geq n}_{R/\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{EM}}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\mathrm{cdh}}(R)\;\text{ if }\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Q},

\operatorname{Nil}{\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{syn}}}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{EM}}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\mathrm{cdh}}(R)\;\text{ if }\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{p},

where
\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\mathrm{cdh}}=a_{\mathrm{cdh}}L^{\operatorname{sm}}\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{sm}}
.
Note that
\operatorname{Nil}\widehat{L\Omega}^{\geq n}_{R/\mathbb{Q}}
and
\operatorname{Nil}{\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{syn}}}(R)
are supported in degrees
\leq n
by Lemma
8.7
and Lemma
8.9
.
Noting that
\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{sm}}
is finitary, the same argument as Lemma
8.9
gives

\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\mathrm{cdh}}(R)=\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\mathrm{cdh}}(R/\mathfrak{N})=(L^{\operatorname{sm}}\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{sm}})(R/\mathfrak{N}).

So,
\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\mathrm{cdh}}(R)
is supported in degrees
\leq n
by Eq.(
43
), which proves the desired assertion.
∎

Corollary 9.9
.

There is an equivalence
\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\mathrm{procdh}}\simeq\mathbb{Z}(n)^{\operatorname{EM}}
in
\mathrm{PSh}(\mathrm{Sch}^{\mathrm{noe}}_{\mathbb{F}},D(\mathbb{Z})))
.

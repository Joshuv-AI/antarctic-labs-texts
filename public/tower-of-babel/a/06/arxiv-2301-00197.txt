Dispersive shocks in diffusive-dispersive approximations of elasticity and quantum-hydrodynamics
Daria Bolbot, Dimitrios Mitsotakis, Athanasios E. Tzavaras

Abstract
. The aim is to assess the combined effect of diffusion and dispersion on shocks in the moderate dispersion regime. For a diffusive dispersive approximation of the equations of one-dimensional elasticity (or p-system), we study convergence of traveling waves to shocks. The problem is recast as a Hamiltonian system with small friction, and an analysis of the length of oscillations yields convergence in the moderate dispersion regime ε , δ → 0 \varepsilon,\delta\to 0 with δ = o ⁡ ( ε ) \delta=o(\varepsilon) , under hypotheses that the limiting shock is admissible according to the Liu E-condition and is not a contact discontinuity at either end state. A similar convergence result is proved for traveling waves of the quantum hydrodynamic system with artificial viscosity as well as for a viscous Peregrine-Boussinesq system where traveling waves model undular bores, in all cases in the moderate dispersion regime.

Introduction

Systems exhibiting interplay of diffusion, dispersion and nonlinear response have been extensively studied in the field of conservation laws,
starting with works on the subject of phase transitions and undercompressive shocks,
e.g.

,

14
,
1

2

An alternative perspective arose from the field of nonlinear dispersive equations with the objective to
study dispersive or dissipative-dispersive shocks
[

[

Peregrine
[

introduced weakly nonlinear and dispersive wave equations in order to study undular bores, a wave appearing
in rivers, atmospheric flows and also in blood vessels composed of a solitary wave followed by undulations.

The Burgers-Korteweg de Vries (KdV) equation

(1)

u_{t}+f(u)_{x}=\varepsilon u_{xx}-\delta u_{xxx}\ ,

has been a testing ground for assessing the interplay of diffusion, dispersion and nonlinearity. Various perspectives of study exist:
(a) to assess the effect of dispersion in the KdV or modified KdV equation (
\varepsilon=0
) on expanding wavetrain solutions connecting two constant states
arising via Whitham modulation theory and termed in the field of dispersive equations as dispersive shock waves; (b) to assess the limiting behavior
of traveling wave solutions when both diffusion and dispersion are present. We refer to
[
9
]
for an in depth presentation of these viewpoints
and their relation.
Here, we focus on a specific aspect of (b), relevant from the viewpoint of systems of conservation laws, namely how oscillatory traveling waves
for diffusive-dispersive systems of two conservation laws approach shocks in the limit
\varepsilon,\delta\to 0
.

Traveling wave solutions have been a focal point for assessing the interplay of diffusive-dispersive systems with studies for the Burgers-KdV equation
with
f(u)=u^{2}

[
4
]
, the modified Burgers-KdV equation with
f(u)=u^{3}

[
14
]
, diffusive-dispersive approximations of elasticity

[

,
3
,
1
]
or hyperbolic-elliptic models for phase transitions
[
23
,
2
]
. Comprehensive presentations
can be found in
[
17
,
10
]
.
The related convergence results from shock profiles to shock waves generally hold in the weak dispersion regime
\delta=O(\varepsilon^{2})
.
Based on such studies and related convergence results from (
1
) to the inviscid Burgers equation
[
22
,
13
]

it was believed for a while that
\delta=O(\varepsilon^{2})
might be the threshold for convergence to Kruzhkov entropy solutions.
This was refuted in
[
20
]
where traveling wave solutions for genuinely nonlinear Burgers-KdV equations were shown to converge to shocks in the range
\delta=o(\varepsilon)
.
In that range traveling waves present relatively strong oscillatory behavior and dispersive effects are significant,
hence it was termed moderate dispersion regime.

The aim here is to examine the convergence of diffusive-dispersive traveling waves
for systems of conservation laws in the moderate dispersion regime.
We note that the use of genuine nonlinearity is avoided and replaced by the Liu shock admissibility condition and
a requirement that the shock is not a (right or left) contact discontinuity.
The analysis is developed for the system of elasticity and extended to other situations where diffusive-dispersive effects play a role:
the quantum hydrodynamic system with diffusion and to diffusive-dispersive models modeling undular bores.

The specific cases analyzed are the following:
We first consider a diffusive-dispersive approximation of the elasticity system

(2)

\displaystyle u_{t}

\displaystyle=v_{x}\ ,

\displaystyle v_{t}

\displaystyle=\sigma(u)_{x}+\varepsilon v_{xx}-\delta u_{xxx}\ ,

where

Our analysis does not cover undercompressive shocks or non-monotone stress-strain relations appearing in phase transitions

2.
Diffusion-dispersion approximation of the elasticity system

We consider a diffusive-dispersive approximation for the one-dimensional elasticity system

(6)

\displaystyle u_{t}

\displaystyle=v_{x}\ ,

\displaystyle v_{t}

\displaystyle=(\sigma(u))_{x}+\varepsilon v_{xx}-\delta u_{xxx}\ ,

where
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R_{+}}\times\mathbb{R}
are the time and space variables,
u
is the strain,
v
the velocity and
\sigma(u)
the stress depending on
u
.
The parameters
\varepsilon>0
,
\delta>0
in (
6
) measure respectively the sizes of diffusion and dispersion.
Throughout this work we assume that
\sigma^{\prime}(u)>0
.

2.1.
Preliminaries: Shocks for the p-system

The system (
6
) is a regularization of the equations of one-dimensional nonlinear elasticity, also called
p
-system:

(7)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}v\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}\sigma(u)\;.\

There are interpretations of (
7
) representing both longitudinal and shear motions; for longitudinal motions
u>0

is interpreted as the longitudinal strain, for shear motions
u\in\mathbb{R}
is a shear strain. When
u
represents longitudinal motions

\varepsilon v_{xx}
needs to be replaced by
(\frac{\varepsilon}{u}v_{x})_{x}
but we will not consider such issues here focusing on the
essential behaviors.

When
\sigma^{\prime}(u)>0
, the system (
7
) is strictly hyperbolic
with wave speeds
\lambda_{1}=-\sqrt{\sigma^{\prime}(u)}
,
\lambda_{2}=\sqrt{\sigma^{\prime}(u)}
.
Shocks are generated by solving the Rankine-Hugoniot conditions

(8)

\displaystyle-s(u_{+}-u_{-})

\displaystyle=(v_{+}-v_{-})\ ,

\displaystyle-s(v_{+}-v_{-})

\displaystyle=(\sigma(u_{+})-\sigma(u_{-}))\ ,

where
s
is the shock speed and
(u_{-},v_{-})
,
(u_{+},v_{+})
the left and right states, respectively.
The shock speed is computed by

(9)

s^{2}=(\sigma(u_{+})-\sigma(u_{-}))/(u_{+}-u_{-})\;,

and (
7
) admits two types of shocks:
1-shocks with
s<0
moving backwards and 2-shocks with
s>0
moving forward.
When
\sigma^{\prime\prime}(u)\neq 0
the system is called genuinely nonlinear; this assumption is not, in general, adopted here
and
\sigma(u)
will be allowed to have inflection points.

Admissibility conditions are imposed on shocks, motivated by either stability considerations
or from requesting that admissible shocks emerge as limits of traveling waves for viscosity regularizations.
We refer to
[
7
, Ch VIII]
for an in depth discussion
of shock-admissibility criteria and
[
24
, Ch 18]
for the construction of shock curves and the solution
of the Riemann problem for (
7
).

For (
7
), under the hyperbolicity assumption
\sigma^{\prime}(u)>0
, the usual admissibility criteria are:

(i)

For genuinely nonlinear systems
\sigma^{\prime\prime}(u)\neq 0
admissible shocks are selected by
the Lax-shock admissibility criterion, stating that admissible 1-shocks (
s<0
) satisfy

(10)

-\sqrt{\sigma^{\prime}(u_{+})}<s<-\sqrt{\sigma^{\prime}(u_{-})}\;,

while admissible 2-shocks (
s>0
) satisfy

(11)

\sqrt{\sigma^{\prime}(u_{+})}<s<\sqrt{\sigma^{\prime}(u_{-})}\;.

(ii)

When
\sigma^{\prime\prime}(u)
changes sign admissible shocks of (
7
) are selected by the
Wendroff E-condition, which for 2-shocks dictates that
\sigma
satisfies

(H
E
)

\frac{\sigma(u)-\sigma(u_{-})}{u-u_{-}}\geq s^{2}\geq\frac{\sigma(u)-\sigma(u_{+})}{u-u_{+}}\ ,

for any
u
between
u_{-}
and
u_{+}
.
The Wendroff E-condition for 1-shocks reads like (
HE
) with the inequalities reversed.

A discriminating criterion capturing the internal stability of shocks and used for the solution
of the Riemann problem for general fluxes is the Liu shock admissibility criterion,
[
7
, Sec 8.4]
.
At the level of the particular system (
7
) the Liu shock admissibility
criterion is equivalent to the Wendroff E-condition. The reader can easily check that the latter ((
HE
) for
s>0
)
implies at the endpoints a weak version of the Lax inequality (
11
),
where strict inequality might be replaced by equality in which case the shock becomes a (right or left) contact
discontinuity.

2.2.
Traveling waves

We look for a traveling wave solution
(u^{\varepsilon,\delta},v^{\varepsilon,\delta})
of (
6
)
in the form

(12)

\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon,\delta}(x,t)

\displaystyle=u\left(\frac{x-st}{\sqrt{\delta}}\right)=u(\tau)\;,

\displaystyle v^{\varepsilon,\delta}(x,t)

\displaystyle=v\left(\frac{x-st}{\sqrt{\delta}}\right)=v(\tau)\,,

connecting states
(u_{-},v_{-})
and
(u_{+},v_{+})
that satisfy the Rankine-Hugoniot conditions (
8
).
Setting
\tau=\frac{x-st}{\sqrt{\delta}}
and retaining the notation
(u(\tau),v(\tau))
for the traveling wave,
we need to solve a system of ordinary differential equations

(13)

\displaystyle-su^{\prime}-v^{\prime}

\displaystyle=0\ ,

\displaystyle-sv^{\prime}-\sigma(u)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{\delta}}v^{\prime\prime}-u^{\prime\prime\prime}\,.

Existence of traveling waves is a well studied problem; the objective is to provide conditions that
guarantee convergence of the traveling wave as
\varepsilon,\delta\to 0
to the associated shock of (
7
) in the moderate dispersion regime

\delta=o(\varepsilon)
.

Consider the problem of constructing traveling wave solutions of (
13
) satisfying

\lim_{\tau\to\pm\infty}u=u_{\pm},\quad\lim_{\tau\to\pm\infty}v=v_{\pm},\quad\lim_{\tau\to\pm\infty}v^{\prime}=\lim_{\tau\to\pm\infty}u^{\prime\prime}=0\,.

Integrating (
13
) in
(-\infty,\tau)
leads to solve the boundary value problem

(14)

\displaystyle u^{\prime\prime}+\frac{s\varepsilon}{\sqrt{\delta}}u^{\prime}-\big(\sigma(u)-\sigma(u_{-})-s^{2}(u-u_{-})\big)

\displaystyle=0\ ,

(15)

\displaystyle u(\pm\infty)=u_{\pm}

\displaystyle\ ,

and define
v
via the equation

(16)

v-v_{-}=-s(u-u_{-})\,.

Necessary for the existence of traveling waves is that the end states satisfy (
8
).

In summary, denoting
c=\frac{s\varepsilon}{\sqrt{\delta}}
,
traveling waves are constructed by solving the ordinary differential equation

(17)

\displaystyle u^{\prime\prime}+cu^{\prime}+\phi(u)

\displaystyle=0\ ,

\displaystyle u(\pm\infty)

\displaystyle=u_{\pm}\ ,

\displaystyle\phi(u)

\displaystyle:=-\big(\sigma(u)-\sigma(u_{-})-s^{2}(u-u_{-})\big)\ .

Following the approach for traveling waves of the KPP equation in
[
11
]
and for the viscous Burgers-KdV equation in
[
20
]
,
Problem (
17
) is viewed as a Hamiltonian system with friction, by setting
w=u^{\prime}

and writing

(18)

\displaystyle\frac{du}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=w\;,

\displaystyle\frac{dw}{d\tau}

\displaystyle=-\phi(u)-cw\;.

Define the potential
\Phi(u)
by
\frac{d\Phi}{du}=\phi(u)
and
\Phi(u_{+})=0
, namely

(19)

\Phi(u)=\int^{u}_{u_{+}}\phi(z)\penalty\ dz=-\int^{u}_{u_{+}}\big(\sigma(z)-\sigma(u_{-})-s^{2}(z-u_{-})\big)\penalty\ dz\ .

The energy of (
18
),
E(u,w)=\frac{w^{2}}{2}+\Phi(u)
, satisfies

(20)

\frac{d}{d\tau}E(u(\tau),w(\tau))=-cw(\tau)^{2}\,.

The associated Hamiltonian system of (
18
) (when
c=0
)
evolves on orbits of constant energy
\tfrac{1}{2}w^{2}+\Phi(u)=E
, and its trajectories are identified
by integrating the differential equations

(21)

\frac{du}{d\tau}=\pm\sqrt{2(E-\Phi(u))}\ .

Existence and uniqueness (up to translation) results for traveling waves of (

The main theme is the study of convergence of traveling waves to shocks in the regime

We first review the framework of pertinent hypotheses

We follow an approach devised in Perthame-Ryzhik

for traveling waves of scalar viscous Burgers-KdV equations (

Consider now the problem (

We adopt the hypotheses (

), (

) and prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1
.

For
\varepsilon,\delta>0
fixed there exists a unique up to horizontal translations traveling wave solution
(u(\tau),v(\tau))
of the form (
12
) to the system (
6
),
connecting the state
(u_{-},v_{-})
on the left to the state

Proof.

There are only two equilibrium points in the range
[u_{-},u_{s}]
of the system (

(u_{-},0)
,
(u_{+},0)
, and the linearized equations around these equilibria
become

\frac{d}{d\tau}\begin{pmatrix}U\\
W\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-\phi^{\prime}(u_{\pm})&-c\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}U\\
W\end{pmatrix}\ .

Consider the equilibrium
(u_{-},0)
. The eigenvalues are computed by

\lambda^{2}+c\lambda-\alpha_{-}=0\qquad\mbox{where \quad$\alpha_{-}=-\phi^{\prime}(u_{-})=\sigma^{\prime}(u_{-})-s^{2}>0$}\ ,

they are

\lambda_{\pm}=-\tfrac{c}{2}\pm\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{c^{2}+4\alpha_{-}}\ ,

both real and satisfy
\lambda_{-}<0<\lambda_{+}
and thus
(u_{-},0)
is a saddle.
The directions of the stable and unstable manifolds are given by the two corresponding eigenvectors

r_{-}=\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
\lambda_{-}\end{matrix}\right)
for the unstable and
r_{+}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\lambda_{+}\end{pmatrix}
for the stable

0<\lambda_{+}=:g(c)=-\tfrac{c}{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{c^{2}+4\alpha_{-}}<\sqrt{\alpha_{-}}\ ,

which is true because

satisfies
g^{\prime}(c)<0
,
g^{\prime\prime}(c)>0
and thus
g(c)<g(0)
.

Next for the equilibrium
(u_{+},0)
, the eigenvalues are computed by

\Lambda^{2}+c\Lambda-\alpha_{+}=0\qquad\mbox{where \quad$\alpha_{+}=-\phi^{\prime}(u_{+})=\sigma^{\prime}(u_{+})-s^{2}<0$}\ ,

and they are

\Lambda_{\pm}=-\tfrac{c}{2}\pm\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{c^{2}+4\alpha_{+}}\ .

We distinguish two cases: (i) When
c^{2}>4|\alpha_{+}|
there are two real roots with
\Lambda_{-}<\Lambda_{+}<0
and the equilibrium
is a stable node. (ii) By contrast, in the range of weak friction

the eigenvalues are complex

\Lambda_{\pm}=-\tfrac{c}{2}\pm\tfrac{1}{2}i\sqrt{|c^{2}+4\alpha_{+}|}\ ,

with negative real part and
(u_{+},0)
is an attracting spiral.

Consider the region

The curves are symmetric with respect to the

therefore

\frac{dw}{du}(0)\sim\pm\left(\sqrt{\sigma^{\prime}(u_{-})-s^{2}}\right)(u-u_{-})\quad\mbox{ for $u>u_{-}$, $u\sim u_{-}$}\ .

Next, in the range
u<u_{s}
and

we have

\displaystyle w^{2}

\displaystyle=2\big(\Phi(u_{-})-\Phi(u)\big)=2\big(\Phi(u_{s})-\Phi(u)\big)

\displaystyle=-2\int_{u}^{u_{s}}\sigma(z)-\sigma(u_{-})-s^{2}(z-u_{-})\;dz\ .

Observe that by (
HoE
) there exist
\alpha>0
,
A>0
such that

s^{2}-A<\frac{\sigma(u)-\sigma(u_{-})}{u-u_{+}}<s^{2}-\alpha\ ,

and thus we can show that for
u\sim u_{s}
,
u<u_{s}
we have

\alpha(u_{s}-u)(u+u_{s}-2u_{+})<w^{2}<A(u_{s}-u)(u+u_{s}-2u_{+})\ ,

which shows that the derivative
\frac{dw}{du}\sim\sqrt{u_{s}-u}
in the vicinity of
u\sim u_{s}
.

The domain
\mathcal{D}
is enclosed by the curves
\tfrac{1}{2}w^{2}+\Phi(u)=\Phi(u_{-})
which is the homoclinic orbit
of the Hamiltonian system (
18
) with
c=0
. The normal to this curve is
N=(\phi(u),w)
. Then we compute that along
the flow of (
18
) it is

\left(\frac{du}{d\tau},\frac{dw}{d\tau}\right)\cdot N=-cw^{2}\leq 0\ .

The domain
\mathcal{D}
is positively invariant along the flow of (
18
) and, by (
23
),
the unstable manifold of the linearized system at
(u_{-},0)
points inside
\mathcal{D}
for
c>0
.

The heteroclinic orbit is constructed as follows. Pick a point on the unstable manifold of (
18
) at
(u_{-},0)
which
for
u\sim u_{-}
is inside
\mathcal{D}
. The flow from this point backwards in time will converge to
(u_{-},0)
. Going forward in time
the flow cannot escape
\mathcal{D}
and by the Poincarè-Bendixon theorem it will converge to
(u_{+},0)
, giving the desired
heteroclinic orbit. This is a one-dimensional object and unique up to time-shifts.
∎

Two regimes distinguish the behavior of the heteroclinic orbit. For
c^{2}>4|\alpha_{+}|
the orbit is monotone.
For
0<c^{2}<4|\alpha_{+}|
using the stable manifold theorem the orbit has an oscillatory behavior (see
[
4
]
).
In the following section we study the size of oscillations of the orbit.

3.
The effect of weak friction on Hamiltonian systems

The aim of this section is to study the limit of traveling wave solutions of (

3.1.
Periods of orbits of the Hamiltonian system

When
c=0
, the system becomes Hamiltonian

(34)

\begin{cases}&\frac{du}{d\tau}=w\;,\\
&\frac{dw}{d\tau}=\phi(u)\;.\end{cases}

The orbit emanating from the saddle
(u_{-},0)
with energy
E=E_{max}=\Phi(u_{-})
is a homoclinic.
The remaining orbits for
0<E<E_{max}
are periodic. Using (
21
) the period is computed by

(35)

T(E)=2\int_{u_{1}(E)}^{u_{2}(E)}\frac{du}{\sqrt{2(E-\Phi(u))}}\;,

where
u_{1}(E)
and
u_{2}(E)
satisfy
\Phi(u_{1}(E))=\Phi(u_{2}(E))=E
, see Fig.
2
.
First we prove the following:

Lemma 4
.

Under hypotheses (
HL
), (
HsE
), (
HoE
), there exists
T_{0}>0
such that

T(E)\geq T_{0}>0\,,\quad\forall E>0\,.

and
T(E)\to\infty
as
E\to E_{max}
.

Proof.

We estimate the period first for
large energies
. The integral in (
35
) is split in three parts

(36)

\frac{1}{2}T(E)=\left(\int_{u_{1}(E)}^{u_{+}-\alpha}+\int_{u_{+}-\alpha}^{u_{+}+\beta}+\int_{u_{+}+\beta}^{u_{2}(E)}\right)\frac{du}{\sqrt{2(E-\Phi(u))}}\ .

The main contribution comes from the interval
(u_{1}(E),u_{+}-\alpha)
and is estimated as follows: From
\Phi(u_{1}(E))=E
, (
22
) and (
30
),
we have

\displaystyle E-\Phi(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{u_{1}(E)}^{u}\big(\sigma(z)-\sigma(u_{-})-s^{2}(z-u_{-})\big)\penalty\ dz

\displaystyle>\int_{u_{1}(E)}^{u}\eta(z-u_{-})\penalty\ dz

\displaystyle=\eta(u-u_{1}(E))\left(\frac{u+u_{1}(E)}{2}-u_{-}\right),\qquad u_{-}<u_{1}(E)<u<u_{+}-\alpha\ ,

and similarly

\displaystyle E-\Phi(u)

\displaystyle<\int_{u_{1}(E)}^{u}H(z-u_{-})\penalty\ dz

\displaystyle=H(u-u_{1}(E))\left(\frac{u+u_{1}(E)}{2}-u_{-}\right),\qquad u_{-}<u_{1}(E)<u<u_{+}-\alpha\,.

Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{H}}F(\rho)\leq\int_{u_{1}(E)}^{u_{+}-\alpha}\frac{du}{\sqrt{2(E-\Phi(u))}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\eta}}F(\rho)\ ,

where
\rho=2(u_{1}(E)-u_{-})
,

F(\rho):=\int_{0}^{(u_{+}-\alpha-u_{1}(E))}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{s(s+\rho)}}<\infty\quad\mbox{for}\quad\rho>0\ ,

and
u_{+}-\alpha-u_{1}(E)
is bounded away from zero in the range of large energies.
Using the monotone convergence theorem,

F(\rho)\to\infty\quad\mbox{as \quad$\rho=2(u_{1}(E)-u_{-})\to 0$}\ ,

that is the contribution of that integral to the period becomes infinite as
E\to E_{max}

and the periodic orbit approaches a homoclinic orbit.

Again we consider
large energies
and focus on the complementary region
u\in(u_{+},u_{2}(E))
.
Using (
22
), (
9
) and (
31
), we deduce

\displaystyle E-\Phi(u)=\Phi(u_{2}(E))-\Phi(u)

\displaystyle=-\int_{u}^{u_{2}(E)}\big(\sigma(z)-\sigma(u_{+})-s^{2}(z-u_{+})\big)\penalty\ dz

\displaystyle>m\int_{u}^{u_{2}(E)}(z-u_{+})\penalty\ dz

\displaystyle=\tfrac{m}{2}(u_{2}(E)-u)\left(u_{2}(E)+u-2u_{+}\right)\ ,

\displaystyle\int_{u_{+}}^{u_{2}(E)}\frac{du}{\sqrt{2(E-\Phi(u)})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\int_{0}^{u_{2}(E)-u_{+}}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{s\big(2(u_{2}(E)-u_{+})-s\big)}}<\infty\,,

that is the contribution of this integral to the period is finite.
Finally, for
E>E_{m}
, in the region
u\in[u_{+}-\alpha,u_{+}+\beta]
, the potential energy satisfies
0<\Phi(u)<E_{m}

and the contribution of the middle integral to the period is easily seen to be bounded,

\frac{\beta-\alpha}{\sqrt{2E}}<\int_{u_{+}-\alpha}^{u_{+}+\beta}\frac{du}{\sqrt{2(E-\Phi(u))}}\leq\frac{\beta-\alpha}{\sqrt{2(E-E_{m})}}\,.

Next, consider the regime of
small energies

0<E<E_{m}
and analyze first the range
u_{+}-\alpha<u_{1}(E)\leq u\leq u_{+}
.
Setting
J_{\alpha}=[u_{+}-\alpha,u_{+}]

and using (
28
) we have

\displaystyle\min_{J_{\alpha}}\sigma^{\prime}(u)

\displaystyle<\frac{\sigma(u_{+})-\sigma(u)}{u_{+}-u}<\max_{J_{\alpha}}\sigma^{\prime}(u)\ ,

\displaystyle-\left(\max_{J_{\alpha}}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(u)\right)(u_{+}-u)

\displaystyle<\sigma(u_{+})-\sigma(u)-s^{2}(u_{+}-u)<-\left(\min_{J_{\alpha}}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(u)\right)(u_{+}-u)\ .

Set
m=\min_{J_{\alpha}}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(u)>0
,
M=\max_{J_{\alpha}}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(u)>0
and use

E-\Phi(u)=\int_{u_{1}(E)}^{u}\sigma(z)-\sigma(u_{+})-s^{2}(z-u_{+})dz\ ,

to obtain

m\int_{u_{1}(E)}^{u}(u_{+}-z)\penalty\ dz<E-\Phi(u)<M\int_{u_{1}(E)}^{u}(u_{+}-z)\penalty\ dz\ ,

and hence

(37)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}I(E)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{u_{1}(E)}^{u_{+}}\frac{du}{\sqrt{2(E-\Phi(u))}}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}I(E)\ ,

\displaystyle\mbox{where}\qquad I(E)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{u_{+}-u_{1}(E)}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{s\big(2(u_{+}-u_{1}(E))-s\big)}}\ .

Again for
small energies

0<E<E_{m}
we consider next the range
u_{+}\leq u<u_{2}(E)<u_{+}+\beta

and similarly obtain the bound

(38)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}J(E)\leq\int_{u_{+}}^{u_{2}(E)}\frac{du}{\sqrt{2(E-\Phi(u))}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}J(E)\ ,

\displaystyle\mbox{where}\qquad J(E)=\int_{0}^{u_{2}(E)-u_{+}}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{s\big(2(u_{2}(E)-u_{+})-s\big)}}\ ,

m=\min_{J_{\beta}}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(u)
,
M=\max_{J_{\beta}}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(u)
,
J_{\beta}=[u_{+},u_{+}+\beta]
and
m,M>0
.

We conclude from (
37
), (
38
) that periodic orbits with small energies have periods
of the order of
I(E)+J(E)
. The limiting behavior as
E\to 0
is computed
via the integral

(39)

K(a):=\int_{0}^{a}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{s(2a-s)}}=\int_{0}^{a}\frac{ds}{\sqrt{a^{2}-(a-s)^{2}}}=\arcsin\frac{\tau}{a}\;\Bigg|_{0}^{a}=\frac{\pi}{2}\ .

Therefore,
J(E)=I(E)=\frac{\pi}{2}
and
T(E)
remains bounded from below as
E\to 0
.
The limit of
T(E)
can be calculated but we do not pursue this point further.
∎

3.2.
Effect of friction on orbits

We study the oscillatory behavior of solutions
(u_{c}(\tau),w_{c}(\tau))
to (
18
) in the range
0<c<c^{\star}
.
Let
x_{n}
be the consecutive maxima of
u_{c}(\tau)
and
y_{n}
the minima. By a shift of the independent variable we set the first maximum
at
x_{0}=0
; this gives the ordering
y_{0}=-\infty<x_{0}=0<y_{1}<x_{1}<\cdots<y_{n}<x_{n}<\cdots
.

Large energies.
The following lemma, for large energies, estimates the distance between two consecutive extrema, and the energy drop between these points.

Lemma 5
.

For
c>0
sufficiently small and energies
E_{m}\leq{E(y_{n})}<E_{max}
, there is a constant
K>0
such that

E(y_{n+1})-E(y_{n})\leq-Kc\,,\qquad y_{n+1}-y_{n}\leq\frac{K}{(cn)^{1/2}}\ .

Proof.

Solutions of (
18
) satisfy the energy dissipation equation (
20
).
Using the normalization
u^{\prime}(0)=0
, we find that the energy drop between
-\infty
and
\tau_{0}=0
is

(40)

\ E_{max}-E(0)=c\int_{-\infty}^{0}w^{2}(t)\penalty\ dt\;.

Let
(u_{c}(\tau),w_{c}(\tau))
be a solution of (
18
) and
(u_{0}(\tau),w_{0}(\tau))
be a solution of the Hamiltonian system (
34
).
Suppose that both solutions emanate from the same initial data, that is

(u_{c}(\tau_{0}),w_{c}(\tau_{0}))=(u_{0}(\tau_{0}),w_{0}(\tau_{0}))=(u_{0},w_{0})\in\mathcal{D}\ ,

where
\mathcal{D}
is the domain in (
24
).
Since
\mathcal{D}
is an invariant domain for both (
18
) and (
34
), using Gronwall’s lemma, we obtain

(41)

\displaystyle|w_{c}(\tau)-w_{0}(\tau)|+|u_{c}(\tau)-u_{0}(\tau)|

\displaystyle\leq cK_{T}\sup_{|\zeta-\tau_{0}|\leq{T}}|w_{c}(\zeta)|

\displaystyle\leq c\,\bar{K}_{T}\qquad\quad\text{ for}\;|\tau-\tau_{0}|\leq{T},

where
K_{T}
depends on the Lipschitz constant (on the domain
\mathcal{D}
) and on
T
.

We will show that

\int_{-\infty}^{0}w_{c}^{2}(\tau)\penalty\ d\tau\geq\kappa>0\;.

Indeed, without loss of generality, taking
\tau_{0}=0
in (
41
), we have
|w_{c}(\tau)-w_{0}(\tau)|\leq c\bar{K}
for

\tau\in[-1,0]
. Hence,

(42)

\int_{-\infty}^{0}w_{c}^{2}(\tau)\penalty\ d\tau\geq\int_{-1}^{0}w_{c}^{2}(\tau)\penalty\ d\tau\geq\int_{-1}^{0}w_{0}^{2}(\tau)\penalty\ d\tau-\tilde{K}c\geq K\ ,

and

E(0)\leq E_{max}-Kc\;.

Let now
x_{n}
be a maximum and
y_{n}
,
y_{n+1}
be consecutive minima of
u_{c}(\tau)
.
Consider two orbits
(u_{c},w_{c})
and
(u_{0},w_{0})
that meet at the same point in phase space, with
u_{c}(y_{n})=u_{0}(y_{n})
,
w_{c}(y_{n})=w_{0}(y_{n})=0
,
and let
T
be the period of the periodic orbit.
We claim that

(43)

|(x_{n}-y_{n})-\tfrac{T}{2}|\leq o(1)\quad\text{and}\quad|(y_{n+1}-y_{n})-T|\leq o(1)\quad\mbox{as $c\to 0$}\,.

This comparison between the period
T
and the time elapsed between two consecutive extrema
x_{n}-y_{n}
is a consequence of the fact
that the orbits
(u_{c},w_{c})
converge to the orbit
(u_{0},w_{0})
as
c\to 0
. To see that observe that by (
41
),

|u_{c}(x_{n})-u_{0}(x_{n})|\leq K_{(x_{n}-y_{n})}c\,,\quad|u_{c}(y_{n}+\tfrac{T}{2})-u_{0}(y_{n}+\tfrac{T}{2})|\leq K_{T}c\,,

and then use (
35
) and Lemma
4
to obtain

\displaystyle(x_{n}-y_{n})-\tfrac{T}{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{u_{0}(y_{n})}^{u_{0}(x_{n})}\frac{du}{\sqrt{2(E-\Phi(u))}}-\int_{u_{0}(y_{n})}^{u_{0}\big(y_{n}+\tfrac{T}{2}\big)}\frac{du}{\sqrt{2(E-\Phi(u))}}

\displaystyle=\int_{u_{0}\big(y_{n}+\tfrac{T}{2}\big)}^{u_{c}(x_{n})+O(c)}\frac{du}{\sqrt{2(E-\Phi(u))}}\to 0\qquad\mbox{as $c\to 0$}\ .

Similarly is proved the second identity in (
43
).

This shows that for
E_{m}\leq{E(y_{n})}<E_{max}
we have
y_{n+1}-y_{n}\geq T_{0}
where
T_{0}
does not depend on
n
, and thus

(44)

E(y_{n+1})-E(y_{n})\leq-c\int_{y_{n}}^{y_{n+1}}w_{c}^{2}(\tau)\penalty\ d\tau\leq-Kc\ ,

and for the energy at
y_{n}
,

(45)

E(y_{n})\leq E_{max}-Kcn\;.

For
(u(\tau),w(\tau))
solution of (
18
), we
proceed to estimate the distance between two consecutive minimum at
y_{n}
and maximum at
x_{n}
.
The domain
[y_{n},x_{n}]
is split to three regions:

(I)

u_{-}<u(y_{n})<u(a_{n})=u_{+}-\alpha
for
y_{n}\leq\tau\leq a_{n}

(II)

u_{+}-\alpha=u(a_{n})<u_{+}<u(b_{n})=u_{+}+\beta
for
a_{n}\leq\tau\leq b_{n}

(III)

u_{+}+\beta=u(b_{n})<u(x_{n})<u_{s}
for
b_{n}\leq\tau\leq x_{n}

In Region (II) we have

0<u(b_{n})-u(a_{n})=w(\tau_{\star})(b_{n}-a_{n})\qquad\mbox{for some $\tau_{\star}\in[a_{n},b_{n}]$}\ .

Since the orbit of
(u(\tau),w(\tau)
is near the orbit
(u_{0}(\tau),w_{0}(\tau))
we have
w(\tau)\geq\kappa>0
and we conclude

b_{n}-a_{n}\leq K
.

Consider next the Region (I) and observe that using (
45
), (
22
), (
30
),

\displaystyle Knc

\displaystyle\leq\Phi(u_{-})-\Phi(u(y_{n}))

\displaystyle=(-\phi(v))(u(y_{n})-u_{-})\qquad\mbox{for some $u_{-}<v<u_{+}-\alpha$}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\max_{u_{-}<v<u_{+}-\alpha}(-\phi(v))\right)(u(y_{n})-u_{-})

\displaystyle\leq\max_{u_{-}<v<u_{+}-\alpha}\big(H(v-u_{-})\big)(u(y_{n})-u_{-})

\displaystyle\leq K^{\prime}(u(y_{n})-u_{-})

Using (
17
) and (
30
),

u^{\prime\prime}+cu^{\prime}=-\phi(u)>\eta(u-u_{-})>\eta(u(y_{n})-u_{-})>Knc

whence

\big(u(\tau)-u_{-}\big)^{\prime}\geq Kn\big(1-e^{-c(\tau-y_{n})}\big)

and integrating once again and using
e^{-cx}\geq 1-cx+\tfrac{1}{2}c^{2}x^{2}-\tfrac{1}{6}c^{3}x^{3}
we derive

\displaystyle u(\tau)-u(y_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{Kn}{c}\Big(c(\tau-y_{n})+e^{-c(\tau-y_{n})}-1\Big)

\displaystyle\geq Knc\tfrac{1}{2}(\tau-y_{n})^{2}\big(1-\frac{1}{3}c(\tau-y_{n})\big)

\displaystyle\geq Knc\tfrac{1}{4}(\tau-y_{n})^{2}\qquad\mbox{provided $c(x_{n}-y_{n})<\tfrac{3}{2}$}

We conclude

\tau-y_{n}\leq\frac{K}{\sqrt{nc}}\qquad\mbox{for $y_{n}\leq\tau\leq a_{n}$}\ .

On the Region (III)
b_{n}\leq\tau\leq x_{n}
the estimate proceeds along similar lines: First using (
31
),

Knc\leq\Phi(u_{s})-\Phi(u(x_{n}))\leq M(u_{s}-u(x_{n}))\ .

Using (
17
) and (
31
)

(u_{s}-u)^{\prime\prime}+c(u_{s}-u)^{\prime}=\phi(u)\geq\min_{u\in[u_{+}+\beta,u_{s}]}\phi(u)=:A>0\ ,

and after an integration

u^{\prime}(\tau)\geq\frac{K}{c}\big(e^{c(x_{n}-\tau)}-1\big)\qquad b_{n}\leq\tau\leq x_{n}\ ,

and another one

\displaystyle u(x_{n})-u(\tau)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{K}{c}\int_{\tau}^{x_{n}}\big(e^{c(x_{n}-z)}-1\big)dz

\displaystyle\geq\frac{K}{2}(x_{n}-\tau)^{2}\qquad\mbox{for $b_{n}\leq\tau\leq x_{n}$}\ .

We conclude that

x_{n}-\tau\leq\Big(\frac{2(u(x_{n})-u(\tau))}{K}\Big)^{\tfrac{1}{2}}\leq K^{\prime}\ .

Putting all together we obtain

(46)

x_{n}-y_{n}\leq\frac{K}{(nc)^{1/2}}\ ,

and similarly by a symmetric argument
y_{n+1}-x_{n}\leq\frac{K}{(nc)^{1/2}}
which completes the proof.
∎

At this point we estimate the ”length” of the highly oscillatory part of the solution.
Since the energy drop per period is of size
Kc
the total number of oscillations is
N=K/c
. The length of this regiion is

(47)

L=\sum_{n=1}^{N=K/c}y_{n+1}-y_{n}\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N=K/c}\frac{K}{(cn)^{1/2}}=\frac{K}{c^{1/2}}\int_{1}^{\tfrac{K}{c}}\frac{dx}{x^{1/2}}\leq\frac{K^{\prime}}{c}\ .

Small Energies.
For energies
E(\tau)\in(0,E_{m})
, we show the exponential damping behavior of the solution. Since
\Phi^{\prime\prime}(u)>0
in this region,
the situation is analogous to the analysis in
[
20
]
. Using the energy dissipation (
20
), we obtain

3.4.
Is
\delta=o(\varepsilon)
optimal ?

The range
\delta=o(\varepsilon)
is optimal for the linearized system associated with (
17

equation around
u_{+}
takes the form

\frac{d^{2}\tilde{u}}{d\tau^{2}}+c\frac{d\tilde{u}}{d\tau}+\alpha\tilde{u}=0\ ,

with
c=\tfrac{s\varepsilon}{\sqrt{\delta}}
,
\alpha=\phi(u_{+})>0
and
\tilde{u}=u-u_{+}
. The characteristic polynomial for the linear differential equation
has complex eigenvalues when
c\ll 1
which are

\rho_{\pm}=-\frac{c}{2}\pm\frac{i}{2}\sqrt{|4\alpha-c^{2}|}
,
and its solution is

\tilde{u}(\tau)=Ae^{-\tfrac{c}{2}\tau}\cos(\omega\tau+\beta)\ ,

where
A
is an amplitude,
\omega=\sqrt{\alpha-\tfrac{c^{2}}{4}}
the frequency and
\beta
a phase shift.
When expressing the solution in terms of the original variables we have

u^{\varepsilon,\delta}\left(\frac{x-st}{\sqrt{\delta}}\right)-u_{+}=Ae^{-\tfrac{\varepsilon}{\delta}\tfrac{s}{2}(x-st)}\cos\left(\left(\sqrt{\alpha-\tfrac{s^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}{4\delta}}\right)\frac{x-st}{\sqrt{\delta}}+\beta\right)\ .

In the regime of moderate dispersion
\delta=o(\varepsilon)
the right side converges to zero as
\delta,\varepsilon\to 0
and the traveling wave converges to a shock.

In the moderate dispersion regime
\delta=o(\varepsilon)
, the convergence to a shock wave is in a strong sense as often expected in shock wave theory.
In the complementary region
\varepsilon=o(\delta)
the solution of the linearized equation oscillates vigorously around the constant state
u_{+}
.
Such an oscillatory tail converges to a constant state in a weak sense since the average of the oscillations around the constant
u_{+}

cancel out.
This behavior characterizes only the linearized problem and it is not clear if it will persist for the nonlinear problem.
Weak convergence could conceivably give a meaning on how the limiting state
u_{+}
is achieved even in a regime of strong dispersion.

4.1.
Shocks in gas dynamics

The system of isentropic gas dynamics

A 2-shock associated to the

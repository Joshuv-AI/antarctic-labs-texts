Abstract
. We obtain an explicit criterion for p p -ary functions to produce association schemes in terms of their Walsh spectrum. Employing this characterization, we explicitly find a correlation between p p -ary bent functions and association schemes; to be more exact, we prove that a p p -ary bent function induces a p p -class association scheme if and only if the function is weakly regular. As applications of our main criterion, we construct many infinite families of few-class association schemes arising from p p -ary functions. Furthermore, we present four classes of p p -ary two-weight linear codes, which are constructed from the association schemes produced in this paper.

1.
Introduction

Association schemes are originated from combinatorial design theory and character theory of finite groups.
In algebraic combinatorics
[
1
,
13
]
, association schemes are connected with both combinatorial designs and coding theory
[
7
]
. There have been many developments on construction of association schemes in an algebraic approach
[
6
]
as well as using many objects such as Schur rings
[
16
]
, nonlinear functions
[
2
,
20
,
21
,
24
]
, partitions
[
12
,
28
,
29
]
, and so on.

Recently, there have been active developments on construction of association schemes using bent functions or plateaued functions. Introducing some of the previous results, Tan
et al.

[
22
]
constructed many strongly regular graphs by using ternary bent functions and then Pott
et al.

[
21
]
obtained
p
-class association schemes by employing
p
-ary weakly regular bent functions, where
p
is an odd prime number. Afterwards, Mesnager and Sinak
[
19
]
extended the construction of association schemes to weakly regular plateaued functions. Most recently, Özbudak and Pelen
[
20
]
constructed some
2
-class symmetric association schemes from non-weakly regular bent functions.
Inspired by the previous works, it is natural to investigate a characterization of
p
-ary functions in terms of
association schemes.

In this paper, we obtain an explicit criterion for
p
-ary functions to produce association schemes in terms of their Walsh spectrum. Employing this characterization, we explicitly find a correlation between
p
-ary bent functions
and association schemes; to be more exact, we prove that a
p
-ary bent function induces a
p
-class association scheme if and only if the function is weakly regular. In fact, there is a previous result on the study of connection of
p
-ary bent functions with strongly regular graphs
[
14
, Theorem A]
. As applications of our main criterion, we construct many infinite families of
t
-class association schemes arising from
p
-ary functions, where
t=2,3,4,5,6.
Furthermore, we present four classes of
p
-ary two-weight linear codes, which are constructed from the association schemes produced in this paper.

Our paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce some necessary definitions and some previous results. In Section 3, using the Walsh spectrum of
p
-ary functions, we obtain a main result on association schemes (Theorem
3.4
). In Section 4, we consider some applications of the main result. Specifically, we first prove
that a
p
-ary bent function induces a
p
-class association scheme if and only if the function is weakly regular (Theorem 4.2). Secondly, we construct several classes of association schemes by considering the
p
-ary functions such that
f(ax)=f(x)
for all
(a,x)\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We find some 2-class, 3-class, 4-class, 5-class, and 6-class association schemes (Propositions 4.6, 4.9, 4.11). Finally, we get some two-weight linear codes from the schemes (Proposition 4.15).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Symmetric association schemes

In this section, we introduce some basic notions regarding association schemes, weakly regular
p
-ary bent functions, and Fourier-reflexive partitions.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a finite set. A
symmetric
d
-class association scheme

(X,\{R_{l}\})_{i=0}^{d}

is a partition of
X\times X
into binary relations
R_{0},R_{1},\ldots,R_{d}
satisfying the following properties:

\bullet

R_{0}=\{(x,x):x\in X\};

\bullet

R_{i}
is symmetric for
i=1,2,\ldots,d,
that is,
(x,y)\in R_{i}
if and only if
(y,x)\in R_{i};

\bullet
for all
i,j,k\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\}
there is an integer

p^{k}_{ij}
such that for all
(u,v)\in R_{k}
,

p^{k}_{ij}=|\{w\in X:(u,w)\in R_{i}\mbox{ and }(w,v)\in R_{j}\}|.

One of the well-known constructions of association schemes uses the Schur rings as follows.
Let
G=\cup_{i=0}^{d}D_{i}
be a finite abelian group, where the union is pairwise disjoint and

D_{i}\neq\emptyset
for every
i=1,2,\ldots,d
, and the following properties are satisfied:

\bullet

D_{0}=\{1_{G}\}
;

\bullet

D^{-1}_{i}=D_{i}
for any
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\}
, where
D^{-1}=\{g^{-1}:g\in D_{i}\}
;

\bullet

D_{i}D_{j}=\sum_{k=0}^{d}p^{k}_{ij}D_{k}
for any
i
and
j
with
0\leq i,j\leq d
, where
p^{k}_{ij}
are integers.

Then the subset
\langle D_{0},\dots,D_{d}\rangle
in
\mathbb{C}[G]
spanned by
D_{0},\ldots,D_{d}
is
so-called a
Schur ring
over
G
. The configuration
(G,{R_{i}})_{0\leq i\leq d}
forms a

d
-class symmetric association scheme on
G
, where
R_{i}=\{(g,h):gh^{-1}\in D_{i}\}
for
0\leq i\leq d
.

2.2.
Weakly regular
p
-ary bent functions

Let
f
be a
p
-
ary function
from
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, where
q=p^{m}
.
We define
D_{f,i}
associated with
f
by the set

D_{f,i}=\{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}:f(\beta)=i\}.

Let
\zeta_{p}
be a primitive
p
-th root of unity in a complex field
\mathbb{C}
.
The
Walsh-Hadamard transform

W_{f}
of a
p
-ary function
f
is a complex-valued
function of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
defined by

W_{f}(\beta)=\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\zeta^{f(x)-\operatorname{Tr}(\beta x)}_{p},

where
\operatorname{Tr}
is the trace function from
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
The inverse Walsh-Hadamard transform of a
p
-ary function
f
is given by

\zeta^{f(\beta)}_{p}=p^{-m}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}}W_{f}(x)\zeta^{\operatorname{Tr}(\beta x)}_{p}.

A
p
-ary function
f
is called

\emph{bent}
if
|W_{f}(\beta)|^{2}=q
for any
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
It is known that if
f
is a
p
-ary bent function, then

\displaystyle W_{f}(\beta)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c l}\pm p^{\frac{m}{2}}\zeta^{g(\beta)}_{p}&\mbox{ if }\;m\mbox{ even, or }m\mbox{ odd and }p\equiv 1~(\mbox{mod }4),\\
\pm\sqrt{-1}p^{\frac{m}{2}}\zeta^{g(\beta)}_{p}&\mbox{ if }m\mbox{ odd and }p\equiv 3~(\mbox{mod }4)\end{array}\right.

for some
p
-ary function
g:\mathbb{F}_{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
, which is
called the
associated function of

f
.
A
p
-ary bent function
f
is
weakly regular
if there
is a complex number
u
with unit magnitude such that
W_{f}(\beta)=up^{\frac{m}{2}}\zeta^{\tilde{f}(\beta)}_{p}

for some
p
-ary function
\tilde{f}:\mathbb{F}_{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
In this case, we call
\tilde{f}
the
dual
of
f
.
In particular, when
u=1
, we say that
f
is
regular

p
-ary bent.
We call
\epsilon
the
sign
of
W_{f}
of a weakly regular
p
-ary bent function
f

when
W_{f}(\beta)=\epsilon\eta(-1)^{m/2}p^{\frac{m}{2}}\zeta^{\tilde{f}(\beta)}_{p}

for every
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\eta(a)=a^{(p-1)/2}
.

2.3.
Fourier-reflexive partitions

It is well known that every additive character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
can be expressed as

\chi_{a}(x)=\zeta_{p}^{\operatorname{Tr}(ax)},\ x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}

for some
a
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
In particular, we call
\chi_{0}
the
trivial additive character
and
\chi_{1}
the
canonical additive character
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The orthogonality relation of additive characters (see
[
15
]
) is given by

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\chi_{a}(x)=q\delta_{0,a},

where
\delta
is the Kronecker delta function.
For a subset
D
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we define

\chi_{\beta}(D):=\sum_{\alpha\in D}\zeta^{\operatorname{Tr}(\beta\alpha)}_{p}.

We say that
P=\{P_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
is a
partition
of a set
X
if all the blocks
P_{i}
are pairwise disjoint and cover
X
. We write
|P|
for the number of blocks in the partition
P
. Two partitions
P
and
Q
of a set X are
called
identical
if
|P|=|Q|
and each block of two partitions coincides after suitable reordering.

Zinoviev and Ericson in
[
29
]
proved the following result.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
29
]

Let
P=\{P_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
be a partition of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
where
P_{0}=\{0\}
,
and let
R_{i}
be a partition of
\mathbb{F}_{q}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}
defined by

(\alpha,\beta)\in R_{i}\Longleftrightarrow\alpha-\beta\in P_{i},\;\;i=0,1,\ldots,d.

Then
(\mathbb{F}_{q},\{R_{l}\})_{l=0}^{d}
is a symmetric association scheme on
\mathbb{F}_{q}

if and only if there is another partition
Q=\{Q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that for any
i,j\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\},

(1)
the sum
\chi_{\alpha}(P_{i})

only depends on the number
j
such that
\alpha\in Q_{j}
and

(2)
the sum
\chi_{\beta}(Q_{j})

only depends on the number
i
such that
\beta\in P_{i}
.

In this case, one can show that the partition
Q
is unique up to permutation on
\{0,1,\ldots,d\}
. The partition
P
in Lemma 2.2 is a so-called
B
-partition

[
29
]
. Moreover, if
P=Q
, then the partition is also called
F
-
partition

[
28
]
.

We define Fourier-reflexive partitions introduced by Gluesing-Luerssen
[
12
]
as follows.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
P=\{P_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
be a partition of
G=\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The dual partition, denoted by
\widehat{P}
, is the partition of the character group
\widehat{G}
defined by the equivalence relation

\chi\sim_{\widehat{P}}\chi^{\prime}\Longleftrightarrow\chi(P_{i})=\chi^{\prime}(P_{i})\mbox{ for all }i=0,\ldots,d,

where
\chi
and
\chi^{\prime}
are characters of
\widehat{G}
. By identifying the group
G
with its character group
\widehat{G}
via
\beta\mapsto\chi_{\beta}
, the equivalence relation is restated as follows:

\beta\sim_{\widehat{P}}\beta^{\prime}\Longleftrightarrow\chi_{\beta}(P_{i})=\chi_{\beta^{\prime}}(P_{i})\mbox{ for all }i=0,\ldots,d,

where
\chi_{\beta}
and
\chi_{\beta^{\prime}}
are characters of
\widehat{G}
.
The partition
P
is called
Fourier-reflexive
if
\widehat{\widehat{P}}=P
.

We point out that if
\widehat{P}=\{Q_{j}\}_{j=0}^{d^{\prime}}
is the dual partition of
P
, then
Q_{j}=\{0\}
for some
j=0,1,\ldots,d^{\prime}
. Indeed,
let
0\sim_{\widehat{P}}\beta
. Then
\chi_{\beta}(P_{i})=|P_{i}|
for all
i=0,1,\ldots,d
; therefore, we have
q=\sum_{i=0}^{d}|P_{i}|=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\chi_{\beta}(P_{i})=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\zeta^{\operatorname{Tr}(\beta x)}_{p}=q\delta_{0,\beta}
. Thus we get
\beta=0
.
From this observation, we may always assume that
Q_{0}=\{0\}
.

We also point out that the blocks
Q_{j}
of
\widehat{P}=\{Q_{j}\}_{j=0}^{d^{\prime}}
become

Q_{j}=\{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}:\chi_{\beta}(P_{i})=c_{i,j}\text{ for all }i=0,\ldots,d\},

where
c_{i,j}
are complex numbers.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
12
, Theorem 2.4]

A partition
P=\{P_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is Fourier-reflexive if and only if
|P|=|\widehat{P}|
.

By using Lemmas
2.2
and
2.4
, we have the following result.

Theorem 2.5
.

[
12
,
29
]

Let
P=\{P_{i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
be a partition of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
where
P_{0}=\{0\}
, and let
\widehat{P}=\{Q_{j}\}_{j=0}^{d^{\prime}}
be the dual partition of
P
, where
Q_{0}=\{0\}
.
Let
R_{i}
be a partition of
\mathbb{F}_{q}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}
defined by

(\alpha,\beta)\in R_{i}\Longleftrightarrow\alpha-\beta\in P_{i},\;\;i=0,1,\ldots,d.

A necessary and sufficient condition such that
(\mathbb{F}_{q},\{R_{i}\})_{i=0}^{d}
is a symmetric association scheme on
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is
d=d^{\prime}
.

3.
A main result

In this section, we discuss the main result of this paper. We find an explicit criterion
for
p
-ary functions to produce association schemes in terms of their Walsh spectrum.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f
be a
p
-ary function. For a given
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, let

N^{f}_{i,j}(\beta)=|\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}:f(x)=i,~\operatorname{Tr}(\beta x)=j\}|.

Then we have

p^{2}N^{f}_{i,j}(\beta)=-q+p|D_{f,i}|+p\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\delta_{j,\operatorname{Tr}(\beta x)}+\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}\left(\zeta_{p}^{-i}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}\zeta_{p}^{jz}W_{f}(z\beta)\right)^{\sigma_{y}},

where
\sigma_{y}
is an automorphism of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})
over
\mathbb{Q}

such that
\sigma_{y}(\zeta_{p})=\zeta_{p}^{y}
for
y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
.

Proof.

We have

p^{2}N^{f}_{i,j}(\beta)=\sum_{y,z\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\zeta_{p}^{y(f(x)-i)+z(\operatorname{Tr}(\beta x)-j)}=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(1+\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}\zeta^{y(f(x)-i)}_{p})(1+\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}\zeta^{z(\operatorname{Tr}(\beta x)-j}_{p}))\\
=q+\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}}\zeta_{p}^{y(f(x)-i)}+\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}}\zeta_{p}^{z(\operatorname{Tr}(\beta x)-j)}+\sum_{y,z\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\zeta^{y(f(x)-i)+z(\operatorname{Tr}(\beta x)-j)}_{p}.

(3.1)

The first two double sums in Eq. (
3.1
) are computed as follows:

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}}\zeta_{p}^{y(f(x)-i)}=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(-1+p\delta_{i,f(x)})=-q+p|D_{f,i}|

and

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}}\zeta_{p}^{z(\operatorname{Tr}(\beta x)-j)}=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(-1+p\delta_{j,\operatorname{Tr}(\beta x)})=-q+p\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\delta_{j,\operatorname{Tr}(\beta x)}.

The last double sum
\sum_{y,z\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\zeta^{y(f(x)-i)+z(\operatorname{Tr}(\beta x)-j)}_{p}

in Eq. (
3.1
) is simplified as the following:

\displaystyle\sum_{y,z\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}\zeta_{p}^{-iy-jz}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\zeta^{yf(x)+z\operatorname{Tr}(\beta x)}_{p}=\sum_{y,z\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}\zeta_{p}^{-iy-jz}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\zeta^{f(x)+\operatorname{Tr}(\frac{z}{y}\beta x)}_{p})^{\sigma_{y}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{y,z\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}\zeta_{p}^{-iy-jz}W_{f}(-\frac{z}{y}\beta)^{\sigma_{y}}=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}(\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}\zeta_{p}^{-i-j\frac{z}{y}}W_{f}(-\frac{z}{y}\beta))^{\sigma_{y}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}(\zeta_{p}^{-i}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}\zeta_{p}^{jz}W_{f}(z\beta))^{\sigma_{y}}.

The result follows immediately by combining the computation results obtained as above all together.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
f
be a
p
-ary function.
Then for
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
i\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we have

\chi_{\beta}(D_{f,i})=\frac{1}{p^{2}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\zeta^{j}_{p}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}\left(\zeta_{p}^{-i}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}\zeta_{p}^{jz}W_{f}(z\beta)\right)^{\sigma_{y}}.

Proof.

The result follows from the fact that
\chi_{\beta}(D_{f,i})=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}N^{f}_{i,j}(\beta)\zeta^{j}_{p}
and using Lemma
3.1
.
∎

The following proposition plays an important role in proving our main result.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
f
be a
p
-ary function and
f(\mathbb{F}_{p})
be the image of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
under
f
. Then for any two distinct elements
\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, we have

\chi_{\beta}(D_{f,i})=\chi_{\beta^{\prime}}(D_{f,i})\text{ for all }i\in f(\mathbb{F}_{p})\iff W_{f}(z\beta)=W_{f}(z\beta^{\prime})\text{ for all }z\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}.

Proof.

Let us put
I=f(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

(\Rightarrow)
The Walsh-Hadamard transform of a
p
-ary function
f
is expressed by

W_{f}(\beta)=\sum_{i\in I}\chi_{-\beta}(D_{f,i})\zeta^{i}_{p}.

By the assumption, for
z\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
we have

\chi_{-z\beta}(D_{f,i})=\chi_{-\beta}(D_{f,i})^{\sigma_{z}}=\chi_{-\beta^{\prime}}(D_{f,i})^{\sigma_{z}}=\chi_{-z\beta^{\prime}}(D_{f,i})

for all
i\in I
. Thus for
z\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
we have

W_{f}(z\beta)=\sum_{i\in I}\chi_{-z\beta}(D_{f,i})\zeta^{i}_{p}=\sum_{i\in I}\chi_{-z\beta^{\prime}}(D_{f,i})\zeta^{i}_{p}=W_{f}(z\beta^{\prime}).

(\Leftarrow)

By Lemma
3.1
and Corollary
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle W_{f}(z\beta)=W_{f}(z\beta^{\prime})\mbox{ for all }z\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}(\zeta_{p}^{jz}-1)W_{f}(z\beta)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}(\zeta_{p}^{jz}-1)W_{f}(z\beta^{\prime})\mbox{ for all }j\in\mathbb{F}_{p}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}\left(\zeta_{p}^{-i}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}(\zeta_{p}^{jz}-1)W_{f}(z\beta)\right)^{\sigma_{y}}=\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}\left(\zeta_{p}^{-i}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}(\zeta_{p}^{jz}-1)W_{f}(z\beta^{\prime})\right)^{\sigma_{y}}

\displaystyle\mbox{ for all }i\in I\text{ and }j\in\mathbb{F}_{p}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle N^{f}_{i,j}(\beta)-N^{f}_{i,0}(\beta)=N^{f}_{i,j}(\beta^{\prime})-N^{f}_{i,0}(\beta^{\prime})\text{ for all }i\in I\text{ and }j\in\mathbb{F}_{p}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\chi_{\beta}(D_{f,i})=\chi_{\beta^{\prime}}(D_{f,i})\mbox{ for all }i\in I.

The last implication follows from the fact that
\{\zeta_{p},\ldots,\zeta^{p-1}\}
is a basis for
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})
over
\mathbb{Q}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

We denote by
S^{*}
the set of nonzero elements of a subset
S
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Let
f
be a
p
-ary function with
f(0)=0
.
Then
P=\{D^{*}_{f,i}\}_{i\in f(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})}
is a partition of
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}
.
Let
R_{i}
be a partition of
\mathbb{F}_{q}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}
defined by

\displaystyle R_{-1}=\{(\beta,\beta):\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\},\text{ and }(\alpha,\beta)\in R_{i}\Longleftrightarrow\alpha-\beta\in D^{*}_{f,i}\text{ for }i\in f(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}).

(3.2)

If
(\mathbb{F}_{q},\{R_{i}\})_{i\in f(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})})
, where
R_{-1}
is deleted,
forms a symmetric
d
-class association scheme, then we say that the partition
P=\{D^{*}_{f,i}\}_{i\in f(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})}
of
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}

induces an
d
-class association scheme

(\mathbb{F}_{q},\{R_{i}\}_{i\in f(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})})
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
f
be a
p
-ary function with
f(0)=0
and let
I=f(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})
. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(i)
The partition
P=\{D^{*}_{f,i}\}_{i\in I}
of
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}
induces an
|I|
-class association scheme
(\mathbb{F}_{q},\{R_{i}\}_{i\in I})
defined in (
3.2
);

(ii)
The sizes of
I
and
\{(W_{f}(\beta),W_{f}(2\beta),\ldots,W_{f}((p-1)\beta)):\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}\}
are same.

Proof.

Let
V(\beta)=(W_{f}(\beta),W_{f}(2\beta),\ldots,W_{f}((p-1)\beta))
be a vector. By Proposition
3.3
, we have

\chi_{\beta}(D_{f,i})=\chi_{\beta^{\prime}}(D_{f,i})\text{ for all }i\in f(\mathbb{F}_{p})\iff V(\beta)=V(\beta^{\prime})

for two distinct elements
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
.
Then the result follows from Theorem
2.5
.
∎

We give the following example for illustration of Theorem
3.4
.

Example 3.5
.

Let
p=3
,
q=27
, and
f(x)=\operatorname{Tr}(2x-x^{5})
. Using Magma, we get the following facts:

(1) The set of the Walsh-Hadamard values of
f
at nonzero elements is
\{0,9,-9\}
.

(2) The set
\{(W_{f}(\beta),W_{f}(2\beta)):\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{27}^{*}\}
is equal to
\{(-9,0),(0,-9),(0,0),(0,9),(9,0)\}
.

By Theorem
3.4
, we can see that the partition

of
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}
cannot induce a
3
-class association scheme.

4.1.
Characterization of
p
-ary bent functions

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f
be a
p
-ary bent function with
f(0)=0
and let
g
be the associated
p
-ary function of
f
. Then following statements are true.

(i)

f
is surjective;

(ii)
the Walsh-Hadamard transform of
f
can be written as

\displaystyle W_{f}(\beta)=\mu(\beta)(p^{*})^{\frac{m}{2}}\zeta^{g(\beta)}_{p},

where
\mu(\beta)=\pm 1
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
p^{*}=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p
and
g
is the associated
p
-ary function of
f
;

(iii)
if
m
is even, then

\sum_{\beta\in D_{g,i}}\mu(\beta)-\sum_{\beta\in D_{g,0}}\mu(\beta)=-p^{\frac{m}{2}}\text{ for all }i\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}

and if
m
is odd, then

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta\in D_{g,i}}\mu(\beta)-\sum_{\beta\in D_{g,0}}\mu(\beta)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c l}p^{\frac{m-1}{2}}&\mbox{ if }i\in S,\\
-p^{\frac{m-1}{2}}&\mbox{ if }i\in NS,\end{array}\right.

where
S
(resp.,
NS
) is the set of squares (resp., non-squares) in
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
.

Proof.

(i)
Assume that there exists
i\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that
D_{f,i}=\emptyset.
By Lemma 3.1, we have
N_{i,0}^{f}(0)=0
,
|D_{f,i}|=0
, and
\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}\left(\zeta_{p}^{-i}W_{f}(0)\right)^{\sigma_{y}}=-q.
Note that
|W_{f}(0)|=\sqrt{q}
; therefore, we have

\bigg|\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}\left(\zeta_{p}^{-i}W_{f}(0)\right)^{\sigma_{y}}\bigg|\leq(p-1)\sqrt{q}<q,

which is a contradiction. This shows that
f
is surjective.

(ii)
This is a direct consequence of Eq. (2.1).

(iii)
Case
1
:
m
is even.

By using
1=-\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\zeta^{i}_{p}
and
q\zeta^{f(0)}_{p}=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}W_{f}(\beta)
for any
p
-ary function
f
, we have

-\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}p^{m}\zeta^{i}_{p}=p^{m}=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}W_{f}(\beta)=p^{\frac{m}{2}}\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mu(\beta)\zeta^{g(\beta)}_{p}\\
=p^{\frac{m}{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\sum_{\beta\in D_{g,i}}\mu(\beta)\zeta^{i}_{p}=p^{\frac{m}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\left(\sum_{\beta\in D_{g,i}}\mu(\beta)-\sum_{\beta\in D_{g,0}}\mu(\beta)\right)\zeta^{i}_{p}.

The result follows from the fact that
\{\zeta_{p},\ldots,\zeta^{p-1}_{p}\}
is a basis for
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})
over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Case
2
:
m
is odd.

We note that

\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\zeta_{p}^{i^{2}}=\sqrt{p^{*}},
where
p^{*}=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p
.

We then have

p^{m}=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}W_{f}(\beta)=(p^{*})^{\frac{m}{2}}\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mu(\beta)\zeta^{g(\beta)}_{p};

thus we get

p^{\frac{m-1}{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\zeta_{p}^{i^{2}}=p^{m}(p^{*})^{-\frac{m}{2}}=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\sum_{\beta\in D_{g,i}}\mu(\beta)\zeta_{p}^{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\left(\sum_{\beta\in D_{g,i}}\mu(\beta)-\sum_{\beta\in D_{g,0}}\mu(\beta)\right)\zeta_{p}^{j}.

We can see that
\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\zeta_{p}^{i^{2}}=1+2\sum_{i\in S}\zeta_{p}^{i}=-\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\zeta^{i}_{p}+2\sum_{i\in S}\zeta_{p}^{i}=\sum_{i\in S}\zeta_{p}^{i}-\sum_{i\in NS}\zeta_{p}^{i}
; this implies that

p^{\frac{m-1}{2}}\left(\sum_{i\in S}\zeta_{p}^{i}-\sum_{i\in NS}\zeta_{p}^{i}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\left(\sum_{\beta\in D_{g,i}}\mu(\beta)-\sum_{\beta\in D_{g,0}}\mu(\beta)\right)\zeta_{p}^{j},

and the result follows.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
p
be an odd prime number, and let
f
be a
p
-ary bent function with
f(0)=0
. The partition
P=\{D^{*}_{f,i}\}_{i\in f(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})}
induces a
p
-class association scheme
(\mathbb{F}_{q},\{R_{i}\}_{i\in f(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})})
defined in Equation (
3.2
) if and only if
f
is weakly regular.

Proof.

Let us put
A_{+}=\{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}:\mu(\beta)=1\}
and
A_{-}=\{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}:\mu(\beta)=-1\}
.

(\Rightarrow)

By Lemma
4.1
, we have
|{f(\mathbb{F}_{q})}|=p
.
Let
\widehat{P}=\{Q_{j}\}_{j=0}^{p-1}
be the dual partition of
P
.
We have
Q_{-1}=\{0\}
.
By Proposition
3.3
, we get that
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
are in the same block of
\widehat{P}

\iff

W_{f}(z\beta)=W_{f}(z\beta^{\prime})
for all
z\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}

\iff

g(z\beta)=g(z\beta^{\prime})
and
\mu(z\beta)=\mu(z\beta^{\prime})
for all
z\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
. This shows that

\displaystyle\text{either }Q_{j}\subseteq A_{+}\;\text{ or }\;Q_{j}\subseteq A_{-}\text{ for }j\in\mathbb{F}_{p}.

(4.1)

Case 1:
m
is even. We divide this case into two subcases as follows.

Case 1-1: assume
D_{g,0}\subseteq A_{+}
. By Lemma
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta\in D_{g,i}}\mu(\beta)=|D_{g,0}|-p^{\frac{m}{2}}\text{ for all }i\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}.

(4.2)

We claim that
D_{g,i}\subseteq A_{+}
for all
i\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
.
Assume not, that is,
D_{g,i}
is not contained in
A_{+}
for some
i\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
, for contradiction.
Then by Eq. (
4.1
), it is enough to consider the following two cases:

(i)

D_{g,i}\subseteq A_{-}
for all
i\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
, or

(ii)

Q_{j}\subseteq A_{+}
and
Q_{l}\subseteq A_{-}
for some
j,l\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
with
j\neq l
.

Assume that
(i)
is true. By Eq. (
4.2
), we have
|D_{g,i}|+|D_{g,0}|=p^{\frac{m}{2}}
for all
i\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
. Adding all these equations together with
|D_{g,0}|+|D_{g,0}|=2|D_{g,0}|
, we have that
q+p|D_{g,0}|=(p-1)p^{\frac{m}{2}}+2|D_{g,0}|
or
(p-2)|D_{g,0}|=-q+(p-1)p^{\frac{m}{2}}<0
,
which is impossible.

Assume that
(ii)
is true. By Eq. (
4.2
), we have that
|D_{g,j}|=|D_{g,0}|-p^{\frac{m}{2}}\geq 0
and
|D_{g,l}|=-|D_{g,0}|+p^{\frac{m}{2}}\geq 0
; thus,
|D_{g,0}|=p^{\frac{m}{2}}
.
By Eq.
(\ref{eqn:4.2})
, we have
\sum_{\beta\in D_{g,i}}\mu(\beta)=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
, and so
|D_{g,i}|=0
for all
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
by Eq. (
4.1
), which is impossible because
p^{\frac{m}{2}}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}|D_{g,i}|=q
.
This proves our claim; therefore,
f
is weakly regular bent.

Case 1-2: assume
D_{g,0}\subseteq A_{-}
. By Lemma
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta\in D_{g,i}}\mu(\beta)=-|D_{g,0}|-p^{\frac{m}{2}}<0\text{ for all }i\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}.

(4.3)

By (
4.1
) and (
4.3
), we have
D_{g,i}\subseteq A_{-}
for all
i\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
, and thus
f
is weakly regular.

Case 2:
m
is odd. We also divide this case into two subcases as follows.

Case 2-1: assume
D_{g,0}\subseteq A_{+}
. By Lemma
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta\in D_{g,i}}\mu(\beta)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c l}|D_{g,0}|+p^{\frac{m-1}{2}}&\mbox{ if }i\in S,\\
|D_{g,0}|-p^{\frac{m-1}{2}}&\mbox{ if }i\in NS,\end{array}\right.

We claim that
D_{g,i}\subseteq A_{+}
for all
i\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
.
Assume not, that is,
D_{g,i}
is not contained in
A_{+}
for some
i\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
, for contradiction.
Since
D_{g,i}\subseteq A_{+}
for
i\in S
by Eqs. (
4.1
) and (4.4),
it is sufficient to consider the following two cases:

(i)

D_{g,i}\subseteq A_{-}
for all
i\in NS
, or

(ii)

Q_{j}\subseteq A_{+}
and
Q_{l}\subseteq A_{-}
for some
j,l\in NS
with
j\neq l
.

Assume that
(i)
is true. By Eq. (4.4), we have
|D_{g,i}|=|D_{g,0}|+p^{\frac{m-1}{2}}

for
i\in S
and
|D_{g,i}|=-|D_{g,0}|+p^{\frac{m-1}{2}}
for
i\in NS
.
Adding all these equations together with
|D_{g,0}|=|D_{g,0}|
leads to
q=|D_{g,0}|+2\frac{p-1}{2}p\frac{m-1}{2}
,
and so
|D_{g,0}|=q-(p-1)p\frac{m-1}{2}

for

|D_{g,i}|=-|D_{g,0}|+p^{\frac{m-1}{2}}=-q+p^{\frac{m+1}{2}}<0
, which is impossible.

Assume that
(ii)
is true. By the same argument as Case 1-1
(ii)
,
we have a contradiction. This proves that

is weakly regular bent.

Case 2-2: assume
D_{g,0}\subseteq A_{-}
.
By Lemma
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta\in D_{g,i}}\mu(\beta)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c l}-|D_{g,0}|+p^{\frac{m-1}{2}}&\mbox{ if }i\in S,\\
-|D_{g,0}|-p^{\frac{m-1}{2}}&\mbox{ if }i\in NS.\end{array}\right.

To show that
D_{g,i}\subseteq A_{-}
for all
i\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
,
assume not, for contradiction.
Since
D_{g,i}\subseteq A_{-}
for

by Eqs. (
4.1
) and
(4.5)
,
it is enough to consider the following two cases:

(i)

D_{g,i}\subseteq A_{+}
for all

, or

(ii)

Q_{j}\subseteq A_{+}
and
Q_{l}\subseteq A_{-}
for some
j,l\in S
with
j\neq l
.

By the same argument as Case 2-1, we also get a contradiction.
This proves that
f
is weakly regular bent.

(\Leftarrow)

Since
f
is weakly regular bent, we have
W_{f}(\beta)=\epsilon\eta(-1)^{m/2}p^{\frac{m}{2}}\zeta^{\tilde{f}(\beta)}_{p}
and

\tilde{f}
is also weakly regular bent. Then

is surjective by Lemma

; thus,
|P|=|\widehat{P}|
by Proposition

. The result follows from Theorem
3.4
.
∎

4.2.
Construction of association schemes

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
f
be a
p
-ary function. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

f(ax)=f(x)
for all
(a,x)\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}
;

(ii)

W_{f}(a\beta)=W_{f}(\beta)
for all
(a,\beta)\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}
;

(iii)

aD_{f,i}=D_{f,i}
for all
(a,i)\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}
;

(iv)

\chi_{\beta}(D_{f,i})
are rational integers for all
(i,\beta)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
. For
a\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
, we have

W_{f}(a\beta)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\zeta_{p}^{f(x)-\operatorname{Tr}(a\beta x)}=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\zeta_{p}^{f(a^{-1}x)-\operatorname{Tr}(\beta x)}=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\zeta_{p}^{f(x)-\operatorname{Tr}(\beta x)}=W_{f}(\beta).

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
.
By using the inverse Walsh-Hadamard transform, for
a\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}

p^{m}\zeta^{f(x)}_{p}=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}W_{f}(\beta)\zeta^{\operatorname{Tr}(\beta x)}_{p}=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}W_{f}(a^{-1}\beta)\zeta^{\operatorname{Tr}(\beta x)}_{p}\\
=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}W_{f}(\beta)\zeta^{\operatorname{Tr}(a\beta x)}_{p}=p^{m}\zeta^{f(ax)}_{p}.

(iii)\Rightarrow(ii)
.
By using
W_{f}(\beta)=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\chi_{\beta}(D_{f,i})\zeta^{i}_{p}
and
\chi_{\beta}(aD_{f,i})=\chi_{a\beta}(D_{f,i})
for
a\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
, we get the result.

(i)\Rightarrow(iii)
. It is straightforward.

(iii)\Rightarrow(iv)
. We have
\chi_{\beta}(D_{f,i})^{\sigma_{a}}=\chi_{\beta}(aD_{f,i})=\chi_{\beta}(D_{f,i})
for all
a\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}
, and the result follows.

(iv)\Rightarrow(iiii).
We have
\chi_{\beta}(aD_{f,i})=\chi_{\beta}(D_{f,i})^{\sigma_{a}}=\chi_{\beta}(D_{f,i})
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The result follows from Lemma IV.2 in
[
14
]
.
∎

By Theorem 3.4 and Lemma 4.1, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
f
be a
p
-ary function with
f(0)=0
and
f(ax)=f(x)
for all
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
and let
I=f(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})
. Then the partition
P=\{D^{*}_{f,i}\}_{i\in I}
of
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}
induces an
|I|
-class association scheme
(\mathbb{F}_{q},\{R_{i}\}_{i\in I})
defined in Equation (
3.2
) if and only if the sizes of
I
and
\{W_{f}(\beta):\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}\}
are same.

In
[
25
]
, the authors presented three classes of monomial functions with three-valued Walsh spectrum. Using the results in
[
25
]
and Corollary 4.5, we have the following propositions.

Let
\phi(n)
be the Euler
\phi
-function, and recall that
f(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})=\{f(x):x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\}
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Assume that
m
is a positive integer,
r
is an odd prime such that
p\geq 3
is a primitive root modulo
r^{m}
. Let
q=p^{\phi(r^{m})}
and
f(x)=\operatorname{Tr}_{q/p}(x^{\frac{q-1}{r^{m}}})
.

(i)
Then
\{D^{*}_{f,i}\}_{i\in f(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})}
induces a 2-class association scheme if and only if
|f(\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q})|=2
; equivalently,
m=1
or
r\equiv 1\pmod{p}
.

(ii)
Then
\{D^{*}_{f,i}\}_{i\in f(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})}
induces a 3-class association scheme if and only if
|f(\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q})|=3
; equivalently,
m>1
and
r\not\equiv 1\pmod{p}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}=\langle\gamma\rangle
. Then
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}=\langle\gamma^{\frac{q-1}{p-1}}\rangle
. By the assumption on
p
and
r
,
\gcd(r,p-1)=1
and
r^{m}\mid q-1=p^{\phi(r^{m})}-1
,
r^{m}\mid\frac{q-1}{p-1}
. Hence,
f(ax)=f(a)
for any
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
, and thus we can use Theorem
3.4
. By the proof of
[
25
, Theorem 6]
, we have
f(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})=\{\phi(r^{m}),-r^{m-1},0\}
, where the frequency of
0
as the image of
f
is
q-1-\frac{q-1}{r^{m-1}}
.
By
[
25
, Theorem 10]
,

\{W_{f}(\beta):\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\}=\{\sqrt{q}\zeta_{p}^{\phi(r^{m})}+A,\sqrt{q}\zeta_{p}^{-r^{m-1}}+A,\sqrt{q}+A\},

where
A=1-\frac{\sqrt{q}+1}{r^{m}}(r^{m}-1+\zeta_{p}^{\phi(r^{m})}+\phi(r)(\zeta_{p}^{-r^{m-1}}-1))
.
Since
\gcd(r,p)=1
, we have that
-r^{m-1}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
and
\phi(r^{m})=r^{m-1}(r-1)\not\equiv-r^{m-1}\pmod{p}
;
thus,
\phi(r^{m})\equiv 0\pmod{p}
if and only if
r\equiv 1\pmod{p}.

Then the result follows from Corollary 4.5.
∎

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}=\langle\gamma\rangle
. Then
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}=\langle\gamma^{\frac{q-1}{p-1}}\rangle
.
From the assumption on
r
and
p

.

f(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})=\bigg\{\frac{\phi(r^{m})}{2},\frac{(\sqrt{r}-1){r^{m-1}}}{2},\frac{(-\sqrt{r}-1){r^{m-1}}}{2},0\bigg\},

.

(ii) If

4.3.
Construction of two-weight linear codes

An
[n,k,d]
linear code
\mathcal{C}
over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}

is a
k
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
with minimum Hamming distance
d
.
Let
A_{i}
be the number of codewords in
\mathcal{C}

with Hamming weight
i
. The weight enumerator of

\mathcal{C}
is defined by

1+A_{1}z+\cdots+A_{n}z^{n}.

The sequence
(1,A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{n})
is called the
weight distribution
of

\mathcal{C}
. We say that a code
\mathcal{C}
has
t
-weight
if the number of nonzero
A_{i}
’s
in the sequence
(A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{n})
is equal to
t
.

Let
D
be a subset of
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}
.
A linear code
\mathcal{C}_{D}
of length
|D|
over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is defined by

\mathcal{C}_{D}=\{c_{D}(\beta)=(\operatorname{Tr}(\beta\alpha))_{\alpha\in D}\;:\;\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}.

(4.8)

The code
\mathcal{C}_{D}
is called a
trace code
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and the set
D
is called the
defining set
of
\mathcal{C}_{D}
. This generic construction was first introduced by Ding
et al.

[
9
,
10
]
. Recent results on this generic construction of linear codes from cryptographic functions are due to the references
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
8
,
11
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
23
,
26
,
27
]
.

The dimension of the linear code
\mathcal{C}_{D}
is determined by the following lemma.

Lemma 4.12
.

[
10
, Theorem 6]
Let
D
be a subset of
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}
. The dimension of the linear code
\mathcal{C}_{D}
is equal to the dimension
of
\left\langle D\right\rangle
, where
\left\langle D\right\rangle
is the subgroup of
\mathbb{F}^{*}_{q}

generated by
D
.

For each
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, the weight of
c_{D}(\beta)\in\mathcal{C}_{D}
is given by

\operatorname{wt}(c_{D}(\beta))=|D|-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\alpha\in D}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\zeta_{p}^{a\operatorname{Tr}(\alpha\beta)}=(1-\frac{1}{p})|D|-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}(\chi_{\beta}(D))^{\sigma_{a}}.

(4.9)

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
f
be a
p
-ary function satisfying that
f(ax)=f(x)
for all
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
.
Then for
i\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
,

\operatorname{wt}(c_{D^{*}_{f,i}}(\beta))=\frac{p-1}{p}|D^{*}_{f,i}|-\frac{p-1}{p^{2}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}}(\zeta^{-i}_{p}W_{f}(\beta))^{\sigma_{y}}.

Proof.

From Corollary
3.2
and the assumption on
f
, it follows that

\chi_{\beta}(D^{*}_{f,i})=\frac{1}{p^{2}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\zeta^{j}_{p}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}\left(\zeta_{p}^{-i}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}\zeta_{p}^{jz}W_{f}(z\beta)\right)^{\sigma_{y}}=\frac{1}{p^{2}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\zeta^{j}_{p}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}\left(\zeta_{p}^{-i}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}\zeta_{p}^{jz}W_{f}(\beta)\right)^{\sigma_{y}}\\
=\frac{1}{p^{2}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}\left(\zeta_{p}^{-i}\sum_{z\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}W_{f}(\beta)\right)^{\sigma_{y}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\zeta^{j}_{p}(-1+p\delta_{0,j})=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}\left(\zeta_{p}^{-i}W_{f}(\beta)\right)^{\sigma_{y}}.

(4.10)

By Lemma
4.4
, (
4.9
), we get

\operatorname{wt}(c_{D^{*}_{f,i}}(\beta))=(1-\frac{1}{p})|D^{*}_{f,i}|-\frac{1}{p}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}^{*}_{p}}(\chi_{\beta}(D^{*}_{f,i}))^{\sigma_{a}}=(1-\frac{1}{p})|D^{*}_{f,i}|-\frac{p-1}{p}\chi_{\beta}(D^{*}_{f,i}).

(4.11)

The proof completes by combing Eq. (
4.10
) with Eq. (
4.11
).
∎

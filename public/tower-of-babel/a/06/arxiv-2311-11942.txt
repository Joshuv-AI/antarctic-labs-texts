Decorrelation estimates for translated measures under diagonal flows
Michael Björklund, Reynold Fregoli, Alexander Gorodnik

Abstract
. A profound link between Homogeneous Dynamics and Diophantine Approximation is based on an observation that Diophantine properties of a real matrix B B are encoded by the corresponding lattice Λ B \Lambda_{B} translated by a multi-parameter semigroup a ⁡ ( t ) a(t) . We establish quantitative decorrelation estimates for measures supported on leaves a ⁡ ( t ) ​ Λ B a(t)\Lambda_{B} with the error terms depending only on the minimum of the pairwise distances between the parameters. The proof involves a careful analysis of the translated measures in the products of the spaces of unimodular lattices and establishes quantitative equidistributions to measures supported on various intermediate homogeneous subspaces.



1.
Introduction

When studying Diophantine properties of a real
(m\times n)
-matrix

B
, it is often natural to consider the corresponding unimodular lattice

\Lambda_{B}:=\{(p+Bq,q):\,\,p\in\mathbb{Z}^{m},\,q\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m+n},

and view it as a point in the space
X
of all
(m+n)
-dimensional unimodular
lattices. It turns out that many Diophantine properties of
B
can be equivalently expressed in
terms of the asymptotic behaviour of the orbit of
\Lambda_{B}
under the
diagonal flow

a(t):=\hbox{diag}\big(e^{t^{(1)}},\ldots,e^{t^{(m)}},e^{-t^{(m+1)}},\ldots,e^{-t^{(m+n)}}\big)\in\operatorname{SL}_{m+n}(\mathbb{R}),

where
t=(t^{(1)},\ldots,t^{(m+n)})
belongs to the additive sub-semigroup
A^{+}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m+n}

defined by

A^{+}:=\big\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m+n}:\,t^{(1)}+\cdots+t^{(m)}=t^{(m+1)}+\cdots+t^{(m+n)}\big\}.

For instance, Kleinbock and Margulis
[
13
]
observed
that Khinchin’s classical theorem in Diophantine approximate is intimately
related to the visits to shrinking cuspidal neighborhoods in
X
of the orbit
of
\Lambda_{B}
under the sub-flow
g_{t}=a(t/m,\ldots,t/m,t/n,\ldots,t/n)
for
t\geq 0
.
In particular, the crucial Borel-Cantelli property needed in their proof was derived
from the asymptotic ”independence” of the lattices
g_{t_{1}}\Lambda_{B}
and
g_{t_{2}}\Lambda_{B}

as
t_{1},t_{2}
and
|t_{1}-t_{2}|
all tend to infinity. More generally, the orbit of
\Lambda_{B}
under larger sub-flows of
a(A^{+})
encode

multiplicative
Diophantine approximation properties of the matrix
B
(see e.g.
[
3
]
).

Systematic implementation of the idea that various objects in Number Theory
are asymptotically independent goes back to the monograph
[
15
]

of W. Philipp, who developed a general framework for proving probabilistic limit theorems
for weakly independent processes having arithmetic applications in mind.
In particular, this led to profound limit theorems for continued fractions,
where the asymptotic independence was derived from properties of the Gauss map.
In the higher-dimensional setting, the asymptotic independence property of translated lattices
a(t)\Lambda_{B}

provides a promising approach to generalize these ideas
and hopefully to establish new limit theorems in Diophantine Approximation
and Geometry of Numbers. For instance, we refer to recent works
[
1
,
4
,
6
,
7
,
10
,
11
]
,
where this approach was successfully applied.

Our paper is devoted to proving a general asymptotic independence result for lattices of the form
a(t)\Lambda_{B}
with a random
(m\times n)
-matrix
B
.
To make this more precise, let us introduce some notation. Fix integers
m,n\geq 1
once and for all. Let
X
denote the space of all unimodular lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{m+n}
and let
\mu
denote the unique
\operatorname{SL}_{m+n}(\mathbb{R})
-invariant probability measure on
X
. Define the sub-manifold

Y:=\big\{\Lambda_{B}:\,B\in\hbox{Mat}_{m\times n}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})\big\}\subset X,

which can be identified with the standard
(m+n)
-torus
\mathbb{R}^{m+n}/\mathbb{Z}^{m+n}
, and equip
Y
with the Lebesgue measure
\nu
coming from this torus. Our aim is to show that, given any smooth
functions
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{r}
on
X
, and
t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\in A^{+}
, we have

\int_{Y}\left(\prod_{s=1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu\approx\prod_{s=1}^{r}\int_{Y}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\,d\nu

(1.1)

with an explicit error term which
only
depends on the function

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}):=\min_{i\neq j}\|t_{i}-t_{j}\|,

where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the maximum norm on
\mathbb{R}^{m+n}
. Note that (
1.1
) only involves
correlations with respect to
\nu
, and not
\mu
. We refer to (
1.1
) as (higher order)
decorrelation
, as opposed to (higher order)
equidistribution
when one instead proves (
1.1
) with
\mu
on the right-hand side, but often with a significantly worse error term (see e.g.
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
), which is really only useful when all coordinates in
t
tend to infinity.
More precisely, the error term in this case depend on the minimum of
\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})

and the function

\min(\lfloor t_{1}\rfloor,\ldots,\lfloor t_{r}\rfloor),

where
\lfloor t\rfloor=\min t^{(s)}
for
t=(t^{(1)},\ldots,t^{(r)})\in A^{+}
. To prove (
1.1
) we need to (quantitatively) understand
all
limits of translated measures
a(t)\nu
as
t
tends to infinity in
A^{+}
(allowing for some coordinates in
t
to stay bounded).

The problem of limiting distribution of measures

a(t)_{*}\nu
in
X

can be studied using Ratner‘s measure classification
[
16
]
for measures invariant under unipotent flows.
In particular, the case of measures supported on horospherical orbits was handled by Dani
[
8
,
9
]
and generalized
by Shah
[
18
]
.
More generally, this approach can be potentially used to study
the distribution of translated measures embedded diagonally in
X\times\cdots\times X
, but
this method does not provide quantitative bounds.
Regarding quantitative estimates,
when
a_{t}
is a
one-parameter
partially hyperbolic flow and

\nu
is a smooth measure supported on an unstable leaf of
a_{t}
, the quantitative asymptotic behaviour of the measures
(a_{t})_{*}\nu
as
t\to\infty

was investigated by Kleinbock and Margulis in
[
12
]
.
The main difficulty of our present setting is that

Y
is not an unstable leaf for the family of transformations
a(t)
.
The first quantitative result in this setting was established by Kleinbock and Margulis in
[
14
]
:

Theorem 1.1

(Kleinbock, Margulis)
.

There exist
\ell\geq 1
and
\delta>0
such that for every
f\in C^{\infty}(Y)
,

\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(X),
and
t\in A^{+}
,

\int_{Y}f\cdot(\varphi\circ a(t))\,d\nu=\left(\int_{Y}f\,d\nu\right)\left(\int_{X}\varphi\,d\mu\right)+O\Big(e^{-\delta\lfloor t\rfloor}\,\|f\|_{C^{\ell}}\,\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(\varphi)\Big),

where
\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(\varphi):=\max\left(\|\varphi\|_{C^{0}},\|\varphi\|_{Lip},\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}_{\ell}}\right)
. Here
\|\cdot\|_{Lip}
and
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{\ell}}
refers to the Lipschitz norm and
the
L^{2}
-Sobolev norm of order
\ell
respectively.

Shi
[
19
]
established the following extension to higher-order correlations:

Theorem 1.2

(Shi)
.

There exist
\ell\geq 1
and
\delta^{\prime}>0
such that for every
f\in C^{\infty}(Y)
,

\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{r}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(X),
and
t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\in A^{+}
,

\displaystyle\int_{Y}f\left(\prod_{s=1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu=

\displaystyle\int_{Y}f\,d\nu\prod_{s=1}^{r}\int_{X}\varphi_{s}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\quad+O\left(e^{-\delta^{\prime}\min\big(\lfloor t_{1}\rfloor,\lfloor t_{2}-t_{1}\rfloor,\ldots,\lfloor t_{r}-t_{r-1}\rfloor\big)}\|f\|_{C^{\ell}}\prod_{s=1}^{r}\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(\varphi_{s})\right).

Later, Björklund and Gorodnik
[
5
]
established a similar extension but with a different error term:

Theorem 1.3

(Björklund, Gorodnik)
.

There exist
d_{r}\geq 1
and
\delta_{r}>0
such that for every
f\in C^{\infty}(Y)
,

\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{r}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(X),
and
t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\in A^{+}
,

\displaystyle\int_{Y}f\left(\prod_{s=1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu=

\displaystyle\int_{Y}f\,d\nu\prod_{s=1}^{r}\int_{X}\varphi_{s}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\quad+O\left(e^{-\delta_{r}\min\big(\lfloor t_{1}\rfloor,\ldots,\lfloor t_{r}\rfloor,\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})\big)}\|f\|_{W}\prod_{s=1}^{r}\mathcal{N}_{\ell,d_{r}}(\varphi_{s})\right),

where

\mathcal{N}_{\ell,d}(\varphi):=\max\left(\|\varphi\|_{C^{0}},\|\varphi\|_{Lip},\|\varphi\|_{L^{2^{d}}_{\ell}}\right)
. Here
\|\cdot\|_{W}
refers to the Wiener norm of functions on
Y
, defined by (
2.3
) below.

The main result of this paper is:

Theorem 1.4
.

There exist
\ell\geq 1
and
\eta_{r}>0
such that for every

\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{r}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(X),
and
t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\in A^{+}
,

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\left(\prod_{s=1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu=\prod_{s=1}^{r}\int_{Y}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\,d\nu+O\left(e^{-\eta_{r}\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})}\prod_{s=1}^{r}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{\ell}}\right).

We again stress that the novelty here is that the error term depends only on the distances

\|t_{i}-t_{j}\|
with
i\neq j
, while the previous results are only applicable when

\min\big(\lfloor t_{1}\rfloor,\ldots,\lfloor t_{r}\rfloor\big)\to\infty
.
We also note that the measures
a(t)_{*}\nu
, as
t\rightarrow\infty
in
A^{+}
, can have many
different limits that need to be analysed in order to prove Theorem
1.4
.
We illustrate this by the following example.

Example 1.1
.

Let
m=2
and
n=1
.
According to Theorem
1.1
, for
\varphi\in C_{c}(X)
,

\int_{Y}\varphi\circ a(s,t)\,d\nu\to\int_{X}\varphi\,d\mu\quad\hbox{as $\min(s,t)\to\infty$.}

However, the family of measures
a(s,t)_{*}\nu
also exhibit
other limiting behaviors: one can show, in particular, that

\int_{Y}\varphi\circ a(s,0)\,d\nu\to\int_{X_{1}}\varphi\,d\mu_{1}\quad\hbox{as $s\to\infty$},

and

\int_{Y}\varphi\circ a(0,t)\,d\nu\to\int_{X_{2}}\varphi\,d\mu_{2}\quad\hbox{as $t\to\infty$},

where

X_{1}:=\left(\begin{tabular}[]{ccc}$*$&$*$&$*$\\
0&$*$&0\\
$*$&$*$&$*$\end{tabular}\right)\Gamma\quad\quad\hbox{and}\quad\quad X_{2}:=\left(\begin{tabular}[]{ccc}$*$&$*$&$*$\\
0&$*$&$*$\\
0&$*$&$*$\end{tabular}\right)\Gamma

are the homogeneous subspaces
of
X
equipped with invariant probability measures
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
.
More generally, let us embed

\nu
in
X\times X
diagonally and
consider
the family of measures
\big(a(s_{1},t_{1}),a(s_{2},t_{2}\big))_{*}\nu

with
(s_{1},t_{1}),(s_{2},t_{2})\in A^{+}
. Here one has 16 possible
limiting behaviors. For example, one can show that
for
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in C_{c}(X)
,

\int_{Y}\big(\varphi_{1}\circ a(s,0)\big)\cdot\big(\varphi_{2}\circ a(0,t)\big)\,d\nu\to\left(\int_{X_{1}}\varphi_{1}\,d\mu_{1}\right)\left(\int_{X_{2}}\varphi_{2}\,d\mu_{2}\right)\quad\hbox{as $s,t\to\infty$.}

(1.2)

In order to prove Theorem
1.4
,
one needs to analyze all such possibilities.
Ultimately, a general version of (
1.2
),
which describes various limiting behaviors,
is given by Theorem
6.1
below.

Organization of the paper.

In Section
2
, we introduce basic notation and, in particular, a family of homogeneous subspaces
X_{I}
that will play a crucial
role for the rest of the paper.
In Section
3
, we study mixing and equidistribution properties on those spaces.
While for certain one-parameter subgroups a required de-correlation

3.2
,
this fails for other one-parameter subgroups.
This issue in addressed in Section
4
,
where additional decorrelation estimates are established.

5
, we prove an

Sections
3
–
4
.
In Section
6
, we prove a general multiple equidistribution
result (Theorem
6.1
) generalizing Theorem
1.3
.
Finally, Theorem
1.4
is proved in Sections
7
–
8
with a help of Theorem
6.1
.

2.
Basic notation

Throughout the paper,
X
denotes the space of unimodular lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{m+n}
:

X\simeq G/\Gamma,\quad\hbox{with $G:=\hbox{SL}_{m+n}(\mathbb{R})$ and $\Gamma:=\hbox{SL}_{m+n}(\mathbb{Z})$},

equipped with the unique
G
-invariant probability measure
\mu
.
We consider the orbit

Y:=U\Gamma\subset X

of the group

U:=\left(\begin{tabular}[]{cc}$I_{m}$&$*$\\
$0$&$I_{n}$\end{tabular}\right),

which can be identified with
(m\times n)
-dimensional torus.
We equip
Y
with the normalized Lebesgue measure
\nu
(which is the unique
U
-invariant probability measure on the
U
-orbit
Y
).

Let us now introduce a family of intermediate homogeneous subspaces
X_{I}

of
X
that will play a crucial role in our arguments.
We call a subset
I\subset\{1,\ldots,m+n\}

admissible
if

I\cap\{1,\ldots,m\}\neq\emptyset\quad\hbox{and}\quad I\cap\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}\neq\emptyset.

(2.1)

Let us first suppose for simplicity that

I=I_{0}:=\{m-k_{1}+1,\ldots,m+k_{2}\}

(2.2)

for some
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 1
. In this case, we define

G_{I_{0}}:=\left(\begin{tabular}[]{ccc}$I_{m-k_{1}}$&$*$&$*$\\
$0$&$\hbox{SL}_{k_{1}+k_{2}}(\mathbb{R})$&$*$\\
$0$&$0$&$I_{n-k_{1}}$\end{tabular}\right).

We note that
G_{I_{0}}=S_{I_{0}}\ltimes U_{I_{0}}
, where

S_{I_{0}}:=\left(\begin{tabular}[]{ccc}$I_{m-k_{1}}$&$0$&$0$\\
$0$&$\hbox{SL}_{k_{1}+k_{2}}(\mathbb{R})$&$0$\\
$0$&$0$&$I_{m-k_{1}}$\end{tabular}\right)\quad\hbox{and}\quad U_{I_{0}}=\left(\begin{tabular}[]{ccc}$I_{m-k_{1}}$&$*$&$*$\\
$0$&$I_{k_{1}+k_{2}}$&$*$\\
$0$&$0$&$I_{m-k_{1}}$\end{tabular}\right).

For general admissible
I
, we take a permutation
\sigma_{I}
such that

\displaystyle\sigma_{I}(\{1,\ldots,m\}\backslash I)

\displaystyle=\{1,\ldots,m-k_{1}\},

\displaystyle\sigma_{I}(I\cap\{1,\ldots,m\})

\displaystyle=\{m-k_{1}+1,\ldots,m\},

\displaystyle\sigma_{I}(I\cap\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\})

\displaystyle=\{m+1,\ldots,m+k_{2}\},

\displaystyle\sigma_{I}(\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}\backslash I)

\displaystyle=\{m+k_{2}+1,\ldots,m+n\}.

Let
w_{I}\in G
be the corresponding permutation matrix and define

G_{I}:=w_{I}G_{I_{0}}w_{I}^{-1},\quad S_{I}:=w_{I}S_{I_{0}}w_{I}^{-1},\quad U_{I}:=w_{I}U_{I_{0}}w_{I}^{-1},

and
G_{I}=S_{I}\ltimes U_{I}
. The orbits

X_{I}:=G_{I}\Gamma\subset X

are finite volume homogeneous spaces. We denote by
\mu_{I}
the unique
G_{I}
-invariant probability
measure on
X_{I}
. For instance, when
I=\{1,\ldots,m+n\}
, we have
X_{I}=X
.

We recall that the space

Y=U\Gamma\simeq\mathbb{R}^{mn}/\mathbb{Z}^{mn}

is (canonically) isomorphic to a torus, and under this isomorphism the measure
\nu
corresponds to the Lebesgue probability measure. Let
\Xi_{Y}
denote the set of continuous characters of
Y
(viewed as a torus).
For
f\in L^{1}(Y)
, we write

\widehat{f}(\xi):=\int_{Y}f\cdot\bar{\xi}\,d\nu.

The
Wiener norm
of
f
is defined by

\|f\|_{W}:=\sum_{\xi\in\Xi_{Y}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|.

(2.3)

When
\|f\|_{W}<\infty
, one has a uniformly convergent expansion

f=\sum_{\xi\in\Xi_{Y}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\xi.

(2.4)

We recall that for
f\in C^{\infty}(Y)
,

\|f\|_{C^{0}}\leq\|f\|_{W}\leq\|f\|_{C^{1}},

(2.5)

and for
f_{1},f_{2}\in C^{\infty}(Y)
,

\|f\|_{W}\leq\|f_{1}\|_{W}\cdot\|f_{2}\|_{W}.

In particular, it follows that for
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(X)
,

\big\|\phi|_{Y}\big\|_{W}\leq\|\phi\|_{C^{1}}.

3.
Mixing and equidistribution in intermediate homogeneous spaces

Let
I\subset\{1,\ldots,m+n\}
be an admissible subset. If
s\in G
, we write
\|s\|
the operator norm of
s
with respect to the
\ell^{\infty}
-norm of
\mathbb{R}^{m+n}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

The action of
S_{I}
on
X_{I}
is exponentially mixing,
that is,
there exist
\ell\geq 1
and
\tau>0
such that for every

\varphi,\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(X_{I})
and
s\in S_{I}
,

\int_{X_{I}}(\varphi\circ s)\cdot\psi\,d\mu_{I}=\left(\int_{X_{I}}\varphi\,d\mu_{I}\right)\left(\int_{X_{I}}\psi\,d\mu_{I}\right)+O\Big(\max\big(1,\|s\|\big)^{-\tau}\,\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}_{\ell}}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}_{\ell}}\Big).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
I
is given as in (
2.2
). In particular,
S_{I}\simeq\hbox{SL}_{k_{1}+k_{2}}(\mathbb{R})
.
The exponential mixing property will follow once we show that
the action of
S_{I}
on
X_{I}
has the spectral gap property,
namely, that the unitary representation of
S_{I}
on
L_{0}^{2}(X_{I})
,
the space of
L^{2}
-integrable functions with integral zero,
is isolated from the trivial representation (see, for instance,
[
12
, Sec. 2.4]
).

We observe that the space of
U_{I}
-invariant functions

L^{2}(X_{I})^{U_{I}}
is invariant under
G_{I}
and

L_{0}^{2}(X_{I})^{U_{I}}\simeq L_{0}^{2}\big(\hbox{SL}_{k_{1}+k_{2}}(\mathbb{R})/\hbox{SL}_{k_{1}+k_{2}}(\mathbb{Z})\big)

as
S_{I}
-spaces. The spectral gap property on the latter space is well-known.
Therefore, it is sufficient to verify the spectral gap property on the orthogonal complement
\mathcal{H}_{I}
of
L_{0}^{2}(X_{I})^{U_{I}}
.
We observe that

U_{I}=U^{(1)}_{I}U^{(2)}_{I}U^{(3)}_{I},

where

\displaystyle U^{(1)}_{I}

\displaystyle:=\left(\begin{tabular}[]{ccc}$I_{m-k_{1}}$&$*$&$0$\\
$0$&$I_{k_{1}+k_{2}}$&$0$\\
$0$&$0$&$I_{n-k_{2}}$\end{tabular}\right),

\displaystyle U^{(2)}_{I}

\displaystyle:=\left(\begin{tabular}[]{ccc}$I_{m-k_{1}}$&$0$&$0$\\
$0$&$I_{k_{1}+k_{2}}$&$*$\\
$0$&$0$&$I_{n-k_{2}}$\end{tabular}\right),

\displaystyle U^{(3)}_{I}

\displaystyle:=\left(\begin{tabular}[]{ccc}$I_{m-k_{1}}$&$0$&$*$\\
$0$&$I_{k_{1}+k_{2}}$&$0$\\
$0$&$0$&$I_{n-k_{2}}$\end{tabular}\right).

We note that
U_{I}
is generated by
U^{(1)}_{I}
and
U^{(2)}_{I}
.

Let
V_{i}
,
i=1\ldots,m-k_{1}
, denote the subgroups
U^{(1)}_{I}

corresponding to the
i
-th row in
\hbox{M}_{m-k_{1},k_{1}+k_{2}}(\mathbb{R})

and
W_{j}
,
j=m+k_{2}+1,m+n
, the subgroup of

U^{(2)}_{I}

corresponding to the
j
-th column in
\hbox{M}_{k_{1}+k_{2},n-k_{2}}(\mathbb{R})
.
We note that

S_{I}V_{i}\simeq\hbox{SL}_{k_{1}+k_{2}}(\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{k_{1}+k_{2}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad S_{I}W_{j}\simeq\hbox{SL}_{k_{1}+k_{2}}(\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{k_{1}+k_{2}}.

When
k_{1}+k_{2}\geq 3
, the group
S_{I}\simeq\hbox{SL}_{k_{1}+k_{2}}(\mathbb{R})

has Kazhdan property (T), so it is sufficient to check that

\mathcal{H}_{I}
contains no non-zero
S_{I}
-invariant vectors.
Suppose that such a vector exists.
We apply Mautner Lemma (see, for instance,
[
2
, Lemma 1.4.8]
) to the representations of

S_{I}V_{i}
and
S_{I}W_{j}
, and deduce that this vector is also invariant under
all
V_{i}
’s and
W_{j}
’s.
Hence, it is invariant under
U^{(1)}_{I}
and
U^{(2)}_{I}
,
so that it is
U_{I}
-invariant and hence is zero
because
\mathcal{H}_{I}
is the orthogonal complement of
L_{0}^{2}(X_{I})^{U_{I}}
.
This verifies that
\mathcal{H}_{I}
contains no non-zero
S_{I}
-invariant vectors and implies the spectral gap property.

Now suppose that
k_{1}+k_{2}=2
, that is,
S_{I}\simeq\hbox{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
In this case, we use that
for any unitary representation of
\hbox{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{2}

without
\mathbb{R}^{2}
-invariant vectors, its restriction to

\hbox{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
has the spectral gap property (see
[
2
, Corollary 1.4.13]
).
In particular, it follows that the action of
S_{I}
on

(\mathcal{H}^{V_{i}})^{\perp}
and on
(\mathcal{H}^{W_{j}})^{\perp}

has the spectral gap property, so that the action of
S_{I}

on

\mathcal{H}^{0}_{I}:=\big(\oplus_{i}(\mathcal{H}_{I}^{V_{i}})^{\perp}\big)\oplus\big(\oplus_{j}(\mathcal{H}_{I}^{W_{j}})^{\perp}\big)

has the spectral gap property. Moreover,

(\mathcal{H}^{0}_{I})^{\perp}=\big(\cap_{i}\mathcal{H}_{I}^{V_{i}}\big)\cap\big(\cap_{j}\mathcal{H}_{I}^{W_{j}}\big)=\mathcal{H}_{I}^{U_{I}^{(1)}}\cap\mathcal{H}_{I}^{U_{I}^{(2)}}=\mathcal{H}_{I}^{U_{I}}=0.

This completes the proof.
∎

Using the above results, and following the proof in
[
5
, Lemma 4.3]
, we get the following corollary:

Corollary 3.2
.

There exist
\ell\geq 1
and
\tau>0
such that
for every
\phi,\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(X_{I})
,
u_{1},u_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
w\in\hbox{\rm Lie}(U\cap S_{I})
,

\displaystyle\int_{X_{I}}(\phi\circ\exp(u_{1}w))\cdot(\psi\circ\exp(u_{2}w))\,d\mu_{I}=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{X_{I}}\phi\,d\mu_{I}\right)\left(\int_{X_{I}}\psi\,d\mu_{I}\right)

\displaystyle+O\left(\max\big(1,|u_{1}-u_{2}|\|w\|\big)^{-\tau}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}_{\ell}}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}_{\ell}}\right).

General version of Theorem
1.1

Given an admissible
I\subset\{1,\ldots,m+n\}
, we set

A^{+}_{I}=\big\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\,:\,t_{j}=0,\enskip\textrm{for all $j\notin I$}\big\}.

For
I\subset\{1,\ldots,m+n\}
and
t\in A^{+}
, we define

\lfloor t\rfloor_{I}:=\min_{i\in I}t_{i}.

The following theorem is a straightforward generalization of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
I\subset\{1,\ldots,m+n\}
be admissible subset.
Then
there exist
\ell\geq 1
and
\delta>0
such that for every
f\in C^{\infty}(Y)
,

\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(X_{I})
, and
t\in A_{I}^{+}
,

\int_{Y}f\cdot(\varphi\circ a(t))\,d\nu=\left(\int_{Y}f\,d\nu\right)\left(\int_{X_{I}}\varphi\,d\mu_{I}\right)+O\Big(e^{-\delta\lfloor t\rfloor_{I}}\|f\|_{C^{\ell}}\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(\varphi)\Big),

where
\mathcal{N}_{\ell}(\varphi):=\max\big(\|\varphi\|_{C^{0}},\|\varphi\|_{Lip},\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}_{\ell}}\big).

Proof.

The proof can be completed as in
[
14
]
(explicit dependence
of the functions in this argument was worked out in
[
4
, Sec. 2]
).
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
I
is given as in (
2.2
).
We also note that the space
X_{I}
is a bundle

\pi_{I}:X_{I}\to S_{I}/(S_{I}\cap\Gamma)

with the base

S_{I}/(S_{I}\cap\Gamma)\simeq\hbox{SL}_{k_{1}+k_{2}}(\mathbb{R})/\hbox{SL}_{k_{1}+k_{2}}(\mathbb{Z})

and compact fibers isomorphic to
U_{I}/s(U_{I}\cap\Gamma)s^{-1}
,
s\in S_{I}
.
We view the base as the space of unimodular lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{k_{1}+k_{2}}

and define an exhaustion of
X_{I}
by compact subsets

K_{\varepsilon}:=\{x\in X_{I}:\,\|v\|\geq\varepsilon\quad\hbox{for all non-zero $v\in\pi_{I}(x)$}\}.

Then one has the non-divergence estimate as
[
14
, Corollary 3.4]
for the translates
a(t)Ux
with
t\in A^{+}_{I}
when
\lfloor t\rfloor_{I}

is sufficiently large. It is also clear that for
x\in K_{\varepsilon}
,
the injectivity radius
\iota(x)
of
x
can be estimated as in

[
14
, Proposition 3.5]
.
Let us also consider a one-parameter sub-semigroup of
A^{+}_{I}

g_{t}:=\hbox{diag}\big(1,\ldots,1,e^{t/k_{1}},\ldots,e^{t/k_{1}},e^{-t/k_{2}},\ldots,e^{-t/k_{2}},1,\ldots,1\big).

We observe that

\hbox{Lie}(G_{I})=L\oplus\hbox{Lie}(U_{I}),

where
L
is the subspace spanned by matrices

\displaystyle E_{ij},\quad 1\leq i\leq m+k_{2},\;m-k_{1}+1\leq j\leq m,

\displaystyle E_{ij},\quad m+1\leq i\leq m+k_{2},\;m+1\leq j\leq m+n.

One easily verifies that the adjoint action
\hbox{Ad}(g_{t})X=g_{t}Xg_{t}^{-1}

is non-expanding on
L
. Moreover, according to Theorem
3.1
,
there exist
\ell\geq 1
and
\delta^{\prime}>0
such that for every

\varphi,\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(X_{I}),
and
t\geq 0
,

\int_{X_{I}}(\varphi\circ g_{t})\cdot\psi\,d\mu_{I}=\left(\int_{X_{I}}\varphi\,d\mu_{I}\right)\left(\int_{X_{I}}\psi\,d\mu_{I}\right)+O\Big(e^{-\delta^{\prime}t}\,\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}_{\ell}}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}_{\ell}}\Big).

Now we can apply the argument as in
[
14
, Theorem 2.3]
to derive quantitative equidistribution of the leaves

g_{t}Ux
:
there exists
c,\gamma>0
such that for every
F\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U_{I})

with
\hbox{dist}(\supp(F),e)\leq\rho
,
x\in X_{I}
with
\iota(x)>2\rho
,

\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(X_{I}),
and
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\int_{U_{I}}F(u)\varphi(g_{t}ux)\,du=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{U_{I}}F(u)\,du\right)\left(\int_{X_{I}}\varphi\,d\mu_{I}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+O\Big(\rho\|F\|_{L^{1}}\|\varphi\|_{Lip}+\rho^{-c}e^{-\gamma t}\|F\|_{C^{\ell}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}_{\ell}}\Big).

With those ingredients the proof can be completed as in

[
14
]
.
∎

4.
Decorrelation estimates

One of the challenges of our present argument is that
the decorrelation estimate in Corollary
3.2
only holds for one-parameter
subgroups in directions
w\in\hbox{Lie}(U\cap S_{I})
.
Nonetheless, we establish that certain ”partial” decorrelation estimates
also hold for other directions.
A basic prototype of this phenomenon is the following elementary result
for circle rotations:

Lemma 4.1
.

For every
\phi,\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
and
L\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\phi(x+s_{1})\overline{\psi(x+s_{2})}\,dx\,ds_{1}ds_{2}=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\phi(x)\,dx\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\overline{\psi(x)}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+O\Big(L^{-2}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}\Big).

Proof.

We have the Fourier expansions:

\phi(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\phi}(n)e^{2\pi inx}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\psi(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\psi}(n)e^{2\pi inx},

so that

\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\phi(x+s_{1})\overline{\psi(x+s_{2})}\,dx=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\phi}(n)\overline{\widehat{\psi}(n)}e^{2\pi i(s_{1}-s_{2})n}.

Since for
n\neq 0
,

\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}e^{2\pi i(s_{1}-s_{2})n}\,ds_{1}ds_{2}\ll n^{-2}L^{-2},

it follows that from the Plancherel Formula that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\phi(x+s_{1})\overline{\psi(x+s_{2})}\,dx\,ds_{1}ds_{2}=

\displaystyle\widehat{\phi}(0)\overline{\widehat{\psi}(0)}+O(L^{-2})\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}},

which proves the lemma.
∎

4.1.
Mean decorrelation for flows - special case

We will need a version of Lemma
4.1

for flows on the space of affine lattices.
Let

\mathcal{L}:=H/\Delta\quad\quad\hbox{with $H:=\hbox{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{d}$
and $\Delta:=\hbox{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})\ltimes\mathbb{Z}^{d}$}

(4.1)

with
d\geq 2
.
We observe that
\mathcal{L}
can be identified with the space of affine unimodular lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\mathcal{L}\simeq\big\{\Lambda+x:\,\hbox{$\Lambda$ -- unimodular lattice in $\mathbb{R}^{d}$},\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Lambda\big\}.

We denote by

\mathcal{L}^{\star}:=\hbox{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})/\hbox{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})

the space of unimodular lattices, and we have the natural projection map

\pi:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}^{\star}:\Lambda+x\mapsto\Lambda,

whose fibers are tori
\pi^{-1}(\Lambda)\simeq\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Lambda
.
For
z\in\mathcal{L}^{\star}
, we denote
\tau_{z}
the probability invariant measure
supported on the torus
\pi^{-1}(z)
. Then the probability invariant measure
on
\mathcal{L}
can be written as

\mu=\int_{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}\tau_{z}\,d\mu^{\star}(z),

where
\mu^{\star}
denotes the invariant probability measure on
\mathcal{L}^{\star}
. For
\phi\in C_{c}(\mathcal{L})
, we define
\phi^{\star}\in C_{c}(\mathcal{L}^{\star})
by

\phi^{\star}(\Lambda):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Lambda}\phi(y+\Lambda)\,d\tau_{\Lambda}(y+\Lambda).

We think of
\phi^{\star}
as a function on
\mathcal{L}
which is constant on the fibers. Finally, given a non-zero
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we get a one-parameter flow
\sigma_{w}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow H
by

\sigma_{w}(u)=(0,uw)\in H,\quad u\in\mathbb{R}.

Theorem 4.2
.

For every
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, and for all
\phi,\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{L})
and
L>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{\mathcal{L}}(\phi\circ\sigma_{w}(u_{1}))\cdot

\displaystyle(\bar{\psi}\circ\sigma_{w}(u_{2}))\,d\mu\,du_{1}du_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{L}}(\phi^{\star}\cdot{\bar{\psi}^{\star}})\,d\mu+O\Big(\max\big(1,L\|w\|\big)^{-2/3}\|\phi\|_{C^{\ell}}\|\psi\|_{C^{\ell}}\Big)

for sufficiently large
\ell
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\|w\|=1
. For a lattice
\Lambda
in
\mathcal{L}^{\star}
, we write

\phi^{\Lambda}(x):=\phi(\Lambda+x)\quad\hbox{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Lambda$}.

Then

(\phi\circ\sigma_{w}(u))^{\Lambda}(x)=\phi^{\Lambda}(x+uw)\quad\hbox{and}\quad(\psi\circ\sigma_{w}(u))^{\Lambda}(x)=\psi^{\Lambda}(x+uw).

The functions
\phi^{\Lambda}
and
\psi^{\Lambda}
have the Fourier expansions:

\phi^{\Lambda}(x)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\perp}}\widehat{\phi}^{\Lambda}(\lambda)e^{2\pi i\left<x,\lambda\right>}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\psi^{\Lambda}(x)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\perp}}\widehat{\psi}^{\Lambda}(\lambda)e^{2\pi i\left<x,\lambda\right>},

where
\Lambda^{\perp}
denotes the dual lattice for
\Lambda
.
Since
\phi
and
\psi
are smooth, the series converge uniformly,
and thus

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{L}}\phi(\sigma(u_{1})z){\bar{\psi}(\sigma(u_{2})z)}\,d\mu(z)=\int_{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Lambda}\phi^{\Lambda}(x+u_{1}w){\bar{\psi}^{\Lambda}(x+u_{2}w)}dx\,d\mu^{\star}(\Lambda),

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Lambda}\phi^{\Lambda}(x+u_{1}w){\bar{\psi}^{\Lambda}(x+u_{2}w)}dx=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\perp}}\widehat{\phi}^{\Lambda}(\lambda)\overline{\widehat{\psi}^{\Lambda}(\lambda)}e^{2\pi i(u_{1}-u_{2})\left<w,\lambda\right>}.

Since

\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}e^{2\pi i(u_{1}-u_{2})\left<w,\lambda\right>}\,du_{1}du_{2}=\omega\big(\pi L\left<w,\lambda\right>\big),

where
\omega(t)=\frac{\sin^{2}(t)}{t^{2}}
for
t\neq 0
and
\omega(0)=1
,
we conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Lambda}\phi^{\Lambda}(x+u_{1}w){\bar{\psi}^{\Lambda}(x+u_{2}w)}dx\,du_{1}du_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\perp}}\widehat{\phi}^{\Lambda}(\lambda)\overline{\widehat{\psi}^{\Lambda}(\lambda)}\omega\big(\pi L\left<w,\lambda\right>\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi^{\star}(\Lambda)\bar{\psi}^{\star}(\Lambda)+\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\perp}\backslash\{0\}}\widehat{\phi}^{\Lambda}(\lambda)\overline{\widehat{\psi}^{\Lambda}(\lambda)}\omega\big(\pi L\left<w,\lambda\right>\big).

Now it remains to estimate the integral of the last sum.
Since for
\phi,\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{L})
,

\big|\widehat{\phi}^{\Lambda}(\lambda)\big|\ll_{\ell}\max\big(1,\|\lambda\|\big)^{-\ell}\,\|\phi\|_{C^{\ell}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\big|\widehat{\psi}^{\Lambda}(\lambda)\big|\ll_{\ell}\max\big(1,\|\lambda\|\big)^{-\ell}\,\|\psi\|_{C^{\ell}}

(4.2)

for every
\ell\geq 1
.
This leads to the estimate

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{\mathcal{L}}\phi(\sigma(u_{1})z){\bar{\psi}(\sigma(u_{2})z)}\,d\mu(z)\,du_{1}du_{2}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}(\phi^{\star}\cdot\bar{\psi^{\star}})\,d\mu^{\star}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+O\Big(\Omega(L)\|\phi\|_{C^{\ell}}\|\psi\|_{C^{\ell}}\Big),

where

\Omega(L):=\int_{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\perp}\backslash\{0\}}\max\big(1,\|\lambda\|\big)^{-2\ell}\omega\big(\pi L\left<w,\lambda\right>\big)\right)\,d\mu^{\star}(\Lambda).

Since
\phi^{\star}\cdot\bar{\psi^{\star}}
is constant on the fibers,

\int_{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}(\phi^{\star}\cdot\bar{\psi^{\star}})\,d\mu^{\star}=\int_{\mathcal{L}}(\phi^{\star}\cdot\bar{\psi^{\star}})\,d\mu.

By Siegel’s Formula
[
17
]
,

\Omega(L)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\max\big(1,\|x\|\big)^{-2\ell}\omega\big(\pi L\left<w,x\right>\big)\,dx.

Applying an orthogonal change of variables,
we may assume that
w=e_{1}
. For
C\geq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\max\big(1,\|x\|\big)^{-2\ell}\omega\big(\pi Lx_{1}\big)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|x_{1}|\leq C^{-1}}\max\big(1,\|x\|\big)^{-2\ell}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{C^{2}}{\pi^{2}L^{2}}\int_{|x_{1}|\geq C^{-1}}\max\big(1,\|x\|\big)^{-2\ell}\,dx

\displaystyle\ll_{\ell}\,C^{-1}+L^{-2}C^{2},

provided that
\ell
is sufficiently large. Taking
C=L^{2/3}
, we obtain the
stated estimate.
∎

4.2.
Mean decorrelation for flows - general version

Let us now extend the previous result to more general homogeneous spaces.
Let us fix
d\geq 2
and
d^{\prime}\geq 1
and set

H:=\left(\begin{tabular}[]{ccc}1&$\hbox{Mat}_{1,d}(\mathbb{R})$&$\hbox{Mat}_{1,d^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})$\\
0&$\hbox{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})$&$\hbox{Mat}_{d,d^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})$\\
0&0&$I_{d^{\prime}}$\end{tabular}\right)\quad\hbox{and}\quad\Delta:=H\cap\hbox{SL}_{d+d^{\prime}+1}(\mathbb{Z}).

(4.3)

We consider the homogeneous space

Z:=H/\Delta.

(4.4)

We note that the space
\mathcal{L}
of affine unimodular lattices, which we introduced above, can be considered as
a degenerate case of this definition with
d^{\prime}=0
.

As above we have the natural projection map

\pi:Z\to Z^{\star},

where
Z^{\star}
denotes
the homogeneous space
of
(d+d^{\prime})
-matrices obtained
by removing the first row and the first column.
The space
Z^{\star}
can be viewed as the spaces of tuples

(\Lambda,v_{1},\ldots,v_{d^{\prime}}),

where
\Lambda
is a unimodular lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{d^{\prime}})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Lambda)^{d^{\prime}}
.
The fibers of this map are tori given by

\pi^{-1}(\Lambda,v)\simeq\mathbb{R}^{d+d^{\prime}}/\Lambda_{v},

where

\Lambda_{v}:=\big\{(\delta,m_{1}-\delta\cdot v_{1},\ldots,m_{d^{\prime}}-\delta\cdot v_{d^{\prime}}):\,\delta\in\Lambda^{\perp},m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d^{\prime}}\big\}.

We note that the dual lattice is given by

\Lambda_{v}^{\perp}=\big\{(\delta+m_{1}v_{1}+\cdots+m_{d^{\prime}}v_{d^{\prime}},m):\,\delta\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d^{\prime}}\big\}.

For
z\in Z^{\star}
, we denote
\tau_{z}
the probability invariant measure
supported on the torus
\pi^{-1}(z)
. Then the probability invariant measure
on
Z
is

\mu=\int_{Z}\tau_{z}\,d\mu^{\star}(z),

where
\mu^{\star}
denotes the invariant probability measure on
Z^{\star}
. For
\phi\in C_{c}(Z)
, we define
\phi^{\star}\in C_{c}(Z^{\star})
by

\phi^{\star}(z):=\int_{Z}\phi\,d\tau_{z}.

We think of
\phi^{\star}
as a function on
Z
which is constant on the fibers. Finally, given a non-zero
w\in\textrm{Mat}_{1,d}(\mathbb{R})
, we get a one-parameter flow

\sigma_{w}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow H
by

\sigma_{w}(u)=\left(\begin{tabular}[]{ccc}1&$uw$&0\\
0&$I_{d}$&$0$\\
0&0&$I_{d^{\prime}}$\end{tabular}\right),\quad\textrm{for $u\in\mathbb{R}$}.

Theorem 4.3
.

For every
w\in\textrm{Mat}_{1,d}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}
and for all
\phi,\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Z)
and
L>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{Z}(\phi\circ\sigma_{w}(u_{1}))\cdot

\displaystyle(\bar{\psi}\circ\sigma_{w}(u_{2}))\,d\mu\,du_{1}du_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Z}(\phi^{\star}\cdot{\bar{\psi}^{\star}})\,d\mu+O\Big(\max\big(1,L\|w\|\big)^{-2/3}\|\phi\|_{C^{\ell}}\|\psi\|_{C^{\ell}}\Big)

for sufficiently large
\ell
.

Proof.

For a tuple
(\Lambda,v)
in
Z^{\star}
, we write

\phi^{(\Lambda,v)}(x,y)=\phi|_{\pi^{-1}(\Lambda,v)}(x,y)\quad\hbox{for $(x,y)\in(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}})/\Lambda_{v}$}.

Then

(\phi\circ\sigma_{w}(u))^{(\Lambda,v)}(x,y)=\phi^{(\Lambda,v)}(x+uw,y)\quad\hbox{and}\quad(\psi\circ\sigma_{w}(u))^{(\Lambda,v)}(x,y)=\psi^{(\Lambda,v)}(x+uw,y).

The function
\phi^{(\Lambda,v)}
and
\psi^{(\Lambda,v)}
have the Fourier expansions:

\phi^{(\Lambda,v)}(x,y)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{v}^{\perp}}\widehat{\phi}^{(\Lambda,v)}(\lambda)e^{2\pi i\left<(x,y),\lambda\right>}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\psi^{(\Lambda,v)}(x,y)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{v}^{\perp}}\widehat{\psi}^{(\Lambda,v)}(\lambda)e^{2\pi i\left<(x,y),\lambda\right>}.

Since
\phi
and
\psi
are smooth, the series converge uniformly.
So we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{Z}\phi(\sigma_{w}(u_{1})z){\bar{\psi}(\sigma_{w}(u_{2})z)}\,d\mu(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Z^{\star}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}})/\Lambda_{v}}\phi^{(\Lambda,v)}(x+u_{1}w,y){\bar{\psi}^{(\Lambda,v)}(x+u_{2}w,y)}dxdy\,d\mu^{\star}(\Lambda,v),

and

\displaystyle\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}})/\Lambda_{v}}\phi^{(\Lambda,v)}(x+u_{1}w){\bar{\psi}^{(\Lambda,v)}(x+u_{2}w)}\,

\displaystyle dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{v}^{\perp}}\widehat{\phi}^{(\Lambda,v)}(\lambda)\overline{\widehat{\psi}^{(\Lambda,v)}(\lambda)}e^{2\pi i(u_{1}-u_{2})\left<(w,0),\lambda\right>}.

Since

\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}e^{2\pi i(u_{1}-u_{2})\left<(w,0),\lambda\right>}\,du_{1}du_{2}=\omega\big(\pi L\left<(w,0),\lambda\right>\big),

we conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}})/\Lambda_{v}}\phi^{(\Lambda,v)}(x+u_{1}w,y){\bar{\psi}^{(\Lambda,v)}(x+u_{2}w,y)}dxdy\,du_{1}du_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{v}^{\perp}}\widehat{\phi}^{(\Lambda,v)}(\lambda)\overline{\widehat{\psi}^{(\Lambda,v)}(\lambda)}\omega\big(\pi L\left<(w,0),\lambda\right>\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi^{\star}(\Lambda,v)\bar{\psi}^{\star}(\Lambda,v)+\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{v}^{\perp}\backslash\{0\}}\widehat{\phi}^{(\Lambda,v)}(\lambda)\overline{\widehat{\psi}^{(\Lambda,v)}(\lambda)}\omega\big(\pi L\left<(w,0),\lambda\right>\big).

Now it remains to estimate the integral of the last sum.
Since the
\phi,\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Z)
,

\big|\widehat{\phi}^{(\Lambda,v)}(\lambda)\big|\ll_{\ell}\max\big(1,\|\lambda\|\big)^{-\ell}\,\|\phi\|_{C^{\ell}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\big|\widehat{\psi}^{(\Lambda,v)}(\lambda)\big|\ll_{\ell}\max\big(1,\|\lambda\|\big)^{-\ell}\,\|\psi\|_{C^{\ell}}

(4.5)

for every
\ell\geq 1
, this leads to the estimate

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{Z}\phi(\sigma_{w}(u_{1})z){\bar{\psi}(\sigma_{w}(u_{2})z)}\,

\displaystyle d\mu(z)\,du_{1}du_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Z^{\star}}(\phi^{\star}\cdot\bar{\psi^{\star}})\,d\mu^{\star}+O\Big(\Omega(L)\|\phi\|_{C^{\ell}}\|\psi\|_{C^{\ell}}\Big),

where

\displaystyle\Omega(L):=

\displaystyle\int_{Z^{\star}}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{v}^{\perp}\backslash\{0\}}\max\big(1,\|\lambda\|\big)^{-2\ell}\omega\big(\pi L\left<(w,0),\lambda\right>\big)\right)\,d\mu^{\star}(\Lambda,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Z^{\star}}\left(\sum_{(\delta,m)\in(\Lambda\times\mathbb{Z}^{d^{\prime}})\backslash\{0\}}\max\Big(1,\Big\|(\delta+{\sum}_{i}m_{i}v_{i},m)\Big\|\Big)^{-2\ell}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\left.\omega\big(\pi L\big<w,\delta+{\sum}_{i}m_{i}v_{i}\big>\big)\right)\,d\mu^{\star}(\Lambda,v).

Since the part of the sum with
m_{1}=\cdots=m_{d^{\prime}}=0
has been handled in the proof of the previous theorem,
it remains to estimate

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}(L)=

\displaystyle\int_{Z^{\star}}\left(\sum_{\delta\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d^{\prime}}\backslash\{0\}}\max\Big(1,\Big\|(\delta+{\sum}_{i}m_{i}v_{i},m)\Big\|\Big)^{-2\ell}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\left.\omega\big(\pi L\big<w,\delta+{\sum}_{i}m_{i}v_{i}\big>\big)\right)\,d\mu^{\star}(\Lambda,v).

Let us suppose, for example, that
m_{1}\neq 0
.
Then we consider the change of variables

(\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Lambda)^{d^{\prime}}\to(\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Lambda)^{d^{\prime}}:\tilde{v}_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{d^{\prime}}m_{i}v_{i},\tilde{v}_{2}=v_{2},\ldots,\tilde{v}_{d^{\prime}}=v_{d^{\prime}}.

We observe that this transformation is
measure-preserving because it is
a composition
of the maps

v_{1}\mapsto m_{1}v_{1},v_{2}\mapsto v_{2},\ldots,v_{d^{\prime}}\mapsto v_{d^{\prime}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad v_{1}\mapsto v_{1}+m_{i}v_{i},v_{2}\mapsto v_{2},\ldots,v_{d^{\prime}}\mapsto v_{d^{\prime}}.

Using this the integration reduces to the space
\mathcal{L}
of affine lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Namely,
we obtain

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}(L)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{L}^{\star}}\left(\sum_{\delta\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}^{d^{\prime}}\backslash\{0\}}\max\big(1,\|(\delta+\tilde{v}_{1},m)\|\big)^{-2\ell}\omega\big(\pi L\left<w,\delta+\tilde{v}_{1}\right>\big)\right)\,d\mu_{\mathcal{L}}(\Lambda,\tilde{v}_{1}),

where
\mu_{\mathcal{L}}
denotes the invariant measure on
\mathcal{L}
.
Applying Siegel’s Formula for the space of affine lattices, we obtain

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}(L)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{m\neq 0}\max\big(1,\|(x,m)\|\big)^{-2\ell}\omega\big(\pi L\left<w,x\right>\big)\,dx

\displaystyle\ll\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{m\neq 0}\big(\|x\|+|m|\big)^{-2\ell}\omega\big(\pi L\left<w,x\right>\big)\,dx

\displaystyle\ll\left(\sum_{m\neq 0}m^{-\ell}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\max\big(1,\|x\|\big)^{-\ell}\omega\big(\pi L\left<w,x\right>\big)\,dx,

where we used the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.
Now the estimate can be completed as in the previous proof.
∎

More generally, we derive a version of the previous result for products of spaces as above.
Let
d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
and
d^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,d^{\prime}_{k}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.
When
d_{s}\geq 2
and
d_{s}^{\prime}=0
, we define
H_{s}
,
\Delta_{s}
, and
Z_{s}
as in (
4.3
)–(
4.4
).
When
d_{s}\geq 2
and
d_{s}^{\prime}=0
, we define
H_{s}
,
\Delta_{s}
, and
Z_{s}
as in (
4.1
).
When
d_{s}=1
, we set

H_{s}:=\mathbb{R},\quad\Delta_{s}:=\mathbb{Z},\quad\hbox{and}\quad Z_{s}:=H_{s}/\Gamma_{s}.

We recall that when
d_{s}\geq 2
the spaces
Z_{s}
are equipped with the invariant probability
measures
\mu_{s}
, and they fiber over the spaces
Z_{s}^{\star}

which is equipped with the invariant probability measure
\mu_{s}^{\star}
.
When
d_{s}\geq 2
and
d^{\prime}_{s}=0
,
Z_{s}^{\star}
is the space of unimodular lattices
\Lambda_{s}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d_{s}}
.
When
d_{s}\geq 2
and
d^{\prime}_{s}\geq 1
,
Z_{s}^{\star}
is the spaces of tuples

(\Lambda_{s},v_{s})
,
where
\Lambda_{s}
is a unimodular lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{d_{s}}
and
v_{s}=(v_{s,1},\ldots,v_{s,d_{s}^{\prime}})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d_{s}}/\Lambda)^{d_{s}^{\prime}}
.
To have consistent notation, we write the elements of
Z_{s}^{\star}
as
(\Lambda_{s},v_{s})
in both cases
thinking that
v_{s}=0
when
d_{s}^{\prime}=0
.
When
d_{s}=1
, we view
Z_{s}^{\star}

as a one-point space equipped with the Dirac measure
\mu_{s}^{\star}
.

Let

w_{s}\in\hbox{Mat}_{1,d_{s}}(\mathbb{R})\subset H_{s}
,
s=1,\ldots,k
,
with
\|w_{1}\|\geq\cdots\geq\|w_{k}\|

and

\sigma_{s}(u)=uw_{s}
be
the corresponding one-parameter subgroups.

Theorem 4.4
.

There exists
\delta>0
such that
for every
\phi_{s},\psi_{s}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Z_{s})
,
s=1,\ldots,k
, and
L>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}

\displaystyle\prod_{s=1}^{k}\int_{Z_{s}}(\phi_{s}\circ\sigma_{s}(u_{1}))\cdot(\bar{\psi}_{s}\circ\sigma_{s}(u_{2}))\,d\mu_{s}\,du_{1}du_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{s=1}^{k}\int_{Z_{s}}(\phi_{s}^{\star}\cdot{\bar{\psi}_{s}^{\star}})\,d\mu_{s}+O\Big(\max\big(1,L\|w_{k}\|\big)^{-\frac{2}{k(k+2)}}{\prod}_{s=1}^{k}\|\phi_{s}\|_{C^{\ell}}\|\psi_{s}\|_{C^{\ell}}\Big)

for sufficiently large
\ell
.

Proof.

As in the previous proof, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d_{s}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{s}^{\prime}})/\Lambda_{s,v_{s}}}\phi^{(\Lambda_{s},v_{s})}(x_{s}+u_{1}w_{s},y_{s})

\displaystyle{\bar{\psi}^{(\Lambda_{s},v_{s})}(x_{s}+u_{2}w_{s},y_{s})}\,dx_{s}dy_{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{s}\in\Lambda_{s,v_{s}}^{\perp}}\widehat{\phi}^{(\Lambda_{s},v_{s})}(\lambda_{s})\overline{\widehat{\psi}^{(\Lambda_{s},v_{s})}(\lambda_{s})}e^{2\pi i(u_{1}-u_{2})\left<(w_{s},0),\lambda_{s}\right>}.

Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}\prod_{s=1}^{k}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d_{s}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{s}^{\prime}})/\Lambda_{s,v_{s}}}\phi^{(\Lambda_{s},v_{s})}(x_{s}+u_{1}w_{s},y_{s}){\bar{\psi}^{(\Lambda_{s},v_{s})}(x_{s}+u_{2}w_{s},y_{s})}dx_{s}dy_{s}\,du_{1}du_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{s}}\prod_{s=1}^{k}\widehat{\phi}^{(\Lambda_{s},v_{s})}(\lambda_{s})\overline{\widehat{\psi}^{(\Lambda_{s},v_{s})}(\lambda_{s})}\,\omega\Big(\pi L{\sum}_{j=1}^{k}\left<(w_{j},0),\lambda_{j}\right>\Big),

where the sum is taken over
\lambda_{1}\in\Lambda_{1,v_{1}}^{\perp}
, …,
\lambda_{k}\in\Lambda_{k,v_{k}}^{\perp}
.
Since the function
\omega
is uniformly bounded,
in view of estimates (
4.5
), this sum converges uniformly. We note that the term with
\lambda_{1}=\cdots=\lambda_{k}=0
gives

\prod_{s=1}^{k}\int_{Z_{s}^{\star}}\widehat{\phi}^{\Lambda_{s}}(0)\overline{\widehat{\psi}^{\Lambda_{s}}(0)}\,d\mu_{s}^{\star}(\Lambda_{s},v_{s})=\prod_{s=1}^{k}\int_{Z^{\star}_{s}}(\phi_{s}^{\star}\cdot{\bar{\psi}_{s}^{\star}})\,d\mu_{s}^{\star}=\prod_{s=1}^{k}\int_{Z_{s}}(\phi_{s}^{\star}\cdot{\bar{\psi}_{s}^{\star}})\,d\mu_{s},

which is the main term in the theorem. Hence, it remains to estimate
the integrals of remaining summands over

(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})\neq(0,\ldots,0)
.
In fact, we only discuss the sum over
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}\neq 0

because the remaining sums reduces to similar estimates
with fewer number of factors. Now we need to estimate the expression

\displaystyle\int_{Z_{1}^{\star}\times\cdots\times Z_{k}^{\star}}{\sum_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}}}^{\!\!\!\!\!*}\,\prod_{s=1}^{k}\max

\displaystyle\big(1,\|\lambda_{s}\|\big)^{-2\ell}

\displaystyle\times\omega\Big(\pi L{\sum}_{j=1}^{k}\left<(w_{j},0),\lambda_{j}\right>\Big)\,d\mu_{1}^{\star}(\Lambda_{1},v_{1})\cdots d\mu_{k}^{\star}(\Lambda_{k},v_{k}),

where the sum is taken
\lambda_{1}\in\Lambda_{1,v_{1}}^{\perp}\backslash\{0\},\ldots,\lambda_{k}\in\Lambda_{k,v_{k}}^{\perp}\backslash\{0\}
.
More explicitly, when
d_{s}^{\prime}\geq 1
, the parameters
\lambda_{s}
run over

\lambda_{s}=(\delta_{s}+m_{s,1}v_{s,1}+\cdots+m_{s,d^{\prime}}v_{s,d^{\prime}},m_{s})\quad\hbox{with $\delta_{s}\in\Lambda_{s}$ and $m_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d_{s}^{\prime}}$.}

One can handle the sum over
\lambda_{s}
as in the previous proof using a change of variables
in
(v_{s,1},\ldots,v_{s,d_{s}^{\prime}})
and the Siegel formula for the affine lattices.
When
d_{s}\geq 2
and
d_{s}^{\prime}=0
, the parameter
\lambda_{s}
runs over
\Lambda_{s}
,
and one can apply the Siegel formula for the space of lattices.
Ultimately, this gives the integral

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}}}\prod_{s=1}^{k}\max\big(1,\|x_{s}\|\big)^{-\ell}\,\omega\Big(\pi L{\sum}_{j=1}^{k}\left<w_{j},x_{j}\right>\Big)\,d\rho_{1}(x_{1})\cdots d\rho_{k}(x_{k}),

(4.6)

where
\rho_{s}
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d_{s}}
when
d_{s}\geq 2

and
\rho_{s}=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}}\delta_{z}
when
d_{s}=1
.
We write
x_{j}=(x_{j}^{(1)},\ldots,x_{j}^{(d_{j})})
.
Applying orthogonal changes of variables in (
4.6
),
we may assume that

\left<w_{j},x_{j}\right>=\|w_{j}\|\cdot|x_{j}^{(1)}|\quad\hbox{for $j=1,\ldots,k$.}

When
d_{s}=1
for some
s
, we observe that for
\max_{j}|x_{j}|\geq 1
,

\omega\Big(\pi L{\sum}_{j=1}^{k}\|w_{j}\|\cdot|x_{j}|\Big)\ll(L\|w_{k}\|)^{-2}.

This also implies the bound
O_{\ell}\big((L\|w_{k}\|)^{-2}\big)
for the integral (
4.6
).

Now let us assume that
d_{s}\geq 2
for all
s=1,\ldots k
.
Let
C\geq 1
.
When we integrate over the domain defined by
|x_{j}^{(1)}|\leq C^{-1}
for all
j=1,\ldots,k
, we obtain the bound:

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\prod_{s=1}^{k}\int_{|x_{s}^{(1)}|\leq C^{-1}}\max\big(1,\|x_{s}\|\big)^{-\ell}\,d\rho_{s}(x_{s})\ll_{\ell}C^{-k}

when
\ell
is sufficiently large.
Also to estimate the integrals over the domains definded by

|x_{j}^{(1)}|\geq C^{-1}
for some
j
, we use the bound:

\omega\Big(\pi L{\sum}_{j=1}^{k}\|w_{j}\|\cdot|x_{j}|\Big)\leq\frac{C^{2}}{\pi^{2}L^{2}\|w_{j}\|^{2}},

which leads to the bound

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{s}}}\max\big(1,\|x_{s}\|\big)^{-\ell}\omega\Big(\pi L{\sum}_{j=1}^{k}\|w_{j}\|\cdot|x_{j}|\Big)\,d\rho_{s}(x_{s})\ll_{\ell}C^{2}(L\|w_{k}\|)^{-2}

provided that
\ell
is sufficiently large.
Ultimately, we conclude that the integral (
4.6
) is bounded up to a constant by

C^{-k}+C^{2}(L\|w_{k}\|)^{-2}.

with a parameter
C\geq 1
. Optimizing in
C
, we get the required bound.
∎

A simple modification of the previous argument also gives:

Corollary 4.5
.

There exists
\delta>0
such that
for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
,
\phi_{s},\psi_{s}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Z_{s})
,
s=1,\ldots,k
, and
L>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}e^{i(u_{1}-u_{2})\xi}

\displaystyle\Big(\prod_{s=1}^{k}\mu_{s}\big((\phi_{s}\circ\sigma_{s}(u_{1}))\cdot(\bar{\psi}_{s}\circ\sigma_{s}(u_{2}))\big)-\prod_{s=1}^{k}\mu_{s}(\phi_{s}^{\star}\cdot{\bar{\psi}_{s}^{\star}})\Big)\,du_{1}du_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\Big(\max\big(1,L\|w_{k}\|\big)^{-\frac{2}{k(k+2)}}\,{\prod}_{s=1}^{k}\|\phi_{s}\|_{C^{\ell}}\|\psi_{s}\|_{C^{\ell}}\Big)

uniformly on
\xi
, for sufficiently large
\ell
.

Proof.

It turns out that the previous proof requires only minor modifications.
As before, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}e^{i(u_{1}-u_{2})\xi}\Big(\prod_{s=1}^{k}

\displaystyle\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d_{s}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{s}^{\prime}})/\Lambda_{s,v_{s}}^{\perp}}\phi^{(\Lambda_{s},v_{s})}(x_{s}+u_{1}w_{s},y_{s})

\displaystyle\times{\bar{\psi}^{(\Lambda_{s},v_{s})}(x_{s}+u_{2}w_{s},y_{s})}\,dx_{s}dy_{s}-\prod_{s=1}^{k}\widehat{\phi}^{\Lambda_{s}}(0)\overline{\widehat{\psi}^{\Lambda_{s}}(0)}\Big)du_{1}du_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\sum_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{s}}}^{\!\!\!\!\!\prime}\,\prod_{s=1}^{k}\widehat{\phi}^{\Lambda_{s}}(\lambda_{s})\overline{\widehat{\psi}^{\Lambda_{s}}(\lambda_{s})}\,\omega\Big(\pi L{\sum}_{j=1}^{k}\left<(w_{j},0),\lambda_{j}\right>+\xi\Big),

where the sum is taken over
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{s})\neq(0,\ldots,0)
.
Using the same argument as above, we obtain the integrals

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{k_{1}}}}\prod_{s=1}^{k_{1}}\max\big(1,\|x_{s}\|\big)^{-\ell}\,\omega\Big(\pi L{\sum}_{j=1}^{k_{1}}\left<w_{j},x_{j}\right>+\xi\Big)\,d\rho_{1}(x_{1})\cdots d\rho_{k_{1}}(x_{k_{1}}),

with
k_{1}\leq k
.
Applying orthogonal changes of variables, we may assume that

\left<w_{j},x_{j}\right>=\hbox{sign}(\xi)\cdot\|w_{j}\|\cdot|x_{j}^{(1)}|\quad\hbox{for $j=1,\ldots,k_{1}$,}

so that we obtain the integral

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{k_{1}}}}\prod_{s=1}^{k_{1}}\max\big(1,\|x_{s}\|\big)^{-\ell}\,\omega\Big(\pi L{\sum}_{j=1}^{k_{1}}\|w_{j}\|\cdot|x_{j}|+|\xi|\Big)\,d\rho_{1}(x_{1})\cdots d\rho_{k_{1}}(x_{k_{1}}).

Then the same argument works.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

Let us consider a slight generalization of the above results.
For
d\geq 2
and
d_{0},d^{\prime}\geq 1
, we set

H:=\left(\begin{tabular}[]{ccc}$I_{d_{0}}$&$\hbox{Mat}_{d_{0},d}(\mathbb{R})$&$\hbox{Mat}_{d_{0},d^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})$\\
0&$\hbox{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{R})$&$\hbox{Mat}_{d,d^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})$\\
0&0&$I_{d^{\prime}}$\end{tabular}\right)\quad\hbox{and}\quad\Delta:=H\cap\hbox{SL}_{d+d^{\prime}+d_{0}}(\mathbb{Z})

(4.7)

and consider the homogeneous space

Z:=H/\Delta.

(4.8)

Let
Z^{\star}
denote the homogeneous space
obtained from
Z
by removing the first
d_{0}
rows and the first
d_{0}
columns.
In fact, the space
Z^{\star}
is exactly as in Theorem
4.3
. Namely, it can be parameterized
by tuples
(\Lambda,v)
where
\Lambda
is a unimodular lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
v\in(\mathbb{R}^{d}/\Lambda)^{d^{\prime}}
.
We have the projection map

\pi:Z\to Z^{\star}

with the fibers

\pi^{-1}(\Lambda,v)\simeq(\mathbb{R}^{d+d^{\prime}}/\Lambda_{v})^{d_{0}}.

(4.9)

Let us take a non-zero element
w\in\hbox{Mat}_{d_{0},d}(\mathbb{R})\subset H
such that
all non-zero components of
w
are contained in the
i_{0}
-th row.
We denote by
U_{w}
the subgroup of
H
whose Lie algebra is generated by
E_{i_{0},j}
,
j=d_{0}+1,\ldots,d+d^{\prime}+d_{0}
.
We note that
U_{w}
is normal in
H
.
The homogeneous subspace
Z_{w}:=U_{w}\Delta
of
Z
is a
(d+d^{\prime})
-dimensional torus and
we denote by
\tau_{w}
the probability invariant measure on
Z_{w}
.
For
\phi\in C_{c}(Z)
, we define
\phi^{\star}\in C_{c}(Z)
by

\phi^{\star}(h\Delta):=\int_{Z_{w}}\phi(hz)\,d\tau_{w}(z),\quad h\in H.

In this set-up, we can apply the above arguments, in particular, decompose
the restriction of the functions to the fibers (
4.9
) into Fourier series on
(\mathbb{R}^{d+d^{\prime}}/\Lambda_{v})^{d_{0}}
.
Because of our choice of
w
, only the
i_{0}
-component is affected by the flow,
so that the previous computations require only minor modifications,
and Theorems
4.3
–
4.4
and Corollary
4.5
can be proved in this setting as above.

We also note that one can take a non-zero element
w\in\hbox{Mat}_{d,d^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})\subset H
such that
all non-zero components of
w
are contained in the
j_{0}
-th row. Clearly, our argument applies in this case as well.

5.
A proposition about weights

Let
I\subset\{1,\ldots,m+n\}
be an admissible subset, i.e. the sets

I^{\prime}:=I\cap\{1,\ldots,m\}\quad\hbox{and}\quad I^{\prime\prime}:=I\cap\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}

are both not-empty. Note that
I=I^{\prime}\sqcup I^{\prime\prime}
.
We define the subsemigroup of
A^{+}
as

A^{+}_{I}:=\{t\in A^{+}:\,t_{i}=0\hbox{ for $i\notin I$}\}.

We consider the adjoint action of
A^{+}
on
\hbox{Lie}(U)
.
Then for
t\in A^{+}
and the elementary matrices
E_{ij}
generating
\hbox{Lie}(U)
,

[t,E_{ij}]=\alpha_{ij}(t)E_{ij},

where the weights
\alpha_{ij}
are given by
\alpha_{ij}(t)=t_{i}+t_{j}
.

We say that the weight
\alpha_{ij}
(or the corresponding weight space
\mathbb{R}E_{ij}
, or a non-zero vector there)
is
mixing for
I
if
i\in I^{\prime}
and
j\in I^{\prime\prime}
.
We note that this condition is equivalent to
\mathbb{R}E_{ij}\subset\hbox{Lie}(S_{I})
, and thus Theorem
3.1
should explain the terminology.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
I_{1}\neq I_{2}
be admissible subsets. Then there exists
\theta>0
such that
for every
s\in A^{+}_{I_{1}}
and
t\in A^{+}_{I_{2}}
, either
there exists a weight
\alpha
mixing for
I_{1}
such that

\alpha(s-t)\geq\theta\,\min\big(\lfloor s\rfloor_{I_{1}},\lfloor t\rfloor_{I_{2}}\big),

(5.1)

or
there exists a weight
\alpha
mixing for
I_{2}
such that

\alpha(t-s)\geq\theta\,\min\big(\lfloor s\rfloor_{I_{1}},\lfloor t\rfloor_{I_{2}}\big).

(5.2)

Proof.

For simplicity we write

\Pi(s,t):=\min\big(\lfloor s\rfloor_{I_{1}},\lfloor t\rfloor_{I_{2}}\big)

and use the following notations:

\displaystyle D_{1}\prec_{\varepsilon}D_{2}\;

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\;D_{1}\leq D_{2}+\varepsilon\,\Pi(s,t),

\displaystyle D_{1}\succ_{\varepsilon}D_{2}\;

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\;D_{1}\geq D_{2}-\varepsilon\,\Pi(s,t),

\displaystyle D_{1}\approx_{\varepsilon}D_{2}\;

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\;|D_{1}-D_{2}|\leq\varepsilon\,\Pi(s,t).

The proof is subdivided into several cases:

Case 1:

I_{1}^{\prime}\backslash I_{2}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
and
I_{1}^{\prime\prime}\backslash I_{2}^{\prime\prime}\neq\emptyset
.

We take
i\in I_{1}^{\prime}\backslash I_{2}^{\prime}
and
j\in I_{1}^{\prime\prime}\backslash I_{2}^{\prime\prime}
. Then

\alpha_{ij}(s-t)=s_{i}+s_{j},

which immediately implies the proposition.

Case 2:

I_{2}^{\prime}\backslash I_{1}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
and
I_{2}^{\prime\prime}\backslash I_{1}^{\prime\prime}\neq\emptyset
.

This can be handled as in
Case 1.

The complement of
Cases 1–2
can be subdivided into the following sub-cases:

•

Case 3:

I_{1}^{\prime}\backslash I_{2}^{\prime}=\emptyset
and
I_{2}^{\prime\prime}\backslash I_{1}^{\prime\prime}=\emptyset

\Longleftrightarrow

I_{1}^{\prime}\subseteq I_{2}^{\prime}
and
I_{2}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq I_{1}^{\prime\prime}
,

•

Case 4:

I_{1}^{\prime}\backslash I_{2}^{\prime}=\emptyset
and
I_{2}^{\prime}\backslash I_{1}^{\prime}=\emptyset

\Longleftrightarrow

I_{1}^{\prime}=I_{2}^{\prime}
,

•

Case 5:

I_{2}^{\prime}\backslash I_{1}^{\prime}=\emptyset
and
I_{1}^{\prime\prime}\backslash I_{2}^{\prime\prime}=\emptyset

\Longleftrightarrow

I_{2}^{\prime}\subseteq I_{1}^{\prime}
and
I_{1}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq I_{2}^{\prime\prime}
,

•

Case 6:

I_{1}^{\prime\prime}\backslash I_{2}^{\prime\prime}=\emptyset
and
I_{2}^{\prime\prime}\backslash I_{1}^{\prime\prime}=\emptyset

\Longleftrightarrow

I_{1}^{\prime\prime}=I_{2}^{\prime\prime}
.

We only discuss
Cases 3–4
since the remaining cases can be treated similarly.

Let us fix
\varepsilon>0
, to be specified later, chosen independently of
s
and
t
, but will depend on
m
and
n
.

Case 3:

I_{1}^{\prime}\subseteq I_{2}^{\prime}
and
I_{2}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq I_{1}^{\prime\prime}
.

We may additionally assume that
I_{1}^{\prime}\subsetneq I_{2}^{\prime}
and
I_{2}^{\prime\prime}\subsetneq I_{1}^{\prime\prime}
because
otherwise we land either in
Case 4
or
Case 6
.

Take
i\in I_{1}^{\prime}
and
k\in I_{1}^{\prime\prime}\backslash I_{2}^{\prime\prime}
.
If

(s_{i}+s_{k})-t_{i}\geq\varepsilon\,\Pi(s,t)
, we can take the weight
\alpha_{ik}
to obtain (
5.1
), so that we may assume that

s_{i}+s_{k}\prec_{\varepsilon}t_{i}\quad\hbox{for all $i\in I_{1}^{\prime}$ and $k\in I_{1}^{\prime\prime}\backslash I_{2}^{\prime\prime}$}.

(5.3)

Take
l\in I_{2}^{\prime}\backslash I_{1}^{\prime}
and
j\in I_{2}^{\prime\prime}
.
If

(t_{l}+t_{j})-s_{j}\geq\varepsilon\,\Pi(s,t)
, we can take the weight
\alpha_{lj}
to obtain (
5.2
), so that we may assume that

t_{l}+t_{j}\prec_{\varepsilon}s_{j}\quad\hbox{for all $l\in I_{2}^{\prime}\backslash I_{1}^{\prime}$ and $j\in I_{2}^{\prime\prime}$}.

(5.4)

When
t_{j}\succ_{\varepsilon}s_{j}
for some
j\in I_{2}^{\prime\prime}
, then
it follows from (
5.3
) that for
i\in I_{1}^{\prime}
and
k\in I_{1}^{\prime\prime}\backslash I_{2}^{\prime\prime}
,

(t_{i}+t_{j})-(s_{i}+s_{j})\succ_{\varepsilon}s_{k}+(t_{j}-s_{j})\succ_{\varepsilon}s_{k}\geq\Pi(s,t).

Taking
\varepsilon<1/3
, we obtain (
5.2
) with the weight
\alpha_{ij}
.
Now we assume that

t_{j}\leq s_{j}-\varepsilon\,\Pi(s,t)\quad\hbox{for all $j\in I_{2}^{\prime\prime}$.}

(5.5)

When
s_{i}\succ_{\varepsilon}t_{i}
for some
i\in I_{1}^{\prime}
, then
it follows from (
5.4
) that for
j\in I_{2}^{\prime\prime}
and
l\in I_{2}^{\prime}\backslash I_{1}^{\prime}
,

(s_{i}+s_{j})-(t_{i}+t_{j})\succ_{\varepsilon}t_{l}+(s_{i}-t_{i})\succ_{\varepsilon}t_{l}\geq\Pi(s,t).

Taking
\varepsilon<1/3
, we obtain (
5.1
).
Now we assume that

s_{i}\leq t_{i}-\varepsilon\,\Pi(s,t)\quad\hbox{for all $i\in I_{1}^{\prime}$.}

(5.6)

From (
5.5
), we obtain

{\sum}_{j\in I_{2}^{\prime\prime}}t_{j}\leq{\sum}_{j\in I_{2}^{\prime\prime}}s_{j}-|I_{1}^{\prime\prime}|\varepsilon\,\Pi(s,t)

(5.7)

From (
5.6
), we obtain

{\sum}_{i\in I_{1}^{\prime}}s_{i}\leq{\sum}_{i\in I_{1}^{\prime}}t_{i}-|I_{1}^{\prime}|\varepsilon\,\Pi(s,t).

(5.8)

Finally, we use that

{\sum}_{i\in I_{1}^{\prime}}s_{i}={\sum}_{j\in I_{1}^{\prime\prime}}s_{j}\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\sum}_{i\in I_{2}^{\prime}}t_{i}={\sum}_{j\in I_{2}^{\prime\prime}}t_{j}.

Then it follows from (
5.7
) and (
5.8
) that

{\sum}_{i\in I_{2}^{\prime}}t_{i}\leq{\sum}_{i\in I_{1}^{\prime}}t_{i}-(|I_{1}^{\prime\prime}|+|I_{1}^{\prime}|)\varepsilon\,\Pi(s,t).

Since
I_{1}^{\prime}\subsetneq I_{2}^{\prime}
, this is only possible when
t_{i}=0
for
i\in I_{2}^{\prime}\backslash I_{1}^{\prime}
and
\Pi(s,t)=0
.
In this case, the proposition holds trivially.

Case 4:

I_{1}^{\prime}=I_{2}^{\prime}
.

Take
i\in I_{1}^{\prime}
and
j\in I_{1}^{\prime\prime}\cap I_{2}^{\prime\prime}
. If

(s_{i}+s_{j})-(t_{i}+t_{j})\geq\varepsilon\,\Pi(s,t)
, we can take the weight
\alpha_{ij}
to obtain (
5.1
). Also if

(t_{i}+t_{j})-(s_{i}+s_{j})\geq\varepsilon\,\Pi(s,t)
, we can take the weight
\alpha_{ij}
to obtain (
5.2
). Hence, it remains to consider the case when

s_{i}+s_{j}\approx_{\varepsilon}t_{i}+t_{j}\quad\hbox{for all $i\in I_{1}^{\prime}$ and $j\in I_{1}^{\prime\prime}\cap I_{2}^{\prime\prime}$}.

(5.9)

Take
i\in I_{1}^{\prime}
and
k\in I_{1}^{\prime\prime}\backslash I_{2}^{\prime\prime}
.
If

(s_{i}+s_{k})-t_{i}\geq\varepsilon\,\Pi(s,t)
, we can take the weight
\alpha_{ik}
to obtain (
5.1
), so that we may assume that

s_{i}+s_{k}\prec_{\varepsilon}t_{i}\quad\hbox{for all $i\in I_{1}^{\prime}$ and $k\in I_{1}^{\prime\prime}\backslash I_{2}^{\prime\prime}$}.

(5.10)

Take
i\in I_{1}^{\prime}
and
l\in I_{2}^{\prime\prime}\backslash I_{1}^{\prime\prime}
.
If

(t_{i}+t_{l})-s_{i}\geq\varepsilon\,\Pi(s,t)
, we can take the weight
\alpha_{il}
to obtain (
5.2
), so that we may assume that

t_{i}+t_{l}\prec_{\varepsilon}s_{i}\quad\hbox{for all $i\in I_{1}^{\prime}$ and $l\in I_{2}^{\prime\prime}\backslash I_{1}^{\prime\prime}$}.

(5.11)

Let us suppose that
I_{1}^{\prime\prime}\backslash I_{2}^{\prime\prime}\neq\emptyset
and

I_{2}^{\prime\prime}\backslash I_{1}^{\prime\prime}\neq\emptyset
.
We write

\Sigma_{1}:={\sum}_{i\in I_{1}^{\prime}}s_{i}={\sum}_{j\in I_{1}^{\prime\prime}}s_{j}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\Sigma_{2}:={\sum}_{i\in I_{2}^{\prime}}t_{i}={\sum}_{j\in I_{2}^{\prime\prime}}t_{j}.

Recall that
I_{1}^{\prime}=I_{2}^{\prime}
.
Let
k\in I_{1}^{\prime\prime}\backslash I_{2}^{\prime\prime}
and

l\in I_{2}^{\prime\prime}\backslash I_{1}^{\prime\prime}
.
Summing (
5.10
) and (
5.11
), we obtain:

\Sigma_{1}+|I_{1}^{\prime}|\,s_{k}\prec_{n\varepsilon}\Sigma_{2}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\Sigma_{2}+|I_{1}^{\prime}|\,t_{l}\prec_{n\varepsilon}\Sigma_{1}.

Hence, it follows that

|I_{1}^{\prime}|\,s_{k}+|I_{1}^{\prime}|\,t_{l}\prec_{2n\varepsilon}0.

Taking
\varepsilon<1/(2n)
, we conclude that that
s_{k}=t_{l}=0
.
Then
\Pi(s,t)=0
and the proposition follows trivially.

Now we can assume that
either
I_{1}^{\prime\prime}\subset I_{2}^{\prime\prime}
and
I_{2}^{\prime\prime}\subset I_{1}^{\prime\prime}
.
We only discuss the case when
I_{1}^{\prime\prime}\subset I_{2}^{\prime\prime}
because
the other case can be treated similarly.
Since
I_{1}\neq I_{2}
and
I^{\prime}_{1}=I^{\prime}_{2}
, we also have
I_{1}^{\prime\prime}\subsetneq I_{2}^{\prime\prime}
.
It follows from (
5.9
) that

|I_{1}^{\prime\prime}|\sum_{i\in I_{1}^{\prime}}s_{i}+|I_{1}^{\prime}|\sum_{j\in I_{1}^{\prime\prime}}s_{j}\approx_{mn\varepsilon}|I_{1}^{\prime\prime}|\sum_{i\in I_{1}^{\prime}}t_{i}+|I_{1}^{\prime}|\sum_{j\in I_{1}^{\prime\prime}}t_{j}.

Therefore,

\Sigma_{1}\approx_{mn\varepsilon}\frac{|I_{1}^{\prime\prime}|}{|I_{1}|}\sum_{i\in I_{1}^{\prime}}t_{i}+\frac{|I_{1}^{\prime}|}{|I_{1}|}\sum_{j\in I_{1}^{\prime\prime}}t_{j}=\Sigma_{2}-\frac{|I_{1}^{\prime}|}{|I_{1}|}\sum_{j\in I_{2}^{\prime\prime}\backslash I_{1}^{\prime\prime}}t_{j}.

(5.12)

Also from (
5.11
), we derive that

\sum_{i\in I_{1}^{\prime}}t_{i}+|I_{1}^{\prime}|t_{l}\prec_{n\varepsilon}\sum_{i\in I_{1}^{\prime}}s_{i}=\Sigma_{1}

(5.13)

for
l\in I_{2}^{\prime\prime}\backslash I_{1}^{\prime\prime}
.
Combining (
5.12
) and (
5.13
), we conclude that

\Sigma_{2}+|I_{1}^{\prime}|t_{l}\prec_{(m+1)n\varepsilon}\Sigma_{2}-\frac{|I_{1}^{\prime}|}{|I_{1}|}\sum_{j\in I_{2}^{\prime\prime}\backslash I_{1}^{\prime\prime}}t_{j},

so that

t_{\ell}\prec_{(m+1)n\varepsilon}0.

When we take
\varepsilon<1/((m+1)n)
, this implies that

t_{\ell}=0
for
\ell\in I_{2}^{\prime\prime}\backslash I_{1}^{\prime\prime}
.
Then
\Pi(s,t)=0
and the proposition follows trivially.
This completes analysis of
Case 4
. Since the other cases
can be treated similarly, this concludes the proof
of the proposition.
∎

6.
Multiple equidistribution

In this section, we prove the following multiple equidistribution result, which generalizes Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}\subset\{1,\ldots,m+n\}

be admissible subsets.
There exist
\ell\geq 1
and
\delta_{r}>0
such that
for every
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Y)
,
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{r}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(X)
, and
t_{1}\in A^{+}_{I_{1}}
,
\ldots
,
t_{r}\in A^{+}_{I_{r}}
,

\displaystyle\int_{Y}f\left(\prod_{s=1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{Y}f\,d\nu\right)\prod_{s=1}^{r}\int_{X_{I_{s}}}\varphi_{s}\,d\mu_{I_{s}}

\displaystyle\quad+O\left(e^{-\delta_{r}\min\big(\lfloor t_{1}\rfloor_{I_{1}},\ldots,\lfloor t_{r}\rfloor_{I_{r}},\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})\big)}\,\|f\|_{W}{\prod}_{s=1}^{r}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{\ell}}\right).

To simplify notation, we write

D(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}):=\min\big(\lfloor t_{1}\rfloor_{I_{1}},\ldots,\lfloor t_{r}\rfloor_{I_{r}},\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})\big).

When
I_{1}=\cdots=I_{r}
, Theorem
6.1
can be proved using the method of
[
5
]
with minor modifications.
Indeed, let us consider the span
W_{I}
of mixing directions
E_{ij}
, namely,
with indices satisfying
i\in I\cap\{1,\ldots,m\}
and
j\in I\cap\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}
.
Since the adjoint action of
A_{I}
on
W_{I}
is proper,
we can run exactly the same argument as in
[
5
]

by considering only one-parameter subgroups
\{\exp(uw)\}_{u\in\mathbb{R}}
with
w\in W_{I}
.
Since those one-parameter subgroups satisfy the required mixing estimate
by Theorem
3.1
, the original argument is applicable.
The base case with
r=1
, which is needed to start this argument, was handled in Theorem
3.3
.

Now we suppose that some of the sets
I_{s}
are distinct.
Then by Proposition
5.1
, there exist
a constant
\theta>0
,
s_{1}\neq s_{2}\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, and a direction
w=E_{i_{0}j_{0}}\in\hbox{Lie}(U)
with the weight
\alpha=\alpha_{i_{0}j_{0}}
such that

\alpha(t_{s_{2}}-t_{s_{1}})\geq\theta\,D(t_{1},\ldots.t_{r})

(6.1)

and
w
is mixing for
I_{s_{2}}
. We change the indexation so that

\alpha(t_{1})\geq\alpha(t_{2})\geq\cdots\geq\alpha(t_{r}).

(6.2)

Then
s_{1}>s_{2}
. It follows from (
6.1
) that
there exists
k=s_{2},\ldots,s_{1}-1
such that

\alpha(t_{k}-t_{k+1})\geq\theta\,D(t_{1},\ldots.t_{r})/r.

(6.3)

From now on, we fix the direction
w=E_{i_{0}j_{0}}
and the index
k
.
It is important for our argument later that for some
s=1,\ldots,k
,
the direction
w
is mixing for
I_{s}
.

For smooth functions
f
on
Y
, we have the uniformly convergent expansion as in (
2.4
):

f=\sum_{\xi\in\Xi_{Y}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\xi.

Then

\displaystyle\int_{Y}f\left(\prod_{s=1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{Y}f\,d\nu\right)\int_{Y}\left(\prod_{s=1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\sum_{\xi\neq 1}\widehat{f}(\xi)\int_{Y}\xi\left(\prod_{s=1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu.

(6.4)

Now we proceed with estimating the integrals

I(\xi):=\int_{Y}\xi\left(\prod_{s=1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu\quad\quad\hbox{with $\xi\in\Xi_{Y}.$}

We claim that for some
\ell\geq 1
and
\delta_{r}>0
,

\displaystyle I(1)

\displaystyle=\prod_{s=1}^{r}\int_{X_{I_{s}}}\varphi_{s}\,d\mu_{I_{s}}+O\left(e^{\delta_{r}D(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})}{\prod}_{s=1}^{r}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{\ell}}\right),

\displaystyle I(\xi)

\displaystyle=O\left(e^{\delta_{r}D(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})}{\prod}_{s=1}^{r}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{\ell}}\right),\quad\hbox{for $\xi\neq 1$},

(6.5)

uniformly on
\xi
.
Once this claim is established, Theorem
6.1

follows directly from (
6.4
).
The proof proceeds by induction on
r
.

We use that the measure
\nu
is invariant under the one-parameter subgroup

\{\exp(uw)\}_{u\in\mathbb{R}}
, so that for any
u\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle I(\xi)=

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\xi\circ\exp(uw)\left(\prod_{s=1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\circ\exp(uw)\right)\,d\nu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi(\exp(uw))\int_{Y}\xi\left(\prod_{s=1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ\exp(uw^{(s)})\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu,

where

w^{(s)}:=\Ad(a(t_{s}))w=e^{\alpha(t_{s})}w.

In particular, it follows from (
6.2
) that

\|w^{(1)}\|\geq\|w^{(2)}\|\geq\cdots\geq\|w^{(r)}\|.

Integrating over
u\in[0,L]
, we deduce that for any
L>0
,

\displaystyle I(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{Y}\xi\left(\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{L}\xi(\exp(uw))\prod_{s=1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ\exp(uw^{(s)})\circ a(t_{s})\,du\right)\,d\nu.

(6.6)

We choose the parameter
L
so that
L\|w^{(s)}\|
is ”large” for
s\leq k

and ”small” for
s\geq k+1
. The exact value of
L
will be given at the end of the argument.

Step I:

Controlling the components
\exp(uw^{(s)})
with
s=k+1,\ldots,r
.

Let us consider a related integral

\displaystyle I_{1}(\xi):=

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\xi\left(\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{L}\xi(\exp(uw))\prod_{s=1}^{k}\varphi_{s}\circ\exp(uw^{(s)})\circ a(t_{s})\,du\right)\left(\prod_{s=k+1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu.

(6.7)

Since
|\xi|=1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle|I(\xi)-I_{1}(\xi)|\leq

\displaystyle\prod_{s=1}^{k}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{0}}

(6.8)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\sup_{u\in[0,L]}\Big\|\prod_{s=k+1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ\exp(uw^{(s)})\circ a(t_{s})-\prod_{s=k+1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\Big\|_{C^{0}},

and by the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\Big\|\prod_{s=k+1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ\exp(uw^{(s)})\circ a(t_{s})-\prod_{s=k+1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\Big\|_{C^{0}}

(6.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\Big\|\prod_{s=k+1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ\exp(uw^{(s)})-\prod_{s=k+1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\Big\|_{C^{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\;

\displaystyle\sum_{l=k+1}^{r}\Big\|\Big(\prod_{s=k+1}^{l}\varphi_{s}\circ\exp(uw^{(s)})\Big)\prod_{s=l+1}^{r}\varphi_{s}-\Big(\prod_{s=k+1}^{l-1}\varphi_{s}\circ\exp(uw^{(s)})\Big)\prod_{s=l}^{r}\varphi_{s}\Big\|_{C^{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\;

\displaystyle\sum_{l=k+1}^{r}\big\|\varphi_{l}\circ\exp(uw^{(l)})-\varphi_{l}\big\|_{C^{0}}\prod_{k+1\leq s\leq r,s\neq\ell}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{0}}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle L\|w^{(k+1)}\|\,{\prod}_{s=k+1}^{r}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{1}}.

Therefore, we conclude that

|I(\xi)-I_{1}(\xi)|\ll L\|w^{(k+1)}\|\,{\prod}_{s=1}^{r}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{1}}.

(6.10)

Step II:

Applying the inductive hypothesis.

We use notation:
I^{(1)}:=I\cup\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}
and
I^{(2)}:=I\cup\{1,\ldots,m\}

When
w=E_{i_{0}j_{0}}
is not mixing with respect to a set
I
,
it is convenient to introduce a unipotent subgroup
U_{I,w}
,
which is defined as follows:

•

If
i_{0}\in I
and
j_{0}\notin I
,
the Lie algebra of
U_{I,w}
is spanned by
E_{ij_{0}}
with
i\in I^{(2)}
.

•

If
i_{0}\notin I
and
j_{0}\in I
,
the Lie algebra of
U_{I,w}
is spanned by
E_{i_{0}j}
with
j\in I^{(1)}
.

•

If
i_{0}\notin I
and
j_{0}\notin I
,
the Lie algebra of
U_{I,w}
is spanned by
E_{i_{0}j_{0}}
.

We note that the spaces
X_{I}
are isomorphic to the spaces from Remark
4.6
.
Indeed, in the first two cases, the action
\exp(\mathbb{R}w)
on
X_{I}
corresponds
to one-parameter subgroups as in Remark
4.6
.
In the last case, since
U_{I,w}
lies in the center of
G_{I}
,
the space
X_{I}
is a bundle with fibers
gU_{I,w}\Gamma
and
the action on the fibers is given by

gz\mapsto g\exp(uw)z,\quad u\in\mathbb{R},\;z\in U_{I,w}\Gamma,

so that we obtain the circle rotation which corresponds to the case
d=1
in Section
4
.
Hence, the results of Section
4
are applicable in our setting.

We denote by
\nu_{I,w}
the invariant probability measure supported on
U_{I,w}\Gamma
.
When
w
is not mixing for
I_{s}
, we set

\varphi^{\star}_{s}(g\Gamma):=\int_{U_{I_{s},w}\Gamma}\varphi_{s}(gy)\,d\nu_{I_{s},w}(y).

We note that
\varphi^{\star}_{s}
is
\exp(\mathbb{R}w)
-invariant,
and
\int_{X_{I_{s}}}\varphi^{\star}_{s}\,d\mu_{I_{s}}=\int_{X_{I_{s}}}\varphi_{s}\,d\mu_{I_{s}}
.

When
w
is mixing for
I_{s}
, we set

\varphi^{\star}_{s}(g\Gamma):=\int_{X_{I_{s}}}\varphi_{s}\,d\mu_{I_{s}}.

Let

\displaystyle I_{2}(\xi):=

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\xi\left(\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{L}\xi(\exp(uw))\prod_{s=1}^{k}\varphi^{\star}_{s}\circ\exp(uw^{(s)})\circ a(t_{s})\,du\right)\left(\prod_{s=k+1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu

and

\displaystyle I_{3}(\xi):=

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\xi\left(\prod_{s=1}^{k}\varphi^{\star}_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\left(\prod_{s=k+1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu.

Arguing as in
Step I
, we obtain that

|I_{2}(\xi)-I_{3}(\xi)|\ll L\|w^{(k+1)}\|\,{\prod}_{s=1}^{r}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{1}}.

(6.11)

Since the functions
\varphi_{s}^{\star}
are
\exp(\mathbb{R}w)
-invariant,

I_{2}(\xi)=\int_{Y}\xi\left(\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{L}\xi(\exp(uw))\prod_{s=1}^{k}\varphi^{\star}_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\,du\right)\left(\prod_{s=k+1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu.

Let us introduce a function
{\Psi}_{\xi,L}:X^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

\displaystyle{\Psi}_{\xi,L}(\underline{x}):=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{L}\xi(\exp(uw))\Big(\prod_{s=1}^{k}\varphi_{s}(\exp(uw^{(s)})x_{s})-\prod_{s=1}^{k}\varphi^{\star}_{s}(\exp(uw^{(s)})x_{s})\Big)\,du

(6.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{L}\xi(\exp(uw))\Big(\prod_{s=1}^{k}\varphi_{s}(\exp(uw^{(s)})x_{s})-\prod_{s=1}^{k}\varphi^{\star}_{s}(x_{s})\Big)\,du

for
\underline{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in X^{k}
.
Setting

\Phi:=\prod_{s=k+1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\quad\hbox{and}\quad a(t):=(a(t_{1}),\ldots,a(t_{k})),

we obtain that

I_{1}(\xi)-I_{2}(\xi)=\nu\big(\xi\cdot(\Psi_{\xi,L}\circ a(t))|_{diag(X^{k})}\cdot\Phi\big).

Hence, by Cauchy-Schwartz inequality,

\displaystyle|I_{1}(\xi)-I_{2}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\nu\big((|\Psi_{\xi,L}|^{2}\circ a(t))|_{diag(X^{k})}\big)^{1/2}\|\Phi\|_{C^{0}}

(6.13)

\displaystyle\leq\nu\big((|\Psi_{\xi,L}|^{2}\circ a(t))|_{diag(X^{k})}\big)^{1/2}{\prod}_{s=k+1}^{r}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{0}}.

Let
\mu^{(k)}:=\prod_{s=1}^{k}\mu_{I_{s}}
be the measure on
X^{k}
.
Using that the inequality

\alpha^{1/2}\leq|\alpha-\beta|^{1/2}+\beta^{1/2}\quad\hbox{ for all $\alpha,\beta\geq 0$,}

we see that

\displaystyle\nu\big((|\Psi_{\xi,L}|^{2}\circ a(t))|_{diag(X^{k})}\big)^{1/2}\leq

\displaystyle\big|\nu\big((|\Psi_{\xi,L}|^{2}\circ a(t))|_{diag(X^{k})}\big)-\mu^{(k)}\big(|{\Psi}_{\xi,L}|^{2}\big)\big|^{1/2}

(6.14)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\mu^{(k)}\big(|{\Psi}_{\xi,L}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}.

We proceed to estimate the first term in (
6.14
).
The function
|\Psi_{\xi,L}|^{2}
can be written as linear combination of functions of the form

{\Omega}_{\xi,L}(\underline{x}):=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}\xi((u_{1}-u_{2})w)\left(\prod_{s=1}^{k}\rho_{1,s}(\exp(u_{1}w^{(s)})x_{s})\rho_{2,s}(\exp(u_{2}w^{(s)})x_{s})\right)\,du_{1}du_{2},

where
\rho_{i,s}
is either
\varphi_{s}
,
\varphi^{\star}_{s}
,
\bar{\varphi}_{s}
,
\bar{\varphi}^{\star}_{s}
.
Let

\omega^{u_{1},u_{2}}_{s}:=\big(\rho_{1,s}\circ\exp(u_{1}w^{(s)})\big)\cdot\big(\rho_{2,s}\circ\exp(u_{2}w^{(s)})\big).

Then

\displaystyle\nu\big((\Omega_{\xi,L}\circ a(t))|_{diag(X^{k})}\big)=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}\xi((u_{1}-u_{2})w)\int_{Y}\left(\prod_{s=1}^{k}\omega_{s}^{u_{1},u_{2}}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu\,du_{1}du_{2}.

Since
k<r
, we can apply the inductive hypothesis to conclude that
for some
\ell\geq 1
and
\delta_{k}>0
,

\int_{Y}\left(\prod_{s=1}^{k}\omega_{s}^{u_{1},u_{2}}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu=\prod_{s=1}^{k}\int_{X_{I_{s}}}\omega_{s}^{u_{1},u_{2}}\,d\mu_{I_{s}}+O\left(e^{-\delta_{k}D(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})}{\prod}_{s=1}^{k}\|\omega_{s}^{u_{1},u_{2}}\|_{C^{\ell}}\right).

Since

\displaystyle\mu^{(k)}\big(\Omega_{\xi,L}\big)=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}\xi((u_{1}-u_{2})w)\left(\prod_{s=1}^{k}\int_{X_{I_{s}}}\omega_{s}^{u_{1},u_{2}}\,d\mu_{I_{s}}\right)\,du_{1}du_{2},

it remains to estimate the error term. We have

\displaystyle\|\omega_{s}^{u_{1},u_{2}}\|_{C^{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|(\rho_{1,s}\circ\exp(u_{1}w^{(s)}))\cdot(\rho_{2,s}\circ\exp(u_{2}w^{(s)}))\big\|_{C^{\ell}}

\displaystyle\ll\big\|\rho_{1,s}\circ\exp(u_{1}w^{(s)})\big\|_{C^{\ell}}\cdot\big\|\rho_{2,s}\circ\exp(u_{2}w^{(s)})\big\|_{C^{\ell}}

\displaystyle\ll\max\big(1,L\|w^{(s)}\|\big)^{2\ell}\|\rho_{1,s}\|_{C^{\ell}}\|\rho_{2,s}\|_{C^{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq\max\big(1,L\|w^{(s)}\|\big)^{2\ell}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{\ell}}^{2}.

Hence, we deduce that

\displaystyle\Big|\nu\big((\Omega_{\xi,L}\circ a(t))|_{diag(X^{k})}\big)-

\displaystyle\mu^{(k)}\big(\Omega_{\xi,L}\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\max\big(1,L\|w^{(1)}\|\big)^{2k\ell}e^{-\delta_{k}D(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})}{\prod}_{s=1}^{k}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{\ell}}^{2}.

Recalling that
|\Psi_{\xi,L}|^{2}
is a linear combination of functions
of the form
\Omega_{\xi,L}
, we also conclude that

\displaystyle\Big|\nu\big((|\Psi_{\xi,L}|^{2}\circ a(t))|_{diag(X^{k})}\big)-

\displaystyle\mu^{(k)}\big(|\Psi_{\xi,L}|^{2}\big)\Big|

(6.15)

\displaystyle\ll\max\big(1,L\|w^{(1)}\|\big)^{2k\ell}e^{-\delta_{k}D(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})}{\prod}_{s=1}^{k}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{\ell}}^{2}.

Step III:

Decorrelation estimates for the flows
\exp(uw^{(s)})
with
s=1,\ldots,k
.

Now we proceed with estimating the second term in (
6.14
).
We write
{\Psi}_{\xi,L}
as

{\Psi}_{\xi,L}(\underline{x})=\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{L}\xi(\exp(uw))\Theta_{u}(\underline{x})\,du

with

\Theta_{u}(\underline{x}):=\prod_{s=1}^{k}\varphi_{s}\big(\exp(uw^{(s)})x_{s}\big)-\prod_{s=1}^{k}\varphi^{\star}_{s}(x_{s}),

so that

\left|{\Psi}_{\xi,L}(\underline{x})\right|^{2}=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}\xi(\exp((u_{1}-u_{2})w))\Theta_{u_{1}}(\underline{x})\overline{\Theta}_{u_{2}}(\underline{x})\,du_{1}du_{2}.

We have

\displaystyle\Theta_{u_{1}}(\underline{x})\overline{\Theta}_{u_{2}}(\underline{x})=

\displaystyle\prod_{s=1}^{k}\varphi_{s}\big(\exp(u_{1}w^{(s)})x_{s}\big)\bar{\varphi}_{s}\big(\exp(u_{2}w^{(s)})x_{s}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\prod_{s=1}^{k}\varphi_{s}\big(\exp(u_{1}w^{(s)})x_{s}\big)\bar{\varphi}^{\star}_{s}(x_{s})-\prod_{s=1}^{k}\varphi^{\star}_{s}(x_{s})\bar{\varphi}_{s}\big(\exp(u_{2}w^{(s)})x_{s}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\prod_{s=1}^{k}\varphi_{s}^{\star}(x_{s})\bar{\varphi}^{\star}_{s}(x_{s}).

Since

\int_{X_{I_{s}}}\big(\varphi_{s}\circ\exp(u_{1}w^{(s)})\big)\cdot\bar{\varphi}^{\star}_{s}\,d\mu_{I_{s}}=\int_{X_{I_{s}}}\varphi^{\star}_{s}\cdot\big(\bar{\varphi}_{s}\circ\exp(u_{2}w^{(s)})\big)\,d\mu_{I_{s}}=\int_{X_{I_{s}}}\varphi^{\star}_{s}\bar{\varphi}^{\star}_{s}\,d\mu_{I_{s}},

we obtain that

\displaystyle\mu^{(k)}\left(\Theta_{u_{1}}\cdot\overline{\Theta}_{u_{2}}\right)=\prod_{s=1}^{k}\mu_{I_{s}}\big(\varphi_{s}\circ\exp(u_{1}w^{(s)})\cdot{\bar{\varphi}_{s}\circ\exp(u_{2}w^{(s)})}\big)-\prod_{s=1}^{k}\mu_{I_{s}}\big(\varphi^{\star}_{s}\cdot\bar{\varphi}^{\star}_{s}\big).

We claim that

\displaystyle\mu^{(k)}\big(|{\Psi}_{\xi,L}|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}\xi(\exp((u_{1}-u_{2})w))\mu^{(k)}\left(\Theta_{u_{1}}\cdot\overline{\Theta}_{u_{2}}\right)\,du_{1}du_{2}

(6.16)

\displaystyle\ll\max\big(1,L\|w^{(k)}\|\big)^{-c}\,{\prod}_{s=1}^{k}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{\ell}}^{2}

for some
c>0

1^{\prime\prime}_{r}
is that at least one of the sets

J^{\prime}_{s}:=\big\{j\in(I^{\prime}_{s})^{c}\,:\,t_{s,j}>(1/\lambda^{2}c_{r})\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})\big\},\quad s=1,\ldots,r,

is non-empty. If this happens, we consider again the new sets

I_{s}^{\prime\prime}:=I^{\prime}_{s}\sqcup J^{\prime}_{s}

and note that

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})\leq\lambda^{2}c_{r}\min\big(\lfloor t_{1}\rfloor_{I_{1}^{\prime\prime}},\ldots,\lfloor t_{r}\rfloor_{I_{r}^{\prime\prime}}\big).

We can now again restart
Case
1_{r}
, but with
\lambda^{2}c_{r}
instead of
\lambda c_{r}
,
and
I_{s}^{\prime\prime}
instead of
I_{s}^{\prime}
. Again at least one of the sets
I_{s}^{\prime\prime}
has strictly increased.
For each such restart, we are either in the favourable
Case
1_{r}^{\prime}
, when we always get a suitable bound, or we are in
the complementary
Case
1_{r}^{\prime\prime}
, which allows us to increase (at least one of) the index sets, and give
Case
1_{r}^{\prime}
a new chance. Since there are at most
r(m+n)
indices in the union of the
r
index sets, there can be at most
r(m+n)
restarts,
after which we must reach
Case
1_{r}^{\prime}
.
Therefore, the resulting bound after these iterations is:

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\left(\prod_{s=1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu=

\displaystyle\prod_{s=1}^{r}\int_{Y}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\,d\nu

\displaystyle\quad\quad+O\left(e^{-\delta/(2\lambda^{r(m+n)}c_{r})\,\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})}\,{\prod}_{s=1}^{r}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{\ell}}\right).

Hence, in
Case
1_{r}
we always get a favourable bound.

The complement of
Case
1_{r}
is:

Case
2_{r}
:

for all admissible
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}\subset\{1,\ldots,m+n\}
, we have

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})>c_{r}\min\big(\lfloor t_{1}\rfloor_{I_{1}},\ldots\lfloor t_{r}\rfloor_{I_{r}}\big).

For
t\in A^{+}
, we set

\lceil t\rceil^{\prime}:=\min\big(\lceil t\rceil_{\{1,\ldots,m\}},\lceil t\rceil_{\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}}\big).

We recall that for
t\in A^{+}
, one has
t_{1}+\cdots+t_{m}=t_{m+1}+\cdots+t_{m+n}
,
so that

\lceil t\rceil_{\{1,\ldots,m\}}\leq n\cdot\lceil t\rceil_{\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad m\cdot\lceil t\rceil_{\{1,\ldots,m\}}\geq\lceil t\rceil_{\{m+1,\ldots,m+n\}}.

Hence,

\lceil t\rceil^{\prime}\geq\max(m,n)^{-1}\cdot\lceil t\rceil.

By varying the sets
I_{s}
over all possible two element admissible sets,
we see that
Case
2_{r}
can be equivalently formulated as:

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})>c_{r}\min\big(\lceil t_{1}\rceil^{\prime},\ldots\lceil t_{r}\rceil^{\prime}\big).

Then

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})>c_{r}\max(m,n)^{-1}\min\big(\lceil t_{1}\rceil,\ldots\lceil t_{r}\rceil\big).

This means that there exists an index
s
such that

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})>c_{r}\max(m,n)^{-1}\lceil t_{s}\rceil.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})>c_{r}\max(m,n)^{-1}\lceil t_{r}\rceil.

(8.4)

In this case, we introduce a function

f_{r-1}:=\varphi_{r}\circ a(t_{r}).

From (
8.4
),

\big\|f_{r-1}|_{Y}\big\|_{W}\leq\|f_{r-1}\|_{C^{1}}\ll e^{2\lceil t_{r}\rceil}\|\varphi_{r}\|_{C^{1}}\ll e^{2\max(m,n)/c_{r}\,\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})}\|\varphi_{r}\|_{C^{1}},

and

\int_{Y}\left(\prod_{s=1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu=\int_{Y}f_{r-1}\left(\prod_{s=1}^{r-1}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu.

Now we apply the above reasoning to the later integral
and consider the corresponding cases:

Case
1_{r-1}
:

we have

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})>c_{r}\max(m,n)^{-1}\lceil t_{r}\rceil,

and there are admissible subsets
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r-1}\subset\{1,\ldots,m+n\}
such that

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})\leq c_{r-1}\min\big(\lfloor{t_{1}}\rfloor_{I_{1}},\ldots,\lfloor{t_{r-1}}\rfloor_{I_{r-1}}\big).

This case splits as above into two complementary subcases:

Case
1^{\prime}_{r-1}
:

we have

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})\geq\lambda c_{r-1}\max\big(\lceil t_{1}\rceil_{I_{1}^{c}},\ldots,\lceil t_{r-1}\rceil_{I_{r-1}^{c}}\big).

As in
Case

1^{\prime}_{r}
above, we apply Lemma
7.1
with
\alpha=c_{r-1}^{-1}
and
\beta=(\lambda c_{r-1})^{-1}<c_{r-1}^{-1}
, and we can conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{Y}f_{r-1}\left(\prod_{s=1}^{r-1}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{Y}f_{r-1}\,d\nu\right)\prod_{s=1}^{r-1}\int_{Y}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\,d\nu

\displaystyle\quad+O\left(e^{-(\delta/c_{r-1}-2r\ell/\lambda c_{r-1})\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})}\,\big\|f_{r-1}|_{Y}\big\|_{W}{\prod}_{s=1}^{r-1}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{\ell}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{s=1}^{r}\int_{Y}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\,d\nu

\displaystyle\quad+O\left(e^{-(\delta/(2c_{r-1})-2\max(m,n)/c_{r})\,\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})}\,{\prod}_{s=1}^{r}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{\ell}}\right).

Case
1^{\prime\prime}_{r-1}
:
at least one of the sets

J_{s}:=\big\{j\in I_{s}^{c}\,:\,t_{s,j}>(1/\lambda c_{r-1})\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})\big\},\quad s=1\ldots,r-1,

is non-empty.

As in
Case
1^{\prime\prime}_{r}
, we define the new sets

I^{\prime}_{s}:=I_{s}\sqcup J_{s},\quad s=1,\ldots,r-1,

and note that

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})\leq\lambda c_{r-1}\min(\lfloor t_{1}\rfloor_{I_{1}^{\prime}},\ldots,\lfloor t_{r}\rfloor_{I_{r}^{\prime}}).

Hence, we can now restart
Case

1_{r-1}
with
\lambda c_{r-1}
instead of
c_{r-1}
and
I_{s}^{\prime}

instead of
I_{s}
. We apply this argument recursively.
If eventually we arrive to
Case

1^{\prime}_{r-1}

and obtain the bound:

\displaystyle\int_{Y}\left(\prod_{s=1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu=

\displaystyle\prod_{s=1}^{r}\int_{Y}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\,d\nu

\displaystyle+O\left(e^{-\big(\delta/(2\lambda^{r(m+n)}c_{r-1})-2\max(m,n)/c_{r}\big)\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})}\,{\prod}_{s=1}^{r}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{\ell}}\right).

This provides a favourable estimate when

\delta/(2\lambda^{r(m+n)}c_{r-1})-2\max(m,n)/c_{r}>0.

The remaining case is:

Case
2_{r-1}
:

we have

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})>c_{r}\max(m,n)^{-1}\lceil t_{r}\rceil,

and for all admissible
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r-1}\subset\{1,\ldots,m+n\}
,

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})>c_{r-1}\min\big(\lfloor t_{1}\rfloor_{I_{1}},\ldots\lfloor t_{r-1}\rfloor_{I_{r-1}}\big).

As above, the last inequality implies that

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})>c_{r-1}\max(m,n)^{-1}\min\big(\lceil t_{1}\rceil,\ldots\lceil t_{r-1}\rceil\big).

This means that there exists
1\leq s\leq r-1
such that

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})>c_{r-1}\max(m,n)^{-1}\lceil t_{s}\rceil.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})>c_{r-1}\max(m,n)^{-1}\lceil t_{r-1}\rceil.

Since
c_{r}\geq c_{r-1}
, we have

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})>c_{r-1}\max(m,n)^{-1}\max\big(\lceil t_{r-1}\rceil,\lceil t_{r}\rceil\big).

In this situation, we introduce the function

f_{r-2}:=\big(\varphi_{r-1}\circ a(t_{r-1})\big)\cdot\big(\varphi_{r}\circ a(t_{r})\big)

and apply the argument as above to
the product
f_{r-2}\left(\prod_{s=1}^{r-2}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)
.
We treat this product by considering
Cases
1_{r-2}
and
2_{r-2}

defined as above. Then we apply this reasoning again to deal with the remaining case and so on.

Generally, the outlined procedure will allow us to reduce the argument
to the situation when

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})>c_{k+1}\max(m,n)^{-1}\max\big(\lceil t_{k+1}\rceil,\ldots,\lceil t_{r}\rceil\big).

(8.5)

Then we introduce the function

f_{k}:=\prod_{s=k+1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s}).

It follows from (
8.5
) that

\displaystyle\big\|f_{k}|_{Y}\big\|_{W}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{s=k+1}^{r}\big\|\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})|_{Y}\big\|_{W}\leq\prod_{s=k+1}^{r}\|\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\|_{C^{1}}\ll\prod_{s=k+1}^{r}e^{2\lceil t_{s}\rceil}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{1}}

\displaystyle\ll e^{2(r-k)\max(m,n)/c_{k+1}\,\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})}\prod_{s=k+1}^{r}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{1}}.

and

\int_{Y}\left(\prod_{s=1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu=\int_{Y}f_{k}\left(\prod_{s=1}^{k}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu.

To analyze this integral, we consider the cases:

Case
1_{k}
:

we have

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})>c_{k+1}\max(m,n)^{-1}\,\max\big(\lceil t_{k+1}\rceil,\ldots\lceil t_{r}\rceil\big),

and
there are admissible subsets
I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}\subset\{1,\ldots,m+n\}
such that

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})\leq c_{k}\min\big(\lfloor{t_{1}}\rfloor_{I_{1}},\ldots,\lfloor{t_{k}}\rfloor_{I_{k}}\big)

Case
1_{k}
splits into two subcases:

Case
1^{\prime}_{k}
:

we have

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})\geq\lambda c_{k}\max\big(\lceil t_{1}\rceil_{I_{1}^{c}},\ldots,\lceil t_{k}\rceil_{I_{k}^{c}}\big).

Applying Lemma
7.1
with
\alpha=c_{k}^{-1}
and
\beta=(\lambda c_{k})^{-1}<c_{k}^{-1}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{Y}f_{k}\left(\prod_{s=1}^{k}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{Y}f_{k}\,d\nu\right)\prod_{s=1}^{k}\int_{Y}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\,d\nu

\displaystyle\quad\quad+O\left(e^{-(\delta/c_{k}-2r\ell/\lambda c_{k})\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})}\,\big\|f_{k}|_{Y}\big\|_{W}{\prod}_{s=1}^{k}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{\ell}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{Y}f_{k}\,d\nu\right){\prod}_{s=1}^{k}\int_{Y}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\,d\nu

\displaystyle\quad\quad+O\left(e^{-\big(\delta/(2c_{k})-2(r-k)\max(m,n)/c_{k+1}\big)\,\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})}\,{\prod}_{s=1}^{k}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{\ell}}\right).

Our inductive assumption (
8.2
), gives that

\int_{Y}f_{k}\,d\nu=\prod_{s=k+1}^{r}\int_{Y}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\,d\nu+O\left(e^{-\eta(r-k)\,\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})}\,{\prod}_{s=k+1}^{r}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{\ell}}\right)

for some
\eta(r-k)>0
.
Therefore, the above estimate gives a favourable bound provided that

\delta/(2c_{k})-2(r-k)\max(m,n)/c_{k+1}>0.

Case
1^{\prime\prime}_{k}
:
at least one of the sets

J_{s}:=\big\{j\in I_{s}^{c}\,:\,t_{s,j}>(1/\lambda c_{k})\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})\big\},\quad s=1\ldots,k

is non-empty.

We introduce the new sets

I^{\prime}_{s}:=I_{s}\sqcup J_{s},\quad s=1,\ldots,k,

and note that

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})\leq\lambda c_{k}\min\big(\lfloor t_{1}\rfloor_{I_{1}^{\prime}},\ldots,\lfloor t_{r}\rfloor_{I_{k}^{\prime}}\big).

We can restart
Case

1_{k}
with
\lambda c_{k}
instead of
c_{k}
and
I_{s}^{\prime}
instead of
I_{s}
. We apply this argument recursively.
Then eventually we arrive to
Case

1^{\prime}_{k}
at some point
and we obtain the bound:

\displaystyle\int_{Y}f_{k}\left(\prod_{s=k+1}^{r}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\right)\,d\nu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{Y}f_{k}\,d\nu\right)\prod_{s=1}^{k}\int_{Y}\varphi_{s}\circ a(t_{s})\,d\nu

\displaystyle\quad\quad+O\left(e^{-(\delta/(2\lambda^{r(m+n)}c_{k})-2(r-k)\max(m,n)/c_{k+1})\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})}\,{\prod}_{s=1}^{r}\|\varphi_{s}\|_{C^{\ell}}\right),

or otherwise we end up in
Case
2_{k}
below.
We note that this bound is favourable provided that

\delta/(2\lambda^{r(m+n)}c_{k})-2(r-k)\max(m,n)/c_{k+1}>0.

(8.6)

The remaining case is:

Case
2_{k}
:

we have

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})>c_{k+1}\max(m,n)^{-1}\max\big(\lceil t_{k+1}\rceil,\ldots\lceil t_{r}\rceil\big),

and for all admissible
I_{1},\ldots,I_{k}\subset\{1,\ldots,m+n\}
, we have

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})>c_{k}\min\big(\lfloor t_{1}\rfloor_{I_{1}},\ldots\lfloor t_{k}\rfloor_{I_{k}}\big).

As above, the last inequality implies that

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})>c_{k}\max(m,n)^{-1}\min\big(\lceil t_{1}\rceil,\ldots\lceil t_{k}\rceil\big).

This means that there exists
1\leq s\leq k
such that

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})>c_{k}\max(m,n)^{-1}\lceil t_{s}\rceil.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})>c_{k}\max(m,n)^{-1}\lceil t_{k}\rceil.

Since
c_{k+1}\geq c_{k}
, we have

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})>c_{k}\max(m,n)^{-1}\max\big(\lceil t_{k}\rceil,\ldots\lceil t_{r}\rceil\big).

We analyze this case by considering
Cases

1^{\prime}_{k-1}
,
1^{\prime\prime}_{k-1}
,
2_{k-1}
and so on.
Proceeding in this way, we end up with the final case when

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})>c_{1}\max(m,n)^{-1}\max\big(\lceil t_{1}\rceil,\ldots\lceil t_{r}\rceil\big).

(8.7)

We observe that for some
1\leq s_{1},s_{2}\leq r
and
1\leq k\leq m+n
,

\Delta(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})=\|t_{s_{1}}-t_{s_{2}}\|=|t_{s_{1},k}-t_{s_{2},k}|\leq\max\big(\lceil t_{s_{1}}\rceil,\lceil t_{s_{2}}\rceil\big).

Since
c_{1}>\max(m,n)
, it follows that (
8.7
) is impossible.
Hence, we have covered all the cases.

It remains to justify the choice of parameters
c_{1},\ldots,c_{r}
. We recall that they must satisfy (
8.3
) and (
8.6
).
Therefore, we can choose them recursively so that
c_{1}>\max(m,n)
and

c_{k+1}>\max\left(1,4(r-k)\max(m,n)\lambda^{r(m+n)}/\delta\right)c_{k}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

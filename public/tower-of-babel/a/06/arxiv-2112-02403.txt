Asymptotics of Schwartz functions: nonarchimedean
Chun-Hsien Hsu

Abstract
. Let G G be a split, simply connected, simple group, and let P ≤ G P\leq G be a maximal parabolic. Braverman and Kazhdan in [ BK02 ] defined a Schwartz space on the affine closure X P X_{P} of X P ∘ := P der \ G X_{P}^{\circ}:=P^{\mathrm{der}}\backslash G . An alternate, more analytically tractable definition was given in [ GHL21 ] , following several earlier works. In the nonarchimedean setting when G G is a classical group or G 2 G_{2} , we show the two definitions coincide and prove several previously conjectured properties of the Schwartz space that will be useful in applications. In addition, we prove that the quotient of the Schwartz space by the space of compactly supported smooth functions on the open orbit is of finite length and we describe its subquotients. Finally, we use our work to study the set of possible poles of degenerate Eisenstein series under certain assumption at archimedean places.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a split, simple, simply connected algebraic group over a local field
F
. Let
\psi:F\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a nontrivial additive character. For a parabolic subgroup
P
of
G
, let
X_{P}^{\circ}:=P^{\mathrm{der}}\backslash G
and
X_{P}:=\mathrm{Spec}(F[X_{P}^{\circ}])
be its affine closure. Generalizing earlier work in
[
BK99
]
, Braverman and Kazhdan in
[
BK02
]
constructed isometries

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{P|Q}:=\mathcal{F}_{P|Q,\psi}:L^{2}(X_{P}(F))\tilde{\longrightarrow}L^{2}(X_{Q}(F))

such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{Q|R}\circ\mathcal{F}_{P|Q}=\mathcal{F}_{P|R}\quad\textrm{ and }\quad\mathcal{F}_{P|P}=\mathrm{Id}

for any parabolic subgroups
P,Q,R
of
G
with the same Levi component
M
. These operators generalize the standard Fourier transform on vector spaces. A nice exposition of their construction is given in
[
Sha18
, §4]
.

Let
M
be a Levi subgroup of
G
and
P
be a parabolic subgroup of
G
with Levi component
M
. Braverman and Kazhdan defined the Schwartz space to be

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{BK}}(X_{P}(F)):=\mathcal{S}(G,M):=\sum_{Q}\mathcal{F}_{Q|P}\left(\mathcal{S}(X_{Q}^{\circ}(F))\right)<L^{2}(X_{P}(F)),

where the sum is taken over all parabolic subgroups
Q
with Levi component
M
. Here
\mathcal{S}(X_{Q}^{\circ}(F))
is simply
C^{\infty}_{c}(X_{Q}^{\circ}(F))
when
F
is nonarchimedean (see e.g.
[
ES18
, Remark 3.2]
for the definition when
F
is archimedean). That is,
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{BK}}(X_{P}(F))<C^{\infty}(X_{P}^{\circ}(F))
is the smallest space of functions that contains
\mathcal{S}(X_{P}^{\circ}(F))
and is stable under Fourier transforms. While this definition of Schwartz space is succinct and natural, it is not known in general how to characterize functions in
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{BK}}(X_{P}(F))
. One main difficulty is that the original construction of the operator
\mathcal{F}_{Q|P}
was only defined on an inexplicit subspace of
C^{\infty}_{c}(X_{Q}^{\circ}(F))
and extended to an
L^{2}
-isometry by unitarity. In particular, little analytic information about functions in
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{BK}}(X_{P}(F))
is understood aside from the case where
X_{P}
is a vector space.

Let
B\leq P
be a Borel subgroup of
G
and
P^{\mathrm{op}}
be the opposite of
P
. In the case
G
is symplectic and
P
is the Siegel parabolic, using the work of Ikeda
[
Ike92
]
, Getz and Liu defined in
[
GL21
]
another Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(F))<C^{\infty}(X_{P}^{\circ}(F)).
Roughly, it is defined to be a space of functions whose Mellin transforms have poles no worse than those of certain local
L
-functions. This definition was later modified in
[
GH20
]
and extended by Getz, Leslie, and the author in
[
GHL21
]
to all split, simple, simply connected groups
G
and maximal parabolics
P
. In
loc. cit.
we showed that functions in
\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(F))
lie in
L^{1}\cap L^{2}(X_{P}(F))
and have good analytic behavior towards infinity. For instance, they are compactly supported in
X_{P}(F)
if
F
is nonarchimedean. Moreover, the isometry
\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}
descends to an isomorphism

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}:\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(F))\tilde{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{S}(X_{P^{\mathrm{op}}}(F))

and comes with an explicit formula. What is not clear is the following
locality
property:

Conjecture 1.1
.

Suppose
F
is a nonarchimedean local field. The Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(F))
is a
C^{\infty}(X_{P}(F))
-module (under multiplication of functions).

For
F
archimedean,
C^{\infty}(X_{P}(F))
is replaced with the subspace of tempered functions (see e.g.
[
AG08
, Definition 4.2.1]
). When
X_{P}
is a vector space, the locality property follows from the fact that the usual Schwartz space is the subalgebra of
C^{\infty}(X_{P}(F))
consisting of rapidly decreasing functions, which plays an important role in Fourier analysis.

Remark 1.2
.

The notion of locality of a function space was introduced in
[
BK00
, §5]
. Their definition in the nonarchimedean setting used continuous sections instead of smooth sections; however, under their definition, the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(F)
will not be local, for it is not stable under the multiplication by the norm function (which is continuous).

1.1.
Properties of Schwartz spaces
\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(F))

Suppose
F
is a nonarchimedean local field and
G
is not of type
E
or type
F
. Let
P
be a maximal parabolic subgroup of
G
. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

We have

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(X_{P}^{\circ}(F))<\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(F))

and the space
\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(F))
is local.
Moreover,
\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(F))/\mathcal{S}(X_{P}^{\circ}(F))
is a smooth
G(F)
-module of finite length.

The inclusion (
1.2
) will follow from an analysis of poles of the intertwining operator
M_{w_{0}}
associated to the long Weyl element (see §
1.2
). We explain how the locality property of
\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(F))
follows from the inclusion (
1.2
).

Proof of Conjecture
1.1
assuming (
1.2
).

Choose a
G
-equivariant closed embedding
X_{P}\longrightarrow V_{P}
into a
F
-vector space such that the point
X_{P}(F)-X_{P}^{\circ}(F)
is mapped to the origin (see §
3
). Let
\mathcal{S}:=\mathrm{Im}(\mathcal{S}(V_{P}(F))\longrightarrow C^{\infty}(X_{P}^{\circ}(F)))
, where the implicit map is the restriction. Note that
\mathcal{S}(X_{P}^{\circ}(F))<\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}/\mathcal{S}(X_{P}^{\circ}(F))=\mathbb{C}
. Since each function in
\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(F))
is compactly supported in
X_{P}(F),
we have

C^{\infty}(X_{P}(F))\cdot\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(F))=\mathcal{S}\cdot\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(F)).
As
\mathcal{S}(X_{P}^{\circ}(F))\cdot\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(F))=\mathcal{S}(X_{P}^{\circ}(F))<\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(F))
by (
1.2
), it suffices to show
\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(F))/\mathcal{S}(X_{P}^{\circ}(F))
is a
\mathcal{S}/\mathcal{S}(X_{P}^{\circ}(F))
-module, i.e., a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space, which is clear.
∎

The quotient
\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(F))/\mathcal{S}(X_{P}^{\circ}(F))
is roughly the kernel of
M_{w_{0}}
at points of reducibility on the left half-plane. A precise description is given in Theorem
4.10
together with an example in §
4.3
. As a result, the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(F))
admits a natural inductive limit topology under which it is nuclear, barreled, and separable (see
[
Li18
, §4.1]
). These topological properties of
\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(F))
are stated as axioms in
[
Li18
]
to validate Weil’s interpretation of zeta integrals as a unique family of tempered distributions. We expect an analogous result in the archimedean setting can be derived similarly.

1.2.
Poles of intertwining operators

Let
T\leq B
be a maximal torus of
G
. Let
\Phi:=\Phi_{G},\Delta,
and
W:=W_{G}
denote the set of roots, simple roots, and the Weyl group of
(G,B,T)
respectively. Let
W/W_{M}
collect the unique element of minimal length in each left coset of
W_{M}
in
W
. Let
\{\omega_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Delta}
be fundamental weights of
T
, and let
\omega_{P}
be the fundamental weight associated to the simple root corresponding to
P
.

We briefly recall the parameters
\Lambda=\{(s_{i},\lambda_{i})\}
considered in
[
BK02
]
to define
\mathcal{F}_{P|P^{\mathrm{op}}}
. Let
P=MN_{P}
be a Levi decomposition. On the dual side, we have a natural action of

\displaystyle\widehat{M}^{\mathrm{ab}}\times\widehat{\mathfrak{m}}

on
\widehat{\mathfrak{n}}_{P}
induced by the adjoint action. Fix a principal
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
\{e,h,f\}
in
\widehat{\mathfrak{m}}
, and let
\widehat{\mathfrak{n}}_{P}^{e}
be the vector space spanned by highest weight vectors. Decompose
\widehat{\mathfrak{n}}_{P}^{e}=\oplus_{i=1}^{k}L_{i}
into one dimensional modules. Let
\lambda_{i}>0
be the
\widehat{M}^{\mathrm{ab}}
-character and
s_{i}\geq 0
be
\tfrac{1}{2}
times the
h
-eigenvalue on
L_{i}
. It is proved in
[
GHL21
, Appendix A]
that one can rearrange the indices so that
s_{k}>\frac{s_{i}}{\lambda_{i}}
for all
k>i
and
\lambda_{k}=1
, which we assume henceforth.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be the ring of integers of
F
and
q
be the cardinality of the residue field. Fixing a uniformizer, we identify groups of quasi-characters
\widehat{F^{\times}}=\mathbb{C}/\tfrac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{\log q}\mathbb{Z}\times\widehat{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}
(see §
2.1
for conventions). For
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}
, put
\chi_{s}:=\chi|\cdot|^{s}
for
s\in\mathbb{C}
. Identifying
M^{\mathrm{ab}}(F)\tilde{\longrightarrow}F^{\times}
via
\omega_{P}
, we view
\chi_{s}
as a quasi-character of
M(F)
.
To prove the inclusion (
1.2
), by the definition of
\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(F))
it suffices to show the intertwining operator

\displaystyle\frac{M_{w_{0}}(\chi_{s})}{\prod_{i=1}^{k}L(-s_{i},\chi_{s}^{\lambda_{i}})}

is holomorphic, where
w_{0}\in W/W_{M}
is the long Weyl element.

Let
\Phi^{\lor}
be the set of coroots and
(\Phi^{\lor})^{+}
(resp.
(\Phi^{\lor})^{-}
) be the set of positive (resp. negative) coroots. For each
w\in W/W_{M}
, let

\displaystyle\Phi_{w}^{\lor}

\displaystyle:=\{\beta^{\lor}:\beta^{\lor}\in(\Phi^{\lor})^{+},w\beta^{\lor}\in(\Phi^{\lor})^{-}\}\subset(\Phi^{\lor})^{+}-(\Phi_{M}^{\lor})^{+}.

Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:X^{\ast}(T)\times X_{\ast}(T)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
denote the canonical pairing on characters and cocharacters. For
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}
and
s\in\mathbb{C}
, define

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}c_{w}(\chi_{s})&:=\prod_{\beta^{\lor}\in\Phi_{w}^{\lor}}\frac{L\left(-\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}\langle\omega_{\alpha},\beta^{\lor}\rangle,\chi_{s+s_{k}+1}^{\langle\omega_{P},\beta^{\lor}\rangle}\right)}{L\left(1-\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}\langle\omega_{\alpha},\beta^{\lor}\rangle,\chi_{s+s_{k}+1}^{\langle\omega_{P},\beta^{\lor}\rangle}\right)},\\
d(\chi_{s})&:=\prod_{i=1}^{k}L(s_{i}+1,\chi_{s}^{\lambda_{i}}).\end{split}

Our notation is consistent with
[
Ike92
]
. Following the idea of
[
Ike92
]
, we show

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose
F
is nonarchimedean. For
w\in W/W_{M}
and
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}
,

\displaystyle d(\chi_{s})c_{w}(\chi_{s})

is a product of L-functions. The intertwining operator

\left(d(\chi_{s})c_{w}(\chi_{s})\right)^{-1}M_{w}(\chi_{s})

is holomorphic.

Therefore, (
1.2
) is a consequence of the identity

(1.4)

\displaystyle d(\chi_{s})c_{w_{0}}(\chi_{s})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}L(-s_{i},\chi_{s}^{\lambda_{i}}),

which can be directly verified (see Lemma
5.4
). Moreover,

Corollary 1.5
.

We have
\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(F))=\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{BK}}(X_{P}(F))
. For any
w\in W/W_{M}
and
f\in\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(F))
,

\displaystyle\left(d(\chi_{s})c_{w}(\chi_{s})\right)^{-1}M_{w}(\chi_{s})f_{\chi_{s}}

is holomorphic, where
f_{\chi_{s}}
is the Mellin transform of
f
along
\chi
defined in (
3.4
).

With Theorem
1.4
and Corollary
1.5
in hand, one can also determine the set of possible poles of an Eisenstein series (see e.g.
[
PSR87
]
for a detailed argument). Let
E
be a global field and
\mathbb{A}_{E}
be its ring of adele. Define the global Schwartz space

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(\mathbb{A}_{E})):={\bigotimes_{v}}^{\prime}\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(E_{v}))

where the restricted tensor product is taken with respect to the basic function
b_{v}
(
v<\infty
). Here
b_{v}
is the right
G(\mathcal{O}_{v})
-invariant function in
\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(E_{v}))
whose Mellin transform (along the trivial character) is
d(1_{s})\phi_{s}
, where
\phi_{s}\in I_{P}(1_{s}):=\mathrm{Ind}_{P(E_{v})}^{G(E_{v})}(1_{s})
is the unique right
G(\mathcal{O}_{v})
-invariant section such that
\phi_{s}\big|_{G(\mathcal{O}_{v})}=1
. For
f\in\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(\mathbb{A}_{E}))
and an idelic unitary character
\omega
, the degenerate Eisenstein series

\displaystyle\textrm{Eis}(g;f_{\omega_{s}}):=\sum_{\gamma\in X_{P}^{\circ}(E)}f_{\omega_{s}}(\gamma g)

converges absolutely for
g\in G(\mathbb{A}_{E})
and
\mathrm{Re}(s)\gg 0
and admits an analytic continuation.

Corollary 1.6
.

Suppose
E
is a function field or
E
is a number field and Corollary
1.5
holds in the archimedean setting. Let

\displaystyle d_{0}:=\max\{\lambda:\langle\omega_{P},\beta^{\lor}\rangle=\lambda,\beta^{\lor}\in\Phi_{w_{0}}^{\lor}=(\Phi^{\lor})^{+}-(\Phi_{M}^{\lor})^{+}\}=\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\lambda_{i}\leq 6.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}(X_{P}(\mathbb{A}_{E}))
and
\omega
be an idelic unitary character. For
g\in G(\mathbb{A}_{E}),
the Eisenstein series
\textrm{Eis}(g;f_{\omega_{s}})

is entire unless
\omega^{d}=1
for some
d\leq d_{0}
. Moreover, there exists a discrete subset
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}
and an integer
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
that depends only on
X_{P}
such that the following holds.

\bullet

The set
\Gamma
is contained inside the vertical strip
-(s_{k}+1)\leq\mathrm{Re(s)}\leq s_{k}+1
.

\bullet

If
E
is a function field, then
\Gamma
is contained in
\bigcup_{1\leq d\leq d_{0}}\frac{1}{d}\left(\mathbb{Z}+\tfrac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{\log q}\mathbb{Z}\right)
and is finite modulo
\tfrac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{\log q}\mathbb{Z}
; if
E
is a number field,
\Gamma
is finite and contained in
\bigcup_{1\leq d\leq d_{0}}\tfrac{1}{d}\mathbb{Z}
.

\bullet

If
\textrm{Eis}(g;f_{\omega_{s}})

has a pole at
s=s_{0}
, then
s_{0}\in\Gamma
and the pole has order not greater than
N
.

Proof.

According to
[
Lan76
]
,
\textrm{Eis}(g;f_{\omega_{s}})
has the same poles (counting multiplicities) as its constant term

(1.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{w\in W/W_{M}}M_{w}(\omega_{s})f_{\omega_{s}}.

By Gindikin-Karpelevič formula
[
Lai80
, Proposition 4.6]
for unramfied places
v
with
\psi_{v}
unramified

\displaystyle\left(d(1_{s})c_{w}(1_{s})\right)^{-1}M_{w}(1_{s})(b_{v})_{1_{s}}|_{G(\mathcal{O}_{v})}=1.

Therefore by Corollary
1.5
and the assumption at the archimedean places

\displaystyle\left(d(\omega_{s})c_{w}(\omega_{s})\right)^{-1}M_{w}(\omega_{s})f_{\omega_{s}}

is entire for all
w\in W/W_{M}
. Consequently the order of poles of
\textrm{Eis}(g;f_{\omega_{s}})
is dominated by the maximum of the order of poles of
d(\omega_{s})c_{w}(\omega_{s})
ranging over
w\in W/W_{M}
. By Theorem
1.4

d(\omega_{s})c_{w}(\omega_{s})
are products of
L
-functions. Since
d(\omega_{s})c_{w}(\omega_{s})
is entire if
\omega^{d}\neq 1
for any
d\leq d_{0}
, the first assertion follows. Take
\Gamma
to be the set of poles of
d(\omega_{s})c_{w}(\omega_{s})
and
N
be the maximal order of poles of
d(\omega_{s})c_{w}(\omega_{s})
, where
w
ranges over all
W/W_{M}
and
\omega
ranges over all
\omega^{d}=1
for some
d\leq d_{0}
. By (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) and the fact that
s_{k}>\frac{s_{i}}{\lambda_{i}}
for all
i<k
, we have

\displaystyle 0<\frac{\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}\langle\omega_{\alpha},\beta^{\lor}\rangle}{\langle\omega_{P},\beta^{\lor}\rangle}\leq s_{k}+(s_{k}+1)

for all
\beta^{\lor}\in\Phi_{w_{0}}^{\lor}=(\Phi^{\lor})^{+}-(\Phi_{M}^{\lor})^{+}
. Therefore, poles of
d(\omega_{s})c_{w}(\omega_{s})
are contained in the vertical strip
\{s:|\mathrm{Re}(s)|\leq s_{k}+1\}
. The rest is clear.
∎

In
[
CG21
]
, Choie and Getz establish the Poisson summation conjecture for certain Schubert varieties under mild assumptions on poles of Eisenstein series. Corollary
1.6
partially justifies
[
CG21
, Conjecture 1.4]
and reduces their assumption to the analysis of poles of
M_{w}(\chi_{s})
in the archimedean case. More work is required to locate the exact set of possible poles and orders, which will give rise to an explicit form of Poisson summation formula on
X_{P}
and the Schubert varieties. Several cases have been treated in
[
Ike92
,
Han18
,
HM15
]
. See
[
CG21
]
for a detailed discussion.

Finally, we remark that our proofs of Theorem
1.3
and Corollary
1.5
rely only on Theorem
1.4
but not on the type of
G
. However, proving Theorem
1.4
eventually boils down to certain combinatorics statements on the set of coroots in
(\Phi^{\lor})^{+}-(\Phi_{M}^{\lor})^{+}
, which requires a case-by-case analysis. Since the proof of type
E
and type
F
is more complicated, we do not carry out the proof.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper,
F
will be a nonarchimedean local field. We denote by
\mathcal{O}
the ring of integers of
F
and fix, once and for all, a choice of uniformizer
\varpi
. Let
q
be the cardinality of the residue field. Let
\mathrm{ord}:F\to\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}
be the discrete valuation with
\mathrm{ord}(\varpi)=1
and let
|\cdot|
be the induced norm on
F
with
|\varpi|=q^{-1}
.

Let
G
be a split, simply connected, simple group over
F
. Fix throughout the paper a maximal torus
T<G
and a pinning
(\Delta,(e_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\Delta})
of
(G,T)
. Let
B
be the Borel subgroup of
G
standard with respect to the pinning. For each
\alpha\in\Delta
, let
\omega_{\alpha}\in X^{\ast}(T)
be the fundamental weight such that

\displaystyle\langle\omega_{\alpha},\beta^{\lor}\rangle=\delta_{\alpha,\beta}\textrm{ for all }\beta\in\Delta,

where
\delta_{\alpha,\beta}
is the Kronecker
\delta
.

Let
P\geq B
be a maximal parabolic subgroup with Levi decomposition
P=MN_{P}
and
\omega_{P}
be the fundamental weight corresponding to the simple root associated to
P
. The attached
Braverman-Kazhdan space
(a.k.a.
pre-flag variety
)

X_{P}^{\circ}:=P^{\mathrm{der}}\backslash G

admits a natural right
M^{\mathrm{ab}}\times G
action, given on points in an
F
-algebra
R
by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}X_{P}^{\circ}(R)\times M^{\mathrm{ab}}(R)\times G(R)&\longrightarrow X_{P}^{\circ}(R)\\
(x,m,g)&\longmapsto m^{-1}xg.\end{split}

Let
X_{P}:=\mathrm{Spec}(F[X_{P}^{\circ}])
be the affine closure of
X_{P}^{\circ}
.

2.1.
Quasi-characters

We have a group isomorphism

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}F^{\times}&\tilde{\longrightarrow}\,\mathbb{Z}\times\mathcal{O}^{\times}\\
a&\mapsto(\mathrm{ord}(a),\tilde{a}),\end{split}

where

\tilde{a}:=a\cdot\varpi^{-\mathrm{ord(a)}}.

Let
\widehat{F^{\times}},\widehat{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}
denote groups of quasi-characters of
F^{\times}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
respectively. The isomorphism (
2.2
) induces a group isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}/\tfrac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{\log q}\mathbb{Z}\times\widehat{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}

\displaystyle\tilde{\longrightarrow}\,\widehat{F^{\times}}

\displaystyle(s,\chi)

\displaystyle\mapsto\left(a\mapsto|a|^{s}\chi(\tilde{a})\right).

We henceforth identify
\widehat{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}
as a subgroup of
\widehat{F^{\times}}
under this isomorphism, and thus
\chi(a)=\chi(\tilde{a})
for
\chi\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}
. Let
\mathrm{ord}(\chi)
be the order of
\chi
, i.e., the smallest positive integer
d
such that
\chi^{d}=1
. For
\chi\in\widehat{F^{\times}}
and
s\in\mathbb{C}/\tfrac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{\log q}\mathbb{Z}
, define
\chi_{s}:=\chi|\cdot|^{s}

2.3.
Multisets

A multiset

consists of a set

. Equivalently, we can understand

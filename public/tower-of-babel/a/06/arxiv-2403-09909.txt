Cokernels of random matrix products and flag Cohen–Lenstra heuristic
Yifeng Huang

Abstract
. In [ NVP22 ] , Nguyen and Van Peski raised the question of whether the surjective flag of ℤ p {\mathbb{Z}}_{p} -modules modeled by cok ( M 1 ⋯ M k ) ↠ … ↠ cok ( M 1 ) \mathrm{cok}(M_{1}\cdots M_{k})\twoheadrightarrow\dots\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{cok}(M_{1}) for independent random matrices M 1 , … , M k ∈ Mat n ⁡ ( ℤ p ) M_{1},\dots,M_{k}\in\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}) satisfies the Cohen–Lenstra heuristic. We answer the question affirmatively when M 1 , … , M k M_{1},\dots,M_{k} follow the Haar measure, and our proof demonstrates how classical ideas in Cohen–Lenstra heuristic adapt naturally to the flag setting. We also prove an analogue for non-square matrices.



1.
Introduction

We start by introducing our notation for surjective flags of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-modules, which is our main subject of investigation.

1.1.
Notation and terminology

Fix
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
and a prime
p
. Let
\mathbf{Mod}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}
denote the category of finitely generated
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-modules. We refer to a diagram of surjections
G_{k}\overset{\phi_{k-1}}{\twoheadrightarrow}\dots\overset{\phi_{1}}{\twoheadrightarrow}G_{1}(\overset{\phi_{0}}{\twoheadrightarrow}G_{0}:=0)
in
\mathbf{Mod}_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}}
as a
k
-
surjective flag
of (finitely generated)
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-modules. We denote by
\mathbf{Fl}_{k}=\mathbf{Fl}_{k}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
the set of
k
-surjective flags of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-modules up to isomorphism. For
\mathbf{G}=(G_{k}\twoheadrightarrow\dots\twoheadrightarrow G_{1})\in\mathbf{Fl}_{k}
, let
\Aut(\mathbf{G})
denote the automorphism of the flag
\mathbf{G}
. See also
[
NVP22
, Def. 18, 19]
.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
and let
\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
denote the set of
n\times n
matrices over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. For
M_{1},\dots,M_{k}\in\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
, consider the flag

(1.1)

\mathrm{cok}(M_{1}\cdots M_{k})\twoheadrightarrow\dots\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{cok}(M_{1}),

where the surjection
\mathrm{cok}(M_{1}\dots M_{i+1})={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}/\im(M_{1}\dots M_{i+1})\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{cok}(M_{1}\dots M_{i})={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}/\im(M_{1}\dots M_{i})
is induced by the inclusion
\im(M_{1}\dots M_{i+1})\subseteq\im(M_{1}\dots M_{i})
. We denote this flag by
\mathbf{cok}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})
.

Since
\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n^{2}}
is a compact topological group, there is a unique probability Haar measure on
\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
. We refer to a random element of
\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
following the Haar measure as a
Haar-random matrix
in
\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
.

1.2.
Background

When
M_{1},\dots,M_{k}
are random matrices in
\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
, we get a probability measure on
\mathbf{Fl}_{k}
modeled by
\mathbf{cok}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})
. In
[
NVP22
]
, Nguyen and Van Peski initiated the investigation of
\mathbf{cok}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})
by studying the joint distribution of
\mathrm{cok}(M_{1}),\dots,\allowbreak\mathrm{cok}(M_{1}\cdots M_{k})
as
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-modules. They proved a universality result in the sense of Wood
[
Woo19
]
, namely, if the
kn^{2}
entries from
M_{1},\dots,M_{k}
are independent and each is not too concentrated mod
p
, then as
n\to\infty
, the limiting joint distribution of
\mathrm{cok}(M_{1}),\dots,\mathrm{cok}(M_{1}\cdots M_{k})
is insensitive to the exact distributions of these
kn^{2}
entries. The limiting joint distribution is also explicitly determined.

Since the datum
(\mathrm{cok}(M_{1}),\dots,\mathrm{cok}(M_{1}\cdots M_{k}))
is just
\mathbf{cok}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})
forgetting the surjections in between, it is natural to expect that the above results are explained by a universal distribution on the level of
\mathbf{cok}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})\in\mathbf{Fl}_{k}
. Nguyen and Van Peski
[
NVP22
, §10]
asked if the above results lift to
\mathbf{cok}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})
; they defined the conjectured limiting distribution in
[
NVP22
, Thm. 1.3]
.

1.3.
Our result

We answer their question affirmatively when
M_{1},\dots,M_{k}
distribute independently and follow the Haar measure. In this case, we also obtain the exact distribution of
\mathbf{cok}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})
for each fixed
n
. For
G\in\mathbf{Mod}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
, let
r(G):=\dim_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}G/pG
be the
rank
of
G
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
. Let
M_{1},\dots,M_{k}\in\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
be independent and Haar-random, and fix
\mathbf{G}=(G_{k}\twoheadrightarrow\dots\twoheadrightarrow G_{1})\in\mathbf{Fl}_{k}
such that
\lvert G_{k}\rvert<\infty
. Then for
n\geq r(G_{k})
,

(1.2)

\Prob_{M_{1},\dots,M_{k}\in\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})}(\mathbf{cok}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})\simeq\mathbf{G})=\frac{1}{\lvert\Aut(\mathbf{G})\rvert}\left\lparen\prod_{i=n-r(G_{k})+1}^{n}\!\!(1-p^{-i})\right\rparen\left\lparen\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-p^{-i})\right\rparen^{k}.

In particular, when
n\to\infty
,

(1.3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Prob_{M_{1},\dots,M_{k}\in\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})}(\mathbf{cok}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})\simeq\mathbf{G})=\frac{1}{\lvert\Aut(\mathbf{G})\rvert}\left\lparen\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}(1-p^{-i})\right\rparen^{k}.

Remark
.

It is clear that if
\lvert G_{k}\rvert=\infty
or
n<r(G_{k})
, then the probability in (
1.2
) is zero:
G_{k}=\mathrm{cok}(M_{1}\cdots M_{k})
must have rank at most
n
, and
\lvert G_{k}\rvert=\infty
only happens when at least one of
\det(M_{1}),\dots,\allowbreak\det(M_{k})
is zero, which happens with probability zero.

The flag
\mathbf{cok}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})
is probably the finest datum one could get from a chain of matrices
(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})
.
1
1

1

One could think of
(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})
as a chain of linear maps
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}\overset{M_{k}}{\to}\dots\overset{M_{1}}{\to}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
.
For example,
\mathrm{cok}(M_{i}M_{i+1}\dots M_{j})
for
1\leq i\leq j\leq k
is isomorphic to the kernel of
G_{j}\twoheadrightarrow G_{i-1}
, a concatenation of several surjections from
\mathbf{cok}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})
. The distribution of
\mathbf{cok}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})
thus encodes the joint distribution of all
\mathrm{cok}(M_{i}M_{i+1}\dots M_{j})
. We note that the joint distribution of certain subsets of these cokernels have natural connections to Hall algebras (see
[
NVP22
, p. 46]
).

An important purpose of the paper is to demonstrate that some classical ideas to study the Cohen–Lenstra heuristic
[
CL84
,
FW89
]
adapt to the (apparently highly refined) flag setting nicely. This point will be evident once we set up the language in §
2.1
. The ease to work with flags will be showcased in our proof of Theorem
1.1
; for example, no knowledge about
\lvert\Aut(\mathbf{G})\rvert
is required.

Indeed when
k\geq 2
, we do not even have a combinatorial classification of
\mathbf{G}

[
NVP22
, p. 44, Rmk. 7]
, let alone a general formula for
\lvert\Aut(\mathbf{G})\rvert
. However, it is known that certain summations involving

are expressed in Hall–Littlewood polynomials
[
NVP22
, §5–7,10]
.
In light of its simplicity, we conjecture that a suitable combination of our method and the general machinery of Sawin and Wood
[

]
would yield a universality version of Theorem

.

1.4.
Further applications

A slight modification of the proof of Theorem
1.1
implies an analogue for non-square matrices.

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
, and
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
. For
1\leq i\leq k
, let
M_{i}\in\Mat_{(n+u_{i-1})\times(n+u_{i})}
, where
u_{0}:=0
, and assume
M_{1},\dots,M_{k}
are independent and Haar-random. Fix
\mathbf{G}=(G_{k}\twoheadrightarrow\dots\twoheadrightarrow G_{0}=0)\in\mathbf{Fl}_{k}
. Then

(1.4)

\Prob_{M_{1},\dots,M_{k}}(\mathbf{cok}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})\simeq\mathbf{G})=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left\lparen\frac{\lvert G_{j}\rvert}{\lvert G_{j-1}\rvert}\right\rparen^{-u_{j}}}{\lvert\Aut(\mathbf{G})\rvert}\left\lparen\prod_{i=n-r(G_{k})+1}^{n}\!\!(1-p^{-i})\right\rparen\left\lparen\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-p^{-i-u_{j}})\right\rparen

if
\lvert G_{k}\rvert<\infty
and
r(G_{k})\leq n
, and zero otherwise. In particular, when
n\to\infty
,

(1.5)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Prob_{M_{1},\dots,M_{k}}(\mathbf{cok}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})\simeq\mathbf{G})=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left\lparen\frac{\lvert G_{j}\rvert}{\lvert G_{j-1}\rvert}\right\rparen^{-u_{j}}}{\lvert\Aut(\mathbf{G})\rvert}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}(1-p^{-i-u_{j}})

if
\lvert G_{k}\rvert<\infty
, and zero otherwise.

Remark
.

It is less obvious
a priori
why
\lvert\mathrm{cok}(M_{1}\cdots M_{k})\rvert<\infty
with probability one, since we cannot use determinants anymore. This will be a consequence of our proof.

The non-flag case (i.e.,
k=1
) was proved in
[
Woo19
]
, together with the universality result. We conjecture that (
1.5
) holds if
M_{1},\dots,M_{k}
are independent and
\varepsilon
-balanced in the definition of
[
Woo19
]
.

As an application of the non-square analogue, we construct a
k
-parameter family of deformations of the “Cohen–Lenstra probability measure” in (
1.2
). For any flag
\mathbf{G}=(G_{k}\twoheadrightarrow\dots\twoheadrightarrow G_{0}=0)\in\mathbf{Fl}_{k}
such that
\lvert G_{k}\rvert<\infty
, we let
n_{1}(\mathbf{G}),\dots,n_{k}(\mathbf{G})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
be defined by

(1.6)

p^{n_{i}(\mathbf{G})}:=\lvert G_{i}\rvert/\lvert G_{i-1}\rvert.

Definition 1.3
.

For
k,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1},t_{1},\dots,t_{k}\in[0,p)
, we define a measure
P_{n,(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})}
on
\mathbf{Fl}_{k}
by

(1.7)

P_{n,(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})}(\mathbf{G}):=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k}t_{j}^{n_{j}(\mathbf{G})}}{\lvert\Aut(\mathbf{G})\rvert}\left\lparen\prod_{i=n-r(G_{k})+1}^{n}\!\!(1-p^{-i})\right\rparen\left\lparen\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-p^{-i}t_{j})\right\rparen

if
\lvert G_{k}\rvert<\infty
and
r(G_{k})\leq n
, and zero otherwise. Similarly, define

(1.8)

P_{\infty,(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})}(\mathbf{G}):=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k}t_{j}^{n_{j}(\mathbf{G})}}{\lvert\Aut(\mathbf{G})\rvert}\left\lparen\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}(1-p^{-i}t_{j})\right\rparen

if
\lvert G_{k}\rvert<\infty
, and zero otherwise.

When
t_{i}=1
, the measure (
1.7
) reduces to (
1.2
); when
t_{i}=p^{-u_{i}},u_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, the measure (
1.7
) reduces to (
1.4
). This means that in these cases, (
1.7
) comes from a random matrix model. A brief argument in §
5
implies:

Corollary 1.4
.

P_{n,(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})}
and
P_{\infty,(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})}
are probability measures on
\mathbf{Fl}_{k}
.

When
k=1
, Definition
1.3
is precisely the measure considered by Fulman and Kaplan
[
FK19
]
. The joint distribution of
(G_{1},\dots,G_{k})
for
\mathbf{G}
distributed according to (
1.7
) is expressible in terms of Hall–Littlewood polynomials and can be extracted from
[
VP21
]
, see Proposition
5.1
.

Part of the content of Corollary
1.4
is that the normalizing constant in Definition
1.3
is correct. For example,
P_{\infty,(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})}
being a probability measure is equivalent to the formal identity

(1.9)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{G}\\
\lvert G_{k}\rvert<\infty\end{subarray}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k}t_{j}^{n_{j}(\mathbf{G})}}{\lvert\Aut(\mathbf{G})\rvert}=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-p^{-i}t_{j}}\in{\mathbb{C}}[[t_{1},\dots,t_{k}]].

Its function field analogue naturally connects to matrices in a parabolic subalgebra; see §
5.1
.

Deformations of the Cohen–Lenstra measure are already interesting without motivations from random matrix models; see
[
DJ14
,
FK19
]
and
[
NVP22
, §7]
for the intrinsic study of some probability measures of this type, and note the ubiquity of Hall–Littlewood polynomials. But furthermore, such deformations also arise as predicted distributions of arithmetic objects: for example
[

]
, if

in (

), then the resulting probability mass function, proportional to

, predicts the distribution of the

{\mathbb{Q}}
(

for imaginary,

for real). We propose the following question to conclude the introduction.

Question 1.5
.

Are there arithmetic settings that naturally produce a flag of finite abelian groups? Is the distribution of its
p
-part predicted by one of the distributions above?

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Dictionary of flags

Given
n,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
and a surjective flag
\mathbf{G}=(G_{k}\twoheadrightarrow\dots\twoheadrightarrow G_{1})\in\mathbf{Fl}_{k}
, a
surjection
from
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
to
\mathbf{G}
simply refers to a surjective
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-linear map
f_{k}:{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}
. Any such surjection
f_{k}
induces a chain of surjections

(2.1)

{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}\overset{f_{k}}{\twoheadrightarrow}G_{k}\twoheadrightarrow\dots\twoheadrightarrow G_{1}\twoheadrightarrow G_{0}=0,

which induces a surjection
f_{i}:{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}\twoheadrightarrow G_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq k
by composition. We can equivalently think of a surjection
f:{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbf{G}
as a collection
(f_{i}:{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}\twoheadrightarrow G_{i})_{i}
, but keeping in mind that
f_{k}
determines the rest.

By a
k
-
injective flag
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
, we mean a tower of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-submodules of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
:

(2.2)

F_{k}\subseteq F_{k-1}\subseteq\dots\subseteq F_{1}\subseteq F_{0}:={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}.

We denote by
\mathcal{F}l_{k}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n})
the set of
k
-injective flags in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between
\mathcal{F}l_{k}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n})
and the set of
k
-surjective flags
\mathbf{G}
together with a surjection
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbf{G}
. To
\mathcal{F}=(F_{k}\subseteq\dots\subseteq F_{0}={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n})
, we associate
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbf{G}=(G_{k}\twoheadrightarrow\dots\twoheadrightarrow G_{0}=0)
, where
G_{i}={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}/F_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq k
and the maps are the natural quotient maps. Conversely, given
f:{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbf{G}
, we recover
\mathcal{F}
by
F_{i}=\ker(f_{i}:{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}\twoheadrightarrow G_{i})
for
0\leq i\leq k
. We introduce the natural notation
\mathbf{G}={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}/\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}=\mathit{ker}(f:{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbf{G})
.

Given matrices
M_{1},\dots,M_{k}\in\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
, we define an injective flag by
F_{i}=\im(M_{1}\dots M_{i})\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
for
1\leq i\leq k
. We denote this flag by
\mathit{im}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})
. We canonically have
\mathbf{cok}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}/\mathit{im}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Fix
\mathbf{G}=(G_{k}\twoheadrightarrow\dots\twoheadrightarrow G_{1})\in\mathbf{Fl}_{k}
with
\lvert G_{k}\rvert<\infty
. Let
r:=r(G_{k})
, and fix
n\geq r
. We separate the major steps of proving Theorem
1.1
into the following lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

The number of
k
-injective flags
\mathcal{F}
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
such that
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}/\mathcal{F}\simeq\mathbf{G}
is

(3.1)

\frac{\lvert G_{k}\rvert^{n}}{\lvert\Aut(\mathbf{G})\rvert}\prod_{i=n-r(G_{k})+1}^{n}(1-p^{-i}).

Proof.

Fix a copy of
\mathbf{G}
, and let
\mathrm{Surj}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n},\mathbf{G})
denote the set of surjections from
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
to
\mathbf{G}
, which is nothing but the set of surjections
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}\twoheadrightarrow G_{k}
. By Nakayama’s lemma,

(3.2)

\lvert\mathrm{Surj}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n},\mathbf{G})\rvert=\lvert G_{k}\rvert^{n}\prod_{i=n-r+1}^{n}(1-p^{-i}).

Let
\Aut(\mathbf{G})
act on
\mathrm{Surj}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n},\mathbf{G})
by composition. As usual, the action is free: if
\sigma=(\sigma_{i})\in\Aut(\mathbf{G})
with
\sigma_{i}\in\Aut(G_{i})
is such that
\sigma_{i}\circ f_{i}=f_{i}
for all
i
, then since
f_{i}
is surjective, we must have
\sigma_{i}=\mathrm{id}
. As a consequence, the orbit space has cardinality given by

(3.3)

\left\lvert\frac{\mathrm{Surj}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n},\mathbf{G})}{\Aut(\mathbf{G})}\right\rvert=\frac{\left\lvert\mathrm{Surj}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n},\mathbf{G})\right\rvert}{\lvert\Aut(\mathbf{G})\rvert}=\frac{\lvert G_{k}\rvert^{n}}{\lvert\Aut(\mathbf{G})\rvert}\prod_{i=n-r+1}^{n}(1-p^{-i}).

Finally, it is easy to verify that the orbit space above is in a canonical bijection with the set of
k
-injective flags
\mathcal{F}
with
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}/\mathcal{F}\simeq\mathbf{G}
. The conclusion then follows.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Fix a
k
-injective flag
\mathcal{F}
such that
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}/\mathcal{F}\simeq\mathbf{G}
. Then if
M_{1},\dots,M_{k}\in\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
are independent and Haar-random, then

(3.4)

\Prob_{M_{1},\dots,M_{k}\in\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})}(\mathit{im}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})=\mathcal{F})=\lvert G_{k}\rvert^{-n}\left\lparen\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-p^{-i})\right\rparen^{k}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}=(F_{k}\subseteq\dots\subseteq F_{1}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n})
. Since
\lvert G_{k}\rvert=\lvert{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}/F_{k}\rvert<\infty
, every
F_{i}
is a free module of rank
n
. We first pick
M_{1}
with the condition that
\im(M_{1})=F_{1}
. This means two things: (1)
M_{1}:{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
has image in
F_{1}
. (2) The induced
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
-linear map
M_{1}:{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}\to F_{1}
is surjective.

The probability that
\im(M_{1})\subseteq F_{1}
is the probability that every column of
M_{1}
lies in
F_{1}
. Hence
\Prob_{M_{1}\in\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})}(\im(M_{1})\subseteq F_{1})
is
\lvert{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}/F_{1}\rvert^{-n}=\lvert G_{1}\rvert^{-n}
. By Nakayama’s lemma, since
F_{1}
is of rank
n
, the probability that a Haar-random linear map
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}\to F_{1}
be surjective is
\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-p^{-i})
. As a result,

(3.5)

\Prob_{M_{1}\in\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})}(\im(M_{1})=F_{1})=\lvert G_{1}\rvert^{-n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-p^{-i}).

Now we fix
M_{1}
and pick
M_{2}
with the condition that
\im(M_{1}M_{2})=F_{2}
. We note that
M_{1}:{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}\to F_{1}
is an isomorphism because it is a surjective map between rank
n
free modules over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. Therefore,
M_{1}
induces an isomorphism of flags from
M_{1}^{-1}(F_{2})\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
to
F_{2}\subseteq F_{1}
. Thus,
\im(M_{1}M_{2})=F_{2}
if and only if
\im(M_{2})=M_{1}^{-1}(F_{2})\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
. By the same argument as above, and the fact that
\lvert{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}/M_{1}^{-1}(F_{2})\rvert=\lvert F_{1}/F_{2}\rvert=\lvert G_{2}\rvert/\lvert G_{1}\rvert
, we get

(3.6)

\Prob_{M_{2}\in\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})}\left(\im(M_{1}M_{2})=F_{2}|\im(M_{1})=F_{1}\right)=\left\lparen\frac{\lvert G_{2}\rvert}{\lvert G_{1}\rvert}\right\rparen^{-n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-p^{-i}).

Repeating the argument and multiplying all of the above probabilities together, we conclude that

(3.7)

\begin{aligned} &\Prob_{M_{1},\dots,M_{k}\in\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})}(\mathit{im}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})=\mathcal{F})\\
&=\lvert G_{1}\rvert^{-n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-p^{-i})\cdot\left\lparen\frac{\lvert G_{2}\rvert}{\lvert G_{1}\rvert}\right\rparen^{-n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-p^{-i})\cdot\dots\cdot\left\lparen\frac{\lvert G_{k}\rvert}{\lvert G_{k-1}\rvert}\right\rparen^{-n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-p^{-i})\\
&=\lvert G_{k}\rvert^{-n}\left\lparen\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-p^{-i})\right\rparen^{k}.\end{aligned}\qed

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Since
\mathbf{cok}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}/\mathit{im}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})
, we have

(3.8)

\Prob_{M_{1},\dots,M_{k}\in\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})}(\mathbf{cok}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})\simeq\mathbf{G})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{F}l_{k}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n})\\
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}/\mathcal{F}\simeq\mathbf{G}\end{subarray}}\Prob_{M_{1},\dots,M_{k}\in\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})}(\mathit{im}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})=\mathcal{F}).

Since the probability in Lemma
3.2
depends only on
\mathbf{G}
but not on
\mathcal{F}
, the proof is complete by multiplying the results of Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We state and prove a convenient lemma first.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
A,B,C
be free modules over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
of finite ranks
a,b,c
, and suppose
f:B\twoheadrightarrow A
is a surjective linear map. Then for a Haar-random linear map
g:C\to B
, we have

(4.1)

\Prob_{g\in\Hom(C,B)}(\im(fg)=A)=\prod_{i=c-a+1}^{c}(1-p^{-i}).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
A={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{a},B={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{b},C={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{c}
and
f:{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{b}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{a}
is the projection to the first
a
coordinates. Write
g\in\Mat_{b\times c}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
as
g=\begin{bmatrix}g_{1}\\
g_{2}\end{bmatrix}
, where
g_{1}\in\Mat_{a\times c}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
and
g_{2}\in\Mat_{(b-a)\times c}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
. Then
fg=g_{1}
. Thus, the probability that
fg
be surjective is the probability that
g_{1}
be surjective, which is
\prod_{i=c-a+1}^{c}(1-p^{-i})
by Nakayama’s lemma.
∎

To prove Theorem
1.2
, we follow the same argument as Theorem
1.1
, except that we need to prove a more general version of Lemma
3.2
. Again, we fix
\mathbf{G}=(G_{k}\twoheadrightarrow\dots\twoheadrightarrow G_{1})\in\mathbf{Fl}_{k}
, but we do not assume
\lvert G_{k}\rvert<\infty
. Fix
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{F}l_{k}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n})
such that
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}/\mathcal{F}\simeq\mathbf{G}
. For
1\leq i\leq k
, let
M_{i}\in\Mat_{(n+u_{i-1})\times(n+u_{i})}
, where
u_{0}:=0
, and assume
M_{1},\dots,M_{k}
are independent and Haar-random.

Lemma 4.2
.

In the setting above, we have

(4.2)

\Prob_{M_{1},\dots,M_{k}\in\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})}(\mathit{im}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})=\mathcal{F})=\lvert G_{k}\rvert^{-n}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left\lparen\frac{\lvert G_{j}\rvert}{\lvert G_{j-1}\rvert}\right\rparen^{-u_{j}}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-p^{-i-u_{j}})

if
\lvert G_{k}\rvert<\infty
, and zero otherwise.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}=(F_{k}\subseteq\dots\subseteq F_{1}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n})
. We first pick
M_{1}\in\Mat_{n\times(n+u_{1})}
with
\im(M_{1})=F_{1}
. If
\lvert G_{1}\rvert=\infty
, then
\lvert{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}/F_{1}\rvert=\infty
, so the probability that each column of
M_{1}
be in
F_{1}
is zero. Therefore, we may assume
\lvert G_{1}\rvert<\infty
from now on. As a result,
F_{1}
is free of rank
n
. By the similar argument in the proof of Lemma
3.2
, we get

(4.3)

\Prob_{M_{1}\in\Mat_{n\times(n+u_{1})}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})}(\im(M_{1})=F_{1})=\lvert G_{1}\rvert^{-(n+u_{1})}\prod_{i=u_{1}+1}^{n+u_{1}}(1-p^{-i})=\lvert G_{1}\rvert^{-(n+u_{1})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-p^{-i-u_{1}}).

Now we fix
M_{1}
and pick
M_{2}\in\Mat_{(n+u_{1})\times(n+u_{2})}
with the condition that
\im(M_{1}M_{2})=F_{2}
, which is equivalent to
\im(M_{2})\subseteq M_{1}^{-1}(F_{2})
. The third isomorphism theorem applied to the surjection
M_{1}:{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n+u_{1}}\to F_{1}
gives an isomorphism
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n+u_{1}}/M_{1}^{-1}(F_{2})\simeq F_{1}/F_{2}
, so
\lvert{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n+u_{1}}/M_{1}^{-1}(F_{2})\rvert=\lvert F_{1}/F_{2}\rvert=\lvert G_{2}\rvert/\lvert G_{1}\rvert
. Thus,

(4.4)

\Prob_{M_{2}\in\Mat_{(n+u_{1})\times(n+u_{2})}}(\im(M_{1}M_{2})\subseteq F_{2}|\im(M_{1})=F_{1})=\left\lparen\frac{\lvert G_{2}\rvert}{\lvert G_{1}\rvert}\right\rparen^{-(n+u_{2})}

if
\lvert G_{2}\rvert<\infty
, and zero otherwise. So again, we may assume
\lvert G_{2}\rvert<\infty
from now on.

We now find the probability that
\im(M_{1}M_{2})=F_{2}
conditioned on
\im(M_{1}M_{2})\subseteq F_{2}
, so
M_{2}
is a Haar-random linear map in
\Hom({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n+u_{2}},M_{1}^{-1}(F_{2}))
. Note that we are in the setting of Lemma
4.1
with
A=F_{2}
,
B=M_{1}^{-1}(F_{2})
,
C={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n+u_{2}}
,
f=M_{1}:M_{1}^{-1}(F_{2})\twoheadrightarrow F_{2}
, and
g=M_{2}:{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n+u_{2}}\to M_{1}^{-1}(F_{2})
. The condition that
\im(M_{1}M_{2})=F_{2}
is equivalent to
\im(fg)=A
. Since
\lvert{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}/F_{2}\rvert=\lvert G_{2}\rvert<\infty
,
A
is free of rank
n
. Since
\lvert{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n+u_{1}}/B\rvert=\lvert G_{2}\rvert/\lvert G_{1}\rvert<\infty
,
B
is free of rank
n+u_{1}
. By Lemma
4.1
with
a=n
and
c=n+u_{2}
, we get

(4.5)

\Prob_{M_{2}\in\Mat_{(n+u_{1})\times(n+u_{2})}}(\im(M_{1}M_{2})=F_{2}|\im(M_{1})=F_{1},\im(M_{1}M_{2})\subseteq F_{2})=\prod_{i=u_{2}+1}^{n+u_{2}}(1-p^{-i}).

Combined with (
4.4
), we get

(4.6)

\Prob_{M_{2}\in\Mat_{(n+u_{1})\times(n+u_{2})}}(\im(M_{1}M_{2})=F_{2}|\im(M_{1})=F_{1})=\left\lparen\frac{\lvert G_{2}\rvert}{\lvert G_{1}\rvert}\right\rparen^{-(n+u_{2})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-p^{-i-u_{2}}).

Repeating the argument inductively and multiplying the probabilities in (
4.3
), (
4.6
), and so on, the desired formula (
4.2
) follows, along with the fact that each
G_{i}
must be finite in order for the probability to be nonzero.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Multiply the results of Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
4.2
.
∎

5.
Probability measures on flags

Here we prove Corollary
1.4
as an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

It is clear that
P_{n,(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})}
is a nonnegative measure, so it suffices to show that
\sum_{\mathbf{G}\in\mathbf{Fl}_{k}}P_{n,(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})}(\mathbf{G})=1
. We note that the measure in (
1.4
) is precisely
P_{n,(p^{-u_{1}},\dots,p^{-u_{k}})}
. In particular, Theorem
1.2
implies that
P_{n,(p^{-u_{1}},\dots,p^{-u_{k}})}
is a probability measure for every
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, so the formal power series
\sum_{\mathbf{G}\in\mathbf{Fl}_{k}}P_{n,(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})}(\mathbf{G})
in
t_{1},\dots,t_{k}
must be 1, completing the proof.

The case of
P_{\infty,(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})}
is similar.
∎

Write
\mathbf{G}=(G_{k}\twoheadrightarrow\dots\twoheadrightarrow G_{1})
. We compute the joint distribution of
(G_{1},\dots,G_{k})
if
\mathbf{G}
is distributed according to (
1.7
). For partitions
\lambda^{(1)},\dots,\lambda^{(k)}
, we write
\mathbf{G}\sim(\lambda^{(1)},\dots,\lambda^{(k)})
if the type of
G_{i}
is
\lambda^{(i)}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Fixing partitions
\lambda^{(1)},\dots,\lambda^{(k)}
. For
P_{n,(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})}(\mathbf{G})
as in (
1.7
), the quantity

(5.1)

\sum_{\mathbf{G}\sim(\lambda^{(1)},\dots,\lambda^{(k)})}P_{n,(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})}(\mathbf{G})

is given by
[
VP21
, p. 19, Prop. 2.6]
with
q=0
,
a^{(i)}_{j}=t_{i}p^{-j}
,
\mathbf{b}=(1,p^{-1},\dots,p^{-(n-1)})
, and
t=1/p
in their notation.

Proof.

If
t_{i}=1
, by Theorem
1.1
, the quantity in question is the probability that
\mathrm{cok}(M_{1}\dots M_{i})
is of type
\lambda^{(i)}
for all
i
, where
M_{1},\dots,M_{k}\in\Mat_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
are independent and Haar-random. This is given by
[
VP21
, p. 27, Cor. 3.4]
with
N_{i}=\infty
in their notation.

To go from the
t_{i}=1
case to the general case, we notice that if we fix
\varnothing=\lambda^{(0)},\lambda^{(1)},\dots,\lambda^{(k)}
and letting
n_{i}=\lvert\lambda^{(i)}\rvert-\lvert\lambda^{(i-1)}\rvert
for
1\leq i\leq k
, then from (
1.7
), we have

(5.2)

\frac{\sum_{\mathbf{G}\sim(\lambda^{(1)},\dots,\lambda^{(k)})}P_{n,(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})}(\mathbf{G})}{\sum_{\mathbf{G}\sim(\lambda^{(1)},\dots,\lambda^{(k)})}P_{n,(1,\dots,1)}(\mathbf{G})}=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left\lparen t_{j}^{n_{j}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1-p^{-i}t_{j}}{1-p^{-i}}\right\rparen.

Combining this with the
t_{i}=1
case above finally gives the desired formula.
∎

5.1.
Relation to matrices over finite fields

We give a direct proof of Proposition
5.2
, a function field analogue of (
1.9
), by establishing (
5.6
) that connects it to counting matrices over finite fields. Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
be the finite field with
q
elements, and let
R
denote the power series ring
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[T]]
. We can similarly define
\mathbf{Fl}_{k}(R)
to be the set of
k
-surjective flags of
R
-modules. Given
1\leq i\leq k
and any flag
\mathbf{G}=(G_{k}\twoheadrightarrow\dots\twoheadrightarrow G_{0}=0)\in\mathbf{Fl}_{k}(R)
such that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}G_{k}<\infty
, let
n_{i}(\mathbf{G}):=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}G_{i}-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}G_{i-1}
. Define the
flag Cohen–Lenstra series
of
R
as

(5.3)

\widehat{Z}_{R}(t_{1},\dots,t_{k}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{G}\in\mathbf{Fl}_{k}(R)\\
\dim G_{k}<\infty\end{subarray}}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k}t_{j}^{n_{j}(\mathbf{G})}}{\lvert\Aut(\mathbf{G})\rvert}\in{\mathbb{Q}}[[t_{1},\dots,t_{k}]].

When
k=1
, this construction is precisely the Cohen–Lenstra series defined in
[
Hua23
]
.

Proposition 5.2
.

We have

(5.4)

\widehat{Z}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[T]]}(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{-i}t_{j}}.

Proof.

To specify a
k
-surjective flag
\mathbf{G}
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[T]]
-modules with given
n_{i}(\mathbf{G})=n_{i}
, it suffices to specify the
k
-surjective flag of underlying
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-vector spaces

(5.5)

V_{k}\overset{\phi_{k-1}}{\twoheadrightarrow}\dots\overset{\phi_{1}}{\twoheadrightarrow}V_{1}\twoheadrightarrow V_{0}=0,

together with
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[T]]
-module structures on
V_{i}
that are compatible with the flag. Up to isomorphism of flags of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-vector spaces, we may assume
V_{i}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{1}+\dots+n_{i}}
, and the surjection
\phi_{i}
is the projection to the first
n_{1}+\dots+n_{i}
coordinates. Then the compatible
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[T]]
-module structures on
V_{i}
are determined by a nilpotent endomorphism
M
on
V_{k}
(as the multiplication by
T
map), such that
M
factors through an endomorphism on
V_{i}
for each
i
. If
W_{i}:=\ker(V_{k}\twoheadrightarrow V_{i})
for
0\leq i\leq k
, then this simply means
MW_{i}\subseteq W_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq k
. Since
W_{i}
is the span of the last
n_{i+1}+\dots+n_{k}
basis vectors in
V_{k}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n_{1}+\dots+n_{k}}
, this happens if and only if
M
is
block-lower-triangular
with respect to the block sizes
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}
in both rows and columns. We denote the set of such block-lower-triangular matrices by
\Mat_{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
.

Let
\GL_{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
,
\mathrm{Nilp}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
be the set of invertible
resp.
nilpotent matrices in
\Mat_{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
. By the discussion above,
k
-surjective flags of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[T]]
-modules with a given dimension vector
(n_{i})
are parametrized by
\mathrm{Nilp}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
. The group
\GL_{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
acts on
\mathrm{Nilp}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
by conjugation. The orbits correspond to isomorphism classes of flags of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[T]]
-modules, and the stabilizers correspond to automorphisms of flags of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[T]]
-modules. By a standard argument involving the orbit-stabilizer theorem, we get

(5.6)

\widehat{Z}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[T]]}(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})=\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\geq 0}\frac{\lvert\mathrm{Nilp}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})\rvert}{\lvert\GL_{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})\rvert}t_{1}^{n_{1}}\dots t_{k}^{n_{k}}.

Since a matrix in
\Mat_{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
is invertible
resp.
nilpotent if and only if every diagonal block is invertible
resp.
nilpotent, the generating series simplifies to

(5.7)

\widehat{Z}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[[T]]}(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{n_{j}\geq 0}\frac{\lvert\mathrm{Nilp}_{n_{j}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})\rvert}{\lvert\GL_{n_{j}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})\rvert}t_{j}^{n_{j}}.

But it is well-known that the sum equals to
\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{-i}t_{j}}
; for instance, we may use the theorem
\lvert\mathrm{Nilp}_{n}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})\rvert=q^{n^{2}-n}
of Fine and Herstein
[
FH58
]
, and then apply an identity of Euler
[
And98
, Eq. (2.2.5)]
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark
.

Of course,
\Mat_{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
and
\GL_{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
are nothing but the parabolic subalgebra and the parabolic subgroup of a suitable partial flag variety of type A. As is made apparent by Equation (
5.6
), the construction (
5.3
) fits naturally into the study of commuting varieties of parabolic subalgebras; we refer the readers to
[
BE16
]
for a geometric aspect of such research.

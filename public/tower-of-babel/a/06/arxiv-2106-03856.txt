High Order Impedance Boundary Condition for the Three-dimensional Scattering Problem in Electromagnetism
OUESLATI Soumaya, DAVEAU Christian, AUBAKIROV Abil

Abstract
In this paper, we propose a variational formulation with the use of high order impedance boundary condition (HOIBC) to solve the scattering problem. We study the existence and uniqueness of the solution. Then, a discretization of this formulation is done with Rao-Wilton-Glisson (RWG). We give validations of the HOIBC obtained with a 3D MoM code that show the improvement in accuracy over the standard impedance boundary condition (SIBC) computations.



I
Introduction

We consider the scattering problem of electromagnetic waves
(\textbf{E},\textbf{H})
by a perfect conducting body with a complex coating. Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain with a Lipschitz-continuous boundary
\Gamma
. Let
n
be the unit normal vector to
\Gamma
directed to the exterior of
\Omega
. We define
\Omega_{+}
as the space of radiating electric fields
E
solutions of Maxwell equations in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\overline{\Omega_{-}}
which have a tangential trace on
\Gamma
.

Waves propagate with constant wave number
k
in the exterior unbounded domain
\Omega
. The electric field
E
satisfies the harmonic Maxwell equation

\nabla\times(\nabla\times\textbf{E})-k^{2}\textbf{E}=0

While the related magnetic field is given by

\textbf{H}=\frac{1}{ik}\nabla\times\textbf{E}

An electric field is said to be radiating if it satisfies the
Silver-Müller radiation condition:

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}r(\textbf{E}\times\textbf{n}_{r}+\textbf{H})=0,

where
r=|\mathbf{x}|
and
\mathbf{n}_{r}=\dfrac{\mathbf{x}}{|\mathbf{x}|}
,
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

The coating is modeled by the following impedance boundary condition

\textbf{E}_{t}-Z(n\times\textbf{H})=0\,\,\,\,\,on\,\,\,\,\Gamma.

(1)

Here,
Z
is the impedance operator that depends on incident angle, subscript tg denotes tangent component on the surface defined as:

\textbf{E}_{t}=n\times(\textbf{E}\times n).

Generally, we take the constant impedance operator, known as standard or Leontovitch impedance boundary conditions
[
1
,
Senior_1995
,
6
]
.
Our problem writes as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}\nabla\times(\nabla\times\textbf{E})-k^{2}\textbf{E}=0\;\Omega_{+},\\
\nabla\times(\nabla\times\textbf{H})-k^{2}\textbf{H}=0\;\Omega_{+},\\
\textbf{E}_{t}-Z(n\times\textbf{H})=0\,\,\,\,\,on\,\,\,\,\Gamma.\\
\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}r(\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{n}_{r}+\mathbf{H})=0.\end{array}\right.

(2)

This paper deals with the implementation of high order impedance boundary condition coupled by an integral equation.

II
high order impedance boundary condition

In this section, we propose a new impedance boundary condition with integral operators. In
[
1
]
the authors approximated impedance condition as a ratio of polynomials of second order
of the sine of the incidence angle in spectral domain. They obtained for the case of a rotational invariant coating a high order impedance boundary condition in spatial domain equations.
We transformed these equations with integral operators to get:

(I+b_{1}L_{D}-b_{2}L_{R})\textbf{E}_{t}=(a_{0}I+a_{1}L_{D}-a_{2}L_{R})(\mathbf{n}\times\textbf{H}).

(3)

This approximation contains the operators
L_{D}
and
L_{R}
are defined: for all vector function
\mathbf{A}
sufficiently smooth, such that
\mathbf{A}.\mathbf{n}=0

L_{D}(\mathbf{A})=\nabla_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{div}_{\Gamma}\mathbf{A}),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,L_{R}(\mathbf{A})=\mathbf{\mathrm{rot}}_{\Gamma}(\mathrm{rot}_{\Gamma}\mathbf{A}).

In
[
4
]
, different methods to calculate these coefficients
(a_{0},a_{j},b_{j})
for
3
are presented. In the following, we want to establish a new variational formulation, applying a boundary integral method for the problem in 3D case with HOIBC.

III
Variational formulation with HOIBC

We introduce current densities
J

M

\Gamma
as follows:

\mathbf{M}=\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{n}\ \ ,\ \mathbf{J}=\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{H}.

We finally obtain the variational formulation

Problem 1

Find
U=(\mathbf{J},\mathbf{M},\tilde{\mathbf{J}},\tilde{\mathbf{M}})\in V=[H^{-1/2}(div,\Gamma)\cap L^{2}(\Gamma)]^{4}
and
\lambda=(\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{J},\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{M})\in[H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)]^{2}

\begin{cases}A(U,\Psi)+B^{T}(\lambda,\Psi)=F(\Psi)\\
B(U,\lambda^{\prime})=0\end{cases}

(4)

for all

and
\lambda^{\prime}=(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}_{J},\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}_{M})\in W=[H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)]^{2}

The bilinear forms are defined by:

B(U,\lambda^{\prime})=\int_{\Gamma}\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}_{J}\cdot(\tilde{\mathbf{J}}-\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{J})ds+\int_{\Gamma}\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}_{M}\cdot(\tilde{\mathbf{M}}-\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{M})ds

and

(5)

+\frac{i}{Z_{0}}\iint_{\Gamma}kG\ (\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{M}\cdot\textbf{M})-\frac{1}{k}G\ \mathrm{div}{\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{M}}\ \mathrm{div}{\textbf{M}}dsds^{\prime}

+\iint_{\Gamma}\nabla^{\prime}G\cdot(\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{J}\times\textbf{M})dsds^{\prime}-i\iint_{\Gamma}\nabla^{\prime}G\cdot(\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{M}\times\textbf{J})dsds^{\prime}

+\frac{a_{0}}{2}\int_{\Gamma}\textbf{J}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{J}ds+\frac{1}{2a_{0}}\int_{\Gamma}\textbf{M}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{M}ds

\int_{\Gamma}\mathbf{g}_{n}(s)\cdot(\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{J})ds=\int_{T^{+}_{n}\cup T^{-}_{n}}\mathbf{g}_{n}(s)\cdot(\mathbf{n}\times\mathbf{J})ds,

Parallel BEM with H-matrices using MPI and OpenMP

Because a dense coefficient HOIBC matrix described in the previous section, the total computational cost and the memory footprint of the method are very high. To remedy these problems,
we developed a software framework that is based in parallel Hierarchical Matrices (H-matrices) with ACA (Adaptive Cross Approximation)

H-matrix computing can be conveniently coupled with parallel computing technologies to distribute the memory load, and to accelerate not only the matrix filling but also the classical matrix/vector product in order to obtain fast iterative solvers.
In next section, We present numerical results which show the effectiveness of both the parallelization and the H-matrix method.

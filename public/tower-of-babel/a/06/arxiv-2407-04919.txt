Abstract
. We construct an Anick type wild automorphism δ \delta in a 3-generated free Poisson algebra which induces a tame automorphism in a 3-generated polynomial algebra. We also show that δ \delta is stably tame. Dedicated to the memory of professor V.A.Roman’kov

1.
Introduction

We recall that a
Poisson algebra
is a vector space
\mathcal{P}
over a field
k
endowed with two bilinear operations: a multiplication denoted by
x\otimes y\mapsto x\cdot y
and a Poisson bracket denoted by
x\otimes y\mapsto[x,y]
, such that
(\mathcal{P},\cdot)
is a commutative associative algebra,
(\mathcal{P},[-,-])
is a Lie algebra, and
\mathcal{P}
satisfies the
Leibniz identity

[x,y\cdot z]=y\cdot[x,z]+z\cdot[x,y].

A Poisson algebra
\mathcal{P}
is called
unital
if
(\mathcal{P},\cdot)
is unital. In this article, we always assume that the Poisson algebra under consideration is unital.
And we usually omit the operation
\cdot
when no confusion arises.

It is clear that polynomial algebras are Poisson algebras with trivial Poisson brackets. On the other hand, assume that
\mathcal{L}
is the free Lie algebra generated by an arbitrary set
X
and assume that
Y
is a linear basis of
\mathcal{L}
. Then the Poisson symmetric algebra of
\mathcal{L}
, namely, the polynomial algebra with free generating set
Y
, is the free Poisson algebra generated by
X

[
11
]
. Since research on automorphisms of polynomial algebras is an interesting subject, it is natural to study automorphisms of free Poisson algebras.

Let us now review some essential terminology. Let
A_{n}
be an arbitrary free algebra with free generating set
X:=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
and denote by
\mathsf{Aut}(A_{n})
the automorphism group of
A_{n}
. We denote an automorphism of the form

\sigma(i,\alpha,f):=(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1},\alpha x_{i}+f,x_{i+1},\dots,x_{n}),

where
1\leq i\leq n
,
0\neq\alpha\in k
, and
f
lies in the subalgebra of
A_{n}
generated by
X\setminus\{x_{i}\}
,
to be an
elementary automorphism
of
A_{n}
.
The subgroup
\mathsf{TA}(A_{n})
of
\mathsf{Aut}(A_{n})
generated by all elementary automorphisms is known as the
tame automorphism group
of
A_{n}
. We refer to the
elements of
\mathsf{TA}(A_{n})
as
tame automorphisms
and those in
\mathsf{Aut}(A_{n})\setminus\mathsf{TA}(A_{n})
as
wild automorphisms
. We denote by

\varphi=(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})

the endomorphism
\varphi
of
A_{n}
defined by the rule
\varphi(x_{i})=f_{i}\in A_{n}
for all
i\leq n
.

Known results have demonstrated close connections between the automorphisms of polynomial algebras, free associative algebras, and free Poisson algebras.
For instance, automorphisms of polynomial algebras (free associative algebras and free Poisson algebras, respectively) in two variables are tame
[
4
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
, where the characteristic of the underlying fields is assumed to be 0 for Poisson algebras. Moreover, the automorphism groups of the aforementioned two-generated free algebras are isomorphic.

On the other hand, automorphisms of a polynomial algebra and of a free associative algebras in three variables can be wild, and the most famous example are the Nagata automorphism
[
10
,
12
,
13
]
and the Anick automorphism
[
16
]
. Since every automorphism of an
n
-generated polynomial algebra can be lifted to an automorphism of an
n
-generated free Poisson algebra, it follows that
3
-generated free Poisson algebras have wild automorphisms as well. However, to our knowledge, it remains an open question whether every wild automorphism of an
n
-generated free Poisson algebra induces a wild automorphism of an
n
-generated polynomial algebra under the projection sending Poisson brackets to zero. In this article we construct an Anick type wild automorphism

(1.1)

\delta:=(x_{1}+[x_{3},x_{1}x_{3}-[x_{3},x_{2}]],x_{2}+(x_{1}x_{3}-[x_{3},x_{2}])x_{3},x_{3})

of a 3-generated free Poisson algebra, which induces a tame automorphism of a polynomial algebra in 3 variables. Since
\delta(x_{1}x_{3}-[x_{3},x_{2}])=x_{1}x_{3}-[x_{3},x_{2}]
, we deduce that
\delta
is an epimorphism. By a similar proof as that in
[
3
, p. 343, Proposition 8.1]
, we conclude that
\delta
is an automorphism.

The article is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we explore the connections between the group of tame automorphisms of a free Poisson algebras and that of a polynomial algebra. In Section
3
, we define Jacobian matrices for endomorphisms of free Poisson algebras and prove the chain rule. Although the chain rule holds in general
[
1
]
, for the sake of completeness and for the readers’ convenience, we still include a proof. In Section
4
, we establish our main result: the automorphism
\delta
in (
1.1
) is wild and stably tame.

Our approach is primarily inspired by
[
16
]
, with modifications to make the proofs simplified.

2.
Tame automorphisms of free Poisson algebras

Our aim in this section is to investigate the connections between tame automorphisms of free Poisson algebras and those of polynomial algebras. Denote by
P_{n}
the free Poisson algebra with free generating set
X:=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
. Let
C_{n}
be the polynomial algebra with variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
, and we always suppose that
C_{n}
is endowed with the trivial Poisson brackets. Then there exists a Poisson algebra homomorphism

\pi_{n}:P_{n}\longrightarrow C_{n},\quad x_{i}\mapsto x_{i},\mbox{ for all }1\leq i\leq n.

The kernel
\mathsf{Ker}(\pi_{n})
of
\pi_{n}
is the ideal of
P_{n}
generated by
\{[x_{i},x_{j}]\mid x_{i},x_{j}\in X,i>j\}
. Finally, for every endomorphism
\varphi=(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
of
P_{n}
, we denote by
\overline{\varphi}=(\pi_{n}(f_{1}),\dots,\pi_{n}(f_{n}))
the induced endomorphism of
\varphi
in
C_{n}
. If
\varphi
is an automorphism, then so is
\overline{\varphi}
.
Obviously, every elementary automorphism
\varphi
of the form

(2.1)

\sigma(i,1,f),f\in\mathsf{Ker}(\pi_{n}),

satisfies that
\overline{\varphi}=\mathsf{id}
, the identity map of the algebra
C_{n}
.

For
p\neq q
, we denote by
\sigma_{pq}
the automorphism of
A_{n}
that interchanges
x_{p}
and
x_{q}
, while the remaining generators are fixed. Then
\sigma_{pq}
is tame and we have

\sigma_{pq}=\sigma(q,-1,x_{p})\sigma(p,1,-x_{q})\sigma(q,1,x_{p}).

It is straightforward to see that the following relations
[
15
,
16
]
between elementary automorphisms hold for
A_{n}
:

(2.2)

\sigma(i,\alpha,f)\sigma(i,\beta,g)=\sigma(i,\alpha\beta,\beta f+g);

(2.3)

\sigma(i,\alpha,f)^{-1}\sigma(j,\beta,g)\sigma(i,\alpha,f)=\sigma(j,\beta,\sigma(i,\alpha,f)^{-1}(g)),

where
i\neq j
and
f
lies in the subalgebra of
A_{n}
generated by
X\setminus\{x_{i},x_{j}\}
;

(2.4)

\sigma_{pq}\sigma(i,\alpha,f)\sigma_{pq}=\sigma(j,\alpha,\sigma_{pq}(f)),

where
1\leq p\neq q\leq n
and
x_{j}=\sigma_{pq}(x_{i})
.

For
j>1
, by straightforward calculations or by
[
16
]
, we know that
\sigma_{1j}
satisfies the property

(2.5)

\sigma_{1j}=\sigma(1,-1,0)\sigma(1,1,x_{j})\sigma(j,1,-x_{1})\sigma(1,1,x_{j}).

And thus, by (
2.4
), we obtain

(2.6)

\sigma(j,\alpha,g)=\sigma_{1j}\sigma(1,\alpha,\sigma_{1j}(g))\sigma_{1j}.

Note that (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) are general facts involving only elementary automorphisms and do not depend on the underlying algebra.

In general, the relations (
2.2
)-(
2.4
) do not form a complete set of defining relations of
\mathsf{TA}(A_{n})
, but they do if
A_{n}=C_{3}
is the polynomial algebra with variables
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
over a field of characteristic 0
[
15
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Pi
be the group homomorphism as follows:

\Pi:\mathsf{TA}(P_{n})\longrightarrow\mathsf{TA}(C_{n}),\ \varphi\mapsto\overline{\varphi}.

Then the kernel of
\Pi
, as a normal subgroup of
\mathsf{TA}(P_{n})
, is generated by all elementary automorphisms of the form given in (
2.1
).

Proof.

Let
N
be the normal subgroup of
\mathsf{TA}(P_{n})
generated by all elementary automorphisms of the form given in (
2.1
). Clearly we have
N\subseteq\mathsf{Ker}(\Pi)
, so we obtain a well-defined group homomorphism

\Pi_{1}:\mathsf{TA}(P_{n})/N\longrightarrow\mathsf{TA}(C_{n}),\ \varphi N\mapsto\overline{\varphi}.

It suffices to show that
\Pi_{1}
is an isomorphism.

For every element
f\in P_{n}
, we have
f=f_{0}+f_{1}
, where
f_{0}
does not involve Poisson brackets and
f_{1}
lies in
\mathsf{Ker}(\pi_{n})
.
Moreover, for
\sigma(i,\alpha,f)\in\mathsf{TA}(P_{n})
, by (
2.2
), we have

\sigma(i,\alpha,f)=\sigma(i,\alpha,f_{0})\sigma(i,1,f_{1}).

So we deduce
\sigma(i,\alpha,f)N=\sigma(i,\alpha,f_{0})N
and

(2.7)

\Pi_{1}(\sigma(i,\alpha,f)N)=\Pi_{1}(\sigma(i,\alpha,f_{0})N)=\overline{\sigma(i,\alpha,f_{0})}=\sigma(i,\alpha,f_{0})\in\mathsf{TA}(C_{n}).

On the other hand, let
\varphi=(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})
be a (tame) automorhphism of
C_{n}
. Considering
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}
as elements in
P_{n}
, we know that
\varphi_{{}_{P}}:=(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})\in\mathsf{Aut}(P_{n})
. Define
a map

\Omega:\mathsf{TA}(C_{n})\longrightarrow\mathsf{TA}(P_{n})/N,\ \varphi\mapsto\varphi_{{}_{P}}N.

Clearly,
\Omega
is a group homomorphism and
we have

(2.8)

\Omega(\sigma(i,\alpha,f))=\sigma(i,\alpha,f)N.

Since elementary automorphisms generate the tame automorphism groups, by (
2.7
) and (
2.8
), we have

\Omega\Pi_{1}=\mathsf{Id}\mbox{ and }\Pi_{1}\Omega=\mathsf{Id^{\prime}},

where
\mathsf{Id}
is the identity map of
\mathsf{TA}(P_{n})/N
and
\mathsf{Id^{\prime}}
is the identity map of
\mathsf{TA}(C_{n})
. The result follows immediately.
∎

3.
Jacobian matrices and the chain rule

In this section, we first recall from
[
17
]
the universal (multiplicative) enveloping algebra
\mathcal{P}^{e}
of an arbitrary Poisson algebra
\mathcal{P}
in the variety of all Poisson algebras. Then we define the Jacobian matrices for endomorphisms of
P_{n}
. Finally, we prove that the chain rule holds.

Let
M_{\mathcal{P}}=\{M_{a}\mid a\in\mathcal{P}\}
and let
H_{\mathcal{P}}=\{H_{a}\mid a\in\mathcal{P}\}
be two copies of
\mathcal{P}
such that
M:\mathcal{P}\longrightarrow M_{\mathcal{P}}
sending
a
to
M_{a}
and
H:P\longrightarrow H_{\mathcal{P}}
sending
a
to
H_{a}
are linear isomorphisms. Then
\mathcal{P}^{e}
is the associative algebra generated by the spaces
M_{\mathcal{P}}
and
H_{\mathcal{P}}
, with defining relations

(3.1)

M_{a}M_{b}=M_{ab},

(3.2)

H_{[a,b]}=H_{a}H_{b}-H_{b}H_{a},

(3.3)

H_{ab}=M_{b}H_{a}+M_{a}H_{b},

(3.4)

H_{a}M_{b}=M_{[a,b]}+M_{b}H_{a},

(3.5)

M_{1}=1

for all
a,b\in\mathcal{P}
. It follows from (
3.3
) and (
3.5
) that
H_{1}=0
.
Moreover, every (unital)
\mathcal{P}
-module
\mathcal{V}
is a left
\mathcal{P}^{e}
-module with the actions

M_{a}(v)=a\cdot v,\mbox{ and }H_{a}(v)=[a,v]

for all
a\in\mathcal{P}
and
v\in\mathcal{V}
. Assume that
\mathcal{P}
is generated by
X
. Rewriting all the terms on the left-hand side of the above equations in (
3.1
)-(
3.4
), we observe that elements of the form
M_{a}H_{x_{i_{1}}}...H_{x_{i_{t}}}
with
t\geq 0
forms a linear generating set of
\mathcal{P}^{e}
.
Define

P_{n}^{e}=(P_{n})^{e}\mbox{ and }C_{n}^{e}=(C_{n})^{e}.

Then we have

Lemma 3.1
.

[
17
, Corollary 3]

Every nonzero element in
P_{n}^{e}
can be uniquely written in the form

\sum_{1\leq i\leq q}M_{a_{i}}w_{i},

where each
a_{i}
lies in
P_{n}
, each
w_{i}
is of the form
H_{x_{i_{1}}}...H_{x_{i_{t}}}
, where
t
depends on
i
, and each
w_{i}
is distinct from the others.

Since
C_{n}
is a Poisson algebra with trivial Poisson brackets, by Equations (
3.1
), (
3.2
) and (
3.4
), we deduce that
C_{n}^{e}
is a commutative algebra. Combining this with Lemma
3.1
, we obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 3.2
.

C_{1}^{e}=P_{1}^{e}
is the polynomial algebra with variables
M_{x_{1}}
and
H_{x_{1}}
.

We further observe that homomorphisms of Poisson algebras induce homomorphisms of their corresponding universal enveloping algebras. Let
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
be two Poisson algebras, and let
\varphi
be a Poisson algebra homomorphism from
\mathcal{P}_{1}
to
\mathcal{P}_{2}
. Since
(\mathcal{P}_{1})^{e}
and
(\mathcal{P}_{2})^{e}
are both defined by relations of the form (
3.1
)-(
3.5
), we can construct an associative algebra homomorphism

\varphi^{e}:(\mathcal{P}_{1})^{e}\longrightarrow(\mathcal{P}_{2})^{e},\ M_{a}\mapsto M_{\varphi(a)},\ H_{a}\mapsto H_{\varphi(a)}

for all
a\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
. In particular, we obtain an algebra homorphism

\pi_{n}^{e}:P_{n}^{e}\longrightarrow C_{n}^{e}.

Now we recall that the Fox derivatives
\frac{\partial(-)}{\partial x_{i}}
is a linear map from
P_{n}
to
P_{n}^{e}
defined by the rule:

\frac{\partial x_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}=\delta_{i,j},

\frac{\partial(ab)}{\partial x_{i}}=M_{a}\frac{\partial b}{\partial x_{i}}+M_{b}\frac{\partial a}{\partial x_{i}},

\frac{\partial[a,b]}{\partial x_{i}}=H_{a}\frac{\partial b}{\partial x_{i}}-H_{b}\frac{\partial a}{\partial x_{i}},

for all
a,b\in P_{n}
, where
\delta_{i,i}=1
and
\delta_{i,j}=0
if
i\neq j
. It is straightforward to show that
\frac{\partial(-)}{\partial x_{i}}
is well-defined.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a Poisson algebra. For all
a_{1},\dots,a_{t}\in\mathcal{P}
, define

[a_{1},\dots,a_{t}]=[\dots[[a_{1},a_{2}],a_{3}],\dots,a_{t}],

where
[a_{1},\dots,a_{t}]
means
a_{1}
if
t=1
. Then we have the following formula for the Fox derivative acting on elements involving Poisson brackets.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a Poisson algebra. Then for all
a_{1},\dots a_{t}\in\mathcal{P}
with
t\geq 2
, we have

\frac{\partial{[a_{1},\dots,a_{t}]}}{\partial x_{r}}=\sum_{1\leq j\leq t}(-H_{a_{t}})\dots(-H_{a_{j+1}})H_{[a_{1},\dots,a_{j-1}]}\frac{\partial a_{j}}{\partial x_{r}}.

Proof.

We use induction on
t
. For
t=2
, we have

\frac{\partial{[a_{1},a_{2}]}}{\partial x_{r}}=(-H_{a_{2}})\frac{\partial a_{1}}{\partial x_{r}}+H_{a_{1}}\frac{\partial a_{2}}{\partial x_{r}}.

Now we assume that
t>2
. We define
b_{1}=[a_{1},a_{2}]
, and
b_{j}=a_{j+1}
for all
2\leq j\leq t-1
. By induction hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{[a_{1},\dots,a_{t}]}}{\partial x_{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq p\leq t-1}(-H_{b_{t-1}})\dots(-H_{b_{p+1}})H_{[b_{1},\dots,b_{p-1}]}\frac{\partial b_{p}}{\partial x_{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{2\leq p\leq t-1}(-H_{a_{t}})\dots(-H_{a_{p+2}})H_{[a_{1},\dots,a_{p}]}\frac{\partial a_{p+1}}{\partial x_{r}}+(-H_{a_{t}})\dots(-H_{a_{3}})\frac{\partial[a_{1},a_{2}]}{\partial x_{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{3\leq j\leq t}(-H_{a_{t}})\dots(-H_{a_{j+1}})H_{[a_{1},\dots,a_{j-1}]}\frac{\partial a_{j}}{\partial x_{r}}+(-H_{a_{t}})\dots(-H_{a_{3}})((-H_{a_{2}})\frac{\partial a_{1}}{\partial x_{r}}+H_{a_{1}}\frac{\partial a_{2}}{\partial x_{r}}).

The proof is completed.
∎

Now we define the Jacobian matrices for endomorphisms of
P_{n}
in a way that differs slightly from
[
17
]
, as it will be more convenient to deal with matrices over a commutative associative algebra rather than a noncommutative one.
For an arbitrary associative algebra
\mathcal{A}
, we denote by
M_{n\times n}(\mathcal{A})
the set of all
n\times n
matrices over
\mathcal{A}
. For every endomorphism
\varphi
of
P_{n}
, we define the Jacobian matrix
J(\varphi)
of
\varphi
to be

J(\varphi)=\pi_{n}^{e}((\frac{\partial\varphi(x_{i})}{\partial x_{j}})_{n\times n})=(\pi_{n}^{e}(\frac{\partial\varphi(x_{i})}{\partial x_{j}}))_{n\times n}\in M_{n\times n}(C_{n}^{e}),

where
\pi_{n}^{e}(\frac{\partial\varphi(x_{i})}{\partial x_{j}})
lies in the
i
-th row and
j
-th column of
J(\varphi)
. Since
C_{n}^{e}
is commutative, the determinant
|J(\varphi)|
of
J(\varphi)
makes sense.

Recall that every endomorphism
\varphi
of
P_{n}
induces an endomorphism
\overline{\varphi}
of
C_{n}
satisfying

\overline{\varphi}\pi_{n}=\pi_{n}\varphi,

namely, the diagram

\textstyle{P_{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{P_{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{n}}
\textstyle{C_{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\overline{\varphi}}
\textstyle{C_{n}}

is commutative.
In particular, it follows immediately that

(3.6)

\overline{\varphi}^{e}\pi_{n}^{e}=(\overline{\varphi}\pi_{n})^{e}=(\pi_{n}\varphi)^{e}=\pi_{n}^{e}\varphi^{e}.

We are now ready to study the form of the chain rule under the aforementioned definition for Jacobian matrices, the proof of which is essentially the same as that in
[
1
]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

(chain rule)
Let
\varphi
and
\psi
be two endomorphisms of
P_{n}
. Then we have

J(\varphi\psi)=\overline{\varphi}^{e}(J(\psi))J(\varphi).

In particular, if
\psi
is an automorphism, then
J(\psi)
is invertible and
J(\psi)^{-1}=\overline{\psi}^{e}(J(\psi^{-1}))
.

Proof.

Assume that
\psi(x_{i})=f=f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
. Then we have
\varphi\psi(x_{i})=f(\varphi(x_{1}),\dots,\varphi(x_{n}))
. It follows that

\displaystyle\pi_{n}^{e}(\frac{\partial\varphi\psi(x_{i})}{\partial x_{j}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq t\leq n}\pi_{n}^{e}\varphi^{e}(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{t}})\pi_{n}^{e}(\frac{\partial{\varphi(x_{t})}}{\partial x_{j}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq t\leq n}\overline{\varphi}^{e}\pi_{n}^{e}(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{t}})\pi_{n}^{e}(\frac{\partial{\varphi(x_{t})}}{\partial x_{j}})

\displaystyle=\overline{\varphi}^{e}(\pi_{n}^{e}(\frac{\partial\psi(x_{i})}{\partial x_{1}}),\dots,\pi_{n}^{e}(\frac{\partial\psi(x_{i})}{\partial x_{n}}))(\pi_{n}^{e}(\frac{\partial{\varphi(x_{1})}}{\partial x_{j}}),\dots,\pi_{n}^{e}(\frac{\partial{\varphi(x_{n})}}{\partial x_{j}}))^{T},

where
(-)^{T}
means the transposed matrix of the underlying matrix.
It follows that
J(\varphi\psi)=\overline{\varphi}^{e}(J(\psi))J(\varphi)
.
Consequently, we obtain

E_{n}=J(\psi^{-1}\psi)=\overline{\psi^{-1}}^{e}(J(\psi))J(\psi^{-1})

and

E_{n}=J(\psi\psi^{-1})=\overline{\psi}^{e}(J(\psi^{-1}))J(\psi),

where
E_{n}
is the identity matrix.
So we have

E_{n}=\overline{\psi}^{e}(E_{n})=\overline{\psi}^{e}\overline{\psi^{-1}}^{e}(J(\psi))\overline{\psi}^{e}(J(\psi^{-1}))=J(\psi)\overline{\psi}^{e}(J(\psi^{-1})).

The result follows immediately.
∎

Now we can deduce more properties of Jacobian matrices by applying the chain rule.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\varphi
be an endomorphism of
P_{n}
such that
\overline{\varphi}=\mathsf{id}
. Then for every automorphism
\psi\in\mathsf{Aut}(P_{n})
, we have

J(\psi\varphi\psi^{-1})=J(\psi)^{-1}\overline{\psi}^{e}(J(\varphi))J(\psi)

and

|J(\psi\varphi\psi^{-1})|=\overline{\psi}^{e}(|J(\varphi)|).

Proof.

By the chain rule, we have

J(\psi\varphi\psi^{-1})=\overline{\psi}^{e}(J(\varphi\psi^{-1}))J(\psi)=\overline{\psi}^{e}(\overline{\varphi}^{e}(J(\psi^{-1}))J(\varphi))J(\psi)=J(\psi)^{-1}\overline{\psi}^{e}(J(\varphi))J(\psi).

The other assertion follows immediately since
\overline{\psi}^{e}
is an algebra homomorphism.
∎

Let
P\{x_{3}\}
be the free Poisson algebra generated by
x_{3}
. Then
P\{x_{3}\}
is the polynomial algebra endowed with trivial Poisson brackets. In order to prove that the Anick type automorphism
\delta
in (
1.1
) is wild, we shall need the following Poisson algebra homomorphism:

\eta:C_{3}\longrightarrow P\{x_{3}\},x_{1}\mapsto 0,\ x_{2}\mapsto 0,\ x_{3}\mapsto x_{3}.

Clearly,
\eta
is well-defined. So for every endomorphism
\varphi
of
P_{3}
, we have

\eta^{e}(J(\varphi))=(\eta^{e}\pi_{3}^{e}(\frac{\partial\varphi(x_{i})}{\partial x_{j}}))_{n\times n}.

We shall need the following observation about Fox derivatives acting on elements of
\mathsf{Ker}(\pi_{3})
with respect to
x_{3}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

For every
f\in\mathsf{Ker}(\pi_{3})
, we have
\eta^{e}\pi_{3}^{e}(\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial x_{3}})=0
.

Proof.

For all
x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{t}}\in X
such that
x_{i_{2}}<x_{i_{1}}\leq 3
, we have
\eta\pi_{3}(x_{i_{2}})=0=\frac{\partial{x_{i_{2}}}}{\partial x_{3}}
. And thus by Lemma
3.3
, we deduce
that

\eta^{e}\pi_{3}^{e}(\frac{\partial{[x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{t}}]}}{\partial x_{3}})=\sum_{1\leq j\leq t}(-H_{\eta\pi_{3}({x_{i_{t}}})})\dots(-H_{\eta\pi_{3}({x_{i_{j+1}}})})H_{\eta\pi_{3}([x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{j-1}}])}\eta^{e}\pi_{3}^{e}(\frac{\partial x_{i_{j}}}{\partial x_{3}})=0.

Therefore, for every element of the form
[x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{t}}]g\in\mathsf{Ker}(\pi_{3})
(
t\geq 2
), we have

Since elements of the form

for endomorphism

Lemma 3.7
.

Since

3.6

4.

Our aim in this section is to establish our main result: the Anick type automorphism

in (

) is wild. For an arbitrary associative algebra
\mathcal{A}
, we call matrices in

of the form

0&1\end{pmatrix}

or

E_{21}(a):=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
a&1\end{pmatrix}

elementary
2\times 2
matrices
or
elementary matrices
, where

. We denote by

the subgroup of the general linear group
GL_{2}(\mathcal{A})
generated by all elementary
2\times 2
matrices.
The following important and useful result is proved in
[
2
]
, see also
[
16
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

[
2
]

Let

be the polynomial algebra with variables

and
y
. Then the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}1+xy&y^{2}\\
-x^{2}&1-xy\end{pmatrix}

does not lie in
E_{2}(k[x,y])

Consequently, the transpose of the above matrix,

\begin{pmatrix}1+xy&-x^{2}\\
y^{2}&1-xy\end{pmatrix}

also does not belong to
E_{2}(k[x,y])
.

Let
\varphi
be an endomorphism of
P_{3}
. Following the notation in
[
16
]
, we define

(4.1)

J_{2}(\varphi)=\begin{pmatrix}\pi_{3}^{e}(\frac{\partial{\varphi(x_{1})}}{\partial x_{1}})&\pi_{3}^{e}(\frac{\partial{\varphi(x_{1})}}{\partial x_{2}})\\
\pi_{3}^{e}(\frac{\partial{\varphi(x_{2})}}{\partial x_{1}})&\pi_{3}^{e}(\frac{\partial{\varphi(x_{2})}}{\partial x_{2}})\end{pmatrix}.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathsf{Aut}(P_{3})
be an elementary automorphism of the form
\sigma(i,1,f)
with

. Then for every tame automorphism
\psi\in\mathsf{TA}(P_{3})
, we have
\eta^{e}(J_{2}(\psi\varphi\psi^{-1}))\in E_{2}(P\{x_{3}\}^{e})
.

Proof.

Since
\psi
is tame, we know that
\psi
is a product of elementary automorphisms of the form
\sigma(j,\alpha,g)
. By Equations (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), we may assume that
\psi
is a product of elementary automorphisms of the form

(4.2)

\sigma(1,\alpha,g)\mbox{ or }\sigma(j,1,-x_{1})\mbox{ with }j\in\{2,3\}.

So we may assume
\psi=\psi_{1}\psi_{2}\dots\psi_{t}
, where each
\psi_{p}
is of the form in (
4.2
).

Now we use induction on
t
to prove the result.
For
t=0
, we have
\psi\varphi\psi^{-1}=\varphi=\sigma(i,1,f)
. By Lemma
3.6
, we have
\eta^{e}\pi_{3}^{e}(\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial x_{3}})=0
. Since
f
does not involve
x_{i}
, we obtain
\eta^{e}\pi_{3}^{e}(\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial x_{i}})=0
. There are several cases to consider. If
i=1
, then by (
4.1
), we have

\eta^{e}(J_{2}(\varphi))=\begin{pmatrix}1&\eta^{e}\pi_{3}^{e}(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{2}})\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\in E_{2}(P\{x_{3}\}^{e});

If
i=2
, then we have

\eta^{e}(J_{2}(\varphi))=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\eta^{e}\pi_{3}^{e}(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}})&1\end{pmatrix}\in E_{2}(P\{x_{3}\}^{e});

If
i=3
, then we have

\eta^{e}(J_{2}(\varphi))=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\in E_{2}(P\{x_{3}\}^{e}).

Now we assume
t>0
. Denote
\psi_{2}\dots\psi_{t}\varphi(\psi_{2}\dots\psi_{t})^{-1}
by
\varphi_{1}
. Then by induction hypothesis and by Lemma
3.7
, we may assume

\eta^{e}(J(\varphi_{1}))=\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}&a_{12}&0\\
a_{21}&a_{22}&0\\
a_{31}&a_{32}&1\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}A_{2\times 2}&0\\
B_{1\times 2}&1\end{pmatrix}

such that
A_{2\times 2}
lies in
E_{2}(P\{x_{3}\}^{e})
.
Denote by
\widetilde{\psi_{1}}
the automorphism of
P\{x_{3}\}
induced by
\overline{\psi_{1}}
. Since the following diagram

\textstyle{C_{3}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\eta}
\scriptstyle{\overline{\psi_{1}}}
\textstyle{C_{3}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\eta}
\textstyle{P\{x_{3}\}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\widetilde{\psi_{1}}}

is commutative, we obtain that

\eta^{e}\overline{\psi_{1}}^{e}=(\eta\overline{\psi_{1}})^{e}=(\widetilde{\psi_{1}}\eta)^{e}=\widetilde{\psi_{1}}^{e}\eta^{e}.

Combining the above formula with Lemma
3.5
, we have

(4.3)

\eta^{e}(J(\psi\varphi\psi^{-1}))=\eta^{e}(J(\psi_{1}\varphi_{1}\psi_{1}^{-1}))=\eta^{e}(J(\psi_{1})^{-1})\widetilde{\psi_{1}}^{e}\eta^{e}(J(\varphi_{1}))\eta^{e}(J(\psi_{1})).

Assume that

\widetilde{\psi_{1}}^{e}\eta^{e}(J(\varphi_{1}))=\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{\psi_{1}}^{e}(A_{2\times 2})&0\\
\widetilde{\psi_{1}}^{e}(B_{1\times 2})&1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}U_{2\times 2}&0\\

Since
\widetilde{\psi_{1}}^{e}
maps elementary matrices over
P\{x_{3}\}^{e}

to elementary matrices over

, we deduce that

.

If
\psi_{1}=\sigma(2,1,-x_{1})
, then we have

\eta^{e}(J(\psi_{1}))=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
-1&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\mbox{ and }\eta^{e}(J(\psi_{1})^{-1})=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\

By (

\displaystyle\eta^{e}(J(\psi\varphi\psi^{-1}))=

0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}U_{2\times 2}&0\\
V_{1\times 2}&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}W_{2\times 2}&G_{2\times 1}\\

Corollary 4.3
.

For

, we may assume that

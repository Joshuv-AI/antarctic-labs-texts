Kupershmidt operators on Hom-Malcev algebras and their deformation
Fattoum Harrathi 1 , Sami Mabrouk 2 , Othmen Ncib 2 , Sergei Silvestrov 3 1 University of Sfax, Faculty of Sciences Sfax, BP 1171, 3038 Sfax, Tunisia. harrathifattoum285@gmail.com 2 University of Gafsa, Faculty of Sciences Gafsa, 2112 Gafsa, Tunisia. mabrouksami00@yahoo.fr, othmen.ncib@fsgf.u-gafsa.tn 3 Mälardalen University, Division of Mathematics and Physics, School of Education, Culture and Communication, Box 883, 72123 Västerås, Sweden. sergei.silvestrov@mdu.se

Abstract
The main feature of Hom-algebras is that the identities defining the structures are twisted by linear maps. The purpose of this paper is to introduce and study a Hom-type generalization of pre-Malcev algebras, called Hom-pre-Malcev algebras. We also introduce the notion of Kupershmidt operators of Hom-Malcev and Hom-pre-Malcev algebras and show the connections between Hom-Malcev and Hom-pre-Malcev algebras using Kupershmidt operators. Hom-pre-Malcev algebras generalize Hom-pre-Lie algebras to the Hom-alternative setting and fit into a bigger framework with a close relationship with Hom-pre-alternative algebras. Finally, we establish a deformation theory of Kupershmidt operators on a Hom-Malcev algebra in consistence with the general principles of deformation theories and introduce the notion of Nijenhuis elements. Keywords : Hom-Malcev algebra, Hom-pre-Malcev algebra, Kupershmidt operator, deformation 2020 MSC : 17D30, 17B61, 17D10, 17A01, 17A30, 17B10

J(x,y,[x,z])=[J(x,y,z),x],

A pre-Malcev algebra is a vector space

{}^{\prime\prime}\cdot^{\prime\prime}

[y,z]\cdot(x\cdot t)+[[x,y],z]\cdot t+y\cdot([x,z]\cdot t)-x\cdot(y\cdot(z\cdot t))+z\cdot(x\cdot(y\cdot t)).

Preliminaries and basics

The purpose of this section is to recall some basics about Hom-alternative algebras introduced in
[
56
,
83
]
. The definition of Hom-Malcev algebras can be viewed as a Hom-alternative algebra via the commutator bracket (see
[
83
]
). We introduce the notion of representation of Hom-Malcev algebra. Also, we construct the dual representation of a representation of a Hom-Malcev algebra
without any additional condition. This is nontrivial. Finally, we introduce the notion of Kupershmidt operator on a Hom-Malcev algebra which is a generalisation of Rota-Baxter operators.

A Hom-algebra is a triple
(A,\mu,\alpha)
in which
A
is a vector space,
\mu:A^{\otimes 2}\longrightarrow A
and
\alpha:A\longrightarrow A
are two linear maps. Hom-algebra is said to be multiplicative if
\alpha\circ\mu=\mu\circ\alpha^{\otimes 2}.
We denote be
End(A)
the space of all linear map
f:A\to A
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
56
]
)
.

A Hom-alternative algebra is a Hom-algebra
(A,\ast,\alpha)
satisfying
for all
x,y,z\in A
,

as_{\alpha}(x,x,y)=as_{\alpha}(y,x,x)=0,

(2.1)

where
as_{\alpha}(x,y,z)=(x\ast y)\ast\alpha(z)-\alpha(x)\ast(y\ast z)
is the Hom-associator
(
\alpha
-associator
)
.

Definition 2.2

(
[
83
]
)
.

A Hom-Malcev algebra is a Hom-algebra
(A,[-,-],\alpha)
such that
[-,-]
is anti-symmetric, and satisfies the Hom-Malcev identity for all
x,y,z\in A
,

J_{\alpha}(\alpha(x),\alpha(y),[x,z])=[J_{\alpha}(x,y,z),\alpha^{2}(x)],

(2.2)

where
J_{\alpha}(x,y,z)=[[x,y],\alpha(z)]+[[y,z],\alpha(x)]+[[z,x],\alpha(y)]
is the Hom-Jacobian of
x,y,z

(
\alpha
-Jacobian
)
.

The Hom-Malcev identity (
2.2
) is equivalent to

\begin{array}[]{ll}[\alpha([x,z]),\alpha([y,t])]&=[[[x,y],\alpha(z)],\alpha^{2}(t)]+[[[y,z],\alpha(t)],\alpha^{2}(x)]\\
&\quad+[[[z,t],\alpha(x)],\alpha^{2}(y)]+[[[t,x],\alpha(y)],\alpha^{2}(z)].\end{array}

(2.3)

When
\alpha=Id_{A}
, we recover the Malcev algebra (see
[
58
]
).

A Hom-Malcev algebra
(A,[-,-],\alpha)
is said to be
regular
, if
\alpha
is inversible.
(A,[-,-],\alpha)
is called
multiplicative
if

\alpha([x,y])=[\alpha(x),\alpha(y)].

Now we introduce the notion of Hom-Malcev admissible algebra and generalize the well-known fact that alternative
algebras are Malcev admissible algebras

Definition 2.3
.

A Hom-algebra
(A,[-,-],\alpha)
is said to be a Hom-Malcev admissible algebra if, for any
elements

, the bracket
[-,-]:A\times A\to A
defined by

[x,y]=x\ast y-y\ast x

satisfies the Hom-Malcev identity.

It is proved in

is Hom-Lie admissible algebra. Also, any alternative algebra is a Malcev admissible algebra. The following theorem shows that
Hom-alternative algebras are related to Hom-Malcev algebras via admissibility

Theorem 2.1
.

Any Hom-alternative algebra

3
Hom-pre-Malcev algebras

In this section, we generalize the notion of pre-Malcev algebras introduced in
[
52

to the Hom

Moreover, we characterize the notion of representation of Hom-pre-Malcev algebras via semidirect product and provide some key constructions.

3.1
Definition and basic properties

Definition 3.1
.

A
Hom-pre-Malcev algebra
is a Hom-algebra
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
satisfying, for any
x,y,z,t\in A
and
[x,y]=x\cdot y-y\cdot x,
the identity

\begin{split}&[\alpha(y),\alpha(z)]\cdot\alpha(x\cdot t)+[[x,y],\alpha(z)]\cdot\alpha^{2}(t)\\
&\qquad+\alpha^{2}(y)\cdot([x,z]\cdot\alpha(t))-\alpha^{2}(x)\cdot(\alpha(y)\cdot(z\cdot t))+\alpha^{2}(z)\cdot(\alpha(x)\cdot(y\cdot t))=0.\end{split}

(3.1)

The identity (
3.1
) is equivalent to

HPM(x,y,z,t)=0
, where for all
x,y,z,t\in A,

\begin{split}HPM(x,y,z,t)&=\alpha(y\cdot z)\cdot\alpha(x\cdot t)-\alpha(z\cdot y)\cdot\alpha(x\cdot t)\\
&\quad+((x\cdot y)\cdot\alpha(z))\cdot\alpha^{2}(t)-((y\cdot x)\cdot\alpha(z))\cdot\alpha^{2}(t)\\
&\quad-(\alpha(z)\cdot(x\cdot y))\cdot\alpha^{2}(t)+(\alpha(z)\cdot(y\cdot x))\cdot\alpha^{2}(t)\\
&\quad+\alpha^{2}(y)\cdot((x\cdot z)\cdot\alpha(t))-\alpha^{2}(y)\cdot((z\cdot x)\cdot\alpha(t))\\
&\quad-\alpha^{2}(x)\cdot(\alpha(y)\cdot(z\cdot t))+\alpha^{2}(z)\cdot(\alpha(x)\cdot(y\cdot t)).\end{split}

(3.2)

Observe that when
\alpha=Id_{A}
, the Hom-pre-Malcev algebra
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
reduces to the pre-Malcev algebra.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
be a Hom-pre-Malcev algebras. It is called as follows:

(1)

Multiplicative Hom-pre-Malcev algebra if
\alpha
satisfies

\alpha(x\cdot y)=\alpha(x)\cdot\alpha(y),\quad\forall x,y\in A,

(3.3)

(2)

Regular Hom-pre-Malcev algebra if
\alpha
is inversible,

(3)

Involutive Hom-pre-Malcev algebra if
\alpha^{2}=Id
.

Hom-pre-Malcev algebras generalize Hom-pre-Lie algebras. A Hom-pre-Lie algebra
is a vector space
A
with a bilinear product "
\bullet
" and a linear map
\alpha
satisfying the Hom-pre-Lie identity for all
x,y,z\in A
,

HPL(x,y,z)=as_{\alpha}(x,y,z)-as_{\alpha}(y,x,z)=0,

where
as_{\alpha}(x,y,z)=(x\bullet y)\bullet\alpha(z)-\alpha(x)\bullet(y\bullet z)
is the Hom-associator. Note that

\displaystyle HPM(x,y,z,t)

\displaystyle=HPL([x,y],\alpha(z),\alpha(t))-HPL([y,x],\alpha(z),\alpha(t))

\displaystyle\quad+HPL(\alpha(x),\alpha(y),[z,t])+HPL(\alpha(y),\alpha(z),[x,t])

\displaystyle\quad-[\alpha^{2}(z),HPL(x,y,t)]+[\alpha^{2}(y),HPL(x,z,t)].

So every Hom-pre-Lie algebra is a Hom-pre-Malcev algebra.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
be a multiplicative Hom-pre-Malcev algebra. Then,

(i)

For

,

where

is a multiplicative Hom-pre-Malcev algebra, called the

derived multiplicative Hom-pre-Malcev algebra.

(ii)

For

,

, where

derived multiplicative Hom-Malcev algebra.

A Hom-pre-Malcev algebra can be viewed as a Hom-Malcev algebra whose
operation decomposes into two compatible pieces.

With the above notations

Proof.

\displaystyle[\beta(b),\beta(c)]_{T}\cdot\beta(a\cdot d)+[[a,b]_{T},\beta(c)]_{T}\cdot\beta^{2}(d)+\beta^{2}(b)\cdot([a,c]_{T}\cdot\beta(d))

(A,[-,-],\alpha,ad)

Definition 3.5

(

A Hom-pre-alternative algebra

\displaystyle(x\succ y)\prec\alpha(z)-\alpha(x)\succ(y\prec z)+(y\prec x)\prec\alpha(z)-\alpha(y)\prec(x\ast z)=0,

\displaystyle(x\succ y)\prec\alpha(z)-\alpha(x)\succ(y\prec z)+(x\ast z)\succ\alpha(y)-\alpha(x)\succ(z\succ y)=0,

(3.8)

\displaystyle(x\ast y)\succ\alpha(z)-\alpha(x)\succ(y\succ z)+(y\ast x)\succ\alpha(z)-\alpha(y)\succ(x\succ z)=0,

(3.9)

\displaystyle(x\prec y)\prec\alpha(z)-\alpha(x)\prec(y\ast z)+(x\prec z)\prec\alpha(y)-\alpha(x)\prec(z\ast y)=0.

3.2
Bimodules and Kupershmidt operators of Hom-pre-Malcev algebras

In this subsection, we introduce and study bimodules of Hom-pre-Malcev algebras and give some constructions.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
be a Hom-pre-Malcev algebra and
V
be a vector space. Let

\ell,r:A\longrightarrow gl(V)
be two linear maps and
\beta\in gl(V)
. Then
(V,\ell,r,\beta)
is called a bimodule of
A
if the
following conditions hold for any
x,y,z\in A
:

\displaystyle\beta\ell(x)=\ell(\alpha(x))\beta,\quad\beta r(x)=r(\alpha(x))\beta,

(3.18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&r(\alpha^{2}(x))\varrho(\alpha(y))\varrho(z)-r(\alpha(z)\cdot(y\cdot x))\beta^{2}+\ell(\alpha^{2}(y))r(z\cdot x)\beta\\
&\quad\quad+\ell(\alpha([y,z]))r(\alpha(x))\beta-\ell(\alpha^{2}(z))r(\alpha(x))\varrho(y)=0,\end{split}

(3.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\ell(\alpha^{2}(y))\ell(\alpha(z))r(x)-r(\alpha^{2}(x))\varrho(\alpha(y))\varrho(z)-\ell(\alpha^{2}(z))r(y\cdot x)\beta\\
&\quad\quad-r(\alpha(z\cdot x))\varrho(\alpha(y))\beta+r([z,y]\cdot\alpha(x))\beta^{2}=0,\end{split}

(3.20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&r(\alpha(y)\cdot(z\cdot x))\beta^{2}+r(\alpha^{2}(x))\varrho([y,z])\beta-\ell(\alpha^{2}(y))\ell(\alpha(z))r(x)\\
&\quad\quad+r(\alpha(y\cdot x))\varrho(\alpha(z))\beta+\ell(\alpha^{2}(z))r(\alpha(x))\varrho(y)=0,\end{split}

(3.21)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\ell([[x,y],\alpha(z)])\beta^{2}-\ell(\alpha^{2}(x))\ell(\alpha(y))\ell(z)+\ell(\alpha^{2}(z))\ell(\alpha(x))\ell(y)\\
&\quad\quad+\ell(\alpha([y,z])\ell(\alpha(x))\beta+\ell(\alpha^{2}(y))\ell([x,z])\beta=0,\end{split}

(3.22)

where
\varrho(x)=\ell(x)-r(x)
and
[x,y]=x\cdot y-y\cdot x
.

Now, define a linear operation
\cdot_{\ltimes}:\otimes^{2}(A\oplus V)\longrightarrow(A\oplus V)
by

(x+a)\cdot_{\ltimes}(y+b)=x\cdot y+\ell(x)(b)+r(y)(a),~~\forall\ x,y\in A,a,b\in V,

and a linear map
\alpha+\beta:A\oplus V\longrightarrow A\oplus V
by

(\alpha+\beta)(x+a)=\alpha(x)+\beta(a),~~\forall\ x\in A,a\in V.

Proposition 3.7
.

With the above notations,
(A\oplus V,\cdot_{\ltimes},\alpha+\beta)
is a Hom-pre-Malcev algebra, which is
denoted by
A\ltimes_{(\ell,~r)}^{\alpha,\beta}V
or simply
A\ltimes V
and called the semi-direct product of the Hom-pre-Malcev algebra
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
and
the bimodule
(V,\ell,r,\beta)
.

Proof.

For any
x,y,z,t\in A
and
a,b,c,d\in V
,

\displaystyle\big((\alpha+\beta)[y+b,z+c]_{\varrho}\big)\cdot_{\ltimes}(\alpha+\beta)\big((x+a)\cdot_{\ltimes}(t+d)\big)

\displaystyle=\alpha([y,z])\cdot\alpha(x\cdot t)+\ell(\alpha([y,z]))\big(\ell(\alpha(x))\beta(d)+r(\alpha(t))\beta(a)\big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+r(\alpha(x\cdot t))\big(\varrho(\alpha(y))\beta(c)-\varrho(\alpha(z))\beta(b)\big),

\displaystyle[[x+a,y+b]_{\varrho},(\alpha+\beta)(z+c)]_{\varrho}\cdot_{\ltimes}(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2})(t+d)

\displaystyle=[[x,y],\alpha(z)]\cdot\alpha^{2}(t)+\ell([[x,y],\alpha(z)])\beta^{2}(d)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+r(\alpha^{2}(t))\big(\varrho([x,y])\beta(c)-\varrho(\alpha(z))(\varrho(x)b-\varrho(y)a)\big),

\displaystyle(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2})(y+b)\cdot_{\ltimes}\big([x+a,z+c]_{\varrho}\cdot_{\ltimes}(\alpha+\beta)(t+d)\big)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}(y)\cdot([x,z]\cdot\alpha(t))+\ell(\alpha^{2}(y))\big(\ell([x,z])\beta(d)+r(\alpha(t))(\varrho(x)c-\varrho(z)a)\big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+r\big([x,z]\cdot\alpha(t)\big)\beta^{2}(b),

\displaystyle(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2})(x+a)\cdot_{\ltimes}\big((\alpha+\beta)(y+b)\cdot_{\ltimes}((z+c)\cdot_{\ltimes}(t+d))\big)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}(x)\cdot(\alpha(y)\cdot(z\cdot t))+\ell(\alpha^{2}(x))\big(\ell(\alpha(y))(\ell(z)d+r(t)c)+r(z\cdot t)\beta(b)\big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+r\big(\alpha(y)\cdot(z\cdot t)\big)\beta^{2}(a),

\displaystyle(\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2})(z+c)\cdot_{\ltimes}\big((\alpha+\beta)(x+a)\cdot_{\ltimes}((y+b)\cdot_{\ltimes}(t+d))\big)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}(z)\cdot(\alpha(x)\cdot(y\cdot t))+\ell(\alpha^{2}(z))\big(\ell(\alpha(x))(\ell(y)d+r(t)b)+r(y\cdot t)\beta(a)\big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+r\big(\alpha(x)\cdot(y\cdot t)\big)\beta^{2}(c).

Hence
(A\oplus V,\cdot_{\ltimes},\alpha+\beta)
is a Hom-pre-Malcev algebra if and only if

(V,\ell,r,\beta)
is a bimodule of
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
(V,\ell,r,\beta)
be a bimodule of a Hom-pre-Malcev algebra
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
and
(A,[-,-],\alpha)

be its associated Hom-Malcev algebra. Then,

(i)

(V,\ell,\beta)
is a representation of
(A,[-,-],\alpha)
,

(ii)

(V,\ell-r,\beta)
is a representation of
(A,[-,-],\alpha)
.

Proof.

(i)
. The statement
(i)
follows immediately from (
3.22
).

(ii)
. By Proposition
3.7
,

A\ltimes_{\ell,r}^{\alpha,\beta}V
is a Hom-pre-Malcev algebra. For its associated Hom-Malcev
algebra
(A\oplus V,\overbrace{[-,-]},\alpha+\beta)
,

\displaystyle\overbrace{[x+a,y+b]}

\displaystyle=(x+a)\cdot_{\ltimes}(y+b)-(y+b)\cdot_{\ltimes}(x+a)

\displaystyle=x\cdot y+\ell(x)b+r(y)a-y\cdot x-\ell(y)a-r(x)b

\displaystyle=[x,y]+(\ell-r)(x)b-(\ell-r)(y)a.

By Proposition
2.1
,
(V,\ell-r,\beta)
is a representation of
(A,[-,-],\alpha)
.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
(V,\ell,r,\beta)
be a bimodule of a Hom-pre-Malcev algebra
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
, and let
(A,[-,-],\alpha)

be its associated Hom-Malcev algebra. Let

be the dual of vector space

, where

.

Proof.

It follows from Proposition
3.8
and Lemma
2.1
.
∎

If

.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let

)

.

It follows from the definition of Hom-Malcev algebra and representation of Hom-Malcev algebra. Then, for any

,

\displaystyle\alpha([y,z])\cdot\alpha(x\cdot t)+[[x,y],\alpha(z)]\cdot\alpha^{2}(t)+\alpha^{2}(y)\cdot([x,z]\cdot\alpha(t))

\displaystyle-\alpha^{2}(x)\cdot(\alpha(y)\cdot(z\cdot t))+\alpha^{2}(z)\cdot(\alpha(x)\cdot(y\cdot t))

.

Let

is called a Kupershmidt operator associated to

if
T
satisfies

\displaystyle T\circ\beta

\displaystyle=\alpha\circ T,

(3.23)

\displaystyle T(a)\cdot T(b)

\displaystyle=T\big(\ell(T(a))b+r(T(b))a\big),\quad\forall\ a,b\in V.

(3.24)

Remark 3.2
.

Let
T
is a Kupershmidt operator of a Hom-pre-Malcev algebra
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
associated to
(V,\ell,r,\beta)
.
Then
T
is a Kupershmidt operator of its associated Hom-MalcevRep pair
(A,[-,-],\alpha,\ell-r,\beta)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.8
, for all
a,b\in V,

\displaystyle[T(a),T(b)]

\displaystyle=T(a)\cdot T(b)-T(b)\cdot T(a)

\displaystyle=T(\ell(T(a))b+r(T(b))a)-T(\ell(T(b))a+r(T(a))b)

\displaystyle=T((\ell-r)(T(a))b-(\ell-r)(T(b))a).\qed

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(V,\ell,r,\beta)
be a bimodule over the multiplicative Hom-pre-Malcev-algebra

, and let

be the associated Hom-Malcev algebra.
Then both

and
(V,\ell^{\alpha}-r^{\alpha},\beta)
are representations over
(A,[-,-],\alpha)
, where

\displaystyle\ell^{\alpha}=\ell\circ(\alpha^{2}\otimes Id)\quad\text{and}\quad r^{\alpha}=r\circ(\alpha^{2}\otimes Id).

Proof.

We prove that
\ell^{\alpha}
satisfy (
2.5
).
For any elements
x,y,z\in A
and
a\in V
,

\displaystyle\ell^{\alpha}([[x,y],\alpha(z)])\beta^{2}(a)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Multiplicativityalpha}}}{{=}}\ell([[\alpha^{2}(x),\alpha^{2}(y)],\alpha^{3}(z)])\beta^{2}(a)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{rep5}}}{{=}}\ell(\alpha^{4}(x))\ell(\alpha^{3}(y))\ell(\alpha^{2}(z))(a)-\ell(\alpha^{4}(z))\ell(\alpha^{3}(x))\ell(\alpha^{2}(y))(a)

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\ell(\alpha^{3}([y,z])\ell(\alpha^{3}(x))\beta(a)-\ell(\alpha^{4}(y))\ell(\alpha^{2}([x,z]))\beta(a)

\displaystyle=\ell^{\alpha}(\alpha^{2}(x))\ell^{\alpha}(\alpha(y))\ell^{\alpha}(z)(a)-\ell^{\alpha}(\alpha^{2}(z))\ell^{\alpha}(\alpha(x))\ell^{\alpha}(y)(a)

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\ell^{\alpha}(\alpha([y,z])\ell^{\alpha}(\alpha(x))\beta(a)+\ell^{\alpha}(\alpha^{2}(y))\ell^{\alpha}([x,z])\beta(a).\qed

4
Infinitesimal deformations of a Kupershmidt operator of Hom-Malcev algebras

In
[
31
,
81
]
, the authors studied the Kupershmidt-(dual-)Nijenhuis structures on a Lie and an alternative algebra
with a representation. Recently, Sami Mabrouk in
[
53
]
studies the infinitesimal deformations of a
Kupershmidt operator of Malcev algebras. Inspired by these works, we consider the infinitesimal deformations of a
Kupershmidt operator of Hom-Malcev algebras. In particular, we introduce the notion of a
Nijenhuis element associated to a Kupershmidt operator, which gives
rise to a trivial infinitesimal deformation of the
Kupershmidt operator. Their relationship with the infinitesimal deformations of
the associated Hom-pre-Malcev algebra is also studied.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be two Kupershmidt operators on a
Hom-MalcevRep
pair

(A,[-,-],\alpha,\varrho,\beta)
. A morphism from
T^{\prime}
to
T
consists of a Hom-Malcev algebra homomorphism
\varphi_{A}:A\longrightarrow A
and a linear map
\varphi_{V}:V\longrightarrow V
such that

\displaystyle T\circ\phi_{V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{A}\circ T^{\prime},

(4.1)

\displaystyle\varphi_{V}\circ\beta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\circ\varphi_{V},

(4.2)

\displaystyle\varphi_{V}\varrho(x)(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varrho(\varphi_{A}(x))(\varphi_{V}(a)),\quad\forall\ x\in A,a\in V.

(4.3)

In particular, if both
\varphi_{A}
and
\varphi_{V}
are invertible,
(\varphi_{A},\varphi_{V})
is called an isomorphism from
T^{\prime}
to
T
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
T
and
T^{\prime}
be two Kupershmidt operators on a
Hom-MalcevRep
pair
(A,[-,-],\alpha,\varrho,\beta)
and
(\varphi_{A},\varphi_{V})
a morphism from
T^{\prime}
to
T
. Then
\varphi_{V}
is a morphism of Hom-pre-Malcev algebras from
(V,\cdot^{\prime})
to
(V,\cdot)
defined by (
3.5
).

Proof.

According to (
4.1
)-(
4.3
), for any
a,b\in V
, we
have

\displaystyle\varphi_{V}(a\cdot^{\prime}b)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{V}\varrho(T^{\prime}(a))(b)=\varrho(\varphi_{A}(T^{\prime}(a)))(\varphi_{V}(b))

\displaystyle=\varrho(T(\varphi_{V}(a)))(\varphi_{V}(v))=\varphi_{V}(a)\cdot\varphi_{V}(b).\qed

Definition 4.2
.

Let
T
be a Kupershmidt operator on a
Hom-MalcevRep
pair
(A,[-,-],\alpha,\varrho,\beta)
and
\mathcal{T}:V\longrightarrow A
a linear map. If
T_{t}=T+t\mathcal{T}
is still a Kupershmidt operator on the
Hom-MalcevRep
pair
(A,[-,-],\alpha,\varrho,\beta)
for all
t
, we say that
\mathcal{T}
generates a one-parameter infinitesimal deformation of the Kupershmidt operator
T
.

By direct computation, we can check that
T_{t}=T+t\mathcal{T}
is a one-parameter
infinitesimal deformation of a Kupershmidt operator
T
if and only if for any
a,b\in V
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}\circ\beta

\displaystyle=\alpha\circ\mathcal{T},

(4.4)

\displaystyle[T(a),\mathcal{T}(b)]+[\mathcal{T},(a),T(b)]

\displaystyle=T(\varrho(\mathcal{T}(a))(b)-\varrho(\mathcal{T}(b))(a))+\mathcal{T}(\varrho(T(a))(b)-\varrho(T(b))(a)),

(4.5)

\displaystyle[\mathcal{T}(a),\mathcal{T}(b)]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(\varrho(\mathcal{T}(a))(b)-\varrho(\mathcal{T}(b))(a)).

(4.6)

Note that (
4.4
) and (
4.6
) mean that
\mathcal{T}
is a Kupershmidt operator on the
Hom-MalcevRep
pair
(A,[-,-],\alpha,\varrho,\beta)
.

Now turning to a Hom-pre-Malcev algebra
(V,\cdot,\beta)
given in (
3.5
), let
\psi:\otimes^{2}V\longrightarrow V
be a linear map. If for any
t\in\mathbb{K}
, the multiplication
{}^{\prime\prime}\cdot_{t}^{\prime\prime}
defined by

a\cdot_{t}b:=a\cdot b+t\psi(a,b),\;\forall\ a,b\in V,

also gives a Hom-pre-Malcev algebra structure, we say that
\psi
generates a one-parameter infinitesimal deformation of the Hom-pre-Malcev algebra
(V,\cdot,\beta)
.

The two types of infinitesimal deformations are related as follows.

Proposition 4.2
.

If
\mathcal{T}
generates a one-parameter infinitesimal deformation of a Kupershmidt operator
T
on a
Hom-MalcevRep
pair
(A,[-,-],\alpha,\varrho,\beta)
, then the product
\psi_{\mathcal{T}}
on
V
defined by

\psi_{\mathcal{T}}(a,b):=\varrho(\mathcal{T}(a))(b),\quad\forall\ a,b\in V,

generates a one-parameter infinitesimal deformation of the associated Hom-pre-Malcev algebra
(V,\cdot,\beta)
.

Proof.

Denote by
{}^{\prime\prime}\cdot_{t}^{\prime\prime}
the corresponding Hom-pre-Malcev algebra structure associated to the Kupershmidt operator
T+t\mathcal{T}
. Then we have

a\cdot_{t}b=\varrho((T+t\mathcal{T})(a))(b)=\varrho(T(a))(b)+t\varrho(\mathcal{T}(a))(b)=a\cdot b+t\psi_{\mathcal{T}}(a,b),\forall\ a,b\in V,

which implies that
\psi_{\mathcal{T}}
generates a one-parameter infinitesimal deformation of
(V,\cdot,\beta)
.
∎

Corollary 4.1
.

If
\mathcal{T}
generates a one-parameter infinitesimal deformation of a Kupershmidt operator
T
on a
Hom-MalcevRep
pair
(A,[-,-],\alpha,\varrho,\beta)
. Then the product
\psi_{\mathcal{T}}
on
V
defined by

\omega_{\mathcal{T}}(a,b):=\varrho(\mathcal{T}(a))(b)-\varrho(\mathcal{T}(b))(a),\quad\forall\ a,b\in V,

generates a one-parameter infinitesimal
deformation of the sub-adjacent Hom-Malcev algebra
(V,[-,-]_{T},\beta)

of the associated Hom-pre-Malcev algebra
(V,\cdot,\beta)
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
T
be a Kupershmidt operator on a
Hom-MalcevRep
pair
(A,[-,-],\alpha,\varrho,\beta)
. Two one-parameter
infinitesimal deformations
T^{1}_{t}=T+t\mathcal{T}_{1}
and

T^{2}_{t}=T+t\mathcal{T}_{2}
are said to be equivalent if there exists
an
x\in A
such that
\alpha(x)=x
and the pair
(Id_{A}+tad_{x},Id_{V}+t\varrho(x))
is a
homomorphism from
T^{2}_{t}
to
T^{1}_{t}
.

Let
(Id_{A}+tad_{x},Id_{V}+t\varrho(x))
be a homomorphism from

T^{2}_{t}
to
T^{1}_{t}
. Then
Id_{A}+tad_{x}
is a Hom-Malcev algebra
endomorphism of

. Thus, we have

(Id_{A}+tad_{x})[y,z]=[(Id_{A}+tad_{x})(y),(Id_{A}+tad_{x})(z)],\;\forall\ y,z\in A,

which implies that
x
satisfies

[[x,y],[x,z]]=0,\quad\forall\ y,z\in A.

(4.7)

Then by (
4.1
), we get

(T+t\mathcal{T}_{1})(Id_{V}+t\varrho(x))(a)=(Id_{A}+tad_{x})(T+t\mathcal{T}_{2})(a),\quad\forall\ a\in V,

which implies

\displaystyle(\mathcal{T}_{2}-\mathcal{T}_{1})(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\varrho(x)(a)+[T(a),x],

(4.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{1}\varrho(x)(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,\mathcal{T}_{2}(a)],\;\forall\ a\in V.

(4.9)

Since
\alpha(x)=x
, by (

), we have

\beta\circ(Id_{V}+t\varrho(x))=(Id_{V}+t\varrho(x))\circ\beta.

Finally, (

(Id_{V}+t\varrho(x))\varrho(y)(a)=\varrho((Id_{A}+tad_{x})(y))(Id_{V}+t\varrho(x))(a),\quad\forall\ y\in A,a\in V,

which implies that
x

\varrho([x,y])\varrho(x)=0,\quad\forall\ y\in A.

(4.10)

Definition 4.4
.

Suppose that
T:V\rightarrow A
is a Kupershmidt operator on a Hom-MalcevRep pair
(A,[-,-],\alpha,\varrho,\beta)
. A linear deformation
T_{t}:T+t\mathcal{T}
is said to be trivial if it is equivalent to the deformation
T_{0}=T
.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
T
be a Kupershmidt operator on a
Hom-MalcevRep
pair
(A,[-,-],\alpha,\varrho,\beta)
. An element
x\in A
is called a

associated to
T
if
x
satisfies
\alpha(x)=x
, the identities (
4.7
), (
4.10
) and the identity

\displaystyle~[x,[T(a),x]+T\varrho(x)(a)]=0,\quad\forall\ a\in V.

(4.11)

Denote by
Nij(T)
the set of Nijenhuis elements associated to a Kupershmidt operator
T
.

By (
4.7
)-(
4.10
), it is obvious that a
trivial one-parameter infinitesimal deformation gives rise to a
Nijenhuis element. The following result is in close analogue to
the fact that the differential of a Nijenhuis operator on a Hom-Malcev
algebra generates a trivial one-parameter infinitesimal
deformation of the Hom-Malcev algebra, justifying the notion of
Nijenhuis elements.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
T
be a Kupershmidt operator on a
Hom-MalcevRep
pair
(A,[-,-],\alpha,\varrho,\beta)
. Then for any
x\in Nij(T)
,
T_{t}:=T+t\mathcal{T}
with
\mathcal{T}(a)=T\varrho(x)(a)+[T(a),x]
, for all
a\in V
, is a trivial one-parameter infinitesimal deformation of the Kupershmidt operator
T
.

Proof.

First
\mathcal{T}
is closed since
\mathcal{T}(a)=T\varrho(x)(a)+[T(a),x]
.
To show that
\mathcal{T}
generates a trivial one-parameter infinitesimal deformation of
the Kupershmidt operator
T
, we only need to verify that
(
4.4
) and (
4.6
) hold. By definition of
\mathcal{T}
and using the condition
\alpha(x)=x
we have,
for all
a\in V
,

\displaystyle\alpha\circ\mathcal{T}(a)

\displaystyle=\alpha(T\varrho(x)(a)+[T(a),x])=T(\beta(\varrho(x)(a))+\alpha([T(a),x]))

\displaystyle=T(\varrho(\alpha(x))(\beta(a)))+[\alpha(T(a)),\alpha(x)]))=T(\varrho(x)(\beta(a)))+[T(\beta(a)),x]))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}(\beta(a)).

Then
\mathcal{T}
satisfies (
4.4
).
Moreover, according to (
4.7
), we have,
for any
a,b\in V
,

\displaystyle[\mathcal{T}(a),\mathcal{T}(b)]-\mathcal{T}(\varrho(\mathcal{T}(a))(b)-\varrho(\mathcal{T}(b))(a))

\displaystyle=[[T(a),x],[T(b),x]]+[[T(a),x],T\varrho(x)(b)]+[T\varrho(x)(a),[T(b),x]]+[T\varrho(x)(a),T\varrho(x)(b)]

\displaystyle-[T\varrho([T(a),x])(b),x]-[T\varrho(T\varrho(x)(a))(b),x]+[T\varrho([T(b),x])(a),x]+[T\varrho(T\varrho(x)(b))(a),x]

\displaystyle-T\varrho(x)\varrho([T(a),x])(b)-T\varrho(x)\varrho(T\varrho(x)(a))(b)+T\varrho(x)\varrho([T(b),x])(a)+T\varrho(x)\varrho(T\varrho(x)(b))(a)

\displaystyle=[[T(a),x],T\varrho(x)(b)]+[T\varrho(x)(a),[T(b),x]]+\underline{T\varrho(T\varrho(x)(a))\varrho(x)(b)}\underbrace{-T\varrho(T\varrho(x)(b))\varrho(x)(a)}

\displaystyle-[T\varrho([T(a),x])(b),x]-[T\varrho(T\varrho(x)(a))(b),x]+[T\varrho([T(b),x])(a),x]+[T\varrho(T\varrho(x)(b))(a),x]

\displaystyle\underline{-T\varrho(x)\varrho([T(a),x])(b)-T\varrho(x)\varrho(T\varrho(x)(a))(b)}\underbrace{+T\varrho(x)\varrho([T(b),x])(a)+T\varrho(x)\varrho(T\varrho(x)(b))(a)}.

The under-braced terms add to zero using (
4.10
) and (
4.11
) and the underlined terms add to zero by the same computation.
For the other terms, by (
4.7
) and (
4.11
), we have

\displaystyle[[T(a),x],T\varrho(x)(b)]-[T\varrho([T(a),x])(b),x]+[T\varrho(T\varrho(x)(b))(a),x]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[T(a),[x,T\varrho(x)(b)]]+[[T(a),T\varrho(x)(b)],x]-[T\varrho([T(a),x])(b),x]+[T\varrho(T\varrho(x)(b))(a),x]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[T(a),[x,[T(b),x]]]+[T\varrho(T(a))\varrho(x)(b),x]-[T\varrho([T(a),x])(b),x]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[x,[T(a),[T(b),x]]]+[T\varrho(x)\varrho(T(a))(b),x]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[x,[T(a),[T(b),x]]]+[x,[T\varrho(T(a))(b),x]].

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle[T\varrho(x)(a),[T(b),x]]-[T\varrho(T\varrho(x)(a))(b),x]+[T\varrho([T(b),x])(a),x]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,[T(b),[T(a),x]]]-[x,[T\varrho(T(b))(a),x]].

Thus

\displaystyle[\mathcal{T}(a),\mathcal{T}(b)]-\mathcal{T}(\varrho(\mathcal{T}(a))(b)-\varrho(\mathcal{T}(b))(a))=0,

Then,
\mathcal{T}
generates a one-parameter infinitesimal deformation of
T
.

Further, since
x
is a Nijenhuis element, it is straightforward that
(Id_{A}+tad_{x},Id_{V}+t\varrho(x))
gives the
desired homomorphism between
T_{t}
and
T
. Thus, the deformation is trivial.
∎

Now we introduce the notion of a Nijenhuis operator on a Hom-pre-Malcev algebra.

Definition 4.6
.

A linear map
N:A\longrightarrow A
on a Hom-pre-Malcev algebra
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
is called a
Nijenhuis operator
if

N\circ\alpha=\alpha\circ N,

(4.12)

N(x)\cdot N(y)=N\big(N(x)\cdot y+x\cdot N(y)-N(x\cdot y)\big),\quad\forall\ x,y\in A.

(4.13)

Proposition 4.3
.

If
N
is a Nijenhuis operator on a Hom-pre-Malcev algebra
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
, then
N
is a
Nijenhuis operator on the sub-adjacent Hom-Malcev algebra
(A,[-,-],\alpha)
.

Proof.

Since
N
is a Nijenhuis operator on
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
, we have
N\circ\alpha=\alpha\circ N
. For all
x,y\in A
, by Proposition
3.2
and Definition
4.13
, we have

\displaystyle[N(x),N(y)]

\displaystyle=N(x)\cdot N(y)-N(y)\cdot N(x)

\displaystyle=N\big(N(x)\cdot y+x\cdot N(y)-N(x\cdot y)-N(y)\cdot x-y\cdot N(x)+N(y\cdot x)\big)

\displaystyle=N\big([N(x),y]+[x,N(y)]-N[x,y]\big).

Thus,
N
is a Nijenhuis operator on
(A,[-,-],\alpha)
.
∎

For its connection with a Nijenhuis element associated to
a Kupershmidt operator, we have

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
x\in A
be a Nijenhuis element associated to a
Kupershmidt operator
T
on a
Hom-MalcevRep
pair
(A,[-,-],\alpha,\varrho,\beta)
. Then
\varrho(x)
is a Nijenhuis operator
on the associated Hom-pre-Malcev algebra
(V,\cdot,\beta)
.

Proof.

For the proof, we just need to check, by
(
4.10
), for all
a,b\in V
,

\displaystyle\varrho(x)(\varrho(x)(a)\cdot b+a\cdot\varrho(x)(b)-\varrho(x)(a\cdot b))-\varrho(x)(a)\cdot\varrho(x)(b)

\displaystyle\quad=\varrho(x)\Big(\varrho(T\varrho(x)(a))(b)+\varrho(T(a))\varrho(x)(b)-\varrho(x)\varrho(T(a))(b)\Big)-\varrho(T\varrho(x)(a))\varrho(x)(b)

\displaystyle\quad=[\varrho(x),\varrho(T\varrho(x)(a))]+[\varrho(x),\varrho([T(a),x])](b)

\displaystyle\quad=\varrho([x,T\varrho(x)(a)+[T(a),x]])(b)=0.\qed

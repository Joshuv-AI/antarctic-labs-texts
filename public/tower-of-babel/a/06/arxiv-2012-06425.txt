Gergely Bérczi

Abstract
. We combine recently developed intersection theory for non-reductive geometric invariant theoretic quotients with equivariant localisation to prove a formula for Thom polynomials of Morin singularities. These formulas use only toric combinatorics of certain partition polyhedra, and our new approach circumvents the poorly understood Borel geometry of existing models.



1.
Introduction

We combine the results of
[
4
]
with a blow-up process to construct a new non-reductive GIT type model for the moduli space of
k
-jets, which parametrises holomorphic
k
-jets of map germs
({\mathbb{C}},0)\to({\mathbb{C}}^{n},0)
modulo polynomial reparametrisations of
{\mathbb{C}}
at the origin. Intersection theory developed in
[
8
]
for non-reductive GIT quotients accompanied with equivariant localisation and a residue vanishing theorem leads us to a closed iterated residue formula for Thom polynomials of Morin singularities. Our formula builds only on the toric geometry of partition polyhedra, and we avoid the use of unknown Borel geometry appeared in the earlier works
[
26
,
11
,
36
]
.

Global singularity theory studies the topology of holomorphic maps between complex manifolds. A fundamental question is how to describe the topological locus in the source space where a generic map has a given type of singularity. A more precise formulation of this problem goes back to Thom
[
42
]
and reads as follows. Let
f:N\to M
be a map between two complex manifolds,
N
and
M
, of dimensions
n\leq m
. We say that
p\in N
is a
singular
point of
f
if the rank of the differential
df_{p}:T_{p}N\to T_{f(p)}M
is less than
n
. In order to introduce a finer classification of singular points, choose local coordinates near
p\in N
and
f(p)\in M
, and consider the resulting
map-germ
f_{p}:({\mathbb{C}}^{n},0)\to({\mathbb{C}}^{m},0)
, which may be thought of as a
sequence of
m
power series in
n
variables without constant terms. Let
J(n,m)
denote the space of such map-germs endowed with the action of the group
\mathrm{Diff}_{n}\times\mathrm{Diff}_{m}
of infinitesimal local coordinate changes. The
\mathrm{Diff}_{n}\times\mathrm{Diff}_{m}
-orbits or, more generally,
\mathrm{Diff}_{n}\times\mathrm{Diff}_{m}
-invariant subsets

O\subset J(n,m)
are called
singularities
. For a singularity
O
and
holomorphic
f:N\to M
, we can define the set

\Sigma_{O}(f)=\{p\in N;\;f_{p}\in O\},

which is independent of any
coordinate choices.
N
. Assuming
N

is compact and
f
is sufficiently generic,
\Sigma_{O}(f)
is an analytic subvariety of
N
giving a Poincaré dual class
[\Sigma_{O}(f)]\in H^{*}(N,\mathbb{Z})
. This problem was first studied by René Thom (cf.
[
42
,
23
]
) in the category of smooth varieties and smooth maps; in this case cohomology with
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
-coefficients is used. Thom discovered
that to every singularity
O
one can associate a bivariant characteristic class
\tau_{O}
, which, when evaluated on the pair
(TN,f^{*}TM)
produces the Poincaré dual class
[\Sigma_{O}(f)]
. One of the consequences of this result is that the class
[\Sigma_{O}(f)]
depends only on the homotopy class of
f
.

The structure of the
\mathrm{Diff}_{n}\times\mathrm{Diff}_{m}
-action on
J(n,m)
is rather complicated; even the parametrization of the orbits is difficult. There is, however, a simple invariant on the space of orbits: to each map-germ
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}):(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)
, we can associate the finite-dimensional nilpotent algebra
A_{f}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
defined as the quotient of the algebra of power series with no constant term by the ideal generated by the pull-back subalgebra
f^{*}(y_{1}\ldots,y_{m})
. This algebra
A_{f}
is trivial if
the map-germ
f
is nonsingular, and it does not change along a
\mathrm{Diff}_{n}\times\mathrm{Diff}_{m}
-orbit. Hence, for a fixed nilpotent algebra
A
with
n
generators one can associate the invariant subset

\Sigma_{A}=\{f\in J(n,m):A_{f}\simeq A\}
,
the set of jets with local algebra isomorphic to
A
. The
Thom principle
in this holomorphic setting (cf
[
27
,
20
,
11
]
) for
\mathscr{O}=\Sigma_{A}
tells that there is a jet order
k
depending only on
A
such that

[\Sigma_{A}(f)]=\mathrm{eP}[\Sigma_{A},J_{k}(n,m)](f^{*}TN,TM),

is obtained by substituting the Chern roots of
TN,f^{*}TM
into the
\mathrm{GL}(n)\times\mathrm{GL}(m)
-equivariant dual of
\Sigma_{A}
in
J_{k}(n,m)
. This equivariant dual sits in the ring
{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]^{S_{n}\times S_{m}}
of bi-symmetric polynomials on
n+m
variables. Damon and Ronga
[
14
,
37
]
proved that
[\Sigma_{A}(f)]
depends on
TN,f^{*}TM
only through the Chern classes of the difference bundle
TN-f^{*}TM
, and hence
[\Sigma_{A}(f)]=\mathrm{Tp}_{A}(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots)
is a polynomial in these classes, which we call the
Thom polynomial of
A
. Calculation of these polynomials remains a major open problem ever since then, see e.g
[
1
,
35
,
27
,
19
]
.

In this paper we study the Morin case, i.e when
A_{k}={\mathbb{C}}[t]/t^{k+1}
for some
k
. The problem of calculating
\mathrm{Tp}_{k}=\mathrm{Tp}_{A_{k}}
goes back to Thom
[
42
,
40
]
. The case
k=1
is the classical formula of Porteous
[
32
]
:
\mathrm{Tp}_{1}=c_{m-n+1}
. The Thom polynomial in the
k=2
case was computed by Ronga in
[
37
]
. An explicit formula for
\mathrm{Tp}_{3}
was proposed in
[
7
]
, and P. Pragacz has given a sketch of its proof
[
33
]
. Finally, using his method of restriction equations, Rimányi
[
35
]
was able to treat the
n=k
case, and computed
\mathrm{Tp}_{k}
for
k\leq 8
in the equidimensional
n=m
case (cf.
[
22
]
for the case
k=4
).

In
[
11
]
we introduced a new approach and used equivariant localisation to prove iterated residue formulas for Morin singularities for any
n,k
. Let
J_{k}(1,n)
denote the
k
-jets of holomorphic map germs
(\mathbb{C},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)
. These jets can be reparametrised by the polynomial reparametrisation group
\mathrm{Diff}_{k}=J_{k}^{reg}(1,1)
formed by jets with nonzero first derivative. The quotient
J_{k}(1,n)/\mathrm{Diff}_{k}
is quasi-projective, it is the moduli of invariant jets of order
k
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.
A key observation of
[
11
]
is that (some open part of)
\Sigma_{k}(n,m)
fibers over the non-reductive quotient
J_{k}(1,n)/\mathrm{Diff}_{k}
with affine fibers. We show in
[
11
]
that the curvilinear component of the Hilbert scheme of
k
points on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
provides a natural compactification of this non-reductive quotient, and
the main message of
[
11
]
is that the Thom polynomials are certain equivariant intersection numbers on the curvilinear Hilbert scheme
.

This formula, however, contains an unknown ingredient, the
Q_{k}
polynomial, which is the equivariant dual of a certain Borel orbit in a
\mathrm{GL}(n)
-module. Our knowledge about
Q_{k}
is very limited. In
[
26
]
, Kazarian reinterprets the defining equations of
Q_{k}
as the ideal of the Morin algebra sitting in a smooth ambient space what he calls the
nonassociative Hilbert scheme
. These equations fully determine
Q_{k}
—however the description of them is out of reach, and a major problem. The problem is that besides the
associativity equations
(which do not cut out even the right dimensional variety–there there are other
exotic equations
, which are determined by the Borel geometry of the nonassociative Hilbert scheme.

In this paper we develop an approach which avoids working with
Q_{k}
and Borel orbits, and reduces the problem to toric geometry. We replace the curvilinear Hilbert scheme with a birational model, namely, the non-reductive GIT quotient
\overline{J_{k}(1,n)}/\mathrm{Diff}_{k}
for some proper projective completion
\overline{J_{k}(1,n)}
of
J_{k}(1,n)
. This compactification will be a result of a blow-up procedure fully determined by the combinatorics of certain partition polyhedra. We combine intersection theory developed in
[
8
]
for non-reductive GIT quotients with equivariant localisation on the blown-up space, and in particular, we use the non-reductive Jeffrey-Kirwan type integration formula proved in
[
8
]
. We prove a residue vanishing theorem, which leads us to closed iterated residue formulas for Thom polynomials of Morin singularities, detailed in the next section.

In particular, this paper provides a combinatorial (toric) approach to a
partial fraction decomposition
of the rational expression
\frac{Q_{k}}{\prod_{1\leq i+j\leq k}(z_{i}+z_{j}-z_{k})}
in the formula of Bérczi-Szenes
[
11
]
, which might serve as a starting point towards Rimányi’s conjecture on Chern positivity of Thom polynomials
[
35
,
34
]
.

As a final remark, we note that although this paper focuses only on Morin singularities, our approach extends to compute Thom polynomials for other contact singularity classes (see
[
36
,
35
]
).

2.
The strategy and the result

Fix the parameters
k\leq n\leq m
. Let
J_{k}(n,m)
denote the
k
-jets of holomorphic map germs
(\mathbb{C}^{n},0)\to(\mathbb{C}^{m},0)
, and
\Sigma_{k}(n,m)

be the set of jets with local algebra
t\mathbb{C}[t]/t^{k+1}
. Its closure,
\overline{\Sigma}_{k}(n,m)
is a
\mathrm{GL}(n)\times\mathrm{GL}(m)
-invariant, singular subvariety of the affine space
J_{k}(n,m)
. The Thom principle (see e.g (see
[
35
,
26
,
11
]
says that the polynomial of
A_{k}
singularities is given by the
\mathrm{GL}(n)\times\mathrm{GL}(m)
-equivariant dual of
\Sigma_{k}(n,m)
in
J_{k}(n,m)
after substituting the Chern roots of
TN
and
f^{*}TM
into the weights:

\mathrm{Tp}_{k}^{n,m}=\mathrm{eP}_{\mathrm{GL}(n)\times\mathrm{GL}(m)}[\Sigma_{k}(n,m),J_{k}(n,m)](TN,f^{*}TM)

Gaffney
[
22
]
observed that a generic germ
\Psi\in\Sigma_{k}
has a test curve
\gamma\in J_{k}^{reg}(1,n)
(
reg
refers to nonvanishing linear part) such that
\Psi\circ\gamma=0
in
J_{k}(n,m)
. Here
\Psi=\Psi^{1}+\ldots+\Psi^{k}
is generic if its linear part is generic, that is, the rank of
\Psi^{1}
is
1
. We denote the set of these jets by
\Sigma_{k}^{0}(n,m)
. The test curve is unique up to polynomial reparametrizations of
(\mathbb{C},0)
; that is, up to the action of the reparametrisation group
\mathrm{Diff}_{k}=J_{k}^{reg}(1,1)
. Therefore
\Sigma_{k}^{0}(n,m)
fibers over the quasi-projective quotient
J_{k}^{reg}(1,n)/\mathrm{Diff}_{k}
, which we call the moduli space of
k
-jets in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. The fibers are linear subspaces in
J_{k}(n,m)
of codimension
km
.

In
[
11
]
we construct an embedding
\phi:J_{k}^{reg}(1,n)/\mathrm{Diff}_{k}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Grass}(k,J_{k}(n,1)^{*})

and we work with the the closure
\overline{\mathrm{im}(\phi)}
as a natural projective completion of the quotient. The key property of this compactification is that the linear fibration
\Sigma_{k}^{0}(n,m)
over
J_{k}^{reg}(1,n)/\mathrm{Diff}_{k}
extends to a bundle
V
over
\overline{\mathrm{im}(\phi)}
which sits in the trivial bundle
V\subset J_{k}(n,m)\times\overline{\mathrm{im}(\phi)}
. We develop equivariant localisation on
\overline{\mathrm{im}(\phi)}
to come up with an iterated residue formula for integrals over
\overline{\mathrm{im}(\phi)}
, and we present Thom polynomials of
A_{k}
singularities as an equivariant intersection number, namely,

\mathrm{eP}[\Sigma_{k}(n,m),J_{k}(n,m)]=\int_{\overline{\mathrm{im}(\phi)}}\mathrm{Thom}(V\subset J_{k}(n,m))

is the integral of the Thom form of
V
in
J_{k}(n,m)\times\overline{\mathrm{im}(\phi)}
, whose restriction to each linear fiber
V_{f}
is the Euler class of the normal bundle of
V_{f}
in
J_{k}(n,m)
.

In
[
3
]
we observed that
k
-jets of smooth curves at the origin on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
correspond to so-called curvilinear subschemes on
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. These are points of the Hilbert scheme
\mathrm{Hilb}^{k+1}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
which are supported at the origin and isomorphic to the algebra
t{\mathbb{C}}[t]/t^{k+1}
. The closure of this locus in the Hilbert scheme is a punctual component, called the curvilinear component, denoted by
\mathrm{CHilb}^{k+1}_{0}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
. Hence this component is a natural projective compactification of the moduli space of jets, and in
[
3
]
we showed that

\mathrm{CHilb}^{k+1}_{0}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})=\overline{\mathrm{im}(\phi)}\subset\mathrm{Grass}_{k}(J_{k}(n,1)^{*})
. Let
\mathscr{E}
denote the tautological rank
k
bundle, and
{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
the trivial
\mathrm{GL}(m)
-equivariant bundle over
\overline{\mathrm{im}(\phi)}
. Then
\mathscr{E}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{m}
is
\mathrm{GL}(n)\times\mathrm{GL}(m)
-equivariant, and in
[
11
]
we show that

\mathrm{Thom}(V\subset J_{k}(n,m))=\mathrm{Euler}(\mathscr{E}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{m})
is the equivariant Euler class, hence

\mathrm{eP}[\Sigma_{k}(n,m),J_{k}(n,m)]=\int_{\mathrm{CHilb}^{k+1}_{0}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})}\mathrm{Euler}(\mathscr{E}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}^{m}).

In this paper we substitute the curvilinear Hilbert scheme with a birational model, namely, the non-reductive geometric invariant theoretic (NRGIT) quotient
\mathrm{Jet}_{k}/\!/\mathrm{Diff}_{k}
for some suitable projective completion

The test jet model of Berczi-Szenes

This model was developed as a compactification of the Morin contact singularity class of type

4.1.1.

If

(7)

J_{1}(u,1)^{*}\subset J_{2}(u,1)^{*}\subset\ldots\subset J_{k}(u,1)^{*}

5.
Non-reductive geometric invariant theory

In

Definition 5.1
.

We say that a linear algebraic group
H=U\rtimes R
has internally graded unipotent radical
U
if there is a central one-parameter subgroup
\lambda:{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to Z(R)
of the Levi subgroup
R
of
H
such that the adjoint action of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
on the Lie algebra of
U
has all its weights strictly positive.
Then
\hat{U}=U\rtimes\lambda({\mathbb{G}}_{m})
is a normal subgroup of
H
and
H/\hat{U}\cong R/\lambda({\mathbb{G}}_{m})
is reductive.

Let

be a linear algebraic group with internally graded unipotent radical
U
acting linearly with respect to an ample line bundle
L
on a projective variety
X
; that is, the action of
H
on
X
lifts to an action on
L
via automorphisms of the line bundle. When
H=R
is reductive, using Mumford’s classical geometric invariant theory (GIT)
[
31
]
, we can define
H
-invariant open subsets
X^{s}\subseteq X^{ss}
of
X
(the stable and semistable loci for the linearisation) with a geometric quotient
X^{s}/H
and projective completion
X/\!/H\supseteq X^{s}/H
which is the projective variety associated to the algebra of invariants

\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes k})^{H}
. The variety

is the image
of a surjective morphism
\phi
from the open subset
X^{ss}
of
X
such that if

then
\phi(x)=\phi(y)
if and only if the closures of the
H

and

meet in

and

can be described using the Hilbert–Mumford criteria for stability and semistability.

Mumford’s GIT does not have an immediate extension to actions of non-reductive linear algebraic groups

is not necessarily finitely generated as a graded algebra when

is not reductive.
It is still possible to define semistable and stable subsets

and

which is an open subset of a so-called enveloping quotient

-invariant morphism

is finitely generated then

is the associated projective variety

[
4
,
16
]
. But in general the enveloping quotient

is not necessarily projective, the morphism

is not necessarily surjective (and its image may be only a constructible subset, not a subvariety, of

Proof.

By the positivity property of the multidegree
\mathrm{mdeg}[I,S]
is indeed a polynomial of the weights
\eta_{i},i=1,\ldots,N
. Let

\mathrm{coeff}(\eta_{i},m)\neq 0\text{ for }1\leq i\leq s;\ \mathrm{coeff}(\eta_{s+1},m)=\ldots=\mathrm{coeff}(\eta_{N},m)=0.

The idea of the proof is to choose an appropriate monomial order on the polynomial ring
S={\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{N}]
to ensure that
y_{1}
does not appear in the corresponding initial ideal.

To that end recall, that a weight function is a linear map
\rho:{\mathbb{Z}}^{N}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
. This defines a partial order
>_{\rho}
on the monomials of
S
, called the weight order associated to
\rho
, by the rule
m=y^{a}>_{\rho}n=y^{b}
iff
\rho(a)>\rho(b)
. Here

are arbitrary multiindices. Any weight order can be extended to a compatible monomial order

(see Eisenbud
[
17
, Ch 15.2]
), which means that

implies
m>n
.
For our purposes define

\rho(y_{1})=-1,\rho(y_{2})=\ldots=\rho(y_{N})=0

and let
>
denote arbitrary compatible monomial order on
S
. By definition for a monomial
m\in S

(23)

\rho(m)<0\Longleftrightarrow y_{1}|m.

Let
p=m_{1}+\ldots+m_{t}\in I
be a polynomial where the
m_{i}
’s are monomials in the variables
y_{1},\ldots,y_{N}
of the same weighted degree. We claim that if
in_{<}(p)
is a basis element of the initial ideal

then not all monomials
m_{i}
of

are divisible by

. Indeed, if

for

then
y_{1}|p
. But

by assumption because this would mean that

and

holds and therefore
in_{<}(p)
is not among the generators of
in_{>}(I)
.
So there is a monomial of

not containing

.
Consequently,
y_{1}
does not divide any of the generators of

are therefore

, giving a maximum total degre

7.2.
The Rossman formula

The Rossmann equivariant localisation formula is an improved version of the Atiyah-Bott/Berline-Vergne localisation for singular varieties sitting in a smooth ambient space.
Let
Z

T

T

. Then one can find local analytic coordinates

\mathrm{T}_{p}Z

p
in
Z
. We denote by
\hat{T}_{p}M
the part of

\mathrm{T}_{p}Z
which corresponds to
M
under this identification;
informally, we will call
\hat{T}_{p}M
the
T
-invariant
tangent cone

of
M
at
p
. This tangent cone is not quite canonical: it depends on
the choice of coordinates; the equivariant dual of

\Sigma=\hat{T}_{p}M
in

the
equivariant multiplicity of
M
in
Z
at
p
:

(24)

\mathrm{emult}_{p}[M,Z]\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\mathrm{eP}[\hat{T}_{p}M,\mathrm{T}_{p}Z].

Remark 10
.

In the algebraic framework one might need to pass to the tangent scheme of
M
at
p
(cf. Fulton
[
21
]
). This is canonically
defined, but we will not use this notion.

The analog of the Atiyah-Bott formula for singular subvarieties of smooth ambient manifolds is the following statement.

[

We transform the right hand side of (

To describe this formula, we will need the notion of an

(cf. e.g.

be affine linear forms on
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}

. We will use the shorthand

for the

be an entire function, and define the

\operatornamewithlimits{Res}_{z_{1}=\infty}\operatornamewithlimits{Res}_{z_{2}=\infty}\dots\operatornamewithlimits{Res}_{z_{k}=\infty}\frac{h(\mathbf{z})\,\,d\mathbf{z}}{\prod_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\right)^{k}\int_{|z_{1}|=R_{1}}\ldots\int_{|z_{k}|=R_{k}}\frac{h(\mathbf{z})\,\,d\mathbf{z}}{\prod_{i=1}^{N}\omega_{i}},

where

.
We will also use the following simplified notation:

In practice, one way to compute the iterated residue (

11.
The blowing up process : polynomial vs monomial maps

We recall that we aim to construct a
\mathrm{Diff}_{k}
-equivariant blow-up of the rational map

\phi:{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}}^{ss}\subset U_{0}\dasharrow\mathrm{Grass}_{k}(\mathrm{Sym}^{\leq k}{\mathbb{C}}^{n})

\phi(t:w_{1},\ldots w_{k})=e_{1}\wedge(w_{2}+te_{1}^{2})\wedge\ldots\wedge(\sum_{i_{1}+2i_{2}+\ldots+ki_{k}=k}t^{i_{1}+\ldots+i_{k}-1}e_{1}^{i_{1}}\ldots e_{k}^{i_{k}}).

from the affine chart
U_{0}\subset{\tilde{\mathbb{P}}}
to the projective space
{\mathbb{P}}[\wedge^{k}\mathrm{Sym}^{\leq k}{\mathbb{C}}^{n}]
. Each basis element
e_{\pi}=e_{1}\wedge e_{\pi_{2}}\wedge\ldots\wedge e_{\pi_{k}}
is labeled by a sequence
\pi=(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{k})
of partitions, where
\pi_{r}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{k}
is a positive partition of
r=i_{1}+\ldots+ri_{r}
for
1\leq r\leq k
. We can and will associate to such a partition
\pi_{r}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{k}
the box centered at
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})
on an
k
-dimensional lattice, and hence
\pi
is given by
k
boxes. We call such a collection a
semi-partition
in dimension
k
and we denote the set of semi-partitions by
\Pi_{k}
. In short, semi-partitions parametrise the coordinates of a subspace of
{\mathbb{P}}(\wedge^{k}\mathrm{Sym}^{\leq k}{\mathbb{C}}^{n})
in which the image of
\phi
sits. Some of these semi-partitions are proper
k
-dimensional partitions, that is

(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})\in\pi\Rightarrow(i_{1}-1,i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}),\ldots,(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}-1)\in\pi

Torus fixed points under the
T^{n}\subset\mathrm{GL}(n)
action on
\mathrm{Hilb}^{k}({\mathbb{C}}^{n})
correspond to proper partitions
\lambda
: indeed, these correspond to monomial ideals
I_{\lambda}
of length
k
fixed by the torus, and those partitions sitting in
\mathrm{Span}(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
correspond to basis of the subspace where
im(\phi)
sits.
So
\phi
has the form

\phi(t,\beta_{ij})=[p_{\pi}(t,\beta_{i,j}):\pi\in\Pi_{k,n}]

where
p_{\pi}
are polynomial functions (Plücker coordinates), see (
10.1
) for the
k=3
case. The indeterminacy locus of
\phi
is the zero set of the ideal

I_{0}=(p_{\pi}:\pi\in\Pi_{k,n}),

generated by the coordinate functions. This indeterminacy locus is highly singular.

Remark 14
.

The zero set is highly singular, and the equivariant dual of its tangent cone at the distinguished point
e_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge e_{k}
encodes the
Q_{k}
polynomial of
[
11
]
, see the Introduction. As we explained, the main difficulty we face immediately working with
Q_{k}
, is that the description of the relations among these generators (i.e the first syzygies) is out of reach, and it involved deep Borel geometry. We have not seen any progress in their calculation in the last 12 years.

However, in our approach, we do not need to work with the syzygies. We need a
\mathrm{Diff}_{k}
-equivariant blow-up, such that
\phi
extends to the NRGIT semistable locus. And we can perform most of these blow-ups at smooth centers, working with monomial ideals.

11.1.
From polynomial to monomial maps

For a polynomial
p\in{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
let
\mathscr{M}(p)
denote the set of monomials of
p
. For a polynomial rational map
f:{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]\dasharrow{\mathbb{P}}^{r}
given by
f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})=[p_{0}(\mathbf{x}),\ldots,p_{r}(\mathbf{x})]
we let

I(f)=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{r})\subset{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]

denote the ideal of coordinate functions. We introduce the notation
\mathscr{M}(f)=\cup_{i=1}^{r}\mathscr{M}(p_{i})
for the set of all monomials (with multiplicity) of
f
and

M(f)=(\mathscr{M}(f))=(\mathscr{M}(p_{i}):\ i=1,\ldots,r)\subset{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]

for the monomial ideal generated by all monomials of the polynomial coordinate functions of
f
. For a fixed order of elements of
\mathscr{M}(f)
) let
f^{\mathrm{mon}}:\mathbb{A}^{m}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{N-1}
,

f^{\mathrm{mon}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})=[m:m\in\mathscr{M}(f)]\in{\mathbb{P}}^{N-1}

denote the corresponding monomial map, where
N=|\mathscr{M}(f)|
. For an ideal
I\subset{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
we use the shorthand notation
Z(I)=\mathrm{Spec}({\mathbb{C}}[\mathbf{x}]/I)
for the scheme determined by
I
and
Z^{\mathrm{red}}(I)
for the underlying reduced variety. A basic but important observation is that

(52)

Z^{\mathrm{red}}(M(f))\subset Z^{\mathrm{red}}(I(f))

holds for any
f
, which simply says that any polynomial vanishes at those points where all its monomials vanish.

The idea is to blow-up along (irreducible components of) the locus
Z^{\mathrm{red}}(M(f))
instead of the poorly understood
Z^{\mathrm{red}}(I(f)
). To do so, we take the primary decomposition

M(f)=Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}\cap\ldots\cap Q_{l}

of
M(f)
, with primary ideals
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{l}
whose radical ideals are the prime ideals
\mathrm{rad}(Q_{i})=P_{i}
. This corresponds to the decomposition

Z(M(f))=m_{1}Z(P_{1})\cup m_{2}Z(P_{2})\cup\ldots\cup m_{l}Z(P_{l})

of the scheme into irreducible (possibly embedded) reduced components
Z(P_{i})
with multiplicity
m_{i}
. Since
M(f)
is monomial, this decomposition has a particularly nice form:

P_{i}=(\mathscr{C}_{i})\text{ for a cluster of variables }\mathscr{C}_{i}\subset\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}

and
Q_{i}=(\tilde{\mathscr{C}}_{i})
is generated by powers of elements in
\mathscr{C}_{i}
. Hence

Z(P_{i})=Z(\mathscr{C}_{i})=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in{\mathbb{A}}^{m}:x_{j}=0\text{ for }j\in\mathscr{C}_{i}\}

is a coordinate subspace of
{\mathbb{A}}^{m}
. Pick
\mathscr{C}:=\mathscr{C}_{i}
for some
i
, and let

\pi_{\mathscr{C}}:\mathrm{Bl}_{\mathscr{C}}{\mathbb{A}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{m}

denote the blow-up of
{\mathbb{A}}^{m}
at
Z(\mathscr{C})
. Any
c\in\mathscr{C}
defines an affine chart
\mathrm{Bl}_{\mathscr{C},c}{\mathbb{A}}^{m}\subset\mathrm{Bl}_{\mathscr{C}}{\mathbb{A}}^{m}
, and the restriction
\pi_{\mathscr{C},c}=\pi_{\mathscr{C}}|_{\mathrm{Bl}_{\mathscr{C},c}{\mathbb{A}}^{m}}
is defined through

(53)

\pi_{\mathscr{C},c}^{*}(x_{j})=\begin{cases}x_{j}&j\notin\mathscr{C}\setminus\{c\}\\
x_{i}x_{j}&j\in\mathscr{C}\setminus\{c\}\end{cases}

We will abuse notation and will use
x_{j}
for the affine coordinate
\pi_{\mathscr{C},c}^{*}(x_{j})
on
\mathrm{Bl}_{\mathscr{C},c}{\mathbb{A}}^{m}
. This way at each stage and on each affine chart we have the same notation for the coordinates, but we need to follow the simple transformation rule (
53
) at each blow-up. The blow-up of the rational map
f:{\mathbb{A}}^{m}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{r}
is obtained on
\mathrm{Bl}_{\mathscr{C},c}{\mathbb{A}}^{m}
by the substitution (
53
):

(54)

\textstyle{\mathrm{Bl}_{\mathscr{C}}{\mathbb{A}}^{m}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{\mathscr{C}}}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Bl}_{\mathscr{C},c}{\mathbb{A}}^{m}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tilde{f}_{\mathscr{C},c}=f\circ\pi_{\mathscr{C},c}^{*}}
\textstyle{{\mathbb{A}}^{m}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{{\mathbb{P}}^{r}}

Note that all coordinate functions of the polynomial map
f\circ\pi_{\mathscr{C},c}^{*}
is divisible by the variable
c
(or even with some power of
c
), and hence we divide by this to get the polynomial map
\tilde{f}_{\mathscr{C},c}
.

We iterate this blow-up process. Take the primary decomposition of
M(\tilde{f}_{\mathscr{C},c})
and pick a component of the monomial indeterminacy locus
Z(M(\tilde{f}_{\mathscr{C},c}))
corresponding to a cluster (linear subspace)
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
of coordinates. This choice, of course, must be compatible throughout the affine charts, so that we blow-up along the same subvariety in the quasi-projective
\mathrm{Bl}_{\mathscr{C}}{\mathbb{A}}^{m}
. In order to see finiteness of this iterated blow-up process, we need to compare these primary decompositions, and apply

Lemma 11.1
.

Let
Z(M(f))=m_{1}Z(P_{1})\cup m_{2}Z(P_{2})\cup\ldots\cup m_{l}Z(P_{l})
be the primary decomposition and assume
Z(P_{1})=Z(\mathscr{C}_{1})
is an irreducible component (not embedded component). Let
\tilde{f}:\mathrm{Bl}_{\mathscr{C}_{1},c}{\mathbb{A}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
be the blow-up map from the affine chart corresponding to
c\in\mathscr{C}_{1}
. Then each component in the primary decomposition of
Z(M(\tilde{f}))
is either (i) the proper transform of a component of
Z(M(f))
, or (ii) a subspace strictly contained in the proper transform of a component of
Z(M(f))
.

This implies that in each step, at least one component of the primary decomposition vanishes, or it splits into smaller subspaces, hence the blow-up process is finite.

11.2.
Smoothening trick

How long can we continue blowing-up along monomial ideals described above? Iterating the blow-up process, the degree of the coordinate functions of
f=[f_{1},\ldots,f_{r+1}]
eventually become lower, and essentially we arrive to a stage where one of these coordinate functions has a linear term. By a linear change of coordinates we can assume that this linear term is one of
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
, and without loss of generality assume that
f_{1}=x_{1}+q(\mathbf{x})
for some polynomial
q
. Then
x_{1}\in\mathscr{M}(f)
, and therefore all clusters in the primary decomposition contain
x_{1}
. For a cluster
\mathscr{C}
the blown-up map
\tilde{f}_{\mathscr{C},x_{1}}:\mathrm{Bl}_{\mathscr{C},x_{1}}{\mathbb{A}}^{m}\dasharrow{\mathbb{P}}^{r}
has first coordinate function

\tilde{f}_{1}=1+\tilde{q}(\mathbf{x})

which is nonzero if
\tilde{q}=0
, and in this case
\tilde{f}_{\mathscr{C},x_{1}}:\mathrm{Bl}_{\mathscr{C},x_{1}}{\mathbb{A}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{r}
is a morphism. But if
\tilde{q}\neq 0
then
\tilde{f}
is not necessarily defined on the whole affine chart
{\mathbb{A}}^{m}
, and since
1\in\mathscr{M}(\tilde{f})
, the blow-up process terminates. In this situation we need a different blow-up, which we call the
smoothening trick
.

Definition 11.2
.

A rational map
f=[f_{1},\ldots,f_{r+1}]
is called
semi-linear
, if
f_{i}=x_{j}+p(\mathbf{x})
for some
1\leq i\leq r+1
,
1\leq j\leq m
with some polynomial
q
of degree
\deg(q)\geq 2
. We will call such an
f_{i}

linear-headed
.

Let
f=[f_{0},\ldots,f_{r}]:{\mathbb{A}}^{m}\dasharrow{\mathbb{P}}^{r}
be a semi-linear rational map such that

f_{i}(\mathbf{x})=\begin{cases}x_{i}+p_{i}(\mathbf{x})&\text{ for }1\leq i\leq s\\
q_{i}(\mathbf{x})&\text{ for }s+1\leq i\leq r+1\end{cases}

with degree of terminality
1\leq s\leq r+1
, and polynomials
p_{i},q_{i}
such that
\deg(q_{i})\geq 2
for
i=s+1,\ldots,r+1
(note that
p_{i}=0
or
\deg(p_{i})\geq 2
). Let
\mathscr{C}
be a cluster corresponding to an irreducible component of the monomial ideal
M(f)
. Due to the linear terms we necessarily have
x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\in\mathscr{C}
, so

\mathscr{C}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{s},x_{c_{1}},\ldots,x_{c_{l}}\}.

for some
c_{1}>s,\ldots,c_{l}>s
. We define the
semi-monomial ideal

M(f,\mathscr{C})=(x_{1}+p_{1}(\mathbf{x}),\ldots,x_{s}+p_{s}(\mathbf{x}),x_{c_{1}},\ldots,x_{c_{l}}),

by substituting
x_{i}
with the entire coordinate function in
\mathscr{C}
if
x_{i}
appears as a linear factor. At this stage, instead of
\mathrm{Bl}_{\mathscr{C}}{\mathbb{A}}^{m}
we will take
\mathrm{Bl}_{M(f,\mathscr{C})}{\mathbb{A}}^{m}
, and study the blow-up rational map
\tilde{f}
on the various affine charts. Note that

(55)

\mathrm{Bl}_{M(f,\mathscr{C})}{\mathbb{A}}^{m}=\{((x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}),[y_{1}:\ldots,y_{s}:z_{1}:\ldots:z_{l}])\in{\mathbb{A}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{s+l-1}:\\
(x_{i}+p_{i})y_{j}=(x_{j}+p_{j})y_{i},x_{c_{i}}z_{j}=x_{c_{j}}z_{i},x_{c_{j}}y_{i}=(x_{i}+p_{i})z_{j}\}

is not necessarily smooth, but nevertheless, the blown-up rational map
\tilde{f}:\mathrm{Bl}_{M(f,\mathscr{C})}{\mathbb{A}}^{m}\dasharrow{\mathbb{P}}^{r}
is well-defined on the affine charts
U(y_{i})=\{y_{i}\neq 0\}
, and more precisely, we will use the following

Lemma 11.3
.

The
i
th projective coordinate of any point in
\tilde{f}|_{U(y_{i})}:U(y_{i})\to{\mathbb{P}}^{r}
is nonzero.

Remark 15
.

As an immediate corollary we will see that the torus fixed-points in
f(U(y_{i}))\subset{\mathbb{P}}(\wedge^{k}\mathrm{Sym}^{\leq k}{\mathbb{C}}^{n})
in our setup will have nonzero
i
th coordinate, and hence the image of most of these affine charts will not contain the distinguished torus fixed-point of Residue Vanishing Theorem, and hence the contribution of these affine charts to the Thom polynomial formula is zero.

Now let’s focus on the other affine charts
U(z_{i})\subset\mathrm{Bl}_{M(f,\mathscr{C})}{\mathbb{A}}^{m}
and the blown-up maps
\tilde{f}_{z_{i}}:=\tilde{f}|_{U(z_{i})}:U(z_{i})\to{\mathbb{P}}^{r}
. Fix
i=1
, then

U(z_{1})=\{((x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}),(y_{1},\ldots,y_{s},z_{2},\ldots,z_{l}))\in{\mathbb{A}}^{m}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{s+l-1}:x_{c_{i}}=x_{c_{1}}z_{i},(x_{i}+p_{i})=y_{i}x_{c_{1}}\}

The presentation of
\tilde{f}_{z_{1}}
is not unique in these affine coordinates:

(56)

\tilde{f}_{z_{1}}=[x_{1}+p_{1}(\mathbf{x}),\ldots x_{s}+p_{s}(\mathbf{x}),q_{s+1}(\mathbf{x}),\ldots,q_{r+1}(\mathbf{x})]=[y_{1},\ldots,y_{s},\hat{q}_{s+1}(\mathbf{x}),\ldots,\hat{q}_{r+1}(\mathbf{x})]

where
\hat{q}_{i}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{x_{c_{1}}}q_{i}(\hat{x}_{1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{m})
with

\hat{x}_{i}=\begin{cases}x_{i}&\text{ if }x_{i}\notin\{c_{2},\ldots,c_{l}\}\\
x_{c_{1}}x_{i}&\text{ if }i\in\{c_{2},\ldots,c_{l}\}\end{cases}

Since
\mathscr{C}
covers all monomials of
f
, at least one term of each monomial in
q_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s},\hat{x}_{s+1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{m})
is divisible by
x_{c_{1}}
, and hence
\hat{q}_{i}(\mathbf{x})
is a polynomial in
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
. Now we would like to write
\hat{q}_{i}(\xi)
as a polynomial in
x_{1}+p_{1},\ldots,x_{s}+p_{s},x_{s+1},\ldots,x_{m}
, but this is, of course, not necessarily possible, as the non-linear change of coordinates

(57)

\xi_{i}=\begin{cases}x_{i}+p_{i}(\mathbf{x})&1\leq i\leq s\\
x_{i}&s+1\leq i\leq m\end{cases}

is not globally invertible on
{\mathbb{A}}^{m}
. However, it is invertible in any finite jet space, that is, up to high order polynomials. Assume
p_{1}\neq 0
and hence
\deg(p_{1})\geq 2
, then for any positive integer
d
one can iteratively write

(58)

\displaystyle x_{1}=\xi_{1}-p_{1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s},x_{s+1},\ldots,x_{m})=\xi_{1}-p_{1}(\xi_{1}-p_{1}(\mathbf{x}),\ldots,\xi_{s}-p_{s}(\mathbf{x}),\xi_{s+1},\ldots,\xi_{m})=\ldots=

\displaystyle=\xi_{1}+p_{1}^{\leq d}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m})+q_{1}^{\geq d}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m},x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}))

for some polynomials

with

. The

is the following: we replace

with its

and think of

as

of
x_{i}
. We aim to replace the original

with an approximation

, such that the latter is not a semi-linear map, and hence the monomial blow-up process proceeds. Take the diagram

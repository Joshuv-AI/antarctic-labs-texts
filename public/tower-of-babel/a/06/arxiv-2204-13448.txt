Spin of protons in NICA and PTR storage rings as an axion antenna
Nikolai N. Nikolaev

Abstract
We discuss a new approach to search for axions in the storage ring experiments, applicable at short coherence time of the in-plane polarization as is the case for protons. The technique can readily be applied at any storage ring equipped with internal polarimeter for the radial polarization of the beam (COSY, NICA, PTR). We point out a possibility of substantial broadening of the range of attianable axion masses in storage rings with the hybrid electric and magnetic bending, the PTR proton storage ring being an example.



Introduction

There is a growing interest in the spin of particles as a highly sensitive spin resonance detector of cosmic axions and axion-like particles. Axions, first proposed by Peccei and Quinn in 1977 [1]
as a solution to strong CP-violation in QCD, are widely discussed as a plausible candidate for the dark matter. One of manifestations of the cold galactic halo axions wil be a NMR-like resonant rotation of the spin in the oscillationg axion field [2-6].
Here the spin serves as an axion antenna and the experimental search by the JEDI coillaboration of the axion signal with polarized deuterons in the storage ring COSY is in progress [7].
Since the axion mass is unknown, one is bound to spin precession frequency scanning. In the JEDI experiment one looks for a buildup of the vertical polarization from the idly precessing in-plane polarization when the resonance condition is met during a scan.

Inherent to the JEDI technique is a need for a long coherence time of the in-plane precessing spin. For instance, it can not be easily extended to protons with arguably short spin coherence time. Besides that, the axion signal depends on the unknown difference of phases of the axion field and idle spin precession. In this communication we suggest the alternative scheme which is free of these limitations and looks preferred
one for searches for axions at the Nuclotron, NICA [8] and PTR [9] storage rings. All one needs is an internal polarimetry which both the Nuclotron and NICA proper will be equipped with, and there is no need in the radofrequency spin rotators. We also comment on exclusive features of the spin frequency scan in hybrid rings with concurrent electric and magnetic bending, the PRT stotrage ring proposed by the CPEDM collaboration being a good example.

A comprehensive introduction into the physics of axions is found in reviews [4,10-12],
here we only mention the principal points of importance to our discussion, alongside the basics of the spin precession in storage rings [10-15].
When the angular frequency of the axion field is about three times the expansion rate of the Universe, coherent oscillations of the cold cosmological axion field do establish [16-18]. Attributing the local energy density of the dark matter
\displaystyle\rho_{\rm DM}\approx 400
MeV/cm
3
[19]
to axions in the invisible halo of our Galaxy, the amplitude of the classical axion field
\displaystyle a(x)=a_{0}\cos\left(\omega_{a}t-{\bm{k}}_{a}\cdot{\bm{x}}\right)
can be evaluated as [2,20]

a_{0}=\frac{1}{m_{a}}\,\sqrt{\frac{2\rho_{\rm DM}\hbar}{c^{3}}}\,.

(1)

Of key importance is Weinberg’s gradient interaction of axions with fermions [21],

L_{a\bar{\psi}\psi}=-\,{\frac{1}{2f_{a}}}\,g_{f}\,\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}\psi\,\partial_{\mu}a(x)\,,

(2)

which can be reinterpreted as an interaction of fermion’s spin with the pseudomagnetic field [22].
The dimensionless constant
\displaystyle g_{f}\sim 1
depends on the specific model, and Weinberg derived the relation,

m_{a}\approx m_{\pi}\frac{f_{\pi}}{f_{a}}\frac{\sqrt{m_{u}m_{d}}}{m_{u}+m_{d}}\,,

(3)

where
\displaystyle m_{\pi}
and
\displaystyle f_{\pi}
are the pion mass and decay constant, and
\displaystyle m_{u,d}
are mases of light quarks. Still another manifestation of the galactic axion field is the
oscillating contribution to the electric dipole moment (EDM) of nucleons [2,23],

\begin{split}d_{N}^{\rm ax}(x)={\frac{a(x)}{f_{a}}}\cdot{\frac{\mu_{N}}{c}}\kappa_{a}\,,\end{split}

(4)

where
\displaystyle\mu_{N}
is the nuclear magneton and the small factor

\begin{split}\kappa_{(a)}\sim\frac{m_{d}m_{u}}{\Lambda_{QCD}(m_{d}+m_{u})}\approx 10^{-2}\end{split}

(5)

describes the chiral suppression of the EDM by small masses of light quarks [24,25].

We consider storage rings with crossed magnetic and electric bending fields. The cyclotron angular velocity equals

\bm{\Omega}_{c}={\frac{q}{m\gamma}}\left(-{\,\bm{B}}+\frac{{\bm{v}}\times{\bm{E}}}{v^{2}}\right)\,.

(6)

The contributions to the spin rotation with respect to the particle momentum,
\displaystyle\bm{\Omega}_{s}=\bm{\Omega}_{s}^{\rm mdm}+\bm{\Omega}_{s}^{\rm edm}
, due to the particle’s magnetic dipole moment (MDM),
\displaystyle\mu=g\mu_{N}
, and the electric dipole moment (EDM),
\displaystyle d
, is given by the generalized BMT equations [15-17]:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bm{\Omega}_{s}^{\rm mdm}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\frac{q}{m}}\left\{-\,G\,\bm{B}+\left(G-{\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}-1}}\right){\frac{\bm{v}\times\bm{E}}{c^{2}}}\right\}\,,

(7)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bm{\Omega}_{s}^{\rm edm}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle-d\{\bm{E}+\bm{v}\times\bm{B}\}\,.{}

(8)

Here
\displaystyle G=(g-2)/2
is the magnetic anomaly,
\displaystyle{\bm{v}}
and
\displaystyle\gamma
are particle’s velocity and the relativistic
\displaystyle\gamma
-factor, and the spin tune,
\displaystyle\nu_{s}
, is defined via
\displaystyle{\bm{\Omega}}_{s}^{\rm mdm}=\nu_{s}{\bm{\Omega}}_{c}
.

Axion effects in all magnetic rings

We consider first all magnetic storage rings,
\displaystyle{\bm{E}}=0
, when
\displaystyle\nu_{s}=G\gamma
. The salient features of spin as an axion antenna are as follows. First of all, in the comoving frame (cmf) the axion field induced EDM interacts with the motional electric field

{\bm{E}}_{cmf}=[{\bm{v}}\times{\bm{B}}]=-(m\gamma/q)\cdot[\vec{v}\times{\bm{\Omega}}_{c}]\,.

(9)

As a spin rotator, it is tantamount to the radiofrequency Wien filter [26]
with the radial magnetic field, operated at the axion field angular velocity

\omega_{a}=\frac{m_{a}c^{2}}{\hbar}\,.

(10)

Secondly, spin interacts with the oscillating pseudomagnetic field, proportional to the particle velocity and the time derivative
\displaystyle\partial_{t}a(x)
[22],
tangential to the particle orbit and acting as a radiofreqency solenoid. Finally, the velocity of particles in a storage ring,
\displaystyle v
, is of the order of the velocity of light and

\displaystyle v\approx 10^{3}v_{a}
, where
\displaystyle v_{a}\sim 250
km/s is the velocity of Earth’s motion in Galaxy. Hence the pseudomagnetic field acting on the spin of stored particles is three orders in magnitude stronger compared to that acting on, for instance, the spin of ultracold neutrons
[27,28].

At the axion resonance
\displaystyle\omega_{a}=\nu_{s}\Omega_{c}
, and the instantaneous angular velocity of the axion-driven resonant spin rotation, derived by Silenko [28],
takes the form

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \bm{\Omega}_{\rm res}=\frac{a_{0}}{f_{a}}\,\Bigl(&g_{f}\omega_{a}\sin(\omega_{a}t){\frac{\bm{v}}{c}}\\
&-\,\kappa_{a}\gamma\cos(\omega_{a}t)\Bigl[\frac{\bm{v}}{c}\times\bm{\Omega}_{c}\Bigr]\Bigr)\,.\end{aligned}

(11)

The phases of two spin rotators differ by
\displaystyle\pi/2
, but they do rotate spin about orthogonal axes. Hence upon solving the generalized BMT equations by the Bogoliubov-Krylov averaging [29,30],
one finds the angular velocity of the resonant up-down rotation of the spin envelope

\Omega_{res}={\frac{a_{0}}{2f_{a}}}\,{\frac{\gamma v}{c}}\,\,|\,g_{f}G-\kappa_{a}\,|\,\Omega_{c}\,.

(12)

Note a strong enhancement of the contribution from the pseudomagnetic field by the factor
\displaystyle g_{f}G/\kappa_{a}\gg 1
compared to the contribution from the axion driven EDM. In the early simulations of operation of the storage-ring spin antenna as an axion detector, the contribution of this pseudomagnetic field was not taken into account in [7]
and the actual sensitivity of the JEDI experiment was greatly underestimated.

Frequency scan search for the axion signal

From now on we focus on the case of the initial vertical polarization
\displaystyle P_{y}
.
At a fixed
\displaystyle\omega_{a}
, we parameterize a variation of the spin precession angular velocity as
\displaystyle\Omega_{s}(t)=\omega_{a}+2\Omega_{t}^{2}t
, where
\displaystyle 2\Omega_{t}^{2}=d\Omega_{s}/dt
. Our convention for time is such that a scan starts at negative time
\displaystyle t=-t_{0}
and the exact resonance takes place at
\displaystyle t=0
. The spin phase during the scan varies as

\theta_{s}(t)=\omega_{a}t+\Omega_{t}^{2}t^{2}

(13)

and duration of the scan is such that
\displaystyle\Omega_{t}^{2}t_{0}^{2}\gg 2\pi
.
A sensitivity to weak axion signal depends on the relationship between the spin coherence time,
\displaystyle\tau_{SCT}=1/\Gamma
, and the speed of the spin precession frequency scan.
Specifically, it is easy to derive the time dependence of the axion-driven in-plane polarization envelope
\displaystyle P_{xz}
:

P_{xz}(t)=P_{y}\Omega_{res}\int_{-t_{0}}^{t}d\tau\mathop{\rm exp}\nolimits\left[-\Gamma(t-\tau)\right]\cos(\Omega_{t}^{2}\tau^{2})\,.

(14)

In the usually discussed scheme with the in-plane initial polarization [6,7,31,32],
the axion signal will be proportional to
\displaystyle\sin\Delta
[29,33],
where
\displaystyle\Delta
is an entirely unknon difference of the spin presession and axion oscillation phases. As a practical remedy, it was proposed to fill the ring with four bunches of different polarizations [6,7].
In contrast to that, a buildup of the in-plane polarization from the initial vertical one is free of this phase ambiguity.

Numerical evaluation of the integral (
14
) is not a problem, here we proceed to simple analytic evaluations, leaving aside the issue of numerical simulations. First we start with the limit of large spin coherence time,

\displaystyle\Gamma\ll\Omega_{t}
, when the exponential damping factor in the integrand can be neglected, what is the case for stored deuterons. Then the envelope
\displaystyle P_{xz}
will exhibit a jump of temporal width
\displaystyle t_{1}\sim 1/\Omega_{t}
and the amplitude

\displaystyle P_{xz}^{max}\sim P_{y}\Omega_{res}/\Omega_{t}
. The observed signal will be a jump in the up- down asymmetry in the polarimeter oscillating with the spin precession frequency. A Fourier analysis technique for extraction of the spin envelope form such an asymmetry data has been developed by the JEDI collaboration [34,35].

The opposite limiting case of
\displaystyle\Gamma\gg\Omega_{t}
is of interest for stored protons which, because of the large magnetic anomaly, will arguably have a short spin coherence time [34].
Here emerges a a new time scale

t_{2}\sim\frac{\Gamma}{\Omega_{t}^{2}}\,.

(15)

Algebraically at times
\displaystyle-t_{2}<t<t_{2}
the envelope
\displaystyle P_{xz}
will oscillate with constant amplitude,

P_{x,z}\approx\frac{\Omega_{res}}{\Gamma}P_{y}\cos(\Omega_{t}^{2}\tau^{2})\,,

(16)

while oscillatios will vanish beyond this interval. The practical Fourier analysis of the up-down asymmetry from the in-plane precessing polarization returns the envelope as a positive defined quanity, and the axion signal will look like a corrugated plateau of the height
\displaystyle|P_{xz}|\sim\Omega_{res}/\Gamma
and width
\displaystyle t_{2}
.

A lesson from searches for resonances is that at similar statistics broad resonances are much harder to be identified. However, in our case a salient feature of the signal (
16
) is a central peak of width
\displaystyle\sim 1/\Omega_{t}
, the peaks at wings will have a spacing
\displaystyle\sim\tau_{SCT}
and can be resolved if sufficient statistics can be accumulated during the spin coherence time. Fits to the well specified function (
16
) will facilitate inentification of the axion signal. Furthermore, the central peak will be better visible at faster ramping of the cyclotron frequency such that

\frac{d\Omega_{c}}{dt}\sim\Gamma^{2}\,.

(17)

Still another option is to run the proton ring at magic energies with strong suppression of the spin decoherence as was suggested in [36]. These magic energies are roots of the equation

\displaystyle 1-\frac{c^{2}}{v^{2}}\cdot\Bigl(1+\frac{K}{G\gamma}\Bigr)\cdot\Bigl(\frac{1}{\gamma_{tr}^{2}}-\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\Bigr)=0\,,\quad K=\pm 1,\pm 2,...

(18)

exact position of magic energies depends on the transition
\displaystyle\gamma
-factor,
\displaystyle\gamma_{tr}
, of the particular ring. For instance, at COSY the roots exist at negative
\displaystyle K
. The lowest magic
kinetic energy for protons is
\displaystyle T_{p}\approx 30
MeV at
\displaystyle K=-2
, and as an axion antenna it will be tuned to search for axions with

\displaystyle\omega_{a}=(2-G_{p})\Omega_{c}
, where
\displaystyle G_{p}=1.793
is the magnetic anomaly of the proton. The next magic energy at
\displaystyle K=-3
is
\displaystyle T_{p}\approx 130
MeV, and COSY the will be tuned to
\displaystyle\omega_{a}=(3-G_{p})\Omega_{c}
. At still higher magic energy
\displaystyle T_{p}\approx 210
MeV one will probe
\displaystyle\omega_{a}=(4-G_{p})\Omega_{c}
. Note that
\displaystyle\Omega_{c}
scales with
\displaystyle\propto\gamma/\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}
.

Axion effects in hybrid storage rings

Note that in all magnetic fields the resonance condition entails
\displaystyle\omega_{a}=p/(Rm)
, where
\displaystyle R
is the ring radius and
\displaystyle p
is the particle momentum. Consequently, as an axion antenna the attainable axion masses are bounded from below by the minimal momentum the storage ring can run at.
The hybrid ring with concurrent magnetic and electric bendings are much more versatile compared to all magnetic rings. An example is provided by the prototype test ring PTR proposed by the CPEDM collaboration [9].
With the radial electric field
\displaystyle E_{0}=7\times 10^{6}
V/m complemented by the vertical magnetic field
\displaystyle B_{0}=0.0327\ {\rm T}
, PTR will provide the frozen spin of protons,

\displaystyle{\bm{\Omega}}_{s}^{\text{m}dm}=0
, it i.e.,
\displaystyle\nu_{s}=0.
. Beyond this point, the electric and magnetic fields must be varied synchronously to preserve the injection energy, the orbit radius and the cyclotron frequency,

\Delta{\bm{B}}=\frac{1}{v^{2}}[{\bm{v}}\times\Delta{\bm{E}}]\,,

(19)

see Eq. (
6
). Then according to Eq. (
7
) the spin tune will vary as

\nu_{s}=-G_{p}\gamma\frac{\Delta E}{E_{0}}\,,

(20)

taking the canonical value,
\displaystyle\nu_{s}=G\gamma
, in the all-magnetic limit of
\displaystyle\Delta E=-E_{0}
. In the same range the magnetic field must be raised concurrently to

B=\frac{G_{p}+1}{1-G_{p}\gamma^{2}\beta^{2}}B_{0}=3.39B_{0}\,.

(21)

The practically attainable range of magnetic fields will depend on the design of the air coil. We emphasize that arguably the spin coherence time
\displaystyle\tau_{SCT}
scales with
\displaystyle 1/\nu_{s}^{2}
[36], which specifically favors operation of the hybrid PTR as an axion antenna to search for small mass axions.

Note that at the fixed orbit of the beam the electric field in the comoving frame, acting on proton’s EDM, does not depend on
\displaystyle\Delta E
, while the axion resonance will take place at

\omega_{a}=-G_{p}\gamma\Omega_{c}\frac{\Delta E}{E_{0}}\,.

(22)

The resulting angular velocity of the axion-driven spin rotation will be given by

\Omega_{res}={\frac{a_{0}}{2f_{(a)}}}\,{\frac{\gamma v}{c}}\,|\,g_{f}G\frac{\Delta E}{E_{0}}+\kappa_{(a)}\,|\,\Omega_{c}\,,

(23)

with the pseudomagnetic field contribution suppressed by the factor
\displaystyle\Delta E/E_{0}
. Increasing the electic field in delectors must be viewed impractical.

A hybrig storage ring operating as a broadband axion antenna at a fixed beam energy has several advantages. We mention injection at a fixed energy, running the radiofrequency cavity in a narrow range of frequencies, a polarimetry optimized for one fixed energy.

Summary

We demonstrated how the spin of polarized protons can be used as an axion antenna in spite of the short spin coherence time of the in-plane polarization of protons. The key point is to look for a buildup of the idly precessing in-plane polarization starting from the stable verical one. Of special interest is a possibility to enhance a sensitivity to axions working at selected beam energies with strong suppression of spin decoherence effects. Hybrid prototype rings emerge as a promising proton spin
antenna sensitive to axions with small masses up to few neV/c
2
. These observations suggest new options for the experimental searches for axions at NICA in Dubna, COSY in Juelich, planned PTR and elsewhere.
We emphasize that our approach can readily be extended to the hydrid deuteron storage rings.

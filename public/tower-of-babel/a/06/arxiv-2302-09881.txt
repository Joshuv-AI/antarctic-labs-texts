Ordinal measures of the set of finite multisets
Isa Vialard

Abstract
Well-partial orders, and the ordinal invariants used to measure them, are relevant in set theory, program verification, proof theory and many other areas of computer science and mathematics. In this article we focus on one of the most common data structure in programming, the finite multiset of some wpo. There are two natural orders one can define on the set of finite multisets M ⁡ ( X ) M(X) of a partial order X X : the multiset embedding and the multiset ordering, for which M ⁡ ( X ) M(X) remains a wpo when X X is. Though the maximal order type of these orders is already known, the other ordinal invariants remain mostly unknown. Our main contributions are expressions to compute compositionally the width of the multiset embedding and the height of the multiset ordering. Furthermore, we provide a new ordinal invariant useful for characterizing the width of the multiset ordering.

Introduction

Measuring partial orders
intervene in many domains, from set theory to proof theory, including infinitary combinatorics, program verification, rewriting theory, proof automation and many more.

There are intuitive notions of measure for a partial order when it is finite: its cardinal obviously, but also its height (the length of a maximal chain) or its width (the length of a maximal antichain).
Similar notions exist for infinite partial orders, as long as they are
well partial orders
(wpo), i.e., well-founded partial orders with no infinite antichains
[
10
,
12
]
.
Two such notions are the
height
, which is the order type of a maximal chain, and the
maximal order type
(m.o.t.), which is the order type of a maximal linearisation, a notion introduced by De Jongh and Parikh in order to measure hierarchies of functions
[
6
]
. These are transfinite measures, hence we call them
ordinal invariants
.
Kříž and Thomas introduced equivalent characterizations for m.o.t. and height, which naturally led to the definition of a third ordinal invariant,
width

[
11
]
. Less studied than its counterparts, the width of a wpo relates to its antichains, even though it cannot be defined as the order type of a maximal antichain. While exploring techniques for program termination, Blass and Gurevich developed these characterizations into a game-theoretical point of view which gives useful intuitions
[
4
]
.

Ordinal invariants have proven helpful to establish program termination or to analyze well-structured transition systems (wsts)
[
5
]
, i.e., transition systems whose set of configurations is a wpo and whose transitions respect this ordering. The study of wsts through controlled bad sequences
[
9
]
, and sometimes controlled antichains
[
14
]
, shows obvious connections between the complexity of verification problems on wsts and the m.o.t. and width of the underlying wpo.

Computing ordinal invariants compositionally
is paramount, as most wpos one might want to measure are built from classical operations on simpler wpos whose invariants are known.
De Jongh and Parikh computed the m.o.t. of the disjoint sum and the cartesian product of wpos
[
6
]
. Schmidt then computed the m.o.t. of word embedding and homeomorphic tree embedding on a wpo
[
13
]
. Abraham and Bonnet pursued this line of study by computing the height of cartesian product, but also the width of disjoint sum and lexicographic product
[
1
]
.
For a complete survey of these results, see
[
8
]
, where Džamonja et al. computed the ordinal invariants of the lexicographic product, but also the height of the multiset word and tree embeddings.

Finite multisets:
In this article, we study the ordinal invariants of the set of finite multisets. Multisets are one of computer science’s most common data structures, especially in rewriting theory. Informally, a finite multiset over a set
X
is a finite subset of
X
where an element can appear finitely many times.
One can see the set of finite multisets on a wpo as the set of finite words quotiented by the equivalence relation “equality up to some permutation”. It comes down to describing a multiset as a word where the order of terms is irrelevant.
A finite multiset can be represented by a function from
X
to
\mathbb{N}
with finite support, which associates each element with its multiplicity.

Two orderings are classically defined on the finite multisets of any ordered set.
The first one is the
multiset ordering
. The intuition behind this ordering is that, from a multiset
m
, one can build a smaller multiset by removing an element
x
from
m
, and replacing it with an arbitrary number of elements smaller than
x
. The multiset ordering often appears in rewriting theory and automation of termination proofs
[
7
]
.

A perhaps more natural, but less known, ordering on the set of finite multisets is the
multiset embedding
,
or term ordering as it is called in
[
15
]
. It was presented by Aschenbrenner and Pong as a natural extension of the embedding order over finite words
[
3
]
.

Some invariants of these two orderings have already been measured:
Van der Meeren, Rathjen, and Weiermann
[
15
]
built on
[
17
]
to compute the m.o.t. of the set of finite multisets on a wpo
X
ordered with the multiset ordering, and provided a new proof for the expression of the m.o.t of the multiset embedding computed in
[
18
]
. Džamonja et al.
[
8
]
proved that the height of the multiset embedding is equal to the height of the set of finite words ordered with word embedding. It is noteworthy that these three results give expressions that are functional in the m.o.t. and height of
X
.
However, the height of invariants of the multiset ordering still needs to be determined, and the width remains unstudied for both orderings.

Our contributions:
In this article, we provide functional expressions for the width of the multiset embedding (
Theorem

20
) and the height of the multiset ordering (
Theorem

26
).

We further show that the width of the multiset ordering is not functional in any of the three ordinal invariants (
Example

27
). Nonetheless, we get around this issue by introducing a fourth ordinal invariant, the
maximal safe order type
(
Definition

31
), in which the width is functional (
Theorem

33
).

Definitions and state of the art

1.1
Width, height and maximal order type

We suppose that the reader is familiar with ordinal arithmetic. Otherwise, the notions used in this article can be found in the appendix.

A sequence
x_{1},\dots,x_{n},\dots
on a partial order is
good
when there exist
i<j
such that
x_{i}\leq x_{j}
, otherwise it is a
bad
sequence. A sequence is an
antichain
when all its elements are pairwise incomparable (i.e.
x_{i}\perp x_{j}
for any
i<j
).

A
well partial order (wpo)
is a partial order that has no infinite bad sequences. Equivalently, a wpo is a partial order that is both well-founded (i.e. no infinite strictly decreasing sequences) and has no infinite antichains (i.e. sequences of pairwise incomparable elements).

Let
X
be a wpo.
We define
{\textit{Bad}}(X)
,
{\textit{Dec}}(X)
and
{\textit{Ant}}(X)
as the trees of bad sequences, strictly decreasing sequences, and antichains of
X
, respectively, ordered by inverse prefix order (a sequence is smaller than its prefixes)
The finiteness of bad sequences, strictly decreasing sequences and antichains in a wpo implies that these trees are well-founded. Therefore, one can define a notion of rank: a sequence has rank
0
when it cannot be extended; otherwise its rank is the smallest ordinal strictly larger than the ranks of smaller sequences (by inverse prefix order).

The
maximal order type
(or m.o.t.) of
X
, denoted with
\bm{o}(X)
, is defined as the rank of the empty sequence in
{\textit{Bad}}(X)
.
Similarly, the
height

\bm{h}(X)
and the
width

\bm{w}(X)
of
X
are defined as the ranks of the empty sequence in
{\textit{Dec}}(X)
and
{\textit{Ant}}(X)
, respectively.
Together,
\bm{o}(X)
,
\bm{h}(X)
and
\bm{w}(X)
are called the
ordinal invariants
of
X
.

Since the rank of the empty sequence is the smallest ordinal strictly larger than the ranks of the sequences of length
1
, these definitions can be reformulated inductively through the following
residual equations
:

\displaystyle\bm{o}(X)

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in X}\left(\bm{o}(X_{\not\geq x})+1\right)

(Res-o)

\displaystyle\bm{h}(X)

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in X}\left(\bm{h}(X_{<x})+1\right)

(Res-h)

\displaystyle\bm{w}(X)

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in X}\left(\bm{w}(X_{\perp x})+1\right)

(Res-w)

X_{*x}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\{\;y\in X\;:\;y*x\;\}
for
*\in\{\;\not\geq,<,\perp\;\}
is what is called a residual of
X
.

Since
{\textit{Dec}}(X)
and
{\textit{Ant}}(X)
are embedded in
{\textit{Bad}}(X)
, we know
\bm{h}(X)\leq\bm{o}(X)
and
\bm{w}(X)\leq\bm{o}(X)
. Kříž and Thomas found that the m.o.t can also be bounded by the width and the height
[
11
]
:

Theorem 1

(Height-Width Theorem)
.

For any wpo
A
,

•

\bm{o}(A)\leq\bm{h}(A)\otimes\bm{w}(A)
.

•

Furthermore, if
\bm{o}(A)
is multiplicatively indecomposable (see Appendix) and
\bm{h}(A)<\bm{o}(A)
(resp.
\bm{w}(A)<\bm{o}(A)
) then
\bm{w}(A)=\bm{o}(A)
(resp.
\bm{h}(A)=\bm{o}(A)
).

1.2
Ordinal invariants of basic data structures

In computer science, sums and products of wpos are the most basic data structures one can find. Their ordinal invariants are easy to compute compositionally, with the notable exception of the width of the cartesian product which is not functional
[
16
]
. In this article, these structures will often be used as examples, or appear in proofs.

Lemma 2

(Disjoint sum
[
6
,
1
,
8
]
)
.

Let
P,Q
be two wpos. The
disjoint sum

P\sqcup Q
is the disjoint union of
P
and
Q
ordered such that for all
p\in P,q\in Q
,
p\perp q
.
It is a wpo and:

•

o(P\sqcup Q)=\bm{o}(P)\oplus\bm{o}(Q)
,

•

w(P\sqcup Q)=\bm{w}(P)\oplus\bm{w}(Q)
.

•

h(P\sqcup Q)=\max(\bm{h}(P),\bm{h}(Q))
.

Lemma 3

(Lexicographic sum
[
8
]
)
.

Let
P,Q
be two wpos. The
lexicographic sum

P+Q
is the disjoint union of
P
and
Q
ordered such that for all
p\in P,q\in Q
,
p\leq q
.
Ti is a wpo and:

•

\bm{o}(P+Q)=\bm{o}(P)+\bm{o}(Q)
,

•

\bm{w}(P+Q)=\max(\bm{w}(P),\bm{w}(Q))
,

•

\bm{h}(P+Q)=\bm{h}(P)+\bm{h}(Q)
.

Lemma 4

(Cartesian product
[
6
]
)
.

Let
P,Q
be two wpos. The
cartesian product

P\times Q
, where elements are compared component-wise,
is a wpo and:

•

\bm{o}(P\times Q)=\bm{o}(P)\otimes\bm{o}(Q)
,

•

\bm{h}(P\times Q)=\sup\{\;\alpha\oplus\beta+1\;:\;\alpha<\bm{h}(P),\beta<\bm{h}(Q)\;\}
.

Lemma 5

(Lexicographic product
[
8
]
)
.

Let
P,Q
be two wpos. The
lexicographic product
of
P
along
Q
, written
P\cdot Q
, has the same support as
P\times Q
, and but is ordered differently:

(p,q)\leq_{P\cdot Q}(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})\quad\text{ iff }\quad q<_{Q}q^{\prime}\text{ , or }q=q^{\prime}\text{ and }p\leq_{P}p^{\prime}\;.

It is a wpo, and:

•

\bm{o}(P\cdot Q)=\bm{o}(P)\cdot\bm{o}(Q)
,

•

\bm{w}(P\cdot Q)=\bm{w}(P)\odot\bm{w}(Q)
.

See the appendix for definitions of the natural sum
\oplus
and product
\otimes
, as well as for the lesser-known Hessenberg-based product
\odot
.

1.3
Comparing wpos

A widely-used and intuitive relation between wpos is the
reflection
relation. A mapping between wpos

f\colon(A,\leq_{A})\rightarrow(B,\leq_{B})

is a
reflection
if
f(x)\leq_{B}f(y)
implies
x\leq_{A}y
, i.e. it is a morphism
from
(A,\not\leq_{A})
to
(B,\not\leq_{B})

When there is a reflection from
A
to
B
, we note
A{\hookrightarrow}B
.

However, in this article, we prefer to use the stronger notions of augmentations and substructures.

Definition 6

(Substructure, augmentation)
.

A wpo
(A,\leq_{A})
is a
substructure
of a wpo
(B,\leq_{B})

whenever
A\subseteq B
and
\leq_{A}
is the restriction of
\leq_{B}

to
A
. This relation is written
A\leq_{\text{st}}B
.
Similarly
(A,\leq_{A})
is an
augmentation
of
(B,\leq_{B})

whenever
A=B
and
{\leq_{B}}\subseteq{\leq_{A}}
.
We write this relation
A\geq_{\text{aug}}B
.

Obviously,
A\leq_{\text{st}}B
or
A\geq_{\text{aug}}B
imply
A{\hookrightarrow}B
.

We denote with
A\equiv B
that
(A,\leq_{A})
is isomorphic to
(B,\leq_{B})
.

We often abuse these notations and write
A\leq_{\text{st}}B
(resp.
B\leq_{\text{aug}}A
) to mean that
A
is isomorphic to a substructure (resp. an augmentation) of
B
.

Example 7
.

It is well-known that the height and the m.o.t. of a wpo
X
are reached by the order type of a maximal chain and a maximal linearisation of
X
, respectively. Therefore
\bm{h}(X)\leq_{\text{st}}X
and
\bm{o}(X)\geq_{\text{aug}}X
.

In this article, when we consider a subset
Y
of a wpo
X
, it is understood that
Y\leq_{\text{st}}X
, i.e.
Y
is ordered with
\leq_{X}
restricted to the subset.

These notions allow us to compare the ordinal invariants of wpos.

Lemma 8
.

Let
A
and
B
be wpos.

If
A\leq_{\text{st}}B
then
\bm{i}(A)\leq\bm{i}(B)
for
\bm{i}\in\{\;\bm{o},\bm{h},\bm{w}\;\}
.

If
A\geq_{\text{aug}}B
then
\bm{o}(A)\leq\bm{o}(B)
and
\bm{w}(A)\leq\bm{w}(B)
. However
\bm{h}(A)\geq\bm{h}(B)
.

The substructure and augmentation relations are monotonous through most operations on wpos.

1.4
Orderings on the set of finite multiset

We note
\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
the finite multiset that contains the elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
. The symbols
\cup,\cap
and
\setminus
denote the union, the intersection and the subtraction, respectively. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
m\times n
means the union of
n
copy of
m
.
For instance, let
m=\langle 1,1,1,2,2,3\rangle
and
m^{\prime}=\langle 1,1,2,4\rangle
. Then
m\cup m^{\prime}=\langle 1,1,1,1,1,2,2,2,3,4\rangle
, and
m\cap m^{\prime}=\langle 1,1,2\rangle
. Furthermore,
m\setminus(m\cap m^{\prime})=\langle 1,2,3\rangle
, and
m^{\prime}\times 2=\langle 1,1,1,1,2,2,4,4\rangle

We denote with
|m|
the number of elements of a multiset
m
.

There are two main orderings classically defined on the set of finite multisets
M(X)
of a partial order
X
:

Definition 9

(multiset embedding)
.

M^{\diamond}(X)=(M(X),\leq_{\diamond})
is ordered with the
multiset embedding
, also known as the term ordering:

m\leq_{\diamond}m^{\prime}
iff there exists
f:m\rightarrow m^{\prime}
injective such that for any
x\in m
,
x\leq f(x)
.

Definition 10

(Multiset ordering)
.

M^{r}(X)=(M(X),\leq_{r})
is ordered with the
multiset ordering
:

m\leq_{r}m^{\prime}\iff m=m^{\prime}\text{ or }\forall x\in m\setminus(m\cap m^{\prime}),\exists y\in m^{\prime}\setminus(m\cap m^{\prime}),x<y\;.

The multiset ordering and the multiset embedding are both augmentations of the word embedding on
X^{*}
. Therefore, according to Higman’s lemma
[
10
]
,
M^{\diamond}(X)
and
M^{r}(X)
are wpos when
X
is.

The main differences between these two ordering are that the multiset embedding gives a lot of importance to the size of a multiset (for any multisets
m,m^{\prime}
,
m\leq_{\diamond}m^{\prime}
implies
|m|\leq|m^{\prime}|
), whereas with the multiset ordering one element can dominate many. For instance,
\langle 1\rangle\geq_{r}\langle 0\rangle\cdot 100
in
M^{r}(\mathbb{N})
.
This is why for any wpo
X
,
M^{\diamond}(X)\leq_{\text{aug}}M^{r}(X)
, as was observed by Aschenbrenner and Pong
[
3
]
.

Note that if
X
is a linear ordering, then
M^{r}(X)
remains linear, while
M^{\diamond}(X)
does not.

Nonetheless,
\leq_{\diamond}
and
\leq_{r}
behave similarly on simple data structures, as we will see with the
following
transformation lemmas
:

Lemma 11

(Disjoint sum transformation)
.

For any wpos
A
and
B
,

M^{*}(A\sqcup B)\equiv M^{*}(A)\times M^{*}(B)
for
*\in\{\diamond,r\}
.

Lemma 12

(Direct sum transformation, multiset ordering)
.

For any wpos
A
and
B
,

M^{r}(A+B)\equiv M^{r}(A)\cdot M^{r}(B)

Lemma 13

(Direct sum transformation, multiset embedding)
.

For any wpos
A
and
B
,

M^{\diamond}(A+B)\leq_{\text{aug}}M^{\diamond}(A)\cdot M^{\diamond}(B)

Proof.

For all
m\in M^{\diamond}(A+B)
,
m=m_{A}\cup m_{B}
for some
m_{A}\in M^{\diamond}(A)
,
m_{B}\in M^{\diamond}(B)
.
Let
m=m_{A}\cup m_{B},m^{\prime}=m^{\prime}_{A}\cup m^{\prime}_{B}
such that
m\leq_{M^{\diamond}(A+B)}m^{\prime}
. By definition there exists an injective function
f:m\rightarrow m^{\prime}
such that
x\leq_{A+B}f(x)
for any
x\in m
. Then
f(m_{B})\subseteq m^{\prime}_{B}
hence
m_{B}\leq_{M^{\diamond}(B)}m^{\prime}_{B}
. If

m_{B}<_{M^{\diamond}(B)}m^{\prime}_{B}
then
(m_{A},m_{B})<_{M^{\diamond}(A)\cdot M^{\diamond}(B)}(m^{\prime}_{A},m^{\prime}_{B})
. Otherwise
m_{B}=m^{\prime}_{B}
. Then
f(m_{A})\subseteq m^{\prime}_{A}
, hence
m_{A}\leq_{M^{\diamond}(A)}m^{\prime}(A)
. Thus
(m_{A},m_{B})\leq_{M^{\diamond}(A)\cdot M^{\diamond}(B)}(m^{\prime}_{A},m^{\prime}_{B})
.
∎

The augmentation and substructure relations are monotone with respect to the multiset ordering and multiset embedding:

Lemma 14
.

Let
A,B
be two wpos. Then
A\leq_{\text{st}}B
implies
M^{\diamond}(A)\leq_{\text{st}}M^{\diamond}(B)
and
M^{r}(A)\leq_{\text{st}}M^{r}(B)
.
Moreover,
A\geq_{\text{aug}}B
implies that
M^{\diamond}(A)\geq_{\text{aug}}M^{\diamond}(B)
and
M^{r}(A)\geq_{\text{aug}}M^{r}(B)

1.6
A tool to compute the width: Quasi-incomparable subsets

Of all three ordinal invariants, the width is the less studied, since it has been introduced more recently. It is also the hardest invariant to study: unlike the m.o.t. and the height that are reached by the order types of the maximal linearisation and the maximal chain of a wpo, respectively, the width is not embodied by a sort of ”maximal antichain”.

A powerful tool for analysing the width of a wpo is the notion of
quasi-incomparable
subsets of a wpo, which was first used in
[
16
]
for the cartesian product of several ordinals.

For any partial order
X
, we denote with
x\perp y
when two elements
x,y\in X
are incomparable. This notation can be extended to subsets
Y,Z
of
X
: we denote with
Y\perp Z
when for every
y\in Y,z\in Z
,
y\perp z
.

Definition 18
.

Let
A
be a wpo, and
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}
be
n
subsets of
A
. Then
(A_{i})_{i\leq n}
is a
quasi-incomparable
family of subsets of
A
iff for any
i<n
, for any finite
Y\subseteq A_{1}\cup\dots\cup A_{i}
, there exists
A_{i+1}^{\prime}\subseteq A_{i+1}
such that
A_{i+1}^{\prime}\perp Y
and
A_{i+1}^{\prime}\equiv A_{i+1}
.

This definition is slightly more restrictive than the one in
[
16
]
, which only required that
\bm{w}(A_{i+1}^{\prime})=\bm{w}(A_{i+1})
.

The idea behind these quasi-incomparable subsets is that sometimes one can slice a wpo
A
into simpler subsets
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}
whose width is known, such that
{\textit{Ant}}(A_{n})+\dots+{\textit{Ant}}(A_{1})
is embedded in
{\textit{Ant}}(A)
. Intuitively, it means that one can combine antichains of
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}
into one antichain of
A
.

This entails a practical relation between the widths of
A
and its subsets:

Lemma 19
.

Let
(A_{i})_{i\leq n}
be a quasi-incomparable family of subsets of
A
. Then
\bm{w}(A)\geq\bm{w}(A_{n})+\dots+\bm{w}(A_{1})
.

Lemma

19
will prove very useful soon, in the proofs of
Lemmas

22
and
29
.

Width of the multiset embedding

In this section we compute the width of
M^{\diamond}(X)
for any wpo
X
, which happens to be functional in the width of
X
:

Theorem 20

(Width of the multiset embedding)
.

For any wpo
X
,
\bm{w}(M^{\diamond}(X))={\omega}^{\widehat{\bm{o}(X)}-1}
.

3
Height and width of the multiset ordering

The m.o.t. is the only invariant we know how to compute compositionally for the multiset ordering.
See
16

Since the multiset ordering of a linear ordering remains linear, we can deduce that for any ordinal
\alpha
,
M^{r}(\alpha)\equiv{\omega}^{\alpha}
.

For the height of
M^{r}(X)
, we have a result similar to
Theorem

16
.

Theorem 26

(Height of the multiset ordering)
.

Let
X
be a wpo.

Then
\bm{h}(M^{r}(X))={\omega}^{\bm{h}(X)}
.

Proof.

Remember that
X\geq_{\text{st}}\bm{h}(X)
, and thus
M^{r}(X)\geq_{\text{st}}M^{r}(\bm{h}(X))\equiv{\omega}^{\bm{h}(X)}
. Therefore
\bm{h}(M^{r}(X))\geq{\omega}^{\bm{h}(X)}
.

We prove the upper bound by induction on
\bm{h}(X)
. If
\bm{h}(X)=0
then
X=\emptyset
and
M^{r}(X)
only contains the empty multiset, so
\bm{h}(M^{r}(X))=1={\omega}^{0}
.

For any multiset
m\in M^{r}(X)
, we write
X_{<m}
for
(\cap_{x\in m}X_{\not\geq x})\cap(\cup_{x\in m}X_{<x})
. Observe that
X_{<m}
is a subset of
X
such that
\bm{h}(X_{m})<\bm{h}(X)
.
Now suppose
X
is not empty and
m
is any multiset on
X
:

\displaystyle M^{r}(X)_{<m}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{m_{1}+m_{2}=m,m_{1}\neq\emptyset}\{\;m^{\prime}+m_{2}\;:\;m^{\prime}\in M^{r}(X_{<m_{1}})\;\}

\displaystyle\equiv\bigcup_{m_{1}\subseteq m,m_{1}\neq\emptyset}M^{r}(X_{<m_{1}})\;.

Since
\bm{h}(X_{m_{1}})<\bm{h}(X)
, by induction hypothesis
\bm{h}(M^{r}(X_{<m_{1}}))\leq{\omega}^{\bm{h}(X_{<m_{1}})}<{\omega}^{\bm{h}(X)}
. Moreover,
{\omega}^{\bm{h}(X)}
is indecomposable. Hence:

\bm{h}(M^{r}(X)_{<m})\leq\bigoplus_{m_{1}+m_{2}=m,m_{1}\neq\emptyset}\bm{h}(M^{r}(\cup_{x\in m_{1}}X_{<x}))<{\omega}^{\bm{h}(X)}\;.

Therefore
\bm{h}(M^{r}(X))\leq{\omega}^{\bm{h}(X)}
according to
Eq.

Res-h
.
∎

Unfortunately, the width of the multiset ordering is harder to compute, as
\bm{w}(M^{r}(X))
is not functional in the ordinal invariants of
X
. The following example exhibits two wpos
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
, with identical invariants, such that
\bm{w}(M^{r}(X_{1}))\neq\bm{w}(M^{r}(X_{2}))
.

Example 27
.

Let
H=\Sigma_{n<{\omega}}\Gamma_{n}
. An interesting property of
H
is that
\bm{w}(H)=\bm{h}(H)=\bm{o}(H)={\omega}
.
Observe that
\bm{w}(M^{r}(H))\leq\bm{o}(M^{r}(H))={\omega}^{{\omega}}
and that
\bm{w}(M^{r}(H))\geq\bm{w}(M^{r}(\Gamma_{n}))={\omega}^{n-1}
for all
0<n<{\omega}
. Hence
\bm{w}(M^{r}(H))={{\omega}^{{\omega}}}
.

Consider
X_{1}=H+H
and
X_{2}=H+{\omega}
, two wpos with the same ordinal invariants:
\bm{o}(X_{i})=\bm{h}(X_{i})={\omega}\cdot 2
and
\bm{w}(X_{i})={\omega}
for
i\in\{\;1,2\;\}
. According to
Lemma

12
,
\bm{w}(M^{r}(X_{1}))=\bm{w}(M^{r}(H))\odot\bm{w}(M^{r}(H))={{\omega}^{{\omega}}}\odot{{\omega}^{{\omega}}}={\omega}^{{\omega}\cdot 2}
and
\bm{w}(M^{r}(X_{1}))=\bm{w}(M^{r}(H))\odot\bm{w}(M^{r}({\omega}))={{\omega}^{{\omega}}}\odot 1={{\omega}^{{\omega}}}
.

We will get around this non-functionality issue by introducing a new invariant in which the width of the multiset ordering is functional. But first we will show upper and lower bounds on width.

Lemma 28
.

Let
X
be a wpo. Then

\bm{w}(M^{r}(X))\leq\sup_{x\in X,n<{\omega}}\bm{w}(M^{r}(X)_{\perp\langle x\rangle})\otimes n+1

Proof.

Let us define the notation
\underline{\perp}
: For any multisets
m,m^{\prime}\in M^{r}(X)
,
m\underline{\perp}m^{\prime}
iff
m\cap m^{\prime}=\emptyset
and
m\perp m^{\prime}
.

In other words,
m\underline{\perp}m^{\prime}
iff
m
and
m^{\prime}
are disjoint and
there exists
x\in m
such that for all
y^{\prime}\in m^{\prime}
,
x\not\geq y^{\prime}
, and
there exists
x^{\prime}\in m^{\prime}
such that for all
y\in m
,
x^{\prime}\not\geq y
.
In particular
x^{\prime}\not\geq x
.
Hence
m\underline{\perp}m^{\prime}
implies there exists
x\in m
such that
\langle x\rangle\underline{\perp}m^{\prime}
, which is equivalent to
\langle x\rangle\perp m^{\prime}
. Now:

\displaystyle M^{r}(X)_{\perp m}

\displaystyle\geq_{\text{aug}}\bigsqcup_{m_{1}+m_{2}=m,m_{1}\neq\emptyset}\{\;m^{\prime}+m_{2}\;:\;m^{\prime}\in M^{r}(X),m^{\prime}\underline{\perp}m_{1}\;\}

\displaystyle\equiv\bigsqcup_{m_{1}\subseteq m,m_{1}\neq\emptyset}M^{r}(X)_{\underline{\perp}m_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq_{\text{st}}\geq_{\text{aug}}\bigsqcup_{m_{1}\subseteq m,m_{1}\neq\emptyset}\;\bigsqcup_{x\in m_{1}}M^{r}(X)_{\perp\langle x\rangle}

Hence according to
Lemma

2
,

M^{r}(X)_{\perp m}\leq\bigoplus_{m_{1}\subseteq m,m_{1}\neq\emptyset}\;\bigoplus_{x\in m_{1}}\bm{w}(M^{r}(X)_{\perp\langle x\rangle})\;.

Let
x\in m
such that
\bm{w}(M^{r}(X)_{\perp\langle x\rangle})
is maximal.
Then
\bm{w}(M^{r}(X)_{\perp m})\leq\bm{w}(M^{r}(X)_{\perp\langle x\rangle})\otimes n
for some
n<{\omega}
.
Hence according to
Eq.

Res-w
,

\bm{w}(M^{r}(X))=\sup_{m\in M^{r}(X)}\bm{w}(M^{r}(X)_{\perp m})+1\leq\sup_{x\in X,n<{\omega}}\bm{w}(M^{r}(X)_{\perp\langle x\rangle})\otimes n+1\;.

∎

Here is a similar lower bound:

Lemma 29
.

Let
X
be a wpo. Then

\bm{w}(M^{r}(X))\geq\sup_{x\in X,n<{\omega}}\bm{w}(M^{r}(X)_{\perp\langle x\rangle})\cdot n+1

Proof.

This proof follows the same structure as the proof of
Lemma

22
: We study the residual of
M^{r}(X)
which contains every element incomparable to some multiset of the form
\langle x\rangle\times n
, and slice this residual into a family of quasi-incomparable subsets.

According to
Eq.

Res-w
,

\displaystyle w(M^{r}(X))

\displaystyle=\sup_{m\in M^{r}(X)}\bm{w}(M^{r}(X)_{\perp m})+1

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{x\in X,n<{\omega}}\bm{w}(M^{r}(X)_{\perp\langle x\rangle\times n})+1\;.

Let us compute a lower bound for
\bm{w}(M^{r}(X)_{\perp\langle x\rangle\times n})
for any
x\in X
and
n<{\omega}
.
For all
k\in[1,n]
, let
M_{k}=\{\;\langle x\rangle\times(n-k)\cup m\;:\;m\in M^{r}(X)_{\perp\langle x\rangle}\;\}
. Observe that
M_{k}\equiv M^{r}(X)_{\perp\langle x\rangle}
for any
k\in[1,n]
, and for all
m\in M_{k}
,
m\perp\langle x\rangle\times n
.
We claim that
(M_{k})_{k\in[1,n]}
is a quasi-incomparable family of subsets of
M^{r}(X)_{\perp(\langle x\rangle\times n)}
:
Let
i<n
and
Y
a finite subset of
M_{1}\cup\dots\cup M_{i}
. We define
m_{Y}
and
M^{\prime}_{i+1}
as

\displaystyle m_{y}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\bigcup_{j\leq i}\;\bigcup_{m\in(M_{j}\cap Y)}(m\setminus(\langle x\rangle\times(n-j)))\;,

\displaystyle M^{\prime}_{i+1}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\{\;\langle x\rangle\times(n-i-1)\cup m_{Y}\cup m\;:\;m\in M^{r}(X)_{\perp\langle x\rangle}\;\}\;.

Observe that
M^{\prime}_{i+1}
is a subset of and isomorphic to
M_{i+1}
,
and
Y\perp M^{\prime}_{i+1}
.

Therefore according to
Lemma

19
,

\bm{w}(M^{r}(X)_{\perp(\langle x\rangle\times n)})\geq\bm{w}(M^{r}(X)_{\perp\langle x\rangle})\cdot n
.
∎

The bounds provided by
Lemmas

28
and
29
actually match. Furthermore, they can be reformulated in such a way that the residual on
M^{r}(X)
boils down to a residual on
X
:

Theorem 30
.

For any non-linear wpo
X
,

\bm{w}(M^{r}(X))=\sup\{\;\bm{w}(M^{r}(X_{\not\geq x}))\cdot{\omega}\;:\;x\in X,X_{\perp x}\neq\emptyset\;\}\;.

(W)

Proof.

For any ordinal
\alpha
,
\sup_{n<{\omega}}(\alpha\cdot n+1)=\sup_{n<{\omega}}(\alpha\otimes n+1)=\alpha\cdot{\omega}
.
Hence according to
Lemmas

29
and
28
,

\bm{w}(M^{r}(X))=\sup_{x\in X}(\bm{w}(M^{r}(X)_{\perp\langle x\rangle})\cdot{\omega})\;.

Let
x\in X
. If
X_{\perp x}=\emptyset
, then
M^{r}(X)_{\perp\langle x\rangle}=\emptyset
.
Otherwise let
y\in X_{\perp x}
.
Then
\{\;\langle y\rangle\cup m\;:\;m\in M^{r}(X_{\not\geq x})\;\}\leq_{\text{st}}M^{r}(X)_{\perp\langle x\rangle}\leq_{\text{st}}M^{r}(X_{\not\geq x})
.
Therefore
\bm{w}(M^{r}(X)_{\perp\langle x\rangle})=\bm{w}(M^{r}(X_{\not\geq x}))
if

, otherwise
\bm{w}(M^{r}(X)_{\perp\langle x\rangle})=0
.
∎

Eq.

W
, even though its inductive formulation makes it impractical, sparks our interest for two reasons:
First, it proves that
\bm{w}(M^{r}(X)
is indecomposable.
Second, its structure mirrors the residual equations: it refers to a residual of
X
, and not the residual that appears in
Eq.

as one could expect, but the residual of
Eq.

.
Furthermore, it is a supremum, indexed not on
X
but only on elements of
X
that are incomparable to other elements. In other words, the supremum is indexed on
X
stripped of its ”linear” elements.

These observations are what led us to the definition of fourth ordinal invariant:
maximal safe order type

Definition 31

(Stripped subset, safe subset, maximal safe order type)
.

For any wpo
X
,
\textit{str}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\{\;x\in X\;:\;X_{\perp x}\neq\emptyset\;\}
is the
stripped subset
of
X
.
A subset
X^{\prime}
of
X
is
safe
if there exists a maximal linearisation
\ell:X^{\prime}\rightarrow\bm{o}(X^{\prime})
(i.e. a morphism) such that for all
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in X^{\prime}
, for all
x\in X^{\prime}
such that
\ell(x)<\ell(x_{i})
for any
i\in[1,n]
,
(X_{\not\geq x_{1},\dots,x_{n}})_{\perp x}\neq\emptyset
. We say that
\ell
verifies the
safety condition
.

The
maximal safe order type
of
X
, denoted with
\operatorname{\underline{\mathbb{o}}}(X)
, is the supremum of the m.o.t.s of safe subsets of
X
:

\operatorname{\underline{\mathbb{o}}}(X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\sup\{\;\bm{o}(X^{\prime})\;:\;X^{\prime}\subseteq X\text{ safe}\;\}\;.

Observe that
\textit{str}(\textit{str}(X))=\textit{str}(X)
, and that for any safe subset
X^{\prime}
of
X
,
X^{\prime}\leq_{\text{st}}\textit{str}(X)
, thus
\operatorname{\underline{\mathbb{o}}}(X)\leq\bm{o}(\textit{str}(X))
. At first glance, it looks like
Eq.

W
could be reformulated to be functional in
\bm{o}(\textit{str}(X)
, but we will soon see that a more restricted condition like the safety condition is exactly what we need to develop this inductive expression.

Lemma 32
.

For any wpo
X
, for any linearisation
\ell:\textit{str}(X)\rightarrow\bm{o}(\textit{str}(X))
, there exists a safe subset
X^{\prime}
of
\textit{str}(X)
such that
\ell
restricted to
X^{\prime}
verifies the safety condition, and
\bm{o}(X^{\prime})\geq\delta(\bm{o}(\textit{str}(X)))
, where

\delta(\alpha)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}\alpha\text{ if $\alpha$ is limit,}\\
\gamma+\lfloor n/2\rfloor\text{ if $\alpha=\gamma+n$ with $\gamma$ limit and $n<{\omega}$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
\downarrow\beta\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\{\;\gamma:\gamma<\beta\;\}
for any ordinal
\beta
.
We prove by induction on
\beta\leq\bm{o}(\textit{str}(X))
that there exists
X_{\beta}\subseteq\ell^{-1}(\downarrow\beta)
a safe subset of
\textit{str}(X)
where
\ell
restricted to
X_{\beta}
verifies the safety condition, such that
\bm{o}(X_{\beta})\geq\delta(\beta)
. Furthermore, for all
\beta<\beta^{\prime}\leq\bm{o}(\textit{str}(X))
,
X_{\beta}\subseteq X_{\beta^{\prime}}
.

Let
X_{0}=\emptyset
, and
X_{\beta}=X_{\beta^{\prime}}
if
\beta=\beta^{\prime}+1
with
\beta
limit.

If
\beta=\beta^{\prime}+2
for some
\beta^{\prime}
(it does not have to be limit), then let
x=\ell^{-1}(\beta^{\prime})
and
x^{\prime}=\ell^{-1}(\beta^{\prime}+1)
.
If
X_{\beta^{\prime}}\cup\{x\}
is a safe subset of
\textit{str}(X)
then let
X_{\beta}=X_{\beta^{\prime}}\cup\{x\}
.
Otherwise it means that there exists
y\in X_{\beta^{\prime}}
such that for any
z\in\textit{str}(X)_{\perp y}
, we have
z\geq x
.
Suppose that
X_{\beta^{\prime}}\cup\{x^{\prime}\}
is not a safe subset either: it would also mean that there exists
y^{\prime}\in X_{\beta^{\prime}}
such that for any
z\in\textit{str}(X)_{\perp y}
, we have
z\geq x^{\prime}
.
We know that
x\not\geq x^{\prime}
and that
y,y^{\prime}\not\geq x,x^{\prime}
, and we can deduce that
y\perp x
and
y^{\prime}\perp x^{\prime}
and
x\geq y^{\prime}
. We also deduce that if
x\perp x^{\prime}
then
x^{\prime}\geq y
, otherwise
x\perp y^{\prime}
. Thus
y\perp y^{\prime}
, which contradicts the supposition.
Therefore
X_{\beta}=X_{\beta^{\prime}}\cup\{x^{\prime}\}
is safe.
In both cases,
\bm{o}(X_{\beta})=\bm{o}(X_{\beta^{\prime}}+1)\geq\delta(\beta^{\prime})+1\geq\delta(\beta)
by induction hypothesis.

If
\beta
is limit, then let
X_{\beta}=\cup_{\beta^{\prime}<\beta}X_{\beta^{\prime}}
. One can see that
X_{\beta}
is safe, and that
\bm{o}(X_{\beta})\geq\sup_{\beta^{\prime}<\beta}\delta(\beta^{\prime})=\beta
by induction hypothesis.
∎

Therefore
\delta(\bm{o}(\textit{str}(X)))\leq\operatorname{\underline{\mathbb{o}}}(X)\leq\bm{o}(\textit{str}(X))
, hence there exists a safe subset whose m.o.t. reaches
\operatorname{\underline{\mathbb{o}}}(X)
. We can also observe that
\operatorname{\underline{\mathbb{o}}}(X)=\bm{o}(\textit{str}(X))
when
\bm{o}(\textit{str}(X))
is a limit ordinal, hence this gives us a sufficient condition for when maximal safe order type reaches m.o.t.

\bm{w}(M^{r}(X))={\omega}^{\operatorname{\underline{\mathbb{o}}}(X)}

Proof.

(\geq)

\bm{o}(X^{\prime})

When

\bm{w}(M^{r}(X))\geq 1

According to

,

\displaystyle w(M^{r}(X))

\displaystyle=\sup\{\;\bm{w}(M^{r}(X_{\not\geq x}))\cdot{\omega}\;:\;x\in X,X_{\perp x}\neq\emptyset\;\}

\displaystyle\geq\sup\{\;\bm{w}(M^{r}(X_{\not\geq x}))\cdot{\omega}\;:\;x\in X^{\prime}\;\}\;,

since for any
x\in X^{\prime}
,
X_{\perp x}\neq 0
.

X^{\prime}
is safe so there exists a maximal linearisation
\ell:X^{\prime}\rightarrow\bm{o}(X^{\prime})
that verifies the safety condition. Let
x=\ell(\beta)
for some
\beta<\bm{o}(X^{\prime})
. Then
\ell^{-1}(\downarrow\beta)
is a safe subset of
X^{\prime}_{\not\geq x}
; the maximal linearisation that verifies the safety condition is
l
restricted to
\ell^{-1}(\downarrow\beta)
. Hence by induction hypothesis,
\bm{w}(M^{r}(X_{\not\geq x}))\geq{\omega}^{\beta}
.
Therefore:

w(M^{r}(X))\geq\sup\{\;{\omega}^{\beta}\cdot{\omega}\;:\;\beta<\bm{o}(X^{\prime})\;\}={\omega}^{\bm{o}(X^{\prime})}\;.

(\leq)

By induction on
\operatorname{\underline{\mathbb{o}}}(X)
.

If
\operatorname{\underline{\mathbb{o}}}(X)=0
then
X
is linear hence
\bm{w}(M^{r}(X))=1
.

Otherwise let
\alpha=\operatorname{\underline{\mathbb{o}}}(X)
.
Assume there exists
x\in X
, with
X_{\perp x}\neq\emptyset
, such that
\operatorname{\underline{\mathbb{o}}}(X_{\not\geq x})=\alpha
. It means that there exists a safe subset
X^{\prime}
of
X_{\not\geq x}
and a maximal linearisation
\ell:X^{\prime}\rightarrow\alpha
that verifies the safety condition. Let
\ell^{\prime}:X_{\not\geq x}\cup\{x\}\rightarrow\alpha+1
such that
\ell^{\prime}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\alpha
and
\ell^{\prime}(y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}l(y)
for any
y\in X^{\prime}
. Then
\ell^{\prime}
is a maximal linearisation that verifies the safety condition for the subset
X^{\prime}\cup\{x\}
of
X
, which has a m.o.t. of
\alpha+1
. Hence
\operatorname{\underline{\mathbb{o}}}(X)\geq\alpha+1
, we reached a contradiction.

For all
x\in X
such that
X_{\perp x}\neq\emptyset
, we now know that
\operatorname{\underline{\mathbb{o}}}(X_{\not\geq x})<\alpha
. Thus
\bm{w}(M^{r}(X_{\not\geq x}))\cdot{\omega}\leq{\omega}^{\alpha}
by induction hypothesis.
Hence, according to
Theorem

30
,
\bm{w}(M^{r}(X))\leq{\omega}^{\operatorname{\underline{\mathbb{o}}}(X)}
.

∎

This opens many questions on this new invariant: we already know that it is closely bounded (
Lemma

32
) and reached by the m.o.t. of a safe subset. We also know that the maximal safe order type of a wpo reaches its m.o.t. as soon as
\bm{o}(\textit{str}(X))=\bm{o}(X)
is a limit ordinal. It is also quite easy to compute compositionally:

Proposition 34
.

For any non empty wpo
A,B
,

•

\operatorname{\underline{\mathbb{o}}}(A+B)=\operatorname{\underline{\mathbb{o}}}(A)+\operatorname{\underline{\mathbb{o}}}(B)
,

•

\operatorname{\underline{\mathbb{o}}}(A\sqcup B)=1+(\bm{o}(A)-1)\oplus(\bm{o}(B)-1)
,

•

\operatorname{\underline{\mathbb{o}}}(A\times B)\geq(\bm{o}(A)-1)\otimes\bm{o}(B)
.

Conclusion

We have computed the width of the finite set of multisets with the multiset embedding. As for the multiset ordering, we have found a simple formula for its height, and showed its width is not functional in any of the usual ordinal invariants. Nonetheless, we introduced a new ordinal invariant, the maximal safe order type, in which the width is functional.

Investigating the maximal safe order type is a subject for further research: Are there equivalent characterisations? How does it relate to other concepts? Can it be computed compositionally ? Can we define a interesting class of wpos where maximal safe order type always coincides with m.o.t. ?

Appendix A
On ordinal arithmetic

We suppose well-known the notions of sum
\alpha+\beta
and product
\alpha\cdot\beta
on ordinals
[
2
]
. However, let us recall some definitions that might be less familiar to the reader.

Definition 35

(Left subtraction)
.

For any ordinals
\alpha\geq\beta
, the subtraction
\alpha-\beta
is the unique ordinal such that
\beta+(\alpha-\beta)=\alpha
.
In particular,

\alpha-1\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\begin{cases}\alpha\text{ if $\alpha$ is infinite,}\\
n-1\text{ if }\alpha=n<{\omega}\;.\end{cases}

Definition 36

(Cantor normal form)
.

Any ordinal
\alpha
can be expressed in Cantor normal form, as
\alpha=\sum_{i<n}{\omega}^{\alpha_{i}}
, where
\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}
are ordinals such that
\alpha_{0}\geq\alpha_{1}\geq\dots\geq\alpha_{n-1}
. This expression is unique.

Definition 37

(Natural operations)
.

Let
\alpha=\sum_{i<n}{\omega}^{\alpha_{i}}
and
\beta=\sum_{i<m}{\omega}^{\beta_{i}}
two ordinals in Cantor normal form.

The
natural sum
, or Hessenberg sum,
\alpha\oplus\beta
is defined as
\gamma=\sum_{i<n+m}{\omega}^{\gamma_{i}}
with
\gamma_{0}\geq\dots\geq\gamma_{n+m-1}
a reordering of
\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{n-1},\beta_{0},\dots,\beta_{m-1}
.

The
natural product

\alpha\otimes\beta
is defined as
\bigoplus_{i<n,j<m}{\omega}^{\alpha_{i}\oplus\beta_{j}}
.

Another product was derived from the Hessenberg sum:

Definition 38

(Hessenberg-based product
[
1
]
)
.

The
Hessenberg-based product

\alpha\odot\beta
is defined inductively by the following relations:

\displaystyle\alpha\odot 0

\displaystyle=0\;,

\displaystyle\alpha\odot(\beta+1)

\displaystyle=(\alpha\odot\beta)\oplus\alpha\;,

\displaystyle\alpha\odot\beta

\displaystyle=\sup\{\;\alpha\odot\gamma\;:\;\gamma<\beta\;\}\text{ for limit $\beta$.}

We always have
\alpha\cdot\beta\leq\alpha\odot\beta\leq\alpha\otimes\beta
.

Definition 39
.

An ordinal
\alpha
is:

•

an
\epsilon
-number
when
{\omega}^{\alpha}=\alpha
.

•

indecomposable
when for all
\beta,\delta<\alpha
, we have
\beta+\delta<\alpha
. Equivalently, indecomposable ordinals are ordinals of the form
{\omega}^{\beta}
where
\beta
is any ordinal. They are sometimes called principal additive ordinals.

•

multiplicatively indecomposable
when for all
\beta,\delta<\alpha
, we have
\beta\cdot\delta<\alpha
. Equivalently, multiplicatively indecomposable ordinals are ordinals of the form
{\omega}^{\beta}
where
\beta
is indecomposable. They are sometimes called principal multiplicative ordinals.

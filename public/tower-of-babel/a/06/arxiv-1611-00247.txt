Foldy-Wouthuysen Green’s function and WKB transfer matrix method for Dirac tunneling through monolayer graphene with a mass gap
Mark Behzad Doost

Abstract
I provide a transfer matrix method for the Foldy-Wouthuysen representation of the Dirac equation. I derive the relationship between the reflection and transmission coefficients of the Dirac spinors and the wavefunction in the transformed representation. I develop a WKB approximation for Dirac fermions that has the same elegant form as the WKB solution to Schrödinger’s equation. My WKB approximation is to all orders and includes the semi-classical turning point. I provide an extension to fully 2 2 -dimensional periodic structures by Fourier methods for band-gap engineering. I verify my methods for all energies by comparison with analytic solutions developed in the Dirac spinor representation. Rich appendices detail my research into the Green’s functions of Dirac fermions, where I rigorously derive the free space Green’s functions for the Foldy-Wouthuysen representation of the Dirac equation.

2
Dirac equation and its Foldy-Wouthuysen representation

The Dirac equation for a scalar potential
V
is given in Hamiltonian form by

\left[c{\bf\alpha}\cdot\hat{p}+\beta mc^{2}\right]\Psi=H\Psi=\left(E+V\right)\Psi\,,

(1)

\gamma_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}{+I}&0\\
0&{-I}\end{pmatrix}\,,\qquad\gamma_{x,y,z}=\begin{pmatrix}0&{+\sigma_{x,y,z}}\\
{-\sigma_{x,y,z}}&0\end{pmatrix}\,,

(2)

I=\begin{pmatrix}{+1}&0\\
0&{+1}\end{pmatrix}\,,\qquad\sigma_{x}=\begin{pmatrix}0&{+1}\\
{+1}&0\end{pmatrix}\,,

(3)

\sigma_{y}=\begin{pmatrix}0&{-i}\\
{+i}&0\end{pmatrix}\,,\qquad\sigma_{z}=\begin{pmatrix}{+1}&0\\
0&{-1}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(4)

with
{\alpha_{x,y,z}}=\gamma_{t}\gamma_{x,y,z}
and
\beta=\gamma_{t}
.

\hat{p}=-i\hbar{\bf\nabla}=-i\hbar\left[\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x},\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y},\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}\right]

(5)

is the momentum operator for the electron which is of mass
m
and energy
E
. The charge and current density are given by the following well-known expressions:

\rho=\Psi^{*}\Psi\,,

(6)

j=\Psi^{*}\alpha\Psi\,.

(7)

There are two linearly independent solutions of the free particle Dirac equation,

\Psi^{\left(\pm\right)}_{\uparrow}=\begin{pmatrix}\mp mc^{2}-E-V\\
0\\
0\\
c\left(p_{x}\pm ip_{y}\right)\end{pmatrix}\exp{\left(\dfrac{ip_{x}x+ip_{y}y}{\hbar}\right)}\,,

(8)

\Psi^{\left(\pm\right)}_{\downarrow}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\mp mc^{2}-E-V\\
c\left(p_{x}\mp ip_{y}\right)\\
0\end{pmatrix}\exp{\left(\dfrac{ip_{x}x+ip_{y}y}{\hbar}\right)}\,,

(9)

each can be normalized to one electron per unit volume by Eq. (
6
).

By making use of the FW transformation, I will decouple the differential
equations of the Dirac equation to give four independent identical equations.
I will show that by calculating the propagation for only one of these components I may obtain the full GF and transmission.

Under unitary transformation the Dirac equation becomes

e^{+iS}He^{-iS}e^{+iS}\Psi=\left(E+V\right)e^{+iS}\Psi\,.

(10)

Foldy and Wouthuysen
[
11
]
found that their rotation

e^{\pm iS}=\cos\theta\pm{\bf\beta}{\bf\alpha}\cdot\dfrac{\hat{p}}{\left|\hat{p}\right|}\sin\theta\,,

(11)

transformed the Dirac Hamiltonian into diagonal and anti-diagonal parts

\beta\left(mc^{2}\cos{\left(2\theta\right)}+c\left|\hat{p}\right|\sin{\left(2\theta\right)}\right)\\
+c\alpha\cdot\hat{p}\left(\cos{\left(2\theta\right)}-\dfrac{mc}{\left|\hat{p}\right|}\sin{\left(2\theta\right)}\right)\\
=e^{+iS}He^{-iS}\,.

(12)

Subsequently it was chosen
[
11
]

\cos\left(2\theta\right)=\dfrac{mc^{2}}{\sqrt{c^{2}\hat{p}^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}}}\,,

(13)

\sin\left(2\theta\right)=\dfrac{c\left|\hat{p}\right|}{\sqrt{c^{2}\hat{p}^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}}}\,,

(14)

so that by direct substitution of Eq. (
13
) and Eq. (
14
) into Eq. (
12
)

\left[{\bf\beta}\sqrt{\hat{p}^{2}c^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}}-\hat{1}\left(V+E\right)\right]\widetilde{\Psi}=0\,.

(15)

Eq. (
15
) shows there are two possibilities for the FW equation

\left[\mp\sqrt{\hat{p}^{2}c^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}}-\left(V+E\right)\right]\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(\pm\right)}=0\,,

(16)

\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(-\right)}
corresponds to the electron,
\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(+\right)}
to the positron. The rotated wavefunction
\widetilde{\Psi}
is related to
\Psi
by

\left[\cos\theta+{\bf\beta}{\bf\alpha}\cdot\dfrac{\hat{p}}{\left|\hat{p}\right|}\sin\theta\right]\Psi=\widetilde{\Psi}\,.

(17)

Taylor expanding
\sqrt{\hat{p}^{2}c^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}}
in Eq. (
16
) gives

\left[\mp\mathcal{L}-\left(V+E\pm mc^{2}\right)\right]\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(\pm\right)}=0\,,

(18)

where

\mathcal{L}=mc^{2}\left[\dfrac{1}{2}\left[{\dfrac{\hat{p}}{mc}}\right]^{2}-\dfrac{1}{8}\left[{\dfrac{\hat{p}}{mc}}\right]^{4}+\dfrac{1}{16}\left[{\dfrac{\hat{p}}{mc}}\right]^{6}-...\right]\,.

(19)

Eq. (
16
) and Eq. (
18
) are two interpretations of the FW equation.

I demonstrate the unsuitability of my FW approach for calculating magnetic barrier tunneling in
Appendix C
. I rigorously derive the GFs of the FW Eq. (
18
) in
Appendix D
and
Appendix F
. I uncover and discuss a GF paradox of the FW Eq. (
18
) in
Appendix E
.

3
Relationship between the transmission of the Dirac equation and its Foldy-Wouthuysen representation

Throughout my numerical and analytic results, Section
14
and
Appendix B
, I will calculate the reflection coefficient
R
to deduce the transmission coefficient
T
as

T=1-R\,.

(20)

Calculating
R
, rather than
T
directly, avoids the need for wavefunction normalization in both the incident and transmitted regions.

Let
\Psi
be given by Eq. (
8
) and Eq. (
9
) as

\Psi=u\exp{\left(\dfrac{ip_{x}x+ip_{y}y}{\hbar}\right)}\,,

(21)

taking
\widetilde{\Psi}
of the form

\widetilde{\Psi}=\widetilde{u}\exp{\left(\dfrac{ip_{x}x+ip_{y}y}{\hbar}\right)}\,,

(22)

I find by application of Eq. (
17
) to Eq. (
21
)

\begin{split}&\widetilde{u}_{1}=u_{1}\cos\theta+\left(d_{x}-id_{y}\right)u_{4}\sin\theta\,,\\
&\widetilde{u}_{2}=u_{2}\cos\theta+\left(d_{x}+id_{y}\right)u_{3}\sin\theta\,,\\
&\widetilde{u}_{3}=u_{3}\cos\theta-\left(d_{x}-id_{y}\right)u_{2}\sin\theta\,,\\
&\widetilde{u}_{4}=u_{4}\cos\theta-\left(d_{x}+id_{y}\right)u_{1}\sin\theta\,,\end{split}

(23)

where
\hat{d}=\hat{p}/|\hat{p}|
.

Eq. (
21
), Eq. (
22
), and Eqs. (
23
) show
\widetilde{\Psi}
in exponential planar form which is useful for calculating the fermion propagation through homogeneous space.

The incident (
i
) and reflected (
r
) waves for planar structures in the Dirac equation description are related to the reflection coefficient in the well-known way:

R=\dfrac{\left(\Psi\cdot\Psi^{*}\right)^{\left(r\right)}}{\left(\Psi\cdot\Psi^{*}\right)^{\left(i\right)}}\,.

(24)

For the two linearly independent solutions of the Dirac equation, Eq. (
8
) and Eq. (
9
), Eq. (
24
) gives rise to two degenerate expressions for the reflection coefficient:

R_{\uparrow}=\dfrac{\left(\left|\Psi_{1}\right|^{2}+\left|\Psi_{4}\right|^{2}\right)^{(r)}}{\left(\left|\Psi_{1}\right|^{2}+\left|\Psi_{4}\right|^{2}\right)^{\left(i\right)}}\,,

(25)

R_{\downarrow}=\dfrac{\left(\left|\Psi_{2}\right|^{2}+\left|\Psi_{3}\right|^{2}\right)^{\left(r\right)}}{\left(\left|\Psi_{2}\right|^{2}+\left|\Psi_{3}\right|^{2}\right)^{\left(i\right)}}\,.

(26)

I take note of Eqs. (
23
) linking
\widetilde{u}
to
u
and calculate when
d
is real

\left|\widetilde{u}_{1}\right|^{2}+\left|\widetilde{u}_{4}\right|^{2}=\left|u_{1}\right|^{2}+\left|u_{4}\right|^{2}\,,

(27)

\left|\widetilde{u}_{2}\right|^{2}+\left|\widetilde{u}_{3}\right|^{2}=\left|u_{2}\right|^{2}+\left|u_{3}\right|^{2}\,,

(28)

therefore
R
described by the Dirac equation can be rewritten in terms of the FW wavefunctions:

R_{\uparrow}=\dfrac{\left(\left|\widetilde{\Psi}_{1}\right|^{2}+\left|\widetilde{\Psi}_{4}\right|^{2}\right)^{\left(r\right)}}{\left(\left|\widetilde{\Psi}_{1}\right|^{2}+\left|\widetilde{\Psi}_{4}\right|^{2}\right)^{\left(i\right)}}\,,

(29)

R_{\downarrow}=\dfrac{\left(\left|\widetilde{\Psi}_{2}\right|^{2}+\left|\widetilde{\Psi}_{3}\right|^{2}\right)^{\left(r\right)}}{\left(\left|\widetilde{\Psi}_{2}\right|^{2}+\left|\widetilde{\Psi}_{3}\right|^{2}\right)^{\left(i\right)}}\,.

(30)

Since the components of
\widetilde{\Psi}
are degenerate I am only required to evaluate the propagation of a fermion by the FW Eq. (
18
) once when calculating
R
and
T
.

4
Boundary Conditions of the Foldy-Wouthuysen equation at a sharp step

Consider a sharp step in potential at
x=a
.
I will integrate the FW Eq. (
18
) through the boundary, normal to the boundary

\lim_{\left({a_{+}}-{a_{-}}\right)\rightarrow 0}\int^{a_{+}}_{a_{-}}\left[\mp\mathcal{L}-\left(V+E\pm mc^{2}\right)\right]\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(\pm\right)}\dfrac{dx}{c\hbar}=0\,.

(31)

In this section, I introduce the operator
\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}
, which commutes with
c\hat{p}_{x}

and is defined by

\mathcal{L}\widetilde{\Psi}=-\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}c\hat{p}_{x}\widetilde{\Psi}=-c\hat{p}_{x}\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}\widetilde{\Psi}\,.

(32)

Appendix A
discusses Eq. (
32
).

Since

\lim_{\left({a_{+}}-{a_{-}}\right)\rightarrow 0}\int^{a_{+}}_{a_{-}}\left(V+E\pm mc^{2}\right)\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(\pm\right)}\dfrac{dx}{c\hbar}=0\,,

(33)

due to the function integrated being finite but the integration range tending to zero, I have

\left[i\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}\widetilde{\Psi}\right]^{a_{+}}_{a_{-}}=\lim_{\left({a_{+}}-{a_{-}}\right)\rightarrow 0}\int^{a_{+}}_{a_{-}}\mathcal{L}\widetilde{\Psi}\dfrac{dx}{c\hbar}=0\,.

(34)

I can understand Eq. (
34
) when I consider that

\mathcal{L}\widetilde{\Psi}=ic\hbar\dfrac{\partial\left(\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}\widetilde{\Psi}\right)}{\partial x}\,.

(35)

Eq. (
34
) shows that
\widetilde{\Psi}
is continuous under the operation of
\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}
.

In order to evaluate
\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}\widetilde{\Psi}
for the mathematical description of the boundary conditions note

\pm\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}c\hat{p}_{x}\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(\pm\right)}=\left(V+E\pm mc^{2}\right)\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(\pm\right)}=\pm cp_{x}\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(\pm\right)}\,,

(36)

therefore

\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(\pm\right)}=\pm\dfrac{V+E\pm mc^{2}}{cp_{x}}\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(\pm\right)}=\mp\alpha^{\left(\pm\right)}\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(\pm\right)}\,.

(37)

For my second boundary condition I note that
\hat{p}\widetilde{\Psi}
always gives a finite observed value, therefore
\widetilde{\Psi}
is continuous everywhere.

My boundary conditions are in stark contrast to the continuity conditions of the Klein-Gordon equation; in that case, continuity is with respect to momentum and wavefunction. I verify my boundary conditions numerically and analytically in Section
14
and
Appendix B
.

Transfer matrices for a rectangular potential barrier

Inside a layer thickness
D
the transfer matrix of
\widetilde{\Psi}
is given by

\begin{pmatrix}{\exp{\left(-\dfrac{iDp_{x}}{\hbar}\right)}}&0\\
0&{\exp{\left(+\dfrac{iDp_{x}}{\hbar}\right)}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{a}_{1}\\
\widetilde{b}_{1}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{a}_{0}\\
\widetilde{b}_{0}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(38)

where
\widetilde{a}_{0}
and
\widetilde{a}_{1}
denote forward traveling FW wave parts and
\widetilde{b}_{0}
and
\widetilde{b}_{1}
denote backward travelling FW wave parts,

0
denotes on the left,
1
denotes on
the right. The fermion is refracted at an angle
\phi
to the layer boundary normal,
where

p_{x}=p\cos\phi\,.

(39)

For a boundary at a sharp step, applying the wavefunction continuity condition set out in Section
4
gives

\widetilde{a}_{0}+\widetilde{b}_{0}=\widetilde{a}_{1}+\widetilde{b}_{1}\,.

(40)

Secondly, applying wavefunction continuity under the operation of
\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}
, derived in Section
4
, to Eq. (
40
) gives

\alpha_{0}\widetilde{a}_{0}-\alpha_{0}\widetilde{b}_{0}=\alpha_{1}\widetilde{a}_{1}-\alpha_{1}\widetilde{b}_{1}\,.

(41)

Eq. (
40
) and Eq. (
41
) can be written in matrix form as

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}{\left(1+\dfrac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{0}}\right)}&{\left(1-\dfrac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{0}}\right)}\\
{\left(1-\dfrac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{0}}\right)}&{\left(1+\dfrac{\alpha_{1}}{\alpha_{0}}\right)}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{a}_{1}\\
\widetilde{b}_{1}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{a}_{0}\\
\widetilde{b}_{0}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(42)

During tunneling, wave modes either decay or grow exponentially. Transfer matrix multiplication combines these large and small values, this process is numerically unstable and inaccurate. Instead, I suggest using the method of Refs.
[
14
,
15
]
to combine my transfer matrices. The method of Refs.
[
14
,
15
]
addresses these instability issues by separating the exponentially growing and decaying terms.

7
{\bf 1st}
order relativistic WKB approximation

Consider the following result from
Appendix D
derived from the correct fermion interpretation of the FW equation in terms of
\mathcal{L}
,

T\left(k\right)=\dfrac{\left(2\alpha_{k}\alpha_{q}\right)^{2}}{\left(\alpha^{2}_{q}-\alpha^{2}_{k}\right)^{2}\sin^{2}\left(\dfrac{2m^{*}qa}{\hbar}\right)+\left(2\alpha_{k}\alpha_{q}\right)^{2}}

(46)

for a rectangular barrier given by

V\left(x\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}V&\mbox{for}\ \ \left|x\right|<a\,,\\
0&\mbox{for}\ \ \left|x\right|>a\,.\end{array}\right.

(47)

Eq. (
46
) can be approximated as

T\left(k\right)\approx\left(\dfrac{2\alpha_{k}\alpha_{q}}{\alpha^{2}_{k}-\alpha^{2}_{q}}\right)^{2}\exp{\left(-\dfrac{4im^{*}qa}{\hbar}\right)}

(48)

by assuming exponential wavefunction decay within the barrier. Taking logarithms of Eq. (
48
), the non-logarithmic term dominates the logarithmic term so that

\log_{e}T\left(k\right)\approx-\dfrac{4im^{*}qa}{\hbar}\,.

(49)

Assuming independence of transmission events, the total transmission probability for crossing an overall barrier is

T\approx\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}T_{i}\,,

(50)

\log_{e}\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}T_{i}\approx\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}-\dfrac{2im^{*}_{i}q_{i}\Delta x}{\hbar}=-\dfrac{2i}{\hbar}\int p_{x}dx\,,

(51)

where the
T_{i}
are the transmission probabilities of the individual barriers. I see from Eq. (
51
) that inside a smoothly varying layer the FW wavefunction is approximately given by

\widetilde{\Psi}\approx\exp{\left({\pm\dfrac{i}{\hbar}\int p_{x}dx}\right)}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left(\dfrac{2\alpha_{k}\alpha_{q}}{\alpha^{2}_{k}-\alpha^{2}_{q}}\right)_{i}\,.

(52)

8
Iterative relativistic WKB approximation

Consider a wavefunction of the form

\widetilde{\Psi}=\exp{\left(\dfrac{i\Phi}{\hbar}\right)}\,,

(53)

then the boson FW interpretation Eq. (
16
) becomes

\left[\sqrt{c^{2}\left(\dfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial x}\right)^{2}-c^{2}i\hbar\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Phi}{\partial x^{2}}+m^{2}c^{4}}-\left(V+E\right)\right]\widetilde{\Psi}=0\,.

(54)

Eq. (
54
) can be exactly rewritten as

c^{2}\left(\dfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial x}\right)^{2}-c^{2}i\hbar\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Phi}{\partial x^{2}}+m^{2}c^{4}=\left(V+E\right)^{2}\,.

(55)

I now make use of
(E+V)^{2}=m^{2}c^{4}+p^{2}c^{2}
in Eq. (
55
) and find that as expected

\hat{p}^{2}_{x}\widetilde{\Psi}=p^{2}_{x}\widetilde{\Psi}\,,\qquad\dfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial x}=\sqrt{p^{2}_{x}+i\hbar\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Phi}{\partial x^{2}}}\,.

(56)

I know from Section
7

\pm\dfrac{\partial\Phi}{\partial x}\approx p_{x}\,,

(57)

suggesting a next approximation on from Eq. (
52
) is

\Phi\approx\pm\int\sqrt{p^{2}_{x}\pm i\hbar\dfrac{\partial p_{x}}{\partial x}}dx\,.

(58)

Eq. (
58
) follows from the integration of Eq. (
56
). This procedure can be iterated to an
n^{th}
order WKB approximation in the boson FW interpretation.

9
{\bf 2nd}
order relativistic WKB approximation

Expanding
\Phi\left(x\right)
in powers of
\hbar

\Phi\left(x\right)=\Phi_{0}\left(x\right)+\hbar\Phi_{1}\left(x\right)+\hbar^{2}\Phi_{2}\left(x\right)+...\,,

(59)

then substituting Eq. (
59
) into Eq. (
56
) gives

\left(\Phi_{0}^{\prime}+\hbar\Phi_{1}^{\prime}+...\right)^{2}-i\hbar\left(\Phi_{0}^{\prime\prime}+\hbar\Phi_{1}^{\prime\prime}+...\right)=p^{2}_{x}\,.

(60)

Equating terms in powers of
\hbar
, in Eq. (
60
), I see

\pm\Phi_{0}^{\prime}=p_{x}\,,\qquad 2\Phi_{0}^{\prime}\Phi_{1}^{\prime}-i\Phi_{0}^{\prime\prime}=0\,,

(61)

so that

\Phi_{0}=\pm\int p_{x}dx\,,\qquad\Phi_{1}=\dfrac{i}{2}\log_{e}\Phi_{0}^{\prime}\,.

(62)

Eqs. (
62
), Eq. (
59
), and Eq. (
53
) give

\widetilde{\Psi}\approx\dfrac{\exp{\left(\pm\dfrac{i}{\hbar}\int p_{x}dx\right)}}{\sqrt{p_{x}}}\,.

(63)

Extension to periodic structures

Consider the periodic potential

(72)

Eq. (

) is a Fourier expansion of the potential, where

and

is positive and negative integers. The wavefunction follows from Bloch’s theorem for periodic crystals

(73)

where
g=2\pi m/a
and

is positive and negative integers.

Substituting Eq. (

) and Eq. (

) into

\left[\mp\mathcal{L}-\left(E\pm mc^{2}+V\right)\right]\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(\pm\right)}=0

(74)

and multiplying through by
\exp{(i(G^{\prime}-k)x/\hbar)}
, where
G^{\prime}=2\pi n^{\prime}/a
, then integrating
x
over one period
a
, I find that the first, second, and third terms are only non-zero for
g=G^{\prime}
while the fourth term is only non-zero for
g-G=G^{\prime}
. Therefore, with rearrangement

\sum_{g}\left(\delta_{gG^{\prime}}W_{g}-V_{g-G^{\prime}}\right)A_{g}=\left(E\pm mc^{2}\right)A_{G^{\prime}}\,,

(75)

where

W_{g}=-c\left(k-g\right)\alpha^{(\pm)}_{k-g}\,.

(76)

The eigenvalue problem described by Eq. (

) can be solved for varying

to give the eigenvalues
\left(E\pm mc^{2}\right)
. The relationship between

and

defines the band structure.

For planar structures of period

I have by Bloch’s theorem

\widetilde{\Psi}\left(x+D\right)=\exp{\left(\dfrac{iKD}{\hbar}\right)}\widetilde{\Psi}\left(x\right)\,,

(77)

which in terms of transfer matrices can be written as

M\widetilde{\Psi}=\exp{\left(\dfrac{iKD}{\hbar}\right)}\widetilde{\Psi}=\lambda\widetilde{\Psi}\implies\left|M-\lambda I\right|=0\,,

(78)

M=\begin{pmatrix}{M_{11}}&{M_{12}}\\

+\dfrac{\left(mc^{2}-m^{*}{c^{*}}^{2}\right)\left(mc^{2}+m^{*}{c^{*}}^{2}\right)}{\left(m^{*}c^{*}\right)^{2}}\dfrac{1}{k^{2}}\,.

(86)

In this work I have developed Dirac fermion transfer matrix methods, for all energies, in the FW representations. I have also developed WKB approximations, to all orders, and discussed the validity of the approximations. I have introduced connection formulae between regions of real and imaginary fermion momentum.

I have demonstrated the applicability of my method to

Appendix A
Discussion of commutation

In this appendix I will provide a discussion of

\mathcal{L}=-\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}c\hat{p}_{x}=-c\hat{p}_{x}\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}\,.

(101)

The operator
\mathcal{L}
takes the form

\ \mathcal{L}=mc^{2}\left[\dfrac{1}{2}\left[{\dfrac{\hat{p}}{mc}}\right]^{2}-\dfrac{1}{8}\left[{\dfrac{\hat{p}}{mc}}\right]^{4}+\dfrac{1}{16}\left[{\dfrac{\hat{p}}{mc}}\right]^{6}-...\right]\,,

(102)

where

\dfrac{1}{2}\left[{\dfrac{\hat{p}}{mc}}\right]^{2}=-\dfrac{\hbar^{2}}{2m^{2}c^{2}}\left[\dfrac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+\dfrac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}\right]\,,

(103)

\dfrac{1}{8}\left[{\dfrac{\hat{p}}{mc}}\right]^{4}=\dfrac{\hbar^{4}}{8m^{4}c^{4}}\left[\dfrac{d^{4}}{dx^{4}}+2\dfrac{d^{4}}{dy^{2}dx^{2}}+\dfrac{d^{4}}{dy^{4}}\right]\,,

(104)

and the series of polynomial differential operators continues up to
n^{th}
order. It is clear that I can write

\dfrac{d^{n+m}}{dx^{n}dy^{m}}=\dfrac{d}{dx}\dfrac{d^{n+m-1}}{dx^{n-1}dy^{m}}=\dfrac{d^{n+m-1}}{dx^{n-1}dy^{m}}\dfrac{d}{dx}\,.

(105)

However, it is also the case that

\dfrac{d^{n}}{dy^{n}}f\left(x,y\right)=\dfrac{d}{dx}\dfrac{dx}{dy}\dfrac{d^{n-1}f}{dy^{n-1}}=\dfrac{d^{n-1}}{dy^{n-1}}\dfrac{dx}{dy}\dfrac{df}{dx}

(106)

when every term in
f
is a function of not just
y
but also
x
. I see from Eq. (
105
) and Eq. (
106
) that I can extract
d/dx
from either side of the operator
\mathcal{L}
, in these cases.

When
f(x,y)=f(y)
, then in general

\dfrac{d^{n}}{dy^{n}}f\left(y\right)\neq 0\,,

(107)

but

\dfrac{d}{dx}f\left(y\right)=0\,,

(108)

therefore in general

0\neq\dfrac{d^{n}}{dy^{n}}f\left(y\right)\neq\dfrac{d^{n-1}}{dy^{n-1}}\dfrac{dx}{dy}\dfrac{d}{dx}f\left(y\right)=0\,.

(109)

In the case described by Eq. (
109
) I cannot extract
d/dx
from either side of the operator
d^{n}/dy^{n}
.

Eq. (
101
) is true when I assume that
\mathcal{L}
acts on a function of not just
y
but also
x

\left[c{\bf\alpha}\cdot\left(\hat{p}-A\right)\right]\Psi=E\Psi\,.

(122)

Using the approach of Section
2
, Eq. (
122
) can be transformed to

\left[\mp c\sqrt{\left(\hat{p}-A\right)^{2}}\right]\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(\pm\right)}=E\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(\pm\right)}\,.

(123)

Taylor expanding Eq. (
123
) around
\hat{p}=0
gives

\left[\mp\mathcal{M}-\left(E\pm cA_{y}\right)\right]\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(\pm\right)}=0\,,

(124)

where
\mathcal{M}
is the corresponding linear differential operator. Using the approach of Section
4
I calculate, that at the barrier, the wavefunction is continuous and also continuous with respect to
\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{M}}}
, where I define

\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{M}}}\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(\pm\right)}=\pm\dfrac{E\pm cA_{y}}{cp_{x}}\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(\pm\right)}\,.

(125)

I now return to the Dirac spinor representation. In the region
A=A_{y}\hat{y}\neq 0
, the general solution for
\hat{p}=\pm q_{x}\hat{x}
is given by
[
22
,
23
]

\Psi=\begin{pmatrix}+E\\
\pm cq_{x}-icA_{y}\end{pmatrix}\exp{\left(\pm\dfrac{iq_{x}x}{\hbar}\right)}\,,

(126)

where
cq_{x}=\sqrt{E^{2}-c^{2}A_{y}^{2}}
. The reflection is calculated by matching wavefunctions at the step in vector potential, similar to
Appendix C
for a step in electrostatic potential.

Making use of my boundary conditions I find the reflection coefficient
R
takes the form of Eq. (
120
), with

\kappa_{d}=\dfrac{c^{*}}{c}\dfrac{q_{x}-iA_{y}}{p_{x}}

(127)

for the Dirac spinor representation and

\kappa_{f}=\dfrac{cp_{x}}{c^{*}q_{x}}\dfrac{E\pm c^{*}A_{y}}{E}

(128)

for the FW representation.
\hat{p}=p_{x}\hat{x}=(E/c)\hat{x}
when
A=0
.

The conflict between Eq. (
127
) and Eq. (
128
) indicates
R
calculated in the FW representation does not reproduce
R
calculated in the Dirac spinor representation. This suggests that FW wavefunctions are unsuitable for calculating tunneling through magnetic barriers. However, this conflict might be resolved by employing the form of the FW transformation in Ref.
[
11
]
that derives the Pauli equation from the Dirac equation in the non-relativistic limit.

Appendix D
Analytic Green’s functions in one dimension

Consider

\left[\mp\mathcal{L}-\left(V+E\pm mc^{2}\right)\right]G^{\left(\pm\right)}\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)=\delta\left(x-x^{\prime}\right)\,.

(129)

Let
\widetilde{\Psi}_{L}
and
\widetilde{\Psi}_{R}
be the solutions of Eq. (
18
) which separately satisfy the boundary conditions
at
x=-a
and
x=+a
. The GF of Eq. (
129
) for the case of a
1
D barrier
can be written as

G^{\left(\pm\right)}\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)=\dfrac{\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(\pm\right)}_{L}\left(x_{<}\right)\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(\pm\right)}_{R}\left(x_{>}\right)}{{W}^{\left(\pm\right)}}\,,

(130)

where
x_{<}=\min{(x,x^{\prime})}
and
x_{>}=\max{(x,x^{\prime})}
and

{W}^{\left(\pm\right)}=\mp\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(\pm\right)}_{L}ic\hbar\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(\pm\right)}_{R}\pm\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(\pm\right)}_{R}ic\hbar\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}\widetilde{\Psi}^{\left(\pm\right)}_{L}

(131)

is the Wronskian, which does not depend on
x
.

To prove Eq. (
130
) and Eq. (
131
), note that by Eq. (
129
)

\mp\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\int^{x^{\prime}+\epsilon}_{x^{\prime}-\epsilon}\mathcal{L}G^{\left(\pm\right)}\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)dx=1\,,

(132)

which implies

\mp ic\hbar\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}G^{\left(\pm\right)}\left(x_{+},x^{\prime}\right)\pm ic\hbar\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}G^{\left(\pm\right)}\left(x_{-},x^{\prime}\right)=1\,,

(133)

then, applying
\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}
to Eq. (
130
) written out explicitly

\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}G\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)=\dfrac{1}{{W}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}\widetilde{\Psi}_{L}\big(x\big)\widetilde{\Psi}_{R}\big(x^{\prime}\big)&\mbox{for}\ \ x<x^{\prime}\,,\\
\widetilde{\Psi}_{L}\big(x^{\prime}\big)\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}\widetilde{\Psi}_{R}\big(x\big)&\mbox{for}\ \ x>x^{\prime}\,,\end{array}\right.

(134)

and substituting into Eq. (
133
) gives Eq. (
131
).

I now calculate the GF for a rectangular barrier of width
2a
.

\widetilde{\Psi}_{1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\exp{\bigg(+\dfrac{imkx}{\hbar}\bigg)}&x>+a\,,\\
C\exp{\left(+\dfrac{im^{*}qx}{\hbar}\right)}+D\exp{\left(-\dfrac{im^{*}qx}{\hbar}\right)}&\left|x\right|<a\,,\\
A\exp{\bigg(+\dfrac{imkx}{\hbar}\bigg)}+B\exp{\bigg(-\dfrac{imkx}{\hbar}\bigg)}&x<-a\,.\end{array}\right.

(135)

Continuity of
\widetilde{\Psi}_{1}(x)
and continuity of
\widetilde{\Psi}_{1}(x)
with respect to
\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}
gives

C=\dfrac{\alpha_{q}+\alpha_{k}}{2\alpha_{q}}\exp{\bigg(+\dfrac{imka}{\hbar}\bigg)}\exp{\bigg(-\dfrac{im^{*}qa}{\hbar}\bigg)}\,,

(136)

D=\dfrac{\alpha_{q}-\alpha_{k}}{2\alpha_{q}}\exp{\bigg(+\dfrac{imka}{\hbar}\bigg)}\exp{\bigg(+\dfrac{im^{*}qa}{\hbar}\bigg)}\,.

(137)

\widetilde{\Psi}_{2}
the left hand solution is given by
\widetilde{\Psi}_{2}(+x)=\widetilde{\Psi}_{1}(-x)
. Therefore I evaluate Eq. (
130
) to give
G(+a,-a)
:

\dfrac{i\alpha_{q}}{ic\hbar\left(\alpha^{2}_{q}+\alpha^{2}_{k}\right)\sin{\left(\dfrac{2m^{*}qa}{\hbar}\right)}-2c\hbar\alpha_{q}\alpha_{k}\cos{\left(\dfrac{2m^{*}qa}{\hbar}\right)}}\,.

(138)

In Eq. (
138
), the Wronskian is given by

{W}=2ic\hbar\alpha_{q}\left(C^{2}-D^{2}\right)\,.

(139)

The transmission for a barrier embedded in a vacuum is given as the ratio of
G(+a,-a)
and the free space GF
G^{fs}(+a,-a)
:

T(k)=|2c\hbar\alpha_{k}G\left(+a,-a\right)|^{2}=\left|\dfrac{G\left(+a,-a\right)}{G^{fs}\left(+a,-a\right)}\right|^{2}\,.

(140)

The free space GF can be written as

\left[\mp\mathcal{L}-\left(E\pm mc^{2}\right)\right]G^{(\pm)}\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)=\delta\left(x-x^{\prime}\right)\,,

(141)

therefore by symmetry, when
x^{\prime}=0
,

G^{(\pm)}(x,0)=A^{\left(\pm\right)}\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\exp{\left(-\dfrac{imkx}{\hbar}\right)}&\mbox{for}\ \ x<0\,,\\
\exp{\left(+\dfrac{imkx}{\hbar}\right)}&\mbox{for}\ \ x>0\,.\end{array}\right.

(142)

Integrating Eq. (
141
) across
\left[0_{-},0_{+}\right]
gives

\mp{i}{c\hbar A^{\left(\pm\right)}}\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}\exp{\left(+\dfrac{imk0_{+}}{\hbar}\right)}\pm{i}{c\hbar A^{\left(\pm\right)}}\mathcal{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize$\frown$}\vskip-1.65764pt}}}{{L}}}\exp{\left(-\dfrac{imk0_{-}}{\hbar}\right)}=1

(143)

and letting
0_{+}\rightarrow 0
and
0_{-}\rightarrow 0
, I find

A^{\left(\pm\right)}=\dfrac{1}{2ic\hbar\alpha^{\left(\pm\right)}_{k}}\,.

(144)

Appendix E
Green’s function paradox

I reveal a GF paradox occurring when transforming between the FW and Dirac representations.

Let

\left[H-\left(E+V\right)\right]\mathcal{G}\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)=\delta\left(r-r^{\prime}\right)\hat{1}\,,

(145)

therefore

e^{+iS}\left[H-\left(E+V\right)\right]e^{-iS}e^{+iS}\left[\mathcal{G}\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)\right]e^{-iS}\\
=e^{+iS}\hat{1}e^{-iS}\delta\left(r-r^{\prime}\right)\,.

(146)

However since

e^{+iS}\hat{1}e^{-iS}\delta\left(r-r^{\prime}\right)=\hat{1}\delta\left(r-r^{\prime}\right)\,,

(147)

and since I have already seen in Section
2

e^{+iS}\left[H-\left(E+V\right)\right]e^{-iS}=\left[\mp\mathcal{L}-\left(V+E\pm mc^{2}\right)\right]\hat{1}\,,

(148)

I have

e^{+iS}\left[\mathcal{G}\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)\right]e^{-iS}=G^{\left(\pm\right)}\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)\hat{1}\\
\implies e^{-iS}G^{\left(\pm\right)}\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)e^{+iS}=\mathcal{G}\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)\hat{1}\,.

(149)

Eqs. (
149
) imply that the valid GFs of the Dirac equation are diagonal. Now consider the identity

\left[H-\left(E+V\right)\right]\left[H+\left(E+V\right)\right]K\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)=\hat{1}\delta\left(r-r^{\prime}\right)\,,

(150)

so that

\left[H+\left(E+V\right)\right]K\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)=\mathcal{G}\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)\,.

(151)

Multiplying out the brackets in Eq. (
150
) I obtain

-c^{2}\hbar^{2}\left[\nabla^{2}+\left(\dfrac{p}{\hbar}\right)^{2}\right]K\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)=\delta\left(r-r^{\prime}\right)\,.

(152)

I see from Eq. (
152
) that in
1
D free space (
V=0
):

c^{2}\hbar^{2}K\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)=-\dfrac{\hbar\exp{\left(\dfrac{ip\left|x-x^{\prime}\right|}{\hbar}\right)}}{2ip}\,,

(153)

in
2
D free space:

c^{2}\hbar^{2}K\left(\rho,\rho^{\prime}\right)=\dfrac{i}{4}H^{(1)}_{0}\left(\dfrac{p\left|\rho-\rho^{\prime}\right|}{\hbar}\right)\,,

(154)

and in
3
D free space:

c^{2}\hbar^{2}K\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)=\dfrac{\exp{\left(\dfrac{ip\left|r-r^{\prime}\right|}{\hbar}\right)}}{4\pi\left|r-r^{\prime}\right|}\,.

(155)

Substituting the identity Eq. (
151
) into Eq. (
149
)

e^{+iS}\left[H+\left(E+V\right)\right]e^{-iS}K\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)=G^{(\pm)}\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)\hat{1}

(156)

and so

\left[\sqrt{\hat{p}^{2}c^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}}+\left(E+V\right)\right]K\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)=G^{\left(\pm\right)}\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)\hat{1}\,.

(157)

By inspection of Eq. (
157
) and the energy momentum relation
(E+V)=\sqrt{p^{2}c^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}}
,

G^{\left(\pm\right)}\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)=2\left(E+V\right)K\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)

(158)

and substituting Eq. (
158
) into Eq. (
149
) I also have

\mathcal{G}\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)=2\left(E+V\right)K\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)\,.

(159)

Let us test Eq. (
158
) and Eq. (
159
) by making an analytic comparison between the GF for
1
D free space Eq. (
142
) and the GF composed of Eq. (
158
) and Eq. (
153
):

2EK=-E\dfrac{\exp{\left(\dfrac{ip\left|x-x^{\prime}\right|}{\hbar}\right)}}{i\hbar pc^{2}}\neq\dfrac{\exp{\left(\dfrac{ip\left|x-x^{\prime}\right|}{\hbar}\right)}}{2ic\hbar\alpha_{k}}

(160)

and so I have an apparent paradox.

Let us attempt to resolve the paradox. I have shown in
Appendix D
that there are two possible forms of the FW GF:

G^{\left(\pm\right)}\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)=\dfrac{\exp{\left(\dfrac{ip\left|x-x^{\prime}\right|}{\hbar}\right)}}{2ic\hbar\alpha^{\left(\pm\right)}_{k}}

(161)

for an electron delta source (
-
) or a positron source (
+
). If I add my two expressions
G^{(\pm)}(x,x^{\prime})
I find

G^{\left(-\right)}\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)+G^{\left(+\right)}\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)=2EK\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)\,.

(162)

However if I try to prove that in general

\mathcal{G}\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)=G^{\left(-\right)}\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)+G^{\left(+\right)}\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)\,,

(163)

I arrive at a contradiction. The Klein-Gordon GF has the continuity conditions of the Klein-Gordon equation,
(E+V)
can be a discontinuous function, therefore
2(E+V)K
can be discontinuous at boundaries. Therefore Eq. (
159
) implies
\mathcal{G}
may be discontinuous.
\mathcal{G}
has the same boundary conditions as
\Psi
, Eq. (
159
) may violate the boundary conditions for
\mathcal{G}
.

Let us now discuss a possible cause of the paradox. To evaluate the FW transformations defined to be

e^{\pm iS}=\cos\theta\pm{\bf\beta}{\bf\alpha}\cdot\dfrac{\hat{p}}{|\hat{p}|}\sin\theta\,,

(164)

I am required to know the momentum
\hat{p}
also at the delta source. To make this calculation of
\hat{p}
at the delta source consider Eq. (
142
) from
Appendix D
,

G\left(x,0\right)=\dfrac{1}{2ic\hbar\alpha_{k}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{lc}\exp{\left(-\dfrac{imkx}{\hbar}\right)}&\mbox{for}\ \ x<0\,,\\
\exp{\left(+\dfrac{imkx}{\hbar}\right)}&\mbox{for}\ \ x>0\,.\end{array}\right.

(165)

I see from Eq. (
165
) that

p_{x}\left(x=0\right)G\left(x,0\right)=\lim_{0_{\pm}\rightarrow 0}\hat{p}G\left(0_{\pm},0\right)={\pm}mkG\left(0,0\right)\,.

(166)

Eq. (
166
) indicates that when
x=x^{\prime}
, the momentum
\hat{p}
is undefined. Therefore at the delta source the FW transformation is undefined. For
1
D space it might be impossible to evaluate
e^{\pm iS}
at the delta source.

Appendix F
Correct free space Foldy-Wouthuysen Green’s functions in two and three dimensions

I begin with the
3
D FW GF in free space

G^{(\pm)}_{\rm 3D}\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)=-\dfrac{p}{c\hbar^{2}\alpha^{(\pm)}_{k}}\dfrac{1}{4\pi\left|r-r^{\prime}\right|}e^{ip|r-r^{\prime}|/\hbar}\,,

(167)

which at the end of this
Appendix F
, I will show to be consistent with the proven
1
D FW GF, Eq. (
142
) and Eq. (
144
). Eq. (
167
) is the solution of

\left[\mp\mathcal{L}-\left(E\pm mc^{2}\right)\right]G^{(\pm)}_{\rm 3D}\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)=\delta_{\rm 3D}\left(r-r^{\prime}\right)\,.

(168)

Eq. (
167
) can be thought of as an outgoing exponential plane FW wavefunction spreading out over a sphere centered on the source, as we move further from the source. In Eq. (
167
) I make a choice of amplitude to obtain agreement with the correct
1
D FW GF.

To derive the
1
D free space FW GF, I integrate Eq. (
167
) in the plane
(y,z)
and find

G^{(\pm)}_{\rm 1D}\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)=\int^{+\infty}_{-\infty}\int^{+\infty}_{-\infty}G^{(\pm)}_{\rm 3D}\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)dydz\,,

(169)

where
r=\left(x,y,z\right)
. Eq. (
169
) is a consequence of

\delta_{\rm 1D}\left(x-x^{\prime}\right)=\int^{+\infty}_{-\infty}\int^{+\infty}_{-\infty}\delta_{\rm 3D}\left(r-r^{\prime}\right)dydz\,.

(170)

Let
\rho=\sqrt{y^{2}+z^{2}}
in the cylindrical coordinates
(\rho,\theta,x)
, and write
\xi=\sqrt{(x-x^{\prime})^{2}+\rho^{2}}
, so that Eq. (
169
) becomes

G^{(\pm)}_{\rm 1D}\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)=-\dfrac{p}{c\hbar^{2}\alpha^{(\pm)}_{k}}\int^{2\pi}_{0}\int^{\infty}_{0}\dfrac{1}{4\pi\xi}e^{ip\xi/\hbar}\rho d\rho d\theta\,.

(171)

Integrating Eq. (
171
) with respect to
\theta
and changing variable to
\xi
gives, using the chain rule

d\xi=\dfrac{\rho}{\sqrt{(x-x^{\prime})^{2}+\rho^{2}}}d\rho=\dfrac{\rho}{\xi}d\rho\,,

(172)

noting in the integration limits that I have
\xi=|x-x^{\prime}|
as a minimum when
\rho=0
:

G^{(\pm)}_{\rm 1D}\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)=-\dfrac{p}{c\hbar^{2}\alpha^{(\pm)}_{k}}\int^{\infty}_{|x-x^{\prime}|}\dfrac{1}{2}e^{ip\xi/\hbar}d\xi\,.

(173)

Eq. (
173
) can be integrated while dropping the oscillating upper integration limit at
\xi=\infty
, assuming the contributions at
\xi=\infty
cancel out leading to a zero net contribution,

\int^{\infty}_{|x-x^{\prime}|}\dfrac{1}{2}e^{ip\xi/\hbar}d\xi=\left[\dfrac{\hbar}{2ip}e^{ip\xi/\hbar}\right]^{\infty}_{|x-x^{\prime}|}=-\dfrac{\hbar}{2ip}e^{ip|x-x^{\prime}|/\hbar}\,.

(174)

Hence I arrive at the required result, Eq. (
142
) combined with Eq. (
144
):

G^{(\pm)}_{\rm 1D}\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)=\dfrac{p}{c\hbar^{2}\alpha^{(\pm)}_{k}}\dfrac{\hbar}{2ip}e^{ip|x-x^{\prime}|/\hbar}=\dfrac{e^{ip|x-x^{\prime}|/\hbar}}{2ic\hbar\alpha^{(\pm)}_{k}}\,.

(175)

The integration in Eq. (
174
) can be seen clearly when I take
p
to be complex with even only a vanishingly small imaginary part so that the upper integration limit is an exponentially decaying function, rather than just an oscillating function.

An interesting comparison can be made between the FW GF Eqs. (
167
) and Eqs. (
175
) and their Klein-Gordon counterparts, Eqs. (
155
) and Eqs. (
153
), which were derived in
Appendix E
. The derivation in this
Appendix F
can be repeated, as a simple checking exercise, for Eqs. (
155
) and Eqs. (
153
). These comparisons and derivations, repeated in
1
D and
2
D space, suggest that the
2
D confined free space FW GF is given by

G^{(\pm)}_{\rm 2D}\left(r,r^{\prime}\right)=-\dfrac{p}{c\hbar^{2}\alpha^{(\pm)}_{k}}\dfrac{i}{4}H^{(1)}_{0}\left(p\left|r-r^{\prime}\right|/\hbar\right)\,.

(176)

Eq. (
167
) and Eq. (
176
) can be simplified with

\dfrac{p}{c\hbar^{2}\alpha^{(\pm)}_{k}}=-\dfrac{E\mp mc^{2}}{c^{2}\hbar^{2}}\,.

(177)

I note that since the FW Eq. (
18
) is a linear differential equation, the Dyson equations for the FW GFs are available for all types of Born approximation
[
12
,
13
]
. There might be cases where the decoupling of electron and positron states by the FW approach could simplify or eliminate certain divergences in the Dyson series, potentially addressing aspects of the renormalization problem.

Automation of antenna subtraction in colour space: gluonic processes Preprint: CERN-TH-2022-047 IPPP/22/15 KA-TP-05-2022 P3H-22-030 ZU-TH 10/22
X. Chen a,b , T. Gehrmann c , E.W.N. Glover d , A. Huss e , M. Marcoli c

Abstract
We present the colourful antenna subtraction method, a reformulation of the antenna subtraction scheme for next-to-next-to-leading order (NNLO) calculations in QCD. The aim of this new approach is to achieve a general and process-independent construction of the subtraction infrastructure at NNLO. We rely on the predictability of the infrared singularity structure of one- and two-loop amplitudes in colour space to generate virtual subtraction terms and, subsequently, we define an automatable procedure to systematically infer the expression of the real subtraction terms, guided by the correspondence between unintegrated and integrated antenna functions. To demonstrate the applicability of the described approach, we compute the full colour NNLO correction to gluonic three-jet production p ​ p ​ ( g ​ g ) → g ​ g ​ g pp(gg)\to ggg , in the gluons-only assumption.

Introduction

Precision measurements of benchmark cross sections are an important pillar of the LHC physics program. In combining these measurements
with equally precise theory predictions, fundamental Standard Model parameters are measured to high accuracy, and indirect constraints on
physics beyond the Standard Model are obtained. The success of the LHC precision physics program relies crucially on
a close interplay between theory and experiment, which calls in particular for highly accurate theoretical predictions.
These predictions are obtained through a perturbation theory expansion to sufficiently high order.
The workflow for the computation of next-to-leading order (NLO) perturbative corrections in QCD and in the electroweak theory combines
the automated generation of one-loop virtual corrections
Ellis:2011cr
with multi-purpose event generator
programs
Alioli:2010xd
;
Alwall:2014hca
;
Kallweit:2015dum
;
Bellm:2015jjp
, enabling NLO-accurate predictions for
any collider process.

Calculations at next-to-next-to-leading order (NNLO) and beyond are performed on a case-by-case basis
Heinrich:2020ybq
and are mostly
limited to final-states corresponding to an underlying two-to-two scattering kinematics. This limitation to low-multiplicity processes arises
from two causes: missing two-loop virtual corrections to higher-multiplicity scattering amplitudes and computational complexity of the real
radiation corrections. The real radiation contributions develop infrared singularities related to soft and collinear particle emissions, which
become explicit only after phase space integration, and which require an infrared subtraction method for the extraction of
the singular contributions, thereby enabling the numerical implementation of the finite remainders.
Several subtraction methods have been developed for NNLO
calculations
Catani:2007vq
;
Gehrmann-DeRidder:2005btv
;
Currie:2013vh
;
Czakon:2010td
;
Boughezal:2011jf
;
Gaunt:2015pea
;
DelDuca:2016ily
;
Caola:2017dug
. Implementations using these methods are largely made on a process-by-process basis, and most methods
scale either poorly or not at all to higher multiplicities.

Important progress has been made most recently on the derivation of two-loop
2\to 3
scattering
amplitudes
Abreu:2019odu
;
Abreu:2021oya
;
Abreu:2021asb
;
Chawdhry:2020for
;
Chawdhry:2021mkw
;
Badger:2021nhg
;
Badger:2021imn
;
Badger:2021ega
;
Badger:2022ncb
, which already entered the calculations for
three-photon production
Chawdhry:2019bji
;
Kallweit:2020gcp
,
diphoton-plus-jet production
Chawdhry:2021hkp
;
Badger:2021ohm

and three-jet production
Czakon:2021mjy
. Among these, only three-jet production contains three final-state QCD objects at Born level and
displays the full complexity of real radiation corrections to
2\to 3
processes. Its NNLO
infrared subtraction has been performed with a sector-improved
residue subtraction approach
Czakon:2010td
;
Czakon:2014oma
. The large variety of phenomenologically
relevant high-multiplicity processes for with NNLO predictions will be in future demand highlights the importance to automate the
workflow of infrared subtraction leading to an algorithmic construction of the subtraction terms in a form directly suitable for numerical implementation.

It is the objective of this paper to enable the automation of
the NNLO antenna subtraction method
Gehrmann-DeRidder:2005btv
;
Currie:2013vh
, which is based up to now on the identification of
single and double real radiation patterns in colour-ordered subprocess contributions. This method has been applied successfully in
computing NNLO corrections to a variety of hadron-collider processes
Gehrmann-DeRidder:2015wbt
;
Chen:2016zka
;
Currie:2017eqf
;
Gehrmann-DeRidder:2017mvr
;
Cruz-Martinez:2018rod
;
Chen:2019zmr
;
Gauld:2019yng
;
Gehrmann:2020oec
;
Gauld:2021ule
.
Working towards a fully automated workflow, we
propose a complete reformulation, named colourful antenna subtraction, based on a colour-space representation of parton-level
subprocesses and subtraction terms. We fully formulate the new method for gluonic processes, and demonstrate its implementation
by generating and computing the NNLO gluons-only contribution to three-jet production at hadron colliders.

The paper is structured as follows. In section
2
we introduce our notation and summarize the colour space techniques. In sections
3
and
4
we delineate the application of the colourful antenna subtraction method for the construction of the subtraction terms at NLO and NNLO. The proof-of-principle application to gluonic three-jet production at NNLO is presented in section
5

We conclude in section
6
with an overview of the remaining steps required to include quarks in this new approach.

2
Colour space

2.1
Gluon amplitudes in colour space

We summarize here the colour space formalism (also called colour dipole formalism) and the associated notation. We choose to treat the QCD amplitudes as objects in colour space since the singularity structure of QCD loop amplitudes is best described working in this framework, as discussed in
Catani:1996jh
;
Catani:1998bh
;
Becher:2009qa
;
DelDuca:2016ily
. In colour space, a
\ell
-loop amplitude with
n
external partons is represented by an abstract vector
\ket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\ell}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})}
. If a set of generating vectors
\left\{\mathbfcal{C}_{n,i}^{\ell}\right\}
is defined, which span the
n
-parton colour space, any amplitude can be decomposed as:

\ket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\ell}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})}=\sum_{i\in I^{\ell}}\mathbfcal{C}_{n,i}^{\ell}\,A^{\ell}_{n,i}(\left\{p\right\}_{n}),

(1)

where
I^{\ell}
indicates a suitable subset of generating vectors. The scalar quantities
A^{\ell}_{n,i}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})
are colour-ordered partial amplitudes. In equation (
1
), the dependence on the helicities of the external partons is implicit and in the following a sum over helicity configurations is always assumed when squared quantities are considered. For
\ell\geq 1
, the dependence on the renormalization scale
\mu_{r}
is understood. Our convention is to strip the partial amplitudes of overall coefficients such as couplings and incoming particles average factors, which are inserted later at the cross section level. In particular, we strip an
\ell
-loop amplitude of an overall factor
\left(\tfrac{\alpha_{s}\bar{C}(\epsilon)}{2\pi}\right)^{\ell}
with respect to the corresponding tree-level amplitude, where
\overline{C}(\epsilon)=(4\pi)^{\epsilon}e^{-\gamma_{E}\epsilon}
.

We focus on amplitudes involving only gluons, for which a convenient choice of generating vectors is given by the so-called trace basis or Chan-Paton basis, which consists of traces of
SU(N_{c})
generators in the fundamental representation
Paton:1969je
;
Mangano:1987xk
. For tree-level amplitudes, the basis corresponds to:

\mathbfcal{C}_{n,\sigma}^{0}=\tr(\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma(1)}}\dots\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma(n)}}),\quad\text{with}\quad\sigma\in S_{n}/Z_{n}

(2)

where
S_{n}/Z_{n}
represents the group of non-cyclic permutations of
n
objects. The
SU(N_{c})
generators are normalized according to:

\tr(\bm{T}^{a}\bm{T}^{b})=\dfrac{1}{2}\delta^{ab}.

(3)

With this choice, a tree-level amplitude with
n
external gluons is given by
Berends:1987cv
:

\ket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}/Z_{n}}\tr(\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma(1)}}\dots\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma(n)}})A_{n}^{0}(\sigma(p_{1}),\dots,\sigma(p_{n})),

(4)

and the corresponding squared matrix element
\mathcal{M}_{n}^{0}
can be computed as its squared norm in colour space:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n}^{0}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})|\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})}

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg.\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in S_{n}/Z_{n}}\left(\mathbfcal{C}^{0}_{n,\sigma}\right)^{\dagger}\mathbfcal{C}^{0}_{n,\sigma^{\prime}}A_{n}^{0}(\sigma(\left\{p\right\}_{n}))^{\dagger}A_{n}^{0}(\sigma^{\prime}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})),

where a sum over colour indices is assumed.

For one-loop amplitudes with gluons only, both as external and internal particles, we have an analogous colour decomposition
Bern:1990ux
:

\ket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{1}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})}=\sum_{c=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}/S_{n,c}}\mathbfcal{C}^{1}_{n,c,\sigma}A^{1}_{n,c}(\sigma(p_{1}),\dots,\sigma(p_{n})),

(6)

where the generating one-loop vectors in colour space are given by:

\displaystyle\mathbfcal{C}^{1}_{n,1,\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{c}\,\mathbfcal{C}^{0}_{n,\sigma}=N_{c}\tr(\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma(1)}}\dots\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma(n)}}),

\displaystyle\mathbfcal{C}^{1}_{n,c,\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tr(\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma(1)}}\dots\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma(c-1)}})\tr(\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma(c)}}\dots\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma(n)}}),\quad\text{for }c>1,

(7)

and
S_{n,c}
represents the subgroup of
S_{n}
which leaves the trace structure of
\mathbfcal{C}^{1}_{n,c}
unaffected. We notice that
\mathbfcal{C}^{1}_{n,2,\sigma}=0
, since the
SU(N_{c})
generators are traceless. The squared one-loop matrix element
\mathcal{M}_{n}^{1}
is given by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n}^{1}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})|\mathcal{A}_{n}^{1}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})}+\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{1}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})|\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})}

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\text{Re}\Bigg\{\Bigg.\sum_{c=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in S_{n}/Z_{n}\\
\sigma^{\prime}\in S_{n}/S_{n,c}\end{subarray}}\left(\mathbfcal{C}^{0}_{n,\sigma}\right)^{\dagger}\mathbfcal{C}^{1}_{n,c,\sigma}A_{n}^{0}(\sigma(\left\{p\right\}_{n}))^{\dagger}A_{n,c}^{1}(\sigma^{\prime}(\left\{p\right\}_{n}))\Bigg.\Bigg\}.,

We note that, both for
\ell=0
and
\ell=1
, the colour-ordered partial amplitudes are not all independent quantities. Several relations can be defined among them such as reflection identities or decoupling equations
Berends:1987me
;
Mangano:1987xk
;
Kleiss:1988ne
;
Bern:1990ux
.

2.2
Gluon exchange in colour space

The coherent emission of a gluon between a dipole formed by parton
i
and parton
j
in a tree-level amplitude is described in colour space by colour correlators as

\bra{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})}\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j}\ket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})},

(9)

where
\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j}=T^{a}_{i}T^{a}_{j}
. These properties hold:

\displaystyle\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bm{T}_{j}\cdot\bm{T}_{i},

(10)

\displaystyle\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bm{T}_{i}^{2}=C_{i}\,\mathbf{Id},

(11)

where
C_{i}
is the Casimir coefficient for the
SU(N_{c})
representation associated to parton
i
and
\mathbf{Id}
represents the identity operator in colour space. For gluons we have
C_{g}=C_{A}
.

For tree amplitudes with only gluons in the trace basis representation, it is possible to compute colour correlators in (
9
) using:

\displaystyle\braket{\mathbfcal{C}_{n,\sigma}^{0}||}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\tr(\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma(1)}}\dots\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma(n)}})\right)^{\dagger}\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j}\tr(\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma^{\prime}(1)}}\dots\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma^{\prime}(n)}})

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\tr(\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma(1)}}\dots\bm{T}^{a_{i}}\dots\bm{T}^{a_{j}}\dots\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma(n)}})\right)^{\dagger}\,\left(T_{i}\right)^{a}_{a_{i}b_{i}}\left(T_{j}\right)^{a}_{a_{j}b_{j}}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\times\tr(\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma^{\prime}(1)}}\dots\bm{T}^{b_{i}}\dots\bm{T}^{b_{j}}\dots\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma^{\prime}(n)}}),

where, for either incoming or outgoing gluons,
\bm{T}_{i}
is in the adjoint representation:

\left(T_{i}\right)^{a}_{bc}=if_{bac}.

(13)

In fact, for the purpose of an NNLO calculation, the single colour dipole insertion at tree-level in (
9
) is not sufficient. The computation of colour correlators representing the exchange of a gluon at one-loop level

\bra{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{1}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})}(\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j})\ket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})}+\bra{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})}(\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j})\ket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{1}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})}

(14)

and the exchange of two gluons at tree-level

\bra{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})}(\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j})(\bm{T}_{k}\cdot\bm{T}_{l})\ket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})}.

(15)

is necessary. It is straightforward
DelDuca:2016ily
to extend (
12
) to compute (
14
) and (
15
) when both
k
and
l
are different from
i
or
j
, namely the gluons are exchanged between two separated dipoles. When a radiator in the second dipole coincides with one in the first dipole, the two gluons are emitted from the same leg, and the colour algebra must be computed accordingly. The same applies if both partons in the second dipole coincide with the ones in the first. For example, if
k=i
and
l\neq j
one has:

\displaystyle\left(\tr(\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma(1)}}\dots\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma(n)}})\right)^{\dagger}(\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j})(\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{l})\tr(\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma^{\prime}(1)}}\dots\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma^{\prime}(n)}})

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\tr(\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma(1)}}\dots\bm{T}^{a_{i}}\dots\bm{T}^{a_{j}}\dots\bm{T}^{a_{l}}\dots\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma(n)}})\right)^{\dagger}\left[\left(T_{i}\right)^{a}_{a_{i}c}\left(T_{j}\right)^{a}_{a_{j}b_{j}}\right]\left[\left(T_{i}\right)^{b}_{cb_{i}}\left(T_{l}\right)^{b}_{a_{l}b_{l}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\times\tr(\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma^{\prime}(1)}}\dots\bm{T}^{b_{i}}\dots\bm{T}^{b_{l}}\dots\bm{T}^{b_{j}}\dots\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma^{\prime}(n)}}).

Finally, we point out that each state
\ket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\ell}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})}
is a colour singlet and so by colour conservation:

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bm{T}_{i}\ket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{\ell}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})}=0.

(17)

Since in what follows we always consider colour singlet states, we can employ the previous identity as
\sum_{j\neq i}\bm{T}_{j}=-\bm{T}_{i}
.

2.3
Leading and subleading colour

When squared matrix elements like (
5
) and (
8
), or colour operator insertions such as (
9
), (
14
) and (
15
) are computed, the result is a real function of the external momenta and possibly the renormalization scale. At tree-level, the general form of such a function is given by:

f_{0}\left(\set{p}_{n}\right)=\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in S_{n}/Z_{n}}c^{0}_{n}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})\,a_{n}^{0}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime};\left\{p\right\}_{n}),

(18)

where the coefficients
c^{0}_{n}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})
are colour factors which depend on
N_{c}
and the number of gluons
n
and

a_{n}^{0}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime};\left\{p\right\}_{n})=\begin{cases}\left|A_{n}^{0}(\sigma(\left\{p\right\}_{n}))\right|^{2}\quad&\text{if }\sigma=\sigma^{\prime},\\
A_{n}^{0}(\sigma(\left\{p\right\}_{n}))^{\dagger}A_{n}^{0}(\sigma^{\prime}(\left\{p\right\}_{n}))\quad&\text{if }\sigma\neq\sigma^{\prime}.\end{cases}

(19)

This quantity represents the squared interference of two colour-ordered partial amplitudes, with generic colour orderings dictated by
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
. When
\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}
, we have squared coherent partial amplitudes, while we refer to the case
\sigma\neq\sigma^{\prime}
as incoherent interference. We notice that even in the case
\sigma\neq\sigma^{\prime}
,
a_{n}^{0}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime};\left\{p\right\}_{n})
is a real quantity, since a sum over helicities is assumed. Indeed, if we consider a helicity configuration for the external particles
\{h\}_{n}
, the opposite configuration where the helicities are all swapped, denoted by
\{-h\}_{n}
also appears in the sum. Restoring the helicity dependence in partial amplitudes, by charge conjugation at tree level we have:

A_{n}^{0}(\sigma(\left\{p\right\}_{n},\{-h\}_{n}))=A_{n}^{0}(\sigma(\left\{p\right\}_{n},\{h\}_{n}))^{\dagger},

(20)

and so

\displaystyle A_{n}^{0}(\sigma(\left\{p\right\}_{n},\{h\}_{n}))^{\dagger}A_{n}^{0}(\sigma^{\prime}(\left\{p\right\}_{n},\{h\}_{n}))+A_{n}^{0}(\sigma(\left\{p\right\}_{n},\{-h\}_{n}))^{\dagger}A_{n}^{0}(\sigma^{\prime}(\left\{p\right\}_{n},\{-h\}_{n}))

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{n}^{0}(\sigma(\left\{p\right\}_{n},\{h\}_{n}))^{\dagger}A_{n}^{0}(\sigma^{\prime}(\left\{p\right\}_{n},\{h\}_{n}))+A_{n}^{0}(\sigma(\left\{p\right\}_{n},\{h\}_{n}))A_{n}^{0}(\sigma^{\prime}(\left\{p\right\}_{n},\{h\}_{n}))^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\text{Re}\left[A_{n}^{0}(\sigma(\left\{p\right\}_{n},\{h\}_{n}))^{\dagger}A_{n}^{0}(\sigma^{\prime}(\left\{p\right\}_{n},\{h\}_{n}))\right],

which implies that the sum over helicities is indeed real. Analogously, at one loop we have:

f_{1}\left(\set{p}_{n}\right)=\sum_{c=1}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in S_{n}/S_{n,c}\\
\sigma^{\prime}\in S_{n}/Z_{n}\end{subarray}}c^{1}_{n,c}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})\,a_{n,c}^{1}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime};\left\{p\right\}_{n}),

(22)

where

a_{n,c}^{1}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime};\left\{p\right\}_{n})=2\text{Re}\left[A_{n}^{0}(\sigma(\left\{p\right\}_{n}))^{\dagger}A_{n,c}^{1}(\sigma^{\prime}(\left\{p\right\}_{n}))\right].

(23)

The
N_{c}
dependence of (
18
) ad (
22
) is in general given by:

f_{\ell}=\dfrac{P(N_{c})}{N_{c}^{m}},

(24)

where

is a polynomial in
N_{c}
. The degree of

and the integer
m
depend on
\ell
and the number of gluons

. It is indeed possible to predict which is the highest power of

The

approximation is defined retaining only the terms coming with the power of
N_{c}
indicated in Table

\frac{N_{c}^{2}-1}{Nc^{2}}
in the definition of the leading colour contribution since this factor is ubiquitous in our calculations and partially retains subleading terms without affecting the complexity. We define the operator
\mathcal{LC}(\cdot)
which extracts the leading colour part of a given quantity. At leading colour, (
18
) and (
22
) have a particularly simple form, since only the coherent contributions with
\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}
survive. This convenient behaviour follows from the fact that the trace basis behaves as an actual orthogonal basis at leading colour:

\mathcal{LC}\left[\left(\tr(\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma(1)}}\dots\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma(n)}})\right)^{\dagger}\tr(\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma^{\prime}(1)}}\dots\bm{T}^{a_{\sigma^{\prime}(n)}})\right]=\dfrac{N_{c}^{n-2}(N_{c}^{2}-1)}{2^{n}}\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}.

(25)

which is a direct consequence of Fierz identity for

T^{a}_{ij}T^{a}_{kl}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}-\dfrac{1}{N_{c}}\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}\right).

(26)

), it is straightforward to obtain the expression for squared matrix elements at leading colour:

\mathcal{LC}\left[\mathcal{M}_{n}^{0}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})\right]=(N_{c})^{n-2}\dfrac{(N_{c}^{2}-1)}{2^{n}}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}/Z_{n}}\left|A_{n}^{0}(\sigma(\left\{p\right\}_{n}))\right|^{2}

(27)

and

\mathcal{LC}\left[\mathcal{M}_{n}^{1}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})\right]=(N_{c})^{n-1}\dfrac{(N_{c}^{2}-1)}{2^{n}}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}/Z_{n}}2\text{Re}\left[A_{n}^{0}(\sigma(\left\{p\right\}_{n}))^{\dagger}A_{n,1}^{1}(\sigma(\left\{p\right\}_{n}))\right].

(28)

\displaystyle\mathcal{LC}\left[\braket{\mathbfcal{C}_{n,\sigma}^{0}||}\right]

\displaystyle-\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\chi_{\sigma}(i,j)\left(\dfrac{N_{c}}{2}\right)\dfrac{N_{c}^{n-2}(N_{c}^{2}-1)}{2^{n}},

(29)

\displaystyle\mathcal{LC}\left[\braket{\mathbfcal{C}_{n,1,\sigma}^{1}||}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{c}\,\mathcal{LC}\left[\braket{\mathbfcal{C}_{n,\sigma}^{0}||}\right]

(30)

and

\mathcal{LC}\left[\braket{\mathbfcal{C}_{n,\sigma}^{0}||}\right]=\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\chi_{\sigma}(i,j)\chi_{\sigma}(k,l)\left(\dfrac{N_{c}}{2}\right)^{2}\dfrac{N_{c}^{n-2}(N_{c}^{2}-1)}{2^{n}},

(31)

where
\chi_{\sigma}(i,j)
is
1
if partons
i
and
j
appear adjacent to each other in the colour ordering represented by
\sigma
and
0
otherwise.

At the subleading colour level, the emergence of incoherent interferences between different colour orderings spoils the pattern observed at leading colour. As pointed out in
Currie:2013vh
, this represented an obstacle to the application of the antenna subtraction method beyond leading colour. When the number of considered partons at the Born level
n_{p}
is small, in particular for
n_{p}<4
, relations among partial amplitudes can be conveniently exploited to convert incoherent interferences into combinations of squared coherent partial amplitudes and proceed as in the leading colour case. However, for
n_{p}\geq 4
this is not possible any more and the treatment of the subleading colour part requires a significant effort. As we show in the rest of this paper, working in colour space allows us to retain the full
N_{c}
dependence in a straightforward way and to consistently approach leading and subleading colour contributions with the same techniques.

In the following sections, we drop the explicit dependence of the amplitudes on the momenta
\left\{p\right\}_{n}
to ease the notation. Moreover, in general we consider Born-level
(n+2)
-parton
amplitudes, to keep
n
as the number of final-state partons. Indices
1
and
2
correspond to initial-state partons, while
i\geq 3
indicates a final-state parton.

3
Colourful antenna subtraction at NLO

The NLO QCD correction to an
n
-jet partonic cross section with parton species
a
and
b
in the initial state is given by:

\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}_{ab,NLO}}=\int_{n}\left(\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{V}_{ab,NLO}}+\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF}_{ab,NLO}}\right)+\int_{n+1}\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{R}_{ab,NLO}},

(32)

where the symbol
\int_{n}
indicates an integration over the
n
final state particles.
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{V}_{ab,NLO}}
and
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{R}_{ab,NLO}}
respectively represent the virtual and real corrections, while
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF}_{ab,NLO}}
is the NLO mass factorization counterterm. The NLO cross section in (
32
), despite being well defined and finite, is not suitable for numerical integration in this form. The virtual correction and the mass factorization contribution contain explicit
\epsilon
-poles and the real correction diverges in soft and collinear infrared (IR) limits. These singularities cancel in the final result, but a proper subtraction procedure is needed to separately remove the singularities in the real and virtual corrections and make both integrals in (
32
) computable with numerical methods.

In the context of antenna subtraction, this is achieved constructing a real subtraction term
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S}_{ab,NLO}}

Currie:2013vh
, which locally removes the singular behaviour of
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{R}_{ab,NLO}}
in the IR limits and can be analytically integrated over the phase space of the unresolved radiation. This latter feature is required to obtain from
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S}_{ab,NLO}}
the virtual subtraction term
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T}_{ab,NLO}}
, which cancels the explicit poles of the virtual correction and contains the mass factorization contribution. The NLO cross section can then be reformulated as:

\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}_{ab,NLO}}=\int_{n}\left[\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{V}_{ab,NLO}}-\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T}_{ab,NLO}}\right]+\int_{n+1}\left[\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{R}_{ab,NLO}}-\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S}_{ab,NLO}}\right],

(33)

with

\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T}_{ab,NLO}}=-\int_{1}\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S}_{ab,NLO}}-\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF}_{ab,NLO}}.

(34)

Both contributions in (
33
) are now free of IR singularities and suitable for a numerical integration through Monte Carlo methods. The procedure we have just depicted represents the traditional antenna subtraction approach: the real subtraction term is constructed first, studying the behaviour of the real radiation matrix elements in soft and collinear limits, and then it is integrated and combined with the mass factorization contribution to obtain the virtual subtraction term.

In the following we give an overview of the colourful antenna subtraction approach at NLO. General subtraction schemes for automated NLO calculations have long been available
Frixione:1995ms
;
Catani:1996jh
, so the purpose of this section is mainly to introduce important concepts concepts behind the new approach, which will be crucial for its application at NNLO. Indeed, despite posing a significantly simpler task with respect to NNLO, some key observations can already be made at the NLO level. The main idea behind the colourful antenna approach is to exploit the predictability of the singularity structure of virtual amplitudes in colour space to directly construct the virtual subtraction term in a general way and subsequently derive the real subtraction term with a systematic procedure.

3.1
IR singularity structure at one loop

The singularity structure of renormalized
(n+2)
-parton one-loop amplitudes in QCD can be described in colour space with
Catani:1998bh
:

\ket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{1}}=\bm{I}^{(1)}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)\ket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}}+\ket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{1,\text{fin}}(\mu_{r}^{2})},

(35)

where
\mu_{r}
is the renormalization scale,
\ket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{1,\text{fin}}(\mu_{r}^{2})}
is a finite remainder and
\bm{I}^{(1)}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)
is the Catani’s IR insertion operator given by
Catani:1998bh
:

\bm{I}^{(1)}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)=\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{e^{\epsilon\gamma_{E}}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\sum_{i=1}^{n+2}\dfrac{1}{\bm{T}_{i}^{2}}\mathcal{V}_{i}(\epsilon)\sum_{j\neq i}(\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j})\left(\dfrac{-s_{ij}}{\mu_{r}^{2}}\right)^{-\epsilon}\,.

(36)

The singular functions
\mathcal{V}_{i}(\epsilon)
contain double and single
\epsilon
-poles. The previous expression can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\bm{I}^{(1)}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n+2}\sum_{j\neq i}\left(\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j}\right)\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{ij}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)

(37)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)}\left(\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j}\right)\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{ij}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right),

where in the last line the sum runs over pairs of partons. For the gluons-only case that is considered here,
we only need the expression of
\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{i_{g}j_{g}}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)
at
N_{f}=0
:

\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{i_{g}j_{g}}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)=\dfrac{e^{\epsilon\gamma_{E}}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\left[\dfrac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}+\dfrac{b_{0}}{\epsilon}\right]\left(\dfrac{-s_{ij}}{\mu_{r}^{2}}\right)^{-\epsilon},

(38)

where
b_{0}
is the gluon component of the one-loop QCD
\beta
-function:

b_{0}=\dfrac{11}{6}\,.

(39)

Using (
35
) it is possible to extract the poles of one-loop matrix elements in the following way:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}oles\left(\mathcal{M}_{n+2}^{1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}oles\left(\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}|\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{1}}+\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{1}|\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}}\right)

(40)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}oles(\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}|\bm{I}^{(1)}\left(\epsilon\right)+\bm{I}^{(1),\dagger}\left(\epsilon\right)|\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}})\,.

The appearance of the sum
\bm{I}^{(1)}\left(\epsilon\right)+\bm{I}^{(1),\dagger}\left(\epsilon\right)
indicates that only the real part of the insertion operator affects the description of the poles at the matrix element level, as expected. In the gluons-only case we can write the previous expression as

\mathcal{P}oles\left(\mathcal{M}_{n+2}^{1}\right)=\mathcal{P}oles\left[\sum_{(i_{g},j_{g})}\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}|\bm{T}_{i_{g}}\cdot\bm{T}_{j_{g}}|\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}}\,2\text{Re}\left(\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{i_{g}j_{g}}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)\right)\right]\,,

(41)

where we moved the colour sandwich outside the real part, since, as argued in section
2.3
it is a real quantity. At the cross section level we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}oles\left({\hat{\sigma}^{V}_{gg,NLO}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{V}_{NLO}\int\text{d}\Phi_{n}(p_{3},\dots,p_{n+2};p_{1},p_{2})\,J_{n}^{(n)}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})

(42)

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{P}oles\left[\sum_{(i_{g},j_{g})}\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}|\bm{T}_{i_{g}}\cdot\bm{T}_{j_{g}}|\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}}2\,\text{Re}\left(\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{i_{g}j_{g}}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)\right)\right].

In (
42
),
\text{d}\Phi_{n}
denotes the standard
(2\to n)
-particle phase space and
J_{n}^{(m)}(\left\{p\right\}_{m})
is the jet algorithm which selects
n
jets from
m
final-state parton momenta. The factor
\mathcal{N}^{V}_{NLO}
is given by

\mathcal{N}^{V}_{NLO}=\left(\dfrac{\alpha_{s}\overline{C}(\epsilon)}{2\pi}\right)\mathcal{N}_{LO},

(43)

where
\mathcal{N}_{LO}
contains the overall factors appropriate for the LO process, such as the strong coupling, symmetry factors and factors coming from the spin- and colour-average over the initial-state partons.

3.2
NLO mass factorization

For processes involving initial-state partons, it is necessary to treat the singularities arising when a final-state parton becomes collinear to the initial-state ones. These singularities are removed defining physical parton density functions which are obtained from the bare ones by means of mass factorization counterterms. At NLO this counterterm is given by

\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF}_{ab,NLO}}=-\left(\dfrac{\alpha_{s}\overline{C}(\epsilon)}{2\pi}\right)\sum_{c,d}\int{\dfrac{\text{d}x_{1}}{x_{1}}}{\dfrac{\text{d}x_{2}}{x_{2}}}\Gamma^{(1)}_{ab;cd}(x_{1},x_{2})\,\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}_{cd,LO}},

(44)

where
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
represent the momentum fractions transferred to the hard process and
\Gamma^{(1)}_{ab;cd}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)
denotes the NLO mass factorization kernel:

\Gamma^{(1)}_{ab;cd}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)=\Gamma^{(1)}_{ca,\text{full}}\left(x_{1}\right)\delta_{db}\delta(1-x_{2})+\Gamma^{(1)}_{db,\text{full}}\left(x_{1}\right)\delta_{ca}\delta(1-x_{1}).

(45)

The
\Gamma^{(1)}_{ca,\text{full}}\left(x_{i}\right)
contain the LO Altarelli-Parisi splitting kernels
p^{0}_{ca}(x_{i})

Altarelli:1977zs
and can be organized into several layers corresponding to different colour factors (the subscript ‘full’ precisely indicates this). In principle, equation (
44
) has both identity preserving and identity changing contributions, respectively when
(c,d)=(a,b)
and
(c,d)\neq(a,b)
. In the gluons-only case, we have
(c,d)=(a,b)=(g,g)
, so only gluon-to-gluon splitting kernels contribute:

\Gamma^{(1)}_{gg;gg}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)=\Gamma^{(1)}_{gg,\text{full}}\left(x_{1}\right)\delta(1-x_{2})+\Gamma^{(1)}_{gg,\text{full}}\left(x_{1}\right)\delta(1-x_{1}),

(46)

where we only extract the gluon component of the splitting kernels at
N_{f}=0
:

\Gamma^{(1)}_{gg,\text{full}}\left(x_{i}\right)=N_{c}\,\Gamma^{(1)}_{gg}\left(x_{i}\right)=-\dfrac{1}{\epsilon}N_{c}\,p^{0}_{gg}(x_{i}),

(47)

with
Altarelli:1977zs
:

p^{0}_{gg}(x)=b_{0}\delta(1-x)+2\left(\dfrac{1}{1-x}\right)_{+}+\dfrac{2}{x}-2x^{2}+2x-4.

(48)

It is possible to express the identity preserving mass factorization kernels in colour space, in analogy to the formalism used in the previous section to represent the singularity structure of the one-loop amplitudes. We define:

\mathbf{\Gamma}^{(1)}_{gg;gg}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)=\mathbf{\Gamma}^{(1)}_{gg,\text{full}}\left(x_{1}\right)\delta(1-x_{2})+\mathbf{\Gamma}^{(1)}_{gg,\text{full}}\left(x_{2}\right)\delta(1-x_{1}),

(49)

where

\mathbf{\Gamma}^{(1)}_{gg,\text{full}}\left(x_{i}\right)=-\Gamma^{(1)}_{gg,\text{full}}\left(x_{i}\right)\dfrac{1}{C_{A}}\sum_{j\neq i}\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j},\quad i=1,2.

(50)

This colour operator is proportional to the identity in colour space when it acts on a colour singlet vector, due to colour conservation (
17
) and (
11
), therefore

\mathbf{\Gamma}^{(1)}_{gg;gg}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)\ket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{\ell}}=\Gamma^{(1)}_{gg;gg}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)\ket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{\ell}},

(51)

which restores the original result, namely that the mass factorization counterterm factorizes onto the full corresponding LO matrix element. We can then rewrite (
44
) in the gluons-only case as

\displaystyle\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF}_{gg,NLO}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathcal{N}^{V}_{NLO}\int{\dfrac{\text{d}x_{1}}{x_{1}}}{\dfrac{\text{d}x_{2}}{x_{2}}}\int\text{d}\Phi_{n}(p_{3},\dots,p_{n+2};x_{1}p_{1},x_{2}p_{2})\,J_{n}^{(n)}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})

(52)

\displaystyle\times\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}|\mathbf{\Gamma}^{(1)}_{gg;gg}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)|\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}}\,.

3.3
NLO virtual subtraction term

The virtual subtraction term at NLO
{\hat{\sigma}^{T}_{gg,NLO}}
has to reproduce the explicit poles of the virtual matrix element as well as include the mass factorization contribution. Traditionally this was achieved summing the mass factorization counterterm with the real subtraction term after analytical integration over the phase space of an unresolved parton, as indicated by (
34
). In this approach we exploit a combination of equations (
42
) and (
52
) to directly construct
{\hat{\sigma}^{T}_{gg,NLO}}
. To do so, we define a NLO singularity dipole operator in colour space for an
(n+2)
-parton process:

\displaystyle\mathbfcal{J}^{(1)}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(i,j)\geq 3}(\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j})\,\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(1)}(i_{g},j_{g})+\sum_{i\neq 1,2}(\bm{T}_{1}\cdot\bm{T}_{i})\,\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(1)}(1_{g},i_{g})

(53)

\displaystyle\,+\sum_{i\neq 1,2}(\bm{T}_{2}\cdot\bm{T}_{i})\,\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(1)}(2_{g},i_{g})+(\bm{T}_{1}\cdot\bm{T}_{2})\,\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(1)}(1_{g},2_{g})\,.

The first sum runs over all pairs of gluons in the final state, the second and the third sums include all pairs with an initial-state gluon (respectively
1_{g}
and
2_{g}
) and a final-state one and the last term addresses the configuration where both gluons are in the initial state. The scalar functions
\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(1)}(i,j)
are colour stripped one-loop integrated dipoles
Currie:2013vh
;
Currie:2013dwa
, given by a combination of integrated three-parton tree-level antenna functions and NLO mass factorization kernels. The explicit expressions of the gluon-gluon integrated dipoles for final-final (FF), initial-final (IF) and initial-initial (II) configurations are the following:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(1)}(i_{g},j_{g})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{3}\mathcal{F}_{3}^{0}(s_{ij}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(1)}(1_{g},j_{g})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{F}_{3,g}^{0}(s_{1j})-\dfrac{1}{2}\Gamma^{(1)}_{gg}\left(x_{1}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(1)}(1_{g},2_{g})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{3,gg}^{0}(s_{12})-\dfrac{1}{2}\Gamma^{(1)}_{gg}\left(x_{1}\right)\delta_{2}-\dfrac{1}{2}\Gamma^{(1)}_{gg}\left(x_{2}\right)\delta_{1},

(54)

where
\delta_{i}=\delta(1-x_{i})
. The structure of the integrated dipoles is chosen in such a way that the singularities carried by the mass factorization kernels cancel with poles in the integrated initial-final and initial-initial antenna functions associated with initial-state collinear divergences. The remaining
\epsilon
-poles exactly match the ones of the virtual matrix element, once the operator in (
53
) is evaluated on the corresponding Born-level amplitude in colour space. In particular, at one loop the following relation holds:

\mathcal{P}oles\left[\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(1)}(i_{g},j_{g})\right]=\mathcal{P}oles\left[\text{Re}\left(\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{i_{g}j_{g}}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)\right)\right],

(55)

where the integrated dipole on the left-hand side can be in the FF, IF or II configuration. Therefore, exploiting the dipole operator defined in (
53
), it is possible to express the NLO virtual subtraction term as

\displaystyle\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T}_{gg,NLO}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{V}_{NLO}\int{\dfrac{\text{d}x_{1}}{x_{1}}}{\dfrac{\text{d}x_{2}}{x_{2}}}\text{d}\Phi_{n}(p_{3},\dots,p_{n+2};x_{1}p_{1},x_{2}p_{2})J_{n}^{(n)}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})

(56)

\displaystyle\times 2\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}|\mathbfcal{J}^{(1)}(\epsilon)|}\,.

This virtual subtraction term describes in a general way the singularity structure of an
(n+2)
-gluon
one-loop matrix element and includes the mass factorization contribution. Once this is subtracted from
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{V}_{gg,NLO}}
, an
\epsilon
-finite quantity is obtained, which can be integrated via Monte Carlo techniques. The explicit expression for the subtraction term in (
56
) is obtained computing the colour sandwiches
\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}|\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j}|\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}}
and dressing them with the associated colour stripped integrated dipoles, as indicated by the structure of (
53
). We remark that (
56
) is a completely general result in the case of gluon scattering: it is valid for any number of external legs
n
and retains the full
N_{c}
dependence.

3.4
NLO real subtraction term

In the traditional approach, the NLO real subtraction term, describing the divergent behaviour of the real emission matrix elements in the IR limits, is constructed combining unintegrated NLO antenna functions with reduced matrix elements. In the colourful antenna approach, as explained in section
3.3
, the starting point is the virtual subtraction term, while the real subtraction term is obtained in an automated way from it. The key concept is the cancellation of the IR singularities between real and virtual corrections. Apart from the mass factorization counterterm, each term in
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T}_{gg,NLO}}
must have an unintegrated counterpart in
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S}_{gg,NLO}}
. Therefore, it is possible to define a correspondence between integrated and unintegrated structures appearing at the virtual and real level respectively:

\mathcal{X}_{3}^{0}(s_{ij})\,a_{n+2}^{0}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},\{.,i,.,j,.\})\,\leftrightarrow\,X_{3}^{0}(i,k,j)a_{n+2}^{0}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},\{.,\widetilde{ik},.,\widetilde{kj},.\}),

(57)

where
\mathcal{X}_{3}^{0}(s_{ij})
is the integrated tree-level antenna function obtained integrating
X_{3}^{0}(i,k,j)
over the phase space of the unresolved parton
k
. The notation
\widetilde{ik}
and
\widetilde{kj}
indicates a suitable momentum mapping
\left\{p_{i},p_{k},p_{j}\right\}\to\{p_{\widetilde{ik}},p_{\widetilde{kj}}\}

Kosower:2002su
from the
(n+1)
-particle phase space to the
n
-particle phase space, obtained after the integration over the phase space of the unresolved gluon
k
. Due to this correspondence, once the virtual subtraction term is obtained, the structure of the real subtraction term can be completely determined by inserting an unresolved gluon between each pair of hard radiators appearing in the integrated dipoles. This involves a transition from an integrated NLO antenna to an unintegrated one and from a genuine LO colour interference to a reduced one where the
n
-particle momenta are meant to be obtained from a
(n+1)
-particle phase space through a suitable mapping. The right-hand-side of (
57
) reproduces the divergent behaviour of the real interference
a_{n+3}^{0}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime};\{.,i,.,k,.,j,.\})
when gluon
k
is unresolved between the hard pair
(i,j)
.

The procedure to obtain
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S}_{NLO,gg}}
from
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T}_{NLO,gg}}
can be summarized as follows:

•

remove the splitting kernels from the expression of the integrated dipoles
\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(1)}
in
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T}_{NLO,gg}}
;

•

replace each integrated gluon-gluon
\mathcal{F}_{3}^{0}
with its unintegrated version inserting an unresolved gluon:

\displaystyle\text{FF: }

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{3}^{0}(s_{ij})

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle 3\,f_{3}^{0}(i,k,j),

(58)

\displaystyle\text{IF: }

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{3,g}^{0}(s_{1i})

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle 2\,f_{3,g}^{0}(1,k,i),

\displaystyle\text{II: }

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{3,gg}^{0}(s_{12})

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle F_{3,gg}^{0}(1,k,2),

where the numerical coefficients are symmetry factors related to the antenna phase space;

•

suitably replace the momenta in the colour interferences, according to the accompanying integrated antenna:

a_{n+2}^{0}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},\{.,i,.,j,.\})\to a_{n+2}^{0}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},\{.,\widetilde{ik},.,\widetilde{kj},.\});

(59)

•

apply the same momenta relabelling to the jet function:

J_{n}^{(m)}(\dots,p_{i},\dots,p_{j},\dots)\to J_{n}^{(m)}(\dots,p_{\widetilde{ik}},\dots,p_{\widetilde{kj}},\dots);

(60)

•

the obtained expression is now a function of
n+3
momenta, so the phase space has to be adjusted accordingly:

\text{d}\Phi_{n}(p_{3},\dots,p_{n+2};x_{1}p_{1},x_{2}p_{2})\to\text{d}\Phi_{n+1}(p_{3},\dots,p_{n+3};x_{1}p_{1},x_{2}p_{2})

(61)

and a sum over suitable permutations of these momenta is needed to have the full real subtraction term;

•

the overall factor used at the virtual level needs to be replaced by the appropriate one for the real correction:

\mathcal{N}^{V}_{NLO}\to\mathcal{N}^{R}_{NLO}=s_{R}\left(4\pi\alpha_{s}\right)\mathcal{N}_{LO},

(62)

where
s_{R}
compensates the different final state symmetry factor for the real radiation and for a process with
n
final-state gluons at the Born level we have:

s_{R}=\dfrac{n!}{(n+1)!}=\dfrac{1}{n+1}.

(63)

With this procedure for the derivation of the real subtraction term, the cancellation of IR singularities between the real and virtual correction is trivially guaranteed. What is left to check is that the constructed
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S}_{NLO,gg}}
correctly reproduces the divergent behaviour of the real correction matrix element in each unresolved limit once and only once. If one inserts an unresolved gluon between each pair in the
(n+2)
-gluon set and correctly sums over the relevant permutations of the resulting set of
n+3
partons (with
n+1
in the final state), it is clear that all possible unresolved limits at the real level are taken into account. The double-counting of any unresolved configuration is avoided since the unintegrated antenna functions in (
58
) are explicitly constructed to only reproduce the divergence arising when gluon
k
is unresolved between the two hard radiators
i
and
j
. In other words, each unintegrated
f_{3}^{0}
addresses one and only one configuration of unresolved gluon and hard radiators pair.

We introduce the following notation for the procedure that we have just illustrated to convert integrated quantities into their unintegrated counterparts:

\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S}_{gg,NLO}}=-\mathcal{I}ns\left[\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T}_{gg,NLO}}\right].

(64)

The action of the
\mathcal{I}ns\left[\cdot\right]
operator on its argument is comprehensive of the steps discussed above and can be systematically formulated as follows:

1.

Removal of the splitting kernels from the integrated dipoles;

2.

Transition from integrated three-parton antenna functions to unintegrated ones, as indicated by (
58
);

3.

Momenta relabeling within colour interferences and jet functions according to the accompanying antenna function;

4.

Sum over permutations of the
n+3
momenta to cover all possible IR limits;

5.

Dressing of the obtained expression with the appropriate phase space and overall coefficient factor.

The minus sign in (
64
) is explained in (
34
) and is needed to make the virtual subtraction term cancel with the integrated real subtract term once the full result is computed. This set of operations is sufficient at NLO, while it needs to be extended to be applied at NNLO. Nevertheless, as we show in section
4
, the majority of the NNLO subtraction terms can be generated through the application of
\mathcal{I}ns\left[\cdot\right]
to integrated quantities, namely through the very same insertion used at NLO. The only exception is represented by configurations that require a simultaneous double insertion of unresolved gluons.

A clear parallelism can be identified between the colourful antenna approach at NLO and the Catani-Seymour dipole formalism
Catani:1996jh
. In both schemes, the real subtraction term is obtained as a combination of dipole contributions exploiting the general factorization properties of QCD in soft and collinear limits. However, in the Catani-Seymour dipole formalism, as well as in other subtraction schemes both at NLO and NNLO, the principal effort precisely lies in the construction of the subtraction term for the real emission. The integrated version of such subtraction term eventually cancels the
\epsilon
-poles in virtual correction. On the opposite, the peculiarity of the described colourful antenna method consists in prioritizing the construction of the virtual subtraction term, from which the real subtraction term is then systematically inferred. As in the traditional subtraction schemes the key requirement is the analytical integrability of the real subtraction term over the phase space of the unresolved radiation, here it is crucial that the virtual subtraction term is expressed in a language suitable for the
unintegration
procedure. The correspondence between integrated antenna functions, collected in (
53
), and their unintegrated versions guarantees this property.

On a practical standpoint, both the computation of colour operators insertions like
\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}|\mathbfcal{J}^{(1)}(\epsilon)|}
and the application of
\mathcal{I}ns\left[\cdot\right]
can be performed with any symbolic algebra program such as FORM,
Mathematica
or
Maple
. Once the symbolic expressions for the subtraction terms are generated, they can be implemented in a numerical code which performs the Monte Carlo integration.

4
Colourful antenna subtraction at NNLO

The NNLO QCD correction to an
n
-jet cross section is given by:

\displaystyle\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}_{ab,NNLO}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{n}\left(\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{VV}_{ab,NNLO}}+\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF,2}_{ab,NNLO}}\right)

(65)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{n+1}\left(\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{RV}_{ab,NNLO}}+\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF,1}_{ab,NNLO}}\right)+\int_{n+2}\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{RR}_{ab,NNLO}},

where
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{VV}_{ab,NNLO}}
represents the double virtual correction,
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{RV}_{ab,NNLO}}
the real virtual correction and
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{RR}_{ab,NNLO}}
the double real correction. The mass factorization counterterm is split into two terms associated with
n
- and
(n+1)
-particle final states, respectively
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF,2}_{ab,NNLO}}
and
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF,1}_{ab,NNLO}}
.

As for the NLO case, the quantity in (
65
) cannot be directly computed with numerical methods. The singular behaviour of both the double real and real virtual corrections in the IR limits must be subtracted and the explicit poles in the double virtual and real virtual matrix elements need to be properly removed. To achieve this, the NNLO cross section is rewritten in the context of antenna subtraction as
Currie:2013vh
:

\displaystyle\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}_{ab,NNLO}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{n}\left[\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{VV}_{ab,NNLO}}-\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U}_{ab,NNLO}}\right]

(66)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{n+1}\left[\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{RV}_{ab,NNLO}}-\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T}_{ab,NNLO}}\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{n+2}\left[\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{RR}_{ab,NNLO}}-\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S}_{ab,NNLO}}\right],

where the subtracted quantities are the double virtual, the real virtual and the double real subtraction term. These contributions have the following form:

\displaystyle\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S}_{ab,NNLO}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S,1}_{ab,NNLO}}+\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S,2}_{ab,NNLO}}\,,

\displaystyle\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T}_{ab,NNLO}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{VS}_{ab,NNLO}}-\int_{1}\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S,1}_{ab,NNLO}}-\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF,1}_{ab,NNLO}}\,,

\displaystyle\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U}_{ab,NNLO}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{1}\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{VS}_{ab,NNLO}}-\int_{2}\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S,2}_{ab,NNLO}}-\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF,2}_{ab,NNLO}}\,.

(67)

The double real subtraction term has been decomposed into two contributions which contain single and double unresolved IR limits. In the real virtual subtraction term,
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{VS}_{ab,NNLO}}
cancels the implicit singular behaviour of the real virtual correction in the soft and collinear limits, while the remaining contributions remove the explicit
\epsilon
-poles.

Analogously to NLO, in the traditional antenna subtraction approach the IR limits
Campbell:1997hg
;
Catani:1999ss
;
Bern:1999ry
;
Catani:2000vq

of the double real and real virtual matrix elements are studied first to construct
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S}_{ab,NNLO}}
and
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{VS}_{ab,NNLO}}
. These terms are then integrated and combined with the mass factorization contributions to obtain the full infrastructure in (
4
).

The application of the colourful antenna subtraction method at NNLO begins by addressing the double virtual correction in colour space. The singularity structure of two-loop amplitudes can be predicted in a general way and therefore the construction of the double virtual subtraction term can be achieved without the need of studying the real emission corrections first. The predictability of the poles of two-loop amplitudes in colour space is neither limited by the number of partons in the process nor conceptually more involved beyond leading colour. For this reason, if one aims at a general application of antenna subtraction at NNLO, starting from the construction of
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U}_{ab,NNLO}}
in colour space is significantly more convenient. Once the double virtual subtraction term is constructed, the real virtual and double real subtraction terms are generated by the insertion of unresolved partons, exploiting the relations between integrated and unintegrated blocks in the subtraction terms in (
4
) and removing any spurious singularity.

4.1
IR singularity structure at two loops

The singularity structure of renormalized two-loop amplitudes in QCD is known
Catani:1998bh
and can be described in colour space by:

\ket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{2}}=\bm{I}^{(1)}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)\ket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{1}}+\bm{I}^{(2)}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)\ket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}}+\ket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{2,\text{fin}}(\mu_{r}^{2})},

(68)

where, as before,
\ket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{2,\text{fin}}(\mu_{r}^{2})}
is a finite remainder. The two-loop Catani IR insertion operator has the following expression
Catani:1998bh
:

\displaystyle\bm{I}^{(2)}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{\beta_{0}}{\epsilon}\bm{I}^{(1)}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2};\left\{p\right\}_{n}\right)-\dfrac{1}{2}\bm{I}^{(1)}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)\bm{I}^{(1)}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)

(69)

\displaystyle+e^{-\epsilon\gamma_{E}}\dfrac{\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\left(\dfrac{\beta_{0}}{\epsilon}+K\right)\bm{I}^{(1)}\left(2\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\bm{H}^{(2)}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)\,.

In the gluons-only case with
N_{f}=0
we have:

\displaystyle\beta_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{0}N_{c}=\dfrac{11}{6}N_{c},

(70)

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{0}N_{c}=\left(\dfrac{67}{18}-\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{6}\right)N_{c}.

(71)

The colour structure of (
69
) is more involved than the colour charge dipole structure of (
36
). As anticipated, products of two colour charge dipoles appear. The last line of (
69
) contains the hard radiation function
\bm{H}^{(2)}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2};\left\{p\right\}_{n}\right)

Catani:1998bh
;
Bern:2003ck
;
Becher:2009qa
, which can be decomposed in the following manner:

\bm{H}^{(2)}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)=\sum_{i}C_{i}\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{i}\left(\epsilon\right)\mathbf{Id}+\check{\bm{H}}^{(2)}(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}),

(72)

where the sum runs over the
n+2
external partons and
C_{i}
are the Casimir coefficients. The first term in (
72
) is proportional to the identity in colour space, while the second term has a non-trivial colour structure, which can not be in general expressed in terms of colour charge dipoles. However, the second term vanishes when sandwiched between tree-level states
Bern:2003ck
;
Becher:2009qa
:

\bra{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}}\check{\bm{H}}^{(2)}(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2})\ket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}}=0.

(73)

For the purpose of describing the IR singularity structure of the two-loop squared matrix elements, the hard radiation function
\bm{H}^{(2)}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)
needs to be evaluated on tree-level states and therefore it is possible to neglect
\check{\bm{H}}^{(2)}(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2})
in its decomposition. As we did for the splitting kernels in section
3.2
, we can express a colour operator proportional to the identity as a sum of colour charge dipoles using colour conservation. We can then rewrite:

\displaystyle\sum_{i}C_{i}\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{i}\left(\epsilon\right)\mathbf{Id}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i}\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{i}\left(\epsilon\right)\sum_{j\neq i}\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j}

(74)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{(i,j)}\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{ij}\left(\epsilon\right)\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j},

where, as usual, the sum runs over pairs of partons and
\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{ij}=\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{i}+\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{j}
. In the gluon-only case we only need
\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{g}\left(\epsilon\right)
for
N_{f}=0
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{g}\left(\epsilon\right)=\dfrac{e^{\epsilon\gamma_{E}}}{4\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\dfrac{N_{c}}{\epsilon}\left[\dfrac{5}{12}+\dfrac{11}{144}\pi^{2}+\dfrac{\zeta_{3}}{2}\right]\,.

(75)

Using (
37
) and (
74
) and neglecting
\check{\bm{H}}^{(2)}(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2})
we can rearrange equation (
69
) as:

\displaystyle\bm{I}^{(2)}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\dfrac{\beta_{0}}{\epsilon}\sum_{(i,j)}\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{ij}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j}

(76)

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{(i,j)}\sum_{(k,l)}\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{ij}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{kl}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)(\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j})(\bm{T}_{k}\cdot\bm{T}_{l})

\displaystyle+\sum_{(i,j)}\mathcal{I}^{(2)}_{ij}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j},

where

\mathcal{I}^{(2)}_{ij}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)=e^{-\epsilon\gamma_{E}}\dfrac{\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\left(\dfrac{\beta_{0}}{\epsilon}+K\right)\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{ij}\left(2\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)-\mathcal{H}^{(2)}_{ij}\left(\epsilon\right)\,.

(77)

We can now use (
35
), (
68
) and (
69
) to express the singularity structure of a two-loop matrix element:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}oles\left(\mathcal{M}_{n}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}oles\left(\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{2}|\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}}+\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}|\mathcal{A}_{n}^{2}}+\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{1}|\mathcal{A}_{n}^{1}}\right)

(78)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}oles\Big\{\Big.\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{1}|\bm{I}^{(1)}\left(\epsilon\right)+\bm{I}^{(1),\dagger}\left(\epsilon\right)|\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}}+\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}|\bm{I}^{(1)}\left(\epsilon\right)+\bm{I}^{(1),\dagger}\left(\epsilon\right)|\mathcal{A}_{n}^{1}}

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{2}\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}|\left(\bm{I}^{(1)}\left(\epsilon\right)+\bm{I}^{(1),\dagger}\left(\epsilon\right)\right)\left(\bm{I}^{(1)}\left(\epsilon\right)+\bm{I}^{(1),\dagger}\left(\epsilon\right)\right)|\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}}

\displaystyle-\dfrac{\beta_{0}}{\epsilon}\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}|\bm{I}^{(1)}\left(\epsilon\right)+\bm{I}^{(1),\dagger}\left(\epsilon\right)|\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}}

\displaystyle+\text{e}^{-\epsilon\gamma_{E}}\dfrac{\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\left(\dfrac{\beta_{0}}{\epsilon}+K\right)\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}|\bm{I}^{(1)}\left(2\epsilon\right)+\bm{I}^{(1),\dagger}\left(2\epsilon\right)|\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}}

\displaystyle+\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}|\bm{H}^{(2)}\left(\epsilon\right)+\bm{H}^{(2),\dagger}\left(\epsilon\right)|\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}}\Big.\Big\}\,.

We see again that only the real part of the insertion operators is needed to describe the singularity structure. Using (
37
) and (
76
) it is possible to recast equation (
78
) as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}oles\left(\mathcal{M}_{n}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}oles\Big\{\Big.\sum_{(i,j)}2\text{Re}\left[\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{ij}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)\right]\left[\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{1}|\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j}|\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}}+\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}|\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j}|\mathcal{A}_{n}^{1}}\right]

(79)

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{(i,j)}\sum_{(k,l)}2\text{Re}\left[\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{ij}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)\right]2\text{Re}\left[\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{lk}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)\right]\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}|(\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j})(\bm{T}_{k}\cdot\bm{T}_{l})|\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}}

\displaystyle-\dfrac{\beta_{0}}{\epsilon}\sum_{(i,j)}2\text{Re}\left[\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{ij}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)\right]\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}|\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j}|\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{(i,j)}2\text{Re}\left[\mathcal{I}^{(2)}_{ij}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)\right]\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}|\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j}|\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}}\Big.\Big\}.

The poles of the double virtual cross section for gluons-only processes are therefore given by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}oles\left({\hat{\sigma}^{VV}_{gg,NNLO}}\right)=\mathcal{N}^{VV}_{NNLO}\int\text{d}\Phi_{n}(p_{3},\dots,p_{n+2};p_{1},p_{2})\,J_{n}^{(n)}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})

(80)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}oles\Big\{\Big.\sum_{(i_{g},j_{g})}2\text{Re}\left[\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{i_{g}j_{g}}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)\right]\left[\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{1}|\bm{T}_{i_{g}}\cdot\bm{T}_{j_{g}}|\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}}+\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}|\bm{T}_{i_{g}}\cdot\bm{T}_{j_{g}}|\mathcal{A}_{n}^{1}}\right]

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{(i_{g},j_{g})}\sum_{(k_{g},l_{g})}2\text{Re}\left[\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{i_{g}j_{g}}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)\right]2\text{Re}\left[\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{l_{g}k_{g}}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)\right]\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}|(\bm{T}_{i_{g}}\cdot\bm{T}_{j_{g}})(\bm{T}_{k_{g}}\cdot\bm{T}_{l_{g}})|\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}}

\displaystyle-\dfrac{\beta_{0}}{\epsilon}\sum_{(i,j)}2\text{Re}\left[\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{i_{g}j_{g}}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)\right]\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}|\bm{T}_{i_{g}}\cdot\bm{T}_{j_{g}}|\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{(i,j)}2\text{Re}\left[\mathcal{I}^{(2)}_{i_{g}j_{g}}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)\right]\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}|\bm{T}_{i_{g}}\cdot\bm{T}_{j_{g}}|\mathcal{A}_{n}^{0}}\Big.\Big\},

where

\mathcal{N}^{VV}_{NNLO}=\left(\dfrac{\alpha_{s}\overline{C}(\epsilon)}{2\pi}\right)^{2}\mathcal{N}_{LO}\,.

(81)

4.2
NNLO mass factorization

At NNLO, as indicated in (
66
) and (
4
), we have two different contributions to the mass factorization counterterm: the double virtual and the real virtual mass factorization terms. The decomposition is meant to separate contributions that are defined on the
n
-particle phase space from terms that are defined on the
(n+1)
-particle phase space.

4.2.1
Double virtual mass factorization term

We first address the double virtual mass factorization term at NNLO. It can be written as
Currie:2013vh
:

\displaystyle\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF,2}_{ab,NNLO}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int{\dfrac{\text{d}x_{1}}{x_{1}}}{\dfrac{\text{d}x_{2}}{x_{2}}}\sum_{c,d}\Bigg\{\Bigg.\left(\dfrac{\alpha_{s}\overline{C}(\epsilon)}{2\pi}\right)\left[\Gamma^{(1)}_{ab;cd}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)\left(\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{V}_{cd,NLO}}-\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T}_{cd,NLO}}\right)\right]

(82)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\left(\dfrac{\alpha_{s}\overline{C}(\epsilon)}{2\pi}\right)^{2}\Big[\Big.\overline{\Gamma}^{(2)}_{ab;cd}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)-\dfrac{\beta_{0}}{\epsilon}\Gamma^{(1)}_{ab;cd}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\left[\Gamma^{(1)}_{ab;\alpha\beta}\otimes\Gamma^{(1)}_{\alpha\beta;cd}\right](x_{1},x_{2})\Big.\Big]\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}_{cd,LO}}\Bigg.\Bigg\}.

The reduced two-loop mass factorization kernel is defined as
Currie:2013vh
:

\overline{\Gamma}^{(2)}_{ab;cd}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)=\overline{\Gamma}^{(2)}_{ca,\text{full}}\left(x_{1}\right)\delta_{db}\delta(1-x_{2})+\overline{\Gamma}^{(2)}_{db,\text{full}}\left(x_{2}\right)\delta_{ca}\delta(1-x_{1}),

(83)

where the
\overline{\Gamma}^{(2)}_{ca,\text{full}}\left(x_{1}\right)
can be written in terms of LO and NLO Altarelli-Parisi spitting kernels
Altarelli:1977zs
;
Curci:1980uw
;
Furmanski:1980cm
and have their own colour decomposition. In the gluons-only case we simply need:

\overline{\Gamma}^{(2)}_{gg;gg}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)=\overline{\Gamma}^{(2)}_{gg,\text{full}}\left(x_{1}\right)\delta(1-x_{2})+\overline{\Gamma}^{(2)}_{gg,\text{full}}\left(x_{2}\right)\delta(1-x_{1}),

(84)

where we discard any contribution in the two-loop splitting kernel except for the gluon-to-gluon one for
N_{f}=0
. Thus we have:

\overline{\Gamma}^{(2)}_{gg,\text{full}}\left(x_{i}\right)=N_{c}^{2}\,\overline{\Gamma}^{(2)}_{gg}\left(x_{i}\right)=-\dfrac{1}{2\epsilon}\left(N_{c}^{2}\,p^{1}_{gg}(x_{i})+\dfrac{\beta_{0}\,N_{c}}{\epsilon}p^{0}_{gg}(x_{i})\right),

(85)

where
p^{1}_{gg}(x_{i})
is the Altarelli-Parisi NLO splitting kernel given by
Furmanski:1980cm
:

\displaystyle p^{1}_{gg}(x)=

\displaystyle\left[\dfrac{67}{9}-4\ln(x)\ln(1-x)+\ln^{2}(x)-\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{3}\right]\left[\left(\dfrac{1}{1-x}\right)_{+}+\dfrac{1}{x}-2+x(1-x)\right]

(86)

\displaystyle+2S_{2}(x)\left[\dfrac{1}{1+x}-\dfrac{1}{x}-2-x(1+x)\right]

\displaystyle+\dfrac{27}{2}(1-x)+\dfrac{67}{9}\left(x^{2}-\dfrac{1}{x}\right)-\left(\dfrac{25}{3}-\dfrac{11}{3}x+\dfrac{44}{3}x^{2}\right)\ln(x)

\displaystyle+4(1+x)\ln^{2}(x)+\left(\dfrac{8}{3}+3\zeta_{3}\right)\delta(1-x),

with

S_{2}(x)=-2\text{Li}_{2}(-x)+\dfrac{1}{2}\ln^{2}(x)-2\ln(x)\ln(1+x)-\dfrac{\pi^{2}}{6}.

(87)

As we did in section
3.2
, it is possible to express the two-loop identity-preserving mass factorization kernel in colour space as:

\overline{\mathbf{\Gamma}}^{(2)}_{gg;gg}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)=\overline{\mathbf{\Gamma}}^{(2)}_{gg,\text{full}}\left(x_{1}\right)\delta(1-x_{2})+\overline{\mathbf{\Gamma}}^{(2)}_{gg,\text{full}}\left(x_{2}\right)\delta(1-x_{1}),

(88)

where

\overline{\mathbf{\Gamma}}^{(2)}_{gg,\text{full}}\left(x_{i}\right)=-\overline{\Gamma}^{(2)}_{gg,\text{full}}\left(x_{i}\right)\dfrac{1}{C_{A}}\sum_{j\neq i}\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j},\quad i=1,2.

(89)

We can then write the expression for the required double virtual mass factorization counterterm as:

\displaystyle\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF,2}_{gg,NNLO}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathcal{N}^{VV}_{NNLO}\int{\dfrac{\text{d}x_{1}}{x_{1}}}{\dfrac{\text{d}x_{2}}{x_{2}}}\text{d}\Phi_{n}(p_{3},\dots,p_{n+2};x_{1}p_{1},x_{2}p_{2})J_{n}^{(n)}(\left\{p\right\}_{n})

(90)

\displaystyle\Big\{\Big.\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}|\mathbf{\Gamma}^{(1)}_{gg;gg}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)|\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{1}}+\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{1}|\mathbf{\Gamma}^{(1)}_{gg;gg}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)|\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}}

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69054pt-2\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}|\left[\mathbf{\Gamma}^{(1)}_{gg;gg}\otimes\mathbfcal{J}^{(1)}(\epsilon)\right](x_{1},x_{2})|\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}}

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69054pt+\dfrac{1}{2}\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}|\left[\mathbf{\Gamma}^{(1)}_{gg;gg}\otimes\mathbf{\Gamma}^{(1)}_{gg;gg}\right](x_{1},x_{2})|\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}}

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69054pt-\dfrac{\beta_{0}}{\epsilon}\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}|\mathbf{\Gamma}^{(1)}_{gg;gg}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)|\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}}

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69054pt+\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}|\overline{\mathbf{\Gamma}}^{(2)}_{gg;gg}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)|\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}}\Big.\Big\},

where we used the expression of
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T}_{cd,NLO}}
given in (

Real virtual mass factorization term

\displaystyle\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF,1,a}_{ab,NNLO}}

\displaystyle-\left(\dfrac{\alpha_{s}\overline{C}(\epsilon)}{2\pi}\right)\sum_{c,d}\int{\dfrac{\text{d}x_{1}}{x_{1}}}{\dfrac{\text{d}x_{2}}{x_{2}}}\Gamma^{(1)}_{ab;cd}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{R}_{cd,NLO}},

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{\alpha_{s}\overline{C}(\epsilon)}{2\pi}\right)\sum_{c,d}\int{\dfrac{\text{d}x_{1}}{x_{1}}}{\dfrac{\text{d}x_{2}}{x_{2}}}\Gamma^{(1)}_{ab;cd}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S}_{cd,NLO}}.

),
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF,1,a}_{ab,NNLO}}
can be rewritten as:

\displaystyle\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF,1,a}_{gg,NNLO}}

\displaystyle-\mathcal{N}^{RV}_{NNLO}\int{\dfrac{\text{d}x_{1}}{x_{1}}}{\dfrac{\text{d}x_{2}}{x_{2}}}\int\text{d}\Phi_{n}(p_{3},\dots,p_{n+2};x_{1}p_{1},x_{2}p_{2})\,J_{n}^{(n+1)}(\left\{p\right\}_{n+1})

-gluon Born process and

\mathcal{N}^{RV}_{NNLO}=s_{RV}(4\pi\alpha_{s})\left(\dfrac{\alpha_{s}\overline{C}(\epsilon)}{2\pi}\right)\mathcal{N}_{LO},\quad\text{with}\quad s_{RV}=s_{R}.

\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF,1,b}_{gg,NNLO}}=\left(\dfrac{\alpha_{s}\overline{C}(\epsilon)}{2\pi}\right)\int{\dfrac{\text{d}x_{1}}{x_{1}}}{\dfrac{\text{d}x_{2}}{x_{2}}}\Gamma^{(1)}_{gg;gg}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S}_{gg,NLO}}.

4.3
NNLO double virtual subtraction term

The double virtual subtraction term at NNLO
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U}_{gg,NNLO}}
, reproduces the explicit poles of the two-loop matrix element and contains the double virtual mass factorization counterterm. In the following we see how to construct
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U}_{gg,NNLO}}
in a general way relying on the results of the previous sections, in particular equations (
80
) and (
90
). In analogy with (
53
), we define a two-loop insertion operator in colour space:

\displaystyle\mathbfcal{J}^{(2)}(\epsilon)

4.4
NNLO real virtual subtraction term

At NLO, once the virtual subtraction term is obtained, it is straightforward to systematically construct the real subtraction term. At NNLO, the structure of the subtraction is significantly more involved, due to the presence of two additional layers besides the double virtual correction: real virtual and double real. The real virtual subtraction term
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T}_{gg,NNLO}}
must cancel the explicit
\epsilon
-poles in the real virtual matrix element, as well as remove the divergent behaviour in single unresolved IR limits. The double real subtraction term
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S}_{gg,NNLO}}
is needed to remove the single and double unresolved divergences in the double real correction. Due to the complexity of the NNLO IR structure, the systematic generation of the real virtual and double real subtraction terms from the double virtual one is non-trivial.

In the following, we illustrate how the real virtual subtraction term can be generated for the gluons-only case. Once again, the correspondence between unintegrated real emission subtraction terms and integrated virtual subtraction term is the key. The deduction of
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T}_{gg,NNLO}}
from
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U}_{gg,NNLO}}
is articulated in two main steps:

•

integrated terms are translated from
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U}_{gg,NNLO}}
to
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T}_{gg,NNLO}}
inserting an unresolved gluon between a pair of hard radiators, via the application of
\mathcal{I}ns\left[\cdot\right]
, in complete analogy to what is done at NLO;

•

suitable terms are generated to remove spurious
\epsilon
-poles.

Any additional contribution which is added at the real virtual level and does not have a direct correspondence to terms in
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U}_{gg,NNLO}}
will eventually generate corresponding terms at the double real level after the insertion of a second unresolved gluon. We note that not the entirety of
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U}_{gg,NNLO}}
will be translated to
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T}_{gg,NNLO}}
, some terms have to undergo a double insertion of unresolved gluons and directly move from
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U}_{gg,NNLO}}
to
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S}_{gg,NNLO}}
, as explained in section
4.5
.

We recall the usual decomposition of
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T}_{gg,NNLO}}
in the context of antenna subtraction
Currie:2013vh
:

\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T}_{gg,NNLO}}=\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,a}}+\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b}}+\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,c}},

(107)

where we dropped the subscript ‘
gg,NNLO
’ in the right-hand-side. The meaning of this decomposition is the following:

•

\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,a}}
reproduces the explicit poles of the real virtual matrix element;

•

\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b}}
describes the divergent behaviour of the real virtual matrix element in single unresolved limits;

•

\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,c}}
removes the overlap in the
single unresolved behaviour between the two terms above
Currie:2013vh
.

4.4.1
d
\sigma^{T,a}

This part of the real virtual subtraction term is needed to remove the explicit poles of the
(n+3)
-particle one-loop matrix element. Moreover, it contains the mass factorization contribution
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF,1,a}_{gg,NNLO}}
given in (
95
). The construction of
\sigma^{T,a}
is completely analogous to the one adopted for the NLO virtual subtraction term in section
3.3
, with the difference that here we have an additional particle. It is clear that this term is not generated by any integrated contribution at the double virtual level and so, as we show in section
4.5.1
below, its unintegrated counterpart will be added to the the double real subtraction term. For the gluons-only case we have:

\displaystyle\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,a}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{RV}_{NNLO}\int{\dfrac{\text{d}x_{1}}{x_{1}}}{\dfrac{\text{d}x_{2}}{x_{2}}}\text{d}\Phi_{(}p_{3},\dots,p_{n+2};x_{1}p_{1},x_{2}p_{2})J_{n+1}^{(n)}(\left\{p\right\}_{n+1})

(108)

\displaystyle\times 2\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n+3}^{0}|\mathbfcal{J}^{(1)}(\epsilon)|}.

4.4.2
d
\sigma^{T,b}

This block of the subtraction term reproduces the divergent behaviour of the real virtual matrix element in single unresolved IR limits. The subtraction of infrared divergences from a one loop matrix element is more involved than the one required at tree-level. In particular, along with tree-level antennae and reduced one-loop matrix elements (tree
\times
loop), suitable combinations of three-particle one-loop antenna functions and tree-level reduced matrix elements (loop
\times
tree) have to be used. Both these structures are present, in their integrated form, in the double virtual subtraction term, since they describe the emission of soft and collinear gluons from the one-loop amplitude. It is possible to systematically generate these terms at the real virtual level starting from
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U}_{gg,NNLO}}
, as we show in the following. According to
Currie:2013vh
, we introduce a suitable decomposition of
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b}}
:

\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b}}=\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{1}}}+\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{2}}}+\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{3}}},

(109)

where the elements on the right-hand-side respectively contain (tree
\times
loop) contributions, (loop
\times
tree) contributions and suitable terms needed to ensure the correct renormalization of loop quantities in the real virtual subtraction term.

We first focus on
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{1}}}
. The respective integrated counterparts appear in the double virtual subtraction term as a combination of a one-loop integrated dipole and a one-loop reduced matrix element. In particular, these contributions are given by
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U,a_{1}}}
. The procedure of inserting an unresolved gluon is the same as the one depicted in section
3.4
, with the tree-level amplitudes replaced by the one-loop ones. The result has the following form:

\mathcal{I}ns\left[\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U,a_{1}}}\right]\sim\sum_{ijk}\,f_{3}^{0}(i,k,j)\,a_{n+2,c}^{1}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime};\{.,\widetilde{ik},.,\widetilde{kj},.\}).

(110)

The resulting contribution partially takes care of the divergent IR limits of the real virtual matrix element. However, the one-loop interferences
a_{n+2,c}^{1}
contain explicit
\epsilon
-poles which must be removed to ensure the finiteness of the real virtual subtraction term. In fact, it is possible to systematically generate suitable terms that cancel these poles, exploiting once again the predictability of the singularity structure of one-loop amplitudes. The following relation holds:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}oles\left\{2\sum_{(i,j)}\left(\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}|\left(\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j}\right)|\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{1}}+\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{1}|\left(\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j}\right)|\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}}\right)\right\}

(111)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}oles\left\{4\sum_{(i,j)}\text{Re}\left(\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}|\left(\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j}\right)|\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{1}}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}oles\left\{4\sum_{(i,j)}\text{Re}\left(\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}|\left(\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j}\right)\left(\sum_{(k,l)}\left(\bm{T}_{k}\cdot\bm{T}_{l}\right)\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{kl}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)\right)|\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\,\mathcal{P}oles\left\{-2\sum_{(i,j)}\sum_{(k,l)}\text{Re}\left[\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{kl}\left(\epsilon,\mu_{r}^{2}\right)\right]\braket{\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}|\left(\bm{T}_{i}\cdot\bm{T}_{j}\right)\left(\bm{T}_{k}\cdot\bm{T}_{l}\right)|\mathcal{A}_{n+2}^{0}}\right\},

which is analogous to state that the
\epsilon^{-4}
and
\epsilon^{-3}
poles in the first and second lines of (
79
) are related by the same factor
-2
we see in the last line here. Equation (
111

\mathcal{I}ns\left[\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U,a_{1}}}\right]
can be obtained in a general way by applying the unresolved parton insertion operator in
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U,b}}
. In particular we have:

\mathcal{P}oles\left\{\mathcal{I}ns\left[\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U,a_{1}}}\right]\right\}=\mathcal{P}oles\left\{-2\,\mathcal{I}ns\left[\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U,b}}\right]-\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF,1,b}}\right\}.

(112)

Equation (
112
) requires some comments. First of all, the
\mathcal{I}ns\left[\cdot\right]
operator removes the splitting kernels from both the
\mathbfcal{J}^{(1)}
present in
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U,b}}
and
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF,1,b}}
, given by (
97
), exactly reconstructs the one-loop integrated dipole which is not affected by the insertion of the unresolved parton. Secondly, step
3
of the list in section
3.4
needs to be extended: the momentum relabelling here affects not only the colour interferences and the jet function, but also the integrated dipole which is not converted into unintegrated antenna functions. This applies in general at NNLO, namely the transition to higher multiplicities via momenta relabelling occurs within any function of the external momenta accompanying the integrated dipole which is converted into unintegrated antennae. The choice of which integrated dipole to convert into an unintegrated antenna function should be done in such a way any pair of hard radiators is addressed once and only once. In practise this can be easily achieved performing the decomposition:

\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(1)}(i,j)\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(1)}(l,k)=\dfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(1)}(i,j)\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(1)}(l,k)+\dfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(1)}(k,l)\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(1)}(i,j)

(113)

and then fixing the first dipole in each term of the symmetrized expression on the right-hand-side to be the one converted into unintegrated antennae. Therefore, the structure of the right-hand-side of (
112

-2\,\mathcal{I}ns\left[\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U,b}}\right]-\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF,1,b}}\sim-2\sum_{ijk,lm}f_{3}^{0}(i,k,j)\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(1)}(l,m)a_{n+2}^{0}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime};\{.,\widetilde{ik},.,\widetilde{kj},.\}),

(114)

where
l
and
m
can represent either
\widetilde{ik}
and
\widetilde{kj}
or any other parton not belonging to the
(i,j)
dipole. We can thus write down the first contribution to the real virtual subtraction term as:

\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{1}}}=-\mathcal{I}ns\left[\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U,a_{1}}}\right]-2\,\mathcal{I}ns\left[\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U,b}}\right]-\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF,1,b}_{gg,NNLO}},

(115)

which is free of
\epsilon
-poles. We observe that, to compensate
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U,b}}
in the double virtual subtraction term, we should add back a factor
+\mathcal{I}ns\left[\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U,b}}\right]
. This contribution is indeed added later in the generation of the real virtual and double real subtraction terms.

We consider now
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{2}}}
, namely (loop
\times
tree) contributions. The core part of this term is given by unintegrated three-parton one-loop antenna functions
X_{3}^{1}
combined with tree-level reduced matrix elements. The integrated counterpart of these terms is contained in
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U,c,\mathcal{X}_{3}^{1}}}

\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U,c,\mathcal{X}_{3}^{1}}}

\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{1}}}

\mathcal{I}ns\left[\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U,c,\mathcal{X}_{3}^{1}}}\right]

\epsilon

This can be done systematically since the singularity structure of the unintegrated three-parton one-loop antenna functions is known and can be expressed by means of one-loop integrated dipoles and three-parton tree-level antennae
Gehrmann-DeRidder:2005btv
;
Currie:2013vh

f_{3}^{1}(i,k,j)\to f_{3}^{1}(i,k,j)+\sum_{(l,m)=1}^{3}\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(1)}(l,m)f_{3}^{0}(i,k,j)-2\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(1)}(\widetilde{ik},\widetilde{kj})f_{3}^{0}(i,k,j),

(116)

where the sum in the second term runs over the
3
pairs of colour-connected gluons in the
f_{3}^{1}(i,k,j)
antenna. The expression obtained after the replacement in (
116

Currie:2013vh
, we label
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{2},X_{3}^{1}}}
,
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{2},J_{X}}}
and
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{2},M_{X}}}
the three blocks coming from the three components in (
116

\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{2},X_{3}^{1}}}=-\mathcal{I}ns\left[\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U,c,\mathcal{X}_{3}^{1}}}\right]\sim\sum_{ijk}f_{3}^{1}(i,k,j)a_{n+2}^{0}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime};\{.,\widetilde{ik},.,\widetilde{kj},.\}).

(117)

The two remaining blocks are treated differently:
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{2},M_{X}}}
comes from
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U,c,\mathcal{X}_{3}^{0}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{3}^{0}}}
after the insertion of an unresolved gluon, while
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{2},J_{X}}}
is a genuinely new contribution added at the real virtual level, which needs to be compensated by its unintegrated counterpart at the double real level. This can be noticed looking at the arguments of the integrated dipoles appearing in the two blocks.
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{2},M_{X}}}
depends on mapped momenta, which come from the insertion of an extra unresolved gluon in the
n
-particle phase space, where the double virtual subtraction term lives. On the other hand, the integrated dipoles in
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{2},J_{X}}}
depend on
(n+1)
-particle phase space momenta, which are not accessible at the double virtual level. It is trivial to verify that the mass factorization kernels in the integrated dipoles in
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{2},J_{X}}}
and
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{2},M_{X}}}
exactly cancel for any configuration of gluons
i
,
j
and
k

\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF,1,b_{2}}}
.

For
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{2},M_{X}}}
one obtains the relation:

\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{2},M_{X}}}=-2\mathcal{I}ns\left[\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U,c,\mathcal{X}_{3}^{0}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{3}^{0}}}\right]-\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF,1,b_{2}}}.

(118)

The structure of
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{2},M_{X}}}
is indeed the required one, according to (

\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{2},M_{X}}}\sim-2\sum_{ijk}f_{3}^{0}(i,k,j)\mathcal{J}_{2}^{(1)}(\widetilde{ik},\widetilde{kj})a_{n+2}^{0}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime};\{.,\widetilde{ik},.,\widetilde{kj},.\}).

(119)

The leftover
+\mathcal{I}ns\left[\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U,c,\mathcal{X}_{3}^{0}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{3}^{0}}}\right]
is used in
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,c}}
, as we show in the next section. In the gluons-only case, the two integrated antennae are identical (see (
4.3
)), so the choice of which one has to be translated into its unintegrated form is irrelevant. In general, the same symmetrization procedure employed for
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U,b}}
can be used here as well. In conclusion, the (loop
\times
tree) block is given by:

\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{2}}}=-\mathcal{I}ns\left[\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U,c,\mathcal{X}_{3}^{1}}}\right]+\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{2},J_{X}}}-2\mathcal{I}ns\left[\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U,c,\mathcal{X}_{3}^{0}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{3}^{0}}}\right]-\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF,1,b_{2}}},

(120)

which is free of
\epsilon
-poles. We remark again that
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{MF,1,b_{2}}}
is compensated by an identical contribution in
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{2},J_{X}}}
and no new mass factorization kernels are actually added in
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{2}}}

The last contribution to
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b}}
is required to fix the renormalization of one-loop quantities in
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{1}}}
and
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{2}}}
. To fix the correct renormalization of both the one-loop matrix elements and the one-loop antenna functions it is sufficient to perform the following replacement
Gehrmann-DeRidder:2005btv
:

X_{3}^{1}(i,k,j)\to X_{3}^{1}(i,k,j)+\dfrac{b_{0}}{\epsilon}X_{3}^{0}(i,k,j)\left(\left(\dfrac{\left|s_{ijk}\right|}{\mu_{r}^{2}}\right)^{-\epsilon}-1\right).

(121)

\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T,b_{3}}}

The last block of the real virtual subtraction term is

4.5
NNLO double real subtraction term

The last ingredient for an NNLO calculation is the double real subtraction term
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S}_{gg,NNLO}}
, which removes the divergent behaviour of the double real matrix element in single and double unresolved limits. In the colourful antenna subtraction approach, the generation of
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S}_{gg,NNLO}}
is performed at the end, with the significant advantage of avoiding to deal with the involved IR structure of the matrix elements arising from the large number of partons. Once the double virtual and the real virtual subtraction terms are available, it is indeed straightforward to complete the subtraction procedure with the missing blocks needed to cancel the unmatched contributions in those two layers. The double real subtraction term is constructed inserting a second unresolved gluon in contributions coming from
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{T}_{gg,NNLO}}
and two unresolved gluons in terms coming from
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U}_{gg,NNLO}}
. The only non-trivial step, as we show later in this section, is related to these latter contributions, in particular to the insertion of an unresolved gluon pair in the integrated four-parton antennae
\mathcal{X}_{4}^{0}
. The appropriate overall coefficient to be used to dress the double real subtraction term is given by:

\mathcal{N}^{RR}_{NNLO}=s_{RR}(4\pi\alpha_{s})^{2}\mathcal{N}_{LO},

(129)

where, in the gluons-only case we have

s_{RR}=\dfrac{n!}{(n+2)!}=\dfrac{1}{(n+1)(n+2)}.

(130)

We recall the usual decomposition of
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S}_{gg,NNLO}}

Currie:2013vh

\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S}_{gg,NNLO}}=\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S,a}}+\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S,b}}+\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S,c}}+\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S,d}}.

(131)

The first term
\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S,a}}
removes single unresolved limits and it is analogous to an NLO real subtraction term for an
(n+3)
-particle Born process. The remaining terms respectively reproduce the divergent behaviour of the double real correction in colour-connected, almost colour-connected and colour-unconnected configurations

;
Currie:2013vh

\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{S,c}}
also contains the large angle soft terms. In the following, we describe how to systematically generate each contribution.

5
Gluonic three-jet production at NNLO

To assess the applicability of the colourful antenna subtraction scheme, we compute the NNLO QCD corrections to the
hadron-collider all-gluons three-jet production process:

pp(gg)\to ggg

(143)

We set
N_{f}=0
and we only consider gluons both as internal and external particles, as if no quarks were present in the theory (pure Yang-Mills). To be consistent with this choice, the renormalization of one- and two-loop amplitudes is performed setting
N_{f}=0
and the mass factorization counterterms only contain gluon-to-gluon splitting kernels. The NNLO correction in the gluons-only scenario is theoretically well defined and all IR divergences cancel in the sum of virtual and real corrections. We perform the calculation in full colour, with the only exception represented by the finite remainder of the two-loop five-gluon amplitude
Abreu:2021oya
, which is only available at leading colour.

From a phenomenological point of view, this calculation is part of the NNLO corrections to three-jet production (which were computed
recently
Czakon:2021mjy
;
Czakon:2014oma
using a residue subtraction technique
Czakon:2010td
, with the same leading-colour restriction on the
finite remainders of the virtual two-loop amplitudes). However, our result can not be considered a faithful approximation of the full calculation, due to the absence of quarks. Both the
N_{f}\neq 0
contributions to gluonic scattering and the quark-induced channels have a large impact, both in terms of the final numerical result and of the scale variation behaviour of the theoretical predictions. Indeed, as we discuss in more detail in section
5.4
below, the gluons-only picture exhibits pathological issues when one assesses the theoretical uncertainty by means of the usual renormalization and factorization scale variation analysis. This is not surprising, given the unphysical nature of the gluons-only scenario.

For these reasons, our calculation should be considered as a proof of concept for the automated implementation of the colourful antenna subtraction formalism, and not yet as a precision phenomenology prediction.

5.1
Computational setup

The computation is performed within the
NNLOjet
framework.
NNLOjet
is a Monte Carlo event generator which implements the antenna subtraction method to compute NNLO QCD corrections to a series of processes. For gluonic three-jet production at NNLO, high multiplicity tree and loop gluonic amplitudes are needed. The computation relies on a mixture of analytical results and numerical implementations for the amplitudes. The tree-level helicity amplitudes for the scattering of five, six and seven gluons
Berends:1987cv
;
Dixon:2010ik
, corresponding to the LO, real and double real contributions, are incorporated in an analytical form in
NNLOjet
. This is also the case for the five-gluon one-loop helicity amplitudes
Bern:1993mq
, which represent the virtual correction.

The planar five-parton two-loop amplitudes have recently been computed
Abreu:2019odu
using a basis of pentagon functions
Chicherin:2017dob
;
Gehrmann:2018yef
;
Chicherin:2020oor
. We rely on a public C
++
code
Abreu:2021oya

\ket{\mathcal{R}^{(2)}}=\ket{\mathcal{A}^{(2)}}-\bm{I}^{(1)}\left(\epsilon\right)\ket{\mathcal{A}^{(1)}}-\bm{I}^{(2)}\left(\epsilon\right)\ket{\mathcal{A}^{(0)}},

(144)

where the

), one-loop (
\ell=1
) and tree-level (
\ell=0
) amplitudes. The quantity in (

\epsilon

\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{VV}_{gg,NNLO}}-\text{d}{\hat{\sigma}^{U}_{gg,NNLO}}
.

The six-gluon one-loop matrix elements are computed with the
OpenLoops
generator
Cascioli:2011va
;
Buccioni:2017yxi
;
Buccioni:2019sur

OpenLoops

Cascioli:2011va

Buccioni:2017yxi

Chen:2021azt

Buccioni:2017yxi

OpenLoops

Collier

OpenLoops
allows for the computation of partial amplitudes, which can be suitably combined to construct the leading colour contribution, according to (
28

The subtraction terms needed for the NNLO calculation are constructed in a systematic way with the colourful antenna subtraction method, as described in sections
3
and
4
. The subtraction terms only depend on five- and six-gluon tree-level amplitudes and five-gluon one-loop amplitudes, so they are implemented in a completely analytical fashion within
NNLOjet
. The real, double real and real virtual subtraction terms have been extensively tested against the corresponding matrix elements to check the pointwise cancellation of IR divergences in single and double unresolved limits, as discussed in section
5.2
.

The NNLO correction to this process is very challenging from the computational point of view. The stability over the whole phase space of the numerical implementations of double virtual and real virtual matrix elements must be ensured exploiting quadruple-precision arithmetic when the standard evaluation fails. Moreover, due to the high multiplicity, the numerical integration of real virtual and double real corrections and their respective subtraction terms requires a very substantial number of evaluations to reach a satisfactory precision.

In double and triple collinear limits, the subleading colour contribution does not exhibit a divergent behaviour. This is motivated by an argument analogous to the one adduced in
Currie:2013dwa
to explain why the six-gluon tree-level matrix element is not divergent in single, double and triple collinear limits at subleading colour. For the seven-gluon tree-level matrix element considered here, one can express the subleading colour contribution as a sum of incoherent interferences within which the two colour orderings share at most a single pair of adjacent gluons. This implies that the subleading colour part of the matrix element is indeed divergent in single collinear limits, but is finite in double and triple collinear limits.

In general we observe that the subtraction terms correctly reproduce the divergent behaviour of the double real correction in both double and single unresolved configurations, with the quality of the agreement increasing with the infrared depth. Once again, the subleading colour contribution presents some numerical noise with respect to the leading colour part, for the same reasons explained in the real virtual case.

5.4
Comments on scale variation

In this section we investigate the anomalous scale variation behaviour observed in the NLO and NNLO correction to gluonic three-jet production. As pointed out above, the coupling constant
\alpha_{s}(\mu_{r})
sees the full content of QCD, since its determination relies on experimental measurements and scale evolution equations which incorporate the dependence on the number of light quark flavours. On the contrary, the matrix elements we implemented in our calculation only contain the gluonic degrees of freedom and are then evaluated for
N_{f}=0
. This mismatch breaks the interplay between the renormalization scale variation of the running coupling and the virtual corrections at higher orders. An analogous observation can be done for the factorization scale
\mu_{f}
. The scale dependence of the PDFs takes into account the presence of light quark flavours. To correctly compensate the PDF’s factorization scale variation at higher orders in QCD, the inclusion of quark-induced channels is necessary. Therefore, the scale variation analysis should provide a consistent estimate of the uncertainty of theoretical predictions for a given process only when the entire set of subprocesses is considered and included in the final result.

We illustrate the effect of the inclusion of quarks on the scale variation analysis with a dedicated study on dijet production. We implement the same setup and cuts described in section
5.3
and we compute the NNLO corrections with the traditional antenna subtraction
approach in the leading colour approximation
Currie:2013dwa
;
Currie:2017eqf
, for three different scenarios:

•

pp(gg)\to jj
with
N_{f}=0
, gluons-only;

•

pp(gg)\to jj
with
N_{f}=5
, where we include the contribution coming from fermionic loops in virtual corrections as well as gluons splitting into quark-antiquark pairs in real emission corrections, but we keep the restriction to gluon-initiated subprocesses only;

•

pp\to jj
, where we consider the whole set of sub-processes that contribute to dijet production, both gluon- and quark-initiated.

The first scenario reflects the same implementation we used for gluonic three-jet production and we expect to observe a similar pattern, namely an anomalous size of the NNLO scale variation bands with respect to NLO. Allowing gluons to split into quark-antiquark pairs should restore the correct compensation at higher orders of the renormalization scale dependence of the strong coupling. Finally, with the inclusion of quark-induced sub-processes, we expect to correctly balance factorization scale variation effects due to the evolution of the PDFs.

6
Conclusions and outlook

The antenna subtraction method for the construction of infrared subtraction terms at NLO and NNLO has originally been formulated
based on colour-ordered amplitudes and squared matrix elements. This formulation results in very compact subtraction terms for
low-multiplicity processes, but extends very poorly to higher multiplicities and requires complicated constructions beyond the leading-colour
contributions. This paper overcomes these constrictions by reformulating the antenna subtraction in a new colour space basis. The resulting
colourful antenna subtraction method allows for a systematic construction of antenna subtraction terms. Starting from an analysis
of the colour structure of purely virtual corrections at NLO and NNLO, we were able to devise an algorithm that translates
infrared poles of virtual corrections into real radiation dipole insertions that constitute the subtraction terms for single real radiation up to one-loop
level and for double real radiation at tree level. We remark once again that the main advantage of this perspective consists in avoiding the direct treatment of the divergent behaviour of real emission corrections. In the case of an NNLO calculation, this feature represents a major simplification, since the double real subtraction term is obtained as the last step of a completely automatable procedure, with no need of dealing with the involved infrared structure of double real radiation matrix elements.

We fully formulated the colourful antenna subtraction method for gluons-only processes and automated its workflow. As proof-of-principle
applications, we rederived the NNLO antenna subtraction terms for gluons-only dijet production
Currie:2013dwa
and newly derived
these terms of gluons-only three-jet production. We verified the pointwise convergence of these subtraction terms towards the respective matrix elements and demonstrated the numerical stability of the predictions in computing various differential three-jet distributions. As a by-product, we
also assessed the reliability of the gluons-only approximation to dijet production, demonstrating in particular the large impact of missing
fermionic contributions and quark-induced subprocesses onto the renormalization and factorization scale dependence of the predictions. The
gluons-only predictions for three jet production are thus not yet of phenomenological relevance, but should only be considered as
proof-of-principle application of the newly developed coloured antenna subtraction method and of its automated implementation.
We also were able to quantify the numerical impact of subleading colour contributions at NNLO, which were found to be
small throughout most distributions, thus lending support to a recent computation of NNLO corrections to three-jet production at leading
colour
Czakon:2021mjy
obtained with a residue subtraction method.

The next step towards a complete, process-independent subtraction scheme at NNLO consists in the inclusion of quarks in the presented formalism. To treat these contributions in the context of the colourful antenna subtraction approach, a crucial distinction must be made between the identity-preserving (IP) and identity-changing (IC) sectors of the subtraction infrastructure. The IP sector contains infrared limits for which the real emission corrections factorize onto a lower multiplicity process with the same initial-state parton species. On the contrary, the IC sector refers to the configurations where a final-state quark becomes collinear to an initial-state gluon or same-flavour quark, effectively changing the initial-state parton species of the associated reduced matrix element in the collinear limit.

After integration over the unresolved radiation, the IP contributions generate
\epsilon
-poles which cancel against the explicit singularities of the corresponding virtual contributions and IP mass factorization counterterms. Therefore, the whole IP sector would be systematically constructed at NNLO following the procedure summarized in Figure
1
, in complete analogy to what was done in the simplified case of pure gluonic scattering. Of course, appropriate colour stripped one- and two-loop integrated dipoles need to be defined to include quark-antiquark and quark-gluon configurations, as well as the correct translation between integrated and unintegrated antenna functions. Nevertheless, these steps do not represent a major issue and most of the ingredients for the extension to the IP sector of sub-processes involving quarks have already been constructed and assessed.

The IC infrared divergences cancel against
\epsilon
-poles in IC mass factorization counterterms and do not communicate with the singularity structure of the virtual corrections. For this reason, these contributions can not be straightforwardly generated with the method we described in this paper,
thus requiring an extension of the approach. The starting point for the construction of the IC sector will be the IC mass factorization structure at one and two loops, which can be written down in a general way from (
44
), (
82
) and (
91
). The main issue is then to dress the splitting kernels with the appropriate IC integrated antenna functions, in such a way suitable universal IC integrated dipoles can be constructed and the entire generation of the subtraction layers can proceed in the same way as it does for the IP sector. At NLO, IC integrated dipoles have already been defined, for example in
Currie:2013vh
, while at NNLO this task still requires additional work.

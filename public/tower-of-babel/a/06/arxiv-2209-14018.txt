No quantum solutions to linear constraint systems in odd dimension from Pauli group and diagonal Cliffords
Markus Frembs, Cihan Okay, Ho Yiu Chung

Abstract
Linear constraint systems (LCS) have proven to be a surprisingly prolific tool in the study of non-classical correlations and various related issues in quantum foundations. Many results are known for the Boolean case, yet the generalisation to systems of odd dimension is largely open. In particular, it is not known whether there exist LCS in odd dimension, which admit finite-dimensional quantum, but no classical solutions. In recent work, [J. Phys. A, 53 , 385304 (2020)] have shown that unlike in the Boolean case, where the n n -qubit Pauli group gives rise to quantum solutions of LCS such as the Mermin-Peres square, the n n -qudit Pauli group never gives rise to quantum solutions of a LCS in odd dimension. Here, we generalise this result towards the Clifford hierarchy. More precisely, we consider tensor products of groups generated by (single-qudit) Pauli and diagonal Clifford operators.

4.1
Abelian subgroups of
K^{\otimes n}_{Q}(p)

From now on, we fix
p
to be an odd prime. In this section we will describe (
p
-torsion) abelian subgroups of
K^{\otimes n}_{Q}(p)
where
Q=T_{(p^{m})}
.
For simplicity, we will use the following notation:

K=K_{Q}(p)\;\;\text{ and }\;\;K^{\otimes n}=K^{\otimes n}_{Q}(p)\;.

We begin with a description of
p
-torsion elements in
K
.

Lemma 3
.

An element
M\in K
is
p
-torsion, i.e.
M^{p}=\mathbbm{1}
, if either

•

M=S_{\xi}
for some
S_{\xi}\in T_{(p)}
, or

•

M=S_{\xi}X^{b}
with
b\neq 0
.

Proof.

Follows from Lm.
2
(1), and our assumption
S_{\xi}\in T
, which implies
\det(S_{\xi})=\prod_{k=0}^{p-1}k\cdot\xi=1
.
∎

Next, we describe commuting pairs of elements in
K
up to a phase.

Lemma 4
.

Let
M=S_{\xi}X^{b}
and
M^{\prime}=S_{\xi^{\prime}}X^{b^{\prime}}
be elements in
K
such that
[M,M^{\prime}]=\omega^{c}\mathbbm{1}
for some
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
Then one of the following cases hold:

1.

b=b^{\prime}=0
and
M,M^{\prime}\in Q
. In particular,
c=0
.

2.

b\neq 0
and
b^{\prime}=0
and
M^{\prime}\in\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. In addition, if
c=0
, then
M^{\prime}\in Z(K)
.

3.

b,b^{\prime}\neq 0
and there exits
a,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
S_{\chi}\in\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
such that
\omega^{a}M^{\prime}=(MS_{\chi})^{y}
. Here
\chi(q)=\omega^{c^{\prime}q}
where
b^{\prime}c^{\prime}=-c
and
yb=b^{\prime}
. In addition, if
c=0
, then
\omega^{a}M^{\prime}=M^{y}
.

Proof.

We will use the alternative notation
(\xi,b)=S_{\xi}X^{b}
.
First, we calculate the commutator:

\displaystyle=(\xi,b)(\xi^{\prime},b^{\prime})(\xi,b)^{-1}(\xi^{\prime},b^{\prime})^{-1}

(13)

\displaystyle=\left(\xi b\cdot\xi^{\prime},b+b^{\prime}\right)\,\,\left((-b)\cdot\xi^{-1},-b\right)\left((-b^{\prime})\cdot\xi^{\prime-1},-b^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\xi b\cdot\xi^{\prime},b+b^{\prime}\right)\left((-b)\cdot\xi^{-1}(-b-b^{\prime})\cdot\xi^{\prime-1},-b-b^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\xi b\cdot\xi^{\prime}(b+b^{\prime}-b)\cdot\xi^{-1}(b+b^{\prime}-b-b^{\prime})\cdot\xi^{\prime-1},b+b^{\prime}-b-b^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\xi b\cdot\xi^{\prime}b^{\prime}\cdot\xi^{-1}\xi^{\prime-1},0\right)\;.

Thus we have
[M,M^{\prime}]=(\hat{\xi},0)
, where
\hat{\xi}(q)=\xi(q)\xi^{\prime}(q-b)\xi^{-1}(q-b^{\prime})\xi^{\prime-1}(q)
for all
q\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Case 1: We assume
b=b^{\prime}=0
. In this case, we have
M,M^{\prime}\in Q
. This group is abelian, thus
c=0
.

Case 2: We assume
b\neq 0
and
b^{\prime}=0
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle[M,M^{\prime}]=\omega^{c}\mathbbm{1}

\displaystyle\iff\xi(q)\xi^{\prime}(q-b)\xi^{-1}(q)\xi^{\prime-1}(q)=\omega^{c}

\displaystyle\iff\xi^{\prime}(q-b)=\xi^{\prime}(q)\omega^{c}

\displaystyle\iff\xi^{\prime}(q)=\xi^{\prime}(q+b)\omega^{c}\;.

Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that
mb=-1
. Thus we have
q+mqb=0
. By the equation above, we have

\xi^{\prime}(q)=\xi^{\prime}(q+b)\omega^{c}=\xi^{\prime}(q+2b)\omega^{2c}=\cdots=\xi^{\prime}(q+mqb)\omega^{mqc}=\xi^{\prime}(0)\omega^{mqc}\;.

By definition (cf. Eq. (
7
)), we have
\prod_{q=0}^{p-1}\xi^{\prime}(q)=1
. Thus we obtain

1=\prod_{q=0}^{p-1}\xi^{\prime}(q)=\prod_{q=0}^{p-1}\xi^{\prime}(0)\omega^{mqc}=(\xi^{\prime}(0))^{p}\omega^{mc\sum_{q=0}^{p-1}q}=(\xi^{\prime}(0))^{p}\;,

where we used that
\sum_{q=0}^{p-1}q=\frac{p(p-1)}{2}
is divisible by
2
, hence,
\sum_{q=0}^{p-1}q
is a multiple of
p
and thus
\omega^{mc\sum_{q=0}^{p-1}q}=1
.
Finally, we set
\xi^{\prime}(0)=\omega^{a}
for some
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and obtain

\xi^{\prime}(q)=\xi^{\prime}(0)\omega^{mqc}=\omega^{a}\omega^{mqc}=\omega^{mcq+a}\;,

therefore
M^{\prime}=(\xi^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. In particular, if
c=0
we have
\xi^{\prime}(q)=\omega^{a}
, and as a result
M^{\prime}\in Z(K)
.

Case 3: Assume that
b,b^{\prime}\neq 0
. We pick
c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
y\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that
b^{\prime}c^{\prime}=-c
and
yb=b^{\prime}
, and we define
\chi(q)=\omega^{c^{\prime}q}
. By Lm.
2
(1), we have

{}(MS_{\chi})^{y}=\left((\xi,b)(\chi,0)\right)^{y}=(\xi b\cdot\chi,b)^{y}=(\bar{\xi},yb)=(\bar{\xi},b^{\prime})\;,

(14)

where
\bar{\xi}=\left(\prod_{i=0}^{y-1}(ib)\cdot\xi\right)\left(\prod_{i=0}^{y-1}(ib+b)\cdot\chi\right)
. Since
[M,M^{\prime}]=\omega^{c}\mathbbm{1}
, by Eq. (
13
), we have

{}\xi(q)\xi^{\prime}(q-b)\xi^{-1}(q-b^{\prime})\xi^{\prime-1}(q)=\omega^{c}\iff\frac{\xi(q)}{\xi(q-b^{\prime})}=\frac{\xi^{\prime}(q)}{\xi^{\prime}(q-b)}\omega^{c}\;.

(15)

Then we calculate

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{\xi}(q)}{\bar{\xi}(q-b)}=\frac{\bar{\xi}}{b\cdot\bar{\xi}}(q)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\prod_{i=0}^{y-1}(ib)\cdot\xi\right)\left(\prod_{i=0}^{y-1}(ib+b)\cdot\chi\right)}{\left(\prod_{i=0}^{y-1}(ib+b)\cdot\xi\right)\left(\prod_{i=0}^{y-1}(ib+b+b)\cdot\chi\right)}(q)

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi\left(\prod_{i=1}^{y-1}(ib)\cdot\xi\right)b\cdot\chi\left(\prod_{i=1}^{y-1}(ib+b)\cdot\chi\right)}{\left(\prod_{i=1}^{y}(ib)\cdot\xi\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{y}(ib+b)\cdot\chi\right)}(q)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\xi}{(yb)\cdot\xi}\right)\frac{b\cdot\chi}{(yb+b)\cdot\chi}(q)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\xi(q)}{\xi(q-b^{\prime})}\right)\frac{\omega^{(q-b)c^{\prime}}}{\omega^{(q-b^{\prime}-b)c^{\prime}}}\,\,\,\,\,\text{(by defintion of $\chi$ and $yb=b^{\prime}$)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi^{\prime}(q)}{\xi^{\prime}(q-b)}\omega^{c}\omega^{b^{\prime}c^{\prime}}\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{(by Eq. (\ref{eq2})})

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi^{\prime}(q)}{\xi^{\prime}(q-b)}\;.

By rearranging this equation, we get

\frac{\bar{\xi}(q)}{\xi^{\prime}(q)}=\frac{\bar{\xi}(q-b)}{\xi^{\prime}(q-b)}\;,

for all
q\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Thus we conclude that there is a constant
x
such that for all
q\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, we have
\bar{\xi}(q)=x\xi^{\prime}(q)
. By definition, we have
\prod_{q=0}^{p-1}\bar{\xi}(q)=\prod_{q=0}^{p-1}\xi^{\prime}(q)=1.
It follows that

1=\prod_{q=0}^{p-1}\bar{\xi}(q)=\prod_{q=0}^{p-1}x\xi^{\prime}(q)=x^{p}\prod_{q=0}^{p-1}\xi^{\prime}(q)=x^{p}\;.

Thus
x=\omega^{a}
for some
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and we have
\bar{\xi}(q)=\omega^{a}\xi^{\prime}(q)
for some
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Combining
\bar{\xi}(q)=\omega^{a}\xi^{\prime}(q)
with Eq. (
14
), we obtain

(MS_{\chi})^{y}=(\bar{\xi},b^{\prime})=(\omega^{a}\xi^{\prime},b^{\prime})=(\omega^{a},0)(\xi^{\prime},b^{\prime})=\omega^{a}M^{\prime}\;.

In particular, if
c=0
, then
\chi(q)=1
and
\omega^{a}M^{\prime}=M^{y}
.
∎

These two results combined gives us the description of
p
-torsion abelian subgroups of
K
.

Corollary 1
.

Maximal
p
-torsion abelian subgroups of
K
fall into two classes:

•

the subgroup
T_{(p)}
, and

•

\langle\omega\mathbbm{1},S_{\xi}X\rangle
where
S_{\xi}\in Q=T_{(p^{m})}
.

Any two distinct maximal
p
-torsion abelian subgroup intersect at the center
Z(K)=\langle\omega\mathbbm{1}\rangle
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Lm.
4
with
c=0
and Lm.
3
.
∎

We reformulate Lm.
4
in a way that will be useful for computing the commutator in terms of a symplectic form.

Corollary 2
.

Let
M=S_{\xi}X^{b}
and
M^{\prime}=S_{\xi^{\prime}}X^{b^{\prime}}
be elements in
K
such that
[M,M^{\prime}]=\omega^{c}\mathbbm{1}
for some
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
Then one of the following cases hold:

(1)

b=b^{\prime}=0
, in which case
c=0
.

(2)

b\neq 0
, in which case
M^{\prime}=S_{\chi^{\prime}}M^{b^{\prime}/b}
where
\chi^{\prime}(q)=\omega^{\alpha^{\prime}_{0}+\alpha^{\prime}_{1}q}
for some
\alpha_{0}^{\prime},\alpha_{1}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
c=-b\alpha^{\prime}_{1}
.

(3)

b^{\prime}\neq 0
, in which case
M=S_{\chi}M^{b/b^{\prime}}
where
\chi(q)=\omega^{\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}q}
for some
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
c=b^{\prime}\alpha_{1}
.

Proof.

We will explain the case where at least one of
b
or
b^{\prime}
is nonzero. Then we are either in case (2) or (3) of Lm.
4
. Assume we are in case (3). We can write

M^{\prime}=S_{\chi^{\prime}}M^{b^{\prime}/b}

for some
S_{\chi^{\prime}}\in\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
using part (2) and (3) of Lm.
4
. Using Eq. (
13
) we compute
c=-b\alpha_{1}^{\prime}
. Note that taking
b^{\prime}=0
we see that Lm.
4
part (2) is also covered by this case. Case (3) can be dealt with in a similar way.
∎

Next we move on to analyzing
K^{\otimes n}
.
Using Thm.
3
in the Appendix we can express
\xi
as follows:

\xi(q)=\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{2\pi i}{p^{j}}f_{j}(q)\right)\;,

(16)

where each
f_{j}
is a polynomial of the form

f_{j}(q)=\sum_{a=0}^{p-1}\vartheta_{j,a}q^{a},\;\;\;\vartheta_{j,a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\;.

(17)

We need a generalized version of Lm.
3
to describe
p
-torsion elements in
K^{\otimes n}
.

Lemma 5
.

An element
M=M_{1}\otimes M_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes M_{n}
in
K^{\otimes n}
is
p
-torsion if there exists a subset
I\subset\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
such that the following holds:

•

M_{i}\notin Q
, that is,
M_{i}=S_{\xi_{i}}X^{b_{i}}
with
b_{i}\neq 0
for all
i\notin I
.

•

For all
i\in I
we have
M_{i}\in Q
such that
\vartheta_{j,a}^{(i)}=0
for all
j\geq 2,a\neq 0
and
\sum_{i\in I}\vartheta_{2,0}^{(i)}=0\mod p
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
M_{i}\in Q
satisfies
M_{i}^{p}=\omega^{c_{i}}\mathbbm{1}
for some
c_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
whenever
\vartheta_{j,a}^{(i)}=0
for all
j\geq 2,a\neq 0
. This follows from Eq. (
16
).
∎

To track the commutator in the case of
K^{\otimes n}
we will need the symplectic form borrowed from the Heisenberg-Weyl group
H^{\otimes n}(p)
. For
v=(v_{Z},v_{X})
and
v^{\prime}=(v^{\prime}_{Z},v^{\prime}_{X})
in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
the symplectic form is defined by

[v,v^{\prime}]=v_{Z}\cdot v^{\prime}_{X}-v^{\prime}_{Z}\cdot v_{X}\mod p\;,

where
\cdot
denotes the inner product on vectors in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{p}
. Using this symplectic form we can describe the commutator of two elements that commute up to a phase.

Corollary 3
.

Let
M=M_{1}\otimes M_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes M_{n}
and
M^{\prime}=M^{\prime}_{1}\otimes M^{\prime}_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes M^{\prime}_{n}
be elements in
K^{\otimes n}
such that
[M,M^{\prime}]=\omega^{c}\mathbbm{1}
. For every
1\leq i\leq n
, write
M_{i}=(\xi_{i},b_{i})
, let
\bar{b}=(b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{n})
and let
\bar{f}=(\vartheta_{1,1}^{(1)},\vartheta_{1,1}^{(2)},\cdots,\vartheta_{1,1}^{(n)})
, where
\vartheta_{1,1}^{(i)}
is the coefficient in Eq. (
17
) that appears in the expansion of
\xi_{i}
in Eq. (
16
); similarly for
\bar{f}^{\prime}
and
\bar{b}^{\prime}
.
Then we have

[M,M^{\prime}]=\omega^{[v,v^{\prime}]}\mathbbm{1}\;,

where
v=(\bar{f},\bar{b})
and
v^{\prime}=(\bar{f}^{\prime},\bar{b}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Since
[M,M^{\prime}]=\omega^{c}\mathbbm{1}
we have
[M_{i},M_{i}^{\prime}]=\omega^{c_{i}}\mathbbm{1}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}=c\mod p
.
Cor.
2
implies that
c_{i}
is either zero or given by the formula in parts (2) and (3) of this result.
Suppose we are in case (2). Then
M^{\prime}_{i}=S_{\chi^{\prime}}M_{i}^{b^{\prime}/b}
where
\chi^{\prime}=\alpha_{0}^{\prime}+\alpha_{1}^{\prime}q
. Using Lm.
2
(1) we can compute the coefficient
\vartheta_{1,1}^{\prime(i)}
in
\xi^{\prime}
. It turns out that

\vartheta_{1,1}^{\prime(i)}=\alpha_{1}^{\prime}+\frac{b^{\prime}}{b}\vartheta_{1,1}^{(i)},

which gives

[M_{i},M^{\prime}_{i}]=\omega^{-b\alpha_{1}^{\prime}}\mathbbm{1}=\omega^{-b(\vartheta_{1,1}^{\prime(i)}-\frac{b^{\prime}}{b}\vartheta_{1,1}^{(i)})}\mathbbm{1}=\omega^{\vartheta_{1,1}^{(i)}b^{\prime}-\vartheta_{1,1}^{\prime(i)}b}\mathbbm{1}\;.

Case (3) can be dealt with similarly. Combining
\omega^{c_{i}}
’s we obtain the formula for the symplectic form.
∎

For each
M=M_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes M_{n}
we will associate the vector
v=(\bar{f},\bar{b})
defined in Cor.
3
. A subspace
W
in
\mathbb{Z}_{d}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}_{d}^{n}
is called isotropic if
[w,w^{\prime}]=0
for all
w,w^{\prime}\in W
. Using this definition we observe that
a subgroup
\langle M^{(1)},M^{(2)},\cdots,M^{(n)}\rangle
of
K^{\otimes n}
is abelian if and only if the subspace
\langle v^{(1)},v^{(2)},\cdots,v^{(n)}\rangle
of
\mathbb{Z}_{d}^{2n}
, where
v^{(i)}=(\bar{f}^{(i)},b^{(i)})
, is isotropic.

4.2
Projecting
p
-torsion abelian subgroups into the Heisenberg-Weyl group

Let
K^{\otimes n}_{(p)}
denote the subset of
p
-torsion elements in
K^{\otimes n}
.
In this section, we will construct a map

\phi:K^{\otimes n}_{(p)}\to H^{\otimes n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\;,

that restricts to a group homomorphism on every
p
-torsion abelian subgroup.
Cor.
3
hints at a “linearization map" that could potentially serve for this purpose. The map we need is closely related to linearization, yet needs to be slightly adjusted when acting on
p
-torsion elements. We will revisit this point in Remark.
1
below, after the definition of the map.

Definition 2
.

For
\xi
given as in Eq. (
16
) we introduce two maps:

R(\xi)=\omega^{\vartheta_{1,0}+\vartheta_{2,0}+\vartheta_{1,1}q}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;P(\xi)=\omega^{\vartheta_{1,0}+\vartheta_{1,1}q}\;.

Using this notation we define a map

\phi:K_{(p)}^{\otimes n}\to H^{\otimes n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})

as follows:

\phi(M_{1}\otimes M_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes M_{n})=\phi_{1}(M_{1})\otimes\phi_{1}(M_{2})\otimes\cdots\otimes\phi_{1}(M_{n})\;,

where
\phi_{1}(M)
for
M=(\xi,b)\in K
is defined by

\phi_{1}(M)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(R(\xi),0)&\text{if $b=0$}\\
(P(\xi),1)&\text{if $b=1$}\end{array}\right.\;.

and
\phi_{1}(M)=\phi_{1}(M^{b_{i}^{-1}})^{b_{i}}
if
1<b<p-1
.

Remark 1
.

Note the additional factor
\omega^{\vartheta_{2,0}}
in the definition of
\phi_{1}(M)
for
M\in Q
. Recall that for
M^{p}=\mathbbm{1}
,

M=(\omega^{\sum_{a=0}^{p-1}\vartheta_{1,a}q^{a}}\sqrt[p]{\omega}^{\vartheta_{2,0}},0)\;,

by Lm.
5
. It is easy to see that defining
\phi_{1}
as a linear projection in both cases, that is for
b=0,1
, would not restrict to a group homomorphism as desired.

To see this, let
p=3
and consider
S_{\xi_{1}}\otimes S_{\xi_{2}}
where
\xi_{1}(q)=\sqrt[3]{\omega}
and
\xi_{2}(q)=\sqrt[3]{\omega}^{2}
:

\displaystyle\phi(M_{1}\otimes M_{2})\phi(M_{1}\otimes M_{2})

\displaystyle=(\phi_{1}(M_{1})\otimes\phi_{1}(M_{2}))(\phi_{1}(M_{1})\otimes\phi_{1}(M_{2}))

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}\otimes\mathbbm{1}\;,

which is not the same as

\displaystyle\phi((M_{1}\otimes M_{2})(M_{1}\otimes M_{2}))

\displaystyle=\phi(M_{1}^{2}\otimes M_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle=\phi(\sqrt[3]{\omega}^{2}\mathbbm{1}\otimes\omega\sqrt[3]{\omega}\mathbbm{1})

\displaystyle=\phi_{1}(\sqrt[3]{\omega}^{2}\mathbbm{1})\otimes\phi_{1}(\omega\sqrt[3]{\omega}\mathbbm{1})

\displaystyle=\omega(\mathbbm{1}\otimes\mathbbm{1})\;.

Lemma 6
.

Let
S_{\xi},S_{\xi^{\prime}}\in Q
be such that
S_{\xi}^{p},S_{\xi^{\prime}}^{p}\in Z(K)
. Then we have

\phi(S_{\xi}S_{\xi^{\prime}})=\phi(S_{\xi})\phi(S_{\xi^{\prime}})\;.

Proof.

The condition
S_{\xi}^{p},S_{\xi^{\prime}}^{p}\in Z(K)
implies that
\vartheta_{j,a}=0
for
j\geq 2,a\neq 0
; similarly for
\vartheta_{j,a}^{\prime}
(according to the proof of Lm.
5
). In other words, we have

\displaystyle\xi(q)

\displaystyle=\omega^{\sum_{a=0}^{p-1}\vartheta_{1,a}q^{a}}\sqrt[p]{\omega}^{\vartheta_{2,0}}

\displaystyle\xi^{\prime}(q)

\displaystyle=\omega^{\sum_{a=0}^{p-1}\vartheta^{\prime}_{1,a}q^{a}}\sqrt[p]{\omega}^{\vartheta^{\prime}_{2,0}}

\displaystyle\xi(q)\xi^{\prime}(q)

\displaystyle=\omega^{\sum_{a=0}^{p-1}(\vartheta_{1,a}+\vartheta_{1,a})q^{a}}\sqrt[p]{\omega}^{\vartheta_{2,0}+\vartheta^{\prime}_{2,0}}.

By definition of
\phi
, we have

\phi(S_{\xi}S_{\xi^{\prime}})(q)=\omega^{\vartheta_{1,0}+\vartheta^{\prime}_{1,0}+(\vartheta_{1,1}+\vartheta^{\prime}_{1,1})q}\sqrt[p]{\omega}^{\vartheta_{2,0}+\vartheta^{\prime}_{2,0}}\;,

which is the same as

\phi(S_{\xi})(q)\phi(S_{\xi^{\prime}})(q)=\omega^{\vartheta_{1,0}+\vartheta_{1,1}q}\sqrt[p]{\omega}^{\vartheta_{2,0}}\omega^{\vartheta^{\prime}_{1,0}+\vartheta^{\prime}_{1,1}q}\sqrt[p]{\omega}^{\vartheta^{\prime}_{2,0}}=\omega^{\vartheta_{1,0}+\vartheta^{\prime}_{1,0}+(\vartheta_{1,1}+\vartheta^{\prime}_{1,1})q}\sqrt[p]{\omega}^{\vartheta_{2,0}+\vartheta^{\prime}_{2,0}}\;.

Thus
\phi(S_{\xi}S_{\xi^{\prime}})=\phi(S_{\xi})\phi(S_{\xi^{\prime}})
.
∎

Lemma 7
.

Let
M\in K
such that
M\not\in Q
and
S_{\chi}\in\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. Then we have

\phi(S_{\chi}M)=\phi(S_{\chi})\phi(M)=S_{\chi}\phi(M)\;.

Proof.

We denote
M=(\xi,b)
and
S_{\chi}=(\chi,0)
.
Define
\bar{\chi}=\prod_{i=0}^{y-1}(ib)\cdot\chi
and
\bar{\xi}=\prod_{i=0}^{y-1}(ib)\cdot\xi
, where
y\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that
yb=1
.
Consider the element
(\chi,b)
. By Lm.
2
, we have

(\chi,b)=((\chi,b)^{y})^{b}=\left(\prod_{i=0}^{y-1}(ib)\cdot\chi,1\right)^{b}=\left(\prod_{j=0}^{b-1}j\cdot\bar{\chi},b\right)\implies\chi=\prod_{j=0}^{b-1}j\cdot\bar{\chi}\;.

Next, consider the element
(\xi,b)
, we have

\displaystyle(\xi,b)=((\xi,b)^{y})^{b}=\left(\prod_{i=0}^{y-1}(ib)\cdot\xi,1\right)^{b}=(\bar{\xi},1)^{b}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\phi(\xi,b)=(P(\bar{\xi}),1)^{b}=\left(\prod_{j=0}^{b-1}j\cdot P(\bar{\xi}),b\right)\;.

For the element
S_{\chi}M
, we have

(\chi,0)(\xi,b)=((\chi\xi,b)^{y})^{b}=\left(\left(\prod_{i=0}^{y-1}(ib)\cdot\chi\right)\left(\prod_{i=0}^{y-1}(ib)\cdot\xi\right),1\right)^{b}=(\bar{\chi}\bar{\xi},1)^{b}.

Notice that since
S_{\bar{\chi}}\in\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, we have
P(\bar{\chi}\bar{\xi})=\bar{\chi}P(\bar{\xi})
. Therefore

\displaystyle\phi((\chi,0)(\xi,b))=(P(\bar{\chi}\bar{\xi}),1)^{b}

\displaystyle=(\bar{\chi}P(\bar{\xi}),1)^{b}

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\prod_{j=0}^{b-1}j\cdot\bar{\chi}\right)\left(\prod_{j=0}^{b-1}j\cdot P(\bar{\xi})\right),b\right)=(\chi,0)\phi(\xi,b)=\phi(\chi,0)\phi(\xi,b)\;.\qed

Lemma 8
.

Let
M=(\xi,b)
and
M^{\prime}=(\xi^{\prime},b^{\prime})
be elements of
K
such that
[M,M^{\prime}]=\omega^{c}\mathbbm{1}
for some
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
b,b^{\prime}\neq 0
. Then we have

\phi(MM^{\prime})=\phi(M)\phi(M^{\prime})\;.

Proof.

We have
M^{\prime}=(\xi^{\prime},b^{\prime})=(\xi_{0}^{\prime},1)^{b^{\prime}}
where
\xi^{\prime}_{0}=\prod_{i=0}^{z-1}(ib^{\prime})\cdot\xi^{\prime}
and
z\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
such that
zb^{\prime}=1
. Thus we have

\phi(M^{\prime})=(P(\xi_{0}^{\prime}),1)^{b^{\prime}}.

By Lm.
4
(3), there exists
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
y\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
where
yb^{\prime}=b
and
S_{\chi}=(\chi,0)\in\hat{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
such that
M=S_{\omega^{a}}(M^{\prime}S_{\chi})^{y}
. Thus we have

Discussion

The Mermin-Peres square in Fig.
1
defines a linear constraint system (LCS) over the Boolean ring
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, which admits a quantum solution in terms of two-qubit Pauli operators. To date, no generalisation of the Mermin-Peres square to qudit systems, more generally no finite-dimensional quantum solution to a LCS over
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
for
d
odd, has been found.
12
12

12

Note that possibly infinite-dimensionsal solutions to LCS over
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
for arbitrary
d
exist by
[
31
]
.
It has been shown that no such examples can be constructed within the Heisenberg-Weyl group, equivalently the qudit Pauli group
[
27
]
. Our main theorem, Thm.
2
, generalises this result beyond Pauli operators to the diagonal Clifford hierarchy, lending support to the conjecture that no quantum solutions exist in all of
SU(d)
.

The connection with the Clifford hierarchy further suggests an avenue either towards generalisations of our result or towards examples of contextual LCS in odd prime dimension. Recall that
K_{Q}(p)
contains the diagonal Clifford hierarchy (up to phase). However,
K_{Q}(p)
does not contain general Clifford operators. Still, for
p=3
every single-qutrit Clifford operator
M
is semi-Clifford, i.e.,

with

and

diagonal
[
17
;
33
]
. Moreover, this result is conjectured to be true for all prime dimensions
[
8
]
.
This hints at a generalisation of our construction to tensor products of groups generated from (semi-)Clifford operators, and, to analyse the existence of quantum solutions to LCS in groups generated from gate sets in quantum computation more generally.

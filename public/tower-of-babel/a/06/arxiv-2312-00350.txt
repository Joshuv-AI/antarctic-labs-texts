The colored Jones polynomial of the figure-eight knot and an SL ⁡ ( 2 , ℝ ) \rm{SL}(2;\mathbb{R}) -representation Thanks: The author is supported by JSPS KAKENHI Grant Numbers JP20K03601, JP22H01117, JP20K03931.
Hitoshi Murakami

Abstract
. We study the asymptotic behavior of the N N -dimensional colored Jones polynomial of the figure-eight knot, evaluated at exp ⁡ ( ( u + 2 ​ p ​ π ​ − 1 ) / N ) \exp\bigl((u+2p\pi\sqrt{-1})/N\bigr) as N N tends to infinity, where u > arccosh ⁡ ( 3 / 2 ) u>\operatorname{arccosh}(3/2) is a real number and p ≥ 1 p\geq 1 is an integer. It turns out that it corresponds to an SL ⁡ ( 2 , ℝ ) \rm{SL}(2;\mathbb{R}) representation of the fundamental group of the knot complement. Moreover, it defines the adjoint Reidemeister torsion and the Chern–Simons invariant associated with the representation.

1.
Introduction

For a link
L
in the three-sphere
S^{3}
and an integer
N\geq 2
, let
J_{N}(L;q)\in\mathbb{Z}[q^{1/2},q^{-1/2}]
be the colored Jones polynomial associated with the
N
-dimensional irreducible representation of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(2;\mathbb{C})
.
We normalize it so that
J_{N}(U;q)=1
for the unknot
U
, and that
J_{2}(L;q)
is (a version of) the original Jones polynomial
[
14
]
satisfying the following skein relation:

qJ_{2}\left(\raisebox{-8.53581pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{positive}};q\right)-q^{-1}J_{2}\left(\raisebox{-8.53581pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{negative}};q\right)=\left(q^{1/2}-q^{-1/2}\right)J_{2}\left(\raisebox{-8.53581pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{null}};q\right).

In
[
15
,
16
]
, R. Kashaev introduced a link invariant
\langle L\rangle_{N}\in\mathbb{C}
by using the cyclic quantum dilogarithm.
He conjectured that his invariant
\langle L\rangle_{N}
grows exponentially with growth rate proportional to the hyperbolic volume of
S^{3}\setminus{L}
provided that
L
is hyperbolic, that is, the complement
S^{3}\setminus{L}
has a complete hyperbolic structure with finite volume
[
17
]
.

In
[
28
]
, J. Murakami and the author proved that Kashaev’s invariant coincides with the
N
-dimensional colored Jones polynomial evaluated at
q=e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/N}
.
So Kashaev’s conjecture turns out to be

(1.1)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log\left|J_{N}(L;e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/N})\right|}{N}=\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(L)}{2\pi}

for a hyperbolic link
L
, where
\operatorname{Vol}(L)
is the hyperbolic volume of
S^{3}\setminus{L}
.
We also generalized it to any knot taking the simplicial volume (Gromov’s invariant)
[
8
]
(see also
[
37
, Chapter 6]
) into account.
Here the simplicial volume
\|K\|
for a knot in
S^{3}
is known to be the sum of the hyperbolic volumes of the hyperbolic pieces in the knot complement.

Conjecture 1.1

(Volume Conjecture,
[
28
, Conjecture 5.1]
)
.

For any knot
K\subset S^{3}
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log\left|J_{N}(K;e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/N})\right|}{N}=\frac{\|K\|}{2\pi}.

Remark 1.2
.

Kashaev’s conjecture is for
any hyperbolic link
and the volume conjecture is for
any knot
, not necessarily hyperbolic.

Conjecture 1.5

Since the colored Jones polynomial has

Letting
(\mu,\lambda)
be the standard meridian/longitude pair of

\rho_{u}\colon\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus{K})\to\rm{SL}(2;\mathbb{C})

\rho_{u}(\mu):=\begin{pmatrix}e^{u/2}&\ast\\
0&e^{-u/2}\end{pmatrix},\quad\rho_{u}(\lambda):=\begin{pmatrix}-e^{-v(u)/2}&\ast\\
0&-e^{-v(u)/2}\end{pmatrix}.

Here we choose the longitude so that the linking number between
\lambda

K
is zero.
Then one can define the homological adjoint Reidemeister torsion of
\rho_{u}
associated with
\mu
, which we denote by
T_{K}(\rho_{u})
, and the
\mathrm{PSL}(2;\mathbb{C})
Chern–Simons invariant of
\rho_{u}
associated with
\bigl(u,v(u)\bigr)
, which we denote by
\operatorname{CS}_{K}(\rho_{u};u,v(u))
.
If
K
is hyperbolic and
\rho_{0}
is the holonomy representation, then we have
\operatorname{CS}_{K}(\rho_{0};0,0)=\sqrt{-1}\operatorname{Vol}(K)-\operatorname{CS}^{\rm{SO}(3)}(K)
.

Conjecture 1.6

(Parametrization of the volume conjecture)
.

For a hyperbolic knot
K
, there exists a neighborhood
U
of
0
in
\mathbb{C}
such that for
u\in U\setminus{\pi\sqrt{-1}\mathbb{Q}}

(1.3)

J_{N}\left(K;e^{(2\pi\sqrt{-1}+u)/N}\right)\\
\underset{N\to\infty}{\sim}\frac{\sqrt{-\pi}}{2\sinh(u/2)}T_{K}(\rho_{u})^{-1/2}\left(\frac{N}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}+u}\right)^{1/2}\exp\left(\frac{S_{K}(u)}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}+u}N\right),

T_{K}(\rho_{u})
is the adjoint Reidemeister torsion, and
\operatorname{CS}_{u,v(u)}(\rho_{u}):=S_{K}(u)-u\pi\sqrt{-1}-uv(u)/4

Moreover we have
v(u)=2\frac{d}{d\,u}S_{K}(u)-2\pi\sqrt{-1}

Theorem 1.7

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{Li}_{2}\left(e^{-u-\varphi(u)}\right)-\operatorname{Li}_{2}\left(e^{-u+\varphi(u)}\right)+u\bigl(\varphi(u)+2\pi\sqrt{-1}\bigr),

Remark 1.9
.

In Theorem

and

are positive.
Since

is amphicheiral, that is,

is equivalent to its mirror image, we have

Therefore if

Therefore, in the following we also assume that both

Lemma 2.2

The functions

Lemma 3.4
.

The function
\varphi(u)

(
u>\kappa
)
is real analytic and satisfies the following properties.

(i)

0<u-\kappa<\varphi(u)<u
,

(ii)

\varphi(u)
is strictly monotonically increasing,

(iii)

u-\varphi(u)
is strictly monotonically decreasing with
\lim_{u\to\infty}\bigl(u-\varphi(u)\bigr)=0
,

(iv)

\varphi(u)/u
is strictly monotonically increasing,

(v)

\varphi^{\prime}(u)
is strictly monotonically decreasing with
\lim_{u\to\kappa}\varphi^{\prime}(u)=\infty
and
\lim_{u\to\infty}\varphi^{\prime}(u)=1
,

(vi)

e^{u}-e^{\varphi(u)}
is strictly monotonically decreasing,

(vii)

\lim_{u\to\infty}\left(e^{u}-e^{\varphi(u)}\right)=1
.

Proof.

Since
u>\kappa=\operatorname{arccosh}(3/2)
, it is clear from the definition (
3.10
) that
\varphi(u)
is real analytic.

(i)

Since we have
\cosh\varphi(u)=\cosh{u}-1/2<\cosh{u}
and
\operatorname{arccosh}
is monotonically increasing, the inequality
\varphi(u)<u
follows.
So we have
\varphi^{\prime}(u)=\frac{\sinh{u}}{\sinh\varphi(u)}>1
, which implies that the function
\varphi(u)-u+\kappa
is strictly increasing and vanishes when
u=\kappa
.
It follows that
\varphi(u)-u+\kappa>0
for
u>\kappa
.

(ii)

This follows since
\varphi^{\prime}(u)=\frac{\sinh{u}}{\sinh\varphi(u)}>1
.

(iii)

Since
\frac{d}{d\,u}\bigl(u-\varphi(u)\bigr)=1-\frac{\sinh{u}}{\sinh\varphi(u)}<0
, we conclude that
u-\varphi(u)
is strictly monotonically decreasing.
The second statement follows since we have

\lim_{u\to\infty}e^{u-\varphi(u)}=\lim_{u\to\infty}\frac{\cosh(u)}{\cosh\varphi(u)}=\lim_{u\to\infty}\frac{\cosh(u)}{\cosh(u)-1/2}=1,

where the first equality holds because
\varphi(u)\to\infty
as
u\to\infty
from (i).

(iv)

The derivative of
\varphi(u)/u
equals
\frac{u\varphi^{\prime}(u)-\varphi(u)}{u^{2}}
, which is greater than
\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(u)-1}{u}
from (i).
Since
\varphi^{\prime}(u)>1
from the proof of (ii), we conclude that
\varphi(u)/u
is increasing.

(v)

We show that
\varphi^{\prime\prime}(u)<0
for
u>\kappa
.
Since
\varphi^{\prime}(u)=\frac{\sinh{u}}{\sinh\varphi(u)}
and
\cosh\varphi(u)=\cosh{u}-1/2
, we have

\begin{split}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(u)=&\frac{\cosh{u}\sinh\varphi(u)-\varphi^{\prime}(u)\sinh{u}\cosh\varphi(u)}{\sinh^{2}\varphi(u)}\\
=&\frac{\cosh{u}\sinh^{2}\varphi(u)-\sinh^{2}{u}\cosh\varphi(u)}{\sinh^{3}\varphi(u)}\\
=&\frac{\cosh{u}\bigl((\cosh{u}-1/2)^{2}-1\bigr)-\sinh^{2}{u}\bigr(\cosh{u}-1/2\bigr)}{\sinh^{3}\varphi(u)}\\
=&\frac{-2\cosh^{2}{u}+\cosh{u}-2}{4\sinh^{3}\varphi(u)}\\
=&\frac{-2\bigl(\cosh{u}-1/4\bigr)^{2}-15/8}{4\sinh^{3}\varphi(u)}<0.\end{split}

Since
\varphi(\kappa)=0
, we have
\lim_{u\to\kappa}\varphi^{\prime}(u)=\infty
.
We also have
\lim_{u\to\infty}\varphi^{\prime}(u)=\lim_{u\to\infty}\frac{e^{u}-e^{-u}}{e^{\varphi(u)}-e^{-\varphi(u)}}=\lim_{u\to\infty}e^{u-\varphi(u)}=1
from (iii).

(vi)

This follows from
\frac{d}{d\,u}\left(e^{u}-e^{\varphi(u)}\right)=e^{u}-\frac{\sinh{u}}{\sinh\varphi(u)}e^{\varphi(u)}<0
.

(vii)

Since
2\cosh{u}-2\cosh\varphi(u)=1
, we have
e^{u}-e^{\varphi(u)}+e^{-u}-e^{-\varphi(u)}=1
.
Letting
u\to\infty
, we obtain the desired limit because
\lim_{u\to\infty}e^{-\varphi(u)}=0
from (i).

\begin{split}&\left|\sum_{m/p<k/N<(m+1)/p}\exp\left(N\times g_{N,m}\left(\frac{2k+1}{2N}\right)\right)\right|\\
\leq&\sum_{m/p<k/N<(m+1)/p}\exp\left(N\times\operatorname{Re}g_{N,m}\left(\frac{2k+1}{2N}\right)\right)\\
<&\sum_{m/p<k/N<(m+1)/p}\exp\left(N\left(\operatorname{Re}G_{m}\left(\frac{2k+1}{2N}\right)+\varepsilon\right)\right).\end{split}

proving the proposition.

Lemma 3.15
.

If

\begin{split}\operatorname{Re}{F(\sigma_{0})}=&\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{S_{\mathscr{E}}(u)}{\xi}\right)\\

We will show that

(3.24)

L_{p}(u):=\operatorname{Li}_{2}\left(e^{-u-\varphi(u)}\right)-\operatorname{Li}_{2}\left(e^{-u+\varphi(u)}\right)+u\varphi(u)+4p\pi^{2}

is positive.

The derivative becomes

(3.25)

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{d\,u}L_{p}(u)\\
=&\bigl(1+\varphi^{\prime}(u)\bigr)\log\left(1-e^{-u-\varphi(u)}\right)+\bigl(-1+\varphi^{\prime}(u)\bigr)\log\left(1-e^{-u+\varphi(u)}\right)\\
&+\varphi(u)+u\varphi^{\prime}(u)\\
=&\log\left(\frac{1-e^{-u-\varphi(u)}}{1-e^{-u+\varphi(u)}}\right)+\varphi^{\prime}(u)\left(\log\bigl(2\cosh{u}-2\cosh\varphi(u)\bigr)-u\right)+\varphi(u)+u\varphi^{\prime}(u)\\

=&\log\left(\frac{\left(e^{\varphi(u)}-e^{-u}\right)\left(1-e^{u+\varphi(u)}\right)}{\left(1-e^{-u+\varphi(u)}\right)\left(1-e^{u+\varphi(u)}\right)}\right)\\

=&\log\Bigl(2\cosh\bigl(u+\varphi(u)\bigr)-2\Bigr)>0.\end{split}

Thus, the function

is monotonically increasing.

Since the function
\sinh\left(\frac{\xi}{2}(1-x)\right)\sinh\left(\frac{\xi}{2}(1+x)\right)
of
x\in\mathbb{R}
vanishes when
x=1
, we see that there exists
0<\delta_{2}<1/p
such that its absolute value is less than
1/4
if
1-\delta_{2}<x<1
.
This means that if we define
h_{N}(k):=\prod_{l=1}^{k}\left(4\sinh\left(\frac{\xi}{2}(1-l/N)\right)\sinh\left(\frac{\xi}{2}(1+l/N)\right)\right)
, then
\left|h_{N}(k)\right|
is decreasing with respect to
k
when
1-\delta_{2}<k/N<1
.
From
[
27
, (8.3)]
, we have

h_{N}(k)=\frac{1-e^{-4pN\pi^{2}/\xi}}{2\sinh(u/2)}\beta_{p,p-1}\exp\left(Ng_{N,p-1}\left(\frac{2k+1}{2N}\right)\right)

for
1-1/p<k/N<1
.
It follows that

\operatorname{Re}g_{N,p-1}\left(\frac{2k+1}{2N}\right)=\frac{1}{N}\log\left|\frac{2\sinh(u/2)h_{N}(k)}{\left(1-e^{-4pN\pi^{2}/\xi}\right)\beta_{p,p-1}}\right|

is also decreasing with respect to
k
whenever
1-\delta_{2}<k/N<1
.

If we put
\tilde{\delta}:=\min\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\}
, then for
k
with
1-\tilde{\delta}<k/N<1
, we can choose
k^{\prime}<k
such that
1-\delta_{1}<\frac{2k^{\prime}+1}{2N}<1-\min\{\nu/p,2\nu\pi/u\}
if
N
is sufficiently large.
So from (
5.6
) we finally conclude that

\operatorname{Re}g_{N,p-1}\left(\frac{2k+1}{2N}\right)<\operatorname{Re}g_{N,p-1}\left(\frac{2k^{\prime}+1}{2N}\right)<\operatorname{Re}G_{p-1}(\sigma_{p-1})-\varepsilon,

where the first inequality follows since
\operatorname{Re}g_{N,p-1}\left(\frac{2k+1}{N}\right)
is decreasing.
∎

6.2.
Chern–Simons invariant

For a knot
K\subset S^{3}
, put
M:=S^{3}\setminus{N(K)}
, where
N(K)
is the open tubular neighborhood of
K
.
Note that the boundary
\partial{M}
of

is a torus.
Then, following
[
19
, P. 543]
, the
\mathrm{PSL}(2;\mathbb{C})
Chern–Simons function
\operatorname{cs}_{K}
is defined as a map from the
\mathrm{PSL}(2;\mathbb{C})
character variety
X(M)
of
M
to
E(M):=\left[\operatorname{Hom}\bigl(\pi_{1}(\partial{M}),\mathbb{C}\bigr)\times\mathbb{C}^{\ast}\right]/\sim
, where
\sim
is the equivalence relation generated by

\begin{split}(\alpha,\beta;z)\sim(\alpha+1/2,\beta;ze^{-4\pi\sqrt{-1}\beta})\sim(\alpha,\beta+1/2;ze^{4\pi\sqrt{-1}\alpha})\sim(-\alpha,-\beta;z).\end{split}

Here we fix a basis
(\mu^{\ast},\lambda^{\ast})
of
\operatorname{Hom}\bigl(\pi_{1}(\partial{M}),\mathbb{C}\bigr)\cong\mathbb{C}^{2}
with
(\mu,\lambda)
the standard meridian/longitude pair.
We denote by
[\alpha,\beta;z]
the equivalence class of
(\alpha,\beta;z)\in\operatorname{Hom}\bigl(\pi_{1}(\partial{M}),\mathbb{C}\bigr)\times\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
.
Let
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(M)\to\rm{SL}(2;\mathbb{C})
be a representation sending the meridian
\mu
to
\begin{pmatrix}e^{m/2}&\ast\\
0&e^{-m/2}\end{pmatrix}
and the preferred longitude
\lambda
to
\begin{pmatrix}e^{l/2}&\ast\\
0&e^{-l/2}\end{pmatrix}
.
Then, the Chern–Simons invariant
\operatorname{CS}_{K}(\rho;m,l)\in\mathbb{C}\pmod{\pi^{2}\mathbb{Z}}
is defined by the following equation:

\operatorname{cs}_{K}([\rho])=\left[\frac{m}{4\pi\sqrt{-1}},\frac{l}{4\pi\sqrt{-1}};\exp\left(\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{-1}}\operatorname{CS}_{K}(\rho;m,l)\right)\right],

where
[\rho]\in X(M)
denotes the equivalence class.

In
[
30
, Theorem 1.5]
, we proved that
\operatorname{CS}_{\mathscr{E}}\bigl(\rho_{u};u,\eta\bigr)=\check{S}_{\mathscr{E}}(u)-u\eta/4
, where
\check{S}_{\mathscr{E}}(u):=S_{\mathscr{E}}(u)-2\pi\sqrt{-1}u
and
\eta=v(u)-2\pi\sqrt{-1}
.
Since the Chern–Simons function
\operatorname{cs}_{\mathscr{E}}([\rho_{u}])
is given by

\begin{split}&\left[\frac{u}{4\pi\sqrt{-1}},\frac{\eta}{4\pi\sqrt{-1}};\exp\left(\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{-1}}\operatorname{CS}_{\mathscr{E}}(\rho_{u};u,\eta)\right)\right]\\
=&\left[\frac{u}{4\pi\sqrt{-1}},\frac{v(u)-2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{4\pi\sqrt{-1}};\exp\left(\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{-1}}\operatorname{CS}_{\mathscr{E}}(\rho_{u};u,\eta)\right)\right]\\
=&\left[\frac{u}{4\pi\sqrt{-1}},\frac{v(u)}{4\pi\sqrt{-1}};\exp\left(\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{-1}}\operatorname{CS}_{\mathscr{E}}(\rho_{u};u,\eta)\right)\times\exp\left(4\pi\sqrt{-1}\times\frac{u}{4\pi\sqrt{-1}}\right)\right]\\
=&\left[\frac{u}{4\pi\sqrt{-1}},\frac{v(u)}{4\pi\sqrt{-1}};\exp\left(\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{-1}}\left(\operatorname{CS}_{\mathscr{E}}(\rho_{u};u,\eta)+\frac{1}{2}\pi\sqrt{-1}u\right)\right)\right],\end{split}

we have

(6.2)

\begin{split}&\operatorname{CS}_{\mathscr{E}}\bigl(\rho_{u};u,v(u)\bigr)\\
=&\operatorname{CS}_{\mathscr{E}}(\rho_{u};u,\eta)+\frac{1}{2}\pi\sqrt{-1}u\\
=&S_{\mathscr{E}}(u)-2\pi\sqrt{-1}u-\frac{1}{4}u\bigl(v(u)-2\pi\sqrt{-1}\bigr)+\frac{1}{2}\pi\sqrt{-1}u\\
=&S_{\mathscr{E}}(u)-\pi\sqrt{-1}u-\frac{1}{4}uv(u).\end{split}

This gives a topological interpretation of the term

in (

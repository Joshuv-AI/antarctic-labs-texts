Generic modules for the category of filtered by standard modules
R. Bautista, E. Pérez, L. Salmerón

Abstract
Here we show that, given a finite homological system ( 𝒫 , ≤ , { Δ u } u ∈ 𝒫 ) ({\cal P},\leq,\{\Delta_{u}\}_{u\in{\cal P}}) for a finite-dimensional algebra Λ \Lambda over an algebraically closed field, the category ℱ ⁡ ( Δ ) {\cal F}(\Delta) of Δ \Delta -filtered modules is tame if and only if, for any d ∈ ℕ d\in\mathbb{N} , there are only finitely many isomorphism classes of generic Λ \Lambda -modules adapted to ℱ ⁡ ( Δ ) {\cal F}(\Delta) with endolength d d . We study the relationship between these generic modules and one-parameter families of indecomposables in ℱ ⁡ ( Δ ) {\cal F}(\Delta) . This study applies in particular to the category of filtered by standard modules for standardly stratified algebras.

Introduction

Denote by
k
a fixed field
and let
\Lambda
be a
finite-dimensional
k
-algebra.
Given a
\Lambda
-module
G
, recall that by definition the

endolength
of
G
is its length as a right

{\rm End}_{\Lambda}(M)^{op}
-module. The
\Lambda
-module
G
is called

generic
if it is indecomposable, of infinite length
as a
\Lambda
-module, but with finite endolength. The
algebra
\Lambda
is called
generically tame
if, for
each
d\in\mathbb{N}
, there is only a finite number of
isoclasses of generic
\Lambda
-modules with endolength
d
.
This notion was introduced by W. W. Crawley-Boevey in

[
15
]
, where he proved that, in case
k
is
algebraically closed, the class of generically tame algebras
coincides
with the class of tame algebras. Recall that an algebra

\Lambda
, over an algebraically closed field
k
, is tame
if the pairwise
non-isomorphic indecomposable modules in each dimension can
be parametrized by a finite number of one-parameter
families. Thus, the
notion of generic tameness generalizes the notion of
tameness for arbitrary fields.

In order to be more precise, we recall some definitions
from
[
6
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

For any
k
-algebra
R
and any

R
-module
M\in R\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
we write
E_{M}={\rm End}_{R}(M)^{op}
. Any
M\in R\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
is naturally a
right
E_{M}
-module. By definition, the
endolength
of

M
, denoted by
\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M)
, is its
length as an
E_{M}
-module. We say that
M
is

endofinite
if it has finite endolength. The

R
-module
M
is called
pregeneric
iff it is an
endofinite indecomposable with infinite dimension over
k
.

If
R
is a finite-dimensional
k
-algebra, the notion of
pregeneric
R
-module coincides with the usual notion of
generic
R
-module.

In this article we study generic
\Lambda
-modules adapted to the category of
\Delta
-filtered modules
{\cal F}(\Delta)
in the case where
\Lambda
is a finite-dimensional algebra, over an algebraically closed field, and
({\cal P},\leq,\{\Delta_{u}\}_{u\in{\cal P}})
is a homological system. In the following, we recall the basic terminology from
[
22
]
and
[
9
]
.

A
preordered set

({\cal P},\leq)
is a non-empty set
{\cal P}
together with a reflexive transitive relation
\leq
. We have the equivalence relation in
{\cal P}
defined by
u\sim v
iff
u\leq v
and
v\leq u
. The set
\overline{\cal P}:={\cal P}/\sim
of equivalences classes is partially ordered by the relation
\overline{u}\leq\overline{v}
iff
u\leq v
, where
\overline{u}
and
\overline{v}
denote the equivalence classes of
u
and
v
, respectively.

Definition 1.2
.

Given a finite-dimensional
k
-algebra
\Lambda
, a
(finite) homological system
({\cal P},\leq,\{\Delta_{u}\}_{u\in{\cal P}})
for
\Lambda
consists of
a finite preordered set
({\cal P},\leq)
and
a family of pairwise non-isomorphic indecomposable finite-dimensional
\Lambda
-modules
\{\Delta_{u}\}_{u\in{\cal P}}
satisfying the following two conditions:

1.

{\rm Hom}_{\Lambda}(\Delta_{u},\Delta_{v})\not=0
implies
u\leq v
;

2.

{\rm Ext}^{1}_{\Lambda}(\Delta_{u},\Delta_{v})\not=0
implies
u\leq v
and
u\not\sim v
.

Given such an homological system, we set
\Delta:=\{\Delta_{u}\mid u\in{\cal P}\}
and denote by
{\cal F}(\Delta)
the full subcategory of
\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
consisting of the trivial module and all those
M\in\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
which admit a
\Delta
-filtration
, that is a filtration of submodules

0=M_{t}\subseteq M_{t-1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq M_{1}\subseteq M_{0}=M

such that
M_{j}/M_{j+1}
is isomorphic to some module in
\Delta
, for each
j\in[0,t-1]
.

A finite homological system
{\cal H}=({\cal P},\leq,\{\Delta_{u}\}_{u\in{\cal P}})
for a finite-dimensional
k
-algebra
\Lambda
is called
admissible
if
\Lambda\in{\cal F}(\Delta)
and the number of isoclasses of indecomposable projective
\Lambda
-modules coincides with the cardinality of
{\cal P}
.
In this case, the modules in
\Delta
are determined up to isomorphism and are known as
the standard modules
;
\Lambda
is called a
pre-standardly stratified algebra
, see
[
9
]
(2.11).

A pre-standardly stratified algebra
\Lambda
, with preordered index set
({\cal P},\leq)
, is called
standardly stratified
if
({\cal P},\leq)
is a partial order. A standardly stratified algebra
\Lambda
, equipped with the poset
({\cal P},\leq)
, is
quasi-hereditary
iff
{\rm End}_{\Lambda}(\Delta_{u})\cong k
, for each
u\in{\cal P}
.

Definition 1.3
.

Given a finite-dimensional
k
-algebra
\Lambda
and a finite homological system
({\cal P},\leq,\{\Delta_{u}\}_{u\in{\cal P}})
we will also consider the full subcategory
\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta)
of
\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
with objects the
\Lambda
-modules
M
which admit a filtration

0=M_{\ell}\subseteq M_{\ell-1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq M_{1}\subseteq M_{0}=M

such that
M_{j}/M_{j+1}
is isomorphic to a (possibly infinite) direct sum of modules in the family
\{\Delta_{u}\}_{u\in{\cal P}}
, for all
j\in[0,\ell-1]
.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
({\cal P},\leq,\{\Delta_{u}\}_{u\in{\cal P}})
be a homological system for a finite-dimensional algebra
\Lambda
.
Then,

1.

A
\Lambda
-module
G
is called a
generic module for
{\cal F}(\Delta)
iff
G
is an endofinite indecomposable module with infinite length as a
\Lambda
-module and
G\in\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta)
.

2.

The category
{\cal F}(\Delta)
is called
generically tame
iff, for each
d\in\mathbb{N}
, there is only a finite number of non-isomorphic generic modules for
{\cal F}(\Delta)
with endolength
d
.

In 2021, we uploaded to arXiv a preprint with the results for
{\cal F}(\Delta)
of this article, in the particular case of the homological system of standard modules over a quasi-hereditary algebra.
This article is a revised version of that one, where some of the arguments are simplified and the results are generalized for
arbitrary homological systems over finite-dimensional algebras.
The main results of this article are the following two statements.

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume that the ground field
k
is algebraically closed and that
\Lambda
is a finite-dimensional algebra.
Let
({\cal P},\leq,\{\Delta_{u}\}_{u\in{\cal P}})
be any homological system for
\Lambda
. Then, if
{\cal F}(\Delta)
is not wild and
G
is a generic
\Lambda
-module for
{\cal F}(\Delta)
, the following statements hold.

1.

There is a rational algebra
\Gamma_{G}
and a
\Lambda\hbox{-}\Gamma_{G}
-bimodule
Z_{G}
such that as a right
\Gamma_{G}
-module
Z_{G}
is free of rank equal to the endolength of
G
. If
Q_{G}
is the field of fractions of
\Gamma_{G}
, then
G\cong Z_{G}\otimes_{\Gamma_{G}}Q_{G}
.

2.

The functor
Z_{G}\otimes_{\Gamma_{G}}-:\Gamma_{G}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta)
preserves isoclasses and indecomposability.

3.

The functor
Z_{G}\otimes_{\Gamma_{G}}-:\Gamma_{G}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\cal F}(\Delta)
preserves Auslander-Reiten sequences.

In the following, when we say that almost all objects in a class
{\cal C}
of modules satisfy some property, we mean that every object in this class has this property, with the exception of those lying in a finite union of isoclasses in
{\cal C}
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Assume that the ground field
k
is algebraically closed and that
\Lambda
is a finite-dimensional algebra.
Let
({\cal P},\leq,\{\Delta_{u}\}_{u\in{\cal P}})
be any homological system for
\Lambda
. Then,
{\cal F}(\Delta)
is generically tame iff it is tame.

Moreover, if
{\cal F}(\Delta)
is generically tame, for any
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
almost every indecomposable
\Lambda
-module

with
\dim_{k}M\leq d
is of the form
M\cong Z_{G}\otimes_{\Gamma_{G}}N
, for some generic
\Lambda
-module
G
for
{\cal F}(\Delta)
with
\hbox{\rm endol}\,(G)\leq d
and some indecomposable
N\in\Gamma_{G}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
, with the notation of (
1.5
).

The proof of the preceding results rely, in case the given homological system is admissible, on the construction of a weak ditalgebra
\underline{\cal A}(\Delta)
associated to the

, in such a way that

is generically tame iff
\underline{\cal A}(\Delta)
is pregenerically tame in the sense of the following definition. The construction of
\underline{\cal A}(\Delta)
in the case of quasi-hereditary algebras is due to Koenig, Külshammer, and

[
21
]
. The construction for a general pre-standardly stratified algebra can be found in
[

]
. The proofs of our main results in the case of a general homological system are reduced to the admissible case using a construction due to Mendoza, Sáenz, and Xi, which associates to a general homological system
({\cal P},\leq,\{\Theta_{v}\}_{v\in{\cal P}})
a pre-standardly stratified algebra with an admissible homological system
({\cal P},\leq,\{\Delta_{v}\}_{v\in{\cal P}})
such that
{\cal F}(\Theta)
and

are equivalent as exact categories, see
[
22
]
and
[
8
]
§11.

We will assume some familiarity with the language introduced
in the first sections of

\underline{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}

(resp.
\underline{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
) denotes the category of modules
(resp. finite-dimensional modules) over an interlaced weak ditalgebra

Definition 1.7
.

Let
\underline{\cal A}=({\cal A},I)
be an interlaced weak ditalgebra, with layer
(R,W)
, see

8
]
(4.1). Given

-bimodule, where

A module

The preceding notion extends the one introduced in
[

]
§2 for layered ditalgebras. As remarked there,
if

The interlaced weak ditalgebra
\underline{\cal A}(\Delta)
associated to the category
{\cal F}(\Delta)

{\cal P}
-oriented interlaced weak ditalgebra, which means that it has the special geometrical characteristics related to the preordered set

explained below.

Definition 1.8
.

A
biquiver

\mathbb{B}
is a triple

\mathbb{B}=({\cal P},\mathbb{B}_{0},\mathbb{B}_{1})
formed by finite sets.
The elements of
{\cal P}

are called
points
, the elements of
\mathbb{B}_{0}
are
full arrows
, and the elements of

\mathbb{B}_{1}
are
dashed arrows
. Each
arrow

\alpha\in\mathbb{B}_{0}\cup\mathbb{B}_{1}
has a
starting point

s(\alpha)\in{\cal P}
and a
terminal point

t(\alpha)\in{\cal P}
.

Given a biquiver
\mathbb{B}
with
n
points, we consider the

k
-algebra
R
defined as the product

R
of
n
copies of the ground field
k
. Thus the unit of
R
is a sum of primitive orthogonal idempotents
1=\sum_{u\in{\cal P}}e_{u}
, where
e_{u}
corresponds to the unit of the
u
-copy of the ground field in the product
R
.
Then, we consider vector spaces
W_{0}
and
W_{1}
with
basis

\mathbb{B}_{0}
and
\mathbb{B}_{1}
, respectively, and set
W:=W_{0}\oplus W_{1}
. If we define

e_{v}\alpha e_{u}=\delta_{u,s(\alpha)}\delta_{t(\alpha),v}\alpha
, we get a
natural structure of
R
-
R
-bimodule
on the space
W
, and
W=W_{0}\oplus W_{1}
is an
R
-
R
-bimodule decomposition.
We have the graded tensor algebra
T=T_{R}(W)
, with
[T]_{0}=T_{R}(W_{0})
and

[T]_{1}=[T]_{0}W_{1}[T]_{0}
, which is called
the tensor algebra of the
biquiver
\mathbb{B}
.

The algebra
T
can be identified with the
path algebra
k\mathbb{B}
of
the biquiver
\mathbb{B}
, with underlying vector space with basis the set of
paths (of any kind of arrows) of
\mathbb{B}
(including one trivial path for
each point
u\in{\cal P}
). Each primitive idempotent
e_{u}
of
R
is
identified with the trivial path at the point
u
.
The homogeneous elements
[k\mathbb{B}]_{d}
of
degree

d
are the linear combinations of paths containing exactly
d
dashed arrows.

Definition 1.9
.

Let
{\cal P}=({\cal P},\leq)
be a finite preordered set and
\mathbb{B}
a biquiver with set of points
{\cal P}
, then we say that
\mathbb{B}
is
{\cal P}
-oriented
iff

1.

Whenever there is a dashed arrow from
u
to
v
, we have
u\leq v
;

2.

Whenever there is a solid arrow from
u
to
v
, we have

\overline{u}<\overline{v}
, .

A weak ditalgebra
{\cal A}=(T,\delta)
will be called a
{\cal P}
-oriented weak ditalgebra
iff its underlying tensor algebra

is the tensor algebra of a
{\cal P}
-oriented biquiver.

A biquiver
\mathbb{B}
is called
directed
iff it admits no oriented
(non-trivial) cycle (composed by any kind of arrows). In a
{\cal P}
-oriented biquiver
\mathbb{B}
, the only (non-trivial) oriented cycles (composed of any kind of arrows) consist of dashed arrows between points in the same
\sim

\mathbb{B}
is not necessarily directed.

Proposition 2.1

(deletion of idempotents)
.

Suppose that

(R,W)

Assume that

\phi_{r}:W_{r}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}eW_{r}e

Set

\phi:T\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}T^{d}

Then, there is a

\underline{\cal A}^{d}=({\cal A}^{d},I^{d})

, and

(regularization)

.
Then, we have isomorphisms

\begin{matrix}{\rm Hom}_{S}(\underline{A},\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_{i})\times{\rm Hom}_{\underline{A}\hbox{-}\underline{A}}(\underline{V},{\rm Hom}_{k}(\underline{A},\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_{i}))\\
\downarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\theta_{1}\times\theta_{2*}$}}$\hss}\\
\bigoplus_{i\in I}{\rm Hom}_{S}(\underline{A},M_{i})\times{\rm Hom}_{\underline{A}\hbox{-}\underline{A}}(\underline{V},\bigoplus_{i\in I}{\rm Hom}_{k}(\underline{A},M_{i}))\\
\downarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle id\times\theta_{3}$}}$\hss}\\
\bigoplus_{i\in I}{\rm Hom}_{S}(\underline{A},M_{i})\times\bigoplus_{i\in I}{\rm Hom}_{\underline{A}\hbox{-}\underline{A}}(\underline{V},{\rm Hom}_{k}(\underline{A},M_{i})).\\
\end{matrix}

Let us show that the composition
\Theta
of these isomorphisms restricts to an isomorphism as in the statement of this lemma. So we take
f\in{\rm Hom}_{\underline{\cal A}}(\underline{A},\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_{i})
and let us show that
\Theta(f)\in\bigoplus_{i\in I}{\rm Hom}_{\underline{\cal A}}(\underline{A},M_{i})
. We have

\begin{matrix}\Theta(f)=\Theta(f^{0},f^{1})&=&(\theta_{1}(f^{0}),\theta_{3}(\theta_{2*}(f^{1})))\hfill\\
&=&(\sum_{i}\pi_{i}f^{0},\sum_{j\in I}p_{j}\theta_{2*}(f^{1}))\hfill\\
&=&(\sum_{i}\pi_{i}f^{0},\sum_{j}p_{j}\theta_{2}f^{1}),\hfill\\
\end{matrix}

where each
p_{j}:\bigoplus_{i\in I}{\rm Hom}_{k}(\underline{A},M_{i})\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\rm Hom}_{k}(\underline{A},M_{j})
is the canonical projection. Thus, for
v\in\underline{V}
, we have
\sum_{j}\sum_{i}p_{j}\pi_{i}f^{1}(v)=\sum_{i}\pi_{i}f^{1}(v)
. Since
f
is a morphism in
\underline{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
, for
a,m\in\underline{A}
, we have

af^{0}(m)=f^{0}(am)+f^{1}(\delta(a))[m]
.
Then, we have
\sum_{i}\pi_{i}af^{0}(m)=\sum_{i}\pi_{i}f^{0}(am)+\sum_{i}\pi_{i}f^{1}(\delta(a))[m]
. Since each
\pi_{i}
is a morphism of
\underline{A}
-modules, we get

\sum_{i}a\pi_{i}f^{0}(m)=\sum_{i}\pi_{i}f^{0}(am)+\sum_{i}\pi_{i}f^{1}(\delta(a))[m]
or, equivalently,

a\Theta(f)^{0}[m]=\Theta(f)^{0}(am)+\Theta(f)^{1}(\delta(a))[m].

So,
\Theta:{\rm Hom}_{\underline{\cal A}}(\underline{A},\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_{i})\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\bigoplus_{i\in I}{\rm Hom}_{\underline{\cal A}}(\underline{A},M_{i})
is a natural isomorphism. Moreover, it is easy to see that indeed we have
\Theta(f)=\sum_{i\in I}\overline{\pi}_{i}f
.
∎

Proposition 6.2

\underline{A}

The preceding filtration gives rise to exact sequences

\begin{matrix}0\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}M^{\prime}_{\ell}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}M^{\prime}_{\ell-1}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}L^{\prime}_{\ell-1}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}0\\

\begin{matrix}0\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\Gamma\otimes_{\underline{A}}M^{\prime}_{\ell}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\Gamma\otimes_{\underline{A}}M^{\prime}_{\ell-1}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\Gamma\otimes_{\underline{A}}L^{\prime}_{\ell-1}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}0\\

\begin{matrix}0\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}M_{\ell}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}M_{\ell-1}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\Gamma\otimes_{\underline{A}}L^{\prime}_{\ell-1}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}0\\

-bimodules, where the compared

-module structures are given by
\Gamma\otimes_{\underline{A}}(N_{E})
and

As a consequence, we get
\ell_{E}(N)\leq\ell_{E}(H(N))

Proof.

Once we know that

,
we have
\ell_{E}(N)\leq\ell_{E}(H(N))
. Indeed, we know that
\underline{A}
is a direct summand of the right
\underline{A}
-module
\Gamma

9
]
(12.8). Hence, we get

In order to prove the first statement, consider the structure of proper
\underline{\cal A}\hbox{-}E
-bimodule
\alpha_{N}:E\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\rm End}_{\underline{\cal A}}(N)^{op}
of

It will be enough to show that, for all

With the notation of

Lemma 6.4
.

Assume that
\underline{\cal A}
is not wild,

\Gamma

\underline{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}

\Gamma

\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M)\leq\hbox{\rm endol}\,(H(M)).

is a pregeneric
\underline{\cal A}
-module. From (

\underline{\cal A}\hbox{-}k(x)
-bimodule. Then,

is an
\underline{A}\hbox{-}k(x)
-bimodule,

\underline{A}\otimes_{k}k(x)

\underline{A}^{k(x)}

Proposition 6.5
.

Consider the exact equivalence
H:\underline{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta^{\prime})
. Take
N\in\underline{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
and consider

is a generic
\Gamma
-module, we have that

is a pregeneric
\underline{\cal A}
-module.

Proof.

Set

, then
N
is an
\underline{\cal A}\hbox{-}E
-bimodule and
M=H(N)
is a
\Gamma\hbox{-}E
-bimodule. The action of

by the right on
N
is given by

, for
n\in N
and
\phi\in E
, and the action of
E
by the right on

is given by
h\phi=H(\phi)[h]=\phi\circ h
, the composition in
\underline{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
, for

and
\phi\in E
.

Notice that the right
{\rm End}_{\Gamma}(M)^{op}
-module
M
is a right

E
-module by restriction using the isomorphism of algebras
H:E\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\rm End}_{\Gamma}(M)^{op}
. This action coincides with the preceding one. We have that
\ell_{E}(M)=\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M)
, which is finite by assumption. We will show that
\ell_{E}(N)

is a pregeneric
\underline{\cal A}

Set
\mathfrak{r}:={\rm rad}E

[
16
]
(4.2;4.4), we know that

is a local ring and that

is nilpotent. Thus, for some natural number

\mathfrak{r}^{l}=0
.
So, we have a filtration of right
E
-modules

0=M_{l}=M\mathfrak{r}^{l}\subseteq M_{l-1}=M\mathfrak{r}^{l-1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq M_{1}=M\mathfrak{r}^{1}\subseteq M_{0}=M,

and we can consider the division

. Each

is a
D
-vector space. Since
\ell_{E}(M)
is finite,
we know that each
M_{i}/M_{i+1}
is a
D
-vector space with finite dimension, say
d_{i+1}
. Consider

.

We also have a filtration
of right
E
-modules

-vector space. We will show that

So, we fix

is generated by classes modulo

of elements of the form

-linearly dependent in

-linearly dependent. So, there are elements

\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)
-projective modules.

Proof.

Since each
U_{v}
is an
{\cal F}(\Theta)
-projective module, we know that
{\rm Ext}^{1}_{\Upsilon}(U_{v},\Theta_{u})=0
, for all
u\in{\cal P}
.
Since
U_{v}
is a finite-dimensional
\Lambda
-module, the functor

{\rm Ext}^{1}_{\Upsilon}(U_{v},-)
commutes with direct limits, see for instance
[
19
]
(3.1.6). But direct sums are direct limits of their finite subdirect sums, so

{\rm Ext}^{1}_{\Upsilon}(U_{v},-)
commutes with direct sums, and we get that

{\rm Ext}^{1}_{\Upsilon}(U_{v},V)=0

for any direct sum
V
of copies of the modules in
\{\Theta_{v}\mid v\in{\cal P}\}
. A simple induction argument shows that
{\rm Ext}^{1}_{\Upsilon}(U_{v},M)=0
, for any
M\in\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)
. So, any such
U_{v}
is an
\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)
-projective.

If
V=\bigoplus_{j\in J}V_{j}
, where each
V_{j}
is an
\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)
-projective module, for instance from
[
24
]
(5.2.53), we have
{\rm Ext}^{1}_{\Upsilon}(V,M)=\prod_{j}{\rm Ext}^{1}_{\Upsilon}(V_{j},M)=0
, so
V
is an
\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)
-projective module.
Now, we can easily adapt the argument in the proof of
[
8
]
(11.2) to show that an exact sequence as described in the statement of this lemma for each
M\in\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)
exists.

If
M
is any indecomposable
\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)
-projective, we have an exact sequence as we have just mentioned, so
M
is an indecompsable direct summand of
W
. As a consequence of
Crawley-Jønsson-Warfield and Azumaya Theorems, see
[
1
]
(12.6; 26.5; and 26.6), we obtain that
M\cong U_{v}
, for some
v\in{\cal P}
.

Finally, the fact that the family
\{U_{v}\}_{v\in{\cal P}}
consists of pairwise non-isomorphic
\Upsilon
-modules is proved in
[
22
]
(3.7).
∎

Definition 7.4
.

Define
U:=\bigoplus_{v\in{\cal P}}U_{v}
and
\Lambda:={\rm End}_{\Upsilon}(U)^{op}
. Moreover, consider the family

\{\Delta_{v}\}_{v\in{\cal P}}
of
\Lambda

It was shown in
[

that
({\cal P},\leq,\{\Delta_{v}\}_{v\in{\cal P}})

In the following, we extend a little their results to show that

The quasi-inverse equivalences will be realized by the appropriate restrictions of the functors

Lemma 7.5
.

The functor
\mathfrak{H}
restricts to a full and faithful exact functor

\mathfrak{H}:\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}.

Proof.

Assume that
V=\bigoplus_{j\in J}V_{j}
is a direct sum of modules
V_{j}
which are isomorphic to some modules in
\{U_{v}\mid v\in{\cal P}\}
. We have the direct sums

\{s_{i}:V_{i}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\bigoplus_{j}V_{j}\}_{i}\hbox{ \ and \ }\{\sigma_{i}:\mathfrak{H}(V_{i})\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\bigoplus_{j}\mathfrak{H}(V_{j})\}_{i}

in
\Upsilon\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
and
\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
, respectively. For
N\in\Upsilon\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
, consider the following commutative diagram

\begin{matrix}{\rm Hom}_{\Upsilon}(\bigoplus_{j}V_{j},N)&\begin{matrix}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\mathfrak{H}}}&{\rm Hom}_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{H}(V),\mathfrak{H}(N))&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\phi^{*}}}\end{matrix}&{\rm Hom}_{\Lambda}(\bigoplus_{j}\mathfrak{H}(V_{j}),\mathfrak{H}(N))\\
\downarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Theta_{s}$}}$\hss}&&\downarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\Theta_{\sigma}$}}$\hss}\\
\prod_{j}{\rm Hom}_{\Upsilon}(V_{j},N)&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\hbox{ \ \ \ \ }\prod{\mathfrak{H}_{V_{j},N}}\hbox{ \ \ \ \ }}}&\prod_{j}{\rm Hom}_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{H}(V_{j}),\mathfrak{H}(N))\\
\end{matrix}

where:
\phi:\bigoplus_{j}\mathfrak{H}(V_{j})=\bigoplus_{j}{\rm Hom}_{\Upsilon}(U,V_{j})\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\rm Hom}_{\Upsilon}(U,\bigoplus_{j}V_{j})=\mathfrak{H}(\bigoplus_{j}V_{j})
is the isomorphism given by
\phi(\sum_{j}h_{j})=\sum_{j}s_{j}h_{j}
;
\Theta_{s}
is the isomorphism given by
\Theta_{s}(f)=\{fs_{j}\}_{j}
and, similarly,
\Theta_{\sigma}
is the isomorphism given by
\Theta_{\sigma}(h)=\{h\sigma_{j}\}_{j}
. It follows that the morphism
\mathfrak{H}
in the diagram is an isomorphism.

Consider the full subcategory
{\rm add}(U)
of

\Upsilon\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
(resp. the full subcategory
{\rm Add}(U)
of
\Upsilon\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
) formed by the direct summands of finite direct sums of
U
(resp. by the direct summands of direct sums of
U
). Here, we adapt the argument used in

[
3
]
(II.2.1) to show that
\mathfrak{H}
restricts to an equivalence of categories
{\rm add}(U)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\rm add}(\Lambda)
. Using the fact that
\mathfrak{H}
commutes with direct sums, we have verified that, for each
W\in{\rm Add}(U)
and
N\in\Upsilon\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
, the functor
\mathfrak{H}
restricts to an isomorphism

{\rm Hom}_{\Upsilon}(W,N)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\rm Hom}_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{H}(W),\mathfrak{H}(N))
. Then,
\mathfrak{H}
restricts to an equivalence of categories
{\rm Add}(U)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\rm Add}(\Lambda)
, where
{\rm Add}(\Lambda)
consists of the projective
\Lambda
-modules.

Given
M\in\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)
, from (
7.3
), there is an exact sequence of the form

W^{\prime}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}W\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\pi}}M\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}0,

where
W,W^{\prime}\in{\rm Add}(U)
. Since
U
is
\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)
-projective,
applying
\mathfrak{H}
, we obtain an exact sequence

\mathfrak{H}(W^{\prime})\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\mathfrak{H}(W)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\pi_{*}}}\mathfrak{H}(M)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}0.

Then, for any

, we have the commutative diagram

\begin{matrix}0&\rightarrow&{\rm Hom}_{\Upsilon}(M,N)&\rightarrow&{\rm Hom}_{\Upsilon}(W,N)&\rightarrow&{\rm Hom}_{\Upsilon}(W^{\prime},N)\\
&&\downarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle$}}$\hss}&&\downarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle$}}$\hss}&&\downarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle$}}$\hss}\\
0&\rightarrow&{\rm Hom}_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{H}(M),\mathfrak{H}(N))&\rightarrow&{\rm Hom}_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{H}(W),\mathfrak{H}(N))&\rightarrow&{\rm Hom}_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{H}(W^{\prime}),\mathfrak{H}(N))\\
\end{matrix}

where the second and third vertical arrows are isomorphisms, then so is the first one. This shows that the restriction of
\mathfrak{H}
to the category
\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)
is full and faithful. This restriction is exact because
U
is an
\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)
-projective module.

Theorem 7.6
.

Given any homological system
({{\cal P}},\leq,\{\Theta_{v}\}_{v\in{\cal P}})
for
\Upsilon
,
consider the algebra
\Lambda={\rm End}_{\Upsilon}(U)^{op}
as before and the triple
({{\cal P}},\leq,\{\Delta_{v}\}_{v\in{\cal P}})
, where
\Delta_{v}={\rm Hom}_{\Upsilon}(U,\Theta_{v})
, for all

.
Then,
({{\cal P}},\leq,\{\Delta_{v}\}_{v\in{\cal P}})
is an admissible homological system for
\Lambda

\mathfrak{H}={\rm Hom}_{\Upsilon}(U,-)
and
\mathfrak{T}=U\otimes_{\Lambda}-
induce quasi-inverse equivalences of exact categories between
\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)
and
\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta)

Proof.

With the preceding notations, we consider the following natural transformations

\alpha:U\otimes_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{H}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}id_{\Upsilon\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}}

and
\beta:id_{\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\mathfrak{H}(U\otimes_{\Lambda}-)
given by

1.

For

, the morphism
\alpha_{M}:U\otimes_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{H}(M)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}M
has the recipe
\alpha_{M}(u\otimes g)=g(u)
.

2.

For
N\in\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
, the morphism
\beta_{N}:N\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\mathfrak{H}(U\otimes_{\Gamma}N)
has the recipe
\beta_{N}(n)[u]=u\otimes n

We already know that
({{\cal P}},\leq,\{\Delta_{v}\}_{v\in{\cal P}})
is an admissible homological system, see
[
9
]
(13.7).
We have to show that we have quasi-inverse exact equivalences

\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\mathfrak{H}}}\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta)\hbox{ \ and \ }\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\mathfrak{T}}}\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta).

Step 1.
\alpha_{M}
is an isomorphism, for all
M\in\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)
.

Since
\alpha_{U}
is the composition
U\otimes_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{H}(U)=U\otimes_{\Lambda}\Lambda\cong U
, we know that
\alpha_{U}
is an isomorphism. It follows that
\alpha_{W}
is an isomorphism, for each
W\in{\rm Add}(U)
. For a given
M\in\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)
, consider an exact sequence

W^{\prime}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}W\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}M\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}0
,
as before, with

. Then, we have a commutative diagram

\begin{matrix}U\otimes_{\Gamma}\mathfrak{H}(W^{\prime})&\rightarrow&U\otimes_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{H}(W)&\rightarrow&U\otimes_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{H}(M)&\rightarrow&0\\
\downarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\alpha_{W^{\prime}}$}}$\hss}&&\downarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\alpha_{W}$}}$\hss}&&\downarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\alpha_{M}$}}$\hss}&&\\
W^{\prime}&\rightarrow&W&\rightarrow&M&\rightarrow&0\\
\end{matrix}

with exact rows, and
\alpha_{M}
is an isomorphism.

Step 2. The functor
\mathfrak{T}=U\otimes_{\Lambda}-
restricts to an exact functor
\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta)\rightarrow\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)
.

As in
[
8
]
(11.5)(step 2), we have that
{\rm Tor}^{\Lambda}_{1}(U,\Delta_{v})=0
, for all
v\in{\cal P}
. Since
{\rm Tor}_{1}^{\Lambda}(U,-)
commutes with direct sums, see
[
24
]
(5.2.50), we get
{\rm Tor}^{\Lambda}_{1}(U,V)=0

. A simple induction argument shows that
{\rm Tor}^{\Lambda}_{1}(U,N)=0
, for any
N\in\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta)

With almost the same argument used in
[
8
]
(11.5)(step 2), we derive from this that
U\otimes_{\Lambda}N\in\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)
, for any
N\in\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta)
.

Moreover, if
0\rightarrow L\rightarrow M\rightarrow N\rightarrow 0
is an exact sequence in
\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta)
, using that
{\rm Tor}_{1}^{\Lambda}(U,N)=0

Step 3.
\beta_{N}
is an isomorphism, for all
N\in\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta)
.

As in
[
8
]
(11.5)(step 3), we show that
\beta_{\Delta_{v}}
is an isomorphism
for

. Then, using again that
\mathfrak{H}
and
\mathfrak{T}
commute with direct sums, we derive that
\beta_{N}
is an isomorphism for all
N\in\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta)
.

We have shown that we can restrict the functors
\mathfrak{H}
and
\mathfrak{T}
to functors

\mathfrak{H}:\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta)
and
\mathfrak{T}:\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)
and that these restrictions are exact. Moreover, the restrictions of the natural transformations
\alpha
and
\beta
determine isomorphisms of functors
\alpha:\mathfrak{T}\mathfrak{H}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}id_{\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)}
and
\beta:id_{\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta)}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\mathfrak{H}\mathfrak{T}
.

Lemma 7.7
.

With the preceding notation, for any
N\in\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)
, we have

\hbox{\rm endol}\,(\mathfrak{H}(N))\leq\dim_{k}U\times\hbox{\rm endol}\,(N)

and, for any
M\in\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta)
, we have

\hbox{\rm endol}\,(U\otimes_{\Lambda}M)\leq\dim_{k}U\times\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M)
.
Therefore, the functors
\mathfrak{H}:\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta)
and
\mathfrak{T}:\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)
preserve generic modules.

Proof.

Given
N\in\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)
, set
E:={\rm End}_{\Lambda}(\mathfrak{H}(N))^{op}
, and consider the
\Upsilon\hbox{-}E
-bimodule
structure on
N
, where
E
acts by the right through the algebra isomorphism

\alpha_{N}:=\mathfrak{H}^{-1}:E\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\rm End}_{\Upsilon}(N)^{op}
.

If
b=\dim_{k}U
, there is an epimorphism
\Upsilon^{b}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}U
of left
\Upsilon
-modules. So, there is a monomorphism

\mathfrak{H}(N)={\rm Hom}_{\Upsilon}(U,N)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\rm Hom}_{\Upsilon}(\Upsilon^{b},N)\cong{\rm Hom}_{\Upsilon}(\Upsilon,N)^{b}\cong N^{b}

of right
E
-modules. Hence,

\hbox{\rm endol}\,(\mathfrak{H}(N))=\ell_{E}(\mathfrak{H}(N))\leq b\times\ell_{E}(N)=b\times\hbox{\rm endol}\,(N).

The other claim in the statement of this lemma is similar (and well known).
∎

Corollary 7.8
.

{\cal F}(\Theta)
is generically tame iff
{\cal F}(\Delta)
is so.

Proof.

From (
7.7
), we know that, given
d\in\mathbb{N}
, whenever
\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta)
has only finitely many
generic
\Lambda
-modules with endolength bounded by
\dim_{k}U\times d
, then
\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)
has only finitely many generic
\Upsilon
-modules with endolength bounded by
d
. So,
{\cal F}(\Theta)
is generically tame whenever
{\cal F}(\Delta)
is so.
The proof of converse statement is similar.
∎

Remark 7.9
.

{\cal F}(\Theta)
is wild iff
{\cal F}(\Delta)
is so.

Indeed, if
{\cal F}(\Theta)
is wild, we have the composition functor

k\langle x,y\rangle\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\ \ Z\otimes_{k\langle x,y\rangle}-\ \ }}{\cal F}(\Theta)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\mathfrak{H}}}{\cal F}(\Delta),

The verification of the converse is simpler.

6

is a generic

-module in
\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta^{\prime})
with
\hbox{\rm endol}\,(M^{\prime})\leq d^{\prime}
. By (
6.7
), there is a pregeneric
{\cal B}
-module
N
such that
T^{\prime}\otimes_{B}N\cong M^{\prime}
. Therefore, we obtain
T\otimes_{B}N\cong U\otimes_{\Lambda}P\otimes_{\Gamma}\Gamma\otimes_{\underline{A}}F(B)\otimes_{B}N\cong M
. Since
\mathfrak{T}\Omega:{\rm End}_{\Gamma}(M^{\prime})^{op}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\rm End}_{\Upsilon}(M)^{op}
is an isomorphism, the splitting of the radical for the second algebra follows from the same property for the first one.
The fact that
M
is a proper
\Upsilon\hbox{-}k(x)
-bimodule follows from the corresponding property for
M^{\prime}
.
∎

8
Generic modules for
{\cal F}(\Theta)

This section is devoted to the proofs of the main results (
1.5
) and (
1.6
). We keep the notation of the preceding section. So, we assume that
\Upsilon
is a finite-dimensional algebra over an algebraically closed field and
({\cal P},\leq,\{\Theta_{v}\}_{v\in{\cal P}})
is any homological system for
\Upsilon
, and we will develop the proofs wih this notation.

Proof of Theorem (
1.5
).

We assume that
{\cal F}(\Theta)
is not wild and that
G
is a generic
\Upsilon
-module for
{\cal F}(\Theta)
.
Let
d:=\hbox{\rm endol}\,(G)
and apply (
7.10
) to obtain the minimal ditalgebra
{\cal B}
and the composition of functors

B\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{L_{\cal B}}}{\cal B}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{F}}\underline{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{H}}\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta^{\prime})\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\Omega}}\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\mathfrak{T}}}\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta),

isomorphic to a suitable tensor functor
T\otimes_{B}-
.

By (
7.10
)(2), there is a generic
B
-module

N\in{\cal B}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
with

\mathfrak{T}\Omega HF(N)\cong T\otimes_{B}N\cong G
. In fact,
N=Be_{i}\otimes_{Be_{i}}Q_{i}
, where
Q_{i}
is the field of fractions of
\Gamma_{i}=Be_{i}
considered as a
\Gamma_{i}
-module. Here
Be_{i}
is a rational
k
-algebra and
Q_{i}=k(x)
.
We have

G\cong T\otimes_{B}N=T\otimes_{B}Be_{i}\otimes_{Be_{i}}Q_{i}\cong Te_{i}\otimes_{Be_{i}}Q_{i},

where
Te_{i}
is a finitely generated right
Be_{i}
-module, so there is some
g\in Be_{i}
such that
Z_{G}:=Te_{i}\otimes_{Be_{i}}(Be_{i})_{g}
is a free finitely generated right
(Be_{i})_{g}
-module. Moreover, the field of fractions
Q_{G}
of the rational algebra
\Gamma_{G}:=(Be_{i})_{g}
coincides with
k(x)=Q_{i}
, and we have

Z_{G}\otimes_{\Gamma_{G}}Q_{G}=Te_{i}\otimes_{Be_{i}}(Be_{i})_{g}\otimes_{(Be_{i})_{g}}Q_{i}\cong Te_{i}\otimes_{Be_{i}}Q_{i}\cong G.

From the last statement of (
7.10
), we know that
\dim_{k(x)}G=\hbox{\rm endol}\,(G)
and from the last displayed formula, the rank of
Z_{G}
is
\dim_{k(x)}G
, so (1) is proved.

(2): Since
{\cal B}
is a minimal ditalgebra, we know that
L_{\cal B}:B\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\cal B}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
preserves isoclasses, indecomposability and maps almost split sequences onto almost split conflations, see for instance
[
10
]
(32.3). Since
\mathfrak{T}
,
\Omega
,
H
, and
F
are full and faithful functors, they all preserve isoclasses and indecomposability. Then, the functor
T\otimes_{B}-\cong\mathfrak{T}\Omega HFL_{\cal B}
preserves isoclasses and indecomposability. The localization morphism
\phi:Be_{i}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}(Be_{i})_{g}=\Gamma_{G}
is an epimorphism which induces a full and faithful functor
F_{\phi}:\Gamma_{G}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}Be_{i}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
which can be composed with the embedding functor
E:Be_{i}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}B\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
. Then the functor
Z_{G}\otimes_{\Gamma_{G}}-\cong\mathfrak{T}\Omega HFL_{\cal B}EF_{\phi}
preserves isoclasses and indecomposables.

(3): We know that, given any Roiter interlaced weak ditalgebra
\underline{\cal A}
, the category
\underline{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
has a natural exact structure
{\cal E}_{\underline{\cal A}}
, see
[
9
]
(11.11). From (
2.10
), we know that
\underline{\cal A}^{z}
is a Roiter interlaced weak ditalgebra whenever
\underline{\cal A}
is so, for
z\in\{a,r,q,d,X\}
. Moreover, by (
5.5
) we know that
\underline{\cal A}(\Delta)\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
has almost split conflations.

By
[
9
]
(11.5), the functor
F^{X}:\underline{\cal A}^{X}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\underline{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
is exact, when

\underline{\cal A}
is a Roiter interlaced weak ditalgebra, see
[
10
]
(16.6). The same observations of
[
10
]
[(9.8),(9.9),(9.10)] show that the functors
F^{z}:\underline{\cal A}^{z}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\underline{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
, for
z\in\{a,r,q,d\}
, as in (
2.1
), (
2.2
), (
2.3
), (
2.4
), are exact, when
\underline{\cal A}
is a Roiter interlaced weak ditalgebra. These statements, and the corresponding ones for reduction functors
F^{z}
of type
z\in\{a,r,d,e,u\}
between module categories of Roiter ditalgebras, imply that the functor
F
is exact (since it is a composition of such exact functors). So the composition
F:{\cal B}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\underline{\cal A}(\Delta)\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
is a full and faithful exact functor between Krull-Schmidt categories with exact structures which admit almost split conflations.

From (
5.7
), we know that there are only finitely many indecomposable
\Gamma_{G}
-modules of the form
E_{\lambda}:=\Gamma_{G}/\langle x-\lambda\rangle
such that
FL_{\cal B}EF_{\phi}(E_{\lambda})\not\cong\tau FL_{\cal B}EF_{\phi}(E_{\lambda})
. If this occurs for
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{t}\in k
, we can replace the polynomial
g
used at the begining of this proof by

to obtain a rational algebra
\Gamma^{\prime}_{G}=(Ee_{i})_{g^{\prime}}
such that
FL_{\cal B}EF_{\phi}(E_{\lambda})\cong\tau FL_{\cal B}EF_{\phi}(E_{\lambda})
, for all such indecomposables
E_{\lambda}
of
\Gamma^{\prime}_{G}
, and we adjust

accordingly. Moreover, (1) and (2), still hold for this

. So we can assume that
\Gamma_{G}
and
Z_{G}
already have this property and we can apply
[
10
]
(32.7), to obtain that

FL_{\cal B}EF_{\phi}
preserves almost split conflations. Since

,
\Omega
, and
\mathfrak{T}
are exact equivalences, so does the composition

.

\square

Proposition 8.1
.

If
{\cal F}(\Theta)
is not wild, then it is generically tame.

Moreover, in this case, for any
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
almost all indecomposable
\Upsilon
-modules
M\in{\cal F}(\Theta)
with
\dim_{k}M\leq d
are of the form
M\cong Z_{G}\otimes_{\Gamma_{G}}N
, for some generic module
G
for
{\cal F}(\Theta)
with
\hbox{\rm endol}\,(G)\leq d
and some indecomposable
N\in\Gamma_{G}\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
, with the notation of (
1.5
).

Proof.

If
{\cal F}(\Theta)
is not wild and
d\in\mathbb{N}
, we can apply (
7.10
) to obtain a minimal ditalgebra
{\cal B}
and a composition of functors

B\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{L_{\cal B}}}{\cal B}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{F}}\underline{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{H}}\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta^{\prime})\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\Omega}}\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\mathfrak{T}}}\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta),

isomorphic to a suitable tensor functor
T\otimes_{B}-
.

Then, from (
7.10
), we know that the generic
\Upsilon
-modules in
\widetilde{\cal F}(\Theta)
with endolength
\leq d
, up to isomorphism, are
G_{1},\ldots,G_{n}
, with
G_{i}=\mathfrak{T}\Omega HFL_{\cal B}(Q_{i})
, for
i\in[1,n]
, where
n
is the number of rational factors of the minimal algebra of the layer of
{\cal B}
. Here
Q_{i}
is the
B
-module described in (
4.7
).

From (
7.10
)(3), we get that any indecomposable
\Upsilon
-module
M\in{\cal F}(\Theta)
with

, for some indecomposable
Be_{i}
-module

with dimension bounded by some integer

, which depends only on

. Now, recall from the preceding proof that each bimodule
Z_{i}=Te_{i}\otimes_{Be_{i}}(Be_{i})_{g}
, associated to a given generic module
G_{i}
for

, involves a localization of

∎

Proposition 8.2
.

If
{\cal F}(\Theta)
is wild, then it is not generically tame.

Proof.

We adapt the argument of the proof of
[
15
]
(4.4). From (
7.9
) and (
7.8
), we already know that
{\cal F}(\Theta)
is wild (resp. generically tame) iff
{\cal F}(\Delta)
is so. Moreover,
{\cal F}(\Delta)
is wild (resp. generically tame) iff
{\cal F}(\Delta^{\prime})
is wild (resp. generically tame), see
[
8
]
(1.8) and
[
10
]
(22.6), it will be enough to prove the statement for
{\cal F}(\Delta^{\prime})
.

Assume
{\cal F}(\Delta^{\prime})
is wild, then there is a
\Gamma\hbox{-}k\langle x,y\rangle
-bimodule
Z
, which is free finitely generated as a right module,
such that the functor

W=Z\otimes_{k\langle x,y\rangle}-:k\langle x,y\rangle\hbox{-}{\rm mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\cal F}(\Delta^{\prime})

preserves isoclasses and indecomposables.
From
[
8
]
(10.4) and the proof of
[
8
]
(1.3), we know that
{\cal F}(\Delta^{\prime})
is wild iff
\underline{\cal A}
is so. Then, we can assume that the bimodule
Z
producing the wildness of
{\cal F}(\Delta^{\prime})
comes from a bimodule producing the wildness of
\underline{\cal A}
, see the proof of
[
8
]
(1.3).
So, by (
6.2
), we may assume that the bimodule
Z
determines a functor

W=Z\otimes_{k\langle x,y\rangle}-:k\langle x,y\rangle\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta^{\prime}),

which preserves isoclasses and idecomposables, see
[
8
]
(6.12). As in
[
10
]
[(31.3) and (31.5)], we consider the
k\langle x,y\rangle
-modules
H_{\lambda}=k(x)[y]/(y-\lambda)
, for
\lambda\in k
, where
k\langle x,y\rangle
acts by restriction through the epimorphism of algebras

k\langle x,y\rangle\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}k[x,y]\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}k[x,y]_{k[x]^{*}}\cong k(x)[y].

The family
\{H_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in k}
is an infinite family of pairwise non-isomorphic generic
k\langle x,y\rangle
-modules with endolength 1. Set
G_{\lambda}:=W(H_{\lambda})
, for
\lambda\in k
. So
\{G_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in k}
, is an infinite family of pairwise non-isomorphic generic
\Gamma
-modules in
\widetilde{\cal F}(\Delta^{\prime})
with bounded endolength. So
{\cal F}(\Delta^{\prime})
is not generically tame.
∎

Theorem 8.3
.

{\cal F}(\Theta)
is generically tame iff
{\cal F}(\Theta)
is tame.

Proof.

From
[
8
]
(1.3), (
7.9
), and (
7.8
), we already know that
{\cal F}(\Theta)
is generically tame (resp. tame) iff
{\cal F}(\Delta)
is tame. So it is enough to show the corresponding statement for
{\cal F}(\Delta)
.
From (
1.10
), we already know that
\underline{\cal A}
is pregenerically tame iff
\underline{\cal A}
is not wild.

If
{\cal F}(\Delta)
is tame then, by
[
8
]
(1.3),
{\cal F}(\Delta)
is not wild. So, by (
6.6
), we know that
\underline{\cal A}
is not wild and, as mentioned before, this means that it is pregenerically tame. Thus given
d\in\mathbb{N}
, by (
6.5
) and (
6.4
), any generic module
G
for
{\cal F}(\Delta)
with
\hbox{\rm endol}\,(G)\leq d
is the image under
\Omega H
of a pregeneric
\underline{\cal A}
-module
M
with bounded endolength, so there are only a finite number of non-isomorphic generic modules for
{\cal F}(\Delta)
with endolength
\leq d
. Hence,
{\cal F}(\Delta)
is generically tame.

Conversely, assume that
{\cal F}(\Delta)
is generically tame. By (
8.2
), it can not be wild. So, by
[
8
]
(1.3), it is tame.
∎

Remark 8.4
.

In
[
25
]
, H. Treffinger constructs a family of examples of non-admissible homological systems for finite-dimensional algebras
\Upsilon
with size which can be arbitrarily large in comparison to the number
of isomorphism classes of simple
\Upsilon
-modules. Our main results apply to
{\cal F}(\Theta)
for each one of these homological systems.

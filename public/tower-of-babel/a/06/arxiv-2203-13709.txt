Incompressible limits of the Patlak-Keller-Segel model and its stationary state
Qingyou He, Hai-Liang Li, Benoît Perthame

Abstract
We complete previous results about the incompressible limit of both the n n -dimensional ( n ≥ 3 ) (n\geq 3) compressible Patlak-Keller-Segel (PKS) model and its stationary state. As in previous works, in this limit, we derive the weak form of a geometric free boundary problem of Hele-Shaw type, also called congested flow. In particular, we are able to take into account the unsaturated zone, and establish the complementarity relation which describes the limit pressure by a degenerate elliptic equation. Not only our analysis uses a completely different framework than previous approaches, but we also establish a novel uniform L 3 L^{3} estimate of the pressure gradient, regularity à la Aronson-Bénilan, and a uniform L 1 L^{1} estimate for the time derivative of the pressure. Furthermore, for the Hele-Shaw problem, we prove the uniqueness of solutions, meaning that the incompressible limit of the PKS model is unique. In addition, we establish the corresponding incompressible limit of the stationary state for the PKS model with a given mass, where, different from the case of PKS model, we obtain the uniform bound of pressure and the uniformly bounded support of density.



Key-words.
Keller-Segel system; Stationary state; Incompressible limit;
Aronson-Bénilan estimate; Complementarity relation; Free boundary; Hele-Shaw problem

Introduction

The Patlak-Keller-Segel (PKS) model can be used to describe the collective dynamics of a large
number of individual agents interacting through a diffusive signal. For instance, it appears for the chemotaxis phenomena of various types of cells, aggregation dynamics of crowds or to describe the gravitational collapse, see
[
56
,
51
,
15
]
, and references therein. With a source term, it is used as a mechanical description of tumor growth
[
47
,
11
]
. Including nonlinear diffusivity and Newtonian interactions, the PKS model is written

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho_{m}=\Delta\rho_{m}^{m}+\nabla\cdot(\rho_{m}\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m})\quad\text{for}\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\quad n\geq 3,\\[5.0pt]
\rho_{m}(x,0)=\rho_{m,0}(x)\geq 0\quad\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad\qquad m>2-2/n,\qquad\text{(subcritical case)}.\end{cases}

(1.1)

For chemotaxis,
\rho_{m}(x,t)\geq 0
represents the cell density and
\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}
represents the chemical substance concentration obtained by convolution with the Newtonian potential

\mathcal{N}(x)=\frac{-1}{n(n-2)\alpha_{n}|x|^{n-2}}\qquad\text{for }\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\},\qquad\Delta\mathcal{N}=\delta,

with
\alpha_{n}>0
being the volume of the
n
-dimensional unit ball and
\delta
the Dirac measure. The conservation of mass for the Cauchy problem Eq. (
1.1
) holds

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{m}(x,t)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{m,0}dx:=M,\qquad\forall\ t\geq 0.

For solutions of Eq. (
1.1
), the pressure denotes a power of the density (Darcy’s law) as

P_{m}:=\frac{m}{m-1}\rho_{m}^{m-1},\qquad\quad P_{m,0}:=\frac{m}{m-1}\rho_{m,0}^{m-1}.

(1.2)

We can rewrite Eq. (
1.1
) for the density
\rho_{m}
and pressure
P_{m}
in terms of the transport
equation with the effective velocity
u_{m}
as

\partial_{t}\rho_{m}=\nabla\cdot(\rho_{m}u_{m}),\qquad u_{m}:=\nabla P_{m}+\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}.

(1.3)

By a direct computation, the pressure satisfies the equation

\partial_{t}P_{m}=(m-1)P_{m}(\Delta P_{m}+\rho_{m})+\nabla P_{m}\cdot(\nabla P_{m}+\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}).

(1.4)

The competition between the degenerate diffusion and the nonlocal aggregation is
the main characteristic of Eq. (
1.1
) or Eq. (
1.3
). This is well
represented by the free energy functional

F_{m}(\rho_{m})=\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{m}^{m}dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}|^{2}dx.

(1.5)

It satisfies the energy identity, which shows that
F_{m}(\rho_{m})
is non-increasing with time,

\frac{dF_{m}(\rho_{m})}{dt}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{m}|\nabla(P_{m}+\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m})|^{2}dx=0.

(1.6)

Since
\frac{\delta F_{m}(\rho_{m})}{\delta\rho_{m}}=P_{m}+\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}
represents the
chemical potential, there exists a gradient flow structure for the PKS model,

\frac{dF_{m}(\rho_{m})}{dt}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{m}|\nabla\frac{\delta F_{m}(\rho_{m})}{\delta\rho_{m}}|^{2}dx=0.

(1.7)

Solutions
\rho_{m,s}
of the stationary PKS system (SPKS) satisfy that the free energy
F_{m}(\rho_{m}(t))
is constant in time and thus are determined by

\nabla\rho_{m,s}^{m}+\rho_{m,s}\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m,s}=0\quad\text{for
}x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad P_{m,s}=\frac{m}{m-1}\rho_{m,s}^{m-1}.

(1.8)

Since a decade, and the paper
[
54
]
motivated by tumor growth, a large literature has been devoted to studying the incompressible (Hele-Shaw) limit, which means the limit as
m\to\infty
, for several variants of the porous medium equations (see below). In particular, establishing this limit when Newtonian interactions are included, as in Eq. (
1.1
) and Eq. (
1.8
), has been a long standing question solved in
[
21
]
based both on optimal transportation methods and viscosity solutions.

Incompressible limit.

Our purpose is to complete the understanding, from
[
21
]
, of the incompressible (Hele-Shaw) limit for the PKS model Eq. (
1.1
) in various directions. Firstly, we introduce a third approach based on weak solutions as described below. In particular, our assumptions on the initial data are more general (not necessarily patches data), and the method can easily be extended to source terms when mass varies. Secondly, we prove new regularity results: an
L^{3}
estimate on
\nabla P_{m}
and regularity à la Aronson-Bénilan showing bounds on the second derivatives of the pressure
P_{m}
. Thirdly, we can prove directly an estimate on the time derivative of the pressure based on a new idea since a direct approach would not work. Finally, we prove a new uniqueness theorem for the limiting Hele-Shaw problem.

Following
[
21
]
, the Hele-Shaw limit system writes

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho_{\infty}=\Delta P_{\infty}+\nabla\cdot(\rho_{\infty}\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{\infty}),\qquad&\text{in }D^{\prime}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\big),\\[5.0pt]
(1-\rho_{\infty})P_{\infty}=0,\qquad\quad 0\leq\rho_{\infty}\leq 1,&\text{a.e. }(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.\end{cases}

(1.9)

This is a weak version of the geometric Hele-Shaw problem including chemotaxis. We also prove the
complementarity relation (in distributional sense)

P_{\infty}(\Delta P_{\infty}+\rho_{\infty})=0.

(1.10)

It describes the limit pressure by a degenerate elliptic equation once we know the regularity of the
set
\{p_{\infty}>0\}
, which is a major challenge for
the Hele-Shaw problem, see
[
13
,
38
,
52
,
33
]
and reference therein. Furthermore, with Eqs. (
1.9
)–(
1.10
) at hand, the limiting free energy functional easily follows,

\begin{cases}F_{\infty}(\rho_{\infty})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{\infty}\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{\infty}dx,\qquad 0\leq\rho_{\infty}\leq 1,\\[10.0pt]
\frac{dF_{\infty}(\rho_{\infty}(t))}{dt}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{\infty}(t)|\nabla\big(P_{\infty}(t)+\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{\infty}(t)\big)|^{2}dx=0.\end{cases}

(1.11)

Compared with
the free energy (
1.5
), the diffusive effect is replaced by the height
constraint
\rho_{\infty}\leq 1
. In the end, we extend the uniqueness
[
6
,
3
]
of solution to the PKS model Eq. (
1.1
) to the uniqueness of solution to the Hele-Shaw limit system Eq. (
1.9
).

In the stationary case, the incompressible (Hele-Shaw) limit from the SPKS model Eq. (
1.8
) as
m\to\infty
, is represented as

\begin{cases}\nabla P_{\infty,s}+\rho_{\infty,s}\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{\infty,s}=0,&\text{in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\\[5.0pt]
(1-\rho_{\infty,s})P_{\infty,s}=0,\qquad\quad 0\leq\rho_{\infty,s}\leq 1,\quad&\text{a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

(1.12)

As before, this corresponds to vanishinf dissipation for the free energy
F_{\infty}(\rho_{\infty}(t))
.

The limits (
1.9
)–(
1.11
) can be formally derived from the PKS model Eq. (
1.1
). Indeed, taking the limit as
m\to\infty
in Eq. (
1.1
), we formally obtain the
first equation in (
1.9
). Since we can prove that the limit pressure
P_{\infty}
is bounded, from (
1.2
) we recover
\eqref{hs}_{2}
. Also, we can formally attain the complementarity relation Eq. (
1.10
) thanks to a direct calculation of Eq. (
1.4
) as
m\to\infty
.
In addition, from (
1.6
), we can formally obtain the limit energy functional (
1.11
). It should be emphasized that the structure of
gradient flow as in (
1.7
) is still present in a weak form as in the optimal
transportation approach, cf.
[
17
,
50
]
. Similarly, the incompressible limit Eq.
\eqref{HSSPKS}_{1}
is formally derived from the SPKS model Eq. (
1.8
) as
m\to\infty
. As it is wellknown, establishing rigorously these limits faces deep difficulties due to the nonlinearities and weak regularity; the limit
\rho_{\infty}
is discontinuous in space and
P_{\infty}
can undergo discontinuities in time.

Difficulties and novelties

However, it should be emphasized that the arguments for passing to incompressible limit in
[
54
,
24
,
25
]
cannot be applied directly to Eq. (
1.1
). This is due to the Newtonian drift in the PKS model, eventhough it is of lower order than the diffusion term. Its singularity gives rise to new and essential challenges for rigorously establishing the incompressible limits (
1.9
)–(
1.11
). Indeed, for the models of tumor growth as Eq. (
1.13
), the source term
\rho_{m}G(P_{m})
helps the authors to obtain a uniform
L^{1}
estimate for the time derivative of both the density and the pressure by Kato’s inequality. But, for Eq. (
1.1
), on the one hand, the nonlocal Newtonian interaction leads to the absence of comparison principle, which means that it is impossible to get a uniform bound for the pressure. On the other hand, one of main challenges is to obtain a uniform
L^{1}
estimate for the time derivative of pressure without the help of the source term, despite the effect of nonlocal interaction. Thus, it is difficult to gain the desired compactness on not only the density but also the pressure for the PKS model. Besides, using the weak formulation approach for the incompressible limit for the SPKS model Eq. (
1.8
) is a new and interesting topic for the diffusion-aggregation equations by the methof of weak solutions, see
[
21
]
for viscosity solution methods.

Therefore, to achieve our goals, we develop new estimates and strategies as follows:

\bullet
We obtain the complementarity relation Eq. (
1.10
) for the PKS model Eq. (
1.1
). We first derive a uniform
L^{3}
estimate on the pressure gradient in the spirit of
[
24
]
. Then, we establish the uniform Aronson-Bénilian (AB) estimates in
L^{3}\cap L^{1}
as initiated in
[
34
]
. In particular, we show a decay rate for the AB estimate in
L^{3}
under the form

\|\min\big\{\Delta P_{m}+\rho_{m},0\big\}\|_{L^{3}(Q_{T})}^{3}\leq\frac{C(T)}{m}.

\bullet
In addition, we establish a new uniform
L^{1}
estimate for the time derivative of pressure. To our knowledge, this is the first time such an estimate is obtained for the high-dimensional porous medium equation (Dracy’s law) with a nonlocal attractive interaction since working directly on the pressure is not sufficient.

\bullet
To prove the uniqueness of the solution to the Hele-Shaw limit system Eq. (
1.9
), the key is to show that the limit pressure is somehow monotone to the limit density. Suppose that
P_{i}\rho_{i}=P_{i}
and
0\leq\rho_{i}\leq 1
for
i=1,2
hold, we find
(P_{1}-P_{2})(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})\geq 0
.

\bullet
To establish the incompressible limit of the SPKS model with a given mass, we gain the uniform bound of the pressure and the uniformly bounded support of the density.

Main results

To state our main results on the incompressible limit of PKS model, we need assumptions on the initial data
\rho_{m,0}
. Firstly, for
\rho_{m,0}
, we assume

\begin{cases}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{m,0}(x)dx=:M<\infty,\quad\|\rho_{m,0}^{m+1}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C,\quad\|\rho_{m,0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty,&\\[2.0pt]
\|\rho_{m,0}-\rho_{\infty,0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\to 0,\quad\text{as }m\to\infty,&\\
{\rm supp}(\rho_{m,0})\subset B_{R_{m}}\text{ for some constant }R_{m}>1.&\end{cases}

(2.1)

Secondly, for some results, in particular the Aronson-Bénilan estimate, we also need additional regularity assumptions on the initial data,

\displaystyle\|P_{m,0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\nabla P_{m,0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\||\Delta P_{m,0}+\rho_{m,0}|_{-}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C.

(2.3)

Furthermore, a compatibility condition is also needed for obtaining the

Finally, to show the compact support of the solution of the Hele-Shaw limit system Eq. (

{\rm supp}(\rho_{m,0})\subset B_{R_{0}}\quad\text{ for a fixed constant }R_{0}>0.

Theorem 2.2

(Stiff limit)

\rho_{\infty}P_{\infty}=P_{\infty},\text{ a.e. }(x,t)\in Q_{T}
. For the case of tumor growth model in
[

converges strongly, which is not available here. Therefore, we argue in two steps. We firstly prove that after extraction,

\rho_{m}P_{m}\rightharpoonup P_{\infty},\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(Q_{T}),\text{ as
}m\to\infty.

(3.13)

For that, thanks to the relation
\rho_{m}P_{m}=\frac{m}{m-1}\rho_{m}^{m}\leq 2\rho_{m}^{m}
, it
follows from Lemma
3.1
that
\rho_{m}P_{m}
is bounded in
L^{2}(Q_{T})
and
thus, after extraction, has a weak limit in
L^{2}(Q_{T})
, which we call

Q_{\infty}
.

Due to Young’s inequality, we have

P_{m}=\frac{m}{m-1}\rho_{m}^{m-1}\leq\frac{m}{m-1}(\frac{m-1}{m}\rho_{m}^{m}+\frac{1}{m})=\frac{m-1}{m}\rho_{m}P_{m}+\frac{1}{m-1}.

In the weak limit, we obtain

P_{\infty}\leq Q_{\infty}.

For the reverse inequality, we consider
A>1
and
m

sufficiently large. Then, we have

\rho_{m}P_{m}=\rho_{m}\min\{A,P_{m}\}+\rho_{m}|P_{m}-A|_{+}\leq(\frac{m-1}{m}A)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}P_{m}+\rho_{m}|P_{m}-A|_{+}.

(3.14)

We can estimate the last term by

\rho_{m}|P_{m}-A|_{+}\leq\chi_{\{P_{m}>A\}}\frac{m}{m-1}\rho_{m}^{m}=\chi_{\{P_{m}>A\}}\frac{m}{m-1}\frac{\rho_{m}^{2m}}{\rho_{m}^{m}}\leq\frac{\rho_{m}^{2m}}{(\frac{m-1}{m}A)^{m/(m-1)}},

and thus, for any non-negative smooth test function
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(Q_{T})
, we conclude

\limsup\limits_{m\to\infty}\int_{Q_{T}}\rho_{m}|P_{m}-A|_{+}\varphi dxdt\leq\limsup\limits_{m\to\infty}\int_{Q_{T}}\frac{\rho_{m}^{2m}}{(\frac{m-1}{m}A)^{m/(m-1)}}dxdt\leq\frac{C}{A}.

On the other hand,
(\frac{m-1}{m}A)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}
converges strongly to
1
.
Therefore by weak-strong convergence
(\frac{m-1}{m}A)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}P_{m}
weakly
converges to
P_{\infty}
. Passing to the weak limit in (
3.14
), we conclude that, for all
A>1

Q_{\infty}\leq P_{\infty}+\frac{C}{A}.

We may take
A\to\infty
and find the desired result, namely (
3.13
).

Secondly, we prove that
\rho_{m}P_{m}\rightharpoonup\rho_{\infty}P_{\infty}
. For any
smooth test function
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(Q_{T})
, we have, recalling the
strong convergence proved in Lemma
3.2
,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}\rho_{m}P_{m}\varphi dxdt=\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\int_{Q_{T}}\Delta\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}P_{m}\,\varphi dxdt

(3.15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\int_{Q_{T}}\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}\cdot\nabla P_{m}\,\varphi dxdt-\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\int_{Q_{T}}\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}\cdot\nabla\varphi P_{m}dxdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{Q_{T}}\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{\infty}\cdot\nabla P_{\infty}\varphi dxdt-\int_{Q_{T}}\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{\infty}\cdot\nabla\varphi P_{\infty}dxdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}\rho_{\infty}P_{\infty}\varphi dxdt.

This means that
\rho_{m}P_{m}\rightharpoonup\rho_{\infty}P_{\infty}
and we have
obtained the result.

Proof of Eq. (
2.7
),
0\leq\rho_{\infty}\leq 1

\displaystyle C(T).

After time integration, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\omega_{m}(t)|_{-}^{2}dx

\displaystyle+2m\int_{Q_{T}}P_{m}|\nabla|\omega_{m}|_{-}|^{2}dxdt+m\int_{Q_{T}}[\rho_{m}|\omega_{m}|_{-}^{2}d+|\omega_{m}|_{-}^{3}]dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(T).

This proves (
4.15
) of Theorem
4.2
.

Next, we multiply (
4.19
) by
-sgn(|\omega_{m}|_{-})
, then we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}|\omega_{m}|_{-}\leq

\displaystyle(m-1)\Delta(P_{m}|\omega_{m}|_{-})+\nabla|\omega_{m}|_{-}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}+sgn(|\omega_{m}|_{-})\frac{1}{2}(\nabla^{2}\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m})^{2}

\displaystyle+2\nabla P_{m}\cdot\nabla|\omega_{m}|_{-}+\rho_{m}|\omega_{m}|_{-}.

After integration on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, due to the singular integral theory
for Newtonian potential (Lemma
C.1
), and Holder’s inequality, we
attain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\omega_{m}|_{-}dx\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\omega_{m}|_{-}\rho_{m}dx+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\omega_{m}|_{-}^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{2}(\nabla^{2}\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m})^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{m}^{2}dx+3\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\omega_{m}|_{-}^{2}dx+C(n)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{m}^{2}dx\leq C(T).

Integrating in
t
as before, we conclude the first estimate of (
4.16
) for Theorem
4.2
, namely

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\omega_{m}(t)|_{-}dx\leq C(T).

(4.24)

Finally, since
|\Delta P_{m}|\leq|\Delta P_{m}+\rho_{m}|+\rho_{m}=\Delta P_{m}+\rho_{m}+2|\omega_{m}|_{-}+\rho_{m}
, we find

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\Delta P_{m}|dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Delta P_{m}+\rho_{m}dx+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\omega_{m}|_{-}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{m}dx

\displaystyle\leq 2C+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\omega_{m}|_{-}dx,

therefore, the second bound of (
4.16
) follows from (
4.24
) that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\Delta P_{m}(t)|dx\leq C(T).

The proof of Theorem
4.2
is completed.
∎

useful to get locally strong compactness of the pressure gradient sequences
\{\nabla P_{m}\}_{m>1}
. We make full use of Kato’s inequality and the specific form of the Newtonian potential to achieve our goal.

We first give two useful preliminary lemmas.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that the initial data
\rho_{m,0}
satisfies the assumptions
(

)–(

), let

be a weak solution to the Cauchy problem Eq. (

) in the sense of Def.

, then it follows

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}\big(\nabla\rho_{m}^{m}\cdot\nabla\rho_{m}^{m+1}+|\nabla\rho_{m}^{m+1}|^{2}+|\nabla\rho_{m}^{m+2}|^{2})dxdt\leq C(T).

Proof.

These estimates can be written as

bounds on
\rho_{m}^{\frac{3}{2}}\nabla P_{m}
,
\rho_{m}^{2}\nabla P_{m}
,
\rho_{m}^{\frac{5}{2}}\nabla P_{m}
. They

) and (

).
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Under the initial assumptions (
2.1
)–(
2.3
), let the pair
(P_{m},\rho_{m})
be a weak solution to the PKS model Eq. (
1.1
)in the sense of Def.

\|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}-\nabla\rho_{m}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}\|_{L^{1}(Q_{T})}\leq C(T).

Proof.

Since

\partial_{t}\rho_{m}-\nabla\rho_{m}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}\geq\rho_{m}\omega_{m},

we have

|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}-\nabla\rho_{m}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}|_{-}\leq\rho_{m}|\omega_{m}|_{-}\leq\frac{1}{2}[\rho_{m}^{2}+|\omega_{m}|_{-}^{2}].

Consequently, using mass conservation, Theorem
4.2
and Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}|\partial_{t}

\displaystyle\rho_{m}-\nabla\rho_{m}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}|dxdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}(\partial_{t}\rho_{m}-\nabla\rho_{m}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m})dxdt+2\int_{Q_{T}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}-\nabla\rho_{m}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}|_{-}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{Q_{T}}\rho_{m}^{2}dxdt+\int_{Q_{T}}|\omega_{m}|_{-}^{2}dxdt\leq C(T).

∎

In the following, we give the
L^{1}
time derivative estimate of pressure.

Theorem 4.3

(
L^{1}
time derivative estimate of pressure)
.

Under the initial assumptions (
2.1
)–(
2.4
), let
\rho_{m}
be a weak solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) for the PKS in the sense of Def.
1.1
, then

\|\partial_{t}P_{m}\|_{L^{1}(Q_{T})}\leq C(T).

Proof.

We cannot work directly on
\partial_{t}P_{m}
because of the power arising in a remainder term, and thus we use
\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m+1}
.
For this reason, we rewrite the cell density equation (
1.1
) with two formulas

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{m}=\rho_{m}(\Delta P_{m}+\rho_{m})+\nabla\rho_{m}\cdot(\nabla P_{m}+\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}),

(4.25)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{m}=\Delta\rho_{m}^{m}+\rho_{m}^{2}+\nabla\rho_{m}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m},

(4.26)

and we give two useful equations

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m+1}=(m+1)\rho_{m}^{m+1}(\Delta P_{m}+\rho_{m})+\nabla\rho_{m}^{m+1}\cdot(\nabla P_{m}+\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}),

(4.27)

\displaystyle\Delta\rho_{m}^{m+1}=\frac{m+1}{m}(\rho_{m}\Delta\rho_{m}^{m}+\nabla\rho_{m}\cdot\nabla\rho_{m}^{m}).

(4.28)

With the help of Kato’s inequality, we differentiate Eq. (
4.27
)
with respect to the time and multiply this by
-sgn(|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}|_{-})
, then
it holds

\displaystyle\partial_{t}

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m+1}|_{-}\leq(m+1)\Big[|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m+1}|_{-}[\Delta P_{m}+\rho_{m}]+\rho_{m}^{m+1}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}|_{-}+\rho_{m}^{m+1}\Delta|\partial_{t}P_{m}|_{-}\Big]

\displaystyle+\nabla|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m+1}|_{-}\cdot\nabla[P_{m}+\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}]+\nabla\rho_{m}^{m+1}\cdot\nabla|\partial_{t}P_{m}|_{-}-sgn_{-}(\partial_{t}\rho_{m})\nabla\rho_{m}^{m+1}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\partial_{t}\rho_{m}

and after integration by parts on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and insertion of Eq. (
4.28
), we find

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m+1}|_{-}dx\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m+1}|_{-}[m\Delta P_{m}+(m+1)\rho_{m}]dx+m\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}P_{m}|_{-}\Delta\rho_{m}^{m+1}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\rho_{m}^{m+1}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\partial_{t}\rho_{m}|dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(m+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m+1}|_{-}\rho_{m}dx+(m+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m}|_{-}\rho_{m}\Delta P_{m}dx

\displaystyle+(m+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m}|_{-}\Delta\rho_{m}^{m}dx+(m+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}P_{m}|_{-}\nabla\rho_{m}\cdot\nabla\rho_{m}^{m}dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\rho_{m}^{m+1}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\partial_{t}\rho_{m}|dx.

We insert Eqs. (
4.25
)–(
4.26
) into this inequality, and use Eq. (
3.9
) for the last term.

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m+1}|_{-}dx\leq(m+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m+1}|_{-}\rho_{m}dx+(m+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}P_{m}|_{-}\nabla\rho_{m}\cdot\nabla\rho_{m}^{m}dx

\displaystyle+(m+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m}|_{-}(\partial_{t}\rho_{m}-\rho_{m}^{2}-\nabla\rho_{m}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}-\nabla\rho_{m}\cdot\nabla P_{m})dx

\displaystyle+(m+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m}|_{-}(\partial_{t}\rho_{m}-\rho_{m}^{2}-\nabla\rho_{m}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m})dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla\rho_{m}^{m}\cdot\nabla\rho_{m}^{m+1}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\rho_{m}^{m+1}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast(\nabla\cdot(\rho_{m}\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}))|dx.

The two terms with
(m+1)|\partial_{t}P_{m}|_{-}\nabla\rho_{m}\cdot\nabla\rho_{m}^{m}
and
-(m+1)|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m}|_{-}\nabla\rho_{m}\cdot\nabla P_{m}
cancel due to
|\partial_{t}P_{m}|_{-}\nabla\rho_{m}\cdot\nabla\rho_{m}^{m}=|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m}|_{-}\nabla\rho_{m}\cdot\nabla P_{m}
,
then it holds

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m+1}|_{-}dx+(m-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m+1}|_{-}\rho_{m}dx+2(m+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m}|_{-}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}|_{-}dx

(4.29)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\underbrace{2(m+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m}|_{-}\nabla\rho_{m}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}dx}_{\mathcal{A}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla\rho_{m}^{m}\cdot\nabla\rho_{m}^{m+1}dx

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\rho_{m}^{m+1}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast(\nabla\cdot(\rho_{m}\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}))|dx}_{\mathcal{B}}.

For
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=

\displaystyle 2(m+1)m\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{m}^{m-1}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}|_{-}\nabla\rho_{m}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(m+1)m\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{m}^{m-1}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}|_{-}^{2}dx+(m+1)m\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{m}^{m-1}|\nabla\rho_{m}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(m+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m}|_{-}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}|_{-}dx+(m+1)\|\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla\rho_{m}^{m}\cdot\nabla\rho_{m}dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\rho_{m}^{m+1}|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast(\nabla\cdot(\rho_{m}\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}))|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\rho_{m}^{m+1}|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}C(n)\|\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{m}^{2}dx.

From Lemma
3.1
in which we let
p=2
, we control in
L^{1}(Q_{T})
the term
\nabla\rho_{m}^{m}\cdot\nabla\rho_{m}
. The terms in
\mathcal{B}
are also controled thanks to the bounds (in particular in Lemma
4.2
), as well as the second term in final expression of
\mathcal{A}
. All together the known bounds reduce (
4.29
) to

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m+1}|_{-}dx+\frac{m^{2}-1}{m+2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m+2}|_{-}dx+(m+1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m}|_{-}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}|_{-}dx\leq(m+1)C(T).

Therefore, it holds

\int_{Q_{T}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m+2}|_{-}dxdt+\int_{Q_{T}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m}|_{-}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}|_{-}dxdt\leq C(T).

(4.30)

Taking account of Lemma
3.1
and Theorem
4.1
, we use Sobolev’s
inequality and obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{m}^{m+2}dx\leq

\displaystyle C\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{m}^{3}P_{m}dx\leq C\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\nabla P_{m}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+C(T)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(T).

Thus, combining the above inequality and (
4.30
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m+2}|dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Q_{T}}\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m+2}dxdt+2\int_{Q_{T}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m+2}|_{-}dxdt

(4.31)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{m}^{m+2}(T)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{m,0}^{m+2}dx+2\int\hskip-4.0pt\int_{Q_{T}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m+2}|_{-}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C(T).

Furthermore, combining (
4.31
), Lemma
3.2
, and Lemma
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}|\partial_{t}

\displaystyle\rho_{m}^{m+2}-\nabla\rho_{m}^{m+2}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}|dxdt

(4.32)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}\Big[|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m+2}|+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\rho_{m}^{m+2}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}|^{2}\Big]dxdt\leq C(T).

By Lemma
4.3
and (
4.32
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m-1}

\displaystyle-\nabla\rho_{m}^{m-1}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}|dxdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m-1}-\nabla\rho_{m}^{m-1}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}|\big[\chi_{\{\rho_{m}\leq\frac{1}{2}\}}+\chi_{\{\rho_{m}>\frac{1}{2}\}}\big]dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(m-1)\frac{1}{2^{m-2}}\int_{Q_{T}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}-\nabla\rho_{m}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}|dxdt

\displaystyle+\frac{8(m-1)}{m+2}\int_{Q_{T}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m+2}-\nabla\rho_{m}^{m+2}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}|dxdt\leq C(T).

Combining this with Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
, we end up with

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}P_{m}|dxdt\leq\int_{Q_{T}}|\partial_{t}P_{m}-\nabla P_{m}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}|dxdt+\int_{Q_{T}}|\nabla P_{m}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}|dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{m}{m-1}\int_{Q_{T}}|\partial_{t}\rho_{m}^{m-1}-\nabla\rho_{m}^{m-1}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}|dxdt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{Q_{T}}\big[|\nabla P_{m}|^{2}dxdt+|\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}|^{2}\big]dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(T),

where the first inequality is the application of triangle inequality and the
second inequality is due to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. The proof is completed.
∎

5
Complementarity relation and semi-harmonicity

Thanks to the a priori regularity estimates provided by Lemmas
3.1
–
3.3
and
Theorems
4.1
–
4.3
, we can obtain the strong compactness on the
pressure gradient, which allows us to obtain the complementarity relation. Moreover, the semi-harmonicity follows from the AB estimate (Theorem

).

Theorem 5.1

(Complementarity relation and semi-harmonicity)
.

Under the initial assumptions (
2.1
)–(
2.4
), then, the complementarity
relation and the semi-harmonicity property, see Eq. (
2.8
), hold.

Proof.

From Lemma
3.1
and Theorems
4.3
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla P_{m}\|_{L^{2}(Q_{T})}\leq C(T),\quad\|\partial_{t}P_{m}\|_{L^{1}(Q_{T})}\leq C(T).

Then, after the extraction of subsequences, we obtain

P_{m}\rightarrow P_{\infty},\text{ strongly in }L^{1}_{loc}(Q_{T}),\text{ as }m\to\infty,

with the help of Sobolev’s compactness embedding. From Theorem
4.1
, we
obtain the weak compactness of the pressure gradient, up to a subsequence, we
have

\nabla P_{m}\rightharpoonup\nabla P_{\infty},\text{ weakly in }L^{3}(Q_{T}),\text{ as }m\to\infty.

We define a smooth cutoff function
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
,
\varphi(x)=1
, for
|x|\leq 1
,
\varphi(x)=0
for
|x|\geq 2
. Then,
for any
R>0
, we let
\varphi_{R}(x)=\varphi(\frac{x}{R})
and
P_{m,R}=\varphi_{R}(x)P_{m}
. By direct computations, we obtain

\|\partial_{t}P_{m,R}\|_{L^{1}(Q_{T})}\leq C(T,R),\quad\|\nabla P_{m,R}\|_{L^{3}(Q_{T})}\leq C(T,R),\quad\|\Delta P_{m,R}\|_{L^{1}(Q_{T})}\leq C(T,R).

(5.1)

For the sake of the above three estimates (
5.1
), inspired by

[
24
, Theorem 6.1]
, we can establish

\nabla P_{m,R}\to\nabla P_{\infty,R},\text{ strongly in }L^{1}(Q_{T}),\text{ as }m\to\infty.

In other words, we can extract a subsequence such that

\nabla P_{m}\rightarrow\nabla P_{\infty},\text{ strongly in }L^{1}_{loc}(Q_{T}),\text{
as }m\to\infty.

Then, using the uniform
L^{3}
bound for the pressure gradient in
Theorem
4.1
, we have

\nabla P_{m}\rightarrow\nabla P_{\infty},\text{ strongly in }L^{q}_{loc}(Q_{T}),\text{
for }1\leq q<3.

Hence, in particular, the case
q=2
is selected to achieve our goal.

Let
\zeta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(Q_{T})
be a test function,
we multiply the pressure equation (
1.4
) by
\zeta
and integrate on
Q_{T}
,
then it follows

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{m-1}\int_{Q_{T}}

\displaystyle P_{m}\partial_{t}\zeta+|\nabla P_{m}|^{2}\zeta+\nabla P_{m}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m}\zeta dxdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}(-|\nabla P_{m}|^{2}\zeta-P_{m}\nabla P_{m}\cdot\nabla\zeta+P_{m}\rho_{m}\zeta)dxdt.

Hence, passing to limit as
m\to\infty
, we obtain the complementarity relation

\int_{Q_{T}}(-|\nabla P_{\infty}|^{2}\zeta-P_{\infty}\nabla P_{\infty}\cdot\nabla\zeta+P_{\infty}\rho_{\infty}\zeta)dxdt=0,

where
\rho_{m}P_{m}\rightharpoonup\rho_{\infty}P_{\infty},\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(Q_{T}),\text{ as }m\to\infty
results from (
3.15
).
This is equivalent to

\int_{Q_{T}}P_{\infty}(\Delta P_{\infty}+\rho_{\infty})\zeta dxdt=0,

which means that the complementarity relation of Eq. (
2.8
) holds.

From Theorem
4.2
, we have
\int_{Q_{T}}|\Delta P_{m}+\rho_{m}|_{-}^{3}dxdt\leq\frac{C(T)}{m}
. Let
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(Q_{T})
be a
nonnegative test function in
Q_{T}
, then we attain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Delta P_{\infty}+\rho_{\infty})\phi dxdt=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Delta P_{m}+\rho_{m})\phi dxdt

(5.2)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\Delta P_{m}+\rho_{m}|_{-}\phi dxdt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\Delta P_{m}+\rho_{m}|_{-}^{3}dxdt)^{\frac{1}{3}}(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi^{\frac{3}{2}}dxdt)^{\frac{2}{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Hence, we establish the second result (semi-harmonicity property) of Eq. (
2.8
).

The proof is completed.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

This result tells us that the limit solution satisfies

.

Under the initial assumptions (
2.1
)–(

), then we have

\rho_{\infty}\nabla P_{\infty}=\nabla P_{\infty},\quad\text{a.e. in }Q_{T}.

On the one hand, we have already proved in Theorem

that

6
Uniqueness, compact support and energy functional

In order to prove the uniqueness of the solution to the Hele-Shaw limit system
(
2.6
)–(
2.7
), we use the lifting method in
\dot{H}^{-1}
as in
[
6
,
3
,
23
]
rather than the duality method
[
54
,
25
]
or the entropy method
[
35
]
. The main new difficulty comes
from the nonlocal interaction. The uniform upper bound for the limit
density, and the property that the limit pressure is somehow monotone to the
limit density, allow us to use the energy method to prove the uniqueness as
in either
[
6
, Theorem 2.4]
or
[
3
, Theorem 3]
for an aggregation
equation with degenerate diffusion.

Proposition 6.1

(Uniqueness)
.

Let
(\rho_{1},P_{1})
and
(\rho_{2},P_{2})
be two solutions to the Cauchy problem
Eq. (
2.6
)–(
2.7
) with initial data satisfying

\rho_{1}(x,0)=\rho_{2}(x,0)\in\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then it follows

\rho_{1}=\rho_{2},\quad P_{1}=P_{2},\quad\text{a.e. in }Q.

Proof.

First of all, we state that the pressure is somehow monotone to the density. Since
\rho_{1}P_{1}=P_{1}
and
\rho_{2}P_{2}=P_{2}

) is radially decreasing symmetric, unique up to a translation, and compactly supported.

In the rest of this section, we carry on the incompressible limit of the stationary state of PKS (SPKS) model Eq. (
1.8
) in the framework of radial symmetry , and
C
is a positive constant independent of the exponent
m

For any given mass

Lemma 7.2

(Uniform bound of pressure)
.

Let
\rho_{m,s}
be a weak solution to the SPKS model Eq. (
1.8
) in the sense of Def.
1.1
with
\|\rho_{m,s}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=M>0
,
m\geq 3
, and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x\rho_{m,s}(x)dx=0
. Set
\alpha_{m}:=\rho_{m,s}(0)=\|\rho_{m,s}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, then it holds

\displaystyle\alpha_{m}\leq\frac{1+\sqrt{1+\frac{8M}{n(n-2)\omega_{n}}}}{2},\quad\alpha_{m}^{m-1}\leq\frac{1+\sqrt{1+\frac{8M}{n(n-2)\omega_{n}}}}{2}+\frac{2M}{n(n-2)\omega_{n}}.

Proof.

From the SPKS model Eq. (
1.8
), we have

\rho_{m,s}(\nabla P_{m,s}+\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m,s})=0.

Due to the radially decreasing symmetric property of
\rho_{m,s}
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

P_{m,s}=(-\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m,s}-C)_{+}\quad\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

(7.2)

and

C\leq\|-\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m,s}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(7.3)

Since
\alpha_{m}=\|\rho_{m,s}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\rho_{m,s}(0)
, we have

\displaystyle-\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m,s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n(n-2)\omega_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\rho_{m,s}(x-y)}{|y|^{n-2}}dy

(7.4)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n(n-2)\omega_{n}}\int_{|y|>1}\frac{\rho_{m,s}(x-y)}{|y|^{n-2}}dy+\alpha_{m}\frac{1}{n(n-2)\omega_{n}}\int_{|y|\leq 1}\frac{1}{|y|^{n-2}}dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n(n-2)\omega_{n}}M+\frac{\alpha_{m}}{2(n-2)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha_{m}}{2}+\frac{1}{n(n-2)\omega_{n}}M.

Combining (
7.2
)–(
7.4
), we obtain

\alpha_{m}^{m-1}\leq\frac{m}{m-1}\alpha_{m}^{m-1}\leq\alpha_{m}+\frac{2M}{n(n-2)\omega_{n}},

(7.5)

where
1\leq\frac{m}{m-1}\leq 2
for
m\geq 3
is used.

The positive solution to the quadratic equations with one unknownn
y^{2}_{2}=y_{2}+\frac{2M}{n(n-2)\omega_{n}}
is

y_{2}=\frac{1+\sqrt{1+\frac{8M}{n(n-2)\omega_{n}}}}{2}>1.

Thus it follows from the property of algebraic equation that

0\leq\alpha_{m}\leq y_{2}\quad\text{for }m\geq 3.

(7.6)

We combine (
7.5
)–(
7.6
) and obtain

\alpha_{m}^{m-1}\leq\alpha_{m}+\frac{2M}{n(n-2)\omega_{n}}\leq\frac{1+\sqrt{1+\frac{8M}{n(n-2)\omega_{n}}}}{2}+\frac{2M}{n(n-2)\omega_{n}}.

∎

Remark 7.2
.

It should be emphasized that

7.23

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\omega_{m,s}|_{-}^{3}dx\leq C/m.

From Lemma
7.4
with a given mass
M>0
, there exists a positive constant
R_{*}(M)
(only depending on
M
) such that

{\rm supp}(|\omega_{m,s}|_{-})\subset B_{R_{*}(M)},

then we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\omega_{m,s}|_{-}dx=\int_{B_{R_{*}(M)}}|\omega_{m,s}|_{-}dx\leq|B_{R_{*}(M)}|^{2/3}\big(\int_{B_{R_{*}(M)}}|\omega_{m,s}|_{-}^{3}dx\big)^{1/3}\leq C/m^{1/3},

so the second estimate of (
7.23
) holds. For the last estimate of (
7.23
), by triangle inequality and direct computations, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\Delta P_{m,s}|dx\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\Delta P_{m,s}+\rho_{m,s}|dx+M

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Delta P_{m,s}+\rho_{m,s})dx+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\omega_{m,s}|_{-}dx+M

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\omega_{m,s}|_{-}dx+2M

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C.

The proof is completed.
∎

Next, we turn to show the
L^{\infty}
estimate of the pressure gradient.

Lemma 7.7

(
L^{\infty}
estimate on pressure gradient)
.

Let
\rho_{m,s}
be a weak solution to the SPKS model Eq. (
1.8
) in the sense of Def.
1.1
with a given mass
M>0
. Then it holds for all
m\geq 3
that

\|\nabla P_{m,s}\|_{L^{\infty}\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C.

Proof.

Since

\rho_{m,s}\nabla P_{m,s}=-\rho_{m,s}\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m,s},

we have

\nabla P_{m,s}=-\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m,s}\quad\text{for }x\in{\rm supp}(P_{m,s}).

It follows from Lemma
7.5
that

|\nabla P_{m,s}|\leq C\quad\text{for }x\in{\rm supp}(P_{m,s}).

From Lemma
7.1
, there exists
R_{m}(M)\leq R_{*}(M)
for all
m\geq 3
such that

{\rm supp}(P_{m,s})=B_{R_{m}(M)}\subset B_{R_{*}(M)},

which together with the radially symmetric property of
P_{m,s}
(Lemma
7.1
) means

\|\nabla P_{m,s}\|_{L^{\infty}\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C.

∎

In the end, with the regularity estimates on
\rho_{m,s},P_{m,s}
, and
\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m,s}
, we are going to prove the incompressible (Hele-Shaw) limit of the SPKS model Eq. (
1.8
).

Lemma 7.8

(Incompressible limit)
.

Let
\rho_{m,s}
be a weak solution to the SPKS
Eq. (
1.8
) in the sense of Def.
1.1
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x\rho_{m,s}(x)dx=0
,
\|\rho_{m,s}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=M
, and
m\geq 3
. Then, after extracting the subsequence, there exist
P_{\infty,s},\nabla P_{\infty,s}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\displaystyle\nabla P_{m,s}\to\nabla P_{\infty,s},\quad\text{strongly in }L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ for }1\leq r<\infty,

\displaystyle\text{ as }m\to\infty,

(7.28)

\displaystyle P_{m,s}\to P_{\infty,s},\quad\quad\ \text{ strongly in }L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

\displaystyle\text{ as }m\to\infty.

(7.29)

Furthermore, there exists
\rho_{\infty,s}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\displaystyle\|\rho_{\infty,s}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=M,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x\rho_{\infty,s}dx=0,

(7.30)

\displaystyle 0\leq\rho_{\infty,s}\leq 1,

\displaystyle\text{a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{n},

(7.31)

\displaystyle(1-\rho_{\infty,s})P_{\infty,s}=0,

\displaystyle\text{a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{n},

(7.32)

\displaystyle(1-\rho_{\infty,s})\nabla P_{\infty,s}=0,

\displaystyle\text{a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{n},

(7.33)

\displaystyle\Delta P_{\infty,s}+\rho_{\infty,s}\geq 0,

\displaystyle\text{in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(7.34)

\displaystyle\nabla P_{\infty,s}+\rho_{\infty,s}\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{\infty,s}=0,

\displaystyle\text{in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(7.35)

Moreover, it holds for
R(M)>0
satisfying
|B_{R(M)}|_{n}=M
that

\rho_{\infty,s}=\chi_{\{P_{\infty,s}>0\}}=\chi_{B_{R(M)}}\quad\text{a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(7.36)

Proof.

Since
\|\nabla P_{m,s}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C
(Lemma
7.7
) and
{\rm supp}(P_{m,s})\subset B_{R_{*}(M)}
(Lemma
7.4
), then there exist
\nabla P_{\infty,s}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
{\rm supp}(P_{\infty,s})\subset B_{R_{*}(M)}
such that

\displaystyle\nabla P_{m,s}\rightharpoonup\nabla P_{\infty,s},\quad\text{weakly in }L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ for }1<r<\infty,\quad\text{as }m\to\infty.

(7.37)

Thanks to
\|\Delta P_{m,s}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C
(Lemma
7.6
) and
{\rm supp}(P_{m,s})\subset B_{R(M)}
(Lemma
7.4
), then it follows from the compactness criterion in
[
10
, (21)]
and
P_{m,s}\in W^{\infty}_{0}(B_{R_{*}(M)})
that

\nabla P_{m,s}\to\nabla P_{\infty,s},\quad\text{strongly in }L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ for }1\leq r<\infty,\quad\text{as }m\to\infty.

(7.38)

By Sobolev’s inequality for gradient (Theorem
C.3
), we obtain

\|P_{m,s}-P_{\infty,s}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|\nabla P_{m,s}-\nabla P_{\infty,s}\|_{L^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\to 0,\quad\text{as }m\to\infty.

So Eq. (
7.28
)–(
7.29
) hold.

In addition, Eq. (
7.30
) follows from
\|\rho_{m,s}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=M
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x\rho_{m,s}dx
=0, and
{\rm supp}(\rho_{m,s})\subset B_{R_{*}(M)}
show that as
m\to\infty

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{m,s}dx\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{\infty,s}dx=M,\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x\rho_{m,s}dx\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x\rho_{\infty,s}dx=0.

Since

\|\rho_{m,s}\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|\rho_{m,s}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{(r-1)/r}\|\rho_{m,s}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1/r}\leq\alpha_{m}^{(r-1)/r(m-1)}M^{1/r}\leq C^{(r-1)/r(m-1)}M^{1/r},

there exists
\rho_{\infty,s}\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
1<r<\infty
such that

\rho_{m,s}\rightharpoonup\rho_{\infty,s},\quad\text{weakly in }L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ for }1<r<\infty,\quad\text{as }m\to\infty.

According to the weak semi-continuity of
L^{r}
norm for
1<r<\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\|\rho_{\infty,s}\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq

\displaystyle\liminf\limits_{m\to\infty}\|\rho_{m,s}\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\liminf\limits_{m\to\infty}(\alpha_{m}^{m-1})^{(r-1)/r(m-1)}M^{1/r}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf\limits_{m\to\infty}(\frac{1+\sqrt{1+\frac{4M^{2}}{n^{2}(n-2)^{2}}}}{2}+\frac{2M}{n(n-2)\omega_{n}})^{(r-1)/r(m-1)}M^{1/r}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M^{1/r}.

We take
r\to\infty
and obtain
\|\rho_{\infty,s}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1
,
which shows Eq. (
7.31
).

Let
\varphi(x)\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a smooth test function. Due to the definition of weak solution (
1.1
), we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{m,s}\nabla P_{m,s}\cdot\nabla\varphi dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{m,s}\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{m,s}\cdot\nabla\varphi dx=0.

(7.39)

Passing (
7.39
) to limit, then we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{\infty,s}\nabla P_{\infty,s}\cdot\nabla\varphi dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{\infty,s}\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{\infty,s}\cdot\nabla\varphi dx=0.

(7.40)

Since
\rho_{m,s}=(\frac{m-1}{m}P_{m,s})^{1/(m-1)}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{\infty,s}P_{\infty,s}\varphi dx=\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{m,s}P_{m,s}\varphi dx

(7.41)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\frac{m-1}{m})^{1/(m-1)}(P_{m,s})^{m/(m-1)}\varphi dx=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}P_{\infty,s}\varphi dx.

Similarly, it holds for
i=1,...,n
that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{\infty,s}\partial_{i}P_{\infty,s}\varphi dx=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho_{m,s}\partial_{i}P_{m,s}\varphi dx

(7.42)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\frac{m-1}{m})^{m/(m-1)}P_{m,s}^{m/(m-1)}\partial_{i}\varphi dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}P_{\infty,s}\partial_{i}\varphi dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{i}P_{\infty,s}\varphi dx.

Using
\rho_{\infty,s},P_{\infty,s},\nabla P_{\infty,s}\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
1<r<\infty
, Eqs. (
7.32
)–(
7.33
) follows from (
7.41
)–(
7.42
).

From Lemma
7.6
, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Delta P_{m,s}+\rho_{m,s})\varphi dx\geq-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\omega_{m,s}|_{-}\varphi dx\geq-C\||\omega_{m,s}|_{-}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\geq-C/m^{1/3},

which means that, after taking the limit, Eq. (
7.34
) holds for a nonnegative smooth test function as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\Delta P_{\infty,s}+\rho_{\infty,s})\varphi dx\geq 0.

Combining (
7.40
) andf40, the last statement Eq. (
7.35
) is gotten as

\nabla P_{\infty,s}+\rho_{\infty,s}\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{\infty,s}=0,\quad\text{a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Since
\rho_{m,s}
is a solution to the SPKS model Eq. (
1.8
) with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x\rho_{m,s}(x)dx=0
,
\rho_{m,s}
and
P_{m,s}
are radially decreasing symmetric. Therefore,
\rho_{\infty,s}
and
P_{\infty,s}
are radially decreasing symmetric, and
\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{\infty,s}
is radially symmetric (Lemma
7.1
). Since
\rho_{\infty,s}P_{\infty,s}=P_{\infty,s}
, there exists
R_{1}\leq R_{2}
such that

{\rm supp}(P_{\infty,s})=B_{R_{1}},\quad{\rm supp}(\rho_{\infty,s})=B_{R_{2}}.

If
R_{1}<R_{2}
, it follows from Lemma
7.8
that

\rho_{\infty,s}(r)\frac{\partial}{\partial_{r}}\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{\infty,s}(r)=0\quad\text{for }x\in(R_{1},R_{2}).

(7.43)

However, it follows from
[
37
, (2.2)]
that for
r>0

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{\infty,s}(r)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\partial B_{r}|_{n-1}}\int_{\partial B_{r}}|\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{\infty,s}|dS_{x}=\frac{1}{|\partial B_{r}|_{n-1}}\int_{\partial B_{r}}\nabla\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{\infty,s}\cdot\nu dS_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\partial B_{r}|_{n-1}}\int_{B_{r}}\Delta\mathcal{N}\ast\rho_{\infty,s}dx\frac{1}{|\partial B_{r}|_{n-1}}\int_{B_{r}}\rho_{\infty,s}dx>0,

which contradicts (
7.43
). Thus, we have
R_{1}=R_{2}
, and the last statement Eq. (
7.36
) is proved.
∎

8
Conclusion, extensions and perspectives

In order to prove the incompressible limit of the Patlak-Keller-Segel system, and
establish the corresponding Hele-Shaw free boundary equation, we have followed
the same lines of proof as initiated in
[
54
]
, with the gradient estimate as
in
[
24
]
. This has the advantage to also prove optimal second order
estimates. A fundamental new ingredient is a uniform
L^{1}
estimate on the time
derivative of pressure. With this new estimate, another possible route to
establish the complementarity condition, the hard part of the problem, would be
to use the pure compactness method in
[
46
,
55
]
. Still another possible
route is through the obstacle problem, see
[
33
]
and the references therein.
We have also established uniqueness, finite propagation speed, and limit energy
functional of solutions to this Hele-Shaw type free boundary problem. In addition, we have studied the incompressible limit for the stationary state of the PKS model, which is new for the diffusion-aggregation equations.

We would like to point out that our analysis for the PKS model is compatible with growth terms, as
they appear naturally when dealing with mechanical models of tumor growth, even
though the technical details have to be checked. Also we treated dimension

n\geq 3
to avoid technical issues with the Sobolev inequalities but we do not
expect difficulties in two dimensions.

Several papers have treated of linear drift terms, see
[
38
,
25
]
. But it is
difficult to extend these cases or Newtonian potential to more general attractive
potential, because our proof of the time derivative estimate of pressure strongly
depends on the structure of Newtonian potential. Among open problems, let us also
mention the convergence rate with
m\to\infty
, which has been obtained in few
papers,
[
1
,
23
]
. Finally the case of systems is only treated without drift, see
[
12
,
46
]
. Large time asymptotic of solutions to the Hele-Shaw system (
2.6
)–(
2.7
) is an interesting topic.

[
21
]
treated the 2-dimensional Hele-Shaw model when the initial density is a patch function.
But that for the n-dimensional (
n\geq 3
) Hele-Shaw case is still largely open. The regularity of free boundary for a
Hele-Shaw problem of tumor growth was obtained in
[
52
]
, also for the porous medium equation with an external drift, cf.
[
38
]
.

(A.6)

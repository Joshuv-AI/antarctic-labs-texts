The number and location of eigenvalues for the two-particle Schrödinger operators on lattices
Saidakhmat N. Lakaev, Mukhayyo O. Akhmadova

Abstract
. We study the Schrödinger operators H γ ​ λ ​ μ ​ ( K ) H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K) , K ∈ 𝕋 K\in\mathbb{T} being a fixed (quasi)momentum of the particles pair, associated with a system of two identical bosons on the one-dimensional lattice ℤ \mathbb{Z} , where the real quantities γ \gamma , λ \lambda and μ \mu describe the interactions between pairs of particles on one site, two nearest neighboring sites and next two neighboring sites, respectively. We found a partition of the three-dimensional space ( γ , λ , μ ) (\gamma,\lambda,\mu) of interaction parameters into connected components and the exact number of eigenvalues of this operator that lie below and above the essential spectrum, in each component. Moreover, we show that for any K ∈ 𝕋 d K\in\mathbb{T}^{d} the number of eigenvalues of H γ ​ λ ​ μ ​ ( K ) H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K) is not less than the corresponding number of eigenvalues of H γ ​ λ ​ μ ​ ( 0 ) H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(0) .

2.
The two-particle Hamiltonian

2.1.
The position-space representation

Let
\mathbb{Z}
be the one-dimensional lattice and
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
be cartesian square of
\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
\ell^{2,s}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z})
be the Hilbert space of square-summable
symmetric functions on
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
.

The free Hamiltonian
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{0}
of a system of two identical particles (bosons), in the position space representation, is usually associated with the following self-adjoint (bounded) Toeplitz-type operator on the Hilbert space
\ell^{2,s}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z})
:

(\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{0}\widehat{f})(x_{1},x_{2})=\sum_{s_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\varepsilon}(x_{1}-s_{1})\widehat{f}(s_{1},x_{2})+\sum_{s_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\varepsilon}(x_{2}-s_{2})\widehat{f}(x_{1},s_{2}),\,\,\widehat{f}\in\ell^{2,s}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z})

where

(2.1)

\widehat{\varepsilon}(s)=\begin{cases}2&\text{if $|s|=0,$}\\
-\frac{1}{2}&\text{if $|s|=1,$}\\
0&\text{if $|s|>1.$}\end{cases}

The interaction operator
\widehat{\mathbb{V}}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}
, in the position space representation, is the multiplication
operator by the function
\widehat{v}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}),i.e.,

\widehat{\mathbb{V}}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}\widehat{f}(x,y)=\widehat{v}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(x-y)\widehat{f}(x,y),\,\,\widehat{f}\in\ell^{2,s}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}),

where

(2.2)

\widehat{v}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(s)=\begin{cases}2\gamma&\text{if $|s|=0,$}\\
\lambda&\text{if $|s|=1,$}\\
\mu&\text{if $|s|=2,$}\\
0&\text{if $|s|>2.$}\end{cases}

The total Hamiltonian

\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}
of a system of two identical particles is described as a bounded self-adjoint operator on
\ell^{2,s}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z})
:

\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}=\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{0}+\widehat{\mathbb{V}}_{\gamma\lambda\mu},\,\,\gamma,\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{R}.

2.2.
The two-particle Hamiltonian: the (quasi)momentum-space representation

Let
\mathbb{T}=\allowbreak\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\equiv(-\pi,\pi]
be the
one-dimensional torus, the Pontryagin dual group of
\mathbb{Z}
. Let
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
be
the Hilbert space of square-integrable functions on

\mathbb{T}
and let

{\mathcal{F}}:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{T}),\,\,{\mathcal{F}}\widehat{f}(p)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{f}(x)e^{ipx}.

is the
standard Fourier transform with the inverse

{\mathcal{F}}^{*}:L^{2}(\mathbb{T})\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}),\,\,{\mathcal{F}}^{*}f(p)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(x)e^{ipx}dx.

The free Hamiltonian
\mathbb{H}_{0}=({\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{F}})\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{0}({\mathcal{F}}^{*}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}^{*})
of a system of two identical particles,in the momentum space representation, is the multiplication operator by the function
\varepsilon(p)+\varepsilon(q)
in the Hilbert space
L^{2,s}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T})
of symmetric functions on the cartesian square
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}
of the torus
\mathbb{T}
:

\mathbb{H}_{0}f(p,q)=[\varepsilon(p)+\varepsilon(q)]f(p,q),

where the continuous function (dispersion relation)
\varepsilon
is given by

\varepsilon(p)=[{\mathcal{F}}\widehat{\varepsilon}](p)=1-\cos p,\quad p\in\mathbb{T}.

The interaction operator
\mathbb{V}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}=({\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{F}})\widehat{\mathbb{V}}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}({\mathcal{F}}^{*}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}^{*})
is the integral operator of convolution type acting in
L^{2,s}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T})
as

(\mathbb{V}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}f)(p,q)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}v_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(p-u)f(u,p+q-u)\mathrm{d}u,

where the kernel function
v_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(\cdot)
is given by

v_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(p)=[{\mathcal{F}}\widehat{v}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}](p)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{v}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(x)e^{ipx}=\frac{1}{\pi}(\gamma+\lambda\cos p+\mu\cos 2p),\quad p\in\mathbb{T}.

The total two-particle Hamiltonian
\mathbb{H}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}
of a system of two identical quantum-mechanical patricles interacting via a finite range attractive potentials
\widehat{v}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}
, in the momentum space representation, is the bounded self-adjoint operator acting in
L^{2,s}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T})
as

\mathbb{H}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}:=({\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{F}})\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}({\mathcal{F}}\otimes{\mathcal{F}})^{*}=\mathbb{H}_{0}+\mathbb{V}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}.

2.3.
The Floquet-Bloch decomposition of
\mathbb{H}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}
and discrete
Schrödinger operators

Since the operator
\widehat{H}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}
commutes with the shift operators on the lattice
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
, we can decompose the space
L^{2,s}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T})
and Hamiltonian
\mathbb{H}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}

into the von Neumann direct integrals as

(2.3)

L^{2,s}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T})\simeq\int\limits_{K\in\mathbb{T}}^{\oplus}L^{2,e}(\mathbb{T})\,\mathrm{d}K\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{H}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}\simeq\int\limits_{K\in\mathbb{T}}^{\oplus}H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K)\,\mathrm{d}K,

respectively, where
L^{2,e}(\mathbb{T})
is the Hilbert space of square-integrable even
functions on
\mathbb{T}
(see, e.g.,
[
3
]
).

The fiber operator
H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K),

K\in\mathbb{T}
is a
self-adjoint operator defined in
L^{2,e}(\mathbb{T})
as

H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K):=H_{0}(K)+V_{\gamma\lambda\mu},

where the unperturbed operator
H_{0}(K)
is the multiplication
operator by the function

{\mathcal{E}}_{K}(p):=2(1-\cos\tfrac{K}{2}\,\cos p)

and the perturbation
V_{\gamma\lambda\mu}
is defined as

V_{\gamma\lambda\mu}f(p)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\gamma+\lambda\cos p\cos q+\mu\cos 2p\cos 2q)f(q)\mathrm{d}q.

In some literature, the parameter
K
is called
quasi-momentum
and the fiber
H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K)

is called
discrete Schrödinger operator
associated to
the two-particle Hamiltonian
\mathbb{H}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}
.

Using the Fourier transform,
\ell^{2,s}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z})
and
\mathbb{H}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}

can also be rewritten as decompositions

\ell^{2,s}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z})\simeq\int\limits_{K\in\mathbb{T}}^{\oplus}\ell^{2,e}(\mathbb{Z})\,\mathrm{d}K\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{H}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}\simeq\int\limits_{K\in\mathbb{T}}^{\oplus}H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K)\,\mathrm{d}K,

respectively, where
\ell^{2,e}(\mathbb{Z})
is the Hilbert space of square-summable
even functions on
\mathbb{Z}
and

(2.4)

\widehat{H}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K):={\mathcal{F}}^{*}H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K){\mathcal{F}}=\widehat{H}_{0}(K)+\widehat{V}_{\gamma\lambda\mu},

and

\widehat{H}_{0}(K)\widehat{f}(x)\ =\ \sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{K}(x-s)f(s),\quad\widehat{f}\in\ell^{2,e}(\mathbb{Z}),

with

\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{K}(x)=2\widehat{\varepsilon}(x)\cos\frac{Kx}{2}

and the operator
\widehat{V}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}
acts in
\ell^{2,e}(\mathbb{Z})
as

\widehat{V}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}\widehat{f}(x)=\widehat{v}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(x)\widehat{f}(x).

2.4.
The essential spectrum of discrete Schrödinger operators

Since
V_{\gamma\lambda\mu}
is a finite rank operator, by well known Weyl’s Theorem (see
[
34
, Theorem XIII.14]
) for
any
K\in\mathbb{T}
the essential spectrum

\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K))
of
H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}
coincides with the spectrum of
H_{0}(K),
i.e.,

(2.5)

\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K))=\sigma(H_{0}(K))=[{\mathcal{E}}_{\min}(K),{\mathcal{E}}_{\max}(K)],

where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{\min}(K):=

\displaystyle\min_{p\in\mathbb{T}}\,{\mathcal{E}}_{K}(p)=2(1-\cos\tfrac{K}{2})\geq 0={\mathcal{E}}_{\min}(0),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{\max}(K):=

\displaystyle\max_{p\in\mathbb{T}}\,{\mathcal{E}}_{K}(p)=2(1+\cos\tfrac{K}{2})\leq 4={\mathcal{E}}_{\max}(0).

n_{-}({H}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(K))\geq n_{-}({H}_{\gamma\lambda\mu}(0))

Lemma 3.2

The set of points

System (

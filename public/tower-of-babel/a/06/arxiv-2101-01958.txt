Finding B c ​ ( 3 ​ S ) B_{c}(3S) States via Their Strong Decays
Rui Ding 1 , Bing-Dong Wan 1 , Zi-Qiang Chen 1,3 , Guo-Li Wang 2 , Cong-Feng Qiao 1,3

Abstract
The experimentally known B c B_{c} states are all below open bottom-charm threshold, which experience three main decay modes, and all induced by weak interaction. In this work, we investigate the mass spectrum and strong decays of the B c ​ ( 3 ​ S ) B_{c}(3S) states, which just above the threshold, in the Bethe-Salpeter formalism and P 0 3 {}^{3}P_{0} model. The numerical estimation gives M ⁡ ( B c ​ ( 3 1 ​ S 0 ) ) = 7273 ​ MeV M(B_{c}(3^{1}S_{0}))=7273\ {\rm MeV} , M ⁡ ( B c ∗ ​ ( 3 3 ​ S 1 ) ) = 7304 ​ MeV M(B_{c}^{*}(3^{3}S_{1}))=7304\ {\rm MeV} , Γ ⁡ ( B c ​ ( 3 1 ​ S 0 ) → B ∗ ​ D ) = 26.02 − 2.21 + 2.33 ​ MeV \Gamma\left(B_{c}(3^{1}S_{0})\to B^{*}D\right)=26.02^{+2.33}_{-2.21}\ {\rm MeV} , Γ ⁡ ( B c ∗ ​ ( 3 3 ​ S 1 ) → B ​ D ) = 3.39 − 0.26 + 0.27 ​ MeV \Gamma\left(B_{c}^{*}(3^{3}S_{1})\to BD\right)=3.39^{+0.27}_{-0.26}\ {\rm MeV} , Γ ⁡ ( B c ∗ ​ ( 3 3 ​ S 1 ) → B ∗ ​ D ) = 14.77 − 1.33 + 1.40 ​ MeV \Gamma\left(B_{c}^{*}(3^{3}S_{1})\to B^{*}D\right)=14.77^{+1.40}_{-1.33}\ {\rm MeV} and Γ ⁡ ( B c ∗ ​ ( 3 3 ​ S 1 ) → B ​ D ∗ ) = 6.14 − 0.54 + 0.58 ​ MeV \Gamma\left(B_{c}^{*}(3^{3}S_{1})\to BD^{*}\right)=6.14^{+0.58}_{-0.54}\ {\rm MeV} . Compared with previous studies in non-relativistic approximation, our results indicate that the relativistic effects are notable in B c ​ ( 3 ​ S ) B_{c}(3S) exclusive strong decays. According to the results, we suggest to find the B c ​ ( 3 ​ S ) B_{c}(3S) states in their hadronic decays to B B and D D mesons in experiment, like the LHCb.

In quantum field theory, the BS equation provides a basic description for bound states.
The BS wave function of a quark-antiquark bound state is defined as

\chi(x_{1},x_{2})=\langle 0|T\psi(x_{1})\bar{\psi}(x_{2})|P\rangle,

(1)

where
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are the coordinates of the quark and antiquark respectively,
P
is the momentum of the bound state,
T
denotes the time ordering operator.
The wave function in momentum space is

\chi_{P}(q)=e^{{-iP}\cdot X}\int d^{4}xe^{{-iq}\cdot x}\chi(x_{1},x_{2}),

(2)

where
q
is the relative momentum between the quark and antiquark.
The “center-of-mass coordinate”
X
and the “relative coordinate”
x
are defined as:

X=\frac{m_{1}}{m_{1}+m_{2}}x_{1}+\frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}+m_{2}}x_{2},~~~x=x_{1}-x_{2},

(3)

where
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
are the masses of the quark and antiquark respectively.
Then the bound state BS equation in momentum space reads

S^{-1}_{1}(p_{1})\chi_{{}_{P}}(q)S^{-1}_{2}(-p_{2})=i\int\frac{d^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}V(P;q,k)\chi_{{}_{P}}(k).

(4)

Here
S_{i}(\pm p_{i})=\frac{i}{\pm\not{p}_{i}-m_{i}}
denotes the fermion propagator;

V(P;q,k)
is the interaction kernel;

p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are the momenta of the quark and anti-quark respectively, which can be expressed as

p_{i}=\frac{m_{i}}{m_{1}+m_{2}}P+Jq,

(5)

where,
J=1
for the quark (
i=1
) and
J=-1
for the antiquark (
i=2
).
With the definitions
p_{i_{P}}\equiv\frac{P\cdot p_{i}}{M}
and
p^{\mu}_{i\perp}\equiv p_{i}^{\mu}-\frac{P\cdot p_{i}}{M^{2}}P^{\mu}
, the propagator
S_{i}(Jp_{i})
can be decomposed as

-iJS_{i}(Jp_{i})=\frac{\Lambda^{+}_{i}(q_{\perp})}{p_{i_{P}}-\omega_{i}+i\epsilon}+\frac{\Lambda_{i}^{-}(q_{\perp})}{p_{i_{P}}+\omega_{i}-i\epsilon},

(6)

where

\displaystyle\Lambda_{i}^{\pm}(q_{\perp})\equiv\frac{1}{2\omega_{i}}\left[\frac{\not{P}}{M}\omega_{i}\pm(\not{p}_{i\perp}+Jm_{i})\right],

(7)

\displaystyle\omega_{i}\equiv\sqrt{m_{i}^{2}-p_{i\perp}^{2}}.

Under the instantaneous approximation, the interaction kernel in the center of mass frame takes the form
V(P;q,k)|_{\vec{P}=0}\approx V(q_{\perp},k_{\perp})
. Then the BS equation can be reduced to

\displaystyle\chi_{{}_{P}}(q)=S_{1}(p_{1})\eta_{{}_{P}}(q_{\perp})S_{2}(-p_{2}),

(8)

with

\eta_{{}_{P}}(q_{\perp})=\int\frac{d^{3}k_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{3}}V(q_{\perp},k_{\perp})\varphi_{{}_{P}}(k_{\perp}),

(9)

where
\varphi_{{}_{P}}(q^{\mu}_{\perp})\equiv i\int\frac{dq_{{}_{P}}}{2\pi}\chi_{{}_{P}}(q)
is the 3-dimensional BS wave function.
By introducing the notation
\varphi^{\pm\pm}_{{}_{P}}(q_{\perp})
as:

\varphi^{\pm\pm}_{{}_{P}}(q_{\perp})\equiv\Lambda^{\pm}_{1}(q_{\perp})\frac{\not{P}}{M}\varphi_{{}_{P}}(q_{\perp})\frac{\not{P}}{M}\Lambda^{{\pm}}_{2}(q_{\perp}),

(10)

the wave function can be decomposed as

\varphi_{{}_{P}}(q_{\perp})=\varphi^{++}_{{}_{P}}(q_{\perp})+\varphi^{+-}_{{}_{P}}(q_{\perp})+\varphi^{-+}_{{}_{P}}(q_{\perp})+\varphi^{--}_{{}_{P}}(q_{\perp}).

(11)

And the BS equation (
8
) can be decomposed into four equations

\displaystyle(M-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})\varphi^{++}_{{}_{P}}(q_{\perp})=\Lambda^{+}_{1}(q_{\perp})\eta_{{}_{P}}(q_{\perp})\Lambda^{+}_{2}(q_{\perp}),

(12)

\displaystyle(M+\omega_{{}_{1}}+\omega_{2})\varphi^{--}_{p}(q_{\perp})=-\Lambda^{-}_{1}(q_{\perp})\eta_{{}_{P}}(q_{\perp})\Lambda^{-}_{2}(q_{\perp}),

(13)

\displaystyle\varphi^{+-}_{{}_{P}}(q_{\perp})=\varphi^{-+}_{{}_{P}}(q_{\perp})=0.

(14)

To solve the BS equation, one must have a good command of the potential between two quarks.
According to lattice QCD calculations, the potential for a heavy quark-antiquark pair in the static limit is well described by a long-ranged linear confining potential (Lorentz scalar
V_{S}
) and a short-ranged one gluon exchange potential (Lorentz vector
V_{V}
)
la
;
lb
;
Qiao:1996re
:

\displaystyle{V(r)}={V_{S}(r)+\gamma_{\mu}\otimes\gamma^{\mu}V_{V}(r)},

\displaystyle{V_{S}(r)}={\lambda r\frac{(1-e^{-\alpha r})}{\alpha r}}+V_{0},

\displaystyle{V_{V}(r)}=-{\frac{4}{3}}{\frac{\alpha_{s}(r)}{r}}e^{-\alpha r}.

(15)

Here, the factor
e^{-\alpha r}
is introduced not only to avoid the infrared divergence but also to incorporate the color screening effects of the dynamical light quark pairs on the “quenched” potential
t13
.
The potentials in momentum space are

\displaystyle V(\vec{p})=(2\pi)^{3}V_{S}(\vec{p})+\gamma_{\mu}\otimes\gamma^{\mu}(2\pi)^{3}V_{V}(\vec{p}),

\displaystyle V_{S}(\vec{p})=-\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}+V_{0}\Big)\delta^{3}(\vec{p})+\frac{\lambda}{\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{(\vec{p}^{\;2}+\alpha^{2})^{2}},

\displaystyle V_{V}(\vec{p})=-\frac{2\alpha_{s}}{3\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{\vec{p}^{\;2}+\alpha^{2}}.

(16)

Here,
\alpha_{s}
is the strong coupling constant, the constants

, where

The transition amplitude for the OZI-allowed two body decay process (with the momenta assigned as in Fig.

\displaystyle\langle P_{1}P_{2}|S|P\rangle=(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(P-P_{1}-P_{2})\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle\times\bar{\chi}_{{}_{P_{1}}}(q_{1})S^{-1}_{1}(p_{1})(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(p_{1}-p_{11})]

\displaystyle=-ig(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(P-P_{1}-P_{2})\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}{\rm Tr}[\chi_{{}_{P}}(q)S_{2}^{-1}(-p_{2})\bar{\chi}_{{}_{P_{2}}}(q_{2})\bar{\chi}_{{}_{P_{1}}}(q_{1})S_{1}^{-1}(p_{1})],

\displaystyle=-ig\int\frac{d^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}{\rm Tr}[\chi_{{}_{P}}(q)S_{2}^{-1}(-p_{2})\bar{\chi}_{{}_{P_{2}}}(q_{2})\bar{\chi}_{{}_{P_{1}}}(q_{1})S_{1}^{-1}(p_{1})]

1.

In the

is the energy of the created light quark.
This factor reduces to

Since the parameter

our widths are in fact suppressed by

By multiplying the compensation factor

In this sense, our results are compatible with those of Ref.

2.

In Ref.

, the coupling constant

This may lead to

3.

The relativistic effect of the wave functions is non-negligible, as discussed in Ref.

.

4.

The masses of

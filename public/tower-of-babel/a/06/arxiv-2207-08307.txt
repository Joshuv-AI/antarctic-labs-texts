An Efficient Randomized Fixed-Precision Algorithm for Tensor Singular Value Decomposition
Salman Ahmadi-Asl

Abstract
The existing randomized algorithms need an initial estimation of the tubal rank to compute a tensor singular value decomposition. This paper proposes a new randomized fixed-precision algorithm which for a given third-order tensor and a prescribed approximation error bound, automatically finds an optimal tubal rank and the corresponding low tubal rank approximation. The algorithm is based on the random projection technique and equipped with the power iteration method for achieving a better accuracy. We conduct simulations on synthetic and real-world datasets to show the efficiency and performance of the proposed algorithm.

\underline{\bf B}\in{\mathbb{R}^{(R+P)\times{I_{2}}\times{I_{3}}}}

[

A closely related concept is the

\underline{\bf X}
do not have fast decaying singular values. In this situation, instead of the data tensor

\underline{\bf X}
and
\underline{\bf Y}

\underline{\bf Y}

Lemma 1
.

For the linear operator defined as follow

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathcal{L}:{\mathcal{V}}\to{\mathcal{V}}\\
\\
\mathcal{L}\left(\underline{\bf X}\right)=\underline{\bf Q}*\underline{\bf X},\end{array}

where
\mathcal{V}
is the space of third-order tensors,
the adjoint of
\mathcal{L}
is
{\mathcal{L}^{adj}}\left(\underline{\bf X}\right)=\underline{\bf Q}^{T}*\underline{\bf X}.

Proof.

From the following straightforward computations, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle{\underline{\bf Q}*\underline{\bf X},\underline{\bf Y}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle{{\rm circ}\left(\underline{\bf Q}\right){\rm unfold}\left(\underline{\bf X}\right),{\rm unfold}\left(\underline{\bf Y}\right)}\right\rangle

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle{{\rm unfold}\left(\underline{\bf X}\right),{\rm circ}{{\left(\underline{\bf Q}\right)}^{T}}{\rm unfold}\left(\underline{\bf Y}\right)}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle{\underline{\bf X},{\underline{\bf Q}^{T}}*\underline{\bf Y}}\right\rangle.

In deriving (
9
), note
{\rm circ}\left({{\underline{\bf Q}^{T}}}\right)={\left({{\rm circ}\left(\underline{\bf Q}\right)}\right)^{T}}.
This completes the proof.
∎

Using Lemma (
1
), it can be easily proved that multiplying a tensor with an orthogonal tensor does not change its Frobenius norm because

\displaystyle\left\|{\underline{\bf Q}*\underline{\bf B}}\right\|_{F}^{2}=\left\langle{\underline{\bf Q}*\underline{\bf B},\underline{\bf Q}*\underline{\bf B}}\right\rangle=\left\langle{{\underline{\bf Q}^{T}}*\underline{\bf Q}*\underline{\bf B},\underline{\bf B}}\right\rangle=\left\|\underline{\bf B}\right\|_{F}^{2}.

IV
Proposed approach

As discussed earlier, the randomized algorithms proposed in (
[
34
,
35
,
36
,
50
]
) require an estimation of the tubal rank which may be a difficult task. To solve this limitation, we propose a new randomized fixed-precision or adaptive algorithm which for a given approximation error bound, it can find an optimal tubal rank of the underlying data tensor and corresponding low tubal rank approximation, automatically. To be more precise, let
\underline{\bf X}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times I_{2}\times I_{3}}
be a given data tensor and
\epsilon
be a given error bound. We aim at finding a tubal rank
R
for which we have

\|\underline{\bf X}-\underline{\bf Q}*\underline{\bf B}\|\leq\epsilon,

(10)

where
\underline{\bf Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times R\times I_{3}}
and
\underline{\bf B}=\underline{\bf Q}^{T}*\underline{\bf X}\in\mathbb{R}^{R\times I_{2}\times I_{3}}
. Then the t-SVD of the tensor
\underline{\bf X}
can be recovered from computing the truncated t-SVD of the smaller tensor
\underline{\bf B}
as discussed in Section
III
.

Our algorithm is a generalization of the randomized fixed-precision algorithm developed in
[
37
]
which is a modification and improved version of the fixed-precision algorithm proposed in
[
38
]
. Similar to the idea presented in
[
37
]
, we propose to gradually increase the size of the second and first modes of
\underline{\bf Q}
and
\underline{\bf B}

10

II
, this means that the new block tensors are concatenated along the second and the first modes of the current blocks
\underline{\bf Q}
and
\underline{\bf B}
, respectively. For a better understanding of the proposed algorithm, let us first start with the randomized blockQB algorithm developed in
[
38
]
for low rank matrix approximation and summarized in Algorithm

and a target rank

\displaystyle=

Inspired by

, in the next theorem, we present an elegant way for computing the residual error term.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\underline{\bf X}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times I_{2}\times I_{3}}
be a given tensor,
\underline{\bf Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{I_{1}\times R\times I_{3}}
be an orthogonal tensor
(R<I_{2}),
and
\underline{\bf B}={\underline{\bf Q}^{T}}*\underline{\bf X}\in\mathbb{R}^{{R\times{I_{2}}\times{I_{3}}}}
then

\left\|{\underline{\bf X}-\underline{\bf Q}*\underline{\bf B}}\right\|_{F}^{2}=\left\|\underline{\bf X}\right\|_{F}^{2}-\left\|\underline{\bf B}\right\|_{F}^{2}.

Proof.

Considering the following straightforward computations

\displaystyle\left\|{\underline{\bf X}-\underline{\bf Q}*\underline{\bf B}}\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle{\underline{\bf X}-\underline{\bf Q}*\underline{\bf B},\underline{\bf X}-\underline{\bf Q}*\underline{\bf B}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\underline{\bf X}\right\|_{F}^{2}-2\left\langle{\underline{\bf X},\underline{\bf Q}*\underline{\bf B}}\right\rangle+\left\|\underline{\bf B}\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\underline{\bf X}\right\|_{F}^{2}-2\left\langle{\underline{\bf Q}^{T}*\underline{\bf X},\underline{\bf B}}\right\rangle+\left\|\underline{\bf B}\right\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\underline{\bf X}\right\|_{F}^{2}-\left\|\underline{\bf B}\right\|_{F}^{2}.

the theorem is proved.
∎

From Theorem
2

\underline{\bf X}-\underline{\bf Q}*\underline{\bf B}
and its Frobenius norm, we only need to compute the Frobenius norm of the tensor

\underline{\bf X}

\underline{\bf Q}_{i}=[\underline{\bf Q}^{(1)},\underline{\bf Q}^{(2)},\ldots,\underline{\bf Q}^{(i)}],\,\,\,\,\,\underline{\bf B}_{i}=\underline{\bf B}_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}\underline{\bf B}^{(1)}\\
\underline{\bf B}^{(2)}\\
\vdots\\
\underline{\bf B}^{(i)}\end{bmatrix}

Proof.

By the following straightforward computations and using Theorem

\displaystyle{E}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\underline{\bf X}\right\|_{F}^{2}-\left\|{{\underline{\bf B}_{i}}}\right\|_{F}^{2}=\left\|\underline{\bf X}\right\|_{F}^{2}-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i-1}{\left\|{{\underline{\bf B}^{(j)}}}\right\|_{F}^{2}}-\left\|{{\underline{\bf B}^{(i)}}}\right\|_{F}^{2}

V
Computational complexity

As discussed in
[
34
]
, the computational complexity of Algorithm
2
for a tensor of Size
I\times J\times K
is

\mathcal{O}(IJK{\rm log}(K))+\mathcal{O}(IJK{\rm min}(I,J))
where the first term is for transforming
the tensor to the Fourier domain while the second is for tensor decomposition. In contrast, the
complexity of randomized Algorithm
4
is
\mathcal{O}(IJKb)
which for
b\ll{\rm min}\{I,J\}
is significantly lower.

VII
Conclusion and future works

In this paper, we proposed a new randomized fixed-precision algorithm for fast computation of tensor SVD (t-SVD). Unlike the existing randomized low tubal rank approximation methods, the proposed algorithm finds an optimal tubal rank and the corresponding low tubal rank approximation automatically given a data tensor and an approximation error bound. Simulations on synthetic and real-world data-sets confirmed that the proposed algorithm is efficient and applicable. This algorithm can be generalized to higher order tensors according to the paper

. This will be our future work and we are planning to use it to develop fast tensor completion algorithms similar to the strategy done in

. A detailed theoretical analysis of the proposed algorithm needs to be investigated. This is also our future work.

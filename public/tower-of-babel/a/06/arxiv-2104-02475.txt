Solving Large Scale Quadratic Constrained Basis Pursuit
Jirong Yi

Abstract




We solve

\displaystyle\min_{x}\|x\|_{1},{\rm s.t.\ }\|y-Ax\|_{2}\leq\eta,

(0.1)

where
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m},A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}(m<d)
, and
\eta>0
are known. We will adopt the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) and the idea of operator splitting to design efficient algorithm for solving the above quadratically constrained basis pursuit problem
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
.

1
Theoretical guarantees

We reformulate (
0.1
) as

\displaystyle\min_{x,z}g(x)+f(z),{\rm\ s.t.\ }Ax=z,

(1.1)

where
g(x)=\|x\|_{1}
, and
f(z):\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
is an indicator function defined as

\displaystyle f(z):=\begin{cases}0,z\in\Omega,\\
\infty,z\notin\Omega,\end{cases}\Omega=\{z:\|z-y\|_{2}\leq\eta\}.

(1.2)

The Lagrangian dual of (
1.2
) is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(v)

\displaystyle:=\min_{x,z}\left(g(x)+f(z)+v^{T}(Ax-z)\right)

\displaystyle=-f^{*}(v)-g^{*}(-A^{T}v),

(1.3)

where
v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the dual variable,
f^{*}(v)
is the convex conjugate of
f(z)
at
v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and
g^{*}(-A^{T}v)
is the convex conjugate of
g(x)
at
-A^{T}v
, i.e.,

\displaystyle g^{*}(-A^{T}v):=\begin{cases}0,-A^{T}v\in\Omega^{\prime},\\
\infty,-A^{T}v\notin\Omega^{\prime},\\
\end{cases}\Omega^{\prime}:=\{x:\|x\|_{\infty}\leq 1\}

(1.4)

and

\displaystyle f^{*}(v):=y+\frac{\eta}{\|v\|_{2}}v.

(1.5)

The dual problem can be formulated as

\displaystyle\max_{v,\mu}-f^{*}(v)-g^{*}(\mu),{\rm\ s.t.\ }-A^{T}v=\mu.

(1.6)

Assume the Slater’s condition holds, i.e., there exists
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\|y-Ax\|_{2}<\eta
, then the convexity of problem (
0.1
) implies that the optimal solution will achieve zero duality gap, i.e.,

\displaystyle g(x)+f(z)=-f^{*}(v)-g^{*}(\mu).

(1.7)

From KKT conditions, the optimal solution must satisfy (
1.7
) and

\displaystyle Ax=z,-A^{T}v=\mu.

(1.8)

Thus, the (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) can be used as optimality certificates or stopping criterion in algorithm design. More specifically, we define primal residual, dual residual, and duality gap with respect to a certain tuple
(x,z,v,\mu)
as

\displaystyle r_{p}:=\|Ax-z\|,r_{d}:=\|A^{T}v+\mu\|,\delta_{g}=g(x)+f(z)+f^{*}(v)+g^{*}(\mu).

(1.9)

2
Algorithm design based on ADMM

We adopt ideas from alternating projection methods, and reformulate (
1.1
) as

\displaystyle\min_{x,z,x^{\prime},z^{\prime}}g(x)+f(z)+I_{\mathcal{G}}(x^{\prime},z^{\prime}),{\rm\ s.t.\ }\left[\begin{matrix}x\\
z\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}x^{\prime}\\
z^{\prime}\end{matrix}\right],

(2.1)

where
I_{\mathcal{G}}(x^{\prime},z^{\prime})
is defined as

\displaystyle I_{\mathcal{G}}(x^{\prime},z^{\prime}):=\begin{cases}0,(x^{\prime},z^{\prime})\in\mathcal{G},\\
\infty,(x^{\prime},z^{\prime})\notin\mathcal{G},\end{cases}\mathcal{G}:=\{(x,z):Ax=z\}.

(2.2)

The augmented Lagrangian of (
2.2
) becomes

\displaystyle L_{\rho}(x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime},v):=g(x)+f(z)+I_{\mathcal{G}}(x^{\prime},z^{\prime})+V^{T}\left(\left[\begin{matrix}x\\
z\end{matrix}\right]-\left[\begin{matrix}x^{\prime}\\
z^{\prime}\end{matrix}\right]\right)+\frac{\rho}{2}\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}x\\
z\end{matrix}\right]-\left[\begin{matrix}x^{\prime}\\
z^{\prime}\end{matrix}\right]\right\|_{2}^{2},

(2.3)

where
V:=[v_{x}^{T}\ v_{z}^{T}]^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+m}
is the dual variable, and
\rho>0
is a parameter. Define

\displaystyle X:=\left[\begin{matrix}x\\
z\end{matrix}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{d+m},X^{\prime}:=\left[\begin{matrix}x^{\prime}\\
z^{\prime}\end{matrix}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{d+m},

(2.4)

and we get the iterations in ADMM are

\displaystyle\begin{cases}X^{k+1}:=\arg\min_{X}L_{\rho}(X,{X^{\prime}}^{k},V^{k}),\\
{X^{\prime}}^{k+1}:=\arg\min_{X^{\prime}}L_{\rho}(X^{k+1},{X^{\prime}},V^{k}),\\
v^{k+1}:=V^{k}+\rho\left(X^{k+1}-{X^{\prime}}^{k+1}\right).\end{cases}

(2.5)

More specifically,

\displaystyle X^{k+1}

\displaystyle:=\arg\min_{X}L_{\rho}(X,{X^{\prime}}^{k},V^{k})

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{matrix}\arg\min_{x}\left(g(x)+{v_{x}^{k}}^{T}(x-{x^{\prime}}^{k})+\frac{\rho}{2}\|x-{x^{\prime}}^{k}\|_{2}^{2}\right)\\
\arg\min_{z}\left(f(z)+{v_{z}^{k}}^{T}(z-{z^{\prime}}^{k})+\frac{\rho}{2}\|z-{z^{\prime}}^{k}\|_{2}^{2}\right)\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{matrix}\arg\min_{x}\left(g(x)+\frac{\rho}{2}\left\|x-\left({x^{\prime}}^{k}-\frac{v_{x}^{k}}{\rho}\right)\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)\\
\arg\min_{z}\left(f(z)+\frac{\rho}{2}\left\|z-\left({z^{\prime}}^{k}-\frac{v_{z}^{k}}{\rho}\right)\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)\end{matrix}\right]

(2.6)

or simply

\displaystyle X^{k+1}=\left[\begin{matrix}{\rm{prox}}_{g}({x^{\prime}}^{k}-\tilde{x}^{k})\\
{\rm{prox}}_{f}({z^{\prime}}^{k}-\tilde{z}^{k})\end{matrix}\right]

(2.7)

where
{\rm{prox}}_{g}(v)
is the proximator of function
g
at
v
which is defined as

\displaystyle{\rm{prox}}_{g}(v)

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{x}\left(g(x)+\frac{\rho}{2}\left\|x-v\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)

(2.8)

The
\tilde{x}^{k}
and
\tilde{z}^{k}
are defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{x}^{k}:=\frac{v_{x}^{k}}{\rho},\tilde{z}^{k}:=\frac{v_{z}^{k}}{\rho}.

(2.9)

More specifically, the proximator of
g
at
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is

\displaystyle{\rm{prox}}_{g}(v)=\mathcal{S}_{1/\rho}(v),

(2.10)

where
\mathcal{S}_{1/\rho}(\cdot)
is the elementwise soft thresholding function, i.e.,

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{S}_{1/\rho}(v)\right]_{i}:=\begin{cases}v_{i}-1/\rho,v_{i}>1/\rho,\\
0,|v_{i}|\leq 1/\rho,\\
v_{i}+1/\rho,v_{i}<-1/\rho.\end{cases}

(2.11)

The proximator of
f
at
v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is

\displaystyle{\rm{prox}}_{f}(v)=\frac{\eta}{\|v-y\|_{2}}(v-y)+y.

(2.12)

The updating rule for
{X^{\prime}}
can be specified as

\displaystyle{X^{\prime}}^{k+1}

\displaystyle:=\arg\min_{X^{\prime}}L_{\rho}(X^{k+1},{X^{\prime}},V^{k})

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}}v^{T}\left(\left[\begin{matrix}x\\
z\end{matrix}\right]-\left[\begin{matrix}x^{\prime}\\
z^{\prime}\end{matrix}\right]\right)+\frac{\rho}{2}\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}x\\
z\end{matrix}\right]-\left[\begin{matrix}x^{\prime}\\
z^{\prime}\end{matrix}\right]\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}}\frac{1}{2}\left\|X^{\prime}-\left(X^{k+1}+\frac{v^{k}}{\rho}\right)\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}}\frac{1}{2}\left\|x^{\prime}-(x^{k+1}+\tilde{x}^{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|z^{\prime}-(z^{k+1}+\tilde{z}^{k})\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\prod(x^{k+1}+\tilde{x}^{k},z^{k+1}+\tilde{z}^{k})

(2.13)

where
\prod(x,z)
is the projection of
(x,z)
onto
G
, i.e., the solution to

\displaystyle\min_{x^{\prime},z^{\prime}}\frac{1}{2}\|x^{\prime}-x\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|z^{\prime}-z\|_{2}^{2},{\rm\ s.t.\ }Ax^{\prime}=z^{\prime}.

(2.14)

Define

\displaystyle\tilde{X}:=\frac{V}{\rho}=\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{v_{x}}{\rho}\\
\frac{v_{z}}{\rho}\end{matrix}\right],

(2.15)

and the updating rule for dual variable
V
can be written as

\displaystyle\tilde{X}^{k+1}=\tilde{X}^{k}+\left(X^{k+1}-{X^{\prime}}^{k}\right).

(2.16)

2.1
Analytic solution to (
2.14
)

Since (
2.14
) is convex, from the KKT conditions of (
2.14
), we know that
x^{\prime},z^{\prime}
are the optimal solution to (
2.14
) if and only if there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}Ax^{\prime}=z^{\prime},\\
x^{\prime}-c+A^{T}\lambda=0,\\
z^{\prime}-d-\lambda=0,\end{cases}

(2.17)

which implies that the optimal
x^{\prime},z^{\prime}
can be obtained from solving the following linear system

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}A&-I_{m\times m}\\
I_{d\times d}&A^{T}\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}x^{\prime}\\
z^{\prime}\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}0\\
A^{T}z+x\end{matrix}\right]

(2.18)

Remarks:
(1) the matrix
\left[\begin{matrix}A&-I_{m\times m}\\
I_{d\times d}&A^{T}\end{matrix}\right]
is highly sparse, and this structure can be combined with other potential structured of
A
to simplify the computation; (2) even simple elimination can be used to simplify the problem, i.e.,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}z^{\prime}=(AA^{T}+I_{m\times m})^{-1}\left(AA^{T}z+Ax\right),\\
x^{\prime}=A^{T}(z-z^{\prime})+x,\end{cases}

(2.19)

or

\displaystyle\begin{cases}x^{\prime}=(A^{T}A+I_{d\times d})^{-1}(x+A^{T}z),\\
z^{\prime}=Ax^{\prime}.\end{cases}

(2.20)

Both the two matrices
AA^{T}+I_{m\times m}
and
A^{T}A+I_{d\times d}
are positive definite, thus factorization techniques can be used to accelerate the computation; (3) since
m<d
, the (
2.19
) will be more efficient; (4) apply Cholesky decomposition once to get
AA^{T}+I_{m\times m}=LL^{T}
; (5) calculate
AA^{T}
once; (6) solve for
z^{\prime}
backward, i.e.,
z^{\prime}=L^{-T}L^{-1}(AA^{T}z+Ax)
;

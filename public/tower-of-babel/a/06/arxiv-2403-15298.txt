On the matching complexes of categorical product of path graphs
Raju Kumar Gupta, Sourav Sarkar, Sagar S. Sawant, Samir Shukla

Abstract
. The matching complex 𝖬 ⁡ ( G ) \mathsf{M}(G) of a graph G G is a simplicial complex whose simplices are matchings in G G . These complexes appears in various places and found applications in many areas of mathematics including; discrete geometry, representation theory, combinatorics, etc. In this article, we consider the matching complexes of categorical product P n × P m P_{n}\times P_{m} of path graphs P n P_{n} and P m P_{m} . For m = 1 m=1 , P n × P m P_{n}\times P_{m} is a discrete graph and therefore its matching complex is the void complex. For m = 2 m=2 , 𝖬 ⁡ ( P n × P m ) \mathsf{M}(P_{n}\times P_{m}) has been proved to be homotopy equivalent to a wedge of spheres by Kozlov. We show that for n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 and 3 ≤ m ≤ 5 3\leq m\leq 5 , the matching complex of P n × P m P_{n}\times P_{m} is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of spheres. For m = 3 m=3 , we give a closed form formula for the number and dimension of spheres appearing in the wedge. Further, for m ∈ { 4 , 5 } m\in\{4,5\} , we give minimum and maximum dimension of spheres appearing in the wedge in the homotopy type of 𝖬 ⁡ ( P n × P m ) \mathsf{M}(P_{n}\times P_{m}) .

1 Introduction

A
matching
in a simple graph
G
is a collection of pairwise disjoint edges. The
matching complex

\mathsf{M}(G)
, of a graph
G
is the simplicial complex whose vertices are the edges, and simplicies are the matchings in
G
. In topological combinatorics, it is an important problem to study the topology of matching complexes of graphs. Matching complexes appeared first time in the thesis of Garst
[
Gar79
]
, where they obtained the matching complexes of complete bipartite graphs
K_{m,n}
as special coset complexes of symmetric groups
S_{n}
. Thereafter, matching complexes appeared and found applications in various places. The matching complexes of complete graphs appeared in the paper of Bouc
[
Bou92
]
in connection with Brown complexes and Quillen complexes. In
[
BLVv94
]
, Björner et al., described the matching complexes of complete bipartite graphs as chessboard complexes to study the geometry of admissible rook configurations on a general
m\times n
chessboard. In
[
vV92
]
,
\mathsf{M}(K_{m,n})
appeared as the complex of all partial, injective functions between
\{1,\ldots,m\}
to
\{1,\ldots,n\}
in connection to the problem of estimating the number of halving hyperplanes of a finite set of points in Euclidean space.
Other than the complete graphs and complete bipartite graphs, the classes of graphs for which matching complexes have been studied include; paths and cycles
[
Koz99
]
, trees
[
MT08
,
MJMV22
]
, polygonal tilings
[
Mat22
,
BJMV23
]
, honeycomb graphs
[
MT08
]
, etc. For more details on these complexes, we refer to a survey article by Wachs
[
Wac03
]
and Chapter 11 of the book
[
Jon08
]
by Jonsson.

Definition 1.1

2 Preliminaries

A
graph

G
is an ordered pair
(V(G),E(G))
, where
V(G)
is a finite set, called the set of vertices and
E(G)\subseteq{V(G)\choose 2}
, the
2
-element subsets of
V(G)
, called the set of edges of
G
. For the sake of brevity, we denote an edge
\{u,v\}
by
uv
. Given a graph
G=(V(G),E(G))
and a vertex
u
in
V(G)
, the
open neighborhood
of
u
is defined as the collection
\{v\in V(G):uv\in E(G)\}
and it is denoted
N(u)
. The
closed neighborhood
of a vertex
u
in the graph
G
is defined as
\{u\}\cup N(u)
and it is denoted by
N[u]
. A map
f:V(G)\rightarrow V(H)
is said to be a
graph homomorphism
if
f(v)f(w)\in E(H)
for all
vw\in E(G)
. An
isomorphism
is a graph homomorphism that is bijective, with its inverse also being a graph homomorphism. If such an isomorphism exists between two graphs
G
and
H
, they are called isomorphic, denoted as
G\cong H
. Let
S=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\}
be a collection of vertices in
V(G)
. Then, by
G\setminus S
we mean the induced subgraph of
G
on the vertex set
V(G)\setminus S
. In particular, if
S
is the singleton set
\{v_{1}\}
, then we denote the graph
G\setminus S
by
G\setminus v_{1}
. Similarly, for a collection of edges
T=\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{s}\}
, by
G+T
and
G-T
we mean the graphs on the vertex set
V(G)
with edge set
E(G)\cup T
and
E(G)\setminus T
, respectively. For more details about the graphs, one may refer to
[
Wes01
]
.

An
(abstract) simplicial complex

\Delta
on a vertex set
V
is a collection of subsets of
V
such that if
\sigma\in\Delta
and
\tau
is a subset of
\sigma
, then
\tau\in\Delta
. If
\sigma\in\Delta
and
|\sigma|=k+1
, then we say
\sigma
is a simplex of dimension
k
or a
k
-simplex. We further assume that the empty set is present in every simplicial complex as the only simplex of dimension
-1
. The
0
-dimensional simplices are called the vertices of
\Delta
, and the set of all vertices of a simplicial complex
\Delta
is denoted by
V(\Delta)
.

A
subcomplex
of a simplicial complex is a sub-collection of simplices that itself forms a simplicial complex. If
\Delta
is a simplicial complex and
\sigma
is a simplex in
\Delta
, then the
link
and the
deletion
of
\sigma
is defined as the subcomplexes
\{\tau\in\Delta:\sigma\cup\tau\in\Delta\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and}\hskip 2.84544pt\sigma\cap\tau=\emptyset\}
and
\{\tau\in\Delta:\tau\nsubseteq\sigma\}
, respectively.
The
join
of two simplicial complexes
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
, where
V(\Delta_{1})\cap V(\Delta_{2})=\emptyset
, is the simplicial complex
\Delta_{1}\ast\Delta_{2}:=\{\sigma\sqcup\tau:\sigma\in\Delta_{1}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and}\hskip 2.84544pt\tau\in\Delta_{2}\}
. In particular, if
a,b
are vertices that are not part of a simplicial complex
\Delta
, then the simplicial complexes
\{a\}\ast\Delta
and
\{a\}\ast\Delta\cup\{b\}\ast\Delta
are called the
cone
and the
suspension
of
\Delta
with the apexes
a
and
\{a,b\}
, respectively. We denote the suspension of
\Delta
with apex
\{a,b\}
by
\Sigma_{a,b}\Delta
(or
\Sigma\Delta
).

In this article, we consider any simplicial
complex as a topological space, namely its geometric realization. For the definition of
geometric realization, we refer to
[
Koz08
]
. For details about homotopy and topological background, one may refer to
[
Hat02
]
.

The following observation follows from the definition of the join of two simplicial complexes:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
G_{1}\sqcup G_{2}
denote the disjoint union of two graphs,

\mathrm{Ind}(G_{1}\sqcup G_{2})\simeq\mathrm{Ind}(G_{1})\ast\mathrm{Ind}(G_{2}).

Clearly, for each vertex

, the link and the deletion of the simplex

Ada12

Proposition 2.2

(Proposition 3.1,
[
Ada12
]
)
.

Let

be a vertex of a graph

\mathrm{Ind}(G\setminus N[v])\xhookrightarrow{}\mathrm{Ind}(G\setminus v)

\mathrm{Ind}(G)\simeq\mathrm{Ind}(G\setminus v)\vee\Sigma\mathrm{Ind}(G\setminus N[v]).

The following propositions exhibit homotopy equivalence between the independence complex of a graph

Proposition 2.3

(Lemma 2.4,
[

]
)

Let

\mathrm{Ind}(G)\simeq{}\mathrm{Ind}(G\setminus w).

Proposition 2.4

(Lemma 2.5,

]
)

Let

Proposition 2.5

(Proposition 3.4,
[
Ada12
]
)

Let

\mathrm{Ind}(G)\simeq\mathrm{Ind}(G\setminus uv)

M(P_{2n+3}\times P_{3})\simeq\mathrm{Ind}(\Lambda_{n,3})\ast\mathrm{Ind}(\tilde{\Lambda}_{n,3})\simeq\bigvee_{2^{n+2}-1}\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}.

∎

6 Future Directions

We proved that for
n\geq 3
and
3\leq m\leq 5
, the matching complex of
P_{n}\times P_{m}
is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of spheres. For
m=3
, we have given a closed-form formula for the number and dimension of spheres appearing in the wedge. Further, for
m\in\{4,5\}
, we have given the minimum and maximum dimension of spheres appearing in the wedge in
\mathsf{M}(P_{n}\times P_{m})
. This exploration naturally leads to the following question:

Question 6.1
.

Can we determine the dimensions of the spheres appearing in the wedge representing the homotopy types of the matching complexes of categorical products
P_{n}\times P_{4}
and
P_{n}\times P_{5}
?

Based on the main result of this article and our computer-based computations, we propose the following:

Conjecture 6.2
.

For
n\geq 3
and
m\geq 6
, the matching complex of categorical product
P_{n}\times P_{m}
is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of spheres.

For odd
m,n
, one component of
P_{n}\times P_{m}
is an induced subgraph of
P_{n}\Box P_{m}
(see
Figure

1
). Since the homotopy type of the matching complexes of
P_{n}\Box P_{m}
is not known for
m\geq 4
, it will be interesting to see if there is any relation between the topology of
\mathsf{M}(P_{n}\times P_{n})
and
\mathsf{M}(P_{n}\Box P_{m})
.

Question 6.3
.

Is there any correlation between the matching complexes of the cartesian product of path graphs and those of the categorical product of path graphs?

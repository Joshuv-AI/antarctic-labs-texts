Sublinear Rigidity of Lattices in Semisimple Lie Groups
Ido Grayevsky

Abstract
Let G G be a real centre-free semisimple Lie group without compact factors. I prove that irreducible lattices in G G are rigid under two types of sublinear distortions. The first result is that the class of lattices in groups that do not admit ℝ \mathbb{R} -rank 1 \ 1 factors is SBE complete : if Λ \Lambda is an abstract finitely generated group that is Sublinearly BiLipschitz Equivalent (SBE) to a lattice Γ ≤ G \Gamma\leq G , then Λ \Lambda can be homomorphically mapped into G G with finite kernel and image a lattice in G G . For such G G this generalizes the well known quasi-isometric completeness of lattices. The second result concerns sublinear distortions within G G itself, and holds without any restriction on the rank of the factors: if Λ ≤ G \Lambda\leq G is a discrete subgroup that sublinearly covers a lattice Γ ≤ G \Gamma\leq G , then Λ \Lambda is itself a lattice.

About the Proof

The proof of sublinear rigidity for
\mathbb{Q}

My proof exploits delicate geometric features of the symmetric space and its lattices, brings forth some of the beautiful geometry of horospheres, and highlights why sublinear distortions in this setting are very rigid. One thing to note is that the argument for sublinear rigidity is carried mostly within the symmetric space itself and not in some boundary object. Further below in this section I give a detailed description of the argument, which is quite involved and mostly geometric.

3.3.2
The Argument

A High-Level Description.

The core of the argument lies in elevating an SBE
f_{0}:X_{0}\rightarrow X_{0}
to an isometry

. There are two gaps to fill: first,
\Gamma
is non-uniform and so

is not even defined on the whole space

is just an SBE.

Assume for a moment that
\Gamma
is uniform and that

is defined on the whole space
X

to an isometry is done by considering the map
C(f):C(X)\rightarrow C(X)
that

[

allows one to conclude that

is, up to a scalar, an isometry. In turn, this isometry induces an isometry

on the spherical building structure of

. This is done by the relation between the Euclidean building structure of

and the spherical building structure of
\partial_{\infty}X
. A theorem of Tits
[

associates to
\partial q
a unique isometry

that induces
\partial f
as its boundary map. By construction, it is then not too difficult to see that

and

are ‘close’. In case
\Gamma
is non-uniform, an SBE

does not readily yield a cone map on

. Overcoming this difficulty requires substantial work and is the heart of Druţu’s proof. In short, she uses dynamical results stating that the vast majority of flats in

are close enough to
X_{0}
. As mentioned in Section
??
, the building structure on
X(\infty)
is determined by the boundaries of flats

. To some extent this is true also for the (Euclidean) building structure of

. Therefore the fact that the majority of flats in

are ‘close enough’ to
X_{0}
results in the fact that
C(X_{0})
composes a large enough portion of

. This is a very rough sketch of the logic behind Druţu’s argument.

The procedure described above results in an isometry

associated to

. To complete the argument one needs to verify that the map

is a group homomorphism between
\mathrm{SBE}(\Gamma)=\mathrm{SBE}(X_{0})
and

. Composing this map with a representation of

yields a map

lies in a sublinear neighbourhood of the image.

Flat Rigidity.

The adaptations that are required for the SBE setting lie mainly in the part of Druţu’s work that concerns

Remark 3.13
.

Druţu’s argument requires many geometric and combinatorial definitions - some classical and widely known (e.g., Weyl chambers of a symmetric space

The proof consists of

1.

The

2.

The horizon of an image of a flat coincides with the horizon of a finite union of Weyl chambers, and the number of chambers in this union depends only on the Lipschitz constant.

3.

The union of Weyl chambers in the previous step limits to an apartment in the Tits building at

. Such a union is called a

.

4.

For each Weyl chamber

such that

have the same horizon. This amounts to an induced map on the Weyl chambers of the Tits building at

(Lemma

5.

Given a flat

asymptotic to the union of Weyl chambers obtained in step

6.

If

The proofs for most of these steps have similar flavour: in any asymptotic cone

3.3.4
Some Remarks On
\mathbb{R}
-rank
\ 1
Factors

Quasi-isometric rigidity holds for groups of
\mathbb{R}
-rank
\ 1
. It is worth mentioning that Schwartz’s proof also relies on ‘flat rigidity’ - but in this case the flats are the horospheres of
\Gamma
. While these are not isometrically embedded flats, the induced metric on horospheres is flat and Schwartz uses that in order to construct the boundary map and find the associated isometry.

The same phenomena occurs in the SBE setting. Considering the compact core
X_{0}\subset X
of
\Gamma
, one can use Proposition
5.6
in Druţu-Sapir
[
17
]
, in order to show that horospheres are mapped sublinearly close to horospheres. In general, their work characterizes and explores a certain class of spaces they call
asymptotically tree graded
, which is very suitable for the study of compact cores of a non-uniform lattices in
\mathbb{R}
-rank
\ 1
.

A key ingredient in Schwartz’s proof is the fact that the boundary map
\partial q
induced by the quasi-isometry is
quasi-conformal
. This in particular implies that it is almost everywhere differentiable. I spent some time trying to generalize the proof of Schwartz to the SBE setting. One obstacle is that it is not clear that the boundary map is going to be differentiable almost everywhere. Gabriel Pallier found that there are SBE of the hyperbolic space whose boundary maps are not quasi-conformal (see Appendix A in
[
45
]
). For this reason Pallier develops the notion of
sublinear quasi-conformality
. While these examples may be differentiable, he told me of examples he constructed where the differential is almost everywhere
0
- a property which also nullifies Schwartz’s argument.

In the context of SBE rigidity, the maps I consider seem to indeed have ‘flat rigidity’, i.e. to map a horosphere to within sublinear distance of a unique horosphere. As in the higher rank flat rigidity, this bound is not uniform but rather grows sublinearly with the distance of the horosphere to a fixed base point. These are very specific maps, that coarsely preserve the compact core of that lattice
X_{0}\subset X
and basically map horospheres to horospheres. This means there might still be hope for these specific maps to induce boundary maps that admit the required analytic properties.

In a subsequent paper
[
54
]
, Schwartz proves quasi-isometric rigidity for lattices in products of
\mathbb{R}
-rank
\ 1
groups, i.e. in Hilbert modular groups. His proof there is different, but it also makes use of the fact that horospheres are mapped to within uniformly bounded distance of horospheres. The fact that in the SBE setting this bound is not uniform seems like a real obstruction to any attempt of generalizing his proofs.

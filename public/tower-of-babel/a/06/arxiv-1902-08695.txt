1.1.
Donaldson-Thomas invariants.

The Donaldson-Thomas invariants of a Calabi-Yau threefold
X
encode
subtle information about the enumerative geometry of
X
. They are a
mathematical incarnation of counts of BPS states in B-model
topological string theory compactified on
X
.

Let
X
be a Calabi-Yau threefold, that is a smooth complex threefold
with trivial canonical class. Let
\beta\in H_{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
be a curve
class, let
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and let

\operatorname{Hilb}^{\beta,n}(X)=\left\{Z\subset X:\quad[Z]=\beta,\,\chi(\mathcal{O}_{Z})=n\right\}

be the Hilbert scheme parameterizing dimension one subschemes in the
class
\beta
and having holomorphic Euler characteristic
n
. The
Donaldson-Thomas invariant
\mathsf{DT}_{\beta,n}(X)
can be defined

[
3
]
as a weighted Euler characteristic of the Hilbert scheme:

\mathsf{DT}_{\beta,n}(X)=e\left(\operatorname{Hilb}^{\beta,n}(X),\nu\right):=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}k\cdot e\left(\nu^{-1}(k)\right)

where
e(\cdot)
is topological Euler characteristic and

\nu:\operatorname{Hilb}^{\beta,n}(X)\to{\mathbb{Z}}

is Behrend’s constructible function. One can regard
\mathsf{DT}_{\beta,n}(X)

as a virtual count of the number of curves in the class
\beta
with Euler
characteristic
n
.

The Donaldson-Thomas partition function is a generating function for
the invariants which we define

Z^{\,}(X)=\sum_{\beta\in H_{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathsf{DT}_{\beta,n}(X)\,Q^{\beta}(-p)^{n}.

Here
Q^{\beta}=Q_{1}^{d_{1}}\dotsb Q_{r}^{d_{r}}
where
\beta=d_{1}C_{1}+\cdots+d_{r}C_{r}

The Donaldson-Thomas partition function is very hard to compute:

l\equiv b+1\mod 2\end{smallmatrix}}q^{\frac{1}{4}(l^{2}+b^{2}-1)}y^{l}(-t)^{b}

The terms with

\displaystyle\operatorname{Coef}_{q^{n}y^{l}}\left[\operatorname{Ell}_{(q,y)}({\mathbb{C}}^{2},t)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c(n,l,k)t^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c(4n-l^{2},k)t^{k}

where

\displaystyle\sum_{a=-1}^{\infty}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c(a,k)Q^{a}t^{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{-t}{(1-t)^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}}Q^{4i+b^{2}-1}(-t)^{b}\delta_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{-tQ^{-1}}{(1-t)^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\delta_{i}(t)Q^{4i}\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}}Q^{b^{2}}(-t)^{b}

\displaystyle=\theta_{1}(Q^{4},t)^{-2}\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{Z}}}Q^{b^{2}}(-t)^{b}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}Q^{k^{2}}(-t)^{k}}{\left(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}+\frac{1}{2}}Q^{2k^{2}}(-t)^{k}\right)^{2}}\quad.

3.
Computing the partition function

3.1.
Overview

Our basic strategy for computing the partition function

Z^{\,}_{\Gamma}(X_{\mathsf{ban}})
is to stratify the Hilbert scheme, where
each strata parameterizes subschemes supported on a prescribed set of
fibers of the map
\pi:X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. Each stratum admits an action
by the Mordell-Weil group of sections on an infinitesimal neighborhood
of a fiber.

We use these group actions to reduce the
\nu
-weighted Euler
characteristic computation to the fixed point set of the group
actions. The fixed points correspond to subschemes which are supported
on an infinitesimal neighborhood of the banana configurations and
which are formally locally given by monomial ideals. We can count
these fixed points (weighted by
\nu
) using a technique adapted from

[
28
]
to our setting. The outcome is an expression for the
partition function in terms of the topological vertex.

The vertex expression we get has essentially been computed in the
physics literature. The bulk of the computation is done by Hollowood,
Iqbal, and Vafa
[
19
]
using geometric
engineering. Their derivation requires a certain geometrically
motivated combinatorial conjecture. The conjecture was a special case
of the Equivariant Crepant Resolution Conjecture for Elliptic Genera
(also called the equivariant DMVV conjecture after
Dijkgraaf-Moore-Verlinde-Verlinde
[
15
]
), which was subsequently
proven by Borisov-Libgobner and Waelder

[
7
,
38
]
using motivic integration. We present
a mathematically self-contained version of the Hollowood-Iqbal-Vafa
derivation in §
4
.

5.
Geometry of the Banana manifold

In this section we compute the Hodge numbers of the banana manifold

X_{\mathsf{ban}}
, we show that the fiber classes are spanned by the banana
curves
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}
, and we prove Proposition
24
which describes the formal neighborhood of the
singular fibers.

Let
p:S\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
be a rational elliptic surface with 12 singular
fibers, each having one node. Let
{p}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\#}}:{S}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\#}}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
be an isomorphic copy of
S
and consider the fibered product

X_{\mathsf{sing}}=S\times_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}{S}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\#}}.

X_{\mathsf{sing}}
is singular at the 12 points where both
p
and

{p}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\#}}
are not smooth, namely at the product of the nodes.
To see that these points are conifold singularities, note that for
each node
n\in S
, and corresponding node
{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\#}}\in{S}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\#}}
, there
exists formal local coordinates

(x,y),({x}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\#}},{y}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\#}}),t
about
n,

{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\#}}
, and
p(n)={p}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\#}}({n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\#}})\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
respectively such that the maps
p
and
{p}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\#}}
are
given by
xy=t
and

{x}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\#}}{y}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\#}}=t
. Consequently,

xy={x}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\#}}{y}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\#}}
is the local equation
of
X_{\mathsf{sing}}
at
(n,{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\#}})
.

Let
\Delta\subset X_{\mathsf{sing}}
be the divisor given by the diagonal in
the fibered product. From the above local description of the
singularities, all of which lie on
\Delta
, we see that

X_{\mathsf{ban}}=\operatorname{Bl}_{\Delta}(X_{\mathsf{sing}}),

the blowup of
X_{\mathsf{sing}}
along the diagonal is smooth and

X_{\mathsf{ban}}\to X_{\mathsf{sing}}

is a conifold resolution.

5.1.
Relation to the Schoen threefold and the Hodge numbers

Lemma 27
.

e(X_{\mathsf{ban}})=24
.

Proof.

Since the map
X_{\mathsf{ban}}\to X_{\mathsf{sing}}
contracts 12
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
s to 12
singular points, we can write the following equation in the
Grothendieck group of varieties,

[X_{\mathsf{ban}}]=[X^{o}_{\mathsf{sing}}]+12[{\mathbb{P}}^{1}]

where
X_{\mathsf{sing}}^{o}\subset X_{\mathsf{sing}}
is the non-singular
locus. Since
e(-)
is a homomorphism from the Grothendieck group to
the integers, we see that
e(X_{\mathsf{ban}})=e(X_{\mathsf{sing}}^{o})+24
. We claim
the Euler characteristic of the fibers of
X_{\mathsf{sing}}^{o}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}

are all zero. The smooth fibers are
E\times E
, a product of smooth
elliptic curves and hence have zero Euler characterisitic. The
singular fibers have a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
action constructed in
§
3.5
. The fixed points of the
action on the fibers of
X_{\mathsf{sing}}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
are exactly the
conifold points and so the action on the fibers of
X^{o}_{\mathsf{sing}}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is free. Consequently, the Euler characteristics of these
fibers are zero. The lemma follows.
∎

The banana manifold is related to the Schoen Calabi-Yau threefold by a
conifold transition. This will allow us to compute the Hodge numbers
of the banana manifold in terms of the (well-known) Hodge numbers of
the Schoen threefold. The Schoen threefold can be defined as

X_{\mathsf{Sch}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1},

the intersection of two generic hypersurfaces of multi-degree

(3,0,1)
and
(0,3,1)
. It is a simply connected Calabi-Yau threefold
with
h^{1,1}(X_{\mathsf{Sch}})=19
and
h^{2,1}(X_{\mathsf{Sch}})=19
.

Proposition 28
.

h^{1,1}(X_{\mathsf{ban}})=20

Proof.

We first show that there is a conifold transition from
X_{\mathsf{Sch}}
to

X_{\mathsf{ban}}
. The generic hypersurface in

and

A.4.
Gromov-Witten potentials via the GW/DT
correspondence

The GW/DT correspondence is a conjectural equivalence between the
Gromov-Witten and the Donaldson-Thomas invariants of a Calabi-Yau
threefold
[
28
]
. It has been proven for a broad class of
Calabi-Yau threefolds including complete intersections in products of
projective spaces
[
32
]
, which
unfortunately does not include
X_{\mathsf{ban}}
.

However, if we assume that the GW/DT correspondence holds for

X_{\mathsf{ban}}
, we may compute the genus
g
Gromov-Witten potentials from
our formula for the Donaldson-Thomas partition function
(Theorem
4
). We define the reduced genus
g

Gromov-Witten potential (for banana curve classes) of
X_{\mathsf{ban}}
by

F^{\prime}_{g}(Q_{1},Q_{2},Q_{3})=\sum_{\mathbf{\underline{d}}>0}GW^{g}_{\mathbf{\underline{d}}}(X_{\mathsf{ban}})Q_{1}^{d_{1}}Q_{2}^{d_{2}}Q_{3}^{d_{3}}.

Here
GW^{g}_{\mathbf{\underline{d}}}(X_{\mathsf{ban}})
denotes the genus
g

Gromov-Witten invariant in the class

\beta_{\mathbf{\underline{d}}}=d_{1}C_{1}+d_{2}C_{2}+d_{3}C_{3}
and
\mathbf{\underline{d}}>0
means

d_{i}\geq 0
and
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})\neq(0,0,0)
.

The GW/DT correspondence asserts that

\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}F^{\prime}_{g}(Q_{1},Q_{2},Q_{3})\lambda^{2g-2}=\log\left(\frac{Z_{\Gamma}(X_{\mathsf{ban}})}{Z_{\Gamma}(X_{\mathsf{ban}})|_{Q_{i}=0}}\right)

under the change of variables
p=e^{i\lambda}
.

We now prove Theorem
33
. Applying
Theorem
4
and using Equation (
16
), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}F^{\prime}_{g}(Q_{1},Q_{2},Q_{3})\lambda^{2g-2}

\displaystyle=\log\left(\prod_{\mathbf{\underline{d}}>0}\,\prod_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(1-p^{k}Q_{1}^{d_{1}}Q_{2}^{d_{2}}Q_{3}^{d_{3}})^{-12c(||\mathbf{\underline{d}}||,k)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{\underline{d}}>0}\,\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}12c(||\mathbf{\underline{d}}||,k)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}p^{nk}Q_{1}^{nd_{1}}Q_{2}^{nd_{2}}Q_{3}^{nd_{3}}

\displaystyle=12\sum_{\mathbf{\underline{d}}>0}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}Q_{1}^{nd_{1}}Q_{2}^{nd_{2}}Q_{3}^{nd_{3}}\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}c_{2g-2}(||\mathbf{\underline{d}}||)n^{2g-2}\lambda^{2g-2}.

Thus we find that

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{g}(Q_{1},Q_{2},Q_{3})

\displaystyle=12\sum_{\mathbf{\underline{d}}>0}c_{2g-2}(||\mathbf{\underline{d}}||)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{2g-3}Q_{1}^{nd_{1}}Q_{2}^{nd_{2}}Q_{3}^{nd_{3}}

\displaystyle=12\sum_{\mathbf{\underline{d}}>0}c_{2g-2}(||\mathbf{\underline{d}}||)\operatorname{Li}_{3-2g}(Q_{1}^{d_{1}}Q_{2}^{d_{2}}Q_{3}^{d_{3}}).

Substituting

Q_{1}=e^{2\pi iz}=y,\quad\quad Q_{2}=e^{2\pi i(\tau-z)}=qy^{-1},\quad\quad Q_{3}=e^{2\pi i(\sigma-z)}=Qy^{-1},

reindexing by

d_{1}=l+n+m,\quad d_{2}=n,\quad d_{3}=m,

so that
||\mathbf{\underline{d}}||=4nm-l^{2}
, and noting that
\mathbf{\underline{d}}>0
is
equivalent to
n,m\geq 0,l\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
l>0
if
n=m=0
, we find that

F_{g}^{\prime}(q,y,Q)=12\sum_{n,m\geq 0}\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}l\in{\mathbb{Z}}\\
l>0\text{ if }(n,m)=(0,0)\end{smallmatrix}}c_{2g-2}(4nm-l^{2})\operatorname{Li}_{3-2g}(Q^{m}q^{n}y^{l}).

For
g>1
, the full genus
g
Gromov-Witten potential is the reduced
potential plus the constant term:

F_{g}=GW^{g}_{\mathbf{\underline{0}}}(X_{\mathsf{ban}})+F^{\prime}_{g}.

Using for example the formulas in
[
28
, § 2.1]
we know

\displaystyle GW^{g}_{\mathbf{\underline{0}}}(X_{\mathsf{ban}})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{g}\frac{1}{2}e(X_{\mathsf{ban}})\frac{|B_{2g}|\cdot|B_{2g-2}|}{2g(2g-2)(2g-2)!}

\displaystyle=12\cdot\left(\frac{-B_{2g-2}}{4g-4}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{-|B_{2g}|}{g(2g-2)!}\right).

Examining the
y^{0}q^{0}
term of
\psi_{2g-2}(q,y)=\phi_{-2,1}\cdot\frac{|B_{2g}|}{2g(2g-2)!}E_{2g}
for
g>1
yields

c_{2g-2}(0)=\frac{-|B_{2g}|}{g(2g-2)!}

and hence we find

F_{g}=12\cdot\left(c_{2g-2}(0)\frac{-B_{2g-2}}{4g-4}+\sum_{n,m\geq 0}\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}l\in{\mathbb{Z}}\\
l>0\text{ if }(n,m)=(0,0)\end{smallmatrix}}c_{2g-2}(4nm-l^{2})\operatorname{Li}_{3-2g}(Q^{m}q^{n}y^{l})\right).

By Lemma
42
, the above is
exactly the
Maass lift of
12\psi_{2g-2}
, and hence we find that
F_{g}
is the
Skoruppa-Maass lift of
a_{g}E_{2g}
where

a_{g}=\frac{6|B_{2g}|}{g(2g-2)!}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
33
.

Remark 43
.

Note that the proof of the theorem also shows that up to constant
terms, the genus 0 and the genus 1 Gromov-Witten potentials are given
by the Maass lifts of
12\phi_{-2,1}
and
12\phi_{-2,1}\wp=\phi_{0,1}
respectively. Although these are weak Jacobi forms of
index 1, they are not of positive weight, and hence their Maass lifts
are not guarenteed to be Siegel forms. See
[
34
]

for a further discussion.

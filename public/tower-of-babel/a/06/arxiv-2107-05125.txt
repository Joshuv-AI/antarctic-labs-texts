Abstract




Inverse problem for Sturm–Liouville operators

with frozen argument on closed sets

Abstract.

In the paper, we study the problem of recovering the potential from the spectrum of the Dirichlet boundary value problem for a Sturm–Liouville equation with frozen argument on a closed set.
We consider the case when the closed set consists of two segments and the frozen argument is at the end of the first segment.
A uniqueness theorem and an algorithm solving the inverse problem are obtained along with necessary and sufficient conditions of its solvability.
The considered case significantly differs from the one of the classical Sturm–Liouville operator with frozen argument.

Keywords:
inverse spectral problems, frozen argument, Sturm–Liouville operators, time scales, closed sets.

34K29, 34B24, 34N05.

Introduction

The aim of the paper is to study an inverse spectral problem for a Sturm–Liouville operator with frozen argument on a closed set
T\subseteq\mathbb{R}.

Differential operators on closed sets generalize classical differential and difference operators since they contain
\Delta
-derivative, see
[
2
,
3
,
1
]
.

Recently, interest has appeared in inverse spectral problems for differential operators on closed sets, see
[
4
,
6
,
5
,
9
,
7
,
8
,
10
]
. Such problems consist in recovering operators from their spectral characteristics. The most complete results in this direction were obtained for the classical Sturm–Liouville operator on an interval, see
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
.
Posing and studying inverse problems substantially depend on the structure of the considered closed set, which leads to the need for some or other restrictions.
By the present time, the most general form of the sets on which the solution of an inverse problem has been obtained is a finite union of segments and isolated points, see
[
7
,
10
]
.

Inverse problems for the Sturm–Liouville operator with frozen argument on an interval were studied in the series of the works
[
17
,
16
,
20
,
18
,
14
,
15
,
19
]
.
This operator is determined by the Sturm–Liouville differential expression with frozen argument

\ell y(x):=-y^{\prime\prime}(x)+q(x)y(\gamma),\quad 0<x<r,

where
\gamma\in[0,r]
is fixed.
Unlike the classical Sturm–Liouville operator, operators with frozen argument are non-local. For this reason, the methods of the classical inverse problems theory
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
are inapplicable for them. Meanwhile, non-local operators have applications in many areas of mathematics and natural science, see
[
23
,
21
,
22
]
.

The problem of recovering the potential
q
from the spectrum of the boundary value problem

\ell y=\lambda y,\quad y^{(\alpha)}(0)=y^{(\beta)}(r)=0,\quad\alpha,\beta\in\{0,1\},

was investigated in the works
[
17
,
16
,
20
,
18
]
.
In
[
18
]
, the case of arbitrary
\gamma/r\in{\mathbb{Q}}
was studied and a complete description of the so-called non-degenerate and degenerate cases was given with respect to the values of the three parameters
\gamma/r,

\alpha
and
\beta.

In particular, the Dirichlet boundary conditions (
\alpha=\beta=0
) correspond to the degenerate case for any
\gamma/r\in{\mathbb{Q}}.

In the non-degenerate case, the potential is uniquely recovered from the spectrum, while in the degenerate one, additional information is required for uniqueness of recovery, e.g., specification of
q
on a part of the interval.
Necessary and sufficient conditions of the inverse problem solvability were also obtained. In the non-degenerate case, the latter ones include only the spectrum asymptotics

\lambda_{n}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{r^{2}}\bigg(n-\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}+\frac{\kappa_{n}}{n}\bigg)^{2},\quad n\geq 1,\quad\{\kappa_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\in l_{2}.

In the degenerate case, the condition is added for the coincidence of some eigenvalues infinite part with eigenvalues of the corresponding operator with zero potential.
For the irrational case
\gamma/r\notin{\mathbb{Q}},
in the work
[
20
]
, uniqueness of the potential recovery from the spectrum was proved for any
\alpha
and
\beta.

In the matter of inverse spectral problems for operators with frozen argument on closed sets, they have not been considered previously.

In this paper, we consider the boundary value problem for the Sturm–Liouville equation with frozen argument on the closed set
T
of a special form:

-y^{\Delta\Delta}(t)+y(\gamma)q(t)=\lambda y(\sigma(t)),\quad t\in T,

(1)

y(0)=y(b)=0,

(2)

where

T=[0,\gamma]\cup[a,b],\quad d:=a-\gamma,\;l:=b-a.

(3)

Structure (
3
) belongs to the simplest ones that allow detecting considerable differences from the case of a segment.

In the paper, we study recovering the potential
q\in C(T)
from the spectrum of boundary value problem (
1
)–(
2
).
We establish the conditions on the quantities
d,

\gamma,
and
l
under which a uniqueness theorem for the inverse problem solution holds, see Theorem
1
.
In particular, uniqueness of recovery holds if
l=k\gamma,

k\in\mathbb{N}.
Here, there is a difference from the case of the equation with frozen argument on a segment, i.e. for
\gamma=a
and
r=a+l,
in which the potential can not be uniquely reconstructed from the spectrum of the Dirichlet boundary value problem for any rational
k.

As well we obltain an algorithm for recovering the potential along with necessary and sufficient conditions of inverse problem solvability, see Algorithm
1
and Theorem
5
.
Special form (
18
) of the characteristic function considerably complicates the study as compared with the case of a segment. In particular, the spectrum characterization is not limited to asymptotics alone, in contrast to the non-degenerate case of the operator on an interval.

Inverse problem statement. A uniqueness theorem

For convenience of the reader, we first provide necessary notions from the theory of differential equations with
\Delta
-derivative
[
2
,
3
]

T
be a closed subset of the real line.
Define on
T
the jump functions
\sigma
and
\sigma_{-}
in the following way:

\sigma(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\inf\{s\in T:\;s>x\},&x\neq\max T,\\[5.69054pt]
\max T,&x=\max T,\end{array}\right.

\sigma_{-}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\sup\{s\in T:\;s<x\},&x\neq\min T,\\[5.69054pt]
\min T,&x=\min T.\end{array}\right.

A point
x\in T
is called left-dense, left-isolated, right-dense, and right-isolated, if
\sigma_{-}(x)=x,

\sigma_{-}(x)<x,

\sigma(x)=x,
and
\sigma(x)>x,
respectively. If
\sigma_{-}(x)<x<\sigma(x),
then
x
is called
isolated
; if

\sigma_{-}(x)=x=\sigma(x),
then
x
is called
dense
.

Denote
T^{0}:=T\setminus\{\max T\},
if
\max T
exists and is left-isolated, and
T^{0}:=T,
otherwise. We also denote by
C(B)
the class of
functions continuous on a subset
B\subseteq T.

A function
f
on
T
is called
\Delta
-differentiable at

if for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that

|f(\sigma(t))-f(s)-f^{\Delta}(t)(\sigma(t)-s)|\leq\varepsilon|\sigma(t)-s|

for all
s\in(t-\delta,t+\delta)\cap T.
The value
f^{\Delta}(t)
is called the
\Delta
-derivative of the function
f
at the point
t.

Consider equation (
1
) on closed set (
3
) with a continuous potential

q\in C(T).
Note that for
T
of form (
3

T^{0}=T.

We denote by
C_{\Delta}^{n}(T),

n\in\mathbb{N},
the class of
functions having the
n

T

\Delta
-derivative.
Define solutions as functions
y\in C_{\Delta}^{2}(T)
for which identity (
1
) holds. Each
\lambda
for which there exist a non-zero solution
y
satisfying the Dirichlet conditions (
2
) is called an eigenvalue.

Since the closed set
T
has form (
3
), for any
f\in C_{\Delta}^{1}(T),

we have

f^{\Delta}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\displaystyle\frac{f(a)-f(\gamma)}{d},&t=\gamma,\\[5.69054pt]
f^{\prime}(t),&t\in[0,\gamma]\cup[a,b],\end{array}\right.

(4)

see
[
5
,
9
]
.
In (

Applying formula (

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}-y^{\prime\prime}(x_{1})+q(x_{1})y(\gamma)=\lambda y(x_{1}),\quad x_{1}\in[0,\gamma];\\

(5)

with the jump conditions

\begin{pmatrix}y(a)\\
y^{\prime}(a)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&d\\
d(q(\gamma)-\lambda)&1-\lambda d^{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}y(\gamma)\\
y^{\prime}(\gamma)\end{pmatrix}.

(6)

From (

is equivalent to the condition

y\in C^{2}[0,\gamma]\cap C^{2}[a,b],

where

Introduce the solutions

For these functions, the following formulae are known (see

S(x,\lambda)=\frac{\sin\rho(x-\gamma)}{\rho},\;C(x,\lambda)=\cos\rho(x-\gamma)+\int_{\gamma}^{x}\frac{\sin\rho(x-t)}{\rho}q(t)\,dt,\quad x\in[0,\gamma];

(7)

here and below
\lambda=\rho^{2}.

Any solution of system (

y(x)=AS(x,\lambda)+BC(x,\lambda).

(8)

Taking into account (

y(x)=\big(y(\gamma)+dy^{\prime}(\gamma)\big)\cos\rho(x-a)+\\
+\big((1-d^{2}\lambda)y^{\prime}(\gamma)-d\lambda y(\gamma)+dy(\gamma)q(\gamma)\big)\frac{\sin\rho(x-a)}{\rho}+B\int_{a}^{x}\frac{\sin\rho(x-t)}{\rho}q(t)\,dt.

Substituting representations (

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\\

A number

is called the characteristic function of boundary value problem (

The spectrum of boundary value problem (

Using formulae (

\begin{split}\Delta(\lambda)=-c_{1}(\lambda)\frac{\sin\rho\gamma}{\rho}-c_{2}(\lambda)\cos\rho\gamma-\frac{\sin\rho\gamma}{\rho}\int_{a}^{b}\frac{\sin\rho(b-t)}{\rho}q(t)\,dt\\

where

c_{1}(\lambda):=\cos\rho l-d\rho\sin\rho l,\quad c_{2}(\lambda):=d\cos\rho l+\frac{1-d^{2}\lambda}{\rho}\sin\rho l.

Further, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Positive zeros of the function
c_{2}(z^{2})
can be selected to a sequence
\{z_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
such that
the systems
\{\sin z_{n}t\}_{n\geq 1}
and
\{1\}\cup\{\cos z_{n}t\}_{n\geq 1}
are Riesz basises in
L_{2}(0,l).

Proof.

Consider the equation

g(z):=\frac{dz}{d^{2}z^{2}-1}-\tan zl=0.

If
z
is a non-zero root of this equation, then the number
z
makes
c_{2}(z^{2})
vanish. Note that the function
g(z)
is continuous and monotone on any interval not containing points
\pm 1/d,

\pm{\pi(n+1/2)/l},

n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Using the intermediate value theorem, one can show that each interval

I_{n}=\Big(\frac{\pi n}{l}-\frac{\pi}{2l},\frac{\pi n}{l}+\frac{\pi}{2l}\Big),\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

contains at least one zero of
g(z).
Let us choose as
z_{n}
any zero from the interval
I_{n},

n\in\mathbb{N},
and then all
z_{n}>0
are distinct.

Using the standard technique
[
13
]
involving application of Rouche’s theorem, one can prove that the sequence of
c_{2}(z^{2})
zeros has the form
\{-z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}\cup\{z_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
together with the asymptotic formulae

z_{n}=\frac{\pi n}{l}+\frac{1}{d\pi n}+O\Big(\frac{1}{n^{3}}\Big),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(11)

Let us prove that
\{\sin z_{n}t\}_{n\geq 1}
is a Riesz basis; for
\{1\}\cup\{\cos z_{n}t\}_{n\geq 1}
the proof is analogous.
According to Proposition 1.8.5 from
[
13
]
, it is sufficient to prove the completeness of the system
\{\sin z_{n}t\}_{n\geq 1}.

Let
f\in L_{2}(0,l)
be such that
\int_{0}^{l}f(t)\sin z_{n}t\,dt=0,

then the function

F(\lambda)=\frac{(\lambda-z^{2}_{0})\int_{0}^{l}f(t)\sin\rho t\,dt}{\rho c_{2}(\lambda)},

is entire in
\lambda.

Using the standard estimate (see
[
13
]
)

|\sin\rho l|\geq M^{-1}e^{|\mathrm{Im}\,\rho|l},\quad|\rho|=\frac{\pi(n+\frac{1}{2})}{l},\;n\in\mathbb{N},\;n\geq N^{*},

we obtain that
|F(\lambda)|\leq M
on the circles
|\lambda|=(\pi(n+1/2)/l)^{2}
for a sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then, the maximum modulus principle and Liouville’s theorem yield that

F(\lambda)\equiv C.
By the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma, we also have

\int_{0}^{l}f(t)\sin\rho t\,dt=o(1),\quad\frac{\rho c_{2}(\lambda)}{\lambda-z^{2}_{0}}=(-1)^{n+1}d^{2}(1+o(1))

for
\rho=\pi(n+1/2)/l.

Hence, it follows that the only possible value is
C=0.
Now
f\equiv 0,
and the completeness of the system
\{\sin z_{n}t\}_{n\geq 1}
is proved.
∎

We consider the following inverse problem.

Inverse problem 1
.

Given the spectrum
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\geq 0},
recover the potential
q.

Now we prove a proposition that allows us to reduce the inverse problem to recovering
q
from the characteristic function.

Proposition 1
.

The characteristic function
\Delta(\lambda)
is reconstructed uniquely by the spectrum
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}.

Proof.

Denote
s(\lambda)=\rho^{-1}\sin\rho\gamma.

From representation (
10
), we easily obtain the asymptotics

\Delta(\lambda)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}d^{2}\rho\sin\rho l\cos\rho\gamma+O(e^{|\mathrm{Im}\,\rho|(\gamma+l)}),&\gamma\leq l\text{ or }q(\gamma)=0,\\[5.69054pt]
-dq(\gamma)s^{2}(\lambda)+O(\rho e^{|\mathrm{Im}\,\rho|(\gamma+l)}),&\gamma>l\text{ and }q(\gamma)\neq 0,\end{array}\quad\lambda\to\infty.\right.

(12)

Let
k_{0}
be the multiplicity of
0
in the spectrum. Without loss of generality, we assume that
0
occurs only among the first
k_{0}
spectrum members, i.e.
\lambda_{0}=\ldots=\lambda_{k_{0}-2}=\lambda_{k_{0}-1}=0.

By Hadamard’s theorem, the characteristic function is determined up to the constant
C\neq 0:

\Delta(\lambda)=CG(\lambda),\quad G(\lambda):=\lambda^{k_{0}}\prod^{\infty}_{n=k_{0}}\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{n}}\right).

(13)

According to (
12
), the type of the constructed infinite product
G(\lambda)
can be equal to either
\gamma+l,
or
2\gamma.
If the type is equal to
\gamma+l,
then
the constant
C
in (
13
) is determined via the first asymptotic formula in (
12
).
Now, let the type be
2\gamma,
which is possible only for
\gamma>l
and
q(\gamma)\neq 0.
We define

C_{1}=\lim_{\lambda\to-\infty}\frac{G(\lambda)}{s^{2}(\lambda)}.

It is clear from (
10
) that
C_{1}=-dq(\gamma)/C.

Also (
10
) and (
13
) yield that

G(\lambda)-C_{1}s^{2}(\lambda)=\frac{d^{2}\rho\sin\rho d_{2}\cos\rho\gamma(1+o(1))}{C},\quad\lambda\to-\infty,

and

C=\lim_{\lambda\to-\infty}\frac{d^{2}\rho\sin\rho l\cos\rho\gamma}{G(\lambda)-C_{1}s^{2}(\lambda)}.

Thus,
C
is uniquely determined, which proves the assertion of the lemma.
∎

1

Theorem 1
.

Denote by
\{\tilde{\lambda}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}

\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}=\{\tilde{\lambda}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}

Proof.

According to Proposition
1
, the function
\Delta(\lambda)
is uniquely determined by specifying its zeros

We denote by
\tilde{\Delta}(\lambda)
the characteristic function of boundary value problem (

Substituting the values

Analogously, we get the equality

Based on formulae (

Algorithm 1
.

Let the functions
c_{2}(\lambda)
and

not have common zeros. To recover the potential from

one should:

2. Compute the coefficients

\{\chi_{n}(t)\}_{n\geq 1}

\{\sin z_{n}t\}_{n\geq 1}
in

Proposition 2
.

It can be ensured that
c_{2}(\lambda)
and
s(\lambda)
do not have common zeros by imposing one of the three restrictions on

\gamma,
and

1) for some
k\in\mathbb{N},

holds;

2) the numbers
\pi l/\gamma
and
\pi d/\gamma
are rational;

3) the numbers
l/\gamma,

\pi d/\gamma
are rational, and
\cos l/d\neq 0.

Proof.

If 1) holds, then
c_{2}\big((\pi n/\gamma)^{2}\big)=(-1)^{nk}d.

Suppose 2) or 3) holds, and
c_{2}\big((\pi n/\gamma)^{2}\big)=0
for some

Then
\cos\pi nl/\gamma\neq 0,
and we have

\bigg(1-\Big(\frac{\pi n}{\gamma}d\Big)^{2}\bigg)\tan\frac{\pi nl}{\gamma}=\frac{\pi n}{\gamma}d.

(16)

If 2) holds, then
\pi nl/\gamma\in\mathbb{Q},
and
\tan\pi nl/\gamma
is an irrational number, see
[
25
]
. Therefore, in the left part of (

) we have either 0 or an irrational number, and in the right part a non-zero rational number, which leads to a contradiction.

If 3) holds, then
\pi nl/\gamma
denotes a rational number of degrees, and, according to
[
25
]
, the inclusion

\tan\frac{\pi nl}{\gamma}\in(\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q})\cup\{0\}\cup\{\pm 1\}

is fulfilled.
The cases when
\tan\pi nl/\gamma
is irrational or zero are analogous to 2). If
\tan\pi nl/\gamma=\pm 1,
then (

) also leads to a contradiction because the rational number
\pi nd/\gamma
can not satisfy any of the quadratic equations

3
Necessary and sufficient conditions

Further, we obtain necessary and sufficient conditions on the spectrum in the case

l=\gamma,\quad q\in W^{1}_{2}[0,\gamma]\cap W^{1}_{2}[a,b].

Proposition 3

=\sin z_{n}l\int_{0}^{l}W(l+t)\cos z_{n}t\,dt+d\int_{0}^{l}g(t)\sin z_{n}(l-t)\,dt\\
=\sin z_{n}l\int_{0}^{l}W(l+t)\cos z_{n}t\,dt+d\sin z_{n}l\int_{0}^{l}g(t)\cos z_{n}t\,dt-d\cos z_{n}l\int_{0}^{l}g(t)\sin z_{n}t\,dt.

Making the substitution
\cos z_{n}l=[dz_{n}-1/(dz_{n})]\sin z_{n}l
in the last term and integrating by parts, we obtain

\Big(d^{2}z_{n}-\frac{1}{z_{n}}\Big)\int_{0}^{l}g(t)\sin z_{n}t\,dt=d^{2}\bigg[u-c\cos z_{n}l+\int_{0}^{l}g^{\prime}(t)\cos z_{n}t\,dt\bigg]+\int_{0}^{l}G_{1}(t)\cos z_{n}t\,dt.

Finally, taking into account (
30
) and
c=-2(C_{0}+1)/d^{2},
we have

\Delta(z_{n}^{2})=\frac{\sin^{2}z_{n}l}{dz_{n}^{2}}+\frac{\sin z_{n}l}{z_{n}}\int_{0}^{l}f(t)\cos z_{n}t\,dt,\quad f(t)=-G_{2}(l-t)-2G_{1}(t)-dg(l).

Equating the obtained expression to the right side in (
35
) and dividing by
z_{n}^{-1}\sin z_{n}l,
we arrive at the formula

\int_{0}^{l}f(t)\cos z_{n}t\,dt=\frac{1}{z_{n}^{2}}\Big[h_{2}-(-1)^{n}h_{1}+\eta_{n}\Big]-\frac{\sin z_{n}l}{z_{n}d}=\frac{1}{z_{n}^{2}}\Big[h_{2}-(-1)^{n}\Big(h_{1}+\frac{1}{d^{2}}\Big)+\tilde{\eta}_{n}\Big],\;n\geq 1,

where
\{\tilde{\eta}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}\in l_{2}.

Applying Lemma
3

f\in W^{2}_{2}[0,l]

G_{2}\in W^{2}_{2}[0,l].

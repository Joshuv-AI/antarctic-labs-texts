Coloring the distance graphs 2020 AMS subject classification 03E35, 14P99, 05C15. Keywords: Solovay model, geometric set theory, Noetherian topology.
Jindřich Zapletal

Abstract
Let n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 be a number. Let Γ n \Gamma_{n} be the graph on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} connecting points of rational Euclidean distance. It is consistent with choiceless set theory ZF+DC that Γ n \Gamma_{n} has countable chromatic number, yet the chromatic number of Γ n + 1 \Gamma_{n+1} is uncountable.

Mutually Noetherian extensions

This section introduces the main technical notion connecting dimension of Euclidean spaces with generic extensions. To start, recall the following standard definitions of algebraic geometry.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\langle X,\mathcal{T}\rangle
be a topological space.

1.

\mathcal{T}
is
Noetherian
if there is no infinite sequence of
\mathcal{T}
-closed sets strictly decreasing with respect to inclusion.

2.

A
\mathcal{T}
-closed set is
irreducible
if it is not the union of finitely many properly smaller
\mathcal{T}
-closed subsets.

3.

The
Krull dimension
of
\mathcal{T}
is the maximum length of chains of irreducible closed sets linearly ordered by inclusion, minus one. if the maximum does not exist, the Krull dimension is infinite.

Note that this definition provides only for finite and not transfinite values of Krull dimension, which is fine for the purposes of this paper. Noetherian topologies are commonly identified by their closed sets. The most basic example is the topology of algebraic subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for a number
n\geq 1
, which has Krull dimension
n
by the Hilbert basis theorem. The following definitions connect Noetherian topologies with descriptive set theory and forcing.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a
K_{\sigma}
Polish space. A topology
\mathcal{T}
on
X
is
analytic
if every
\mathcal{T}
-closed set is closed in the Polish topology, and the collection of all
\mathcal{T}
-closed sets is an analytic subset of the standard Borel space
F(X)
of closed subsets of
X
.

For an analytic topology on a
K_{\sigma}
space
X
, standard complexity and Shoenfield absoluteness arguments show that the properties such as Noetherian status, irreducible status of closed sets, and the Krull dimension are all absolute between transitive models of ZFC containing all ordinals.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a Polish space and
\mathcal{T}
be a Noetherian topology on
X
. Let
M
be a transitive model of set theory containing the code for
X
, and let
A\subset X
be a set. The symbol
C(M,A)
denotes the inclusion-smallest
\mathcal{T}
-closed set coded in
M
which contains
A
as a subset.

Note that the set
C(M,A)
is well-defined since the search for ever smaller
\mathcal{T}
-closed sets coded in
M
containing
A
as a subset cannot go on forever by the Noetherian property of
\mathcal{T}
. The dependence of the set
C(M,A)
on the topology is suppressed as
\mathcal{T}
is always clear from the context.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
n\geq 1
be a natural number. For generic extensions
V[G_{0}],V[G_{1}]
, say that
V[G_{0}]
is
n
-
Noetherian over

V[G_{1}]
if for every Polish

-space

and every analytic Noetherian topology
\mathcal{T}
of Krull dimension smaller than
n
coded in the ground model, and every set

in
V[G_{1}]
,

. If in addition

is
n
-
Noetherian over

-Noetherian
.

Proposition 2.5
.

In Definition
2.4
, it is only necessary to consider irreducible
\mathcal{T}
-closed sets

.

Proof.

Given an arbitrary set

in the model
V[G_{1}]
, working in
V[G_{0},G_{1}]
find the smallest
\mathcal{T}
-closed set

containing

as a subset, and write it as

\mathcal{T}
-closed sets with smallest possible

Observe is that the sets

as well as

for

Corollary 2.7

Mutually generic extensions are mutually Noetherian.

4.1
Preliminaries

The proof of Theorem

requires a fair amount of familiarity with real algebraic geometry. The following remarks record some of the facts used. The theory of

uses the ordering symbol, addition, multiplication, and

constants, and includes the axioms of ordered fields as well as axioms stating that every polynomial of odd degree has a root. A good reference for treatment of real closed fields is
[

Fact 4.2
.

The theory of real closed fields admits quantifier elimination.

Corollary 4.3
.

If

is a real closed subfield then

is an elementary submodel of
\mathbb{R}
.

All of the consequences of these facts needed below concern algebraic subsets of Euclidean spaces.

Corollary 4.4
.

Every algebraic set in a Euclidean space is either finite or uncountable.

Proof.

This is true even for semi-algebraic sets. Let
n\geq 1
be a number and
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a semi-algebraic set. Consider the projections
A_{i}
of
A
into each coordinate

. If these sets are all finite, then
A
is finite. Assume that some
A_{i}
is infinite. Since it is definable by a quantifier-free formula in the language of real closed fields and it is infinite, it must contain a nonempty open interval. Thus,
A_{i}
is uncountable and so is

.
∎

If
F\subset\mathbb{R}
is a real closed subfield and
n\geq 1
is a number, a set

is
algebraic over

if there is a polynomial
\phi(\bar{u})
of
n
many variables and coefficients in
F
such that
A=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon\phi(x)=0\}
. Several closure properties of the class of sets algebraic over
F
will be needed. It is immediate that this class is closed under unions, intersections, and sections indexed by elements of
F
. The following closure properties are more delicate.

Corollary 4.5
.

If
F\subset\mathbb{R}
is a real closed field,
n,m\geq 1
, and
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
are sets algebraic over

, then the set
C=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon\forall y\in A\ \langle x,y\rangle\in B\}
is a set algebraic over

.

Proof.

For each

let
C_{y}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon\langle x,y\rangle\in B\}
; this is an algebraic set and

. By the Hilbert basis theorem, there is a finite set
a\subset A
such that
C=\bigcap_{y\in a}C_{y}
. Let
k\in\omega
be the cardinality of the set

. The existence of a set

of cardinality

such that
\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\ (\forall y\in b\ \langle x,y\rangle\in B)\to(\forall y\in A\ \langle x,y\rangle\in B)
is a first-order statement in the language of real closed fields and as such it is reflected to
F
. Let
b\subset F
be a witness that
F
finds, and observe that
C=\bigcap_{y\in b}C_{y}
is a set algebraic over
F
.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

Suppose that
F\subset\mathbb{R}
is a real closed field and a set

is algebraic over

. If

is reducible, then its irreducible composants are algebraic over

.

Proof.

The decomposition of

is well-known to be unique: among all ways of expressing the set

as a union of finitely many algebraic sets none of which is covered by the others, it is the one in which there is the largest number of sets possible. If the number of composants is

and all of them are given by polynomials of degree at most

, the existence of such a decomposition is a first order statement in the language of real closed fields, which is then reflected by the field

.
∎

Corollary 4.7
.

A finite set

algebraic over

is a subset of

.

Proof.

Let

be the cardinality of

. Existence of a list of

is a first-order statement of the language of real closed fields, and therefore reflected by

.
∎

Finally, there is a proposition about algebraic sets which is needed in one critical spot of the proof of Theorem
4.1
. While it seems to be either folkloric or otherwise well-known, I did not find a good reference for it and I include the proof.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
n\geq 1
be a number. Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an irreducible algebraic set, and
A\subseteq\bigcup_{i}B_{i}
for some algebraic sets
B_{i}
for

. Then there is an index
i\in\omega
such that
A\subseteq B_{i}
.

Proof.

This does not follow from a straightforward application of the Baire category theorem to the closed set

, since irreducible algebraic sets in Euclidean spaces may contain for example points isolated in the sense of the Euclidean topology. It is necessary to apply the Baire category theorem to a certain relatively open subset of
A
. Below, the dimension of an irreducible algebraic set is its Krull dimension, and the dimension of an arbitrary algebraic set is the maximal Krull dimension of its irreducible components.

Without loss, assume that
A\neq 0
. Consider the set
\mathrm{Reg}(A)\subseteq A
consisting of all non-singular points of

–these are the points where the dimension of the Zariski tangent space to

is exactly equal to
\dim(A)
. It is well-known
[

that

is an algebraic subset of

of dimension strictly smaller than
\dim(A)
; in particular,
\mathrm{Reg}(A)\neq 0
and
\mathrm{Reg}(A)
is a relatively open subset of

.

Claim 4.9
.

Suppose that
B
is a proper algebraic subset of
A
. Then
B
is nowhere dense in the set
\mathrm{Reg}(A)
in the topology inherited from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

Since every algebraic set is a finite union of its irreducible components, it is enough to prove the claim for irreducible
B
. Since algebraic sets are closed, the statement is equivalent to showing that
\mathrm{Reg}(A)\setminus B
is dense in
\mathrm{Reg}(A)
. The argument proceeds by induction on
m=\dim(B)
, which is necessarily smaller than
\dim(A)
. The base step
m=1
is subsumed in the induction step. For the induction step, suppose that the statement is known for some number
m
,
\dim(B)=m+1
, and

is a nonempty relatively open set; I need to produce a point in

.
\mathrm{Sing}(B)
is an algebraic set of dimension smaller than that of

, so by the induction hypothesis it is possible to shrink

if necessary to contain no points in
\mathrm{Sing}(B)
. Now suppose towards a contradiction that

. This is a relatively open set of non-singular points of both

and

. Therefore, at any point in it, its

-tangent space coincides with the Zariski tangent space for both

and

and

. Since

, this is impossible.
∎

The proposition now follows by an application of the Baire category theorem to the set

, which is Polish in the topology inherited from

.
∎

shows that the incompatibility relation is Borel as well. Nonemptiness follows from the proposition applied to an empty set of conditions.
∎

Corollary 4.19
.

For every number
m\in\omega
, the poset
P
is
m,2
-centered.

This is to say that if
a
is a collection of cardinality
m
consisting of pairwise compatible conditions, then it has a common lower bound. This is immediate from Proposition
4.12
.

Corollary 4.20
.

The poset
P
forces the union of its generic filter to be a total
\Gamma
-coloring.

By a genericity argument, this is to say that for each point
x\in X
and each condition
p\in P_{n}
, there is
q\leq p
such that
x\in\mathrm{dom}(q)
. This is immediate from Proposition
4.12
applied to the singleton
\{p\}
.

Corollary 4.21
.

Under the Continuum Hypothesis, the poset
P
is
n+1
-Noetherian balanced.

Unlike the usual theorems in
[
6
]
, I do not bother to produce an exact classification of balanced virtual conditions, which in this case is complicated and brings nothing new to the proofs. I do not know if the CH assumption can be dropped.

Proof.

Let
p\in P_{n}
be a condition. Let
\langle x_{\alpha}\colon\alpha\in\omega_{1}\rangle
be an enumeration of
\mathbb{R}
. By recursion on
\alpha\in\omega_{1}
build conditions
p_{\alpha}\in P_{n}
so that

p=p_{0}
,
p_{\alpha+1}\leq p_{\alpha}
is a condition such that
x_{\alpha}\in\mathrm{supp}(p_{\alpha+1})
, and
p_{\alpha}=\bigcup_{\beta\in\alpha}p_{\beta}
for limit ordinals
\alpha
. The function
c=\bigcup_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}
is a total
\Gamma
-coloring which satisfies Definition
4.10
(B). It is clear that
\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\mathbb{R})\Vdash\check{c}\leq p
is a condition in the poset
P
. By Proposition
4.12
, it will be enough to show that the pair
\langle\mathrm{Coll}(\omega,\mathbb{R}),\check{c}\rangle
is
n+1
-Noetherian balanced.

To do this, suppose that
V[G_{0}],V[G_{1}]
are mutually
n+1
-Noetherian extensions. Suppose that
p_{0},p_{1}\in P_{n}
are conditions in the respective models stronger than
c
; I must show that
p_{0},p_{1}
are compatible. This will be done using Proposition
4.12
.

To show that
p_{0}\cup p_{1}
is a
\Gamma
-coloring, suppose that
x_{0}\in\mathrm{dom}(p_{0})
and
x_{1}\in\mathrm{dom}(p_{1})
are
\Gamma
-related points. Let
m\in\omega
be a number such that
\phi_{m}(x_{0},x_{1})=0
. If both points
x_{0},x_{1}
belong to one and the same condition, then they must receive distinct colors as that condition is a
\Gamma
coloring. The only remaining configuration is that
x_{0}\in V[G_{0}]\setminus V
and
x_{1}\in V[G_{1}]\setminus V
. Note that
B(c,x_{1})=B(p_{0},x_{1})\subset\{y\in X\colon\phi_{m}(y_{0},x)=0\}
holds by the Noetherian assumption on the models
V[G_{0}]
and
V[G_{1}]
. Since
p_{1}\leq c
holds, there must be a point
x^{\prime}_{1}\in\mathrm{dom}(c)\cap B(c,x_{1})
distinct from
x_{0}
such that
p_{1}(x_{1})=c(x^{\prime}_{1})
. By the irreflexivity assumption on the polynomial
p_{m}
, it must be the case that
x_{0}\neq x^{\prime}_{1}
. Since
p_{1}
is a
\Gamma
coloring, it follows that
p_{0}(x_{0})\neq c(x^{\prime}_{1})=p_{1}(x_{1})
holds as desired.

Finally, let
A\subset X
be a set algebraic over
\mathrm{supp}(p_{0})
and
x\in A\cap\mathrm{dom}(p_{1})
be a point; I must find a point
x^{\prime}\in A\cap\mathrm{dom}(p_{0})
which receives the same color as
x
. Use the Noetherian assumption to see that
B(p_{0},x)=B(c,x)\subseteq A
. Use the
p_{1}\leq c
assumption to find a point
x^{\prime}\in B(c,x)
such that
c(x^{\prime})=p_{1}(x)
. The point
x^{\prime}
works as desired.
∎

Finally, the stage has been set to prove Theorem
1.2
. Let
n\geq 1
be a number. Let
\Gamma
be a graph on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
without a perfect clique. Let
\kappa
be an inaccessible cardinal and let
W
be the choiceless Solovay model derived from
\kappa
;
W
is a model of ZF+DC
[
4
, Theorem 26.14]
. Let
P
be the coloring poset isolated in Theorem
4.1
. Let
G\subset P
be a filter generic over
W
. The model
W[G]
is a
\sigma
-closed extension of
W
by Proposition
4.11
and as such it is a model of ZF+DC. In addition,
\bigcup G
is a total coloring of the graph
\Gamma
with countably many colors by Corollary
4.20
. By Corollary
4.21
and Theorem
3.2
, in the model
W[G]
every nonmeager subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
contains point of arbitrary small enough distance. By the Baire category theorem, it must be the case that the chromatic number of
\Gamma_{n+1}
is uncountable in
W[G]
as required. Thus, the model
W[G]
witnesses the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
; in case that
\Gamma=\Gamma_{n}
, it witnesses the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
.

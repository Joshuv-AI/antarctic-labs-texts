Superconductivity Induced Ferromagnetism In The Presence of Spin-Orbit Coupling
Yao Lu, I. V. Tokatly, F. Sebastian Bergeret

Abstract
We investigate the behavior of magnetic impurities placed on the surface of superconductor thin films with spin-orbit coupling. Our study reveals long-range interactions between the impurities, which decay according to a power law, mediated by the supercurrents. Importantly, these interactions possess a ferromagnetic component when considering the influence of the electromagnetic field, leading to the parallel alignment of the magnetic moments in the case of two impurities. In a Bravais lattice of magnetic impurities, superconductivity facilitates the establishment of ferromagnetic order within specific parameter ranges. These findings challenge the conventional understanding that ferromagnetism and superconductivity are mutually exclusive phenomena. Our theoretical framework provides a plausible explanation for the recently observed remanent flux and transport signature of ferromagnetism in iron-based superconductors, particularly Fe(Se,Te).



Introduction.-

Ferromagnetic ordering is often seen as incompatible with conventional superconductivity due to the presence of an effective exchange field in ferromagnets. This exchange field has the effect of breaking up Cooper pairs, which are composed of electrons in a singlet state
Anderson and Suhl 1959
.
The coexistence of these two orders, however, does exist in hybrid superconductor/ferromagnet (S/F) structures
Buzdin 2005
;
Bergeret et al. 2005
.
In these structures, the proximity effect plays a crucial role in enabling the coexistence of superconductivity and ferromagnetism.
Singlet pairs can be transformed into triplet pairs through the exchange field of the F region. As a result, a local magnetic moment is produced, extending over distances of the order of superconducting coherence length,
\xi_{s}
. This phenomenon is referred to as the magnetic or inverse proximity effect
Bergeret et al. 2004a
;
Xia et al. 2009
.
The generated magnetic moment is oriented in the opposite direction to the magnetization of the ferromagnetic region. In the case of a small ferromagnetic island, this results in the screening of its magnetic moment
Bergeret et al. 2004b
.
If a second ferromagnetic region (F region) is positioned at a distance smaller than
\xi_{s}
from the first ferromagnet, the energetically favorable arrangement is an anti-parallel orientation of the magnetizations of the two F regions.
This anti-parallel alignment serves as the basis for the FSF superconducting spin-valve
De Gennes 1966
;
Tagirov 1999
;
Izyumov et al. 2002
;
Ojajärvi et al. 2022
.
The studies on FSF structures with conventional superconductors indicate an anti-ferromagnetic coupling between the magnets, mediated by the inverse proximity effect. This coupling strength decreases exponentially with the distance between the ferromagnetic regions.

This situation changes in thin superconducting films with spin-orbit coupling (SOC).

;

.
These anomalous currents are spatially localized
Bergeret and Tokatly 2020
over the coherence length from the impurity. Charge conservation
implies the emergence of a phase gradient , ensuring
\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{j}=0
and the appearance of circulating currents

(black arrows in Fig.

If we assume that a magnetic moment

\bm{m}_{1}
points in the positive
x
direction (Fig.
1
), then it generates a non-local circular supercurrent which flows in the negative
y
direction at the position of the magnetic impurity
\bm{m}_{2}
and to the positive
y
direction at the position of
\bm{m}_{3}
.
The orientations of
\bm{m}_{2,3}

are determined by minimization of the free energy: to reduce the kinetic energy of the superflow they will generate anomalous currents that suppress the currents induced by
\bm{m}_{1}
. Consequently,
\bm{m}_{2}
will point in the positive
x
direction and
\bm{m}_{3}

to the negative
x
direction. In other words, the supercurrent mediated magnetic interaction is ferromagnetic between
\bm{m}_{1}
and
\bm{m}_{2}
while it is antiferromagnetic for
\bm{m_{1}}
and
\bm{m}_{3}
. Thus, in general, for two magnetic impurities,
\bm{m}_{1}
and
\bm{m}_{2}
the magnetic interaction will have the form:

F_{I}=J_{\perp}\bm{m}_{1\perp}\bm{m}_{2\perp}-J_{\parallel}\bm{m}_{1\parallel}\bm{m}_{2\parallel}\;,

(1)

where
\perp
(
\parallel
) denotes the component in the direction perpendicular (parallel) to
\bm{r}=\bm{r}_{1}-\bm{r}_{2}
, and both
J{\perp}
and
J_{\parallel}
are positive.
Previous studies on Rashba superconductors
Pershoguba et al. 2015
;
Mal’Shukov 2018
, specifically regarding the current distribution around a magnetic impurity and the induced magnetic interaction have obtained an interaction resembling the 2D dipole-dipole interaction (DDI) form with
J_{\perp}=J_{\parallel}
, which does not result in either a ferromagnetic or an anti-ferromagnetic ground state for two impurities. However, those studies
have neglected the influence of the electromagnetic (EM) field. On the other hand, we know from Pearl’s seminal work
Pearl 1964
that the EM field plays a crucial role in determining the current distribution in conventional superconducting thin films.
This leads to the natural question of the effect of the EM field on the magnetic coupling between impurities in a superconductor with spin-orbit coupling (SOC).

In this work, we present a theory
elucidating the impact of the electromagnetic field on the magnetic coupling between impurities in a superconductor with SOC.
We demonstrate that the presence of EM fields alters drastically the spatial dependence of the couplings
J_{\perp(\parallel)}
. We establish that the supercurrent-mediated magnetic interaction exhibits the form of a DDI that is generated by the so-called Keldysh potential
Keldysh 1979
;
Cudazzo et al. 2011
, and interpolates between the 2D and 3D DDI. It can also be viewed as a 2D DDI combined with a ferromagnetic interaction, leading to a ferromagnetic ground state for two impurities. Furthermore, we emphasize the crucial role of the electromagnetic field in a 2D impurity lattice. Without the electromagnetic field, the interaction energy density becomes unphysically divergent as the system size approaches infinity. However, when the electromagnetic field is taken into account, the energy density converges in the limit of large system size.
In the remainder of this paper, we provide a detailed derivation of these results and discuss recent experimental results suggesting a superconducting-induced ferromagnetic order of impurities in Fe(SeTe)
Xiang et al. 2023
.

Theory.-

We first consider magnetic impurities on top of a two-dimensional superconducting system with SOC, which can, for example, be a thin film on a substrate or superconductivity induced at the surface of a topological insulator. We assume all the magnetic impurities are polarized in the in-plane directions. In these systems, the inversion symmetry is broken because of the presence of the intrinsic polar vector – the normal
\hat{z}
to the transport plane. Additionally, the exchange field
\bm{h}
induced by the magnetic impurity locally breaks the time-reversal symmetry. The breaking of these two symmetries allows for the existence of a spontaneous current, known as the anomalous current, given by
Bergeret and Tokatly 2015
;
Strambini et al. 2020

\bm{j}=-e^{2}D\bm{a}
, where
D
is the 2D superfluid weight and
\bm{a}
is the effective gauge field
\bm{a}=\frac{1}{e}\Gamma\bm{h}\times\hat{z}
. Here
\Gamma
is a constant that depends on the details of the system. For example, in a superconductor with strong Rashba SOC
\Gamma=\alpha/v_{F}^{2}
(see section 4 of supplementary material
sup
), where
\alpha
is the SOC strength; or
\Gamma=1/v_{F}
in a Dirac material
Zyuzin et al. 2016
;
Kokkeler et al. 2022
.

The total free energy change due to the supercurrent is given by

F=\int d^{3}\bm{r}\frac{1}{8}D\left[\bm{\nabla}\phi-2e\bm{A}-2e\bm{a}\right]^{2}\delta(z)+\frac{1}{2\mu_{0}}\bm{B}^{2}\;.

(2)

The first term on the right-hand side is the free energy of the superconductor
F_{SC}
and the second term is the electromagnetic field contribution
F_{EM}
,
where
\mu_{0}
is the magnetic constant,
e
is the electron charge,
\bm{A}
is the vector potential of the electromagnetic field
\bm{B}=\bm{\nabla}\times\bm{A}
. We assume the superconductor film is located at
z=0
. Note that
\bm{A}
lives in 3 dimensions while
\bm{\nabla}\phi
and
\bm{a}
are defined in the 2D superconductor. Taking the derivative of
F_{SC}
with respect to
-\bm{A}
, one obtains the 2D supercurrent flowing in the plane of the superconductor,

\bm{j}=\frac{1}{2}eD\left[\bm{\nabla}\phi-2e\bm{A}-2e\bm{a}\right]\delta(z).

(3)

Minimizing the free energy with respect to
\phi
gives the charge conservation
\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{j}=0
. In the following, we choose the Coulomb gauge
\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{A}=0
. This implies that the phase gradient cancels the longitudinal part
\bm{a}_{l}
of the effective gauge field, which is written in the momentum
\bm{q}
space as,
\bm{q}\phi=2e\bm{a}_{l}=2e\bm{q}(\bm{q}\cdot\bm{a})/q^{2}
. The current of Eq. (
S3
) is then fully determined by the trasverce component
\bm{a}_{t}=\bm{a}-\bm{a}_{l}
, that is,
\bm{j}=-e^{2}D(\bm{A}+\bm{a}_{t})
.

By minimizing the total free energy with respect to the vector potential
\frac{\partial F}{\partial\bm{A}}=0
, we obtain the Maxwell equation
\mu_{0}\bm{j}=\bm{\nabla}\times\bm{B}
, which in the Coulomb gauge reads,

\bm{\nabla}^{2}\bm{A}=e^{2}\mu_{0}D\left[\bm{A}+\bm{a}_{t}\right]\delta(z).

(4)

The solution of this equation for a given distribution of the effective gauge field
\bm{a}(\bm{r})
determines the induced vector potential and the charge current.
At the solution point, by substituting the Maxwell equation back into Eq. (
S1
), we get the change of free energy due to supercurrents generated by the external exchange field, (see section 1 of the supplementary material
sup
)

F=-\frac{1}{2}\int d^{2}\bm{r}\,\bm{j}\bm{a}.

(5)

Remarkably, the free energy is determined by the supercurrent in the regions in which
\bm{a}(\bm{r})
is finite,
i.e.
the regions where the exchange field
\bm{h}(\bm{r})
is finite.

We now consider a set of magnetic regions (impurities) located in the superconducting plane at the points
\bm{r}_{i}
, such that the distance between the regions is much larger than their size. In this case, the distribution of the induced supercurrents almost everywhere as well as the change of the free energy become independent on the size/shape of the impurities and the corresponding exchange field can be approximated as
\bm{h}(\bm{r})=\sum_{i}J_{0}\bm{m}_{i}\delta(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{i})
. Here
\bm{m}_{i}
is the total magnetization (spin) of the ith impurity, and
J_{0}
is the electron-impurity exchange coupling.

By solving the Maxwell-London equation (
4
) and inserting the supercurrent
\bm{j}=-e^{2}D(\bm{A}+\bm{a}_{t})
into Eq. (
5
) we can identify the part of the free energy responsible for the supercurrent-induced long-range magnetic interaction
(see section 2 of supplementary material
sup
):

F_{I}=-\frac{Z}{2}\sum_{i\neq i}(\bm{m}_{i}\cdot\nabla)(\bm{m}_{j}\cdot\nabla)V(r_{ij})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}F_{I}^{ij},

(6)

where
Z=DJ_{0}^{2}\Gamma^{2}/2\pi
,
r_{ij}=|\bm{r}_{i}-\bm{r}_{j}|
is the distance between magnetic impurities, and
V(r)
is the dimensionless Keldysh potential,

V(r)=\frac{\pi}{2}\left[H_{0}\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)-Y_{0}\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)\right].

(7)

Here
H_{0}
is the Struve function,
Y_{0}
is the second kind Bessel function, and
r_{0}
is the Pearl length
r_{0}=2/e^{2}D\mu_{0}

Pearl 1964
.

The appearance of the Keldysh potential is quite remarkable. Usually, it describes the electrostatic potential of a point charge confined to a polarizable insulating plane
Keldysh 1979
;
Cudazzo et al. 2011
. It interpolates between the 2D (
V\sim-\ln r
) and the 3D (
V\sim{1}/{r}
) forms of the Coulomb potential with the crossover scale given by
r_{0}
in Eq. (
7
). Here it plays a similar role by interpolating between the 2D and 3D form of the induced DDI between the impurity spins.

Working out the derivatives in the pairwise part
F_{I}^{ij}
of the interaction energy Eq. (
6
) we find,

F_{I}^{ij}=J_{\perp}(r_{ij})\bm{m}_{i\perp}\bm{m}_{j\perp}-J_{\parallel}(r_{ij})\bm{m}_{i\parallel}\bm{m}_{j\parallel}\;,

(8)

with

J_{\perp}(r)=-Z\frac{1}{r}\frac{dV}{dr},\quad J_{\parallel}(r)=Z\frac{d^{2}V}{dr^{2}}.

(9)

To gain insight into the
r
-dependence, it is instructive to use a highly accurate representation of

V(r)
in terms of elementary functions
Cudazzo et al. 2011
, which yields

J_{\perp}=Z\frac{r_{0}}{r^{2}(r+r_{0})},\quad J_{\parallel}=Z\frac{r_{0}(2r+r_{0})}{r^{2}(r+r_{0})^{2}}.

(10)

The interaction as a function of the Pearl length is shown in Fig.
2
a. The important general property is that for any finite
r_{0}
, one obtains
J_{\parallel}>J_{\perp}
.

Let us analyze the case of two impurities. For convenience, we write the interaction as

F_{I}=\frac{J_{\perp}+J_{\parallel}}{2}\left(m_{1\perp}m_{2\perp}-m_{1\parallel}m_{2\parallel}\right)-\frac{J_{\parallel}-J_{\perp}}{2}\bm{m}_{1}\cdot\bm{m}_{2}.

(11)

The first term on the right-hand side alone does not generate a difference in the free energies of the ferromagnetic and anti-ferromagnetic states but rather leads to degenerate ground states with
\theta_{1}=-\theta_{2}
, where
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
are the angles between the impurities magnetic moments and
\bm{r}
as shown in Fig.
2
b. The second term, with the form of isotropic ferromagnetic interaction, breaks the ground state degeneracy, resulting in a ferromagnetic ground state. This ferromagnetic interaction stems from the
\bm{B}^{2}
term in the free energy, which was ignored in previous works
Pershoguba et al. 2015
;
Mal’Shukov 2018
. In superconductors with large Rashba SOC
\Gamma
and
D
are given by
\Gamma=\alpha/v_{F}^{2}
, and
D=\frac{1}{2}\pi v_{F}^{2}N_{0}T\sum_{n}\frac{\Delta^{2}}{(\omega_{n}^{2}+\Delta^{2})^{3/2}}
(see section 3 of supplementary material
sup
). In the limit where the EM field can be neglected
\mu_{0}\rightarrow 0
and
r_{0}\rightarrow\infty
, we get
J_{\parallel}=J_{\perp}
and recover the result of Ref.
Mal’Shukov 2018

The supercurrent
\bm{j}(\bm{r})
induced by a magnetic impurity with magentization
\bm{m}
at the origin, takes the form
sup

\bm{j}(\bm{m},\bm{r})=\frac{1}{\Gamma J_{0}}\left[J_{\parallel}\bm{m}\times\hat{z}-(J_{\parallel}+J_{\perp})(\bm{m}\times\hat{z}\cdot\hat{r})\hat{r}\right],

(12)

where
\hat{r}\equiv\bm{r}/|\bm{r}|
. Due to the linearity of the problem, the total current induced by all impurities is given by sum
\bm{j}_{\rm tot}=\sum_{i}\bm{j}(\bm{m}_{i},\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{i})
. The current distribution for two impurities is shown in Fig.
2
c. Experimentally, the supercurrent distribution can be determined by measuring the local current-induced magnetic field
Lin et al. 2023

The magnetic field
\bm{B}=\bm{\nabla}\times\bm{A}
induced by one impurity at the origin, and evaluated at
z=0
reads,

\bm{B}(\bm{m},\bm{r})=\frac{\Gamma J_{0}}{4\pi}\left[\frac{2}{(r+r_{0})^{3}}+\frac{2r+r_{0}}{r^{2}(r+r_{0})^{2}}\right](\bm{m}\cdot\hat{r})\hat{z}.

(13)

The total magnetic field at
z=0
is then
\bm{B}_{\rm tot}=\sum_{i}\bm{B}(\bm{m}_{i},\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{i})

Next, we consider an infinite Bravais lattice of magnetic impurities, as shown in Fig.

and the shape of the lattice is controlled by the angle

E=\frac{1}{2V}\sum_{i\neq j}F_{I}^{ij}
, where

We notice that the inclusion of the EM field is crucial for computing energy. Otherwise, the interaction
F_{I}^{ij}
scales as
1/r^{2}
and the energy density unphysically diverges in the thermodynamics limit.
With the screening effect of the EM field, according to Eq. (
10
),
F_{I}^{ij}
crosses over from the 2D to 3D DDI and decays as
1/r^{3}
at

, resulting in convergent and extensive energy.

The 3D DDI induced ordered state in a 2D electric dipole lattice has been studied in
Rozenbaum 1991
. It has been shown that a ferroelectric state forms in a triangular lattice, while a square lattice favors a layered anti-ferroelectric state. Since the superconductivity-induced magnetic interaction has a form between 2D and 3D DDI, we expect similar ground states in our model. By minimizing the energy we find that at
T=0
the ground state can be either a ferromagnetic state, Fig.
3
a, induced by the net ferromagnetic interaction
\frac{J_{\parallel}-J_{\perp}}{2}
, or layered anti-ferromagnetic state, Fig.
3

\frac{J_{\parallel}+J_{\perp}}{2}
. At finite temperatures, the order parameter
\Delta(T)
needs to be determined self-consistently.
The superfluid weight
D
and the Pearl length
r_{0}
are calculated using

. The corresponding temperature dependence of
J_{\parallel}
and

is shown in Fig.

With increasing temperature, the superfluid weight is decreased, leading to the suppression of the magnetic interaction. In addition, the relative value of the net ferromagnetic interaction
\frac{J_{\parallel}-J_{\perp}}{2}
compared with the 2D DDI
\frac{J_{\parallel}+J_{\perp}}{2}
becomes smaller at higher temperatures, suggesting a transition to an antiferromagnetic phase at some finite temperature. The phase diagram obtained numerically is shown in Fig.

\mu_{0}\rightarrow 0
, we find that the ground state is always an antiferromagnetic state without the magnetic field.

Conclusion.-

To conclude, we have demonstrated that superconductivity in materials with SOC can induce long-range magnetic interaction. The effects of London-Pearl screening are crucial for proving this result. The exact analytic solution shows that the induced
magnetic interaction has a form of 2D DDI combined with a ferromagnetic coupling. We also demonstrate that in an oblique Bravais lattice of magnetic impurities, the superconductivity can induce a ferromagnetic state in certain parameter regimes. To the best of our knowledge, these results provide the first example of singlet superconductivity-induced ferromagnetic coupling.

In principle, our predictions can be tested in any superconductor that exhibits a sizable Rashba SOC or in a Dirac superconductor with magnetic impurities. Notably, recent experiments
Xiang et al. 2023
;
Qiu et al. 2023
;
Zaki et al. 2021
;
McLaughlin et al. 2021
;
Farhang et al. 2023
, provides compelling evidence of observing such a ferromagnetic state. Specifically, in Ref.
Xiang et al. 2023
, a hysteretic magnetization was observed in Fe(SeTe) with Fe impurities. The magnetization disappears at temperatures above the superconducting critical temperature, indicating that the ferromagnetism is induced by the superconductivity. This observation can be considered as evidence of supercurrent-mediated magnetic interaction in the presence of surface Dirac states.
The realistic values for the parameters of the surface Dirac band in Fe(Te,Se)
Xiang et al. 2023
are given by
Jiang et al. 2019
;
Thampy et al. 2012
:
v_{F}=0.216eV\AA
,
E_{F}=4.5meV
,
\Delta=1.5meV
and
{\cal J}_{0}=50meV
,
|\bm{m}|=5
and the distance between the nearest impurities is
d=2nm
. Here
{\cal J}_{0}
is the exchange interaction defined in a lattice model. In our continuous model, the exchange interaction is
J_{0}={\cal J}_{0}{\cal A}
, where
{\cal A}
is the effective area of the impurity which is assumed to be the lattice constant square
{\cal A}=(0.4nm)^{2}
. We obtain at
T=0
the supercurrent-mediated magnetic interaction is roughly
2meV
, of the same order as the superconducting gap. Thus, the thermal fluctuation can be neglected for low enough temperatures
T\ll\Delta
. Note that the effective magnetic interaction is proportional to
{\cal A}^{2}
, so it can be easily enhanced by increasing the size of the magnetic impurity, for example using islands of a ferromagnetic insulator with a large size. According to Ref.
Qiu et al. 2023
;
Farhang et al. 2023
, the ferromagnetism dwells on the surface of the superconductor, supporting our theory that the SOC is crucial for the formation of the ferromagnetic state.

\bm{j}(r\rightarrow 0)\rightarrow 0
. Thus the free energy is given by

F=-\frac{1}{2}\int d\bm{r}\,\bm{j}\bm{a}.

(S9)

This is equation (4) in the main text.

S2
S2. Derivation of the supercurrent and the interaction energy

Here we consider two magnetic impurities
\bm{m}_{1}
and
\bm{m}_{2}
located at
\bm{r}_{1}
and
\bm{r}_{2}
respectively. The free energy can be written as

F=-\frac{1}{2}\int d\bm{r}\left(\bm{j}_{1}+\bm{j}_{2}\right)\left[\bm{a}_{1}\delta(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{1})+\bm{a}_{2}\delta(\bm{r}-\bm{r}_{2})\right],

(S10)

where
\bm{a}_{1}
and
\bm{a}_{2}
are the effective gauge fields induced by
\bm{m}_{1}
and
\bm{m}_{2}
, respectively
\bm{a}_{1,2}=\Gamma J\bm{m}_{1,2}
.
\bm{j}_{1}
(
\bm{j}_{2}
) is the supercurrent induced by
\bm{m}_{1}
(
\bm{m}_{2}
). Working out the integral we obtain

F=-\frac{1}{2}\left[\bm{j}_{1}(\bm{r}_{2})\bm{a}_{2}+\bm{j}_{2}(\bm{r}_{1})\bm{a}_{1}+j_{1}(r_{1})\bm{a}_{1}+\bm{j}_{2}(\bm{r}_{2})\bm{a}_{2}\right].

(S11)

The first two terms in Eq. (
S11
) represent the interaction energy

F_{I}=-\frac{1}{2}\left[\bm{j}_{1}(\bm{r}_{2})\bm{a}_{2}+\bm{j}_{2}(\bm{r}_{1})\bm{a}_{1}\right]=\bm{j}_{1}(\bm{r}_{2})\bm{a}_{2}.

(S12)

Here we have used
\bm{j}_{1}(\bm{r}_{2})\bm{a}_{2}=\bm{j}_{2}(\bm{r}_{1})\bm{a}_{1}
. The main task is to solve the current distribution
\bm{j}_{1}
. Without loss of generality, we place
\bm{m}_{1}
at the origin
\bm{r}_{1}=0
and calculate the current at position
\bm{r}
. We begin with the expression of the current density

\bm{j}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}eD\left[\bm{\nabla}\phi-2e\bm{A}-2e\bm{a}_{1}\right]\delta(z).

(S13)

Here the effective gauge field
\bm{a}_{1}
has both longitudinal component
\bm{a}_{1l}
and transverse component
\bm{a}_{1t}
. In the momentum space,
\bm{a}_{1l}
and
\bm{a}_{1t}
are defined as

\bm{a}_{1l}(\bm{q})=\bm{q}\left(\bm{q}\cdot\bm{a}_{1}\right)/q,

(S14)

and

\bm{a}_{1t}=\bm{a}_{1}-\bm{a}_{1l}.

(S15)

Due to the charge conservation, the total current density
\bm{j}_{1}
has to be a transverse field with zero longitudinal component
\bm{\nabla}\times\bm{j}_{1}=0
. For convenience, we choose the Coulomb gauge for the
\bm{A}
field,
\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{A}=0
. Thus the phase gradient term should cancel the longitudinal component of
\bm{a}

\bm{\nabla}\phi=2e\bm{a}_{1l}.

(S16)

Then the current density becomes

\bm{j}=-e^{2}D\left(\bm{A}+\bm{a}_{1t}\right)\delta(z).

(S17)

Combining the above expression of the current and the Maxwell equation we arrive at

\bm{\nabla}^{2}\bm{A}=-\mu_{0}\bm{j}=e^{2}D\mu_{0}\left(\bm{A}+\bm{a}_{1t}\right)\delta(z)

(S18)

In the momentum space, we have

-q^{2}\bm{A}=e^{2}D\mu_{0}\int dq_{z}\bm{A}+e^{2}D\mu_{0}\bm{a}_{1t}.

(S19)

Moving
q^{2}
to the right-hand side of Eq. (
S19
) and integrating the equation over
q_{z}
leads to

-K(q_{\parallel})=\frac{e^{2}\mu_{0}DK(q_{\parallel})}{2q_{\parallel}}+\frac{e^{2}\mu_{0}D\bm{a}_{1t}}{2q_{\parallel}}

(S20)

with

K(q_{\parallel})=\bm{A}(z=0)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int dq_{z}\bm{A},

(S21)

and

q_{\parallel}=\sqrt{q_{x}^{2}+q_{y}^{2}}.

(S22)

Solving Eq. (
S20
), we obtain

K(q_{\parallel})=-\frac{\bm{a}_{1t}}{1+q_{\parallel}r_{0}},

(S23)

where
r_{0}
is Pearl length
Pearl 1964
defined as

r_{0}=\frac{2}{e^{2}\mu_{0}D}.

(S24)

Thus the current is given by

\bm{j}_{1}=-e^{2}D\frac{q_{\parallel}r_{0}\bm{a}_{1t}}{1+q_{\parallel}r_{0}}.

(S25)

We perform the 2D Fourier transformation and obtain the current in the real space

\bm{j}_{1}=\frac{e^{2}D}{2\pi}\left[\bm{a}_{1}\partial^{2}_{\bm{r}}-(\bm{a}_{1}\cdot\partial_{\bm{r}})\partial_{\bm{r}}\right]V(r),

(S26)

where
V(r)
is the Keldysh potential givn by

V(r)=\frac{\pi}{2}\left[H_{0}\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)-Y_{0}\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)\right]\delta(z),

(S27)

where
H_{0}
is the Struve function and
Y_{0}
is the second kind Bessel function. Substituting Eq. (
S26
) into Eq. (
S9
) we get the effective magnetic interaction between
\bm{m}_{1}
and
\bm{m}_{2}

F_{I}=-\frac{e^{2}D}{2\pi}\left[\bm{a}_{1}\cdot\bm{a}_{2}\partial^{2}_{\bm{r}}-(\bm{a}_{1}\cdot\partial_{\bm{r}})(\bm{a}_{2}\cdot\partial_{\bm{r}})\right]V(r).

(S28)

In terms of
\bm{m}
, this interaction can be written as

F_{I}=-Z\sum_{\alpha\beta}m_{1\alpha}m_{2\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}V(r),

(S29)

with
Z=e^{2}DJ_{0}^{2}\Gamma^{2}/2\pi
,
\alpha,\beta\equiv x,y
. This is Eq. (6) in the main text. By the simplest possible matching of the two asymptotic behaviors we can construct an approximated expression for the Keldysh potential in terms of elementary functions
Cudazzo et al. 2011

V_{eff}=-\left[\log\left(\frac{r}{r+r_{0}}\right)+\left(r-\log 2\right)e^{-r/r_{0}}\right].

(S30)

Using this approximate expression of the Keldysh potential, we get the approximate interaction

F_{I}=J_{\perp}\bm{m}_{1\perp}\bm{m}_{2\perp}-J_{\parallel}\bm{m}_{1\parallel}\bm{m}_{2\parallel}\;,

(S31)

with

J_{\perp}=Z\frac{r_{0}}{r^{2}(r+r_{0})},\quad J_{\parallel}=Z\frac{r_{0}(2r+r_{0})}{r^{2}(r+r_{0})^{2}}.

(S32)

These are Eq. (8) and (9) in the main text. The supercurrent can be obtained by substituting Eq. (
S30
) into Eq. (
S26
)

\bm{j}(\bm{r}_{j})=\sum_{i}\frac{1}{\Gamma J_{0}}\left[J_{\parallel}\bm{m}_{i}\times\hat{z}-(J_{\parallel}+J_{\perp})(\bm{m}_{i}\times\hat{z}\cdot\hat{r}_{ij})\hat{r}_{ij}\right].

(S33)

This is Eq. (13) in the main text.

S3
S3. Derivation of superfluid weight in Rashba superconductors

We consider a 2D Rashba superductor. We assume that the Rashba spin-orbit coupling is much larger than the pairing
\Delta
so that one can project the Hamiltonian onto the two helical bands denoted by
\lambda=\pm
. This can be described by the following Eilenberger equation
houzet2015quasiclassical

-v_{F}\bm{n}\cdot\left(\bm{\nabla}\hat{g}_{\lambda\bm{n}}-ie\bm{A}[\tau_{3},\hat{g}_{\lambda\bm{n}}]\right)=[\omega_{n}\tau_{3}+\Delta\tau_{1},\hat{g}_{\lambda\bm{n}}],

(S34)

where
\hat{g}_{\lambda\bm{n}}
is the quasiclassical Green function of band
\lambda
with momentum direction
\bm{n}
.
\tau
is the Pauli matrix acting on the particle-hole space.
\Delta
is the pairing gap.
\omega_{n}
is the Matsubara frequency
\omega_{n}=(2n+1)T
, with
n\in Z
and
T
denoting the temperature.
v_{F}
is the Fermi velocity and
e
is the electron charge. Soling the Eilenberger equation with the normalization condition
\hat{g}_{\lambda\bm{n}}^{2}=1
, we get the quasiclassical Green function

\hat{g}_{\lambda\bm{n}}=\frac{(\omega_{n}-iv_{F}e\bm{A}\cdot\bm{n})\tau_{3}+\Delta\tau_{1}}{\sqrt{\left(\omega_{n}-iv_{F}e\bm{A}\cdot\bm{n}\right)^{2}+\Delta^{2}}}.

(S35)

For small gauge field, we can expand
\hat{g}_{\lambda\bm{n}}
up to the first order in
\bm{A}

\hat{g}_{\lambda\bm{n}}\approx\frac{\omega_{n}\tau_{3}+\Delta\tau_{1}}{\sqrt{\omega_{n}^{2}+\Delta^{2}}}-\frac{iv_{F}e\Delta^{2}\bm{A}\cdot\bm{n}\tau_{3}}{\sqrt{\omega_{n}^{2}+\Delta^{2}}^{3}}

(S36)

The supercurrent flowing in the superconductor is given by

\bm{j}=-\frac{1}{2}i\pi e\sum_{\lambda,n}N_{\lambda}T\text{Tr}\langle v_{F}\bm{n}\tau_{3}\hat{g}_{\lambda\bm{n}}\rangle,

(S37)

where
\langle\cdot\rangle
means the average over all the directions.
N_{\lambda}
is the band-dependent density of states near the Fermi energy

N_{\lambda}=\frac{N_{0}}{2}\left(1+\lambda\frac{\alpha}{v_{F}}\right),

(S38)

where
N_{0}
is the total density of states. Substituting Eq. (
S36
) into Eq. (
S37
), we have

\bm{j}=-\frac{1}{2}\pi e^{2}v_{F}^{2}N_{0}T\bm{A}\sum_{n}\frac{\Delta^{2}}{\sqrt{\omega_{n}^{2}+\Delta^{2}}^{3}}

(S39)

The superfluid weight is given by

D=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{e^{2}}\frac{\partial\bm{j}}{\partial\bm{A}}=2\pi v_{F}^{2}N_{0}T\sum_{n}\frac{\Delta^{2}}{\sqrt{\omega_{n}^{2}+\Delta^{2}}}.

(S40)

We use this superfluid weight to determine the Pearl length in Eq. in the main text.

S4
S4. Derivation of
\Gamma
in Rashba superconductors at clean limit

Here we consider a 2D Rashba superconductor with an exchange field described by the Eilenberger equation

-\bm{n}\cdot\left(v_{F}\bm{\nabla}\hat{g}_{\lambda\bm{n}}-ie\lambda\bm{h}\times\hat{z}[\tau_{3},\hat{g}_{\lambda\bm{n}}]\right)=[\omega_{n}\tau_{3}+\Delta\tau_{1},\hat{g}_{\lambda\bm{n}}].

(S41)

The solution of this Eilenber equation is

\hat{g}_{\lambda\bm{n}}=\frac{(\omega_{n}-i\lambda e\bm{h}\times\hat{z}\cdot\bm{n})\tau_{3}+\Delta\tau_{1}}{\sqrt{\left(\omega_{n}-i\lambda e\bm{h}\times\hat{z}\cdot\bm{n}\right)^{2}+\Delta^{2}}}.

(S42)

Using the same method as we did in section S3, we get the anomalous current induced by the exchange field

\bm{j}^{a}=-\frac{1}{2}\pi e^{2}\alpha\bm{h}\times\hat{z}N_{0}T\sum_{n}\frac{\Delta^{2}}{\sqrt{\omega_{n}^{2}+\Delta^{2}}}.

(S43)

The
\Gamma
parameter by definition is obtained from Eq. (
S40
) and Eq. (
S43
)

\Gamma=\frac{\alpha}{v_{F}^{2}}.

(S44)

This is what we show above Eq. (2) in the main text.

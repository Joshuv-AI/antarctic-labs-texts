GRAVITATIONAL WAVES IN SCALAR-TENSOR THEORY TO ONE-AND-A-HALF POST-NEWTONIAN ORDER
David TRESTINI

Abstract
We compute the gravitational waves generated by compact binary systems in a class of massless scalar-tensor (ST) theories to the 1.5 post-Newtonian (1.5PN) order beyond the standard quadrupole radiation in general relativity (GR). Using and adapting to ST theories the multipolar-post-Minkowskian and post-Newtonian formalisms originally defined in GR, we obtain the tail and non-linear memory terms associated with the dipole radiation in ST theory. The multipole moments and GW flux of compact binaries are derived for general orbits including the new 1.5PN contribution, and comparison is made with previous results in the literature. In the case of quasi-circular orbits, we present ready-to-use templates for the data analysis of detectors, and for the first time the scalar GW modes for comparisons with numerical relativity results.

We consider a generic class of scalar-tensor theories in which a single massless scalar field

2
The Multipolar post-Minkowskian formalism in scalar-tensor theories

2.1
The scalar-tensor multipole moments

In order to solve the vacuum field equations
\Box h^{\mu\nu}=\Lambda^{\mu\nu}
and
\Box\psi=\Lambda_{s}
in the exterior region of the isolated matter system, we perform a multipolar decomposition (indicated by
\mathcal{M}
) conjointly with a non-linear post-Minkowskian expansion
[
4
]
. In the exterior region we can formally write the multipolar-post-Minkowskian expansion of the tensor and scalar field as

\displaystyle h^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{ext}}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathcal{M}(h^{\mu\nu})=Gh_{1}^{\mu\nu}+G^{2}h_{2}^{\mu\nu}+{\mathcal{O}}\left(G^{3}\right)\,,

(6a)

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathrm{ext}}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathcal{M}(\psi)=G\psi_{1}+G^{2}\psi_{2}+{\mathcal{O}}\left(G^{3}\right)\,.

(6b)

To obtain the expressions of the source and current moments
I_{L}
and
J_{L}
(relative to the tensor sector) as functions of the source, the procedure is essentially the same as in GR, with the caveat that the moments are defined with respect to the conformal metric. The multipole expansion of
h^{\mu\nu}
is obtained using a matching to the PN expansion in the near zone of the source
[
5
]
. In addition to
I_{L}
and
J_{L}
, we introduce a new set of scalar multipole moments which we call
I_{L}^{s}
, also chosen to be symmetric-trace-free (STF). We find

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\psi)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\mathrm{FP}}_{B=0}\,\Box^{-1}_{\mathrm{ret}}\Bigl[\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)^{B}\mathcal{M}(\Lambda^{s})\Bigr]+G\psi_{1}\,,

(7a)

\displaystyle\text{with}\quad\psi_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{c^{2}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-)^{\ell}}{\ell!}\,\partial_{L}\!\left[\frac{I_{L}^{s}(u)}{r}\right]\,.

(7b)

Similarly to the GR case, the scalar moments are obtained in closed form and we find that

I^{s}_{L}(u)=\mathop{\mathrm{FP}}_{B=0}\int\differential^{3}\mathbf{x}\,\tilde{r}^{B}\int_{-1}^{1}\differential z\,\delta_{\ell}(z)\,\hat{x}_{L}\Sigma^{s}(\mathbf{x},u+zr/c)\,.

(8)

where

\Sigma_{s}\equiv\frac{\varphi}{\phi_{0}(3+2\omega)}\sqrt{-g}\left(T-2\varphi\frac{\partial T}{\partial\varphi}\right)-\frac{c^{4}}{8\pi G}\Lambda_{s}

Note that in contrast to the tensor monopole
I
, the scalar monopole
I^{s}
is not constant but its time-variation will be a post-Newtonian effect,
i.e.

\differential I^{s}/\differential t=\mathcal{O}(c^{-2})
.

2.2
Non-linear effects in ST theory

Once the vacuum linearized solutions for scalar (
7b
) and tensor fields are obtained, with explicit expressions for the multipole moments as integrals over the PN expansions of
\tau^{\mu\nu}
and
\tau_{s}
, the non-linear contributions can be computed by adapting to ST theories the Multipolar-post-Minkowskian (MPM) algorithm
[
4
]
.

The equations to be solved for the quadratic metric are

\displaystyle\Box h^{\mu\nu}_{2}

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{\mu\nu}_{\text{GR},2}(h_{1},h_{1})+\Lambda^{\mu\nu}_{\phi,2}(\psi_{1},\psi_{1})\,,

(9a)

\displaystyle\Box\psi_{2}

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{(h\times\psi)}_{\text{S},2}(h_{1},\psi_{1})+\Lambda^{(\psi\times\psi)}_{\text{S},2}(\psi_{1},\psi_{1})\,,

(9b)

where the source terms are reduced to their quadratic contributions with respect to
\psi_{1}
and
h_{1}
.

Following the MPM algorithm, we can solve these equations and find a solution valid in the exterior zone and in harmonic gauge. However, its expression contains logarithms at spatial infinity. Of course, this is only a coordinate effect, which can be cured by introducing a radiative type coordinate system
(T,R)
, with
U\equiv T-R/c
being an asymptotically null coordinate such that
U=u-\frac{2GI}{c^{3}}\ln\left(\frac{r}{cb}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)

where
I
is the mass monopole moment and
b
is an arbitrary constant time-scale.

2.3
Some non-local effects: tails and memory

At 1.5PN order, the non-linear metric includes non-local tail and memory terms. Tails can be interpreted as linear waves reflecting against the curvature of space-time: the lowest order tail effect is a quadrupole-mass interaction for gravitational waves and a dipole-mass interaction for a scalar wave. Conversely, memory effects can be seen as linear waves generated by linear waves. In our case, it is a scalar dipole-dipole interaction that enters the gravitational waveform. The non-local expressions for the tail and memory terms that enter the quadratic waveform are:

\displaystyle h^{ij}_{2,\text{TT}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2G^{2}}{c^{7}R}\perp^{\text{TT}}_{ijab}\Bigg\{\frac{2M}{\phi_{0}}\int_{-\infty}^{U}\!\differential V\overset{(4)}{I_{ij}}(V)\left[\ln{\left(\frac{U-V}{2b} \right)}+\frac{11}{12}\right]+\frac{3+2\omega_{0}}{3}\int_{-\infty}^{U}\!\differential V\overset{(2)}{I^{s}_{\langle i}}(V)\overset{(2)}{I^{s}_{j\rangle}}(V)\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\qquad+\text{(instantaneous terms)}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{c^{8}}\right)\,,

(10a)

\displaystyle\psi_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4G^{2}M}{c^{6}R\phi_{0}}\Bigg\{N_{i}\int_{-\infty}^{U}\differential V\overset{(3)}{I_{i}^{s}}(V)\left[\ln{\left(\frac{U-V}{2b} \right)}+1\right]+\frac{1}{c\phi_{0}}\int_{-\infty}^{U}\differential V\overset{(2)}{E^{s}}(V)\ln\left(\frac{U-V}{2b}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\frac{N_{ij}}{2c}\int_{-\infty}^{U}\differential V\overset{(4)}{I^{s}_{ij}}(V)\left[\ln{\left(\frac{U-V}{2b} \right)}\right]\Bigg\}+\text{(instantaneous terms)}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{c^{8}}\right)\,,

(10b)

where
F^{(n)}(t)\equiv\differential^{n}F/\differential t^{n}
,
\perp^{\text{TT}}_{ijab}
is the transverse-traceless (TT) projector,
M\equiv I/\phi_{0}
and
E_{s}
is defined such that
\differential E_{s}/\differential t\equiv c^{2}\phi_{0}\,\differential I_{s}/\differential t=\mathcal{O}(1)
, since
\differential I^{s}/\differential t=\mathcal{O}(c^{-2})
.

3
Results

3.1
Spherical harmonic decomposition of the fields

For quasi-circular orbits, we introduce the dimensionless PN variable
x\equiv(\tilde{G}\alpha m\omega/c^{3})^{2/3}
, thanks to which the tail and memory terms can be integrated explicitly. The expressions for
h^{\mu\nu}
and
\psi
simplify considerably, and we can easily decompose the tensor field on its “plus” and “cross” modes, and perform the following spherical harmonic expansion for the tensor and scalar modes

h_{+}-\mathrm{i}h_{-}=\sum_{\ell=2}^{+\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}h^{\ell m}\,_{-2}Y^{\ell m}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\quad\psi=\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\psi^{\ell m}\ Y^{\ell m}

(11)

where we have used spin-weighted spherical harmonics of weight -2 and 0. We then define the phase variable
\psi
so as to remove most of the logarithms from the expressions of
h^{\ell m}
and
\psi^{\ell m}
, which can always be written as

h^{\ell m}=\frac{2\tilde{G}(1-\zeta)m\nu x}{Rc^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{16\pi}{5}}\,\hat{H}^{\ell m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}m\psi}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\psi^{\ell m}=\frac{2i\tilde{G}\zeta\sqrt{\alpha}\mathcal{S}_{-}m\nu\sqrt{x}}{Rc^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{8\pi}{3}}\,\hat{\Psi}^{\ell m}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}m\psi}\,,

(12a)

with normalized modes
\hat{H}^{\ell m}
and
\hat{\Psi}^{\ell m}
defined such that
\hat{H}^{22}=1+\mathcal{O}(x)
and
\hat{\Psi}^{11}=1+\mathcal{O}({x})
.

In this work, we computed all relevant tensor and scalar modes to 1.5PN order. The scalar modes had never been computed, and the dominant
\psi^{11}
mode is given by :

\displaystyle\hat{\Psi}^{11}

\displaystyle=1+x\Bigg\{-\frac{9}{5}-\frac{2}{3}\bar{\beta}_{+}-\frac{1}{3}\bar{\gamma}+2\bar{\beta}_{+}\bar{\gamma}^{-1}+\frac{2\bar{\beta}_{-}\bar{\gamma}^{-1}\mathcal{S}_{+}}{\mathcal{S}_{-}}+\delta\Big[\frac{2}{3}\bar{\beta}_{-}-2\bar{\beta}_{-}\bar{\gamma}^{-1}+\frac{4\mathcal{S}_{+}}{5\mathcal{S}_{-}}-\frac{2\bar{\beta}_{+}\bar{\gamma}^{-1}\mathcal{S}_{+}}{\mathcal{S}_{-}}\Big]+\frac{14}{15}\nu\Bigg\}

\displaystyle+x^{3/2}\Bigg\{\frac{(2+\bar{\gamma})\pi}{2}-i\bigg(\frac{\left(2+\bar{\gamma}\right)\left(1+12\ln(2)\right)}{12}+\frac{1}{3}\zeta\left(\mathcal{S}_{+}^{2}+\mathcal{S}_{-}^{2}\right)+\frac{4}{3}\zeta\mathcal{S}_{-}^{2}\nu+\frac{2}{3}\zeta\mathcal{S}_{-}\mathcal{S}_{+}\delta\bigg)\Bigg\}\,,

(13)

where the different post-Newtonian parameters are defined in our main paper
[
6
]
.

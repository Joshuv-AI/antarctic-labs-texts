Local freeness in frame bundle prolongations of C ∞ C^{\infty} actions
Scot Adams

Abstract
. We prove a local freeness result for C ∞ C^{\infty} actions.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
57Sxx, 58A05, 58A20, 53A55

1.
Introduction

Let a real Lie group have a
C^{\infty}
action on a
C^{\infty}
real manifold
M
.
Let
G^{\circ}
be the identity component of
G
.
Assume the fixpoint set of any nontrivial element of
G^{\circ}
has empty interior in
M
.
Let
n:=\hbox{dim\,}G
.
Assume
n\geq 1
.
Let
F
be the frame bundle of
M
of order
n-1
.
We prove (Theorem
12.1
): there exists a
G
-invariant dense open subset
Q
of
F

such that the
G
-action on
Q
has discrete stabilizers.

6.
Fixpoint rare for connected locally compact groups

LEMMA 6.1
.

Let
G
be a connected locally compact Hausdorff topological group
acting on a Hausdorff topological space
X
.
Assume, for all
g\in G
, that the map
x\mapsto gx:X\to X
is continuous.
Assume, for all
x\in X
, that the map
g\mapsto gx:G\to X
is continuous.
Assume, for every nonempty
G
-invariant open subset
V
of
X
,
that the
G
-action on
V
is effective.
Then the
G
-action on
X
is fixpoint rare.

Proof.

Let
g_{0}\in G\backslash\{1_{G}\}
be given, and let
F_{0}:=\hbox{Fix}_{X}(g_{0})
.
We wish to show that
F_{0}
has empty interior in
X
.
Let
V_{0}
be the interior in
X
of
F_{0}
.
Assume that
V_{0}\neq\emptyset
.
We aim for a contradiction.

Let
V_{1}:=GV_{0}
.
As
V_{1}
is a nonempty
G
-invariant open subset of
X
,
by hypothesis, the
G
-action on
V_{1}
is effective.
Then
\hbox{Fix}_{V_{1}}(g_{0})\subsetneq V_{1}
.
Choose
x_{1}\in V_{1}
such that
x_{1}\notin\hbox{Fix}_{V_{1}}(g_{0})
.
Then we have
g_{0}x_{1}\neq x_{1}
.
Let
H:=\hbox{Stab}_{G}(x_{1})
.
Then
g_{0}\notin H
.
Let
U_{0}:=G\backslash H
be the complement in
G
of
H
.
Then
U_{0}
is an open neighborhood in
G
of
g_{0}
.
Let
U:=g_{0}^{-1}U_{0}
.
Then
U
is an open neighborhood in
G
of
1_{G}
.
By the Approximation Theorem in §4.6, p. 175 of
[
MZ
]
,
choose a compact normal subgroup
K
of
G
such that
K\subseteq U

and such that
G/K
admits a Lie group structure compatible with its quotient topology.
Let
\overline{G}:=G/K
, and give
\overline{G}

its quotient topology and compatible Lie group structure.
Then give the Lie group
\overline{G}
its natural
C^{\omega}
manifold structure.
Let
\pi:G\to\overline{G}
be the canonical homomorphism.
Let
\overline{g_{0}}:=\pi(g_{0})
.

Since
x_{1}\in V_{1}=GV_{0}
,
choose
c\in G
and
x_{0}\in V_{0}
such that
x_{1}=cx_{0}
.
Let
A:=\{g\in G\,|\,gx_{1}\in V_{0}\}
.
Then
Ax_{1}\subseteq V_{0}
.
As
c^{-1}x_{1}=x_{0}\in V_{0}
, it follows that
c^{-1}\in A
, and so
A\neq\emptyset
.
Also, since
g\mapsto gx_{1}:G\to X
is continuous,
we see that
A
is open in
G
.
Let
\overline{A}:=\pi(A)
.
Since
\pi:G\to\overline{G}
is an open mapping
and since
A
is a nonempty open subset of
G
,
we see that
\overline{A}
is a nonempty open subset of
\overline{G}
.
Let
\overline{H}:=\pi(H)
.
Then
\pi^{*}(\overline{H})=HK
.
Since
H
is closed in
G
and
K
is compact,
it follows that
HK
is closed in
G
.
Then
\overline{H}
is closed in
\overline{G}
.
Let
Y:=\overline{G}/\,\overline{H}
.
Let
p:\overline{G}\to Y
be the canonical map.
By the theory of homogeneous spaces of Lie groups,
give
Y
the unique
C^{\omega}
manifold structure under which
p:\overline{G}\to Y
is a
C^{\omega}
submersion.
Let
y_{1}:=p(1_{\overline{G}})
.
Then
\hbox{Stab}_{\overline{G}}(y_{1})=\overline{H}
.

Claim 1:

\forall y\in Y,\,\overline{g_{0}}y=y
.

Proof of Claim 1:

Let
W:=p(\overline{A})
.
Since
G
is connected,
\overline{G}
is connected, so
Y
is connected.
Because
p:\overline{G}\to Y
is open and because
\overline{A}
is a nonempty open subset of
\overline{G}
,
we see that
W
is a nonempty open subset of
Y
.
Then, as
y\mapsto\overline{g_{0}}y:Y\to Y
is
C^{\omega}
,
it suffices to show, for all
w\in W
, that
\overline{g_{0}}w=w
.
Let
w\in W
be given.
We wish to prove that
\overline{g_{0}}w=w
.

As
w\in W=p(\overline{A})=p(\pi(A))
,
choose
a\in A
such that

.
Let
g_{1}:=a^{-1}g_{0}a
.
Let
\overline{g_{1}}:=\pi(g_{1})
and
\overline{a}:=\pi(a)
.
Then
\overline{a}\,\overline{g_{1}}\,\overline{a}^{-1}=\overline{g_{0}}
.
Because

,
we see that

, so
g_{1}x_{1}=a^{-1}g_{0}ax_{1}=a^{-1}ax_{1}=x_{1}
.
Then

,
and it follows that

\overline{g_{1}}=\pi(g_{1})\in\pi(H)=\overline{H}=\hbox{Stab}_{\overline{G}}(y_{1})
.

\overline{g_{1}}y_{1}=y_{1}
,

.

.

End of proof of Claim 1.

By Claim 1, we have
\overline{g_{0}}y_{1}=y_{1}
, so
\overline{g_{0}}\in\hbox{Stab}_{\overline{G}}(y_{1})
.
It follows that
\pi(g_{0})=\overline{g_{0}}\in\hbox{Stab}_{\overline{G}}(y_{1})=\overline{H}
.
Then

.
Choose
h\in H
,

such that

.
Then

, so

.
Then
h\in H
and
h\notin H
.
Contradiction.
∎

In Lemma
6.1
, we cannot drop the assumption that

is connected,
even if we add the assumption that

is connected:

Example:

Let

.
Let
{\mathcal{D}}

.

.

7.
Induced maps on polynomial spaces

Let
j,d\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Let
E:={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Let
V:={\mathcal{P}}_{E,E}^{0,j}
.
Let
z:=0_{E}=0_{d}
.
Define
\zeta:V\to E
by
\zeta(P)=P(z)
.
For all
\omega\in C_{O}^{\infty}(E,E)
,
define
\omega_{*}:\zeta^{*}(\hbox{dom}[\omega])\to V
by
\omega_{*}(P)=\hbox{Tay}_{z}^{j}(\omega\circ P)
;

\omega_{*}\in C_{O}^{\infty}(V,V)
.

LEMMA 7.1
.

Let
f\in C_{O}^{\infty}(E,E)
.
Let
i\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
Let
Q\in V
,
and assume that
f\sim{\bf 0}_{E,E}\,\,[[i+j,Q(z)]]
.
Then

.

Proof.

Let
V_{0}:={\mathcal{P}}_{E,{\mathbb{R}}}^{0,j}
.
Let
(P,y)\mapsto P\odot y:V_{0}\times E\to V

be the bilinear map defined by
(P\odot y)(w)=[P(w)]y
.
Let
A:=\{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}~\hbox{s.t.}~|\alpha|\leq j\}
.
Let
U:=\hbox{dom}[f]
and let
p:=Q(z)
.
For all
\alpha\in A
, let
H_{\alpha}:E\to V_{0}

be defined by
\displaystyle{(H_{\alpha}(x))(w)=\frac{(w-x)^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}}
,
and let
\eta_{\alpha}:U\to V

be defined by
\eta_{\alpha}(x)=[H_{\alpha}(x)]\odot[(\partial^{\alpha}f)(x)]
.
Since
f\sim{\bf 0}_{E,E}\,\,[[i+j,p]]
,
it follows, for all
\alpha\in A
, that
\partial^{\alpha}f\sim{\bf 0}_{E,E}\,\,[[i,p]]
.
So, by the Product Rule for
\odot
,
we conclude, for all
\alpha\in A
, that
\eta_{\alpha}\sim{\bf 0}_{E,V}\,\,[[i,p]]
.

Define
\tau:U\to V
by
\tau(x)=\hbox{Tay}_{x}^{j}(f)
.
For all
x\in U
, for all
w\in E
,

\displaystyle(\tau(x))(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in A}\,\,\left[\frac{(w-x)^{\alpha}}{\alpha!}\right]\,\left[(\partial^{\alpha}f)(x)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in A}\,\,[(H_{\alpha}(x))(w)]\,[(\partial^{\alpha}f)(x)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in A}\,\,\bigg([H_{\alpha}(x)]\odot[(\partial^{\alpha}f)(x)]\bigg)(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in A}\,\,(\eta_{\alpha}(x))(w).

Then
\displaystyle{\tau=\sum_{\alpha\in A}\,\eta_{\alpha}}
.
Then
\tau\sim{\bf 0}_{E,V}\,\,[[i,p]]
.
So, since
\zeta(Q)=Q(z)=p
,
it follows that
\tau\circ\zeta\sim{\bf 0}_{E,V}\circ\zeta\,\,[[i,Q]]
.
Let
\mu_{0}:=\tau\circ\zeta:\zeta^{*}(U)\to V
.
Let
\nu_{0}:={\bf 0}_{V,V}:V\to V
.
Then we have
\nu_{0}={\bf 0}_{E,V}\circ\zeta
.
It follows that
\mu_{0}\sim\nu_{0}\,\,[[i,Q]]
.
Define
\mu:\zeta^{*}(U)\to V\times V
and
\nu:V\to V\times V
by

\mu(P)=(\mu_{0}(P),P)\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad\nu(P)=(\nu_{0}(P),P).

Then
\mu\sim\nu\,\,[[i,Q]]
.
Define
\Omega:V\times V\to V
by
\Omega(P,\Pi)=\hbox{Tay}_{z}^{j}(P\circ\Pi)
.
Then
\Omega\circ\mu\sim\Omega\circ\nu\,\,[[i,Q]]
.
It therefore suffices to show both that
f_{*}=\Omega\circ\mu
and that
{\bf 0}_{V,V}=\Omega\circ\nu
.

For all
P\in V
, we have
{\bf 0}_{V,V}(P)=0_{V}={\bf 0}_{E,E}
, and so

\displaystyle(\Omega\circ\nu)(P)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega(\nu(P))\,\,=\,\,\Omega(\nu_{0}(P),P)\,\,=\,\,\Omega({\bf 0}_{V,V}(P),P)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega({\bf 0}_{E,E},P)\,\,=\,\,\hbox{Tay}_{z}^{j}({\bf 0}_{E,E}\circ P)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbox{Tay}_{z}^{j}({\bf 0}_{E,E})\,\,=\,\,{\bf 0}_{E,E}\,\,=\,\,{\bf 0}_{V,V}(P).

Then
\Omega\circ\nu={\bf 0}_{V,V}
.
It remains to show that

.

Because
\hbox{im}[\mu]\subseteq V\times V=\hbox{dom}[\Omega]
,
we get
\hbox{dom}[\Omega\circ\mu]=\hbox{dom}[\mu]
.
Then
\hbox{dom}[\Omega\circ\mu]=\zeta^{*}(U)
.
Also,
\hbox{dom}[f_{*}]=\zeta^{*}(\hbox{dom}[f])=\zeta^{*}(U)
.
Let
P\in\zeta^{*}(U)
be given.
We wish to prove that
f_{*}(P)=(\Omega\circ\mu)(P)
.

Let

.
Then

.

.

,
let
R^{\prime}_{\sigma}:=\{\rho\in R_{\sigma}~\hbox{s.t.}~|\sigma-\rho|\leq j\}
.
Then, for all
w,x\in E
,

\displaystyle(\Lambda(w))(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(F_{*}(T_{w}))(x)\,\,=\,\,(\hbox{Tay}_{z}^{j}(F\circ T_{w}))(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta\in D}\,\,\sum_{\sigma\in S}\,\,c_{\delta}^{\sigma}\sum_{\rho\in R^{\prime}_{\sigma}}\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}\sigma\\
\rho\end{array}\!\!\!\right)w^{\rho}x^{\sigma-\rho}\varepsilon_{\delta}.

Let
\sigma_{0}\in S
and
\delta_{0}\in D
be given.
We wish to show that
c_{\delta_{0}}^{\sigma_{0}}=0
.

Let
A:=\{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}~\hbox{s.t.}~|\alpha|\leq i\}
.
Let
B:=\{\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}~\hbox{s.t.}~|\beta|\leq j\}
.
Then
A+B=\{\sigma\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}~\hbox{s.t.}~|\sigma|\leq i+j\}=S
.
So, since
\sigma_{0}\in S=A+B
,
choose
\alpha_{0}\in A
and
\beta_{0}\in B
such that
\sigma_{0}=\alpha_{0}+\beta_{0}
.
Then
\alpha_{0}\leq\alpha_{0}+\beta_{0}=\sigma_{0}
, so
\alpha_{0}\in R_{\sigma_{0}}
.
Also, we have
|\sigma_{0}-\alpha_{0}|=|\beta_{0}|\leq j
.
Then
\alpha_{0}\in R^{\prime}_{\sigma_{0}}
.
Let
S^{\prime}:=\{\sigma\in S~\hbox{s.t.}~\alpha_{0}\in R^{\prime}_{\sigma}\}
.
Then
\sigma_{0}\in S^{\prime}
.
Let
\displaystyle{\xi:=\frac{(\partial^{\alpha_{0}}\Lambda)(z)}{\alpha_{0}!}\in V}
.
Then, for all
x\in E
, we have

\displaystyle\xi(x)\quad

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{\delta\in D}\,\,\sum_{\sigma\in S^{\prime}}\,\,c_{\delta}^{\sigma}\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}\sigma\\
\alpha_{0}\end{array}\!\!\!\right)x^{\sigma-\alpha_{0}}\varepsilon_{\delta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{\delta\in D}\,\,\,\sum_{\beta\in S^{\prime}-\alpha_{0}}\,\,\,c_{\delta}^{\alpha_{0}+\beta}\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}\alpha_{0}+\beta\\
\alpha_{0}\end{array}\!\!\!\right)x^{\beta}\varepsilon_{\delta}.

Since
\alpha_{0}\in A
, we have
|\alpha_{0}|\leq i
.
So, as
\Lambda\sim{\bf 0}_{E,V}\,\,[[i,z]]
,
we get
(\partial^{\alpha_{0}}\Lambda)(z)=0_{V}
.
Then
\xi=0_{V}={\bf 0}_{E,E}
.
Since
\alpha_{0}+\beta_{0}=\sigma_{0}\in S^{\prime}
, we get
\beta_{0}\in S^{\prime}-\alpha_{0}
.
Let
\displaystyle{u:=\frac{(\partial^{\beta_{0}}\xi)(z)}{\beta_{0}!}\in E}
.
Then

u\,\,=\,\,\sum_{\delta\in D}\,\,c_{\delta}^{\alpha_{0}+\beta_{0}}\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}\alpha_{0}+\beta_{0}\\
\alpha_{0}\end{array}\!\!\!\right)\varepsilon_{\delta}\,\,=\,\,\sum_{\delta\in D}\,\,c_{\delta}^{\sigma_{0}}\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}\sigma_{0}\\
\alpha_{0}\end{array}\!\!\!\right)\varepsilon_{\delta}.

As
\xi={\bf 0}_{E,E}
, we see that
(\partial^{\beta_{0}}\xi)(z)=0_{E}
.
Then
u=0_{E}=0_{d}
.
Let
\pi:E\to{\mathbb{R}}
denote projection onto the
\delta_{0}
coordinate,
defined by
\pi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})=x_{\delta_{0}}
.
Then
c_{\delta_{0}}^{\sigma_{0}}\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}\sigma_{0}\\
\alpha_{0}\end{array}\!\!\!\right)=\pi(u)=\pi(0_{d})=0
.
So, since
\left(\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}\sigma_{0}\\
\alpha_{0}\end{array}\!\!\!\right)\neq 0
,
we conclude that
c_{\delta_{0}}^{\sigma_{0}}=0
, as desired.
∎

8.
Induced maps on frame bundles

Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
and let
M
and
N
both be
d
-dimensional manifolds.

LEMMA 8.1
.

Let
f,\phi\in D_{O}^{\infty}(M,N)
,

i,j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
q\in F^{j}M
,
p:=\pi_{M}^{j}(q)
.
Then:
{\bm{(}}\,f\sim\phi\,\,[[i+j,p]]\,{\bm{)}}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad{\bm{(}}\,F^{j}f\sim F^{j}\phi\,\,[[i,q]]\,{\bm{)}}
.

Proof.

If
j=0
, then, by identifying
F^{j}f
with
f
and
F^{j}\phi
with
\phi
, the result follows.
We therefore assume that
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
We also assume

{\bm{(}}\,p\in\hbox{dom}[f]\,{\bm{)}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\bm{(}}\,p\in\hbox{dom}[\phi]\,{\bm{)}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\bm{(}}\,f(p)=\phi(p)\,{\bm{)}};

otherwise, both
{\bm{(}}\,f\sim\phi\,\,[[i+j,p]]\,{\bm{)}}

and
{\bm{(}}\,F^{j}f\sim F^{j}\phi\,\,[[i,q]]\,{\bm{)}}
are false.

Let
h:=\phi^{-1}\circ f\in D_{O}^{\infty}(M,M)
.
Let
\iota:={\rm id}_{M}:M\to M
.
Then
h(p)=p=\iota(p)
and
F^{j}h=(F^{j}\phi)^{-1}\circ(F^{j}f)
.
It suffices to prove:

{\bm{(}}\,h\sim\iota\,[[i+j,p]]\,{\bm{)}}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad{\bm{(}}\,F^{j}h\sim F^{j}\iota\,[[i,q]]\,{\bm{)}}.

Let
E:={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
z:=0_{d}
,
I:={\rm id}_{E}:E\to E
,
\sigma:=J_{E}^{j}I\in F^{j}E
.
Then
\pi_{E}^{j}(\sigma)=\pi_{E}^{j}(J_{E}^{j}I)=I(z)=z
.
Choose
\lambda\in{\mathcal{R}}_{M}^{0}
such that
q=J_{M}^{j}\lambda
.
Then
(F^{j}\lambda)(\sigma)=(F^{j}\lambda)(J_{E}^{j}I)=J_{M}^{j}(\lambda\circ I)=J_{M}^{j}\lambda=q
.
Also, we have
p=\pi_{M}^{j}(q)=\pi_{M}^{j}(J_{M}^{j}\lambda)=\lambda(z)
.
Let
\psi:=\lambda^{-1}\circ h\circ\lambda\in D_{O}^{\infty}(E,E)
.
Then
\psi(z)\!=\!z\!=\!I(z)

and
F^{j}\psi\!=\!(F^{j}\lambda)^{-1}\circ(F^{j}h)\circ(F^{j}\lambda)
.
It suffices to prove:

{\bm{(}}\,\psi\sim I\,[[i+j,z]]\,{\bf)}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad{\bf(}\,F^{j}\psi\sim F^{j}I\,[[i,\sigma]]\,{\bf)}.

Let
V:={\mathcal{P}}_{E,E}^{0,j}
.
Let
\hbox{det\,}:{\mathcal{P}}_{E,E}^{1}\to{\mathbb{R}}
denote the determinant function.
Let
V^{\times}:=\{P\in V\,|\,\hbox{det\,}(\hbox{lin}_{z}^{P})\neq 0\}
.
Define
T:F^{j}E\to V^{\times}
by
T(J_{E}^{j}\lambda)=\hbox{Tay}_{z}^{j}(\lambda)
.
Then
T:F^{j}E\to V^{\times}
is a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism.

Let
U:=\hbox{dom}[\psi]
.
Then
U
is an open neighborhood in
E
of
z
.
Let
\chi:=\psi-I:U\to E
.
Define
\zeta:V\to E
by
\zeta(P)=P(z)
.
For all
\omega\in C_{O}^{\infty}(E,E)
,
define
\omega_{*}:\zeta^{*}(\hbox{dom}[\omega])\to V
by
\omega_{*}(P)=\hbox{Tay}_{z}^{j}(\omega\circ P)
;
then
\omega_{*}\in C_{O}^{\infty}(V,V)
.
Since
\chi=\psi-I
, we get
\chi_{*}=\psi_{*}-I_{*}
.

Claim 1:
Let
\omega\in D_{O}^{\infty}(E,E)
.
Then
\omega_{*}\circ T=T\circ(F^{j}\omega)
.

Proof of Claim 1:

Because we have
\hbox{im}[F^{j}\omega]\subseteq F^{j}E=\hbox{dom}[T]
,
we see that
\hbox{dom}[T\circ(F^{j}\omega)]=\hbox{dom}[F^{j}\omega]
.
For all
\lambda\in{\mathcal{R}}_{E}^{0}
,

\displaystyle(\zeta\circ T)(J_{E}^{j}\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\zeta(T(J_{E}^{j}\lambda))\,\,=\,\,\zeta(\hbox{Tay}_{z}^{j}(\lambda))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\hbox{Tay}_{z}^{j}(\lambda))(z)\,\,=\,\,\lambda(z)\,\,=\,\,\pi_{E}^{j}(J_{E}^{j}\lambda).

Then
\zeta\circ T=\pi_{E}^{j}
.
Let
Q:=(\pi_{E}^{j})^{*}(\hbox{dom}[\omega])
.
Then
Q\subseteq\hbox{dom}[\pi_{E}^{j}]
.
Also,

\displaystyle\hbox{dom}[\omega_{*}\circ T]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{*}(\hbox{dom}[\omega_{*}])\,\,=\,\,T^{*}(\zeta^{*}(\hbox{dom}[\omega]))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\pi_{E}^{j})^{*}(\hbox{dom}[\omega])\,\,=\,\,Q.

Also,
\hbox{dom}[T\circ(F^{j}\omega)]=\hbox{dom}[F^{j}\omega]=(\pi_{E}^{j})^{*}(\hbox{dom}[\omega])=Q
.
Let
q\in Q
be given.
We wish to show that
\omega_{*}(T(q))=T((F^{j}\omega)(q))
.

Since
q\in Q\subseteq\hbox{dom}[\pi_{E}^{j}]=F^{j}E
,
choose
\mu\in{\mathcal{R}}_{E}^{0}
such that
q=J_{E}^{j}\mu
.
Let
P:=\hbox{Tay}_{z}^{j}(\mu)
.
Then
T(q)=T(J_{E}^{j}\mu)=\hbox{Tay}_{z}^{j}(\mu)=P
.
Then
\omega_{*}(T(q))=\omega_{*}(P)=\hbox{Tay}_{z}^{j}(\omega\circ P)
.
Also,
\pi_{E}^{j}(q)=\pi_{E}^{j}(J_{E}^{j}\mu)=\mu(z)
.
Then
\mu(z)=\pi_{E}^{j}(q)\in\pi_{E}^{j}(Q)=\pi_{E}^{j}((\pi_{E}^{j})^{*}(\hbox{dom}[\omega]))\subseteq\hbox{dom}[\omega]
.

We have
(F^{j}\omega)(q)=(F^{j}\omega)(J_{E}^{j}\mu)=J_{E}^{j}(\omega\circ\mu)
.
Moreover, we have
T(J_{E}^{j}(\omega\circ\mu))=\hbox{Tay}_{z}^{j}(\omega\circ\mu)
.
As
P=\hbox{Tay}_{z}^{j}(\mu)
,
we get
P\sim\mu\,\,[[j,z]]
.
Then
\omega\circ P\sim\omega\circ\mu\,\,[[j,z]]
,
so
\hbox{Tay}_{z}^{j}(\omega\circ P)=\hbox{Tay}_{z}^{j}(\omega\circ\mu)
.
Then

\displaystyle\omega_{*}(T(q))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbox{Tay}_{z}^{j}(\omega\circ P)\,\,=\,\,\hbox{Tay}_{z}^{j}(\omega\circ\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(J_{E}^{j}(\omega\circ\mu))\,\,=\,\,T((F^{j}\omega)(q)),

as desired.

End of proof of Claim 1.

By Claim 1,
\psi_{*}\circ T=T\circ(F^{j}\psi)
and
I_{*}\circ T=T\circ(F^{j}I)
.
Also, we have
T(\sigma)=T(J_{E}^{j}I)=\hbox{Tay}_{z}^{j}(I)=I
.
Then

{\bm{(}}\,\psi_{*}\,\sim\,I_{*}\,\,[[i,I]]\,{\bm{)}}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad{\bm{(}}\,F^{j}\psi\sim F^{j}I\,\,[[i,\sigma]]\,{\bm{)}}.

By Lemma
7.2
,

(

\chi\sim{\bf 0}_{E,E}\,\,[[i+j,z]]

)

\Leftrightarrow

(

\chi_{*}\sim{\bf 0}_{V,V}\,[[i,I]]

)
.
So, since
\chi=\psi-I
and since
\chi_{*}=\psi_{*}-I_{*}
, we get

{\bm{(}}\,\psi\sim I\,\,[[i+j,z]]\,{\bm{)}}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad{\bm{(}}\,\psi_{*}\sim I_{*}\,[[i,I]]\,{\bm{)}}.

Putting this together, we have shown:

\displaystyle{\bm{(}}\,\psi\sim I\,\,[[i+j,z]]\,{\bm{)}}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle{\bm{(}}\,\psi_{*}\sim I_{*}\,[[i,I]]\,{\bm{)}}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle{\bm{(}}\,F^{j}\psi\sim F^{j}I\,\,[[i,\sigma]]\,{\bm{)},}

as desired.
∎

The
i=0
special case of
\Leftarrow

of Lemma
8.1

is often useful, and it admits a simple proof,
so we present it separately:

LEMMA 8.2
.

Let
f,\phi\in D_{O}^{\infty}(M,N)
.
Let
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
Let
q\in F^{j}M
.
Assume that
q\in(\hbox{dom}[F^{j}f])\cap(\hbox{dom}[F^{j}\phi])

and that
(F^{j}f)(q)=(F^{j}\phi)(q)
.
Let
p:=\pi_{M}^{j}(q)
.
Then
f\sim\phi\,\,[[j,p]]
.

Proof.

Since
q\in F^{j}M
, choose
\lambda\in{\mathcal{R}}_{M}^{0}
such that
q=J_{M}^{j}\lambda
.
Then
(F^{j}f)(q)=J_{N}^{j}(f\circ\lambda)
and
(F^{j}\phi)(q)=J_{N}^{j}(\phi\circ\lambda)

and
\pi_{M}^{j}(q)=\lambda(0_{d})
.

We have
J_{N}^{j}(f\circ\lambda)=(F^{j}f)(q)=(F^{j}\phi)(q)=J_{N}^{j}(\phi\circ\lambda)
.
It follows that
f\circ\lambda\sim\phi\circ\lambda\,\,[[j,0_{d}]]
.
Since
p=\pi_{M}^{j}(q)=\lambda(0_{d})
, we get
\lambda^{-1}(p)=0_{d}
.
Then
f\circ\lambda\circ\lambda^{-1}\sim\phi\circ\lambda\circ\lambda^{-1}\,\,[[j,p]]
.
Then
f\sim\phi\,\,[[j,p]]
.
∎

9.
Iterated frame bundles contain frame bundles

Let
X
be a manifold.
Let
d:=\hbox{dim\,}X
,
E:={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
z:=0_{d}
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
m:=\hbox{dim\,}({\mathcal{P}}_{E,E}^{0,j})
.
Let
\kappa:{\mathcal{P}}_{E,E}^{0,j}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be a fixed vector space isomorphism.
Recall (from §
2
):
C_{\kappa}:F^{j}E\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is defined by

\forall\nu\in{\mathcal{R}}_{E}^{0},\qquad C_{\kappa}(J_{E}^{j}\nu)\,\,=\,\,\kappa(\hbox{Tay}_{z}^{j}(\nu)).

Then
C_{\kappa}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{F^{j}E}
.
Let
I:={\rm id}_{E}
.
Let
q_{0}:=J_{E}^{j}I\in F^{j}E
.
Then we have
\pi_{E}^{j}(q_{0})=\pi_{E}^{j}(J_{E}^{j}I)=I(z)=z
.
Let the function
B_{\kappa}:F^{j}E\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be defined by
B_{\kappa}(q)=[C_{\kappa}(q)]-[C_{\kappa}(q_{0})]
;
then
B_{\kappa}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{F^{j}E}
and
B_{\kappa}(q_{0})=0_{m}
.
Let
R_{\kappa}:=B_{\kappa}^{-1}
; then
R_{\kappa}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{F^{j}E}^{0}
and
R_{\kappa}(0_{m})=q_{0}
.
For all
\lambda\in{\mathcal{R}}_{X}^{0}
,

R_{\kappa}(0_{m})\,\,=\,\,q_{0}\,\,\in\,\,(\pi_{E}^{j})^{*}(z)\,\,\subseteq\,\,(\pi_{E}^{j})^{*}(\hbox{dom}[\lambda])\,\,=\,\,\hbox{dom}[F^{j}\lambda],

and we define
R_{\kappa}^{\lambda}:=(F^{j}\lambda)\circ R_{\kappa}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{F^{j}X}^{0}
.
Let
i\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.

LEMMA 9.1
.

For all
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathcal{R}}_{X}^{0}
, we have:

{\bm{(}}\,\,\lambda\sim\mu\,\,[[i+j,z]]\,\,{\bm{)}}\,\,\Leftrightarrow\,\,{\bm{(}}\,\,R_{\kappa}^{\lambda}\sim R_{\kappa}^{\mu}\,\,[[i,0_{m}]]\,\,{\bm{)}}.

Proof.

For all
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathcal{R}}_{X}^{0}
, because of the definitions above,
we have both
R_{\kappa}^{\lambda}=(F^{j}\lambda)\circ R_{\kappa}
and
R_{\kappa}^{\mu}=(F^{j}\mu)\circ R_{\kappa}
;
so, since
R_{\kappa}(0_{m})=q_{0}
,

{\bm{(}}\,F^{j}\lambda\sim F^{j}\mu\,\,[[i,q_{0}]]\,{\bm{)}}\,\,\Leftrightarrow\,\,{\bm{(}}\,\,R_{\kappa}^{\lambda}\sim R_{\kappa}^{\mu}\,\,[[i,0_{m}]]\,\,{\bm{)}}.

By Lemma
8.1
(with
M
replaced by
E
,
N
by
X
,
q
by
q_{0}
and
p
by
z
),
we conclude that: for all
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathcal{R}}_{X}^{0}
,

{\bm{(}}\,\,\lambda\sim\mu\,\,[[i+j,z]]\,\,{\bm{)}}\,\,\Leftrightarrow\,\,{\bm{(}}\,\,F^{j}\lambda\sim F^{j}\mu\,\,[[i,q_{0}]]\,\,{\bm{)}}.

Putting this together, for all
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathcal{R}}_{X}^{0}
,

\displaystyle{\bm{(}}\,\,\lambda\sim\mu\,\,[[i+j,z]]\,\,{\bm{)}}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle{\bm{(}}\,\,F^{j}\lambda\sim F^{j}\mu\,\,[[i,q_{0}]]\,\,{\bm{)}}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle{\bm{(}}\,\,R_{\kappa}^{\lambda}\sim R_{\kappa}^{\mu}\,\,[[i,0_{m}]]\,\,{\bm{)}},

as desired.
∎

LEMMA 9.2
.

There exists a unique function
\Phi:F^{i+j}X\to F^{i}(F^{j}X)

satisfying the condition:
\forall\lambda\in{\mathcal{R}}_{X}^{0}
,
\Phi(J_{X}^{i+j}\lambda)=J_{F^{j}X}^{i}R_{\kappa}^{\lambda}
.

Proof.

This follows from
\Rightarrow
of Lemma
9.1
.
∎

LEMMA 9.3
.

Let the function
\Phi:F^{i+j}X\to F^{i}(F^{j}X)
satisfy the condition:

\forall\lambda\in{\mathcal{R}}_{X}^{0}
,
\Phi(J_{X}^{i+j}\lambda)=J_{F^{j}X}^{i}R_{\kappa}^{\lambda}
.
Then
\Phi
is injective.

Proof.

This follows from
\Leftarrow
of Lemma
9.1
.
∎

10.
Naturality of inclusion in iterated frame bundles

Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Let
i,j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
Let
E:={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Let
m:=\hbox{dim\,}({\mathcal{P}}_{E,E}^{0,j})
,
and let
\kappa:{\mathcal{P}}_{E,E}^{0,j}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be a fixed vector space isomorphism.
Define
R_{\kappa}
as in §
9
.
For any
d
-dimensional manifold
X
,
for any
\lambda\in{\mathcal{R}}_{X}^{0}
,
define
R_{\kappa}^{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{R}}_{F^{j}X}^{0}
as in §
9
.
Following Lemma
9.2
,
for every
d
-dimensional manifold
X
,
let
\Phi_{X,\kappa}^{i,j}:F^{i+j}X\to F^{i}(F^{j}X)
be the unique function satisfying the condition:

\forall\lambda\in{\mathcal{R}}_{X}^{0}
,
\Phi_{X,\kappa}^{i,j}(J_{X}^{i+j}\lambda)=J_{F^{j}X}^{i}R_{\kappa}^{\lambda}
.

With
i
,
j
and
\kappa
fixed, we show that
\Phi_{X,\kappa}^{i,j}
is natural in
X
:

LEMMA 10.1
.

Let
M
and
N
both be
d
-dimensional manifolds,
and let
f\in D_{O}^{\infty}(M,N)
.
Then
(F^{i}(F^{j}f))\circ\Phi_{M,\kappa}^{i,j}=\Phi_{N,\kappa}^{i,j}\circ(F^{i+j}f)
.

Proof.

Let
M^{\prime}:=F^{j}M
,
N^{\prime}:=F^{j}N
,
f^{\prime}:=F^{j}f\in D_{O}^{\infty}(M^{\prime},N^{\prime})
.
Let
S:=\Phi_{M,\kappa}^{i,j}
,
T:=\Phi_{N,\kappa}^{i,j}
.
We wish to prove:
(F^{i}f^{\prime})\circ S=T\circ(F^{i+j}f)
.

Let
\Pi:=\pi_{M}^{j}\circ\pi_{M^{\prime}}^{i}:F^{i}M^{\prime}\to M
and
\pi:=\pi_{M}^{i+j}:F^{i+j}M\to M
.
Let
I:={\rm id}_{E}:E\to E
and
q_{0}:=J_{E}^{j}I\in F^{j}E
.
For all
\lambda\in{\mathcal{R}}_{M}^{0}
,

(F^{j}\lambda)(q_{0})=(F^{j}\lambda)(J_{E}^{j}I)=J_{M}^{j}(\lambda\circ I)=J_{M}^{j}\lambda
.
Then, for all
\lambda\in{\mathcal{R}}_{M}^{0}
,

\displaystyle(\Pi\circ S)(J_{M}^{i+j}\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\pi_{M}^{j}\circ\pi_{M^{\prime}}^{i}\circ\Phi_{M,\kappa}^{i,j})(J_{M}^{i+j}\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\pi_{M}^{j}\circ\pi_{M^{\prime}}^{i})(J_{M^{\prime}}^{i}R_{\kappa}^{\lambda})\,\,=\,\,\pi_{M}^{j}(R_{\kappa}^{\lambda}(0_{m}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi_{M}^{j}({\bm{(}}(F^{j}\lambda)\circ R_{\kappa}{\bm{)}}{\bm{(}}0_{m}{\bm{)}})\,\,=\,\,\pi_{M}^{j}((F^{j}\lambda)(q_{0}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi_{M}^{j}(J_{M}^{j}\lambda)\,\,=\,\,\lambda(0_{d})\,\,=\,\,\pi(J_{M}^{i+j}\lambda).

Then
\Pi\circ S=\pi
.
Also,
\hbox{dom}[f^{\prime}]=\hbox{dom}[F^{j}f]=(\pi_{M}^{j})^{*}(\hbox{dom}[f])
.
Then

\displaystyle\Pi^{*}(\hbox{dom}[f])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\pi_{M^{\prime}}^{i})^{*}((\pi_{M}^{j})^{*}(\hbox{dom}[f]))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\pi_{M^{\prime}}^{i})^{*}(\hbox{dom}[f^{\prime}])\,\,=\,\,\hbox{dom}[F^{i}f^{\prime}].

Because we have
\hbox{im}[F^{i+j}f]\subseteq F^{i+j}N=\hbox{dom}[T]
,
it follows that
\hbox{dom}[T\circ(F^{i+j}f)]=\hbox{dom}[F^{i+j}f]
.
Let
Q:=\pi^{*}(\hbox{dom}[f])
.
Then

\displaystyle\hbox{dom}[(F^{i}f^{\prime})\circ S]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{*}(\hbox{dom}[F^{i}f^{\prime}])\,\,=\,\,S^{*}(\Pi^{*}(\hbox{dom}[f]))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi^{*}(\hbox{dom}[f])\,\,=\,\,Q.

Also,
\hbox{dom}[T\circ(F^{i+j}f)]=\hbox{dom}[F^{i+j}f]=\pi^{*}(\hbox{dom}[f])=Q
.
Let
q\in Q
be given.
We wish to prove that

(F^{i}f^{\prime})(S(q))=T((F^{i+j}f)(q))
.

We have
q\in Q=\pi^{*}(\hbox{dom}[f])\subseteq\hbox{dom}[\pi]=F^{i+j}M
,
so choose
\mu\in{\mathcal{R}}_{M}^{0}
such that
q=J_{M}^{i+j}\mu
.
Then
S(q)=\Phi_{M,\kappa}^{i,j}(J_{M}^{i+j}\mu)=J_{M^{\prime}}^{i}R_{\kappa}^{\mu}
.
Also,
\mu(0_{d})=\pi(J_{M}^{i+j}\mu)=\pi(q)\in\pi(Q)=\pi(\pi^{*}(\hbox{dom}[f]))\subseteq\hbox{dom}[f]
.

Let
\phi:=f\circ\mu\in{\mathcal{R}}_{N}^{0}
.
By functoriality of
F^{j}
,
F^{j}\phi=(F^{j}f)\circ(F^{j}\mu)
.
We have
R_{\kappa}^{\mu}=(F^{j}\mu)\circ R_{\kappa}

and
R_{\kappa}^{\phi}=(F^{j}\phi)\circ R_{\kappa}
.
Then

f^{\prime}\,\circ\,R_{\kappa}^{\mu}\,\,=\,\,(F^{j}f)\,\circ\,(F^{j}\mu)\,\circ\,R_{\kappa}\,\,=\,\,(F^{j}\phi)\,\circ\,R_{\kappa}\,\,=\,\,R_{\kappa}^{\phi}.

Then

S_{0}:=\{p\in M_{0}\,|\,\hbox{dim\,}{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{p}<k\}
.
Then
S_{0}
contains a dense open subset of
M_{0}
.
Then
M_{0}\subseteq\overline{S_{0}}
.
Since
S_{0}=M_{0}\cap S\subseteq S
, we get
\overline{S_{0}}\subseteq\overline{S}
.
Then
M_{1}\backslash\overline{U}=M_{0}\subseteq\overline{S_{0}}\subseteq\overline{S}
.
Then
M_{1}\subseteq\overline{U}\cup(M_{1}\backslash\overline{U})\subseteq\overline{S}\cup\overline{S}=\overline{S}
,
and so
\overline{M_{1}}\subseteq\overline{S}
.
Then
M=\overline{M_{1}}\subseteq\overline{S}
,
so

is dense in

, as desired.
∎

Local freeness

Here is a frame bundle analogue of Theorem 6.14 of
[
Olver
]
:

THEOREM 12.1
.

Let a Lie group
G
act on a manifold
M
.
Assume that the action is
C^{\infty}
.
Let
G^{\circ}
denote the identity component of
G
.
Assume that the
G^{\circ}
-action on
M
is fixpoint rare.
Let
n:=\hbox{dim\,}G
.
Assume that
n\geq 1
.
Then there exists a
G
-invariant dense open subset
Q
of
F^{n-1}M

such that the
G
-action on
Q
is locally free.

We will argue below that, at each level in the frame bundle tower,
if the generic stabilizer dimension is positive,
then, at the next level up, it will decrease by at least one.
By fixpoint rarity, it starts out less than
n
, so,
after
n-1
transitions, it must be zero.
Details follow.

Proof.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}:=T_{1_{G}}G
.
For all
p\in M
, let
G_{p}:=\hbox{Stab}_{G}(p)
,
{\mathfrak{g}}_{p}:=T_{1_{G}}G_{p}
.

Since
\hbox{dim\,}G^{\circ}=\hbox{dim\,}G=n\geq 1
, we have
G^{\circ}\neq\{1_{G}\}
.
Choose
g_{0}\in G^{\circ}\backslash\{1_{G}\}
.
Let
F_{0}:=\hbox{Fix}_{X}(g_{0})
.
By continuity of
p\mapsto g_{0}p:M\to M
,

F_{0}
is closed in
M
.
The
G^{\circ}
-action on
M
is fixpoint rare,
so
F_{0}
has empty interior in
M
.
Let
U_{0}:=M\backslash F_{0}
.
Then
U_{0}
is a dense open subset of
M
.

Claim 1:
Let
p\in U_{0}
. Then
\hbox{dim\,}{\mathfrak{g}}_{p}<n
.

Proof of Claim 1:

Since
{\mathfrak{g}}_{p}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}
and since
n=\hbox{dim\,}G=\hbox{dim\,}{\mathfrak{g}}
,
it suffices to show that
{\mathfrak{g}}_{p}\neq{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Assume that
{\mathfrak{g}}_{p}={\mathfrak{g}}
.
We aim for a contradiction.

Since
{\mathfrak{g}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}_{p}
, we get
G^{\circ}\subseteq G_{p}
.
Then
g_{0}\in G^{\circ}\subseteq G_{p}
, so
g_{0}p=p
.
Then
p\in\hbox{Fix}_{M}(g_{0})=F_{0}
.
Also,
p\in U_{0}=M\backslash F_{0}
, so
p\notin F_{0}
.
Then both
p\in F_{0}
and
p\notin F_{0}
.
Contradiction.

End of proof of Claim 1.

For all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, let
M_{j}:=F^{j}M
and
\pi_{j}:=\pi_{M}^{j}
.
The
G
-action on
M
induces a
G
-action on
M_{j}
and this,
in turn, induces a
G
-action on
TM_{j}
.
For all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, for all
q\in M_{j}
,
let
G_{q}:=\hbox{Stab}_{G}(q)
, let
G^{\prime}_{q}:=\hbox{Stab}^{\prime}_{G}(q)
,
let
{\mathfrak{g}}_{q}:=T_{1_{G}}G_{q}
,
and let
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{q}:=T_{1_{G}}G^{\prime}_{q}
.
For all
g\in G
, let
\overline{g}:M\to M
be defined by
\overline{g}(p)=gp
.
Let
I:={\rm id}_{M}:M\to M
.
For all
p\in M
, for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
let
G_{p}^{(j)}:=\{g\in G\,|\,\overline{g}\sim I\,\,[[j,p]]\}
,
and let
{\mathfrak{g}}_{p}^{(j)}:=T_{1_{G}}G_{p}^{(j)}
.

Claim 2:
Let
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
Let
q\in M_{j}
and let
p:=\pi_{j}(q)
.
Then
{\mathfrak{g}}_{q}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}_{p}^{(j)}
.

Proof of Claim 2:

It suffices to show that
G_{q}\subseteq G_{p}^{(j)}
.
Let
g\in G_{q}
be given.
We wish to prove that
g\in G_{p}^{(j)}
.

Since
g\in G_{q}
,
gq=q
.
Then
(F^{j}\overline{g})(q)=gq=q=(F^{j}I)(q)
.
So, by Lemma
8.2
,

\overline{g}\sim I\,\,[[j,p]]
.
So
g\in G_{p}^{(j)}
.

End of proof of Claim 2.

Claim 3:
Let
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
Let
q\in M_{j}
and let
p:=\pi_{j}(q)
.
Then
{\mathfrak{g}}_{p}^{(j+1)}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{q}
.

Proof of Claim 3:

It suffices to show that
G_{p}^{(j+1)}\subseteq G^{\prime}_{q}
.
Let
g\in G_{p}^{(j+1)}
be given.
We wish to prove that
g\in G^{\prime}_{q}
.

Since
g\in G_{p}^{(j+1)}
, we get
\overline{g}\sim I\,\,[[j+1,p]]
.
So, by
\Rightarrow
of Lemma
8.1
(with
i
replaced by
1
),

F^{j}\overline{g}\sim F^{j}I\,\,[[1,q]]
.
Then
(d(F^{j}\overline{g}))_{q}=(d(F^{j}I))_{q}
.
Then, for all
v\in T_{q}M_{j}
, we have
gv=(d(F^{j}\overline{g}))_{q}(v)=(d(F^{j}I))_{q}(v)=v
.
It follows that
g\in\hbox{Stab}^{\prime}_{G}(q)=G^{\prime}_{q}
, as desired.

End of proof of Claim 3.

Let
m:=n-1
.
Let
Q:=\{q\in M_{m}\,|\,{\mathfrak{g}}_{q}=\{0_{\mathfrak{g}}\}\}
.
Then we have
Q\subseteq M_{m}=M_{n-1}=F^{n-1}M
.
Also,
Q
is
G
-invariant, and, moreover, the
G
-action on
Q
is locally free.
Also,
Q=\{q\in M_{m}\,|\,\hbox{dim\,}{\mathfrak{g}}_{q}<1\}
,
so, by upper semi-continuity of the mapping
q\mapsto\hbox{dim\,}{\mathfrak{g}}_{q}:M_{m}\to{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
we see that
Q
is an open subset of
M_{m}
.
It remains to show that
Q
is dense in
M_{m}
.
It suffices to show that
Q
contains a dense open subset of
M_{m}
.
That is, it suffices to prove:
\forall^{\circ}q\in M_{m}
,
{\mathfrak{g}}_{q}=\{0_{\mathfrak{g}}\}
.

The preimage, under
\pi_{m}:M_{m}\to M
, of a dense open set is dense open.
It therefore suffices, by Claim 2, to show:
\forall^{\circ}p\in M
,
{\mathfrak{g}}_{p}^{(m)}=\{0_{\mathfrak{g}}\}
.

For all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
let
K_{j}:=\{k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\,|\,\forall^{\circ}p\in M,\,\hbox{dim\,}{\mathfrak{g}}_{p}^{(j)}\leq k\}
.
Then
K_{0}\subseteq K_{1}\subseteq K_{2}\subseteq\cdots
.
We wish to show that
0\in K_{m}
.
We will, in fact, show, for all integers
j\in[0,m]
, that
m-j\in K_{j}
.

For all
p\in M
, we have
{\mathfrak{g}}_{p}^{(0)}={\mathfrak{g}}_{p}
.
So, by Claim 1, for all
p\in U_{0}
, we have
\hbox{dim\,}{\mathfrak{g}}_{p}^{(0)}<n
,
so
\hbox{dim\,}{\mathfrak{g}}_{p}^{(0)}\leq n-1=m
.
Then, because
U_{0}
is a dense open subset of
M
, we get
m\in K_{0}
.
Therefore, by induction, it suffices to show, for every integer
j\in[0,m-1]
, that

{\bm{(}}\,m-j\in K_{j}\,{\bm{)}}\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad{\bm{(}}\,m-j-1\in K_{j+1}\,{\bm{)}}.

Let an integer
j\in[0,m-1]
be given, let
k:=m-j
,
and assume that
k\in K_{j}
.
We wish to show that
k-1\in K_{j+1}
.

We have
j\leq m-1
.
Then
k=m-j\geq 1
.
Then
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Since
k\in K_{j}
, we get:
\forall^{\circ}p\in M
,
\hbox{dim\,}{\mathfrak{g}}_{p}^{(j)}\leq k
.
The preimage, under
\pi_{j}:M_{j}\to M
, of a dense open set is dense open.
Therefore, by Claim 2,
\forall^{\circ}q\in M_{j}
,
\hbox{dim\,}{\mathfrak{g}}_{q}\leq k
.
Since
\pi_{j}:M_{j}\to M
is open and
G
-equivariant,
and since the
G^{\circ}
-action on
M
is fixpoint rare,
we see that the
G^{\circ}
-action on
M_{j}
is fixpoint rare as well.
Then, by Lemma
11.2
(with
M
replaced by
M_{j}
),
we have:
\forall^{\circ}q\in M_{j}
,
\hbox{dim\,}{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}_{q}\leq k-1
.
The image, under
\pi_{j}:M_{j}\to M
, of a dense open set is dense open.
Therefore, by Claim 3,
\forall^{\circ}p\in M
,
\hbox{dim\,}{\mathfrak{g}}_{p}^{(j+1)}\leq k-1
.
Then
k-1\in K_{j+1}
, as desired.
∎

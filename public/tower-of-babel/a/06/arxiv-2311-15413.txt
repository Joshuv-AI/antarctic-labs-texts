The Cauchy problem for a helical vortex filament in 3D Navier Stokes
Francisco Gancedo, Antonio Hidalgo-Torné

Abstract
This paper studies the Cauchy problem for a helical vortex filament evolving by the 3D incompressible Navier-Stokes equations. We prove global-in-time well-posedness and smoothing of solutions with initial vorticity concentrated on a helix. We provide a local-in-time well-posedness result for vortex filaments periodic in one spatial direction, and show that solutions with helical initial data preserve this symmetry. We follow the approach of [ 4 ] , where the analogue local-in-time result has been obtained for closed vortex filaments in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} . Next, we apply local energy weak solutions theory with a novel estimate for helical functions in non-helical domains to uniquely extend the solutions globally in time. This is the first global-in-time well-posedness result for a vortex filament without size restriction and without vanishing swirl assumptions.

Introduction

This paper is devoted to the study of helical vortex filaments in Navier-Stokes equations.
To this end, we consider the Navier-Stokes equations with one periodic coordinate

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u(t,x)+u(t,x)\cdot\nabla u(t,x)-\nu\Delta u(t,x)+\nabla p(t,x)=0,\quad t\in(0,T),x\in{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}},\\
&\nabla\cdot u(t,x)=0,\\
&u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
u_{0}
is the prescribed initial data and
\nu>0
is the viscosity. We fix
\nu=1
for the sake of simplicity. Applying the curl operator in the previous system, we obtain the Navier-Stokes equations in vorticity form. Denoting
\omega=\nabla\wedge u
, we get

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\omega(t,x)+u(t,x)\cdot\nabla\omega(t,x)-\omega(t,x)\cdot\nabla u(t,x)-\Delta\omega(t,x)=0,\quad t\in(0,T),x\in{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}},\\
&u(t,x)=\nabla\wedge\Delta^{-1}\omega(t,x),\\
&\omega(0,x)=\omega_{0}(x)=\nabla\wedge u_{0}(x).\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

In this paper, we consider an initial vorticity
\omega_{0}
supported on a curve
\Gamma
, which is called a vortex filament. We denote it as

\omega_{0}=\alpha\delta_{\Gamma},

(1.3)

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
is any constant and the measure
\delta_{\Gamma}
satisfies

\langle\delta_{\Gamma},\varphi\rangle=\int_{\Gamma}\varphi\cdot d\vec{s},\quad\forall\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}}).

Denoting
\mathbf{d}(x)
the distance from
x
to
\Gamma
, this particular vorticity configuration implies that the velocity field grows as
|\mathbf{d}(x)|^{-2}
. Therefore, the kinetic energy is infinite near the filament. In the literature, the term vortex filament is often relaxed and refers to vorticities approximately concentrated along a curve.

The study of vortex filaments in Euler equations, which are (
1.1
) with
\nu=0

[
28
]
and Kelvin
[
40

[
11
]
it was shown that an inviscid vortex filament should behave as the binormal flow to leading order. The binormal flow, also known as the self-induction approximation or the vortex-filament equation, has proven to be a very interesting equation by itself. See
[
30

1
,
2
]
and the references therein. In
[

, results are given in favor of the vortex filament conjecture. This conjecture states that it is always possible to find solutions of Euler equations whose vorticity remains close to a curve solving the binormal flow. For now, this conjecture is only known to be true in the case of a straight line, a circular line
[

]

Regarding the Navier-Stokes equations in

with initial data including (

&\omega_{0}(x)=\nabla\wedge u_{0}(x).\end{aligned}\right.

In this work, we prove the first global-in-time well-posedness result for a vortex filament without vanishing swirl assumptions. We achieve this for

)
with helical

First, we prove local-in-time well-posedness for any

-periodic, non-self-intersecting. The Biot-Savart and other kernels in

Remark 1.4

The initial data can be taken more general. As in

4

We adapt the strategy in

We also need control of the linearized equation around the Lamb-Oseen vortex, which controls the evolution for short times near the filament. The distinction of near and far from the filament is made rigorous by splitting the unknown vorticity in two, and treating each part appropriately.

The most difficult part in the adaptation of the proof is that the kernels of the heat and Biot-Savart operator are no longer explicit in
{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}}
and also have different decay at infinity, being similar to the decay in two dimensions. This changes the integrability of the velocity while keeping the same integrability in the vorticity. For example, in
[
4

the velocity field belongs to

for any positive time, but in our case it does not for any time. Note that in
{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}}
the Biot-Savart law decays as

2.1

The purpose of this subsection is to prepare the ingredients needed to construct the approximate solution.

\Gamma_{r}\coloneqq\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}:dist(y,\Gamma)<32r\},

\Sigma_{r}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}:|\widetilde{x}|<32r\}.

Then define
\Phi:\Sigma_{r}\to\Gamma_{r}
as

\Phi(x)\coloneqq\gamma(x_{3})+x_{1}\mathfrak{n}(x_{3})+x_{2}\mathfrak{b}(x_{3}),

which will be bijective if
r
is small enough. From now on, we will assume without saying that
r
is small enough so that
\Phi
is bijective. Taking derivative respect to
x_{3}
in the previous equation, we obtain

\partial_{z}\Phi(x,z)=c_{s}\mathfrak{t}+x_{1}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\cdot\mathfrak{t})\mathfrak{t}+x_{1}(\mathfrak{n}^{\prime}\cdot\mathfrak{b})\mathfrak{b}+x_{2}(\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\cdot\mathfrak{t})\mathfrak{t}+x_{2}(\mathfrak{b}^{\prime}\cdot\mathfrak{n})\mathfrak{n}\eqqcolon\mathfrak{D}\mathfrak{t}+\mathfrak{E}\mathfrak{n}+\mathfrak{F}\mathfrak{b},

so the jacobian of
\Phi
is

J=\nabla\Phi=\left[\mathfrak{n}\quad\mathfrak{b}\quad\mathfrak{D}\mathfrak{t}+\mathfrak{E}\mathfrak{n}+\mathfrak{F}\mathfrak{b}\right].

The determinant of
J
, due to orthogonality, is
\mathfrak{D}\approx c_{s}
in the domain.
Now, we define the maps that will be used to transform velocity fields (
P_{\Phi}
) and vorticity fields (
Q_{\Phi}
) from the straightened frame to the curved frame:

\displaystyle P_{\Phi}v(y)

\displaystyle=Jv(\Phi^{-1}(y)),

(2.1)

\displaystyle Q_{\Phi}\eta(y)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{D}^{-1}J\eta(\Phi^{-1}(y)).

The reason for using different mappings is that they have good properties together, as we can see in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
v,\eta:\Sigma_{r}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
be smooth vector fields defined in the straightened frame. We have the identities:

(a)

Divergence operator.

\nabla\cdot(Q_{\Phi}\eta)=(\mathfrak{D}^{-1}\nabla\cdot\eta)\circ\Phi^{-1}.

(b)

Curl operator.

\nabla\times(Q_{\Phi}\eta)=Q_{\Phi}\curl_{\Phi}\eta,

where the modified curl operator is defined as

\curl_{\Phi}\eta\coloneqq\nabla\times\eta+E^{j}\partial_{j}\eta+F\eta,

where the matrix
F
is smooth and bounded, and the matrices
E^{j}
satisfies bounds that we will state in the next lemma.

(c)

Bilinear operator.

B[P_{\Phi}v,Q_{\Phi}\eta]=Q_{\Phi}B[v,\eta].

(d)

Laplacian.

\Delta Q_{\Phi}\eta=Q_{\Phi}\Delta_{\Phi}\eta,

where the modified Laplacian is defined as

\Delta_{\Phi}\coloneqq\Delta+A^{i,j}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}+B^{j}\partial_{j}+C,

where the matrices
B^{j},C
are smooth and bounded and the matrix
A
satisfies bounds that we will state in the next lemma.

We detail now the estimates of different elements from this subsection that will be used later.

Lemma 2.2
.

Provided
r
is small enough and
x\in\Gamma_{r}
, for any
j\geq 0
,
i\in\{1,2,3\}
and multi-index
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
,

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{D}|\gtrsim 1,

\displaystyle|\partial^{j}_{x_{3}}(\mathfrak{D}-c_{s})|+|\partial_{x_{3}}^{j}E^{i}|+|\partial_{x_{3}}^{j}A|\lesssim|\widetilde{x}|,

\displaystyle|\nabla^{\alpha}_{x}\Phi|+|\nabla^{\alpha}_{x}X|+|\nabla^{\alpha}_{x}\mathfrak{D}|\lesssim|\widetilde{x}|,

where
X
is any of
J,A,B^{i},C,E^{i},F
.

Notice that due to the previous bounds the operators
\curl
and
\curl_{\Phi}
are equal at higher order, except for terms that are small on
\Gamma_{r}
. The same occurs for
\Delta
and
\Delta_{\Phi}
. The last result of this subsection is consequence of the previous lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Provided
r
is small enough, for any
x,x^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{r}
we have

|\Phi(x)-\Phi(x^{\prime})|\approx|\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{x}^{\prime}|+|x_{3}-x_{3}^{\prime}|.

We will simultaneously work on the straightened frame and in the curved frame. The previous lemma together with lemma
2.2
will help us to control the change of variables.

Since the change of variables is only available close to the curve
\Gamma
, we need a bump function to control it rigorously. In the straightened frame we use the bump function
\chi_{r}
defined in subsection
1.1
. The corresponding bump functions on the curved frame
\widetilde{\chi}_{r}
are defined as
\widetilde{\chi}_{r}\circ\Phi\coloneqq\chi_{r}
. Observe that with this definition, we have

\widetilde{\chi}_{r}P_{\Phi}(\cdot)=P_{\Phi}(\chi_{r}\cdot),\quad\widetilde{\chi}_{r}Q_{\Phi}(\cdot)=Q_{\Phi}(\chi_{r}\cdot).

2.2
Approximate solution

First of all, since we are going to work with vorticities and velocities defined on the straightened and curved frame, we fix more notation. We will denote with
\eta
vorticities defined on the straightened frame, and
\omega
vorticities on the curved frame. For velocities, we will use
v
for the ones defined on the straightened frame, and
u
for the ones on the curved frame.

To make the fixed point argument work, we need to construct a good initial approximation so the remaining terms are small. As we remarked in subsection
1.2
, a suitable mapping of the Lamb-Oseen vortex might be a good candidate. We denote the Lamb-Oseen vortex and its correspondent velocity as

\displaystyle\eta^{g}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\left[0\kern 5.0pt0\kern 5.0pt\frac{\alpha}{4\pi t}e^{-\frac{|\widetilde{x}|^{2}}{4t}}\right]^{t},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\overline{v}^{g}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{\alpha\widetilde{x}^{\bot}}{2\pi|\widetilde{x}|^{2}}\!\left(1-e^{-\frac{|\widetilde{x}|^{2}}{4t}}\right)\kern 5.0pt0\right]^{t},

where
(x_{1},x_{2})^{\bot}\coloneqq(-x_{2},x_{1})
. The corresponding magnitudes on the curved frame are denoted as

\omega^{g}=Q_{\Phi}(\chi_{2r}\eta^{g}),\quad\overline{u}^{g}=P_{\Phi}(\chi_{4r}\overline{v}^{g}).

(2.3)

We must check which equation satisfies
\omega^{g}
to know which equation must satisfy the remaining terms. Note that
\eta^{g}
solves the heat equation since the bilinear term vanishes due to symmetry. Mollification commutes with derivatives, and again by symmetry the bilinear term vanishes for
\eta^{g}
, so we can write

\partial_{t}\eta^{g}+B[\chi_{r}\overline{v}^{g},\eta^{g}]=\Delta\eta^{g}

and now apply
Q_{\Phi}(\chi_{2r}\cdot)
and Lemma
2.1
to obtain

\partial_{t}\omega^{g}+B[\widetilde{\chi}_{r}\overline{u}^{g},\omega^{g}]=\Delta\omega^{g}+\underbrace{Q_{\Phi}(\chi_{2r}\Delta\eta^{g})-\Delta\omega^{g}}_{\mathcal{E}^{g}}.

(2.4)

2.3
Unknowns and fixed point scheme

By construction, the vorticity
\omega^{g}
attains as initial data
\delta_{\Gamma}
, so we can expect the remaining vorticity
\omega^{c}
to be small for short times provided that equation (
2.4
) is a good approximation to NS. It is convenient to split the remaining vorticity into two terms:
\omega^{c_{1}}
to track the evolution close to the curve, and
\omega^{c_{2}}
to do the rest. We will keep the same superindexes for the associated vorticity in the straightened frame and velocities. We leave without superindex the total vorticity
\omega=\omega^{g}+\omega^{c}=\omega^{g}+\omega^{c_{1}}+\omega^{c_{2}}
. Regarding velocities,
u
will always be related to the corresponding
\omega
by the Biot-Savart law

u^{*}=\nabla\times(-\Delta)^{-1}\omega^{*},

and except for
\overline{v}^{g}
and
\overline{u}^{g}
,
v
and
u
will always be related by

v^{*}\coloneqq P_{\Phi}^{-1}(\widetilde{\chi}_{r}u^{*}).

(2.5)

With respect to vorticities, we choose the following relations

\omega^{g}\coloneqq Q_{\Phi}(\chi_{2r}\eta^{g}),\quad\omega^{c_{1}}\coloneqq Q_{\Phi}(\chi_{2r}\eta^{c_{1}}),\quad\eta^{c_{2}}\coloneqq Q_{\Phi}^{-1}(\widetilde{\chi}_{2r}\omega^{c_{2}}).

(2.6)

We define
\eta^{c_{1}}
through the equation

\partial_{t}\eta^{c_{1}}+B[v,\eta^{c}]+B[v-\chi_{r}\overline{v}^{g},\eta^{g}]=\Delta\eta^{c_{1}},

(2.7)

with null initial data. This implies that (applying
Q_{\Phi}(\chi_{2r}\cdot)
)

\partial_{t}\omega^{c_{1}}+B[\widetilde{\chi}_{r}u,\omega^{c}]+B[\widetilde{\chi}_{r}(u-\overline{u}^{g}),\omega^{g}]=\Delta\omega^{c_{1}}+\underbrace{Q_{\Phi}(\chi_{2r}\Delta\eta^{c_{1}})-\Delta\omega^{c_{1}}}_{\mathcal{E}^{c}}.

(2.8)

The remaining
\omega^{c_{2}}
must close Navier-Stokes on the curved frame, which is the physical one, so

\partial_{t}\omega^{c_{2}}+B[(1-\widetilde{\chi}_{r})u,\omega]=\Delta\omega^{c_{2}}-\mathcal{E}^{g}-\mathcal{E}^{c}.

(2.9)

To set the fixed point scheme, the next step is to define the norm for each variable.

4
Preservation of helical symmetry

In this section we check the symmetry of the solution obtained in Theorem (
3.12
), provided the initial data have helical symmetry, which is defined in Definition
1.5
. Due to rotation invariance of the Navier-Stokes equations, any single filament with helical symmetry can be reduced to
\gamma(x_{3})=(\rho\cos(x_{3}),\rho\sin(x_{3}),\kappa x_{3}).
Once fixed, the parameters
\rho,\kappa
do not play any role. To simplify the exposition, we will assume
\rho=\kappa=1
, so that the initial vorticity is supported on the curve

\gamma(x_{3})=(\cos(x_{3}),\sin(x_{3}),x_{3}).

Navier-Stokes equations preserve helical symmetry
[
38
]
, but we cannot apply this property directly because the initial data is not smooth. We could try to smooth the initial data and show convergence, but this approach does not work. More precisely, if we use time-independent mollifiers and denote the smoothed initial data in the straightened variables by
\eta_{0}^{\varepsilon}
, one can check that
\left|\left|\eta_{0}-\eta_{0}^{\varepsilon}\right|\right|_{N^{0}}\nrightarrow 0
. We now check the conservation of symmetry in our construction.

\omega^{g}

Following subsection

, we obtain that

\mathfrak{t}(x_{3})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(-\sin(x_{3}),\cos(x_{3}),1),\quad\mathfrak{n}(x_{3})=(-\cos(x_{3}),-\sin(x_{3}),0),\quad\mathfrak{b}(x_{3})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\sin(x_{3}),-\cos(x_{3}),1),

\Phi(x)=(\cos(x_{3}),\sin(x_{3}),x_{3})+x_{1}(-\cos(x_{3}),-\sin(x_{3}),0)+\frac{x_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}(\sin(x_{3}),-\cos(x_{3}),1),

(4.2)

and determinant

. We prove that

is helical in three steps.

Lemma 4.1

5

By the previous section, we know that the solution to the Navier-Stokes equations obtained in theorem

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
u\in\mathring{E}_{2}
helical and for any
\varepsilon>0

Following
[

and using cylinders instead of balls, we obtain a function

supported in

\displaystyle\divergence v^{R}

\displaystyle=u\cdot\nabla\chi_{R},

(5.1)

\displaystyle\left|\left|v^{R}\right|\right|_{L^{2}_{uloc}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\left|\left|u\right|\right|_{L^{2}_{uloc}}}{R}.

In the construction, it is not clear whether
v^{R}
is helical or not. However, using that
\divergence v^{R}
is helical, we can construct a helical version of

. Take cylindrical coordinates
(r,\theta,z)
and make the change

\bar{v}^{R}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}v^{R}(r,\xi,z+s)ds.

By construction,
\bar{v}^{R}
is helical and also satisfies estimates (

).
We conclude that

is divergence free and

\left|\left|u-u^{R}\right|\right|_{L^{2}_{uloc}}\leq\left|\left|u-u\chi_{R}\right|\right|_{L^{2}_{uloc}}+\left|\left|\bar{v}^{R}\right|\right|_{L^{2}_{uloc}}<\varepsilon

if

. We mollify

to get

.
∎

Theorem 5.2

So far, we have shown that the helical symmetry is preserved but we have not exploited it. We do that in the following lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
u
be a smooth helical vector field,
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}}

Then

\left|\left|u\right|\right|_{L^{4}(C(x,1))}^{4}\lesssim\left|\left|u\right|\right|_{L^{2}(C(x,2))}^{2}\left|\left|\nabla u\right|\right|_{L^{2}(C(x,2))}^{2}+\left|\left|u\right|\right|_{L^{2}(C(x,2))}^{4}.

Proof.

The idea is to use the symmetry to reduce the estimate to a two dimensional estimate, then recover the three dimensional domain. We use
(\rho,\theta,z)
as cylindrical coordinates,

\xi=\theta+z

Observe that if

\displaystyle\left|\left|u\right|\right|_{L^{4}(C(x,1))}^{4}

\displaystyle=\int_{\{(\rho,\theta,z)\in C(x,1)\}}|u(\rho,\theta,z)|^{4}\rho d\rho d\theta dz\lesssim\int_{\{(\rho,\xi,z)\in C(x,1)\}}|\underline{u}(\rho,\xi)|^{4}\rho d\rho d\xi dz

\displaystyle\left|\left|u\right|\right|_{L^{4}(C(x,1))}^{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim\dfrac{1}{1+\rho_{x}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{\{|\rho-\rho_{x}|<1\}}|\underline{u}(\rho,\xi)|^{2}\rho d\rho d\xi\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{\{|\rho-\rho_{x}|<1\}}(|\partial_{\rho}\underline{u}(\rho,\xi)|^{2}+\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}|\partial_{\xi}\underline{u}(\rho,\xi)|^{2})\rho d\rho d\xi

\displaystyle+\left(\int_{\{(\rho,\xi,z)\in C(x,2)\}}|\underline{u}(\rho,\xi)|^{2}\rho d\rho d\xi dz\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\int_{\{(\rho,\theta,z)\in C(x,2)\}}|u(\rho,\theta,z)|^{2}\rho d\rho d\theta dz\right)^{2}=\left|\left|u\right|\right|_{L^{2}(C(x,2))}^{2}\left|\left|\nabla u\right|\right|_{L^{2}(C(x,2))}^{2}+\left|\left|u\right|\right|_{L^{2}(C(x,2))}^{4}.

In the second step, we used that fixed
\rho,\xi
such that

(Global-in-time well-posedness for a helical vortex filament)

and any
\Gamma
helical curve, there exists a unique global-in-time smooth helical solution to the Navier-Stokes equations with initial vorticity given by (

\left|\left|Q_{\Phi}^{-1}\left(\widetilde{\chi}_{8r}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}Q_{\Phi}\nabla_{x,z}^{\gamma}f(s)ds\right)\right|\right|_{N^{0}}\lesssim t^{1-\frac{|\gamma|}{2}}\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{N^{\beta}}.

Proof.

See

4
, Lemma 7.12]
. In this case
f_{2}
can be nonzero far from

Lemma A.11
.

If
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
and

\left|\left|fg\right|\right|_{M^{p}_{q}}\leq\left|\left|f\right|\right|_{M^{p_{1}}_{q_{1}}}\left|\left|g\right|\right|_{M^{p_{2}}_{q_{2}}}.

Proof.

Use the usual Hölder inequality.
∎

Lemma A.12
.

\displaystyle L^{p}

\displaystyle\subset M^{p}_{q}\quad\forall\,1\leq q\leq p,

\displaystyle B_{x_{3}}L^{q}_{x}

\displaystyle\subset M_{q}^{\frac{3q}{2}}.

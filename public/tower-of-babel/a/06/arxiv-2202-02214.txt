Infinite transitivity for automorphism groups of the affine plane Thanks: The first author was supported by the grant RSF 19-11-00172.
Alisa Chistopolskaya, Gregory Taroyan

Abstract
. This paper is dedicated to the problem of infinite transitivity for algebraically generated automorphism groups of the affine plane. We provide a necessary and sufficient condition of infinite transitivity for a large family of subgroups generated by one-parameter additive groups.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
14R20, 14R10

1.
Introduction

This paper is devoted to the study of the automorphism group
\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{2})
of the affine plane
\mathbb{A}^{2}
over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero.
One approach to the study of this group is to look for "elementary" subgroups in it. The first type of groups that comes to mind is that of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-groups. By definition
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-groups are subgroups of the group
{\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{2})}
that are isomorphic to the algebraic group of invertible elements in the ground field. Actions of these groups are very important and very well studied. The second type of nice subgroups one may think about is that of
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-groups. That is subgroups in
\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{2})
that are isomorphic to the additive group of the ground field. Although seemingly even more simple than
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-groups,
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-groups and their actions turn out to be far more complicated.
Below we describe two families
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-actions on the plane
\mathbb{A}^{2}
. These two families are the main subjects of study in this work.

For any
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, define the following one-parameter subgroups of
\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{2})
:

H_{a}\colon(x,y)\mapsto(x+\alpha y^{a},y)\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;K_{b}\colon(x,y)\mapsto(x,y+\beta x^{b}),\;\;\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{K}\,.

Let
\SAut(\mathbb{A}^{2})
be the subgroup of
\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{2})
generated by all one-parameter subgroup. Importance of families
H_{*},K_{*}
now stems from the fact that they generate the whole group
\SAut(\mathbb{A}^{2})
of the plane by Jung–Van der Kulk theorem.

The importance of transitive group actions in geometry is well understood. One may ask when an action of a group is not only transitive, but is "very" transitive. In more concrete terms an action of a group
G
on a set
S
is called
m
-transitive for some positive integer
m
if the diagonal action on the configuration space of
m
pairwise distinct ordered points in
S
is transitive. In these terms transitive actions become
1
-transitive actions. Now it is natural to define "very" transitive actions to be those which are
m
-transitive for an arbitrary
m\geq 1.
Such actions are called
infinitely transitive
.

In
2018
, D.Lewis, K.Perry, and A.Straub proved
[
8
]
that the subgroup of
\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{2})
generated by the pair
H_{1},K_{2}
acts on
\mathbb{A}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
infinitely transitively.
This result is the answer to the question of I.Arzhantsev, K.Kuyumzhiyan, and M.Zaidenberg
[
2
]
. It has been proven in
[
2
]
that if
ab\neq 2
, the subgroup
G=\langle H_{a},K_{b}\rangle
of
\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{2})
can not act infinitely transitively.
We obtain an easier way to prove the result from the work
[
8
]
, and also generalize these facts to any number of the above one-parameter subgroups.
We consider the group
G
generated by a finite number of one-parameter subgroups of the form
H_{a}
and
K_{b}
and give an equivalent condition to the infinite transitivity of the group
G
in combinatorial terms.

This paper is organized as follows. In section
2
we give a brief summary of the results needed. In particular in subsection
2.1
we describe in detail the standard structure of an affine toric variety on the plane
\mathbb{A}^{2}.
Subsection
2.2
is dedicated to the homogeneous derivations of the coordinate algebra of the affine plane. In subsection
2.3
we recall combinatorial description of LND in
\mathbb{K}[x,y].
In subsection
2.4
we collect important properties of LNDs in
\mathbb{K}[x,y].
Finally in subsection
2.5
we deal with the structure of an Ind-group
\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{2}).
Results of this subsection are at the cornerstones of our work. These results allow us to determine the closure for subgroups of
\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{2})
generated by one-parametric subgroups. Section
3
contains our main results. In section
4
we give several examples of our results as well as formulate two conjectures generalizing Theorem
2
. Proof of these conjectures is the main goal of our planned subsequent research. Additionally, in the same section we pose a question similar in nature to our results, but for non-affine toric varieties. Answering this question could help develop the theory in a broader context.

Summary of results

The main results of this paper are the following theorems:

Theorem

(
1
)
.

Let
m
be a positive integer. Then the group
\langle H_{1},K_{d_{1}},\ldots,K_{d_{m}}\rangle
acts on
\mathbb{A}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
infinitely transitively if and only if
\GCD(d_{1}-1,\ldots,d_{m}-1)=1.

Theorem

(
2
)
.

Let
m,s
be positive integers. Then the group
G=\langle H_{c_{1}},\ldots,H_{c_{s}},K_{d_{1}},\ldots,K_{d_{m}}\rangle
acts on
\mathbb{A}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
infinitely transitively if and only if

\mathbb{Z}\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}-1&c_{1}\end{pmatrix},\ldots,\begin{pmatrix}-1&c_{s}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}d_{1}&-1\end{pmatrix},\ldots,\begin{pmatrix}d_{m}&-1\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle=\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

These theorems generalize results of
[
8
]
and provide an effective criterion of infinite transitivity for an infinite family of algebraically generated automorphism groups.

We are grateful to Ivan Arzhantsev for his support and Sergey Gayfullin for fruitful discussions and valuable comments.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Toric variety
\mathbb{A}^{2}

Let
\mathbb{K}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero and
\mathbb{A}^{2}
be the two-dimensional affine space over
\mathbb{K}
.
Two-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}\simeq(\mathbb{K}^{\times})^{2}
acts on
\mathbb{A}^{2}
via the
formula

(t_{1},t_{2})\circ(x,y)=(t_{1}x,t_{2}y),\quad t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{K}^{\times},\;x,y\in\mathbb{K}.

We call this torus action
standard
. Let
M
be the character lattice of the torus
\mathbb{T}
and
N
be the lattice of one-parameter subgroups. The natural pairing
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle\colon N\times M\to\mathbb{Z}
extends to the pairing
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle\colon N_{\mathbb{Q}}\times M_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mathbb{Q}
between the associated vector spaces
N_{\mathbb{Q}}=N\otimes\mathbb{Q}
and
M_{\mathbb{Q}}=M\otimes\mathbb{Q}
.
Fix a basis of
N
and its dual basis of
M
.
Let
\sigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\sigma^{\vee}\subseteq M_{\mathbb{Q}}
be the positive quadrants.
Notice that
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\vee}
are dual with respect to natural pairing, i.e.

\sigma^{\vee}=\{u\in M_{\mathbb{Q}}\mid\langle v,u\rangle\geq 0\,\,\,\forall v\in\sigma\}.

For
m=(m_{1},m_{2})\in M
by
\chi^{m}
we denote a Laurent monomial
x_{1}^{m_{1}}x_{2}^{m_{2}}
.
One can easily see that the standard torus action corresponds to the standard grading of the coordinate ring of
\mathbb{A}^{2}
, which coincides with the natural
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-grading of the polynomial algebra:

\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2}]=\bigoplus_{m\in M\cap\,\sigma^{\vee}}\mathbb{K}\chi^{m}.

For the combinatorial description of a general toric affine variety see
[
3
,
5
]
.

2.2.
Homogeneous derivations of the coordinate ring of
\mathbb{A}^{2}

Let us recall that a derivation
\partial\in\Der(\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2}])
of the polynomial algebra
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2}]
is a linear map
\partial\colon\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2}]\to\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2}]
satisfying the Leibniz rule
\partial(fg)=\partial(f)g+f\partial(g)
for any
f,g\in\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2}]
.

Definition 1
.

The derivation is called
homogeneous
with respect to the standard grading discussed in the previous section if the image of any homogeneous polynomial is homogeneous as well. In this case, there is an element
\deg\partial\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
called the
degree
of the derivation
\partial
such that
\deg\partial+\deg f=\deg\partial(f)
for any homogeneous
f\in\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2}]
.

Definition 2
.

The derivation
\partial
is called
locally nilpotent
if for any
f\in\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2}]
there exists a positive integer
n
such that
\partial^{n}(f)=0
. The set of locally nilpotent derivations is denoted by
\LND(\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2}])
.

Let
\partial\in\Der(\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2}])
be a locally nilpotent derivation. There exists a group
\exp(\mathbb{K}\partial)
which is
a
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-subgroup
(one-parameter group) of
\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{2}).
Elements of
\exp(\mathbb{K}\partial)
act on functions from
\mathbb{K}([x_{1},x_{2}])
via the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula:

(1)

\exp(t\partial)(f)=f+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}t^{n}\frac{\partial^{n}(f)}{n!},\quad t\in\mathbb{K},\;f\in\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2}].

Since
\partial
is locally nilpotent, for every
f
this sum has finite number of non-zero summands and the definition is correct. One can see that
\exp
is one-to-one correspondence between
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-subgroups of
\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{2})
, normalized by standard torus action, and homogeneous locally nilpotent derivations of
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2}]
.

2.3.
Demazure roots

Let us denote by
\Xi=\{\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\}
the set of ray generators of a cone
\sigma
.

Definition 3
.

A
Demazure root
which corresponds to a primitive ray generator
\rho_{i}\in\Xi
is a vector
e\in M
such that the following conditions hold:

(1)

\langle\rho_{i},e\rangle=-1
;

(2)

\langle\rho_{j},e\rangle\geq 0
for all
j\neq i
.

For more detail, see
[
4
]
,
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
.

A Demazure root
e
which corresponds to a primitive ray generator
\rho\in\Xi
gives a homogeneous locally nilpotent derivation
\partial_{\rho,e}
of degree
e
via the formula

\partial_{\rho,e}(\chi^{m})=\langle\rho,m\rangle\chi^{m+e}.

All homogeneous
\LND
s arise this way (see
[
11
, Theorem 2.7]
). Thus we have one-to-one correspondence between Demazure roots and
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-subgroups of
\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{2})
normalized by the torus
\mathbb{T}.

2.4.
Two important properties of LND’s in
\mathbb{K}[x,y]

Below we formulate
[
10
, Lemma 1.10]
,
[
2
, Lemma 4.7]
for the case of the polynomial algebra in two variables.

Lemma 1
.

(a)

Any derivation
\partial\in\Der(\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2}])
admits a decomposition

(2)

\partial=\sum_{e\,\in\,\sigma^{\vee}\cap\,M}\partial_{e}

where
\partial_{e}
is a homogeneous derivation of degree
e
.

(b)

The set
S(\partial)=\{e\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap M|\partial_{e}\neq 0\}
is finite. Its convex hull
N(\partial)

is a polygon called the
Newton polygon of

\partial
.

(c)

If
\partial\in{\LND}\,(\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2}])
then for any face
\tau
of
N(\partial)
one has

\partial_{\tau}:=\sum_{e\in\tau\cap M}\partial_{e}\in{\LND}\,(\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2}])\,.

In particular, for any vertex
e
of
N(\partial)
one has
\partial_{e}\in{\LND}\,(\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2}])
.

Every one-parameter subgroup
T
of the torus
\mathbb{T}
corresponds to the vector
\rho\in\sigma.
Moreover,
\rho
determines a linear form
\mathcal{L}_{T}
on the lattice
M
via the formula

\mathcal{L}_{T}(e)=\langle e,\rho\rangle,\quad e\in M.

Let us recall that the adjoint action of the Lie algebra
\Der(\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2}])
on itself is given by the formula

\ad_{\partial}(U)=[\partial,U]=\partial\circ U-U\circ\partial.

The following lemma describes the adjoint action of
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-subgroups, which arise from locally nilpotent derivations
\partial.

Lemma 2
.

[
12
]

If
\partial\in\LND(\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2}])
then the corresponding adjoint action operator
{\ad}_{\partial}\in{\rm End}\,(\Der(\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2}]))
is locally nilpotent.
Moreover, adjoint action of
\exp(\mathbb{K}\partial)
on
U
is described by the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula:

(3)

\displaystyle\Ad_{\exp(\mathbb{K}\partial)}(U)=U+\sum_{n=1}\frac{\ad^{n}_{\partial}(U)}{n!}.

Definition 4
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{T}=\alpha
be a supporting affine line of the Newton polytope
N(\partial)
. Then we define the
T-principal part

\partial_{T}
of the derivation
\partial
corresponding to this line by

\partial_{T}:=\sum_{e\,\in\,\{\mathcal{L}_{T}=\alpha\}\cap S(\partial)}\partial_{e}.

According to Lemma
1
, if
\partial
is locally nilpotent then also
\partial_{T}
is.

2.5.
Ind-group structure on
\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{2})

The group of regular automorphisms of
\mathbb{A}^{2}
has the structure of an ind-group
[
6
,
7
]
.

It means that

G=\lim_{\longrightarrow}\Sigma_{s}.

Here
\Sigma_{1}\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{2}\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{3}\hookrightarrow\ldots
is a sequence of closed embeddings of algebraic varieties
\Sigma_{s}
, and for each pair
(i,j)
there exists a number
n(i,j)
such that
\Sigma_{i}\times\Sigma_{j}\to\Sigma_{n(i,j)}
,
(g,h)\mapsto gh^{-1}
is a morphism of algebraic varieties.

The closure of a subgroup
G\subseteq\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{2})
is defined as

\overline{G}=\lim_{\longrightarrow}\overline{G\cap\Sigma_{s}}.

Definition 5
.

A group
G
acts on a set
X

infinitely transitively
, if it acts
m
-transitively for any positive integer
m
, which means that for any two sets of pairwise distinct points
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
and
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})
there exists
g\in G
such that
g.x_{i}=y_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,m
.

The next propositions are crucial for our understanding of subgroups closure of
\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{2})
.

Proposition 1
.

[
2
, Proposition 4.16]
Consider a subgroup
G\subseteq\Aut(X)

normalized by a one-parameter subgroup
T
of the torus
\mathbb{T}
. Let
H=\exp(\mathbb{K}\partial)
be a
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-subgroup
of
G
where
\partial\in{\LND}\,(\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2}])
, and let
\partial_{T}\in{\LND}\,(\mathbb{K}[x_{1},x_{2}])
be the
T
-principal part of
\partial
.
Then
H_{T}=\exp(\mathbb{K}\partial_{T})
is a
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-subgroup of
\overline{G}
.

Note that any algebraic group is an ind-group. Indeed, let
H
be an algebraic group, then sequence of
\Sigma_{i}=H
for all
i\geq 1
defines a structure of an ind-group on
H.

Definition 6
.

A subgroup
H
of an ind-group
\mathcal{G}
is called
algebraic
if there exists a structure of an algebraic group on
H
such that
H\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}
is a morphism of ind-groups.

Definition 7
.

A subgroup
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
generated by a family of connected algebraic subgroups of
\Aut(X)

is called
algebraically generated
.

Orbits of an algebraically generated automorphism group
G
are locally closed subsets of
X

in the Zariski topology
[
1
, Propsition 1.3]
.

Proposition 2
.

[
2
, Proposition 3.4]

Let
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
be an algebraically generated subgroup. Then the following holds.

(a)

The orbits of
G
and of
\overline{G}
in
X
are the same. In particular, if
\overline{G}
acts on
X
with an open orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\overline{G}}
then also
G
does and
\mathcal{O}_{G}=\mathcal{O}_{\overline{G}}.

(b)

If
\overline{G}
acts
m
-transitively on
{{\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{G}}}
then
G
does also.

(c)

If
\overline{G}
acts infinitely transitively on
{{\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{G}}}
then also
G
does.

3.
Main Results

Let us introduce two special types of
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-subgroups of automorphism group
\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{2}).

For any
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

H_{a}\colon(x,y)\mapsto(x+\alpha y^{a},y)\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;K_{b}\colon(x,y)\mapsto(x,y+\beta x^{b}),\;\;\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{K}\,.

In fact, these groups and groups which arise from homogeneous locally nilpotent derivations are the same: one can see that
H_{a}=\exp(\mathbb{K}y^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial x})
and
K_{b}=\exp(\mathbb{K}x^{b}\frac{\partial}{\partial y})
.
Let us also denote by
K_{s}^{\infty}
the group generated by
K_{b}
for
b\geqslant s\,
:
K_{s}^{\infty}=\langle K_{s},K_{s+1},\ldots,K_{s+n},\ldots\rangle
. Notice that
K_{s}^{\infty}
consists of all automorphisms mapping
(x,y)
to
(x,y+S(x)),
where
S(x)
is divisible by
x^{s}.

Lemma 3
.

Let
G=\langle H_{t},K_{s}^{\infty}\rangle
for some
s,t\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Then
G
acts on
\mathbb{A}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
infinitely transitively.

Proof.

For a point P, we denote by
x(P)
the
x
-coordinate of
P
and by
y(P)
the
y
-coordinate of
P
. Let us show that for any distinct points
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m},P,Q\in\mathbb{A}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
we can find an element
g\in G
such that
g.P_{i}=P_{i}
for all
i=1,\ldots,m
and
g.P=Q
.

Step
1
.
Let us construct an element
g_{1}\in G
such that

(4)

x(g_{1}.P_{i})\neq x(g_{1}.P)\,\text{ for all }\,i=1,\ldots,m\;\text{ and }\;x(g_{1}.P)\neq 0.

Since the ground field
\mathbb{K}
is infinite and the points
P_{i}
are distinct from the point
P
, we can find an element
\alpha\in\mathbb{K}
such that
x(P)+\alpha y(P)^{t}\neq 0
and the inequality

\alpha(y(P_{i})^{t}-y(P)^{t})\neq x(P)-x(P_{i})\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x(P_{i})+\alpha y(P_{i})^{t}\neq x(P)+\alpha y(P)^{t}

holds for all
i=1,\ldots,m
. Then an element
g_{1}\in H_{t}
,
g_{t}\colon(x,y)\mapsto(x+\alpha y^{t},y)
satisfies the condition (
4
).

Step
2
.
Let us construct an element
g_{2}\in G
such that

(5)

g_{2}g_{1}.P_{i}=g_{1}.P_{i}\,\text{ for all }\,i=1,\ldots,m\;\text{ and }\;y(g_{2}g_{1}.P)=y(g_{1}.Q).

By means of Hermite interpolation, we can construct a polynomial
R(t)
with coefficients in
\mathbb{K}
such that following conditions hold:

(a)

t^{s}
divides
R(t)
;

(b)

R(x(g_{1}.P_{i}))=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,m
;

(c)

R(x(g_{1}.P))=y(g_{1}.Q)-y(g_{1}.P)
.

Indeed, by step 1 the numbers
x(g_{1}.P_{i})
differ from
x(g_{1}.P)
and
x(g_{1}.P)
differs from zero, so there is a polynomial
R
which satisfies
R(0)=R^{\prime}(0)=\ldots=R^{(s-1)}(0)=0
and the conditions
(b)
,
(c)
.

We take an element
g_{2}\colon(x,y)\mapsto(x,y+R(x))
, then
g_{2}\in K_{s}^{\infty}
according to the condition
(a)
. From the the condition
(c)
it follows that
y(g_{2}g_{1}.P)=y(g_{1}.Q)
, and because of the condition
(b)
the equality
g_{2}g_{1}.P_{i}=g_{1}.P_{i}
holds for all
i=1,\ldots,m
.

Notice that for constructed elements
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
we have

(6)

y(g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}g_{1}.P)=y(Q)\;\text{ and }\;g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}g_{1}.P_{i}=P_{i}.

Indeed,
y(g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}g_{1}.P)=y(g_{2}g_{1}.P)=y(g_{1}.Q)=y(Q)
.

Step
3
.
Let us denote
P_{0}=g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}g_{1}.P
. Similarly to the step
1
we choose
g_{3}\in H_{t}
such that inequalities

x(P_{i})\neq x(P_{j})\neq x(Q)\neq 0
hold for all
i,j=0,\ldots,m
.
Let
S(t)
be an element of the polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[t]
such that following conditions hold:

(a)

t^{s}
divides
S(t)
;

(b)

y(g_{3}.P_{i})+S(x(g_{3}.P_{i}))=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,m
;

(c)

y(g_{3}.P_{0})+S(x(g_{3}.P_{0}))=y(g_{3}.Q)+S(x(g_{3}.Q))\neq 0
.

We take an element
g_{4}\in K_{s}^{\infty}
,
g_{4}\colon(x,y)\mapsto(x,y+S(x))
. Because of the conditions above,
y(g_{4}g_{3}.P_{i})=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,m
and
y(g_{4}g_{3}.P_{0})=y(g_{4}g_{3}.Q)\neq 0
.

Step
4
.
Now we have
y(g_{4}g_{3}.P_{0})=y(g_{4}g_{3}.Q)\neq 0
and
y(g_{4}g_{3}.P_{i})=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,m
. There is an element
g_{5}\in H_{t}
, that maps
g_{4}g_{3}.P_{0}
to
g_{4}g_{3}.Q
. Indeed, if

\beta=\frac{x(g_{4}g_{3}.Q)-x(g_{4}g_{3}.P_{0})}{y(g_{4}g_{3}.P_{0})^{t}},

then
g_{5}\colon(x,y)\mapsto(x+\beta y^{t},y)
maps
g_{4}g_{3}.P_{0}
to
g_{4}g_{3}.Q
. And since for all
i
holds
y(g_{4}g_{3}.P_{i})=0
, we have
g_{5}g_{4}g_{3}.P_{i}=g_{4}g_{3}.P_{i}
for all
i=1,\ldots,m
.

Step
5
.

We take an element
g=g_{3}^{-1}\circ g_{4}^{-1}\circ g_{5}\circ g_{4}\circ g_{3}\circ g_{1}^{-1}\circ g_{2}\circ g_{1}
. One can see that
g.P_{i}=P_{i}
for any
i=1,\ldots,m
and
g.P=Q
.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
G=\langle H_{1},K_{d},K_{a}\rangle
for some integers
a,d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
Then
K_{d+a-1}\subseteq\overline{G}
.

Proof.

Let us denote by
T
one-dimensional torus, corresponding to the standard basis vector
e_{2}\in N.
Consider the group
L=\Ad_{K_{a}}(\Ad_{K_{d}}(H_{1}))
.
Since
H_{1}
is a one-parameter subgroup in
\Aut(\mathbb{A}^{2})
, we see that
L
is also a
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-subgroup. Thus we have
L=\exp(\mathbb{K}\partial)
where
\partial
is some locally nilpotent derivation. In addition,
L\subseteq G
, so from Proposition
1
we have
L_{T}\subseteq\overline{G}
.
Using Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula (
3

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{r,\varepsilon},\,\partial_{\rho,e_{1}}\right](\chi^{m})=\partial_{r,\varepsilon}\circ\partial_{\rho,e_{1}}(\chi^{m})-\partial_{\rho,e_{1}}\circ\partial_{r,\varepsilon}(\chi^{m})=

\displaystyle=\langle\langle r,e_{1}\rangle\rho-\langle\rho,\varepsilon\rangle r,m\rangle\chi^{m+e_{1}+\varepsilon}.

\displaystyle f_{n+1}=(-1)^{n+1}\Bigg\langle\langle\rho,\varepsilon\rangle^{\underline{n-1}}\langle r,e_{1}+\ldots+e_{n}\rangle\rho-\langle\rho,\varepsilon\rangle^{\underline{n}}r,e_{n+1}\Bigg\rangle\cdot\rho+

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+2}\Bigg\langle\rho,\varepsilon+e_{1}+\ldots+e_{n}\Bigg\rangle\cdot\Bigg(\langle\rho,\varepsilon\rangle^{\underline{n-1}}\langle r,e_{1}+\ldots+e_{n}\rangle\rho-\langle\rho,\varepsilon\rangle^{\underline{n}}r\Bigg)=

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n+1}\langle\rho,\varepsilon\rangle^{\underline{n-1}}\langle r,e_{1}+\ldots+e_{n}\rangle\cdot\langle\rho,e_{n+1}\rangle\cdot\rho+(-1)^{n}\langle\rho,\varepsilon\rangle^{\underline{n}}\cdot\langle r,e_{n+1}\rangle\rho+

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n}\Bigg\langle\rho,\varepsilon+e_{1}+\ldots+e_{n}\Bigg\rangle\cdot\langle\rho,\varepsilon\rangle^{\underline{n-1}}\langle r,e_{1}+\ldots+e_{n}\rangle\rho+

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+1}\Bigg\langle\rho,\varepsilon+e_{1}+\ldots+e_{n}\Bigg\rangle\cdot\langle\rho,\varepsilon\rangle^{\underline{n}}r

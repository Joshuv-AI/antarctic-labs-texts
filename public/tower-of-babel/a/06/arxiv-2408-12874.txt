Enumeration of dihypergraphs with specified degrees and edge types
Catherine Greenhill, Tamás Makai

Abstract
A directed hypergraph (dihypergraph) consists of a set of vertices and a set of hyperarcs, where each hyperarc is partitioned into a head and a tail. Directed hypergraphs are useful in many applications, including the study of chemical reactions or relational databases. We provide asymptotic formulae for the number of directed hypergraphs with given in-degree sequence, out-degree sequence, and with the head and tail sizes of all hyperarcs specified. Our formulae hold when none of the following parameters are too large: the maximum out-degree, the maximum in-degree, the maximum head size and the maximum tail size.

Introduction

Given a finite set of vertices
V
,
a
hyperarc
is an ordered pair
e=(e^{+},e^{-})
of nonempty disjoint subsets of
V
. We say that

e^{+}
is the
tail
and
e^{-}
is the
head
of
e
.
A
directed hypergraph
(dihypergraph)
H=(V,E)
consists of a finite set
V
of vertices
and a set
E
of hyperarcs. It follows from this definition that a directed hypergraph does not contain any repeated hyperarcs or “loops”, which are hyperarcs that contain a repeated vertex.
Directed hypergraphs arise in many applications, for example when
modelling chemical reactions
[
8
,
12
]
or in the study of relational
databases and satisfiability formulae
[
1
]
.
There is increasing interest in directed hypergraphs (or
dihypergraphs
, for short) from the network
science community, for example
[
16
,
21
]
.

Our dihypergraphs definition
follows that used by Gallo, Longo, Pallottino and Nguyen
[
11
]
and Klamt, Haus and Theis
[
15
]
, except that
[
11
]
allows the tail or head of a hyperarc to be empty. Our definition is more general than some in the literature. For example,
the survey paper of Ausiello and Laura
[
1
]
uses a definition
which restricts
e^{-}
to always consist of a single vertex, called the
head of
e
. Other works using this definition are
[
3
,
10
]
.
Gallo et al.
[
11
]
refer to such hypergraphs as
B-hypergraphs
,
and define
F-hypergraphs
to be
dihypergraphs in which
|e^{+}|=1
for every hyperarc
e
.
They also consider BF-hypergraphs in which every hyperarc satisfies either

|e^{-}|=1
or
|e^{+}|=1
. Thakur and Tripathi
[
23
]
compare these three
dihypergraph models.

Qian
[
22
]
gave an exact enumeration result
for unlabelled B-hypergraphs and unlabelled
k
-uniform B-hypergraphs
with a given number of vertices. Here “unlabelled” means that these dihypergraphs
were counted up to isomorphism. We are not aware or any other enumeration
result for dihypergraphs in the literature.

It is useful to have asymptotic enumeration formulae for families of
combinatorial structures. For example, asymptotic enumeration results for graphs
and bipartite graphs
with given degrees have been applied both within mathematics
[
7
,
9
]
and in
other disciplines
[
4
,
20
]
.
Asymptotic enumeration formulae for directed graphs with given degree sequence
have been obtained by Liebenau and Wormald
[
17
]
for a wide range
of degrees, and by Greenhill and McKay
[
13
]
for dense degree
sequences, see also Barvinok
[
2
]
.

Our aim in this paper is to use the switching method to
provide an asymptotic formula for the number of
dihypergraphs when the degrees and hyperarc sizes are not too large.
We will use the connection between dihypergraphs and directed bipartite graphs,
which allows us to make use of existing enumeration formulae for sparse
bipartite graphs.

1.1
Notation and statement of our results

Let
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
denote the natural numbers
and let
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
denote the positive integers.
Given a directed hypergraph
H=(V,E)
with
|V|=n
,
let
m:=|E|
be the number
of hyperarcs in
H
. Vertex
v\in V
has out-degree

d^{+}_{v}
and in-degree
d^{-}_{v}
defined by

d^{+}_{v}:=|\{e\in E\mid v\in e^{+}\}|,\qquad d^{-}_{v}:=|\{e\in E\mid v\in e^{-}\}|,

and the degree sequence of
H
is
{\boldsymbol{d}}:=({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-})
where

{\boldsymbol{d}}^{+}:=\big(d^{+}_{1},d^{+}_{2},\ldots,d^{+}_{n}\big),\qquad{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-}:=\big(d^{-}_{1},d^{-}_{2},\ldots,d^{-}_{n}\big).

We also use a function
\sigma
to capture the number of vertices in the head and tail of each hyperarc.
Specifically,
\sigma:\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}\to\mathbb{N}
is a function such that there are exactly
\sigma(a^{+},a^{-})
hyperarcs
e\in E
with
|e^{+}|=a^{+}
and
|e^{-}|=a^{-}
.
Then the number of hyperarcs in the directed hypergraph is
m=\sum_{(a^{+},a^{-})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}}\,\sigma(a^{+},a^{-})
and

M^{+}:=\sum_{v\in[n]}d_{v}^{+}=\sum_{(a^{+},a^{-})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}}a^{+}\,\sigma(a^{+},a^{-}),\quad M^{-}:=\sum_{v\in[n]}d_{v}^{-}=\sum_{(a^{+},a^{-})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}}a^{-}\,\sigma(a^{+},a^{-}).

(1.1)

Let
{\vec{\mathcal{H}}(\boldsymbol{d}^{+},\boldsymbol{d}^{-},\sigma)}
be the set of directed hypergraphs with degrees
{\boldsymbol{d}}^{+}
,
{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-}
and with head and tail sizes given by
\sigma
, and let

{\vec{H}(\boldsymbol{d}^{+},\boldsymbol{d}^{-},\sigma)}:=|{\vec{\mathcal{H}}(\boldsymbol{d}^{+},\boldsymbol{d}^{-},\sigma)}|
. We want to approximate
{\vec{H}(\boldsymbol{d}^{+},\boldsymbol{d}^{-},\sigma)}
in the sparse
setting, where the maximum degrees and maximum head and tail sizes
are not too large.
To avoid trivialities we assume
throughout the paper that
M^{+}>0
and
M^{-}>0
.

To state our results, we need some more definitions.
It is convenient
to define two vectors
{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+}={\boldsymbol{k}}^{+}(\sigma)=(k_{1}^{+},\ldots,k_{m}^{+})

and
{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-}={\boldsymbol{k}}^{-}(\sigma)=(k_{1}^{-},\ldots,k_{m}^{-})

using the following deterministic process:

•

Create a list of length
m
which contains
\sigma(a^{+},a^{-})

copies of the ordered pair
(a^{+},a^{-})
, for all
(a^{+},a^{-})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}
;

•

Rearrange this list
into reverse lexicographic order;

•

Rename the pairs in this new order as
(k_{1}^{+},k_{1}^{-}),\,\,(k_{2}^{+},k_{2}^{-}),\ldots,(k_{m}^{+},k_{m}^{-})
.

Given any dihypergraph
H=(V,E)\in{\vec{\mathcal{H}}(\boldsymbol{d}^{+},\boldsymbol{d}^{-},\sigma)}
, there are exactly
\prod_{(a^{+},a^{-})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}}\sigma(a^{+},a^{-})!
ways to order the hyperarcs of
H
as
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{m}
such that

k^{+}_{j}=|e_{j}^{+}|
and
k_{j}^{-}=|e_{j}^{-}|
for all
j\in[m]
.
By a slight abuse of notation, by fixing one of these orderings we will write
k^{+}_{e}
and
k^{-}_{e}
instead of
k^{+}_{j}
and
k^{-}_{j}
, where
e=e_{j}
. As none of our quantities depend on the particular ordering of the hyperarcs, this does not cause any harm.
Using this notation, we can rewrite condition (
1.1
) as

M^{+}=\sum_{v\in V}d_{v}^{+}=\sum_{e\in E}k_{e}^{+},\qquad M^{-}=\sum_{v\in V}d_{v}^{-}=\sum_{e\in E}k_{e}^{-}.

Define

d_{\max}^{+}:=\max_{v\in V}d_{v}^{+},\qquad d_{\max}^{-}:=\max_{v\in V}d_{v}^{-},\qquad k_{\max}^{+}:=\max_{e\in E}k_{e}^{+},\qquad k_{\max}^{-}:=\max_{e\in E}k_{e}^{-}

and let

\Delta:=\max\{d_{\max}^{+},d_{\max}^{-},k_{\max}^{+},k_{\max}^{-}\}.

Write
{\boldsymbol{d}}=({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-})
and
{\boldsymbol{k}}=({\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})

and define

R({\boldsymbol{d}})=\sum_{v\in V}d_{v}^{+}d_{v}^{-},\qquad R({\boldsymbol{k}})=\sum_{e\in E}k_{e}^{+}k_{e}^{-}.

(1.2)

Let
\kappa
be the minimum hyperarc size, defined by

\kappa:=\min\big\{a^{+}+a^{-}\,:\,(a^{+},a^{-})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2},\,\sigma(a^{+},a^{-})>0\big\}=\min\big\{k_{e}^{+}+k_{e}^{-}\,:\,e\in E\big\}.

We will focus on dihypergraphs with
\kappa\geq 3
, that is, every hyperarc contains at least three vertices.
Asymptotics are as
n\to\infty
, and we assume that

M^{+}=M^{+}(n)
and
M^{-}=M^{-}(n)
are positive integers which satisfy

M^{+}(n)\to\infty\quad\text{ and }\quad M^{-}(n)\to\infty\quad\text{ as }\,n\to\infty.

Let
(a)_{b}:=a(a-1)\cdots(a-b+1)
denote the falling factorial, for all
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
b\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.
Given two nonnegative integer vectors
\boldsymbol{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{p})
and
\boldsymbol{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{q})
which both sum to
S
, let
S_{r}:=\sum_{i\in[p]}(s_{i})_{r}
and
T_{r}:=\sum_{j\in[q]}(t_{j})_{r}
for
r=2,3

and define

F(\boldsymbol{s},\boldsymbol{t}):=-\frac{S_{2}T_{2}}{2S^{2}}-\frac{S_{2}T_{2}}{2S^{3}}+\frac{S_{3}T_{3}}{3S^{3}}-\frac{S_{2}T_{2}(S_{2}+T_{2})}{4S^{4}}-\frac{S_{2}^{\,2}T_{3}+S_{3}T_{2}^{\,2}}{2S^{4}}+\frac{S_{2}^{\,2}T_{2}^{\,2}}{2S^{5}}.

(1.3)

This function
F
arises
from the asymptotic enumeration formula for sparse bipartite graphs with given degrees (see Theorem
2.1
).

We now state our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-})
and
\sigma
satisfy
(
1.1
)
, and that

\kappa=\kappa(\sigma)\geq 3
.
Define

\eta=\eta({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},\sigma):=\frac{\Delta^{2}}{\min\{M^{+},M^{-}\}}+\frac{\Delta^{4}}{\max\{M^{+},M^{-}\}}+\frac{(d_{\max}^{+}k_{\max}^{+})^{3}}{(M^{+})^{2}}+\frac{(d_{\max}^{-}k_{\max}^{-})^{3}}{(M^{-})^{2}}

If
\eta=o(1)
then the number of directed hypergraphs with parameters
({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},\sigma)
satisfies

\displaystyle{\vec{H}(\boldsymbol{d}^{+},\boldsymbol{d}^{-},\sigma)}

\displaystyle=\frac{M^{+}!\,M^{-}!}{\prod_{v\in V}d_{v}^{+}!\,d_{v}^{-}!\,\prod_{e\in E}k_{e}^{+}!\,k_{e}^{-}!\,\prod_{(a^{+},a^{-})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}}\sigma(a^{+},a^{-})!}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+})+F({\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})-\frac{R({\boldsymbol{d}})R({\boldsymbol{k}})}{M^{+}\,M^{-}}+O(\eta)}}\right).

Remark 1.2
.

In this remark we wish to clarify our use of this asymptotic notation.
The assumptions of Theorem
1.1
can be rewritten in the form
\varepsilon_{n}=o(1)
for some expression
\varepsilon_{n}
. The theorem provides an asymptotic formula with an error term which can be written as
O\big(g_{n}\big)
, where
g_{n}
is a positive function of
n
. Our proof establishes that there exist positive absolute constants
\varepsilon
and
C
such that if
\varepsilon_{n}<\varepsilon
then the absolute value of the error term is at most
Cg_{n}
.
We use asymptotic notation in this way throughout the paper, and remark that this usage is consistent with
[
14
]
.

Remark 1.3
.

The expression for
\eta
always contains an unnecessary term. For example, if
M^{-}\leq M^{+}
then the assumption that
\eta=o(1)
implies that
\Delta^{4}=o(M^{+})
, and hence

\frac{(d_{\max}^{+}k_{\max}^{+})^{3}}{(M^{+})^{2}}\leq\frac{\Delta^{6}}{(M^{+})^{2}}=O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\Delta^{4}}{M^{+}}}}\right).

However, it is convenient to write
\eta
in this symmetric form, to avoid having two different definitions depending on which of
M^{+}
,
M^{-}
is larger.

The following corollary considers the special case of B-hypergraphs and F-hypergraphs.

Corollary 1.4
.

Suppose that
({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-})
and
\sigma
satisfy
(
1.1
)
and

\kappa=\kappa(\sigma)\geq 3
.

(a)

[B-hypergraphs]:
Suppose that

\sigma(a^{+},a^{-})=0
whenever
a^{-}\neq 1
. If
\Delta^{4}=o(M^{+})
then

\displaystyle{\vec{H}(\boldsymbol{d}^{+},\boldsymbol{d}^{-},\sigma)}

\displaystyle=\frac{M^{+}!\,m!}{\prod_{v\in V}d_{v}^{+}!\,d_{v}^{-}!\,\prod_{e\in E}k_{e}^{+}!\,\prod_{a^{+}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\sigma(a^{+},1)!}\,\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+})-\frac{R({\boldsymbol{d}})}{m}+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\Delta^{4}}{M^{+}}}}\right)}}\right).

(b)

[F-hypergraphs]:
Suppose that

\sigma(a^{+},a^{-})=0
whenever
a^{+}\neq 1
. If
\Delta=o(M^{-})
then

\displaystyle{\vec{H}(\boldsymbol{d}^{+},\boldsymbol{d}^{-},\sigma)}

\displaystyle=\frac{M^{-}!\,m!}{\prod_{v\in V}d_{v}^{+}!\,d_{v}^{-}!\,\prod_{e\in E}k_{e}^{-}!\,\prod_{a^{-}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\sigma(1,a^{-})!}\,\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F({\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})-\frac{R({\boldsymbol{d}})}{m}+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\Delta^{4}}{M^{-}}}}\right)}}\right).

Proof.

For (a),
the result follows from Theorem
1.1
after observing that
\min\{M^{+},M^{-}\}=m
,
M^{+}\leq\Delta m
and

.
The result for (b) follows by symmetry.
∎

1.2
Our approach

In order to make a connection between directed hypergraphs and directed bipartite graphs,
we start by introducing
hyperarc-labelled directed hypergraphs
.
These are
directed hypergraphs where the hyperarcs are labelled
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{m}
.
Let
L({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})
denote the number of hyperarc-labelled directed hypergraphs with degree sequence
{\boldsymbol{d}}:=({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-})
and hyperarc size sequence
{\boldsymbol{k}}:=({\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})
.
Observe that

\frac{L({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})}{{\vec{H}(\boldsymbol{d}^{+},\boldsymbol{d}^{-},\sigma)}}=\prod_{(a^{+},a^{-})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}}\sigma(a^{+},a^{-})!\,,

(1.4)

since the vectors
{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+}
,
{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-}
are fixed, so the only hyperarcs that can be permuted are those
with the same value of
(|e^{+}|,|e^{-}|)
.

Next we will exploit the connection between dihypergraphs and directed bipartite graphs.

is

, where one part contains the vertices in

and the other part contains vertices corresponding to the (labelled) elements of

.
The arc set

is defined as follows: if

then

contains the arc from

to

, while if

then

contains the arc from

to

.

Equivalently, we can partition

into

, where

from

,
and

. After “forgetting” the directions of elements of

.
We will use the terminology “hyperarc” for dihypergraphs, “arc” for directed bipartite graphs and “edge” for bipartite graphs.

For

we write

for the in-neighbourhood of

.
By definition, in any dihypergraph

we have
e^{+}\cap e^{-}=\emptyset
for all

, and
there are no repeated hyperarcs. For the corresponding directed bipartite graph

, these conditions state that

has no directed 2-cycles and

\text{ for all distinct $e,f\in E$},\,\,\text{ either }\,\,N^{+}_{G}(e)\neq N_{G}^{+}(f)\,\,\text{ or }\,\,N_{G}^{-}(e)\neq N^{-}_{G}(f).

That is, no two vertices

in

have the same in-neighbours and the same out-neighbours in
G
.

Each directed bipartite graph

with no directed 2-cycles
which satisfies (
1.5
)
uniquely defines a hyperarc-labelled directed hypergraph by letting

e^{+}=N^{-}_{G}(e)\qquad\text{ and }\qquad e^{-}=N^{+}_{G}(e)

for all

. See Figure

Let

\vec{\mathcal{B}}({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})
be the set of directed bipartite graphs

such that

has bipartite out-degree sequence
({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})
and

({\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+})
.
Denote by
\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{0}({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})
the set of all

\vec{\mathcal{B}}({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})
with no directed

{\vec{B}}_{0}({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})=|\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{0}({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})|
.
Denote by
P({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})
the probability that
a uniformly chosen
G\in\vec{\mathcal{B}}_{0}({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})

satisfies (

).
Then

L({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})={\vec{B}}_{0}({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})\,P({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-}).

(1.6)

This leads to a strategy for approximating

which applies whenever it is unlikely that (

)
fails (that is, when
P({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})
is close to 1):

(i)

Define vectors
{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-}
as described in Section
1.1
.

(ii)

Use a switching argument to obtain an asymptotic formula for
{\vec{B}}_{0}({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})
.

(iii)

Prove that only a negligible proportion of these directed bipartite graphs
G

fail (
1.5
).

(iv)

Apply (
1.4
) and (
1.6
) to deduce an
asymptotic enumeration formula for
H({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},\sigma)
.

We remark that in order to have a repeated hyperarc, there must be at least
one pair
(a^{+},a^{-})
with
\sigma(a^{+},a^{-})\geq 2
. If
\sigma
only
takes values in
\{0,1\}
then
P({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})=1

and step (iii) is unnecessary.

In Section

.

2
Preliminaries

Let
V
and
W
be disjoint finite sets.
We work with bipartite degree sequences
(\boldsymbol{s},\boldsymbol{t})
of nonnegative integers such that
s_{v}
is the degree of vertex
v
for all
v\in V
and
t_{w}
is the degree of vertex
W
for all
w\in W
, and
S=\sum_{v\in V}s_{v}=\sum_{w\in W}t_{w}
.
Denote by
s_{\max},t_{\max}
the maximum component of the vectors
\boldsymbol{s},\boldsymbol{t}
respectively, and define
F(\boldsymbol{s},\boldsymbol{t})
as in (

).

Let
\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{s},\boldsymbol{t})
be the set of bipartite graphs with vertex bipartition
V\cup W

and bipartite degree sequence
(\boldsymbol{s},\boldsymbol{t})
.
We will use the asymptotic enumeration result for
|\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{s},\boldsymbol{t})|
,
restated from
[
14
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
14
, Theorem 1.3]
)
.

Suppose that
S\to\infty
, and that
1\leq s_{\max}t_{\max}=o(S^{2/3})
. Then

\displaystyle|\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{s},\boldsymbol{t})|=\frac{S!}{\prod_{v\in V}s_{v}!\prod_{w\in W}t_{w}!}\,\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F(\boldsymbol{s},\boldsymbol{t})+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{s_{\max}^{3}t_{\max}^{3}}{S^{2}}}}\right)}}\right).

Note that there are other asymptotic enumeration results for bipartite graphs by degree sequence which hold under less restrictive conditions on the degree sequence, including
[
13
]
in the dense regime, and most recently by Liebenau and Wormald
[
17
]
for a wide range of near-regular degrees. However, the switching argument in Section
3
imposes an upper bound on the maximum degrees and hyperarc sizes, which implies that the result of Theorem
2.1
is suitable for our purposes.

We can apply this result to write down an asymptotic enumeration formula for
the number of sparse directed bipartite graphs with given degrees, using the fact that

{\vec{B}}({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})=|\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+})|\,|\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})|.

Corollary 2.2
.

Suppose that
(d_{\max}^{+}\,k_{\max}^{+})^{3/2}=o(M^{+})
and
(d_{\max}^{-}\,k_{\max}^{-})^{3/2}=o(M^{-})
.
Then

\displaystyle{\vec{B}}({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})

\displaystyle=\frac{M^{+}!\,M^{-}!}{\prod_{v\in V}d_{v}^{+}!\,d_{v}^{-}!\,\prod_{e\in E}k_{e}^{+}!\,k_{e}^{-}!}

\displaystyle\quad\times\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(F({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+})+F({\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{(d_{\max}^{+}k_{\max}^{+})^{3}}{(M^{+})^{2}}+\frac{(d_{\max}^{-}k_{\max}^{-})^{3}}{(M^{-})^{2}}}}\right)}}\right).

We will use the following result of McKay
[

]
to obtain upper bounds on the probability of various bad cases occuring. In fact we use a restatement of a special case of McKay’s result, using the wording from

Theorem 2.3

(

•

The out-degree of each vertex in
N_{G}^{+}(e)
is decreased by 2, and the in-degree of each vertex in
N_{G}^{-}(e)
is decreased by 2.

We analyse this situation in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-}
be vectors of positive integers with minimum hyperarc size
\kappa\geq 3
. Then for

\Delta^{2}=o\bigl(\min\{M^{+},M^{-}\}\bigr)
and

\Delta^{4}=o\bigl(\max\{M^{+},M^{-}\}\bigr)
we have

Q({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})={\vec{B}}_{0}({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})\,O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\Delta^{3}}{\max\{M^{+},M^{-}\}}}}\right).

Proof.

Let
e,f\in E
with
k_{e}^{+}=k_{f}^{+}
and
k_{e}^{-}=k_{f}^{-}
. In addition, let
U_{e}^{+},U_{e}^{-}\subseteq V
be disjoint sets of vertices such that

|U_{e}^{+}|=k_{e}^{+},\quad|U_{e}^{-}|=k_{e}^{-},\quad d^{+}_{u}\geq 2\,\text{ for all }\,u\in U_{e}^{+},\quad d^{-}_{u}\geq 2\,\text{ for all }\,u\in U_{e}^{-}.

Then
Q({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})
can be estimated from above by summing
{\vec{B}}_{0}(\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{-},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{-})
over all such pairs
e,f
and sets
U_{e}^{+},U_{e}^{-}
, where
\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{-},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{-}
are constructed from
{\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-}
as follows:

•

The vector
\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{+}
matches
{\boldsymbol{d}}^{+}
in every component except those corresponding to
u\in U_{e}^{+}
, where
\hat{d}_{u}^{+}=d_{u}^{+}-2
.

•

Similarly the vector
\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{-}
matches
{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-}
in every component except those corresponding to
u\in U_{e}^{-}
, where
\hat{d}_{u}^{-}=d_{u}^{-}-2
.

•

Finally
\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{+}
matches
{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+}
(respectively,
\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{-}
matches
{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-}
) in every component except those corresponding to
e,f
, where
\hat{k}_{e}^{+}=\hat{k}_{f}^{+}=\hat{k}_{e}^{-}=\hat{k}_{f}^{-}=0
.

The proof has three steps.

Step 1:
Compare
{\vec{B}}_{0}(\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{-},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{-})
with
{\vec{B}}(\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{-},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{-})
.

Let
\hat{M}^{+},\hat{M}^{-},\hat{\Delta}
correspond to
M^{+},M^{-},\Delta
in the natural way. Then
M^{+}-2\Delta\leq\hat{M}^{+}\leq M^{+}
and
M^{-}-2\Delta\leq\hat{M}^{-}\leq M^{-}
, implying that

\hat{M}^{+}=\bigl(1+O(\Delta/M^{+})\bigr)M^{+}\quad\text{ and }\quad M^{-}=\bigl(1+O(\Delta/M^{-})\bigr)M^{-}.

As the value of any component of the vectors was only decreased we also have
\hat{\Delta}\leq\Delta
.
In addition, for
\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}=(\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{-})
we have

R({\boldsymbol{k}})-2\Delta^{2}\leq R(\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}})\leq R({\boldsymbol{k}})

as two terms of
R({\boldsymbol{k}})
which each may have been as large as
\Delta^{2}
have been replaced by 0 in
R(\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}})
.
Similarly, for
\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}=(\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{-})
we have

R({\boldsymbol{d}})-4\Delta^{2}\leq R(\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}})\leq R({\boldsymbol{d}})

as decreasing any
d_{u}^{+}
or
d_{u}^{-}
by 2 decreases
R({\boldsymbol{d}})
by at most
2\Delta
, and at most
2\Delta
values have been changed. Therefore, using (
3.1
),

\frac{R(\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}})R(\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}})}{\hat{M}^{+}\hat{M}^{-}}=\frac{R({\boldsymbol{d}})R({\boldsymbol{k}})}{M^{+}M^{-}}+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\Delta^{2}\bigl(R({\boldsymbol{d}})+R({\boldsymbol{k}})\bigr)}{M^{+}M^{-}}}}\right)=\frac{R({\boldsymbol{d}})R({\boldsymbol{k}})}{M^{+}M^{-}}+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\Delta^{3}}{\max\{M^{+},M^{-}\}}}}\right).

Combining this with Corollary
3.5
, we deduce that

\displaystyle{\vec{B}}_{0}(\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{-},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{-})

\displaystyle={\vec{B}}(\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{-},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{-})\,\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\frac{R({\boldsymbol{d}})R({\boldsymbol{k}})}{M^{+}M^{-}}+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\Delta^{4}}{\max\{M^{+},M^{-}\}}+\frac{\Delta^{2}}{\min\{M^{+},M^{-}\}}}}\right)}}\right)

\displaystyle={\vec{B}}(\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{-},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{-})\,\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\frac{R({\boldsymbol{d}})R({\boldsymbol{k}})}{M^{+}M^{-}}+o(1)}}\right).

(4.1)

Step 2:
Compare
B(\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{-},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{-})
with
B({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})
.

Note,
{\vec{B}}(\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{-},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{-})
equals the number of directed bipartite graphs
G
in
\vec{\mathcal{B}}({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})
which contain all arcs in

\{(u,e),(u,f):u\in U_{e}^{+}\}\cup\{(e,u),(f,u):u\in U_{e}^{-}\}.

This follows since
\hat{k}_{e}^{+}=\hat{k}_{e}^{-}=\hat{k}_{f}^{+}=\hat{k}_{f}^{-}=0
, so
none of the above arcs can be present in any element of
\vec{\mathcal{B}}(\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{-},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{-})
.
Applying Theorem
2.3
to
G^{+}
and
G^{-}
gives, for
n
sufficiently large,

\displaystyle\frac{{\vec{B}}(\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{-},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{-})}{{\vec{B}}({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(k_{e}^{+}!)^{2}(k_{e}^{-}!)^{2}\prod_{u\in U_{e}^{+}}(d_{u}^{+})_{2}\prod_{u\in U_{e}^{-}}(d_{u}^{-})_{2}}{\big(M^{+}-O(\Delta^{2})\big)_{2k_{e}^{+}}\,\big(M^{-}-O(\Delta^{2}\big)_{2k_{e}^{-}}}

\displaystyle\leq(k_{e}^{+}!)^{2}(k_{e}^{-}!)^{2}\prod_{u\in U_{e}^{+}}(d_{u}^{+})_{2}\prod_{u\in U_{e}^{-}}(d_{u}^{-})_{2}\,\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{2}{M^{+}}}}\right)^{2k_{e}^{+}}\,\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{2}{M^{-}}}}\right)^{2k_{e}^{-}}.

(4.2)

Step 3:
Sum over all choices of
e
,
f
,
U_{e}^{+}
,
U_{e}^{-}
.

First we will sum over all choices of
U_{e}^{+}
and
U_{e}^{-}
of the appropriate size, ignoring the disjointness condition for an upper bound.
Observe that
(d_{u}^{+})_{2}\leq\Delta d_{u}^{+}
and
(d_{u}^{-})_{2}\leq\Delta d_{u}^{-}
for all
u\in V
, and that

k_{e}^{+}!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U_{e}^{+}\subseteq V\\
|U_{e}^{+}|=k_{e}^{+}\end{subarray}}\prod_{u\in U_{e}^{+}}d_{u}^{+}\leq\bigg(\sum_{u\in V}d_{u}^{+}\bigg)^{k_{e}^{+}}=\big(M^{+}\big)^{k_{e}^{+}}\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,k_{e}^{-}!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U_{e}^{-}\subseteq V\\
|U_{e}^{-}|=k_{e}^{-}\end{subarray}}\prod_{u\in U_{e}^{-}}d_{u}^{-}\leq\big(M^{-}\big)^{k_{e}^{-}}.

Using these inequalities with (
4.2
) gives

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U_{e}^{+}\subseteq V\\
|U_{e}^{+}|=k_{e}^{+}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U_{e}^{-}\subseteq V\\
|U_{e}^{-}|=k_{e}^{-}\end{subarray}}\frac{{\vec{B}}(\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{-},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{-})}{{\vec{B}}({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})}\leq(k_{e}^{+}!)(k_{e}^{-}!)\,\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{4\Delta}{M^{+}}}}\right)^{k_{e}^{+}}\,\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{4\Delta}{M^{-}}}}\right)^{k_{e}^{-}}.

Under our assumptions, the right hand side is maximised when
k_{e}^{+}
and
k_{e}^{-}
are as small as possible. Due to our condition that
\kappa\geq 3
,
for an upper bound we can assume that either
k_{e}^{+}=2
and
k_{e}^{-}=1
or
k_{e}^{+}=1
and
k_{e}^{-}=2
.
Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U_{e}^{+}\subseteq V\\
|U_{e}^{+}|=k_{e}^{+}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U_{e}^{-}\subseteq V\\
|U_{e}^{-}|=k_{e}^{-}\end{subarray}}\frac{{\vec{B}}(\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{-},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{-})}{{\vec{B}}({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})}=O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\Delta^{3}}{M^{+}\,M^{-}\,\min\{M^{+},M^{-}\}}}}\right).

(4.3)

Finally, for a worst case we assume that all
\binom{|E|}{2}
choices of unordered pairs of hyperarc

\{e,f\}
have the same head and tail sizes as each other. Since the tail and head of
each hyperarc is nonempty, it follows that
|E|\leq\min\{M^{+},M^{-}\}
. Hence, summing the above expression over all pairs
\{e,f\}
and combining with Corollary
3.5
, (
4.1
) and (
4.3
) gives

\displaystyle Q({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e,f\in E\\
e\neq f\end{subarray}}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U_{e}^{+},U_{e}^{-}\subseteq V\\
|U_{e}^{+}|=k_{e}^{+},|U_{e}^{-}|=k_{e}^{-}\end{subarray}}\,{\vec{B}}_{0}(\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{-},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{-})

\displaystyle=\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\frac{R({\boldsymbol{d}})R({\boldsymbol{k}})}{M^{+}\,M^{-}}+o(1)}}\right)\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e,f\in E\\
e\neq f\end{subarray}}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U_{e}^{+},U_{e}^{-}\subseteq V\\
|U_{e}^{+}|=k_{e}^{+},|U_{e}^{-}|=k_{e}^{-}\end{subarray}}\,B(\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{+},\hat{{\boldsymbol{d}}}^{-},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{-})

\displaystyle=\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\frac{R({\boldsymbol{d}})R({\boldsymbol{k}})}{M^{+}\,M^{-}}}}\right)\,B({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e,f\in E\\
e\neq f\end{subarray}}O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\Delta^{3}}{M^{+}\,M^{-}\,\min\{M^{+},\,M^{-}\}}}}\right)

\displaystyle={\vec{B}}_{0}({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e,f\in E\\
e\neq f\end{subarray}}O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\Delta^{3}}{M^{+}\,M^{-}\,\min\{M^{+},\,M^{-}\}}}}\right)

\displaystyle={\vec{B}}_{0}({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})\,O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\Delta^{3}}{\max\{M^{+},M^{-}\}}}}\right),

completing the proof of Lemma
4.1
.
∎

We can now put all the pieces together to prove our main result.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

First note that

P({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})=1-\frac{Q({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})}{{\vec{B}}_{0}({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})}=1-O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\Delta^{3}}{\max\{M^{+},M^{-}\}}}}\right)

using Lemma
4.1
.
Hence, combining (
1.6
), Corollary
3.5
and Lemma
4.1
shows that

\displaystyle L({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-})

\displaystyle=\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\frac{R({\boldsymbol{d}})R({\boldsymbol{k}})}{M^{+}M^{-}}+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\Delta^{2}}{\min\{M^{+},M^{-}\}}+\frac{\Delta^{4}}{\max\{M^{+},M^{-}\}}}}\right)}}\right)\,{\vec{B}}({\boldsymbol{d}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{+},{\boldsymbol{d}}^{-},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{-}).

The proof is completed using (
1.4
) and Corollary
2.2
.
∎

Selfsimilar Hessian manifolds
Pavel Osipov

Abstract
A selfsimiar manifold is a Riemannian manifold ( M , g ) \left(M,g\right) endowed with a homothetic vector field ξ \xi . We characterize global selfsimilar manifolds and describe the structure of local selfsimilar manifolds. We prove that any selfsimilar manifold with a potential homothetic vector field is a conical Riemannian manifold or a Eucledean space. A radiant Hessian manifold is selfsimilar Hessian manifold ( M , ∇ , g , ξ ) \left(M,\nabla,g,\xi\right) such that ∇ ξ = λ ​ Id \nabla\xi=\lambda\text{Id} . We prove that any selfsimilar Hessian manifold with a potential homothetic vector field is locally isomorphic to a product radiant Hessian manifolds and describe the local structure of radiant selfsimialar Hessian manifolds.

Introduction

We study Riemannian manifolds endowed with a homothetic vector fields. Precisely, a
selfsimilar manifold
is a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
endowed with a vector field
\xi
satisfying
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g=2g
. Moreover, if the field
\xi
is complete then we say that
\left(M,g,\xi\right)
is a
globally selfsimilar manifold
.

Riemannian cones

\left(\hat{M}=M\times\mathbb{R}^{>0},\hat{g}=s^{2}g_{M}+ds^{2},\hat{\xi}=s\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)
are example of selfsimilar manifolds. Riemannian cones have important applications in supegravity (
[
ACDM
]
,

[
ACM
]
,
[
CDM
]
,
[
CDMV
]
). It is well-known that a geodesically complete selfsimilar manifold is isometric to a Euclidean space. If the holonomy group of a Riemannian cone
\left(\hat{M},\hat{g},\hat{\xi}\right)
is decomposable then
(M,g)
has a constant curvature 1 (
[
Ga
]
). Holonomy and geometry of pseudo-Riemannian cones are studied in
[
ACGL
]
and
[
ACL
]
. Selfsimilar Lorentzian manifolds are characterized in
[
A
]
. A
conical Riemannian manifold
is a Riemannian manifold endowed with a vector field
\xi
satisfying
D\xi=\text{Id}
, where
D
is a Levi-Civita connection. Any conical Riemannian manifold is locally isometric to a Riemannian cone. Hence, any conical Riemannian manifold is a selfsimilar manifold.

In Subsection

, we prove that any selfsimilar manifold is locally isomorphic to a global selfsimilar manifold. The results of subsections

Theorem 1.1
.

Any global selfsimilar manifold
(C,g,\xi)
is isomorphic to one of the following:

(i)

\left(\mathbb{R}^{n},\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(dx^{i}\right)^{2},\rho+\eta\right)
,
where
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}
is a radiant vector field and
\eta\in\text{so}(n)
is a Killing vector field.

(ii)

\left(\hat{M}=M\times\mathbb{R}^{>0},\hat{g}=s^{2}g_{M}+sds\cdot\alpha+ds^{2},s\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)
, where

s
is a coordinate on
\mathbb{R}^{>0}
,
g_{M}
is a Riemannian metric on
M
,
\alpha
a 1-form on
M
, and

g_{M}(X,X)+2\alpha(X)+1>0,\ \ \ \text{for any}\ \ \ X\in TM.

The identification
(C,g,\xi)\simeq\left(\hat{M},\hat{g},s\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)
is defined by

s=\sqrt{g(\xi,\xi_{)}},\ \ \ c\ =\ \left(\gamma_{c}\cap\{s=1\}\right)\times s\ \in\ M\times\mathbb{R}^{>0},\ \ \ g_{M}=g|_{M\times 1},\ \ \ \alpha=\iota_{\xi}g|_{M\times 1},

where
c\in C

\gamma_{c}
is the integral curve of
\xi

Any selfsimilar manifold is locally isomorphic to a global selfsimilar manifold.

A selfsimilar manifold can be locally isomorphic to a global selfsimilar manifold from item (i) at a neighborhood of a point and isomorphic to one from item (ii) at a neighborhood of another point (see Example
2.15
).

We say that
(C,g,\xi)
is a selfsimilar manifold with a
potential homothetic vector field
if
\xi
is locally defined as a gradient of a function. Denote If
\xi=\text{grad}\ f
on a domain
U
then
\iota_{\xi}g=0|_{U}=df
. Moreover, a form is closed if and only if it is locally exact. Therefore, the field
\xi
is potential if and only if
d\iota_{\xi}g=0
.

A Riemannian cone and a Euclidean space with a radiant vector field are selfsimilar manifolds with a potential vector field (see Example
2.17
and Example
2.18

The following theorem implies that any selfsimilar manifold with a potential homothetic vector field is locally isometric to a Eucledean space or a Riemannian cone.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\left(M,g,\xi\right)
is a global selfsimilar manifold with a potential homothetic vector field.

(i)

If
\xi
vanishes at a point then
\left(M,g,\xi\right)
is Euclidean space with a radiant vector field
\left(\mathbb{R}^{n},\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(dx^{i}\right)^{2},\sum x^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\right)
.

(ii)

If
\xi
does not vanishes at any point then
\left(M,g,\xi\right)
is a Riemannian cone
\left(\hat{M},\hat{g},\hat{\xi}\right)
.

If a global selfsimilar manifold
(C,g,\xi)
is isometric to a Riemannian cone then the identification
C\simeq M\times\mathbb{R}^{>0}
is defined as in Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(C,\nabla,g,\xi)
be an
n+1
-dimensional radiant selfsimilar Hessian manifold and
p\in C

(i)

Let
\xi_{p}=0

(ii)

Let
\xi_{p}\neq 0

\left(x^{1},\ldots x^{n+1}\right)
at a neighborhood of
p
such that

x^{n+1}>0,\ \ \ \xi=\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ g=\text{Hess}\left(\left(x^{n+1}\right)^{2\lambda^{-1}}\psi\right),

\displaystyle\left(4\lambda^{-2}-2\lambda^{-1}\right)\psi>0,

(1.3)

\displaystyle X^{2}\psi>\max\left(\left(2-4\lambda^{-1}\right)X\left(\psi\right)-\left(4\lambda^{-2}-2\lambda^{-1}\right)\psi,0\right),

\displaystyle\text{if and only if}\ \ \ X^{2}\psi>\left(2-4\lambda^{-1}\right)X\left(\psi\right)-\left(4\lambda^{-2}-2\lambda^{-1}\right)\psi,

Selfsimilar manifolds

Definition 2.1
.

A
selfsimilar manifold

(C,g,\xi)
is a Riemannian manifold
(C,g)
endowed with a field
\xi
satisfying

\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g=2g.

If
\xi
is complete then the manifold is called a
global selfsimilar manifold
.

It follows from the definition that a global selfsimilar manifold is a Riemannian manifold endowed with a 1-parameter group of homothetic automorphims
\{\varphi_{t}\}
such that
\varphi_{t}^{*}g=e^{2t}g
. The term "selfsimilar" is motivated by the fact that for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{>0}
a global selfsimilar manifold
(C,g)
is isometric to
(C,\lambda g)
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
\left(\hat{M}=M\times\mathbb{R}^{>0},\hat{g}=s^{2}g_{M}+ds^{2}\right)
be a
Riemannian cone
and
\xi=s\frac{\partial}{\partial s}
. Then
\left(\hat{M},\hat{g},\xi\right)
is a globally selfsimilar manifold.

Example 2.3
.

A
conical Riemannian manifold

(C,g,\xi)
is a Riemannian manifold
(C,g)
endowed with a nowhere vanishing vector field
\xi
satisfying

D\xi=\text{Id},

where
D
is the Levi-Civita connection. The collection
(C,g,\xi)
is locally isomorphic to a Riemannian cone
{\left(M\times\mathbb{R}^{>0},s^{2}g_{M}+ds^{2},s\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)}
(see
[
ACHK
]
). Therefore, any conical Riemannian manifold is a selfsimilar manifold.

Global selfsimilar manifolds

2.1.1
The case of a nowhere vanishing homothetic vector field

We will denote the symmetric product of tensors
\alpha
and
\beta
by
\alpha\cdot\beta
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
s
be a coordinate on
\mathbb{R}^{>0}
,
g_{M}
a Riemannian metric on
M
,
\alpha
a 1-form on
M
, and
f
a positive definite function on
M
. Then the bilinear form

g=s^{2}g_{M}+sds\cdot\alpha+fds^{2}

is positive if and only if

g_{M}(X,X)+2\alpha(X)+f>0,\ \ \ \text{for any}\ \ \ X\in TM.

Proof.

Any tangent vector admits a form
as\frac{\partial}{\partial s}
,
X
, or
as\frac{\partial}{\partial s}+aX
, where
X\in TM
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
f
is positive definite,

g\left(as\frac{\partial}{\partial s},as\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)=a^{2}s^{2}f>0.

Moreover,

g(X,X)=s^{2}g_{M}(X,X)>0.

Finally, we have

g\left(as\frac{\partial}{\partial s}+aX,as\frac{\partial}{\partial s}+aX\right)=a^{2}s^{2}\left(g_{M}(X,X)+2\alpha(X)+f\right).

That is,

g\left(as\frac{\partial}{\partial s}+aX,as\frac{\partial}{\partial s}+aX\right)>0\ \ \ \text{if and only if}\ \ \ g_{M}(X,X)+2\alpha(X)+f>0.

Thus,

g=s^{2}g_{M}+sds\cdot\alpha+fds^{2}

is positive if and only if

g_{M}(X,X)+2\alpha(X)+f>0,\ \ \ \text{for any}\ \ \ X\in TM.

∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
g
be a symmetric bilinear form on
M\times\mathbb{R}^{>0}
. Then
(M\times\mathbb{R}^{>0},g,s\frac{\partial}{\partial s})
is a selfsimilar manifold if and only if we have

g=s^{2}g_{M}+sds\cdot\alpha+fds^{2},

g_{M}(X,X)+2\alpha(X)+f>0,\ \ \ \text{for any}\ \ \ X\in TM,

(2.1)

where
s
is a coordinate on
\mathbb{R}^{>0}
,
g_{M}
a Riemannian metric on
M
,
\alpha
a 1-form on
M
, and
f
a positive definite function on
M
.

Proof.

The bilinear form

g=s^{2}g_{M}+sds\cdot\alpha+fds^{2}

satisfies
\mathcal{L}_{s\frac{\partial}{\partial s}}g=2g
. According to Lemma
2.4
,
g
is positive definite if and only if the condition (
2.1
) holds.

Let
\left(M\times\mathbb{R}^{>0},g,s\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)
be a selfsimilar manifold. The condition
\mathcal{L}_{s\frac{\partial}{\partial s}}g=2g
is equivalent to the condition
\lambda_{t}g=t^{2}g
, where the map
\lambda_{t}:M\times\mathbb{R}^{>0}\to M\times\mathbb{R}^{>0}
defined by the rule
\lambda_{t}(x\times s)=x\times ts
. Let
\pi:M\times\mathbb{R}^{>0}\to M
be the projection. Then the metric
g
lies in

\text{Sym}^{2}\left(\pi^{*}TM\oplus\ker\pi\right)^{*}=\text{Sym}^{2}\left(\pi^{*}TM\right)^{*}\oplus\text{Sym}^{2}\left(\pi^{*}TM\otimes\ker\pi\right)^{*}\oplus\text{Sym}^{2}\left(\ker\pi\right)^{*}.

Let
\pi_{2,0}
,
\pi_{1,1}
, and
\pi_{0,2}
be the projections of
\text{Sym}^{2}\left(\pi^{*}TM\oplus\ker\pi\right)^{*}
on the three summands from the decomposition above. Define
g|_{M\times 1}=g_{M}
. For any
X_{1},X_{2}\in\pi^{*}TM
we have

g(X_{1},X_{2})(m\times s)=\lambda_{s}^{*}g(X_{1},X_{2})(m\times 1)=s^{2}g(X_{1},X_{2})(m\times 1).

Thus,

\pi_{2,0}g=s^{2}g_{M}.

(2.2)

For any
X\in\pi^{*}TM
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\in\ker\pi
, since

{\lambda_{s}}_{*}\left(s^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)=\frac{\partial}{\partial s},

we have

g\left(X,\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)(m\times s)=\lambda_{s}^{*}g\left(X,s^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)(m\times 1)=sg\left(X,\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)(m\times 1).

(2.3)

Define

\alpha:=\left.\left(\iota_{\frac{\partial}{\partial s}}g\right)\right|_{M\times 1}.

Then, by (
2.3
), we get

g\left(X,\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)(m\times s)=s\alpha(X)=sds\cdot\alpha\left(X,\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right).

Hence,

\pi_{1,1}g=sds\cdot\alpha,

(2.4)

Finally, we have

g\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial s},\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)(m,s)=\lambda_{s}^{*}g\left(s^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial s},s^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)(m,1)=g\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial s},\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)(m,1).

Therefore, we get

\pi_{0,2}g=fds^{2},

(2.5)

where the function

f(m)=g\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial s},\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)(m,1)

is a function on
M
.

Combining (
2.2
), (
2.4
), and (
2.5
), we get

g=s^{2}g_{M}+sds\cdot\alpha+fds^{2}.

By Lemma
2.4
,
g
is positive definite if and only if the condition (
2.1
) holds.
∎

Theorem 2.6
.

Any global selfsimilar manifold
(C,g,\xi)
with a nowhere vanishing homothetic vector field is isomorphic to

\left(\hat{M}=M\times\mathbb{R}^{>0},\hat{g}=s^{2}g_{M}+sds\cdot\alpha+ds^{2},s\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right),

where

s
is a coordinate on
\mathbb{R}^{>0}
,
g_{M}
a Riemannian metric on
M
,
\alpha
a 1-form on
M
, and

g_{M}(X,X)+2\alpha(X)+1>0,\ \ \ \text{for any}\ \ \ X\in TM.

The identification
(C,g,\xi)\simeq\left(\hat{M},\hat{g},s\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)
is defined by

s=\sqrt{g(\xi,\xi_{)}},\ \ \ c\ =\ \left(\gamma_{c}\cap\{r=1\}\right)\times s\ \in\ M\times\mathbb{R}^{>0},\ \ \ g_{M}=g|_{M\times 1},\ \ \ \alpha=\iota_{\xi}g|_{M\times 1},

where
c\in C
and
\gamma_{c}
is the integral curve of
\xi
containing
c
.

Proof.

The vector field
\xi
does not vanish at any point of
C
. The Lie derivative of a metric is defined by

(\mathcal{L}_{X}g)(Y,Z)=\mathcal{L}_{X}(g(Y,Z))-g([X,Y],\ Z)-g(Y,\ [X,Z]).

Therefore,

\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\ (g(\xi,\xi))=(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\ g)(\xi,\xi)=2g(\xi,\xi)>0.

Thus, the function
s:=|\xi|=\sqrt{g(\xi,\xi)}
strictly increases along
\xi
. For any point
p\in C
denote the integral curve of
\xi
containing a point
p
by
\gamma_{p}
. Since
\xi_{p}\neq 0
, the curve
\gamma_{p}
is non-degenerated. Then any integral curve
\gamma_{p}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}
because there exists a strictly increasing function along
\gamma
(
s
strictly increases along
\gamma
). Let
u_{p}
be a coordinate on
\gamma_{p}
such that

\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{p}}=\xi.

Then
s=|\xi|
satisfies

\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{p}}s^{2}=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}(g(\xi,\xi))=2g(\xi,\xi)=2s^{2}.

Therefore,

s=ae^{u_{p}},

where
a\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant. Moreover
a\in\mathbb{R}^{>0}
because
s
is positive defined. Then
s
identifies
\gamma
with
\mathbb{R}^{>0}
and

\xi=\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{p}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\ln(a^{-1}s)}=\left(\frac{\partial\ln(a^{-1}s)}{\partial s}\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}=s\frac{\partial}{\partial s}.

Denote by

M:=\{p\in C|\ s(p)=1\}

the level set which is a smooth submanifold of a codimension
1
. Since for any
p\in C
, the map
s|_{\gamma_{p}}:\gamma_{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{>0}
is an isomorphism,
\gamma_{p}
has an unique intersection with
M
. Define

\alpha:C\to M\times\mathbb{R}^{>0}

by the rule

\alpha(p)=(\gamma_{p}\cap M)\times s(p).

For any
p\in C
the restriction
s|_{\gamma_{p}}
is an isomorphism.
Moreover,

C=\bigcup_{p\in C}\gamma_{p}=\bigcup_{p\in M}\gamma_{p}.

Therefore,
\alpha
is an isomorphism.

Thus, we have
C=M\times\mathbb{R}^{>0}
and
\xi=s\frac{\partial}{\partial s}
, where
s
is a coordinate on
M
. That is,
(M\times\mathbb{R}^{>0},g,s\frac{\partial}{\partial s})
is a selfsimilar Riemannian manifold. Thus,
g
satisfies the conditions of Proposition
2.5
.

∎

The vector field
\xi

\gamma
be a path between the points
p
and

,
l
the length of
\gamma
,
\Phi_{t}
the flow along the field

Proposition 2.8

Let

vanishes at a point

Proof.

Theorem 2.9

Lemma 2.10

Lemma 2.12

Lemma 2.13
.

Let

Definition 2.16
.

We say that

is a selfsimilar manifold with a

if

is locally defined as a gradient of a function. If

on a domain

\iota_{\xi}g|_{U}=df
. Moreover, a form is closed if and only if it is locally exact. Therefore, the field

is potential if and only if

.

Since

0=\frac{\partial b}{\partial s}=w=\frac{-\frac{49s}{62}+\frac{-19s}{62}}{s^{2}}\neq 0.

Therefore, there are not potential homothetic vector fields on
\left(S^{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{>0},g=s^{2}d\varphi^{2}+sds\cdot d\varphi+ds^{2}\right)
.

3
Selfsimilar Hessian manifolds

Definition 3.1
.

A
flat affine manifold

(M,\nabla)
is a differentiable manifold
M
equipped with a flat torsion-free connection
\nabla
. Equivalently, it is a manifold equipped with an atlas such that all translation maps between charts are affine transformations (see e.g.
[
FGH
]
). A
radiant manifold

(C,\nabla,\rho)
is a flat affine manifold
(C,\nabla)
endowed with a
radiant vector field

\rho
i.e. a field satisfying

\nabla\rho=\text{Id}.

(3.1)

Equivalently, it is a manifold equipped with an atlas such that all translation maps between charts are linear transformations. In the corresponding local coordinates we have

\rho=\sum x^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}

(see e.g.
[
Go
]
).

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(M,\nabla)
be a flat affine manifold. If a Riemannian metric
g
is locally expressed by a Hessian of a function

\text{Hess}\ \varphi=\nabla d\varphi

then
g
is called a
Hessian metric
and the triple
(M,\nabla,g)
is called a
Hessian manifold
(see e.g.
[
Sh
]
). More concretely, we have

g(X,Y)=XY(\varphi)-\nabla_{X}Y(\varphi).

Definition 3.3
.

A
selfsimilar Hessian manifold

(C,\nabla,g,\xi)
is a Hessian manifold
(C,\nabla,g)
endowed with a vector field
\xi
such that
(C,g,\xi)
is a selfsimilar manifold and the flow along
\xi
preserves
\nabla
. If
\xi
is complete then
C
is called a
global selfsimilar Hessian manifold
.

Lemma 3.5

Let

g(X,\nabla_{Y}\xi)-g(Y,\nabla_{X}\xi)=0.

Proof.

Combining this with the flatness of

Proposition 3.11

g(\rho,\rho)=\rho^{2}\left(\left(x^{n+1}\right)^{2\lambda^{-1}}\psi\right)-\nabla_{\rho}\rho\left(\left(x^{n+1}\right)^{2\lambda^{-1}}\psi\right).

We have
\nabla_{\rho}=\rho
,

Therefore,

if and only if

\left(4\lambda^{-2}-2\lambda^{-1}\right)\psi>0.

We have

g(X,\rho)=X\rho\left(\left(x^{n+1}\right)^{2\lambda^{-1}}\psi\right)-\nabla_{X}\rho\left(\left(x^{n+1}\right)^{2\lambda^{-1}}\psi\right)=X\rho\left(\left(x^{n+1}\right)^{2\lambda^{-1}}\psi\right)-X\left(\left(x^{n+1}\right)^{2\lambda^{-1}}\psi\right).

(3.7)

Since
\rho(\psi)=0
and

, we have

\rho\left(\left(x^{n+1}\right)^{2\lambda^{-1}}\psi\right)=\psi\rho\left(\left(x^{n+1}\right)^{2\lambda^{-1}}\right)=\psi\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\left(\left(x^{n+1}\right)^{2\lambda^{-1}}\psi\right)=2\lambda^{-1}\left(x^{n+1}\right)^{2\lambda^{-1}}\psi.

Combining this with
3.7
, we get

g(X,\rho)=2X\left(\lambda^{-1}\left(x^{n+1}\right)^{2\lambda^{-1}}\psi\right)-X\left(\left(x^{n+1}\right)^{2\lambda^{-1}}\psi\right)=\left(2\lambda^{-1}-1\right)\left(x^{n+1}\right)^{2\lambda^{-1}}X\left(\psi\right).

Therefore,

g(X+\rho,X+\rho)=g(X,X)+2g(X,\rho)+g(\rho,\rho)=

=\left(x^{n+1}\right)^{2\lambda^{-1}}\left(X^{2}+\left(4\lambda^{-1}-2\right)X+4\lambda^{-2}-2\lambda^{-1}\right)(\psi)>0.

Since

, we have
g(X+\rho,X+\rho)>0
if and only if

Thus,
g

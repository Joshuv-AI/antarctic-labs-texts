The Chow ring of the stack of plane nodal curves
Alessio Cela, Ajith Urundolil Kumaran, Xiaohan Yan

Abstract
We compute the rational Chow ring of the moduli stacks of planar smooth and at worst nodal curves of fixed degree and express it in terms of tautological classes. Along the way, we extend Vial’s results on Chow groups of Brauer-Severi varieties to G G -equivariant settings.

1.1
Results and methods

Denote by
c_{i}\ (i=1,2,3)
the Chern classes of the tautological bundle over
\mathrm{BGL}_{3}
. The stack
\mathrm{BGL}_{3}
arises naturally in our construction (see Lemma

\mathrm{CH}^{*}(\mathsf{PNC}_{d})
and
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(\mathsf{PSC}_{d})
are algebras over
\mathrm{CH}^{*}(\mathrm{BGL}_{3})

Theorem 1
.

\mathrm{CH}^{*}(\mathsf{PNC}_{d})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Q}[c_{1},c_{2}]&\quad d=1,2,\\
\mathbb{Q}[c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}]/(c_{1}^{2},c_{1}c_{2},c_{1}c_{3})&\quad d=3,\\
\mathbb{Q}[c_{1}]/(c_{1}^{4})&\quad d\geq 4.\end{cases}

Theorem 2
.

\mathrm{CH}^{*}(\mathsf{PSC}_{d})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Q}[c_{1},c_{2}]&\quad d=1,\\
\mathbb{Q}[c_{2}]&\quad d=2,\\
\mathbb{Q}&\quad d\geq 3.\end{cases}

Preliminaries about
\mathsf{PNC}_{d}

\mathsf{PSC}_{d}

In this section, we prepare ourselves by identifying the rational Chow rings of our moduli stacks
\mathsf{PSC}_{d}
and
\mathsf{PNC}_{d}

3.1
Description as quotient stacks

The next proposition provides a presentation for
\mathsf{PSC}_{d}
and
\mathsf{PNC}_{d}
. Recall that we denote by
V
the standard representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
, so
\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(V^{\vee})
is the affine space of degree-
d
homogeneous polynomials on
V=\mathbb{C}^{3}
, equipped with the natural
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
-action.

Proposition 4
.

(i)

\mathsf{PSC}_{d}
is naturally isomorphic to the quotient stack

\Bigg[\frac{\mathbb{P}\big(\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(V^{\vee})\big)\smallsetminus\Delta_{\mathrm{sm}}}{\mathrm{PGL}_{3}}\Bigg]

where
\Delta_{\mathrm{sm}}
is the (projective) locus of polynomials defining singular curves on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

(ii)

\mathsf{PNC}_{d}
is naturally isomorphic to the quotient stack

\Bigg[\frac{\mathbb{P}\big(\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(V^{\vee})\big)\smallsetminus\Delta_{\mathrm{node}}}{\mathrm{PGL}_{3}}\Bigg]

where
\Delta_{\mathrm{node}}
is the (projective) locus of polynomials defining curves on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with at least one worse-than-nodal singularity.

Proof.

We prove
(ii)
, the proof of
(i)
is similar. We have a morphism

\mathbb{P}\big(\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(V^{\vee})\big)\smallsetminus\Delta_{\mathrm{node}}\to\mathsf{PNC}_{d}

given by associating to
[F:S\to\mathbb{P}\big(\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(V^{\vee})\big)\smallsetminus\Delta_{\mathrm{node}}]
the nodal curve
C=V(F)\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2}_{S}
over
S
, where
V(F)
denotes the zero locus of
F
. For any object
(C\to\mathsf{P}\to S)
in
\mathsf{PNC}_{d}
, we can find an étale covering
S^{\prime}\to S
such that there exists an isomorphism on
S^{\prime}
between the pullback to
S^{\prime}
of
(C\to\mathsf{P}\to S)
and an object of the form
(C^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}_{S^{\prime}}\to S^{\prime})
. Any two of such isomorphisms differ by an element of
\mathrm{PGL}_{3}(S^{\prime})
, so the morphism at the beginning of this proof is a
\mathrm{PGL}_{3}
-torsor. The proposition follows from here.

∎

We work over the base field
\mathbb{C}
, and by

\mathbb{C}
-scheme of finite type.

Let

be a fixed non-negative integer. Throughout, let

denote a flat, projective and surjective morphism from a quasi-projective variety
X

to a quasi-projective variety

.

, Theorem 3.2]

Let

and

In the above,

The primary example to keep in mind for the purposes of this paper is when

.

is
G
-equivariant with a
G
-linearized relatively ample line bundle

.
This means that there exists a
G
-linearized vector bundle

on

such that

factors as

where

is a
G
-equivariant closed embedding and

is the projective bundle map with

. Assume further that the base
B
is a quasi-projective variety equipped with a
G
-linearized ample line bundle.

Consider a
G
-representation

with

such that there exists an open subset

on which

. We denote by

,

We may choose

.

This is classical. We supply the reader with the references.

First, by
[

, we can assume

Appendix by Alexis Aumonier: on the cycle map

Using the notation of Sections

\displaystyle\mathrm{CH}^{*}(\mathsf{PNC}_{d})\cong\mathrm{CH}^{*}_{\mathrm{GL}_{3}}(\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(V^{\vee})\smallsetminus\widetilde{\Delta}_{\mathrm{node}})

from equivariant Chow groups to (Borel) equivariant cohomology. In this appendix, we explain how to apply the results of

]

Let us first consider the case of smooth plane curves. In

The main advantage of the continuous section space resides in its amenability to techniques of homotopy theory. In particular, its rational cohomology is easy to compute:

\mathcal{P}^{2}(\mathcal{O}(d))\smallsetminus\mathfrak{T}_{\mathrm{node}}
. By construction, this bundle is homotopy equivalent to the fibrewise join

S(\mathcal{P}^{1}(\mathcal{O}(d)))\ \ast_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\ (\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})\otimes\mathcal{O}(d)\smallsetminus\mathbb{V})

(20)

where
S(-)
denotes the sphere bundle. Let
x\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
be a point, write
D_{x}=(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{2}})\otimes\mathcal{O}(d)\smallsetminus\mathbb{V})|_{x}
for the fibre of the second factor above
x
, and notice that the fibre of the first factor is the
5
-sphere
S^{5}\cong S(\mathbb{C}^{3})\cong S(\mathcal{P}^{1}(\mathcal{O}(d)))|_{x}
. Using that joining with an
n
-sphere amounts up to homotopy to suspending
n+1
times, we obtain that the fibre of (
20
) is homotopy equivalent to

S^{5}\ast D_{x}\simeq\Sigma^{6}D_{x}.

By
[
FHT01
, Proposition 13.9]
, the suspension
\Sigma^{6}D_{x}
is rationally equivalent to wedge of Moore spaces. A computation, see eg.
[
Dam16
, Table 2, page 808]
, shows that
H^{*}(D_{x};\mathbb{Q})\cong\Lambda(x_{1})
, hence
D_{x}
is rationally a Moore space, and so is its suspension. We conclude that
\Sigma^{6}D_{x}
is rationally equivalent to the Moore space
M(\mathbb{Q},7)
, or equivalently the Eilenberg–MacLane space
K(\mathbb{Q},7)
. In fact, using the complex orientation of the complex vector bundle
\mathcal{P}^{2}(\mathcal{O}(d))
, one deduces that the monodromy action is trivial on the rational cohomology of the fibre of
\mathcal{P}^{2}(\mathcal{O}(d))\smallsetminus\mathfrak{T}_{\mathrm{node}}
. Therefore, its fibrewise rationalisation is a principal
K(\mathbb{Q},7)
-bundle over
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. By definition, such a bundle is pulled back from the universal bundle over
K(\mathbb{Q},8)
, hence classified by the represented cohomology class (usually called the Euler class, see
[
FHT01
, Example 4, page 202]
). This cohomology class is in degree
8
, hence must vanish on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, thus showing that the fibrewise rationalised bundle is trivial.

Now, the rational cohomology of the continuous section space is isomorphic to the cohomology of the section space of the fibrewise rationalised bundle, see eg.
[
Mol87
, Theorem 5.3]
. Using the triviality established above, we obtain rational equivalences:

\Gamma_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{P}^{2}(\mathcal{O}(d))\smallsetminus\mathfrak{T}_{\mathrm{node}})_{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq\mathrm{Map}(\mathbb{P}^{2},K(\mathbb{Q},7))\simeq K(\mathbb{Q},3)\times K(\mathbb{Q},5)\times K(\mathbb{Q},7).

For the last equivalence, see
[
Hae82
]
for example. The result then follows from the computation of the cohomology of the right hand side.
∎

We now turn our attention to the
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
-equivariant cohomology and the cycle class map. The following is the main result of this appendix.

Theorem 22
.

Let
d\geq 6
. Then the cycle class map

\mathrm{CH}^{*}_{\mathrm{GL}_{3}}(\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(V^{\vee})\smallsetminus\widetilde{\Delta}_{\mathrm{node}})\to H^{*}_{\mathrm{GL}_{3}}(\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(V^{\vee})\smallsetminus\widetilde{\Delta}_{\mathrm{node}})

is injective. Furthermore, it is an isomorphism in the range of degrees
*<d
.

Remark 23
.

When
d=4
, the cohomology groups of
\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(V^{\vee})\smallsetminus\widetilde{\Delta}_{\mathrm{node}}
are completely given by
[
BGK14
, Theorem 2]
. The proof below then shows that the cycle class map is an isomorphism in this case.

Remark 24
.

It is very plausible that the result also holds when
d=5
as the bound of Theorem
20
is perhaps not optimal. In fact, one could wonder if the cycle class map is an isomorphism also for
d\geq 5
. However we do not have any evidence for this stronger claim, nor a reliable strategy to prove or disprove it.

Proof.

In
[
Kon22
, Theorem 2]
Konovalov shows that for
d\geq 4
there is an isomorphism of rational cohomology rings

H^{*}(\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(V^{\vee})\smallsetminus\widetilde{\Delta}_{\mathrm{node}})\cong H^{*}(\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{C}))\otimes H^{*}_{\mathrm{SL}_{3}}(\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(V^{\vee})\smallsetminus\widetilde{\Delta}_{\mathrm{node}}).

Combined with Corollary
21
and the computation
H^{*}(\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{C}))\cong\Lambda(x_{3},x_{5})
(see eg.
[
Dam16
, Table 1, page 807]
), we obtain an isomorphism of graded rings

H^{*}_{\mathrm{SL}_{3}}(\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(V^{\vee})\smallsetminus\widetilde{\Delta}_{\mathrm{node}})\cong\Lambda(x_{7})

(21)

in degrees
*<d
. In particular the reduced cohomology vanishes in degrees
*\leq 5
when
d\geq 6
. From the short exact sequence of groups

0\to\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{C})\to\mathrm{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{C})\overset{\mathrm{det}}{\to}\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to 0

we obtain a fibration

(\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(V^{\vee})\smallsetminus\widetilde{\Delta}_{\mathrm{node}})\sslash\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{C})\to(\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(V^{\vee})\smallsetminus\widetilde{\Delta}_{\mathrm{node}})\sslash\mathrm{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{C})\to B\mathbb{C}^{\times},

where
-\sslash-
denotes the Borel construction. The Serre spectral sequence associated to this fibration has signature

E^{p,q}_{2}=H^{p}(B\mathbb{C}^{\times})\otimes H^{q}_{\mathrm{SL}_{3}}(\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(V^{\vee})\smallsetminus\widetilde{\Delta}_{\mathrm{node}})\implies H^{p+q}_{\mathrm{GL}_{3}}(\operatorname{Sym}^{d}(V^{\vee})\smallsetminus\widetilde{\Delta}_{\mathrm{node}}).

By the vanishing result above, there is no room for differentials hitting the
0
th row in degrees
*\leq 6
in the Serre spectral sequence. The classes there survive and are therefore non-zero in the cohomology of the total space. But these classes are exactly
1,c_{1},c_{1}^{2}
and
c_{1}^{3}
. The injectivity part of the theorem then follows from Theorem
1
and the fact that the cycle class map is a morphism of
H^{*}(\mathrm{BGL}_{3}(\mathbb{C}))
-algebras. In fact, more is true by this linearity: as
c_{1}^{4}
vanishes in the Chow ring it has to vanish in cohomology as well. This means that the differential
d_{8}\colon E^{0,7}_{8}\to E^{8,0}_{8}=E^{8,0}_{2}\cong\mathbb{Q}\cdot c_{1}^{4}
is surjective. Combined with the Leibniz rule, this shows that all the differentials
d_{8}\colon E^{*,7}_{8}\to E^{8+*,0}_{8}
from the seventh row are surjective. Using the computation (
21
), one sees that these differentials are isomorphisms when
d>7
and are the only differentials between groups in total degree
*<d
as long as
d\geq 6
. This proves the second claim.
∎

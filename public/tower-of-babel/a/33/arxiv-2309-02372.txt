Ascent and descent of Gorenstein homological properties
Jian Liu, Wei Ren

Abstract
. Let φ : R → A \varphi\colon R\rightarrow A be a ring homomorphism, where R R is a commutative noetherian ring and A A is a finite R R -algebra. We provide criteria for detecting the ascent and descent of Gorenstein homological properties. We observe that the ascent and descent of Gorenstein homological property can detect the Gorenstein properties of rings along φ \varphi . Finally, we describe when φ \varphi induces a triangle equivalence between the stable categories of finitely generated Gorenstein projective modules over R R and A A .

1.
Introduction

The study of the Gorenstein homological algebra can be traced back to the 1960s. Auslander and Bridger
[
1
]
introduced the notion of the Gorenstein projective modules under the name “totally reflexive modules”, and they generalized the well-known Auslander-Buchsbaum formula to modules of finite Gorenstein dimensions. For an Iwanaga-Gorenstein ring, Buchweitz
[
10
]
established a triangle equivalence between the stable category of finitely generated Gorenstein projective modules and the singularity category; this celebrated theorem highlighted the theory of the Gorenstein homological algebra.

As stated in
[
5
]
, a systematic emphasis on the study of morphisms was an innovative aspect of Grothendieck’s approach to algebraic geometry and commutative algebra. For a surjective ring homomorphism
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow A
of commutative noetherian local rings, if the kernel of
\varphi
is generated by a regular sequence, then it is well-known that
A
is Cohen-Macaulay (resp. Gorenstein, complete intersection) if and only if so is
R
.
Moreover, such ascent and descent of ring properties along a ring homomorphism can be determined by certain homological properties; see for example
[
4
,
6
,
19
]
.

Let
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow A
be a ring homomorphism. We say
\varphi
has
ascent and descent of Gorenstein projective property
if each finitely generated left or right
A
-module is Gorenstein projective if and only if the underlying
R
-module is Gorenstein projective. An interesting example is due to Buchweitz
[
10
, 8.2]
; he observed that for the integral group ring extension
\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}G
of a finite group
G
, a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}G
-module (or equivalently, an integral representation of
G
) is Gorenstein projective if and only if the underlying
\mathbb{Z}
-module is Gorenstein projective (or equivalently,
\mathbb{Z}
-free).

Notice that the above
\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}G
is a classical example of Forbenius extension
[
27
]
. Inspired by this fact, Chen
[
11
]
introduced the totally reflexive extension of rings and proved that such extension has ascent and descent of Gorenstein projective property. This motivates the subsequent works of Ren
[
32
]
and Zhao
[
34
]
on the ascent and descent of Gorenstein projective property for (not necessarily finitely generated) modules along Frobenius extension of rings.

However, there are ring homomorphisms that satisfy the ascent and descent of Gorenstein projective property, but may not be a Frobenius extension of rings (see Examples
4.8
and
4.9
). Inspired by the aforementioned facts, it is natural to ask: how a ring homomorphism might behave if it has ascent and descent of Gorenstein projective properties?

To study the ascent and descent of Gorenstein projective property, we first investigate in Section
3
the ring homomorphism
\varphi
which has
ascent and descent of finite Gorenstein dimension property
. The main result in this section is a characterization of this property; see Theorem
3.12
.
Then, in Section
4
, we get the following result which concerns our question.

Theorem 1.1
.

(See
4.3
)
Let
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow A
be a ring homomorphism, where
R
is a commutative noetherian ring and
A
is a finite
R
-algebra.
The following two conditions are equivalent
\colon

(1)

A
is Gorenstein projective over
R
and
\Hom_{R}(A,R)
is projective over
A
on both sides.

(2)

\varphi
has ascent and descent of Gorenstein projective property.

Recall that
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow A
is a Frobenius extension provided that
A
is a finitely generated projective
R
-module and
\Hom_{R}(A,R)\cong A
as
R
-

-bimodules; this condition is sufficient but not necessary for

having ascent and descent of Gorenstein projective property. It is of interest to compare the condition (1) in the above with the condition of Frobenius extension.

By making use of Theorem
1.1
, we show that the ascent and descent of Gorenstein projective property is a local property, i.e.
\varphi
has ascent and descent of Gorenstein projective property if and only if
\varphi_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon R_{\mathfrak{p}}\rightarrow A_{\mathfrak{p}}
does so for each prime ideal (equivalently, maximal ideal)

of

; see Corollary

.

In Section

, we study how the Gorenstein properties of rings behave along the ring homomorphism
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow A
. The main result in this section is Theorem

. By combining Theorem

with Theorem

, we can reobtain a result of Avramov and Foxby; see Corollary

Theorem 1.2
.

(See

)

For a noetherian ring

.
In Section

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout
A
will be a two-sided noetherian ring, that is,
A
is noetherian as a left and as a right
A
-module. In what follows
A
-modules will mean left

-modules, and

-modules are identified with right

2.1
.

Gorenstein projective modules.
An unbounded acyclic complex of projective left
A
-modules

\mathbf{P}=\cdots\longrightarrow P_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}P_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}P_{-1}\longrightarrow\cdots

is called
totally acyclic
provided that
\Hom_{A}(\mathbf{P},Q)
is still acyclic for any projective left
A
-module
Q
. A left
A
-module
M
is
Gorenstein projective
if there is a totally acyclic complex
\mathbf{P}
such that
M
is isomorphic to the image of
\partial_{0}
. Any projective
A
-module is Gorenstein projective. For each totally acyclic complex
\mathbf{P}
, the image of
\partial_{i}
, denoted by
{\rm Im}(\partial_{i})
, is Gorenstein projective for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The following characterization is well-known; see
[
1
, Proposition 3.8]
. Let
M
be a finitely generated left
A
-module. Then
M
is Gorenstein projective if and only if
\Ext^{i}_{A}(M,A)=0=\Ext^{i}_{A^{\rm op}}(\Hom_{R}(M,A),A)=0
for all
i>0
, and the evaluation homomorphism
{\rm e}_{M,A}\colon M\rightarrow\Hom_{A^{\rm op}}(\Hom_{A}(M,A),A)
is an isomorphism. Thus, the finitely generated Gorenstein projective module
M
is also called a
totally reflexive module
; see for example
[
1
]
.

2.2
.

Derived categories.
Let
\mathsf{D}(A)
denote the derived category of complexes of left
R
-modules. It is a triangulated category with the suspension functor
[1]
; for each complex

A complex

(resp.

(resp.

Let

denote the right derived functor of

. For each

in
\mathsf{D}(A)
, the complex

can be represented by either
\Hom_{A}(P,N)
or
\Hom_{A}(M,I)
, where

is a homotopy injective resolution.

Let

denote the left derived functor of

. For each

in
\mathsf{D}(A^{\rm op})
and

in
\mathsf{D}(A)
, the complex

can be represented by either

or

, where

and

are homotopy projective resolutions over

and

, respectively.

2.3
.

Gorenstein dimensions.
Let
M
be a complex in
\mathsf{D}^{f}_{b}(A)
. A quasi-isomorphism
G\xrightarrow{\simeq}M
is a
Gorenstein projective resolution
of
M
provided that
G_{i}
is a Gorenstein projective module for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and
G_{i}=0
for
i\ll 0
. The
Gorenstein dimension
of
M
, denoted by
\Gdim_{A}(M)
, is the smallest integer
n
such that there is a Gorenstein projective resolution
G\xrightarrow{\simeq}M
such that
G_{i}=0
for
i>n
and
G_{n}\neq 0
; see for example
[
13
, Definition 2.3.2]
.

For Gorenstein dimension of modules, we consider any module as a stalk complex concentrated in degree zero. Then, for each
A
-module

,
\Gdim_{A}(M)
is precisely the Gorenstein projective dimension of
M
; see for example
[
22
, Definition 2.8]
. For each
M\in A\mbox{-mod}
, it is clear that
\Gdim_{A}(M)\leq\pd_{A}(M)
, where
\pd_{A}(M)
is the projective dimension of
M
over
A
; the equality holds if the latter is finite.

2.4
.

Iwanaga-Gorenstein rings.

A noetherian ring
A
is said to be
Iwanaga-Gorenstein
provided that
A
has finite injective dimension as both a left module and a right module. It follows from
[
33
, Lemma A]
that if

is Iwanaga-Gorenstein, then

\id_{A}(_{A}A)=\id_{A}(A_{A})<\infty
. In this case, we simply write this as

.

Let
A
be an Iwanaga-Gorenstein ring. For each finitely generated left
A
-module
M
,
\Gdim_{A}(M)\leq\id_{A}(A)
; see
[
16
, Proposition 11.5.7]
.
In this case, each complex in
\mathsf{D}^{f}_{b}(A)
has finite Gorenstein dimension.

2.5
.

Perfect complexes.
Let
A
be a left noetherian ring. A complex
X
in
\mathsf{D}(A)
is said to be
perfect
provided that it is isomorphic in
\mathsf{D}(A)
to a bounded complex of finitely generated projective
A
-modules; equivalently,
X
is compact as an object in
\mathsf{D}(A)
. See
[
10
, Chapter 1]
and
[
28
]
for more details about perfect complexes.

The full subcategory of
\mathsf{D}(A)
consisting of perfect complexes is precisely the smallest triangulated subcategory of
\mathsf{D}(A)
which contains
A
and is closed under direct summands.

2.6
.

Semi-dualizing complexes.
Let
D
be complex of
A\mbox{-}A
bimodules.
D
is a
semi-dualizing complex
over
A
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1) The total homology
\h(D)
is finitely generated over
A
and

;

(2)

for

and each

is injective over

and

;

(3) The homothety morphisms

{\rm m}_{D}\colon A\rightarrow\Hom_{A^{\rm op}}(D,D);a\mapsto(x\mapsto ax)

and

{\rm m}^{\prime}_{D}\colon A^{\rm{op}}\rightarrow\Hom_{A}(D,D);a\mapsto(x\mapsto xa)

are quasi-isomorphisms. If, in addition,

is a bounded complex of injective modules over

, then

When

is commutative, the above definition of the semi-dualizing complex coincides with the definition in
[
14

(2) By the adjunction, there is an isomorphism

Consider the following right

∎

Example 3.6

Lemma 3.10

Remark 3.13
.

Keep the assumption as Theorem

. It is natural to ask whether (

The condition (

Remark 3.15

yields the following well-known result; see

4.
Ascent and descent of Gorenstein projective properties

In this section, we give a characterization of the ascent and descent of Gorenstein projective property; see Theorem
4.3
. This characterization yields that the ascent and descent of Gorenstein projective property is a local property;
see Corollary
4.6
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow A
be a ring homomorphism between noetherian rings.
We say
\varphi
has
ascent and descent of Gorenstein projective property
if the following two conditions are satisfied
\colon

(1)

For each finitely generated left
A
-module, it is Gorenstein projective over
A
if and only if it is Gorenstein projective over
R
;

(2)

For each finitely generated right
A
-module, it is Gorenstein projective over
A^{\rm op}
if and only if it is Gorenstein projective over
R^{\rm op}
.

The following is known; see
[
13
, Corollary 2.3.8]
for the commutative case. We give a new argument here by using Lemma
3.11
; compare with
[
24
, Lemma 6.2]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
A
be a noetherian ring. If
M
is a finitely generated left
A
-module with
\Gdim_{A}(M)=n<\infty
and
\Ext^{i}_{A}(M,A)=0
for all
i>0
, then
M
is Gorenstein projective.

Proof.

We abbreviate
\RHom_{A}(-,A)
and
\RHom_{A^{\rm op}}(-,A)
as
(-)^{\ast}
. Applying the functor
\RHom_{A^{\rm op}}(\RHom_{A}(-,A),A)
to the exact triangle in Lemma
3.11
, one has an exact triangle

K^{\ast\ast}[n-1]\longrightarrow P^{\ast\ast}\longrightarrow M^{\ast\ast}=\RHom_{A^{\rm op}}(M^{\ast},A)\longrightarrow K^{\ast\ast}[n]

in
\mathsf{D}(\mathbb{Z})
. Note that
K^{\ast\ast}\simeq K
and
P^{\ast\ast}\simeq P
. Comparing with the exact triangle in Lemma
3.11
, we have
M\xrightarrow{\cong}M^{\ast\ast}
and
\Ext^{i}_{A^{\rm op}}(M^{\ast},A)=0
. Thus,
M
is Gorenstein projective over
A
.
∎

Let
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow A

is a finite

\varphi

Now assume
\Gdim_{R}(M)=0

(2)\Rightarrow(1)

. We infer that the sequence is split from

\Ext^{1}_{A}(C,\Hom_{R}(A,R))\cong\Ext^{1}_{R}(C,R)=0;

the isomorphism here follows from
\RHom_{R}(A,R)\simeq\Hom_{R}(A,R)
and the adjunction
({\rm Res},\RHom_{R}(A,-))
.
Hence,
\Hom_{R}(A,R)
is a projective left
A
-module. The same argument shows that
\Hom_{R}(A,R)
is projective as a right
A
-module.
This completes the proof.
∎

From the above result and
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
in Theorem

, we get a natural observation: if
\varphi
has ascent and descent of Gorenstein projective property, then
\varphi
has ascent and descent of finite Gorenstein dimension property.

The following is immediate from
[

, Theorem 3.3.7]
and Theorem

.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\varphi\colon(R,\mathfrak{m})\rightarrow(A,\mathfrak{n})
be a finite local homomorphism of Gorenstein local rings with the same Krull dimension. Then
\varphi
has ascent and descent of Gorenstein projective property.

The following is clear, which can be proved by using the characterization of Gorenstein projective modules; see
2.1
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring and
A
a finite
R
-algebra. For any finitely generated left
A
-module

, the following are equivalent
\colon

(1)

is Gorenstein projective over
A
.

(2)

M_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Gorenstein projective over

for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of

.

(3)

is Gorenstein projective over

for each maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of

.

If we replace “Gorenstein projective” in Lemma

with “projective”, the above statement still holds.
Combining this with Theorem
4.3
and Lemma

, we obtain the following which shows that the ascent and descent of Gorenstein projective property is a local property; compare this with the question in Remark

.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow A
be a ring homomorphism, where

is a commutative noetherian ring and

is a finite

(1)

\varphi\colon R\rightarrow A

(2)

\varphi_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon R_{\mathfrak{p}}\rightarrow A_{\mathfrak{p}}

\mathfrak{p}
of

(3)

\varphi_{\mathfrak{m}}\colon R_{\mathfrak{m}}\rightarrow A_{\mathfrak{m}}

of

.

The notion of Frobenius extension of rings is a generalization of Frobenius algebra
[
27
]
, which includes many interesting examples; see for example
[
26
]
. A ring extension
S\subseteq A
is called a
Frobenius extension
if the following equivalent conditions hold:

(1)

is finitely generated projective as a left
S
-module and there is an isomorphism
{}_{A}A_{S}\cong\Hom_{S}(_{S}A_{A},S)
as
A\mbox{-}S
bimodules.

(2)
A
is finitely generated projective as a right
S
-module and there is an isomorphism
{}_{S}A_{A}\cong\Hom_{S^{\rm op}}(_{A}A_{S},S)
as
S\mbox{-}A
bimodules.

Using Theorem
4.3
, one can immediately get the following. Indeed, a strong result that any Frobenius extension has ascent and descent of Gorenstein projective property was established in
[

,
32
,
34
]
. However, as shown in Example

, the converse does not hold in general.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow A
be a ring extension, where
R
is a commutative noetherian ring and
A
is a finite
R
-algebra. If it is a Frobenius extension, then
\varphi
has ascent and descent of Gorenstein projective property.

The next example shows that the converse of Corollary

is not true.

Example 4.8
.

Consider the injection map which maps

to
x^{2}
:

R=k\llbracket t\rrbracket/(t^{2})\hookrightarrow A=k\llbracket x,y,z\rrbracket/(x^{2}-y^{2},x^{2}-z^{2},xy,xz,yz).

Both

and

are Gorenstein local noetherian rings; see
[

, Example 3.2.11]
. This map has ascent and descent of Gorenstein projective property, but it is not a Frobenius extension. Since

and
\dim_{k}(A)=5
,

cannot be free over

. This implies that

is not projective over

as

is local.

We end this section with an example that has ascent and descent of Gorenstein projective property, but that is not a Frobenius extension, not a ring homomorphism between Iwanaga-Gorenstein rings, and not a complete intersection map.

Example 4.9
.

Let

be a commutative noetherian ring which is not Iwanaga-Gorenstein. Consider the canonical surjection

One can check directly that

5.
Testing Iwanaga-Gorenstein rings

The main result of this section is Theorem
5.1
. As a consequence, one can reobtain a result of Avramov and Foxby; see Corollary
5.2
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow A
be a ring homomorphism, where
R
is a commutative noetherian ring of finite Krull dimension and
A
is a finite
R
-algebra. Assume
\varphi
has ascent and descent of finite Gorenstein dimension property. If
R
is an Iwanaga-Gorenstein ring, then so is
A
. The converse holds if, in addition, the fibre
A\otimes_{R}R/\mathfrak{m}
is nonzero for each maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
.

Proof.

Let
R
be an Iwanaga-Gorenstein ring. Take a minimal injective resolution
R\xrightarrow{\simeq}I
over
R
.
Note that for each injective
R
-module
E
,
\Hom_{R}(A,E)
is injective both as a left
A
-module and as a right
A
-module. Combining with
\id_{R}(R)<\infty
, then
D\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Hom_{R}(A,I)
is a bounded complex of
A\mbox{-}A
bimodules and each term
D_{i}
is injective over
A
and
A^{\rm op}
.

Since
\varphi
has ascent and descent of finite Gorenstein dimension property, Theorem
3.12
yields that
D
is perfect over
A
on both sides. Then one can choose a projective resolution
\pi\colon Q\xrightarrow{\simeq}D
over
A^{\rm op}
such that
Q
is a bounded complex of finitely generated projective right
A
-modules.

For any finitely generated projective right
A
-module
P
,
\Hom_{A^{\rm op}}(P,A)
is a projective left
A
-module.
For any
A\mbox{-}A
bimodule
E
, if
E
is injective as a left
A
-module, then it follows from
[
16
, Theorem 3.2.16]
that as a left
A
-module,
\Hom_{A^{\rm op}}(P,E)\cong E\otimes_{A}\Hom_{A^{\rm op}}(P,A)
is also injective.
Hence,
\Hom_{A^{\rm op}}(Q,D)
is a bounded complex of injective left
A
-modules.

Consider the left
A
-linear morphisms

\displaystyle A\xrightarrow{\simeq}\Hom_{A^{\rm op}}(D,D)\xrightarrow{\pi^{\ast}}\Hom_{A^{\rm op}}(Q,D),

where the first quasi-isomorphism is from Proposition
3.5
. Since
D
is homotopy injective,
\pi^{\ast}
is also a quasi-isomorphism. Then, we conclude
\id_{A}(_{A}A)<\infty
. The same argument will show
\id_{A}(A_{A})<\infty
. Therefore,
A
is an Iwanaga-Gorenstien ring.

Conversely, we assume
A
is an Iwanaga-Gorenstein ring and
A\otimes_{R}R/\mathfrak{m}\neq 0
for each maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
. For each
i
and each left
A
-module
M
, there is an isomorphism

\Ext^{i}_{R}(M,R)\cong\Ext^{i}_{A}(M,D).

Note that
D
is perfect over
A
on both sides by Theorem
3.12
. Combining this with
\id_{A}(A)<\infty
, we conclude that there exists an positive integer
j
such that
\Ext^{>j}_{R}(M,R)=0
for all
M\in A\mbox{-Mod}
. For each maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
, by assumption
A\otimes_{R}R/\mathfrak{m}
is not zero, and then is a direct sum of some copies of
R/\mathfrak{m}
over
R
. If we choose
M
to be
A\otimes_{R}R/\mathfrak{m}
, then
\Ext^{>j}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m},R)=0
.
In particular, we have

\Ext^{>j}_{R_{\mathfrak{m}}}(R_{\mathfrak{m}}/\mathfrak{m}R_{\mathfrak{m}},R_{\mathfrak{m}})=0.

This implies that
\id_{R_{\mathfrak{m}}}(R_{\mathfrak{m}})\leq j
; see
[
9
, Proposition 3.1.14]
. Since
\mathfrak{m}
is arbitrary,
\id_{R}(R)\leq j
.
Hence,
R
is an Iwanaga-Gorenstein ring. This completes the proof.
∎

Following
[
9
, Definition 3.1.18]
, a commutative noetherian ring
A
is said to be
Gorenstein
provided that
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Iwanaga-Gorenstein for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
A
. A commutative Gorenstein ring need not be Iwanaga-Gorenstein. However, a commutative Gorenstein ring with finite Krull dimension must be Iwanaga-Gorenstein; see for example
[
10
, Theorem 4.1.1]
.

As a consequence of Theorem
3.12
and Theorem
5.1
, one can get the following result due to Avramov and Foxby
[
4
, 4.4.4 and 7.7.2]
; see also
[
25
, Theorem 6.2]
.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\varphi\colon(R,\mathfrak{m},k)\rightarrow(A,\mathfrak{n},l)
be a finite local ring homomorphism. If
A
is Gorenstein, then

R
is Gorenstein if and only if
\Gdim_{R}(A)
is finite.

Proof.

The “only if” part is clear since
R
is a Gorenstein ring and
A
is a finitely generated
R
-module.

For the “if” part, assume
\Gdim_{R}(A)
is finite. It follows from Lemma
3.2
that there is an
A
-linear quasi-isomorphism

A\xrightarrow{\simeq}\RHom_{R}(\RHom_{R}(A,R),R).

This yields the following:

\displaystyle\RHom_{A}(l,A)

\displaystyle\simeq\RHom_{A}(l,\RHom_{R}(\RHom_{R}(A,R),R))

\displaystyle\simeq\RHom_{R}(l\otimes_{A}^{\rm L}\RHom_{R}(A,R),R).

Since
\Gdim_{R}(A)<\infty
,
\RHom_{R}(A,R)
is in
\mathsf{D}^{f}_{b}(R)
. This implies that
\RHom_{R}(A,R)
is in
\mathsf{D}^{f}_{b}(A)
. Thus, there is a minimal resolution
F\xrightarrow{\simeq}\RHom_{R}(A,R)
, that is,
F
is a bounded below complex of finitely generated free left
A
-modules and
\partial(F)\subseteq lF
; see
[
18
, (2.3.c)]
for the existence of minimal free resolutions. Then

l\otimes_{A}^{\rm L}\RHom_{R}(A,R)\simeq l\otimes_{A}F=\coprod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}l^{\beta_{i}}[i],

where
\beta_{i}=\rank_{A}(F_{i})
.
Since
A
is Gorenstein,
\RHom_{A}(l,A)\simeq l[-d]
, where
d
is the Krull dimension of
A
. We conclude by the above that

\RHom_{R}(\coprod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}l^{\beta_{i}}[i],R)\simeq l[-d].

Then, there exists an index
j
such that
\beta_{j}=1
and
\beta_{i}=0
for
i\neq j
; if not, the homology of
\RHom_{R}(\coprod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}l^{\beta_{i}}[i],R)
will not concentrate on only one degree. Thus,
\RHom_{R}(A,R)\simeq A[j]
. In particular,
\RHom_{R}(A,R)
is perfect over
A
. Combining this with the assumption that
\Gdim_{R}(A)
is finite, Theorem
3.12
yields that
\varphi
has ascent and descent of finite Gorenstein dimension property. Thus,
R
is Gorenstein by Theorem
5.1
.
∎

6.
Triangle equivalence of stable categories

In this section, we study when the stable categories of finitely generated Gorenstein projective modules along a ring homomorphism are equivalent; see Theorem
6.4
. To this end, we need some preparations.

Let
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow A
be a ring homomorphism. In this section, we consider
A
as an
A
-
R
-bimodule, and
\omega=\Hom_{R}(A,R)
as an
R
-
A
bimodule. There is an adjoint pair

F=\omega\otimes_{A}-\colon A\mbox{-mod}\rightleftarrows R\mbox{-mod}\colon G=\Hom_{R}(\omega,-).

We denote the unit by
\eta\colon{\rm Id}\rightarrow GF
, and the counit by
\varepsilon\colon FG\rightarrow{\rm Id}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow A
be a ring homomorphism, where
R
is a commutative noetherian ring and
A
is a finite
R
-algebra. Assume that
A
is a projective
R
-module and
\omega=\Hom_{R}(A,R)
is a projective right
A
-module. Then there is an adjoint pair

F\colon A\mbox{-{\rm Gproj}}\rightleftarrows R\mbox{-{\rm Gproj}}\colon G

between the subcategories of Gorenstein projective modules.

Proof.

The hypothesis yields that
F=\omega\otimes_{A}-
preserves finitely generated projective modules. For each
M\in A\mbox{-Gproj}
, choose a totally acyclic complex of finitely generated projective left
A
-modules
\mathbf{P}
such that
M\cong{\rm Im}(\partial_{0}^{\mathbf{P}})
. Note that
\omega\otimes_{A}\mathbf{P}
is an acyclic complex of finitely generated projective
R
-modules. Moreover, we conclude that
\omega\otimes_{A}\mathbf{P}
is totally acyclic by the following isomorphisms

\Hom_{R}(\omega\otimes_{A}\mathbf{P},R)\cong\Hom_{A}(\mathbf{P},\Hom_{R}(\omega,R))\cong\Hom_{A}(\mathbf{P},A).

This will imply that
\omega\otimes_{A}M\in R\mbox{-Gproj}
. Thus,
F
preserves finitely generated Gorenstein projective modules.

Since
A
is a finitely generated projective
R
-module, for any
R
-module
X
, by the Hom-evaluation we have an isomorphism of abelian groups

\theta_{X}\colon A\otimes_{R}X=A\otimes_{R}\Hom_{R}(R,X)\rightarrow\Hom_{R}(\omega,X)

which is given by
a\otimes x\mapsto(f\mapsto f(a)x)
; moreover, it is direct to check that
\theta_{X}
is a homomorphism of left
A
-modules. Hence,
G\cong A\otimes_{R}-
.

Let
N
be a finitely generated Gorenstein projective
R
-module. There is a totally acyclic complex of finitely generated projective
R
-modules
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}
such that
N\cong{\rm Im}(\partial_{0}^{\mathbf{P}^{\prime}})
. It is clear that

G(\mathbf{P}^{\prime})\cong A\otimes_{R}\mathbf{P}^{\prime}
is an acyclic complex of projective left
A
-modules. Since
{\rm Im}(\partial_{i}^{\mathbf{P}^{\prime}})
are Gorenstein projective modules over
R
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\Ext^{j}_{A}(A\otimes_{R}{\rm Im}(\partial_{i}^{\mathbf{P}^{\prime}}),A)\cong\Ext^{j}_{R}({\rm Im}(\partial_{i}^{\mathbf{P}^{\prime}}),A)=0

for any
j\geq 1
. This yields that
G(\mathbf{P}^{\prime})\cong A\otimes_{R}\mathbf{P}^{\prime}
is indeed a totally acyclic complex of projective left
A
-modules, and then
G(N)
is a Gorenstein projective left
A
-module.
Consequently, we get a restricted adjoint pair

F\colon A\mbox{-{\rm Gproj}}\rightleftarrows R\mbox{-{\rm Gproj}}\colon G

between subcategories of Gorenstein projective modules.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Keep the conditions as above. The functor
F\colon A\mbox{-{\rm Gproj}}\rightarrow R\mbox{-{\rm Gproj}}
is faithful. Moreover,
\eta_{M}\colon M\rightarrow GF(M)
is a monomorphism for each
M\in A\mbox{\rm-Gproj}
.

Proof.

For each
M\in A\mbox{-Gproj}
, consider the exact sequence

0\rightarrow K\xrightarrow{\iota}M\xrightarrow{\eta_{M}}GF(M)

in
A\mbox{-mod}
. We claim that
K
is zero. This is equivalent to that
\iota
is a zero map.

Note that
F(\eta_{M})
is injective by the identity
\id_{F(M)}=\varepsilon_{F(M)}\circ F(\eta_{M})
. By applying the exact functor
F=\omega\otimes_{A}-
to the above sequence, we infer that
F(\iota)=0
.
We have the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{\Hom_{R}(F(M),R)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\scriptstyle{\Hom_{R}(F(\iota),R)}
\textstyle{\Hom_{R}(F(K),R)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{\Hom_{A}(M,A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota^{\ast}}
\textstyle{\Hom_{A}(K,A),}

where the vertical isomorphisms are from the adjunction
(F,G)
and the
A
-linear isomorphism
\Hom_{R}(\omega,R)\cong A
. Thus,
\iota^{\ast}=\Hom_{A}(\iota,A)=0
.

Now, consider the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{K\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota}
\scriptstyle{{\rm e}_{K,A}}
\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\scriptstyle{{\rm e}_{M,A}}
\textstyle{\Hom_{A^{\rm op}}(\Hom_{A}(K,A),A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota^{\ast\ast}}
\textstyle{\Hom_{A^{\rm op}}(\Hom_{A}(M,A),A),}

where the isomorphism
{\rm e}_{M,A}
is from the assumption that
M\in A\mbox{-Gproj}
. Hence, we infer from
\iota^{\ast\ast}=\Hom_{A^{\rm op}}(\iota^{\ast},A)=0
that
\iota=0
, as desired.

Assume that
f:X\rightarrow Y
is a morphism in
A\mbox{-{\rm Gproj}}
such that
F(f)=0
.
Analogous to the above, we can prove that
f=0
. Hence, the functor

F\colon A\mbox{-{\rm Gproj}}\rightarrow R\mbox{-{\rm Gproj}}

is faithful. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow A
be a ring homomorphism, where
R
is a commutative noetherian ring and
A
is a finite
R
-algebra. If
A
is a projective generator in
R\mbox{-mod}
, then the functor
G\colon R\mbox{-{\rm mod}}\rightarrow A\mbox{-{\rm mod}}
is faithful. Moreover, for any
N\in R\mbox{-mod}
,
\varepsilon_{N}\colon FG(N)\rightarrow N
is an epimorphism.

Proof.

As an
R
-module,
A
is a projective generator if and only if
A
is faithfully flat; in this case,
G\simeq A\otimes_{R}-
is a faithful functor. Moreover, the proof of
[
12
, Lemma 2.1 (2)]
implies that for any
N\in R\mbox{-mod}
,
\varepsilon_{N}\colon FG(N)\rightarrow N
is an epimorphism.
∎

The following is the main result of this section; compare with
[
29
, Theorem I]
and
[
30
, Theorem 0.3]
.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
\varphi\colon R\rightarrow A
be a ring homomorphism, where
R
is a commutative noetherian ring and
A
is a finite
R
-algebra. Assume that
A
is a projective generator as an
R
-module and
\omega=\Hom_{R}(A,R)
is projective as a right
A
-module. Then the following are equivalent
\colon

(1)

The induced adjoint pair yields mutually inverse equivalences

F\colon A\mbox{-\lx@text@underline{\rm Gproj}}\rightleftarrows R\mbox{-\lx@text@underline{\rm Gproj}}\colon G.

(2)

For any
M\in A\mbox{-{\rm Gproj}}
and
N\in R\mbox{-{\rm Gproj}}
, the projective dimensions of
{\rm Coker}(\eta_{M})
and
{\rm Ker}(\varepsilon_{N})
are finite.

Proof.

It follows immediately from Lemma
6.1
that there is an induced adjoint pair of stable categories

F\colon A\mbox{-\lx@text@underline{\rm Gproj}}\rightleftarrows R\mbox{-\lx@text@underline{\rm Gproj}}\colon G.

The condition
(1)
is equivalent to, for each
M\in A\mbox{-Gproj}
and
N\in R\mbox{-Gproj}
,
\eta_{M}
and
\varepsilon_{N}
are isomorphic in
A\mbox{-\lx@text@underline{Gproj}}
and
R\mbox{-\lx@text@underline{Gproj}}
, respectively.

For each
M\in A\mbox{-{\rm Gproj}}
, by Lemma
6.2
we have a short exact sequence of left
A
-modules

0\rightarrow M\xrightarrow{\eta_{M}}GF(M)\longrightarrow{\rm Coker}(\eta_{M})\rightarrow 0,

which implies that
\Gdim_{A}({\rm Coker}(\eta_{M}))\leq 1
.

There is a canonical fully faithful functor

\mathsf{can}\colon A\mbox{-\lx@text@underline{\rm Gproj}}\rightarrow\mathsf{D}_{sg}(A)=\mathsf{D}^{f}_{b}(A)/\mathsf{perf}(A)

which sends a module to the corresponding stalk complex, where
\mathsf{perf}(A)
is the full subcategory of
\mathsf{D}^{f}_{b}(A)
consisting of perfect complexes, and
\mathsf{D}_{sg}(A)
stands for the singularity category; see for example
[
8
, Theorem 3.1]
. From the above embedding, we conclude that
\eta_{M}
is isomorphic in
A\mbox{-\lx@text@underline{Gproj}}
if and only if
{\rm Coker}(\eta_{M})
has finite projective dimension over
A
.

For each
N\in R\mbox{-{\rm Gproj}}
, we infer from Lemma
6.3
that there is a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow{\rm Ker}(\varepsilon_{N})\longrightarrow FG(N)\xrightarrow{\varepsilon_{N}}N\rightarrow 0,

which also implies that
{\rm Ker}(\varepsilon_{N})\in R\mbox{-{\rm Gproj}}
; see
[
2
, Proposition 5.1]
. Moreover, the above short exact sequence yields an exact triangle

{\rm Ker}(\varepsilon_{N})\longrightarrow FG(N)\xrightarrow{\varepsilon_{N}}N\rightarrow\Sigma{\rm Ker}(\varepsilon_{N})

in the stable category
R\mbox{-\lx@text@underline{\rm Gproj}}
, where
\Sigma
is the suspension functor; see
[
21
, Chapter 1]
for more details on stable category of Frobenius categories. Hence, combining with a well-known fact that the projective dimension of any Gorenstein projective module is either zero or infinite (
[
16
, Proposition 10.2.3]
), we infer that
\varepsilon_{N}
is isomorphic in
R\mbox{-\lx@text@underline{Gproj}}
if and only if
{\rm Ker}(\varepsilon_{N})
has finite projective dimension. ∎

Remark 6.5
.

(1) Theorem
6.4
is inspired by

, Proposition 4.2]
for Frobenius pair of faithful functors. However, our new ingredient is that we do not require

. Moreover,

is not required to be a Frobenius pair. If

is a Frobenius pair of functors.

(2) We recall from
[

, Definition 1.1]
that an
R
-algebra

is

if the
R
-module
A
is finitely generated and projective, and for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of

with

, the ring

is Iwanaga-Gorenstein. For the Gorenstein

that the composition of

and the Gorenstein projective approximation functor induces an equivalence

A\mbox{-\lx@text@underline{Gproj}}\rightarrow A\mbox{-\lx@text@underline{Gproj}}

of the stable category, where
\omega=\Hom_{R}(A,R)
is an
A
-bimodule. If
A
is a finite projective
R
-algebra over a commutative Gorenstein ring

, then

is a Gorenstein

The Poisson bracket invariant for open covers consisting of topological disks on surfaces
Kun Shi, Guangcun Lu

Abstract
L. Buhovsky, A. Logunov and S. Tanny proved the (strong) Poisson bracket conjecture by Leonid Polterovich in dimension 2 2 . In this note, instead of open cover consisting of displaceable sets in their work, we consider open cover constituted of topological discs and give a necessary and sufficient condition that Poisson bracket invariants of these covers are positive.



1
Introduction and results

Throughout this note, we always assume that
(M,\omega)
is a closed connected symplectic manifold
and that
\|\cdot\|:C^{\infty}(M)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
denotes the
C^{0}
-norm or
L^{\infty}
-norm of smooth functions on
M
.
Recall that a subset

X
of
M
is called
displaceable
if there exists a Hamiltonian diffeomorphism

\phi\in{\rm Ham}(M,\omega)
such that
\phi(X)
is disjoint with
X
(
[
8
]
).
Under the assumption that
(M,\omega)
is also semi-positive,
Entov and Polterovich
[
4
]
proved a surprising link between
non-displaceability and Poisson commutativity,
the so-called non-displaceable fiber theorem, which claimed that if a smooth map
\vec{f}=(f_{1},\cdots,f_{N}):M\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
has
pairwise Poisson commuting coordinate functions
f_{i}
,
i=1,\cdots,N
, then there exists one fiber
(\vec{f})^{-1}(p)
which is non-empty and
non-displaceable.
In the book
[
17
]
by Polterovich and Rosen, the assumption of semipositivity
was removed out (see
[
17
, Theorem 6.1.8]
), and there exists the following
rigidity of partitions of unity (see
[
5
]
): any finite open cover of
M
by open displaceable
sets does not admit a Poisson commuting partition of unity.

We say an open cover
\mathcal{U}
of
M
to be

connected
(resp.
displaceable
, resp.

connected and displaceable
)
if each element of
\mathcal{U}
is connected (resp.
displaceable, resp. connected and displaceable) in
M
.

In order to measure the Poisson noncommutativity of a smooth partition of unity

\mathcal{F}:=\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
subordinated to a connected and displaceable
finite open cover
\mathcal{U}:=\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
of
M
, Polterovich
[
15
]
defined the
magnitude
of its Poisson non-commutativity by

\nu_{c}(\mathcal{F}):=\max_{a,b\in[-1,1]^{N}}\left\|\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}f_{i},\sum_{j=1}^{N}b_{j}f_{j}\right\}\right\|,

and the
Poisson bracket invariant
of
\mathcal{U}
by

{\rm pb}(\mathcal{U}):=\inf_{\mathcal{F}}\nu_{c}(\mathcal{F}),

where the infimum is taken over all partitions of unity
\mathcal{F}
subordinate to

\mathcal{U}
. Polterovich
[
16
]
gave several lower bounds for
{\rm pb}(\mathcal{U})
and proposed:

Question 1.1

(
[
16
, Question 8.1]
)
.

Is it true that
{\rm pb}(\mathcal{U})\geq C/e(\mathcal{U})
, where the constant
C
depends only on
the symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
, and where
e(\mathcal{U}):=\max_{i\in I}e(U_{i})
and

e(U_{i})
is the displacement energy of
U_{i}
?

This was called the
strong Poisson bracket conjecture

in
[
13
]
, and studied in references
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
6
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
18
]
.

Recently, Buhovsky, Logunov and Tanny affirmatively answered this question in dimension
2
(see
[
2
]
).
Let
\mathcal{U}
be a finite open cover on smooth manifold
M
.
A set
U
in
\mathcal{U}
is called
essential
if
\mathcal{U}\setminus\{U\}
is not a cover (see
[
2
, Def.1.6]
).
Denote by
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{U})\subset\mathcal{U}
the collection
of essential sets of
\mathcal{U}
. Let

|\mathcal{U}|
denotes the number of open subsets in
\mathcal{U}
.
A subcover
\mathcal{U}_{0}
of
\mathcal{U}
is said to be
smallest
if
|\mathcal{U}_{0}|
is equal to

\kappa(\mathcal{U}):=\min_{\mathcal{U}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{U}}\{|\mathcal{U}^{\prime}|\;\big|\;\mathcal{U}^{\prime}\;\text{is also a cover of }\;M\}.

Clearly, the smallest subcover of
\mathcal{U}
is not necessarily unique, and each of them is
a minimal cover of
M
. Moreover, a subcover of
\mathcal{U}
which is a minimal cover of
M
is not always a smallest subcover
of
\mathcal{U}
, since the cardinality of a minimal cover of
M
may be greater than
\kappa(\mathcal{U})
.
Each element of
\mathcal{U}_{0}
is essential for the cover
\mathcal{U}_{0}
, but it not always essential for
\mathcal{U}
.
Buhovsky, Logunov and Tanny proved the following results.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
2
, Theorem 1.7 and Remark 1.8]
)
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed and connected symplectic surface. Let
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a finite open cover of
M
by topological discs of area less than
{\rm Area}(M)/2
, and let
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be any partition of unity subordinate to
\mathcal{U}
. Then there exists an absolute constant
c>0
, which is independent of
(M,\omega)
,
\mathcal{U}
and
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in I}
,
such that

{\rm pb}(\mathcal{U})\geq\frac{|\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{U})|c}{{\rm Area}(M)}\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\rm pb}(\mathcal{U})\geq\frac{c}{\min_{U\in\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{U})}{\rm Area}(U)},

where we set the minimum to be infinity if
\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{U})
is empty.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
2
, Theorem
1.5^{\prime}
]
)
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed and connected symplectic surface. Let
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
,
\mathcal{V}=\{V_{j}\}_{j\in J}
be finite
open covers of
M
, and let
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in I}
,
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in J}
be partitions of unity subordinate to
\mathcal{U}
,
\mathcal{V}
correspondingly. Then,

\int_{M}\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in J}|\{f_{i},g_{j}\}|\ \omega\geq\frac{{\rm Area}(M)}{2\cdot\max(e(\mathcal{U}),e(\mathcal{V}))}.

(1.1)

Note that the definition of displaceability at the bottom of
[
2
, page 1]

is slightly different from ours. They called a subset

X
of
M

displaceable
if the closure
\overline{X}
of
X
is displaceable in our definition above.
Thus, in our language,
[
2
, Corollary 1.9]
may be reformulated as:

Corollary 1.4

(
[
2
, Corollary 1.9]
)
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed and connected symplectic surface, and let
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be
a finite connected open cover of
M
such that the closure of each
U_{i}
is displaceable. Then for an absolute constant
c>0
, it holds that

{\rm pb}(\mathcal{U})\geq\frac{c}{e(\mathcal{U})}.

(1.2)

In Theorem
1.2
, since the second inequality holds trivially if

\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{U})=\emptyset
, it is necessary to assume that
the cover
\mathcal{U}
contains at least one essential set
so that
\min_{U\in\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{U})}{\rm Area}(U)
is a positive number.
Note also that the condition

“
\max_{1\leq i\leq N}{\rm Area}(U_{i})<{\rm Area}(M)/2
”

in Theorem
1.2
implies
\kappa(\mathcal{U})\geq 3
(because
M
is closed and each
U_{i}
is
a topological disc of area less than
{\rm Area}(M)/2
). It is obvious that the converse is not true in general.

Example 1.5
.

Let
\omega
be the standard area form on

M=S^{2}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,|\,x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}=1\},

and
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{1},U_{2},U_{3}\}
, where

\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}&U_{1}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in S^{2}\,|\,x_{3}<1/2\},\\
&U_{2}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in S^{2}\,|\,x_{3}>0,\;x_{1}>-1/4\},\\
&U_{3}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in S^{2}\,|\,x_{3}>0,\;x_{1}<1/4\}.\end{array}\right\}

(1.3)

Clearly,
\kappa(\mathcal{U})=3
, but
{\rm Area}(U_{1})>{\rm Area}(M)/2
.

The assumptions in Theorem
1.2
can be weakened. In fact, we have
the following result.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed and connected symplectic surface, and let
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be
a finite open cover of
M
made by topological discs and with
\kappa(\mathcal{U})\geq 3
. Then
there exists an absolute constant
c>0
such that

{\rm pb}(\mathcal{U})\geq\frac{|\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{U})|c}{{\rm Area}(M)}\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\rm pb}(\mathcal{U})\geq\frac{c}{\min_{U\in\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{U})}{\rm Area}(U)}.

As an improvement of a special case of
[
2
, Theorem 1.5 or
1.5^{\prime}
]
, we have
the following analogue of Theorem
1.3

(
[
2
, Theorem
1.5^{\prime}
]
).

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed connected symplectic surface and let

\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a finite open cover consisting of topological discs. If
\kappa(\mathcal{U})\geq 3
, then for any partition of unity subordinate to
\mathcal{U}
,
\mathcal{F}:=\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
, there holds

\int_{M}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}|\{f_{i},f_{j}\}|\omega\geq 2.

Moreover, there exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that

{\rm pb}(\mathcal{U})\geq\frac{C}{{\rm Area}(M)}.

(1.4)

Remark 1.8
.

Since the Lusternik-Schnirelmann category of every closed surface of genus
g\geq 1
is at least
3
,
the condition
\kappa(\mathcal{U})\geq 3
in Theorem
1.7
is automatically satisfied in such closed surfaces.
Hence (
1.4
) holds. In this case (
1.4
) may also follow from

[
13
, Theorem 1.4.14]
as pointed out by Payette
[
14
]
. J. Payette called (
1.4
)
the
weak Poisson bracket conjecture

(see
[
13
]
).

Clearly, if an open cover
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
of a closed connected symplectic surface

(M,\omega)
consists of topological discs, then
\kappa(\mathcal{U})\geq 2
. We claim that
{\rm pb}(\mathcal{U})=0
if
\kappa(\mathcal{U})<3
.
In fact, in this case, any smallest subcover
\mathcal{U}_{0}
of
\mathcal{U}
only contains two sets, saying

\mathcal{U}_{0}=\{U_{1},U_{2}\}
, and any smooth partition of unity
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
subordinated to
\mathcal{U}_{0}
satisfies
\{f_{1},f_{2}\}=\{f_{1},1-f_{1}\}=0
and so

0={\rm pb}(\mathcal{U}_{0})\geq{\rm pb}(\mathcal{U})\geq 0
.
From these and Theorem
1.7
, we arrive at:

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed connected symplectic surface and
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a open cover of
M
consisting of topological discs.
Then
{\rm pb}(\mathcal{U})>0
if and only if
\kappa(\mathcal{U})\geq 3
.

Under the assumptions of Corollary
1.4
(
[
2
, Corollary 1.9]
), the inequality in (
1.4
) may follow from (
1.2
), since
e(\mathcal{U})\leq{\rm Area}(M,\omega)/2
.
Observe that the assumptions of Theorem
1.7
do not imply the assumptions of Corollary
1.4
, and vice-versa.
Actually, from Theorem
1.7
we can also derive:

Corollary 1.10
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a closed and connected symplectic surface and let

\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}^{N}_{i=1}
be a finite connected open
cover of
M
such that the closure of each
U_{i}
is
displaceable. Then for an absolute constant
c>0
, it holds that

{\rm pb}(\mathcal{U})\geq\frac{c}{{\rm Area}(M,\omega)}.

(1.5)

Proof.

Since
U_{i}
is connected, so is the closure
\overline{U}_{i}
of it.
Moreover,
\overline{U}_{i}
is displaceable, and compact due to the fact that
M
is a closed surface.
By
[
2
, Remark 1.2]
,
we have embedded open topological discs
V_{i}\subset M
with smooth boundaries
such that
U_{i}\subset V_{i}
and
{\rm Area}(V_{i})\leq{\rm Area}(M)/2
,
i=1,\cdots,N
.
In fact, by Exercise 3 in
[
7
, Chapter 3, §1]
,
\overline{U}_{i}
has a closed neighborhood

W_{i}
which is a smooth submanifold of
M
.
Moreover, since
W_{i}
can be required to be arbitrarily close
\overline{U}_{i}
,
we may assume that it is displaceable, connected and has area less than
{\rm Area}(M)/2
.
As Payette
[
14
]
told us, using the Jordan-Schoenflies theorem and Exercise 3 in
[
7
, Chapter 3, §1]

may yield a smoothly embedded closed disc
\overline{D}_{i}\supseteq W_{i}
of area
{\rm Area}(\overline{D}_{i})\leq{\rm Area}(M)/2

(see also
[
12
, Theorem 4.1(2)-(i)]
for a detailed proof).
Then
V_{i}:={\rm Int}(\overline{D}_{i})
satisfies our requirements.
Clearly,
\mathcal{V}=\{V_{i}\}^{N}_{i=1}
is an open cover of
M
, and
\kappa(\mathcal{V})\geq 3
. Hence
(
1.5
) follows from Theorem
1.7
, since

{\rm pb}(\mathcal{U})\geq{\rm pb}(\mathcal{V})
by definition.
∎

Finally, it should be pointed out that Example
1.5
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
1.7
,
but does not those of Corollary
1.4
(
[
2
, Corollary 1.9]
).
Notice that the cover
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{1},U_{2},U_{3}\}
in Example

is

-localized (

]
for the precise definition) because
we can choose three points
p_{i}\in S^{2}
,

for

.
For example, set

p_{1}=(0,0,-1),\quad p_{2}=(\sqrt{7}/4,0,3/4),\quad p_{3}=(-\sqrt{7}/4,0,3/4).

Hence, the weak Poisson bracket conjecture for the cover
\mathcal{U}
in Example
1.5

can also follows from Theorem 1.4.12 of
[

]
. However, we can add an open topological disk

U_{4}\subset S^{2}
to
\mathcal{U}
so that the new cover
\mathcal{V}:=\{U_{1},U_{2},U_{3},U_{4}\}
of

also satisfies

, but cannot be

-localized.
Hence, the weak Poisson bracket conjecture for the cover
\mathcal{V}
cannot follows from Theorem 1.4.12 of

]
.
Indeed, by (

) it is direct to check that

\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}&U_{1}\cap U_{2}^{c}\cap U_{3}^{c}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in S^{2}\,|\,x_{3}\leq 0\},\\
&U_{2}\cap U_{1}^{c}\cap U_{3}^{c}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in S^{2}\,|\,x_{3}\geq 1/2,\;x_{1}\geq 1/4\},\\

(1.6)

and hence that the complementary set of union of these three subsets in

is

\left\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in S^{2}\,\Big|\,0<x_{3}<\frac{1}{2},\;|x_{1}|\leq 1\right\}\bigcup\left\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in S^{2}\,\Big|\,x_{3}\geq\frac{1}{2},\;|x_{1}|<\frac{1}{4}\right\}.

Let
U_{4}\subset S^{2}
be an open topological disk which contains

,
U_{2}\cap U_{3}^{c}\cap U_{1}^{c}
and

.
Then it is easily checked that the new cover

of

cannot be

-localized
and therefore does not satisfy the conditions of
[

, Theorem 1.4.12]
.
On the other hand, we can require

to have area close to that of union of those three subsets in (

)
so that
\{U_{i},U_{4}\}
cannot cover

for each

.

.

Remark 1.11
.

As with Buhovsky-Logunov-Tanny
[
2
]
and Payette
[

]
,

,

Proof of Theorem
1.6

Our proof will be completed by the similar argument to
[
2
, Theorem 1.7]
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
2
, Lemma 1.3]
)
.

Let
(M^{2n},\omega)
be a closed symplectic manifold of dimension
2n
. Then there exists a constant
c(n)>0
depending only on the dimension
2n
of
M
such that for every finite collection of smooth functions
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
on
M
,

\max_{a,b\in[-1,1]^{N}}\left\|\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}f_{i},\sum_{j=1}^{N}b_{j}f_{j}\right\}\right\|\geq c(n)\cdot\max_{M}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}|\{f_{i},f_{j}\}|.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
2
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

Let
M
be a closed connected surface and
\omega
be an area form. For given two smooth functions
f,g:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, let
\Phi:=(f,g):M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Consider the function
K:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
defined by

K(s,t):=\#(f^{-1}(s)\cap g^{-1}(t))=\#\Phi^{-1}(s,t).

Then for any Lebesgue measurable set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\int_{\Phi^{-1}(\Omega)}|\{f,g\}|\omega=\int_{\Omega}K(s,t)dsdt.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a partition of unity
subordinated to a given open cover
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}^{N}_{i=1}

of
M
.
For
t\geq 0
, we define

U_{i}(t):=\{x\in M\,|\,f_{i}>t\},\quad i=1,\cdots,N.

(2.1)

Theorem
1.6
may be derived from Lemma
2.1
and the following:

Lemma 2.3
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.6
,
for any partition of unity
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
subordinated to
\mathcal{U}
, it holds that

\int_{M}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}|\{f_{i},f_{j}\}|\omega\geq|\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{U})|,

\max_{M}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}|\{f_{i},f_{j}\}|\geq\frac{1}{\min_{U_{i}\in\mathcal{J}(\mathcal{U})}{\rm Area}(U_{i})},

where the minimum of an empty set is understand to be infinity.

Proof.

This will be proved as in
[
2
, Theorem 1.7]
.
Since the
open cover
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
contains at least one essential set, we can fix an essential set

U_{i}\in\mathcal{U}
.
Then there exists a point
z_{i}\in U_{i}
such that
z_{i}\notin U_{j}

for all
j\in\{1,\cdots,N\}\setminus\{i\}
. It follows that
f_{i}(z_{i})=1
, and hence
z_{i}\in U_{i}(s)
for all
s\in(0,1)
.
For every regular value
s\in(0,1)
of
f_{i}
, let
V_{i}(s)
denote the connected component of
U_{i}(s)
that contains
z_{i}
.
Denote by
\tilde{V}_{i}(s)
the open topological disc of minimal area which contains
V_{i}(s)
and is contained in
U_{i}
,
and by
\gamma^{s}:=\partial\tilde{V}_{i}(s)
.
Then
\gamma^{s}
is connected and is contained in
\{f_{i}=s\}\cap\partial V_{i}(s)
. Fix
y^{s}\in\gamma^{s}
and set

t^{s}_{j}:=f_{j}(y^{s})\in\mathbb{R}
for each
j\neq i
.

For a fixed
j\neq i
, and let
t\in(0,t_{j}^{s})
be a regular value of
f_{j}
. Then
y^{s}\in U_{j}(t)
.
Denote by
D_{j}(t)
the closure of connected component of
U_{j}(t)
that contains
y^{s}
, and
by
\tilde{D}_{j}(t)
the closed topological disc of minimal area which contains
D_{j}(t)

and is contained in
U_{j}
. Then
\partial\tilde{D}_{j}(t)\subset\partial D_{j}(t)\cap\{f_{j}=t\}
.

We claim that
\gamma^{s}
has at least two points of intersection with
\partial\tilde{D}_{j}(t)
.
In fact, since the interior of
\tilde{D}_{j}(t)
intersects
\gamma^{s}
,
we need only to prove that
\gamma^{s}
is not contained in
\tilde{D}_{j}(t)
.
Equivalently, it suffice to show that both
\tilde{V}_{i}(s)
and its complement
\tilde{V}_{i}(s)^{c}
are not contained in
\tilde{D}_{j}(t)
. Since
D_{j}(t)\subset U_{j}
and
\partial\tilde{D}_{j}(t)\subset\{f_{j}=t\}\subset U_{j}
,
we have
\tilde{D}_{j}(t)\subset U_{j}
. Moreover,
\tilde{V}_{i}(s)
contains
z_{i}\notin U_{j}
, and so

\tilde{V}_{i}(s)\nsubseteq\tilde{D}_{j}(t)
. In order to get the desired claim, we also need to show that
\tilde{V}_{i}(s)^{c}\nsubseteq\tilde{D}_{j}(t)
.
Suppose that
U_{i}^{c}\subset U_{j_{0}}
for some
j_{0}\in\{1,\cdots,N\}\setminus\{i\}
.
Then
\{U_{i},U_{j_{0}}\}
covers
M
, and thus
\kappa(\mathcal{U})\leqslant 2
, which contradicts the assumption

\kappa(\mathcal{U})\geq 3
. Hence,
U_{i}^{c}\nsubseteq U_{j}
for any
j
with
j\neq i
. Note that

U_{i}^{c}\subset\tilde{V}_{i}(s)^{c}
and
\tilde{D}_{j}(t)\subset U_{j}
. We conclude that
\tilde{V}_{i}(s)^{c}\nsubseteq\tilde{D}_{j}(t)
.

Denote by
{\rm cv}(f_{k})
the set of critical values of
f_{k}
,
k=1,\cdots,N
. Set

\Omega_{ij}:=\{(s,t)\,|\,s\in(0,1)\setminus{\rm cv}(f_{i})\;\&\;t\in(0,t^{s}_{j})\setminus{\rm cv}(f_{j})\}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Phi_{ij}^{-1}(\Omega_{ij})}|\{f_{i},f_{j}\}|\omega

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{ij}}K_{ij}(s,t)dsdt

\displaystyle 2\int_{(0,1)\setminus{\rm cv}(f_{i})}(1-f_{i}(y^{s}))ds.

Summing over all

In order to prove the second inequality, let us choose

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{U_{i_{0}}}|\{f_{i_{0}},f_{j}\}|\omega

we arrive at:
(s_{i,k},s_{i^{\prime},k^{\prime}})
is a common regular value of all maps
\Phi_{i,i^{\prime}}
,

(t_{j,l},t_{j^{\prime},l^{\prime}})
is that of all maps
\Psi_{j,j^{\prime}}
,
and
(s_{i,k},t_{j,l})
is that of all maps
\Upsilon_{i,j}
.

In particular, for such a pair
(s,t)
, the boundaries
\partial U_{i,k}^{s}
and
\partial V_{j,l}^{t}

(resp.
\partial U_{i,k}^{s}
and
\partial U_{i^{\prime},k^{\prime}}^{s}
,
\partial V_{j,l}^{t}
and
\partial V_{j^{\prime},l^{\prime}}^{t}
)
intersect transversely.
Moreover, if
\partial U_{i,k}^{s}\cap\partial V_{j,l}^{t}\cap\partial V_{j^{\prime},l^{\prime}}^{t}\neq\emptyset
(resp.

\partial U_{i,k}^{s}\cap\partial U_{i^{\prime},k^{\prime}}^{s}\cap\partial V_{j,l}^{t}\neq\emptyset
), then

(s_{i,k},t_{j,l},t_{j^{\prime},l^{\prime}})
(resp.
(s_{i,k},s_{i^{\prime},k^{\prime}},t_{j,l})
)

is in the image of

\displaystyle\Phi_{i,j,j^{\prime}}:M\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3},x\mapsto(f_{i}(x),g_{j}(x),g_{j^{\prime}}(x))

\displaystyle\hbox{(resp. $\Psi_{i,i^{\prime},j}:M\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3},x\mapsto(f_{i}(x),f_{i^{\prime}}(x),g_{j}(x))$)}.

Since the images
{\rm Im}(\Phi_{i,j,j^{\prime}})
and
{\rm Im}(\Psi_{i,i^{\prime},j})

have the codimension at least
1
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

and thus both
\cup_{i,j,j^{\prime}}{\rm Im}(\Phi_{i,j,j^{\prime}})
and
\cup_{i,i^{\prime},j}{\rm Im}(\Psi_{i,i^{\prime},j})

have measure zero, we deduce that for almost all
(s,t)\in\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}
, the covers
\mathcal{U}^{s}
and
\mathcal{V}^{t}
are good and in generic positions.

It remains to prove that (ii) of Lemma
3.4
is satisfied.
Note that

U_{i,k}^{s}:=\{f_{i}>s_{i,k}\}\subset{\rm supp}f_{i}\subset U_{i}\quad\hbox{and}\quad V_{j,l}^{t}:=\{g_{j}>t_{j,l}\}\subset{\rm supp}g_{j}\subset V_{j}.

Taking
\widetilde{U_{i,k}^{s}}=U_{i}
and
\widetilde{V_{j,l}^{t}}=V_{j}
, it is easy to see that they satisfy (ii) of Lemma
3.4
.

In summary, after
L
is replaced by
\hat{L}
,
\mathcal{U}^{s}
and
\mathcal{V}^{t}
satisfy the assumptions of Lemma
3.4
for almost all
(s,t)\in\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}
.
Therefore, for almost all
(s,t)\in\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}
, we obtain

\#\cup_{i,k,j,l}\left(\partial U_{i,k}^{s}\cap\partial V_{j,l}^{t}\right)\geq 2(\hat{L}+1)^{2}.

Averaging this inequality over
(s,t)\in\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}
with respect to the normalized product measure
\mu\times\nu
,
where
\mu:=\prod_{i,k}\mu_{i,k}
and
\nu:=\prod_{j,l}\nu_{j,l}
, we obtain

\displaystyle 2(\hat{L}+1)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}}\#\cup_{i,k,j,l}\left(\partial U_{i,k}^{s}\cap\partial V_{j,l}^{t}\right)\,d\mu(s)d\nu(t)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}}\sum_{i,k,j,l}\#\left(\partial U_{i,k}^{s}\cap\partial V_{j,l}^{t}\right)d\mu(s)d\nu(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,k,j,l}\int_{\frac{k-1}{L}}^{\frac{k}{L}}\int_{\frac{l-1}{L}}^{\frac{l}{L}}\#\left(\partial U_{i,k}^{s}\cap\partial V_{j,l}^{t}\right)d\mu_{i,k}(s_{i,k})d\nu_{j,l}(t_{j,l})

\displaystyle=L^{2}\sum_{i,k,j,l}\int_{\frac{k-1}{L}}^{\frac{k}{L}}\int_{\frac{l-1}{L}}^{\frac{l}{L}}\#\left(\partial U_{i,k}^{s}\cap\partial V_{j,l}^{t}\right)ds_{i,k}dt_{j,l},

since
\mu_{i,k}:=Lds_{i,k}
and
\nu_{j,l}:=Ldt_{j,l}
.
Moreover, for any values of
s_{i,k}
and
t_{j,l}
, we have

\partial U_{i,k}^{s}=\partial\{f_{i}>s_{i,k}\}\subset f_{i}^{-1}(s_{i,k})\quad\hbox{and}\quad\partial V_{j,l}^{t}=\partial\{g_{j}>t_{j,l}\}\subset g_{j}^{-1}(t_{j,l}).

Hence,

2(\hat{L}+1)^{2}\leq L^{2}\sum_{i,k,j,l}\int_{\frac{k-1}{L}}^{\frac{k}{L}}\int_{\frac{l-1}{L}}^{\frac{l}{L}}\#\left(f_{i}^{-1}(s_{i,k})\cap g_{j}^{-1}(t_{j,l})\right)ds_{i,k}dt_{j,l}.

Recall that
\Upsilon_{i,j}=(f_{i},g_{j}):M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Set

\Omega_{k,l}:=\left(\frac{k-1}{L},\frac{k}{L}\right)\times\left(\frac{l-1}{L},\frac{l}{L}\right)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}.

From Lemma
2.2
, we deduce

\displaystyle 2(\hat{L}+1)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq L^{2}\sum_{i,k,j,l}\int_{\Omega_{k,l}}\int_{\frac{l-1}{L}}^{\frac{l}{L}}\#\left(f_{i}^{-1}(s_{i,k})\cap g_{j}^{-1}(t_{j,l})\right)ds_{i,k}dt_{j,l}

\displaystyle=L^{2}\sum_{i,k,j,l}\int_{\Phi^{-1}_{i,j}(\Omega_{k,l})}|\{f_{i},g_{j}\}|\omega

\displaystyle\leq L^{2}\sum_{i,j}\int_{M}|\{f_{i},g_{j}\}|\omega

and thus

\sum_{i,j}\int_{M}|\{f_{i},g_{j}\}|\omega\geq\frac{2(\hat{L}+1)^{2}}{L^{2}}=\frac{2(L+1-|I|-|J|)^{2}}{L^{2}}.

Letting
L\rightarrow\infty
, the conclusion is proved.
∎

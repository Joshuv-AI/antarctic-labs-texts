Chordal Matroids arising from Generalized parallel connections
James Dylan Douthitt, James Oxley

Abstract
. A graph is chordal if every cycle of length at least four has a chord. In 1961, Dirac characterized chordal graphs as those graphs that can be built from complete graphs by repeated clique-sums. Generalizing this, we consider the class of simple G ​ F ​ ( q ) GF(q) -representable matroids that can be built from projective geometries over G ​ F ​ ( q ) GF(q) by repeated generalized parallel connections across projective geometries. We show that this class of matroids is closed under induced minors. We characterize the class by its forbidden induced minors; the case when q = 2 q=2 is distinctive.

Introduction

The notation and terminology in this paper will follow
[

]
for graphs and
[

]
for matroids. Unless stated otherwise, all graphs and matroids considered here are simple. Thus every contraction of a set from a matroid is immediately followed by the simplification of the resulting matroid. A

of
G
that is not in
C
such that both vertices of

are vertices of
C
. A graph is
chordal
if every cycle of length at least four has a chord. Such graphs were called

by Dirac
[
8
]
and

by Berge
[
2
]
. Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be graphs and
V(G_{1})\cap V(G_{2})=V

is a complete graph

has edge set

. The

of

is the graph with vertex set

and edge set

together across the clique

]

Theorem 1.1
.

A graph
G
is chordal if and only if

can be constructed from complete graphs by repeated clique-sums.

Let

and

be matroids whose ground sets intersect in a set

is a modular flat of

, and
M_{1}|T=M_{2}|T=~N
. The

and

whose flats are those subsets

is a flat of

, and

. We denote this matroid by

. Note that

.

For a prime power

, we will denote the projective geometry

be the class of matroids that can be built from projective geometries over

An

is highlighted by the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

For all

, the class
\mathcal{M}_{q}
is closed under taking induced minors.

Our main results are the following characterizations of the forbidden induced minors for the class

Theorem 1.3
.

The set of forbidden induced minors for the class

Theorem 1.4

For each

Several different notions of chordal matroids have been given over the last forty years

2.
Preliminaries

Before beginning the discussions of forbidden induced minors for the class
\mathcal{M}_{q}
, we show that this class is closed under taking induced minors. We shall use the following well-known property of generalized parallel connections (see, for example,
[
12
, 11.23]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

For every flat
F
of
P_{N}(M_{1},M_{2})
,

r(F)=r(F\cap E(M_{1}))+r(F\cap E(M_{2}))-r(F\cap E(N)).

Lemma 2.2
.

The class
\mathcal{M}_{q}
of
GF(q)
-chordal matroids is closed under induced restrictions.

Proof.

It is enough to show that the class
\mathcal{M}_{q}
is closed under restricting to a hyperplane. Let
M
be a minimum-rank matroid in
\mathcal{M}_{q}
such that
M|H\not\in\mathcal{M}_{q}
for some hyperplane
H
of
M
. Then
M
is not a projective geometry, so
M=P_{N}(M_{1},M_{2})
where
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are in
\mathcal{M}_{q}
, and
N
is a projective geometry over
GF(q)
. Let
H_{i}=H\cap E(M_{i})
for each
i
in
\{1,2\}
and let
H_{N}=H\cap E(N)
. Then, since
H
is a both flat of a generalized parallel connection and a hyperplane of
M
,

r(M_{1})+r(M_{2})-r(N)-1=r(H)=r(H_{1})+r(H_{2})-r(H_{N}).

(2.1)

Suppose
H
contains all of
E(N)
. Then
r(H_{N})=r(N)
, so, from
(2.1)
, we have

r(M_{1})+r(M_{2})-1=r(H_{1})+r(H_{2}).

This implies that, for some
i
and
j
such that
\{i,j\}=\{1,2\}
, the hyperplane
H
contains
E(M_{i})
, and
H_{j}
is a hyperplane of
M_{j}
. It follows by the minimality of
M
that
M|H
is in
\mathcal{M}_{q}
, a contradiction.

We now assume that
H
does not contain
E(N)
. Hence
H
does not contain
E(M_{1})
or
E(M_{2})
. By
[
5
]
,
E(M_{1})
is a modular flat of
P_{N}(M_{1},M_{2})
, so

r(H)=r(H\cup E(M_{1}))+r(H_{1})-r(M_{1}).

(2.2)

As
E(M_{1})\not\subseteq H
, we deduce that
r(H\cup E(M_{1}))>r(H)
, so
r(H\cup E(M_{1}))=r(H)+1
. It follows from
(2.2)
that
r(H_{1})=r(M_{1})-1
. By symmetry,
r(H_{2})=r(M_{2})-1
. Hence
H_{i}
is a hyperplane of
M_{i}
for each
i
in
\{1,2\}
. Therefore, by
(2.1)
and
(2.2)
,

\displaystyle r(M_{1})+r(M_{2})-r(N)-1=r(M_{1})-1+r(M_{2})-1-r(H_{N}).

Thus
r(H_{N})=r(N)-1
. Hence
H_{N}
is a hyperplane of
N
, so
M|H_{N}
is a projective geometry. By
[
5
]
(see also
[
12
, Proposition 11.4.15]
),
M|H=P_{M|H_{N}}(M|H_{1},M|H_{2})
, so
M|H
is a generalized parallel connection of members of
\mathcal{M}_{q}
along a projective geometry and is therefore a member of
\mathcal{M}_{q}
, a contradiction.
∎

In the next result, where we show that
\mathcal{M}_{q}
is closed under contractions, we shall make repeated use of the fact that every contraction is followed by a simplification. We observe that a consequence of this result is that
\mathcal{M}_{q}
is closed under parallel minors.

Lemma 2.3
.

The class
\mathcal{M}_{q}
of
GF(q)
-chordal matroids is closed under taking contractions.

Proof.

Let
M
be a
GF(q)
-chordal matroid. We argue by induction on
|E(M)|
that
M/e
is a
GF(q)
-chordal matroid for all
e
in
E(M)
. The result is certainly true if
|E(M)|\leq 2
. Suppose the result holds when
|E(M)|<k
and let
|E(M)|=k
. The result holds if
M
is a projective geometry, so we may assume that
M=P_{N}(M_{1},M_{2})
, where
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are in
\mathcal{M}_{q}
, and
N
is a projective geometry over
GF(q)
. Suppose that
e\in E(M_{1})-E(N)
. Then
M_{1}/e
is in
\mathcal{M}_{q}
by the induction hypothesis and
M_{1}/e
has
N
as a restriction. As
M/e=P_{N}(M_{1}/e,M_{2})
, we deduce that
M/e
is in
\mathcal{M}_{q}
. We may now assume that
e\in E(N)
. This implies that
e
is in both
E(M_{1})
and
E(M_{2})
, and, by the induction hypothesis,
M_{1}/e
and
M_{2}/e
are in
\mathcal{M}_{q}
and contain the projective geometry
N/e
as a restriction. By
[
5
]
,
M/e=P_{N/e}(M_{1}/e,M_{2}/e)
, and we again get that
M/e
is in
\mathcal{M}_{q}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

This is an immediate consequence of combining Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
.
∎

3.
Characterizing
GF(q)
-chordal matroids

In this section, we prove Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
. An
induced-minor-minimal non-
GF(q)
-chordal matroid
is a
GF(q)
-representable matroid that is not a
GF(q)
-chordal matroid such that every proper induced minor of
M
is a
GF(q)
-chordal matroid. When

, the only rank-2 binary matroids are

and

both of which are
GF(2)
-chordal. Clearly, neither
M(K_{4})
nor
U_{3,4}
is
GF(2)
-chordal. It follows that each is an induced-minor-minimal non-
GF(2)
-chordal matroid. When

, the matroids in
\{U_{2,3},U_{2,4},\dots,U_{2,q}\}
are induced-minor-minimal non-
GF(q)
-chordal matroids.

For the remainder of this paper, it will be convenient to view a
GF(q)
-representable matroid

of rank

as a restriction of
P_{r}
by coloring the elements of
E(M)

green
and coloring the other elements

. Note that when we contract an element

from

as follows. Take a hyperplane

of

that avoids

onto

. Because

is green, an element

is green in the contraction precisely when there are at least two green points on the line
{\rm cl}_{P_{r}}(\{e,z\})
of

.

For a positive integer

of the ground set of a matroid

is a

-separation
of

if

. This vertical

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(X,Y)
be a vertical
2
-separation in a matroid
M
such that each of
M|{\rm cl}(X)
and
M|{\rm cl}(Y)
is in
\mathcal{M}_{q}
. Then either

(i)

|{\rm cl}(X)\cap{\rm cl}(Y)|=1
and
M\in\mathcal{M}_{q}
; or

(ii)

|{\rm cl}(X)\cap{\rm cl}(Y)|=0
and
M
has
U_{3,4}
and
U_{2,3}
as induced minors.

Proof.

Observe that if
|{\rm cl}(X)\cap{\rm cl}(Y)|=1
, then
M
is the parallel connection of
M|{\rm cl}(X)
and
M|{\rm cl}(Y)
, so
M\in\mathcal{M}_{q}
. Now suppose that
{\rm cl}(X)\cap{\rm cl}(Y)=\emptyset
. Let
C
and
D
be circuits of
M
each of which meets both
X
and
Y
such that
|C\cap X|
is a minimum and
|D\cap Y|
is a minimum. Because

can be written as a
2
-sum with basepoint

of matroids with ground sets
X\cup b
and
Y\cup b
, it follows that
(C\cap X)\cup(D\cap Y)
is a circuit of
M
. Clearly both

and
M|{\rm cl}(D\cap Y)
are in
\mathcal{M}_{q}
. Let
X_{1}
and
Y_{1}
be subsets of
C\cap X
and
D\cap Y
, respectively, such that
|X_{1}|=|C\cap X|-2
and
|Y_{1}|=|D\cap Y|-2
. Then each of
(M|{\rm cl}(C\cap X))/X_{1}
and
(M|{\rm cl}(D\cap Y))/Y_{1}
is a rank-2 matroid in
\mathcal{M}_{q}
. Moreover, in
M/X_{1}
, there is no element
x
of
X
that is in the closure of
Y
otherwise
(X_{1}\cup x)\cup(D\cap Y)
contains a circuit of

that contains
x\cup(D\cap Y)
and violates the choice of

. Hence the rank-2 matroid
(M|{\rm cl}(C\cap X))/X_{1}
, which is in
\mathcal{M}_{q}
, is not isomorphic to

. Thus it is isomorphic to
U_{2,2}
.
By symmetry,
(M|{\rm cl}(D\cap Y))/Y_{1}\cong U_{2,2}
. Hence
(M|{\rm cl}((C\cap X)\cup(D\cap Y)))/(X_{1}\cup Y_{1})\cong U_{3,4}
. Thus both
U_{3,4}
and

are induced minors of

.
∎

In the next result, we denote by
P_{r+1}\backslash P_{r-i}
the matroid that is obtained from

by deleting the elements of a rank-
(r-i)
flat. Clearly this matroid does not depend on the choice of the rank-
(r-i)

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
M
be a binary rank-
(r+1)
matroid. Then
M/e\cong P_{r}
for all

in

if and only if
M\cong P_{r+1}\backslash P_{r-i}
for some

in
\{0,1,\dots,r\}
.

Proof.

If
M\cong P_{r+1}\backslash P_{r-i}
for some

in
\{0,1,\dots,r\}
, then, by, for example,
[
12
, Corollary 6.2.6]
,
M/e\cong P_{r}
for all

in
E(M)
.

Now suppose
E(M)\cong P_{r}
for each
e
in
E(M)
. We may assume that
|E(P_{r+1})-E(M)|\geq 2
otherwise the result certainly holds. Let
x
and

be distinct elements of

. Then the third element

on the line of

that is spanned by

must also be in

otherwise

is not isomorphic to

. It follows that, for a basis

of

, by
[

{\rm cl}_{P_{r+1}}(X)=E(P_{r+1})-E(M)
. Since

has rank

, we deduce that

is a projective geometry of rank at most

∎

We omit the straightforward proof of the next result.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
r\geq 3
, if
M
is the binary matroid
P_{r}\backslash P_{r-i}
for some

in the set
\{1,2,\dots,r-1\}
, then

has a flat isomorphic to either

or

.

For all

, the only rank-2 members of

are

and

. Natural obstructions to membership of

are the lines that contain more than two but fewer than

. In rank three, the only matroid that is not in

and has no member of

. By Bose

\mathcal{N}=\{U_{2,3},U_{2,4},\dots,U_{2,q},U_{3,q+2}\}

. The next result highlights some structure in matroids that have members of
\mathcal{N}
as induced minors.

Lemma 3.4
.

For some
q>2
, let
M
be a
GF(q)
-representable matroid having rank at least three. Suppose that
M\not\cong P_{r}
but that
M/e\cong P_{r-1}
for all

in

. Then

has a member of
\mathcal{N}
as an induced minor.

Proof.

Suppose
M
has no member of
\mathcal{N}
as an induced minor. Suppose
r(M)=3
. Since the contraction of any element would result in a
(q+1)
-point line,
E(M)
must have at least
q+2
elements. Moreover, since
M
is not
U_{3,q+2}
, we deduce that
M
contains a triangle
\{p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\}
. This triangle must be contained in a full
(q+1)
-point line of

, otherwise

would contain a member of
\mathcal{N}
as an induced restriction. Label this line
\{p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{q+1}\}
. Since
|E(M)|\geq q+2
, we may choose an element
e
in
E(M)-\{p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{q+1}\}
. If
e
is unique, then
M/p_{1}
is isomorphic to

, a contradiction. Without loss of generality, suppose there is a third point on the line
{\rm cl}(\{e,p_{1}\})
. Then this line is a full
(q+1)
-point line. Therefore, there are two full lines meeting at

. If this were the entire matroid, then

consists of only two points. Hence there is an additional element
f
in
M
not on either of the lines
{\rm cl}(\{e,p_{1}\})
or
\{p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{q+1}\}
. Each point of the line
{\rm cl}(\{e,p_{1}\})

defines a line that meets the line

. If any of the points on the line

is absent, then, for some

, the line
{\rm cl}(\{e,p_{i}\})
contains only

is full and

must be a projective geometry, a contradiction. Thus the result holds when

.

Now suppose the result holds for

and let

. Let

be an element of

. Since

, each of the lines of

that contains

must contain a second point of

. There must be such a line, say

, that contains exactly two points of

otherwise every such line has exactly

, a contradiction. Let

. Take a point

. It follows by the induction assumption that

. Hence

is a
(q+1)
-point line, a contradiction. We conclude, by induction, that the lemma holds.

L

{\rm cl}_{P_{r}}(X)\cap{\rm cl}_{P_{r}}(Y)
. In the green-red coloring of
P_{r}
, every element of
L
is red. But every element of
L
is green in the coloring of
P_{r}/e
. Thus, in
P_{r}
, for each of the points
z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{q+1}
of
L
, there is a green point on the line
{\rm cl}_{P_{r}}(\{e,z_{i}\})
other than
e
. Thus, when
q=2
, we see that the four green points in
{\rm cl}_{P_{r}}(L\cup e)
form a 4-circuit, a contradiction as
M|{\rm cl}_{M}(X)
is
GF(2)
-chordal. When
q>2
, because all of the elements of
L
are red, the set of points in
{\rm cl}_{P_{r}}(L\cup e)
contains no line with more than
q
points. Since
M|{\rm cl}_{M}(X)
is in
\mathcal{M}_{q}
, it follows that each line in
{\rm cl}_{P_{r}}(L\cup e)
that contains at least two green points contains exactly two green points. It follows that
M
has
U_{3,q+2}
as an induced restriction, a contradiction.

We may now assume that
r(X)=k=r(Y)
. Since
(X,Y)
is an exact
k
-separation,
r(X)+r(Y)-r(M)=k-1
, so
r(M)=k+1
. As
M
has at least
2k
elements, it has an element
f
that is not a coloop. Then the construction of members of
\mathcal{M}_{q}
implies that
f
is on a
(q+1)
-point green line of
M
. This line must meet
{\rm cl}_{P_{r}}(X)\cap{\rm cl}_{P_{r}}(Y)
so
{\rm cl}_{P_{r}}(X)\cap{\rm cl}_{P_{r}}(Y)
is not entirely red, a contradiction.

We conclude
{\rm cl}_{M}(X)\cap{\rm cl}_{M}(Y)
contains at least one point, say
z
. In
M/z
, there is an exact vertical
(k-1)
-separation
(X^{\prime\prime},Y^{\prime\prime})
corresponding to
(X-z,Y-z)
. We deduce, by the minimality of
M
, that
{\rm cl}_{P_{r}/z}(X^{\prime\prime})\cap{\rm cl}_{P_{r}/z}(Y^{\prime\prime})
is a projective geometry of rank
k-2
. By Lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
, we deduce that
M|\left({\rm cl}_{M}(X)\cap{\rm cl}_{M}(Y)\right)
must have, as an induced minor, a matroid that is not in
\mathcal{M}_{q}
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

For all
k\geq 1
, an induced-minor-minimal non-
GF(q)
-chordal matroid has no vertical
k
-separations.

A matroid

is

if

has no two disjoint cocircuits. Equivalently,

for which

has a vertical
k
-separation (see, for example,

). The following result is immediate from the constructive definition of

-chordal matroids.

Lemma 3.7
.

A rank-

in

is round if and only if

.

The following is a straightforward consequence of the definition.

Lemma 3.8
.

A simple binary matroid is chordal if and only if it does not have

as an induced minor.

Lemma 3.9
.

All

Proof.

Let

-chordal matroids is closed under induced minors,

∎

Since

-chordal matroids.

4.
Dirac’s Other Characterization

In
[
8
]
, another characterization is given of chordal graphs. In a graph
G
, a
vertex separator
is a set of vertices whose deletion produces a graph with more connected components than
G
.

Theorem 4.1
.

A graph is chordal if and only if every minimal vertex separator induces a clique.

It is shown in Lemma
3.5
that, if
M
is a
GF(q)
-chordal matroid, then, for every exact vertical
k
-separation
(X,Y)
of
M
, the restriction
M|({\rm cl}(X)\cap{\rm cl}(Y))\cong P_{k-1}
. However, the converse of this is not true. For example, the matroid
P_{U_{2,3}}(M(K_{4}),M(K_{4}))
is not
GF(2)
-chordal, but the only exact vertical
k
-separation has
k=3
and has
U_{2,3}
as the intersection of the closures of the two sides of the vertical
3
-separation. A
divider
in a matroid is an exact vertical
k
-separation for some
k
. A divider
(X,Y)
is
minimal
if there is no vertical
k^{\prime}
-separation
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
such that
{\rm cl}(X^{\prime})\cap{\rm cl}(Y^{\prime})\subsetneqq{\rm cl}(X)\cap{\rm cl}(Y)
. Recall that, for sets
X
and
Y
in a matroid
M
, the
local connectivity
between
X
and
Y
, denoted
\sqcap(X,Y)
or
\sqcap_{M}(X,Y)
, is given by
\sqcap(X,Y)=r(X)+r(Y)-r(X\cup Y)
. Let
\mathcal{N}_{q}
be the class of
GF(q)
-representable matroids
N
such that, for every minimal divider
(X,Y)
of
N
, the matroid
N|({\rm cl}(X)\cap{\rm cl}(Y))
is a projective geometry of rank
\sqcap(X,Y)
. Since round matroids have no vertical
k
-separations, all
GF(q)
-round matroids are in
\mathcal{N}_{q}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(X,Y)
be a minimal divider of a matroid
N
. Then
{\rm cl}(X)\cap{\rm cl}(Y)={\rm cl}(X)\cap{\rm cl}(Y-{\rm cl}(X))
.

Proof.

Suppose
y\in{\rm cl}(X)\cap Y
. Then
r(Y)=r(Y-y)
; otherwise,
y
is a coloop of
N|Y
and
(X\cup y,Y-y)
is a divider with
{\rm cl}(X\cup y)\cap{\rm cl}(Y-y)
properly contained in
{\rm cl}(X)\cap{\rm cl}(Y)
, a contradiction. We deduce that
{\rm cl}(Y)={\rm cl}(Y-{\rm cl}(X))
.
∎

The next theorem is an analog of Theorem
4.1
.

Theorem 4.3
.

A matroid
M
is in
\mathcal{N}_{q}
if and only if
M
can be constructed from round
GF(q)
-representable matroids by a sequence of generalized parallel connections across projective geometries.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{q}
be the class of matroids that can be constructed from round
GF(q)
-representable matroids by a sequence of generalized parallel connections across projective geometries. It suffices to prove that a connected matroid
M\in\mathcal{N}_{q}
if and only if it is in
\mathcal{R}_{q}
. Suppose
M\in\mathcal{N}_{q}
. If
M
has no dividers, then
M
is round, so
M\in\mathcal{R}_{q}
.
Hence, we may assume
M
has a minimal divider
(X,Y)
. Then
M|({\rm cl}(X)\cap{\rm cl}(Y))
is a projective geometry
N
of rank
\sqcap(X,Y)
, and
M=P_{N}(M|{\rm cl}(X),M|{\rm cl}(Y))
. Letting
M_{X}=M|{\rm cl}(X)
, we see that
M_{X}
is either round or has a minimal divider
(U,V)
, where
M_{X}|({\rm cl}(U)\cap{\rm cl}(V))
is a projective geometry,
N^{\prime}
, of rank
\sqcap_{M_{X}}(U,V)
, and
M_{X}
is equal to
P_{N^{\prime}}(M_{X}|{\rm cl}(U),M_{X}|{\rm cl}(V))
. Continuing in this way, we see that every matroid in
\mathcal{N}_{q}
can be obtained in the manner prescribed. Hence
\mathcal{N}_{q}\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{q}
.

We will prove that
\mathcal{R}_{q}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{q}
by induction on the number
n
of round matroids used to construct a connected member
M
of
\mathcal{R}_{q}
. If
n=1
, then
M
is round and so
M
is in
\mathcal{N}_{q}
. Now suppose that the result holds when
n\leq t-1
and assume that
M
is constructed by using exactly
t
round matroids. Then
M\cong P_{N}(M_{1},M_{2})
, where
M_{2}
is a round matroid and
N
is a projective geometry. Let
(X,Y)
be a minimal divider of
M
and let
F={\rm cl}(X)\cap{\rm cl}(Y)
. We need to show that
M|F\cong P_{k}
where
r(F)=k
. Let
X_{N}=X\cap E(N)
and
Y_{N}=Y\cap E(N)
. Also let
X_{i}=(X\cap E(M_{i}))-X_{N}
and
Y_{i}=(Y\cap E(M_{i}))-Y_{N}
for each

in
\{1,2\}
. Since
N
is a projective geometry, we may suppose that
X_{N}
spans
Y_{N}
. Therefore
{\rm cl}(Y_{N})\subseteq F
. As
M_{2}
is round and has
(X_{N}\cup Y_{N}\cup X_{2},Y_{2})
as a partition, either
X_{N}\cup X_{2}
spans
Y_{2}
, or

spans
M_{2}
. In the latter case,

, so

. Now,
(E(M_{1}),E(M_{2})-E(N))
is a divider of

and
{\rm cl}(E(M_{1}))\cap{\rm cl}(E(M_{2})-E(N))=E(N)
. Because
(X,Y)
is a minimal divider, we have

, so

where

.

We deduce that
X_{N}\cup X_{2}
spans
Y_{2}
. Then
{\rm cl}(X)
contains
E(M_{2})
and, by Lemma
4.2
, we may assume that
Y\subseteq E(M_{1})-E(N)
. Thus

F={\rm cl}(X)\cap{\rm cl}(Y)={\rm cl}_{M_{1}}(X\cap E(M_{1}))\cap{\rm cl}_{M_{1}}(Y).

(4.1)

We show next that

is a minimal divider of

. It is a divider of

unless

or

.
In the exceptional case, as

does not span

, we see that

does not span

. Thus

spans
M_{1}
, so

.
Hence

, and

as desired. Thus
(X\cap E(M_{1}),Y)
is a divider of

. Suppose it is not minimal.
Then, by (

),

has a minimal divider

such that

. Now we may assume that

, so

∎

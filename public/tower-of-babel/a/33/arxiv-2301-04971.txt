Fully-dynamic risk measures: horizon risk, time-consistency, and relations with BSDEs and BSVIEs
Giulia Di Nunno, Emanuela Rosazza Gianin

Abstract
In a dynamic framework, we identify a new concept associated with the risk of assessing the financial exposure by a measure that is not adequate to the actual time horizon of the position. This will be called horizon risk . We clarify that dynamic risk measures are subject to horizon risk, so we propose to use the fully-dynamic version. To quantify horizon risk, we introduce h-longevity as an indicator. We investigate these notions together with other properties of risk measures as normalization, restriction property, and different formulations of time-consistency. We also consider these concepts for fully-dynamic risk measures generated by backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs), backward stochastic Volterra integral equations (BSVIEs), and families of these. Within this study, we provide new results for BSVIEs such as a converse comparison theorem and the dual representation of the associated risk measures. Keywords: Fully-dynamic risk measures, time-consistency, BSDEs, BSVIEs, converse comparison theorem for BSVIEs, dual representation, horizon risk, h-longevity MSC2020: 60H10, 60H20, 91B70, 91G70

Introduction

Monetary risk assessment spans across time horizons with different length, from the very short ones for trading operations to the decades-long ones typical of sovereign wealth or pension funds.
Hence the use of adequate risk measures across time as well as a time-consistent evaluation of risk are important factors in risk quantification and management.

We address the problem of using an appropriate risk evaluation for the given time horizon. To explain, it is not correct to use a risk measure designed for long term positions to evaluate risks that occur in the short term.
This is considered a “common error” in risk quantification by some quants, see e.g.
[
12
]
.
Indeed, bearing in mind that the purpose of risk assessment and management is not avoiding risk, but giving the rightful possibility to invest in risky assets with a reasonable control on what is acceptable to the investor, then it is clear that this form of “horizon risk” should be tackled.
This is particularly evident in life insurance with an impact on pension funds and health insurance. For example, a wrong estimate of the individual remaining lifetime brings to a wrong use of the associated mortality tables and, consequently, of the risk of an underestimation of insurance premia and, by domino effect, a wrong estimation of capital requirements and their associated financial management.
In
this work, we identify
horizon risk
and we propose a way to quantify this via the
horizon longevity
,
or
h-longevity
in short.
For this we work with the general framework of fully-dynamic risk measures that embeds the classical dynamic risk measures. Indeed,
a fully-dynamic risk measure
is a family of risk measures
(\rho_{su})_{s,u}\triangleq(\rho_{su})_{0\leq s\leq u\leq T}

(\text{with }T<\infty)
indexed by two time parameters, the first represents the evaluation time, the second the horizon to which the risk pertains. Instead,
a dynamic risk measure
is a family of risk measures
(\rho_{s})_{s}\triangleq(\rho_{s})_{0\leq s\leq T}
indexed by only one time parameter representing the time of risk evaluation.
So we have that

\rho_{s}=\rho_{sT}.

(1)

Whenever we consider a risk
X
occurring at time
t\in[0,T]
(hence
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable for some given information flow), we can consider different risk evaluations at
s
, either
\rho_{st}(X)
or
\rho_{su}(X)
, for any other horizon
u>t
(even much later than
t
), since
\mathcal{F}_{t}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{u}
.
Horizon longevity penalizes the use of the “wrong” risk measure, that is the one that does not pertain to the right time horizon.

The impact of the length of the time horizon on risk evaluation has already been detected in the work
[
16
]
on epistemic uncertainty. There, it comes out that ambiguity on the model choice is growing with the time horizon. While
[
16
]
is not related with our considerations, it shows anyhow that the time horizon effects the precision on risk quantification.

Another concept under investigation in this paper is
time-consistency
, which has different formulations and it plays an important role in dynamic risk evaluation. See e.g.
[
1
,
4
,
6
,
7
]
.

In our study we highlight that both h-longevity and time-consistency are linked to the
restriction property
(see
[
7
]
), that is

\rho_{st}(X)=\rho_{su}(X),\qquad\text{for}\;\mathcal{F}_{t}\text{-measurable}\;X,\quad u\geq t.

(2)

Indeed, the presence of restriction induces that the different formulations of time-consistency are equivalent, while the absence of restriction provides the very possibility to introduce h-longevity.
The restriction property is naturally satisfied by dynamic risk measures
(\rho_{s})_{s}
(see (
1
)) and this may be a reason for which the horizon risk has not been earlier identified in its own being and hence quantified. Similarly, also
normalization
, i.e.

\rho_{su}(0)=0,

(3)

is discovered to play a crucial role in the relationships among the concepts above. Note that normalization is often standardly assumed in many financial risk evaluations. However, it is questionable in the evaluation of environmental strategies, namely a non-action (zero investment) can carry a positive risk. Note that, by ESG regulations, these positions and their entailed risks have to be reported.

In the first part of this paper, we work in full generality with an axiomatic setting. In the second part we concentrate on risk measures induced by backward dynamics.
We focus on the Brownian framework (see, e.g.,
[
3
,
18
,
22
,
25
]
) and use a BSDE with driver
g
to generate a fully-dynamic risk measure

(\rho_{su})_{s,u}
. Then we study how the properties of the driver are connected to the concepts of time-consistency, h-longevity, restriction, and normalization.

Since a fully-dynamic risk measure
(\rho_{su})_{s,u}
depends on the horizons
u\in[0,T]
, then we can also generate it from a
family
of BSDEs with drivers
\mathcal{G}=(g_{u})_{u}
. In this way it is emphasized that each period
[s,u]
is associated to the BSDE with driver
g_{u}
providing the risk evaluation
\rho_{su}
.
In this framework we investigate again the relationships among the concepts of interest with the family of drivers. In this context we prove a Comparison Theorem for BSDEs with different time horizons useful for the study of h-longevity.

Recently, backward stochastic Volterra integral equations (BSVIEs) have been suggested to generate dynamic risk measures, see
[
29
,
2
]
. However, at that stage, some important results on BSVIEs were still lacking. Here we provide a dual representation of risk measures induced by BSVIEs and a converse comparison theorem for BSVIEs. With these two results we have a full picture of the relationship between the properties of the driver and those of the corresponding risk measures as well as a comprehensive study of time-consistency and horizon longevity in the Volterra setting.

We recall that a BSVIE is induced by a family of BSDEs, see
[
30
]
. This has been a further motivation to study fully-dynamic risk measures induced by a BSVIE with Volterra driver
g=g(t,s,\cdot)
,
s\geq t
. As a final stage we have also considered
families
of BSVIEs with Volterra drivers
\mathcal{G}=(g_{u})_{u}
again to emphasize the role of the time horizon in the risk evaluation.

Working with families of backward equations has made evident the surprisingly crucial role of the restriction property in risk measurement across time. For example, it turns out that the only way to have a strong (recursive) time-consistency is to work with fully-dynamic risk measures induced by a single standard BSDE.

Summarizing, in this work we have first identified horizon risk in its own being and proposed one way to quantify it. We have hence considered fully-dynamic risk measures and studied the different forms of time-consistency and h-longevity, showing how the restriction property is actually playing a crucial role in these matters. Our work has covered both the general axiomatic approach and the risk measures generated by backward dynamics, both single and in families, both of standard and of Volterra type.
In this we have seen how the properties of the drivers reflect the properties of the fully-dynamic risk measures in respect of the risk evaluations across time. In between we have also provided results on BSDE and BSVIEs of independent interest.

2
Fully-dynamic risk measures, time-consistency, and horizon risk

In the sequel, we will focus on fully-dynamic risk measures that have been recently introduced by Bion-Nadal and Di Nunno
[
7
]
. See also
[
5
]
, where this concept was actually simply called dynamic risk measure.

Definition 1

A
fully-dynamic risk measure
is a family
(\rho_{st})_{s,t}=(\rho_{st})_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T}
of risk measures indexed by two time parameters

\rho_{st}:L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})\to L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{s}),\mbox{ with }p\in[1,+\infty],

that for all
X,Y\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
are:

-

monotone:
\rho_{st}(X)\leq\rho_{st}(Y)
for
X\geq Y
,

-

convex:
\rho_{st}(\lambda X+(1-\lambda)Y)\leq\lambda\rho_{st}(X)+(1-\lambda)\rho_{st}(Y)
for all
\lambda\in[0,1]
,

-

\mathcal{F}_{s}
-translation invariant (cash-invariant):
\rho_{st}(X+Y)=\rho_{st}(X)-Y
for all
Y\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{s})

and, for
p=\infty
,

-

continuous from below: if
X_{n}\uparrow X
as
n\to+\infty
, then
\rho_{st}(X_{n})\to\rho_{st}(X)
in
L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{s})
, as
n\to+\infty
.

In the definition above, continuity from below is assumed only in the case where
p=\infty
.
In fact, for any
p\in[1,\infty)
, it is implied by the other assumptions, see
[
7
, Remark 2.5]
. Furthermore, fully-dynamic risk measures satisfy
weak
\mathcal{F}_{s}
-homogeneity
, i.e.

1_{A}\rho_{st}(X)=1_{A}\rho_{st}(1_{A}X),\quad\mbox{ for any }X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t}),A\in\mathcal{F}_{s}

(see
[
7
, Remark 2.6]
) and have the following dual representations

\displaystyle\rho_{st}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{st}}{\operatorname{ess.\!max}}\;\{E_{Q}[-X|\mathcal{F}_{s}]-\alpha_{st}(Q)\}

(4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\ll P:\\
E_{P}[\alpha_{st}(Q)]<\infty\end{subarray}}{\operatorname{ess.\!max}}\{E_{Q}[-X|\mathcal{F}_{s}]-\alpha_{st}(Q)\}

(5)

where

\mathcal{Q}_{st}\triangleq\left\{Q\mbox{ on }\mathcal{F}_{t}:Q\ll P\mbox{ and }Q|_{\mathcal{F}_{s}}\equiv P|_{\mathcal{F}_{s}}\right\}

(6)

and

\alpha_{st}(Q)\triangleq\underset{X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})}{\operatorname{ess.\!sup}}\;\{E_{Q}[-X|\mathcal{F}_{s}]-\rho_{st}(X)\}

(7)

is the
minimal penalty functional
. See
[
7
, Proposition 2.8]
for details.

We stress that, in general, the risk measures in the family of the fully dynamic risk measure are not normalized, i.e.
\rho_{st}(0)\neq 0
, and the family does not satisfy the restriction property, that is
\rho_{rt}(Y)\neq\rho_{rs}(Y)
for
Y\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{s})
, for
s\leq t
.

In view of the dynamic nature of the risk evaluation, we consider the relationships among the inter-temporal evaluations given by the fully-dynamic risk measure.
For this, we recall here below the notions of time-consistency mostly used in the literature and their connections expressed in the present setting. See, e.g.,
[
1
,
4
,
6
]
, among others.

Definition 2

•

Strong time-consistency
(or
recursivity
): for any
s,t,u\in[0,T]
with
s\leq t\leq u
,

\rho_{st}(-\rho_{tu}(X))=\rho_{su}(X)\quad\mbox{ for any }X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{u}).

(8)

•

Order time-consistency
: for any
s,t,u\in[0,T]
with
s\leq t\leq u
,

\rho_{tu}(X)=\rho_{tu}(Y),\quad X,Y\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{u})\Longrightarrow\rho_{su}(X)=\rho_{su}(Y).

(9)

•

Weak time-consistency
: for any
s,t,u\in[0,T]
with
s\leq t\leq u
,

\rho_{su}(\rho_{tu}(0)-\rho_{tu}(X))=\rho_{su}(X)\quad\mbox{ for any }X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{u}).

(10)

Observe that strong time-consistency is equivalent to the
cocycle condition
on the minimal penalties:

\alpha_{su}(Q)=\alpha_{st}(Q|_{\mathcal{F}_{t}})+E_{Q}[\alpha_{tu}(Q)|\mathcal{F}_{s}],\quad\forall s,t,u,Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{su},

(11)

together with the
m-stability
of the Radon-Nykodym derivatives associated to the corresponding sets of measures
(\mathcal{Q}_{st})_{s,t}
, i.e. the probability measure
S
defined by

\frac{dS}{dP}=\frac{dQ}{dP}\,\frac{dR}{dP},\quad\textrm{for all }Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{st},R\in\mathcal{Q}_{tu}

(12)

belongs to
\mathcal{Q}_{su}
.
See
[
5
, Theorem 2.5]
.
The operation in (
12
) is called
pasting
of probability measures, also shortly denoted
Q\cdot R
.
Naturally, any measure
S\in\mathcal{Q}_{su}
admits a representation in the form (
12
). In fact, we have

\frac{dS}{dP}=\frac{dS|_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}}{dP}\,\frac{dR}{dP}\quad\textrm{with }\frac{dR}{dP}=\frac{\frac{dS}{dP}}{E_{P}\big[\frac{dS}{dP}|\mathcal{F}_{t}\big]}1_{A}+1_{A^{c}}

where
A\triangleq\big\{\omega\in\Omega:E_{P}\big[\frac{dS}{dP}|\mathcal{F}_{t}\big](\omega)>0\big\}
.

We recall from Proposition 2.13 and Corollary 2.14 of Bion-Nadal and Di Nunno
[
7
]
that, for fully-dynamic risk measures, strong time-consistency implies order time-consistency; furthermore, for
normalized
fully-dynamic risk measures strong time-consistency is equivalent to the restriction property plus order time-consistency.

It is worth emphasizing that, differently from strong time-consistency, the formulation of weak time-consistency is in terms of risk measures with the same time horizon
u
.
The notion of weak time-consistency is motivated by the discussion done by Bion-Nadal and Di Nunno
[
7
]
, where it was shown that risk indifference pricing does not satisfy strong time-consistency, but only a weaker version.

Notice that under the additional assumption of normalization (
3
), weak time-consistency (
10
) becomes

\rho_{su}(-\rho_{tu}(X))=\rho_{su}(X)\quad\mbox{ for any }X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{u}),

(13)

while, under both normalization and the restriction property, weak time-consistency (
10
) reduces to the classical strong time-consistency (
8
).

Again from
[
7
]
and from the arguments above, it emerges that the restriction property has not only an extremely important role on time-consistency but also on risk measures. Indeed, once the restriction property is dropped, we can explicitly quantify
the increasing cost of riskiness for longer time horizons.

Definition 3

A fully-dynamic risk measures
(\rho_{st})_{s,t}=(\rho_{st})_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T}
is said to take into account
Horizon longevity
(or
h-longevity
, for short) if for any fixed
s\in[0,T]
,

\rho_{st}(X)\leq\rho_{su}(X)\quad\mbox{ for any }0\leq t\leq u,\;X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t}).

(14)

Naturally, a risk measure satisfying restriction property (see (
2
)) trivially satisfies h-longevity. Then we talk about
strict h-longevity
to exclude this trivial case.

Differently from weak time-consistency (
10
) that has an impact on the first time parameter, horizon longevity (
14
) focuses on the behavior of the second time parameter, that is the time horizon. Quantifying horizon risk is particularly important in life insurance and pension funds, as reported in the introduction of this work.

Our aim is to investigate weak time-consistency and h-longevity on fully-dynamic risk measures
(\rho_{st})_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T}
from an axiomatic point of view as well as their impact on the relation between fully-dynamic risk measures and BSDEs or BSVIEs.

2.1
Time-consistency

The next results focus on weak time-consistency and on its characterizations.
Also we introduce the new concept of
sub time-consistency
, see Proposition
5
, which will turn out to interplay with h-longevity.

Proposition 4

A fully-dynamic risk measure
(\rho_{st})_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T}
is weakly time-consistent if and only if it satisfies order time-consistency.

Proof.
Assume order time-consistency. Let
X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{u})
and
t\leq u
be arbitrary. By
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-translation invariance of
\rho_{tu}
it follows

\rho_{tu}(X)=\rho_{tu}(0-\rho_{tu}(X))-\rho_{tu}(0)=\rho_{tu}(\rho_{tu}(0)-\rho_{tu}(X)).

(15)

Since
Y\triangleq\rho_{tu}(0)-\rho_{tu}(X)\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
, (
15
) and order time-consistency imply that
\rho_{su}(X)=\rho_{su}(Y)
for any
s\leq t
. The thesis is therefore proved.

Conversely, suppose that
\rho_{tu}(X)=\rho_{tu}(Y)
holds for some
X,Y\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{u})
and
t\leq u
. By weak time-consistency it follows that

\rho_{su}(X)=\rho_{su}(\rho_{tu}(0)-\rho_{tu}(X))=\rho_{su}(\rho_{tu}(0)-\rho_{tu}(Y))=\rho_{su}(Y)

for any
s\leq t
.

Naturally, since strong time-consistency implies order time-consistency, the result above gives that strong time-consistency implies also weak time-consistency.

, then

\rho_{st}(-\rho_{tu}(X))\leq\rho_{su}(X)\quad\mbox{ for any }X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{u}),

(26)

called sub (strong) time-consistency in the following. Notice that
\rho_{tu}(0)\leq 0
is equivalent to
\operatorname{ess.\!inf}_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{tu}}\alpha_{tu}(Q)\geq 0
.

On the other hand, sub (strong) time-consistency (
26
) together with
\rho_{tu}(0)\geq 0
, for any
t,u
, imply h-longevity. For any
\bar{X}\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
, indeed,

\rho_{st}(\bar{X})\leq\rho_{st}(\bar{X}-\rho_{tu}(0))=\rho_{st}(-\rho_{tu}(\bar{X}))\leq\rho_{su}(\bar{X}),

where the first inequality is due to
\rho_{tu}(0)\geq 0
and monotonicity, the last to sub time-consistency, while the equality follows by
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-translation invariance.

Proceeding similarly to Acciaio and Penner
[
1
]
, it is easy to prove the relation between sub time-consistency and acceptance sets for fully-dynamic risk measures, here formulated as follows.

Proposition 7

For any fully-dynamic risk measure
(\rho_{st})_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T}
the following are equivalent:

(i)
Sub (strong) time-consistency, that is
\rho_{st}(-\rho_{tu}(X))\leq\rho_{su}(X)
for any
X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{u})
,
s\leq t\leq u
;

(ii)

\mathcal{A}_{su}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{st}+\mathcal{A}_{tu}
where
\mathcal{A}_{su}\triangleq\{Z\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{u}):\rho_{su}(Z)\leq 0,P\mbox{-a.s.}\}
is the acceptance set associated to
\rho_{su}
;

(iii)

\alpha_{su}(Q)\leq\alpha_{st}(Q|_{\mathcal{F}_{t}})+E_{Q}[\alpha_{tu}(Q)|\mathcal{F}_{s}]
for any
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{su}
,
s\leq t\leq u
.

2.2
Horizon risk and h-longevity

We recall that h-longevity (
14
) has been formulated as

\rho_{st}(X)\leq\rho_{su}(X)\quad\mbox{ for any }0\leq s\leq t\leq u,X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t}).

From the formulation of h-longevity it is clear that the risk measure with longer horizon
\rho_{su}
is relevant only restricted on
L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
. In other words, longevity aims to penalize the risk measurement of a position done with the wrong risk measure, i.e. with the risk measure suitable for a longer horizon.

For this reason, in the sequel, we denote the restriction of
\rho_{su}
on
L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
by
\bar{\rho}^{t}_{su}
, while
\bar{\alpha}^{t}_{su}
is the corresponding minimal penalty function, i.e.

\bar{\alpha}^{t}_{su}(Q)=\underset{X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})}{\operatorname{ess.\!sup}}\;\{E_{Q}[-X|\mathcal{F}_{s}]-\bar{\rho}^{t}_{su}(X)\},\quad Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{st}.

It then follows that

\bar{\alpha}^{t}_{su}(Q_{|\mathcal{F}_{t}})\leq\alpha_{su}(Q),\qquad Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{su}.

In fact,

\displaystyle\alpha_{su}(Q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{u})}{\operatorname{ess.\!sup}}\{E_{Q}[-|X\mathcal{F}_{s}]-\rho_{su}(X)\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\underset{X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})}{\operatorname{ess.\!sup}}\{E_{Q}[-X|\mathcal{F}_{s}]-\bar{\rho}^{t}_{su}(X)\}=\bar{\alpha}^{t}_{su}(\left.Q\right|_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}).

Using the notation above, h-longevity naturally leads to

\bar{\rho}^{t}_{su}(X)=\rho_{su}(X)=\rho_{st}(X)+\gamma(s,t,u,X)

(27)

for any
0\leq s\leq t\leq u
,
X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
, and
for a suitable
\mathcal{F}_{s}
-measurable
\gamma(s,t,u,X)\geq 0
that may depend on the position
X
, on the time of evaluation
s
, on the time parameter
t
referring to the measurability of
X
, and on the “wrong” time horizon
u
used for evaluating
X
.
The term
\gamma
can be then seen as an indicator quantifying the horizon risk or, roughly speaking, as an additive term of adjustment/calibration.
It follows that, for any
s\leq t\leq u
and
X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
,
\gamma(s,t,t,X)=0
and, by translation invariance of the fully-dynamic risk measure, that
\gamma(s,t,u,X+c_{s})=\gamma(s,t,u,X)
for any
c_{s}\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{s})
.
The restriction property (
2
) is then equivalent to
\gamma(s,t,u,X)=0
for all
0\leq s\leq t\leq u
,
X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
.

The following result characterizes longevity in terms of acceptance sets and of penalty functions.

Proposition 8

For a fully dynamic risk measure
(\rho_{st})_{s,t}
:

(a) longevity is equivalent to
\mathcal{A}_{su}\cap L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{st}
for any
s\leq t\leq u
.

(b) restriction is equivalent to
\mathcal{A}_{su}\cap L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})=\mathcal{A}_{st}
for any
s\leq t\leq u
.

(c) longevity implies that
\alpha_{st}(Q)\geq\bar{\alpha}^{t}_{su}(Q)
and also

\bar{\alpha}^{t}_{su}(Q)\geq\alpha_{st}(Q)-\operatorname{ess.\!sup}_{X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})}\gamma(s,t,u,X),

for any
s\leq t\leq u
and
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{st}
.

(d) restriction implies that
\alpha_{st}(Q)=\bar{\alpha}^{t}_{su}(Q)
for any
s\leq t\leq u
and
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{st}
.

Proof.
(a) For any
X\in\mathcal{A}_{su}\cap L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
it follows that
\rho_{su}(X)\leq 0
and, by longevity, also that
\rho_{st}(X)\leq 0
. Hence
\mathcal{A}_{su}\cap L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{st}
.
Conversely, from the representation of risk measures in terms of acceptance sets and from the condition on acceptance sets, it follows that, for any
X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
,

\displaystyle\rho_{su}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{ess.\!inf}\{m_{s}\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{s})|\;m_{s}+X\in\mathcal{A}_{su}\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\operatorname{ess.\!inf}\{m_{s}\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{s})|\;m_{s}+X\in\mathcal{A}_{st}\}=\rho_{st}(X).

(b) Assume now restriction. From (
27
), we see that restriction can be interpreted as h-longevity with
\gamma(s,t,u,X)=0
. Hence, from (a), it remains to prove the reverse inclusion for acceptance sets.
For any
X\in\mathcal{A}_{st}
(hence
X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
) we have that
\rho_{st}(X)\leq 0
. By restriction,
\rho_{su}(X)=\rho_{st}(X)\leq 0
, hence
\mathcal{A}_{su}\cap L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})\supseteq\mathcal{A}_{st}
.
Conversely, assume
\mathcal{A}_{su}\cap L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})=\mathcal{A}_{st}
. From the representation of risk measures in terms of acceptance sets, we obtain that, for any
X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
,

\displaystyle\rho_{su}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{ess.\!inf}\{m_{s}\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{s})|\>m_{s}+X\in\mathcal{A}_{su}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{ess.\!inf}\{m_{s}\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{s})|\>m_{s}+X\in\mathcal{A}_{st}\}=\rho_{st}(X).

(c) Proceeding similarly to Föllmer and Schied
[
15
]
, Thm. 4.16, by translation invariance of
\rho_{su}
, we have

\alpha_{st}(Q)=\underset{X\in\mathcal{A}_{st}}{\operatorname{ess.\!sup}}\,E_{Q}[-X|\mathcal{F}_{s}]

for any
s\leq t
and
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{st}
.
By (a), h-longevity and translation invariance imply that, for any
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{st}
,

\displaystyle\alpha_{st}(Q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{X\in\mathcal{A}_{st}}{\operatorname{ess.\!sup}}\,E_{Q}[-X|\mathcal{F}_{s}]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\underset{X\in\mathcal{A}_{su}\cap L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})}{\operatorname{ess.\!sup}}E_{Q}[-X|\mathcal{F}_{s}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t}):\\
\rho_{su}(X)\leq 0\end{subarray}}{\operatorname{ess.\!sup}}E_{Q}[-X|\mathcal{F}_{s}]=\bar{\alpha}^{t}_{su}(Q).

Moreover, in terms of the indicator
\gamma
in (
27
), we have that

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}^{t}_{su}(Q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})}{\operatorname{ess.\!sup}}\{E_{Q}[-X|\mathcal{F}_{s}]-\bar{\rho}^{t}_{su}(X)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})}{\operatorname{ess.\!sup}}\{E_{Q}[-X|\mathcal{F}_{s}]-\rho_{st}(X)-\gamma(s,t,u,X)\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\underset{X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})}{\operatorname{ess.\!sup}}\{E_{Q}[-X|\mathcal{F}_{s}]-\rho_{st}(X)\}-\underset{X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})}{\operatorname{ess.\!sup}}\gamma(s,t,u,X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{st}(Q)-\underset{X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})}{\operatorname{ess.\!sup}}\gamma(s,t,u,X).

(d) By the equivalence between restriction and the condition on acceptance sets in (b), it follows that for any
Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{st}

\displaystyle\alpha_{st}(Q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{X\in\mathcal{A}_{st}}{\operatorname{ess.\!sup}}\,E_{Q}[-X|\mathcal{F}_{s}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{X\in\mathcal{A}_{su}\cap L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})}{\operatorname{ess.\!sup}}\,E_{Q}[-X|\mathcal{F}_{s}]=\bar{\alpha}^{t}_{su}(Q).

The proof is then complete.

With (c), we see that the greater is
\operatorname{ess.\!sup}_{X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})}\gamma(s,t,u,X)
for
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable positions, the lower is the lower bound
\alpha_{st}(\left.Q\right|_{\mathcal{F}_{t}})-\operatorname{ess.\!sup}_{X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})}\gamma(s,t,u,X)
of
\alpha_{su}(Q)
. The term
\operatorname{ess.\!sup}_{X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})}\gamma(s,t,u,X)
can be then interpreted as the maximal error (at the level of the penalty function) for a wrong use of
\rho_{su}
for positions
X
belonging to
L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
, that is,
\rho_{s\cdot}
with a wrong time horizon.

From a motivational point of view, one can imagine
\gamma
dependent on the time horizon, that is,
\gamma(s,t,u,X)=\gamma_{s,t}(h,X)
with
h=u-t
or, even,
\gamma_{s,t}(h)
independent from
X
. In particular,
h=u-t
can be interpreted as the length of the time interval over which there is an uncorrect use of the risk measure (
\rho_{su}
versus
\rho_{st}
). See Example
12
and Example
26
.

3
Relation with BSDEs

Hereafter, we consider a fully-dynamic risk measure induced by a single BSDE with driver
g
and also measures generated by a family of BSDEs associated to the drivers
\mathcal{G}=(g_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
where the index

We will restrict our attention to

On the probability space

According to Peng

In our work, we consider a driver

•

adapted,

•

denotes the Euclidean norm in

•

.

Under these conditions, equation (

, where we have set

\mathbb{H}^{2}_{[a,b]}(\mathbb{R}^{k})\hskip-2.84526pt\triangleq\hskip-2.84526pt\Big\{\mbox{adapted }\mathbb{R}^{k}\mbox{-valued processes }(\eta_{s})_{s\in[a,b]}:\hskip-2.84526ptE\Big[\hskip-2.84526pt\int_{a}^{b}\hskip-5.69054pt|\eta_{s}|^{2}\,ds\Big]\hskip-2.84526pt<\hskip-2.84526pt\infty\Big\}.

For further details see, e.g., El Karoui et al.
[
13
]
.

In Peng
[

]
and Rosazza Gianin
[

If

.
Also

[

[

Beyond the Lipschitz condition on the driver, the connection between BSDEs and risk measures has been studied in terms of maximal solutions of BSDEs (see, e.g., Barrieu and El Karoui

3.1
Risk measures generated by a single BSDE

In this work we have assumed cash-invariant risk measures, then we consider

To simplify the notation, we will occasionally adopt the Peng’s notation where

Proposition 9

.

Proof.
Let
s\leq t\leq u
and
X\in L^{p}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
. The risk measures

\rho_{st}(X)
and
\rho_{su}(X)
satisfy the following BSDEs:

\displaystyle\rho_{st}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-X+\int_{s}^{t}g(v,Z_{v}^{t})dv-\int_{s}^{t}Z_{v}^{t}dB_{v}

\displaystyle\rho_{su}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-X+\int_{s}^{u}g(v,Z_{v}^{u})dv-\int_{s}^{u}Z_{v}^{u}dB_{v},

respectively. Set now the
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued process

\bar{Z}_{v}^{t}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}Z_{v}^{t};&v\leq t\\
0;&t<v\leq u\end{array}\right.;\quad\widetilde{Z}_{v}=Z_{v}^{u}-\bar{Z}_{v}^{t}.

Then

\displaystyle\rho_{su}(X)-\rho_{st}(X)

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{u}\hskip-2.84526pt[g(v,Z_{v}^{u})-g(v,\bar{Z}_{v}^{t})]dv+\hskip-2.84526pt\int_{t}^{u}\hskip-2.84526ptg(v,\bar{Z}_{v}^{t})dv-\hskip-2.84526pt\int_{s}^{u}\hskip-2.84526pt[Z_{v}^{u}-\bar{Z}_{v}^{t}]dB_{v}-\hskip-2.84526pt\int_{t}^{u}\hskip-2.84526pt\bar{Z}_{v}^{t}dB_{v}

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{u}[g(v,Z_{v}^{u})-g(v,\bar{Z}_{v}^{t})]dv-\int_{s}^{u}\widetilde{Z}_{v}dB_{v}+\int_{t}^{u}g(v,0)dv

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{u}\Delta_{z}g(v)\cdot\widetilde{Z}_{v}dv-\int_{s}^{u}\widetilde{Z}_{v}dB_{v}+\int_{t}^{u}g(v,0)dv.

(31)

Furthermore, (
31
) can be rewritten also as

\delta\rho_{s}=\Gamma^{t,u}+\int_{s}^{u}\Delta_{z}g(v)\cdot\widetilde{Z}_{v}dv-\int_{s}^{u}\widetilde{Z}_{v}dB_{v},

(32)

where
\delta\rho_{s}\triangleq\rho_{su}(X)-\rho_{st}(X)
and
\Gamma^{t,u}\triangleq\int_{t}^{u}g(v,0)dv
represents the final condition at time
u
(which depends on
t
but not on
s
) of the linear BSDE (
32
).

Since
\Gamma^{t,u}\geq 0
for any
t
by hypothesis and
\Delta_{z}g(v)\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{[s,u]}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by the assumption of
g
Lipschitz in
z
, by Prop. 2.2 of El Karoui, Peng and Quenez
[
13
]
it follows that
\delta\rho_{s}\geq 0
for any
s\leq t
.

By applying Girsanov Theorem, (
31
) becomes

\displaystyle\rho_{su}(X)-\rho_{st}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{u}\Delta_{z}g(v)\cdot\widetilde{Z}_{v}dv-\int_{s}^{u}\widetilde{Z}_{v}dB_{v}+\int_{t}^{u}g(v,0)dv

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{s}^{u}\widetilde{Z}_{v}dB^{\widetilde{Q}_{X}}_{v}+\int_{t}^{u}g(v,0)dv,

where
B^{\widetilde{Q}_{X}}_{v}\triangleq B_{v}-B_{s}-\int_{s}^{v}\Delta_{z}g(r)\,dr
,
v\in[s,u]
, is a
\widetilde{Q}_{X}
-Brownian motion. Hence, by taking the conditional expectation with respect to
\widetilde{Q}_{X}
,

\displaystyle\rho_{su}(X)-\rho_{st}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\widetilde{Q}_{X}}\left[-\int_{s}^{u}\widetilde{Z}_{v}dB^{\widetilde{Q}_{X}}_{v}+\int_{t}^{u}g(v,0)dv\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\widetilde{Q}_{X}}\left[\int_{t}^{u}g(v,0)dv\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right].

By assumption on
g(\cdot,0)
, it follows that
\rho_{su}(X)-\rho_{st}(X)\geq 0
and that
\gamma(s,t,u,X)=\rho_{su}(X)-\rho_{st}(X)=E_{\widetilde{Q}_{X}}\left[\int_{t}^{u}g(v,0)dv|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]
.

As discussed in Section
2.2
,
\gamma
may depend on the time horizon, that is,
\gamma(s,t,u,X)=\gamma_{s,t}(h,X)
with
h=u-t
or, even,
\gamma_{s,t}(h)
independent from
X
. The following example provides some cases covering the situation above.

Example 12

Let
g(v,0)\geq 0
for any
v\in[0,T]
. Hence, by the result above, h-longevity holds and
\gamma(s,t,u,X)=E_{\widetilde{Q}_{X}}\left[\int_{t}^{u}g(v,0)dv|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]
for any
X\in L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
.

a) If
g(v,0)=c
for any
v\in[0,T]
, with
c\geq 0
, then
c
is necessarily deterministic (since it should be measurable for any
v\geq 0
) and, consequently,

\gamma(s,t,u,X)=E_{\widetilde{Q}_{X}}\left[\int_{t}^{u}g(v,0)dv\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]=(u-t)c.

Hence
\gamma
only depends on
h=u-t
, that is, roughly speaking, on the length of the time interval over which there is an uncorrect use of the risk measure (
\rho_{su}
versus
\rho_{st}
).

b) If
g(v,0)=\exp(-r\,v)
for any
v\in[0,T]
, with
r\geq 0
, then
r
is necessarily deterministic (for the same arguments as above) and, consequently,

\gamma(s,t,u,X)=\frac{e^{-rt}\left(1-e^{-r(u-t)}\right)}{r}

In other words,
\gamma
only depends on the “right” time horizon
t
(referring to the measurability of
X
) and on the length of the time interval
[t,u]
.

3.2
Risk measures generated by a family of BSDEs

Now we consider general risk measures induced by a family of BSDEs of type (
28
) with drivers
\mathcal{G}=(g_{u})_{u\in[0,T]}
depending on the time horizon
u
of
\rho_{tu}
.
While the focus of this paper is to work with BSDEs where the driver is independent on
y
(leading to cash-invariant risk measures), we still introduce some concepts and results for the general case which serves the cash-subadditive case of El Karoui and Ravanelli
[
14
]
.

For later use, by
increasing family

\mathcal{G}=(g_{u})_{u\in[0,T]}
of BSDE drivers it is meant, for any
t\leq u
,

g_{t}(v,y,z)\leq g_{u}(v,y,z)\mbox{ for any }v\in[0,t],y\in\mathbb{R},z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Suppose that, for any
t\leq u
, the risk measure
\rho_{tu}
comes from a
g_{u}
-expectation with a driver depending on the maturity
u
. This means that

\rho_{tu}(X)=\rho_{tu}^{\mathcal{G}}(X)=\mathcal{E}^{g_{u}}(-X|\mathcal{F}_{t}),\mbox{ for any }X\in L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{u}).

(33)

Assume now that
(g_{u})_{u\in[0,T]}
is a family of drivers depending on the maturity
u
, independent of
y
, Lipschitz, and convex in
z
. Then the risk measure
(\rho^{\mathcal{G}}_{tu})_{t,u}
defined by (
29
), (
33
) satisfies monotonicity, convexity, continuity from above/below, and translation invariance. Furthermore, for
u\in[0,T]
, if
g_{u}(v,0)=0
for any
v\leq u
, then
\rho^{\mathcal{G}}_{tu}(0)=0
for any
t\leq u
. In general, however,
g_{u}(v,0)=0
for any
v\leq u

does not
imply the restriction property.

In a Brownian setting,
\rho_{tu}^{\mathcal{G}}
can be represented as

\rho_{tu}(X)=\rho_{tu}^{\mathcal{G}}(X)=\underset{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{tu}}{\operatorname{ess.\!sup}}\,\left\{E_{Q}\left[-X|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]-\alpha_{tu}^{\mathcal{G}}(Q)\right\},\quad X\in L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{u}),

where
\mathcal{Q}_{tu}
is defined in (
6
)
and the penalty term
\alpha_{tu}^{\mathcal{G}}
associated to
\rho_{tu}^{\mathcal{G}}
is given by

\alpha_{tu}^{\mathcal{G}}(Q)=E_{Q}\left[\left.\int_{t}^{u}g^{*}_{u}(v,q_{v})dv\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right],

where
g^{*}_{u}
denotes the convex conjugate of the function
g_{u}
. See Delbaen et al.
[
10
]
.

The following result shows that, for
g_{u}(r,0)=0
for any
r,u
, the restriction property is satisfied only for risk measures induced by a BSDE with a single driver
g
(that is, constant with respect to the maturity time).

Proposition 13

For any
u\in[0,T]
, let
g_{u}(v,0)=0
for any
v\leq u
.

The restriction property (
2
) holds if and only if
g_{u}
is constant in
u
.

Proof.
If
g_{u}
is constant in
u
, the restriction property follows directly by Proposition
9
.

Conversely, assume that the restriction property holds, i.e.
\rho_{tu}(X)=\rho_{tv}(X)
for any
t\leq u\leq v
and
X\in L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{u})
.
Proceeding as in the proof of the Converse Comparison Theorem of Briand et al.
[
8
]
, Thm. 4.1, and Jiang
[
18
]
, Lemma 2.1,

\displaystyle g_{u}(s,z)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\rho_{su}(-z\cdot(B_{s+\varepsilon}-B_{\varepsilon}))}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle g_{v}(s,z)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\rho_{sv}(-z\cdot(B_{s+\varepsilon}-B_{\varepsilon}))}{\varepsilon}

with convergence in
L^{p}
with
p\in[1,2)
, for any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
u\leq v
and
s\in[0,u]
. By extracting a subsequence to obtain convergence
P
-a.s. and passing to the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
, it holds that

\frac{\rho_{sv}(-z(B_{s+\varepsilon}-B_{\varepsilon}))}{\varepsilon}=\frac{\rho_{su}(-z(B_{s+\varepsilon}-B_{\varepsilon}))}{\varepsilon}\longrightarrow g_{u}(s,z),\quad\epsilon\to 0,\quad P-a.s.

where the equality is due to restriction. The thesis then follows because

\frac{\rho_{sv}(-z(B_{s+\varepsilon}-B_{\varepsilon}))}{\varepsilon}\longrightarrow g_{v}(s,z),\quad\epsilon\to 0,\quad P-a.s.

By this we end the proof.

As done in Example
10
for a single driver, we provide here below some examples where restriction fails.

Example 14

a) Consider the driver
g_{u}(t,z)=a_{u}
for any
t\in[0,u],z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
a_{u}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
depending on the maturity
u
.
It can be checked easily that, for any
t\in[0,T]
and
X\in L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{T})
,

\rho_{tu}(X)=E_{P}\left[\left.-X\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]+(u-t)a_{u}.

This means that, for
a_{u}\neq 0
,
(\rho_{tu})_{t,u}
is not normalized and does not satisfy the restriction property. Instead, it satisfies h-longevity whenever
a_{u}>0
is increasing in
u
.

b) Consider now the following driver:
g_{u}(t,z)=bz+a_{u}
for any
t\in[0,u],z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
a_{u},b\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
a_{u}
depending on the maturity
u
.
Similarly to Example
10
, it follows that

\rho_{tu}(X)=E_{Q}\left[\left.-X\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]+(u-t)a_{u},

where
E\left[\left.\frac{dQ}{dP}\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]=\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}b^{2}t+b\cdot B_{t}\right\}
.
As before, therefore, for
a_{u}\neq 0
,
(\rho_{tu})_{t,u}
is not normalized and does not satisfy the restriction property. Instead, it satisfies h-longevity whenever
a_{u}>0
is increasing in
u
.

3.2.1
Time-consistency

Thanks to the Comparison Theorem (see El Karoui et al.
[
13
]
) and to the Converse Comparison Theorem (see Briand et al.
[
8
]
), the following result establishes that monotonicity of the family
\mathcal{G}=(g_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is equivalent to sub time-consistency.

Theorem 15

Let
(\rho_{tu})_{t,u}
be induced by a BSDE with a family of drivers
\mathcal{G}=(g_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
as in (
33
).

a) The family
\mathcal{G}
is increasing if and only if
(\rho_{tu})_{t,u}
satisfies sub time-consistency.

b)
\mathcal{G}=\{g\}
if and only if
(\rho_{tu})_{t,u}
satisfies strong time-consistency.

Proof.
a) By definition (
33
), sub time-consistency of
(\rho_{tu})_{t,u}
can be written as

\mathcal{E}^{g_{t}}(\mathcal{E}^{g_{u}}(-X|\mathcal{F}_{t})|\mathcal{F}_{s})\leq\mathcal{E}^{g_{u}}(-X|\mathcal{F}_{s})

(34)

for any
s\leq t\leq u
and
X\in L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{u})
.

By definition of
\mathcal{E}^{g_{t}}
, the left-hand and right-hand sides of the previous equation can be rewritten as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{g_{t}}(\mathcal{E}^{g_{u}}(-X|\mathcal{F}_{t})|\mathcal{F}_{s})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{g_{u}}(-X|\mathcal{F}_{t})+\int_{s}^{t}g_{t}(v,Z_{v})\,dv-\int_{s}^{t}Z_{v}\,dB_{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-X+\int_{t}^{u}g_{u}(v,\tilde{Z}_{v})\,dv-\int_{t}^{u}\tilde{Z}_{v}\,dB_{v}+\int_{s}^{t}g_{t}(v,Z_{v})\,dv-\int_{s}^{t}Z_{v}\,dB_{v}

and

\mathcal{E}^{g_{u}}(-X|\mathcal{F}_{s})=-X+\int_{s}^{u}g_{u}(v,\hat{Z}_{v})\,dv-\int_{s}^{u}\hat{Z}_{v}\,dB_{v}.

(35)

Set now

\bar{g}(v,z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}g_{t}(v,z);&\quad v\in[0,t]\\
g_{u}(v,z);&\quad v\in(t,u]\end{array}\right.\quad\bar{Z}_{v}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}Z_{v};&\quad v\in[0,t]\\
\tilde{Z}_{v};&\quad v\in(t,u]\end{array}\right.

By the arguments above, the left-hand side of (
34
) becomes

\mathcal{E}^{g_{t}}(\mathcal{E}^{g_{u}}(-X|\mathcal{F}_{t})|\mathcal{F}_{s})=-X+\int_{s}^{u}\bar{g}(v,\bar{Z}_{v})\,dv-\int_{s}^{u}\bar{Z}_{v}\,dB_{v}.

(36)

On the one hand, if
\bar{g}\leq g_{u}
on
[0,u]
(or, equivalently,
g_{t}\leq g_{u}
on
[0,t]
), then by the Comparison theorem of BSDE (see El Karoui et al.
[
13
]
) sub time-consistency (
34
) is satisfied.

On the other hand, by (
35
) and (
36
) and Converse Comparison Theorem of Briand et al.
[
8
]
, sub time-consistency (
34
) implies that
\bar{g}\leq g_{u}
on
[0,u]
, hence
g_{t}\leq g_{u}
on
[0,t]
.

b) The case of time-consistency can be obtained by replacing inequalities with equalities in the proof above.

In other words, the result above guarantees that strong time-consistency is fulfilled if and only if the family of drivers reduces to a singleton, that is, the drivers
g_{t}
do not depend on the maturity
t
.

Remark 16

In the previous result, the proof of item a) can be done also by means of the penalty term characterization. In this case, one could use

\alpha_{su}^{\mathcal{G}}(Q)=E_{Q}\left[\left.\int_{s}^{u}g^{*}_{u}(v,q_{v})dv\right|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]

and the fact that, by the Fenchel-Moreau biconjugate Theorem, the condition
g^{*}_{t}\geq g^{*}_{u}
is equivalent to
g_{t}\leq g_{u}
. Indeed, if
g^{*}_{t}\geq g^{*}_{u}
then

g_{t}(s,z)=\sup_{q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\{q\cdot z-g_{t}^{*}(s,q)\}\leq\sup_{q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\{q\cdot z-g_{u}^{*}(s,q)\}=g_{u}(s,z).

The converse implication can be checked similarly.

3.2.2
H-longevity and families of BSDEs

In order to investigate h-longevity, we need to compare BSDEs with different horizons. Hereafter, we suggest a comparison theorem for this. Note that in this result we consider more general BSDEs (
28
) with driver depending on
y
, as the result is of interest well beyond the use of BSDEs in risk measures modelling.

Let
T_{1},T_{2}>0
be two different time horizon with
T_{2}>T_{1}
. Let
(Y_{t}^{T_{i},\xi_{i}},Z_{t}^{T_{i},\xi_{i}})_{t\in[0,T_{1}]}
(for
i=1,2
) be the solution of a BSDE with final condition
\xi_{i}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{T_{i}})
and driver
g^{T_{i}}
satisfying the usual assumptions. More precisely,

Y_{t}^{T_{i}}=\xi_{i}+\int_{t}^{T_{i}}g^{T_{i}}(s,Y_{s}^{T_{i}},Z_{s}^{T_{i}})ds-\int_{t}^{T_{i}}Z_{s}^{T_{i}}dB_{s}.

(37)

In the terminology of Peng
[
21
]
,
Y_{t}^{T_{i}}=\mathcal{E}^{g^{T_{i}}}(\xi_{i}\big|\mathcal{F}_{t})
.

Theorem 17

(Horizon Comparison Theorem)
If
g^{T_{2}}(s,y,z)\geq g^{T_{1}}(s,y,z)
for any
s\in[0,T_{1}],y\in\mathbb{R},z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
g^{T_{2}}(s,y,z)\geq 0
for any
s\in[T_{1},T_{2}],y\in\mathbb{R},z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\xi_{2}\geq\xi_{1}
, then
Y_{t}^{T_{2}}\geq Y_{t}^{T_{1}}
for any
t\in[0,T_{1}]
and
Y_{t}^{T_{2}}\geq\xi_{1}
for any
t\in[T_{1},T_{2}]
.

Proof.
By (
37
), it follows that

\displaystyle Y_{t}^{T_{2}}-Y_{t}^{T_{1}}=

\displaystyle\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}+\int_{t}^{T_{2}}g^{T_{2}}(s,Y_{s}^{T_{2}},Z_{s}^{T_{2}})ds-\int_{t}^{T_{1}}g^{T_{1}}(s,Y_{s}^{T_{1}},Z_{s}^{T_{1}})ds

(38)

\displaystyle-\int_{t}^{T_{2}}Z_{s}^{T_{2}}dB_{s}+\int_{t}^{T_{1}}Z_{s}^{T_{1}}dB_{s}.

Let us now extend
g^{T_{1}}(s,y,z)
,
Y_{s}^{T_{1}}
and
Z_{s}^{T_{1}}
also to
[T_{1},T_{2}]
as follows:

\displaystyle\overline{g}^{(T_{1})}(s,y,z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}g^{T_{1}}(s,y,z);&s\in[0,T_{1}]\\
0;&s\in(T_{1};T_{2}]\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\overline{Y}_{s}^{T_{1}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}Y_{s}^{T_{1}};&s\in[0,T_{1}]\\
\xi_{1};&s\in(T_{1};T_{2}]\end{array}\right.;\qquad\overline{Z}_{s}^{T_{1}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}Z_{s}^{T_{1}};&s\in[0,T_{1}]\\
0;&s\in(T_{1};T_{2}]\end{array}\right.

For
t\in[0,T_{2}]
, equation (
38
) becomes then

Y_{t}^{T_{2}}-\overline{Y}_{t}^{T_{1}}=\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}+\int_{t}^{T_{2}}\hskip-5.69054pt\big[g^{T_{2}}(s,Y_{s}^{T_{2}},Z_{s}^{T_{2}})-\overline{g}^{T_{1}}(s,\overline{Y}_{s}^{T_{1}},\overline{Z}_{s}^{T_{1}})\big]ds\\
-\int_{t}^{T_{2}}\hskip-2.84526pt(Z_{s}^{T_{2}}-\overline{Z}_{s}^{T_{1}})dB_{s}

(41)

Set now
\widetilde{Y}_{s}\triangleq Y_{s}^{T_{2}}-\overline{Y}_{s}^{T_{1}}
,
\widetilde{\xi}\triangleq\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}
,
\widetilde{Z}^{i}_{s}\triangleq Z_{s}^{T_{2},i}-\overline{Z}_{s}^{T_{1},i}
for
i=1,...,d
, and

\displaystyle\Delta_{s}g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{T_{2}}(s,\overline{Y}_{s}^{T_{1}},\overline{Z}_{s}^{T_{1}})-\overline{g}^{T_{1}}(s,\overline{Y}_{s}^{T_{1}},\overline{Z}_{s}^{T_{1}})

\displaystyle\Delta_{s}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g^{T_{2}}(s,Y_{s}^{T_{2}},Z_{s}^{T_{2}})-g^{T_{2}}(s,\overline{Y}_{s}^{T_{1}},Z_{s}^{T_{2}})}{Y_{s}^{T_{2}}-\overline{Y}_{s}^{T_{1}}}1_{\{Y_{s}^{T_{2}}\neq\overline{Y}_{s}^{T_{1}}\}}

\displaystyle(\Delta_{s}z)^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g^{T_{2}}(s,\overline{Y}_{s}^{T_{1}},Z_{s}^{T_{2}})-g^{T_{2}}(s,\overline{Y}_{s}^{T_{1}},\overline{Z}_{s}^{T_{1}})}{d(Z_{s}^{T_{2},i}-\overline{Z}_{s}^{T_{1},i})}1_{\{Z_{s}^{T_{2},i}\neq\overline{Z}_{s}^{T_{1},i}\}}

for
i=1,...,d
. Hence (
41
) can be rewritten as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}-d\widetilde{Y}_{t}&=\left[\Delta_{t}y\,\widetilde{Y}_{t}+\Delta_{t}z\cdot\widetilde{Z}_{t}+\Delta_{t}\,g\right]dt-\widetilde{Z}_{t}dB_{t}\\
\widetilde{Y}_{T_{2}}&=\widetilde{\xi}\end{array}\right.

(42)

Since
\Delta_{t}y\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{[0,T]}(\mathbb{R}),\Delta_{t}z\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{[0,T]}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\Delta_{t}\,g
is in
\mathbb{H}^{2}_{[0,T]}(\mathbb{R})
by the assumption of
g^{T_{i}}
Lipschitz, the solution of (
42
) exists uniquely by Prop. 2.2 of El Karoui, Peng and Quenez
[
13
]
. Moreover, since
g^{T_{2}}(s,y,z)\geq\overline{g}^{T_{1}}(s,y,z)
for any
s\in[0,T_{2}],y,z
and
\widetilde{\xi}\geq 0
, then
\widetilde{Y}_{t}\geq 0
for any
t\in[0,T_{2}]
which concludes the proof.

The previous result guarantees that for fully-dynamic risk measures induced by
g
-expectations as in (
33
), h-longevity holds when
g_{u}
is increasing in
u
and
g_{u}\geq 0
.

Proposition 18

a) If
\mathcal{G}
is an increasing family of drivers and
\rho_{tu}(0)\geq 0
for any
t\leq u
, then
(\rho_{tu})_{t,u}
satisfies h-longevity.

b) If
\mathcal{G}
is an increasing family of drivers and
g_{t}\geq 0
for any
t\in[0,T]
, then
(\rho_{tu})_{t,u}
satisfies h-longevity.

Proof.
a) follows by Remark
6
and Proposition
15
.

b) By Theorem
17
, increasing monotonicity of the family
\mathcal{G}
and
g_{t}\geq 0
for any
t\in[0,T]
imply h-longevity.

Notice that requiring
g_{u}\geq 0
implies that
\rho_{tu}(X)\geq E_{P}[-X|\mathcal{F}_{t}]
for any
X\in L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{u})
. Indeed, by definition of
\rho_{tu}(X)
as in (
33
) and from
g_{u}\geq 0
, it follows that

\displaystyle\rho_{tu}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{P}\left[\left.-X+\int_{t}^{u}g_{u}(v,Z_{v})\,dv-\int_{t}^{u}Z_{v}\,dB_{v}\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{P}\left[-X|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]+E_{P}\left[\left.\int_{t}^{u}g_{u}(v,Z_{v})\,dv\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle E_{P}\left[-X|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right].

3.2.3
Beyond the Lipschitz driver: the entropic case

Next, we consider the entropic risk measure, which is generated by a quadratic BSDE. Even though the Lipschitz condition is clearly not satisfied in this case, the solution of the BSDE is still unique. This discussion on the uniqueness of solutions is developed in
[
3
]
. The entropic risk measure is particularly interesting because of its explicit connection with utility functions as investment preference criteria.
In the following example, we propose a variation to the classical entropic risk measure able to capture h-longevity.

Example 19

In the one-dimensional case, for every
u\in[0,T]
, consider the driver
g_{u}(t,z)=\frac{b_{u}}{2}z^{2}+a_{u}(t)

In the same spirit of risk measures generated by BSDEs, we can consider risk measures induced by BSVIEs as Agram

Let us consider a BSVIE of type

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{g^{1}}(X|\mathcal{F}_{t})\leq\mathcal{E}^{g^{2}}(X|\mathcal{F}_{t})\mbox{ for any }X\in L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{T})

(55)

[

where

Assume now that, for a given fixed

\displaystyle 0=\mathcal{E}^{g^{1}}\left(\left.X-\mathcal{E}^{g^{1}}(X|\mathcal{F}_{r})\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right)

Going back to BSVIEs, denote by

4.1.2

Corollary 24

If

Proof.

Proposition 25

If

for any
s\leq v

\gamma(s,t,u,X)=E_{\widetilde{Q}_{s,X}}\left[\int_{t}^{u}g(s,v,0)dv|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]

Here above
\Delta_{z}g(v)=(\Delta_{z}^{i}g(v))_{i=1,...,d}

\Delta_{z}^{i}g(v)\triangleq\frac{g(s,v,Z^{u}(s,v))-g(s,v,\bar{Z}^{t}(s,v))}{d\left(Z^{u,i}(s,v)-\bar{Z}^{t,i}(s,v)\right)}1_{\{Z^{u,i}(s,v)\neq\bar{Z}^{t,i}(s,v)\}}.

Let
s\leq t\leq u
and let
X\in L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
be fixed arbitrarily.
The risk measures
\rho_{st}
and
\rho_{su}
satisfy, respectively, the following BSVIEs:

\displaystyle\rho_{st}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-X+\int_{s}^{t}g(s,v,Z^{t}(s,v))dv-\int_{s}^{t}Z^{t}(s,v)dB_{v}

\displaystyle\rho_{su}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-X+\int_{s}^{u}g(s,v,Z^{u}(s,v))dv-\int_{s}^{u}Z^{u}(s,v)dB_{v}.

Set now

\bar{Z}^{t}(s,v)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}Z^{t}(s,v);&s\leq v\leq t\\
0;&t<v\leq u\end{array}\right.;\quad\widetilde{Z}(s,v)=Z^{u}(s,v)-\bar{Z}^{t}(s,v).

Then

\displaystyle\rho_{su}(X)-\rho_{st}(X)

(56)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{u}[g(s,v,Z^{u}(s,v))-g(s,v,\bar{Z}^{t}(s,v))]dv+\int_{t}^{u}g(s,v,\bar{Z}^{t}(s,v))dv

\displaystyle-\int_{s}^{u}[Z^{u}(s,v)-\bar{Z}^{t}(s,v)]dB_{v}-\int_{t}^{u}\bar{Z}^{t}(s,v)dB_{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{u}[g(s,v,Z^{u}(s,v))-g(s,v,\bar{Z}^{t}(s,v))]dv+\int_{t}^{u}g(s,v,0)dv

\displaystyle-\int_{s}^{u}\widetilde{Z}(s,v)dB_{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{u}\Delta_{z}g(s,v)\cdot\widetilde{Z}(s,v)dv-\int_{s}^{u}\widetilde{Z}(s,v)dB_{v}+\int_{t}^{u}g(s,v,0)dv.

Furthermore, (
56

\delta\rho_{s}=\Gamma^{t,u}_{s}+\int_{s}^{u}\Delta_{z}g(s,v)\cdot\widetilde{Z}(s,v)dv-\int_{s}^{u}\widetilde{Z}(s,v)dB_{v},

(57)

where

and

represents the final condition at time

(which depends on

and also on

Since
\Gamma^{t,u}\geq 0
for any

\Delta_{z}g(s,v)\in\mathbb{H}^{2}_{[0,T]}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by the assumption of

Lipschitz in

[
28

) it follows the longevity, i.e.

for any

.
Moreover, by applying Girsanov Theorem, (

\displaystyle\delta\rho_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{u}g(s,v,0)dv+\int_{s}^{u}\Delta_{z}g(s,v)\cdot\widetilde{Z}(s,v)dv-\int_{s}^{u}\widetilde{Z}(s,v)dB_{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{u}g(s,v,0)dv-\int_{s}^{u}\widetilde{Z}(s,v)dB^{\widetilde{Q}_{s,X}}_{v},

where

,

It then follows that

As discussed in Sections

Example 26

Let

for any

. Hence, by the result above, h-longevity holds and

In other words,

Compared to the BSDE case (see Example

Finally, we provide two examples of BSVIEs: the former with a linear driver, the latter going beyond the Lipschitz case and similarly to Section

.

Example 27

Consider the driver

for any

and

By applying Girsanov Theorem, in the same line of Hu and Øksendal
[

\displaystyle Y_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-X+\int_{t}^{T}[a(t,s)\cdot Z(t,s)+b(t,s)]ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z(t,s)dB_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-X+\int_{t}^{T}b(t,s)ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z(t,s)dB^{\widetilde{Q}_{t}}_{s}

where
\frac{d\widetilde{Q}_{t}}{dP}\triangleq\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{T}|a(t,s)|^{2}ds+\int_{t}^{T}a(t,s)dB_{s}\right\}

\widetilde{Q}_{t}

Example 28

Consider the driver
g(t,s,z)=b(t)\frac{|z|^{2}}{2}+a(t,s)
for any

and

, where the deterministic function

is positive and the process

Hence

\displaystyle Y_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-X+\int_{t}^{T}\left[b(t)\frac{|Z(t,s)|^{2}}{2}+a(t,s)\right]ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z(t,s)dB_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-X+\int_{t}^{T}a(t,s)ds+\int_{t}^{T}b(t)\frac{|Z(t,s)|^{2}}{2}ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z(t,s)dB_{s}

4.2
Risk measures generated by a family of BSVIEs

Suppose that, for any
t\leq u
, the risk measure
\rho_{tu}
comes from a BSVIE with a driver

depending on the maturity

\rho_{tu}^{\mathcal{G}}(X)=\mathcal{E}^{g_{u},V}\left(-X|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right),\quad X\in L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{u}),

(58)

where

(59)

Assume now that
\mathcal{G}=(g_{u})_{u\in[0,T]}
is a family of drivers depending on the maturity
u
, independent of

, Lipschitz, and convex in

. Each risk measure
\rho^{\mathcal{G}}_{tu}
is of type (

By applying Theorem

g_{u}
parameterized by

\rho_{tu}^{\mathcal{G}}
can be represented as

\rho_{tu}(X)=\rho_{tu}^{\mathcal{G}}(X)=\underset{Q_{t}\in\mathcal{Q}_{tu}}{\operatorname{ess.\!sup}}\left\{E_{Q_{t}}\left[-X|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]-\alpha^{\mathcal{G}}_{tu}(Q_{t})\right\},\quad X\in L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{u}),

where
\mathcal{Q}_{tu}
is defined in (

g_{u}^{*}(t,s,\cdot)

\alpha^{\mathcal{G}}_{tu}(Q_{t})=E_{Q_{t}}\left[\left.\int_{t}^{u}g_{u}^{*}(t,s,q(t,s))ds\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right].

Furthermore, if
g_{u}(t,s,0)=0
for any

\rho_{tu}(0)=0

Proposition 29

Let

for any

.

is constant in

Proof.
Assume that the restriction property holds, i.e.
\rho_{tu}(X)=\rho_{tv}(X)
for any

and

.
Similarly to (
54

\eta^{t,X}_{ru}=-X+\int_{r}^{u}\bar{g}_{u}^{t}(s,\zeta_{v}^{t})dv-\int_{r}^{u}\zeta_{v}^{t}dB_{v},\quad r\in[t;u]

where

\zeta^{t}_{v}=Z(t,v);\quad\bar{g}_{u}^{t}(v,\zeta^{t}_{v})=g(t,v,Z(t,v))\quad\mbox{and}\quad\rho_{tu}(X)=\eta^{t,X}_{tu}.

Assumptions on
g_{u}
guarantee that
\bar{g}^{t}_{u}(s,0)=0
for any

and that
\bar{g}^{t}_{u}(s,\cdot)
is continuous in

.
Proceeding as in the proof of the Converse Comparison Theorem of Briand et al.
[
8
]
, Thm. 4.1, and of Jiang
[

]
, Lemma 2.1,

\displaystyle\bar{g}^{t}_{u}(s,z)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\eta^{t,z\cdot(B_{s+\varepsilon}-B_{\varepsilon})}_{su}}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\bar{g}^{t}_{v}(s,z)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\eta^{t,z\cdot(B_{s+\varepsilon}-B_{\varepsilon})}_{sv}}{\varepsilon},

with convergence in

with

, for any

,

and

\frac{\eta^{t,z\cdot(B_{s+\varepsilon}-B_{\varepsilon})}_{sv}}{\varepsilon}=\frac{\eta^{t,z\cdot(B_{s+\varepsilon}-B_{\varepsilon})}_{su}}{\varepsilon}\longrightarrow\bar{g}^{t}_{u}(s,z),\quad\epsilon\to 0,\>P\mbox{-a.s.}

where the equality is due to restriction. The thesis then follows because

\frac{\eta^{t,z\cdot(B_{s+\varepsilon}-B_{\varepsilon})}_{sv}}{\varepsilon}\longrightarrow\bar{g}^{t}_{v}(s,z),\quad\epsilon\to 0,\>P\mbox{-a.s.}

The converse follows immediately by Proposition
20
.

4.2.1
Time-consistency

The following result provides a necessary and sufficient condition for a fully-dynamic risk measure induced by a family of BSVIEs to satisfy sub time-consistency. Note that the condition on the monotonicity of
g_{\cdot}(t,\cdot,\cdot)
is the same as for a BSVIE with a single driver, while the condition on the monotonicity of the family

is new.

Proposition 30

Let
(\rho_{tu})_{t,u}
be induced by a BSVIE with a family of drivers
(g_{u})_{u\in[0,T]}
as in (

(\rho_{tu})_{t,u}

\mathcal{G}

is decreasing in

.

b)
(\rho_{tu})_{t,u}

\mathcal{G}\hskip-2.84526pt=\hskip-2.84526pt\{g\}

g_{\cdot}(t,\cdot,\cdot)
is constant in
t

Proof.
a) Assume sub time-consistency holds.
By (

\rho_{tu}
is given by

\alpha^{\mathcal{G}}_{tu}(Q_{t})=E_{Q_{t}}\left[\left.\int_{t}^{u}g_{u}^{*}(t,v,q(t,v))dv\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

for any
Q_{t}\in\mathcal{Q}_{tu}

Let

with
s\leq t\leq u
and let
Q_{s}\in\mathcal{Q}_{st},Q_{t}\in\mathcal{Q}_{tu}
be fixed arbitrarily. Set now
\bar{Q}
the pasting of
Q_{s}
on
[s,t]
and of
Q_{t}
on
[t,u]
, hence
\bar{Q}\in\mathcal{Q}_{su}
. Denote by

,

and
\bar{q}(s,v)
the corresponding processes as in (

).
From the characterization of the penalty term for sub time-consistency in Proposition
7
(iii) it follows that

\displaystyle{\displaystyle E_{\bar{Q}}\left[\left.\int_{s}^{u}g_{u}^{*}(s,v,\bar{q}(s,v))dv\right|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\leq E_{\bar{Q}}\left[\left.\int_{s}^{t}g_{t}^{*}(s,v,\bar{q}(s,v))dv\right|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]+E_{\bar{Q}}\left[\left.E_{\bar{Q}}\left[\left.\int_{t}^{u}g_{u}^{*}(t,v,\bar{q}(t,v))dv\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\right|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]},

hence

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054pt{\displaystyle 0\leq}

\displaystyle\hskip-11.38109pt{\displaystyle E_{Q_{s}}\left[\left.\int_{s}^{t}\left[g_{t}^{*}(s,v,\bar{q}(s,v))-g_{u}^{*}(s,v,\bar{q}(s,v))\right]dv\right|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]}

(61)

\displaystyle\hskip-11.38109pt{\displaystyle+E_{Q_{s}}\left[\left.E_{Q_{t}}\left[\left.\int_{t}^{u}\left[g_{u}^{*}(t,v,\bar{q}(t,v))-g_{u}^{*}(s,v,\bar{q}(s,v))\right]dv\right|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]\right|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]}.

Since (
61
) should hold for any
s\leq t\leq u
and any
Q_{s}\in\mathcal{Q}_{st},Q_{t}\in\mathcal{Q}_{tu}
, it follows that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}g_{t}^{*}(s,v,\bar{q})\geq g_{u}^{*}(s,v,\bar{q}),&\quad\mbox{ for any }s\leq v\leq t\leq u\mbox{ and }\bar{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
g_{u}^{*}(t,v,\bar{q})\geq g_{u}^{*}(s,v,\bar{q}),&\quad\mbox{ for any }s\leq t\leq v\leq u\mbox{ and }\bar{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{array}\right.

Hence,
g_{u}
is increasing in
u
and
g_{\cdot}(t,\cdot,\cdot)
is decreasing in
t
.

Conversely, sub time-consistency of
(\rho_{tu})_{t,u}
induced by a family of BSVIEs can be written in the following notation

\mathcal{E}^{g_{t},V}(\mathcal{E}^{g_{u},V}(-X|\mathcal{F}_{t})|\mathcal{F}_{s})\leq\mathcal{E}^{g_{u},V}(-X|\mathcal{F}_{s})

(62)

for any
s\leq t\leq u
and
X\in L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{u})
.
By (
59
), the right-hand and left-hand sides of the previous equation can be rewritten, respectively, as follows:

\mathcal{E}^{g_{u},V}(-X|\mathcal{F}_{s})=-X+\int_{s}^{u}g_{u}(s,v,\hat{Z}(s,v))\,dv-\int_{s}^{u}\hat{Z}(s,v)\,dB_{v}

(63)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{g_{t},V}(\mathcal{E}^{g_{u},V}(-X|\mathcal{F}_{t})|\mathcal{F}_{s})

(64)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-X+\int_{t}^{u}g_{u}(t,v,\tilde{Z}(t,v))\,dv-\int_{t}^{u}\tilde{Z}(t,v)\,dB_{v}

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{t}g_{t}(s,v,Z(s,v))\,dv-\int_{s}^{t}Z(s,v)\,dB_{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-X+\int_{s}^{u}\left[g_{t}(s,v,Z(s,v))1_{[s,t]}(v)+g_{u}(t,v,\tilde{Z}(t,v))1_{(t,u]}(v)\right]\,dv

\displaystyle-\int_{s}^{u}\left[Z(t,v)1_{[s,t]}(v)+\tilde{Z}(t,v)1_{(t,u]}(v)\right]\,dB_{v}.

Furthermore, (
64
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{g_{t},V}(\mathcal{E}^{g_{u},V}(-X|\mathcal{F}_{t})|\mathcal{F}_{s})

(65)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-X+\int_{s}^{u}\left[g_{t}(s,v,Z(s,v))1_{[s,t]}(v)+g_{u}(s,v,\tilde{Z}(t,v))1_{(t,u]}(v)\right]\,dv

\displaystyle-\int_{s}^{u}\left[Z(t,v)1_{[s,t]}(v)+\tilde{Z}(t,v)1_{(t,u]}(v)\right]\,dB_{v}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-X+\int_{s}^{u}\left[g_{u}(s,v,Z(s,v))1_{[s,t]}(v)+g_{u}(s,v,\tilde{Z}(t,v))1_{(t,u]}(v)\right]\,dv

\displaystyle-\int_{s}^{u}\left[Z(t,v)1_{[s,t]}(v)+\tilde{Z}(t,v)1_{(t,u]}(v)\right]\,dB_{v},

where the former inequality is due to decreasing monotonicity of
g_{\cdot}(t,\cdot,\cdot)
, the latter from increasing monotonicity of the family of drivers.
By setting

\bar{Z}(s,v)=Z(s,v)1_{[s,t]}(v)+\tilde{Z}(t,v)1_{(t,u]}(v),

(
65
) becomes

\mathcal{E}^{g_{t}}(\mathcal{E}^{g_{u}}(-X|\mathcal{F}_{t})|\mathcal{F}_{s})\leq-X+\int_{s}^{u}g_{u}(s,v,\bar{Z}(s,v))\,dv-\int_{s}^{u}\bar{Z}(s,v)\,dB_{v}.

(66)

Sub time-consistency then follows by comparing (
66
) and (
63
) and
by the uniqueness of the solution of a BSVIE.

4.2.2
H-longevity and families of BSVIEs

The following result provides sufficient conditions for h-longevity, similarly to Proposition
18
for BSDEs.

Proposition 31

a) If
\mathcal{G}
is an increasing family of drivers,
g_{\cdot}(t,\cdot,\cdot)
is decreasing in
t
, and
\rho_{tu}(0)\geq 0
for any
t\leq u
, then
(\rho_{tu})_{t,u}
satisfies h-longevity.

b) If
\mathcal{G}
is an increasing family of drivers and
g_{u}\geq 0
for any
u
, then h-longevity holds.

Proof.
a) follows by Remark
6
and Proposition
30
.

b) Let
s\leq t\leq u
and
X\in L^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{t})
be fixed arbitrarily.
The risk measures
\rho_{st}
and
\rho_{su}
satisfy, respectively, the following BSVIEs:

\displaystyle\rho_{st}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-X+\int_{s}^{t}g_{t}(s,v,Z^{t}(s,v))dv-\int_{s}^{t}Z^{t}(s,v)dB_{v}

\displaystyle\rho_{su}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-X+\int_{s}^{u}g_{u}(s,v,Z^{u}(s,v))dv-\int_{s}^{u}Z^{u}(s,v)dB_{v}.

Set now

\bar{Z}^{t,u}(s,v)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}Z^{t}(s,v);&s\leq v\leq t\\
0;&t<v\leq u\end{array}\right.;\quad\widetilde{Z}(s,v)=Z^{u}(s,v)-\bar{Z}^{t,u}(s,v).

Then

\displaystyle\delta\rho_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{su}(X)-\rho_{st}(X)

(67)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{u}[g_{u}(s,v,Z^{u}(s,v))-g_{t}(s,v,\bar{Z}^{t,u}(s,v))]dv+\int_{t}^{u}g_{t}(s,v,0)dv

\displaystyle-\int_{s}^{u}[Z^{u}(s,v)-\bar{Z}^{t,u}(s,v)]dB_{v}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{u}[g_{u}(s,v,Z^{u}(s,v))-g_{u}(s,v,\bar{Z}^{t,u}(s,v))]dv-\int_{s}^{u}\widetilde{Z}(s,v)dB_{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{u}\Delta_{z}g_{u}(s,v)\cdot\widetilde{Z}(s,v)dv-\int_{s}^{u}\widetilde{Z}(s,v)dB_{v},

where the inequality is due to the hypothesis on the drivers and where

\Delta_{z}^{i}g_{u}(s,v)\triangleq\frac{g_{u}(s,v,Z^{u}(s,v))-g_{u}(s,v,\bar{Z}^{t,u}(s,v))}{d\left(Z^{u,i}(s,v)-\bar{Z}^{t,u,i}(s,v)\right)}1_{\{Z^{u,i}(s,v)\neq\bar{Z}^{t,u,i}(s,v)\}}

for
i=1,...,d
. By Girsanov Theorem, (
67
) becomes

\displaystyle\delta\rho_{s}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\int_{s}^{u}\widetilde{Z}(s,v)dB^{\widetilde{Q}_{s}}_{v},

where
\frac{d\widetilde{Q}_{s}}{dP}\triangleq\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{u}|\Delta_{z}g_{u}(s,v)|^{2}dv+\int_{s}^{u}\Delta_{z}g_{u}(s,v)dB_{v}\right\}
and
B^{\widetilde{Q}_{s}}_{v}\triangleq B_{v}-B_{s}-\int_{s}^{v}\Delta_{z}g_{u}(s,v)dv
, for
v\geq s
, is a Brownian motion with respect to
\widetilde{Q}_{s}
with initial value
B^{\widetilde{Q}_{s}}_{s}=0
. Hence, by taking the conditional expectation with respect to
\widetilde{Q}_{s}
,

\delta\rho_{s}\geq E_{\widetilde{Q}_{s}}\left[\left.-\int_{s}^{u}\widetilde{Z}(s,v)dB^{\widetilde{Q}_{s}}_{v}\right|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]=0.

This completes the proof.

In the spirit of Example
19
, we can easily extend Example
27
and Example
28
to cover the case of families of BSVIEs.

The random periodic solutions for McKean-Vlasov stochastic differential equations
Jianhai Bao, Gonçalo Dos Reis, Yue Wu

Abstract
In this paper, we study well-posedness of random periodic solutions of stochastic differential equations (SDEs) of McKean-Vlasov type driven by a two-sided Brownian motion, where the random periodic behaviour is characterised by the equations’ long-time behaviour. Given the well-known connection between McKean-Vlasov SDEs and interacting particle systems, we show propagation of chaos and that the key properties of the interacting particle systems recover those of the McKean-Vlasov SDEs in the particle limit. All results in the present work are shown under two settings: fully and partially dissipative case. Each setting has its challenges and limitations. For instance, weakening full dissipativity to partial dissipativity demands stronger structural assumptions on the equations’ dynamics and yields random periodic behaviour in the weak sense instead of pathwise sense (as in the full dissipativity case). The proof mechanisms are close but fundamentally different.

Owing to the involvement of measure variables in the coefficients, there are major discrepancies on distributional properties between classical SDEs and McKean-Vlasov SDEs. For instance, the solution processes of classical SDEs are related to linear Markov processes whereas the McKean-Vlasov SDEs correspond to nonlinear Markov processes

As far as a classical SDE is concerned, the corresponding Markov operator is a linear semigroup while the one associated with a McKean-Vlasov SDE is no longer a semigroup (see e.g.

Furthermore, concerning classical SDEs, the localization procedure is powerful to handle e.g., existence and uniqueness of strong/weak solutions, ergodic properties, numerical methods, to name a few. Unfortunately, such a strategy cannot carry over (at least directly) to the setting of McKean–Vlasov SDEs; see e.g.,

In this paper, our first goal is to establish a comprehensive framework to understand

The two assumption archetypes we explore lead to different formulations of the main results, where in one formulation the results hold in a pathwise sense while in the second formulation they hold in a distributional sense. The second block of assumptions addresses the partially dissipative case but requires further structural assumptions on the McKean-Vlasov dynamics and the proof makes use of coupling arguments.

&\quad+\Big(|b_{t}({\bf 0},\delta_{\bf 0})|^{2}/\varepsilon^{**}+(1+1/\varepsilon^{**})\|\sigma_{t}({\bf 0},\delta_{\bf 0})\|_{\rm HS}^{2}\Big)|x|^{\varepsilon^{*}}\\
&\leq\bigg(K_{1}(t)+\varepsilon^{**}(1+K_{3}(t))+\frac{(1+K_{2}(t)+\varepsilon^{**}K_{3}(t))\varepsilon^{*}}{2+\varepsilon^{*}}\bigg)|x|^{2+\varepsilon^{*}}\\
&\quad+\frac{2(K_{2}(t)+\varepsilon^{**}K_{3}(t))}{2+\varepsilon^{*}}\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\delta_{\bf 0})^{2+\varepsilon^{*}}\\
&\quad+\frac{2}{2+\varepsilon^{*}}\Big(|b_{t}({\bf 0},\delta_{\bf 0})|^{2}/\varepsilon^{**}+(1+1/\varepsilon^{**})\|\sigma_{t}({\bf 0},\delta_{\bf 0})\|_{\rm HS}^{2}\Big)^{1+\frac{\varepsilon^{*}}{2}}.\end{split}

(24)

Moreover, because of (
5
), using the same trick from Young’s inequality above, we infer that for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\begin{split}|x|^{\varepsilon^{*}}\|\sigma_{t}(x,\mu)\|_{\rm HS}^{2}&\leq 2|x|^{\varepsilon^{*}}\big(\|\sigma_{t}(x,\mu)-\sigma_{t}({\bf 0},\delta_{{\bf 0}})\|_{\rm HS}^{2}+\|\sigma_{t}({\bf 0},\delta_{{\bf 0}})\|_{\rm HS}^{2}\big)\\
&\leq 2|x|^{\varepsilon^{*}}\big(K_{3}(t)|x|^{2}+K_{3}(t)\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\delta_{\bf 0})^{2}+\|\sigma_{t}({\bf 0},\delta_{{\bf 0}})\|_{\rm HS}^{2}\big)\\
&\leq\frac{4K_{3}(t)+2(1+2K_{3}(t))\varepsilon^{*}}{2+\varepsilon^{*}}|x|^{2+\varepsilon^{*}}\\
&\quad+\frac{4K_{3}(t)}{2+\varepsilon^{*}}\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\delta_{\bf 0})^{2+\varepsilon^{*}}+\frac{4}{2+\varepsilon^{*}}\|\sigma_{t}({\bf 0},\delta_{{\bf 0}})\|_{\rm HS}^{2+\varepsilon^{*}}.\end{split}

(25)

Next, applying Itô’s formula and taking expectations yields

\begin{split}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\int_{s}^{t}K^{*}(u)\,\text{\rm{d}}u}\mathbb{E}|X_{s,t}^{\xi}|^{2+\varepsilon^{*}}&\leq\mathbb{E}|\xi|^{2+\varepsilon^{*}}+\int_{s}^{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\int_{s}^{u}K^{*}(v)\,\text{\rm{d}}v}\big(-K^{*}(u)\mathbb{E}|X_{s,u}^{\xi}|^{2+\varepsilon^{*}}\\
&\quad+\mathbb{E}\big[(1+\varepsilon^{*}/2)|X_{s,u}^{\xi}|^{\varepsilon^{*}}\big(2\langle X_{s,u}^{\xi},b_{u}(X_{s,u}^{\xi},\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,u}^{\xi}})\rangle\\
&\quad+\|\sigma_{u}(X_{s,u}^{\xi},\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,u}^{\xi}})\|_{\rm HS}^{2}\big)\big]\\
&\quad+(1+\varepsilon^{*}/2)\varepsilon^{*}\mathbb{E}\big[|X_{s,u}^{\xi}|^{\varepsilon^{*}}\|\sigma_{u}(X_{s,u}^{\xi},\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,u}^{\xi}})\|_{\rm HS}^{2}\big]\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}u.\end{split}

Next, substituting (
24
) and (
25
) into the inequality above yields

\begin{split}&\text{\rm{e}}^{-\int_{s}^{t}K^{*}(u)\,\text{\rm{d}}u}\mathbb{E}|X_{s,t}^{\xi}|^{2+\varepsilon^{*}}\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}|\xi|^{2+\varepsilon^{*}}+(1+\varepsilon^{*}/2)\int_{s}^{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\int_{s}^{u}K^{\varepsilon}(v)\,\text{\rm{d}}v}\widetilde{K}^{*}(u)\big(-\mathbb{E}|X_{s,u}^{\xi}|^{2+\varepsilon^{*}}+\mathbb{W}_{2}\big(\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,u}^{\xi}},\delta_{{\bf 0}}\big)^{2+\varepsilon^{*}}\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}u\\
&\quad+C_{0}(\varepsilon^{*},\varepsilon^{**})\int_{s}^{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\int_{s}^{u}K^{\varepsilon}(v)\,\text{\rm{d}}v}\big(|b_{u}({\bf 0},\delta_{\bf 0})|^{2}+\|\sigma_{u}({\bf 0},\delta_{\bf 0})\|_{\rm HS}^{2}\big)^{1+\frac{\varepsilon^{*}}{2}}\text{\rm{d}}u,\end{split}

for some constant
C_{0}(\varepsilon^{*},\varepsilon^{**})>0.

Subsequently, combining with the fact that for any
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2+\varepsilon^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\delta_{{\bf 0}})^{2+\varepsilon^{*}}\leq\mathbb{W}_{2+\varepsilon^{*}}(\mu,\delta_{{\bf 0}})^{2+\varepsilon^{*}}=\mu(|\cdot|^{2+\varepsilon^{*}}),

we obtain that

\mathbb{E}|X_{s,t}^{\xi}|^{2+\varepsilon^{*}}\leq\text{\rm{e}}^{\int_{s}^{t}K^{\varepsilon}(u)\,\text{\rm{d}}u}\mathbb{E}|\xi|^{2+\varepsilon^{*}}+C_{0}(\varepsilon^{*},\varepsilon^{**})\int_{s}^{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{\int_{u}^{t}K^{*}(v)\,\text{\rm{d}}v}\big(|b_{u}({\bf 0},\delta_{\bf 0})|^{2}+\|\sigma_{u}({\bf 0},\delta_{\bf 0})\|_{\rm HS}^{2}\big)\text{\rm{d}}u.

Finally, by making use of Lemma
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]
and taking the continuity and the periodicity of
K_{1},K_{2},K_{3}
into consideration, there is a constant
C_{1}(\varepsilon^{*},\varepsilon^{**})>0
(independent of
\xi
and time parameters
t,s
) such that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}|X_{s,t}^{\xi}|^{2+\varepsilon^{*}}&\leq C_{1}(\varepsilon^{*},\varepsilon^{**})\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda^{*}\lfloor(t-s)/\tau\rfloor}\mathbb{E}|\xi|^{2+\varepsilon^{*}}+C_{1}(\varepsilon^{*},\varepsilon^{**})\int_{s}^{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda^{*}\lfloor(t-u)/\tau\rfloor}\text{\rm{d}}u.\end{split}

Whence, the assertion (
22
) immediately follows.
∎

The following lemma reveals that the solution processes are continuous in the mean-square sense w.r.t. the initial values uniformly w.r.t. the time parameters.

Lemma 3.2

(Moments of differences)
.

Under assumptions of Theorem
2.1
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
(s,t)\in\Delta
and
\xi,\eta\in L^{2}(\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathscr{F}_{s},\mathbb{P})
,

\mathbb{E}|X_{s,t}^{\xi}-Y_{s,t}^{\eta}|^{2}\leq C\,\text{\rm{e}}^{-\frac{\lambda}{\tau}(t-s)}\mathbb{E}|\xi-\eta|^{2}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathbb{W}_{2}\big(\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,t}^{\xi}},\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,t}^{\eta}}\big)^{2}\leq C\,\text{\rm{e}}^{-\frac{\lambda}{\tau}(t-s)}\mathbb{W}_{2}\big(\mathscr{L}_{\xi},\mathscr{L}_{\eta}\big)^{2},

(26)

where
\lambda>0
was defined in (
6
).

Proof.

In the sequel, we still write
(s,t)\in\Delta
and
\xi\in L^{2}(\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathscr{F}_{s},\mathbb{P})
and moreover set

\psi_{s,t}:=X_{s,t}^{\xi}-Y_{s,t}^{\eta}
for the sake of notation brevity.
For
\overline{K}(t):=K_{1}(t)+K_{2}(t)
,
t\in\mathbb{R},
applying Itô’s formula, we deduce from (
4
) that

\begin{split}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\int_{s}^{t}\overline{K}(u)\,\text{\rm{d}}u}\mathbb{E}|\psi_{s,t}|^{2}&=\mathbb{E}|\xi-\eta|^{2}+\int_{s}^{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\int_{s}^{u}\overline{K}(v)\,\text{\rm{d}}v}\big(-\overline{K}(u)\mathbb{E}|\psi_{s,u}|^{2}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+2\mathbb{E}\langle\psi_{s,u},b_{u}(X_{s,u}^{\xi},\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,u}^{\xi}})-b_{u}(X_{s,u}^{\eta},\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,u}^{\eta}})\rangle\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\mathbb{E}\|\sigma_{u}(X_{s,u}^{\xi},\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,u}}^{\xi})-\sigma_{u}(X_{s,u}^{\eta},\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,u}}^{\eta})\|_{\rm HS}^{2}\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}u\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}|\xi-\eta|^{2}+\int_{s}^{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\int_{s}^{u}\overline{K}(v)\,\text{\rm{d}}v}K_{2}(u)\big(-\mathbb{E}|\psi_{s,u}|^{2}+\mathbb{W}_{2}\big(\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,u}^{\xi}},\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,u}^{\eta}}\big)^{2}\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}u\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}|\xi-\eta|^{2},\end{split}

where in the last display we used the basic fact that

\mathbb{W}_{2}\big(\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,u}^{\xi}},\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,u}^{\eta}}\big)^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}|X_{s,u}^{\xi}-Y_{s,u}^{\eta}|^{2}=\mathbb{E}|\psi_{s,u}|^{2},\qquad u\geq s.

(27)

Therefore, we arrive at

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}|\psi_{s,t}|^{2}\leq\text{\rm{e}}^{\int_{s}^{t}\overline{K}(u)\,\text{\rm{d}}u}\mathbb{E}|\xi-\eta|^{2}.\end{split}

Then, invoking
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]
and recalling the definition of
\lambda
introduced in (
6
) yields

\mathbb{E}|\psi_{s,t}|^{2}\leq\exp\bigg(-\lambda\lfloor(t-s)/\tau\rfloor+\int_{s-\lfloor s/\tau\rfloor\tau}^{t-(\lfloor s/\tau\rfloor+\lfloor(t-s)/\tau\rfloor)\tau}\overline{K}(u)\,\text{\rm{d}}u\bigg)\mathbb{E}|\xi-\eta|^{2}.

This, together with the local integrability of
\overline{K}

due to the corresponding continuity of
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
, leads to the desired assertion, i.e., the first inequality in (
26
).
The second inequality in (
26
) is followed by choosing
\xi,\eta\in L^{2}(\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathscr{F}_{s},\mathbb{P})
satisfying
\mathbb{E}|\xi-\eta|^{2}=\mathbb{W}_{2}\big(\mathscr{L}_{\xi},\mathscr{L}_{\eta}\big)^{2}
on the RHS of the first inequality in (
26
) while using (
27
) for it on the LHS.
∎

The subsequent lemma is concerned with the stochastic semi-flow property of the solution process, which is the foundation to treat existence of random periodic solutions.

Lemma 3.3
.

Under Assumption
({\bf A})
, the solution process
(X_{s,t}^{\xi})_{t\geq s}
admits the semi-flow property:

X_{s,t}^{\xi}=X_{r,t}^{X_{s,r}^{\xi}},\quad s\leq r\leq t,\quad\xi\in H:=L^{2}(\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathscr{F}_{s},\mathbb{P}).

(28)

Proof.

For any
T\geq s
and
\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, let

\mathcal{C}_{s,T}^{\nu}=\big\{\mu\in C\big([s,T];\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big):\mu_{s}=\nu\big\}.

Define a metric on
\mathcal{C}_{s,T}^{\nu}
as below: for any
\lambda>0,

\mathbb{W}_{2,\lambda}\big(\mu^{1},\mu^{2}\big):=\sup_{s\leq t\leq T}\big(\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda(t-s)}\mathbb{W}_{2}\big(\mu^{1}_{t},\mu^{2}_{t}\big)\big),\quad\mu^{1},\mu^{2}\in\mathcal{C}_{s,T}^{\nu}.

Under the metric
\mathbb{W}_{2,\lambda}
,
\mathcal{C}_{s,T}^{\nu}
is a Polish space. For a fixed
\mu\in\mathcal{C}_{s,T}^{\nu}
, we consider the following distribution-frozen SDE associated with (
1
)

\text{\rm{d}}Y_{s,t}^{\mu,\xi}=b_{t}(Y_{s,t}^{\mu,\xi},\mu_{t})\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma_{t}(Y_{s,t}^{\mu,\xi},\mu_{t})\,\text{\rm{d}}W_{t},\qquad(s,t)\in\Delta,\qquad Y_{s,s}^{\mu,\xi}=X_{s,s}^{\xi}=\xi\sim\nu,

(29)

where
X_{s,s}^{\xi}
is the initial value of the McKean-Vlasov SDE (
1
).
Under Assumption (
A
), the classical time-inhomogeneous SDE (
29
) is strongly well-posed (see e.g.
[
43
, Theorem 3.1.1, p.44]
) so the associated solution process
(Y_{s,t}^{\mu,\xi})_{t\geq s}

possesses the semi-flow property (in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and hence in
L^{2}
), i.e.,

Y_{s,t}^{\mu,\xi}=Y_{r,t}^{\mu,Y_{s,r}^{\mu,\xi}},\quad s\leq r\leq t,\quad\xi\in H.

(30)

For any
\mu\in\mathcal{C}_{s,T}^{\nu}
, define the mapping
\Phi(\mu)
as below

\big(\Phi(\mu)\big)_{s,t,\xi}=\mathscr{L}_{Y_{s,t}^{\mu,\xi}},\quad s\leq t\leq T,\quad\xi\in H.

(31)

Provided that the mapping
\Phi\in\mathcal{C}_{s,T}^{\nu}
defined above admits a unique fixed point
\mu\in\mathcal{C}_{s,T}^{\nu}
, i.e.,

\mu=\Phi(\mu)\qquad\mbox{ so }\qquad\big(\Phi(\mu)\big)_{s,t,\xi}=\mu_{t},\quad s\leq t\leq T.

Thus, in addition to (
31
), we can replace the measure variable
\mu_{t}
in (
29
) by
\mathscr{L}_{Y_{s,t}^{\mu,\xi}},
so the SDE (
29
) is exactly the same as the McKean-Vlasov SDE (
1
). Consequently, we find

X_{s,t}^{\xi}=Y_{s,t}^{\mu,\xi},\quad s\leq t\leq T,\quad\xi\in H.

This, besides (
30
), enables us to derive that

X_{s,t}^{\xi}=X_{r,t}^{X_{s,r}^{\xi}},\quad s\leq r\leq t\leq T,\quad\xi\in H.

Accordingly, the semi-flow property (
28
) holds true.

Based on the analysis above, to achieve the semi-flow property (
28
), it remains to show that the mapping
\Phi
defined in (
31
) has a unique fixed point in
\mathcal{C}_{s,T}^{\nu}
. To this end, it suffices to justify

(i)

\Phi
maps
\mathcal{C}_{s,T}^{\nu}
into itself, i.e.,
\Phi:\mathcal{C}_{s,T}^{\nu}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{s,T}^{\nu}
;

(ii)

\Phi
is contractive in
\mathcal{C}_{s,T}^{\nu}
under the metric
\mathbb{W}_{2,\lambda}
for some suitably chosen
\lambda>0
, i.e., there exists a constant
c\in(0,1)
such that for some
\lambda>0
to be figured,

\mathbb{W}_{2,\lambda}\big(\Phi(\mu^{1}),\Phi(\mu^{2})\big)\leq c\,\mathbb{W}_{2,\lambda}\big(\mu^{1},\mu^{2}\big).

In the first place, we verify the assertion (i). For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\rho\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we infer from
(
2
) and (
3
) that

2\langle x,b_{t}(x,\rho)\rangle\leq\big(1+\overline{K}_{1}(t)\big)|x|^{2}+\overline{K}_{1}(t)\mathbb{W}_{2}(\rho,\delta_{{\bf 0}})^{2}+|b_{t}({\bf 0},\delta_{{\bf 0}})|^{2},

(32)

and

\|\sigma_{t}(x,\rho)\|_{\rm HS}^{2}\leq 2\overline{K}_{2}(t)\big(|x|^{2}+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\rho,\delta_{{\bf 0}})^{2}\big)+2\|\sigma_{t}({\bf 0},\delta_{{\bf 0}})\|_{\rm HS}^{2}.

(33)

Next,
applying Itô’s formula followed by the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy (BDG) inequality, we deduce that for any
\xi\in H
and
s\leq t\leq T
,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{s\leq r\leq t}|Y_{s,r}^{\mu,\xi}|^{2}\Big)&\leq\mathbb{E}|\xi|^{2}+\int_{s}^{t}\big\{2\langle Y_{s,u}^{\mu,\xi},b_{u}(Y_{s,u}^{\mu,\xi},\mu_{u})\rangle+\|\sigma_{u}(Y_{s,u}^{\mu,\xi},\mu_{u})\|_{\rm HS}^{2}\big\}\,\text{\rm{d}}u\\
&\quad+2\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sup_{s\leq r\leq t}\int_{s}^{r}\langle Y_{s,u}^{\mu,\xi},\sigma_{u}(Y_{s,u}^{\mu,\xi},\mu_{u})\text{\rm{d}}W_{u}\rangle\bigg)\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}|\xi|^{2}+\int_{s}^{t}\big\{2\langle Y_{s,u}^{\mu,\xi},b_{u}(Y_{s,u}^{\mu,\xi},\mu_{u})\rangle+65\|\sigma_{u}(Y_{s,u}^{\mu,\xi},\mu_{u})\|_{\rm HS}^{2}\big\}\,\text{\rm{d}}u+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{s\leq r\leq t}|Y_{s,r}^{\mu,\xi}|^{2}\Big).\end{split}

Thereby, (
32
) and (
33
) yield

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{s\leq r\leq t}|Y_{s,r}^{\mu,\xi}|^{2}\Big)&\leq 2\mathbb{E}|\xi|^{2}+2\int_{s}^{t}\big\{2\langle Y_{s,u}^{\mu,\xi},b_{u}(Y_{s,u}^{\mu,\xi},\mu_{u})\rangle+65\|\sigma_{u}(Y_{s,u}^{\mu,\xi},\mu_{u})\|_{\rm HS}^{2}\big\}\,\text{\rm{d}}u\\
&\leq 2\mathbb{E}|\xi|^{2}+\int_{s}^{t}\lambda_{u}\big(\mathbb{E}|Y_{s,u}^{\mu,\xi}|^{2}+\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{u},\delta_{{\bf 0}})^{2}+C_{u}\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}u,\end{split}

where

\lambda_{u}:=\overline{K}_{1}(u)+130\overline{K}_{2}(u)+1\quad\mbox{ and }\quad C_{u}:=|b_{u}({\bf 0},\delta_{{\bf 0}})|^{2}+130\|\sigma_{u}({\bf 0},\delta_{{\bf 0}})\|^{2}_{\rm HS},\qquad u\geq s.

Thus, applying Gronwall’s inequality leads to

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{s\leq r\leq t}|Y_{s,r}^{\mu,\xi}|^{2}\Big)&\leq\bigg(2\mathbb{E}|\xi|^{2}+\int_{s}^{t}\big\{\lambda_{u}\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{s,u},\delta_{{\bf 0}})^{2}+C_{u}\big\}\,\text{\rm{d}}u\bigg)\text{\rm{e}}^{\int_{s}^{t}\lambda_{u}\,\text{\rm{d}}u}.\end{split}

(34)

This, combining
\mu\in\mathcal{C}_{s,T}^{\nu}
with the fact that

t\mapsto\overline{K}_{1}(t)+\overline{K}_{2}(t)
and
t\mapsto|b_{t}({\bf 0},\delta_{{\bf 0}})|^{2}+\|\sigma_{t}({\bf 0},\delta_{{\bf 0}})\|^{2}_{\rm HS}
are locally integrable, yields
\mathscr{L}_{Y_{s,t}^{\mu,\xi}}\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
\mu\in\mathcal{C}_{s,T}^{\nu}
,
\xi\in H
and
s\leq t\leq T
. In the following, to examine
\Phi\in\mathcal{C}_{s,T}^{\nu}
,
we further need to claim the continuity, ie,

\lim_{\triangle t\downarrow 0}\mathbb{W}_{2}\big(\mathscr{L}_{Y_{s,t+\triangle t}^{\mu,\xi}},\mathscr{L}_{Y_{s,t}^{\mu,\xi}}\big)=0.

(35)

In the light of the semi-flow property of
\mathscr{L}_{Y_{s,t}^{\mu,\xi}}
, it is sufficient to prove

\lim_{\triangle s\downarrow 0}\mathbb{W}_{2}\big(\mathscr{L}_{Y_{s,s+\triangle s}^{\mu,\xi}},\mathscr{L}_{\xi}\big)=0.

(36)

To end this, define the stopping time for each integer
n\geq 1
,

\tau_{n}:=\inf\big\{t\geq s:|Y_{s,t}^{\mu,\xi}|\geq n\big\}.

In the following analysis, we shall stipulate
\triangle s\in(0,1)
.
Apparently, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}|Y_{s,s+\triangle s}^{\mu,\xi}-\xi|^{2}&=\mathbb{E}\big(|Y_{s,s+\triangle s}^{\mu,\xi}-\xi|^{2}{\bf 1}_{\{s+\triangle s<\tau_{n}\}}\big)+\mathbb{E}\big(|Y_{s,s+\triangle s}^{\mu,\xi}-\xi|^{2}{\bf 1}_{\{{s+}\triangle s\geq\tau_{n}\}}\big)\\
&=:I_{1}(n,\triangle s)+I_{2}(n,\triangle s).\end{split}

Since, for each
t\in\mathbb{R},

b_{t}
and
\sigma_{t}

are continuous and bounded on bounded sets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have for each fixed
n\geq 1
,

\lim_{\triangle s\rightarrow 0}I_{1}(n,\triangle s)=0\quad\mathbb{P}\textrm{-a.s.}

(37)

On the other hand, it is easy to see that

\begin{split}I_{2}(n,\triangle s)\leq 2\big(\mathbb{E}\big(|Y_{s,s+\triangle s}^{\mu,\xi}|^{2}+|\xi|^{2}\big){\bf 1}_{\{{s+}\triangle s\geq\tau_{n}\}}\big).\end{split}

By the apparent fact that

\{{s+}\triangle s\geq\tau_{n}\}\subseteq\Big\{\sup_{s\leq r\leq s+1}|Y_{s,s+r}^{\mu,\xi}|\geq n\Big\},

we are ready to see that

\begin{split}I_{2}(n,\triangle s)\leq 4\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{s\leq r\leq s+1}|Y_{s,r}^{\mu,\xi}|^{2}{\bf 1}_{\big\{\sup_{s\leq r\leq s+1}|Y_{s,r}^{\mu,\xi}|\geq n\big\}}\Big).\end{split}

Consequently, taking (
34
) into consideration and making use of the uniform integrability of the sequence
\sup_{s\leq r\leq s+1}|Y_{s,s+r}^{\mu,\xi}|
, we arrive at

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}I_{2}(n,\triangle s)=0.

Therefore, combining this with (
37
), we reach (
36
).

In the later context, we aim to demonstrate the assertion (ii). Below, we shall assume that
s\leq t\leq T
,
\mu^{1},\mu^{2}\in\mathcal{C}_{s,T}^{\nu}
and
\xi\in H
.
Again, by invoking Itô’s formula, we deduce from (
2
) and (
3
) that

\mathbb{E}|Y_{s,t}^{\mu_{1},\xi}-Y_{s,t}^{\mu_{2},\xi}|^{2}\leq\int_{s}^{t}\big(\overline{K}_{1}(u)+\overline{K}_{2}(u)\big)\big(\mathbb{E}|Y_{s,u}^{\mu_{1},\xi}-Y_{s,u}^{\mu_{2},\xi}|^{2}+\mathbb{W}_{2}\big(\mu^{1}_{u},\mu_{u}^{2}\big)^{2}\big)\text{\rm{d}}u.

Subsequently, Gronwall’s inequality yields

\mathbb{E}|Y_{s,t}^{\mu_{1},\xi}-Y_{s,t}^{\mu_{2},\xi}|^{2}\leq\int_{s}^{t}\big(\overline{K}_{1}(u)+\overline{K}_{2}(u)\big)\mathbb{W}_{2}\big(\mu^{1}_{u},\mu_{u}^{2}\big)^{2}\text{\rm{d}}u\exp\bigg(\int_{s}^{t}\big(\overline{K}_{1}(u)+\overline{K}_{2}(u)\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}u\bigg).

This further implies that for any
\lambda>0
,

\begin{split}\text{\rm{e}}^{-2\lambda(t-s)}\mathbb{E}|Y_{s,t}^{\mu_{1},\xi}-Y_{s,t}^{\mu_{2},\xi}|^{2}&\leq\int_{s}^{t}\big(\overline{K}_{1}(u)+\overline{K}_{2}(u)\big)\text{\rm{e}}^{-2\lambda(t-u)}\text{\rm{e}}^{-2\lambda(u-s)}\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu^{1}_{u},\mu_{u}^{2})^{2}\text{\rm{d}}u\\
&\quad\times\exp\bigg(\int_{s}^{T}\big(\overline{K}_{1}(u)+\overline{K}_{2}(u)\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}u\bigg)\\
&\leq\sup_{s\leq t\leq T}\int_{s}^{t}\big(\overline{K}_{1}(u)+\overline{K}_{2}(u)\big)\text{\rm{e}}^{-2\lambda(t-u)}\text{\rm{d}}u\\
&\quad\times\exp\bigg(\int_{s}^{T}\big(\overline{K}_{1}(u)+\overline{K}_{2}(u)\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}u\bigg)\mathbb{W}_{2,\lambda}\big(\mu^{1},\mu^{2}\big)^{2}.\end{split}

(38)

Observe that

\begin{split}\mathbb{W}_{2,\lambda}(\Phi(\mu^{1}),\Phi(\mu^{2}))&=\sup_{s\leq t\leq T}\big(\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda(t-s)}\mathbb{W}_{2}\big((\Phi(\mu^{1}))_{s,t,\xi},(\Phi(\mu^{2}))_{s,t,\xi}\big)\big)\\
&\leq\sup_{s\leq t\leq T}\big(\text{\rm{e}}^{-2\lambda(t-s)}\mathbb{E}|Y_{s,t}^{\mu_{1},\xi}-Y_{s,t}^{\mu_{2},\xi}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}.\end{split}

Thus, we conclude from (
38
) that the assertion (ii) is valid by using the fact that both
\overline{K}_{1}(\cdot)
and
\overline{K}_{2}(\cdot)
are locally integrable followed by approaching
\lambda>0
sufficiently large.
∎

Lemma 3.4

(Periodicity)
.

Let Assumption (
A
) hold and suppose further that, for all fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
t\mapsto b_{t}(x,\mu)
and
t\mapsto\sigma_{t}(x,\mu)
are
\tau
-periodic for some constant
\tau>0
.
Then, for any
(s,t)\in\Delta
,
\omega\in\Omega
and
\xi\in L^{2}(\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathscr{F}_{s},\mathbb{P})
,

X_{s+\tau,t+\tau}^{\xi}(\omega)=X_{s,t}^{\xi}(\theta_{\tau}\omega).

(39)

Proof.

In the following analysis, we prescribe
s\leq t\leq T
,
\omega\in\Omega
and
X_{s,s}^{\xi}=\xi\in L^{2}(\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathscr{F}_{s},\mathbb{P})
with
\nu:=\mathscr{L}_{\xi}

) with (
58
) enables us to derive that for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\begin{split}&\langle b_{t}(x,\mu)-b_{t}(y,\nu),x-y\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\|\widehat{\sigma}_{t}(x)-\widehat{\sigma}_{t}(y)\|_{\rm HS}^{2}\\
&\leq\alpha_{t}\big((K_{0}+K_{1})|x-y|^{2}\mathds{1}_{\{|x-y|\leq\ell_{0}\}}-(K_{1}-K_{2})|x-y|^{2}\big)+K_{2}\alpha_{t}\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu,\nu)|x-y|,\end{split}

(59)

Whence, according to
[
49
, Theorem 2.1]
, the McKean-Vlasov SDE (
16
) under investigation has a unique strong solution.

By taking advantage of (
19
) and (
59
), in addition to the fact that
\mathbb{W}_{1}\leq\mathbb{W}_{2}
, for any
\varepsilon>0,t\in\mathbb{R},x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
there exists a positive
\tau
-periodic function
\beta_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
independent of
x
such that

\begin{split}2\langle b_{t}(x,\mu),x\rangle+\|\widehat{\sigma}_{t}(x)\|_{\rm HS}^{2}&=2\langle b_{t}(x,\mu)-b_{t}({\bf 0},\delta_{\bf 0}),x\rangle+\|\widehat{\sigma}_{t}(x)-\widehat{\sigma}_{t}({\bf 0})\|_{\rm HS}^{2}\\
&\quad+2\langle b_{t}({\bf 0},\delta_{\bf 0}),x\rangle+2\langle\widehat{\sigma}_{t}(x)-\widehat{\sigma}_{t}({\bf 0}),\widehat{\sigma}_{t}({\bf 0})\rangle_{\rm HS}+\|\sigma_{t}({\bf 0})\|_{\rm HS}^{2}\\
&\leq 2\alpha_{t}\big((K_{0}+K_{1})|x|^{2}\mathds{1}_{\{|x|\leq\ell_{0}\}}-(K_{1}-K_{2})|x|^{2}\big)+2K_{2}\alpha_{t}\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\delta_{\bf 0})|x|\\
&\quad+2\langle b_{t}({\bf 0},\delta_{\bf 0}),x\rangle+2\langle\widehat{\sigma}_{t}(x)-\widehat{\sigma}_{t}({\bf 0}),\sigma_{t}({\bf 0})\rangle_{\rm HS}+\|\widehat{\sigma}_{t}({\bf 0})\|_{\rm HS}^{2}\\
&\leq\beta_{\varepsilon}(t)-2\alpha_{t}(K_{1}-3K_{2}/2-\varepsilon)|x|^{2}+K_{2}\alpha_{t}\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\delta_{\bf 0})^{2}.\end{split}

(60)

Consequently,
from Itô’s formula and the basic fact that
\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu,\delta_{\bf 0})^{2}=\mu(|\cdot|^{2})
for
\mu\in\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it follows that for all
t\geq s
and
\varepsilon>0,

\text{\rm{e}}^{2(K_{1}-2K_{2}-\varepsilon)\int_{s}^{t}\alpha_{u}\,\text{\rm{d}}u}\mathbb{E}|X_{s,t}|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}|X_{s,s}|^{2}+\int_{s}^{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{2(K_{1}-2K_{2}-\varepsilon)\int_{s}^{u}\alpha_{v}\,\text{\rm{d}}v}\beta_{\varepsilon}(u)\,\text{\rm{d}}u.

In particular, choosing
\varepsilon=(K_{1}-2K_{2})/2>0
due to
K_{1}>2K_{2}
and taking the
\tau
-periodic properties of
\alpha_{\cdot}
and
\beta_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
, and
\int_{0}^{\tau}\alpha_{u}\,\text{\rm{d}}u\in(0,\infty)
into consideration results in the desired assertion (
57
).
∎

The following Lemma demonstrates that the distribution flow starting from different initial distributions is weakly
\mathbb{W}_{1}
-contractive once the perturbation intensity is small (i.e., the corresponding Lipschitz constant of
\widetilde{b}_{t}
is small enough).

Lemma 5.2

(Exponential ergodicity)
.

Assume (
H
) with
\int_{0}^{\tau}\alpha_{u}\,\text{\rm{d}}u>0
. Then, there exist constants
C,\lambda,K_{2}^{*}>0
such that for all
(s,t)\in\Delta
,

K_{2}\in(0,K_{2}^{*}]
and
\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\mathbb{W}_{1}\big(\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,t}^{\mu}},\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,t}^{\nu}}\big)\leq C\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda(t-s)}\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu,\nu),

(61)

where
\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,t}^{\mu}}
stands for the law of
X_{s,t}
, the solution to (
16
), with
\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,s}}=\mu
.

Proof.

In the sequel, we consider the following decoupled SDE associated with (
16
): for any
(s,t)\in\Delta
,

\text{\rm{d}}Y_{s,t}^{\nu,\mu}=\big(\,\widehat{b}_{t}(Y_{s,t}^{\nu,\mu})+(\widetilde{b}_{t}*\mu_{s,t})(Y_{s,t}^{\nu,\mu})\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\text{\rm{d}}B_{t}+\widehat{\sigma}_{t}(Y_{s,t}^{\nu,\mu})\,\text{\rm{d}}W_{t},\quad Y_{s,t}^{\nu,\mu}\sim\nu,

(62)

where
\mu_{s,t}:=\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,t}^{\mu}}
. Due to the strong well-posedness (so weak well-posedness) of (
16
), we have
\mu_{s,t}=\mathscr{L}_{Y_{s,t}^{\mu,\mu}}
.

Below, to avoid the singularity on the diagonal, we introduce the following cut-off function:
for each
\varepsilon>0
,

\phi_{\varepsilon}(r):=\begin{cases}0&,r\in[0,\frac{5\varepsilon}{8}],\\
1-384\varepsilon^{-3}\big(\frac{1}{3}\big(r-\frac{7\varepsilon}{8}\big)+\frac{\varepsilon}{8}\big)\big(r-\frac{7\varepsilon}{8}\big)^{2}&,\frac{5\varepsilon}{8}\leq r\leq\frac{7\varepsilon}{8},\\
1&,r\geq\frac{7\varepsilon}{8}.\end{cases}

(63)

Obviously,
\phi_{\varepsilon}\in C^{1}([0,\infty);[0,1])
.
With the cut-off function
\phi_{\varepsilon}
at hand, we shall work with the following coupled SDE:

\begin{cases}\text{\rm{d}}\overline{Y}_{s,t}^{\mu}=b_{t}(\overline{Y}_{s,t}^{\mu},\mu_{s,t})\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\phi_{\varepsilon}(|Z_{s,t}|)^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{\rm{d}}\overline{B}_{t}\\
\qquad\qquad+\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\big(1-\phi_{\varepsilon}(|Z_{s,t}|)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\text{\rm{d}}\widehat{B}_{t}+\widehat{\sigma}_{t}(\overline{Y}_{s,t}^{\mu})\,\text{\rm{d}}W_{t},\quad\overline{Y}_{s,s}^{\mu}\sim\mu\\
\text{\rm{d}}\widehat{Y}_{s,t}^{\nu}=b_{t}(\widehat{Y}_{s,t}^{\nu},\nu_{s,t})\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\phi_{\varepsilon}(|Z_{s,t}|)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Pi(Z_{s,t})\text{\rm{d}}\overline{B}_{t}\\
\qquad\qquad+\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\big(1-\phi_{\varepsilon}(|Z_{s,t}|)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\text{\rm{d}}\widehat{B}_{t}+\widehat{\sigma}_{t}(\widehat{Y}_{s,t}^{\nu})\,\text{\rm{d}}W_{t},\quad\widehat{Y}_{s,s}^{\nu}\sim\nu,\\
\end{cases}

(64)

where
b_{t}(x,\mu):=\widehat{b}_{t}(x)+(\widetilde{b}_{t}*\mu)(x)
for
t\in\mathbb{R},x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
;
Z_{s,t}:=\overline{Y}_{s,t}^{\mu}-\widehat{Y}_{s,t}^{\nu}
;

(\overline{B}_{t})_{t\geq s}
and
(\widehat{B}_{t})_{t\geq s}
, independent of
(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
,
are mutually dependent
d
-dimensional Brownian motions; for
{\bf n}(x)=\frac{x}{|x|}\mathds{1}_{\{x\neq{\bf 0}\}}
,

\displaystyle\Pi(x):=I_{d\times d}-2{\bf n}(x){\bf n}(x)^{*}\mathds{1}_{\{x\neq{\bf 0}\}}.

(65)

In terms of Lévy’s characterization for Brownian motions, we conclude that
(\overline{Y}_{s,t}^{\mu},\widehat{Y}_{s,t}^{\nu})_{t\geq s}
is a coupling of
(Y_{s,t}^{\mu,\mu},Y_{s,t}^{\nu,\nu})_{t\geq s}
. Owing to existence of optimal couplings, in the analysis below, we shall choose the initial values
\overline{Y}_{s,s}^{\mu}

and
\widehat{Y}_{s,s}^{\nu}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\overline{Y}_{s,s}^{\mu}-\widehat{Y}_{s,s}^{\nu}|=\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu,\nu).

(66)

For any
\delta>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, set
V_{\delta}(x):=(\delta+|x|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
. A direct calculation shows that for any
\varepsilon>0,

\displaystyle\frac{x}{V_{\delta}(x)}\overset{\delta\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}{{\bf n}(x)}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\frac{\delta\phi_{\varepsilon}(|x|)}{V_{\delta}(x)^{3}}\leq\frac{\delta}{(\delta+\varepsilon^{2}/4)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\overset{\delta\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}0.

(67)

Note trivially from (
64
) that

\displaystyle Z_{s,t}=

\displaystyle\big(b_{t}(\overline{Y}_{s,t}^{\mu},\mu_{s,t})-b_{t}(\widehat{Y}_{s,t}^{\nu},\nu_{s,t})\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}t+2\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\phi_{\varepsilon}(|Z_{s,t}|)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf n}(Z_{s,t}){\bf n}(Z_{s,t})^{*}\,\text{\rm{d}}\overline{B}_{t}

\displaystyle\quad+\big(\widehat{\sigma}_{t}(\overline{Y}_{s,t}^{\mu})-\widehat{\sigma}_{t}(\widehat{Y}_{s,t}^{\nu})\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}W_{t}.

Applying Itô’s formula and taking (
59
) into consideration enables us to derive that

\displaystyle\text{\rm{d}}V_{\delta}(Z_{s,t})\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{V_{\delta}(Z_{s,t})}\langle Z_{s,t},b_{t}(\overline{Y}_{s,t}^{\mu},\mu_{s,t})-b_{t}(\widehat{Y}_{s,t}^{\nu},\nu_{s,t})\rangle+\frac{2\delta\alpha_{t}\phi_{\varepsilon}(|Z_{s,t}|)}{V_{\delta}(Z_{s,t})^{3}}+\frac{1}{2V_{\delta}(Z_{s,t})}\|\widehat{\sigma}_{t}(\overline{Y}_{s,t}^{\mu})-\widehat{\sigma}_{t}(\widehat{Y}_{s,t}^{\nu})\|_{\rm HS}^{2}\bigg)\,\text{\rm{d}}t

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\phi_{\varepsilon}(|Z_{s,t}|)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{V_{\delta}(Z_{s,t})}\langle Z_{s,t},\text{\rm{d}}\overline{B}_{t}\rangle+\frac{1}{V_{\delta}(Z_{s,t})}\langle Z_{s,t},(\widehat{\sigma}_{t}(\overline{Y}_{s,t}^{\mu})-\widehat{\sigma}_{t}(\widehat{Y}_{s,t}^{\nu}))\,\text{\rm{d}}W_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha_{t}}{V_{\delta}(Z_{s,t})}\big((K_{0}+K_{1})|Z_{s,t}|^{2}\mathds{1}_{\{|Z_{s,t}|\leq\ell_{0}\}}-(K_{1}-K_{2})|Z_{s,t}|^{2}+K_{2}\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu_{s,t},\nu_{s,t})|Z_{s,t}|\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}t

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2\delta\alpha_{t}\phi_{\varepsilon}(|Z_{s,t}|)}{V_{\delta}(Z_{s,t})^{3}}\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\frac{2\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\phi_{\varepsilon}(|Z_{s,t}|)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{V_{\delta}(Z_{s,t})}\langle Z_{s,t},\text{\rm{d}}\overline{B}_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{V_{\delta}(Z_{s,t})}\langle Z_{s,t},(\widehat{\sigma}_{t}(\overline{Y}_{s,t}^{\mu})-\widehat{\sigma}_{t}(\widehat{Y}_{s,t}^{\nu}))\,\text{\rm{d}}W_{t}\rangle.

This, together with (
67
), leads to

\begin{split}\text{\rm{d}}|Z_{s,t}|\leq&\alpha_{t}\big(\varphi(|Z_{s,t}|)+K_{2}\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu_{s,t},\nu_{s,t})\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}t\\
&\quad+2\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\phi_{\varepsilon}(|Z_{s,t}|)^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle{\bf n}(Z_{s,t}),\text{\rm{d}}\overline{B}_{t}\rangle+\langle{\bf n}(Z_{s,t}),(\widehat{\sigma}_{t}(\overline{Y}_{s,t}^{\mu})-\widehat{\sigma}_{t}(\widehat{Y}_{s,t}^{\nu}))\,\text{\rm{d}}W_{t}\rangle,\end{split}

(68)

where for any
r\geq 0,

\displaystyle\varphi(r):=(K_{0}+K_{1})r\mathds{1}_{\{r\leq\ell_{0}\}}-(K_{1}-K_{2})r.

Define the following function:

\displaystyle f(r)=c_{1}r+\int_{0}^{r}\text{\rm{e}}^{-c_{2}u}\,\text{\rm{d}}u,\quad r\geq 0,

(69)

where

c_{1}:=\text{\rm{e}}^{-c_{2}\ell_{0}}
and
c_{2}:=2(K_{0}+K_{2})\ell_{0}
.
It is easy to see that for any
r\geq 0,

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(r)=c_{1}+\text{\rm{e}}^{-c_{2}r}>0\quad\mbox{ and }\quad f^{\prime\prime}(r)=-c_{2}\text{\rm{e}}^{-c_{2}r}<0.

(70)

Once more, for the parameter

c_{*}:=\frac{c_{1}((2(K_{0}+K_{2}))\wedge(K_{1}-K_{2}))}{1+c_{1}},

applying Itô’s formula gives that

\displaystyle\text{\rm{d}}\Big(\text{\rm{e}}^{c_{*}\int_{s}^{t}\alpha_{r}\,\text{\rm{d}}r}f(|Z_{s,t}|)\Big)\leq\alpha_{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{c_{*}\int_{s}^{t}\alpha_{r}\,\text{\rm{d}}r}\big(

\displaystyle c_{*}f(|Z_{s,t}|)+\psi_{\varepsilon}(|Z_{s,t}|)+K_{2}f^{\prime}(|Z_{s,t}|)\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu_{s,t},\nu_{s,t})\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\text{\rm{d}}M_{s,t},

for some martingale
(M_{s,t})_{t\geq s}
, in which for any
r\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\psi_{\varepsilon}(r):=f^{\prime}(r)\varphi(r)+2f^{\prime\prime}(r)\phi_{\varepsilon}(r).

It is ready to see from (
70
) that for
K_{1}>K_{2}
and some constant
c_{0}>0,

\displaystyle\psi_{\varepsilon}(r)

\displaystyle\leq\big((c_{1}+\text{\rm{e}}^{-c_{2}r})(K_{0}+K_{2})r-2c_{2}\text{\rm{e}}^{-c_{2}r}\big)\phi_{\varepsilon}(r)\mathds{1}_{\{r\leq\ell_{0}\}}-c_{1}(K_{1}-K_{2})r\mathds{1}_{\{r>\ell_{0}\}}

\displaystyle\quad+(c_{1}+\text{\rm{e}}^{-c_{2}r})(K_{0}+K_{2})r(1-\phi_{\varepsilon}(r))\mathds{1}_{\{r\leq\ell_{0}\}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\text{\rm{e}}^{-c_{2}r}\big((K_{0}+K_{2})r-c_{2}\big)\phi_{\varepsilon}(r)\mathds{1}_{\{r\leq\ell_{0}\}}-c_{1}(K_{1}-K_{2})r\mathds{1}_{\{r>\ell_{0}\}}

\displaystyle\quad+2(K_{0}+K_{2})r(1-\phi_{\varepsilon}(r))\mathds{1}_{\{r\leq\ell_{0}\}}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{c_{2}c_{1}}{\ell_{0}}r\mathds{1}_{\{r\leq\ell_{0}\}}-c_{1}(K_{1}-K_{2})r\mathds{1}_{\{r>\ell_{0}\}}+c_{0}r(1-\phi_{\varepsilon}(r))\mathds{1}_{\{r\leq\ell_{0}\}}

\displaystyle\leq-c_{*}f(r)+c_{0}r(1-\phi_{\varepsilon}(r))\mathds{1}_{\{r\leq\ell_{0}\}},

where in the second inequality we used the fact that
c_{1}\leq\text{\rm{e}}^{-c_{2}r}
for
r\leq\ell_{0}
, and the third inequality and the last inequality hold true due to the alternative of
c_{2}
. Whereafter, by invoking the property of
\phi_{\varepsilon}
, we have for any
r\geq 0,

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\psi_{\varepsilon}(r)\leq-c_{*}f(r).

This further implies that

\displaystyle\text{\rm{d}}\Big(\text{\rm{e}}^{c_{*}\int_{s}^{t}\alpha_{r}\,\text{\rm{d}}r}f(|Z_{s,t}|)\Big)\leq K_{2}(1+c_{1})\alpha_{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{c_{*}\int_{s}^{t}\alpha_{r}\,\text{\rm{d}}r}\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu_{s,t},\nu_{s,t})\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\text{\rm{d}}M_{s,t}.

(71)

Recall that
(\overline{Y}_{s,t}^{\mu},\widehat{Y}_{s,t}^{\nu})_{t\geq s}
is a coupling of
(Y_{s,t}^{\mu,\mu},Y_{s,t}^{\nu,\nu})_{t\geq s}
,
\mu_{s,t}=\mathscr{L}_{Y_{s,t}^{\mu,\mu}}
, and
\nu_{s,t}=\mathscr{L}_{Y_{s,t}^{\nu,\nu}}
. Thus, in addition to
\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq 1+c_{1}
,
we deduce that

\displaystyle\text{\rm{e}}^{c_{*}\int_{s}^{t}\alpha_{r}\,\text{\rm{d}}r}\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu_{s,t},\nu_{s,t})

\displaystyle\leq\text{\rm{e}}^{c_{*}\int_{s}^{t}\alpha_{r}\,\text{\rm{d}}r}\mathbb{E}f(|Z_{s,t}|)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}f(|Z_{s,s}|)+K_{2}(1+c_{1})\int_{s}^{t}\alpha_{u}\text{\rm{e}}^{c_{*}\int_{s}^{u}\alpha_{r}\,\text{\rm{d}}r}\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu_{s,u},\nu_{s,u})\,\text{\rm{d}}u.

Subsequently, the Gronwall inequality yields that

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu_{s,t},\nu_{s,t})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}f(|Z_{s,s}|)\text{\rm{e}}^{-(c_{*}-K_{2}(1+c_{1}))\int_{s}^{t}\alpha_{r}\,\text{\rm{d}}r}.

Obviously, there exists a constant
K_{2}^{*}>0
such that
K_{1}>K_{2}
and
c_{*}>K_{2}(1+c_{1})

for any
K_{2}\in(0,K_{2}^{*}]
. As a consequence, the desired assertion (
61
) follows directly by taking (
66
) into consideration.
∎

).
So far, there are considerable literature on the PoC in a finite horizon; see the monographs
[
6
]
and
[
47
]
. In contrast, the research on the PoC in an infinite horizon is scarce.
Recently, there is a great progress on the uniform-in-time PoC for IPSs corresponding to granular media type SDEs with additive noises; see the excellent work
[
20

. In the following context, by following essentially the idea in
[
20
]
, we proceed to tackle the issue on the uniform-in-time PoC for stochastic IPSs concerned with time-inhomogenenous McKean-Vlasov SDE (

) with
multiplicative noise. Furthermore, it is worthy to point out that, to handle the singularity at the zero point, the auxiliary function
\phi_{\varepsilon}
defined in (

Lemma 5.3

(Uniform-in-time PoC)
.

Assume (
H
) with
\int_{0}^{\tau}\alpha_{u}\,\text{\rm{d}}u\in(0,\infty)
, and suppose further
\mathbb{E}|X_{s,s}^{i,N}|^{2}<\infty
for each
i\in\mathbb{S}_{N}
.
Then, there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2},\lambda,K_{2}^{*}>0
such that for all
K_{2}\in(0,K_{2}^{*}]
,
N\geq 2
, and
(s,t)\in\Delta
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{1}\Big(\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,t}^{i}},\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,t}^{i,N}}\Big)\leq C_{1}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda(t-s)}\mathbb{W}_{1}\Big(\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,s}^{i}},\mathscr{L}_{X_{s,s}^{i,N}}\Big)+\frac{C_{2}}{\sqrt{N}},

(72)

where
C_{1},C_{2}
is independent of
(s,t)\in\Delta
.

Proof.

To begin, we recall that
\phi_{\varepsilon}
and
\Pi
were defined respectively in (
63
) and (
65
).
Consider the following SDE for all
i\in\mathbb{S}_{N}
,
(t,s)\in\Delta)
, and
\varepsilon>0,

\begin{cases}\text{\rm{d}}\overline{X}_{s,t}^{i,\varepsilon}=\big(\,\widehat{b}_{t}(\overline{X}_{s,t}^{i,\varepsilon})+(\widetilde{b}_{t}*\overline{\mu}_{s,t}^{i,\varepsilon})(\overline{X}_{s,t}^{i,\varepsilon})\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\phi_{\varepsilon}(|\overline{Z}_{s,t}^{i,N,\varepsilon}|)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\text{\rm{d}}B_{t}^{*,i}\\
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\big(1-\phi_{\varepsilon}(|\overline{Z}_{s,t}^{i,N,\varepsilon}|)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\text{\rm{d}}\widehat{B}_{t}^{*,i}+\widehat{\sigma}_{t}(\overline{X}_{s,t}^{i,\varepsilon})\,\text{\rm{d}}W_{t}^{i},\quad\quad\overline{X}_{s,s}^{i,\varepsilon}\sim X_{s,s}^{i},\\
\text{\rm{d}}\overline{X}_{s,t}^{i,N,\varepsilon}=\big(\,\widehat{b}_{t}(\overline{X}_{s,t}^{i,N,\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\widetilde{b}_{t}(\overline{X}_{s,t}^{i,N,\varepsilon},\overline{X}_{s,t}^{j,N,\varepsilon})\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}t+\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\phi_{\delta}(|\overline{Z}_{s,t}^{i,N,\delta}|)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Pi(\overline{Z}_{s,t}^{i,N,\delta})\,\text{\rm{d}}B_{t}^{*,i}\\
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\big(1-\phi_{\varepsilon}(|\overline{Z}_{s,t}^{i,N,\varepsilon}|)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\text{\rm{d}}\widehat{B}_{t}^{*,i}+\widehat{\sigma}_{t}(\overline{X}_{s,t}^{i,N,\varepsilon})\,\text{\rm{d}}W_{t}^{i},\quad\quad\overline{X}_{s,s}^{i,N,\varepsilon}\sim X_{s,s}^{i,N},\end{cases}

(73)

where
\overline{\mu}_{s,t}^{i,\varepsilon}:=\mathscr{L}_{\overline{X}_{s,t}^{i,\varepsilon}}
,
\overline{Z}_{s,t}^{i,N,\varepsilon}:=\overline{X}_{s,t}^{i,\varepsilon}-\overline{X}_{s,t}^{i,N,\varepsilon},

and
(B^{*,i}_{t})_{t\geq s}
and
(\widehat{B}^{*,i}_{t})_{t\geq s}
are independent copies of
(B^{i}_{t})_{t\geq s}
. We further assume that
(\overline{X}_{s,s}^{i,\varepsilon},\overline{X}_{s,s}^{i,N,\varepsilon}),i\in\mathbb{S}_{N},
are i.i.d random variables with finite second moments.

In the sequel, for the sake of notation brevity, we shall write
\overline{\mu}_{s,t}^{i}
,

\overline{X}_{s,t}^{i}
,
\overline{X}_{s,t}^{i,N}
and
\overline{Z}_{s,t}^{i,N}
instead of
\overline{\mu}_{s,t}^{i,\varepsilon}
,

\overline{X}_{s,t}^{i,\varepsilon}
,
\overline{X}_{s,t}^{i,N,\varepsilon}
and
\overline{Z}_{s,t}^{i,N,\varepsilon}
, respectively.
Set for any
(t,s)\in\Delta,

\displaystyle\overline{{\bf X}}_{s,t}^{N}=(\overline{X}_{s,t}^{1},\cdots,\overline{X}_{s,t}^{N})\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\overline{{\bf X}}_{s,t}^{N,N}=(\overline{X}_{s,t}^{1,N},\cdots,\overline{X}_{s,t}^{N,N}).

Via Lévy’s characterisation for Brownian motions, we infer that
(\overline{{\bf X}}_{s,t}^{N},\overline{{\bf X}}_{s,t}^{N,N})_{t\geq s}
is a coupling of
({\bf X}_{s,t}^{N},{\bf X}_{s,t}^{N,N})_{t\geq s}
, determined by (
20
) and (
21
).

By following the strategy to derive (
68
), we deduce that

\begin{split}\text{\rm{d}}|\overline{Z}_{s,t}^{i,N}|\leq&\alpha_{t}\big(\varphi(|\overline{Z}_{s,t}^{i,N}|)+|\Theta_{s,t}^{i,N}|\big)\,\text{\rm{d}}t\\
&\quad+2\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\phi_{\varepsilon}(|\overline{Z}_{s,t}^{i,N}|)^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle{\bf n}(\overline{Z}_{s,t}^{i,N},\text{\rm{d}}\overline{B}_{t}^{*,i}\rangle+\langle{\bf n}(\overline{Z}_{s,t}^{i,N}),(\widehat{\sigma}_{t}(\overline{Y}_{s,t}^{\mu})-\widehat{\sigma}_{t}(\widehat{Y}_{s,t}^{\nu}))\,\text{\rm{d}}W_{t}^{i}\rangle,\end{split}

(74)

where
\varphi(r):=(K_{0}+K_{1})r\mathds{1}_{\{r\leq\ell_{0}\}}-K_{1}r,r\geq 0,
and

\Theta_{s,t}^{i,N}:=(\widetilde{b}_{t}*\overline{\mu}_{s,t}^{i})(\overline{X}_{s,t}^{i})-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\widetilde{b}_{t}(\overline{X}_{s,t}^{i,N},\overline{X}_{s,t}^{j,N}).

Let the function
f
be defined as in (
69
) with
c_{1}:=\text{\rm{e}}^{-c_{2}\ell_{0}}
and
c_{2}=2(K_{0}+K_{1})\ell_{0}
. Then,
by mimicking the procedure to obtain (
71
), for
c_{*}:=\frac{K_{1}c_{1}}{1+c_{1}}
,
we find that

Remark 5.1
.

In terms of the construction of (

employ the synchronous coupling provided that the distance between the marginal processes is smaller than

In Lemma

, we achieve the optimal decay rate (i.e.,

), which is independent of the dimension

w.r.t. the particle number once one part of the drift part is written as a convolution type and the initial distribution of IPS enjoy finite second moments. Indeed, for the issue on the uniform-in-time PoC, the McKean-Vlasov SDEs can be much more general by taking advantage of

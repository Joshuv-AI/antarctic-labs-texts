Introduction

These notes form the second part of a detailed account of the theory of nilspaces developed by Camarena and Szegedy in the paper
[
9
]
. The material in these notes expands on the third chapter of their paper, providing more detailed proofs of the main results. To that end we also include several additional results that are implicit in
[
9
]
. This material relies strongly on the first part of our exposition, given in
[
8
]
. We shall have to assume some familiarity with the basic theory of nilspaces and some of their algebraic properties, but we shall always refer to the relevant results in
[
8
]
.

Recall the notion of a nilspace from
[
8
, Definition 1.2.1]
. The principal objects treated in these notes are
compact nilspaces
. These are nilspaces equipped with a compact topology that is compatible with the cube structure. To give the precise definition, we adopt the following terminology.

Definition 0.1
.

Throughout the sequel, to be concise we shall use the term
compact space
to mean a compact, Hausdorff, second-countable topological space.

In particular, compact spaces in these notes are Polish spaces
[
28
, Theorem 5.3]
. More generally, topological spaces will usually be assumed to be Hausdorff and second-countable.

Recall from
[
8
, Definition 1.2.3]
the notion of a cubespace.

Definition 0.2
.

A cubespace
\ns
is called a
compact cubespace
if
\ns
is a compact space and
\cu^{n}(\ns)
is a closed subset of
\ns^{\{0,1\}^{n}}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. A
compact nilspace
is a compact cubespace satisfying the ergodicity and corner-completion axioms from
[
8
, Definition 1.2.1]
.

The main goal in the sequel is to characterize compact nilspaces.

A central result in this direction is a description of a general compact nilspace as an inverse limit of simpler spaces, namely compact nilspaces of finite rank (these are defined in Section
6
). This result is treated in Section
8
(see Theorem
8.3
).

Another central result concerns finite-rank compact nilspaces, and involves
nilmanifolds
, i.e. compact homogeneous spaces of nilpotent Lie groups, the study of which goes back to
[
31
]
. To situate the result, let us note that nilmanifolds provide important examples of compact nilspaces. Indeed, a nilmanifold becomes a compact nilspace when, given a filtration on the Lie group (with certain topological properties), the nilmanifold is equipped with the natural cubes associated with the filtration, namely the cubes introduced by Host and Kra
[
25
,
26
]
. This construction is treated from a purely algebraic viewpoint in
[
8
, Section 2.3]
, and its basic topological aspects are detailed in Section
1
below. The central result in question here goes in the converse direction, and states that if a compact nilspace of finite rank has connected structure groups,
1

.
then it is isomorphic to a nilmanifold with a cube structure of the kind mentioned above. This is treated in Section

(see Theorem

).

The work toward these main theorems yields several other results of inherent interest, including the following: endowing every compact nilspace with a Borel probability measure that generalizes the Haar measure on compact abelian groups (Proposition
3.5
); an automatic continuity result for Borel morphisms between compact nilspaces (Theorem
5.6
); a rigidity result for morphisms into compact nilspaces of finite rank (Theorem
9.2
). Several of the main tools used to obtain these results rely on the theory of
continuous systems of measures
, which provides a natural framework for measure theoretic aspects of compact nilspaces. This is detailed in Section
3
.

An alternative treatment of compact nilspaces is given by Gutman, Manners, and Varjú in the series of papers
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
.

Let us end this introduction by evoking one of the central motivations for the study of compact nilspaces. This motivation concerns the analysis of
uniformity norms
. These norms were introduced in arithmetic combinatorics by Gowers
[
15
]
, and independently in ergodic theory (as the analogous
uniformity seminorms
) by Host and Kra
[
25
]
. The uniformity norms, or
U^{d}
norms (one such norm for each integer
d\geq 2
), are defined on the space of complex-valued functions on a finite (or more generally a compact) abelian group. These norms provide useful tools to control averages of bounded functions over certain linear configurations in abelian groups, configurations such as arithmetic progressions (such control can be obtained via the so-called
generalized Von Neumann theorems
); see
[
17
]
and
[
18
]
. A major topic concerning these norms is the analysis, for each
d\geq 2
, of the harmonics that determine whether the
U^{d}
norm of a function is small or large. For the
U^{2}
norm these characteristic harmonics are simply the characters from Fourier analysis. For
d>2
, this topic leads to the theory of
higher order Fourier analysis
. Central to this theory are the results known as
inverse theorems
for the
U^{d}
norms, proved by Green, Tao and Ziegler
[
19
]
, and independently by Szegedy
[
39
]
. Essentially, these theorems tell us that, for the
U^{d}
norm of functions on
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
, one can use as characteristic harmonics the functions known as
(d-1)
-step nilsequences
. In the approach to these theorems developed by Szegedy (consisting principally in
[
39
]
and his work with Camarena
[
9
]
), compact nilspaces play a fundamental role. Indeed, roughly speaking, in this approach a nilsequence on
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
is obtained as a composition
F\co\varphi
, where
\varphi
is a nilspace morphism from
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
to a compact nilspace
\ns
, and
F
is some continuous function
\ns\to\mathbb{C}
. In this way, compact nilspaces play a role in higher order Fourier analysis that generalizes the role played by the circle group
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
in the definition of characters in Fourier analysis. In these notes we shall concentrate on the theory of compact nilspaces in itself. For further introduction to higher order Fourier analysis and its applications, we refer the reader to the survey
[
16
]
.

1
Filtered nilmanifolds as compact nilspaces

Recall from
[
8
, Section 2.2]
the notion of a filtration
G_{\bullet}
on a group
G
and the basic properties of cube sets of the form
\cu^{n}(G_{\bullet})
.

Definition 1.1
.

A
nilmanifold
is a quotient space
G/\Gamma
where
G
is a connected nilpotent Lie group and
\Gamma
is a discrete, cocompact subgroup. If
G_{\bullet}
is a filtration on
G
of degree at most
k
, with terms
G_{i}
being closed subgroups of
G
, and with each subgroup
\Gamma\cap G_{i}
being cocompact in
G_{i}
, then we call
(G/\Gamma,G_{\bullet})
a
filtered nilmanifold
of degree at most
k
.

The groups in the filtration
G_{\bullet}
are sometimes required to be connected (in particular in applications of nilmanifolds in arithmetic combinatorics; see for instance
[
17
, Definition 1.3]
), but we shall not need this assumption here.

A compact nilspace
\ns
is
connected
if the underlying topological space is connected. We now show that every filtered nilmanifold of degree
k
can be viewed as a
k
-step connected compact nilspace, in the following sense.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
G
be a connected nilpotent Lie group with a degree-
k
filtration
G_{\bullet}
of closed subgroups, let
\Gamma\leq G
be discrete, and for each
i\in[k]=\{1,2,\dots,k\}
let
\Gamma_{i}=\Gamma\cap G_{i}
. Let
\ns
denote the coset space
G/\Gamma
equipped with the sets
\cu^{n}(\ns)=\{\pi_{\Gamma}\co\q:\q\in\cu^{n}(G_{\bullet})\}
, where
\pi_{\Gamma}
is the quotient map
G\to G/\Gamma
. Then
\ns
is a connected compact nilspace if and only if
\Gamma_{i}
is cocompact in
G_{i}
for each
i\in[k]
.

Proof.

The nilspace axioms are proved for
\ns
in
[
8
, Proposition 2.3.1]
. To complete the proof, we show that the compactness of each
G_{i}/\Gamma_{i}
is necessary and sufficient for each set
\cu^{n}(\ns)
to be closed in
\ns^{\{0,1\}^{n}}
.

To see the sufficiency, it is enough to deduce that
\cu^{n}(G_{\bullet})\cap\Gamma^{\{0,1\}^{n}}
is cocompact in
\cu^{n}(G_{\bullet})
, as then
\cu^{n}(\ns)
is homeomorphic to the compact set
\cu^{n}(G_{\bullet})/\big(\cu^{n}(G_{\bullet})\cap\Gamma^{\{0,1\}^{n}}\big)
(see
[
30
, Proposition 2.7]
). This cocompactness is proved elsewhere in the literature but we include a proof here for completeness, following the argument from
[
18
, Lemma E.10]
. By Definition
1.1
there is a compact set
K_{j}\subseteq G_{j}
such that the product set
K_{j}\,\Gamma_{j}
equals
G_{j}
. Recall from
[
8
, Lemma 2.2.5]
that every cube
\q\in\cu^{n}(G_{\bullet})
has a unique factorization
\q=g_{0}^{F_{0}}g_{1}^{F_{1}}\cdots g_{2^{n}-1}^{F_{2^{n}-1}}

with
g_{i}\in G_{\codim(F_{i})}
for each
i
, where the
F_{i}
are the upper faces of
\{0,1\}^{n}
, of the form
F_{i}=F(v_{i})=\{v:\supp(v)\supset\supp(v_{i})\}
and the
v_{i}
are ordered in the colex order. We then have the following inductive formula for the coefficients
g_{i}
:

g_{0}=\q(v_{0}),\text{ and for each }i>0,\;g_{i}=\Big(\prod_{j<i:\;\supp(v_{j})\subset\supp(v_{i})}g_{j}^{F_{j}}\Big)^{-1}\cdot\q\,(v_{i}).

(1)

Let
H_{0}=\cu^{n}(G_{\bullet})
and for each
i\in[2^{n}]
let
H_{i}=\{\q\in\cu^{n}(G_{\bullet}):g_{0}=\dots=g_{i-1}=\mathrm{id}_{G}\}
. It follows from (
1
) that
\q\in H_{i}
if and only if
\q(v_{j})=\mathrm{id}_{G}
for all
j<i
. We deduce that
H_{i}
is a normal subgroup of
\cu^{n}(G_{\bullet})
. We shall now use these subgroups to show that every
\q\in\cu^{n}(G_{\bullet})
is a product of an element of
\cu^{n}(G_{\bullet})\cap\Gamma^{\{0,1\}^{n}}
with an element of
K:=\{k_{0}^{F_{0}}\dots k_{2^{n}-1}^{F_{2^{n}-1}}:k_{i}\in K_{i}\}
. This will prove the claimed sufficiency, since the set
K
is compact. Let
i\in[2^{n}]
and suppose that
\q\in H_{i-1}
. Then
\q=g_{i}^{F_{i}}\,\q^{\prime}
, where
\q^{\prime}\in H_{i}
. We then have
g_{i}^{F_{i}}=(k_{i}\gamma_{i})^{F_{i}}
where
k_{i}\in K_{\codim(F_{i})}
and
\gamma_{i}\in\Gamma_{\codim(F_{i})}
. Since
H_{i}
is normal, we have

\q=k_{i}^{F_{i}}\;\q^{\prime\prime}\;\gamma_{i}^{F_{i}}
with
\q^{\prime\prime}\in H_{i}
. Starting with any cube in
H_{0}
and repeating this procedure, we eventually obtain the desired factorization showing that
\q\in K\cdot\big(\cu^{n}(G_{\bullet})\cap\Gamma^{\{0,1\}^{n}}\big)
.

To see the necessity of each set
G_{i}/\Gamma_{i}
being compact, let
Q_{i}
denote the set of cubes
\q\in\cu^{i}(\ns)
such that
\q(v)
is the trivial coset
\Gamma
for all
v\neq 0^{i}
. Since
\cu^{i}(\ns)
is compact, so is
Q_{i}
. We have
\q=\pi_{\Gamma}\co\q^{\prime}
for some
\q^{\prime}\in\cu^{i}(G_{\bullet})
with
\q^{\prime}(v)\in\Gamma
for
v\neq 0^{i}
. The fact that
\q^{\prime}\in\cu^{i}(G_{\bullet})
implies that the Gray-code product
\sigma_{i}(\q^{\prime})
lies in
G_{i}
(recall
[
8
, Definition 2.2.22 and Proposition 2.2.25]
). By the formula for the Gray-code product, we deduce that
\sigma_{i}(\q^{\prime})\q^{\prime}(0^{i})^{-1}\in\Gamma
. It follows that
\q^{\prime}(0^{i})\in G_{i}\,\Gamma
. Thus the projection
\pi_{0^{i}}:\q\to\q(0^{i})
maps
Q_{i}
into
(G_{i}\,\Gamma)/\Gamma
. Conversely, for every coset
g\Gamma
with
g\in G_{i}
there is a cube
\q\in Q_{i}
with
\pi_{0^{i}}(\q)=g\Gamma
, namely
\q=\pi_{\Gamma}\co\q^{\prime}
where
\q^{\prime}(0^{i})=g
and
\q^{\prime}(v)=\mathrm{id}_{G}
otherwise. It follows that
\pi_{0^{i}}(Q_{i})=(G_{i}\,\Gamma)/\Gamma
, so the compactness of
Q_{i}
implies that
(G_{i}\,\Gamma)/\Gamma
is compact, whence
G_{i}/\Gamma_{i}
is compact.
∎

Characterization of compact nilspaces

2
Topological preliminaries

In order to characterize a general compact nilspace, we first have to examine how the basic algebraic structures treated in
[
8
]
behave under the additional topological assumptions in Definition
0.2
.

We begin with a result showing that the closure condition on
\cu^{n}(\ns)
needs to be checked only for
n=k+1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

A
k
-step nilspace
\ns
is a compact nilspace if and only if
\ns
is a compact space and
\cu^{k+1}(\ns)
is a closed subset of
\ns^{\{0,1\}^{k+1}}
.

Proof.

Note that for all
n\leq m
we can view
\cu^{n}(\ns)
as the projection of
\cu^{m}(\ns)
to
\ns^{\{0,1\}^{n}}
(by the composition axiom, embedding
\{0,1\}^{n}
as a face in
\{0,1\}^{m}
). In particular, if we assume that
\cu^{k+1}(\ns)
is closed then for every
n<k+1
the projection
\cu^{n}(\ns)
is also closed. If
n>k+1
, then for any
(k+1)
-dimensional face
F
let

Q_{F}=\big\{f:\{0,1\}^{n}\to\ns~|~f\co\phi_{F}\in\cu^{k+1}(\ns)\big\},

where
f\co\phi_{F}
is the restriction of
f
to
F
(recall
[
8
, Definition 1.1.5]
). Thus
Q_{F}
is the preimage of a closed set under the restriction map
f\mapsto f\co\phi_{F}
, and is therefore closed. Then, by
[
8
, Lemma 3.2.13]
, we have
\cu^{n}(\ns)=\bigcap_{F}Q_{F}
where
F
runs through
(k+1)
-dimensional faces of
\{0,1\}^{n}
.
∎

In the remainder of this section we establish topological properties of the structures in
[
8
, Chapter 3]
under the compactness assumption. The main result that we shall obtain along these lines is that if a finite-step nilspace is compact, then the associated abelian bundle obtained in
[
8
, Theorem 3.2.19]
is endowed with a topology making it an iterated
continuous
abelian bundle (in a sense that we shall formalize in Definition
2.6
), with structure groups being compact abelian groups.

We begin by looking at the behaviour of some basic constructions. Recall from
[
8
, Definitions 3.1.19 and 3.1.22]
the notions of arrow spaces and the spaces
\partial_{x}\ns
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\ns
be a compact nilspace, let
\ab
be a compact abelian group, and fix any
x\in\ns
. Then the arrow spaces
\ns\Join_{i}\ns
,
i\geq 1
, the nilspaces
\cD_{k}(\ab)
,
k\geq 1
, and the space
\partial_{x}\ns
, are all compact nilspaces.

Proof.

The space
\ns\times\ns
underlying
\ns\Join_{i}\ns
is compact and
\cu^{k+1}(\ns\Join_{i}\ns)
is the image under a continuous map of a closed set of cubes in
\cu^{k+1+i}(\ns)
(see
[
8
, (3.4)]
), so it is a compact set as required. The cube set
\cu^{k+1}(\cD_{k}(\ab))
is also compact, since it is the set of solutions of a linear equation (recall
[
8
, (2.9)]
). Finally, the cube set
\cu^{k+1}(\partial_{x}\ns)
is a closed subset of
\cu^{k+1}(\ns\Join_{1}\ns)
(recall
[
8
, Definition 3.1.22]
).
∎

We now look at the more involved constructions, especially those using quotients by equivalence relations.

Remark 2.3
.

We shall use the following standard facts.

(i)

Let
X,Y
be compact spaces (as in Definition
0.1
). Then a function
f:X\rightarrow Y
is continuous if and only if its graph is closed. (This is a special case of the closed graph theorem
[
32
, Ex. 8, p. 171]
.)

(ii)

Compact spaces are metrizable. (This follows from Urysohn’s metrization theorem
[
32
, Thm. 34.1, p. 215]
, using that a compact Hausdorff space is regular
[
32
, Ex. 3, p. 205]
.)

(iii)

Let
X
be a compact space and let
\sim
be a closed equivalence relation on
X
, that is
\{(x,y):x\sim y\}
is a closed subset of
X\times X
. Then
X/\sim
is compact in the quotient topology
[
6
, Prop. 8, p. 105]
.

Recall from
[
8
, Definition 1.2.3]
that
\ns
is said to be
k
-fold ergodic
if
\cu^{k}(\ns)=\ns^{\{0,1\}^{k}}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\ns
be a
k
-step
k
-fold ergodic compact nilspace. Then
\ns
is isomorphic as a compact nilspace to
\cD_{k}(\ab)
for some compact abelian group
\ab
.

Proof.

As seen in
[
8
, Proposition 2.4.1]
, in the case
k=1
, if we fix any
e\in\ns
as a distinguished point, then the cube structure on
\ns
yields a commutative group operation on
\ns
with identity element
e
, making
\ns
a principal homogeneous space of the resulting abelian group
\ab
. Let us check the continuity of addition and inverse on
\ab
. The graph of addition can be written

\{(x_{00},x_{10},x_{01},x_{11})\in\cu^{2}(\ns):x_{00}=e\}.

The graph of the inverse is similarly defined by the equation
x_{00}=x_{11}=e
. These are closed sets, so the operations are continuous.

For
k>1
, as in
[
8
, Proposition 3.2.14]
we consider the 1-step nilspace
\partial_{e}^{k-1}(\ns)
, which is here a compact nilspace, and therefore isomorphic to
\cD_{1}(\ab)
for some compact abelian group
\ab
. We then argue just as in the proof of
[
8
, Proposition 3.2.14]
to show that
\cu^{n}(\ns)=\cu^{n}(\cD_{k}(\ab))
for all
n
.
∎

Recall from
[
8
, Definition 3.2.3]
the relation
\sim_{k}
on a nilspace
\ns
and the corresponding nilspace factor
\cF_{k}(\ns)
from
[
8
, Lemma 3.2.10]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\ns
be a compact nilspace and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\cF_{k}(\ns)
with the quotient topology is a compact nilspace.

Proof.

As a subset of
\ns\times\ns
, the relation
\sim_{k}
consists of restrictions to the 1-face
\{0^{k+1},(1,0,\dots,0)\}
of the cubes satisfying the condition in
[
8
, Lemma 3.2.4]
. The set of these cubes is

\cu^{k+1}(\ns)\;\cap\;\big\{f\in\ns^{\{0,1\}^{k+1}}~:~\forall\,v,v^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{k+1}\setminus\{0^{k+1}\},\;f(v)=f(v^{\prime})\big\},

and is therefore closed.
∎

We now move toward the main result of this section, namely the addition of suitable topological properties to the bundle decomposition of a nilspace.

2.1
Continuous abelian bundles

Recall from
[
8
, Definition 3.2.17]
the notion of a
k
-fold abelian bundle.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\bnd
be an abelian bundle with base
S
, structure group
\ab
and projection
\pi
. We say that
\bnd
is
continuous
if the following conditions hold:

(i)

\bnd
and
S
are topological spaces.

(ii)

\ab
is an abelian topological group.

(iii)

The action
\alpha:\ab\times\bnd\to\bnd
is continuous.

(iv)

A set
U\subset S
is open if and only if
\pi^{-1}(U)
is open in
\bnd
.

We say that
\bnd
is a
compact
abelian bundle if, in addition to these conditions,
\bnd,S,\ab
are compact spaces.

Remark 2.7
.

Recall that by
[
8
, Definition 3.2.17]
the projection
\pi
yields a bijection between
S
and the orbits of
\alpha
, namely the bijection
s\mapsto\pi^{-1}(s)
. It follows that we obtain a definition equivalent to Definition
2.6
if we replace condition (iv) with the following:

(iv’)  Denoting by
\bnd/\alpha
the set of
\ab
-orbits equipped with the quotient topology, we have that the map

S\to\bnd/\alpha
,
\;s\mapsto\pi^{-1}(s)
is a homeomorphism.

Thus the base
S
is topologically identified with the orbit space for the
\ab
-action on
\bnd
. In particular, since the orbit map
\bnd\to\bnd/\alpha
,
x\mapsto x+\ab
is an open map, we have that the projection
\pi
is an open map. Note also that if
\ab
is compact, then the orbit map is also a closed map, and hence so is
\pi
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\ns=\bnd_{k}
be a
k
-fold abelian bundle, with factors
\bnd_{i}
,
i=0,\ldots,k
. We say that
\ns
is a
k
-fold compact abelian bundle
if for each
i\in[k]
the factor
\bnd_{i}
is a compact
\ab_{i}
-bundle with base
\bnd_{i-1}
. A
compact degree-
k
bundle
is a
k
-fold compact abelian bundle that is also a compact cubespace with respect to the same topology, and such that condition (3.5) from
[
8
, Definition 3.2.18]
is satisfied, that is, for every
i\in[0,k-1]
and every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\cu^{n}(\bnd_{i})=\{\pi_{i}\co\q:\q\in\cu^{n}(\ns)\}
, and for every
\q\in\cu^{n}(\bnd_{i+1})
we have

\{\q_{2}\in\cu^{n}(\bnd_{i+1}):\pi_{i}\co\q=\pi_{i}\co\q_{2}\}=\{\q+\q_{3}:\q_{3}\in\cu^{n}(\cD_{i+1}(\ab_{i+1}))\}.

(2)

We can now establish the main result of this subsection.

Proposition 2.9
.

A compact cubespace
\ns
is a compact degree-
k
bundle if and only if
\ns
is a
k
-step compact nilspace.

Proof.

The algebraic part of the statement is given by
[
8
, Theorem 3.2.19]
, so we only need to check the topological properties in each direction.

If
\ns
is a compact degree-
k
bundle then in particular it is a compact cubespace satisfying the nilspace axioms, so it is a
k
-step compact nilspace. Conversely, if
\ns
is a
k
-step compact nilspace then, to show that the degree-
k
bundle structure given by
[
8
, Theorem 3.2.19]
is compact, it suffices to show that the abelian structure group
\ab_{k}
can be equipped with a topology making it a compact abelian group such that
\ns
is a compact
\ab_{k}
-bundle with base
\cF_{k-1}(\ns)
(if we show this then the result follows by repeating the argument for
\ab_{k-1}
and so on by induction). For this it suffices to show that
\ab_{k}
can be equipped with a compact topology such that the action of
\ab_{k}
on
\ns
is continuous (since Lemma
2.5
then implies the remaining condition (iv’) in Definition
2.6
).
Let
F
be a class of
\sim_{k-1}
in
\ns
. From the proof of
[
8
, Corollary 3.2.16]
we know that
F
together with the
F
-valued cubes in each
\cu^{n}(\ns)
is a
k
-fold ergodic
k
-step nilspace. Using
[
8
, Lemma 3.2.4]
we deduce that
F
is closed and it follows that
F
is a compact nilspace. Then by Lemma
2.4
there is a compact topology on
\ab_{k}
such that
F
is isomorphic to
\cD_{k}(\ab_{k})
as a compact nilspace. We now check that the action of
\ab_{k}
is continuous on
F
by showing that its graph
\{(a,y_{0},y_{0}+a):a\in\ab,y_{0}\in\ns\}
is closed in
\ab_{k}\times\ns^{2}
. This graph can be described using the arrow space
\nss=\ns\Join_{1}\ns
. Indeed, recall from
[
8
, Lemma 3.2.15]
that for
x=(x_{0},x_{1})
,
y=(y_{0},y_{1})
in
F^{2}\subset\nss
we have
x\sim_{k-1}y
if and only if
x_{0}-x_{1}=y_{0}-y_{1}
in
\ab_{k}
. It follows that, fixing some
e\in F
, the graph above is homeomorphic to the closed set
\{(x,y)\in F^{2}\times F^{2}:x\sim_{k-1}y,\;x_{0}=e\}
. We have thus shown that from any given fibre
F
the group
\ab_{k}
acquires a compatible compact topology making its action on
F
continuous. To see that this topology is the same for every fibre, recall that by
[
8
, Lemma 3.2.24]
for any two fibres
F_{0},F_{1}
and fixed points
x_{i}\in F_{i}
, we have an isomorphism
\vartheta:\ab_{F_{0}}\to\ab_{F_{1}}
, defined by
\vartheta(a)=b
if and only if
(x_{0},x_{1})\sim_{k-1}(x_{0}+a,x_{1}+b)
. We then see that
\vartheta
is continuous by identifying its graph with the closed set
\{y\in F_{0}\times F_{1}:y\sim_{k-1}(x_{0},x_{1})\}
. It follows that
\vartheta
is a homeomorphism.
∎

Recall the notions of a bundle morphism
[
8
, Definition 3.3.1]
and of a sub-bundle
[
8
, Definition 3.3.3]
. These notions are transferred to the setting of compact abelian bundles in a straightforward manner, by specifying in their definitions that all the maps involved are continuous. The same holds for the kernel of a bundle morphism
\psi:\bnd\to\bnd^{\prime}
(recall
[
8
, Definition 3.3.4]
). Note that such a kernel
K
is indeed a compact abelian bundle, since each set
K_{i}
in
[
8
, Definition 3.3.4]
is a closed subset of
\bnd_{i}\times\bnd^{\prime}
and therefore compact, and every structure group
\ker(\alpha_{i})
is compact.

Recall also that in the purely algebraic setting we had a description of a set of restricted morphisms
\hom_{f}(P,\ns)
as a sub-bundle of
\ns^{P}
, namely
[
8
, Lemma 3.3.11]
. This has the following version for compact nilspaces.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
P
be a subcubespace of
\{0,1\}^{n}
with the extension property, let
S
be a subcubespace of
P
with the extension property in
P
, let
\ns
be a
k
-step compact nilspace and let
f:S\to\ns
be a morphism. Then
\hom_{f}(P,\ns)
is a compact
k
-fold abelian bundle that is a sub-bundle of
\ns^{P}
, with factors
\hom_{\pi_{i}\co f}(P,\ns_{i})
and structure groups
\hom_{S\to 0}(P,\cD_{i}(\ab_{i}))
, where
\ab_{i}
is the
i
-th structure group of
\ns
.

Proof.

We just have to check the topological properties. The set
\hom_{f}(P,\ns)
is closed in
\ns^{P}
and therefore compact. For each
i\in[k]
the abelian group
\hom_{S\to 0}(P,\cD_{i}(\ab_{i}))
is also compact, as a closed subgroup of
\ab_{i}^{P}
, and the action of
\hom_{S\to 0}(P,\cD_{i}(\ab_{i}))
inherits continuity from that of
\ab_{i}
. Condition (iv) from Definition
2.6
is also inherited from
\ns^{P}
since the topology on each factor
\hom_{\pi_{i}\co f}(P,\ns_{i})
is the subspace topology from
\ns_{i}^{P}
.
∎

2.2
Metrics

By Remark
2.3
(ii), we know that a compact nilspace can always be equipped with a compatible metric. We shall often use the fact that this metric can be assumed to be invariant under the action of the last structure group. We record this fact as follows.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\ns
be a
k
-step compact nilspace and let
d_{0}
be a metric on
\ns
generating its topology. Define
d(x,y)=\int_{z\in\ab_{k}}d_{0}(x+z,y+z)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\ab_{k}}(z)
, for any
x,y\in\ns
. Then
d
is a
\ab_{k}
-invariant metric that generates the same topology on
\ns
.

This invariant metric is a basic construction in the theory of
G
-spaces (see
[
33
, Proposition 1.1.12]
).

Proof.

It is clear that
d
is a
\ab_{k}
-invariant metric. To see that
d
generates the same topology as
d_{0}
, note first that the function
\ns\times\ns\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
(x,y)\mapsto d(x,y)
is continuous and in particular for a fixed
x\in\ns
we have that
y\mapsto d(x,y)
is continuous (relative to
d_{0}
). It follows that the topology generated by
d
is a subtopology of the given topology on
\ns
. In particular the former topology is compact. However, that topology is also Hausdorff, so it is maximal among the compact topologies on
\ns
, and so it must be the original topology on
\ns
(see
[
6
, §9.4, Corollary 3]
).
∎

From now on we always assume that a metric on
\ns
is
\ab_{k}
-invariant in this sense. Given such a metric
d
on
\ns
, we may define the following quotient metric on
\cF_{k-1}(\ns)
(see
[
33
, Proposition 1.1.12]
):

d^{\prime}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=\inf\big\{d(x,y):x,y\in\ns,\;\pi_{k-1}(x)=x^{\prime},\;\pi_{k-1}(y)=y^{\prime}\big\}.

(3)

In the sequel we shall use the following fact several times.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
\ns
be a
k
-step compact nilspace, and let
\cor^{k+1}(\ns)
denote the set of
(k+1)
-corners on
\ns
with the subspace topology obtained from the product topology on
\ns^{\{0,1\}^{k+1}\setminus\{1^{k+1}\}}
. Let
\comp:\cor^{k+1}(\ns)\to\ns
denote the map sending a
(k+1)
-corner
\q^{\prime}
to
\q(1^{k+1})
, where
\q
is the unique completion of
\q^{\prime}
. Then
\comp
is continuous.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. If
\comp
were not continuous then there would exist some corner
\q^{\prime}
, some open set
U\ni\comp(\q^{\prime})
and a sequence of corners
\q_{n}^{\prime}
converging to
\q^{\prime}
such that for all
n
we have
\comp(\q_{n}^{\prime})\not\in U
. Let
\q_{n}
denote the completion in
\cu^{k+1}(\ns)
of
\q^{\prime}_{n}
. By the compactness of
\cu^{k+1}(\ns)
there exists a subsequence
(\q_{m})
of
(\q_{n})
such that
\q_{m}
converges to some cube
\q\in\cu^{k+1}(\ns)
. This convergence combined with the assumption that
\q_{n}^{\prime}\to\q^{\prime}
implies that for each
v\neq 1^{k+1}
we have
\q(v)=\q^{\prime}(v)
, so by uniqueness of completion we have
\q(1^{k+1})=\comp(\q^{\prime})\in U
. On the other hand, the convergence
\q_{m}\to\q
also implies that
\q_{m}(1^{k+1})\to\q(1^{k+1})
. Since by assumption
\q_{m}(1^{k+1})=\comp(\q_{m}^{\prime})\not\in U
for all
m
, we have
\q(1^{k+1})\not\in U
, a contradiction.
∎

Let us record also the following consequence of Lemma
2.12
, to the effect that proximate cubes on
\cF_{k-1}(\ns)
can always be lifted to proximate cubes on
\ns
.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
\ns
be a
k
-step compact nilspace and let
d,d^{\prime}
be the metrics on
\ns
,
\cF_{k-1}(\ns)
given by Lemma
2.11
and (
3
) respectively. Then for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that the following holds. If
\q_{1},\q_{2}\in\cu^{k+1}(\cF_{k-1}(\ns))
satisfy

for all
v
, then there exist cubes
\tilde{\q}_{1},\tilde{\q}_{2}\in\cu^{k+1}(\ns)
such that
\pi_{k-1}\co\tilde{\q}_{i}=\q_{i}
for
i=1,2
and
d\big(\tilde{\q}_{1}(v),\tilde{\q}_{2}(v)\big)\leq\epsilon
for all

.

Proof.

Let
P=\{0,1\}^{k+1}\setminus\{1^{k+1}\}
and note that, by definition of

and
[
8
, Remark 3.2.12]
, the corners
\q_{i}|_{P}
can be lifted to corners
\q_{i}^{\prime}
,
i=1,2
such that

for every

. By Lemma

, if

is sufficiently small then the unique completions
\tilde{\q}_{i}
of

.
∎

3
Measure-theoretic preliminaries

In this section we collect the main tools from measure theory that we shall use in the sequel.

A notion that unifies most of these tools is that of a
continuous system of measures
, discussed in Subsection
3.1
.

Using continuous systems of measures, we shall then define in Subsection
3.2
an invariant measure on a continuous abelian bundle, and use this to construct a generalization of the Haar measure for compact nilspaces.

In Subsection
3.3
we collect several constructions of probability spaces that will be used repeatedly later in the chapter.

3.1
Continuous systems of measures

Recall that a measure
\mu
on a measure space
X
is said to be
concentrated on

S\subset X
if
\mu(X\setminus S)=0
.

The study of continuous systems of measures goes back at least to Bourbaki (see
[
7
, Ch. V, §3]
). A recent treatment is given in
[
10
]
. We shall use the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X,Y
be compact spaces and let
\pi:X\to Y
be continuous. A
continuous system of measures
(CSM) on the map
\pi
is a family of Borel measures
\{\mu_{y}:y\in Y\}
on
X
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

For every
y\in Y
the measure
\mu_{y}
is concentrated on
\pi^{-1}(y)
.

(ii)

For every continuous function
f:X\to\mathbb{C}
, the function
Y\to\mathbb{C}
,
y\mapsto\int_{\pi^{-1}(y)}f\;\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{y}
is continuous.

As explained in
[
10
]
, replacing
\mathbb{C}
by
\mathbb{R}
in condition (
ii
) yields an equivalent definition.

Note that all the CSMs that we shall consider in these notes consist of strictly positive measures
\mu_{y}
, i.e. Borel measures taking positive values on non-empty open sets.

A simple example of a CSM is obtained by taking a product space, as follows.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
V
and
Y
be compact spaces, let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure on
V
, let
X=V\times Y
and let
\pi:X\to Y,\,(v,y)\mapsto y
. For each
y\in Y
let
\mu_{y}
denote the measure on
X
concentrated on
\pi^{-1}(y)
, where it equals the pushforward of
\mu
under the map
V\to\pi^{-1}(y)
,
v\mapsto(v,y)
. Then
\{\mu_{y}:y\in Y\}
is a CSM on
\pi
.

Proof.

Let
f:V\times Y\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function. Then
f
is a uniform limit of functions of the form
F:(v,y)\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i,1}(v)f_{i,2}(y)
where
f_{i,1}:V\to\mathbb{R}
,
f_{i,2}:Y\to\mathbb{R}
are continuous. Indeed, the algebra of such functions
F
satisfies the assumptions of the Stone-Weierstrass theorem
[
37
, Appendix A, §A14]
. (The fact that this algebra separates points follows from Urysohn’s lemma
[
32
, Theorem 33.1]
.) Now, for a function
f_{i,1}(v)f_{i,2}(y)
as above, it is clear that integrating over
v
yields a continuous function of
y
. It follows that
y\mapsto\int_{\pi^{-1}(y)}f\;\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{y}
is a uniform limit of continuous functions and is therefore continuous.
∎

One of our main uses of CSMs below is to equip any compact abelian bundle with a probability measure that is invariant under the action of the structure group. This measure will be obtained as a simple application of the following result concerning general CSMs.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\{\mu_{y}:y\in Y\}
be a family of Borel probability measures on
X
forming a CSM on
\pi:X\to Y
, and let
\nu
be a Borel probability measure on
Y
. Then the following function on Borel sets
E\subset X
is a Borel probability measure:

\mu(E)=\int_{Y}\mu_{y}\big(E\cap\pi^{-1}(y)\big)\,\,\mathrm{d}\nu(y).

Proof.

This is a special case of
[
10
, Corollary 3.7]
, using
[
10
, Proposition 2.23]
.
∎

3.2
Haar measure on compact abelian bundles and nilspaces

Recall that every compact abelian group can be equipped with a regular Borel probability measure that is invariant under translation, called the (normalized) Haar measure
[
37
]
. In this subsection we shall construct a generalization of this measure for compact nilspaces. As we shall see, there is a natural inductive construction of a probability measure on a
k
-fold compact abelian bundle, starting with the Haar measure on a compact abelian group. This construction will yield the desired measure on a
k
-step compact nilspace, thanks to
[
8
, Theorem 3.2.19]
.

Our first step, then, is to define a Haar measure on a compact abelian bundle.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\bnd
be a compact abelian bundle with base
S
, structure group
\ab
and projection
\pi
. Let
\mu_{S}
be a regular Borel probability measure on
S
. Then there is a unique regular Borel probability measure
\mu
on
\bnd
that is invariant under the action of
\ab
and satisfies
\mu_{S}=\mu\co\pi^{-1}
.

We call
\mu
the
Haar measure
on
\bnd
(given
\mu_{S}
).

Proof.

For each
s\in S
the set
\pi^{-1}(s)
is a principal homogeneous space of
\ab
, so there is a
\ab
-equivariant homeomorphism
f:\ab\to\pi^{-1}(s)
. Let
\mu_{s}
be the pushfoward measure
\mu_{\ab}\co f^{-1}
on
\pi^{-1}(s)
, where
\mu_{\ab}
is the Haar measure on
\ab
. If we show that
\{\mu_{s}:s\in S\}
is a CSM on
\pi
, then by Lemma
3.3
we can define a Borel measure
\mu
on
\bnd
by the following formula:

\mu(E)=\int_{S}\;\mu_{s}\big(\pi^{-1}(s)\cap E\big)\;\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{S}(s),

(4)

for every Borel set
E\subset\bnd
.

To show that
\{\mu_{s}:s\in S\}
is a CSM on
\pi
we check condition (
ii
) from Definition
3.1
. Given any continuous function
f:\bnd\to\mathbb{R}
, we need to show that
g:S\to\mathbb{R}
,

s\mapsto\int_{\pi^{-1}(s)}f\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{s}

is continuous. Since
S
has the quotient topology (Definition
2.6
), it suffices to show that
g\circ\pi:\bnd\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous.
Now
g(\pi(b))=\int_{\ab}f(a+b)\;\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\ab}(a)=\int_{\ab}f^{\prime}(a,b)\;\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\ab}(a)
, where the function
f^{\prime}:\ab\times\bnd\to\mathbb{R},\;(a,b)\mapsto f(a+b)
is continuous. Therefore, the continuity of
g\circ\pi
follows from (the proof of) Lemma
3.2
applied with
V=\ab
and
Y=\bnd
.

We thus obtain the Borel measure
\mu
given by (
4
), and
\mu
is regular by the standard fact that any Borel probability measure on a metric space is regular
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
. The
\ab
-invariance of
\mu
clearly follows from that of each measure
\mu_{s}
. The uniqueness can be deduced from uniqueness of Haar measure on
\ab
combined with a disintegration result for invariant measures, such as
[
36
, Proposition 2]
.
∎

As mentioned above, this lemma yields the following result.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\bnd
be a compact
k
-fold abelian bundle, with factors
\bnd_{0},\bnd_{1},\dots,\bnd_{k}=\bnd
and structure groups
\ab_{1},\dots,\ab_{k}
. Then there is a unique regular Borel probability measure
\mu
on
\bnd
with the following property: for each
i\in[k]
, letting
\pi_{i}
be the projection
\bnd\to\bnd_{i}
, we have that the Borel probability measure
\mu\co\pi_{i}^{-1}
on
\bnd_{i}
is invariant under the action of
\ab_{i}
.

In particular, for every
k
-step compact nilspace
\ns
, applying the proposition to the bundle structure we obtain the measure that we shall call the
Haar measure on

\ns
.

Proof.

By induction on
i\in[k]
, starting with the Haar probability on
\ab_{1}
and applying Lemma
3.4
for
i=2,\dots,k
.
∎

Recall from
[
8
, Definition 3.3.1]
that a bundle morphism
\phi:\bnd\to\bnd^{\prime}
between
k
-fold abelian bundles is said to be
totally surjective
if the structure morphism
\alpha_{i}:\ab_{i}\to\ab_{i}^{\prime}
is surjective for each
i\in[k]
.

The following basic result is the analogue for nilspaces of the fact that continuous surjective homomorphisms between compact abelian groups preserve the Haar measures.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\bnd,\bnd^{\prime}
be compact
k
-fold abelian bundles, and let
\phi:\bnd\to\bnd^{\prime}
be a totally surjective continuous bundle morphism. Then
\phi
preserves the Haar measures, that is, for every Borel set
E\subset\bnd^{\prime}
we have
\mu_{\bnd}\big(\phi^{-1}(E)\big)=\mu_{\bnd^{\prime}}(E)
.

Proof.

We argue by induction on
k
. The case
k=1
follows from the fact recalled above. For
k>1
, recall that by
[
8
, Definition 3.3.1]
the map
\phi
induces a totally surjective continuous bundle morphism
\phi_{k-1}:\bnd_{k-1}\to\bnd^{\prime}_{k-1}
. Let
\{\mu_{s}:s\in\bnd_{k-1}\}
and
\{\mu^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}:s^{\prime}\in\bnd^{\prime}_{k-1}\}
be the CSMs on
\pi_{k-1}:\bnd\to\bnd_{k-1}
,
\pi_{k-1}^{\prime}:\bnd^{\prime}\to\bnd_{k-1}^{\prime}
respectively (as in the proof of Lemma
3.4
). For
s\in\bnd_{k-1}
and any Borel set
E\subset\bnd^{\prime}
, let
f_{\bnd}(s)=\mu_{s}\big(\pi_{k-1}^{-1}(s)\cap\phi^{-1}(E)\big)
, and for
s^{\prime}\in\bnd_{k-1}^{\prime}
let
f_{\bnd^{\prime}}(s^{\prime})=\mu^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}\big({\pi^{\prime}}^{-1}_{k-1}(s^{\prime})\cap E\big)
. The restriction of
\phi
to a fibre
\pi_{k-1}^{-1}(s)
, being a continuous affine homomorphism onto some fibre
{\pi^{\prime}}^{-1}_{k-1}(s^{\prime})
, preserves the measures
\mu_{s},\mu^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}
. Hence we have
f_{\bnd}(s)=f_{\bnd^{\prime}}(\phi_{k-1}(s))
for each
s\in\bnd_{k-1}
. Since
\phi_{k-1}
is measure-preserving (by the induction hypothesis), we have

\mu_{\bnd}\big(\phi^{-1}(E)\big)=\int_{\bnd_{k-1}}f_{\bnd}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\bnd_{k-1}}=\int_{\bnd_{k-1}}f_{\bnd^{\prime}}\co\phi_{k-1}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\bnd_{k-1}}=\int_{\bnd_{k-1}^{\prime}}f_{\bnd^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\bnd_{k-1}^{\prime}}=\mu_{\bnd^{\prime}}(E).\qed

Recall from
[
8
, Definition 3.3.7]
the notion of a fibre-surjective morphism.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
\ns,\ns^{\prime}
be
k
-step compact nilspaces and let
\phi:\ns\to\ns^{\prime}
be a continuous fibre-surjective morphism. Then
\phi
preserves the Haar measures.

Proof.

We combine Lemma
3.6
with
[
8
, Lemma 3.3.8]
.
∎

In the sequel we shall have to use CSMs on certain maps from cube sets
\cu^{n}(\ns)
to
\ns
, such as the evaluation map
\q\mapsto\q(0^{n})
. These CSMs will be obtained as special cases of the construction in the next lemma.

Recall from
[
8
, Definition 3.2.17]
the notion of a
relative

k
-fold abelian bundle. We may similarly define a
compact
relative
k
-fold abelian bundle
\bnd
with ground set
\bnd_{0}
. Note that each fibre of the projection
\pi_{0}:\bnd\to\bnd_{0}
is then itself a compact
k
-fold abelian bundle and so it has a Haar measure by Lemma
3.4
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\bnd
be a compact relative
k
-fold abelian bundle with factors
\bnd_{0},\bnd_{1},\ldots,\bnd_{k-1}
. Then the Haar measures on the fibres of the projection
\pi_{0}:\bnd\to\bnd_{0}
form a CSM on
\pi_{0}
.

To prove this we shall have to compose several CSMs, in the following sense.

Definition 3.9
.

Given CSMs
\{\mu_{y}:y\in Y\}
on
\pi:X\to Y
and
\{\mu_{z}:z\in Z\}
on
\tau:Y\to Z
, their
composition
is the CSM
\{\nu_{z}:z\in Z\}
on
\tau\co\pi:X\to Z
consisting of measures defined for Borel sets
E\subset X
by

\nu_{z}(E)=\int_{\tau^{-1}(z)}\mu_{y}\big(\pi^{-1}(y)\cap E\big)\;\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{z}(y).

(5)

The fact that this composition is indeed a CSM is established in
[
10
, Proposition 3.3]
.

Proof of Lemma
3.8
.

By the proof of Lemma
3.4
, for each
i\in[k]
there is a CSM on the bundle map
\pi_{i-1,i}:\bnd_{i}\to\bnd_{i-1}
consisting of the pushforward of the Haar measure on
\ab_{i}
to each fibre of
\pi_{i-1,i}
. Let us compose these CSMs for
i\in[k]
, and let
\{\mu_{x}:x\in\bnd_{0}\}
be the resulting CSM on
\pi_{0}
. For each
x\in\bnd_{0}
, a straightforward inductive argument using (
5
) shows that the measure
\mu_{x}
on the compact
k
-fold abelian bundle
\pi^{-1}(x)
satisfies the properties of the measure in Proposition
3.5
, so it must be the Haar measure on
\pi^{-1}(x)
.
∎

The following result is an analogue for compact abelian bundles of the quotient integral formula.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\bnd,\bnd^{\prime}
be compact
k
-fold abelian bundles, with Haar measures
\mu,\mu^{\prime}
respectively, and let
\phi:\bnd\to\bnd^{\prime}
be a totally surjective continuous bundle morphism. For each
t\in\bnd^{\prime}
let
\mu_{t}
denote the Haar measure on
\phi^{-1}(t)
. Then the measures
\mu_{t}
,
t\in\bnd^{\prime}
disintegrate
\mu
relative to
\mu^{\prime}
, that is for every Borel set
E\subset\bnd
we have
\mu(E)=\int_{\bnd^{\prime}}\mu_{t}\big(\phi^{-1}(t)\cap E\big)\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\prime}(t)
.

Recall from
[
8
, Definition 3.3.4]
the notion of the kernel of a bundle morphism.

Proof.

The kernel of

\bnd^{\prime}

are compact

-fold abelian bundles (recall

\nu(E)=\int_{\bnd^{\prime}}\mu_{t}\big(\phi^{-1}(t)\cap E\big)\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\prime}(t).

(6)

We have to show that

. For this it suffices to show that

has the invariance properties that characterize the Haar measure

, namely that for each projection

\ab_{i}

For

Supposing now that the claim holds for

(as in (

\nu\co\pi_{k}^{-1}(E)\;=\;\int_{\bnd^{\prime}}\mu_{t}\big(\pi_{k}^{-1}(E)\cap\phi^{-1}(t)\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\prime}(t)\;=\;\int_{\bnd^{\prime}_{k}}\Big(\int_{{\pi^{\prime}_{k}}^{-1}(s)}\mu_{t}\big(\pi_{k}^{-1}(E)\cap\phi^{-1}(t)\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\prime}_{s}(t)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\prime}_{k}(s).

For each
s\in\bnd^{\prime}_{k}
and each

\phi^{-1}(t)

\mu_{t}\big(\pi_{k}^{-1}(E)\cap\phi^{-1}(t)\big)=\mu_{s}\big(E\cap\phi_{k}^{-1}(s)\big)

\nu\co\pi_{k}^{-1}(E)=\int_{\bnd^{\prime}_{k}}\Big(\int_{{\pi^{\prime}_{k}}^{-1}(s)}\mu_{s}\big(E\cap\phi_{k}^{-1}(s)\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{s}^{\prime}(t)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}^{\prime}(s)=\int_{\bnd^{\prime}_{k}}\mu_{s}\big(E\cap\phi_{k}^{-1}(s)\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}^{\prime}(s).

The right side here is indeed the measure on

constructed in the same way as
\nu
in (

is
\ab_{k+1}
-invariant.

Again we use the disintegration of
\mu^{\prime}
relative to
\mu^{\prime}_{k-1}
, so that for any Borel set

\nu(E)=\int_{\bnd^{\prime}_{k}}\Big(\int_{{\pi^{\prime}_{k}}^{-1}(s)}\mu_{t}\big(E\cap\phi^{-1}(t)\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{s}^{\prime}(t)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{k}^{\prime}(s).

For each

, we claim that the inner integral on the right side here integrates

over a union of orbits of
\ab_{k+1}
. Indeed, we have
\mu_{t}\big(E\cap\phi^{-1}(t)\big)=\int_{\phi^{-1}(t)}1_{E}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(x)

\int_{{\pi^{\prime}_{k}}^{-1}(s)}\;\int_{\phi_{k}^{-1}(s)}\;\int_{\pi_{k}^{-1}(y)\cap\phi^{-1}(t)}1_{E}(x)\;\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\pi_{k}^{-1}(y)}(x)\;\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{s}(y)\;\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{s}^{\prime}(t).

By Fubini’s theorem we can interchange the two outer integrals, so this equals

\int_{\phi_{k}^{-1}(s)}\;\int_{{\pi^{\prime}_{k}}^{-1}(s)}\;\int_{\pi_{k}^{-1}(y)\cap\phi^{-1}(t)}1_{E}(x)\;\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\pi_{k}^{-1}(y)}(x)\;\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{s}^{\prime}(t)\;\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{s}(y).

For each

{\pi^{\prime}_{k}}^{-1}(s)
is a
\ab_{k+1}^{\prime}
-torsor (or principal homogeneous space of

\int_{\ab_{k+1}^{\prime}}\int_{\pi_{k}^{-1}(y)\cap\phi^{-1}(t_{0}+r)}1_{E}(x)\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\pi_{k}^{-1}(y)}(x)\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\ab_{k+1}^{\prime}}(r)=\int_{\ab_{k+1}^{\prime}}\int_{\ker(\alpha_{k+1})}1_{E}(x_{r}+u)\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\ker(\alpha_{k+1})}(u)\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\ab_{k+1}^{\prime}}(r).

Note that the sets
\{x_{r}+u:u\in\ker(\alpha_{k+1})\}
,

form a partition of
\pi_{k}^{-1}(y)
. It follows from the quotient integral formula

that for any

\int_{\ab_{k+1}}1_{E}(x_{0}+z)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\ab_{k+1}}(z)=\int_{\pi_{k}^{-1}(y)}1_{E}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\pi_{k}^{-1}(y)}(x).

This is clearly invariant under shifting

∎

Recall the fact that the Haar measure on a compact abelian group is strictly positive. This generalizes to compact nilspaces.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\ns
be a
k
-step compact nilspace, with Haar measure
\mu
. Then for every open set
U\subset\ns
we have
\mu(U)>0
.

Proof.

We argue by induction on
k
. The case
k=1
follows from the fact recalled above. Let
k>1
and let
U
be open in
\ns
. By Remark
2.7
we have that
\pi_{k-1}
is an open map, so
B=\pi_{k-1}(U)
is open in
\cF_{k-1}(\ns)
. Hence, letting
\mu^{\prime}
denote the Haar measure on
\cF_{k-1}(\ns)
, we have by induction that
\mu^{\prime}(B)>0
. By regularity of
\mu^{\prime}
there is a compact subset
C\subset B
such that
\mu^{\prime}(C)>0
, and then since
\pi_{k-1}
preserves Haar measures we have
\mu(\pi_{k-1}^{-1}(C))>0
. Now by Remark
2.7
we also have that
\pi_{k-1}
is a proper map, so
\pi_{k-1}^{-1}(C)
is a compact set. This set is covered by the union of open sets
\bigcup_{z\in\ab_{k}}U+z
, so there is a finite set of translates of

by elements of
\ab_{k}
that covers
\pi_{k-1}^{-1}(C)

\mu(U)>0
.
∎

3.3
Probability spaces of morphisms

Recall from
[
8
, Definition 3.1.3]
that given a cubespace
P
and a subcubespace
S
of
P
, we say that
S
has the
extension property
in
P
if for every non-empty nilspace
\ns
and every morphism
g^{\prime}:S\to\ns
there is a morphism
g:P\to\ns
with
g|_{S}=g^{\prime}
.

In this subsection we collect several constructions of probability spaces of morphisms between nilspaces, which will be used repeatedly in the sequel. These constructions rely on two basic ideas.

Firstly, every set of restricted morphisms
\hom_{f}(P,\ns)
has a compact abelian-bundle structure, as described in Lemma
2.10
, which we restate here for convenience.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
P
be a subcubespace of
\{0,1\}^{n}
with the extension property, let
S
be a subcubespace of
P
with the extension property in
P
, let
\ns
be a
k
-step compact nilspace and let
f:S\to\ns
be a morphism. Then
\hom_{f}(P,\ns)
is a compact
k
-fold abelian bundle that is a sub-bundle of
\ns^{P}
, with factors
\hom_{\pi_{i}\co f}(P,\ns_{i})
and structure groups
\hom_{S\to 0}(P,\cD_{i}(\ab_{i}))
, where
\ab_{i}
is the
i
-th structure group of
\ns
.

As a consequence of this structure we have a Haar measure on
\hom_{f}(P,\ns)
, by Proposition
3.5
.

The second idea consists in giving a simple description of the measure-theoretic properties of a restriction map from one set of such morphisms to another. In particular, we want a convenient criterion to decide whether, for a subcubespace
S
of
P
, the restriction map
\hom(P,\ns)\to\hom(S,\ns)
preserves the Haar measures. The following notion provides a general criterion of this type.

Definition 3.13
.

Let
P
be a cubespace, and let
P_{1},P_{2}
be subcubespaces of
P
. We call the pair of sets
P_{1},P_{2}
a
good pair
if
P_{1}
and
P_{1}\cap P_{2}
both have the extension property in
P
and, for every abelian group
\ab
and every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, every morphism
f^{\prime}:P_{2}\to\cD_{k}(\ab)
satisfying
f^{\prime}|_{P_{1}\cap P_{2}}=0
extends to a morphism
f:P\to\cD_{k}(\ab)
satisfying
f|_{P_{1}}=0
.

Note that in particular if
S
has the extension property in
P\subset\{0,1\}^{n}
and
P_{1}=\emptyset
then
P_{1},S
is a good pair in
P
.
The main purpose of this definition is the following result.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
P\subset\{0,1\}^{n}
be a subcubespace with the extension property, and let
P_{1},P_{2}
be a good pair in
P
. Let
\ns
be a
k
-step nilspace and let
f:P_{1}\to\ns
be a morphism. Then the restriction map

\phi:\hom_{f}(P,\ns)\to\hom_{f|_{P_{1}\cap P_{2}}}(P_{2},\ns)

(7)

is a totally-surjective bundle morphism. In particular, if
\ns
is a
k
-step compact nilspace, then
\phi
preserves the Haar measures. Moreover, the Haar measures on the fibres
\phi^{-1}(t)
form a CSM on
\phi
that disintegrates the Haar measure on
\hom_{f}(P,\ns)
relative to the Haar measure on
\hom_{f|_{P_{1}\cap P_{2}}}(P_{2},\ns)
.

Proof.

We prove that
\phi
is a totally-surjective bundle morphism by induction on
k
. For
k=0
the claim holds trivially, so let
k\geq 1
and suppose that
\ns
is a
k
-step nilspace and that the lemma holds for
(k-1)
-step nilspaces. To see that condition (i) from
[
8
, Definition 3.3.1]
holds, note that by a straightforward calculation we have that
\phi
induces a map
\phi_{k-1}:\hom_{\pi_{k-1}\co f}(P,\ns_{k-1})\to\hom_{\pi_{k-1}\co f|_{P_{1}\cap P_{2}}}(P_{2},\ns_{k-1})
well-defined by
\pi_{k-1}\co g\mapsto\pi_{k-1}\co\phi(g)
, so the condition holds for

. We also have that
\phi_{k-1}
is precisely the restriction map on
\hom_{\pi_{k-1}\co f}(P,\ns_{k-1})
, so by induction the condition holds for all

being the restriction
\hom_{P_{1}\to 0}(P,\cD_{i}(\ab_{i}))\to\hom_{P_{1}\cap P_{2}\to 0}(P_{2},\cD_{i}(\ab_{i}))
. To check that
\phi
is totally surjective, by induction it suffices to check that
\alpha_{k}
is surjective, which holds by Definition
3.13
. Lemma
3.6
then implies that

preserves the Haar measures. The last sentence of the lemma follows from Lemma
3.10
.
∎

Another useful feature of good pairs is the following extension result for morphisms.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
P
be a subcubespace of
\{0,1\}^{n}
with the extension property, and let

be a good pair in

has the following extension property: any morphism

-step nilspace

Proof.

We argue again by induction on
k
. The statement is trivial for
k=0
, as
\ns
is then a 1-point space, so suppose that the lemma holds for
(k-1)
-step nilspaces and let
\ns
be a

-step nilspace.
Let
f:P_{1}\cup P_{2}\to\ns
be a morphism and let
f_{2}:P\to\cF_{k-1}(\ns)
be an extension of
\pi_{k-1}\co f
. By
[
8
, Lemma 3.2.11]
there is a morphism
f_{2}^{\prime}:P\to\ns
such that
\pi_{k-1}\co f_{2}^{\prime}=f_{2}
. Let
g=f^{\prime}_{2}|_{P_{1}}-f|_{P_{1}}
. By Definition
3.13
, there is an extension
f_{3}:P\to\cD_{k}(\ab_{k})
of

g_{2}=f_{2}^{\prime}-f_{3}

g_{2}|_{P_{1}}=f|_{P_{1}}
. Now let

be an extension of

to

∎

For the remainder of this section we apply the results above to define various probability spaces of morphisms. We begin with the cube sets
\cu^{n}(\ns)
on a compact nilspace. Note that

Lemma 3.16
.

Let

-step compact nilspaces and let

By
[
8
, Lemma 3.3.12 (ii)]
we have that

is a totally surjective bundle morphism, so it preserves the Haar measures by Lemma

. Moreover, by
[

, Lemma 3.3.12 (iii)]

.

into a CSM on the projection

Lemma 3.17
.

Let

By Lemma

with

, with

4
Topological spaces associated with continuous systems of measures

Definition 4.1
.

Let
X,Y
be compact spaces and let
\mu
be a Borel measure on
X
. We denote by
L(X,Y)
the quotient of the set of Borel measurable functions
f:X\to Y
by the equivalence relation
\sim
defined by
f\sim g\;

\Leftrightarrow

\;\mu\big(\{x\in X:f(x)\neq g(x)\}\big)=0
.

In this section, given a continuous system of measures, we construct an associated topological space that will be crucial in the sequel. In order to motivate the construction, let us outline how it will be used.

The central application will be given in the next section, which is one of the main sections of this chapter. The aim there is to show that given a compact nilspace
\ns
and a compact abelian group
\ab
, given a cocycle
\rho:\cu^{k}(\ns)\to\ab
(recall
[
8
, Definition 3.3.14]
), if
\rho
is Borel measurable then the extension
M(\rho)
constructed in
[
8
, Proposition 3.3.26]
can be equipped with a compact nilspace structure compatible with that of
\ns
. The purpose of the construction in this section is to provide an ambient continuous abelian bundle from which
M(\rho)
will inherit the desired topological structure. The construction will be completed with Proposition
4.13
.

Let us now turn to the definition of the topological space in the construction. To motivate this, consider the following general theme of the application mentioned above: given a Borel measurable function on a space (e.g. the cocycle
\rho
), we have to build a topological space that is associated with the function in a useful way. A very basic example of such a construction is the following: let
\ab
be a compact abelian group, suppose that we only know the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
\ab
(not the underlying topology), and that we are given a Borel function
f:\ab\to\mathbb{R}
. Then we can build a topology on
\ab
naturally associated with
f
using the
L^{1}
seminorm on the Borel functions on
\ab
(relative to the Haar measure), by restricting this seminorm to the translates of
f
, i.e. the functions
f_{z}:x\mapsto f(x+z)
,
z\in\ab
. More precisely, we can define a pseudometric
d
on
\ab
by
d(z,z^{\prime})=\|f_{z}-f_{z^{\prime}}\|_{L^{1}(\ab)}
, and then take the topology generated by the open balls with respect to
d
. The central application mentioned above will use a more elaborate version of this idea. Indeed, recall from
[
8
, (3.11)]
that the extension generated by the cocycle
\rho
is
M(\rho)=\bigcup_{x\in\ns}\{\rho_{x}+z:z\in\ab\}
, where for each
x
we denote by
\rho_{x}
the restriction of
\rho
to the set of rooted cubes
\cu^{k}_{x}(\ns)=\{\q\in\cu^{k}(\ns):\q(0^{k})=x\}
. To put a useful topology on
M(\rho)
, we shall first put an analogue of the
L^{1}
-topology on each family of shifts
\rho_{x}+z,\,z\in\ab
, for each
x\in\ns
, and we shall then want to tie together adequately these different topologies over different points
x
into a global topology. Now the Haar measures on sets
\cu^{k}_{x}(\ns)
form a CSM, by Lemma
3.17
. This leads to the following definition, which concerns CSMs more generally, and which provides a topology that will be adequate for our purposes.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
V,W,Z
be compact spaces, and let
\{\mu_{w}:w\in W\}
be a family of strictly positive Borel probability measures forming a CSM on a continuous map
\pi:V\to W
. For each function
f
in
\bigcup_{w\in W}L\big(\pi^{-1}(w),Z\big)
, let
\tilde{\pi}(f)
be the element
w\in W
such that
f\in L(\pi^{-1}(w),Z)
. We define the topological space
\cL(V,Z)
to be the set
\bigcup_{w\in W}L\big(\pi^{-1}(w),Z\big)
equipped with the coarsest topology making the following functions continuous:

\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}:f\mapsto\int_{\pi^{-1}(\tilde{\pi}(f))}F_{1}(f(v))\;F_{2}(v)~\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\tilde{\pi}(f)}(v),

(8)

for every pair of continuous functions
F_{1}:Z\to\mathbb{C}
and
F_{2}:V\to\mathbb{C}
.

We shall discuss below how this topology relates to the
L^{1}
topology, but for now let us record some of its basic properties.

Proposition 4.3
.

The topological space
\cL(V,Z)
is regular Hausdorff and second-countable, and the map
\tilde{\pi}
is continuous.

In particular
\cL(V,Z)
is metrizable, by Urysohn’s theorem
[
32
, Theorem 34.1]
.

Proof.

We show first that
\tilde{\pi}
is continuous. Fix a metric on
W
, and let
U
be any open subset of
W
. Then for any
w\in U
there is an open ball
B_{r}(w)\subset U
of radius
r>0
. By Urysohn’s lemma
[
32
, Theorem 33.1]
there exists a continuous function
f_{w}
with value 1 on the closure of
B_{r/2}(w)
and value 0 outside
B_{r}(w)
. Let
B_{w}^{\prime}
denote the open set
\{x\in W:f_{w}(x)>0\}
, and note that
U=\bigcup_{w\in U}B_{w}^{\prime}
. By definition of
\cL(V,Z)
, for every
w\in U
the function
\varphi_{1,f_{w}\co\pi}
is continuous. Since
\varphi_{1,f_{w}\co\pi}(f)=f_{w}(\tilde{\pi}(f))
, this function is positive precisely on
\tilde{\pi}^{-1}(B_{w}^{\prime})
, so this set is open. Therefore
\tilde{\pi}^{-1}(U)=\bigcup_{w\in U}\tilde{\pi}^{-1}(B_{w}^{\prime})
is an open set.

Next we show that
\cL(V,Z)
is Hausdorff. Let
f,f^{\prime}
be distinct points in
\cL(V,Z)
. We distinguish two cases. In the first case
f,f^{\prime}
lie in different fibres of
\tilde{\pi}
. Then since
W
is Hausdorff there exist disjoint open sets
U,U^{\prime}
containing
\tilde{\pi}(f)
,
\tilde{\pi}(f^{\prime})
respectively. The preimages of these sets under
\tilde{\pi}
are open sets separating
f,f^{\prime}
. In the second case we have
f,f^{\prime}
in the same fibre
L(\pi^{-1}(w),Z)
. Then there are Borel functions
f_{0},f_{0}^{\prime}
in the classes
f,f^{\prime}
respectively such that the set
D=\{v:f_{0}(v)\neq f_{0}^{\prime}(v)\}
has
\mu_{w}(D)>0
. For some
\epsilon<\mu_{w}(D)
to be fixed later, by Lusin’s theorem
[
37
, Appendix E, §E8]
there exist continuous functions
g,g^{\prime}
and a set
C\subset\pi^{-1}(w)
of probability at most
\epsilon
such that
f_{0}=g
and
f_{0}^{\prime}=g^{\prime}
outside
C
. Then there is a point
v_{0}\in D\setminus C
where
g(v_{0})\neq g^{\prime}(v_{0})
. By continuity there exist
r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}>0
such that for all
v\in B_{r_{1}}(v_{0})\subset V
we have
g(v)\in B_{r_{2}}(g(v_{0}))\subset Z
,
g^{\prime}(v)\in B_{r_{3}}(g^{\prime}(v_{0}))
, and
B_{r_{3}}(g^{\prime}(v_{0}))\cap B_{2r_{2}}(g(v_{0}))=\emptyset
. Urysohn’s lemma gives us continuous functions
F_{1}:Z\to[0,1]
,
F_{2}:V\to[0,1]
, such that
1_{B_{r_{2}}(g(v_{0}))}\leq F_{1}\leq 1_{B_{2r_{2}}(g(v_{0}))}
and
1_{B_{r_{1}/2}(v_{0})}\leq F_{2}\leq 1_{B_{r_{1}}(v_{0})}
. We then have
\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}(g)\geq\mu_{w}(B_{r_{1}/2}(v_{0}))>0
, whereas
\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}(g^{\prime})=0
. We also have
|\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}(g)-\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}(f_{0})|\leq 2\mu_{w}(C)
and similarly for
g^{\prime},f_{0}^{\prime}
. Hence, choosing
\epsilon
small enough, we deduce that
\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}(f_{0})>\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}(f_{0}^{\prime})
.

To see that
\cL(V,Z)
is regular, first note that since by definition the finite intersections of sets
\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}^{-1}(U)
form a base for the topology, we have that for every closed set
S
and
f\not\in S
, there exist
\varphi_{F_{1,i},F_{2,i}}^{-1}(U_{i})
,
i\in[N]
such that
f\in\bigcap_{i}\varphi_{F_{1,i},F_{2,i}}^{-1}(U_{i})
and
S\subset\bigcup_{i}\varphi_{F_{1,i},F_{2,i}}^{-1}(\mathbb{C}\setminus U_{i})
. Since
\mathbb{C}
is regular, for each
i
there exist disjoint open sets
U_{i}^{\prime},U_{i}^{\prime\prime}
such that
\varphi_{F_{1,i},F_{2,i}}(f)\in U_{i}^{\prime}\subset U_{i}
and
\mathbb{C}\setminus U_{i}\subset U_{i}^{\prime\prime}
. Then the open sets
\bigcap_{i}\varphi_{F_{1,i},F_{2,i}}^{-1}(U_{i}^{\prime})
and
\bigcup_{i}\varphi_{F_{1,i},F_{2,i}}^{-1}(U_{i}^{\prime\prime})
are disjoint, the former contains
f
, and the latter includes
S
.

Finally, we show that
\cL(V,Z)
is second-countable. It suffices to find sequences of continuous functions
F_{1,i}:Z\to\mathbb{C}
,
F_{2,j}:V\to\mathbb{C}
and open sets
U_{k}\subset\mathbb{C}
,
i,j,k\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for every
\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}
, every open set
U\subset\mathbb{C}
, and every
f\in\cL(V,Z)
with
\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}(f)\in U
, there exist
i,j,k
such that
f\in\varphi_{F_{1,i},F_{2,j}}^{-1}(U_{k})\subset\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}^{-1}(U)
. Indeed, if this holds then the finite intersections of sets
\varphi_{F_{1,i},F_{2,j}}^{-1}(U_{k})
form a countable base for the topology on
\cL(V,Z)
.
Let
\{U_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a base of open discs in
\mathbb{C}
. The spaces of continuous functions
C(V,\mathbb{C})
and
C(Z,\mathbb{C})
, equipped with the uniform metric, are separable
[
28
, Theorem (4.19)]
. Let
(F_{1,i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(F_{2,j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be separating sequences for
C(V,\mathbb{C})
and
C(Z,\mathbb{C})
respectively. Given any
\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}
, open set
U\subset\mathbb{C}
, and
f\in\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}^{-1}(U)
, for some
\epsilon>0
there is a disc
U_{k}\subset U
of radius at most
\epsilon
with center at most
\epsilon/2
away from
\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}(f)
and such that the distance from
U_{k}
to the complement of
U
is at least
\epsilon
. There exist also
i,j
such that
\|F_{1}-F_{1,i}\|_{\infty}<\epsilon/(4(1+\|F_{2}\|_{\infty}))
and
\|F_{2}-F_{2,j}\|_{\infty}<\epsilon/(4(1+\|F_{1,i}\|_{\infty}))
. Then for every
g\in\cL(V,Z)
, letting
w=\tilde{\pi}(g)
, we have

|\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}(g)-\varphi_{F_{1,i},F_{2,j}}(g)|\leq\int_{\pi^{-1}(w)}|F_{1}(g(v))F_{2}(v)-F_{1,i}(g(v))F_{2,j}(v)|\;\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}(v).

By the triangle inequality, this is at most
\|F_{2}\|_{\infty}\,\|F_{1}-F_{1,i}\|_{\infty}+\|F_{1,i}\|_{\infty}\,\|F_{2}-F_{2,j}\|_{\infty}<\epsilon/2
.
This implies that
f\in\varphi_{F_{1,i},F_{2,j}}^{-1}(U_{k})\subset\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}^{-1}(U)
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

There are spaces
\cL(V,Z)
that do not admit any complete metric. For example, let
V
be the one-point compactification of the half-strip
[0,\infty)\times[0,1]\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, let
W
be the one-point compactification of
[0,\infty)
, let
\pi:V\to W
be the projection to the first coordinate, let
Z=\{0,1\}
, and for each
w\in W\setminus\{\infty\}
let the interval
\pi^{-1}(w)
be equipped with the Lebesgue probability measure. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
f_{n}
be the function on
\pi^{-1}(\{n\})
that takes value
0
on
\{n\}\times[0,1/2)
and
1
on
\{n\}\times[1/2,1]
. The sequence
(f_{n})
is Cauchy for any compatible metric on
\cL(V,Z)
but it does not converge to a function
\{\infty\}\to Z
. To see this, we can view
\cL(V,Z)
as a subspace of a larger space
D(V,Z)=\bigcup_{w\in W}L\big(\pi^{-1}(w),P(Z)\big)
, where each fibre
L\big(\pi^{-1}(w),P(Z)\big)
consists of probability-valued functions
\tilde{f}
on
\pi^{-1}(w)
, i.e. functions mapping each
v\in\pi^{-1}(w)
to a probability measure
p
on
Z
. (We can identify
L\big(\pi^{-1}(w),P(Z)\big)
with
L\big(\pi^{-1}(w),[0,1]\big)
by identifying
\tilde{f}
with the map
v\mapsto p_{v}(\{0\})
.) The topology on
D(V,Z)
is the initial topology generated by the functions
\tilde{f}\mapsto\int_{\pi^{-1}(w)}\mathbb{E}\big(F_{1}\,|\,\tilde{f}(v)\big)\;F_{2}(v)~\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}(v)
. This restricts to the original topology on
\cL(V,Z)
. A straightforward calculation shows that
f_{n}
converges in
D(V,Z)
to the function
\tilde{f}
mapping
\infty
to the uniform distribution on
\{0,1\}
. (I am grateful to Szegedy for providing this example.)

The topology on
\cL(V,Z)
can be viewed as a generalization of the
L^{1}
topology, as illustrated by the following result.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
V,W
be compact spaces, let
Z
be the closed unit disc in
\mathbb{C}
, and let
w\in W
. Then a sequence
(f_{n})
in
L(\pi^{-1}(w),Z)
converges to
f
in
\cL(V,Z)
if and only if it converges to
f
in
L^{1}(\pi^{-1}(w),Z)
.

This is a special case of a more general result that we shall use in the sequel, namely Lemma
4.7
below. This result involves the following generalization of the
L^{1}
distance for functions taking values in a compact metric space.

Definition 4.6
.

Given a compact space
Y
with a Borel probability measure, and a compact space
Z
with a compatible metric
d
, we define the metric
d_{1}
on
L(Y,Z)
by

d_{1}(f_{1},f_{2})=\mathbb{E}_{v\in Y}\;d\big(f_{1}(v),f_{2}(v)\big).

(9)

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
V,W,Z
be compact spaces, let
\{\mu_{w}:w\in W\}
be a CSM on
\pi:V\to W
, let
d
be a compatible complete metric on
Z
, and let
w\in W
. Then a sequence
(f_{n})
in
L(\pi^{-1}(w),Z)
converges to
f
in
\cL(V,Z)
if and only if
d_{1}(f,f_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}
be any of the functions generating the topology on
\cL(V,Z)
. We then have

|\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}(f_{n})-\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}(f)|\leq\|F_{2}\|_{\infty}\int_{\pi^{-1}(w)}|F_{1}(f_{n}(v))-F_{1}(f(v))|\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}(v).

We claim that if
d_{1}(f,f_{n})\to 0
then the last integral tends to 0 as
n\to\infty
. To see this fix any
\epsilon>0
and note that by uniform continuity of
F_{1}
there is
\delta_{0}>0
such that if
d(f_{n}(v),f(v))<\delta_{0}
then
|F_{1}(f_{n}(v))-F_{1}(f(v))|<\epsilon/2
. Fix a positive
\delta<\min(\delta_{0},\epsilon/4\|F_{1}\|_{\infty})
. For
n
sufficiently large we have
d_{1}(f_{n},f)<\delta^{2}
, whence, by Markov’s inequality, the set
D_{\delta}=\{v\in\pi^{-1}(w):d(f_{n}(v),f(v))\geq\delta\}
has
\mu_{w}(D_{\delta})\leq\delta
. It follows that

\int_{\pi^{-1}(w)}|F_{1}(f_{n}(v))-F_{1}(f(v))|\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}\;\leq\;\epsilon/2+2\|F_{1}\|_{\infty}\,\mu_{w}(D_{\delta})\;\leq\;\epsilon.

The claim follows, and so
d_{1}(f_{n},f)\to 0
implies that
f_{n}\to f
in
\cL(V,Z)
.

For the converse, suppose that
f_{n}\to f
in

\mathcal{L}(V,Z)
. By
[
28
, Theorem (4.17)]
and
[
2
, Theorem 1]
, there exists a compact subset
B
of the real Hilbert space
\ell^{2}
such that there is a homeomorphism
F:Z\to B
. We have that
F\co f_{n}
,
F\co f
are Bochner measurable functions in
L^{2}(V,\ell^{2})
. We claim that if
F\co f_{n}\to F\co f
in
L^{2}(V,\ell^{2})
(that is
\int_{\pi^{-1}(w)}\|F\co f_{n}(v)-F\co f(v)\|_{\ell^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}\to 0
) then
d_{1}(f_{n},f)\to 0
as required. Indeed, for any
\epsilon>0
, the set
E=\{(z,z^{\prime}):d(z,z^{\prime})\geq\epsilon\}
is closed in
Z\times Z
so compact, and therefore the continuous function
\frac{d(z,z^{\prime})}{\|F(z)-F(z^{\prime})\|_{\ell^{2}}}
on
E
reaches a maximum
C
, whence for
n
sufficiently large we have

\displaystyle d_{1}(f_{n},f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\pi^{-1}(w)}d\big(f_{n}(v),f(v)\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}\;\;\leq\;\;\epsilon+C\int_{\pi^{-1}(w)}1_{E}\big(f_{n}(v),f(v)\big)\,\|F\co f_{n}(v)-F\co f(v)\|_{\ell^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\epsilon.

This proves our claim. We now show that
F\co f_{n}\to F\co f
in
L^{2}(V,\ell^{2})
.

Firstly, for every
F_{0}\in L^{2}(V,\ell^{2})
, we show that as
n\to\infty
we have
\int_{\pi^{-1}(w)}\langle F\co f_{n}(v),F_{0}(v)\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}\to\int_{\pi^{-1}(w)}\langle F\co f(v),F_{0}(v)\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}
. By definition of the Bochner integral, it suffices to prove this for simple functions
F_{0}
. By linearity, it then suffices to prove it for
F_{0}
of the form
\alpha\,1_{A}
where
A\subset\pi^{-1}(w)
is measurable and
\alpha\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. But in this case the function
F_{1}:Z\to\mathbb{R}
,
z\mapsto\langle F(z),\alpha\rangle
is continuous, and the function
1_{A}:\pi^{-1}(w)\to\mathbb{R}
can be approximated in
L^{1}(\mu_{w})
by continuous functions, hence in this case the convergence follows from the definition of the topology on
\cL(V,Z)
.

Secondly, we show that
\int_{\pi^{-1}(w)}\|F\co f_{n}(v)\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}
converges to
\int_{\pi^{-1}(w)}\|F\co f(v)\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}
. This follows from the fact that
F_{1}:Z\to\mathbb{R}
,
z\to\|F(z)\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}
is continuous, so that letting
F_{2}
be the function with value 1 on all of
V
we have
\int_{\pi^{-1}(w)}\|F\co f_{n}(v)\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}=\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}(f_{n})
, which converges to
\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}(f)
as required.

From the last two paragraphs we deduce the desired convergence by a standard argument, namely

\displaystyle\int_{\pi^{-1}(w)}\|F\co f_{n}(v)-F\co f(v)\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}\;=\;\int_{\pi^{-1}(w)}\langle F\co f_{n}-F\co f,F\co f_{n}-F\co f\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\|F\co f_{n}\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}-2\langle F\co f_{n},F\co f\rangle+\|F\co f\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}\;\to\;\int 2\|F\co f\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}-2\langle F\co f,F\co f\rangle\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}\;=\;0.\qed

It is a basic fact that if
(f_{n})
is a sequence of real-valued functions on a standard probability space such that
f_{n}\to f
in
L^{1}
and each
f_{n}
is constant almost surely, then the limit
f
is also constant almost surely. We shall use the following analogous fact for
\cL(V,Z)
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
V,W,Z
be compact spaces, let
\{\mu_{w}:w\in W\}
be a CSM on
\pi:V\to W
, and let
(f_{n})
be a convergent sequence in
\cL(V,Z)
such that each
f_{n}
is constant almost surely on its fibre
\pi^{-1}(w_{n})
. Then the limit is also constant almost surely.

Proof.

Let
f
be the limit of the sequence
(f_{n})
. By Urysohn’s lemma on
Z
, it suffices to show that for every continuous function
F:Z\to\mathbb{R}
the function
F\co f
is constant. Now since
F
and
F^{2}
are both continuous, by definition of the topology on
\cL(V,Z)
we have
\int_{\pi^{-1}(w_{n})}F\co f_{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w_{n}}\to\int_{\pi^{-1}(w)}F\co f\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}
and
\int_{\pi^{-1}(w_{n})}F^{2}\co f_{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w_{n}}\to\int_{\pi^{-1}(w)}F^{2}\co f\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}
. On the other hand, since each
f_{n}
is constant almost everywhere, we have
\Big(\int_{\pi^{-1}(w_{n})}F\co f_{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w_{n}}\Big)^{2}-\int_{\pi^{-1}(w_{n})}F^{2}\co f_{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w_{n}}=0
. Taking limits as
n\to\infty
, we deduce that
\Big(\int_{\pi^{-1}(w)}F\co f\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}\Big)^{2}-\int_{\pi^{-1}(w)}F^{2}\co f\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}=0
. By the equality case of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, this implies that
F\co f
is constant almost everywhere.
∎

The following result will be used several times in the next section.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
V,W,Z
be compact spaces, and let
\{\mu_{w}:w\in W\}
be a CSM on
\pi:V\to W
. Suppose that
(f_{n})
and
(g_{n})
are sequences in
\cL(V,Z)
converging to
f
and
g
respectively, where
f_{n},g_{n}
are defined on
\pi^{-1}(w_{n})
and
f,g
are defined on
\pi^{-1}(w)
. Then the functions
v\mapsto(f_{n}(v),g_{n}(v))
converge to
v\mapsto(f(v),g(v))
in
\cL(V,Z\times Z)
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that for any continuous functions
F_{1}:Z\times Z\to\mathbb{C}
and
F_{2}:V\to\mathbb{C}
we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\pi^{-1}(w_{n})}F_{1}(f_{n}(v),g_{n}(v))\,F_{2}(v)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w_{n}}(v)=\int_{\pi^{-1}(w)}F_{1}(f(v),g(v))\,F_{2}(v)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}(v)
.
By the Stone-Weierstrass theorem the function
F_{1}
can be approximated arbitrarily closely in the uniform metric by finite linear combinations of functions of the form
(z_{1},z_{2})\mapsto F_{1,1}(z_{1})F_{1,2}(z_{2})
where
F_{1,1},F_{1,2}:Z\to\mathbb{C}
are continuous, so we may assume that
F_{1}
is of this form. Fix any
\epsilon>0
. By Lusin’s theorem there exists a continuous function
q^{\prime}:\pi^{-1}(w)\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\|F_{1,2}\co g-q^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}<\epsilon
and
\|q^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq\|F_{1,2}\|_{\infty}
, and then by the Tietze extension theorem
[

there exists a continuous function
q:V\to\mathbb{C}
such that the restriction
q|_{\pi^{-1}(w)}
equals
q^{\prime}
and
\|q\|_{\infty}\leq\|F_{1,2}\|_{\infty}
. For each

let
q_{n}=q|_{\pi^{-1}(w_{n})}
. Now

\displaystyle\|F_{1,2}\co g_{n}-q_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\pi^{-1}(w_{n})}|F_{1,2}\co g_{n}-q_{n}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w_{n}}

(10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\pi^{-1}(w_{n})}|F_{1,2}\co g_{n}|^{2}-2\tRe\,(F_{1,2}\co g_{n}\,\overline{q_{n}})+|q_{n}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w_{n}}.

Using that
g_{n}\to g
in
\cL(V,Z)
, that

, and the uniform continuity of

, we deduce that the integral above converges to
\|F_{1,2}\co g-q^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
. Setting
h_{n}=(F_{1,1}\co f_{n})(F_{1,2}\co g_{n}-q_{n})

F_{1}\co(f_{n},g_{n})=(F_{1,1}\co f_{n})\big(q_{n}+(F_{1,2}\co g_{n}-q_{n})\big)=(F_{1,1}\co f_{n})\,q_{n}+h_{n}

and similarly
F_{1}\co(f,g)=(F_{1,1}\co f)\,q^{\prime}+h
, where for
n
sufficiently large we have
\|h_{n}\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|h\|_{L^{2}}
both at most
2\epsilon\|F_{1,1}\|_{\infty}
. The convergence

also implies that

\int_{\pi^{-1}(w_{n})}(F_{1,1}\co f_{n})\,q_{n}\,F_{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w_{n}}\;\to\;\int_{\pi^{-1}(w)}(F_{1,1}\co f)\,q^{\prime}\,F_{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}.

Combining the above estimates we deduce that for all

\Big|\int_{\pi^{-1}(w_{n})}F_{1}\co(f_{n},g_{n})\,F_{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w_{n}}-\int_{\pi^{-1}(w)}F_{1}\co(f,g)\,F_{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}\Big|\leq 5\epsilon\|F_{1,1}\|_{\infty}\|F_{2}\|_{\infty}.

Since

was arbitrary, the result follows.
∎

For the main applications of the space
\cL(V,Z)
in the sequel, we will suppose that

is a compact abelian group. We now show that in this case the group has a continuous action on the space.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
V,W
be compact spaces, let
\ab
be a compact abelian group, and let
\{\mu_{w}:w\in W\}
be a CSM on
\pi:V\to W
. Then we have the following free continuous action of
\ab
on
\cL(V,\ab)
:

\alpha\,:\;\ab\times\cL(V,\ab)\;\to\;\cL(V,\ab),\quad\alpha(z,f):v\mapsto f(v)+z.

Proof.

The topology on
\cL(V,\ab)
is generated by the functions
\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}
, so to check continuity of
\alpha
it suffices to show that for every
\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}
the map
\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}\circ\alpha
is continuous. Thus, it suffices to show that for any continuous functions
F_{1}:\ab\to\mathbb{C}
and
F_{2}:V\to\mathbb{C}
, the following function is continuous:

q:\;(z,f)\;\mapsto\;\int_{\pi^{-1}(\tilde{\pi}(f))}F_{1}(f(v)+z)\;F_{2}(v)\;\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\tilde{\pi}(f)}(v).

(11)

Applying the Stone-Weierstrass theorem as in previous proofs, the continuous function
\ab\times\ab\to\mathbb{C}
defined by
(z_{1},z_{2})\mapsto F_{1}(z_{1}+z_{2})
can be approximated uniformly by linear combinations of functions of the form
(z_{1},z_{2})\mapsto h_{1}(z_{1})\,h_{2}(z_{2})
where
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
are continuous. Substituting any of these into (

q^{\prime}:\;(z,f)\;\mapsto\;h_{2}(z)\int_{\pi^{-1}(\tilde{\pi}(f))}h_{1}(f(v))\;F_{2}(v)\;\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\tilde{\pi}(f)}(v)\;=\;h_{2}(z)\,\varphi_{h_{1},F_{2}}(f).

This is continuous by definition of
\cL(V,\ab)
. Hence

is a uniform limit of continuous functions and is therefore continuous.
∎

In our uses of
\cL(V,\ab)
, we shall want to view this space as a continuous
\ab
-bundle, for some appropriate base

and projection
\cE
. We would like

to have the following features. Firstly, the projection should send two functions

in

to the same point of

if and only if

and

are in the same

for some

). Secondly, we would like

to be also of the form
\cL(Y,\ab)
, for some other similar space

having a projection to
W
, as this would help to relate the topologies on

and
\cL(V,\ab)
.

A natural way to obtain these features is to define
\cE
as a difference map, sending

to the function
\cE(f):V\times V\to\ab
,
(v_{0},v_{1})\mapsto f(v_{0})-f(v_{1})
. We are thus led to the following construction.

Definition 4.11
.

Let
V,W
be compact spaces and let
\{\mu_{w}:w\in W\}
be a CSM on
\pi:V\to W
. We define the compact space

V\times_{W}V=\{(v_{0},v_{1}):v_{0},v_{1}\in V,\;\pi(v_{0})=\pi(v_{1})\},

and define the projection
\pi^{\prime}:V\times_{W}V\to W
by
\pi^{\prime}(v_{0},v_{1})=\pi(v_{0})
.

Lemma 4.12
.

The family of measures
\{\mu_{w}\times\mu_{w}:w\in W\}
on
V\times_{W}V
is a CSM on
\pi^{\prime}
.

Proof.

For every
w\in W
the measure
\mu_{w}
is concentrated on
\pi^{-1}(w)
, so in
V\times V
the measure
\mu_{w}\times\mu_{w}
is concentrated on
\pi^{-1}(w)\times\pi^{-1}(w)={\pi^{\prime}}^{-1}(w)
. For any continuous function
f:V\times_{W}V\to\mathbb{C}
, the continuity of
w\mapsto\int_{{\pi^{\prime}}^{-1}(w)}f\,\mathrm{d}(\mu_{w}\times\mu_{w})
is proved by an argument similar to previous ones (e.g. in the proof of Lemma
4.10
), showing that this function is a uniform limit of continuous functions
F_{n}
, where
F_{n}
is obtained by approximating

to within

in the supremum norm by a finite linear combination of functions of the form
(v_{0},v_{1})\mapsto h_{0}(v_{0})\,h_{1}(v_{1})
with
h_{i}:V\to\mathbb{C}
continuous, and then using the continuity of
w\mapsto\prod_{i=0,1}\int_{\pi^{-1}(w)}h_{i}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}
guaranteed by the CSM on
\pi
.
∎

We are now able to complete the construction of the continuous abelian bundle announced at the beginning of this section, by showing that the map
\cE
has the required properties to be a suitable projection.

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
V,W
be compact spaces, let
\{\mu_{w}:w\in W\}
be a CSM on
\pi:V\to W
, and let
\ab
be a compact abelian group. Let
\cE:\cL(V,\ab)\to\cL(V\times_{W}V,\ab)
be defined by
\cE(g):(v_{0},v_{1})\mapsto g(v_{0})-g(v_{1})
. Then
\cL(V,\ab)
is a continuous
\ab
-bundle over
\cE(\cL(V,\ab))
with projection
\cE
.

Proof.

Recall that the topology on
\cL(V\times_{W}V,\ab)
is generated by functions
\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}
sending
f:V\times_{W}V\to\ab
with
\tilde{\pi}(f)=w
to
\int_{{\pi^{\prime}}^{-1}(w)}F_{1}(f(v_{0},v_{1}))\,F_{2}(v_{0},v_{1})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w}(v_{0},v_{1})
. To see that
\cE
is continuous, fix any such function and suppose that
g_{n}\to g
in
\cL(V,\ab)
. Then, approximating the continuous functions
(z_{0},z_{1})\mapsto F_{1}(z_{0}-z_{1})
on
\ab^{2}
and
F_{2}
on
{\pi^{\prime}}^{-1}(w)=\pi^{-1}(w)\times\pi^{-1}(w)
by combinations of continuous functions
h_{0}\,h_{1}
as in previous proofs, we approximate
\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}(\cE(g_{n}))
by finite combinations of products
\varphi_{0}(g_{n})\varphi_{1}(g_{n})
, where
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}
are functions among those generating the
\cL(V,\ab)
topology. By assumption, each of these products satisfies
\varphi_{0}(g_{n})\varphi_{1}(g_{n})\to\varphi_{0}(g)\varphi_{1}(g)
, and it follows that
\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}(\cE(g_{n}))\to\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}(\cE(g))
. This proves the continuity of
\cE
.

To ensure that we have a continuous
\ab
-bundle, it only remains to check the ‘if’ part of condition (iv) in Definition
2.6
. Equivalently, it now suffices to prove that if
\cE^{-1}(U)
is closed then
U
is closed. For that, we claim it is enough to show that if for a sequence of functions
f_{n}\in\cL(V,\ab)
the images
\cE(f_{n})
converge, then there is a convergent subsequence
(f_{m})
of
(f_{n})
. Indeed, suppose that this holds and suppose for a contradiction that
U
is not closed. Then (using the surjectivity of
\cE
) there is a sequence of functions
\cE(f_{n})\in U
converging to a function
\cE(f)\not\in U
. But then letting
f^{\prime}
be the limit of the guaranteed subsequence
(f_{m})
, the closure of
\cE^{-1}(U)
implies that
f^{\prime}\in\cE^{-1}(U)
, and so by continuity of
\cE
we have
U\ni\cE(f^{\prime})=\lim_{m\to\infty}\cE(f_{m})=\cE(f)\not\in U
, a contradiction. This proves our claim.

So let us suppose that
\cE(f_{n})
converges to
\cE(f)
for some
f\in\cL(V,\ab)
. Let
w_{n}=\tilde{\pi}(f_{n})
and
w=\tilde{\pi}(f)
, and note that
w_{n}\to w
in
W
by continuity of
\tilde{\pi}
.

Let
\cC
denote the circle group, viewed as the unit circle in
\mathbb{C}
. We shall prove the following fact:

\displaystyle\textrm{There exists a subsequence }(f_{m})\textrm{ and some }t\in\ab\textrm{ such that, for every character }

(12)

\displaystyle\chi:\ab\to\cC\textrm{ in }\widehat{\ab},\textrm{ we have }\chi\co f_{m}\to\chi\co\,(f+t)\textrm{ in }\cL(V,\cC)\textrm{ as }m\to\infty.

We prove this below, but before that let us show how it implies that
f_{m}
converges to
f+t
in
\cL(V,\ab)
.

Let
\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}
be any of the functions generating the
\cL(V,\ab)
topology, and note that by the Stone-Weierstrass theorem for every
\epsilon>0
there exists a trigonometric polynomial
F_{1}^{\prime}=\sum_{j\in[K]}\lambda_{j}\chi_{j}
on

such that
\|F_{1}-F_{1}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon
. Therefore it suffices to show that
\varphi_{F_{1}^{\prime},F_{2}}(f_{m})\to\varphi_{F_{1}^{\prime},F_{2}}(f+t)
for any such polynomial

. We have

\varphi_{F_{1}^{\prime},F_{2}}(f_{m})=\sum_{j\in[K]}\lambda_{j}\int_{\pi^{-1}(w_{m})}(\chi_{j}\co f_{m})\,F_{2}\;\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{w_{m}}=\sum_{j\in[K]}\lambda_{j}\,\varphi_{1,F_{2}}(\chi_{j}\co f_{m}).

Since
\varphi_{1,F_{2}}
is one of the functions generating the topology on

, we have
\varphi_{1,F_{2}}(\chi_{j}\co f_{m})\to\varphi_{1,F_{2}}\big(\chi_{j}\co\,(f+t)\big)
by assumption, and the desired convergence follows.

We now prove (
12
). Given
\chi\in\widehat{\ab}
, the function
\chi\circ f
is in

. Continuous functions are dense in this space, and any such function can be extended to a continuous function on
V
(by the Tietze extension theorem). Hence, for every
\delta>0
there exists a continuous function
\chi_{\delta}:V\to\cC
whose restriction to
\pi^{-1}(w)
satisfies
\|\chi_{\delta}-\chi\co f\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{w})}\leq\delta
. We claim that for every
\chi\in\widehat{\ab}
and
\epsilon>0
, there exist
\delta>0
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\lambda\in\cC
, such that for all
n>N
we have
\|(\chi\co f_{n})\,\overline{\chi_{\delta}}-\lambda\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{w_{n}})}\leq\epsilon
.

To prove this claim, let
\cE^{\prime}:\cL(V,\cC)\to\cL(V\times_{W}V,\cC)
be defined by
\cE^{\prime}(g):(v_{0},v_{1})\mapsto g(v_{0})\overline{g(v_{1})}
. We have that
\cE^{\prime}
is continuous, by an argument similar to the one used above for

. It is also clear that for any

we have
\cE^{\prime}(g_{1}g_{2})=\cE^{\prime}(g_{1})\cE^{\prime}(g_{2})
, and that
\cE^{\prime}(\chi\circ g)=\chi\circ\cE(g)
for every
\chi\in\widehat{\ab}
,

. Now, from the definition of
\chi_{\delta}
, the continuity of
\cE^{\prime}
, and Lemma

, it follows that for

sufficiently small we have
\big\|\cE^{\prime}\big((\chi\circ f)\,\overline{\chi_{\delta}}\big)-1\big\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{w}\times\mu_{w})}\leq\epsilon/2
. On the other hand, we have
\cE^{\prime}\big((\chi\circ f_{n})\overline{\chi_{\delta}}\big)=\cE^{\prime}(\chi\circ f_{n})\,\cE^{\prime}(\overline{\chi_{\delta}})=\chi\co\cE(f_{n})\,\cE^{\prime}(\overline{\chi_{\delta}})
and similarly for

. This together with the assumption

sufficiently large we have
\big\|\cE^{\prime}\big((\chi\circ f_{n})\,\overline{\chi_{\delta}}\big)-1\big\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{w_{n}}\times\mu_{w_{n}})}\leq\epsilon
. Taking a minimum over

in this integral, we deduce that there exists

such that, for

, we have indeed

.

Since there are at most countably many elements in
\widehat{\ab}
, it follows by a standard diagonalization argument (using the above claim with smaller and smaller

for each

) that for some sequence
(m_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive integers, for every
\chi\in\widehat{\ab}
there is a constant
\lambda_{\chi}\in\cC
such that
\chi\co f_{m_{n}}\to\lambda_{\chi}(\chi\co f)
in
\cL(V,\cC)
as

. A straightforward calculation shows that the function

is a homomorphism, hence a character on

, which by duality must be of the form

for some

. This confirms (
12
) and completes the proof.
∎

We close this section with two technical lemmas that we shall use several times.

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
\{\mu_{w}:w\in W\}
be a CSM on
\pi:V\to W
. Let

be a compact space with a Borel measure

. Suppose that

be a compact space and let

By definition of the topology on
\cL(V,Z)
it suffices to show that for every
\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}
the function

F_{1}:Z\to\mathbb{C}
and

, that is

. By Lusin’s theorem there exists a continuous function

5

Let

\ab
be a compact abelian group. The purpose of this section is to show that any measurable cocycle

-bundle over

Recall that for each

Definition 5.1
.

We denote by

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\cE(\rho_{x}).

To prove the continuity of

, the key idea is to express this function in terms of compositions of
\rho
with certain tricube morphisms
T_{k}\to\ns
(recall
[
8
, Definition 3.1.13]
), and thereby to use these morphisms in a smoothening operation that turns the measurability of
\rho
into the continuity of
g^{\prime}
.

Let
Q=\hom(T_{k},\ns)
and
Q_{x}=\hom_{1^{k}\to x}(T_{k},\ns)
. For each
v\in\{0,1\}^{k}\setminus\{0^{k}\}
, let

g_{v}:\ns\to\cL(Q,\ab),\;\;x\;\mapsto\;\big(g_{v}(x):t\mapsto\rho(t\co\Psi_{v})\,\in L(Q_{x},\ab)\big).

We claim that
g_{v}
is continuous. Indeed, by Lemma
3.23
if
v\neq 0^{k}
then
t\mapsto t\circ\Psi_{v}
is measure-preserving from
Q_{x}
to
\cu^{k}(\ns)
, so we can deduce the continuity of
g_{v}
by applying Lemma
4.14
(setting
V=Q
,
K=\cu^{k}(\ns)
,
W=\ns
,
f_{2}=\rho
,
h=g_{v}
).

Now consider the following function:

g\;:\;\ns\to\cL(Q,\ab),\quad x\;\mapsto\;\sum_{v\in\{0,1\}^{k}\setminus\{0^{k}\}}(-1)^{|v|}\,g_{v}.

The continuity of each function
g_{v}
implies that
g
is continuous. Indeed, letting
P=\{0,1\}^{k}\setminus\{0^{k}\}
, by Lemma
4.9
the function
\ns\to\cL(Q,\ab^{P})
,
x\,\mapsto\,\prod_{v\in P}g_{v}(x)
is continuous, and then Lemma
4.15
gives the continuity of
g
. Now, by
[
8
, Lemma 3.3.31]
, for each
x\in\ns
we have that
g(x)
is the function
t\mapsto\rho_{x}(t\circ\omega_{k})-\rho_{x}(t\circ\Psi_{0^{k}})
(defined on
Q_{x}
). In particular, given any
t\in Q_{x}
, we have that
\q_{0}:=t\co\omega_{k}
and
\q_{1}:=t\co\Psi_{0^{k}}
are both cubes in
\cu^{k}_{x}(\ns)
(
\q_{0}
is a cube by
[
8
, Lemma 3.1.16]
), and
g(x)(t)=\rho_{x}(\q_{0})-\rho_{x}(\q_{1})=g^{\prime}(x)(\q_{0},\q_{1})
. Given this equality, we now claim that continuity of
g^{\prime}
follows from continuity of
g
. Indeed, we know that
g^{\prime}
is continuous if for any of the functions
\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}
generating the topology on
\cL(Y,\ab)
we have that
\ns\to\mathbb{C}
,
x\mapsto\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}(g^{\prime}(x))
is continuous. Letting
Y_{x}=\cu^{k}_{x}(\ns)\times\cu^{k}_{x}(\ns)
, we have

\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}(g^{\prime}(x))=\int_{Y_{x}}F_{1}(g^{\prime}(x)(\q_{0},\q_{1}))\;F_{2}((\q_{0},\q_{1}))\;\,\mathrm{d}\mu((\q_{0},\q_{1})),

where
F_{1}:\ab\to\mathbb{C}
and
F_{2}:Y_{x}\to\mathbb{C}
are both continuous.
Now let
\theta:Q_{x}\to Y_{x}
,
t\mapsto(t\co\omega_{k},t\co\Psi_{0^{k}})
. If we show that this map is surjective and measure-preserving, then since
g^{\prime}(x)\co\theta(t)=g(x)(t)
, we would conclude that

\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}(g^{\prime}(x))=\int_{Q_{x}}F_{1}(g(x)(t))\;F_{2}(\theta(t))\;\,\mathrm{d}\mu(t)

and continuity of
g^{\prime}
would then indeed follow from that of
g
(via approximating the measurable function
F_{2}\co\theta
by continuous functions as in previous proofs).

The surjectivity of
\theta
follows from combining Lemma
3.21
and
3.15
. (To apply the latter result recall that we are implicitly embedding
T_{k}
as a subcubespace in
\{0,1\}^{2k}
, using
[
8
, Lemma 3.1.17]
.)

To see that
\theta
is measure-preserving, first note that by Lemma
3.14
there is a CSM on the map
Q_{x}\to\cu^{k}_{x}(\ns),\;t\mapsto t\circ\omega_{k}
consisting of the Haar probabilities on the fibres, giving a disintegration of the Haar probability on
Q_{x}
. On each of these fibres, of the form
\hom_{\q\co\omega_{k}^{-1}}(T_{k},\ns)
,
\q\in\cu^{k}_{x}(\ns)
, it follows from Corollary
3.22
that the map
\hom_{\q\co\omega_{k}^{-1}}(T_{k},\ns)\to\cu^{k}_{x}(\ns)
,
t\mapsto t\co\Psi_{0^{k}}
preserves the Haar measures. Using this, a straightforward calculation shows that for product sets
A_{1}\times A_{2}\subset\cu^{k}_{x}(\ns)\times\cu^{k}_{x}(\ns)
we have
\mu_{Q_{x}}\co\theta^{-1}(A_{1}\times A_{2})=\mu_{\cu^{k}_{x}(\ns)\times\cu^{k}_{x}(\ns)}(A_{1}\times A_{2})
, and it follows that
\theta
is measure-preserving as claimed.
∎

Let us now recall the definition of cubes on
M
from
[
8
, Definition 3.3.25]
. Note that, by definition of
M
, for every function
f:\{0,1\}^{k}\to M
there is some function
a=a_{f}:\{0,1\}^{k}\to\ab
such that
\rho_{x}(v)=f(v)+a(v)
, where
x=\tilde{\pi}(f(v))\in\ns
.

Definition 5.3
.

We define
\cu^{k}(M)
to be the set of functions
f:\{0,1\}^{k}\to M
such that
\tilde{\pi}\co f\in\cu^{k}(\ns)
and

\rho(\tilde{\pi}\co f)=\sigma_{k}(a).

(15)

For
n\neq k
, a function
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\to M
is declared to be in
\cu^{n}(M)
if
\tilde{\pi}\co f\in\cu^{n}(\ns)
and every
k
-dimensional face-restriction of

is in
\cu^{k}(M)
.

We can now complete the main task of this section.

Proposition 5.4
.

The space
M
together with the cube sets
\cu^{n}(M)
is a compact nilspace.

Proof.

By
[
8
, Proposition 3.3.26]
we have that
M
is a nilspace, and by Proposition
5.2
we have that
M
is a compact space. Arguing as in the proof of Lemma
2.1
, we see that it suffices to show that
\cu^{k}(M)
is a closed subset of
M^{\{0,1\}^{k}}
.
We use the probability space
Q=\hom(T_{k},\ns)
again, but this time we let
\pi_{o}:Q\to\cu^{k}(\ns)
be the map
t\mapsto t\co\omega_{k}
, and apply Lemma
3.14
(with
P=T_{k}
embedded in
\{0,1\}^{2k}
,
P_{1}=\emptyset
, and
P_{2}=\omega_{k}(\{0,1\}^{k})
also embedded in
\{0,1\}^{2k}
) to obtain a CSM on
\pi_{o}
consisting of the Haar measures on the fibres
\pi_{o}^{-1}(\q)=\hom_{\q\co\omega_{k}^{-1}}(T_{k},\ns)
. Let
\tilde{\pi}
be the projection
M\to\ns
,
\rho_{x}+z\mapsto x
, and let

R_{k}(M)=\{f:\{0,1\}^{k}\to M:\tilde{\pi}\co f\in\cu^{k}(\ns)\}.

Since
\tilde{\pi}
is continuous and
\cu^{k}(\ns)
is closed, we have that
R_{k}(M)
is closed in
M^{\{0,1\}^{k}}
. We define the following map:

\displaystyle\phi\;:\;\;R_{k}(M)

\displaystyle\quad\to\quad

\displaystyle L\big(\pi_{o}^{-1}(\tilde{\pi}\co f),\ab\big)\;\subset\;\mathcal{L}(Q,\ab)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\quad\mapsto\quad

\displaystyle\phi_{f}(t)=\sum_{v\in\{0,1\}^{k}}(-1)^{|v|}\,f(v)(t\co\trem_{v}).

Arguing as in the proof of
[
8
, Lemma 3.3.32]
, we see that
\phi_{f}
is actually a constant function for every
f\in R_{k}(M)
, namely the constant
\rho(\tilde{\pi}\co f)-\sigma_{k}(a)\in\ab
. By
[
8
, (3.16)]
we have that
\cu^{k}(M)=\phi^{-1}(0)
. Hence it now suffices to show that
\phi
is continuous, as then
\cu^{k}(M)
is closed as required.

To see the continuity of
\phi
, we first express it as the composition of simpler functions. For each
v\in\{0,1\}^{k}
, let

\displaystyle\phi_{v}\;:\;\;R_{k}(M)

\displaystyle\quad\to\quad

\displaystyle L\big(\pi_{o}^{-1}(\tilde{\pi}\co f),\ab\big)\;\subset\;\mathcal{L}(Q,\ab)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\quad\mapsto\quad

\displaystyle\phi_{v,f}(t)=f(v)(t\co\trem_{v}).

We claim that
\phi_{v}
is continuous. To see this, we suppose that
f_{n}\to f
in
R_{k}(M)
, which implies that the component
f_{n}(v)
converges to
f(v)
in
M
, and we want to show that then
\phi_{v,f_{n}}\to\phi_{v,f}
in
\cL(Q,\ab)
. For this it suffices to show that
\varphi_{F_{1}^{\prime},F_{2}^{\prime}}(\phi_{v,f_{n}})\to\varphi_{F_{1}^{\prime},F_{2}^{\prime}}(\phi_{v,f})
for any continuous functions
F_{1}^{\prime}:\ab\to\mathbb{C}
,
F_{2}^{\prime}:Q\to\mathbb{C}
, where

\varphi_{F_{1}^{\prime},F_{2}^{\prime}}(\phi_{v,f})=\int_{\pi_{o}^{-1}(\tilde{\pi}\co f)}F_{1}^{\prime}(\phi_{v,f}(t))\;F_{2}^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\tilde{\pi}\co f}(t)=\int_{\pi_{o}^{-1}(\tilde{\pi}\co f)}F_{1}^{\prime}\big(f(v)(t\co\trem_{v})\big)\;F_{2}^{\prime}(t)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\tilde{\pi}\co f}(t).

We shall now show that, for one of the functions
\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}
generating the topology on
M
, we have
\varphi_{F_{1}^{\prime},F_{2}^{\prime}}(\phi_{v,f})=\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}(f(v))
. Recall that for any
g\in M
we have

\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}(g)=\int_{\pi^{-1}(\tilde{\pi}(g))}F_{1}(g(\q))\;F_{2}(\q)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\tilde{\pi}(g)}(\q).

(16)

Now for each
t\in Q
let
Q_{t}
denote the space of morphisms
t^{\prime}\in Q
such that
\trem_{v}\co t^{\prime}=\trem_{v}\co t
, and let
\mu_{Q_{t}}
denote the Haar measure on this space given by Corollary
3.20
. These measures form a CSM by Lemma
3.14
. We then have

\displaystyle\varphi_{F_{1}^{\prime},F_{2}^{\prime}}(\phi_{v,f})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\pi_{o}^{-1}(\tilde{\pi}\co f)}\;\int_{Q_{t}}F_{1}^{\prime}\big(f(v)(t^{\prime}\co\trem_{v})\big)\;F_{2}^{\prime}(t^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{Q_{t}}(t^{\prime})\;\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\tilde{\pi}\co f}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\pi_{o}^{-1}(\tilde{\pi}\co f)}F_{1}^{\prime}\big(f(v)(t\co\trem_{v})\big)\;\Big(\int_{Q_{t}}F_{2}^{\prime}(t^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{Q_{t}}(t^{\prime})\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\tilde{\pi}\co f}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\pi_{o}^{-1}(\tilde{\pi}\co f)}F_{1}^{\prime}\big(f(v)(t\co\trem_{v})\big)\;F_{2}^{\prime\prime}(t\co\trem_{v})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\tilde{\pi}\co f}(t),

where
F_{2}^{\prime\prime}(t\co\trem_{v})=\int_{t^{\prime}\in Q_{t}}F_{2}^{\prime}(t^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{Q_{t}}(t^{\prime})
is a continuous function of
\q=t\co\trem_{v}\in\cu^{k}(\ns)
, by Definition
3.1
. We can now use the fact that
t\mapsto t\co\trem_{v}
is measure-preserving from
\pi_{o}^{-1}(\tilde{\pi}\co f)
to
\pi^{-1}(\tilde{\pi}\co f(v))
, to conclude that for
f(v)\in M
we have

\varphi_{F_{1}^{\prime},F_{2}^{\prime}}(\phi_{v,f})=\int_{\pi^{-1}(\tilde{\pi}\co f(v))}F_{1}^{\prime}\big(f(v)(\q)\big)\;F_{2}^{\prime\prime}(\q)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\tilde{\pi}\co f}(\q).

This is of the form
\varphi_{F_{1}^{\prime},F_{2}^{\prime\prime}}(f(v))
as in (
16
), and the continuity of
\phi_{v}
follows.

The continuity of each function
\phi_{v}
implies that the following function is continuous:

\displaystyle\phi_{1}\;:\;\;R_{k}(M)

\displaystyle\quad\to\quad

\displaystyle L\big(\pi_{o}^{-1}(\tilde{\pi}\co f),\ab^{\{0,1\}^{k}}\big)\;\subset\;\mathcal{L}(Q,\ab^{\{0,1\}^{k}})

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\quad\mapsto\quad

\displaystyle\phi_{1,f}(t)=(f(v)(t\co\trem_{v}))_{v\in\{0,1\}^{k}}.

Indeed, if
f_{n}\to f
in
R_{k}(M)
then for each
v
by continuity of
\phi_{v}
we have
\phi_{v,f_{n}}\to\phi_{v,f}
in
\cL(Q,\ab)
. This then implies that
\phi_{1,f_{n}}\to\phi_{1,f}
in
\cL(Q,\ab^{\{0,1\}^{k}})
, by Lemma
4.9
.

The proof can now be completed using Lemma
4.15
. Indeed, for
P=\{0,1\}^{k}
and appropriate choices of coefficients
\lambda_{r}
in that lemma, the original function
\phi:R_{k}(M)\to\cL(Q,\ab)
is the composition
\Sigma_{P}\co\phi_{1}
. The lemma tells us that
\Sigma_{P}
is continuous, and so
\phi
is continuous.
∎

Recall from
[
8
, Lemma 3.3.28]
that, up to isomorphisms of nilspaces, every extension of
\ns
by
\ab
is of the form
M(\rho)
above for some cocycle
\rho
. We now want an analogue of this result for compact nilspaces, which requires that the isomorphism be also a homeomorphism. To ensure this, recall first from
[
8
, Lemma 3.3.21]
that given a degree-
k
extension of a nilspace, every cross section
\cs
for this extension generates a cocycle, denoted
\rho_{\cs}
. We now show that for compact nilspaces one can take
\rho_{\cs}
to be Borel measurable.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\ns
be a compact nilspace, and let
\nss
be a compact nilspace that is a degree-
k
extension of
\ns
by a compact abelian group
\ab
. Then there is a Borel cross section
\cs
for this extension and therefore a Borel cocycle
\rho_{\cs}:\cu^{k+1}(\ns)\to\ab
.

Proof.

Let
\pi:\nss\to\ns
be the projection of the extension. Let
P=\{(x,y)\in\ns\times\nss\,:\,\pi(y)=x\}
. A cross-section for
\pi
is a subset of
P
which happens to be the graph of a function
\ns\to\nss
. Let
\mathcal{P}(\nss)
denote the set of Borel probability measures on
\nss
. By
[
28
, Corollary 18.7]
, a sufficient condition for a Borel cross section to exist is that for some Borel function
\mu:\ns\to\mathcal{P}(\nss)
we have
\mu_{x}(P\cap(\{x\}\times\nss))>0
. The measures
\mu_{x}
from the CSM structure on
\pi
satisfy this, and the function
x\to\mu_{x}
is continuous, by Definition
3.1
. There is therefore a Borel cross section
\cs:\ns\to\nss
. We can then define a Borel function
\rho:\cu^{k+1}(\nss)\to\ab
by
\rho(\q^{\prime})=\sigma_{k+1}(\cs\co\pi\co\q^{\prime}-\q^{\prime})
. Moreover, as is shown in the proof of
[
8
, Lemma 3.3.21]
, the function
\rho
is constant on each fibre of the map
\cu^{k+1}(\nss)\to\cu^{k+1}(\ns)
,
\q^{\prime}\mapsto\pi\co\q^{\prime}
, thus yielding the well-defined cocycle
\rho_{\cs}:\cu^{k+1}(\ns)\to\ab
. It can then be seen by general results that
\rho_{\cs}
is also Borel measurable. For instance, one can use that by
[
8
, Definition 3.3.13]
the space
\cu^{k+1}(\nss)
is a continuous abelian bundle over
\cu^{k+1}(\ns)
with Polish structure group
\cu^{k+1}(\cD_{k}(\ab))
, so that by
[
28
, Theorem (12.16) and Corollary (15.2)]
there is a Borel cross section
\q\mapsto\q^{\prime}
for this bundle.
∎

We shall also need the following useful generalization of a classical automatic-continuity result. The classical result in question is a theorem of Kleppner stating that a Borel measurable homomorphism between two locally compact groups must be continuous
[
29
, Theorem 1]
. We extend the case of compact abelian groups as follows.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
\ns,\nss
be compact nilspaces of finite step, and let
\phi:\ns\to\nss
be a Borel measurable morphism. Then
\phi
is continuous.

Proof.

Suppose that
\nss
is a
(k-1)
-step nilspace, and consider the CSM on
\pi:\cu^{k}(\ns)\to\ns
,
\q\mapsto\q(0^{k})
, given by Lemma
3.17
. For each
v\in\{0,1\}^{k}\setminus\{0^{k}\}
let
\pi_{v}:\cu^{k}(\ns)\to\ns
,
\q\mapsto\q(v)
. We have that
\pi_{v}
is continuous and each of its restrictions to a space
\cu^{k}_{x}(\ns)
is measure-preserving, by Lemma
3.14
(applied with
P_{1}=\{0^{k}\}
,
P_{2}=\{v\}
and
f:0^{k}\mapsto x
, identifying
\hom_{f|_{P_{1}\cap P_{2}}}(P_{2},\ns)
with
\ns
). Now for each
v
let
f_{v}:\ns\to\cL(\cu^{k}(\ns),\nss)
be the function sending
x
to the restriction
\phi\co\pi_{v}|_{\cu_{x}^{k}(\ns)}
. Then
f_{v}
is continuous, by Lemma
4.14
(applied with
V=\cu^{k}(\ns)
,
W=K=\ns
,
f_{1}=\pi_{v}
and
f_{2}=\phi
).

Let
f:\ns\to\cL\big(\cu^{k}(\ns),\nss\big)
be the function mapping
x\in\ns
to the constant function with value
\phi(x)
on
\cu_{x}^{k}(\ns)
. From the definition of
\cL\big(\cu^{k}(\ns),\nss\big)
it follows that if
f
is continuous then so is
\phi
.

To see that
f
is continuous, recall that
\cor^{k}(\nss)
denotes the space of
k
-corners on
\nss
and consider the following function:

\xi:\ns\to\cL\big(\cu^{k}(\ns),\cor^{k}(\nss)\big),\;\;\;x\mapsto\;\;\Big(\xi_{x}:\q\in\cu^{k}_{x}(\ns)\;\mapsto\;\big(f_{v}(x)(\q)\big)_{v\neq 0^{k}}=\big(\phi\co\q(v)\big)_{v\neq 0^{k}}\Big).

Combining the continuity of each function
f_{v}
with Lemma
4.9
, we deduce that
\xi
is continuous. Now let
\cK
denote the function
\cL\big(\cu^{k}(\ns),\cor^{k}(\nss)\big)\to\cL\big(\cu^{k}(\ns),\nss\big)
that sends a function
g
to
\comp\co g
, where
\comp:\cor^{k}(\nss)\to\nss
is the unique completion of
k
-corners on
\nss
. By Lemma
2.12
we know that
\comp
is continuous, and we claim that
\cK
is therefore continuous. Indeed, suppose we have a sequence
g_{n}
in
\cL\big(\cu^{k}(\ns),\cor^{k}(\nss)\big)
converging to
g
, and let
F_{1}:\nss\to\mathbb{C}
and
F_{2}:\cu^{k}(\ns)\to\mathbb{C}
be continuous. Then

\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}(\cK(g_{n}))\;=\;\int_{\cu^{k}_{x_{n}}(\ns)}F_{1}(\comp\co g_{n}(\q))\;F_{2}(\q)\;\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\q)\;=\;\varphi_{F_{1}\co\comp,F_{2}}(g_{n}),

where
\varphi_{F_{1}\co\comp,F_{2}}
is one of the functions generating the topology on
\cL(\cu^{k}(\ns),\cor^{k}(\nss))
. Hence by assumption this converges to
\varphi_{F_{1}\co\comp,F_{2}}(g)=\varphi_{F_{1},F_{2}}(\cK(g))
, so
\cK
is continuous.

Finally, note that
f=\cK\co\,\xi
, so
f
is continuous, which completes the proof.
∎

We are now able to prove the analogue of
[
8
, Lemma 3.3.28]
for compact nilspaces.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
\ns
be a compact nilspace of finite step. Let
\nss
be a degree-
k
extension of
\ns
by a compact abelian group
\ab
. Let
\cs:\ns\to\nss
be a Borel cross-section and let
\rho=\rho_{\cs}
be the associated Borel cocycle. Then
\nss
is isomorphic as a compact nilspace to the extension
M(\rho)
from Proposition
5.2
.

Proof.

Let
\pi:\nss\to\ns
be the projection for the extension. The isomorphism is given by the following map:

\theta:\nss\to M,\;\;x\mapsto\rho_{\pi(x)}+(x-\cs\co\pi(x)).

(17)

It is checked in the proof of
[
8
, Lemma 3.3.28]
that
\theta
is an isomorphism of nilspaces. Since
\nss
and
M
are both compact Hausdorff spaces, if we show that
\theta
is continuous then it is a homeomorphism
[

, Theorem 26.6]
. Since
\theta
is Borel measurable, it is continuous by Theorem
5.6
.
∎

Recall from
[
8
, Subsection 3.3.3]
that
H_{k}(\ns,\ab)
denoted the quotient of the abelian group of degree-
k
cocycles by the subgroup of coboundaries. In the category of compact nilspaces we restrict these groups to the Borel measurable cocycles. Combining the results from this section, we now deduce the following analogue of
[

, Corollary 3.3.29]
for compact nilspaces.

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
\Phi
denote the map which sends each class
C\in H_{k}(\ns,\ab)
to the isomorphism class of

, for any choice of
\rho\in C
. Then
\Phi
is a surjection from

to the set of isomorphism classes of compact degree-

extensions of
\ns
by
\ab
.

The proof is similar to that of
[
8
, Corollary 3.3.29]
.

6
Compact abelian bundles of finite rank, and averaging

In this section we define a notion of rank for compact abelian bundles and nilspaces, and we give a first topological description of nilspaces of finite rank (Lemma
6.3
). We then define an averaging operation for certain functions on these spaces, which enables us to prove a useful rigidity result concerning cocycles (Lemma
6.7
).

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\bnd
be a
k
-fold compact abelian bundle, with structure groups
\ab_{1},\dots,\ab_{k}
. We define the
rank
of
\bnd
by

\mathrm{rk}(\bnd)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathrm{rk}(\widehat{\ab_{i}})

where
\widehat{\ab_{i}}
is the Pontryagin dual of
\ab_{i}
and
\mathrm{rk}(\widehat{\ab_{i}})
is the minimal number of generators of
\widehat{\ab_{i}}
. If
\ns
is a
k
-step compact nilspace then we define its rank
\mathrm{rk}(\ns)
to be
\mathrm{rk}(\bnd)
for the associated bundle
\bnd
given by Proposition
2.9
.

When working with a compact
\ab
-bundle
\bnd
over
S
, we shall often want to ensure that the bundle is locally trivial, meaning that for every
x\in\bnd
there is an open set
U\subset S
containing the projection
\pi(x)
, and a homeomorphism
\pi^{-1}(U)\to U\times\ab
of the form
x\mapsto(\pi(x),\varphi(x))
where
\varphi:\pi^{-1}(U)\to\ab
is
\ab
-equivariant (that is
\varphi(x+z)=\varphi(x)+z
for every
x\in\pi^{-1}(U)
,
z\in\ab
). A theorem of Gleason
[
14
, Theorem 3.3]
tells us that this local triviality holds provided that
\ab
is a Lie group (see also
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
). We record the special case of this result that we shall use.

Proposition 6.2

(Consequence of Gleason’s slice theorem)
.

Let
\ab
be a compact abelian Lie group, and let
\bnd
be a continuous
\ab
-bundle. Then
\bnd
is locally trivial.

Recalling that a compact abelian group is a Lie group if and only if it has finite rank, we deduce the following result.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\ns
be a
k
-step compact nilspace of finite rank with structure groups
\ab_{1},\dots,\ab_{k}
and factors
\ns_{0},\dots,\ns_{k}
. Then
\ns_{i}
is a locally trivial
\ab_{i}
-bundle over
\ns_{i-1}
for each
i\in[k]
.

Thus, topologically a
k
-step compact nilspace of finite rank is a finite dimensional manifold.

Definition 6.4
.

Recall that every compact abelian Lie group
\ab
is isomorphic to a direct product
F\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
for some finite abelian group
F
and
n\geq 0
. We can then define a natural metric
d_{2}
on
\ab
as follows. For two elements
x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{n}
we define
d_{2}(x,y)
as the minimum of the Euclidean distances between preimages of
x
and
y
under the map
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. If
\ab
is not connected, then for points
x,y
in different connected components we declare that
d_{2}(x,y)=\infty
.

We now turn to the definition of averaging for a function on a nilspace taking values in a compact abelian group
\ab
. Here we face the problem that in general the average of a
\ab
-valued function is not well-defined. This is clear already in the simple case of the circle group
\ab=\mathbb{T}
, where there is no suitable notion of multiplication by a real number. However, if we assume that the function takes all its values in a short interval, then we can lift this
\mathbb{T}
-valued function to a real-valued one, then take the usual average, and then project the obtained value back to
\mathbb{T}
. We capture this idea more generally as follows.

Definition 6.5
.

Let
\ab\cong F\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
be a compact abelian Lie group. Let
f
be a Borel measurable function from a probability space
X
to
\ab
, and suppose there exists
z=(g,\theta)\in\ab
, (
\theta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
) such that every value of
f
lies in the ball
B_{1/4}(z)
in the metric
d_{2}
. Then, letting
\pi
denote the projection
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{T}^{n}
, for any fixed lift
\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
there is a unique function
f^{\prime}:X\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
f(x)=(g,\pi\co f^{\prime}(x))
and
f^{\prime}
takes all its values in
B_{z^{\prime}}(1/4)
in the euclidean distance. We then define the average value
\mathbb{E}f
to be
(g,\pi(\mathbb{E}f^{\prime}))
.

Note that
\mathbb{E}(f)
does not depend on the choice of the lift of
z
. Let us say that a function
f:X\to\ab
is
\delta
-concentrated
if there exists
z\in\ab
such that
f(x)\in B_{\delta}(z)
for all
x\in X
.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
\ab
be a compact abelian Lie group, and let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for any
(1/4m)
-concentrated measurable functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{m}
from a probability space
X
to
\ab
, the average of
f_{1}+\cdots+f_{m}
is well defined and satisfies
\mathbb{E}(f_{1}+\cdots+f_{m})=\mathbb{E}f_{1}+\cdots+\mathbb{E}f_{m}
.

Proof.

The assumption implies that
f_{1}+\cdots+f_{m}
is
1/4
-concentrated, so its average is well-defined. The additivity then follows in a straightforward way from the additivity of the usual average for real-valued functions.
∎

As a first application of this notion of averaging, we obtain the following rigidity result for cocycles. Stronger versions of this result will be very useful in later sections. Recall from
[
8
, Definition 3.3.18]
that a function
\cu^{k}(\ns)\to\ab
is called a coboundary if it is of the form
\q\mapsto\sigma_{k}(f\co\q)
for some function
f:\ns\to\ab
.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
\ns
be a compact
\ell
-step nilspace and let
\ab
be a compact abelian group of finite rank. There exists
\epsilon>0
such that, for every Borel measurable cocycle
\rho:\cu^{k}(\ns)\to\ab
, if
d_{2}(\rho(\q),0)\leq\epsilon
for every
\q\in\cu^{k}(\ns)
then there exists a Borel measurable function
g:\ns\to\ab
such that
\rho
is the coboundary
\q\mapsto\sigma_{k}(g\co\q)
.

Proof.

For any
x\in\ns
, consider the space of cubes
\cu^{k}_{x}(\ns)
with the Haar probability given by Lemma
3.17
. By assumption
\rho
is
\epsilon
-concentrated on
\cu^{k}_{x}(\ns)
, so we may define

g:\ns\to\ab,\;x\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{\cu^{k}_{x}(\ns)}\,\rho(\q).

Fix any
\q\in\cu^{k}(\ns)
and let
\Omega=\hom_{\q\co\omega_{k}^{-1}}(T_{k},\ns)
. For any
t\in\Omega
, by
[
8
, Lemma 3.3.31]
we have
\rho(\q)=\sum_{v\in\{0,1\}^{k}}(-1)^{|v|}\rho(t\co\Psi_{v})
. By Corollary
3.22
, we have that for each
v
the map
t\mapsto t\co\Psi_{v}
,
\Omega\to\cu^{k}_{\q(v)}(\ns)
preserves the Haar measures (where the Haar measure on
\Omega
is given by Corollary
3.20
). Hence, by averaging over
t
in the last equation, we obtain
\rho(\q)=\sum_{v\in\{0,1\}^{k}}(-1)^{|v|}g(\q(v))=\sigma_{k}(g\co\q)
.
∎

7
Counting isomorphism classes of compact finite-rank nilspaces

In this section we use the parametrization of compact extensions by cocycles, given in Corollary
5.8
, to prove the following result.

Theorem 7.1
.

There are countably many isomorphism classes of compact nilspaces of finite rank.

To prove this we shall use the following lemma, that strengthens Lemma
6.7
by allowing its premise to fail on a null set.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\ns
be a compact
\ell
-step nilspace and
\ab
be a compact abelian group of finite rank. Then there exists
\epsilon>0
such that every Borel measurable cocycle
\rho:\cu^{k}(\ns)\to\ab
satisfying
d_{2}(\rho(\q),0)\leq\epsilon
for almost every
\q\in\cu^{k}(\ns)
is a coboundary.

This relies on the following fact.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
\ns
be a compact
\ell
-step nilspace and let
\ab
be a compact abelian group of finite rank. Let
\rho:\cu^{k}(\ns)\to\ab
be a Borel measurable cocycle such that
\rho=0
for almost every element in
\cu^{k}(\ns)
. Then
\rho
is a coboundary.

Proof.

Let
S=\{x\in\ns:\rho_{x}=0\textrm{ almost surely on }\cu_{x}^{k}(\ns)\}
. It follows from the properties of the CSM on
\cu^{k}(\ns)\to\ns
,
\q\mapsto\q(0^{k})
(given by Lemma
3.17
) that
S
has probability
1
. For every
x\in S
the set
\rho_{x}+\ab\,\subset\cL_{k}(\ns,\ab)
consists of functions that are constant almost everywhere. Since by Proposition
3.11
every open set in
\ns
has positive measure, we have that
S
is dense in
\ns
. We claim that for every
x\in\ns
the function
\rho_{x}
equals a constant almost everywhere. Indeed, by density of
S
there exists a sequence of points
x_{n}\in S
converging to
x
. For each
n
choose an element
\rho_{x_{n}}+a_{n}\in M(\rho)
equal to a constant almost surely. By Proposition
5.2
the extension
M
is compact and so there is a subsequence of such functions
\rho_{x_{m}}+a_{m}
converging in
M
. By Lemma
4.8
, the limit is constant almost everywhere, and (as a point of
M
) is
\rho_{x}+a
for some
a\in\ab
, which proves our claim. From this, we deduce that the function
g:\ns\to\ab
,
x\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{\q\in\cu^{k}_{x}(\ns)}\rho(\q)
is well-defined and that for each
x\in\ns
we have
\rho(\q)=g(x)
for almost every
\q\in\cu^{k}_{x}(\ns)
. We now claim that for every
x\in\ns
, we have
\rho(\q)=\sigma_{k}(g\co\q)
for almost every
\q\in\cu^{k}(\ns)
. This follows from an argument similar to the second paragraph in the proof of Lemma
6.7
. Thus, denoting by
f
the coboundary
\q\mapsto\sigma_{k}(g\co\q)
, the cocycle
\rho^{\prime}=\rho-f
has the property that for every
x\in\ns
we have
\rho^{\prime}_{x}=0
almost surely on
\cu_{x}^{k}(\ns)
. This implies that
\rho^{\prime}=0
everywhere. Indeed, for an arbitrary fixed
\q\in\cu^{k}(\ns)
, and any
t\in\hom_{\q\co\omega_{k}^{-1}}(T_{k},\ns)
, by
[
8
, Lemma 3.3.31]
we have
\rho^{\prime}(\q)=\beta(t,\rho^{\prime})
, and then, averaging both sides of this equation over
t
, the right side vanishes since
\rho_{x}^{\prime}
is 0 almost everywhere (using again that each map
t\mapsto t\circ\Psi_{v}
is probability-preserving, by Corollary
3.22
).
∎

Proof of Lemma
7.2
.

We argue as in the proof of Lemma
6.7
. Let
S
be the set of elements
x\in\ns
for which
\rho_{x}
is almost surely within
\epsilon
of
0
in
d_{2}
. By the assumption, we have that
S
has probability
1
. We define
g:\ns\to\ab
for
x\in S
by
g(x)=\mathbb{E}_{\cu^{k}_{x}(\ns)}\,\rho(\q)
, and for
x\in\ns\setminus S
by setting
g(x)=0
. Letting
\rho_{2}(\q)=\sigma_{k}(g\co\q)
, the same averaging argument shows that
\rho_{2}=\rho
almost surely. By Lemma
7.3
the difference
\rho-\rho_{2}
is a coboundary, whence
\rho
is a coboundary.
∎

We can now prove the main result of this section.

Proof of Theorem
7.1
.

We claim that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, for every compact
(k-1)
-step nilspace
\ns
of finite rank and every compact abelian Lie group
\ab
, there are at most countably many non-isomorphic degree-
k
extensions of
\ns
by
\ab
. This implies the theorem by induction on
k
.

To prove the claim, we first associate with each
\ab
-extension of
\ns
a measurable cocycle
\rho_{\cs}
generated by a piecewise-continuous cross section
\cs
for the extension. We construct
\cs
as follows.

Since
\ns
is a finite-dimensional compact manifold, there exist disjoint open sets
U_{1},U_{2},\dots,U_{r}
, each homeomorphic to the open unit Euclidean ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some
n\geq 0
, such that
U=\bigcup_{i\in[r]}U_{i}
has measure
1
in
\ns
. Let
M
denote the given degree-
k
extension of
\ns
by
\ab
, with projection
\pi:M\to\ns
. Every
\ab
-bundle over a contractible space is trivial
[
27
, Ch. 4, Corollary 10.3]
. Therefore there exists a continuous cross section
\cs_{i}
locally on each
U_{i}
, and combining these we obtain a piecewise continuous (hence Borel-measurable) cross section
\cs:\ns\to M
, with
\cs|_{U_{i}}=\cs_{i}
. Let
T\subseteq\cu^{k+1}(\ns)
denote the set of cubes whose vertices are all in
U
. We can partition the cubes
\q\in T
according to which set
U_{i}
contains each value
\q(v)
, that is
T=\bigsqcup_{i\in[r]^{\{0,1\}^{k+1}}}T_{i}
, where
T_{i}\,=\,T\;\cap\prod_{v\in\{0,1\}^{k+1}}U_{i_{v}}
. The cocycle
\rho_{\cs}:\cu^{k+1}(\ns)\to\ab
is defined by
\rho_{\cs}(\q)=\sigma_{k+1}(\cs\co\q-\q^{\prime})
for any
\q^{\prime}\in\cu^{k+1}(M)
with
\pi\co\q^{\prime}=\q
(recall
[
8
, Lemma 3.3.21]
). Note that
\rho_{\cs}
is continuous on each part
T_{i}
.

Now let
\epsilon>0
be as given by Lemma
7.2
. Then for any other degree-
k
extension
M^{\prime}
, we can generate a cocycle
\rho_{\cs^{\prime}}
as above, and if
d_{2}\big(\rho_{\cs}(\q),\rho_{\cs^{\prime}}(\q)\big)<\epsilon
for almost every
\q
then by the lemma
\rho_{\cs^{\prime}}
must equal
\rho_{\cs}
plus a coboundary, which implies, by Corollary
5.8
, that
M^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
M
.

Let
d_{\infty}
denote the uniform metric on the set of continuous functions
\cu^{k+1}(\ns)\to\ab
, defined by
d_{\infty}(f_{1},f_{2})=\sup_{\q}d_{2}\big(f_{1}(\q),f_{2}(\q)\big)
. For each
i
, the space of continuous functions
T_{i}\to\ab
with
d_{\infty}
is separable (essentially by separability of the space
C(Y,\ab)
for any compact subset
Y
of a Euclidean space
[
28
, Theorem (4.19)]
). Therefore there exists a countable
\frac{\epsilon}{2}
-net
(f_{i,j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of such continuous functions on each
T_{i}
. Then, by the previous paragraph, for each choice of functions
\{f_{i,j_{i}}:i\in[r]^{\{0,1\}^{k+1}}\}
from these nets, there can only be at most one isomorphism class of extensions having an associated cocycle
\rho_{\cs}
satisfying
d_{\infty}(\rho_{\cs}|_{T_{i}},f_{i,j_{i}})<\epsilon/2
for each
i
.
∎

8
Compact nilspaces as inverse limits of finite-rank nilspaces

In this section we treat one of the central results from
[
9
]
, namely that every compact nilspace can be expressed as an inverse limit of compact nilspaces of finite rank. Before we give the formal statement, let us detail the inverse limit construction in this category.

Definition 8.1
.

An
inverse system
(or projective system) of compact nilspaces over
\mathbb{N}
is a family of continuous nilspace morphisms
\{\varphi_{ij}:\ns_{j}\to\ns_{i}~|~i,j\in\mathbb{N},i\leq j\}
, where
\ns_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
are compact nilspaces, such that
\varphi_{jj}
is the identity morphism for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
, and for all
i,j,k\in\mathbb{N}
with
i\leq j\leq k
we have
\varphi_{ij}\co\varphi_{jk}=\varphi_{ik}
. The inverse system is said to be
strict
if every morphism
\varphi_{ij}
is fibre-surjective.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
S=(\varphi_{ij}:\ns_{j}\to\ns_{i})_{i\leq j}
be a strict inverse system of compact nilspaces. Let

\ns=\Big\{(x_{i})\in\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\ns_{i}~|~\varphi_{ij}(x_{j})=x_{i}\;\;\forall\,i,j\in\mathbb{N},\;i\leq j\Big\},

and for each
n\geq 0
let
\cu^{n}(\ns)=\big\{(\q_{i})\in\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\cu^{n}(\ns_{i})~|~\varphi_{ij}\co\q_{j}=\q_{i}\;\;\forall\,i,j\in\mathbb{N},\;i\leq j\big\}
. The space
\ns
together with the cube sets
\cu^{n}(\ns)
is a compact nilspace, called the
inverse limit
of
S
, and denoted
\varprojlim_{i}\ns_{i}
. We have that
\ns
is
k
-step if and only if every
\ns_{i}
is
k
-step.

We call the maps
\varphi_{ij}
the
transition morphisms
. The
projections
on
\ns
, denoted by
\varphi_{j}
, are the coordinate projections on
\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\ns_{i}
restricted to
\ns
. If the inverse system is strict then the projections are also fibre-surjective morphisms. The
\ell
-th structure group of
\varprojlim_{i}\ns_{i}
is the inverse limit of the system
(\alpha_{\ell,ij}:\ab_{\ell,j}\to\ab_{\ell,i})_{i\leq j}
, where
\ab_{\ell,j}
is the
\ell
-th structure group of
\ns_{j}
and
\alpha_{\ell,ij}
is the
\ell
-th structure morphism of
\varphi_{ij}
.

Proof.

A straightforward argument with convergent sequences shows that
\ns
is a closed subspace of the compact space
\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\ns_{i}
, and similarly for each set
\cu^{n}(\ns)
. The ergodicity and composition axioms are clear. Let us prove that
\ns
satisfies the completion axiom. Let
\q^{\prime}
be an
n
-corner on
\ns
. Then for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
the projection
\q_{i}^{\prime}=\varphi_{i}\co\q^{\prime}
is an
n
-corner on
\ns_{i}
. Now
\q_{1}^{\prime}
has a completion
\q_{1}
and by fibre-surjectivity there exists
\tilde{\q}_{1}\in\cu^{n}(\ns_{2})
such that
\varphi_{12}\co\tilde{\q}_{1}=\q_{1}
(recall
[
8
, Lemma 3.3.9]
).
The restriction of
\tilde{\q}_{1}
to
P=\{0,1\}^{n}\setminus\{1^{n}\}
is in the same fibre of the map
\q^{\prime}\mapsto\varphi_{12}\co\q^{\prime}
as
\q_{2}^{\prime}
, so by
[
8
, Lemma 3.3.12 (ii)]
these two corners differ by an
n
-corner on
\cD_{k}(\ker(\alpha_{k}))
, where
\alpha_{k}
is the
k
-th structure morphism of
\varphi_{12}
. Now by modifying
\tilde{\q}_{1}
, we can obtain a cube
\q_{2}
that is still in this fibre (so that
\varphi_{12}\co\q_{2}=\q_{1}
) and such that
\q_{2}|_{P}=\q_{2}^{\prime}|_{P}
. Indeed, we can obtain
\q_{2}
by adding first an appropriate element of
\ker(\alpha_{k})
to the values of
\tilde{\q}_{1}
at the vertices of the 1-face
\{0^{n},(1,0,\dots,0)\}
so that it agrees with
\q_{2}^{\prime}
at
0^{n}
, then repeat this for each subsequent 1-face along a Hamiltonian path in the graph of 1-faces on
\{0,1\}^{n}
(similar arguments were used several times in
[
8
]
, for instance in the proof of
[
8
, Lemma 3.2.25]
). We have thus obtained a cube
\q_{2}
completing
\q_{2}^{\prime}
and such that
\varphi_{12}\co\q_{2}=\q_{1}
. Now we repeat this operation to obtain
\q_{3}
completing
\q_{3}^{\prime}
such that
\varphi_{23}\co\q_{3}=\q_{2}
, and so on. This yields a sequence of cubes
\q_{i}\in\cu^{n}(\ns)
such that for each
i
we have
\varphi_{j}\co\q_{i}
is a completion of
\q_{j}^{\prime}
for all
j\leq i
. The limit of a convergent subsequence of
(\q_{i})
is a completion of
\q^{\prime}
. To see the last claim in the lemma, recall from
[
8
, Definition 3.3.7]
that a fibre-surjective morphism preserves the property of being
k
-step.
∎

We can now state the main result of this section.

Theorem 8.3
.

Every
k
-step compact nilspace is an inverse limit of compact nilspaces of finite rank.

The basic case of this theorem, concerning 1-step nilspaces, is given by the standard result that every compact abelian group is an inverse limit of compact abelian Lie groups (see
[
24
, Corollary 2.43]
). This standard result also yields the following example of an inverse limit of nilspaces, which will be used in the proof of Theorem
8.3
.

Proposition 8.4
.

Let
\ns
be a
k
-step compact nilspace. Then
\ns
is an inverse limit of
k
-step nilspaces
\ns_{i}
, each with
k
-th structure group a compact abelian Lie group, and with
\cF_{k-1}(\ns_{i})
isomorphic to
\cF_{k-1}(\ns)
.

Proof.

By the standard result for compact abelian groups recalled above, for the
k
-th structure group
\ab_{k}
of
\ns
we have for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
a compact abelian Lie group
\ab_{k,i}
and a surjective continuous homomorphism
\alpha_{i}:\ab_{k}\to\ab_{k,i}
such that
\ab_{k}=\varprojlim\ab_{k,i}
. (For
i\leq j
the transition morphism
\alpha_{i,j}
is
x\mapsto x+\ker\alpha_{i}
for
x\in\ab_{k,j}\cong\ab_{k}/\ker\alpha_{j}
.) Define for each
i
the
k
-step compact nilspace
\ns_{i}
as the image of
\ns
under the map
\varphi_{i}
that sends
x\in\ns
to the orbit
x+\ker\alpha_{i}
. A straightforward calculation shows that
\ns_{i}
, with the quotient cube-structure of
\ns
by
\ker\alpha_{i}
and the quotient topology, is a
k
-step compact nilspace with
k
-th structure group
\ab_{k,i}
. The projections
\ns\to\cF_{k-1}(\ns)
and
\ns_{i}\to\cF_{k-1}(\ns_{i})
are both given by the orbit map for the
\ab_{k}
-action, and it follows that
\cF_{k-1}(\ns)\cong\cF_{k-1}(\ns_{i})
as compact nilspaces. The transition morphisms
\varphi_{ij}:\ns_{j}\to\ns_{i}
, defined by
x+\ker\alpha_{j}\mapsto x+\ker\alpha_{i}
, are all clearly fibre-surjective.
∎

Recall from
[
8
, Lemma 3.3.21]
that given a degree-
k
extension
\nss
of
\ns
by
\ab
, and given a cross section
\cs:\ns\to\nss
, letting
f:\nss\to\ab
,
y\mapsto\cs\co\pi(y)-y
, the cocycle generated by
\cs
is the degree-
k
cocycle
\rho_{\cs}:\cu^{k+1}(\ns)\to\ab
defined by
\rho_{\cs}(\q)=\sigma_{k+1}(f\co\q^{\prime})
, for any
\q^{\prime}\in\cu^{k+1}(\nss)
such that
\pi\co\q^{\prime}=\q
.

For the proof of Theorem
8.3
, it will be useful to be able to tell in a simple way whether a given cocycle on a nilspace
\ns
induces a well-defined cocycle on another nilspace
\ns^{\prime}
via some given fibre-surjective morphism
\ns\to\ns^{\prime}
. The following definition provides such a criterion.

Definition 8.5
.

Let
\ns
be a
k
-step compact nilspace, and for some compact nilspace
\ns^{\prime}
let
\psi:\cF_{k-1}(\ns)\to\ns^{\prime}
be a continuous fibre-surjective morphism. We say that a measurable cross section
\cs:\cF_{k-1}(\ns)\to\ns
is
consistent with the factor

\ns^{\prime}
if the cocycle
\rho_{\cs}:\cu^{k+1}(\cF_{k-1}(\ns))\to\ab_{k}
satisfies
\rho_{\cs}(\q_{1})=\rho_{\cs}(\q_{2})
for every
\q_{1},\q_{2}
such that
\psi\co\q_{1}=\psi\co\q_{2}
.

Thus if
\cs
is consistent with
\ns^{\prime}
then
\rho_{\cs}
induces a cocycle
\rho^{\prime}:\cu^{k+1}(\ns^{\prime})\to\ab_{k}
, well-defined by
\rho^{\prime}(\q^{\prime})=\rho_{\cs}(\q)
for any
\q
such that
\psi\co\q=\q^{\prime}
. Note that we shall often refer to the image of a fibre-surjective morphism on
\ns
as a
fibre-surjective factor
of
\ns
.

Lemma 8.6
.

Let
\psi:\cF_{k-1}(\ns)\to\ns^{\prime}
be a fibre-surjective morphism and let
\cs:\cF_{k-1}(\ns)\to\ns
be a cross section consistent with
\ns^{\prime}
. Let
\sim
be the equivalence relation on
\ns
defined by

x\sim y\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;\big(\;\psi\co\pi_{k-1}(x)=\psi\co\pi_{k-1}(y)\textrm{ and }x-\cs\co\pi_{k-1}(x)=y-\cs\co\pi_{k-1}(y)\;\big).

Then the quotient nilspace

is a fibre-surjective factor of
\ns

Proof.

Let

Lemma 8.7
.

Let

Consider the local translation

8.1
Proof of the inverse limit theorem

To prove Theorem

, we argue by induction on

, starting with the trivial case

. For

we suppose that
\ns
is a
k
-step compact nilspace such that the nilspace
\nss=\cF_{k-1}(\ns)
is the inverse limit of a strict inverse system
(\tau_{ij}:\nss_{j}\to\nss_{i})_{i,j\in\mathbb{N},i\leq j}
. We denote by

the projection
\ns\to\nss
, and for each

we denote by

the projection
\nss\to\nss_{i}
and let

8.1.1
The inductive step: construction of the sequence
S

We now apply Proposition
8.4
to
\ns
. Let
B_{0}=\ab_{k}
be the
k
-th structure group of
\ns
, and let
B_{i}=\ker\alpha_{i}\leq\ab_{k}
for each

(for the homomorphisms
\alpha_{i}
from the proof of the proposition). Then
B_{0}\geq B_{1}\geq\cdots
, with
\bigcap_{i\in\mathbb{N}}B_{i}=\{0\}

\ns=\varprojlim_{i}\ns/_{B_{i}}

q_{i-1,i}
denote the transition morphism
\ns/_{B_{i}}\to\ns/_{B_{i-1}}
and

the projection
\ns\to\ns/_{B_{i}}

has
k
-th structure group

The main goal of the inductive step is to construct, starting with the 1-point nilspace
\ns_{0}
, a sequence
S=\{(\ns_{i},\psi_{i},\psi_{i}^{\prime},h_{i}):i\in\mathbb{N}\}
, where for each

we have a compact nilspace
\ns_{i}
of finite rank, and fibre-surjective morphisms
\psi_{i}:\ns/_{B_{i}}\to\ns_{i}
and
\psi_{i}^{\prime}:\ns_{i}\to\ns_{i-1}
, with the following properties:

(i)

For every

, the restriction of
\psi_{i}
to each class of
\sim_{k-1}
in
\ns/_{B_{i}}
is injective. In other words, the

-th structure group of

is the same as that of

.

(ii)

For every

, we have
\psi^{\prime}_{i}\circ\psi_{i}=\psi_{i-1}\co q_{i-1,i}
.

(iii)

Letting
\pi_{i}
denote the projection
\ns_{i}\mapsto\cF_{k-1}(\ns_{i})
, and
\kappa_{i}
denote the projection

\cF_{k-1}(\ns_{i})\cong\nss_{h_{i}}
and

Properties (i) and (iii) imply that any two distinct points in

are separated by a map

for some

\ns
generated by these maps is Hausdorff. Since the original topology on

is compact, it follows that this initial topology equals the original topology (see
[
6

). The maps
\varphi_{ij}=\psi^{\prime}_{i+1}\co\cdots\co\psi^{\prime}_{j}

is the inverse limit of the system

.

We shall now construct the sequence
S
inductively starting from
\ns_{0}
. Suppose that
(\ns_{i},\psi_{i},\psi^{\prime}_{i},h_{i})
has been constructed for

. Then, to begin with, we construct a Borel cocycle
\cu^{k+1}(\nss)\to\ab_{k}/B_{m+1}
generated by a cross section
\cs
that is almost consistent with some appropriate factor of
\nss
.

Constructing a measurable cross section
\cs
:
since
\ns_{m}
is a compact locally-trivial
\ab_{k}/B_{m}
-bundle over
\nss_{h_{m}}
, there is a finite cover of
\nss_{h_{m}}
by closed subsets

such that every preimage
\pi_{m}^{-1}(W_{i})\subset\ns_{m}
is a trivial
\ab_{k}/B_{m}
-bundle over
W_{i}
. For each
i\in[r]
, let
\theta_{i}:W_{i}\to\ns_{m}
be a continuous cross section (thus
\pi_{m}\co\theta_{i}
is the identity map on
W_{i}
).

For each

let
W^{\prime}_{a}
denote the preimage of the set
\theta_{i}(W_{a})
under
\psi_{m}\co q_{m,m+1}:\ns/_{B_{m+1}}\to\ns_{m}
. Note that

is a
\ab_{k}/B_{m+1}
-bundle over
\tau_{h_{m}}^{-1}(W_{a})\subset\nss

. Let

be a metric on

generating its topology.

Now fix an arbitrary
\epsilon>0
. We claim that for every

there exists an open set
U_{p}\subset\tau_{h_{m}}^{-1}(W_{a})
containing

with the following properties:

(iv)

There exists a continuous cross section
\cs_{p}:U_{p}\to\ns/_{B_{m+1}}
.

(v)

The diameter of
\cs_{p}(U_{p})
in the metric

is at most

.

(vi)

For some

, the set

is in the collection

Property (iv) is ensured by the local triviality of the bundle, and it can be satisfied together with (v) by letting

be a ball of sufficiently small radius, say. Property (vi) can also be satisfied since

generates the topology on

and for every

is compact there exists a finite cover by such sets

, thus for some points

\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{p_{i}}\,\supset\,\tau_{h_{m}}^{-1}(W_{a})

we have

. Then, by dividing
\tau_{h_{m}}^{-1}(W_{a})
into the atoms of the Boolean algebra generated by

, and then using one of the cross sections

for each atom, we can construct a Borel measurable cross section
\cs_{a}:\tau_{h_{m}}^{-1}(W_{a})\to\ns/_{B_{m+1}}
with the following properties:

(vii)

The cross section
\cs_{a}
is continuous on
\tau_{t_{a}}^{-1}(v)
for every

.

(viii)

The diameter of
\cs_{a}(\tau_{t_{a}}^{-1}(v))
in

is at most
\epsilon
for every

.

Note that we obtain (viii) from (v) because each
\tau_{t_{a}}^{-1}(v)
is entirely contained in an atom of the Boolean algebra, which contains
\tau_{t_{a}}^{-1}(Q)
for some open
Q\supset v
. Let

. Using these partial cross sections, we now claim that we can construct a global Borel cross section
\cs:\nss\to\ns/_{B_{m+1}}

(ix)

The cross section
\cs

\tau_{t}^{-1}(v)
where
v\in\nss_{t}
.

(x)

The diameter of
\cs(\tau_{t}^{-1}(v))

\epsilon

(xi)

If
v_{1},v_{2}\in\nss

\tau_{h_{m}}(v_{1})=\tau_{h_{m}}(v_{2})

\psi_{m}\co q_{m,m+1}(\cs(v_{1}))=\psi_{m}\co q_{m,m+1}(\cs(v_{2}))

Indeed, we can construct
\cs
by again dividing
\nss
into the atoms of the Boolean algebra generated by the sets
\tau_{h_{m}}^{-1}(W_{a})

\cs_{a}
on each atom. This immediately gives (ix) and (x), and then (xi) follows from (iii) above.

Let
\rho_{\cs}:\cu^{k+1}(\nss)\to\ab_{k}/B_{m+1}
be the cocycle generated by
\cs
.

Now for any fixed
\epsilon_{2}>0
, we note that if
\epsilon>0
is small enough, then property (x) implies the following fact, which tells us that
\cs
is almost consistent with the factor
\nss_{t}

\forall\,\q_{1},\q_{2}\in\cu^{k+1}(\nss)\textrm{ with }\tau_{t}\co\q_{1}=\tau_{t}\co\q_{2},\textrm{ we have }d_{2}\big(\rho_{\cs}(\q_{1})-\rho_{\cs}(\q_{2})\big)\leq\epsilon_{2}.

(18)

Indeed
\tau_{t}\co\q_{1}=\tau_{t}\co\q_{2}

\q_{1}(v),\q_{2}(v)

\tau_{t}

for every

. Finally, from the expression

Note that property (xi) also implies that

\forall\,\q_{1},\q_{2}\in\cu^{k+1}(\nss)\textrm{ with }\tau_{h_{m}}\co\q_{1}=\tau_{h_{m}}\co\q_{2},\textrm{ we have }\rho_{\cs}(\q_{1})-\rho_{\cs}(\q_{2})\in B_{m}/B_{m+1}.

\cs\co\q_{2}(v)
are in the same orbit of

for every

. In particular we have

To complete the proof we shall now use
\rho_{\cs}
to find a new cross section
\cs^{\prime}:\nss\to\ns/B_{m+1}
that is consistent with the factor
\nss_{t}
, and that is also congruent to

B_{m}

. If we find

then, firstly, thanks to the consistency, we can apply Lemma
8.6
with map
\psi=\tau_{t}
, obtaining a new fibre-surjective factor

of

that is an extension of

by
\ab_{k}/B_{m+1}
, and so (i) holds. Secondly
\psi_{m}\co q_{m,m+1}
factors through
\ns_{m+1}
, which yields
\psi_{m+1}^{\prime}
satisfying (ii). Indeed, by definition of the relation
\sim
in Lemma
8.6
we have that

are in the same fibre of
\psi_{m+1}
if and only if
\tau_{t}\co\kappa_{m+1}(x)=\tau_{t}\co\kappa_{m+1}(y)
and

.

Averaging
\rho_{\cs}
to obtain a fibre-surjective factor
\ns_{m+1}
of
\ns/_{B_{m+1}}
:
to find
\cs^{\prime}

\rho^{\prime}
by averaging

Recall that, by

-fold sub-bundle of

We define

The averaging here is relative to the Haar measure on

\forall\,\q_{1},\q_{2}\in\cu^{k+1}(\nss)\textrm{ with }\tau_{t}\co\q_{1}=\tau_{t}\co\q_{2},\textrm{ we have }\rho^{\prime}(\q_{1})=\rho^{\prime}(\q_{2}).

(21)

We shall now prove that
\rho^{\prime}
is a cocycle by showing that it inherits the required properties from
\rho_{\cs}
thanks to the linearity of the averaging in (

and linearity using the fact that any set

and

and

is the restriction of

to

. Note that the existence of

is guaranteed by simplicial completion
[

, Lemma 3.1.5]

[

, Lemma 3.3.12 (iii)]
, we have a Haar probability on
\Omega=\beta_{k+2}^{-1}(\beta_{k+2}(\q))
. Then, by
[

, Lemma 3.3.12 (iv)]
, the probability spaces
\beta_{k+1}^{-1}(\beta_{k+1}(\q_{i}))

are faithfully embedded as factors and coupled in the big probability space

. Using the concatenation property for
\rho
in
\Omega
(when the random cube is restricted to

) and linearity of expectation we obtain that
\rho^{\prime}(c_{3})=\rho^{\prime}(c_{1})+\rho^{\prime}(c_{2})

It remains to prove that the cocycle

\rho^{\prime\prime}

By (

is consistent with the factor

. Applying Lemma

.

9
Rigidity of morphisms

Given a set
X
, a metric space
(Y,d)
, and maps
\phi,\phi^{\prime}:X\to Y
, we say that
\phi^{\prime}
is an
\epsilon
-modification
of
\phi
if for every
x\in X
we have
d(\phi(x),\phi^{\prime}(x))\leq\epsilon
.

Definition 9.1
.

Let
\ns
be a compact nilspace, and let
\nss
be a
k
-step compact nilspace with a metric
d
generating its topology. A map
\phi:\ns\to\nss
is a
\delta
-quasimorphism
if for every
\q\in\cu^{k+1}(\ns)
there exists
\q^{\prime}\in\cu^{k+1}(\nss)
such that
d\big(\phi\co\q(v),\q^{\prime}(v)\big)\leq\delta
for every
v\in\{0,1\}^{k+1}
.

In this section we establish the following result.

Theorem 9.2
.

Let
\nss
be a
k
-step compact nilspace of finite rank, with a metric
d
generating its topology. Then for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that the following holds. If
\ns
is a compact nilspace and
\phi:\ns\to\nss
is a Borel
\delta
-quasimorphism, then
\phi
has an
\epsilon
-modification that is a continuous morphism.

In the proof we shall use the fact that the metric
d
can be assumed to be invariant under the action of the structure group
\ab_{k}
(Lemma
2.11
). We shall also use the following ‘rectification result’ for cubes.

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
\ns
be a
k
-step compact nilspace with metric
d
. For every
\epsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that the following holds. If
\q\in\cu^{k+1}(\ns)
satisfies
d^{\prime}\big(\pi_{k-1}\co\q(v,0),\pi_{k-1}\co\q(v,1)\big)\leq\delta
for every
v\in\{0,1\}^{k}
, then

10
Characterizing compact connected nilspaces of finite rank

Recall from
[
8
, Subsection 3.2.4]
that given a
k
-step nilspace
\ns
we denote by
\tran_{i}(\ns)
the group of translations of height
i
on
\ns
(or
i
-translations). By a slight abuse of notation, when
\ns
is a compact nilspace we shall write
\tran_{i}(\ns)
for the group of
i
-translations that are also
continuous
functions. A central goal in this section is to show that if
\ns
has finite rank then
\tran_{i}(\ns)
is a Lie group for each
i
and
\tran_{1}(\ns)
acts transitively on the connected components of
\ns
. This will then enable us to show that if
\ns
has connected structure groups then it can be identified with a filtered nilmanifold (Theorem
10.17
).

Throughout this section we shall abbreviate ‘compact and finite-rank’ by writing ‘
cfr
’.

Lemma 10.1
.

Let
\ns
be a
cfr

k
-step nilspace. Then every element of
\tran(\ns)
is a homeomorphism
\ns\to\ns
preserving the Haar measure.

Proof.

Each translation is an invertible continuous map from the compact Hausdorff space
\ns
to itself and so its inverse is also continuous
[
32
, Theorem 26.6]
. Moreover, the translation is a fibre-surjective automorphism of
\ns
, so it is measure preserving by Corollary
3.7
.
∎

For a compact space
X
and a space
Y
with metric
d
, recall that
C(X,Y)
denotes the space of continuous functions
f:X\to Y
, with the topology induced by the uniform metric
d_{\infty}(f_{1},f_{2})=\sup_{x\in X}d(f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x))
. We record the following basic fact.

Lemma 10.2
.

Let
\ns
be a
cfr

k
-step nilspace and let
d
be a metric generating the topology on
\ns
. Then for every
i\in[k]
, the group
\tran_{i}(\ns)
equipped with the restriction of the uniform metric on
C(\ns,\ns)
is a Polish group.

Proof.

The group
H(\ns)
of homeomorphisms on
\ns
, with the relative topology from
C(X,X)
, is a Polish group
[
3
, §1.3, Example (v)]
. It follows from the definition of translations
[
8
, Definition 3.2.27]
and the closure of cube sets that
\tran_{i}(\ns)
is a closed subgroup of
H(\ns)
, hence it is also a Polish group. By definition the metric
d_{\infty}
generates the topology on
\tran_{i}(\ns)
.
∎

Recall that by
[
8
, Lemma 3.2.31]
a translation maps every class of the relation
\sim_{k-1}
onto another such class. This enables us to define a translation on the factor
\ns_{k-1}=\cF_{k-1}(\ns)
, as in the following lemma. Recall also that the topology on
\ns_{k-1}
is the quotient topology from
\ns
, which can be metrized by the quotient metric

defined in (
3
). We then denote by

the uniform metric on
\tran(\ns_{k-1})
relative to

.

Lemma 10.3
.

For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
let
h
be the map sending each
\alpha\in\tran_{i}(\ns)
to the map
h(\alpha)
on
\ns_{k-1}
defined by
h(\alpha)(y)=\pi_{k-1}(\alpha(x))
, for any
x\in\ns
such that
\pi_{k-1}(x)=y
. Then
h
is a continuous homomorphism
\tran_{i}(\ns)\to\tran_{i}(\ns_{k-1})
.

Proof.

That
h
is a homomorphism
\tran_{i}(\ns)\to\tran_{i}(\ns_{k-1})
follows from the definitions. The continuity follows similarly, thus

\displaystyle d^{\prime}_{\infty}\big(h(\alpha_{1}),h(\alpha_{2})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\ns_{k-1}}d^{\prime}\big(h(\alpha_{1})(y),h(\alpha_{2})(y)\big)\;\;=\;\;\sup_{x\in\ns}d^{\prime}\big(\pi_{k-1}(\alpha_{1}(x)),\pi_{k-1}(\alpha_{2}(x))\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\ns}\;\;\;\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}\sim_{k-1}\alpha_{1}(x)\\
x_{2}\sim_{k-1}\alpha_{2}(x)\end{subarray}}d(x_{1},x_{2})\;\;\leq\;\;\sup_{x\in\ns}d\big(\alpha_{1}(x),\alpha_{2}(x)\big)=d_{\infty}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}).

∎

An important result toward our goal in this section is that if an

-translation on
\ns_{k-1}
is sufficiently close to the identity then it can be lifted to an

-translation on
\ns

Lemma 10.4

Let
\ns

We shall use notation from

is a class of pairs
(x_{0},x_{1})\in\pi_{k-1}^{-1}(\q(v))\times\pi_{k-1}^{-1}(\alpha\co\q(v))
satisfying
d(x_{0},x_{1})\leq\epsilon
and corresponding to a local translation
\phi
, thus
x_{1}=\phi(x_{0})
for every such pair. The difference
\cs\co\q(v)-\q^{\prime}(v)
is then the element
a_{v}\in\ab_{k}
which has to be added to
\tilde{\q}_{0}(v)
in order to have
\phi(\tilde{\q}_{0}(v)+a_{v})=\tilde{\q}_{1}(v)
, namely
a_{v}=\phi^{-1}(\tilde{\q}_{1}(v))-\tilde{\q}_{0}(v)
. Thus, letting
a
denote the function
\{0,1\}^{k-i+1}\to\ab_{k}
,
v\mapsto a_{v}
, we have
\rho_{\cs}(\cs\co\q-\q^{\prime})=\sigma_{k-i+1}(a)
. To show that this must be a small element of
\ab_{k}
, we argue as follows. By assumption on
\alpha
, the cube
\tilde{\q}
satisfies the premise of Lemma
9.3
with
\delta=\epsilon
. Thus, given
\epsilon_{1}>0
to be fixed later, if
\epsilon<\epsilon_{1}
is sufficiently small then by that lemma there must exist
\q^{*}=\q^{*}_{0}\times\q^{*}_{1}\in\cu^{k-i+1}(\cT)
such that for all
v
we have
d(\q^{*}_{i}(v),\tilde{\q}_{i}(v))\leq\epsilon_{1}
for
i=0,1
, and with
\pi_{k-1}\co\q^{*}_{1}=\pi_{k-1}\co\q^{*}_{0}
. For each
v
let
a^{*}(v)=\q^{*}_{1}(v)-\q^{*}_{0}(v)\in\ab_{k}
. It now suffices to show that
d_{2}(a^{*}(v),a(v))\leq\epsilon_{2}/2^{k-i+1}
for every
v
, for then, by virtue of
\langle\q^{*}_{0},\q^{*}_{1}\rangle_{1}
being a cube, we must have
\sigma_{k-i+1}(a^{*})=0
and so
\sigma_{k-i+1}(a)\leq\epsilon_{2}
as claimed. To show that
d_{2}(a^{*}(v),a(v))
is small we can apply Lemma
8.7
with
\delta=2\epsilon_{1}
, since we know on one hand that
d(\q_{0}^{*}(v),\tilde{\q}_{0}(v))\leq\epsilon_{1}
, and on the other hand that
d(\phi^{-1}(\tilde{\q}_{1}(v)),\tilde{\q}_{1}(v))\leq\epsilon
and
d(\q_{1}^{*}(v),\tilde{\q}_{1}(v))\leq\epsilon_{1}
, so that
d(\phi^{-1}(\tilde{\q}_{1}(v)),\q_{1}^{*}(v))\leq\epsilon+\epsilon_{1}\leq 2\epsilon_{1}
.

We can now apply Lemma
6.7
. Thus if
\epsilon_{2}
is sufficiently small then there exists a Borel function
g:\cF_{k-1}(\ns)\to\ab_{k}
such that
\rho_{\cs}(\q)=\sigma_{k-1+i}(g\co\q)
for all
\q\in\cu^{k-i+1}\big(\cF_{k-1}(\ns)\big)
. Let
m:\cF_{k-1}(\ns)\to\cT^{*}
be the measurable function
x\mapsto\cs(x)-g(x)
. We have that
m
is a morphism, indeed for any
\q\in\cu^{n}(\cF_{k-1}(\ns))
, on one hand we clearly have that the
\cT^{*}
-valued map
m\co\q
is a lift of
\q
, and on the other hand for some (any) lift
\q^{\prime}\in\cu^{n}(\cT^{*})
of
\q
we have
\sigma_{k-i+1}(m\co\q-\q^{\prime})=\sigma_{k-i+1}(\cs\co\q-\q^{\prime})-\sigma_{k-i+1}(g\co\q)=\rho_{\cs}(\q)-\rho_{\cs}(\q)=0
, so by
[
8
, Definition 3.3.13 (ii)]
we have indeed
m\co\q\in\cu^{n}(\cT^{*})
. Now
[
8
, Proposition 3.3.39]
gives us an
i
-translation
\beta
on
\ns
that is a lift of
\alpha
, and is defined as follows: for each
x\in\ns
we have
\beta(x)=\phi(x)
, where
\phi
is the local translation corresponding to the equivalence class
m\co\pi_{k-1}(x)\in\cT^{*}
. It only remains to check that
\beta
is Borel measurable, as then, being a morphism, it must be continuous by Theorem
5.6
. By
[
8
, Definition 3.3.34 and Proposition 3.3.36]
, the nilspace
\cT
is a continuous
\ab
-bundle over
\cT^{*}
, for the polish group
\ab=\{(z,z):z\in\ab_{k}\}\leq\ab_{k}\times\ab_{k}
. Therefore, arguing as in the end of the proof of Lemma
5.5
, we obtain a Borel cross section
\lambda:\cT^{*}\to\cT
, and so the map
\lambda\co m\co\pi_{k-1}:\ns\to\cT
is Borel. Moreover, letting
\lambda\co m\co\pi_{k-1}(x)=(y_{0},y_{1})
, we have
\beta(x)=y_{1}+(x-y_{0})
, so
\beta
is indeed Borel.
∎

We shall need a useful criterion for a translation to lie in the kernel of
h
. Note that if
\alpha\in\ker(h)
then, since by
[
8
, Lemma 3.2.31]
the restriction of
\alpha
to each fibre of
\pi_{k-1}
is a local translation, we deduce that the map
x\mapsto\alpha(x)-x
is a constant element of
\ab_{k}
for every
x
in a given fibre. Thus
\alpha
induces a well-defined map
\alpha^{\prime}:\ns_{k-1}\to\ab_{k}
,
\pi_{k-1}(x)\mapsto\alpha(x)-x
. Conversely, given
\alpha^{\prime}:\ns_{k-1}\to\ab_{k}
we can define
\alpha:\ns\to\ns
,
x\mapsto x+\alpha^{\prime}(\pi_{k-1}(x))
, and we can ask when is
\alpha
in
\tran(\ns)
.

Lemma 10.5
.

Let
i<k
. Then
\alpha\in\ker(h)\cap\tran_{i}(\ns)
if and only if
\alpha^{\prime}\in\hom(\ns_{k-1},\cD_{k-i}(\ab_{k}))
.

Proof.

By
[
8
, Lemma 3.2.32]
we know that
\alpha\in\tran_{i}(\ns)
if and only if for every
\q\in\cu^{n}(\ns)
we have
\langle\q,\alpha\co\q\rangle_{i}\in\cu^{n+i}(\ns)
. If
\alpha\in\ker(h)\cap\tran_{i}(\ns)
then we can take the difference
\alpha\co\q-\q=\alpha^{\prime}\co\q^{\prime}
for
\q^{\prime}=\pi_{k-1}\co\q
, and by definition of degree-
k
extensions this difference must be a cube in
\cD_{k}(\ab_{k})
. But we also have
\langle\q,\alpha\co\q\rangle_{i}-\langle\q,\q\rangle_{i}\in\cu^{n+i}(\cD_{k}(\ab_{k}))
, and this equals
\langle 0,\alpha^{\prime}\co\q^{\prime}\rangle_{i}
. By
[
8
, Lemma 3.3.37]
, we have
\langle 0,\alpha^{\prime}\co\q^{\prime}\rangle_{i}\in\cu^{n+i}(\cD_{k}(\ab_{k}))
if and only if
\alpha^{\prime}\co\q^{\prime}\in\cu^{n}(\cD_{k-i}(\ab_{k})
. The converse follows similarly.
∎

The next main tool that we need, Lemma
10.7
below, is a type of rigidity result for morphisms between abelian torsors of the form
\cD_{k}(\ab)
. The proof will use the following basic fact.

Lemma 10.6
.

Let
\ab^{\prime}
be a
cfr
abelian group, and let
d_{2}
be the metric from Definition
6.4
. Then there exists
\eta>0
such that for every compact abelian group
\ab
and every non-constant continuous affine homomorphism
\phi:\ab\to\ab^{\prime}
, there exist
x,y\in\ab
such that
d_{2}(\phi(x),\phi(y))\geq\eta
.

Proof.

We can assume without loss that
\phi
is a homomorphism (not just an affine one). If
\ab^{\prime}
is a finite group then the claim is verified by any two points
x,y
such that
\phi(x)\neq\phi(y)
. We may therefore suppose that
\ab^{\prime}=F\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
with
n>0
and
F
a finite abelian group, and that for some
i\in[n]
the projection
\pi_{i}:\mathbb{T}^{n}\to\mathbb{T}
,
x\mapsto x_{i}
satisfies that
\pi_{i}\co\phi(\ab)
is a nontrivial subgroup of
\mathbb{T}
. Identifying
\mathbb{T}^{n}
with
[0,1)^{n}
, there is then
y\in\ab
such that
|\pi_{i}\co\phi(y)-0|_{\mathbb{T}}\geq 1/4
and so with
x=0_{\ab}
we have
d_{2}(\phi(x),\phi(y))\geq 1/4
.
∎

Lemma 10.7
.

Let
k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\ab,\ab^{\prime}
be compact abelian groups, and suppose that
\ab^{\prime}
has finite rank. Then there exists
\epsilon=\epsilon(\ell,\ab^{\prime})>0
such that if
\phi\in\hom(\cD_{k}(\ab),\cD_{\ell}(\ab^{\prime}))
satisfies
d_{2}(\phi(x),\phi(y))\leq\epsilon
for every
x,y\in\ab
then
\phi
is a constant function.

Proof.

Since
\hom(\cD_{k}(\ab),\cD_{\ell}(\ab^{\prime}))\subseteq\hom(\cD_{1}(\ab),\cD_{\ell}(\ab^{\prime}))
we can assume that
k=1
. Let
\phi
be an arbitrary non-constant morphism from
\cD_{1}(\ab)
to
\cD_{\ell}(\ab^{\prime})
. As mentioned in
[
8
, Example 2.2.13]
(recall also
[
8
, Definition 2.2.30]
), the map
\phi
is a polynomial map of degree at most
\ell
. Thus, if for every
t\in\ab
and every
f:\ab\to\ab^{\prime}
we denote by
\Delta_{t}f
the function
x\mapsto f(x+t)-f(x)
, then there is
i<\ell
and elements
t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{i}\in\ab
such that
\phi^{\prime}=\Delta_{t_{1}}\co\Delta_{t_{2}}\co\cdots\co\Delta_{t_{i}}\phi
is non-constant but
\Delta_{t}\phi^{\prime}
is constant for every
t\in\ab
. It follows that
\phi^{\prime}
is a non-constant continuous affine homomorphism from
\ab
to
\ab^{\prime}
, so by Lemma
10.6
there are
x,y\in\ab
with
d_{2}(\phi^{\prime}(x),\phi^{\prime}(y))\geq\eta(\ab^{\prime})>0
. This cannot hold if
\max_{x,y}d_{2}(\phi(x),\phi(y))
is too small, so this quantity must have a positive lower bound (depending only on
\ab^{\prime}
and
\ell
).
∎

Lemma
10.7
has the following consequence.

Corollary 10.8
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\ns
be a
k
-step compact nilspace, and let
\ab
be a
cfr
abelian group. Then there exists
\epsilon=\epsilon(\ell,\ab)>0
such that if
\phi\in\hom(\ns,\cD_{\ell}(\ab))
satisfies
d_{2}(\phi(x),\phi(y))\leq\epsilon
for every
x,y\in\ns
then
\phi
is constant. In particular, for any
x_{0}\in\ns
,
z_{0}\in\ab
, the set
Y=\{\phi\in\hom(\ns,\cD_{\ell}(\ab)):\phi(x_{0})=z_{0}\}
, equipped with the metric
d_{\infty}(\phi,\phi^{\prime})=\max_{x\in\ns}d_{2}(\phi(x),\phi^{\prime}(x))
, is discrete.

Proof.

Suppose that
d_{2}(\phi(x),\phi(y))<\epsilon
for every
x,y\in\ns
, where
\epsilon=\epsilon(\ell,\ab)
is the constant from Lemma
10.7
. We prove by induction on
k
that
\phi
is constant.

If
k=1
then
\ns
is abelian and the result follows from Lemma
10.7
. Suppose that the statement holds for
k-1
. Then Lemma
10.7
tells us that
\phi
is constant on the
\sim_{k-1}
classes of
\ns
. This means that
\phi
can be regarded as a function on
\cF_{k-1}(\ns)
, and so by our assumption
\phi
is constant.

To see the last sentence in the corollary, note that if
\phi,\phi^{\prime}\in Y
satisfy
d_{\infty}(\phi,\phi^{\prime})=d_{\infty}(\phi-\phi^{\prime},0)\leq\epsilon/2
, then by the triangle inequality
\phi-\phi^{\prime}
satisfies the premise of the previous sentence in the corollary, and then by that sentence and the definition of
Y
we deduce that
\phi-\phi^{\prime}
must be the constant
0
.
∎

With these results we can now give a useful description of the kernel of
h
.

Lemma 10.9
.

Let
\ns
be a
cfr

k
-step nilspace, and let
h:\tran(\ns)\to\tran(\ns_{k-1})
be the homomorphism from Lemma
10.3
. Then
\ker(h)
is isomorphic as a topological group to
F\times\ab_{k}
for some discrete group
F
. In particular
\ker(h)
is a Lie group.

Proof.

Let
x\in\ns
be arbitrary and let
F
be the stabilizer
\stab_{\ker(h)}(x)=\{\alpha\in\ker(h):\alpha(x)=x\}
. Let
\tau:\ab_{k}\to\ker(h)
be the map sending
z\in\ab_{k}
to the translation
\tau_{z}:x\mapsto x+z
, and note that
\tau
is an isomorphism of topological groups between
\ab_{k}
and
\tau(\ab_{k})\leq\ker(h)
. Now given any
\alpha\in\ker(h)
, letting
z=\alpha(x)-x
we have that
\alpha-\tau_{z}\in F
, so
\ker(h)=F\cdot\tau(\ab_{k})
. Moreover, since every
\alpha\in F
must in fact stabilize every point in the fibre of
x
(as the trivial local translation from this fibre to itself), it follows that
F\lhd\,\ker(h)
. We also have
\tau(\ab_{k})\lhd\,\ker(h)
. (In fact
\tau(\ab_{k})\subset Z(\ker(h))
.) Using Lemma
10.5
and Corollary
10.8
we see that
F
is discrete. It follows that
\ker(h)\cong F\times\ab_{k}
as claimed. Since
\ab_{k}
is a Lie group, the proof is complete.
∎

If
G
is a topological group then we denote the connected component of
\mathrm{id}_{G}
by
G^{0}
. This is a closed normal subgroup of
G
. It is also standard that
G
is a Lie group if and only if
G^{0}
is a Lie group.

Theorem 10.10
.

Let
\ns
be a
cfr

k
-step nilspace and let
i\in[k]
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

\tran_{i}(\ns)
is a Lie group,

(ii)

h\big(\tran_{i}(\ns)^{0}\big)=\tran_{i}(\ns_{k-1})^{0}
.

Proof.

We prove the statements by induction on
k
. The case
k=0
being trivial, let
k>0
and suppose that the statements hold for
k-1
.

To see statement
(i)
, note first that since
h
is a continuous homomorphism and
\tran_{i}(\ns)
is closed, we have that
h(\tran_{i}(\ns))
is a closed subgroup of
\tran_{i}(\ns_{k-1})
. By induction the latter is a Lie group, so by Cartan’s theorem
h(\tran_{i}(\ns))
is a Lie group
[
41
, Theorem 2.12.6]
. By Lemma
10.9
we have that
\ker(h)
is a Lie group, and so
H:=\tran_{i}(\ns)\cap\ker(h)
is a Lie group (being a closed subgroup of
\ker(h)
). On the other hand
h(\tran_{i}(\ns))
is isomorphic to
\tran_{i}(\ns)/H
as a topological group (see
[
3
, Theorem 1.2.6]
). Therefore
\tran_{i}(\ns)/H
must be a Lie group (this can be seen using
[
13
, Theorem 3.1]
). We can now deduce that
\tran_{i}(\ns)
is itself a Lie group, by
[
25
, Lemma A.3]
.

Next we show that

\tran_{i}(\ns_{k-1})^{0}\subseteq h(\tran_{i}(\ns)).

(23)

To see this we use that
\tran_{i}(\ns_{k-1})
is a Lie group and so every element
\alpha\in\tran_{i}(\ns_{k-1})^{0}
is connected to the identity by a continuous path
p:[0,1]\to\tran_{i}(\ns_{k-1})
with
p(0)=\mathrm{id}
and
p(1)=\alpha
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
\alpha_{i}=p((i-1)/n)^{-1}p(i/n)
for
i\in[n]
and let
\alpha=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}
.
Lemma
10.4
implies that if
n
is sufficiently large then for every
\alpha_{i}
there is
\beta_{i}\in\tran_{i}(\ns)
with
h(\beta_{i})=\alpha_{i}
. Let
\beta=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}
. Since
h
is a homomorphism we have
h(\beta)=\alpha
.

To obtain statement
(ii)
, we first show that

h(\tran_{i}(\ns)^{0})=h(\tran_{i}(\ns))^{0}.

(24)

On one hand we have
h(\tran_{i}(\ns)^{0})\subset h(\tran_{i}(\ns))^{0}
since
h
is continuous. On the other hand
h(\tran_{i}(\ns))^{0}
is open in
h(\tran_{i}(\ns))

and
h
is an open map
\tran_{i}(\ns)\to h(\tran_{i}(\ns))
(again by
[
3
, Theorem 1.2.6]
), so
h(\tran_{i}(\ns)^{0})
is open in
h(\tran_{i}(\ns))
. Since it is also closed, we have that both
h(\tran_{i}(\ns)^{0})
and its complement in
h(\tran_{i}(\ns))^{0}
are open, so this complement must be empty, since
h(\tran_{i}(\ns))^{0}
is connected, and (
24
) follows. Now in statement
(ii)
, the inclusion
h(\tran_{i}(\ns)^{0})\subset\tran_{i}(\ns_{k-1})^{0}
follows by continuity of
h
, and for the opposite inclusion, note that by (
23
) we have
\tran_{i}(\ns_{k-1})^{0}\subseteq h(\tran_{i}(\ns))^{0}
, so by (
24
) we have
\tran_{i}(\ns_{k-1})^{0}\subseteq h(\tran_{i}(\ns)^{0})
.
∎

Remark 10.11
.

It follows from statement
(ii)
above that
\tran_{i}(\ns)^{0}
is a topological extension of
\tran_{i}(\ns_{k-1})^{0}
by
\ker(h)\cap\tran_{i}(\ns)^{0}
, that is we have the short exact sequence

0\;\;\xrightarrow{\makebox[14.22636pt]{}}\;\;\ker(h)\cap\tran_{i}(\ns)^{0}\;\;\xrightarrow{\makebox[14.22636pt]{$\iota$}}\;\;\tran_{i}(\ns)^{0}\;\;\xrightarrow{\makebox[14.22636pt]{$h$}}\;\;\tran_{i}(\ns_{k-1})^{0}\;\;\xrightarrow{\makebox[14.22636pt]{}}\;\;0.

This gives another way to obtain statement
(i)
by induction given
(ii)
, using that a topological extension of a Lie group by a Lie group is again a Lie group (see
[
40
, §2.6]
).

Corollary 10.12
.

The Lie group
\tran(\ns)^{0}
acts transitively on the connected components of
\ns
.

Proof.

We argue by induction on
k
. For
k>0
suppose that
\tran(\ns_{k-1})^{0}
acts transitively on the connected components of
\ns_{k-1}
. Let
\tau
be the isomorphism
\ab_{k}\to\tau(\ab_{k})\leq\tran(\ns)
in the proof of Lemma
10.9
. Consider the group
\tau(\ab_{k})\cdot\tran(\ns)^{0}=\{\tau(z)\,\alpha:z\in\ab_{k},\,\alpha\in\tran(\ns)^{0}\}\leq\tran(\ns)
, and let
M
be any connected component of
\ns
. We claim that
\tau(\ab_{k})\cdot\tran(\ns)^{0}
acts transitively on
M
. Indeed, for any
x,y\in M
, by Theorem
10.10
(ii) and the induction hypothesis, there exists
\alpha\in\tran(\ns)^{0}
such that
\pi_{k-1}\co\alpha(x)=\pi_{k-1}(y)
. Combining this with the transitivity of
\ab_{k}
on the fibres of
\pi_{k-1}
we deduce that there exists
\alpha^{\prime}\in\tau(\ab_{k})\cdot\tran(\ns)^{0}
such that
\alpha^{\prime}(x)=y
, which proves our claim. It follows that
\tran(\ns)
acts transitively on
M
, and we can then deduce that
\tran(\ns)^{0}
also acts transitively on
M
by standard results (e.g.
[
23
, Chapter II, Theorem 3.2 and Proposition 4.3]
).
∎

Example 10.13
.

The main results of this section so far can be illustrated with the Heisenberg nilmanifold
H/\Gamma
, where
H=\begin{pmatrix}1&\mathbb{R}&\mathbb{R}\\[1.00006pt]
&1&\mathbb{R}\\[1.00006pt]
&&1\end{pmatrix}
,
\Gamma=\begin{pmatrix}1&\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{Z}\\[1.00006pt]
&1&\mathbb{Z}\\[1.00006pt]
&&1\end{pmatrix}
(recall
[
8
, Example 2.3.2]
). Let
\ns
denote
H/\Gamma
equipped with the cubes associated with the lower central series on
H
. We then have that
\ns
is a 2-step compact nilspace, with 1-step factor
\ns_{1}=\mathbb{T}^{2}
equipped with the standard degree-1 cubes, and we compute that

\tran_{1}(\ns)=\begin{pmatrix}1&\mathbb{R}&\mathbb{T}\\[1.00006pt]
&1&\mathbb{R}\\[1.00006pt]
&&1\end{pmatrix},\;\;\tran_{2}(\ns)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\mathbb{T}\\[1.00006pt]
&1&0\\[1.00006pt]
&&1\end{pmatrix}\cong\mathbb{T},\;\;\tran(\ns_{1})=\mathbb{T}^{2}.

We also check that
\ker(h)=\begin{pmatrix}1&\mathbb{Z}&\mathbb{T}\\[1.00006pt]
&1&\mathbb{Z}\\[1.00006pt]
&&1\end{pmatrix}\;\cong\;\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}
. Note that the structure groups of
\ns
are tori (
\ab_{2}=\mathbb{T}
,
\ab_{1}=\mathbb{T}^{2}
). Thus
\ns
is also an example of the type of connected
cfr
nilspace to which we turn next.

10.1
Toral nilspaces

Recall that a
cfr
abelian group
\ab
has torsion-free dual group if and only if
\ab\cong\mathbb{T}^{n}
for some
n\geq 0
.

Definition 10.14
.

We say that a
k
-step nilspace
\ns
is
torsion-free
if every structure group of
\ns
has torsion-free dual group. We call
\ns
a
toral nilspace
if it is
cfr
and torsion-free.

Thus a
k
-step compact nilspace is toral if and only if its structure groups are all tori. One of the reasons for treating these nilspaces in particular is that they occur naturally in the study of the regularizations of ultralimits of functions on abelian groups, which can be used to prove inverse theorems for the Gowers uniformity norms (see the characteristic-0 case of
[
39
, Definition 1.3]
).

Lemma 10.15
.

Every toral nilspace is connected.

Proof.

Recall the general fact that a fibre bundle is connected if the base space and the fibre are connected (see
[
38
, Part I, §2.12]
). Thus if
\ab
has finite rank and is connected then a continuous
\ab
-bundle over a connected space is connected. Now given a
k
-step toral nilspace
\ns
, with factors
\ns_{i}
,
i\in[k]
, a simple induction on
i
using this fact shows that
\ns
is connected.
∎

Remark 10.16
.

The converse of Lemma
10.15
is false. Consider for example the connected 2-step nilspace
\ns
consisting of the circle group
\mathbb{T}
with cube sets
\cu^{n}(G_{\bullet})
for the degree-2 filtration
G_{\bullet}
with
G_{0}=G_{1}=\mathbb{T}
and
G_{2}=\{0,1/2\}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
(viewing
\mathbb{T}
as
[0,1)
with addition mod 1). A straightforward calculation shows that
x\sim_{1}y
in
\ns
if and only if
x-y\in G_{2}
, whence the structure group
\ab_{2}
is the disconnected group
G_{2}
.

We can now establish the main result of this section.

Theorem 10.17
.

Let
\ns
be a
k
-step toral nilspace, let
G=\tran(\ns)^{0}
, let
G_{\bullet}
denote the degree-
k
filtration
(\tran_{i}(\ns)^{0})_{i\geq 0}
, and for an arbitrary fixed
x\in\ns
let
\Gamma=\stab_{G}(x)
. Then
\ns
is isomorphic as a compact nilspace to the nilmanifold
G/\Gamma
with cube sets
\cu^{n}(\ns)=(\cu^{n}(G_{\bullet})\cdot\Gamma^{\{0,1\}^{n}})/\Gamma^{\{0,1\}^{n}}
,
n\geq 0
.

Note that for the nilmanifold obtained here, every group in the filtration
G_{\bullet}
is connected. (The fact that
G_{\bullet}
here is indeed a filtration follows from the fact that if
H_{1},H_{2}
are closed subgroups of a topological group and one of these subgroups is connected, then
[H_{1},H_{2}]
is connected.)

Proof.

We argue by induction on
k
. For
k=1
, by Lemma
2.4
we have that
\ns
is the principal homogeneous space of a torus with cubes being the projections of the standard cubes on the torus, so the statement holds. Supposing then that the statement holds for
k-1
, we fix
x\in\ns
and let
\Gamma=\stab_{G}(x)
.

We first claim that
\Gamma
is discrete. To see this, note that
h(\Gamma)
is a subgroup of the stabilizer of
\pi_{k-1}(x)
in
\tran(\ns_{k-1})
, so by induction it is discrete. Then using that
h^{-1}(h(\Gamma))
is a union of cosets of
\ker(h)
, we have that it suffices to show that
\Gamma\cap\ker(h)
is discrete. But this follows from Lemma
10.9
, since no non-trivial element of
\tau(\ab_{k})
stabilizes
x
.

By Corollary
10.12
the Lie group
\tran(\ns)^{0}
acts transitively on the connected space
\ns
, and it follows that
\ns
is homeomorphic to the coset space
G/\Gamma
(see
[
23
, Chapter II, Theorem 3.2]
). Therefore
\Gamma
is cocompact.

It now only remains to determine the cubes on
\ns
. Recall from
[
8
, Definition 3.2.38]
that two cubes
\q_{1},\q_{2}\in\cu^{n}(\ns)
are said to be translation equivalent if there is an element
\q\in\cu^{n}(G_{\bullet})
such that
\q_{2}(v)=\q(v)\cdot\q_{1}(v)
. We claim that for every cube
\q\in\cu^{n}(\ns)
there is a cube
\q^{\prime}\in\cu^{n}(\ns)
that is translation equivalent to the constant
x
cube and such that
\pi_{k-1}\co\q=\pi_{k-1}\co\q^{\prime}
. Indeed, given
\q\in\cu^{n}(\ns)
, we have
\pi_{k-1}\co\q\in\cu^{n}(\ns_{k-1})
, and by induction the latter cube is translation equivalent to the cube with constant value
x^{\prime}=\pi_{k-1}(x)
, i.e. it is of the form
\tilde{\q}\cdot x^{\prime}
for some cube
\tilde{\q}
on the group
\tran(\ns_{k-1})^{0}
with the filtration
\big(\tran_{i}(\ns_{k-1})^{0}\big)_{i\geq 0}
. Now by the unique factorization result for these cubes
[
8
, Lemma 2.2.5]
, we have
\tilde{\q}={\tilde{g}_{0}}^{F_{0}}\cdots{\tilde{g}_{2^{n}-1}}^{F_{2^{n}-1}}
where
\tilde{g}_{j}\in\tran_{\codim(F_{j})}(\ns_{k-1})^{0}
. Theorem
10.10
(ii) then tells us that for each
j\in[0,2^{n})
there is
g_{j}\in\tran_{\codim(F_{j})}(\ns)^{0}
such that
h(g_{j})=\tilde{g}_{j}
.
Let
\q^{*}
be the cube in
\cu^{n}(\tran(\ns)^{0})
defined by
\q^{*}={g_{0}}^{F_{0}}\cdots{g_{2^{n}-1}}^{F_{2^{n}-1}}
. Let
\q^{\prime}=\q^{*}\cdot x
. This is in
\cu^{n}(\ns)
, and is translation equivalent to the constant
x
cube. Moreover, by construction we have

\pi_{k-1}\co\q^{\prime}=\pi_{k-1}(\q^{*}\cdot x)=\Big(\prod_{j}h(g_{j})^{F_{j}}\Big)\cdot x^{\prime}=\Big(\prod_{j}\tilde{g}_{j}^{F_{j}}\Big)\cdot x^{\prime}=\tilde{\q}\cdot x^{\prime}=\pi_{k-1}\co\q.

This proves our claim.

It follows from
[
8
, Theorem 3.2.19]
and the definition of degree-
k
bundles (in particular
[
8
, (3.5)]
) that
\q-\q^{\prime}\in\cu^{n}(\cD_{k}(\ab_{k}))
. But then using translations from
\tau(\ab_{k})
we can correct
\q^{\prime}
further to obtain
\q
, thus showing that
\q
is itself a translation cube with translations from
\tran(\ns)^{0}
. (Such a correction procedure has been used in previous arguments, see for instance the proof of
[
8
, Lemma 3.2.25]
.)

We have thus shown that
\cu^{n}(\ns)\subset(\cu^{n}(G_{\bullet})\cdot\Gamma^{\{0,1\}^{n}})/\Gamma^{\{0,1\}^{n}}
. The opposite inclusion is clear, by definition of the groups
\tran_{i}(\ns)
.
∎

Remark 10.18
.

In Theorem
10.17
we can replace the groups
G_{i}
by
simply connected
Lie groups
\tilde{G}_{i}
while conserving the nilspace isomorphism with
\ns
. More precisely, let
\tilde{G}
be the universal covering group of the connected Lie group
G
, with covering homomorphism
p:\tilde{G}\to G
(see
[
35
, §47, Theorem 61]
). Let
\tilde{\Gamma}=p^{-1}(\Gamma)
, and for each
i\geq 0
, let
\tilde{G}_{i}
be the identity component of the closed (hence Lie) subgroup
p^{-1}(G_{i})
of
\tilde{G}
. Then
\tilde{G}_{\bullet}=(\tilde{G}_{i})_{i\geq 0}
is a degree-
k
filtration of closed connected (hence simply-connected
[
11
, Corollary 1.2.2]
) Lie subgroups of
\tilde{G}
. It can be checked in a straightforward way that
\tilde{G}/\tilde{\Gamma}
with the cubes determined by
\tilde{G}_{\bullet}
is isomorphic to
\ns
as a compact nilspace.

We know by a theorem of Palais and Stewart that
\ns
is the total space of a principal torus bundle over a torus if and only if it is a 2-step nilmanifold (see
[
34
, Theorem 1]
). Theorem
10.17
can be viewed as a generalization of this result for arbitrary step, in the category of compact nilspaces (in particular, making use of the assumed cube structure).

One may wonder whether, by adding some simple assumption in Theorem
10.17
, the description of the filtered nilmanifold can then be made even more precise. For instance, having seen Example
10.13
, we may seek some simple additional condition under which Theorem
10.17
yields the Heisenberg nilmanifold
H/\Gamma
. In this direction we have the following result.

Proposition 10.19
.

Let
\ns
be a 2-step toral nilspace of dimension 3. Then
\ns
is isomorphic to one of the following compact nilspaces:

(i)

The torus
\mathbb{R}^{3}/\mathbb{Z}^{3}
with cube structure determined by a filtration of degree at most 2 on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

(ii)

The Heisenberg nilmanifold
H/\Gamma
with cube structure determined by the lower central series on
H
.

Here the dimension is that of
\ns
when viewed as a manifold (a view which we noted after Lemma
6.3
).

Proof.

Applying Theorem
10.17
and Remark
10.18
, we have that
\ns
is isomorphic as a compact nilspace to
G/\Gamma
for a simply-connected Lie group
G
with a filtration
G_{\bullet}
of degree at most 2 consisting of closed (therefore Lie) simply-connected groups
G_{i}\leq G
, and a discrete cocompact subgroup
\Gamma\leq G
. The assumption that
\ns
has dimension 3 implies that
G
is 3-dimensional.

If
\ns
is a 1-step nilspace then
\ns
is isomorphic to the 3-torus
\mathbb{R}^{3}/\mathbb{Z}^{3}
in case
(i)
, with the standard degree-1 cube structure.

If
\ns
is not 1-step then, if
G
is abelian, we have again that
\ns
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{3}/\mathbb{Z}^{3}
but now the cube structure is given by a degree-2 filtration on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
(for example it could be the filtration determining the degree-2 structure on
\mathbb{R}^{3}/\mathbb{Z}^{3}
defined in
[
8
, Definition 2.2.30]
). The remaining possibility is that
G
is non-abelian 2-step nilpotent. The simply-connected Lie group
G
is uniquely determined by its Lie algebra, which is a real non-abelian, 2-step nilpotent Lie algebra of dimension 3. It follows from the Bianchi classification
[
4
]
that the only such Lie algebra is the 3-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra, and so
G
is the Heisenberg group. Finally, we have that the only degree-2 filtration of closed connected subgroups of
G
is the lower central series.
∎

To close this section, we prove additional pleasant properties of toral nilspaces. In particular, property
(iii)
below gives a simple relation between the translation groups and the structure groups.

Proposition 10.20
.

Let
\ns
be a
k
-step toral nilspace. For each
j\in[k]
let
h_{j-1,j}
denote the continuous surjective homomorphism
\tran(\ns_{j})^{0}\to\tran(\ns_{j-1})^{0}
defined as in Lemma
10.3
, and for each
i\in[k]
let
h_{i}=h_{i,i+1}\co\cdots\co h_{k-1,k}:\tran(\ns)^{0}\to\tran(\ns_{i})^{0}
. Fix an arbitrary element
x\in\ns
, and for each
i\in[k]
let
\Gamma_{i}=\tran_{i}(\ns)^{0}\cap\stab_{\tran(\ns)^{0}}(x)
. Then for each
i
, the following statements hold:

(i)

\tran_{i}(\ns)^{0}
acts transitively on each fibre of
\pi_{i-1}:\ns\to\ns_{i-1}
.

(ii)

\ker(h_{i})=\Gamma_{i}\cdot\tran_{i+1}(\ns)^{0}
.

(iii)

\tran_{i}(\ns)^{0}\;/\;\big(\Gamma_{i}\cdot\tran_{i+1}(\ns)^{0}\big)\;\cong\;\ab_{i}
.

Proof.

We prove
(i)
by downward induction on
i
. For the case
i=k
, since each fibre of
\pi_{k-1}
is a homogeneous space of
\ab_{k}
, it suffices to show that
\tran_{k}(\ns)^{0}=\tau(\ab_{k})
. This is immediate from the following equality, which actually holds for a general nilspace and is established in
[
8
, Lemma 3.2.37]
:

\tran_{k}(\ns)=\tau(\ab_{k}).

(25)

Suppose now that
i\leq k-1
and that the statement holds for
i+1
, and note that any fibre of
\pi_{i-1}=\pi_{i-1,i}\co\pi_{i}
has the form
\pi_{i}^{-1}(\pi_{i-1,i}^{-1}(y))
for some
y\in\ns_{i-1}
. Given
w,w^{\prime}
in such a fibre, we have
\pi_{i}(w),\pi_{i}(w^{\prime})\in\pi_{i-1,i}^{-1}(y)
. Since
h_{i}(\tran_{i}(\ns)^{0})=\tran_{i}(\ns_{i})^{0}=\tau(\ab_{i})
, we have that this acts transitively on
\pi_{i-1,i}^{-1}(y)
and so there is
\alpha\in\tran_{i}(\ns)^{0}
such that
\pi_{i}(\alpha(w))=\pi_{i}(w^{\prime})
. Then, since
\tran_{i}(\ns)^{0}
contains
\tran_{i+1}(\ns)^{0}
, by induction it acts transitively on each
\pi_{i}
-fibre and so there exists
\alpha^{\prime}\in\tran_{i}(\ns)^{0}
such that
\alpha^{\prime}\alpha(w)=w^{\prime}
. This proves statement
(i)
.

To see statement
(ii)
, note that
\tran_{i+1}(\ns)^{0}\leq\ker(h_{i})
, since
h_{i}
sends
\tran_{i+1}(\ns)^{0}
into the trivial group
\tran_{i+1}(\ns_{i})^{0}
. We also have that
\Gamma_{i}\subset\ker(h_{i})
, because by (
25
) we have
h_{i}(\Gamma_{i})\subset\tau(\ab_{i})
but also each element of
h_{i}(\Gamma_{i})
fixes
\pi_{i}(x)
, so must be the trivial translation in
\tau(\ab_{i})
. It follows that
\Gamma_{i}\cdot\tran_{i+1}(\ns)^{0}\subset\ker(h_{i})
. To see the opposite inclusion, note that for
\alpha\in\ker(h_{i})
we have from the definitions that
\pi_{i}(\alpha(x))=h_{i}(\alpha)(\pi_{i}(x))=\pi_{i}(x)
. Therefore, by statement
(i)
there exists
\alpha^{\prime}\in\tran_{i+1}(\ns)^{0}
such that
\alpha^{\prime}\alpha(x)=x
, so
\alpha^{\prime}\alpha\in\Gamma_{i}
, and so
\alpha\in\tran_{i+1}(\ns)^{0}\cdot\Gamma_{i}
.

Statement
(iii)
now follows from
(ii)
, the first isomorphism theorem for
h_{i}
, and (
25
) for
k=i
.
∎

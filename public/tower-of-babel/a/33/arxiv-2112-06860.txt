Test Vectors for Archimedean Period Integrals
Peter Humphries and Yeongseong Jo

Abstract
. We study period integrals involving Whittaker functions associated to generic irreducible Casselman–Wallach representations of GL n ​ ( F ) \mathrm{GL}_{n}(F) , where F F is an archimedean local field. Via the archimedean theory of newforms for GL n \mathrm{GL}_{n} developed by the first author, we prove that newforms are weak test vectors for several period integrals, including the GL n × GL n \mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n} Rankin–Selberg integral, the Flicker integral, and the Bump–Friedberg integral. By taking special values of these period integrals, we deduce that newforms are weak test vectors for Rankin–Selberg periods, Flicker–Rallis periods, and Friedberg–Jacquet periods. These results parallel analogous results in the nonarchimedean setting proven by the second author, which use the nonarchimedean theory of newforms for GL n \mathrm{GL}_{n} developed by Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro, and Shalika. By combining these archimedean and nonarchimedean results, we prove the existence of weak test vectors for certain global period integrals of automorphic forms.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Test Vectors for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}
Rankin–Selberg Integrals

A period integral of automorphic forms over a number field
F
is said to be
Eulerian
if it factorises as a product of period integrals over local fields. A quintessential example is the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}
Rankin–Selberg integral

\int\limits_{\mathrm{Z}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}\varphi_{\pi}(g)\varphi_{\sigma}(g)E(g,s;\Phi,\omega_{\pi}\omega_{\sigma})\,dg

involving two automorphic forms
\varphi_{\pi}
and
\varphi_{\sigma}
lying in two automorphic representations
\pi
and
\sigma
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
as well as a distinguished Eisenstein series
E(g,s;\Phi,\omega_{\pi}\omega_{\sigma})
associated to a Schwartz–Bruhat function
\Phi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{n})
and a product
\omega_{\pi}\omega_{\sigma}
of central characters. If
\varphi_{\pi}
,
\varphi_{\sigma}
, and
\Phi
are pure tensors, then by unfolding, this global period integral factorises as a product over all places
v
of
F
of local
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}
Rankin–Selberg integrals

\Psi(s,W_{\pi_{v}},W_{\sigma_{v}},\Phi_{v})\coloneqq\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{n}(F_{v})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F_{v})}W_{\pi_{v}}(g_{v})W_{\sigma_{v}}(g_{v})\Phi_{v}(e_{n}g_{v})\left|\det g_{v}\right|_{v}^{s}\,dg_{v},

where
W_{\pi_{v}}
and
W_{\sigma_{v}}
are the local Whittaker functions associated to
\varphi_{\pi}
and
\varphi_{\sigma}
.

The local period integrals
\Psi(s,W_{\pi_{v}},W_{\sigma_{v}},\Phi_{v})
represent the local
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}
Rankin–Selberg
L
-function
L(s,\pi_{v}\times\sigma_{v})
, where
\pi_{v}
and
\sigma_{v}
are the generic irreducible admissible smooth representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F_{v})
occurring in the tensor product decompositions of
\pi
and
\sigma
. More precisely, if
v
is a nonarchimedean place with residue field of order
q
, then the quotient
\Psi(s,W_{\pi_{v}},W_{\sigma_{v}},\Phi_{v})/L(s,\pi_{v}\times\sigma_{v})
is a polynomial in
q^{s}
and
q^{-s}
, and in particular is entire. If
v
is an archimedean place, then the quotient
\Psi(s,W_{\pi_{v}},W_{\sigma_{v}},\Phi_{v})/L(s,\pi_{v}\times\sigma_{v})
is entire and of finite order in vertical strips.

While the quotient
\Psi(s,W_{\pi_{v}},W_{\sigma_{v}},\Phi_{v})/L(s,\pi_{v}\times\sigma_{v})
is always entire regardless of the choice of Whittaker functions
W_{\pi_{v}}
and
W_{\sigma_{v}}
and Schwartz–Bruhat function
\Phi_{v}
, for many applications, one requires something stronger, namely that for particular choices of
W_{\pi_{v}}
,
W_{\sigma_{v}}
, and
\Phi_{v}
, this quotient be nicely behaved — in particular, nonvanishing apart from a prescribed collection of values of
s\in\mathbb{C}
. When the representations
\pi_{v}
and
\sigma_{v}
are both unramified, there exists an explicit Schwartz–Bruhat function
\Phi_{v}
for which this quotient is
exactly
equal to
1
when
W_{\pi_{v}}
and
W_{\sigma_{v}}
are chosen to be
spherical
Whittaker functions. This motivates the following problem.

Strong Test Vector Problem
.

Given generic irreducible admissible smooth representations
\pi_{v}
and
\sigma_{v}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F_{v})

\pi_{v}

for the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}

When

and
\sigma_{v}
by Kim
[
40
, Chapters 4 and 5]

•

When

[
35
, Theorem 7.2]
, S-W. Zhang
[

When at least one of the two representations

We focus on a weaker yet more tractable problem, where we are satisfied with finding a triple
(W_{\pi_{v}},W_{\sigma_{v}},\Phi_{v})
for which
\Psi(s,W_{\pi_{v}},W_{\sigma_{v}},\Phi_{v})
is an explicit polynomial multiple of

\pi_{v}
and
\sigma_{v}

unramified
representations

\sigma_{v,\mathrm{ur}}

.

Given generic irreducible admissible smooth representations
\pi_{v}
and
\sigma_{v}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F_{v})

W_{\pi_{v}}
and
W_{\sigma_{v}}
in the Whittaker models of
\pi_{v}
and
\sigma_{v}
and a Schwartz–Bruhat function
\Phi_{v}\in\mathscr{S}(F_{v}^{n})

\Psi(s,W_{\pi_{v}},W_{\sigma_{v}},\Phi_{v})=L(s,\pi_{v,\mathrm{ur}}\times\sigma_{v,\mathrm{ur}}).

\sigma_{v}

1.3.
Comparisons between Archimedean and Nonarchimedean Results

Our results on test vectors for archimedean period integrals parallel analogous results of the second author
[
37
]
in the nonarchimedean setting. The strategy of proof in both settings is similar: via the Iwasawa decomposition and the fact that Whittaker newforms transform on the right under the maximal compact subgroup
K_{v}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F_{v})
in a prescribed manner, these integrals can be reduced to integrals over a diagonal torus
\mathrm{A}_{n}(F_{v})
.

When
F_{v}
is nonarchimedean, the behaviour of the Whittaker newform when restricted to the diagonal torus has a closed form via the Shintani–Casselman–Shalika formula
[
9
,
50
]
together with work of Matringe
[
42
]
and Miyauchi
[
46
]
. This allows one to
directly
evaluate the period integrals of interest.

When
F_{v}
is archimedean, on the other hand, no such closed form exists for the Whittaker newform when restricted to the diagonal torus. Instead, there exists a
propagation formula
due to the first author
[
23
, Lemmata 9.8 and 9.17]
that gives a recursive formula for this Whittaker newform in terms of a Whittaker newform associated to a representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F_{v})
; for unramified representations, such an identity is due independently to Gerasimov, Lebedev, and Oblezin
[
19
, Proposition 4.1]
and to Ishii and Stade
[
27
, Proposition 2.1]
. In this way, the period integrals of interest can be evaluated via
induction
. This approach has been previously used to successfully solve the strong test vector problem for the unramified
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}
Rankin–Selberg integral
[
53
, Theorem 1.1]
and the unramified Bump–Friedberg integral
[
52
, Theorem 3.3]
. For the modified
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}
and
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}
Rankin–Selberg integrals introduced by Sakellaridis and for the Flicker integral, our results are new even for the unramified case.

Known results for weak test vectors for nonarchimedean period integrals encompass more than just the analogues of the archimedean results listed in
Section
1.2
. Weak test vectors have been determined for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{m}
Rankin–Selberg integrals with
n>m

[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
, for Jacquet–Shalika integrals
[
45
, Theorem 1.1]
, and for Bump–Ginzburg integrals
[
37
, Theorem 6.3]
. In turn, these give weak test vectors for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{m}
Rankin–Selberg periods, Jacquet–Shalika periods, and Bump–Ginzburg periods.

In the archimedean setting, on the other hand, we do
not
expect to be able to find such weak test vectors for these various period integrals, with the exception of the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}
Rankin–Selberg integral, if we enforce the condition that these test vectors be right
K_{v}
-finite. Indeed, even if all representations are unramified and all Whittaker functions are spherical, it is widely believed that these period integrals are
not
a nonzero polynomial multiple of the
L
-function associated to such a period integral
[
8
, §2.6]
. A prototypical example of this phenomenon is the identity for the unramified
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n-2}
archimedean Rankin–Selberg integral due to Ishii and Stade
[
27
, Theorem 3.2]
, namely

\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{n-2}(\mathbb{R})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n-2}(\mathbb{R})}W_{\pi_{\infty}}\begin{pmatrix}g_{\infty}&0\\
0&1_{2}\end{pmatrix}W_{\sigma_{\infty}}(g_{\infty})\left|\det g_{\infty}\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{s-1}\,dg_{\infty}\\
=L(s,\pi_{\infty}\times\sigma_{\infty})\frac{1}{4\pi i}\int_{\sigma-i\infty}^{\sigma+i\infty}\frac{L\left(w,\widetilde{\pi_{\infty}}\right)}{L(s+w,\sigma_{\infty})}\,dw.

2.
Preliminaries

From here on, we work over an archimedean local field. Since all that follows is local, we drop the usage of the subscript
v
. We list below some standard facts on the representation theory of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
with
F
archimedean. Much of this is well-known and appears elsewhere in the literature; see in particular
[
23
, §2]
.

2.1.
Groups and Measures

2.1.1.
Local Fields

Let
F
be an archimedean local field, so that
F
is either
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
. We denote by
|\cdot|_{F}
the absolute value on
F
, which we normalise such that

|x|_{F}=\begin{cases}\max\{x,-x\}&if $F=\mathbb{R}$,\\
x\overline{x}&if $F=\mathbb{C}$.\end{cases}

When the local field is clear from the context, we omit the subscript
F
in our notation and simply write
|\cdot|=|\cdot|_{F}
. We also let
\|\cdot\|\coloneqq|\cdot|^{1/2}_{\mathbb{C}}
denote the standard module on
\mathbb{C}
.

We fix a nontrivial additive character
\psi_{F}=\psi
of
F
. For
F=\mathbb{R}
, we choose
\psi(x)\coloneqq\exp(2\pi ix)
, while for
F=\mathbb{C}
, we choose
\psi(x)\coloneqq\exp(2\pi i(x+\overline{x}))
; in
Section
6
, we will also work with a slightly different nontrivial additive character
\psi_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}}
of
\mathbb{C}
that is trivial when restricted to
\mathbb{R}
. We normalise the Haar measure
dx
on
F
so that it is self-dual with respect to
\psi
. For
F=\mathbb{R}
,
dx
is simply the Lebesgue measure, whereas for
F=\mathbb{C}
,
dx
is twice the Lebesgue measure. The multiplicative Haar measure
d^{\times}x
on
F^{\times}
is
\zeta_{F}(1)|x|^{-1}dx
, where

\zeta_{F}(s)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\pi^{-\frac{s}{2}}\Gamma\left(\dfrac{s}{2}\right)&if $F=\mathbb{R}$,\\
2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)&if $F=\mathbb{C}$.\end{cases}

2.1.2.
Groups and Haar Measures

We write
1_{n}
to denote the
n\times n
identity matrix. Let
\{e_{i}.:1\leq i\leq n\}
be the standard row basis of
F^{n}
. Let
\mathrm{P}(F)=\mathrm{P}_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})}(F)
denote the standard upper parabolic subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
of type
(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r})
with
n=n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{r}
. This has the Levi decomposition
\mathrm{P}(F)=\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{P}}(F)\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{P}}(F)
, where the block-diagonal Levi subgroup
\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{P}}(F)
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{GL}_{n_{1}}(F)\times\cdots\times\mathrm{GL}_{n_{r}}(F)
, while the unipotent radical
\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{P}}(F)
of
\mathrm{P}(F)
consists of upper triangular matrices with block-diagonal entries
(1_{n_{1}},\ldots,1_{n_{r}})
. When
\mathrm{P}(F)
is the standard Borel (and minimal parabolic) subgroup
\mathrm{P}_{(1,\ldots,1)}(F)\eqqcolon\mathrm{B}_{n}(F)
of upper triangular matrices, we write
\mathrm{N}_{\mathrm{P}}(F)\eqqcolon\mathrm{N}_{n}(F)\cong F^{n(n-1)/2}
, the subgroup of unipotent upper triangular matrices, and
\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{P}}(F)\eqqcolon\mathrm{A}_{n}(F)\cong(F^{\times})^{n}
, the subgroup of diagonal matrices. We write
\mathrm{Z}_{n}(F)\cong F^{\times}
to denote to the centre consisting of scalar matrices. We define
\mathrm{P}_{n}(F)
, the mirabolic subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
, given by

\mathrm{P}_{n}(F)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}h&\prescript{t}{}{x}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}:h\in\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F),\ x\in F^{n-1}\right\}.

The modulus character of a parabolic subgroup
\mathrm{P}(F)=\mathrm{P}_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})}(F)
is

\delta_{\mathrm{P}(F)}(m)=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left|\det m_{j}\right|^{n-2(n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{j-1})-n_{j}}

for any
m=\blockdiag(m_{1},\ldots,m_{r})\in\mathrm{M}_{\mathrm{P}}(F)
. We let
K_{n}
denote the maximal compact subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
, unique up to conjugacy, so that

K_{n}=\begin{cases}\mathrm{O}(n)&if $F=\mathbb{R}$,\\
\mathrm{U}(n)&if $F=\mathbb{C}$.\end{cases}

We have the Iwasawa decomposition
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)=\mathrm{N}_{n}(F)\mathrm{A}_{n}(F)K_{n}
. We normalise the Haar measure
dg
on
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)\ni g
via the Iwasawa decomposition
g=uak
with
u\in\mathrm{N}_{n}(F)
,
a\in\mathrm{A}_{n}(F)
, and
k\in K_{n}
, so that

dg=\delta^{-1}_{\mathrm{B}_{n}(F)}(a)\,du\,d^{\times}a\,dk.

Here
du=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}du_{i,j}
, where
du_{i,j}
is the Haar measure on
F
, while
d^{\times}a=\prod_{j=1}^{n}d^{\times}a_{j}
, where
d^{\times}a_{j}
is the Haar measure on
F^{\times}
, and
dk
is the probability Haar measure on
K_{n}
. The modulus character of
\mathrm{B}_{n}(F)
is simply
\delta_{\mathrm{B}_{n}(F)}(a)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}|a_{j}|^{n-2j+1}
.

The Iwasawa decomposition also gives rise to the Haar measure on
\mathrm{N}_{n}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
via
dg=\delta_{B_{n}(F)}^{-1}(a)\,d^{\times}a\,dk
for
g=ak
with

2.2.
Representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)

2.2.1.
Isobaric Sums

Given representations

(\pi(h)\cdot f)(g)\coloneqq f(gh)

for any

2.2.2.
Essential Square-Integrable Representations

Essential square-integrable representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
exist only for
n=1
. An essentially square-integrable representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{1}(\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}^{\times}
must be a character of the form
\pi(x)=e^{i\kappa\arg(x)}|x|^{t}_{\mathbb{C}}
for some
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}
and
t\in\mathbb{C}
, where
e^{i\arg(x)}\coloneqq x/\|x\|

Essential square-integrable representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
exist only for
n\in\{1,2\}
. An essentially square-integrable representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{1}(\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}^{\times}
must be a character of the form
\pi(x)=\sgn(x)^{\kappa}|x|^{t}_{\mathbb{R}}
for some
\kappa\in\{0,1\}
and
t\in\mathbb{C}
, where
\sgn(x)\coloneqq x/\|x\|
.
We view
\mathrm{GL}_{1}(\mathbb{C})
as a subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
via the identification
a+ib\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
-b&a\end{pmatrix}
. For
\kappa\neq 0
, the essential discrete series representation of weight
\|\kappa\|+1
,

D_{\|\kappa\|+1}\otimes\left|\det\right|^{t}_{\mathbb{R}}\coloneqq\Ind^{\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})}_{\mathrm{GL}_{1}(\mathbb{C})}e^{i\kappa\arg}|\cdot|^{t}_{\mathbb{C}}\cong\Ind^{\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})}_{\mathrm{GL}_{1}(\mathbb{C})}e^{-i\kappa\arg}|\cdot|^{t}_{\mathbb{C}}

is essentially square-integrable. Every essentially square-integrable representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is of the form
\pi=D_{\kappa}\otimes\left|\det\right|^{t}_{\mathbb{R}}
for some integer
\kappa\geq 2
and
t\in\mathbb{C}
.

2.2.3.
Induced Representations of Whittaker and Langlands Types

A representation
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
is said to be an
induced representation of Whittaker type
if it is the isobaric sum of
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\ldots,\pi_{r}
and each
\pi_{j}
is essentially square-integrable. Such a representation is an admissible smooth Fréchet representation of moderate growth and of finite length. Induced representations of Whittaker type are Casselman–Wallach representations
[
10
,
55
]
, which is to say admissible smooth Fréchet representations of moderate growth and finite length. In addition, if each
\pi_{j}
is of the form
\sigma_{j}\otimes\left|\det\right|^{t_{j}}
, where
\sigma_{j}
is irreducible, unitary, square-integrable, and
\Re(t_{1})\geq\Re(t_{2})\geq\cdots\geq\Re(t_{r})
, then
\pi
is said to be an
induced representation of Langlands type
.

Induced representations of Whittaker and Langlands type need not be irreducible. Nonetheless, every generic irreducible Casselman–Wallach representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
is isomorphic to some (necessarily irreducible) induced representation of Langlands type. For this reason, we will work more generally with induced representations of Langlands type, since this encompasses generic irreducible Casselman–Wallach representations.

A
spherical induced representation of Whittaker type
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
is a representation of the form
\pi=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigoplus}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}}\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n}\pi_{j}
with each
\pi_{j}
an unramified character of
F^{\times}
, namely a character of the form
\pi_{j}=|\cdot|^{t_{j}}
with
t_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
. Such a representation has a
K_{n}
-fixed vector, which is unique up to scalar multiplication; this is called the
spherical vector
.

2.2.4.
The Whittaker Model

Let
(\pi,V_{\pi})
be an induced representation of Whittaker type. We let
\psi_{n}
denote an additive character of
\mathrm{N}_{n}(F)
defined by

\psi_{n}(u)\coloneqq\psi\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}u_{j,j+1}\right)

for all
u\in\mathrm{N}_{n}(F)
. A
Whittaker functional

\Lambda:V_{\pi}\to\mathbb{C}
is a continuous linear functional that satisfies

\Lambda(\pi(u)\cdot v)=\psi_{n}(u)\Lambda(v)

for all
v\in V_{\pi}
and
u\in\mathrm{N}_{n}(F)
. If
\pi
is additionally irreducible, then the space
{\Hom}_{\mathrm{N}_{n}(F)}(\pi,\psi)
of Whittaker functionals of
\pi
is at most one-dimensional. If the space is one-dimensional, it admits a unique functional up to scalar multiplication, and the representation
\pi
is said to be
generic
.

Let
\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
denote the
Whittaker model
of
\pi
, which is the image of
V_{\pi}
under the map
v\mapsto\Lambda(\pi(\cdot)\cdot v)
. The Whittaker model
\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
consists of Whittaker functions
W:\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)\to\mathbb{C}
of the form
W(g)\coloneqq\Lambda(\pi(g)\cdot v)
. Every induced representation of Langlands type is generic and isomorphic to its unique Whittaker model
\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
. Although an induced representation of Whittaker type
\pi
affords a one-dimensional space of Whittaker functionals, the map
v\mapsto\Lambda(\pi(\cdot)\cdot v)
need not to be injective, so the Whittaker model may only be a model of a quotient of
\pi
.

Let
\pi=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigoplus}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}}\displaylimits_{j=1}^{r}\pi_{j}
be an induced representation of Whittaker type of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
, so that each
\pi_{j}
is of the form
e^{i\kappa_{j}\arg}|\cdot|^{t_{j}}
if
F=\mathbb{C}
or either
\sgn^{\kappa_{j}}|\cdot|^{t_{j}}
or
D_{\kappa_{j}}\otimes\left|\det\right|^{t_{j}}
if
F=\mathbb{R}
. The induced model of
\pi
consists of smooth functions
f:\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)\to\bigotimes_{j=1}^{r}V_{\pi_{j}}
. Each

may itself be identified with a space of smooth functions from
\mathrm{GL}_{n_{j}}(F)
to
\mathbb{C}

[
23

1_{n_{j}}
for each

of the induced model

of

as a smooth function from
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
to
\mathbb{C}

[
23
, Corollary 8.2]
.

Given

, we define the
Jacquet integral

W_{f}(g)\coloneqq\int_{\mathrm{N}_{n}(F)}f(w_{n}ug)\overline{\psi_{n}}(u)\,du,

where
w_{n}\coloneqq\antidiag(1,\ldots,1)
denotes the long Weyl element in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
. This integral converges absolutely if

and defines a Whittaker function

3.
Archimedean Newform Theory

We now survey the theory of newforms for induced representations of Whittaker type of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
introduced by the first author
[
23
]
. While newforms over nonarchimedean fields are usually defined in terms of vectors fixed under certain congruence subgroups, newforms over archimedean fields are instead defined in terms of vectors lying in distinguished
K_{n}
-types. We first recall some properties of representations of
K_{n}
, as well as properties of distinguished models of such representations.

3.1.
Representation Theory of
K_{n}

The equivalence classes of finite-dimensional irreducible representations of the orthogonal group

\mathrm{O}(n)\coloneqq\{k\in\Mat_{n\times n}(\mathbb{R}):k\prescript{t}{}{k}=1_{n}\}

are parametrised by the set of highest weights, which may be identified with
n
-tuples of nonnegative integers of the form

\mu=\{\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{m},\underbrace{\eta,\ldots,\eta}_{\text{$n-2m$ times}},\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{\text{$m$ times}}\}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},

where
m\in\{0,\ldots,\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor\}
,
\mu_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{m}\geq 1
, and
\eta\in\{0,1\}
.

Similarly, the equivalence class of finite-dimensional irreducible representations of the unitary group

\mathrm{U}(n)\coloneqq\{k\in\Mat_{n\times n}(\mathbb{C}):k\prescript{t}{}{\overline{k}}=1_{n}\}

are parametrised by the set of highest weights, which may be identified with
n
-tuples of integers
\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
that are nonincreasing, so that
\mu_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{n}
.

In both settings, to each
\tau\in\widehat{K_{n}}
, the set of equivalence class of irreducible representations of
K_{n}
, one can associate a nonnegative integer
\deg\tau
called the
Howe degree
of
\tau

[
22
]
. The Howe degree of an irreducible representation
\tau
of highest weight
\mu
is

\deg\tau\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\mu_{j}\|.

When
n=1
, these irreducible representations are simply characters. Characters
\chi
of
K_{1}
are of the form

\chi(x)=\begin{cases}\sgn(x)^{\kappa}&if $K_{1}=\mathrm{O}(1)$,\\
e^{i\kappa\arg(x)}&if $K_{1}=\mathrm{U}(1)$,\end{cases}

where
\kappa\in\{0,1\}
if
K_{1}=\mathrm{O}(1)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}
if
K_{1}=\mathrm{U}(1)\cong\mathbb{R}/\penalty\mathbb{Z}\cong S^{1}
. In either case, the conductor exponent of
\chi
is
c(\chi)\coloneqq\|\kappa\|
.

3.2.
Spaces of Homogeneous Harmonic Polynomials

Let
\chi
be a character of
\mathrm{O}(1)
. We let
m
be a nonnegative integer for which
m\geq c(\chi)
and
m\equiv c(\chi)\pmod{2}
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{\chi,m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the space of degree
m
homogeneous polynomials with central character
\chi
, and let
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the subspace of
\mathcal{P}_{\chi,m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of harmonic homogeneous polynomials;
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a model for the irreducible representation of
\mathrm{O}(n)
with central character
\chi
and highest weight
(m,0,\ldots,0)
, which we denote by
\tau_{\chi,m}
.

Similarly, let
\chi
be a character of
\mathrm{U}(1)
. We let
m
be a nonnegative integer for which
m\geq c(\chi)
and
m\equiv c(\chi)\pmod{2}
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{\chi,m}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
denote the space of degree
m
homogeneous polynomials with central character
\chi
, and let
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
denote the subspace of
\mathcal{P}_{\chi,m}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
of harmonic homogeneous polynomials;
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
is a model for the irreducible representation of
\mathrm{U}(n)
with central character
\chi
and highest weight
(m_{1},0,\ldots,0,-m_{2})
, which we also denote by
\tau_{\chi,m}
. The nonnegative integers
m_{1},m_{2}
are such that
m_{1}+m_{2}=m
and
m_{1}-m_{2}=\ell
, where
\chi=e^{i\ell\arg}
, so that
c(\chi)=\max\{\ell,-\ell\}
, and elements of
\mathcal{P}_{\chi,m}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
have bidegree
(m_{1},m_{2})
.

In both cases, every irreducible representation of
K_{n}
whose restriction to
K_{n-1}
contains the trivial representation is of the form
\tau_{\chi,m}
for some character
\chi
of
K_{1}
and some integer
m\geq c(\chi)
; moreover, the Howe degree
\deg\tau_{\chi,m}
of
\tau_{\chi,m}
is simply the nonnegative integer
m
. The action of the group
K_{n}\ni k
on the space
\mathcal{P}_{\chi,m}(F^{n})\ni P
is via right translation, namely
(\tau_{\chi,m}(k)\cdot P)(x)\coloneqq P(xk)
. The homogeneity of a polynomial
P
in
\mathcal{P}_{\chi,m}(F^{n})
means that

(3.1)

P(\lambda x)=\chi\left(\frac{\lambda}{\|\lambda\|}\right)\|\lambda\|^{m}P(x)

for all
x\in F^{n}
and
\lambda\in F^{\times}
, while polynomials in
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(F^{n})
are additionally annihilated by the Laplacian

\Delta=\begin{cases}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}_{j}}&if $F=\mathbb{R}$,\\
4\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}\partial\overline{x_{j}}}&if $F=\mathbb{C}$.\end{cases}

We record the following key properties of homogeneous polynomials in
\mathcal{P}_{\chi,m}(F^{n})
.

Proposition 3.2

(Cf.
[
23
, Lemmata 7.1 and 7.7]
)
.

There exists a unique
K_{n-1}
-invariant polynomial
P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(F^{n})
satisfying
P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}(e_{n})=1
, where the group
K_{n-1}
is embedded in
K_{n}
via
k^{\prime}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}k^{\prime}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

k\in K_{n}
, we have that

(3.3)

P_{{\chi},m}^{\circ}(e_{n}k)=\overline{P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}}(e_{n}k^{-1}).

We define an inner product on
\mathcal{P}_{\chi,m}(F^{n})\ni P,Q

\langle P,Q\rangle\coloneqq\int_{K_{n}}P(e_{n}k)\overline{Q}(e_{n}k)\,dk.

Our first utilisation of
P^{\circ}_{{\chi},m}
is the following, which is known as the addition theorem for

Proposition 3.4

(Cf.
[

Let
\{Q_{\ell}\}

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\dim\tau_{\chi,m}}Q_{\ell}(x)\overline{Q_{\ell}}(e_{n}k)=\dim\tau_{\chi,m}P_{{\chi},m}^{\circ}(xk^{-1}).

We make crucial use of the fact that for all

and

Proposition 3.5

(Cf.
[

The reproducing kernel for
\mathcal{H}_{\chi,m}(F^{n})

(3.6)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=c(\chi)\\
m\equiv c(\chi)\hskip-5.69046pt\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}^{\ell}(x\prescript{t}{}{\overline{x}})^{\frac{\ell-m}{2}}(\dim\tau_{\chi,m})P^{\circ}_{\chi,m}(x),

(3.7)

\displaystyle P(e_{n}k)

\displaystyle=\int_{K_{n}}P(e_{n}k^{\prime}k)(\dim\tau_{\chi,m})\overline{P_{\chi,m}^{\circ}}(e_{n}k^{\prime})\,dk^{\prime}

for all

(3.8)

\displaystyle P(e_{n}k)

\displaystyle=\int_{K_{n}}P(e_{n}k^{\prime}k)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=c(\chi)\\

Proof.

The first assertion is justified in

Let

Theorem 3.9

(
[

Let

Definition 3.11

)
.

Let
(\pi,V_{\pi})
be an induced representation of Whittaker type of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
. We define the
newform
K_{n}
-type

\tau_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}
to be the
K_{n}
-type of minimal Howe degree
m=c(\pi)
whose restriction to
K_{n-1}
contains the trivial representation. We define the
conductor exponent

c(\pi)
of
\pi
to be the Howe degree of the newform
K_{n}
-type. The nonzero
\tau_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}
-isotypic
K_{n-1}
-invariant vector
v^{\circ}\in V_{\pi}
, unique up to scalar multiplication, is called the
newform
of
\pi
.

Remark 3.12
.

As proven by the first author in
[
24
]
, this definition of the conductor exponent and the newform, as well as the existence of the newform
K_{n}
-type, is consistent with the
nonarchimedean
definition of the conductor exponent and the newform first introduced by Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro, and Shalika
[
29
]
.

When
V_{\pi}
is the induced model of
\pi
, we may normalise the newform
v^{\circ}\in V_{\pi}
as in
[
23
, Corollary 8.17 and Definition 9.2]
, which gives us a
canonically normalised newform
. When
\pi
is an induced representation of Langlands type, the Whittaker model
\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
is a model of
\pi
, which is given by the analytic continuation of the Jacquet integral of the induced model. The image of the canonically normalised newform under this map is called the
Whittaker newform
and denoted by
W_{\pi}^{\circ}

[
23
, §9.1]
. When
\pi
is unramified, the Whittaker newform
W_{\pi}^{\circ}
is simply the spherical Whittaker function.

The following lemma may be thought as the archimedean analogue of
[
24
, Theorem 4.16]
.

Lemma 3.13

(Cf.
[
23
, Theorem 4.17]
)
.

For all
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
and
k^{\prime}\in K_{n-1}
, the Whittaker newform
W_{\pi}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
of an induced representation of Langlands type
(\pi,V_{\pi})
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
satisfies

(3.14)

\displaystyle\dim\tau_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}\int_{K_{n}}W_{\pi}^{\circ}(gk)P_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}^{\circ}(e_{n}k^{-1})\,dk

\displaystyle=W_{\pi}^{\circ}(g),

(3.15)

\displaystyle W^{\circ}_{\pi}\left(g\begin{pmatrix}k^{\prime}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=W^{\circ}_{\pi}(g).

Proof.

The identity (
3.15
) is simply the fact that
W_{\pi}^{\circ}
is right
K_{n-1}
-invariant. Since
W_{\pi}^{\circ}
is additionally
\tau_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}
-isotypic, it satisfies

\dim\tau_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}\int_{K_{n}}W_{\pi}^{\circ}(gk)\Tr\tau_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}(k^{-1})\,dk=W_{\pi}^{\circ}(g)

for all
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
. Upon replacing
g
with
g\begin{pmatrix}k^{\prime}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
, integrating over
K_{n-1}\ni k^{\prime}
, and making the change of variables
k^{\prime}\mapsto k^{\prime-1}
and
k\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}k^{\prime}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}k
, we deduce that

\dim\tau_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}\int_{K_{n}}W_{\pi}^{\circ}(gk)\int_{K_{n-1}}\Tr\tau_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}\left(k^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}k^{\prime}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right)\,dk^{\prime}\,dk=W_{\pi}^{\circ}(g).

From
Proposition
3.2
and (
3.7
), the inner integral is equal to

\frac{\left\langle\tau_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}(k^{-1})\cdot P_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}^{\circ},P_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}^{\circ}\right\rangle}{\left\langle P_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}^{\circ},P_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}^{\circ}\right\rangle}=P_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}^{\circ}(e_{n}k^{-1}).\qed

4.
Langlands Parameters

We now associate an induced representation of Langlands type
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
to a distinguished spherical induced representation of Langlands type
\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
defined in terms of the Langlands parameters of
\pi
.

Definition 4.1
.

The Langlands parameters associated to an induced representation of Whittaker type
\pi=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigoplus}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}}\displaylimits_{j=1}^{r}\pi_{j}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
are the
n
-tuple of complex numbers
(\alpha_{\pi,1},\ldots,\alpha_{\pi,n})
given by

\alpha_{\pi,\ell}\coloneqq\begin{cases}t_{j}+\frac{\|\kappa_{j}\|}{2}&if $\ell=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}$ and $\pi_{j}=e^{i\kappa_{j}\arg}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{C}}^{t_{j}}$,\\
t_{j}+\kappa_{j}&if $\ell=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}$ and $\pi_{j}=\sgn^{\kappa_{j}}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{R}}^{t_{j}}$,\\
t_{j}+\frac{\kappa_{j}+1}{2}&if $\ell=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}-1$ and $\pi_{j}=D_{\kappa_{j}}\otimes\left|\det\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{t_{j}}$,\\
t_{j}+\frac{\kappa_{j}-1}{2}&if $\ell=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}$ and $\pi_{j}=D_{\kappa_{j}}\otimes\left|\det\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{t_{j}}$.\end{cases}

Proposition 4.2
.

Given an induced representation of Whittaker type
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
, there exists a spherical induced representation of Langlands type
\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
for which
L(s,\pi)=L(s,\pi_{\mathrm{ur}})
.

Proof.

There exists a permutation
\sigma
for which the Langlands parameters of
\pi
are such that
\Re(\alpha_{\pi,\sigma(1)})\geq\cdots\geq\Re(\alpha_{\pi,\sigma(n)})
. Let
\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}\coloneqq\mathop{\vphantom{\bigoplus}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}}\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n}|\cdot|^{\alpha_{\pi,\sigma(j)}}
. This isobaric sum of unramified characters is a spherical induced representation of Langlands type of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
that satisfies

\displaystyle L(s,\pi_{\mathrm{ur}})

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\zeta_{F}\left(s+\alpha_{\pi,\sigma(j)}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\begin{cases}\zeta_{\mathbb{C}}\left(s+t_{j}+\frac{\|\kappa_{j}\|}{2}\right)&if $\pi_{j}=e^{i\kappa_{j}\arg}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{C}}^{t_{j}}$,\\
\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}(s+t_{j}+\kappa_{j})&if $\pi_{j}=\sgn^{\kappa_{j}}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{R}}^{t_{j}}$,\\
\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}\left(s+t_{j}+\frac{\kappa_{j}-1}{2}\right)\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}\left(s+t_{j}+\frac{\kappa_{j}+1}{2}\right)&if $\pi_{j}=D_{\kappa_{j}}\otimes\left|\det\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{t_{j}}$,\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{r}L(s,\pi_{j})

\displaystyle=L(s,\pi).\qed

Remark 4.3
.

Proposition
4.2
ensures the existence of such a spherical representation
\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
, but does not guarantee the
uniqueness
. Indeed, if there are at least two Langlands parameters whose real parts are equal but whose imaginary parts are not, then there exists more than one permutation
\sigma
for which the condition
\Re(\alpha_{\pi,\sigma(1)})\geq\cdots\geq\Re(\alpha_{\pi,\sigma(n)})
is met, and hence more than one spherical induced representation of Langlands type having the same
L
-function as
\pi
. Nonetheless, the spherical Whittaker function
W_{\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi_{\mathrm{ur}},\psi)

is
uniquely determined, since if two spherical induced representations of Langlands type have the same
L
-function, then their spherical Whittaker functions are equal due to the Whittaker–Plancherel theorem (see
[
23
, Lemma 10.5]
and
Lemma
4.6
).

Remark 4.4
.

A result analogous to
Proposition
4.2
also holds in the nonarchimedean setting. A notable difference in this setting is that if
\pi
is ramified, then
\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}
is a spherical representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{m}(F)
with
m<n
.

The central character
\omega_{\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}}
of
\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}
is closely related to the central character
\omega_{\pi}
of
\pi
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\pi
be an induced representation of Langlands type of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
. Then for all
z\in F^{\times}
,

\omega_{\pi}(z)\overline{\chi_{\pi}}\left(\frac{z}{\|z\|}\right)\|z\|^{c(\pi)}=\omega_{\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}}(z).

Proof.

We write
\pi=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigoplus}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}}\displaylimits_{j=1}^{r}\pi_{j}
, so that the central character of
\pi
is
\omega_{\pi}=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\omega_{\pi_{j}}
, where the central character
\omega_{\pi_{j}}
of
\pi_{j}
is

\omega_{\pi_{j}}=\begin{cases}e^{i\kappa_{j}\arg}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{C}}^{t_{j}}&if $\pi_{j}=e^{i\kappa_{j}\arg}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{C}}^{t_{j}}$,\\
\sgn^{\kappa_{j}}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{R}}^{t_{j}}&if $\pi_{j}=\sgn^{\kappa_{j}}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{R}}^{t_{j}}$,\\
\sgn^{\kappa_{j}\hskip-5.69046pt\pmod{2}}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{R}}^{t_{j}}&if $\pi_{j}=D_{\kappa_{j}}\otimes\left|\det\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{t_{j}}$.\end{cases}

Thus for
F=\mathbb{C}
,
\omega_{\pi}=e^{i\kappa_{\pi}\arg}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{C}}^{t_{\pi}}
where
\kappa_{\pi}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\kappa_{j}
and
t_{\pi}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}t_{j}
, while for
F=\mathbb{R}
, we have that
\omega_{\pi}=\sgn^{\kappa_{\pi}}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{R}}^{t_{\pi}}
, where
\kappa_{\pi}\in\{0,1\}
is such that
\kappa_{\pi}\equiv\sum_{j=1}^{r}\kappa_{j}\pmod{2}
and
t_{\pi}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{r}t_{j}
. In particular,

\chi_{\pi}\left(\frac{z}{\|z\|}\right)=\begin{cases}e^{i\kappa_{\pi}\arg(z)}&if $F=\mathbb{C}$,\\
\sgn(z)^{\kappa_{\pi}}&if $F=\mathbb{R}$.\end{cases}

From
[
23
, Theorem 4.15 and Section 5.2.1]
, we have that
c(\pi)=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\|\kappa_{j}\|
, so that

\|z\|^{c(\pi)}=\begin{cases}|z|_{\mathbb{C}}^{(\|\kappa_{1}\|+\cdots+\|\kappa_{r}\|)/2}&if $F=\mathbb{C}$,\\
|z|_{\mathbb{R}}^{\kappa_{1}+\cdots+\kappa_{r}}&if $F=\mathbb{R}$.\end{cases}

From this, we see that

\omega_{\pi}(z)\overline{\chi_{\pi}}\left(\frac{z}{\|z\|}\right)\|z\|^{c(\pi)}=|z|^{(t_{1}+\cdots+t_{r})+(\|\kappa_{1}\|+\cdots+\|\kappa_{r}\|)/d_{F}}.

On the other hand, the central character of
\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}
is
\omega_{\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}}=|\cdot|^{t_{\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}}}
with
t_{\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{r}(t_{j}+\|\kappa_{j}\|/d_{F})
. Thus

\omega_{\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}}(z)=|z|^{(t_{1}+\cdots+t_{r})+(\|\kappa_{1}\|+\cdots+\|\kappa_{r}\|)/d_{F}}.\qed

We introduce the following by-now well-known lemma. Over nonarchimedean fields, this lemma plays an important role in the proofs of the stability of
\gamma
-factors
[
33
, Lemma 3.2]
and of the local converse theorem
[
56
]
. Our case is the archimedean analogue (cf.
[
23
, Lemma 10.5]
) of a result of Jacquet, Piateski-Shapiro, and Shalika
[
29
, Lemme (3.5)]
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\pi
be an induced representation of Langlands type of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
. Then

W_{\pi}^{\circ}\begin{pmatrix}g&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}=W_{\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}}^{\circ}\begin{pmatrix}g&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{for all $g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F)$.}

Proof.

We claim that for every spherical induced representation of Langlands type
\sigma
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F)
with spherical Whittaker function
W_{\sigma}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{W}(\sigma,\overline{\psi})
,

\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{n-1}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F)}\left(W_{\pi}^{\circ}\begin{pmatrix}g&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}-W_{\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}}^{\circ}\begin{pmatrix}g&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right)W_{\sigma}^{\circ}(g)\left|\det g\right|^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\,dg=0

for
\Re(s)
sufficiently large; by the Whittaker–Plancherel theorem (see
[
23
, Lemma 10.5]
), this implies the desired equality. Indeed, we have by
[
23
, Theorem 4.17]
that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{n-1}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F)}W_{\pi}^{\circ}\begin{pmatrix}g&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}W_{\sigma}^{\circ}(g)\left|\det g\right|^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\,dg

\displaystyle=L(s,\pi\times\sigma),

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{n-1}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F)}W_{\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}}^{\circ}\begin{pmatrix}g&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}W_{\sigma}^{\circ}(g)\left|\det g\right|^{s-\frac{1}{2}}\,dg

\displaystyle=L(s,\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}\times\sigma),

and these are equal as we can write
\sigma=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigoplus}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}}\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n-1}|\cdot|^{t_{j}}
for some
t_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
, and then

L(s,\pi\times\sigma)=\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}L(s+t_{j},\pi)=\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}L(s+t_{j},\pi_{\mathrm{ur}})=L(s,\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}\times\sigma).\qed

5.
Rankin–Selberg Integrals

Given induced representations of Whittaker type
\pi
and
\sigma
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
, Whittaker functions
W_{\pi}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
and
W_{\sigma}\in\mathcal{W}(\sigma,\overline{\psi})
, and a Schwartz–Bruhat function
\Phi\in\mathscr{S}(F^{n})
, the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}
Rankin–Selberg integral is defined by

\Psi(s,W_{\pi},W_{\sigma},\Phi)\coloneqq\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{n}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)}W_{\pi}(g)W_{\sigma}(g)\Phi(e_{n}g)\left|\det g\right|^{s}\,dg.

The local Rankin–Selberg
L
-function
L(s,\pi\times\sigma)
is defined via the local Langlands correspondence as delineated in
[
41
]
. This integral converges absolutely for
\Re(s)
sufficiently large and extends meromorphically to the entire complex plane. Jacquet
[
36
, Theorem 2.3]
has shown that
\Psi(s,W_{\pi},W_{\sigma},\Phi)
is a holomorphic multiple of
L(s,\pi\times\sigma)
and that the quotient

\frac{\Psi(s,W_{\pi},W_{\sigma},\Phi)}{L(s,\pi\times\sigma)}

is of finite order in vertical strips.

5.1.
Test Vectors for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}
Rankin–Selberg Integrals and Periods

For
x\in\Mat_{n\times m}(F)
, we define the Schwartz–Bruhat function
\Phi_{\mathrm{ur}}\in\mathscr{S}(\Mat_{n\times m}(F))
by

(5.1)

\Phi_{\mathrm{ur}}(x)\coloneqq\exp(-d_{F}\pi\Tr(x\prescript{t}{}{\overline{x}}))=\begin{cases}\exp(-\pi\Tr(x\prescript{t}{}{x}))&if $F=\mathbb{R}$,\\
\exp(-2\pi\Tr(x\prescript{t}{}{\overline{x}})&if $F=\mathbb{C}$.\end{cases}

It is readily seen that
\Phi_{\mathrm{ur}}(xk)=\Phi_{\mathrm{ur}}(k^{\prime}x)=\Phi_{\mathrm{ur}}(x)
for all
k\in K_{m}
and
k^{\prime}\in K_{n}
; that is,
\Phi_{\mathrm{ur}}
is right
K_{m}
-invariant and left
K_{n}
-invariant.

Our goal is to evaluate the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}
Rankin–Selberg integral when
\pi
and
\sigma
may be ramified. Our first step is to reduce this integral to an integral over
\mathrm{N}_{n-1}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F)
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\pi
and
\sigma
be induced representations of Langlands type of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
with Whittaker newforms
W_{\pi}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
and
W_{\sigma}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{W}(\sigma,\overline{\psi})
. Let
\Phi\in\mathscr{S}(F^{n})
be the right
K_{n}
-finite Schwartz–Bruhat function of the form
\Phi(x)=P(x)\exp(-d_{F}\pi x\prescript{t}{}{\overline{x}})
, where the distinguished homogeneous polynomial
P\in\mathcal{P}_{\overline{\chi_{\pi}\chi_{\sigma}},c(\pi)+c(\sigma)}(F^{n})
is given by

(5.3)

P(x)\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=c(\chi_{\pi}\chi_{\sigma})\\
m\equiv c(\chi_{\pi}\chi_{\sigma})\hskip-5.69046pt\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}^{c(\pi)+c(\sigma)}(x\prescript{t}{}{\overline{x}})^{\frac{c(\pi)+c(\sigma)-m}{2}}(\dim\tau_{\chi_{\pi}\chi_{\sigma},m})\overline{P_{\chi_{\pi}\chi_{\sigma},m}^{\circ}}(x).

Then the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}
Rankin–Selberg integral
\Psi(s,W_{\pi}^{\circ},W_{\sigma}^{\circ},\Phi)
is equal to

(5.4)

L(ns,\omega_{\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}}\omega_{\sigma_{\mathrm{ur}}})\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{n-1}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F)}W_{\pi}^{\circ}\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}W_{\sigma}^{\circ}\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\left|\det h\right|^{s-1}\,dh.

Proof.

The absolute convergence of the ensuing integral can be justified from
[
36
, Proposition 3.3 and Lemma 3.5]
. By the Iwasawa decomposition
g=(z1_{n})\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}k
for
\mathrm{N}_{n}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
, the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}
Rankin–Selberg integral
\Psi(s,W_{\pi}^{\circ},W_{\sigma}^{\circ},\Phi)
is equal to

\int_{F^{\times}}\omega_{\pi}\omega_{\sigma}(z)|z|^{ns}\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{n-1}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F)}\left|\det h\right|^{s-1}\\
\times\int_{K_{n}}W_{\pi}^{\circ}\left(\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}k\right)W_{\sigma}^{\circ}\left(\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}k\right)\Phi(ze_{n}k)\,dk\,dh\,d^{\times}z.

We now insert the identities (
3.14
) for both
W_{\pi}^{\circ}(g)
and
W_{\sigma}^{\circ}(g)
with
g
replaced by
\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}k
and the variables of integration being
k_{1}\in K_{n}
for
W_{\pi}^{\circ}
and
k_{2}\in K_{n}
for
W_{\sigma}^{\circ}
. We then interchange the order of integration and make the change of variables
k\mapsto k^{-1}
,
k_{1}\mapsto k^{-1}k_{1}
, and
k_{2}\mapsto k^{-1}k_{2}
, arriving at

\int_{F^{\times}}\omega_{\pi}\omega_{\sigma}(z)|z|^{ns}\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{n-1}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F)}\left|\det h\right|^{s-1}\int_{K_{n}}W_{\pi}^{\circ}\left(\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}k_{1}\right)\int_{K_{n}}W_{\sigma}^{\circ}\left(\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}k_{2}\right)\\
\times\dim\tau_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}\dim\tau_{\chi_{\sigma},c(\sigma)}\int_{K_{n}}P_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}^{\circ}(e_{n}k_{1}^{-1}k)P_{\chi_{\sigma},c(\sigma)}^{\circ}(e_{n}k_{2}^{-1}k)\Phi(ze_{n}k^{-1})\,dk\,dk_{2}\,dk_{1}\,dh\,d^{\times}z.

By the addition theorem,
Proposition
3.4
, the last line is

\sum_{\ell_{1}=1}^{\dim\tau_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}}\sum_{\ell_{2}=1}^{\dim\tau_{\chi_{\sigma},c(\sigma)}}Q_{\ell_{1}}(e_{n}k_{1}^{-1})Q_{\ell_{2}}^{\prime}(e_{n}k_{2}^{-1})\int_{K_{n}}\overline{Q_{\ell_{1}}}(e_{n}k)\overline{Q_{\ell_{2}}^{\prime}}(e_{n}k)\Phi(ze_{n}k^{-1})\,dk,

where
\{Q_{\ell_{1}}\}
and
\{Q_{\ell_{2}}^{\prime}\}
are orthonormal bases of
\mathcal{H}_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}(F^{n})
and
\mathcal{H}_{\chi_{\sigma},c(\sigma)}(F^{n})
.

To proceed further, we observe that for
z\in F^{\times}
and
k\in K_{n}
,we have that

\Phi(ze_{n}k^{-1})=\overline{\chi_{\pi}\chi_{\sigma}}\left(\frac{z}{\|z\|}\right)\|z\|^{c(\pi)+c(\sigma)}e^{-d_{F}\pi\|z\|^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=c(\chi_{\pi}\chi_{\sigma})\\
m\equiv c(\chi_{\pi}\chi_{\sigma})\hskip-5.69046pt\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}^{c(\pi)+c(\sigma)}(\dim\tau_{\chi_{\pi}\chi_{\sigma},m})P_{\chi_{\pi}\chi_{\sigma},m}^{\circ}(e_{n}k)

by the definition of the Schwartz–Bruhat function
\Phi\in\mathscr{S}(F^{n})
, the homogeneity of
P_{\chi_{\pi}\chi_{\sigma},m}^{\circ}
as in (
3.1
), and the identity (
3.3
). By (
3.6
), the sum over
m
is the reproducing kernel for
\mathcal{P}_{\overline{\chi_{\pi}\chi_{\sigma}},c(\pi)+c(\sigma)}(F^{n})
, and so the integral over
K_{n}\ni k
is
\overline{Q_{\ell_{1}}}(e_{n})\overline{Q_{\ell_{2}}^{\prime}}(e_{n})
by (
3.8
). Using the addition theorem,
Proposition
3.4
, in
reverse
and then using (
3.14
) to evaluate the integrals over
K_{n}\ni k_{1}
and
K_{n}\ni k_{2}
, we find that
\Psi(s,W_{\pi}^{\circ},W_{\sigma}^{\circ},\Phi)
is equal to

\int_{F^{\times}}\omega_{\pi}\omega_{\sigma}(z)\overline{\chi_{\pi}\chi_{\sigma}}\left(\frac{z}{\|z\|}\right)\|z\|^{c(\pi)+c(\sigma)}|z|^{ns}e^{-d_{F}\pi\|z\|^{2}}\,d^{\times}z\\
\times\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{n-1}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F)}W_{\pi}^{\circ}\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}W_{\sigma}^{\circ}\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\left|\det h\right|^{s-1}\,dh.

It remains to recall
Lemma
4.5
, which, by (
2.1
), shows that the integral over
F^{\times}\ni z
is
L(ns,\omega_{\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}}\omega_{\sigma_{\mathrm{ur}}})
.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

The same proof remains valid in the nonarchimedean setting using the theory of the newform
K_{n}
-type and
p
-adic spherical harmonics
[
24
]
.

With this in hand, we are now able to evaluate this Rankin–Selberg integral by reducing to the spherical case.

Theorem 5.6
.

With the notation and hypotheses of
Proposition
5.2
, the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}
Rankin–Selberg integral

\Psi(s,W_{\pi}^{\circ},W_{\sigma}^{\circ},\Phi)

is equal to
L(s,\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}\times\sigma_{\mathrm{ur}})
.

Proof.

According to
Lemma
4.6
and
Proposition
5.2
, we have the identity

\Psi(s,W_{\pi}^{\circ},W_{\sigma}^{\circ},\Phi)=L(ns,\omega_{\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}}\omega_{\sigma_{\mathrm{ur}}})\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{n-1}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F)}W_{\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}}^{\circ}\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}W_{\sigma_{\mathrm{ur}}}^{\circ}\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\left|\det h\right|^{s-1}\,dh.

On the other hand, with
\Phi_{\mathrm{ur}}\in\mathscr{S}(F^{n})
given by (
5.1
), the same calculation shows that the right-hand side above is equal to
\Psi(s,W_{\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}}^{\circ},W_{\sigma_{\mathrm{ur}}}^{\circ},\Phi_{\mathrm{ur}})
. Stade’s formula
[
53
, Theorem 1.1]
then gives the identity

\Psi(s,W_{\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}}^{\circ},W_{\sigma_{\mathrm{ur}}}^{\circ},\Phi_{\mathrm{ur}})=L(s,\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}\times\sigma_{\mathrm{ur}}).\qed

Remark 5.7
.

Stade’s formula is only proved for
F=\mathbb{R}
in
[
53
]
, though from
[
51
, Proposition 2.1]
, the same method can be used to prove this formula for
F=\mathbb{C}
. Different proofs of Stade’s formula, valid for
F\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\}
, were given in more general settings by the first author
[
23
, Theorem 4.18]
and independently by Ishii and Miyazaki
[
26
, Theorem 2.9]
; both proofs are based on the work of Jacquet
[
36
]
.

As previously highlighted, the triple
(W_{\pi}^{\circ},W_{\sigma}^{\circ},\Phi)
is a weak test vector, rather than a strong test vector, for the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}
Rankin–Selberg integral, since in general the naïve Rankin–Selberg
L
-function
L(s,\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}\times\sigma_{\mathrm{ur}})
is not equal to
L(s,\pi\times\sigma)
. Nonetheless, these two
L
-functions are closely related.

Proposition 5.8
.

Given induced representations of Whittaker type
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
and
\sigma
of
\mathrm{GL}_{m}(F)
, there exists a polynomial
p(s)
for which

L(s,\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}\times\sigma_{\mathrm{ur}})=p(s)L(s,\pi\times\sigma).

Proof.

For
\pi=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigoplus}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}}\displaylimits_{j=1}^{r}\pi_{j}
and
\sigma=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigoplus}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}}\displaylimits_{\ell=1}^{\rho}\sigma_{\ell}
, with each
\pi_{j}
and
\sigma_{\ell}
essentially square-integrable, we have that

L(s,\pi\times\sigma)=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\prod_{\ell=1}^{\rho}L(s,\pi_{j}\times\sigma_{\ell})

via the local Langlands correspondence
[
41
]
. Thus it suffices to consider the case where
\pi
and
\sigma
are both essentially square-integrable.

Suppose first that
F=\mathbb{C}
, so that
\pi=e^{i\kappa\arg}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{C}}^{t}
, and
\sigma=e^{i\lambda\arg}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{C}}^{u}
. Then since
\zeta_{\mathbb{C}}(s)\coloneqq 2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)
, and recalling the fact that
\Gamma(s+1)=s\Gamma(s)
, we have that

\frac{L(s,\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}\times\sigma_{\mathrm{ur}})}{L(s,\pi\times\sigma)}=\frac{\zeta_{\mathbb{C}}\left(s+t+u+\frac{\|\kappa\|+\|\lambda\|}{2}\right)}{\zeta_{\mathbb{C}}\left(s+t+u+\frac{\|\kappa+\lambda\|}{2}\right)}\\
=\begin{cases}1&if $\sgn(\kappa)=\sgn(\lambda)$,\\
(2\pi)^{-\min\{\|\kappa\|,\|\lambda\|\}}\prod_{m=0}^{\min\{\|\kappa\|,\|\lambda\|\}-1}\left(s+t+u+\frac{\|\kappa+\lambda\|}{2}+m\right)&if $\sgn(\kappa)\neq\sgn(\lambda)$.\end{cases}

Next suppose that
F=\mathbb{R}
and that
\pi=\sgn^{\kappa}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{R}}^{t}
, and
\sigma=\sgn^{\lambda}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{R}}^{u}
. Then since
\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}(s)\coloneqq\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)
, we have that

\frac{L(s,\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}\times\sigma_{\mathrm{ur}})}{L(s,\pi\times\sigma)}=\frac{\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}\left(s+t+u+\kappa+\lambda\right)}{\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}\left(s+t+u+\|\kappa-\lambda\|\right)}=\begin{cases}1&if $(\kappa,\lambda)\neq(1,1)$,\\
\pi^{-1}\left(\frac{s+t+u}{2}\right)&if $(\kappa,\lambda)=(1,1)$.\end{cases}

If
F=\mathbb{R}
,
\pi=D_{\kappa}\otimes\left|\det\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{t}
, and
\sigma=\sgn^{\lambda}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{R}}^{u}
, then

\displaystyle\frac{L(s,\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}\times\sigma_{\mathrm{ur}})}{L(s,\pi\times\sigma)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}\left(s+t+u+\frac{\kappa-1}{2}+\lambda\right)\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}\left(s+t+u+\frac{\kappa+1}{2}+\lambda\right)}{\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}\left(s+t+u+\frac{\kappa-1}{2}\right)\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}\left(s+t+u+\frac{\kappa+1}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&if $\lambda=0$,\\
\pi^{-1}\left(\frac{s+t+u+\frac{\kappa-1}{2}}{2}\right)&if $\lambda=1$.\end{cases}

An analogous identity holds if
\pi=\sgn^{\kappa}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{R}}^{t}
, and
\sigma=D_{\lambda}\otimes\left|\det\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{u}
.

Finally, if
F=\mathbb{R}
,
\pi=D_{\kappa}\otimes\left|\det\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{t}
, and
\sigma=D_{\lambda}\otimes\left|\det\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{u}
, then

\displaystyle\frac{L(s,\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}\times\sigma_{\mathrm{ur}})}{L(s,\pi\times\sigma)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}\left(s+t+u+\frac{\kappa+\lambda}{2}-1\right)\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}\left(s+t+u+\frac{\kappa+\lambda}{2}\right)}{\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}\left(s+t+u+\frac{\kappa+\lambda}{2}-1\right)\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}\left(s+t+u+\frac{\kappa+\lambda}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\times\frac{\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}\left(s+t+u+\frac{\kappa+\lambda}{2}\right)\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}\left(s+t+u+\frac{\kappa+\lambda}{2}+1\right)}{\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}\left(s+t+u+\frac{\|\kappa-\lambda\|}{2}\right)\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}\left(s+t+u+\frac{\|\kappa-\lambda\|}{2}+1\right)}

\displaystyle=\pi^{-\min\{\kappa,\lambda\}}\prod_{m=0}^{\left\lceil\frac{1}{2}\min\{\kappa,\lambda\}\right\rceil-1}\left(\frac{s+t+u+\frac{\|\kappa-\lambda\|}{2}}{2}+m\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\times\prod_{m=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2}\min\{\kappa,\lambda\}\right\rfloor-1}\left(\frac{s+t+u+\frac{\|\kappa-\lambda\|}{2}+1}{2}+m\right).\qed

When
s=1
, the integral appearing in (
5.4
) is known as the
Rankin–Selberg period
. In general, this integral need not converge at
s=1
. When
\pi
and
\sigma
are
unitary
, however, convergence is guaranteed by the following lemma. We omit the proof of this lemma, since it is standard
[
32
, Proposition 3.17]
; it relies upon bounds for Whittaker functions by a gauge, namely
[
34
, §4 Propositions 2 and 3]
.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
\pi
and
\sigma
be unitary generic irreducible Casselman–Wallach representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
. For any
W_{\pi}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
and
W_{\sigma}\in\mathcal{W}(\sigma,\overline{\psi})
, the integral

\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{n-1}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F)}W_{\pi}\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}W_{\sigma}\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\left|\det h\right|^{s-1}\,dh

converges absolutely for
\Re(s)\geq 1
.

Archimedean components of cuspidal automorphic representations are unitary generic irreducible Casselman–Wallach representations twisted by a (possibly nonunitary) unramified character. In this regard, our unitary assumption is sufficient for potential global applications therein (cf.
Section
8
). For
\sigma=\widetilde{\pi}
, the Rankin–Selberg period (in a slightly modified form as the inner product) appears in the work of Feigon, Lapid, and Offen
[
13
, Appendix A.1]
, Gelbart, Jacquet, and Rogawski
[
18
, Lemma 3.3]
, and W. Zhang
[
58
, (3.2)]
), which are all based on the pioneering result of Jacquet and Shalika
[
32
, Proposition 3.17]
. Notably, Venkatesh
[
54
, §7]
has evaluated the Rankin–Selberg period for nonarchimedean
F
when
\sigma=\widetilde{\pi}
and both Whittaker functions are newforms, so that this is simply the square of the
L^{2}
-norm of the newform. We prove an archimedean analogue.

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
\pi
and
\sigma
be unitary generic irreducible Casselman–Wallach representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
with Whittaker newforms
W_{\pi}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
and
W_{\sigma}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{W}(\sigma,\overline{\psi})
. Then the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}
Rankin–Selberg period

(5.11)

\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{n-1}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F)}W_{\pi}^{\circ}\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}W_{\sigma}^{\circ}\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\,dh

is equal to

\frac{L(1,\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}\times\sigma_{\mathrm{ur}})}{L(n,\omega_{\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}}\omega_{\sigma_{\mathrm{ur}}})}.

Proof.

Having
Lemma
5.9
in mind, we take
s=1
in
Proposition
5.2
and invoke
Theorem
5.6
.
∎

We say that
\pi\otimes\sigma
is
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
-distinguished
if
\Hom_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)}(\pi\otimes\sigma,\mathbf{1})
is nontrivial. If
\pi
and
\sigma
are irreducible, this condition amounts to saying that

.

Remark 5.12

(
[
4
, §10]
)
.

We define a
\mathrm{P}_{n}(F)
-invariant bilinear form

by

5.2.
Modified Rankin–Selberg Integrals by Sakellaridis

Sakellaridis
[
49
, §5]
has introduced new types of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}
and
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}
Rankin–Selberg integrals that extend the classical theory of Rankin–Selberg integrals due to Jacquet, Piatetski-Shapiro, and Shalika
[
30
]
. We solve the weak test vector problems for these integrals. For these modified Rankin–Selberg integrals, we let
G^{\diag}
denote the image of
G
under the diagonal embedding
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}
given by
g\mapsto(g,g)
with
G
a subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
.

Theorem 5.14
.

Let
\pi
and
\sigma
be induced representations of Langlands type of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
with Whittaker newforms
W^{\circ}_{\pi}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
and
W^{\circ}_{\sigma}\in\mathcal{W}(\sigma,\overline{\psi})
. Let
\Phi\in\mathscr{S}(F^{n})
be the right
K_{n}
-finite Schwartz–Bruhat function of the form
\Phi(x)=P(x)\exp(-d_{F}\pi x\prescript{t}{}{\overline{x}})
, where the distinguished homogeneous polynomial
P\in\mathcal{P}_{\overline{\chi_{\pi}\chi_{\sigma}},c(\pi)+c(\sigma)}(F^{n})
is given by (
5.3
), and let
\Phi^{\ast}\in\mathscr{S}(\Mat_{n\times n}(F))
be the bi-
K_{n}
-finite Schwartz–Bruhat function of the form
\Phi^{\ast}(x)=P^{\ast}(e_{n}x)\exp(-d_{F}\pi\Tr(x\prescript{t}{}{\overline{x}}))
, where the distinguished homogeneous polynomial
P^{\ast}\in\mathcal{H}_{\overline{\chi_{\sigma}},c(\sigma)}(F^{n})
is given by

P^{\ast}(x)\coloneqq(\dim\tau_{\chi_{\sigma},c(\sigma)})\overline{P_{\chi_{\sigma},c(\sigma)}^{\circ}}(x).

Then for
\Re(s_{1})
and
\Re(s_{2})
sufficiently large, the modified
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
Rankin–Selberg integral by Sakellaridis

(5.15)

\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}^{\diag}_{n}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)}W^{\circ}_{\pi}(g_{1})W^{\circ}_{\sigma}(g_{2})\Phi^{\ast}(g^{-1}_{1}g_{2})\Phi(e_{n}g_{1})\left|\frac{\det g_{2}}{\det g_{1}}\right|^{s_{1}}\left|\det g_{1}\right|^{s_{2}}\,dg_{2}\,dg_{1}

is equal to

L\left(s_{2},\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}\times\sigma_{\mathrm{ur}}\right)L\left(s_{1}-\frac{n-1}{2},\sigma\right).

Proof.

We make the change of variables
g_{2}\mapsto g_{1}g_{2}
. The ensuing integral becomes

\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{n}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)}W^{\circ}_{\pi}(g_{1})\Phi(e_{n}g_{1})\left|\det g_{1}\right|^{s_{2}}\int_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)}W^{\circ}_{\sigma}(g_{1}g_{2})\Phi^{\ast}(g_{2})\left|\det g_{2}\right|^{s_{1}}\,dg_{2}\,dg_{1}.

The absolute convergence of the above double integral follows from
[
36
, Lemma 3.2 (ii), Proposition 3.3, and Lemma 3.5]
. Applying
[
23
, Lemma 9.6]
to the innermost integral yields

L\left(s_{1}-\frac{n-1}{2},\sigma\right)\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{n}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)}W^{\circ}_{\pi}(g_{1})W^{\circ}_{\sigma}(g_{1})\Phi(e_{n}g_{1})\left|\det g_{1}\right|^{s_{2}}\,dg_{1}.

The latter is the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n}
Rankin–Selberg integral, which is
L(s_{2},\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}\times\sigma_{\mathrm{ur}})
by
Theorem
5.6
.
∎

Theorem 5.16
.

Let
\pi
be an induced representation of Langlands type of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
with Whittaker newform
W^{\circ}_{\pi}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
and let
\sigma
be a spherical induced representation of Langlands type of
\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F)
with spherical Whittaker function
W^{\circ}_{\sigma}\in\mathcal{W}(\sigma,\overline{\psi})
. Then for
\Re(s_{1})
and
\Re(s_{2})
sufficiently large, the modified
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)\times\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F)
Rankin–Selberg integral by Sakellaridis

(5.17)

\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}^{\diag}_{n-1}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F)\times\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F)}W^{\circ}_{\pi}\begin{pmatrix}g_{1}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}W^{\circ}_{\sigma}(g_{2})\Phi_{\mathrm{ur}}\left(g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}\right)\left|\frac{\det g_{2}}{\det g_{1}}\right|^{s_{1}}\left|\det g_{1}\right|^{s_{2}}\,dg_{2}\,dg_{1}

is equal to

L\left(s_{2}+\frac{1}{2},\pi\times\sigma\right)L\left(s_{1}-\frac{n-2}{2},\sigma\right)

where the Schwartz–Bruhat function
\Phi_{\mathrm{ur}}\in\mathscr{S}(\Mat_{(n-1)\times(n-1)}(F))
is given by (
5.1
).

Proof.

We make the change of variables
g_{2}\mapsto g_{1}g_{2}
in order to obtain

\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{n-1}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F)}W^{\circ}_{\pi}\begin{pmatrix}g_{1}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\left|\det g_{1}\right|^{s_{2}}\int_{\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F)}W^{\circ}_{\sigma}(g_{1}g_{2})\Phi_{\mathrm{ur}}(g_{2})\left|\det g_{2}\right|^{s_{1}}\,dg_{2}\,dg_{1}.

The absolute convergence of the above double integral can be shown by
[
36
, Lemma 3.2 (ii) and Section 8.3]
. Then
[
23
, Lemma 9.6]
implies that this is equal to

L\left(s_{1}-\frac{n-2}{2},\sigma\right)\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{n-1}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F)}W^{\circ}_{\pi}\begin{pmatrix}g_{1}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}W^{\circ}_{\sigma}(g_{1})\left|\det g_{1}\right|^{s_{2}}\,dg_{1}.

From
[
23
, Theorem 4.17]
, the latter integral is simply
L(s_{2}+1/2,\pi\times\sigma)
.
∎

We take this occasion to complete weak test vector problems for modified Rankin–Selberg integrals over a nonarchimedean local field
F
. The missing ingredient is a convolution section identity for the Whittaker newform, namely the nonarchimedean analogue of
[
23
, Lemma 9.6]
. The identity can be viewed as a generalization of an identity of Godement–Jacquet
[
20
, Lemma 6.10]
(cf.
[

).

Lemma 5.18
.

Let
F
be a nonarchimedean local field and let
\pi
be an induced representation of Langlands type of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
with Whittaker newform
W^{\circ}_{\pi}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
. Then for all
h\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
and for
\Re(s)
sufficiently large, we have that

\int_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)}W^{\circ}_{\pi}(hg)\Phi^{\ast}(g)\left|\det g\right|^{s+\frac{n-1}{2}}\,dg=L(s,\pi)W^{\circ}_{\pi}(h),

where
\Phi^{\ast}\in\mathscr{S}(\Mat_{n\times n}(F))
is the bi-
K_{n}
-finite Schwartz–Bruhat function

(5.19)

\Phi^{\ast}(x)\\
\coloneqq\begin{cases}1&if $c(\pi)=0$, $x\in\Mat_{n\times n}(\mathcal{O})$, and $x_{n,1},\ldots,x_{n,n}\in\mathcal{O}$,\\
\frac{\omega_{\pi}^{-1}(x_{n,n})}{\vol(K_{0}(\mathfrak{p}^{c(\pi)}))}&if $c(\pi)>0$, $x\in\Mat_{n\times n}(\mathcal{O})$, $x_{n,1},\ldots,x_{n,n-1}\in\mathfrak{p}^{c(\pi)}$, and $x_{n,n}\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}$,\\

\mathcal{O}

\pi

, where

\sigma=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigoplus}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}}\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n}|\cdot|^{t_{j}}

\sigma_{0}\coloneqq\mathop{\vphantom{\bigoplus}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}}\displaylimits_{j=2}^{n}|\cdot|^{t_{j}}

\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
and
\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(F)

and
W_{\sigma_{0}}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{W}(\sigma_{0},\overline{\psi})

\Psi(s,W_{\pi}^{\circ},W_{\sigma}^{\circ},\Phi^{\circ})\coloneqq\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{n}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)}W_{\pi}^{\circ}(g)W_{\sigma}^{\circ}(g)\Phi^{\circ}(e_{n}g)\left|\det g\right|^{s}\,dg

where

\Phi^{\circ}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\coloneqq\begin{cases}1&if $c(\pi)=0$ and $x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathcal{O}$,\\

\Psi(s,W_{\pi}^{\circ},W_{\sigma}^{\circ},\Phi^{\circ})\\

. Since

\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(\mathcal{O})
-invariant, as we may make the change of variables
g\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}k^{\prime-1}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}g
and use the fact that

-invariant, we therefore have that
W_{\pi}^{\prime}\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\

0&1\end{pmatrix}
for all

by
[

. Invoking the uniqueness of the Kirillov model of

, we deduce that
W_{\pi}^{\prime}(g)=W_{\pi}^{\circ}(g)
for all

.
∎

We present following consequences of the convolution section identity in

6.
Flicker Integrals

We define an additive character on
\mathbb{C}
by
\psi_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}}(z)\coloneqq e^{-2\pi(z-\overline{z})}
, so that
\psi_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}}(x+iy)=e^{-4\pi iy}
for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
; this additive character is trivial when restricted to
\mathbb{R}
. We let

\psi_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R},n}(u)\coloneqq\psi_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}u_{j,j+1}\right)

denote the corresponding character of
\mathrm{N}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\ni u
. Given an induced representation of Whittaker type
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, a Whittaker function
W_{\pi}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}})
, and a Schwartz–Bruhat function
\Phi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
Flicker integral
[
16
,
14
]
is defined by

\Psi(s,W_{\pi},\Phi)\coloneqq\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{n}(\mathbb{R})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})}W_{\pi}(g)\Phi(e_{n}g)\left|\det g\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{s}\,dg.

Once more, this integral converges absolutely for
\Re(s)
sufficiently and extends meromorphically to the entire complex plane. The local Asai
L
-function
L(s,\pi,\mathrm{As})
is defined via the local Langlands correspondence as described accurately in
[
5
, Section 3.2]
. Beuzart–Plessis
[
5
, Theorem 3.5]
has shown that
\Psi(s,W_{\pi},\Phi)
is a holomorphic multiple of
L(s,\pi,\mathrm{As})
and that the quotient

\frac{\Psi(s,W_{\pi},\Phi)}{L(s,\pi,\mathrm{As})}

is of finite order in vertical strips.

6.1.
The Spherical Calculation

We recall the Iwasawa decomposition

\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})=\mathrm{N}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\mathrm{A}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\mathrm{U}(n).

Since every element of
\mathrm{A}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
may be written as the product of an element of
\mathrm{A}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
and of
\mathrm{A}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\cap\mathrm{U}(n)
, we also have the Iwasawa decomposition

\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})=\mathrm{N}_{n}(\mathbb{C})\mathrm{A}_{n}(\mathbb{R})\mathrm{U}(n).

We write
g=uak
, where
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,
u\in\mathrm{N}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,
a=\diag(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathrm{A}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, and
k\in\mathrm{U}(n)
. The Haar measure on
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
becomes

dg=2^{n}\delta_{\mathrm{B}_{n}(\mathbb{R})}^{-2}(a)\,dk\,d^{\times}a\,du.

Here the additional factor of
2^{n}
arises from the fact that the diagonal torus is chosen to be
\mathrm{A}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
in place of
\mathrm{A}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, recalling that the Haar measure on
\mathbb{C}
is twice the Lebesgue measure, while for
a\in\mathrm{A}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, we observe that
\delta_{\mathrm{B}_{n}(\mathbb{R})}^{-2}(a)=\delta_{\mathrm{B}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}^{-1}(a)
.

We first require the following propagation formula.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\pi=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigoplus}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}}\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{C}}^{t_{j}}
and
\pi_{0}\coloneqq\mathop{\vphantom{\bigoplus}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}}\displaylimits_{j=2}^{n}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{C}}^{t_{j}}
be spherical induced representations of Langlands type of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and
\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{C})
respectively with spherical Whittaker functions
W_{\pi}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}})
and
W_{\pi_{0}}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi_{0},\psi_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}})
. Then for
a=\diag(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathrm{A}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, we have that

(6.2)

W_{\pi}^{\circ}(a)=2^{n-1}\left|\det a\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{2t_{1}}\delta_{\mathrm{B}_{n}(\mathbb{R})}(a)\int_{\mathrm{A}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})}W_{\pi_{0}}^{\circ}(a^{\prime})\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\exp\left(-2\pi a_{j}^{\prime 2}a_{j+1}^{-2}\right)\\
\times\exp\left(-2\pi a_{j}^{\prime-2}a_{j}^{2}\right)\left|\det a^{\prime}\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{-2t_{1}}\delta_{\mathrm{B}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})}^{-1}(a^{\prime})\,d^{\times}a^{\prime}.

Proof.

From
[
23
, Lemma 9.14]
, we have the identity

W_{\pi_{0}}^{\circ}\begin{pmatrix}g&0\\
0&a_{n}\end{pmatrix}=\left|\det g\right|_{\mathbb{C}}^{t_{1}+\frac{n-1}{2}}|a_{n}|_{\mathbb{C}}^{t_{1}-\frac{n-1}{2}}\\
\times\int_{\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{C})}W_{\pi_{0}}^{\circ}(h)\Phi_{1}(h^{-1}g)\Phi_{2}(a_{n}^{-1}e_{n-1}h)\left|\det h\right|_{\mathbb{C}}^{-t_{1}-\frac{n}{2}+1}\,dh

for
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and
a_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, where
\Phi_{1}\in\mathscr{S}(\Mat_{(n-1)\times(n-1)}(\mathbb{C}))
and
\Phi_{2}\in\mathscr{S}(\Mat_{1\times(n-1)}(\mathbb{C}))
are given by

\Phi_{1}(x_{1})\coloneqq\exp(-2\pi\Tr(x_{1}\prescript{t}{}{\overline{x_{1}}})),\qquad\Phi_{2}(x_{2})\coloneqq\exp(-2\pi x_{2}\prescript{t}{}{\overline{x_{2}}}).

We employ the Iwasawa decomposition in order to write
h=u^{\prime}a^{\prime}k^{\prime}
, where
u^{\prime}\in\mathrm{N}_{n-1}(\mathbb{C})
,
a^{\prime}=\diag(a_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{n-1}^{\prime})\in\mathrm{A}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})
, and
k^{\prime}\in\mathrm{U}(n-1)
. As
W_{\pi_{0}}^{\circ}(u^{\prime}a^{\prime}k^{\prime})=\psi_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R},n-1}(u^{\prime})W_{\pi_{0}}^{\circ}(a^{\prime})
, the integral over
\mathrm{U}(n-1)\ni k^{\prime}
is trivial.

Now we specify
g=\diag(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1})\in\mathrm{A}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})
and
a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
, so that
\Phi_{2}(a_{n}^{-1}e_{n-1}h)=\exp(-2\pi a_{n-1}^{\prime 2}a_{n}^{-2})
. We make the change of variables
u^{\prime}\mapsto u^{\prime-1}
, then
u_{i,j}^{\prime}\mapsto a_{i}^{\prime}a_{j}^{-1}u_{i,j}^{\prime}
, and finally evaluate the integrals over
\mathbb{C}\ni u_{i,j}^{\prime}
; they are equal to
1
if
j\neq i+1
and to
\exp(-2\pi a_{i}^{\prime 2}a_{i+1}^{-2})
if
j=i+1
. This gives the desired identity.
∎

We also require a convolution section identity.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\pi=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigoplus}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}}\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{C}}^{t_{j}}
be a spherical induced representation of Langlands type of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
with spherical Whittaker functions
W_{\pi}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}})
. Then for
a^{\prime}=\diag(a_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{n}^{\prime})\in\mathrm{A}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, we have that for
\Re(s)
sufficiently large,

(6.4)

L(s,\pi)W_{\pi}^{\circ}(a^{\prime})\\
=2^{n}\int_{\mathrm{A}_{n}(\mathbb{R})}W_{\pi}^{\circ}(a^{\prime}a)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\exp(-2\pi a_{j}^{2})\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\exp\left(-2\pi a_{j}^{\prime 2}a_{j+1}^{\prime-2}a_{j+1}^{-2}\right)\left|\det a\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{2s}\delta_{\mathrm{B}_{n}(\mathbb{R})}^{-1}(a)\,d^{\times}a.

Proof.

From
[
23
, Lemma 9.6]
, we have the identity

L(s,\pi)W_{\pi}^{\circ}(a^{\prime})=\int_{\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{C})}W_{\pi}^{\circ}(a^{\prime}h)\Phi_{\mathrm{ur}}(h)\left|\det h\right|_{\mathbb{C}}^{s+\frac{n-1}{2}}\,dh,

with
\Phi_{\mathrm{ur}}\in\mathscr{S}(\Mat_{n\times n}(\mathbb{C}))
given by (
5.1
). We employ the Iwasawa decomposition in order to write
h=uak
, where
u\in\mathrm{N}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,
a=\diag(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathrm{A}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, and
k\in\mathrm{U}(n)
. As
W_{\pi}^{\circ}(a^{\prime}uak)=\psi_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R},n-1}(a^{\prime}ua^{\prime-1})W_{\pi}^{\circ}(a^{\prime}a)
, the integral over
\mathrm{U}(n)\ni k
is trivial. We make the change of variables
u_{i,j}\mapsto a_{j}^{-1}u_{i,j}
, and finally evaluate the integrals over
\mathbb{C}\ni u_{i,j}
; they are equal to
1
if
j\neq i+1
and to
\exp(-2\pi a_{i}^{\prime 2}a_{i+1}^{\prime-2}a_{i+1}^{-2})
if
j=i+1
. We arrive at the identity (
6.4
).
∎

We are now able to prove that when
\pi
is a spherical induced representation of Langlands type of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, the spherical Whittaker function
W_{\pi}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}})
is a strong test vector for the Flicker integral.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
\pi=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigoplus}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}}\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{C}}^{t_{j}}
be a spherical induced representation of Langlands type of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
with spherical Whittaker function
W_{\pi}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}})
. Then with
\Phi_{\mathrm{ur}}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
given by (
5.1
), the Flicker integral
\Psi(s,W_{\pi}^{\circ},\Phi_{\mathrm{ur}})
is equal to

L(s,\pi,\mathrm{As})\coloneqq\prod_{j=1}^{n}\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}(s+2t_{j})\prod_{1\leq j<\ell\leq n}\zeta_{\mathbb{C}}(s+t_{j}+t_{\ell}).

Proof.

We prove this by induction. The base case
n=1
is trivially true since the
\mathrm{GL}_{1}
Flicker integral is simply

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\times}}\exp(-\pi x^{2})|x|_{\mathbb{R}}^{s+2t_{1}}\,d^{\times}x=\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}(s+2t_{1})=L(s,\pi,\mathrm{As}).

For the general case, we shall show that if
\pi=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigoplus}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}}\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{C}}^{t_{j}}
and
\pi_{0}\coloneqq\mathop{\vphantom{\bigoplus}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}}\displaylimits_{j=2}^{n}|\cdot|_{\mathbb{C}}^{t_{j}}
, then the
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
Flicker integral satisfies the identity

(6.6)

\Psi(s,W_{\pi}^{\circ},\Phi_{\mathrm{ur}})=\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}(s+2t_{1})L(s+t_{1},\pi_{0})I(s,W_{\pi_{0}}^{\circ},\Phi_{\mathrm{ur}}),

where the
\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}
Flicker integral on the right-hand side involves the spherical Whittaker function
W_{\pi_{0}}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi_{0},\psi_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}})
and the Schwartz–Bruhat function
\Phi_{\mathrm{ur}}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
given by (
5.1
). Since

L(s+t_{1},\pi_{0})=\prod_{\ell=2}^{n}\zeta_{\mathbb{C}}(s+t_{1}+t_{\ell}),

this implies the result by the induction hypothesis.

We first note that the
\Psi(s,W_{\pi}^{\circ},\Phi_{\mathrm{ur}})
is equal to

\int_{\mathrm{A}_{n}(\mathbb{R})}W_{\pi}^{\circ}(a)\exp(-\pi a_{n}^{2})\left|\det a\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{s}\delta_{\mathrm{B}_{n}(\mathbb{R})}^{-1}(a)\,d^{\times}a

via the Iwasawa decomposition
g=ak
, since the integral over
\mathrm{O}(n)\ni k
is trivial. We insert the propagation formula (
6.2
) and relabel
a=\diag(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathrm{A}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
as
\begin{pmatrix}y&0\\
0&a\end{pmatrix}
, where now
y\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
and
a=\diag(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1})\in\mathrm{A}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})
. The Flicker integral
\Psi(s,W_{\pi}^{\circ},\Phi_{\mathrm{ur}})
becomes

2^{n-1}\int_{\mathrm{A}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})}\exp(-\pi a_{n-1}^{2})\left|\det a\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{s+2t_{1}}\int_{\mathrm{A}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})}W_{\pi_{0}}^{\circ}(a^{\prime})\prod_{j=1}^{n-2}\exp\left(-2\pi a_{j+1}^{\prime-2}a_{j}^{2}\right)\\
\times\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\exp\left(-2\pi a_{j}^{\prime 2}a_{j}^{-2}\right)\left|\det a^{\prime}\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{-2t_{1}}\delta_{\mathrm{B}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})}^{-1}(a^{\prime})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\times}}\exp(-2\pi a_{1}^{\prime-2}y^{2})|y|_{\mathbb{R}}^{s+2t_{1}}\,d^{\times}y\,d^{\times}a^{\prime}\,d^{\times}a.

We make the change of variables
y\mapsto 2^{-1/2}a_{1}^{\prime}y
, then the change of variables
a_{j}^{\prime}\mapsto 2^{-1/2}a_{j}^{\prime}a_{j}
and
a_{j}\mapsto 2^{1/2}a_{j}^{\prime-1}a_{j+1}^{\prime}a_{j}
, and finally
a_{n-1}\mapsto 2^{1/2}a_{n-1}
. We arrive at the identity

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\times}}\exp(-\pi y^{2})|y|_{\mathbb{R}}^{s+2t_{1}}\,d^{\times}y\int_{\mathrm{A}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})}\exp(-\pi a_{n-1}^{\prime 2})\left|\det a^{\prime}\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{s}\delta_{\mathrm{B}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})}^{-1}(a^{\prime})\\
\times 2^{n-1}\int_{\mathrm{A}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})}W_{\pi_{0}}^{\circ}(a^{\prime}a)\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\exp(-2\pi a_{j}^{2})\prod_{j=1}^{n-2}\exp\left(-2\pi a_{j}^{\prime 2}a_{j+1}^{\prime-2}a_{j+1}^{-2}\right)\\
\times\left|\det a\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{2s+2t_{1}}\delta_{\mathrm{B}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})}^{-1}(a)\,d^{\times}a\,d^{\times}a^{\prime}.

The integral over
\mathbb{R}^{\times}\ni y
is
\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}(s+2t_{1})
, while the last two lines are
L(s+t_{1},\pi_{0})W_{\pi_{0}}^{\circ}(a^{\prime})
by the convolution section identity (

). So this is

\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}(s+2t_{1})L(s+t_{1},\pi_{0})\int_{\mathrm{A}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})}W_{\pi_{0}}^{\circ}(a^{\prime})\exp(-\pi a_{n-1}^{\prime 2})\left|\det a^{\prime}\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{s}\delta_{\mathrm{B}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})}^{-1}(a^{\prime})\,d^{\times}a^{\prime},

which is precisely (
6.6
) by the Iwasawa decomposition
g=a^{\prime}k^{\prime}
, since the integral over
\mathrm{O}(n-1)\ni k^{\prime}
is trivial.
∎

6.2.
Test Vectors for Flicker Integrals and Flicker–Rallis Periods

We proceed to the more general case where
\pi
may be ramified. Our first step is to reduce the Flicker integral to an integral over
\mathrm{N}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
\pi
be an induced representation of Langlands type of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
with Whittaker newform
W_{\pi}^{\circ}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}})
. Let
\Phi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the right
\mathrm{O}(n)
-finite Schwartz–Bruhat function of the form
\Phi(x)=P(x)\exp(-\pi x\prescript{t}{}{x})
, where the distinguished homogeneous polynomial
P\in\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{\pi}|_{\mathrm{O}(1)},c(\pi)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is given by

P(x)\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=c(\chi_{\pi}|_{\mathrm{O}(1)})\\
m\equiv c(\chi_{\pi}|_{\mathrm{O}(1)})\hskip-5.69046pt\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}^{c(\pi)}\left(x\prescript{t}{}{x}\right)^{\frac{c(\pi)-m}{2}}(\dim\tau_{\chi_{\pi}|_{\mathrm{O}(1)},m})\overline{P_{\chi_{\pi}|_{\mathrm{O}(1)},m}^{\circ}}(x).

Then the Flicker integral
\Psi(s,W_{\pi}^{\circ},\Phi)
is equal to

(6.8)

L\left(ns,\omega_{\pi_{\mathrm{ur}}}|_{\mathbb{R}^{\times}}\right)\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})}W_{\pi}^{\circ}\begin{pmatrix}g&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\left|\det g\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{s-1}\,dg.

The proof of this proceeds along similar lines to that of
Proposition
5.2
.

Proof.

We use the Iwasawa decomposition
g=(z1_{n})\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}k
for
\mathrm{N}_{n}(\mathbb{R})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
to see that the Flicker integral
\Psi(s,W_{\pi}^{\circ},\Phi)
is equal to

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\times}}\omega_{\pi}(z)|z|_{\mathbb{R}}^{ns}\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})}\left|\det h\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{s-1}\int_{\mathrm{O}(n)}W_{\pi}^{\circ}\left(\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}k\right)\Phi(ze_{n}k)\,dk\,dh\,d^{\times}z.

We insert the identity (
3.14
) for
W_{\pi}^{\circ}(g)
with
g
replaced by
\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}k
and the variable of integration being
k^{\prime}\in\mathrm{U}(n)
, then interchange the order of integration and make the change of variables
k\mapsto k^{-1}
and
k^{\prime}\mapsto kk^{\prime}
. We end up with

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\times}}\omega_{\pi}(z)|z|_{\mathbb{R}}^{ns}\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R})}\left|\det h\right|_{\mathbb{R}}^{s-1}\int_{\mathrm{U}(n)}W_{\pi}^{\circ}\left(\begin{pmatrix}h&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}k^{\prime}\right)\\
\times\dim\tau_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}\int_{\mathrm{O}(n)}P_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}^{\circ}(e_{n}k^{\prime-1}k^{-1})\Phi(ze_{n}k^{-1})\,dk\,dk^{\prime}\,dh\,d^{\times}z.

By the addition theorem,
Proposition
3.4
, the last line is

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\dim\tau_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}}Q_{\ell}(e_{n}k^{\prime-1})\int_{\mathrm{O}(n)}\overline{Q_{\ell}}(e_{n}k)\Phi(ze_{n}k^{-1})\,dk,

where
\{Q_{\ell}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}_{\chi_{\pi},c(\pi)}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
. By the homogeneity of
Q_{\ell}
, (
3.1
), we observe that the restrictions of these polynomials to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
are elements of
\mathcal{P}_{\chi_{\pi}|_{\mathrm{O}(1)},c(\pi)}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

We now use the fact that

\Phi(ze_{n}k^{-1})=\overline{\chi_{\pi}}\left(\frac{z}{\|z\|}\right)\|z\|^{c(\pi)}e^{-\pi z^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m=c(\chi_{\pi}|_{\mathrm{O}(1)})\\

7.
Bump–Friedberg Integrals

For
n=2m
, we define the embedding
J:\mathrm{GL}_{m}(F)\times\mathrm{GL}_{m}(F)\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
by

J(g,g^{\prime})_{k,\ell}\coloneqq\begin{cases}g_{i,j}&if $k=2i-1$ and $\ell=2j-1$,\\
g^{\prime}_{i,j}&if $k=2i$ and $l=2j$,\\
0&otherwise.\end{cases}

We denote by
\mathrm{M}_{m,m}(F)\cong\mathrm{GL}_{m}(F)\times\mathrm{GL}_{m}(F)
the standard Levi subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
associated with the partition
(m,m)
. Let

w_{m,m}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}1&2&\cdots&m&|&m+1&m+2&\cdots&2m\\
1&3&\cdots&2m-1&|&2&4&\cdots&2m\end{pmatrix}.

We then set

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{m,m}(F)

\displaystyle\coloneqq w_{m,m}\mathrm{M}_{m,m}(F)w^{-1}_{m,m}

\displaystyle=\left\{J(g,g^{\prime})=w_{m,m}\diag(g,g^{\prime})w^{-1}_{m,m}:\diag(g,g^{\prime})\in\mathrm{M}_{m,m}(F)\right\}.

Similarly, for
n=2m+1
, we define the embedding
J:\mathrm{GL}_{m+1}(F)\times\mathrm{GL}_{m}(F)\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
by

J(g,g^{\prime})_{k,\ell}\coloneqq\begin{cases}g_{i,j}&if $k=2i-1$ and $\ell=2j-1$,\\
g^{\prime}_{i,j}&if $k=2i$ and $\ell=2j$,\\
0&otherwise.\end{cases}

We denote by
\mathrm{M}_{m+1,m}(F)\cong\mathrm{GL}_{m+1}(F)\times\mathrm{GL}_{m}(F)
the standard Levi subgroup associated to the partition
(m+1,m)
of
2m+1
. Let
w_{m+1,m}\coloneqq w_{m+1,m+1}|_{\mathrm{GL}_{2m+1}(F)}
, so that

w_{m+1,m}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}1&2&\cdots&m+1&|&m+2&m+3&\cdots&2m&2m+1\\
1&3&\cdots&2m+1&|&2&4&\cdots&2m-2&2m\end{pmatrix},

and then set

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{m+1,m}(F)

\displaystyle\coloneqq w_{m+1,m}\mathrm{M}_{m+1,m}(F)w^{-1}_{m+1,m}

\displaystyle=\left\{J(g,g^{\prime})=w_{m+1,m}\diag(g,g^{\prime})w^{-1}_{m+1,m}:\diag(g,g^{\prime})\in\mathrm{M}_{m+1,m}(F)\right\}.

To make the above description much more transparent, we provide prototypical elements in the cases of
\mathrm{H}_{2,2}(F)
and
\mathrm{H}_{3,2}(F)
.

Example 7.1
.

The group
\mathrm{H}_{2,2}(F)
consists of invertible matrices of the form

J\left(\begin{pmatrix}g_{1,1}&g_{1,2}\\
g_{2,1}&g_{2,2}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}g^{\prime}_{1,1}&g^{\prime}_{1,2}\\

g_{2,1}&0&g_{2,2}&0\\

The group
\mathrm{H}_{3,2}(F)
consists of invertible matrices of the form

g_{2,1}&0&g_{2,2}&0&g_{2,3}\\
0&g^{\prime}_{2,1}&0&g^{\prime}_{2,2}&0\\
g_{3,1}&0&g_{3,2}&0&g_{3,3}\end{pmatrix}.

Given an induced representation of Whittaker type
\pi
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)

&\hskip-85.35826pt for $n=2m$,\\
\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{m}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{m}(F)}\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{m+1}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{m+1}(F)}W_{\pi}(J(g,g^{\prime}))\Phi(e_{m+1}g)\left|\det g\right|^{s_{1}}\left|\det g^{\prime}\right|^{s_{2}-s_{1}}\,dg\,dg^{\prime}&\\
&\hskip-85.35826pt for $n=2m+1$.\end{cases}

Alternatively, one can write this as an integral over
(\mathrm{N}_{n}(F)\cap\mathrm{H}_{m,m}(F))\backslash\mathrm{H}_{m,m}(F)
for
n=2m
and as an integral over
(\mathrm{N}_{n}(F)\cap\mathrm{H}_{m+1,m}(F))\backslash\mathrm{H}_{m+1,m}(F)
for

(cf.
[
43
,
44
]
). The Bump–Friedberg integrals converges absolutely for
\Re(s)
sufficiently large and extends meromorphically to the entire complex plane. The local exterior square

-function

is defined via the local Langlands correspondence as illustrated in
[

]
and
[

. We take the local Bump–Friedberg

to be

. For our purposes, it will often be convenient to write

in place of

and

Proposition 7.5
.

Let

(i)

(ii)

\int_{\mathrm{A}_{n}(F)}W^{\circ}_{\pi}(b)\exp(-d_{F}\pi\|b_{n}\|^{2})\left|b_{1}b_{3}\cdots b_{n-2}\right|^{s_{1}}\left|b_{2}b_{4}\cdots b_{n-1}\right|^{s_{2}-s_{1}}\delta^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\mathrm{B}_{n}(F)}(b)\,d^{\times}b.

Theorem 7.6
.

Let
\pi=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigoplus}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}}\displaylimits_{j=1}^{n}|\cdot|^{t_{j}}
be a spherical induced representation of Langlands type of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)

with spherical Whittaker function
W^{\circ}_{\pi}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi,\psi)
, and for
m=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
, let
\Phi_{\mathrm{ur}}\in\mathscr{S}(F^{m})
be given by (
5.1
). Then the Bump–Friedberg integral
B(s_{1},s_{2},W_{\pi}^{\circ},\Phi_{\mathrm{ur}})
is equal to

L(s_{1},s_{2},\pi,\mathrm{BF})\coloneqq L(s_{1},\pi)L(s_{2},\pi,\wedge^{2})=\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}\zeta_{F}(s_{1}+t_{\ell})\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\zeta_{F}(s_{2}+t_{j}+t_{k}).

Proof.

We prove this by induction. For the base case
n=2
, the Bump–Friedberg integral is

\int_{F^{\times}}\int_{F^{\times}}W_{\pi}^{\circ}\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}&0\\
0&a_{2}\end{pmatrix}\exp(-d_{F}\pi\|a_{2}\|^{2})|a_{1}|^{s_{1}-\frac{1}{2}}|a_{2}|^{s_{2}-s_{1}+\frac{1}{2}}\,d^{\times}a_{1}\,d^{\times}a_{2}.

From (

), this is equal to

\int_{F^{\times}}\int_{F^{\times}}\exp(-d_{F}\pi\|a_{2}\|^{2})|a_{1}|^{s_{1}+t_{1}}|a_{2}|^{s_{2}-s_{1}+t_{1}}\\
\times\int_{F^{\times}}\exp(-d_{F}\pi\|a^{\prime}a_{2}^{-1}\|^{2})\exp(-d_{F}\pi\|a^{\prime-1}a_{1}\|^{2})|a^{\prime}|^{t_{2}-t_{1}}\,d^{\times}a^{\prime}\,d^{\times}a_{1}\,d^{\times}a_{2}.

We interchange the order of integration and make the change of variables
a_{1}\mapsto a^{\prime}a_{1}
and
a^{\prime}\mapsto a^{\prime}a_{2}

8.1.
Global Rankin–Selberg Periods and the Petersson Inner Product

Given a Schwartz–Bruhat function
\Phi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{n})
, we may form the
\Theta
-series

\Theta_{\Phi}(a,g)\coloneqq\sum_{\xi\in F^{n}}\Phi(a\xi g)\quad\text{for $a\in\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}$ and $g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{F})$}.

Associated to this
\Theta
-series is an Eisenstein series, which is essentially the Mellin transform of
\Theta
. To be more precise, for a (unitary) Hecke character

\eta:F^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, we set

(8.2)

E(g,s;\Phi,\eta)\coloneqq\left|\det g\right|^{s}_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}\int_{F^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}}\Theta^{\prime}_{\Phi}(a,g)\eta(a)|a|^{ns}_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}\,d^{\times}a,

where
\Theta^{\prime}_{\Phi}(a,g)\coloneqq\Theta_{\Phi}(a,g)-\Phi(0)
. This is absolutely convergent for
\Re(s)>1
and extends to a meromorphic function of
s\in\mathbb{C}
. The Eisenstein series
E(g,s;\Phi,\eta)
is entire unless
\eta
is the trivial unitary Hecke character, in which case it has a simple pole at
s=1
with residue
[
32
, Lemma 4.2]

(8.3)

\frac{\vol(F^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}^{1}_{F})}{n}\widehat{\Phi}(0),

where the volume is taken with regards to the Tamagawa measure. Notably, the implicit constant denoted by
c
in
[
32
, Lemma 4.2]
is determined to be the volume of

in
[
58
, Proof of Proposition 3.1]
.

Proof of
Proposition
7.18
.

The proof is inspired by work of Gelbart, Jacquet, and Rogawski
[
18
, p. 185–186]
. The result follows from the global theory. To be more precise, we first observe that
\omega_{\pi_{v}}
must be trivial, as
(\pi_{v},V_{\pi_{v}})
is
\mathrm{H}_{m,m}(F_{v})
-distinguished. The global Bump–Friedberg integral
Z(s,2s,\varphi_{\pi},\Phi)
is Eulerian (cf.
[
43
, Theorem 4.4]
), so that it is equal, up to multiplication by a positive constant dependent only on the normalisation of the measures
dg
and
dg^{\prime}
, to

\prod_{v}B(s,W_{\varphi_{\pi},v},\Phi_{v}).

We may choose a factorisable Schwartz–Bruhat function
\Phi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{n})
such that
\Phi_{v}
is the characteristic function of
\mathcal{O}_{v}^{m}
for all nonarchimedean places
v\notin S
, and a factorisable cuspidal automorphic form
\varphi_{\pi}\in V_{\pi}
such that
W_{\varphi_{\pi},v}=W^{\circ}_{\pi_{v}}
with respect to the unramified character
\psi_{v}
for all nonarchimedean places
v\notin S
. Taking (
8.3
) into account, we see that by taking the residue of
Z(s,2s,\varphi_{\pi},\Phi)
at
s=1/2
,

(8.24)

\frac{\vol(F^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}^{1}_{F})}{m}\widehat{\Phi}(0)\int\limits_{[\mathrm{GL}_{m}\times\mathrm{GL}_{m}]}\varphi_{\pi}(J(g,g^{\prime}))\,dg\,dg^{\prime}\\
=\Res_{s=1/2}\Lambda^{S}(s,\pi,\mathrm{BF})\prod_{v\in S}B\left(\frac{1}{2},W_{\varphi_{\pi},v},\Phi_{v}\right).

We now consider
B(1/2,W_{\varphi_{\pi},v},\Phi_{v})
for each nonarchimedean place
v\in S
and each archimedean place
v
. On the one hand, we use the Iwasawa decomposition, and then evaluate the resulting integral over
\mathrm{N}_{m}(F_{v})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{m}(F_{v})\ni g_{v}
and
\mathrm{N}_{m}(F_{v})\backslash\mathrm{P}_{m}(F_{v})\ni p^{\prime}_{v}
, leading to the identity

B\left(\frac{1}{2},W_{\varphi_{\pi},v},\Phi_{v}\right)=\int_{K_{m,v}}\int_{F_{v}^{\times}}\vartheta_{v}^{\sharp}(\pi_{v}(J(1,k^{\prime}_{v}))W_{\varphi_{\pi},v})\Phi_{v}(z^{\prime}_{v}e_{m}k^{\prime}_{v})\left|z^{\prime}_{v}\right|^{m}_{v}\,d^{\times}z^{\prime}_{v}\,dk^{\prime}_{v}.

On the other had, via the Iwasawa decomposition once more,

\widehat{\Phi_{v}}(0)=\int_{F_{v}^{n}}\Phi_{v}(x_{v})\,dx_{v}=\int\limits_{\mathrm{P}_{m}(F_{v})\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{m}(F_{v})}\Phi_{v}(e_{m}g^{\prime}_{v})\,dg^{\prime}_{v}=\int_{K_{m,v}}\int_{F_{v}^{\times}}\Phi_{v}(z^{\prime}_{v}e_{m}k^{\prime}_{v})\left|z^{\prime}_{v}\right|^{m}_{v}\,d^{\times}z^{\prime}_{v}\,dk^{\prime}_{v}.

Comparing the integrals over
K_{m,v}\ni k^{\prime}_{v}
and
F_{v}^{\times}\ni z^{\prime}_{v}
of both sides of (
8.24
), there should be a linear functional
\gamma^{\sharp}
on the Whittaker model
\mathcal{W}(\pi_{v},\psi_{v})
such that

\int_{K_{m,v}}\int_{F_{v}^{\times}}\vartheta_{v}^{\sharp}(\pi_{v}(J(1,k^{\prime}_{v}))W_{\varphi_{\pi},v})\Phi_{v}(z^{\prime}_{v}e_{m}k^{\prime}_{v})\left|z^{\prime}_{v}\right|^{m}_{v}\,d^{\times}z^{\prime}_{v}\,dk^{\prime}_{v}\\
=\gamma_{v}^{\sharp}(W_{\varphi_{\pi},v})\int_{K_{v}}\int_{F_{v}^{\times}}\Phi_{v}(z^{\prime}_{v}e_{m}k^{\prime}_{v})\left|z^{\prime}_{v}\right|^{m}_{v}\,d^{\times}z^{\prime}_{v}\,dk^{\prime}_{v}

for any Whittaker function
W_{\varphi_{\pi},v}\in\mathcal{W}(\pi_{v},\psi_{v})
and any Schwartz–Bruhat function
\Phi_{v}\in\mathscr{S}(F_{v}^{m})
. Each
\Phi_{v}\in\mathscr{S}(F^{m}_{v})
defines a smooth function on
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F_{v})
, left invariant by
\mathrm{P}_{m}(F_{v})
, via
g\mapsto\Phi(e_{m}g)
. This implies that any smooth function
f

K_{m,v}
that is invariant under
\mathrm{P}_{(m-1,1)}(F_{v})\cap K_{m,v}

\int_{K_{m,v}}\int_{F_{v}^{\times}}\vartheta_{v}^{\sharp}(\pi_{v}(J(1,k^{\prime}_{v}))W_{\varphi_{\pi},v})f(k^{\prime}_{v})\left|z^{\prime}_{v}\right|^{m}_{v}\,d^{\times}z^{\prime}_{v}\,dk^{\prime}_{v}=\gamma_{v}^{\sharp}(W_{\varphi_{\pi},v})\int_{K_{m,v}}f(k^{\prime}_{v})\,dk^{\prime}_{v}.

We can move one step further to claim that the same relation holds for all smooth functions
f
on
K_{m,v}
, as
\vartheta_{v}^{\sharp}
is a
\mathrm{P}_{2m}(F_{v})\cap\mathrm{H}_{m,m}(F_{v})
-invariant form. It follows that
\gamma^{\sharp}_{v}=\theta^{\sharp}_{v}
and that
\vartheta_{v}^{\sharp}
is invariant under

\vartheta_{v}^{\sharp}
is invariant under
\mathrm{H}_{m,m}(F_{v})

We only deal with the case when

Remark 8.28

(Jacquet–Shalika Periods)
.

We let
\mathcal{M}_{m}(F)
denote the set of
m\times m
matrices and
\mathcal{N}_{m}(F)
denote the subset of upper triangular matrices. The Shalika subgroup
\mathrm{S}_{2m}(F)
is defined to be

\mathrm{S}_{2m}(F)\coloneqq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1_{m}&X\\

We fix a nontrivial Shalika character
\Theta

\mathrm{S}_{2m}(F)
such that

\Theta\left(\begin{pmatrix}1_{m}&X\\
0&1_{m}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}g&0\\

We say that a cuspidal automorphic representation
(\pi,V_{\pi})
of
\mathrm{GL}_{2m}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
admits a

(or
(\mathrm{S}_{2m}(\mathbb{A}_{F}),\Theta)
-period)
[
12
, (11)]

\varphi_{\pi}\in V_{\pi}
for which

\int_{[\mathrm{GL}_{m}]}\int\limits_{\mathcal{M}_{m}(F)\backslash\mathcal{M}_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}\varphi_{\pi}\left(\begin{pmatrix}1_{m}&X\\
0&1_{m}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}g&0\\
0&g\end{pmatrix}\right)\overline{\psi}(\Tr X)\,dX\,dg\neq 0.

Such a representation is referred to be
(\mathrm{S}_{2m}(\mathbb{A}_{F}),\Theta)
-distinguished
. The celebrated result of Jacquet and Shalika
[
34
]
shows that the partial global exterior square
L
-function

has a pole at
s=1
if and only if
\omega_{\pi}
is trivial and
(\pi,V_{\pi})
admits a
(\mathrm{S}_{2m}(\mathbb{A}_{F}),\Theta)
-period. For the rest of the discussion, we assume that
(\pi,V_{\pi})
is unitary such that
(\pi,V_{\pi})
affords the
(\mathrm{S}_{2m}(\mathbb{A}_{F}),\Theta)
-period. We analogously define a local Shalika functional
\vartheta_{v}^{\dagger}:\mathcal{W}(\pi_{v},\psi_{v})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

\vartheta_{v}^{\dagger}(W_{\pi_{v}})\coloneqq\int\limits_{\mathrm{N}_{m}(F_{v})\backslash\mathrm{P}_{m}(F_{v})}\int\limits_{\mathcal{N}_{m}(F_{v})\backslash\mathcal{M}_{m}(F_{v})}W_{\pi_{v}}\left(w_{m,m}\begin{pmatrix}1_{m}&X_{v}\\

0&p_{v}\end{pmatrix}\right)\overline{\psi}(\Tr X_{v})\,dX_{v}\,dp_{v}.

The linear functional
\vartheta_{v}^{\dagger}
is
a priori
(
\mathrm{P}_{2m}(F_{v})\cap\mathrm{S}_{2m}(F_{v}),\Theta_{v})

-quasi-invariant functional (cf. the proof of
Proposition
7.18
and
[
12

34

12

can be deduced, with some changes of notations, just as in the proof of

8.23

\int_{[\mathrm{GL}_{m}]}\int\limits_{\mathcal{M}_{m}(F)\backslash\mathcal{M}_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}\varphi_{\pi}\left(\begin{pmatrix}1_{m}&X\\
0&1_{m}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}g&0\\
0&g\end{pmatrix}\right)\overline{\psi}(\Tr X)\,dX\,dg

is equal, up to multiplication by a positive constant dependent only on the normalisation of the measures

and

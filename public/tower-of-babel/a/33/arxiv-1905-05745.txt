Introduction

One motivation for definability questions (such as determining a universal definition of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)

\Z

\Q

\Q

\Z
in
\Q
, the other is via H10 over other rings and fields. The former approach is useful as follows: if one had an existential definition of
\Z
in
\Q

\mbox{Th}_{\exists}(\Q)
follows from the undecidability of
\mbox{Th}_{\exists}(\Z)
. The latter approach is more philosophical; if one understood the behaviour of H10 over different rings and fields, one could possibly gain a deeper insight into the problem and use this to solve H10 over
\Q
, and go further with further generalisations such as H10 over
K
or
\mathcal{O}_{K}
where
K
is a number field.

This paper lies in the middle between these two paths. A significant addition to the definability approach of solving H10 over
\Q
was made in 2016 by Koenigsmann
[
8
]
, who provided a universal definition (later improved by Daans
[
1
,
2
]
) and a
\forall\exists
-definition of
\Z
in
\Q

one
universal quantifier. For the latter more philosophical path it is worth noting that both
\mbox{Th}_{\exists}(\mathbb{F}_{q}[t])
and
\mbox{Th}_{\exists}(\mathbb{F}_{q}(t))
are undecidable (in
\mathcal{L}_{\mbox{\tiny rings},t}
)
[
4
,
11
,
14
]
which seems almost to suggest (by the
function field analogy
) that a more complete understanding of H10 made in the function field context would be useful for determining H10 over
\Q
. This paper is not the first to explore definability questions in function fields; Eisenträger & Morrison
[
6
]
adapted Park’s
[
10
]
universal definition of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
in
K
from number fields to function fields, and this definition was vastly simplified by Daans
[

]
who in fact provided a universal definition of the ring of

Let

Lemma 2.5

Given a prime

denote

\displaystyle\exists c,d\Big(

“
c
and
d
are of opposite parity in degree”

\displaystyle\land\Big[\big\{\phi(c)\land a=zc\land b^{q-1}=d^{q-1}\big\}\lor\big\{\phi(d)\land b=zd\land a^{q-1}=c^{q-1}\big\}\Big]\Big).

Then
\psi(a,b)
is equivalent to an existential formula.

Proof.
For
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
, consider
\phi(c)
: “
c
as an element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}\!\left(\!\left({}^{1}{\mskip-5.0mu/\mskip-3.0mu}_{t}\right)\!\right)
is a square”. Quantifying over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
, this is captured by

\displaystyle\exists f\Big(\deg(c)=\deg(f^{2})\land\exists g\big(\deg(c)>\deg(g)\land c=f^{2}+g\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mbox{ }\exists f\big(\deg(c)=\deg(f^{2})\land\deg(c)\geq\deg(t(c-f^{2}))\big)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mbox{ }\exists f\big(\deg(f^{2})\geq\deg(t(c-f^{2}))\big)

What if we additionally wanted to say “and
d
is of odd degree”? This would be

\displaystyle\mbox{\hskip 3.27205pt}\exists f\big(\deg(f^{2})\geq\deg(t(c-f^{2}))\big)\land\exists h\big(\deg(f^{2})=\deg(th^{2}d)\big)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\exists f,h\big(\deg(th^{2}d)\geq\deg(t(c-f^{2}))\big)\land\exists g\big(\deg(f^{2})>\deg(g)\land f^{2}=k\cdot th^{2}d+g\big)

\displaystyle\mbox{ for some }k\in\mathbb{F}_{q},

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\exists f,h\big(\deg(th^{2}d)\geq\deg(t(c-f^{2}))\land\deg(f^{2})\geq\deg(t(f^{2}-k\cdot th^{2}d))\big)

\displaystyle\mbox{ for some }k\in\mathbb{F}_{q}.

Let
\chi(c,d)
denote

\displaystyle\exists f,h\big(\deg(th^{2}d)\geq\deg(t(c-f^{2}))\land\big\{

\displaystyle\deg(f^{2})\geq\deg(t(f^{2}-th^{2}d))

\displaystyle\lor\deg(f^{2})\geq\deg(t(f^{2}-z\cdot th^{2}d))\big\}\big).

Then, by the above argument and
Lemma
2.1
, “the degree of
c
is even, the degree of
d
is odd, and the leading coefficient of
c
is a square” is represented by this formula. Therefore
\psi(a,b)
is equivalent to

(1)

\chi(za,b)\lor\chi(zb,a).

The formula “
\deg(A)\geq\deg(B)

5

, the set
\{z\in K\mbox{ : }v_{\p}(z)\geq 0\}

is indeed equivalent to an existential formula and moreover requires

).

Corollary 2.9
.

There is a universal definition of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
given by 89 quantifiers.

Proof.
By
Theorem
2.7
, we have

(2)

f(t)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]\cup\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]\right)_{\infty}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\forall a,b\left((a,b)\not\in D\lor f(t)\in\widetilde{R_{a,b}}\right).

By
[
6
, Lemma 3.19]
,
\widetilde{R_{a,b}}
is universally defined, hence as
D
is existentially defined, (
2
) is indeed a universal formula for
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]\cup\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]\right)_{\infty}
. Denote this formula by
\Phi
. Recall that the number of quantifiers needed to define
\widetilde{R_{a,b}}
is one more than is required to define its Jacobson radical. By
[
10
, Lemma 3.17]
,
J(\widetilde{R_{a,b}})
has an explicit description of

\displaystyle\{0\}\cup\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)^{\times}\mbox{ : }\exists y_{1},y_{2}\mbox{ s.t. }

\displaystyle y_{1},x-y_{1}\in a\cdot\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)^{2}\cdot T_{a,b}^{\times}\cap(1-\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)^{2}\cdot T_{a,b}^{\times})

\displaystyle y_{2},x-y_{2}\in b\cdot\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)^{2}\cdot T_{a,b}^{\times}\cap(1-\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)^{2}\cdot T_{a,b}^{\times})\},

where
T_{a,b}=S_{a,b}+S_{a,b}
and

S_{a,b}=\{2x_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)\mbox{ : }\exists x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\mbox{ s.t. }x_{1}^{2}-ax_{2}^{2}-bx_{3}^{2}+abx_{4}^{2}=1\}.

The set
S_{a,b}
requires three quantifiers to define, hence
T_{a,b}
is defined by 7 quantifiers, as is
T^{\times}_{a,b}
(by
[
2
, Lemma 4.3]
). Thus
J(\widetilde{R_{a,b}})
requires at most 66 quantifiers to define it. Thus the number of universal quantifiers need to define
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]\cup\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]\right)_{\infty}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
using (
2
) is at most
2+20+66+1=89
.

What about the definition of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
? This is simply

f(t)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\Phi(f(t))\land\left(\deg(f(t))>0\lor f(t)^{q}=f(t)\right).

Note that “
\deg(f)>0
” is universally defined by 9 quantifiers (
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
) and thus
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
is universally defined in
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
by
\max\{89,9\}=89
quantifiers, as required.
\hfill\blacksquare

Corollary 2.10
.

There is a universal
\emptyset
-definition of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
given by 90 quantifiers.

Proof.
In the universal definition presented in
Corollary
2.9
, there are three places parameters are in use: the nonsquare

, in
\widetilde{R_{a,b}}
, and in all statements about degree. Examination of
[
6
, Lemma 3.19]
reveals
\widetilde{R_{a,b}}
is defined without use of parameters other than

and

without parameters we can define elements of

Abstract
. We study infinite groups interpretable in three families of valued fields: V V -minimal, power bounded T T -convex, and p p -adically closed fields. We show that every such group G G has unbounded exponent and that if G G is dp-minimal then it is abelian-by-finite. Along the way, we associate with any infinite interpretable group an infinite type-definable subgroup which is definably isomorphic to a group in one of four distinguished sorts: the underlying valued field K K , its residue field k (when infinite), its value group Γ \Gamma , or K / 𝒪 K/{\mathcal{O}} , where 𝒪 {\mathcal{O}} is the valuation ring. Our work uses and extends techniques developed in [ 11 ] to circumvent elimination of imaginaries.

When – in the above notation –

was a field, we were able to eliminate the discrete sorts

and
\Gamma
in the

-adically closed case (in fact, there this was done for any P-minimal field), and the remaining distinguished sorts turned out to be rather tame topological structures (that we called SW-uniformities – to be described in more detail below). In that setting (and for

a field) we were able to strengthen Fact

to obtain a definable bijection between

and a subset of one of the distinguished sorts. In the present setting some extra work is needed to obtain a weaker result, i.e. a finite-to-one function from an infinite definable subset of our group

into one of the distinguished sorts. Extending our terminology from
[
11
]
we define:

Definition 2.6
.

A definable set

is
A
-almost strongly internal to a definable set

if there exists an
A
-definable finite-to-one function

, for some

. The set

is
locally almost strongly internal to

if there exists a parameter set

and an infinite

-definable

that is

-almost strongly internal to

.

Recall that

is
strongly internal
to

if we can find an injective

and

to

if some definable infinite

. As we shall see in Example

, a set which is locally almost strongly internal to

. The upshot of this situation is that we have to develop the theory, in parallel, for subsets of

almost strongly internal to a distinguished sort

, and for subsets of

strongly internal (possibly to the same sort

). Since the statements and the proofs are, as a rule, similar in both cases we usually state both results simultaneously; e.g.

“If

with the convention that either all brackets are included or all brackets are omitted. I.e., the two statements included in the above formulation are “If

2.3.
Simon-Walsberg uniformities and group topologies

Though the setting of the present paper requires that we take into account the discrete sorts
\Gamma
and
K/{\mathcal{O}}
, in
p
-adically closed fields, SW-uniformities still have a significant role to play, and even in these two cases some arguments are modelled after the analogous arguments in the topological setting. So we remind:

Definition 2.7
.

A definable set
D
in a multi-sorted structure
{\mathcal{M}}
is an SW-uniformity if:

(1)

D
is dp-minimal.

(2)

D
has a definable uniform structure (or, uniformity) giving rise to a Hausdorff topology (see
[
9
, IX.11]
). I.e., there exists a formula
\varphi(x,y,z)
and a definable set
S
in
{\mathcal{M}}
such that
\{\varphi(D^{2},s):s\in S\}
is a uniform structure on
D
, and the intersection of all
\varphi(D^{2},s)
, for
s\in S
, is the diagonal.

(3)

Every definable subset of
D
has non-empty interior with respect to the uniform topology.

(4)

D
has no isolated points in the uniform topology.

As we already noted, the sorts
K
in all settings,
\Gamma
and
K/{\mathcal{O}}
in the power bounded T-convex and V-minimal settings and
k
in the power bounded
T
-convex setting, are all SW uniformities (the fact that
K/{\mathcal{O}}
is not stably embedded is not a problem since the definition allows for that).

One of the most important tools in
[
11
]
was the technical fact below. We shall prove here an a analogue of this result in other settings as well, and eventually include a variant of it as one of the axioms of our
ad hoc
setting of vicinic sorts.

Fact 2.8

(
[
11
, Corollary 3.13]
)
.

Let
D
be an SW-uniformity and
X\subseteq D^{n}
,
Y\subseteq X
be definable sets. Let
b\in D^{k}
be any element,
a\in Y
in the relative interior of
Y
in
X
and
A
a small set of parameters. Then there exist
B\supseteq A
and a
B
-definable open subset
U=U_{1}\times\dots\times U_{n}\subseteq D^{n}
such that
a\in U\cap X\subseteq Y
and
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a,b/B)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a,b/A)
.

We also require the following result (implicit in
[
11
]
) that plays a key role in our definition of
D
-groups, the main object of study of Section
4
and Section
5
:

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be some structure and let
D
be an SW-uniformity definable in
{\mathcal{M}}
,
W,Z
definable sets, and
f:Z\to W
a definable finite-to-one surjection.

(1)

If
Z
is strongly internal to
D
then there exists
a definable
W_{1}\subseteq W
, with
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(W_{1})=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(W)
such that
W_{1}
is strongly internal to
D
.

(2)

If
Z
is almost strongly internal to
D
then
there exists a definable
W_{1}\subseteq W
,
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(W_{1})=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(W)
, such that
W_{1}
is almost strongly internal to
D
.

Proof.

Assume everything is definable over some parameter set
A
.

(1) Since
Z
is strongly internal to
D
we may assume that
Z\subseteq D^{k}
for some
k
. We fix
z_{0}\in Z
such that
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(z_{0}/A)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(Z)
. We first find a definable open set
V\subseteq D^{k}
such that
|[z_{0}]_{f}\cap V|=1
, where
[z_{0}]_{f}=f^{-1}(f(z_{0}))
.
By Fact
2.8
there exists an open set
U
,
z_{0}\in U\subseteq V
, definable over some
B\supseteq A
, such that
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(z_{0}/A)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(Z)
, and
[z_{0}]_{f}\cap U=\{z_{0}\}
. But now, the set
Z_{1}=\{z\in Z:[z]_{f}\cap U=\{z\}\}
is defined over

and contains
z_{0}
hence
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(Z_{1})=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(Z)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(W)
. Now
f\restriction Z_{1}
is injective, and its image
W_{1}
is our desired set.

(2) Since
Z
is almost strongly internal to
D
there exists
\sigma:Z\to D^{k}
with finite fibres. Let

C=\{(w,y)\in W\times D^{k}:y\in\sigma(f^{-1}(w))\};

it is a finite-to-finite correspondence. Choose
y_{0}\in\pi_{2}(C)
(the projection of

into

) with
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(y_{0}/A)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(\pi_{2}(C))
. Since

is finite-to-finite the set of all

such that
C^{y}\cap C^{y_{0}}\neq\emptyset
is finite, so there exists an open

such that for all

,
C^{y}\cap C^{y_{0}}=\emptyset
.

As above, we may replace

with an open
U
defined over

such that
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(y_{0}/B)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(y_{0}/A)
. Then

is a graph of a finite-to-one function,

. Since

is in the range of that function and
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(y_{0}/A)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(W)
, necessarily
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(\dom(F))\geq\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(W)
, so equality must hold, as required.
∎

Much of Section

is dedicated to making sense of the above results for

in

-adically closed fields and proving an appropriate analogue. Similar results for

-groups are easier and follow from known properties of such groups.

When

is locally strongly internal to an SW-uniformity, the group

we obtain in Theorem

admits a group topology. This will allow us to topologise

, using the following two easy observations.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
G
be a group and
H
a subgroup endowed with a Hausdorff group topology, with
\mathcal{B}_{H}
a basis for the
e
neighbourhoods in
H
. For
V\in\mathcal{B}_{H}
, let
U_{V}=\{(x,y)\in G\times G:x^{-1}y\in V\}
.

Then the collection
\mathcal{U}^{G}=\{U_{V}:V\in\mathcal{B}_{H}\}
is a left-invariant uniformity on
G
extending the associated uniformity on
H
.

The uniformity
\mathcal{U}^{G}
induces a topology on
G
, call it
\tau^{G}
, whose basis is the collection of all sets
U_{V}(h,G)
, as
h
varies in
G
and
V
varies in
\mathcal{B}_{H}
. Though this need not in general be a group topology, for every
g\in G
the map
x\mapsto gx
is continuous. We can show, however:

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
G
be a group and
H\leq G
a topological group such that,

(i)

For every
g\in G
there is an open
V\subseteq H
such that
V\subseteq H^{g}\cap H
.

(ii)

For every
g\in G
, the function
x\mapsto x^{g}=gxg^{-1}
, from
H
into
H^{g}
, is continuous at
e
with respect to the topology on
H
(this makes sense due to (i)).

Then the uniformity
\mathcal{U}^{G}
defined above makes
G
a topological group.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}_{H}
denote a neighbourhood base for
H
at
e
. We may assume that each
W\in{\mathcal{B}}_{H}
is symmetric. Note also that by Lemma
2.10
for each
g\in G
, the family
\{gV:V\in{\mathcal{B}}_{H}\}
is a basis for the
\tau^{G}
-neighbourhoods at
g
.

We prove first that group inverse is continuous.
Assume that
g^{-1}V
is an open neighbourhood of
g^{-1}
. We need to find an open neighbourhood
gW
of
g
such that
W^{-1}g^{-1}\subseteq g^{-1}V
. By our assumptions, the map
x\mapsto gxg^{-1}
is continuous at
e
and hence there is
W\in{\mathcal{B}}_{H}
(in particular
W^{-1}=W
), such that
gWg^{-1}\subseteq V
. It follows that
Wg^{-1}\subseteq g^{-1}V
.

To prove continuity of multiplication let
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
, and assume that
g=g_{1}g_{2}
, and
gV
is a basic neighbourhood of
g
and fix some
V^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{H}
such that
V^{\prime}\subseteq H^{g}\cap H
, as provided by (i). Using the continuity at

of

, we can shrink

so that
V^{\prime}\subseteq V^{g}
. Using the continuity of

and the fact that

is a topological group, we can find

, such that

(g_{1}W_{1}g_{1}^{-1})(gW_{2}g^{-1})\subseteq V^{\prime}.

It follows that

g_{1}W_{1}g_{2}W_{2}g^{-1}\subseteq gVg^{-1}
, and hence
g_{1}W_{1}g_{2}W_{2}\subseteq gV
, as needed. Thus
\mathcal{U}^{G}
gives rise to a group topology.
∎

3.
Some results on
p
-adically closed fields

An important tool in
[
11
]
is the analysis of interpretable fields via subsets that are strongly internal to SW-uniform sorts. Such an analysis is not available to us when studying interpretable groups in a

-adically closed field

, as neither

nor

are SW-uniform sorts. In the present section we endow

with a topology – neither definable nor Hausdorff – where, nonetheless, many of the tools available in SW-uniformities can still be applied.

We prove two geometric properties that play a crucial role in the sequel. The first, comprising the heart of Section
3.1
, is an analogue of Fact
2.8
, that can be interpreted as asserting the existence of generic neighbourhoods (of generic points) with respect to the above topology. This is formulated precisely in Proposition
3.8
(3). Using this result we then proceed to proving an analogue of Lemma

. Section

is dedicated to the second main property that we need: local linearity of definable functions in

, culminating in Corollary

. The strategy of proof is similar to that carried out in
[
11
, §6.2]
, using results on 1-h-minimal fields, but working in mixed characteristic requires a little more care.

While the results of Section
3.2
may be of independent interest, the results of Section

are more technical. Readers unfamiliar with the analogous results from
[
11
]
may wish to first read Section
4.1
for a more detailed discussion of the underlying geometric motivation.

From this point up to Section
4
,
{\mathcal{K}}:=(K,+,\cdot,v)
is a
p
-adically closed field
. We note, however, that the results of Section
3.1
hold in any
P
-minimal field. For Section

we need, in addition, 1-h-minimality and definable Skolem functions (as discussed in the introduction).

We remind that
p
-adically closed fields are elementarily equivalent (in the Macintyre language) to a finite extension, denoted
\mathbb{F}
, of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. We work in a large saturated
p
-adically closed field,
{\mathcal{K}}=(K,v)
, and so we may assume that
(K,v)
is an elementary extension
of
(\mathbb{F},v)
. We add constants for all elements of
\mathbb{F}
; since its value group
\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
as an ordered abelian group for simplicity of notation we identify
\Gamma_{\mathbb{F}}
with
\mathbb{Z}
, and note that
\textbf{k}_{K}=\textbf{k}_{\mathbb{F}}
. It is well known that, as an abelian group,
\mathbb{F}/\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}
is isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}(p^{\infty})
, where
\mathbb{Z}(p^{\infty})
is the Prüfer

-group and

. Throughout, we will use without further reference the fact that

is well-defined on

and denote
v(0)=\infty
(for

). As usual

extends to

by setting
v(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=\min\{v(a_{i}):i=1,\dots,n\}.

Fact 3.1
.

(1)

For every
a\in K
with
v(a)\in\mathbb{Z}
, there is an element
r\in\mathbb{F}
with
v(a-r)>\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

For every
b\in K/{\mathcal{O}}
, if
v(b)\in\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}
then
b\in\mathbb{F}/{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}}\leq K/{\mathcal{O}}
.

(3)

For all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there are only finitely many
b\in K/{\mathcal{O}}
such that
nb=0
; in fact every such

is in
\mathbb{F}/\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}}
.

Proof.

(1) This follows from the completeness of
\mathbb{F}
as a valued field. Indeed, for
t\in\mathbb{F}
with
v(at)=0
we can find

with
v(at-c)>0
thus
v(a-ct^{-1})>-v(t)
; continuing inductively we construct a pseudo-Cauchy sequence in

, converging, by completeness,
to a limit in

. This limit has the desired properties.

(2) Every

with

is of the form

for some

with

. By (1) there is

with

, so

as required.

(3) It suffices to show that if

then

and

, for some

.
∎

3.1.
Local analysis in
K/{\mathcal{O}}

As noted above, the valuation on
K
is well-defined on

, but the topology it induces on

is discrete. For that reason we introduce a coarser (non-Hausdorff) topology retaining some properties of SW-uniformities. The main technical property we need regarding this topology is the existence of generic neighbourhoods (Proposition
3.8
), but we also take the opportunity to single out several other properties that will be of use in the sequel. As an application we obtain Corollary
3.10
on the torsion of definable subgroups of
K/{\mathcal{O}}
, that is used in the sequel, but may be of independent interest. We first need some preliminaries:

Definition 3.2
.

A ball
B\subseteq K^{n}
is

if
r(B)
, the valuative radius of
B
, satisfies
r(B)<\mathbb{Z}
.

Let
\pi:K\to K/{\mathcal{O}}
be the quotient map, we also write
\pi:K^{n}\to(K/{\mathcal{O}})^{n}
for any integer

.

Definition 3.3
.

A

U\subseteq(K/{\mathcal{O}})^{n}
is the image under

of a large ball in

.

We consider the

on
(K/{\mathcal{O}})^{n}
whose basis is the family of balls. Thus, for

, this is the product topology on
(K/{\mathcal{O}})^{n}
.

Remark 3.4
.

The ball topology on

is not Hausdorff, in fact, not even

: for

, if

then every open set containing

also contains

. In addition, the ball topology has no definable basis (since the set of balls of infinite radius in

is not definable),

Below, all topological notions in

and

refer to the ball topology.
Despite the above remark, some amount of local analysis in

is possible using the lemma below. For

be the maximal

of

is algebraically independent and

(this is well-defined even when

Lemma 3.5
.

Let

be a tuple of elements in

and

\dim_{\acl}(a/A)=n
. Then for any
\gamma_{0}<\gamma_{1}<\mathbb{Z}

\dim_{\acl}(a/A\gamma)=n

By passing to a sub-tuple of

.

By replacing

with

,
\gamma_{0}<\gamma<\mathbb{Z}

.

For

\hat{a}_{i}:=(a_{1},\dots,a_{i-1},a_{i+1},\dots,a_{n})

below

. We now consider all formulas

By compactness, there are formulas

To conveniently apply the result of the last lemma, we make the following definition:

Definition 3.19

We now turn to proving that definable functions on

-definable (recall that

in some open ball around

.

By assumption

for some

, there exists

and

.

Definition 3.24

A set

Remark 3.25
.

It is easy to see that a set

has minimal fibres if and only if
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(S)=k
and there exists a coordinate projection
\pi:S\to(K/{\mathcal{O}})^{k}

\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(\pi^{-1}(\pi(S^{\prime})))\setminus S^{\prime})<\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(S)

Notice that if some

of rank

has minimal fibres with respect to the same projection. Indeed, we just choose
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
with the property that the fibres of

are of minimal size among all definable subsets of

Lemma 3.26
.

Let

A

Let

and set

By Proposition

, there exists a large ball

has finite fibres and as

satisfying that

For any function

.

Let
X\subseteq(K/{\mathcal{O}})^{r}
be

-definable with minimal fibres,
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(X)=n
. Let

-definable ball

-definable subgroup

By Lemma

and permuting the coordinates, we may assume that the fibres of the projection

with a subgroup of

Let

,

is

to find some ball

The next example shows that a group almost locally strongly internal to a distinguished sort
D
need not be strongly internal to it. This distinction, that apparently can not be avoided, will have to be taken into account in our work here:

Example 3.33
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a residual quadratic extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and let
{\mathcal{K}}\succ\mathbb{F}
be a sufficiently saturated extension. Let
C_{p}\leq K/{\mathcal{O}}
be a cyclic subgroup of order
p
, and
G:=(K/{\mathcal{O}})/C_{p}
. Then
G
is locally almost strongly internal to
K/{\mathcal{O}}
but not locally strongly internal to
K/{\mathcal{O}}
.

On the other hand, letting
H=\{x\in G:px=0\}
the group
G/H
is strongly internal to
K/{\mathcal{O}}
.

Proof.

Since the quotient map
\pi:K/{\mathcal{O}}\to G
is finite-to-one it follows directly from Lemma
3.9
(2) that
G
is almost strongly internal to
K/{\mathcal{O}}
.

We now verify that
G
is not locally strongly internal to
K/{\mathcal{O}}
. Assume towards a contradiction that there exists a definable injection
f:X\to(K/{\mathcal{O}})^{r}
with
X
infinite. Let
V=\pi^{-1}(X)
and
\widehat{f}:V\to(K/{\mathcal{O}})^{r}
the (clearly, definable) lifting of
f
to
K/{\mathcal{O}}
. As
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(V)=1
, we may assume – shrinking
V
if needed – that
V
is an open ball. Assume everything is definable over
\emptyset
. Let
a\in V
with
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a)=1
. Shrinking
V
further, we may apply Proposition
3.21
, to deduce that
\widehat{f}:=(\widehat{f}_{1},\dots,\widehat{f}_{r})
where each
\widehat{f}_{i}
is of the form
L_{i}(x-a)+\widehat{f}_{i}(a)
on
V
, for some scalar endomorphism,
L_{i}
.
By Fact
3.1
(3) and Lemma
3.10
,
V+C_{p}=V
. Because
\widehat{f}
factors through
C_{p}
on
V
, it follows that the scalar endomorphisms
L_{i}
are all invariant under
C_{p}
, and therefore
C_{p}\subseteq\ker(L_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,r
.

Since
\mathbb{F}
is a residual extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, the subgroup
H=\{x\in K/{\mathcal{O}}:v(x)\geq-1\}
, where
1\in\Gamma
is the minimal positive element, is readily seen to contain
C_{p}
, and
|H|=|\textbf{k}_{K}|=p^{2}
. It is also easy to verify that for every scalar endomorphism
\lambda
of
K/{\mathcal{O}}
with non-trivial kernel, we have
H\subseteq\ker(\lambda)
, so
C_{p}\subsetneq H\subseteq\ker(L_{i})
for every
L_{i}
.

Thus, the function
\widehat{f}
is invariant under the group
H\supseteq C_{p}
,
contradicting our assumption that
f
was injective.

For the final statement, since
\mathbb{F}/{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}(p^{\infty})\oplus\mathbb{Z}(p^{\infty})
,
G/H
is definably isomorphic to
K/{\mathcal{O}}
as witnessed by the map
x\mapsto px
.
∎

Remark 3.34
.

It can be shown that if
\mathbb{F}
is a residual quadratic extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, as above, and
H\subseteq\mathbb{F}/{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}}
is a finite subgroup, then
(\mathbb{F}/{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}})/H
is abstractly isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}/{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{F}}
. However, the above example shows that a definable isomorphism need not exist.

In contrast, it can be shown that for every finite subgroup
H\subseteq\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, the group
(\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})/H
is definably isomorphic to
\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Furthermore, this remains true in elementary extensions of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

4.
Vicinic sorts and their properties

In the present section we develop an
ad hoc
axiomatic setting allowing us to unify the treatment of all unstable distinguished sorts considered in the sequel. We then apply the technical tools we develop to show that, given a group
G
almost strongly internal to such a sort
D
, there exists a finite normal subgroup
H\trianglelefteq G
such that
G/H
is strongly internal to
D
and furthermore, it is “a
D
-group” (to be defined below). This suffices for the construction of an infinitesimal subgroup in
G/H
.

Throughout Section
4
we let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a multi-sorted,
|T|^{+}
-saturated structure and
D
a dp-minimal definable set.

4.1.
The key geometric properties

To motivate our axiomatic setup, we isolate key geometric properties shared by SW-uniformities,
{\mathbb{Z}}
-groups and
K/{\mathcal{O}}
, for
{\mathcal{K}}
a
p
-adically closed field. Those are, in the latter setting, Corollary
3.7
(asserting that dp-rank is the same as
\acl(\cdot)
-dimension) and Proposition
3.8
(asserting the existence of generic neighbourhoods). These two properties constitute our axiomatisation of vicinic sorts (Section
4.2
).

An important property shared by stable groups, groups definable in o-minimal structures and other tame settings is that the product of two independent generic elements of the group is itself generic. It turns out that a variant of this property can be deduced, in the
p
-adically closed case, from Lemma
3.9
(2). Statements analogous to Lemma
3.9
(2) (Lemma
2.9
for SW-uniformities, Lemma
4.3
for
{\mathbb{Z}}
-groups) imply the same property of groups in the remaining cases covered by our setting. This geometric property of groups is encapsulated in our notion of
D
-groups (Section
4.5
)

In all of our settings, the induced structure on
K/{\mathcal{O}}
does not satisfy Exchange (there are definable locally constant functions with infinite image). Nevertheless, the fact below, together with its analogue, Corollary
3.7
, for
K/{\mathcal{O}}
in the
p
-adic case, give dp-rank enough geometric flavour to get us going in all the unstable distinguished sorts:

Fact 4.1
.

If
D
is an SW-uniformity (in some structure) or a stably embedded
{\mathbb{Z}}
-group then
\dim_{\acl}(a/A)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a/A)
for all
a\in D^{n}
and any parameter set
A
.

Proof.

For SW-uniformities this follows from
[
34
, Propsition 2.4]
noting that by Simon’s work dp-rank is local in dp-minimal theories,
[
32
, Theorem 0.3]
. If
D
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-group, then this follows from
[
32
, Theorem 0.3]
since
D
satisfies exchange.
∎

The existence of generic neighbourhoods, originally proved for SW-uniformities (Fact
2.8
), and extended above to
K/{\mathcal{O}}
in the
p
-adically closed setting (Proposition
3.8
), is crucial for carrying out local analysis at a generic point. The following may be viewed as the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-group analogue:

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(D,+,<)
be a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-group. Let
A
be any set of parameters,
g\in D^{n}
,
h\in D^{m}
. For any set of parameters
B
and
B
-definable
X\subseteq D^{n}
, if
g\in X
and
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(g/B)=n
then there exists
C\supseteq A
and a
C
-definable set
X_{1}\subseteq X
such that
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(X_{1})=n
,
g\in X_{1}
and
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(g,h/A)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(g,h/C)
.

Proof.

By
[
11
, Lemma 3.14(2)]
, we can find a small model
L\supseteq B
for which
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(g/L)=n
. By
[
24
, Lemma 3.4]
, there exists an
L
-definable subset
X_{0}\subseteq X
containing
g=(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})
of the form
I_{1}\times\dots\times I_{n}
, where for each
i\leq n
,
g_{i}\in I_{i}=\{\alpha_{i}\leq x\leq\beta_{i}:x\equiv_{N_{i}}c_{i}\}
, for some
N_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
, where both intervals
[\alpha_{i},g_{i}]
and
[g_{i},\beta_{i}]
are infinite and
0\leq c_{i}<N_{i}
.

For simplicity, we prove the result for
n=1
. The general case follows by induction. We use compactness to find
\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{1}^{\prime}<g_{1}
such that
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(\alpha_{1}^{\prime}/g_{1},hL)=1
and
[\alpha_{1}^{\prime},g_{1}]
infinite and then
g_{1}<\beta_{1}^{\prime}<\beta_{1}
such that
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(\beta_{1}^{\prime}/g_{1},h,\alpha_{1}^{\prime}L)=1
and
[g_{1},\beta_{1}^{\prime}]
infinite. We have
g_{1}\in I_{1}^{\prime}=\{\alpha_{1}\leq x\leq\beta_{1}^{\prime}:x\equiv_{N_{i}}c_{i}\}\subseteq I_{1}
and by exchange
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(g_{1},h/\alpha_{1}^{\prime},\beta_{1}^{\prime},L)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(g_{1},h/L)
.
∎

The next lemma is a rather simple first approximation to more technical results which follow:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be any structure, and
S
a stably embedded definable set. If
S
has elimination of imaginaries,
X
is (almost) strongly internal to
S
and
g:X\to Y
is a definable surjection then
Y
is (almost) strongly internal to
S
.

Proof.

We consider the almost strongly internal case only, as the strongly internal case is similar and easier. Let
f:X\to S^{n}
be a finite-to-one definable function. For any
y\in Y
consider the definable set
W_{y}=f(g^{-1}(y))\subseteq S^{n}
. By elimination of imaginaries in
S
, there exists a definable function
h:Y\to S^{m}
such that
h(y_{1})=h(y_{2})
if and only if
W_{y_{1}}=W_{y_{2}}
. It will suffice to show that
h
is finite-to-one. So assume towards a contradiction that this is not the case. I.e., there exists
b\in S
with
H:=h^{-1}(b)
infinite. Fix
y_{1}\in H
. Then for any other
y_{2}\in H
, obviously,
g^{-1}(y_{1})\cap g^{-1}(y_{2})=\emptyset
(but
g^{-1}(y_{2})\neq\emptyset
because
g
is onto). Since
f
is finite to one and
H
is infinite, for
w\in W_{y_{1}}
there is
y_{2}\in H
such that
f^{-1}(w)\cap g^{-1}(y_{2})=\emptyset
, contradicting the assumption that
W_{y_{1}}=W_{y_{2}}
.
∎

Since Presburger Arithmetic eliminates imaginaries, this gives the analogue of Lemma
3.9
(2) for stably embedded
{\mathbb{Z}}
-groups. For SW-uniformities, the analogous statement is Lemma
2.9
.

4.2.
Vicinic dp-minimal sets

We now introduce the axiomatic setting we are interested in, but first:

Definition 4.4
.

Let
X
be an
A
-definable set of finite dp-rank,
a\in X
and
B\supseteq A
a set of parameters.

(1)

The point
a
is
B
-
generic
in
X
(or,
generic in
X
over
B
) if
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a/B)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(X)
.

(2)

For an
A
-generic
a\in X
, a set
U\subseteq X
is
a
B
-generic vicinity of
a
in
X
if
a\in U
,
U
is
B
-definable, and
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a/B)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(X)
(in particular,
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(U)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(X)
).

Note that if
D
is an SW-uniformity, then a generic vicinity of a generic point
a
in a set
X\subseteq D^{k}
is, in fact, a neighbourhood of
a
in the relative topology of
X
. The existence of generic neighbourhoods in SW-uniformities is given by Fact
2.8
.

Definition 4.5
.

A dp-minimal set
D
is
vicinic
if it satisfies the following axioms:

(A1)

\dim_{\acl}=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}
; i.e. for any tuple
a\in D^{n}
and set
A
,
\dim_{\acl}(a/A)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a/A)
.

(A2)

For any sets of parameters
A
and
B
, for every
A
-generic elements
b\in D^{n}
,
c\in D^{m}
and any
B
-generic vicinity
X
of
b
in
D^{n}
, there exists
C\supseteq A
and a
C
-generic vicinity of
b
in
X
such that
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(b,c/A)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(b,c/C)
.

Note that in Axiom (A2) it is crucial that the parameter set
B
need not contain
A
. The topological intuition is that if
b
is in the interior of a
B
-definable set
X
then we can find a smaller neighbourhood of
b
defined over a new parameter set
C
, that is generic with respect to all the initial data.

Remark 4.6
.

By Axiom (A1), for every parameter set
A
, every
c\in D^{m}
is inter-algebraic over
A
with an
A
-generic
c^{\prime}\in D^{k}
, for
k=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(c/A)
. Thus, (A2) remains true if we drop the requirement that
c
is generic.

Let us note that indeed all the unstable distinguished sorts in our various settings are vicinic:

Fact 4.7
.

(1)

Every SW-uniformity is vicinic.

(2)

If
{\mathcal{K}}=(K,+,\cdot,v,\dots)
is either V-minimal, power-bounded T-convex, or a
p
-adically closed valued field, then all the distinguished sorts, except
k
in the V-minimal setting, are vicinic.

Proof.

When
D
is an SW-uniformity, Axiom (A1) holds by
[
34
, Proposition 2.4]
and Axiom (A2) is Fact
2.8
. This proves (1) and (2) follows in all cases except when
D=K/{\mathcal{O}}
and
D=\Gamma
in the
p
-adic setting.

When
{\mathcal{K}}
is
p
-adically closed, then for
K/{\mathcal{O}}
, see Corollary
3.7
for (A1), and Proposition
3.8
for (A2). For
\Gamma
, see Fact
4.1
, for (A1) and Lemma
4.2
, for (A2).
∎

Question 4.8
.

Does every dp-minimal distal structure satisfy Axiom (A2)?
We cannot expect the converse to hold, since by the above fact, the valued field sort of an algebraically closed field of equi-characteristic
0
satisfies Axioms (A2), but it is not distal.

For the rest of Section
4
, we assume that
D
is a vicinic sort.

We first note an immediate implication of Axiom (A1):

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
X
be a definable set
a\in X
and assume that
X
is almost strongly internal to
D
over
A
. Then for any
B\supseteq A
we have
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a/B)=k
if and only if there exists
a^{\prime}\in D^{k}
such that
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a^{\prime}/B)=k
,
a^{\prime}\in\dcl(aA)
and
a\in\acl(a^{\prime}B)
.

We now start developing the technical tools needed for the construction of infinitesimal groups in the setting of vicinic structures. We first want to show that boxes form vicinity-bases at generic points in the following sense:

Lemma 4.10
.

For any
A\subseteq{\mathcal{M}}
,
A
-generic
b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})\in D^{n}
and
c\in D^{m}
, and any
A
-generic vicinity
X
of
b
in
D^{n}
, there exists
C\supseteq A
and
C
-generic vicinities
I_{i}\ni b_{i}
in
D
, for
i=1,\dots,n
such that
I_{1}\times\dots\times I_{n}\subseteq X
and
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(b,c/C)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(b,c/A)=n+m
.

Proof.

We use induction on
n
, where the case
n=1
is an immediate application of (A2) to
b\in D
, and
c\in D^{m}
.

We now consider
b=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})
and
X
an
A
-vicinity of
b
in
D^{n}
. Let
b^{\prime}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-1})
, and apply (A2) to
b_{n}\in X_{b^{\prime}}\subseteq D
.

We then find
C^{\prime}\supseteq A
and a
C^{\prime}
-vicinity
I\subseteq X_{b^{\prime}}
of
b_{n}
in
D
, such that

\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(b_{n},(b^{\prime},c)/C^{\prime})=n+m
. Let

X_{1}:=\{x\in D^{n-1}:(\forall y)(y\in I\to(x,y)\in X)\}.

This is a
C^{\prime}
-definable set, containing
b^{\prime}
. So it is a
C^{\prime}
-vicinity of
b^{\prime}
in
D^{n-1}
. By induction (now replacing
c
with
b_{n},c
), there exist
I_{i}\subseteq D
,
i=1,\ldots,n-1
, defined over
C\supseteq C^{\prime}
, such that
b^{\prime}\in\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}I_{i}\subseteq X_{1}
, with
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(b^{\prime},b_{n},c/C)=n+m
. The set
\prod_{i=1}^{n}I_{i}\subseteq X
is the desired vicinity of
b
.
∎

4.3.
Functions with minimal fibres

The notion of definable sets with minimal fibres, with respect to some finite-to-one projection, appeared already in Section
3
.
We slightly generalize.

Definition 4.11
.

(1)

For
X
a definable set of finite dp-rank, a definable function
f:X\to Y
has
minimal fibres
if there exists some
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
y\in Y
, we have
|f^{-1}(y)|\leq m
, and there is no definable
X_{1}\subseteq X
(possibly over additional parameters), with
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(X_{1})=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(X)
, such that for every
y\in Y
,
|f^{-1}(y)\cap X_{1}|<m.

(2)

A set
X\subseteq D^{n}

has minimal fibres (in
D
)
if there exists a coordinate projection
\pi:D^{n}\to D^{m}
such that
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(X)=m
, and
\pi
has minimal fibres.

Remark 4.12
.

(1)

Notice that an
A
-definable finite-to-one
f:X\to Y
has minimal fibres if and only if for every
B\supseteq A
and every
B
-generic
a\in X
, all elements of
[a]_{f}
satisfy the same type over
f(a)B
.

(2)

Every definable set
Z\subseteq D^{r}
has a definable subset
Z_{1}\subseteq Z
(possibly over additional parameters) such that
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(Z_{1})=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(Z)
and
Z_{1}
has minimal fibres.

(3)

If
Z\subseteq D^{r}
has minimal fibres and
X^{\prime}\subseteq X
with
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(X^{\prime})=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(X)
then
X^{\prime}
has minimal fibres as well.

Before proceeding to the next lemma we recall that for any function
f:X\to Y
, we write
[x]_{f}=f^{-1}(f(x))
.

Our next goal is to show that Axiom (A2) can be pulled back via maps with minimal fibres, in the following sense:

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
X
be definable in
{\mathcal{M}}
with
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(X)=n
and
f:X\to D^{n}
an
A
-definable function with minimal fibres. Let

be
A
-generic and

inter-algebraic over

with some

.

Then, for every parameter set
B
and every
B
-generic vicinity
Y\subseteq X
of
b
, there exists
C\supseteq A
and a
C
-generic vicinity
Y_{1}\subseteq Y
of
b
, such that
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(b,c/C)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(b,c/A)
.

Proof.

Let
B
be any set of parameters and
Y
a
B
-generic vicinity of
b
in
X
. Since
f
has finite fibres (because it has minimal fibres) our assumptions imply that
f(b)
is
A
-generic in
D^{n}
, and
f(Y)
is a
B
-generic vicinity of
f(b)
in

. Thus, by (A2), there exists

, and a
C
-generic vicinity

of

, such that
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(f(b),d/C)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(f(b),d/A)

\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(b,c/C)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(b,c/A)
as well.

Let

be the size of maximal

-fibres.
Since

has minimal fibres, for every
C
-generic

, we have
|[y]_{f}|=m
, so
[y]_{f}\subseteq Y
. Thus, the set
W_{1}\subseteq W
, of all

such that
|f^{-1}(w)|=m
satisfies
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(W_{1})=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(W)
, and we have

. The set

is the desired

-generic vicinity of

.
∎

Now we wish to pull back the conclusion of Lemma
4.10
via functions with minimal fibres. We first note:

Lemma 4.14
.

Assume that
f_{i}:X_{i}\to Y_{i}
,
i=1,2
, have minimal fibres. Then
(f_{1},f_{2}):X_{1}\times X_{2}\to Y_{1}\times Y_{2}
has minimal fibres. In particular, if
X_{i}\subseteq D^{m_{i}}
has minimal fibres, for

, then so does
X_{1}\times X_{2}\subseteq D^{m_{1}}\times D^{m_{2}}
.

Proof.

Let
f=(f_{1},f_{2}):X_{1}\times X_{2}\to Y_{1}\times Y_{2}
and assume for simplicity of notation that it is

-definable. We apply Remark

(1). Let

be generic over some

. Then, by sub-additivity of dp-rank,

is
Bb

, so all elements of

realize the same type over

. Similarly, all elements in
\{a\}\times[b]_{f_{2}}
satisfy the same type over

. It easily follows that all elements of
[(a,b)]_{f}
realize the same type over

, as needed.
∎

.

For

let
X_{i}
be

-definable sets,
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(X_{i})=n_{i}

f_{i}:X_{i}\to D^{n_{i}}

-definable functions with minimal fibres. Let
(d_{1},d_{2})\in X_{1}\times X_{2}
be

-generic. Then for any

-generic vicinity

of
(d_{1},d_{2})
, there exists

and a
B
-generic vicinity of
(d_{1},d_{2})
of the form

.

Proof.

Let

, and Lemma

[(d_{1},d_{2})]_{f}\cap Y=[(d_{1},d_{2})]_{f}
.
Let

W=\{w\in f(Y):f^{-1}(w)\cap Y=f^{-1}(w)\}.

Then,
(f(d_{1}),f(d_{2}))\in W

.

By Lemma

of

∎

4.4.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
and

be as before (so

is still vicinic). Recall Definition

of a set

and

to

. We remind (and expand) a definition from
[

]
:

Definition 4.16
.

Let
S
be a definable set of finite dp-rank.

(1)

A definable
X\subseteq S
is
m
-internal to
D
if there exists an
m
-to-one
f:X\to D^{n}
,

(2)

A definable set
X\subseteq S
is

-critical (for

) if
X
is strongly internal to
D
, and has maximal dp-rank among all such subsets of
S
. Its dp-rank is the
D
-critical rank of

.

(3)

A definable set
X\subseteq S
is
almost
D
-critical (for
S
)
if (i) it is almost-strongly internal to
D
and has maximal dp-rank among all such subsets of
S
, and (ii) it is
m
-internal for minimal
m
among all sets satisfying (i). We call
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(X)
the
almost
D
-critical rank of
S
.

The set
X
is called (almost)
D
-critical
over
A
if the corresponding map of
X
into
D^{n}
is defined over

.

(4)

A definable set
X\subseteq S
is an (almost)
D
-set over

, if

is an (almost)
D
-critical set, witnessed by an
A
-definable

, such that

has minimal fibres.

Notice that the

-critical rank of

is always bounded above by the almost

-critical rank of

, but the ranks need not be equal, as we shall now see. Thus, a

-critical set is not necessarily almost

-critical.

Example 4.17
.

Let
{\mathcal{K}}
be an elementary extension of a quadratic residual extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, as in Example
3.33
. Let

be a cyclic subgroup of order

and

as in that example. Let

. Then

is almost strongly internal to

, since both

and

are; so its almost

-critical rank is

.

We claim that the critical

-rank of

is

: The definable set

is strongly internal to

, so we only need to note that

has no definable subset of dp-rank

which is strongly internal to

. Indeed, if

, then, just like in Lemma

, with

then

would be locally strongly internal to

, contradicting Example

.

Remark 4.18
.

Let

be an almost

-critical set, witnessed by

. Then it is not hard to see that there exists

-set.

We will use the following remark implicitly throughout.

Remark 4.19
.

(1)

If

witnesses that

is an (almost)
D
-set for

then, since

has minimal fibres, we may compose

with an appropriate coordinate projection to obtain a map with minimal fibres

, such that

.
Thus, in Lemma
4.13
the assumption that

can be dropped when

is an (almost)

-set.
Similarly, Lemma
4.15
holds when

are (almost)

-sets.

(2)

If

are (almost)

-sets then so is

. Indeed, by Lemma

it is sufficient to show that

is (almost)

-critical in

: If

then so are the fibres

and

for every

. In particular

is the (almost)

-critical rank of

.

(3)

If

-set and

then

is also an (almost)
D
-set.

Below, if the ambient set is clear from the context or immaterial, we will just refer to (almost)
D
-critical sets, without explicit mention of

(though such an

of finite dp-rank is always assumed to exist in the background).

We end this section with a result on generic vicinities, which will play an important role in the next sections.

as follows: first apply the lemma to

4.5.
D
-groups

We proceed with a series of technical lemmas ultimately allowing us to construct, inside a group that is (almost) strongly internal to
D
, a definable subset that is both (almost) strongly internal and sufficiently closed under the group operation.

Lemma 4.21
.

Let
G
be an
A
-definable group. Let
X,Y\subseteq G
be
A
-definable with
X
(almost) strongly internal to
D
over
A
, and fix an
A
-generic
(g,h)\in X\times Y
.

If
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(g/A,g\cdot h)<\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(X)
then there exists a finite-to-one definable function from a subset of
X\times Y
onto a set
W\subseteq X\cdot Y\subseteq G
satisfying
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(W)>\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(Y)
.

Proof.

For simplicity of notation, assume that
A=\emptyset
and write
k=gh\in G
. Assume that
d:=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(g/k)<m:=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(X)
, and let
n=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(Y)
.

Because
X
is (almost) strongly internal to
D
we may apply Lemma
4.9
to obtain
a\in D^{d}
,
a\in\dcl(g)
such that
g\in\acl(a,k)
.

Notice that each two pairs of
g,h,k
are interdefinable over
\emptyset
. E.g., the map
(x,y)\mapsto(x,xy)
sends
(g,h)
to
(g,k)
. Thus, we have

\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a,k)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(g,k)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(g,h)=m+n
.

Since
a\in D^{d}
, we have
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a)\leq d
so by sub-additivity of dp-rank we have
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(k/a)\geq n+m-d>n
.

Let
\varphi(x,k)
be the formula over
a
isolating
\tp(g/a,k)
.
Let
l:=|\varphi(X,k)|
and

Z=\{(x,y)\in X\times Y:\varphi(x,x\cdot y)\land(\exists^{\leq l}x^{\prime})(x^{\prime}\in X\land\varphi(x^{\prime},x\cdot y)\}.

This is an
a
-definable set containing
(g,h)
, thus its image under
(x,y)\mapsto x\cdot y
, call it
W
, is also
a
-definable and contains
k
, so
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(W)\geq\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(k/a)>n
.
Our assumption on
\varphi
implies that the restriction of the group multiplication to
Z
is a finite-to-one map. ∎

The following statement is rather convoluted, although its proof is simple, based on what we already know. It is the main technical tool towards the definition of an (almost)
D
-group.

Corollary 4.22
.

Let
G
be an
A
-definable group.

(1) Assume that

(
\star
)

for every definable
X,Y\subseteq G
and a finite-to-one surjection
f:X\to Y
, if
X
is strongly internal to
D
then there exists a definable subset
Y^{\prime}\subseteq Y
with
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(Y^{\prime})=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(Y)
, such that
Y^{\prime}
is strongly internal to
D
.

Let
X_{1}\subseteq G
be strongly internal to
D
and
X_{2}\subseteq G
be
D
-critical, both over
A
.
Then for every
A
-generic
(g,h)\in X_{1}\times X_{2}
, we have
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(g/A,g\cdot h)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(X_{1})
.

(2) Assume that

(
\star_{a}
)

for every definable
X,Y\subseteq G
and a finite-to-one surjection
f:X\to Y
, if
X
is almost strongly internal to
D
there exists a definable subset
Y^{\prime}\subseteq Y
with
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(Y^{\prime})=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(Y)
, such that
Y^{\prime}
is almost strongly internal to
D
.

Let
X_{1}\subseteq G
be almost strongly internal to
D
and
X_{2}\subseteq G
be almost
D
-critical, both over
A
.
Then for every
A
-generic
(g,h)\in X_{1}\times X_{2}
, we have
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(g/A,g\cdot h)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(X_{1})
.

Proof.

(1)
Assume
X_{1}
is strongly internal to
D
,
X_{2}
is
D
-critical and assume towards a contradiction that
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(g/A,g\cdot h)<m:=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(X_{1})
; then by Lemma
4.21
, there exists a finite-to-one definable function
f
from a subset

of

onto a definable subset

Since

are both strongly internal to

, and hence also

. By (
\star
), applied to

with

and an injective map from

is strongly internal to

.

(2) This proof is almost identical, replacing everywhere “strongly internal” with “almost strongly internal” and using
(\star_{a})
instead of

.
∎

and

are in bijection with

However, the two subgroups generate

.

4.6.
From almost strong internality to strong internality

In order to put the machinery for the construction of infinitesimal subgroups in gear we need to work inside a group that is locally strongly internal to a vicinic sort
D
. As we have seen in Example
3.33
, in the case
D=K/{\mathcal{O}}
in the
p
-adic setting, it may happen that a group is locally almost strongly internal to
D
, but not locally strongly internal. In this section, we show that modding out a finite normal subgroup resolves this problem. The proof is inspired by a result of a similar nature due to Hrushovski and Rideau-Kikuchi,
[
17
, Lemma 2.25]
.

We need (also for later use) an elementary fact from group theory:

Fact 4.31
.

Let
G
be a group,
A,B\subseteq G
arbitrary subsets,
a\in A
,
b\in B
. Assume that

a\cdot B=A\cdot B=A\cdot b.

Then there is a sub-monoid
S\leq G
(i.e.
e\in S
and
S
closed under multiplication) such that
A=aS
and
B=Sb
.
In particular, if
A,B
are finite, then
S
is a subgroup.

Proof.

Note that
a^{-1}A=Bb^{-1}
. We first claim that
the set
S_{1}:=\{g\in G:gB\subseteq B\}
equals
a^{-1}A
. Indeed, if
gB\subseteq B
then
gb\in B
, so
g\in Bb^{-1}=a^{-1}A
. Conversely, since
aB=AB
then
a^{-1}AB=B
, so the claim follows. Clearly,
e\in S_{1}
and
S_{1}
is closed under multiplication. By symmetry,
S_{2}:=\{g\in G:Ag^{-1}\subseteq A\}=Bb^{-1}
, is also a sub-monoid of
G
.
As we just noted, we have
S_{1}=S_{2}
, call it
S
, and
A=aS
,
B=Sb
.

Now, if
A
and
B
are finite (if one is finite then so is the other) then
S
is a finite sub-monoid of a group, so a subgroup.
∎

Lemma 4.32
.

Let
G
be a definable group of finite dp-rank and let
D
be any definable set. Let
X_{1},X_{2}\subseteq G
,
f_{i}:X_{i}\to D^{k}
and
h:X_{1}\cdot X_{2}\to D^{p}
be generically
m
-to-one definable functions, with
f_{1},f_{2},h
defined over
A
.
Assume, moreover, that no
X_{i}^{\prime}\subseteq X_{i}
of the same dp-rank is
n
-internal to
D
for any
n<m
.

Then for every
(a,b)\in X_{1}\times X_{2}
generic over
A
, there is a finite subgroup
H(a,b)\subseteq G
such that
[a]_{f_{1}}=a\cdot H(a,b)
and
[b]_{f_{2}}=H(a,b)\cdot b
.

Proof.

By the assumption on
X_{1}
, for any
B\supseteq A
, any
B
-definable function
F:X_{1}\to D^{q}
(any
q
) with finite fibres is, generically, at least
m
-to-one.

Fix
(a,b)\in X_{1}\times X_{2}
generic over
A
. Consider the function
F:X_{1}\to D^{k+p}
given by
x\mapsto(f_{1}(x),h(x\cdot b))
. Notice that
[x]_{F}=[x]_{f_{1}}\cap([x\cdot b]_{h}\cdot b^{-1})
).

Since
[a]_{F}\subseteq[a]_{f_{1}}
, the assumption that
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a/Ab)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(X_{1})
and the minimality of
m
forces
|[a]_{F}|\geq m
, so it must be that, in fact,
[a]_{F}=[a]_{f_{1}}
. It follows that
[a]_{f_{1}}\subseteq[a\cdot b]_{h}\cdot b^{-1}
. By assumption
|[a\cdot b]_{h}|\leq m
, so necessarily
[a]_{f_{1}}=[a\cdot b]_{h}\cdot b^{-1}
, and so
[a]_{f_{1}}\cdot b=[a\cdot b]_{h}
. If
a^{\prime}\in[a]_{f_{1}}
then, as
a,a^{\prime}
are interalgebraic, we have
\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a^{\prime},b/A)=\mathrm{dp\text{-}rk}(a,b/A)
, so we also get
[a^{\prime}]_{f_{1}}\cdot b=[a^{\prime}\cdot b]_{h}
, but
[a^{\prime}]_{f_{1}}=[a]_{f_{1}}
so
[a^{\prime}\cdot b]_{h}=[a\cdot b]_{h}
. Since the roles of
a
and
b
are symmetric, we conclude also that
[a\cdot b^{\prime}]_{h}=[a\cdot b]_{h}
and so
[a]_{f_{1}}\cdot b^{\prime}=[a\cdot b]_{h}
for all
b^{\prime}\in[b]_{f_{2}}
. Therefore,

[a]_{f_{1}}\cdot b=[a]_{f_{1}}\cdot[b]_{f_{2}}=a\cdot[b]_{f_{2}},

so by Fact
4.31
there is a finite group as claimed.
∎

The second of the facts appearing in

Let

5.
Infinitesimal groups

In the present section, we develop the notion of an infinitesimal subgroup in

-groups
for a vicinic sort

. This generalises analogous results from
[

]
, proved in the topological context of SW-uniformities. In all of our settings, whenever

is locally strongly internal to an unstable sort

, this will be the type-definable group from Theorem

.

We first establish in Section

some technical results which are later used to define the infinitesimal subgroup and ensure that it has the correct properties.

Throughout Section

,
{\mathcal{M}}
is a

-saturated structure,

a vicinic sort and

an

-definable

5.1.
Maříková’s Method

Definable functions in the distinguished sorts in our various settings are generically well-behaved. For example, when
D
is an SW-uniformity, definable functions are
generically continuous
(i.e., continuous at all generic points), and in some contexts generically differentiable with respect to an underlying field. In the
p
-adically closed setting, when
D=K/{\mathcal{O}}
or
\Gamma
, we saw that definable functions are generically given by translates of additive homomorphisms. Our aim is to make use of this generically tame behaviour in order to show that if a group
G
is locally strongly internal to
D
then
G
has a (type) definable subgroup with similar properties (e.g. topological, differentiable, linear).
The argument which we now describe goes back to Weil’s group-chunk theorem, first cast in a model theoretic setting independently by v.d. Dries
[
37
]
and Hrushovski
[
30
, Theorem 4.13]
. In the o-minimal and
p
-adic setting (for a definable group
G\subseteq K^{n}
), this first appeared in Pillay’s
[
26
]
, and the specific technique used below is due to Maříková,
[
21
]
(this was similarly used in
[
11
]
).

Below we will frequently use some earlier results, which for convenience we collect here.

The combination of Theorem

Proposition A.1

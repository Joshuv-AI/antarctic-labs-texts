On a General Class of A A -functions
Joseph Vade Burnett, Alex Taylor

Abstract
Expanding upon recent work, a new class of A A -functions is introduced that can be viewed as an appropriate generalization of the class of regular A A -functions, the class of structured A A -functions, and the class of perfect A A -functions. Algebraic results are discussed and analysis is performed to deduce new results concerning rational arithmetical functions.



1
Introduction

1.1
A
-functions

Denote by
D(n)
the set of positive divisors of
n
. Let
A:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})
be any function such that
1\in A(n)\subseteq D(n)
. Then
A
is called an
A
-function
.

Examples of
A
-functions have appeared in the literature for many years, but usually in different forms. To each
A
-function

a relation
\divi_{A}
may be associated, where
d\divi_{A}n
if and only if
d\in A(n)
. In this context we may call such

“
A
-divisors of

” and discuss the “
A
-divisibility relation”.

Denote by

the set of complex-valued arithmetical functions. If

is an

-function, an arithmetical convolution-type operation

may be associated to it, where for

(f*_{A}g)(n):=\sum_{d\in A(n)}f(d)g\left(\frac{n}{d}\right).

(1)

In this case

is called the “
A
-convolution”, and we may discuss algebraic properties of this operator. Since the term “
A
-function” was only recently introduced, yet satisfies a need to be able to refer to divisibility relations and arithmetical convolutions in the abstract, we shall refer to such divisibility relations and arithmetical convolutions appearing in the literature as
A
-functions, and speak of them in terms of
A
-functions in order to have a uniform discussion.

-functions to be introduced was done so via the unitary divisibility relation of Vaidyanathaswamy

]

-functions were introduced, viewing the usual divisibility relation (which corresponds to the

-function

-functions that preserved some property or theme (see
[
1
,
3
,
9
,
10
,

,

).

Certain classes of

]

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

Other generalizations include the generalizations of the infinitary divisibility relation of Cohen (see

1.3
Purpose

In this work, we describe the paradigm through which we view many of the
A
-functions in the literature. In particular, the class of regular
A
-functions, the class of structured
A
-functions, and the class of perfect
A
-functions are portrayed as being the three sides of a diagrammatic triangle (see figure below) whose vertices are the
A
-functions
D
,
D^{1}
, and
\Theta
, corresponding to the Dirichlet, Unitary, and Infinitary relations/convolutions, respectively.

One may then view the regular, structured, and perfect
A
-functions as being those
A
-functions that preserve properties common to
D
and
D^{1}
, to
\Theta
and
D
, and to
D^{1}
and
\Theta
, respectively.

We introduce a new broad class of
A
-functions that contains as special subclasses the regular, structured, and perfect
A
-functions. From this broad class we extract a subclass, which we call the
paradigmatic

A
-functions, whose members are
A
-functions possessing many properties common to the regular, structured, and perfect
A
-functions. Algebraic properties pertaining to the corresponding arithmetical convolution, numerical factorization properties tied to the concept of multiplicativity, and analytic properties resulting from a natural metric on
\mathcal{A}
are explored.

1.4
Outline

In Section
2
we outline some preliminary definitions and results concerning both
A
-functions in the general and in specific instances when
A
is regular or structured or perfect. The notion of the

is recalled for later use, and we discuss various arithmetical functions which will appear later, including rational arithmetical functions and a natural metric on

.

Special attention is given to the

In Section

, in terms of certain generalized multiplicativity conditions. A unique decomposition of each

We introduce the concept of the “maximal class” of an arithmetical function

Finally, in Section

2.6

The infinitary

There are many equivalent ways of defining

Proposition 1
.

Suppose
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}

such that every integer

. Then if

, then
S=S^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Clearly

\mathbb{P}\subseteq S
, as every prime number must be represented as itself. Furthermore,

for all primes

, since there is no non-singleton subset of

, the product of whose elements is the representation of

as

.

is represented uniquely as

, and

must represent itself by the same reasoning as for

. Continuing in this manner, we can see that

.

To see that
S\subseteq S^{\prime}
, note that every integer

may be represented uniquely as a product of elements of
S^{\prime}
in the following manner:

1.

First, decompose

according to its prime decomposition:

n=p_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots p_{l}^{a_{l}}

2.

Then, write each

in terms of its binary representation as powers of 2:

a_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}b_{i,j}2^{j},

where
b_{i,j}\in\{0,1\}
.

3.

Then

n=p_{1}^{b_{1,0}2^{0}+b_{1,1}2^{1}+\dots}\cdots p_{k}^{b_{l,0}2^{0}+b_{l,1}2^{1}+\dots}=q_{1}\cdots q_{r},

(7)

where each

is one of the

.

Hence,

and we are done.
∎

The set

]
and hence with the

-primitive elements

as mentioned in
[

]

Definition 3
.

For all

.

Observe that if

. Since

2.7

Narkiewicz

Proposition 2

The

-function

We introduce the following new characterization of regular

-functions:

Theorem 2
.

For
A\in\mathbb{A}
,

is associative and commutative.

To show this, we borrow a very useful lemma from Narkiewicz:

Lemma 1

(Associativity condition)

Suppose
A\in\mathbb{A}
. Then
*_{A}
is associative if and only if the following two conditions are equivalent:

1.

d\in A(m)
and
m\in A(n)

2.

d\in A(n)
and
\frac{m}{d}\in A\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)
.

Note that

is commutative if and only if

is symmetric (cf.
[
23
]

Proof.

The forward implication is immediate and follows from the definition of regularity in Narkiewicz
[
23
]
and Proposition 1 of
[
4
]
. To see the reverse implication, observe that if we can show condition 2 of Proposition
2
then our result follows.

To this end, let
p
be an arbitrary prime. For

and
a=2
, there is nothing to show. Assume that we have demonstrated condition 2 of Proposition
2
for all

. Then for

r_{0}=a_{0}
we are done. Otherwise,

and so

is a non-trivial statement. Observe that

implies transitivity), so

To see that no other elements are present in

will carry with it (by transitivity) a different

∎

Thus, we may think of regular

\displaystyle C_{A}

\displaystyle=C_{A_{1}}\otimes\gamma_{1}

\displaystyle C_{A_{1}}

\displaystyle=C_{A_{2}}\otimes\gamma_{2}

(homogeneously)

(P2)

3

3.1
Complete

For the remainder of this paper, we will be considering

is a countably-infinite set since

Definition 6
.

Consider the enumeration of
A
-primitive elements in increasing order (i.e.

,
q_{2}=3
, etc.). Let

be the set of all real-valued arithmetical functions

with the property that for all

of

In general we will make use of the above enumeration of a set of

-primitive elements unless otherwise mentioned.

Since

Proposition 4
.

V(A)

Proof.

Let
A
be an
A
-function. Since
f(n)\equiv 0
is in

,

is non-empty. Now suppose

and

are in

. Then there is
g_{1}
and

in

such that

. But then

and so

. Hence,

.

Now suppose
f\in V(A)
and

. Then if

is the associated arithmetical function in

, then

as well, so that
\log(g^{c}(q_{k}))=c\log(g(q_{k}))=cf(k)
. Hence,

is a vector subspace of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{R}}
.
∎

The following defines what will become our most general class of

3.3

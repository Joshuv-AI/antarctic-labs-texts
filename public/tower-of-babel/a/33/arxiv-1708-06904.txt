Random walks on products of trees and certain totally disconnected, locally compact groups
John J. Harrison

Abstract
. The Poisson boundary of a finite direct product of affine automorphism groups of homogeneous trees is considered. The Poisson boundary is shown to be a product of ends of trees with a hitting measure for spread-out, aperiodic measures of finite first moment whose closed support generates a subgroup which is not fully exceptional . The Poisson boundary of a semi-direct product V − ⁣ − ⋊ ⟨ α ⟩ V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle for any automorphism α \alpha and tidy compact open subgroup V V in a locally compact, totally disconnected group G G is also shown to be the space of ends of the tree with the hitting measure under similar assumptions. Necessary and sufficient conditions for boundary triviality are given in both cases. The method of proof is largely an extension of the prior work of Cartwright, Kaimanovich and Woess on affine automorphism groups of homogeneous trees.



0.
Introduction and preliminaries

Cartwright, Kaimanovich and Woess studied random walks on the
affine automorphism group of a homogeneous tree
, which we denote by
\operatorname{Aff}\mathcal{T}
, in
[
3
]
. They established a law of large numbers, a central limit theorem and described the Poisson boundary for measures which have finite first moment with respect to a gauge function,
|\cdot|_{\mathcal{T}}
and whose support generates a closed subgroup which are
non-exceptional
.

In the preliminary section, we recall conventions, definitions and results from the literature relating to totally disconnected, locally compact groups, random walks, the Poisson boundary, gauges, gauge functions and the affine automorphism group of a homogeneous tree. Particular attention is given to the results of the Cartwright, Kaimanovich and Woess paper.

We extend their work in two directions. In the first part, we consider closed subgroups in a finite direct product of affine automorphism groups of homogeneous trees,
P=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\operatorname{Aff}\mathcal{T}_{{i}}
. We define
partially exceptional
and
fully exceptional
closed subgroups of
P
, and explore the relationships between these properties, the modular function, transience of the random walk and the scale function. To ensure transience, we restrict our analysis to random walks for probability measures on
P
whose support generates a subgroup which is not fully exceptional.

When the probability measure on
P
is spread-out, aperiodic, and has finite first moment with respect to a subadditive gauge function,
|\cdot|_{P}
, we show that the Poisson boundary,
(B,\nu)
, is the direct product of the space ends of each tree with a hitting measure. We give necessary and sufficient conditions for boundary triviality. We conclude by showing that a transitive action of
\Gamma
on the ends is equivalent to
\Gamma
being compactly generated simplifies the proof that
(B,\nu)
is the Poisson boundary.

In the last part, we consider groups of the form
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
, where
V
is a subgroup, tidy for an automorphism
\alpha
, of a totally disconnected, locally compact group. According to Baumgartner and Willis
[
1
]
, these groups act naturally on an infinite homogeneous tree with degree dependent on the scale of
\alpha
. We describe the Poisson boundary for random walks on these groups in the case where the probability measure generates a
non-extraordinary
subgroup, is spread-out and has finite first moment with respect to a gauge function closely related to
|\cdot|_{\mathcal{T}}
.

1.
Preliminaries

1.1.
Totally disconnected, locally compact groups

Many definitions and results in this section are due to Willis
[
17
,
18
]
. Let
G
be any totally disconnected, locally compact (
t.d.l.c
) group and let
\alpha
be a continuous automorphism of
G
. For each compact open subgroup
V
of
G
, let

V_{+}=\bigcap_{k\geq 0}\alpha^{k}(V)\ \textrm{and}\ V_{-}=\bigcap_{k\geq 0}\alpha^{-k}(V).

The subgroup
V_{0}=V_{+}\cap V_{-}
is always closed. If
V=V_{+}V_{-}
, then
V
is
tidy above
with respect to
\alpha
and if the subgroup

V_{++}=\bigcup_{k\geq 0}\alpha^{k}(V_{+})

is closed, then
V
is
tidy below
with respect to
\alpha
. The subgroup

V_{--}=\bigcup_{k\geq 0}\alpha^{-k}(V_{-})

is closed if and only if
V_{++}
is closed. If
V
is both tidy above and tidy below, then it is
tidy for
\alpha
. Tidy compact open subgroups for
\alpha
can be constructed from arbitrary compact open subgroups with a
tidying procedure
, as detailed in Willis
[
19
]
. The
scale of
\alpha
is the positive integer

s(\alpha):=\min\{[\alpha(U):U\cap\alpha(U)]\mid U\ \textrm{is a compact open subgroup of}\ G\}.

Van Dantzig’s Theorem ensures the existence of at least one compact open subgroup
U
. The scale is finite because
[\alpha(U):U\cap\alpha(U)]
is finite by the compactness of
U
. Any compact open subgroup
V
satisfying
s(\alpha)=[\alpha(V):V\cap\alpha(V)]
is
minimizing for
\alpha
. It was shown, by Willis
[
20
]
, that every compact open subgroup which is tidy for
\alpha
is minimizing for
\alpha
. Hence, the scale of
\alpha
is given by

s(\alpha)=[\alpha(V):V\cap\alpha(V)]=[\alpha(V_{+}):V_{+}],

where
V
is any compact open subgroup tidy for
\alpha
.

Suppose that
g
is a group element in
G
. The
scale of
g
is given by

s(g)=s(\alpha_{g}),

where
\alpha_{g}
is conjugation by
g
. The scale function
s
from
G
to the natural numbers satisfies

\Delta(g)=\frac{s(g)}{s(g^{-1})},

where
\Delta
is the modular function on
\operatorname{Aut}(G)
. A subset of group elements
E
is
uniscalar
if the scale function is identically
1
on
E
. It follows that uniscalar groups are unimodular.

1.2.
Random walks and the Poisson boundary

Suppose that
G
is a second countable locally compact group with identity
e
and that
\mu
is a probability measure on
G
. Any such pair
(G,\mu)
is called a
random walk
.

The
space of trajectories
is the set
G^{\mathbb{N}}
with the product
\sigma
-algebra, where
G^{\mathbb{N}}
is infinite Cartesian product of countably many copies of
G
. An element
\omega
in
G^{\mathbb{N}}
is a
trajectory
or
path
.

We denote by
\mathbb{P}^{\mu}
the pushforward of
\mu^{\mathbb{N}}
with respect to the map
S
on
G^{\mathbb{N}}
,
\mathbb{P}^{\mu}=\mu^{\mathbb{N}}\circ S^{-1}
, given by

S(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3},\dots,\omega_{k},\dots)=(\omega_{1},\omega_{1}\omega_{2},\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\omega_{3},\dots,\omega_{1}\dots\omega_{k},\dots).

The measure
\mathbb{P}^{\mu}
is called the
path measure
, and the pair
(G^{\mathbb{N}},\mathbb{P}^{\mu})
is the
path space
. We identify the random walk
(G,\mu)
with a discrete time-homogeneous Markov chain
\{R_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, called the
right random walk
, where each random variable
R_{n}
is the projection from the path space,

R_{n}(\omega)=\omega_{n}

The group
G
acts diagonally on elements of the path space. This action extends to one on probability measures on
G^{\mathbb{N}}
, namely, if
m
is any probability measure on
G^{\mathbb{N}}
, then

g\cdot m(E)=m(g^{-1}E)

for each measurable set
E
and
g
in
G
.

There are many equivalent definitions of the Poisson boundary of a random walk. See e.g. Erschler
[
5
]
, Furstenberg
[
6
,
7
]
, or Kaimanovich and Vershik
[
14
]
.

If
X
is a topological space, then the pair
(X,\cdot)
is a
G
-space
if
G
acts on
X
and the map
(g,f)\mapsto g\cdot f
from
G\times X
to
X
is continuous with respect to the product topology on
G\times X
.

A measure
\nu
on a
G
-space
B
is said to be
\mu
-stationary
if
\mu\ast\nu=\nu
. A
G
-space
B
equipped with a
\mu
-stationary measure
\nu
is called a
(G,\mu)
-space
.

Let
B
be a second countable
G
-space and
(B,\nu)
be a
(G,\mu)
-space. Then,
(B,\nu)
is said to be a
\mu
-boundary
if there exists a random variable
\mathbf{bnd}
from
G^{\mathbb{N}}
to
B
, called the
boundary map
, such that
R_{n}(\omega)\cdot\nu
converges in the weak* topology to a point measure
\delta_{\mathbf{bnd}(\omega)}
for
\mathbb{P}^{\mu}
-almost every
\omega
in
G^{\mathbb{N}}
.

A function
f
in
L^{\infty}(G)
is a bounded
\mu
-harmonic function if it satisfies the convolution identity
f=f\ast\mu
. The set of all bounded
\mu
-harmonic functions with pointwise addition, complex conjugation and the multiplication

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left((f\cdot g)\ast{\mu}^{\ast n}\right)(x)

is a
C^{*}
algebra, which we denote by
H^{\infty}_{luc}(G,\mu)
. If
B
is a compact
(G,\mu)
-space with a
\mu
-stationary measure
\nu
, then

P_{\nu}(\varphi)(g)=\int_{B}\varphi(gb)\,d\nu(b).

is an element of
H^{\infty}_{luc}(G,\mu)
. The map
P_{\nu}
is called the

. A second countable
(G,\mu)
-space
(B,\nu)
is a
\mu
-boundary if and only if Poisson transformation
P_{\nu}
is a
\ast
-homomorphism.

A continuous map
\gamma
from a
G
-space
B
to a
G
-space
B^{\prime}
is
equivariant
if
\gamma(g\cdot b)=g\cdot\gamma(b)
for all
b
in
B
and
g
in
G
. If
(B,\nu)
and
(\bar{B},\bar{\nu})
are
\mu
-boundaries of the random walk
(G,\nu)
then
(\bar{B},\bar{\nu})
is an
equivariant image of
(B,\nu)
if there exists an equivariant map
\gamma
from
B
to
B^{\prime}
, such that the pushforward measure
{\gamma}_{*}{\nu}
is equal to
\bar{\nu}
.

Given any random walk
(G,\mu)
, there is a
\mu
-boundary
(\Pi_{\mu},\nu)
, such that the Poisson map
P_{\nu}
is an isometric *-isomorphism and every other
\mu
-boundary
(B,\eta)
is an equivariant image of
(\Pi_{\mu},\nu)
.

A
gauge
is an increasing sequence
\mathcal{A}
of measurable sets
\mathcal{A}_{j}
which exhaust
G
. A
gauge function
is a non-negative integer-valued function
\delta
for which there exists a non-negative constant
K
, such that

\delta(gh)\leq\delta(g)+\delta(h)+K

for all
g
and
h
in
G
. A gauge function is subadditive if

\delta(gh)\leq\delta(g)+\delta(h)

for all
g
and
h
in
G
. Let
\mathcal{A}=\{\mathcal{A}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a gauge. Then,
\mathcal{A}
is
subadditive
if the
gauge map

|\gamma|_{\mathcal{A}}=\min\left\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\gamma\in\mathcal{A}_{k}\right\}

is a subadditive gauge function. If
\delta
is a gauge function, then the sequence
\mathcal{A}^{\delta}=\{\mathcal{A}^{\delta}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
given by

\mathcal{A}^{\delta}_{j}=\{g\in G:\delta(g)\leq j\}

is a gauge. If
\delta
is a subadditive gauge function, then
\mathcal{A}^{\delta}_{j}
is a subadditive gauge. We say that the gauge
\mathcal{A}
is
C
-temperate
, or just
temperate
, if
\lambda_{G}(\mathcal{A}_{j})\leq e^{Cj}
for all natural numbers
k
and some positive real number
C
. A sequence of gauges
\mathcal{A}^{(j)}
is
uniformly temperate
if there is a positive real number
C
, such that
\mathcal{A}^{(j)}
is
C
-temperate for each natural number
j
. See Kaimanovich
[
10
,
12
]
for more details about gauges and gauge functions.

We make use of a formulation of Kaimanovich’s ray criterion for topological groups, given in Cartwright, Kaimanovich and Woess
[
3
]

Theorem 1.2.1

(Kaimanovich’s ray criterion for topological groups
[
3
,
11
]
)
.

Let
G
be a second countable Hausdorff topological group. Let
\mu
be an aperiodic, spread-out probability measure on
G
with finite first moment with respect to a subadditive gauge
\mathcal{A}
on
G
. Suppose that
(B,\nu)
is a
\mu
-boundary with boundary map
\mathbf{bnd}
. If for
\nu
almost every point
b
in
B
there is a uniformly temperate sequence of gauges
\mathcal{G}^{n}=\mathcal{G}^{n}(b)
, such that

\frac{1}{n}|\omega_{n}|_{\mathcal{G}^{n}(\mathbf{bnd}(\omega))}\rightarrow 0

for almost every path
\omega=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\dots)
, then
(B,\nu)
is the Poisson boundary of the pair
(G,\mu)
.

Corollary 1.2.2
.

Let
G
,
\mu
,
B
,
\nu
and
\gamma
be as in Theorem
1.2.1
. Suppose that
G
is generated by a compact set
K
. Let

be the word length metric. If there exists a sequence of measurable
approximation maps

\Pi_{m}\colon B\rightarrow G
, such that

\frac{1}{n}d(\Pi_{m}(\mathbf{bnd}(\omega)),\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\dots\omega_{n})\rightarrow 0

for almost every path
\omega=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\dots)
, then
(B,\nu)
is the Poisson boundary of the pair
(G,\mu)
.

1.3.
The affine automorphism group of a homogeneous tree

Suppose that
d
is a natural number greater than one. Let
\mathcal{T}
be the
homogeneous tree of valency
d
, the unique connected graph with no cycles for which every vertex has
d
neighbours. We suppose that
d
is at least
3
, and use the term
tree
to mean a homogeneous tree with some valency. Let
o
be a distinguished vertex of
\mathcal{T}
.

A
path
in a tree is a sequence of successive neighbouring vertices without backtracking. Paths may be
finite
,
singly infinite
or
doubly infinite
. The
length
of a finite path
v=\{v_{i}\}_{i=j}^{k}
is denoted by
|v|
and equal to
k-j
. The
length
of an infinite path is
+\infty
. Let
v
and
w
be vertices of the tree
\mathcal{T}
. The
geodesic segment between
v
and
w
is the unique finite path,
\overline{vw}
, from
v
to
w
. The
distance
between
v
and
w
,
d(v,w)
is the number of edges between the two vertices,
\left|\overline{vw}\right|-1
. The distance from the distinguished vertex
o
to a vertex
v
in
\mathcal{T}
is
|v|=d(o,v)
.

A
geodesic ray
in a tree is a singly infinite sequence of successive neighbours without backtracking. Two geodesic rays are
equivalent
if their intersection is infinite. Each equivalence class of rays is an
end
. The set of ends of a tree
\mathcal{T}
is denoted by
\partial\mathcal{T}
and the disjoint union of the tree with its ends by
\mathcal{T}\cup\partial\mathcal{T}
. If
\xi
is in
\mathcal{T}\cup\partial\mathcal{T}
and
v
is in
\mathcal{T}
, then there is a unique geodesic ray,
\overline{v\xi}
starting at
v
which represents
\xi
. Let
\omega
be a distinguished end of the tree
\mathcal{T}
. The set of all ends except
\omega
is denoted by
\partial^{*}\mathcal{T}
.

Let
u
and
v
be elements of
\mathcal{T}\cup\partial\mathcal{T}
. Denote by
u\curlywedge v
the last common element on the geodesics
\overline{ou}
and
\overline{ow}
, unless
u=v
and they are both in
\partial{\mathcal{T}}
, in which case set
u\curlywedge v=u
. Let

\theta(u,v)=\begin{cases}q^{-|u\curlywedge v|}&\textrm{if}\ u\neq v,\\
0&\textrm{if}\ u=v.\end{cases}

Lemma 1.3.1
.

The map
\theta
is an ultrametric on
\mathcal{T}\cup\partial\mathcal{T}
. With the topology from
\theta
,
\mathcal{T}\cup\partial\mathcal{T}
is a totally disconnected, compact space. The set
\mathcal{T}
is an open, dense and discrete subset in
\mathcal{T}\cup\partial\mathcal{T}
and
\partial\mathcal{T}
is a closed, compact subset of
\mathcal{T}\cup\partial\mathcal{T}
.

Proof.

The ultrametric inequality follows from the identity

|u\curlywedge v|\geq\min\{|u\curlywedge w|,|v\curlywedge w|\}

for all
u
,
v
and
w
in
\mathcal{T}\cup\partial\mathcal{T}
. Every ultrametric space is totally disconnected.

If
u
is a point in
\partial\mathcal{T}
, then, for every natural number
k
,

\displaystyle B_{q^{-k}}\cap T

\displaystyle=\{v\in T,\theta(u,v)\leq q^{-k}\}

\displaystyle=\{v\in T,|u\curlywedge v|\geq k\}

is always non-empty. Hence,
T
is dense in
\mathcal{T}\cup\partial\mathcal{T}
.

Suppose that
v=\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence in
\mathcal{T}\cup\partial\mathcal{T}
. If the sequence
\{|v_{i}|\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is bounded, then
v
visits only finitely many points, so it has a convergent subsequence. If
\{|v_{i}|\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is unbounded, then there is an end
w
, such that for every natural number
k
, the open ball of radius
q^{-k}
around
w
,
U_{k}=B_{q^{-k}}(w)
, contains infinitely many points from
v
. Choose a sequence of natural numbers
\{n_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
, such that
v_{n_{k}}
is in
U_{k}
. Then,
\{v_{n_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a subsequence of
v
convergent to
w
. So
\mathcal{T}\cup\partial\mathcal{T}
is sequentially compact, hence compact.

Suppose that
u
is a point in
T
. Let
k
be any natural number greater than
|u|
. Then,

B_{q^{-k}}(u)=\{v\in\mathcal{T}\cup\partial\mathcal{T}:|u\curlywedge v|\geq k\}\cup\{u\}.

Suppose that
v
is an element of
\mathcal{T}\cup\partial\mathcal{T}
distinct from
u
. If
|u\curlywedge v|=0
, then
v
is not in
B_{q^{-k}}(u)
. If
|u\curlywedge v|\neq 0
, then there exists an end
w
in
\partial\mathcal{T}
, such that
u
is in
\overline{ow}
and either
u\curlywedge v=u\curlywedge w=u
or
u\curlywedge v=v\curlywedge w
. In the first case,

k>|u\curlywedge v|=|u|

and in the second case,

k>|u\curlywedge w|=|u\curlywedge v|.

So
\{v\in\mathcal{T}\cup\partial\mathcal{T}:|u\curlywedge v|\geq k\}
is empty and
B_{q^{-k}}(u)
is just the singleton containing
u
. Since
u
was an arbitrary vertex,
T
is discrete and open. Since
\partial\mathcal{T}
is the compliment of an open set, it is a closed, compact subset of
\mathcal{T}\cup\partial\mathcal{T}
.

An
automorphism

\varphi
of the tree
\mathcal{T}
is a permutation of the vertex set of
\mathcal{T}
, such that

d(\varphi(v),\varphi(w))=d(v,w)

for all vertices
v
and
w
in
\mathcal{T}
. The set of all automorphisms of
\mathcal{T}
forms a group under function composition, called the
automorphism group of
\mathcal{T}
and denoted by
\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{T}
.

Tits
[
16
]
showed that every automorphism of a tree is either
elliptic
, fixing a vertex or inverting an edge, or
hyperbolic
, acting as a translation along a doubly infinite path. Every automorphism of
\mathcal{T}
extends naturally to a homeomorphism of
\mathcal{T}\cup\partial\mathcal{T}
.

The automorphism group of
\mathcal{T}
is a totally disconnected, locally compact group with the
compact-open topology
. The open neighbourhoods of this topology are unions of sets of the form

\mathcal{U}(\alpha,\mathcal{F})=\left\{\beta\in\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{T}:\alpha v=\beta v\ \textrm{for all}\ v\ \textrm{in}\ \mathcal{F}\right\},

where
\mathcal{F}
is any finite set of vertices and
\alpha
is any automorphism of
\mathcal{T}
. The compact open topology is also known as the
topology of pointwise convergence
, because a sequence of automorphisms
\alpha_{n}
converges to
\alpha
if and only if for every finite set of vertices
F
,
\alpha_{n}(v)
and
\alpha(v)
eventually agree for all
v
in
F
. As the degree of each vertex in
\mathcal{T}
is finite, the
stabilizer of a vertex
v
in
\mathcal{T}
,

\operatorname{stab}_{\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{T}}(v)=\{\beta\in\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{T},\beta{v}=v\}

is a profinite group because it is isomorphic to an iterated wreath product of finite groups. All profinite groups are compact. If
\mathcal{F}
is any finite set of vertices and
\alpha
is an automorphism, then

\mathcal{U}(\alpha,\mathcal{F})=\bigcap_{v\in\mathcal{F}}\operatorname{stab}_{\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{T}}(\alpha v),

so each set
\mathcal{U}(\alpha,\mathcal{F})
is compact. It follows that
\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{T}
is locally compact. It is totally disconnected because there are arbitrarily small compact, open, hence closed, neighbourhoods of every point. The topology is Hausdorff, because if
\alpha
and
\beta
are distinct automorphisms in
\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{T}
, then there is a vertex
v
, such that
\alpha v\neq\beta v
, so
\mathcal{U}(\alpha,\{v\})
and
\mathcal{U}(\beta,\{v\})
are neighbourhoods of
\alpha
and
\beta
respectively, but

\mathcal{U}(\alpha,\{v\})\cap\mathcal{U}(\beta,\{v\})=\varnothing.

Let
v=\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a breadth-first sequence containing every vertex in
\mathcal{T}
, i.e. such that
|v_{i}|\leq|v_{j}|
whenever
i
is less than
j
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{k}
be the power set of the first
k
vertices in
v
. Then, there is a natural number
k
, such that the set

U_{k}=\left\{\mathcal{U}(\alpha,\mathcal{F}):\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}\right\}

contains
\mathcal{U}(\alpha,\mathcal{F})
for any
\alpha
in
\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{T}
and finite set of vertices
\mathcal{F}
. It follows that
\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{T}
is
\sigma
-compact and second countable. Let
\lambda_{\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{T}}
be the right Haar measure on
\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{T}
, normalised so that vertex stabilizers have measure
1
. Since
\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{T}
is not compact,
\lambda_{\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{T}}
is an infinite measure.

Suppose that
\omega
is a distinguished end of
\mathcal{T}
. Let the
Busemann function
,
h
be the map from the vertices of
\mathcal{T}
to the natural numbers given by

h(v)=d(v,c)-d(o,c),

for each vertex
v
, where
c
is the first common vertex on the geodesic rays
\overline{x\omega}
and
\overline{o\omega}
. The
horocyclic map

\phi
from
\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{T}
to
\mathbb{Z}
given by

\phi(\alpha)=h(\alpha o)=h(\alpha v)-h(v)

for any vertex
v
in
\mathcal{T}
, is a well defined homomorphism, because automorphisms of
\mathcal{T}
are distance preserving under
d
, hence

h(u)-h(v)=h(\alpha u)-h(\alpha v)

for all automorphisms
\alpha
in
\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{T}
and vertices
u
and
v
in
\mathcal{T}
.
The
horocyclic group
, denoted by
\operatorname{Hor}\mathcal{T}
, is the kernel of
\phi
; the closed subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{T}
which preserves each set of
horocycles
,

H_{m}=\{v\in\mathcal{T}:h(v)=m\}.

An element of
\operatorname{Hor}\mathcal{T}
is a
horocyclic
automorphism. Every horocyclic automorphism
\alpha
fixes the common ancestor of
v
and
\alpha v
for every vertex
v
in
\mathcal{T}
.

The
affine group of the homogeneous tree

\mathcal{T}
is the closed subgroup of all automorphisms of
\mathcal{T}
that fix the distinguished end
\omega
, with the subspace topology, and is denoted by

Let

is not uniscalar, let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
be conjugation by
(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}^{-1})
. Let

be the stabilizer in

of a pair of vertices

. Then

. Since

and

are both fixed,

\displaystyle O\cap\alpha(O)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{stab}_{P}(\sigma_{1}o_{1},o_{2})

Hence,

which by assumption on

is greater than one. We need to check that

is tidy above and tidy below. Since

is fixed,

and

∎

2.2.
Random walks and gauges

Suppose that
\mu
is an aperiodic, spread-out probability measure on
P
that has finite first moment with respect to
|\cdot|_{P}
. Suppose that the closed semigroup generated by the support of
\mu
is a closed subgroup
\Gamma
of
P
which is not fully exceptional. Let
\{R_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be the right random walk associated with
(P,\mu)
. For each natural number

less than or equal to

, let
\mu_{{j}}
be the pushforward measure

\mu_{{j}}(E)={\phi_{j}}_{*}{\mu}.

For each element

in

|\varphi|_{P}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}d(o_{i},\varphi_{i}o_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\varphi_{i}|_{\mathcal{T}{i}}.

The map
|\cdot|_{P}
is a subadditive gauge function, where the sequence

\mathcal{A}^{P}=\left\{\left\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\sum_{i=1}^{k}d(o_{i},\gamma_{i}o_{i})\leq j\right\}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty}.

is the corresponding subadditive gauge.

Lemma 2.2.1
.

Suppose that
\mu
a probability measure on
P
. Let
j
be any natural number less than or equal to
k
. If
\mu
has finite first moment with respect to
|\cdot|_{P}
, then
{\pi_{j}}_{*}{\mu}
has finite first moment with respect to
|\cdot|_{\mathcal{T}_{{j}}}
and
\mu_{j}
has finite first moment with respect to the ordinary absolute value on the integers.

Proof.

If
m_{1}(\mu)
is finite, then
m_{1}({\pi_{j}}_{*}{\mu})
is finite, since

\displaystyle m_{1}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}|x|_{P}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma}\sum_{i=1}^{k}|x_{i}|_{\mathcal{T}_{{i}}}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\int_{\Gamma}|x|_{\mathcal{T}_{{i}}}\,d{\pi_{i}}_{*}{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{1}({\pi_{i}}_{*}{\mu})

\displaystyle\geq m_{1}({\pi_{j}}_{*}{\mu}).

The argument
m_{1}(\mu_{j})
is finite whenever
m_{1}(\mu)
is finite is trivial.
∎

Corollary 2.2.2
.

Suppose that
\mu
is a probability measure on

Proof.

This is a special case of Theorem

Let

where

Proposition 2.2.3
.

Suppose that
\mu
is an aperiodic, spread-out probability measure on
P
that has finite first moment with respect to
|\cdot|_{P}
. Suppose that the closed semigroup generated by the support of
\mu
is a closed subgroup
\Gamma
of
P
which is not fully exceptional. Let
u
be an element of
B
and let
n
be a natural number. Let

\mathcal{A}^{(n)}(u)=\left\{\left\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\sum_{i=1}^{k}d\left(\left(\overline{o_{i}u_{i}}\right)_{\left\lfloor{nm_{1}(\mu_{i})}\right\rfloor},\gamma_{i}o_{i}\right)\leq j\right\}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty}.

Then,
\{\mathcal{A}^{(r)}(u)\}_{r=1}^{\infty}
is a uniformly temperate sequence of gauges on
\Gamma
, and

\left|\varphi\right|_{\mathcal{A}^{(n)}(u)}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}d\left(\left(\overline{o_{i}u_{i}}\right)_{\left\lfloor{nm_{1}(\mu_{i})}\right\rfloor},\gamma_{i}o_{i}\right)

is the corresponding gauge function on P.

Proof.

Each gauge is well defined because the finite first moment condition and Lemma
2.2.1
ensures that
m_{1}(\mu_{i})
is always finite. If
\gamma
is an element of
\Gamma
, then the distance from
\gamma_{i}o_{i}
to any vertex is finite. It follows that

\Gamma=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\left\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\sum_{i=1}^{k}d\left(\left(\overline{o_{i}u_{i}}\right)_{\left\lfloor{nm_{1}(\mu_{i})}\right\rfloor},\gamma_{i}o_{i}\right)\leq j\right\}.

Let
\gamma
and
\phi
be elements of
\Gamma
. The gauge map corresponding to
\mathcal{A}^{(n)}(u)
is

\displaystyle|\gamma|_{\mathcal{A}^{(n)}(u)}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{j\in\mathbb{N}:\gamma\in\mathcal{A}_{j}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}d\left(\left(\overline{o_{i}u_{i}}\right)_{\left\lfloor{nm_{1}(\mu_{i})}\right\rfloor},\gamma_{i}o_{i}\right).

Let
K
be the non-negative constant given by

K=|e|_{\mathcal{A}^{(n)}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}d\left(\left(\overline{o_{i}u_{i}}\right)_{\left\lfloor{nm_{1}(\mu_{i})}\right\rfloor},o_{i}\right)

where
e
is the identity element in
\Gamma
. By repeated use of the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle|\gamma\phi|_{\mathcal{A}^{(n)}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}d\left(\left(\overline{o_{i}u_{i}}\right)_{\left\lfloor{nm_{1}(\mu_{i})}\right\rfloor},\gamma_{i}\phi_{i}o_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}d\left(o_{i},\gamma_{i}\phi_{i}o_{i}\right)+K

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}d\left(\gamma_{i}^{-1}o_{i},\phi_{i}o_{i}\right)+K

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}d\left(\gamma_{i}^{-1}o_{i},o_{i}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}d\left(o_{i},\phi_{i}o_{i}\right)+K

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}d\left(o_{i},\gamma_{i}o_{i}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}d\left(o_{i},\phi_{i}o_{i}\right)+K

\displaystyle=|\gamma|_{\mathcal{A}^{(n)}}+|\phi|_{\mathcal{A}^{(n)}}+K.

So
|\cdot|_{\mathcal{A}^{(n)}}
is a gauge function. For each natural number
j
, let

M_{j}=\lambda_{\Gamma}\left(\left\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\sum_{i=1}^{k}d\left(\left(\overline{o_{i}u_{i}}\right)_{\left\lfloor{nm_{1}(\mu_{i})}\right\rfloor},\gamma_{i}o_{i}\right)\leq j\right\}\right).

where
\lambda_{\Gamma}
is a right Haar measure on
\Gamma
, normalised so the compact open stabilizer of an element in
\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{T}_{{i}}
has measure
1
. From the definition, there is a real constant
C
, such that

M_{j}\leq C\left(\max_{i=1}^{k}\{d_{i}\}\right)^{j}

where
d_{i}
is the degree of
\mathcal{T}_{{i}}
. Hence, the sequence
\{\mathcal{A}^{(r)}(u)\}_{r=1}^{\infty}
is uniformly temperate.
∎

Proposition 2.2.4
.

Suppose that
\mu
is an aperiodic, spread-out probability measure on
P
that has finite first moment with respect to
|\cdot|_{P}
. Suppose that
\Gamma=\operatorname{sgr}\mu
is a closed subgroup of
P
which is not fully exceptional. Let
(B,\nu)
be the Poisson boundary. If there is a natural number
j
less than or equal to
k
, such that

then

Proof.

We adapt the arguments of Proposition 2 and Theorem 3 in Cartwright, Kaimanovich and Woess
[
3
]
to the product of trees case.

Let
\partial\Gamma
be the set of accumulation points of an orbit
\Gamma v
in

for some

in
\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{T}_{{i}}
. Since
\Gamma
is a subgroup, this orbit is not dependent on the particular choice of

. Choose any natural number

, such that

. Let
\partial_{j}\Gamma
be the set of accumulation points of an orbit
\pi_{j}(\Gamma)v_{j}
in
\pi_{j}(B)
. Since
\pi_{j}(\Gamma)
is non-exceptional, there exists an element
\alpha
in
\Gamma
, such that

\phi_{j}(\alpha)=\min\{\phi_{j}(\gamma)\geq 0:\gamma\in\Gamma\}.

Then
\alpha_{j}
is hyperbolic, and so
\alpha
acts on
\mathcal{T}_{{j}}
by translation along a doubly infinite sequence
\overline{u_{j}\omega_{j}}
, where
u
is an element of
B
distinct from
\omega
. Since
\pi_{j}(\Gamma)
is non-exceptional, there is a
\beta
in
\Gamma
, such that
\beta_{j}u_{j}
is an end in
\partial\mathcal{T}_{{j}}
distinct from
u_{j}
. Let
\xi=\beta\alpha\beta^{-1}
. Choose any sequence of natural numbers

. For each natural number
j
, let

x_{j}^{(k)}=\prod_{i=1}^{j}\alpha^{k_{i}}\xi^{k_{i}}.

Choose
v
to be the common ancestor of
u_{j}
and
\beta_{j}u_{j}
in
\mathcal{T}_{{j}}
. The sequence

is convergent to an end for each choice of

, because it is Cauchy with respect to the ultrametric
\theta
, and
(x_{i}^{(k)}\curlywedge x_{i+1}^{(k)})v
tends to
\infty
as

does. Furthermore, each distinct choice of

results in convergence to a distinct end in
\partial\mathcal{T}_{{j}}
, because the sequences are eventually contained in disjoint open balls. We conclude
\partial_{j}\Gamma
is uncountable. Since

is a closed subset of a product of compact second countable spaces, there is a subsequence
\{x_{n_{i}}^{(k)}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
, such that
\{x_{n_{i}}^{(k)}o_{r}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is convergent for all natural numbers
r
less than or equal to
k
. Hence,
\partial\Gamma
is uncountable too.

Suppose that
\alpha=\{\alpha^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence in
\pi_{j}(\Gamma)
, such that
\alpha^{(i)}o_{j}
converges to an element
v
in
\partial\mathcal{T}_{{j}}\setminus\{\omega_{j}\}
. Let

\beta^{(i)}=\left(\alpha^{(i)}\right)^{-1}.

Then
\beta^{(i)}o_{j}
converges to
\omega_{j}
and
\omega_{j}
is in
\partial_{j}\Gamma
. Taking
\varphi_{r}=\beta^{(r)}
,
\xi=\omega_{j}
and
\eta=v
, Lemma 2.2 in Cartwright and Soardi
[
4
]
gives that

converges to

for every

in
\partial\mathcal{T}_{{j}}\setminus\{\omega,v\}
. Hence,
\omega_{j}
is an accumulation point of
\pi_{j}(\Gamma)u
for every
u
in
\partial\mathcal{T}_{{j}}\setminus\{\omega_{j}\}
. Using Lemma 2.2 in Cartwright and Soardi again with
\varphi_{r}=\alpha^{(r)}
,
\xi=v
and
\eta=\omega_{j}
gives that
\beta^{(i)}_{j}u
converges to
v
for every
u
in
\partial\mathcal{T}_{{j}}\setminus\{\omega_{j}\}
. Given some

in
\partial\mathcal{T}_{{j}}\setminus\{\omega_{j}\}
, choose
\eta
in
\Gamma
, such that
\eta_{j}u
and

are distinct ends. Either
\{\alpha^{(i)}u\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
or

has a subsequence of elements distinct from

. Hence,

is a limit point of
\Gamma u\setminus\{v\}
. Thus, for each

in

, such that

is not equal to

the orbit

is dense in
\partial_{j}\Gamma
.

Since

,
\nu(\pi^{-1}(\{\omega_{j}\}))
is zero, because
\pi_{j}(R_{\infty})
is in
\partial^{*}\mathcal{T}_{{j}}\setminus\{\omega\}
almost surely by Theorem

. Let

Suppose that

is positive. Then the set

is non-empty and finite, because

is a probability measure. Since
\nu
is
\mu
-stationary,
\gamma S=S
for all

in
\operatorname{supp}\mu
. But then

. So

, which is a contradiction, because

.

∎

Theorem 2.2.5
.

Suppose that
\mu
is an aperiodic, spread-out probability measure on
P
that has finite first moment with respect to
|\cdot|_{P}
. Suppose that
\Gamma=\operatorname{sgr}\mu
is a closed subgroup of
P
which is not fully exceptional. Then,
(B,\nu)
is the Poisson boundary of
(P,\mu)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
j
is a natural number less than or equal to
k
. Let
u
be in
B
, let
n
be a natural number and let
\mathcal{A}^{(n)}(u)
be the gauge from Proposition
2.2.3
,

\mathcal{A}^{(n)}(u)=\left\{\left\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\sum_{i=1}^{k}d\left(\left(\overline{o_{i}u_{i}}\right)_{\left\lfloor{nm_{1}(\mu_{i})}\right\rfloor},\gamma_{i}o_{i}\right)\leq r\right\}\right\}_{r=1}^{\infty}.

Theorem
1.3.8
gives that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}d\left(\pi_{j}(R_{n})o_{j},\pi_{j}(R_{n+1})o_{j}\right)=0

almost surely, and

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}|\pi_{j}(R_{n})|=\left|m_{1}\left({{\phi}_{*}{\mu}}\right)\right|

almost surely and in

. In particular,
\pi_{j}(R_{n})o_{j}
is almost surely a regular sequence of vertices in
\mathcal{T}_{{j}}
, with rate of escape

. Thus, for almost every path
\psi
in the random walk, there is an end

in

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}d\left(\pi_{j}(R_{n}(\psi))o_{j},(\overline{o_{j}u_{j}(\psi)})_{\left\lfloor{m_{1}(\mu_{j})n}\right\rfloor}\right)=|m_{1}\left({\mu_{{j}}}\right)|.

If

, then

, so

is unique. By Theorem

,

if

in

Hence, for almost all paths

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\left|R_{n}(\gamma)\right|_{\mathcal{A}^{(n)}(R_{\infty}(\gamma))}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{k}d\left(\pi_{i}(R_{n}(\gamma))o_{i},\left(\left(\overline{o_{i}\left(R_{\infty}(\gamma)\right)_{i}}\right)_{\left\lfloor{m_{1}(\mu_{i})n}\right\rfloor}\right)\right)

By assumption,

∎

Suppose that
G
is a totally disconnected, locally compact group. Let
\alpha
be an automorphism of
G
. Let
V
be a compact open subgroup of
G
which is tidy for
\alpha
. Suppose that the order of
\alpha
is infinite. Let
V_{-},V_{--},V_{+},V_{++}
,
V_{0}
and the scale function
s
be as in the preliminary section.

Consider the semi-direct product
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
. Identify
V_{--}
with the subgroup
V_{--}\times\{e\}
and
V_{-}
with the subgroup
V_{-}\times\{e\}
. For each
v
in
V_{--}
and each integer
m
, let
(v,m)
be the left coset
v\alpha^{m}(V_{-})
of
V_{-}
in
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
. Let
\mathcal{T}
be the directed graph whose vertices are the left cosets of
V_{-}
in
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
with an edge from
(v,m)
to
(w,n)
if and only if
n=m+1
and
w
is an element of
v\alpha^{m}(V_{-}).

Lemma 3.0.1
.

Every vertex of
\mathcal{T}
has degree
s(\alpha^{-1})+1
, with one inward facing edge at each vertex and
s(\alpha^{-1})
outward facing edges at each vertex.

Proof.

Suppose
(v,n)
is any vertex of the tree
\mathcal{T}
. Let
(w_{1},m_{2})
and
(w_{1},m_{2})
be any vertices such that there are edges from them to
(w,n)
. Then,
m:=m_{1}=m_{2}=n-1
, and
v
must be in the intersection of
w_{1}\alpha^{m}(V_{-})
and
w_{2}\alpha^{m}(V_{-})
. As the left cosets of
V_{-}
in
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
must be equal or disjoint,

w_{1}\alpha^{m}(V_{-})=w_{2}\alpha^{m}(V_{-}),

i.e.
(w_{1},m_{1})
and
(w_{2},m_{2})
are equal. Each vertex of
\mathcal{T}
therefore has only one inward facing edge.

For the outward facing edges, suppose that
(w_{2},\alpha^{n_{2}})
and
(w_{2},\alpha^{n_{2}})
are elements of
V_{--}
which represent the same left coset of
V_{-}
. Then,
n:=n_{2}=n_{2}
, and
(w_{2},n)=(w_{2},n)
, i.e.
w_{2}^{-1}w
is in
\alpha^{n}(V_{-})
. Suppose that there is an edge from a vertex
(v,m)
to
(w_{2},n)
. Then, both
w_{2}
and
w_{2}
are in
v\alpha^{n-1}(V_{-})
, hence
w_{2}^{-1}w_{2}
is in
v\alpha^{n-1}(V_{-})
. Therefore, there are
[\alpha^{n-1}(V_{-}):\alpha^{n}(V)]
choices of
(w,n)
which result in distinct edges. Since
s(\alpha)=[\alpha(V_{+}):V_{+}]
,

s(\alpha^{-1})=[\alpha^{-1}(V_{-}):V_{-}]=[\alpha^{m}(V_{-}):\alpha^{m+1}(V_{-})]

each vertex has
s(\alpha^{-1})
outward facing edges and every vertex of
\mathcal{T}
has degree
s(\alpha^{-1})+1
.
∎

The double path
P=\{(e,n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is infinite as the order of
\alpha
is infinite. Therefore,
\mathcal{T}
is infinite. Let
-\omega
be the end of
\mathcal{T}
corresponding to infinite path
\{(e,n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, and let
\omega
be the end corresponding to the infinite path
\{(e,-n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
If
(v,m)
and
(w,n)
are vertices of
\mathcal{T}
, then the paths
\{(v,m+k)\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{(w,n+k)\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
both eventually ascend to an element of the path
P
, which is connected, so
\mathcal{T}
is connected.
There are no cycles in
\mathcal{T}
because any cycle without backtracking must be a directed path, but the power of
\alpha
increases strictly along directed edges, and there is only one edge directed into each vertex.

Because a vertex
v
is a parent of a vertex
w
if and only if
v=\left(\overline{w-\omega}\right)_{1}
, there is no loss of information regarding
\mathcal{T}
as an undirected graph. We do so from now on. Then,
\mathcal{T}
is a homogeneous tree of degree
s(\alpha^{-1})+1
. We treat
V_{-}
as a distinguished vertex, which we gave label
o
in previous sections.

Suppose that the scale of
\alpha
is not one, so that the degree of each vertex in
\mathcal{T}
is at least
3
. It is easy to verify that the left action of
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
is a bijection on the vertex set of
\mathcal{T}
, and it preserves adjacency of vertices. The element
(e,1)
in
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
acts by translation by one edge on
P
away from the distinguished end
\omega
towards
-\omega
. Since every path descends from
\omega
, it is fixed by the action of
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
. The action is transitive on the other ends.

As before, denote by
\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{T}
the group of all automorphisms of
\mathcal{T}
with the compact-open topology, by
\operatorname{Aff}\mathcal{T}
the closed subgroup of automorphisms which fix
\omega
and by
\operatorname{Hor}\mathcal{T}
the subgroup of elliptic elements. Let
\pi
be map from
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
to
\operatorname{Aut}\mathcal{T}
, which is the representation of the action of
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
on
\mathcal{T}
. Then:

(i)

\pi
is continuous,

(ii)

the image of
\pi
is a closed subgroup of
\operatorname{Aff}\mathcal{T}
.

(iii)

the kernel of
\pi
is the largest compact, normal,
\alpha
-stable subgroup of
V_{--}
and

(iv)

the image of
V_{--}
under
\pi
is contained in
\operatorname{Hor}\mathcal{T}
.

Let
\eta=\phi\circ\pi
, so that if
(v,n)
is in
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
, then
\eta(v,n)=n
. Let
\rho
be the quotient map from
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
to
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle/\ker\pi
. The representation of
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle/\ker\pi
in
\operatorname{Aff}\mathcal{T}
is faithful. Any closed subgroup of the quotient group
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle/\ker\pi
isomorphic to a closed subgroup of
\operatorname{Aff}\mathcal{T}
.

The group
\operatorname{Aff}\mathcal{T}
is amenable. The quotient
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle/\ker\pi
is amenable, because it is isomorphic to a closed subgroup of
\operatorname{Aff}\mathcal{T}
. The kernel of the map
\pi
is amenable as it is compact, hence
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
is amenable.

According to Kaimanovich
[
13
]
, if
G
is any locally compact group with an amenable, closed, normal subgroup
H
and
q
is the quotient homomorphism from
G
to
G/H
, then for any Borel probability measure
\mu^{\prime}
on
G/H
, there is a Borel probability measure
\mu
on
G
, such that
{q}_{*}{\mu}=\mu^{\prime}
and so that the Poisson boundaries of
(G,\mu)
and
(G/H,\mu^{\prime})
are isomorphic.

In particular, if
\mu^{\prime}
is any Borel probability measure
\mu^{\prime}
on
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle/\ker\pi
. Then there is a Borel probability measure
\mu
on
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
, such that
{\pi}_{*}{\mu}=\mu^{\prime}
and so that the Poisson boundaries of
(V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle,\mu)
and
(V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle/\ker\pi,\mu^{\prime})
are isomorphic.

We will now describe the Poisson boundary of
(V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle,\mu)
for the case where
\mu
is an aperiodic, spread-out Borel probability measure on
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
, such that
\operatorname{sgr}\mu
is a closed subgroup of
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
. We show that the Poisson boundaries of
(V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle,\mu)
and
(V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle/\ker\pi,{\pi}_{*}{\mu})
are isomorphic in this case by adapting arguments from the single tree case.

For each element
(v,n)
in
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
, let

|(v,n)|_{V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle}=|\pi(v,n)|_{\mathcal{T}}=d(V_{-},v\alpha^{n}(V_{-})).

Lemma 3.0.2
.

The map
|\cdot|_{V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle}
is a subadditive gauge function, and
\mu
has finite first moment with respect to
|\cdot|_{V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle}
if and only if the pushforward measure
{\pi}_{*}{\mu}
has finite first moment with respect to
|\cdot|_{\mathcal{T}}
.

Proof.

Let
(v_{1},n_{1})
and
(v_{2},n_{2})
be elements of
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
. Then,

\displaystyle|(v_{1},n_{1})(v_{2},n_{2})|_{V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle}

\displaystyle=d(V_{-},(v_{1},n_{1})(v_{2},n_{2})\cdot V_{-})

\displaystyle=d((v_{1},n_{1})^{-1}\cdot V_{-},(v_{2},n_{2})\cdot V_{-})

\displaystyle\leq d((v_{1},n_{1})^{-1}\cdot V_{-},V_{-})+d(V_{-},(v_{2},n_{2})V_{-})

\displaystyle=|(v_{1},n_{1})|_{V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle}+|(v_{2},n_{2})|_{V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle}.

So
|\cdot|_{V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle}
is a subadditive gauge function. Since
|\cdot|_{V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle}=|\pi(\cdot)|_{\mathcal{T}}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\operatorname{Aff}\mathcal{T}}|x|\,d{\pi}_{*}{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\operatorname{Aff}\mathcal{T}}|x|\,d{\pi}_{*}{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle}|x|_{V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle}\,d\mu(x)

which gives the second part of the lemma.
∎

Suppose that
\Gamma
is a closed subgroup of
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle

\rho(\Gamma)
is closed and
\pi(\Gamma)
is isomorphic to
\pi(\Gamma)
. Hence,

is closed. We say that

, the closed subgroup

is isomorphic to an infinite cyclic group.

and Theorem

Theorem 3.0.3

Let
R_{m}=(v^{(m)},r^{(m)})
be the right random walk associated with

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}d\left(R_{m}V_{-},R_{m}V_{-}\right)=0

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}|R_{m}|_{V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle}=\left|m_{1}\left({{\eta}_{*}{\mu}}\right)\right|

almost surely and in

Theorem 3.0.4
.

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure such that the closure of the semigroup generated by
\mu
is a closed, non-exceptional subgroup
\Gamma
in

(i)

converges almost surely to

(ii)

If

, with respect to
|(v,n)|_{V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle}
, is finite and the mean of

is positive, then

(iii)

If

, with respect to
|(v,n)|_{V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle}

For the remainder, suppose that

has finite first moment with respect to

Proposition 3.0.5
.

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure such that the closure of the semigroup generated by
\mu
is a closed, non-exceptional subgroup
\Gamma
in
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
. Suppose that
\mu
is has finite first moment with respect to
|\cdot|_{V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle}
. Let
\Gamma
be the image of
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
under
\pi
. Let
R_{m}
be the right random walk associated with
(V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle,\mu)
. Let
V_{-}
be a distinguished vertex of
\mathcal{T}
, and let
\omega
be the fixed end. Let
u
be an element of
B
and let
n
be a natural number. Let

\mathcal{B}^{(n)}(u)=\left\{\left\{g\in V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle:d\left(\left(\overline{V_{-}u}\right)_{\left\lfloor{nm_{1}({\eta}_{*}{\mu})}\right\rfloor},gV_{-}\right)\leq j\right\}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty}.

for each natural number
j
. Then,
\{\mathcal{B}^{(r)}(u)\}_{r=1}^{\infty}
is a uniformly temperate sequence of gauges on
\Gamma
, and

\left|g\right|_{\mathcal{B}^{(n)}(u)}=d\left(\left(\overline{V_{-}u}\right)_{\left\lfloor{nm_{1}({\eta}_{*}{\mu})}\right\rfloor},gV_{-}\right)

is a gauge function on
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
.

Proof.

The argument is similar to Proposition
2.2.3
. If
g
is in
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
, then the distance from

to any vertex is always finite. It follows that

V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\left\{g\in V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle:d\left(\left(\overline{V_{-}u}\right)_{\left\lfloor{nm_{1}({\eta}_{*}{\mu})}\right\rfloor},gV_{-}\right)\leq j\right\},

i.e.
\mathcal{B}^{(n)}(u)
eventually exhausts
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
.
∎

Let
R_{\infty}
be the map from
(V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle)^{\mathbb{N}}
to
\partial T
given by

\displaystyle R_{\infty}\left(\gamma\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\omega&\textrm{if}\ m_{1}({\eta}_{*}{\mu})\leq 0,\\
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}R_{m}(\gamma)V_{-}&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Let
\nu={R_{\infty}}_{*}{\mu}
be the hitting measure. Then
\nu
is
\mu
-stationary, because

\displaystyle\int_{V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle}\int_{B}f(xb)\,d\nu(b)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle}\int_{(V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle)^{\mathbb{N}}}f(x\,R_{\infty}(\gamma))\,d\mathbb{P}^{\mu}(\gamma)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{(V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle)^{\mathbb{N}}}f(R_{\infty}(T\gamma))\,d\mathbb{P}^{\mu}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\int_{(V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle)^{\mathbb{N}}}f(R_{\infty}(\gamma))\,d\mathbb{P}^{\mu}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\int_{B}f(b)\,d\nu(b)

where
T
is the left shift map and
f
is any function in
C(B)
. The
(G,\mu)
space
(B,\nu)
is a
\mu
-boundary because

is shift invariant in the sense of Kaimanovich
[

]
or Brofferio and Schapira
[

]
Proposition 2.1. It follows that
(\partial\mathcal{T},\nu)
is a
\mu
–boundary of
(V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle,\mu)
.

Theorem 3.0.6
.

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure such that the closure of the semigroup generated by
\mu
is a closed, non-exceptional subgroup
\Gamma
in
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
. Suppose that
\mu
is aperiodic, spread-out and has finite first moment with respect to
|\cdot|_{V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle}
. Then,
(\partial T,\nu)
is the Poisson boundary of
(V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle,\mu)
.

Proof.

The argument is similar to Theorem
2.2.5
. Suppose that
j
is a natural number less than or equal to
k
. Let
u
be in
\partial\mathcal{T}
, let
n
be a natural number and let the gauge from Proposition
3.0.5
,

\mathcal{B}^{(n)}(u)=\left\{\left\{(v,r)\in V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle:d\left(\left(\overline{V_{-}u}\right)_{\left\lfloor{nm_{1}({\eta}_{*}{\mu})}\right\rfloor},(v,r)V_{-}\right)\leq j\right\}\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty}.

Theorem
3.0.3
gives that

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}d\left(R_{m}V_{-},(v^{(m+1)},R_{m}V_{-}\right)=0

almost surely, and

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{m}|R_{m}|_{V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle}=\left|m_{1}\left({{\eta}_{*}{\mu}}\right)\right|

almost surely and in
L_{1}
. In particular, the conditions of
1.3.3
are satisfied, that is,
R_{m}V_{-}
is almost surely a regular sequence of vertices in
\mathcal{T}
, with rate of escape
|m_{1}\left({{\eta}_{*}{\mu}}\right)|
. Thus, for almost every path in the random walk, there is an end
u
in
\partial\mathcal{T}
, such that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}d\left(R_{m}V_{-},(\overline{V_{-}u(\psi)})_{\left\lfloor{m_{1}({\eta}_{*}{\mu}}\right\rfloor}\right)=|m_{1}({\eta}_{*}{\mu})|.

If
|m_{1}({\eta}_{*}{\mu})|\neq 0
, then
R_{m}V_{-}\rightarrow u(\psi)
, so
u
is unique. By Theorem
1.3.9
,
u=\omega
if
m_{1}({\eta}_{*}{\mu})<0
and
u
in
\partial^{*}\mathcal{T}_{{p}}
if
m_{1}({\eta}_{*}{\mu})>0
. If
|m_{1}({\eta}_{*}{\mu})|=0
, then
u_{j}(\psi)
is arbitrary and the statement is just that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}d\left(R_{m}V_{-},V_{-}\right)=0.

Hence, for almost all paths
\gamma
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\left|R_{m}(\gamma)\right|_{\mathcal{B}^{(n)}(R_{\infty}(\gamma))}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}d\left(R_{m}V_{-},\left(\overline{V_{-}R_{\infty}(\gamma)}\right)_{\left\lfloor{nm_{1}({\eta}_{*}{\mu})}\right\rfloor}\right)

\displaystyle=0.

By assumption,
\mu
has finite first moment with respect to the subadditive gauge associated with
|\cdot|_{V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle}
. Hence, by Kaimanovich’s ray criterion for topological groups, Theorem
1.2.1
,
(\partial\mathcal{T},\nu)
is the Poisson boundary.
∎

Corollary 3.0.7
.

Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure such that the closure of the semigroup generated by
\mu
is a closed, non-exceptional subgroup
\Gamma
in
V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle
. Suppose that
\mu
is aperiodic, spread-out and has finite first moment with respect to
|\cdot|_{V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle}
. Then,

m_{1}({\eta}_{*}{\mu})\leq 0

if and only if the Poisson boundary of
(V_{--}\rtimes\langle\alpha\rangle,\mu)
is trivial.

Proof.

The argument is the same as Proposition
2.2.4
. If
m_{1}({\eta}_{*}{\mu})\leq 0
, then it follows from the definition of
\nu
that the boundary is a single point. If
m_{1}({\eta}_{*}{\mu})>0
, then
\nu
is not a point measure.
∎

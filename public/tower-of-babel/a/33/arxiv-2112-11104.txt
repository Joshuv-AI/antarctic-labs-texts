Free boundary partial regularity in the thin obstacle problem
Federico Franceschini, Joaquim Serra

Abstract
. For the thin obstacle problem in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , we prove that at all free boundary points, with the exception of a ( n − 3 ) (n-3) -dimensional set, the solution differs from its blow-up by higher order corrections. This expansion entails a C 1 , 1 C^{1,1} -type free boundary regularity result, up to a codimension 3 set.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The thin obstacle problem

This paper is concerned with the (local) free boundary regularity for the thin obstacle problem.
Namely, let
n\geq 2
, and consider
u\colon B_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
(
B_{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes the ball of radius

\int_{B_{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx<\infty

(1.1)

\begin{split}\int_{B_{2}}|\nabla v|^{2}\geq\int_{B_{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}&\quad\mbox{for all $v\in H^{1}(B_{2})$}\\
&\hskip-28.45274pt\mbox{satisfying $v\geq 0$ on $\{x_{n}=0\}$ and $v=u$ on $\partial B_{2}$.}\end{split}

In this framework the linear space

is called the
thin space
and the set

\Lambda(u):=\{u=0,x_{n}=0\}

is called the
contact set
. When there is no ambiguity, we will write
\Lambda
instead of
\Lambda(u)
.
This paper is concerned with the fine structure and regularity properties of boundary of the contact set inside the thin space, which is called the
free boundary
(actually in this paper we will choose a slightly different convention and define the free boundary as the set of points of frequency
>1
. This set always contains the boundary of the contact set, but may be larger in some cases).

Now, one can easily see that if

satisfies (
1.1
) then
u^{\rm even}(x^{\prime},x_{n})=\frac{1}{2}(u(x^{\prime},x_{n})=u(x^{\prime},x_{n}))

also satisfy (
1.1
). In addition,
u^{\rm even}
has the same contact set as
u
, while
u^{\rm odd}
is a harmonic odd function. Hence, we may (and do) assume that

. In other words

u(x^{\prime},x_{n})=u(x^{\prime},-x_{n})

It is well-known (see for instance
[

]
) that

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\Delta u\leq 0&\text{ in }B_{2},\\
u\geq 0&\text{ in }\{x_{n}=0\}\cap B_{2},\\
\Delta u=0&\text{ in }(\{u>0\}\cup\{x_{n}\neq 0\})\cap B_{2},\\
u(x^{\prime},x_{n})=u(x^{\prime},-x_{n})&\text{ for all }(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in B_{2}.\end{cases}

1.4.
The question of free boundary regularity

One of the main contributions of Athanasopoulous, Caffarelli, and Salsa in
[
ACS
]
was to provide a very detailed understanding of points of frequency less than two. More precisely, they proved that

\Lambda_{[0,2)}=\Lambda_{1}\cup\Lambda_{3/2}.

Moreover, it follows — using a standard Federer-type “dimension reduction” argument based Almgren’s frequency formula — that the set

\Lambda\setminus\Lambda_{1}=\Lambda_{3/2}\cup\Lambda_{[2,\infty)}

is closed and has dimension

\Lambda(u)
4
4

4

Some authors would exclude from the free boundary odd frequency points which may lie in the interior of
\Lambda

. The main question is:

Does the free boundary in the thin obstacle problem enjoy

A first very important contribution in this direction was given by
[

]
. They showed that
\Lambda_{3/2}

, for some

[

]

[

,
DS2
]
, where

regularity is proven with a different method). Unluckily, in general the set
\Lambda_{[2,\infty)}
is not small, nor the set
\Lambda_{3/2}
is necessarily there. There are known examples of solutions

of (
1.2
) for which
\Lambda_{3/2}(v)=\emptyset
and
\Lambda(v)=\{x_{n}=0,x_{n-1}\leq 0\}
. Thus there are “genuine” smooth free boundaries completely made of points in
\Lambda_{[2,\infty)}
.

Although several important works (see Section
1.6

\Lambda_{[2,\infty)}
, all these results were only applicable to rather exceptional subsets of
\Lambda_{[2,\infty)}
(and in particular they did not cover the “genuine” examples referred above).

The results of this paper, described in the next section, establish for the first time enhanced regularity properties of the whole free boundary except for a lower (
n-3
) dimensional set of “bad” points.

1.6.
A survey of previous results

As said above, several important works investigated the fine structure of
\Lambda_{[2,\infty)}
, obtaining very interesting results which nevertheless can only be applied to “exceptional” subsets
\Lambda_{[2,\infty)}
.
In order to explain them, it is helpful to further split
\Lambda_{[2,\infty)}
into special subsets:

\Lambda_{[2,\infty)}=\Lambda_{2+2\mathbb{N}}\cup\Lambda_{3+2\mathbb{N}}\cup\Lambda_{\frac{3}{2}+2\mathbb{N}}\cup\Lambda_{\rm other},

the union being disjoint (here
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
). The common feature of these results was establishing estimates of the following type: if
x_{\circ}\in B_{1/2}
belongs to one of these special sets and has frequency
\lambda
then, for some
\lambda
-homogeneous solution
q_{x_{\circ}}
and for some modulus of continuity

, it holds

(1.7)

\sup_{B_{r}}|u(x_{\circ}+\cdot)-q_{x_{\circ}}|\leq r^{\lambda}\omega_{x_{\circ}}(r)\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})}\quad\text{ for all }r\in(0,1).

More precisely, the current picture is:

•

The set
\Lambda_{2+2\mathbb{N}}
(points with even frequency) was investigated for the first time in
[
GP
]
, using Monneau-type monotonicity formulas, it was showed that (
1.7
) holds at every
x_{\circ}\in\Lambda_{2\mathbb{N}}
, for a suitable
\lambda
-homogeneous harmonic polynomial
q_{x_{\circ}}
and abstract modulus of continuity.

This expansion was improved in
[
CSV1
]
(and further in
[
SY2
]
). In both cases, using the “epiperimetric inequality approach” introduced by Weiss
[
W
]
, the authors showed that
\omega_{x_{\circ}}(r)\leq C(n,\lambda)|\log r|^{-\alpha}
, for an explicit exponent
\alpha=\alpha(n,\lambda)>0
.

In
[
FeJ
]
, using the the ideas conceived in
[
FS
]
for the obstacle problem (and which are also applicable to the analysis of
\Lambda_{2\mathbb{N}+2}
in the thin obstacle problem since

is always harmonic for those points), it was proved that actually
\omega_{x_{\circ}}(r)=O(r)
for all
x_{\circ}\in\Lambda_{2+2\mathbb{N}}\setminus E
, where
E
has Hausdorff dimension at most
n-3
(see
[
FeJ
, Proposition 1.10 and Remark 1.12]
).

Points in
\Lambda_{2+2\mathbb{N}}
have sometimes been called

\Lambda
has vanishing

[

]

(n-3)
-dimensional set, the tangency between the thin obstacle and the solution is at infinite order.

In a similar direction, examples exhibiting even homogeneity points can be constructed “artificially” in the following way: take any harmonic function

•

\Lambda_{3+2\mathbb{N}}
consists of points with odd frequency (
\geq 3
). In all the known examples

lies in the interior of

. The interest in such points is motivated mainly because they may arise in the 3

Very recently, a striking result from

•

\Lambda_{\frac{3}{2}+2\mathbb{N}}

Assume instead that for all

2.3.
Proof of the second step

This step holds at each point so we assume
x_{\circ}=0
. We set

and assume that for some

We prove that we can improve such decay from

, just by studying the frequency function on

and the problem solved by its blow-ups. Namely, we will prove that, for any fixed large
\gamma\geq 0
the function

(0,r_{\circ})\ni r\mapsto\frac{D(r,w)+\gamma r^{2\gamma}}{H(r,w)+r^{2\gamma}}+Cr^{\alpha\beta/\lambda},

is increasing, where
\alpha=\alpha(n)>0
.
This “truncated frequency function” was already introduced in
[
FRS1
]
in the context of the obstacle problem. The striking novely here is that can prove its monotonicity for
w=u-q
, where
q
is a 2D solution of homogeneity in
2\mathbb{N}+\frac{3}{2}
, and hence not harmonic. All the previous proof relied (both in Almgren’s original works or
[
FRS1
]
) used in a crucial way harmonicity of
q
, and with our approach here we see for the first time that harmonicity is not as essential as it seemed!

Fix
\gamma\geq\lambda+1
and let
\mu
be the value of the truncated frequency function as
r\downarrow 0
. The monotonicity of the frequency is a very powerful tool, since it allows to do a second blow-up, showing that for some sequence

we have

\lim_{k}\frac{w_{r_{\ell}}}{\|w_{r_{\ell}}\|_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}}=Q,

in the

is

. But then, using (

, so it must be

, which was essentially our goal.

The (completely new) proof of the monotonicity (
2.3
) relies on several ingredients, an important one is the following “nonlinear” estimate (cf.

3.12
and

)

(2.4)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}|w_{r}\Delta w_{r}|\leq Cr^{\alpha\beta/\lambda}\|w_{r}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{2}\setminus B_{1})}.

The proof of this estimate turns out to be quite short (but nontrivial). The main difficulty is that points of half-integral frequency display a two-fold behaviour: in the half space where

q\equiv 0
, they behave like points with odd frequency. For points of even frequency the Almgren’s frequency was known to be exactly monotone, but for points of odd frequency, even after some neat simplifications, some errors appear, thanks to estimate (

) we are able to keep such terms under control.

4.1.

For convenience we gather in this section some facts that are already known, and we state them in a convenient fashion.

For each
\lambda\geq 0
,
u
solving (
1.2
),
\varepsilon>0
and

(4.1)

\begin{split}\mathcal{P}_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}(r,u):=&\text{``there exists a linear space $L\subset\{x_{n}=0\}$ such that}\\

We define now the set of bad points of frequency

•

4.2.
2D blow-ups

5.2.
A Monneau-type monotonicity formula

We prove the following monotonicity formula, which is inspired by a similar computation in
[

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
w:=u-\tau\psi_{\lambda}\circ S

\tau>0
and

\mu\in(0,\lambda)
the function

is increasing in the interval
[\varrho,1/2]
where

(5.2)

\varrho:=C_{1}\sup_{B_{1/2}}|w/\tau|^{1/\lambda},

for
C_{1}=C_{1}(n,\lambda,\mu)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can prove the statement under the assumptions
\tau=1
and
S=\bm{1}
. Let
\varrho
be as in (
5.2
) with
C_{1}(n,\lambda,\mu)
to be chosen.

At point
r_{\circ}\in(\varrho,1/2)
there are only two cases:

(a)

\widetilde{H}_{\mu}(r_{\circ},w)>H_{\mu}(r_{\circ},w)
;

(b)

\widetilde{H}_{\mu}(r_{\circ},w)=H_{\mu}(r_{\circ},w)
.

In case (a), by continuity there must exist
\theta>1
very close to
1
, such that

\max_{s\in[1,2]}s^{-2\mu}r_{\circ}^{-2\mu}H_{0}(sr_{\circ},w)\leq\max_{s\in[\theta,2]}s^{-2\mu}r_{\circ}^{-2\mu}H_{0}(sr_{\circ},w).

This allows us to compute for all
t\in[1,\theta]

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}_{\mu}(tr_{\circ},w)

\displaystyle=\max_{s\in[1,2]}(str_{\circ})^{-2\mu}H_{0}(str_{\circ},w)

\displaystyle=\max_{\bar{s}\in[\rho,2\rho]}(\bar{s}r_{\circ})^{-2\mu}H_{0}(\bar{s}r_{\circ},w)

\displaystyle\geq\max_{s\in[1,2]}(sr_{\circ})^{-2\mu}H_{0}(sr_{\circ},w)=\widetilde{H}_{\mu}(r_{\circ},w),

and this proves monotonicity in case (a).

Let us address case (b), where by assumption we know

(5.3)

\displaystyle H_{0}(sr_{\circ},w)\leq s^{2\mu}H_{0}(r_{\circ},w)\quad\text{ for all }s\in[1,2].

It is sufficient to show that the (right) derivative of
r\mapsto H_{\mu}(r,w)
at

is non-negative, since for
\varepsilon>0

\frac{\widetilde{H}_{\mu}(r_{\circ}+\varepsilon,w)-\widetilde{H}_{\mu}(r_{\circ},w)}{\varepsilon}\geq\frac{H_{\mu}(r_{\circ}+\varepsilon,w)-H_{\mu}(r_{\circ},w)}{\varepsilon},

where we used that
\widetilde{H}_{\mu}(r,w)\geq H_{\mu}(r,w)
, with equality if
r=r_{\circ}
(thanks to (b)). We claim that

(5.4)

\frac{d}{dr}H_{\mu}(r,w)\geq\frac{2r^{-2\mu}}{r}\left((\lambda-\mu)\int_{\partial B_{1}}w_{r}^{2}-\int_{B_{1}}w_{r}\Delta w_{r}\right)\text{ for all }r\in(0,1),

and we show how to conclude from here. We apply
Lemma

3.12
to
w_{r_{\circ}}
and that as a consequence of (

) we have
\|w_{r_{\circ}}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2}\setminus B_{1})}^{2}\leq 2^{\mu+n/2}\|w_{r_{\circ}}\|_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}^{2}
, to obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}|w_{r_{\circ}}\Delta w_{r_{\circ}}|

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\lambda)\left(\frac{\|w_{r_{\circ}}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2}\setminus B_{1})}}{\tau r_{\circ}^{\lambda}}\right)^{\alpha/\lambda}\|w_{r_{\circ}}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2}\setminus B_{1})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\lambda)\left(\frac{\|w_{r_{\circ}}\|_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}}{\tau r_{\circ}^{\lambda}}\right)^{\alpha/\lambda}\|w_{r_{\circ}}\|_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}^{2}.

Now, since
\varrho\leq r_{\circ}\leq 1/2
, where
\varrho
is and since
w^{2}
is subharmonic (and hence

is nondecreasing in

) we have

∎

We can also prove
Corollary

1.2
.

Proof of
Corollary

1.2
.

Let
u
solve (
1.2
) and let
\lambda\in\{\tfrac{3}{2},\tfrac{7}{2},\ldots\}\cup\{1,2,3,4,\ldots\}
. We partition
B_{1/2}\cap(\Lambda_{\lambda}(u)\setminus\Sigma(u))=\cup_{N\geq 1}E_{N}
where
x_{\circ}\in E_{N}
if and only if

\sup_{B_{r}}|u(x_{\circ}+\cdot)-q_{x_{\circ}}|\leq Nr^{\lambda+1}\text{ for all }r\in(0,1/2),\quad\|q_{x_{\circ}}\|_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}>\frac{1}{N},

where
q_{x_{\circ}}
is the 2D blow-up given by
Theorem

1.1
. Let us work out case
\lambda\in\{\tfrac{3}{2},\tfrac{7}{2},\tfrac{11}{2},\ldots\}
, the other cases being completely analogous. We define
e_{\circ}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
with
|e_{x_{\circ}}|=1
by the property

q_{x_{\circ}}(x^{\prime},0)=c_{x_{\circ}}(e_{x_{\circ}}\cdot x^{\prime})_{+}^{\lambda}\text{ for all }x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},

where
c_{x_{\circ}}
is comparable to
\|q_{x_{\circ}}\|_{L^{2}(\partial B_{1})}\geq\frac{1}{N}
.
We claim that the map
E_{N}\to\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
defined by
x_{\circ}\mapsto(0,e_{x_{\circ}})
is a
C^{1,1}
Whitney data. This implies that there exist
f\in C^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
such that, for all
x_{\circ}\in E_{N}
,
f(x_{\circ})=0
and
\nabla f(x_{\circ})=e_{x_{\circ}}
. Applying the implicit function theorem to
f
, we conclude that
E_{N}
is locally covered by
C^{1,1}
manifolds of dimension
n-2
.

We prove the claim. Take any
x_{0},x_{1}\in E_{N}
and set
r_{\circ}:=3|x_{0}-x_{1}|
, without loss of generality we can assume
r_{\circ}<1/4
. By definition of
E_{N}
and the triangular inequality we find

\sup_{B_{r}}|q_{x_{0}}-q_{x_{1}}(\cdot-(x_{0}-x_{1}))|\leq\sup_{B_{r}}|u(x_{0}+\cdot)-q_{x_{0}}|+\sup_{B_{2r}}|u(x_{1}+\cdot)-q_{x_{1}}|\leq 3Nr^{\lambda+1}.

So, scaling and setting
b:=e_{x_{1}}\cdot(x_{0}-x_{1})/r
we get

\sup_{x\in B_{1}}|c_{x_{\circ}}(e_{x_{0}}\cdot x^{\prime})_{+}^{\lambda}-c_{x_{1}}(e_{x_{1}}\cdot x^{\prime}-b)_{+}^{\lambda}|\leq 3Nr,

since by construction
|b|\leq 1/3
this is easily implies

(7.2)

|e_{x_{0}}-e_{x_{1}}|+|b|\leq CN^{2}r

for some constant
C=C(n,\lambda)
. In fact, the map sending
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\times\mathbb{R}\to C(\overline{B}_{1})
defined by
(\tau,e,b)\mapsto\tau(e\cdot x^{\prime}-b)_{+}^{\lambda}
is a bi-lipschitz homeomorphism with its image, when restricted to
[1/2,2]\times\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\times[-1/3,1/3]
. We can use the classical
C^{1}
Whitney extension thanks to the bound on
b
in (
7.2
). The resulting function will have Lipschitz gradient because of the bound on
|e_{x_{0}}-e_{x_{1}}|
in (
7.2
).
∎

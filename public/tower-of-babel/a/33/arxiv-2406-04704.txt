Introduction

All the groups under consideration are finite. The modular subgroup
[
1
]
, as a modular element in the lattice of all subgroups of a group, is one of the generalizations of a normal subgroup.
A subgroup
M
of a group
G
is called
modular
in
G
if the following are provided that
(1)
\langle X,M\cap Z\rangle=\langle X,M\rangle\cap Z
for all
X\leq G
,
Z\leq G
such that
X\leq Z
and
(2)
\langle M,Y\cap Z\rangle=\langle M,Y\rangle\cap Z
for all
Y\leq G
,
Z\leq G
with
M\leq Z
.

Modular, like normal subgroups, do not have the property of transitivity. However, the concept of a submodular subgroup introduced in
[
2
]
is free of this drawback. A subgroup
H
of a group
G
is called
submodular
in
G
if
H
can be connected to
G
by a chain of subgroups in which each previous subgroup is modular in the next. In
[
2
]
, the properties of such subgroups were studied. In
[
3
]
, classes of groups with submodular Sylow subgroups were investigated.
Currently, groups with given systems of submodular subgroups are studied
in the works of many authors (see, for example, [4–10]).

R. Schmidt in
[
11
]

proved that the subgroup
M
of the group
G
is a maximal modular subgroup in
G
if and only if either
M
is a maximal normal subgroup of
G
or
G/\mathrm{Core}_{G}(M)
is a non-abelian group of order
pq
for some primes
p
and
q
. Here
\mathrm{Core}_{G}(M)
is the intersection of all subgroups of
G
conjugate to
M
.
We introduce the following

Definition 1
.

Let
n
be a natural number.
A subgroup
H
of a group
G
will be called

-modularly embedded in

if either
H\unlhd G
or
G/\mathrm{Core}_{G}(M)
is a non-nilpotent group of order

with
|G:H|=p
for some primes
p
and
q
.

Definition 2
.

Let
k
be a fixed natural number. A subgroup
H
of a group
G
will be called
k
-submodular in

if there exists a chain of subgroups

H=H_{0}\leq H_{1}\leq\cdots\leq H_{m-1}\leq H_{m}=G,

(1.1)

such that

is

Lemma 12

-submodular in

.

(2)

, or

is a non-nilpotent biprimary group with a cyclic Sylow
q
-subgroup
M/\mathrm{Core}_{G}(M)
and a normal Sylow
p
-subgroup

such that
|M/\mathrm{Core}_{G}(M)|=q^{n}

|P/\mathrm{Core}_{G}(M)|=p

.

Assume that
(1)
holds. Let
D=\mathrm{Core}_{G}(M)
.
If
M=D
, then
M\unlhd

G
. Let’s assume that
M\not=D
.
Then
G/D
is non-nilpotent.
From the maximality of
M
in
G
we have that
M
is
n
-modularly embedded in
G

|G:M|=p

Lemma 15
.

Let

and

be subgroups of a group

(1)

-submodular in

-submodular in

-submodular in

Proof.

(1) Suppose that
H
is
k
-submodular in
G
. We can assume that
H\not=G
. There is a chain of subgroups (
1.1
) such that
H_{i-1}
is
n_{i}
-modularly embedded in
H_{i}
for some

.
Consider the chain of subgroups

H\cap U=H_{0}\cap U\leq H_{1}\cap U\leq\cdots\leq H_{m-1}\cap U\leq H_{m}\cap U=U.

If

, then

is
k
-submodular in

.

Let’s assume that
H_{j-1}\cap U\ntrianglelefteq H_{j}\cap U
for some

. In that case

and
H_{j-1}\ntrianglelefteq H_{j}
. Let

. Since

is
n_{j}
-modularly embedded in

|H_{j}:H_{j-1}|=p

|H_{j}/L|=pq^{n_{j}}

p
and
q
. Let

. Then

. From Lemma
12

and
H_{j-1}/L
is a cyclic subgroup. We have two cases.

1. Let’s assume that
p
does not divide
|(H_{j}\cap U)L/L|

(H_{j}\cap U)L/L\leq(H_{j-1}/L)^{xL}
for some

is cyclic. From

.

2. Suppose that
p
divides
|(H_{j}\cap U)L/L|
. Since

, we have
PL/L\leq(H_{j}\cap U)L/L
by Sylow’s theorem. Therefore
(H_{j}\cap U)L/L=PL/L\cdot(H_{j-1}\cap U)L/L.

It follows that

p=|PL/L|=|(H_{j}\cap U)L/L:(H_{j-1}\cap U)L/L|=|(H_{j}\cap U):(H_{j-1}\cap U)|.

Let
D=\mathrm{Core}_{H_{j}\cap U}({H_{j-1}\cap U)}
. From
H_{j-1}\cap U\ntrianglelefteq H_{j}\cap U
we have
D\not=H_{j-1}\cap U
and
(H_{j}\cap U)/D=PD/D\cdot(H_{j-1}\cap U)/D
. Note that
(H_{j-1}\cap U)/(L\cap U)\cong(H_{j-1}\cap U)L/L\leq H_{j-1}/L
is a cyclic
q
-group. Since
L\cap U\unlhd H_{j}\cap U
and
L\cap U\leq H_{j-1}\cap U
, we have
L\cap U\leq D
. It follows that
(H_{j-1}\cap U)/D\cong(H_{j-1}\cap U)/(L\cap U)/D/(L\cap U)

q

|(H_{j-1}\cap U)/D|=q^{n}

such that

. We have proved that

is
n
-modularly embedded in
H_{j}\cap U
for

. Thus

is
k
-submodular in
U
.

(2) The statement follows from (1) and Lemma
14
(2).

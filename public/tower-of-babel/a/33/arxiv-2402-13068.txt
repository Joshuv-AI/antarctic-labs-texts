Abstract
This paper is concerned with the problem of recovering third-order tensor data from limited samples. A recently proposed tensor decomposition (BMD) method has been shown to efficiently compress third-order spatiotemporal data. Using the BMD, we formulate a slicewise nuclear norm penalized algorithm to recover a third-order tensor from limited observed samples. We develop an efficient alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) scheme to solve the resulting minimization problem. Experimental results on real data show our method to give reconstruction comparable to those of HaLRTC (Liu et al., IEEE Trans Ptrn Anal Mchn Int, 2012), a well-known tensor completion method, in about the same number of iterations. However, our method has the advantage of smaller subproblems and higher parallelizability per iteration.

Introduction

Missing data can arise in many scenarios in real-world video and image processing applications. For instance, malfunctioning sensors or cameras can lead to missing data in photos and video recordings. Environmental factors like adverse weather conditions, poor lighting, or occlusions can affect the quality of the data and result in missing or distorted frames. Studying higher-order tensor completion problems incorporating the multidimensional structure of the color images, hyperspectral images or grayscale videos has drawn great attention in recent years
[
10
,
18
]
. A survey on the recent developments in methods and algorithms for solving the tensor completion problem is available in
[
14
]
.

A common assumption for solving the tensor completion problem is that the underlying unknown data tensor can be well approximated by a low rank tensor. The definition of the tensor rank can vary depending on the underlying rank assumption of a given tensor
[
14
]
. Commonly studied tensor ranks include the tensor CP rank
[
9
]
, the Tucker rank
[
4
]
, and the tensor tubal-rank known from the t-SVD factorization
[
8
]
.

More recently, a novel tensor Bhattacharya-Mesner decomposition (BMD) has been proposed
[
15
]
for third-order tensors. It is demonstrated that third-order spatiotemporal data is highly compressible. In particular, we observed that the spatiotemporal slices of the surveillance video exhibit low-rankness. Moreover, a generative low BM-rank video model was introduced and has shown that the motions of objects traversing the background scene over time can be captured within a few slices of the BMD factor tensors. We expect that these factor tensor slices are low rank. As for the factor tensor slices corresponding to the spatial dimension, they are less expected to be low rank. Motivated by these observations, we propose a novel BM-factor tensor slicewise nuclear norm minimization (BMNN) method for the tensor completion problem. In this work, we focus on third-order tensors.

This paper is organized as follows. In section
2
, we present the notations and an overview of the tensor BMD. In section
3
, we formulate the tensor completion problem with tensor BMD and introduce a tractable relaxation of the rank function into our factor tensor slicewise rank minimization model. Then we will provide an efficient alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) scheme to solve the proposed problem. In section
4
, we will present our experimental findings applied to real-world videos and hyperspectral images. We compare our reconstruction performances with a well-known tensor completion method, namely the HaLRTC algorithm in
[
10
]
. Finally, we conclude in section
5
.

Background and Notation

The tensor BMD is defined based on the BM-product of higher-order tensors, which were first introduced by Bhattacharya and Mesner as a ternary association scheme on triplets
[
12
,
13
]
and later studied by Gnang and Filmus
[
5
,
6
]
. For the third-order tensors, the definition of the BM-product is given as follows

Definition 1
.

For a third-order conformable tensor triplet
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times\ell\times p};\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{B}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n\times\ell}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{C}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell\times n\times p}
, the BM-product
{\mbox{\tt bmp}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{B}}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{C}}}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n\times p}
is given entry-wise by

{\mbox{\tt bmp}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{B}}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{C}}}\right)_{i,j,k}=\sum_{1\leq t\leq\ell}\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i,t,k}\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{B}}}_{i,j,t}\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{C}}}_{t,j,k}.

(1)

When
\ell=1
, the BM-product of the lateral slice
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times 1\times n}
, the frontal slice
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{B}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times p\times 1}
, and the horizontal slice
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{C}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times p\times n}
is a BM outer-product of matrix slices. Equivalently, we can write the tensor BM-product as a sum of
\ell
BM-outer products of matrix slices

{\mbox{\tt bmp}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{B}}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{C}}}\right)=\sum_{1\leq t\leq\ell}{\mbox{\tt bmp}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{:,t,:}\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{B}}}_{:,:,t}\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{C}}}_{t,:,:}\right),

(2)

as shown in the visual illustration in fig. (
1
).

{\mbox{\tt bmp}}(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{B}}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{C}}})
=
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}}}}_{:,1,:}
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{B}}}_{:,:,1}
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{C}}}_{1,:,:}
+
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}}}}_{:,2,:}
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{B}}}_{:,:,2}
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{C}}}_{2,:,:}
+\cdots+
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}}}}_{:,\ell,:}
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{B}}}_{:,:,\ell}
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{C}}}_{\ell,:,:}

Figure 1:
Illustration of the BM-product of a conformable tensor triplet
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{B}}}
, and
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{C}}}
as a sum of BM-outer products of matrix slices.

Then the BM-rank can be conveniently defined as follows.

Definition 2
.

The BM-rank of a third-order tensor
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n\times p}
is given by the minimum number of the BM-outer products that add up to
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}
.

Throughout the paper, we will use the
Matlab

squeeze
notation to denote the operation which maps a lateral slice
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times 1\times n}
or a horizontal slice
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times m\times n}
into a matrix
\mathbf{{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}

[
7
]
, i.e.
\mathbf{{A}}={\mbox{\tt squeeze}}(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}})
. We will also use the
Matlab

permute
operator to represent the map that rearranges the dimensions of an array. In particular, the tensor transposes are defined based on the cyclic permutations of the indices of each entry
[
15
]
.

Definition 3
.

Suppose
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}
is a third-order tensor of size
m\times n\times p
, then
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}
has the following transpose operations which are given by cyclic permutations of the indices of each entry:

\begin{split}\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}^{\top}&={\mbox{\tt permute}}(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}},[2,3,1]);\ \boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p\times m}.\\
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}^{\top^{2}}&=\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}^{\top}\right)^{\top}={\mbox{\tt permute}}(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}},[3,1,2]);\ \boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}^{\top^{2}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times m\times n}.\end{split}

As a result,
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}^{\top^{3}}=\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}
.

Definition 4
.

When
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}
is a BM-product of tensors
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{B}}}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{C}}}
, i.e.
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}={\mbox{\tt bmp}}(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{B}}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{C}}})
, then the transpose of the BM-product is a BM-product of transposes such that

\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}^{\top}={\mbox{\tt bmp}}(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{B}}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{C}}})^{\top}={\mbox{\tt bmp}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{B}}}^{\top},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{C}}}^{\top},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}^{\top}\right).

We also review the notations introduced in
[
15
]
for computing the tensor BMD. The
Tvec
operation vectorizes a third-order tensor to a column vector. Specifically, given a tensor
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n\times p}
, we can vectorize it into a
mnp\times 1
vector with

\mathbf{x}={\mbox{\tt Tvec}}(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}})=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{x}^{(1,1)}\\
\vdots\\
\mathbf{x}^{(i,j)}\\
\vdots\\
\mathbf{x}^{(m,p)}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}{\mbox{\tt squeeze}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}_{1,1,:}\right)\\
\vdots\\
{\mbox{\tt squeeze}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}_{i,j,:}\right)\\
\vdots\\
{\mbox{\tt squeeze}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}_{m,p,:}\right)\end{array}\right],

(3)

where
\mathbf{x}^{(i,j)}={\mbox{\tt squeeze}}(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}_{i,j,:})\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times 1}
. The
Tfold
operation is then defined to be the inverse action of
Tvec
, i.e.
{\mbox{\tt Tfold}}\left({\mbox{\tt Tvec}}(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}})\right)=\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}
. Moreover, the
Mat
operation flattens a pair of tensors of specific sizes into a block-diagonal matrix. That is, given tensors
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times\ell\times p}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{B}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell\times n\times p}
,
{\mbox{\tt Mat}}(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{B}}})
yields a block-diagonal matrix
\mathbf{{H}}\in\mathbb{R}^{mnp\times mn\ell}
such that

\mathbf{{H}}={\mbox{\tt Mat}}(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{B}}})=\underset{i,j}{\oplus}\mathbf{{H}}^{(i,j)},\quad\forall\ 1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n,

(4)

where the block-matrices
\mathbf{{H}}^{(i,j)}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times\ell}
are given entry-wise by
\mathbf{{H}}^{(i,j)}_{k,t}=\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i,t,k}\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{B}}}_{t,j,k}
. The symbol
\oplus
denotes the direct-sum operation.

We will also use the following definition of the Frobenius norm
[
9
]
of a tensor
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n\times p}
, i.e.
\displaystyle{\|\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}\|_{F}=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{p}|\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}_{i,j,k}|^{2}}}
.

3
BM-decomposition for Completion

As shown in
[
15
]
, given a grayscale video tensor
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
, which has a stationary background and moving objects in the foreground. Assume the video frames are ordered from front to back. Then in a BM-rank
\ell
,
\ell\ll\min\{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\}
, decomposition of
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}
, i.e.
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}={\mbox{\tt bmp}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}\right)
, we expect that the frontal slices of
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times\ell}
consists of the background image information and hence are less likely to be low-rank. The lateral slices of
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times\ell\times n_{3}}
and the horizontal slices of
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
contain positions of the objects over time, and we can expect them to be low-rank if the motions are not complex. Motivated by the observations, we formulate a BM-factor tensor slicewise rank constrained tensor completion problem, and later we will solve the problem by an efficient ADMM scheme.

3.1
Problem Formulation

Suppose the unknown tensor
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
has a low BM-rank. Based on the BM-rank
\ell
decomposition
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}
, we form the following BM-factor tensor slicewise rank minimization model:

\begin{split}\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i}^{(t)}}&\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{t=1}^{\ell}\alpha_{i,t}\text{rank}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i}^{(t)}\right)\\
\text{subject to }&\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}={\mbox{\tt bmp}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}\right),\,\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}_{\Omega}=\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{T}}}_{\Omega},\end{split}

(5)

where the matrix slices are defined to be

\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1}^{(t)}:=\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1}(:,t,:)
,
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2}^{(t)}:=\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2}(:,:,t)
, and
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}^{(t)}:=\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}(t,:,:)
. The equation
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}_{\Omega}=\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{T}}}_{\Omega}
represents that
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}_{i,j,k}=\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{T}}}_{i,j,k}
for all
(i,j,k)\in\Omega
, where
\Omega
denotes the indices of the observed entries of
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{T}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}\times n_{3}}
.

Since the matrix rank is not a convex function, we use the nuclear norm relaxation of the rank
[
3
,
2
]
. Additionally, we relax the BMD constraint and we rewrite the problem as follows,

\begin{split}\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i}^{(t)}}&\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{t=1}^{\ell}\alpha_{i,t}\|\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i}^{(t)}\|_{*}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}-{\mbox{\tt bmp}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}\right)\right\|_{F}^{2}\\
\text{s.t. }&\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}_{\Omega}=\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{T}}}_{\Omega}.\end{split}

(6)

Furthermore, we assume that
\alpha_{i,t}=\alpha_{i},\forall\ 1\leq t\leq\ell
, and rearrange the matrix slices
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i}^{(t)}
into a block-diagonal matrix
{\mbox{\tt bdiag}}(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i})
. In particular, we define the following three matrices

\begin{split}{\mbox{\tt bdiag}}(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1})&=\underset{1\leq t\leq\ell}{\oplus}{\mbox{\tt squeeze}}(A_{1}\left(:,t,:\right)),\\
{\mbox{\tt bdiag}}(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2})&=\underset{1\leq t\leq\ell}{\oplus}A_{2}\left(:,:,t\right),\\
{\mbox{\tt bdiag}}(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3})&=\underset{1\leq t\leq\ell}{\oplus}{\mbox{\tt squeeze}}(A_{3}\left(t,:,:\right)).\end{split}

(7)

Then we can rewrite the slicewise nuclear norm minimization problem in eq. (
6
) as

\begin{split}\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i}}&\sum_{i=1}^{3}\alpha_{i}\left\|{\mbox{\tt bdiag}}(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i})\right\|_{*}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}-{\mbox{\tt bmp}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}\right)\right\|_{F}^{2}\\
\text{s.t. }&\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}_{\Omega}=\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{T}}}_{\Omega}.\end{split}

(8)

The above model in eq. (
8
) is called a BM-factor tensor slicewise nuclear norm minimization (BMNN) approach.

3.2
Main algorithm

We present the ADMM algorithm for solving the proposed BMNN model given in eq. (
8
). First, we reformulate the problem into the following equivalent form

\begin{split}\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{i}}&\sum_{i=1}^{3}\alpha_{i}\left\|{\mbox{\tt bdiag}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{i}\right)\right\|_{*}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}-{\mbox{\tt bmp}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}\right)\right\|_{F}^{2}\\
\text{subject to }&\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}_{\Omega}=\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{T}}}_{\Omega},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i}=\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{i},\quad 1\leq i\leq 3,\end{split}

(9)

where
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{i},1\leq i\leq 3
are the auxiliary variables.

The partial augmented Lagrangian function for eq. (
9
) is given by

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{2},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{3},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{2},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{3},\mu\right)\\
=&\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(\alpha_{i}\left\|{\mbox{\tt bdiag}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{i}\right)\right\|_{*}+\left\langle{\mbox{\tt bdiag}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{i}\right),{\mbox{\tt bdiag}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i}\right)\right\rangle+\frac{\mu}{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i}\right\|_{F}^{2}\right)\\
+&\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}-{\mbox{\tt bmp}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}\right)\right\|_{F}^{2}\end{split}

(10)

where
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{i}
are the Lagrange multipliers and are of the same sizes as
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{i}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i}
, and
\mu>0
is a penalty parameter.

Next, we present the ADMM iterative scheme to minimize
\mathcal{L}
with respect to the variables

\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{2},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{3},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{2},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{3},\mu\right)
.

We first update
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq 3
fixing other variables. The optimization subproblem is formulated as

\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{i}}\alpha_{i}\left\|{\mbox{\tt bdiag}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{i}\right)\right\|_{*}+\left\langle{\mbox{\tt bdiag}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{i}\right),{\mbox{\tt bdiag}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i}\right)\right\rangle+\frac{\mu}{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i}\right\|_{F}^{2}.

Equivalently, we can rewrite the above minimization problem as

\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{i}}\alpha_{i}\left\|{\mbox{\tt bdiag}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{i}\right)\right\|_{*}+\frac{\mu}{2}\left\|{\mbox{\tt bdiag}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{i}/\mu\right)\right\|_{F}^{2}.

This problem is well studied in literature
[
1
,
11
]
and has a closed-form solution:

\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{i}^{k+1}=\text{SVT}_{\alpha_{i}/\mu^{k}}\left({\mbox{\tt bdiag}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i}^{k}-\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{i}^{k}/\mu^{k}\right)\right),

(11)

where
\text{SVT}_{\delta}\left(\mathbf{{A}}\right)=\mathbf{{U}}\diag\left(\max\left\{\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\delta\right),0\right\}\right)\mathbf{{V}}^{\top}
is the singular value thresholding operator. The SVD of
\mathbf{{A}}
is given by
\mathbf{{A}}=\mathbf{{U}}\diag\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right)\mathbf{{V}}^{\top}
, and the
\max(\cdot,\cdot)
operation is taken element-wise.

We then update the BMD factor tensors
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i},1\leq i\leq 3
. Fixing other variables, we obtain the following subproblems

\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{\mu}{2}\left\|{\mbox{\tt bdiag}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{i}/\mu\right)\right\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\|{\mbox{\tt bmp}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}\right)-\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}\right\|_{F}^{2}.

Since the Frobenius norm is defined entry-wise, we can remove the block-diagonal operator, i.e.

\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{\mu}{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{i}/\mu\right\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\|{\mbox{\tt bmp}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}\right)-\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}\right\|_{F}^{2},

which we solve by the regularized alternating least-squares (RALS) algorithm.
Holding
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}
fixed, we update
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2}
by solving the following least-squares problem

\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2}}\frac{\mu}{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2}-(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{2}+\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{2}/\mu)\right\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\|{\mbox{\tt bmp}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}\right)-\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}\right\|_{F}^{2}.

(12)

Utilizing the tensor vectorization and matricization operators introduced in sec
2
, we can equivalently write eq. (
12
) into a linear least-squares problem via vectorizing the tensors denoted
\mathbf{a}_{2}={\mbox{\tt Tvec}}(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2})
,
\mathbf{x}_{2}={\mbox{\tt Tvec}}(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}})
, and
\mathbf{v}_{2}={\mbox{\tt Tvec}}(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{2}+\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{2}/\mu)
, and metricizing the tensor pair denoted
\mathbf{{H}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}}={\mbox{\tt Mat}}(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3})
. Then the problem becomes

\min_{\mathbf{a}_{2}}\frac{\mu}{2}\|\mathbf{a}_{2}-\mathbf{v}_{2}\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\mathbf{{H}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}}\mathbf{a}_{2}-\mathbf{x}_{2}\|_{F}^{2}

(13)

The matrix
\mathbf{{H}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}\times n_{1}n_{2}\ell}
is block diagonal with a total number of
n_{1}n_{2}
smaller block matrices. Each block
\mathbf{{H}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}}^{(i,j)}
is of size
n_{3}\times\ell
. Then the problem (
13
) can decouple into
n_{1}n_{2}
smaller least-squares subproblems as follows

\min_{\mathbf{a}_{2}^{(i,j)}}\frac{\mu}{2}\|\mathbf{a}_{2}^{(i,j)}-\mathbf{v}_{2}^{(i,j)}\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\mathbf{{H}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}}^{(i,j)}\mathbf{a}_{2}^{(i,j)}-\mathbf{x}_{2}^{(i,j)}\|_{F}^{2},

(14)

for all
1\leq i\leq n_{1}
and
1\leq j\leq n_{2}
. Equivalently, we can write eq. (
14
) as

\min_{\mathbf{a}_{2}^{(i,j)}}\frac{1}{2}\left\|\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{{H}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}}^{(i,j)}\\
\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\lambda}}\mathbf{{I}}_{\ell}\end{array}\right]\mathbf{a}_{2}^{(i,j)}-\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{x}_{2}^{(i,j)}\\
\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\lambda}}\mathbf{v}_{2}^{(i,j)}\end{array}\right]\right\|_{F}^{2},

(15)

where
\mathbf{{I}}_{m}
denotes the
m\times m
identity matrix.

Similarly, holding
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2}
fixed, and solve for
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}
, we can formulate the least-squares subproblem as

\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}^{\top}}\frac{\mu}{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}^{\top}-(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{3}^{\top}+\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{3}^{\top}/\mu)\right\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\|{\mbox{\tt bmp}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2}^{\top},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}^{\top},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1}^{\top}\right)-\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}^{\top}\right\|_{F}^{2}.

(16)

Holding
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}
fixed, and solve for
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1}
, we can formulate the least-squares subproblem as

\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1}^{\top^{2}}}\frac{\mu}{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1}^{\top^{2}}-(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{1}^{\top^{2}}+\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{1}^{\top^{2}}/\mu)\right\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\|{\mbox{\tt bmp}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}^{\top^{2}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1}^{\top^{2}},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2}^{\top^{2}}\right)-\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}^{\top^{2}}\right\|_{F}^{2}.

(17)

We note that since by transposes, the RALS steps (
16
) and (
16
) are in the same form as the first step in eq. (
13
). Therefore, we can similarly apply the
Tvec
and
Mat
operators and decouple the problems into smaller least-squares subproblems. Hence, the RALS updates have the potential of parallelization.

To update the variable
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}
, we solve the following subproblem

\begin{split}\min_{\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}}&\left\|\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}-{\mbox{\tt bmp}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}\right)\right\|_{F}^{2}\\
\text{subject to }&\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}_{\Omega}=\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{T}}}_{\Omega}.\end{split}

The optimal solution satisfies the following equations

\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}^{k+1}_{\Omega}=\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{T}}}_{\Omega}\text{ and }\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}^{k+1}_{\Omega^{C}}={\mbox{\tt bmp}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1}^{k+1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2}^{k+1},\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}^{k+1}\right)_{\Omega^{C}},

(18)

where
\Omega^{C}
denotes the complement index set of
\Omega
.

We also update the multiplier
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{i},1\leq i\leq 3
, by computing

\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{i}^{k+1}=\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{i}^{k}+\mu^{k}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{H}}}_{i}^{k+1}-\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i}^{k+1}\right).

(19)

Lastly, we update the penalty parameter
\mu
by computing

\mu^{k+1}=\min\left(\rho\mu^{k},\mu_{\max}\right),

(20)

where
\rho>1
is a customized parameter, and
\mu_{\max}=10^{10}
is predefined.

Remark

We compute the total cost of the proposed BMNN algorithm. The main cost lies within the singular value thresholding and solving the regularized least-square subproblems steps. The cost of implementing the matrix SVD for a matrix of size
m\times p
with
m\leq p
is
O(mp^{2})

[
16
]
. Assume
n_{1}\approx n_{2}\approx n_{3}\approx n
for simplicity. In the first subproblem in eq. (
11
), we need to compute the SVDs of the block diagonal matrix
{\mbox{\tt bdiag}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{i}/\mu\right)
,
\forall\ 1\leq i\leq 3
. However, this can be done in parallel on each of the lateral slices
(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1}-\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{1}/\mu)_{:,t,:}
, the frontal slices
(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{2}-\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{2}/\mu)_{:,:,t}
, and the horizontal slices
(\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{3}-\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{Y}}}_{3}/\mu)_{t,:,:}
,
\forall\ 1\leq t\leq\ell
. Since each slice is of size
n\times n
, the cost of the slicewise SVDs is hence
O(n^{3})
. So the total cost of the SVT update in eq. (
11
) is
O(\ell n^{3})
. Next, we compute that the total cost to update the factor tensor
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{i}
is
O(n^{2}\ell(n+\ell)^{2})
, and can be done in parallel as well. More specifically, for instance, to update the factor tensor
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{A}}}_{1}
, we solve the regularized least-squares problem given in eq. (
13
). As we have shown before, the problem decouples into
n^{2}
smaller subproblems given in eq. (
15
). Each block matrix is of size
(n+\ell)\times\ell
and hence the time complexity of computing these smaller block matrix least-squares problems is
O(\ell(n+\ell)^{2})
.

We next compute the time complexity of the HaLRTC algorithm
[
10
]
. We note that the main cost lies in the singular-value thresholding step of the flattened tensors
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{{X}}}_{(i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n^{2}}
in HaLRTC. The total cost of computing the SVDs of the flattened tensors is in the order of
O(n^{5})
. However, since the SVD steps are not parallelizable in HaLRTC, our proposed BMNN algorithm has the advantage of computational efficiency if its parallelizability is utilized.

5
Conclusions

In this work, we have presented a novel method for tensor completion based on the recently proposed low BM-rank decomposition of third-order tensors. The high compressibility of spatiotemporal data using the tensor BMD suggests that the BMD-based tensor analysis and methods can potentially handle more general data. Numerical experiments on the real-world video and hyperspectral image datasets verified the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm. In the future, we will investigate the significance of fine-tunning the parameters in the ADMM algorithm as well as an adaptive BM-rank selection mechanism in the decomposition.

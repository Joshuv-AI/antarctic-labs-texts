Normal form for transverse instability of gZK equation for the line soliton with nearly critical speed.
Yakine Bahri, Hichem Hajaiej

Abstract
In this paper, we study the transverse instability of generalized Zakharov-Kuznetsov equation for the line soliton with critical speed. We derive and justify a normal form reduction for a bifurcation problem of the stationary nonlinear KdV equation on the product space ℝ × 𝕋 \mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T} .



1
Introduction

We consider the generalized Zakharov–Kuznetsov (gZK) equation with a general power non-linearity

u_{t}+\partial_{x}(u^{k+1})+u_{xxx}+u_{xyy}=0,\quad(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T},

(1.1)

where
k\geq 2
.
It is well-known that the following quantities are conserved for smooth solutions of the ZK equation :

M(u)=\int u^{2}dxdy,

(1.2)

and

H(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int\big(|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{1}{k+2}u^{k+2}\big)dxdy.

(1.3)

The gZK equation admits a traveling wave solution. A traveling wave with speed
c
, is a particular solution of the form
u(t,x,y)=u_{c}(x-ct,y)
where
u_{c}
is a non-trivial solution to the following stationary equation,

-\partial_{xx}u_{c}-\partial_{yy}u_{c}+cu_{c}-u_{c}^{3}=0\qquad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}.

(1.4)

The main part of our work covers the instability of the line soliton over the 2D flow. We recall the definition of the orbital stability.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that a solitary wave
Q(x-ct,y)
is orbitally stable in
H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{L}\right)
if for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that for all initial data
u_{0}\in H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{L}\right)
with
\left\|u_{0}-Q\right\|_{H^{1}}<\delta
, the solution
u(t)
of
(1.1)
with
u(0)=u_{0}
exists globally in positive time and satisfies

\sup_{t>0}\inf_{\left(x_{0},y_{0}\right)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{L}}\left\|u(t,\cdot,\cdot)-Q\left(\cdot-x_{0},\cdot-y_{0}\right)\right\|_{H^{1}}<\varepsilon

Otherwise, we say the solitary wave
Q(x-ct,y)
is orbitally unstable in
H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{L}\right)
.

In the multidimensional case, de Bouard
[
4
]
showed the orbital stability of positive solitary waves of the generalized Zakharov–Kuznetsov equation while assuming the well-posedness on the energy space. For the two dimensional case, Côte, Muñoz, Pilod and Simpson
[
3
]
have proved the asymptotic stability of positive solitary waves and multi-solitary waves of the Zakharov–Kuznetsov equation using the argument of Martel and Merle
[
6
]
. A numerical study for two-dimensional solitary wave interactions and the formation of singularities in the modified Zakharov–Kuznetsov (mZK) equation was considered in
[
17
]
. This is a generalization of the Korteweg–deVries equation discussed in
[
15
]
.

Note that,
u_{c}(x,y)=u_{c}(x)
is also a solution in the energy space. In this case,
u_{c}
is the unique positive solution to

-\partial_{xx}u_{c}+cu_{c}-u_{c}^{k+1}=0\qquad\mbox{in $\mathbb{R}$}.

(1.5)

We recall that the explicit form of the solution is given by

u_{c}(x):=c^{\frac{1}{k}}\left(\frac{k+2}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}\sech^{\frac{2}{k}}\left(\frac{k}{2}\sqrt{c}\ x\right).

(1.6)

The stability of this traveling wave with respect to the KdV flow (the one-dimensional version of ZK) has been fully described in the literature. The orbital stability was proven to occur only for
k<4
(see
[
1
,
13
]
for more details). In addition, Martel and Merle proved in
[
6
]
the asymptotic stability in the energy space. The instability holds for any
k\geq 4
. We refer the reader to
[
2
]
for
k>4
and to
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
for the
L^{2}
critical case
k=4
. We conclude that the line traveling wave
u_{c}
is unstable under the ZK flow perturbation for
k\geq 4
. For this, we restrict our study to
2\leq k<4
.

For
k=1
, Yamazki studied in
[
18
]
the orbital and asymptotic transverse stability. He proved that the results depend on the speed of the traveling wave. More precisely, he showed that only traveling waves with subcritical as well as critical speed are asymptotically stable, and are unstable with the super-critical speed under a
2
D perturbation. He used the method of Evans’ function and the argument of Rousset and Tzvetkov
[
16
]
. The asymptotic stability for orbitally stable line solitary waves of Zakharov–Kuznetsov equation was proved by using the argument of Martel and Merle
[
6
]
and a Liouville type theorem combined with a modified virial type estimate since the linearized operator of the stationary equation is degenerate. In
[
20
]
, Yamazaki constructed center stable manifolds around unstable line solitary waves with critical speed. To recover the degeneracy of the linearized operator around line solitary waves with critical speed, the auther proved the stability condition of the center stable manifold for critical speed by applying to the estimate of the 4th order term of a Lyapunov function in
[
18
]
and
[
19
]
. In addition, Pelinovsky proved in
[
14
]
the same results using a normal form argument. The normal form was derived by means of
symplectic projections and near-identity transformations. The justification of this normal form is provided with an energy method. More precisely; the main result of the work was to derive and to justify the first-order differential equation

\frac{db}{dt}=\lambda^{\prime}\left(c_{*}\right)\left(c-c_{*}\right)b+\gamma|b|^{2}b,\quad t>0

(1.7)

where
\lambda^{\prime}\left(c_{*}\right)>0,\gamma<0
are real-valued numerical coefficients,
c_{*}
is the critical speed of the line soliton,
c\in\mathbb{R}
depends on the initial conditions, and
b(t):\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is an amplitude of transverse perturbation. The above differential equation describes the nonlinear dynamics of a small transverse perturbation of a fixed period to the line soliton with a nearly critical speed
c_{*}
and is referred to as ”normal form for transverse instability of the line soliton with a nearly critical speed of propagation”.

In this paper, our main goal is to derive the normal form (
1.7
) corresponding to (
1.1
). We will show that in our case that
\gamma>0
which implies instability of the line soliton with a nearly critical speed
c_{*}
. In the Appendix, we will prove a conjecture stated by Pelinovsky in
[
14
]
for the quadratic case (
k=1
).

1.1
Preliminaries

For the sake of simplicity, we will restrict our self to
k=2
while tackling the transverse instability. Note that, our argument applies to any
k\in[2,4)
. Let us consider

u_{t}+\partial_{x}(u^{3})+u_{xxx}+u_{xyy}=0,\quad(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T},

(1.8)

The one-dimensional linearized operator around the line soliton is given by

L_{c}:=-\partial^{2}_{x}+c-3u_{c}^{2}.

(1.9)

The Schrödinger operator
L_{c}:H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is known
[
5
]
to have the kernel spanned by
u^{\prime}_{c}
, the essential
spectrum located on
[c,\infty)
and a simple isolated negative eigenvalue

\lambda=-3c,\quad\psi=u_{c}^{2}.

Let us consider the
2\pi
-periodic transverse perturbation to the line solitons (
1.6
).
The linearized solution around the soliton
u(x,t)=u_{c}(\xi)+U(\xi)e^{\lambda t+iky}
with
\xi:=x-ct

and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
provided the following spectral problem at the linear level

\partial_{\xi}(L_{c}+k^{2})U=\lambda U,\quad k\in\mathbb{Z},

(1.10)

where
\partial_{\xi}L_{c}:H^{3}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is the linearized operator for the gKdV equation.

We recall that the spectrum of

\partial_{\xi}L_{c}:H^{3}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
consists of a double zero eigenvalue and a continuous spectrum on
i\mathbb{R}
(see
[
12
]
for more details). The double zero eigenvalue is associated with the following Jordan block of the operator
\partial_{\xi}L
:

\partial_{\xi}L_{c}\partial_{\xi}u_{c}=0,\quad\partial_{\xi}L_{c}\partial_{c}u_{c}=-\partial_{\xi}u_{c},

(1.11)

where the derivatives of
u_{c}
in
\xi
and
c
are exponentially decaying functions of
\xi
. The following lemma characterizes the spectral problem (
1.10
) for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 1.1
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the spectral problem (
1.10
)
has a pair of real eigenvalues
\pm\lambda_{k}(c)

if
c>c_{n}:=\frac{n^{2}}{3}
. No eigenvalues with
{\rm Re}(\lambda)\neq 0

exist if
c\in(0,c_{n})
.

Note that, it is possible to show nonlinear orbital stability of
the line soliton with
c\in(0,c_{*})
and nonlinear instability of the line soliton using energy method with
c>c_{*}
, where

c_{*}:=\min\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}c_{n}\equiv\frac{1}{3}.

In this work, we will consider the critical speed case i.e.
c=c_{*}
. We first study the spectral problem (
1.10
) in the exponentially weighted space ( see
[
12
,
13
]
for more details)

H^{s}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})=\left\{u\in H^{s}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}):\quad e^{\mu\xi}u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\right\},\quad s\geq 0,\quad\mu>0.

(1.12)

The following lemma reviews the spectral property of (
1.10
).

Lemma 1.2
.

There is
\mu_{0}>0
such that for every
\mu\in(0,\mu_{0})
, the spectral problem (
1.10
)
with
k=1
and
c=c_{*}
considered in
L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})
admits a simple zero eigenvalue with the eigenfunction

\psi_{*}\in H^{3}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})
and the adjoint eigenfunction
\eta_{*}\in H^{3}_{-\mu}(\mathbb{R})
, where

\psi_{*}(\xi)=2c_{*}{\rm sech}^{2}(\sqrt{c_{*}}\xi),\quad\eta_{*}(\xi)=2c_{*}\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}{\rm sech}^{2}(\sqrt{c_{*}}\xi^{\prime})d\xi^{\prime},

(1.13)

hence,

\langle\eta_{*},\psi_{*}\rangle_{L^{2}}=\frac{(2c_{*})^{2}}{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm sech}^{2}(\sqrt{c_{*}}\xi)d\xi\right)^{2}=8c_{*}.

(1.14)

Moreover, for a given
\mu\in(0,\mu_{0})
, there exists an interval
(c_{-},c_{+})
with

c_{-}<c_{*}<c_{+}
such that the spectral problem (
1.10
)
with
k=1
and
c\in(c_{-},c_{+})
considered in
L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})

admits a small eigenvalue
\lambda(c)
, where the mapping
c\mapsto\lambda
is smooth and
is given by

\lambda(c)=\lambda^{\prime}(c_{*})(c-c_{*})+\mathcal{O}((c-c_{*})^{2})\quad\mbox{\rm as}\quad c\to c_{*},

(1.15)

with

\lambda^{\prime}(c_{*})=2\sqrt{c_{*}}.

(1.16)

1.2
Instability result

We use a modulation argument to construct a translation parameter
a(t)
and a speed parameter
c(t)
of the line solitons as well as its perturbation
\tilde{u}(t)
defined in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})\cap H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
for every
t\in[0,T)
. This allows us to consider the traveling coordinate
\xi=x-a(t)
and use the decomposition

u(x,y,t)=u_{c(t)}(\xi)+\tilde{u}(\xi,y,t),\quad\xi=x-4a(t)

The time evolution of the varying parameters
a(t)
and
c(t)
and the perturbation term
\tilde{u}(t)
are to be found from the evolution problem

\tilde{u}_{t}=\partial_{\xi}\left(L_{c}-\partial_{y}^{2}+4(\dot{a}-c)\right)\tilde{u}+4(\dot{a}-c)\partial_{\xi}u_{c}-\dot{c}\partial_{c}u_{c}-6\partial_{\xi}\tilde{u}^{2}

where the differential expression for
L_{c}
is the linearized operator around the line soliton (see (
1.9
) for more details).

The following theorem represents the normal form for transverse instability of the line soliton with a nearly critical speed of propagation.

Theorem 1.1
.

Consider the Cauchy problem for the evolution equation (
1.1
) with

\tilde{u}(0)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})\cap H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}),

where
\mu>0
is sufficiently small. There exist
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
C_{0}>0
such that if the initial data satisfy the bound

\left\|\tilde{u}(0)-2\varepsilon\cos(y)\psi_{*}\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})\cap H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}+\left|c(0)-c_{*}\right|\leqslant\varepsilon^{2},

for some
\varepsilon\in\left(0,\varepsilon_{0}\right)
, then there exist unique functions
a,b,c\in C^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}\right),
and the unique solution

\tilde{u}(t)\in C\left(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})\cap H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})\right)

of the evolution equation (
1.1
) satisfying the bound

\left\|\tilde{u}(t)-\left(b(t)e^{iy}+\bar{b}(t)e^{-iy}\right)\psi_{*}\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}+\left|c(t)-c_{*}\right|+|\dot{a}(t)-c(t)|\leqslant C_{0}\varepsilon^{2},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Furthermore, the function
b(t)
satisfies the normal form

\dot{b}=\lambda^{\prime}\left(c_{*}\right)\left(c_{+}-c_{*}\right)b+\gamma|b|^{2}b,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},

with
b(0)=\varepsilon
and
c_{+}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\left|c_{+}-c_{*}\right|\leqslant C_{0}\varepsilon^{2}
, where
\lambda^{\prime}\left(c_{*}\right)>0
is given by (2.13) and
\gamma>0
is a specific numerical coefficient given by (
2.50
) below. Consequently,
|b(t)|>\frac{C_{0}}{\varepsilon}
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

2
Transverse Instability

2.1
Transversely modulated solitary waves

In this section, we construct a solution to (
1.4
) which bifurcates from the line soliton with critical speed. We define the space of even functions
both in
\xi
and
y
:

H^{s}_{\rm even}=\left\{u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}):\quad u(-\xi,y)=u(\xi,y)=u(\xi,-y)\right\},\quad s\geq 0.

(2.1)

The following lemma describes this bifurcation.

Lemma 2.1
.

There exists
c_{+}>c_{*}
such that for every
c\in(c_{*},c_{+})
,
the nonlinear elliptic problem (
1.4
) has a nontrivial solution
u_{b}
in
H^{2}_{\rm even}

in addition to the line soliton (
1.6
). The solution
u_{b}
is expressed by
the expansion

u_{b}(\xi,y)=u_{c_{*}}(\xi)+b\cos(y)\psi_{*}(\xi)+\tilde{u}_{b}(\xi,y),

(2.2)

where
b\in\mathbb{R}
is a nonzero root of the algebraic equation

\alpha(c-c_{*})b+\beta b^{3}=0

(2.3)

and
\tilde{u}_{b}\in H^{2}_{\rm even}
satisfies the bound
\|\tilde{u}_{b}\|_{H^{2}}\leq Ab^{2}

for a positive constant
A
independently of
b
and
c
. Here

\alpha=8(c_{*})^{\frac{3}{2}}>0

and
\beta<0
is a numerical coefficient given by (
2.16
) below.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the one of Lemma 1.3 in
[
14
]

and relies on the method of Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction
[
11
]
.
We denote by
v_{1}(\xi,y):=\cos(y)\psi_{*}(\xi)
the eigenfunction of the kernel of
L_{c_{*}}-\partial_{y}^{2}:H^{2}_{\rm even}\to L^{2}_{\rm even}
. Let
c=c_{*}+\delta
with
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
being
sufficiently small. We plug the decomposition (
2.2
) in (
1.4
), to obtain the projection equations
of the Lyapunov–Schmidt reduction method:

(L_{c_{*}}-\partial_{y}^{2}+\delta)\tilde{u}_{b}=\Pi\tilde{F},

(2.4)

with

\tilde{F}:=-\delta u_{c_{*}}-\delta bv_{1}+(bv_{1}+\tilde{u}_{b})^{3}+3u_{c_{*}}(bv_{1}+\tilde{u}_{b})^{2}

(2.5)

and
\Pi
is an orthogonal projection operator in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
on the subspace spanned by
v_{1}
.
This means that

\frac{1}{2\pi}\langle v_{1},\tilde{F}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}=0,

which yields

-\frac{\delta}{2}b\|\psi_{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\frac{3}{8}b^{3}\|\psi_{*}\|^{4}_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R})}+\frac{3}{\pi}b\langle v_{1}^{2},u_{c_{*}}\tilde{u}_{b}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}+\mathcal{O}(b^{5})=0.

(2.6)

Since the kernel of the linear operator

L_{c_{*}}-\partial_{y}^{2}:H^{2}_{\rm even}\to L^{2}_{\rm even}
is one-dimensional, we know that there are
B>0
and
\delta_{0}>0
such that

\|\Pi(L_{c_{*}}-\partial_{y}^{2}+4\delta)^{-1}\Pi\|_{L^{2}_{\rm even}\to L^{2}_{\rm even}}\leq B,\quad\forall|\delta|<\delta_{0}.

(2.7)

Thus, for every small
b\in\mathbb{R}
and small
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
,
we apply the fixed-point argument to
solve equation (
2.4
) with (
2.5
) in
H^{2}_{\rm even}

and to obtain a unique
\tilde{u}_{b}\in H^{2}_{\rm even}
satisfying the bound

\|\tilde{u}_{b}\|_{H^{2}}\leq A(|\delta|+b^{2}),

(2.8)

where the positive constant
A
is independent of
\delta
and
b
. Now, we shall derive the algebraic equation (
2.3
). To do so, we perform a near-identity transformation

\tilde{u}_{b}(\xi,y)=b^{2}\cos(2y)w_{2}(\xi)+b^{2}w_{0}(\xi)+\delta\partial_{c}u_{c_{*}}(\xi)+\tilde{w}_{b}(\xi,y),

(2.9)

where

w_{0}
and
w_{2}
are given by

L_{c_{*}}w_{0}=\frac{3}{2}u_{c_{*}}\psi_{*}^{2}

(2.10)

and

(L_{c_{*}}+4)w_{2}=\frac{3}{2}u_{c_{*}}\psi_{*}^{2}.

(2.11)

On the other hand,
\tilde{w}_{b}
satisfies the transformed equation

\displaystyle(L_{c_{*}}-\partial_{y}^{2}+\delta)\tilde{w}_{b}=\Pi\tilde{G},

(2.12)

with

\displaystyle\tilde{G}:=-\delta^{2}\partial_{c}u_{c_{*}}-\delta b^{2}\cos(2y)w_{2}-\delta b^{2}w_{0}-\delta bv_{1}+6bu_{c_{*}}v_{1}\tilde{u}_{b}+3u_{c_{*}}\tilde{u}_{b}^{2}++(bv_{1}+\tilde{u}_{b})^{3}

for
\tilde{u}_{b}
is defined by (
2.9
). Similarly to the above competition, for every small
b\in\mathbb{R}
and
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
,
there exists a unique solution
\tilde{w}\in H^{2}_{\rm even}
of equation (
2.12
) satisfying the bound

\|\tilde{w}\|_{H^{2}}\leq A(\delta^{2}+|\delta||b|+|b|^{3}),

(2.13)

where the positive constant
A
is independent of
\delta
and
b
.

Substituting the near-identity transformation (
2.9
) into the bifurcation equation
(
2.6
) and using the bound (
2.13
)
for the component
\tilde{w}\in H^{2}_{\rm even}
, we rewrite
(
2.6
) in the equivalent form

\alpha\delta b+\beta b^{3}+\mathcal{O}(\delta b^{2},b^{4})=0,

(2.14)

where we have introduced numerical coefficients
\alpha
and
\beta
as follows:

2\alpha:=-\|\psi_{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+6\langle\psi_{*}^{2},u_{c_{*}}\partial_{c}u_{c_{*}}\rangle_{L^{2}}

(2.15)

and

\beta:=\frac{3}{4}\langle\psi_{*}^{2}u_{c_{*}},2w_{0}+w_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}}+\frac{3}{8}\|\psi_{*}\|^{4}_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R})}.

(2.16)

Plugging (
1.6
) and (
1.13
) into (
2.15
), we get

\alpha=-\frac{1}{2}\langle\psi_{*},L_{c_{*}}^{\prime}\psi_{*}\rangle_{L^{2}}=8(c_{*})^{\frac{3}{2}},

where
L_{c_{*}}^{\prime}:=1-6u_{c_{*}}\partial_{c}u_{c_{*}}
. On the other hand, we are not able to provide an explicit form to
\beta
. This is why we will determine a bound to it. We first compute
w_{0}
using (
2.10
). More precisely, we have

w_{0}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\left(u_{c_{*}}^{3}-4c_{*}u_{c_{*}}\right).

(2.17)

For
w_{2}
, since
L_{c_{*}}+4:H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is strictly positive, we just write equation (
2.11
) as
w_{2}=\frac{3}{2}(L_{c_{*}}+4)^{-1}(u_{c_{*}}\psi_{*}^{2}).
. Thus, using the bound
\|(L_{c_{*}}+4)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1}{3}
, we obtain

\beta\leq\frac{3}{2}\langle\psi_{*}^{2}u_{c_{*}},w_{0}\rangle_{L^{2}}+\frac{3}{8}\|\psi_{*}\|^{4}_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R})}+\frac{1}{4}\|\psi_{*}^{2}u_{c_{*}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\frac{2^{6}}{315}(c_{*})^{\frac{7}{2}}\left(-45+\frac{32}{3}\right)<0.

(2.18)

Here, we have used

\displaystyle\|\psi_{*}\|^{4}_{L^{4}}=\frac{2^{9}}{35}(c_{*})^{\frac{7}{2}},\quad\langle\psi_{*}^{2}u_{c_{*}},w_{0}\rangle_{L^{2}}=-\frac{2^{10}}{105}(c_{*})^{\frac{7}{2}}\quad\|\psi_{*}^{2}u_{c_{*}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\frac{2^{13}}{315}(c_{*})^{\frac{9}{2}}.

This finishes the proof of this lemma.
∎

2.2
Derivation and justification of the normal form

In this section, we derive and justify the normal form. We follow the steps of the proof in
[
14
]
.

2.2.1
Modulation equations for parameters
a
and
c

We start by stating the modulation theory in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
T>0
. There exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
,
\mu_{0}>0
, and
C_{0}>0
such that if

u\in C([0,T),H^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})\cap H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}))

with
\mu\in(0,\mu_{0})
is a solution to the ZK equation (
1.8
) satisfying

\varepsilon:=\inf_{a\in\mathbb{R}}\|u(x+a,y,t)-u_{c_{*}}(x)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})\cap H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\leq\varepsilon_{0},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},

(2.19)

then there exist
a,c\in C([0,T))
and
\tilde{u}\in C([0,T),H^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})\cap H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}))

such that the decomposition

u(x,y,t)=u_{c(t)}(\xi)+\tilde{u}(\xi,y,t),\quad\xi=x-a(t)

(2.20)

holds with
\tilde{u}(t)\in[X_{c(t)}^{*}]^{\perp}
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, where

[X_{c(t)}^{*}]^{\perp}=\left\{\tilde{u}\in L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}):\quad\langle u_{c(t)},\tilde{u}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}=\langle\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial_{c}u_{c(t)},\tilde{u}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}=0\right\}.

(2.21)

Note that the modulation parameters
a,c\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

satisfy the following system

\displaystyle S\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\dot{c}\\
\dot{a}-c\end{array}\right]=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\langle\partial_{c}u_{c},\tilde{u}^{3}+3u_{c}\tilde{u}^{2}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\\
\langle\partial_{\xi}u_{c},\tilde{u}^{3}+3u_{c}\tilde{u}^{2}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\end{array}\right]

with the coefficient matrix

S:=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{2}(M^{\prime}(c))^{2}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\langle\partial_{\xi}^{-1}\partial^{2}_{c}u_{c},\tilde{u}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}&P^{\prime}(c)+\frac{1}{2\pi}\langle\partial_{c}u_{c},\tilde{u}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\\

Remark 2.1
.

Since
\gamma>0
, the zero solution to (
2.49
) is unstable.

Appendix A
The quadratic case.

For k=1, Pelinovsky used in
[
14
]
numerical simulations in order to show that
\gamma<0
. We provide a complete proof of that claim. First, we recall that in the quadratic case
\gamma
is given by

\gamma:=12\langle\psi_{*}^{2},w_{0}+w_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}}+144\langle\psi_{*}^{2},\partial_{c}u_{c_{*}}\rangle_{L^{2}}-48\|\psi_{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

where
w_{0}
and
w_{2}
are solutions to the following linear inhomogeneous equations:

L_{c_{*}}w_{0}=12\psi_{*}^{2}

and

\left(L_{c_{*}}+4\right)w_{2}=6\psi_{*}^{2}

with

L_{c}:=-\partial^{2}_{x}+c-2u_{c},\quad\psi_{*}(\xi)=\operatorname{sech}^{3}\left(\sqrt{c_{*}}\xi\right)\quad{\rm and}\quad c_{*}=\frac{1}{5}.

From
[
14
]
, we recall that

\langle\psi_{*}^{2},w_{0}\rangle_{L^{2}}=\frac{-160}{21\sqrt{c}_{*}},\quad\langle\psi_{*}^{2},\partial_{c}u_{c_{*}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=\frac{4}{5\sqrt{c}_{*}},\quad{\rm and}\quad\|\psi_{*}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\frac{16}{15\sqrt{c}_{*}}.

On the other hand, we know that

w_{2}=6(L_{c_{*}}+4)^{-1}(\psi_{*}^{2}).

Thus, from the bound
\|(L_{c_{*}}+4)^{-1}\|\leq 1/3,
we have

\langle\psi_{*}^{2},w_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}}\leq 2\|\psi_{*}^{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\frac{1024}{693\sqrt{c}_{*}}.

Finally, we conclude that

\gamma\leq 12\left(-\frac{160}{21\sqrt{c}_{*}}+\frac{1024}{693\sqrt{c}_{*}}\right)+\frac{576}{5\sqrt{c}_{*}}-\frac{256}{5\sqrt{c}_{*}}=-12\frac{4256}{693\sqrt{c}_{*}}+\frac{64}{\sqrt{c}_{*}}=-\frac{6720}{693\sqrt{c}_{*}}<0.
